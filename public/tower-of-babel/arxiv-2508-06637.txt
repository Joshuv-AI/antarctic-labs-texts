Double-functorial representation of regular structures
José Siqueira

Abstract
It is well-known that bicategories of spans capture the Beck-Chevalley relationship underlying the adjunctions suitable for the semantics of existential quantification. By viewing hyperdoctrines from a double-categorical lens, this paper shows that we can also characterise the Frobenius property: (generalised) regular hyperdoctrines correspond to those lax symmetric monoidal double pseudofunctors from spans to quintets whose monoidal laxators provide companion commuter cells (in the sense of Paré). This suggests a new notion of regular double hyperdoctrine . As an application, we discuss how we can recover a form of graphical regular logic suitable for modelling specifications of systems (e.g., port-plugging systems) that compose operadically.

Introduction

One of the most fruitful modern insights into logic was Lawvere’s observation that quantifiers ought to be thought of as adjoint functors

[
13
]
, which is at the heart
of the notion of
hyperdoctrine
: an indexed poset (whose fibres we think of as
predicates
of a certain type)
that satisfies properties enabling the adequate
interpretation of a target fragment of logic (chiefly the Beck-Chevalley condition). Fundamentally, this means that the data of a logical doctrine
manifests itself in two directions: that of
context operations
, and that of the
action on predicates
.

Double Category Theory provides the tools to capture these two actions and
their relationship concomitantly: in a double category of spans, re-indexing
and acting on predicates can be packaged as tight and loose arrows respectively,
and in there tight arrows always have suitably constrained conjoints. By mapping these conjoints into
a double category of quintets, we obtain adjunctions internal to a 2-category
(classically, of posets) which satisfy the Beck-Chevalley condition, and thus serve
as semantics for substitution and existential quantification.

What was described thus far is well-known in the literature. The way spans come into the picture is very natural if one is interested in logical semantics: start with a category and add adjoints to every morphism,
but make them constrained by the Beck-Chevalley condition so that you can define quantification. If the category
had pullbacks to begin with, you end up with a bicategory of spans (c.f.
[
3
]
). The first record of the universal property of spans and the relationship to
the Beck-Chevalley condition was given by Hermida
[
11
]
and referred to as folklore. Dawson, Paré and Pronk further explored spans as a bicategory and the adjointness structure

[
4
]
: sinister morphisms of bicategories
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}

(i.e., whose morphism images are left adjoints) satisfying the Beck-Chevalley condition are equivalent to strong morphisms
\mathcal{S}pan(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{B}
.
The same authors extended this to a double categorical picture in
[
5
]
, where the notions of
gregarious double category
(also called
fibrant double categories

or
framed bicategories
in
[
17
]
) and Beck-Chevalley double category where introduced. Notably, the double-categorical span construction
provides a canonical way to obtain a Beck-Chevalley double category out of a category with pullbacks, and has better universal properties than its bicategorical counterpart.

The main contribution of this paper is to extend these ideas to the study of
regular logic
and analogous systems: by thinking in terms of double
functors and factoring in a monoidal structure, one can also capture the Frobenius condition for regular hyperdoctrines. In its simplest form, our main result establishes
a correspondence between regular hyperdoctrines
P
over a cartesian category
\mathsf{C}
of contexts and certain (cartesian) symmetric
monoidal double functors
Q\colon\mathbb{S}\mathsf{pan}(\mathsf{C})^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\to\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathsf{Pos})
— those for which
the cell components of the monoidal laxator are
commuters
in the sense of Paré (see
[
16
]
).

There is no reason to restrict ourselves to
\land
-semilattices and classical regular logic, though. More generally,
we will allow the fibres of
P

to be symmetric pseudo-monoids in a fixed cartesian 2-category
\mathcal{K}

(which are still subject to the Beck-Chevalley and Frobenius conditions).
We refer to such analogues of regular hyperdoctrines as
regular indexed monoidal structures
in
\mathcal{K}
.

The idea for this concept is implicit in works like
[

, but not formalised.

Moreover, the indexing category

might only have pullbacks (and satisfy Beck-Chevalley) for certain kinds of morphisms,

Theorem 1.1

(Double functorial representation of regular indexed monoidal structures)
.

Let
(\mathsf{C},L,R)
be a cartesian adequate triple,
and
\mathcal{K}
be a cartesian monoidal 2-category. Every
(\mathsf{C},L,R)
-regular
indexed monoidal structure in
\mathcal{K}
can be extended to a lax symmetric monoidal double pseudofunctor

\mathbb{S}\mathsf{pan}(\mathsf{C},L,R)^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\to\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})
whose laxators are companion commuter cells. Conversely,
if the class
L
includes all diagonals, then the tight component of every such double pseudofunctor is a
(\mathsf{C},L,R)
-regular
indexed monoidal structure in
\mathcal{K}
.

This paper will be approached in an elementary fashion. Section
2
introduces the preliminary notions required on both the logical and double
categorical sides, as well as some representative examples. We recall the definition of
adequate triple
needed for a general span construction, then
discuss the notion of
regular indexed monoidal structure
and how it captures
natural structures analogous to regular hyperdoctrines. The reader versed in double category theory may wish to skip the remainder of this
section, but should check the notational conventions made and note that we work with
double pseudofunctors
and
tight pseudotransformations

— this is because in some desirable instances the tight (vertical) component of our double mapping is only pseudofunctorial.

Section
3
is divided in two subsections, each of which is dedicated to proving one direction of the representation theorem: in Subsection
3.1

we show how an indexed monoidal structure can be extended to a suitable type of double pseudofunctor, and in Subsection
3.2
we argue that one extra
condition on the adequate triples (which is trivially satisfied for the conventional spans) yields a converse. The final section
4
is comprised
of a discussion on how this result suggests a broader notion of regular hyperdoctrine, allowing for potential
actions on predicates that do not necessarily arise from quantification, as well as an application to the double categorical
theory of systems (as in
[
14
]
). We conclude by mentioning a few directions for future work.

The simplest notion of regular hyperdoctrine views a category

with finite limits as a
category of contexts
and associates to each object

assumed that each resulting map has a left adjoint (as a map of orderings) that serves the role of existential quantification. These adjunctions are then subject to the
Beck-Chevalley and Frobenius conditions, stating that substitution is suitably compatible with quantification.

one also broadens the notion of predicate to allow for richer structures than orderings, potentially replacing

the following notion:

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{0.3608,0.3608,0.8392}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f,\mathrm{id}_{A})}

.

The latter (hence the former) is the inverse of
\hat{B}
by definition, whence the overall composite
is the identity on
Q(\Delta_{B})\circ\mu_{B,B}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{QB}\times\exists f)
. Composing the other way also yields
the identity, by a similar computation.∎

Putting the results together, we can prove:

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(\mathsf{C},L,R)
be a cartesian adequate triple,
and
\mathcal{K}
be a cartesian monoidal 2-category. Every
(\mathsf{C},L,R)
-regular
indexed monoidal structure in
\mathcal{K}
can be extended to a lax symmetric monoidal double pseudofunctor

\mathbb{S}\mathsf{pan}(\mathsf{C},L,R)^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\to\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})
whose laxators are companion commuter cells. Conversely,
if the class
L
includes all diagonals, then the tight component of every such double pseudofunctor is a
(\mathsf{C},L,R)
-regular
indexed monoidal structure in
\mathcal{K}
.

Proof.

If
P
is a
(\mathsf{C},L,R)
-regular indexed monoidal structure in
\mathcal{K}
, then
(P^{\bullet})_{0}=P
as lax symmetric monoidal pseudofunctors by construction.

To see that
Q=(Q_{0})^{\bullet}
for any double functor
Q\colon\mathbb{S}\mathsf{pan}(\mathsf{C},L,R)^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\to\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})
, note that any span
X=X_{1}\xleftarrow{a_{1}}X\xrightarrow{x_{2}}X_{2}
can be expressed as
x_{1}^{*}\odot{x_{2}}_{!}
, and therefore
its image under
Q
is
Q(x_{1}^{*})\odot Q({x_{2}}_{!})=\exists x_{2}\circ Q(x_{1})=Q_{0}(X)
. Similarly, as pointed out in p. 305 of
[
5
]
, every morphisms of spans
can be factored as a composite of identities and maps of the form
\hat{\eta^{f}}
and
\hat{\epsilon}^{f}
. Doing so shows that
Q
agrees with
(Q_{0})^{\bullet}
on cells.

If
Q
is lax symmetric monoidal with
laxator
\mu
, we further have that
\mu_{X,Y}=\mu_{x_{1}^{*}\odot{x_{2}}_{!},y_{1}^{*}\odot{y_{2}}_{!}}=\mu_{x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*}}\mid\mu_{{x_{2}}_{!},{y_{2}}_{!}}
, since

\mu
is a tight transformation. We previously established that
\mu_{x_{1}^{*},y_{1}^{*}}=(\mu_{0})_{x_{1},y_{1}}^{-1}
and that
\mu_{{x_{2}}_{!},{y_{2}}_{!}}
is
the mate of
\mu_{x_{2},y_{2}}
as cells in
\mathcal{K}
, and therefore
\mu_{X,Y}=(\mu_{0})^{\bullet}
.

It is then clear that the maps
P\mapsto P^{\bullet}
and
Q\mapsto Q_{0}
are inverse to each other.∎

4
Discussion

We have shown that generalised regular hyperdoctrines correspond to certain
double pseudofunctors
Q
between (the opposite of) a double category of spans and a quintet double category. In this construction,
the key features being used are that spans form a cartesian Beck-Chevalley double category (in the sense of
[
5
]
), that

\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})
is cartesian and has all companions, and that the monoidal constraints of
Q
are companion commuters. In this
way, we could equally deem, for example, a lax symmetric monoidal double pseudofunctor
\mathbb{R}\mathsf{el}(\mathcal{E})^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\to\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})

with companion commuter constraints to be a type of hyperdoctrine. This
suggests the following as a more general notion of regular hyperdoctrine:

Definition 4.1

(Regular double hyperdoctrine)
.

Let
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{tx}
be a cartesian Beck-Chevalley double category and
\mathbb{D}
be a symmetric monoidal double category.
A
regular double hyperdoctrine
over
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{tx}
with predicates in
\mathbb{D}
is a lax symmetric monoidal double pseudofunctor

Q\colon\mathbb{C}\mathsf{tx}^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\to\mathbb{D}
such that:

•

For each pair of objects
A,B\in\mathbb{C}\mathsf{tx}
, the tight component
\mu_{A,B}\colon QA\otimes QB\to Q(A\times B)
of the
monoidal laxator of
Q
has a companion in
\mathbb{D}
;

•

For each pair of loose arrows
X,Y\in\mathbb{C}\mathsf{tx}
, the cell component
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{QX_{1}\otimes QY_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{QX_{2}\otimes QY_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Q(X_{1}\times Y_{1})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Q(X_{2}\times Y_{2})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces QX\otimes QY}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\shortmid}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{X_{1},Y_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{X_{2},Y_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q(X\times Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\shortmid}
\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{0.3608,0.3608,0.8392}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{X,Y}}

is a companion commuter cell.

Naturally, this could be weakened just as for traditional hyperdoctrines by factoring in a cartesian adequate triple — we relax the assumption that
\mathbb{C}tx
is
a Beck-Chevalley double category by just assuming
that there is a cartesian adequate triple structure on
(\mathbb{C}tx)_{0}
, demand the existence of
companions to maps in the left class and conjoints for maps in the right class, and ask that, given any adequate tight pullback

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

,

the induced square

in
\mathbb{C}tx
is a
conjoint commuter
. Note the duality that Beck-Chevalley asks for
conjoint
commuters, while Frobenius requires
companion
commuters.

The conceptual picture is that the tight component of
Q
moderates adjustments to contexts, while the loose components encode the
possible actions on predicates. This is in line with the zeitgeist of double categorical logic, where the tight dimension handles a theory and the loose
maps handle models (c.f.
[
12
]
).

Note that for
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{tx}=\mathbb{S}\mathsf{pan}(\mathsf{C},L,R)
and
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{tx}=\mathbb{R}\mathsf{el}(\mathcal{E})
, regular double hyperdoctrines are “loosely trivial",
in the sense that they are fully determined by their tight components. This is because both those
double categories of contexts have effective tabulators, enabling us to canonically factor any loose maps as a composite of companions and conjoints
and run the argument in the proof of Theorem
3.8
. This is not the case for other possible cartesian Beck-Chevalley double categories of contexts, such as

\mathbb{C}\mathsf{tx}=\mathsf{V}\mathbb{M}at
, where
V
is a monoidal category with pullbacks and whose tensor distribute over sums (see
[
5
]
).
It is thus plausible to obtain genuinely new notions of hyperdoctrine from such contexts, where the actions on predicates don’t necessarily arise
from substitution and quantification alone.

These ideas can be applied to the double categorical approach to compositionality of systems, where a theory of systems
is interpreted as a symmetric monoidal loose right module
on a symmetric monoidal double category. Theorem 3.3.2.2 in
[
14
]
gives that the restriction of a loose bimodule
along a lax symmetric monoidal pseudo-double functor with companion commuter monoidal constraints is again symmetric monoidal. More precisely, if

P\colon\mathsf{C}^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\to\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})
is a
(\mathsf{C},L,R)
-regular indexed monoidal structure in
\mathcal{K}
, then we can
restrict the bimodule
\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})(-,-)
along the
P^{\bullet}\colon\mathbb{S}\mathsf{pan}(\mathsf{C},L,R)^{op}\to\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})
on the right
(and
*\xrightarrow{*}\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})
on the left) to obtain a new systems theory
\mathbb{Q}\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{K})(*,P^{\bullet})
. A system for this theory consists
of a context
\Gamma\in\mathsf{C}
, together with a predicate
\phi\in P(\Gamma)
. Its interfaces are objects of
\mathsf{C}
, and interactions are given by the logical action of
(\mathcal{C},L,R)
-spans.

As a particular case of interest, let
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{P}os
,
T
be a finite set of labels and
\mathsf{C}=(\mathsf{Finset}\downarrow T)^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}
be the free cartesian category on
T
.
If
P\colon\mathcal{C}^{{}^{\mathsf{op}}}\to\mathcal{P}os
is a conventional regular hyperdoctrine, then the restriction above provides a theory of systems
with
\mathbb{C}\mathsf{ospan}(\mathsf{Finset}\downarrow T)
as its symmetric monoidal double category of interfaces and interactions. Its loose part is then a hypergraph category in the sense of
[
7
]
;
we can interpret the cospans of labelled finite sets as
undirected wiring diagrams
, which can then be used to model the operadic compositionality of port-plugging systems
[
19
]
. The construction in this paper therefore explains how substitution and existential quantification should be performed when undirected wiring diagrams are composed by nesting.
This provides a way to reason about the specifications of such systems via a graphical regular logic — it is very similar to the mechanism explained in
[
6
]
, but here we use the free cartesian
category on
T
rather than the free regular category on
T
, at the cost of assuming inhabitedness in the logic (see Remark 4.12 in
[
6
]
).

There are a few clear directions for further work. We chose to focus on regular logic in this paper, but in order to capture first-order or linear logic in a similar fashion, one would like to speak of doctrines for which
the fibres are monoidal-closed. While there is some nuance to consider, this should not be a difficult task
given Shulman’s work on
closed monoidal fibrations

[
17
]
; we plan to do so in a future article, alongside a proper exploration of double regular hyperdoctrines. Finally,
a suitable double-categorical notion of
generic predicate
(in the sense of tripos theory) is desirable, so that higher-order logic can also be put into this double-categorical framework.

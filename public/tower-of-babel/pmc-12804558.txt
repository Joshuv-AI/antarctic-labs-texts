Combining machine learning and molecular docking to unravel the molecular network of bladder cancer induced by 2-naphthylamine
Authors: Ruize Qin, Cheng Li, Changxue Liu, Chengquan Shen, Ding Hu, Xinzhao Zhao, Huaixi Ge, Jianhang Zhu, Yonghua Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveBladder cancer (BLCA) is among the most common malignant tumors found in the urinary tract. However, the carcinogenic mechanism of 2-Naphthylamine (2-NA), a class I carcinogen with a well-established association with BLCA, remains inadequately elucidated. The objective of research is to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying 2-NA-induced BLCA.MethodsAnalysis of differential expression was performed on various datasets to pinpoint target genes linked to BLCA. We used a comprehensive process integrating machine learning, network toxicology, molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations to study the binding interactions between 2-NA and target proteins.ResultsOur research identified 29 potential targets implicated in 2-NA-induced BLCA. Subsequent analyses utilizing machine learning techniques prioritized 14 core genes (SKP2, SCN11A, DHCR7, AURKB, PFKFB4, EBP, CA2, NUDT1, DUSP2, KIF20A, BLM, IGFBP2, EZH2, TPX2) as pivotal regulators. SKP2 and EBP were identified as the most impactful predictors, showing notable effects in advancing bladder cancer. Furthermore, molecular docking simulations and molecular dynamics simulations indicated strong binding specificity between 2-NA and the target proteins SKP2 and EBP.ConclusionThe findings of our study indicate 2-NA potentially facilitates BLCA pathogenesis by influencing specific genes and signaling pathways. Utilizing machine learning techniques, 14 key genes were identified. Subsequent molecular docking analyses and molecular dynamics simulations verified the strong binding affinity of 2-NA to the critical targets SKP2 and EBP. These results provide valuable insights for further mechanistic investigations into 2-NA-induced bladder carcinogenesis.

Introduction

Bladder cancer (BLCA) ranks among the most prevalent malignant tumors of the urinary system, occupying the 9th position in incidence and the 13th in mortality among systemic malignant tumors [1]. The pathogenesis of BLCA is a multifaceted process characterized by multiple factors and stages, primarily involving prolonged exposure to specific carcinogens. Notably, aromatic amines found in tobacco smoke and certain industrial chemicals are implicated in this process, leading to genetic damage and epigenetic modifications in the epithelial cells of the urinary tract [2, 3]. Research in epidemiology has shown that individuals with chronic occupational exposure to 2-naphthylamine (2-NA) exhibit a BLCA incidence rate that is several-fold higher than that of the general population, highlighting the significant impact of this exposure as one of the most potent environmental risk factors for BLCA [4].

At the molecular level, bladder carcinogenesis is driven by mutations in critical genes and disruptions in signaling pathways, causing urinary tract epithelial cells to transform malignantly. This transformation is facilitated by a combination of genomic instability, epigenetic dysregulation, and a chronic inflammatory microenvironment [5].

2-Na has been classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) as a Group I carcinogen, particularly associated with BLCA. Cigarette smoke and fumes from heated cooking oils are the primary exposure sources for the general public [6]. The bladder carcinogenesis initiated by 2-NA involves its metabolic activation in the liver, mediated by CYP1A2-catalyzed N-hydroxylation, following dermal or inhalation exposure. The resultant metabolite is subsequently excreted into the bladder via the renal system, where it undergoes further transformation by the enzyme β-glucuronidase, leading to the release of highly reactive electrophilic aryl hydroxylamine intermediates. These intermediates directly interact with uroepithelial DNA, forming guanine C8/N² adducts and inducing characteristic G→T transversion mutations. Such mutations result in critical alterations in gene function, including the inactivation of TP53, which compromises DNA repair mechanisms and promotes genomic instability; the activation of HRAS, which perpetuates the RAS-MAPK pro-proliferative signaling pathway; and aberrant receptor tyrosine kinase signaling due to mutations in the FGFR3 locus [7, 8]. These genetic alterations act synergistically to drive the carcinogenic process. Epidemiological data revealed an 86.7-fold increase in mortality due to BLCA within a UK cohort of 2-NA producers, thereby affirming a robust dose-response relationship [9]. Furthermore, animal studies demonstrated that dogs developed BLCA following 26 months of oral 2-NA administration at a dosage of 6.25 mg/kg per day [10]. Similarly, rats exhibited a progression from uroepithelial hyperplasia to bladder cancer after prolonged exposure to 2-NA at a dosage of 300 mg/kg per week [11].

The emergence of network toxicology has instigated a paradigm shift in the analysis of complex toxicant-host interactions by systematically elucidating the cascading effects of oncogenic pathways through the integration of dynamic network analysis and computational structure-activity relationships [12]. The carcinogenic potential of 2-NA in bladder cancer has been corroborated by epidemiological studies and related animal experiments, although certain limitations persist. This study adopts a triangular systems toxicology approach, which includes: the reconstruction of networks involved in 2-NA-driven bladder cancer pathogenesis through multi-omics data integration; the application of machine learning for the identification of key target genes via topological and functional enrichment analysis; and lineage molecular docking to validate target splicing and interaction thermodynamics. By elucidating the multi-scale oncogenic network of 2-NA, this research aims to identify druggable targets for the chemoprevention and precision treatment of bladder cancer, thereby addressing the clinical needs of high-risk occupationally exposed populations.

Materials and methods

Obtaining targets associated with diseases

Four transcriptome datasets related to BLCA (GSE13507, GSE37815, GSE7476, and GSE65635) were extracted from the NCBI GEO database. GSE13507 and GSE37815 were designated as the discovery cohort, whereas GSE7476 and GSE65635 were utilized as the validation cohort. The comprehensive normalization strategy was employed to tackle batch effects effectively. Initially, in the discovery cohort, potential confounding variables were identified and adjusted for utilizing the Surrogate Variable Analysis (SVA) package. Subsequent harmonization was achieved through the ComBat method, which operates on a parametric empirical Bayesian framework. Finally, sample clustering was assessed using corrected Principal Component Analysis (PCA) in a reduceddimensional space, which demonstrated effective clustering of samples across different batches.

Acquisition of 2-NA chemical composition and targets

Acquisition of 2-NA Chemical Composition and Targets: The characterization of 2-Naphthylamine was achieved by integrating multiple databases. The physicochemical characteristics and biological metrics were methodically gathered from PubMed, along with the standard 2D structural descriptors [SMILES: C1 = CC = C2C = C(C = CC2 = C1) N], which were sourced from the PubChem database. Target predictions were conducted using a tripartite method: the ChEMBL database provided profiles of interactions between ligands and receptors; SwissTargetPrediction offered predictions based on chemogenomics and PharmMapper utilized 3D pharmacophore alignment.

Analysis of differential gene expression

The limma package was used to analyze transcriptome data, identifying differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with FDR-corrected thresholds of P < 0.05 and log2FC > 0.585 (1.5-fold change). The results were displayed using ggplot2.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

The construction of scale-free co-expression networks was performed utilizing the WGCNA package. Initially, sample quality control was conducted by removing outliers through hierarchical clustering. Subsequently, soft thresholding was applied to determine the optimal power using dynamic tree cutting, ensuring a scale-free topology with R² greater than 0.85. For module detection, hierarchical clustering of topological overlap matrices (TOM) was employed, with a minimum module size of 30 and a merge cut height of 0.25 as parameters. Module-trait associations were then assessed by examining the correlation between module-characterized genes and phenotypes, utilizing Pearson’s correlation coefficient where the R value exceeds 0.5 and the P value is below 0.05. Finally, pivotal genes were identified based on intra-module connectivity, with a kME value exceeding 0.8.

Identification of 2-Naphthylamine-associated disease targets

An analysis was performed to find core targets linked to 2-NA-induced BLCA by crossing DEGs and WGCNA hub genes with predicted 2-NA targets. These core targets were subsequently visualized using Venn diagrams.

Functional enrichment analysis

The clusterProfiler software package was used to perform Gene Ontology (GO) analyses, which included biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions, along with KEGG pathway analyses (P < 0.05). These analyses elucidated the mechanistic role of 2-NA in the pathogenesis of BLCA.

Machine learning approaches for screening core genes

We created a detailed machine learning prediction framework that combines various algorithms to identify diagnostic markers linked to 2-NA in BLCA systematically. We used expression profiling data from the training set and applied ten traditional machine learning algorithms—Lasso, glmBoost, Ridge, XGBoost, Enet, GBM, SVM, RF, LDA, Stepglm, and Naive Bayes—to build 113 predictive models. Hyperparameter optimization was carried out using 5-fold cross-validation, employing stratified sampling to split the data into training and internal validation sets. The performance of the models was thoroughly evaluated using metrics such as the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Models with high confidence (AUC > 0.9) were chosen to pinpoint potential key genes. Ultimately, the pheatmap software package was employed to visualize gene expression patterns.

Model interpretation

We employed the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) algorithm to quantitatively measure how much each feature contributed to the prediction results. This approach assigns a SHAP value to each feature, enabling a precise evaluation of its impact on the model’s predictions.

Molecular docking analysis

Molecular docking simulations were performed to confirm the interaction between 2-NA and the identified genes. The validation process involved acquiring the molecular structure of the primary active compound from the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and saving it in SDF format. Concurrently, the three-dimensional crystal structures of the target proteins were retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (PDB). Subsequent processing of the protein structures, including the elimination of water molecules and ligands, was executed using PyMOL software. The parameters for the docking pocket were determined utilizing the Getbox Plugin. The prepared protein and active compound files were imported into AutoDock Tools 1.5.6 and converted to PDBQT format. Molecular docking was subsequently performed using AutoDock Vina 1.1.2. The results of the molecular docking were visualized with PyMOL 2.6.0 software (see Table 1).

Molecular dynamics simulation

The Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD) is a robust metric utilized for evaluating the conformational stability of proteins and ligands by quantifying the extent of deviation in atomic positions from their initial configurations. A reduced deviation is indicative of enhanced conformational stability. Consequently, RMSD was employed to assess the equilibrium state of the simulated system. Radius of Gyration (Rg) characterizes overall structural changes and is instrumental in assessing the compactness of a protein structure. Solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) serves as an indicator for evaluating protein surface area. In this simulation, we calculated the solvent-accessible surface area between the target protein and the small molecule. Hydrogen bonds play a crucial role in ligand-protein binding interactions. In our study, we visualized the number of hydrogen bonds formed between the small molecule and the target protein. Root mean square fluctuation (RMSF) was employed to quantify the flexibility of amino acid residues within the protein structure.

Results

Identification of 2-naphthylamine target proteins

2-Naphthylamine’s molecular structure was sourced from the PubChem database (Fig. 1A). Three complementary databases were used to systematically predict potential biological targets for 2-NA, including ChEMBL (Selected Bioactive Molecules), PharmMapper (Reverse Pharmacophore Localization), and SwissTargetPrediction (Ligand-based Target Prediction). After integrating the data and removing duplicates, 1,285 potential targets were pinpointed (Fig. 1B).

Uncovering target genes associated with BLCA

The gene expression matrix was fully normalized after merging the GSE13507 and GSE37815 datasets to minimize batch effects. The batch-corrected principal component analysis (PCA) scatterplot illustrates the integration of the GSE13507 and GSE37815 datasets, demonstrating a diminished batch effect (Fig. 2A). Differential expression analysis revealed significant changes in 1,788 genes associated with BLCA, which were illustrated using volcano plots and heat maps (Fig. 2B and C). To perform weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), we initially identified the best soft-threshold power (β) to maintain a scale-free network topology. A systematic assessment of power values from 1 to 20 revealed that β = 5 was the lowest value meeting the scale-free topology criterion (R2 ≥ 0.8). Utilizing this parameter, we developed a topological overlap matrix (TOM) and executed hierarchical clustering to pinpoint co-expression modules (Fig. 2D). This analysis produced eight separate gene modules, each visually distinguished by color. Analysis of module-trait relationships revealed significant associations (P < 0.05) between specific modules and BLCA (Fig. 2E). After removing duplicates, we combined the differentially expressed genes from traditional analysis with the modular genes from WGCNA to identify a final set of 313 genes linked to BLCA (Fig. 2F).

Identification of 2-NA-related disease targets in BLCA

An analysis comparing 2-NA target proteins with genes related to BLCA identified 29 crucial targets involved in bladder carcinogenesis caused by 2-NA (Fig. 3A and B). Functional characterization through Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses provided comprehensive molecular insights. GO analysis revealed significant enrichment in mitotic cell cycle phase transition、cell cycle G2/M phase transition (biological processes), pronucleus and microtubule associated complex (cellular components), as well as DNA helicase activity and microtubule motor activity (molecular functions) (Fig. 3C). KEGG pathway analysis underscored significant roles in cell cycle、steroid biosynthesis and cellular senescence (Fig. 3D). These findings collectively suggest that 2-Naphthylamine exposure significantly activates mitotic cell cycle phase transition, cell cycle G2/M phase transition, and microtubule-DNA dysfunction, thereby facilitating the aggressive advancement of BLCA.

Identification of key genes in BLCA due to 2-NA

By conducting an extensive machine learning analysis on 29 potential targets, we developed multiple predictive models to determine the key genes implicated in 2-NA-associated BLCA. The integrated Random Forest (RF) and Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) model exhibited exceptional performance, achieving the top accuracy in training and validation stages. The analysis uncovered 14 significant genes: SKP2, SCN11A, DHCR7, AURKB, PFKFB4, EBP, CA2, NUDT1, DUSP2, KIF20A, BLM, IGFBP2, EZH2, and TPX2 (Fig. 4A). Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was employed to demonstrate the diagnostic potential of these core genes, and volcano plots were used to visualize their differential expression in BLCA (Fig. 4B). Notably, SKP2, DHCR7, AURKB, PFKFB4, EBP, CA2, NUDT1, KIF20A, BLM, EZH2, and TPX2 were identified as key upregulated genes in 2-NA-induced bladder carcinogenesis (Fig. 4C). SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analyses revealed varying functional contributions, with SKP2 (SHAP value = 0.0565) and EBP (SHAP value = 0.0485) emerging as the most influential predictors. Swarm plots further confirmed that SKP2 and EBP exhibited significant pro-bladder cancer development effects (Fig. 4D and G).

Molecular Docking validation of 2-NA with the core gene

We performed a comprehensive molecular docking analysis to confirm the potential binding interactions between 2-NA and key targets (SKP2, SCN11A, DHCR7, AURKB, PFKFB4, EBP, CA2, NUDT1, DUSP2, KIF20A, BLM, IGFBP2, EZH2, TPX2). The results indicate robust binding affinities between 2-NA and 14 target proteins, with binding energies consistently less than − 5 kcal per mol, indicating stable and spontaneous interactions at the molecular level (Table 2). Binding energies under 0 kcal per mol in molecular docking studies indicate spontaneous binding, whereas energies below − 5.0 kcal per mol represent strong binding affinity. Visualization of binding conformations revealed stable docking poses for all 2-NA-protein complexes (Fig. 5A and N). This research offers structural proof of direct molecular interactions between 2-Naphthylamine and the primary BLCA-related targets identified by our machine learning method.

Molecular dynamics simulations validate the binding interactions between 2-NA and the target proteins EBP and SKP2

We performed molecular dynamics simulations on the two most influential target proteins identified by SHAP analysis (EBP and SKP2). Results showed that the EBP-2-NA complex system reached equilibrium after 10 ns, ultimately fluctuating around 0.5 nm. The SKP2-2-NA complex system achieved equilibrium after 90 ns, ultimately fluctuating around 0.53 nm (Fig. 6A). Consequently, 2-NA demonstrates significant stability upon binding to both EBP and SKP2 target proteins. The EBP-2-NA complex showed relatively stable fluctuations throughout its motion, suggesting the absence of significant expansion or contraction within the small molecule-target protein complex during this process. In contrast, the SKP2-2-NA complex system displayed slight fluctuations during motion, indicative of minor conformational changes in the small molecule-target protein complex (Fig. 6B). Furthermore, upon binding of the EBP-2-NA receptor to its ligand, the SASA of the complex showed no significant change, suggesting minimal structural impact of ligand binding on the protein. The SKP2-2-NA complex system exhibited slight fluctuations. This demonstrates that small molecule binding alters the binding microenvironment, leading to minor SASA changes (Fig. 6C). The number of hydrogen bonds between the EBP-2-NA small molecule and its target proteins ranged from 0 to 3, with approximately 1 bond typically present. Similarly, the SKP2-2-NA small molecule typically formed about 1 hydrogen bond with its target protein. This indicates strong hydrogen bonding interactions between this ligand and both target proteins (Fig. 6D). The RMSF values for the EBP-2-NA complex are relatively low (mostly below 4 Å), and similarly, the RMSF values for the SKP2-2-NA complex are relatively low (mostly below 3 Å). Consequently, both complexes exhibit low flexibility and high stability (Fig. 6E).

Discussion

2-Naphthylamine, a well-established aromatic amine and industrial carcinogen, is unequivocally classified as a Group 1 human carcinogen by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) [13]. It is intricately linked, at the mechanistic level, to the pathogenesis of bladder transitional migratory cell carcinoma. Following activation via N-hydroxylation metabolism by hepatic N-acetyltransferase, highly reactive electrophilic intermediates are generated and subsequently excreted in urine, accumulating in the bladder lumen. Within the bladder epithelium, these active metabolites form stable adducts with DNA, leading to characteristic gene mutations that drive tumorigenesis and contribute to genomic instability. The genotoxic risk associated with these metabolites is significantly influenced by genetic polymorphisms in host detoxification enzymes, which account for the observed population heterogeneity in bladder cancer risk [14–17]. Epidemiological and experimental studies collectively demonstrate that chronic exposure to these metabolites synergistically promotes malignant transformation through various non-genotoxic mechanisms, including epigenetic modifications (such as DNA hypermethylation of key tumor suppressor gene promoters), persistent bladder epithelial inflammation (characterized by the release of pro-inflammatory factors and reactive oxygen species), and the inhibition of DNA damage repair processes [18, 19]. Historically, high-risk exposures have predominantly occurred in dye manufacturing, rubber production, and specific chemical industries, leading to a marked increase in the incidence of BLCA among affected occupational groups due to inhalation or dermal exposure pathways. The prolonged retention of the compound in the bladder, coupled with the resultant chronic inflammatory microenvironment, is a fundamental aspect of its tissue-specific carcinogenicity [20, 21]. Consequently, a comprehensive understanding of the complex mechanisms underlying the carcinogenicity of 2-Naphthylamine is crucial for the development and implementation of effective source control, biomonitoring, and early screening strategies within high-risk occupational populations.

Through a comprehensive analysis of multi-omics datasets integrated with cutting-edge machine learning, we pinpointed molecular targets that link 2-NA exposure to the pathogenesis of BLCA. Our network toxicology and bioinformatics analyses identified 29 potential genes, with 14 core genes deemed particularly significant. Differential expression analysis indicated that SKP2, DHCR7, AURKB, PFKFB4, EBP, CA2, NUDT1, KIF20A, BLM, EZH2, and TPX2 were upregulated in BLCA, whereas SCN11A, DUSP2, and IGFBP2 were downregulated. The diagnostic value of these genetic features was validated by machine learning models, with SHAP interpretability analysis pointing to SKP2 and EBP as the most impactful predictors. The biological significance of our findings was further validated by molecular docking simulations, which demonstrated strong binding affinities between 2-NA and key targets through specific amino acid interactions. It hypothesizes interactions that may occur after the well-established metabolic activation process. Furthermore, molecular dynamics simulations reveal that the compound 2-NA establishes stable complexes with the target proteins EBP and SKP2, characterized by pronounced hydrogen bonding interactions. These findings suggest that 2-NA exhibits a strong binding affinity for both target proteins. Overall, our findings imply the key genes identified could be crucial molecular targets (especially EBP and SKP2) for 2-NA’s oncogenic effects in bladder cancer development.

The core genes identified play various roles in 2-NA-induced BLCA, with particular emphasis on two highly influential genes: SKP2 and EBP. SKP2, a pivotal member of the F-box protein family, is integral to regulating cell cycle progression, apoptosis, metabolism, and genomic stability [22]. For BLCA, SKP2 has been shown to specifically recognize phosphorylated p27, facilitating its ubiquitination and subsequent degradation. This process alleviates the inhibitory effect of p27 on the CDK2/cyclin E complex, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation. Clinical evidence indicates a significant association between elevated SKP2 expression and increased tumor infiltration, as well as poor prognosis [23, 24]. These findings offer a novel perspective on the molecular mechanisms underlying 2-NA-induced BLCA. Given the pivotal role of SKP2 within the 2-NA-associated signaling network, accurately regulating the expression or activity of this protein may represent an innovative therapeutic option for preventing 2-NA-induced BLCA. The EBP gene encodes the core enzyme involved in the cholesterol synthesis pathway, primarily facilitating the critical isomerization step in cholesterol biosynthesis. Overexpression of EBP results in the accumulation of cholesterol and its intermediates, supplying essential raw materials for membrane synthesis and signaling in tumor cells [25]. Recent studies have identified that aberrant expression or mutation of EBP exerts a pro-carcinogenic effect in bladder cancer, particularly in high-risk subtypes. EBP promotes the proliferation and colony development of BLCA by triggering the expression of uroepithelial cell carcinoma antigen 1 (UCA1), which is a key molecular mechanism that directly regulates the transcription of UCA1, thereby driving the malignant proliferation of BLCA [26]. Considering the significant role of EBP within the regulatory network of BLCA, targeting its activity or inhibiting its mediated activation of associated pathways could potentially lead to novel advancements in BLCA prevention strategies and precision-targeted therapeutic regimens.

This study is subject to several limitations. Firstly, the absence of multicenter cohort validation may limit the generalizability of the BLCA-associated gene set. Secondly, while SHAP analysis identified a synergistic positive correlation between SKP2 and EBP, the model did not elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying their interaction. Thirdly, additional in vivo and ex vivo experimental validation is required to ascertain the biological relevance and accuracy of the findings. In our forthcoming research agenda, we intend to empirically validate the interactions through cell-based assays to investigate the functional impact of 2-NA on the identified targets. This will involve techniques such as knockdown or inhibition of SKP2/EBP to evaluate their effects on proliferation and migration within bladder cancer cell lines. Additionally, in vivo validation will be conducted using animal models exposed to 2-NA to observe alterations in the expression and activity of these targets throughout the process of bladder carcinogenesis. Nevertheless, the comprehensive analysis of 2-NA toxicity mechanisms presented in this study holds significant public health implications. Future research should prioritize: [1] the development of bladder organoid models with targeted gene knockouts to investigate dose-dependent interactions through single-cell sequencing; [2] the integration of bladder cancer risk assessment models for NAP exposure; and [3] the exploration of epigenetic modification networks in the oncogenic effects of 2-NA.

Conclusions

This study utilizes multi-omics and machine learning methodologies to elucidate a potential mechanism through which the environmental carcinogen 2-NA may contribute to the pathogenesis of bladder cancer by directly interacting with critical molecular targets, including EBP and SKP2. Molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations corroborate that 2-NA demonstrates a stable binding affinity with both target proteins. These findings enhance our understanding of the molecular pathways implicated in 2-NA-induced bladder carcinogenesis and provide a theoretical foundation for the development of biomarkers for high-risk populations, as well as for the formulation of targeted prevention strategies.

Vertex degrees in grid graphs associated with 213-avoiding permutations
N. B. Huamaní

Abstract
Given a permutation of size n n , we consider its associated grid graph whose i i th column has height equal to the i i th entry, with vertical edges between consecutive levels and horizontal edges between equal levels in adjacent columns. We study global degree statistics of these graphs when the permutation is chosen from the Catalan avoidance class Av n ​ ( 213 ) \mathrm{Av}_{n}(213) (and, by reversal, also from Av n ​ ( 312 ) \mathrm{Av}_{n}(312) ). We first obtain an explicit closed form for the total number of horizontal edges summed over all permutations in Av n ​ ( 213 ) \mathrm{Av}_{n}(213) . We then determine, for each degree r ∈ { 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 } r\in\{1,2,3,4\} , the total number of degree- r r vertices accumulated over the same class, yielding closed expressions in terms of central binomial coefficients and powers of four. The proofs rely on the Catalan decomposition induced by the position of the minimum entry, which leads to gluing identities and algebraic functional equations for ordinary generating functions, completed using global vertex and degree-sum identities. As a consequence, we derive asymptotic degree proportions for a uniform random permutation in Av n ​ ( 213 ) \mathrm{Av}_{n}(213) : the distribution concentrates and the proportion of degree- 4 4 vertices tends to 1 1 , with a deficit of order n − 1 / 2 n^{-1/2} .

Introduction

We study global degree statistics in the grid graphs associated with permutations, restricting the underlying permutation to a Catalan
avoidance class.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Let
S_{n}
denote the set of all permutations of
[n]
,
that is, all words
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{n}
containing each element of
[n]
exactly once.
Following
1

\pi\in S_{n}
we associate a
grid graph

G_{\pi}
with
n

column
i

\pi_{i}
; we place vertical edges between consecutive levels within each column and horizontal edges between
equal levels in adjacent columns. For

has degree in
\{1,2,3,4\}

(
1
)
.

Preliminaries

We collect the basic definitions and notation: the grid graph
G_{\pi}
, local degree-by-level criteria,
Catalan enumeration for

2.1
Grid graphs

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\pi=\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{n}\in S_{n}
. The
grid graph

G_{\pi}
has vertex set

V(G_{\pi})=\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq n,\ 1\leq j\leq\pi_{i}\}.

Two vertices
(i,j)
and
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
are adjacent if:
(i)
i=i^{\prime}
and
|j-j^{\prime}|=1
(a vertical edge), or
(ii)
|i-i^{\prime}|=1
and
j=j^{\prime}
(a horizontal edge), provided both vertices exist.

For
n\geq 2
, every vertex of
G_{\pi}
has degree in
\{1,2,3,4\}

(
1
)
.
We write
\deg(v)
for the degree of
v
, and
E_{\rm vert}(G_{\pi})
,
E_{\rm hor}(G_{\pi})
for the sets of vertical and horizontal edges.

2.2
Local degree criteria by level

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
and consider a column
i
of height
b=\pi_{i}
.

(i) If
2\leq i\leq n-1
(internal column) and
a=\pi_{i-1}
,
c=\pi_{i+1}
, then for
1\leq s\leq b

\deg(i,s)=\mathbf{1}_{\{s>1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{s<b\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq a\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq c\}}.

(1)

(ii) If
i=1
(left external column) and
b=\pi_{1}
,
c=\pi_{2}
, then for
1\leq s\leq b

\deg(1,s)=\mathbf{1}_{\{s>1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{s<b\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq c\}}.

Similarly, if
i=n
and
a=\pi_{n-1}
, then

\deg(n,s)=\mathbf{1}_{\{s>1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{s<b\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq a\}}.

Proof.

In any column, vertical neighbors exist exactly when
s>1
(a lower neighbor) and
s<b
(an upper neighbor).
In an internal column there are two possible horizontal neighbors: to the left if
s\leq a
and to the right if
s\leq c
.
In an external column there is only one possible horizontal neighbor. Summing these contributions yields the stated formulas.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

In an internal column with triple
(a,b,c)
, the number of degree-
4
vertices in the middle column is

\max\{0,\min(a,c,b-1)-1\}.

Proof.

By (
1
), a level
s
has degree
4
if and only if
2\leq s\leq b-1
and also
s\leq a
and
s\leq c
.
This is equivalent to
2\leq s\leq\min(a,c,b-1)
, and the count is immediate.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
2\leq i\leq n-1
. The top vertex
(i,\pi_{i})
has degree
1
if and only if
\pi_{i}\geq 2
and

\pi_{i}>\max\{\pi_{i-1},\pi_{i+1}\}
.

Proof.

Apply (
1
) with
s=b
. Then
\mathbf{1}_{\{s<b\}}=0
and
\mathbf{1}_{\{s>1\}}=1
if and only if
b\geq 2
.
Moreover
\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq a\}}=\mathbf{1}_{\{b\leq a\}}
and
\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq c\}}=\mathbf{1}_{\{b\leq c\}}
.
Hence the degree equals
1
exactly when
b\geq 2
and
b>a
and
b>c
.
∎

2.3
Pattern avoidance, Catalan numbers, and reversal

Let
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)\subseteq S_{n}
be the set of permutations avoiding the pattern
213
.
It is well known (see, e.g.,
(
2
, Ch. 4)
) that
|\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)|
is the
n
th Catalan number:

C_{n}:=|\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)|=\mathrm{Cat}_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.

Write
B_{n}:=\binom{2n}{n}
, so
C_{n}=B_{n}/(n+1)
. The Catalan ordinary generating function is

C(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{n}x^{n}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4x}}{2x},

(2)

and it satisfies
C(x)=1+xC(x)^{2}
.

For
\pi=\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{n}\in S_{n}
, define its
reversal
by

\mathrm{rev}(\pi):=\pi_{n}\cdots\pi_{1}.

Lemma 2.5
.

For every
\pi\in S_{n}
, the graphs
G_{\pi}
and
G_{\mathrm{rev}(\pi)}
are isomorphic via

\varphi:V(G_{\pi})\to V(G_{\mathrm{rev}(\pi)}),\qquad\varphi(i,s)=(n+1-i,s).

In particular, for every
r\in\{1,2,3,4\}
we have
Q_{r}(\pi)=Q_{r}(\mathrm{rev}(\pi))
and also
H(\pi)=H(\mathrm{rev}(\pi))
.

Proof.

The map
\varphi
is a bijection and preserves adjacencies: vertical edges remain within the same column at consecutive levels,
and horizontal edges are reflected between adjacent columns. Hence degrees and degree counts are preserved. Moreover,

H(\mathrm{rev}(\pi))=\sum_{t=1}^{n-1}\min\{\pi_{n+1-t},\pi_{n-t}\}=H(\pi).

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

The map
\pi\mapsto\mathrm{rev}(\pi)
restricts to a bijection
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Av}_{n}(312)
.
Consequently, all results of this paper for
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
hold verbatim for
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(312)
, with the same closed formulas.

Proof.

Reversal sends the pattern
213
to
312
(and conversely). The rest follows from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

3
Catalan decomposition by the position of the minimum

We establish the Catalan block decomposition of
213
-avoiding permutations via the position of the minimum entry
1
,
which is the structural input for all subsequent gluing identities and generating-function equations.

The key structural fact is that, in a
213
-avoiding permutation, all entries to the left of
1
are larger than all entries to the right.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
and let
k
be the position of
1
, i.e.
\pi_{k}=1
.
Then every entry to the left of
1
is larger than every entry to the right of
1
.
In particular, if
i=k-1
and
j=n-k
, there exist
\alpha\in\mathrm{Av}_{i}(213)
and
\beta\in\mathrm{Av}_{j}(213)
such that

\pi=(\alpha+j+1)\ 1\ (\beta+1),

(3)

where
\alpha+j+1
means adding
j+1
to each entry of
\alpha
, and
\beta+1
means adding
1
to each entry of
\beta
.
Conversely, every concatenation of the form (
3
) lies in
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
.

Proof.

If there were positions
p<k<q
with
\pi_{p}<\pi_{q}
, then the triple
(\pi_{p},1,\pi_{q})
would realize the pattern
213
:
indeed,
1
is the smallest,
\pi_{q}
the largest, and
\pi_{p}
lies strictly between them, a contradiction.
Hence every entry left of
1
is larger than every entry right of
1
.
Standardizing the left and right blocks yields (
3
).
The converse is immediate: in (
3
) no
213
pattern can cross the
1
, and within each block avoidance is preserved under standardization.
∎

In particular, we recover the usual Catalan convolution:

C_{n}=\sum_{i+j=n-1}C_{i}C_{j}\qquad(n\geq 1).

4
Horizontal edges

We derive a gluing identity for the horizontal-edge statistic
H(\pi)
under the Catalan decomposition, translate it into a functional equation
for
H(x)
, and solve it to obtain a closed form for
H_{n}
.

For
\pi\in S_{n}
define

H(\pi)=\sum_{t=1}^{n-1}\min\{\pi_{t},\pi_{t+1}\},\qquad H_{n}:=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)}H(\pi).

For a statement
A
we write
\mathbf{1}_{\{A\}}\in\{0,1\}
for its indicator.

4.1
A gluing identity and a functional equation

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
with decomposition
\pi=(\alpha+j+1)\,1\,(\beta+1)
as in (
3
),
where
|\alpha|=i
,
|\beta|=j
, and
i+j=n-1
. Then

H(\pi)=H(\alpha)+H(\beta)+\mathbf{1}_{\{i\geq 2\}}(i-1)(j+1)+\mathbf{1}_{\{j\geq 2\}}(j-1)+\mathbf{1}_{\{i\geq 1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{j\geq 1\}}.

(4)

Proof.

Split the sum defining
H(\pi)
into adjacent pairs according to their position relative to the decomposition

\pi=(\alpha+j+1)\,1\,(\beta+1)
.

If
i\geq 2
, the pairs
(t,t+1)
with
1\leq t\leq i-1
lie entirely in the left block. For each such pair,

\min\{\alpha_{t}+j+1,\alpha_{t+1}+j+1\}=(j+1)+\min\{\alpha_{t},\alpha_{t+1}\},

so the total contribution is
H(\alpha)+(i-1)(j+1)
. If
i\leq 1
there are no such pairs, captured by
\mathbf{1}_{\{i\geq 2\}}(i-1)(j+1)
.

Similarly, if
j\geq 2
, the internal pairs in the right block contribute

H(\beta)+(j-1),

since
\min\{\beta_{t}+1,\beta_{t+1}+1\}=1+\min\{\beta_{t},\beta_{t+1}\}
. If
j\leq 1
there is no contribution, captured by
\mathbf{1}_{\{j\geq 2\}}(j-1)
.

Finally, consider the pairs involving the column of
1
. If
i\geq 1
, the pair between the last column of the left block and
1

contributes
\min\{\alpha_{i}+j+1,1\}=1
; and if
j\geq 1
, the pair between
1
and the first column of the right block contributes

\min\{1,\beta_{1}+1\}=1
. This yields
\mathbf{1}_{\{i\geq 1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{j\geq 1\}}
.

Collecting terms gives (
4
).
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
H(x):=\sum_{n\geq 1}H_{n}x^{n}
. Then

(1-2xC(x))\,H(x)=x\bigl(xC^{\prime}(x)-C(x)+1\bigr)\bigl(xC^{\prime}(x)+2C(x)\bigr)+2\bigl(C(x)-1-xC(x)\bigr).

(5)

Proof.

Sum (
4
) over all
\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
, grouping by
i+j=n-1
and the choices

\alpha\in\mathrm{Av}_{i}(213)
,
\beta\in\mathrm{Av}_{j}(213)
.

The contributions
H(\alpha)
and
H(\beta)
yield the convolution

\sum_{i+j=n-1}(C_{j}H_{i}+C_{i}H_{j})
, which translates into
2xC(x)H(x)
in ordinary generating functions.

Terms depending only on lengths become products of OGFs:

\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathbf{1}_{\{i\geq 2\}}(i-1)C_{i}x^{i}\cdot\sum_{j\geq 0}(j+1)C_{j}x^{j}=\bigl(xC^{\prime}(x)-C(x)+1\bigr)\bigl(xC^{\prime}(x)+C(x)\bigr),

and similarly,

\sum_{i\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 0}C_{i}x^{i}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{j\geq 2\}}(j-1)C_{j}x^{j}=C(x)\bigl(xC^{\prime}(x)-C(x)+1\bigr).

Finally,

\sum_{n\geq 1}\Bigl(\sum_{i+j=n-1}(\mathbf{1}_{\{i\geq 1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{j\geq 1\}})C_{i}C_{j}\Bigr)x^{n}=2\sum_{n\geq 1}(C_{n}-C_{n-1})x^{n}=2\bigl(C(x)-1-xC(x)\bigr).

Combining all contributions and moving
2xC(x)H(x)
to the left yields (
5
).
∎

4.2
Closed form

Theorem 4.3
.

For
n\geq 1
,

H_{n}=\frac{n}{2}\binom{2n}{n}-4^{\,n-1}.

Equivalently, the ordinary generating function
H(x)=\sum_{n\geq 1}H_{n}x^{n}
is

H(x)=x(1-4x)^{-3/2}-x(1-4x)^{-1}.

(6)

Proof.

Starting from (
5
), substitute the Catalan identities

C(x)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4x}}{2x}
and
1-2xC(x)=\sqrt{1-4x}
,
and simplify to obtain (
6
).

To extract coefficients, use

(1-4x)^{-1}=\sum_{m\geq 0}4^{m}x^{m},\qquad(1-4x)^{-1/2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\binom{2m}{m}x^{m},

and

(1-4x)^{-3/2}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dx}(1-4x)^{-1/2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}x^{m}.

Hence,

[x^{n}]\,x(1-4x)^{-3/2}=(2n-1)\binom{2n-2}{n-1}=\frac{n}{2}\binom{2n}{n},\qquad[x^{n}]\,x(1-4x)^{-1}=4^{n-1},

which gives the claimed formula for
H_{n}
.
∎

5
Degree counts: global definitions

We introduce the global degree totals
Q_{r}(n)
and record two universal global identities: the total number of vertices and the sum of degrees,
which later close the system for
(Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3},Q_{4})
.

For
r\in\{1,2,3,4\}
define

Q_{r}(n):=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)}\#\{v\in V(G_{\pi}):\deg(v)=r\}.

We also use two global identities (valid for any subclass of
S_{n}
).

Proposition 5.1
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)}|V(G_{\pi})|=|\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)|\cdot\frac{n(n+1)}{2}=\frac{n}{2}\binom{2n}{n},

(7)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)}\sum_{v\in V(G_{\pi})}\deg(v)=|\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)|\cdot n(n-1)+2H_{n}=\frac{2n^{2}}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}-2\cdot 4^{\,n-1}.

(8)

In particular, for
n\geq 2
,

Q_{1}(n)+Q_{2}(n)+Q_{3}(n)+Q_{4}(n)=V_{n},\qquad Q_{1}(n)+2Q_{2}(n)+3Q_{3}(n)+4Q_{4}(n)=\Sigma_{n}.

(9)

Proof.

For each
\pi\in S_{n}
, the total number of vertices is
|V(G_{\pi})|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}=\binom{n+1}{2}
.
Summing over
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
gives (
7
).

For
\Sigma_{n}
, use the handshaking lemma:
\sum_{v}\deg(v)=2|E(G_{\pi})|
.
Moreover,
|E_{\rm vert}(G_{\pi})|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\pi_{i}-1)=\binom{n}{2}
is constant on
S_{n}
,
whereas
|E_{\rm hor}(G_{\pi})|=H(\pi)
. Summing over
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
yields

\Sigma_{n}=2\Bigl(|\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)|\cdot\binom{n}{2}+H_{n}\Bigr),

and substituting
H_{n}
from Theorem
4.3
gives (
8
).
The system (
9
) is the direct translation of (
7
) and (
8
) in terms of
Q_{r}(n)
.
∎

Degree-
1

We compute

6.1
A local criterion in external columns

The following criterion matches
1
and also follows directly from Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
and let
G_{\pi}
be its grid graph.
In the first column, the number of degree-
1
vertices equals

\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{1}=1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{1}>\pi_{2}\}},

and in the last column it equals

\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{n}=1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{n-1}<\pi_{n}\}}.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
2.2
.
In the first column, with
b=\pi_{1}
and
c=\pi_{2}
, for
s<b
we have
\mathbf{1}_{\{s<b\}}=1
and also
\mathbf{1}_{\{s\leq c\}}=1
,
so
\deg(1,s)\geq 2
. Hence only the top vertex
(1,b)
can have degree
1
, and

\deg(1,b)=\mathbf{1}_{\{b>1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{b\leq c\}}.

Thus
\deg(1,b)=1
iff
b=1
or
b>c
, i.e.
\pi_{1}=1
or
\pi_{1}>\pi_{2}
.

The last column is identical: with
a=\pi_{n-1}
and
b=\pi_{n}
, only
(n,b)
can have degree
1
, and

\deg(n,b)=\mathbf{1}_{\{b>1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{b\leq a\}}.

Hence
\deg(n,b)=1
iff
b=1
or
a<b
, i.e.
\pi_{n}=1
or
\pi_{n-1}<\pi_{n}
.
∎

6.2
Boundary counts in
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)

Lemma 6.2
.

For
m\geq 2
, define

D_{m}:=\#\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Av}_{m}(213):\gamma_{1}>\gamma_{2}\},\qquad A_{m}:=\#\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Av}_{m}(213):\gamma_{m-1}<\gamma_{m}\}.

Then
D_{m}=A_{m}=C_{m-1}
.

Proof.

We prove
D_{m}=C_{m-1}
; the proof for
A_{m}
is analogous by symmetry (reversing left–right).

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{Av}_{m}(213)
and write
\gamma=(\alpha+j+1)\,1\,(\beta+1)
with
|\alpha|=i
,
|\beta|=j
, and
i+j=m-1

(Lemma
3.1
). If
i=0
, then
\gamma_{1}=1
and there is no initial descent. If
i=1
, then necessarily

\gamma_{1}=m
and
\gamma_{2}=1
, so an initial descent always occurs and the choice reduces to
\beta\in\mathrm{Av}_{m-2}(213)
,
contributing
C_{m-2}
possibilities. If
i\geq 2
, then the first two entries of
\gamma
lie in the left block and

\gamma_{1}>\gamma_{2}
is equivalent to
\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}
. Therefore, for
m\geq 2
,

D_{m}=C_{m-2}+\sum_{i=2}^{m-1}D_{i}\,C_{m-1-i}.

Using induction and the Catalan convolution
\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}C_{p}C_{m-2-p}=C_{m-1}
yields
D_{m}=C_{m-1}
.
∎

6.3
External and internal columns

Lemma 6.3
.

For
n\geq 2
, the total number of degree-
1
vertices contributed by the first and last columns satisfies

Q_{1}^{\rm ext}(n)=4\,C_{n-1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
,

Q_{1}^{\rm ext}(n)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)}\Bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{1}=1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{1}>\pi_{2}\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{n}=1\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{n-1}<\pi_{n}\}}\Bigr).

Each of the four sums equals
C_{n-1}
. Indeed,
\pi_{1}=1
forces
1
to be in the first position; by Lemma
3.1

this means
i=0
and leaves
\beta\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(213)
free, so
\sum_{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{1}=1\}}=C_{n-1}
. Similarly,
\pi_{n}=1
forces

j=0
and leaves
\alpha\in\mathrm{Av}_{n-1}(213)
free, giving
\sum_{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{n}=1\}}=C_{n-1}
.

For the comparative events, Lemma
6.2
gives

\sum_{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{1}>\pi_{2}\}}=D_{n}=C_{n-1}
and
\sum_{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{\{\pi_{n-1}<\pi_{n}\}}=A_{n}=C_{n-1}
.
Hence
Q_{1}^{\rm ext}(n)=4C_{n-1}
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

For
n\geq 3
, the total number of degree-
1
vertices in internal columns satisfies

Q_{1}^{\rm int}(n)=(n-2)\,C_{n-1}.

Proof.

Let
P_{n}:=Q_{1}^{\rm int}(n)
. Fix
\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
with decomposition

\pi=(\alpha+j+1)\,1\,(\beta+1)
, where
|\alpha|=i
,
|\beta|=j
, and
i+j=n-1
.

Internal peaks of
\pi
split into those entirely within
\alpha
, those entirely within
\beta
, and possible peaks created at the boundary
with the column of
1
. The first two contributions yield, after summing over all choices, the convolution term

\sum_{i+j=n-1}(C_{j}P_{i}+C_{i}P_{j})=2\sum_{i+j=n-1}C_{j}P_{i}
.

For the boundary: if
i\geq 2
, the last column of the
\alpha
-block is an internal peak of
\pi
iff it exceeds its left neighbor,
since its right neighbor is
1
; this is equivalent to
\alpha
ending with a final ascent. Similarly, if
j\geq 2
, the first column of the

\beta
-block is an internal peak of
\pi
iff
\beta
begins with an initial descent. By Lemma
6.2
,
the number of
\alpha\in\mathrm{Av}_{i}(213)
with a final ascent is
A_{i}=C_{i-1}
, and the number of
\beta\in\mathrm{Av}_{j}(213)
with an initial descent is

D_{j}=C_{j-1}
. Summing over all choices yields the additional term

\sum_{i+j=n-1}(C_{j}A_{i}+C_{i}D_{j})=2\sum_{i+j=n-1}C_{j}C_{i-1}.

By Catalan convolution,

\sum_{i+j=n-1}C_{j}C_{i-1}=\sum_{p=0}^{n-2}C_{p}C_{n-2-p}=C_{n-1},

and hence, for
n\geq 3
,

P_{n}=2\sum_{i+j=n-1}C_{j}P_{i}+2C_{n-1}.

Passing to OGFs, with
P(x):=\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{n}x^{n}
and
P_{0}=P_{1}=P_{2}=0
, the recurrence becomes

P(x)=2xC(x)P(x)+2x\bigl(C(x)-1-x\bigr),

so

P(x)=\frac{2x\bigl(C(x)-1-x\bigr)}{1-2xC(x)}=x\bigl(xC^{\prime}(x)-C(x)+1\bigr).

Extracting coefficients gives
P_{n}=(n-2)C_{n-1}
for
n\geq 3
.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

For
n\geq 2
,

Q_{1}(n)=Q_{1}^{\rm ext}(n)+Q_{1}^{\rm int}(n)=(n+2)\,C_{n-1}=\frac{n+2}{2(2n-1)}\binom{2n}{n}.

Proof.

Sum Lemmas
6.3
and
6.4
, and use

C_{n-1}=\frac{1}{n}\binom{2n-2}{n-1}=\frac{1}{2(2n-1)}\binom{2n}{n}
.
∎

7
Degree-
4
vertices

We obtain
Q_{4}(n)
using a local degree-
4
criterion together with a uniform-shift analysis under the Catalan gluing,
leading to a closed generating function and an explicit coefficient formula.

7.1
Uniform shifts

The local criterion for counting degree-
4
vertices in an internal column was established in Corollary
2.3
.
We now record the effect of uniform shifts in a triple of columns, which will be the gluing ingredient for the global closure.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let an internal column have height
b
with neighbors of heights
a
and
c
.
If one replaces
(a,b,c)
by
(a+t,b+t,c+t)
with
t\geq 1
, then the number of degree-
4
vertices
in the middle column increases by

\begin{cases}t,&b\geq 2,\\
t-1,&b=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Corollary
2.3
, before the shift the number of degree-
4
vertices in the middle column is

\max\{0,\min(a,c,b-1)-1\}
, and after the shift it is

\max\{0,\min(a+t,c+t,(b+t)-1)-1\}=\max\{0,\,t+\min(a,c,b-1)-1\}.

If
b\geq 2
, then
b-1\geq 1
and since
a,c\geq 1
we have
\min(a,c,b-1)\geq 1
, so the increment is exactly
t
.
If
b=1
, the first term is
0
and the second is
\max\{0,t-1\}=t-1
.
∎

7.2
Closure for
Q_{4}(n)

Let
Q_{4}(n)
be the global total of degree-
4
vertices as
\pi
ranges over
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
.
There are no degree-
4
vertices in external columns, so
Q_{4}(n)
equals the internal contribution.

Write
(t)_{+}:=\max\{t,0\}
. Also define, for
m\geq 0
,

J_{m}:=\#\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Av}_{m}(213):\text{the entry }1\text{ lies in an internal position}\}=\begin{cases}C_{m}-2C_{m-1},&m\geq 2,\\
0,&m\leq 1.\end{cases}

Theorem 7.2
.

For
n\geq 2
,

Q_{4}(n)=\frac{4n^{3}-7n^{2}+29n-20}{4(n+1)(2n-1)}\binom{2n}{n}-7\cdot 4^{\,n-2}.

Proof.

Fix
\pi\in\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
and write
\pi=(\alpha+j+1)\,1\,(\beta+1)
, with
|\alpha|=i
,
|\beta|=j
, and
i+j=n-1

(Lemma
3.1
). By Corollary
2.3
, an internal column cannot contain degree-
4
vertices if one of its
neighbors has height
1
. In particular, the columns adjacent to
1
in
\pi
contribute nothing to
Q_{4}(\pi)
.
Thus every contribution comes from internal columns fully contained in
\alpha
or fully contained in
\beta
.

When embedding the left block into
\pi
, all its heights increase uniformly by
t=j+1
.
Applying Lemma
7.1
to each internal column of
\alpha
, the shift creates
t
new degree-
4
vertices per internal column,
except possibly at the internal column whose original height is
1
, where it creates
t-1
.
Since
\alpha
has
(i-2)_{+}
internal columns and there is exactly one entry of value
1
in
\alpha
, the total increment is

(j+1)(i-2)_{+}-\mathbf{1}_{\{1\text{ is internal in }\alpha\}}
.
For
\beta
, the shift is
t=1
, and the total increment is

(j-2)_{+}-\mathbf{1}_{\{1\text{ is internal in }\beta\}}
.
Therefore, for each pair
(\alpha,\beta)
,

Q_{4}(\pi)=Q_{4}(\alpha)+Q_{4}(\beta)+(j+1)(i-2)_{+}+(j-2)_{+}-\mathbf{1}_{\{1\text{ internal in }\alpha\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{1\text{ internal in }\beta\}}.

(10)

Summing (
10
) over
\alpha\in\mathrm{Av}_{i}(213)
,
\beta\in\mathrm{Av}_{j}(213)
and then over
i+j=n-1
yields a Catalan convolution recurrence.
Passing to OGFs leads to the linear functional equation

(1-2xC(x))Q_{4}(x)=xA(x)\bigl(C(x)+(xC(x))^{\prime}\bigr)-2xC(x)J(x),

(11)

where
Q_{4}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}Q_{4}(n)x^{n}
,
A(x)=\sum_{m\geq 0}(m-2)_{+}C_{m}x^{m}
, and
J(x)=\sum_{m\geq 0}J_{m}x^{m}
.

From the definitions,

A(x)=xC^{\prime}(x)-2C(x)+2+x,\qquad J(x)=(1-2x)C(x)+x-1.

Substitute these into (
11
) and use
C(x)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4x}}{2x}
and
1-2xC(x)=\sqrt{1-4x}
.
After algebraic simplification, one obtains

Q_{4}(x)=\frac{8x^{4}-150x^{3}+157x^{2}-50x+5+(36x^{3}-87x^{2}+40x-5)\sqrt{1-4x}}{2x(1-4x)^{2}}.

(12)

Finally, (
12
) can be written as a linear combination of
(1-4x)^{-1}
,
(1-4x)^{-2}
, and
(1-4x)^{-3/2}
,
and coefficients are extracted using standard expansions (see, e.g.,
(
3
, Ch. VI)
).
This yields, for
n\geq 2
,

Q_{4}(n)=\frac{4n^{3}-7n^{2}+29n-20}{4(n+1)(2n-1)}\binom{2n}{n}-7\cdot 4^{\,n-2}.

∎

8
Closed forms for
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{4}

We package the closed forms for
Q_{1}(n)
and
Q_{4}(n)
and then solve for
Q_{2}(n)
and
Q_{3}(n)
from the global linear identities,
yielding explicit formulas for all degree totals.

Theorem 8.1
.

For
n\geq 2
, with
B_{n}=\binom{2n}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{1}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{2(2n-1)}\,B_{n},

\displaystyle Q_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{(12n^{2}+44n-112)\,B_{n}+(2n^{2}+n-1)\,4^{n}}{16(n+1)(2n-1)},

\displaystyle Q_{3}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{(8n^{2}-96n+88)\,B_{n}+(6n^{2}+3n-3)\,4^{n}}{8(n+1)(2n-1)},

\displaystyle Q_{4}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{(4n^{3}-7n^{2}+29n-20)\,B_{n}}{4(n+1)(2n-1)}-7\cdot 4^{\,n-2}.

Proof.

The formula for
Q_{1}(n)
is Corollary
6.5
, and the formula for
Q_{4}(n)
is Theorem
7.2
.
For
Q_{2}(n)
and
Q_{3}(n)
, use the linear system (
9
) with
V_{n}
and
\Sigma_{n}
given by
(
7
) and (
8
). Set

S_{1}:=V_{n}-Q_{1}(n)-Q_{4}(n)=Q_{2}(n)+Q_{3}(n),\qquad S_{2}:=\Sigma_{n}-Q_{1}(n)-4Q_{4}(n)=2Q_{2}(n)+3Q_{3}(n).

Then
Q_{3}(n)=S_{2}-2S_{1}
and
Q_{2}(n)=S_{1}-Q_{3}(n)
.
Substituting the closed expressions for
V_{n},\Sigma_{n},Q_{1}(n),Q_{4}(n)
and simplifying gives the stated formulas.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

If
\pi
is chosen uniformly at random from
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
, then

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\#\{v\in V(G_{\pi}):\deg(v)=r\}\bigr]=\frac{Q_{r}(n)}{C_{n}}\qquad(r=1,2,3,4),

9
Asymptotics and comparison with the unrestricted case

We use standard asymptotics for central binomial coefficients to extract limiting proportions and leading corrections for
Q_{r}(n)/V_{n}
,
and we compare the resulting degree distribution in
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
with the unrestricted case studied in
1
.

Let
V_{n}=\frac{n}{2}B_{n}
be the total number of vertices summed over
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
.
Using the classical asymptotic
B_{n}\sim\dfrac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}
(see, e.g.,
(
3
, App. A)
) together with Theorem
8.1
, we obtain

\frac{Q_{1}(n)}{V_{n}}\to 0,\qquad\frac{Q_{2}(n)}{V_{n}}\to 0,\qquad\frac{Q_{3}(n)}{V_{n}}\to 0,\qquad\frac{Q_{4}(n)}{V_{n}}\to 1.

Moreover, the subdominant term of
Q_{4}(n)
is
-7\cdot 4^{n-2}
, and therefore

\frac{Q_{4}(n)}{V_{n}}=1-\frac{7}{8}\sqrt{\pi}\,n^{-1/2}+O(n^{-1}),

in contrast with the unrestricted case
(
1
)
, where the limiting proportion for degree
4
equals
1/2
.

Corollary 9.1
.

Let
V_{n}=\frac{n}{2}B_{n}
. As
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{1}(n)}{V_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}+O(n^{-2}),

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{2}(n)}{V_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{8}\,n^{-1/2}+O(n^{-1}),

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{3}(n)}{V_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\,n^{-1/2}+O(n^{-1}),

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{4}(n)}{V_{n}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{7\sqrt{\pi}}{8}\,n^{-1/2}+O(n^{-1}).

Proof.

Use
B_{n}\sim\dfrac{4^{n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}
(e.g.
(
3
, App. A)
) together with the closed forms in Theorem
8.1
.
For
Q_{2}
and
Q_{3}
, the
4^{n}
terms dominate the
B_{n}
terms by a factor of
\sqrt{n}
; their coefficients tend to
1/16
and
3/8
,
respectively, yielding the stated constants after dividing by
V_{n}\sim\dfrac{\sqrt{n}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,4^{n}
.
For
Q_{4}
, the main term matches
V_{n}
and the first correction comes from
-7\cdot 4^{n-2}=-(7/16)4^{n}
,
giving the constant
\frac{7\sqrt{\pi}}{8}
at order
n^{-1/2}
.
Finally,
Q_{1}(n)\sim\frac{1}{4}B_{n}
and dividing by
V_{n}\sim\frac{n}{2}B_{n}
gives
Q_{1}/V_{n}\sim(2n)^{-1}
.
∎

10
Conclusions

We complete the program proposed by
1
for the Catalan class
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(213)
.
Starting from the decomposition induced by the position of the minimum
1
, we obtain closed functional equations for global statistics
of the grid graph
G_{\pi}
, and from them we deduce explicit formulas for both the total number of horizontal edges
H_{n}

and the global degree totals
Q_{r}(n)
(
r=1,2,3,4
).
The case
213
exhibits behavior qualitatively different from the unrestricted one: avoidance rigidifies the Catalan gluing and concentrates
the degree distribution, so that the proportion of degree-
4
vertices tends to
1
, with a leading deficit of order
n^{-1/2}
.
Moreover, by reversal, all results transfer immediately to
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(312)
.

On the other hand, the case
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(132)
(and, by reversal,
\mathrm{Av}_{n}(231)
) has already been resolved recently in
(
4
)
.
Consequently, only the two remaining Catalan avoidance classes of length
3
,

\mathrm{Av}_{n}(123)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Av}_{n}(321),

remain open. Although they share Catalan enumeration, the structural constraints imposed by avoidance are substantially different and typically
require pattern-specific tools (a suitable choice of pivot, more rigid decompositions, and, in some instances, auxiliary boundary statistics)
to close the functional equations and obtain explicit formulas and asymptotic proportions.

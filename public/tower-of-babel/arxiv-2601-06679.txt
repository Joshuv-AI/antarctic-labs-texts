Comparing Two Notions of Coaction Invariance of Ideals in C*-Algebras
Matthew Gillespie, Benjamin Jones, S. Kaliszewski, John Quigg

Abstract
. Given a coaction δ \delta of a locally compact group G G on a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra A A , we study the relationship between two different forms of coaction invariance of ideals of A A and the ideals of the corresponding crossed product C ∗ C^{*} -algebra A ⋊ δ G A\rtimes_{\delta}G . In particular, we characterize when these two notions of invariance are equivalent.

Primary 46L55

1.
Introduction

Given a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
(A,G,\alpha)
with
\mathscr{I}(A)
the lattice of ideals of
A
, one can form a sub-lattice
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
of the action invariant ideals. That is,
\alpha_{s}(I)\subseteq I
for all
s\in G
. However in the dual situation, there are many different ways to define coaction invariance. Given a cosystem
(A,G,\delta)
, Nilsen in
[
Nil98
]
defines coaction invariance in terms of induced regular representations. Namely if
I=\ker q
, then
I
is Nilsen
\delta
-invariant provided
\ker q=\ker((q\otimes\lambda)\circ\delta)
. Nilsen used this notion of coaction invariance to establish lattice isomorphisms between the
\alpha
and
\delta
-invariant ideals of
A
and the
\widehat{\alpha}
and
\widehat{\delta}
invariant ideals of the crossed products
A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
,
A\rtimes_{\delta}G
respectively. Recently in
[
GKQW
]
(and even more recently in
[
Gil
]
) an analogous result was established using what is referred to as strong
\delta
-invariance in the literature. Strong
\delta
-invariance of
I
simply requires that
\delta|_{I}
defines a coaction of
G
on the
C^{*}
-algebra
I
. These two notions coincide for amenable
G
as we demonstrate, but easily fail to be whenever
G
is non-amenable. In
Section
2

we establish the needed definitions and theorems.
In Section
3
, we show that Nilsen’s definition of action invariance aligns with the standard notion and characterize when Nilsen and strong invariance are equal for coactions of groups. We also establish a simple lattice isomorphism between the lattices of strong and Nilsen invariant ideals
when
\delta
is maximal or normal.

C^{*}

C^{*}

Definition 2.1
.

A coaction is an injective non-degenerate
*
-homomorphism

\delta:A\to M(A\otimes C^{*}(G))

\delta:A\to M(A\otimes C^{*}(G))
satisfying

(a)

\delta(A)(1_{A}\otimes C^{*}(G))\subseteq A\otimes C^{*}(G)
;

(b)

(\delta\otimes\text{id}_{C^{*}(G)})\circ\delta=(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\delta_{G})\circ\delta

where
\delta_{G}:C^{*}(G)\to M(C^{*}(G)\otimes C^{*}(G))
is the integrated form of the strictly continuous unitary homomorphism
s\mapsto s\otimes s
. Finally, we say
\delta
is continuous provided that

\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\delta(A)(1_{A}\otimes C^{*}(G))\}=A\otimes C^{*}(G).

We will always assume our coactions are continuous and many times may refer to the data
(A,G,\delta)
as a coaction (similarly for actions). Given an action
(A,G,\alpha)
and a coaction
(A,G,\delta)
, we denote the full crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras as
A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
and
A\rtimes_{\delta}G
where full crossed products come with universal covariant homomorphisms
(i^{\alpha}_{A},i^{\alpha}_{G})
for actions and
(j^{\delta}_{A},j^{\delta}_{G})
for coactions respectively (we will sometimes write
j_{A}
instead of
j_{A}^{\delta}
if the context is clear, and similarly for the other maps mentioned; also, we frequently write

rather than
\rtimes_{\delta}G
, and similarly for the crossed product by the dual action.
In fact, we can (and will) take

and

, see
[
EKQR06
, Definition A.39]
. We denote the reduced crossed product of an action by

which is the image of
A\rtimes_{\alpha}G
under the regular representation
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{A}:A\rtimes_{\alpha}G\to M(A\otimes\mathcal{K})
, which is determined by

(a)

\Lambda^{\alpha}_{A}\circ i_{A}=(\text{id}_{A}\otimes M^{-1})\circ\overset{\sim}{\alpha}
;

(b)

\Lambda^{\alpha}_{A}\circ i_{G}=1_{A}\otimes\lambda

where
\overset{\sim}{\alpha}:A\to C_{b}(G,A)\subseteq M(A\otimes C_{0}(G))\cong C_{b}(G,M^{\beta}(A))

. In other words,
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{A}=((\text{id}_{A}\otimes M^{-1})\circ\overset{\sim}{\alpha})\rtimes(1_{A}\otimes\lambda)
.
We will write
\Lambda_{A}
, or even
\Lambda
, if no confusion is likely.
There
is
also
the

canonical surjection

of the double crossed

onto the stabilization of

denoted by

\Phi_{A}^{\delta}:A\rtimes_{\delta}G\rtimes_{\widehat{\delta}}G\to A\otimes\mathcal{K}
determined by

(a)

\Phi_{A}^{\delta}\circ i_{A\rtimes_{\delta}G}\circ j_{A}=(\text{id}_{A}\otimes\lambda)\circ\delta
;

(b)

\Phi_{A}^{\delta}\circ i_{A\rtimes_{\delta}G}\circ j_{G}=1_{A}\otimes M
;

(c)

\Phi_{A}^{\delta}\circ i_{G}=1_{A}\otimes\rho
.

That is,
\Phi_{A}^{\delta}=[((\text{id}_{A}\otimes\lambda)\circ\delta)\rtimes(1_{A}\otimes M)]\rtimes(1_{A}\otimes\rho)

We write
\Phi

\Phi_{A}

To discuss Nilsen’s notion of action and coaction invariance of (closed two-sided) ideals, we must introduce some notation and recall some concepts from her work. Many of the following definitions and lemmas are directly from

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\phi:A\to M(B)
be a
*
-homomorphism. Define

(a)

\text{Res}_{\phi}:\mathscr{I}(B)\to\mathscr{I}(A)
by
\text{Res}_{\phi}(\ker\pi)=\ker(\pi\circ\phi)
;

(b)

\text{Ext}_{\phi}:\mathscr{I}(A)\to\mathscr{I}(B)
by
\text{Ext}_{\phi}(I)=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{B\phi(I)B\}
.

It is well-known that the definition of
\text{Res}_{\phi}
is independent of the choice of non-degenerate representation one chooses when writing an ideal of
B
as a kernel. For an action
(A,G,\alpha)
or coaction
(A,G,\delta)
and
\phi=j_{A}
or

, we denote the

map as
\text{Res}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\text{Res}_{i_{A}}
and
\text{Res}_{\delta}\coloneqq\text{Res}_{j_{A}}
. We use the same convention for

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(A,G,\alpha)
and
(A,G,\delta)
be an action and coaction respectively. Moreover, let
\pi:A\to M(B)
be a
*

(a)

The pair

called the induced regular homomorphism of

associated to
\alpha
.

(b)

The pair
((\pi\otimes\lambda)\circ\delta,1_{B}\otimes M)
is covariant with integrated form denoted
\text{Ind}_{\delta}\pi:A\rtimes_{\delta}G\to M(B\otimes\mathcal{K})
called the induced regular homomorphism of
\pi
associated to
\delta
.

When

for some Hilbert space

, we say induced regular representation.

The induced regular homomorphisms give rise to a map on the lattices of ideals
\text{Ind}_{\alpha}:\mathscr{I}(A)\to\mathscr{I}(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
by
\text{Ind}_{\alpha}(\ker\pi)=\ker(\text{Ind}_{\alpha}\pi)
.
\text{Ind}_{\delta}
is defined in the analogous way. We now are in a position to define and discuss the five different notions of action and coaction invariance of ideals:

.

Let
(A,G,\alpha)
and

(a)

(b)

\delta

-algebra

We omit the “

3.
Comparing Strong and Nilsen Invariance

There are multiple relationships between the different notions of invariance given in definition
2.6
. The story for actions is short and simple. In particular, Nilsen’s definition of invariance for an action
(A,G,\alpha)
is equivalent to
\alpha
-invariance.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given an action
(A,G,\alpha)
, we have
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)=\mathscr{I}^{N}_{\alpha}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
*
-representation with
I=\ker\pi
. Suppose first that
I\in\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
so that
\alpha_{s}(I)\subseteq I
for all
s\in G
. Let
a\in I
. Observe that
(\pi\otimes M^{-1})\circ\alpha:A\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\otimes L^{2}(G))\cong\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}))
. Then for
s\in G
, and
\xi\in L^{2}(G,B)

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes M^{-1})\circ\alpha(a)\xi(s)

\displaystyle=\pi(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a))\xi(s)

\displaystyle\subseteq\pi(I)\xi(s)

\displaystyle=\{0\}

so that
\pi\otimes M^{-1}\circ\alpha(a)=0
and hence
a\in\ker((\pi\otimes M^{-1})\circ\alpha)
. On the other hand if
a\in\ker((\pi\otimes M^{-1})\circ\alpha)
, then for any
h\in\mathcal{H}
with
\xi\in L^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
and
\xi(e)=h
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\pi\otimes M^{-1})\circ\alpha(a)\xi(e)

\displaystyle=\pi(\alpha_{e}(a))h

\displaystyle=\pi(a)h.

Hence
a\in\ker\pi
so that
\ker\pi=\ker((\pi\otimes M^{-1})\circ\alpha)
, implying that
I\in\mathscr{I}^{N}_{\alpha}(A)
. Now suppose that
I\in\mathscr{I}^{N}_{\alpha}(A)
and let
a\in I
with
s\in G
. It suffices to show
\pi(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a))=0
. Since
I=\ker((\pi\otimes M^{-1})\circ\alpha)
we have for any
h\in\mathcal{H}
and fixed
s\in G
, one can pick
\xi\in L^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
such that
\xi(s)=h
so that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\pi\otimes M^{-1})\circ\alpha(a)\xi(s)

\displaystyle=\pi(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a))h.

This forces
\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a)\in\ker\pi=I
for each
s\in G
. Hence
I\in\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
, proving the equality.

∎

Thus for actions we will denote
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}^{N}(A)
as just
\mathscr{I}_{\alpha}(A)
. Characterizing when strong and Nilsen invariance are equivalent is a bit trickier. To do so, we require a few lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(A,G,\delta)
be a coaction and let
I\in\mathscr{I}^{s}
. Then
\text{Ext}_{\delta}(I)=I\rtimes_{\delta}G

Proof.

Observe that since
I\in\mathscr{I}^{s}
, we have that
I\rtimes_{\delta|_{I}}G
makes sense so that
I\rtimes_{\delta}G
is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
A\rtimes_{\delta}G
. In particular it’s given by

\displaystyle(\iota\rtimes G)(I\rtimes_{\delta|_{I}}G)

\displaystyle=(\iota\rtimes G)\left(\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j^{\delta|_{I}}_{I}(I)j^{\delta|_{I}}_{G}(C_{0}(G))\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=(j_{A}^{\delta}\circ\iota\rtimes j_{G}^{\delta})\left(\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j^{\delta|_{I}}_{I}(I)j^{\delta|_{I}}_{G}(C_{0}(G))\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(I)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\right\}.

In particular by
*
-invariance we have that

\displaystyle I\rtimes_{\delta}G

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(I)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))j_{A}^{\delta}(I)\right\}.

Thus, we compute to see that

\displaystyle\text{Ext}_{\delta}(I)

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{(A\rtimes_{\delta}G)j_{A}(I)(A\rtimes_{\delta}G)\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))j_{A}^{\delta}(I)j_{A}^{\delta}(A)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))j_{A}^{\delta}(I)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(I)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))j_{A}^{\delta}(I)\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))j_{A}^{\delta}(I)\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))j_{A}^{\delta}(I)\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))j_{A}^{\delta}(I)\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(I)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(A)j_{A}^{\delta}(I)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\right\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\left\{j_{A}^{\delta}(I)j_{G}^{\delta}(C_{0}(G))\right\}

\displaystyle=I\rtimes_{\delta}G.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

If
(A,G,\delta)
is a coaction and

\mathscr{I}^{s}\subseteq\mathscr{I}^{N}
, then
\delta
is normal and
\mathscr{I}^{s}=\mathscr{I}^{N}
.

Proof.

Since
\{0\}\in\mathscr{I}^{s}
, we conclude that it’s also Nilsen invariant so that

\displaystyle\{0\}

\displaystyle=\ker\text{id}_{A}

\displaystyle=\ker\bigl((\text{Ind}_{\delta}\text{id}_{A})\circ j_{A}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\ker j_{A}.

Thus
\delta
is normal. Now, let
I\in\mathscr{I}^{N}
. Then
\text{Ind}_{\delta}(I)\in\mathscr{I}_{\widehat{\delta}}(A\rtimes_{\delta}G)
so that
\text{Ind}_{\delta}(I)=J\rtimes_{\delta}G
for some
J\in\mathscr{I}^{s}\subseteq\mathscr{I}^{N}
by Theorem
2.8
. Hence
J\rtimes_{\delta}G=\text{Ext}_{\delta}(J)
by Lemma
3.2
. However then

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\text{Res}_{\delta}\circ\text{Ind}_{\delta}(I)

\displaystyle=\text{Res}_{\delta}(J\rtimes_{\delta}G)

\displaystyle=\text{Res}_{\delta}\circ\text{Ext}_{\delta}(J)

\displaystyle=J

by Theorem
2.7
so that
I=J\in\mathscr{I}^{s}
and hence that
\mathscr{I}^{s}=\mathscr{I}^{N}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(A,G,\delta)
be a coaction and
I\in\mathscr{I}^{w}
. Then
I\in\mathscr{I}^{N}
if and only if
\delta^{I}
is normal.

Proof.

First suppose that
\ker q=I\in\mathscr{I}^{N}
for
q:A\to A/I
the canonical quotient map. Then
I\in\mathscr{I}^{w}
so that
\delta^{I}
is a well-defined coaction of
G
on

Angela and the electric dipole response – giant and pygmy, hot and cold, isoscalar and isovector
Peter von Neumann-Cosel

Abstract
The impact of Angela Bracco’s work on the electric dipole response of nuclei is discussed using three examples of current nuclear structure problems: disentangling different contributions to the decay width of the giant dipole resonance, the equivalence of photoabsorption and -emission and the nature of the pygmy dipole resonance.

Electric dipole strength, damping of the giant dipole resonance, Brink-Axel hypothesis, pygmy dipole resonance

2
Fine structure and the damping of giant resonances

Interacting fermion systems with finite particle number like the nucleus form collective modes involving most of the particles, commonly called giant resonances
harakeh01
.
They appear at high excitation energies well above the particle thresholds and show widths of several MeV.
Much experimental work has gone into trying to establish their global features like centroid energies and widths.
While the former are reasonably well understand in terms of simple mass dependence, describing the widths remains a challenging theoretical problem.
The total width
\Gamma

\Gamma^{\uparrow}

1h

2h

-

\Gamma^{\downarrow}

\Gamma=\Delta\Gamma+\Gamma^{\uparrow}+\Gamma^{\downarrow}.

Angela Bracco and her coworkers Pier Francesco Bortignon and Ricardo Broglia at the University of Milano have made important contributions to a solution of this problem

Experimentally, it is expected that the coupling scheme leads to fine structure of the giant resonances.
While studies of the gross features of giant resonances typically have limited energy resolution, in the last 20 years a series of experiments has been performed trying to establish and understand the fine structure phenomenon

The wavelet analysis proceeds via the calculation of a wavelet coefficient

\sigma(E)

C(\delta E,E_{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta E}}\int\sigma(E)\Psi^{*}\left(\frac{E_{x}-E}{\delta E}\right)dE,

(2)

where

For studies of nuclear giant resonances, the complex Morlet wavelet

\Psi(x)=\pi^{-1/4}\,e^{ik_{0}x}\,e^{-x^{2}/2},

(3)

k_{0}=5

The continuous wavelet transform (CWT) is used, where the fit procedure can be adjusted freely to the required precision providing optimum balance between resolution of excitation energy and energy scale.
Further details can be found in Ref.

Understanding nuclear structure requires systematic studies of the nuclear response as a function of key parameters like mass, spin or excitation energy.
The latter is of particular importance for the description nuclear reactions in stellar environments, where temperatures can be sufficiently high that in equilibrium significant parts of the nuclei are in excited states.
This immediately raises the question whether the strength functions change as a function of temperature.
Theoretical work based on shell model
johnson15
;
sieja23
or QRPA
hung17
;
wibovo22
predict this is generally the case for collective modes, except maybe for the
E1
response.

For the latter, the Brink-Axel (BA) hypothesis

;
axel62

The concept is illustrated in the l.h.s. of Fig.

A typical

It is dominated by the GDR, but for many astrophysical applications only the part below the neutron threshold is relevant.
There, a resonance-like is observed in neutron-rich nuclei called pygmy dipole resonance (PDR) discussed in more detail in Sec.
4

Nucleosynthesis reaction network calculations are typically performed within a statistical approach, and the BA hypothesis dramatically simplifies the problem for reactions including
\gamma

However, there is no real justification and its applicability is a topic of controversial discussion.

The observation of the GDR in the
\gamma

;
bortignon91
;
broglia92
;
bracco95

At temperatures of several MeV, the GDR exhibits shape changes violating the BA hypothesis, but for
T\leq 1

For the GSF below neutron threshold most important in astrophysical applications, conflicting results have been reported claiming confirmation

;
crespocampo18
;

This problem has been recently adressed in a study of the

with quasicontinuum

The latter results permit a test of the independence of the GSF from initial and final energy demonstrated in Fig.

The upper and lower rows show the GSFs extracted for specific excitation energy (red circles) or final energy (blue circles) windows.
Clearly, they are all compatible within uncertainties with the result averaged over the full accessible energy range (light blue band) as expected from the BA hypothesis.

In the energy region covered by both experiments, the GSFs agree within error bands.
The shape method

However, it is not clear yet to what extent they can be generalized.
Future work has to explore the limits systematically with respect to mass, excitation energy and level density.
Limits for latter are e.g. indicated by deviations of the shape method at energies below 5 MeV.

4
Nature of the PDR

A current topic of low-energy nuclear physics is the observation of a local enhancement of the electric dipole response in heavy nuclei with neutron excess, commonly called PDR
savran13
.
Besides understanding the underlying structure, it impacts on
(n,\gamma
) cross sections in various astrophysical processes contributing to the nucleosynthesis of heavy elements
wiedeking24
.
Angela Bracco has made decisive contributions
bracco19
to its understanding, in particular to measurements of the isovector strength in exotic neutron-rich nuclei
wieland09
;
wieland18
, its isoscalar response
crespi14
;
pellegri14
;
crespi21
and the possible connection to the equation of state of neutron-rich matter
carbone10
.

The nature of the PDR is a subject of long-standing discussion
lanza23
.
Three possible interpretations are illustrated in Fig.
8
by their characteristic current distributions.
It may be seen as oscillation of the neutron skin relative to an isospin-saturated core
paar07
, as low-energy branch of the dipole compression mode
rocamaza18
, or as a toroidal mode
repko13
;
repko19
.
An interpretation as low-energy compression mode can most likely be excluded because experimental isoscalar strengths are so large
poelhekken92
that it would significantly alter the compressibility extracted from the isoscalar GDR to disagree with the value derived from the GMR
rocamaza18
.

The claim of the PDR representing a neutron skin oscillation is mainly based on the peculiar form of the radial transition density in density functional models, which shows an approximately isoscalar behavior in the interior and a pronounced peak of the neutron part at the nuclear surface.
If true, its isovector strength and evolution with neutron excess could provide information on the neutron skin thickness and parameters of the symmetry energy.

The Sn isotope chain, were the proton shell closure stabilizes the low-energy structure, is probably the best case to investigate the role of neutron excess in the PDR strength.
Results from a recent study covering the masses
A=111-124
sheds new light on this problem
markova25
.
Combining studies of the low-energy isovector
E1
strength from relativistic Coulomb excitation
bassauer20a
and from the Oslo method
markova24
permit a decomposition into the low-energy tail of the GDR and additional resonance-like structures.
Integrating over an energy region
4-10
MeV, the resulting strengths in terms of the TRK sum rule and centroid energies are displayed in Figs.
9
(a) and (b), respectively.
Besides the approximately constant contribution from the GDR, a pronounced resonance around 8 MeV with a strength of about 2% of the TRK sum rule is consistently observed.
For
A\geq 118
a second resonance peaked at about 6.5 MeV with much smaller strength (
\leq 0.5
%) is demanded by the data.

The latter peak can be identified as the PDR based on the following experimental signatures:
PDR states dominantly decay to the ground state and a peak-like structure around 6.5 MeV is observed in
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
experiments on Sn isotopes
oezeltashenov14
.
A key feature of the PDR is its simultaneous observation in experiments with isovector and isoscalar probes.
This was demonstrated for the energy region around 6.5 MeV while the peak at higher excitation energies is absent in the isoscalar response
endres10
;
pellegri14
,
The latter also has a different structure with dominant statistical decay
krumbholz15
;
muescher20
.

QRPA calculations grossly overestimate the isovector PDR strength, they are rather a measure of the total low-energy response.
In order to achieve more realistic low-energy strength distributions one has to invoke the coupling to complex configurations
oezeltashenov14
;
markova25
.
This also changes the predicted transition densities in such a way that in the PDR region the characteristic ”PDR-like” pattern described above is found while at higher excitation energies they change to isovector dominance
markova25
.

The small isovector strengths cast doubt on the picture of a collective neutron-skin oscillation and a possible connection to bulk properties like the symmetry energy
carbone10
.
However, isoscalar cross section are large when compared to excitation of the isoscalar GDR.
It is also theoretically predicted that a possible collectivity of the PDR rather manifests in the isoscalar channel
lanza23
.
Thus, it would be worthwile to measure the low-energy isoscalar dipole strength along the Sn isotopic chain.

An alternative interpretation describes the PDR as low-energy branch of the toroidal
E1
mode
repko13
.
Such a mode is consistently predicted in fluid-dynamical
bastrukov93
and microscopic density functional
vretenar02
;
ryezayeva02
models and should be a general phenomenon across the nuclear chart
repko19
.
Experimental evidence of the toroidal mode was lacking so far because it is very fragmented and strongly mixed with the isoscalar and isovector GDR.
However, low-energy toroidal strength is predicted to be relatively pure
nesterenko18
.

The measured electron scattering cross sections all show a strong increase towards backward angles indicating large transverse form factors.
Transverse form factors are a measure of the transition current distributions.
These are expected to be negligible for the isocalar and isovector GDR which represent lateral (i.e., irrotational) motion.
On the other hand, rotational current distributions are characteristic for toroidal motion.
Theoretical predictions of the electron scattering cross sections with DFT can describe the experimental data in

How does a study of
58
Ni with
N\approx Z
relate to the discussion of the PDR in nuclei with sufficient neutron excess?
The QRPA models successfully describing the toroidal
mode in
58
Ni
vonneumanncosel24
also predict a toroidal nature of the PDR in neutron-rich nuclei
repko19
.
Experimentally, the toroidal states identified in
58
Ni exhibit the same features as low-lying
1^{-}
states in neutron-rich nuclei forming the PDR, namely large
ground-state branching ratios
brandherm24
, large
B(E1)
strengths with respect to average low-energy isovector strengths and a strong isoscalar response
poelhekken92
.
Since the toroidal
E1
mode is generic, these findings also suggest a toroidal nature of PDR modes.

A measurement of transverse electron scattering form factors in a nucleus with large neutron excess would be an important step to resolve this long-standing issue.
A recently commissioned setup for
(e,e^{\prime}\gamma)
experiments

at the S-DALINAC facility

E1

vonneumanncosel24

5
Summary

Angela Bracco’s work in nuclear structure physics spans a wide range of topics, but a recurrent theme throughout her scientific career was the study of the nuclear electric dipole response, understanding giant and pygmy contributions, at high and low excitation energies, in hot and cold nuclei, with isoscalar and isovector probes.
Illustrated by three examples, I tried to demonstrate how her work has impacted on current topics in nuclear structure: the damping of the GDR, the equivalence of photonuclear absorption and emission at zero and finite temperatures, expressed as generalized Brink-Axel hypothesis, and the structure underlying the PDR phenomenon.
None of these topics is solved, but her work has made esssential contributions and helped to shape the progress towards their understanding.

Shear viscosity at finite magnetic field for graphene, non-relativistic and ultra-relativistic cases Preprint: APS/123-QED
Cho Win Aung, Thandar Zaw Win, Subhalaxmi Nayak, Sabyasachi Ghosh

Abstract
Present article has addressed finite magnetic field extension of previous work by Cho et al. ( Phys. Rev. B 108 , 235172, 2023) on microscopic calculation of shear viscosity for electron fluid in graphene system. Our calculation is based on the kinetic theory approach in the relaxation time approximation. In the absence of magnetic field, transport is governed by a single shear viscosity coefficient, whereas the application of a finite magnetic field induces anisotropy, give rise to the five independent shear viscosity coefficients associated with distinct velocity gradient tensors. These coefficient can be physically categorized into perpendicular, parallel and Hall components relative to the magnetic field direction. When the scattering time equals to the cyclotron time, the perpendicular component is suppressed by 80 % 80\% and parallel component by 50 % 50\% , and Hall effect can reach maximum. Corresponding magnetic field strength for electron fluid in graphene is around 0.01 − 0.1 0.01-0.1 Tesla and the same for non-relativistic electron fluid and ultra-relativistic quark fluid are around 10 10 Tesla and 10 14 10^{14} Tesla respectively. They may be considered as required magnetic field strength in three different fluid systems to observe noticeable magnetic field response in their shear viscosity coefficients.

Introduction

When there is a conventional flow of electrons in metals, electrons dissipate most of their energy in collisions with the lattice imperfections at low temperatures, called disorder scattering that are formed because of chemical impurities, vacant lattice sites, and extra atoms not in regular lattice positions. In contrast, the dissipation is mainly in electron-phonon interactions at high temperatures when phonon populations are higher. But, there is a possible electron-electron interaction which is less emphasized in the context of electron transport in metals for a few reasons, primarily due to the relative energy scales, the Pauli exclusion principle, and the screening effects in metals. Present work is aimed at the microscopic calculation of the shear viscosity of this electron fluid in graphene system, which may be called in short as graphene fluid (GF). When one microscopically calculates the expression of the shear viscosity of GF, it will be different from its standard expression for non-relativistic fluid (NRF) as well as for relativistic fluid (RF) or ultra-relativistic fluid (URF).

IV
Summary and Interpretation

In summary, we have calculated shear viscosity tensor of electron fluid in graphene system at finite magnetic field using the relaxation time approximation based on kinetic theory framework. In contrast to the isotropic shear viscosity coefficient in absence of magnetic field, an anisotropic transportation will be created due to magnetic field, which give rise to five independent shear viscosity coefficients by accompanying with five velocity gradient tensors. These coefficients are physically categorized by three components: perpendicular, parallel and hall components according to the applied magnetic field direction. At zero magnetic field limit, there will be no Hall viscosity and no anisotropy, which means that parallel and perpendicular component remain same and equal to the isotropic value. As magnetic field increases, anisotropy in viscous tensor starts, which means that parallel and perpendicular components of viscosity become unequal. However, for building a noticeable anisotropy, magnetic field should cross a threshold values, whcih are around
10^{-2}-10^{-1}
Tesla,
10
Tesla and
10^{14}-10^{15}
Tesla for electron fluid in graphene system, non-relativistic electron fluid and ultra-relativistic quark/hadronic fluid respectively. Near charge neutrality point
(\frac{\mu}{T}\rightarrow 0)
, Hall viscosity of electron fluid in graphene is disappeared due to cancellation of electron and hole contribution. Same is true for quark/hadronic fluid at zero (net) quark/baryon density or chemical potential, expected in RHIC and LHC experiments. So, for a getting a non-zero Hall viscosity, we need a finite electron Fermi energy and quark chemical potential for which electron and quark density becomes larger than the hole and anti-quark density respectively. Former case for graphene system can be achieved via doping method and later case for quark/hadronic fluid at finite quark/baryon chemical potential can be produced via low center of mass energy collision experiments like CBM and NICA. However, there is a possibility to break down the fluid property due to fluid to non-fluid transition as density increases. So, for seeing non-zero Hall ciscosity, we have to stay in such a small but non-zero density, where fluid property does not break down. When relaxation time scale is equal to cyclotron time scale, perpendicular component reduced to
80\%
, parallel component reduced to
50\%
and Hall was showing maximum. To see the noticeable anisotropy and maximum Hall viscosity, different fluid system need different magnetic field strengths as mentioned earlier, where interesting news is that graphene requires only
0.01-0.1
Tesla to observe maximum Hall viscosity.

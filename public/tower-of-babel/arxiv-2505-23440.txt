Abstract
. Volume comparison theorem is a type of fundamental results in Riemannian geometry. In this article, we extend the volume comparison result in [ 2 ] to the comparison of total σ l \sigma_{l} -curvature with respect to σ k \sigma_{k} -curvature ( l < k l<k ). In particular, we prove the comparison holds for metrics close to strictly stable positive Einstein metric with l < n 2 l<\frac{n}{2} . As for negative Einstein metrics, we prove a similar comparison result provided certain assumptions on sectional curvature holds for the manifold.



1.
Introduction

As a type of important problem in Riemannian geometry, the comparison problem is usually described as followed: whether certain geometric functional is bounded by the reference manifold provided certain curvature conditions hold?

The geometric functional that has been studied the most extensively is undoubtedly volume. For example, the famous Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem said that the volume of a complete manifold is upper bounded by the round sphere if its Ricci curvature is lower bounded by a corresponding positive constant. People tried to improve this result by replacing the assumption of Ricci curvature by a weaker one. However, it’s shown by Bray
[
5
]
, scalar curvature is not sufficient to control the volume for the case of positive curvature. As for the negative curvature, Schoen
[
23
]
proposed a conjecture that the volume of a closed manifold is lower bounded by the closed hyperbolic manifold if its scalar curvature is lower bounded by the corresponding scalar curvature. The evidences of this conjecture can be found in
[

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Definitions

We begin by recalling the definition of Einstein manifolds.

Definition 2.1

(Einstein manifold)
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
is said to be Einstein with Einstein constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
if its Ricci curvature satisfies

\mathrm{Ric}_{g}=(n-1)\lambda g.

We denote the space of transverse-traceless symmetric 2-tensors by
S_{2,g}^{TT}(M)
and we define the following Einstein operator

\Delta_{E}=\Delta_{g}+2\mathrm{Rm}_{g}

on
S_{2,g}^{TT}(M)
, where
\mathrm{Rm}_{g}
denotes the curvature tensor. The positivity of this operator plays a crucial role in the study of Einstein manifolds. To further analyze the geometric properties of Einstein manifolds, we introduce the notion of stability for Einstein metrics. This concept is closely tied to the spectral properties of the Einstein operator
\Delta_{E}
on transverse-traceless tensors.

Definition 2.2

(Stability of Einstein metric)
.

For a closed Einstein manifold
(M^{n},g)
where dimension n is at least 3, metric
g
is said to be stable if the Einstein operator
\Delta_{E}
is non-positive on
S_{2,g}^{TT}(M)\backslash\{0\}
, that is

\min\text{spec}_{TT}(-\Delta_{E})=\inf_{h\in S_{2,g}^{TT}(M)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{M}\langle h,-\Delta_{E}h\rangle_{g}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}|h|_{g}^{2}dv_{g}}\geq 0.

Moreover,
g
is called strictly stable if the inequality is strict.

For a closed Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, the Schouten curvature is

S_{g}=\mathrm{Ric}_{g}-\frac{R_{g}}{2(n-1)}g,

where
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}
and
R_{g}
are Ricci curvature and scalar curvature of
g
respectively. The
\sigma_{k}
-curvature is defined as the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues of
S_{g}
. Namely,

Definition 2.3

(
\sigma_{k}
-curvature)
.

For a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, let
S_{g}
denote the Schouten tensor and
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
its eigenvalues. The
\sigma_{k}
-curvature of
g
is defined as the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of these eigenvalues:

\sigma_{k}:=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},

where
1\leq k\leq n
.

In particular,
\sigma_{1}
is the scalar curvature (up to normalization) and
\sigma_{n}
gives the sectional curvature determinant. Following Reilly
[
21
,
22
]
, an equivalent but more practical definition of
\sigma_{k}
-curvature was proposed in
[
7
]
:

(2.1)

\sigma_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}S_{j_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots S_{j_{k}}^{i_{k}},

where
\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}
is the generalized Kronecker delta defined as

\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }j_{1}\cdots j_{k}\text{ are distinct integers with even permutation of }i_{1}\cdots i_{k};\\
-1,\text{ if }j_{1}\cdots j_{k}\text{ are distinct integers with odd permutation of }i_{1}\cdots i_{k};\\
0,\text{ in other cases.}\end{cases}

2.2.
Geometric variational formulas

In the study of Riemannian geometry, the behavior of geometric quantities under variations of the metric tensor plays a pivotal role in understanding the structure and stability of manifolds. These variations are fundamental in diverse areas such as general relativity, geometric analysis, and the calculus of variations, where they inform the analysis of critical points, stability conditions, and geometric flows.

In this section, we will list some variational formulas that will be used later. We emphasize that any unspecified calculations are performed in the reference metric
\bar{g}
and assume that the linearization of the metric is
h\in\mathcal{S}_{2,\bar{g}}(M)
, which is the spaces of all symmetric 2-tensors on
M
.

2.2.1.
Basic variational formulas

In this part, we will give basic variational formulas for inversed metric, volume, Ricci curvature and scalar curvature, all of detailed proof can be found in
[
25
,
26
]
. First of all, we recall the variational formulas of the inverse metric:

Lemma 2.1
.

In local coordinates, the first and second variations of the inverse metric are

D\bar{g}^{jk}\cdot h=-h^{jk}

and

D^{2}\bar{g}^{jk}\cdot(h,h)=2h_{i}^{j}h^{ik}.

For volume:

Lemma 2.2
.

The first and second variations of volume are

D\text{Vol}_{M}(\bar{g})\cdot h=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left(tr_{\bar{g}}h\right)dv_{\bar{g}}

and

D^{2}\text{Vol}_{M}(\bar{g})\cdot(h,h)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}\left(\left(tr_{\bar{g}}h\right)^{2}-2|h|^{2}\right)dv_{\bar{g}}.

For Ricci curvature:

Lemma 2.3
.

The first variations of Ricci curvature is

DRic_{\bar{g}}\cdot h=-\frac{1}{2}\big(\Delta_{L}h+\mathscr{L}_{X}\bar{g}\big)

where
\Delta_{L}
is the Lichnerowicz Laplacian defined by

\Delta_{L}h_{jk}=\Delta_{E}h_{jk}-R_{ji}h_{k}^{i}-R_{ki}h_{j}^{i},

in local coordinates and
X=\big(\frac{1}{2}d(trh)+\delta h\big)^{\#}
. The second variations of Ricci curvature, in local coordinates, is

\begin{split}D^{2}Ric_{jk}\cdot(h,h)=&h^{pi}(R_{ijkl}h_{p}^{l}+R_{ijpl}h_{k}^{l}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}h_{jp}+\nabla_{i}\nabla_{p}h_{jk}+\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}h_{ip}-\nabla_{j}\nabla_{p}h_{ik})\\
&+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{j}h_{k}^{p}+\nabla_{k}h_{j}^{p}-\nabla^{p}h_{jk})\big(2(\delta h)_{p}+\nabla_{p}(trh)\big)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{i}h_{k}^{p}+\nabla_{k}h_{i}^{p}-\nabla^{p}h_{ik})(\nabla_{j}h_{p}^{i}-\nabla_{p}h_{j}^{i}+\nabla^{i}h_{jp}).\end{split}

For scalar curvature:

Lemma 2.4
.

The first and second variations of scalar curvature are

DR_{\bar{g}}\cdot h=\gamma_{\bar{g}}h:=-\Delta(trh)+\delta^{2}h-Ric\cdot h

and

\begin{split}D^{2}R_{\bar{g}}\cdot(h,h)=&-2\gamma_{\bar{g}}(h\times h)-\Delta(|h|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla h|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|d(trh)|^{2}\\
&+2\langle h,\nabla^{2}(trh)\rangle-2\langle\delta h,d(trh)\rangle+\nabla_{i}h_{jk}\nabla^{j}h^{ik}.\end{split}

2.2.2.
Variations for
\sigma_{k}
-curvature when
\bar{g}
is Einstein

In this subsection, we derive the variation formula for the
\sigma_{k}
-curvature under the assumption that the reference metric
\bar{g}
is an Einstein metric with Einstein constant
\lambda
. For such metrics, the Schouten tensor
S_{\bar{g}}
and the
\sigma_{k}
-curvature are explicitly given by:

S_{\bar{g}}=\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\bar{g},\quad\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})=\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k}\binom{n}{k}.

This simplification arises because the eigenvalues of
S_{\bar{g}}
are all equal to
\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda
, reducing the
\sigma_{k}
-curvature to a power of the constant
\lambda
multiplied by the binomial coefficient
\binom{n}{k}
. These expressions will serve as foundational inputs for computing the variation formula in the subsequent analysis.

For the variations of
\sigma_{k}
-curvature, instead of using the direct definition, the equivalent expression (1) in 2.1 will be more convenient for calculation. Also, in order to make it looks more concise, we will use simplified symbols, such as
\dot{R}
and
\ddot{R}
, to denotes the first and second variations of the corresponding geometric functional.

The first variation of
\sigma_{k}
-curvature is

\begin{split}D\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\cdot h&=\frac{1}{k!}\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}S_{j_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots S_{j_{k-1}}^{i_{k-1}}\times k(\dot{\bar{g}}^{i_{k}p_{k}}S_{j_{k}p_{k}}+\bar{g}^{i_{k}p_{k}}\dot{S}_{j_{k}p_{k}})\\
&=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-1}\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}\delta_{j_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots\delta_{j_{k-1}}^{i_{k-1}}\left(-\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda h_{j_{k}}^{i_{k}}+\bar{g}^{i_{k}p_{k}}\dot{S}_{j_{k}p_{k}}\right).\end{split}

Using the contraction rule of the generalized Kronecker delta:

\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{p}}\delta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}=p!\frac{(n-k+p)!}{(n-k)!}\delta_{i_{p+1}\cdots i_{k}}^{j_{p+1}\cdots j_{k}}

for
p<k
, we have

D\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\cdot h=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-1}\left(-\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda tr_{\bar{g}}h+tr_{\bar{g}}\dot{S}\right),

where

\dot{S}=\dot{Ric}-\frac{\dot{R}}{2(n-1)}\bar{g}-\frac{R}{2(n-1)}h.

Combining lemma 2.3 and 2.4, we deduce that

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\bar{g}
is an Einstein metric with Einstein constant
\lambda
, then the first variation of the
\sigma_{k}
-curvature is given by:

D\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\cdot h=\frac{1}{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{k}\lambda^{k-1}\big(-\Delta trh+\delta^{2}h-(n-1)\lambda trh\big).

Similarly, for the second derivatives, we have

\displaystyle D^{2}\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\cdot(h,h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-1}\left(\ddot{\bar{g}}^{ij}S_{ij}+2\dot{\bar{g}}^{ij}\dot{S}_{ij}+tr_{\bar{g}}\ddot{S}\right)

\displaystyle+\binom{n-2}{k-2}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-2}\delta_{ik}^{jl}\left(\dot{\bar{g}}^{ip}\dot{\bar{g}}^{kq}S_{jp}S_{lq}+2\dot{\bar{g}}^{ip}\bar{g}^{kq}S_{jp}\dot{S}_{lq}+\bar{g}^{ip}\bar{g}^{kq}\dot{S}_{jp}\dot{S}_{lq}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-1}\left((n-2)\lambda|h|^{2}-2h^{ij}\dot{S}_{ij}+tr\ddot{S}\right)

\displaystyle+\binom{n-2}{k-2}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-2}\biggl(\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{2}\left(\left(tr_{\bar{g}}h\right)^{2}-|h|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-(n-2)\lambda\left((tr_{\bar{g}}h)(tr_{\bar{g}}\dot{S})-h^{ij}\dot{S}_{ij}\right)+\left((tr_{\bar{g}}\dot{S})^{2}-|\dot{S}|^{2}\right)\biggr).

Observed that for arbitrary Riemannian metric
g
,

tr_{g}S_{g}=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}R_{g}.

Thus

\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\gamma_{\bar{g}}h=\dot{\bar{g}}^{ij}S_{ij}+\bar{g}^{ij}\dot{S}_{ij}=-\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda tr_{\bar{g}}h+tr_{\bar{g}}\dot{S}.

Therefore, we deduce

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\bar{g}
is an Einstein metric with Einstein constant
\lambda
, then the second variation of the
\sigma_{k}
-curvature is given by:

(2.2)

\begin{split}D^{2}\sigma_{k}&(\bar{g})\cdot(h,h)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-1}\\
&\times\bigg(tr_{\bar{g}}\ddot{S}-\frac{2(k-1)}{\lambda(n-1)(n-2)}|\dot{S}|^{2}-\frac{2(n-k)}{n-1}h^{ij}\dot{S}_{ij}\\
&\qquad+\frac{(k-1)(n-2)}{2\lambda(n-1)^{3}}(\gamma_{\bar{g}}h)^{2}+\frac{(2n-k-1)(n-2)}{2(n-1)}\lambda|h|^{2}\bigg),\end{split}

where

\ddot{S}=\ddot{\mathrm{Ric}}-\frac{\ddot{R}}{2(n-1)}\bar{g}-\frac{\dot{R}}{n-1}h.

3.
The key functional and variations

For the volume comparison theorems with respect to scalar curvature
[
26
]
, Q-curvature
[
17

18
]
or
\sigma_{k}
-curvature
[

\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}(g)=\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(g)dv_{g}\right)^{2k}\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{k}(g)dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{n-2l}

where
l<k
and
g

M^{n}

\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}(g)
is a scaling invariant with respect to
g
.

Remark 3.1
.

When
l=\frac{n}{2}
, since now
\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}
is independent of
\sigma_{k}
, we can not deal with this case through the functional. On the other hand, according to
[
8
]

As a generalized functional designed for Einstein manifolds,
\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}
should satisfies the basic requirement about the first variation:

Proposition 3.1
.

The Einstein metric
\bar{g}
is a critical point of
\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}
.

Proof.

The first variation of
\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}
at
\bar{g}
is

\begin{split}D\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}({\bar{g}})\cdot h=&2k\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{2k-1}\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{k}(g)dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{n-2l}\\
&\qquad\times\left(\int_{M}(D\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})\cdot h)dv_{\bar{g}}+\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})(Ddv_{\bar{g}}\cdot h)\right)\\
&+\left(n-2l\right)\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{2k}\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{k}(g)dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{n-2l-1}\int_{M}\left(D\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\cdot h\right)dv_{\bar{g}}.\end{split}

From lemma 2.5,

\int_{M}\left(D\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\cdot h\right)dv_{\bar{g}}=-\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k}\int_{M}\left(tr_{\bar{g}}h\right)dv_{\bar{g}}.

Therefore, combining the above result and Lemma 2, we have

\begin{split}D\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}({\bar{g}})\cdot h=&\left(\mathrm{Vol}_{M}(\bar{g})\right)^{n+2k-2l-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{nk}\binom{n}{k}^{n-2l}\binom{n}{l}^{2k}\\
&\times\left(2k\left(-\frac{l}{n}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-(n-2l)\frac{k}{n}\right)\int_{M}(tr_{\bar{g}}h)dv_{\bar{g}}=0.\end{split}

∎

For the second variation, to minimize the calculation, we take the TT-gauge condition for symmetric two tensor
h
:

h=\mathring{h}+\frac{tr_{\bar{g}}h}{n}\bar{g}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathring{h}\in S_{2,\bar{g}}^{TT}.

\bar{g}

\mathcal{F}_{\bar{g}}
at

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{M}\big(D\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})\cdot h)dv_{\bar{g}}+\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})(Ddv_{\bar{g}}\cdot h)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{M}D^{2}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})\cdot(h,h)dv_{\bar{g}}+2\int_{M}(D\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})\cdot h)(Ddv_{\bar{g}}\cdot h)+\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})(D^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}\cdot(h,h))\right)

\displaystyle+4k(n-2l)\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{2k-1}\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{n-2l-1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{M}(D\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})\cdot h)dv_{\bar{g}}+\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})(Ddv_{\bar{g}}\cdot h)\right)\int_{M}\big(D\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\cdot h\big)dv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle+(n-2l)(n-2l-1)\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{2k}\left(\int_{M}\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}\right)^{n-2l-2}\int_{M}\big(D^{2}\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\cdot(h,h)\big)dv_{\bar{g}}.

From (2),

\begin{split}\int_{M}D^{2}\sigma_{k}&(\bar{g})\cdot(h,h)dv_{\bar{g}}=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-1}\\
&\times\bigg(\int_{M}(tr_{\bar{g}}\ddot{S})dv_{\bar{g}}-\frac{2(k-1)}{\lambda(n-1)(n-2)}\int_{M}|\dot{S}|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}-\frac{2(n-k)}{n-1}\int_{M}h^{ij}\dot{S}_{ij}dv_{\bar{g}}\\
&\qquad+\frac{(k-1)(n-2)}{2\lambda(n-1)^{3}}\int_{M}(\gamma_{\bar{g}}h)^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}+\frac{(2n-k-1)(n-2)}{2(n-1)}\lambda\int_{M}|h|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}\bigg),\end{split}

Since
h=\mathring{h}+\frac{trh}{n}\bar{g}\in S^{TT}_{2,\bar{g}}(M)\oplus(C^{\infty}(M)\cdot\bar{g})
,

|h|^{2}=|\mathring{h}|^{2}+\frac{(trh)^{2}}{n}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \delta h=-\frac{d(trh)}{n}.

After rewriting lemma 2.2 to 2.4 under this decompositions, we get (for detailed calculations, please refer to
[
7
]
)

\displaystyle\int_{M}tr_{\bar{g}}\ddot{S}dv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left[\mathring{h}\cdot\left(-\frac{3n-2}{2(n-1)}\Delta_{E}+(n-2)\lambda\right)\mathring{h}-\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{2n^{2}}\left|dtr_{\bar{g}}h\right|^{2}\right]dv_{\bar{g}};

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\dot{S}\right|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}\left[\mathring{h}\cdot\left(\Delta_{E}-(n-2)\lambda\right)^{2}\mathring{h}\right]+\left(\frac{n-2}{n}\right)^{2}\left[\left(\Delta tr_{\bar{g}}h\right)^{2}-(n-1)\lambda\left|dtr_{\bar{g}}h\right|^{2}\right]dv_{\bar{g}};

\displaystyle\int_{M}h^{ij}\dot{S}_{ij}dv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left[\mathring{h}\cdot\left(-\Delta_{E}+(n-2)\lambda\right)\mathring{h}+\frac{n-2}{n^{2}}\left|dtr_{\bar{g}}h\right|^{2}\right]dv_{\bar{g}};

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\gamma_{\bar{g}}h)^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle=(n-1)^{2}\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left(\Delta tr_{\bar{g}}h\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{n}\lambda\left|dtr_{\bar{g}}h\right|^{2}+\lambda^{2}\left|tr_{\bar{g}}h\right|^{2}\right)dv_{\bar{g}}.

Combining these expressions, we have

\begin{split}\int_{M}D^{2}\sigma_{k}&(\bar{g})\cdot(h,h)dv_{\bar{g}}=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\right)^{k-1}\\
&\times\int_{M}\bigg[\frac{k-1}{2\lambda(n-1)(n-2)}\mathring{h}\cdot\Delta_{E}\left(-\Delta_{E}+\frac{\lambda(n-2)^{2}}{2(k-1)}\right)\mathring{h}+\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda|\mathring{h}|^{2}\\
&\qquad\quad+\frac{(n-2)(n+2k)}{4n^{2}}\left(-|dtrh|^{2}+\frac{2n(k+1)}{n+2k}\lambda|trh|^{2}\right)\bigg]dv_{\bar{g}}.\end{split}

Finally, we get the formula of

by inserting the above result back into the initial expression.

Now, we discuss the case for

Remark 4.1
.

The stability condition is necessary. Otherwise, it’s easy to construct a counterexample.

According to
[
15
]
, the prototypical example of an unstable Einstein manifold is the product of two Einstein manifolds with identical positive Einstein constants. Assume
n
is even and
(N^{\frac{n}{2}}_{i},g_{i})\ (i=1,2)
are two Einstein manifolds with
\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{i}}=\lambda g_{i}\ (\lambda>0)
. Then
(M=N_{1}\times N_{2},\bar{g}=g_{1}+g_{2})
is an unstable Einstein manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{\bar{g}}=\lambda\bar{g}
. Set

g_{t}=\frac{1}{1+t}g_{1}+\frac{1}{1-t+\frac{t^{2}}{n}}g_{2},

then for
k=0,1,2,\cdots,n
,

\begin{split}\sigma_{k}(g)=&\left(\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\lambda\right)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\left\{1+\left[\left(\frac{k^{2}}{4}+k+\frac{3k(n-k)}{4(n-1)}\right)\left(\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\right)^{-2}+\frac{k}{2n}\right]t^{2}\right\}\\
&+o(t^{2})\\
\geq&\left(\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\lambda\right)^{k}\binom{n}{k}=\sigma_{k}(\bar{g})\end{split}

provided
|t|
small enough. On the other hand,

dv_{g_{t}}=\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)t^{2}+\frac{1}{n}t^{3}\right)^{-\frac{n}{4}}dv_{\bar{g}}\geq dv_{\bar{g}}.

provided
|t|
small enough. Therefore for
l=0,1,2,\cdots,n
,

\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(g)dv_{g}\geq\int_{M}\sigma_{l}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}

provided
|t|
small enough. This is contrary to the above result of stable Einstein manifold.

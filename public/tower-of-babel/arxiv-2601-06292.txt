The discrete second moment of mixed derivatives of the Riemann zeta function
Benjamin Durkan, Christopher Hughes, Andrew Pearce-Crump

Abstract
. We establish the full asymptotic for the discrete second moment of the Riemann zeta function of mixed derivatives evaluated at the zeta zeros, providing both unconditional and conditional error terms. This was first studied by Gonek, where only the leading order asymptotic was given, later extended by Conrey–Snaith and Milinovich to include the lower order terms for the first derivative. We extend the case of the first derivative to all derivatives.



1.
Introduction

The central object of this paper is the discrete second moment of mixed derivatives of the Riemann zeta function, given by the sum

(1.1)

I(\mu,\nu):=I(\mu,\nu;T)=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\zeta^{(\mu)}(\rho)\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-\rho),

as
T\to\infty
, where the sum is over non-trivial zeros of zeta
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
, and where
\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)
denotes the
\mu^{\text{th}}
derivative of the Riemann zeta function. We will establish a full asymptotic expansion for this sum, with a power-saving error term under the Riemann Hypothesis.

This type of discrete moment was introduced by Gonek
[
9
]
, who proved for positive integers
\mu,\nu
, a leading order asymptotic of the form

(1.2)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\zeta^{(\mu)}(\rho)\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-\rho)\\
=(-1)^{\mu+\nu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu+\nu+1}-\frac{1}{(\mu+1)(\nu+1)}\right)\frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{\mu+\nu+2}+O\left(T(\log T)^{\mu+\nu+1}\right)

as
T\to\infty
.

The special case
\mu=\nu
, called the discrete second moment of zeta, is given under the Riemann Hypothesis by

(1.3)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\zeta^{(\nu)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)\right|^{2}=\frac{\nu^{2}}{(2\nu+1)(\nu+1)^{2}}\frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{2\nu+2}+O\left(T(\log T)^{2\nu+1}\right)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
.

Conrey and Snaith
[
3
]
conjectured under the Riemann Hypothesis that for
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary and
L=\log t/2\pi
,

\displaystyle\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}|\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)|^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}L^{4}+\frac{2\gamma_{0}}{3}L^{3}+\left(\gamma_{0}^{2}-2\gamma_{1}\right)L^{2}-\left(2\gamma_{0}^{3}+2\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\right)L

\displaystyle+\left(2\gamma_{0}^{4}+2\gamma_{0}^{2}\gamma_{1}+14\gamma_{1}^{2}+8\gamma_{0}\gamma_{2}+\frac{10\gamma_{3}}{3}\right)\Bigg)\ dt+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right),

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where the
\gamma_{n}
are the Stieltjes coefficients from the expansion of
\zeta(s)
around
s=1
,

\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}(s-1)+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2!}(s-1)^{2}+\cdots+(-1)^{n}\frac{\gamma_{n}}{n!}(s-1)^{n}+\cdots.

Milinovich
[
18
]
proved this conjecture under the assumption of the Riemann Hypothesis, writing the asymptotic as

(1.4)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\zeta^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)\right|^{2}=\frac{T}{2\pi}P_{4}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where
P_{4}(x)
is a degree four polynomial given by

\displaystyle P_{4}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}x^{4}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2\gamma_{0}-1}{3}\right)x^{3}

\displaystyle+\left(1-2\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}-2\gamma_{1}\right)x^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(-2+4\gamma_{0}-2\gamma_{0}^{2}-2\gamma_{0}^{3}-10\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}+4\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}\right)x

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{6+6\gamma_{0}(5\gamma_{1}+4\gamma_{2}-2)+6\gamma_{0}^{2}(\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{0}^{2}+6\gamma_{1}+1)-12\gamma_{1}+42\gamma_{1}^{2}+3\gamma_{2}+10\gamma_{3}}{3}\right)

Remark
.

The equivalence of Milinovich’s result and the conjecture of Conrey and Snaith follows by performing the integral.

Some examples of other generalisations of the results discussed above are shifted second moment results

2.
Statement of Results

We generalise the results of both (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) by proving a full asymptotic expansion for the discrete second moment of mixed derivatives of the Riemann zeta function (
1.1
), both unconditionally and conditionally under the Riemann Hypothesis.

Theorem 1
.

For positive integers
\mu,\nu
, we have

(2.1)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\zeta^{(\mu)}(\rho)\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-\rho)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\nu}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)+O\left(Te^{-C\sqrt{\log T}}\right)

as
T\to\infty
, where
C
is a positive constant and where
\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\nu}(x)
is the polynomial of degree
\mu+\nu+2
given by

(2.2)

\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\nu}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}(-1)^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{k}C_{1}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)x^{m}\\
+\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}(-1)^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{k}\left(C_{1}^{(\nu,\mu)}(m,k)+C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)\right)x^{m},

where

C_{1}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)=\\
\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m}(\nu-k)}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+\nu+1-m}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}+\\
\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{\mu+\nu+2-m}}{(k+m-\nu)!}&m\geq\nu-k\\[12.91663pt]
\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m}(\nu-k)}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+k+2}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}&m\leq\nu-k-1\end{cases}

where
c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}
are the Laurent series coefficients around
s=1
of

(2.3)

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}(s-1)^{-\mu-k-3+j},

and where

C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)=\\
\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m}(\mu-k+1)}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+\nu+1-m}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}+\\
\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{d_{\mu+\nu+2-m}^{(\nu,k)}}{(k+m-\mu-1)!}&m\geq\mu-k+1\\[12.91663pt]
\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m}(\mu-k+1)}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu+k+1}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}&m\leq\mu-k\end{cases}

where
d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}
are the Laurent series coefficients around
s=1
of

\zeta^{(\nu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}(s-1)^{-\nu-k-2+j}.

If one assumes the Riemann Hypothesis, the error term may be replaced with
O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)
for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.

We now state several corollaries of this result, with Corollary
1
clearly following immediately from this theorem and with Corollaries
2
and
3
proved in Section
6
.

Corollary 1
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. For
\nu
a positive integer, the discrete second moment of zeta for all derivatives is given by

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\zeta^{(\nu)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)\right|^{2}\\
=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{2\nu+2}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}(-1)^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{k}\left(2C_{1}^{(\nu,\nu)}(m,k)+C_{2}^{(\nu,\nu)}(m,k)\right)\right)\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{m}+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right),

where
C_{1}^{(\nu,\nu)}(m,k)
and
C_{2}^{(\nu,\nu)}(m,k)
are defined in Theorem
1
.

Corollary 2
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. In the case
\mu=\nu=1
, (
2.1
) recovers the polynomial
P_{4}(x)
in (
1.4
).

Corollary 3
.

For
\mu,\nu
positive integers, the leading order coefficient of
\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\nu}(x)
agrees with the leading order given in (
1.2
).

As we see in Milinovich’s polynomial
P_{4}(x)
in (
1.4
), the coefficients quickly become unwieldy. We give an example of our result for discrete second moment of the second derivative in Appendix
A
by explicitly writing out the polynomial for the second derivative. Finally, in Appendix
B
we demonstrate the theorem by plotting the graphs for the first and second derivatives.

3.
Brief outline of the proof

For
c=1+\frac{1}{\log T}
and
\mathcal{R}
the rectangular contour with vertices
c+i
,
c+iT
,
1-c+iT
, and
1-c+i
, we may use Cauchy’s theorem to write

\displaystyle I(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\zeta^{(\mu)}(\rho)\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{R}}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-s)\ ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}+\int_{c+iT}^{1-c+iT}+\int_{1-c+iT}^{1-c+i}+\int_{1-c+i}^{c+i}\right)\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-s)\ ds

(3.1)

\displaystyle=I_{1}(\mu,\nu)+I_{2}(\mu,\nu)+I_{3}(\mu,\nu)+I_{4}(\mu,\nu).

It is immediate that
I_{4}(\mu,\nu)=O(1)
since the integral has finite length and bounded integrand. Furthermore, as shown by Gonek
[
9
]
, we have
I_{2}(\mu,\nu)=O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)
with the harmless restriction
that
T
lies a distance
\gg 1/\log T
from a zero ordinate
\gamma
. We shall use this restriction without loss of generality throughout our proof. From this it follows that

(3.2)

I(\mu,\nu)=I_{1}(\mu,\nu)+I_{3}(\mu,\nu)+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

In Section
4
we deal with the right-hand vertical segment
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
. The starting point is by introducing a functional equation which allows us to write
\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-s)
in terms of
\zeta^{(k)}(s)
for
k
between
0
and
\nu
. As a consequence we may write
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
as an integral of an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series. We then use the method of stationary phase to rewrite this integral as the sum

\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}\leq\frac{T}{2\pi}}\Lambda(n_{1})(\log n_{2})^{\mu}(\log n_{3})^{k}(\log n_{1}n_{2}n_{3})^{\nu-k},

plus a small error that is subsumed by our error term written above.

We evaluate this sum without the
(\log n_{1}n_{2}n_{3})^{\nu-k}
factor through Perron’s formula. This gives the sum as the residue of a Dirichlet series at
s=1
, which in turn is a polynomial of degree
\mu+\nu+2
. Finally we reinsert the logarithmic term via partial summation.

In Section
5
we handle the left-hand segment
I_{3}(\mu,\nu)
. We use another functional equation to relate the left vertical segment of our integral to the right vertical segment, and then follow similar methods to those used in Section
4
to establish the asymptotics for the left vertical segment. Combining these two contours, together with the error term that we carry through the proof, gives Theorem
1
.

Finally, as mentioned in the introduction, in Section
6
we prove some corollaries of our result.

4.
The right-hand vertical segment

4.1.
Initial manipulations

We start by writing
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
in a form more amenable to analysis. Recall that

I_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(c+it)\zeta^{(\mu)}(c+it)\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-c-it)\ dt.

We apply the functional equation for
\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-c-it)
so we can write the resulting expression in terms of convergent Dirichlet series.

Lemma 1
.

[
14
, Lemma 4]

For
s=\sigma+it
, with
\sigma\geq 1
and
t\geq 1
we have

(4.1)

\zeta^{(\nu)}(1-s)=(-1)^{\nu}\chi(1-s)\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{k}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{\nu-k}\zeta^{(k)}(s)+O\left(t^{\sigma-\frac{3}{2}}(\log t)^{\nu}\right),

where
\chi(s)
is the factor from the functional equation
\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s)
.

Substituting (
4.1
) into our expression for
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
gives

I_{1}(\mu,\nu)\\
=\frac{(-1)^{\nu}}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{k}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(c+it)\zeta^{(\mu)}(c+it)\zeta^{(k)}(c+it)\chi(1-c-it)\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{\nu-k}\ dt\\
+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

Each term in the integrand can be expressed as a Dirichlet series since
\Re(s)=c>1
, namely

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{s}}\text{ and }\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)=(-1)^{\mu}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log n)^{\mu}}{n^{s}},

where
\Lambda(n)
is the von Mangoldt function.

It follows that, by multiplying the Dirichlet series in the integral above together, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\mu+k+1}\left(\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n_{1})}{n_{1}^{s}}\right)\left(\sum_{n_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log n_{2})^{\mu}}{n_{2}^{s}}\right)\left(\sum_{n_{3}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log n_{3})^{k}}{n_{3}^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}}{n^{s}}

where

(4.2)

A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}:=(-1)^{\mu+k+1}\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}=n}\Lambda(n_{1})(\log n_{2})^{\mu}(\log n_{3})^{k}.

The integral
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
can then be written as

I_{1}(\mu,\nu)\\
=\frac{(-1)^{\nu}}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{k}\int_{1}^{T}\chi(1-c-it)\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{\nu-k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}}{n^{c+it}}\right)\ dt+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log T)^{\nu}\right).

We now use a lemma due to Gonek
[
9
, Lemma 5]
which comes from the method of stationary phase which will permit us to write this integral as a sum, which we shall subsequently evaluate via the method of Perron.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complex numbers that satisfy
a_{n}\ll n^{\varepsilon}
for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Let
c=1+\frac{1}{\log T}
, and let
m
be a non-negative integer. Then for
T\geq 1
we have

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\ \chi(1-c-it)\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{m}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{c+it}}\right)\ dt=\sum_{1\leq n\leq\frac{T}{2\pi}}a_{n}(\log n)^{m}+O\left(T^{c-\frac{1}{2}}(\log T)^{m}\right).

Then by Lemma
2
, we can rewrite
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
as the sum

(4.3)

I_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}(-1)^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{k}\sum_{n\leq\frac{T}{2\pi}}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}(\log n)^{\nu-k}+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

4.2.
Evaluating the inner sum without the logarithm

Throughout this section we we set
Y=\frac{T}{2\pi}
for notational convenience.

We now evaluate the sum in (
4.3
). One could use Perron’s formula, relying on the Dirichlet series expansion

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}(\log n)^{\nu-k}}{n^{s}}=(-1)^{\nu-k}\frac{d^{\nu-k}}{ds^{\nu-k}}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\right).

However it turns out to be simpler and more convenient to use Perron’s formula to calculate
\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}
without any logarithms, and then use partial summation to reinsert them later.

We start by using a truncated version of Perron’s formula.

Lemma 3
.

Let
A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}
be given by (
4.2
). For
2\leq V\leq Y
, as
Y\to\infty
,

\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iV}^{c+iV}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\ ds+R_{1}(Y,V),

where

R_{1}(Y,V)=O\left(\frac{Y}{V}(\log Y)^{\mu+k+3}\right).

Proof.

Bounding
R_{1}(Y,V)
is all we need to do to prove the lemma, since the integral comes directly from Perron’s formula (and the truncation gives the error term). By
[
19
, Cor 5.3]
we can bound the remainder as

\displaystyle R_{1}(Y,V)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\frac{Y}{2}<n<2Y}|A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}|\min\left(1,\frac{Y}{V|Y-n|}\right)+\frac{4^{c}+Y^{c}}{V}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{|A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}|}{n^{c}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=A+B,

say. To evaluate the error, we start by giving an upper bound for
A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}
. Note that
\Lambda(n)\leq\log n
with equality holding if and only if
n
is prime. We bound
A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}
crudely by

\left|A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}\right|\leq\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}=n}(\log n)(\log n)^{\mu}(\log n)^{k}=(\log n)^{\mu+k+1}d_{3}(n),

where
d_{3}(n)
is the
3
-fold divisor function. Bounding
A
in (
4.4
) by the maximum of
A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}
and using the notation
\ell=|Y-m|
,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\ll(\log Y)^{\mu+k+1}\sum_{\frac{Y}{2}<m<2Y}d_{3}(m)\min\left(1,\frac{Y}{V\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\ll(\log Y)^{\mu+k+1}\sum_{\ell\leq Y}\frac{Y}{V}\frac{1}{\ell}d_{3}(\ell)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{Y}{V}(\log Y)^{\mu+k+4},

using the fact that
\sum_{\ell\leq Y}\frac{d_{3}(\ell)}{\ell}\ll(\log Y)^{3}
(see, for example,
[
19
, p.43]
).

Next we turn to bounding
B
in (
4.4
). Since
c=1+\frac{1}{\log T}
the Dirichlet series converges, and so

B=\frac{4^{c}+Y^{c}}{V}\left|\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(c)\zeta^{(\mu)}(c)\zeta^{(k)}(c)\right|\ll\frac{Y}{V}(\log Y)^{\mu+k+3},

using the fact that
Y=\frac{T}{2\pi}
and also that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log T}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\log T,

and

(4.6)

\displaystyle\zeta^{(\mu)}\left(1+\frac{1}{\log T}\right)

\displaystyle\ll(\log T)^{\mu+1}

as required.
∎

Next we write the integral in Lemma
3
in terms of the residue of the integrand at
s=1
. We obtain an error term from completing the contour into a rectangle which we will deal with in Lemma
4
. We remark that this lemma is the only place where we get different errors depending on whether we assume the Riemann Hypothesis or not, so we shall consider both cases separately.

Lemma 4
.

For
Y=\frac{T}{2\pi}
, as
V,Y\to\infty
, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iV}^{c+iV}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\ ds=\res_{s=1}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\right)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(Y,V),

where
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu,k;Y)
is given by

(1)

\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu,k;Y)=\left(Ye^{-C\sqrt{\log Y}}\right)
unconditionally for a positive constant
C

(2)

\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu,k;Y)=\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)
under the Riemann Hypothesis.

Proof of Lemma
4
.

We consider the positively oriented rectangular contour with vertices at
c\pm iV,c^{\prime}\pm iV
, where
\frac{1}{2}<c^{\prime}<1
is large enough that no zeros of zeta with
|\gamma|\leq V
lie on or to the right of the
c^{\prime}
line. Therefore the only pole contained within this contour is at
s=1
, and by Cauchy’s Residue Theorem,

\res_{s=1}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\right)\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{c-iV}^{c+iV}+\int_{c+iV}^{c^{\prime}+iV}+\int_{c^{\prime}+iV}^{c^{\prime}-iV}+\int_{c^{\prime}-iV}^{c-iV}\right)\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\ ds.

By rearranging this expression and switching the orientation of some of the integrals we have that the desired integral is equal to

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iV}^{c+iV}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\ ds=\res_{s=1}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{c^{\prime}+iV}^{c+iV}+\int_{c^{\prime}-iV}^{c^{\prime}+iV}-\int_{c^{\prime}-iV}^{c-iV}\right)\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\ ds.

The result will follow from finding appropriate bounds for the three line integrals. We consider the conditional and unconditional cases separately as they involve different values of
c^{\prime}
and different bounds for zeta.

The unconditional case

As noted in Titchmarsh
[
22
, p.54]
, there exists some absolute constant
C>0
such that for
c^{\prime}=1-\frac{C}{\log V}
, any zero of
\zeta(s)
lies a distance
\gg\frac{1}{\log V}
away from the line between
c^{\prime}-iV
and
c^{\prime}+iV
.

Therefore, on the two horizontal pieces we may apply the bound of Gonek
[
9
, §2]
which applies uniformly for
-1\leq\sigma\leq 2

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(\sigma\pm iV)\ll(\log V)^{2}.

We also use the convexity bounds inside and to the right of the critical strip

(4.7)

\zeta^{(\mu)}(\sigma\pm iV)\ll\begin{cases}V^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\sigma)}(\log V)^{\mu+1}&\text{ if $0\leq\sigma\leq 1$}\\
(\log V)^{\mu+1}&\text{ if $\sigma\geq 1$,}\end{cases}

which may be established by using Ivic’s bound for
\nu=1

[
16
]
and applying Cauchy’s estimate for derivatives of analytic functions around a disc of radius
1/\log V
with centre at
\sigma+iV
.

With these bounds we deduce that

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c^{\prime}\pm iV}^{c\pm iV}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\ d\sigma\ll(\log V)^{\mu+k+4}\frac{Y^{c}}{V}(c-c^{\prime})\ll\frac{Y}{V}(\log V)^{\mu+k+3},

where we have used the fact that
c-c^{\prime}\ll\frac{1}{\log V}
and
Y^{c}=\ll Y
.

Now we turn to the vertical segment of the contour. Away from the pole, we can use the same bounds as above, and for
t\approx 0
we bound the terms in the integral by the appropriate Laurent expansions, (
4.5
) and (
4.6
). Therefore on the line
s=c^{\prime}+it
,
-V\leq t\leq V
, we can bound the integral by

Y^{c^{\prime}}(\log V)^{\mu+k+4}+\int_{1}^{V}(\log V)^{\mu+k+4}\frac{Y^{c^{\prime}}}{t}\ dt\ll Y^{c^{\prime}}(\log V)^{\mu+k+5}.

Since
Y^{c^{\prime}}=Y\exp\left(-\frac{C\log Y}{\log V}\right)
this completes the proof of the unconditional case.

The conditional case

Under the assumption of the Riemann Hypothesis, we can both shift the line of integration further to the left without crossing any zeros, and also use better (conditional) bounds on zeta and its derivatives. Specifically we shift the contour to just to the right of the critical line, letting
c^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log V}
, and we use
\zeta^{(n)}(s)\ll t^{\varepsilon}
for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.

At any point on the vertical segment the closest zero is
\geq\frac{1}{\log V}
away, so we have
\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\ll(\log V)^{2}
and so the vertical integral is bounded by

Y^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(\frac{\log Y}{\log V}\right)V^{\varepsilon}

for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
.

The horizontal segments are dealt with similarly to before, only this time the length of integration is bounded and we will bound terms like
\log V
by
V^{\varepsilon}
since that’s sufficient for our purposes. The two horizontal pieces are bounded by

\frac{Y}{V}V^{\varepsilon}

and this completes the proof in the conditional case.
∎

Completion of the proof of Lemma
4

Using Lemma
3
, thus far in Lemma
4
we have shown that

\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}=\res_{s=1}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\right)+R_{1}(Y,V)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(V,Y).

where we now pick an optimal
V
in terms of
Y
, depending on whether we are bounding the error terms conditionally or unconditionally.

In the unconditional case, we can choose
V=\exp(\sqrt{C\log Y})
to optimise the error terms. This then gives an error term of

R_{1}(Y,V)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(V,Y)\ll Ye^{-\sqrt{C\log Y}}(\log Y)^{\frac{\mu+k+5}{2}}\ll Y\exp(-\tilde{C}\sqrt{\log Y})

for some
\tilde{C}>0
.

In the conditional case, we take
V=Y
and find

R_{1}(Y,V)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(V,Y)\ll Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},

for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, as stated in Lemma
4
.

We now compute the residue term. In the following lemma we will write the residue in terms of the Laurent coefficients of our triple Dirichlet series given in (
4.2
) around
s=1
.

Lemma 5
.

We have

(4.8)

\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\right)=Y\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+k+2}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}(\log Y)^{\mu+k+2-j},

where
c^{\mu,k}_{j}
are the Laurent series coefficients around
s=1
of

(4.9)

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}(s-1)^{-\mu-k-3+j}.

Proof.

The function
\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{1}{s}
has a pole of order
\mu+k+3
at
s=1
. We write this expansion as the series given in (
4.9
).

The expansion of
Y^{s}
about
s=1
is

Y^{s}=Y\left(1+(s-1)\log Y+\frac{(s-1)^{2}}{2!}(\log Y)^{2}+\cdots+\frac{(s-1)^{k}}{k!}(\log Y)^{k}+\cdots\right).

and so the residue of
\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\zeta^{(\mu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}
is given by the sum of

\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}(\log Y)^{\mu+k+2-j}

for each
j=0,\dots,\mu+k+2
.
∎

4.3.
Reinserting the logarithm in the inner sum

Throughout this section we we set
Y=\frac{T}{2\pi}
for notational convenience.

Lemma 6
.

For
0\leq k\leq\nu
, we have that

\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}(\log n)^{\nu-k}=Y\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}C_{1}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)(\log Y)^{m}+O\left(Ye^{-C\sqrt{\log Y}}\right),

where for
m\geq\nu-k
,

C_{1}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)=\frac{(-1)^{m}(\nu-k)}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+\nu+1-m}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}+\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{\mu+\nu+2-m}}{(k+m-\nu)!}

and for
m<\nu-k

C_{1}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)=\frac{(-1)^{m}(\nu-k)}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+k+2}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}

Remark
.

The differences in the two expressions for
C_{1}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)
are the extra term for larger
m
, and the different upper limit on the
j
-sums. Those two upper limits are the same when
m=\nu-k-1
. Finally, when
m=\mu+\nu+2
, which is the largest it can be, the
j
-sum is empty.

Proof.

We have shown using Lemmas
3
,
4
and
5
that

\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}=Y\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+k+2}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}(\log Y)^{\mu+k+2-j}+O\left(Ye^{-C\sqrt{\log Y}}\right).

By partial summation we have

(4.10)

\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}(\log n)^{\nu-k}=\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}\right)(\log Y)^{\nu-k}-(\nu-k)\int_{1}^{Y}\left(\sum_{n\leq t}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}\right)\frac{(\log t)^{\nu-k-1}}{t}\ dt.

Clearly

\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}\right)(\log Y)^{\nu-k}=Y\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+k+2}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}(\log Y)^{\mu+\nu+2-j}\\
+O\left((\log Y)^{\nu-k}Ye^{-C\sqrt{\log Y}}\right),

where
C>0
isn’t necessarily the same constant throughout. Relabeling the sum so
m=\mu+\nu+2-j
(to help make the power of
\log Y
clear), the first piece in (
4.10
) is

(4.11)

\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}(\log Y)^{\nu-k}=Y\sum_{m=\nu-k}^{\mu+\nu+2}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{\mu+\nu+2-m}}{(m+k-\nu)!}(\log Y)^{m}+O\left(Ye^{-C\sqrt{\log Y}}\right).

Next, note that the second term in (
4.10
) is

(4.12)

(\nu-k)\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+k+2}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}\int_{1}^{Y}(\log t)^{\mu+\nu+1-j}\ dt+O\left(Ye^{-C\sqrt{\log Y}}\right).

Note that, by repeated integration by parts,

(4.13)

\int_{1}^{Y}(\log t)^{n}\ dt=(-1)^{n}n!Y\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log Y)^{m}}{m!}+O(1),

so using (
4.13
) with
n=\mu+\nu+1-j
we have that

\int_{1}^{Y}\left(\sum_{n\leq t}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}\right)\frac{(\log t)^{\nu-k-1}}{t}\ dt\\
=\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+k+2}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}(-1)^{\mu+\nu+1-j}(\mu+\nu+1-j)!Y\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+1-j}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log Y)^{m}}{m!}\\
+O\left(Ye^{-C\sqrt{\log Y}}\right).

Next swap the order of summation in the double sum to give

(4.14)

Y\sum_{m=0}^{\nu-k-1}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log Y)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+k+2}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}(-1)^{\mu+\nu+1-j}(\mu+\nu+1-j)!\\
+Y\sum_{m=\nu-k}^{\mu+\nu+1}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log Y)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+\nu+1-m}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}(-1)^{\mu+\nu+1-j}(\mu+\nu+1-j)!

Combining the pieces (
4.11
), (
4.12
), and (
4.14
) gives

(4.15)

\sum_{n\leq Y}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}(\log n)^{\nu-k}=Y\sum_{m=\nu-k}^{\mu+\nu+2}\frac{c^{(\mu,k)}_{\mu+\nu+2-m}}{(m+k-\nu)!}(\log Y)^{m}\\
+(\nu-k)Y\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+1}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log Y)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{\mu+\nu+1-m\,,\,\mu+k+2\}}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\mu+k+2-j)!}c^{(\mu,k)}_{j}\\
+O\left(Ye^{-C\sqrt{\log Y}}\right)

for some
C>0
, as required.
∎

Finally, since
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
is equal to

\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}(-1)^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{k}\sum_{n\leq\frac{T}{2\pi}}A_{n}^{(\mu,k)}(\log n)^{\nu-k}+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right),

we have by Lemma
6
that

(4.16)

I_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu}(-1)^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{k}C_{1}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{m}+O\left(Te^{-C\sqrt{\log T}}\right)

with the
C_{1}^{\mu,\nu}(m,k)
given in the statement of Lemma
6
.

5.
The left-hand vertical segment

The evaluation of the left-hand segment
I_{3}(\mu,\nu)
follows closely that that of
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
, so in this section we give the overview and highlight where the differences between the two calculations lie.

5.1.
Initial manipulations

We start by writing
I_{3}(\mu,\nu)
, given in (
3.1
), in a form which relates
I_{3}(\mu,\nu)
to
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
. We have

I_{3}(\mu,\nu)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(1-c+it)\zeta^{(\mu)}(1-c+it)\zeta^{(\nu)}(c-it)\ dt.

By
[
9
, Lemma 6]
we have (where
t\neq 0
)

(5.1)

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(1-s)=-\log\left(\frac{|t|}{2\pi}\right)-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)+O\left(\frac{1}{1+|t|}\right).

so

\displaystyle I_{3}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\left(\log\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)+\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(c-it)+O\left(\frac{1}{1+|t|}\right)\right)\zeta^{(\mu)}(1-c+it)\zeta^{(\nu)}(c-it)\ dt

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\overline{J(\mu,\nu)}+\overline{I_{1}(\nu,\mu)}+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right),

where the overline denotes complex conjugate, and where we write for
s=c+it

J(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{1}^{T}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)\zeta^{(\mu)}(1-s)\zeta^{(\nu)}(s)\ dt.

We have already evaluated
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
(although note that
\mu
and
\nu
are switched here). It therefore only remains to compute
J(\mu,\nu)
. This follows a similar approach, the difference being only minor technical details (in particular, we have a logarithm term in our integrand in place of the logarithmic derivative of zeta in
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
).

Remark
.

We will evaluate
J(\mu,\nu)
explicitly in terms of the Stieltjes coefficients to be consistent with our approach for
I_{1}(\mu,\nu)
. However, an alternative approach would be to adapt Ingham’s approach
[
15
]
, which yields

J(\mu,\nu)=\int_{1}^{T}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)\left.\frac{\partial^{\mu+\nu}}{\partial\alpha^{\mu+\nu}}\left(\zeta(1+\alpha)+\left(\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{-\alpha}\zeta(1-\alpha)\right)\right|_{\alpha=0}dt+O\left(T^{1/2+\epsilon}\right).

5.2.
Initial Manipulations of
J(\mu,\nu)

We start by using Lemma
1
on
\zeta^{(\mu)}(1-s)
to write
J(\mu,\nu)
as

\frac{(-1)^{\mu}}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{k}\int_{1}^{T}\zeta^{(\nu)}(c+it)\zeta^{(k)}(c+it)\chi(1-c-it)\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{\mu-k+1}\ dt+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

Since we are in the region
\Re(s)=c>1
we can make use of the Dirichlet series representations of these terms, namely

\zeta^{(\nu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}}{n^{s}}

where

B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}=(-1)^{\nu+k}\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}=n}(\log n_{1})^{\nu}(\log n_{2})^{k}

so that

J(\mu,\nu)=\frac{(-1)^{\mu}}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{k}\int_{1}^{T}\chi(1-c-it)\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{\mu-k+1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}}{n^{c+it}}\ dt+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

Next, by applying Lemma
2
we have

(5.3)

J(\mu,\nu)=(-1)^{\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{k}\sum_{n\leq\frac{T}{2\pi}}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}(\log n)^{\mu-k+1}+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

5.3.
Evaluating the inner sum

Throughout this section we we set
Y=\frac{T}{2\pi}
for notational convenience.

We have the following lemmas, closely resembling Lemmas
3
and
4
. The key difference here is that we don’t have a
\zeta^{\prime}/\zeta(s)
term (or, equivalently, we don’t need to perform a sum over primes), meaning we don’t need to split the error term depending on whether we assume the Riemann Hypothesis or not, as we do in Lemma
4
.

Lemma 7
.

As
Y\to\infty
we have

(5.4)

\sum_{n\leq Y}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}=\res_{s=1}\left(\zeta^{(\nu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\right)+O\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

Proof.

As in Lemma
3
we have for
2\leq V\leq Y
, as
Y\to\infty
we have

\sum_{n\leq Y}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iV}^{c+iV}\zeta^{(\nu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}ds+O\left(\frac{Y}{V}(\log Y)^{\nu+k+2}\right).

Similar to Lemma
4
, we shift the contour to the vertical line
c^{\prime}=1/2
, noting that the integrand has no singularities other than at
s=1
. On the two horizontal pieces
\sigma\pm iV
we use the unconditional convexity bounds for
\zeta^{(\mu)}(\sigma+iV)
given in (
4.7
), giving an error of size

\int_{1/2}^{1}V^{(1-\sigma)}(\log V)^{\mu+\nu+2}\frac{Y^{\sigma}}{V}d\sigma\ll\frac{Y}{V}V^{\epsilon}.

The vertical contour on the line
c^{\prime}=1/2
is bounded by

\ll\int_{1}^{V}\left|\zeta^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\right|\left|\zeta^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\right|\frac{Y^{1/2}}{t}dt\\
\ll Y^{1/2}\left(\int_{1}^{V}\frac{\left|\zeta^{(\mu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\right|^{2}}{t}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{1}^{V}\frac{\left|\zeta^{(\nu)}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\right|^{2}}{t}\right)^{1/2}\ll Y^{1/2}V^{\epsilon}

the last inequality following from Ingham’s
[
15
]
evaluation of the second moment of derivatives of zeta on the critical line. Picking any
V
satisfying
\sqrt{Y}\leq V\leq Y
produces the desired error, and proves the lemma.
∎

Evaluating the residue in (
5.4
) can be done in a similar way to Lemma
5
.

Lemma 8
.

We have

\res_{s=1}\left(\zeta^{(\nu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{Y^{s}}{s}\right)=Y\sum_{j=0}^{\nu+k+1}\frac{d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}(\log Y)^{\nu+k+1-j}

where
d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}
are the Laurent series coefficients around
s=1
of

\zeta^{(\nu)}(s)\zeta^{(k)}(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{j\geq 0}d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}(s-1)^{-\nu-k-2+j}.

By combining Lemma
7
and
8
we have

\sum_{n\leq Y}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}=Y\sum_{j=0}^{\nu+k+1}\frac{d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}(\log Y)^{\nu+k+1-j}+O\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

The next lemma is analogous to what was proved in Lemma
6
, where we reinsert the logarithm in the sum above.

Lemma 9
.

For
k=0,1,\dots,\mu
we have that

\sum_{n\leq Y}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}(\log n)^{\mu-k+1}=Y\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)(\log Y)^{m}+O\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right),

where for
m\geq\mu-k+1
,

C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)=\frac{(-1)^{m}(\mu-k+1)}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+\nu+1-m}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}\\
+\frac{d_{\mu+\nu+2-m}^{(\nu,k)}}{(k+m-\mu-1)!}

and for
m\leq\mu-k
,

C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)=\frac{(-1)^{m}(\mu-k+1)}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu+k+1}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}

Remark
.

In the case when
m=\mu+\nu+2
(which is the largest
m
can be), the sum in
C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)
is empty.

Proof.

As with the right hand vertical segment in Lemma
6
, we use partial summation to reinsert the logarithmic term.
By the partial summation formula we have

\sum_{n\leq Y}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}(\log n)^{\mu-k+1}=\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}\right)(\log Y)^{\mu-k+1}\\
-(\mu-k+1)\int_{1}^{Y}\left(\sum_{n\leq t}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}\right)\frac{(\log t)^{\mu-k}}{t}\ dt.

The first term is

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\leq Y}B_{n}^{(\nu,k)}\right)(\log Y)^{\mu-k+1}

\displaystyle=Y\sum_{j=0}^{\nu+k+1}\frac{d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}(\log Y)^{\mu+\nu+2-j}+O\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=Y\sum_{m=\mu-k+1}^{\mu+\nu+2}\frac{d_{\mu+\nu+2-m}^{(\nu,k)}}{(m+k-\mu-1)!}(\log Y)^{m}+O\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)

and the second is equal to

-(\mu-k+1)\sum_{j=0}^{\nu+k+1}\frac{d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}\int_{1}^{Y}(\log t)^{\mu+\nu+1-j}dt+O\left(Y^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)

and using (
4.13
), this evaluates to

-(\mu-k+1)\sum_{j=0}^{\nu+k+1}\frac{d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}(-1)^{\mu+\nu+1-j}(\mu+\nu+1-j)!Y\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+1-j}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log Y)^{m}}{m!}

Swapping the order of summation, the double sum is

(\mu-k+1)Y\sum_{m=0}^{\mu-k}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log Y)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu+k+1}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}\\
+(\mu-k+1)Y\sum_{m=\mu-k+1}^{\mu+\nu+1}\frac{(-1)^{m}(\log Y)^{m}}{m!}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu+\nu+1-m}(-1)^{\mu+\nu-j}\frac{(\mu+\nu+1-j)!}{(\nu+k+1-j)!}d_{j}^{(\nu,k)}.

Combining the two pieces gives the formula for
C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)
and completes the proof.
∎

Applying Lemma
9
to (
5.3

(5.5)

J(\mu,\nu)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}(-1)^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{k}C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{m}+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

Switching the role of
\mu
and
\nu
in (
4.16
), we have that

(5.6)

I_{1}(\nu,\mu)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}(-1)^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{k}C_{1}^{(\nu,\mu)}(m,k)\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{m}+O\left(Te^{-C\sqrt{\log T}}\right)

so combining
\overline{J(\mu,\nu)}
and
\overline{I_{1}(\nu,\mu)}
in (

) gives

(5.7)

I_{3}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}(-1)^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{k}\left(C_{1}^{(\nu,\mu)}(m,k)+C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)\right)\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{m}\\
+O\left(Te^{-C\sqrt{\log T}}\right)

+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu+\nu+2}\sum_{k=0}^{\mu}(-1)^{\mu}\binom{\mu}{k}\left(C_{1}^{(\nu,\mu)}(m,k)+C_{2}^{(\mu,\nu)}(m,k)\right)\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{m}+O\left(Te^{-C\sqrt{\log T}}\right),

.

Proof of the Corollaries

As already noted, Corollary
1
follows immediately from Theorem
1
.

Proof of Corollary
2
.

We show that our theorem with
\mu=\nu=1
recovers precisely Milinovich’s full asymptotic expansion for the first derivative, stated in (
1.4
).

Theorem
1
states that, under the Riemann Hypothesis,

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\left|\zeta^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)\right|^{2}\\
=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^{4}(-2C_{1}^{(1,1)}(m,0)-C_{2}^{(1,1)}(m,0)-2C_{1}^{(1,1)}(m,1)-C_{2}^{(1,1)}(m,1))\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{m}\\
+O\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right).

We now calculate the various terms in this expansion. We have

C_{1}^{(1,1)}(m,0)=\begin{cases}c^{(1,0)}_{0}-c^{(1,0)}_{1}+c^{(1,0)}_{2}-c^{(1,0)}_{3}&\text{ if }m=0\\[6.45831pt]
-c^{(1,0)}_{0}+c^{(1,0)}_{1}-c^{(1,0)}_{2}+c^{(1,0)}_{3}&\text{ if }m=1\\[6.45831pt]
\frac{1}{2}c^{(1,0)}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}c^{(1,0)}_{1}+c^{(1,0)}_{2}&\text{ if }m=2\\[6.45831pt]
-\frac{1}{6}c^{(1,0)}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}c^{(1,0)}_{1}&\text{ if }m=3\\[6.45831pt]
\frac{1}{6}c^{(1,0)}_{0}&\text{ if }m=4\end{cases}

where
c^{(1,0)}_{j}
are the coefficients of

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{s}=\frac{c^{(1,0)}_{0}}{(s-1)^{4}}+\frac{c^{(1,0)}_{1}}{(s-1)^{3}}+\frac{c^{(1,0)}_{2}}{(s-1)^{2}}+\frac{c^{(1,0)}_{3}}{(s-1)}+\dots

and where

C_{2}^{(1,1)}(m,0)=\begin{cases}6d^{(1,0)}_{0}-4d^{(1,0)}_{1}+2d^{(1,0)}_{2}&\text{ if }m=0\\[6.45831pt]
-6d^{(1,0)}_{0}+4d^{(1,0)}_{1}-2d^{(1,0)}_{2}&\text{ if }m=1\\[6.45831pt]
3d^{(1,0)}_{0}-2d^{(1,0)}_{1}+d^{(1,0)}_{2}&\text{ if }m=2\\[6.45831pt]
-d^{(1,0)}_{0}+d^{(1,0)}_{1}&\text{ if }m=3\\[6.45831pt]
\frac{1}{2}d^{(1,0)}_{0}&\text{ if }m=4\end{cases}

where
d^{(1,0)}_{j}
are the coefficients of

Proof of Corollary

.

By explicitly evaluating the highest-order term in the polynomial in Theorem

), that is,

See the small lesions: Frequency-guided spatial debiasing GAN for multimodal medical image fusion
Authors: Tao Zhou, Jiaqi Wang, Huiling Lu, Mingzhe Zhang, Zhe Zhang, Hongqiang Yu, Pengfei Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryMultimodal medical image fusion uses these features from different modalities to generate fused images with richer information for diagnosis. However, in medical images, the lesion area only constitutes a small portion, and feature extraction is dominated by background regions, which weakens lesion representation and leads to pixel-level unfairness. To address this issue, this paper proposes frequency-guided spatial debiasing GAN (FGSD-GAN). The proposed method introduces a frequency-guided debiasing framework with a spatial debiasing branch, a frequency-domain debiasing branch, and a debiasing loss function. The frequency-domain branch enhances structural and contour information through subband decomposition and adaptive fusion, while the spatial branch strengthens lesion-related features and suppresses redundant background information. In addition, the debiasing loss further improves the balance between lesion enhancement and structure preservation. Experiments on the CT lung window, CT mediastinal window, and Whole Brain Atlas datasets demonstrate that FGSD-GAN achieves effective and robust fusion results across different medical imaging scenarios.Subject areas: Biocomputational method, Medical imaging

Introduction

Medical image fusion is a key problem in multimodal medical image research. Complementary information from different medical images is fully integrated through image fusion. A more comprehensive and accurate reference is thus provided for clinical diagnosis and treatment. For example, PET and CT images. PET images reveal the metabolic and functional characteristics of lesion regions, but their spatial resolution is limited. CT images contain rich anatomical structure and texture information, but the ability to present changes in lesion activity is limited. A single modality is often difficult to balance functional characteristics and structural representation. Lesion features are highlighted through multimodal image fusion. Anatomical details are maintained during this process. Complementary information is thus realized. More importantly, medical image fusion not only enhances lesion features but also provides an effective way to eliminate bias. The pixel proportion of the lesion region in the entire image is extremely small. During training, the model is dominated by background information, , along with the background information of medical images, accounts for a large proportion. Resulting in pixel-level bias and insensitivity to lesion features. DesTrans is a deep fusion framework proposed by Song et al.1 Built on transformer and dense connections, it preserves edges and boosts detail and contrast, enabling more complete lesion depiction in fused images. Meanwhile, Zhang et al.2 propose StrFilter, a novel multi-modal medical image fusion framework that leverages structured state-space Mamba and Gromov-Wasserstein metric to adaptively filter redundant features and align distributional differences across modalities, thereby enhancing organ boundaries and visual consistency while mitigating background-dominance bias. Recently, Diwakar et al.3 proposed a multi-modal medical image fusion framework using co-occurrence filtering and local extrema in the NSST domain, which helps preserve edges, textures, and contrast through multi-scale directional decomposition. Zhou et al.4 further introduced a wavelet-attention-based method, which combines wavelet-domain representation with attention mechanisms to improve frequency-feature extraction and cross-modal information interaction. Through cross-modal information interaction and enhancement, image fusion achieves a balance between global structure and local lesions. The bias caused by uneven pixel distribution is effectively reduced. It is very important for building a fair and reliable medical artificial intelligence system.

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are widely applied in multimodal medical image fusion. Das et al.5 proposed an end-to-end content-aware GAN for multimodal medical image fusion, which improves the fusion process by adaptively preserving complementary information from different medical modalities in a unified fused image. Zhou et al.6 proposed UCP2-ACGAN, which introduces U-shaped context perception and multi-granularity feature interaction to better highlight lesion-related and structural information. Zhang et al.7 further proposed a parallel frequency-invertible adversarial network, which processes decomposed frequency subbands in parallel to better balance global structures and fine-grained textures. Wang et al.8 proposed an interactive compensatory attention adversarial learning framework to strengthen cross-modal feature interaction and improve the complementary use of different modalities. In summary, GAN achieves multimodal medical image fusion through the “generator-discriminator” game, and by increasing the proportion of lesions and contrast, it realizes pixel-level debiasing.

At present, spatial domain fusion and frequency domain fusion are two important technical paths for multimodal medical image fusion. In the spatial domain fusion, MFIFusion9 by Dong et al. adopts a decomposition, multi-level feature injection, and reconstruction pipeline that differentiates shallow/deep features and fuses high-frequency subbands during reconstruction to suppress noise, enhance edges/textures, and improve object saliency and detail fidelity. In medical scenarios, MACTFusion10 dynamically selects and enhances lesion local tokens via cross-axis attention and spatially adaptive fusion, achieving pixel-level cross-modal emphasis. In the frequency domain fusion, Zhang et al. propose SFFT,11 modeling spatial and frequency information in parallel with mutual interaction and adaptively reweighting high/low-frequency components at the pixel level to enhance lesion details while maintaining structural consistency. Wang et al.12 propose an efficient frequency-domain fusion network that uses subbands as lightweight carriers, emphasizing band-level high-frequency textures and boundaries to boost contrast and the visibility/proportion of small lesions. The problem, “low proportion of lesion pixels, high proportion of background pixels,” is not well solved. This fusion strategy leads to an increasingly serious phenomenon where the features of small lesions are continuously weakened during the feature extraction process. The problem of debiasing still exists.

Aiming at the pixel-level unfairness problem, “low proportion of lesion pixels, high proportion of background pixels,” resulting in the features of small objects, such as lesions, being continuously weakened during the feature extraction process. The paper proposes FGSD-GAN, a frequency-domain guided spatial debiasing GAN network for medical image fusion. The main contributions are as follows.

1The paper proposes a frequency-domain guided debiasing GAN fusion framework: A frequency-domain guided debiasing GAN network is designed with a single generator and dual discriminators. There are a spatial debiasing module and a frequency-domain debiasing module in the model. These modules realize cross-domain alignment and complementarity.

The paper proposes a frequency-domain guided debiasing GAN fusion framework: A frequency-domain guided debiasing GAN network is designed with a single generator and dual discriminators. There are a spatial debiasing module and a frequency-domain debiasing module in the model. These modules realize cross-domain alignment and complementarity.

2Frequency domain debiasing module: This module applies NSCT to PET and CT images, yielding a low-frequency subband and eight directional high-frequency subbands. The low-frequency subbands adopt an AG adaptive weighting fusion strategy to retain the global contour and structural information. In the high-frequency subbands, a regional-energy maximization strategy to enhance the edge and texture detail features of the lesion, and suppress background redundant information and noise interference.

Frequency domain debiasing module: This module applies NSCT to PET and CT images, yielding a low-frequency subband and eight directional high-frequency subbands. The low-frequency subbands adopt an AG adaptive weighting fusion strategy to retain the global contour and structural information. In the high-frequency subbands, a regional-energy maximization strategy to enhance the edge and texture detail features of the lesion, and suppress background redundant information and noise interference.

3Spatial domain debiasing module: ViT performs cross-modal alignment and fusion between PET and CT. The cosine similarity between the class token and the image token is calculated for token selection. An adaptive weighting strategy is adopted to enhance the selected token, thereby achieving local highlighting of the lesion and suppression of background information.

Spatial domain debiasing module: ViT performs cross-modal alignment and fusion between PET and CT. The cosine similarity between the class token and the image token is calculated for token selection. An adaptive weighting strategy is adopted to enhance the selected token, thereby achieving local highlighting of the lesion and suppression of background information.

4Debiasing loss function: A debiasing loss function is designed. It consists of three components: lesion reweighted loss, background reweighted loss, and gradient loss. This loss addresses the lesion background pixel imbalance. It amplifies gradients in key regions to achieve a balance between the expression of lesion features and the preservation of structural details.

This loss addresses the lesion background pixel imbalance. It amplifies gradients in key regions to achieve a balance between the expression of lesion features and the preservation of structural details.

Results

Datasets

The dataset is derived from 95 patients with lung-tumor who undergo whole-body PET/CT examinations at a grade A tertiary hospital in Ningxia from January 2018 to June 2020, including 46 females (48%, 30–80 years, mean 54 years) and 49 males (52%, 27–74 years, mean 51 years). All scans follow the standard PET/CT protocol. Whole-body and lung PET/CT images are collected and preprocessed via rotation, mirroring, augmentation, and segmentation, yielding 2575 slices for each modality (PET/CT, PET, CT). Image validity is confirmed by a joint review of three clinical experts.

For model training and testing, 1000 PET images and 1000 CT images are selected for training, and 500 images are used for testing. All images are converted to JPG format and resized to 356 × 356 pixels.

Generalization is further evaluated on the public Whole Brain Atlas dataset. A total of 286 PET-MRI image pairs are used, with 269 pairs selected for training and 15 pairs for testing from slices 1–23 along the horizontal and coronal axes. During training, 90° and 180° rotations are applied to expand the training data to 806 pairs, while testing is performed without augmentation using entire images as inputs.

Comparison methods

To verify the effectiveness of the FGSD_GAN algorithm, three sets of methods are adopted for comparison in this paper. The first group consists of deep learning-based fusion methods that extract and enhance multimodal complementary information: Method 1: Res2Fusion.13 Method 2: MURF.14 Method 3: MUFusion.15 Method 4: MMIF-INet.16 The second group includes image fusion methods based on decomposition transformation: Method 5: LRD.17 Method 6: image fusion method based on LatLRR.18 The third group consists of deep learning-based fusion methods that combine attention mechanisms and feature separation: Method 7: EMMA.19 Method 8: DATFuse.20 Method 9: CDDFuse.21 To ensure the fairness of the comparison, all parameter values of the above methods are set to the default values specified by the original authors.

Comparison experiment of fusion results between CT lung window images and PET images

Quantitative comparison: As shown in Table 2 and Figure 3, the proposed method achieves performance comparable to LatLRR, MMIF-INet, and CDDFuse in terms of SSIM, SCD, and QAB/F, respectively. However, it clearly outperforms all competing methods on IE, AG, SD, and SF. Notably, the AG metric shows an average improvement of 46.42% over the best competing result and 234.34% over the worst. For IE, SD, and SF, the average improvements over the best competing methods are 11.01%, 15.62%, and 39.87%, respectively. These higher metric values indicate richer information content, stronger contrast, and sharper lesion details, which is consistent with the clearer lesion boundaries and anatomical structures observed in the fused images. Overall, both quantitative and qualitative results verify the effectiveness of the proposed method for CT and PET lung image fusion.

Qualitative comparison: Figure 4 shows that LRD, LatLRR, and MMIF-INet produce relatively clear fused images, but the contrast between lesion and background regions remains low, so lesion boundaries are indistinct. LRD and MMIF-INet yield overly bright results with weak textures, while LatLRR outputs low contrast degree images with inconspicuous lesions. DATFuse, MUFusion, and Res2Fusion generate overexposed images with poor visual quality, which reduces detail visibility and complicates lesion localization. EMMA and MURF produce visually clear images, yet gradient information is insufficient, leading to blurred lesion edges and bone contours from the CT source images. CDDFuse preserves CT edge and texture information relatively well, but the lesion background contrast degree is still limited. In contrast, FGSD-GAN produces fused images that retain CT edge and contour information and highlight lesion metabolism in PET, resulting in clearer lesion presentation and more distinct anatomical structures.

Hyperparameter analysis of the debiasing loss

In the “debiasing loss function,” the proposed debiasing loss (Equation 14) consists of three weighted components: the lesion reweighting loss LPET, the background reweighting loss LCT, and the gradient loss Lgrad, with corresponding hyperparameters λPET, λCT, and λgrad, respectively. These weights jointly control the trade-off between enhancing small lesion regions and preserving anatomical background structures. To determine an optimal coefficient combination and to clarify the contribution of each weight to lesion enhancement and structural preservation, we conduct a systematic hyperparameter ablation study on the CT lung-window validation set (100 image pairs). For each candidate combination, FGSD-GAN is retrained from scratch while keeping all other settings unchanged, and the fused images are evaluated using seven objective metrics.

To efficiently explore the hyperparameter space under a manageable computational budget while capturing the interactions among the three coefficients, we select a representative set of combinations based on prior knowledge and preliminary experiments. Specifically, the primary candidate values for each weight are set to {0.5,1.0,1.5}, and an additional extreme value 2.0 is introduced to examine potential saturation or over-enhancement effects. In total, 12 combinations are evaluated, as summarized in Table 9. For each combination, the model is retrained on the CT lung-window validation set, and the corresponding evaluation metrics are computed. To reduce randomness, each experiment is repeated three times, and the averaged results are reported as the final quantitative performance for that configuration.

Discussion

The paper focuses on the problem that small objects are weakened in the process of feature extraction and optimization caused by “low proportion of lesion pixels, high proportion of background pixels” in multimodal medical image fusion, and constructs a frequency-guided spatial debiasing network (FGSD-GAN). In this network, a frequency domain debiasing module, a spatial debiasing module, a FGFM, and a debiasing loss function are designed in the framework of a single generator and dual discriminator. The network alleviates the problem of pixel-level unfairness from both the frequency domain and the spatial domain levels, and the fusion result retains the lesion clarity degree and the anatomical structure features.

To verify the effectiveness of FGSD-GAN, comparison experiments and ablation experiments are conducted on 3 datasets: CT lung window, CT mediastinal window, and Whole Brain Atlas. The experiment results show that, comparing with the second-best comparing experiment, on the CT lung window dataset, the evaluation indicators of IE, AG, SD, SF, SCD, QAB/F and SSIM increased by 11.01%, 46.42%, 15.62%, 39.87%, 1.49%, 6.47%, and 1.87%, respectively. On the CT mediastinal window dataset, the evaluation metrics of IE, AG, SD, SF, SCD, QAB/F, and SSIM increased by 6.14%, 50.09%, 26.64%, 49.71%, 3.38%, 16.76%, and 10.08%, respectively. On the Whole Brain Atlas dataset, the evaluation metrics of IE, AG, SD, SF, SCD, QAB/F, and SSIM increased by 0.37%, 27.89%, 5.15%, 31.14%, 4.54%, 5.68%, and 4.58%, respectively. Overall, the improvement of IE and SD indicates that the contrast degree of the fused image is enhanced, and the difference in gray values between the lesion region and the background region is increased. The improvement of AG and SF indicates that the edge and texture features of the lesion are strengthened, and the effective “weight” of the lesion pixels in the entire image is amplified. The increase of SCD and QAB/F indicates that the lesion region and edge structure are prominent. Meanwhile, the SSIM is improved on the 3 sets of datasets, indicating that while enhancing the contrast degree of the lesions, the skeletal contour features and anatomical structure features are retained. From a subjective visual perspective, the contrast degree of the fusion results in the lesion region is enhanced, and the paper achieves the goal of “see the unclear, see the unseen.” Ablation experiments show that the frequency domain debiasing module, the spatial domain debiasing module, and the debiasing loss function contributed to improving lesion significance and inhibiting background dominance.

Limitations of the study

Although FGSD-GAN realizes the alleviation of pixel-level unfairness. However, in practical applications, there are 3 aspects that need to be studied. Firstly, in the frequency-domain fusion part, it is expanded from single-layer NSCT to multi-layer NSCT. The approach utilizes richer directionality and translational invariance to better extract the features of lesions at different scales. Secondly, in the spatial domain fusion part, we address the issue of fixed ViT patch sizes. The model adopts an adaptive patch partitioning strategy and a hybrid structure of a lightweight transformer and a convolution-transformer to balance the enhancement of small lesions and computational costs. Thirdly, in the adversarial learning section. Based on the existing dual discriminators, the model explores cross-modal joint discrimination and intermodal contrast constraints, enhancing the saliency of lesions while suppressing artifacts, providing a new method for multimodal medical image fusion.

Method details

Network architecture

About “low proportion of lesion pixels, high proportion of background pixels” in multimodal medical image fusion, it causes continuous weakening of small objects during feature extraction. The paper proposes the FGSD-GAN network, as shown in Figure 15. Figure 15 consists of 4 parts. Figure 15A is the spatial domain debiasing module in the generator, Figure 15B is the frequency domain debiasing module, Figure 15C is the frequency domain guided fusion module, and Figure 15D is the discriminator. Firstly, the spatial debiasing module is designed in the generator. Cross-modal alignment and fusion of PET and CT images are performed through ViT. The cosine similarity between the class token and the image token is calculated for token selection. The selected tokens are enhanced by adopting an adaptive weighting strategy to obtain the spatial domain feature Fspa. Secondly, the paper designs a frequency-domain debiasing module. The PET and CT images are decomposed by NSCT to obtain the low frequency subbands and the high frequency subbands in 8 directions. The low-frequency subbands adopts an average gradient adaptive weighting fusion strategy to retain the contour features. The high-frequency subbands adopt the strategy of maximizing regional energy, retaining the edge and texture feature of the lesion. The frequency-domain enhanced feature image Ffreq is obtained through the inverse NSCT transformation. Thirdly, Ffreq is used to balance and provide information compensation for the spatial domain feature Fspa, and the fused image Ffuse is output. Finally, adversarial learning is carried out using dual discriminators, including D_CT and D_PET. Among them, D_CT takes CT as the reference for the structure and texture feature, and D_PET takes PET as the reference to enhance the contour feature.

Frequency domain debiasing module

The spatial resolution of CT images is high, so it clearly shows bones, organ edges and anatomical structures. PET images highlight the metabolic activity of lesions. The NonSubsampled Contourlet Transform (NSCT) is an improvement on the contourlet transform. It consists of NonSubsampled Pyramid Filter Bank (NSPFB) and NonSubsampled Directional Filter Bank (NSDFB). Pseudo-Gibbs artifacts that appear in contourlet reconstruction are effectively avoided. Therefore, the paper designs frequency-domain debiasing module, as shown in Figure 15B. The module obtains low-frequency and high-frequency subbands through NSCT decomposition. Designing different fusion strategies for different subbands, highlighting the global structure features of the lesion, and highlighting the local contour features of the lesion.

In this section, PET and CT images are decomposed using NSCT. The specific decomposition process is as follows: Firstly, the low-frequency subband and high-frequency subband are obtained through NSPFB. Then, three-layer NSDFB decomposition is performed on the high-frequency subbands to obtain 8 directional subbands, and directions are 22.5°, 45°, 67.5°, 90°, 112.5°, 135°, 157.5°, and 180° respectively.

Low-frequency subbands fusion rules: The low-frequency subbands decomposed by NSCT include global luminance and the overall structure information. If the gray values of the image are averaged, it is easy to reduce the contrast degree between the lesion and the surrounding tissues. To suppress the unfairness of background dominance weight distribution, the section adopts an average gradient adaptive weighting strategy during low-frequency fusion. For each local window, calculate the average gradient of the low-frequency subbands and perform pixel-level weight allocation based on the average gradient. Regions with high average gradients are assigned greater weights, while regions with low average gradients are assigned smaller weights. In this fusion strategy, the average gradient adaptive weighting is used to assign more weight to the lesion regions with severe gray value changes. Therefore, it not only retains the characteristics of high density resolution and clear anatomical structure of CT images, but also retains the characteristics of functional metabolic imaging of PET images. It reflects the activity of tissues at the molecular level and avoids the reduction of image contrast degree caused by averaging gray values.

High-frequency subbands fusion rules: Unlike the low-frequency subbands, the high-frequency subbands focuses on features such as the edges, textures, and local gray-scale changes of the image. In medical images, the lesion region is characterized by a small pixel ratio and sparse spatial distribution. Spatial domain debiasing module

In the process of multimodal image fusion, pixel-level unfairness problems such as “low proportion of lesion pixels, high proportion of background pixels” are prone to occur. Thus, to realize the integration of cross-modal features in the spatial domain and enhance the features of the lesion region, the paper designs a spatial domain debiasing module in the generator, as shown in Figure 16. The module consists of 2 stages: global feature alignment and local semantic selection. Firstly, ViT29 is adopted to conduct global cross-modal fusion of PET and CT images, realizing the alignment of CT structural information and PET lesion information. Secondly, calculating the cosine similarity between the class token and the image token to select the tokens. Adopting an adaptive weighting strategy to enhance the selected tokens. Thirdly, the functional metabolic information of the lesion in PET images and the anatomical structure information of CT images are integrated at the global level. Meanwhile, the contrast degree and texture features of the lesion region are enhanced at the local level, and the interference of background information on the lesion region during the training process is suppressed. Finally, the module outputs the spatial domain debiasing feature Fspa.

Frequency-guided fusion module

The frequency-domain fusion method retains the global anatomical structure and the contrast degree of lesion, but the perception ability of local texture features and edge features is limited. The spatial domain fusion method enhances the texture and edge features of the lesion and improves the clarity degree of the lesion, but it is insufficient in preserving the integrity of the global anatomical structure. Therefore, the paper designs a frequency-guided fusion module (FGFM). The structure is shown in Figure 17A. The generator outputs a spatial-domain feature image Fspa. The frequency-domain feature image Ffreq from the frequency-domain debiasing module provides guiding information that refines and compensates Fspa. The network outputs the fused image Ffuse.

Discriminator

The discriminator is a dual discriminator architecture in this paper, as shown in Figure 15D. Among them, D_PET is the core component of the adversarial game against the Generator. Its core function is to distinguish PET images from fused images. Essentially, it is a binary classification model. Meanwhile, it provides an optimization direction for the generator through backpropagation and promotes the generator to learn real images. D_CT has the same function as D_PET.

In terms of network structure, it contains 4 convolutional blocks, the first convolutional layer, the second convolutional layer and the third convolutional layer are composed of convolutional blocks and LeakyReLU activation, and the last layer is made up of convolutional layers and Tanh activation. The convolution kernel size is 3×3 and the step size is 2. A scalar is obtained through Tanh activation to represent the degree which the input image originates from the source image rather than the generated result.

Debiasing loss function

In multimodal medical image fusion, a single optimization objective is often insufficient to cover both lesion features and structural details. Moreover, the lesion region has a relatively low pixel proportion in the entire image. The model training is easily dominated by the large region background, resulting in insensitivity to the lesion. Therefore, this section designs a Debiasing Loss function (LDBLoss). It achieves a dynamic balance between the expression of lesion features and the preservation of structural details.

In terms of the expression of lesion features, a lesion reweighting loss function and a background reweighting loss function are designed. In terms of preserving the structural details of the lesion, a gradient loss function is designed.

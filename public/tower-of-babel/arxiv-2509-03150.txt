Sparsity, Stress-Independence and Globally Linked Pairs in Graph Rigidity Theory
Dániel Garamvölgyi, Bill Jackson, Tibor Jordán

Abstract
A graph is ℛ d \mathcal{R}_{d} -independent (resp. ℛ d {\mathcal{R}}_{d} -connected) if its d d -dimensional generic rigidity matroid is free (resp. connected). A result of Maxwell from 1867 implies that every ℛ d \mathcal{R}_{d} -independent graph satisfies the sparsity condition | E ⁡ ( H ) | ≤ d ​ | V ⁡ ( H ) | − ( d + 1 2 ) |E(H)|\leq d|V(H)|-\binom{d+1}{2} for all subgraphs H H with at least d + 1 d+1 vertices. Several other families of graphs G G arising naturally in rigidity theory, such as minimally globally d d -rigid graphs, are known to satisfy the bound | E ⁡ ( G ) | ≤ ( d + 1 ) ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | − ( d + 2 2 ) |E(G)|\leq(d+1)|V(G)|-\binom{d+2}{2} . We unify and extend these results by considering the family of d d -stress-independent graphs which includes many of these families. We show that every d d -stress-independent graph is ℛ d + 1 \mathcal{R}_{d+1} -independent. A key ingredient in our proofs is the concept of d d -stress-linked pairs of vertices. We derive a new sufficient condition for d d -stress linkedness and use it to obtain a similar condition for a pair of vertices of a graph to be globally d d -linked. This result strengthens a result of Tanigawa on globally d d -rigid graphs. We also show that every minimally ℛ d {\mathcal{R}}_{d} -connected graph G G is ℛ d + 1 \mathcal{R}_{d+1} -independent and that the only subgraphs of G G that can satisfy Maxwell’s criterion for ℛ d + 1 \mathcal{R}_{d+1} -independence with equality are copies of K d + 2 K_{d+2} . Our results give affirmative answers to two conjectures in graph rigidity theory.

Introduction

In this paper we investigate the relationship between rigidity and global rigidity of graphs, as well as unify and extend known sparsity results for various graph classes appearing in combinatorial rigidity theory. A graph is
d
-rigid
(resp.
globally
d
-rigid
) if its generic realisations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
cannot be deformed continuously (resp. arbitrarily) such that the edge lengths are preserved. (Precise definitions are given in the next section.)
Rigidity and global rigidity are both well-studied graph properties with a number of applications, both within and outside mathematics. From a combinatorial perspective,
d
-rigidity can be seen as a matroidal analogue of
d
-connectivity. Indeed, it is well-known that every
d
-rigid graph on at least
d+1
vertices is
d
-connected, and that the edge sets of
d
-rigid graphs on
n
vertices are the spanning sets of the
d
-dimensional generic rigidity matroid

\mathcal{R}_{d}(K_{n})
of the complete graph
K_{n}
.

In contrast, there is no matroid underlying global rigidity, which makes the combinatorial study of globally
d
-rigid graphs more challenging. To circumvent this difficulty, one can try to find necessary and sufficient conditions for global
d
-rigidity in terms of
d
-rigidity.
It is immediate that every globally
d
-rigid graph is
d
-rigid, and by a seminal result of Hendrickson
[
8
]
, globally
d
-rigid graphs on at least
d+2
vertices are also
redundantly
d
-rigid
, which means that they remain rigid after the removal of any edge. In the other direction, we have the following key result of Tanigawa
[
20
]
. We say that a
d
-rigid graph
G
is
vertex-redundantly
d
-rigid
if
G-v
is
d
-rigid for every vertex
v
of
G
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
20
]

Every vertex-redundantly
d
-rigid graph is globally
d
-rigid.

We can use
Theorem

1.1
and standard techniques in rigidity theory to deduce the following “dimension dropping” result.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
13
]

Every
(d+1)
-rigid graph is globally
d
-rigid.

We may also compare minimally
d
-rigid and minimally globally
d
-rigid graphs.
Minimally
d
-rigid graphs on
n
vertices correspond to the bases of the
d
-dimensional generic rigidity matroid
\mathcal{R}_{d}(K_{n})
. In particular, they are
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-independent
, which means that their edge set is independent in
\mathcal{R}_{d}(K_{n})
, and for
n\geq d+1
, they have exactly
dn-\binom{d+1}{2}
edges.

Again, global
d
-rigidity does not have an associated matroid, and hence the structure of minimally globally
d
-rigid graphs is less understood. It follows from Hendrickson’s result that a globally
d
-rigid graph on at least
d+2
vertices cannot be minimally
d
-rigid. On the other hand, the following recent result implies that a
(d+1)
-rigid graph on at least
d+3

-rigid, simply because it has too many edges.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
5

Let

be a minimally globally
d
-rigid graph on at least

|E|\leq(d+1)|V|-\binom{d+2}{2},

where equality holds if and only if

.

The first main result of this paper is the following direct connection between global rigidity in dimension
d
and rigidity in dimension

.

Theorem 1.4
.

Every minimally globally
d
-rigid graph is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-independent.

Our proof of

is based on two main ideas. The first is to prove local analogues of
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
. Given a vertex pair

is
d
-linked
in
G
(resp.

d
-linked
in

, each continuous (resp. arbitrary) deformation of

and

is
d
-rigid (resp. globally

-rigid) if and only if every pair of vertices of

-linked (resp. globally

-linked) in

We prove the following strengthening of

-linked

-linked in

Theorem 1.5

Let

be a graph on at least three vertices and

2.1
Rigid graphs and the generic rigidity matroid

We recall the definitions and results from rigidity theory that we shall use. We refer the reader to
[
19
,

A

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a pair
(G,p)
consisting of a graph

and a mapping

and the map

and

are

if
\lVert p(u)-p(v)\rVert=\lVert q(u)-q(v)\rVert

if the above equality holds for every pair of vertices
u,v\in V
.

To each framework
(G,p)
we associate its

R(G,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|E|\times d|V|}

indexed by

, and in the row corresponding to the edge

, the entries in the
d
columns corresponding to vertices

and

contain the vectors
p(u)-p(v)
and

, respectively, while the remaining entries are zeros.
The
d
-dimensional generic rigidity matroid

\mathcal{R}_{d}(G)
of

is the row matroid of

, for any generic framework
(G,p)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

denote the rank function of

for the rank of
\mathcal{R}_{d}(G)

A graph

-rigid
if

. The graph

-independent
if

is a free matroid, and is an
{\cal R}_{d}
-circuit
if

is a circuit in

and

has no isolated vertices. If

is

-independent, on at least
d
vertices, then the above mentioned result of Maxwell

|E(G)|=r_{d}(G)\leq d|V(G)|-\binom{d+1}{2},

(1)

and since subgraphs of
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-independent graphs are also
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-independent, the bound in

We will need the following folklore result from linear algebra.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
and
B\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times\ell}
be matrices such that
AB=0
. Fix
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and define
\hat{S}=\{1,\ldots,n\}-S
. If the rows of
B
indexed by
\hat{S}
are linearly independent, then the columns of
A
indexed by
S
span the column space of
A
.

Proof.

The validity of the statement does not change by reordering the columns of
A
and the rows of
B
according to the same permutation. Thus we may assume that
S=\{1,\ldots,k\}
for some
k
. By performing column operations on
B
, as well as possibly deleting some columns, we may also assume that
B
is of the form

B=\begin{pmatrix}B^{\prime}\\
I\end{pmatrix},

where
I
is an
(n-k)\times(n-k)
identity matrix.
Since
AB=0
, we can use the
i
-th column of
B
to express the
(k+i)
-th column of
A
as a linear combination of its first
k
columns, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-k\}
. This shows that the first
k
columns of
A
indeed span its column space.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
be frameworks in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
(G,p)
is in general position. Fix
\omega\in S(G,p)
, and let
U\subseteq V
be the set of vertices where the equilibrium conditions
Equation

2
are not satisfied for
\omega
and
(G,q)
. If
U\neq\varnothing
, then
|U|\geq d+2
.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction that
1\leq|U|\leq d+1
.
Let
\Omega\in{\mathbb{R}}^{V\times V}
be the stress matrix of
\omega
, let
P,Q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|V|\times d}
be the matrices whose rows are the vectors
p(v),v\in V
and
q(v),v\in V
, respectively, and let
\widetilde{P}
and
\widetilde{Q}
be obtained from

and

by adding a column of ones at the end.

The row of
\Omega\widetilde{Q}
indexed by a vertex

is zero if and only if the equilibrium conditions

2
are satisfied at

for
\omega
and

. Hence the definition of

implies that

for all

and we can obtain the required contradiction by showing that

. To this end, it will suffice to show that the rows of

corresponding to

∎

-connected

-connected case this is not true (see the

The

Theorem 4.6
.

If
G=(V,E)
is a
2
-stress-independent graph with
|V|\geq 8
, then
|E|\leq 3|V|-9
.

The bound

along a vertex and adding an edge is

-stress-independent with

Before proving

Theorem 4.7

Let

-stress-independent graph. For all subgraphs

of

Let us add a set

-bridges to

.

[

Let

Next, we prove a sequence of technical lemmas. The first is a simple analogue of
Theorem

4.9
. Let

-rigid

Lemma 4.10
.

If

\mathcal{R}_{2}
-connected graph on at least five vertices, then

\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit on at least five vertices.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that every

Lemma 4.11
.

Every minimally globally
2
-rigid graph on six vertices is an
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit.

Proof.

By
Theorem

2.5
, every globally
2
-rigid graph on at least four vertices arises from

by a sequence of

-dimensional edge split operations and edge additions. If only edge splits are used, then the graph obtained is an
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit. If an edge addition is used, then to obtain a minimally globally

-rigid graph at the end of the sequence, we have to later perform an edge split along it. By combining these observations, we conclude that the only graphs we need to check are those obtained from

by adding a new edge and then performing an edge split along it. Up to isomorphism, there are two such graphs (depending on whether the center of the wheel is a neighbour or not of the vertex we add last), and it is easy to check that neither of them is minimally globally

Recall the notation
s_{d}(G)=|V(G)|-k_{d}(G)
, where

denotes the

-circuit

. The goal of the next two results is to characterise the

-circuits

with

.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
C=(V,E)
be an
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-circuit which is a
2
-sum of two
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-circuits
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. Then we have
s_{d}(C)=s_{d}(C_{1})+s_{d}(C_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
V(C_{1})\cap V(C_{2})=\{u.v\}
, and let us fix a generic framework
(C,p)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Since
C_{i}
is an
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-circuit,
(C+uv,p)
has a unique nonzero stress
\omega_{i}
supported on the edges of
C_{i}
and satisfying
\omega_{i}(uv)=1
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Moreover,
\omega=\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}
is the unique nonzero stress of
(C,p)
, up to scalar multiplication.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
and
\Omega
be the stress matrices associated to
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
and
\omega
, respectively. Since
G_{i}
is an
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-circuit, we have
s_{d}(C_{i})=\rk(\Omega_{i}),i\in\{1,2\}
, and similarly,
s_{d}(C)=\rk(\Omega)
. Hence we only need to show that
\rk(\Omega)=\rk(\Omega_{1})+\rk(\Omega_{2})
. From
\Omega=\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2}
we have
\rk(\Omega)\leq\rk(\Omega_{1})+\rk(\Omega_{2})
. To show that
\rk(\Omega)\geq\rk(\Omega_{1})+\rk(\Omega_{2})
also holds, consider the submatrix
\Omega^{\prime}_{i}
of
\Omega_{i}
formed by the rows and columns indexed by
V(C_{i})-\{u,v\}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Since
\Omega
contains the block matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\Omega^{\prime}_{1}&0\\
0&\Omega^{\prime}_{2}\end{pmatrix}

as a submatrix, we have
\rk(\Omega)\geq\rk(\Omega^{\prime}_{1})+\rk(\Omega^{\prime}_{2})
. Hence it suffices to show that
\rk(\Omega^{\prime}_{i})=\rk(\Omega_{i})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
; by symmetry, we may assume that
i=1
.

We proceed as in the proof of
Lemma

3.2
. Let
P_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{|V(C_{1})|\times d}
be the matrix whose rows are the vectors
p(z),z\in V(C_{1})
, and let
\widetilde{P_{1}}
be the matrix obtained from
P_{1}
by adding a column of ones at the end. Observe that the rows and columns of
\Omega_{1}
corresponding to
V(C_{2})-\{u,v\}
are all zero, and hence by deleting them, the rank of
\Omega_{1}
does not change. In this way, we may consider
\Omega_{1}
as a
|V(C_{1})|\times|V(C_{1})|
matrix. Then
\Omega_{1}\widetilde{P}_{1}=0
, and the rows of
\widetilde{P}_{1}
indexed by
\{u,v\}
are linearly independent, since
\{p(u),p(v)\}
is affinely independent.
Lemma

3.1
now implies that
\rk(\Omega_{1})=\rk(\Omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})
, where
\Omega_{1}^{\prime\prime}
is the submatrix of
\Omega_{1}
obtained by deleting the columns corresponding to
p(u)
and
p(v)
. Since
\Omega_{1}
is symmetric, we also have
(\Omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})^{T}\widetilde{P}_{1}=0
, and by using
Lemma

3.1
again we deduce that

\rk(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})=\rk(\Omega_{1}^{\prime\prime})=\rk(\Omega_{1}),

as claimed.
∎

Let
K_{4}\oplus_{2}K_{4}
denote the

-sum of two copies of

.

.

Let

be an
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit. We have

, and

(b)

, then

It follows from

2.5
and

2.3
that

is either globally
2
-rigid, or arises as the
2
-sum of two
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuits

and

. If

is globally

-rigid, then we have

. Hence in this case

, and similarly,

We proceed as in the proof of

]
.
Let

\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit in

,

be an edge of

, and

be obtained by extending

-independent subgraph

Consider the following two cases.

Case 1:

-rigid.

Case 2:

.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-connected
2
-stress-independent graph. Then

(a)

every separating pair of vertices is nonadjacent in
G
, and

(b)

for any
2
-separation
(G_{1},G_{2})
of

,

is not isomorphic to either
K_{4}^{+}
or
W_{5}
.

Proof.

(a)
Suppose, for a contradiction, that
u,v
is a separating pair of vertices of

and

. We can deduce from
Lemma

2.3
that
G-uv
is also
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-connected and satisfies
\kappa(u,v;G-uv)\geq 3
. Hence by
Theorem

2.10
,
\{u,v\}
is
2
-stress-linked in

, contradicting the assumption that

is
2
-stress-independent.

(b)
Let

. By part

,
u
and

are nonadjacent in
G
. Let us first suppose for a contradiction that
G_{1}
is isomorphic to
K_{4}^{+}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
u
is the vertex of degree two in
G_{1}
. Let
w
be a common neighbour of

and

in

. It is easy to verify that

is isomorphic to

and hence is

that

and

that

is

-stress-independent.

The case when

. Let

Proof of

.

We consider two cases.

Case 1:

is not
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-connected.
In this case we can use the proof idea of
[

, Theorem 1.4]
. By adding

-bridges to

-rigid. (Recall that by

, adding

-bridges preserves the property of being

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\left(|E_{0}|+\sum_{i=1}^{q}r_{2}(G_{i})\right)-\frac{|E_{0}|+3q}{2}

. Moreover, from our assumption that

As we noted in the previous section,

-connected graph is

Conjecture 5.1
.

Every minimally
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected graph is

-stress-independent.

In support of this conjecture, we show that parts
(a)
and
(b)
of

also hold for a family of graphs which includes all minimally
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
-connected graphs. This verifies the first half of
[

, Conjecture 4.2]
. Let us note that the second half of
[
12
, Conjecture 4.2]
is the analogue of

for minimally
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected graphs.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be a graph in which every nontrivial
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-component is minimally
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected. Then

(a)

G
is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-independent;

(b)

For all subgraphs
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
G
with
|V^{\prime}|\geq d+2
, we have

|E^{\prime}|\leq r_{d}(G^{\prime})+|V^{\prime}|-d-1\leq(d+1)|V^{\prime}|-\binom{d+2}{2},

where the first inequality holds with equality if and only if
G^{\prime}=K_{d+2}
.

Our proof of
Theorem

5.2
is based on the concept of ear-decompositions from matroid theory, which we introduce next.
Let
{\cal M}
be a matroid on ground set
E
, and let
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{t}
be a sequence of circuits of
{\cal M}
.
Let
D_{0}=\varnothing
, and
D_{j}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}C_{i}
for
1\leq j\leq t
.
The set
C_{i}-D_{i-1}
is called the
lobe
of the circuit
C_{i}
, and is denoted by
\widetilde{C}_{i}
, for each
1\leq i\leq t
.
Following
[
2
]
, we say that
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{t}
is a
partial ear-decomposition
of
{\cal M}
if the following properties hold for all
2\leq i\leq t
:

(E1)

C_{i}\cap D_{i-1}\neq\varnothing
,

(E2)

C_{i}-D_{i-1}\neq\varnothing
,

(E3)

no circuit
C_{i}^{\prime}
satisfying
(E1)
and
(E2)
has
C_{i}^{\prime}-D_{i-1}
properly contained in
C_{i}-D_{i-1}
.

An
ear-decomposition
of
{\cal M}
is a partial ear-decomposition with

D_{t}=E
.
Parts
(a)
and
(b)
of the following lemma are from
[
2
]
.
Part
(c)
follows easily from the definitions.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\cal{M}
be a matroid with
rank function
r
. Then

(a)

\cal M
is connected if and only if
\cal M
has an ear-decomposition.

(b)

If
\cal M
is connected, then any partial ear-decomposition of
\cal M

can be extended to an ear-decomposition of
\cal M
.

(c)

If
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{t}
is an ear-decomposition of
\cal M
, then

r(D_{i})-r(D_{i-1})=|\widetilde{C}_{i}|-1\ \hbox{ for }\ \ 1\leq i\leq t.

The next lemma is from
[
12
]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
{\cal M}
be a minimally connected matroid on ground set
E
with
|E|\geq 2
and let

C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{t}
be an ear-decomposition of

{\cal M}
.
Then

(a)

|\widetilde{C}_{i}|\geq 2
for all
1\leq i\leq t
,

(b)

{\cal M}|_{D_{i}}
is minimally connected for all
1\leq i\leq t
.

Proof of
Theorem

5.2
.

(a)
It follows from
Theorem

2.1
that every
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-component of
G
is the union of some
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-components of
G
. Hence it suffices to show that each
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-component of
G
is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-independent. In the case of a trivial
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-component this is clear. Thus we may restrict our attention to a nontrivial
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-component, and hence assume that
G
is minimally
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected.

By
Lemma

5.3
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}(G)
has an ear-decomposition
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{t}
. We proceed by induction on
t
. If
t=1
, then
G
is an
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
-circuit and every pair of edges of
G
is a cocircuit of
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}(G)
.
Lemma

2.4
(b) now implies that every edge of
G
is a bridge in
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}(G)
, and hence
G
is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-independent. Thus we may assume that
t\geq 2
.

Put
G^{\prime}=G[\bigcup_{i=1}^{t-1}C_{i}]
and
J=E-\bigcup_{i=1}^{t-1}C_{i}
. Then

is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-independent by
Lemma

5.4
(b) and induction, and hence
r_{d+1}(E-J)=|E-J|
. In addition,
|J|\geq 2
by
Lemma

5.4
(a). The fact that
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{t}
is an ear-decomposition of
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}(G)
and
Lemma

5.3
(c) imply that every pair of edges of
J
is a cocircuit of
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}(G)
. Thus every edge of

is a bridge of
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}(G)
by
Lemma

2.4
(b). This implies that
r_{d+1}(E)=|E-J|+|J|=|E|
, and hence
G
is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-independent.

(b)
We proceed as in the proof of
Theorem

4.4
(b). Let us add a set
F
of
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-bridges to
G^{\prime}
so that
G^{\prime}+F
is
d
-rigid. Every nontrivial
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-component of
G^{\prime}+F
is minimally
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected, and hence part
(a)
implies that
G^{\prime}+F
is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-independent. Hence by a simple calculation we have,

\displaystyle|E^{\prime}|=|E(G^{\prime}+F)|-|F|

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)|V^{\prime}|-\binom{d+2}{2}-|F|

(6)

\displaystyle=r_{d}(G^{\prime}+F)+|V^{\prime}|-d-1-|F|

\displaystyle=r_{d}(G^{\prime})+|V^{\prime}|-d-1,

which gives the first part of
(b)
.

If equality holds in

, then
G^{\prime}+F
is minimally
(d+1)
-rigid, and hence globally

-rigid by
Theorem

1.2
.
Theorem

2.6
now implies that

. If

is minimally globally

-rigid, then

by
Theorem

1.3
. Hence we may assume that
G^{\prime}-e
is globally

-rigid for some

. We can now use
Theorem

2.6
to deduce that both

and

are
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
-connected.

It follows that
G^{\prime}
is contained in some
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-component
G_{0}
of

. Note that

is necessarily nontrivial, and hence minimally
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected. By using parts
(a)
and
(b)
of

, we can construct an ear-decomposition

of

and then extend it to an ear-decomposition of

. It follows from

(b) that

is minimally
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
-connected, contradicting the fact that

\mathcal{R}_{d}
-connected.
∎

Proposition 6.1

Let

to prove the following.

.

If
G
is an
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-connected

-stress-independent graph on at least five vertices, then

does not contain a copy of
K_{4}
.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that

contains a subgraph

. Let us fix vertices

. Since

is
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-connected and

, there exists another
\mathcal{R}_{2}
-circuit

in

with

. It follows from

that

. Since

cannot be a subgraph of

, there exists a pair of vertices

such that

.

Let us consider the subgraph

of

. Since

is

, and hence

is not

-stress-linked in

. Since

, it is also

. But by

(a), every separating vertex pair in a

6.2
Minimally
k
-connected graphs

We relate
Theorems

4.4
and
4.7
to the existing literature on the sparsity of minimally
k
-connected graphs.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a minimally
k
-connected graph. As mentioned in the introduction, Mader
[
17
]
proved that
|E|\leq k|V|-\binom{k+1}{2}
, and that if
|V|\geq 3k-2
, then
the stronger bound
|E|\leq k(|V|-k)
applies. The latter result is tight, as shown by the complete bipartite graph
K_{k,n-k}
for
n\geq 2k
.

Mader’s result can be extended to subgraphs by a result of Nishizeki and Poljak
[
18
]
which tells us
that
E
can be covered by
k
forests and hence implies that

|E(H)|\leq k|V(H)|-k
for all subgraphs
H\subseteq G
.

Proposition

4.1
and

Theorem

4.4
immediately give the following stronger result.
For completeness, we present a more direct proof.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
k
be a positive integer, and let
G
be a
minimally
k
-connected graph. Then

(a)

G
is
\mathcal{R}_{k}
-independent;

(b)

For all subgraphs
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
G
with
|V^{\prime}|\geq k+1
, we have

|E^{\prime}|\leq r_{k-1}(G^{\prime})+|V^{\prime}|-k\leq k|V^{\prime}|-\binom{k+1}{2},

where the first inequality holds with equality if and only if
G^{\prime}=K_{k+1}
.

Proof.

(a)
It is known that if
uv
is an edge in an
\mathcal{R}_{k}
-circuit
H
, then
\kappa(u,v;H-uv)\geq k
holds (see, e.g.,
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
).
The minimality of
G
implies that
\kappa(u,v;G-uv)\leq k-1

for all
uv\in E(G)
. Thus
G
is
\mathcal{R}_{k}
-independent.

(b)

We proceed as in the proofs of
Theorem

4.4
(b) and
Theorem

5.2
(b). Let us add a set
F
of
\mathcal{R}_{d-1}
-bridges to
G^{\prime}
so that
G^{\prime}+F
becomes
(k-1)
-rigid. The edges in
F
are
\mathcal{R}_{k}
-bridges as well, and hence part
(a)
implies that
G^{\prime}+F
is
\mathcal{R}_{k}
-independent. As before, this implies

\displaystyle|E^{\prime}|=|E(G^{\prime}+F)|-|F|

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)|V^{\prime}|-\binom{d+2}{2}-|F|

\displaystyle=r_{d}(G^{\prime}+F)+|V^{\prime}|-d-1-|F|

\displaystyle=r_{d}(G^{\prime})+|V^{\prime}|-d-1,

as claimed. If equality holds, then
G^{\prime}+F
is
k
-rigid, and thus globally
(k-1)
-rigid by
Theorem

1.2
. It follows from
Theorem

2.6
that
F=\varnothing
. If
G^{\prime}
is minimally globally
(k-1)
-rigid, then
G^{\prime}=K_{k+1}
by
Theorem

1.3
. Hence we may assume that
G^{\prime}-e
is globally
(k-1)
-rigid for some
e\in E^{\prime}
. Since every globally
(k-1)
-rigid graph is
k
-connected, it follows
that
\kappa(u,v;G-uv)\geq\kappa(u,v;G^{\prime}-uv)\geq k
, where
e=uv
, which contradicts the minimality of
G
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem

6.4
shows that Mader’s bound on the size of a minimally
k
-connected graph
G
holds for all subgraphs
H
of
G
of order at least
k
. It is natural to ask whether Mader’s stronger bound for sufficiently large graphs can also
be extended to subgraphs. This question was recently answered affirmatively by Lou and He
[

, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
G
be a minimally
k
-connected graph. Then, for all subgraphs
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
G
with
|V^{\prime}|=h\geq 5k-4
, we have

E^{\prime}\leq k(h-k)
, with equality if and only if
G^{\prime}=K_{k,h-k}
.

Theorem

6.5
verifies
4.5
for the special case when

and
G
is a subgraph of a minimally
k
-connected graph.
The lower bound on
h
in
Theorem

6.5
is not best possible. We believe that it can be replaced by

, for all
k\geq 1
. This is obvious
for
k=1
, and for
k=2
it is implied by the
d=1
case of
Theorem

5.2
by using the fact that
2
-connectivity and
{\cal R}_{1}
-connectivity are the same.
The
k=3
case can be shown by carefully following the proof of
Theorem

4.6
to verify that the graph obtained by gluing together two copies of
K_{4}
at a vertex and adding an edge is the unique 2-stress-independent graph with 7 vertices and 13 edges, and noting that this graph is not
3
-connected. We omit the details.

We close by stating the specialisation of
Theorem

4.7
to minimally
3
-connected graphs.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
G
be a minimally
3
-connected graph. For all subgraphs
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
of
G
with
|V^{\prime}|\geq 8
, we have

|E^{\prime}|\leq r_{2}(G^{\prime})+|V^{\prime}|-6\leq 3(|V^{\prime}|-3).

Compared to the

case of

,

gives a slightly better bound on the number of edges if

is low, or equivalently, if the generic realisations of

in

have a high degree of freedom.

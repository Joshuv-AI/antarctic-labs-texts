52 | Timing of Delivery in Growth‐Restricted or Discordant Dichorionic Twin Pregnancies: A Population‐Based Study

M. D. Huiskamp1; R. J. Burger2; J. W. Nijkamp3; A. C. J. Ravelli2; B. W. J. W. Mol4; N. Holowko5; M. Davies‐Tuck5

1Monash University, Utrecht University, Amsterdam UMC, Melbourne, Australia; 2Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 3UMC Utrecht, Utrecht, Netherlands; 4Monash University, Melbourne, Australia; 5The Ritchie Centre, Hudson Institute of Medical Research, Melbourne, Australia

Objective: To determine whether birthweight discordance affects the gestational age at which the risk difference between stillbirth and neonatal death reaches its nadir in dichorionic twin pregnancies, and whether this differs by birthweight percentile.

Study Design: A population‐based cohort study using data from the Victorian Perinatal Data Collection (Victoria, Australia, 2000–2020). Week‐specific risks of stillbirth and neonatal death were calculated from 34 weeks of gestation. Stillbirth risk was expressed per 1000 ongoing pregnancies and neonatal death risk per 1000 deliveries. The risk difference between stillbirth and neonatal death was assessed acrossgestational weeks to identify the gestational age at which this difference reached its nadir. Analyses were stratified by birthweight percentile and level of birthweight discordance.

Results: We included 7270 individuals with dichorionic twin pregnancies. The prospective risk of stillbirth increased with gestational age, from 0.6 per 1000 ongoing pregnancies at 34 weeks to 2.9 per 1000 at 38 weeks. Neonatal mortality decreased from 7.5 per 1000 deliveries at 34 weeks to 0.8 per 1000 at 38 weeks. Greater birthweight discordance was associated with higher absolute risks of stillbirth and neonatal death, with risks increasing markedly above 20% discordance (Figure 1). The nadir of the risk difference remained at 36–37 weeks across strata of birthweight discordance (Figure 2), and was similar across birthweight percentiles.

Conclusion: In dichorionic twin pregnancies, greater birthweight discordance was associated with higher absolute risks of stillbirth and neonatal death. The gestational age at which the risk difference reached its nadir remained at 36–37 weeks regardless of discordance or birthweight percentile. These findings do not support earlier delivery in discordant pregnancies, but underscore the need for close surveillance in pregnancies with more than 20% discordance.

53 | Stillbirth as a Risk Marker for Postpartum Cardiovascular Disease Readmission: A Population‐Based Retrospective Cohort Study

Anushka Pande1; Ruby Lin2; Rachel Lee3; Emily B. Rosenfeld2; Minxiu Shi4; Cande V. Ananth4

1Rutgers Robert Wood Johnson Medical School, New Brunswick, NJ; 2Division of Maternal‐Fetal Medicine, Department of Obstetrics, Gynecology, and Reproductive Sciences, Rutgers Robert Wood Johnson Medical School, New Brunswick, NJ; 3Division of Epidemiology and Biostatistics, Department of Obstetrics, Gynecology, and Reproductive Sciences, Rutgers Robert Wood Johnson Medical School, New Brunswick, NJ; 4Division of Epidemiology and Biostatistics, Department of Obstetrics, Gynecology, and Reproductive Sciences, New Brunswick, NJ

Objective: Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in the first postpartum year. Stillbirth and CVD share etiologic pathways, and prior studies demonstrate elevated long‐term CVD risk after stillbirth. However, whether stillbirth unmasks short‐term CVD is less well characterized. We examined whether stillbirth is associated with hospital readmission for CVD within the calendar year after delivery.

Study Design: We conducted a population‐based retrospective cohort study using the Nationwide Readmissions Database (2010–2020) that included women (15–54 years) with a singleton delivery. We examined CVD‐related readmission or in‐hospital mortality within the calendar year of delivery. Associations were estimated using adjusted Cox proportional hazards models.

Results: Among 18,253,997 singleton delivery hospitalizations, 117,230 (6.5 per 1000) resulted in stillbirth. Of 37.3 million person‐months, the median follow‐up was 6.4 months (interquartile range 3.7–9.1). The rate of CVD‐related readmission within the postpartum calendar year was higher after stillbirth compared with live birth (404.0 vs 166.0 per 100,000 deliveries; rate difference 238.0 per 100,000, 95% confidence interval [CI] 192.0–285.0). Stillbirth was associated with higher hazard of CVD readmission (adjusted hazard ratio [HR], 2.03; 95% CI, 1.78–2.32), including heart disease (HR, 2.00; 95% CI, 1.72–2.32), stroke (HR, 2.29; 95% CI, 1.80–2.92), and CVD‐related mortality (HR, 2.08; 95% CI, 1.12–3.84). Risk was elevated within 30 days postpartum and peaked from 60–89 days (Figure 1). Bias correction slightly attenuated estimates. Associations were stronger with co‐occurring chronic hypertension, ischemic placental disease, or diabetes (Figure 2).

Conclusion: Stillbirth was associated with increased risk of CVD‐related readmission within the calendar year of delivery. These findings extend prior evidence of long‐term CVD risk after stillbirth by demonstrating excess cardiovascular burden in the early postpartum period.

54 | Placental Pathology at Delivery Predicts Brain Injury and Neurodevelopmental Outcomes in Fetal Growth Restriction

Youzhen Zhang; Jingyu Liu; Nana Huang; Xiya Sun; Jing Yang; Yangyu Zhao

Peking University Third Hospital, Beijing, China (People's Republic)

Objective: Fetal growth restriction (FGR) increases neonatal brain injury risk, yet prenatal Doppler indices inadequately stratify injury severity. The placenta develops synchronously with the fetal brain and is obtainable non‐invasively at delivery, offering a window to predict neurological risk. We evaluated Amsterdam consensus‐classified placental pathologies to identify phenotypes predictive of brain injury and 2–3 year neurodevelopmental outcomes in FGR.

Study Design: We prospectively enrolled 1,567 FGR and age‐matched appropriate‐for‐gestational‐age (AGA) controls (2022–2025). Placental histopathology was classified per Amsterdam criteria. Brain injury was diagnosed by sequential cranial ultrasound with intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) and periventricular leukomalacia (PVL) grading. Neurodevelopment was assessed at 2–3 years via Ages and Stages Questionnaire‐3 (ASQ‐3). Logistic regression identified features associated with injury. Machine learning (ML) models integrating clinical, Doppler, and placental variables were built with cross‐validation to predict severe injury.

Results: FGR neonates had significantly higher brain injury rates, particularly severe injury. Among all placental pathologies, villous stromal fibrosis was the only phenotype associated with brain injury, increasing stepwise across severity groups (none: 8.3%; mild: 40.7%; severe: 80.0%). Doppler indices did not differ across groups. ML models incorporating placental pathology outperformed clinical‐Doppler‐only models for severe injury prediction (AUC: 0.960 vs 0.905), with fibrosis as the top‐ranked feature. At 2–3 years, fibrosis correlated negatively with communication and problem‐solving development.

Conclusion: Among evaluated placental pathologies, villous stromal fibrosis uniquely predicts brain injury and adverse neurodevelopment in FGR beyond Doppler surveillance. This fibrotic remodeling may reflect disrupted placenta‐brain axis signaling. Placental assessment at delivery combined with ML‐based risk stratification could enable early identification of high‐risk neonates for targeted intervention.

55 | Comparing Outcomes of Intrapartum Uterine Rupture in Individuals With and Without One Prior Cesarean Delivery

Adam Wright, Jr.1; Mackenzie Cronin1; Sneha Paranandi1; Fabrizio Zullo2; Matthew K. Hoffman3; Rachel L. Wiley4; Suneet P. Chauhan1; Daniele Di Mascio2

1ChristianaCare, Newark, DE; 2Sapienza University of Rome, Rome, Italy; 3Columbia University Medical Center, New York, NY; 4University of California, San Diego, San Diego, CA

Objective: To discern whether composite neonatal and maternal adverse outcomes in intrapartum uterine rupture differ between patients without versus with one prior Cesarean delivery (CD).

Study Design: Using US Vital Statistics Period‐Linked Birth and Infant Death Data files from 2016–2013, this is a population‐based, retrospective cohort study of all multiparous pregnancies with non‐anomalous singletons who labored between 24 to 41 weeks gestation and had a uterine rupture. Exclusion criteria included >2 prior CD. Primary outcomes were composite neonatal outcomes (CNAO) including Apgar < 5 at 5 minutes, assisted ventilation >6 hours, neonatal seizure, and death; and composite maternal outcomes (CMAO) including intensive care unit admission, blood transfusion, and unplanned hysterectomy. Multivariable Poisson regression was used to estimate relative risk and adjust for confounding variables. Results are presented as adjusted relative risks (aRR) with 95% confidence intervals.

Results: 29,972,027 births were considered for inclusion. Among those meeting inclusion criteria, 4446 uterine ruptures were identified. 1864 (41.9%) had no prior CD, and 2582 (58.1%) had one prior CD. CNAO did not differ significantly between the two groups (aRR 1.05; 95% CI 0.92–1.19). Neonatal seizure was more prevalent in cases of rupture without prior CD (aRR 1.60; 95% CI 1.10–2.35); other components of CNAO did not differ significantly. CMAO (aRR 1.85; 95% CI 1.68–2.05), including each individual component, was significantly higher in cases of rupture without prior CD. Infant death was similar between groups (aRR 1.51; 95% CI 0.99–2.31).

Conclusion: Composite neonatal adverse outcomes did not significantly differ in cases of uterine rupture with and without one prior Cesarean delivery. However, cases of intrapartum uterine rupture without prior Cesarean delivery were associated with increased composite maternal adverse outcomes, warranting investigation into risk factors associated with rupture in those without prior Cesarean delivery.

56 | Long‐Term Reproductive Outcomes of Cesarean Scar Ectopic Pregnancy: Retrospective Study From Three Large Academic Institutions

Hillary Hosier1; Nancy Fang2; Jennifer E. Kaiser3; Lisbet S. Lundsberg4; Jennifer F. Culhane4; Sara Giuliano4; Khadija Alshowaikh4; Victoria Grechukhina4; Leilah Zahedi‐Spung5; Alexandra Adler5; Susan E. Nourse3; Sonya S. Abdel‐Razeq4; Olga Grechukhina4

1Emory University, Atlanta, GA; 2University of Colorado, Denver, CO; 3University of Utah, Salt Lake City, UT; 4Yale University, New Haven, CT; 5Colorado University, Denver, CO

Objective: Cesarean scar ectopic pregnancy (CSEP), where the embryo implants into a prior cesarean scar, is associated with high rates of maternal morbidity and is usually terminated. There is limited data on the long‐term impact of CSEP on subsequent reproductive health. We aim to describe gynecological and obstetrical outcomes of patients with a history of CSEP.

Study Design: This is a retrospective cohort study of CSEP cases managed at 3 academic institutions (University of Utah Hospital, UCHealth University of Colorado Hospital and Yale‐New Haven Hospital System) from 9/2015 to 9/2024. For each CSEP case data were manually collected from electronic health records using a standardized data collection tool, de‐identified, and analyzed in aggregate form. Descriptive statistics were used to characterize the study population.

Results: A total of 245 CSEP cases were included. Detailed description of the cohort with patient demographics and the index CSEP characteristics are listed in Table 1. Among all cases of CSEP, 91 (37.1%) were classified as on the scar (endophytic), 124 (50.6%) as in the niche (exophytic) and 30 (12.2%) as location unknown. Most common treatment modalities were surgical with uterine preservation (ultrasound‐guided suction curettage, n = 114, 46.5%), medical or balloon therapy (n = 72, 29.39%) and hysterectomy (n = 18, 7.4%). Overall, gynecological complications post‐CSEP were rare (Table 1). The rate of new infertility after CSEP was 11/186 (5.9%). Of 186 patients who retained their uteri and were not lost to follow up, 89 had at least one subsequent pregnancy. Of those 15 (16.9%) were recurrent CSEP and were terminated, 16 (18.0%) had a miscarriage and 49 (55.1%) had a preterm or term live birth (Table 2).

Conclusion: Patients with history of CSEP, although infrequently, may have long‐term gynecologic complications. In subsequent pregnancies the recurrence rate is high. If normal implantation occurs the risk of miscarriage appears to be higher than what is expected in the general population. Subsequent pregnancies that continue to viability demonstrate favorable maternal and newborn outcomes.

57 | Effectiveness and Neonatal Outcomes of the Hook & Roll Maneuver for Shoulder Dystocia

Cassidy A. O'Sullivan1; Savanah Richter1; Erica Heilman1; Matthew K. Hoffman2

1ChristianaCare, Newark, DE; 2Columbia University Medical Center, New York, NY

Objective: To evaluate the effectiveness and neonatal outcomes of the Hook & Roll maneuver for shoulder dystocia compared with other internal maneuvers in an expanded cohort.

Study Design: We conducted a retrospective cohort study of shoulder dystocia cases at a single tertiary care center from July 2021 to January 2026. Cases were identified from a quality improvement database and confirmed by chart review. The Hook & Roll maneuver involves traction on the posterior axilla with simultaneous rotation of the anterior shoulder. Outcomes were compared when Hook & Roll was used as the first internal maneuver versus other internal maneuvers, and when it was the successful maneuver versus other successful maneuvers. Success rates were calculated per maneuver attempt. The primary outcome was neonatal injury, including brachial plexus injury and fracture. Analysis was performed using Wilcoxon rank‐sum and Fisher's exact tests.

Results: Among 385 cases, Hook & Roll was used as the first internal maneuver in 35 (9.1%). When used first, it was associated with shorter time to delivery (75.0 vs 93.7 seconds, p = 0.01) and fewer maneuvers (2.83 vs 3.79, p < 0.01). No neonatal injuries occurred when Hook & Roll was the first internal maneuver (0.0% vs 25.6%, p < 0.001). Hook & Roll had the highest success rate (91.4%, 74/81), compared with posterior arm delivery (41.8%, 99/237) and rotational maneuvers (40.3%, 54/134). When comparing successful maneuvers, Hook & Roll was associated with lower rates of neonatal injury (12.2% vs 27.2%, p = 0.01) and in particular, brachial plexus injury (8.1% vs 21.6%, p = 0.01).

Conclusion: In this expanded cohort, the Hook & Roll maneuver was associated with efficient resolution of shoulder dystocia and lower neonatal injury rates, particularly when used early. These findings support its use as a primary internal maneuver.

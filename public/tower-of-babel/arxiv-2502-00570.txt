Abstract
. An example of an infinite regular feebly compact quasitopological group is presented such that all continuous real-valued functions on the group are constant. The example is based on the use of Korovin orbits in X G X^{G} , where X X is a special regular countably compact space constructed by S. Bardyla and L. Zdomskyy in 2023 and G G is an abstract Abelian group of an appropriate cardinality. Also, we study the interplay between the separation properties of the space X X and Korovin orbits in X G X^{G} . We show in particular that if X X contains two nonempty disjoint open subsets, then every Korovin orbit in X G X^{G} is Hausdorff.

1.
Introduction

According to the celebrated theorem of T. Banakh and A. Ravsky in
[
3
]
,
every regular paratopological group is Tychonoff, that is, completely regular.
Actually, the same article provides a more general fact:
Every regular
topological monoid with open shifts is completely regular.
It is also shown
there that every Hausdorff paratopological group is functionally Hausdorff,
that is, continuous real-valued functions on the group separate points.

These results suggest that one could extend the theorem about the
coincidence of regularity and complete regularity to other objects of
Topological Algebra, such as semitopological groups or the smaller
class of quasitopological groups.

We show in Theorem
5.1
that this is not the case and that
there exists a regular
quasitopological group

H
(that is, a
group with a topology such that the left and right translations and inversion
in the group are continuous), but every continuous real-valued function on

H
is constant, that is,
H
is
\mathbb{R}
-rigid
. Hence,
H

is neither completely regular nor functionally Hausdorff. Additionally,
the space
H
is
feebly compact
, that is, every locally finite
family of open sets in
H
is finite.

The group
H
in Theorem
5.1
is a
Korovin orbit

(see Section
4
for more details). Every Korovin orbit
is a subspace of a product space
X^{G}
, for some space
X
and
an abstract Abelian group
G
, that fills all countable subproducts
in
X^{G}
.

The relationships between the separation properties of a product
space
X=\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
and a dense subspace
Y
of
X

that fills all finite (or countable) subproducts of
X
are studied in
Section
3
. We also provide some conditions on
Y

and the factors
X_{i}
implying that
Y
is either
C
-embedded
in
X
or
\mathbb{R}
-rigid. The results obtained there are applied
in Section
4
for the study of the same problems
for Korovin orbits
G_{f}
in place of
Y
.

In the very short Section
5
, we prove the aforementioned
Theorem
5.1
by applying results from Sections
3

and
4
. Four open problems in Section
6

show a further direction of the study of dense subspaces of uncountable
topological products and have a close connection with the contents of
the article.

2.
Notation, terminology and preliminary facts

2.1.
Subspaces of topological products

Given a topological product
X=\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
and a nonempty
subset
J
of the index set
I
, we denote by
\pi_{J}
the projection
of
X
onto the subproduct
X_{J}=\prod_{i\in J}X_{i}
. A subset
Y

of
X

fills countable (finite) subproducts in
X
if
\pi_{J}(Y)=X_{J}
,
for each countable (finite) set
J\subseteq I
. Evidently, a set
Y\subseteq X

that fills finite (countable) subproducts of
X
is dense in
X
.

The proof of the subsequent fact requires a standard application
of the
\Delta
-lemma (see Theorem on page 87 of
[
9
]
)
and, hence, is omitted.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X=\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
be a topological product such that
the subproduct
X_{J}=\prod_{i\in J}X_{i}
is feebly compact,
for each countable set
J\subseteq I
. Then
X
is also feebly
compact. Furthermore, if a subspace
D
of
X
fills all
countable subproducts of
X
, then
D
is also feebly compact.

A space is said to be
pseudo-
\aleph_{1}
-compact

if every locally finite family of open sets in the space is countable.
The following result is a special case of Theorem on page 90 of

[
6
]
if one takes
\varkappa=\omega
and
\alpha=\omega_{1}
:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
D
be a subspace of a product
X=\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
that fills
all countable subproducts of
X
. Let also
g\colon D\to Z
be a
continuous mapping to a metrizable space
Z
. If the space
X

is pseudo-
\aleph_{1}
-compact, then so is

and one can find a
countable set
J\subseteq I
and a continuous mapping

.

2.2.
Axioms of separation

Let
X
be a space and
i\in\{0,1,2,3,3.5,4\}
.
We say that
X
is a
T_{i}
-space if it satisfies condition
(T_{i})
:

(T_{0})

Given distinct points
x,y\in X
, the exists an open
set
U
in
X
that contains exactly one of the points
x,y
.

(T_{1})

Given distinct points
x,y\in X
, the exists an open
set
U
in
X
such that
x\in U
and
y\notin U
.

(T_{2})

The Hausdorff separation property (two distinct points
in
X
have disjoint open neighborhoods).

(T_{3})

If
U
is an open neighborhood of a point
x\in X
, then
there exists an open neighborhood
V
of
x
such that

\overline{V}\subset U
.

(T_{3.5})

If a closed set
F\subseteq X
does not contain a point

x\in X
, then there exists a continuous real-valued
function
f
on
X
satisfying
f(x)=1
and
f(F)\subseteq\{0\}
.

(T_{4})

Every two closed disjoint subsets of
X
have disjoint
open neighborhoods in
X
.

Hausdorff spaces are the spaces satisfying condition
(T_{2})
.
Regular spaces are defined to satisfy conditions
(T_{3})
and
(T_{1})
.
The conjunction
(T_{3.5})\,\&\,(T_{1})
defines completely regular
(Tychonoff) spaces, whereas the combination
(T_{4})\,\&\,(T_{1})

delineates normal spaces.
A functionally Hausdorff
space

X
is a space in which continuous real-valued functions separate
points of
X
.

2.3.
Groups with a topology

A group with a topology is called
semitopological
if multiplication
in the group is separately continuous. Ellis
[
7
]
proved that
a locally compact semitopological group is a topological group, that is,
multiplication and inversion in the group are continuous. In his work

[
10
]
, Korovin demonstrated that a completely regular,
countably compact Abelian semitopological group is a topological group.
Additionally, he provided an example of a pseudocompact semitopological
Abelian group with discontinuous multiplication. In
[
11
]
,
Korovin’s theorem is extended to the non-Abelian case.

In the same article
[
10
]
, a wide class of pseudocompact
spaces was introduced that were afterwards called
Korovin spaces

in
[
12
,
13
]
. This class
contains countably compact spaces, pseudocompact spaces of countable
tightness and some other important subclasses of pseudocompact
spaces. If a semitopological group
G
is a Korovin space, then

G
is a topological group
[
11
]
(in the case

G
is Abelian, this is a Korovin theorem in
[
10
]
).
A detailed study of the class of Korovin spaces is presented in

[
12
]
.

A semitopological group
G
is called
quasitopological

if inversion in the group is continuous. Every boolean semitopological
group is evidently a quasitopological group.

2.4.
Korovin orbits

In
[
10
]
, A. Korovin presented an original method to constructing
pseudocompact semitopological groups that are not topological groups.
An analysis of Korovin’s method led to the notions of
Korovin map

and
Korovin orbit

[
1
,
8
,
13
]

(see also Theorem 2.4.13 in
[
2
]
). Korovin orbits are also used to construct pseudocompact quasitopological groups with various additional properties
[
1
,
8
,
5
,
14
]
.
Nontrivial Korovin orbits cannot be Korovin spaces.

Let
X
be a space with
|X|>1
and
G
be an infinite Abelian
group. The group
G
naturally acts on the product space
X^{G}
.
The action is defined by the formula
(gf)(x)=f(xh)
for all
g,x\in G

and
f\in X^{G}
. We denote by
G_{f}=Gf=\{gf:g\in G\}
the

orbit
of an element
f\in X^{G}
under the action of
G

on
X^{G}
. The element
f
is called a
Korovin mapping

if
G_{f}
fills in all countable subproducts in
X^{G}
,
\pi_{M}(G_{f})=X^{M}

for each countable set
M\subseteq G
. In this case,
G_{f}
is called
a
Korovin orbit.

A sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}\subset G
is said to be
exact

if
g_{k}\neq g_{n}
for distinct
k,n\in\omega
. A mapping
f\colon G\to X

is a Korovin mapping if and only if the following condition holds:

(K_{\omega})

If
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}\subset G
is an exact sequence
and
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\subset X
, then there exists
g\in G
such that

f(g_{n}g)=x_{n}
for each
n\in\omega
.

Theorem 2.3

(
\mathbf{A.\,V.\penalty\ Korovin}
)
.

Let
\kappa
be an infinite cardinal with
\kappa=\kappa^{\omega}
,

X
be a topological space satisfying
1<|X|\leq\kappa
, and

G
an Abelian group such that
|G|=\kappa
. Then there exists
a Korovin mapping
f\colon G\to X
and, hence, there exists a
Korovin orbit in
X^{G}
.

It is easy to see that the mapping
{\mathfrak{i}}\colon G\to G_{f}
,
g\mapsto gf

is a bijection
[
2
, Proposition 2.4.14 (1)]
. This
bijection is used to define multiplication in
G_{f}
making use
of multiplication in the group
G
as follows:
{\mathfrak{i}}(g){\mathfrak{i}}(h)={\mathfrak{i}}(gh)
for
all
g,h\in G
. Hence, the groups
G
and
G_{f}
are algebraically
isomorphic. When
G_{f}
carries the topology of a subspace of
X^{G}
,
this multiplication turns
G_{f}
into a semitopological group (see

[
10
]
or
[
2
, Proposition 2.4.14 (3)]
).
In what follows we will sometimes identify
G
with the corresponding
Korovin orbit
G_{f}
. If the group
G
is boolean, then
G_{f}
is a
quasitopological group.

In
[
13
]
, the following quite
unexpected properties of Korovin orbits are established:

Theorem 2.4

(
[
13
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let

be an Abelian group,

be a space with

, and

(1)

If

of the Korovin orbit

(2)

-embedded in

.

If there exists an unbounded continuous real-valued function

-embedded in

.

Let

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that a subspace

fills all

-space,

-space.

Assume that the subspace

4.

Let
X

G_{f}\subseteq X^{G}

Proposition 4.1
.

If a space
X
is not anti-discrete, then every Korovin orbit

G_{f}
in
X^{G}
is a
T_{1}
-space.

Proof.

By item (1) of Theorem
2.4
, every singleton in

G_{f}
is a closed set.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Every (equivalently, some) Korovin orbit
G_{f}
in
X^{G}
is Hausdorff
if and only if the space
X
contains two disjoint nonempty open sets.

Proof.

Let
U
and
V
be nonempty disjoint open sets in
X
. Take
distinct elements
u
and
v
of a Korovin orbit
G_{f}\subseteq X^{G}
. There
exist
g_{0},g_{1}\in G
such that
u=g_{0}f
and
v=g_{1}f
. Choose elements

g_{n}\in G
for
n>1
such that the sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is exact.

Pick points
x_{0}\in U
and
x_{1}\in V
. For every
n>1
, we put

x_{n}=x_{1}
. Condition
(K_{\omega})
implies that there exists
g\in G
such
that
f(g_{n}g)=x_{n}
for each
n\in\omega
. Then
u(g)=(g_{0}f)(g)=f(gg_{0})=x_{0}

and
v(g)=(g_{1}f)(g)=f(gg_{1})=x_{1}
. Hence,
G_{f}\cap\pi_{g}^{-1}(U)
and

G_{f}\cap\pi_{g}^{-1}(V)
are disjoint open neighborhoods of the points

u
and
v
in
G_{f}
, where
\pi_{g}\colon X^{G}\to X_{(g)}
is the
projection.

Conversely, assume that a Korovin orbit
G_{f}
in
X^{G}
is Hausdorff.
Take distinct elements
u,v\in G_{f}
and choose disjoint open neighborhoods

O_{u}
and
O_{v}
of
u
and
v
, respectively, in
G_{f}
. Then one
can find canonical open neighborhoods
W_{u}
and
W_{v}
of
u
and

v
in
X^{G}
such that
W_{u}\cap G_{f}\subseteq O_{u}
and
W_{v}\cap G_{f}\subseteq O_{v}
. Let
W_{u}=\prod_{g\in G}W_{u,g}
and
W_{v}=\prod_{g\in G}W_{v,g}
, where
W_{u,g}
and
W_{v,g}
are open in
X
for each

g\in G
. Let
F
be a finite subset of
G
such that
W_{u,g}=W_{v,g}=X

for all
i\in I\setminus F
. If
W_{u,g}\cap W_{v,g}\neq\emptyset
for each
g\in F
,
then
W_{u}\cap W_{v}
is a nonempty open set in
X^{G}
, so the density
of
G_{f}
in
X^{G}
implies that

\emptyset\neq W_{u}\cap W_{v}\cap G_{f}=(W_{u}\cap G_{f})\cap(W_{v}\cap G_{f})\subseteq O_{u}\cap O_{v}=\emptyset.

This contradiction shows that there exists
g\in F
such that the nonempty
open subsets
W_{u,g}
and
W_{v,g}
of the space
X
are disjoint.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

If a space
X
admits a non-constant real-valued function, then
every Korovin orbit
G_{f}
in
X^{G}
is functionally Hausdorff.

Proof.

Let
u
and
v
be distinct elements of
G_{f}
. It follows from
item (2) of Theorem
2.4
that there exists a continuous
real-valued function
q
on
G_{f}
such that
q(u)=0
and
q(v)=1
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

A Korovin orbit
G_{f}
is a
T_{3}
-space if and
only if
X
is a
T_{3}
-space.

Proof.

Since
G_{f}
fills all countable subproducts in
X^{G}
,
the required conclusion follows directly from
Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
.
∎

The case of
T_{3.5}
separation axiom is considered in the
following result.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
f\colon G\to X
be a Korovin mapping and
G_{f}\subseteq X^{G}

the corresponding Korovin orbit. Suppose that one of the following
holds true:

(a)

the space
X^{\omega}
is pseudo-
\aleph_{1}
-compact;

(b)

X
is first-countable;

(c)

X
is separable.

Then
G_{f}
is a
T_{3.5}
-space if and only if
X
is a
T_{3.5}
-space.

Proof.

Since
X
is a
T_{3.5}
-space, so are the spaces
X^{G}
and

G_{f}\subseteq X^{G}
. Hence, it suffices to verify the converse implication.
We assume, therefore, that
G_{f}
is a
T_{3.5}
-space.

Since
G_{f}
fills all countable subproducts in
X^{G}
,
Proposition
3.5
implies that
G_{f}
is
C
-embedded
in
X^{G}
. Then we apply Proposition
3.3
to conclude
that
X
is a
T_{3.5}
-space.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

If a space
X
is not anti-discrete and has a base of clopen sets,
then every Korovin orbit
G_{f}
in
X^{G}
is a regular zero-dimensional
space.

Proof.

Since
X
has a base of clopen sets, the same is valid for the
spaces
X^{G}
and
G_{f}
. Also,
X
is a
T_{1}
-space, by
Proposition
4.1
. It follows that the space
G_{f}

is regular and zero-dimensional.
∎

The subsequent example shows that the conclusion of
Proposition
4.5
does not hold without

.

Example 4.7
.

Let
X
be the product of the discrete space
D=\{0,1\}
and
the same set
D
that carries the anti-discrete topology. Also,
denote by
G
the boolean group
\mathbb{Z}(2)^{\omega}
. Then,
by Proposition
4.6
, every Korovin orbit
G_{f}
in
X^{G}

is completely regular, but
X
is not even a
T_{0}
-space.

Together, Corollory
3.6
and the fact that a Korovin orbit

G_{f}
fills all countable subproducts in

imply the following.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
X
be a separable
{\mathbb{R}}
-rigid space. Then every Korovin
orbit
G_{f}
in
X^{G}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-rigid.

5.
An example

The results presented in Sections
2
and
4
as
well as the existence of an infinite regular separable countably compact

\mathbb{R}
-rigid space
[
4
]
enable us to give a short proof of
the following theorem announced in the abstract.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists an infinite regular feebly compact
{\mathbb{R}}
-rigid
quasitopological group
H
. Therefore,
H
is neither completely
regular nor functionally Hausdorff.

Proof.

Let
X
be a regular separable,
totally countably compact

{\mathbb{R}}
-rigid space with
|X|=2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, where
\mathfrak{c}=2^{\omega}
(see

[
4
, Theorem 3.4]
). Total countable compactness of
X

means that every infinite set
A\subseteq X
contains an infinite
subset
B
such that the closure of
B
in
X
is compact. The
latter property of
X
implies that
X^{\omega}
is also totally countably
compact. In particular,
X^{\omega}
is feebly compact.

Let also
G=\mathbb{Z}(2)^{\mathfrak{c}}
, where
\mathbb{Z}(2)
is the
Boolean group
\{0,1\}
. By Theorem
2.3
, there
exists a Korovin mapping
f\colon G\to X
. Denote by
H=G_{f}

the corresponding Korovin orbit considered as a subspace of
X^{G}
.

Since
X^{\omega}
is countably compact, Proposition
2.1

implies that the space
G_{f}
is feebly compact. The regularity of
X

guarantees that the spaces
X^{G}
and
G_{f}
are also regular. By
Corollary
4.8
,
G_{f}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-rigid.
∎

6.
Open problems

This article’s results raise several unanswered questions.

Let us call a Korovin orbit
G_{f}\subseteq X^{G}

nontrivial

if
|X|\geq 2
and
|G|\geq 2^{\omega}
.

Problem 6.1
.

Can a non-trivial Korovin orbit be a normal space?

Problem 6.2
.

Can a non-trivial Korovin orbit be a paracompact space?

The next problem is a more general version of the previous one.

Problem 6.3
.

Let a subspace
D
of
X=\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
fills countable subproducts
of
X
, where
|I|>\omega
and each
X_{i}
is not compact. Can
D
be
paracompact?

Problem 6.4
.

Let a subspace
D
of
X=\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
fills all countable (finite)
subproducts of
X
and assume that
X
is a
T_{1}
-space.

(1)

If
D
is paracompact, is each factor
X_{i}

also paracompact?

(2)

If
D
normal, is each factor
X_{i}
normal?

(3)

If
D
is completely regular, is each factor

X_{i}
completely regular?

The group

in Theorem
5.1
is not separable because

are closed (see
item (1) of Theorem

). For this reason, we inquire

:

Problem 6.5
.

Is there a regular separable semitopological (quasitopological)

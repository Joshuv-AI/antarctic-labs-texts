Abstract

This paper deals with explicit upper and lower bounds for principal eigenvalues and the maximum principle associated to generalized Lane-Emden systems (GLE systems, for short). Regarding the bounds, we generalize the upper estimate of Berestycki, Nirenberg and Varadhan [Comm. Pure Appl. Math. (1994), 47-92] for the first eigenvalue of linear scalar problems on general domains to the case of strongly coupled GLE systems
with
m\geqslant 2
equations on smooth domains. The explicit lower estimate we obtain is also used to derive a maximum principle to GLE systems relying in terms of quantitative ingredients. Furthermore, as applications of the previous results, upper and lower estimates for the first eigenvalue of weighted poly-Laplacian eigenvalue problems with
L^{p}
weights
(p>n)
and Navier boundary condition are obtained. Moreover, a strong maximum principle depending on the domain and the weight function for scalar problems involving the poly-Laplacian operator is also established.

Introduction

We consider the following eigenvalue problem which is called generalized Lane-Emden system (GLE system, for short):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rlllr}-{\cal L}u&=&\Lambda\rho(x)S_{\alpha}(u)&{\rm in}&\Omega,\\
u&=&0&{\rm on}&\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open subset with
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}
,
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}):\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
m\geqslant 2
,
{\cal L}u=({\cal L}_{1}u_{1},\ldots,{\cal L}_{m}u_{m})
, where for each
i=1,\ldots,m

{\cal L}_{i}=a^{i}_{\kappa l}(x)\partial_{\kappa l}+b^{i}_{l}(x)\partial_{l}+c_{i}(x),\ \ \kappa,l=1,\ldots,n

is a uniformly elliptic linear operator of second order in
nondivergence
form in
\Omega
,
\Lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m})\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
positive function in
\Omega
,
p>n
and
\Lambda\rho(x)S_{\alpha}(u)=(\lambda_{1}\rho_{1}(x)S_{\alpha_{1}}(u),\ldots,

\lambda_{m}\rho_{m}(x)S_{\alpha_{m}}(u))
, where
S_{\alpha}(u)
is given by

(S_{\alpha_{1}}(u),\ldots,S_{\alpha_{m}}(u))=(|u_{2}|^{\alpha_{1}-1}u_{2},|u_{3}|^{\alpha_{2}-1}u_{3},\ldots,|u_{m}|^{\alpha_{m-1}-1}u_{m},|u_{1}|^{\alpha_{m}-1}u_{1})

with
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in(0,\infty)^{m}
and
\Pi\alpha:=\Pi_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}=1
.

We assume that the coefficients of each operator
{\cal L}_{i}
satisfy
a^{i}_{\kappa l}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and there are constants
c_{0},C_{0}>0
and
b_{0}\geqslant 0
such that

c_{0}|\xi|^{2}\leqslant a^{i}_{\kappa l}(x)\xi_{\kappa}\xi_{l}\leqslant C_{0}|\xi|^{2},\ \ \forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ x\in\Omega

and

\left(\sum_{l=1}^{n}|b^{i}_{l}(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\ |c_{i}(x)|\leqslant b_{0},\ \ \forall x\in\Omega,\ \ \ \ i=1,\ldots,m.

We recall that the operator
{\cal L}_{i}
satisfies the weak maximum principle (
(WMP)
, for short) in
\Omega
if, for any function
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
such that
{\cal L}_{i}u\leqslant 0
in
\Omega
and
u\geqslant 0
on
\partial\Omega
, we have
u\geqslant 0
in
\Omega
. If, in addition, either
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega
or
u>0
in
\Omega
, we say that the operator
{\cal L}_{i}
satisfies the strong maximum principle (
(SMP)
, for short) in
\Omega
. Equivalently, see
[
4
]
and
[
24
]
, the
(SMP)
holds in
\Omega
if, and only if, the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}({\cal L}_{i},\Omega)
of
{\cal L}_{i}
, for the Dirichlet problem, is positive, so
[
5
, Theorem 10.1]
assures the validity of the
(SMP)
for
{\cal L}_{i}
in
\Omega
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, under the following conditions:

\|b^{i}\|^{n}_{\infty}|\Omega|\leqslant\left(c_{0}\sqrt{\Sigma}\right)^{n}|B_{1}|

(2)

and

c_{0}\Sigma|B_{1}|^{\frac{2}{n}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{n}}-\|b^{i}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\Sigma}|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{n}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{n}}-\inf_{\Omega}c_{i}>0,

(3)

where
B_{1}
is the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|\cdot|
stands for the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\Sigma:=\lambda_{1}(-\Delta,B_{1})\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \|b^{i}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{n}(b_{l}^{i})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the GLE system (
1
), we say that
\Lambda_{0}=(\lambda_{01},\ldots,\lambda_{0m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is said to be an eigenvalue if the problem (
1
) admits a nontrivial strong solution
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m})\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})

is said to be principal if the GLE system (
1
) admits an eigenfunction

such that all components

are positive in
\Omega
,

. We say that
\Lambda_{0}

\psi=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m})
, there exists
\eta=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}

In

{\bf\Lambda_{1}}=\{\Lambda\in(0,\infty)^{m}:\,\lambda_{1}\lambda^{\alpha_{1}}_{2}\ldots\lambda^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{m-1}}_{m}=\lambda_{*}\}

(4)

We say that

associated to (

In Theorem 1.1 of

Main results

1.2
Applications to poly-Laplacian equations

It is known that the eigenfunctions associated with the first eigenvalue of the bi-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary condition are not always one-signed functions (see Subsection 3.1.3 in
[
14
]
). This is due to the lack of a maximum principle to that class of problems. Further, Faber-Krahn type inequalities are also more delicate in this case (see Subsection 1.2.3.4 in
[
16
]
). Nevertheless, the topic of obtaining lower and upper bounds on eigenvalues of the poly-Laplacian operator with arbitrary order and Dirichlet boundary condition have been widely investigated, see for instance
[
2
,
8
,
9
,
17
,
23
,
29
]
.

On the other hand, the study of estimates for eigenvalues of higher order elliptic equations with Navier boundary conditions seems to be little addressed in the literature. Some contributions on this subject are also provided in this paper. To the best of our knowledge, the only work in this direction is due to Lin and Zhu
[
22
]
. In that paper, the authors have proven, among many other results, a delicate upper estimate of nodal sets of eigenfunctions of the poly-Laplacian with Navier boundary condition on real analytic domains
\Omega
via analytic estimates of Morrey-Nirenberg and Carleman estimates.

Consider the following Navier boundary value problem involving the poly-Laplacian operator

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}(-\Delta)^{m}v=\lambda\rho_{1}(x)v&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega,\\

\Lambda_{0}=(\lambda_{01},\ldots,\lambda_{0m})\in{\bf\Lambda_{1}}
, with
\lambda_{01}=\lambda_{*}
and
\lambda_{0i}=1
,
i=2,\ldots,m
. Then, for all principal eigenfunction
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m})
of (
31
) corresponding to
\Lambda_{0}
, we have
\varphi_{2}
is a solution of the problem (
18
). Furthermore,
\lambda_{01}
is a positive eigenvalue of the problem (
18
) that admits eigenfunction
v\in W^{2m,p}(\Omega)
satisfying
v,(-\Delta)^{j}v>0
in
\Omega
,
j=1,\ldots,m-1
.
We take
\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1}):=\lambda_{01}=\lambda_{*}
.

Suppose that there exists
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})
eigenvalue to (
18
), that is, there is a nontrivial function
z\in H^{m}_{\vartheta}(\Omega)
satisfying

(z,\phi)_{H^{m}_{\vartheta}}=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\rho_{1}(x)z\phi\,dx\quad\forall\phi\in H^{m}_{\vartheta}(\Omega)

and
z=(-\Delta)^{j}z=0
on
\partial\Omega
,
j=1,\ldots,m-1
.

Using Sobolev embedding, Theorem 2.20 of
[
14
]
and a bootstrap argument, we obtain
z\in W^{2m,p}(\Omega)
.

Now, we take
u_{2}=z,\ u_{3}=-\Delta u_{2},\ u_{4}=-\Delta u_{3}=(-\Delta)^{2}u_{2},\ldots,u_{m}=(-\Delta)^{m-2}u_{2}
and
u_{1}=(-\Delta)^{m-1}u_{2}
. Then,
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\in W^{2,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a strong solution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rlllr}-\Delta u_{1}&=&\lambda\rho_{1}(x)u_{2}&{\rm in}&\Omega,\\
-\Delta u_{2}&=&u_{3}&{\rm in}&\Omega,\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\\
-\Delta u_{m-1}&=&u_{m}&{\rm in}&\Omega,\\
-\Delta u_{m}&=&u_{1}&{\rm in}&\Omega,\\
u&=&0&{\rm on}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Thus
(\lambda,1,\cdots,1)\in{\cal R}_{1}
(see Figure 2 for
m=2
).

Figure 2:
For

,

But it’s a contradiction, because by Theorem 6.1 of

20
]
, there isn’t any eigenvalue to (
1
) in the region
(\overline{{\cal R}}_{1}\setminus{\bf\Lambda_{1}})\cup-(\overline{{\cal R}}_{1}\setminus{\bf\Lambda_{1}})
.

From the simplicity of
{\bf\Lambda_{1}}
, it follows that
\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})
is simple.

6
Bounds for eigenvalues of (
18
) and proof of Theorems
1.5
and
1.6

In this section, we consider the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})
of the problem (
18
) obtained through the GLE system (
31
). We separate the estimates for
\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})
into three items below.

1) Lower estimate for
\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})
when
\rho_{1}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, with
p>n

Let
C=\max\{1,\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}\}
and
\Omega
satisfying (
12
).

\bullet
For
n=1
and
\Omega
satisfying

|\Omega|<\frac{|B_{1}|\Sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(C(C_{1})^{1/p})^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}},

by Theorem
1.2
, we get

\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})\geqslant\frac{1}{4C(C_{1})^{1/p}}\left(\Sigma|B_{1}|^{2}|\Omega|^{-2}\right)^{1-1/p},

where
\theta=\frac{p-1}{p}
.

\bullet
For
n=2
and
\Omega
satisfying

|\Omega|<\frac{\left(|B_{1}|^{2}\Sigma^{2}\right)^{\theta}}{(C(C_{2})^{2/3})^{2}},

by Theorem
1.2
, we derive

\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})\geqslant\frac{1}{4C(C_{2})^{2/3}}\left(\Sigma^{\frac{1}{3}}|B_{1}|^{\frac{1}{3}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),

where
\theta=\frac{1}{3}
.

\bullet
For
n\geqslant 3
and
\Omega

|\Omega|<\frac{|B_{1}|\Sigma^{\frac{n}{2}}}{(C(C_{n})^{1-\theta})^{\frac{n}{2\theta}}},

1.2
, we obtain

\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})\geqslant\frac{1}{4C(C_{n})^{1-\theta}}\left(\Sigma|B_{1}|^{\frac{2}{n}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{\theta},

where
\frac{p-1}{2p}=\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{(1-\theta)(n-2)}{2n}
.

2) Lower estimate for
\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})
when
\rho_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)

In this case, by Theorem
1.1
, for any
\Omega
satisfying

(32)

we have

\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})\geqslant\frac{1}{D}\left(\Sigma|B_{1}|^{\frac{2}{n}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{n}}\right),

(33)

where

.

3) Upper estimate for
\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})
when

By Theorem
1.3
, if
\rho_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)

in
\Omega

0\in\Omega

\lambda_{1}((-\Delta)^{m},\Omega,\rho_{1})\leqslant\left(\dfrac{64n(2n+1)}{\varepsilon_{0}R^{2}}\right)^{m}.

(34)

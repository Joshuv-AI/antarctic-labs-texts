Entrustable professional activities for radiology residents: a case-based assessment framework
Authors: Anam Khan, Shaista Afzal, Saira Samnani, Shayan Sirat Maheen Anwar, Zainab Hussain
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEntrustable professional activities were formally integrated into the radiology residency program in 2024 through a structured approach, with assessments conducted across all four years of training by the Department of Postgraduate Medical Education, in alignment with the guidelines of the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education. This study evaluates the Entrustable professional activities framework, its implementation process, and the outcomes of case-based assessments within the radiology residency program.MethodsA total of 26 residents participated in the study, including eight from Year 1 and six each from Years 2, 3, and 4. Each resident received a list of EPAs appropriate to their level of training. A range of teaching and learning strategies, along with multiple assessment methods, were employed to evaluate performance. A minimum score of 80% was required to pass the assessment. Residents scoring below this threshold received targeted feedback and were scheduled for remedial assessments. Feedback from both faculty and residents was collected following the completion of the activity.ResultsOf the 26 residents assessed, 24 (92%) achieved scores above the 80% passing threshold. The two residents who scored below this benchmark received individualized feedback and were scheduled for remediation.ConclusionThe implementation of the EPA-based learning and assessment framework for radiology residents proved to be effective, providing a structured and reliable approach for evaluating core competencies. The integration of structured teaching and learning activities, systematic assessments, and interactive feedback enhanced resident engagement and improved learning outcomes, establishing a strong foundation for continued improvements in residency training.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-025-08227-y.Keywords: Entrustable professional activities, Radiology residency, Case-based assessment, Competency-based education, Radiology training

Introduction

Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs) have emerged as an effective competency-based medical education framework, offering a practical approach to assessing trainees’ readiness to perform essential clinical tasks with minimal supervision. EPAs define tasks or responsibilities that residents must master, integrating the knowledge, skills, and attitudes essential for professional practice. In 2024, EPAs were introduced for radiology residents in a structured manner, with assessments conducted across all four residency years by postgraduate medical education (PGME) in accordance with accreditation council for graduate medical education (ACGME) guidelines. This study evaluates the EPA framework, implementation process, and outcomes of case-based assessments in a radiology residency program.

Methods

The EPAs (Table 1) for radiology residents that can serve as core component of the curriculum were selected after achieving consensus from educational experts and residency committee members of the radiology department at a tertiary care hospital in Karachi, Pakistan. It was ensured that the EPA addresses the competencies i.e. medical knowledge, patient care, system-based practice, practice-based learning and improvement, interpersonal and communication skills and professionalism. The teaching and learning methodologies for non-procedural competencies included reporting rounds, case-based discussion, tutorials, literature review etc. For the procedural competencies, we employed dedicated tutorials, simulation-based teaching and practice, observations in real life etc. The assessment methods for entrustment included formative assessments during reporting rounds and image based summative evaluation. The procedural skills were evaluated via direct observation of procedural skills (DOPS). The multisource feedback from faculty, especially trainee’s supervisor, helped in the holistic evaluation.

Residents cohort

The program involved 26 radiology residents i.e. 8 first year residents and 6 residents each in year II, III and IV.

Assessment process

Regular meetings of the assessors were held prior to the activity to review cases and establish standardized scoring criteria, thereby minimizing assessment bias. Following the Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) format, core clinical findings were evaluated using a pass/fail criterion. All four evaluating faculty members were trained and calibrated to ensure consistent application of these criteria, enabling each assessor to reliably determine a pass or fail based on resident performance. Each resident was assessed on 10 cases, and a minimum of 8 passes was required for promotion to the next residency year.

Residents in each year were evaluated using 10 anonymized cases aligned with the EPAs designated for their training level (Table 2). Each resident independently interpreted the cases on a reporting workstation, documenting their findings. One faculty per year of residency was assigned to achieve consistent assessment as everyone was evaluated by same faculty. This ensured that all residents of the same year were assessed using the same scoring system, which may have contributed to consistency in evaluations with the same level of complexity. The faculty members conducted interactive feedback sessions after the assessments. The focus in these sessions was to provide a detailed response on the residents’ performance by facilitating open questions, and clarifications along with identification of areas for improvement.

Scoring and feedback

Each resident was assessed on 10 equally weighted cases, with no cumulative or numerical scoring system applied. Each case was evaluated independently by the assigned faculty member and marked as pass or fail based on the resident’s ability to identify key findings, perform the required tasks, and meet the defined performance standards. Residents were required to pass at least 8 out of 10 cases to qualify for promotion to the next residency year. Those who did not meet this criterion received targeted feedback and underwent a structured remediation process, which involved (1) updating their theoretical knowledge through guided self-study, (2) practicing additional cases and documenting these in a logbook, and (3) reappearing for the EPA assessment after three months, which they successfully passed. Feedback from both faculty and residents was collected following the activity.

Results

All 26 residents participated in the assessments. Twenty-four residents (92%) successfully passed by achieving a pass in at least 8 out of 10 cases. Two residents who did not meet this criterion received individualized feedback and underwent a structured remediation process. Both residents successfully passed the subsequent reassessment. Overall, residents and assessors provided positive feedback on the process, citing the interactive format as highly beneficial. Feedback also highlighted the need for improved time management and anonymized case preparation.

Discussion

The introduction of EPAs in radiology residency programs marks a significant shift towards competency-based training. By linking specific tasks to essential competencies, EPAs offer a structured way to assess residents’ readiness to perform clinical tasks independently [1]. This commentary supports the idea that EPAs enhance the alignment of training with real-world clinical practice, as demonstrated by the use of case-based assessments tailored to residents’ training levels.

EPAs serve as a link between theoretical knowledge and practical skills, ensuring that trainees are evaluated based on the competencies critical for their profession. This approach aligns with the work of Carraccio et al. [2], who advocated for competency-based models in which progression is determined by mastery of key tasks rather than time spent in training. The use of case-based assessments, such as interpreting chest X-rays, CT scans, MRIs, and ultrasounds, allows residents to demonstrate their clinical decision-making skills in a realistic setting.

While feedback from residents and faculty was largely positive, we identified challenges related to time management and case preparation. These issues are consistent with critiques of competency-based education, where logistical hurdles can impact assessment quality [3]. To address these challenges, we suggest improving time allocation, standardizing reporting formats, and refining case preparation to ensure fair and effective evaluations.

Although EPAs are a robust tool for assessing interpretive skills, evaluating procedural skills effectively remains a challenge. While DOPS is an effective formative assessment tool for procedures [4], there is still a need to establish clear, experience-based eligibility criteria to mitigate the risk of complications during procedures performed by the trainees. Issues such as the availability of trained supervisors, the potential bias of performing procedures under self-selected assessors, and the awareness of being assessed have introduced biases and limitations in DOPS [5]. However, residents should have allocated time to ensure the task completion.

The implementation of EPAs and the associated administrative tasks require time for execution. Compared to the traditional summative examinations, the assessments for competency-based medical education (CBME) comprises of multiple assessment methods with generation of abundant data that requires time for compilation and analysis. Successful implementation entails faculty development, alignment with the curriculum and stakeholders i.e. faculty and trainees’ orientation. The other requirements being the readiness for change, infrastructure, IT support etc [6].

Conclusion

The EPA-based learning and assessment framework was successfully implemented for radiology residents, offering a robust method for evaluating core competencies. Structured teaching/learning, assessments and interactive feedback improved resident engagement and learning outcomes, laying the foundation for continued improvements in residency training.

Several strategies can be applied to address the challenges in the implementation of CBME like stakeholders’ collaboration, engagement, faculty development etc. However, additional research on radiology CBME is needed to highlight the specific advantages of this educational strategy, ensuring patient safety and maintaining the academic standards of the institution and program.

Solar heat transport analysis of Falkner Skan Casson ternary hybrid nanoparticles over a permeable wedge
Authors: Khan Mohsin, Sheheryar Shah, Amra Al Kenany, Meshal Shutaywi, M N Abrar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the modern era, energy has become a vital concern across all sectors, while efficient energy generation remains a significant challenge for scientists and engineers. To overcome this issue, researchers have developed ternary hybrid nanoparticles, which exhibit enhanced thermal and physical characteristics compared to conventional mono- and binary nanoparticles. Motivated by these advantages, the present study investigates the flow of a thermally radiating Falkner-Skan Casson ternary hybrid nanofluid over a permeable wedge. Moreover, the flow model is developed by considering the combined influences of magnetohydrodynamics, thermal radiation, Joule heating, and viscous dissipation. The formulated mathematical model, consisting of a system of highly nonlinear ordinary differential equations, was numerically solved using the Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg 45 (RKF-45) method in conjunction with a shooting technique. Practically, such mathematical models are widely applicable across modern engineering industries, particularly in energy conversion and thermal systems such as solar HVAC systems, solar thermal collectors, nuclear reactors, and advanced cooling technologies, where efficient heat transfer is of critical importance. Effects of pertinent parameters on flow velocity and temperature distributions are presented and discussed for three wedge positions (i.e. static wedge, forward and backward movements of wedge). Few of the pertinent results are: (a) it is noted that the skin friction coefficient increase effectively for the growing magnitude of Darcy number for all three scenario of wedge ( ), (b) it is observed that the temperature profile increases effectively for the enlarging values of radiation parameter and (c) the magnitude of Nusselt number coefficient decreases effectivly with the growing magnitude of Eckert and Prandtl number.Keywords: Ternary hybrid nanofluid, thermal radiation, & Casson fluid

Introduction

In today’s era, energy has become the backbone of human development and survival, driving progress in every sphere of life. From powering industries, transportation, and communication networks to meeting domestic needs such as heating, cooling, and lighting, energy is indispensable. With rapid urbanization, technological advancement, and population growth, the demand for reliable and sustainable energy is increasing at an unprecedented rate. Moreover, the global shift towards digitalization and automation has further intensified our dependence on uninterrupted energy supply. At the same time, the challenges of depleting natural resources and rising energy demands highlight the urgent need for a transition towards sustainable energy sources. Thus, energy is not only a fundamental requirement for economic growth and improved living standards but also a crucial factor in ensuring long-term sustainability and securing the future of upcoming generations. To address the issue, scientists and researchers are looking for sustainable energy solutions in terms of nanofluids. Nanofluids are engineered colloidal suspensions of nanoparticles in conventional fluid (e.g., water, oil). These fluids exhibit improved thermal properties compared to conventional fluids, making them highly effective in heat transfer applications due to their large surface area and the continuous random motion of nanoparticles. The conceptual framework of nanofluid as a thermal heat transfer was introduced by Choi [1] in 1995. Nanofluids provide higher heat transfer rates as compared to the conventional fluids, making them ideal for modern thermal management applications. Nanofluids have gained significant attention due to their wide range of practical and demanding applications across various fields. For instance, they play a vital role in solar energy systems [2] by enhancing the thermal efficiency of solar collectors. In cooling systems [3], such as electronics, automobile radiators, and nuclear reactors, nanofluids are employed to achieve efficient heat dissipation. Similarly, in industrial heat exchangers [4], including those in power plants, chemical processing, and refrigeration systems, nanofluids improve overall heat transfer performance. Beyond engineering applications, nanofluids are also making strides in targeted drug delivery [5], where functionalized nanoparticles can selectively target diseased cells (e.g., cancer cells), thereby minimizing toxicity and reducing side effects. In the energy sector, geothermal systems [6] utilize nanofluids in underground heat exchangers to enhance energy extraction efficiency. Moreover, in HVAC and refrigeration systems [7], nanofluids significantly improve heat transfer in air conditioning, refrigeration, and heat pump applications. Finally, the automotive industry [8] employs nanofluids in radiators, engine cooling, and exhaust gas recirculation systems, leading to improved thermal management and fuel efficiency.

In view of the above cited applications, the study of mono and hybrid nanofluids has become a highly sought-after research area for scientists and engineers. Samad et al. [9] numerically analyzed the heat transfer abilities of Casson nanofluid using the thermophoresis and Brownian motion phenomena. The study describes that the flow energy increases with an increment in the heat transfer Biot number. Razzaq et al. [11] numerically examined the heat transport characteristics for the magneto-hybrid nanofluid using the built-in commands of MATLAB. Abrar et al. [12] numerically investigated the flow of tangent hyperbolic hybrid nanofluid, while maintaining the low/weak concentration of micro-elements. In the end of 2nd decade of the 21st century, scientists began to recognize the limitations of hybrid nanofluids in terms of stability, thermal conductivity, and overall performance for advanced engineering applications. To overcome these challenges, the concept of ternary hybrid nanofluids was introduced, which involves the suspension of three different types of nanoparticles in a base fluid. This novel formulation not only enhances thermal conductivity and heat transfer characteristics but also improves stability, rheological behavior, and adaptability across diverse industrial, biomedical, and energy systems. Thermal radiation is the process of heat transfer through electromagnetic waves, primarily in the infrared spectrum, without requiring a physical medium. It plays a significant role in many engineering and industrial applications, particularly where conduction and convection are limited. In the context of heat transfer, thermal radiation is vital in high-temperature systems such as furnaces, boilers, and solar collectors, ensuring efficient energy utilization. Within the HVAC industry, controlling and optimizing thermal radiation is crucial for maintaining indoor comfort, reducing heat losses, and improving the energy efficiency of air conditioning and refrigeration systems. Sheikholeslami and Ghasemi [16] numerically analyzed the heat transfer mechanism for the unsteady nanofluid flow using the finite element technique. Rahman et al. [17] numerically analyzed the heat transfer characteristics of a thermally radiative fluid flow over a linearly stretching vertical plate. It was observed that both the heat transfer Biot number and the radiation parameter contributed to a reduction in the skin friction coefficient. Venkatesh et al. [18] analyzed the heat and mass transfer analysis for the viscous fluid flow of thermally radiative heat flux. It was found that the temperature distributions become flatter with an increase in the Prandtl number. Saini et al. [19] investigated the steady MHD flow of viscous fluid under the Boussinesq approximation using the well-known Chebyshev spectral collocation method. The results revealed that a gradual increase in the radiation parameter leads to a more pronounced enhancement in the velocity profile for fluids with variable thermal conductivity than for those with variable viscosity. Sreehari et al. [20] explored the entropy generation phenomena in Casson fluid flow over a stretching sheet under the influence of thermal radiation and magnetohydrodynamics. Chandel and Sharma [21] numerically investigated the impact of nanoparticles on stagnation-point flow induced by power law velocity slip and thermal jump. Furthermore, this study incorporates the effects of viscous dissipation, thermal radiation, and an exponential heat source. The study has potential applications in advanced cooling technologies and aerospace systems. Chandel and Sood [22] conducted a numerical study on blood flow containing ternary hybrid nanoparticles over a permeable stretching sheet, incorporating the effects of thermal radiation as well as suction and injection at the surface. Garalleh [23] investigated the flow of a ternary hybrid nanofluid over a stretching/shrinking wedge geometry. The study further examined the effects of activation energy and chemical reactions on the ternary hybrid nanofluid flow. The results demonstrated that these parameters significantly influenced the flow behavior, heat transfer, and mass transport characteristics. Nasir et al. [24] developed a computational framework to investigate the integration of Sutterby magneto-hybrid nanofluid (SMHNF) boundary layer flow in solar-powered heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems, aiming to enhance thermal performance and energy efficiency using artificial intelligence techniques. Razzaq et al. [25] investigated a Casson fluid model for mixed convection flow using an artificial neural network framework in a three-dimensional flow geometry, with a particular focus on mixed convection heat transfer effects.

Non-Newtonian fluids have attracted considerable attention in fluid mechanics due to their complex rheological behavior [26], in which the relationship between shear stress and shear rate is nonlinear. Unlike Newtonian fluids [27], these fluids do not exhibit constant viscosity and are widely encountered in industrial, biological, and engineering applications. Among various non-Newtonian models, the Casson fluid [28] model has emerged as an important class due to its ability to describe yield-stress characteristics. The Casson fluid behaves like a solid until a critical yield stress is exceeded, after which it flows like a viscous fluid. This property makes it particularly suitable for modeling blood flow [29], as well as other suspensions such as inks [30], paints [31], and polymeric liquids [32]. Owing to its practical relevance, Casson fluid flow has been extensively studied under different physical effects [33–35], including magnetic fields, thermal radiation, porous media, and nanoparticle suspensions, to better understand its transport and heat transfer behavior in engineering and biomedical systems.

To date, no study has examined the impact of solar-HVAC performance using Falkner-Skan Casson ternary hybrid nanofluid flow over a permeable wedge. In this work, the Rosseland approximation is employed to model the thermal radiation heat transfer in optically thick medium. The primary objective of this study is to numerically investigate the behavior of a thermally radiating Falkner-Skan Casson ternary hybrid nanofluid over a porous wedge, while incorporating the combined effects of magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), Joule heating, and viscous dissipation. The analysis is carried out using nanoparticles to enhance the thermal characteristics of the base fluid and to improve heat transfer performance. The study aims to provide a detailed understanding of the flow and heat transfer mechanisms under the influence of these significant physical effects. The governing mathematical model is numerically solved using the RKF-45 method in conjunction with a shooting technique. Such models hold great significance in real-world industries, including solar-HVAC systems, solar thermal systems, nuclear reactors, and advanced cooling technologies. Numerical schemes

The nonlinear system of Eqs. (7–9) does not admit a closed-form analytical solution due to its inherent complexity and the presence of nonlinear, coupled terms involving velocity and temperature functions. Therefore, a robust numerical technique, the Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg method of order 45 (RKF-45), is employed to obtain the approximate solution. To initiate the solution procedure, the initial guesses for the unknown boundary conditions are estimated using the shooting method. This method provides adaptive step-size control and high accuracy, making it suitable for solving stiff and nonlinear boundary value problems. Prior to the application of RKF-45, the boundary value problem is transformed into an initial value problem (IVP) using the shooting method. This technique involves guessing the unknown initial conditions; typically derivatives at the wall and iteratively refining them to ensure that the boundary conditions at infinity are satisfied. The accuracy of this approach depends heavily on the choice of initial guesses and the convergence of the iterative scheme, which is achieved here using a combination of Newton-Raphson-based updates and tolerance-based stopping criteria. Figure 3b highlights the influence of the magnetic parameter on the dimensionless velocity profile for all three wedge configurations. It is clearly observed that an increase in the magnetic parameter results in a significant reduction in the velocity profile across all cases. This behavior can be physically explained through the action of the Lorentz force, which is generated due to the presence of applied magnetic field. The Lorentz force acts as a resistive drag force that opposes the fluid motion, thereby decelerating the flow. Consequently, the suppression effect becomes more pronounced as the magnetic field strength increases. This trend remains consistent for all three wedge scenarios, although the extent of velocity reduction varies depending on the wedge motion relative to the flow direction. Among them, the highest velocity occurs when the wedge moves with the flow (for ), while the lowest velocity is observed when the wedge opposes the flow direction (for ).

Figure 3c illustrates the effect of the Darcy number on the dimensionless velocity profile. The Darcy number represents the permeability of the porous medium; lower values indicate a more resistive medium, while higher values correspond to higher permeability. Interestingly, the graph shows that as the Darcy number increases, the velocity profile decreases. This behavior indicates that in the present configuration, higher permeability enhances the resistance to flow, possibly due to stronger interactions between the fluid and the porous structure or due to dominant viscous drag effects within the medium. As a result, the fluid slows down more significantly when \:Da increases, contrary to classical porous flow assumptions.

Figure 3d depicts the effect of the wedge angle parameter on the dimensionless velocity profile. Variations in reflect changes in the wedge angle, which influence how the flow accelerates or decelerates along the permeable wedge. The results demonstrate that by increasing the wedge angle parameter leads to an enhancement in the velocity profile. Physically, larger values of (corresponding to larger wedge angles) are associated with a more favorable pressure gradient, which promotes acceleration of the boundary layer flow. In contrast, smaller values introduce an adverse pressure gradient, increasing flow resistance and reducing the velocity near the wall.

Figure 4a illustrates the influence of the Prandtl number on the dimensionless temperature profile in a Casson hybrid nanofluid flow over a wedge surface. The wedge geometry is characterized by a pressure gradient parameter, which governs the nature of the external flow field, while the fluid exhibits non-Newtonian behavior due to the Casson model. In addition, thermal transport is enhanced through the suspension of hybrid nanoparticles. The results indicate that an increase in the Prandtl number leads to a rise in the temperature profile within the thermal boundary layer. Physically, higher Prandtl numbers correspond to lower thermal diffusivity relative to momentum diffusivity, which thickens the thermal boundary layer and intensifies heat retention within the fluid. As a result, heat diffusion becomes less dominant, allowing higher temperature levels to persist in the flow region.

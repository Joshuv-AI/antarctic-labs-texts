On minimizing the Wiener index of unicyclic graphs with fixed girth and given degree sequence


Abstract
. The Wiener index of a graph is the sum of all the distances between any pair of vertices. We aim to describe graphs which minimize the Wiener index among all unicyclic graphs with fixed girth and given degree sequence. Depending on where the centroid of the graph is, we will present three candidates for the minimization, namely the greedy unicyclic graph, the cycle-centered graph and the out-greedy unicyclic graph.

1.
Introduction

The Wiener index, which is the sum of the distances between all pairs of vertices, was conceived by the chemist Wiener in 1947
[
15
]
, who introduced the idea to predict chemical and physical properties of a compound by analysing the structure of its molecules using techniques from graph theory. The Wiener index has attracted considerable attention from researchers in mathematics and chemistry, and it continues to be studied extensively. Many researchers have focused on the class of trees (see for example
[
3
]
), because many graphs arising from molecules are trees, and because they have a relatively simple structure. The Wiener index of trees with a given degree sequence has been studied by several authors, for example
[
14
,
18
]
. For unicyclic graphs (graphs obtained from trees by adding a single edge), the Wiener index was first considered by Gutman and al.
[
7
]
, later some authors investigated various restrictions such as given diameter
[
4
]
, given matching number
[
5
,
2
,
1
]
, a given girth
[
16
]
, a given number of pendant or cut vertices
[
12
]
, and a given bipartition
[
10
,
9
]
. To our knowledge, the Wiener index of unicyclic graphs with a given degree sequence has not been studied in the literature, and it is the main motivation of this paper to fill this gap. Some of the recent works on unicyclic graphs for a distance-based invariant can be found in
[
6
]
, where they focused on fixed diameters and in
[
17
]
, where they characterized unicyclic graph with given maximum degree.

All graphs considered in this paper are simple, finite and undirected. Let
G
be a graph, and
u,v
be vertices of
G
. The
distance
between
u
and
v
, denoted by
d_{G}(u,v)
(we may omit the
G
if there is no confusion) is the length of the shortest path between
u
and
v
. Moreover, the

distance of a vertex

u
in
G
, denoted by
D_{G}(u)
, is the sum of distances from
u
and
all other vertices of
G
. The subgraph of
G
induced by the vertices with minimum
distance is called the
centroid
of
G
, and is denoted by
M(G)
. The Wiener index can be formally written as follows:

\displaystyle W(G)=\sum_{\{u,v\}}d_{G}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u\in V(G)}D_{G}(u).

The paper is structured as follows: in Section
2
, we will recall results obtained for trees with given degree sequence. Since trees and unicyclic graphs only differ by one edge, it is necessary to understand the structure of the tree that minimizes the Wiener index. Next, in Section
3
, we will discuss the position of the vertices on the cycle itself according to their subtrees/degrees. Before attacking the vertices outside the cycle, we need to comprehend the influence of adding an edge to a tree to get a unicyclic graph, which will be in Section
4
. In Section
5
, we then figure out the position of the vertices not belonging to the cycle. Lastly, in section
6
, we characterize the graphs that minimizes the Wiener index in the set
\mathbb{U}_{D}^{\gamma}
, which is the set of all unicyclic graphs with given degree sequence
D
and fixed girth
\gamma
.

The following lemma describes the

Lemma 3

In an optimal tree, for a maximal path with labelling as Figure

Remark 4

Let
S_{n}
be the set of all permutations of
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Let
A=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

B=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
be sequences of nonnegative numbers. We say that

majorizes

\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}.

If for any

(a_{\sigma(1)},\dots,a_{\sigma(n)})

A\preceq B.

In

Theorem 5

(
[

Let

Structure of the optimal graph for the vertices along the cycle

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph and

be its cycle. After removing the edges of

In
[
5

(1)

\displaystyle W(G)

\displaystyle=\left(n-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+(\gamma-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma}W(T_{i})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\gamma}\ell_{i}\alpha_{j}+\ell_{i}\ell_{j}d_{C_{\gamma}}(v_{i},v_{j})+\ell_{j}\alpha_{i},

where

and

.

Lemma 6
.

In the
(x_{0},y_{0})
-labelling, if
|V(X_{i})|\geq|V(Y_{i})|
for
i=1,\dots,k_{1}
,
|V(X^{\prime}_{>})|\geq|V(Y^{\prime}_{>})|
, and
|V(X^{\prime}_{i})|\geq|V(Y^{\prime}_{i})|
for

(2)

|V(X_{0})|\geq|V(Y_{0})|

in an optimal unicyclic graph.

Proof.
.

Suppose for contradiction that (
2
) does not hold. We will prove that switching
X_{0}
and
Y_{0}
will not increase the Wiener index.
In this operation, the distances between vertices only change if exactly one of the end vertices is in

However, for a labelling with

(resp.
z^{\prime}
), the distance with one end vertex in

(resp.

) and a vertex in
V(X_{0})\cup V(Y_{0})
does not change.

For any vertex in
X_{i}

(i=1,\dots,k_{1})
and any vertex in
X_{0}
(resp.
Y_{0}
), this operation increases (resp. decreases) the distance by

. On the other hand, for any vertex in
Y_{i}

(i=1,\dots,k_{1})
and any vertex in
X_{0}
(resp.
Y_{0}
), the distance decreases (resp. increases) by the same amount

.

Similarly, the same holds for any vertex in
X^{\prime}_{>}
and any vertex in
X_{0}
(resp.
Y_{0}
) and for any vertex in
Y^{\prime}_{>}
and any vertex in
X_{0}
(resp.

), with the amount of change which is now instead

.

Finally, for any vertex in
X^{\prime}_{i}

(i=\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma}{2}\right\rfloor-\delta+1,\dots,k_{2})
and any vertex in
X_{0}
(resp.
Y_{0}
and for any vertex in
Y^{\prime}_{i}

(i=\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma}{2}\right\rfloor-\delta+1,\dots,k_{2})

(resp.

Thus the total amount of changes that occur is

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}(\delta-2i)(|V(X_{i})|-|V(Y_{i})|)(|V(X_{0})|-|V(Y_{0})|)

\displaystyle+\delta(|V(X^{\prime}_{>})|-|V(Y^{\prime}_{>})|)(|V(X_{0})|-|V(Y_{0})|)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma}{2}\right\rfloor-\delta+1}^{k_{2}}(\gamma-\delta-2i)(|V(X^{\prime}_{i})|-|V(Y^{\prime}_{i})|(|V(X_{0})|-|V(Y_{0})|)\leq 0.

In an optimal unicyclic graph labelled as in Figure

\displaystyle|X^{\prime}_{>}|=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|V(U_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}|V(W_{i})|=|Y^{\prime}_{>}|

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}|V(U_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|V(W_{i})|

which yields a contradiction.
∎

Claim 2:

Proof of Claim 2.

Suppose for contradiction that

\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}|V(U_{i})|\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}|V(W_{i})|

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}|V(U_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}|V(W_{i})|

\displaystyle|V(U_{2})|+\sum_{i=3}^{k-1}|V(U_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=3}^{k-1}|V(W_{i})|+|V(W_{2})|

\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{k-1}|V(U_{i})|

We use the same approach as in Claim 2, to prove that if

Moreover, the following theorem from Hardy, Littlewood and Pólya
[

that connects non-negative sequences and bilinear forms will play a central role in the upcoming lemmas. For a sequence

of the form
a_{-n},\dots a_{-n+1},\dots,a_{-1},a_{0}

,a_{1},\dots

a_{n-1},a_{n}
, let

be the sequence obtained by rearranging the elements so that

a^{+}_{0}\geq a^{+}_{1}\geq a^{+}_{-1}\geq a^{+}_{2}\geq a^{+}_{-2}\geq\cdots\geq a^{+}_{n}\geq a^{+}_{-n}.

Theorem 13

(
[
8
, Theorem 371]
)
.

Suppose that
c,x
, and
y
are non-negative sequences, and
c
is symmetrically decreasing, so that

c_{0}\geq c_{1}=c_{-1}\geq c_{2}=c_{-2}\geq\cdots\geq c_{2k}=c_{-2k},

while the
x
and
y
are given except for their order. Then the bilinear
form

\sum_{r=-k}^{k}\sum_{s=-k}^{k}c_{r-s}x_{r}y_{s}

attains its maximum when
x
is
x^{+}
and
y
is
y^{+}
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{U}_{D}^{\gamma}
, be optimal. Let
T_{1}
be the tree obtained from
G
by removing the edge
e_{1}=u_{k}w_{k-1}
(resp.
u_{k}w_{k}
) and
T_{2}
a tree obtained by removing any other edge

on
C_{\gamma}
. Then,
W(T_{1})\leq W(T_{2})
.

Proof.
.

The proof basically follows the argument found in
[
14
]
. The Wiener index of a tree can be written as
W(T)=\sum_{uv\in E(T)}n_{uv}(T)n_{vu}(T)
, where
n_{uv}(T)
is the number of vertices closer to
u
after removing
uv
and
n_{vu}(T)
the number of vertices closer to
v
. We may notice that the contribution of any edges not on
C_{\gamma}\backslash\{e_{1}\}
or
C_{\gamma}\backslash\{e_{2}\}
remains the same for both cases. The only difference is then for the edges along
C_{\gamma}\backslash\{e_{1}\}
and
C_{\gamma}\backslash\{e_{2}\}
. We will end up with a bilinear form and from Theorem
13
, the contribution on the Wiener index of
T_{1}
from
C_{\gamma}\backslash\{e_{1}\}
is smaller than the contribution of the Wiener index of
T_{2}
from
C_{\gamma}\backslash\{e_{2}\}
.
∎

4.
Difference between Wiener indices of trees and unicyclic graphs

Let
G\in\mathbb{U}_{D}^{\gamma}
and label its cycle by
C_{\gamma}=u_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{k}(w_{k})w_{k-1}\dots w_{2}w_{1}
.
Let
e
be an edge of
C_{\gamma}
. From Lemma
14
, we may assume
e=u_{k}w_{k-1}
(resp.
u_{k}w_{k}
). After the removal of the edge
e
we are left with a tree
T_{G}
. Let us compute the difference between the Wiener indices of the unicyclic graph
G
and the corresponding tree
T_{G}
.

Lemma 16
.

Let
G
be any unicyclic graph with cycle
C_{\gamma}=u_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{k}(w_{k})

w_{k-1}\dots w_{1}u_{1}
and
e=u_{k}w_{k-1}
(resp.
u_{k}w_{k}
). Let
T_{G}
be the tree obtained after the removal of the edge
e
. We set
\Delta(G)=W(T_{G})-W(G)
. Then, for a cycle of even order
2k
:

\displaystyle\Delta(G)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=k+2-i}^{k}2(i+j-k-1)|V(U_{i})||V(W_{j})|,

and for a cycle of odd order
2k-1
:

\displaystyle\Delta(G)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{j=k+1-i}^{k-1}(2(i+j-k)-1)|V(U_{i})||V(W_{j})|.

Proof.
.

For a pair of vertices
(x,y)
in
G
, the distances in the unicyclic graph and the new obtained tree remain the same, i.e.,
d_{G}(x,y)=d_{T_{G}}(x,y)
unless we use the edge
e
as a “shortcut”. In that case
d_{T_{G}}(x,y)>d_{G}(x,y)=d_{T_{G}}(x,u_{k})+1+d_{T_{G}}(w_{k-1},y)
for an even cycle and
d_{T_{G}}(x,y)>d_{G}(x,y)=d_{T_{G}}(x,u_{k})+1+d_{T_{G}}(w_{k},y)
for an odd cycle. The latter happens if and only if
x\in U_{i}
and
y\in W_{j}
such that
d_{T}(u_{i},w_{j})>k
and the difference is exactly:

\displaystyle d_{T_{G}}(x,y)-d_{G}(x,y)

\displaystyle=d_{T_{G}}(u_{i},w_{j})-d_{G}(u_{i},w_{j})

\displaystyle=d_{T_{G}}(u_{i},u_{1})+1+d_{T_{G}}(w_{1},v_{j})

\displaystyle-d_{T_{G}}(u_{i},u_{k})-d_{T_{G}}(v_{j},v_{k})-1

\displaystyle=i-1+1+j-1-k+i-k+j-1

\displaystyle=2(i+j-k-1),

for an even cycle
2k
and

\displaystyle d_{T_{G}}(x,y)-d_{G}(x,y)

\displaystyle=d_{T_{G}}(u_{i},w_{j})-d_{G}(u_{i},w_{j})

\displaystyle=d_{T_{G}}(u_{i},u_{1})+1+d_{T_{G}}(w_{1},w_{j})

\displaystyle-d_{T_{G}}(u_{i},u_{k})-d_{T_{G}}(w_{j},w_{k-1})-1

\displaystyle=i-1+1+j-1-k+i-k+j

\displaystyle=2(i+j-k)-1,

for an odd cycle
2k-1
.
Hence, the statement holds.
∎

Lemma 17
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{U}_{D}^{\gamma}
with
G=C_{\gamma}(U_{1},\dots,U_{k},(W_{k}),W_{k-1},\dots,W_{1},U_{1})
and
e=u_{k}w_{k-1}
(resp.
u_{k}w_{k}
). The difference
\Delta(G)
is maximized if the subtrees along the cycle respect the following arrangement:

|V(U_{1})|\leq|V(W_{1})|\leq|V(U_{2})|\leq|V(W_{2})|\leq\cdots\leq|V(U_{k})|\leq|V(W_{k})|,

for an even cycle
2k
and

|V(U_{1})|\leq|V(W_{1})|\leq|V(U_{2})|\leq|V(V_{2})|\leq\cdots\leq|V(U_{k-1})|\leq|V(W_{k-1})|\leq|V(U_{k})|,

for an odd cycle
2k-1
.

Proof.
.

We denote by
A
the sequence whose indices are from
-k
to
k-1
(resp.
k
) such that

\displaystyle A_{-k}

\displaystyle=|V(U_{1})|,A_{-k+1}=|V(U_{2})|,\dots,A_{-1}=|V(U_{k})|,

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=|V(W_{k})|,A_{1}=|V(W_{k-1})|,A_{2}=|V(W_{k-2})|,\dots,A_{k-1}=|V(W_{1})|,

for a cycle of even order
2k
and

\displaystyle A_{-k+1}

\displaystyle=|V(U_{1})|,A_{-k+1}=|V(U_{2})|,\dots,A_{-1}=|V(U_{k-1})|,

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=|V(U_{k})|,A_{1}=|V(W_{k-1})|,A_{2}=|V(W_{k-2})|,\dots,A_{k-1}=|V(W_{1})|,

for a cycle of odd order
2k-1
.
We provide only the proof for an even cycle, the other case can be shown in a similar manner. By a change of indices, from Lemma
16
we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta(G)=\sum_{r=-k+1}^{-1}\sum_{s=0}^{r+k-1}2(k-(s-r))A_{r}A_{s}.

Since
A_{r}A_{s}=A_{s}A_{r}
, we set

d_{r-s}=d_{s-r}=\begin{cases}k-|s-r|,&|s-r|\leq k-1\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Then,

\displaystyle\Delta(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=-k+1}^{-1}\sum_{s=0}^{r+k-1}2(k-(s-r))A_{r}A_{s}=\sum_{r=-k+1}^{-1}\sum_{0\leq s-r\leq k-1}2(k-(s-r))A_{r}A_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=-k+1}^{-1}\sum_{0\leq s-r\leq k-1}(k-(s-r))A_{r}A_{s}+\sum_{s=-k+1}^{-1}\sum_{0\leq r-s\leq k-1}(k-(r-s))A_{r}A_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=-k}^{k-1}\sum_{s=-k}^{k-1}d_{r-s}A_{r}A_{s},

where
d_{r-s}
is symmetrically decreasing:

\displaystyle d_{0}=k\geq d_{1}=d_{-1}=k-1\geq d_{2}=d_{-2}=k-2

\displaystyle\geq\cdots\geq d_{k-1}=d_{-k+1}=1\geq d_{k}=d_{-k}=\cdots d_{2k-1}=d_{-2k+1}=0.

Hence, by using Theorem
13
,
\Delta
attains its maximum if
A
is
A^{+}
. Therefore,

|V(U_{1})|\leq|V(W_{1})|\leq|V(W_{2})|\leq\cdots\leq|V(U_{k})|=|V(W_{k})|.

∎

That concludes the proof of Case 2 and the whole claim holds.
∎

5.2.
Interplay between
\mathfrak{L}_{i_{0},s}
and
\mathfrak{G}_{i_{0}}

We have seen that in order to minimize
W(T_{G})
, we must have Equations
5
for Case 1 and
6
for Case 2. We perfomed a switch operation between a branch along the cycle and one outside the cycle. However, the same operation will decrease
\Delta(G)
and the reverse will increase
\Delta(G)
, this later is called “gain”. In other words,
let us again fix
i_{0}\in\{2,\dots,k\}
and
i_{1}\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
such that
i_{0}>i_{1}
. We now assume that
|V(W_{i_{0}})|<|V(Z_{i_{1}})|
(resp.
|V(U_{i_{0}})|<|V(Z_{i_{1}})|
). We observe that switching back the branches
W_{i_{0}}
and
Z_{i_{1}}
(resp.
U_{i_{0}}
and
Z_{i_{1}}
) will increase
\Delta(G)
. From Lemma
16
, we can compute the maximum gain
\mathfrak{G}_{i_{0}}
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{i_{0}}=\sum_{j=k-i_{0}+2}^{k}2(j-(k-i_{0})-1)|V(U_{j})|\left(|V(Z_{i_{1}})|-|V(W_{i_{0}})|\right),\quad\text{in Case 1}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{i_{0}}=\sum_{j=k-i_{0}+2}^{k}2(j-(k-i_{0})-1)|V(W_{j})|\left(|V(Z_{i_{1}})|-|V(U_{i_{0}})|\right),\quad\text{in Case 2}.

We now can compare the amount of decrease of the Wiener index of
T_{G}
, called a gain and the decrease by
\Delta(G)
called the loss.

Remark 21
.

If
i_{0}
increases, the gain
\mathfrak{G}_{i_{0}}
will increase.
If
s
increases, the loss
\mathfrak{L}_{i_{0},s}
will increase.

We get the following corollaries:

Corollary 22
.

If the loss exceeds the gain, it implies that the optimal unicyclic graph satified the concavity of all maximal paths in
T_{G}
. In other words, the construction of the optimal graph starts first from a greedy tree
T_{G}
, where we will then add the edge to vertices with lowest degrees (leaves if possible).

Corollary 23
.

If the gain exceeds the loss, this implies, all the highest degrees were given to the vertices on the cycle.

Assume that Corollary
23
applies, namely all the highest degrees correspond to vertices on the cycle. Those vertices are already ordered as in Theorem
10
in the optimal graph. The ordering of the rest of the vertices is performed as follows:

Lemma 24
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{U}_{D}^{\gamma}
and
G=C_{\gamma}(T_{1},\dots,T_{\gamma})
. Let
T^{\prime}
be the tree obtained from
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{\gamma}
by identifying all the roots
v_{i}
as a single vertex
r
in such a way that
|V(T_{1})|\geq|V(T_{2})|\geq\cdots\geq|V(T_{\gamma})|
.
Let
\mathcal{G}(T^{\prime})
be the greedy tree with the same degree sequence as
T^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{G}(T^{\prime})
can be decomposed as
T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2},\dots,T^{\prime}_{\gamma}
such that the degrees of the roots of
T_{i}
and
T^{\prime}_{i}
remain the same.
Let
G^{\prime}=C_{\gamma}(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2},\dots,T^{\prime}_{\gamma})
.
Then
W(G)\geq W(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.
.

Substituting
T^{\prime}
in Equation
1
, we have

\displaystyle W(G)=W(T^{\prime})+\left(n-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+(\gamma-1)\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\gamma}\ell_{i}\ell_{j}d_{C_{\gamma}}(v_{i},v_{j}).

Let us denote
\alpha^{\prime}_{i}=D_{\mathcal{G}(T^{\prime})}(v_{i})
, and
\ell_{i}^{\prime}=|V(T_{i}^{\prime})|-1
where
T_{i}^{\prime}
are the branches in the greedy tree
\mathcal{G}(T^{\prime})
. As the girth
\gamma
is fixed, the term
\left(n-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left\lfloor\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
is invariant under the reshuffling of the vertices. From Theorem
2
,

W(T^{\prime})\geq W(\mathcal{G}(T^{\prime})).

Moreover,

\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma}\alpha_{i}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma}\alpha^{\prime}_{i},

(note that this is true only if vertices with highest degrees are on the cycle) and the sequence
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{\gamma})
is majorized by
(\ell^{\prime}_{1},\ell^{\prime}_{2},\dots,\ell^{\prime}_{\gamma})
which implies

\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\gamma}\ell_{i}\ell_{j}d_{C_{\gamma}}(v_{i},v_{j})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{\gamma}\ell^{\prime}_{i}\ell^{\prime}_{j}d_{C_{\gamma}}(v_{i},v_{j}).

∎

6.
Optimal unicyclic graphs with given degree sequence and fixed girth

In this section, we will discuss the structure of the optimal unicyclic graphs that minimizes the Wiener index among unicyclic graphs with given degree sequence and fixed girth
\gamma
.
Note that the “optimal” graph may not be unique.

We first need the following lemma on centroids of unicyclic graphs:

Lemma 25
.

(
[
13
]
)
For a unicyclic graph
G
with cycle
C_{\gamma}
, the centroid
M(G)
is either a
K_{1}
or
K_{2}
or

Let us build three special graphs, which possess different types of centroids.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
D
be a degree sequence of a unicyclic graph
G
, with fixed girth
\gamma
. The following construction leads to
M(G)\subset C_{\gamma}
and
M(G)
is a
K_{1}
or
K_{2}
. We call such graph “the greedy unicyclic graph”, and it is denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{u}(D,\gamma)
. It is obtained from the subsequent “greedy algorithm”:

i)

The vertex with largest degree will be on the cycle and will be labelled
v
(the root);

ii)

Label the two neighbors of
v
on the cycle as
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
, and the neighbors of
v
outside the cycle as
v_{3},\dots,
assign the largest degrees available to them such that
\deg(v_{1})\geq\deg(v_{2})\geq\deg(v_{3})\dots
;

iii)

Label the neighbors of
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
(except
v
)
v_{11},v_{12},v_{13},\dots

and
v_{21},v_{22}

,v_{23},\dots
, respectively, with
v_{11}
and
v_{21}
on the cycle. Assign the largest degrees available such that

\deg(v_{11})\geq\deg(v_{21})\geq\deg(v_{12})\geq\deg(v_{13})\geq\dots\deg(v_{21})\geq\dots

iv)

Then label the neighbors of
v_{3},v_{4},\dots
such that they take all the largest degrees available and that
\deg(v_{31})\geq\deg(v_{32})\geq\dots\geq\deg(v_{41})\geq\deg(v_{42})\geq\dots
.

Note that the degree of a vertex on the cycle must be at least 2. Hence, we should keep some greater or equal to 2 degrees for the vertices on the cycle not yet labelled.

v)

Repeat (iii) for all the newly labelled vertices on the cycle, always start with the neighbors of the labeled vertex with largest degree which are on the cycle and whose neighbors are not labeled yet.

vi)

Repeat (iv) for the newly labelled vertices not on the cycle.

vii)

When all the vertices on the cycle have been labelled, continue with the labelling as in Lemma
24
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
D
be a degree sequence of a unicyclic graph
G
, with fixed girth
\gamma

M(G)\subset C_{\gamma}
and
M(G)\neq K_{1},K_{2}
. We call such graph “cycle-centered graph” and it is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(D,\gamma)
and is obtained as follows:

i)

We assign the largest degrees to all the vertices on the cycle
C_{\gamma}
ordered according to Corollary

ii)

We use Lemma

Let

Definition 6.3
.

Let
D

\gamma

Theorem 27
.

Let
D
be a degree sequence and
G\in\mathbb{U}_{D}^{\gamma}

W(G)\geq\min\{W(\mathcal{G}_{u}(D,\gamma)),W(\mathcal{C}(D,\gamma)),W(\mathcal{G}_{u}^{*}(D,\gamma))\}.

Proof.
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{U}_{D}^{\gamma}
. We may assume the vertices on the cycle are ordered as in Theorem
10
. After removing the edge
e
between the lowest degrees, we obtain a tree
T
. The following formula then holds:

W(G)=W(T_{G})-\Delta(G).

In order to minimize
W(G)
, we have to minimize
W(T_{G})
, while still trying to maximize
\Delta(G)
.

We need to discuss the three cases depending on the centroids of the graphs.

Case 1:
M(G)\not\subset C_{\gamma}
. In other words the centroid is either a vertex or an edge not on the cycle. Note that this case only happens if
\frac{\gamma}{2}<h(T_{G})
. After removing the edge

17
and the centroid of the tree will be the centroid of

. On the other hand, to maximize
\Delta(G)
, we need to balance all the vertices on the cycle. The only possible option is to attach as much branches on the cycle and still preserving the ordering on each path. This is exactly the construction of the outside greedy unicyclic graph
\mathcal{G}_{u}^{*}(D,\gamma)
.

Case 2:
M(G)\subset C_{\gamma}\,\text{and is a}\,K_{1}\,\text{or}\,K_{2}

We still have the ordering of all the other paths not from the cycle following Lemma
17
and even for the path obtained from the cycle. This is exactly the construction of the greedy unicyclic graph
\mathcal{G}_{u}(D,\gamma)
.

Case 3:
M(G)\subset C_{\gamma}\,\text{different from}\,K_{1}\,\text{or}\,K_{2}

From Remark
18
and Lemma
24
, the unicyclic graph that maximizes
\Delta(G)
, while having the least
W(T)
is
\mathcal{C}(D,\gamma)
.
∎

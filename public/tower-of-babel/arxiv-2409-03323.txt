Abstract




Abstract

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs. Denote by
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{F})
the maximum number of hyperedges in an
n
-vertex connected
r
-uniform hypergraph which contains no member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subhypergraph.
Denote by
\mathcal{B}C_{k}
the Berge cycle of length
k
, and
by
\mathcal{B}P_{k}
the Berge path of length
k
. Füredi, Kostochka and Luo, and independently Győri, Salia and Zamora determined
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
provided
k
is large enough compared to
r
and
n
is sufficiently large. For the case
k\leq r
, Kostochka and Luo obtained an upper bound for
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
.
In this paper, we continue investigating the case
k\leq r
.
We precisely determine
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
when
n
is sufficiently large and
n
is not a multiple of
r
. For the case
k=r+1
, we determine
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
asymptotically.

Key Words
: Berge cycles; Berge paths; Connected Turán numbers

AMS Subject Classification (2020)
: 05C65, 05C35

1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs.
An
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is called
\mathcal{F}
-free if it does not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as its subhypergraph.
The Turán number of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{F})
, is the maximum number of hyperedges in an
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph.
When
\mathcal{F}
contains only one hypergraph
F
, we write
\ex_{r}(n,F)
, and for
r=2
, we simply write
\ex(n,F)
.
For the graph case, when
G
is not bipartite the asymptotic behavior of
\ex(n,F)
follows from the famous Erdős-Stone-Simonovits Theorem
[
4
,
5
]
.
However, for the case when
F
is bipartite, much less about the
\ex(n,F)
is known (for reference, see the survey
[
12
]
).

In this paper, we are interested in paths and cycles.
Denote by
P_{k}
the path of length
k
, by
C_{k}
the cycle of length
k
and by
\mathcal{C}_{\geq k}
the family of cycles of length at least
k
.
In 1959, Erdős and Gallai
[
3
]
proved the following results on
\ex(n,P_{k})
and
\ex(n,\mathcal{C}_{\geq k})
.

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős and Gallai,
[
3
]
)
.

Fix integers
n
and
k
such that
n\geq k\geq 1
.
Then

\ex(n,P_{k})\leq\frac{(k-1)n}{2}
,
with equality holding if and only if
G
is the disjoint union of complete graphs on
k
vertices.

Theorem 1.2

(Erdős and Gallai,
[
3
]
)
.

Fix integers
n
and
k
such that
n\geq k\geq 3
.
Then,

\ex(n,\mathcal{C}_{\geq k})\leq\frac{(k-1)(n-1)}{2}
.

Later, Faudree and Schelp
[
7
]
determined
\ex(n,P_{k})
for all
n
and
k
, satisfying
n\geq k\geq 1
, with the corresponding extremal graphs.
Note that the extremal graph in Theorem
1.1
is not connected.
By considering connected graphs, in 1977, Kopylov
[
18
]
determined
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}(n,P_{k})
, where
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}(n,P_{k})
denotes the classical Turán number
for connected graphs.

In 2008, Balister, Győri, Lehel and Schelp
[
1
]
improved Kopylov’s result by characterizing the extremal graphs for all
n
.
The stability version of
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}(n,P_{k})
was proved by Füredi, Kostochka and Verstraëte
[
11
]
.
To state their result, we need some further notation. Denote by
K_{k}
and
E_{k}
the complete graph and empty graph on
k
vertices, respectively.
Let
G
and
H
be two disjoint graphs.
Denote by
G\cup H
the union of
G
and
H
, which is the graph with vertex set
V(G)\cup V(H)
and edge set
E(G)\cup E(H)
.
Denote by
G+H
the join of
G
and
H
, which is the graph with vertex set
V(G)\cup V(H)
and edge set
E(G)\cup E(H)\cup\{uv:u\in V(G),v\in V(H)\}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Kopylov,
[
18
]
, Balister et al.,
[
1
]
)
.

Fix integers
n\geq k\geq 4
. Then,

\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}(n,P_{k})=\max\bigg\{\binom{k-1}{2}+(n-k+1),\binom{\left\lceil\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rceil}{2}+\bigg(\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor\bigg)\bigg(n-\left\lceil\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rceil\bigg)\bigg\}.

The extremal graphs are
(K_{k-2}\cup E_{n-k+1})+K_{1}
,
(K_{1}\cup E_{n-\frac{k+1}{2}})+K_{\frac{k-1}{2}}
for odd
k
and
(K_{2}\cup E_{n-\frac{k}{2}-1})+K_{\frac{k}{2}-1}
for even
k
.

It is natural to study the Turán numbers for paths and cycles in an
r
-uniform hypergraph.
Note that there are several ways to define paths and cycles in
r
-uniform hypergraphs for
r\geq 3
.
In this paper, we consider the definition due to Berge.

Definition 1.1
.

A Berge cycle
\mathcal{B}C_{k}
of length
k
in a hypergraph is a set of
k
distinct vertices
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}

and
k
distinct edges
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}\}
such that
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subset e_{i}

with indices taken modulo
k
.
The vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}

are called the defining vertices, and the hyperedges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}
are called the defining hyperedges of the Berge cycle.

Definition 1.2
.

A Berge path
\mathcal{B}P_{k}
of length
k
in a hypergraph is a set of
k+1
distinct vertices
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k+1}\}

and
k
distinct edges
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}\}
such that
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subset e_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
. The vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k+1}

are called the defining vertices and the hyperedges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}
are called the defining hyperedges of the Berge path.

In particular, when the hypergraph under consideration is
2
-uniform, containing a Berge cycle (Berge path)
\mathcal{B}C_{k}
(
\mathcal{B}P_{k}
) is equivalent to containing a cycle
C_{k}
(path
P_{k}
).
Denote by
\mathcal{BC}_{\geq k}
the family of Berge cycles of length at least
k
.

The study of the Turán numbers
\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
was initiated in 2016 by Győri, Katona and Lemons
[
14
]
, who determined these numbers in the cases
k\leq r
and
k>r+1
.
It turned out that the extremal hypergraphs behave very differently when
k\leq r
and
k>r+1
.

Theorem 1.4

(Győri, Katona and Lemons,
[
14
]
)
.

Fix integers
k
and
r
such that
k>r+1>3
. Then

\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})\leq\frac{n}{k}\binom{k}{r}.

Equality holds if and only if
k|n
, and the only extremal
r
-uniform hypergraph is the disjoint union of
\frac{n}{k}

copies of the complete
k
-vertex
r
-uniform hypergraph.
Fix integers
k
and
r
such that
r\geq k>2
. Then

\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})\leq\frac{n}{r+1}(k-1).

Equality holds if and only if
(r+1)|n
, and the extremal
r
-uniform hypergraph is the disjoint union of
\frac{n}{r+1}
sets of
size
r+1
containing
k-1
hyperedges each.

Not much later, Davoodi, Győri, Methuku, and Tompkins
[
2
]
showed that
ex_{r}(n,P_{r+1})\leq n
, which matches the above upper bound of the case
k>r+1
.

Similarly, the function
\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{BC}_{\geq k})
behaves very differently when
k\geq r+2
and
k\leq r+1
with an exceptional third case when
k=r
.
Füredi, Kostochka and Luo
[
8
,
10
]
initiated the study of
\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{BC}_{\geq k})
.
In
[
8
]
, they obtained sharp bounds and extremal constructions for
k\geq r+3\geq 6
and infinitely many
n
.
In
[
10
]
, they obtained the exact bounds and extremal constructions for
k\geq r+4
and all
n
.
Later for the case
k\leq r-1
, Kostochka and Luo
[
19
]
obtained a sharp upper bound for infinitely many
n
.
For the cases
k=r+1
and
k=r+2
, Ergemlidze, Győri, Methuku, Salia, Tompkins and Zamora
[
6
]
obtained sharp bounds.
In 2021, Győri, Lemons, Salia and Zamora
[
15
]
provided a simple proof of Kostochka and Luo’s result of
[
19
]
when
k<r
, and they also determined
\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{BC}_{\geq r})
.

Theorem 1.5

(Füredi, Kostochka and Luo,
[
8
,
10
]
)
.

Fix integers
k
and
r
such that
k\geq r+3\geq 6
.
Then,

\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{BC}_{\geq k})\leq\frac{n-1}{k-2}\binom{k-1}{r}.

Theorem 1.6

(Ergemlidze et al.,

[
6
]
)
.

Fix integers
r
and
k
such that
k\geq 4
.
If
k=r+1
, then
\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{BC}_{\geq r+1})\leq n-1
. If
k=r+2
, then
\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{BC}_{\geq r+2})\leq\frac{(n-1)(r+1)}{r}
.

Theorem 1.7

(Kostochka and Luo,
[
19
]
)
.

Fix integers
k
and
r
such that
r\geq k+1\geq 5
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
r
-uniform multi-hypergraph, each edge of which has multiplicity at most
k-2
.
If
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{BC}_{\geq k}
-free, then
e(\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{(k-1)(n-1)}{r}
.

Theorem 1.8

(Győri et al.,
[
15
]
)
.

Fix integers
k
and
r
such that
r>k\geq 3
.
Then,

\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{BC}_{\geq k})=(k-1)\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{r}\right\rfloor+\mathbbm{1}_{r|n},

where
\mathbbm{1}_{r|n}=1
if
r|n
and
\mathbbm{1}_{r|n}=0
otherwise.

Theorem 1.9

(Győri et al.,
[
15
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 3
and
n
be positive integers.
Then,

\ex_{r}(n,\mathcal{BC}_{\geq r})=\max\bigg\{(r-1)\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{r}\right\rfloor,~n-r+1\bigg\}.

Theorem 1.10

(Győri et al.,
[
15
]
)
.

Fix integers
n,k
and
r
such that
2\leq k\leq r
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
r
-uniform multi-hypergraph which is
\mathcal{BC}_{\geq k}
-free.
Then,

e(\mathcal{H})\leq(k-1)\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{r-1}\right\rfloor.

Analogously to graphs, a hypergraph is connected if for any two of its vertices, there is a Berge path containing both vertices.
Note that the extremal
r
-uniform hypergraph when forbidding Berge paths is not connected in general.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
r
-uniform hypergraphs. Denote by
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{F})
the maximum number of hyperedges in an
n
-vertex connected
\mathcal{F}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph.
In 2018, Győri, Methuku, Salia, Tompkins and Vizer
[
16
]
determined the asymptotics of
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
. For the case
k\geq 4r\geq 12
, Füredi, Kostochka and Luo
[
9
]
determined
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
when
n
is large enough.
Independently in a different range, these numbers were also determined by Győri, Salia and Zamora
[
17
]
, who also proved the uniqueness of the extremal hypergraphs.
Recently, Gerbner, Nagy, Patkós, Salia and Vizer
[
13
]
obtained a stability version of Győri et al’s result from
[
17
]
.

Theorem 1.11

(Győri et al.,
[
16
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{n,k}
be a connected
r
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph of maximum size with no Berge paths of length
k
. Then,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|E(\mathcal{H}_{n,k})|}{k^{r-1}n}=\frac{1}{2^{r-1}(r-1)!}.

Theorem 1.12

(Füredi, Kostochka and Luo,
[
9
]
)
.

Let
n\geq n^{\prime}_{k,r}\geq k\geq 4r\geq 12
. Then,

\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})\leq\binom{\left\lceil\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rceil}{r}+\bigg(n-\left\lceil\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rceil\bigg)\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor}{r-1}.

Theorem 1.13

(Győri, Salia and Zamora,
[
17
]
)
.

For all integers
n,k
and
r
there exists an
N_{k,r}
such that for
n>N_{k,r}
and
k\geq 2r+13\geq 18
,

\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})=\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor}{r-1}\bigg(n-\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor\bigg)+\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor}{r}+\mathbbm{1}_{2|k}\binom{\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor}{r-2}.

The extremal hypergraph is unique.

For the case
k\leq r
, Kostochka and Luo
[
19
]
obtained an upper bound.

Theorem 1.14

(Kostochka and Luo,
[
19
]
)
.

Fix
r\geq k\geq 3
. Then,

\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})\leq\max\bigg\{k-1,\frac{k}{2r-k+4}n\bigg\}.

In Section
2
we present the new contributions of this paper.
In Section
3
, we give the proofs of our main results.
In Section
4
, we present our postponed proof of Lemma
2.2
.
Finally, in Section
5
, we conclude this paper with some final remarks.

2
Results

In this paper, we present some improvements of Theorem
1.14
.
First, we discuss the situation for some small values of
k
.
For
k=2
, we observe that
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{2})=1
when
n=r
and it is not defined for larger
n
.
In the following, we consider the cases
k=3
,
k=4
and
k\geq 5
, respectively.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix
n\geq r\geq 3
. If
n\leq 2r-2
, then
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{3})=2
and the extremal hypergraphs consist of two hyperedges sharing at least two common vertices.
If
n\geq 2r-1
and
n-1
is a multiple of
r-1
, then
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{3})=\frac{n-1}{r-1}
and the extremal hypergraph is a star hypergraph composed of
\frac{n-1}{r-1}
hyperedges sharing one common vertex.
For the case
n\geq 2r-1
and
n-1
is not a multiple of
r-1
, the function
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{3})
is not defined.

Proposition 2.2
.

Fix
n\geq r\geq 4
. If
n\leq r+4
, then

\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{4})=4.

If
n\geq r+5
, then

\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{4})\leq\max\bigg\{\frac{n-5}{r-1}+3,\frac{n-4}{r-2}+2\bigg\}.

Moreover, if
n\geq r+5
, then
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{4})
is not defined if
n-5
is not a multiple of
r-1
and
n-4
is not a multiple of
r-2
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix integers
n,r,k
with
r\geq k\geq 5
.
When
n
is sufficiently large and
n
is not a multiple of
r
, we have

ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})=\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{r}\right\rfloor+\mathbbm{1}_{2|k},

where
\mathbbm{1}_{2|k}=1
if
2|k
and
\mathbbm{1}_{2|k}=0
otherwise.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix integers
n,r,k
with
n\geq r>k\geq 3
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a connected
n
-vertex
\mathcal{B}P_{k}
-free
r
-uniform multi-hypergraph.
If
r\leq n\leq 2r-2
, we have
e(\mathcal{H})\leq k-1
.
If
n\geq 2r-1
and
n
is not a multiple of
r-1
, we have

e(\mathcal{H})\leq\max\bigg\{k-1+\frac{n-k+1}{r-\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor},\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{r-1}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rfloor+\mathbbm{1}_{2|k}\bigg\}.

For the case
k=r+1
, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
r\geq 3
.
When
n
is sufficiently large, we have

\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{r+1})=n-r+1.

To prove Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we need the following key lemma, the proof of which we postpone to Section
4
.
Before stating this lemma, we introduce the following
definition which we adopted from
[
15
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph and let
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
be a vertex subset of
\mathcal{H}
.
Denote by

N_{\mathcal{H}}(S)=\{h\in E(\mathcal{H})|h\cap S\neq\emptyset\}

the hyperedge neighborhood of
S
, that is the set of all hyperedges that are incident with at least one vertex of
S
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r,k,n
and
m
be positive integers with
r\geq k\geq 3
, and let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex connected
r
-uniform
\mathcal{B}P_{k}
-free hypergraph such that every hyperedge has multiplicity at most
m
.
Assume that a longest Berge path in
\mathcal{H}
has length
t
, and that
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{B}C_{t}
-free.
Then at least one of the following holds.

(
i
) There exists
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
of size
2r-2
such that
|N_{\mathcal{H}}(S)|\leq m+1
.

(
ii
) There exists
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
of size at least
2r-1
such that
|N_{\mathcal{H}}(S)|\leq t\leq k-1
.

3
Proofs of our main results

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{H}
is an
n
-vertex connected
r
-uniform hypergraph containing no Berge path of length 3. Let
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
.
If there exists another hyperedge
f\in E(\mathcal{H})
such that
|e\cap f|=0
, then by the connectivity of
\mathcal{H}
there must be a Berge path of length
3
, a contradiction.
Thus, every hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}
meets
e
.
Let
f,g\in E(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{e\}
.
If
f
and
g
meet outside of
e
, then
e,f,g
forms a Berge path of length
3
, a contradiction.
If
f
and
g
do not meet outside of
e
, then
f,e,g
forms a Berge path of length
3
, a contradiction.
If
h\neq e
is a hyperedge in
\mathcal{H}
such that
|h\cap e|\geq 2
, then
E(\mathcal{H})=\{e,h\}
and
n=|V(\mathcal{H})|=r+r-|e\cap f|\leq 2r-2
since
\mathcal{H}
is connected.
Otherwise, if
e^{\prime}
is a hyperedge in
\mathcal{H}\setminus\{e,h\}
, then
e^{\prime},e,h
forms a Berge path of length
3
, a contradiction.

Let
n\geq 2r-1
and
|h\cap e|=1
for each hyperedge
h\neq e
. Then all hyperedges in
E(\mathcal{H})\setminus\{e\}
meet
e
at the same vertex.
Hence,

e(\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{n-r}{r-1}+1=\frac{n-1}{r-1},

with equality holding only when
(r-1)|(n-1)
and the extremal hypergraph is a star hypergraph which is composed of
\frac{n-1}{r-1}
hyperedges sharing one common vertex.

Thus if
n\leq 2r-2
, then
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{3})=2
and the extremal hypergraphs consist of two hyperedges sharing at least two common vertices.
If
n\geq 2r-1
and
n-1
is a multiple of
r-1
, then
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{3})=\frac{n-1}{r-1}
, and the extremal hypergraph is a star hypergraph which is composed of
\frac{n-1}{r-1}
hyperedges sharing one common vertex.
For the case when
n\geq 2r-1
and
n-1
is not a multiple of
r-1
, the function is not defined.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{H}
is an
n
-vertex connected
\mathcal{B}P_{4}
-free
r
-uniform hypergraph.
If
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{B}P_{3}
-free, then by Proposition
2.1
we have

e(\mathcal{H})\leq\max\bigg\{2,\frac{n-1}{r-1}\bigg\}<\frac{n-3}{r-1}+2.

Now we may assume that
\mathcal{H}
contains a Berge path
P
of length 3.
Without loss of generality, we denote it by

P=v_{1},e_{1},v_{2},e_{2},v_{3},e_{3},v_{4}

such that
v_{i},v_{i+1}\in e_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
.
If there exists another hyperedge

v_{1},e_{1},w,e_{j_{p}},v_{j_{p}},e_{j_{p}+1},v_{j_{p}+1},\ldots,e_{t-1},v_{t-1},e_{t},v_{j_{p}-1},e_{j_{p}-1},v_{j_{p}-2},\ldots,e_{2},v_{1}

is a Berge cycle of length
t
, a contradiction.

Note that for
1\leq p\leq s
, the only possible defining vertices in
A_{p}
are
v_{i_{1}-1},v_{i_{2}-1},\ldots,v_{i_{p-1}-1}
.
Therefore
v_{i_{p}-1}
is not contained in
A_{p}
.
For
s+1\leq p\leq\ell
, the only possible defining vertices in
A_{p}
are
v_{i_{1}-1},v_{i_{2}-1},\ldots,v_{i_{s-1}-1},\ldots,v_{j_{s+1}-1},v_{j_{s+2}-1},\ldots,v_{j_{p-1}-1}
, therefore
v_{j_{p}-1}
is not contained in
A_{p}
. Next we will show that the defining vertices in
A_{p}
are distinct for
s+1\leq p\leq\ell
.

Claim 4.3
.

There are no indices
1\leq p\leq s
and
s+1\leq q\leq\ell
such that
v_{i_{p}-1}=v_{j_{q}-1}
, where
v_{i_{p}-1}\in A_{p+1}
and
v_{j_{q}-1}\in A_{q+1}
.

Proof.

Assume that there exist
1\leq p\leq s
and
s+1\leq q\leq\ell
such that
v_{i_{p}-1}=v_{j_{q}-1}
, where
v_{i_{p}-1}\in A_{p+1}
and
v_{j_{q}-1}\in A_{q+1}
.

Since
v_{i_{p}-1}\in A_{p+1}
, there must exist an index
1\leq p^{\prime}\leq p-1
such that
(e_{i_{p^{\prime}}}\setminus U)\cap(e_{i_{p}}\setminus U)\neq\emptyset
. Note that
v_{i_{p^{\prime}}},v_{i_{p}}\in e_{1}\cap U
and
v_{i_{p}-1}\in e_{t}\cap U
.
If we suppose that
w\in(e_{i_{p^{\prime}}}\setminus U)\cap(e_{i_{p}}\setminus U)
, then

\displaystyle P^{\prime}=

\displaystyle v_{i_{p}},e_{i_{p}+1},v_{i_{p}+1},\ldots,v_{t-1},e_{t},v_{i_{p}-1},e_{i_{p}-1},v_{i_{p}-2},\ldots,

\displaystyle e_{i_{p^{\prime}}+1},v_{i_{p^{\prime}}},e_{1},v_{1},e_{2},v_{2},\ldots,v_{i_{p^{\prime}}-1},e_{i_{p^{\prime}}},w,e_{i_{p}},v_{i_{p}}

is a Berge cycle of length
t
, a contradiction.
∎

Let us denote
A=A_{\ell+1}
. Then, we have

|A_{p}|<|A_{p+1}|
for all
1\leq p\leq\ell
. Hence,
|A|\geq|A_{1}|+\ell\geq 2r-1
, by Lemmas
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.3
we have that
|N_{\mathcal{H}}(A)|\leq k-1
.

This completes the proof.

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we determined
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
when
n
is sufficiently large and
n
is not a multiple of
r
. For the case
k=r+1
, we determined
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
asymptotically. We conjecture that the behavior of the function
\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})
for the case
r+2\leq k\leq 2r-1
will be very similar to that for
k=r+1
.

Conjecture 5.1
.

Fix integers
k
and
r
such that
r+1\leq k\leq 2r-1
. Then for sufficiently large
n
,

\ex^{\mathrm{conn}}_{r}(n,\mathcal{B}P_{k})=n-(k-2)+\binom{k-2}{r}.

Indeed, we can construct an extremal
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
on
n
vertices as follows. First, let
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
be a vertex subset
of
\mathcal{H}
containing
k-2
vertices such that
S
forms a complete
r
-uniform subhypergraph in
\mathcal{H}
. For each vertex
v\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus S
, choose an
(r-1)
-subset in
S
such that
S\cup\{v\}
forms a hyperedge in
\mathcal{H}
. It can be easily checked that
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{B}P_{k}
-free and connected.

Integrated study reveals molecular mechanisms by which bisphenol A promotes ovarian cancer
Authors: Haiyan Geng, Jianjun Zhu, Wentao Zhang, Ming Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBisphenol A (BPA) is an endocrine-disrupting chemical, and long-term low-level exposure is closely associated with ovarian cancer (OC). However, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain unclear. Utilizing the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) and the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) database, combined with LASSO Cox regression, we constructed a BPA exposure-associated OC risk prognosis model composed of CDKN1B, TIPARP, DYRK1B, EPCAM, and WDR77. Functional analysis indicated that the risk model and its constituent genes are associated with humoral immune activation and cellular immunosuppression. Furthermore, the activation of neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction signaling appears to be a common molecular mechanism through which these risk genes contribute to OC. Molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and cellular experiments confirmed a stable binding interaction between BPA and the CDKN1B protein. These findings provide scientific data for a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms linking BPA to OC, aiding risk prediction and personalized prevention for exposed individuals.Subject areas: toxicology, in silico toxicology, cancer

Introduction

Bisphenol A (BPA) is a ubiquitous chemical used in polycarbonate, polyvinyl chloride plastics, and epoxy resin, materials that are commonly used in food additives and beverage containers.1,2 Polluted air and water are also sources of BPA that can enter the human body.3 It is reported that BPA and its substitutes have reproductive toxicity risks and potential endocrine-disrupting effects.4 Both long-term low-level and high-level exposures to BPA have been proven to exert reproductive toxicity.5 Although the mechanism of BPA-induced reproductive toxicity is not fully understood, it has the ability to affect estrogen androgen activity and disrupt the hypothalamic pituitary gonadal axis.3 As an endocrine disruptor, BPA is also associated with obesity, diabetes, the cardiovascular system, and cancer.6,7 In addition, there is a correlation between BPA exposure and allergies, asthma, and immune dysfunction, and BPA might be an environmental factor triggering autoimmunity.8 Feng et al. studied the effects of BPA exposure on psoriasis through network toxicology.9 Dallio et al. proved that BPA can induce trained immunity in a healthy cohort.10 These studies indicate a certain correlation between BPA and diseases as well as immunity.

Ovarian cancer (OC) is one of the three most serious gynecological malignancies worldwide, and approximately 240,000 new cases are reported each year globally.11,12 At present, the treatment of OC mainly comprises surgery and drug therapy, such as chemotherapy, PARP inhibitors, bevacizumab, hormone therapy, and immunotherapy.13 However, with the increase in the global adolescent incidence rate and mortality, and the emergence of drug resistance, strategies for the treatment of OC rely on a deep understanding of the pathogenesis, involving the development of drugs targeting different growth pathways.14,15 Therefore, leveraging biomarkers is essential to enhance early diagnosis and guide appropriate treatment selection. The OC risk prediction model can help identify high-risk populations for preventive intervention or early detection.16

OC is a complex tumor characterized by strong heterogeneity.16 The principal histological subtypes of OC include high-grade serous, low-grade serous, endometrioid, clear cell, and mucinous.17 Ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma is the most common type of OC, accounts for approximately 90% of all ovarian malignancies, and high-grade ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma accounts for 70–80% of OC deaths.18,19 Although endometrioid cancer (accounting for about 10% proportion of invasive cancers) and clear cell carcinoma (accounting for about 6% proportion of invasive cancers) constitute only a small part, these two types are closely related to hormone levels.20 Research has shown that FSH-stimulated VEGF expression is associated with prognostic evaluation and treatment of ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma.21 Immunohistochemical analysis of estrogen and progesterone receptor expression in primary and recurrent tumor samples of patients with stage II-IV high-grade serous OC (HGSOC) showed a significant decrease in progesterone receptor expression in patients with platinum-sensitive HGSOC with recurrence.22 The strong expression of progesterone receptors in HGSOC is also associated with improved patient survival, indicating that progesterone receptors may serve as a prognostic biomarker for HGSOC. Future stratified clinical trials are needed to validate their potential application in endocrine therapy.23 Collective evidence delineates the multifaceted oncogenic mechanisms by which hormonal disorders contribute to ovarian carcinogenesis.

BPA exposure has been shown to be associated with tumorigenesis, including OC. Yuan et al. found that low-dose BPA treatment of OC cells significantly increased the expression of cancer stem cell markers such as OCT4, NANOG, and SOX2, and promoted tumor metastasis in animals.24 BPA can induce epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in OC cells through an estrogen receptor-dependent pathway and enhance cell migration ability.25 BPA exposure can also promote the formation and expansion of OC stem cells by regulating microRNA biosynthesis via G protein-coupled receptor 30, which is independent of estrogen receptors.26 These studies indicate that BPA exposure is associated with OC, and the specific molecular mechanisms need further investigation.

In this study, we systematically explored the potential toxicity and molecular mechanisms of BPA in OC. BPA exposure-related OC genes were downloaded from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) to construct a network toxicology framework via protein-protein interaction (PPI) and functional enrichment analysis. By intersecting with the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)-OC differentially expressed genes (DEGs), we obtained more accurate BPA exposure-OC DEGs and conducted network construction and functional analysis. Using the LASSO machine learning method combined with Cox regression, the potential hub toxicological targets were screened, and a BPA exposure-OC-related risk model was constructed based on the TCGA database, and this prognostic model was validated in patients from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets. We also conducted a detailed analysis of the correlation between model risk, model genes, and immunity. Single-cell sequencing data was used to analyze the expression distribution of risk model genes in the OC microenvironment. Finally, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and cell experiments confirmed the binding of BPA and model protein CDKN1B. Figure 1 illustrates the flow chart of this study. These findings elucidate the molecular mechanisms of BPA-induced OC, providing scientific evidence for assessing BPA exposure toxicity and insights for preventing and treating OC associated with environmental pollution.

Results

Screening and identification of BPA exposure-OC DEGs and enrichment analysis

The chemical structure of BPA, chemical name: 2,2-bis (4-hydroxyphenyl) propane, consists of two methyl groups and two 4-hydroxyphenyl groups (-C6H4-OH) attached to one carbon atom (Figure 2A). CTD statistics show that BPA has common interacting genes with some carcinogens, such as benzo(a)pyrene, tetrachlorodibenzodioxin, valproic acid, sodium arsenite, and acetaminophen (Figure 2B). A total of 126 genes related to both BPA exposure and OC were downloaded from the CTD database (Table S1). GO analysis results (ranked top 5 in terms of differential p-values) showed that in terms of biological process (BP), the genes were enriched in gland development, cellular response to chemical stress, epithelial cell proliferation, regulation of epithelial cell proliferation, and cellular response to oxidative stress. In terms of cellular component (CC), the genes are enriched in membrane raft, membrane microdomain, plasma membrane raft, caveola, and germ cell nucleus. In terms of molecular function (MF), genes enriched in phosphatase binding, protein kinase regulator activity, protein phosphatase binding, kinase regulator activity, and ATP-dependent DNA damage sensor activity (Figure 3A). KEGG enrichment results (top 10) show that genes are enriched in various cancers and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, FoxO signaling pathway, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor resistance class pathways (Figure 3B). A total of 126 gene PPI network from Metascape showed these genes main interacted in four pathway modules including gastric cancer, endometrial cancer, cancer pathways (molecular complex detection, MCODE 1), regulation of cell cycle process, colorectal cancer and chromosome organization (MCODE 2), and Huntington disease, cytokine signaling in immune system and cellular responses to stress (MCODE 4) (Figure 3C). No signaling pathway related to MCODE 3 has been provided. Figure S1A showed the PPI of 126 genes encoded proteins from STRING.

PPI shows that there are interactions among 48 out of 60 genes, including the top upregulated genes FOLR1, MSLN, EPCAM, MUC16, and BIRC5 in OC, as well as the top downregulated genes NR5A1, IL11RA, CAV1, ZEB1, and HDAC6 (Figure 4B). The downregulated 23 BPA exposure-OC DEGs enriched in the FOXO-mediated transcription of cell cycle genes, ncRNAs involved in STAT3 signaling in hepatocellular carcinoma, sensory organ development, tube morphogenesis, and focal adhesion inhibition of signaling pathways (Figure 4C). KEGG results showed the 37 upregulated genes enriched in platinum drug resistance, antifolate resistance, and folate transport and metabolism, and other cancer-related pathways (Figure 4D). Especially, the Platinum drug resistance pathway is related to all cancer pathways (Figure 4E). Enrichment analysis in DisGeNET from Metascape suggests the strongest correlation between upregulated genes and OC (Figure S1B).

Construction and validation of the BPA exposure OC related risk prognostic model

We conducted univariate Cox regression, LASSO regression, and multivariate Cox regression on 60 BPA exposure-OC DEGs, and selected the 5 most predictive genes, WDR77, EPCAM, CDKN1B, DYRK1B, and TIPARP. WDR77 and EPCAM are protective genes in OC [Hazard ratio (HR) < 1], while CDKN1B, DYRK1B, and TIPARP are risk genes (HR > 1) (Figures 5A–5D). The risk score of each patient was calculated using gene expression and regression coefficient according to the following formula: Risk score = CDKN1B × 0.166495 + DYRK1B × 0.216257175 + TIPARP × 0.165785377 - WDR77 × 0.220016816 - EPCAM × 0.174511973. Based on the median risk score, patients are divided into high-risk (194 patients) and low-risk (195 patients) groups. The information of the 389 patients is displayed in Table S4. The survival curve shows a significant correlation between the high-risk group and worse overall survival compared to the low-risk group (Figure 5E). The risk plot showed, compared with the low-risk group, more dead patients were distributed at the bottom in the high-risk group (Figure 5F). The time-dependent ROC curves for the risk score model showed the area under the curve (AUC) manifested values for 3-year, 6-year, and 8-year were 0.602, 0.689, and 0.675, respectively (Figure 5G). The nomogram model based on the gene expression values of 5 model genes can predict the 3-year, 6-year, and 8-year survival probability of patients with OC (Figure 5H). The independent OC datasets GSE26193 and GSE26712 validated the risk prognostic model. Compared with the low-risk group, patients in the high-risk group have a shorter survival period (HR = 1.73 and 1.92, p < 0.05, respectively). The time-dependent ROC curves showed the AUC values for 3-year, 6-year, and 8-year were from 0.603 to 0.699. And the risk plot displayed more dead patients in the high-risk group (Figure S2).

Model genes, prognosis, and expression analysis

Survival analysis of the model genes illustrated that high expression of CDKN1B, TIPARP, and DYRK1B was associated with worse prognosis compared to low expression patients (HR = 1.5, 1.75, 1.42, respectively), while patients with high expression of WDR77 and EPCAM had better prognosis (HR = 0.72, 0.74, respectively) (Figure 6A). Functional enrichment of prognostic model and model genes in OC

In order to further understand the prognostic mechanism of the constructed risk model in OC, we screened for DEGs with the high-risk group vs. the low-risk group and conducted enrichment analysis. A total of 776 DEGs were screened, including 442 up-regulated genes and 334 down-regulated genes (Table S5). The GO analysis results showed that the DEGs are enriched in phagocytosis, recognition, complement activation, classical pathway, B cell receptor signaling pathway, phagocytosis, engulfment, humoral immune response mediated by circulating immunoglobulin, these immune-related pathways in BP. The DEGs are mainly enriched in the external side of the plasma membrane, immunoglobulin complex, circulating, and intrinsic component of the synaptic membrane in CC. Mainly enriched in antigen binding, immunoglobulin receptor binding, receptor ligand activity, and gated channel activity in MF (Figure 7A). KEGG enrichment analysis indicated that DEGs are enriched in neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, hormone signaling, neuroactive ligand signaling, and renin-angiotensin system (Figure 7B). GO BP enrichment cnetplot exhibited all genes are upregulated DEGs, and most genes belong to the IGHV gene family and are enriched in multiple pathways (Figure 7C). We conducted enrichment analysis on each model gene using the DEGs between gene high expression and low expression (Tables S6, S7, S8, S9, and S10). The GO enrichment results showed similar results to the model: in terms of BP, DEGs were enriched in humoral immunity and related pathways (Figures S3–S7).

Analysis of the correlation between the prognostic model, model genes, and immunity

We analyzed the correlations of the model and its constituent genes with immune scores, the infiltration levels of 28 immune cell types, and the expression of 11 immune checkpoints. The results showed that the proportion of immune cells in the high-risk group showed an increasing trend of activated B cells, activated dendritic cells, various CD4 and CD8 cells (except activated CD4+ and CD8+ T cells), regulatory T cells (Treg), and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) (Figure 9A). The correlation between immune cell infiltration and models showed that the risk score and TIPARP expression are positively correlated with most immune cell infiltration, while DYRK1B, EPCAM, and WDR77 are negatively correlated with most immune cell infiltration (Figure 9B). The model risk score, CDKN1B, and TIPARP were positively correlated with the expression of immune co-inhibitors, while EPCAM and WDR77 were mostly negatively correlated with the expression of immune co-inhibitors (Figure 9C). The model risk score was positively correlated with stromal score and negatively correlated with tumor purity. DYRK1B, EPCAM, and WDR77 showed the opposite results to the model risk score (Figure 9D).

BPA-CDKN1B interaction in molecular docking, dynamics simulation, and validation

We selected CDKN1B, which showed the lowest Vina score in the molecular docking of BPA with model genes (Figure 11A), for docking visualization and follow-up study. On BPA receptor CDKN1B protein (PDB ID: ATH), Asp145 and Glu51 residues form a hydrogen bond interaction with BPA, Ala144, Ala31, Val64, and Leu134 residues form a hydrophobic interaction, Lys33, Phe146, Leu55, Glu81, and Phe82 residues form van der Waals interaction, and Val17 residue forms a pi sigma interaction with BPA (Figure 11B).

RMSD, root-mean-square deviation; Rg, radius of gyration; SASA, solvent accessible surface area; RMSF, Root-mean-square fluctuation.

The root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), which measures the positional deviation of atoms from their starting coordinates, serves as a key indicator of conformational stability. Lower RMSD values reflect greater stability and a well-equilibrated system. The RMSD curves from three independent simulation runs of the CDKN1B-BPA complex fluctuate slightly around 2.0 Å in 200ns. Their highly overlapping trends indicate that the complex maintains a stable conformation throughout the simulations, with excellent reproducibility across repeats. The radius of gyration (Rg) describes the overall structural compactness. The Rg values remain stable between 25 and 26 Å with only minor fluctuations, suggesting no significant global expansion or contraction of the protein during the simulations. The solvent accessible surface area (SASA) was calculated to evaluate the protein surface area exposed to the solvent. The high similarity in both values and fluctuation patterns across the three replicates indicates a stable solvent-accessible surface, further confirming conformational consistency. The root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) reflects the flexibility of individual amino acid residues. The RMSF profiles of the complex are largely consistent. Observed peaks in specific regions (e.g., residues 50–100 and 200–250 in chain A) correspond to inherently flexible loops, which is physiologically reasonable. Hydrogen bonds are critical for ligand-protein binding. The number of hydrogen bonds between the ligand and CDKN1B throughout the simulations remains stable (predominantly 1–3) with similar trends across all replicates, confirming consistent hydrogen-bond interactions. In summary, the CDKN1B-BPA complex demonstrates high stability throughout the simulations, supported by consistent RMSD, Rg, SASA, RMSF, and hydrogen-bond analyses. The stable binding interface, characterized by maintained hydrogen bonds, confirms the ligand’s ability to bind the target protein effectively (Figure 11C).

Lysate-based cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) was used to verify the direct binding ability of CDKN1B protein and BPA. The results showed that drug treatment significantly improved the thermal stability of the target protein in cells. Compared with the solvent control (dimethyl sulfoxide, DMSO) group, the amount of soluble target protein remaining in the experimental group was significantly higher at multiple heating temperature points (such as 47°C, 57°C, 62°C), suggesting that BPA binding increases CDKN1B thermal stability. This phenomenon directly proves that BPA can combine directly with CDKN1B (Figure 11D).

BPA affects the expression of risk model genes and promotes the proliferation of OC cells

To assess the effect of BPA on risk-model gene expression in OC cells, we treated OC cells with 100 nM BPA and measured gene expression levels by reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) using the primers of these 5 model genes (Table 1). Compared to the DMSO control, BPA treatment of OC cells for 24 h resulted in the significant upregulation of CDKN1B, DYRK1B, TIPARP, and WDR77 genes and the downregulation of the EPCAM gene (Figure 12A). EdU assay and CCK-8 test showed that, compared with the DMSO solvent control group, the cell proliferation and cell viability in the 100 nM BPA-treated group were increased significantly (Figures 12B–12D).

Discussion

The widespread use of BPA in daily life and environmental pollution has resulted in long-term exposure to low doses of BPA.2,3 This chemical can be linked to OC through endocrine disruption, and the molecular mechanisms still remain to be elucidated. In this study, we constructed a prognostic model of BPA exposure-associated OC risk composed of five genes: CDKN1B, TIPARP, EPCAM, DYRK1B, and WDR77 through network toxicology, Cox regression, and LASSO machine learning. Functional enrichment and immune correlation analysis suggested that the BPA exposure-OC prognostic model and risk genes may promote OC progression by activating humoral immunity and inhibiting cellular immunity. Risk model DEGs were significantly enriched in the neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction and hormone signaling pathways, implying that a neuron-tumor crosstalk is operative in BPA exposure-induced OC. Molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulation, and cell experiments confirmed the stable binding of BPA and protein CDKN1B.

CTD shows that BPA, the Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) type 1 name is 4,4′ isopropylidinediphenol, has a comparable set of interacting genes to carcinogens benzo(a)pyrene, Tetrachlorodibenzodioxin, and sodium arsenite. The common interacting genes with estradiol are 12,298, which is well proven to function as an endocrine-disrupting compound. CTD provides manually curated information about chemical, disease, and gene interactions, facilitating network toxicology research. In our study, the 126 BPA-OC related genes from CTD were enriched in BP related to gland development, cellular response to chemical stress, epithelial cell proliferation, and regulation of epithelial cell proliferation, suggesting these BPA-responsive genes can promote tumor progression by regulating cell proliferation. KEGG enrichment revealed these genes were enriched in cancer-related pathways, including the PI3K-Akt and FoxO signaling pathways, which are critically involved in cancer development and represent important therapeutic targets.27,28 The network composed of these genes mainly consists of the following modules: cancer pathways, regulation of the cell cycle process, and cytokine signaling in the immune system, suggesting BPA may interfere with the immune system of OC. 37 upregulated BPA exposure-OC DEGs enriched in macrophage-stimulating protein MSP signaling, regulation of the PTEN gene transcription signaling pathway. The importance of MSP-related signaling pathways in carcinogenesis has been validated in a variety of model systems, showing their important role in the aggressive growth of different types of cancer.29 And MSP can inhibit epithelial cell apoptosis by activating PI3K/AKT or the MAPK pathway.30 Whole-genome sequencing of patients with OC reveals PTEN inactivation promotes chemoresistance.31 And the PTEN/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway can mediate OC cell apoptosis.32 The enrichment of 23 downregulated BPA exposure-OC DEGs in the focal adhesion pathway suggests their potential role in promoting cell migration. Notably, platinum drug resistance was found in the KEGG enrichment analysis of upregulated genes. This finding provides guidance for the treatment of patients with BPA-exposed OC, or helps to study the mechanism of platinum drug resistance in patients with OC.

We constructed a BPA exposure-associated OC risk prognostic model consisting of five genes (CDKN1B, TIPARP, DYRK1B, EPCAM and WDR77). Both in the training and validation sets, the risk model showed a robust prognostic effect for patients' 3-, 6-, and 8-year survival times. Patients with high expression of CDKN1B, TIPARP, and DYRK1B genes (OC risk genes) had a poor prognosis, and with low expression of EPCAM and WDR77 genes (OC protective genes) had a good prognosis. CDKN1B encodes a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor, known as a tumor suppressor in prostate cancer, head and neck cancer, and breast cancer.33,34,35 In our study, CDKN1B was lowly expressed in patients with OC compared to normal controls, which is consistent with its function as a cancer suppressor gene. However, the data showed that in patients with OC, high expression of CDKN1B has a poor prognosis, meaning that CDKN1B again functioned as a risk gene for OC progression. This apparent contradiction may be explained by the mislocalization (subcellular translocation) or stress-induced dysregulation of CDKN1B in patients with OC. Despite high expression levels, these alterations impair its nuclear function as an inhibitor of cell proliferation and its anti-cancer activity. A key exception involves exposure to specific toxins: despite high CDKN1B expression, these toxins bind the protein, inhibit its tumor-suppressive function, and consequently promote tumor progression. In the study on perfluorooctanoic acid exposure and OC by Li et al., a similar problem was observed: the PDE2A gene was lowly expressed in OC, but patients with high expression had a poor prognosis, and finally, molecular docking also predicted a stable binding between perfluorooctanoic acid and PDE2A.36 Rather coincidentally, when we performed molecule docking of these five proteins encoded by model genes and molecular dynamics simulations of CDKN1B, the data showed that CDKN1B had the strongest and most stable binding to BPA. TIPARP is a mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase that mediates mono-ADP-ribosylation, a post-translational modification.37 It is reported that TIPARP can decrease ligand-dependent estrogen receptor α signaling and suppress cell proliferation in human breast cancer cells.38 However, in male papillary thyroid carcinoma, high TIPARP expression is associated with high-grade and lymph node metastasis.39 In our study, high expression of TIPARP may promote tumor progression in patients with OC. DYRK1B, a tyrosine phosphorylation-regulated kinase, promotes ovarian and pancreatic cancer progression by enhancing cancer cell survival, chemotherapy resistance, and recurrence through effects on both tumor cells and the microenvironment.40 In our study, DYRK1B high-expression patients have a poor prognosis, and GSEA enrichment results showed the high group was enriched in breast cancer and pathways affected in adenoid cystic carcinoma. EPCAM, epithelial cell adhesion molecule, has been widely shown to be highly expressed in OC.41,42 In our study, EPCAM showed high expression in patients with OC compared with normal controls in both TCGA data and single-cell sequencing analysis, while in patients with OC, the high expression group had a long survival time. Perelmuter VM et al. observed that the hybrid EMT phenotype was more prevalent among EpCAM-negative tumor cells compared with EpCAM-positive cells in early-stage breast cancer.43 We speculate that a decrease in EPCAM in patients may promote tumor metastasis. Moreover, EPCAM may be only a prognostic indicator independent of its expression level. WDR77, a WD-40 domain-containing protein, is found highly expressed in a variety of tumors and is associated with poor patient prognosis.44 Further studies are needed to investigate the function of WDR77 in OC. Patients with high expression of EPCAM and WDR77 show enrichment in estradiol response pathways, indicating a correlation between these genes and estrogen signaling, which plays a critical role in OC development.

We also conducted enrichment analysis on the DEGs from the risk model and risk model genes to speculate their role in OC. The GO-BP results showed that DEGs of the risk model and model genes were enriched in pathways related to phagocytosis, recognition, complement activation, B cell receptor signaling pathway, humoral immune response mediated by circulating immunoglobulin, and immune-related pathways. Humoral immunity has a dual role in tumors, and in a study of patients with highly graded plasmacytoid OC, a strong protective humoral response was found to be dominated by polyclonal IgA, which directs myeloid cells to attack tumor cells and induces cell death through tumor B-cell-mediated transport.45 In a study of 20 types of human cancers, it was found that tumor-infiltrating B cells exhibit significant heterogeneity.46 And the enrichment showed that IGHVs contribute to these pathways. The model constructed by Shen S et al., which can predict the efficacy of initial platinum-based chemotherapy in patients with epithelial OC after surgery, includes the IGHV3-35 gene.47 This suggests that IGHV related to B cell differentiation has potential research value in OC. Based on our data results of enriched immunoglobulin synthesis, complement activation, and cytokine pathways, we hypothesize that this humoral immunity is related to exposure to the toxic substance BPA and constitutes a long-term chronic humoral immunity that may lead to the deposition of immune complexes and inflammatory stimulation, promoting the progression of OC.

The KEGG enrichment results of our study indicate that risk model DEGs are enriched in neuroactive ligand receptor interaction, hormone signaling, and renin-angiotensin system pathways, suggesting that patients with high risk scores may exhibit accelerated OC progression, potentially mediated through neuroendocrine signaling pathways. The DEGs between high and low expression of the 5 risk model genes are all enriched in neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, highlighting their potential centrality in OC progression. The study has found that overactivation of the neuroactive ligand receptor interaction pathway is associated with immune suppression, prognosis, and immune therapy response in colon cancer.48 In the study of gastric cancer, it has also been found that prognostic risk model genes are involved in the neuroactive ligand receptor interaction pathway.49 Surprisingly, the enriched hormone signals are independent of ovarian-related hormone signaling pathways, but are found in the brain, heart, intestine, and adipose tissue (Figure S9). This finding further confirms our speculation that the neuroendocrine network is activated in the progression of BPA exposure-OC. The plastic additive bisphenol S (BPS), a substitute for BPA, has been proven to disrupt the blood-brain barrier and exert adverse effects on the brain, and promote the onset of depression by modulating the neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction pathway.50 Zhang N et al. reported that prostate cancer tissue with perineural invasion exhibits enhanced activity in neuroactive ligand-receptor interactions. This crosstalk between tumor cells and neurons not only promotes tumor progression but also influences neural function.51 Our study provides the first evidence in patients with BPA-exposed OC that BPA may promote tumor perineural invasion by regulating the neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction pathway. This suggests the existence of a closed-loop pathway interconnecting BPA, OC cells, peripheral nerves, neurotransmitters, their receptors, the central nervous system, and hormonal signals.

The analysis of immune cell infiltration showed that most of the immune cells in the high-risk model group increased, including various B cells. It should be noted that the proportion of immune suppressive cells, Treg and MDSCs, also increased, while there was no difference in activated CD4+T cells and activated CD8+T cells between the high-risk and low-risk groups. Schwartz et al. described that B cells attenuate innate and/or adaptive anti-tumor immune responses by suppressing T cell-mediated tumor immunity and/or promoting the conversion of T cells to Treg. B regulatory cell (Breg) subpopulations inhibit T cell and/or natural killer cell responses through the expression of cytokines and/or the expression of immunomodulatory ligands, such as PD-L1, and can affect a wide range of cell subtypes, including MDSCs.52 We assume that in the high-risk group, this widespread infiltration of immune cells manifests as an immunosuppressive state. The immune cell infiltration correlation results showed that the genes CDKNB1 and TIPARP, as well as the risk score, have similar immune infiltration profiles, which also confirmed the risk roles of the two genes in the OC process. Additionally, the correlation analysis of immune checkpoints also supports our speculation, as the expression of most immune co-inhibitors is positively correlated with risk score and risk gene expression. These results provide reference data for OC immunotherapy. It should be noted that both risk scores and model genes are positively correlated with CD276 expression. The mechanism needs further in-depth research in the future, and this finding may be applied to personalized adjuvant therapy for patients with high CD276 expression. The correlation analysis of immune scores shows that risk scores are positively correlated with stromal scores and negatively correlated with tumor purity, suggesting that patients with high scores may have a higher degree of stromal cell infiltration and a higher possibility of OC metastasis.53,54

To further investigate whether BPA works by directly binding to these target proteins, we conducted molecular docking between BPA and proteins encoded by these target genes. The results showed that CDKN1B had the strongest binding affinity with BPA (with the lowest Vina score). Three independent molecular dynamics simulation data and the CETSA experiment also fully demonstrate a stable direct dynamic binding between BPA and CDKN1B. This result provides a good explanation for the poor prognosis of patients with high expression of CDKN1B, although it mostly inhibits tumors. It is possible that the binding of BPA inhibits the protein function of CDKN1B. Based on the analysis of single-cell sequencing data, this inhibition may be widely present across multiple cells because CDKN1B is widely expressed in multiple cells. Another possibility is that CDKN1B provides attachment for BPA, which exerts toxic functions in cells. Cell-based experiments demonstrated that BPA modulates the expression of risk model genes in OC cells and increases OC cell proliferation. Further in-depth research is needed on the specific related mechanisms.

In conclusion, we utilized comparative toxicogenomics combined with bulk sequencing data, single-cell sequencing analysis, and cell experiments to delineate the pathogenic mechanism of BPA exposure-associated OC. We established a robust 5-gene prognostic signature through machine learning. We identified key genes and pathways of BPA exposure associated with OC, BPA exposure-OC DEGs, and a risk model, and elucidated the hierarchical molecular landscape underlying BPA exposure-OC. Our findings provide early-detection biomarkers for environmental risk assessment and actionable therapeutic targets for precision intervention in patients with BPA-exposed OC.

Limitations of the study

Although we have systematically analyzed the functions of BPA exposure, OC related genes, and identified a prognostic model for BPA exposure patients with OC through extensive data analysis, there are still some limitations in this study. First, the TCGA data used for analysis mainly focused on ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma, with less consideration given to the heterogeneity of OC. The reliability of the results needs to be validated in different types of OC in the future. Second, there are currently no confirmed BPA exposure-related OC cohorts to validate the efficacy of the risk prognostic model. Third, our analysis was confined to female patients due to the sex-specific nature of OC. Therefore, the results cannot be generalized to male populations or to other cancers that occur across both sexes. Fourth, there is hope for more in-depth future experimental verification based on database analysis results. Furthermore, several mechanisms remain to be elucidated, such as the suppression of cellular immunity by humoral immunity upon toxins, and the role of the Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction pathway in OC.

Method details

Public data acquisition

TCGA-OC transcriptome sequencing and clinical data were downloaded from National Cancer Institute (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Excluding samples with unknown survival time and less than 30 (possibly due to reasons other than OC), 389 OC samples (from a total of 401) were involved in the analysis. The transcriptome datasets of GSE26193 and GSE26712 related to OC were downloaded from GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Excluding samples with a survival time of less than 30 days, 105 OC samples of the GSE26193 dataset and 152 OC samples of the GSE26712 dataset were included in the study. The TCGA dataset is used as the training dataset, while the GSE26193 and GSE26712 datasets are used as the validation datasets. The single-cell sequencing dataset GSE184880 for OC from GEO includes 5 normal ovaries and 7 OC tissues.

Screening of potential target genes for OC under BPA exposure environment

CTD is a robust, publicly available database that aims to advance understanding about how environmental exposures affect human health. It provides manually curated information about chemical-gene/protein interactions, chemical-disease and gene-disease relationships and updated on July 1, 202560 (http://ctdbase.org). Use “BPA” as the keyword to search for BPA exposure related diseases “Ovarian Neoplasms”, and download related genes. OC DEGs were downloaded from GEPIA website (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index) (|Log2FC| Cutoff: 1; q-value Cutoff: 0.01; Differential Methods: LIMMA). GEPIA2 is a gene expression analysis tool based on TCGA and GTEx databases, mainly used for RNA sequencing data analysis of tumor and normal samples. Because the input TCGA TPM (Transcript per million) values are already normalized, GEPIA2 performs differential expression analysis by applying limma directly to the log2(TPM+1)-transformed data, thus omitting the need for TMM (trimmed mean of m-values) normalization.58,61 By taking the intersection of genes downloaded from the CTD and GEPIA2 websites, we obtained BPA exposure-OC DEGs.

Gene enrichment and PPI analysis

We used the “clusterProfiler” and “enrichplot” R packages for GO and KEGG enrichment analysis, visualized the result on the bioinformatics website (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/). Metascape 3.5 (released on Hb 19, 2024) was used for extensive gene enrichment analysis.59 STRING (https://cn.string-db.org/) constructs PPI network, and Cytoscape 3.10.3 (https://cytoscape.org/) visualizes the PPI network.62,63,68

Construction of risk prognosis model

Using BPA exposure-OC DEGs, the most predictive genes were identified through univariate Cox regression, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression, and multivariate Cox regression, and a prognostic risk model was constructed. Based on the expression of these genes and the multivariate Cox regression coefficient, the risk score was calculated using the mathematical formula (Riskscore = ∑ ni = 1 Expi ∗ Coei). Patients are divided into high risk and low risk groups based on the median risk score. Analyze the survival prognosis of high risk and low risk patients through the R packages “survival” and “survivminer”. Use the R package “survivalROC” to analyze the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve of the subjects, and evaluate the effectiveness of the risk model in predicting the prognosis of OC patients. The risk curve is generated by the online tool Hiplot (ORG) (https://hiplot.cn/).64 Two independent OC datasets GSE26193 and GSE26712 were used to validate the risk prognostic model. The patients were divided into high risk and low risk groups by calculating the risk score of OC patients using the 5 risk model genes expression, the survival curve, ROC curves and risk plot in two groups were drawn.

Construction of nomogram

Based on the Cox risk model, the R package “rms” and “survival” were used to construct nomograms of relevant genes in the model. Scores were assigned to each gene, and these scores were added up to calculate the total score of the patient. The total score was then used to evaluate the patient’s 3-year, 6-year and 8-year survival outcomes.

Model gene survival and expression analysis

Based on the expression of model genes, the best cutoff method was used to obtain the optimal expression value as the classification criteria. Patients were divided into high expression group and low expression group, and the risk model related gene survival prognosis was analyzed using R packages (“survival” and “survivminer”). TNM plot (https://tnmplot.com/analysis/) platform provides 133 control samples and 374 OC samples, with data from NCBI-GEO database, TCGA database, and GTEx database.65 Download the expression levels of model related genes from TNM plot online platform and plot the expression heatmaps of model genes in control samples and OC samples.

Functional enrichment analysis of risk models and model genes

Based on the median risk score, the TCGA-OC patients will be divided into high risk and low risk groups. Use the R package “DESeq2” to screen DEGs between high and low groups, with the screening criteria of | logFC | > 1, p < 0.05. To mitigate the influence of low-expression genes on the estimation of normalization factors, DESeq2 applies a pre-filter to exclude genes with low expression. Subsequently, the DESeq() function is applied to the dataset object, which automatically performs normalization using the median-of-ratios method to calculate a size factor for each sample. These size factors are then used internally to standardize the raw counts, effectively scaling each sample’s counts to account for differences in sequencing depth. This normalization is integrated into the statistical model and does not require manual intervention.66 Perform GO and KEGG functional enrichment analysis on DEGs using R packages. For each model gene, OC samples were divided into high expression group and low expression group according to the expression. Enrichment analysis of these two groups was conducted using GSEA software (4.2.3), and the significant enriched pathways were chosen with p < 0.05.67 The enrichment results are visualized using R package “GseaVis” and “ggplot 2”.

Analysis of tumor immune microenvironment

The enrichment scores of 28 immune cell types were calculated using the R package “GSVA” with the ssGSEA algorithm. The fraction of these 28 immune cell types between low risk and high risk groups was compared. And correlation analyses were performed between the risk model (and its constituent genes) and these 28 immune cell types to gain deeper insight into the relationship between the model genes and the OC immune microenvironment. Additionally, the correlations between the risk score/expression levels of model genes and classical immune checkpoint molecules were analyzed. The R package “estimate” was employed to calculate stromal scores, immune scores, and ESTIMATE scores, and their correlations with the risk score and model gene expression were subsequently analyzed.

Single-cell sequencing analysis

OC related single-cell transcriptome data GSE184880 was downloaded, consisting of three files: code, features, and matrix for each cell, totaling 60701 cells. Standardize data using the “Seurat” R package. After data quality control, 46767 cells were ultimately retained. Standardize single-cell gene expression data using the “NormalizeData” function. Use the Find Variable Features option in Seurat to calculate highly variable genes, perform principal component analysis (PCA) on the top 2000 highly variable genes for dimensionality reduction, and set a clustering resolution parameter of 0.4 to divide cell functional subgroups. Nonlinear visualization is achieved through t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) and uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP). Use the five types of human datasets in the “SingleR” package (BlueprintEncodeData, DataImmuneCellExpressionData, HumanPrimaryCellAtlasData, MouseRNAseqData, and NovershtemnHematotoxic Data) to automatically annotate the data, combined with the online website CellMarker2.0 (http://117.50.127.228/CellMarker/) to verified.69 Use R packages “Seurat” and “ggplot2” to plot the expression levels of model genes in various cell clusters, as well as the expression distribution of model genes in normal and OC samples. Among them, blue represents low expression and red represents high expression.

Molecular docking

Through PubChem database (http://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) obtain the 2D structure of BPA, input the 2D structure into ChemOffice software Chem3D 23.1.1 to create its 3D structure. Then use the RCSB PDB database (http://www.rcsb.org/) screen protein targets with high resolution crystal structures as molecular docking receptors, use PyMOL software to perform dehydration, phosphate removal, and other operations on proteins. AutoDock Vina1.5.6 software was used for molecular docking to explore the interactions between protein and BPA. Using Autodock to process the structure of proteins and BPA, hydrogenation and dehydration of proteins, hydrogenation of BPA, determination of torsional forces, followed by determination of docking box coordinates. By comparing the scores of docking results, the optimal conformation for molecular simulation is finally obtained. Use Discovery Studio 2019 software to visualize the 2D and 3D analysis of the interactions between compounds and key residues. When the binding energy is less than −5.0 kcal/mol, it indicates good binding activity, and less than −7.0 kcal/mol indicates strong binding activity. The lower the binding energy, the stronger the binding activity, the higher the affinity, and the more stable the conformation between protein and BPA.

Molecular dynamics simulation

Gromacs 2022 was used for molecular dynamics simulations.70 Obtain force field parameters through Gromacs pdb2gmx tool. In this study, sobtop_1.0 (dev3.1) software was used to generate the topology file of GAFF2 force field according to the ligand structure, and the charge distribution of the ligand was carried out by resp method, so as to ensure that the charge distribution conforms to the physical and chemical characteristics. The receptor was parameterized with the AMBER14SB force field, while ligand topology was generated using sobtop_1.0 (dev3.1) (http://sobereva.com/soft/Sobtop/).71 The system was solvated in a 1 nm cubic TIP3P water box, and Na+/Cl− ions were added to 0.15 M NaCl to maintain electroneutrality and physiological ionic strength. Long-range electrostatic interactions were treated with the Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) method with a 1 nm cutoff, and bond constraints were enforced using the LINCS algorithm. The system underwent energy optimization before molecular dynamics simulation. The process of energy minimization includes 3000 steps of steepest descent optimization, followed by 2000 steps of conjugate gradient optimization. During the simulation, the temperature was maintained at 310 K using a Nosé–Hoover thermostat, and the pressure was maintained at 1 bar using a Parrinello–Rahman barostat. The simulation was conducted for 200 ns under the NPT (Number of particles, Pressure, Temperature; isothermal-isobaric) ensemble with an integration time step of 2 fs. During the simulation process, g-rmsd, g-rmsf, g-hbond, g-Rg, and g-sasa tools were used to calculate RMSD, RMSF, hydrogen bonds (HBonds), Rg, and SASA, respectively. To assess the robustness of the BPA-CDKN1B interaction, we performed three independent molecular dynamics simulations, each employing different initial random velocities and extend the simulation time to 200ns.

Cell culture and BPA treatments

Human ovarian cancer cell lines SKOV3 was obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Culture Repository (Shanghai, China). BPA (>99.0%) were purchased from Glpbio (cat.no. GC39480). Cells were maintained in complete growth medium consisting of McCOY’s 5A Medium (Gibco, cat. no. 16600082) supplemented with 10% (v/v) heat-inactivated FBS and 1% (v/v) Penicillin-Streptomycin (100 U/mL penicillin and 100 μg/mL streptomycin). Cells were incubated in a humidified atmosphere of 95% air and 5% CO2 at 37°C. The culture medium was refreshed every 2–3 days.

mRNA extraction and reverse transcription-quantitative (RT-q) PCR

Total RNA of OC cells was extracted using RNAiso Plus (Takara Bio, Inc. cat. no. 9108). After washing and quantifying the RNA, cDNA was synthesized using the PrimeScript RT Master Mix Reverse Transcription Kit (Takara Bio, Inc. cat. no. RR036A). Two-step qPCR with gene-specific primers was used to test five risk model genes expression by using TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Takara Bio, Inc. cat. no. CN830A) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. The 2−ΔΔCq method with ACTB as the internal control was used to calculate the relative expression levels of genes. The primers were synthetized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co.,Ltd. and Table 1 list the sequences. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0. Group comparisons were conducted using Student’s t test, and a p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

CCK-8 assay

SKOV3 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded into a 96-well plates at 5000 cells per well. After treatment with conditioned medium, 10 μL of CCK 8 solution (MeilunBio Co., Ltd., cat. no. MA0218-2) was added to each well at 0 h, 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h post medium change, followed by incubation in the dark at 37°C for 90 min. Absorbance (OD) was immediately measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

EdU staining experiment

After 24-h treatment by DMSO or 100 nM BPA, SKOV3 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded into a 96-well plate at a density of 15,000 cells per well. An EdU working solution (50 μM) (Cell-Light EDU Apollo643 In Vitro Kit, Guangzhou RiboBio Co., Ltd., cat. no. C10310-2) was prepared by mixing the EdU stock solution with culture medium at a 1:1000 ratio, and 100 μL of this solution was added to each well for a 2-h incubation. Subsequently, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and 2 mg/mL glycine, permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100, and stained using the Apollo reaction cocktail. Following another permeabilization step with 0.5% Triton X-100, nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst 33342. Images were acquired by confocal microscopy, and the number of proliferating cells was quantified using ImageJ software.

Cellular thermal shift assay

Cells were lysed and protein was extracted using MPER buffer (ThermoFisher Scientific, cat. no. 3213855). The lysate was equally divided into BPA and DMSO incubation groups. After overnight incubation at 4°C, equal aliquots of the lysate were subjected to a thermal shift assay across a temperature gradient from 37°C to 62°C in increments of 5°C. Samples were then centrifuged, denatured, and analyzed by Western blot. Proteins and the marker (Mei5 Biotechnology Co., Ltd, cat. no. MF212) were separated by SDS-PAGE and subsequently transferred onto a PVDF membrane. The membrane was then blocked and incubated sequentially with primary anti-CDKN1B primary antibody (proteintech, cat.no. 25614-1-AP, 1:4000) and secondary antibodies HRP Conjugated Affinipure Goat Anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) (Boster,cat.no.BA1054, 1:10000). Target proteins were finally visualized using a chemiluminescent substrate Omni-ECLTM chemiluminescence (Shanghai Epizyme Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd, cat. no. SQ203) and imaging system.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using R and GraphPad Prism 8 software. The comparison of gene expression between the two groups was conducted using t test. Spearman’s rank correlation was used for immune correlation calculation. Two-way ANOVA with post-hoc tests were performed for multiple comparisons in CETSA and CCK8 tests analysis. The experimental data are presented as the mean ± standard error of mean (SEM). All cell experiments were performed with three independent biological replicates. p < 0.05 indicates statistical significance.

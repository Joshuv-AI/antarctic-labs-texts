Mutual-visibility of the disjointness graph of segments in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2}


Abstract
. Let G = ( V ⁡ ( G ) , E ⁡ ( G ) ) G=(V(G),E(G)) be a simple graph, and let U ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) U\subseteq V(G) . Two distinct vertices x , y ∈ U x,y\in U are U U -mutually visible if G G contains a shortest x x - y y path that is internally disjoint from U U . U U is called a mutual-visibility set of G G if any two vertices of U U are U U -mutually visible. The mutual-visibility number μ ⁡ ( G ) \mu(G) of G G is the size of a largest mutual-visibility set of G G . Let P P be a set of n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 points in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} in general position. The disjointness graph of segments D ⁡ ( P ) D(P) of P P is the graph whose vertices are all the closed straight line segments with endpoints in P P , two of which are adjacent in D ⁡ ( P ) D(P) if and only if they are disjoint. In this paper we establish tight lower and upper bounds for μ ⁡ ( D ⁡ ( P ) ) \mu(D(P)) , and show that almost all edge disjointness graphs have diameter 2.

The following two subsections offer a brief historical overview of mutual visibility and disjointness graphs of segments, highlighting key prior results.

1.1.
Mutual visibility in graphs

In 2022, Stefano introduced the concept of mutual visibility
[
20
]
, drawing inspiration from classical Euclidean geometry problems like Sylvester’s and the “no-three-in-line” problems. Stefano
[
20
]
demonstrated that identifying a mutual-visibility set exceeding a specified size is NP-complete, while verifying a set’s mutual visibility is solvable in polynomial time. Initial connections to other graph-theoretic parameters were also established.

Building on this foundation, Cicerone, Stefano, and Klavžar
[
4
]
reduced the computation of
\mu(K_{m}\square K_{n})
to the well-known Zarankiewicz problem and characterized triangle-free graphs
G
with
\mu(G)=3
.

In 2024, Cicerone, Stefano, Klavžar, and Yero
[
5
]
introduced variations of mutual visibility, including total, outer, and dual mutual visibility, and studied the corresponding parameters in diameter-two graphs. Their analysis focused on these parameters within diameter-two graphs, exploring potential research avenues and examining computational complexity.

Another variant, the “mobile mutual-visibility number” Mob
{}_{\mu}(G)
, was defined in 2024 by Dettlaff et al.
[
8
]
, where robots placed on a mutual-visibility set must maintain the property while traversing the graph. It was shown that Mob
{}_{\mu}(G)\leq\mu(G)
, with subsequent work identifying graphs where equality holds and proving that finding the mobile mutual-visibility number is NP-hard.

Finally, exact values for the mutual-visibility number in Kneser, bipartite Kneser, and Johnson graphs were provided by Ekinci and Bujtás in 2025. Their work also established connections between total mutual visibility in Kneser and bipartite Kneser graphs and the minimum size of covering designs
[
9
]
. Further results on this topic can be found in the references of
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
9
]
.

1.2.
Disjointness graphs of segments in the plane

The concept of disjointness graphs of segments in the plane was introduced by Araujo, Dumitrescu, Hurtado, Noy, and Urrutia in 2005
[
2
]
as a geometric counterpart to Kneser graphs. Recall that for
k,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
k\leq m/2
, the Kneser graph
KG(m,k)
has vertices representing the
k
-subsets of
\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, with edges connecting disjoint
k
-subsets. In 1978
[
18
]
Lovász proved that
\chi(KG(m,k))=m-2k+2
, which was conjectured in 1955 by Kneser
[
14
]
.

The chromatic number
\chi(D(P))
of
D(P)
has been investigated in
[
2
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. However, determining the exact value of
\chi(D(P))
remains an open problem. A general lower bound for
\chi(D(P))
was established in
[
2
]
. The exact value of

\chi(D(P))
is known for two families of point sets: for sets of
n
points in convex position
C_{n}
,
\chi(D(C_{n}))=n-\lfloor\sqrt{2n+\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\rfloor
(see
[
12
,
13
]
), and for double chains with
k
points in the upper convex chain and
l\geq k
points in the lower convex chain
C_{l,k}
,
\chi(D(C_{l,k}))=k+l-\lfloor\sqrt{2l+\frac{1}{4}}-\frac{1}{2}\rfloor
(see
[
11
]
). It is well known that
\chi(D(P))\leq|P|-2
, and recently García-Davila et al.
[
7
]
proved that this upper bound is attained only if
|P|\in\{3,4,\ldots,16\}
.

Pach, Tardos, and Tóth investigated the chromatic number and clique number of
D(P)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 2
in
[
19
]
. Specifically, they demonstrated that
\chi(D(P))
is bounded above by a polynomial function of its clique number
\omega(D(P))
and that determining either
\chi(D(P))
or
\omega(D(P))
is NP-hard.

The connectivity number
\kappa(D(P))
of
D(P)
has been studied in
[
10
]
and
[
15
]
, establishing the following upper and lower bounds, respectively:

\binom{\lfloor\frac{n-2}{2}\rfloor}{2}+\binom{\lceil\frac{n-2}{2}\rceil}{2}\leq\kappa(D(P))\leq\frac{7n^{2}}{18}+\Theta(n).

We recall that
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq V(D(P))
is independent in
D(P)
if no two elements of
\mathcal{Q}
are adjacent. While Aichholzer, Kynčl, Scheucher, and Vogtenhuber established an asymptotic upper bound on the maximum size of certain independent sets in
D(P)

[
1
]
, Leaños, Ndjatchi, and Ríos-Castro
[
16
]
more recently provided a complete characterization of all those segment disjointness graphs that are Hamiltonian.

1.4.
Main results

Our goal in this section is to present our main results about the mutual-visibility number of the disjointness graphs of segments.

Recall that if
G
is a disconnected graph and

are its connected components, then
\mu(G)=\max\{\mu(G_{1}),\ldots,\mu(G_{k})\}

Our main results are the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let

be a set of

{\hbox{\rm diam}}(D(P))\in\{2,3,4\}
if

,
{\hbox{\rm diam}}(D(P))\in\{2,3\}
if

, and
{\hbox{\rm diam}}(D(P))=2
if

.

Theorem 2
.

Let

be a set of

points in general position in the plane. Then

\mu(D(P))\geq\binom{n}{2}-9
.

Proposition 3
.

Consider the set
P=\{p_{1}=(121,204),\penalty\ p_{2}=(175,196),\penalty\ p_{3}=(216,82),\penalty\ p_{4}=(189,51),\penalty\ p_{5}=(44,96),\penalty\ p_{6}=(36,140),\penalty\ p_{7}=(127,135)\}

It is easy to see that if

Proof of Proposition
6
. By the first part of the proof of Theorem
4
we need only to show
that
\mu(D(C_{p,q}))\geq{n\choose 2}-4.
Let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{p}
be the points of
C_{p,q}\subset A
from left to right. Similarly, let
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{q}
be the points of
C_{p,q}\subset B
from left to right. Let
h_{1}:=b_{1}b_{2}
,
h_{2}:=b_{3}b_{4},h_{3}:=b_{5}b_{6}
and
{\mathcal{B}}:=\{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\}
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}:=\{a_{1}a_{2}\}\cup{\mathcal{B}}
. We remark that the segments in
{\mathcal{S}}
are clean in
{\mathcal{C}}_{p,q}
and pairwise disjoint.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{C}}_{p,q}\setminus\mathcal{S}
. Consider two distinct segments
a,b\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
such that
a\cap b\neq\emptyset
. By Lemma
7
(
2
), it is enough to show that
{\mathcal{S}}
has a segment
f
such that
(a\cup b)\cap f=\emptyset
.

We may assume that
a\cup b
intersects
a_{1}a_{2}
, as otherwise
f=a_{1}a_{2}
is the required. Assume w.l.o.g. that
a
intersects to
a_{1}a_{2}
.
This last implies that
a
is disjoint from at least two segments of
{\mathcal{B}}
, say
h_{i}
and
h_{j}
. Then
b
must intersect to both

h_{i}
and
h_{j}
, as otherwise
f\in\{h_{i},h_{j}\}
. Then
b\cap h_{k}=\emptyset
for

h_{k}\in{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{h_{i},h_{j}\}
. Since if
a\cap h_{k}=\emptyset
, then
f=h_{k}
is the required, we must have that
a\cap h_{k}\neq\emptyset
, which implies
a\cap b=\emptyset
, a contradiction.
\square

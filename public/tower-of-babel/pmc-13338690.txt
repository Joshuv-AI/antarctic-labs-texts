Post-COVID-19 Resurgence of Human Adenovirus Type 3 Infection in Korean Children
Authors: Bin Ahn, Dayun Kang, Hye Jung Moon, Ki Wook Yun, Eun Hwa Choi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We investigated the distribution of human adenovirus (HAdV) types and their associated clinical features during the post-coronavirus diseases 2019 period among Korean children. After their near absence in 2020–2021, HAdV-1 and HAdV-2 began circulating in early 2022, followed by a large HAdV-3-driven outbreak between June and September 2023. Children aged 2–5 years (43.0%) were the most affected, and 33.1% presented with lower respiratory tract infections, including pneumonia (25.4%). Overall, 12.4% of the patients required oxygen therapy, including 3.5% who required intensive care. These findings highlight the post-pandemic resurgence of HAdV infections in Korea, contributing to increased healthcare demands among children.

Human adenovirus (HAdV) is a non-enveloped, double-stranded DNA virus belonging to the Mastadenovirus genus within the Adenoviridae family. It is an important pathogen in children, causing various diseases, including respiratory infections, keratoconjunctivitis, and gastroenteritis. HAdVs comprise seven species (A–G) based on their biological and molecular properties; over 100 types have been identified to date [12]. Disease severity and clinical presentation vary by type, with types 1, 2, and 5 (species C) and types 3 and 7 (species B) being most commonly linked to pediatric respiratory infections [34].

Several longitudinal studies in Korea have elucidated the epidemiological trends of HAdVs. A 17-year surveillance study (1991–2007) conducted in a single tertiary hospital identified HAdV-3 as the most prevalent type among children with acute respiratory infections, followed by HAdV-7, HAdV-2, and HAdV-1. Notably, outbreaks of fatal pneumonia associated with HAdV-7 infections were observed between 1995 and 2000 [5]. A subsequent 12-year study (2008–2019) revealed that HAdV-3 remained the most dominant type, with recurrent outbreaks occurring every 3–4 years. Species C types (HAdV-1, -2, -5, and -6) circulate endemically, whereas HAdV-7 was absent throughout the entire period, suggesting a shift in circulation patterns [6].

The emergence of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic led to a marked decline in the circulation of many respiratory viruses, including HAdVs, largely owing to non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs) such as mask-wearing and social distancing [7]. However, after the relaxation of these measures in early 2022, viruses, including respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) and influenza, re-emerged with altered seasonality [8]. Recent nationwide surveillance in Korea reported an increasing trend in HAdV activity following the COVID-19 pandemic [9]. Hence, we conducted an epidemiological study of HAdVs isolated from pediatric respiratory specimens at a single tertiary hospital between 2020 and 2024 to investigate their post-pandemic type distribution and clinical characteristics.

From January 2020 to December 2024, nasopharyngeal aspirates (NPAs) were collected for diagnostic purposes from patients aged ≤19 years who presented with respiratory symptoms or fever to the emergency department or were admitted to Seoul National University Children’s Hospital, a tertiary referral center. Only community-acquired cases were included, defined as infections with onset ≤48 hours after admission and without a history of hospitalization within the preceding 14 days. NPAs were inoculated onto HEp-2 cell monolayers and observed for cytopathic effects at 37°C for up to 4 weeks. HAdV infection was confirmed by indirect immunofluorescence; positive samples were stored at −70°C. Only HAdV-positive samples were included in this study.

The HAdV-positive samples were subjected to the hexon gene sequencing for genotyping. Viral DNA was extracted using a QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany); hypervariable regions 1–6 of the hexon gene were amplified by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and sequenced according to established protocols [10]. Sequence data were compared with reference strains retrieved from GenBank for type determination (Supplementary Table 1). All sequences were inspected, edited, and aligned using the CLC Genomics Workbench (QIAGEN, Germany). Samples that failed PCR amplification or showed ambiguous homology with reference sequences were classified as non-typeable.

We employed a descriptive epidemiological approach to analyze the data. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Age groups were categorized as follows: <1 year, 1–<2 years, 2–<5 years, and ≥5 years. Monthly and yearly trends were analyzed descriptively and illustrated using bar graphs. Data analysis and visualization were performed using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA).

A total of 142 children with HAdV infection were identified during the study period (Table 1). The overall median age was 3.2 years (interquartile range [IQR], 1.6–5.3 years). The largest proportion of cases occurred in children aged 2–<5 years (n=61, 43.0%), followed by those aged ≥5 years (n=40, 28.2%) and 1–<2 years (n=28, 19.7%), whereas infants aged <1 year accounted for the smallest proportion (n=13, 9.2%). Underlying medical conditions were present in 50.0% of the patients, including immunocompromised conditions (n=18, 12.7%), neuromuscular diseases (n=11, 7.7%), cardiovascular diseases (n=9, 6.3%), chronic lung disease (n=2, 1.4%), and prematurity (n=10, 7.0%).

Clinical manifestations varied, with fever being the most common (n=137, 96.5%), followed by respiratory symptoms (n=108, 76.1%), gastrointestinal symptoms (n=40, 28.2%), conjunctival injections (n=24, 16.9%), and neurologic symptoms (n=10, 7.0%). Prolonged fever lasting ≥7 d was observed in 21.1% of patients. Regarding clinical diagnosis, 45.8% had an upper respiratory tract infection and 33.1% had a lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI), including pneumonia (25.4%), representing 76.6% of LRTI cases.

Oxygen supplementation was required in 11.3% of patients, including 4.2% who received a high-flow nasal cannula and 3.5% who required mechanical ventilation. The median hospital length of stay was 4 d (IQR, 1–8 d); intensive care unit admission was required in 3.5% of patients. One patient (0.7%) died during hospitalization because of progressive pneumonia in the context of an underlying malignancy.

In total, 142 HAdV isolates were typed during the study period. The overall distribution of HAdV species and types is summarized in Table 2. Species B was the most common (69 isolates, 48.6%), followed by species C (47 isolates, 33.1%), whereas species D and E were identified in one (0.7%) and two (1.4%) isolates, respectively. HAdV-3 was the predominant type (64 isolates, 45.1%), followed by HAdV-2 (21 isolates, 14.8%) and HAdV-1 (18 isolates, 12.7%). Other types included HAdV-5 (7, 4.9%), HAdV-68 (4, 2.8%), HAdV-4 (2, 1.4%), HAdV-6 (1, 0.7%), HAdV-14 (1, 0.7%), and HAdV-37 (1, 0.7%). Twenty-three isolates (16.2%) remained non-typeable, largely due to failure of hexon gene PCR amplification, likely reflecting insufficient or degraded viral DNA.

The number of HAdV isolates varied substantially between years (Table 2). In 2020, no HAdV isolates were detected during the COVID-19 pandemic, followed by the detection of only one isolate in December 2021. In 2022, HAdV-1 and HAdV-2 reappeared in February and continued to circulate at low levels throughout the year. In early 2023, their circulation increased transiently but was subsequently replaced by a large outbreak caused by HAdV-3 between June and September 2023, corresponding to the sharp epidemic peak observed in Figure 1. During this period, the number of isolates surged to 116 (81.7% of all detections during the study period), followed by a marked decline to 11 by 2024.

HAdV-3, the predominant type, was detected exclusively in 2023–2024, with 63 cases (98.4%) in 2023, accounting for the outbreak. Conversely, HAdV-1 and HAdV-2 circulated more consistently from 2022 to 2024.

Overall, culture positivity rates of HAdV during the study period were 0.0% (0/1,997) in 2020, 0.06% (1/1,620) in 2021, 0.69% (14/2,039) in 2022, 3.55% (116/3,272) in 2023, and 0.66% (14/2,120) in 2024. The sharp increase in detection rate in 2023 indicates a true resurgence of HAdV circulation rather than an artifact of increased testing volume.

This study provides valuable insights into the post-pandemic epidemiology and clinical characteristics of HAdV infections in Korean children. We observed a marked resurgence of HAdV detection in 2023, following a significant decline during the COVID-19 pandemic. During the study period, HAdV-3 was the predominant type, largely driven by a substantial outbreak in 2023. Among HAdV-3 infections, preschool-aged children comprised half of the cases.

HAdV-3 was the most prevalent and primary driver of the large 2023 outbreak. This observation is consistent with previous research from our center that documented periodic outbreaks of HAdV-3 [6]. Post-COVID rebounds of HAdV-3 have also been documented in both China and the United States (US). In southern China, a large pediatric outbreak was dominated by HAdV-3 in the winter of 2023 to early 2024, with genomes closely related to the 2017 Beijing strain. This finding is consistent with the reseeding of pre-existing lineages rather than the emergence of a novel variant [11]. Similarly, in the US, national data showed that HAdV-3 predominated in 2023, with tertiary-center data likewise reporting HAdV-3 as the leading type and showing the greatest burden in children ≤5 years [1213]. In our study, children aged 2–5 years were most commonly affected (43.0%).

In Korea, a recent national surveillance study described the post-pandemic resurgence of enveloped respiratory viruses, including RSV and influenza, following the relaxation of NPIs [8]. However, because that investigation covered only the first half of 2023, the subsequent surge of HAdV, particularly the HAdV-3 outbreak that occurred in late 2023, was not captured. Our findings complement these national data by demonstrating that HAdV-3 also played a major role in pediatric hospitalizations and emergency department visits during the post-pandemic period. Nevertheless, changes in healthcare utilization and viral testing practices during and after the COVID-19 pandemic should be considered when interpreting these temporal trends. During the pandemic, emergency department visits and respiratory virus testing markedly declined owing to hospital avoidance and NPIs, whereas a rebound in healthcare utilization occurred after their relaxation [7]. These shifts may have partially influenced the observed post-pandemic increase in HAdV detection. Collectively, these observations underscore that HAdV-3, along with RSV and influenza, is a key viral pathogen contributing substantially to the pediatric healthcare burden.

Compared with non-HAdV-3 infections, children with HAdV-3 infection were older (median age 4.1 vs. 2.8 years, P=0.010) and more frequently had conjunctival involvement (26.6% vs. 9.0%, P=0.005). Indicators of clinical severity were generally comparable between groups. Mechanical ventilation was rarely required overall and was observed only in children with non-HAdV-3 infections (Supplementary Table 2). These findings suggest that the post-pandemic HAdV-3 resurgence primarily affected older preschool-aged children, without a marked increase in overall clinical severity compared with non-HAdV-3 infections.

Compared with data from an earlier cohort at our institution, the proportion of HAdV-3 cases among children aged <1 year was significantly lower during the study period (13.3% vs. 7.8%, P=0.037), accompanied by a trend toward an older median age, although this difference was not statistically significant (3.2 vs. 4.1 years, P=0.059) [6]. Despite these age-related shifts, the overall clinical severity of HAdV-3 infection did not differ substantially between the two periods. Key severity indicators, including prolonged fever, lower respiratory tract infection, oxygen requirement, use of high-flow nasal cannula, and mechanical ventilation, were comparable before and after the COVID-19 pandemic. HAdV-3 remained the predominant and clinically relevant genotype, whereas species C types (mainly HAdV-1 and -2) circulated consistently throughout 2022–2024, reflecting their endemic patterns [6]. No HAdV-7 isolates were identified, contrasting with type 7-associated severe pneumonia outbreaks reported in the late 1990s [13].

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective, single-center design may not fully reflect the nationwide epidemiology, although the tertiary referral nature of our hospital provides a broad catchment and robust sample size. Approximately half of the patients had underlying medical conditions, which may have influenced severity outcomes and should be considered when generalizing these findings to otherwise healthy children. Nonetheless, these data provide a detailed snapshot of post-pandemic HAdV activity in Korean children, encompassing both epidemiological and clinical characteristics. The study also captured the reestablishment of endemic circulation and the large HAdV-3-driven outbreak.

In conclusion, our findings highlight the post-pandemic resurgence of HAdV infections in the pediatric population, driven by a large HAdV-3 outbreak that contributed to the increased demand for healthcare services. Continuous surveillance of HAdV types is essential for responding to future outbreaks, adapting clinical guidelines, and informing public health strategies.

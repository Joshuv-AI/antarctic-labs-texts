On the Maximum Number of Spanning Trees in C 4 C_{4} -Free Graphs
András London

Abstract
. We introduce a “Kirchhoff–Turán” variant of the extremal C 4 C_{4} problem: among all simple connected n n -vertex C 4 C_{4} -free graphs G G , maximize the number of spanning trees τ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G) . For the projective-plane orders n = q 2 + q + 1 n=q^{2}+q+1 we compute an exact formula for the Erdős–Rényi orthogonal polarity graph E ​ R q ER_{q} , namely τ ⁡ ( E ​ R q ) = n ( n − 3 ) / 2 \tau(ER_{q})=n^{(n-3)/2} , via a polarity spectral identity and Kirchhoff’s matrix–tree theorem. We also give an explicit general upper bound on st ⁡ ( n , C 4 ) \mathrm{st}(n,C_{4}) at these n n using a sharp degree-sequence inequality for τ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G) and a degree-balancing argument; this matches the lower bound in the leading exponential term.

1.
The spanning-tree Turán problem for
C_{4}

For a connected graph
G
, let
\tau(G)
denote the number of spanning trees of
G
.

Definition 1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

\mathrm{st}(n,C_{4}):=\max\{\tau(G):|V(G)|=n,\;G\text{ is simple, connected, and }C_{4}\text{-free}\}.

Remark 1
.

Adding an edge strictly increases
\tau(G)
, so every maximizer in Definition
1

is edge-maximal
C_{4}
-free. (Connectivity may be imposed since disconnected graphs have
\tau(G)=0
.)

Warm-up: the triangle-free case.

Define analogously

\mathrm{st}(n,C_{3}):=\max\{\tau(G):|V(G)|=n,\;G\text{ is simple, connected, and }C_{3}\text{-free}\}.

Mantel’s theorem identifies the edge-extremal
C_{3}
-free graph as the balanced complete bipartite graph

K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}
. Moreover, for complete bipartite graphs one has

\tau(K_{a,b})=a^{\,b-1}b^{\,a-1}.

Thus a natural guess is that
K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}
also maximizes
\tau(G)
among
triangle-free graphs. In contrast, for
C_{4}
-freeness the edge-extremal constructions at the projective-plane
orders
n=q^{2}+q+1
come from finite geometry (polarity graphs), suggesting that the corresponding
Kirchhoff–Turán problem may single out these algebraic constructions.

2.
Two ingredients: a degree bound and degree balancing

Theorem 1

(Klee–Narayanan–Sauermann
[
1
]
)
.

For every simple graph
G
on
n
vertices,

\tau(G)\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\prod_{v\in V(G)}(\deg(v)+1).

Remark 2
.

Inequalities upper-bounding
\tau(G)
by expressions involving degree data go back further;
for background and related references see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.

Lemma 1

(Balancing)
.

Fix integers
n\geq 1
and
S\geq 0
.
Among all integer sequences
(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
with
\sum_{i}d_{i}=S
,
the product
\prod_{i}(d_{i}+1)
is maximized when the degrees differ by at most
1
,
i.e.
d_{i}\in\{a,a+1\}
for some integer
a
.

Proof.

If
x\geq y+2
, replacing
(x,y)
by
(x-1,y+1)
preserves the sum and changes the factor

(x+1)(y+1)
to
x(y+2)
. Since
x(y+2)-(x+1)(y+1)=x-y-1\geq 1
, the product increases.
Iterating removes all gaps
\geq 2
.
∎

Theorem 2

(Füredi; see e.g.
[
5
,
4
]
)
.

If
q\geq 14
is an integer and
n=q^{2}+q+1
, then

\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4})\leq\frac{1}{2}\,q(q+1)^{2}.

3.
Orthogonal polarity graphs and an exact spanning-tree count

We follow the standard projective-plane definition (see
[
2
, §1]
).

Definition 2

(Orthogonal polarity graph
ER_{q}
)
.

Let
q
be a prime power and let
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
be the Desarguesian projective plane:
its points are the 1-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
.
Fix an
orthogonal polarity

\varphi
, i.e. a polarity with exactly
q+1
absolute points

[
2
, §1]
.
The (simple) orthogonal polarity graph
ER_{q}
has vertex set the points of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
, and
distinct vertices
p,p^{\prime}
are adjacent if and only if
p\in\varphi(p^{\prime})
.

Remark 3

(Degrees)
.

Let
n=q^{2}+q+1
.
In the looped polarity-graph model (loops at absolute points), the polarity graph is
(q+1)
-regular.
Deleting the
q+1
loops produces the simple graph
ER_{q}
, in which the
q+1
absolute points have
degree
q
and the remaining
q^{2}
vertices have degree
q+1
; see
[
2
, §1]
.

Loop convention

We briefly use the
looped
polarity-graph model: a loop at a vertex contributes
+1
to
the degree and
+1
to the diagonal entry of the adjacency matrix. Hence loops cancel in the Laplacian

L=D-A
, so the Laplacian (and therefore
\tau
) is unchanged if one deletes loops.

Lemma 2

(Spectrum of polarity graphs; multiplicities from absolute points
[
3
, Lem. 2.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a polarity graph of a projective plane of order
q
in the looped model,
with adjacency matrix
A
and
n=q^{2}+q+1
.
Then

A^{2}=J+qI.

Consequently, the adjacency eigenvalues are
q+1
and
\pm\sqrt{q}
.
Moreover,
q+1
has multiplicity
1
, and the multiplicities of
\pm\sqrt{q}

are uniquely determined by
\operatorname{tr}(A)
, i.e. by the number of absolute points (loops).

Proposition 1

(Exact formula for
\tau(ER_{q})
)
.

Let
q
be a prime power and
n=q^{2}+q+1
. Then

\tau(ER_{q})=n^{\frac{n-3}{2}}.

Proof.

Work in the looped model (loops do not affect the Laplacian). By Lemma
2
,
the adjacency eigenvalues are
q+1
and
\pm\sqrt{q}
.

For an orthogonal polarity, there are exactly
q+1
absolute points
[
2
, §1]
,
so
\operatorname{tr}(A)=q+1
. Lemma
2
then forces the multiplicities of

+\sqrt{q}
and
-\sqrt{q}
to be equal, i.e. each equals
(n-1)/2
.

Since the looped polarity graph is
(q+1)
-regular,
L=(q+1)I-A
and the nonzero Laplacian eigenvalues
are
(q+1)\pm\sqrt{q}
, each with multiplicity
(n-1)/2
.
Kirchhoff’s matrix–tree theorem in spectral form yields

\tau(ER_{q})=\frac{1}{n}\Bigl((q+1)-\sqrt{q}\Bigr)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Bigl((q+1)+\sqrt{q}\Bigr)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}=\frac{1}{n}\bigl((q+1)^{2}-q\bigr)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}=\frac{1}{n}\,n^{\frac{n-1}{2}}=n^{\frac{n-3}{2}}.

∎

In particular, for
n=q^{2}+q+1
we have the lower bound

\mathrm{st}(n,C_{4})\geq\tau(ER_{q})=n^{\frac{n-3}{2}}.

Remark 4

(Connectivity of
ER_{q}
)
.

In the looped model the graph is
(q+1)
-regular and has adjacency eigenvalue
q+1
with multiplicity
1

(Lemma
2
). For a regular graph, the multiplicity of the eigenvalue equal to the
degree equals the number of connected components. Hence the looped polarity graph is connected, and therefore
the simple graph
ER_{q}
is connected as well.

4.
An explicit upper bound at
n=q^{2}+q+1

Proposition 2
.

Let
n=q^{2}+q+1
with
q\geq 14
. For every
C_{4}
-free graph
G
on
n
vertices,

\tau(G)\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,(q+1)^{q+1}\,(q+2)^{\,n-(q+1)}.

Consequently,

\mathrm{st}(n,C_{4})\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,(q+1)^{q+1}\,(q+2)^{\,n-(q+1)}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2
,
e(G)\leq\frac{1}{2}q(q+1)^{2}
, hence

\sum_{v\in V(G)}\deg(v)=2e(G)\leq q(q+1)^{2}=:S_{\max}.

The right-hand side of Theorem
1
is monotone in each degree, so under the
single

constraint
\sum_{v}\deg(v)\leq S_{\max}
it is maximized when
\sum_{v}\deg(v)=S_{\max}
.
Lemma
1
then shows that among all integer sequences of sum
S_{\max}
,
the product
\prod_{v}(\deg(v)+1)
is maximized by degrees in
\{q,q+1\}
with exactly
q+1
copies of
q

and
n-(q+1)
copies of
q+1
, since

S_{\max}=q(q^{2}+q+1)+q^{2}=qn+(n-(q+1)).

Thus

\prod_{v}(\deg(v)+1)\leq(q+1)^{q+1}(q+2)^{n-(q+1)},

and Theorem
1
gives the bound on
\tau(G)
.
∎

Remark 5

(Matching the leading exponential term)
.

Combining Proposition
1
and Proposition
2
gives, for
n=q^{2}+q+1
,

\log\mathrm{st}(n,C_{4})=\frac{n}{2}\log n+O(\sqrt{n}\log n),

so the lower and upper bounds agree in the leading
\frac{n}{2}\log n
term.

5.
A quantitative deficit bound in a near-extremal regime

For
n=q^{2}+q+1
define the balanced-product envelope

P(S):=\max\Big\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+1):d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}=S\Big\}.

By Lemma
1
, writing
S=na+r
with
a=\lfloor S/n\rfloor
and
0\leq r<n
, we have

(1)

P(S)=(a+2)^{r}(a+1)^{n-r}.

Lemma 3

(One-step ratio)
.

For every
S\geq 1
, writing
S=na+r
with
a=\lfloor S/n\rfloor
and
0\leq r<n
,

\frac{P(S-1)}{P(S)}\leq\frac{a+1}{a+2}\leq\exp\!\Big(-\frac{1}{a+2}\Big).

Proof.

From (
1
), if
r\geq 1
then
P(S)/P(S-1)=(a+2)/(a+1)
, hence

P(S-1)/P(S)=(a+1)/(a+2)
. If
r=0
, then
S=na
and
S-1=n(a-1)+(n-1)
, giving

P(S)/P(S-1)=(a+1)/a
and so
P(S-1)/P(S)=a/(a+1)\leq(a+1)/(a+2)
.
Finally,
(a+1)/(a+2)=1-\frac{1}{a+2}\leq e^{-1/(a+2)}
.
∎

Proposition 3

(Edge deficit suppresses
\tau
(for
t\leq q^{2}/2
))
.

Let
q\geq 14
,
n=q^{2}+q+1
, and let
G
be
C_{4}
-free on
n
vertices with

e(G)=\frac{1}{2}q(q+1)^{2}-t\qquad(0\leq t\leq q^{2}/2).

Then

\tau(G)\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,(q+1)^{q+1}\,(q+2)^{\,n-(q+1)}\cdot\exp\!\Big(-\frac{2t}{q+2}\Big).

Proof.

Write
S:=\sum_{v}\deg(v)=2e(G)=S_{\max}-2t
, where
S_{\max}:=q(q+1)^{2}
.
By Theorem
1
,
\tau(G)\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}P(S)
.

Iterating Lemma
3
for
S_{\max}-S=2t
steps gives

P(S)\leq P(S_{\max})\exp\!\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{2t}\frac{1}{a_{j}+2}\Big),\qquad a_{j}:=\Big\lfloor\frac{S_{\max}-j}{n}\Big\rfloor.

Since
S_{\max}=q(q+1)^{2}=qn+q^{2}
, so for every
1\leq j\leq 2t
we have

\frac{S_{\max}-j}{n}\leq\frac{S_{\max}-1}{n}=q+\frac{q^{2}-1}{q^{2}+q+1}<q+1,

hence
a_{j}\leq q
. Therefore

\tfrac{1}{a_{j}+2}\geq\tfrac{1}{q+2}

for all
1\leq j\leq 2t
,
which yields

P(S)\leq P(S_{\max})\exp\!\Big(-\frac{2t}{q+2}\Big).

Finally,
P(S_{\max})=(q+1)^{q+1}(q+2)^{n-(q+1)}
as in Proposition
2
.
∎

Conjecture 1
.

Let
q
be a prime power and
n=q^{2}+q+1
. Then

\mathrm{st}(n,C_{4})=\tau(ER_{q})=n^{\frac{n-3}{2}},

and the maximizers are precisely the orthogonal polarity graphs.

Remark 6
.

A plausible route to Conjecture
1
is to connect near-maximality of
\tau(G)
to near-extremality of

e(G)
, and then invoke known stability results for
C_{4}
-free graphs to deduce that
G
is close to a polarity
construction. Monotonicity of
\tau
under edge addition (Remark
1
) would then force equality.

6.
Future directions

6.1.
The case of general
n

While the polarity graphs
ER_{q}
provide a natural candidate when
n=q^{2}+q+1
,
the behavior of
\mathrm{st}(n,C_{4})
for general
n
remains open.

Problem 1.

Determine the asymptotic behavior of
\log\mathrm{st}(n,C_{4})
for
n
far from projective-plane orders.

Problem 2.

Establish a “spectral stability” result: if a
C_{4}
-free graph
G
satisfies

\tau(G)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\tau(ER_{q})
, then
G
is close to
ER_{q}
in edit distance.

6.2.
Forbidding
K_{2,t}
and
C_{2k}

The definition extends to any forbidden subgraph
H
by

\mathrm{st}(n,H):=\max\{\tau(G):|V(G)|=n,\;G\text{ is simple, connected, and }H\text{-free}\}.

The inequality of Klee–Narayanan–Sauermann (Theorem
1
) applies verbatim for all
H
.
Hence, once one has an upper bound on the degree sum (via
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)
), Lemma
1

yields explicit (envelope) upper bounds on
\mathrm{st}(n,H)
.

6.2.1.
The family
K_{2,t}

Fix
t\geq 2
. If
G
is
K_{2,t}
-free then any two vertices have at most
t-1
common neighbors,
which implies the standard 2-path counting inequality:

\sum_{v\in V(G)}\binom{\deg(v)}{2}\leq(t-1)\binom{n}{2}.

Moreover,
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t})=O(\sqrt{t}\,n^{3/2})
by the Kővári–Sós–Turán theorem
[
7
]
,
and a sharp asymptotic upper bound due to Füredi
[
8
]
gives

(2)

\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{t-1}\,n^{3/2}+O(n)\qquad(t\ \text{fixed}).

Proposition 4
.

Fix
t\geq 2
. There exists a constant
C_{t}>0
such that for all
n
and all
K_{2,t}
-free graphs
G
on
n
vertices,

\tau(G)\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigl(1+C_{t}\sqrt{n}\Bigr)^{n}.

Consequently,

\log\mathrm{st}(n,K_{2,t})\leq\frac{n}{2}\log n+O_{t}(n).

Proof.

From (
2
),
\sum_{v}\deg(v)=2e(G)\leq C_{t}n^{3/2}
for some
C_{t}
.
By Theorem
1
it suffices to upper bound
\prod_{v}(\deg(v)+1)
under the single constraint

\sum_{v}\deg(v)\leq C_{t}n^{3/2}
. Lemma
1
shows the product is maximized when the degrees
are as equal as possible, i.e. average degree
O(\sqrt{n})
, yielding
\prod_{v}(\deg(v)+1)\leq(1+C_{t}\sqrt{n})^{n}
.
∎

6.2.2.
Even cycles
C_{2k}

Fix
k\geq 2
. The theorem of Bondy–Simonovits
[
9
]
implies

\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})=O_{k}(n^{1+1/k})
. Sharper bounds are known; for instance
Bukh–Jiang
[
10
]
and He
[
11
]
prove bounds of the form

(3)

\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{2k})\leq C_{k}\,n^{1+1/k}+O_{k}(n).

Proposition 5
.

Fix
k\geq 2
. There exists a constant
D_{k}>0
such that for all
n
and all
C_{2k}
-free graphs
G
on
n
vertices,

\tau(G)\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigl(1+D_{k}n^{1/k}\Bigr)^{n}.

Consequently,

\log\mathrm{st}(n,C_{2k})\leq\frac{n}{k}\log n+O_{k}(n).

Proof.

From (
3
),
\sum_{v}\deg(v)=2e(G)\leq 2C_{k}n^{1+1/k}+O_{k}(n)
.
As before, Lemma
1
yields the envelope bound with average degree
O_{k}(n^{1/k})
.
∎

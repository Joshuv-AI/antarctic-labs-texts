Controlling the initial separation distance between two electron plasma vortices in a Malmberg–Penning trap
Authors: A Ogawa, Y Nakajima, A Sanpei, H Himura
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a novel method to continuously control the initial separation distance d between two plasma vortices of electrons. In this method, the two electron columns are initially confined individually in two coaxial Malmberg–Penning traps with different axial lengths. They rotate around the trap axis at different rotation frequencies due to the drift. Their different rotation rates cause periodic changes in their relative azimuthal positions. The initial conditions are established when the two columns are stretched along magnetic field lines of a unified Malmberg–Penning trap. This method precisely provides a simple and continuous adjustment of the initial d and facilitates efficient investigation of the interaction of the two plasma electron vortices.Keywords: Vortex, Merging, Malmberg–Penning trap, Nonneutral plasmaSubject terms: Engineering, Physics

Introduction

Vortex motion in a potential field has long interested various scientific fields1–4. In plasma physics, electron plasma columns confined in a Malmberg–Penning trap have been used to experimentally investigate the interaction and merging of two-dimensional (2-D) vortices5–8. When the fast axial bouncing of electrons averages over the axial variation and the 2-D guiding center theory is valid, the electron columns evolve according to the 2-D Euler equation. For this condition, the vorticity is proportional to the electron density9. The initial radial distribution of the electrons evolves as an identical initial vorticity distribution observed in a 2-D ideal fluid. Past studies10–12 found that if the ratio of the separation distance between two vortices to the vortex radii was less than a critical value, the vortices merged into a single vortex. Therefore, the initialization process for the distance d between two electron columns is crucial to investigate the vortex merging in electron plasmas.

Past experiments used two methods different from the one presented in this paper to create two electron columns with a specific d. A “static” method generated two electron columns with a desired d using two fixed electron sources installed separately by d in the vacuum vessel in advance13. In contrast, a “dynamic” method produced the initial condition by manipulating the trapped electron columns6. The static method straightforwardly created the initial d. However, because the electron sources were installed in a vacuum vessel, it was difficult to freely change their positions during experiments. The dynamic method could arbitrarily vary the positions of the electron columns through a suitable operation, and some manipulation techniques were established14. However, the operation conducted in the previous study6 required complex manipulation. The electron columns were manipulated in operation by an azimuthal-divided electrode into four sectors. The quick application of voltages to the two sectors, which separated diagonally, induced a drift in an electron column. Consequently, its center of mass began to move. After the electron column drifted off the machine axis to a desired position, the electron column axially separated into two independent columns by externally applying voltage to the segmented electrode. Subsequently, the two independent axially separated columns moved to symmetric positions about the trap axis and expanded axially into the original region.

Such a complex manipulation was required to initialize the vorticity distribution of the two electron vortices separated from each other by d. On the other hand, this study clearly demonstrates a more refined way of creating the initial conditions for d. This novel approach arbitrarily controls d using the synchronized motions of the two electron columns.

Thermionic electrons enter each trap from the left-hand side of Electrode I at and form the electron columns. An off-axis displaced electron column in a cylindrical trap rotates around the machine axis of the traps due to drift. This rotation is understood as the finite length diocotron mode. The radial electric field driving the azimuthal drift has contributions from the image charges on the wall and the confining end potentials, and the drift velocity is determined by the z-averaged at the center of the column15.

Previous studies16,17 found that the diocotron frequency depended on the axial energy distribution of the electrons because it determines the position of the reflection at the end potential. Therefore, the relaxation of the energy distribution decreases the z-averaged and the diocotron frequency. However, before their energy distribution relaxes sufficiently, the electron columns have an almost constant axial length and an azimuthal velocity. According to past work, the finite diocotron frequency also depends on the confined plasma length because it affects the -averaged : the longer the plasma, the weaker the z-averaged 15,18. In this operation, we set the confining time short enough to allow the columns to have constant frequencies. In addition, we create two traps with different axial lengths, and , to make the two columns rotate with different frequencies.

The proposed operation consists of three steps. First, an electron column forms at in the right trap by electrodes I and II. The trapped electron column starts to rotate around the machine axis at a frequency of . After the predetermined time, the second electron column appears at in the left trap by electrodes II and III. The second trapped electron column also rotates around the machine axis independently at a frequency of .

Due to the difference frequency , the relative difference in the coordinate of the two electron columns changes correspondingly. We will come back to this point later to explain the details of this manipulation. After confining the two columns independently and separately, we unified the two traps by grounding electrode II. Consequently, the two electron columns, which have the same length but different densities, coexist simultaneously because the longer trap confined more electrons than the shorter one. Because the strength of the vortex is proportional to the number of electrons, we can adjust the strength of the electron vortex by varying the original axial length of the column. The ratio of the vortex strength can thus be determined by the ratio of the two lengths of the trap , and d can be precisely preset by the relative difference in the coordinate when the traps are unified. Consequently, the desired vortex intensity ratio and d, are precisely set. After that, the two vortices mutually interact in the unified trap by electrodes I and III.

After the initial conditions are attained, the two electron vortices immediately interact while orbiting the machine axis. They first corotate around each other and then merge to become a single asymmetric electron vortex. In this measurement, only the weaker vortex is stretched and destroyed. Such a phenomenon was classified as “straining-out” in the past study about unequal vortex merging21. In addition, we have confirmed that the shorter the initial d, the faster the rate of the vortex merging as reported in the previous study6. These results indicate that the presented method can reproducibly and accurately create identical initial conditions to observe that the slight variation in d affects the vortex merging dynamics. Therefore, this method is of use to experimentally investigate the effect of the inequality of the vortices and the initial d on the vortex merging.

Summary

In summary, we presented a novel method for continuously adjusting the initial distance between two electron plasma vortices by varying their rotation frequencies, determined by their different axial lengths. As anticipated, this approach enables precise control over the separation distance. Its advantages include simplified manipulation, flexible separation adjustment, and the absence of dedicated devices. Moreover, this method facilitates the efficient exploration of the interaction and merging of asymmetric vortices, which are still incompletely understood. This method promises to elucidate the relationship between the initial distance of two vortices and their merger dynamics by continuously adjusting the parameters.

A priori error estimates for finite element discretization of semilinear elliptic equations with non-Lipschitz nonlinearities
Boris Vexler

Abstract
In this paper we develop numerical analysis for finite element discretization of semilinear elliptic equations with potentially non-Lipschitz nonlinearites. The nonlinearity is essecially assumed to be continuous and monotonically decreasing including the case of non-Lipschitz or even non-Hölder continuous nonlinearities. For a direct discretization (without any regularization) with linear finite elements we derive error estimates with respect to various norms and illustrate these results with a numerical example.

Introduction

In this paper we consider a semilinear elliptic equation of the following type

\displaystyle-\upDelta u(x)+d(x,u(x))

\displaystyle=f(x)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega

(1)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega

with a nonlinearity
d\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, posted on a convex polygonal/polyhedral domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N=2,3
. Essentially we only assume that the nonlinearity
d
is monotonically non-decreasing and continuous with respect to its second argument
u
, see below for precise assumptions. This means, that non-differential, non-Lipschitz and even non-Hölder continuous nonlinearities are allowed. A typical example is a combustion type nonlinearity

d(x,u)=\varphi(x)r(u-\psi(x))\quad\text{with }\;r(u)=\operatorname{sgn}(u)\lvert u\rvert^{s},\quad 0<s<1,

and some
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\varphi(x)\geq 0
for almost all
x\in\Omega
, see
remark

2
below. Such nonlinearities play a role by modeling of combustion problems and of flow problems in porous media, see,
[
16
,
13
]
and the references therein. We refer also to
[
6
]
for recent results on optimal control of semilinear elliptic equations with such nonlinearities.

For the finite element approximation
u_{h}
in the space of linear finite elements
V_{h}
, see below for details, we will derive a priori error estimates for the error
u-u_{h}
. For the case of a Lipschitz continuous nonlinearity error estimates are well known and have the same quality as for discretization of linear equations, i.e.,

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h\lVert\nabla(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2-\frac{N}{2}},

where the constant
c
depends on
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and the nonlinearity
d
, see, e.g.,
[
5
]
. The case of a non-Lipschitz continuous nonlinearity is treated in
[
16
]
using a regularization approach. There, the domain
\Omega
is assumed to be smooth and an
L^{1}
-
L^{\infty}
duality argument is used to prove error estimates for
u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon h}
with respect to the
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
norm, where
u_{\varepsilon}
is the solution of the equation with the regularized non-linearity and
u_{\varepsilon h}
is its finite element approximation. Similar approach is also applied in
[
1
]
for a special Hölder continuous nonlinearity, see also
[
2
]
. In
[
13
]
a parabolic problem with a Hölder continuous nonlinearity on a smooth domain is considered. There, also a variant of an
L^{1}
-
L^{\infty}
duality argument is applied without any regularization to prove error estimates for a semi-discretization in space. We mention also the paper
[
11
]
, where error estimates for the obstacle problem on a polygonal/polyhedral domain are shown. The authors prove there an interior pointwise estimate and then an
L^{2}
estimate on strip along the boundary. This approach results in an optimal (up to some logarithmic terms) estimate with respect to the
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm. We take a similar approach for the problem under consideration.

In the following we describe our contributions. For all results we do not require smoothness of the boundary of the domain
\Omega
. We assume throughout that
\Omega
is polygonal for
N=2
, polyhedral for
N=3
and convex. Moreover, we do not require any regularization of the nonlinearity.

•

Under general assumptions (see
1
below) on the nonlinearity
d
(essentially monotonicity and continuity with respect to the second argument) we prove uniform boundedness of
\lVert u_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, see
corollary

18
and the estimates

\lVert\nabla(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2-\frac{N}{2}}

for
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, see
corollary

20
and
theorem

23
. For
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
the pointwise estimate is improved to

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{1+\gamma}\lvert\ln h\rvert

for every
0<\gamma<\gamma_{\Omega}
with some
\gamma_{\Omega}\in(0,1]
depending only on
\Omega
, see
corollary

21
.

•

Under an additional assumption (see
4
below), that the nonlinearity is Lipschitz-continuous in a neighborhood of boundary values, i.e., in our case in the interval
[-\rho,\rho]
for some
\rho>0
, we prove

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert^{2}

for an interior subdomain
\Omega_{0}
with
\bar{\Omega}_{0}\subset\Omega
and
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, see
theorem

24
.
Moreover, we obtain the global estimate

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert^{2}

under the same assumptions, see
theorem

27
, which is our main result.

Throughout the paper we use standard notation for Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces.

The paper is structured as follows: In the following section we state precise assumptions on the nonlinearity
d
and discuss properties of the solution of
eq.

1

Section

Section

1
.
Section

2
Semilinear and dual equations

In this section we specify assumptions on the nonlinearity
d
and discuss existence, uniqueness and regularity for the state equation under consideration. Moreover, we discuss a dual equation to be used in the error analysis below.

Assumption 1
.

For the nonlinearity
d\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
we assume

•

For every
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, there holds
d(\cdot,u_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)

•

For almost all
x\in\Omega
, the function
d(x,\cdot)\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is monotonically non-decreasing

•

For every
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
the nonlinearity
d
is equicontinuous at
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
in the following sense: For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\lvert d(x,u)-d(x,u_{0})\rvert<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }u\in\mathbb{R}\text{ with }\lvert u-u_{0}\rvert<\delta\text{ and }\text{for almost all }x\in\Omega.

Remark 2
.

For the class of nonlinearities given
by

d(x,u)=\varphi(x)r(u-\psi(x))

with continuous and monotonically non-decreasing
r\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\varphi(x)\geq 0
for almost all
x\in\Omega
,
1
is fulfilled. To see this, one only has to discuss the equicontinuity. For given
u_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
consider the interval
I=[u_{0}-2\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},u_{0}+2\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}]
. The function
r
is continuous and therefore
uniformly continuous on
I
. Thus, for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
, which can be chosen
with
\delta<\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, such that

\lvert r(\xi)-r(\eta)\rvert<\frac{\varepsilon}{\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\quad\text{for all }\xi,\eta\in I\text{ with }\lvert\xi-\eta\rvert<\delta.

Let now
u\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert u-u_{0}\rvert<\delta
. Then

u+\psi(x),u_{0}+\psi(x)\in I\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert(u+\psi(x))-(u_{0}+\psi(x))\rvert<\delta\quad\text{for almost all }x\in\Omega.

Therefore we obtain

\lvert d(x,u)-d(x,u_{0})\rvert<\varepsilon\quad\text{for almost all }x\in\Omega.

In the following, we will use the fact that a nonlinearity
d
fulfilling

1
is
uniformly equicontinuous
on every bounded interval.

Lemma 3
.

Let
d\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
fulfill
1
. Then
d
, is uniformly equicontinuous on every compact interval
I\subset R
in the following sense: For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that

\lvert d(x,u)-d(x,v)\rvert<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }u,v\in I\text{ with }\lvert u-v\rvert<\delta\text{ and almost all }x\in\Omega.

Proof.

The proof follows the lines of the classical proof that every continuous function is uniformly continuous on compact intervals.

For some of the results below we will require an additional assumption on the nonlinearity
d
.

Assumption 4
.

There are
\rho>0
and
L>0
such that for every
u,v\in[-\rho,\rho]
there holds

\lvert d(x,u)-d(x,v)\rvert\leq L\lvert u-v\rvert

for allmost all
x\in\Omega
.

Remark 5
.

The above assumption is fulfilled, e.g., for the nonlinearity

d(x,u)=\varphi(x)r(u-\psi(x))\quad\text{with }\;r(u)=\operatorname{sgn}(u)\lvert u\rvert^{s},\quad 0<s<1,

and some
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

\varphi(x)\geq 0,\;\psi(x)\geq\delta>0

for almost all
x\in\Omega
.

Remark 6
.

4
requires Lipschitz continuity in a neighborhood of boundary values. It can be correspondingly modified in the case of non-homogeneous Dirirchlet boundary conditions.

In the sequel we will use also a modified (cut) nonlinearity
d_{M}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to R
with a cutting parameter
M>0
:

d_{M}(x,u)=\begin{cases}d(x,-M),&\text{for }u<-M,\\
d(x,u),&\text{for }{-M}\leq u\leq M,\\
d(x,M),&\text{for }u>M.\\
\end{cases}

(2)

Lemma 7
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with some
p>\frac{N}{2}
and let
1
be fulfilled. Then there is a unique weak solution
u_{M}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
fulfilling

(\nabla u_{M},\nabla\varphi)+(d_{M}(u_{M}),\varphi)=(f,\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(3)

Moreover, there is a constant
c
independent of
M
such that

\lVert\nabla u_{M}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert u_{M}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert d(0)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right).

Proof.

The uniqueness follows directly from monotonicity of
d
. The proof of existence of
u_{M}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is based on theory of monotone operators, see
[
20
, Theorem 26.A]
and follows the lines of the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 4.4]
(by changing Neumann to Dirichlet boundary conditions). The uniform (in
M
) boundedness of
\lVert u_{M}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
is based on the Stampacchia methods
[
12
]
and the ideas from
[
4
]
, see
[
19
, Theorem 4.7]
for details.

The uniform (in
M
) boundedness of
\lVert u_{M}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
leads directly to the existence of a solution with the original nonlinearity.

Theorem 8
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with some
p>\frac{N}{2}
and let
1
be fulfilled. Then there is a unique weak solution
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
fulfilling

(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)+(d(u),\varphi)=(f,\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Moreover, there is a constant
c
such that

\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert d(0)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right).

If
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
then
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
and the following estimate holds

\lVert\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert d(M)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\lVert d(-M)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right),

where

M=c\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert d(0)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)

and the constant
c
is independent of
f
and
d
.

Proof.

The existence, uniqueness and the first estimate follow directly from the previous lemma. Then a bootstrapping argument and
H^{2}
regularity on convex domains for the linear Poisson equation lead to the corresponding estimate for
\lVert\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

For the numerical analysis below we require the dual equation

\displaystyle-\upDelta z(x)+b(x)z(x)

\displaystyle=\psi(x)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega

(4)

\displaystyle z(x)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega

for given
b\geq 0
and
\psi
to be specified later. The following
result, cf.
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
, provides an estimate with a constant independent of
b
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
b\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
b\geq 0
almost everywhere on

\Omega
. Then, the solution
z\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
of

(\nabla z,\nabla\varphi)+(bz,\varphi)=(\psi,\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

(5)

fulfills

\lVert z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\upDelta z\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\lVert bz\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

where constant
c
on the right-hand side of the estimate is independent of
b
and
\psi
.

3
Finite element discretization

Let
\set{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
be a family of shape-regular, quasi-uniform meshes. Let
V_{h}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
be the space linear finite elements on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We will use Lagrange interpolation operator
i_{h}\colon C_{0}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
, which fulfills standard interpolation estimates. Moreover, we will use the Ritz projection
R_{h}\colon W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
defined by

(\nabla(u-R_{h}u),\nabla\varphi_{h})=0\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}.

(6)

We will use the following estimates for
u-R_{h}u
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then there holds

\lVert u-R_{h}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h\lVert\nabla(u-R_{h}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert\upDelta u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

and

\lVert u-R_{h}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2-\frac{N}{2}}\lVert\upDelta u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

These results are classical, see, e.g.,
[
3
]
.

The following improved pointwise estimate holds provided
\upDelta u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 11
.

There is
\gamma_{\Omega}\in(0,1]
depending only on the domain
\Omega
and a constant
c>0
such that for every
0<\gamma<\gamma_{\Omega}
and every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\upDelta u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
there holds

\lVert u-R_{h}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{1+\gamma}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert\upDelta u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

First we discuss the Hölder continuity of the derivatives of the solution
u
to a linear elliptic equation

\displaystyle-\upDelta u(x)

\displaystyle=f(x)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega

with
f=-\upDelta u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. For
N=2
we can use convexity of
\Omega
and argue by
W^{2,p}
regularity. By
[
9
, Theorem 4.4.3.7]
there exists
p_{\Omega}>2
such that
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
holds for every
2\leq p<p_{\Omega}
. By a classical embedding theorem we that obtain the existence of
0<\gamma_{\Omega}\leq 1
such that
u\in C^{1,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})
holds for every
0<\gamma<\gamma_{\Omega}
with

\lVert u\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

For
N=3
the corresponding result follows from Hölder estimates of derivatives of the Green’s function on a convex polyhedral domain, see
[
10
, Theorem 1]
.

As the next step we use a best approximation (up to a logarithmic term) result on convex domains for the error in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

\lVert u-R_{h}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\lvert\ln h\rvert\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\lVert u-v_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

see
[
7
]
for
N=2
and
[
14
]
for
N=3
. We choose
v_{h}=i_{h}u
and obtain by standard interpolation estimates

\lVert u-R_{h}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u-i_{h}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{1+\gamma}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq ch^{1+\gamma}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

This completes the proof.

Remark 12
.

For
N=2
, we have

\gamma_{\Omega}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{\Omega}-1&\text{for }\lambda_{\Omega}<2,\\
1&\text{for }\lambda_{\Omega}\geq 2,\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{\Omega}=\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{\omega_{\Omega}}}
and
\omega_{\Omega}
is the largest interior angle of
\Omega
. For
N=3
, the value of
\gamma_{\Omega}
depends on both the geometry of vertices and edges of

\Omega
, see discussion in
[
10
]
. For
\Omega=(0,1)^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
being a cube, there holds
\gamma_{\Omega}=1
.

The following result provides an interior estimate.

Lemma 13
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
be interior subdomains with
\bar{\Omega}_{0}\subset\Omega_{1}
and
\bar{\Omega}_{1}\subset\Omega
. Let
\upDelta u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\upDelta u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
. Then there holds

\lVert u-R_{h}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert^{2}\left(\lVert\upDelta u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}+\lVert\upDelta u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Proof.

We consider an intermediate subdomain
\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
with
\bar{\Omega}_{0}\subset\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
and
\bar{\Omega}_{0}^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{1}
. By the interior regularity result we obtain
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega_{0}^{\prime})
for every
2\leq p<\infty
with

\lVert u\rVert_{W^{2,p}(\Omega_{0}^{\prime})}\leq cp\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),

where
f=-\upDelta u
and a constant
c
is independent of
u
and
p
. The explicit dependence on
p
in the previous estimate can be traced from the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 9.9]
.

By the interior estimate from
[
18
, Theorem 5.1]
we get for every such
p

\displaystyle\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq c\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u-i_{h}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0}^{\prime})}+\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2-\frac{N}{p}}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{0}^{\prime})}+\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq cph^{2-\frac{N}{p}}\lvert\ln h\rvert\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)+\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where we used standard interpolation estimates. We choose
p=\lvert\ln h\rvert
and obtain using

h^{-\frac{N}{p}}=h^{-\frac{N}{\lvert\ln h\rvert}}=h^{\frac{N}{\ln h}}=e^{N}

the estimate

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\leq ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert^{2}\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)+\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which completes the proof by the estimate for
\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
from
lemma

10
.

We require also error estimates for the Ritz projection of the solution with low regularity, especially if
\upDelta u\in L^{1}(\Omega)

Lemma 14

\lVert u-R_{h}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2-\frac{N}{2}}\lVert\upDelta u\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

The straightforward proof uses a duality argument and the pointwise estimate from
lemma

10
.

Lemma 15
.

Let

11

. Let

4
General error estimates

The discrete approximation to
eq.

1
is defined by

u_{h}\in V_{h}\quad:\quad(\nabla u_{h},\nabla\varphi_{h})+(d(u_{h}),\varphi_{h})=(f,\varphi_{h})\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}.

(7)

Theorem 16
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with some
p>\frac{N}{2}
and let
1
be fulfilled. Then, there exists a
unique solution of
eq.

7
. There is a constant
c
independent of

f
,
d
, and
h
such that

\lVert\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\lVert d(0)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right).

Proof.

The proof is based on Brouwer’s fixed point theorem, see
[
17
, Chapter 1, Satz 2.23]
for the idea of the proof.

For given
M>0
we define an approximation
u_{M,h}\in V_{h}
of the solution to the equation with cut nonlinearity
eq.

3
the same way as
eq.

7
replacing
d
by
d_{M}
. We obtain the following Galerkin orthogonality relations:

(\nabla(u-u_{h}),\nabla\varphi_{h})+(d(u)-d(u_{h}),\varphi_{h})=0\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}

(8)

and

(\nabla(u_{M}-u_{M,h}),\nabla\varphi_{h})+(d_{M}(u_{M})-d_{M}(u_{M,h}),\varphi_{h})=0\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}.

(9)

The next theorem provides a pointwise estimate for
u_{M}-u_{M,h}
.

Theorem 17
.

Let the nonlinearity
d
fulfill
1
. Let

f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Let
u_{M}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C(\bar{\Omega})
be the solution of
eq.

3
and

u_{M,h}\in V_{h}
its discrete approximation. Then there holds

\lVert u_{M}-u_{M,h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{M}h^{2-\frac{N}{2}}\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+1\right),

where the constant
c_{M}
may depend on
M
but is independent of
h
,
f
, and
d
.

Proof.

We denote
e=u_{M}-u_{M,h}
.

Step 1: Construction of an adjoint problem.
For a duality argument, we will consider the adjoint problem

z\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\colon\quad(\nabla z,\nabla v)+(bz,v)=(\psi,v)\quad\text{for all }v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

(10)

for some
\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq 1
. Since the nonlinearity
d
is, in general, not differentiable, we can not use the derivative of
d
for the definition of
b
. Also, a
differential quotient can not be used directly since
d
is not necessarily Lipschitz continuous.
The following definition of the coefficient
b
is inspired by
[
13

\displaystyle=(\nabla u_{M},\nabla(z-R_{h}z))+(d_{M}(u_{M})-d_{M}(u_{M,h}),z-R_{h}z)+(e_{b},z),

Theorem 23

Let the nonlinearity
d
fulfill the
1
.

We denote the error by

is the Ritz projection of

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla e\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

This results in

\displaystyle\lVert\omega\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-(d(u)-d(R_{h}u),\omega^{2}\xi_{h})+(d(u)-d(u_{h}),(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\omega^{2}\xi_{h}))

\displaystyle+(\nabla\xi_{h},\nabla(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\omega^{2}\xi_{h}))-2(\nabla\xi_{h},\omega\xi_{h}\nabla\omega)

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.

We estimate the four terms separately. From
eq.

14
and the fact that

\lVert u-R_{h}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2-\frac{N}{2}}\lVert\upDelta u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

we obtain

\lvert R_{h}u(x)\rvert\leq\rho\quad\text{for all }\;x\in D_{4\delta}

and thus by
4
we get

\lvert d(x,u(x))-d(x,R_{h}u(x))\rvert\leq L\lvert\eta(x)\rvert\quad\text{for almost all }\;x\in D_{4\delta}.

Therefore, we obtain

J_{1}\leq L\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(D_{3\delta})}\leq ch^{4}+\frac{1}{2\delta^{2}}\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

where we used estimate from
lemma

10
for
\eta
. For
J_{2}
we use boundedness of
u
and
u_{h}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\omega^{2}\xi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\left(\lVert\omega\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(D_{3\delta}\setminus D_{\delta})}+\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(D_{3\delta}\setminus D_{\delta})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq ch^{4}+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\omega\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})},

where we used superapproximation estimate from
lemma

25
. To estimate
J_{3}
we observe that
(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\omega^{2}\xi_{h})
vanishes on all cells
K
with
K\cap D_{\delta}\neq\emptyset
, cf. proof of
lemma

25
. Therefore we get by by inverse estimate and
lemma

25

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}\lVert\nabla(\operatorname{id}-i_{h})(\omega^{2}\xi_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{-1}\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}h\left(\lVert\omega\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(D_{3\delta}\setminus D_{\delta})}+\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(D_{3\delta}\setminus D_{\delta})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\omega\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}.

For
J_{4}
we get

J_{4}\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\omega\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}.

Putting terms together and absorbing
\frac{3}{4}\lVert\omega\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
into the left-hand side, we complete the proof.

The following theorem provides a global estimate for
u-u_{h}
with respect to the
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm.

Theorem 27
.

Let the nonlinearity
d
fulfill the
1
and
4
. Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C_{0}(\Omega)
be the solution of

eq.

1
. Let
u_{h}\in V_{h}
be the solution of

eq.

7
. Then, there holds

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert^{2}.

Proof.

We use again the decomposition
eq.

16
for
e=u-u_{h}=\eta+\xi_{h}
. For
\eta
we have by
lemma

10

\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert\upDelta u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

To estimate
\xi_{h}
we use the Poincare inequality on
D_{\delta}

\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(D_{\delta})}\leq\delta\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(D_{\delta})}\quad\text{for every }v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(D_{\delta})}+\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2}\lVert\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(D_{\delta})}+c\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{2}\lVert\omega\nabla\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}.

Using
lemma

26
and
theorem

24
we get

\displaystyle\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle ch^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+c\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus D_{\delta})}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{4}\lvert\ln h\rvert^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\xi_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Absorbing last term into the left-hand side completes the proof.

Remark 28
.

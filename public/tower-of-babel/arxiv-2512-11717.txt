Renormalization of mixing angles and computation of the hadronic W W decay widths
Simonas Draukšas

Abstract
We compute the 1-loop hadronic W W –boson decay widths in the Standard Model with different renormalization schemes of the quark mixing matrix found in the literature. Importantly, we provide the first numerical results for a variant of the On-Shell scheme which does not require mixing matrix counterterms at all, i.e. δ ​ V = 0 \delta V=0 . The scheme is based on the fact that one can always choose a basis in which there are no mixing matrices (angles) and, therefore, the corresponding counterterms are superfluous. For full consistency, the principles of this scheme are employed both for the quark mixing matrix and for the Weinberg angle.

2
Renormalization of the hadronic
W
decay at 1-loop

2.1
Renormalization of mixing angles

Mixing angles are derived parameters coming from the preference to work in a basis in which the mass matrix is diagonal. Mixing angles are then treated as independent parameters which are to be renormalized, despite the fact that they are an artifact of basis change. One can notice this fact by a rather simple procedure: take a multiplet of real massless scalar fields
\hat{\phi}_{i}
, a multiplet of real scalar fields
\hat{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}
with a non-diagonal mass matrix, and introduce a diagonal interaction between these with another real scalar field, say
\hat{\Phi}
. Then the relevant part of the Lagrangian is as follows

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\hat{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}(\hat{M}^{2}_{\chi})_{jj^{\prime}}\hat{\chi}^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}-\lambda\sum_{i}\hat{\Phi}\hat{\phi}_{i}\hat{\chi}^{\prime}_{i}\,.

(2.1)

Here the hats indicate bare quantities. We can change the basis of the fields
\hat{\chi}^{\prime}_{j}=\hat{U}_{jj^{\prime}}\chi_{j^{\prime}}
such that the mass matrix
\hat{U}^{T}\hat{M}^{2}_{\chi}\hat{U}=\hat{m}_{\chi}^{2}
is diagonal after the orthogonal transformation. While the transformation matrix can be easily included in the mass matrix, it remains in the interaction term

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\hat{\chi}_{j}(\hat{m}^{2}_{\chi})_{jj}\hat{\chi}_{j}-\lambda\sum_{i,j}\hat{\Phi}\hat{\phi}_{i}\hat{U}_{ij}\hat{\chi}_{j}\,.

(2.2)

This remaining matrix of basis transformation is then called a mixing matrix and is usually renormalized as
\hat{U}_{ij}=U_{ij}+\delta U_{ij}
(the renormalization of other parameters and fields is irrelevant at the moment, so we keep them bare for simplicity)

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\hat{\chi}_{j}(\hat{m}^{2}_{\chi})_{jj}\hat{\chi}_{j}-\lambda\sum_{i,j}\hat{\Phi}\hat{\phi}_{i}\left(U_{ij}+\delta U_{ij}\right)\hat{\chi}_{j}\,.

(2.3)

It is important to note that basis transformations must remain available in the renormalized version, hence, we are free to transform the field
\chi_{j}
by the inverse of
U
, i.e.
\hat{\chi}_{j}=U^{T}_{jj^{\prime}}\hat{\chi}^{\prime\prime}_{j^{\prime}}
, then we have

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\hat{\chi}^{\prime\prime}_{i^{\prime}}(\hat{M}^{2}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}})_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\hat{\chi}^{\prime\prime}_{j^{\prime}}-\lambda\sum_{i,j^{\prime}}\hat{\Phi}\hat{\phi}_{i}\left(\delta_{ij^{\prime}}+\left(\delta UU^{T}\right)_{ij^{\prime}}\right)\hat{\chi}^{\prime\prime}_{j^{\prime}}\,,

(2.4)

where we have defined
\hat{M}^{2}_{\chi^{\prime\prime}}=U\hat{m}_{\chi}U^{T}
, which is non-diagonal. Importantly, the counterterm
\delta\hat{U}=\delta UU^{T}
remains and is in general non-vanishing even though there is no mixing matrix anywhere else in the Lagrangian so that the counterterm does not have an associated parameter. It follows that for consistency with basis rotations the mixing angle (matrix) counterterms should not be introduced, i.e. the bare mixing matrix should be identified with the renormalized one

U=\hat{U}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\delta U=0\,.

(2.5)

Shortly put, basis rotations should not be renormalized as they are non-physical.

On the other hand, mixing itself is a physical phenomenon and should be renormalized, but renormalization of mixing matrices singles out a basis, which is not consistent. Instead, the proposal is to renormalize mass matrices

\hat{M}^{2}_{ij}=M^{2}_{ij}+\delta M^{2}_{ij}

(2.6)

and diagonalize just the renormalized part

\left(U^{T}\hat{M}^{2}U\right)_{ij}=\left(U^{T}M^{2}U\right)_{ij}+\left(U^{T}\delta M^{2}U\right)_{ij}=m^{2}_{ii}\delta_{ij}+\delta m^{2}_{ij}\,.

(2.7)

Importantly, even with a diagonal renormalized mass the mass counterterm
\delta m^{2}
is in general non-diagonal and removes the need for mixing matrix counterterms. Note that there is and should not be any difference, whether one first does a basis rotation and renormalizes, or renormalizes and then diagonalizes since basis rotations should always be possible without changing the results. This is ensured if there are no counterterms associated with basis rotations.

Having briefly laid out the main idea — introduce off-diagonal mass counterterms to not have mixing angle counterterms — in the following subsections we apply it to the relevant sectors of the Standard Model, namely, the electroweak (EW) sector and the quark sector. We present the application in detail as in some certain steps it differs from the standard approaches.

2.2
Renormalization of the Standard Model electroweak sector and the Weinberg angle

2.2.1
Setup of counterterms in the electroweak sector

We begin with a rather simple case of the Standard Model electroweak (EW) sector. The covariant derivative is

D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-i\hat{g}T^{a}\hat{W}^{a}_{\mu}-i\hat{g}^{\prime}Y\hat{B}_{\mu}\,,

(2.8)

where
\hat{g}
and
\hat{g}^{\prime}
are the
SU(2)_{L}
and
U(1)_{Y}
gauge couplings, respectively,
T^{a}=\tau^{a}/2
are the

U(1)_{Y}

\hat{W}^{a}_{\mu}
and
\hat{B}_{\mu}
are the respective gauge fields, and the hats continue to indicate bare quantities. Mass terms of the gauge bosons are generated via the Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking (SSB) mechanism by the vacuum expectation value
\hat{v}
of the Higgs doublet
\hat{H}
(
SU(2)_{L}
doublet with hypercharge

\hat{H}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{G}^{+}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{v}+\hat{h}+i\hat{G}^{0})\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.9)

where
\hat{h}
is the physical Higgs boson and
\hat{G}^{+}
and
\hat{G}^{0}
are the would-be Goldstone bosons. The kinetic term of the Higgs doublet at the minimum produces the well known mass matrix for the gauge bosons

\hat{M}^{2}_{VV^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{M}^{2}_{WW}&0_{2\times 2}\\
0_{2\times 2}&\hat{M}^{2}_{WB}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.10)

with the
2\times 2
diagonal block for the
\hat{W}^{1,2}

\hat{M}^{2}_{WW}=\frac{\hat{g}^{2}\hat{v}^{2}}{4}I=\hat{m}^{2}_{W}I\,,

(2.11)

with

2\times 2

\hat{W}^{3}
and
\hat{B}

\hat{M}^{2}_{WB}=\frac{\hat{v}^{2}}{4}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{g}^{2}&-\hat{g}\hat{g}^{\prime}\\
-\hat{g}\hat{g}^{\prime}&\hat{g}^{\prime 2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{m}^{2}_{W}&-\hat{m}_{B}\hat{m}_{W}\\
-\hat{m}_{B}\hat{m}_{W}&\hat{m}^{2}_{B}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.12)

The standard step would be to diagonalize
\hat{M}^{2}_{WB}
by an orthogonal transformation, however, we first renormalize the mass matrices

\hat{M}^{2}_{WW}=M^{2}_{WW}+\delta M^{2}_{WW}\,,\quad\hat{M}^{2}_{WB}=M^{2}_{WB}+\delta M^{2}_{WB}

(2.13)

or, equivalently,

\hat{m}^{2}_{W}=m^{2}_{W}+\delta m^{2}_{W}\,,\quad\hat{m}^{2}_{B}=m^{2}_{B}+\delta m^{2}_{B}\,.

(2.14)

To first order the counterterm matrix
\delta M^{2}_{WB}
is

\delta M^{2}_{WB}=\begin{pmatrix}\delta m^{2}_{W}&-\frac{m_{W}}{2m_{B}}\delta m^{2}_{B}-\frac{m_{B}}{2m_{W}}\delta m^{2}_{W}\\
-\frac{m_{W}}{2m_{B}}\delta m^{2}_{B}-\frac{m_{B}}{2m_{W}}\delta m^{2}_{W}&\delta m^{2}_{B}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.15)

Diagonalization of the now renormalized mass matrix
M^{2}_{WB}
by
R
in analogy with Eq. (
2.7
) relates the
W^{3}
and
B
fields to the mass eigenstates, which are the photon
A
and the
Z

\begin{pmatrix}W^{3}_{\mu}\\
B_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}=R\begin{pmatrix}Z_{\mu}\\
A_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}c&s\\
-s&c\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Z_{\mu}\\
A_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.16)

where the Weinberg angle
\theta
is defined as usual

c\equiv\cos\theta=\frac{m_{W}}{m_{Z}}\,,\qquad s\equiv\sin\theta=\sqrt{1-c^{2}}

(2.17)

and the
Z

m_{Z}^{2}=m^{2}_{B}+m^{2}_{W}\,.

(2.18)

The same transformation by
R

R^{T}\delta M^{2}_{WB}R=\begin{pmatrix}\delta m^{2}_{Z}&\delta m^{2}_{ZA}\\
\delta m^{2}_{AZ}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.19)

with

\delta m^{2}_{Z}=\delta m^{2}_{B}+\delta m^{2}_{W}

(2.20)

and

\delta m^{2}_{AZ}=\delta m^{2}_{ZA}=\frac{1}{2sc}\left(\delta m_{W}^{2}-c^{2}\delta m_{Z}^{2}\right)\,.

(2.21)

It is worth noting that the photon is massless and the corresponding mass counterterm is zero, as expected due to gauge invariance. The Weinberg angle is defined entirely in terms of renormalized masses and does not require a counterterm. It is rather simple to see that the counterterm
\delta m^{2}_{AZ}
replaces the usual counterterm for the Weinberg angle as the two are related by

\frac{\delta m^{2}_{AZ}}{m^{2}_{Z}}=-\frac{\delta\sin\theta_{W}}{\sin\theta_{W}}\,,

as can be seen by comparing with the definitions found, for example, in
[
11

. Having discussed the inconsistency of mixing angle counterterms, we do not use
\delta\sin\theta_{W}
and work only with
\delta m^{2}_{AZ}
.

Returning to Eq. (
2.12
) and renormalizing the gauge couplings as

\hat{g}=g+\delta g\,,\quad\hat{g}^{\prime}=g^{\prime}+\delta g^{\prime}\,,

(2.22)

we may note that the same rotation by
R
also diagonalizes the matrix of renormalized gauge couplings, so that

c=\frac{g}{\sqrt{g^{2}+g^{\prime 2}}}

(2.23)

and one may find the relation between the gauge coupling and
\delta m^{2}_{AZ}
counterterms

\frac{\delta g}{g}-\frac{\delta g^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{cs}\frac{\delta m^{2}_{AZ}}{m^{2}_{Z}}

(2.24)

by identifying the off-diagonal elements of the counterterm matrices resulting from the rotated version of Eq. (
2.12
) and using
v=2cm_{Z}/g
. Another relation comes from the diagonal element where we have the bare
Z

\hat{m}_{Z}^{2}=m_{Z}^{2}+\delta m_{Z}^{2}=\frac{\hat{v}^{2}}{4}\left(c\hat{g}+s\hat{g}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\,.

(2.25)

Upon using the conventional definitions of the (renormalized) electric charge

e=gs=g^{\prime}c\,,

(2.26)

the combination of bare couplings in the parentheses can be rewritten as

\begin{split}c\hat{g}+s\hat{g}^{\prime}&=\frac{e}{sc}+c\delta g+s\delta g^{\prime}\,.\end{split}

(2.27)

In other words, we may define the bare electric charge as

\hat{e}=sc\left(c\hat{g}+s\hat{g}^{\prime}\right)

(2.28)

with the corresponding counterterm

\frac{\delta e}{e}=s^{2}\frac{\delta g}{g}+c^{2}\frac{\delta g^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}}\,.

(2.29)

Using both relations in Eqs. (
2.24
) and (

\delta g/g
and
\delta g^{\prime}/g^{\prime}

\begin{split}\frac{\delta g}{g}&=\frac{\delta e}{e}+\frac{c}{s}\frac{\delta m^{2}_{AZ}}{m^{2}_{Z}}\,,\\
\frac{\delta g^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}}&=\frac{\delta e}{e}-\frac{s}{c}\frac{\delta m^{2}_{AZ}}{m^{2}_{Z}}\,.\end{split}

(2.30)

The counterterms
\delta g/g

D^{Z-A}_{\mu}=-i\begin{pmatrix}(g+\delta g)T^{3}&(g^{\prime}+\delta g^{\prime})Y\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}c&s\\
-s&c\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1+\delta Z_{ZZ}&\delta Z_{ZA}\\
\delta Z_{AZ}&1+\delta Z_{AA}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}Z_{\mu}\\
A_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2.31)

where we have put the gauge couplings together with the corresponding generators in a row vector, used Eq. (

\delta Z_{VV^{\prime}}

\begin{split}Z_{\mu}&:-i\frac{g}{c}\left(T^{3}-Qs^{2}+Qs^{2}\left(\frac{c}{s}\delta Z_{AZ}-\frac{\delta g^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}}-\delta Z_{ZZ}\right)+T^{3}\left(\delta Z_{ZZ}+s^{2}\frac{\delta g^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}}+c^{2}\frac{\delta g}{g}\right)\right)\,,\\

(2.41)

where we assume that the loop functions are real (we implicitly use the
\widetilde{Re}
operator
[
9
]
, which drops the so-called absorptive parts of loop functions.).

The renormalized transverse parts of the two-point functions
\Pi^{R}_{V^{\prime}V}
for the SM gauge bosons are

\displaystyle\Pi^{R}_{WW}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{WW}(p^{2})-2\left(p^{2}-m_{W}^{2}\right)\delta Z_{W}+\delta m_{W}^{2}\,,

(2.42)

\displaystyle\Pi^{R}_{ZZ}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{ZZ}(p^{2})-2\left(p^{2}-m_{Z}^{2}\right)\delta Z_{ZZ}+\delta m_{Z}^{2}\,,

(2.43)

\displaystyle\Pi^{R}_{AA}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{AA}(p^{2})-2p^{2}\delta Z_{AA}\,,

(2.44)

\displaystyle\Pi^{R}_{AZ}(p^{2})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{AZ}(p^{2})-\delta Z^{T}_{AZ}\left(p^{2}-m^{2}_{Z}\right)-p^{2}\delta Z_{AZ}+\delta m_{AZ}^{2}\,.

(2.45)

Proceeding with the traditional On-Shell renormalization conditions
[
3
]
, we require that the renormalized masses are the pole masses, i.e.
\Pi^{R}_{VV}(m_{V}^{2})=0
, which fixes the mass counterterms of the
Z
and
W
bosons. This leads to the following counterterms

\delta m_{W}^{2}=-\Pi_{WW}(m_{W}^{2})\,,\quad\delta m_{Z}^{2}=-\Pi_{ZZ}(m_{Z}^{2})\,.

(2.46)

As we have included tadpole diagrams in the self-energies, these mass counterterms are gauge-independent
[
22
]
. Consequently, via Eq. (
2.21
), these mass counterterms also produce the off-diagonal mass counterterm

\delta m_{AZ}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2sc}\left(\Pi_{WW}(m_{W}^{2})-c^{2}\Pi_{ZZ}(m_{Z}^{2})\right)\,.

(2.47)

The tadpoles cancel in this counterterm, hence, it is gauge-independent irrespective of the tadpole treatment.

The unit residue requirement leads to
\partial_{p^{2}}\Pi^{R}_{VV}|_{p^{2}=m_{V}^{2}}=0
and results in the following field renormalization counterterms

\delta Z_{W}=\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\Pi_{WW}(p^{2})}{\partial p^{2}}\right|_{p^{2}=m_{W}^{2}}\,,\quad\delta Z_{ZZ}=\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\Pi_{ZZ}(p^{2})}{\partial p^{2}}\right|_{p^{2}=m_{Z}^{2}}\,,\qquad\delta Z_{AA}=\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\Pi_{AA}(p^{2})}{\partial p^{2}}\right|_{p^{2}=0}\,.

(2.48)

Lastly, the propagators should be diagonal on-shell, hence, the
Z-A
self-energy at
p^{2}=\{m_{Z}^{2},0\}
should vanish, which leads to

\delta Z_{AZ}=\frac{1}{m_{Z}^{2}}\left(\Pi_{AZ}(m_{Z}^{2})+\delta m^{2}_{AZ}\right)\,,\quad\delta Z^{T}_{AZ}=\delta Z_{ZA}=-\frac{1}{m_{Z}^{2}}\left(\Pi_{AZ}(0)+\delta m^{2}_{AZ}\right)\,.

(2.49)

It is also worth noting the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of the
Z-A
field renormalization constants

\begin{split}\delta Z^{S}_{AZ}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta Z_{AZ}+\delta Z^{T}_{AZ}\right)=\frac{1}{2m_{Z}^{2}}\left(\Pi_{AZ}(m_{Z}^{2})-\Pi_{AZ}(0)\right)\,,\\
\delta Z^{A}_{AZ}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta Z_{AZ}-\delta Z^{T}_{AZ}\right)=\frac{1}{2m_{Z}^{2}}\left(\Pi_{AZ}(m_{Z}^{2})+\Pi_{AZ}(0)+2\delta m^{2}_{AZ}\right)\,.\end{split}

(2.50)

Here the sign change under transposition is ensured by noting that the denominator
1/m_{Z}^{2}
is actually a difference of squared masses
1/(m_{Z}^{2}-m_{A}^{2})
with
m_{A}=0
, which changes sign under transposition. There is a general feature: the off-diagonal mass counterterms are related with the anti-symmetric (anti-hermitian) part of the field renormalization constants, while the symmetric (hermitian) part is independent of mass counterterms
[
2
,
15
]
. It is also interesting to note that in our approach the anti-symmetric part of the field renormalization is UV finite as can be checked explicitly. One can find that the UV divergences contained in the bare self-energies cancel against the ones of
\delta m^{2}_{AZ}
in the expression for
\delta Z^{A}_{AZ}
, which can be employed in the renormalization of the effective leptonic Weinberg angle in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme
[
23
]
due to the correspondence between
\delta m^{2}_{AZ}
and
\delta\sin\theta_{W}
.

Having all the field and mass counterterms defined in terms of the self-energies we may employ Eqs. (
2.36
), (
2.37
), (
2.47
), (
2.48
), and (
2.49
) to express the gauge coupling counterterms in terms of self-energies

\displaystyle\frac{\delta g^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\Pi_{AA}(p^{2})}{\partial p^{2}}\right|_{p^{2}=0}-\frac{s}{c}\frac{\Pi_{AZ}(0)}{m^{2}_{Z}}+\frac{1}{2c^{2}m^{2}_{Z}}\left(\Pi_{WW}(m_{W}^{2})-c^{2}\Pi_{ZZ}(m_{Z}^{2})\right)\,,

(2.51)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta g}{g}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\Pi_{AA}(p^{2})}{\partial p^{2}}\right|_{p^{2}=0}-\frac{s}{c}\frac{\Pi_{AZ}(0)}{m^{2}_{Z}}-\frac{1}{2s^{2}m_{Z}^{2}}\left(\Pi_{WW}(m_{W}^{2})-c^{2}\Pi_{ZZ}(m_{Z}^{2})\right)\,.

(2.52)

Having these gauge coupling counterterms it is not hard to find the electric charge counterterm from Eq. (
2.29
)

\frac{\delta e}{e}=-\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\partial\Pi_{AA}(p^{2})}{\partial p^{2}}\right|_{p^{2}=0}-\frac{s}{c}\frac{\Pi_{AZ}(0)}{m^{2}_{Z}}\,,

(2.53)

which corresponds to the usual On-Shell counterterm
[
9
,
39
,
4
,
11
]
up to minus signs and factors of 2 due to our conventions of self-energies, choice of the rotation
R
and field renormalization convention.

One can see that the presence of the counterterm
\delta m^{2}_{AZ}
, i.e. the triviality of the Weinberg angle counterterm, amounts to a relabeling of counterterms in the gauge-sector. This is because the gauge-sector is quite restricted by gauge-invariance and two-point functions are fully sufficient to renormalize the sector. As we see in the sections below, this not the case in the fermion sector, where the Yukawa couplings are not required by gauge-invariance and there is more freedom.

2.3
Renormalization of the Standard model quark sector and the CKM matrix

2.3.1
Setup of counterterms in the quark sector

To renormalize the quarks we only need the kinetic and mass terms of the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\text{kin.+mass}}^{\text{quark}}=-i\sum_{u}\overline{\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{u}}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{u}-\sum_{u,u^{\prime}}\overline{\hat{\psi}_{u}^{\prime}}\hat{M}_{uu^{\prime}}\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{u^{\prime}}-i\sum_{d}\overline{\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{d}}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{d}-\sum_{d,d^{\prime}}\overline{\hat{\psi}_{d}^{\prime}}\hat{M}_{dd^{\prime}}\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}}\,,

(2.54)

where
\hat{\psi}_{u,d}
are the up- and down-type quark fields in flavor basis such that the indices
u
and
d
can correspond to any of the flavors,
\hat{M}_{uu\prime}
and
\hat{M}_{dd\prime}
are the respective quark mass matrices, and the covariant derivative is understood to be written after SSB, so that it includes only photon and gluon terms. We may rotate the quark fields into a different basis via

\begin{split}\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{u}&=\sum_{uu^{\prime}}\mathcal{U}_{uu\prime}\hat{\psi}_{u^{\prime}}=\left((U_{L})_{uu^{\prime}}P_{L}+(U_{R})_{uu^{\prime}}P_{R}\right)\hat{\psi}_{u^{\prime}}\,,\\
\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{d}&=\sum_{dd^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{dd\prime}\hat{\psi}_{d^{\prime}}=\left((D_{L})_{dd^{\prime}}P_{L}+(D_{R})_{dd^{\prime}}P_{R}\right)\hat{\psi}_{d^{\prime}}\,.\end{split}

(2.55)

Here
P_{L,R}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\mp\gamma^{5}\right)
and the rotation matrices
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{D}
are identified with the renormalized ones, i.e. their counterterms are trivial. Then Eq. (
2.54
) becomes

\mathcal{L}_{\text{kin.+mass}}^{\text{quark}}=-i\sum_{u}\overline{\hat{\psi}_{u}}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\hat{\psi}_{u}-\sum_{u,u^{\prime}}\overline{\hat{\psi}_{u}}\hat{m}_{uu^{\prime}}\hat{\psi}_{u^{\prime}}-i\sum_{d}\overline{\hat{\psi}_{d}}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\hat{\psi}_{d}-\sum_{d,d^{\prime}}\overline{\hat{\psi}_{d}}\hat{m}_{dd^{\prime}}\hat{\psi}_{d^{\prime}}\,,

(2.56)

where we have defined

\begin{split}\sum_{u^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime\prime\prime}}\gamma^{0}(\mathcal{U}^{\dagger})_{uu^{\prime\prime}}\gamma^{0}\hat{M}_{u^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime\prime\prime}}\mathcal{U}_{u^{\prime\prime\prime}u^{\prime}}&=\hat{m}_{uu^{\prime}}\,,\\
\sum_{d^{\prime\prime},d^{\prime\prime\prime}}\gamma^{0}(\mathcal{D}^{\dagger})_{d^{\prime\prime}}\gamma^{0}\hat{M}_{d^{\prime\prime}d^{\prime\prime\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{d^{\prime\prime\prime}d^{\prime}}&=\hat{m}_{dd^{\prime}}\,.\end{split}

(2.57)

The bare mass matrices
\hat{m}
are renormalized in analogy with Eq. (
2.7
)

\begin{split}\hat{m}_{uu^{\prime}}&=m_{u}\delta_{uu^{\prime}}+\delta m_{uu^{\prime}}\,,\\
\hat{m}_{dd^{\prime}}&=m_{d}\delta_{dd^{\prime}}+\delta m_{dd^{\prime}}\,.\end{split}

(2.58)

Since the renormalized masses
m
are diagonal, we call this the mass eigenstate basis and the indices
u
and
d
are to be understood as any of the following sets
\{u\,,c\,,t\}
and
\{d\,,s\,,b\}
, respectively. Hermiticity of the Lagrangian also imposes a relation for the mass counterterms

\gamma^{0}\delta m\gamma^{0}=\delta m^{\dagger}\,.

(2.59)

The fields are renormalized as usual (although without square roots)

\hat{\psi}_{u}=Z_{uu^{\prime}}\psi_{u^{\prime}}\,,\qquad\hat{\psi}_{d}=Z_{dd^{\prime}}\psi_{d^{\prime}}\,,

(2.60)

with

Z=I+\delta Z_{L}P_{L}+\delta Z_{R}P_{R}\,.

(2.61)

Finally, upon going to the mass eigenstate basis, the
W
vertex

\mathcal{L}_{Wud}=-\frac{\hat{g}}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{u,d}\overline{\hat{\psi}^{\prime}}_{u}\gamma^{\mu}\hat{W}^{+}_{\mu}\delta_{ud}P_{L}\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{d}=-\frac{\hat{g}}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{u,d}\overline{\hat{\psi}}_{u}\gamma^{\mu}\hat{W}^{+}_{\mu}(U^{\dagger}_{L}D_{L})_{ud}P_{L}\hat{\psi}_{d}

(2.62)

leads to the CKM matrix

V_{ud}=(U^{\dagger}_{L}D_{L})_{ud}\,,

(2.63)

which is derived from renormalized parameters so that the counterterm is trivial by definition

\delta V_{ud}=0\,.

(2.64)

(2.65)

with the usual decomposition

\Sigma_{ji}(\cancel{p})=\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(p^{2})\cancel{p}P_{L}+\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(p^{2})\cancel{p}P_{R}+\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(p^{2})P_{L}+\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(p^{2})P_{R}\,.

(2.66)

Here it is again assumed that the loop-functions are real and the self-energies obey the pseudo-hermiticity constraint

\left[\Sigma^{\gamma L,\ \gamma R}_{ji}(p^{2})\right]^{\dagger}=\Sigma^{\gamma L,\ \gamma R}_{ji}(p^{2})\,,\qquad\left[\Sigma^{sL,\ sR}_{ji}(p^{2})\right]^{\dagger}=\Sigma^{sR,\ sL}_{ji}(p^{2})\,.

(2.67)

By employing the definitions of the previous section, we have the following renormalized self-energy to first order

\Sigma_{ji}^{R}(\cancel{p})=\Sigma_{ji}(\cancel{p})+\gamma^{0}\delta Z_{ji}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\left(\cancel{p}-m_{i}\right)+\left(\cancel{p}-m_{j}\right)\delta Z_{ji}-\delta m_{ji}\,,

(2.68)

where the indices
i,\ j
are either
u,\ u^{\prime}
or
d,\ d^{\prime}
.

The diagonal counterterms are fixed in the usual way. The requirement that the corrections vanish at the pole,
\Sigma^{R}_{ii}(\cancel{p})u_{i}=0
, produces the diagonal mass counterterm

\delta m_{ii}=\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ii}(m_{i}^{2})m_{i}P_{R}+\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ii}(m_{i}^{2})m_{i}P_{L}+\Sigma^{sL}_{ii}(m_{i}^{2})P_{L}+\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})P_{R}\,.

(2.69)

The diagonal field counterterms come from the unit residue condition,
\lim_{\cancel{p}\rightarrow m_{i}}\frac{1}{\cancel{p}-m_{i}}\Sigma^{R}_{ii}(\cancel{p})u_{i}=u_{i}
, which results in

\begin{split}\delta Z_{ii}=&\left(-\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})-\left.\partial_{p^{2}}\left(m^{2}_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(p^{2})+m^{2}_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(p^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(p^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(p^{2})\right)\right|_{p^{2}=m_{i}^{2}}\right)P_{L}\\
&+\left(-\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})-\left.\partial_{p^{2}}\left(m^{2}_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(p^{2})+m^{2}_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(p^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(p^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(p^{2})\right)\right|_{p^{2}=m_{i}^{2}}\right)P_{R}\,.\end{split}

(2.70)

For the off-diagonal elements we require to have a diagonal propagator,
\Sigma^{R}_{ji}(\cancel{p})u_{i}=0
, which leads to the following condition

\left[(m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2})\delta Z_{ji}-m_{j}\delta m_{ji}-\delta m^{\dagger}_{ji}m_{i}\right]u_{i}=-(\cancel{p}+m_{j})\Sigma_{ji}(\cancel{p})u_{i}\,.

(2.71)

Here we have two unknowns: the field and mass counterterms, but only one constraint. The anti-hermitian part of the above relation can be used to define the hermitian part of the field renormalization

\begin{split}\delta Z_{ji}^{H}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}}&\left(m^{2}_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}m_{j}\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{j}\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})-H.C.\right)P_{L}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}}&\left(m^{2}_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}m_{j}\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{j}\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})-H.C.\right)P_{R}\,,\\
\end{split}

(2.72)

where
H.C.
stands for Hermitian conjugation, which also acts on the arguments of the self-energy scalar functions, i.e.
[\Sigma(m_{i}^{2})]^{\dagger}=\Sigma^{\dagger}(m_{j}^{2})
. The hermitian part of Eq. (
2.71
) gives a relation between the anti-hermitian part of the field renormalization and the mass counterterms, however, unlike in the gauge sector, neither are fixed and one cannot solve for both the counterterms simultaneously. We go around the problem as in
[
14
]
and define the anti-hermitian part of the field renormalization as the coefficient of the
(m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2})
mass structure

\begin{split}\delta Z^{A}_{ji}\equiv&-\frac{1}{2}\left.\left(m^{2}_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}m_{j}\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{j}\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+H.C.\right)P_{L}\right|_{m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}}\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\left.\left(m^{2}_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}m_{j}\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{j}\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+H.C.\right)P_{R}\right|_{m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(2.73)

This definition is motivated by the Nielsen Identities
[
36
,
22
]
and the structure of UV divergences, see
[
14
]
and references therein. The practical application is discussed in Appendix
A
, while here we give the simple result for the Standard Model quarks that we get from Eq. (
A.5
) in the appendix

\begin{split}\delta Z^{A}_{uu^{\prime}}&=-\frac{\alpha}{32\pi s^{2}m_{W}^{2}}\sum_{d}V_{ud}V^{\star}_{u^{\prime}d}\left(m_{d}^{2}-m_{u^{\prime}}^{2}+\xi_{W}m_{W}^{2}\right)B_{0}\left[m_{u^{\prime}}^{2},m_{d}^{2},\xi_{W}m_{W}^{2}\right]P_{L}-H.C.\,,\\
\delta Z^{A}_{dd^{\prime}}&=-\frac{\alpha}{32\pi s^{2}m_{W}^{2}}\sum_{u}V_{ud^{\prime}}V^{\star}_{ud}\left(m_{u}^{2}-m_{d^{\prime}}^{2}+\xi_{W}m_{W}^{2}\right)B_{0}\left[m_{d^{\prime}}^{2},m_{u}^{2},\xi_{W}m_{W}^{2}\right]P_{L}-H.C.\,,\end{split}

(2.74)

where
\alpha=e^{2}/(4\pi)
is the fine structure constant and
B_{0}\left[\dots\right]
is the Passarino–Veltman function
[
18
,
37
]
with the conventions of
[
7
]
. It is worth noting that the resulting anti-hermitian parts of the field renormalization are purely left-handed, reflecting the chirality of the weak interaction, and minimal in the sense that they consist only of gauge-dependent terms needed to cancel the gauge-dependence in the
W
amplitude. In addition, one may explicitly check that the anti-hermitian parts are UV finite, which means that these counterterms are trivial in
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
schemes.

Having fully defined the field renormalization we may solve for the mass counterterms, which are

\begin{split}\delta m_{ji}=&\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{j}\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(m_{j}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{sL}_{ji}(m_{j}^{2})\right)P_{L}\\
&\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{j}\Sigma^{\gamma R}_{ji}(m_{j}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma L}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{s}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{sR}_{ji}(m_{j}^{2})\right)P_{R}\\
&-m_{j}\delta Z^{A}_{ji}+m_{i}\gamma^{0}\delta Z^{A}_{ji}\gamma^{0}\,.\end{split}

(2.75)

With these mass and field counterterm definitions the CKM counterterm is trivial

\displaystyle\delta V_{ud}=0\,.

(2.76)

Note that the counterterms defined in our scheme
[
14
]
presented above comprise a fully consistent On-Shell scheme without the need of any additional counterterms, as is not usually the case
[
21
,
42
,
dienerkniehl
,
32
,
29
,

]
.

4
Discussion & Conclusions

In this work we have provided a table with partial and total hadronic
W
decay widths in the spirit of
[
almasy2009err
]
with the extension by the very first numerical results for the quark mixing matrix renormalization scheme found in
[
14
]
and re-introduced in this work. We have found, that numerically the scheme produces very similar results as compared to other schemes found in the literature. Notably, the scheme of this work seems to give the lowest corrections to decay channels with the largest mass splittings of the outgoing quarks.

Perhaps more important is the construction of the scheme which we presented in Section
2.3
and its features. Most notably, the scheme uses the traditional On-Shell renormalization conditions, but on a different set of counterterms, where the mass counterterms are non-diagonal and the quark mixing matrix counterterm is trivial. In this approach the CKM matrix is derived from the renormalized masses and, therefore, is itself always renormalized.
We believe that the construction of the scheme is perhaps the cleanest there is since all the counterterms are derived from the traditional On-Shell conditions, which is not always the case. The scheme of
[
8
]
is indeed a traditional On-Shell scheme, but it leads to a gauge-dependent
W
decay amplitude, whereas our scheme does not have difficulties with gauge-dependence.

The only real difficulty with the definitions of our scheme is that it is rather difficult to uniquely use the mass structures and practically retrieve the anti-hermitian part of the field renormalization counterterms. In Appendix
A
we provide a method that easily works for the Standard Model quarks, but seems to overshoot in models with more complex mixing angle implementations
4
4

4

We have used the so called Grimus–Neufeld model
[
GrimusNeufeld
]
familiar to the author as an example.
and is therefore not yet fully universal. In contrast, such difficulties do not arise in the gauge sector and the renormalization of the Weinberg angle, since the masses and couplings are more constrained in the gauge sector.

Appendix A
Fermion field renormalization

In this appendix we provide a method of using the definition of the anti-hermitian part of the field renormalization in Eq. (
2.73
) and getting the results of Eq. (
2.74
). The definition and the idea rely on the presence of the distinct mass structure of
{m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}}
, which is not trivially seen on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
2.71
). It is also notable, that the situations for the hermitian and anti-hermitian parts of the field renormalization are different.

In the hermitian part of the field renormalization defined in Eq. (
2.72
) the factor of
1/(m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2})
cancels once the self-energies are Taylor expanded around one of the masses. In contrast, such an expansion for the anti-hermitian part does not produce the needed mass structure, therefore it must appear as some intrinsic combination of the self-energies, in other words the self-energies are likely not fine-grained enough to be sensitive to this structure and a different decomposition than in Eq. (
2.66
) is needed.

As we are after the masses it is worth noting that in models with spontaneous symmetry breaking the fermion masses are related to the Yukawa couplings, which become the couplings to the Goldstone bosons. Importantly, in 2-point functions, at most two (Yukawa) couplings can be coupled to the external legs, and in turn, the self-energies are at most bilinear in the external fermion masses
5
5

5

One needs to be careful if the Yukawa couplings are diagonal and the particles in the loop are of the same type as the external ones. Then the Yukawa coupling would make the external index appear in the loop functions. This is not the case for quarks and
G^{\pm}
bosons, since for external up-type quarks the down-type quarks are in the loop.
. All the other dependence is on the couplings, momentum, and loop masses. In turn, each scalar function in the usual self-energy decomposition in Eq. (
2.66
) may be further decomposed into four scalar functions

\Sigma^{X}_{ji}(p^{2})=\Sigma^{X,(0)}_{ji}(p^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{X,(i)}_{ji}(p^{2})+m_{j}\Sigma^{X,(j)}_{ji}(p^{2})+m_{i}m_{j}\Sigma^{X,(ij)}_{ji}(p^{2})\,,

(A.1)

where
X=\{\gamma L,\ \gamma R,\ sL,\ sR\}
and all four functions are completely free of the external leg masses. Since this holds for arbitrary momenta, one may use pseudo-hermiticity in Eq. (
2.67
) to further constrain the scalar-functions

\begin{split}\Sigma^{\gamma L,\gamma R,(j)}_{ji}(p^{2})&=\left[\Sigma^{\gamma L,\gamma R,(i)}_{ji}(p^{2})\right]^{\dagger}\,,\\
\Sigma^{sR,(0)}_{ji}(p^{2})&=\left[\Sigma^{sL,(0)}_{ji}(p^{2})\right]^{\dagger}\,,\\
\Sigma^{sR,(i)}_{ji}(p^{2})&=\left[\Sigma^{sL,(j)}_{ji}(p^{2})\right]^{\dagger}\,,\\
\Sigma^{sR,(j)}_{ji}(p^{2})&=\left[\Sigma^{sL,(i)}_{ji}(p^{2})\right]^{\dagger}\,,\\
\Sigma^{sR,(ij)}_{ji}(p^{2})&=\left[\Sigma^{sL,(ij)}_{ji}(p^{2})\right]^{\dagger}\,.\end{split}

(A.2)

Note that just like pseudo-hermiticity, these restrictions hold below particle production thresholds or if the absorptive parts are dropped.

Returning to Eq. (
2.71
) and having this new decomposition in mind, we may note that acting with the spinor
u_{i}
can only produce the mass
m_{i}
, but not
m_{j}
. Therefore, the appearance of the mass structure
m_{i}^{2}-m_{j}^{2}
on the l.h.s. is rather limited on the r.h.s. In turn, we may select all the terms that have
m_{j}^{2}
on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
2.71
), which leads to

\begin{split}\text{r.h.s. Eq.\penalty\ \eqref{eq:fermionfieldmassrelation}}\xrightarrow{\sim m_{j}^{2}}&-m_{j}^{2}\left(m_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma R,(i)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sL,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}^{2}\Sigma^{\gamma R,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{sL,(j)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})\right)P_{L}\\
&-m_{j}^{2}\left(m_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma L,(j)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sR,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}^{2}\Sigma^{\gamma L,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{sR,(j)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})\right)P_{R}\,,\end{split}

(A.3)

where we have used the pseudo-hermiticity constraints. Then, we may find the anti-hermitian part of the field renormalization by taking the anti-hermitian part of the
-m_{j}^{2}
coefficient

\begin{split}\delta Z^{A}_{ji}=&\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma R,(i)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sL,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}^{2}\Sigma^{\gamma R,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{sL,(j)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})-H.C.\right)P_{L}\\
&\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}\Sigma^{\gamma L,(j)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}\Sigma^{sR,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+m_{i}^{2}\Sigma^{\gamma L,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{sR,(j)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})-H.C.\right)P_{R}\,.\end{split}

(A.4)

This method of finding the anti-hermitian part of the field renormalization works flawlessly for the Standard Model and produces the results of Eq. (
2.74
), which also match our results in
[
14
,
16
]
, where we used a more naive approach. Even more so, the Standard Model is simple enough so that most of the terms are 0 and the above equation reduces to

\mathrm{SM:}\quad\delta Z^{A}_{ji}=\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{i}^{2}\Sigma^{\gamma R,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})+\Sigma^{sL,(j)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})-H.C.\right)P_{L}\,.

(A.5)

Notably, this method works in any gauge, so that, for example, one may work in the Feynman gauge from the beginning and still find the same field renormalization.

Going beyond the SM we have found that this method rather overshoots and produces gauge-independent and UV divergent terms in the field renormalization. The presence of UV divergences in the anti-hermitian part of the field renormalization is a problem, since that automatically forces a mixing angle counterterm, which is inconsistent with basis rotations. As an example, we took the Grimus–Neufeld (GN) model
[
GrimusNeufeld
]
, that has a Majorana singlet in addition to the 3 SM neutrinos and the mixing matrix is split into 2 parts

U=\begin{pmatrix}U_{L}^{\dagger}&U_{R}^{T}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(A.6)

where
U_{L}
is
3\times 4
and
U_{R}
is
1\times 4
. Due to the construction of the model, instead of the full mixing matrix
U
, either
U_{L}
or
U_{R}
, which are not unitary by themselves, appear in the Lagrangian separately. Then taking the anti-hermitian part of
m_{i}^{2}\Sigma^{\gamma R,(ij)}_{ji}(m_{i}^{2})
and taking just the UV part produces

\mathrm{GN:}\qquad\left.\left(m_{\nu}^{2}\Sigma^{\gamma R,(\nu\nu^{\prime})}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(m_{\nu}^{2})-H.C.\right)\right|_{\mathrm{UV}}\sim(m_{\nu}^{2}-m^{2}_{\nu^{\prime}})\left(U^{\dagger}_{L}U_{L}\right)_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}\neq 0\,,

(A.7)

where
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
go over all four neutrinos of the GN and there is an analogous result for
\Sigma^{\gamma L,(\nu\nu^{\prime})}
terms for the right-handed part. On the other hand, similar terms are produced in the Standard Model too. For up-type quarks we get

\mathrm{SM:}\qquad\left.\left(m_{u}^{2}\Sigma^{\gamma R,(uu^{\prime})}_{u^{\prime}u}(m_{u}^{2})-H.C.\right)\right|_{\mathrm{UV}}\sim(m_{u}^{2}-m^{2}_{u^{\prime}})\left(VV^{\dagger}\right)_{u^{\prime}u}=(m_{u}^{2}-m^{2}_{u^{\prime}})\delta_{u^{\prime}u}=0\,,

(A.8)

where we have used the unitarity of the CKM matrix, such that the final result is UV finite.

It seems, that the formula for the anti-hermitian part of the field renormalization in Eq. (
A.4
) will work for models with non-split mixing matrices, but will produce unwanted UV divergences if the mixing is implemented in a more contrived way. However, note that this is not a problem with the definition of the anti-hermitian part in Eq. (
2.73
), but with its implementation outlined here. At the time of writing, we are unaware of a remedy that would make the proposed method truly universal, but at the very least it works very well for the Standard Model.

It is worth noting, that the scheme in
[
29
]
also relies on the structure of the self-energy to define the self-mass and wave function renormalization contributions. There the authors are faced with an analogous problem of finding
\cancel{p}-m_{i}
and
\cancel{p}-m_{j}
terms, which is ambiguous. In
[
30
]
they construct these terms already for scalar self-energy functions, but that definition is bound to lead to gauge-dependent mass counterterms beyond the Standard Model, since the employed combination of self-energy functions is gauge-independent only for the Standard Model.

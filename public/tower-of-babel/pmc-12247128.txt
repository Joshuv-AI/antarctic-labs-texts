Inhibition of USP22 by miR-200b-5p represses gastric cancer cell proliferation and migration by targeting the NF-κB signaling pathway
Authors: Yingying Guo, Panpan Zhang, Zhixing Gao, Xiaotian Liu, Chen Su, Su Chen, Tao An, Jingjing Hou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Gastric cancer (GC) is an aggressive tumor type with an intricate pathogenesis and limited therapeutic options. Ubiquitin-specific protease 22 (USP22) is a protein implicated in cell proliferation, metastasis, and tumorigenesis. However, the regulatory mechanisms governing USP22 in GC are still not fully understood. In this study, we perform bioinformatics analysis to identify conserved miRNA recognition sites for miR-200b-5p within the 3′UTR of USP22. Validation via luciferase reporter assay confirms the transcriptional regulation of USP22 by miR-200b-5p. Overexpression of miR-200b-5p markedly inhibits the proliferation and migration of GC cells in vitro and suppresses tumor growth in vivo. Conversely, ectopic expression of USP22 reversed this effect by modulating the NF-κB signaling pathway. Additionally, qPCR analysis reveals an inverse correlation between the miR-200b-5p level and USP22 expression in GC. Collectively, our findings indicate that miR-200b-5p-mediated inhibition of USP22 attenuates cell proliferation by targeting the NF-κB signaling pathway in GC, suggesting that miR-200b-5p and USP22 could serve as potential diagnostic or therapeutic targets for gastric cancer and other related human diseases.

Introduction

Gastric cancer is one of the most aggressive malignant tumors. It is the second leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide and the fourth most commonly diagnosed cancer [1]. Treatment for GC is still limited due to a lack of effective targets. Surgical resection remains the most effective treatment approach, although recurrence rates postsurgery in many patients remain high, and the five-year survival rate is less than 20% [ 1– 3] . Owing to the complex pathogenesis and numerous pathogenic factors of GC, the potential molecular pathways involved in its development are not fully understood.

USP22 is a member of the ubiquitin-specific processing enzymes on human chromosome 17 that encodes an approximately 66 kDa protein product. USP22 is a subunit of the hSAGA complex that regulates a series of biochemical reactions in cells, mainly through deubiquitination modification, including cell growth, metabolism, tumor formation, the cell cycle, transcriptional activation, and signal transduction. Recent studies have shown that USP22 is involved mainly in tumorigenesis by regulating factors such as Bmi-1, MYC, FBP1, and TRF1 [ 4– 9] ; however, how USP22 regulates and participates in tumorigenesis is still unclear.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a type of endogenous, single-stranded, noncoding chromosomal RNA that exerts crucial regulatory functions in cells by explicitly targeting the messenger RNA (mRNA) 3′ untranslated region (UTR), causing mRNA degradation and affecting translation. miRNAs regulate pathways that affect development, virus defense, hematological processes, organ formation, cell proliferation and death, fat metabolism, etc., and may act as oncogenes or tumor suppressors [ 10– 13] . Previous research has demonstrated that USP22 is a target of microRNA-362-3p, which inhibits retinoblastoma growth by reducing the deubiquitination of LSD1 [14]. Moreover, another study suggested that the A1BG-AS1/miR-148a-3p/USP22 axis controls osteosarcoma cell growth and metastasis by stabilizing SIRT1 expression [15]. Additionally, microRNA-30e-5p inhibits the tumorigenesis of non-small cell lung cancer by regulating USP22-mediated Sirt1 [16]. All these data suggest a robust regulatory connection between microRNAs and USP22.

This study identified a novel miRNA, named miR-200b-5p, in GC cells. Our bioinformatics analysis revealed that the 3′ UTR of USP22 contains a conserved miRNA recognition site for miR-200b-5p. Its overexpression dramatically reduced GC cell growth, proliferation, and migration and inhibited tumor growth in nude mice xenografted with GC cells. Luciferase reporter assay revealed that miR-200b-5p inhibited the activity of luciferase reporter genes containing the USP22 3′UTR. The reduction in the proliferation and migration of GC cells mediated by miR-200b-5p was reversed by the overexpression of USP22. To identify which signaling pathway miR-200b-5p targets to regulate the participation and effects of UPS22, we examined the effects of miR-200b-5p and USP22 on NF-κB transcriptional activity. Our results showed that miR-200b-5p had an inhibitory effect on USP22, inhibiting cell growth and migration by targeting the NF-κB signaling pathway in cancer. In contrast to the upregulation of USP22 mRNA, qPCR revealed significant downregulation of miR-200b-5p in clinical cancer tissues. Our data indicate that USP22 is a direct target of miR-200b-5p and that the suppression of USP22 by miR-200b-5p inhibits gastric cancer cell proliferation by targeting the NF-κB signaling pathway. USP22 and miR-200b-5p may be crucial therapeutic or diagnostic targets for treating human diseases such as gastric cancer.

Materials and Methods

Plasmid construction

A fragment of the human miR-200b-5p gene was amplified via PCR from the genomic DNA of human gastric cells. The PCR-amplified fragment was then introduced into the lentiviral vector pLV-EF1α-MCS-IRES-Puro (pLV-ctrl) to produce pLV-miR-200b-5p. To create plasmids that overexpress human USP22, full-length cDNA encoding USP22 was amplified via PCR, and the PCR fragment was subsequently subcloned and inserted into the pCMV-HA/Myc, pCMV-FLAG, and pLL3.7-CMV vectors. PGL-3-NF-κB and PGL-3-Ctrl luciferase reporter plasmids were kind gifts from Professor Chundong Yu (Xiamen University, Xiamen, China). pMDL, pVSVG, and pREV were originally stored in our lab. DNA sequencing was performed by Sango (Shanghai, China) to confirm the validity of all the constructs obtained from the PCR products.

RNA interference

To conduct RNA interference experiments, the lentivirus-based pLL3.7 vector was used. The pLL3.7 vector was used to clone oligonucleotides that target USP22 (USP22 shRNA-1, 5′-GAAGCAUUCACGAGCAU-3′; USP22 shRNA-2, 5′-GAAGCATAUUCACGAGCAU-3′). The packaging plasmids VSV-G, RSV-REV, and pMDL were cotransfected with recombinant lentiviral plasmids into 293T cells. The viral supernatants were used to infect target cells in the presence of 6 μg/mL polybrene (Sigma-Aldrich, Shanghai, China) after 48 h, after which they were passed through 0.45-μm filters.

Cell culture and transient transfection

Human gastric cancer cells (GES, HGC27, MGC803, SGC7901, MKN45, and BGC823) and 293T cells were acquired from the Shanghai Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China) and the ATCC (Manassas, USA). The cells were maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) and Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium (Cellmax, Lanzhou, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Amobio, Xiamen, China) and incubated in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO 2/95% air at 37°C. TurboFect transfection reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA) was used according to the manufacturer’s instructions to transfect plasmid DNA, miRNA mimics, and miRNA inhibitors (antisense oligonucleotides) into cells.

CCK-8 assay

Relative cell viability was measured with a CCK-8 kit (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan). Briefly, cells were plated into 96-well plates containing 100 μL of growth medium. Post-transfection for 72 h, CCK-8 reagent (10 mL/well) was added and incubated for 3 h at 37°C in a 5% CO 2 incubator. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. The cell viability was calculated as follows: Cell viability = [(A1–AB)/(A0–AB)] × 100%, where A1 is the absorbance of treatment group, A0 is the absorbance of control group and AB is the absorbance of the blank group.

Colony formation assay

Cells were treated and then 1 × 10 3 cells/well were seeded in 6-well plates with medium changed every two days. Cells were fixed with methanol and stained with crystal violet after 10 days. Colonies were counted and analyzed for clonogenicity.

Western blot analysis

Total cell extracts were prepared in cell lysis bufer (20 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 20 mM β-glycerophosphate, 10 mM NaF, 1 mM sodium orthovanadate, 1 mM PMSF, 2 μg/mL aprotinin, 10 μg/mL leupeptin, 1% Triton X-100, and 1 mM EDTA) for immunoprecipitations. Immunocomplexes were resolved by 10% SDS-PAGE, and western blotting was performed with the following Abs: anti-actin monoclonal Abs (Sigma-Aldrich) and anti-USP22 monoclonal Abs (Abcam, Cambridge, UK).

Wound healing assay

GC cells were cultivated in 6-well plates. After the cells reached 90% confluence, a line was drawn using a marker on the bottom of the dish, after which a sterile 100-μL pipet tip was used to scratch three separate wounds through the cells, moving perpendicularly to the line. The cells were gently rinsed twice with PBS to remove floating cells and incubated in 5 mL of medium containing 10% FBS at 37°C. Images of the scratches were taken via an inverted microscope at 10× magnification after 0 and 48 h of incubation.

Cell invasion and migration assays

BioCoat Matrigel invasion chambers (BD Biosciences, San Jose, USA) were used for the in vitro invasion assay. The upper chamber was loaded with cell suspensions (6 × 10 4 cells/mL) in 0.5 mL of serum-free DMEM, while the lower chambers were filled with complete media. After 48 h, a sterile cotton swab was used to remove the cells from the upper chambers, while the cells that penetrated through the Matrigel layer were stained and counted. Similar cell migration assays were carried out without the use of Matrigel. Phase-contrast microscopy was used to obtain images of the cultures. Three duplicates of Each experiment was carried out three times in duplicate.

Luciferase reporter assay

At 80% confluence, HEK293T and GC cells were transfected in 6-well plates with 0.5 μg of various reporters and additional plasmids in various combinations, as specified. To check the effectiveness of the transfection, 0.5 μg of pCMV5-LacZ, which expresses β-galactosidase, was added to each sample. The cells were harvested 24 h after transfection, and luciferase activity was assessed. All transfections were performed at least three times in triplicate.

Real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cultivated cells and frozen tissue samples via TRIzol (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA) for qPCR studies. Using the SuperScript II RT kit (Invitrogen), reverse transcription was performed on 2 μg of total RNA from each sample. qRT-PCR was performed via the Step One Plus Real-Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems Inc., Foster City, USA) and SYBR Green Real-Time PCR Master Mix (Cat#QPK-212; Toyobo, Shanghai, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Clinical samples

All clinical samples were collected with informed consents obtained from all the patients, and the study protocols were in compliance with the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki (1975) and approved by the Institutional Medical Ethics Committee of Zhongshan Hospital of Xiamen University (protocol code xmzsyyky-2020-060 and date of approval: March 25, 2020).

Animal experiments

BALB/c female athymic mice, 6–8 weeks old and weighing 20–25 g, were obtained from Experimental Animal Center of Xiamen University (Xiamen, China). All the animals were housed in an environment with a temperature of 22 ± 1°C, a relative humidity of 50% ± 1%, and a light/dark cycle of 12/12 h. All experimental procedures involving animals were performed using protocols approved by the Institutional Animal Use and Care Committee of Xiamen University (protocol code: XMULAC20190624 and date of approval: June 23, 2019) in compliance with the AAALAC and the IACUC guidelines.

To create a xenograft model, SGC7901 cells (1 × 10 6) infected with pLV-miR-200b-5p or pLV-Ctrl and suspended in 150 μL of PBS were injected into the right side of the posterior flank. A Vernier caliper was used to measure the tumor size every five days, starting on the seventh day following the injection, along two perpendicular axes. The volume of each tumor was determined via the following equation: volume = length × width 2 × 0.52. The mice were sacrificed thirty days after the injection, and the tumors were collected for subsequent RT-PCR analyses of miR-200b-5p and USP22 expressions.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, USA) was used for all the statistical analyses. Data are expressed as the mean ± SD for a minimum of three independent experiments. One-way ANOVA with Bonferroni’s post-test was used for multiple comparisons; two-tailed Student’s t test was used for pairwise comparisons. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

USP22 is a direct target of miR-200b-5p in GC cells

To identify miRNAs that can regulate USP22, we examined the 3′UTR sequence of human USP22 via prediction tools including TargetScan ( http://www.targetscan.org), miRanda ( http://www.microrna.org), and miRDB ( http://www.mirdb.org). The results of our bioinformatics study revealed that the human USP22 3′UTR contains a single miRNA recognition element (MRE) ( Figure 1A). To further identify the specific target of miR-200b-5p regulation of USP22, a luciferase activity experiment was performed to confirm the targeting of USP22 by miR-200b-5p. We identified two potential miRNA MREs in the 3′UTR of USP22. We subsequently created two USP22-3′UTR-luciferase reporter plasmids and a mutant (Mut) USP22-3′UTR-luciferase reporter plasmid in accordance with the location shown in Figure 1A. The results suggested that miR-200b-5p obviously inhibited the luciferase activity of the wild-type (Wt) USP22-3UTR but not that of the Mut- USP22-3′UTR in both types of 293T cells ( Figure 1B,C). Additionally, our qPCR analyses revealed that, compared with the control, transfection of miR-200b-5p significantly increased miR-200b-5p expression in 293T and SGC7901 cells ( Figure 1B,C; left panel).

Next, we measured the amount of USP22 mRNA in SGC7901 and BGC823 cells transfected with the corresponding miRNAs. The miR-200b-5p mimic dramatically reduced the expression of USP22 mRNA ( Figure 2C); in contrast, the miR-200b-5p inhibitor significantly increased the expression of USP22 mRNA in GC cells ( Figure 1D,E). On the basis of these data, we hypothesized that miR-200b-5p regulates and controls USP22 mRNA by targeting the USP22 3′UTR. miR-200b-5p inhibits GC cell proliferation and migration in vitro

To determine the biological function of miR-200b-5p in GC, SGC7901 and BGC823 cells were transfected with miR-200b-5p mimics or miR-NC (control cells). As shown in Figure 2A‒F, compared with the control, overexpression of miR-200b-5p dramatically decreased SGC7901 and BGC823 cell proliferation. Moreover, wound scratch assay revealed that miR-200b-5p decreased cell migration ( Figure 2G); the percentage of migrating cells is depicted on the right side of the picture and revealed that SGC7901 and BGC823 cells overexpressing miR-200b-5p had decreased cell migration ( P < 0.01). These findings clearly indicate that miR-200b-5p suppresses GC cell migration and proliferation.

Overexpression of USP22 reverses the inhibition of cell proliferation and metastasis induced by miR-200b-5p in GC cells

Given that miR-200b-5p can target USP22, we next assessed the effect of USP22 on GC cell growth. SGC7901 and BGC823 cells were transfected with a viral packaging plasmid expressing pLV-USP22. Upon transfection, the expression of USP22 was significantly increased ( Figure 3A). To determine whether USP22 can reverse the inhibition of cell proliferation and metastasis caused by miR-200b-5p in SGC7901 and BGC823 cells, USP22 was subsequently added to cells transfected with this miRNA. The results of the CCK-8 and colony formation assays revealed that overexpression of USP22 reversed the miR-200b-5p-mediated inhibition of GC cell proliferation ( Figure 3B–D). Additionally, Transwell experiments demonstrated that USP22 prevents metastasis in GC cells ( Figure 3E). These data indicate that USP22 reverses the inhibition of cell proliferation and metastasis induced by miR-200b-5p in GC cells.

miR-200b-5p represses the NF-κB signaling pathway by targeting USP22

The NF-κB signaling pathway has been strongly linked to malignant cell growth, proliferation, and metastasis [ 18, 19] . Next, we examined the effects of miR-200b-5p and USP22 on the transcriptional activity of NF-κB via a luciferase reporter assay in 293T and GC cells with or without TNF-α treatment, as the TNF-α signals are predominantly mediated by NF-κB. As shown in Figure 4A, overexpression of miR-200b-5p dramatically suppressed NF-κB-mediated gene activation. However, when USP22 was overexpressed, the miR-200b-5p-induced inhibition of NF-κB was reversed ( Figure 4B). USP22 expression is depicted in Figure 4C. Next, we downregulated the expression of USP22 in SGC7901 cells through lentiviruses expressing either pLV-Ctrl or pLV-shUSP22. As shown in Figures 4D–E, USP22 mRNA and protein expression levels were drastically reduced after infection with lentiviruses encoding pLV-shUSP22. Luciferase reporter assay revealed that USP22 knockdown decreased NF-κB reporter gene activity after TNF-α stimulation ( Figure 4F). These findings indicate that knockdown of USP22 enhances the effect of miR-200b-5p on NF-κB reporter activity. Therefore, we believe that miR-200b-5p represses the NF-κB signaling pathway by targeting USP22.

miR-200b-5p suppresses tumorigenicity in nude mice

A xenograft tumor growth assay was performed to confirm the function of miR-200b-5p and investigate its effect on the tumorigenicity of GC cells. SGC7901 cells were infected with lentiviruses expressing either pLV-Ctrl or pLV-miR-200b-5p. Following screening, stable SGC7901-control and SGC7901-miR-200b-5p-expressing cell lines were injected into nude BALB/c female mice. The sizes of the tumors were measured every 4 days. The tumors in the miR-200b-5p-expressing group grew more slowly than did the tumors in the control group, as shown in Figure 5A–C. By observing the volume and total volume inhibition rates, we found that the tumor volume and weight in the miR-200b-5p group were significantly lower than those in the control group ( Figure 5D). The expressions of miR-200b-5p and USP22 in mouse tumors were subsequently determined via RT‒PCR. miR-200b-5p was significantly expressed in tumors, whereas USP22 was weakly expressed in tumors. In addition, we also determined whether USP22 is expressed in randomly selected xenograft mouse tumors. We found that tumors overexpressing miR-200b-5p presented reduced USP22 expression ( Figure 5E). These data indicate that miR-200b-5p suppresses tumorigenicity in nude mice.

Inverse correlation between miR-200b-5p expression and USP22 mRNA expression in GC clinical samples and cell lines

We selected clinical samples to study the relationship between the expression of miR-200b-5p and the expression of USP22 via qPCR analysis. Compared with those in the control group, in approximately 67% of human GC tissues, miR-200b-5p (16 out of 30) was significantly downregulated, whereas USP22 mRNA expression level was increased (21 out of 30) ( Figure 6A,B). Among the 30 pairs of GC samples, the correlation analysis performed via GraphPad Prism 9.3.1 revealed substantial inverse correlations between the expressions of miR-200b-5p and USP22 mRNA ( P < 0.01; Figure 6C). miR-200b-5p was correlated with TNM and lymph node status but not with patient age, sex, or tumor site ( Table 2). The expression of miR-200b-5p was also downregulated in four GC cell lines, SGC-7901, BGC823, MKN-45, and HGC-27, compared with that in one normal gastric mucosa cell line (GES), whereas USP22 mRNA expression in the four cell lines was upregulated compared with that in the GES cell line ( P < 0.05; Figure 6D,E). These findings revealed that in GC patients and cell lines, miR-200b-5p is inversely correlated with the expression of USP22. Discussion

Gastric cancer is one of the most common malignancies worldwide, with a high incidence and prevalence in China, a complex pathology, and limited treatment options [ 2, 20, 21] , with surgery being the leading cause. In addition, due to a lack of clear markers, there are certain limitations in the treatment process. Herein, we discovered a novel miRNA that controls the proliferation of GC cells and USP22. The key findings of this study are as follows: (1) USP22 is a direct target of miR-200b-5p in GC cells ( Figure 1); (2) miR-200b-5p inhibits GC cell proliferation and migration in vitro ( Figure 2); (3) overexpression of USP22 reverses the inhibition of cell proliferation and metastasis induced by miR-200b-5p in GC cells ( Figure 3); (4) miR-200b-5p represses USP22 and then inhibits the NF-κB signaling pathway ( Figure 4); (5) miR-200b-5p suppresses tumorigenicity in nude mice ( Figure 5); and (6) USP22 is inversely expressed with miR-200b-5p in GC patients and cell lines ( Figure 6). These findings revealed that miR-200b-5p plays a critical regulatory role in GC by inhibiting NF-κB signaling and suppressing the expression of USP22. miR-200b-5p and USP22 may be crucial markers or therapeutic targets for gastric cancer and other human diseases.

USP22 performs a wide range of biological functions by altering the deubiquitination of substrate target molecules. In vitro data have shown that USP22 can separate ubiquitin from ubiquitinated target proteins, although this action is dependent on the presence of cysteine 185. USP22 interacts with genes in eukaryotic cells by regulating TRRAP, ADA2, ADA3, TAF9L, TAF10, ataxin7, and the acetyltransferase hGCN5 via its hSAGA subunits. USP22 interacts with SAGA and can hydrolyze ubiquitin on histone H2B, which is required for SAGA-induced gene transcription [ 5, 6, 15, 22] . As a component of the cancer death signature, USP22 controls several biochemical processes in cells via deubiquitination. Numerous studies have confirmed its importance in tumorigenesis and development. However, research on its specific mechanism of action is still lacking. Additionally, since USP22 is a marker for metastasis and controls the cell cycle, targeted inhibitors of USP22 may be useful in treating tumors. As a result, thorough investigation into the biological role and mechanism of the USP22 protein may not only aid in further elucidating the biological traits of tumor cells as well as the molecular mechanisms of tumorigenesis, development, and metastasis but also help with early diagnostics and cancer gene therapy. Cell function may be impacted by additional techniques that we utilize to limit USP22 expression and function. We believe that miR-200b-5p may affect additional pathways in addition to the NF-κB signaling system; nonetheless, this requires further exploration. Our research consistently revealed that miR-200b-5p inhibits USP22 and suppresses the NF-κB signaling pathway. Nevertheless, the present study still has some limitations, so we plan to validate the use of these cell lines further. Larger clinical studies are also warranted to validate our conclusions.

miRNAs, a subclass of short noncoding RNAs, are important regulators in both invertebrates and vertebrates. Currently, some miRNAs have been predicted via bioinformatics analysis together with their biological traits, and some miRNAs have been confirmed via molecular biology techniques; however, many miRNAs have not been identified. Although the functions of many miRNAs have been elucidated, many still remain unclear [ 10, 23– 25] . Additionally, the way miRNAs work is still not entirely understood. Our future research will examine how various miRNAs affect the regulation of genes in cells. We still need to examine the role of miR-200b-5p and USP22 in other cancer types to determine whether their effects are consistent with those in gastric cancer or if there is a similar trend and to explore the feasibility of their potential targeted therapies. Targeting the miR-200b-5p/USP22 axis can be used for potential clinical applications for GC therapy. Clinically, gastric cancer can be treated by inhibiting the expression of USP22 via miRNAs.

In conclusion, our results demonstrated for the first time that miR-200b-5p inhibits USP22, which in turn inhibits the NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby targeting cell proliferation in gastric cancer. miR-200b-5p and USP22 may also be important targets for the diagnostic or therapeutic inhibition of gastric cancers and other human diseases.

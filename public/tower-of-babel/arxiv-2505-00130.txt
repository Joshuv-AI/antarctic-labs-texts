Pancyclicity in hypergraphs with large uniformity
Teegan Bailey, Isaiah Hollars, Yupei Li, Ruth Luo

Abstract
A Berge cycle of length ℓ \ell in a hypergraph ℋ \mathcal{H} is a sequence of alternating vertices and edges v 0 ​ e 0 ​ v 1 ​ e 1 ​ … ​ v ℓ ​ e ℓ ​ v 0 v_{0}e_{0}v_{1}e_{1}...v_{\ell}e_{\ell}v_{0} such that { v i , v i + 1 } ⊆ e i \{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subseteq e_{i} for all i i , with indices taken modulo ℓ \ell . For n n sufficiently large and r ≥ ⌊ n − 1 2 ⌋ − 1 r\geq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor-1 we prove exact minimum degree conditions for an n n -vertex, r r -uniform hypergraph to contain Berge cycles of every length between 2 2 and n n . Introduction

A
hypergraph

\mathcal{H}
is a set of vertices
V(\mathcal{H})
and a set of edges
E(\mathcal{H})
such that every edge is a subset of the vertices. We say a hypergraph is
r
-uniform
if every edge contains exactly
r
vertices. In particular, a
2
-uniform hypergraph is simply a graph.
The
degree
of a vertex
v
in a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, denoted
d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
, is the number of edges containing
v
. We denote by
\delta(\mathcal{H})
the
minimum degree
over all vertices in
\mathcal{H}
. Given a pair of vertices
u
and
v
, the
co-degree
of the pair, denoted
d_{\mathcal{H}}(u,v)
, is the number of edges containing both
u
and
v
. When
\mathcal{H}
is clear from context, we may drop the subscripts in the definitions above.

A
hamiltonian cycle (path)
in a graph is a cycle (path) which covers all of its vertices. We say a graph is
hamiltonian
if it contains such a cycle. The first extremal result for hamiltonian cycles was due to Dirac.

Theorem 1

(Dirac
[
9
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 3
. If
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq n/2
, then
G
is hamiltonian.

Bondy
[
4
]
studied a much stronger property in graphs called
pancyclicity
. An
n
-vertex graph is
pancyclic
if it contains cycles of every length from
3
to
n
. The following is one of many results by Bondy on pancyclicity. In particular, it strengthens Dirac’s Theorem.

Theorem 2

(Bondy
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex hamiltonian graph with
|E(G)|\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4}
. Then either
G
is pancyclic or
K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}
.

Thus the same minimum degree condition in Dirac’s Theorem not only yields a cycle of length
n
but rather cycles of
all
lengths, unless
G
is the complete bipartite graph which misses all odd cycles.

In
[
4
]
, Bondy proposed a so-called meta-conjecture:
“almost any
nontrivial condition on a graph which implies that the graph is hamiltonian also implies that the
graph is pancyclic.”
He later added
“…there may be a simple family of exceptional graphs.”

See for example
[
8
,
10
,
19
,
22
]
for some results in support of Bondy’s meta-conjecture.

The goal of this paper is to extend pancyclic results to hypergraphs. First we define our notion of cycles in hypergraphs—while many definitions of cycles in hypergraphs exist, e.g., tight cycles, loose cycles, we consider a general definition called
Berge cycles
.

A
Berge cycle
of length
k
in a hypergraph is a set of
k
distinct vertices
\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1}\}
and
k
distinct edges
\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{k-1}\}
such that for all
0\leq i\leq k-1
,
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subseteq e_{i}
. For convenience, we write Berge cycles as an alternating sequence of vertices and edges
v_{0}e_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n-1}e_{n-1}v_{0}
. We often say
k
-Berge cycle
to refer to a Berge cycle of length
k
.

A
hamiltonian Berge cycle
in an
n
-vertex hypergraph is a Berge cycle of length
n
. We call a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}

hamiltonian
if it contains a hamiltonian Berge cycle. Moreover, we say
\mathcal{H}
is
pancyclic
if it contains Berge cycles of every length from
2
to
n
. Note that unlike in the graph case,
r
-uniform hypergraphs may have
2
-Berge cycles.

Sufficient conditions for long Berge cycles and Berge cycles of fixed length have been well studied in a number of papers. See for instance
[
14
,
12
,
13
,
17
,
15
,
21
,
20
,
7
]
. In particular, the following theorem gives a Dirac-type condition for the existence of hamiltonian Berge cycles in uniform hypergraphs. Note that the minimum degree required depends on the uniformity
r
, and there is a transition around
r=n/2
.

Theorem 3

(Kostochka, Luo, McCourt
[
18
]
)
.

Let
3\leq r<n
, and let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
r
-uniform hypergraph. If
r\leq\left\lfloor(n-1)/2\right\rfloor
and
\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq\binom{\left\lfloor(n-1)/2\right\rfloor}{r-1}+1
or
r\geq n/2
and
\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq r
, then
\mathcal{H}
contains a Berge hamiltonian cycle.

In
[
2
]
, three of the present authors proved that for
r\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-2
, the minimum degree condition in Theorem
3
in fact guarantees the stronger condition of pancyclicity when
n
is sufficiently large.

Theorem 4

(Bailey, Li, Luo
[
2
]
)
.

Let
n
and
r
be positive integers with
3\leq r\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-2
, such that
n\geq 70
if
r\in\{3,4\}
and
n\geq 39
if
r\geq 5
. If
\mathcal{H}
is an
n
-vertex,
r
-uniform hypergraph with minimum degree
\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq\binom{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}{r-1}+1
then
\mathcal{H}
is pancyclic.

The goal of this paper is to complete the remaining cases, i.e.,
r\geq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-1
. The minimum degree bounds are the same as in Theorem
3
, thus we can view our theorem as a strenghtening of the hamiltonian result, akin to Bondy’s meta-conjecture.

Theorem 5
.

Let
n
and
r
be integers with
r\geq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-1
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
r
-uniform hypergraph. If

(a)

n\geq 31
,
r\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
and

\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq\binom{\left\lfloor(n-1)/2\right\rfloor}{r-1}+1
or

(b)

n\geq 55,r\geq n/2
and
\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq r
,

then
\mathcal{H}
is pancyclic.

The bounds for
\delta
and
r
are sharp due to the following constructions. We note that we do not think the bounds
n\geq 31
and
n\geq 55
are best possible, and they can likely be improved with more careful arguments.

Construction 1
. Let
r\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
consist of two cliques on vertex sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
where
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=(n+1)/2
and
|V_{1}\cap V_{2}|=1
if
n
is odd, and
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=n/2
and
|V_{1}\cap V_{2}|=0
if
n
is even. In addition, when
n
is even,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
may have one extra edge joining
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
.

Construction 2
. Let
r\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
. Let
V(\mathcal{H}_{2})
be partitioned into sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
with
|V_{1}|=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor,|V_{2}|=\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil
. The edge set
E(\mathcal{H}_{2})
contains all edges with at most one vertex in
V_{2}
. Moreover, when
n
is even, we may add one additional edge containing multiple vertices in
V_{2}
.

Construction 3
. Let
r\geq n/2
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{3}^{\prime}
be any
r
-uniform,
r
-regular,
n
-vertex hypergraph (such hypergraphs exist, for instance the tight
n
-cycle). Let
\mathcal{H}_{3}
be obtained from
\mathcal{H}_{3}^{\prime}
by removing exactly one edge.

All three constructions do not have hamiltonian Berge cycles—
\mathcal{H}_{1}
has either a vertex or an edge whose removal disconnects
\mathcal{H}_{1}
;
\mathcal{H}_{2}
does not have enough edges connecting multiple vertices in
V_{2}
to yield a spanning Berge cycle;
\mathcal{H}_{3}
contains only
n-1
edges. Since these examples are not hamiltonian, they also fail to be pancyclic. Moreover, one can check that the minimum degrees of the graphs are one less than those in Theorem
5
.

2
Proof outline and tools

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph with
V(\mathcal{H})=\{v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1}\}
and
r\geq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-1
. Throughout, we will have the necessary degree conditions to apply Theorem
3
, which guarantees the existence of a Berge hamiltonian cycle. Let
C=v_{0}e_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n-1}e_{n-1}v_{0}
be such a Berge cycle where
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\subset e_{i}
for all
i
.

Remark
: Throughout this paper, all arithmetic for vertex indices are taken modulo
n
. We will compare vertex indices in
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
with
<
. For
\bar{a},\bar{b}\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a unique choice of
a,b\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
such that
\bar{a}=a+n\mathbb{Z}
and
\bar{b}=b+n\mathbb{Z}
. We say
\bar{a}<\bar{b}
if
a<b
. For readability, we omit the use of bar notation when referring to the indices.

In any
r
-uniform hamiltonian hypergraph with
r\geq 3
, there is a vertex
v
with degree at least
3
. Since we have
r>(n-1)/3
, there exists a pair of edges containing
v
that intersect in another point
u
. This yields a Berge cycle of length
2
. In the rest of the paper, we focus our attention on finding Berge cycles with lengths in
\{3,\ldots,n\}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=E(\mathcal{H})-E(C)
denote the set of edges in
\mathcal{H}
that are not used in the Berge hamiltonian cycle
C
. We will often refer to these as the
extra edges
of
\mathcal{H}
. For
0\leq i\leq n-1
, let
\mathcal{E}_{i}=\{e\in\mathcal{E}:v_{i}\in e\}
. Define
\mathcal{U}=\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal{E}}e
to be the set of vertices contained in the edges of
\mathcal{E}
and
similarly define
\mathcal{U}_{i}=\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{i}}e
.

In some parts of the proofs, we consider an auxiliary graph where cycles in the auxiliary graph correspond to Berge cycles in the hypergraph. We can then make use of the following pancyclic-type results for graphs.

A graph
G
is called
weakly pancyclic
if it contains every cycle length from its girth to its circumference.

The
girth
of a graph
G
,
g(G)
, is the length of its shortest cycle. The
circumference
of
G
,
c(G)
, is the length of its longest cycle. In the case where
g(G)=3
and
c(G)=n
, being weakly pancyclic is equivalent to being pancyclic.

Theorem 6

(Brandt
[
5
]
)
.

Every nonbipartite graph
G
of order
n
with minimum degree at least
\frac{n+2}{3}
is weakly pancylic, with girth either 3 or 4.

We say a bipartite graph
G
is
bipancyclic
if it contains cycles of every even length from
4
to
|V(G)|
. Similarly, we say
G
is
weakly bipancyclic
if it contains cycles of every even length from its girth to its circumference.

Theorem 7

(Hu, Sun
[
16
]
)
.

Every hamiltonian bipartite graph on
2n
vertices with minimum degree at least
\frac{n}{3}+4
is bipancyclic.

In
[
16
]
, a stronger version is proved which will be useful for us.

Theorem 8

(Hu, Sun
[
16
]
)
.

If
G=(X\sqcup Y,E)
is a bipartite graph with minimum degree at least
\frac{m}{3}+4
, where
m=\max\{|X|,|Y|\}
, then
G
is a weakly bipancyclic graph with girth 4.

See
[
6
,
3
]
for more results on weakly pancyclic graphs and
[
23
,
1
,
24
]
for more results on bipancyclic graphs.

We will prove a stronger version of Theorem
5
for the cases
r\geq\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil
. The stronger statements of the following two theorems along with Theorem
3
will imply Theorem

.

Theorem 9
.

Let
n
and
r
be integers with
r\geq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph with a hamiltonian Berge cycle
C
.
Define

c_{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}6&\text{if }r=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor,\\
1&\text{if }r\geq n/2.\end{array}\right.

If there exists a vertex
v
contained in at least
c_{r}
edges outside of

, then
\mathcal{H}
is pancyclic.

Theorem 10
.

Let
n\geq 19
be an integer and set
r=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-1
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-uniform hypergraph with a hamiltonian Berge cycle
C
. If every vertex in
\mathcal{H}
is contained in at least
5(r-1)+2
edges outside of

, then
\mathcal{H}
is pancyclic.

Proof of Theorem
5
assuming Theorems
9
and
10
.

By Theorem
3
,
\mathcal{H}
has a hamiltonian Berge cycle
C
.
If
r=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-1
and
\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq\binom{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}{r-1}+1
, then each vertex is contained in at least
\binom{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}{r-1}+1-n=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\lfloor(n-3)/2\rfloor+1-n
edges outside of
E(C)
. This is at least
5(r-1)+2
when
n\geq 31
, so we may apply Theorem
10
.

Now suppose
r\geq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
and
fix an
r
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the statement of Theorem
5
. For
r=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
,
\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq\binom{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}{r-1}+1=r+1
, and for
r\geq n/2
,
\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq r
. In either case,
\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq n/2
.

Set
X=V(\mathcal{H})
and
Y=E(\mathcal{H})
. The
incidence bipartite graph
of
\mathcal{H}
is the bipartite graph
G=X\sqcup Y
where
v_{i}e\in E(G)
if and only if
v_{i}\in e
. A cycle of length
2\ell
in
G
yields a Berge cycle of length
\ell
in
\mathcal{H}
, and vice versa. Thus if
G
is weakly bipancyclic with girth
4
and circumference
2n
, then
\mathcal{H}
is pancyclic. By Theorem
3
,
G
has a cycle of length
2n
.

Observe that if
|E(\mathcal{H})|=m
, then
G
has
m+n
vertices, where
m\geq n
. Moreover,
d_{G}(x)\geq\delta(\mathcal{H})\geq n/2
and
d_{G}(y)=r
for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. Thus
\delta(G)\geq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
.

By Theorem
8
, if
\delta(G)\geq m/3+4
, then
G
is weakly pancyclic with girth
4
. That is,
\mathcal{H}
is pancyclic. So suppose
\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\leq\delta(G)\leq m/3+4
. This implies
m\geq 3n/2-15
. Hence the set of extra edges,
\mathcal{E}=E(\mathcal{H})-E(C)
, contains at least
n/2-15
edges. Since each edge has

vertices, there exists a vertex with
d_{\mathcal{E}}(v)\geq\frac{r(n/2-15)}{n}\geq c_{r}
for

. Thus we may apply Theorem

to obtain that

is pancyclic.
∎

In the remainder of the paper we prove Theorems

and

2.1
Shifting functions

Recall that
\mathcal{H}
has hamiltonian cycle
C=v_{0}e_{0}v_{1}...v_{n-1}e_{n-1}v_{1}
.
Let
A\subset V(\mathcal{H})
. For
s\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}

A^{+s}=\{v_{i+s}:v_{i}\in A\},

where addition of indices is performed modulo

Observation 1

If an extra edge

contains a

Indeed, the Berge cycle

For

define the shifting function
S_{s}:V(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow V(\mathcal{H})
by

S_{s}(v_{i})=\begin{cases}v_{i+s}&i+s\leq n-1\\
v_{i+s+1}&i+s\geq n\end{cases}.

We call

the

. For a set

Fix

(a)

with equality if and only if
v_{0},v_{n-1}\in A
and

(b)

\mathcal{H}
has an
(n-s+1)
-Berge cycle (provided

is injective when restricted to

holds.

We now prove part

2.2
Self-Shift Complementary Sets

When forbidding
k
-chords for some
k\in[n-1]
, we will often consider sets
\mathcal{A}\subset V(\mathcal{H})
where
v_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
implies
v_{i+k}\notin\mathcal{A}
. If
A=\{i:v_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\}
is the set of indices from
\mathcal{A}
, then
A\cap(k+A)=\emptyset
, where
k+A\coloneqq\{k+a\pmod{n}:a\in A\}
. Moreover, when
|A|=n/2
, then
A=\overline{k+A}
, the complement of
k+A
in
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
.
For
A\subset\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, we say that
A
is
k
-self-shift complimentary
(
k
-SSC for short) if
|A|=n/2
and
A\cap(k+A)=\emptyset
.

In the following proposition, we use some elementary group theory. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
\langle k\rangle
the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
generated by
k
, i.e.,
\langle k\rangle=\{ik\pmod{n}:i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.
Then for
j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
,
j+\langle k\rangle
is a coset of the subgroup
\langle k\rangle
in
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 1

(SSC structure)
.

Let
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Suppose
A\subset\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
with
0\in A
,
|A|=n/2
and
(k+A)\cap A=\emptyset
. For
d=\gcd(n,k)
, the following hold.

(i)

\frac{n}{d}
is even,

(ii)

(d+A)\cap A=\emptyset
, and

(iii)

A
can be partitioned into
d
sets
O_{0},\ldots,O_{d-1}
defined as follows. For
j\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}
, we define

O_{j}=\begin{cases}j+\langle 2d\rangle&\text{ if }\,j\in A,\\
(j+d)+\langle 2d\rangle&\text{ if }\,j\notin A.\end{cases}

Each
O_{j}

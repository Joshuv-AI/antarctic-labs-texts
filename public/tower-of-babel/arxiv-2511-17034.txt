Affine Jacobi–Trudi formulas and q , t q,t -Rogers–Ramanujan identities
S. Ole Warnaar

Abstract
We conjecture affine or Hall–Littlewood analogues of the dual Jacobi–Trudi formulas for orthogonal and symplectic Schur functions indexed by rectangular partitions of maximal height. These conjectures are then used to derive t t -analogues of many known Rogers–Ramanujan identities for the characters of standard modules of affine Lie algebras. This includes t t -analogues of the classical Rogers–Ramanujan identities, (some of) the Andrews–Gordon identities and the C n ( 1 ) \mathrm{C}_{n}^{(1)} , A 2 ​ n ( 2 ) \mathrm{A}_{2n}^{(2)} and D n + 2 ( 2 ) \mathrm{D}_{n+2}^{(2)} GOW identities. We also prove an affine analogue of the dual Jacobi–Trudi formula for Schur functions indexed by rectangular partitions of arbitrary height.

Jacobi–Trudi formulas, Rogers–Ramanujan identities,

Introduction

1.1
The Jacobi–Trudi and dual Jacobi–Trudi formulas

A partition
\lambda
of length
l(\lambda)
equal to
\ell
is a weakly
decreasing sequence
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
of nonnegative integers such
that
\lambda_{i}>0
for
i\leqslant\ell
and
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i>\ell
.
For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
\lambda
a partition, the Schur function

s_{\lambda}(x)
is defined as

s_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\det_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}(x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+n-j})}{\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})}

(1.1)

if
l(\lambda)\leqslant n
, while
s_{\lambda}(x)=0
if
l(\lambda)>n
.
It is a standard result in the theory of symmetric functions
that
s_{\lambda}(x)
for
l(\lambda)\leqslant n

corresponds to the character of the polynomial representation of

\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
indexed by
\lambda
and admits the
combinatorial description

s_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{T\in\SSYT_{n}(\lambda)}x^{T}.

(1.2)

Here
\SSYT_{n}(\lambda)
is the set of semistandard Young tableaux
of shape
\lambda
on
\{1,\dots,n\}
, and

x^{T}:=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
, where

\alpha_{i}
is the number of boxes or squares of
T
with filling
i
.

The complete and elementary symmetric functions
h_{r}
and
e_{r}

are the Schur function for partitions whose Young diagram consists of
a single row or column of
r
boxes, respectively.
That is,
h_{r}=s_{(r)}
and
e_{r}=s_{(1^{r})}
, or, more explicitly,

h_{0}=e_{0}=1
and

\displaystyle h_{r}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}\leqslant i_{2}\leqslant\dots\leqslant i_{r}\leqslant n}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{r}},

\displaystyle e_{r}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1<i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r}\leqslant n}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{r}}

for
r
a positive integer.
Hence
e_{r}(x)=0
for
r>n
.
Also defining
e_{r}=h_{r}=0
for
r<0
, two classical results in
the theory of symmetric functions, known as the Jacobi–Trudi
identity and dual Jacobi–Trudi (or Nägelsbach–Kostka) identity,
express the Schur functions in terms of determinants
with entries given by the complete and elementary symmetric functions,
respectively:

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n}\big(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}(x)\big)

(1.3a)

\displaystyle=\det_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k}\big(e_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-i+j}(x)\big).

(1.3b)

In (
1.3b
), the partition
\lambda^{\prime}
is the conjugate of
\lambda
and

k
is an arbitrary integer such that
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\leqslant k
.

Surprisingly little appears to be known about Jacobi–Trudi identities
for important generalisations of the Schur functions such as the
Hall–Littlewood, Jack and Macdonald polynomials.
Matsumoto
[
50
, Theorem 5.1]
discovered
Jacobi–Trudi-like formulas for the Jack polynomials
for partitions of rectangular shape, provided the Jack parameter

\alpha
is a positive integer or the reciprocal
of a positive integer.
In Matsumoto’s formulas, determinants are replaced by hyperdeterminants
of (even) order depending on the value of
\alpha
.
This was subsequently generalised to partitions of near rectangular
shape by Belbachir, Boussicault and Luque
[
8
]
.
In this paper we add to these results by proving a
dual Jacobi–Trudi formula for ordinary (or
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
)
Hall–Littlewood polynomials indexed by rectangular shapes
and by conjecturing dual Jacobi–Trudi formulas
for
\mathrm{B}_{n}
,
\mathrm{C}_{n}
and
\mathrm{BC}_{n}

Hall–Littlewood polynomials indexed by rectangular
partitions of length
n
.

For

.

(1.4)

For

, let

Then the right-hand side of (

[

For integers

Consequently, for

2.3
The

,
\mathrm{C}_{n}

\mathrm{BC}_{n}

Let

\displaystyle P^{(\mathrm{BC}_{n})}_{\lambda}(x;t,s_{1},s_{2})=\frac{1}{(s_{1}s_{2};t)_{n-l(\lambda)}v_{\lambda}(t)}

(2.18b)

\lambda

6
Open problems

To conclude the paper we list a number of open problems pertaining to
affine Jacobi–Trudi and
q,t
–Rogers–Ramanujan identities,
beyond the obvious problem of proving
Conjectures
1.2
–
1.4
.

One such problem is to better understand algebraically for what values
of the parameters
t,s
in
P_{(k^{n})}^{\mathrm{B}_{n}}(x;t,s)
and

P_{(k^{n})}^{\mathrm{C}_{n}}(x;t,s)
, or

t,s_{1},s_{2}
in
P_{(k^{n})}^{\mathrm{C}_{n}}(x;t,s_{1},s_{2})

one should expect further Jacobi–Trudi formulas.
Our list of results is definitely not complete, and for example we
conjecture that the additional
\mathrm{A}_{2k-1}^{(2)}
identity

P_{(k^{n})}^{\mathrm{B}_{n}}(x;t,t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\det_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k}\Big((-1)^{y_{i}}t^{\frac{1}{2}Ky_{i}^{2}-(j-1)y_{i}}\big(\ddot{e}_{n-i+j-Ky_{i}}(x)+\ddot{e}_{n+i+j-Ky_{i}-1}(x)\big)\Big)

holds, where
K=2k
.
What is unusual about this Jacobi–Trudi formula is that there does not
appear to be a companion for
\dot{e}_{r}
.
As a consequence, the
q,t
-Rogers–Ramanujan identity

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\[1.0pt]
\lambda_{1}\leqslant 2k\end{subarray}}q^{|\lambda|}\bigg(\,\prod_{i=1}^{2k-1}(t;t^{2})_{\lceil m_{i}(\lambda)/2\rceil}\bigg)P_{\lambda}\big(1,q,q^{2},\dots;t\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{(p^{2};p^{2})_{\infty}(p;p)_{\infty}^{k-1}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\theta\big(pq^{2i-1};p^{2}\big)\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}\theta\big(q^{j-i},q^{i+j-1};p\big),

where
p=tq^{2k}
, does not appear to have a counterpart for

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\[1.0pt]
\lambda_{1}\leqslant 2k\end{subarray}}q^{|\lambda|/2}\bigg(\,\prod_{i=1}^{2k-1}(t;t^{2})_{\lceil m_{i}(\lambda)/2\rceil}\bigg)P_{\lambda}\big(1,q,q^{2},\dots;t\big).

It remains unclear if this is an isolated case or that further
such results exist.
In particular we note that the affine Lie algebras

\mathrm{B}_{k}^{(1)}
and
\mathrm{D}_{k}^{(1)}
are currently
missing from in our list of
q,t
–Rogers–Ramanujan identities.

It would also be interesting to consider the determinants in
Conjectures
1.2
–
1.4
with

(where

is one of
0,1,2

for

For example, what are the

and

(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{-k}P_{\lambda}(x;t)

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\det_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant k}\Big(t^{(k+\ell+1)y_{i}^{2}-jy_{i}}\big(\dot{e}_{n-i+j-2(k+\ell+1)y_{i}}(x)-\dot{e}_{n+i+j-2(k+\ell+1)y_{i}}(x)\big)\Big)?

(6.1)

The answers to these questions should imply further character identities.
For instance, in the simplest possible case, namely

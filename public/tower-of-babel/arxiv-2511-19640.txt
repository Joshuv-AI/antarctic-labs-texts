Plasmon polariton assisted second-harmonic generation in graphene
João M. Alendouro Pinho 1 , Simão S. Cardoso 2 , Yuliy V. Bludov 2 , João M. Viana Parente Lopes 1 , Vladimir V. Konotop 3 , Joel D. Cox 4,5 , Nuno M.R. Peres 2,4,6

Abstract
In this paper we present a theoretical examination of second-harmonic generation (SHG) in a graphene monolayer integrated within an attenuated total internal reflection (ATR) configuration. By embedding graphene in this optical setup, we explore the enhancement in the nonlinear optical response, particularly focusing on the efficiency of SHG. Our analysis reveals that the excitation of surface plasmon-polaritons (SPPs) plays a central role in significantly boosting the efficiency of SHG. The unique electronic properties of graphene, combined with the resonant characteristics of SPPs, create a synergistic effect that amplifies the nonlinear optical signals. This enhancement is attributed to the strong field confinement and the resonant nature of SPPs, which effectively increase the interaction between the incident light and the graphene monolayer. Furthermore, we analyze the underlying mechanisms that govern this process, providing a comprehensive theoretical framework that elucidates the interplay between graphene’s electronic structure and the optical fields. Our findings suggest that the ATR scheme not only facilitates the excitation of SPPs but also optimizes the conditions for SHG.



I
Introduction

Beyond its many unusual and intriguing properties, graphene–a two-dimensional carbon monolayer–also demonstrates a relatively high nonlinear optical response
6
;
24
. The large cubic nonlinearity in graphene, because of the conical dispersion of free electrons, was first predicted theoretically
35
and later confirmed experimentally in measurements of four-wave mixing
25
and third-harmonic generation
26
;
30
. At the same time, quadratic optical nonlinearity in graphene cannot be triggered by an external electromagnetic wave impinging normally on a graphene sheet owing to its centrosymmetric crystal structure. As a result, quadratic nonlinear optical phenomena in graphene necessitate mechanisms that break inversion symmetry
13
. Among a series of other possibilities (including curving or straining graphene
31
;
33
; applied dc current
12
;
51
;
42
;
10
;
5
; valley polarization
50
;
23
; massive graphene, which is relevant for graphene on top of a substrate
47
, and patterning graphene
16
;
34
;
18
), the breaking of inversion symmetry can be achieved in the situation of excitation by optical fields with nonzero in-plane wavevector
37
, which can happen in the case of oblique incidence
20
. Nevertheless, in this situation, the nonlinear response is proportional to the in-plane momentum itself
22
, and thus is relatively weak in the general case.

The nonlinear response of graphene can be enhanced considerably if graphene is coupled to some resonant system
27
, like an optical cavity
7
;
41
;
52
;
44
or photonic crystal
48
. One possible way to effectively increase the magnitude of the in-plane wavevector component is to couple light with surface plasmon polaritons in graphene. Surface plasmon polaritons (SPPs), being surface waves, i.e., for which the electromagnetic field decays exponentially with increasing distance from graphene, possess higher in-plane wavevectors than bulk waves
8
. Earlier it has been demonstrated that excitation of propagating plasmons
32
;
4
;
21
;
3
or localized ones
16
;
43
;
1
;
17
in graphene leads to considerable enhancement of various nonlinear optical phenomena. At the same time, in the case of quadratic nonlinearity, the inverse situation can take place
15
, where the excitation of surface plasmon polaritons in graphene from free space illumination was achieved by means of three-wave mixing.

In this paper, we consider nonlinear light-matter interactions in a graphene monolayer incorporated into an attenuated total reflectance (ATR) structure. Specifically, we consider that a graphene sheet is deposited on a semi-infinite dielectric substrate and is excited by an evanescent wave, produced by total internal reflection at the boundary between vacuum and a dielectric semi-infinite prism. The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we present particular solutions of Maxwell’s equations in different spatial domains of layered media. In Sec.
III
, we obtain an expression for the 2D nonlinear current using the Boltzmann transport equation formalism. In Sec.
IV
, we describe particular forms of boundary conditions, which, after being applied, give the nonlinear system of coupled equations describing second-harmonic generation (SHG) processes. In Sec.
V
, we demonstrate that the excitation of SPPs considerably enhances SHG efficiency. A brief conclusions section ends the central part of the paper. A set of appendices provide details of the calculations and an analysis of the best conditions for SHG in graphene.

II
Problem statement: fields in Layered Media

We consider a flat interface between two semi-infinite dielectric media, one of which (with dielectric constant
\varepsilon_{2}
) occupies a finite space
-d<z<0
, while the other (with dielectric permeability
\varepsilon_{1}
) occupies the half-space
z>0
(see schematics in Fig.
1
). A 2D graphene sheet exhibiting a finite second-harmonic response is positioned at the interface
z=0
. In the ATR configuration, a prism with dielectric constant
\varepsilon_{3}
is arranged in the half-space
z<-d
, and TM-polarized incident plane waves with angular frequency
\omega
propagate in the positive
z
-direction and fall on the prism surface with an angle of incidence
\theta>\theta_{crit}
, where
\theta_{crit}=\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\max\left(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\right)/\varepsilon_{3}}\right)
is a critical angle for total internal reflection. In this case, the TM-polarized wave has electromagnetic field components
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}_{m}}=\left(0,\mathcal{H}_{m,y},0\right)
,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}_{m}}=\left(\mathcal{E}_{m,x},0,\mathcal{E}_{m,z}\right)
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}_{m}}=\left(\mathcal{D}_{m,x},0,\mathcal{D}_{m,z}\right)
,
where
m
is the index of the medium occupied by the dielectric with relative dielectric permittivity
\varepsilon_{m}
. Since we are interested in the SHG process, we will seek the solutions of Maxwell’s equations (
42a
)–(
42c
), expanding the electromagnetic field up to the second-harmonic of the incident wave, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}_{m}\left(x,z,t\right)=\mathbf{H}_{m}^{(1)}\left(x,z\right)e^{-i\omega t}+\mathbf{H}_{m}^{(2)}\left(x,z\right)e^{-2i\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_{m}\left(x,z,t\right)=\mathbf{E}_{m}^{(1)}\left(x,z\right)e^{-i\omega t}+\mathbf{E}_{m}^{(2)}\left(x,z\right)e^{-2i\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}}_{m}\left(x,z,t\right)=\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)\mathbf{E}_{m}^{(1)}\left(x,z\right)e^{-i\omega t}+\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon\left(2\omega\right)\mathbf{E}_{m}^{(2)}\left(x,z\right)e^{-2i\omega t}+{\rm c.c.}
, where
\mathbf{H}^{(n)}\left(x,z\right)
,
\mathbf{E}^{(n)}\left(x,z\right)
are vector amplitudes of first
\left(n=1\right)
and second
\left(n=2\right)
harmonics of the electromagnetic field. The dielectric
permittivity
\varepsilon\left(\omega\right)
generally depends on the frequency. However, to simplify the model, focusing on the main phenomenon, below we consider the relative dielectric permittivity as frequency-independent.

III
Two-dimensional Nonlinear Current in Graphene

In the previous section, all dielectric media are assumed to respond linearly to electric fields. As a result, Maxwell’s equations (
42a
)–(
42c
) in these media can be solved separately for first and second harmonics. At the same time, the nonlinear optical response of the graphene layer facilitates the interaction between first and second harmonics. In what follows, the current induced in the graphene layer will be characterized as a combination of linear,
\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(L)}\left(x,t\right)
, and nonlinear,
\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(NL)}\left(x,t\right)
, currents according to

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}\left(x,t\right)=\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(L)}\left(x,t\right)+\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(NL)}\left(x,t\right).

(1)

To obtain an expression for the current in graphene, we start from the Boltzmann equation for the non-equilibrium distribution function
f\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial t}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_{\parallel}(\mathbf{r},t)\cdot\frac{\partial f\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{q})\cdot\frac{\partial f\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial\mathbf{r}}+\gamma\left[f\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)-f_{0}(\mathbf{q})\right]=0,

(2)

where
\mathbf{q}
is the quasimomentum,
e
is the elementary charge,
f_{0}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution, which for zero temperature will be written as
f_{0}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=1-\Theta\left(q-q_{F}\right)
[
\Theta\left(x\right)
being the Heaviside step function,
q=\left|\mathbf{q}\right|=\sqrt{q_{x}^{2}+q_{y}^{2}}
, and
q_{F}
is the Fermi wavevector],
\gamma
is the inverse relaxation time,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_{\parallel}(\mathbf{r},t)=\left(\mathcal{E}_{1,x}(x,z,t),0,0\right)
is the electric field component parallel to graphene [the perpendicular component is not influenced owing to the 2D motion of charge carriers in graphene]. In further considerations, we admit the Dirac cone approximation for the energy spectrum of charge carriers in monolayer graphene,
\epsilon(\mathbf{q})=\hbar v_{F}q
(with
v_{F}=10^{6}\,
m/s being the Fermi velocity). In this case, in Eq. (
III
), the group velocity of charge carriers
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{q})=\hbar^{-1}\partial\epsilon(\mathbf{q})/\partial\mathbf{q}=v_{F}\mathbf{q}/q
. Also, we consider that graphene is doped by electrons, so charge
e<0
and Fermi energy
E_{F}=\hbar v_{F}q_{F}>0
.

Assuming that in
Eq. (
III
), the electric field is considered as a small perturbation, the solution can be represented as an expansion into a series
f\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)=f_{0}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)+f_{1}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)+f_{2}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)+...
(where
f_{2}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)\ll f_{1}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)\ll f_{0}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)
, and
f_{n}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)\propto\left|\mathbf{\mathcal{E}}\right|^{n}
, as shown in Appendix
B
). Equating the terms of the same order in the electric field leads to the set of equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{n}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial t}-\frac{|e|}{\hbar}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}_{\parallel}(\mathbf{r},t)\cdot\frac{\partial f_{n-1}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{v}\cdot\frac{\partial f_{n}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial\mathbf{r}}+\gamma\left[f_{n}\left(\mathbf{r},t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)-f_{0}(\mathbf{q})\right]=0,

the solution of which can be represented as

\displaystyle f_{n}(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega)=\frac{|e|}{\hbar}\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)]

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{E}_{1,x}\left(x-v_{x}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right),0,t^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\partial f_{n-1}\left(x-v_{x}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right),t^{\prime},\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial q_{x}},

(3)

for an electric field polarized along the
x
axis. Equation (
III
) can be used as a bottom-up approach to obtain
f_{n}(x,t)
iteratively. Therefore, using the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{0}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\partial q_{x}}=-\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)\frac{q_{x}}{q},

(4)

and the expression for the electric field (
A
), the first-order correction
f_{1}(x,t)
yields

\displaystyle f_{1}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)=-\frac{|e|}{\hbar}\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{\left(n\right)}\frac{\exp\left[in(k_{x}x-\omega t)\right]}{in(k_{x}v_{x}-\omega)+\gamma}

\displaystyle\times\frac{q_{x}}{q}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)+{\rm c.c.}

(5)

For small
k_{x}v_{F}\ll\omega
(beyond the region of Landau damping),
Eq. (
III
) can be expanded into a Taylor series as

\displaystyle f_{1}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)=-\frac{|e|}{\hbar}\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{\left(n\right)}\frac{\exp\left[in(k_{x}x-\omega t)\right]}{\gamma-in\omega}

\displaystyle\times\left(1-\frac{ink_{x}v_{x}}{\gamma-in\omega}\right)\frac{q_{x}}{q}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)+{\rm c.c.}

(6)

The first-order correction defines the linear current as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(L)}\left(x,t\right)=-\frac{4\left|e\right|}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int d\mathbf{q}v_{x}f_{1}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right),

(7)

where the factor 4 comes from spin and valley degeneration. In this case, substituting Eq. (
III
) into Eq. (
7
) and integrating, we obtain the linear contribution in the electric field due to linear conductivity

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(L)}\left(x,t\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{2}\sigma_{xx}\left(n\omega\right)E_{x}^{(n)}e^{in(k_{x}x-\omega t)}{\rm c.c.}

(8)

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}\left(\omega\right)=\frac{e^{2}E_{F}}{\pi\hbar^{2}}\frac{1}{\gamma-i\omega}

is the Drude linear conductivity of graphene. The nonlinear current is defined by the correction of the second order
f_{2}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)
, which can be obtained by differentiating the first-order correction Eq. (
III
),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial q_{x}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{|e|}{\hbar}\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{\left(n\right)}\Gamma_{n}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)e^{in(k_{x}x-\omega t)}+c.c.,

(9)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=\frac{\Upsilon\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\gamma-in\omega}-ink_{x}v_{F}\frac{\Phi\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\left(\gamma-in\omega\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=\frac{q_{y}^{2}}{q^{3}}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)+\frac{q_{x}^{2}}{q^{2}}\frac{\partial\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)}{\partial q},

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=2\frac{q_{x}q_{y}^{2}}{q^{4}}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)+\frac{q_{x}^{3}}{q^{3}}\frac{\partial\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)}{\partial q}.

Substituting this result into
Eq. (
III
) we obtain

f_{2}(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega)=\sum_{n=1}^{2}f_{2}^{(n)}(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega)+{\rm c.c.}

(10)

Here

\displaystyle f_{2}^{(n)}(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega)=-\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\frac{K^{(n)}}{\gamma-in\omega}\left(1-\frac{ink_{x}v_{x}}{\gamma-in\omega}\right)e^{in(k_{x}x-\omega t)},

\displaystyle K^{(1)}=E_{1,x}^{(2)}\overline{E_{1,x}^{(1)}}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{1}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}+\Gamma_{2}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)\right),

\displaystyle K^{(2)}=\left(E_{1,x}^{(1)}\right)^{2}\Gamma_{1}\left(\mathbf{q}\right),

and hereafter an overbar stands for complex conjugation.
The nonlinear current can be calculated by integrating the second-order correction in the momentum plane (details can be found in Appendix
B
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(NL)}\left(x,t\right)=-\frac{4\left|e\right|}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int d\mathbf{q}v_{x}f_{2}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right),

(11)

and in explicit form expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(NL)}\left(x,t\right)=\sigma_{xxx}^{(21)}E_{1,x}^{(2)}\overline{E_{1,x}^{(1)}}\exp\left[ik_{x}x-i\omega t\right]

\displaystyle+\sigma_{xxx}^{(11)}\left(E_{1,x}^{(1)}\right)^{2}\exp\left[2ik_{x}x-2i\omega t\right]+{\rm c.c.},

(12)

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{xxx}^{(11)}=ik_{x}\frac{\left|e\right|^{3}v_{F}^{2}}{4\pi\hbar^{2}}\frac{1}{\gamma-i\omega}\frac{1}{\gamma-2i\omega}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma-i\omega}+\frac{4}{\gamma-i2\omega}\right)

(13)

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{xxx}^{(21)}=

\displaystyle ik_{x}\frac{\left|e\right|^{3}v_{F}^{2}}{4\pi\hbar^{2}}\frac{1}{\gamma-i\omega}\left[-\frac{1}{\left(\gamma+i\omega\right)^{2}}+\frac{2}{\gamma-i\omega}\frac{1}{\gamma+i\omega}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{2}{\left(\gamma-i2\omega\right)^{2}}+\frac{2}{\gamma-i\omega}\frac{1}{\gamma-i2\omega}\right],

(14)

are nonlinear conductivities.
Notice that both nonlinear conductivities in Eqs. (
13
) and (
III
) are proportional to the in-plane component of the wavevector
k_{x}
. In other words, these nonlinear conductivities are nonzero only for the case of oblique incidence, when the inversion symmetry is broken. The expression for the conductivity (
13
) corresponds to second-harmonic generation, and is in agreement with the results reported in Refs.
18
;
14
;
49
;
38
for a purely intraband (free electron) response at energies
\hbar\omega\lesssim E_{\rm F}
. Equation (
III
) describes a cascaded nonlinear optical process
38
, where by the generated second-harmonic field oscillating as
e^{-2i\omega t}
mixes with the counterrotating component of the fundamental field oscillating with
e^{i\omega t}
.

V
Reflectance and SHG Efficiency

In order to obtain the reflectance and transmittance coefficients, we start from the Poynting vector. In general form, the Poynting vector, averaged in time, will be written as
\boldsymbol{S}=\mathrm{Re}\left\{\boldsymbol{E}\times\overline{\boldsymbol{H}}\right\}/2
. Here,

\times
stands for the vector product. Since the direction normal to interfaces is the
z
axis, the reflectance and transmittance coefficients will be proportional to the
z
components of the Poynting vector, i.e.,

\displaystyle S_{z}=E_{x}\overline{H_{y}}/2.

(32)

For the incident wave, the respective components of the electromagnetic field can be obtained from Eq. (
A
), and the
z
component of the Poynting vector will be
S_{z,i}=E_{i}\overline{H_{i}}/2=\left|E_{i}\right|^{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{3}}/\left(2Z_{0}\cos\theta\right)
. The Poynting vector of both harmonics of the reflected wave can be obtained from the respective in-plane components of the electromagnetic field in Eq. (
A
) and represented as
S_{3,z}^{(n)}=E_{3,x}^{(n)}\overline{H_{3,y}^{(n)}}/2=-\left|E_{3,x}^{(n)}\right|^{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{3}}/\left(2Z_{0}\cos\theta\right)
. Here, the negative sign means the energy flux in the negative direction of the
z
axis, which corresponds to the backward-propagating wave. For the transmitted wave (in the semi-infinite medium with permittivity
\varepsilon_{1}
), the situation turns out to be more complicated. The respective tangential components of the electromagnetic field [see Eq. (
A
)], being substituted into the general expression for the
z
component of the Poynting vector (
32
), give

\displaystyle S_{1,z}^{(n)}=E_{1,x}^{(n)}\overline{H_{1,y}^{(n)}}/2

\displaystyle=\left|E_{1,x}^{(n)}\right|^{2}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\varepsilon_{1}/\left(2Z_{0}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{3}\sin^{2}\theta}\right)\right\}.

In the above-mentioned case of the ATR structure, since the angle of incidence
\theta
is bigger than the critical angle,
\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{3}\sin^{2}\theta}
is a purely imaginary value, and, hence,
S_{1,z}^{(n)}\equiv 0
(this property stems from the fact that the evanescent wave does not transfer energy). Nevertheless, for the case
\varepsilon_{3}<\varepsilon_{1}
(this case will also be considered further in the paper), the wave in the substrate turns out to be propagating, and
S_{1,z}^{(n)}\neq 0
.
Thus, the reflectance
R^{(1)}
and transmittance
T^{(1)}
of the first harmonics will be defined as

\displaystyle R^{(1)}=-\frac{S_{3,z}^{(1)}}{S_{z,i}},\qquad T^{(1)}=\frac{S_{1,z}^{(1)}}{S_{z,i}},

while the respective SHG efficiencies are

\displaystyle R^{(2)}=-\frac{S_{3,z}^{(2)}}{S_{z,i}},\qquad T^{(2)}=\frac{S_{1,z}^{(2)}}{S_{z,i}}.

The reflectance minimum occurs when the frequency and in-plane wavevector of the incident wave match those of the SPP eigenmode: In this case, the energy of the incident wave is effectively transferred into that of the SPP, thus reducing the intensity of the reflected wave. In the case of nonlinear graphene, the nonzero in-plane wavevector
k_{x}\neq 0
imposes quadratic nonlinearity in graphene owing to the breaking of inverse symmetry, which in its turn leads to the possibility of SHG. The respective SHG efficiency, depicted in Fig.

\sim-59\,

(c)]. For comparison, Fig.

\displaystyle D^{(n)}\left(\infty\right)=\lim_{d\to\infty}D^{(n)}\left(d\right)

VI
Bistability in SHG

The system of equations (
28
) and (
29
) that governs SHG can be rewritten in terms of the first-harmonic in-plane component of the electric field
E_{1,x}^{(1)}
as

\displaystyle\alpha E_{1,x}^{(1)}+\beta\left|E_{1,x}^{(1)}\right|^{2}E_{1,x}^{(1)}=GE_{i},

(39)

where
\alpha=D^{(1)}\left(d\right)
,
\beta=-Z_{0}^{2}\sigma_{xxx}^{(21)}\sigma_{xxx}^{(11)}/D^{(2)}\left(d\right)
. Without loss of generality,
E_{1,x}^{(1)}
can be considered as a real value (its complex phase can be made zero by choosing the proper phase of
E_{i}
). In this case, after introducing parameters
X=\left(E_{1,x}^{(1)}\right)^{2}
and
Y=\left|GE_{i}\right|^{2}
, the condition of equality of absolute values of left and right-hand parts of Eq. (
39
) gives the cubic polynomial function

\displaystyle X\left(\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime}X\right)^{2}+X\left(\alpha^{\prime\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}X\right)^{2}=Y,

(40)

where
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}
and
\alpha^{\prime\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}
are real and imaginary parts of respective parameters, such as
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+i\alpha^{\prime\prime}
,
\beta=\beta^{\prime}+i\beta^{\prime\prime}
. For a particular choice of parameters, this cubic polynomial function can exhibit two different scenarios of behavior. In the first scenario [see Figs.
5
(a) and
5
(b)], there is a domain of the incident-wave electric field
\left|E_{i}^{(\min)}\right|\leq\left|E_{i}\right|\leq\left|E_{i}^{(\max)}\right|
, where three values of the first-harmonic electric field
E_{1,x}^{(1)}
(as well as three values of SHG efficiency
R^{(2)}
) correspond to one value of
E_{i}
–this situation is known as bistability, which is typical for graphene
36
;
28
;
29
;
2
;
19
. In the second scenario, there is a strict correspondence–one value of
E_{i}
corresponds to one value of
E_{1,x}^{(1)}
(like in the upper insets of Fig.
4
).

gives two extrema (one minimum and one maximum) of function

(41)

\displaystyle\left.\pm\sqrt{\left(\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}+\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}-3\left(\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}-\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime}\right)^{2}}\right].

Thus, bistability takes place when

\displaystyle-\left(\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}+\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}\right)>\sqrt{3}\left|\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}-\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime}\right|

VII
Conclusions

To conclude, we considered theoretically SHG in the ATR scheme, consisting of the dielectric prism with graphene below it. We demonstrated that when conditions for the excitation of SPPs in the ATR scheme are met, the polariton-assisted SHG becomes considerably more efficient than in the nonresonant case. It is shown that the frequency dependence of SHG efficiency exhibits two peaks. The physical reason for this phenomenon is either resonant enhancement of the first harmonic owing to SPP excitation (with consequent more efficient SHG), or direct resonant enhancement of the second harmonics. We show that the double-resonance model can be described by the coupled-mode SPP equations. It is also demonstrated that the described ATR scheme with SHG can exhibit bistability at certain domains of parameters. The main results and theory formalism presented here can also be applied to other thin plasmonic systems that are characterized by two-dimensional optical conductivities, such as ultrathin noble metals, where plasmon-driven SHG can occur in the mid-infrared to visible spectral range
39
.

Appendix B

B.1
Solution of Boltzmann equation

To solve the Boltzmann equation (
III
), we consider the electric field as a small perturbation of order
\rho\ll 1
, i.e.,
\mathcal{E}_{1,x}\left(x,t\right)=\rho\Lambda\left(x,t\right)
, where
\Lambda\left(x,t\right)
is the scaled electric field. In this case, it is convenient to seek the solution of the Boltzmann equation in the form of a series

\displaystyle f=\psi_{0}+\rho\psi_{1}+\rho^{2}\psi_{2}

(73)

with respect to powers of the small parameter
\rho
(the zeroth term corresponds to the nonperturbed Fermi-Dirac distribution function
\psi_{0}=f_{0}
). Taking into account that for the Fermi-Dirac distribution
\partial f_{0}/\partial t=\partial f_{0}/\partial x\equiv 0
, substituting Eq. (
73
) into the Boltzmann equation (
III
), and collecting terms in front of different powers of
\rho
, we obtain the recurrence relation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi_{n}}{\partial t}+v_{x}\frac{\partial\psi_{n}}{\partial x}+\gamma\psi_{n}=\frac{\left|e\right|}{\hbar}\Lambda\left(x,t\right)\frac{\partial\psi_{n-1}}{\partial q_{x}}.

(74)

This equation can be solved by means of the Green’s function

\displaystyle\psi_{n}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt^{\prime}G\left(x-x^{\prime},t-t^{\prime}\right)

(75)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\left|e\right|}{\hbar}\Lambda\left(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}\right)\frac{\partial\psi_{n-1}\left(x^{\prime},t^{\prime},\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial q_{x}},

where the Green’s function
G\left(x,t\right)
is obtained as the solution of the equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}+v_{x}\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}+\gamma G=\delta\left(t\right)\delta\left(x\right).

(76)

Here,
\delta\left(\cdot\right)
is the Dirac delta. Defining the Fourier transform of the Green’s function as

\displaystyle g\left(\kappa,\omega\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dxdtG\left(x,t\right)\exp\left(-i\kappa x+i\omega t\right),

substituting the inverse Fourier transform

\displaystyle G\left(x,t\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega d\kappa g\left(\kappa,\omega\right)\exp\left(i\kappa x-i\omega t\right)

into Eq. (
76
), and taking into account the inverse Fourier transform of delta functions,

\displaystyle\delta\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\kappa\exp\left(i\kappa x\right),

(77)

\displaystyle\delta\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\exp\left(-i\omega t\right),

(78)

we obtain an expression for the Fourier transform of the Green’s function

\displaystyle g\left(\kappa,\omega\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\gamma-i\omega+iv_{x}\kappa},

and, as a result

\displaystyle G\left(x,t\right)=\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega d\kappa\frac{\exp\left(i\kappa x-i\omega t\right)}{\gamma-i\omega+iv_{x}\kappa}.

(79)

Figure 6:
Integration contours for Eq. (
79
) in the complex plane of frequency

.

An integral in the above expression is calculated separately for the cases
t<0
and
t>0
. For the first case,
t<0
, the integration contour should be closed in the upper half-plane of the complex frequency plane (red contour in Fig.
6
). In this situation, the integral along the arc in the upper half-plane is zero, the pole
\omega=v_{x}\kappa-i\gamma
of the integral in Eq. (
79

\displaystyle G\left(x,t\right)\equiv 0.

(80)

For the second case,
t>0
, the integration contour should be closed in the lower half-plane (green contour in Fig.
6

\displaystyle G\left(x,t\right)=-2\pi i\mathrm{Res}\left.\left[\frac{i}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\kappa\frac{\exp\left(i\kappa x-i\omega t\right)}{\omega-v_{x}\kappa+i\gamma}\right]\right|_{\omega=v_{x}\kappa-i\gamma}.

(81)

Since the pole in Eq. (
81

\displaystyle G\left(x,t\right)=\exp\left[-\gamma t\right]\delta\left(x-v_{x}t\right).

This expression, combined with Eq. (

\displaystyle G\left(x-x^{\prime},t-t^{\prime}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&\qquad t^{\prime}>t\\
\exp\left[-\gamma\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\right]\delta\left(x-x^{\prime}-v_{x}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\right),&\qquad t^{\prime}<t\end{array}\right..

This Green’s function, being substituted into Eq. (
75
), for
f_{n}=\rho^{n}\psi_{n}
gives Eq. (
III

Further, substituting the electric field [first line in Eq. (
A
)] as well as the derivative (
4

III
), we obtain an expression for the first-order correction in the form

\displaystyle f_{1}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)=-\frac{\left|e\right|}{\hbar}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)\frac{q_{x}}{q}\exp\left[-\gamma t\right]\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{(n)}\exp\left[ink_{x}\left(x-v_{x}t\right)\right]\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\exp\left[\gamma t^{\prime}+ink_{x}v_{x}t^{\prime}-in\omega t^{\prime}\right],

which after integration gives Eq. (
III
). Differentiating Eq. (
III
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)}{\partial q_{x}}=-\frac{\left|e\right|}{\hbar}\left\{\frac{d\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)}{dq}\frac{q_{x}^{2}}{q^{2}}+\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)\frac{q_{y}^{2}}{q^{3}}\right\}\times

\displaystyle\times\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{\left(n\right)}\frac{\exp\left[ink_{x}x-in\omega t\right]}{\gamma-in\omega}\left(1-\frac{ink_{x}v_{F}}{\gamma-in\omega}\frac{q_{x}}{q}\right)+{\rm c.c.}\right\}+

(83)

\displaystyle+\frac{\left|e\right|}{\hbar}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)\frac{q_{x}}{q}\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{\left(n\right)}\frac{\exp\left[ink_{x}x-in\omega t\right]ink_{x}v_{F}}{\left(\gamma-in\omega\right)^{2}}\frac{q_{y}^{2}}{q^{3}}+{\rm c.c.}\right\}.

Here, we take into account that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{x}}\left(\frac{q_{x}}{q}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{x}}\left(\frac{q_{x}}{\sqrt{q_{x}^{2}+q_{y}^{2}}}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_{x}^{2}+q_{y}^{2}}}-\frac{q_{x}^{2}}{\left(q_{x}^{2}+q_{y}^{2}\right)^{3/2}}=\frac{q_{y}^{2}}{q^{3}}.

With collecting terms in front of amplitudes
E_{1,x}^{\left(n\right)}
, Eq. (
83
) can be written in a more compact form–see Eq. (
9
). Substituting Eq. (
9
) into Eq. (
III
), we have

\displaystyle f_{2}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)=-\frac{\left|e\right|^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\exp\left[-\gamma\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\right]\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{(n)}\exp\left[ink_{x}\left(x-v_{x}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\right)-in\omega t^{\prime}\right]+{\rm c.c.}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\left\{\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{(n^{\prime})}\Gamma_{n^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)\exp\left[in^{\prime}k_{x}\left(x-v_{x}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\right)-in^{\prime}\omega t^{\prime}\right]+{\rm c.c.}\right\}.

Neglecting harmonics higher than the second ones, the above equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle f_{2}\left(x,t,\mathbf{q},\omega\right)=-\frac{\left|e\right|^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\exp\left[-\gamma t\right]\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\exp\left[\gamma t^{\prime}\right]\left\{\left|E_{1,x}^{(1)}\right|^{2}\Gamma_{1}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)+\left|E_{1,x}^{(2)}\right|^{2}\Gamma_{2}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\exp\left[ik_{x}\left(x-v_{x}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\right)-i\omega t^{\prime}\right]E_{1,x}^{(2)}\overline{E_{1,x}^{(1)}}\left(\overline{\Gamma_{1}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}+\Gamma_{2}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\left.+\exp\left[i2k_{x}\left(x-v_{x}\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)\right)-i2\omega t^{\prime}\right]\left(E_{1,x}^{(1)}\right)^{2}\Gamma_{1}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)+{\rm c.c.}\right\}.

This expression, after being integrated and expanded into a Taylor series, transforms into Eq. (
10
).

B.2
Linear and nonlinear currents

In order to calculate the linear current, we perform the integration in the momentum plane of Eq. (
7
). In this case, it is convenient to integrate in polar coordinates

\displaystyle q_{x}=q\cos\left(\varphi_{q}\right),\quad q_{x}=q\sin\left(\varphi_{q}\right).

Thus, substituting Eq. (
III
) into Eq. (
7
), and representing the result in polar coordinates, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(L)}\left(x,t\right)=\frac{4\left|e\right|^{2}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}\hbar}\intop_{0}^{\infty}qdq\intop_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi_{q}

\displaystyle\times v_{F}\cos^{2}\left(\varphi_{q}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{\left(n\right)}\frac{\exp\left[in(k_{x}x-\omega t)\right]}{\gamma-in\omega}

\displaystyle\times\left(1-\frac{ink_{x}v_{F}\cos\left(\varphi_{q}\right)}{\gamma-in\omega}\right)\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)+{\rm c.c.}

(84)

Using the properties of the delta function, the above equation can be represented as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(L)}\left(x,t\right)=\frac{4\left|e\right|^{2}v_{F}q_{F}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}\hbar}\sum_{n=1}^{2}E_{1,x}^{\left(n\right)}\frac{\exp\left[ink_{x}x-in\omega t\right]}{\gamma-in\omega}\left(\intop_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{2}\left(\varphi_{q}\right)d\varphi_{q}-\frac{ink_{x}v_{F}}{\gamma-in\omega}\intop_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{3}\left(\varphi_{q}\right)d\varphi_{q}\right)+{\rm c.c.}

(85)

In Eq. (
85
), the second integral is zero, so spatial dispersion (dependence of
k_{x}
) appears only as a correction of order
k_{x}^{2}
and higher. At the same time, integration of the first integral in Eq. (
85
) gives the final result for the linear current (
8
).

For the nonlinear current, substitution of Eq. (
10
) into Eq. (
11
), and transforming the result into polar coordinates, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(NL)}\left(x,t\right)=\frac{4\left|e\right|^{3}v_{F}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}\hbar^{2}}\intop_{0}^{\infty}qdq\intop_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi_{q}\cos\left(\varphi_{q}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{2}K^{(n)}\frac{\exp\left[in(k_{x}x-\omega t)\right]}{\gamma-in\omega}\left(1-\frac{ink_{x}v_{F}}{\gamma-in\omega}\cos\left(\varphi_{q}\right)\right).

(86)

Introducing the notation for integrals included into Eq. (
86
),

\displaystyle\eta_{ln}=\int_{0}^{\infty}qdq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi_{q}\cos\varphi_{q}\Gamma_{l}\left(1-\frac{ink_{x}v_{F}\cos\varphi_{q}}{\gamma-in\omega}\right)

(87)

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}qdq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\cos\varphi_{q}\left[\frac{\Upsilon\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\gamma-il\omega}-ilk_{x}v_{F}\frac{\Phi\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\left(\gamma-il\omega\right)^{2}}-\frac{\Upsilon\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\gamma-il\omega}\frac{ink_{x}v_{F}\cos\varphi_{q}}{\gamma-in\omega}\right],

\displaystyle\zeta_{ln}=\int_{0}^{\infty}qdq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi_{q}\cos\varphi_{q}\overline{\Gamma_{l}}\left(1-\frac{ink_{x}v_{F}\cos\varphi_{q}}{\gamma-in\omega}\right)

(88)

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}qdq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\cos\varphi_{q}\left[\frac{\Upsilon\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\gamma+il\omega}+ilk_{x}v_{F}\frac{\Phi\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\left(\gamma+il\omega\right)^{2}}-\frac{\Upsilon\left(\mathbf{q}\right)}{\gamma+il\omega}\frac{ink_{x}v_{F}\cos\varphi_{q}}{\gamma-in\omega}\right],

we can represent the nonlinear current as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{x}^{(NL)}\left(x,t\right)=\frac{\left|e\right|^{3}v_{F}}{\left(\pi\hbar\right)^{2}}\left\{\frac{\left|E_{1,x}^{(1)}\right|^{2}}{\gamma}\eta_{10}+\frac{\left|E_{1,x}^{(2)}\right|^{2}}{\gamma}\eta_{20}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+E_{1,x}^{(2)}\overline{E_{1,x}^{(1)}}\left(\zeta_{11}+\eta_{21}\right)\frac{\exp\left[ik_{x}x-i\omega t\right]}{\gamma-i\omega}+\left(E_{1,x}^{(1)}\right)^{2}\eta_{12}\frac{\exp\left[i2k_{x}x-i2\omega t\right]}{\gamma-i2\omega}\right\}+{\rm c.c.}

(89)

Taking into account that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}qdq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\cos\varphi_{q}\Upsilon\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\cos\varphi_{q}\left\{\sin^{2}\varphi_{q}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)+q\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\frac{\partial\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)}{\partial q}\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}qdq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\cos\varphi_{q}\Phi\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi_{q}\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\left\{2\sin^{2}\varphi_{q}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)+q\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\frac{\partial\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)}{\partial q}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi_{q}\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\left\{2\sin^{2}\varphi_{q}-\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\right\}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)=-\frac{\pi}{4},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}qdq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\Upsilon\left(\mathbf{q}\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\left\{\sin^{2}\varphi_{q}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)+q\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\frac{\partial\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)}{\partial q}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\left\{\sin^{2}\varphi_{q}-\cos^{2}\varphi_{q}\right\}\delta\left(q-q_{F}\right)=-\frac{\pi}{2},

Eqs. (
87
) and (
88
) can be represented as

\displaystyle\eta_{ln}=\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{ik_{x}v_{F}}{\gamma-il\omega}\left[\frac{l}{\gamma-il\omega}+\frac{2n}{\gamma-in\omega}\right],

\displaystyle\zeta_{ln}=-\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{ik_{x}v_{F}}{\gamma+il\omega}\left[\frac{l}{\gamma+il\omega}-\frac{2n}{\gamma-in\omega}\right].

Substituting these expressions into Eq. (
89
) and neglecting the zeroth harmonics, it is possible to obtain the expression for the nonlinear current in the form of Eq. (
III
).

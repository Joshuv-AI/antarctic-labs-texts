Algebra of double cosets of a symmetric group by a smaller symmetric group

Abstract




Fix a natural
\alpha

Consider the symmetric group

and

1.1. Algebras of double cosets. Preliminaries.
For a group

KgK

with respect
to
K

we denote the space of double cosets,
i.e., the quotient of

with respect to the equivalence relation

k_{1}gk_{2}\sim g,\qquad\text{where $k_{1}$, $k_{2}\in K$.}

Let a group
G
be finite. Let
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
be its group algebra, we denote
the product in
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
(the convolution) as the usual product.
We say, that a function on
G
is
K
-biinvariant

if it is constant on double cosets
KgK
.
Denote
by

{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]\subset{\mathbb{C}}[G]

the subalgebra consisting of
K
-biinvariant functions
(for more details, see, e.g.,
[
3
]
).

For
g\in G
, denote by
\delta_{g}
the element

considered as an
element of
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
. Denote

\delta_{K}=\frac{1}{\#K}\sum_{k\in K}\delta_{K},

where the symbol
\#X
denotes the number of elements of a finite set
X
.
For a double coset
\mathfrak{g}=KgK
, we define

\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}=\delta_{K}\cdot\delta_{g}\cdot\delta_{K}=\frac{1}{\#(KgK)}\sum_{q\in KgK}\delta_{q}.

Let
\rho
be an irreducible representation of
G

in a Euclidean space
H_{\rho}
. Denote by
H_{\rho}^{K}\subset H_{\rho}

the subspace of
K
-fixed vectors (it can be trivial).
Denote by
P_{\rho}^{K}
the operator
of orthogonal projection to
H_{\rho}^{K}
,

P_{\rho}^{K}=\rho(\delta_{K}).

For
g\in G
we define the operator

\widetilde{\rho}(g):H_{\rho}^{K}\to H_{\rho}^{K}

by

\widetilde{\rho}(g):=P_{\rho}^{K}\rho(g)\Bigr|_{H_{\rho}^{K}}.

It is easy to see that this operator depends only on the double
coset
\mathfrak{g}
containing
g
, so we can write
\widetilde{\rho}^{K}(\mathfrak{g})
. Decomposing

H_{\rho}=H_{\rho}\oplus(H_{\rho}^{K})^{\bot}
, we get

\rho(\delta_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho(\delta_{K})\,\rho(g)\,\rho(\delta_{K})=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\rho}^{K}(\mathfrak{g})&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

So,
\widetilde{\rho}(\mathfrak{g})
is a representation of the algebra

{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]
in
H_{\rho}^{K}
.

This easily implies that
{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]
is isomorphic
to a direct sum
of matrix algebras,

(1.1)

{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]\simeq\bigoplus_{\rho\in\widehat{G}}\operatorname{End}(H_{\rho}^{K}),

where the summation is taken over the set
\widehat{G}
of
all irreducible representations
of
G
(actually we have only summands, for which

H_{\rho}^{K}\neq 0
).
So,

\dim{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]=\sum_{\rho\in\widehat{G}}(\dim H_{\rho}^{K})^{2}.

Next, we define some additional simple structures
on algebras
{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]
.
We have a
trace
on
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
defined by

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\Bigl(\sum_{g\in G}c_{g}\delta_{g}\Bigr):=c_{1},

where
1
is the unit of the group. It is easy to see
that this trace satisfies

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\Phi\Psi)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\Psi\Phi).

Restricting the trace to
{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]

we get a trace on
{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]
,

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\Bigl(\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in K\backslash G/K}a_{\mathfrak{g}}\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}\Bigr)=\frac{1}{\#K}a_{K},

where
a_{K}
is the coefficient at
\delta_{K}
.

We define an antilinear
involution
in
{\mathbb{C}}[G]
by

\bigl(\,\sum c_{g}\delta_{g}\,\bigr)^{*}:=\sum\overline{c}_{g}\delta_{g^{-1}}.

Clearly,

(\Phi+\Psi)^{*}=\Phi^{*}+\Psi^{*},\quad(\Phi\Psi)^{*}=\Psi^{*}\Phi^{*},\quad\Phi^{**}=\Phi.

Also, we have a positive definite
inner product

\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle:=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\Phi\Psi^{*}),

Equivalently,

\bigl\langle\sum b_{g}\delta_{g},\sum c_{g}\delta_{g}\bigr\rangle=\sum_{g\in G}b_{g}\overline{c}_{g}.

So, we get the involution and the inner product on
{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]
.

Finally, we have a homomorphism
{\mathbb{C}}[G|\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

\iota\Bigl(\sum c_{g}\delta_{g}\Bigr):=\sum c_{g}.

Its restriction to
{\mathbb{C}}[K\backslash G/K]
is given by

\iota\Bigl(\sum_{\mathfrak{g}\in K\backslash G/K}a_{\mathfrak{g}}\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}\Bigr)=\sum a_{\mathfrak{g}}.

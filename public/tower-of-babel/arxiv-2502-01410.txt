Finite convergence and minimizer extraction in moment relaxations with correlative sparsity
Giovanni Fantuzzi 1, Federico Fuentes 2

Abstract
. We identify a new sufficient condition for the finite convergence of moment relaxations of polynomial optimization problems with correlative sparsity. This condition, which follows from a solution to a correlatively sparse version of the classical truncated moment problem, requires that certain moment matrices admit a flat extension and that the variable cliques underpinning the relaxation satisfy a ‘running intersection’ property. We also describe an algorithm that, when these conditions are met, extracts at least as many minimizers for the original polynomial optimization problem as the largest rank of the moment matrices in its relaxation. Our results, along with the necessity of the running intersection property, are illustrated with examples.



1.
Introduction

Moment-sum-of-squares (moment-SOS) relaxations are an established approach to bound from below the global minimum of a polynomial optimization problem (POP) by solving semidefinite programs
[
10
,
19
,
13
]
.
Raising the relaxation order to improve the lower bound rapidly increases the size of these semidefinite programs, resulting in high or outright prohibitive computational costs and memory footprint.
To avoid this, one can modify standard relaxations to exploit properties of the POP such as symmetries
[
7
,
14
,
22
,
3
]
and sparsity
[
23
,
11
,
24
,
27
,
26
,
25
,
28
,
15
]
.
A key question is whether these modified relaxations enjoy the same good theoretical properties as the standard ones.
In particular, one would like to have asymptotic convergence results for the lower bounds, sufficient conditions to detect finite convergence, and algorithms to extract one or more POP minimizers when these conditions hold.

In this work, we identify new sufficient conditions to detect the finite convergence of moment relaxations of POPs with
correlative sparsity

[
23
,
11
]
. We briefly review these relaxations next, and give a precise account of our contributions in
Section

1.2
. The relation between our results and existing ones is explained in
Section

1.3
.

1.1.
Correlatively sparse POPs and their moment relaxations

Denote the non-negative integers by
\mathbb{N}
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
positive and cover the set
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
by subsets
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}\subseteq[n]
called
cliques
. We refer to the set of cliques as the
clique cover
.
We assume without loss of generality that no clique is contained in another clique.
Given a ‘global’ vector
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}
be the ‘clique subvector’ indexed by the elements of
\Delta_{i}
.
A POP is correlatively sparse if it has the form

(POP)

f^{*}:=\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{g}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0\quad\forall i\in[m]

for polynomials
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{\left|\Delta_{i}\right|}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{g}_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{\left|\Delta_{i}\right|}\to\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}
with
N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. The inequality constraints are understood element-wise.

Fix a
relaxation order

\omega\in\mathbb{N}
with
2\omega\geq\max\{1,\deg f,\deg\mathbf{g}_{1},\ldots,\deg\mathbf{g}_{m}\}
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}
be the set of multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with degree
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\leq 2\omega
and support contained in at least one clique (see
Section

2.1
for a precise definition). We call
\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}
the set of
correlatively sparse multi-indices
subordinate to the clique cover. Let
\mathbf{f}
be the vector of coefficients of
\smash{\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}})}
with respect to the sparse monomial vector

\smash{\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}=\left(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}}
.
Finally, let
\smash{\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{y})}
and
\smash{\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{g}_{i}\mathbf{y})}
denote the usual
moment
and
localizing matrices
of order
\omega
associated with the clique
\Delta_{i}
(see
Section

2.2
for definitions).
Using angled brackets to indicate inner products, the sparse moment relaxation of
POP
of order
\omega
is

(MOM)

f_{\omega}^{*}:=\min_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}|}}\quad\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{y}\rangle\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\begin{cases}\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{y})\succeq 0&\forall i\in[m],\\
\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{g}_{i}\mathbf{y})\succeq 0&\forall i\in[m],\\
y_{\mathbf{0}}=1.\end{cases}

It is known that
\smash{f_{\omega}^{*}\leq f^{*}}
(see, e.g.,
[
23
,
11
]
). Lasserre
[
11
]
showed that
\smash{f_{\omega}^{*}\to f^{*}}
as
\omega\to\infty
if the feasible set of
POP
satisfies a suitable compactness condition and if the cliques
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
satisfy the so-called
running intersection property
:

(RIP)

\forall i>1,\quad\exists j\in[i-1]\quad\text{such that}\quad\Delta_{i}\cap(\Delta_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{i-1})\subset\Delta_{j}.

Estimates for the rate of this asymptotic convergence were established recently
[
9
]
. As we explain next, we identify sufficient rank conditions for the moment matrices, and certain submatrices thereof, that imply
\smash{f_{\omega}^{*}=f^{*}}
for finite
\omega
.

1.2.
Main results

Let

(1.1)

K:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\quad\mathbf{g}_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0\quad\forall i\in[m]\right\}

be the feasible set of
POP
.
Our first contribution is to solve a correlatively sparse version of the truncated moment problem on
K
, which asks whether the entries of a vector
\mathbf{y}=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}
are the moments of a positive Borel measure (called a
representing measure
) supported on
K
.
Specifically, let
\smash{\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{j}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})}
be the largest principal submatrix of the moment matrix
\smash{\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{i}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})}
that, up to a symmetric permutation, is also a principal submatrix of
\smash{\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{j}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})}
. For each
i\in[m]
, set

(1.2)

d_{i}=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\tfrac{1}{2}\deg\mathbf{g}_{i}\right\rceil\right\}

and let
\delta_{\mathbf{x}}
be the Dirac measure at
\mathbf{x}
.
In
Section

3
we prove the following statement.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix a nonzero vector
\mathbf{y}=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}
.
Assume that:

(1)

The cliques
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
satisfy
RIP
;

(2)

For every
i\in[m]
, there exists
j\in[i-1]
satisfying
RIP
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{y})\succeq 0,

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{g}_{i}\mathbf{y})\succeq 0,

(1.3a)

\displaystyle\parentlabel{e:rank-conditions-ff}\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{i}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{i}}^{\omega-d_{i}}(\mathbf{y}),

(1.3b)

\displaystyle\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{j}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{j}}^{\omega-1}(\mathbf{y}).

Then, there exist an integer
r\geq\max_{i\in[m]}\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{i}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})
, points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{r}
of
K
, and scalars
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}>0
such that the atomic measure
\lambda_{1}\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{r}}
represents
\mathbf{y}
. In particular,

(1.4)

\mathbf{y}=\lambda_{1}\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{1}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{r}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}.

Using this result, we can derive the sufficient condition for the finite convergence of
MOM
announced above. The proof is short, so we give it immediately.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose the cliques
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
satisfy
RIP
.
If
\mathbf{y}
is optimal for
MOM
and satisfies the rank conditions in
, then
f^{*}_{\omega}=f^{*}
and the points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{r}
in the atomic decomposition
1.4
are minimizers of
POP
.

Proof.

The conditions of
Theorem

1.1
are satisfied by assumption, so
\mathbf{y}
admits the atomic decomposition
1.4
with points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{r}\in K
and scalars
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}>0
. In particular,

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}=y_{\mathbf{0}}=1
. Then, since
f(\mathbf{x})=\langle\mathbf{f},\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}\rangle
and since the points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{r}
are feasible for
POP
, we obtain

(1.5)

f^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}f^{*}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\langle\mathbf{f},\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}\rangle\overset{{\text{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{e:atomic-y}}}}{=}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{y}\rangle=f_{\omega}^{*}\leq f^{*}.

All inequalities must clearly be equalities, giving
f_{\omega}^{*}=f^{*}
. Since
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}>0
, we must also have
f(\mathbf{x}_{1})=\cdots=f(\mathbf{x}_{r})=f^{*}
, so
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{r}
are optimal for
POP
.
∎

Before discussing how
Theorem

1.1
and
Corollary

1.2
relate to existing results in the literature, some technical remarks are in order.
Firstly, as demonstrated in
Section

5.3
and by
[
18
, Example 3.4]
, it is not possible to drop
RIP
without additional assumptions.
Secondly, in some cases when
RIP
does not hold, the cliques can be reordered so that it does. Our notation
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
always refers to the reordered cliques and we say that
RIP
fails if there is no clique reordering for which it holds.
Thirdly, while the asymptotic convergence result of
[
11
]
requires
K
to be compact,
Theorem

1.1
and
Corollary

1.2
apply also in the noncompact case.
Finally, in contrast to the classical
K
-moment problem (see
[
5
,
13
]
for reviews of the subject), the representing measure in
Theorem

1.1
is far from unique. For example, with
n=3
and cliques
\Delta_{1}=\{1,2\}
and
\Delta_{2}=\{2,3\}
, the measures

(1.6)

\mu_{\lambda}=\lambda\,\delta_{(1,0,1)}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda\right)\delta_{(1,0,-1)}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda\right)\delta_{(-1,0,1)}+\lambda\delta_{(-1,0,-1)}

generate the same correlatively sparse moment vector
(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}
for every
\smash{\lambda\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]}
irrespective of
\omega\in\mathbb{N}
.
This lack of uniqueness arises because a correlatively sparse moment sequence only determines the
\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}
-marginals of a representing measure, which in general do not uniquely characterize the representing measure itself.

1.3.
Relation to previous work

The conditions for the finite convergence of
MOM
in
Corollary

1.2
generalize those in
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
, which restricts the matrix ranks in
to coincide and asks for
1.3b
to hold for all pairs of intersecting cliques.
Sections

5.1
and
5.2
give two examples demonstrating that dropping these unnecessary requirements is important to detect finite convergence in practice.
Our result differs also from
[
11
, Theorem 3.7]
, which does not assume
RIP
but strengthens
1.3b
by imposing that
\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{j}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})=1
for every pair of intersecting cliques. This is a strong restriction because it requires the vectors
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{r}
to have the same entries in positions indexed by clique intersections. In our experience, this is rarely true in practice.
On the other hand, we are not aware of any sufficient conditions that allow for weaker rank conditions without requiring the running intersection property
RIP
.

To the best of our knowledge, we are also the first to explicitly solve the truncated correlatively sparse
K
-moment problem. This is a result of independent interest beyond its role in sparse moment-SOS relaxations. In fact, as we now explain, a secondary contribution of our work is to recognize that prior convergence analyses of
MOM
implicitly rely on results similar to our
Theorem

1.1
, which however are not stated explicitly. For example,
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
follows from our
Theorem

1.1
if one requires the matrix ranks in
to coincide. Likewise, Lasserre’s proof of the asymptotic convergence of
MOM

[
11
]
tacitly uses the following solution to the
full
correlatively sparse
K
-moment problem, where the finite vector
\mathbf{y}=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}
is replaced by the infinite sequence
\mathbf{y}=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}
. (We attribute the statement to Lasserre even though it does not appear in
[
11
]
because we conclude it from his arguments.)

Theorem 1.3

(Lasserre)
.

Fix a nonzero sequence
\mathbf{y}=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}
. Assume that:

(1)

The cliques
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
satisfy
RIP
;

(2)

For every
\omega\in\mathbb{N}
and every
i\in[m]
,
\mathbf{y}
satisfies
\smash{\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{y})}\succeq 0
and
\smash{\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{g}_{i}\mathbf{y})}\succeq 0
;

(3)

For every
i\in[m]
, there exist an integer
R_{i}
and sums-of-squares polynomials
\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{N_{i}}:\mathbb{R}^{\left|\Delta_{i}\right|}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma_{0}(\mathbf{z})+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}g_{i,j}(\mathbf{z})\sigma_{j}(\mathbf{z})=R_{i}^{2}-|\mathbf{z}|^{2}
.

Then,
\mathbf{y}
has a representing measure supported on
K
.

Separating the solution of sparse moment problems from their application to moment-SOS relaxations also helps us clarify their proofs. For example, to prove
Theorem

1.3
, one first applies to each clique
\Delta_{i}
a measure-theoretic version of Putinar’s Positivstellensatz
[
20
, Lemma 4.1]
, which states that the ‘clique subsequence’ of
\mathbf{y}
indexed by the multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
supported on
\Delta_{i}
has a unique representing measure
\mu_{\Delta_{i}}
when assumptions
2
and
3
hold. Then, one verifies that the measures
\mu_{\Delta_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{\Delta_{m}}
have consistent marginals on the clique intersections. Finally, one assembles these consistent ‘local’ measures into the ‘global’ representing measure
\mu
through a technical construction based on
RIP

[
11
, Lemma 6.4]
.

Our
Theorem

1.1
can be proven following the same strategy because, as we will show, its assumptions guarantee that the ‘clique subvectors’ of
\mathbf{y}=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}
have representing measures
\mu_{\Delta_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{\Delta_{m}}
with consistent marginals.
Specifically, conditions
1.3
and
1.3a
enable a
flat extension
of the moment matrices for each clique and ensure the existence of
finitely atomic
representing measures
\mu_{\Delta_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{\Delta_{m}}
for the clique subvectors (see
[
4
,
5
]
, as well as
[
13
]
for an introductory exposition). Condition
1.3b
, instead, ensures that these measures have consistent marginals.
We provide the technical details in
Section

3
.

In
Section

4
, we give an elementary and explicit version of Lasserre’s measure assembly procedure for our atomic setting. This contribution is important for two reasons. First, it results into a new, simple algorithm for recovering the ‘atomic decomposition’
1.4
in practice (see
Algorithm

1
). This broadens the class of POPs for which minimizers can be extracted algorithmically. Second, our explicit construction enables us to prove that this algorithm returns an atomic representing measure with
maximal support
(in the language of
[
6
,
2
]
, it constructs the
core variety
of the Riesz functional associated with
\mathbf{y}=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}
). In the context of moment-SOS relaxations of POPs, this means our algorithm extracts
all
POP minimizers encoded by the solution of
MOM
.
As we explain, however, ‘sparser’ representing measures can also be obtained if desired, using convex programming.

Finally, let us mention that many of the sufficient conditions to detect the finite convergence of
MOM
from
[
11
,
18
]
were recently generalized to sparse polynomial matrix optimization problems
[
16
]
. We expect analogues of
Theorem

1.1
and
Corollary

1.2
to hold in this setting, too, but leave the problem to future work.

2.
Preliminaries

Before proving
Theorem

1.1
, we define more precisely the notion of correlatively sparse multi-indices and monomials already introduced above. We also recall the definition of the moment and localizing matrices appearing in
Theorem

1.1
, and we introduce some ‘clique projection’ operators that will be useful in its proof.

2.1.
Correlatively sparse multi-indices and monomials

We say that a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is supported on a clique
\Delta_{i}
, written
\supp\alpha\subseteq\Delta_{i}
, if
\alpha_{j}=0
for all indices
j\notin\Delta_{i}
. For every
\omega\in\mathbb{N}
, the subset of
correlatively sparse
multi-indices of degree up to
2\omega
subordinate to the clique cover
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
is

(2.1)

\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}:={\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{m}}\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:\;\supp\alpha\subseteq\Delta_{i},\quad\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\leq 2\omega\right\}.

We write
\smash{\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}=\left(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}}
for the vector of correlatively sparse monomials.

2.2.
Moment and localizing matrices

Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{N}
.
The
Riesz functional
associated with a vector
\mathbf{y}=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}}
, denoted by
{L}_{\mathbf{y}}
, is the unique continuous linear functional on the polynomial space spanned by the correlatively sparse monomials
\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}
that satisfies

{L}_{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})=y_{\alpha}
for every exponent
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}
.

For every
i\in[m]
, recall that
\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}
is the subvector of
\mathbf{x}
indexed by clique
\Delta_{i}
. We list the full set of monomials with indeterminate
\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}
and total degree up to
d
in the vector

(2.2)

\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}\right\rrbracket_{d}:=\left(\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}^{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{|\Delta_{i}|}_{d}}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbb{N}^{|\Delta_{i}|}_{d}:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{|\Delta_{i}|}:\;\sum_{j=1}^{|\Delta_{i}|}\alpha_{j}\leq d\right\}.

The moment matrix of order
d\leq\omega
associated with clique
\Delta_{i}
is then defined as

(2.3)

\mathbb{M}^{d}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{y}):={L}_{\mathbf{y}}\bigg(\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}\right\rrbracket_{d}\otimes\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}\right\rrbracket_{d}\bigg),

where
\otimes
denotes the usual tensor product.
With
d_{i}
defined as in
1.2
, instead, the localizing matrix of order
d\in[d_{i},\omega]
associated with the clique
\Delta_{i}
and the entry
g_{i,j}
of the polynomial vector
\mathbf{g}_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{|\Delta_{i}|}\to\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}
is

(2.4)

\mathbb{M}^{d}_{\Delta_{i}}(g_{i,j}\mathbf{y}):={L}_{\mathbf{y}}\bigg(g_{i,j}(\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}})\,\big(\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}\right\rrbracket_{d-d_{i}}\otimes\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}\right\rrbracket_{d-d_{i}}\big)\bigg).

The localizing matrix associated with the polynomial vector
\mathbf{g}_{i}
is the block-diagonal matrix

(2.5)

\mathbb{M}^{d}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{g}_{i}\mathbf{y})=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{M}^{d}_{\Delta_{i}}(g_{i,1}\mathbf{y})\\
&\ddots\\
&&\mathbb{M}^{d}_{\Delta_{i}}(g_{i,N_{i}}\mathbf{y})\end{bmatrix}.

Note that, for fixed
i\in[m]
, the moment and localizing matrices depend not on the full vector
\mathbf{y}
, but only on (a subset of) the entries of the subvector

(2.6)

\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{i}}:={L}_{\mathbf{y}}\left(\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}\right\rrbracket_{2\omega}\right)\,.

These subvectors list precisely the entries
y_{\alpha}
indexed by multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}
supported on the clique
\Delta_{i}
.

2.3.
Clique projections and pushforwards

For any index set
\Delta\subseteq[n]
, denote by
\mathbf{x}_{\Delta}
the subvector of
\mathbf{x}
indexed by the elements of
\Delta
. Let

\smash{P_{\Delta}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{|\Delta|}}

be the projection operator satisfying

P_{\Delta}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\Delta}

and denote its pseudoinverse by

\smash{P_{\Delta}^{\dagger}:\mathbb{R}^{|\Delta|}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.
We can identify
P_{\Delta}
with the
|\Delta|\times n
matrix listing the rows of the
n\times n
identity matrix indexed by
\Delta
, and
\smash{P_{\Delta}^{\dagger}}
with its transpose. Note that
\smash{P_{\Delta}^{\dagger}\mathbf{z}}
simply ‘lifts’ a vector
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\Delta|}
into a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by placing
\mathbf{z}
in the entries indexed by
\Delta
and padding the rest with zeros.

3.1.
The vectors
\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{i}}
are not zero

We begin with a useful observation about a general
dense
moment matrix

(3.1)

\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}}):={L}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}\left(\left\llbracket\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right\rrbracket_{\omega}\otimes\left\llbracket\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right\rrbracket_{\omega}\right),

defined here for general vectors
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{n}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\binom{\tilde{n}+2\omega}{\tilde{n}}}
. Later, we will apply this observation to the clique moment matrices
\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{i}})=\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{y})
, which are obtained with
\tilde{n}=|\Delta_{i}|
,
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}_{\Delta_{i}}
, and
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}=\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{i}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix integers
\omega\geq d\geq 1
. Suppose
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\binom{\tilde{n}+2\omega}{\tilde{n}}}

satisfies

\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})\succeq 0
,

\rank\mathbb{M}^{\omega-d}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})=\rank\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
, and

{L}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}(1)=0
.
Then
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}={0}
.

Proof.

If
\omega=d
, then
\rank\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})=\rank\mathbb{M}^{0}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})={L}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}(1)=0
, which is true if and only if
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}=0
by the definition of
\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
.

Next, suppose
\omega>d
. For every
t\in\{0,\ldots,\omega\}
, the moment matrix
\mathbb{M}^{t}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
is a principal submatrix of
\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
. We claim that

(3.2)

\mathbb{M}^{t}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})={0}\quad\implies\quad\mathbb{M}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{2}(t+\omega)\rfloor}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})={0}.

Indeed, since
\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
is positive semidefinite and
\mathbb{M}^{t}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
is one of its principal submatrices,

\mathbb{M}^{t}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})={0}
implies that all rows of
\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
indexed by the monomials in
\left\llbracket\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right\rrbracket_{t}
must vanish, that is,

(3.3)

{L}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}\left(\left\llbracket\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right\rrbracket_{t}\otimes\left\llbracket\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right\rrbracket_{\omega}\right)={0}.

This is equivalent to
{L}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}(\left\llbracket\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\right\rrbracket_{t+\omega})={0}
. Since the matrix
\mathbb{M}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{2}(t+\omega)\rfloor}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
depends only on (a subset of) this vector, it is the zero matrix.

Now,
\mathbb{M}^{0}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})={L}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}(1)=0
by assumption. Since
\omega>d\geq 1
by assumption, for all
t\in\{0,\ldots,\omega-d-1\}
we have that
\lfloor\frac{1}{2}(t+\omega)\rfloor\geq t+1
. Then, we can repeatedly apply
3.2
to conclude that
\mathbb{M}^{\omega-d}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})={0}
. Since
\rank\mathbb{M}^{\omega-d}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})=\rank\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})
by assumption, we conclude that
\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\tilde{\mathbf{y}})={0}
. Again, this holds if and only if
\tilde{\mathbf{y}}={0}
.
∎

The next result implies that if
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}|}
is nonzero and satisfies the rank conditions in
, then all subvectors
\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{i}}
are nonzero.

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{E}_{2\omega}|}
satisfy the rank conditions in
. There exists
i\in[m]
such that
\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{i}}={0}
if and only if
\mathbf{y}={0}
.

Proof.

The ‘if’ part is obvious. For the converse, pick
i\in[m]
such that
\smash{\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{i}}={0}}
. The matrix
\smash{\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{i}}(\mathbf{y})=\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{i}})}
must then be zero and, since
{L}_{\mathbf{y}}(1)
is one of its diagonal entries, we conclude that
{L}_{\mathbf{y}}(1)=0
.

Next, for each
j\in[m]
the vector
\smash{\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{j}}}
satisfies
\smash{{L}_{\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{j}}}(1)={L}_{\mathbf{y}}(1)=0}
. Using
, the dense moment matrix
\smash{\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{j}})=\mathbb{M}^{\omega}_{\Delta_{j}}(\mathbf{y})}
is positive semidefinite and satisfies
\smash{\rank\mathbb{M}^{\omega-d_{j}}(\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{j}})=\rank\mathbb{M}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{j}})}
. We then conclude from
Lemma

3.1
that
\mathbf{y}_{\Delta_{j}}={0}
for every
j\in[m]
, which in turn implies
\mathbf{y}={0}
.
∎

of the atoms of

is therefore proved.

4.3.
Finding other atomic representing measures

Atomic representing measures with the largest possible support are desirable in the context of moment relaxations of a POP because they allow one to maximize the number of distinct minimizers for the POP that one recovers. Other times, however, one may want to find atomic representing measures that are optimal with respect to different criteria.

Alternatives to the maximal representing measure
\mu
constructed in
Section

4.1
, when they exist, can be easily found using computational tools for convex optimization.
Specifically, let
\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{r}
be the (known) atoms of
\mu
and fix any convex lower-semicontinuous function
c:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}
.
One can then solve the convex program

(4.13)

\displaystyle\min_{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}}

\displaystyle c(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r})

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{1}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}+\cdots+\gamma_{r}\left\llbracket\mathbf{x}_{r}\right\rrbracket^{\rm sp}_{2\omega}=\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}\geq 0

and obtain an atomic representing measure
\gamma_{1}\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{1}}+\cdots+\gamma_{r}\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{r}}
for
\mathbf{y}
. Note that the strict feasibility of this problem follows from the construction of
Section

4.1
. Note also that the feasible set is compact because the constraints include the equation
\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{r}=y_{{0}}
, so the minimum is attained.

It is clear that varying
c
should lead to different representing measures in general, although it may not be easy to design a cost function to single out a representing measure with desired properties (e.g., with the minimum number of atoms). Representing measures that are
extreme
in the sense of convex analysis, however, can be found simply by taking
c
to be a linear cost function. In fact, in this case
4.13
is a standard-form linear program and an extreme optimal point, corresponding to an extreme atomic representing measure, can be found with the simplex method. Since extreme solutions to linear programs are often sparse, this approach can help one find atomic representing measures with small support.

5.2.2.
Detecting finite convergence

Using the moment matrices reported above, one can check that the rank conditions in

and

(5.13a)

\displaystyle\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{1}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})=\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{1}}^{\omega-d_{1}}(\mathbf{y})=4,

(5.13b)

\displaystyle\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{2}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})=\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{2}}^{\omega-d_{2}}(\mathbf{y})=2,

(5.13c)

\displaystyle\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{3}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})=\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{3}}^{\omega-d_{3}}(\mathbf{y})=2,

(5.13d)

\displaystyle\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}}^{\omega}(\mathbf{y})=\rank\mathbb{M}_{\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}}^{\omega-1}(\mathbf{y})=2,

that the sparse moment relaxation for

(the overlap matrix

5.2.4.
Further comments

We conclude with a remarkable observation: by exploiting correlative sparsity and using
Corollary

1.2
, we not only detect finite convergence and recover minimizers for the POP
5.9
, but also do so with a relaxation order smaller than that required by a dense (i.e., single-clique) moment relaxation. Indeed, by
[
1
, Theorems 3.18(iii) and 3.19]
, the dense relaxation of
5.9
can be exact at relaxation order
\omega
only if, for each of the minimizers
\mathbf{x}^{*}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{*}_{8}
, there exists an ‘interpolator polynomial’
p_{i}
of degree
\omega-1
that satisfies
p_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{i})=1
and vanishes at all other minimizers. For our example, this requires
\omega\geq 4
.

The same reasoning, of course, can be used in general to conclude that the sparse moment relaxation cannot be exact unless the projections of the POP minimizers on each clique
\Delta_{i}
have interpolator polynomials of degree
\omega-d_{i}
. However, this is often a much weaker requirement because the number of unique projections is never larger than the number of minimizers, and is often much smaller in practice. In our example, for instance, interpolator polynomials of the right degree for all clique projections of the minimizers
\mathbf{x}^{*}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{*}_{8}
exist as soon as
\omega\geq 3
.
Based on these observations, we are led to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.1
.

If the sparse moment relaxation
MOM
exhibits finite convergence, then it does so at a relaxation order no larger than the relaxation order at which the dense relaxation is exact.

The arguments in
[
1
]
, however, are not enough to prove this conjecture: while they give conditions under which one can expect a flat extension for the clique moment matrices in
1.3a
, they do not account for the additional ‘overlap’ conditions in
1.3b
. We leave proving or disproving
Conjecture

5.1
to future work.

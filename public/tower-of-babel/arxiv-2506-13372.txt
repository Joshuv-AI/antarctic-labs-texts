Tunneling conductance in superconducting junctions with p p -wave unconventional magnets breaking time-reversal symmetry
Yuri Fukaya, Keiji Yada, Yukio Tanaka

Abstract
A new type of magnet called p p -wave unconventional magnet is proposed, stimulated by the discovery of altermagnet. We study the tunneling conductance of p p -wave unconventional magnet/superconductor junctions by adopting the effective Hamiltonian of p p -wave unconventional magnets with time-reversal symmetry breaking, suggested in Ref [arXiv: 2309.01607 (2024)]. The tunneling conductance shows an asymmetric behavior with respect to bias voltage in the helical p p -wave superconductor junctions. It is caused by the lack of helical edge states contributing to the charge conductance owing to the momentum-dependent spin-split feature of the Fermi surface in p p -wave unconventional magnets. In chiral d d and p p -wave superconductor junctions, the resulting spin-resolved tunneling conductance takes a different value for spin sectors due to the time-reversal symmetry breaking in superconductors. Our results qualitatively reproduce the results based on the simplified Hamiltonian in Ref [J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 93 , 114703 (2024)], where only the odd function of the exchange coupling of p p -wave unconventional magnets is taken into account, which gives the shift of the Fermi surface and preserves the time-reversal symmetry similar to the spin-orbit coupling.

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied the tunneling conductance in superconducting junctions with PUMs
using the effective PUM model in Ref.
Hellenes et al. 2024
.
In spin-singlet and spin-triplet
p_{x}
and
p_{y}
-wave SC junctions, the line shape of the tunneling conductance is not sensitive to the PUM order, even though the range of
k_{x}
contributing to the spin-resolved conductance is restricted.
The tunneling conductance in the helical
p
-wave SC junction becomes asymmetric with respect to
eV
due to the lack of helical edge states contributing to the conductance.
It is caused by the spin-split feature of the Fermi surface in PUMs.
In chiral
d
and chiral
p
-wave SC junctions, we found that the tunneling conductance is sensitive to the direction of the injected spin because of the time reversal symmetry breaking in SCs.

We have clarified that our obtained results based on the effective model Hamiltonian of PUM with the time-reversal symmetry breaking in PUM/SC junctions are qualitatively similar to those in Ref.
Maeda et al. 2024
, where the exchange coupling appearing in the Hamiltonian for PUMs is described by the odd function of Fermi momentum
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{PUM}}\propto k
preserving the time-reversal symmetry.
Likewise, we expect a similar behavior of the tunneling conductance in PUM/SC junctions along the
x
-direction to Ref.
Maeda et al. 2024
.
In the present calculation, we chose the chemical potential as
\mu_{p}=-3.85t
and the Fermi surface shown in Fig.
1
(b) for PUMs.
We confirmed that our results are generic by changing the chemical potential of PUMs and SCs.
Hence, it indicates that the configuration of the electronic structure described by the simplified Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{PUM}}\propto k_{x},k_{y}

Maeda et al. 2024
;
Brekke et al. 2024
does not qualitatively deviate from that of the effective model with time-reversal symmetry breaking proposed in Ref.
Hellenes et al. 2024
, within the calculations of the tunneling conductance.

For future perspective, the Josephson junctions with PUM without the time-reversal symmetry need to be discussed.
In Ref.
Fukaya et al. 2025b
, the Josephson current is calculated by using the simplified PUM Hamiltonian
with time reversal symmetry
Maeda et al. 2024
.
Indeed, the role of the time-reversal symmetry breaking of PUM should also be clarified in Josephson junctions with PUMs.
Also, the analysis of the pair amplitude, including the odd-frequency pairing

Tanaka et al. 2012
is needed beyond previous studies
based on the simplified Hamiltonian
Fukaya et al. 2025b
;
Maeda et al. 2025
.

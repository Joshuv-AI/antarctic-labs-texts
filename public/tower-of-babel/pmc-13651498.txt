Design of a novel multi-epitope monovalent vaccine against Hantaan virus using an immunoinformatics approach
Authors: Tahera Lasker, Foeaz Ahmed, Md Shariful Islam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHantaan virus is a well-known causative agent of Hemorrhagic Fever with Renal Syndrome (HFRS) which is distributed broadly in the Eastern Asia, particularly in Korea, Russia, and China. Despite its significance, no FDA-approved vaccine currently exists for this virus. Therefore, this study aimed to design a novel multi-epitope monovalent vaccine against Hantaan virus utilizing an immunoinformatics approach, targeting the envelopment polyprotein and nucleoprotein. To develop potential candidate vaccines 6 CTL, 6 HTL, and 12 BCL epitopes were combined with suitable adjuvants, linkers and a PADRE sequence. V1 construct (MW: 39.2 kDa) is the best in length (373 aa), antigenic score (0.8426), and solubility (0.718) and it exhibits basic characteristics (Theoretical pI: 9.28), hydrophilic nature (GRAVY: -0.412), thermostability (Aliphatic index: 70.56), and significant stability (Instability index: 29.60). The secondary structure showed 20.38% alpha helix, 28.42% β sheet, and 44.24% random coil and the refined structure confirmed the validity. The vaccine could strongly interact with numerous MHC molecules and immune receptors of human, including DAF/CD55, integrins β3, gC1qR, and protocadherin-1 (PCDH1) and exhibited minimal deformability. The optimized CAI value was 0.986 having 50.491% GC content. Finally, the vaccine was inserted into the pET30a (+) vector for expression in E. coli strain K12 to assess microbial expression as well as translational potency. However, in vitro experiments using model organisms is essential for experimental validation.Keywords: Hantaan virus, Monovalent vaccine, Envelope polyprotein, Nucleoprotein, Immunoinformatics, In Silico cloning

Introduction

Hantaan virus (HTNV) is an emerging pathogen belongs to the Hantaviridae family of Bunyavirales order which possess a single-stranded, negative-sense RNA genome with three segments entitled large (L), medium (M), and small (S).1, 2, 3 S segment contains about 1700-2100 nucleotides (nt), where M and L segments contain 3600-3700 nt and ∼6500 nt, respectively.4, 5 HTNV, also belongs to “Old World Hantaviruses”, is the causative agent of a zoonotic disease termed as Hemorrhagic Fever with Renal Syndrome (HFRS) in humans.6 During the Korean conflict (1950-53), 3200 cases of HFRS with unknown pathogen among United Nations soldiers were reported and the outbreak gained worldwide attention.7 In 1978, the etiologic agent of HFRS was first isolated from blood samples of hemorrhagic fever patients8 and named as Hantaan virus, after the Hantan River of South Korea.9

Global estimates of Hantavirus infection cases are as many as 200,000 annually, of which more than 50% cases are HFRS.10 Ninety percent (90%) of the global cases of HFRS are reported in China, primarily infected with HTNV strain.11 The frequent outbreaks of Hantavirus disease have posed a widespread danger to human fitness in recent years. An individual from Yunnan Province in China died of Hantavirus in the course of COVID-19 pandemic in March 2020, resulting panic in China and around the world.12 Geographic distribution of HTNV is not restricted to China; it also spans South Korea and Russia.13 HTNV strain 76-118 had been isolated from Apodemus agrarius (striped field mice) in 1981.14 Virus transmission to humans occurs most commonly through the inhalation of virus contaminated aerosols or coming into contact with bodily fluids such as urine, blood, saliva and feces from infected rodents.3, 15 Besides, climatic conditions (temperature and rainfall) can affect rodent growth and virus transmission and subsequently drive the outbreaks of HTNV infection over period of time.16 HFRS is clinically characterized by fever, hemorrhage, kidney disease, headache, back pain, abdominal pain and hypotension,17, 18 and the clinical course of HFRS is classified into five phases such as febrile, hypotensive, oliguric, diuretic and convalescent.17, 19

As of now, there are no treatments approved by the US FDA, including antivirals, vaccines or immunotherapeutics, are available for HFRS.3 Only Supportive therapies are applied for the treatment of HFRS caused by HTNV infection.20 Consequently, the development of potential therapeutic agents targeting HTNV is a top priority for global public health. In absence of effective treatments, vaccination could be the strategy of preventing viral infection. Currently, there are many types of vaccines such as inactivated vaccine, DNA vaccine, subunit vaccine, virus-like particle vaccine against HTNV are in pre-clinical and clinical stages of development.21 In South Korea, the first inactivated HTNV vaccine, known as Hantavax®, was developed in 1988 and has been widely used for many years.22 However, it was found that Hantavax® efficacy against HFRS progress is statistically insignificant.23 In China, inactivated HFRS vaccine has also been used for several years.24 But the immunogenicity of such vaccine is limited due to poor induction of antibody and cell-mediated immunity.25 Thus, it is imperative to develop more effective and safe vaccine against HTNV causing HFRS.

Epitope-based vaccines design is a powerful strategy to elicit immune response against the targeted epitopes, offering increased safety and specificity as key advantages.26 Both structural proteins, the envelop glycoprotein (GP) and nucleocapsid protein (NP) are responsible for the antigenicity properties of Hantaviruses.27 Therefore, a potent epitope-based peptide vaccine design based on HTNV structural proteins could be a promising strategy for effective control of HFRS. Several genome-based methods have facilitated the selection of functional vaccination candidates and antigen prediction without the need to generate the pathogen in laboratory.28, 29 In this research endeavor, we applied immunoinformatics approaches to design a highly immunogenic and thermostable multi-epitope monovalent vaccine against Hantaan virus utilizing the envelop polyprotein (GP) and nucleoprotein (NP).

Materials and methods

Here, we employed immunoinformatics approaches for the development of multi-epitope monovalent vaccine against HTNV. The schematic representation of the workflow followed in this research work is depicted in Fig. 1.

An overview of multi-epitope monovalent vaccine construction strategy.

Selection of viral strain, protein sequences retrieval and analysis

HTNV strain 76-118 was chosen from ViralZone (http://viralzone.expasy.org/), a Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics online tool. This platform was used to access all the related information on the virus including its class, family, host, route of transmission, associated diseases, genome structure, and proteome. The entire protein repertoire of HTNV strain 76-118 was retrieved from UniProtKB (https://www.uniprot.org/uniprot/?query=X55901+or+M14627&sort=score). This collection comprises six proteins, consisting of three reviewed and three unreviewed entries. The immune system’s ability to recognize the antigens is termed as antigenicity. The VaxiJen v2.0 server (http://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/) was used to determine the antigenicity of the proteins.30 The antigenic score helped to choose the strongest structural proteins from the whole proteome. For the prediction of various physicochemical characteristics of identified proteins, ProtParam (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/) was employed.31

Selection of T-cell epitopes

MHC-I (http://tools.iedb.org/mhci/) and MHC-II (http://tools.iedb.org/mhcii/) predicting tools from Immune Epitope Database (IEDB) had been used to predict the binding potential of epitopes as MHC-I bounded CD8+ Cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) and MHC-II bounded CD4+ CTLs play important roles in controlling viral contamination.32 Therefore, the polypeptide sequences of the envelope polyprotein and nucleoprotein were independently submitted to those prediction tools to select the best T cell epitope candidates.

Antigenic score and transmembrane topology for screening of T cell epitopes

VaxiJen v2.0 server (http://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/)30 was used to verify the antigenic score of the epitopes and TMHMM server (http://www.cbs.dtu.dk/services/TMHMM/)33 was used to predict the transmembrane topology of the targeted proteins. Epitopes with highest antigenic score were chosen for further analysis.

Analysis of allergenicity, toxicity and conservancy of T-cell epitopes

AllergenFP,34 AllerTOP,35 AlgPred v.2.0,36 and Allermatch37 were used to analyze the allergenic pattern of the identified epitopes and ToxinPred server (http://crdd.osdd.net/raghava/toxinpred/)38 was used to assess the toxicity pattern of selected non-allergenic epitopes. Moreover, epitope conservancy analysis was conducted by IEDB’s epitope conservancy analysis tool (http://tools.iedb.org/conservancy/) which is a crucial step to determine the efficacy of candidate epitopes that can induce broad spectrum immunity.

Analysis of population coverage

As HLA genes distribution vary geographically, population coverage must be considered in rational design of vaccine candidates to ensure broad effectiveness. So, IEDB population coverage tool (http://tools.iedb.org/population) was employed to estimate the population coverage of each of the single individual epitope.39 Based on HLA composition frequencies and MHC binding efficiency, we estimated how many people are likely to respond to the predicted epitope set.40

Cluster analysis of MHC restricted alleles

To classify the MHC protein super-families with similar binding specificities, a structure-based clustering approach was applied. For this, the Cluster v2.0 server had been used to generate graphical tree-based visualizations and heat-maps, presenting the functional relationship among different MHC variants.41

Selection of B-cell epitopes

B-cell epitopes selection is essential for identifying the prospective peptides capable of binding with B lymphocytes and triggering immune responses. As potential epitopes are associated with antigenicity, flexibility, and surface accessibility, the IEDB database and six different algorithms, including the Kolaskar and Tongaonkar antigenicity scale,42 Emini surface accessibility prediction,43 Karplus and Schulz flexibility prediction,44 Bepipred linear epitope prediction analysis,45 Chou and Fasman beta-turn prediction46 and Parker hydrophilicity prediction,47 were calculated to identify the most promising B cell epitopes.

Construction of vaccines

Subunit vaccines are characterized by having an antigenic part of a pathogen capable of eliciting the protective immunity within the host. To generate the ultimate vaccine construct, anticipated epitopes were combined in a systematic fashion. There was an adjuvant for each of the vaccine proteins which were followed by the strongest epitopes of CTL, HTL and BCL with linkers. V1, V2, and V3 were projected vaccine sequences having three different adjuvants, i.e., beta defensin (a 45 mer peptide), L7/L12 ribosomal protein and HABA protein (M. tuberculosis, accession number: AGV15514.1), respectively. Strong immunoreactions and polarization of CTL responses might be triggered by interaction of adjuvants with Toll-like receptors (TLRs).48 TLR1, TLR2, and TLR4 receptors will be agonisted by beta defensin adjuvant; however, TLR4 will only be agonisted by HABA protein and L7/L12 ribosomal protein. Joining PADRE sequence with adjuvant peptides might diminish the problems presented by extremely polymorphic HLA alleles. Linker, EAAAK, was accustomed to a part of the adjuvant and CTL epitopes. Additionally, GGGS, GPGPG, and KK linkers were added to connect the epitopes of CTL, HTL, and BCL, respectively. The linkers ensured that separate epitopes were effectively partitioned.49, 50

Detection of allergenicity, antigenicity and solubility of constructed vaccines

To foresee the allergic or non-allergic character of the built vaccines, AllergenFP,34 and AllerTOP35 servers had been utilized, while VaxiJen v2.0 server30 was additionally employed for the assessment of vaccines’ antigenicity and explore the prevalent immunization candidate. To investigate the immunostimulatory properties of the proteins, those servers choose an alignment-independent formula. Candidate vaccines’ solubility was determined by Prot-Sol server (http://protein-sol.manchester.ac.uk).51

Analysis of physicochemical characteristics of final vaccine construct

ProtParam, a resource offered by Expasy server, had been employed to evaluate the physicochemical characteristics of ultimate vaccine construct by calculating isoelectric pH, atomic weight, estimated half-life, hydropathicity, aliphatic and instability index, and grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY) values.31 The server calculates the protein’s primary structure by measuring the pKa values of targeted amino acids. Aliphatic index shows the measurement of the volume of aliphatic side chains of a given protein. The hydropathicity of all amino acid residues within the protein sequence was summed and then divided by the total number of amino acid residues to compute the GRAVY values.

Prediction of secondary and tertiary structure

PSIPRED v3.352 and NPS@ server53 predicted the alpha helix, extended strand, and random coil of final vaccine construct. However, the recommended vaccine's 3D structure was built using the I-TASSER and RaptorX servers, which counted the similarity degree between target protein and PDB supplied guide structure.54, 55

Refinement and validation of tertiary structure

Refinement was conducted utilizing 3Drefine: Protein Structure Refinement server (http://sysbio.rnet.missouri.edu/3Drefine/)56, 57, 58 to enhance the precision of the anticipated 3D modeled structure. Utilizing physical composition and knowledge-based technologies, 3Drefine server searched for source models using hydrogen bonding (HB) network optimization and minimization of atomic-level energy of optimized models. This has led to substantial improvements in the source model's global and local structural quality, bringing it closer to its native state in a computationally efficient manner. Ramachandran plot assessment at RAMPAGE was done to validate the improved protein structure.59

Disulfide engineering

Disulfide linkages improve protein’s geometric shape and provide tremendous stability. For the produced vaccine protein, DbD2, an online resource, was used to form such linkages.60 When amino acids are modified to cysteine individually, the server finds and shows the list of residue pairings with acceptable shape that can produce disulfide bonds.

Molecular docking of protein

Molecular docking refers to a technique for determining the ligand binding efficiency to the specific receptor.61 Using the PatchDock server,62 a method of protein-protein docking had been conducted to examine the specified subunit vaccine’s binding affinity with several HLA alleles and four immunological receptors, i.e., integrins β3, DAF/CD55, gC1qR, and protocadherin-1 (PCDH1).63, 64 The RCSB protein database was used to obtain the 3D structure of various MHC molecules as well as the responsible human receptors. The PatchDock server was used to acquire the desired complexes having best free binding energy and electrostatic interaction. It yielded a variety of solutions, which were then refined via FireDock server.

Molecular dynamics simulation of selected protein

Molecular dynamics investigation is necessary to validate the protein-protein complex stability and to support any in silico prediction. To determine the stability, assimilation of major protein dynamics to their normal modes is frequently used.65, 66 Being substitute for atomistic simulation and less expensive, it serves as a useful tool.67, 68 The iMODS server elucidates the normal modes (NMA) in internal coordinates to investigate the collective movement of proteins.69 Because of its extensive, speedier, and more accurate assessments than other tools, this server was used to examine the selected protein’s structural dynamics.70, 71 Deformability, B-factor, Eigen value, variance, covariance matrix, and elastic network analysis were analyzed to explain the motion stiffness and intrinsic movement of protein-protein complex. The capability to deform at each residue of a specific molecule determines the main chain's deformability. The motion stiffness is described by Eigen value correlated with every normal mode which is connected to the energy needed for structural deformation. Lesser Eigen value indicates the simpler deformation step and contrariwise.72

Codon adaptation and in silico cloning of final vaccine construct

Codon adaptation strategies are applied to accelerate the expression of well-studied prokaryotic cells in order to elucidate the adapting codon. The required techniques are used to tailor codon adaptation to well-characterized prokaryotic life forms to their expression rate. For cloning of intended vaccine construct, E. coli strain K12 was chosen as host. This strategy was used to generate greater transcriptional efficiency and expression level of V1 vaccine construct within the hand-picked host owing to the absence of commonality between human and E. coli codon utilization. Rho independent transcription termination, prokaryotic ribosome binding site, and cleavage sites of numerous restriction enzymes (i.e. HindIII and XbaI) were maintained a strategic distance from JCAT server function.73 The optimized V1 vaccine sequence was reversed and coupled with HindIII and XbaI restriction sites at the N-terminal and C-terminal locations, respectively. Using SnapGene's restriction cloning module, the adapted sequence was cloned between the restriction site of HindIII and XbaI of the pET30a (+) vector.61

The two viral proteins, namely envelope polyprotein (Accession ID: P08668; Antigenic Score: 0.538) and nucleoprotein (Accession ID: P05133; Antigenic Score: 0.5329) having highest antigenic score were selected among the six viral proteins. Identification of B-cell epitopes

B-cell epitopes were determined using six major algorithms from the IEDB: Bepipred Linear Epitope Prediction, Chou & Fasman Beta-Turn Prediction, Emini Surface Accessibility Prediction, Karplus & Schulz Flexibility Prediction, Kolaskar & Tongaonkar Antigenicity, and Parker Hydrophilicity Prediction. Top BCL epitopes for envelope polyprotein and nucleoprotein were screened for antigenicity, toxicity, allergenicity, and transmembrane topology before being used to construct the ultimate vaccine protein (Supplementary material 1).

Construction of vaccines

Three vaccines were constructed in total, designated as V1, V2, and V3, having lengths of 373, 458, and 555 amino acids, respectively (Supplementary material 2) and their immunogenic potential was tested. Each vaccine construct contains an adjuvant protein followed by a PADRE sequence to optimize the viability and potency of the peptide vaccine, and selected T-cell and B-cell epitopes with their corresponding linkers. Six CTL epitopes, six HTL epitopes, and twelve BCL epitopes were joined in a compatible way to set up the vaccines.

Allergenicity detection, antigenicity, and solubility scoring of constructed vaccines

The constructed vaccines were confirmed to be non-allergenic, but V1 performed the finest regarding safety, antigenicity (0.8426), solubility (0.718) (Fig. 4) and the ability to stimulate a favored immune response (Supplementary material 2). So, construct V1 was identified as a prospective vaccine candidate.

Solubility of designed candidate vaccine protein V1 via Prot-sol server.

Physicochemical characterization of candidate vaccine protein

Physicochemical characterization of V1 vaccine protein was performed to analyze the primary structure. Having 39.2 kDa molecular weight and 9.28 theoretical pI, the V1 vaccine candidate showed an excellent antigenic potential. This means that the protein was basic in nature. If the absorption level of all cysteine residues were reduced to 0.1 percent, the extinction co-efficient would be 37930. Estimated half-life of construct V1 was 1h in mammalian reticulocytes in vitro and >10 hours in E. coli in vivo. Aliphatic index and GRAVY value indicated its thermostability and hydrophilic nature which were 70.56 and -0.412, respectively. The protein was stable enough as its instability index was 29.60.

Prediction of secondary and tertiary structure

The produced vaccine V1 has a secondary structure that contains 12.06 percent alpha helix, 28.69 percent sheet, and 59.25 percent coil (Fig. 5). Five possible tertiary structures of construct V1 were predicted by I-TASSER. To generate these, top 10 threading template had been used by LOMETS in which TM score (0.41±0.14) and RMSD (13.1±4.2Å) were assessed depending on C score which was least for Model-1 (-2.66). The result ensured its superior quality (Fig. 6A). Homology modeling was carried out by identifying and using 1kj6A (p-value 3.14e-06) from the Protein Data Bank (PDB) as a suitable template for the V1 vaccine construct, with 100% modeling of all 373 amino acid residues and just 38% of residues in the disordered region. The lower P value confirmed its better 3D model quality which was14-e06 for the predicted vaccine protein.

Disulfide engineering

The residues of the protein sequence in the highly mobile area were changed to cysteine to undertake disulfide engineering by using DbD2 server. The server identified a total of 72 pairs of amino acid residues enabling for disulfide bond formation. The three most successful pairings for disulfide bond formation were GLY 21-ARG 47, LEU 156-GLY 167, and LYS 234-LYS 266 (Fig. 8). These residue pairs were selected based on energy, chi3, and B-factor assessment, where considerable energy was <2.5 and chi3 value was between -87 and +97.

Molecular dynamic simulation

iMODS server was employed to explore the collective functional motions of proteins using normal mode analysis (NMA) in internal coordinates of the docked complex. The complex's deformability was presented by the location of the chain ‘hinges’ which showed minimal deformability (Fig. 10A). There was a similarity between experimental B-factors and NMA predicted mobilities (Fig. 10B). The Eigen value was 1.510275e-05 (Fig. 10C), picturing an inverse relationship with the variance associated to each normal mode (Fig. 10D). It gave evidence of modal stiffness. Covariance matrix indicated the movement of the complex in a correlated, uncorrelated, and anticorrelated fashion with red, white, and blue hues, respectively (Fig. 10E). In addition, an elastic model was created based on the stiffness level of the atomic pairs connected by springs (the darker the grays, the stiffer the springs) (Fig. 10F).

Molecular dynamic simulation of constructed vaccine V1 and TLR-3 receptor. Stability of protein-protein complex was examined by (A) deformability; (B) B-factor; (C) Eigenvalue; (D) variance; (E) covariance matrix and (F) elastic network analysis.

Codon adaptation and in silico cloning of final vaccine construct

Due to differences in Homo sapiens and E. coli regulatory systems, codon adaptation was achieved by considering the host's expression system. Construct V1 had codon adaptation index (CAI) of 0.986. It ensured that the most common codons were covered to a greater extent. The optimized codons' GC content (50.491%) was also noteworthy. The construct no longer featured HindIII and XbaI restriction sites, allowing for safe cloning. At last, the optimized codons had been incorporated into pET30a (+) vector between HindIII and XbaI restriction sites (Fig. 11A), yielding a cloned V1 construct (Fig. 11B) of 6336 bp comprising 1131bp insert sequence (shown in blue color and the others belonging to the vector only).

(A) Restriction digestion and (B) in silico cloning of the gene sequence of ultimate vaccine construct V1 into pET30a (+) expression vector. Blue color represented the inserted target sequence between HindIII and XbaI.

Discussion

The focus of this study was on utilizing in silico analysis to predict a potential broad-spectrum vaccine against the Hantaan virus. For this purpose, the viral antigenic protein sequences were analyzed to find out the potential vaccine candidate. This method of vaccine establishment has already been prosperous in different pathogenic organisms including Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, HIV, SARS-CoV-2, Nipah virus, Bandavirus, Jamestown Canyon virus (JCV), Monkeypox virus, Hendra virus, Yersinia pestis, Opisthorchis viverrini, Staphylococcus cornubiensis, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Klebsiella pneumoniae, where artificial peptide vaccines were developed utilizing reverse vaccinology methodologies.74, 75, 76, 77, 78, 79, 80, 81, 82, 83, 84 Several of them showed better efficacy in clinical trials. This trending technology is also applied to develop vaccines against different pathogenic bacteria such as Acinetobacter baumannii and Staphylococcus aureus.85, 86 Conventional vaccines containing attenuated or killed viruses rely upon satisfactory in vitro antigen expression in culture models, which might take a year or more to generate. So, these methodologies are not generally practicable as microorganisms are typically problematic to culture and in certain occasions, it may result in unfavorable immune reactions.87 Besides, majority of these strategies are time-consuming and costly. In the case of standard vaccinations, the attenuated vaccine's virulent properties may revert, or the inactivation approach may fail in suppressing the virulence trait.88, 89 Safety concerns have been raised in recent clinical investigations, though the standard vaccine development process has been emphasized.90, 91 Subunit vaccinations, on the other hand, may be able to overcome these unfavorable outcomes. Recombinant subunit vaccines are comparatively safe as they contain only antigenic components and there is no possibility of emergent virulence.

Vaccine research is currently shifted to pre-screening program that are preeminent economical and less time devouring.92, 93 New vaccines can be developed by practicing reverse vaccinology, which is a novel gateway to merge immunogenomics and immunogenetics.94 With the current progression of genome and protein databases, the in silico vaccine development system has attained a massive acceptance93 and here it is being used to establish a novel multi-epitope monovalent vaccine against Hantaan virus.

To facilitate vaccine development and understand the mechanism of infective agents’ entry and immune reaction, in silico analysis of structural protein is remarkable.95 That’s why, we analyzed both of the envelope polyprotein and nucleoprotein of HTNV strain 76-118 to propose potential peptide vaccine candidate. But Rahman and Pervege,95 identified only HNTV glycoprotein G2 for vaccine construction. Similarly, another research was done by screening peptide from glycoprotein.96 Furthermore, a study was conducted on vaccine design against HNTV utilizing nucleoprotein, RNA-directed RNA polymerase L and envelope polyprotein.97 Compared with these studies, we focused on selecting the foremost potent antigenic proteins and found that envelope polyprotein and nucleoprotein were most antigenic protein among the six viral proteins of the whole proteome of HTNV, suggesting that these leading proteins have their ability to offer immunity and permitted additional investigations.

Monovalent vaccines have the power to activate T and B cell responses against infectious agents or organisms.98 Cytotoxic T cells (CD8+), also known as CTLs, are produced in the thymus. Peptides are delivered to the CD8+ T lymphocytes via MHC class I molecules, which are expressed on nucleated cells. When a CD8+ T cell recognizes the antigens, it triggers and secretes anti-tumor or anti-microbial cytokines which inhibit the agents’ unfolding inside the human body.99 An epitope-based T cell immunization against virus-like infectious agents is a potential method to enrich robust immune response.100 Predicting T-cell and B-cell epitopes is a crucial aspect of vaccine design, enhancing the precision of in silico epitope mapping. To accomplish that, we applied a multi-method approach using the IEDB analysis tool. Based on antigenic score and transmembrane topology screening, the highest number of HLA allele binding epitopes was picked up. Nowadays, the immune system is being protected from an allergic response by most vaccines.101 Non-allergic and non-toxic epitopes from both proteins were selected and further analyzed. Consequently, it appears that the projected T-cell epitopes can cover approximately 90% of the global population. In the vaccine development and pharmaceuticals production, MHC superfamilies show a critical role. So, it is necessary to investigate functional link between MHC variants by undertaking MHC cluster analysis. All the epitopes showed a significant correlation during cluster analysis. However, we aimed to create a vaccine with multi-epitope subunits to provide enhanced immune protection. Applying six algorithms, we identified the top six epitopes of B cell for each selected structural protein. To generate the ultimate vaccine proteins, we selected permissive six CTL, six HTL, and twelve BCL epitopes. Joshi et al.96 predicted only one epitope named MIGLLSSRI by targeting glycoprotein for in silico vaccine development. Also, Ismail et al.102 used ten epitopes for constructing a multi-antigenic epitope vaccine through immunoinformatics. Furthermore, Ghafoor et al.97 constructed a multi-epitope vaccine against HTNV using only 2 CTL, 3 HTL, and 1 BCL epitopes, which in turn supports the superiority of our predicted vaccine construct. Immunogenicity and non-allergic effects of the produced vaccines were also tested. To increase the immunogenicity of the vaccine construct, adjuvant should be linked with the epitopes.103 We used three different adjuvants, i.e., beta defensin, L7/L12 ribosomal protein and HABA protein for constructing vaccine V1, V2 and V3, respectively. Adjuvants were connected with CTL epitopes via EAAAK linker. Also, individual CTL, HTL and BCL epitopes were linked by GGGS, GPGPG, and KK linkers in the vaccine construct, respectively to demonstrate the robust immune responses. Similarly, Ismail et al.102 designed vaccine constructs with three different adjuvants namely TLR4-agonist, beta defensin and 50S ribosomal protein L7/L12 using EAAAKl inker and epitopes were joined by GPGPG linkers. According to research findings, vaccines containing PADRE sequence had better CTL responses than vaccines that do not contain PADRE.104 So, we joined PADRE sequence for formulating vaccine construct. Previous studies did not include PADRE sequence in vaccine construction for HTNV.96, 102

Vaccine construct V1 was found to be the most prevalent in characteristics of length (373aa), allergenicity (non-allergen), solubility (0.718), and antigenicity (0.8426). Before estimating and optimizing the V1’s 3D structure, primary and secondary structures were examined. Molecular weight (39.2 kDa) was within the ideal range (30-70 kDa), theoretical pI (9.28) indicated that the protein is basic in nature, aliphatic index (70.56) revealed its thermostability and GRAVY value (-0.412) indicated its hydrophilic nature. Also, the instability index (29.60) was <40, which demonstrates that the V1 construct was stable enough. It was more stable than MEV (instability index- 36.28) predicted by Ghafoor et al..97 In addition, secondary structure analysis showed that the vaccine had 20.38%, 28.42%, and 44.24% alpha helix, extended strand, and random coil, respectively in the structure. Predicted 3D structure of vaccine construct V1 improved greatly after refinement.

We examined the interactivity of our designed vaccine with a variety of HLA molecules (DRB1*0101, DRB3*0202, DRB5*0101, DRB3*0101, DRB1*0401, and DRB1*0301) to verify our prediction. Construct V1 had an excellent free binding energy than V2 and V3 constructs, having lower global energy of 7.18, 1.68, -14.43, -39.14, -8.58, and 7.31, respectively. Binding affinity of construct V1 with human immunological receptors i.e., integrins β3, DAF/CD55, gC1qR, and protocadherin-1 (PCDH1), which are major receptors of the HTNV, was also investigated using docking analysis. The estimated global energy was -21.71, -12.12, -29.78, and -7.52 independently. But protein-protein docking was performed only against TLR-4 receptor in previous research.97, 102 A molecular dynamics investigation was also carried out to ascertain the complex stability. Utilizing covariance analysis and subsets of atoms, structural dynamics were studied.65 Literature studies have linked macromolecule’s durability to atomic fluctuations.105, 106 In our study, the iMODS server was used to compare the essential dynamics of proteins to their normal modes to assess their durability. Because the placement of hinges inside the chain was insignificant, the research indicated a low probability of deformability for any individual residue, bolstering our hypothesis. Before insertion of V1 construct into the pET30a (+) vector for heterologous cloning and expression, the constructed V1 was reverse transcribed and suited for E. coli strain K12. The codon adaptation index (CAI) was 0.986 which was more significant than previously predicted vaccine’s CAI (0.93).97, 102 A considerable GC content (50.491%) was also contained by the optimized codon. An overall analysis revealed that, our predicted vaccine construct is promising, which might be a cost-effective ground that significantly may decrease the number of candidates for experimental testing, thus saving time and resources compared to conventional approaches. However, detailed structural, immunological, and physicochemical analyses presented in this work create a strong theoretical basis for prioritizing these candidates for immediate wet-lab investigation including synthetic peptide-based in vitro assays for testing the predicted epitope, MHC binding affinity and their ability to activate T-cells using Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells (PBMCs). For subsequent validation of the constructed vaccine, expression, purification, and thorough analyses of immunogenicity, including antibody production, cytokine profile, and cell-mediated immune responses should be performed. Finally, advanced preclinical assessment strategies may validate the safety profiles in animal models and in vivo immunization strategies.

Conclusion

Computational vaccinology is an emerging science that is being utilized to solve the difficulty of developing new vaccines for a variety of deadly diseases. They have numerous advantages including enhancement of safety, broad improvement, and vaccine potency. It is essential to have a detailed postulation of targeted immunogenic protein’s amino acid sequences for designing these vaccines. In our study, we have focused on in silico designing of a novel multi-epitope based monovalent vaccine against HTNV. The results showed that both cell-mediated and humoral immune responses could be stimulated by the proposed vaccine against this virus. However, the constructed vaccine must undergo further wet lab-based investigations for experimental validation to push forward into human trials.

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe purpose of the current study was to analyze and validate the existing gap in knowledge, by conducting a differential expression analysis and validation of NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9 in breast, cervical, and glioblastoma cancer and targeting these proteins through development of novel site specific inhibitor with favorable pharmacokinetic and safety profile, using open-source databases. The analysis revealed that the targeted kinases were overexpressed in all three types of cancer. Their expression was significantly linked to overall survival rates, which suggests that they play a major role in the development and progression of these cancers. After, having the prognostic importance of These findings provided a rationale for synthesizing novel compound i.e., dimethyl 2,2’-((methylenebis(2-(2H-benzo[d][1,2,3]triazol-2-yl)-4-(2,4,4-trimethylpentan-2-yl)-6,1phenylene))bis(oxy))diacetate (TAJ4)), capable of effectively targeting these proteins using in-vitro cytotoxicity assays and comprehensive computational approaches. Then the inhibitory potential of TAJ4 was evaluated against cell lines of the respective cancers (HeLa cells, MCF-7 cells, and Vero cells). The growth inhibitory values (GI50) suggested that TAJ4 exhibited strong inhibitory potential towards MCF-7 cells (GI50 = 3.18 ± 0.11 µM) in comparison to the HeLa cell line (GI50 = 8.12 ± 0.43 µM), surpassing that of standard drugs. Furthermore, in-silico investigations, including density functional theory (DFT) calculations and molecular docking studies, revealed a substantial reactivity profile of TAJ4, with promising molecular interactions against NEK7, NEK9, TP53, NF-KAPPA-B, and caspase-3 proteins. Introduction

Cancer is still one of the main causes of death in the world. Breast, cervical, and glioblastoma cancers contribute to an enormous percentage of the global cancer burden. Breast cancer and cervical cancer are two of the most prevalent cancers affecting women globally, with breast cancer leading in incidence and cervical cancer ranking fourth [1]. The common sites for invasive breast cancer to metastasize are the lung, liver, bone and brain while for cervical cancer, the frequent metastatic sites are the lungs, extra-pelvic nodes, liver and bones [2]. Understanding these metastatic patterns aids in surveillance strategies and therapeutic approaches, helping tailor treatments to improve patient outcomes and manage symptoms effectively [3]. Because the development of cancer is so complicated, it’s crucial to find specific biomarkers that can help with early diagnosis, prognosis, and the development of targeted therapies [4]. The search for novel and effective cancer biomarkers and therapeutic targets is a major focus of oncological research, motivated by the urgent need to improve diagnostic precision and treatment outcomes. In this context, the Never in Mitosis Gene A (NIMA)-related kinases (NEKs) have emerged as critical players in cell cycle regulation and have been implicated in the tumorigenesis of various cancers including breast and cervical cancers [5]. Among these, NEK2, NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9 have garnered attention for their potential roles as biomarkers and therapeutic targets. NEK2, NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9 are serine/threonine kinases involved in the regulation of spindle assembly and mitotic progression [6]. Aberrant expression of these kinases has been associated with the disruption of cell cycle checkpoints, leading to genomic instability and cancer progression. Among different NEKs, NEK7 has been identified as a key player in the progression of various cancers, including pancreatic, cervical, gastric, and hepatocellular carcinoma. Its overexpression is correlated with poor prognosis, indicating its potential as a biomarker for these cancers [7, 8]. NEK7 also interacts with components of the immune response, such as the NLRP3 inflammasone (NOD-, LRR- and pyrin domain-containing protein 3), which is involved in inflammatory processes and may influence cancer progression [9].

Along with NEK7, NEK6 has potential role in mitotic cell division which is phosphorylated and activated by NEK9 [10]. Like NEK7, NEK6 also play potential role in the progression of different cancers and its specific role in breast, cervical, and glioblastoma cancers is still unexplored. But its role in mitotic progression and its activation by NEK9 suggest that it may also be significant in the prognosis and diagnosis of targeted cancers and other associated malignancies. The third targeted protein i.e., NEK9 has been shown to be associated with distant metastasis in various cancer, serving as an independent predictive factor for the aggressive cancer behavior [7]. Like NEK7, NEK6 and NEK9, NEK2 is very closely related to NEK6 and NEK9, has been found to be overexpressed in breast cancer and is implicated in poor recurrence-free survival and overall survival [11]. NEK2’s role in various cancers, including cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma, has been highlighted with a significant fold change in expression, suggesting that NEK family members may have a broad impact on cancer prognosis and diagnosis [8, 12].

The upregulation of these kinases in all three forms of cancer and their notable correlation with overall survival rates indicate their significant involvement in the emergence and propagation of cancer. A comprehensive analysis of their expression patterns and functional roles in breast, cervical, and glioblastoma cancers is still remained unexplored and therefore there is an urgent need of exploration of expression pattern of theses NEKs in targeted cancers. The differential expression patterns of NEKs proteins will not only acts as a possible indicator for these cancers but also offers a justification to produce novel compounds that can specifically target these enzymes. Indeed, developing potent and selective inhibitors targeting NEK (NIMA-related kinase) enzymes could significantly advance therapeutic strategies for NEK-associated cancers [13]. Prior research has revealed that only a limited number of compounds have been identified as inhibitors of the NEK family of kinases, specifically NEK7 and NEK6. The limited availability of selective and specific inhibitors has necessitated the development of new compounds that can effectively target the putative biomarkers associated with these malignancies. The Figure S1 (supplementary data) displays a few previously reported NEK6 and NEK7 inhibitors [14] .

The main objective of the present investigation is to address the existing gap in knowledge, by conducting an differential expression analysis and validation of NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9 in breast, cervical, and glioblastoma cancer and targeting these proteins through development of novel site specific inhibitor with favorable pharmacokinetic and safety profile. Initially, the expression profiling of NEK6, NEK7 and NEK9 was executed by utilizing the various open source databases including UALCAN platform (http://ualcan.path.uab.edu/), which is grounded in TCGA OMICS data. Whereas, benzotriazole scaffold based potential inhibitor i.e., dimethyl 2,2’-((methylenebis(2-(2 H-benzo[d][1,2,3]triazol-2-yl)−4-(2,4,4-trimethylpentan-2-yl)−6,1phenylene))bis(oxy))diacetate (TAJ4) was synthesized through multiple synthetic pathways (Scheme 1).The Benzotriazole scaffold was initially synthesized by the combination of acetylenes with azides via 1,3 dipolar cycloaddition reaction [15]. This reaction requires a high energy of activation and produces 1,4- and 1,5-regioisomers but copper catalyzed (CuAAC) version of this reaction gives 100% regioselectivity producing only the 1,4- disubstituted regioisomer along with substantial yield under mild conditions [16].

Synthesis of Dimethyl 2,2'-((methylenebis(2-(2H-benzo[d][1,2,3]triazol-2-yl)−4-(2,4,4-trimethylpentan-2-yl)−6,1 phenylene))bis(oxy))diacetate (TAJ4)

The Benzotriazole scaffold based derivative was selected as it is believed as a privileged class of compound for its numerous bioactivities and have drawn unique attention for their prominent medicinal applications [17] including antibacterial [18], cytotoxic [19], antiproliferative [20], antituberculosis [21], anti-HIV [22], and antioxidant [23] as shown in Figure S2 provided in supplementary data. Moreover, the production of benzotriazole has broadly been studied for its medicinal applications such as, neuroprotective agents [24], anti-anxiety [25], antichagasic agents [26], antileishmanial [27], anti-inflammatory [28] and antibacterial [29].

After synthesizing the Benzotriazole derivative (TAJ4), the inhibitory potential was evaluated via in-vitro cytotoxicity assays against cancer cell lines i.e., one breast cell line MCF-7 cell line (HTB-22™) and one human cervical cancer cell line (the HeLa cell line and the CRM-CCL-2™). Moreover, the role of the synthesized compound TAJ4 against the targeted cancers was justified using different computational approaches, revealing its significant efficacy against both cancers along with the role in metastasis as well. The therapeutic potential of the TAJ4 is shown by the fact that it is able to outperform conventional chemotherapeutic drugs like cisplatin and doxorubicin throughout treatment. Further elucidation of the reactivity profile of TAJ4 and its molecular interactions with NEK kinases is provided by the in-silico investigations via density functional theory (DFT) calculations, molecular docking studies, and MD simulations. The significant binding affinity of TAJ4 against NEK2, in particular, suggests its potential as a therapeutic agent, warranting further investigation.

Materials and methods

Synthesis protocol

To a stirred solution of Initially 100 mg (0.152 mmol) of 6,6’-methylenebis(2-(2 H-benzo[d][1,2,3]triazol-2-yl)−4-(2,4,4- trimethylpentan-2-yl)phenol) (1) was allowed to stirred in 15 ml acetone along with the addition of 51 mg (0.0364 mmol) K2CO3. After few minutes, the addition of 0.04 ml (0.395 mmol) of methyl 2-chloroacetate was carried out in dropwise manner. The reaction mixture was heated at reflux temperature of acetone for 18 h. The progress of the reaction was monitored using TLC in which the complete consumption of the reactant i.e., 6,6’-methylenebis(2-(2Hbenzo[d][1,2,3]triazol-2-yl)−4-(2,4,4-trimethylpentan-2-yl)phenol) (1) was also observed. On completion, the reaction mixture was washed out thrice with dichloromethane (DCM)/water (H2O) followed by drying using magnesium sulphate (MgSO4). At the end, the solvents were allowed to evaporate on rotary evaporator and crude product was purified through silica gel using column chromatography where n-Hexane and Dichloromethane (8:2, v/v) were used to afford the purified dimethyl 2,2’-((methylenebis(2-(2 H benzo[d][1,2,3] triazol-2-yl)−4-(2,4,4trimethylpentan-2-yl) 6,1phenylene))bis(oxy))diacetate (3) in excellent yield.

Biological activities

MTT Assay (cell viability assay)

The compound TAJ4 underwent a comprehensive assessment of its anticancer efficacy against two distinct human cancer cell lines, namely HeLa, a cell line associated with cervical cancer, and MCF-7, a cell line associated with breast cancer. The present evaluation was based on the established methodology proposed by Mosmann (1983) and Nik and Otto (1990), as described in our previous studies [30–32]. The study was conducted using 96-well flat-bottom plates, with a volume of 90 µL of media and a seeding density of 10 × 104 cells per well. A volume of 10 µL (100 µM) of TAJ4 solution was introduced into each well, followed by a 24-hour incubation period at a temperature of 37 °C and a CO2 concentration of 5%. 10 µL of the standard drug (cisplatin) was added to the positive control well, whereas 100 µL of cells medium (without chemical) was added to the negative control well. Subsequently, 10 µL of MTT reagent was added to each well and allowed to incubate for 4 hours at a temperature of 37 °C. Subsequently, a volume of 100 µL of a 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate solution was introduced to aid in the solubilization of crystals. The resulting mixture was then kept at the room temperature for a duration of 30 min, with intermittent agitation. The optical density was determined to be 570 nm, while the background absorbance was measured at 630 nm. The experiment was conducted three times, and the results were reported as percentages of growth inhibition, as previously explained [33].

Statistical analysis

Data is presented in the form of % growth reduction in tables along with standard error of means and in graphs using One-way ANOVA using Prism statistical software package (GraphPad Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA) with P < 0.005* which was considered statistically significant. The IC50 values were also calculated by fitting the sigmoidal dose response model using GraphPad Software version 5.0.

In-silico investigations

Differential expression analysis and methylation profiling

In the realm of oncological research, the present study meticulously employed a suite of public databases to scrutinize and substantiate the expression patterns of NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9 within the context of three divergent cancer types: breast cancer, cervical cancer, and brain tumors. This investigation extended to the examination of the interplay between the expression of these proteins and their corresponding genes across the specified cancer types. Utilizing the UALCAN platform [34] (http://ualcan.path.uab.edu/), which is grounded in TCGA OMICS data [35], the study conducted expression and methylation profiling for NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9, encompassing the aforementioned cancer varieties. The UALCAN database’s default parameters facilitated the application of the Student’s t-test, with a p-value threshold of less than 0.05 earmarked as the benchmark for statistical significance. Further analytical rigor was applied through the GEPIA 2 database [36] (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/), which served as a tool for correlation analysis, overall survival (OS) metrics, and the elucidation of isoform specifics for the genes of interest within the three cancer types under study. The GEPIA 2’s default settings were harnessed, and the ANOVA test [37] was employed as the differential method, thereby reinforcing the diagnostic and prognostic utility of the targeted genes. In pursuit of biomarker discovery, the study traversed the TCGA and GTEx databases [38], seeking to unearth the biomarker potential of NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9 in breast, cervical, and glioblastoma cancers. This exploration was pivotal in identifying the relevance of these genes as biomarkers in the oncological landscape. The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) [39] (http://www.proteinatlas.org/) was instrumental in the analysis of the proteomic expression levels of NEK6 and NEK7 in both cancerous and normal tissues from the breast, cervix, and brain. The proteomic expression was meticulously categorized based on staining intensity, ranging from non-detectable to high. Lastly, the study delved into the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network pertaining to the genes in question, utilizing the STRING database (https://string-db.org/) [40]. A low confidence value of 0.15 was established as the principal parameter for the construction of the PPI model, offering insights into the intricate web of interactions between these proteins.

Density function theory studies (DFTs)

The present study employed Density Functional Theory (DFT) [41] as a robust computational strategy for the determination of electronic structure and reactivity of the TAJ4 compound and facilitated. The DFT calculations facilitated the optimization of the ground state geometries [42] and the assessment of reactivity parameters for the TAJ4 compound, culminating in the acquisition of its stable conformation post the most energetically favorable descent [43]. The computational methodology hinged on the B3LYP functional, a hybrid correlation-functional, in conjunction with the 6-31G* basis set [44]. This particular basis set is instrumental in the accurate determination of electron density and chemical reactivity, as it provides a balanced trade-off between computational efficiency and the precision required for such evaluations. The choice of the B3LYP/6-31G* level of theory is predicated on its established reliability in capturing the essential quantum mechanical features necessary for a comprehensive understanding of molecular behavior. The computational procedures were executed using the Gaussian 09 software suite [45], a state-of-the-art program for electronic structure modeling. The initial configuration files were meticulously prepared, and the resultant output was visualized with the aid of GaussView 6.0 [46], a graphical interface that enhances the interpretability of computational results. The construction and significance of the basis set employed in this methodology are paramount. The basis set, a collection of functions used to describe the electronic orbitals of the atoms within a molecule, is a foundational component in DFT calculations. It determines the level of detail at which the molecular electronic structure is described and, consequently, influences the accuracy of the predicted chemical properties. The 6-31G* basis set, in particular, is a split-valence basis set with polarization functions that enhance its ability to model the anisotropic distribution of electron density, which is critical for the accurate depiction of reactivity parameters.

Molecular docking analysis

Molecular docking stands as a promising avenue for unraveling the mechanistic insights and binding patterns of small molecules with biological targets, thereby facilitating a deeper comprehension of their behavior within active site amino acid residues [47]. In the context of the present study, molecular docking analyses were conducted to visually depict the binding affinities between a newly synthesized compound and a spectrum of targeted proteins, including NEK2, NEK7, NEK9, TP53, NF-KAPPA-B, and caspase-3, leveraging their 3D crystal structures sourced from the Protein Data Bank [48] (www.rcsb.com, PDB IDs:2W5A, 2WQN, 3ZKE, 3DCY, 1NFI, and 3DEI). Prior to the docking analyses, meticulous preparations were undertaken to ensure the integrity and reliability of the protein structures. This involved the elimination of heteroatoms and water molecules, the addition of polar hydrogen atoms, the incorporation of Kollman charges, and the visualization and supplementation of missing amino acid residues, all conducted via MGL Tools [49]. Concurrently, the 3D structure of the newly synthesized compound was meticulously refined using ChemDraw 3D [50], culminating in the attainment of its most energetically stable configuration. The subsequent molecular docking endeavors were executed utilizing AutoDock’s [51] default genetic algorithm as the scoring function [52], with the grid box dimensions meticulously tailored to each targeted protein as discussed earlier [49]. For each target protein a 100 distinct configurations were obtained. The most stable pose with least binding energy was selected and subjected for 2D and 3D analysis to describe interactions. The Novel derivatives with better binding affinities towards target proteins can be synthesized from the outcomes of this current study.

Furthermore, the robustness and reliability of the molecular docking protocol were rigorously validated to bolster the authenticity of the adopted methodology. This validation entailed the re-docking of the native ligand of the biological target within the activation loop of the protein, with the ensuing comparison of the Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD) value between the native and regenerated poses. An RMSD value below 2 angstroms was the criterion set for deeming the docking methodology efficient, thereby ensuring the veracity of the docking analyses [53].

Molecular dynamic simulations

Computational approaches known as Molecular Dynamic (MD) simulations enable the investigation of the strength of molecular interactions within docked complexes over a period of time [54]. The dynamic nature of protein-ligand complexes obtained from molecular docking was investigated using Nanoscale Molecular Dynamics (NAMD) software version 1.9.3 [55]. The software demonstrated strong binding affinity for the corresponding proteins. Initially, the CHARMM-GUI web server was utilized to produce the topology files for the simulations [56], where all molecules, including ligands and proteins, were characterized by CHARMM36 force field parameters subject to periodic boundary conditions [57, 58]. In order to simulate the normal physiological conditions, same method were used as discussed in our previously published articles [53], and production run was carried out for 50ns. The Various analytical metrics, including root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) and root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF), were employed to assess the stability of the protein-ligand complex. The electrostatic interactions observed during MD simulations were taken into account using Particle Mesh Ewald summation method [59]. All output files were visualized using VMD 1.9.3 [60].

X-ray crystallography and structure refinement

The crystallographic data for the TAJ4 compound were obtained using a Rigaku Oxford Diffraction Eos Gemini diffractometer with Cu K radiation (= 1.5418). Using the multi-scan absorption correction applied to the to the data, the structures were solved and refined using SHELX software packages [61], while ORTEP-3 [62] programs were employed in drawings. Uiso (H) = k X Ueq (C, N) constraints were applied to refine the geometric positions of hydrogen atoms at distances of 0.88 (for NH), 0.95 (for CH), and 0.98 (for CH3). For NH, CH, and CH3 hydrogens, k = 1.2 and 1.5 for CH3 hydrogens, respectively. The investigation of Hirshfeld surfaces (HS) is often regarded as the most reliable approach for examining interactions within crystal structures [63]. The crystal structure of the TAJ4 molecule was analyzed using Crystal Explorer 17.5 to determine the intermolecular interactions [64] The Hirshfeld surface distance from the nearest nucleus was measured and represented by di and de, respectively, on both the inside and outside of the surface. Dnorm was used to indicate the normal contact distance. The colors white, blue, and red have been used to represent Dnorm. Cell viability assay

Cancer cells are characterized by cellular sources; as a result they display special activities in a defined pathway. Therefore, human epithelial cervical cancer cells could play a different manner compared with breast cancer cells [65]. To determine the effect of TAJ4 on the viability of human breast cancer cells, the MTT assay was conducted. The effect of the compound TAJ4 on various cell lines, such as HeLa cells, MCF cells, and normal Vero cells, is illustrated in Table 1. The different forms of cancer each have their own unique genetic and metabolic traits, which results in a variety of responses to the medications that are used to treat them. For MCF-7 cells and HeLa cells, the experimental values of GI50, 3.18 ± 0.11 and 8.12 ± 0.43 µM, respectively, could indicate differences in signaling pathways between these two cell types. In-silico investigations

Differential expression analysis, promoter methylation profiling and protein-protein interactions (PPI)

Publicly available databases have been instrumental in examining the expression levels of NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9 across three different types of cancer: breast cancer, cervical cancer, and glioblastoma. The GEPIA 2 platform served as the primary resource for conducting a comparative expression analysis of these kinases between cancerous and normal tissue samples. The comprehensive analysis indicated that all three NEK kinases—NEK6, NEK7, and NEK9—demonstrated increased transcriptional activity in cancer tissues [66]. Specifically, NEK6 was found to be significantly overexpressed in glioblastoma compared to normal brain tissue, with a statistically significant p-value of less than 0.05. Correlation analysis between two or more genes serves as a critical diagnostic and prognostic biomarker parameter, playing a pivotal role in the development and progression of tumorigenesis. Correlation analysis is a statistical technique used to measure the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two variables. In genetics, correlation analysis is often used to investigate the relationships between gene expression levels. A correlation coefficient, such as the Pearson correlation coefficient, is used to quantify the correlation between two genes. The Pearson correlation coefficient ranges from − 1 to + 1. A correlation coefficient of + 1 indicates a perfect positive correlation, meaning that the two genes are always expressed together. A correlation coefficient of −1 indicates a perfect negative correlation, meaning that as the expression of one gene increases, the expression of the other gene decreases. A correlation coefficient of 0 indicates no correlation between the two genes. Interestingly, protein-complex demonstrated significant stability pattern with RMSD trajectory remained stable through the MD simulation. The average RMSD for protein-ligand complex was 2.3 angstroms which quite acceptable. These stability pattern of protein-ligand complex was due to establishment of stronger hydrophobic and hydrophilic interactions between TAJ4 and active site residues. Particularly, CYS163 was engaged hydrogen bonding for approximately 50% of simulation time. In addition, TYR204, TRP206, ARG207 and PHE256 was significantly engaged in hydrophobic interactions which is signifying the stability of protein-ligand complex.

Furthermore, residue wise fluctuations of targeted protein was evaluated through RMSF metric. It was notable that all residues remained stable and fluctuations were found to be under acceptable limit. Interestingly, amino acid residues in contact with TAJ4 exhibited least fluctuations which lend enough testimony on stability of protein-ligand complex. The detailed evolution of RMSD pattern for C-alpha atoms of Apo protein, protein ligand complex, RMSF of amino acid residues and contact map is illustrated in Fig. 13 (A, B and C).

The MMGBSA analysis of the NEK2-TAJ4 and NF-KAPPA-B-TAJ4 complexes, as presented in Table 6, provides valuable insights into the energetics and stability of these protein-ligand interactions. The energy contributors, including ΔEVDW, ΔEel, ΔEGB, ΔEsurf, and ΔG, offer a comprehensive view of the binding free energies and the individual components contributing to the overall stability of the complexes. In the NEK2-TAJ4 complex, the ΔEVDW of −35.20 kcal/mol and ΔEel of −1.55 kcal/mol indicate a substantial contribution from van der Waals interactions, while the solvation free energy (ΔEGB) and surface energy (ΔEsurf) further contribute to the overall binding free energy (ΔG) of −29.39 kcal/mol. Similarly, in the NF-KAPPA-B-TAJ4 complex, the energy contributors demonstrate a comparable pattern, albeit with slightly lower values, suggesting a favorable binding affinity with a ΔG of −23.3 kcal/mol.

The MMGBSA is crucial in understanding the thermodynamic aspects of protein-ligand interactions. These methods allow for the decomposition of the free energy of binding into enthalpic and entropic contributions, providing a detailed understanding of the molecular recognition process. The EMM term, encompassing internal energies, electrostatic interactions, and van der Waals forces, along with solvation free energy contributions, collectively influence the overall stability and binding affinity of the complexes. Furthermore, the comparison between the NEK2-TAJ4 and NF-KAPPA-B-TAJ4 complexes reveals distinct energetic profiles, indicating specific molecular interactions and binding modes unique to each complex. The differences in energy contributors highlight the diverse nature of protein-ligand interactions and underscore the significance of understanding the underlying energetics for rational drug design and optimization. Overall, the MMGBSA analyses presented in Table 6 offer a comprehensive assessment of the energetics of the NEK2-TAJ4 and NF-KAPPA-B-TAJ4 complexes, providing valuable data for elucidating the molecular mechanisms of binding and informing the development of potential therapeutic agents targeting these protein targets.

Hirshfeld surface analysis

Intermolecular interactions of the compound and Hirshfeld surface (HS) was plotted using Hirshfeld surface analysis technique employed in crystal explorer. HS surface was categorized with blue and white colored regions as shown in supplementary file (Figure S14). The white colored region indicate contacts with distances equal to the sum of van der Waals radii, and the red and blue colors indicate distances shorter (in close contact) or longer (distinct contact) than the van der Waals radii, respectively. In addition, The comprehensive two-dimensional fingerprint plot, depicted in supplementary file (Figure S15), delineates various interactions, including H … H, H … C/C … H, H … O/O … H, H … N/N … H, C … N/N … C, C … C, and N … N in addition with their contribution toward HS surface is sufficiently elaborated in supplementary file (Table S1).

Conclusion

In conclusion, the synthesis of the benzotriazole derivative TAJ4 has yielded a compound with remarkable potential as a novel therapeutic agent for cancer treatment. Through biological evaluation and in-silico investigations, TAJ4 has demonstrated substantial inhibitory potential against various cancer proteins, particularly exhibiting superior efficacy compared to standard doxorubicin and cisplatin, especially in HeLa cell lines. Additionally, differential expression analysis has identified NEK kinases as potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers, further emphasizing the significance of TAJ4. DFT studies, molecular docking, and MD simulation studies have collectively highlighted TAJ4’s reactive nature, strong binding interactions with targeted proteins, and significant stability of the protein-ligand complex over time. The compound’s reactivity profile has been further validated through Hirshfeld surface analysis. These findings underscore the promising potential of TAJ4 as an anticancer agent and warrant further in-vivo studies to fully explore its therapeutic effects.

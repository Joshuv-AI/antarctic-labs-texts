THE CYCLOSYNTOMIC REGULATOR OF A NUMBER FIELD
tess bouis and q q - uentin gazda

Abstract
We construct a q q -deformation of the p p -adic regulator of a number field, called the cyclosyntomic regulator , building on the Habiro ring of Garoufalidis–Scholze–Wheeler–Zagier. The key new ingredient in our construction is a refinement of Sulyma’s norm maps in prismatic cohomology, which interpolate between classical powers and Frobenius maps at various prime numbers p p . Furthermore, we compute the values of the cyclosyntomic regulator at units of the form 1 − ζ 1-\zeta , where ζ \zeta is a root of unity.

Using these cohomological constructions, we now return to the number theoretic questions that motivated the study of these regulators. In the

Theorem B

Let

\mathscr{O}_{K}[\Delta_{K}^{-1}]

c_{1}^{\mathrm{CycSyn}}:u\longmapsto\frac{1}{d}\log\Big(\frac{\tilde{\Pi}_{d}(\tilde{u})}{\mathrm{Frob}_{d}^{\mathrm{Hab}}(\tilde{u})}\Big)\in\emph{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CycSyn}}(R,\Z(1)^{(d)})

Although slightly intractable for completely general computations, the previous formula for the (cyclo)syntomic first Chern class can be made more explicit on the subgroup of cyclotomic units,

In the context of regulators, the role of these cyclotomic units was highlighted by Deligne’s construction of a variation of mixed Hodge–Tate structure
\mathscr{L}\!\!\!\>\mathit{og}^{H}
on

[

[

(see also

\operatorname{Li}_{1}^{(p)}(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geqslant 1\\

whose special values at roots of unity were later proved to appear naturally as classes in weight one syntomic cohomology

Given the prominent role of roots of unity in the context of Habiro cohomology, the following result can be seen as an attempt to better understand the relation between polylogarithms and cyclotomic units.

Theorem C

(First
q
-polylogarithm as cyclosyntomic Chern class; see Theorem
4.25
)
.

Let
K
be a number field,
R
be the étale
\Z
-algebra
\mathscr{O}_{K}[\Delta_{K}^{-1}]
, and
d\geqslant 2
be an integer. For every root of unity

is given by

c_{1}^{\mathrm{CycSyn}}:1-\zeta\longmapsto-\operatorname{Li}_{1}^{(d)}([\zeta])_{q}\in\emph{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{CycSyn}}(R,\Z(1)^{(d)})

where

\operatorname{Li}_{1}^{(d)}(z)_{q}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geqslant 1\\
d\nmid k\end{subarray}}\frac{z^{k}}{[k]_{q}}

is the first
q
-polylogarithm seen as a convergent

-power series in a suitable sense (Section

is a natural sequence of Habiro lifts of the cyclotomic norms of

In particular, via the

We end this introduction by mentioning two further potential directions of research. First, it is possible to assemble the cyclosyntomic complexes of Definition
1.1
for various

into a nontrivial complex of abelian groups. The resulting cyclosyntomic complex computes extensions of Tate objects in a category of

-modules equipped with compatible

Moreover, we expect that the first cyclosyntomic Chern class constructed here should be part of a more general theory of higher Chern classes, which would relate the higher
K

-polylogarithms. While such a general theory seems currently out of reach, we expect that ongoing progress in the theory of Habiro cohomology may provide insight for further progress in this direction.

q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}[T])\cong\sum_{e|m}[e]_{q^{m/e}}\Z[T^{m/e},q]/(q^{m}-1)\subseteq\Z[T,q]/(q^{m}-1)
(
[

). Equivalently,

is naturally identified with the kernel of the operator

\mathbb{Z}[T,q]/(q^{m}-1)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[T,q]/(q^{m}-1),\quad f(T,q)\longmapsto(q-1)\nabla_{q}(f(T,q))

where
\nabla_{q}(f(T,q))
is the
q
-derivative of
f(T,q)
(see
[
32
, Theorem 4.27]
for a similar computation after
(q-1)
-completion).

Construction 2.9

(Frobenii and Verschiebungen)
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Given integers
m,m^{\prime}\geqslant 1
satisfying
m|m^{\prime}
, and
d:=\frac{m^{\prime}}{m}
, the big
q
-Witt vectors of

are equipped with natural
Frobenius and Verschiebung maps

F_{d}:q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)\longrightarrow q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)\quad\quad V_{d}:q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)\longrightarrow q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)

of
\Z[q]
-algebras and of
\Z[q]
-modules, respectively (
[

, proof of Lemma 2.9]
). These are characterised by their
\Z[q]
-linearity and compatibility with the Frobenius and Verschiebung maps on big Witt vectors (Construction
2.3
). They satisfy the relations
F_{d}\circ V_{d}=d
and
V_{d}\circ F_{d}=[d]_{q^{m}}
.

Remark 2.10
.

The big
q
-Witt vectors
q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)
of a commutative ring
R
, together with their Frobenius and Verschiebung maps, satisfy a universal property: namely, the system of
\Z[q]
-algebras
(q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R))_{m\geqslant 1}
is the initial
q
-FV-system over
R
(
[
32
, Definition 2.8 and Lemma 2.9]
). Also note that for every integer
m\geqslant 1
, we have
q^{m}-1\in\mathbb{I}_{m}
(Definition
2.7
(i) for
e=m
), so that
q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)
is a commutative
\Z[q]/(q^{m}-1)
-algebra.

Warning 2.11
.

Despite the name, big
q
-Witt vectors are not a
q
-deformation of big Witt vectors,
i.e.
, the natural morphism of commutative rings
\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)\rightarrow q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)/(q-1)
is in general not an isomorphism (
[
32
, Remark 2.11]
).

Remark 2.12
.

The restriction maps
R_{m^{\prime}/m}:\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)\rightarrow\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)
of Definition
2.2
do not extend to morphisms of commutative
\Z[q]
-algebras

for general commutative rings

(

, 2.14]
). In particular, it is not possible to define the ring of big

-Witt vectors

. Instead, in light of Remark

between

-Witt vectors and Habiro rings, we will rather consider the limit

along the Frobenius maps of Construction

in this paper, which we will typically refer to as

(Section

).

In

Construction 2.13

(Cyclotomic

Given integers

6

Lemma 2.16

(

-Dwork’s lemma)
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and
m\geqslant 1
be an integer. Assume that for every prime number
p
dividing
m
, there exists a ring homomorphism
\varphi_{p}:R\rightarrow R/p^{v_{p}(m)}R
such that
\varphi_{p}(x)\equiv x^{p}
modulo
pR
. Then for every element
(c_{e}(q))_{e|m}\in\prod_{e|m}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

(c_{e}(q))_{e|m}\in\prod_{e|m}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)
is in the image of the
q
-ghost map
q
-
\mathrm{gh}
;

(2)

there exists a lift
(\tilde{c}_{e}(q))_{e|m}\in\prod_{e|m}R[q]
of
(c_{e}(q))_{e|m}\in\prod_{e|m}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)
such that, for every prime number

(R/p^{v_{p}(m/e)})[q]

\varphi_{p}:R[q]\rightarrow(R/p^{v_{p}(m/e)})[q]

By construction, there is a commutative diagram of

-algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)[q]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{e|m}R[q]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{e|m}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{e|m}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)}
gh
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q\text{-gh}}

where the top horizontal map is induced by the ghost map of Section

Notation 2.18

Let

2.4
Norm maps on
q
-Witt vectors

In
[
1
]
, Angeltveit constructs multiplicative norm maps on big Witt vectors, as a natural generalisation of the Teichmüller lift. In this subsection, we prove that big
q
-Witt vectors similarly admit multiplicative norm maps
\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}:q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)\rightarrow q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)
(Proposition
2.20
).

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and
m,m^{\prime}\geqslant 1
be integers satisfying
m|m^{\prime}
. Assume that for every prime number
p
dividing
m
, there exists a ring homomorphism
\varphi_{p}:R\rightarrow R/p^{v_{p}(m^{\prime})}R
such that
\varphi_{p}\equiv x^{p}
modulo
pR
. Then for every element
c\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)
with
q
-ghost coordinates
(c_{e}(q))_{e|m}
,
the element

\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}((c_{e}(q))_{e|m}):=\big(c_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[e,m]}}\big)_{e|m^{\prime}}\in\prod_{e|m^{\prime}}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)

is in the image of the
q
-ghost map
q\text{-}\mathrm{gh}:q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)\rightarrow\prod_{e|m^{\prime}}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)
.

Proof.

First note that the morphism of multiplicative monoids

\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}:\prod_{e|m}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)\rightarrow\prod_{e|m^{\prime}}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)

is well-defined, because
\Phi_{e}(q)
divides
\Phi_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})
in the commutative ring
\Z[q]
. Moreover, for any integers
m,m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\geqslant 1
satisfying
m|m^{\prime}|m^{\prime\prime}
, we have the equality

\Pi_{m^{\prime\prime}/m^{\prime}}\circ\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}=\Pi_{m^{\prime\prime}/m}.

Indeed, this is a consequence of the series of equalities

c_{((e,m^{\prime}),m)}\big(q^{\frac{(e,m^{\prime})}{((e,m^{\prime}),m)}\cdot\frac{e}{(e,m^{\prime})}}\big)^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[(e,m^{\prime}),m]}\cdot\frac{m^{\prime\prime}}{[e,m^{\prime}]}}=c_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[(e,m^{\prime}),m]}\cdot\frac{m^{\prime\prime}}{[e,m^{\prime}]}}=c_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime\prime}}{[e,m]}}

where the equality
\tfrac{m^{\prime}}{[(e,m^{\prime}),m]\cdot[e,m^{\prime}]}=\tfrac{1}{[e,m]}
follows from the fact that for any integers
a,b,c\geqslant 0
satisfying
c\leqslant b
, we have the equality

b-\max(\min(a,b),c)-\max(a,b)=-\max(a,c)
.

In particular, it suffices to prove the desired claim when
m^{\prime}/m
is a prime number, which we assume from now on.

Let
(c_{e}(q))_{e|m}\in\prod_{e|m}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)
be an element in the image of the
q
-ghost map. We use the
q
-Dwork lemma (Lemma
2.16
) to prove that
\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}((c_{e}(q))_{e|m})\in\prod_{e|m^{\prime}}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)
is in the image of the
q
-ghost map. Let
(\tilde{c}_{e}(q))_{e|m}\in\prod_{e|m}R[q]
be a lift of
(c_{e}(q))_{e|m}\in\prod_{e|m}R[q]/\Phi_{e}(q)
such that, for every prime number
p
and every integer
e\geqslant 1
satisfying
pe|m
, we have
\tilde{c}_{e}(q)\equiv\varphi_{p}(\tilde{c}_{pe}(q))
in
(R/p^{v_{p}(m/e)})[q]
. It then suffices to prove that, for every prime number
p
and every integer
e\geqslant 1
satisfying
pe|m^{\prime}
, we have
\varphi_{p}\big(\tilde{c}_{(pe,m)}(q^{\frac{pe}{(pe,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[pe,m]}}\big)\equiv\tilde{c}_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[e,m]}}
in
(R/p^{v_{p}(m^{\prime}/e)})[q]
. We distinguish several cases.

Assume first that
v_{p}(e)>v_{p}(m)
. Because
m^{\prime}/m
is a prime number, this implies that
m^{\prime}/m=p
, so the condition
pe|m^{\prime}
can not be satisfied in this case,
and the desired statement is vacuously true.

Assume now that
v_{p}(e)=v_{p}(m)
. If
v_{p}(e)=v_{p}(m^{\prime})
, then
v_{p}(m^{\prime}/e)=0
and the desired statement is again vacuously true, so we assume that
v_{p}(e)=v_{p}(m)<v_{p}(m^{\prime})
. Because
m^{\prime}/m
is a prime number, this implies that
m^{\prime}=pm
and that
v_{p}(m^{\prime}/e)=1
. The desired statement then follows from the series of equalities

\varphi_{p}\big(\tilde{c}_{(pe,m)}(q^{\frac{pe}{(pe,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[pe,m]}}\big)=\varphi_{p}\big(\tilde{c}_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{pe}{(e,m)}})\big)^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{p\cdot[e,m]}}\equiv\tilde{c}_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{p\cdot\frac{m^{\prime}}{p\cdot[e,m]}}=\tilde{c}_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[e,m]}}

where the congruence holds modulo
p
, using that
\varphi_{p}:R\rightarrow R/p^{v_{p}(m^{\prime})}R
is a lift of Frobenius.

Assume finally that
v_{p}(e)<v_{p}(m)
. In this case, the desired statement follows similarly from the series of equalities

\varphi_{p}\big(\tilde{c}_{(pe,m)}(q^{\frac{pe}{(pe,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[pe,m]}}\big)=\varphi_{p}\big(\tilde{c}_{p\cdot(e,m)}(q^{\frac{pe}{p\cdot(e,m)}})\big)^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[e,m]}}\equiv\tilde{c}_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[e,m]}}=\tilde{c}_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[e,m]}}

where the congruence holds modulo
p^{v_{p}(m^{\prime}/e)}
, as a consequence of the assumption on
(\tilde{c}_{e}(q))_{e|m}
.
More precisely, either
m^{\prime}/m
is different from
p
, in which case
v_{p}(m^{\prime}/e)=v_{p}(m/(e,m))
and this is indeed a consequence of the assumption, or
m^{\prime}/m=p
, in which case we use the fact that
a\equiv b
modulo
p^{v_{p}(m/e)}
implies
a^{p}\equiv b^{p}
modulo
p^{v_{p}(m^{\prime}/e)}
.
∎

Proposition 2.20

(Cyclotomic norms)
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Then for any integers
m,m^{\prime}\geqslant 1
satisfying
m|m^{\prime}
, there exists a natural morphism of multiplicative monoids

\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}:q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)\longrightarrow q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)

given by
(c_{e}(q))_{e|m}\mapsto(c_{e}^{\prime}(q))_{e|m^{\prime}}:=\big(c_{(e,m)}(q^{\frac{e}{(e,m)}})^{\frac{m^{\prime}}{[e,m]}}\big)_{e|m^{\prime}}
in
q
-ghost coordinates. This morphism is uniquely determined by the fact that it is functorial in
R
and given by the previous formula on
q
-ghost coordinates.

Proof.

First assume that
R=\Z[T_{i},i\in I]
is a polynomial
\Z
-algebra (potentially in infinitely many variables). For every prime number
p
, let
\varphi_{p}:R\rightarrow R
be the lift of Frobenius given by
T_{i}\mapsto T_{i}^{p}
. The
\Z
-algebra
R
is flat, so the
q
-ghost maps of Definition
2.14
are injective (
[
32
, Lemma 2.23]
). The formula in
q
-ghost coordinates is moreover multiplicative by construction, so it suffices to prove that an element
(c_{e}(q))_{e|m}
in the image of the
q
-ghost map is sent to an element
(c_{e}^{\prime}(q))_{e|m^{\prime}}
in the image of the
q
-ghost map. Using the lifts of Frobenius
\varphi_{p}
, this is a consequence of Lemma
2.19
.

Assume now that
R
is a general commutative ring. Let
P\twoheadrightarrow R
be a surjective morphism of commutative rings, where
P
is a polynomial
\Z
-algebra. There is a natural diagram of commutative
\Z[q]
-algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}}

where the vertical maps are surjective (
[
32
, Corollary 2.29]
). In particular, given an element
c\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)
, any lift
\tilde{c}\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P)
induces an element
\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}(c)\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)
, which is compatible with the desired formula on
q
-ghost coordinates by the previous paragraph and the functoriality of
q
-ghost maps. Note that this construction a priori depends on the surjection
P\twoheadrightarrow R
and on the lift
\tilde{c}\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)
. To prove that it is independent of these choices, let
P_{1}\twoheadrightarrow R
,
\tilde{c}_{1}\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P_{1})
and
P_{2}\twoheadrightarrow R
,
\tilde{c}_{2}\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P_{2})
be two such choices. Let
P:=P_{1}\times P_{2}
. By
[
32
, Corollary 2.29]
, there is a natural isomorphism of commutative
\Z[q]
-algebras
q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P)\cong q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P_{1})\times q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P_{2})
. In particular, there exists an element
\tilde{c}\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(R)
mapping to
\tilde{c}_{1}\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P_{1})
and
\tilde{c}_{2}\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m}(P_{2})
via the natural projection maps, and the element
\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}(c)\in q\text{-}\mathbb{W}_{m^{\prime}}(R)
is well-defined.
∎

Remark 2.21
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. For any integers
m,m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\geqslant 1
satisfying

satisfy the identity
\Pi_{m^{\prime\prime}/m^{\prime}}\circ\Pi_{m^{\prime}/m}\circ\Pi_{m^{\prime\prime}/m}

2.19
. In particular, for any integers

, where the notation here is compatible with that of the Teichmüller lift (Notation

).

Definition 2.22

(Cyclotomic ring)
.

Let

Examples 2.23

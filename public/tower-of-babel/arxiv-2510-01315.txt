Neutral hydrogen in and around galaxies during the Epoch of Reionization
V. Gelli, C. Mason, A. Pallottini, K. E. Heintz, Z. Chen, V. D’Odorico, A. Ferrara, J. Fynbo, M. Kohandel, C. L. Pollock, C. Robinson, S. Salvadori

Abstract
Context. JWST spectra revealing Lyman- α \alpha (Ly α \alpha ) absorption in z ∼ 5 − 14 z\sim 5-14 galaxies offer a unique opportunity to probe the earliest stages of reionization. However, disentangling absorption by the increasingly neutral intergalactic medium (IGM) from that in galaxies’ interstellar and circumgalactic medium (ISM, CGM) remains challenging due to the poorly constrained nature of gas in and around galaxies at these redshifts. Aims. We use cosmological zoom-in simulations to characterize the distribution and redshift evolution of neutral hydrogen (HI) along sightlines to star-forming regions during the Epoch of Reionization, to interpret the contribution of local HI (ISM+CGM) to Ly α \alpha absorption in z > 5 z>5 galaxy spectra. Methods. We analyze ∼ \sim 100 z = 6 − 9.5 z=6-9.5 galaxies from the high resolution serra simulations, which have been shown to reproduce z > 6 z>6 cold ISM properties, generating mock sightlines from star formation peak for each galaxy. We explore the resulting sightline distribution of HI column densities ( N HI N_{\mathrm{HI}} ) and its variation with radius, halo mass, and redshift. Results. We find broad sightline variation in N HI N_{\mathrm{HI}} (0.5–1.5 dex) arising from complex ISM morphology driven by bursty star formation, with median, log ⁡ ( N HI / cm − 2 ) ≃ 21 \log({N_{\mathrm{HI}}}/{\rm cm}^{-2})\simeq 21 –22. Dense gas in galaxies’ ISM is the dominant origin of damped Ly α \alpha absorption (DLA) systems along sightlines towards star-forming regions, outweighing gas in the CGM, filaments, and proximate absorbers. Median N HI N_{\mathrm{HI}} increases with halo mass, scaling approximately with the virial radius, as expected due to larger potential wells and more extended CGM, but shows no significant redshift evolution at fixed halo mass. We suggest this implies N HI N_{\mathrm{HI}} distributions measured at the end of reionization may provide useful priors for interpreting IGM damping wings at higher redshifts. We investigate N HI > 10 22 {N_{\mathrm{HI}}}>10^{22} cm -2 sightlines to interpret strong z > 5 z>5 galaxy-DLA candidates identified with JWST. We find these trace dense, metal-enriched, neutral ISM within < 1 <1 kpc of massive halos ( M h ≳ 10 11 ​ M ⊙ M_{h}\gtrsim 10^{11}\,M_{\odot} ), a scenario that can be tested with higher-resolution spectroscopy.

Introduction

The scattering of Lyman-
\alpha
(Ly
\alpha

(see e.g.,

Ouchi et al. 2020

When the mean neutral hydrogen fraction,

\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}\;$}20-30\%

Excitingly, JWST has opened a new window on galaxies, and the impact of the IGM on their spectra, deep into the earliest stages of reionization at

Cosmological hydrodynamic simulations provide a powerful tool to investigate the expected properties of
\mbox{high-}z

There has been significant focus on using simulations to interpret DLAs observed in quasar spectra, which probe gas at varying transverse distances from foreground galaxies

Interpreting JWST observations requires a new approach: we need to investigate sightlines directly

N_{\mathrm{HI}}

z=3

To interpret JWST observations, it is now necessary to extend such an analysis to
z>5

In this paper, we use the
serra

Pallottini et al. 2022

\mbox{high-}z

\alpha

{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\gtrsim 10^{20}
cm

Where is the highest HI column density gas located? Does this gas primarily trace dense star-forming regions in the ISM, dense inflowing filaments or gas rich low-mass halos in the more extended CGM, or chance intersections through other galaxies? What column densities are expected, and how do they evolve with cosmic time and galaxy properties?

2
The simulations

We adopt the
serra
suite of high-resolution cosmological zoom-in simulations
(
Pallottini et al. 2022
)
. It is designed to model the evolution of typical Lyman-break galaxies during the Epoch of Reionization, successfully reproducing observed ISM properties
(
Kohandel et al. 2024
;
Kohandel et al. 2025
;
Gelli et al. 2025
;
Vallini et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
thanks to its treatment of non-equilibrium chemistry and high resolution (
\simeq 30\rm pc
) that resolves star-forming regions down to molecular cloud scales.

The simulations are initialized at
z=100
using the
music
code
(
Hahn & Abel 2011
)
to generate initial conditions within a cosmological volume of
(20\,{\rm Mpc}/h)^{3}
, adopting the cosmological parameters from
(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2014
)
.
After a target dark matter (DM) halo is selected, the simulations zoom-in by following the evolution of DM, gas, and stars, which are simulated using a customized version of the
ramses
Adaptive Mesh Refinement code
(
Teyssier 2002
)
, achieving a baryonic mass resolution of
1.2\times 10^{4}{\rm M}_{\odot}
and a spatial resolution reaching
\sim 30
pc in the zoom-in regions.

Radiative transfer is incorporated on-the-fly via
ramses-rt

(
Rosdahl et al. 2013
)
, where photons are divided into 5 energy bins in the range
6.0-24.59\rm\penalty\ eV
tracked separately. The cosmic UV background (UVB) contribution is neglected as the typical ISM densities are large enough to ensure self-shielding. In the EoR and down to
z\sim 5
, statistically, the ionization from the UVB is negligible for gas with density
n\gtrsim 10^{-2}

(
Rahmati et al. 2013
;
Chardin et al. 2015
)
as local sources dominate the photon budget.
The interaction between gas, dust, and photons is regulated using a non-equilibrium chemical network generated with
krome

(
Grassi et al. 2014
;
Pallottini et al. 2017b
)
. This includes the individual tracking of
\rm H,H^{+},H^{-},He,He^{+},He^{++},H_{2},H_{2}^{+}
and electrons, and a total of 48 reactions
(
Bovino et al. 2016
;
Pallottini et al. 2017a
)
.
The formation of molecular hydrogen, fundamental in regulating star formation, happens in both the gas-phase and on dust grain surfaces
(
Jura 1974
)
.
For the dust, we assume that the dust-to-gas mass ratio scales with metallicity. To account for the unresolved effects of the first stars and mini-halos, the initial gas metallicity is set to a floor value of
Z_{\text{floor}}=10^{-3}\rm Z_{\odot}

(
Wise et al. 2012
;
Pallottini et al. 2014a
)
.

Star formation follows a Schmidt-Kennicutt relation
(
Kennicutt 1998
)
, dependent on the molecular hydrogen gas density, and assumes a Kroupa
(
Kroupa 2001
)
initial mass function (IMF) for stellar particles. Stellar feedback mechanisms, including supernova explosions, winds, and radiation pressure, are modeled as described in
Pallottini et al. 2017a
. The energy inputs and chemical yields, which depend on stellar age and metallicity, are computed using
starburst99

(
Leitherer et al. 1999
)
with Padova stellar tracks
(
Bertelli et al. 1994
)
, covering a metallicity range of
Z_{\star}/Z_{\odot}=0.02-1.0
.

To investigate the properties and spatial distribution of dense neutral gas in the ISM and CGM of high-
z
galaxies, we select a sample of
\sim 100
central galaxies.
The sample consists of central galaxies selected to uniformly span the halo mass range
M_{h}=10^{9.5-11.5}M_{\odot}
and the redshift range
z=9.5
–6.0, in order to investigate the dependence of galaxy properties on both halo mass and redshift. These halos correspond to galaxies with UV magnitudes in the range
-22.5\lesssim M_{\rm UV}\lesssim-18
, in the range of JWST spectroscopy. Our focus here is on generally understanding the nature and evolution of dense HI in and around galaxies, so we leave a more detailed comparison to observations, accounting for selection functions, to future work.

serra
galaxies in these mass and redshift ranges have been shown to successfully reproduce several observed properties of
\mbox{high-}z
galaxies, such as the normalization and scatter of the mass metallicity relation
(
Pallottini et al. 2025
)
, the burstiness of the star formation histories
(
Pallottini & Ferrara 2023
;
Gelli et al. 2025
)
from JWST, as well as ISM and CGM gas tracers – [C
ii
]-
158\mu
m and [O
iii
]-
88\mu
m emission observed by ALMA
(
Pallottini et al. 2022
;
Kohandel et al. 2024
;
Vallini et al. 2025
)
. For these reasons, we expect the simulations to reasonably reproduce the distributions and abundances of neutral gas in the ISM and CGM of
\mbox{high-}z
galaxies.

In this work, we take advantage of this diverse sample of
\mbox{high-}z
galaxies to investigate how the distribution of neutral hydrogen depends on their properties and redshift evolution. A key step involves deriving column densities
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
in a manner analogous to observations of galaxies with JWST.
To do this, for each galaxy, we (i) extract a
100
kpc size box centered on each galaxy (to ensure that it includes the entire virial halo of the galaxies in the mass range considered), (ii) identify the location of peak of star formation by locating the center of mass of young stars (
<5
Myr), corresponding to the brightest UV-emitting region typically targeted by JWST, and (iii) generate sightlines originating from this point that extend radially to the edge of the simulation box in
\sim 1000
random directions. We then compute the column density along each sightline by integrating the neutral hydrogen density profile:
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}=\int_{\rm Sightline}n_{HI}(r)\,\mathrm{\rm d}r
, with
n_{HI}
from
krome
.

3.1
Neutral gas distribution around a

To get a first look at high-z galaxies’ complex structure and gas distribution, in Fig.
1

z

\rm 100\penalty\ kpc

Throughout this work, we refer to the ISM as all gas within 2.5 kpc of the central star-forming region, and to the CGM as all gas beyond this radius. The 2.5 kpc threshold is a conservative choice that ensures we include the entirety of the ISM, as the typical gas half-mass radius in high-redshift galaxies in the mass range considered is
\lesssim 1.5
kpc
(
Pallottini et al. 2019
;
Pallottini et al. 2022
;
Kohandel et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
.

Fig.
1
shows that the highest HI gas concentration is reached in the inner 2.5 kpc, where the column density can reach up to
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\sim 10^{23}\rm cm^{-2}

(similarly to the simulated galaxy
Althæa
, see
Behrens et al. 2019
, in particular Fig. 2 therein)
.
The insets zooming onto the ISM of
Amana
show that most of the stars are located in the densest central regions, where winds from massive stars and supernovae efficiently enrich the medium with metals up to
Z_{\rm gas}>0.1\rm\penalty\ Z_{\odot}

(
Pallottini et al. 2025
, see also)
. At the same time, mechanical feedback produces winds and shock-driven outflows, while metal-poor gas is continuously accreted from the surrounding medium. The interplay of these processes (mechanical and chemical feedback, frequent mergers, gas accretion) gives rise to bursty star formation
(
Pallottini & Ferrara 2023
)
and temporary quenching
(
Gelli et al. 2025
)
in early galaxies, which imprints a complex morphology to the neutral gas, distributed irregularly around the central disk and with column densities that can vary by up to
\sim 5
orders of magnitudes in the inner
\sim 2.5
kpc.

Moving outward from the innermost region, we see the neutral gas in the CGM is mostly being accreted onto the galaxy through multiple dense filaments, with
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\gtrsim 10^{20}\rm cm^{-2}
. Within and in proximity to these filaments, the gas is seen to condense in numerous clumps reaching
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\gtrsim 10^{22}\rm cm^{-2}
. With the exception of a few star-forming satellite galaxies (typically associated with metal enriched gas, see also
Gelli et al. 2020
), the vast majority of these dense HI clumps are not forming stars and are composed exclusively of pristine gas.
They are present in large numbers around
\mbox{high-}z
galaxies, with a few tens of clumps typically residing within the virial radius of
\sim 20
kpc for a
10^{11}{\rm M}_{\odot}
halo.
However, as we will show below, the probability of intersecting such a clump along the line-of-sight is low.

To assess the relative contributions of HI gas at different distances in affecting observations of high-redshift sources and their Ly
\alpha
absorption profiles, we analyze the spatial distribution of this intervening dense medium along narrow-beam sightlines targeting the central galaxy.
In the top panel of Fig.
2
, we show the radial profile of neutral hydrogen density for 3 independent sightlines targeting the central star forming region of
Amana
, centered on the peak of star formation of the central galaxy.
In the central
<100
pc regions, the density can reach up to
\gtrsim 10^{2}\rm cm^{-3}
, corresponding to the dense star-forming molecular clouds in the galaxy’s ISM. However, already within
\lesssim 1
kpc the profiles can significantly differ from one another. Depending on whether a sightline intercepts a low-density channel cleared by supernovae feedback, inflowing gas accreting through a filament or other star-forming regions, the density can vary by up to
\sim 7
dex.
We note, numerical relaxation may also displace star particles from their birth clouds, which could also contribute to a wide range of HI densities in the inner regions.
All profiles tend to drop to
<10^{-3}\rm cm^{-3}
as we move towards the outer CGM and beyond the virial radius, but still with strong variation between different sightlines.
As a consequence, the inferred neutral hydrogen column densities show a strong sightline dependency and can vary by several orders of magnitude for the same galaxy depending on the observing angle.

To quantify the column density sightline variability, we draw 3000 random line-of-sight directions from the central galaxy and plot the probability distribution of
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
in the bottom panel of Fig.
2
(blue histogram).
The distribution is broad, varying from
10^{19}

10^{23.5}\rm cm^{-2}
, and with median
10^{21.5}\rm cm^{-2}
.
The width of the distribution, spanning over
\gtrsim 3

This may be driven by the complex gas morphology in both the ISM, governed by star formation–related processes (stellar feedback driving outflows, frequent merger events), and the CGM, characterized by filamentary and clumpy structures.

To understand where the gas driving these column densities is predominantly located, we separate the contributions of the ISM and CGM to the inferred column densities. We do this by recalculating
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
for the same 3000 sightlines but excluding the inner
2.5

The obtained PDF (shown in lilac) is still wide, highlighting how the CGM itself can cause variations of
>4

{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
, ranging from
10^{17.5}
to
10^{21.5}\rm cm^{-2}
, and median at
10^{18.4}\rm cm^{-2}

{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}>10^{21}\rm cm^{-2}
in the CGM is
\sim 2\%

Comparing the

Interestingly, we find our example HI column density distribution is very similar to that derived from observations of
z\sim 2-6
GRB afterglows
(
Tanvir et al. 2019
)
.
This suggests that
\mbox{high-}z
galaxy DLAs probe similar star-forming regions as lower-

z
galaxy sightlines appear to trace, on average, gas distributions similar to those revealed by GRB-DLAs.
We note the

distribution inferred for
Amana
shows a higher fraction of sightlines with
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}\;$}10^{22.5}

.
Amana

These differences between the simulated and inferred GRB-DLA distribuion may be related to the resolution limit of our simulations that cannot spatially resolve HII regions
(
Pallottini et al. 2019
;
Decataldo et al. 2019
)
and therefore possibly underestimate the abundance of low-density channels and overestimate high-density regions. We discuss this further in Section

We next extend our analysis to a larger sample of galaxies across the epoch of reionization to determine whether these findings hold for different galaxy properties and redshifts.

3.2
What is the origin of high-z galaxy-DLAs?

The analysis of
Amana
revealed that the local ISM is the dominant contributor to high neutral hydrogen column densities, with its complex morphology leading to a high sightline-to-sightline variation. We now proceed to assess whether this holds across a broader sample, and how the inferred column densities depend on galaxies’ properties and redshift. We do this by studying
\sim 100

serra

z=6-9.5
and halo masses in the range
M_{h}=10^{9.5}-10^{11.5}M_{\odot}

For every galaxy, we evaluate the distribution of

The increasing trend with halo mass supports the picture in which more massive halos, with their deeper potential wells, are more efficient at accreting and retaining gas from the cosmic web; at the same time, their larger virial radii naturally increase the path length through which neutral gas is integrated, further boosting the column densities.

with
\mathrm{SFR}_{3}
and

N_{\mathrm{HI}}
and ISM metallicity. This may arise from more massive halos halos — on average with higher

To quantify the relative importance of dense neutral gas located at different distances from the emitting galaxy, we vary the lower integration limit of
r_{\rm min}
for each sightline (where

The figure highlights multiple key trends. Across all galaxies in our sample, the central
\lesssim 1

of

, who found that
\gtrsim 60\%
of galaxies in the JWST-PRIMAL sample at

The color coding in Fig.

sightlines.
The mass dependence is particularly evident also in the CGM component, where cold gas accretion through filaments is primarily regulated by the depth of the gravitational potential.

When rescaled by virial radius, we find the profiles of all halos converge more closely, indicating self-similarity in the gas density distribution
(
Pallottini et al. 2014b
;
Rahmati et al. 2015
;
Stern et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
.

The observed mass dependence has important implications for observations. Since lower mass halos are more abundant in the early universe, we expect the HI covering fractions of the observed galaxy population to decrease on average. This trend could help interpret recent results from low-ionization absorption lines (LIS) studies at
z\sim 3-7
by
Pahl et al. 2023
and
Glazer et al. 2025
, who derived decreasing HI covering fractions with increasing redshift, which may reflect the lower average halo masses at earlier times. As we discuss in in Sec.
4.2
, simple clustering analyses may be able to test this picture, as more massive halos are expected to have a higher number of close companions.

We note, throughout the paper, all
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
estimates are derived using gas within
\lesssim 50
kpc of galaxies. However, intervening dense gas located in clumps and galaxies at larger distances could also contribute to the observed column densities as proximate absorbers
(
Hennawi & Prochaska 2007
;
Christensen et al. 2023
;
Davies et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
. We tested the potential impact of such absorbers by extrapolating the
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
distribution into the regions outside the CGM (
r>40
kpc) over a redshift range of
\Delta z\sim 0.2
, where absorbers could produce damping wing absorption around rest-frame Ly
\alpha
wavelengths. We find fewer than
10\%
of sightlines exceed
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}>10^{21}\rm{cm}^{-2}
. This represents a very conservative upper limit, as the estimate is based on extrapolation of very dense environments where we selected our halos. We therefore expect that high column density absorption from intervening gas at larger distances is very uncommon and does not affect our conclusions.

In the next section, we examine how the neutral gas distribution and column densities around the halos in our sample evolve with redshift.

3.3
Redshift evolution

Observationally, most studies suggest minimal redshift evolution in HI column density distributions
(
Reddy et al. 2016
;
Umeda et al. 2024
;
Mason et al. 2025
;
Heintz et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, but a potential decrease in HI covering fractions at higher redshifts
(
Pahl et al. 2020
;
Glazer et al. 2025
)
. However, the extremely high levels of scatter in the inferred
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distributions from large samples of galaxies makes it challenging to identify and interpret these trends. In principle, higher redshifts halos are expected to have higher gas densities, which could favor increased column densities. Conversely, if massive DM halos are the primary contributors to large
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
, their declining abundance at higher redshifts may lead to lower average column densities.

To investigate this evolution, we analyze how the expected HI column densities in
serra
galaxies change from
z\sim 9.5
to
6.5
. In Fig.
6
, we show galaxies with similar halo masses of
M_{h}\sim 10^{11}{\rm M}_{\odot}
at different redshifts. From the maps we see no significant redshift evolution in the overall distribution of HI within
\lesssim 50
kpc, with the central galaxy residing at the intersection of at least multiple filaments and reaching high column densities in the central ISM (
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}>10^{21}\rm cm^{-2}
). Dense neutral gas in the CGM is more abundant at higher redshifts, while at lower redshifts the CGM is instead characterized by more extended, lower-density regions.

To quantify the evolution of ISM and CGM column densities, Figure
7
shows the median of the
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
probability distribution function as a function of redshift for the entire galaxy sample also analysed in the previous sections. The filled and empty points identify the two components, total (ISM+CGM) and CGM only respectively (defined as in Figure

We divide the galaxies in two halo mass bins to highlight the mass dependence, and the solid line shows a linear fit of the evolution in each subsample to guide the eye.

At all epochs, the typical total median column densities span
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\sim 10^{21.0}-10^{22.5}\rm cm^{-2}
, with no significant redshift evolution – only a mild increase scaling as
(1+z)^{0.3}
.
The color coding once again reveals that more massive galaxies tend to have higher median
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
, indicating that halo mass dependence dominates over redshift evolution at fixed mass. The CGM-only column densities are characterised by much lower values and similar scatter

.

We also emphasize that, as seen in Sec.

.

4
Discussion

Our results indicate that galaxy-DLAs at
z>6
primarily arise in the dense gas in the ISM of
\mbox{high-}z
galaxies, with large sightline variation towards star-forming regions due to inhomogeneous gas distributions in the ISM and CGM. In this section we discuss the implications of this broad sightline variance for interpreting high-redshift spectra in the context of reionization (Section
4.1
); the nature of extremely strong DLAs and predictions for their environments and metallicity (Section
4.2
); and discuss the role of evolution in the ISM and CGM during the epoch of reionization (Section
4.3
).

4.1
The implications of sightline variance in HI distributions

In Section
3.1
we showed that sightlines towards star-forming regions at
z\sim 6-9.5
exhibit a broad distribution of HI column densities due to inhomogeneous gas distributions in the ISM and CGM. We find the highest
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
absorbers (
>10^{22}
cm
-2
) arise in the inner ISM (
<1
kpc). Our results suggest that the halo mass is the primary driver of the HI gas distribution and derived column density distribution around galaxies.

These findings are qualitatively consistent with previous studies using cosmological simulations to analyze HI around galaxies at lower redshifts (
z<6
).
Many previous works have focused on interpreting absorption in background quasar spectra. For example,
Faucher-Giguère et al. 2015
, and more recently
Tortora et al. 2024
, found a steep decrease in the abundance of Lyman limit systems (
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\gtrsim 10^{17}
cm
-2
) with increasing radius from the center of galaxies in the
fire
simulations at
z\sim 0-6
, and a higher covering fraction of HI for more massive halos (which we will discuss more below), consistent with our results. Similar qualitative trends are also found in the
eagle
simulations
(
Rahmati & Schaye 2014
;
Rahmati et al. 2015
)
.
Our results are also consistent with previous work examining sightlines directly towards star-forming regions.

Pontzen et al. 2010
investigated the origin of GRB afterglow HI absorption at
2<z<4
, simulating sightlines emerging from galaxies, similar to our approach. Their recovered
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distribution is very similar to ours, with a predicted median column density of
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}=10^{21.5}\rm cm^{-2}
, comparable with the median we find in
serra
for
M_{h}\sim 10^{9.5}-10^{11.5}{\rm M}_{\odot}
halos.
Based on their recovered
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distribution, they inferred GRB-DLAs arise within
\lesssim 1
kpc from the center of the halo.
High resolution simulations of gas around star-forming regions focused on understanding the escape of Lyman continuum and Lyman-
\alpha
photons
(
Ma et al. 2020
;
Kimm et al. 2019
;
Kimm et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
have also found similarly broad
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distributions, peaked around
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}=10^{20-22}\rm cm^{-2}
, arising from the interplay of feedback in dense star-forming clouds.
Large sightline variance in the HI distribution in the ISM is also predicted to play a major role in driving the observed variance in Ly
\alpha
emission line strengths and shapes
(
Smith et al. 2019
;
Blaizot et al. 2023
;
Kimm et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, consistent with an observed anti-correlation between Ly
\alpha
equivalent width and the HI covering fraction derived by
Reddy et al. 2022
.

Nevertheless we find a high covering fraction,
\gtrsim 80-90\%
of DLAs (
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}>10^{20.3}
cm
-2
) towards our halos, which increases with increasing halo mass. This is consistent with observations at
z\sim 2-3
finding a median
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\approx 10^{20.3}
cm
-2
and covering fraction
\approx 90
%
(
Reddy et al. 2016
)
, as well as recent JWST observations implying median
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\approx 10^{20-21}
cm
-2
in
z\sim 5-14
galaxies
(
Mason et al. 2025
)
. Such a high covering factor of high column density neutral gas is also consistent with low inferred Lyman continuum escape fraction at all redshifts
(
Steidel et al. 2016
;
Begley et al. 2022
;
Kreilgaard et al. 2024
, with average escape fractions
\lesssim 10
% in star-forming galaxies, e.g.,)
.

In Figure
5
we have shown that the covering fraction of dense HI increases with increasing halo mass. Observations of low ionization interstellar (LIS) metal absorption lines have demonstrated a decline in line equivalent width at fixed
M_{\mathrm{UV}}
with increasing redshift, which may be interpreted as a decrease in the covering fraction of neutral gas
(
Jones et al. 2013
;
Du et al. 2018
;
Pahl et al. 2020
;
Glazer et al. 2025
, e.g,.)
. Due to rising baryon accretion rates, galaxies at fixed
M_{\mathrm{UV}}
are expected to trace lower mass halos with increasing redshifts
(
Mason et al. 2015
;
Behroozi & Silk 2015
, e.g.,)
. Our results thus imply that a decrease in covering fraction may be expected at higher redshifts: as we observe increasingly lower mass halos which are less able to retain high density HI.
We note that because we focus on zoom-in simulations of central galaxies, our sample is likely biased toward relatively massive systems (
M_{\ast}\gtrsim 10^{9.5}\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}
) and their satellites. Future work could extend this analysis to include more isolated, lower mass, galaxies, to better capture the impact of local environment on HI gas distributions.

Redshift evolution of the medians of the column densities distribution of

kpc).

We also note the spatial resolution of our simulations, limited to
\sim
30 pc (comparable to giant molecular clouds), will not fully capture the complex, multi-phase, substructure of the ISM.
For example, observations of Ly
\alpha
emission with
\sim 100-200
km/s red peak velocity offsets (implying
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\sim 10^{19-20}
cm
-2
coincident with DLA absorption troughs implying
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\sim 10^{20-22}
cm
-2
suggest a complex, multi-phase ISM structure
(
Hu et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
which is not resolved by galaxy-scale hydrodynamic simulations
(
Gronke et al. 2017
, see discussion by)
. It is difficult for Ly
\alpha
to escape when molecular clouds are unresolved in simulations
(
Behrens et al. 2019
)
.
In particular, without sufficient resolution, low density channels carved by stellar feedback, such as ionizing radiation winds and supernovae, are smoothed over
(
Decataldo et al. 2020
;
Di Mascia et al. 2025
)
, thus potentially underestimating the presence of low-column-density sightlines and overestimating the abundance of high column density sightlines. As a result, while the peak of the
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
distribution is robustly set by the average ISM densities, we may be missing a low-
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
tail – we find no sightlines through the ISM with
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}<10^{17}\rm cm^{-2}
. This component will also be important for enabling the escape of Lyman Continuum photons.

The
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distribution is also sensitive to feedback prescriptions
(
Rahmati et al. 2015
;
Faucher-Giguère et al. 2015
;
Bennett & Sijacki 2020
)
, with particular recent attention on the impact of cosmic ray (CR) feedback
(
Girichidis et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. Our simulations include CR feedback only via ionization
(
Pallottini et al. 2017a
)
, however, cosmic rays can provide non-thermal pressure, which can drive smooth, slow gas outflows, resulting in a more uniform and extended HI distribution in the ISM and CGM
(
Girichidis et al. 2018
)
.
Gronke et al. 2018
and
Kimm et al. 2025
have shown that this reduces the number of very low and high
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
sightlines, producing slightly narrower
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distributions, with
\sim 1\,
dex lower median
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
compared to simulations without CR-driven outflows, and that this may also help produce more realistic Ly
\alpha
emission properties.
Overall, pushing toward more realistic, higher-resolution simulations will be important for understanding more complex ISM structures on sub-parsec scales. However, because of the expected increase of density, is difficult to properly resolve HII regions even in simulations focusing their computational power on single molecular clouds
(
Decataldo et al. 2019
;
Decataldo et al. 2020
)
, so progress will likely require a multi-scale approach. While there is uncertainty in the exact form of the
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distribution, our conclusion that it is broad remains unchanged.

A broad
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distribution has important implications for interpreting galaxy damping wings at
z>5
. It implies that individual galaxies may have a significant diversity in damping wing profiles due to the combination of inhomogeneous HI distributions in the ISM, CGM and IGM, motivating dedicated fits to individual galaxy spectra which can marginalise over the expected variance.
We find minimal evolution in the local
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distribution with redshift at fixed halo mass as we discuss in Section
4.3
. This suggests it may be possible to establish typical
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distributions for star-forming galaxies at the end of reionization, which can be used as priors for
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
at higher redshifts when the IGM damping wing becomes significant, similar to the approaches used in interpreting the decline in Ly
\alpha
emission at
z\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}\;$}6

(
Schenker et al. 2014
;
Treu et al. 2013
;
Mason et al. 2018
;
Tang et al. 2024b
;
Tang et al. 2024a
;
Nakane et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.
Future deep moderate resolution spectroscopy (
R\gtrsim 1000
) with JWST and ELT will enable a better separation of IGM and local HI damping wings (see Section
4.2
), enabling this scenario to be tested more robustly in observations.
Furthermore, we note the sightline variance in
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
implies conversions from
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
to global HI properties (e.g. gas mass) for individual galaxies may be subject to large systematic scatter, and may be better recovered with sample averages.

Finally, we note that JWST spectroscopy collects light through an aperture, integrating emission from nearby star-forming regions and all the gas in their foreground (
0.20^{\prime\prime}\times 0.46^{\prime\prime}
for NIRSpec), whereas throughout the paper we compute column densities using narrow beams tracing the line of sight to star-forming regions.
To test the impact of this, we also compute column densities within cylindrical apertures of diameter
\sim 0.4^{\prime\prime}
(
\sim 2
kpc at
z=8
), measuring the mean gas density across the entire aperture.
We find that the resulting column density distributions are not significantly affected by using a wider aperture, the main effect being that the typical standard deviations of the
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distribution are lower, going from an typical value of
\sigma_{\log{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}}\sim 0.8
to
\sim 0.4
.
We note this is a conservative estimate, as we are averaging equally all the gas within the aperture. In reality, the observed spectrum will be dominated by emission (and thus absorption) from the peak of the star-forming region, and the observed absorbing column density would be closer to a luminosity-weighted average over the aperture – more similar to our narrow beam approach. Moreover, at
z>6
typical UV sizes of galaxies have been shown to be smaller than a single MSA shutter
(
Yang et al. 2022
;
Morishita et al. 2024
,
R_{\mathrm{eff}}\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{<}}\;$}400\,
pc,
\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{<}}\;$}0.08
arcsec, e.g.,)
, ensuring that all the emission from the star forming regions should be captured for sources well-centered in the MSA. Therefore, the impact of spatial apertures does not impact our conclusions.

4.2
The nature of extremely strong galaxy-DLAs and predictions for observations

Recent JWST observations have uncovered a small fraction of
z\gtrsim 5
galaxies with extremely strong Ly
\alpha
break turnovers. If interpreted as Ly
\alpha
damping, albeit with uncertainties in UV continuum emission modelling, these may correspond to column densities
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}\;$}10^{22}
cm
-2

(
Hsiao et al. 2023
;
Heintz et al. 2024a
;
Chen et al. 2024
;
Mason et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. Such high column densities are comparable to those seen in the strongest GRB-DLAs at
z\sim 2-6

(
Jakobsson et al. 2006
;
Watson et al. 2006
;
Tanvir et al. 2019
;
Saccardi et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
and so-called “Extremely Strong” DLAs (esDLAs) in QSO spectra
(e.g.,
Noterdaeme et al. 2015
;
Ranjan et al. 2020
;
Krogager et al. 2024
, which have been shown to have similar metallicities and kinematics as GRB-DLAs at
z\sim 2
)
, both of which are expected to trace gas in the inner ISM
(
Prochaska et al. 2007
;
Pontzen et al. 2010
,
<1\,\rm kpc
,)
.
Our results suggest these high column densities inferred at
z>5
also arise in the ISM (
\lesssim 500
pc, see Figure
5
). Thus, we consider it likely that the extremely strong galaxy-DLAs detected with JWST are also mostly associated with dense gas in the inner ISM. Furthermore, we find
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}\;$}10^{22}
cm
-2
DLAs are predicted to be more common for more massive halos:
\sim 30\%
of sightlines to
M_{h}\gtrsim 10^{11}\,\rm M_{\odot}
halos intersecting such high column density gas, compared to
<10\%
for
M_{h}\lesssim 10^{10.5}\,\rm M_{\odot}
halos.

If the JWST-detected strong galaxy-DLAs trace the inner ISM of massive halos, this should provide testable predictions for the environment of these sources and their spectra.
High-mass halos typically reside in the densest regions of the cosmic web, at the intersection of multiple filaments. These environments enable efficient gas and dark matter accretion, leading to frequent mergers, especially at high redshift. As a result, galaxies in more massive halos are expected to be surrounded by a larger number of satellites and companion galaxies. The number of nearby galaxies could therefore serve as a tracer of halo mass, potentially providing a way to test its correlation with
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}
.

We analyze the number of neighboring galaxies within
\lesssim 100
kpc of each system in the
serra
sample. We define neighbors as galaxies located at distances between
5-50
kpc and brighter than
M_{\rm UV}\lesssim-19
, ensuring their detectability in JWST surveys
(
Gelli et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
.
We find that stronger absorbers are more likely to have more neighboring galaxies: all galaxies with more than 4 neighbors have a median
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}>10^{21.5}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-2}
.

Interestingly, recent JWST results by
Chen et al. 2024
report the discovery of three strong DLA candidates at
z\sim 8
in a protocluster of more than ten spectroscopically confirmed galaxies spanning
\lesssim 60
kpc, estimated to have a halo mass
>4\times 10^{11}\,\rm M_{\odot}

(
Morishita et al. 2023
)
. By estimating
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
from photometry,
Witten et al. 2025
find that the galaxies in this protocluster with the highest inferred
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
are in a small region of the field,
\lesssim 5\,{\rm kpc}
, consistent with our picture that high HI column densities arise in the inner regions of massive halos. Similarly,
Mason et al. 2025
find that five out of six robust DLA candidates at
z\sim 5.5-6
in GOODS-S have spectroscopically confirmed close neighbors within
\lesssim 500
kpc, suggesting that strong DLAs trace the most massive halos.
Currently, observational constraints are limited by the completeness of JWST spectroscopy. More complete spectroscopy around strong DLA candidates should be able to systematically test the connection between the prevalence of high column density systems and galaxy halo mass.

Another key prediction for the ISM origin of strong DLAs is the presence of metal absorption lines. In Sec.
3.1
, we have shown the metal distribution is highly inhomogeneous in the ISM: gas is efficiently enriched by ongoing star formation and metals are driven outwards by SN outflows, while concurrent accretion of pristine neutral hydrogen from the CGM dilutes the gas inflowing through filaments.
However, in the immediate vicinity of the star-forming regions (
\lesssim 500
pc), the metallicity always exceeds
Z\gtrsim 0.01\rm Z_{\odot}
and can even reach almost solar values. Beyond
\sim 5\,{\rm kpc}
, metallicity drops to the simulation floor (
Z=10^{-3}\rm Z_{\odot}
) and the filamentary and clumpy CGM structures can be considered entirely pristine.

This stark contrast between the inner ISM and CGM, where the former is enriched and the latter is considerably lower metallicity, has direct implications for the nature of strong DLAs observed at high redshift. If the observed
\mbox{high-}z
DLAs predominantly trace the dense gas in the ISM star-forming regions, we expect the presence of a non-negligible amount of metals, traced by low ionization metal absorption lines at redshifts consistent with the systemic redshift of the galaxy.
This would be consistent with GRB-DLAs and extremely strong QSO-DLAs, which have been shown to trace higher metallicity gas than typical QSO-DLAs
(
Prochaska et al. 2007
;
Ranjan et al. 2020
)
and are thought to trace the inner ISM. Interestingly, especially high, near to solar, metallicities have been inferred for the highest column density GRB-DLAs with
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}>10^{22}
cm
-2

(
Prochaska et al. 2009
;
Krühler et al. 2013
)
.

High-resolution spectra could directly test this for high-
z
galaxies by detecting metal absorption lines. Low ionization interstellar absorption lines have been detected in correspondence with high density HI in
z\sim 2-5
galaxies
(
Reddy et al. 2016
;
Pahl et al. 2023
, e.g.)
, indicating that metal enriched gas is more abundant for galaxies with larger HI covering fractions, and our predictions shows a similar trend is expected beyond
z>6
.
We estimate line-of-sight metallicity for high column density sightlines with
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}>10^{22}
cm
-2
in our sample, finding that the average mass-weighted gas metallicity is
Z\gtrsim 0.2\penalty\ \rm Z_{\odot}
. Assuming solar abundance ratios, this corresponds to column densities of the key elements like C, Si, O, and Fe of
14\lesssim\log N_{X}/\rm cm^{-2}\lesssim 17
. Such high column densities should result in significant absorption from low ionization lines such as CII, SiII, OI, FeII, which are expected to be saturated and with equivalent widths of the order of
\rm EW=1-5\penalty\ \textrm{A\kern-5.59721pt\raisebox{2.58334pt}{${}^{\circ}$}}

(
de Ugarte Postigo et al. 2012
, e.g.,)
. The prospects for obtaining constraints on these absorption lines in individual
z\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}\;$}6
galaxies are promising with JWST.
\rm EW\approx 1-5\penalty\ \textrm{A\kern-5.59721pt\raisebox{2.58334pt}{${}^{\circ}$}}
absorption lines have been detected in both stacked
R\gtrsim 1000

z>6
NIRSpec spectra
(
Glazer et al. 2025
)
, and individually in a handful of intrinsically bright and gravitationally lensed sources
(
Boyett et al. 2023
;
Topping et al. 2024
;
Topping et al. 2025a
)
.
Deep
R\gtrsim 1000
resolution spectroscopy of
z\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}\;$}6
sources with JWST should thus be able to confirm if strong galaxy-DLAs trace the inner ISM.

A potential caveat of our analysis concerns the modeling of H
2
formation and the consequent relative abundance of HI and H
2
in the ISM, which introduces uncertainty in the abundance of the highest
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
(
\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}\;$}10^{22}
cm
-2
) sightlines. Due to self-shielding from UV photons, at such high column densities, a high fraction of HI is predicted to be converted to molecular hydrogen
(
Schaye 2001a
;
Gurman et al. 2025
)
. The relative ratio of H
2
to HI depends on the local UV radiation (which photo-dissociates H
2
) and the dust mass and grain size (which catalyses H
2
formation). In
serra
the catalysis of H
2
on dust is independent of the grain size
(
Jura 1974
)
; in principle a larger grain size distribution
(
Markov et al. 2025
;
McKinney et al. 2025
, as recently suggested at high redshift, e.g.,)
may impact the efficiency of H
2
formation
(
Soliman et al. 2024
, see e.g.,)
. We leave further exploration of this to future work.

4.3
Redshift evolution in the ISM and CGM

In Section
3.3
) we found an increase in the median CGM (
>2.5
kpc) column density, scaling approximately with cosmic density,
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\sim(1+z)^{2}
, over
z\sim 6-10
.
This is consistent with findings from other simulations which have found an increase in the covering fraction of LLS in the CGM with increasing redshift
(
Rahmati et al. 2015
;
Tortora et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. This likely reflects increasing cosmic gas densities and gas accretion rates, though as we noted above the covering fraction of low density gas in the CGM can be sensitive to feedback and resolution effects
(
Rahmati et al. 2015
;
Faucher-Giguère et al. 2015
;
Bennett & Sijacki 2020
;
Kimm et al. 2025
)
.

By contrast, we find no strong redshift evolution in ISM column densities for a given halo mass where the highest column density gas (
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\gtrsim 10^{20.5}
cm
-2
), which produces damping wing absorption, arises.
Such weak evolution in ISM column densities may be expected if these are primarily driven by collapsed gas clouds, whose properties higher-resolution simulations have found to be nearly universal across cosmic time
(
Guszejnov et al. 2020
;
Wang et al. 2025
)
.
The weak increase we find in the median column density (
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\sim(1+z)^{0.3}
) is also close to what would be expected based on estimates of electron densities in galaxies’ star forming regions with JWST
(
Isobe et al. 2023
;
Topping et al. 2025b
,
n_{e}\sim(1+z)^{1.5}
, e.g.,)
.
The relationship between electron densities and HI densities is complicated due to the formation of HII regions. However, if the majority of HI absorption occurs in the ISM around massive stars, a better understanding of ISM densities and ionization, as well as selection effects which may increase the visibility of sources with high ISM densities
(
Topping et al. 2025a
, thought to be linked to strong star formation bursts, boosting UV luminosity)
, will be important for interpreting DLAs in galaxy spectra.

Overall, as discussed by
Mason et al. 2025
, the stronger
\sim(1+z)^{2}
redshift evolution in the CGM is still not sufficient to explain the observed increase in Ly
\alpha
damping at
z\gtrsim 6
, implying the increasingly neutral IGM plays a major role in shaping
z>6
spectra.

We note our simulations only consider local radiation, and do not include a UV background. Gas at densities
n>10^{-2}\rm cm^{-3}
is expected to be self-shielded from the UVB
(
Gnedin 2010
;
Rahmati et al. 2013
;
Chardin et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
, and at high redshift, the local radiation field from local star-forming regions dominates over the background.
Thus, we expect our results for high column density gas to be minimally affected by the lack of UVB.
Our results in the CGM are likely to be conservative – as the inclusion of the UVB would decrease the HI fraction in lower density gas
(
Schaye 2001b
;
Bolton & Haehnelt 2013
;
Nasir et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
.
Therefore, this approximation should not significantly affect our conclusions about the origin of galaxy-DLAs.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have used high-resolution cosmological zoom-in simulations from the
serra
suite to investigate neutral hydrogen in the ISM and CGM of galaxies during the Epoch of Reionization. Motivated by recent low resolution JWST spectroscopy showing damped Ly
\alpha
absorption in
z>5
galaxies, where local HI absorption can be hard to disentangle from absorption by the increasingly neutral IGM, our goal was to investigate the location and evolution of dense local (ISM+CGM) neutral gas in and around
\mbox{high-}z
galaxies with redshift and galaxy properties.
We analyzed
\sim 100
galaxies at
z\sim 6-9.5
.
Our main findings can be summarized as follows:

•

The neutral gas distribution of
\mbox{high-}z
galaxies is complex and irregular in both the ISM – continuously shaped by bursty star formation through feedback and frequent mergers and accretion – and the CGM, characterized by dense pristine HI gas in filaments accreting onto the galaxies, and numerous HI dense clumps.

•

By generating mock sightlines towards star-forming regions in galaxies, we find the distribution of HI column densities,
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
, to individual galaxies is very broad (
0.5-1.5
dex), due to strong sightline-to-sightline variations. We find typical median column densities,
\log{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}/\rm cm^{-2}\sim 21-22
, comparable to the observed GRB-DLA
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distribution at
z\sim 2-6

(
Tanvir et al. 2019
)
, with
\sim 30\%
of sightlines classified as strong DLAs
\log{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}/\rm cm^{-2}>22
, comparable to that inferred from JWST spectra
(
Mason et al. 2025
)
.

•

We find dense neutral gas in the ISM, close to the emitting star-forming regions,
<1
kpc, is the dominant source of high column density absorption (
\log{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}/\rm cm^{-2}\gtrsim 21
), with negligible contributions from the CGM or intervening proximate absorbers. For all galaxies across mass and redshift, the probability of intersecting a
\log{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}/\rm cm^{-2}\gtrsim 21
sightline within
<0.25
kpc is
>50\%
, decreasing rapidly at larger distances.

•

The median
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
increases with halo mass,
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\sim M_{h}^{0.38}
, as expected if
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
scales with the virial radius (
{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}\sim R_{\mathrm{vir}}\sim M_{h}^{1/3}
) reflecting a more extended CGM and the capability of deeper potential wells to accrete and retain more gas, adding to the dominant ISM HI column densities.

•

We find no significant redshift evolution of
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distributions towards star-forming regions at fixed halo mass across the EoR, though we find a mild increase in CGM column densities scaling with mean cosmic density, as seen in other simulations.

•

We investigate the origin of extremely strong galaxy-DLAs (
\log{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}/\rm cm^{-2}\gtrsim 22
) detected with JWST.
We predict these are associated with dense ISM gas along the line of sight to star-forming regions in massive halos (
M_{h}\gtrsim 10^{11}M_{\odot}
). We show the number of neighboring galaxies can provide a probe of this association with massive halos, as systems with at least 4 neighboring galaxies within 100 kpc are predicted to typically have
\log{N_{\mathrm{HI}}}/\rm cm^{-2}>21.5
. Furthermore, we show that the absorbing gas in the ISM is expected to be metal enriched and produce low ionization metal absorption lines reaching EW
\sim 1-5\penalty\ \textrm{A\kern-5.59721pt\raisebox{2.58334pt}{${}^{\circ}$}}
.

Our results show that
\mbox{high-}z
galaxy-DLAs in
serra
have HI column densities comparable to those of
z\sim 2-4
GRB-DLAs. Our results imply that observed galaxy-DLAs likely trace neutral gas located close (
<0.25
kpc) to star-forming regions. This motivates future higher-resolution simulations to uncover the impact of possible pc-scale structures in the HI distribution in the ISM, and higher resolution spectroscopy to associate strong galaxy-DLAs with their host galaxies via low ionization metal absorption lines.

Our results demonstrate that strong sightline-to-sightline variation is expected in the HI column density distribution for individual galaxies, and we predict little evolution in the distribution from
z\sim 6
to
z\sim 10
. This suggests it may be challenging to identify individual sources affected by galaxy-DLAs based on galaxy properties alone when using only low resolution spectra (
R<1000
). However, it implies that
N_{\mathrm{HI}}
distributions derived from spectra near the end of reionization at
z\sim 5-6
, where the IGM has a minimal impact on Ly
\alpha
damping wings, could provide valuable priors to interpret JWST spectra probing the earliest stages of reionization at
z>6
, similar to approaches taken for interpreting the evolution of Ly
\alpha
emission during reionization
(
Treu et al. 2012
;
Mason et al. 2018
;
Tang et al. 2024b
;
Tang et al. 2024a
;
Nakane et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.

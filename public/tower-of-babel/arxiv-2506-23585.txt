Quasi-Isometric embeddings of Ramanujan complexes
Hyein Choi

Abstract
. Ramanujan complexes were defined as high dimensional analogues of the optimal expanders, Ramanujan graphs. They were constructed as quotients of the Euclidean building (also called the affine building and the Bruhat-Tits building) of PGL d ​ ( 𝔽 p ​ ( ( y ) ) ) \mathrm{PGL}_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{p}((y))) for any prime p p by Lubotzky-Samuels-Vishne. We distinguish the Ramanujan complexes up to large-scale geometry. More precisely, we show that if p p and q q are distinct primes, then the associated Ramanujan complexes do not quasi-isometrically embed into one another. The main tools are the box space rigidity of Khukhro-Valette and the Euclidean building rigidity of Kleiner-Leeb and Fisher-Whyte.

1.2.
Motivation from expanders

Above theorems are motivated by
infinite families of expander graphs
which have many applications in mathematics and computer science due to their distinctive property, namely highly connected (sharing a common Cheeger constant) but also sparse (finite graphs of uniformly bounded degree whose numbers of vertices go to infinity)

HLW06

Since Mendel-Naor and Ostrovskii considered distinguishing infinite families of expander graphs up to large-scale geometry

1.3.
Ramanujan: the optimal expanders

A
k
-regular Ramanujan graph
is a

k
-regular graph whose eigenvalues
\lambda

the adjacency matrix satisfy either

|\lambda|=k
(trivial eigenvalues) or

|\lambda|\leq 2\sqrt{k-1}
(non-trivial eigenvalues).
Lubotzky-Philips-Sarnak constructed

\rm{PGL}_{2}(F)
, which is a tree, by cocompact lattices of
\rm{PGL}_{2}(F)

[
LPS88
]
.
In other words, the non-trivial spectrum of Ramanujan graphs is bounded by the spectrum of their covering tree.
A remarkable feature is that infinite families of Ramanujan graphs form infinite families of expander graphs with the optimal spectrum in the sense of Alon-Boppana
[
Alo86
]
on which the random walk is distributed in the fastest way (see
[
LP20
, Section 5]
and the references therein).

Generalizing this to high dimensions, Lubotzky-Samuels-Vishne defined and constructed
Ramanujan complexes
as quotients of the Euclidean building of
\rm{PGL}_{d}(F)
for
d\geq 3
by cocompact lattices of
\rm{PGL}_{d}(F)
whose non-trivial spectrum is derived from their covering Euclidean buildings
[
LSV05
]
.
Similar to 1-dimension, infinite families of Ramanujan complexes have the optimal spectrum in the sense of high dimensional Alon-Boppana shown by Li
[
Li04
]
.

One might expect that Ramanujan complexes form families of “high dimensional expanders” ideally exhibiting the distinctive property of expander graphs not only through edges (1-dimension) but also through triangles (2-dimension), tetrahedra (3-dimension), and so on. However, high dimensional expanders are not yet defined and we only have several proposed concepts. See
[
Lub14
]
,
[
Lub18
]
for candidates of a definition and how they are related to each other.

Among them, we focus on
Gromov high dimensional expanders
since they meet the natural expectation as follows. By an
infinite family of Gromov high dimensional expanders

Gromov
c
-overlapping property
for some fixed constant

(highly connected) defined in
[
Gro09
]
, that is, there exists a point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

c

(resp.

1.4.
Proof strategy

The strategy to prove Theorem
1.1
is similar to
[
KV17
]
. The first step is to show a large-scale geometry rigidity, and the second step is to produce coarsely disjoint expanders from different primes
p
.
The first step is done by Proposition
4.4
basically
saying that “similar” Ramanujan complexes should be built from “similar” Euclidean buildings. Since box spaces and finite quotients of Euclidean buildings have similar structure, the first step is obtained naturally by translating Khukhro-Valette’s Proposition
4.1
for box spaces in terms of Euclidean buildings.

For the second step, while Khukhro-Valette apply the quasi-isometry rigidity of Farb-Schwartz to the lattices
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{p}])
[
FS96
]
, we use Proposition
3.2

showing that two Euclidean buildings (or even symmetric spaces) from “different” fields do not quasi-isometrically embed into one another. Proposition
3.2
follows from the quasi-isometry/quasi-isometric embedding rigidity of
Kleiner-Leeb and Fisher-Whyte
[
KL97
]
,
[
FW18
]
together with the classical building theory. While we do not use it, the work of Eskin-Farb is also relevant
[
EF97
]
.

2.1.
Definitions of buildings

For a concrete background on spherical buildings, Euclidean buildings, and their relationship,

From a classical point of view

A

(1)

(2)

Each apartment can be thought of as being modeled on the sphere

(resp. Euclidean space

Definition 2.2

Let

Proposition 2.3

[

Every subbuilding

of a building

In other words, subbuildings share the same Coxeter structure as the ambient buildings with possibly losing some information.

From a geometric point of view

Buildings are equipped with the metrics from their apartments.
A spherical (resp. Euclidean) building is a

(resp.
\rm{CAT}(0)

(resp.

) together with the

In particular, each Euclidean building has an additional structure, the

Consider an apartment

2.2.
Groups and buildings

Definition 2.5
.

A building is said to be
thick
if every codimension one simplex called
panel
of chambers is a face of at least three chambers.

A notable feature of thick buildings is that their automorphism groups are rich enough. As in
[
KL97
, Section 3.7, Section 4.9]
, irreducible buildings of sufficiently high rank can be reduced to thick buildings and they were classified by Jacques Tits. It can be reformulated as follows.

Theorem 2.6

(Classification of buildings)
.

We have the following one-to-one correspondences up to isomorphisms:

•

Every thick irreducible spherical building of rank at least
3
corresponds to a classical group, a simple algebraic group, or a mixed group defined over a field.

•

Every thick irreducible Euclidean building of rank at least
4
corresponds to a classical group, an absolutely simple algebraic group, or a mixed group defined over a field that is complete with respect to a discrete valuation.

There are some exceptional cases (in particular, associated with skew fields) but Theorem
2.6
captures the essence of the classification and we will mainly focus on the buildings of type
A_{n}
associated with fields.

Theorem
2.6
guarantees that thick irreducible higher-rank buildings arise from groups. One reason that buildings are regarded as an analogue of symmetric spaces is that the associated groups act on them nicely.

Proposition 2.7
.

For a building
X
, the action of the associated group

on

is simplicial (i.e., sending
i
-simplices to
i

To give a quick and explicit example of this group action, we consider type

.

Group actions on spherical buildings of type
A_{n-1}

Let
F
be a field,

, and

Given two flags

The associated group

Each flag corresponds to a parabolic subgroup via the stabilizer map. Hence one can identify the simplices of

cocompact
(or
uniform
) if
G/\Gamma
is compact and
nonuniform
otherwise.

The celebrated Margulis Arithmeticity Theorem
[
Mar91
]
says that irreducible lattices of higher rank semisimple Lie groups are arithmetic subgroups. We remark that the converse also holds by Borel-Harish-Chandra
[
BHC62
]
. A well-known technique to obtain arithmetic lattices is the
restriction of scalars
: Let
G
be an algebraic group over a number field
F
(i.e.,
[F:\mathbb{Q}]<\infty)
, and
\cal{O}
be the ring of integers of
F
. Then
\Gamma=G(\cal{O})
embeds diagonally into
\tilde{G}=\prod_{\sigma}G^{\sigma}
and it is an arithmetic subgroup of
\tilde{G}
, where
\sigma
are the embeddings
F\to\mathbb{C}
(e.g.
Galois automorphisms of
F
over
\mathbb{Q}
) and
G^{\sigma}
is the image of
G
under
\sigma
. In particular, we can obtain cocompact arithmetic lattices as follows.

Proposition 2.11
.

[
Mor15
, Corollary 5.5.10, Appendix C]

If
G^{\sigma}
is compact for some
\sigma
, then the diagonally embedded
\Gamma
is a cocompact arithmetic lattice in
\prod_{\sigma}G^{\sigma}
.

We refer to
[
Mor15
]
for a comprehensive background.

3.
Euclidean buildings and associated fields

For a Euclidean building, its associated group and field coincide with those associated with its spherical building at infinity.
In particular, the fields are determined by the root groups, which play a crucial role in classifying the buildings.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a spherical building associated to an
F
-split group
G
defined over a field
F
.
If
\Delta^{\prime}
is a spherical subbuilding of
\Delta
,
then the field
F^{\prime}
associated with
\Delta^{\prime}
is isomorphic to a subfield of
F
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be a root in
\Delta^{\prime}
. Since
\Delta^{\prime}
is a subbuilding of
\Delta
, its apartments are apartments of
\Delta
. Thus
\alpha
is a root also in
\Delta
.
Denote by
U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
and
U_{\alpha}
the root groups
of
\alpha
in
\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta
, respectively.
By
[
AB08
, Proposition 7.37]
,

U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
is a subgroup of
U_{\alpha}
. In the split case, the root groups are isomorphic to the additive groups of the associated fields. Hence
F^{\prime}
is an additive subgroup of
F
. It remains to show that
F^{\prime}
is closed under the multiplication of
F
.

Fix a root group sequence
\Theta^{\prime}=\{U_{i}^{\prime}\}
from the root group labeling of
\Delta^{\prime}
. Then
\Delta
has the root group sequence
\Theta=\{U_{i}\}
induced by the same directed edge of the Coxeter diagram.
In particular,
U_{i}^{\prime}
and
U_{i}
correspond to the same root and
U_{i}^{\prime}
is a non-trivial subgroup of
U_{i}
for each
i
.
Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the roots corresponding to the first and last root groups of
\Theta^{\prime}
, respectively.
As in
[
Wei04
, Proposition 11.22]
,
for the root
\beta
in an apartment
\Sigma
,
define a subgroup
H_{\beta}=\langle\mu_{(}g)\mu(h)|g,h\in{U_{\beta}}^{*}=U_{\beta}\backslash\{e\}\rangle
of
G
, where
\mu(g)
is the unique element in
{U_{-\beta}}^{*}g{U_{-\beta}}^{*}
mapping
\Sigma
to itself and
\beta
to its opposite root
-\beta
for each
g\in U_{\beta}
.
By
[
Wei04
, Lemma 12.22]
, the action of
H_{\beta}
on
U_{\alpha}
is generated by the multiplicative maps
x\mapsto xt
of
F
for all
t\in F^{*}
by identifying
U_{\alpha}
with
F
.
Then
H_{\beta}^{\prime}=\langle\mu(g^{\prime})\mu(h^{\prime})|g^{\prime},h^{\prime}\in U_{\beta}^{\prime*}\rangle
is a subgroup of
H_{\beta}
and the action of
H_{\beta}^{\prime}
on
U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
is a restriction of the action of
H_{\beta}
on
U_{\alpha}
. Since the action was the multiplication of
F
,
F^{\prime}
is closed under the multiplication of
F
by identifying
U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
with
F^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition
3.1
is mentioned in
[

, p.390]
for general cases without proof. In the non-split cases, the root groups may not be isomorphic to the additive groups of the associated fields. We can still see the additive group of the field in two ways: first by identifying the root groups with vector spaces over the field, and second, by noting that the centers of the root groups are isomorphic to the field.

In
[

]
, Kleiner-Leeb considered spherical buildings as Tits boundaries, which we denote by
\partial_{T}X
.
A common idea to prove quasi-isometry rigidity results since the work of Mostow is to get a nice map on Tits boundaries which eventually induces a desired consequence.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be irreducible symmetric spaces or Euclidean buildings of equal rank
r\geq 4
associated with split groups. Assume that the type of
(X,Y)
is none of the following:

(A_{n},B_{n}),(A_{n},C_{n}),(A_{n},F_{4}),(D_{4},A_{4}),\text{and }(\text{any type},G_{2}).

If there is a quasi-isometric embedding
X\to Y
, then the field associated with
X
is isomorphic to a subfield of the field associated with
Y
.

Proof.

Let
\phi:X\rightarrow Y
be a quasi-isometric embedding.
As shown in
[
FW18
, Proof of Theorem 1.8]
, the boundary
\partial_{T}\phi(X)
at infinity of
\phi(X)
is a top-dimensional spherical subbuilding of
\partial_{T}Y
.
By
[
KL06
, Theorem 3.1]
, such a subbuilding is of the form
\partial_{T}\phi(X)
=
\partial_{T}Y^{\prime}
for some symmetric subspace or Euclidean subbuilding
Y^{\prime}
of
Y
.
Then
\phi(X)
and
Y^{\prime}
are at bounded distance so we have a quasi-isometry
\phi_{0}:X\to Y^{\prime}
by restricting the target of
\phi
.

It follows from
[
KL97
, Proof of Theorem 1.1.3]
that
the quasi-isometry
\phi_{0}:X\to Y^{\prime}
induces an isomorphism

\partial_{T}X\to\partial_{T}Y^{\prime}
of the spherical buildings at infinity. Indeed, it is shown that such
\phi_{0}
is a homothety with a fixed additive error, and so we may assume that
\phi_{0}
is a
(1,c)
-quasi-isometry for some fixed
c>0
by rescaling the metrics. Then such a
(1,c)
-quasi-isometry induces a map on the spherical buildings at infinity which preserves the Tits metrics. This induced map is the desired isomorphism on the Tits boundaries.

By Theorem
2.6
, the field
F_{X}
associated with
\partial_{T}X
is isomorphic to the field
F_{Y^{\prime}}
associated with
\partial_{T}Y^{\prime}
. In particular, those fields are also associated with
X
and
Y^{\prime}
, respectively. Since
\partial_{T}Y^{\prime}
is a spherical subbuilding of
\partial_{T}Y
, Proposition
3.1
implies that
F_{Y^{\prime}}
is a subfield of
F_{Y}
. Hence
F_{X}
is isomorphic to the subfield
F_{Y^{\prime}}
of
F_{Y}
.
∎

The assumption rank

is necessary to use the classification of buildings Theorem

. We exclude some types of
(X,Y)
to use
[

[

, Corollary 1.10]
.

to the type
(A_{n},A_{n})
.

4.
From box spaces to Ramanujan complexes

Let
G
and
H
be finitely generated residually finite groups with decreasing sequences
\{N_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{K_{i}\}_{i}
of finite index normal subgroups of
G
and
H
, respectively, with trivial intersection.
Heuristically,
G/N_{i}
is similar to
G
around small neighborhoods of the identities and such neighborhoods get larger as
i
increases.
Hence
G/N_{i}
looks more like
G
as
i\to\infty
. Based on this, Khukhro-Valette showed the following box space rigidity.

Proposition 4.1

(Box space rigidity)
.

[
KV17
, Proposition 16]

Let
G
,
H
,
\{N_{i}\}_{i}
, and
\{K_{i}\}_{i}
be defined as above.
If the box space
\{\mathrm{Cay}(G/N_{i})\}_{i}
is quasi-isometric to

\{\mathrm{Cay}(H/K_{i})\}_{i}
, then
G
is quasi-isometric to
H
.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

Khukhro-Valette show Proposition
4.1
for quasi-isometries but the same proof works for quasi-isometric embeddings as well. The embedding maps with the common quasi-isometry constants are obtained by the diagonal limit argument in their proof.

(2)

Their definition of quasi-isometry between two families of metric spaces is with respect to the
metrized disjoint union
, that is considering the distances between different components as well. In
[
KV17
, Lemma 3]
, they derive the component-to-component metric as in Definition
2.1
.

(3)

The original statement is written for a more general setting, namely, for any finitely generated groups
G
and
H
, and any sequences converging to
G
and
H
with respect to Chabauty topology, rather than just finite quotients.

We can approximate Euclidean buildings by their 1-skeleton graphs similarly to
[
BH13
, Proposition 8.45]
considering the set of all chambers as a partition of a building.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\cal{B}
be a Euclidean building and
\cal{B}(0)
be the set of all vertices equipped with the graph metric i.e., the length of shortest paths in the
1
-skeleton graph of
\cal{B}
.
Then
\cal{B}(0)
is
(3,c)
-quasi isometric to
\cal{B}
, where
c
is the diameter of a chamber in
\cal{B}
.

Proof.

We will show that the inclusion map
i:\cal{B}(0)\hookrightarrow\cal{B}
is a
(3,c)
-quasi-isometry.
Denote by
d
and
d_{g}
the metric on
\cal{B}
and the graph metric on
\cal{B}(0)
, respectively.
Since any point in
B
is contained in a chamber,
i
is
c
-coarsely surjective.
The right inequality of the following is also obvious as
d(u,v)\leq d_{g}(u,v)
for all
u,v\in\cal{B}(0)
.

\frac{1}{3}d_{g}(u,v)-c\leq d(i(u),i(v))\leq 3d_{g}(u,v)+c

What remains is to show the left inequality. Given
u
,
v\in\cal{B}(0)
, set
N
to be the smallest positive integer such that
d(u,v)+c<N
.
Consider a path
\gamma
from
u
to
v
in
\cal{B}
whose length is
d(u,v)
. Pick
N
distinct points
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}
on
\gamma
with
p_{1}=u
and
p_{N}=v
. Since the set of all chambers forms a partition of
\cal{B}
, for each
p_{i}
, there exists
q_{i}\in\cal{B}(0)
such that
p_{i}
and
q_{i}
are in the same chamber so that
d(p_{i},q_{i})<c
.
Then we have

d(q_{i},q_{i+1})\leq d(q_{i},p_{i})+d(p_{i},p_{i+1})+d(p_{i+1},q_{i+1})\leq 2c+\frac{d(u,v)}{N}\leq 3c

for every
i
. We remark that
c\geq 1
by setting the length of the edges (i.e., 1-simplices) to be 1 with respect to
d
.
Since
c
is the diameter of the chambers,
for the vertices
q_{i}
and
q_{i+1}
, there exist consecutively adjacent three chambers
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
, and
C_{3}
(not necessarily distinct) such that
q_{i}\in C_{1}
and
q_{i+1}\in C_{3}
.
This implies that
d_{g}(q_{i},q_{i+1})\leq 3

and hence we obtain
d_{g}(u,v)\leq 3(N-1)
by taking
q_{1}=u
and
q_{N}=v
.
By the minimality of
N
,
d_{g}(u,v)\leq 3(d(u,v)+c)
, as desired.
∎

We have the following Euclidean building version of Proposition
4.1
.

.

Let
\cal{B}
and
\cal{C}
be Euclidean buildings of locally compact second countable groups
G
and
H
, respectively.
Let
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
be cocompact lattices of
G
and
H
, and
\{\Gamma_{i}\}
and

\{\Lambda_{i}\}
be decreasing sequences of finite index normal subgroups of
\Gamma

If an infinite family
\{\Gamma_{i}\backslash\cal{B}\}_{i}
of quotient buildings quasi-isometrically embeds in
\{\Lambda_{i}\backslash\cal{C}\}_{i}
,

.

Proof.

Denote by
\cal{B}(0)
the set of vertices of
\cal{B}
.
We equip
\cal{B}(0)
with the graph metric.
Identifying
\cal{B}(0)
with
G/K
for a compact subgroup

of

\Gamma

\cal{B}(0)
isometrically, properly discontinuously, and cocompactly. By Milnor-Švarc,

\cal{B}(0)

,
\Gamma
and

So are their quotients

of

Then given quasi-isometric embeddings

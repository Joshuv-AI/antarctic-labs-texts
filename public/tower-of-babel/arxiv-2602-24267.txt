Dynamics of spinning particles in pp-wave spacetimes University of Lodz, Faculty of Physics and Applied Informatics. Poland, Lodz, Pomorska 149/153, 90-236; e-mail: krzysztof.andrzejewski@uni.lodz.pl


Abstract
In this work, we study the dynamics of a spinning particle in pp-waves spacetimes; in particular, plane gravitational waves and impulsive shockwaves. W pay special attention to analytical considerations; this is possible due to an appropriate choice of the spin supplementary condition, various Hamiltonian formalisms (including a non-minimal one) and constants of motion associated with conformal fields. Based on these results, we establish a relation between the motions of a spinning particle in pp-waves and electromagnetic fields suggested by a gauge-gravity duality.

\{x^{\alpha},P_{\beta}\}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta},\quad\{P_{\alpha},P_{\beta}\}=-\frac{1}{2}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\lambda}S^{\gamma\lambda},\quad\{S^{\alpha\beta},P_{\gamma}\}=\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\gamma\delta}S^{\beta\delta}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\gamma\delta}S^{\alpha\delta},

For the TD condition the Hamiltonian is more complicated and can be found in Ref.

The Hamiltonian approach described above open a new way in the analysis of the dynamics of spinning body, because we can introduce other Hamiltonians motivated by various physical considerations (e.g. symmetry). In this work, we analyse the dynamics of a spinning body in pp-waves spacetimes, in particular plane gravitational waves and impulsive gravitational shockwaves. W pay particular attention to analytical considerations; this is possible due to the application of the OKS condition, various Hamiltonian formalisms and non-standard constants of motion; the latter are our starting point. 2
Conformal fields and spinning particles

It is well-know that the Killing fields lead to the integrals of motion, and thus can be helpful in the analysis of dynamics. Remarkably, this observation was extended to spinning particles
[
38
]
. A nice example of such a situation is given in Ref.
[
39
]
where, for de Sitter space and an arbitrary spin condition, momentum and spin are explicitly expressed in terms of the spacetime coordinates.
On the other hand, it turns out that for spinless particles the conformal Killing fields can also lead to new constants of motion
[
40
]
. The homothetic fields are the simplest example of such a situation; however, there are spaces where this occurs for proper conformal fields
[
41
]
. In view of the above, we will analyse this issue for spinning particles. Next, the results obtained will be applied in Sec.
4
.

Let us start with the conformal Killing vector field
Y
, i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{Y}g=2\psi g
where
\psi
is a function. Then using (skew)symmetry of the tensor
g_{\alpha\beta}
(
S^{\alpha\beta}
, respectively) and the MPDS equations, after some calculations, we obtain that the quantity
\tilde{I}=P^{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}Y_{\alpha;\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}
satisfies along the trajectory the identity

\tilde{I}^{\prime}=\psi P^{\alpha}{x^{\prime}_{\alpha}}+S^{\alpha\beta}x^{\prime}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\psi.

(2.1)

For the Killing fields (i.e.
\psi=0
) we recover the well-know integral of motion.

For the FMP condition we have
P^{\alpha}x_{\alpha}^{\prime}=-m=const.
; moreover, the last term in eq. (
2.1
) vanishes. Consequently, we arrive at the formula
\tilde{I}^{\prime}=-m\psi
.
This result can also be confirmed at the Hamiltonian level, see (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.8
).
Namely, after straightforward but tedious computations, we get

\tilde{I}^{\prime}=\{\tilde{I},H_{1}\}=\frac{1}{m}\psi P^{\alpha}(P_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{S^{2}}S_{\alpha\beta}{S^{\beta}}_{\gamma}P^{\gamma})+\frac{1}{m}S^{\delta\alpha}(P_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{S^{2}}S_{\alpha\beta}{S^{\beta}}_{\gamma}P^{\gamma})\partial_{\delta}\psi.

(2.2)

Now, taking into account the relation between momenta and velocities, we obtain formula (
2.1
). Thus, for the FMP condition, we get an integral of motion
I=\tilde{I}+m\int\psi
. Obviously, this integral is generally non-local (it depends on the trajectory); however, it can be shown that there exists a parametrization when it localises
[
40
]
. Moreover, in some special cases such a localisation holds for the proper time parametrization and then
I
reduces to an ordinary constant of motion. For example, this holds for a homothetic vector field
Y
, i.e. when
\psi=const.
; other examples including proper conformal Killing fields, are provided by pp-wave metrics, see
[
41
]
. In view of the above, these results also hold for the spinning particle with the FMP condition.

For the OKS condition we have
P^{\alpha}=mx^{\prime\alpha}
and
m
is a constant, thus the first term in eq. (
2.1
) is the same as previously; however, the second term, in general, is not zero, making this case more complicated. This can be confirmed by direct calculations at the Hamiltonian level
(see eqs. (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
)):

\tilde{I}^{\prime}=\{\tilde{I},H_{0}\}=\frac{1}{m}(\psi P^{2}+S^{\alpha\beta}P_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\psi)=-\psi m+S^{\alpha\beta}x^{\prime}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\psi.

(2.3)

Integrating eq. (
2.3
), we get a generalized integral of motion
I=\tilde{I}+\int(m\psi-S^{\alpha\beta}x^{\prime}_{\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\psi)
.
Obviously, it localises for homothetic fields. In Sec.
4
, we will show that for some special forms of plane gravitational waves the integral term in
I
is an explicit function of
\tau
and thus can be directly computed, leading in this way to a new integral of motion.

4
Plane gravitational waves and the OKS condition

In this section we consider the motion of a spinning body in plane waves spacetimes. This is an interesting problem due to propagation of gravitational waves; therefore, it has been considered from various points of view. The TD condition has been studied in Refs.
[
42
,
43
]
; however, due to the complicated relation between momentum and velocity, only some particular results were obtained. Recently, it has been show that these difficulties disappear at the linear order in spin

[
45

4.1
Some examples

The most simple case of the plane waves is given by the constant matrix

\dot{\tilde{I}}=-\frac{m^{2}}{p_{v}}u-u(S^{vu}+S^{au}\dot{x}^{a}).

Using eq. (

\dot{\tilde{I}}=-\frac{m^{2}}{p_{v}}u-\frac{1}{p_{v}}\hat{K}_{ab}S^{ua}S^{ub}.

To analyse

5
Impulsive gravitational shockwaves

The impulsive gravitational shockwaves are pp-waves with
\delta(u)
profile. More precisely, they are described by the metric (
3.1
) with the function
K
of the form

K=f(x^{1},x^{2})\delta(u).

(5.1)

The famous example is the Aichelburg-Sexl metric with
f(x^{1},x^{2})\propto\ln(r)
; it describes spacetime produced by a particle moving at the speed of light.
The second important example is the impulsive plane gravitational wave defined by

K(x^{1},x^{2})\propto\delta(u)((x^{1})^{2}-(x^{2})^{2})
.

For such spacetimes a mathematically rigorous analysis of dynamics of spinless particles is quite subtle
[
50
]
. Thus, for a spinning particle, we will make only a preliminary description.
To this end, let us observe that putting
\kappa=4\epsilon^{3}/\pi
in the profile (
4.11
) and next taking the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain an impulsive gravitational wave. Now, taking the same limit in solutions obtained in Sec.
4.1
, we find the dynamics of a spinning particle in the impulsive plane gravitational wave. Namely, we obtain that
x^{a}
exhibits a velocity jump at
u=0
. On the other hand, the matrix
\hat{K}_{ab}
tends to the step function
\theta(u)
. In consequence, the dynamics of
z^{a}
(see eq. (
4.9
)) is described by the step function. As a results, we observe a jump at
u=0
in the dynamics of the spin tensor.

For an arbitrary impulsive shockwave (described by
f
) the transverse equations take the form

\overset{..}{x}^{a}=\frac{\delta(u)}{2}\partial_{a}(f-\frac{1}{p_{v}}S^{ub}\partial_{b}f),

(5.2)

thus
x^{a}=x_{0}^{a}+v_{1}^{a}u
for
u<0
and
x^{a}=x_{0}^{a}+v_{2}^{a}u
for
u>0
, i.e. they are continuous function with a velocity jump. This jump can be found by integrating equation (
5.2
):

v_{2}^{a}-v_{1}^{a}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{a}(f-\frac{1}{p_{v}}\partial_{b}fS^{ub})(x_{0}).

(5.3)

Thus, we observe that the velocity jump depends on the spin components
S^{ua}
. Similarly, as above for the impulsive plane wave, we also get the jump in the spin dynamics.

6
Non-minimal Hamiltonian

The Hamiltonians described in Sec.
1
can form a starting point for other descriptions of the spinning body. Such a reasoning was applied, e.g., in Ref.
[
32
,
33
,
34
]
where a non-minimal extension of the Hamiltonian
H_{0}
has been proposed. Namely, motivated by the gravitational counterpart of the Stern-Gerlach force, the spin-spin interaction via space-time curvature has been proposed:

H_{2}=H_{0}+\frac{\kappa}{4}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}S^{\alpha\beta}S^{\gamma\delta}=\frac{P^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\kappa}{4}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}S^{\alpha\beta}S^{\gamma\delta}.

(6.1)

A remarkable property of this extension is that the integrals of motion are the same as for
H_{0}
.
Here, let us note that the relation
P^{\alpha}=mx^{\prime\alpha}
is still valid; substituting this into the Hamiltonian (
6.1
) we obtain that
x^{\prime}_{\alpha}x^{\prime\alpha}
is not constant, in consequence
\tau
is not, in general, the proper time parametrization.

The non-minimal part of
H_{2}
implies an additional terms in the equations of motion. Namely, we have to add
-\frac{\kappa}{4}S^{\alpha\beta}S^{\gamma\delta}\nabla_{\epsilon}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
in eq. (
1.1
).
While for the spin tensor we have

D_{\tau}S^{\alpha\beta}=\kappa S^{\gamma\delta}({R^{\alpha}}_{\epsilon\gamma\delta}S^{\beta\epsilon}-{R^{\beta}}_{\epsilon\gamma\delta}S^{\alpha\epsilon}).

(6.2)

To better understand the meaning and consequence of the proposed modification it is crucial to analyse the dynamics for various gravitational backgrounds. For black holes such investigations have been carried out in Ref.
[
36
]
; here, we make it for other backgrounds.

Let us start with de Sitter (or, in general, of constant curvature) space. Then, the non-minimal term is proportional to
S^{2}
and thus the dynamics of
x^{\alpha}
and
S^{\alpha\beta}
is the same as for
H_{0}
.

Now, we let us take pp-wave spacetimes. Then for the metric (
3.1
) we obtain

H_{2}=\frac{1}{2m}P^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}KS^{ua}S^{ub}.

(6.3)

Using
H_{2}
together with the brackets (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) we obtain that
u^{\prime}
and
S^{ua}
are constant (as in the OKS case discussed in Sec.
3
).
Equation for
v
is extended by the term
\frac{\kappa}{2}\partial_{u}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}KS^{ua}S^{ub}
. Despite this fact, it can be easily integrated due to the Hamiltonian constraint:

m(2v^{\prime}u^{\prime}+Ku^{\prime 2}+x^{\prime a}x^{\prime}_{a})-\kappa\partial_{a}\partial_{b}KS^{ua}S^{ub}=2H_{2}=const.

(6.4)

Finally, the transverse part of the equations takes the form

m{x^{a}}{{}^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{p_{v}^{2}}{2m}\partial_{a}K-\frac{p_{v}}{2m}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}KS^{ub}+\frac{\kappa}{2}S^{ub}S^{uc}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\partial_{c}K;

(6.5)

thus, in general, there is a modification of the transverse dynamics. However, for plane gravitational waves,
K=K_{ab}x^{a}x^{b}
the non-minimal term disappears and the transversal dynamics is the same as in Sec.
4
.
In summary, for pp-waves the form of the coordinates dynamics is very similar to that obtained for the OKS condition, and for plane gravitational waves the difference is in the
v
-coordinate only.

Now, let us focus on the spin dynamics. To better see this issue in what follows we will restrict ourselves to plane waves backgrounds (this case is especially interesting because for the OKS condition equation for the
v
-coordinate is exactly integrated). So let us consider a plane gravitational wave, i.e.
K=K_{ab}x^{a}x^{b}
. Then, equation for
S^{uv}
reduces to

S^{\prime uv}=u^{\prime}K_{ab}x^{b}S^{au}+2\kappa K_{ab}S^{ua}S^{ub}.

(6.6)

Since for the plane waves the transverse dynamics is the same as in Sec.
4

4.1
) to integrate the above equation; the final result (in the
u
parametrization) reads

S^{uv}=-\dot{x}^{a}S^{ua}-\frac{1-2\kappa m}{p_{v}}\hat{K}_{ab}S^{ua}S^{ub},

(6.7)

comparing eq. (
4.6
) and (
6.7
) we see that the non-minimal correction translates only the factor in the numerator of the last expression.

Now, let us consider
S^{ab}
. Then, the non-minimal correction takes the form
2\kappa S^{ud}(K_{bd}S^{ua}-K_{ad}S^{ub})
. Again, using the transverse equation we obtain

\dot{S}^{ab}=\overset{..}{x}^{a}S^{ub}-\overset{..}{x}^{b}S^{ua}+\frac{1-2m\kappa}{p_{v}}S^{ud}(K_{ad}S^{ub}-K_{bd}S^{ua}).

(6.8)

We easily integrate the above equation and the result is as in eq. (
4.7
) with the replacement
1/p_{v}\rightarrow\frac{1-2\kappa m}{p_{v}}
, i.e. the same as for
S^{uv}
. Finally, let us consider

S^{av}{{}^{\prime}}=\frac{p_{v}}{m}K_{bc}x^{c}S^{ba}-\frac{p_{v}}{m}K_{ac}x^{c}S^{vu}+(K)^{\prime}S^{ua}+2\kappa(K_{ab}S^{ub}S^{vu}+K_{cb}S^{ac}S^{ub}).

Now, using eqs. (

6.7

S^{av}
is given by eq. (
4.8

1/p_{v}\rightarrow\frac{1-2\kappa m}{p_{v}}

At the end, let us return to the
v

we check by straightforward calculations that eq. (

2vu^{\prime}+x^{\prime a}x^{a}-\frac{S^{uv}}{m}-\frac{2\tau}{m}H_{2}=const.

(6.10)

In summary, for spinning particles in the plane gravitational waves, described by the non-local Hamiltonian

7
Spinning particles and double copy aspects

In recent years, many analogies between gravitational and gauge fields have been investigated. An example of such a relation is provided by the so-called classical double copy conjecture (a limit of the color-kinematics duality), see among others
[
51
,
52
,
53
]
. According to this idea some gravitational aspects can be explained by means of gauge fields theory. For example, in this approach pp-waves metrics correspond to some electromagnetic fields (abelian gauge).
More specifically, the electromagnetic counterpart of the pp-wave is defined by the potential

A_{\tilde{u}}=A(\tilde{u},\tilde{x}^{a})
(the remaining components are zero),
where
\tilde{u},\tilde{v},\tilde{x}^{a}
are the light-cone coordinates in the Minkowski spacetime
5
5

5

To make considerations more transparent the dynamical variables related to electromagnetic sector are decorated by tilde.
, the corresponding electromagnetic field is of the form
F_{a\tilde{u}}=\partial_{a}A
.

Our aim is to show that the motion of spinning particles in pp-waves and the above electromagnetic fields is very similar when

A_{\tilde{u}}=A=\frac{p_{v}}{2q}K,

(7.1)

K
is the profile in pp-wave metric (
3.1
).
In this way we continue investigations for spinless particles, see e.g.
[
54
,
55
,
56
]
as well as the gravito-electromagnetic analogies, e.g.
[
8
]
.

To this end we need equations for charged spinning body in the electromagnetic field (in pole-dipole approximation). Such a problem was studied in various approaches and spin conditions, see
[
6
,
10
]
for review. Here, as in previous sections, we follow the Hamiltonian approach, see
[
32
,
57
]
. Namely, such a Hamiltonian formalism can be naturally defined in the Minkowski spacetime (
\eta_{\mu\nu}
) by the following Poisson brackets:

\{\tilde{x}^{\alpha},\tilde{P}_{\beta}\}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta},\quad\{\tilde{P}_{\alpha},\tilde{P}_{\beta}\}=qF_{\alpha\beta},\quad

(7.2)

\{\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta},\tilde{S}^{\gamma\delta}\}=\eta^{\alpha\gamma}\tilde{S}^{\beta\delta}-\eta^{\alpha\delta}\tilde{S}^{\beta\gamma}+\eta^{\beta\delta}\tilde{S}^{\alpha\gamma}-\eta^{\beta\gamma}\tilde{S}^{\alpha\delta},

(7.3)

together with the Hamiltonian

H_{3}=\frac{1}{2m}\tilde{P}^{\alpha}\tilde{P}_{\alpha}-\frac{k}{2}F_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta}.

(7.4)

This formalism gives the following equations of motion
[
32
,
57
]
:

\displaystyle m{{\tilde{x}}^{\alpha}}{{}^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=q{F^{\alpha}}_{\beta}\tilde{x}^{\beta}{{}^{\prime}}+\frac{k}{2}\tilde{S}^{\beta\gamma}\partial^{\alpha}F_{\beta\gamma}\,,

(7.5)

\displaystyle{\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta}}{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=k{F^{\alpha}}_{\gamma}\tilde{S}^{\gamma\beta}-k\tilde{S}^{\alpha\gamma}{F_{\gamma}}^{\beta}\,.

(7.6)

We see that equations for the electromagnetic case are qualitatively different from gravitational ones. For example, note that in this approach due to the relation
\tilde{P}^{\alpha}=m{\tilde{x}^{\alpha}}{{}^{\prime}}
and the additional term in
H_{3}
the parametrization, in general, does not agree with the proper time.
Despite this fact and the difference between eqs. (
7.5
), (
7.6
) and their gravitational counterparts, we will show that the dynamics of a spinning particle in the pp-waves (under OKS condition) and electromagnetic field (
7.1
) (with an appropriate SSC) can be directly related, provided we take
k=q/m
.

To this end, first, we should define a spin supplementary condition which is compatible with the equations (
7.5
) and (
7.6
). It turns out that, see
[
47
]
, this holds for a field
V
satisfying equation

{\tilde{V}^{\alpha}}{{}^{\prime}}=k{F^{\alpha}}_{\beta}\tilde{V}^{\beta},

(7.7)

(note that, similarly to the OKS condition, we need to know
\tilde{V}
at the initial parameter only).
As a result we have two separate conditions
S^{\alpha\beta}V_{\beta}=0=\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{V}_{\beta}

where
V
is defined by
D_{\tau}V=0
(for the gravitational fields) while
\tilde{V}
by eq. (
7.7
) (for the electromagnetic fields).

To get first insight into relation between dynamics in the both kinds of backgrounds let us take
V=\partial_{v}
and
\tilde{V}=\partial_{\tilde{v}}
(we readily check that the latter satisfies eq. (
7.7
)). Then, for the electromagnetic fields described by the potential (
7.1
) we have
\tilde{S}^{\tilde{u}\alpha}=0
; in consequence,
F_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta}=0
. Thus,
\tilde{x}^{\alpha}
satisfy the ordinary Lorentz force equation; in particular, the transverse part (corresponding to
\tilde{x}^{a}
) has the same form as in eq. (
4.1
).
A similar conclusion concerns spin tensor; namely, for
k=q/m
, the components
\tilde{S}^{ab}
are constant (as in the gravitational case) and
\tilde{S}^{\tilde{v}a}
also satisfy the same equations as their gravitational counterparts. Only equation for the
v
-coordinate differs from the gravitational counterpart due to the difference between the velocity length in the Minkowski and pp-wave metrics (non-affine and affine parametrization, respectively).
In summary, for the SSC (
7.7
) with
\tilde{V}=\partial_{\tilde{v}}
the dynamics of spinning particle in the electromagnetic field (
7.1
) is equivalent (except the
v
-coordinate) to the dynamics in pp-waves (under the OKS condition with
V=\partial_{v}
).

Now, let us analyse the above issue for an arbitrary
V
satisfying the OKS condition and
\tilde{V}
eq. (
7.7
). In this case the situation is more complicated since, despite the same initial condition, the evolution of
V
and
\tilde{V}
can be different for both backgrounds, see also the discussion at end of this section.

First, let us note that
\partial_{\tilde{v}}
is a Killing field in the Minkowski spacetime and preserves electromagnetic field (
7.1
), i.e.
{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial_{\tilde{v}}}F_{\alpha\beta}=0
; thus it generates a constant of motion
p_{\tilde{v}}
, which in turn gives the relation
\tilde{\tau}=\tilde{u}m/{p_{\tilde{v}}}
. Next, after straightforward but tedious computations, we obtain that the equations in the electromagnetic field (
7.1
) (in
\tilde{\tau}
parametrization) coincide with their gravitational counterparts after the following identification

p_{v}\leftrightarrow p_{\tilde{v}},\quad\tau\leftrightarrow\tilde{\tau},\quad\quad\tilde{x}^{\alpha}\leftrightarrow x^{\alpha},\quad S^{au}\leftrightarrow\tilde{S}^{au},\quad\tilde{S}^{uv}\leftrightarrow S^{uv},\quad\tilde{S}^{ab}\leftrightarrow S^{ab}.

(7.8)

The only difference between equations of motion for both backgrounds concerns
\tilde{v},\tilde{S}^{va}
; we analyse these cases in some details below. Namely, for the
v
-coordinate and electromagnetic field (
7.1
) we get

m\tilde{v}{{}^{\prime\prime}}=-q\partial_{a}A\tilde{x}^{a}{{}^{\prime}}+k\tilde{S}^{au}\partial_{\tilde{u}}\partial_{a}A.

(7.9)

Eq. (
7.9
) can be integrated due to Hamiltonian constraint:

\tilde{x}^{a}{{}^{\prime}}\tilde{x}_{a}{{}^{\prime}}+2\tilde{v}^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{\prime}-2\frac{k}{m}\tilde{S}^{au}\partial_{a}A=cosnt.

(7.10)

thus, as in the spinless case, the difference between the dynamics of the
v
-coordinate results from the difference in the velocity lengths in the both backgrounds.

It remains to compare
\tilde{S}^{va}
and
S^{av}
components. To this end let us make the substitution (
7.8
); then, after straightforward calculations, we obtain the following relation

\tilde{S}^{av}=S^{av}+\frac{1}{2}(K-K(\tau_{0}))S^{au},

(7.11)

(

, thus is a function

In view of the above we have a relation between spin components
\tilde{S}^{av}
and
S^{av}
; however, it is less direct than in the simplest case
V=\partial_{v}
(i.e.
S^{ua}=0
).

To better understand relation (
7.11
) let us compare
V
and
\tilde{V}
assuming the same initial conditions
V^{\alpha}(\tau_{0})=\tilde{V}^{\alpha}(\tilde{\tau}_{0})
. Considering both spin conditions and backgrounds separately, and next making identification (
7.1
), we obtain the following relations between fields

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{u}

\displaystyle=V^{u}=V^{u}(\tau_{0})=const.

(7.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{a}

\displaystyle=V^{a},

(7.13)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{v}

\displaystyle=V^{v}-\frac{V^{u}}{2}(K-K(\tau_{0})).

(7.14)

Thus, despite the same initial condition at
\tau_{0}=\tilde{\tau}_{0}
, in general, the evolution of the
v
-component of
V
is different for gravitational and electromagnetic backgrounds (they are time-like; for null-like case, as above, they can coincide). On the other hand, the difference between evolution of the fields
V
and
\tilde{V}
is reflected in the dynamics of the spin tensors
S^{\alpha\beta}
and
\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta}
, this is exactly encoded in eq. (

7.8
), (

) and (

S^{\alpha\beta}V_{\beta}=0
if and only if
\tilde{S}^{\alpha\beta}\tilde{V}_{\beta}=0
.
In summary, starting with the same initial condition the evolution of
V
and
\tilde{V}
is different for pp-waves and electromagnetic fields (
7.1

S^{va}

Another way to look on the above problem can be obtained if we take into account the fact that
S^{2}
and
\tilde{S}^{2}

2\tilde{S}^{2}=-2(\tilde{S}^{uv})^{2}+\tilde{S}^{ab}\tilde{S}_{ab}+4\tilde{S_{a}}^{v}\tilde{S}^{au},

(7.15)

while for the pp-wave metric we get

2S^{2}=-2(S^{uv})^{2}+S^{ab}S_{ab}+4{S_{a}}^{u}(S^{av}+\frac{K}{2}S^{au}).

Comparing eqs. (

In summary, in this section we analysed the dynamics of a charged spinning particle in electromagnetic fields resulting from the double copy conjecture. It turned out that the motion in pp-waves and such electromagnetic fields is very similar. There are only two differences. The first one, which appears also for spinless particle, is related to the
v
-coordinate (this is due to the form of velocity vector). The second, concerning
S^{av}

V^{\alpha}(\tau_{0})
for both backgrounds.

8
Conclusion and outlook

Let us summarize. In this work we have investigated the dynamics of spinning bodies in pp-waves spacetimes. Namely, using the OKS condition (alternatively, a suitable Hamiltonian formalism), we showed that the solution of the equations of motion can be formulated in step-by-steep algorithm. Moreover, we showed that the vector field defining the OKS condition can be used to find spin components. These results are particularly efficient for plane gravitational waves; they provide analytically solutions and enable to find the change of spin after the passage of the wave. Moreover, in this case, we saw a notable role of integrals of motion associated with the conformal Killing fields (e.g. homothetic field in the integrability of the
v
-coordinate). We also applied our reasoning to the impulsive gravitational shockwaves (in particular, for the impulsive plane waves this was done by a special choice of the profile and limiting procedure). For such spacetimes, we found a jump in the velocities and spin dynamics. In the next step, we considered a non-minimal Hamiltonian (extended by the spin-curvature-spin interaction). In particular, we showed that in the case of plane gravitational waves, equations can be integrated and the spin components are only shifted by a suitable change in parameters (if they are properly selected, they significantly simplify equations). Finally, we showed that the motions of the spinning particle in pp-wave and electromagnetic field (following from the double copy conjecture) are very similar and the main difference is in one spin component; this, in turn, is related to a different evolution of the vector field defining the corresponding spin conditions.

Turning to further developments, it is worth analysing the role of conformal fields for other examples of pp-waves (see e.g.
[
41
,
49
]
) and non-minimal Hamiltonians. We can also try to extend the results obtained to spacetime with non-zero torsion
[
58
]
or deflection and lensing problems
[
59
]
. On the other hand, we have seen that the OKS condition is compatible with a relevant spin condition for charged spinning particle placed in a electromagnetic field. In view of this it would be interesting to see what happens when we apply the OKS condition for the Kaluza-Klein metric (in analogously to the FMP case studied in Ref.
[
60
]
). Finally, the analysis of the non-minimal Hamiltonians deserves for further attention, especially the role of special coupling values discussed here and in Ref.
[
33
]
.

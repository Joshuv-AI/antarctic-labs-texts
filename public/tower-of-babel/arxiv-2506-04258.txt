Differential measurement of m e / m p m_{\rm e}/m_{\rm p} : 3 mm methanol lines toward Sagittarius B2(N) and (M) molecular clouds
J. S. Vorotyntseva 1, S. A. Levshakov 1, C. Henkel 2

Abstract
Differential measurement of the fundamental physical constant μ = m e / m p \mu=m_{\rm e}/m_{\rm p} (the electron-to-proton mass ratio) toward two sources located near the Galactic center – the Sagittarius (Sgr) B2(N) and (M) molecular clouds – show a lower μ \mu compared to its terrestrial value. Based on methanol (CH 3 OH) emission lines from the frequency range 80–112 GHz (IRAM 30-m telescope archival data), the calculated difference Δ ​ μ / μ ≡ ( μ obs − μ lab ) / μ lab \Delta\mu/\mu\equiv(\mu_{\rm obs}-\mu_{\rm lab})/\mu_{\rm lab} is equal to ( − 3.0 ± 0.6 ) × 10 − 7 (-3.0\pm 0.6)\times 10^{-7} ( 1 ​ σ 1\sigma C.L.) in the whole cloud Sgr B2. The revealed 5 ​ σ 5\sigma signal in μ \mu -variations agrees within the margin of error with the recently obtained result based on methanol emission lines from the higher frequency range 542–543 GHz ( Herschel space telescope archival data) for Sgr B2(N): Δ ​ μ / μ = ( − 4.2 ± 0.7 ) × 10 − 7 \Delta\mu/\mu=(-4.2\pm 0.7)\times 10^{-7} .

but their origin remains unexplained till our days.
It has been suggested by Dirac

variables corresponding to the local age of the world. However, numerous laboratory
experiments with atomic and, more recently, nuclear clocks have never detected their changes.

III
Method

It is known that the electro-vibro-rotational transitions
in molecular spectra have a specific dependence on
\mu
, individual for
each transition
VL
,
KL
.
The response of the molecular transition to small changes in
\mu
can be
characterized by the dimensionless sensitivity coefficient

Q
which is defined as
LKR

Q=\frac{\Delta f/f}{\Delta\mu/\mu}\,,

(1)

where
\Delta f/f
is the relative frequency offset, and
|\Delta\mu/\mu|\ll 1
.

To estimate
\Delta\mu/\mu
, a
pair of molecular lines with different sensitivity coefficients
Q_{i}
,
Q_{j}
are required
LKR
:

\frac{\Delta\mu}{\mu}=\frac{V_{i}-V_{j}}{c(Q_{j}-Q_{i})},

(2)

where
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
are the measured radial velocities
of these lines, and
c
is the speed of light.

It was found that the torsion-rotational
transitions in methanol (CH
3
OH) and its isotopologues have very high sensitivity coefficients.
For instance, their
Q
values lie in the interval from
-53
to +43 for CH
3
OH
LKR
;
J11
,
which makes methanol the most favorable molecule when searching for hypothetical
\mu
-variations.
The sensitivity coefficients for these transitions
are calculated by quantum mechanical methods as described in
LKR
,
J11
,

;

Given the complex spatial and dynamical structure of molecular clouds discussed in Sect.
II
,
it is first necessary to define what is the center of the emission line
which probes the gas distribution and kinematics on spatial scales covered by the aperture of the telescope.
In this case, the line profile reflects the integral characteristics
of the radiating region
Weis
.
Therefore, by the center of the emission line we will mean the point at which the
envelope of the line profile reaches its maximum.
In the case of a single line with symmetric Doppler kernel,
this definition coincides exactly with the line centroid.
But in the case of more complex profiles with blends in the wings, this definition
provides more robust estimates of radial velocities
compared to the line centroid because the Sgr B2(N) and (M) spectra
contain many weak emission features, some of which overlap with the strong lines of CH
3
OH.

In this approach,
the envelope curves can be determined by a multicomponent Gaussian model
(with the smallest number of Gaussian subcomponents)
and its fitting to the observed methanol lines
under the condition of achieving the global minimum of the objective function at

\chi^{2}_{\nu}\;\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$<$\kern-7.5pt\raise-4.73611pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\;1
, where
\nu
is the number of degrees of freedom.
Such fits are displayed in Figs.

and
2
where the original spectra are presented by black dots and
the resulting envelope curves are shown in red.
For each line the
SNR

VI
Discussion

It is well known that many torsion-rotational transitions in methanol exhibit
bright maser lines under certain physical conditions in the environment (e.g.,
Leu
).
Therefore, the assessment of the effect of maser activity is important in our case
due to the fact that, on the one hand, spectral lines in methanol have a hyperfine
structure and, on the other hand,
the mechanisms of maser emission are not fully understood.
For instance, the hyperfine splitting of torsion-rotation transitions
in methanol is
\Delta f\sim 40-50
kHz but only one specific hyperfine transition
may form the maser emission and this
transition changes from source to source
Lan18
;
A24

That is why methanol thermal or quasi-thermal lines, whose formation involves all hyperfine transitions,
are more preferable in robust estimates of
\Delta\mu/\mu
(e.g.,
D17

Maser emission is known to be a characteristic feature of star-forming regions.
In the Sgr B2 clouds, masers on various molecules are present, and interferometric

VII
Conclusion

In conclusion, we give the main results of the current work which are listed below.

•

From the publicly available spectra of the molecular clouds Sgr B2(N) and (M) obtained with the IRAM
30-m telescope in the 80-112 GHz frequency range, we selected a number of methanol emission
lines using the selection criteria formulated in Sect.
IV
.

•

For transitions with unknown sensitivity coefficients
Q

to small variations of the fundamental physical constant

\mu=m_{\rm e}/m_{\rm p}
– the electron-to-proton mass ratio,
the corresponding values of
Q
were calculated.

•

The selected spectral lines were processed using a multi-component Gaussian model
to determine the optimal envelope curves minimizing the objective
function with a minimum number of Gaussian subcomponents
under the condition
\chi^{2}\;\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$<$\kern-7.5pt\raise-4.73611pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\;1
per degree of freedom.

•

As a result of this optimization, the positions
of the maxima of the envelope curves, their widths, and their corresponding errors were determined.

•

The measured errors of
\Delta\mu/\mu
were corrected for correlations
in the radial velocity data set.

•

The obtained parameters allowed us to perform further selection by choosing lines with
approximately equal widths (
FWHM
) and discarding those in which hidden blends distort
the Doppler kernels of spectral lines.

•

The relative changes in
\mu
,

\Delta\mu/\mu=(\mu_{\rm obs}-\mu_{\rm lab})/\mu_{\rm lab}
,
in the clouds Sgr B2(N) and (M) were calculated from the remaining methanol
lines as described in Sect.
V
and in the Appendix.

•

The final result can be formulated as follows:

\Delta\mu/\mu=(-3.0\pm 0.6)\times 10^{-7}
was found in the Sgr B2 molecular cloud.
This value is in line with
\Delta\mu/\mu=(-4.2\pm 0.7)\times 10^{-7}

which was obtained from the
Herschel
space telescope methanol spectra
VL25
.

A few remarks on the result obtained should be added to conclude this work.

First
, despite the detected
5\sigma
signal in the
\mu
variation from the
Sgr B2 molecular clouds, we are inclined to regard this result as tentative
because it was obtained from archival spectra not originally designed for
precise measurements of the positions of spectral lines.

Second
, it is desirable to have more accurate values of laboratory frequencies
of methanol lines to reduce errors in the measured radial velocities
and to verify frequency shifts between methanol lines used in this study.

Third
, a search for other molecules in the vicinity of the Galactic center
suitable for
\mu
estimates is highly desirable for independent future tests of the detected
signal.

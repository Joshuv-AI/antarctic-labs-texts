A new analytical technique of the fully implicit Crank-Nicolson discontinuous Galerkin method for the Ginzburg-Landau Model
Xianxian Cao, Zhen Guan, Junjun Wang

Abstract
In this paper, a fully implicit Crank-Nicolson discontinuous Galerkin method is proposed for solving the Ginzburg-Landau equation. By leveraging a novel analytical technique, we rigorously establish the unique solvability of the constructed numerical scheme, as well as its unconditionally optimal error estimates under both the L 2 L^{2} -norm and the energy norm. The core of the proof hinges on the L 2 L^{2} -norm boundedness of the numerical solution and the refined estimation of the cubic nonlinear term. Finally, two numerical examples are presented to validate the theoretical findings.



1
Introduction

The Ginzburg-Landau equation is a class of important complex-valued nonlinear partial differential equations, first proposed by physicists Vitaly Ginzburg and Lev Landau in the 1950s to model low-temperature superconductivity. This model finds significant applications in fields including condensed matter physics, nonlinear optics, and nonequilibrium hydrodynamic systems
Guo2020
. Under different application contexts, the Ginzburg-Landau equation exhibits distinct formulations, such as the nonlinear Ginzburg-Landau equation with a Caputo derivative
Chen2023
, the Ginzburg-Landau-Schrödinger equation
Yao2026
, the Kuramoto-Tsuzuki complex equation with strong nonlinear effects
Zhang2025
, the dynamical Ginzburg-Landau equation under the Coulomb gauge
Gao2023
, and the time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau equation under the temporal gauge
Ma2023
.

In this paper, we are devoted to the discontinuous Galerkin approximation of the following two-dimensional Ginzburg-Landau equation with cubic nonlinearity in a bounded convex polygonal domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

\displaystyle u_{t}-(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)\Delta u+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)|u|^{2}u-\gamma u=0,\quad(\bm{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],

(1.1)

subject to the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition

\displaystyle u(\bm{x},t)=0,\quad(\bm{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],

(1.2)

and the initial condition

\displaystyle u(\bm{x},0)=u^{0}(\bm{x}),\quad\bm{x}\in\bar{\Omega},

(1.3)

where
\nu>0,\kappa>0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are given real constants,
\partial\Omega
represents the boundary of
\Omega
,
\bm{x}=(x,y)
,
\Delta
is the Laplace operator,
\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}
denotes the imaginary unit and
u^{0}(\bm{x})
is a sufficiently smooth complex-valued function with a zero boundary trace.

Due to the significance of this equation, numerous scholars have proposed various effective numerical methods for solving this model, including the finite difference method (FDM)
ZhangYang2025
,
XuChang2011
,
HuChenChang2015
, the finite element method (FEM)
LiCaoZhang2019
,
Shi2020
,
Yang2025
, the meshless method
Li2025
, and the discontinuous polygonal method
GuanCao2025
. More precisely, Zhang and Yang
ZhangYang2025
proposed a linearized three-layer finite difference scheme for the nonlinear variable-coefficient Kuramoto-Tsuzuki complex equation. The scheme employs standard central finite differences for spatial discretization, combines central averaging with central differences for temporal discretization, and adopts a semi-implicit linearization approach to handle the nonlinear terms.
Li and Cao
LiCaoZhang2019
developed a linearized Galerkin finite element method for solving the two-dimensional and three-dimensional Kuramoto-Tsuzuki equation. By classifying and refining the treatment of the nonlinear terms, the authors derived the optimal
L^{2}
-norm error estimate for the numerical solution. Li et al.
Li2025
studied a fast element-free Galerkin (EFG) method for solving the nonlinear complex Ginzburg-Landau equation. Using the space-time error splitting technique, they achieved optimal order convergence. Recently, Guan and Cao
GuanCao2025
proposed a discontinuous Galerkin method on polygonal meshes, implemented via the weighted
\theta
scheme.
They proved the boundedness and optimal convergence under various norms. From the aforementioned literature review, it can be observed that most numerical studies employ semi-implicit schemes, wherein the nonlinear terms are treated explicitly. While such schemes afford computational convenience, they compromise certain inherent properties of the original equation to a limited extent.
Motivated by this consideration, a fully implicit numerical scheme is proposed in this paper, which combines the discontinuous Galerkin method for spatial discretization with the Crank-Nicolson scheme for temporal discretization. To the best of our knowledge, such a fully implicit Crank-Nicolson discontinuous Galerkin scheme for the Ginzburg-Landau equation has not been reported in the existing literature.

As is well established in the field, two distinct classes of numerical schemes are commonly employed for nonlinear partial differential equations: fully implicit scheme and linearized scheme. For linearized scheme, the unconditionally optimal error estimates are usually derived using the space-time error splitting technique proposed by Li and Sun
Li2012
. The core idea is to first introduce a time-discrete system, and then use the inverse inequality to prove the boundedness of the numerical solution in the infinity norm. More precisely,

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{h}U^{n}\|_{\infty}+\|u_{h}^{n}-R_{h}U^{n}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\|U^{n}\|_{2}+Ch^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|u_{h}^{n}-R_{h}U^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|U^{n}\|_{2}+Ch^{2-\frac{d}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C,

where
U^{n}
is the numerical solution of time-discrete system at the
n
-th level,
R_{h}
is a suitable elliptic projection operator,
h
and
\tau
denote the spatial and temporal mesh sizes, respectively. Another method employed in the literature is to prove the boundedness of numerical solution in
H^{1}
-norm by means of mathematical induction
Sun2017
.
However, those methods are only applicable to linearized numerical scheme, and fail for fully implicit algorithm. For fully implicit scheme, there are generally two analytical methods, one of which is the duality argument technique proposed by Feng et al.
FengLiMa2021
. The key points of its proof lie in the Schaefer’s fixed point theorem and the discrete Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality. This method has been successfully applied to solving other nonlinear partial differential equations, including the Schrödinger-Helmholtz equation
GuoChenZhou2025
and the coupled Schrödinger equation
LiLiSun2023
. Another approach involves the cutoff function technique
WangWang2023
, the core idea lies in truncating the nonlinear term to satisfy the global Lipschitz condition. Both of the aforementioned techniques merely establish the existence of solution to the fully discrete numerical scheme and the uniqueness of numerical solution in a neighborhood of exact solution. However, they fail to address the global unique solvability of the scheme.

To establish the global unique solvability of the proposed fully implicit numerical scheme, we develop a novel analytical approach for its proof, drawing on the core concepts of the two aforementioned techniques. This constitutes the primary contribution of this paper.
First, we establish the uniform boundedness of the numerical solution in
L^{2}
-norm. Subsequently, by utilizing this result and conducting a refined estimate of the cubic nonlinear term, we derive an estimation formula for the numerical solution and Ritz projection of the true solution. Finally, we analyze the time step
\tau
and spatial step
h
under two cases (
\tau\leq h
and
\tau>h
), obtain the boundedness of the numerical solution in energy norm, and further establish the unique solvability and optimal convergence rate of the fully discrete numerical scheme. We conjecture that the analytical technique proposed in this paper can be extended to other types of nonlinear partial differential equations.

The structure of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notations, lemmas, and the numerical scheme under investigation. Section
3
is dedicated to the theoretical analysis of the numerical scheme, encompassing the global existence and uniqueness of the numerical solution, as well as its convergence analysis. In Section
4
, two numerical examples are presented to validate the theoretical results. Finally, Section
5
provides a summary of the entire paper and an outlook for future research.

2
The fully implicit Crank-Nicolson discontinuous Galerkin Scheme

In this section, we first introduce key notations and preliminary lemmas employed throughout the paper, upon which we formulate the fully implicit Crank-Nicolson discontinuous Galerkin method.

Through the paper, we adopt the standard Sobolev space notation commonly used in the discontinuous finite element literature. For any integer
m\geq 1
, let
|\cdot|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}
denotes the seminorm and norm of the Sobolev space
H^{m}(\Omega)
, respectively. The norm of the complex-valued Banach space
L^{p}(\Omega)
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
, where

1\leq p\leq\infty
. When
p=2
, the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with respect to the following inner product and norm:

\displaystyle(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}uv^{*}\text{d}\bm{x},\quad\|u\|=\sqrt{(u,u)},\quad u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where
v^{*}
denotes the conjugate of
v
. When no ambiguity arises, this paper adopts similar notations for the inner product and norm of vector functions. Finally, for a strongly measurable function
v:(0,T)\rightarrow X
, where
X
is a complex Banach space, we introduce the Bochner space defined as

L^{p}({0,T;X})=\left\{v:\int_{0}^{T}\|v\|_{X}^{p}\text{d}t<\infty\right\},

with the associated norm

\|v\|_{L^{p}({0,T;X})}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|v\|_{X}^{p}\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

when
p=\infty
, the norm and integral defined above are implicitly subject to the usual modification.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}
denote a sequence of quasi-uniform triangulations of
\Omega
, where
h
denotes the mesh size (defined as the diameter of the largest element in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
). For simplicity, we assume
\mathcal{K}_{h}
is conforming: the intersection of any two distinct elements in the triangulation is either the empty set, a single vertex, or a complete edge. We denote the set of all edges in the partition
\mathcal{K}_{h}
by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
. Moreover, interior edges are collected in the set
\mathcal{E}^{i}_{h}
and boundary edges in
\mathcal{E}^{b}_{h}
. For every element
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
,
h_{K}
is the diameter of
K
. Similarly, define
h_{E}
as the length of each edge
E
. We orient each edge
E
with a fixed normal vector
\bm{n}_{E}
. When
E
is on the boundary of the region
\Omega
, take
\bm{n}_{E}
as the unit outward normal vector of the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
v
be a scalar-valued function defined on
\Omega
, and suppose
v
is sufficiently smooth. This smoothness ensures that
v
has a possibly two-valued trace on every
E\in\mathcal{E}^{i}_{h}
. Then, for all
E\in\mathcal{E}^{i}_{h}
shared by two adjacent elements
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, the average and jump are defined as

\displaystyle\{v\}=\frac{1}{2}(v|_{K_{1}}+v|_{K_{2}}),\quad[v]=v|_{K_{1}}-v|_{K_{2}},

respectively, where the outer normal vector of
K_{1}
on the edge
E
is consistent with
\bm{n}_{E}
. The concepts of average and jump can be extended to the boundary edge
E\in\mathcal{E}^{b}_{h}
:

\{v\}=[v]=v|_{K}.

Next, for a fixed positive integer
k
, we give the complex-valued discontinuous Galerkin approximation subspace
V_{h}^{k}
defined as

\displaystyle V_{h}^{k}=\{v_{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega):v_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K),~\forall~K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)
denotes the space of polynomials of total degree less than or equal to
k
. In the finite-dimensional space
V_{h}^{k}
, the discontinuous Galerkin norm is defined as follows

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{\text{DG}}:=\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla v_{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{E}}\int_{E}[v_{h}]^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/2},

(2.1)

where
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Based on the arguments in Reference
PietroErn2010
, it can be concluded that the following Sobolev embedding inequality holds

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{p}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{\text{DG}},\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

(2.2)

We further present the discrete Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality
GazcaOrozcoKaltenbach2025
which is essential to the derivation process of the optimal error estimate

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{4}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{\text{DG}}^{1/2}\|v_{h}\|^{1/2},\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

(2.3)

We also recall the following global inverse inequality
Chave2016
,
GuanCao2025
:

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq Ch^{-1}\|v_{h}\|,\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

(2.4)

Moreover, let us define the discrete bilinear form corresponding to the continuous bilinear form
(\nabla u,\nabla v)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v_{h},w_{h})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}w_{h}^{*}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{E}\{\nabla_{h}v_{h}\}\cdot\bm{n}_{E}[w_{h}^{*}]\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{E}[v_{h}]\{\nabla_{h}w_{h}^{*}\}\cdot\bm{n}_{E}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\frac{\lambda}{h_{E}}\int_{E}[v_{h}][w_{h}^{*}]\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall~v_{h},w_{h}\in V_{h}^{k},

where
\nabla\cdot
is the piecewise-defined gradient operator and parameter
\lambda
, known as the penalty term, is a sufficiently large non-negative real number in this context.
It is a well-established fact that the discrete bilinear form introduced earlier satisfies the coercivity and continuity properties outlined below

\displaystyle C_{1}\|v_{h}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}\leq a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})=:\|v_{h}\|^{2}_{a_{h}}\leq C_{2}\|v_{h}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2},\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

(2.5)

On this basis, we define the elliptic projection operator
R_{h}:H^{k+1}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{h}^{k}
satisfying

\displaystyle a_{h}(R_{h}u,v_{h})=-(\Delta u,v_{h}),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

(2.6)

From the argument in
DiPietroErn2012
,
Riviere2008
, for every
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
, we know the following boundedness and approximation properties

\displaystyle\|u-R_{h}u\|_{\infty}+\|R_{h}u\|_{\infty}\leq C\|u\|_{k+1},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|R_{h}u-u\|+h\|R_{h}u-u\|_{\text{DG}}\leq Ch^{k+1}\|u\|_{k+1}.

(2.8)

Let
\{t_{n}:n=0,1,2,\cdots,N\}
be the uniform partition of the interval
[0,T]
with the time step
\tau=T/N
and denote
t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}:=(n-\frac{1}{2})\tau
. For a series of functions
\{\phi^{n}:n=0,1,2,\cdots,N\}
defined on the domain
\Omega
, we write

\displaystyle D_{\tau}\phi^{n}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\phi^{n}-\phi^{n-1}}{\tau},\quad\hat{\phi}^{n}:=\frac{\phi^{n}+\phi^{n-1}}{2},\quad n=1,2,\cdots,N-1,N.

Using some of the notations given in the preceding text, the fully implicit Crank-Nicolosn discontinuous Galerkin algorithm is to seek
u_{h}^{n}\in V_{h}^{k}
such that for
n=1,2,\cdots,N-1,N

\displaystyle(D_{\tau}u_{h}^{n},v_{h})+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)a_{h}(\hat{u}_{h}^{n},v_{h})+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)(|\hat{u}^{n}_{h}|^{2}\hat{u}_{h}^{n},v_{h})-\gamma(\hat{u}_{h}^{n},v_{h})=0,\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k},

(2.9)

where
u_{h}^{0}
is taken as the Ritz projection
R_{h}u^{0}
of
u^{0}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem
Akrivis1991
,
Akrivis1993
)
Let
(H,(\cdot,\cdot))
be a finite-dimensional inner product space, with
\|\cdot\|
the associated norm, and
\Pi:H\to H
be continuous. Assume moreover that there exists an
a>0
, for any
z\in H
and
\|z\|=a
, it holds that

\operatorname{Re}(\Pi(z),z)\geqslant 0.

Then there is a
z^{\prime}\in H
satisfying
\|z^{\prime}\|\leqslant a
such that
\Pi(z^{\prime})=0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Discrete Gronwall inequality
HeywoodRannacher1990
)
Let
A\geq 0,B\geq 0
,
\{\eta^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{\xi^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be two sequences of non-negative real numbers satisfying

\quad\eta^{n}+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi^{i}\leq A+B\tau\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta^{i},\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

Then, the following holds when
\tau\leq\frac{1}{2B}

\eta_{n}+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi^{i}\leq A\exp(2Bn\tau),\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Discrete Gronwall inequality
Sun2023
)
Suppose
\{F^{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
is a nonnegative sequence,
C
and
G
are two non-negative constants satisfying

\displaystyle F^{n}\leqslant(1+C\tau)F^{n-1}+\tau G,\quad n=1,2,\cdots,N,

then

\displaystyle F^{n}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{Cn\tau}\left(F^{0}+\frac{G}{C}\right),\quad n=1,2,\cdots,N.

(2.10)

Lemma 2.4
.

(Transfer Inequality
Wang2014
) For a normed linear space
\mathcal{V}
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{V}}
, and for elements
v^{0},v^{1},\ldots,v^{N}\in\mathcal{V}
, the result below holds

\displaystyle\|v^{n}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\leq 2\sum_{m=1}^{n}\left\|\hat{v}^{m}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|v^{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}},\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

(2.11)

3
Numerical analysis of the fully implicit discontinuous Galerkin scheme

In this section, we initiate the theoretical analysis of the proposed numerical scheme. To this end, we first establish the existence of the numerical solution, followed by a rigorous proof of its
L^{2}
-norm boundedness. Based on this result, we further derive the boundedness of the numerical solution under the energy norm, as well as the optimal order error estimates in both the
L^{2}
-norm and the energy norm.

3.1
Existence of the discontinuous Galerkin solution

Theorem 3.1
.

When
\tau\gamma<2
, the fully discrete discontinuous Galerkin algorithm (
2.9
) has a solution.

Proof.

Assume that the numerical solution
u_{h}^{n-1}
at the
(n-1)
-th layer has been obtained. Then (
2.9
) can be interpreted as a nonlinear system in terms of the average solution
\hat{u}_{h}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\tau}\left(\hat{u}_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right)+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)a_{h}(\hat{u}_{h}^{n},v_{h})+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)(|\hat{u}^{n}_{h}|^{2}\hat{u}_{h}^{n},v_{h})-\gamma(\hat{u}_{h}^{n},v_{h})=0,\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

(3.1)

Obviously, if we can prove the existence of a solution to equation (
3.1
), then we can easily know that
u_{h}^{n}=2\hat{u}_{h}^{n}-u_{h}^{n-1}
is
a solution of (
2.9
). For this purpose, define the operator
\Pi:V_{h}^{k}\rightarrow V_{h}^{k}
by

\displaystyle\left(\Pi(w),v_{h}\right)=\frac{2}{\tau}\left(w-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right)+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)a_{h}(w,v_{h})+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)(|w|^{2}w,v_{h})-\gamma(w,v_{h}),\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

(3.2)

Next, we will prove that this operator is continuous. In fact, given
w_{1},w_{2}\in V_{h}^{k}
, it can be deduced from (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\left(\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2}),v_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\tau}\left(w_{1}-w_{2},v_{h}\right)+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)a_{h}(w_{1}-w_{2},v_{h})+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)(|w_{1}|^{2}w_{1}-|w_{2}|^{2}w_{2},v_{h})

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma(w_{1}-w_{2},v_{h}),\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

(3.3)

By taking
v_{h}=\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})
in (
3.3
) and employing the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we find that

\displaystyle\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{a_{h}}\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|_{a_{h}}+C\left\||w_{1}|^{2}w_{1}-|w_{2}|^{2}w_{2}\right\|\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left(C+\frac{2}{\tau}\right)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\text{DG}}\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|_{\text{DG}}+C\left\||w_{1}|^{2}w_{1}-|w_{2}|^{2}w_{2}\right\|\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left(C+\frac{2}{\tau}\right)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{h^{2}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|+\left(C+\frac{2}{\tau}\right)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{\infty}+\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}\|w_{2}\|_{\infty}+\|w_{2}\|^{2}_{\infty}\right)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|,

(3.4)

where we have also used (
2.5
), (
2.4
) and the following elementary inequality

\displaystyle\left||z_{1}|^{2}z_{1}-|z_{2}|^{2}z_{2}\right|\leq\left(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{1}||z_{2}|+|z_{2}|^{2}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|,\quad\forall~z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}.

(3.5)

By dividing both sides of inequality (
3.4
) by
\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|
simultaneously, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\Pi(w_{1})-\Pi(w_{2})\|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{h^{2}}+1+\frac{2}{\tau}+\|w_{1}\|^{2}_{\infty}+\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}\|w_{2}\|_{\infty}+\|w_{2}\|^{2}_{\infty}\right)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|.

(3.6)

From (
3.6
), we can easily see that the operator
\Pi
is continuous. Furthermore, choosing
v_{h}=w
in (
3.2
), taking the real part of both sides and using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality give that

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left(\Pi(w),w\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\tau}\left(\|w\|^{2}-\text{Re}(u_{h}^{n-1},w)\right)+\nu\|w\|_{a_{h}}+\kappa\|w\|_{4}^{4}-\gamma\|w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\tau}\left(\|w\|^{2}-\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|\|w\|\right)-\gamma\|w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\tau}\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\gamma\tau\right)\|w\|-\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|\right]\cdot\|w\|.

(3.7)

Obviously, when
\tau\gamma<2
and
\|w\|=\frac{\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|}{1-\frac{1}{2}\tau\gamma}+1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left(\Pi(w),w\right)\geq 0.

By the Brouwer fixed point theorem described in Lemma
2.1
, we can conclude that there exists a
w^{\prime}
satisfying
\|w^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|}{1-\frac{1}{2}\tau\gamma}+1
such that

\Pi(w^{\prime})=0.

Therefore, there is a solution to (
2.9
).
All this completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Boundedness of the discontinuous Galerkin solution

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
u_{h}^{n}
is the solution of the fully discrete numerical scheme (
2.9
). Then when
\tau\gamma\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|\leq e^{2T\max\{0,\gamma\}}\|u_{h}^{0}\|,\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

Proof.

Taking
v_{h}=\hat{u}_{h}^{n}
in (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\left(D_{\tau}u_{h}^{n},\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\right)+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)\|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)\|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}^{4}-\gamma\|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}=0.

(3.8)

Noticing the fact that

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left(D_{\tau}u_{h}^{n},\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\right)=\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\|u_{h}^{n}\|^{2}-\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}\right).

Taking the real part of both sides of equation (
3.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|^{2}-\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}\leq 2\tau\gamma\|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2},\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

(3.9)

Therefore, when
\gamma\leq 0
, it arrives that

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|\leq\|u_{h}^{0}\|,\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

(3.10)

When
\gamma>0
, it follows from (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|^{2}-\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}\leq 2\tau\gamma\|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\tau\gamma}{2}\left(\|u_{h}^{n}\|+\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|\right)^{2}.

A simple transformation gives

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\tau\gamma}{2}\right)\|u_{h}^{n}\|\leq\left(1+\frac{\tau\gamma}{2}\right)\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|,\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

When
\tau\gamma\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|\leq\frac{1+\frac{\tau\gamma}{2}}{1-\frac{\tau\gamma}{2}}\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|=\left(1+\frac{\tau\gamma}{1-\frac{\tau\gamma}{2}}\right)\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|\leq\left(1+2\tau\gamma\right)\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|,\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

By virtue of the discrete Gronwall inequality given in Lemma
2.3
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|\leq e^{2\gamma n\tau}\|u_{h}^{0}\|\leq e^{2\gamma T}\|u_{h}^{0}\|,\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

(3.11)

It can be concluded that the theorem holds true by combining inequalities (
3.10
) and (
3.11
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

By utilizing the definition of the initial approximate solution
u_{h}^{0}
and the approximation property (
2.8
) of the Ritz projection, we can easily obtain that

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{0}\|=\|R_{h}u^{0}\|\leq\|R_{h}u^{0}-u^{0}\|+\|u^{0}\|\leq C.

(3.12)

Using the conclusions of Theorem
3.2
and (
3.12
), it is known that there exists a positive constant
C
such that the following equation holds

\|u_{h}^{n}\|\leq C,\quad 0\leq n\leq N.

i.e., the numerical solution
\|u_{h}^{n}\|
is bounded in the
L^{2}
-norm. Furthermore, with the help of the estimate (
2.8
) of the Ritz projection
R_{h}
, we know that

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|=\|R_{h}u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\|\leq\|R_{h}u^{n}\|+\|u^{n}_{h}\|\leq C,\quad 0\leq n\leq N.

(3.13)

To prove the boundedness of the numerical solution under the energy norm, we present the following crucial lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u_{h}^{n}
be the solution of the equation (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) and
u(t_{n})
is the solution of the discrete problem (
2.9
). Denote

\displaystyle\xi^{n}=u^{n}-R_{h}u^{n},\quad\eta_{h}^{n}=R_{h}u^{n}-u_{h}^{n},\quad 0\leq n\leq N.

Then there exist a positive constant
\tau_{1}
such that when
\tau\leq\tau_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|+\tau\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq C\left(\tau^{2}+h^{k+1}\right),\quad 0\leq n\leq N.

(3.14)

Proof.

By virtue of the strong consistency of the interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin scheme (
2.9
), it can be concluded that the exact solution satisfies the following equation at time
t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}
:

\displaystyle(D_{\tau}u^{n},v_{h})+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)a_{h}(\hat{u}^{n},v_{h})+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)(|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n},v_{h})-\gamma(\hat{u}^{n},v_{h})=(R^{n},v_{h}),\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k},

(3.15)

where

\displaystyle R^{n}

\displaystyle=D_{\tau}u^{n}-u_{t}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)\left(\Delta u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}-\Delta\hat{u}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)\left(|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n}-|u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}|\right)-\gamma\left(\hat{u}^{n}-u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right),

and

\displaystyle u_{t}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}=u_{t}(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}),\quad u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}=u(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}).

Subtracting (
3.15
) from (
2.9
) and utilizing the fact
u^{n}=\xi^{n}+\eta_{h}^{n}
, we have the system of error equations

\displaystyle\quad(D_{\tau}\eta_{h}^{n},v_{h})+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)a_{h}(\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n},v_{h})+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)\left(|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n}-|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}_{h}^{n},v_{h}\right)-\gamma(\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n},v_{h})

\displaystyle=(R^{n},v_{h})-(D_{\tau}\xi^{n},v_{h})+\gamma(\hat{\xi}^{n},v_{h}),\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k}.

(3.16)

Choosing
v_{h}=\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}
in (
3.16
) and taking the real part of the resulting equation, it arrives at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(\|u_{h}^{n}\|^{2}-\|u_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}\right)+\nu\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\text{Re}\left\{(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)\left(|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n}-|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}_{h}^{n},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\right)\right\}+\gamma\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\text{Re}(R^{n},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})-\text{Re}(D_{\tau}\xi^{n},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})+\gamma\text{Re}(\hat{\xi}^{n},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}).

(3.17)

Next, we estimate each term on the right-hand side of equation (
3.17
). Noticing that

\displaystyle|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n}-|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\left(\hat{\xi}^{n}+R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\right)-|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\left(R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}-\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{\xi}^{n}+R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\left(|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}-|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\right)+|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{\xi}^{n}+R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\left(\hat{u}^{n}(\hat{u}^{n}-\hat{u}_{h}^{n})^{*}+(\hat{u}^{n}-\hat{u}_{h}^{n})(\hat{u}_{h}^{n})^{*}\right)+|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{\xi}^{n}+R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\left(\hat{u}^{n}(\hat{\xi}^{n}+\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})^{*}+(\hat{\xi}^{n}+\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})(R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}-\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})^{*}\right)+|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}

\displaystyle=|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{\xi}^{n}+R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\left(\hat{u}^{n}(\hat{\xi}^{n})^{*}+\hat{u}^{n}(\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})^{*}+\hat{\xi}^{n}(R_{h}\hat{u}^{n})^{*}-\hat{\xi}^{n}(\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})^{*}+\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}(R_{h}\hat{u}^{n})^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-(R_{h}\hat{u}^{n})\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}(\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})^{*}+|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}.

Then we can readily obtain the estimation for the first term on the right-hand side of equation (
3.17
). In fact,

\displaystyle\quad-\text{Re}\left\{(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)\left(|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n}-|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}_{h}^{n},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\hat{u}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\hat{\xi}^{n}\|\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|+\|R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\|_{\infty}\big(\|\hat{u}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\hat{\xi}^{n}\|\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|+\|\hat{u}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\|R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\hat{\xi}^{n}\|\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|+\|\hat{\xi}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}+\|R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}\big)+\|R_{h}\hat{u}^{n}\|_{\infty}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}^{2}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\hat{\xi}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|_{4}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\hat{\xi}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|\right),

(3.18)

where the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is applied in the first inequality above, the boundedness (
3.13
) of
R_{h}
in
L^{2}
-norm is used in the second inequality, and the discrete Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality (
2.3
) is employed in the third inequality.
Next, the Taylor expansion formula with an integral remainder term can be used to obtain

\displaystyle\text{Re}(R^{n},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|D_{\tau}u^{n}-u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\|+\|\Delta u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}-\Delta\hat{u}^{n}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left\||\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n}-|u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|+\|\hat{u}^{n}-u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\|\Big)\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq C(\tau^{4}+\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}),

(3.19)

where we have used the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|D_{\tau}u^{n}-u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}(s-t_{n-1})^{2}u_{ttt}(s)\text{d}s+\int_{t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}}^{t_{n}}(s-t_{n})^{2}u_{ttt}(s)\text{d}s\right\|\leq C\tau^{2},

\displaystyle\|\Delta u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}-\Delta\hat{u}^{n}\|\leq C\tau\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\|\Delta u_{tt}\|\text{d}s\leq C\tau^{2},

\displaystyle\left\||\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n}-|u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\||\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}(\hat{u}^{n}-u^{n-\frac{1}{2}})+\left(|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}-|u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\right)u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\||\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}(\hat{u}^{n}-u^{n-\frac{1}{2}})+\text{Re}\left((\hat{u}^{n}-u^{n-\frac{1}{2}})(\hat{u}^{n}+u^{n-\frac{1}{2}})^{*}\right)u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{2},

and

\displaystyle\|u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{u}^{n}\|\leq C\tau\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\|u_{tt}\|\text{d}s\leq C\tau^{2}.

In the last, applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the approximation property (
2.8
), it follows that

\displaystyle-\text{Re}(D_{\tau}\xi^{n},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})+\gamma\text{Re}(\hat{\xi}^{n},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|D_{\tau}\xi^{n}\|+\|\hat{\xi}^{n}\|)\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(R_{h}(u_{t})-u_{t})\text{d}s\right\|+\|\hat{\xi}^{n}\|\right)\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(h^{2k+2}+\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}\right).

(3.20)

Substituting (
3.18
)–(
3.20
) into (
3.17
) and multiplying both sides of the resulting equation by
2\tau
yields

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|^{2}-\|\eta_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}+2\nu C_{1}\tau\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\tau^{5}+\tau h^{2k+2}+\tau\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}+\tau\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\tau^{5}+\tau h^{2k+2}+\tau\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}\right)+\nu C_{1}\tau\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}.

Combining like terms, we have

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|^{2}-\|\eta_{h}^{n-1}\|^{2}+\nu C_{1}\tau\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}\leq C\left(\tau^{5}+\tau h^{2k+2}+\tau\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}\right),\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

(3.21)

Replacing
n
by
j
in (
3.21
) and summing over
j
from
1
to
n
, we get

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|^{2}+\nu C_{1}\tau\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{j}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}\leq C(\tau^{4}+h^{2k+2})+C\tau\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\|\eta_{h}^{j}\|^{2},\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

According to the Gronwall inequality presented in Lemma
2.3
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|^{2}+\nu C_{1}\tau\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{j}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}\leq C(\tau^{4}+h^{2k+2}).

(3.22)

Employing the transfer inequality (
2.11
), (
3.22
) and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality give that

\displaystyle\tau\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq 2\tau\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{j}\|\leq C{\sqrt{n\tau}}\sqrt{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\nu C_{1}\tau\|\hat{\eta}_{h}^{j}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}}\leq C(\tau^{2}+h^{k+1}).

(3.23)

In combination with (
3.22
), it follows that the conclusion in (
3.14
) is true.
∎

With the above preparations, the boundedness of the numerical solution under the energy norm follows immediately.

Theorem 3.3
.

The solution
u_{h}^{n}
of the fully discrete algorithm (
2.9
) is bounded in
DG
-norm, i.e., there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq C,\quad 0\leq n\leq N.

Proof.

The core idea of the argument is to conduct a classified discussion on the spatial parameter
h
and the temporal parameter
\tau
. when
\tau\leq h
, it follows from the global inverse inequality (
2.4
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq Ch^{-1}\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|\leq Ch\leq C.

(3.24)

When
h<\tau
, we can easily obtain from (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq C\tau^{-1}(\tau^{2}+h^{k+1})\leq C\tau\leq C.

(3.25)

Through the combination of (
3.24
) and (
3.25
) and the fact that
\|R_{h}u^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq C\|u^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}
, the truth of the theorem can be established.
∎

Using the boundedness of the numerical solution under the energy norm, the uniqueness of the numerical solution follows immediately.

Theorem 3.4
.

The solution of the discrete Galerkin scheme (
2.9
) is unique for sufficiently small
\tau
.

Proof.

Adopt the background in Lemma
3.1
. To prove the uniqueness of the numerical solution, suppose that
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\tau}\left(w_{1}-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right)+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)a_{h}(w_{1},v_{h})+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)(|w_{1}|^{2}w_{1},v_{h})-\gamma(w_{1},v_{h})=0,\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k},

(3.26)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\tau}\left(w_{2}-u_{h}^{n-1},v_{h}\right)+(\nu+\mathrm{i}\alpha)a_{h}(w_{2},v_{h})+(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)(|w_{2}|^{2}w_{2},v_{h})-\gamma(w_{2},v_{h})=0,\quad\forall~v_{h}\in V_{h}^{k},

(3.27)

respectively. Subtracting (
3.27
) from (
3.26
), taking
v_{h}=w_{1}-w_{2}
and considering the real part on both sides of the corresponding equation give

\displaystyle\quad\frac{2}{\tau}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|^{2}+\nu\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-Re\left((\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)(|w_{1}|^{2}w_{1}-|w_{2}|^{2}w_{2},w_{1}-w_{2})\right)+\gamma\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left((\|w_{1}\|_{8}^{2}+\|w_{1}\|_{8}\|w_{2}\|_{8}+\|w_{2}\|_{8}^{2})\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{4}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left((\|w_{1}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}+\|w_{1}\|_{\text{DG}}\|w_{2}\|_{\text{DG}}+\|w_{2}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2})\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\text{DG}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|^{\frac{3}{2}}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|^{2}+\nu\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{a_{h}}^{2},

(3.28)

where we have used the elementary inequality (
3.5
) and Hölder inequality in the first inequality, followed by the imbedding inequality (
2.2
) and discrete Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality (
2.3
) in the second equality, and the Young inequality in the third inequlity.
From (
3.28
), we can immediately obtain

\displaystyle\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|\leq C\tau\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|.

Therefore, for a suitably small
\tau
, we have
w_{1}=w_{2}
, i.e., the scheme (
2.9
) is uniquely solvable.
∎

In tha last, we establish the unconditional optimal error estimates of the numerical solution in both the
L^{2}
-norm and the energy norm.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
u^{n}
and
u_{h}^{n}
be the solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) and (
2.9
), respectively, then there exist two positive constants
\tau_{0}
and
h_{0}
such that when
\tau\leq\tau_{1}
,
h\leq h_{1}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\|\leq C\left(\tau^{2}+h^{k+1}\right),\quad\|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq C\left(\tau^{2}+h^{k}\right).

(3.29)

Proof.

We first present the optimal order error estimate in the
L^{2}
-norm. For this purpose, utilizing the triangle inequality and Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\|\leq\|\xi^{n}\|+\|\eta^{n}_{h}\|\leq C\left(\tau^{2}+h^{k+1}\right).

(3.30)

Next, taking
v_{h}=D_{\tau}\eta_{h}^{n}
in (
3.16
) and the real part together with the similar procedure as in Lemma
3.1
lead to

\displaystyle\quad\|D_{\tau}\eta_{h}^{n}\|^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2\tau}\left(\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}-\|\eta_{h}^{n-1}\|_{a_{h}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\text{Re}\left\{(\kappa+\mathrm{i}\beta)\left(|\hat{u}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}^{n}-|\hat{u}_{h}^{n}|^{2}\hat{u}_{h}^{n},D_{\tau}\eta^{n}_{h}\right)\right\}-\gamma\left(\hat{\eta}_{h}^{n},D_{\tau}\eta_{h}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\text{Re}(R^{n},D_{\tau}\eta_{h}^{n})-\text{Re}(D_{\tau}\xi^{n},D_{\tau}\eta_{h}^{n})+\gamma\text{Re}(\hat{\xi}^{n},D_{\tau}\eta_{h}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\tau^{4}+h^{2k+2}\right)+\|D_{\tau}\eta_{h}^{n}\|^{2}+C\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}.

(3.31)

It should be emphasized here that we used the
L^{2}
-norm error estimation (
3.30
) in the above argument.
Multiplying both sides of inequality (
3.31
) by
2\tau
and summing up, we get

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq C\tau\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}\|\eta_{h}^{m}\|_{\text{DG}}^{2}+C\left(\tau^{2}+h^{2k+2}\right).

Applying the discrete Gronwall inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq C\left(\tau^{2}+h^{k+1}\right).

It follows from the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-u_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq\|\xi^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}+\|\eta_{h}^{n}\|_{\text{DG}}\leq C\left(\tau^{2}+h^{k}\right).

The proof is completed.
∎

Conclusions

Based on the Crank-Nicolson scheme, this paper proposes a fully implicit discontinuous Galerkin fully discrete scheme. The primary innovation of this work resides in the development of a novel analytical technique, through which the unique solvability and optimal error estimates of the numerical solution are rigorously derived. This work’s proof framework can be effectively utilized as a reference for analyzing fully implicit numerical schemes applied to a broader class of nonlinear partial differential equations. For future research, several promising directions are worthy of exploration. First, extending the proposed analytical reasoning technique to three-dimensional settings merits further investigation. Second, this study only employs the second-order Crank-Nicolson scheme for analytical purposes. In fact, it remains an open question whether the developed analytical method can be generalized to high-order temporal discretization schemes, such as the spectral collocation method and the discontinuous Galerkin time-stepping method.

Non-vanishing of central values of L-functions with angular restrictions
Filippo Berta and Svenja zur Verth

Abstract
. We study the angular restrictions for the second moment of toroidal families of L L -functions using the general theory of trace functions. With the mollification technique we deduce non-vanishing of a positive proportion. Our two main ingredients are classification results of Katz to determine the sheaves at play and a recent result of Fouvry, Kowalski, Michel and Sawin to bound bilinear sums of their trace functions.

We briefly discuss the strategy for the proof of Theorem

. After applying standard techniques, like the approximate functional equation, for the study of Dirichlet
L

to obtaining estimates for bilinear correlation sums of the following shape:

(1.4)

\frac{1}{(qMN)^{1/2}}\sum_{m\sim M}\sum_{n\sim N}\alpha_{m}K(m^{a}n^{b})\ll_{a,b,k}q^{-\eta},

where

is depending on
a
,

and
k
and of the shape

\Kl_{k}(u;q)\coloneqq\frac{1}{q^{(k-1)/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{k}\equiv u\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{x_{1}+\dots+x_{k}}{q}\right)

or

\K_{k}^{a,b}(u;q)\coloneqq\frac{1}{q^{(k+1)/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{k},y_{1},y_{2}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{k}y_{1}^{a}y_{2}^{b}\equiv u\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{x_{1}+\dots+x_{k}+y_{1}+y_{2}}{q}\right).

As it is often the case in analytic number theory, this

is the trace function of a complex of
\ell
-adic sheaves. Although hoping for cancellation as in (
1.4
) for every trace function is maybe too optimistic, in
[

]
, Fouvry, Kowalski, Michel and Sawin prove cancellation for a broad family of such trace functions, that is, trace functions whose attached complex of
\ell
-adic sheaves is

[
Kat90
]
who developed the theory of how to identify the monodromy groups. Our paper is structured in the following way.

•

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{k/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geqslant 1\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mn)^{1/2}}V\left(\frac{mn}{X}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\\
\xi m^{a}n^{b}x_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{k}\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{x_{1}+\ldots+x_{k}}{q}\right),

where in the last step we have used orthogonality of characters for the sum over
\chi
.
We treat the remaining three summands analogously and obtain

\displaystyle M_{a,b,k}^{even}(\xi;q)=\frac{1}{2q^{1/2}}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geqslant 1\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mn)^{1/2}}V\left(\frac{mn}{X}\right)\left(\Kl_{k}(\bar{\xi}m^{-a}n^{-b};q)+\Kl_{k}(-\bar{\xi}m^{-a}n^{-b};q)\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geqslant 1,\pm\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mn)^{1/2}}V\left(\frac{mn}{Y}\right)\left(\K^{a,b}_{k}(\bar{\xi}m^{a}n^{b};q)+\K^{a,b}_{k}(-\bar{\xi}m^{a}n^{b};q)\right)\Bigg).

For the odd part we note that
t(\chi^{a})+t(\chi^{b})
depends only on the parity of
a,b
if the parity of
\chi
is set to be odd. We have
t(\chi^{a})+t(\chi^{b})=(a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{2}))+(b\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{2}))
. We then analogously, with this time

, have

\displaystyle M_{a,b,k}^{odd}(\xi;q)=\frac{(-i)^{\tau}}{2q^{1/2}}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geqslant 1\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mn)^{1/2}}V\left(\frac{mn}{X}\right)\left(\Kl_{k}(\bar{\xi}m^{-a}n^{-b};q)-\Kl_{k}(-\bar{\xi}m^{-a}n^{-b};q)\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geqslant 1,\pm\\
(mn,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mn)^{1/2}}V\left(\frac{mn}{Y}\right)\left(\K^{a,b}_{k}(\bar{\xi}m^{a}n^{b};q)-\K^{a,b}_{k}(-\bar{\xi}m^{a}n^{b};q)\right)\Bigg),

where
\tau=(a\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{2}))+(b\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{2}))
.
Using the definitions of
M_{a,b,\Kl_{k}}(u)
and
M_{a,b,\K_{k}^{a,b}}(u)
this shows the claim.
For
k<0
note that
\varepsilon(\chi)^{-1}=\varepsilon(\bar{\chi})\chi(-1)
. Therefore the above analysis is analogous and leads to the same objects for
|k|
.
∎

3.
Reduction to hypergeometric sums

While the Kloosterman sums
\Kl_{k}
are classically related to a well studied
\ell
-adic sheaf, this is not the case for
\K_{k}^{a,b}
. In this section we establish a relation of the latter to a well known
\ell
-adic sheaf using the Hasse-Davenport Relation. This section closely follows upcoming work of Fouvry, Kowalski, Michel and Sawin,
[
Fou+25a
]
.
Recall that

\K_{k}^{a,b}(u;q)\coloneqq\frac{1}{q^{(k+1)/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{k},y_{1},y_{2}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{k}y_{1}^{a}y_{2}^{b}\equiv u\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{x_{1}+\dots+x_{k}+y_{1}+y_{2}}{q}\right).

More generally, for every finite field
F
and a choice of a non-trivial
\ell
-adic character
\psi
on
F
we define

(3.1)

\K_{k}^{a,b}(u;\psi,F)\coloneqq\frac{1}{|{F}|^{(k+1)/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{k},y_{1},y_{2}\in F\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{k}y_{1}^{a}y_{2}^{b}=u\end{subarray}}\psi(x_{1}+\dots+x_{k}+y_{1}+y_{2}).

For two functions
f,g
on
F^{\times}
, we define the multiplicative convolution

f\ast g(u)=\frac{1}{{|{F}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\sum_{xy=u}f(x)g(y).

We see that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x^{a}=u_{1}}\psi(ax)\ast\sum_{x^{b}=u_{2}}\psi(bx)\ast\psi\ast\dots\ast\psi\right)(u)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\frac{1}{|{F}|^{\frac{k+1}{2}}}\sum_{x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}y_{1}\cdots y_{k}=u}\psi(ax_{1}+bx_{2}+y_{1}+\cdots+y_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\K_{k}^{a,b}(a^{a}b^{b}u;\psi,F)

By the general theory of convolution
[
Kat12
]
(see also
[
Kat90
]
), we deduce that every
\K_{k}^{a,b}(u;\psi,F)
is the trace function of a complex of
\ell
-adic sheaves, mixed of weight
\leqslant 0
and lisse on
\mathbb{G}_{m/F}
, which we denote
\mathcal{K}_{k}^{a,b}(\psi)
.

To further study the correlation properties of
\K_{k}^{a,b}(\bullet;\psi)
we first relate
\mathcal{K}^{a,b}_{k}(\psi)
to a well-studied
\ell
-adic sheaf.

Let
F
be a finite field and let
\bm{\chi}
and
\bm{\theta}
denote multisets (possibly empty) of
\ell
-adic multiplicative characters defined on
F^{\times}
of cardinality
r
and
t
respectively. The hypergeometric function attached to
\bm{\chi}
and
\bm{\theta}
is the following function on
F^{\times}
:

\Hyp(u,\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta};\psi,F)=\frac{1}{|F|^{\frac{r+t-1}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\ldots,x_{r},y_{1},\ldots,y_{t}\\
\frac{x_{1}\cdots x_{r}}{y_{1}\cdots y_{t}}=u\end{subarray}}\psi(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{r}-y_{1}-\ldots-y_{t})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\chi_{i}(x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{t}\bar{\theta}_{i}(y_{i}).

Each function
\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta};\psi,F)
is the trace function of
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta},\psi)
, the so-called
hypergeometric sheaf
, lisse over
{\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{/F}
. Hypergeometric sheaves have been extensively studied in
[
Kat90
]
, Chapter 8.

We set some notation: we denote by
\sqcup
the union of multisets, in particular if
\rho\in\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta}
has multiplicity
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
respectively, then
\rho
has multiplicity
r_{1}+r_{2}
in
\bm{\chi}\sqcup\bm{\theta}.
For two multisets
\bm{\chi}
and
\bm{\theta}
as above, the intersection
\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta}
is the multiset where
\rho
appear in
\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta}
with multiplicity equal to the minimum of the multiplicities of
\rho
in
\bm{\chi}
and
\bm{\theta}
. The multiset
\bm{\chi}\smallsetminus\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta}
is obtained by subtracting multiplicities, that is if
\rho
has multiplicity
m
in
\bm{\chi}
and multiplicity
n
in
\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta}
, then
\rho
has multiplicity
m-n
in
\bm{\chi}\smallsetminus\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta}
. We say that
\bm{\chi}
and
\bm{\theta}
are distinct if
\bm{\chi}\smallsetminus\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta}
or
\bm{\theta}\smallsetminus\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta}
is non-empty (that is, it has an element of multiplicity strictly bigger than
0
).

The following can be found in
[
Kat88
]
, § 5.6.

Lemma 3.1

(Hasse-Davenport Relation)
.

Let
F
be a finite field. Let
\psi\colon F\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\chi\colon F^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
characters. We denote by
\varepsilon(\psi,\chi)=\frac{1}{|{F}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{x\in F^{\times}}\psi(x)\chi(x)
the normalized Gauss sum. For
\lambda\in F
we denote the character
F\ni x\mapsto\psi(\lambda x)
by
\psi_{\lambda}
.

Then we have for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
so that
N||{F^{\times}}|
that

-\varepsilon(\psi_{N},\chi^{N})=\varepsilon_{N}(\psi)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\widehat{F^{\times}}\\
\rho^{N}=1\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(\psi,\chi\rho),

where
\varepsilon_{N}(\psi)
is a complex number of absolute value
1
.

Recall the definition of
\bm{\rho}[a]
from the notation section. We deduce the following

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}
and let
F
be a finite field so that
a,b||{F^{\times}}|
. Let
u\neq 0.

We can express the sum
\K_{k}^{a,b}
as a hypergeometric sum in the following way:

(3.2)

\K_{k}^{a,b}(a^{a}b^{b}u;\psi,F)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[a,b]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\varnothing;\psi,F)&a,b>0,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[a]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[b];\psi,F)&a>0>b,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[b]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[a];\psi,F)&b>0>a,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[a]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[b];\psi,F)&0>a,b.\end{cases}

Proof.

We orient ourselves at
[
Fou+25a
]
. Suppose first
a>0
, we compute the discrete Mellin transform of the following function in two ways

(3.3)

\widehat{F^{\times}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\chi\mapsto-\varepsilon(\psi_{a},\chi^{a}).

By the definition of discrete Mellin transform we have first

\displaystyle F^{\times}\ni u\mapsto

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{|{F^{\times}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{F^{\times}}}\varepsilon(\psi_{a},\chi^{a})\overline{\chi}(u)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|{F^{\times}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|{F}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{x\in F^{\times}}\psi(ax)\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{F^{\times}}}\chi(x^{a})\overline{\chi}(u)=-\frac{|{F^{\times}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|{F}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{x\colon x^{a}=u}\psi(ax).

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.1
we have

\displaystyle F^{\times}\ni u\mapsto

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|{F^{\times}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{F^{\times}}}\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\widehat{F^{\times}}\\
\rho^{a}=1\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(\psi,\chi\rho)\overline{\chi}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)}{|{F^{\times}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|{F}|^{\frac{a}{2}}}\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{a}}\psi(x_{1}+\dots+x_{a})\rho_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\rho_{a}(x_{a})\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{F^{\times}}}\chi(x_{1}\cdots x_{a})\overline{\chi}(u)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{|{F^{\times}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)}{|{F}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Kl(u;\rho[a];\psi,F).

We deduce for
a>0
that

(3.6)

-\sum_{x:x^{a}=u}\psi(ax)=\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\Kl(u;\rho[a];\psi,F).

Similarly, we compute for
a<0

-\sum_{x:x^{a}=u}\psi(ax)=\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u,\varnothing,\rho[a];\psi,F)=\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\Kl(u^{-1},\rho[a];\overline{\psi},F).

Recall that,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x^{a}=u_{1}}\psi(ax)\ast\sum_{x^{b}=u_{2}}\psi(bx)\ast\psi\ast\dots\ast\psi\right)(u)=\K_{k}^{a,b}(a^{a}b^{b}u;\psi,F)

and similarly

\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\Kl(\bullet^{\operatorname{sgn}(a)};\rho[a];\psi^{\operatorname{sgn}(a)},F)*\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\Kl(\bullet^{\operatorname{sgn}(b)};\rho[b];\psi^{\operatorname{sgn}(b)},F)*\psi*\dots*\psi\right)(u)=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[a,b]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\varnothing;\psi,F)&a,b>0,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[a]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\rho[b];\psi,F)&a>0>b,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[b]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[a];\psi,F)&b>0>a,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi)\varepsilon_{b}(\psi)\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[a]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[b];\psi,F)&0>a,b.\end{cases}

∎

For any positive integer
n
let
F_{n}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite extension of degree
n
. We denote by
\psi_{n}
the standard additive character of
F_{n}
, that is
\psi_{n}(u)=e_{q}(\operatorname{tr}_{F_{n}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(u))
and denote
\K_{k}^{a,b}(u;\psi_{n},F_{n})=\K_{k}^{a,b}(u;F_{n})
. Similarly we denote
\operatorname{Hyp}(u,\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta};\psi_{n},F_{n})=\operatorname{Hyp}(u,\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta};F_{n})
, here is implicitly understood that
\bm{\chi}
and
\bm{\theta}
are family of characters in
F_{n}^{\times}
. We denote the attached complexes of sheaves lisse over
\mathbb{G}_{m/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
by
\mathcal{K}_{k}^{a,b}
and
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta},\psi)
, respectively.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
u\neq 0.
For every finite extension
F_{n}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
in which
X^{a}-1
,
X^{b}-1
split we have the equality of trace functions

K_{k}^{a,b}(a^{a}b^{b}u;F_{n})=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{a}(\psi_{n})\varepsilon_{b}(\psi_{n})\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[a,b]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\varnothing;F_{n})&a,b>0,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi_{n})\varepsilon_{b}(\psi_{n})\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[a]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[b];F_{n})&a>0>b,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi_{n})\varepsilon_{b}(\psi_{n})\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[b]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[a];F_{n})&b>0>a,\\
\varepsilon_{a}(\psi_{n})\varepsilon_{b}(\psi_{n})\operatorname{Hyp}(u;\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[a]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[b];F_{n})&0>a,b,\end{cases}

which implies that
\mathcal{K}_{k}^{a,b}
is geometrically isomorphic (in particular, they have same geometric monodromy group and local monodromies) to

[\times(a^{a}b^{b})^{-1}]^{*}\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}(\bm{\rho}[a,b]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\varnothing)&a,b>0,\\
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\rho}[a]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[b])&a>0>b,\\
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\rho}[b]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[a])&b>0>a,\\
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\rho}[1]^{k},\bm{\rho}[a]\sqcup\bm{\rho}[b])&0>a,b.\end{cases}

Now that we have identified the sheaves
\mathcal{K}_{k}^{a,b}
with the (pullback of) hypergeometric sheaves, we introduce a practical tool to simplify the hypergeometric sheaves
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta},\psi)
, valid over any finite field
F
, which is used in Section
6
.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
Kat90
]
, Thm. 8.4.10)
.

Suppose
\bm{\chi}
and
\bm{\theta}
are distinct, then the semisimplification of
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta},\psi)
is isomorphic to

\mathcal{H}(\bm{\chi}\smallsetminus\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}\smallsetminus\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta},\psi)\oplus\bigoplus_{\rho\in\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta}}G_{\rho}^{\otimes\operatorname{deg}}.\mathcal{L}_{\rho},

where
G_{\rho}^{\otimes\operatorname{deg}}
denote the geometrically constant sheaf associated to some algebraic number
G_{\rho}
satisfying
|{G_{\rho}}|\leqslant|{k}|^{-1/2}
.

The upshot of the Lemma is that the pure component of weight
0
of
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta};\psi)
is isomorphic to the pure component of weight
0
of
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\chi}\smallsetminus\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}\smallsetminus\bm{\chi}\sqcap\bm{\theta};\psi)
so that we can reduce our attention to those
\mathcal{H}(\bm{\chi},\bm{\theta})
where
\bm{\chi}
and
\bm{\theta}
are disjoint.

We delete the intersection of the two character sets in the hypergeometric sheaf from Corollary
3.3
and associate the resulting hypergeometric sheaf to
\K_{k}^{a,b}
.

4.
Dyadic partition and Pólya-Vinogradov method

By Proposition
2.2
we want to study the sums
M_{a,b,\Kl_{k}}
and

. In this section, we reduce the range of summation to dyadic intervals in the Pólya-Vinogradov range

and

for

Let

or

.

Let

.

Lemma 4.2

For a function

\widehat{T}(u)=\frac{1}{q^{1/2}}\sum_{x\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}T(x)e_{q}(xu),\qquad u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Let

.

and

Proof.

If

, then

Suppose now

.
Let
q^{o(1)}\leqslant L=q^{\alpha}<q^{1/2}
be a parameter to be chosen precisely later. For
\chi\in\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}
we define the mollifier

M_{L}(\chi)=\sum_{l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)\chi(l)}{l^{1/2}}P\left(\frac{\log(L/l)}{\log L}\right),

where
P\in\mathbb{R}[X]
is some polynomial (of absolutely bounded degree), so that
P(0)=0
and
P(1)=1
.
Applying the triangle inequality yields

(8.1)

|{M_{L}(\chi)}|\ll L^{1/2}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|{P(x)}|.

We furthermore need the following bound.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
b\geqslant 1
be an integer and
L>2
. Then we have the following.

\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1},l_{2}\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l_{1})\mu(l_{2})}{l_{1}l_{2}^{\frac{b+1}{2}}}P_{L}\left(\frac{\log(L/l_{1})}{\log L}\right)P_{L}\left(\frac{\log(L/l_{2})}{\log L}\right)\ll\frac{1}{\log L}

Proof.

After opening the polynomial
P_{L}
we see that it is sufficient to obtain the following bounds for
j\geqslant 0
and
r>1
:

\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)(\log l)^{j}}{l^{\frac{r+1}{2}}}\ll_{j}1,\qquad\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)\log(l)^{j+1}}{l}\ll_{j}1,\qquad\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)}{l}\ll\frac{1}{\log L}.

By a version of the Prime number theorem (see
[
MV06
]
, (6.18)), there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)}{l}\ll e^{-C\sqrt{\log L}}\ll\frac{1}{\log L}.

Also, since
r>1
, one has

\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)(\log l)^{j}}{l^{\frac{r+1}{2}}}=\left(\frac{1}{\zeta}\right)^{(j)}\left(\frac{r+1}{2}\right)-\sum_{l>L}\frac{\mu(l)(\log l)^{j}}{l^{\frac{r+1}{2}}}

and the tail is bounded by

\sum_{l>L}\frac{\mu(l)(\log l)^{j}}{l^{\frac{r+1}{2}}}\ll\int_{L}^{\infty}\frac{(\log t)^{j}}{t^{\frac{r+1}{2}}}\ \mathrm{d}t\ll_{j}(\log L)^{j}L^{-\frac{r-1}{2}}.

Finally, we have by summation by parts,

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)\log(l)^{j+1}}{l}=

\displaystyle-j\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant t}\frac{\mu(l)}{l}\right)\frac{(\log t)^{j}}{t}\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+(\log L)^{j+1}\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)}{l}+j\int_{L}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant t}\frac{\mu(l)}{l}\right)\frac{(\log t)^{j}}{t}\ \mathrm{d}t.

Again, the tail is bounded by

(\log L)^{j+1}\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l)}{l}+j\int_{L}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant t}\frac{\mu(l)}{l}\right)\frac{(\log t)^{j}}{t}\ \mathrm{d}t\ll_{j}(\log L)^{j+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-C\sqrt{\log L}}.

∎

We define the mollified second moment

(8.2)

\mathcal{Q}_{2}(a,b,k,M_{L})=\frac{1}{q-1}\mathop{{\sum}^{\ast}}\limits_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}L\left(\chi^{a},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)L\left(\chi^{b},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)M_{L}(\chi^{a})M_{L}(\chi^{b})\varepsilon(\chi)^{k}

and the mollified fourth moment

\mathcal{Q}_{4}(a,b,M_{L})=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}\left|{L\left(\chi^{a},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)L\left(\chi^{b},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)M_{L}(\chi^{a})M_{L}(\chi^{b})}\right|^{2}.

From Iwaniec and Sarnak,
[
IS99
]
, one deduces that for
P(X)=X
one has

(8.3)

\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}|{M_{L}(\chi)L(\chi,\tfrac{1}{2})}|^{2}\ll_{\alpha}1.

The same bound holds for
P(X)=X^{2}
(for example using (
8.4
) and Cauchy-Schwarz).

Proposition 8.5

(Evaluation of second mollified moment,
k=0
)
.

For any positive integers
a,b
there exist
c_{2}
and
\eta>0
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}(a,b,0,M_{L})

\displaystyle=P(1)^{2}+O(Lq^{-\eta}).

Proof.

After opening the mollifiers we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}(a,b,0,M_{L})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l_{1})\mu(l_{2})}{(l_{1}l_{2})^{1/2}}P\left(\frac{\log L/l_{1}}{\log L}\right)P\left(\frac{\log L/l_{2}}{\log L}\right)\mathop{{\sum}^{\ast}}\limits_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}\chi(l_{1}^{a})\chi(l_{2}^{b})L(\chi^{a},\tfrac{1}{2})L(\chi^{b},\tfrac{1}{2}).

By Proposition
7.2
we have

\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}\chi(l_{1}^{a})\chi(l_{2}^{b})L(\chi^{a},\tfrac{1}{2})L(\chi^{b},\tfrac{1}{2})=\begin{cases}1+O(q^{-\eta})&\text{if }l_{1}=l_{2}=1\\
O(q^{-\eta})&\text{else.}\end{cases}

Removing the contribution from the trivial character and using (
8.1
) we conclude the proposition.
∎

Proposition 8.6

(Evaluation of second mollified moment,
k\neq 0
)
.

For positive integers
a\leqslant b
not both equal to 1 and any integer
k\neq 0
, there exist constants
c_{2,k}=c_{2,k}(a,b,P)>0
and
\eta>0
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}(a,b,k,M_{L})

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2,k}Lq^{-\eta}

\displaystyle\text{if }(a,b,k)\neq(1,b,-1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}(a,b,k,M_{L})

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2,-1}\frac{1}{\log L}

\displaystyle\text{if }(a,b,k)=(1,b,-1).

Proof.

We again open the mollifiers and obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}(a,b,k,M_{L})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l_{1})\mu(l_{2})}{(l_{1}l_{2})^{1/2}}P\left(\frac{\log L/l_{1}}{\log L}\right)P\left(\frac{\log L/l_{2}}{\log L}\right)M_{a,b,k}(l_{1}^{a}l_{2}^{b};q).

For
a,b\neq 1
,
since
k\neq 0
, we have by Theorem
1.2
that
M_{a,b,k}(l_{1}^{a}l_{2}^{b};q)=O(q^{-\eta})
for some
\eta>0
independent of
k
, but possibly depending on
a,b
.
In the second case we have by Theorem
1.2
, with the error term as in the proof of Proposition
7.2
, that
M_{1,b,-1}(l_{1}l_{2}^{b};q)
is estimated as

\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l_{1}l_{2}^{b}}}\zeta\left(\frac{b+1}{2}\right)+O\left(\left(\frac{X}{L^{b+1}}\right)^{-1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Plugging this into our original question (with
P(X)=X^{2}
) we are led to investigate

\displaystyle\zeta\left(\frac{b+1}{2}\right)\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}\leqslant L}

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(l_{1})\mu(l_{2})}{l_{1}l_{2}^{\frac{b+1}{2}}}P\left(\frac{\log L/l_{1}}{\log L}\right)P\left(\frac{\log L/l_{2}}{\log L}\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\frac{L^{\frac{b+1}{2}}}{X^{1/2}}\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}\leqslant L}\frac{\mu(l_{1})\mu(l_{2})}{l_{1}l_{2}^{\frac{b+1}{2}}}P\left(\frac{\log L/l_{1}}{\log L}\right)P\left(\frac{\log L/l_{2}}{\log L}\right)\right).

Using (
8.1
) and
P(x)=x^{2}
the error term is bounded by

\ll\frac{L^{\frac{b+3}{2}}}{X^{1/2-\varepsilon}}.
For

X

\eta>0
.
Finally, by Lemma

∎

Proposition 8.7

(Bounds for the fourth mollified moment)
.

For any non-zero integers
a,b
and
P(X)=X^{2}
there exists
c_{4}>0
(independent of
q
) so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{4}(a,b,M_{L})\leqslant c_{4}.

Proof.

By
[
Zac19
]
, Theorem 1.2 we know that for
P(X)=X^{2}
and any
0<\lambda<\frac{11}{8064}
we have

(8.4)

\frac{1}{q-1}\mathop{{\sum}^{\ast}}\limits_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}|L\left(\chi,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)M_{L}(\chi)|^{4}=\sum_{i=0}^{4}d_{i}\lambda^{-i}+O_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{\log q}\right)

for some calculable coefficients

.
Using Cauchy-Schwarz we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{4}(a,b,M_{L})

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\mathop{{\sum}^{\ast}}\limits_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}|L\left(\chi^{a},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)M_{L}(\chi^{a})|^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\mathop{{\sum}^{\ast}}\limits_{\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,{q})}|L\left(\chi^{b},\tfrac{1}{2}\right)M_{L}(\chi^{b})|^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(ab)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{4}d_{i}\lambda^{-i}+O_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{\log q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{4}.\qed

8.1
.

a,b

with large

Abstract
We study the generalized cusp anomalous dimension, or quark-antiquark potential on the three-sphere, in the presence of a large R R -charge L L and at strong coupling. Considering the insertion of a local scalar operator of charge L L on a cusped Wilson loop, we investigate the general solution of the dual string configuration for any value of cusp angles, paying particular attention to the different ranges of the physical parameters and obtaining a unified description that simultaneously captures the near BPS, the antiparallel lines and the large- L L regimes. We observe, in the limit of small separation between the lines, a non-analytic change in the behavior of the cusp anomaly at a critical value of L L : one crosses from a region dominated by the familiar Coulomb-like singularity to a “deconfined” situation describing the leading Lüscher corrections at large L L of the generalized cusp anomalous dimension. We derive the equations for small fluctuations around the classical string solution, generalizing previous analysis of the uncharged case, and we study the behavior of the spectrum near the transition region. Finally, we speculate on the field theory origin of the transition and on its implications for the fusion of Wilson lines.

Quantum fluctuations and normal modes.

The next step of our investigation concerns the spectrum of the quadratic quantum fluctuations around the classical string solution, dual to the cusped Wilson loop with
R
-charge insertions. We focus on its semiclassical (WKB) approximation and its behavior in several physically interesting limits.

The eight physical transverse bosonic fluctuations
\xi^{i}
(
i=1,\dots,8

\mathcal{K}_{ij}\,\xi^{j}=0,\qquad i,j=1,\dots,8,

(1.16)

where the operator matrix
\mathcal{K}_{ij}
is obtained following the general procedure outlined in
[
36

37
]

The system naturally decomposes into two sectors:

•

Uncoupled modes
(
i=1,\dots,4

•

Coupled modes
(
i=5,\dots,8
): The exact quantization of these modes is considerably more challenging due to the presence of both a non-diagonal mass matrix and a non-trivial
SO(4)
connection on the normal bundle. A possible approach toward their systematic WKB treatment is discussed in Appendix
D.2

Given these difficulties, we have employed the WKB approximation to study all bosonic frequencies, while obtaining more precise results for the uncoupled sector. For large quantum numbers

, we find a universal asymptotic expression for the physical frequencies. At leading order, it reads

\omega_{\text{phys},\ell}=\frac{\pi\ell}{2\sqrt{n-m}\,K(m)}\,,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.17)

This formula serves as the starting point for our analysis of various regimes.

1.3
Discussion and Outlook

It is instructive to compare our results with earlier related investigations. In the
L=0
case studied in
[
33
]
, the frequencies (
1.19
) were interpreted as excitations of quark–antiquark bound states near the dissociation threshold
E=0
at strong coupling (
\sqrt{\lambda}\gg 1
). The authors argued that the flux-tube dynamics at strong coupling differs dramatically from its weak-coupling counterpart: the string in AdS
5
possesses a rich spectrum of discrete energy levels between the ground state (determined by the Coulomb potential) and
E=0
, reflecting the many internal degrees of freedom of a fluctuating flux tube. Finding no evidence for such bound states at weak coupling, they concluded that a critical coupling
{\lambda_{c}}
must exist, above which these excitations emerge. Remarkably, our transition line
\Theta_{\text{eff}}=\pi
corresponds precisely to configurations with zero classical string energy, consistently with their picture.

Another setup we consider for comparison is the following. In the
D3

The physical transverse bosonic fluctuations are governed by the Lagrangian computed in section

From there we can compute the equations of motion, which can be schematically written as

\mathcal{K}_{ij}\xi^{j}=0\,,\qquad i,j=1,\dots,8\,,

(4.9)

according to (

In this section we analyze the frequencies of these eight bosonic mode

in a WKB approximation.
The first four bosonic modes
\xi^{i}
(
i=1,\dots,4
) are uncoupled, see equations (

) and (

), and their computation can be performed exactly, as shown in Appendix

. However, computing exactly the frequencies for the remaining coupled modes (

In app.
D.1
we show that the semiclassical expansion of the exact frequencies for the uncoupled scalar fields agree with the WKB analysis described in this section.

We start by illustrating the general procedure for the transverse uncoupled AdS modes (
4.6a
). By Fourier transforming the modes
\partial_{\tau}\to e^{i\omega\tau}
, one obtains a one-dimensional Schroedinger equation for the radial component, which we rename
\hat{\xi}(\sigma)
:

-\partial_{\sigma}^{2}\hat{\xi}^{i}(\sigma)-\omega^{2}\hat{\xi}^{i}(\sigma)+(1+2\mathcal{X}^{2}(\sigma))\hat{\xi}^{i}(\sigma)=0\,,\qquad i=1,2\,.

(4.10)

The potential
V(\sigma)=1+2\mathcal{X}^{2}(\sigma)
behaves as a well potential, where at the center of the worldsheet (
\sigma\to 0
) approaches a constant, while it blows up at the boundaries
\sigma\to\pm s/2
.
We impose Dirichlet conditions at the boundaries, that is
\hat{\xi}(\pm s/2)=0
.
We want to compute the frequencies
\omega
in a semi-classical approximation, which implies a large
\omega
expansion. Then, we expand the modes
\hat{\xi}^{i}(\sigma)
in a WKB approximation where the parameter of the expansion is indeed
\omega
:

\hat{\xi}(\sigma)\sim e^{i\int_{-s/2}^{\sigma}P(\sigma^{\prime})\dif\sigma^{\prime}}\,,\qquad\text{with}\quad P(\sigma)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\omega^{1-\ell}{P_{\ell}(\sigma)}\,,

(4.11)

where
M
determines the order of the approximation and the overall amplitude does not matter for our discussion. The second-order equation (
4.10
) becomes a first-order Riccati equation for the function
P
, which can be solved order by order in
\omega
.
The odd functions
P_{2\ell+1}
(with
\ell\geq 0
) contribute only to the overall amplitude of the wave function
\hat{\xi}
, while the first even functions are given by

P_{0,\pm}(\sigma)=\pm 1\,,\qquad P_{2,\pm}(\sigma)=\mp(\tfrac{1}{2}-\kappa^{2}\cosh^{2}\rho(\sigma))=\mp(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathcal{X}^{2}(\sigma))\,,\qquad

(4.12)

and higher terms can be easily determined recursively. The Dirichlet boundary conditions for
\hat{\xi}
result in a sine wave function, which has to vanish at
\sigma=s/2
:

\hat{\xi}^{i}(s/2)\sim\sin\Big(\omega\int_{-s/2}^{s/2}\dif\sigma^{\prime}\Big(P_{0,+}(\sigma^{\prime})+{1\over\omega^{2}}P_{2,+}(\sigma^{\prime})+\dots\Big)\Big)=0\,,\qquad i=1,2\,.

This is equivalent to impose the following quantization condition

\omega_{\ell}\int_{-s/2}^{s/2}\dif\sigma^{\prime}\Big(P_{0,+}(\sigma^{\prime})+{1\over\omega_{\ell}^{2}}P_{2,+}(\sigma^{\prime})+\dots\Big)=\ell\pi,\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{N}\,.

(4.13)

The integrals of the functions
P_{0,+},P_{2,+}
have been already computed in section
2.1
, and we have

\int_{-s/2}^{s/2}\dif\sigma^{\prime}P_{0,+}(\sigma^{\prime})=s\,,\qquad\int_{-s/2}^{s/2}\dif\sigma^{\prime}P_{2,+}(\sigma^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\left(s-{\kappa\over g}E\right)\,,

(4.14)

if we use equation (
2.20
). The integral of
P_{2,+}
is divergent, and we choose to regularizing it as in (
2.20
), such that the quantization condition reads now

\omega_{\ell}\left(s+\frac{1}{2\omega_{\ell}^{2}}\left(s-4\kappa\mathcal{E}\right)+\dots\right)=\ell\pi\,,\qquad\ell=1,2\,,\dots\,,\qquad\text{i.e.}~~\ell\in\mathbb{N}\,,

(4.15)

in terms of the rescaled regularized energy
\mathcal{E}
(
2.21

The higher order corrections

cannot be written in such a neat form in terms of

However, it should be kept in mind that our semiclassical analysis is only valid at very large
\ell

The analysis for the uncoupled transverse directions in the five-sphere is completely analogous. Given the operator (

. Thus, in this regime, our result for the potential energy leads to a prediction for the asymptotic spectrum of defect three-point functions involving heavy bulk operators in the presence of a straight Wilson line defect with
Z^{L}
insertions. If
\mathcal{L}>\mathcal{L}_{c}^{\theta}
,
E_{\text{Cas}}=0
and the fusion limit is governed by subleading contributions in the effective action (

D.2
WKB analysis of the normal mode frequencies

In this section we elucidate the WKB analysis for the coupled transverse modes
\xi^{i}
, with
i=5,\dots,8
.
For these modes, the equation of motions (
4.9
) represent a system of coupled
4\times 4
differential equations.
After Fourier transforming, we write schematically the differential equation as

\left(-\partial_{\sigma}^{2}-\omega^{2}-2i\omega\mathbb{A}_{\tau}+2\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}+\partial_{\sigma}\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}+\mathbb{A}_{\tau}^{2}-\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}^{2}-\sqrt{h}\mathcal{M}\right)\hat{\Xi}(\sigma)=0\,,

(D.24)

where now
\hat{\Xi}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{4}
wave function. We have indicated
\mathbb{A}_{\tau},\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}
the
4\times 4
antisymmetric matrices whose components are the connections (
4.7a
), (
4.7b
) respectively, computed in section
4.1

We can always rescale the radial component
\hat{\Xi}
to remove the connection
\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}
, however we find more convenient to not perform such a transformation.
We assume that there exists a
4\times 4
matrix
\mathrm{P}
, which is defined by the relation

\partial_{\sigma}\hat{\Xi}(\sigma)=i\,\mathrm{P}(\sigma)\,\hat{\Xi}(\sigma)\,.

(D.25)

In general,
\mathrm{P}
is a (smooth) function of the worldsheet coordinate
\sigma
. Using the above relation, the equation (
D.24
) becomes a Riccati matrix equation for
\mathrm{P}
:

-i\partial_{\sigma}\mathrm{P}+\mathrm{P}^{2}-\omega^{2}-2i\omega\mathbb{A}_{\tau}+2i\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}\mathrm{P}+\partial_{\sigma}\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}+\mathbb{A}_{\tau}^{2}-\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}^{2}-\sqrt{h}\,\mathcal{M}=0\,.

(D.26)

In complete analogy to the semiclassical expansion (
4.11
), we can assume the following large
\omega
approximation for the matrix
\mathrm{P}

\mathrm{P}=\omega\left(\mathrm{P}_{0}+\frac{1}{\omega}\mathrm{P}_{1}+\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\mathrm{P}_{2}+\dots\right)\,,

(D.27)

where now all the
\mathrm{P}_{p}
, with
p=0,1\dots
are
4\times 4
matrices.
With the ansatz (
D.27
), the Riccati equation (
D.26
) can be solved order by order in large
\omega
. At the leading order, this is nothing but

\mathrm{P}_{0}^{2}-\mathrm{I}=0\,,\qquad\text{that is}\qquad\mathrm{P}_{0,\pm}=\pm\mathrm{I}\,,

(D.28)

where
\mathrm{I}
is the
4\times 4
identity matrix.
At the next leading order, we obtain an algebraic equation for
\mathrm{P}_{1}

\left\{\mathrm{P}_{0},\mathrm{P}_{1}\right\}-2i\mathbb{A}_{\tau}+2i\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}\mathrm{P}_{0}=0\,,\qquad\text{which gives}\qquad\mathrm{P}_{1,\pm}=-i\left(\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}\mp\mathbb{A}_{\tau}\right)\,.

(D.29)

At the next-to-next leading order, the algebraic equation for
\mathrm{P}_{2}
reads

-i\partial_{\sigma}\mathrm{P}_{1}+\left\{\mathrm{P}_{0},\mathrm{P}_{2}\right\}+\mathrm{P}_{1}^{2}+2i\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}\mathrm{P}_{1}+\partial_{\sigma}\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}+\mathbb{A}_{\tau}^{2}-\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}^{2}-\sqrt{h}\,\mathcal{M}=0\,,

and a formal solution for

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{2,\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm{1\over 2}\left(i\partial_{\sigma}\mathrm{P}_{1,\pm}-\mathrm{P}_{1,\pm}^{2}-2i\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}\mathrm{P}_{1,\pm}-\partial_{\sigma}\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}-\mathbb{A}_{\tau}^{2}+\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}^{2}+\sqrt{h}\,\mathcal{M}\right)

\Pi(n|m)=\frac{\Pi\left(\frac{n}{n-1}|\frac{m}{m-1}\right)}{\sqrt{1-m}(1-n)}\text{/;}\,\,|\arg(1-m)|<\pi\land|\arg(1-n)|<\pi\,,

(E.22)

as well as the identity

\begin{split}\Pi(n|m)=&K(m)+\frac{i\pi}{2\sqrt{\frac{m}{n}-m+n-1}}-\Pi\left(\!\left.\frac{m}{n}\right|m\!\right)\\
&\text{/;}(0<n<1\land 0<m<1)\lor(0<n<1\land 0<\arg(m)\leq\pi).\end{split}

(E.23)

A useful integral representation of
\Pi(m,n)

In this appendix we derive an integral representation for
\Pi(m,n)
that is
well suited to analyze the joint limit

m\to 1,\qquad n\to 1,

(E.24)

with the ratio

\frac{1-m}{1-n}=\alpha

(E.25)

kept finite. Since we always assume

0\leqslant m\leqslant n\leqslant 1,

we have
\alpha\geqslant 1
.
We start from the canonical integral representation

\Pi(m,n)=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{d\varphi}{\bigl(1-n\sin^{2}\varphi\bigr)\sqrt{1-m\sin^{2}\varphi}}\,.

(E.26)

The potential singularity in the limit
m,n\to 1
comes from the region near
the upper endpoint
\varphi\simeq\pi/2
, where
\sin^{2}\varphi\simeq 1
.
We now perform the change of variables

\varphi=\mathrm{arccot}(t),

which maps the interval
(0,\pi/2)
to the positive half-line
(0,\infty)
and
moves the singular region to
t=0
. In these variables the integral becomes

\Pi(m,n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;\frac{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-m+t^{2}}\,\bigl(1-n+t^{2}\bigr)}\,.

(E.27)

Next we make explicit the dependence on
n
by rescaling

t\to\sqrt{1-n}\,t
and using the parametrization
m=1-\alpha(1-n)
(see eq. (
E.25
)).
This gives

\Pi(m,n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\;\frac{\sqrt{1+(1-n)t^{2}}}{(1-n)(1+t^{2})\sqrt{t^{2}+\alpha}}.

(E.28)

The overall factor
1/(1-n)
makes the leading divergence manifest as

n\to 1
. However, if one naively expands the square root in the numerator
in powers of
(1-n)
, all the resulting integrals over
t
diverge,
except for the leading-order term. To obtain a
representation suitable for a systematic expansion in
(1-n)
, we rewrite the
square root using the integral identity

\sqrt{1+(1-n)t^{2}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\;\frac{1+(1-n)t^{2}}{u^{2}+1+(1-n)t^{2}},

(E.29)

which follows from

\int_{0}^{\infty}\!du/(u^{2}+a^{2})=\pi/(2a)

with
a^{2}=1+(1-n)t^{2}
.
Inserting (
E.29
) into (
E.28
) we obtain the
double integral

\Pi(m,n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{\infty}du\;\frac{2\bigl[1+(1-n)t^{2}\bigr]}{\pi(1-n)\,(1+t^{2})\sqrt{t^{2}+\alpha}\;\bigl[u^{2}+1+(1-n)t^{2}\bigr]}\,.

(E.30)

We now exchange the order of integration and first integrate over
t
. For
this purpose it is convenient to use the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{2\bigl(1+(1-n)t^{2}\bigr)}{\pi(n-1)\bigl(t^{2}+1\bigr)\bigl(u^{2}+1+(1-n)t^{2}\bigr)}=\frac{2}{\pi(u^{2}+n)}\left[\underbrace{\frac{n}{(1-n)\bigl(t^{2}+1\bigr)}}_{\mathds{I}_{1}}+\underbrace{\frac{u^{2}}{u^{2}+1+(1-n)t^{2}}}_{\mathds{I}_{2}}\right].

(E.31)

We first consider the contribution from
\mathds{I}_{1}
. In this case both
integrations over
t
and
u
can be carried out explicitly, and we obtain

\frac{\sqrt{n}\,\sec^{-1}\!\bigl(\sqrt{\alpha}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\alpha-1}\,(1-n)}.

(E.32)

For the term associated with
\mathds{I}_{2}
, integrating over
t
and
simplifying the result yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}du\;\frac{u^{2}\bigl[2\log\!\bigl(\sqrt{1+u^{2}}+\sqrt{1+u^{2}-(1-n)\alpha}\bigr)-\log(1-n)-\log\alpha\bigr]}{\pi\,\sqrt{1+u^{2}}\,\bigl(n+u^{2}\bigr)\,\sqrt{1+u^{2}-(1-n)\alpha}}\,.

(E.33)

Collecting the two contributions we arrive at the final representation

\Pi(m,n)=\frac{\sqrt{n}\,\sec^{-1}\!\bigl(\sqrt{\alpha}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\alpha\!-\!1}\,(1\!-\!n)}\!+\!\int_{0}^{\infty}du\;\frac{u^{2}\bigl[2\log\!\bigl(\sqrt{1\!+\!u^{2}}\!+\!\sqrt{1\!+\!u^{2}\!-\!(1\!-\!n)\alpha}\bigr)\!-\!\log(1\!-\!n)\!-\!\log\alpha\bigr]}{\pi\,\sqrt{1\!+\!u^{2}}\,\bigl(n\!+\!u^{2}\bigr)\,\sqrt{1\!+\!u^{2}\!-\!(1\!-\!n)\alpha}}\,.

(E.34)

In terms of the original parameter
m
and
n
, the above formula reads

\Pi(m,n)=\frac{\sqrt{n}\,\sec^{-1}\!\bigl(\sqrt{\frac{1-m}{1-n}}\bigr)}{\sqrt{(1-n)(n-m)}}\!+\!\int_{0}^{\infty}du\;\frac{u^{2}\bigl[2\log\!\bigl(\sqrt{1\!+\!u^{2}}\!+\!\sqrt{m\!+\!u^{2}}\bigr)\!-\!\log(1\!-\!m)\bigr]}{\pi\,\sqrt{1\!+\!u^{2}}\,\bigl(n\!+\!u^{2}\bigr)\,\sqrt{m\!+\!u^{2}}}\,.

(E.35)

The advantage of (
E.35
) is that the divergent
contribution as
m\to 1
is completely factorized. The integrand
naturally splits into a term proportional to
-\log(1-m)
, which
gives the explicit logarithmic divergence, and a remainder that is
finite at
m,n=1
. Moreover, expanding the integrand in powers of

(1-n)
and
(1-m)
generates only convergent integrals over
u
,
so that (
E.34
) provides a convenient starting
point for a controlled asymptotic expansion around
m,n\to 1

with fixed
\alpha=(1-m)/(1-n)
. Performing this double expansion
and integrating each term, we arrive at the following expansion:

On Time-Evolution in Quantum Gravity


Abstract
We derive an explicit BRST-exact operator identity for the bulk Hamiltonian in quantum gravity, working within a BRST-invariant quantization of General Relativity, treated as a low-energy effective field theory. We show that, up to a boundary term, the Hamiltonian can be written elegantly as the anticommutator of the BRST charge and the temporal ghost field. This form makes manifest that the Hamiltonian flow acts as a time-reparameterization on the correlation functions of the physical degrees of freedom. We demonstrate that the BRST-exactness of the bulk Hamiltonian does not trivialize the time evolution of gravitational backgrounds or bulk correlators, nor does it trivialize scattering amplitudes.

1
Introduction

The concept of time-evolution has a special place within the quantum field theoretic description of our universe, which is founded on the Hamiltonian description of fundamental degrees of freedom. In quantum gravity, the challenges in the definition of time as the Hamiltonian flow has been widely discussed in the literature. This puzzling observation is connected to the vanishing of the classical Hamiltonian (modulo boundary contributions) upon satisfaction of constraint equations, which has dire consequences for the quantum theory. The question is how to sensibly quantize General Relativity (GR) while retaining time as the variable of the Hamiltonian flow. One of the criteria being the ability to accommodate the classical dynamics. Gravity being a gauge theory, one must take extra care in the identification of the physical Hilbert space, preserved by the dynamics.

The important observations have been made in seminal papers
[
1
,
2
]
, which sourced the discussion on this matter. On one hand, a certain attempt of canonical quantization of GR supplemented with the promotion of classical constraints into the physicality condition on the Hilbert space
[
1
]
seemed to yield an over-constrained Hamiltonian flow, with the boundary term of the Hamiltonian being the sole contributor to the time-dependence of physical states. This was argued to be somewhat undesirable as it did not make it transparent how one could recover the expected time-evolution of observables, consistent with classical gravity. On the other hand, the path-integral formulation of GR as the means for obtaining the scattering
S
-matrix elements seemed to be well-defined
[
2
]
. There, the question was left open as to whether a certain framework could consistently unify both of this avenues.

These questions are important for the identification of quantum states that serve as a fundamental description of classical backgrounds in gravity. The existence of time as the aforementioned variable of the Hamiltonian flow is essential for backgrounds corresponding to the time-dependent classical configurations.

In contrast, the issue of time does not appear in the computation of cosmological correlators for quantum fluctuations around the classical cosmological backgrounds. Therefore, if we hope to derive these results as an approximation of the background field method applied to a microscopic theory, the latter must possess the similar notion of time.

In
[
3
]
, we have demonstrated that the BRST quantization of GR
[
4
]
as a low energy effective field theory is ready-made for unifying the path-integral description of gravitational scattering amplitudes in Minkowski space-time and allowing for the well-defined notion of time as the parameter of the Hamiltonian flow for correlation functions under a single framework, reproducing classical dynamics in appropriate limit. The key observation of
[
3
]
was that the quantum Hamiltonian and momentum constraints do not annihilate physical states within the quantization in question. As a result, physical states are not annihilated by the bulk Hamiltonian.

In this work we would like to further elucidate key aspects of this approach and confront the lore that there is no time in quantum gravity.

2
Framework

Our starting point is the BRST-invariant Lagrangian formulation of GR
[
4
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm EH}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm GF}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm FP}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm EH}=\sqrt{-g}M_{\rm pl}^{2}R\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm GF}=M_{\rm pl}b_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\right)\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm FP}=i\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}c^{\nu}-\partial_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\sigma\nu}\right)c^{\mu}\right)\,.

(4)

Here, in order to facilitate the quantization, the gauge has been fixed by supplementing the Einstein-Hilbert theory by
\mathcal{L}_{\rm GF}
, where
b_{\nu}
is the auxiliary field, the equation of motion for which imposes de Donder gauge on the metric

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\right)=0\,.

(5)

Depending on the problem, a different gauge-fixing condition may be more convenient, particularly if one aims to recover the standard quantum field theory in curved spacetime around a specific background and in a chosen gauge (e.g. cosmological backgrounds in a particular slicing), as a limiting case of the background field method derived from our fundamental quantization approach
[
3
]
; see also
[
5
]
for related remarks on loop corrections to cosmological correlators. Moreover, for the realization of the BRST invariance, which is instrumental for the unitarity of the theory in the physical Hilbert space, the theory comes with the entourage of Faddeev-Popov ghost term
\mathcal{L}_{\rm FP}
for fermionic ghost/anti-ghost fields. This contribution has been rewritten compared to
[
4
]
by means of integration by parts to simplify the identification of canonical variables.

The theory at hand enjoys the BRST symmetry, characterised by the Grassmannian parameter
\theta
. The transformation of the metric under this symmetry is identical to its properties under the coordinate reparameterizations with the parameter
(c^{\mu}\theta)
. The full set of fields at hand transform as follows:

\displaystyle\delta g_{\mu\nu}=-M_{\rm pl}^{-1}\big((\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu})c^{\rho}+g_{\mu\rho}\partial_{\nu}c^{\rho}+g_{\nu\rho}\partial_{\mu}c^{\rho}\big)\theta\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\delta c^{\mu}=M_{\rm pl}^{-1}c^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}c^{\mu}\theta\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\delta\bar{c}_{\mu}=ib_{\mu}\theta\,,

(8)

\displaystyle\delta b_{\mu}=0\,.

(9)

The corresponding Noether charge
Q
is nilpotent,

\displaystyle Q^{2}=0\,,

(10)

and, together with the conserved ghost number, plays central role in the identification of the physical Hilbert space over which the evolution/
S
-matrix is unitary.

3
Canonical Quantization

At this point the quantization is relatively straightforward, since all fields have canonical conjugate momenta. The special case being
b_{\mu}
, which virtually constitutes the conjugate momentum of the temporal metric components. The canonical variables can be selected by convenience following
[
3
]
:

•

The spatial components of the metric are the obvious choice for characterising the on-shell propagating degrees of freedom, which we will denote by
\gamma_{ij}\equiv g_{ij}
. The corresponding canonical conjugate momentum is given by the well known form

\displaystyle\Pi^{ij}=-M_{\rm pl}^{2}\sqrt{\gamma}\left(K^{ij}-\gamma^{ij}K\right)\,,

(11)

where
K_{ij}
stands for the extrinsic curvature as usual. One of the advantages of writing the ghost Lagrangian in the above form is to maintain this classical expression for
\Pi^{ij}
.

•

The temporal components of the metric are most convenient to parameterize by means of
A^{\mu}\equiv\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu 0}
. The corresponding conjugate momentum reduces to

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu}=M_{\rm pl}b_{\mu}-i(\partial_{\nu}\bar{c}_{\mu})c^{\nu}\,.

(12)

•

The conjugate momenta for
\bar{c}_{\mu}
and
c^{\nu}
are

\displaystyle\Pi_{\bar{c}}^{\mu}=i\left(A^{\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}c^{\mu}-\partial_{\sigma}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\sigma\mu}\right)c^{0}\right)\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu}^{c}=-iA^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}_{\nu}\,.

(14)

In these canonical variables the Einstein-Hilbert Hamiltonian takes the familiar form up to the boundary term

\displaystyle H_{\rm EH}=\int d^{3}x\left[-\frac{1}{A^{0}}\mathcal{H}_{0}+\frac{A^{i}}{A^{0}}\mathcal{P}_{i}\right]\,,

(15)

where
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{P}_{i}
are the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints respectively

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}\equiv\frac{1}{2M_{\rm pl}^{2}}\left(\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{j\ell}+\gamma_{i\ell}\gamma_{jk}-\gamma_{ij}\gamma_{k\ell}\right)\Pi^{ij}\Pi^{kl}-M_{\rm pl}^{2}\gamma R^{(3)}\,,

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{i}\equiv-2\gamma_{ik}\partial_{j}\Pi^{kj}-(2\partial_{k}\gamma_{ji}-\partial_{i}\gamma_{jk})\Pi^{jk}\,.

(17)

This is obviously supplemented with the Hamiltonian for the auxiliary sector, which is given by

\displaystyle H_{FP+GF}=\int d^{3}x\left(\frac{i}{A^{0}}\Pi^{c}_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu}_{\bar{c}}+\frac{A^{k}}{A^{0}}\left(\Pi_{\mu}^{c}\partial_{k}c^{\mu}-\partial_{k}\bar{c}_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu}_{\bar{c}}\right)+i\frac{1}{A^{0}}\gamma\gamma^{ij}\partial_{i}\bar{c}_{\mu}\partial_{j}c^{\mu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\Pi_{0}\partial_{k}A^{k}-\Pi_{i}\partial_{j}\left(\frac{1}{A^{0}}\left(-\gamma\gamma^{ij}+A^{i}A^{j}\right)\right)\right)\,.

(18)

In the formulation at hand, the constraint equations are promoted to the dynamical equations for auxiliary fields. In fact, we can use these equations to rewrite the Hamiltonian in a concise (yet a non-canonical) form

\displaystyle H=H_{\rm EH}+H_{FP+GF}=\int d^{3}x~A^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{0}\,.

(19)

This is connected to the fact that both the Hamiltonian and the momentum constraints appear in Hamilton’s equation for
\Pi_{0}
.

The quantization is straightforward and constitutes the promotion of the above-listed fields and conjugate momenta into operators, followed by the prescription of the canonical equal-time commutation/anti-commutation relations
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\gamma}_{ij}(x),\hat{\Pi}^{k\ell}(y)\right]=\frac{i}{2}\left(\delta_{i}^{k}\delta_{j}^{\ell}+\delta_{i}^{\ell}\delta_{j}^{k}\right)\delta^{(3)}(x-y)\,,

(20)

\displaystyle\left[\hat{A}^{\mu}(x),\hat{\Pi}_{\nu}(y)\right]=i\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\delta^{(3)}(x-y)\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{c}^{\mu}(x),\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{\nu}(y)\right\}=i\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\delta^{(3)}(x-y)\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{\bar{c}}_{\nu}(x),\hat{\Pi}_{\bar{c}}^{\mu}(y)\right\}=i\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\delta^{(3)}(x-y)\,.

(23)

Henceforth, hats over quantities will indicate operators, while
[\ldots]
and
\{\ldots\}
will be denoting the commutators and anti-commutators respectively.

As a result of the straightforward computation, the Hamiltonian (
19
) can be rewritten, up to a surface term, in terms of the BRST charge operator as

\displaystyle\hat{H}=M_{\rm pl}\int d^{3}x\left\{\hat{Q},i\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}\right\}\,.

(24)

This equality is the direct conequence of the BRST transformation property of
\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}
.
In other words, the bulk Hamiltonian, including the Einstein-Hilbert and ghost parts, is BRST exact. This is in perfect agreement with the general expectation that, for systems whose Hamiltonian is a linear combination of first-class constraints, it becomes BRST-exact up to BRST-invariant (non-exact) surface terms
[
6
]
.
This is in a complete analogy of the world-line quantization of a relativistic particle
[
7
]
. The fact that the Hamiltonian in general relativity takes a BRST-exact form was previously identified in
[
8
]
, albeit in a different parametrization and context, without drawing a connection to the concept of time as the Hamiltonian flow or lack thereof.

In this work we would like to focus on the effects of (
24
), which is the sole contributor to the evolution of the bulk correlation functions. In the presence of the boundary, one should keep in mind that there might be BRST-invariant surface contributions in addition, governing the evolution of the boundary correlators. In turn, the Hamiltonian (
24
) tells the full story in the absence of the boundary.

The BRST invariance of the theory implies
[\hat{Q},\hat{H}]=0
, which is straightforwardly satisfied by (
24
). Furthermore, one of the classical features of General Relativity is that the Hamiltonian flow is equivalent to the reparametrization of time. In our framework, this is a direct consequence of (
24
) and the fact that BRST symmetry acts on fields (with the exception of the auxiliary gauge-fixing sector) as the coordinate reparamterization with the parameter of the transformation given by the ghost fields; see e.g. (
6
). In fact, it is straightforward to show that for any operator
\hat{O}
that commutes with
\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{O}(x)=i[\hat{H},\hat{O}(x)]=iM_{\rm pl}\int d^{3}z\left[\left\{\hat{Q},i\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}(z)\right\},\hat{O}(x)\right]=-iM_{\rm pl}\int d^{3}z\left\{\left[\hat{Q},\hat{O}(x)\right],i\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}(z)\right\}\,,

(25)

where we have used the Jacobi identity in the last equality. Here, the commutator yields the BRST variation of
\hat{O}
, while the anti-commutator with
\hat{\Pi}_{0}
projects the BRST transformation to the coordinate reparameterization in the time-direction. For instance, the verification of this statement for the infinitesimal evolution of
\hat{\gamma}_{ij}
is straightforward. Namely, as a direct consequence of (
6
), we have

\displaystyle i\left[\hat{Q},\hat{\gamma}_{ij}\right]=M_{\rm pl}^{-1}\Big((\partial_{\rho}\hat{\gamma}_{ij})c^{\rho}+g_{i\rho}\partial_{j}c^{\rho}+g_{j\rho}\partial_{i}c^{\rho}\Big)\,.

(26)

As a result

\displaystyle\left\{\left[\hat{Q},\hat{\gamma}_{ij}(x)\right],i\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}(z)\right\}=iM_{\rm pl}^{-1}\Big((\partial_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{ij})\delta^{(3)}(x-z)+g_{i0}(x)\partial_{j}\delta^{(3)}(x-z)+g_{j0}(x)\partial_{i}\delta^{(3)}(x-z)\Big)\,,

where partial derivatives are with respect to
x
. Therefore, integration by
z
would pass right through and nullify the last two terms, leaving us with

\displaystyle-iM_{\rm pl}\int d^{3}z\left\{\left[\hat{Q},\hat{\gamma}_{ij}(x)\right],i\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}(z)\right\}=\partial_{t}\hat{\gamma}_{ij}\,.

(27)

This provides the explicit verification of (
25
) for the spatial metric.

4
Physical States

The full Hilbert space harbors unphysical states as well as physical ones, containing ghosts and unphysical polarizations of the gauge field. However, as it was thoroughly framed in
[
9
]
, the BRST quantization provides a mechanism for defining the physical subspace of the Hilbert space with unitary
S
-matrix. In particular, the physical Hilbert space
\mathcal{V}_{\rm phys}
is defined as a space of states with (somewhat obvious) vanishing ghost number and (more non-trivial) BRST charge

\displaystyle\hat{Q}|f\rangle=0\,,\qquad{\rm with}\qquad\langle f|f\rangle\geq 0\,.

(28)

In other words, there is a subspace
\mathcal{V}_{0}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\rm phys}
with vanishing norm

\displaystyle\langle f|f\rangle=0\,,\qquad\forall~|f\rangle\in\mathcal{V}_{0}\,.

(29)

And the BRST-invariant states that differ by an element of
\mathcal{V}_{0}
are physically equivalent, i.e. have identical
S
-matrix elements. Then, as it was shown in
[
9
]
, the unitary physical
S
-matrix can be defined in the quotient space

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\rm phys}\equiv\mathcal{V}_{\rm phys}/\mathcal{V}_{0}\,.

(30)

In the absence of the boundary, as it straightforwardly follows from (
24
), each element of
\mathscr{H}_{\rm phys}
is invariant under the Hamiltonian flow. This might naively be interpreted as the absence of time and of the Hamiltonian. However, it is important to appreciate that the invariance of the elements of
\mathscr{H}_{\rm phys}
under the time-evolution is perfectly consistent with the time dependence of physical states and of the correlation functions. In fact, it is absolutely essential for recovering the property of GR that the time evolution is equivalent to the reparameterization of time.

To further elucidate this point, we will first consider the case of compactified spacial dimensions, before moving on to discuss the boundary Hamiltonian and its effects. We do so simply because the evolution of the bulk correlation functions is driven by the bulk Hamiltonian in any case.

5
No-Boundary: Compact Space

In this section, we focus on the case where the spatial manifold is closed, for instance, a three-sphere. We acknowledge the possible non-perturbative instabilities of such spacetimes
[
10
]
, but we will set these concerns aside and consider compact spatial dimensions for illustrative purposes. The primary aim of this discussion is to isolate the time dependence of correlation functions arising from the bulk Hamiltonian. We emphasize that our ultimate interest lies in spacetimes with Minkowski asymptotics.

In the absence of the boundary, the Hamiltonian of quantum gravity is fully given by (
24
). Which implies that the physical states are annihilated by the Hamiltonian up to an image (zero-norm state), i.e.

\displaystyle\hat{H}|f\rangle=\hat{Q}|\psi\rangle\,,\qquad{\rm with}\qquad|\psi\rangle=M_{\rm pl}\int d^{3}x~i\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}|f\rangle\,.

(31)

As far as
\mathscr{H}_{\rm phys}
is concerned,
\hat{Q}|\psi\rangle
corresponds to its null element. This is precisely what one usually means when referring to the BRST-exact Hamiltonian to be equivalent to zero. The repetitive application of the Hamiltonian yields the similar result, due to nilpotency
\hat{Q}^{2}=0
, resulting in

\displaystyle e^{-i\hat{H}t}|f\rangle=|f\rangle+\hat{Q}|\Psi\rangle\,,

(32)

where
|\Psi\rangle
is yet another time-dependent unphysical state. The direct consequence of this observation is that the matrix element of the time evolution operator in physical states is time-independent

\displaystyle\langle f_{1}|e^{-i\hat{H}t}|f_{2}\rangle=\langle f_{1}|f_{2}\rangle\,.

(33)

In other words, the entire time-dependence of the state (
32
) is the image and, consequently, the Hamiltonian flow does not change the elements of
\mathscr{H}_{\rm phys}
.

To see, in what sense does such a Hamiltonian flow have absolutely reasonable consequences, it suffices to examine the expectation value of any non-diffeomorphism invariant operator in the state (
32
). It is immediately clear that the image of the state, which captures the entire time-dynamics, gives a non-vanishing contribution to the matrix element, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle f|\hat{O}|f\rangle(t)=\langle f|e^{i\hat{H}t}\hat{O}e^{-i\hat{H}t}|f\rangle\neq\langle f|\hat{O}|f\rangle(t=0)\,.

(34)

This follows from the fact that the image of (
32
) does not generically have a vanishing contribution to the expectation value of gauge-variant operators, simply due to the physicality of
|f\rangle
; e.g.
\langle f|\hat{O}\hat{Q}|\Psi\rangle\neq 0
. In order to illustrate the point, the latter matrix element can be rewritten in terms of the BRST variation as
\langle f|{\rm i}\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm B}\hat{O}|\Psi\rangle
in general. Taking into account that the variation of the gravitational field is linear in the ghost field (
6
), the matrix element in question to be non-vanishing it is necessary for
|\Psi\rangle
to contains a state with a single ghost on top gravitational degrees of freedom, similar to
|\psi\rangle
from (
31
). All this is also reinforced by (
25
), and in fact follows directly from it.

In summary, the physicality of the state, along with (
33
), does not preclude the time-evolution of correlation functions of reparametrization-non-invariant operators.

5.1
Nitty-Gritty of State Construction

Having demonstrated that, let us discuss what type of BRST-invariant states would lead to the trivial time-evolution of all correlation functions, i.e. exhibiting features of DeWitt’s states
[
1
]
. These are the states that at the first glance would have been most desirable to have due to their manifest BRST invariance, built by the action of an arbitrary BRST-invariant operator on a (BRST-invariant) Hamiltonian eigenstate, say a vacuum
|\Omega\rangle
. In other words, as a direct consequence of (
25
), we have

\displaystyle\hat{H}\hat{O}|\Omega\rangle=0\,,

(35)

for a (fermionic) bosonic operator
O
that (anti-)commutes with
\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}
and satisfies
[\hat{Q},\hat{O}]=0
.

Now let us ask, if not invariant what should be the transformation property of ghost-independent operator
\hat{O}
so that
\hat{O}|\Omega\rangle
is annihilated by
\hat{Q}
but at the same time is not an eigenstate of
\hat{H}
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\hat{Q}\hat{O}|\Omega\rangle=0\,,\qquad\text{as long as}\qquad[\hat{Q},\hat{O}]=\hat{\Delta}\hat{Q}\,,

(36)

for an arbitrary
\hat{\Delta}
. Furthermore, according to (
25
)

\displaystyle[\hat{H},\hat{O}]\neq 0\,,\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\int d^{3}z\left\{\hat{\Delta}\hat{Q},i\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}(z)\right\}\neq 0\,.

(37)

The important question is whether we can relax (
36
).
The particular relaxation we are interested in concerns with the introduction of nontrivial c-number functions in
\hat{O}
. Indeed, due to Poincaré invariance, the BRST invariance of the vacuum state
|\Omega\rangle
implies that it is also annihilated by the BRST charge density. In particular, denoting the latter by
\hat{\rho}_{\rm BRST}
, invariance of the vacuum can be written as

\displaystyle\int d^{3}xe^{i\hat{P}\cdot x}\hat{\rho}_{\scriptscriptstyle BRST}(0)|\Omega\rangle=0\,,

(38)

where we have used the translation invariance of the vacuum. This opens up options to relax (
36
), e.g. to a following possibility:

\displaystyle[\hat{Q},\hat{O}]=\int d^{3}x\hat{\Delta}(x)\hat{\rho}_{\scriptscriptstyle BRST}(x)\,.

(39)

Here,
\hat{\Delta}
is once again an arbitrary operator that would depend on c-number functions defining
\hat{O}
. The explicit construction of such operators will be pursued elsewhere.

6
Boundary: Asymptotic Minkowski

Following the series of works
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
11
,
3
,
18
]
, we envision our effective field theory formulation to be defined with the Minkowski vacuum, which is essential for the
S
-matrix formulation of the theory. Because of this, it is essential to extend the prior discussion to the asymptotically Minkowski spacetimes. Part of the discussion of the previous section changes significantly in the case of non-compact space with the boundary. In particular, the total Hamiltonian acquires the boundary contribution, which is necessary for the consistency of the variation principle at the spatial boundary
[
19
]
and at the same time equates the total Hamiltonian to the Arnowitt–Deser–Misner (ADM) mass
[
20
]
. The inclusion of the ADM surface term to the Hamiltonian in the context of quantum gravity goes back to
[
1
]
.

The need for additional surface contribution to (
15
), for the consistency of the variation principle, is evident from the explicit form of the Hamiltonian constraint alone. In particular, a problematic contribution to the Einstein-Hilbert Hamiltonian can be cast in the form of the following surface integral

\displaystyle H_{\rm EH}\supset M_{\rm pl}^{2}\int d^{3}x~\partial_{k}\left(\frac{1}{A^{0}}\gamma\gamma^{ij}\gamma^{k\ell}\left(\partial_{i}\gamma_{\ell j}-\partial_{\ell}\gamma_{ij}\right)\right)\,,

(40)

which arises from the spatial Ricci scalar appearing in (
16
).

As it is well-known, the consistency requires the removal of such boundary contributions. This, in turn, will lead to the modification of (
24
) by surface terms. So that the total Hamiltonian takes the following form

\displaystyle\hat{H}=M_{\rm pl}\int d^{3}x\left\{\hat{Q},i\hat{\Pi}^{c}_{0}\right\}+\int d^{3}x~\partial_{k}\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\,.

(41)

In classical GR it would have sufficed to require the variation of this surface contribution to cancel the variation of (
40
). As a matter of fact, as we are focusing on asymptotically Minkowski spacetimes,
\mathcal{A}_{k}
would be dictated by the linearized form of (
40
); see
[
19
]
for the detailed discussion of the classical surface terms.

In the case of BRST quantization of GR, there are additional requirements that the surface term of (
41
) must satisfy. For starters, it must be BRST invariant. This immediately tells us few important things. First and foremost, the matrix element of the Hamiltonian in physical states, and the expectation value as a special case among them, is given purely by the aforementioned surface term

\displaystyle\langle f_{1}|\hat{H}|f_{2}\rangle=\int dS^{k}\langle f_{1}|\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{k}|f_{2}\rangle\equiv\langle f_{1}|{\hat{E}_{ADM}}|f_{2}\rangle\,,

(42)

where
dS^{k}
denotes the surface element on the spatial boundary of the Minkowski space, and
E_{ADM}
is the ADM mass/energy.

Furthermore, due to the BRST exactness of the bulk Hamiltonian the similar relation holds for arbitrary powers of the Hamiltonian operator, which at the end of the day leads to the following physical matrix element of the time-evolution operator

\displaystyle\langle f_{1}|e^{-i\hat{H}t}|f_{2}\rangle=\langle f_{1}|f_{2}\rangle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-it)^{n}}{n!}\langle f_{1}|\prod_{m=1}^{n}\int dS^{k}(z_{m})\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(z_{m})|f_{2}\rangle\,.

(43)

Therefore, unlike (
33
), the matrix elements evolve in time albeit purely due to boundary contribution. However, it is important to keep in mind that at the same time the Hamiltonian flow of the correlation functions in the spatial bulk follow the BRST exact bulk Hamiltonian, just as it did in the no-boundary scenario. Generic states of physical relevance (e.g. coherent states) are expected to be wave packets, rather than eigenstates of the ADM energy
[
1
]
. Hence, they will possess nontrivial time evolution even without bulk contribution. Providing a glimpse of the time-dependence of the scalar product (
43
).

7
Conserved Charges and the
S
-Matrix

From the previous section, it is evident that we can define the energy associated with a physical state
\ket{f}
, for Minkowski asymptotics.
Irrespectively whether the state is an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian or a coherent state, the expectation value of the energy will be conserved:

\frac{d}{dt}E_{ADM}=\frac{d}{dt}\bra{f}\hat{H}\ket{f}=0.

(44)

This makes energy a good quantum number for the classification of physical states. Obviously,
the physical states will be characterized by other quantum numbers. These were found in

[
19
]
, and they span the global Poincaré group, see e.g.
[
21
]
, and are represented by Casimirs and other conserved quantities which should be used to classify the physical Hilbert space, i.e., mass, spin, and energy-momentum. This means that we can define
the asymptotic particle states and their interactions according to these quantum numbers.

The full theory can be specified by identifying the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
which defines the spectrum, and accounts for the rest in the form of interactions, see e.g.
[
22
]
. This is equivalent to finding the full
S
-matrix, which includes all elementary and bound/composite states. In practice, this is a difficult task, and we resort to perturbative methods. We approximate
H_{0}
from non-interacting limit of the theory, treating the rest as perturbation.

At large distances, the perturbative description of GR simplifies to its quadratic form with the spectrum given by the graviton, which was studied in the BRST framework in
[
11
]
. In the linear approximation, the charges take asymptotic forms as well, namely,

E_{ADM}=M_{\rm pl}^{2}\int d^{3}x~\partial_{\ell}\left(\partial_{i}\gamma_{\ell i}-\partial_{\ell}\gamma_{ii}\right)=\int d^{3}xT_{00}\,,

(45)

where
T_{00}
denotes the global time-translation Noether charge density for the linearized graviton.
The BRST charge
Q
of the quadratic theory also tells us that asymptotic states of the theory
at low energies
contain only gravitons, and the non-linearities should be understood as their interactions, giving the usual
S
-matrix

In this framework, large black holes should be understood as
intermediate
multi-graviton resonances that eventually decay into the high multiplicity of asymptotic gravitons
[
12
]
.

We conclude this section with a brief discussion of a caveat related to the
S
-matrix in quantum gravity within the adopted framework. By construction, fully consistent physical states – prior to truncating the perturbation theory at any given order – are annihilated by the BRST charge. Consequently, the
S
-matrix elements defined via asymptotic states satisfying this condition cannot receive nontrivial contributions from the bulk Hamiltonian, for the reasons that led to (
43
). At first glance, this may seem problematic for particle scattering, such as that of gravitons, since boundary terms typically do not contribute to scattering amplitudes of Fock states. However, BRST-invariant asymptotic states correspond to infrared-dressed states rather than undressed Fock states without soft graviton dressing. In this respect, the situation closely parallels that in quantum electrodynamics (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
). At tree level, however, such dressing is usually neglected because infrared divergences are absent at that order. Consequently, the corresponding asymptotic states are not fully BRST invariant, and their scattering amplitudes do receive contributions from the bulk Hamiltonian. Commonly, the
S
-matrix analysis is based on the Lagrangian path-integral formalism, which we demonstrated in
[
3
]
to be equivalent to the Hamiltonian formalism within the BRST-invariant framework.

Thus, on Minkowski space, BRST quantization naturally restores the usual
S
-matrix and the classical equations of GR by consistency.

8
Outlook

There is a common negative sentiment about the BRST quantization of GR, because Einstein’s gravity has a non-linear constraint algebra (i.e. the structure “constants” depend on the metric fields) and is expected to require the extension of the framework by introducing the so-called anti-fields
[
24
]
. Yet, Kugo and Ojima formulated a BRST-invariant quantization of Einstein’s theory
[
4
]
. They asserted that demonstrating BRST invariance of the (gauge-fixed) action is straightforward. In our own consistency checks we have confirmed that it does indeed hold, without invoking equations of motion.
We have likewise verified explicitly that the BRST transformation is nilpotent in the Lagrangian formalism, again without employing the field equations.

Moving to canonical Hamiltonian variables is a more subtle step: one must use the operator form of Hamilton’s equations to deduce the BRST transformation properties of the conjugate momenta. However, doing so does not necessarily imply an on-shell assumption. The Hamilton operator equations encode full time evolution (on- and off-shell) and are distinct from the saddle-point path integral trajectories. Indeed, the full path integral is underpinned by Hamilton’s operator dynamics via the time-evolution operator
{\rm e}^{-i\hat{H}t}
.

To the best of our knowledge, Ref.
[
8
]
is the only place where the full BRST-exactness of the Hamiltonian in canonical GR has been stated, i.e. expressing the total Hamiltonian entirely as a BRST anticommutator of the form
H=\int d^{3}x\{Q,\Psi\}
, without residual Hamiltonian constraint terms. Although no discussion of consequencies for the time-evolution as the Hamiltonian flow on the physical Hilbert space is given. The general discussion connecting the constraint part of the Hamiltonian, in addition with auxiliary gauge-fixing sector, to the BRST-exact form has been discussed in
[
6
]
. There it has also been shown on a concrete simplified example that if the Hamiltonian consists of constraints alone, modulo gauge-fixing/Faddeev-Popov contributions, then it is fully BRST-exact and the Hamiltonian flow generates the time-reparameterization.

In this work we have verified that the bulk Hamiltonian of GR can be indeed written in the aforementioned BRST-exact form, and found its explicit compact expression. Therefore, the full Hamiltonian of quantum gravity with Minkowski vacuum reduces to this bulk term supplemented with the boundary contribution, corresponding to the ADM energy. We have, furthermore, demonstrated that the presence of this BRST-exact bulk contribution is vital in providing with the time-dependent bulk correlation functions. Although the latter was already shown in
[
3
]
, here we solidify the argument by linking it with this simplified form of the bulk Hamiltonian, in contrast to the common belief that BRST-exact Hamiltonian is equivalent to the triviality of the Hamiltonian flow. We show that, although the cohomology of the BRST charge – the space of equivalence classes that defines physical states – is itself time-independent, particular representatives of those classes can carry time-dependence through their image. As a result, expectation values or correlation functions of gauge-variant operators (e.g. metric, curvature) acquire time-dependence expected from the semi-classical analysis. In fact, quantum Hamiltonian acts as a time-reparameterization on all fields, with the exclussion of the auxiliary sector. Most importantly, our work strengthens the idea that the BRST-quantization of GR answers the puzzle posed by DeWitt
[
2
]
, where the author noticed the mismatch between seemingly over-restricitve canonical quantization and the path-integral formulation of quantum gravity. We even identify the possibility of the existence of the BRST-invariant states that would be analogs of DeWitt’s physical states. In particular, the states constructed by the action of the BRST-invariant operators on the vacuum are automatically eigenstates of the bulk Hamiltonian, which was the entire Hilbert space in case of DeWitt’s construction. We also pave the road for building the wider class of states within our framework.

Last but not least, we have highlighted how the nontrivial
S
-matrix of GR emerges despite the BRST-exactness of the bulk Hamiltonian.

A Model-Free Universal AI
Yegon Kim, Juho Lee

Abstract
In general reinforcement learning, all established optimal agents, including AIXI, are model-based, explicitly maintaining and using environment models. This paper introduces Universal AI with Q-Induction (AIQI), the first model-free agent proven to be asymptotically ε \varepsilon -optimal in general RL. AIQI performs universal induction over distributional action-value functions, instead of policies or environments like previous works. Under a grain of truth condition, we prove that AIQI is strong asymptotically ε \varepsilon -optimal and asymptotically ε \varepsilon -Bayes-optimal. Our results significantly expand the diversity of known universal agents.

Background

2.1
General Reinforcement Learning

We establish the basic notation for general reinforcement learning.
We also provide a summary of all the important symbols in
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{O}
, and
\mathcal{R}\subseteq[0,1]
denote the finite sets of actions, observations, and rewards, respectively. The percept space is
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{R}
, and a history
h_{1:t}\in\mathcal{H}:=(\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{E})^{\ast}
is a sequence of actions and percepts.
We write
h_{1:t}=a_{1}e_{1}\dots a_{t}e_{t}
for the history up to time
t
.

\mathcal{H}_{t}:=(\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{E})^{t}
denotes the set of all histories up to time
t
.
A policy
\pi:\mathcal{H}\to\Delta\mathcal{A}
maps histories to action distributions, while an environment
\nu:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{A}\to\Delta\mathcal{E}
maps history-action pairs to percept distributions.
When policy
\pi
interacts with environment
\nu
, they induce a distribution
\nu^{\pi}
over
\mathcal{H}_{t}
:

\displaystyle\nu^{\pi}(h_{1:t})

\displaystyle:={\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}\pi(a_{1})}\,{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}\nu(e_{1}|a_{1})}\,\cdots\,{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}\pi(a_{t}|h_{<t})}\,{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}\nu(e_{t}|h_{<t}a_{t})}

\displaystyle\phantom{:}=\textstyle{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\pi(a_{i}\mid h_{<i})}\,{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\nu(e_{i}\mid h_{<i}a_{i})}

\displaystyle\phantom{:}={\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}\pi(a_{1:t}\parallel e_{<t})}\,{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}\nu(e_{1:t}\parallel a_{1:t})}

This is a valid distribution, that is, the probabilities sum to 1 across all histories
h_{1:t}
. A direct consequence is that for a deterministic policy
\pi^{\prime}
,

\sum_{e_{1:t}\in\mathcal{E}^{t}}\nu\left(e_{1:t}\parallel\pi^{\prime}\left(e_{<t}\right)\right)=1,

(1)

where
\pi^{\prime}(e_{<t})
is the sequence of actions
a_{1:t}
taken by
\pi^{\prime}
under fixed percepts
e_{<t}
.
We also define the sample space
\Omega:=(\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{E})^{\infty}
, which consists of all
infinite
action-percept sequences
h
. Every history
h_{1:t}
is a prefix of some outcome
h
.

Given a discount factor
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the (full)
return
at time
t
is defined as

R_{t}:=(1-\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}r_{t+k},

where
{1-\gamma}=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\right)^{-1}
ensures that returns lie in
[0,1]
, like the rewards.
This definition assumes a geometric discount, and we discuss the generalization to general discount sequences in
.

The
value function
for policy
\pi
in environment
\nu
is

V^{\pi}_{\nu}(h_{<t}):=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\nu}\left[R_{t}\mid h_{<t}\right],

where
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\nu}
is the expectation with respect to
\nu^{\pi}
.
The optimal value is defined as
V^{\ast}_{\nu}=\sup_{\pi}V^{\pi}_{\nu}
, and an optimal policy
\pi^{\ast}_{\nu}
is any policy that achieves this supremum.
The action-value, or Q-value function, is
Q^{\pi}_{\nu}(h_{<t},a_{t}):=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\nu}\left[R_{t}\mid h_{<t}a_{t}\right]
.

Value functions satisfy the Bellman equations, which allow us to express one with the other:

\displaystyle V_{\nu}^{\pi}(h_{<t})

\displaystyle\!=\!\mathbb{E}_{a_{t}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid h_{<t})}\left[Q_{\nu}^{\pi}(h_{<t},a_{t})\right]

\displaystyle Q_{\nu}^{\pi}(h_{<t},a_{t})

\displaystyle\!=\!\mathbb{E}_{e^{\prime}_{t}\sim\nu(\cdot\mid h_{<t},a_{t})}\left[(1-\gamma)r_{t}+\gamma V_{\nu}^{\pi}(h^{\prime}_{<t+1})\right]

Obtaining the full return requires simulating or experiencing an infinite length trajectory. Given some horizon length
H\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the
H
-step return is defined as

R_{t,H}:=(1-\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\gamma^{k}r_{t+k},

which can be computed with
H
steps of agent-environment interactions.
The
H
-step value function is defined as

V^{\pi}_{\nu,H}(h_{<t}):=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\nu}\left[R_{t,H}\mid h_{<t}\right]
.
Finally, we define the effective horizon as follows.

Definition 2.1

(Effective horizon)
.

For
\eta>0
, the
\eta
-effective horizon is

H(\eta):=\min\left\{H\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\;\middle|\;(1-\gamma)\sum_{k=H}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}\leq\eta\right\}.

One can show that
the
H(\eta)
-step return differs from the full return by at most
\eta
, and likewise for the value function:

R_{t}-R_{t,H(\eta)}=(1-\gamma)\sum_{k=H(\eta)}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}r_{t+k}\leq\eta
.

2.2
AIXI

To define AIXI, we first need to introduce the mixture environment
\xi
.

Definition 2.2

(Mixture environment)
.

For a countable environment class
\mathcal{M}
and a prior distribution
w(\nu)>0
over the environments
\nu
in
\mathcal{M}
, the mixture environment
\xi
is defined as the unique environment that satisfies

\xi(e_{1:t}\parallel a_{1:t})=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}}w(\nu)\,\nu(e_{1:t}\parallel a_{1:t}).

Equivalently,
\xi^{\pi}(\cdot)=\sum_{\nu}w(\nu)\nu^{\pi}(\cdot)
for any policy
\pi
.

AIXI is then defined as the optimal policy in the mixture environment
\xi
. By its definition, AIXI is the Bayes-optimal agent with respect to the prior
w(\nu)
.

Definition 2.3

(AIXI)
.

AIXI is the optimal policy
\pi^{\ast}_{\xi}
in the mixture environment
\xi
:

\pi^{\ast}_{\xi}(h_{<t}):=\argmax_{a}Q^{\ast}_{\xi}(h_{<t},a).

3
Universal AI with Q-Induction

We present Universal AI with Q-Induction (AIQI), a model-free approach to general reinforcement learning.

For a discretization level
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
the
M
-discretized
H
-step return at time
t
is defined as

z_{t}:=\frac{\left\lfloor MR_{t,H}\right\rfloor}{M},\quad z_{t}\in\mathcal{Z}:=\left\{0,\frac{1}{M},\dotsc,\frac{M-1}{M}\right\}.

Note that
z_{t}
is an approximation of
R_{t,H}
whose error gets smaller as
M\to\infty
. More precisely, it always holds that
R_{t,H}-z_{t}<1/M
.
We choose
z_{t}
as the target of prediction instead of
R_{t,H}
.
Although this choice isn’t necessary for our case of geometric discounting, there exist general discount sequences (
) where
R_{t,H}
can take an infinite number of possible values, which would complicate our analysis, and also the implementation.

We would like to be able to predict the discretized return
z_{t}
conditioned on any history
h_{<t}
and action
a_{t}
. One can naturally consider augmenting the history with returns,

\dots{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}a_{t-3}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{t-3}}{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}e_{t-3}}{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}a_{t-2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{t-2}}{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}e_{t-2}}{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}a_{t-1}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{t-1}}{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}e_{t-1}}{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}a_{t}},

which would be passed in to a Bayesian sequence predictor to predict
z_{t}
.
However, a complication arises:
computing the ground truth return
z_{t-1}
requires the yet to be observed reward

in
\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}e_{t}

Similarly, the returns

Therefore, instead of augmenting the sequence with returns at every step, we augment with a period

\dots{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}a_{t-2N}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{t-2N}}{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}e_{t-2N}}\dots{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}a_{t-N}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}z_{t-N}}{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}e_{t-N}}\dots{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}a_{t-1}}{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}e_{t-1}}{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}a_{t}}.

Then, the augmented returns require rewards at times only up to
t-N+H-1\leq t-1
, which are all available.
Why we don’t simply consider
N=H
will be revealed in the proof of
.
The periodically augmented sequence can be passed in to a Bayesian sequence predictor, to predict the next return
z_{t}
.
We formalize this below.

,

\par Next, we define AIQI as follows. We also provide a pseudocode in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{alg:aiqi}.
\begin{definition}Fix a return horizon $H$, a discretization level $M$, an augmentation period $N\geq H$, an exploration rate $\tau>0$, and a unified predictor $\psi$.
AIQI, denoted $\hat{\pi}^{H,M,N,\tau}_{\psi}$ or $\hat{\pi}$, is defined as:
$$\hat{\pi}(a\mid h_{<t}):=(1-\tau)\mathds{1}\left[a=a^{\ast}\right]+\tau/|\mathcal{A}|,$$where $a^{\ast}=\argmax_{a_{t}}\hat{Q}(h_{<t},a_{t})$ and
$$\hat{Q}(h_{<t},a_{t})=\sum_{\tilde{z}_{t}\in\mathcal{Z}}\tilde{z}_{t}\cdot\psi(\tilde{z}_{t}\mid h_{<t}a_{t}).$$In case multiple actions maximize $\hat{Q}$, we assume a fixed tie-breaking rule.
\end{definition}
Note that AIQI doesn't simulate the environment or plan for the future.
Instead, AIQI directly predicts its own action-value, and at every step chooses the action that maximizes it.
As evidence accumulates, the value predictions become more and more accurate. In the limit, AIQI chooses the action that maximizes its true action-value, and will keep doing so in all future steps, thereby turning into a globally optimal policy.
Another way to look at AIQI is that it is performing policy evaluation at every step, treating the whole trajectory as having been generated by a single policy, and performing policy improvement in choosing the next action.

\par As is common in the universal AI literature, AIQI faces the grain of truth (self-referential) problem \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{kalai1993rational,leike2016formal,meulemans2025embedded}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}.
Roughly speaking, every mixture return-predictor $\psi_{n}$ in $\psi$ has to contain in its class the true conditional return distribution under the AIQI policy $\hat{\pi}_{\psi}$, which itself depends on $\psi$.
The formal statement is given below.
\begin{definition}Fix the AIQI parameters $H,M,N,\tau$.
A unified predictor $\psi$ has a grain of truth w.r.t. environment $\nu$ if: every $\psi_{n}$ is a mixture of a class $\mathcal{P}_{n}$ that contains the return-predictor
$\phi^{\ast}$ given by
$$\phi^{\ast}(\tilde{z}_{i}\mid\tilde{h}_{<i}^{\mathchoice{\raisebox{-1.83333pt}{\resizebox{768499}{360448}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\displaystyle(n)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.83333pt}{\resizebox{768499}{360448}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$\textstyle(n)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.83331pt}{\resizebox{662269}{360448}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.22499pt}{$\scriptstyle(n)$}}}}}{\raisebox{-1.83333pt}{\resizebox{662269}{360448}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle(n)$}}}}}}a_{i})=\nu^{\hat{\pi}}(z_{i}=\tilde{z}_{i}\mid h_{<i}a_{i}),\kern 5.0pti\equiv n\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N),$$where $\hat{\pi}=\hat{\pi}^{H,M,N,\tau}_{\psi}$ and the right hand side is the conditional return distribution under $\nu^{\hat{\pi}}$.
\end{definition}
A nontrivial solution can be obtained by fixing a reflective oracle $O$ \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{fallenstein2015reflective,leike2016formal}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}, and setting $\mathcal{P}_{n}$ and $\mathcal{M}$ to be the class of all $O$-computable return-predictors and environments, respectively.
Under this setup, the AIQI policy $\hat{\pi}$ is $O$-computable, and for every $\nu\in\mathcal{M}$, $\phi^{\ast}=\nu^{\hat{\pi}}$ is also $O$-computable, hence inside $\mathcal{P}_{n}$.
\par We now introduce a notion of optimality that AIQI will be shown to satisfy. It is a more lenient form of strong asymptotic optimality, which is one of the strongest forms of optimality considered in the universal AI literature.
\begin{definition}Given $\varepsilon>0$ and a class of environments $\mathcal{M}$, a policy $\pi$ is strong asymptotically $\varepsilon$-optimal if, for all environments $\nu\in\mathcal{M}$,
$$\limsup_{t\to\infty}V_{\nu}^{\ast}(h_{<t})-V_{\nu}^{\pi}(h_{<t})\leq\varepsilon,\quad\text{$\nu^{\pi}$-a.s.}$$\end{definition}
It has been shown by \cite[citet]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{lattimore2011asymptotically}{\@@citephrase{[}}{\@@citephrase{, Theorem\penalty\ 8]}}} that any deterministic policy, including AIXI, is not strong asymptotically $\varepsilon$-optimal for all $\varepsilon<1/4$.

An elementary property of TV distance is that, for a countable set $\{E_{i}\}$ of pairwise disjoint events,
\begin{equation}\textstyle\sum_{i}|P(E_{i})-Q(E_{i})|\leq 2\cdot D(P,Q).\end{equation}

Fix an environment $\nu\in\mathcal{M}$, and let $L=N-H+1$.
Fix some $\beta>0$, and with it, choose $S\subseteq\Omega$ as in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:error-return-predictor}. For any $h\in S$, choose $t_{0}$, again as in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:error-return-predictor}. \@@amsalign
Recall that
$L\geq\log_{\gamma}\frac{\varepsilon}{5}$,
$\tau\leq\frac{\varepsilon(1-\gamma)}{10}$,
$M\geq\frac{10}{\varepsilon(1-\gamma)}$,
and
$\eta\leq\frac{\varepsilon(1-\gamma)}{10}$.
If we use
$\beta=\frac{\varepsilon\tau}{20}|\mathcal{A}|^{-2}(\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}(\gamma|\mathcal{A}|/\tau)^{l})^{-1}$ in choosing $S$ and $t_{0}$,

$$\limsup_{t\to\infty}\delta_{\infty}(h_{<t})\leq\varepsilon,\quad\text{$\nu^{\pi}$-a.s.}\qed$$

\par We can also show that strong asymptotic $\varepsilon$-optimality in the environment class $\mathcal{M}$ implies asymptotic optimality in the mixture environment $\xi$.
\begin{lemma}Given a mixture $\xi$ of environment class $\mathcal{M}$,
and a policy $\pi$ that is strong asymptotically $\varepsilon$-optimal with respect to $\mathcal{M}$,
the same policy $\pi$ is asymptotically $\varepsilon$-optimal in $\xi$:
$$\limsup_{t\to\infty}V_{\xi}^{\ast}(h_{<t})-V_{\xi}^{\pi}(h_{<t})\leq\varepsilon$$holds both $\xi^{\pi}$-a.s. and, for all $\nu\in\mathcal{M}$, $\nu^{\pi}$-a.s.
\end{lemma}
\par As a consequence, AIQI is also asymptotically optimal in $\xi$.
In other words, AIQI approximates \emph{Bayes-optimality}, which is the defining feature of AIXI.
\begin{theorem}For any mixture $\xi$ of environment class $\mathcal{M}$,
the AIQI policy $\hat{\pi}^{H,M,N,\tau}_{\psi}$ with the parameters in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:aiqi-eps-optimal} is asymptotically $\varepsilon$-optimal in $\xi$.
\end{theorem}
\@proof\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:aiqi-eps-optimal} and \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:strong-implies-bayes}.
\qed\end@proof
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Off-Policy Behavior}
\par Until now we have solely discussed the on-policy setting, where the policy that interacts with the environment is the same as the policy that we are optimizing.
In the \emph{off-policy setting}, we must infer an optimal policy from the interactions generated by some ``historic policy'' $\pi^{\prime}$ that
we {do not} have control over.
We can formalize this notion of optimality with the \emph{self-optimizing} property.
\begin{definition}A policy $\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}\bar{\pi}$ is called self-optimizing for a class of environments $\mathcal{M}$ and a historic policy $\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}\pi^{\prime}$, if for every $\nu\in\mathcal{M}$,
$$\lim_{t\to\infty}V_{\nu}^{\ast}(h_{<t})-V_{\nu}^{{\color[rgb]{0.5508,0.1953,0.1953}\bar{\pi}}}(h_{<t})=0,\quad\nu^{\color[rgb]{0.1172,0.2734,0.4688}\pi^{\prime}}\text{-a.s.}$$\end{definition}
\cite[citet]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{hutter2002self}{\@@citephrase{[}}{\@@citephrase{]}}} showed a remarkable property of AIXI, that if there \emph{exists} a self-optimizing policy for $\mathcal{M}$ and $\pi^{\prime}$, then AIXI is one of those self-optimizing policies.
On the other hand, we can expect that AIQI does not satisfy this property, since it is an on-policy MC control algorithm.
\par In fact, if the predictor $\psi$ supports the return-predictor $\phi^{\ast}=\nu^{\pi^{\prime}}$, AIQI will eventually choose actions with the highest value under the historic policy $\pi^{\prime}$, and not its own value---the optimality of AIQI is thus no longer guaranteed. Even if we relax the self-optimizing property to the $\varepsilon$-self-optimizing property by replacing $\lim=0$ with $\limsup\leq\varepsilon$, the following can be shown.
\begin{theorem}Suppose the unified predictor $\psi$ is built from classes $\mathcal{P}_{n}$ that contain all computable variable-order Markov models.
For any period $N$, exploration rate $\tau$, horizon $H\geq 2$, tolerance $\varepsilon$ small enough (depending on $H$), and discretization level $M$ large enough
(depending on $H,\varepsilon,\tau$), there exist
an environment class $\mathcal{M}$, a historic policy $\pi^{\prime}$, and a discount factor
$\gamma\in(0,1)$, for which there exists a self-optimizing policy, but the AIQI policy $\hat{\pi}^{H,M,N,\tau}_{\psi}$ is not even $\varepsilon$-self-optimizing.
\end{theorem}
\par This completes our investigation of the theoretical properties of AIQI.
We have assumed a geometric discount setting for simplicity,
but we can extend all our results to the general discount setting, where returns are defined as $R_{t}=\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}r_{k}/\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}$ for a general discount sequence $\gamma_{k}$.
Specifically, our results can be generalized to cases where the discount sequence decays faster than a geometric sequence.
We detail the generalization in \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{sec:arbitrary_discount}.

\par\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{tab:comparison} summarizes notable RL methods, grouped by whether they are proven to be optimal in general environments, and whether they use a model of the environment.
Below we discuss in detail several lines of work that are related to ours.
\par Compress and Control \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{veness2015compress}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}, CNC;]} is an algorithm that shares many similarities to AIQI such as return prediction, but has only been analyzed for fully observable MDPs.
Both CNC and AIQI are on-policy Monte Carlo control methods with a distributional flavour \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{bellemare2017distributional}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}, since they predict the return distribution instead of the average return.
CNC uses a model-based approach for MC evaluation, unlike AIQI.
\par\par Feature reinforcement learning \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{daswani2015generic}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} converts a general environment to a finite state MDP through a learned feature map, and then performs planning \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{hutter2009feature}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} or model-free RL \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{nguyen2011feature,daswani2013q}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} inside the MDP.
\cite[citet]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{hutter2014extreme}{\@@citephrase{[}}{\@@citephrase{]}}} shows that any general environment can be mapped to an MDP where the optimal policy is still $\varepsilon$-optimal in the general environment.
\cite[citet]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{majeed2018q}{\@@citephrase{[}}{\@@citephrase{]}}} prove that Q-learning is optimal when the mapping satisfies ``state-uniformity''.
However, these theoretical results do not tell us about the learning of such mappings, and results that are concerned with the learning \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{sunehag2010consistency}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} do not concern themselves with control.
\par AIXI{tl} \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{hutter2000theory}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} is an approximation of AIXI that considers a set of candidate policies that output an estimate of its value under $\xi$.
It keeps only the policies that can be proven to output conservative estimates, and chooses the policy with the highest value estimate.
Conceptually, the search for a proof that the value estimate under $\xi$ is conservative, can be considered a generalization of planning under $\xi$.
AIXI{tl} doesn't have an analogue in practical RL.
\par Self-AIXI \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{catt2023self}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} is a variant of AIXI that learns both a model of the environment and a model of itself. At every step, it uses both models to estimate its own action-values, by simulating the model of itself interacting with the model of the environment.
It then chooses the action with the highest estimated value.
Although Self-AIXI gets rid of planning, it is clearly model-based in that it learns and uses an environment model.
Our work was inspired by Self-AIXI, and can be seen as a modification that further gets rid of environment models by directly predicting action-values.
\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Discussion}
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Proof Technique}
\par To prove that AIQI is strong asymptotically $\varepsilon$-optimal, we first showed that the action-value estimates converge to the true $\nu^{\pi}$ action-values (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:error-q-estimation}), and that choosing the action with the highest (estimated) $\nu^{\pi}$ value makes the policy converge to optimal actions (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:aiqi-eps-optimal}).
There are two key novelties in our proofs compared to existing work in universal AI.
First, we had to deal with the problem of delayed feedback using periodic augmentations. This was not needed in previous works which model policies or environments, since actions and percepts are never delayed, unlike $H$-step returns.
Second, we introduced a chain of inequalities using \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:one-step-and-global}, to make the $\delta_{\infty}$ term in the upper bound vanish with $\gamma^{L}$.
This allowed us to bypass ad-hoc assumptions such as off-policy sensibility
\cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{catt2023self}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}, Theorem\penalty\ 16]}.
Overall, our work provides a blueprint for the analysis of policy iteration algorithms in general environments.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Partial Observability}
\par Real-world RL tasks are almost never plain MDPs, due to partial observability \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{aastrom1965optimal}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}.
The most straightforward way to extend MDP methods to partially observable MDPs (POMDPs) is by viewing the whole history as a state, and using function approximators, sequence models in particular, to handle this exponentially larger state space.
In fact, this is exactly the dominant approach in modern RL, where sequence models such as LSTMs \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{hochreiter1997long}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} or transformers \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{vaswani2017attention}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} are trained to model value functions \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{hausknecht2015deep}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}, policies \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{mnih2016asynchronous}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}, or environments \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{ha2018world}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}.
\par The theory of universal AI provides a particularly compelling account that is in line with practice: it regards the sequence model as a flexible learner with some broad prior over mappings whose domain consists of arbitrary length sequences.
In particular, it abstracts away the idea of a hidden MDP or a hidden state space.
Our work is the first to analyze a model-free algorithm under this account.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Continual Reinforcement Learning}
\par The class of general environments is equivalent to the class of infinite-state POMDPs, which might seem unnecessarily large.
However, there are nontrivial settings that cannot be captured by finite-state POMDPs, one such example being continual RL \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{ring1994continual}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}.
Continual RL studies environments that never stop changing \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{khetarpal2022towards}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}, and where agents must keep learning in order to act optimally \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{abel2023definition}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}.
The theory of universal AI sets aside problems with continual \emph{learning}, e.g., catastrophic forgetting, by assuming a perfect Bayesian learner with a broad prior.
This lets it focus on a different question:
how to design principled \emph{objectives} for continual RL, assuming continual learning is approximately solved.
Our work shows that learning distributional action-values is one such objective.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Temporal Difference Learning}
\par Most practical methods in model-free RL involve temporal difference (TD) learning \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{sutton1988learning}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]},
a form of stochastic approximation \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{robbins1951stochastic}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]}.
As has been noted by \cite[citet]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{engel2003bayes,engel2005reinforcement}{\@@citephrase{[}}{\@@citephrase{]}}}, a simple Bayesian version of TD learning does not model the correct generative process of a value function, except in deterministic environments.
A possible workaround is to perform full expectation updates by learning an environment model.
This is analogous to Dyna \cite[citep]{[\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{sutton1990integrated}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}]} with one-step rollouts.
We hypothesize that distributional, one-step Dyna-SARSA and Dyna-Q are, respectively, asymptotically optimal and self-optimizing.
\par\par
\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Conclusion}
\par We introduced Universal AI with Q-Induction (AIQI), the first model-free universal agent. AIQI performs universal induction over distributional action-value functions---rather than over environments or policies as in prior work.
It is essentially a Monte Carlo control algorithm, the most primitive form of model-free RL.
We prove that AIQI is strong asymptotically $\varepsilon$-optimal, asymptotically $\varepsilon$-Bayes-optimal, and, as expected from an on-policy MC control algorithm, not self-optimizing.
Remarkably, the only assumption we make is grain of truth, which is standard in universal AI and has a known resolution via reflective oracles.
\par Our findings significantly broaden the known landscape of universal agents.
We hope this provides both a theoretical foundation for understanding MC control with sequence models in partially observable or continual settings,
and a blueprint for the analysis of other policy iteration algorithms in general environments.
Promising directions for future work include
exploring whether a single unified predictor can replace the phase-indexed family of return-predictors,
investigating whether a Bayesian version of temporal difference learning can be used to construct asymptotically optimal or self-optimizing policies,
and analyzing the properties of AIQI with exploration strategies that are more sophisticated than $\varepsilon$-greedy, such as Thompson sampling.

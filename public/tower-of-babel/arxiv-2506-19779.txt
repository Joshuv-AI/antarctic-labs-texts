Role of non-thermal processes in the quiescent and active millimeter spectrum of a young M-dwarf. Journal: ApJ Facilities: NOEMA Numpy ( Harris et al. 2020 ) , Astropy ( Astropy Collaboration et al. 2013 ) , Matplotlib ( Hunter 2007 ) , Pandas ( pandas development team 2020 ) , Scipy ( Virtanen et al. 2020 ) , CASA, CLIC
Atul Mohan, Peter H. Hauschildt, Birgit Fuhrmeister, Surajit Mondal, Vladimir Airapetian, Sven Wedemeyer

Abstract
Millimeter (mm) emission from F - M dwarfs (cool stars) primarily traces chromospheric activity, with thermal emission thought to dominate in quiescence. Despite the high chromospheric activity, the quiescent mm spectral fluence (mm- S ⁡ ( ν ) \mathrm{S(\nu)} ) of young ( < < 1 Gyr) M dwarfs (dMs) remain largely unexplored. We present the quiescent mm- S ⁡ ( ν ) \mathrm{S(\nu)} of a young dM, AD Leo, observed around 94 GHz using the Northern Extended Millimetre Array (NOEMA). The observed quiescent mm- S ⁡ ( ν ) \mathrm{S(\nu)} exceeds the thermal flux density from a 1D chromospheric model, constrained by optical-UV spectroscopic data, by up to a factor of 7. This indicates a quasi-steady non-thermal emission powered by supra-thermal electrons unlike in old ( > > 1 Gyr) cool stars , whose quiescent mm- S ⁡ ( ν ) \mathrm{S(\nu)} generally agree with 1D thermal models. The mm-brightness temperature spectral index ( α mm \mathrm{\alpha_{mm}} ; T ( ν ) B {}_{\mathrm{B}}(\nu) ∼ ν − α mm \sim\nu^{\rm-\alpha_{mm}} ) of AD Leo deviates by a factor of 3 from the α mm \mathrm{\alpha_{mm}} –T eff scaling law for old sun-like stars ( Mohan et al. 2022 ) , while UV Ceti, an older M6V star, follows the trend. Also, we report a double-hump flare with second-scale variability in flux density and spectral index, and a frequency-rising nature with brightness increasing with frequency. The flare resemble certain solar events, but is unlike the second-scale events reported in dMs. The non-thermal flare humps suggest multiple injections of accelerated electrons. The mean flare luminosity ( ∼ 2 − 5 × 10 15 \sim 2-5\times 10^{15} erg s -1 Hz -1 ) and duration ( 18 ± 2 18\pm 2 s) are comparable to flares reported in AU Mic and Proxima Cen, but 100 - 1000 times weaker than the minutes-long dM flares observed by the South Pole Telescope.



I
Introduction

Low-mass main-sequence stars (M-dwarfs; dMs) host most of the known Earth-like exoplanets residing in habitable zones, making them interesting targets for exploring the active outer atmospheric structure and the emergence of activity that can impact exoplanetary habitability
(
Bashi et al. 2020
;
Airapetian et al. 2020
)

Emission at millimeter (mm) frequencies is expected primarily to trace the chromospheric thermal emission across heights in cool main-sequence stars (Spectral type: F - M)
(
Wedemeyer et al. 2016
;
White et al. 2020
;
Mohan et al. 2021
;
Mohan 2023
)
. However, during flares, the mm spectrum can have non-thermal contributions from flare-accelerated electrons in active regions, providing insights on the local particle acceleration and magnetic field strengths
(
MacGregor et al. 2020
)
.
Detecting the quiescent mm emission of nearby stars remained difficult until recently due to the lack of sensitive instruments. Even with the advent of modern telescopes like ALMA and NOEMA, the data on main sequence stars are still too sparse to make any robust statistical inferences or constrain stellar chromospheric models for different spectral types
(
MacGregor et al. 2020
;
Mohan et al. 2021
)
.

Mohan et al. 2021
compiled all robust detections of quiescent mm emission from main-sequence stars in the ALMA science archive and explored chromospheric heating across the stellar main sequence.
Using this sample,
Mohan et al. 2022
derived the mm-brightness temperature spectrum (T
{}_{\mathrm{B}}(\nu)
) for each star and estimated its spectral index (T
{}_{\mathrm{B}}(\nu)
\propto\nu^{-{\rm\alpha_{mm}}}
;
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
).
The authors explored the relationship between
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
and stellar physical parameters, especially the effective surface temperature T
eff
, demonstrating how
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
traces the atmospheric heating gradient across the main-sequence.
Their analysis suggested that
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
can be a meaningful metric of steady chromospheric heating and activity.

Though studies in mm band are sparse, the nature of quiescent chromospheric emission and its trends with physical parameters have been explored over decades using high energy spectroscopic metrics
(
Wilson 1968
;
Noyes et al. 1984
;
Herbig 1985
;
Stepien 1994
;
Vidotto et al. 2014
;
Marsden et al. 2014
;
Linsky 2017
;
Pandit et al. 2023
;
Ferreira et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.
Based on the trends shown by the common chromospheric activity metrics R
{}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{HK}}
(Ratio of Ca-II H & K flux to the bolometric flux),
Barnes 2003
showed that the stars fall under two different activity groups, namely ‘C’ and ‘I’, in the rotational period (
{\rm P_{rot}}
) - age - T
eff
plane. The C branch consists of young (
<
1 Gyr) fast rotators (
{\rm P_{rot}}
\lesssim
5 d), while the I branch comprises old (
>
1 Gyr) slow-rotating stars.
Stars tend to migrate from the active C to the I branch over time as they spin down due to magnetic braking and wind-driven mass loss
(
van Saders et al. 2016
;
Garraffo et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
.
Hence, to understand the nature of chromospheric structure and emergent activity, it is important to sample both populations across the
{\rm P_{rot}}
-age-T
eff
plane.
The study by
Mohan et al. 2022
was restricted to a small sample of F - K type I branch stars, for all of which the mm-emission was demonstrated by various authors
(
Wedemeyer et al. 2016
;
Trigilio et al. 2018
;
White et al. 2020
;
Mohan et al. 2021
, e.g.)
to be consistent with chromospheric thermal emission using data-constrained 1D models.
However, there has been no systematic study on a sample of active C branch stars due to lack of clear detections of quiescent stellar mm-emission across frequencies. A major technical hurdle has also been contamination from unresolved companions and circumstellar disks, both of which can have significant mm emission
(
Lestrade & Thilliez 2015
;
Marino et al. 2017
;
MacGregor et al. 2017
;
Booth et al. 2017
;
Long et al. 2019
;
Ansdell et al. 2020
;
MacGregor et al. 2020
;
Damian et al. 2023
, e.g.)
.
In the sample of
(
Mohan et al. 2021
)
, the authors had systematically removed candidate C branch stars with known unresolved disks based on IR and optical survey data.
The work presented here is part of an ongoing effort to sample F–M dwarfs across the
{\rm P_{rot}}
–age–T
eff
plane and systematically characterize their chromospheric quiescent and flare emission, as probed by mm-T
{}_{\mathrm{B}}(\nu)
, by choosing stars with no known companions or disks within the telescope resolution limit.

This study addresses AD Leo, a young (
\sim
250 Myr) M3.5V star close to the fully convective internal core transition boundary owing to its mass
(
Sebastian et al. 2021
, 0.4M
⊙
;)
and T
eff

(
Soubiran et al. 2016
,
\sim
3477 K; )

(
Morin et al. 2008
;

;
Chabrier & Baraffe 2000
;
Donati & Landstreet 2009
)
.
Located at a distance of
\sim 5
pc
(
Gaia Collaboration 2021
)
, the star shows a high optical flaring rate
\approx
0.6 h
-1

(
Dal 2020
)
.
Studies that monitored the star in spectral lines and X-ray continuum suggest high levels of chromospheric and coronal activity
(
van den Besselaar et al. 2003
;
Robrade & Schmitt 2005
;
Crespo-Chacón et al. 2006

Recently,
Mohan et al. 2024

Hence, AD Leo is a compelling candidate to probe the mm-T
{}_{\mathrm{B}}(\nu)

II.1.1
Quiescent spectrum

The CASA task
tclean
was used to image the calibrated L mode data with varying time-frequency averaging in STOKES I.
The images across the spectro-temporal axes in which a phase center source is detected at
\gtrsim 5\sigma
significance, where
\sigma
is the root mean square noise level in the black sky region, were chosen.
The following conditions were imposed on every chosen image to ensure that the detection is reliable, despite imaging artifacts.

1.

Gaussianity of the blank sky noise pixel flux density distribution.

2.

No negative flux pixels with absolute flux
>50
% of the phase center source are present in the field of view.

3.

The presence of the deepest negative point is justified given the blank sky noise statistics and the number of pixels in the image.

The first condition ensures that the
5\sigma
thresholding is meaningful given the true image plane noise statistics.
Imposing these conditions led to the further discarding of images, despite a
>5\sigma
phase center source detection.
The point source flux densities of the final set of reliable stellar detections were estimated using the standard CASA task
imfit

III.2.2
The photosphere + chromosphere model (S
{}_{mod}^{ch}
)

A Chromospheric 1D model typically consists of an underlying radial photospheric model in radiative equilibrium, terminated at some height to allow for a chromospheric temperature rise consistent with spectroscopic data.
We use a re-calculation of the model presented in
Fuhrmeister et al. 2005
for AD Leo, who also gives a detailed description of the model construction. The model was calculated using the atmosphere code PHOENIX
(
Hauschildt & Baron 1999

and the temperature structure was inferred based on the parameterized grid, that best reproduces the UVES spectrum containing various emission lines, including Balmer and Fe I lines. The re-calculation used here considered all lines of H I, He I, He II, C I-C III, N I-N III, O I-O III, Mg I-Mg III, Ca I-Ca III, K I-K III, and Fe I-Fe III in NLTE, compatible with the newest PHOENIX version.
Based on this atmospheric model,
\mathrm{S^{ch}_{mod}(\nu)}
was computed using the radiative transfer module in PHOENIX.

\mathrm{S^{ph}_{mod}(\nu)}
with the chromospheric model
\mathrm{S^{ch}_{mod}(\nu)}

IV
Discussion

IV.1
Quiescent spectrum

The significant deviation of
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
from the trend of I-branch stars and UV Ceti, and the failure of the approach to explain the mm-
\mathrm{S(\nu)}
based on a 1D chromospheric emission model constrained by UVES spectroscopic data are notable. The latter approach to explain the mm-
\mathrm{S(\nu)}
as thermal chromospheric emission has been quite successful in the case of I-branch stars. The high
\mathrm{\Delta S/S^{ch}_{mod}}
values questions the general expectation of the chromospheric thermal origin of quiescent mm emission in cool stars when it comes to active young dMs like AD Leo and strongly suggest the need to incorporate non-thermal emission mechanisms.
The observed mm-
\mathrm{S(\nu)}
index,
\delta=-2.7
, can arise via either as an optically thin non-thermal gyrosynchrotron emission or various coherent emission processes
(
Ginzburg & Zhelezniakov 1958
;
Tsytovich & Kaplan 1969
;
Melrose & Sy 1972
;
Dulk et al. 1979
;
Dulk 1985
;
Melrose 2017
)
.
A full STOKES spectrum could help identify any coherent emission signatures, which often show high circular polarization levels
(
Osten & Bastian 2006
;
Mohan et al. 2024
, e.g.)
.

There have been various reports of impulsive solar and stellar flares powered by various non-thermal processes in the GHz range
(
Nindos 2020
;
Das & Chandra 2021
;
Gary 2023
;
Mondal et al. 2024
)
.
The observed quiescent AD Leo spectrum could arise as a superposition of frequent small-scale impulsive events supplying supra-thermal electrons to the stellar atmosphere.
Earlier studies of AD Leo had revealed signatures of persistent small-scale activity in the photometric U and V bands, spectral lines and radio frequencies within 0.2 - 1.5 GHz that probe stellar emission across chromosphere to corona
(
Lang & Willson 1986
;
Crespo-Chacón et al. 2006
;
Villadsen & Hallinan 2019
;
Dal 2020
, e.g.)
.
Recently, using spectral line data,
Muheki et al. 2020
estimated a flaring rate of a few to 10 per hour for flares of energies within 10
29
- 10
31
erg in AD Leo.
Such high rates of strong flares can provide the necessary supply of energetic particles that can drive the observed emission.
Additionally, modeling the stellar quiescent X-ray emission,
Namekata et al. 2020
showed the presence of a steady high-energy coronal source with an effective temperature of 7 MK, distinct from the 3 MK plasma that prevails elsewhere.
Besides, spectro-polarimetric observations of quiescent AD Leo reveal a mean line of sight magnetic field strength of
\sim
3.5 kG in STOKES I data
(
Reiners et al. 2022
;
Bellotti et al. 2023
)
. The stellar active regions could have even higher field strengths at smaller scales in particle acceleration regions powering weak flares (energy
<
10
28
erg) at rates of a few every minute given the high flaring rates
(
Muheki et al. 2020
)
.
All the aforementioned aspects point to a likely steady supply of accelerated electrons in the atmosphere that could power the observed quiescent non-thermal emission.
Simultaneous soft X-ray, radio and spectral line observations are essential alongside wide band full STOKES mm data to better constrain the stellar quiescent emission model.

IV.2
The frequency-rising double-hump flare

We report the detection of a second-scale double-hump frequency-rising mm flare in 85 - 105 GHz range for possibly the first time in an active dM.
The flare
{\rm T_{B}}
reaches an order of 10
{}^{7}/f\,
K, where
f
is the fractional size of the flare region with respect to the stellar disk.
Modeling the modulations observed in various optical flares in AD Leo,
a few recent studies estimated the typical size of the flaring region
(
Stelzer et al. 2022
;
Ram et al. 2025
, e.g.)
.
Based on these results, we assume f
\sim
1% implying a
{\rm T_{B}}\sim 10^{9}\,
K for our mm-flare.
Such high
{\rm T_{B}}
, along with the second-scale variability in the STOKES I spectral index that occasionally exceeds 2 is difficult to explain
via a thermal flare emission mechanism
(
Fleishman & Kontar 2010
;
Wedemeyer et al. 2016
;
Nindos 2020
;
Fleishman et al. 2021
)
.
Various coherent and incoherent non-thermal emission mechanisms modulated by local or line of sight dynamics of plasma and magnetic fields have been proposed to explain the mm-flares with second-scale modulations in
\delta
and polarization
(
Silva et al. 1997
;
Kaufmann et al. 2001
;
Trottet et al. 2008
;
Fleishman & Kontar 2010
;
Zaitsev et al. 2014
;
Klopf et al. 2014
, e.g.,)
.
Most of these models are proposed in the context of solar flares, and local physical parameters can differ between stars, but plasma physics and emission mechanisms are extendable to cool stars
(
Güdel 2002
;
White 2004
;
Mohan 2023
)
.
The solar mm-flares for which a mechanism could be identified with reliable conviction had the advantage of simultaneous multi-waveband data, wide band GHz spectra with polarization calibration, and spatially resolved imaging
(
Kaufmann et al. 1985
;
Asai et al. 2001
;
Shen et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
.
The multi-waveband spectro-polarimetric data is vital to probe the thermal and non-thermal dynamics across various atmospheric heights, making up for the lack of spatially resolved stellar observations.

Meanwhile observation of the second-scale modulations in the STOKES I flare profile along with the frequency-rising character is noteworthy.
Several cases of
\lesssim
1 s scale quasi-periodic modulations in flare profiles have been reported in solar flares across 90 - 500 GHz, unlike in stellar flare research
(see,
Krucker et al. 2013
, for an overview)
.
These are often signatures of multiple episodes of particle acceleration and injection into the local magnetic field structures, irrespective of the details of the emission mechanism
(
Silva et al. 1997
;
Raulin et al. 2003
, e.g.,)
.
As mentioned earlier, without full STOKES spectra we cannot distinguish between a coherent and incoherent emission process, both of which can produce all observed emission features in a disk averaged stellar flare spectrum.

Although the L
95GHz
and duration of the AD Leo flare lie within the ranges reported for 230 GHz flares in AU Mic
(
MacGregor et al. 2020
)
and Proxima Cen
(
MacGregor et al. 2018
;
MacGregor et al. 2020
;
Howard et al. 2022
)
, the AD Leo flare stands out by exhibiting a frequency-rising behavior when the flare flux density is above 50% of the peak, unlike the others.
Together the flares in AU Mic, Proxima Cen and AD Leo span a luminosity range of
\sim
10
13
-
5\times
10
15
erg s
-1
Hz
-1
and mean spectral index rage within -2 to 1.5.
Meanwhile, these individual events are 1 - 3 orders of magnitude weaker than dM flares (luminosity range:
\sim 5\times
10
16
- 10
18
erg s
-1
Hz
-1
) in the SPT95 sample with a similar
\delta
range (see, Fig.
4
d), suggesting that the SPT95 flares are stronger cousins of the flares reported in AU Mic, Proxima Cen, and now in AD Leo.
Given the SPT sensitivity and the survey cadence of
\sim
2 minute, the survey will miss relatively weak flares with sub-minute scale durations.

V
Conclusion

The quiescent millimeter emission from cool main sequence stars (F - M type) is thought to primarily originate as chromospheric thermal emission across height
(
Wedemeyer et al. 2016
;
Trigilio et al. 2018
;
White et al. 2020
;
Mohan et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
. Hence
Mohan et al. 2022
proposed to use the mm brightness temperature (T
{}_{\mathrm{B}}(\nu)
\sim{\rm\nu^{-\alpha_{mm}}}
) spectral index,
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
as a proxy to chromospheric thermal gradient and derived its trend with the effective surface temperature, T
eff
, for old (
>
1 Gyr) slow-rotating sun-like (‘I’ branch) stars.
We present observations of quiescent emission from a young, rapidly rotating dM, AD Leo, in the millimeter band (mm-
\mathrm{S(\nu)}
). The mm-
\mathrm{S(\nu)}
of AD Leo is
\sim
2 - 7 times brighter than the thermal spectrum (
\mathrm{S^{ch}_{mod}(\nu)}
) expected from a 1D atmospheric model that includes the chromospheric component and is constrained by optical and UV spectroscopic data.
The
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
of AD Leo is also significantly higher than the values observed in
\epsilon
Eridani and the old dM, UV Ceti.
The deviation of mm-
\mathrm{S(\nu)}
from
\mathrm{S^{ch}_{mod}(\nu)}
and the high
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
suggest the relevance of non-thermal processes from supra-thermal electrons, in the quasi-steady mm-emission.
Meanwhile, the
\mathrm{\alpha_{mm}}
of UV Ceti is consistent with the prediction from the scaling law for old sun-like stars.

We report an
\sim
18 s long double-hump flare with a ‘frequency-rising’ nature, meaning the flux density rises with frequency, during times when the flare flux density was
\gtrsim
50% of its peak. To the best of our knowledge, a frequency-rising double-hump flare with second-scale spectro-temporal variability is not reported in dMs, though several reports exist in the Sun.
The flare brightness varies by
\sim
200 - 1000 times the quiescent level in a few second timescale with significant variability in the STOKES I spectral index.
The double-hump flare profile suggests multiple particle acceleration episodes producing non-thermal emission.
The AD Leo flare is
\sim
1–3 orders of magnitude fainter than the 95 GHz dM flares observed by SPT
Tandoi et al. 2024
, although its spectral index (
\delta
) remains within the SPT flare range.
However, the luminosity and duration of the flares reported in Proxima Cen and AU Mic compare well with the AD Leo flare, making these individual events weaker cousins of SPT flares.
In order to better constrain physical mechanisms contributing to the quiescent and flare emission in the mm bands we need simultaneous spectro-polarimetric observations across mm to X-ray wavelengths.

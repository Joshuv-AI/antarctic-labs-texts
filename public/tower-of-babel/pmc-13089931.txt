A plain language summary of the CheckMate 9DW study: nivolumab in combination with ipilimumab for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (advanced liver cancer)
Authors: Thomas Yau, Peter R Galle, Thomas Decaens, Bruno Sangro, Shukui Qin, Leonardo G da Fonseca, Hatim Karachiwala, Jean-Frederic Blanc, Joong-Won Park, Edward Gane, Matthias Pinter, Ana Matilla Peña, Masafumi Ikeda, David Tai, Armando Santoro, Gonzalo Pizarro, Chang-Fang Chiu, Michael Schenker, Aiwu He, Hong Jae Chon, Joanna Wojcik-Tomaszewska, Gontran Verset, Qi Qi Wang, Caitlyn Stromko, Jaclyn Neely, Prianka Singh, Maria Jesus Jimenez Exposito, Masatoshi Kudo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Plain Language Summary What is this summary about? This is a summary of a clinical study called CheckMate 9DW. The study was published in a medical journal called The Lancet in May 2025. In this study, researchers looked at a type of liver cancer called hepatocellular carcinoma (or HCC for short). The study focused on HCC that has spread throughout the body or is too large to be removed by surgery (which is called unresectable HCC).Before this study, doctors usually treated unresectable HCC with drugs called lenvatinib or sorafenib. These drugs helped some people, but they did not work as well as hoped and they had many side effects. Newer treatments that help the body’s own immune system to fight against HCC (called immunotherapy) have shown slightly better results. But more therapies that can help people live longer are needed.The goal of the CheckMate 9DW study was to find out if the combination of two immunotherapy drugs (nivolumab plus ipilimumab) works better than the older drugs (lenvatinib or sorafenib) in people with unresectable HCC. What were the results? Participants in this study were monitored for a period of time ranging approximately 31 to 40 months after being assigned to a treatment group. Researchers found that compared with people treated with lenvatinib or sorafenib, those treated with nivolumab plus ipilimumab: Lived longer overallWere more likely to have their tumors get smaller in size or completely disappear in scans, and remain that way for a longer timeHad manageable side effects and no new safety concernsTook longer to feel their cancer symptoms getting worseWere more likely to report improvements in their health-related quality of life What do the results mean? The results from the CheckMate 9DW clinical study show that the combination of two immunotherapy drugs, nivolumab plus ipilimumab, was an effective first treatment for people with unresectable HCC. This study included patients from 25 countries and showed that people treated with this combination were able to live longer with manageable side effects compared with previously available treatment options, lenvatinib or sorafenib. Further, more people who received nivolumab plus ipilimumab had a measurable effect on their tumors than those who received lenvatinib or sorafenib.Based on these results, many countries, including the United States, those in the European Union, China, and Japan, have now approved this treatment for people with unresectable HCC or with HCC that has spread to a different part of the body, who have not had any other treatments for their advanced liver cancer.Clinical trial number: NCT04039607

Surgical management of a patient with severe and complex maxillofacial injuries caused by a bear mauling in the forest: A case report from Thailand
Authors: Nutthawut Akaranuchat, Boonyaporn Kotistienkul, Wanchalerm Chungsiriwattana
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
A 60-year-old Thai male sustained severe and complex maxillofacial injuries, along with superficial neck and abdominal injuries, from being mauled by a black bear in Thailand. The accident caused massive injury to the face, including the right eye and periorbital soft tissue, right cheek, total nose, total upper lip, partial lower lip, and underlying bone structures. A computed tomography scan showed comminuted pan-facial fractures. Lifesaving procedures were performed at nearby hospitals, and then he was referred to our center one month after the injury. Our initial multi-stage surgical management strategy for treating this patient included debridement, right eyeball enucleation, coverage of the entire defect with anterolateral thigh with vastus lateralis chimeric free flap, and gastrostomy tube insertion to ensure appropriate nutrition. The next phase of treatment involved total nasal reconstruction with the two-stage folded paramedian forehead flap with cadaveric costal cartilage grafts. The outcomes of treatments provided the patient with spontaneous breathing via neonostrils and eating directly through his mouth. This case report demonstrates that satisfactory results can be achieved using a well-planned multi-stage reconstruction strategy.

Introduction

Complex facial injuries resulting from a bear mauling have been reported in a few case reports and a small number of case series.1, 2, 3, 4 The incidence of bear attacks is shifting toward suburban areas as a result of deforestation and the bears need to expand the area size to find food.5, 6 Bear attacks are extremely rare in Thailand, but most of the cases that have been reported were tourism-related. A previous study from Kashmir reported that the face area is the primary site of injury, accounting for 80.57 % of bear attacks.2 In this study, we report a case of severe and complex maxillofacial injury caused by a bear mauling in the forest, and our multi-phase, multi-step surgical reconstruction strategy.

Case report

A 60-year-old Thai male sustained a severe and complex maxillofacial injury caused by a bear attack while he was fishing in the forest. He suddenly encountered a black bear (Ursus thibetanus) near the river after which he was mauled (by paw swipe) on his face, neck, and abdomen. He was transported to the nearest community hospital where he was intubated with an endotracheal tube to protect his airway, then referred to the provincial hospital. At the provincial hospital, further examinations revealed severe multiple maxillofacial injuries with a large avulsion wound at his face, the laceration wounds at the neck and abdominal wall (Figure 1).Figure 1Photograph of the patient during the first presentation; The patient had extensive tissue loss in his face including the right periorbital orbital area, nose, both cheeks, upper lip, and lower lip.Figure 1

Emergency and lifesaving surgical procedures under general anesthesia were performed starting with a tracheostomy. The patient had extensive tissue loss at the right periorbital area with an intact eyeball, total nose defect, total upper lip defect, and one-third lower lip defect. Neck exploration showed no injury to the great vessels, no other aerodigestive tract injury, and bleeding was controlled. The depth of the abdominal laceration wound was to the subcutaneous layer, so debridement was performed, and the wound was sutured. An orogastric tube was positioned during that same operation. The wound was dressed with wet dressing with gauze soaked in povidone-iodine solution twice daily. Additionally, antibiotics including ceftriaxone and clindamycin were administered to control infection.

After his condition stabilized, computed tomography (CT) was performed. CT imaging showed non-displaced fractures at the bilateral anteromedial wall of the frontal sinus with patency of the left nasofrontal duct. Bilateral comminuted naso-orbital-ethmoidal fractures, comminuted fracture of right zygoma with partial bone loss, and comminuted fracture of the nasal septum, vomer, and ethmoid bone. The patient also had comminuted Lefort II fractures with the split palatal fracture at his midface; however, his mandible remained intact (Figure 2).Figure 2Computed tomography (CT) scan of the patient during the first presentation. The patient was then referred to our center one month after the attack for surgical reconstruction. On examination, all bony structures are already malunion without fixation. An ophthalmologic examination of his right eye demonstrated traumatic optic neuropathy, loss of visual acuity, and loss of all extra-ocular muscle movements in all directions. Fortunately, the visual acuity of his left eye was found to be within normal limits. We then developed the following multi-phase, multi-step surgical strategy to manage the patient's facial injury.

Phase 1. Surgical debridement, soft tissue coverage, and gastrostomy tube insertion.

Phase 2. Total nasal reconstruction.

Phase 3. Touch-up procedures to improve facial contour and appearance.

The first operation started with wound debridement and enucleation of the right eyeball, followed by reconstruction with anterolateral thigh (ALT) with vastus lateralis (VL) chimeric free flap coverage to cover the entire defect, including the right maxillary defect. The flap was sutured and fixated the fascia to the periosteum at the lateral rim of nasal cavity. The donor site was covered with split thickness skin graft from the contralateral thigh. Gastrostomy tube insertion was then performed by general surgeons. (Supplement data 1)

One month later, total nose reconstruction with delayed folded paramedian forehead flap and cadaveric costal cartilage grafts was performed. Firstly, a rectangular-shaped tissue expander was inserted into the left side of the forehead in the subgaleal plane via a left hemi-coronal incision. The tissue expander was then infiltrated and expanded once a week until the desired size was achieved.

Three months later, partial elevation of the ALT with VL chimeric free flap was performed. We also created a framework for the neonose using cadaveric costal cartilage grafts (cadaveric grafts for cantilever dorsal augmentation, dorsal spreader graft, columellar strut graft with bilateral alar grafts). The expanded left paramedian forehead flap was then mobilized to cover the cartilage grafts. The entire surface of the cartilage grafts was covered with the folded part of the flap. One month later, the pedicle of the paramedian forehead flap was detached, which coincided with bilateral commissuroplasty and left medial canthoplasty. Left medial canthoplasty was performed by raising the left lower eyelid and cheek flap and wiring fixation of the medial canthal ligament to the nasal bone. The lower part of the bipartitioned ALT flap was rotated and advanced in V-Y fashion at the left oral commissure and later commissuroplasty was done at both oral commissures to restore the function of the mouth. At two months post-operation, the patient can breathe spontaneously through his nostrils, can eat directly through his mouth, and can close his left eye completely without any ectropion or scleral show. Discussion

Most bear-mauling patients are middle-aged males, who were attacked in the forest. However, the trend is shifting towards more populated areas.1, 2, 3,7 Facial injury was reported to be the most common site of injuries.1, 2, 3 However, major soft tissue loss, such as in our patient, was found in 25 % of victims, and loss of bony structure was found in only 10 %.3

Bear mauling wounds can cause polymicrobial infections, such as Serratia marcescins, Aeronomas hydrophilia, Bacillus cereus, and Enterococcus durans. Culture in our patient showed positive for multiple organisms, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Acinetobacter baumannii, Enterococcus faecalis, and Coryneform bacteria. Appropriate intravenous antibiotics were given based on the drug susceptibility results.

Structured multi-phase management of severe and complex maxillofacial injuries has been proven to improve the final clinical and aesthetic outcomes.8, 9, 10 From this case, the authors found that multi-stage surgical management was efficacious approach to managing the severe and complex maxillofacial injuries in a bear mauling and recommend the same strategy in any type of animal attack. The following management protocol is recommended:1.Lifesaving procedure to protect the airway and stop bleeding.2.Debridement and wound coverage as soon as possible.3.Steps to ensure nutritional support to improve the patient's general condition, nutritional status, and to promote wound healing.4.Major soft tissue and bony reconstruction to yield good functional outcomes, especially for breathing via nose and eating via oral route.5.Touch-up procedures to improve facial contour and appearance, such as flap debulking, bone grafting, liposuction, lipofilling, and eye prosthesis.

Conclusion

This case report demonstrates a case of Thai man who sustained severe and complex maxillofacial injuries with profound loss of many maxillofacial structures. The satisfactory results can be achieved by using a well-planned multi-stage reconstruction strategy.

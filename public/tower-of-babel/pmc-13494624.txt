Diffuse testicular microlithiasis is associated with successful sperm retrieval in men with nonobstructive azoospermia
Authors: Yuji Tsuji, Kazuma Nakano, Shoichi Nariyoshi, Noriko Abe, Gen Sukegawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate whether ultrasonographic patterns of testicular microlithiasis are associated with sperm retrieval outcomes by microdissection testicular sperm extraction in men with nonobstructive azoospermia.DesignRetrospective cohort study.SubjectsA total of 975 men with histologically confirmed nonobstructive azoospermia who underwent microdissection testicular sperm extraction between July 2003 and September 2025 at two specialized male infertility clinics.ExposurePreoperative scrotal ultrasonography, with classification of testicular microlithiasis into four patterns: no microlithiasis, limited microlithiasis, classic microlithiasis, and diffuse microlithiasis characterized by a snowstorm appearance.Main Outcome MeasuresSuccessful sperm retrieval by microdissection testicular sperm extraction.ResultsDiffuse testicular microlithiasis characterized by a snowstorm appearance (n = 6) was associated with uniformly successful sperm retrieval. In contrast, limited and classic forms of testicular microlithiasis showed no independent association with sperm retrieval outcomes. Histological parameters, particularly Johnsen’s score, demonstrated the strongest association with successful sperm retrieval.ConclusionsDiffuse testicular microlithiasis with a snowstorm appearance was associated with uniformly successful sperm retrieval by microdissection testicular sperm extraction in men with nonobstructive azoospermia, suggesting preserved focal spermatogenesis in this subgroup.

Nonobstructive azoospermia (NOA) represents one of the most severe forms of male infertility and is characterized by the complete absence of spermatozoa in the ejaculate because of intrinsic testicular failure. Since the introduction of microdissection testicular sperm extraction (TESE), sperm retrieval has become feasible even in men with severely impaired spermatogenesis (1, 2); however, retrieval rates remain highly variable, and reliable noninvasive predictors of successful sperm retrieval are limited (3, 4, 5). In this context, rare but distinct ultrasonographic phenotypes may provide important biological and clinical insights into the heterogeneity of spermatogenic failure in men with NOA.

Testicular microlithiasis (TML) is an ultrasonographic finding characterized by multiple small intratesticular hyperechoic foci measuring approximately 1–3 mm in diameter without posterior acoustic shadowing (6). Although often detected incidentally, TML has been associated with testicular germ cell tumors, cryptorchidism, and impaired spermatogenesis (7, 8). In men with NOA, most available clinical studies have suggested that the presence of TML is associated with poorer spermatogenic potential and/or lower sperm retrieval rates; however, the overall evidence base remains limited and heterogeneous, and its clinical significance for sperm retrieval outcomes has not been fully established (9, 10).

Previous studies evaluating TML in men with NOA have yielded heterogeneous findings (5, 10). Importantly, most investigations have treated TML as a binary variable (present or absent), without accounting for its extent or intratesticular distribution, which may partly explain the heterogeneity of reported outcomes. Moreover, the relationship between ultrasonographic patterns of TML and underlying testicular histopathology has not been systematically examined in large cohorts of men with NOA.

We have previously investigated ultrasonographic assessment of seminiferous tubule morphology as a noninvasive surrogate marker of spermatogenic function (4, 11). Using high-resolution scrotal ultrasonography, we demonstrated that alterations in the ultrasonographic appearance of seminiferous tubules reflect changes in spermatogenesis after gonadotropin treatment in men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (11) and that ultrasonographically determined seminiferous tubule size predicts successful sperm retrieval by microdissection TESE in men with NOA (4). During these investigations, we encountered a subset of patients in whom diffuse intratesticular microlithiasis produced a snowstorm appearance that precluded ultrasonographic visualization of seminiferous tubule morphology. Notably, despite the inability to assess seminiferous tubule architecture, these patients frequently achieved successful sperm retrieval by microdissection TESE, prompting further investigation into the clinical significance of diffuse TML.

From a pathophysiological perspective, TML is generally considered an intratubular calcification related to degenerating germ cells or intraluminal debris (7, 8) and may therefore reflect current or prior germ cell activity. On this basis, we hypothesized that the prognostic significance of TML in men with NOA depends on its ultrasonographic distribution pattern rather than its mere presence. Accordingly, we classified TML into four distinct ultrasonographic patterns (6) and examined their associations with sperm retrieval outcomes by microdissection TESE and underlying histopathological findings in a large cohort of men with histologically confirmed NOA.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient population

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at two specialized male infertility clinics. The medical records of patients who underwent microdissection TESE between July 2003 and September 2025 were reviewed. A total of 975 patients with histologically confirmed NOA were included in the analysis. Men were clinically suspected of having NOA on the basis of at least two semen analyses confirming azoospermia in the absence of clinical evidence of obstructive azoospermia. The final diagnosis of NOA was confirmed histologically using testicular tissue obtained at microdissection TESE. Patients with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy, or incomplete clinical data were excluded. This study was approved by the institutional review board of Ebisu Tsuji Clinic (IRB number 23005), and the requirement for written informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study.

Ultrasonography

Preoperative scrotal ultrasonography using a high-frequency linear transducer (10–14 MHz: Aloka SSD3500, Aloka; Toshiba SSA-580A, Toshiba) was routinely performed in all patients before microdissection TESE as part of the standard clinical evaluation. Testicular microlithiasis was defined as the presence of multiple small intratesticular hyperechoic foci measuring approximately 1–3 mm in diameter without posterior acoustic shadowing, consistent with established ultrasonographic definitions (6). For the present study, TML was further classified into four ultrasonographic patterns on the basis of its extent and intratesticular distribution: no TML (Fig. 1A), limited TML, classic TML, and diffuse TML. This four-pattern classification scheme was developed in the present study to characterize the heterogeneity of ultrasonographic presentations rather than to reflect previously established categorical definitions. Limited TML was defined as fewer than five (<5) hyperechoic foci per field (Fig. 1B), whereas classic TML was defined as five or more (≥5) hyperechoic foci per field (Fig. 1C). In contrast, quantification of diffuse TML using a ‟per-field” approach was considered unreliable because the field of view varies depending on probe settings and magnification. Therefore, to objectively represent TML density, we evaluated the threshold at which intratesticular architecture could no longer be reliably visualized on ultrasonography. On the basis of this assessment, diffuse microlithiasis was defined as the presence of 40 or more microliths per 1 cm2 of testicular cross-sectional area (Fig. 1D), or as an extensive aggregation of microliths resulting in acoustic attenuation that precluded adequate visualization of intratesticular structures (Supplemental Fig. 1, available online). All examinations were performed by board-certified sonographers (K.N. and S.N.) throughout the study period, and the presence and distribution pattern of TML were documented in the medical record at the time of examination; these contemporaneous chart records (rather than retrospective rereview of stored images) were used for classification in the present study.Figure 1Representative ultrasonographic appearances of testicular microlithiasis. Testicular volume was calculated using the largest dimension on the ultrasonographic images according to the Lambert formula: (length) × (width) × (height) × 0.71 (12), and the mean bilateral testicular volume was used for analysis (12).

Hormonal assessment

Serum levels of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), and total testosterone were measured in all patients using chemiluminescent immunoassays. Blood samples were obtained once at baseline, typically during morning clinic hours, although the exact sampling time was not strictly standardized. Assays were performed by external certified clinical laboratories.

Genetic analysis

All patients underwent karyotype analysis using standard G-banding techniques. Chromosomal findings were classified as normal karyotype, Klinefelter syndrome, sex chromosome abnormalities other than Klinefelter syndrome, or autosomal abnormalities. In addition, microdeletions of the deleted in azoospermia (DAZ) gene or the azoospermia factor (AZF) region on the Y chromosome were assessed using multiplex polymerase chain reaction (13). The DAZ gene deletions were categorized as present or absent. The AZF deletions were classified as AZFa/b deletions, AZFc deletions, partial AZFc deletions, or no deletion. Genetic evaluation was performed according to the testing methods available at the time of examination. In earlier cases, DAZ gene deletion analysis was performed. After Y-chromosome AZF microdeletion testing became clinically available, AZF deletion analysis was conducted, and DAZ gene testing was no longer routinely performed. Complete AZFa or AZFb deletions were considered to have an extremely low likelihood of sperm retrieval (14) and were therefore not indications for microdissection TESE at our institutions. Partial AZFa or partial AZFb deletions were not assessed in our routine testing panel.

Microdissection testicular sperm extraction (TESE)

All patients underwent microdissection TESE under local anesthesia by experienced reproductive urologists (Y.T. and G.S.), following a standardized protocol throughout the study period. After a wide longitudinal incision of the tunica albuginea, seminiferous tubules were examined under an operating microscope at magnifications of ×25. Dilated and opaque tubules suggestive of active spermatogenesis were selectively harvested (1). Retrieved tissue was immediately processed for sperm search using a standardized laboratory workflow (mechanical mincing and microscopic examination) by an embryology team; two senior embryologists (K.N. and S.N.) supervised this process throughout the study period and are listed as investigators. Sperm retrieval was defined strictly as identification of at least one unequivocal spermatozoon; sperm-like cells without definitive sperm morphology were not counted as sperm. In men with AZFc microdeletions, when ejaculated sperm were identified on repeat semen analyses, ejaculated sperm were preferentially used for ICSI and microdissection TESE was reserved for men with persistently confirmed azoospermia.

Histopathological evaluation

Testicular tissue specimens were fixed in Bouin’s solution, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin for histological evaluation. Histopathological diagnosis was categorized into hypospermatogenesis, maturation arrest, Sertoli cell-only syndrome, and tubular hyalinization according to established criteria. The degree of spermatogenesis was semiquantitatively assessed using Johnsen’s scoring system on a scale from 1 to 10 (15). For each patient, the mean Johnsen’s score was calculated on the basis of the evaluation of multiple seminiferous tubules.

Outcomes

The primary outcome measure was the sperm retrieval rate by microdissection TESE according to the pattern of TML. Secondary outcome measures included serum hormone levels, testicular volume, histopathological findings, Johnsen’s scores, chromosomal abnormalities, Y-chromosome microdeletions, and patient age.

Statistical analysis

The continuous variables of interest (including age) showed a substantial departure from the normal distribution in terms of skewness and kurtosis. Accordingly, continuous variables were summarized as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR; first and third quartiles) by TML category. Overall differences among the four groups were assessed using the Kruskal–Wallis test, and comparisons with the no TML group were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were summarized as proportions; overall differences were assessed using the χ2 test, and comparisons with the no TML group were performed using Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Adjusted means of testicular volume and circulating sex hormone levels across TML categories were estimated using multiple linear regression with robust standard errors. These comparisons of hormonal and other clinical parameters across TML categories were considered secondary/exploratory; therefore, no formal adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied, and results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating.

The primary endpoint was successful sperm retrieval by microdissection TESE (dichotomous outcome). Associations with sperm retrieval were examined using logistic regression, and odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Continuous variables (except Johnsen’s score) were categorized into tertiles. Because more than half of the patients had a Johnsen’s score of 2, Johnsen’s score was categorized as <2, 2, and >2. Because genetic testing strategy and surgical indications evolved during the study period, the calendar period was categorized as 2003–2012 and 2013–2025 and included as a covariate. Overall associations were assessed using the likelihood ratio test. Multivariable analysis was performed using Firth penalized logistic regression when complete separation occurred (16). Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P value <.05. All the statistical analyses were carried out by Stata version 13 (StataCorp, College Station, TX).

Results

Baseline clinical, hormonal, histopathological, chromosomal, and genetic characteristics according to TML category are summarized in Table 1. Among 975 men, the distribution of ultrasonographic microlithiasis patterns was as follows: no TML (n = 830), limited TML (n = 90), classic TML (n = 49), and diffuse (“snowstorm”) TML (n = 6). Patients with limited TML had smaller testicular volume, higher LH levels, and lower testosterone levels compared with patients without TML. Patients with diffuse microlithiasis exhibited higher Johnsen’s scores than those in the other TML categories. Although age differed among groups, no consistent differences in FSH levels were observed across categories. Hypospermatogenesis was most prevalent in patients with diffuse TML, whereas maturation arrest was most frequently observed in patients with classic TML. No significant differences were observed among TML categories with respect to Y-chromosome AZFc or DAZ deletions. After adjustment for the other listed variables, calendar period, age, Johnsen’s score, TML category, histopathology, and history of mumps orchitis remained associated with successful sperm retrieval in likelihood-ratio tests. Limited and classic TML were not independently associated with sperm retrieval compared with no TML. Adjusted ORs for categories with 100% sperm retrieval were not presented because, although Firth penalized logistic regression yields finite estimates under complete separation, these estimates were considered clinically uninterpretable owing to the very small number of patients.

Discussion

In this large retrospective cohort of men with histologically confirmed NOA, we demonstrated that the prognostic significance of TML varies substantially according to its ultrasonographic distribution pattern. Specifically, diffuse TML characterized by a snowstorm appearance was associated with uniformly successful sperm retrieval by microdissection TESE, whereas limited and classic forms showed no independent association with sperm retrieval outcomes. These findings directly address the heterogeneity of prior reports and underscore the importance of considering the ultrasonographic extent and distribution of TML rather than its mere presence (10).

A key observation of the present study is the close relationship between diffuse TML and underlying testicular histopathology. Patients with a snowstorm appearance frequently demonstrated hypospermatogenesis and higher Johnsen’s scores, indicating preserved focal spermatogenesis despite extensive intratubular calcifications. In contrast, limited and classic TML were not associated with favorable histological features or improved sperm retrieval outcomes. Together, these results suggest that diffuse microlithiasis represents a distinct biological phenotype within the spectrum of NOA, characterized by residual spermatogenic activity that can be effectively identified by microdissection TESE.

The association between older age and successful sperm retrieval may appear counterintuitive. However, it may reflect differences in the underlying histopathological spectrum of NOA rather than a direct beneficial effect of aging. In our previous study, Sertoli cell-only syndrome was more frequent in younger men, whereas hypospermatogenesis was more frequent in older men, and the association between age and sperm retrieval was attenuated after adjustment for histopathology (4). This pattern is biologically plausible because some older men with NOA may have developed azoospermia as a consequence of age-related decline in spermatogenesis in testes that previously contained germ cells and may once have produced sperm. Thus, the higher sperm retrieval rate observed in older men should be interpreted as reflecting underlying histopathology rather than age itself. Similarly, the higher sperm retrieval rate observed in the later study period may partly reflect changes in genetic testing and surgical indication after the introduction of AZF microdeletion testing, after which patients with AZFa or AZFb deletions were no longer considered candidates for microdissection TESE.

The pathophysiological mechanisms underlying TML remain incompletely understood. Microliths are widely considered to represent intraluminal calcifications derived from degenerating germ cells or cellular debris, potentially related to impaired phagocytic function of Sertoli cells (7, 8). From this perspective, the presence of microlithiasis may imply residual or prior germ cell activity, distinguishing these testes from Sertoli cell-only syndrome, which is characterized by the absence of germ cells. In cases of diffuse microlithiasis, extensive accumulation of microliths may obscure ultrasonographic visualization of seminiferous tubule morphology, producing the characteristic snowstorm appearance. Nevertheless, our histopathological findings indicate that such testes may still harbor focal areas of active spermatogenesis, consistent with the uniformly successful sperm retrieval observed in this subgroup. Diffuse (“snowstorm”) microlithiasis may represent a rare and pathobiologically distinct phenotype rather than an advanced form of scattered microlithiasis. The mechanisms underlying this diffuse pattern remain unknown. In the present study, diffuse microlithiasis was not associated with a history of mumps orchitis, and no patients with diffuse microlithiasis had a clinical history of other overt orchitis. In addition, we did not observe histopathological features suggestive of inflammation or overt tissue injury. These findings do not support postinflammatory testicular injury as the primary explanation for the diffuse microlithiasis phenotype in this cohort; however, any mechanistic interpretation should be considered hypothesis-generating because of the small number of diffuse cases.

One possible explanation for the discrepancy between preserved focal spermatogenesis and the absence of sperm in the ejaculate in men with diffuse microlithiasis is the presence of microobstruction within the seminiferous tubules caused by extensive intratubular calcifications (17, 18). Although this hypothesis remains speculative and was not directly assessed histologically in the present study, it is consistent with both the snowstorm appearance observed on ultrasonography and the high sperm retrieval rates achieved by microdissection TESE. Intratubular microobstruction may promote intraluminal stasis and debris accumulation, potentially contributing to microlith formation, conceptually analogous to urinary stone formation in the setting of urinary stasis. Prolonged intratubular stasis might facilitate progressive accretion and enlargement of microliths, potentially contributing to the heterogeneous microlith sizes observed in diffuse cases on ultrasonography and histology. These hypotheses warrant direct histopathological and functional confirmation in future studies.

Our findings help reconcile previously conflicting evidence regarding the clinical significance of TML in men with NOA. Prior studies have reported microlithiasis as a negative prognostic factor for sperm retrieval (10); however, such associations may largely reflect the confounding influence of severe underlying testicular pathology when microlithiasis is analyzed as a binary variable. In particular, cohorts dominated by Sertoli cell-only syndrome are likely to demonstrate poor sperm retrieval outcomes regardless of the presence of microlithiasis. By stratifying microlithiasis according to ultrasonographic distribution patterns and examining corresponding histopathological features, our study provides a more nuanced interpretation of these earlier observations.

The present study has several strengths. The large sample size, inclusion of only histologically confirmed NOA, standardized microdissection TESE technique, and detailed ultrasonographic classification of TML allowed robust evaluation of the relationships among imaging findings, histopathology, and clinical outcomes. In addition, integration of Johnsen’s score provided biological validation of the observed associations between diffuse microlithiasis and favorable sperm retrieval outcomes.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The retrospective design may introduce selection bias, and genetic testing methods evolved during the study period. In addition, although our ultrasonographic classification was operationally defined, interobserver variability in microlithiasis assessment cannot be entirely excluded. Furthermore, diffuse TML was rare in this cohort (n = 6), and thus the generalizability of the findings for this phenotype should be interpreted with caution. Although we discuss potential biological interpretations of the diffuse (‟snowstorm”) phenotype, the underlying mechanism could not be evaluated in this retrospective study, and no specific histopathological features supporting inflammation or overt tissue injury were identified. Finally, because sperm retrieval was successful in all men with diffuse TML and in all men with hypospermatogenesis, conventional logistic regression was affected by complete separation. We therefore used Firth-penalized logistic regression for multivariable analysis; however, adjusted ORs for categories with 100% sperm retrieval were not presented because the resulting estimates were considered clinically uninterpretable owing to the very small number of patients. This finding highlights the strong association between these features and successful sperm retrieval.

Conclusion

Diffuse TML with a snowstorm appearance identifies a distinct subgroup of men with NOA with a consistently high likelihood of successful sperm retrieval by microdissection TESE. These findings suggest preserved focal spermatogenesis in this subgroup and support a pattern-based interpretation of TML in the evaluation of men with NOA. Despite the limited number of cases with diffuse TML, the uniformly successful sperm retrieval observed in this subgroup suggests that this ultrasonographic pattern represents a biologically meaningful phenotype rather than a random observation.

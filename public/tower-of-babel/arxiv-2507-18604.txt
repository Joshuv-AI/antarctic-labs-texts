A Microlocal Theory for Zariski-Constructible Sheaves on Rigid Analytic Varieties
Tong Zhou

Abstract
† † July 2025 We develop a microlocal theory, in the sense of Kashiwara-Schapira, for Zariski-constructible sheaves on rigid analytic varieties. We define and study monodromic sheaves, the monodromic Fourier transform, specialisation, microlocalisation, micro-hom, and singular support in this context. Some questions and conjectures are formulated in the end. The appendix contains infinity-categorical characterisations of monodromic sheaves.



1
Introduction

In the 1960s, Mikio Sato, when studying partial differential equations, introduced the microlocal point of view, which systematically studies objects on a manifold via constructions involving the cotangent bundle. This point of view spread to other fields. In the 1970s–90s, Masaki Kashiwara and Pierre Schapira developed a microlocal theory for sheaves on real and complex manifolds, and studied their relation to D-modules (
[
KS90
]
). It has since had applications to partial differential equations, symplectic geometry, geometric representation theory, real geometric Langlands, and other fields.

On real manifolds, the basic constructions of microlocal sheaf theory are as follows. Let
Z\hookrightarrow X
be a closed immersion of manifolds, and consider
\mathbf{R}
-valued sheaves for definiteness. Recall that a sheaf on a vector bundle is called
conic
if it is constant on each
\mathbf{R}_{>0}
-orbit. (1) The specialisation
\nu_{Z}(-)
is a functor from sheaves on
X
to conic sheaves on the normal bundle
T_{Z}X
. It is a version of nearby cycles for higher codimensions and without the choice of a function. (2) The microlocalisation
\mu_{Z}(-)
is a functor from sheaves on
X
to conic sheaves on the conormal bundle
T^{*}_{Z}X
, defined as the Fourier-Sato transform of
\nu_{Z}
. It is a version of vanishing cycles for higher codimensions and without the choice of a function. (3) The micro-hom
\mu hom(-,-)
is a functor from a pair of sheaves on
X
to conic sheaves on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}X
. It is a sheaf-theoretic analogue of the micro-hom of D-modules. (4) For every sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, the singular support
SS(\mathcal{F})
is a closed conical subset in
T^{*}X
, measuring the directions in which
\mathcal{F}
is not locally constant. (5)
\mu sh
is a stack on
T^{*}X
, which is a sheaf-theoretic analogue of microdifferential-modules on
T^{*}X
. (6) For every constructible sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, the characteristic cycle
CC(\mathcal{F})
is a
\mathbf{Z}
-cycle supported on
SS(\mathcal{F})
, with coefficients equal to the “local Euler characteristics” of
\mathcal{F}
. There are various relations among these constructions, most notably, we have
\mu hom(\underline{\mathbf{R}}_{Z},-)\simeq\mu_{Z}(-)
,
SS(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{supp}\,\mu hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})
, and
H^{0}(\mu hom(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F})_{v})\simeq Hom_{\mu sh_{v}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F})
for
v\in T^{*}X
. The last relation is crucial in Kashiwara and Schapira’s proof of the theorem of coisotropicity of the singular support—a theorem of central importance. These together make precise that a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
gives a “microlocal-sheaf
\mu\mathcal{F}
” on
T^{*}X
:
SS(\mathcal{F})
is the support of
\mu\mathcal{F}
and
\mu hom
’s are homomorphisms between them. This is the sheaf-theoretic analogue of obtaining a microdifferential-module on
T^{*}X
from a D-module on
X
, and has precise relations to the constructions on the D-module side through Riemann-Hilbert.

A natural question is if one can develop microlocal theories in other sheaf-theoretic contexts. For
\ell
-adic étale sheaves on algebraic varieties over a field, a complete theory of
SS
and
CC
has been developed by Alexander Beilinson and Takeshi Saito for finite coefficients (
[
Bei16
]
,
[
Sai17
]
), and extended to
\ell
-adic coefficients in
[
Bar23
]
and
[
UYZ20
]
. No satisfactory theory of the specialisation, microlocalisation, micro-hom and
\mu sh
exists so far, due to the difficulty of wild ramifications in positive characteristic (the specialisation defined in
[
Ver83
]
does not see wild ramifications,
c.f.

[
AS06
]
).

In this work, we develop a microlocal theory for
\Lambda
-valued Zariski-constructible sheaves on rigid analytic varieties over
K
, where
K
is a non-archimedean field which is non-trivially valued, complete, algebraically closed, and of characteristic
(0,p)

Conventions

We use the six-functor formalism of étale sheaves on adic spaces constructed in
[
Hub96
]
. The derived categories
D(X,\Lambda)
are in the triangulated sense.
\mathrm{Sh}(X,\Lambda)
denotes the abelian category of sheaves. The coefficient
\Lambda
is often omitted. All sheaf-theoretic functors are derived. By a “local system”
\mathcal{L}
on
X
we mean an object of
D^{(b)}_{zc}(X)
such that each
\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathcal{L})
is locally constant with finite type stalks. For
\mathcal{F}\in D(X)
and

, we use
\mathcal{F}\langle d\rangle
to denote
\mathcal{F}(d)[2d]
, where
(-)
(resp.
[-]
) denotes the Tate twist (resp. shift).

By a “rigid analytic variety” we mean an adic space locally topologically of finite type over
\mathrm{Spa}(K,K^{\circ})
. We use the terms “analytic closed subset” and “Zariski-closed subset” interchangeably. For a Huber ring
A
topologically of finite type over
K
, we abbreviate
\mathrm{Spa}(A,A^{\circ})
as
\mathrm{Spa}(A)
. For
r\in|K^{\times}|
,
D(r):=\mathrm{Spa}(K\langle\frac{T}{r}\rangle)
denotes the closed unit disc of radius
r
,
D(1)
is abbreviated as
D
, punctured discs
D(r)-\{0\}
are denoted by
D^{\times}(r)
. By a “classical point” of
X
we mean a
K
-point,
i.e.
, an element of
X(K)
. For
\lambda\in K^{\times}
and
n\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\mu_{n}(\lambda)
denotes the
n
-th roots of
\lambda
in
K
. We abbreviate
\mu_{n}(1)

\mu_{n}
, and denote the profinite group

. The spaces
\mathbf{G}_{m},\mathbf{A}^{1}

K

We use “
\simeq

3
Monodromic sheaves

The target of the microlocal functors will be monodromic sheaves on certain vector bundles. In this section, we define and study monodromic sheaves in our context. The set-up is as in §
1
.

Monodromic sheaves were defined by Jean-Louis Verdier (
[
Ver83
]
) in the algebraic context. Our context is similar, but one needs to (1) deal with the non-quasi-compactness of a general rigid analytic variety, and (2) check Zariski-constructibility is preserved in various operations. For (1), working with triangulated categories, we deal with it by a gluing lemma (Lemma
3.6
), which requires the underlying space to be quasi-separated. In the appendix, we give an
\infty
-categorical characterisation of monodromic sheaves, which removes the quasi-separatedness assumption but requires
p>0
.

8
The singular support

In this last section, we define and study the singular support. This is a closed conical subset of the cotangent bundle recording the directions in which the input sheaf is not locally constant. The classical reference is
[
KS90
, Chapter V]
, see
[
Bei16
]
for the algebraic setting. The set-up is as in §
1
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid analytic variety. For
\mathcal{F}\in D^{(b)}_{zc}(X)
, the
singular support

SS(\mathcal{F})
is defined as
\{(x,\xi)\subseteq(T^{*}X)(K)\,|\,\text{there exists a test function $f$ at $(x,\xi)$ such that}\,\,\phi_{f}(\mathcal{F})_{x}\neq 0\}^{cl}
. Here, “a test function
f
at
(x,\xi)
” means a function
f
on some open neighbourhood of
x
in
X
such that
f(x)=0
and
df(x)=\xi
, and “
cl
” means taking closure in
T^{*}X
. We will abbreviate the set in “
\{...\}
” by
\{\dddot{\mathcal{F}}\}
(so
SS(\mathcal{F})=\{\dddot{\mathcal{F}}\}^{cl}
).

Remark 8.2
.

(1) We emphasise that
\{\dddot{\mathcal{F}}\}
is a subset of classical points in
T^{*}X
, and the closure is taken in the analytic topology rather than the Zariski topology. It is thus a priori not clear if
SS(\mathcal{F})
is an analytic subset. See Question
9.4
.

(2) By Lemma
2.3
,
SS(\mathcal{F})
is a conical subset of
T^{*}X
.

(3) The same definition makes sense for all
\mathcal{F}\in D(X)
, but we have restricted to
\mathcal{F}\in D^{(b)}_{zc}(X)
because
\{\dddot{\mathcal{F}}\}
does not “see” points other than classical points, so the above definition is not the “correct” one for general
\mathcal{F}
. For example, let
\mathcal{F}=j_{!}\underline{\Lambda}_{D}
, where
j:D\hookrightarrow\mathbf{A}^{1}
is the open immersion. Then
SS(\mathcal{F})=D^{cl}
with the above definition. It is desirable to extend the notion of singular support to all sheaves without constructibility restrictions, as has been done in complex analytic as well as algebraic settings (c.f. the footnote to Remark
1.3
.2).

Here is an equivalent definition of the singular support (
c.f.

[
Bei16
,
\nopp
1.5]
).

Definition 8.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth rigid analytic variety,
C\subseteq T^{*}X
be a conical closed subset, and
f:X\rightarrow\mathbf{A}^{1}
a map. Denote by
\Gamma_{df}
the graph of the differential of
f
. We say
f
is
C
-transversal
if
((\Gamma_{df}-T^{*}_{X}X)\cap C)(K)=\varnothing
, i.e., for every classical point
(x,\xi)\in C
, we have
df(x)\neq\xi
.

Definition 8.4
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow\mathbf{A}^{1}
be a map of rigid analytic varieties, and
\mathcal{F}\in D^{(b)}_{zc}(X)
. We say

is
\phi
-locally acyclic
(
\phi
-LA) if for every

\displaystyle\simeq\varprojlim_{\Delta}\varinjlim_{m}R\Gamma(E\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m)\times_{K}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m),a_{i}^{*}g_{m}^{*}R\underline{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})).

Denote
a_{0}^{*}R\underline{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})
by
A
. Then
a_{i}^{*}g_{m}^{*}R\underline{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})\simeq p^{*}A
for
p:E\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m)\times_{K}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m)\rightarrow E
the projection. By smooth base change,
R\Gamma(E\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m)\times_{K}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m),p^{*}A)\simeq R\Gamma(\mathbf{G}_{m}(m)\times_{K}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m),\underline{R\Gamma(E,A)})
. One may then use Lemma
3.3
and induction to see that the last term is isomorphic to
R\Gamma(\mathbf{G}_{m}(m)\times_{K}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m),\Lambda)\otimes_{\Lambda}\underline{R\Gamma(E,A)}
. Consequently:

\displaystyle\varprojlim_{\Delta}\varinjlim_{m}R\Gamma(E\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m)\times_{K}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m),a_{i}^{*}g_{m}^{*}R\underline{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))

\displaystyle\simeq\varprojlim_{\Delta}\varinjlim_{m}(R\Gamma(\mathbf{G}_{m}(m)\times_{K}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{G}_{m}(m),\Lambda)\otimes_{\Lambda}\underline{R\Gamma(E,A)})

\displaystyle\simeq\varprojlim_{\Delta}\underline{R\Gamma(E,A)}\simeq R\Gamma(E,A).

As
R\Gamma(E,A)\simeq R\Gamma(E,g^{*}R\underline{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))\simeq R\Gamma(E,R\underline{Hom}(g^{*}\mathcal{F},g^{*}\mathcal{G}))=RHom(g^{*}\mathcal{F},g^{*}\mathcal{G})
, this finishes
Step 2
.

Step 3
. We show (2) and (3).

Denote the essential image of
\mathcal{D}^{(b)}_{zc}(E/\widetilde{\mathbf{G}_{m}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{(b)}_{zc}(E)
(resp.
\varinjlim_{n}\mathcal{D}^{b}_{zc}(E/\mathbf{G}_{m}(n))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{(b)}_{zc}(E)
) by
\mathcal{C}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
). It is clear that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{mon}(E)
. Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{D}_{mon}(E)
, to show
\mathcal{F}
lies in
\mathcal{C}
, it suffices to show this locally on
X
because
\mathcal{D}^{(b)}_{zc}(E_{U}/\widetilde{\mathbf{G}_{m}})
is a stack for
U
ranging over open subsets in
X
with the usual topology. In other words, we have reduced to showing (2) and (3) for
X
quasi-compact. Note
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}_{mon}(E)
are stable under truncations and taking cones: this is clear for
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
(using
RHom_{\varinjlim\mathcal{D}^{b}_{zc}(E/\mathbf{G}_{m}(n))}\simeq\varinjlim RHom_{\mathcal{D}^{b}_{zc}(E/\mathbf{G}_{m}(n))}
) and
\mathcal{C}
, for
\mathcal{D}_{mon}(E)
this follows from Proposition
3.2
. So, by induction on the amplitude, we may assume
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{Sh}_{mon}(E)
, thus reducing (2) and (3) to the following statement: for
X
quasi-compact, every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{Sh}_{mon}(E)
comes from
\mathrm{Sh}(E/\mathbf{G}_{m}(n))
for some
n
depending on
\mathcal{F}
.

It is well-known that to endow an object
\mathcal{G}\in\mathrm{Sh}(Y)
with a
G
-equivariant structure with respect to a connected algebraic group
G
acting on a space
Y
, it suffices to provide an isomorphism
a^{*}\mathcal{G}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\mathrm{pr}^{*}\mathcal{G}
, where
a
(resp.
\mathrm{pr}
) is the action (resp. projection) map
G\times Y\rightarrow Y
(see, for example,
[
Jan04
,
\nopp
12.6]
). In our situation, let
n
be such that
\theta(n)^{*}\mathcal{F}
is constant on
\mathbf{G}_{m}\times v
for every
v\in E
(notations as in Notation
3.1
), then the composition
\theta(n)^{*}\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{pr}^{!}\mathrm{pr}_{!}(\theta(n)^{*}\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathrm{pr}^{!}\mathrm{pr}_{!}(\theta(n)^{*}\mathcal{F}))
gives a desired isomorphism
\theta(n)^{*}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\mathrm{pr}^{*}\mathcal{F}
. This is the same argument as the second paragraph in the proof of Proposition
3.2
. We refer to that paragraph for details.
∎

We can now generalise the definitions of monodromic sheaves and their monodromy (Definitions
3.7
,
3.8
) to the case over a general rigid analytic variety without quasi-separatedness assumptions.

Definition 10.2
.

Let
E\rightarrow X
be a vector bundle over a rigid analytic variety. A sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{D}^{(b)}_{zc}(E)
is called
monodromic
if there exists an isomorphism
\theta_{\lambda}^{*}\mathcal{F}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\sim$}}\mathcal{F}
for all
\lambda\in K^{\times}
. The
\infty
-fullsubcategory in
\mathcal{D}^{(b)}(E)
of monodromic sheaves is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{mon}(E)
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{D}_{mon}(E)
is the essential image of
\mathcal{D}^{(b)}_{zc}(E/\widetilde{\mathbf{G}_{m}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{(b)}_{zc}(E)
. For
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{D}_{mon}(E)
, its
monodromy
is the
\mu
-action induced by
\mu\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf{G}_{m}}(K)
and the
\widetilde{\mathbf{G}_{m}}
-equivariance structure on
\mathcal{F}
.

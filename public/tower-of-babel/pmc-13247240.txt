The relationship between abdominal obesity indices and the risk of metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Shi-Long Li, Shu-Xia Guo, Ming-Han Zhang, Jie Guo, Zhi-Hao Hou, Hong-Wei Zhang, Shi-Jie Zhang, Jiang Li, Xin-Yu Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbdominal obesity is a crucial risk factor for the onset of metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD). Chinese visceral adiposity index (CVAI), body roundness index (BRI), lipid accumulation product (LAP), and waist-to-hip ratio (WHR) are established indices of abdominal obesity. This study investigates the relations between these abdominal obesity indices and MAFLD onset. A total of 10,703 rural residents were included in this cohort study. Kaplan–Meier graphs, Cox regression modeling, restricted cubic spline (RCS) scores, Subgroup analyses and sensitivity analysis were used to conduct the study. During the median follow-up period of 5.79 years, 596 participants (5.57%) developed MAFLD. As the quartiles of the abdominal obesity indices increased, the incidence of MAFLD exhibited an increasing pattern. CVAI had the strongest association with MAFLD risk both before and after adjusting for confounders(P < 0.05). The associations between the indices and MAFLD risk were largely consistent across most subgroups, and the findings remained robust in sensitivity analyses. The RCS curves displayed linear correlations of CVAI with MAFLD risk (p for non-linear = 0.146) . CVAI, LAP, BRI, and WHR are all significantly and positively correlated with the risk of MAFLD. Among the female population, CVAI exhibited the strongest correlation with MAFLD risk. Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) is a chronic liver disease highly relevant to metabolic dysfunction, involving a range of hepatic diseases, from simple steatosis (fat content in liver cells > 5%) to metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), as well as hepatic fibrosis and cirrhosis, and even hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)1. With the worldwide epidemic of obesity and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), MAFLD has become the most frequent chronic liver disease worldwide, affecting about 38% of the global population2–5, which is estimated to further increase in the next decade2,6. A modeling analysis on disease burden in eight countries predicts that the MAFLD population in China will increase by 29.1% from 2016 to 2030, reaching 314.58 million cases7. Numerous studies have corroborated that MAFLD can promote the initiation and progression of cardiovascular disease8, chronic kidney disease9, polycystic ovary syndrome10, and various malignant tumors11, seriously endangering human health and imposing a considerable socio-economic burden. Therefore, it is crucial to seek an early and non-invasive approach for diagnosing MAFLD.

Existing research indicates that abdominal obesity, characterized by visceral fat accumulation, is a critical risk factor for MAFLD, exerting a significant role in its initiation and progression12. At present, abdominal obesity can be evaluated through a variety of strategies, like ultrasound, CT/MRI, and anthropometric indices. Due to the time-consuming, high cost, and radiation issues associated with imaging examinations, anthropometric indices, which are simple, economical, and safe, are preferred for clinical and large-scale screening13. Over the recent years, multiple anthropometric indices have been established for evaluating abdominal obesity. Chinese visceral adiposity index (CVAI), body roundness index (BRI), waist-to-hip ratio (WHR), and lipid accumulation product (LAP) are employed for abdominal obesity evaluation by calculating baseline indices such as age, height, waist circumference (WC), BMI, triglycerides (TG), and high-density lipoprotein (HDL)14–17. In addition, the Fatty Liver Index (FLI)18 and the Hepatic Steatosis Index (HSI)19, as commonly used non‑invasive tools for assessing hepatic steatosis, also incorporate parameters closely related to abdominal obesity such as waist circumference and BMI in their calculation formulas, and thus may also be associated with MAFLD risk. Previous studies have explored the associations between various abdominal obesity indices and MAFLD. Motamed et al. conducted a cross-sectional study in 2016 among the general Iranian population (n = 4872) and found that BRI was more strongly associated with MAFLD risk than WHR20. A recent systematic review and meta-analysis (2025) further confirmed that BRI has good diagnostic accuracy for MAFLD (AUC = 0.803)21. Additionally, Lee et al. compared the associations of MAFLD defined by FLI versus ultrasonography with renal function decline, and found that FLI based on metabolic parameters (BMI, waist circumference, triglycerides, etc.) better reflected the metabolic disorder characteristics of MAFLD than imaging methods alone22. However, these studies were mostly cross-sectional in design or based on specific populations, and prospective cohort studies focusing on the rural Uyghur population in China remain scarce. The predictive value and clinical applicability of these indices in this population require further evaluation. This study aimed to investigate the relationships between CVAI, BRI, LAP, WHR and MAFLD risk in a rural Uyghur population in Xinjiang. Meanwhile, in the Cox regression models, the above indices were included together with FLI and HSI to systematically compare their association strength with MAFLD risk. The findings of this research may provide important references for early identification of high‑risk populations and primary prevention of MAFLD.

Materials and methods

Research population

This research is a prospective cohort study based on the Uyghur population. A multistage random sampling method was employed to construct the study cohort. To ensure homogeneity in the ethnic background of the study population, we specifically selected the 51st Regiment of the Third Division of the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps as the study unit, as it is the only regiment in the region with a predominantly Uyghur population. Subsequently, simple random sampling was used to select Companies 6, 8, 11, 12, and 13 from all companies under this regiment. Within these five selected companies, baseline surveys were conducted on all permanent Uyghur residents aged 18 years and older. The baseline investigation was conducted from August to September 2016, followed by four follow-up visits (2019, 2020, 2021, 2022). At the baseline investigation, 14,376 research participants were enrolled. Among them, 3618 participants were excluded, including migrant workers with unstable residence, pregnant women, participants with serious diseases, unclear consciousness, or unwillingness to cooperate with the investigation (n = 827), participants with missing ultrasound data (n = 239), participants lost to follow-up (n = 516, 3.59%), and participants with MAFLD (n = 2091). Eventually, 10,703 individuals were included (Fig. 1). All participants signed a written informed consent form. The study gained approval from the Ethics Review Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University (IERB no.: 2016–121–01).

Flow diagram of the research cohort, including inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Data acquisition

Baseline characteristics of the population, physical examination, questionnaire survey, and laboratory data were collected by highly-trained doctors, nurses, and investigators from the hospital’s physical examination center. The content of the baseline survey was consistent with that of the follow-up surveys. The baseline characteristics of the population contained gender, age, marital status, and education. Physical examination involved height, weight, WC, systolic (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP). The average value of three measurement was computed as the final BP value. The questionnaire survey included smoking, alcohol drinking, exercise status, and a history of T2DM. Smoking in this survey was defined as smoking ≥ 1 cigarette/day, lasting ≥ 6 months. Alcohol drinking in this survey was defined as drinking alcohol for at least 1 day per week for a duration of at least 6 months. T2DM was diagnosed in conformity with the 2012 American diabetes Association Standards23. Laboratory data encompassed serum total bilirubin (TBIL), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), TG, total cholesterol (TC), HDL cholesterol (HDL-C), LDL-C, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and γ-glutamyl transferase (GGT), all of which were analyzed with the assistance of an automated biochemical analyzer (Olympus Au 2700; Olympus Diagnostics, Hamburg, Germany). To ensure the accuracy and integrity of the results, hospital and medical insurance records were collected for each participant.

MAFLD diagnostic criteria

MAFLD26 was defined as the existence of one of three circumstances (see below) on the basis of hepatic steatosis: 1) overweight/obesity(BMI ≥ 23 kg/m2); 2) the existence of T2DM (FPG ≥ 7.1 mmol/L); 3) thin/normal body weight with metabolic disorders (BMI < 23 kg/m2). Metabolic disorders were defined as the manifestation of at least two metabolic abnormalities: (a) WC ≥ 90 cm in men and WC ≥ 80 cm in women; (b) blood pressure ≥ 130/85 mmHg; (c) TG ≥ 1.70 mmol/L; (d) male HDL-C < 1.0 mmol/L and female HDL-C < 1.3 mmol/L; (e) pre-diabetes (FPG of 5.6 to 6.9 mmol/L). Hepatic steatosis was ultrasonically identified by a clinical specialist.

Statistical analysis

SPSS26 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) were adopted for analysis. Figures were generated using Office software and R software (version 4.4.2), and p < 0.05 (two-sided) denoted statistical significance. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages), and continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation. A chi-square test was utilized for the comparisons of categorical variables, while the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables. The incidence density of MAFLD was expressed as per 1000 person-years. The Kaplan–Meier curve and log-rank test were utilized to estimate the cumulative incidence of MAFLD. The relations between abdominal obesity indices (CVAI, BRI, WHR, LAP) and MAFLD incidence were assessed using Cox regression models, including Model 1 (unadjusted), Model 2 (adjustment for gender and age), and Model 3 (adjustment for gender, age, smoking, alcohol drinking, LDL-C, GGT, ALT, TBIL). Subgroup analyses were adopted to evaluate differences in the association between abdominal obesity indices (CVAI, BRI, WHR, LAP) and MAFLD among gender-, age-, smoking-, alcohol drinking-, and ALT-based subgroups. We assessed multiplicative interactions by introducing product terms (e.g., CVAI × age group). To investigate the non-linear relationships between abdominal obesity indices (CVAI, BRI, WHR, LAP) and the risk of MAFLD, we employed a multivariate-adjusted restricted cubic spline (RCS) model using the “rms” package. A sensitivity analysis was performed to evaluate robustness and minimize potential reverse causality by excluding participants diagnosed with MAFLD within the initial two years of follow-up, after which the associations were reassessed using Cox proportional hazards models.

The incidence of MAFLD in the research population

During a median follow-up period of 5.79 years, 596 participants (5.57%) developed MAFLD, with an incidence density of 9.74/1000 person-years. CVAI, BRI, WHR, and LAP are grouped according to their quartiles. The participants were grouped based on the quartiles of CVAI, BRI, WHR, and LAP, and the cumulative incidences of MAFLD in the quartiles of indices was visualized in Fig. 2. With the increase of CVAI, BRI, and LAP quartiles (Q1 to Q4), the cumulative incidence of MAFLD gradually increased, showing a significant difference (p < 0.001). The cumulative incidence of BRI decreased slightly in Q2 (Q1: 4.78%, Q2: 4.46%), but it increased significantly from Q3, with the overall changes still on the rise (p < 0.001).

Subgroup analyses

In the subgroups regarding gender, age, smoking, alcohol drinking, and ALT, significant positive correlations existed between indices (CVAI, LAP, BRI, WHR) and increased risk of MAFLD. The interaction among gender, age, smoking, alcohol drinking, ALT, and multiple indices on MAFLD was depicted in Table 3. CVAI was strongly associated with the risk of MAFLD occurrence in different subgroups. Significant interactions were observed for CVAI with gender and with age. The female participants (HR = 10.57,95% CI: 6.23, 17.94) showed a stronger association strength between CVAI and MAFLD occurrence than the male participants (HR = 2.48, 95% CI: 1.49, 4.13). In the young population (< 40 years old), a significant positive association existed between CVAI and MAFLD occurrence (HR = 17.04, 95% CI: 9.83, 29.55). In the elderly population (≥ 40 years old), no significant association was noted between CVAI and MAFLD occurrence (HR = 0.72, 95% CI: 0.46, 1.14; p = 0.163). Statistically significant interaction was found between BRI and age (interaction p < 0.001), LAP and age (interaction p < 0.001), as well as WHR and age (interaction p = 0.008) in the risk of MAFLD.

Sensitivity analyses

To evaluate the robustness of our findings, we performed a sensitivity analysis. In this analysis, participants who developed new-onset MAFLD within the first 2 years of follow-up were excluded. The results demonstrated that, in the Cox proportional hazards models, abdominal obesity indices (CVAI, BRI, WHR, LAP) remained positively associated with the risk of MAFLD onset (Additional file 1: Table S1). This suggests that our main findings are robust.

Discussion

In this prospective cohort investigation, we examined the relationships between abdominal obesity indices (CVAI, LAP, BRI, WHR) and MAFLD risk in rural Uyghur populations in Xinjiang. We found that even after adjustment for potential confounding factors, CVAI, LAP, BRI, and WHR were significantly and positively associated with MAFLD risk. Among these indices, CVAI showed the strongest association with MAFLD risk, and notably, in the female population, the strength of the association between CVAI and MAFLD risk was significantly greater than that of HSI and FLI. Subgroup analyses uncovered the association between the aforementioned indices and MAFLD risk across different subgroups. Additionally, the RCS curve exhibited linear correlations of CVAI and BRI with MAFLD risk, as well as non-linear correlations of LAP and WHR and MAFLD risk. The research results suggested that CVAI might be the preferred parameter for future development of MAFLD risk prediction models.

The associations between several abdominal obesity indices and MAFLD have been described in the past literature. Cai et al.27 conducted a survey on the male population after physical examination at Fangchenggang First People’s Hospital in China and found that indices such as LAP and WHR were remarkably correlated with the prevalence of MAFLD in the male population, with LAP displaying a stronger correlation than WHR. Tang et al.28 reported a significant positive association between CVAI and MAFLD in T2DM patients (OR = 4.739, 95% CI: 2.778, 8.085).Cross-sectional studies from the adult population in the United States also support LAP as an effective predictor of MAFLD, with its elevation significantly associated with MAFLD risk and demonstrating high diagnostic accuracy for identifying MAFLD risk (AUC = 0.851) 29.Meanwhile, the study by Chen et al.30 further indicates that among the Han Chinese population in China, CVAI exhibits stronger association strength and diagnostic value for MAFLD (AUC = 0.868) compared to waist circumference (WC), visceral adiposity index (VAI), and LAP. However, the above studies were mostly cross-sectional designs, and our prospective cohort investigation further validated the relations between these abdominal obesity indices and MAFLD risk.

Abdominal obesity is primarily manifested by excessive visceral and subcutaneous fat accumulation, among which the accumulation of visceral fat is the core driving factor for the initiation and development of MAFLD12. The possible mechanisms involved limited storage capacity of subcutaneous fat, that is, after visceral fat overgrowth and hypertrophy owing to persistent energy excess, the lipid storage capacity is saturated, and free fatty acids “overflow” and enter the liver directly through the portal vein, causing hepatocyte steatosis31; meanwhile, dysfunctional visceral adipose tissue secretes a mass of pro-inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-6, etc.) and reduces protective adiponectin, resulting in chronic low-grade inflammation and insulin resistance, thereby activating the hepatic inflammatory signaling pathway (such as NF-κB)32; additionally, it also works synergistically with gut microbiota disorders that increases intestinal permeability to enable endotoxins and other substances to enter the liver, activating immune responses and jointly facilitating liver inflammation, oxidative stress, and fibrosis processes27.Furthermore, racial differences exert an important role in fat distribution and metabolic risk. Studies have illustrated that under the same BMI, the Asian population has a higher visceral fat content than the Caucasian population, while the degree of visceral obesity is relatively low in the African population32,33. This highlights the need to optimize abdominal obesity assessment indices for specific populations. Our research findings indicated that among all abdominal obesity indices, the relation between CVAI and MAFLD risk was the most significant. One potential explanation is that CVAI is specifically designed for the Chinese population, and its calculation formula also incorporates multiple parameters (such as age, BMI, WC, TG, HDL-C) and logarithmically converts TG values, effectively weakening the impact of extreme values, with coefficients gender-calibrated25,34. In contrast, WHR only reflects central obesity using a ratio of waist circumference to hip circumference and cannot distinguish between subcutaneous fat and the metabolically more detrimental visceral fat34. Secondly, LAP is an index established and validated based on the white population, initially applicable to identify cardiovascular risk in American adults. Its calculation only involves the linear product of WC and TG, lacking key metabolic information such as HDL-C35. BRI, on the other hand, is an index derived from WC and BMI to describe human body shape, but its lack of lipid parameters also makes it difficult to distinguish fat types14.

In subgroup analyses, we systematically explored the relation between CVAI and MAFLD onset under different clinical characteristics and illustrated a statistically significant positive association in multiple subgroups. Of note, significant differences existed in the correlation strength between CVAI and MAFLD among populations of different genders and ages (p values for interaction < 0.05), suggesting that these factors may modulate the association strength between CVAI and MAFLD. Of note, the relation between CVAI and MAFLD was more evident in females than in males (HR = 10.57 vs. 2.48). This is consistent with the findings of Chen et al.30, whose research indicates that compared to WC, VAI, and LAP, CVAI demonstrates superior predictive performance for MAFLD in females. To further delineate the relative strength of CVAI in females, we compared it with two other widely used non-invasive indices for MAFLD assessment: the Fatty Liver Index (FLI)18 and the Hepatic Steatosis Index (HSI)19. Notably, HSI, similar to CVAI, uses a sex-specific calculation formula. Gender-stratified Cox regression analyses (Supplementary Table S3) revealed that among females, the association between CVAI and MAFLD risk (HR = 10.57) was not only stronger than that of the other abdominal obesity indices examined (LAP, BRI, WHR) but also surpassed that of both FLI and HSI. This finding further underscores CVAI as a composite index with a particularly strong association with MAFLD risk in women. We postulate that this may be attributable to CVAI’s integration of age, lipid profile (TG, HDL-C), and anthropometric parameters. Its algorithm, derived from a Chinese population, might more sensitively capture the specific metabolic disturbances concomitant with visceral fat accumulation in women. Moreover, a significant positive relation was detected regarding CVAI and MAFLD risk in the younger population (< 40 years old) (HR = 17.04, 95% CI: 9.83, 29.55), while no significant association was observed in the older population (≥ 40 years old) (HR = 0.72, 95% CI: 0.46, 1.14, p = 0.163). Based on this, the predictive value of CVAI for MAFLD may be more prominent in young populations, possibly because younger individuals may have more reactive metabolic pathways and greater susceptibility to lipotoxic injury36–38. The insignificant association between CVAI and MAFLD in the elderly population (≥ 40 years old) may be attributed to several factors, including the influence of other confounding factors or relatively insufficient samples. More importantly, metabolic complications are more prevalent in this age group. Conditions such as diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and chronic kidney disease may share pathophysiological mechanisms with MAFLD, and their clinical phenotypes could mask or confound the independent association between CVAI and MAFLD. This complexity requires further research for verification. In summary, our subgroup analyses not only confirmed the universal association between CVAI and MAFLD risk in different populations but also revealed significant differences in the strength of the association among specific factors such as gender and age, providing key evidence for the formulation of precise MAFLD risk stratification and primary prevention strategies in the future.

Advantages and limitations

This research is a large-scale prospective cohort investigation, providing important epidemiological evidence supporting the relations between abdominal obesity indices and MAFLD in the Chinese population. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the diagnosis of MAFLD was based on hepatic steatosis detected by abdominal ultrasound, which has limited sensitivity for mild steatosis. For example, research by Dasarathy et al.39 indicated that the diagnostic sensitivity of ultrasound for hepatic steatosis with a fat content < 20% is approximately 60.9%, whereas it reaches 100% when the fat content is ≥ 20%. Therefore, some individuals with existing metabolic abnormalities might not be diagnosed with MAFLD due to mild steatosis undetected by ultrasound, potentially leading to an underestimation of cases and a possible attenuation of the observed association between abdominal obesity indicators and MAFLD risk. Second, this study is based on a rural Uyghur population in Xinjiang, and its findings are primarily applicable to populations with similar genetic backgrounds and lifestyles. Extrapolation of the conclusions to broader populations should be cautious, given the differences in body fat distribution, metabolic risk, and susceptibility to MAFLD across ethnic groups. Although the Chinese Visceral Adiposity Index (CVAI), developed based on Chinese populations, may remain applicable to other Asian ethnic groups such as the Han Chinese, its performance in non-Asian populations requires further validation. Additionally, this study did not account for the temporal trajectories of abdominal obesity indicators or their dynamic effects on MAFLD risk. Future research should incorporate these aspects to provide a more comprehensive assessment of the impact of abdominal obesity indicators on MAFLD incidence.

Conclusion

This study reveals that in the rural Uyghur population in Xinjiang, CVAI, LAP, BRI, and WHR are all significantly positively associated with the risk of MAFLD. Among them, the strength of association between CVAI and MAFLD is the strongest in the overall population and in females, whereas it is slightly lower than that of BRI in males. This indicates that CVAI is expected to serve as the preferred index for developing MAFLD risk prediction models for females in the future. Its predictive performance will be further validated through ROC analysis to promote its application in primary prevention.

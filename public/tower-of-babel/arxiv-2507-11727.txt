Implicit representations of codimension-2 submanifolds and their prequantum structure
Albert Chern, Sadashige Ishida

Abstract
This paper explores the geometry of the space of codimension-2 submanifolds. We implicitly represent these submanifolds by a class of complex-valued functions. This reveals a prequantum bundle structure over the space of submanifolds, equipped with the well-known Marsden–Weinstein symplectic structure. This bundle allows a new physical interpretation of the Marsden–Weinstein structure as the curvature of a connection form, which measures the average of volumes swept by the deformation of the 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -family of hypersurfaces, defined as the phases of a complex function implicitly representing a submanifold. Keywords . Symplectic structure, space of submanifolds, prequantum bundle 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . 53D05, 58D10, 58A25, 58B25

Introduction

We explore the geometry of the space of codimension-2 submanifolds, with a particular focus on their implicit representations and a prequantumm structure on the space of these implicit representations.

Codimension-1 submanifolds—such as curves on a surface and surfaces in a three dimensional space—can be implicitly represented using a level set function. This representation naturally extends to multi-codimensional submanifolds using multiple level set functions.
Specifically, when the codimension is 2, submanifolds—such as curves in a three dimensional space and surfaces in a four dimensional space—can be expressed as the zero sets of complex-valued functions. Such implicit representations via complex functions are useful for studying the geometry and dynamics of submanifolds.
For example, the authors of this article recently leveraged the infinite redundancy in the implicit representation of space curves to numerically simulate curve dynamics arising in fluid dynamics and differential geometry
[
IWC22
]
. Implicit representations using complex-valued functions also appear in earlier and recent literature, where they describe the dynamics of space curves, such as curve-shortening flow
[
RMXO01
]
and quantum vortex filaments governed by the Gross-Pitaevskii equation
[
OTH02
,
VKPS16
,
JS18
]
.

The codimension-2 case is also significant in the study of Hamiltonian dynamical systems of shapes. The space of codimension-2 unparametrized embeddings (or immersions) into an ambient manifold equipped with a volume form is known to be an infinite-dimensional symplectic manifold (or an orbifold, for immersions), endowed with the so-called Marsden–Weinstein (MW) symplectic structure
[
MW83
,
Khe12
]
. This structure is considered
canonical
as it formally corresponds to the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau form when submanifolds are viewed as linear functionals on the Lie algebra of divergence-free vector fields on the ambient manifold
[
MW83
, Theorem 4.2]

[
AK98
, Chapter VI, Proposition 3.6]
.
The MW structure plays a crucial role in the study of dynamical systems, as it gives rise to many physically motivated equations, including those governing fluid dynamics and elastica
[
HV03
,
Khe12
,
CKPP20
]
.

In this article, we investigate the space of codimension-2 submanifolds implicitly represented by the zero sets of complex functions and study its geometric structures related to the MW structure.
Let

\mathcal{O}

.

This bundle has a natural geometric interpretation.
Each complex function

Any motion of this configuration induces the average volume swept out by these phase hypersurfaces.
In each fiber, we define two hypersurface configurations as equivalent (

The space of equivalence classes is denoted by

(

1.1
Related work

We briefly comment the previous work on Liouville forms and prequantum bundles over the codimension-2 shape space
\mathcal{O}
equipped with the MW form, which are particularly relevant to this article.

Liouville forms for the explicit and the implicit settings

Recent works
[
Tab17
,
PCK+19
]
point out the existence of a Liouville form for the MW form (
i.e.
, a 1-form
\eta
such that
\omega=d\eta
) on the space of space curves. As an additional contribution to our main result, we extend this finding to the space of arbitrary oriented and closed codimension-2 submanifolds in an ambient manifold equipped with an exact volume form.
The proof for the case of space curves presented in
[
Tab17
]
relies on an explicit parameterization of the curve, and therefore does not readily generalize to higher dimensional settings. We provide a parametrization-free proof using geometric measure theory.

Furthermore, we observe that the Liouville form introduced by
[
Tab17
]
for the MW form and our generalized ones on the explicit shape space are invariant only under a finite-dimensional subgroup of the volume-preserving diffeomorphisms of the ambient space. In contrast, the connection form
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
we define, which serves as a Liouville form via the fibration
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}
(
i.e.
,
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\omega=d\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
), remains invariant under the full group of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms of the ambient space (
Section 7.1.2
).

Prequantum bundles over the codimension-2 shape spaces

There are other prequantum bundles over
(\mathcal{O},\omega)
. In
[
HV03
]
, Haller and Vizman constructed a prequentum circle bundle by covering
\mathcal{O}
with a collection of open sets
\{U_{i}\}_{i}
and gluing the sets
\{\mathbb{S}^{1}\times U_{i}\}_{i}
using carefully chosen transition functions that ensure a prequantum bundle structure. Another approach, utilizing differential characters, was introduced in
[
DJNV20
]
.
Presently we are not aware of any explicit relationships between their proposed prequantum bundles and ours.

However, we emphasize that, unlike these previously established prequantum bundle structures, our framework is inherently local. Specifically, we focus on submanifolds that are exact in homology, meaning they must bound codimension-1 domains. Consequently, the existence of our prequantum bundle is restricted to such exact submanifolds as we explain in
Section 4.1
.

Future work

The codimension-2 shape space admits not only the MW structure but also other symplectic structures, as recently shown in
[
BIM24
]
. In this article, we focus specifically on the MW structure. Extending our framework to a wider class of symplectic structures is an interesting direction for future work.

Secondly, we restrict our attention to the space of embeddings modulo reparametrization, meaning that self-intersecting shapes are outside the scope of this study, even though the MW form is defined on the space of immersions. On the other hand, the tools we employ from geometric measure theory, such as currents, are capable of handling such singularities. In the future, we aim to extend our framework to accommodate topological changes and reconnection events in shapes.

1.2
Organization of the paper

In
Section 2
, we review currents from geometric measure theory and introduce a diffeomorphism action on currents. This allows us to treat shapes in a parameterization-free manner in both explicit and implicit settings, and to rigorously handle the superposition of infinitely many hypersurfaces given as the phase level sets of implicit representations.

In
Section 3
, we review the MW symplectic structure on the explicit shape space
\mathcal{O}
of codimension-2 submanifolds. We do so in terms of diffeomorphism actions on currents, highlighting that the deformation of a shape can be seen as the transport of a current along a vector field.
Along the way, we extend the previous result on the existence of a Liouville form, which was known only for space curves (i.e., embeddings of a circle into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
), to a more general setting of embeddings of an oriented, closed manifold
S
into a manifold
M
equipped with an exact volume form.

In
Section 4
, we define implicit representations of codimension-2 submanifolds. We then study the geometry of the space of these implicit representations
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
as a fiber bundle over
\mathcal{O}
.

In
Section 5
, we define a 1-form
\Theta
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
and show that it defines a Liouville form for the MW form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{O})
in the prequantum sense, i.e.,
\Pi^{*}\omega=d\Theta
via the fibration
\Pi\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathcal{O}
. Our proof, using geometric measure theory, offers a physical interpretation of
\Theta
in terms of the swept volumes of phase hypersurfaces.

In
Section 6
, we define the volume class
\mathcal{P}
as explained above. We show that
\Theta
factored onto
\mathcal{P}
becomes a connection form
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
forming a prequantum
G
-bundle
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon(\mathcal{P},\Theta_{\mathcal{P}})\to(\mathcal{O},\omega)
with the structure group
G=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

In
Section 7
, we study additional structures that implicit representations and prequantum structures reveal, such as horizontal Hamiltonian vector fields on
\mathcal{P}
with respect to the connection
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
, and a degenerate formal Kähler structure on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Preliminary: Diffeomorphism action on currents

We briefly review the notion of currents from geometric measure theory as a preliminary and define a diffeomorphism action on them. For readers interested in the general theory of currents, we refer to the literature
[
Fed14
,
dR84
,
Mor08
]
.
Currents are a natural language for our implicit description of shapes embedded in an ambient manifold as they allow us to treat differential forms, submanifolds, and their generalizations in a unified manner.
Using currents, we will describe relations between quantities defined on an ambient manifold and a submanifold (possibly with a boundary). This includes, for example, the flux of a vector field through a surface, and a superposition of the fluxes through infinitely many surfaces.

For details, see
[

]

We note that

\displaystyle\langle h,\alpha\rangle\coloneqq\int_{M}h\wedge\alpha,\quad h\in\Omega^{m-k}(M),\alpha\in\Omega_{c}^{k}(M).

We call a special class of currents de Rham-Dirac currents (or simply de Rham currents). These currents extend the Dirac measures for points (codimension-

For a

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{\Sigma},\alpha\rangle=\int_{\Sigma}\alpha,\quad\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(M).

We denote the collection of de Rham-

The boundary operators

\displaystyle\langle\partial\delta_{\Sigma},\alpha\rangle=\int_{\Sigma}d\alpha=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\alpha=\langle\delta_{\partial\Sigma},\alpha\rangle,\quad\forall\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(M).

2.2
Orbits under diffeomorphism groups

In this article, we represent unparametrized shapes by currents and describe the deformation of shapes in terms of diffeomorphism actions.
To do so, we define the action of diffeomorphism groups on currents and the Lie derivative as its infinitesimal generator.

Let us denote by
\Diff(M)

\displaystyle\langle f_{*}h,\alpha\rangle\coloneqq\langle h,f^{*}\alpha\rangle,\quad h\in\mathcal{D}_{k}(M),\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(M).

.
Note also that

On an orbit

Preliminary: Symplectic structure on the codimension-2 shape space

This section provides a preliminary review of the space of
explicit representations
for codimension-2 shapes, namely, embeddings of an
n
-dimensional manifold
S
into an
(n+2)
-dimensional ambient manifold
M
. We revisit earlier work on the canonical symplectic structure on this space, the Marsden-–Weinstein (MW) structure, as studied in
[
MW83
,
HV03
,
Tab17
,
PCK+19
]
. Our presentation is framed in terms of currents, which will play a central role in the next section when we introduce implicit representations and relate them to the explicit framework.

In addition, we obtain new results (
Theorem 3.5
,
Theorem 3.8
): the MW structure on the codimension-2 shape space is exact if the volume form
\mu
of the ambient manifold is exact. This extends earlier results for closed curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
Tab17
,
PCK+19
]
to a broader class of submanifolds in arbitrary dimensions.

We conclude this section by considering the shape space of all codimension-2 submanifolds. That is, we allow the submanifold
S
to range over all oriented and closed
n
-manifolds, rather than fixing one
S
in advance. This broader viewpoint naturally leads to the introduction of implicit representations in the next section.

3.1
Spaces of parametrized and unparametrized codimension-2 embeddings

We begin with the case where the dimension of the ambient manifold is greater than 2, postponing remarks on the 2-dimensional case to the end of this section. Let

be an

be a closed (i.e., compact and without boundary), oriented manifold of dimension

Consider the space of smooth embeddings

]

{\tilde{\gamma}}\in\operatorname{Emb}(S,M)
is given by the pullback bundle:

\displaystyle T_{\tilde{\gamma}}\operatorname{Emb}(S,M)={\tilde{\gamma}}^{*}TM.

(3.2)

That is, a tangent vector
\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}\in T_{\tilde{\gamma}}\operatorname{Emb}(S,M)
assigns to each point
s\in S
a vector
\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(s)\in T_{{\tilde{\gamma}}(s)}M
. For example, on the space of space curves, that is, when
S=\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
M=\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the tangent space
T_{\tilde{\gamma}}\operatorname{Emb}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})
is identified with
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

On
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)
we define a right action of the orientation-preserving diffeomorphism group
\Diffp(S)
representing reparametrizations. Taking the quotient by this action, we obtain the
shape space of unparametrized shapes
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)\coloneqq\operatorname{Emb}(S,M)/\Diffp(S).

(3.3)

The space
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
is also referred to as the
nonlinear Grassmannian of type
S

[
HV03
]
.

Note that
\Diffp(S)
may have multiple connected components. For example, if
S=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\mathbb{S}^{2}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\mathbb{T}^{2}
, then
\Diffp(S)
is isomorphic to the product of
\Diff^{+}_{0}(S)
, the component containing
\operatorname{id}_{S}
, and the symmetric groups
\operatorname{Sym}(k_{1})
and
\operatorname{Sym}(k_{2})
. The operation of permuting identical copies of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
or
\mathbb{T}^{2}
is included in
\Diffp(S)
.

The shape space
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
is an infinite-dimensional manifold
[
BBM14
]
. In what follows, we denote an element of
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
by
\gamma
and any representative of
\gamma
by
\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)
.

With the fibration
\pi\colon\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)\to\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)

the tangent space is written as

\displaystyle T_{\pi({\tilde{\gamma}})}\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)=d\pi|_{\tilde{\gamma}}(T_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)).

(3.4)

Note that
\operatorname{ker}d\pi|_{\tilde{\gamma}}=d{\tilde{\gamma}}(\diff(S))
where
\diff(S)
is the space of smooth vector fields on
S
, which is the Lie algebra of
\Diffp(S)
. Hence
\operatorname{ker}d\pi
consists of the components of tangent vectors which do not change
the shape
of
\gamma
.

Example 3.1

(Tangent space at a unparametrized space curve)
.

When
S=\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
M=\mathbb{R}^{3}
equipped with the standard Euclidean metric, we have
\operatorname{ker}d\pi|_{\tilde{\gamma}}=\{a\partial_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}}\mid a\in C^{\infty}(S)\}
. Hence

T_{\gamma}\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)=\{\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}+d{\tilde{\gamma}}\diff(S)\mid\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}\in T_{\tilde{\gamma}}\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)\}
is identified with
\{\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}\colon S\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(s)\perp\partial_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}}(s),\forall s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\}
, vector fields on
{\tilde{\gamma}}(S)
that are everywhere perpendicular to the tangent vector
\partial_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}}
.

We now define a left action of
\Diff(M)
on
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)

by

\displaystyle f\rhd\gamma=\pi(f\circ\tilde{\gamma}),\quad f\in\Diff_{0}(M),\gamma\in\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)

(3.5)

where
\pi
is the projection
\pi\colon\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)\to\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
and
{\tilde{\gamma}}\in\pi^{-1}\gamma
is any parametrization of
\gamma
.

Each connected component of
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
is an orbit of the action of
\Diff_{0}(M)
, the connected component of
\Diff(M)
containing
\operatorname{id}_{M}
. This is a classical result due to Thom (see, e.g.,
[
Hir12
]
). Consequently, any tangent vector
\dot{\gamma}\in T_{\gamma}\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
can be written as

\dot{\gamma}=v\circ\gamma\coloneqq d\pi|_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}(v\circ{\tilde{\gamma}})

for some
v\in\diff(M)
. With this in mind, we will restrict our attention to a single
\Diff_{0}(M)
-orbit in
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
, denoted by
\mathcal{O}
.

In fact, the action by the subgroup
\SDiff_{0}(M)\subset\Diff_{0}(M)
of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms is transitive on each
\Diff_{0}(M)
-orbit in
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)

[
HV03
, Proposition 2]
. Much of the theory in this section remains the same after restricting the diffeomorphism group
\Diff_{0}(M)
to
\SDiff_{0}(M)
and the space
\diff(M)
of vector fields to the space
\sdiff(M)
of divergence-free vector fields.

Remark 3.2

(Labeled and unlabeled shapes)
.

There are two standard definitions of unparametrized shapes
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
. One defines
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
as the quotient of
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)
by the orientation-preserving diffeomorphism group
\Diffp(S)
, and the other uses
\Diff_{0}(S)
, the connected component in
\Diff(S)
containing the identity map
[
BBM14
]
.

The quotient by
\Diff_{0}(S)
retains labels on identical components of the same shape. For example, if
S=\mathbb{S}^{1}\sqcup\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then two embeddings
\gamma_{a}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\gamma_{b}=(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{1})
, with non-intersecting embeddings
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, represent distinct elements in
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)/\Diff_{0}(S)
but the same element in
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)/\Diffp(S)
.

In this article, we consider
unlabeled shapes
, defined as
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)/\Diffp(S)
. This convention better aligns with the implicit representations of shapes introduced in
Section 4
(See also
Section 4.1
).
However, we note that the theorems and propositions in this section hold under both definitions.

3.2
Shapes as de Rham currents

Since the shape space
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
is a subset of the space of
(m-2)
-dimensional submanifolds in
M
, there is a natural injection into the space of de Rham currents:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\colon\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{D}^{dR}_{m-2}(M)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\mapsto\delta_{\gamma}.

(3.7)

We denote the image of
\mathcal{I}
by
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)}
, and for a
\Diff_{0}(M)
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
, we write
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{O})
.

The tangent space at
\delta_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
is given by the pushforward of the tangent space at
\gamma
:

\displaystyle T_{\delta_{\gamma}}\mathcal{O}=d\mathcal{I}|_{\gamma}\left(T_{\gamma}\mathcal{O}\right).

(3.8)

More explicitly,

\displaystyle d\mathcal{I}|_{\gamma}\colon{\dot{\gamma}}=v\circ\gamma\mapsto-\mathcal{L}_{v}\delta_{\gamma},\quad\text{for some }v\in\diff(M),

(3.9)

where
\mathcal{L}_{v}
denotes the Lie derivative in the sense of currents, as defined in
Section 2.2
. Therefore, the tangent space at
\delta_{\gamma}
is

\displaystyle T_{\delta_{\gamma}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}=\{-\mathcal{L}_{v}\delta_{\gamma}\mid v\in\diff(M)\}.

Note also that the injection
\mathcal{I}
commutes with the
\Diff_{0}(M)
-action in the sense that
f_{*}\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}\circ f
for all
f\in\Diff_{0}(M)
, as discussed in
Section 2.2
.
In the remainder of the paper, we will occasionally identify
\mathcal{O}
with
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
, and
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
with
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)}
, and their tangent bundles, without explicitly stating so.

3.3
Symplectic structure

The space
\mathcal{O}\cong\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
is a
weak symplectic manifold
. That is, it is equipped with a closed and
weakly non-degenerate
2-form. The Marsden–Weinstein (MW) form on
\mathcal{O}
is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{\delta_{\gamma}}(\dot{\delta}_{\gamma},\ring{\delta}_{\gamma})=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mu\rangle=\int_{\gamma}\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mu,

(3.10)

where
\dot{\delta}_{\gamma}=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\delta_{\gamma}
and
\ring{\delta}_{\gamma}=-\mathcal{L}_{v}\delta_{\gamma}
for vector fields
u,v\in\Gamma(TM)
. When convenient, we also write
\omega_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\ring{\gamma})\coloneqq\omega_{\delta_{\gamma}}(\dot{\delta}_{\gamma},\ring{\delta}_{\gamma})
for
\dot{\gamma}=u\circ\gamma
and
\ring{\gamma}=v\circ\gamma
.

The MW form
\omega
is closed and weakly-nondegnerate in the sense that the associated flat operator

\displaystyle\flat^{\omega}\colon T\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle\to T^{*}\mathcal{O}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}

\displaystyle\mapsto\iota_{\dot{\gamma}}\omega

(3.12)

is injective. Throughout this article, we refer to such weak symplectic forms simply as
symplectic
. For background on weak symplectic geometry, we refer the reader to
[
Mic84
, Chapter VI]
and
[
BIM24
, Appendix A]
.

On the parametrized shape space
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
, where
\pi\colon\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)\to\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
is the natural projection, the pullback 2-form
\tilde{\omega}\coloneqq\pi^{*}\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{O}})
is written as

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{\tilde{\gamma}}(\dot{\tilde{\gamma}},\ring{\tilde{\gamma}})=\int_{S}\mu(\dot{\tilde{\gamma}},\ring{\tilde{\gamma}},\partial_{s_{1}}\tilde{\gamma},\ldots,\partial_{s_{n}}\tilde{\gamma})\,ds_{1}\ldots ds_{n}

(3.13)

in local coordinates
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
on
S
. From this expression, we see that
\tilde{\omega}
has a nontrivial kernel consisting of infinitesimal reparametrizations, that is,
\operatorname{ker}\tilde{\omega}|_{\tilde{\gamma}}=\operatorname{ker}d\pi|_{\tilde{\gamma}}=d{\tilde{\gamma}}(\diff(S))
at each
{\tilde{\gamma}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
.
Thus,
\tilde{\omega}
is not symplectic, but merely presymplectic
i.e.
, a closed 2-form.

Example 3.3

(MW structure on the space of space curves)
.

On the space of space curves
\mathrm{UEmb}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})
where
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is equipped with the standard Euclidean volume form
\mu=dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{3}
, the MW form takes the expression

\displaystyle\omega_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\ring{\gamma})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\operatorname{det}(\partial_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}},\dot{\tilde{\gamma}},\ring{\tilde{\gamma}})\,ds,

(3.14)

where
{\tilde{\gamma}}\in\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)
is any parametrization of
\gamma\in\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
and the integrand denotes the determinant of the
3\times 3
matrix whose columns are the vectors
\partial_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}}(s)
,
\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(s)
, and
\ring{\tilde{\gamma}}(s)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Note that
\operatorname{ker}\tilde{\omega}|_{\tilde{\gamma}}
consists of infinitesimal reparametrizations of the form
a\partial_{s}\tilde{\gamma}
with
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, corresponding to tangent vector fields along the curve
\gamma
viewed as a submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

3.3.1
Hamiltonian vector fields

For a function
\mathcal{H}\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
with
d\mathcal{H}\in\operatorname{im}\flat^{\omega}
, the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{\mathcal{H}}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{O})
with respect to the MW form
\omega
is the unique vector field satisfying

\displaystyle d\mathcal{H}=\iota_{X_{\mathcal{H}}}\omega

(3.15)

and the Hamiltonian system is the flow along
X_{\mathcal{H}}
. That is,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\gamma=X_{\mathcal{H}}(\gamma).

(3.16)

Example 3.4
.

On
\mathrm{UEmb}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})
where
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is equipped with the standard Euclidean metric, the length function as the Hamiltonian yields the binormal equation,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\gamma=D_{s}\gamma\times D_{s}^{2}\gamma.

(3.17)

Here the right-hand side is understood as
d\pi|_{\tilde{\gamma}}(D_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}}\times D_{s}^{2}{\tilde{\gamma}})
with fibration
\pi\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathcal{O}
and any parametrization
{\tilde{\gamma}}\in\pi^{-1}(\gamma)
, where
D_{s}=\partial_{s}/|\partial_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}}|
is the derivative with respect to the arc-length parameter.

More generally, on
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,\mathbb{R}^{m})
with an oriented closed
n
-manifold
S
, choosing the
n
-dimensional volume of
\gamma
as the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
yields the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{\mathcal{H}}
as the mean curvature normal on the submanifold
\gamma(S)
, rotated by 90 degrees in the normal bundle
N\gamma(S)

[
HV03
,
Khe12
]
. While the binormal equation (
3.17
) is known to be an infinite-dimensional integrable system forming a KdV-type hierarchy
[
CKPP20
]
, the integrability of its higher-dimensional analogues remains less understood.

3.4
Liouville form

On a symplectic manifold, a
Liouville form
is a 1-form whose exterior derivative equals to the symplectic form.
Previous work
[
PCK+19
,
Tab17
]
showed that the MW structure
\omega
on the space of space curves
\mathrm{UEmb}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})
is exact by providing an explicit Liouville form. The 1-form
\eta
defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma})=\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\operatorname{det}({\tilde{\gamma}},\dot{\tilde{\gamma}},\partial_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}})\,ds

(3.18)

where
{\tilde{\gamma}}
is any parametrization of
\gamma
, satisfies
\omega=d\eta
.

A natural question is to consider its higher-dimensional generalization. However, the proof in
[
Tab17
]
relies on integration by parts using an explicit parametrization of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, which does not directly extend to higher dimensions.

Here, we present a new result for a generic closed and oriented
n
-dimensional manifold
S
and
M=\mathbb{R}^{m}
equipped with the Euclidean volume form
\mu=dx_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{m}
(
Theorem 3.5
), and then extend it to a slightly more general setting (
Theorem 3.8
).

For
\dot{\delta_{\gamma}}=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\delta_{\gamma}\in T_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
, define

\displaystyle\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}(\dot{\delta_{\gamma}})=\frac{1}{m}\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle=\frac{1}{m}\int_{\gamma}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu.

(3.19)

This 1-form (
3.19
) admits an explicit representation analogous to (
3.18
):

\displaystyle\eta_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma})=\frac{1}{m}\int_{S}\operatorname{det}({\tilde{\gamma}},\dot{\tilde{\gamma}},\partial_{s_{1}}{\tilde{\gamma}},\ldots,\partial_{s_{m-2}}{\tilde{\gamma}})\,ds_{1}\ldots ds_{m-2}

(3.20)

where
\dot{\gamma}=u\circ\gamma
and
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
is local coordinates.

Theorem 3.5

(Liouville form on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
)
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is equipped with the standard volume form
\mu=dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{m}
. Then we have
d\eta=\omega
.

A special case of
Theorem 3.5
for
\mathrm{Emb}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})
is given in
[
Tab17
, Proposition 2.1]
, where the proof is based on integration by parts using an explicit parametrization of a space curve.

However, this approach does not extend to the general setting
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)
unless
S
admits an explicit global parametrization, such as
S=\mathbb{T}^{n}
. In contrast, we prove the general case of
Theorem 3.5
in a parametrization-free fashion.

We formulate the proof using currents, as this computational routine will be reused throughout the article.
To that end, we introduce an auxiliary result. First, note that each vector field
u\in\Gamma(TM)
induces a vector field
X^{u}
on the
\Diff_{0}(M)
-orbit
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
in
\mathcal{D}_{m-2}(M)
, defined by

\displaystyle X^{u}({\delta_{\gamma}})=-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}}.

(3.21)

In other words,
X^{u}
is the fundamental vector field associated with the
\Diff_{0}(M)
-action on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
, since
-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}}=\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}{\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{t}}_{*}{\delta_{\gamma}}
, where
\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{t}\in\Diff_{0}(M)
is the time-
t
flow of
u
on
M
(see
Section 2.2
).
This defines the following homomorphism;

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
be a
\Diff_{0}(M)
-orbit in the space of
k
-currents
\mathcal{D}_{k}(M)
. Then the map

\displaystyle\Gamma(TM)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\Gamma(T\hat{\mathcal{D}})

(3.22)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle X^{u}

is a Lie algebra homomorphism. That is, for
u,v\in\Gamma(TM)
, we have

\displaystyle[X^{u},X^{v}]=X^{[u,v]}.

(3.23)

Proof.

To compute
[X^{u},X^{v}]=\mathcal{L}_{X^{u}}X^{v}
, we use the formula for the Lie derivative:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X^{u}}X^{v}(h)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}D\Phi^{-t}_{X^{u}}\left[X^{v}(\Phi^{t}_{X^{u}}(h))\right],\quad h\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}.

(3.24)

Here
\Phi^{t}_{X}\colon\hat{\mathcal{D}}\to\hat{\mathcal{D}}
denotes the flow map on
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
along a given vector field
X\in\Gamma(T\hat{\mathcal{D}})
, defined by the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\Phi^{t}_{X}(h))=X(\Phi^{t}_{X}(h)),

(3.25)

\displaystyle\Phi^{0}_{X}=\operatorname{id}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}

(3.26)

and
D\Phi^{t}_{X}|_{h}\colon T_{h}\hat{\mathcal{D}}\to T_{\Phi^{t}_{X}(h)}\hat{\mathcal{D}}
is the differential of
\Phi^{t}_{X}
at
h
.

For the fundamental vector field
X^{u}
, the flow is given by
\Phi^{t}_{X^{u}}(h)={\operatorname{Fl}^{t}_{u}}_{*}h
by definition. Using this, we have

\displaystyle X^{v}(\Phi^{t}_{X^{u}}(h))

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}{\operatorname{Fl}_{v}^{s}}_{*}(\Phi^{t}_{X^{u}}(h))=\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}{\operatorname{Fl}_{v}^{s}}_{*}{\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{t}}_{*}h,

(3.27)

and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}D\Phi^{-t}_{X^{u}}\left[X^{v}(\Phi^{t}_{X^{u}}(h))\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi^{-t}_{X^{u}}\left({\operatorname{Fl}_{v}^{s}}_{*}{\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{t}}_{*}h\right)

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}{\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{-t}}_{*}{\operatorname{Fl}_{v}^{s}}_{*}{\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{t}}_{*}h

(3.29)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\mathcal{L}_{v}h+\mathcal{L}_{v}\mathcal{L}_{u}h

(3.30)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{[u,v]}h=X^{[u,v]}(h),

(3.31)

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

At first glance,
Section 3.4
may seem to contradict the standard result that the fundamental vector field mapping
\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(TN)
on a manifold
N
is an
anti
Lie algebra homomorphism when the
G
-action on
N
is a left action
[
Lee12
, Theorem 20.18]
. The sign difference in our setting occurs because of our identification of vector fields
\diff(M)
with derivations
\operatorname{Der}(C^{\infty}(M))=\Gamma(TM)
, where the mapping

\operatorname{der}\colon\diff(M)\to\operatorname{Der}(C^{\infty}(M))
by
u\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{u}
is an anti Lie algebra homomorphism.

Proof of
Theorem 3.5
.

For
\dot{\delta_{\gamma}}=-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}},\ring{\delta_{\gamma}}=-\mathcal{L}_{v}{\delta_{\gamma}}
with some
u,v\in\diff(M)
, we compute
d\eta_{\delta_{\gamma}}(\dot{\delta_{\gamma}},\ring{\delta_{\gamma}})=d\eta_{\delta_{\gamma}}(X^{u},X^{v})
explicitly, using the fundamental vector fields
X^{u},X^{v}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}})
given by
X^{u}({\delta_{\gamma}})=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\delta_{\gamma}
,
X^{v}({\delta_{\gamma}})=-\mathcal{L}_{v}{\delta_{\gamma}}
. We use the formula

\displaystyle d\eta_{\delta_{\gamma}}(X^{u},X^{v})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X^{u}}\iota_{X^{v}}\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}-\mathcal{L}_{X^{v}}\iota_{X^{u}}\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}-\iota_{[X^{u},X^{v}]}\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}.

(3.32)

First, by
Section 3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}([X^{u},X^{v}])

\displaystyle=\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}(X^{[u,v]})=\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}(-\mathcal{L}_{[u,v]}{\delta_{\gamma}})=\frac{1}{m}\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},\iota_{[u,v]}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle.

(3.33)

Next, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X^{u}}\iota_{X^{v}}\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\eta(X^{v}(\Phi_{X^{u}}^{t}({\delta_{\gamma}})))=\frac{1}{m}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\langle{\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{t}}_{*}{\delta_{\gamma}},\iota_{v}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle

(3.34)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},{\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{t}}^{*}\iota_{v}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle=\frac{1}{m}\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{v}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle,

(3.35)

where
\Phi^{t}_{X^{u}}
is the time-
t
flow map of
X^{u}
, given by
\Phi_{X^{u}}^{t}({\delta_{\gamma}})=(\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{t})_{*}{\delta_{\gamma}}
, as explained in the proof of
Section 3.4
. Similarly, we have

\mathcal{L}_{X^{v}}\iota_{X^{u}}\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}=\frac{1}{m}\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},\mathcal{L}_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle.

Combining these results, we obtain

\displaystyle d\eta_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}(X^{u},X^{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\left(\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{v}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle-\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},\mathcal{L}_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle-\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},\iota_{[u,v]}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle\right)

(3.36)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\left(\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{v}\iota_{x}\mu-\mathcal{L}_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu-\iota_{[u,v]}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle\right).

(3.37)

By direct computation, we simplify the expression inside the dual pairing;

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{v}\iota_{x}\mu-\mathcal{L}_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu-\iota_{[u,v]}\iota_{x}\mu

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{v}\iota_{x}\mu-\mathcal{L}_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu-\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{v}\iota_{x}\mu+\iota_{v}\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{x}\mu

(3.38)

\displaystyle=\iota_{v}\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{x}\mu-\mathcal{L}_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu

(3.39)

\displaystyle=\iota_{v}d\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu+\iota_{v}\iota_{u}d\iota_{x}\mu-d\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu-\iota_{v}d\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu

(3.40)

\displaystyle=\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mathcal{L}_{x}\mu-d\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu.

(3.41)

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{x}\mu=m\mu
for the volume form
\mu=dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{m}
, and that
\partial{\delta_{\gamma}}=\delta_{\partial\gamma}=0
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle d\eta_{\delta_{\gamma}}(X^{u},X^{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mathcal{L}_{x}\mu-d\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle

(3.42)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mu\rangle-\frac{1}{m}\langle\partial\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\iota_{x}\mu\rangle

(3.43)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\delta_{\gamma}}(X^{u},X^{v})-0.

(3.44)

Thus we have shown
d\eta=\omega
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
can be extended from
\mathbb{R}^{m}
to a general manifold
M
equipped with an exact volume form;

Theorem 3.8

(Liouville form on
M
with an exact volume form)
.

Let
\mu
be a volume form on
M
such that
\mu=d\nu
for some
\nu\in\Omega^{m-1}(M)
. Define a 1-form
\eta
on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
by

\displaystyle\eta_{\delta_{\gamma}}(-\mathcal{L}_{u}\delta_{\gamma})=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{u}\nu\rangle.

(3.45)

Then
d\eta=\omega
.

Example 3.9
.

The Liouville form
\eta
in

is a special case of
Theorem 3.8
with
(M,\mu)=(\mathbb{R}^{m},dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{m})
and

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}x_{i}\,dx_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{i+m-1},

(3.46)

where the indices are taken modulo

.

Proof of
Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\dot{\delta_{\gamma}}=-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}},\ring{\delta_{\gamma}}=-\mathcal{L}_{v}{\delta_{\gamma}}
with some

. Using the same computational routine as in the proof of
Theorem 3.5
, with the fundamental vector fields
X^{u},X^{v}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}})
,
we compute:

\displaystyle d\eta_{\delta_{\gamma}}(\dot{\delta}_{\gamma},\ring{\delta}_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X^{u}}\iota_{X^{v}}\eta-\mathcal{L}_{X^{v}}\iota_{X^{u}}\eta-\iota_{[X^{u},X^{v}]}\eta

(3.47)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\mathcal{L}_{u}\iota_{v}\nu\rangle-\langle\delta_{\gamma},\mathcal{L}_{v}\iota_{u}\nu\rangle-\langle\delta_{\gamma},\mathcal{L}_{[u,v]}\nu\rangle

(3.48)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}d\nu-d\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\nu\rangle

(3.49)

\displaystyle=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mu\rangle-\langle\partial\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\nu\rangle

(3.51)

∎

Remark 3.10

Remark 3.11

(Liouville form on a closed manifold)
.

We are not aware of the existence or non-existence of a Liouville form on a general closed manifold
(M,\mu)
.
There are a few special cases where exactness has been proved. For example, the space of two distinct points on the sphere, equipped with the MW form (see
Section 3.6
), is symplectomorphic to the cotangent bundle of a certain space and therefore admits a Liouville form
[
OU13
]
.

However, we speculate that exactness of the MW form is unlikely in the general case when the volume form
\mu
is not exact, or at least that a Liouville form may not admit an explicit expression.

In contrast, we will explicitly define a Liouville form in the prequantum sense (
Section 5
) on the space of implicit representations of submanifolds in a generic closed ambient manifold. Our result does not, however, directly extend to unbounded manifolds, including simple cases such as
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Remark 3.12

(The Liouville form
\eta
is not a connection form)
.

The fibration
\pi\colon\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)\to\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
is a principal
G
-bundle, where the structure group
G
is the group of reparametrizations
\Diffp(S)
. Using the Liouville form
\eta
defined in
Theorem 3.8
, we define a 1-form
\tilde{\eta}=\pi^{*}\eta
on
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)
. By construction,
d\tilde{\eta}=\pi^{*}\omega
, and
\tilde{\eta}
is equivariant under the
\Diffp(S)
-action. However, it is not a connection form, as it lacks vertical reproducibility: since
\operatorname{ker}d\pi\subset\operatorname{ker}\tilde{\eta}
, we have
\tilde{\eta}_{\tilde{\gamma}}(\hat{\xi})=0
for any
\xi\in\diff(S)
, where
\hat{\xi}({\tilde{\gamma}})=d{\tilde{\gamma}}(\xi)
is the fundamental vector field corresponding to
\xi
at
{\tilde{\gamma}}
.

On the other hand, the Liouville form on the space of implicit representations that we will define does yield a connection form (
Section 6
).

3.4.1
Momentum maps on
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,\mathbb{R}^{m})

The Liouville form
\eta
of the MW form
\omega
can be used to describe conserved quantities of Hamiltonian systems on
\mathcal{O}
. As in finite-dimensional symplectic geometry, when a Lie group
G
acts on
\mathcal{O}
, a map
J\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is called a momentum map if

\displaystyle d\langle J(\cdot),\xi\rangle\big|_{\gamma}=\iota_{\hat{\xi}}\omega\big|_{\gamma}

(3.52)

for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}
,
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, and its fundamental vector field
\hat{\xi}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{O})
. If a given Hamiltonian function
\mathcal{H}\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
is invariant under the
G
-action, then
J(\gamma_{t})\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is conserved along the Hamiltonian flow
\gamma_{t}
.

When the Liouville form
\eta
is also invariant under the
G
-action, the momentum map can be expressed as

\displaystyle d\langle J(\gamma),\xi\rangle=\iota_{\hat{\xi}}\omega=\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\xi}}\eta-d\iota_{\hat{\xi}}\eta=-d(\eta(\hat{\xi})),

(3.53)

so that
\langle J(\gamma),\xi\rangle=-\eta(\hat{\xi})
, up to addition of a constant.

Example 3.13
.

Consider the rotation action of
\operatorname{SO}(3)
on
\mathrm{UEmb}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})\cong\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{UEmb}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})}
. The fundamental vector field
\hat{\xi}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{UEmb}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3})})
associated with
\xi\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
is given at each
{\delta_{\gamma}}
by

\hat{\xi}({\delta_{\gamma}})=-\mathcal{L}_{R_{\xi}}{\delta_{\gamma}}

where
R_{\xi}\in\sdiff(M)
is the rotational vector field defined by
R_{\xi}(x)=\xi\times x
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Since the Liouville form
\eta
(
3.19
) is invariant under rotation, we have
\langle J(\cdot),\xi\rangle=-\eta(\hat{\xi})
, up to an additive constant. Therefore, the
angular momentum

J({\delta_{\gamma}})\in\mathfrak{so}^{*}(3)
at
{\delta_{\gamma}}
can be computed from
R_{\xi}
and and the expression (
3.18
). Since
R_{\xi}(x)=\xi\times x=\operatorname{curl}\left(-\frac{1}{3}x\times(\xi\times x)\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle J(\gamma),\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\operatorname{det}(\partial_{s}{\tilde{\gamma}},{\tilde{\gamma}},\hat{\xi}\circ{\tilde{\gamma}})\,ds

(3.54)

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\left(-\frac{1}{3}x\times(\xi\times x)\right)^{\flat}

(3.55)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Flux}_{m-2}^{\operatorname{ex}}({\delta_{\gamma}},R_{\xi}),\quad\xi\in\mathfrak{so}(3),

(3.56)

where
{\tilde{\gamma}}
is any parametrization of
\gamma
and the flat operator
\flat\colon\diff(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is defined with respect to the Euclidean metric.

The quantity
\langle J(\gamma),\xi\rangle
can be interpreted as the signed volume of the surface of revolution obtained by rotating
\gamma
about the axis
\xi/|\xi|\in\mathfrak{so}(3)\cong\mathbb{R}^{3}
, multiplied by
|\xi|
.

On
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the Liouville form
\eta
is not invariant under
\SDiff_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
; for instance, its value depends on the choice of origin and is therefore not translation-invariant. It is preserved only by volume-preserving linear transforms
x\mapsto fx
with
f\in\operatorname{SL}(m)
, which form a finite-dimensional subgroup of
\SDiffp(\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

In contrast, the Liouville form on the space of implicit representations we will introduce in
Section 5
is invariant under
\SDiffp(M)
for any closed manifold
M
(see
Section 7.1.2
).

3.5
Riemannian and formal Kähler structure

So far, we introduced the MW structure using only a volume form on the ambient manifold
M
. When a Riemannian metric is given on
M
, we can additionally equip
\mathcal{O}
with a Riemannian structure.

Assume now that
M
is equipped with a Riemannian metric
g
inducing the volume form
\mu
. We first define an almost complex structure, that is, a vector bundle homomorphism
\mathcal{J}\colon T\mathcal{O}\to T\mathcal{O}
satisfying
\mathcal{J}^{2}=-\operatorname{id}
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}
be the parametrization space of
\mathcal{O}
via embeddings. We define an operator
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}\colon T\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\to T\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
by projecting each
\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}\in T_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
onto the normal bundle
N_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
, and then applying a 90-degree rotation in
N_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
with respect to the metric
g
, oriented so that

\displaystyle\mu(\partial_{s_{1}}{\tilde{\gamma}}(s),\ldots,\partial_{s_{n}}{\tilde{\gamma}}(s),\tilde{\mathcal{J}}\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(s),\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(s))\geq 0,

(3.57)

where
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
is any local chart consistent with the orientation of
{\tilde{\gamma}}
.

Since
\operatorname{ker}\tilde{\mathcal{J}}=\operatorname{ker}d\pi
and
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}
is invariant under reparametrization, it descends to an almost complex structure
\mathcal{J}
on
\mathcal{O}
, defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\dot{\gamma}=d\pi|_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}\dot{\tilde{\gamma}})

(3.58)

with any representatives
{\tilde{\gamma}}
of
\gamma
and
\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}
of
\dot{\gamma}
, respectively.

With
\mathcal{J}
and the MW form
\omega
, we define a Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{O}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\ring{\gamma})=\omega_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\mathcal{J}(\ring{\gamma})).

(3.59)

In terms of de Rham currents, this becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}(-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}},-\mathcal{L}_{v}{\delta_{\gamma}})=\langle{\delta_{\gamma}},\iota_{\hat{v}}\iota_{u}\mu\rangle,

(3.60)

where
\hat{v}
is any vector field on
M
satisfying
\hat{v}\circ\gamma=\mathcal{J}(v\circ\gamma)
.

We remark that the triple
(\mathcal{G},\omega,\mathcal{J})
defines a formal Kähler structure in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{J}\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,\cdot).

(3.61)

Strictly speaking, this is not a Kähler structure in the classical sense, which additionally requires a complex structure
i.e.
, the existence of holomorphic coordinates
[
Lem93
,
MZ96
]
.

In some infinite-dimensional settings, including our case, the Nijenhuis tensor

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\dot{\gamma},\ring{\gamma})\coloneqq[\mathcal{J}\dot{\gamma},\mathcal{J}\ring{\gamma}]-\mathcal{J}[\mathcal{J}\dot{\gamma},\ring{\gamma}]-\mathcal{J}[\dot{\gamma},\mathcal{J}\ring{\gamma}]-[\dot{\gamma},\ring{\gamma}],\quad\dot{\gamma},\ring{\gamma}\in T_{\gamma}\mathcal{O}

(3.62)

vanishes
[
Bry09
, Theorem 3.4.3]
[
Hen09
, Theorem 2.5]
, but this does not imply the existence of a complex structure
[
Lem93
, Theorem 10.5]
. This contrasts with the finite-dimensional case, where vanishing of the Nijenhuis tensor is equivalent to integrability. For details, see
[
Hen09
, Chapter 2]
for example.

3.6
Base Dimension
2

We briefly discuss the case where the dimension
m
of the ambient manifold
M
is
2
, which is a finite-dimensional setting in contrast to
m>2
. Although many aspects are simplified in this case, the symplectic structure requires additional care: orientations of points must be supplied explicitly, as they are not encoded in the embeddings themselves.

We regard the domain
S
as a collection of
N
distinct points. The space of embeddings is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)\coloneqq M^{N}\setminus\Delta=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in M^{N}\mid x_{j}\neq x_{k}\text{ for }j\neq k\},

(3.63)

which is a smooth
2N
-dimensional manifold.

In contrast to higher-dimensional settings, this space does not carry orientation data, that is, whether each point is embedded positively or negatively. Hence we need to manually attach the orientations
I=(I_{1},\dots,I_{N})\in\{\pm 1\}^{N}
to the symplectic structure. Namely we define

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}(\dot{\tilde{\gamma}},\ring{\tilde{\gamma}})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N}I_{j}\pi_{j}^{*}\mu(\dot{\tilde{\gamma}},\ring{\tilde{\gamma}})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}I_{j}\mu(\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}_{j},\ring{\tilde{\gamma}}_{j}),

(3.64)

where
\dot{\tilde{\gamma}},\ring{\tilde{\gamma}}\in T_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)\cong T_{x_{1}}M\times\cdots\times T_{x_{N}}M
, and
\pi_{j}
denotes projection onto the
j
-th factor. Since the volume form
\mu
is symplectic on a 2-dimensional manifold
M
,
\tilde{\omega}_{{\tilde{\gamma}}}
is non-degenerate and thus already symplectic.

We may still consider the unparametrized shape space

\displaystyle\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)\coloneqq\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)/\Diffp(S),

(3.65)

by quotienting out the reparametrization group
\Diffp(S)\coloneqq\mathrm{Sym}(k_{1})\times\mathrm{Sym}(k_{2})
, which permutes the
k_{1}
positively oriented and
k_{2}
negatively oriented points, with
k_{1}+k_{2}=N
.

Using currents (which are simply distributions in base dimension 2), we can encode orientations directly to the shape
{\delta_{\gamma}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)}\subset\mathcal{D}_{0}(M)
by

\displaystyle\delta_{\gamma}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{N}I_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}

(3.66)

with the Dirac delta distribution
\delta_{x_{j}}
at each
x_{j}
.

Then, for
\dot{\delta}_{\gamma}=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\delta_{\gamma}
and
\ring{\delta}_{\gamma}=-\mathcal{L}_{v}\delta_{\gamma}
with vector fields
u,v\in\Gamma(TM)
, the MW structure is defined

\displaystyle\omega_{\delta_{\gamma}}(-\mathcal{L}_{u}\delta_{\gamma},-\mathcal{L}_{v}\delta_{\gamma})=\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mu\rangle

(3.67)

in the same way as for higher dimensional cases.
This evaluates to

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{\gamma},\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mu\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}I_{j}\langle\delta_{x_{j}},\mu(u,v)\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}I_{j}\mu(\dot{\gamma}_{j},\ring{\gamma}_{j}),

(3.68)

which agrees with the pointwise expression of the MW form (
3.64
).

3.7
Toward implicit representations: the space of all the codimension-2 shapes

We considered the explicit shape space consisting of embeddings modulo reparametrization for each fixed manifold
S
. We conclude this section by defining their disjoint union:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{S}\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)

(3.69)

where
S
runs through all oriented, closed
n
-dimensional manifolds. Thus, the explicit shape space
\mathcal{X}
is the set of all closed
n
-dimensional submanifolds of
M
, also known as the space of
n
-dimensional nonlinear Grassmannians
[
HV03
]
.
Each
\gamma\in\mathcal{X}
lies in a unique
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
, and we have
T_{\gamma}\mathcal{X}=T_{\gamma}\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
. Hence, the symplectic structure
\omega
defined on each
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
naturally extends to
\mathcal{X}
. For simplicity, we will identify
\mathcal{X}
with its current counterpart
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
via the injection
\mathcal{I}\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{D}_{n}(M)
without further mention.

Example 3.14
.

If
m=2
, the space
\mathcal{X}=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}I_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}\mid I_{j}\in\{-1,1\},\ x\in M^{k}\setminus\Delta\right\}
is all the possible configurations of finitely many unordered oriented points.
If
m=3
,
\mathcal{X}=\bigsqcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{UEmb}\left(\bigsqcup^{k}\mathbb{S}^{1},M\right)
consists of all smooth links with finitely many knot components.
If
m>3
, then
S
may be a disjoint union of different types of manifolds.

4
Implicit representations of codimension-2 shapes

So far, we have reviewed the explicit shape space of codimension-2 submanifolds in the ambient manifold
M
. In this section, we introduce their
implicit representations
and study the geometry of the space of these implicit representations as a fiber bundle over the explicit shape space. Throughout this and the following sections, we identify the explicit shape space
\mathcal{X}
with its counterpart
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
in terms of de Rham currents, as well as each
\Diff_{0}(M)
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
with the corresponding currents
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
, without explicitly restating this identification.

4.1
Implicit representations

We define the space
\mathcal{F}
of implicit representations of submanifolds as a subset of
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
as follows.
The space
\mathcal{F}
consists of functions
\psi
such that the zero set
\psi^{-1}(0)
is nonempty, and the differential
d\psi|_{x}:T_{x}M\to\mathbb{C}
is surjective at each
x\in\psi^{-1}(0)
.

Define a fibration
\Pi\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{X}
by assigning to each
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
a unique
\gamma\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\operatorname{im}\gamma=\psi^{-1}(0)
, with the orientation of
\psi
determined as follows.
For
\gamma\in\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
with some
S
having
k
connected components, there are
2^{k}
possible orientations. Among these, we select the unique one satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma}\operatorname{Im}\frac{d\psi}{\psi}=2\pi\,\Sigma\cap\gamma(S)

(4.1)

for any oriented
(m-1)
-dimensional topological disk
\Sigma
intersecting transversely with
\gamma(S)
, where
\Sigma\cap\gamma(S)
denotes the signed intersection number between
\Sigma
and
\gamma(S)
.

Existence of implicit representations

The fibration
\Pi\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{X}
is not surjective. More precisely, our implicit representation of shapes is limited to codimension-2 submanifolds that are exact in homology; that is, each
\gamma
must be the boundary of an oriented codimension-1 submanifold
\Sigma
.

In this case, we can construct a map
\theta\colon M\setminus\operatorname{im}\gamma\to\mathbb{T}^{1}
as the solution to the Dirichlet boundary problem:

\displaystyle\Delta\theta(x)=0\quad\text{for }x\notin\Sigma,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\theta(x)\to 2\pi\quad\text{as }x\to\Sigma_{\mathrm{f}},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\theta(x)\to 0\quad\ \ \text{as }x\to\Sigma_{\mathrm{b}},

(4.4)

where
\Sigma_{\mathrm{f}}
and
\Sigma_{\mathrm{b}}
denote the front and back sides of
\Sigma
, respectively. Given any smooth non-negative function
\rho
such that
\rho^{-1}(0)=\operatorname{im}\gamma
, we can define an implicit representation
\psi
by
\psi(x)=\rho(x)e^{i\theta(x)}
. For
M=\mathbb{S}^{m}
, viewed as the one-point compactification of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the so-called solid angle field (visualized in
Figure 1
) is a special case of such a phase field
e^{i\theta}

[
BDR20
,
CI24
]
.

Remark 4.1
.

In this article, we consider
unlabeled shapes
by taking the quotient of
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)
by
\Diff^{+}(S)
, rather than by
\Diff_{0}(S)
, as noted in
Section 3.1
. Our implicit representations are naturally suited to this setting, since
the level set of each
\psi
is unlabeled and unparametrized, yet having the orientation as explained above.

Remark 4.2

(Relation with open book decomposition)
.

Each
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
can be interpreted as a (relaxed) instance of an
open book decomposition
in contact geometry: a decomposition of the ambient manifold
M
into an
m-2
dimensional submanifold
B
called the
binder
, and a family of
m-1
dimensional submanifolds
\{\Sigma_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}
, called
pages
, such that
\partial\Sigma_{s}=B
for each
s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

[
Gei08
,
Etn05
]
.

For a given
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
, if the phase map
\phi\colon M\setminus\psi^{-1}(0)\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
, defined by
\phi(x)=\frac{\psi(x)}{|\psi(x)|}

is a submersion, then the zero set
\{\psi=0\}
serves as the binder, and the level sets
\{\phi^{-1}(s)\}_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}
form the pages. Even if
\phi
fails to be a submersion, its level sets still form a foliation of
M
, and almost every

is a binder by Sard’s theorem.

Orbit space

The full space
\mathcal{F}
is large and, in particular, consists of multiple connected components. Just as in the case of the explicit representation space
\mathcal{X}
, where each connected component is a
\Diff_{0}(M)
-orbit

, we focus here on a single orbit
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{F}
under the action of the following group.

We define the semi-direct product group

\displaystyle\SemiD\coloneqq\Diff_{0}(M)\ltimes\operatorname{Exp}(C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})),

(4.5)

where
\Diff_{0}(M)

\displaystyle f\rhd e^{\varphi}=e^{\varphi}\circ f^{-1},\quad f\in\Diff_{0}(M),e^{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Exp}(C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})).

(4.6)

The Lie group
\SemiD
has a Lie algebra
\AlgSemiD=\diff(M)\ltimes C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
with Lie bracket

\displaystyle[(u,a),(v,b)]=([u,v],-\mathcal{L}_{u}b+\mathcal{L}_{v}a),\quad u,v\in\diff(M),\;a,b\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C}),

(4.7)

where
[u,v]
denotes the Lie bracket of vector fields on
M
.

Proposition 4.3
.

The operation
\AlgSemiD\times\AlgSemiD\to\AlgSemiD
given in (

i.e.

Proof.

These properties can be verified by a straightforward computation.
∎

We now define an
\SemiD
-action on
\mathcal{F}
by

\displaystyle(f,e^{\varphi})\rhd\psi=(\psi\circ f^{-1})\cdot e^{\varphi},\quad(f,e^{\varphi})\in\SemiD,\psi\in\mathcal{F}.

(4.8)

Let
\mathcal{U}_{\psi}\subset\mathcal{F}
denote the
\SemiD
-orbit containing
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
. The tangent space at

\psi
is

\displaystyle T_{\psi}\mathcal{U}_{\psi}=\{-\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi+\varphi\psi\mid(u,\varphi)\in\AlgSemiD\}.

(4.9)

Given
\gamma=\Pi\psi\in\mathcal{X}
and

Remark 4.4

Proof strategy

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\lambda_{\psi}\wedge\beta=\int_{M^{\operatorname{reg}\phi}}\lambda_{\psi}\wedge\beta=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\delta_{(\phi^{-1}(\cdot)\cap M^{\operatorname{reg}\phi})},\beta\rangle\sigma

(5.7)

where

denotes the set of regular points of

m-1

\|\Lambda(\psi)\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m-1}(M)}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\sigma\sup_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\|\delta_{\partial(\phi^{-1}(s)\cap M^{\operatorname{reg}\phi})}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m-1}(M)}=2\pi\sup_{s\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\|\delta_{\partial(\phi^{-1}(s)\cap M^{\operatorname{reg}\phi})}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{m-1}(M)}

Lemma 5.8
.

For

\displaystyle[Y^{(u,a)},Y^{(v,b)}](\psi)=-\mathcal{L}_{[u,v]}\psi+(\mathcal{L}_{u}b-\mathcal{L}_{v}a)\psi.

Proof.

To compute the Lie derivative of vector fields
[Y^{(u,a)},Y^{(v,b)}]=\mathcal{L}_{Y^{(u,a)}}Y^{(v,b)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y^{(u,a)}}Y^{(v,b)}(\psi)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}D\Phi^{-t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}\left(Y^{(v,b)}(\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi))\right),\quad\psi\in\mathcal{U}

(5.10)

where
\Phi^{t}_{Y}\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}
is the flow map along
Y\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{U})
defined by the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\Phi^{t}_{Y}(\psi))=Y(\Phi^{t}_{Y}(\psi)),

(5.11)

\displaystyle\Phi^{0}_{Y}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{U}},

(5.12)

and
D\Phi^{t}_{Y}|_{\psi}\colon T_{\psi}\mathcal{U}\to T_{\Phi^{t}_{Y}(\psi)}\mathcal{U}
is its differential at
\psi
. For the fundamental vector field

associated to

\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi)={\operatorname{Fl}_{u}^{-t}}^{*}\psi\cdot e^{ta}

Since

\displaystyle Y^{(v,b)}(\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi))

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi^{s}_{Y^{(v,b)}}\left(\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi)\right),

(5.13)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}D\Phi^{-t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}\left(Y^{(v,b)}(\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi^{-t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}\left(\Phi^{s}_{Y^{(v,b)}}\left(\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi)\right)\right)

(5.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi^{-t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}\left(\Phi^{s}_{Y^{(v,b)}}(\psi)\right)+\Phi^{s}_{Y^{(v,b)}}\left(\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi)\right)

(5.15)

using the product rule.
We then compute the first term:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi^{-t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}\left(\Phi^{s}_{Y^{(v,b)}}(\psi)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}{\operatorname{Fl}^{t}_{u}}^{*}\left({\operatorname{Fl}^{-s}_{v}}^{*}\psi\cdot e^{sb}\right)\cdot e^{-ta}

(5.16)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}{\operatorname{Fl}^{t}_{u}}^{*}\left({\operatorname{Fl}^{-s}_{v}}^{*}\psi\right)\cdot{\operatorname{Fl}^{t}_{u}}^{*}e^{sb}\cdot e^{-ta}

(5.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\mathcal{L}_{u}\left({\operatorname{Fl}^{-s}_{v}}^{*}\psi\right)\cdot e^{sb}+\left({\operatorname{Fl}^{-s}_{v}}^{*}\psi\right)\mathcal{L}_{u}(e^{sb})-\left({\operatorname{Fl}^{-s}_{v}}^{*}\psi\right)\cdot e^{sb}\cdot a

(5.18)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi+\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi\cdot b-\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi\cdot 0+\psi\mathcal{L}_{u}b+\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi\cdot a-\psi\cdot b\cdot a

(5.19)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi+b\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi+a\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi+\psi\mathcal{L}_{u}b-ab\psi

(5.20)

where we used
\mathcal{L}_{u}(e^{sb})=s\mathcal{L}_{u}b\cdot e^{sb}
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi^{s}_{Y^{(v,b)}}\left(\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi)\right)=\mathcal{L}_{v}\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi-a\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi-b\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi-\psi\mathcal{L}_{v}a+ab\psi.

(5.21)

Summing these results, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi^{-t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}\left(\Phi^{s}_{Y^{(v,b)}}(\psi)\right)+\Phi^{s}_{Y^{(v,b)}}\left(\Phi^{t}_{Y^{(u,a)}}(\psi)\right)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi+\mathcal{L}_{v}\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi+\psi\mathcal{L}_{v}b-\psi\mathcal{L}_{v}a

(5.22)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{[u,v]}\psi+(\mathcal{L}_{u}b-\mathcal{L}_{v}a)\psi.

(5.23)

We thus obtained the stated expression.

Lemma 5.10

Lemma 5.11
.

Let

Proof.

Clearly we have

Remark 5.15

(Physical interpretation of
\Theta_{\psi}
)
.

By Sard’s theorem,

Together with

captures the infinitesimal phase shift of

-family of hypersurfaces

In this section, we have shown that
\Theta
is a Liouville form for
\omega
in the prequantum sense, that is,

. We conclude this section by noting that
\Theta
does not directly induce a Liouville form for the symplectic structure
\omega
on
\mathcal{O}
in the classical sense. This is because we cannot factor
\Theta
onto
\mathcal{O}

is not contained within the kernel of

, but the values

and
\Theta(\ring{\psi})=\frac{\Vol(M)c_{2}}{2\pi}
are different.

However, it is still possible to take a quotient space of
\mathcal{U}
where the Liouville form can descend onto. This construction actually leads to a prequantum bundle structure, as we will explain in the next section.

6
Prequantum structure

We have shown that each
\SemiD
-orbit
\mathcal{U}
in the fiber bundle
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}=\Pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}
admits a Liouville form in the prequantum sense for the MW structure on
\mathcal{O}
. The full fiber
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
inherits these symplectic and Liouville structures as
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
is a
\Diff_{0}(M)\ltimes C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
-orbit, foliated by
\SemiD
-orbits, as discussed in
Section 4.2
.

Building on this, we now construct a prequantum bundle as a quotient bundle of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
, equipped with a connection form whose curvature form recovers the MW symplectic form on
\mathcal{O}
. This framework enables us to define a unique horizontal lift in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
over a path in
\mathcal{O}
.

Definition 6.1

(Prequantum
G
-bundle)
.

A prequantum
G
-bundle
over a symplectic manifold
(B,\beta)
is a principal
G
-bundle
\pi\colon E\to B
equipped with a connection form
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(E;\mathfrak{g})
satisfying

\displaystyle\pi^{*}\beta=d\alpha+\frac{1}{2}[\alpha\wedge\alpha].

(6.1)

If
G
is abelian, the relation (
6.1
) reduces to
\pi^{*}\beta=d\alpha
, which is always the case in this article.

For a prequantum
G
-bundle, the vertical and horizontal distributions are defined in the standard way for principal bundles:

Definition 6.2

(Vertical and horizontal distributions, and horizontal lift)
.

Let
\pi\colon(E,\alpha)\to(B,\beta)
be a prequantum
G
-bundle. At each point

, the tangent space splits as
T_{x}E=V_{x}E\oplus H_{x}E
, where the vertical distribution is defined by
V_{x}E=\operatorname{ker}d\pi|_{x}
, and the horizontal distribution is given by
H_{x}E=\operatorname{ker}\alpha|_{x}
. A
horizontal lift
of a path
\{\gamma_{t}\}_{t}\subset B
is a path
\{\ell_{t}\}_{t}\subset E
satisfying
\pi\circ\ell_{t}=\gamma_{t}
and
\partial_{t}\ell_{t}\in H_{\ell_{t}}E
for all
t
. Such a lift is uniquely determined by the choice of the initial point
\ell_{0}
.

Theorem 5.3
states that the fibration
\Pi\colon(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}},\Theta)\to(\mathcal{O},\omega)
is a formal prequantization
i.e.
,
\Pi^{*}\omega=d\Theta
. However, it is not a prequantum
G
-bundle since
\Theta
is not a connection form. Consequently, it does not define a unique horizontal lift: for a path
\{\gamma_{t}\}_{t}\subset\mathcal{O}
, there exist infinitely many paths
\{\ell_{t}\}_{t}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
with fixed initial point
\ell_{0}
satisfying
d\Pi(\partial_{t}\ell_{t})=\partial_{t}\gamma_{t}
and
\partial_{t}\ell_{t}\in\operatorname{ker}\Theta_{\ell_{t}}
for all
t
.
To define a bundle on which
\Theta
becomes a genuine connection 1-form, we take the quotient of the tangent space at each
\psi\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
by the intersection
\operatorname{ker}\Theta\cap\operatorname{ker}d\Pi
. This quotient process is characterized by the following equivalence relation.

Definition 6.3

(Volume class and volume bundle)
.

For
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
, we say
\psi_{0}\sim_{\mathcal{P}}\psi_{1}
if there is a path
\{\psi_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
in a
\SemiD
-orbit joining
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
such that
\partial_{t}\psi_{t}\in\operatorname{ker}d\Pi_{\psi_{t}}\cap\operatorname{ker}\Theta_{\psi_{t}}
for any
t
. We call each equivalence class
[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}

a volume class
and the resulting quotient space
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}/\sim_{\mathcal{P}}

the volume bundle
. The tangent space at each
[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}
is
T_{[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}}\mathcal{P}=T_{\psi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}/\sim_{\mathcal{P}}
where
\dot{\psi}\sim_{\mathcal{P}}\ring{\psi}
if
\dot{\psi}-\ring{\psi}\in\operatorname{ker}d\Pi|_{\psi}\cap\operatorname{ker}\Theta|_{\psi}
.

We note that the projection
\Pi\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathcal{O}
decomposes into two projections:
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathcal{P}
and
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{O}
.
By construction, the Liouville form
\Theta
descends to a 1-form
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{P})
by
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}=\Theta
. Since

, we have

, and therefore the fibration
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon(\mathcal{P},\Theta_{\mathcal{P}})\to(\mathcal{O},\omega)
is a formal prequantization.

In addition, the volume bundle
\mathcal{P}
forms a principal
G
-bundle over
\mathcal{O}
, where the structure group is
G=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{Z})

\mathcal{P}
as follows. We first define a circle action by constant phase shift:

\displaystyle e^{ic}\rhd[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}\coloneqq[\psi e^{ic}]_{\mathcal{P}},\quad\text{for }\psi\in\mathcal{F},\;e^{ic}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}.

(6.2)

As shown in

.
The

We may further specify a group action of

as a deck transformation between the connected components of each fiber. That is, it is a fiber-preserving action that maps points from one connected component of a fiber to another component.
We fix a lattice basis

for
H_{dR}^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, where

is the first Betti number of

. Recall that there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathfrak{i}\colon C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C}^{\times})/\operatorname{Exp}(C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C}^{\times}))

\displaystyle\to H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{Z})

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left[\kappa\right]\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\mapsto\left[\operatorname{Im}\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d\kappa}{\kappa}\right].

(6.4)

Choose arbitrary representatives

of

for

Using these functions

\displaystyle[\eta]\rhd[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}\coloneqq[\tau_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{c_{n}}\cdot\psi]_{\mathcal{P}},\quad\psi\in\mathcal{F},\ [\eta]=[c_{1}\eta_{1}+\ldots+c_{n}\eta_{n}]\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{Z}).

These

\displaystyle(e^{ic},[\eta])\rhd[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}=[e^{ic}\tau_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{c_{n}}\psi]_{\mathcal{P}},\quad e^{ic}\in\mathbb{S}^{1},[\eta]\in H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{Z}),

(6.6)

Proposition 6.4
.

The fibration
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{O}
equipped with the above action of

G
-bundle.

Furthermore,
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
defines a connection form on this principal
G
-bundle:

Proposition 6.5
.

On the principal
G
-bundle
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{O}
defined above, the 1-form
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
is a connection form. That is,
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies the following two properties:

1.

Equivariance under the
G

\Phi_{g}\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}

\Phi_{g}^{*}\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}=\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
for every

2.

Vertical reproducibility; i.e., there exists a nonzero constant
C\neq 0
such that
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}(\hat{\xi})=C\xi
for any

and its fundamental vector field
\hat{\xi}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{P})
.
§
§

§

A standard definition requires

or

or

Proof.

We first verify equivariance. Let
g=(e^{ic},[\sum_{i}c_{i}\eta_{i}])\in G=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, and denote by
\Phi_{g}
the

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}|_{\Phi_{g}([\psi]_{\mathcal{P}})}([\Phi_{g*}\dot{\psi}]_{\mathcal{P}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\operatorname{Im}\frac{\dot{\psi}e^{ic}\tau_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{c_{n}}\cdot\overline{\psi e^{ic}\tau_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{c_{n}}}}{|\psi e^{ic}\tau_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{c_{n}}|^{2}}\ \mu

(6.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\operatorname{Im}\frac{\dot{\psi}\cdot\bar{\psi}}{|\psi|^{2}}\ \mu=\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}|_{[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}}([\dot{\psi}]_{\mathcal{P}}),

(6.8)

where

and

Next, we verify vertical reproducibility. Let

be the corresponding fundamental vector field, defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}([\psi]_{\mathcal{P}})=\left[\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}e^{i\xi t}\psi\right]_{\mathcal{P}}=[i\xi\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}.

(6.9)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}|_{[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}}(\hat{\xi})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\operatorname{Im}\frac{i\xi\psi\bar{\psi}}{|\psi|^{2}}\ \mu=\frac{\xi\operatorname{Vol}(M)}{2\pi}.

(6.10)

Combining the results that

) and a principal

The fibration
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon(\mathcal{P},\Theta_{\mathcal{P}})\to(\mathcal{O},\omega)
is a prequantum
G
-bundle with
G=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times H^{1}_{dR}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

Using the connection form
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
, each tangent space

splits into the vertical distribution

Hence we can now define a unique horizontal lift on

This also reveals a geometric interpretation of the MW form as the curvature form of the Liouville form

over a closed path in

Corollary 6.7

(Average swept volume)
.

Consider a closed path
\partial\Sigma

\mathcal{O}

\Sigma

[\psi_{t}]_{\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{\sigma_{t}^{s}}\iota_{\partial_{t}{\sigma}_{t}^{s}}\mu\ ds=0,

Remark 6.9
.

7
Additional structures

Our implicit representations of shapes and the resulting prequantum bundle structure reveal some additional structures.

7.1
Hamiltonian systems on the space of implicit representations

In
Section 3.3.1
, we reviewed Hamiltonian vector fields on each
\Diff_{0}(M)
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
in the explicit shape space with respect to the MW form
\omega
. We now consider Hamiltonian vector fields in the implicit shape space
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}=\Pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}
.

Define a closed 2-form
\tilde{Z}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
by
\tilde{Z}\coloneqq\Pi^{*}\omega
. The closedness of
\tilde{Z}
follows from that of
\omega
. Note that
\tilde{Z}
is degenerate with
\operatorname{ker}\tilde{Z}=\operatorname{ker}d\Pi
, which is infinite-dimensional. Hence,
\tilde{Z}
is not symplectic but merely a
presymplectic
form, in contrast to
\omega
being symplectic.

Consequently, Hamiltonian vector fields are defined only up to
\operatorname{ker}\tilde{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{H}\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function with
d\mathcal{H}\in\operatorname{im}\flat^{\omega}
, and define
H\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathbb{R}
by
H=\mathcal{H}\circ\Pi
. We say that any vector field
X_{H}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}})
satisfying
dH=\iota_{X_{H}}\tilde{Z}
is a Hamiltonian vector field of
H
, despite the non-uniqueness.

7.1.1
Horizontal Hamiltonian vector fields

The prequantum structure we have built allows a canonical choice among these Hamiltonian vector fields.
We can define a unique
horizontal Hamiltonian
vector field on the prequantum bundle
\mathcal{P}
over a Hamiltonian vector field on
\mathcal{O}
, using
\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}
as the connection form.

Since
H=\mathcal{H}\circ\Pi
is by construction constant along each fiber
\Pi^{-1}\gamma
over
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}
, it descends onto
\mathcal{P}
as a function
H_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{H}\circ\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}
defined using
\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{O}
.
Then there is one degree of freedom within the vector fields
X_{H_{\mathcal{P}}}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{P})
satisfying

dH_{\mathcal{P}}=\iota_{X_{H_{\mathcal{P}}}}Z_{\mathcal{P}}

where
Z_{\mathcal{P}}\coloneqq\Pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\omega
is a presymplectic structure on
\mathcal{P}
.
Among these, the horizontal Hamiltonian vector field is the unique one additionally satisfying the horizontality condition
X_{H_{\mathcal{P}}}\in H_{[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}}\mathcal{P}=\operatorname{ker}\Theta_{\mathcal{P}}|_{[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}}
at each
[\psi]_{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{P}
.

7.1.2
Momentum maps

Momentum maps can also be defined for presymplectic structures
\tilde{Z}=\Pi^{*}\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}})
in much the same way as for the MW symplectic structure
\omega
explained in
Section 3.4.1
. Given an action of a Lie group
G
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
, we say that
J\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a momentum map if

d\langle J(\cdot),\xi\rangle=\iota_{\hat{\xi}}\tilde{Z}
for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and its corresponding fundamental vector field
\hat{\xi}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}})
. When the Hamiltonian
H
is invariant under the
G
-action,
J(\psi_{t})\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is conserved along the Hamiltonian flow
\psi_{t}
.

Similar to the case of the Liouville form
\eta
for the MW symplectic form
\omega
on the explicit shape space
\mathcal{O}
, the Liouville form
\Theta
of the presymplectic form
\tilde{Z}
in the implicit shape space
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
can describe conserved quantities of Hamiltonian flows. When
\Theta
is invariant under the
G
-action, the corresponding conserved quantity
J(\psi)
satisfies

\langle J(\psi),\xi\rangle=-\Theta(\hat{\xi}(\psi))
, up to an additive constant.

As observed in
Section 3.4.1
, the Liouville form
\eta
for the MW form
\omega
on
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is not invariant under the action of the entire
\SDiffp(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, but only under the volume-preserving linear transformations in
\operatorname{SL}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. In contrast, the Liouville form
\Theta
in the implicit shape space
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
is invariant under the entire group
\SDiffp(M)
. We, however, acknowledge that their settings differ:
\eta
on
\mathcal{O}
is the Liouville form in the classical sense
i.e.
,
d\eta=\omega
and the ambient manifold
M
has an exact volume form, whereas
\Theta
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
is defined in the prequantum sense
i.e.
,
d\Theta=\pi^{*}\omega
and the ambient manifold is a closed manifold
M
.

To see the invariance of
\Theta
under the
\SDiffp(M)
-action, denote by
R_{f}\colon\psi\mapsto\psi\circ f^{-1}
the action of
f\in\SDiffp(M)
on
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
. A direct computation shows that

{R_{f}}_{*}\dot{\psi}=\dot{\psi}\circ f^{-1}
for
\dot{\psi}\in T_{\psi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
.
Then, using
f^{*}\mu=\mu
, we have

\displaystyle\Theta_{R_{f}(\psi)}({R_{f}}_{*}\dot{\psi})

\displaystyle=\Theta_{\psi\circ f^{-1}}(\dot{\psi}\circ f^{-1})

(7.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\operatorname{Im}\frac{\dot{\psi}\circ f^{-1}\cdot\overline{\psi}\circ f^{-1}}{|\psi\circ f^{-1}|^{2}}\,\mu

(7.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\operatorname{Im}\frac{\dot{\psi}\cdot\overline{\psi}}{|\psi|^{2}}\,f^{*}\mu=\Theta_{\psi}(\dot{\psi}).

(7.3)

Note that the fundamental vector field
\hat{u}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}})
of
u\in\sdiff(M)
with respect to the
\SDiffp(M)
-action on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
is given by
\hat{u}(\psi)=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi
.
Therefore, the corresponding momentum map
J\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}\to\sdiff(M)^{*}
is computed as

\displaystyle\langle J(\psi),u\rangle=-\Theta_{\psi}(-\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\operatorname{Flux}_{m-1}(\Lambda(\psi),u),

(7.4)

where
\Lambda(\psi)\in\mathcal{D}_{m-1}(M)
is the circle differential current (
Section 5
) and
\operatorname{Flux}_{m-1}
is the flux functional (
Section 2.3
).
In the case where
u
is exact divergence-free
i.e.
,
\iota_{u}\mu
is exact, this further reduces to

\langle J(\psi),u\rangle=\operatorname{Flux}_{m-2}^{\operatorname{ex}}(\delta_{\Pi\psi},u)
. This refers only to the explicit shape
\Pi\psi\in\mathcal{O}
and coincides with the momentum map on
\mathcal{O}
given in
Section 3.4.1
.

7.2
Marsden–Weinstein structure in terms of implicit representations

Just as
\mathrm{Emb}(S,M)
provides parametrizations of unparametrized shapes
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
, we may regard
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}=\Pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}
as another parametrization space for an orbit
\mathcal{O}
in
\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
. This perspective allows us to express the MW form
\omega
in terms of implicit representations.

Let
\tilde{Z}=\Pi^{*}\omega
be the presymplectic form on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
defined in the previous subsection, and let us now suppose that
(M,\mu)
is equipped with a Riemannian metric inducing the volume form
\mu
. Then
\tilde{Z}
admits an explicit expression, as we describe below.

At each zero
p\in\psi^{-1}(0)
, the tangent space decomposes as
T_{p}M=T_{p}\gamma(S)\oplus N_{p}\gamma(S)
with respect to the given Riemannian metric
g
. We can choose a unit frame field
(n_{1},n_{2})
over the normal bundle
N\gamma(S)
such that
\mu(\partial_{s_{1}}\gamma,\ldots,\partial_{s_{n}}\gamma,n_{1},n_{2})>0
for any local coordinates
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
consistent with the orientation of
S
.

Let
d\psi_{N}\colon N_{p}\gamma(S)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the restriction of the differential
d\psi\colon T_{p}M\to\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
to the normal space
N_{p}\gamma(S)
. This is a linear map between real 2-dimensional vector spaces, and is invertible since
d\psi
is surjective on
\psi^{-1}(0)
by the definition of
\mathcal{F}
. We define the value
\operatorname{det}(d\psi_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{det}(d\psi_{N})\,\nu_{N}(\cdot,\cdot)=\nu_{\mathbb{C}}(d\psi_{N}\,\cdot,d\psi_{N}\,\cdot)

(7.5)

using the area form
\nu_{N}
on
N\gamma(S)
induced by
g
, and the standard area form
\nu_{\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathbb{C}
, defined by
\nu_{\mathbb{C}}(h,k)=\operatorname{Re}(hi\bar{k})
for
h,k\in\mathbb{C}
.
Note that
\operatorname{det}d\psi_{N}
depends on the choice of Riemannian metric
g
, but is independent of the choice of the frame field
(n_{1},n_{2})
. The following proposition gives an explicit expression for
\tilde{Z}_{\psi}
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Given a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, the presymplectic form
\tilde{Z}_{\psi}
is explicitly written as

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}_{\psi}(\dot{\psi},\ring{\psi})=\int_{\{\psi=0\}}\operatorname{det}(d\psi_{N}^{-1})\operatorname{Re}(\dot{\psi}\,i\,\bar{\ring{\psi}})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

(7.6)

where
d\mathcal{H}^{n}
is the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure induced by
g
.

We note that in (
7.6
), both
\operatorname{det}(d\psi_{N}^{-1})
and
d\mathcal{H}^{n}
depend on the metric
g
, but their product is independent of
g
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma=\Pi\psi
and
\dot{\psi}=-\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi+a\psi
,
\ring{\psi}=-\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi+b\psi
with
u,v\in\mathrm{Diff}(M)
and
a,b\in C^{\infty}(M;\mathbb{C})
. From
d\Pi(\dot{\psi})=-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}}\in T_{{\delta_{\gamma}}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{O}}
and
d\Pi(\ring{\psi})=-\mathcal{L}_{v}{\delta_{\gamma}}
, we have

\displaystyle\Pi^{*}\omega(\dot{\psi},\ring{\psi})

\displaystyle=\omega(-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}},-\mathcal{L}_{v}{\delta_{\gamma}})=\int_{\gamma}\iota_{v}\iota_{u}\mu=\int_{\gamma}\iota_{v^{\perp}}\iota_{u^{\perp}}\mu

(7.7)

where
u^{\perp},v^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal projections of
u,v
onto the normal space with respect to
g
on the zeros, which are defined on
\psi^{-1}(0)
.

From
u^{\perp}=d\psi_{N}^{-1}\dot{\psi}
and
v^{\perp}=d\psi_{N}^{-1}\ring{\psi}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\iota_{v^{\perp}}\iota_{u^{\perp}}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\mu(d\psi_{N}^{-1}\dot{\psi},d\psi_{N}^{-1}\ring{\psi},\cdots)

(7.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\nu_{N}\left(d\psi_{N}^{-1}\dot{\psi},d\psi_{N}^{-1}\ring{\psi}\right)\iota_{n_{2}}\iota_{n_{1}}\mu

(7.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\operatorname{det}(d\psi_{N}^{-1})\nu_{\mathbb{C}}(\dot{\psi},\ring{\psi})\iota_{n_{2}}\iota_{n_{1}}\mu,

(7.10)

which reads the stated expression.

∎

7.2.1
Riemannian and Kähler structures

We can also describe Riemmanian and formal Kähler structures we defined in
Section 3.5
in terms of implicit representations.

We first observe a relation between the presymplectic form
\tilde{Z}
and the standard symplectic structure on the space of complex valued functions. The expression (
7.6
) of
\tilde{Z}
in
Section 7.2
appears similar to the symplectic structure on
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Z}(\dot{\psi},\ring{\psi})=\int_{M}\operatorname{Re}(\dot{\psi}i\bar{\ring{\psi}})\mu

(7.11)

for
\dot{\psi},\ring{\psi}\in T_{\psi}C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})=C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
, which forms a Kähler structure, together with a Riemannian metric

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}(\dot{\psi},\ring{\psi})=\int_{M}\operatorname{Re}(\dot{\psi}\bar{\ring{\psi}})\mu

(7.12)

and the almost complex structure
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}\colon\dot{\psi}\mapsto i\dot{\psi}
on
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
. In light of this, it is tempting to define a degenerate Riemannian metric
\tilde{B}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
by
\tilde{B}(\cdot,\cdot)=\tilde{Z}(\cdot,\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}\cdot)
. Explicitly,

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{\psi}(\dot{\psi},\ring{\psi})=\int_{\{\psi=0\}}\operatorname{det}{d\psi_{N}^{-1}}\operatorname{Re}(\dot{\psi}\bar{\ring{\psi}})\ d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

(7.13)

Then
(\tilde{B},\tilde{Z},\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i})
is formally a degenerate Kähler structure. At a glance, this setting seems natural and intrinsic as it does not require any Riemannian metric of the ambient manifold unlike the formal Kähler structure
(\mathcal{G},\omega,\mathcal{J})
on the explicit shape space, explained in
Section 3.5
.

However, we emphasize that the triple
(\tilde{B},\tilde{Z},\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i})
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
does not descend onto the base space
\mathcal{O}
.
To see this, first note that the presymplectic form
\tilde{Z}
is by design invariant under the action of
\SemiD_{\gamma}=\Diff_{\gamma}(M)\ltimes C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
on each fiber
\Pi^{-1}\gamma
over
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}
and
\operatorname{ker}\tilde{Z}_{\psi}
agrees with

\displaystyle\operatorname{ker}d\Pi_{\psi}=\{-\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi+\varphi\psi\mid(u,\varphi)\in\diff_{0}(M)\ltimes C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})\text{ s.t. }-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}}=0\}.

(7.14)

Hence
\tilde{Z}
descends onto
\mathcal{O}
and defines the MW symplectic structure
\omega
by
\tilde{Z}=\Pi^{*}\tilde{\omega}
.

However, both
\tilde{B}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}
do not descend onto
\mathcal{O}
, and hence fail to define a Riemannian metric and an almost complex structure on
\mathcal{O}
, as shown in the following example.
In particular,
the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{O}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{O}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{J}}

does not commute.

Example 7.2
.

The almost complex structure
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}
behaves differently at
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
in the same fiber
\Pi^{-1}\gamma
if they are in different conformal classes (
Section 4.2
).

Consider the setting in
Section 4.2
where
\psi_{0}(x,y)=x+iy
and
\psi_{1}(x,y)=x+y+iy
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
so
\Pi\psi_{j}=\delta_{0}
for
j=0,1
. With a fixed vector field
u(x,y)=(1,0)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi_{j}=1
and
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}(\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi_{j})=i\cdot 1=i
for both
j=1,2
.
However, observe that
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}(\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi_{j})=i=\mathcal{L}_{u_{j}}\psi_{j}
with different fixed vector fields
u_{0}(x,y)=(0,1)
and
u_{1}(x,y)=(1,1)
. Namely
d\Pi(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}(\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi_{j}))=d\Pi|_{\psi_{j}}(i)=-\mathcal{L}_{u_{j}}\delta_{0}
for
j=1,2
yield different motions of the zero
i.e.
,
-\mathcal{L}_{u_{0}}\delta_{0}\neq-\mathcal{L}_{u_{1}}\delta_{0}
.

The ill-definedness of
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}
for different conformal classes also affects the degenerate Riemannian structure
\tilde{B}
. For the above
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{det}(d\psi_{j})=\operatorname{det}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=1

(7.15)

for both
j=0,1
with respect to the standard Euclidean metric, and hence
\tilde{B}_{\psi_{j}}(\dot{\psi}_{j},\ring{\psi}_{j})=\operatorname{Re}(\dot{\psi}_{j}\bar{\ring{\psi}}_{j})
. With a vector field
v=(0,1)
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi_{0}=i
and
\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi_{1}=1+i
, namely

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{\psi_{0}}(\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi_{0},\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi_{0})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\left(i\cdot(-i)\right)=1,

(7.16)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{\psi_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi_{1},\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi_{1})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\left((1+i)\cdot(1-i)\right)=2.

(7.17)

Thus neither of
\tilde{\mathcal{J}}_{i}
nor
\tilde{B}
descends to
\mathcal{O}
.

We may still construct a degenerate Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
that descends onto
\mathcal{O}
. Let us define a degenerate metric
\tilde{G}
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
by pulling back the metric
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathcal{O}
defined in
Section 3.5
:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}=\Pi^{*}\mathcal{G}.

(7.18)

Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{\psi}(-\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi+a\psi,-\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi+b\psi)=\mathcal{G}_{\delta_{\gamma}}(-\mathcal{L}_{u}{\delta_{\gamma}},-\mathcal{L}_{v}{\delta_{\gamma}})=\int_{\gamma}\iota_{\hat{v}}\iota_{u}\mu,

(7.19)

for
-\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi+a\psi,-\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi+b\psi\in T_{\psi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}

where
\gamma=\Pi\psi
, and
\hat{v}
is any vector field satisfying
\hat{v}\circ\gamma=\mathcal{J}(v\circ\gamma)
for the almost complex structure
\mathcal{J}\colon T\mathcal{O}\to T\mathcal{O}
defined on
\mathcal{O}
.

The degenerate metric
\tilde{G}
is compatible with the presymplectic form
\tilde{Z}
through the homomorphism
\tilde{J}\colon T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}\to T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{\psi}(-\mathcal{L}_{v}\psi+\varphi\psi)=-\mathcal{L}_{\hat{v}}\psi+i\varphi\psi,

(7.20)

where, again,
\hat{v}
is chosen so that
\hat{v}\circ\gamma=\mathcal{J}(v\circ\gamma)
. In general,
\tilde{J}
may not satisfy
\tilde{J}^{2}=-\operatorname{id}
pointwise, so it may fail to be an almost complex structure on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
. However, the failure is controlled: the difference
\tilde{J}\circ\tilde{J}(\dot{\psi})-(-\operatorname{id})(\dot{\psi})=(\tilde{J}\circ\tilde{J}+\operatorname{id})(\dot{\psi})
lies in
\operatorname{ker}d\Pi|_{\psi}
for any
\dot{\psi}\in T_{\psi}\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
. Thus, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{O}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\mathcal{O}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{J}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{J}}

By construction, both
\tilde{G}
and
\tilde{J}
descend to the shape space
\mathcal{O}
, defining the Riemmanian and the almost complex structure
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{J}
, hence forming a formal Kähler structure
(\mathcal{G},\omega,\mathcal{J})
on
\mathcal{O}
.

Appendix A
Liouville form via the tilde calculus

An alternative approach to prove
Theorem 3.8
is using the so-called tilde calculus presented in
[
HV03
,
Viz11
]
.
The tilde calculus is driven by the tilde operator which takes as input a differential form in a finite-dimensional manifold and outputs another differential form in an infinite-dimensional manifold.

Let
M
be an
m
-dimensional oriented manifold and
S
be
n(<m)
dimensional closed (i.e., compact & boundary-less) oriented manifold.
Let us define the tilde operator

\displaystyle\widetilde{}\ \colon\Omega^{k}(M)

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{k-n}(\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M))

(A.1)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\widetilde{\alpha}

(A.2)

for
k>n
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}_{\gamma}(u_{1}\circ\gamma,\ldots,u_{k-n}\circ\gamma)=\int_{S}\gamma^{*}(i_{u_{k-n}}\ldots i_{u_{1}}\alpha)

(A.3)

where
\gamma\in\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
and
u_{1}\circ\gamma,\ldots,u_{k-n}\circ\gamma\in T_{\gamma}\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M)
with some
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k-n}\in\Gamma(TM)
.

The framework of the tilde calculus allows a simple expression of the Marsden–Weinstein structure:

Example A.1

(The Marsden–Weinstein form)
.

Let
\mu
be a volume form on the ambient manifold
M
and let the dimension of
S
be
n=m-2
. Then the Marsden–Weinstein form (
3.10
) is written as
\tilde{\mu}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathrm{UEmb}(S,M))
. Eplicitly, it is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}_{\gamma}(u\circ\gamma,v\circ\gamma)=\int_{M}\gamma^{*}i_{v}i_{u}\mu.

(A.4)

The tilde calculus has useful properties. In particular, we can use the commutativity between
d
and
\widetilde{}

[
HV03
, Lemma 1]
to show the exactness of the Marsden–Weinstein form.

Proposition A.2
.

We have
d\widetilde{\alpha}=\widetilde{d\alpha}
.

With this, the exactness of the Marsden–Weinstein form
\widetilde{\mu}
is a direct consequence of the exactness of
\mu
.

.

The 1-form
\eta

to

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}=\widetilde{d\nu}=d\widetilde{\nu}.

(A.5)

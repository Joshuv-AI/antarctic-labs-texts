On a class of critical Markov branching processes with non-homogeneous Poisson immigration
Kosto V. Mitov

Abstract
The paper studies a class of critical Markov branching processes with infinite variance of the offspring distribution. The processes admit also an immigration component at the jump-points of a non-homogeneous Poisson process, assuming that the mean number of immigrants is infinite and the intensity of the Poisson process converges to a constant. The asymptotic behavior of the probability for non-visiting zero is obtained. Proper limit distributions are proved, under suitable normalization of the sample paths, depending on the offspring distribution and the distribution of the immigrants.



Key words:
Infinite variance; Limit theorems; Markov branching
process; Non-homogeneous immigration

Introduction

The paper deals with Markov branching processes with immigration in the
time-moments generated by Poisson measure with a local intensity
r(t).
We
consider the critical case when the offspring mean is equal to one, but the
offspring variance is infinite. The distribution of immigrants belongs to
the class of stable laws with infinite mean and

2
Description of the models

A single type Markov branching process can be described as follows. The
particles of a given type evolve independently of each-other, lives random
time
\tau
with exponential distribution function
G(t)=\mathbf{P}\left\{\tau\leq t\right\}=1-e^{-\mu t}
,
t\geq 0,\mu>0,
and at the end of its
life the particle produces random number
\xi\geq 0
of new particles of
the same type. The number of particles
Z(t),t\geq 0,
form the stochastic
process, known as Markov branching process (see
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
, and

[
23
]
). We assume as usually that this evolution started at time
t=0
with one new particle. Denote by
h(s)=\mathbf{E}\left[s^{\xi}\right]
the offspring probability generating function (p.g.f.) and

F(t;s)=\mathbf{E}\left[s^{Z(t)}|Z(0)=1\right],\ \ \ t\geq 0,\ \ s\in[0,1],

the p.g.f. of the process
Z(t)
,
t\geq 0
.

It is well known that (see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F(t;s)}{\partial t}=\mu[h\left(F(t;s)\right)-F(t;s)],

(1)

with initial condition
F(0;s)=s
. Under mild regularity conditions, it is
the only solution of this equation in the class of p.g.f.

Let us now suppose that along the Markov branching process
Z(t)
there is a
sequence of random vectors
\left(S_{k},I_{k}\right)
,
k=0,1,2,\ldots
,
independent of
Z(t)
, where

0=S_{0}<S_{1}<S_{2}<S_{3}<\cdots

are the jump points of an non-homogeneous Poisson process
\nu(t)

independent of
Z(t)
and the random variables
\{I_{k}\}
are i.i.d. with
non-negative integer values. Denote by
r(t)
the intensity of
\nu(t)

with a mean measure
\displaystyle R(t)=\int_{0}^{t}r(u)du
. Let
\displaystyle g(s)=\mathbf{E}\left[s^{I_{k}}\right]
be the p.g.f. of the immigrants.

Assume that at every jump-point
S_{k}
, a random number
I_{k}
of new
particles immigrate into the process
Z(t)
and they participate in the
evolution as the other particles. Let us denote the new process by
Y(t)
.
It can be strictly defined as follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\nu(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{k}}Z^{(k,j)}\left(t-S_{k}\right),\ \displaystyle t\geq 0,

where
\left\{Z^{(k,j)}(t)\right\}
are independent and identical copies of
Z(t)
.

Definition 2.1
.

The process
Y(t)
,
t\geq 0,
is called Markov branching process with
non-homogeneous Poisson immigration (MBPNPI).

The p.g.f.
\Phi(t;s):=\mathbf{E}\left[s^{Y(t)}\right]
of the process
Y(t)

has the following form

\displaystyle\Phi(t;s)=\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{t}r(t-u)(1-g(F(u;s)))du\right\},\ \Phi(t;0)=1.

(2)

The proof is given in
[
26
]
and in the more general multitype case in

[
14
]
.

For the intensity of the Poisson process, we assume additionally the
following condition

\displaystyle\displaystyle r(t)\to\rho>0,\ \ \displaystyle t\to\infty.

(3)

3
Basic assumptions and preliminary results

For the branching mechanism we assume that the offspring p.g.f.

f(s)
has the following representation

\displaystyle f(s)=s+(1-s)^{\gamma+1}L\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right),\ \ \ s\in[0,1),

(4)

where
\gamma\in(0,1]
and
L(.)
is a function slowly varying at infinity
(s.v.f.). Thus, the process
Z(t),t\geq 0,
is critical. If
\gamma<1

the the offspring variance in infinite.

Comment 3.1
.

Let us note that if
\gamma=1
and
L(t)\rightarrow b
then
the offspring variance is finite. The results for this case follows directly
from the corresponding results for the multitype Markov processes with
non-homogeneous Poisson immigration studied in
[

]
. If
\gamma=1

the offspring variance can also be infinite, depending on the properties of
the slowly varying function
L(.)
.

The process has an invariant measure whose p.g.f.

is given by

\displaystyle U(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{du}{f(u)-u},\ \ \ \ 0\leq s\leq 1.

The Kolmogorov backward equation (
1
) can be written as follows

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{F(t;s)}\frac{du}{f(u)-u}=\mu t.

This leads to
U(F(t;s))=U(s)+\mu t
. Denote by

V(x)=U\left(1-\frac{1}{x}\right)=\int_{0}^{1-1/x}\frac{du}{f(u)-u},\ \ \ \ x\geq 1,

and let
W(y)
be the inverse function of
V(x)
. Using the above relations
we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-F(t;s)}=W\left(\mu t+V\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\right),\ \ s\in[0,1),

(5)

Substituting
s=0
in the above equation we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-F(t;0)}=W(\mu t).

(6)

For
V(x)
one has

V(x)=\int_{1}^{x}\frac{u^{\gamma-1}}{L(u)}du,x\geq 1.

So
V(x)
is regularly varying with exponent
\gamma
. Then its inverse

is regularly varying with exponent

, and from (

) we obtain that

\displaystyle 1-F(t;0)\sim t^{-1/\gamma}L_{1}(t),\ \ t\to\infty,

(7)

where

is a slowly varying at infinity function. Let us note that
V(x)
is increasing and
W(y)
is also increasing (see e.g.
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
).

For the p.g.f. of the immigrants we will assume that

\displaystyle g(s)=1-(1-s)^{\alpha}l\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right),\ \ s\in(0,1)

(8)

where
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
l(x)
is a function slowly varying at infinity.

Comment 3.2
.

If
\alpha\in(0,1)
the mean number of immigrants is
infinite. In the case when
\alpha=1
the mean number of immigrants can be
infinite or finite depending on the s.v.f.

. If
\alpha=1
, and

then
E[I_{k}]=m
is finite.

Let us denote

The function

is non decreasing in

. Let us denote by

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\Psi}(x),\ x\geq 1,
its inverse function. It
is also non-decreasing in
[1,\infty)
.

Then
g(s)
can be written in the following form

g(s)=1-\frac{1}{\Psi\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)},\ \ s\in[0,1].

Further for convenience we will denote (see also (
5
))

\displaystyle q(t;s):=1-g(F(t;s))=(1-F(t;s))^{\alpha}l\left(\frac{1}{1-F(t;s)}\right)

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[W\left(\mu t+V\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\right)\right]^{-\alpha}l\left(W\left(\mu t+V\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Psi\left(W\left(\mu t+V\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\right)\right)\right)}.

one gets

I_{2}(t;s(t))=\int_{t-T}^{t}r(t-u)q(u;s(t))du\leq q(t)\int_{0}^{T}r(u)du\rightarrow 0,t\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}I(t;s(t))=\log(1+\lambda^{\gamma})^{\rho C}

which together with (
12
) completes the convergence to the Laplace
transform
\varphi(\lambda)=(1+\lambda^{\gamma})^{-C\rho}
. Since

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}(1-G_{\gamma}(x))dx=\lambda^{-1}(1-\varphi(\lambda))\sim\rho C\lambda^{\gamma-1},\lambda\rightarrow 0,

then by the Tauberian theorem (see
[
6
]
, Ch. XIII,
Theorem 5.4) one obtains the statement of this case.

(iii) Since
q(t;s)\leq q(t)
then
\displaystyle\Delta(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}q(u;s)du<\infty
for
s\in[0,1]
. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be fixed
and

I(t;s)=\int_{0}^{t}r(t-u)q(u;s)du=\int_{0}^{t\delta}+\int_{t\delta}^{t}=I_{1}(t;s)+I_{2}(t;s).

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be fixed. Then for
t
large enough one has

\rho(1-\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t\delta}q(u;s)du\leq I_{1}(t;s)\leq\rho(1+\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t\delta}q(u;s)du.

Therefore

\rho(1-\varepsilon)\Delta(s)\leq\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}I_{1}(t;s)\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}I_{1}(t;s)\leq\rho(1+\varepsilon)\Delta(s).

On the other hand for
I_{2}(t;s)
we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq I_{2}(t;s)=\int_{t\delta}^{t}r(t-u)q(u;s)du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q(t)\int_{0}^{t(1-\delta)}r(u)du\sim q(t)t(1-\delta)\sup_{0\leq x\leq t(1-\delta)}r(x)\rightarrow 0,\ t\rightarrow\infty.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary then we conclude that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}I(t;s)=\rho\Delta(s),
which is equivalent to
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(t,s)=\exp(-r\Delta(s))
(see (
12
)). Then from

\mathbf{E}\left[s^{Y(t)}|Y(t)>0\right]=1-\frac{1-\Phi(t,s)}{1-\Phi(t,0)}

with Theorem
4.1
(ii) one obtains the result.

(iv) Introduce

B(x)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{x}\frac{du}{\Psi(W(u))}\right)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{x}\frac{uq(u/\mu)}{u}du\right).

Since
tq(t)\rightarrow 0,t\rightarrow\infty,
then
B(x)
is slowly
varying at infinity and
B(x)
is nondecreasing. Denote by
\overleftarrow{B}(x)
the inverse function of
B(x)
. Clearly
A(x)=B(V(x)).
Let us consider
the integral (see (
2
) and (
9
))

I(t;s(t))=\int_{0}^{t}r(t-u)q(u;s(t))du,

where
s(t)=\exp(-\lambda/W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))))
for
0<\sigma<1,\ \lambda>0,t\geq 0.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be fixed.
There exists
T>0
such that for every
t\geq T
, one has
\rho(1-\varepsilon)\leq r(t)\leq\rho(1+\varepsilon).
Then

I(t;s(t))=\int_{0}^{t-T}+\int_{t-T}^{t}=I_{1}(t;s(t))+I_{2}(t;s(t)).

For every
t\geq T
one has

\rho(1-\varepsilon)J(t;s(t))\leq I_{1}(t;s(t))\leq\rho(1+\varepsilon)J(t;s(t)),

where (see also (
9
))

\displaystyle J(t;s(t)):=\int_{0}^{t-T}q(u;s(t))du=\int_{0}^{t-T}\frac{du}{\Psi\left[W\left(\mu u+V\left(\frac{1}{1-s(t)}\right)\right)\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\log B\left(\mu u+V\left(\frac{1}{1-s(t)}\right)\right)\right|_{0}^{t-T}=\log\frac{B\left(\mu(t-T)+V\left(\frac{1}{1-s(t)}\right)\right)}{B\left(V\left(\frac{1}{1-s(t)}\right)\right)}.

For
1-s(t)
one has that

1-s(t)=1-\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda}{W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))}\right)\sim\frac{\lambda}{W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))},t\rightarrow\infty,

because
W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))\rightarrow\infty
for every
fixed
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Recall that
V(.)
and
W(.)
are inverse to each
other and
V(.)
varies regularly with exponent
\gamma\in(0,1]
, we get

V\left(\frac{1}{1-s(t)}\right)\sim V\left(\frac{W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))}{\lambda}\right)\sim\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\lambda^{\gamma}},\ t\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore

B\left(V\left(\frac{1}{1-s(t)}\right)\right)\sim B\left(\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\lambda^{\gamma}}\right)\sim\sigma B(t),t\rightarrow\infty,

because
B(x)
is a slowly varying function at infinity. Further we have

B\left(\mu(t-T)+V\left(\frac{1}{1-s(t)}\right)\right)\sim B\left(t-T+\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}\right).

By the mean value theorem one has

B\left(t-T+\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}\right)-B(t)=B^{\prime}(\xi_{t})\left[\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}-T\right],

where
\displaystyle\xi_{t}\in\left(t,t+\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}-T\right).
Having in mind that
\displaystyle B^{\prime}(x)=\frac{B(x)}{\Psi(W(x))}
we get

\displaystyle B\left(t-T+\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}\right)-B(t)=\frac{B(\xi_{t})}{\Psi(W(\xi_{t}))}\left[\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}-T\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{B(t+\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}})}{\Psi(W(t))}\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}=\frac{B(t+\frac{t}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}})}{\Psi(W(t))}\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(t(1+\frac{1}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}))\frac{tq(t/\mu)}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}},

because
\displaystyle\Psi(W(x))=\frac{1}{q(x/\mu)}
,
B(.)
, and resp.
\overleftarrow{B}(.)
are nondecreasing. Therefore,

B(t)\leq B\left(t-T+\frac{\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t))}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}\right)\leq B(t(1+\frac{1}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}))\left(1+\frac{tq(t/\mu)}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}\right),

and for large enough
t
we have

\log\frac{B(t)}{\sigma B(t)}\leq J(t;s(t))\leq\log\frac{B(t(1+\frac{1}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}))(1+\frac{tq(t/\mu)}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}})}{\sigma B(t)}

or

\log\frac{1}{\sigma}\leq J(t;s(t))\leq\log\frac{1}{\sigma}+\log\frac{B(t(1+\frac{1}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}}))(1+\frac{tq(t/\mu)}{\mu\lambda^{\gamma}})}{B(t)}.

Having in mind that
B(t)
is a s.v.f. and
tq(t)\rightarrow 0,t\rightarrow\infty,
we obtain that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}J(t;s(t))=-\log\sigma,

and then

\rho(1-\varepsilon)(-\log\sigma)\leq\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}I_{1}(t;s(t))\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}I_{1}(t;s(t))\leq\rho(1+\varepsilon)(-\log\sigma).

For
I_{2}(t;s(t))
we get

I_{2}(t;s(t))=\int_{t-T}^{t}r(t-u)q(u;s(t))du\leq q(t-T)\int_{0}^{T}r(u)du\rightarrow 0,t\rightarrow\infty.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary it follows that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}I(t;s(t))=-\log\sigma,\ \ \ \sigma\in(0,1).

Therefore (see (
12
))

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(t;s(t))=\sigma,\ \ \ \sigma\in(0,1),

which implies that for every
x>0

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{Y(t)}{W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))}\leq x\right)=\sigma,

that is

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{Y(t)}{W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))}\leq 1\right)=\sigma.

By the following chain of equalities

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{Y(t)}{W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))}\leq 1\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(Y(t)\leq W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(B(V(Y(t)))\leq B(V(W(\overleftarrow{B}(\sigma B(t)))))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(B(V(Y(t)))\leq\sigma B(t)\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{B(V(Y(t)))}{B(t)}\leq\sigma\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{B(V(Y(t)))}{B(V(W(t)))}\leq\sigma\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{A(Y(t))}{A(W(t))}\leq\sigma\right),

we complete the proof.

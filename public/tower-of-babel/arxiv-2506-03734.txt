Supersymmetric localisation of 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,2) theories on a spindle
Imtak Jeon a, Hyojoong Kim b, Nakwoo Kim c, Aaron Poole d, and Augniva Ray b

Abstract
We consider two-dimensional 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,2) supersymmteric field theories living on a weighted projective space 𝕎 ​ ℂ ​ ℙ [ n 1 , n 2 ] 1 \mathbb{WCP}_{[n_{1},n_{2}]}^{1} , often referred to as a spindle. Starting from the spindle solution of five-dimensional minimal gauged supergravity, we construct a two-dimensional 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,2) theory on a spindle, preserving supersymmetry via the anti-twist mechanism and admitting two Killing spinors of opposing R R -charge. We apply the technique of supersymmetric localisation to compute the exact partition function for a theory consisting of an abelian vector multiplet and a chiral multiplet, finding that the path integral localises to a real moduli space of vector multiplet fluctuations. We compute the one-loop determinants via the equivariant index, using both the method of unpaired eigenvalues and the fixed point theorem, finding agreement between the two approaches. We conclude with the explicit example of a charged chiral multiplet in the presence of a Fayet-Iliopoulos term. This work paves the way towards uncovering two-dimensional dualities, such as mirror symmetry, for field theories defined on orbifold backgrounds.

The vector multiplet consists of a vector, two real scalars, two Dirac spinors and an auxiliary real scalar

3.3
Cohomological variables

It is convenient to reorganise the fields into a certain representation of supersymmetry called “cohomological variables”, which we will do for the chiral multiplet. The cohomological variables consist of the

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\{\phi\,,\widetilde{\phi}\}\,,\quad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle Q_{eq}\Phi=\{Q_{eq}\phi\,,Q_{eq}\widetilde{\phi}\}\,,

(55)

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=\{\epsilon\psi\,,\tilde{\epsilon}\widetilde{\psi}\}\,,\qquad\qquad\quad

\displaystyle Q_{eq}\Psi=\{Q_{eq}\left(\epsilon\psi\right)\,,Q_{eq}\bigl(\tilde{\epsilon}\widetilde{\psi}\bigr)\}\,,

3.4
Supersymmetric action on the spindle

In order to use supersymmetric localisation to compute the partition function, we first need to specify the theory that we consider on the spindle geometry. General supersymmetric actions on curved manifolds are discussed in some detail in
Closset:2014pda
and here we will consider a simple case of such an action. Following
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
, we consider a theory of the form

S=S_{\text{v.m.}}+S_{\text{c.m.}}+S_{\text{FI}}+S_{\text{top}}\,,

(61)

where

is the super-renormalizable gauge coupling and the total derivative term which is required in order to make the action supersymmetric is ignored as the spindle does not have a boundary. The bulk Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{\text{bulk}}_{\text{v.m.}}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{F}+\mathrm{i}(G\sigma-H\rho)\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\sigma\partial^{\mu}\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\rho\partial^{\mu}\rho\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{D}-\mathrm{i}(H\sigma+G\rho)\right)^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\tilde{\lambda}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\lambda\\

(62)

where as in
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
we used the field redefinition of

(63)

The second term in (

S_{\text{c.m.}}=\frac{1}{g^{2}_{\text{YM}}}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}\left[\mathcal{L}_{\text{c.m.}}^{\text{bulk}}+D_{\mu}V_{\text{c.m.}}^{\mu}\right]=\frac{1}{g^{2}_{\text{YM}}}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}\mathcal{L}_{\text{c.m.}}^{\text{bulk}}\,,

(64)

\mathcal{L}^{\text{bulk}}_{\text{c.m.}}=D_{\mu}\tilde{\phi}D^{\mu}\phi+M_{\phi}^{2}\tilde{\phi}\phi+\tilde{\mathfrak{F}}\mathfrak{F}-\mathrm{i}\tilde{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi+\tilde{\psi}M_{\psi}\psi-\mathrm{i}\tilde{\psi}\lambda\phi-\mathrm{i}\tilde{\phi}\tilde{\lambda}\psi\,,

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{\phi}^{2}&=\left(\sigma+\frac{r}{2}H\right)^{2}+\left(\rho+\frac{r}{2}G\right)^{2}+\frac{r}{4}R_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}+\mathrm{i}\hat{D}\,,\\

(66a)

The final terms we include are the Fayet-Iliopoulos (FI) and topological terms. The combination of these two terms takes the form

S_{\text{FI}}+S_{\text{top}}=\mathrm{i}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}}\left(-\xi\hat{D}+\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\mathcal{F}\right)\,,

where

General localisation argument

In this paper the main object of interest is the partition function

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{D\varphi}\,e^{-S[\varphi]}

(68)

where
S
is the action of our supersymmetric theory (
60
) and
\varphi
denotes all dynamical fields in the chiral (
46
) and vector (
41
) multiplets. In order to compute this path integral exactly we will make use of the technique of
supersymmetric localisation

Duistermaat:1982vw
;
Atiyah:1984px
;
Witten:1988ze
;
Witten:1988xj
;
Nekrasov:2002qd
;
Pestun:2007rz
, see also the review
Cremonesi:2013twh
.

We first provide a short discussion of the general procedure of localisation. One first considers a
Q_{eq}
-exact deformation of the action in the path integral (
68
)

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}(t)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{D}\varphi\,e^{-S[\varphi]-tQ_{eq}\mathcal{V}}\,,

(69)

where
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a deformation parameter and
\mathcal{V}
is a fermionic quantity which satisfies

Q_{eq}^{2}\mathcal{V}=({\cal L}_{\xi}+\delta_{R}(\Lambda^{R})+\delta_{G}(\Lambda^{\mathcal{G}}_{0}))\mathcal{V}=0\,,

(70)

as well as the bosonic part of the deformation being positive semi-definite

\left.Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}\right|_{\text{bos}}\geq 0\,.

(71)

Using (
70
) and (
71
) we can show that the deformed path integral is in fact independent of
t
. In order to do this, we take a derivative

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}^{\prime}(t)&=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{D}\varphi\,Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}e^{-S[\varphi]-tQ_{eq}\mathcal{V}}\\
&=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{D}\varphi\,Q_{eq}\left(\mathcal{V}e^{-S[\varphi]-tQ_{eq}\mathcal{V}}\right)\\
&=0\,,\end{split}

(72)

where in the second line we used the fact that the theory is supersymmetric
Q_{eq}S=0
together with (
70
) and in the third line we used that fact that the
Q_{eq}
-exact term is a total derivative in the space of fields (the measure is invariant under both
Q
and
Q_{\text{BRST}}
). This
t
independence is also explained by the Ward identity as it implies expectation value of symmetry transformation of an operator is zero, i.e.
\langle Q_{eq}{\cal V}\rangle=0
.

The fact that the partition function is independent of the value of
t
means that we are free to choose any value of
t
, and in particular we may take the limit
t\rightarrow\infty
. Due to the positive semi-definite property (
71
), in this limit the stationary points of
Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}
will dominate the path integral as all other configurations will be exponentially suppressed. These stationary points will form a moduli space known as the
localisation locus
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{\text{loc}}
, of the theory, with the full path integral (
68
) boiling down to an integral over the localisation locus

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}(t)=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{\text{loc}}}\mathcal{D}\varphi\,e^{-S[\varphi]}Z^{Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}}_{1-\text{loop}}[\varphi]\,,

(73)

where
Z^{Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}}_{1-\text{loop}}
is a one-loop super determinant arising from quadratic fluctuations in the fields around each point in
\mathcal{M}_{\text{loc}}
. We will soon see that the zeroes of
Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}
coincide with the stationary points and thus the deformation will vanish on the localisation locus. It is important to note that this result is an
exact
expression for the partition function of our theory - no higher loop terms are present as they are suppressed by positive powers of
t

Cremonesi:2013twh
.

We now need to make a choice of
Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}
which satisfies the properties (
70
) and (
71
) and evaluate the form of the localisation equations that this choice gives. In what follows we will select the ‘canonical’ choice of

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}^{\text{v.m.}}+\mathcal{V}^{\text{c.m.}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}\left[(Q_{eq}\lambda)^{\dagger}\lambda+\tilde{\lambda}(Q_{eq}\tilde{\lambda})^{\dagger}+(Q_{eq}\psi)^{\dagger}\psi+\tilde{\psi}(Q_{eq}\tilde{\psi})^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(74)

where the first two terms on the right are associated with the vector multiplet deformation and the second two terms with the chiral multiplet deformation. Acting with the equivariant supercharge and extracting the bosonic part, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left.Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}\right|_{\text{bos}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}\big[&(Q_{eq}\lambda)^{\dagger}(Q_{eq}\lambda)+(Q_{eq}\tilde{\lambda})(Q_{eq}\tilde{\lambda})^{\dagger}\\
&+(Q_{eq}\psi)^{\dagger}(Q_{eq}\psi)+(Q_{eq}\tilde{\psi})(Q_{eq}\tilde{\psi})^{\dagger}\big]\,,\end{split}

(75)

which manifestly satisfies (
71
) by virtue of being a sum of squares. As this action is a sum of squares, this means that the stationary points coincide with the zeroes of the integrand, and thus the stationary points of the action (
75
) are simply

Q_{eq}\lambda=Q_{eq}\tilde{\lambda}=Q_{eq}\psi=Q_{eq}\tilde{\psi}=0\,.

(76)

Note that the equations above are only the stationary points of the full deformation term
Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}
when one also has

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left.Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}\right|_{\text{ferm}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x\,\sqrt{g}\big[&(Q_{eq}(Q_{eq}\lambda)^{\dagger})\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}(Q_{eq}(Q_{eq}\tilde{\lambda})^{\dagger})\\
&+(Q_{eq}(Q_{eq}\psi)^{\dagger})\psi-\tilde{\psi}(Q_{eq}(Q_{eq}\tilde{\psi})^{\dagger})\big]=0\,,\end{split}

(77)

which is satisfied for

\lambda=\tilde{\lambda}=\psi=\tilde{\psi}=0\,,

(78)

i.e. the vanishing of all fermions. Putting (
76
) and (
78
) together, we see that the localisation locus is given by solutions to the BPS equations, and thus we can write

\mathcal{M}_{\text{loc}}=\{\varphi\>|\>\text{fermions}=0\,,\,Q_{eq}(\text{fermions})=0\}\,,

(79)

and from here on we will refer to the localisation locus as the BPS locus.
We also note that
\left.Q_{eq}\mathcal{V}\right|_{\mathcal{M}_{\text{loc}}}=0
, demonstrating explicitly why such a term does not appear in (
73
).

This concludes the description of the general procedure of supersymmetric localisation which we will apply to our theory (
60
). We will first solve the BPS equations (
76
) and (
78
), before moving on to tackle the more challenging calculation of the one-loop determinant in Section
5
.

4.2
BPS locus

Before solving (

) explicitly, we begin with a discussion of the
U(1)_{\mathcal{G}}
flux through the spindle, noting that the vector multiplet BPS locus will be characterised by such flux. The general formula for quantised

\mathfrak{f}_{\mathcal{G}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}\mathcal{A}=\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(80)

We can parameterise our
U(1)_{\mathcal{G}}
gauge field on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
as

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{z}(y)dz\,,

(81)

because we can always remove the the component
\mathcal{A}_{y}(y)
via a gauge transformation. As discussed in

;
Inglese:2023tyc
this gauge field is the representative of an

orbibundle on
\mathbb{\Sigma}

to be the following

(82)

from which one can see that the gauge-flux quantisation condition (
80
) is satisfied and the relation

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{2}n_{1}-\mathfrak{m}_{1}n_{2}\,,

(83)

must hold. As was pointed out in
Inglese:2023tyc

in terms of
\mathfrak{m}
and additional integers

\mathfrak{m}_{1}=\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{2}=\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}_{2}\,,\qquad 1=\mathfrak{a}_{2}n_{1}-\mathfrak{a}_{1}n_{2}\,,

(84)

where we note that the last equation implies gcd(
\mathfrak{a}_{1},n_{1}
) = gcd(
\mathfrak{a}_{2},n_{2}
) = 1. Furthermore, given a pair
(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\mathfrak{a}_{2})
, the pair
(\mathfrak{a}_{1}+n_{1}\delta\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}_{2}+n_{2}\delta\mathfrak{a})
for
\delta\mathfrak{a}\in\mathbb{Z}
also solves the constraint. We expect physical observables to be independent of
\delta\mathfrak{a}
, which we will see in Section

to indeed be the case for our partition function.
We can perform a gauge transformation of the form

\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{A}_{z}+\beta)dz\,,

(85)

with
\beta
a constant in each patch. We thus obtain a regular gauge field at each pole if we choose

\left.\beta\right|_{\mathcal{U}_{1,2}}=-\frac{2\pi}{\Delta z}\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{1,2}}{n_{1,2}}\,.

(86)

Note that this discussion somewhat parallels the discussion around (
21
) where we considered the gauge transformations of the
U(1)_{R}
field to similar effect.

5
One-loop determinants

5.1
One-loop determinant from equivariant index

We begin by reviewing how the computation of the index relates to the one-loop determinant. In the localisation formulation, the exact one-loop determinant is obtained by computing one-loop for the quadratic action of a choice of
Q_{eq}
-exact action. We first focus on the chiral multiplet and recall the canonical choice for the
Q_{eq}
-exact action given by

\displaystyle Q_{eq}{\cal V}^{\text{c.m.}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\textrm{d}^{2}x~\sqrt{g}~Q_{eq}\left((Q_{eq}\psi)^{\dagger}\psi+(Q_{eq}\widetilde{\psi})^{\dagger}\widetilde{\psi}\right)\,.

(109)

Let us first illustrate how the one-loop determinant is related to a choice of a
Q_{eq}{\cal V}^{\text{c.m.}}
action. We formally rewrite the functional
{\cal V}^{\text{c.m.}}
in terms of the cohomological variables given in (
3.3
) as

\displaystyle{\cal V}^{\text{c.m.}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\textrm{d}^{2}x~\sqrt{g}\Biggl[\bigl(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{eq}\Phi&\quad\Psi\end{array}\bigr)\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}D_{00}&D_{01}\\
D_{10}&D_{11}\end{array}\Biggr)\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi\\
Q_{eq}\Psi\end{array}\Biggr)\Biggr]\,,

for matrix valued differential operators
D_{ij}
. This gives the following

Q_{eq}{\cal V}^{\text{c.m.}}=\int\textrm{d}^{2}x~\sqrt{g}~\Biggl[\bigl(\Phi\quad Q_{eq}\Psi\bigr)\mathcal{K}_{b}\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi\\
Q_{eq}\Psi\end{array}\Biggr)+\bigl(Q_{eq}\Phi\quad\Psi\bigr)\mathcal{K}_{f}\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{eq}\Phi\\
\Psi\end{array}\Biggr)\Biggr]\,,

(116)

where the quadratic kinetic operators are

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}-Q_{eq}^{2}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\Biggr)\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}D_{00}&D_{01}\\
D_{10}&D_{11}\end{array}\Biggr)+\frac{1}{2}\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}D^{\text{T}}_{00}&D^{\text{T}}_{10}\\
D^{\text{T}}_{01}&D^{\text{T}}_{11}\end{array}\Biggr)\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{eq}^{2}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\Biggr)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-Q_{eq}^{2}\end{array}\Biggr)\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}D^{\text{T}}_{00}&D^{\text{T}}_{10}\\
D^{\text{T}}_{01}&D^{\text{T}}_{11}\end{array}\Biggr)-\frac{1}{2}\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}D_{00}&D_{01}\\
D_{10}&D_{11}\end{array}\Biggr)\Biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&Q_{eq}^{2}\end{array}\Biggr)\,.

The above derivation rests on the fact that
Q_{eq}
is a Grassmann odd operator and that the bosonic and fermionic kinetic operators are naturally symmetric and anti-symmetric respectively. From the expression in (
5.1
), we can see that the kinetic operators satisfy

\biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&~0\\
0&-Q_{eq}^{2}\end{array}\biggr)\mathcal{K}_{b}=\mathcal{K}_{f}\biggl(\begin{array}[]{cc}Q_{eq}^{2}&0\\
0&~1\end{array}\biggr)\,.

(135)

Therefore, the one-loop determinant is reduced to the determinant of
Q_{eq}^{2}
over the elementary bosons
\Phi
and fermions
\Psi
as

\displaystyle Z^{Q_{eq}{\cal V}^{\text{c.m.}}}_{1\text{-loop}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\det_{Q_{eq}\Phi\,,\Psi}\mathcal{K}_{f}}{\det_{\Phi,Q_{eq}\Psi}\mathcal{K}_{b}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\text{det}_{Q_{eq}\Psi}Q_{eq}^{2}}{\text{det}_{Q_{eq}\Phi}Q_{eq}^{2}}}\;=\;\sqrt{\frac{\text{det}_{\Psi}Q_{eq}^{2}}{\text{det}_{\Phi}Q_{eq}^{2}}}\,.

(136)

Here, we note that the zero mode sectors,
Q_{eq}^{2}=0
, do not appear in the above result. This is because the determinant of the kinetic operators (
5.1
) on those sectors give
(\det D_{10})^{2}
both for bosons and fermions, and they are exactly cancelled by each other as the
D_{10}
is non-degenerate
GonzalezLezcano:2023cuh
;
Gupta:2015gga
. For the last equality of (
136
) we used the fact that

\mathfrak{a}_{i}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}_{i}+n_{i}\delta\mathfrak{a}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
,
\delta\mathfrak{a}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Physical observables should be invariant under such a shift, a property which was originally discussed below (
84
). We need to perform the sum over
\mathfrak{m}
and a useful technique in doing this is Poisson resummation
Benini:2012ui
:

\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}f(\mathfrak{m})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{f}(n)\,,

(285)

where

\hat{f}(n)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\mathfrak{m})e^{-2\mathrm{i}\pi n\mathfrak{m}}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,,

(286)

essentially the Fourier transform of
f(\mathfrak{m})
.

Using this approach we will try and evaluate the partition function in the most general case, finding that some convenient parameter choices will be helpful in evaluating the final form explicitly. The general formula for
n_{1},n_{2},r,q_{\mathcal{G}}
all included is

\displaystyle\hskip-15.00002ptZ_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=\frac{e^{-\tilde{t}\frac{r}{2}\chi}}{2\pi|q_{\mathcal{G}}|}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dy\,e^{-\mathrm{i}yr\frac{\chi_{-}}{2}+2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin y}\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{m}\frac{q_{\mathcal{G}}y+\theta}{n_{1}n_{2}}-\frac{(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}y)\llbracket q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{m}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}}-\frac{(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}y)\llbracket q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{m}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}}}\,.\quad

(287)

The next step is to remove
\mathfrak{m}_{1,2}
using equation (
84
) and then we can further split
\mathfrak{m}
up using

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}n_{1}n_{2}+\mathfrak{l}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{l}=0\,,\ldots,\,n_{1}n_{2}-1\,,

(288)

which gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}&=\frac{e^{-\tilde{t}\frac{r}{2}\chi}}{2\pi|q_{\mathcal{G}}|}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dy\,\bigg(e^{-\mathrm{i}yr\frac{\chi_{-}}{2}+2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin y}\sum_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(q_{\mathcal{G}}y+\theta)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{l}\frac{q_{\mathcal{G}}y+\theta}{n_{1}n_{2}}-\frac{(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}y)\llbracket q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}}-\frac{(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}y)\llbracket q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}}}\bigg)\,.\end{split}

(289)

We now apply Poisson resummation (
285
) in order to perform the summation over
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}&=\frac{e^{-\tilde{t}\frac{r}{2}\chi}}{q_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dy\,\bigg(e^{-\mathrm{i}yr\frac{\chi_{-}}{2}+2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin y}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta\left(y+\frac{\theta+2\pi n}{q_{\mathcal{G}}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}e^{-\mathrm{i}\mathfrak{l}\frac{q_{\mathcal{G}}y+\theta}{n_{1}n_{2}}-\frac{(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}y)\llbracket q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}}-\frac{(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}y)\llbracket q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}}}\bigg)\,,\end{split}

(290)

and then perform the integral over
y

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}&=\frac{e^{-\frac{r}{2}\left(\tilde{t}\chi-\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{q_{\mathcal{G}}}\chi_{-}\right)}}{q_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}}\sum_{n=n^{*}}^{n^{*}+|q_{\mathcal{G}}|-1}\bigg(e^{-2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin\left(\frac{\theta+2\pi n}{q_{\mathcal{G}}}\right)}\\
&\qquad\times\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}e^{\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}n}{q_{\mathcal{G}}}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}}{n_{2}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}}{n_{1}}\right\rfloor\right)-\frac{(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{q_{\mathcal{G}}})\llbracket q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}}-\frac{(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{q_{\mathcal{G}}})\llbracket q_{\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}}}\bigg)\,,\end{split}

(291)

where
n^{*}
is an integer determined by the constraint

\frac{\theta+2\pi n}{q_{\mathcal{G}}}\in(-\pi,\pi)\,,\,\,\textrm{for}\,\,n\in\{n^{*},n^{*}+1,\ldots n^{*}+|q_{\mathcal{G}}|-1\}\,.

(292)

In order to make some progress in evaluating this term,
henceforth
we set
q_{\mathcal{G}}=1
. This brings the partition function into the form

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{r}{2}\left(\tilde{t}\chi-\mathrm{i}\theta\chi_{-}\right)}e^{-2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin\theta}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}e^{-\left(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}\theta\right)\frac{\llbracket\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}}-\left(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}\theta\right)\frac{\llbracket\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}}}\,,

(293)

and then we use the following decomposition

\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}n_{1}+\mathfrak{o}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}\in\{0,1,\ldots,n_{2}-1\}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{o}\in\{0,1,\ldots,n_{1}-1\}\,,

(294)

under which we see

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{r}{2}\left(\tilde{t}\chi-\mathrm{i}\theta\chi_{-}\right)}e^{-2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin\theta}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}=0}^{n_{2}-1}\sum_{\mathfrak{o}=0}^{n_{1}-1}e^{-\left(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}\theta\right)\frac{\llbracket\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}}{n_{1}}-\left(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}\theta\right)\frac{\llbracket\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{a}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}\rrbracket_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}}}\,,

(295)

where we again made use of (

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{r}{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}\theta}{n_{1}}+\frac{\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}\theta}{n_{2}}\right)}e^{-2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin\theta}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n_{1}-1}e^{-\left(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}\theta\right)\frac{k_{1}}{n_{1}}}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{n_{2}-1}e^{-\left(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}\theta\right)\frac{k_{2}}{n_{2}}}\,,

where in evaluating the summation over

\{\llbracket\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}|\mathfrak{o}=0,\ldots n_{1}-1\}=\{\llbracket\mathfrak{o}\rrbracket_{n_{1}}|\mathfrak{o}=0,\ldots n_{1}-1\}\,,

which is true due to the fact that gcd

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=e^{-\frac{r}{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}\theta}{n_{1}}+\frac{\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}\theta}{n_{2}}\right)}e^{-2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin\theta}\frac{(1-e^{-(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}\theta)})(1-e^{-(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}\theta)})}{(1-e^{-(\tilde{t}-\mathrm{i}\theta)/n_{1}})(1-e^{-(\tilde{t}+\mathrm{i}\theta)/n_{2}})}\,.

in the expression above reduces to

Z_{S^{2}}=Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}\big|_{n_{1}=n_{2}=1}=e^{-\tilde{t}r}e^{-2\mathrm{i}e^{-\tilde{t}}\sin\theta}\,,

(299)

which matches

up to

(

6.2
Vanishing classical contribution and vacuum degeneracy

Our spindle partition function (
298
) allows us to compute some simple subcases, one of which is the
S^{2}
limit of
n_{1,2}\rightarrow 1
as discussed in (
299
). Another simple case is that of vanishing action on the BPS locus

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\frac{\textrm{d}\sigma_{0}}{2\pi}~\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{c}-1}{2}+\frac{r}{4}\chi-\gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\mathfrak{b}}{2}-\left(\frac{\mathfrak{c}-1}{2}+\frac{r}{4}\chi-\gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\right)\right)}\,,

(300)

which can be obtained from (
259
) by setting
\xi=\tilde{t}=\theta=0
, i.e. turning off the FI and theta parameters. We can obtain the result directly from (
298
) (e.g. using by using L’Hôpital’s rule) in order to obtain

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=n_{1}n_{2}\,,

(301)

which seems to count some additional vacuum degeneracy when compared to the
S^{2}
case
Benini:2012ui
and is reminiscent of similar features present found for the three-dimensional
\mathbb{\Sigma}\times S^{1}
partition functions computed in
Inglese:2023tyc
. In order to see the vacua counting more clearly, we find it helpful to go back a few steps and evaluate the contour integral directly from (
283
) by setting
\tilde{t}=\theta=0
and obtain

Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\,dy\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-2\mathrm{i}y\kappa+2\mathrm{i}\sin y\right)\,.

(302)

We recall

2\kappa=\mathfrak{b}-1=\left\lfloor\frac{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}}{n_{2}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}}{n_{1}}\right\rfloor\,,\qquad\mathfrak{a}_{2}n_{1}-\mathfrak{a}_{1}n_{2}=1\,,

(303)

and we can break
\mathfrak{m}
up via

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}n_{1}n_{2}+\mathfrak{l}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\qquad\mathfrak{l}=0,\ldots,n_{1}n_{2}-1\,,

(304)

under which we see

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{b}(\mathfrak{m})-1&=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{2}n_{1}-\mathfrak{a}_{1}n_{2})+\left\lfloor\frac{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{2}-\frac{r}{2}}{n_{2}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{\mathfrak{l}\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{r}{2}}{n_{1}}\right\rfloor\\
&=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{b}(\mathfrak{l})-1\,,\end{split}

(305)

and thus the partition function becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{\mathbb{\Sigma}}&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\,dy\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}\sum_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}y(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{b}(\mathfrak{l})-1)+2\mathrm{i}\sin y\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}=0}^{n_{1}n_{2}-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\,dy\sum_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}y\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}+2\mathrm{i}\sin y\right)\\
&=n_{1}n_{2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\,dy\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(y-2\pi n)\exp\left(2\mathrm{i}\sin y\right)\\
&=n_{1}n_{2}\,.\end{split}

(306)

In the second equality, we used the fact that
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{Z}
in order to absorb an integer shift into the summation over
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
which we relabeled as
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}
. Note that this makes the summation over
\mathfrak{l}
trivial, which thus gives the factor of
n_{1}n_{2}
in the final result.

6.3
Comment on the structure of the partition function

A brief comment is in order about the nature of localisation we have performed here. Clearly, the solutions of the BPS equations, especially as seen from that of the chiral matter in (
99
), suggest that we are doing localisation in the Coulomb branch. Yet, the full partition function in (
298
) separates nicely into the product of two contributions coming from the north and the south pole respectively. This is reminiscent of a similar phenomenon that occurs in
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theory on the round 2-sphere
S^{2}

Benini:2012ui
;
Doroud:2012xw
as well as for the
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell-Chern Simons theory on the squashed sphere
S_{b}^{3}
as shown in
Pasquetti:2011fj
, whereby the partition function admits a block-wise factorisation receiving contributions only from the fixed points. In the 2d case, this was interpreted as Higgs branch localisation, where the contributions were associated with vortices and anti-vortices at the fixed points. Although we have not performed localisation on the Higgs branch - specifically, as seen from the vanishing BPS values of the scalars
(\phi,\tilde{\phi})
in (
99
) - the factorisation of our partition function is suggestive of a similar mechanism.

It would also be interesting to see if one can reproduce (
298
) by performing analogous localisation calculations on a singular disc
\mathbb{D}^{2}

Bah:2021mzw
;
Bah:2021hei
. The factorised form suggests that one may be able to reproduce the same result by gluing the partition functions of two discs (with interior conical singularities of deficit angle
2\pi(1-1/n_{1,2})
respectively) along their respective boundaries. While the general structure of the partition function is suggestive of such a construction, there may be additional subtleties involved due to the additional degrees of freedom that live at the boundary of
\mathbb{D}^{2}

Beem:2012mb
;
Longhi:2019hdh
. Such an undertaking may involve specifying certain boundary conditions for the fields and altering the form of the allowed terms in the action in order to preserve supersymmetry. We leave these explicit checks as future directions to be pursued.

7
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper we have performed the first entirely two-dimensional computation of supersymmetric localisation on an orbifold background, allowing us to compute the full partition function for abelian gauge theories defined on the spindle
\mathbb{\Sigma}
. We obtained our 2d theory, in particular the defining functions
G
and
H
of the Killing spinor equation (
28
), by considering the spindle part of the solution to
D=5
minimal gauged supergravity
Ferrero:2020laf
. This choice means that our analysis is entirely restricted to theories which preserve supersymmetry on the spindle via the
anti-twist
condition (
10
). We considered an abelian theory of vector and chiral multiplets together with an FI term (
60
) and used localisation in order to compute the partition function of such a theory, with our main result being that of a charged chiral multiplet coupled to a dynamical vector multiplet, given in (
298
).

Although our approach in focusing directly on two dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theories is original, we are able to confirm several of our results with those obtained via the dimensional reduction of the three dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2
theories considered in
Inglese:2023tyc
. In particular, the one loop determinants for 2d
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theories on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
were anticipated in equation (3.83) of that paper, a result which precisely matches our equation (
206
). We also note that the 2d results presented there were entirely computed using the spindle index, and in this work we have provided an additional check via computation of the one-loop determinants using both the index and the unpaired eigenvalue method.

While our work is consistent with that of
Inglese:2023wky
;
Inglese:2023tyc
;
Pittelli:2024ugf
, there are several interesting differences in our calculations which may be of note for future work. Firstly, by utilizing the cohomological complex (
3.3
) consisting of both chiral and anti-chiral fields together with the formula for the one-loop determinant (
136
) we are able to entirely fix the phase factors appearing in the one loop determinants for the anti-twisted spindle. Factors of
\sigma_{\pm}=\pm 1
are ignored in their one-loop results, and thus the anti-twist case (
\sigma_{-}=-1
) had an ambiguous sign.

Secondly, and more interestingly, our fields in both vector and chiral multiplets are taken to be entirely real. This is in contrast to
Inglese:2023tyc
, where the fields (including the metric) are allowed to be complex and the BPS locus is also complex. From the perspective of localisation, the complex BPS locus there appears to correspond to a different choice of localisation scheme, as it still satisfies the 3d
\mathcal{N}=2
BPS equations of the form (
79
). One of the main advantages of our real BPS locus is that it is immediately clear how to perform the integration over the moduli space
(\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{R})
of the vector multiplet BPS locus in the partition function (
259
). If the moduli space was taken to be complex then it is less clear how to construct the integration contour, with one possibility being to integrate over the real fluctuations as suggested in that work. Despite these difficulties, it would be interesting to see whether a relaxation of the reality conditions on our fields allows us to reproduce the same results as this work via localisation around a complex saddle, or whether such conditions are not necessary in studying 2d
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theories on the spindle.

There are several generalisations of our work which would be of interest to study in the future. Possibly the most natural direction to take is to perform localisation for the
twisted
spindle, as this work focused solely on the anti-twisted case. One possible way to do this would be to start from the
D=5
spindle solution to the STU model
Ferrero:2021etw
instead of the minimal gauged model, which includes both twist and anti-twist solutions. Alternatively, one could attempt to work even more generically as in
Inglese:2023wky
;
Inglese:2023tyc
;
Pittelli:2024ugf
and consider generic choices of the defining functions in the Killing spinor equation i.e. not inherited from any particular supergravity solution. Additional extensions include those of non-abelian theories with a generic gauge group
\mathcal{G}
(a conceptually straightforward undertaking, as the one-loop determinants are already given in this work), or localisation in the presence of additional multiplets, following along the lines of analogous calculations considered on
S^{2}

Benini:2012ui
;
Doroud:2012xw
;
Gomis:2012wy
. This would pave the way for applications to Calabi-Yau sigma models (where
\sum q_{\mathcal{G}}=0
) with the spindle (somewhat exotically) playing the role of the string worldsheet.

On a related note, it would be interesting to investigate whether the spindle partition function can be used to understand the physics of (orbifold) worldsheet instantons. This idea follows from an analogous construction on
S^{2}

Jockers:2012dk
;
Gomis:2012wy
, where the exact partition function computes the contributions of worldsheet instantons in Calabi-Yau sigma models via Gromov-Witten invariants
Gromov
;
Witten:1988xj
. The study of Gromov-Witten invariants on orbifolds is a somewhat active area
huang2025gromovwitteninvariantsmathbbp1orbifoldstopological
, and it would be interesting to see if similar results can be obtained using suitable extensions of the partition function computed in this work.

The additional extensions mentioned above will be important in the study of two-dimensional supersymmetric dualities
Hori:2000kt
;
Hori:2006dk
(see also
Hori:2002fa
and Section 5 of
Benini:2016qnm
for reviews) involving the spindle partition function. A first duality to explore is the abelian Mirror symmetry of our partition function (
298
), as worked out in
Hori:2000kt
. This duality states that our theory should admit an equivalent “mirror pair” description in terms of a twisted chiral multiplet
\Sigma
as introduced in (
344
) as well as another twisted chiral multiplet
Y
. It was shown that for supersymmetric theories in flat space the mirror theory is generated by a twisted superpotential of the form

\widehat{W}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\left[\Sigma\left(t-\mathrm{i}\theta-q_{\mathcal{G}}Y\right)-\mathrm{i}\mu e^{-Y}\right]\,,

(307)

which can also be extended to supersymmetric theories on curved manifolds e.g. the
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theories on
S^{2}
as considered in
Benini:2012ui
. This duality is checked by evaluating the action for this superpotential (
347
) on the BPS locus and finding equivalence with the partition function of an abelian gauge theory with charged chiral multiplet. It would be natural to attempt to arrive at a similar result for
\mathbb{\Sigma}
, although we state that our first attempt at such a calculation leads us to the conclusion that the
twisted superpotential for the mirror dual to the spindle requires modification
. This is perhaps unsurprising due to the fact that
\mathbb{\Sigma}
is a bad orbifold without a universal cover and thus one would expect the periodicity of the twisted chiral multiplet
Y
to be suitably modified in order to account for such structure. In addition to this, the non-trivial values of the functions which appear in the Killing spinor equation (
28
) make the process of conjecturing the form of the twisted superpotential somewhat subtle. It is possible that the works
Hosomichi:2015pia
;
Okuda:2015yra
;
Hosomichi:2017dbc
may be helpful in developing the notion of mirror symmetry for the spindle. We leave the details of this duality to future work but feel this represents an interesting avenue for further study, potentially opening a window to a wider variety of “orbifold” dualities for supersymmetric field theories.

Finally, it would be interesting to see if there are holographic applications of our spindle partition function, akin to those found in higher dimensions. It is well known that the theories living at the conformal boundary of accelerating
AdS_{4}
black hole solutions
Ferrero:2020twa
;
Cassani:2021dwa
;
Kim:2023ncn
are examples of
\mathcal{N}=2
field theories on
\mathbb{\Sigma}\times S^{1}
and it has recently been shown
Colombo:2024mts
that one can reproduce the accelerating black hole entropy via the large-
N
limit of the spindle index. In the case of
AdS_{3}
/CFT
2
, the situation is far less clear. The dual gravitational description to our
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theory on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
will consist of asymptotically locally
AdS_{3}
solutions with boundary
\mathscr{I}\cong\mathbb{\Sigma}
but examples of such solutions are not explicitly known. They will certainly be different from the known accelerating black holes in three spacetime dimensions
Astorino:2011mw
;
Arenas-Henriquez:2022www
which have
\mathscr{I}\cong S^{1}\times S^{1}_{\text{pinched}}
. It may be of interest to perform a large-
N
calculation analogous to that of
Colombo:2024mts
for the 2d
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theory on
\mathbb{\Sigma}
as a stepping stone in constructing the holographic duals, potentially resulting in additional precision checks of entropy counting and a possible quantum origin of accelerating
AdS_{3}
black hole entropy.

A.2
Gamma matrix conventions

In five dimension with Lorentzian signature, a consistent choice of gamma matrix satisfies the following relations:

\begin{array}[]{llll}\Gamma_{A}^{\dagger}\;=\;-A\Gamma_{A}A^{-1}\,,&~A\;=\;\Gamma_{0}\,,&~A^{\dagger}\;=\;A^{-1}\;=\;-\Gamma_{0}\,,&\\
\Gamma_{A}^{T}\;=\;{\cal{C}}\Gamma_{A}{\cal{C}}^{-1}\,,&~{\cal{C}}^{T}\;=\;-{\cal{C}}\,,&~{\cal{C}}^{\dagger}={\cal{C}}^{-1}\,,&\\
\Gamma_{A}^{*}\;=\;-B\Gamma_{A}B^{-1}\,,&~B^{T}\;=\;{\cal{C}}A^{-1}\,,&~B^{\dagger}\;=\;B^{-1}\,,~~~~B^{*}B\;=\;-1\,.\end{array}

(309)

This is followed by the property, regarding the charge conjugation matrix
{\cal C}

({\cal{C}}\Gamma_{A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}})^{T}=-(-)^{p(p-1)/2}{\cal{C}}\Gamma_{A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}}\,.

(310)

Due to the property of the charge conjugation matrix, we can use the spinor representation satisfying the symplectic-Majorana condition

(\psi^{i})^{\dagger}\gamma_{0}\;=\;\varepsilon_{ij}(\psi^{j})^{T}\mathcal{C}\,,\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad(\psi^{i})^{\ast}=\varepsilon_{ij}B\psi^{j}\,,

(311)

where
i
is an SU(2)
R
index
with
\varepsilon_{ij}
being the SU(2) symplectic metric
\varepsilon_{12}=-\varepsilon_{21}=1
.

Appendix B
Coordinates on the spindle

In this Appendix we demonstrate that the spindle has conical singularities of deficit angle

Here we prove the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum^{k-1}_{l=0}\frac{\omega_{k}^{-\alpha l}}{1-\omega^{l}_{k}u}=\frac{u^{\alpha-k\lfloor\frac{\alpha}{k}\rfloor}}{1-u^{k}}\,,

\alpha
and

are integers,

is defined via

First, let us investigate the behaviour of

\frac{N}{k}

vanishes as the numerator is zero with the denominator being finite. To evaluate the expression when both

are integers, let us assume

for some

and investigate the behaviour of the ratio in the limit

is an integer, i.e.,

. Therefore, we have the identity

&0\quad\textrm{ otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

(368)

(370)

(372)

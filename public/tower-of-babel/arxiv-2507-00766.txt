A tableaux formula for q q -rook numbers
Tirtharaj Basu, Aritra Bhattacharya

Abstract
. We provide a formula for the Garsia-Remmel q q -rook numbers as a sum over standard Young tableaux. We connect our formula with the coefficients in q q -Whittaker expansion of unicellular LLT functions.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 05E05; Secondary: 05A10, 05A30

1.
Introduction

The Garsia-Remmel
q
-rook numbers
R_{k}(\lambda;q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[q]
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
counts the number of ways to place
k
non-attacking rooks on a Ferrers board of a partition
\lambda
with certain
q
weight. We provide a tableaux formula for
R_{k}(\lambda;q)
, which we describe now.

Let
\pi
be a Dyck path of semilength
n
and let
\lambda(\pi)
denote the partition formed by the shape above
\pi
inside the
n\times n
grid. For
1\leq i<j\leq n
let
i<_{\pi}j
if
(i,j)\notin\mathrm{Area}(\pi)
, i.e, the cell
(i,j)
is above the Dyck path
\pi
. The set
\mathrm{SYT}^{\pi}_{\mu}
is the set of standard Young tableaux of shape
\mu
such that if
i
is above
j
in the same column then
i<_{\pi}j
. Let

\displaystyle\gamma(T)=\#\{(b,c)\in\mu\times\mu\,|\,\mathrm{coleg}(b)>\mathrm{coleg}(c)\hbox{ and }(T(c),T(b))\in\mathrm{Area}(\pi)\}

be the number of pairs of boxes
(b,c)
such that
c
is in some row above
b
in the Young diagram (English notation) and
T(c)<T(b)
but
T(c)\nless_{\pi}T(b)
.

We can now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}
and
\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{n}
is such that
\lambda(\pi)=\lambda
. Then for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

R_{k}(\lambda;q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\mu_{1}=n-k\end{subarray}}q^{n(\mu^{\prime})-\#\mathrm{Area}(\pi)}\sum_{T\in\mathrm{SYT}^{\pi}_{\mu}}q^{\gamma(T)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mu\\
\mathrm{coleg}(b)>0\end{subarray}}[\mathrm{arm}_{<_{\pi}T(b)}(\mathrm{up}(b))+1]_{q}.

(1.1)

A more detailed explanation of all the notations used above is given in §
3.6
.

In fact, for
n=N\geq\lambda_{1}+\lambda^{\prime}_{1}
, the above formula only runs over the partition
(N-k,k)
and so (
Proposition 6.3
)

R_{k}(\lambda;q)=q^{|\lambda|-(N-k)k}\sum_{T\in\mathrm{SYT}^{\pi}_{(N-k,k)}}q^{\gamma(T)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mu\\
\mathrm{coleg}(b)>0\end{subarray}}[\mathrm{arm}_{<_{\pi}T(b)}(\mathrm{up}(b))+1]_{q}.

In
[
AN21a
]
,
[
CMP23
]
and
[
RS23
]
relations between
q
-rook numbers and symmetric functions appearing in the Macdonald functions universe are explored. We use the formula above to make yet another such connection.
By using result of
[
GMR+25
]
, we can relate our formula to the coefficients of unicellular LLT functions
\chi_{\pi}(q)
for a Dyck path
\pi
in the basis of
q
-Whittaker functions
(W_{\lambda}(q):\lambda\in\mathrm{Par})
. For
\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{n}
and partitions
\mu\vdash n
, let
c_{\pi,\mu}(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
be defined by

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}(q)=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}(1-q)^{n-\mu_{1}}c_{\pi,\mu}(q)W_{\mu}(q).

Then (
Corollary 5.2
)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\mu_{1}=n-k\end{subarray}}q^{n(\mu^{\prime})-\#\mathrm{Area}(\pi)}c_{\pi,\mu}(q)=R_{k}(\lambda(\pi);q).

The recent paper
[
KLY25
]
obtains another proof of the above identity.

Based on the formula for
e

[
AN21b

q
-rook number of certain partitions to the

-expansion coefficients in

2.5.
Partitions

The set of all integer partitions is denoted by
\mathrm{Par}
. We think of the Young diagram in the English convention, as in Macdonald’s book
[
Mac95
]
, and follow Macdonald’s definition and convention throughout the paper concerning partitions. In particular, for a partition
\lambda
, its conjugate is denoted
\lambda^{\prime}
, the weighted size

n(\lambda^{\prime})=\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{\lambda_{i}}{2}.

The
\mathrm{arm},\mathrm{leg},\mathrm{coarm},\mathrm{coleg}
of a box in the Young diagram is denoted
a,l,a^{\prime},l^{\prime}
respectively in
[
Mac95
]
. In particular, the cell co-ordinates of a box
b
equals
(\mathrm{coleg}(b)+1,\mathrm{coarm}(b)+1)
and

\mathrm{arm}(b)+\mathrm{coarm}(b)+1=\lambda_{\mathrm{coleg}(b)+1},\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad\mathrm{leg}(b)+\mathrm{coleg}(b)+1=\lambda^{\prime}_{\mathrm{coarm}(b)+1}.

For a box
b\in\lambda
, we denote by
\mathrm{up}(b)
the box directly above it in the previous row, if
\mathrm{coleg}(b)>0
. So,

\mathrm{coarm}(\mathrm{up}(b))=\mathrm{coarm}(b)\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad\mathrm{coleg}(\mathrm{up}(b))+1=\mathrm{coleg}(b).

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}
. Denote by
\lambda\pm\varepsilon_{i}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_{i}\pm 1,\lambda_{i+1},\ldots)
the composition obtained by adding or removing a box in the
i
th row of
\lambda
. If
\lambda_{i-1}>\lambda_{i}
then
\lambda+\varepsilon_{i}\in\mathrm{Par}
and if
\lambda_{i}>\lambda_{i+1}
then
\lambda-\varepsilon_{i}\in\mathrm{Par}
.

3.
q
rook numbers

In this section, we recall the definition and recursion of
q
-rook numbers as defined by Garsia and Remmel in
[
GR86
]
. We then provide a proof of our main result, a standard tableaux formula for the
q
-rook numbers.

3.1.
Definition of
q
-rook numbers

Given a partition
\lambda
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, a
rook placement
with
k
rooks on
\lambda
is the the number of ways to select
k
cells called
rooks
from the Young diagram of
\lambda
, such that no two rooks lie in the same row or column. Denote the set of rook placements with
k
rooks on
\lambda
by
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\lambda)
. Given such a rook placement
C\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(\lambda)
,
[
GR86
]
defines
\mathrm{inv}(C)
to be the number of cells remaining after cancelling all the cells in the same column above and in the same row to the left of the rooks. Then

R_{k}(\lambda;q)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(\lambda)}q^{\mathrm{inv}(C)}.

(3.1)

Figure 2
gives an example of a rook placement with the
\mathrm{inv}
statistics.

\bullet
\bullet
\bullet

Figure 2
.
C\in\mathcal{C}_{3}((6,4,4,2,1))
with
\mathrm{inv}(C)=6

Since conjugation interchanges cells in the same column above with cells in the same row to the left of a given cell,

R_{k}(\lambda;q)=R_{k}(\lambda^{\prime};q).

(3.2)

Because two rooks can not lie in the same row or in the same column,
R_{k}(\lambda;q)=0
for
k>\lambda_{1}
or
k>\ell(\lambda)
.

3.2.
Recursions for
q
-rook numbers

For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)\in\mathrm{Par}
, let
\widetilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)
be the partition obtained by removing the first row. The
q
-rook numbers
R_{k}(\lambda;q)
for
0\leq k\leq\lambda_{1}
are determined by the recursions
[
GR86
, Theorem 1.1]

\displaystyle R_{k}(\lambda;q)=q^{\lambda_{1}-k}R_{k}(\widetilde{\lambda};q)+[\lambda_{1}-k+1]_{q}R_{k-1}(\widetilde{\lambda};q),

(3.3)

with initial conditions
R_{0}(\lambda;q)=q^{|\lambda|}
.

3.3.
q
-Stirling numbers

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\rho_{n}=(n-1,n-2,\ldots,1,0)
be the staircase partition. Then

R_{n-k}(\rho_{n};q)=S_{q}(n,k)\qquad\hbox{ for }\ 0\leq k\leq n

(3.4)

are the
q
-Stirling numbers of second kind
[
GR86
, (I.9)]
. They satisfy the recursions

S_{q}(n,k)=q^{k-1}S_{q}(n-1,k-1)+[k]_{q}S_{q}(n-1,k)\hbox{ for }0\leq k\leq n,

and
S_{q}(0,0)=1
,
S_{q}(n,k)=0
for
k<0
or
k>n
.

Rectangular

-rook numbers

a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
0\leq i\leq\min(a,b)
.
[
CMP23
, Proposition 2.15]
gives

R_{i}((b^{a});q)=q^{(a-i)(b-i)}\dfrac{[a]_{q}!}{[a-i]_{q}!}{b\brack i}_{q}

3.6.
Tableaux formula for
q
-rook numbers

Let
\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\mu\vdash n
. Let
T\in\mathrm{SYT}^{\pi}_{\mu}
. Recall that

\displaystyle\gamma(T)=\#\{(b,c)\in\mu\times\mu\,|\,\mathrm{coleg}(b)>\mathrm{coleg}(c)\hbox{ and }(T(c),T(b))\in\mathrm{Area}(\pi)\},

(3.7)

and for a box
b\in\mu
, let

\displaystyle\gamma(T,b)=\#\{c\in\mu\,|\,\mathrm{coleg}(b)>\mathrm{coleg}(c)\hbox{ and }(T(c),T(b))\in\mathrm{Area}(\pi)\},

(3.8)

then

\gamma(T)=\sum_{b\in\mu}\gamma(T,b).

For a box
b\in\mu
, denote by
\mathrm{arm}_{<_{\pi}j}(b)
the number of boxes
c
in the right of
b
in the same row such that
T(c)<_{\pi}j
, i.e,

\displaystyle\mathrm{arm}_{<_{\pi}j}(b)=\#\{c\in\mu\,|\,\mathrm{coleg}(c)=\mathrm{coleg}(b),\,\mathrm{coarm}(c)>\mathrm{coarm}(b),\,T(c)<_{\pi}j\}.

(3.9)

Let

\mathrm{wt}(T;q)=q^{n(\mu^{\prime})-\#\mathrm{Area}(\pi)+\gamma(T)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mu\\
\mathrm{coleg}(b)>0\end{subarray}}[\mathrm{arm}_{<_{\pi}T(b)}(\mathrm{up}(b))+1]_{q},

(3.10)

where recall that
\#\mathrm{Area}(\pi)
is the number of cells below
\pi
strictly above the diagonal and
n(\mu^{\prime})=\sum_{i}\binom{\mu_{i}}{2}
.

We restate
Theorem 1.1
from the introduction here.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}
and
\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{n}
is such that
\lambda(\pi)=\lambda
. Then for

with respect to

with respect to

, where

For the path

. For example, the sets

Lemma 6.1

7.3.
Multiplicative and modular

Lemma 7.3
.

Define
G:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{Z}[q]
by

G(\pi)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}[n-\lambda(\pi)_{j}-j+1]_{q}\hbox{ for }\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{n}.

Then
G
is multiplicative and satisfies the modular law.

Proof.

For showing that
G
is multiplicative, let
\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{n},\eta\in\mathbb{D}_{m}
and
\pi\cdot\eta
denotes the concatenation of those two Dyck paths. Then

\lambda(\pi\cdot\eta)_{j}=\begin{cases}\lambda(\pi)_{j}&\hbox{ for }1\leq j\leq n,\\
\lambda(\eta)_{j-n}+n&\hbox{ for }n+1\leq j\leq m.\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle G(\pi)\cdot G(\eta)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}[n-\lambda(\pi)_{i}-i+1]_{q}.\prod_{j=1}^{m}[m-\lambda(\eta)_{j}-j+1]_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}[n+m-\lambda(\pi)_{i}-(i+m)+1]_{q}.\prod_{j=1}^{m}[m+n-(\lambda(\eta)_{j}+n)-j+1]_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m+n}[n+m-\lambda(\pi\cdot\eta)_{j}-j+1]_{q}=G(\pi\cdot\eta).

Hence,
G
is multiplicative.

Let
\pi^{0},\pi^{1}.\pi^{2}
be Dyck paths satifying conditions
(1)
or
(2)
of modular law of
[
AN21a
, Definition 2.1]
with
\lambda(\pi^{(i)})=\lambda^{(i)}
for
i\in\{0,1,2\}
. Using the transformation given by §
2.8
, one of the following is true

(1)

\lambda^{(0)}=\lambda^{(1)}+\varepsilon_{s},\hbox{ and }\lambda^{(2)}=\lambda^{(1)}-\varepsilon_{s},
for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

or,

(2)

\lambda^{(2)}=\lambda^{(1)}-\varepsilon_{r},\,\lambda^{(0)}=\lambda^{(1)}+\varepsilon_{r+1},\,\hbox{ and }\lambda^{(1)}_{r}-\lambda^{(1)}_{r+1}=1
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,

To show that modular law holds we need to show that for any three Dyck paths
\pi^{0},\pi^{1},\pi^{2}
with associated partitions
\lambda^{(0)},\lambda^{(1)},\lambda^{(2)}
satisfying
(1)
or
(2)
as above,

(1+q)G(\pi^{(1)})=qG(\pi^{(0)})+G(\pi^{(2)}).

Both of these cases are proved by using

(1+q)[a]_{q}=q[a-1]_{q}+[a+1]_{q},\qquad\hbox{ for }a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

(7.6)

Assume that condition
(1)
is true. In this case, since the difference is only on one component
s
, taking

a=n-\lambda^{(1)}_{s}-s+1

gives

n-\lambda^{(0)}_{s}-s+1=a-1,\hbox{ and }n-\lambda^{(2)}_{s}-s+1=a+1,

so using (
7.6
) shows that modular law holds true in this case.

Next, assume that condition
(2)
is true. Let

a=n-\lambda^{(1)}_{r}-r+1=n-\lambda^{(1)}_{r+1}-(r+1)+1\hbox{ and }b=\prod_{j\neq r,r+1}[n-\lambda^{(1)}_{j}-j+1]_{q}.

Then using

\displaystyle n-\lambda^{(2)}_{r}-r+1=n-(\lambda^{(1)}_{r}-1)-r+1=a+1,

\displaystyle n-\lambda^{(2)}_{r+1}-(r+1)+1=n-\lambda^{(1)}_{r+1}-r=n-\lambda^{(1)}_{r}-r+1=a,

\displaystyle n-\lambda^{(0)}_{r}-r+1=n-\lambda^{(1)}_{r}-r+1=a,

\displaystyle n-\lambda^{(0)}_{r+1}-(r+1)+1=n-(\lambda^{(1)}_{r+1}+1)-(r+1)+1=a-1,

we get,

\displaystyle qG(\pi^{(0)})+G(\pi^{(2)})=b\cdot([a+1]_{q}[a]_{q}+q[a]_{q}[a-1]_{q})=b\cdot(1+q)[a]_{q}[a]_{q}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\neq r,r+1}[n-\lambda^{(1)}_{j}-j+1]_{q}\cdot(1+q)[n-\lambda^{(1)}_{r}-r+1]_{q}[n-\lambda^{(1)}_{r+1}-(r+1)+1]_{q}

\displaystyle=(1+q)G(\pi^{(1)}).

Hence,
G
satisfies the modular law.

∎

8.1.
A formula for the
q
-Stirling numbers

Recall from (

) that the
q
-Stirling numbers of second kind are
S_{q}(n,k)=R_{n-k}(\rho_{n};q)
, where
\rho_{n}=(n-1,\ldots,0)
is the staircase partition.
Theorem 3.1
in this case becomes a sum over the usual standard Young tableaux.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the
q
-Stirling numbers of second kind has the formula

S_{q}(n,k)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\mu_{1}=k\end{subarray}}q^{n(\mu^{\prime})}\sum_{T\in\mathrm{SYT}_{\mu}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mu\\
\mathrm{coleg}(b)>0\end{subarray}}[\mathrm{arm}_{<T(b)}(\mathrm{up}(b)+1)]_{q}.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
\lambda=\rho_{n}=(n-1,n-2,\ldots,1,0)
. Let
\pi=\pi(\lambda)\in\mathbb{D}_{n}
. Then
\#\mathrm{Area}(\pi)=0
and
i,j\in[n]
,
i<_{\pi}j
if and only if
i<j
. So for
\mu\vdash n
,
\mathrm{SYT}^{\pi}_{\mu}=\mathrm{SYT}_{\mu}
and for
T\in\mathrm{SYT}_{\mu}
and
b\in\mu
,
\gamma(T,b)=0
. Then

\mathrm{wt}(T;q)=q^{n(\mu^{\prime})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mu\\
\mathrm{coleg}(b)>0\end{subarray}}[\mathrm{arm}_{<T(b)}(\mathrm{up}(b))+1]_{q},

so
Theorem 3.1
gives the result.

∎

8.2.
Matrix counting over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

Let
\pi\in\mathbb{D}_{n}
and
\lambda=\lambda(\pi)
,

be the number of

\mathbb{F}_{q}
of rank

\pi
. Then by
[

, Theorem 1]

P_{k}(\pi;q)=(q-1)^{k}q^{|\lambda|-k}R_{k}(\lambda;q^{-1})=(1-q^{-1})^{k}q^{|\lambda|}R_{k}(\lambda;q^{-1}).

Then using
Corollary 5.2
,

P_{k}(\pi;q)=q^{|\lambda|}(1-q^{-1})^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\mu_{1}=n-k\end{subarray}}\widetilde{c}_{\pi,\mu}(q)=q^{|\lambda|}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\vdash n\\
\mu_{1}=n-k\end{subarray}}[\widetilde{W}_{\mu}(q)]\widetilde{\chi}_{\pi}(q),

(8.1)

where
[\widetilde{W}_{\mu}(q)]\widetilde{\chi}_{\pi}(q)
denotes the coefficient of
\widetilde{W}_{\mu}(q)
in the
\widetilde{W}
-expansion of
\widetilde{\chi}_{\pi}(q)
.

Since

is the total number of matrices such that all non-zero entries lie above

, we get

\sum_{\mu\vdash n}[\widetilde{W}_{\mu}(q)]\widetilde{\chi}_{\pi}(q)=1.

Constant sensitivity on the CDAWGs
Rikuya Hamai, Hiroto Fujimaru, Shunsuke Inenaga

Abstract
Compact directed acyclic word graphs ( CDAWGs ) [Blumer et al. 1987] are a fundamental data structure on strings with applications in text pattern searching, data compression, and pattern discovery. Intuitively, the CDAWG of a string T T is obtained by merging isomorphic subtrees of the suffix tree [Weiner 1973] of the same string T T , and thus CDAWGs are a compact indexing structure. In this paper, we investigate the sensitivity of CDAWGs when a single character edit operation is performed at an arbitrary position in T T . We show that the size of the CDAWG after an edit operation on T T is asymptotically at most 8 times larger than the original CDAWG before the edit.



1
Introduction

Compact directed acyclic word graphs
(
CDAWGs
)
[
5
]
are a fundamental data structure on strings with applications in text pattern searching, data compression, and pattern discovery.
Intuitively, the CDAWG of a string
T
(denoted
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
) is a minimal partial DFA that is obtained by merging isomorphic subtrees of the suffix tree
[
25
]
of the same string
T
.
CDAWGs permit pattern matching in optimal
O(m+occ)
time
for a pattern
P
of length
m
when
P
occurs
occ
times in
T
.
In practice, CDAWGs enjoy applications in natural language processing
[
24
]
and in analysis of text generated by language models (LMs)
[
18
]
.
In a more theoretical perspective, CDAWGs are used as a space-efficient data structure that allows for optimal-time detection of “unusual words” (such as minimal absent words (MAWs)
[
7
]
and minimal unique substrings (MUSs)
[
11
]
) from the input string
[
3
,
15
,
19
]
.

A substring
w
of
T
which occurs at least twice in
T
is said to be a
maximal repeat
of
T
,
if extending
w
to the left or to the right in
T
decreases
the number of occurrences in
T
.
It is known that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the internal nodes of
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
and the maximal repeats in
T
.
Also, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the out-edges of
the internal nodes of
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
and
the right-extensions of the maximal repeats in
T
.
Let
\mathsf{e}
denote the size (i.e. the number of edges) of
the CDAWG for the input string.
It is known that
\mathsf{e}\leq 2n-2
holds
[
5
]
for any strings of length
n
,
and
\mathsf{e}
can be much smaller for some highly repetitive strings:

\mathsf{e}\in\Theta(\log n)
holds for
Fibonacci words, Standard Sturmian words, and Thue-Morse words of length
n

[
22
,
2
,
20
]
.
This contrasts to the suffix tree and the (uncompacted) directed acyclic word graph (DAWG)
[
4
]
each requiring
\Theta(n)
space
for any string of length
n
.
CDAWGs can thus be regarded as a compressed text indexing structure
which can be stored in
O(\mathsf{e})
space
[
3
,
12
]
without explicitly storing the string.
In addition, a grammar compression of size
O(\mathsf{e})

based on the CDAWG exists
[
3
]
.

The
sensitivity
of string compressors,
first proposed by Akagi et al.
[
1
]
,
measures how much a single-character-wise edit operation on the input string
can increase the size of the compressed string,
which is formalized as follows:
Let
C
be a compression algorithm and let
C(T)
denote
the size of the output of
C
applied to the input string
T
.
The worst-case
multiplicative sensitivity
of
C

is defined by

\max_{T\in\Sigma^{n},T^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{n^{\prime}}}\{C(T^{\prime})/C(T):\mathsf{ed}(T,T^{\prime})=1\},

where
\mathsf{ed}(T,T^{\prime})
denotes the edit distance between
T
and
T^{\prime}
,

n^{\prime}=n
for substitutions,
n=n+1
for insertions,
and
n^{\prime}=n-1
for deletions.
This is a natural measure for the robustness of compression algorithms
in terms of errors and/or dynamic changes occurring in the input string.
Such errors and dynamic changes are common in real-world scenario including
DNA sequencing and versioned document maintenance.

Following the earlier work of Lagarde and Perifel
[
17
]

and Akagi et al.
[
1
]
,
string compressors and repetitiveness measures can be categorized into three classes:

(A) Stable:

Those whose sensitivity is
O(1)
;

(B) Changeable:

Those whose sensitivity is
\mathrm{polylog}(n)
;

(C) Catastrophic:

Those whose sensitivity is
O(n^{c})
with some constant
0<c\leq 1
.

For instance, it is shown in
[
1
]
that
Class (A) includes
the substring complexity
[
16
]
,
the smallest macro scheme
[
23
]
,
the Lempel-Ziv 77 families
[
26
,
23
]
,
and the smallest grammar
[
21
,
6
]
.
On the other hand,
Class (B) includes run-length Burrows-Wheeler transform (RLBWT)
[
1
,
9
,
10
]
,
and the Lempel-Ziv 78
[
27
]
belongs to Class (C)
[
17
]
.

The focus of this present article is to analyze the sensitivity of CDAWGs.
In case where the edit operation to the string
T
is performed at either end of
T
,
then the multiplicative sensitivity of CDAWGs is known to be asymptotically at most
2
, and it is tight
[
13
,
8
]
.
However, the general case with an arbitrary single-character-wise edit on
T
was not well understood for CDAWGs.
In this paper, we prove that any edit operation at an arbitrary position
on the string can increase the size of the CDAWG asymptotically
at most 8 times larger
than the original, implying that CDAWGs belong to Class (A).
We emphasize that the only known upper bound
for the sensitivity of CDAWGs is
O(n/\log n)
,
which trivially follows since
\mathsf{e}\in O(n)
and
\mathsf{e}\in\Omega(\log n)
for any string of length
n

[
5
,
3
]
.
Our technique for proving the constant sensitivity of CDAWGs is purely combinatorial, which involves new and original ideas that were not present in the special case of left/right-end edits
[
13
,
8
]
.
Also, all our arguments hold without a common sentinel symbol
\$
at the right-end of strings.

Although the CDAWG size
\mathsf{e}
is regarded as a weak string repetitiveness measure
(as
\mathsf{e}\in\Theta(n)
for string
\mathtt{a}^{n-1}\mathtt{b}

[
5
]
, and reversing the string can increase
\mathsf{e}
by a factor of
O(\sqrt{n})

[
14
]
for some string),
our result shows another virtue of CDAWGs being stable in terms of sensitivity and thus being robust against errors and edits.
To our knowledge, CDAWGs are the first compressed indexing structure
proven to achieve
O(1)
multiplicative sensitivity.

2.1
Strings

.

For string

,

,

, and

are called a

,

,
and
suffix
of
T
, respectively.
The sets of prefixes, substrings, and suffixes of string
T
are denoted by

\mathsf{Prefix}(T)
,
\mathsf{Substr}(T)
, and
\mathsf{Suffix}(T)
, respectively.
For a string

of length

,

denotes the

th character of
T

for
1\leq i\leq n
,
and
T[i..j]=T[i]\cdots T[j]

and ends at position

on

.

2.2
Maximal substrings and maximal repeats

A substring
w\in\mathsf{Substr}(T)
of
T
is said to be

left-maximal
if (1)
w\in\mathsf{Prefix}(T)

or (2) there exist two distinct characters
a,b\in\Sigma
such that

aw,bw\in\mathsf{Substr}(T)
,
and it is said to be
right-maximal

if (1)
w\in\mathsf{Suffix}(T)
or (2) there exist two distinct characters
a,b\in\Sigma
such that
wa,wb\in\mathsf{Substr}(T)
.
These substrings

that occur at least twice in
T

are also called
left-maximal repeats

and
right-maximal repeats
in
T
, respectively.

Let
\mathsf{LeftM}(T)
and
\mathsf{RightM}(T)
denote the sets of left-maximal
and right-maximal substrings in
T
.
Let
\mathsf{M}(T)=\mathsf{LeftM}(T)\cap\mathsf{RightM}(T)
.
The elements in
\mathsf{M}(T)
are called
maximal substrings
in
T
,
and the elements in
\mathsf{M}(T)\setminus\{T\}
are called

maximal repeats
in
T
.
A character
a\in\Sigma
is said to be
a
right-extension
of a maximal repeat
w
of
T

if
wa\in\mathsf{Substr}(T)
.

For any substring
w
of a string
T
,
we define its
left-representation
and
right-representation

by
\mathsf{lexp}_{T}(w)=\alpha w
and
\mathsf{rexp}_{T}(w)=w\beta
,
where
\alpha,\beta\in\Sigma^{*}
are the shortest strings
such that
\alpha w
is left-maximal in
T

and
w\beta
is right-maximal in
T
, respectively.

2.3
CDAWGs

The
compact directed acyclic word graph
(
CDAWG
) of a string
T
,
denoted
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
,
is the minimal DFA that recognizes all substrings of
T
,
in which each transition (edge) is labeled by a non-empty substring of
T
.

\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
has a unique source that represents the empty string
\varepsilon

and a unique sink that represents
T
.
All the other internal nodes represent the maximal repeats in
T
,
namely, the set of the longest strings represented by the nodes of
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
are equal to
\mathsf{M}(T)
.
See Figure
1
for a concrete example of CDAWGs.

The
size
of
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
for a string
T
of length
n

is the number
\mathsf{e}(T)
of edges in
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
,
which is equal to the number of right-extensions of maximal repeats in
T
.

In what follows, we will identify
maximal substrings with CDAWG nodes,
and right-extensions of maximal repeats with CDAWG edges,
respectively.

For each
x\in\mathsf{M}(T)
,
let
\mathsf{d}_{T}(x)
denote the number of out-edges of of node
x
in
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
.
It is clear that
\mathsf{e}(T)=\sum_{x\in\mathsf{M}(T)}\mathsf{d}_{T}(x)
.

Figure 1:
Illustration for
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
of string
T=\mathrm{(ab)^{2}c(ab)^{2}d}
. The longest strings represented by the nodes of
\mathsf{CDAWG}(T)
are the maximal substrings in
\mathsf{M}(T)=\{\mathrm{\varepsilon,ab,(ab)^{2},(ab)^{2}c(ab)^{2}d}\}
.

2.4
Sensitivity of CDAWG size and our results

Using the measure
\mathsf{e}
, we define
the worst-case multiplicative
sensitivity
of the CDAWG
with edit operations (resp. insertion, deletion, and substitution) by:

\displaystyle\mathsf{MS}_{\mathrm{Ins}}(\mathsf{\mathsf{e}},n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{T\in\Sigma^{n},T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}(T,1)\cap\Sigma^{n+1}}\{\mathsf{e}(T^{\prime})/\mathsf{e}(T)\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{MS}_{\mathrm{Del}}(\mathsf{e},n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{T\in\Sigma^{n},T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}(T,1)\cap\Sigma^{n-1}}\{\mathsf{e}(T^{\prime})/\mathsf{e}(T)\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{MS}_{\mathrm{Sub}}(\mathsf{e},n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{T\in\Sigma^{n},T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}(T,1)\cap\Sigma^{n}}\{\mathsf{e}(T^{\prime})/\mathsf{e}(T)\}.

We will prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

For any string
T
of length
n
,

\mathsf{MS}_{\mathrm{Ins}}(\mathsf{\mathsf{e}},n)\leq(8\mathsf{e}+4)/\mathsf{e}
,

\mathsf{MS}_{\mathrm{Del}}(\mathsf{\mathsf{e}},n)\leq(8\mathsf{e}+4)/\mathsf{e}
,

\mathsf{MS}_{\mathrm{Sub}}(\mathsf{\mathsf{e}},n)\leq(8\mathsf{e}+4)/\mathsf{e}
hold,
where
\mathsf{e}=\sum_{x\in\mathsf{M}(T)}\mathsf{d}_{T}(x)
.

Our proof for Theorem
1

handles all cases of insertions, deletions, and substitutions.

3
Occurrences of maximal repeats crossing the edited position

To present an upper bound for the sensitivity of CDAWG size,
it is essential to consider new occurrences of maximal repeats
that contain or touch the edited position

.
In this section, we introduce several new definitions regarding

For ease of discussions, we sometimes identify an interval where

3.1
Crossing occurrences

\displaystyle S_{x_{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\varepsilon&\mbox{if $x_{L}$ touches $i$ from left,}\\

\displaystyle\begin{cases}T^{\prime}[j^{\prime}..i-1]&\mbox{if $x_{L}$ touches $i$ from left or contains $i$,}\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\varepsilon&\hskip 17.07164pt\mbox{if $x_{L}$ touches $i$ from left,}\\
T^{\prime}[i..k^{\prime}]&\hskip 17.07164pt\mbox{if $x_{L}$ contains $i$ or touches $i$ from right.}\end{cases}

3.2
New/Existing maximal repeats

In Definitions
3
and
4
below, we introduce
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{Q}
which are respectively
the sets of new maximal repeats and existing maximal repeats in
T^{\prime}
,
such that
\mathsf{N}\cup\mathsf{Q}=\mathsf{M}(T^{\prime})\setminus\{T^{\prime}\}
.
We then partition each of them into smaller subsets that are suitable for our needs.

Definition 3
.

Let

\mathsf{N}=(\mathsf{M}(T^{\prime})\setminus\mathsf{M}(T))\setminus\{T^{\prime}\}

denote the set of new maximal repeats in
T^{\prime}
.
We divide
\mathsf{N}
into the three following disjoint subsets:

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}\cap\mathsf{RightM}(T),

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}\cap\mathsf{LeftM}(T),

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{N}\setminus(\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{2}}).

Further, we divide
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3}}
into two disjoint subsets
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3B}}
and
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
as follows:

\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3B}}
is the set of strings
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3}}
such that
(1)
x
is of Type
\rm{(v)}
, and
(2) there is no other right-extension of
x
in

than the right-extension(s) of the crossing occurrence(s) of

,
and
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}=\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3}}\setminus\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3B}}
.

We note that the following properties hold for

and
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3}}
by definition:

•

.

•

because

.

•

.

Definition 4
.

Let

\mathsf{Q}=(\mathsf{M}(T^{\prime})\cap\mathsf{M}(T))\setminus\{T^{\prime}\}

denote
the set of existing maximal repeats in
T^{\prime}
.
We divide
\mathsf{Q}
into the two following subsets:

\displaystyle\mathsf{Q}_{\mathrm{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathsf{Q}\mid\mathsf{d}_{T^{\prime}}(x)>\mathsf{d}_{T}(x)\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{Q}_{\mathrm{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathsf{Q}\mid\mathsf{d}_{T^{\prime}}(x)\leq\mathsf{d}_{T}(x)\}.

Namely,
\mathsf{Q}_{\mathrm{1}}
(resp.
\mathsf{Q}_{\mathrm{2}}
) is the set of existing nodes of the CDAWG
for which the number of out-edges increase (resp. do not increase).

4
Upper bound for total out-degrees of nodes w.r.t.
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}

In this section, we show an upper bound for the total out-degrees of the nodes corresponding to strings in
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}\subseteq\mathsf{M}(T^{\prime})
.
Recall that
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
implies
x\notin\mathsf{LeftM}(T)
.

We first describe useful properties of
strings
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
.

Lemma 1
.

Any
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
occurs in
T
.

Proof.

In the case
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}
, since
x\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
,

x
occurs in
T
.

Let us consider the case
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
that is of Type
\rm{(i)}
,
\rm{(ii)}
,
\rm{(iii)}
or
\rm{(iv)}
.
Since
x
is not of Type
\rm{(v)}
,
if all occurrences of
x
in
T^{\prime}
are crossing occurrences of
x
in
T^{\prime}
, then
x\notin\mathsf{M}(T^{\prime})
.
Therefore,
x
occurs in
T
.

Let us consider the case
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
that is of Type
\rm{(v)}
.
Due to the definition of
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
, there exists a distinct right-extension of
x
in
T^{\prime}
other than the right-extension(s) of the crossing occurrence(s) of
x
.
Therefore, there is a non-crossing occurrence of
x
in
T^{\prime}

as shown in Figure
5
,
implying that
x
occurs in
T
.
∎

For any

satisfying

|y|>|x|

S_{x_{L}}=S_{{y}_{G}}
.
In what follows, we consider the case that
x_{L}
is not a prefix of
T^{\prime}
.

For a contrary, suppose that for
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}

\rm{(i)}
,
\rm{(ii)}

\rm{(iii)}
,

.

,
y
occurs in

that is a suffix of

, however, this contradicts that

\rm{(i)}
,
\rm{(ii)}
or
\rm{(iii)}

x
with
S_{x_{L}}
.
For any

\rm{(iv)}

\rm{(v)}

if there does not exist

|y|>|x|

occurs in

Lemma 4
.

For any
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
,
U(x)\in\mathsf{M}(T)
.

Proof.

By Definition
5
,
U(x)=\mathsf{lexp}_{T}({S_{x_{G}}})\in\mathsf{LeftM}(T)
.
Therefore, it suffices for us to prove
U(x)\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
.
From now on, we consider the four following cases:

Case (a)
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}
:

In this case,
x\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
, therefore
S_{x_{G}}
that is a suffix of
x
also satisfies
S_{x_{G}}\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
.
Hence,
U(x)=\mathsf{lexp}_{T}({S_{x_{G}}})\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
.

Case (b)
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
and
x
is of Type
\rm{(i)}
,
\rm{(ii)}
or
\rm{(iv)}
:

Let the character immediately after all crossing occurrences of
x
in
T^{\prime}
be
a
.
There exists
xb\>(b\neq a)
in
T^{\prime}
or
x\in\mathsf{Suffix}(T^{\prime})
because
x\in\mathsf{M}(T^{\prime})
.
Since the character immediately after all crossing occurrences of
x
in
T^{\prime}
is
a
,
then there exists
xb\>(b\neq a)
or
x\in\mathsf{Suffix}(T)
in
T
.
Hence,
S_{x_{G}}\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
since the character immediately after
S_{x_{G}}
is
a
.
Thus,
U(x)=\mathsf{lexp}_{T}({S_{x_{G}}})\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
.

Case (c)
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
and
x
is of Type
\rm{(iii)}
:

Since
x
is of Type
\rm{(iii)}
, we associate
x
with
S_{x_{L}}
.
Because
x
is of Type
\rm{(iii)}
and
S_{x_{L}}
is a suffix of a
S_{x_{R}}
,
S_{x_{G}}\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
holds.
Thus,
U(x)=\mathsf{lexp}_{T}({S_{x_{G}}})\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
.

Case (d)
x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}
and
x
is of Type
\rm{(v)}
:

Let the character immediately after
S_{x_{G}}
be
a
.
Since there exists a distinct right-extension of
x
in
T^{\prime}
other than the right-extension(s) of the crossing occurrence(s) of
x
, there exists
xb

(b\neq a)
in
T
.
Therefore,
S_{x_{G}}\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
.
Thus,
U(x)=\mathsf{lexp}_{T}({S_{x_{G}}})\in\mathsf{RightM}(T)
.

Consequently, we have
U(x)\in\mathsf{M}(T)
.
∎

The next lemma states the uniqueness of

Lemma 5
.

For any

x\neq y

U(x)\neq U(y)
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exist

x\neq y

U(x)=U(y)
.
Let
x
and
y
correspond to

and
S_{y_{F}}
, respectively,
where
G,F\in\{L,R\}
.
Let
U(x)=AS_{x_{G}},U(y)=BS_{y_{F}}\>(A,B\in\mathsf{Substr}(T))
,
and assume without loss of generality that
|S_{x_{G}}|<|S_{y_{F}}|
.
Then
|A|>|B|
because

.
Since

by Lemma
4
, and since

is a prefix of

(see Figure
8

.
This contradicts

∎

4.2
Upper bound w.r.t.
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}

Lemma 6
.

\sum_{x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}}\mathsf{d}_{T^{\prime}}(x)\leq 3\mathsf{e}+2
.

Proof.

Let
U(x)=\mathsf{lexp}_{T}({S_{x_{G}}})
, where
G\in\{L,R\}
.
Since
S_{x_{G}}
is a suffix of
x
,
\mathsf{d}_{T}(x)\leq\mathsf{d}_{T}(U(x))
.
Since there are at most two distinct characters immediately after the crossing occurrences of
x
,

\mathsf{d}_{T^{\prime}}(x)\leq\mathsf{d}_{T}(U(x))+2
.
For
U(x)\neq T
, we have
\mathsf{d}_{T}(U(x))\geq 1
. Thus
\mathsf{d}_{T^{\prime}}(x)\leq\mathsf{d}_{T}(U(x))+2\leq 3\mathsf{d}_{T}(U(x))
.
For
U(x)=T
, we have
\mathsf{d}_{T}(U(x))=0
. Thus
\mathsf{d}_{T^{\prime}}(x)\leq 2
.
By using Lemma
5
and summing up these, we get

\sum_{x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}}\mathsf{d}_{T^{\prime}}(x)\leq{\sum_{x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{1}}\cup\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3A}}}3\mathsf{d}_{T}(U(x))}+2\leq 3\mathsf{e}+2
.
∎

5

\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{2}}\cup\mathsf{Q}

In this section, we show an upper bound for the total out-degrees of nodes corresponding to strings that are elements of
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{2}}\cup\mathsf{Q}\subseteq\mathsf{M}(T^{\prime})
.

We first present properties of the strings in
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{2}}\cup\mathsf{Q}

In particular, we focus on the strings in
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{2}}\cup\mathsf{Q}_{\mathrm{1}}

\mathsf{Q}_{\mathrm{2}}

Lemma 7
.

Any

T

Proof.

Since

x\in\mathsf{LeftM}(T)

x\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{2}}\cup\mathsf{Q}_{\mathrm{1}}
occurs in
T
.
∎

\rm{(i)}
,
\rm{(ii)}

P_{x_{G}}=P_{y_{F}}

2
, in which

\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{2}}
or
\mathsf{Q}_{\mathrm{1}}
.
Let us consider the case that

.
Suppose that for

\rm{(i)}
,
\rm{(ii)}
or
\rm{(iv)}
,
there is

|y|>|x|
and
P_{x_{G}}=P_{y_{F}}
, where

.
If
x_{R}
is a suffix of
T^{\prime}
, then there is no
y
such that
|y|>|x|

.
From now on consider the case that

Let

.

\rm{(i)}
,
\rm{(ii)}

\rm{(iv)}

6.2
Upper bound w.r.t.

Lemma 15
.

.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathsf{N}_{\mathrm{3B}}
,

x
in
T^{\prime}
than the right-extension(s) of the crossing occurrence(s) of

∎

7
Conclusions

This paper proved that the worst-case multiplicative sensitivity
of CDAWGs is asymptotically at most 8.
Our analysis is based fully on new combinatorial properties
of maximal repeats and their right-extensions,
that are incidental to an edit operation on the strings.
The only known lower bound for the multiplicative sensitivity of CDAWGs
is asymptotically 2
[
8
]
.
It is intriguing future work to close the gap between
these upper bound and lower bound.

On a doubly sublinear fractional p p -Laplacian equation
Antonio Iannizzotto, Sunra Mosconi

Abstract
. We prove a bifurcation result for a Dirichlet problem driven by the fractional p p -Laplacian (either degenerate or singular), in which the reaction is the difference between two sublinear powers of the unknown. In our argument, a fundamental role is played by a Sobolev vs. Hölder minima principle, already known for the degenerate case, which here we extend to the singular case with a simpler proof.

1.
Introduction and main result

The present note is mainly devoted to the study of the following Dirichlet type problem for a nonlinear, nonlocal elliptic equation:

(P_{\lambda})\qquad\begin{cases}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u=\lambda u^{q-1}-u^{r-1}&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u>0&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\Omega$.}\end{cases}

Here
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
(
N\geqslant 2
) is a bounded domain with a
C^{1,1}
-smooth boundary,
p>1
,
s\in(0,1)
are s.t.
ps<N
, and the leading operator is the

-fractional
p
-Laplacian, namely, the gradient of the functional

u\mapsto\frac{1}{p}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy,

defined in a convenient fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. If
p\geqslant 2
and
u
is smooth enough, such operator admits the pointwise representation

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u(x)=2\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dy.

The reaction involves two

-sublinear powers of

The literature on boundary value problems driven by the fractional

-Laplacian includes a wide variety of results, starting from

10

Our main result states that problem

may admit two, one, or no solutions, depending on the value of

The inequalities in Theorem

It is notable that this a purely nonlocal feature. Indeed, in the case of the local

Also, the use of truncations requires some care in handling the function spaces. Indeed, the energy functional

for problem

is naturally defined in the fractional Sobolev space

[

Passing to the fractional framework, the situation becomes more involved. Indeed, weak solutions of fractional order Dirichlet problems, despite being fairly regular in the interior of the domain, only attain

The Sobolev vs. Hölder minimizers coincidence result is then rephrased for the fractional framework, by comparing the topologies of
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and of
C^{0}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})
. As a matter of fact, under very general assumptions, local minimizers of the energy functional in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and

, respectively, do coincide. Such coincidence was proved in
[

]
for the degenerate fractional

-Laplacian (

) by using a variant of the method of
[
8
]
. In this paper we come back to this result in Theorem

, and extend the coincidence result to all

, which is especially suitable for dealing with the singular case (

The structure of the paper is the following: in Section

we recall some preliminary results on the fractional

-Laplacian; in Section

2.
Preliminaries

Here we recall some preliminary results on fractional Sobolev spaces, the fractional
p
-Laplacian, and related problems. First, for all open
U\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and all measurable functions

[u]_{s,p,U}=\Big[\iint_{U\times U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Accordingly we define the fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,p}(U)=\big\{u\in L^{p}(U):\,[u]_{s,p,U}<\infty\big\}.

From now on, let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
-smooth boundary, and assume
ps<N
. In order to incorporate the Dirichlet condition, we define the subspace

W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\big\{u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\,u=0\ \text{in $\Omega$}\big\},

which is a uniformly convex, separable Banach space with norm

, whose dual space is denoted by
W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. The embedding
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
is continuous for all
q\in[1,p^{*}_{s}]
and compact for all

, where

. We also introduce the following special space:

\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\,\exists\,U\Supset\Omega\ \text{s.t. $u\in W^{s,p}(U)$,}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{(1+|x|)^{N+ps}}\,dx<\infty\Big\}.

For a comprehensive introduction to the fractional Sobolev spaces, see
[
17
]
. We can define the fractional
p
-Laplacian as a nonlinear operator
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,:\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)\to W^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
by setting for all
u\in\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
,
\varphi\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u,\varphi\rangle=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{p-1}(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy

(with the notation
a^{p-1}=|a|^{p-2}a
established above). Such definition is equivalent to the one given in Section
1
. Clearly we have
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\widetilde{W}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, and the restriction of

to

is a continuous, maximal monotone,

:

Remark 3.2
.

The approach followed here to prove Theorem

differs from that used in

4.
Bifurcation result

In this section we finally come back to problem
(P_{\lambda})
and we prove Theorem
1.1
. Set

\Lambda=\big\{\lambda>0:\,\text{$(P_{\lambda})$ has a solution}\big\}.

Also, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
we set

\mathcal{S}(\lambda)=\big\{u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):\,\text{$u$ solution of $(P_{\lambda})$}\big\}.

Most of the section will be devoted to studying the properties of the sets defined above. Fix
\lambda>0
. Set for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

f_{\lambda}(t)=\lambda(t^{+})^{q-1}-(t^{+})^{r-1}.

Clearly
f_{\lambda}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
{\bf H}_{q}
(with
q<p
). Set further

F_{\lambda}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f_{\lambda}(\tau)\,d\tau,

and for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\Phi_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p}}{p}-\int_{\Omega}F_{\lambda}(u)\,dx.

As in Section
3
,
\Phi_{\lambda}\in C^{1}(W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))
and its critical points satisfy weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u=f_{\lambda}(u).

For clarity, we will divide the proof in several steps. Our first result states the non-existence of (positive) solutions of problem
(P_{\lambda})
, for
\lambda>0
small:

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
\lambda>0
small enough,
(P_{\lambda})
has no solutions.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\lambda}_{1}>0
be the principal eigenvalue of
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
(see
[
19
]
) and choose
\varepsilon\in(0,\hat{\lambda}_{1})
. Setting
\lambda_{0}=\min\{1,\varepsilon\}>0
, we claim that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
and all
t\geqslant 0
it holds

(4.1)

f_{\lambda}(t)\leqslant\varepsilon t^{p-1}.

Indeed, from
\lambda\leqslant\lambda_{0}\leqslant 1
and
0<r<q
we immediately infer
f_{\lambda}(t)\leqslant 0
for
t\in[0,1]
. For
t\geqslant 1
we instead have, due to
p>q
and
\lambda\leqslant\lambda_{0}\leqslant\varepsilon
, that

f_{\lambda}(t)\leqslant\lambda t^{q-1}\leqslant\varepsilon t^{p-1},

concluding the proof of (
4.1
). Now let
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
,
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a critical point of
\Phi_{\lambda}
. Then, by (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda}(u)u\,dx\leqslant\varepsilon\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

By
\varepsilon<\hat{\lambda}_{1}
and the variational characterization of the principal eigenvalue, we have
u=0
. So,
(P_{\lambda})
has no (positive) solutions, or equivalently,
\lambda\notin\Lambda
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
\lambda>0
big enough,
(P_{\lambda})
has at least one solution.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
. The functional
\Phi_{\lambda}
, defined above, is sequentially weakly l.s.c. in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Also,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is coercive in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Indeed, by the continuous embedding
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
, for all
u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p}}{p}-\lambda\frac{\|u\|_{q}^{q}}{q}+\frac{\|u\|_{r}^{r}}{r}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p}}{p}-C\lambda\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}^{q},

and the latter tends to
\infty
as
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\to\infty
(recall that
q<p
). So, there exists
u_{\lambda}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
s.t.

\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=\inf_{u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\Phi_{\lambda}(u)=:m_{\lambda}.

In particular,
u_{\lambda}
is a critical point of
\Phi_{\lambda}
, i.e., a weak solution of

(4.2)

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda}=f_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda}).

By Proposition
2.4
we have
u_{\lambda}\in C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})
. Testing (
4.2
) with
-u_{\lambda}^{-}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and applying Proposition
2.1

(
i
)

, we get

\displaystyle\|u_{\lambda}^{-}\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda},-u_{\lambda}^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})(-u_{\lambda}^{-})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u_{\lambda}<0\}}f_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})u_{\lambda}\,dx=0.

Therefore,
u_{\lambda}\geqslant 0
in
\Omega
. Now fix any
\bar{u}\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}\setminus\{0\}
, then we have

\Phi_{\lambda}(\bar{u})=\frac{\|\bar{u}\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p}}{p}-\lambda\frac{\|\bar{u}\|_{q}^{q}}{q}+\frac{\|\bar{u}\|_{r}^{r}}{r},

and the latter tends to
-\infty
as
\lambda\to\infty
. So, for all
\lambda>0
big enough we have

m_{\lambda}\leqslant\Phi_{\lambda}(\bar{u})<0,

hence
u_{\lambda}\neq 0
. By Proposition
2.5
(with
h=-f_{\lambda}
) we get
u_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C^{0}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
. So we have proved that
\lambda\in\Lambda
for all
\lambda>0
big enough.
∎

By Lemmas

,

we may set

\lambda_{*}=\inf\,\Lambda>0.

We will now prove that
\lambda_{*}\in\Lambda
:

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a solution
u_{*}\in{\rm int}(C^{0}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})
of
(P_{\lambda_{*}})
.

Proof.

Let
(\lambda_{n})
be a sequence in
\Lambda
, s.t.
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda_{*}
. Clearly, for all

we have
\lambda_{n}\leqslant M
(

independent of

), and there exists a solution

of
(P_{\lambda_{n}})
. For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

\displaystyle|f_{\lambda_{n}}(t)|

\displaystyle\leqslant M|t|^{q-1}+|t|^{r-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(1+|t|^{q-1}),

with
C>0
independent of
n
and
q<p
. By Proposition
2.3

(

⁢
i
i
i

)

, we have

Test

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{n},u_{n}-u_{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{n}u_{n}^{q-1}-u_{n}^{r-1})(u_{n}-u_{*})\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant M\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q-1}\|u_{n}-u_{*}\|_{q}+\|u_{n}\|_{r}^{r-1}\|u_{n}-u_{*}\|_{r},

and the latter tends to

Remark 4.4
.

Arguing as in Lemma

\displaystyle\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,\underline{u}-(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda},(\underline{u}-u_{\lambda})^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u_{\lambda}<\underline{u}\}}\big[f_{\lambda}(\underline{u})-f_{\lambda}(\underline{u})\big](\underline{u}-u_{\lambda})\,dx=0.

By Proposition

(

⁢
i
i

)

we have

in

and

(with

Applying Lemma

and the mountain pass theorem, we can now prove multiplicity and comparison of solutions for
(P_{\lambda})
for

Lemma 4.6
.

For all
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
there exist two solutions
u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda}\in{\rm int}(C^{0}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})

Proof.

Let

be given by Lemma

. We have weakly in
\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{*}=\lambda_{*}u_{*}^{q-1}-u_{*}^{r-1}<\lambda u_{*}^{q-1}-u_{*}^{r-1},

hence

is a strict subsolution of
(P_{\lambda})
. Applying Lemma
4.5
with

u_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{S}(\lambda)
s.t.
u_{\lambda}\geqslant u_{*}
in
\Omega
, along with
u_{\lambda}\neq u_{*}

\Omega

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{*}+u_{*}^{r-1}=\lambda_{*}u_{*}^{q-1}<\lambda u_{\lambda}^{q-1}=(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u_{\lambda}+u_{\lambda}^{r-1},

by Proposition
2.6
(with
h(t)=(t^{+})^{r-1}
) we have
u_{\lambda}-u_{*}\in{\rm int}(C^{0}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})

u_{\lambda}
is a global minimizer of the truncated functional
\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Define now

U=\big\{u_{*}+v:\,v\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap{\rm int}(C^{0}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})_{+})\big\},

a relatively open set in

s.t.

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u_{*}}f_{\lambda}(u_{*})\,d\tau+\int_{u_{*}}^{t}f_{\lambda}(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{\lambda}(u)

Lemma 4.7

For all

Persistence modules induced by inner functions
Jiaxing He, Bingzhe Hou, Xiao Wang, Yue Xin

Abstract
As well-known, inner functions play an important role in the study of bounded analytic function theory. In recent years, persistence module theory, as a main tool applied to Topological Data Analysis, has received widespread attention. In this paper, we aim to use persistence module theory to study inner functions. We introduce the persistence modules arised from the level sets of inner functions. Some properties of these persistence modules are shown. In particular, we prove that the persistence modules (potentially not of locally finite type) induced by a class of inner functions have interval module decompositions. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the interleaving distance of the persistence modules is continuous with respect to the supremum norm for a class of Blaschke products, which could be used to discuss the path-connectedness of Blaschke products. As an example, we provide an explicit formula for the interleaving distance of the persistence modules induced by the Blaschke products with order two.

The path-connectedness in the space of inner functions is an important research subject. Nestoridis studied the invariant and noninvariant connected components of the inner functions space

Through the level sets of functions, we are able to establish a connection between bounded analytic function theory and persistent homology theory. Based on the definition of the persistence modules induced by inner functions, we can analyze the topological properties of inner functions by observing the persistence modules and their corresponding barcodes.

Definition 2.17
.

A Blaschke product is an inner function of the form

B(z)=\lambda z^{m}\prod_{n}\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha_{n}|}{\alpha_{n}}(\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n}-z}{1-\overline{\alpha_{n}}z}),

where
m
is a nonnegative integer,
\lambda
is a complex number with
|\lambda|=1
, and
\{\alpha_{n}\}
is a sequence of points in
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying the Blaschke condition
\displaystyle\sum_{n}(1-|\alpha_{n}|)<\infty
.

If for every bounded sequence of complex numbers
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, there exists
f
in
H^{\infty}
satisfying
f(\alpha_{n})=w_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then both the sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and the Blaschke product
B(z)
are called interpolating. Following from a celebrated result of Carleson
[
13
]
, one can see
that

is an interpolating Blaschke product if and only if

is a uniformly separated sequence, i.e.,

\displaystyle\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\prod_{k\neq n}{\big|}\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{n}}{1-\overline{\alpha_{k}}\alpha_{n}}{\big|}>0.

Furthermore, a Blaschke product is called Carleson-Newman if it is a product of finitely many interpolating Blaschke products.

\displaystyle=~\ln\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\geq~\displaystyle\frac{-\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}{1-\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=~\displaystyle\frac{-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq~-2\alpha\sqrt{-\ln\delta_{0}}

\displaystyle=~\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{\sqrt{-\ln\delta_{0}}}\cdot\ln\delta_{0}.

Consequently, one can see that

\displaystyle\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}\geq\delta_{0}^{{2\alpha}/{\sqrt{-\ln\delta_{0}}}}=\delta_{0}^{\alpha(\gamma-1)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\rho(y,z)

\displaystyle\geq~\displaystyle\frac{\rho(x,z)-\delta_{0}}{1-\delta_{0}\rho(x,z)}

\displaystyle\geq~\delta_{0}^{\alpha}\cdot\displaystyle\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq~\delta_{0}^{\alpha}\cdot\delta_{0}^{\alpha(\gamma-1)}

\displaystyle=~\delta_{0}^{\alpha\gamma}

\displaystyle=~\rho(x,z)^{\gamma}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

n=2
, it will become a metric.

In the following context, the persistence module obtained from a Blaschke product
B
only cares about its modulus
|B|
. Without loss of generality, we take
B(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{+\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n}-z}{1-\overline{\beta_{n}}z}
.

For the Blashcke products
B_{1}(z)
and
B_{2}(z)
with two zeros, we can get the following theorem,

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
B_{1}
be the Blashcke product with two zero points
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
, and let
B_{2}
be the Blashcke product with two zero points
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
. Let
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}
be the persistence modules induced by
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. Then,

d_{int}(\mathbb{B}_{1},\mathbb{B}_{2})=\displaystyle\min\left(\displaystyle\max(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}},\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}),|\ln\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}}|\right),

where
w_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}}{1-\overline{\beta_{0}}\beta_{1}}
,
w_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}}{1-\overline{\gamma_{0}}\gamma_{1}}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three cases.

1.

Suppose that
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are the Blaschke products with two pairwise distinct zero points.

Let
\psi_{1}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{|w_{1}|}{w_{1}}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{0}-z}{1-\overline{\beta_{0}}z}
, then
\mathbb{B}_{1}\cong\mathbb{B}_{1}(\psi_{1})
by Lemma
3.1
, where
\mathbb{B}_{1}(\psi_{1})
is the persistence module induced by
B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}
. And by Theorem A, we only need to know the critical points to reconstruct the persistence modules
\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}
. Next, we compute the critical points of
B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}
. Denote by
c_{1}
the critical point of
B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}
.

Since the critical point
c_{1}
satisfying that
(B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}(z))^{\prime}|_{c_{1}}=0
, following from

\displaystyle B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}(z)=-z\cdot\displaystyle\frac{|w_{1}|-z}{1-|w_{1}|z},

we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{1}}+\displaystyle\frac{|w_{1}|^{2}-1}{(1-|w_{1}|c_{1})(|w_{1}|-c_{1})}=0,

and then,

\displaystyle c_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{1-\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}{|w_{1}|}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}(c_{1})=-\displaystyle\frac{(1-\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}})^{2}}{|w_{1}|^{2}},

\displaystyle|B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}(c_{1})|=\displaystyle\frac{(1-\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}})^{2}}{|w_{1}|^{2}}.

Since

\displaystyle\ln\displaystyle\frac{1+|B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}(c_{1})|}{1-|B_{1}\circ\psi_{1}(c_{1})|}=\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}},

we have the persistence module
\mathbb{B}_{1}\cong\mathbb{F}(0,+\infty)\oplus\mathbb{F}(0,\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}]
.
Let
w_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}}{1-\overline{\gamma_{0}}\gamma_{1}}
, then
\mathbb{B}_{2}\cong\mathbb{F}(0,+\infty)\oplus\mathbb{F}(0,\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}}]
.

Since two barcodes
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are
\Delta
-matched with a finite
\Delta
if and only if they have the same number of infinite bars (of form
(a,+\infty)
) and by Remark
2.14
, we have

d_{int}(\mathbb{B}_{1},\mathbb{B}_{2})=\displaystyle\min(\displaystyle\max(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}},\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}),|\ln\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}}|).

2.

Suppose that
B_{1}
is the Blaschke products with zero point
\beta
of order two and
B_{2}
is the Blaschke products with zero point
\gamma
of order two.

There is only one generator for each persistence module. By Theorem A,
\mathbb{B}_{1}\cong\mathbb{F}(0,+\infty)
and
\mathbb{B}_{2}\cong\mathbb{F}(0,+\infty)
,

d_{int}(\mathbb{B}_{1},\mathbb{B}_{2})=0.

3.

Suppose that
B_{1}
is the Blaschke products with two pairwise distinct zero points
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
,
B_{2}
is the Blaschke products with zero point
\gamma
of order two.

The persistence module
\mathbb{B}_{1}\cong\mathbb{F}(0,+\infty)\oplus\mathbb{F}(0,\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}]
. And
\mathbb{B}_{2}\cong\mathbb{F}(0,+\infty)
, then we have

\displaystyle d_{int}(\mathbb{B}_{1},\mathbb{B}_{2})=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\min(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}},|\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}.

Combining all three cases, we can summarize all cases in the following formula,

d_{int}(\mathbb{B}_{1},\mathbb{B}_{2})=\displaystyle\min(\displaystyle\max(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}},\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}),|\ln\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}}|).

∎

For any
a
=
(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{D}
, denote the Blaschke product with zero points
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\}
by
B_{\bf a}(z)
. We define
\bar{d}_{n}:\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{D}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
\bar{d}_{n}({\bf a},{\bf b})=d_{int}(\mathbb{B}_{\bf a},\mathbb{B}_{\bf b})
. We say
{\bf a}\sim_{M}{\bf b}
if there exists
\varphi\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
such that
{\bf b}=\varphi({\bf a})=(\varphi(\alpha_{1}),\cdots,\varphi(\alpha_{n}))
.

Proposition 5.2
.

\bar{d}_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
induces a pseudo-metric
d_{n}
on
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{D}/\sim_{M}
.

Proof.

We define
d_{n}([{\bf a}],[{\bf b}])=\bar{d}_{n}({\bf a},{\bf b})
. By Lemma
3.1
, if
{\bf a}\sim_{M}{\bf b}
we have

\bar{d}_{n}({\bf a},{\bf b})=d_{int}(\mathbb{B}_{\bf a},\mathbb{B}_{\bf b})=0,

which implies
d_{n}
is well-defined.

Since interleaving distance is a pseudo-metric, the symmetry and triangle inequality of
d_{n}
follow from the property of
d_{int}
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

d_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
is a metric on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}/\sim_{M}
.

Proof.

For points
{\bf a}=(\beta_{0},\beta_{1})
,
{\bf b}=(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})
, if
d_{2}([{\bf a}],[{\bf b}])=0
, there are two cases.

In case 1,

0=\displaystyle\max(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}},\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}})\leq|\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}}-\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}|,

i.e.,
|w_{2}|=|w_{1}|=0
, which means each of the Blaschke products
B_{\bf a}
and
B_{\bf b}
is with zero points of order two. There exists a Möbius transformation
\psi
such that
B_{\bf a}=B_{\bf b}\circ\psi
, then
{\bf a}\sim_{M}{\bf b}
.

In case 2,

0=|\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}}-\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}|\leq\displaystyle\max(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{2}|^{2}}},\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\ln\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|w_{1}|^{2}}}),

i.e.,
|w_{2}|=|w_{1}|
, there exists Möbius transformations
\psi_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{|w_{1}|}{w_{1}}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{0}-z}{1-\overline{\beta_{0}}z}
and
\psi_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{|w_{2}|}{w_{2}}\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{0}-z}{1-\overline{\gamma_{0}}z}
such that
B_{\bf a}\circ\psi_{1}=B_{\bf b}\circ\psi_{2}
, then
{\bf a}\sim_{M}{\bf b}
, which shows that
d_{2}
is a metric.
∎

On the additive index of the Diffie-Hellman mapping and the discrete logarithm
Pierre-Yves Bienvenu, Arne Winterhof

Abstract
. Several complexity measures such as degree, sparsity and multiplicative index for cryptographic functions including the Diffie-Hellman mapping and the discrete logarithm in a finite field have been studied in the literature. In 2022, Reis and Wang introduced another complexity measure, the additive index, of a self-mapping of a finite field. In this paper, under certain conditions, we determine lower bounds on the additive index of the univariate Diffie-Hellman mapping and a self-mapping of 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} which can be identified with the discrete logarithm in a finite field.



1.
Introduction

For the whole paper, we fix a prime number
p
and an integer
n\geq 1
and put
q=p^{n}
.
Every notion of linear algebra should be understood with respect to the ground field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

1.1.
Diffie-Hellman mapping and discrete logarithm

For a divisor
T
of
q-1
, let
\gamma\not=0
be an element of (multiplicative) order
T

in the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
q
elements. Then the
Diffie-Hellman mapping to the base
\gamma
is the bivariate function
D(X,Y)
defined by

D(\gamma^{x},\gamma^{y})=\gamma^{xy},\quad 0\leq x,y\leq T-1.

Any efficient way of calculating
D(\gamma^{x},\gamma^{y})
for a large set of pairs
(x,y)
would have an important impact to public-key cryptography, in particular, the Diffie-Hellman key-exchange could be attacked, see for example
[
11
, Chapter 3]
.
However, there are several results in the literature that this is not the case in view of several complexity measures for
D
, see in particular the monograph
[
14
]
.
For example, it can be shown that
D(X,Y)
cannot be represented by a low-degree polynomial (for large
T
),
see
[
2
,
16
]
.

The
univariate Diffie-Hellman mapping to the base
\gamma
is the function
d=d_{\gamma}
defined by

d(\gamma^{x})=\gamma^{x^{2}},\quad 0\leq x\leq T-1.

Since

D(\gamma^{x},\gamma^{y})^{2}=d(\gamma^{x+y})d(\gamma^{x})^{-1}d(\gamma^{y})^{-1},\quad 0\leq x,y\leq T-1,

and both inversion in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
and square-root finding can be done in (probabilistic) polynomial time, see for example
[
1
, Sections 5.4, 7.1 and 7.2]
, any fast evaluation method for
d(\gamma^{x})
would lead to one for
D(\gamma^{x},\gamma^{y})
. Again any univariate polynomial which interpolates
d(X)
on a large subset must have large degree as well as large sparsity, see
[
9
,
10
,
14
]
and references therein.

Moreover, the
discrete logarithm
or
index
of
\gamma^{x}
to the base
\gamma
is

{\rm ind}_{\gamma}(\gamma^{x})=x,\quad 0\leq x\leq T-1.

If
T<p
, in particular, if
q=p
is a prime, we may identify the discrete logarithm with a mapping from the subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
of order
T
into
\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, however, for non-prime
q
we get only the discrete logarithm modulo the characteristic
p
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and lose information about the exact integer value of the discrete logarithm. Hence, it is more natural to study the following mapping:

Let
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
be an ordered basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and

\xi_{x}=x_{1}\beta_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}\beta_{n}

whenever

x=x_{1}+x_{2}p+\cdots+x_{n}p^{n-1},\quad 0\leq x_{i}<p,

be an ordering of the elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
For a fixed element
\gamma
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
of order
T
the discrete logarithm ind
γ
can be identified with a mapping
P
of the subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
of order
T
into
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
defined by

P(\gamma^{x})=\xi_{x},\quad x=0,1,\ldots,T-1.

More recently, the multiplicative index of
f(X)=d(X)
and
f(X)=P(X)
was studied in
[
6
]
,
that is the index of the largest subgroup
S
of
G
such that
f(X)X^{-r}
is constant on each coset of
S
for some positive integer
r
.

In this paper we prove similar results on the additive index introduced by Reis and Wang in
[
13
]
for
d(X)
and
P(X)
, respectively.
In fact, in the spirit of the references mentioned above, we provide lower bounds (depending on
m
) for the additive index of any self-mapping
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
that coincides with either of these mappings on all but
m
elements of their domain

\langle\gamma\rangle
, that is interpolation polynomials of these two mappings.

1.2.
The additive index

A polynomial over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of the form

M(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}X^{p^{j}},\quad a_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},

is called
linearised
(or
p
-polynomial
), see for example
[
12
, Section 2.1.6]
.
Note that
M(X)
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear map if we consider
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
a vector space, that is,

M(a\xi+b\zeta)=aM(\xi)+bM(\zeta),\quad a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\quad\xi,\zeta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

In fact a simple counting argument shows that any
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-endomorphism of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
(
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear self-mapping of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
) is induced by a linearised polynomial; we provide the details in the appendix (Proposition
13
).
A linearised polynomial is sparse and has at most
n
non-zero coefficients. Moreover, in a normal basis representation, calculating
p^{j}
th powers is just a shift of coordinates and thus considered free, see for example
[
8
, Section 2.3]
. Hence linearised polynomials can be efficiently evaluated in
O(n)

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-operations in normal basis representation
and in
O(n^{2}\log p)

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-operations using repeated squaring, see
[
1
, Section 5.4]
, taking an arbitrary basis representation.
Here, as usual,
f=O(g)
means
|f|\leq cg
for some constant
c>0
.
Sometimes we also use
f\ll g
or
g\gg f
instead. If the implied constant may depend on some parameter
\varepsilon
, we write
f\ll_{\varepsilon}g
.

A mapping
F:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is of
additive index
p^{k}

and
codimension

k

if there is a linearised polynomial
M(X)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]

and a linear subspace
U_{0}
of dimension
n-k
with disjoint cosets
U_{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,p^{k}-1
, such that

F(\xi)=M(\xi)+a_{i},\quad\xi\in U_{i},\quad i=0,1,\ldots,p^{k}-1,

for some
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{p^{k}-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, see
[
13
, Definition 4.2]
.

Remark
.

The counting argument alluded to above reveals that
M
may be assumed to have degree at most

p^{n-k-1}
, see Corollary
7
in the appendix.

Note that a mapping of additive index
p^{k}
and codimension
k
is also a mapping of additive index
p^{k^{\prime}}
and codimension
k^{\prime}
for any
k^{\prime}
with
k\leq k^{\prime}\leq n
.
The
least codimension
of
F
is the smallest
k
such that
F
has codimension
k
. Then
p^{k}
is the
least additive index
of
F
.
Thus if the least codimension is
0
, the mapping
F
is simply an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-affine function.
In the contrary, if the least codimension is as large as possible,
that is
n
, the mapping
F
shows no linear behaviour.
It is worth noting that when
q
is large, the proportion of maps

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
that have codimension strictly less than
n

among all self-mappings of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

becomes
vanishingly small, see Corollary
9
in the appendix.
A generic mapping
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
has therefore least codimension
n
, and a mapping of codimension
<n
is very much untypical, “non-random”.
Besides degree, sparsity and multiplicative index, the additive index is therefore another measure for the complexity of a function, that is, its unpredictability and thus suitability in cryptography.

Partly we will use a slight abuse of terminology when we write additive index instead of least additive index. Moreover, the additive index of the functions
d
or
P
, respectively, which are defined only on a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, refers to any function on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
which coincides with
d
or
P
on this subset.

1.3.
Statement of some results and structure of the paper

We have a variety of results dealing with various cases – different regimes according to the cardinality
T
of

\langle\gamma\rangle
as a function of
q
, different methods according to certain arithmetic conditions on
p
and
n
– so that it would be too complicated to state all results here.
Further, we are able to tolerate functions departing from the Diffie-Hellman or discrete logarithm mappings on few points; thus, we provide lower bounds for the additive index of not only
these two mappings, but of any mappings coinciding with either of them on all but
m
elements of
\langle\gamma\rangle
, the quality of the bound decaying with
m
increasing.
However, we state here some appealing and simple results, corresponding to the important cases
T=q-1

and
m=0
. We start with the Diffie-Hellman mapping.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
p>3
or
p=3
and
n
is even. Assume
\gamma
is a generator of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
, that is
T=q-1
.
Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that the additive index of
d_{\gamma}
is at least
C_{\varepsilon}\frac{q^{1-\varepsilon}}{p}
.

The result is even better and more general for the discrete logarithm.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose again
T=q-1
. Then the additive index of the discrete logarithm mapping is at least
\frac{q}{n+2}
.

We point out that we have much more general results, accommodating, under reasonable hypotheses,

T
as small as
q^{\varepsilon}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
. Further, for the Diffie-Hellman mapping, we also have results for the case

p=2
, and in certain cases we even obtain that the additive index is maximal, that is
q
. But to keep the introduction neat and tidy, we terminate here the exposition of our results.

The paper is organised as follows.
We start with some preliminary results in Section
2
.
In Section
3
we prove lower bounds on the additive index of the univariate Diffie-Hellman mapping
d(X)
and in Section
4
of the mapping
P(X)
which represents the discrete logarithm.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Weil bound

We recall the Weil bound for multiplicative character sums, see for example
[
8
, Theorem 5.41]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of order
s>1
and

f(X)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
be not of the
f(X)=ag(X)^{s}
with
d
different zeros (in its splitting field). Then we have

\left|\sum_{\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(f(\xi))\right|\leq(d-1)q^{1/2}.

We will use the following consequence for certain character sums over subgroups.

Corollary 1
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
and
\chi
a non-trivial multiplicative character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Then we have

\left|\sum_{\xi\in G}\chi(\xi-\xi^{-1})\right|\leq 2q^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
T
be the order of
G
. Then the absolute value to be estimated equals

\frac{T}{q-1}\left|\sum_{\zeta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}}\chi(\zeta^{(q-1)/T}-\zeta^{-(q-1)/T})\right|=\frac{T}{q-1}\left|\sum_{\zeta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi((\zeta^{2(q-1)/T}-1)\zeta^{(q-2)(q-1)/T})\right|\leq 2q^{1/2},

where we used
\xi^{-1}=\xi^{q-2}
and the Weil bound Proposition
1
.
∎

2.2.
Number of solutions to the equation
x^{2}\equiv j\bmod T

For an integer
j
let
L_{T}(j)
be the number of solutions
x
of

x^{2}\equiv j\bmod T,\quad x=0,1,\ldots,T-1,

and
L_{T}
be the maximum of
L_{T}(j)
over all integers
j
. The following bounds on
L_{T}
are well-known.

Proposition 2
.

We have

L_{T}=O(\sqrt{T}).

Further, if
T=rk
, where
r
is prime and
k
is coprime to
r
,
then we have

L_{T}=O(\sqrt{k}).

The second statement is particularly interesting when
r
is large.

Proof.

The first statement is a particular case of
[
7
, Theorem 2]
,
which proves an analogous result for polynomial equations of arbitrary degree.
The second statement is a direct consequence, since

L_{T}=L_{r}\cdot L_{k}
by the Chinese remainder theorem and
L_{r}=2
for
any odd prime
r
(while
L_{2}=1
).
∎

2.3.
Number of squares modulo
T

Let
N(T)
be the number of squares modulo
T
, that is the residues
a\in\{0,1,\ldots,T-1\}
such that the equation

x^{2}\equiv a\bmod T
admits a solution.
By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, this is a multiplicative function;
thus
N(T)=\prod_{p}N(p^{v_{p}(T)})
where
v_{p}(T)
is the
p
-adic valuation of
T
for every prime
p
, and it suffices to evaluate
N
on prime powers.
We quote the following results from
[
15
]
.

Proposition 3
.

(1)

Let
p
be an odd prime and
\ell\geq 1
an integer.
Then

N(p^{\ell})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{p^{\ell+1}+p+2}{2(p+1)},&\ell\text{ even},\\[8.0pt]
\frac{p^{\ell+1}+2p+1}{2(p+1)},&\ell\text{ odd}.\end{array}\right.

(2)

For
p=2
and
\ell\geq 1
we have

N(2^{\ell})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{2^{\ell-1}+4}{3},&\ell\text{ even},\\[6.0pt]
\frac{2^{\ell-1}+5}{3},&\ell\text{ odd}.\end{array}\right.

Corollary 2
.

For any odd prime
p
and integer
\ell\geq 1
, we have

N(p^{\ell})\geq\frac{p^{\ell}}{3}\quad\mbox{and}\quad N(2^{\ell})\geq\frac{2^{\ell}}{6}.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and integer
T\geq 1
, we have

N(T)\gg_{\varepsilon}T^{1-\varepsilon}.

Proof.

The lower bounds for
N(p^{\ell})
follow from Proposition
3
. As a result, by multiplicativity,

N(T)\geq T/(3^{\omega(T)}\cdot 2)
, where
\omega
denotes the number of distinct prime factors of
T
.
As is well known, we have
\omega(T)\ll\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}

so
3^{\omega(T)}\leq T^{O(1/\log\log T)}\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{\varepsilon}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We will need the fact that no single class modulo
s
for some
s|T
concentrates almost all the squares modulo
T
. To make this precise, for any integer
a
and
s
where
s\mid T
, let
N(a,s,T)
be the number
of squares
x^{2}\bmod T
such that

x\equiv a\bmod s
and
N(s,T)=\max_{a}N(a,s,T)
.
Obviously
N(T,T)=1
and
N(1,T)=N(T)
. For
1<s<T
we use the following
result.

Proposition 4
.

For any integers
s,T
where
s\mid T
and
s>1
, we have

N(s,T)\leq\frac{8N(T)}{9}.

Proof.

Write
s=\prod_{p}p^{k_{p}}

and

T=\prod_{p}p^{\ell_{p}}
, where
k_{p}\leq\ell_{p}
for every
p
.
Then by the Chinese Remainder Theorem,

N(s,T)=\prod_{p}N(p^{k_{p}},p^{\ell_{p}})
.
Thus it suffices to bound

N(p^{k},p^{\ell})
for
\ell\geq k>0
, which in turn is at most

N(p,p^{\ell})
.
Since
N(p)\geq 2=2N(p,p)
for all
p
, we can suppose
\ell>1

For an odd prime
p
, we find

N(a,p,p^{\ell})=p^{\ell-1}
if
\gcd(a,p)=1
.
Otherwise
N(a,p,p^{\ell})\leq p^{\ell-2}
since
x\equiv 0\bmod p

implies
x^{2}\equiv 0\bmod p^{2}
.
Given the formula for
N(p^{\ell})
in Proposition
3
, it is obvious that

N(p^{\ell})<pN(p^{\ell-1})
. Thus

N(p^{\ell})/p^{\ell-1}
decreases with
\ell
. The limit as
\ell
tends to infinity is
p^{2}/2(p+1)\geq 9/8
for odd primes (minimal for
p=3
).

For
p=2
, we have

N(2,4)=1=N(4)/2
. Further for
\ell\geq 3
,

N(0,2,2^{\ell})=N(2^{\ell-2})
since an even square mod
2^{\ell}

is of the form
4y
where
y
is a square mod
2^{k-2}
.
A quick look at the formulas for
N(2^{\ell})
reveals that

N(2^{\ell-2})\leq(2/3)N(2^{\ell})
.
And
N(1,2,2^{\ell})
is the number of odd squares mod
2^{k}
, which is the number of squares congruent to 1 mod 8, which is
2^{\ell-3}
since every number congruent to 1 mod 8 is a square mod
2^{\ell}
.
Again a quick look at the formulas shows that

N(2^{\ell})\geq 2^{\ell-1}/3\geq(4/3)2^{\ell-3}
so we are done.
∎

2.4.
Number of
\xi\in G
with
\xi-\xi^{-1}\in G

Proposition 5
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of cardinality
T
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
.
Put

N=|\{\xi\in G:\xi-\xi^{-1}\in G\}|.

Then we have

N\geq\frac{T^{2}}{q}-3q^{1/2}.

Proof.

For
T=q-1
we have

N=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}q-3,&q\mbox{ odd},\\
q-2,&q\mbox{ even}.\end{array}\right.

since
\xi-\xi^{-1}\not\in G
if and only if
\xi^{2}=1
, and thus the result is trivial. Hence, we may assume
T\leq(q-1)/2
.

Let
\chi
be a multiplicative character of order
(q-1)/T
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
.
Then we have

\frac{T}{q-1}\sum_{j=0}^{(q-1)/T-1}\chi^{j}(\zeta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&\zeta\in G,\\
0,&\zeta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}\setminus G.\end{array}\right.

It follows that

N=\sum_{\xi\in G\setminus\{-1,1\}}\frac{T}{q-1}\sum_{j=0}^{(q-1)/T-1}\chi^{j}(\xi-\xi^{-1}).

(Note that
\xi=\pm 1
is not counted by
N
.)
Separating the contribution of the trivial character
\chi^{0}
we get
with

\epsilon=|G\cap\{-1,1\}|\in\{1,2\},

(1)

\left|N-\frac{(T-\epsilon)T}{q-1}\right|\leq\max_{j=1,\ldots,(q-1)/T-1}\left|\sum_{\xi\in G}\chi^{j}(\xi-\xi^{-1})\right|.

(Here we used the convention
\chi(0)=0
.)
For
j=1,\ldots,(q-1)/T-1
the absolute value of the sum over
\xi

is at most
2q^{1/2}
by Corollary
1
.

Combining this bound with (
1
), we now get

N\geq\frac{(T-2)T}{q-1}-2q^{1/2}

and the result follows since we assumed
T\leq(q-1)/2
.
∎

2.5.
Sums of elements of a subgroup

We use the Minkowski sum notation: given subsets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
of the group
({\mathbb{F}}_{q},+)
,
we define their sum set by

\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}:(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\right\}.

We simplify the notation if all
A_{i}
’s are equal:

mA=\sum_{i=1}^{m}A
.
Similarly we define the product set
AB

of two sets
A
and
B
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by

AB=\{ab:(a,b)\in A\times B\}
. Again we may iterate this construction
and get
A^{m}=\{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}:a_{i}\in A\,\forall i\}
; in theory, this notation may provoke a clash of notation with the cartesian product, but we will ensure no ambiguity arises.

We quote
[
4
, Theorem 5]
. A subset
A\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is
symmetric
if
a\in A
implies
-a\in A
and
antisymmetric
if
a\in A
implies
-a\not\in A
. Note that a subgroup
G
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
is either symmetric (if
|G|
is even and thus
-1\in G
) or antisymmetric (if
|G|
is odd and thus
-1\not\in G
).

Proposition 6
.

Assume that
A\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and

B\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
are such that
B
is symmetric or antisymmetric. If additionally
|A||B|>q
then
8AB={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

For sets
A
of size not larger than
\sqrt{q}
, there may be no integers
m,N
such that

NA^{m}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
because
A
may be included in a proper subfield, or more generally

A\subset\xi{\mathbb{F}}
for some
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and a proper subfield
{\mathbb{F}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. In such a case,
A
is called
degenerate
. It turns out this degeneracy
is the only impediment to obtaining
NA^{m}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for some integers
m,N
:
this follows from
[
3
, Theorem 6]
, which we reformulate below.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
.
Suppose
A\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is non-degenerate and

|A|>q^{\varepsilon}
.
Let
m=\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}+1/2\rceil
and
r=160\cdot 6^{m-3}\left(1+\frac{\log m}{\log 2}\right)
.
Then
rA^{2m-2}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Theorem 6]
, if

|A|>q^{1/(m-\delta)}
for some
\delta\in(0,1)
and integer
m\geq 3
, then we have
NA^{2m-2}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
where

N=6^{m-3}\max\left\{30\left(3+\frac{\log\delta^{-1}}{\log 2}\right),160\left(1+\frac{\log m}{\log 2}\right)\right\}.

Choosing
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
and
m=\lceil\varepsilon^{-1}+\delta\rceil
we get

q^{1/(m-\delta)}\leq q^{\varepsilon}<|A|
,
\delta^{-1}\leq m
so
N\leq r
and the result follows.
∎

Let
G
be a subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
of order
T
.
Let
r(G)
be the smallest integer (if it exists) such that
each element
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
can be written
as sum of
r(G)
summands of
G
, that is,

\alpha=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{r(G)}\quad\mbox{for some }x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r(G)}\in G.

Note that for
T\geq 2
and any
r>r(G)
there is a solution
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r})\in G^{r}
of
x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{r}=\alpha
for any
\alpha\not=0
. In the sum set notation,
r(G)
is the smallest integer
r
such
that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}\subset rG
.

Proposition 8
.

For
s=2,3,\ldots,7
we have

r(G)\leq s\quad\mbox{if }T>q^{1/2+1/2s}.

Moreover, we have

r(G)\leq 8\mbox{ if }T>q^{1/2}.

If
G
is not a subset of a proper subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, that is,
T
is not a divisor of
p^{d}-1
for any divisor
d<n
of

,
then we have for any
\varepsilon
with
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
,

where

that is,

is bounded by a constant depending only on

\varepsilon
.

Proof.

For the first result see
[

.
The second result follows from
Proposition

since

is either symmetric or antisymmetric and the third result follows from Proposition

.
∎

3.
The Diffie-Hellman mapping

In this section, we fix a prime
p
, an integer
n\geq 1
, and an element
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. Let
G=\langle\gamma\rangle
and
T=|G|
. We fix
m\leq T
and a self-mapping
F
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

that coincides with the Diffie-Hellman mapping
d=d_{\gamma}
on all but
at most
m
elements of
G
.

Our goal is to prove that the least additive index of
F
is large.
Since we have information on
F
on only at most
T
points, we do not expect
this index to be much more than
T
in general. Indeed, if
k>\lceil\log_{p}T\rceil
,
it becomes conceivable (and very probable as
k
grows) that there
exists a subspace
U\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of codimension
k
each of whose translates contains at most one point of
G
; then
F
could be constant on each of
these translates.

To achieve this goal, we fix a subspace
U\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and denote
by
k\leq n
its codimension. Let
{\mathcal{V}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}/U
be the collection of all
cosets (aka translates) of
U
. Assume there exist
M\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
linearised
and
(a_{V})_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathcal{V}}
such that

(2)

F(\xi)=M(\xi)+a_{U+\xi}

for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
We need to show that
k
is large.
To do this, we will use frequently the following lemma.

Proposition 9
.

Let
t=\gcd(T,p-1)
and
g=\gamma^{T/t}
.
For
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
let

S_{j}=\{\xi\in\langle\gamma\rangle:F(g^{\epsilon}\xi)=d_{\gamma}(g^{\epsilon}\xi)\text{ for any }\epsilon\in\{0,j\}\}.

Then for any
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\xi=\gamma^{x}\in S_{j}
we have

(3)

d(\xi)(g^{j(2x+jT/t)}-g^{j})=a_{U+g^{j}\xi}-g^{j}a_{U+\xi}.

In particular, if
t
is even,
we have

(4)

d(\xi)((-1)^{T/2}+1)=a_{U-\xi}+a_{U+\xi}\text{ for all }\xi\in S_{t/2}.

Note that
g\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, so
U+g^{j}\xi=g^{j}(U+\xi)
.
Therefore, an important feature of Equation (
3
) is that the right-hand side depends only on
j\bmod t
and
U+\xi
.

Proof.

Fix
V\in{\mathcal{V}}
,
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\xi=\gamma^{x}\in U_{V}\cap S_{j}
. By Equation
(
2
), the fact that
g^{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and linearity of
M
,
we have on the one hand

\displaystyle F(g^{j}\xi)=g^{j}M(\xi)+a_{g^{j}V}=g^{j}d(\xi)+a_{g^{j}V}-g^{j}a_{V}

and on the other hand

\displaystyle F(g^{j}\xi)

\displaystyle=d(g^{j}\xi)=\gamma^{(x+jT/t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{x^{2}}g^{2jx+j^{2}T/t}=d(\xi)g^{2jx+j^{2}T/t}.

Therefore

g^{j}d(\xi)+a_{g^{j}V}-g^{j}a_{V}=d(\xi)g^{2jx+j^{2}T/t}
.
This supplies the first desired conclusion.
Now if
t
(and hence
T
) is even
we take
j=t/2
, so

g^{j}=-1
in Equation (
3
) to derive (
4
).
∎

Various hypotheses on
T
and
t
combined with Proposition
9
yield remarkable results, which we state and prove separately for more legibility. Recall that
N(T)
is the number of squares modulo
T
, equivalently the number of distinct values of
d=d_{\gamma}
.

Theorem 3
.

If
T\equiv 0\bmod 4
, we have
p^{k}\geq N(T)-2m
.

Proof.

By hypothesis

p^{n}\equiv 1\bmod 4
so
p\equiv 1\bmod 2
, whence
t
is even.
Further
(-1)^{T/2}=1
and
thus Equation (
4
) from Proposition
9
implies that

d(\xi)=(a_{-V}+a_{V})/2
is constant on
V\cap S_{t/2}
,
for every
V\in{\mathcal{V}}
.
As a result, the number of distinct values of
F
on
S_{t/2}

is at most
|{\mathcal{V}}|
, which is
p^{k}
.
Moreover
|G\setminus S_{t/2}|\leq 2m
.
It follows that the number
N(T)
of distinct values of
d_{\gamma}
is at most
p^{k}+2m
, and we are done.
∎

Theorem 4
.

If
f>2
is a common divisor of
T
and
p-1
, we have
p^{k}\geq\frac{N(T)}{9f}-\frac{2m}{f}
.

Proof.

Let
j=t/f
and
h=g^{j}
.
Let
s=f/2
if

is even and
s=f
if
f
is odd.
Then Equation (
3
) rewrites

(5)

d(\xi)(h^{2x+T/f}-h)=a_{hV}-ha_{V}\text{ for all }\xi\in V\cap S_{t/f}.

Whenever
\xi=\gamma^{x}\in V\cap S_{j}
and
2x+T/f\not\equiv 1\bmod f
,
Equation (
5
) implies that

d(\xi)=(h^{2x+T/f}-h)^{-1}(a_{hV}-ha_{V})

thus
d(\xi)
takes only at most
s
distinct values on
V\cap S_{t/f}
for each

.
In total, the number of distinct values of

on the set of all
\gamma^{x}\in S_{j}
where

is therefore at most
sp^{k}
.

Consider the number
M
of squares

such that

2x+T/f\equiv 1\bmod f.

We just proved
2m+sp^{k}+M\geq N(T)
.
Since
M\leq N(s,T)\leq 8N(T)/9
by Proposition
4
,
we conclude.
∎

We are now able to prove Theorem
1
, which we first restate for convenience

Theorem
1
.

Suppose
p>3
or
p=3
and

is even. Assume
\gamma
is a generator of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
, that is

.

, there exists a positive constant

is at least

In view of the hypotheses, either

or

, possibly both.
Therefore we may apply at least one of Theorem

or

,

∎

We have yet another result for

, which covers certain cases that Theorem
1
does not (but conversely not all cases that Theorem
1
does), and yields even stronger results when it applies,
namely the maximal result

when

.

Theorem 5
.

If
T=q-1
,
p>2
, and
n\not\equiv 0\bmod\frac{p-1}{2}
,
then
p^{k}\geq\frac{q}{4m+1}
.

Proof.

Since
T=q-1
, we have
t=\gcd(T,p-1)=p-1
.
Since
p
is odd,
t=p-1
and
T
are both even.
We focus on the neutral coset

U
and denote by
a=a_{U}
the corresponding constant.
In that case,
for any

\xi\in U\cap S_{j}
, Equation (
3
) becomes

(6)

d(\xi)(g^{j(2x+jT/t)}-g^{j})=(1-g^{j})a\text{ for all }\xi\in U\cap S_{j},

Taking
j=t/2
and
\xi\in U\cap S_{t/2}
(if it exists) in (
6
) yields

\left(1+(-1)^{T/2}\right)d(\xi)=2a,

that is,

0\neq d(\xi)=a\quad\mbox{if }\frac{T}{2}\mbox{ is even},

and

a=0\quad\mbox{if }\frac{T}{2}\mbox{ is odd}.

Assume that
U\cap S_{t/2}\neq\emptyset
.
If
T/2=(q-1)/2
is odd (thus
a=0
), Now we consider
j=\pm 1
.
This time Equation (
3
) yields

(7)

2x+jT/t\equiv 1\bmod t\text{ for any }j\in\{-1,1\}\text{ and }\gamma^{x}\in U\cap S_{1}\cap S_{-1}.

Taking the difference of the equations (
7
) for
j=1
and
j=-1
yields

T/t\equiv 0\bmod t/2

as soon as
U\cap S_{1}\cap S_{-1}\neq\emptyset
.
Further
\frac{T}{t}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{p-1}\equiv n\bmod p-1
,
so we find
n\equiv 0\bmod\frac{p-1}{2}
.
This is contrary to the hypothesis so either
U\cap S_{t/2}=\emptyset

or
U\cap S_{1}\cap S_{-1}=\emptyset
. Therefore
p^{n-k}-1-3m\leq 0
and we conclude.

In the case that

\frac{T}{2}
is even,
Equation (
6
) simplifies to (using
0\neq d(\xi)=a
)

2jx+j^{2}\frac{T}{t}\equiv 0\bmod t,\quad j\in\left\{1,\frac{t}{2},-1\right\}

whenever
\xi=\gamma^{x}\in U\cap S_{1}\cap S_{t/2}\cap S_{-1}
.
In particular, assuming for a contradiction

U\cap S_{1}\cap S_{t/2}\cap S_{t-1}\neq\emptyset
,
adding one to another the equations above for

and

, we get again

\frac{T}{t}\equiv 0\bmod\frac{t}{2},

thus

.
This contradicts
the result, thus
0=|U\cap S_{1}\cap S_{t/2}\cap S_{t-1}|\geq p^{n-k}-1-4m

which concludes.
∎

The only cases where none of the results above work are

•

\gcd(T,p-1)=2

and
T\equiv 2\bmod 4
,

•

\gcd(T,p-1)=1
.

For that case, we have another method. Its applicability is not restrained by arithmetic conditions – in contrary to all results so far, it therefore applies when
p=2
– but the bounds obtained are weaker, and
T
needs to be large (at least a constant times
q^{3/4})
.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
T^{2}\geq 6q^{3/2}+6mq
.
Then
p^{k}\gg T^{3/4}/\sqrt{q}
.

Proof.

Let

S=\{\xi\in G:\xi-\xi^{-1}\in G,\,F\text{ and }d\text{ coincide on }\{\xi,\xi^{-1},\xi-\xi^{-1}\}\}.

Further, for every
(V,W)\in{\mathcal{V}}^{2}
, denote

S_{V,W}=S\cap V\cap W^{-1},

where
{W}^{-1}=\{\xi^{-1}:\xi\in W\setminus\{0\}\}
.
Since
d(\xi)=d(\xi^{-1})
, we infer

\displaystyle 0=d(\xi)-d(\xi^{-1})=M(\xi)+a_{V}-M(\xi^{-1})-a_{W}=M(\xi-\xi^{-1})+a_{V}-a_{W}

for all
\xi\in S_{V,W}
.
Therefore we have

d(\xi-\xi^{-1})=M(\xi-\xi^{-1})+a_{V-W}=a_{V-W}+a_{W}-a_{V},\quad\mbox{for all }\xi\in S_{V,W}.

By the pigeonhole principle, since
\bigcup_{V,W}S_{V,W}=S
,
there exist
(V,W)\in{\mathcal{V}}^{2}
such that

|S_{V,W}|\geq p^{-2k}|S|
.
Moreover, for any
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, the equation
\xi-\xi^{-1}=b
has only at most two solutions, being equivalent to a quadratic equation
in the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Let
a=a_{V-W}+a_{W}-a_{V}
and
N=|\{\zeta\in G:d(\zeta)=a\}|
.
This is at most the maximal number of square roots of an integer
j
modulo
T
, which is denoted by
L_{T}
.
Further,
the argument above yields

L_{T}\geq N\geq\frac{1}{2}|S_{V,W}|\geq\frac{1}{2p^{2k}}\left(|\{\xi\in G:\xi-\xi^{-1}\in G\}|-3m\right).

Applying Proposition
5
and Proposition
2
,
we find

(8)

T^{1/2}\gg L_{T}\geq\frac{1}{2p^{2k}}\left(\frac{T^{2}}{q}-3q^{1/2}-3m\right).

By hypothesis, the term in the parenthesis is at least
T^{2}/(2q)
,
so that
T^{1/2}\gg T^{2}/(qp^{2k})
and we conclude.
∎

Remark
.

In the important case where
T
is a prime, or more generally
T=rb

where
r
is a prime and
b
is bounded, we have
L_{T}=O(\sqrt{b})=O(1)
so Equation (
8
) gives the much better bound

p^{k}\gg T/\sqrt{q}
provided that
T^{2}\geq 6q^{3/2}+6mq
.

Theorem
6
requires
T\gg q^{3/4}.

We supply one last result, again free of any arithmetic constraints on
T
and
p
, that kicks in as soon as
T\gg q^{1/2}
.
Our argument seems unable to accommodate the
m
values where
F
and
d
may disagree, so we will assume

m=0
from now on, thus
F=d
on
G
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer.
Let
(V_{i},W_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,r}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{2r}
.
The map

(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r})\mapsto F\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}^{-1})\right)

is constant on
\prod_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\cap W_{i}^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r})\in\prod_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\cap W_{i}^{-1}
.
Then

\displaystyle F\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}^{-1})\right)

\displaystyle=M\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}^{-1})\right)+a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}(V_{i}-W_{i})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(M(\xi_{i})-M(\xi_{i}^{-1}))+a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}(V_{i}-W_{i})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(F(\xi_{i})-a_{V_{i}}-F(\xi_{i}^{-1})+a_{W_{i}})+a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}(V_{i}-W_{i})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(d(\xi_{i})-d(\xi_{i}^{-1}))+a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}(V_{i}-W_{i})}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(a_{W_{i}}-a_{V_{i}})

\displaystyle=a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}(V_{i}-W_{i})}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(a_{W_{i}}-a_{V_{i}}).

The last member of this sequence of equalities clearly is constant
on
\prod_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}\cap W_{i}^{-1}
so we are done.
∎

Recall the notation
hB=\{b_{1}+\ldots+b_{h}:b_{1},\ldots,b_{h}\in B\}
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
B=\{\xi-\xi^{-1}:\xi\in G\}
.
Suppose
G\subset hB
for some

.
Then

p^{k}\geq N(T)^{1/(2h)}.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
10
with
r=h
,
we conclude that
d
takes on at most
p^{2hk}
pairwise distinct values on

G
. Since by definition
d
takes on exactly
N(T)
pairwise distinct values,
we deduce that
p^{2hk}\geq N(T)
as desired.
∎

Theorem 7
.

Suppose
T>2q^{1/2}
. Then
p^{k}\geq N(T)^{1/32}\gg_{\varepsilon}T^{1/32-\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

Let
B=\{\xi-\xi^{-1}:\xi\in G\}
and
A=\{\xi+\xi^{-1}:\xi\in G\}
.
Then
|A|\geq|G|/2
and
|B|\geq|G|/2
,
since for every
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, both equations
\xi-\xi^{-1}=a
and
\xi+\xi^{-1}=a
admit at most two solutions.
Further
B
is symmetric (that is
B=-B
).
By hypothesis

|A|\cdot|B|\geq\frac{T^{2}}{4}>q,

which implies

8AB={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by Proposition

.

Note that
(\xi-\xi^{-1})(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})=\xi\zeta-(\xi\zeta)^{-1}+\xi\zeta^{-1}-(\xi\zeta^{-1})^{-1}
,
so that

.
In particular, we infer that

.
We conclude by an appeal to Corollary
3
and Corollary

.
∎

To conclude this section, we state one more result which covers the cases

.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose

,

,

Remark
.

(1)

The proof provides a constant

which decays
exponentially with

, that is

.

(2)

The condition

is not included in a proper subfield of

is equivalent to the condition: for every proper divisor

of

,

divides neither

nor

.

4.
Discrete logarithm

Now we consider the mapping
P
described in the introduction, which can be identified with the discrete logarithm.
Fix a prime
p
, an integer
n\geq 1

and
T\mid q-1
where
q=p^{n}
.
Let
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
have multiplicative order
T
and let
P:\langle\gamma\rangle\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be the corresponding discrete logarithm mapping.

4.1.
Some results on the mapping
x\mapsto\xi_{x}

First we need a couple of lemmas about the associated mapping

x\mapsto\xi_{x}
. Recall that this mapping, described in the introduction, depends on the choice, which we henceforth assume fixed, of
an ordered basis
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
as a vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Proposition 11
.

For
x=0,1,\ldots,q-2

the difference

\zeta_{x}=\xi_{x+1}-\xi_{x}

is

\zeta_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\beta_{i},

where
j
is the minimum in
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
x_{j}\leq p-2
in the
p
-adic expansion

x=x_{1}+x_{2}p+\cdots+x_{n}p^{n-1},\quad 0\leq x_{i}<p,

of
x
.

Proof.

Note that the minimum
j
exists since
x\leq q-2
.
We have

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-1)(1+p+\cdots+p^{j-2})+x_{j}p^{j-1}+x_{j+1}p^{j}+\cdots+x_{n}p^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{j-1}-1+x_{j}p^{j-1}+x_{j+1}p^{j}+\cdots+x_{n}p^{n-1}

and

x+1=(x_{j}+1)p^{j-1}+x_{j+1}p^{j}+\cdots+x_{n}p^{n-1}.

We find

\xi_{x}=-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\beta_{j}+\sum_{i=j}^{n}x_{i}\beta_{i},

\xi_{x+1}=(x_{j}+1)\beta_{j}+\sum_{i=j+1}^{n}x_{i}\beta_{i},

and thus

\xi_{x+1}=\xi_{x}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\beta_{i},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Thus, the difference
\xi_{x+1}-\xi_{x}
has at most
n
possible values.
In general, the difference
\xi_{x+a}-\xi_{x}
, for a fixed
a
,
can take on more distinct values, but at most
2^{n-1}
as the next proposition shows.

Proposition 12
.

Fix a positive integer
a\in\{0,\ldots,T-1\}
.

(1)

Suppose
0\leq x<T-a
.
Write the base
p
decomposition

a=a_{1}+a_{2}p+\cdots+a_{n}p^{n-1},\quad 0\leq a_{i}<p.

Then

P(\gamma^{x+a})-P(\gamma^{x})=\xi_{x+a}-\xi_{x}\in a_{1}\beta_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\{a_{j}\beta_{j},(a_{j}+1)\beta_{j}\}.

If additionally
a_{1}=a_{2}=\cdots=a_{j}=0
for some
j\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, the coordinate of
\xi_{x+a}-\xi_{x}
along

\beta_{j+1}
in the basis
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
is
a_{j+1}.

(2)

If
T-a\leq x<T
, let

b=T-a=b_{1}+b_{2}p+\cdots+b_{n}p^{n-1},\quad 0\leq b_{i}<p.

Then

P(\gamma^{x+a})-P(\gamma^{x})=\xi_{x+a-T}-\xi_{x}\in-b_{1}\beta_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\{-b_{j}\beta_{j},-(b_{j}+1)\beta_{j}\}.

If additionally
b_{1}=b_{2}=\cdots=b_{j}=0
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the coordinate of
\xi_{x+a-T}-\xi_{x}
along

\beta_{j+1}
in the basis
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
is
-b_{j+1}.

Proof.

We prove the first claim.
Write the base
p
decomposition

x=x_{1}+x_{2}p+\cdots+x_{n}p^{n-1},\quad 0\leq x_{i}<p.

Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
If

\sum_{i=1}^{j}x_{i}p^{i-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}p^{i-1}<p^{j},

then the digit of
p^{j}
in
x+a
is
x_{j+1}+a_{j+1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
or
x_{j+1}+a_{j+1}-p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
; otherwise it is

x_{j+1}+a_{j+1}+1\in{\mathbb{Z}}
or
x_{j+1}+a_{j+1}+1-p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Therefore the
j
th coordinate of
\xi_{x+a}
in the base
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
is either
x_{j}+a_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
or
x_{j}+a_{j}+1\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{j}x_{i}p^{i-1}<p^{j}
, the first case is always true when

a_{1}=a_{2}=\cdots=a_{j}=0
.
This concludes the proof of the first assertion.

For the second one, write
y=x+a-T=x-b
, so
0\leq y<a=T-b
.
Then
\xi_{x+a-T}-\xi_{x}=-(\xi_{y+b}-\xi_{y})
and we can apply the first claim.
∎

4.2.
Bounds for the additive index of the discrete logarithm

For this section, we
fix an element
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of order
T
, an integer
m
with

0\leq m\leq T

and a self-mapping
F
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

that coincides with
P
on all but
at most
m
elements of
G=\langle\gamma\rangle
.
Our goal is to show that the least additive index of
F
is large.

Like for the Diffie-Hellman mapping, since we have information on
F
on only
T
points, we do not expect
this index to be much more than
T
in general.

To achieve this goal, we fix a subspace
U\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and denote
by
k\leq n
its codimension. Let
{\mathcal{V}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}/U
be the collection of all
cosets (aka translates) of
U
. Assume there exist
M\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
linearised
and
(a_{V})_{V\in{\mathcal{V}}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\mathcal{V}}
such that

(9)

F(\xi)=M(\xi)+a_{U+\xi}

for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
We need to show that
k
is large.

First, we get an excellent result in the case where
T=q-1
, that is,
\gamma
is a generator of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. In the case
m=0
, we recover Theorem
2
.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
T=q-1
.
Then

p^{k}\geq\frac{q}{n+2m+2}.

Proof.

By Equation (
9
),
for any
V\in{\mathcal{V}}
and

\gamma^{x}\in U,\gamma^{z}\in V
,
we have

\displaystyle F(\gamma^{x}+\gamma^{z})

\displaystyle=M(\gamma^{x}+\gamma^{z})+a_{V}

\displaystyle=M(\gamma^{x})+M(\gamma^{z})+a_{V}

\displaystyle=F(\gamma^{x})-a_{U}+F(\gamma^{z}).

Let
y\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that
\gamma-1=\gamma^{y}
.
When
z=y+x
, the calculation above yields

(10)

F(\gamma^{x+1})=F(\gamma^{x}+\gamma^{y+x})=F(\gamma^{x})-a_{U}+F(\gamma^{y+x}).

Let

S=\{x\in\{0,\ldots,T-2\}:\gamma^{x}\in U,\,F\text{ and }P\text{ coincide on both }\gamma^{x},\gamma^{x+y}\}.

By hypothesis,
|S|\geq|U|-2-2m=p^{n-k}-2-2m
.

When
x\in S
, Equation (
10
) gives

(11)

\xi_{x+1}-\xi_{x}+a_{U}=P(\gamma^{y+x}).

When
x
ranges in
S
, the element
\xi_{x+1}-\xi_{x}
, and hence the left-hand side of Equation (
11
) can take only
at most
n
values by Proposition
11
.
The right-hand side, by injectivity of the discrete logarithm, takes on exactly
|S|
values.
Thus
p^{n-k}-2-2m\leq|S|\leq n
which concludes.
∎

We can work with smaller
T
, representing
\gamma-1
as a sum of elements from
\langle\gamma\rangle
, but the quality of the bound decays quickly with the number of summands required. The proof runs initially along similar lines as above.

Theorem 10
.

Assume

\gamma-1\in rG=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\xi_{i}:(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r})\in G^{r}\right\},

where
r
is an integer coprime to
p
.
Suppose
m<\frac{T-1}{r+2}
.
Then

p^{k}\geq\left(\frac{T-(r+2)m-1}{rn}\right)^{1/(r+1)}.

Proof.

Assume the contrary thus

(12)

\frac{T-(r+2)m-1}{p^{(r+1)k}}>rn.

By Equation (
9
), for any integers
x,z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
, we have

(13)

F\left(\gamma^{x}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\gamma^{y_{i}}\right)=F(\gamma^{x})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F(\gamma^{z_{i}})+a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}+W}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{V_{i}}-a_{W},

where

W,V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
are the cosets of
U
containing
\gamma^{x},\gamma^{z_{1}},\ldots,\gamma^{z_{r}}
, respectively.
By hypothesis
\gamma-1\in rG
.
Therefore, let
0\leq y_{1}\leq\cdots\leq y_{r}\leq T-1
be such that

\gamma^{y_{1}}+\cdots+\gamma^{y_{r}}=\gamma-1
.
Then
\gamma^{y_{1}+x}+\cdots+\gamma^{y_{r}+x}=\gamma^{x+1}-\gamma^{x}
for any integer
x
,
so that Equation (
13
)
gives

(14)

F(\gamma^{x+1})=F(\gamma^{x})+\sum_{i=1}^{r}F(\gamma^{y_{i}+x})+a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}+W}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{V_{i}}-a_{W},

where again

W,V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
are the classes modulo
U
of
\gamma^{x},\gamma^{y_{1}+x},\ldots,\gamma^{y_{r}+x}
, respectively.
Let

S=\{x\in\{0,\ldots,T-2\}:F\text{ and }P\text{ coincide on all of }\gamma^{x},\gamma^{x+1},\gamma^{y_{1}+x},\ldots,\gamma^{y_{r}+x}\}.

By definition,
|S|\geq T-1-(r+2)m
.
When
x\in S
, Equation (
14
) becomes

(15)

\xi_{x+1}=\xi_{x}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}P(\gamma^{y_{i}+x})+a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}+W}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{V_{i}}-a_{W},

When
x
ranges in
S
,
the
(r+2)
-tuple

(U+\gamma^{x},U+\gamma^{y_{1}+x},\ldots,U+\gamma^{y_{r}+x},\xi_{x+1}-\xi_{x})

can take at most
p^{(r+1)k}n
values in
({\mathbb{F}}_{q}/U)^{r+1}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by Proposition
11
.
By the pigeonhole principle, since

|S|/(p^{(r+1)k}n)>r
by (
12
),
one of these values
(W,V_{1},\ldots,V_{r},v)
, which we henceforth fix, is taken at least
(r+1)
times.
Thus by Equation (
15
), there exist
0\leq x_{0}<\cdots<x_{r}<T
such that

(16)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}P(\gamma^{y_{i}+x_{0}})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}P(\gamma^{y_{i}+x_{1}})=\cdots=\sum_{i=1}^{r}P(\gamma^{y_{i}+x_{r}})=v-a_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}+W}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{V_{i}}+a_{W}.

When
r=1
, this simply yields

P(\gamma^{y_{1}+x_{0}})=P(\gamma^{y_{1}+x_{1}})

which, by injectivity of the map

x\mapsto P(\gamma^{x})
on the interval
\{0,\ldots,T-1\}

and periodicity, implies
x_{1}\equiv x_{0}\bmod T
, a contradiction.

The general case is more intricate.
Denote
y_{i}^{(j)}=y_{i}+x_{j}\in\{0,\ldots,2T-2\}
, for
i=1,\ldots,r
,
and
j=0,\ldots,r
.
For any fixed
j
, this is a non-decreasing sequence in
i
; and for any fixed
i
, it is an increasing sequence in
j
.
Let
f(i,j)=\left\lfloor\frac{y_{i}^{(j)}}{T}\right\rfloor
be the Euclidean quotient of
y_{i}^{(j)}
by
T
.
Thus the sequence

i\mapsto f(i,j)
is non-decreasing. Also the sequence
j\mapsto f(i,j)

is non-decreasing. Further
0\leq f(i,j)\leq 1
for every
j
.
Therefore, for every
j
, there exist
\ell_{j}\in\{0,\ldots,r\}

such that

f(i,j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}0,&i\leq\ell_{j}\\
1,&i>\ell_{j}\end{array}\right.

and the sequence
(\ell_{j})_{j\in\{0,\ldots,r\}}
is non-increasing.
We distinguish two cases.

(1)

There exist
j

such that
\ell_{j}=\ell_{j+1}
.
Thus
f(i,j)=f(i,j+1)
for all
i
.
Write
z_{i}=y_{i}^{(j)}
and
b=x_{j+1}-x_{j}
, so that
z_{i}=y_{i}+x_{j}
and
z_{i}+b=y_{i}+x_{j+1}
have the same quotient
f(i,j)
in the Euclidean division by
T
.
Then taking the difference of
the
(j+2)
th and
(j+1)
th term from the left in Equation (
16
), we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}(P(\gamma^{z_{i}+b})-P(\gamma^{z_{i}}))=0

and
z_{i}
and
z_{i}+b
have the same quotient
q_{i}
in the Euclidean division by
T
for each
i
.
Thus

\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\xi_{z_{i}-q_{i}T+b}-\xi_{z_{i}-q_{i}T})=0,

where
0\leq z_{i}-q_{i}T<T-b
for each
i
.
Applying Proposition
12
, with the base
p
decomposition

b=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}p^{i-1}
,
we find that

rb_{1}\equiv 0\bmod p
, whence
b_{1}=0
.
Applying Proposition
12
repeatedly, we find successively that all the digits of
b
are 0 so that
b=0
, a contradiction.

(2)

The sequence
\ell_{j}
is decreasing, hence

\ell_{0}=r
and
\ell_{r}=0
.
We infer
f(i,0)=0
and
f(i,r)=1
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, in particular,
y_{i}+x_{r}\geq T
and
y_{i}+x_{0}<T
.

Write
z_{i}=y_{i}^{(0)}
and
b=x_{r}-x_{0}
, so that
T-b\leq z_{i}<T
for all
i
.
Then
taking the difference of
the
(r+1)
th term and the first term from the left in Equation (
16
),
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}(P(\gamma^{z_{i}+b})-P(\gamma^{z_{i}}))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\xi_{z_{i}+b-T}-\xi_{z_{i}})=0

and we can apply Proposition
12
repeatedly as above to obtain

b=T
, which is also a contradiction.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark
.

In view of the proof and Proposition
11
,

.
We therefore apply Theorem
10
, with

(and

) if

,

Corollary 6

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a constant integer

Our next theorem, Theorem
11
, provides a different lower bound for the additive index of the discrete logarithm.
When it applies, Theorem
11
is usually superior to
Theorem
10
. But it applies only under the arithmetic condition
\gcd(T,p-1)>1
,
which in particular requires
p>2
. The method of proof is a combination of the one used so far in this section and the method used for Theorems
3
and

4
.

Theorem 11
.

Assume
\gcd(T,p-1)>1
.
Then
p^{k}\geq\frac{T-2m}{2^{n}}
.

Proof.

Let
t=\gcd(T,p-1)>1
and

g=\gamma^{\frac{T}{t}}
, thus
g\in G\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
and
g\neq 1
.
Let

S=\{\xi\in G:F\text{ and }P\text{ coincide on }\{\xi,g\xi\}\}.

By hypothesis,

|S|\geq T-2m
.
By Proposition
12
, the expression

P(g\gamma^{x})-P(\gamma^{x})=P(\gamma^{x+\frac{T}{t}})-P(\gamma^{x})

takes on at most
2^{n}
pairwise distinct values as
x
ranges in
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular
P(g\xi)-P(\xi)
takes on at most
2^{n}
pairwise distinct values as
\xi
ranges in

G
.
In addition,
by Equation (
9
),
we have

P(g\xi)=gP(\xi)-a_{gV}+ga_{V}
for every
V\in{\mathcal{V}},\,\xi\in V\cap S
.
Thus, for any
\xi\in S
,
we have

(17)

P(\xi)=(g-1)^{-1}(P(g\xi)-P(\xi)+a_{gV}-ga_{V})

where
V=U+\xi
.
As
\xi
ranges in
G
, the left-hand side of (
17
) admits exactly
|S|\geq T-2m
pairwise distinct values since the discrete logarithm is
injective, whereas the right-hand side takes on at most
2^{n}p^{k}
distinct values.
We infer
p^{k}\geq(T-2m)/2^{n}
.
∎

Appendix: Linearised polynomials and linear maps

Proposition 13
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
, where
q=p^{n}
and
n\geq 1
.
Let
r\leq n
be an integer.
Let
U\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of dimension
r
.
Then the set
V
of all
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linear maps
U\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is equal to the set
W
of all maps

U\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
that are induced by linearized polynomials of degree at most
p^{r-1}
.

Proof.

First we observe that both sets are subspaces of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{U}
of all maps
U\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
Next, we point out that a map
U\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
induced by a linearised polynomial is linear,
which provides one inclusion
W\subset V
of a subspace in another.
It suffices to prove the equality of dimensions to conclude.
The dimension of
V
is
r
.
Thus it suffices to show that the linear map

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{r}\rightarrow W,\quad a=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{r-1})\mapsto F_{a}=(x\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}a_{i}x^{p^{i}})

is injective.
Now let
a=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{r-1})
be such that
F_{a}=0
.
Thus
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}a_{i}x^{p^{i}}=0
for all
x
in
U
.
Then the polynomial
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}a_{i}X^{p^{i}}
of degree at most
p^{r-1}
admits
|U|>p^{r-1}
roots, which implies that this is the zero polynomial, whence
a=0
and we are done.
∎

Corollary 7
.

Let
f
be a map
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
The following are equivalent.

(1)

f
has codimension
k
.

(2)

There exists a subspace
U\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of codimension
k
, a linear map
\ell:U\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and constants
b=(b_{i})_{i=0,\ldots,p^{k-1}}
,
such that,
given a system
(v_{i})_{i=0,\ldots,p^{k-1}}
of representatives of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
modulo
U
, we have

\forall i\in\{0,\ldots,p^{k}-1\},\,f(u+v_{i})=\ell(u)+b_{i}.

(3)

There exists a subspace
U\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of codimension
k
, a linearized polynomial
M\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X]
of degree at most
p^{n-k-1}

and constants
(a_{i})_{i=0,\ldots,p^{k-1}}

such that, denoting by
U_{i},i=0,\ldots,p^{k-1}
the cosets of
U
,
we have
f(\xi)=M(\xi)+a_{i}
for any
i
and
\xi\in U_{i}
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2):

If
f
has codimension
k
,
there exists a subspace
U\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of codimension
k
,
whose cosets are denoted by
U_{i},i=0,\ldots,p^{k-1}

a linearized polynomial
M
and constants
(a_{i})_{i=0,\ldots,p^{k-1}}

such that for all
\xi\in U_{i}
we have
f(\xi)=M(\xi)+a_{i}
.
Given a system
(v_{i})_{i=0,\ldots,p^{k-1}}
of representatives of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
modulo
U
, which may be ordered in such a way that

U_{i}=U+v_{i}
for each
i
, we therefore have

f(u+v_{i})=M(u+v_{i})+a_{i}=M(u)+M(v_{i})+a_{i}=M(u)+b_{i}
, for each
i
as desired,
where
b_{i}=a_{i}+M(v_{i})
. Since
u\mapsto M(u)
is a linear map
U\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
we are done.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: Suppose
f
is as in the hypothesis of the corollary,
and let
U,\ell,b
be as in this hypothesis.
There exists a linearized polynomial
M
of degree at most
p^{n-k-1}
that induces the map
\ell
on
U
by Proposition
13
.
Let
U_{i}=U+v_{i}
be the cosets of
U
.
For any
i\in\{0,\ldots,p^{k}-1\}
and
\xi\in U_{i}
, write
\xi=u+v_{i}
where
u\in U
, so that
M(\xi)=M(u)+M(v_{i})=\ell(u)+M(v_{i})=f(u)-b_{i}+M(v_{i})=f(u)+a_{i}

where

a_{i}=M(v_{i})-b_{i}
.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
is obvious.
∎

Proposition 14
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a field of characteristics
p
, where
q=p^{n}
and
n\geq 1
. For any
0\leq k\leq n
, let
I_{k}=I_{k}(q)
be the number of self-mappings of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of codimension
k
.
Then

q^{p^{k}+n-k}\leq I_{k}(q)\leq q^{p^{k}+n-k+\min(n-k,k)}.

Proof.

We prove first the lower bound.
Fix a subspace
U\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of codimension
k
. The number of
linear maps
\ell
on
U
is
q^{n-k}
. The number of tuples

a=(a_{i})_{i=0,\ldots,p^{k}-1}
is
q^{p^{k}}
.
Two pairs
(\ell_{1},a_{1}),(\ell_{2},a_{2})
yieldings the same map
must be equal because then
\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}=a_{1}-a_{2}
is a constant linear map, whence both terms are 0.
By Corollary
7
, this implies the lower bound.

Let us turn to the upper bound.
A map of codimension
k
is entirely determined by a triple
(U,\ell,a)
where
U\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is a subspace of codimension
k
,

\ell:U\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is linear, and
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{p^{k}}
.
We already counted pairs
(\ell,a)
above. A space
U
of codimension
k
is entirely determined by a basis
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-k})
, of which there are at most
q^{n-k}
. It is alternatively determined by a basis
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
of its orthogonal
U^{\perp}
(with respect to a fixed inner product of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
). Thus, there
are at most
q^{\min(n-k,k)}
choices for
U
.
∎

Corollary 8
.

As soon as
p>2
or
k>2
, we have
I_{k}(q)\geq qI_{k-1}(q)
, in particular
I_{k-1}(q)=o(I_{k}(q))
as
q
tends to infinity.
Further, in the regime where
p^{k}/n
tends to infinity, we have
I_{k-1}(q)\ll_{\varepsilon}I_{k}(q)^{1/p+\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

We use Proposition
14
, which gives
I_{k}\geq q^{p^{k}+n-k}

and
I_{k-1}\leq q^{p^{k-1}+\min(2(n-k+1),k-1)}
.
When
k-1\leq n/2
, we have
I_{k-1}\leq q^{p^{k-1}+n}
and
p^{k}-p^{k-1}\geq k+1
as soon as
p>2
or
k>2
, whence
I_{k}(q)\geq qI_{k-1}(q)
as desired. Otherwise
k-1<n/2
, so that

n-k+1+\min(n-k+1,k-1)=2(n-k)<n-k+k

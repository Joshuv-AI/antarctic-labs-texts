MicroRNAs diagnostic value or IgAN: a meta-analysis
Authors: Mengmeng Zhang, Zhifen Zhao, Bing Li, Meng Yang, Guang Li, WenSheng Zhai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundKidney biopsy, an invasive procedure with risks such as bleeding, is currently the gold standard for IgA nephropathy (IgAN) detection. Nevertheless, it may not be available in some centres. MicroRNAs have been investigated in some investigations as possible indicators for IgAN diagnosis. This investigation will assess the diagnostic utility of microRNAs for IgAN.MethodsSeveral databases were thoroughly examined up to July 20, 2023, including Wanfang Data, Embase, PubMed, Web of Science, SINOMED, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), China Science and Technology Journal Database (VIP), and Cochrane Library.We used Stata software (version 16.0) and Meta-Disc software (version 1.4) for the analysis. The negative and positive likelihood ratios (NLR and PLR), diagnostic odds ratio (DOR), pooled specificity and sensitivity were all calculated. Furthermore, the area under the curve (AUC) was computed and the summary receiver operating characteristic (SROC) curve was presented. Additionally, the Deeks funnel plot approach was employed to assess publication bias. We also used Fagan’s column-line plots to examine the clinical diagnostic utility of microRNA testing for IgAN.ResultsThe analysis comprised nine diagnostic tests in total, including 3359 individuals. The demonstrated that the pooled DOR, specificity, and sensitivity of microRNA in IgAN detection was 18.39 (95% confidence interval, CI: 11.13 to 30.39), 0.78 (95% CI: 0.76 to 0.80), and 0.80 (95% CI: 0.78 to 0.82), respectively. Moreover, an AUC of 0.83 was determined. Subgroup analysis showed that the study’s specimen type had a substantial impact on the precision of the microRNA-based IgAN diagnosis. The Deeks funnel plot (p = 0.56) showed no publication bias. Eventually, the Fagan column-line plot showed that using miRNA to diagnose IgAN improved diagnostic accuracy.ConclusionsMicroRNAs demonstrate promise as diagnostic biomarkers for IgAN, showing high sensitivity and specificity in this meta-analysis. However, further large-scale and high-quality studies are needed to validate these findings and establish their definitive clinical utility.Keywords: IgAN, MicroRNA, Diagnostic assay, Meta-analysis, Biomarker

Introduction

The commonest immune-mediated primary glomerulonephritis globally is immunoglobulin A Nephropathy (IgAN). Its characteristic pathology is the deposition of complement C3 and IgA in the glomerular mesangium, usually accompanied by co-depositions of IgG and/or IgM, mesangial cell proliferation, and extracellular matrix expansion [1, 2]. The total incidence is estimated to be at least 2.5 cases per 100,000 population, while changes in biopsy methods may have an impact on the actual incidence [3, 4]. IgAN is a prominent clinical entity and a major cause of chronic kidney failure(CKF), in which 20–40% of patients advance to kidney failure within 10–20 years of diagnosis [5, 6]. Therefore, it is essential to identify and treat IgAN early to prevent its progression into CKF. The current gold standard for IgAN detection has been kidney biopsy. Nevertheless, Kidney biopsy is an invasive procedure during which there may be a bleeding risk [7]. Its poor repeatability is also a result of its invasiveness and the possibility of postoperative problems [8]. Therefore, finding a reliable and noninvasive biomarker is essential for diagnosing and tracking IgAN.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs), as small non-coding RNA molecules, play a crucial role in gene expression regulation. The study of their expression and function helps in understanding the cellular regulatory networks and the pathogenesis of diseases. Since their dysregulation is often associated with specific diseases, miRNAs have diagnostic and therapeutic potential. In terms of sample sources for miRNA analysis, due to their tissue-specific expression, appropriate tissue or body fluid samples need to be selected according to the research question and the nature of the disease. miRNAs are mainly located in the cytoplasm within the cell, though some can also be found in other compartments. Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) technology can be used to observe their localization. Regarding positive results, it includes identifying differentially expressed miRNAs between different groups, which may be related to diseases, and validating their target genes to clarify their functional significance. As for detection methods, quantitative reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) is used for accurately quantifying expression levels. Microarray analysis can detect a large number of miRNAs simultaneously but has relatively lower sensitivity. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) can sequence all miRNAs in a sample, enabling the discovery of novel miRNAs, but it is costly and requires complex data analysis.

Endogenous non-coding RNAs known as microRNAs (miRNAs) mainly influence gene expression by enhancing the degradation of miRNAs and/or inhibiting protein translation [9, 10]. Because miRNAs are easy to identify and extract, they have great potential as diagnostic markers in the laboratory and as a tool for clinical research and marketing [11]. miR-148b-3p inhibits C1GALT1 gene expression, thereby reducing the synthesis of galactosylated IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) and promoting immune complex deposition [12]. Furthermore, serum levels of miR-148b-3p in IgAN patients are significantly negatively correlated with Gd-IgA1.Multiple studies have explored the utility of miRNAs as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers in IgA nephropathy [13]. For instance, demonstrated that miR − 21 was upregulated in renal tissues of IgA nephropathy patients. Functional experiments in vitro showed that miR − 21 promoted the proliferation and fibrosis of renal mesangial cells, suggesting its potential role in the pathogenesis of IgA nephropathy and as a potential therapeutic target [14]. Another study reported that serum miR − 192 levels were significantly increased in IgA nephropathy patients compared with healthy controls. Receiver - operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis indicated that miR − 192 had good diagnostic value for differentiating IgA nephropathy patients from normal individuals, highlighting its potential as a non - invasive diagnostic biomarkert [15]. As a result, miRNAs have become an interesting topic in the search for non-invasive biomarkers. Our goal in conducting this research was to help with clinical diagnosis using non-invasive means by conducting a systematic review and meta-analysis of miRNAs in IgAN individuals.

Materials and methods

Protocol registration

Every step of the current study was conducted according to the guidelines shown within the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) declaration [16]. Registration number CRD42023459899 was assigned to the relevant review protocol in the PROSPERO database during the prospective registration process.

Data sources and search strategy

A comprehensive search of eight databases was employed, namely Wanfang Data, Embase, PubMed, Web of Science, SINOMED, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), China Science and Technology Journal Database (VIP), and Cochrane Library, for studies published between their start and July 20, 2023. We also searched full-texts and references of all possible papers, including pertinent reviews and meta-analyses, in addition to the references of the included articles, to find any more relevant articles. Our search was not restricted by language. Several phrases, including “IgAN,” “microRNA,” and “Diagnosis,” were part of our search strategies. The comprehensive search approach used in PubMed is depicted in Figure 1 as an example. Other database search strategies are detailed in the Appendix. In cases when two reviewers (MZ and ZZ) could not agree, a third reviewer (BL) was asked to help settle the dispute and complete the screening process. Furthermore, we searched for additional relevant research by carefully reading the full texts, reference lists, and bibliographies of all the found publications. This included relevant reviews and meta-analyses.

Data extraction

The NoteExpress® program made data administration easier. All data extraction was carried out by two separate reviewers (MZ and ZZ), and any conflicts were settled via discussion. A third party (BL) was approached to reach an agreement if needed. Every study considered for inclusion had its first author’s name, participant details, publication year, sample size and origin, region of origin, microRNA expression findings, diagnostic FN, FP, TN, TP, specificity, sensitivity, and AUC meticulously documented.

Quality assessment

Employing the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2 (QUADAS-2), a reliable tool for assessing the quality of systematic reviews in investigations of diagnostic accuracy, two authors (MZ and ZZ) independently evaluated the included studies’ qualities [17].

Statistical analysis

Metadisc® software was used to incorporate the test assessment indicators. We next plotted the SROC and determined the AUC. Greater diagnostic efficiency was indicated by higher AUC values, with a value closer to 1 indicating better performance. To further investigate whether a threshold effect was present, we computed the Spearman correlation coefficient. For determining whether there was any variation between the studies regarding each outcome indicator, the inconsistency index (I2) and the Q test were also employed. The random effects model was utilized for analysis when I2 was determined to be 50% or higher, indicating significant heterogeneity. When I2 was less than 50%, which indicates little heterogeneity, the fixed effects model was used in contrast. Finally, potential publication bias and assessments of heterogeneity were performed using Stata software.

Ethics

This study did not need ethical approval since it did not collect any personally identifiable information from patients.

Results

Search results

The article selection procedure is shown in the flowchart (Fig. 2). Among 703 articles that were initially included via a strategic search approach; there were 553 items left for screening after 150 duplicates were removed manually. Letters, animal experiments, cell line research, and review papers made up 425 publications deemed irrelevant after manual inspection of abstracts and titles. Screening 128 articles via full texts led to the exclusion of 119 due to non-eligibility. Eventually, the current meta-analysis included 20 studies from nine articles [18–26].

MicroRNA diagnostic accuracy in IgAN

Forest plots were used to examine the specificity and sensitivity of miRNAs in the nine studies. Based on all the data, the following were determined: sensitivity: 0.80 (95% CI: 0.78–0.82), specificity: 0.78 (95% CI: 0.76–0.80), PLR: 3.79 (95% CI: 2.92–4.92), NLR: 0.23 (95% CI: 0.17–0.31), and DOR: 18.39 (95% CI: 11.13–30.39). Figure 4 shows that the AUC was 0.883. Findings like these show that miRNAs are quite accurate in diagnosing IgAN.

Merged index results (the figures are forest plots of combined (A) sensitivity, (B) specificity, (C) positive likelihood ratios, (D) negative likelihood ratios, and (E) diagnostic ratio ratios, while (F) is a curve of the composite subject’s work characteristics)

Meta-regression analysis

The investigations had marked heterogeneity, according to the pooled sensitivity (I2 = 85.8%, p = 0.00) and specificity (I2 = 84.4%, p = 0.00). Three factors—specimen type, sample size, and location—were included in a meta-analysis to investigate the causes of this heterogeneity. Fig. 5 shows the results of our investigation showing that the kind of specimen had a substantial impact on sensitivity and specificity.

Sensitivity analysis

Conducting a sensitivity analysis for assessing our meta-analysis stability was approached. The results were consistently unaffected when studies were sequentially removed, indicating the stability and reliability of our findings (Fig. 6).

Publication bias

Deeks’ funnel plot approach was employed for assessing bias risk. The funnel plots showed a P-value of > 0.05 (Fig. 7), which means that there is no obvious publication bias.

Clinical diagnostic value

A diagnostic meta-analysis incorporating nine studies was conducted to evaluate the clinical utility of miRNA biomarkers for IgA nephropathy (IgAN) using a Fagan nomogram. Key findings revealed remarkable predictive potential: With a pre-test probability set at 50%, the implementation of miRNA testing significantly altered diagnostic probabilities. Specifically:1.Positive miRNA results increased the post-test probability to 81%;2.Negative miRNA results reduced the post-test probability to 19%.

This demonstrates substantial clinical impact: A positive miRNA test more than doubles the diagnostic confidence (from 50% to 81%), while a negative result reduces the likelihood by over 60% (from 50% to 19%). The combination of a moderately strong PLR (4) and an excellent NLR (0.22) indicates that miRNA biomarkers serve as an effective rule-in and rule-out tool for IgAN diagnosis. These quantitative findings, as illustrated in Fig. 8, establish miRNA testing as a valuable adjunct to enhance diagnostic accuracy in clinical practice.

Discussion

The study found that miRNA exhibits high diagnostic capability in detecting IgAN. The diagnostic odds ratio, specificity, and sensitivity of miRNA are all relatively high, and the area under the curve is also relatively large, indicating that miRNA has great potential as a diagnostic marker for IgAN. The study also discovered that the e sample type could affect the accuracy of miRNA detection. Additionally, no publication bias was found, indicating the research results are relatively reliable. miRNA testing can improve the accuracy of IgAN diagnosis, helping to confirm or rule out the disease.

Blood, as a comprehensive biological fluid, can reflect the overall physiological state of the body and provide a systematic perspective for detection. Its components interact complexly with the target analytes. Some substances may interfere with the sensitivity of the detection as they can disrupt the binding between the detection reagents and the target microRNAs. However, blood is also rich in miRNAs released by various tissues and organs, which can provide valuable diagnostic information. The expression levels of certain miRNAs (such as miR-155) in the blood of patients significantly differ from those in healthy individuals. This may be related to the activation of the immune response in the disease state [27].

Urine is a filtrate of metabolic waste products and contains miRNAs excreted by the kidneys or released from other tissues and then eliminated through the urinary system. The non-invasive collection of urine samples is an advantage. However, the concentration of miRNAs in urine is generally low, and it is affected by pH and electrolytes, posing challenges for detection. The level of miR-451 in the urine of patients is lower than that in healthy individuals [28]. The decreased expression may reflect changes in physiological functions related to the disease, highlighting its potential as a non-invasive biomarker for distinguishing patients from healthy individuals. Urinary deposits are increasingly being used as non-invasive biomarkers for various kidney problems, and the gradual realization of miRNAs’ potential as biomarkers for kidney diseases has been noteworthy in recent years [29–33]. Biomarkers involving microRNAs have been studied extensively, particularly in the context of IgAN. Specifically, Wang et al. found an inverse correlation between the degree of proteinuria and miR-429, miR-200b, and miR-200a expression levels [34]. Moreover, a positive correlation was detected between urine protein levels and miR-146a and miR-155 concentrations, which were considerably greater in IgAN patients [35]. Essential microRNAs are crucial for IgAN pathogenesis. According to Serino et al., the deregulation of miR-148b is vital for IgAN development and may contribute to galactose insufficiency among sick subjects [36]. A different research study found that miR-21 inhibition might inhibit IgAN from activating the PTEN/Akt pathway, preventing podocyte and kidney tubular cell fibrotic activation [37].

This study examined 20 miRNAs, including the following: let-7b, miR-148b, miR-196b, miR-21, miR-25-3p, miR-144-3p, miR-486-5p, and the combination of let-7b and miR-148b. Additional miRNA combinations that were examined included four (miR-148a-3p+miR-150-5p+miR-20a-5p+miR-425-3p) and three (miR-25-3p+miR-144-3p+miR-486-5p).The following microRNAs were downregulated in IgAN patients: miR-485-3p, miR-590, miR-33a-5p, miR-29, miR-15a-5p, miR-34a, miR-205, and miR-155. Conversely, there was a notable upregulation in the miR-148b and let-7b levels.

In particular, miR-148b regulates IgAN by aiming at glycoprotein-N-acetylgalactosamine-3-beta-galactosyltransferase 1 (C1GALT1), a critical enzyme for glycosylation. The amount of galactose in the hinge region of the IgA1 heavy chain is decreased when the expression of the C1GALT1 gene is decreased. At the same time, let-7b binds to UDP-GalNAc: polypeptide N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase 2 (GALNT2), an enzyme that connects IgA1‘s hinge region to threonine or serine residues [33].

Moreover, miR-155 increases IgA levels by suppressing the suppressor of cytokine signaling-1 (SOCS-1), which in turn reduces COSMC gene expression [38]. Podocyte apoptosis in IgAN patients can be prevented by overexpressing miR-25-3p, which decreases IgA1 accumulation [39]. In addition, miR-150-5p has been linked to mucosal immunology and a possible role in IgAN pathogenesis [40, 41].

There is growing evidence that these microRNAs control many molecular processes involved in IgAN’s physiological and pathological manifestations. This could lead to the development of non-invasive methods of diagnosing the disease.

A total of 3359 subjects were recruited within the nine included publications, of whom 1770 were diagnosed with IgAN, whereas 1589 were not. The evidence in the meta-analysis points to miRNAs as a potential biomarker for IgAN diagnosis, which is sensitive and specific. Results showed that specimen type greatly affected sensitivity and specificity. There was no publication bias (p = 0.56) since the literature was evenly distributed around the regression line, as shown by Deeks’ funnel plot. Using miRNA to diagnose IgAN improves diagnostic accuracy, as Fagan’s nomogram shows.

While this study demonstrates the potential of miRNAs as non-invasive diagnostic tools for IgAN, exhibiting high sensitivity and specificity, it also acknowledges several limitations. Differences in sample types may lead to inconsistent results, affecting the universality and reliability of the findings. Additionally, the current number and quality of studies are insufficient to establish miRNAs as diagnostic biomarkers fully. Furthermore, the study fails to delve deeply into other potential factors that may influence miRNA expression and detection accuracy. Future research should further explore the impact of different sample types on diagnosis, expand the scale of studies to validate the effectiveness of miRNAs, and thoroughly consider and control other potential influencing factors to improve and optimize IgAN diagnostic techniques continuously.

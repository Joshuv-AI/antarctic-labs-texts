Uniform Bounds on the Level of Cyclotomic Division Fields of Elliptic Curves
Sam Allen, Tyler Genao

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that for each number field F F there exists a uniform bound on the prime levels p p of elliptic curves E / F E/F for which F ⁡ ( E ⁡ [ p ] ) = F ⁡ ( ζ p ) F(E[p])=F(\zeta_{p}) . Under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, we also give uniform bounds on p p for which F ⁡ ( E ⁡ [ p ] ) / F F(E[p])/F is abelian, provided that F F has no rational complex multiplication. These are generalizations of results of González-Jiménez and Lozano-Robledo [ 7 ] to general number fields.



1.
Introduction

Given a characteristic zero field
F
and an elliptic curve
E/F
, for each integer
n>0
the
n
-division field of
E
over
F
, denoted
F(E[n])
, is the field obtained by adjoining to
F
the
x
and
y
-coordinates of all points
P\in E
whose order divides
n
. The
n
-division field also appears as the fixed field under the action of the absolute Galois group
G_{F}:=\textrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
on the
n
-torsion subgroup
E[n]
of
E
. The
n
-division field is a finite Galois extension of
F
, with Galois group isomorphic to a subgroup of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
, the general linear group of
2\times 2
matrices over
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
.
In analogy to the Kronecker-Weber theorem and roots of unity over
\mathbb{Q}
, the coordinates of
n
-torsion points of elliptic curves can lend an explicit description of class field theory for imaginary quadratic fields.

For an elliptic curve
E/F
, the existence of the perfect, Galois-invariant
n
-Weil pairing on
E
guarantees that
F(E[n])
contains a primitive
n
’th root of unity
\zeta_{n}

[
16
, Corollary III.8.1.1]
. A natural question is as follows:
how much larger is the
n
-division field
F(E[n])
compared to the
n
-cyclotomic field
F(\zeta_{n})
?
Intuitively, the
n
-division field should be strictly larger “most of the time” due to characteristics of the mod-
n
Galois representation
\rho_{E,n}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
, which describes the action of
G_{F}
on
E[n]
and whose kernel has fixed field
F(E[n])
. For example, for any elliptic curve
E
defined over its field of definition
F=\mathbb{Q}(j(E))
, if
E
has CM (complex multiplication) then Hamakiotes and Lozano-Robledo showed that
F(E[n])=F(\zeta_{n})
implies
n\leq 3

[
8
]
. On the other hand, for any number field
F
and any non-CM elliptic curve
E/F
, by Serre’s Open Image Theorem there exists a constant
c:=c(E,F)>0
such that for any prime
p>c
one has
\rho_{E,p}(G_{F})=\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})

[
15
]
, which forces
F(E[p])
to be nonabelian.

In light of the above, one can ask if for each number field
F
there exists a uniform bound on integers
n>0
such that there exists an elliptic curve
E/F
where
F(E[n])=F(\zeta_{n})
. For example, as a consequence of progress towards Serre’s uniformity question over
\mathbb{Q}
(whether the constant
c:=c(E,\mathbb{Q})
in the preceding paragraph can be chosen independently of
E
) one has for all primes
p>37
that
\mathbb{Q}(E[p])\neq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
.
In fact,
González-Jiménez and Lozano-Robledo have proven a
sharp
upper bound over
\mathbb{Q}

[
7
]
.

Theorem 1
.

[
7
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. If for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
one has
\mathbb{Q}(E[n])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
, then
n\leq 5
. More generally, if
\mathbb{Q}(E[n])/\mathbb{Q}
is abelian then
n\in\{2,3,4,5,6,8\}
, and
\emph{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[n])/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to 1 of 17 finite abelian groups.

González-Jiménez and Lozano-Robledo proved their result through a close analysis of Galois representations of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, in large part via group-theoretic considerations and explicit calculations with modular curves over
\mathbb{Q}
. However, given that considerably less is known about Galois representations over nontrivial number fields, many of their results do not immediately generalize beyond
\mathbb{Q}
.

In this paper, we initiate a study of cyclotomic division fields over general number fields
F
, where
cyclotomic
means
F(E[n])=F(\zeta_{n})
. Our first result shows that uniform bounds on prime levels of cyclotomic division fields exist over any number field
F
. We will use
\Delta_{F}
to denote the discriminant of
F/\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
F
be a number field. Then for all elliptic curves
E/F
and for all primes
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
F(E[p])=F(\zeta_{p})
, if
p\nmid\Delta_{F}
and
p>13
(resp.
p\mid\Delta_{F}
and
p>12[F:\mathbb{Q}]+1
),
then
E
has an order
p
point over an extension
K/F
where
[K:F]\leq 6
(resp.
[K:F]\leq 6[F:\mathbb{Q}]
). In particular,
p
is uniformly bounded.

We additionally prove more general results for when the mod-
p
image is abelian. In the following, by GRH we mean the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. Also recall that a number field is said to have rational CM if it contains an imaginary quadratic field.

Theorem 3
.

Let
F
be a number field. Then for all elliptic curves
E/F
and for all primes
p\geq 17
where
F(E[p])/F
is abelian, if
p\nmid\Delta_{F}
or
p>(6[F:\mathbb{Q}])!+1
, then
\rho_{E,p}(G_{F})
is contained in either the split or nonsplit Cartan subgroup mod-
p
. In particular, if GRH is true and
F
has no rational CM, then

is contained in a finite set

from
[
10

, and thus

It is worth noting that if

In contrast to Theorem

matrices over

2.
Preliminary Results

In this section, we will review some key definitions, notation and results we will need for Galois representations of elliptic curves.

2.1.
Definitions and notation

Fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
. Throughout this paper, we let
F
denote an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
; thus
F\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
E/F
an elliptic curve, i.e., a nonsingular projective curve of genus one with a point over
F
. We can assume that
E
is given by an equation

E:y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}

where each
a_{i}\in F
. The set
E:=E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
of algebraic points on
E
forms a group under a geometric chord and tangent law. There is a natural action of the absolute Galois group
G_{F}:=\textrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/F)
on
E
: for
\sigma\in G_{F}
and
P:=(x,y)\in E
, we let
\sigma
act on
P
by
\sigma(P):=(\sigma(x),\sigma(y))
. Let
E[\textrm{tors}]
denote the subgroup of
torsion points
of
E
(points with finite order). Then this action restricts to an action on
E[\textrm{tors}]
; in fact, for each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have an induced action on
E[n]
, the group of
n
-torsion points (points with order dividing
n
). This latter action is called the
mod-
n
Galois representation of
E
,
and is written as

\rho_{E,n}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{Aut}(E[n]).

The kernel of this action is
\textrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/F(E[n]))
, where
F(E[n])
is the
n
-division field of
E
;
this field is obtained by adjoining all coordinates of
n
-torsion points on
E
to
F
. Thus
F(E[n])/F
is a Galois extension, and we have a faithful action

\rho_{E,n}\colon\textrm{Gal}(F(E[n])/F)\hookrightarrow\textrm{Aut}(E[n]).

Recall that
E[n]
is a free rank two
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-module
[
16
, Corollary III.6.4]
. In particular, fixing a basis
\{P,Q\}
of
E[n]
given an isomorphism
\textrm{Aut}(E[n])\cong\textrm{GL}_{2}(n)
, where
\textrm{GL}_{2}(n):=\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
is the group of
2\times 2
invertible matrices over
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, we can write the mod-
n
Galois representation as

\rho_{E,n,P,Q}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(n).

Explicitly, if for
\sigma\in G_{F}
we have
\sigma(P)=aP+cQ
and
\sigma(Q)=bP+dQ
, then
\rho_{E,n,P,Q}(\sigma)=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
. Changing the basis of
E[n]
from
\{P,Q\}
conjugates this image. By abuse of notation, we will often write
\rho_{E,n}
instead of
\rho_{E,n,P,Q}
, with some implicit basis
\{P,Q\}
in mind.

In parallel to the above, recall that for an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
there is an action of
G_{F}
on the subgroup of
n
’th roots of unity
\mu_{n}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. This action is described by the
mod-
n
cyclotomic character

\chi_{n}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{Aut}(\mu_{n}).

Fixing a primitive
n
’th root
\zeta_{n}\in\mu_{n}
, this action is explicitly

\displaystyle\chi_{n}(\sigma):=a_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\text{where }\sigma(\zeta_{n})=\zeta_{n}^{a_{\sigma}}.

The value
\chi_{n}(\sigma)
is independent of the choice of
\zeta_{n}
.

The Galois action on the
n
-torsion subgroup of an elliptic curve and the subgroup of
n
’th roots of unity are closely connected. For an elliptic curve
E/F
, a consequence of properties of the
n
-Weil pairing (see
[
16
, Proposition III.8.1]
) is that the determinant of
\rho_{E,n}
is the mod-
n
cyclotomic character: for any
\sigma\in G_{F}
, one has

\det\rho_{E,n}(\sigma)=\chi_{n}(\sigma).

The Weil pairing also implies that
\zeta_{n}\in F(E[n])
, where
\zeta_{n}
is a primitive
n
’th root of unity
[
16
, Corollary III.8.1.1]
. Let us say that the division field
F(E[n])
is
cyclotomic
if
F(E[n])=F(\zeta_{n})
.

2.2.
Subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)

In his landmark paper, Serre
[
15
]
proved that for any number field
F
and any non-CM elliptic curve
E/F
, one has for sufficiently large primes
p\gg_{E,F}0
that
\rho_{E,p}(G_{F})=\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
. An important step towards proving this was understanding what the action of inertia on
E[p]
could be over a field of characteristic zero. In this section, we will review these results of Serre, since they play a crucial role in our analysis of cyclotomic division fields – intuitively, these inertia images will be too large for our mod-
p
Galois images if
p
is unbounded.

Let us review a classification result for subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
first, given by Serre
[
15
]
and essentially due to work of Dickson
[
3
]
. First, for an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we let
\textrm{SL}_{2}(n):=\textrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
denote the subgroup of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(n)
of matrices with determinant
1
. Assume that
p\geq 3
is prime; we define the
split Cartan group mod-
p
as the subgroup of diagonal matrices,

C_{s}(p):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)\right\}.

Its normalizer is denoted
N_{s}(p)
; explicitly, we have

N_{s}(p)=C_{s}(p)\cup\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}C_{s}(p)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\
c&0\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)\right\}.

We call elements of
C_{s}(p)

diagonal,
and elements of
N_{s}(p)\smallsetminus C_{s}(p)

antidiagonal.

Each diagonal matrix
m:=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in C_{s}(p)
has a
flip,
which is
m_{f}:=\begin{bmatrix}d&0\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}
.
For a subgroup
H\subseteq C_{s}(p)
, we let
H_{f}
denote the subgroup of all flips of elements from
H
.

We will also define the
nonsplit Cartan group mod-
p
,
denoted
C_{ns}(p)
, as follows. We will write
\mathbb{F}_{p}:=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.
Fix the least positive integer
\epsilon
which generates
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
: then we have

C_{ns}(p)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\epsilon\\
b&a\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)\right\}.

This is the regular representation of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\sqrt{\epsilon}]^{\times}
acting on itself via multiplication with respect to the basis
\{1,\sqrt{\epsilon}\}
. Its normalizer is

N_{ns}(p)=C_{ns}(p)\cup\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}C_{ns}(p)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\epsilon\\
b&a\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}a&b\epsilon\\
-b&-a\end{bmatrix}\right\}.

Let us also define the
Borel subgroup mod-
p
as the subgroup of upper triangular matrices,

B_{0}(p):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)\right\}.

We recall that for any subgroup
H\subseteq B_{0}(p)
, its
semisimplification
is

H^{\text{ss}}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p):\exists b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\text{ such that }\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)\right\}.

We define the
semi-Cartan subgroup mod-
p
as

D:=D(p):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)\right\}.

Finally, we use
Z:=Z(p)
to denote the subgroup of scalar matrices in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
.

Here is the Serre-Dickson classification result. For subgroups
G,H\subseteq\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
, when we write
H\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}G
we mean that
H
is contained in
G
up to conjugacy.

Theorem 4

(Classification of subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
)
.

[
3
,
15
]

Fix a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and let
G\subseteq\emph{GL}_{2}(p)
be a subgroup. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

G
contains
\emph{SL}_{2}(p)
(
\emph{SL}_{2}
case).

(2)

We have
G\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}B_{0}(p)
(Borel case).

(3)

We have
G\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}N_{s}(p)
or
G\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}N_{ns}(p)
(normalizer of Cartan case).

(4)

The projective quotient
G/Z\cap G
is isomorphic to
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
or
A_{5}
(exceptional cases).

2.3.
Bounds on the level of Cartan Galois images

To wrap this section up, we will describe a result of Larson and Vaintrob on isogeny characters of elliptic curves; this will give us uniform bounds on prime levels
p
of abelian
p
-division fields, which is Theorem
3
. Recall that for an elliptic curve
E/F
, a finite cyclic subgroup
C\subseteq E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
is
F
-rational
if it is stable under the action of
G_{F}
, i.e., for each
R\in C
and
\sigma\in G_{F}
one has
\sigma(R)\in C
. Setting
n:=|C|
, the associated representation

r\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{Aut}(C)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

is called the
isogeny character of
C
.
Explicitly, writing
C=\langle P\rangle
one has for each
\sigma\in G_{F}
that

\displaystyle r(\sigma):=a_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\text{where }\sigma(P)=a_{\sigma}P.

Theorem 5
.

[
10
, Theorem 1]

For a number field
F
, there exists an effectively computable set
S_{F}
of prime numbers such that the following holds. Let
p
be a prime not in
S_{F}
, and let
E/F
be an elliptic curve such that
E[p]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
is reducible, say with degree one associated character
r\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{\times}
. Then one of the following holds.

(1)

There exists a CM elliptic curve
E^{\prime}/F
with CM field
K\subseteq F
, and associated characters
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{\times}
such that up to conjugation

\rho_{E^{\prime},p}(G_{F})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}=\emph{im}\begin{bmatrix}\psi&0\\
0&\psi^{\prime}\end{bmatrix},

where
\psi^{12}=r^{12}
.

(2)

GRH fails for
F[\sqrt{-p}]
, and one has

r^{12}=\chi_{p}^{6}.

Moreover,
E
has an
F
-rational
p
-isogeny and
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.

Let us explain how Theorem
5
gives uniform bounds on the level of absolutely reducible mod-
p
Galois images. Fix an elliptic curve
E/F
and a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and set
G:=\rho_{E,p}(G_{F})
. If
G\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}B_{0}(p)
, then
E
has an
F
-rational
p
-isogeny, i.e., there exists a
G_{F}
-stable subspace of
E[p]
. This implies that
E[p]
is reducible, and so if
p\not\in S_{F}
, then Theorem
5
implies that
F
contains a CM field or that GRH fails for
F[\sqrt{-p}]
. Similarly, if
G\subseteq C_{ns}(p)
, then
E[p]
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
but reducible over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
(since
G\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
is diagonalizable). Therefore, if
G
is contained in
B_{0}(p)
,
C_{s}(p)
or
C_{ns}(p)
up to conjugacy, and both GRH is true and
F
has no rational CM, then
p\in S_{F}
, and thus
p
is uniformly bounded.

3.
Galois Representations and the Image of Inertia

In this section, we will prove various results on Galois representations of elliptic curves. In particular, we will provide alternate group-theoretic characterizations of cyclotomic and abelian division fields of elliptic curves, and give a description of the mod-
p
images of inertia subgroups for elliptic curves over local fields, following
[
15
]
. We will also give several explicit descriptions of abelian subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
.

3.1.
An alternate description of cyclotomic and abelian division fields

Let us first characterize cyclotomic and abelian division fields in terms of images of Galois representations. The main result of this paper is Theorem
2
, which says that for a number field
F
, for sufficiently large primes
p\gg_{F}0
and for all elliptic curves
E/F
, one has
F(E[p])\neq F(\zeta_{p})
.
The guiding philosophy is that the mod-
p
Galois image is “relatively uniformly large” when
p
is uniformly large.
This has been demonstrated over
\mathbb{Q}
by progress towards Serre’s uniformity question
[
11
,
4
]
, and over numbers fields without rational CM
[
5
,
9
,
6
]
.

Here is our characterization of cyclotomic and abelian division fields in terms of Galois representations. Note that a cyclotomic division field is automatically abelian.

Proposition 6
.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}
be an algebraic extension,
E/F
an elliptic curve and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Set
G:=\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})
.

(1)

One has that
G
is abelian if and only if
F(E[n])/F
is abelian.

(2)

One has that
G\cap\emph{SL}_{2}(n)=1
iff
F(E[n])=F(\zeta_{n})
. In such a case, one has that
G
is abelian, with
\det\colon G\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
being an injection.

Proof.

First, let us observe that the mod-
n
Galois representation

\rho_{E,n}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(n)

has kernel
\textrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/F(E[n]))
. Modding out by this kernel induces a faithful representation

\rho_{E,n}\colon\textrm{Gal}(F(E[n])/F)\hookrightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(n),

and thus we have
G\cong\textrm{Gal}(F(E[n])/F)
. In particular, we have that
G
is abelian if and only if
F(E[n])/F
is abelian. This proves part a.

For part b., observe that the kernel of the determinant map
\det\colon G\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is
G\cap\textrm{SL}_{2}(n)
, and thus
G\cap\textrm{SL}_{2}(n)
can be identified with a subgroup of
\textrm{Gal}(F(E[n])/F)
whose fixed field
K
is Galois over
F
. We claim that
K=F(\zeta_{n})
. To see this, by the
n
-Weil pairing one has for all
\sigma\in G_{F}
that

\sigma(\zeta_{n})=\zeta_{n}^{\det\rho_{E,n}(\sigma)}.

It follows that an automorphism
\sigma\in G_{F}
fixes
\zeta_{n}
if and only if
\rho_{E,n}(\sigma)\in G\cap\textrm{SL}_{2}(n)
, iff
\sigma
fixes
K
. We deduce that
K=F(\zeta_{n})
, and so

G\cap\textrm{SL}_{2}(n)\cong\textrm{Gal}(F(E[n])/F(\zeta_{n})).

Therefore, we conclude that
G\cap\textrm{SL}_{2}(n)=1
iff
F(E[n])=F(\zeta_{n})
. In this case, the kernel of
\det\colon G\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is trivial.
∎

Remark 7
.

Proposition
6
shows that if an elliptic curve
E/F
has a cyclotomic
n
-division field, then
\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})
is isomorphic to its image
\det\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})
. If each prime
p\mid n
is unramified in
F
, then one can show that the mod-
n
cyclotomic character
\chi_{n}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is surjective, so from
\det\rho_{E,n}=\chi_{n}
we conclude that
\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
under the determinant map.

3.2.
The image of inertia à la Serre

Let us review Serre’s work on describing the image of the inertia subgroup under a mod-
p
Galois representation of an elliptic curve
[
15
]
.
Fix a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and let
K
be an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
; let
k
denote the residue field of
K
. Also let
K_{\text{nr}}
denote the maximal unramified extension of
K
. Then the
inertia group of
K
is
I:=I_{K}:=\textrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K_{\text{nr}})
. The inertia group fits into a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow I\rightarrow G_{K}\rightarrow G_{k}\rightarrow 1.

Let
K_{t}
denote the maximal tamely ramified extension of
K
; thus
K\subseteq K_{\text{nr}}\subseteq K_{t}
. Let
I_{p}:=I_{K,p}:=\textrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K_{t})
denote the
wild inertia group of
K
,
and
I_{t}:=I_{K,t}:=I/I_{p}\cong\textrm{Gal}(K^{t}/K^{\text{nr}})
the
tame inertia group of
K
.
We note that
I_{p}
is the largest pro-
p
-group in
I
.

More can be said about
I_{t}
. Fixing a uniformizer
\pi
of
K_{\text{nr}}
, we know that
K_{t}
is a compositum of the Galois extensions
K(\sqrt[d]{\pi})/K
where each
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is coprime to
p
. Therefore,
I_{t}
can be identified as an inverse limit

I_{t}=\varprojlim_{d:\gcd(p,d)=1}\textrm{Gal}(K(\sqrt[d]{\pi})/K).

Thus,
I_{t}
is a pro-cyclic group.
For each
d
considered above, we have a natural isomorphism
\theta_{d}\colon\textrm{Gal}(K(\sqrt[d]{\pi})/K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mu_{d}
, where
\mu_{d}
is the subgroup of
d
’th roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
(which is isomorphic to the subgroup of
d
’th roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
). In particular, we have an induced surjection
\theta_{d}\colon I_{t}\twoheadrightarrow\mu_{d}
. When
d=p^{r}-1
for some
r>0
, we can identify
\mu_{d}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}^{\times}
; in this case, we call
\theta_{p^{r}-1}\colon I_{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}^{\times}
a
fundamental character of level
r
.
These characters describe the action of the tame inertia group on
p
-torsion subgroups of elliptic curves
[
15
]
.

The following result is essentially from
[
15
]
, which was proven in the semistable case where the absolute ramification index
e:=e(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is trivial; however, Serre essentially gave the ingredients for the proof where
e>1
. Lozano-Robledo proved an analogous result for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
which are allowed to have additive reduction
[
13
]
. Here, we state Serre’s result as a mild generalization of both cases, where both additive reduction and a nontrivial absolute ramification index are allowed.

Theorem 8
.

[
13
, Theorem 3.2]

Fix a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and a finite extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of degree
d
. Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve. Then there exists a finite extension
L/K
such that
E/L
is semistable, whose ramification index satisfies
e:=e(L/K)\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
.

Let
e_{0}:=e(K/\mathbb{Q})
denote the absolute ramification index of
K
.
Let
I
denote the inertia group of
L
, and
I_{p}
the wild inertia group.

(1)

Suppose that
E/L
has good ordinary or bad multiplicative reduction. Then
E
has an
L
-rational
p
-isogeny. Fix a basis of
E[p]
such that
\rho_{E,p}(G_{L})
is upper triangular. Then we have the following:

(a)

If the wild inertia group
I_{p}
acts trivially on
E[p]
, then the mod-
p
image of
I
is semi-Cartan. More precisely, we have

\rho_{E,p}(I)=\emph{im}\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{p-1}^{ee_{0}}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=D^{ee_{0}}.

(b)

If
I_{p}
acts nontrivially on
E[p]
, then we have

\rho_{E,p}(I)=\left\langle\emph{im}\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{p-1}^{ee_{0}}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\gamma\right\rangle=\left\langle D^{ee_{0}},\gamma\right\rangle,

where
\gamma:=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
.

(2)

Suppose that
E/L
has good supersingular reduction.

(a)

If
I_{p}
acts trivially on
E[p]
, then up to conjugacy the mod-
p
image of
I
is the
ee_{0}
’th power of the non-split Cartan group mod-
p
, i.e., we have

\rho_{E,p}(I)=C_{ns}(p)^{ee_{0}}

for an appropriate choice of basis for
E[p]
.

(b)

If
I_{p}
acts nontrivially on
E[p]
, then with respect to an appropriate basis, we have that
\rho_{E,p}(I)\subseteq B_{0}(p)
. Furthermore, we have
\gamma\in\rho_{E,p}(I)
, and can write the semisimplification of
\rho_{E,p}(I)
as

\rho_{E,p}(I)^{\emph{ss}}=\emph{im}\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{p-1}^{ee_{0}-e^{\prime}}&0\\
0&\theta_{p-1}^{e^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix},

where
e^{\prime}:=v(a_{p})
is the valuation of the
p
’th coefficient
a_{p}
in the formal group expansion of the multiplication-by-
p
map on
E
, which satisfies
0\leq e^{\prime}\leq ee_{0}
. In particular, we have
D^{(6d)!}\subseteq\rho_{E,p}(I)
.

Remark 9
.

As we will see when considering cyclotomic
p
-division fields, a quick size argument rules out the case where wild inertia acts nontrivially. However, wild inertia still needs consideration when studying abelian division fields.

3.3.
Abelian subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)

The way we prove Theorems
2
and
3
involves analyzing the possible “shapes” of subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
, using the maximal subgroup classification in Theorem
4
. To this end, we first prove which shapes are permissible for an abelian subgroup of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
p\geq 3
be prime, and let
G\subseteq\emph{GL}_{2}(p)
be a subgroup.

(1)

If
G
is abelian, then
G
is contained in
B_{0}(p)
,
N_{s}(p)
or
N_{ns}(p)
up to conjugacy.

(2)

If
G\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}B_{0}(p)
, then
G
is diagonalizable iff
p\nmid\#G
. When
G\subseteq B_{0}(p)
is abelian and
p\mid\#G
, one has
G=\langle\gamma,G\cap Z\rangle
where
\gamma:=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
.

(3)

For an abelian subgroup
G
that is contained in
N_{s}(p)
(resp.
N_{ns}(p)
) but not
C_{s}(p)
(resp.
C_{ns}(p)
), one has
G=\langle n,G\cap Z\rangle
for any element
n\in G\smallsetminus C_{s}(p)
(resp
n\in G\smallsetminus C_{ns}(p)
).

Before we prove this, let us prove a small lemma which allows us to analyze the two Cartan cases almost identically.

Lemma 11
.

Suppose that
N
is a finite group with an index two subgroup
C\unlhd N
. Let
G\subseteq N
be any subgroup, and suppose that
G\not\subseteq C
. Then for any
n\in G\smallsetminus C
one has
G=\langle n,G\cap C\rangle
, as well as
\langle n\rangle\cap C=\langle n^{2}\rangle
,
2\mid|n|
and
[G:G\cap C]=2
.

Proof.

To begin with, suppose that
n\in G\smallsetminus C
. Observe that for any
g\in G\smallsetminus C
, the cosets
nC
and
gC
are equal, and thus
n^{-1}g\in C
. Since
n^{-1}g\in G
, we deduce that
n^{-1}g\in G\cap C
, i.e.,
x\in\langle n,G\cap C\rangle
. This proves that
G=\langle n,G\cap C\rangle
.

Let us show that
\langle n\rangle\cap C=\langle n^{2}\rangle
. Since the coset
(nC)^{2}=1C
, we have
\langle n^{2}\rangle\subseteq\langle n\rangle\cap C
. For the other direction, we let
x\in\langle n\rangle\cap C
. From
x\in\langle n\rangle
, we can write
x=n^{k}
for some
0\leq k<|n|
. If
k
is odd, then we can write

xC=(n^{k-1}C)\cdot(nC)=nC,

which contradicts that
n\not\in C
. We deduce that
k
is even, whence we conclude
\langle n\rangle\cap C=\langle n^{2}\rangle
. Next, from the formula

|G|=\frac{|n|\cdot|G\cap C|}{|\langle n\rangle\cap(G\cap C)|}=\frac{|n|\cdot|G\cap C|}{|n^{2}|}

and from
|n^{2}|=\frac{|n|}{\gcd(2,|n|)}
, we also conclude that

|G|=\gcd(2,|n|)\cdot|G\cap C|.

From
G\not\subseteq C
we have
G\cap C\neq G
, which forces
\gcd(2,|n|)=2
. We conclude that both
2\mid|n|
and
[G:G\cap C]=2
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
10
.

By Theorem
4
on the classification of subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
, if
G
is abelian, up to conjugacy
G
is a subgroup of
B_{0}(p)
,
N_{s}(p)
or
N_{ns}(p)
. This proves part a. For part b., we note that
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
acts by left multiplication on the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
V:=\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
, where
e_{1}:=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix}
and
e_{2}:=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix}
. Thus, any subgroup
G\subseteq\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
also acts on
V
. By Maschke’s Theorem, if
p\nmid\#G
then
V
is a semisimple
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-module; in particular, any
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-submodule
W\subseteq V
has a complementary
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
-submodule
X\subseteq V
, so that
V=W\oplus X
. Thus, if
p\nmid\#G
then
V
has a
G
-stable subspace if and only if
G
is diagonalizable. Therefore, if we assume that
G\subseteq B_{0}(p)
, then
\langle e_{1}\rangle
is a
G
-stable subspace of
V
, whence we conclude that
p\nmid\#G
iff
G
is diagonalizable.

Suppose then that
G\subseteq B_{0}(p)
is abelian and
p\mid\#G
. In the following two paragraphs, we will show that
G=\langle\gamma,G\cap Z\rangle
. First, we show
\gamma\in G
: by the preceding paragraph, we have
G\not\subseteq C_{s}(p)
, and so there exists
m=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in G
with
b\neq 0
. We claim that we may choose
m\in G
such that
a=d
: if this were not the case, then all such matrices in
G
have distinct entries on their diagonal, and are thus individually diagonalizable; since
G
is abelian, this then implies that all elements of
G
are simultaneously diagonalizable, see e.g.
[
2
]
– this is impossible. Thus, we may assume that
a=d
. It follows that
m^{p-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&e\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
where
e:=(p-1)a^{p-2}b\neq 0
. Then choosing
x\in\mathbb{Z}
with
ex\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, we deduce that
m^{(p-1)x}=\gamma\in G
.

Next, we claim that
G=\langle G\cap C_{s}(p),\gamma\rangle
. This follows from the fact that for any
m=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in G
with
b\neq 0
, one has

m=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\cdot\gamma^{x}

where
x\in\mathbb{Z}
is chosen so that
x\equiv a^{-1}b\pmod{p}
. This shows that
G\subseteq\langle G\cap C_{s}(p),\gamma\rangle
, whence the claim follows.
Finally, one checks for any
m=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in G
that commutativity
\gamma\cdot m=m\cdot\gamma
forces
a=d
, and thus
G\cap C_{s}(p)=G\cap Z
. We conclude that
G=\langle\gamma,G\cap Z\rangle
, which proves part b.

For part c., we first assume that
G\subseteq N_{s}(p)
with
G\not\subseteq C_{s}(p)
. Then by Lemma
11
, we have for any element
n\in G\smallsetminus C_{s}(p)
that
G=\langle n,G\cap C_{s}(p)\rangle
. It suffices to show that
G\cap C_{s}(p)=G\cap Z
. This is similar to part b.: writing
n=\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\
c&0\end{bmatrix}
, we have for all
m=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in G\cap C_{s}(p)
that
nm=mn
, which forces
a=d
. This proves that
G=\langle n,G\cap Z\rangle
. The case where
G\subseteq N_{ns}(p)
with
G\not\subseteq C_{ns}(p)
is near identical by Lemma
11
: one checks here that for any
n=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\epsilon\\
-b&-a\end{bmatrix}\in G
and
m=\begin{bmatrix}c&d\epsilon\\
d&c\end{bmatrix}\in G\cap C_{ns}(p)
, one has from
nm=mn
that
d=0
, and thus
m\in G\cap Z
, which shows that
G=\langle n,G\cap C_{ns}(p)\rangle=\langle n,G\cap Z\rangle
.
∎

In our proofs of Theorems
2
and
3
, we will often need to argue that the image of inertia is an explicitly known subgroup of
\rho_{E,p}(G_{F})
, not just up to conjugacy. The following lemma lets us do this via the classification of subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
given in Theorem
4
. This lemma focuses on the case where the image contains a conjugate of the power of the semi-Cartan group or nonsplit Cartan subgroup mod-
p
.

Lemma 12
.

Fix a prime
p\geq 3
and an integer
k>0
. Set
\gamma:=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\in\emph{GL}_{2}(p)
.

(1)

If
D^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}\langle\gamma,Z\rangle
then
p-1\mid k
.

(2)

For any subgroup
G\subseteq C_{s}(p)
, if
D^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}G
and
p-1\nmid k
then
D^{k}\subseteq G
or
D^{k}_{f}\subseteq G
. If
D^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}N_{s}(p)\smallsetminus C_{s}(p)
, then
p-1\mid 2k
.

(3)

If
D^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}C_{ns}(p)
then
p-1\mid k
, and if
D^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}N_{ns}(p)
then
p-1\mid 2k
.

(4)

If
C_{ns}(p)^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}\langle\gamma,Z\rangle
then
p+1\mid\gcd(k,p^{2}-1)
.

Proof.

For this proof, let us fix a generator
g
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
; then it follows that
n:=\begin{bmatrix}g^{k}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
generates
D^{k}
, and has order
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(k,p-1)}
.

For part a., suppose that
D^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}\langle\gamma,Z\rangle
. Then for some
m=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
, we have
mD^{k}m^{-1}\subseteq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}x&xy\\
0&x\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)\right\}
. We check that

(1)

mnm^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}adg^{k}-bc&-ab(g^{k}-1)\\
cd(g^{k}-1)&ad-bcg^{k}\end{bmatrix}\frac{1}{ad-bc}.

This forces
cd(g^{k}-1)=0
. Since
|g^{k}|=\frac{p-1}{\gcd(k,p-1)}
, if
p-1\nmid k
then
\gcd(k,p-1)\neq p-1
, and thus
g^{k}\neq 1
, which implies that
cd=0
. If
c=0
, then we see that

mnm^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}g^{k}&-bd^{-1}(g^{k}-1)\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},

which means
g^{k}=1
, a contradiction. Thus
c\neq 0
, which implies that
d=0
, and so

mnm^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&ac^{-1}(g^{k}-1)\\
0&g^{k}\end{bmatrix},

again an impossibility. We conclude that
p-1\mid k
.

For part b., writing
mnm^{-1}\subseteq G\subseteq N_{s}(p)
for some
m:=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(p)
, we have that
mnm^{-1}
is either a diagonal or antidiagonal matrix. Assuming that
p-1\nmid k
, we have
g^{k}\neq 1
; and so (
1
) implies that either
ab=cd=0
(diagonal case), or
adg^{k}=bc
and
bcg^{k}=ad
(antidiagonal case).

In the diagonal case, if
c=0
then invertibility of
m
implies that
ad\neq 0
, and thus
b=0
, which shows by (
1
) that

mnm^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}g^{k}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=m;

thus
mD^{k}m^{-1}=D^{k}\subseteq G
. On the other hand, if
c\neq 0
then
d=0
, and thus
bc\neq 0
and
a=0
. This shows that

mnm^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&g^{k}\end{bmatrix}=m_{f}.

This implies that
mD^{k}m^{-1}=D^{k}_{f}\subseteq G
.

In the antidiagonal case, we have both
adg^{2k}=ad
and
bcg^{2k}=bc
. If
a=0
or
d=0
, then
bc\neq 0
; we thus have
ad\neq 0
or
bc\neq 0
, so that
g^{2k}=1
, and hence
p-1\mid 2k
. This concludes part b.

For part c., suppose that
D^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}N_{ns}(p)
. The eigenvalues of elements of
D^{k}
come in pairs
(x^{k},1)
where
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
; on the other hand, eigenvalues of elements from
C_{ns}(p)
come in pairs
u\pm v\sqrt{\epsilon}
, while eigenvalues from elements of
N_{ns}(p)\smallsetminus C_{ns}(p)
come in pairs
\pm\sqrt{u^{2}-v^{2}\epsilon}
(N.B.: in both cases we assume
(u,v)\neq(0,0)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
). In particular, from
D^{k}\overset{\sim}{\subseteq}N_{ns}(p)
we know that for some
(u,v)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
we have
g^{k}=u+v\sqrt{\epsilon}
and
1=u-v\sqrt{\epsilon}
, or (without loss of generality)
g^{k}=\sqrt{u^{2}-v^{2}\epsilon}
and
1=-\sqrt{u^{2}-v^{2}\epsilon}
. In the former case, this forces
u=1
and
v=0
, and thus
g^{k}=1
, which implies that
p-1\mid k
. In the latter case, we deduce that
g^{2k}=1
, and thus
p-1\mid 2k
.

Finally, for part d. we observe that elements of
\langle\gamma,Z\rangle
have repeated eigenvalues. As noted in part c., elements of
C_{ns}(p)
have eigenvalues of the form
a\pm b\sqrt{\epsilon}
, which forces elements of any conjugate of
C_{ns}(p)^{k}
in
\langle\gamma,Z\rangle
to be contained in
Z
. This implies that
\frac{p^{2}-1}{\gcd(k,p^{2}-1)}\mid p-1
, and so
p+1\mid\gcd(k,p^{2}-1)
.
∎

Lemma 13

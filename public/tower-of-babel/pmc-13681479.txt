Aggressive features and immune landscape of macrotrabecular hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Lixia Wu, Meidan Li, Xuefang Wang, Qionglan Wu, Yi Chen, Bin Wang, Lihong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•MT-HCC exhibits more aggressive clinical and pathological features than CV-HCC.•Higher AFP, larger tumor size and more vascular invasion are observed in MT-HCC.•Distinct immune cell distribution patterns are identified in different tumor regions.•CD20+ B cells in the tumor margin correlate with favorable prognosis.•FOXP3+ regulatory T cells and CTLA-4+ lymphocytes are linked to poorer outcomes.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, ranking as the sixth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the third most lethal [1,2]. The development of HCC is closely associated with chronic liver diseases, including hepatitis B and C infections, alcohol consumption, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease [3,4]. Despite advancements in diagnostic methods and treatments such as surgery, ablation, and systemic therapies, the overall survival rate of HCC patients remains low [5,6]. High recurrence rates, late diagnosis, and limited therapeutic efficacy are the primary challenges in improving patient outcomes, necessitating further exploration of HCC’s biological and clinical characteristics [7,8].

Among the recognized histological subtypes of HCC, macrotrabecular hepatocellular carcinoma (MT-HCC) is particularly notable due to its aggressive nature [9]. It is characterized by large trabeculae that exceed 10 cells in thickness, alongside significant vascular spaces and pronounced cellular atypia [10]. These pathological features often correlate with more severe clinical outcomes, as MT-HCC is commonly associated with higher rates of vascular invasion, the presence of satellite nodules, and areas of necrosis, all of which contribute to its poor prognosis [10,11]. In comparison to conventional hepatocellular carcinoma (CV-HCC), which tends to have a more indolent course, MT-HCC is more likely to progress rapidly and exhibit resistance to conventional therapies [9]. While previous studies have explored the distinct clinical and histopathological features of MT-HCC, there is still a substantial gap in understanding its underlying molecular mechanisms [12,13]. Additionally, the role of the immune microenvironment in MT-HCC progression and treatment response remains an area for further research and exploration [9].

The tumor microenvironment (TME) is a key player in cancer progression, immune evasion, and resistance to treatment. In HCC, the TME is shaped by various immune cells, including regulatory T cells (Tregs), cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), and B cells, which collectively influence tumor behavior and patient outcomes [14,15]. Tregs are particularly important for immune suppression, as they inhibit the activity of anti-tumor immune cells like CTLs. The presence of high levels of Tregs has been associated with poor prognosis in several cancer types, including HCC, due to their ability to promote immune tolerance and support tumor growth [[15], [16], [17]]. On the other hand, CTLs are critical for recognizing and eliminating tumor cells, and their infiltration into the TME often correlates with better clinical outcomes [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. B cells, though less understood in HCC, may either support or hinder tumor progression through the production of antibodies and interaction with other immune cells [[22], [23], [24]]. While these immune cell populations are recognized for their role in tumor immunity, there is a notable lack of studies focusing on the immune landscape of MT-HCC, an aggressive subtype of HCC. Specifically, the spatial distribution and functional dynamics of immune cells across different tumor regions—such as the tumor center, margin, and peritumoral areas—remain poorly characterized. These distinct regions may harbor unique immune profiles that significantly influence tumor behavior, progression, and patient prognosis. Filling this knowledge gap is essential for developing tailored immunotherapies that can more effectively target the immune microenvironment in MT-HCC.

The objective of this study is to address this gap by investigating the clinical, pathological, and immune characteristics of MT-HCC. We aim to analyze the spatial distribution of immune cells within different tumor regions and evaluate their correlation with patient outcomes. By elucidating the immune microenvironment of MT-HCC, this study seeks to provide novel insights into its pathogenesis and identify potential immunological targets for therapeutic intervention.

Materials and methods

Study population

This study included 83 patients diagnosed with MT-HCC and 111 patients diagnosed with CV-HCC. All patients underwent liver resection at Fujian Medical University Mengchao Hepatobiliary Hospital between January 2016 and December 2017. The inclusion criteria were: (1) first-time liver cancer resection without prior treatment, (2) pathological confirmation of MT-HCC or CV-HCC, (3) complete tumor resection with clear surgical margins, (4) availability of complete clinical and pathological data, and (5) written informed consent. Patients with other primary malignancies or incomplete follow-up data were excluded from the study. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University Mengchao Hepatobiliary Hospital. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to participation in the study. Based on prior literature reporting a 60% vs. 20% difference in AFP >100 ng/mL between MT-HCC and CV-HCC, we calculated the required sample size using G*Power software. With α=0.05 (two-sided), power=0.80, and effect size w = 0.3, the minimum required sample size was 74 patients per group. Our actual sample sizes (MT-HCC: n = 83; CV-HCC: n = 111) exceed this requirement, providing adequate statistical power for the clinicopathological comparisons.

Clinical data collection

The following clinical data were collected: age, gender, serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) level, hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection status, hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, cirrhosis, tumor size, tumor number, microvascular invasion (MVI), macroscopic vascular invasion, satellite nodules, tumor necrosis, and pathological stage. The follow-up period was measured from the date of surgery until the last valid follow-up visit or death. For all cases, the cut-off date is when the follow-up period has reached 5 years, and the survival time is measured in months. Overall survival (OS) and recurrence rates were calculated based on Kaplan-Meier methods.

Histopathological analysis

Histological examination was performed on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue samples. The tissues were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) to determine the histological features, including tumor cell arrangement and degree of cellular atypia. MT-HCC was identified by its characteristic trabecular structure, where trabeculae thickness exceeded 10 cells and vascular spaces were observed between trabeculae. The degree of cellular atypia was also assessed, with most MT-HCC cases showing significant cellular atypia.

Immunohistochemical analysis

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was performed on 48 MT-HCC samples to analyze the distribution of various immune cells in three distinct regions: the tumor center (MT-HCC-T), the tumor margin (MT-HCC-M), and the peritumoral region (MT-HCC-P). For the IHC analysis, 48 MT-HCC samples were selected based on the availability of tissue microarrays (TMAs) with sufficient tissue quality. These cases were consecutively enrolled without selection bias. The tissue sections were deparaffinized, antigen-retrieved, and blocked before incubation with primary antibodies. A secondary antibody was applied, and positive staining was visualized with a DAB (diaminobenzidine) substrate. The immune cell counts were assessed by two independent pathologists using a double-blind method.

Quantification of immune cells

The immune cells in each region were quantified by counting the number of positive cells within hotspot regions (× 200 magnification). Hotspot regions were defined as tissue areas exhibiting the highest density of positively stained cells, these regions were identified through screening by two independent pathologists under a 200 × objective lens, after excluding necrotic areas and large blood vessels. Ten hot spots were selected for each tissue section for counting. Cells were considered positive if they showed brown-yellow or brownish staining in the cell membrane or cytoplasm for CD3, CD4, CD8, CD20, PD-1, and PD-L1, or brown-yellow or brownish staining in the nucleus for FOXP3. For CTLA-4, positive cells were those showing brown-yellow or brownish staining in the membrane. Based on prior studies of immune prognostic markers in HCC, assuming a hazard ratio of 2.0, α=0.05, and power=0.80, the minimum required sample size was approximately 38 patients. Our IHC cohort of 48 MT-HCC patients meets this basic requirement, though we acknowledge that larger validation cohorts are needed.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 26.0. The clinical and pathological characteristics between MT-HCC and CV-HCC were compared using t-tests or Fisher's exact tests as appropriate. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan-Meier method and the log-rank test. Spearman’s rank correlation was used to analyze the correlations between immune cell markers in different tumor regions. Statistical plots and analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 and Origin 2024 software. To fully adjust for well‑established prognostic confounders of hepatocellular carcinoma, three independent multivariate Cox proportional‑hazards regression models were constructed for the tumor core, invasive tumor border, and peritumoral region, respectively. Each model incorporated all immune markers from the corresponding compartment together with clinical confounders yielding P < 0.1 in univariate analysis, including maximum tumor diameter, serum AFP level, pathological T stage, macrovascular invasion, and MVI. A full‑variable enter method was applied: all variables were forced into each model in their original continuous form without prior elimination of confounders. The reported hazard ratios (HRs) for each immune marker represented adjusted HRs after accounting for all clinical confounders as well as other immune parameters within the same compartment, so as to evaluate the independent prognostic value of each immune‑infiltration index. Only variables with clinically meaningful prognostic associations were presented in the results table. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Survival prognostic analysis showed that in the present study, the median follow-up duration for both cohorts reached 60.0 months; the mean follow-up duration for MT-HCC patients was 34.7 months, versus 47.7 months for CV-HCC patients. The follow-up was censored on December 31, 2022. Over the entire follow-up period, 60 patients (30.9%) succumbed to disease, and tumor recurrence occurred in 115 patients (59.3%). The maximum observed overall survival (OS) was 60 months, which aligns with the 5-year postoperative follow-up endpoint set for this study. As of the cutoff date, 51 patients remained alive with follow-up durations shorter than 5 years, and their follow-up times conformed to a continuous distribution (median: 38.1 months; range: 5.6–59.2 months). Among the total 194 enrolled patients, only one patient was lost to follow-up with a follow-up duration of < 1 year, translating to a loss-to-follow-up rate of <<1%. The mean survival time was 41.2 months for the MT-HCC group, compared with 51.6 months for the CV-HCC group. For MT-HCC patients, the median overall survival (mOS) was not reached, and the 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year overall survival rates were 78.9%, 66.5%, and 59.5%, respectively; the median recurrence-free survival (mRFS) was 14.2 months, with corresponding 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year recurrence-free survival rates of 57.1%, 40.5%, and 33.1%, respectively. Kaplan–Meier survival curve analysis indicated that CV-HCC patients exhibited a lower overall recurrence rate and a higher overall survival rate relative to MT-HCC patients (Figs. 1C and D).

Distribution of immune cells in MT-HCC

Immunohistochemical analysis of immune cell markers (CD3, CD4, CD8, CD20, PD-1, PD-L1, FOXP3, CTLA-4) across different regions of MT-HCC—tumor (MT-HCC-T), tumor margin (MT-HCC-M), and peritumoral region (MT-HCC-P)—revealed distinct patterns of immune cell distribution. The results showed that immune cells were mainly concentrated at the tumor margin. CD4+, CD8+, CD20+, PD-L1+, and FOXP3+ cells were more abundant in the MT-HCC-M region than in the MT-HCC-T and MT-HCC-P regions. CD3+ and PD-1+ cells were more abundant in the MT-HCC-P region than in the MT-HCC-M and MT-HCC-T regions. Meanwhile, the number of CTLA-4+ lymphocytes was higher in the MT-HCC-M region than in the MT-HCC-T and MT-HCC-P regions (Fig. 3). In general, immune cells were predominantly located in the tumor margin and peritumoral areas. Quantitative analysis of immune cells and prognostic implications

Quantitative analysis of immune cell counts in the different regions of MT-HCC revealed significant differences in the immune infiltrates across the tumor, margin, and peritumoral regions. The number of CD4+ lymphocytes was higher in the MT-HCC-M region than in the MT-HCC-P region, and this difference was statistically significant (P = 0.012). Similarly, the number of CTLA-4+ lymphocytes was higher in the MT-HCC-M region than in the MT-HCC-P region, with a statistically significant difference (P < 0.001). However, there were no significant differences in the numbers of CD3+, CD8+, CD20+, PD-1+, PD-L1+, and FOXP3+ lymphocytes between the MT-HCC-M and MT-HCC-P regions. Correlation analysis of immune cells

Spearman correlation analysis of immune cells in different MT-HCC regions showed that in the MT-HCC-T region, all immune cells exhibited positive correlations with each other, with correlation coefficients (r values) ranging from 0.133 to 0.701. In the MT-HCC-M region, PD-L1 showed a negative correlation with CD3, CD8, CD20, PD-1, FOXP3, and CTLA-4 (r values ranging from −0.314 to −0.014), while other immune cells exhibited positive correlations (r values ranging from 0.047 to 0.555). In the MT-HCC-P region, FOXP3 showed a negative correlation with CD20, PD-1, and PD-L1 (r values ranging from −0.059 to −0.003), whereas other immune cells exhibited positive correlations (r values ranging from 0.025 to 0.590) (Fig. 4). Prognostic value of immune cell counts

This study employed a multivariate Cox regression model to adjust for AFP, maximum tumor diameter, macrovascular invasion, MVI, and AJCC stage, followed by a stratified analysis to evaluate the impact of three regional immune markers on overall survival (OS) in patients with macrotrabecular hepatocellular carcinoma (MT-HCC). The results demonstrated that FOXP3+ Tregs were independent adverse prognostic indicators across the intratumoral, tumor-margin, and peritumoral tissues, collectively mediating the immunosuppressive microenvironment of the liver. At the tumor margin, multiple high-risk factors were observed: tumor size, presence of macrovascular invasion, and high infiltration of exhausted CD8+ T cells significantly increased the risk of mortality; furthermore, the macrovascular invasion exhibited a highly strong predictive effect for mortality in both the tumor-margin and peritumoral models. Peritumoral CD4+ T cells represented the sole independent protective immune marker, with high infiltration correlating with reduced mortality risk. Additionally, high CTLA-4 infiltration within the tumor or peritumoral regions showed a borderline adverse trend, while PD-L1 expression at the tumor margin displayed a weak protective trend; however, none of these associations reached statistical significance and require validation through larger sample sizes. Discussion

This study systematically compared the clinicopathological features between macrotrabecular hepatocellular carcinoma (MT-HCC) and conventional hepatocellular carcinoma (CV-HCC) and uncovered substantial biological disparities between the two subtypes. MT-HCC exhibited markedly aggressive malignant behaviors, characterized by a higher postoperative recurrence rate and inferior overall survival, conferring significant prognostic predictive value for HCC patients undergoing curative resection. Further focusing on the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), we comprehensively investigated the spatial distribution of immune cells in MT-HCC and its clinical prognostic significance, aiming to provide novel mechanistic insights and potential therapeutic targets for this aggressive HCC subtype.

Consistent with previous reports identifying MT-HCC as a highly invasive HCC subtype [[25], [26], [27]], the present study demonstrated that MT-HCC harbors more malignant clinicopathological phenotypes than CV-HCC, including higher AFP positivity, larger tumor volume, and elevated incidences of gross vascular tumor thrombus, satellite nodules, and tumor necrosis. In combination with its typical histological features, such as macrotrabecular architectural distortion and prominent cellular atypia, our multidimensional clinical, pathological, and prognostic evidence further validates the intrinsically aggressive biological characteristics of MT-HCC.

The spatial architectural pattern of the TIME is a pivotal determinant of antitumor immune responses, tumor invasion, distant metastasis, and immunotherapeutic responsiveness. In this study, compartmental immune profiling revealed prominent spatial immune heterogeneity and a typical immune-excluded microenvironment in MT-HCC. Adaptive immune cells (CD3⁺, CD4⁺, CD8⁺ T cells and CD20⁺ B cells) exhibited a consistent spatial gradient, with sparse infiltration in the tumor parenchyma and abundant accumulation at the tumor border and peritumoral tissues. The unique macrotrabecular structure of MT-HCC may form a physical barrier that impedes the infiltration of functional peripheral immune cells into tumor lesions, creating an intratumoral immune desert and facilitating tumor immune escape. Accumulated studies have confirmed that the enrichment of immunosuppressive components in the HCC microenvironment impairs cytotoxic immune responses. The crosstalk between dysfunctional immune cells, neutrophils, dendritic cells, and B cells further disrupts innate and adaptive immune surveillance, ultimately fostering an immunosuppressive TIME in HCC [28].

Immune correlation analysis further clarified the unique immune regulatory landscape of MT-HCC. Intratumoral CD8⁺ T cell density was strongly and positively correlated with PD-1 expression (r = 0.701, P < 0.001). Additionally, CTLA-4 expression was synchronously upregulated with CD3, CD8, and PD-1, indicating that residual intratumoral effector T cells are predominantly in a dual PD-1/CTLA-4 exhausted state with defective cytotoxic function. Multiple immune checkpoint molecules, including the PD-1/PD-L1 axis, LAG3, CTLA-4, and TIGIT, synergistically constitute a critical immunosuppressive barrier in malignancies [29]. Among these molecules, PD-1 and CTLA-4 serve as core suppressors of T cell activation and effector function, driving the malignant progression of MT-HCC [30,31]. The concurrent upregulation of PD-1 and CTLA-4 provides a robust theoretical basis for dual immune checkpoint combination therapy for MT-HCC. Notably, PD-L1 expression was barely detectable across intratumoral, border, and peritumoral compartments of MT-HCC, demonstrating that its immune escape mechanism is PD-L1-independent. This finding explains the poor clinical response of MT-HCC patients to single-agent PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, a phenomenon widely documented in previous clinical studies [[32], [33], [34]].

We further identified a distinctive spatial expression pattern of CTLA-4 in MT-HCC, with the highest expression at the tumor border, moderate expression within the tumor parenchyma, and the lowest expression in peritumoral tissues, suggesting that the tumor border acts as the core region mediating local immune suppression in MT-HCC. As the leading frontier of tumor invasion and micrometastasis, elevated CTLA-4 at the tumor border persistently inhibits lymphocyte activation and proliferation, remodels a localized immunosuppressive microenvironment, and provides favorable immune conditions for tumor invasive progression [31]. Moreover, FOXP3⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs) were highly enriched at the tumor border and peritumoral regions. Tregs effectively suppress the activity of effector T cells, collaborating with CTLA-4 signaling to mediate hierarchical immune escape and accelerate MT-HCC progression.

Collectively, MT-HCC exploits a compartmentalized, synergistic immune escape strategy dependent on spatial immune heterogeneity. Intratumoral immune suppression is primarily driven by PD-1/CTLA-4-mediated T cell exhaustion, while the tumor border forms a robust immunosuppressive barrier via high CTLA-4 expression and Treg infiltration. Pan-regional Treg enrichment further ablates antitumor immune surveillance in a PD-L1-independent manner. This spatially differentiated immune crosstalk mechanistically elucidates the inherent high invasiveness and high recurrence propensity of MT-HCC, and also provides a solid immunological foundation for the prognostic value of immune biomarkers identified in this study.

After adjusting for multiple confounding factors, including AFP level, maximum tumor diameter, gross vascular tumor thrombus, microvascular invasion, and AJCC stage, multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that elevated FOXP3⁺ Treg infiltration in intratumoral, border, and peritumoral regions are independent adverse prognostic factors for MT-HCC patients. To date, the prognostic role of FOXP3 in HCC remains controversial. FOXP3⁺ Tregs primarily exert immunosuppressive and oncogenic effects, whereas tumor cell-derived FOXP3 may exhibit tumor-suppressive properties [[35], [36], [37], [38], [39]]. Such conflicting biological functions are largely determined by cellular origin, subcellular localization, and microenvironmental context. Our findings highlight the spatial distribution characteristics of FOXP3⁺ Tregs as promising novel prognostic biomarkers for MT-HCC.

Notably, high CD8⁺ T cell infiltration at the tumor border was identified as an independent risk factor for poor overall survival in MT-HCC, representing a typical paradoxical immune phenotype. This counterintuitive phenomenon indicates that border-accumulated CD8⁺ T cells in MT-HCC are predominantly dysfunctional and exhausted, rather than functionally cytotoxic. Their high infiltration merely reflects sustained ineffective antitumor immune responses and chronic inflammatory status, which fail to restrain tumor progression and instead indicate aggressive tumor biology. Accumulating single-cell sequencing and large-scale clinical studies have verified extensive infiltration of exhausted CD8⁺ T cells (TEXs) and Tregs in HCC, which are negatively correlated with clinical prognosis and immunotherapeutic response [40,41]. The tumor border constitutes a high-risk prognostic region, where large tumor volume, gross vascular thrombus, and exhausted CD8⁺ T cell infiltration synergistically increase patients’ mortality risk. The absence of prognostic significance of border PD-L1 further corroborates the PD-L1-independent immune escape hallmark of MT-HCC. Although single-agent immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) show limited efficacy in HCC, combination immunotherapy has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy. A phase I/II clinical trial demonstrated that combined treatment with nivolumab and ipilimumab yielded a median overall survival of 22.8 months in advanced HCC patients [42]. Furthermore, unsupervised clustering analysis of 66 immune biomarker genes identified distinct HCC immune subtypes, among which subtypes with elevated CTLA-4, PD-1, and PD-L1 expression exhibited potential responsiveness to ICI therapy [43]. These findings strongly support the clinical application potential of dual immune checkpoint blockade for MT-HCC.

Elevated intratumoral and peritumoral CTLA-4 expression showed a marginal trend toward unfavorable prognosis and was closely associated with T cell exhaustion and immunosuppressive microenvironment formation, suggesting that CTLA-4-targeted intervention is a promising immunotherapeutic strategy for MT-HCC. In contrast, peritumoral CD4⁺ T cell infiltration was the only independent protective immune biomarker in this cohort. This implies that maintaining peritumoral immune homeostasis and enhancing postoperative local hepatic immune surveillance can eliminate minimal residual lesions and reduce the high postoperative recurrence rate of MT-HCC.

Our results reinforce the well-established consensus that the TIME plays a decisive role in HCC progression [44]. The coexistence of widespread exhausted immune cells and infiltrative Tregs synergistically deteriorates patient prognosis, while functional peritumoral CD4⁺ T cells preserve local immune surveillance and improve long-term survival. Although similar immune regulatory patterns have been reported in other malignancies, this study provides novel insights into the unique tumor-host immune crosstalk specific to MT-HCC [[45], [46]].

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective study design may lead to inherent selection bias. Second, although we systematically profiled the spatial distribution of immune biomarkers in MT-HCC, the precise molecular mechanisms by which these immune cells modulate tumor progression remain incompletely elucidated. Further in vitro and in vivo functional experiments are required to validate the underlying regulatory mechanisms. In addition, integrating immune biomarkers with molecular characteristics (e.g., gene mutations and microRNA profiles) may improve the accuracy of prognostic prediction models. Furthermore, this study only performed immunohistochemical analysis on MT-HCC samples without matched CV-HCC immune data. Accordingly, this study only performed immunohistochemical analysis on MT-HCC samples without matched CV-HCC immune data. Accordingly, it remains unclear whether the identified immune features are unique to MT-HCC or universal characteristics of HCC. Further comparative immune profiling of CV-HCC is warranted to define the specific immune microenvironment of MT-HCC.

In conclusion, this study systematically characterizes the distinct clinical, pathological, and spatial immune microenvironment features of MT-HCC. We identified a novel hierarchical immune escape mechanism in MT-HCC, which is CTLA-4-dominated and PD-L1-independent, overcoming the generalized research limitations of conventional HCC immunological studies. These findings provide innovative mechanistic insights and precise therapeutic targets for individualized immunotherapy for this highly aggressive HCC subtype.

Limitation statement

Given the limited sample size of the IHC cohort (n = 48), the multivariate analysis results should be interpreted with caution, and validation in larger independent cohorts is warranted. In addition, this study relied solely on immunohistochemical protein profiling; multi‑omics datasets such as single‑cell transcriptomics are lacking to further dissect fine immune cell subpopulations.

Endoreversible Stirling cycles: plasma engines at maximal power
Gregory Behrendt, Sebastian Deffner

Abstract
Endoreversible engine cycles are a cornerstone of finite-time thermodynamics. We show that endoreversible Stirling engines operating with a one-component plasma as working medium run at maximal power output with the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency. As a main result, we elucidate that this is actually a consequence of the fact that the caloric equation of state depends only linearly on temperature and only additively on volume. In particular, neither the exact form of the mechanical equation of state, nor the full fundamental relation are required. Thus, our findings immediately generalize to a larger class of working plasmas, far beyond simple ideal gases. In addition, we show that for plasmas described by the photonic equation of state the efficiency is significantly lower. This is in stark contrast to endoreversible Otto cycles, for which photonic engines have an efficiency larger than the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency.



I
Introduction

Among all the various states of matter, plasmas have a unique place. In particular, quantum plasmas exist only in the most extreme environments, such as the interior of stars, in the early universe, or more mundanely in highly intense laser fields
Conde 2020
. In its simplest form, a plasma is a superheated gas, in which electrons have been stripped from atoms, creating a mixture of positively charged ions and negatively charged electrons
Conde 2020
. It provides the circumstances for nuclear fusion, which is a nuclear reaction where two light atomic nuclei combine to form a heavier one, releasing a tremendous amount of energy
Morse 2018
. The potential technological applications of nuclear fusion are tremendous, which is why its realization is considered one of the “14 Grand Challenges for Engineering in the 21st Century”
Department of Energy
.

From a thermodynamic point of view, also nuclear reactions in plasmas can be understood as heat engine cycles
Segantin et al. 2020
;
Terahara and Tanabe 2023
. In fact, the electrostatic interactions within a plasma behave very akin to the usual mechanical pressure in typical gases
Avinash 2010
. Interestingly, the somewhat natural engine cycle for plasma engines is the Stirling cycle
Avinash and Chaudhary 2014
. The question then arises what the efficiency of such an engines is at maximal power output.

In the present work, we answer this question within the framework of endoreversible thermodynamics
Hoffmann et al. 1997
. In endoreversible thermodynamics one assumes that all processes are slow enough that the system
locally equilibrates
, yet the processes are too fast for the system to reach a state of equilibrium with the environment. More specifically, imagine an engine, whose working medium is at equilibrium at temperature
T
. However,
T
is not equal to the temperature of the heat bath,
T_{\mathrm{bath}}
, and thus there is a temperature gradient at the boundaries of the engine. Now further imagine that the engine undergoes a slow, cyclic state transformation, where slow means that the working medium remains
locally
in equilibrium at all times. Then, from the point of view of the environment the device undergoes an irreversible cycle. Such state transformations are called
endoreversible

Hoffmann et al. 1997
, which means that locally the transformation is reversible, but globally irreversible.

In a seminal work, Curzon and Ahlborn showed
Curzon and Ahlborn 1975
that the efficiency of a Carnot engine undergoing an endoreversible cycle at maximal power is given by,

\eta_{\mathrm{CA}}=1-\sqrt{\frac{T_{c}}{T_{h}}}\,,

(1)

where
T_{c}
and
T_{h}
are the temperatures of the cold and hot reservoirs, respectively. Since its discovery the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency (
1
) has received a great deal of attention. For instance, it has been found that also endorversible Otto
Deffner 2018
and Brayton
Ferketic and Deffner 2023
engines operating with ideal gases have the same efficiency. However, it has also been shown that whether or not a finite time Carnot cycle really assumes
\eta_{\mathrm{CA}}
is determined by the “symmetry” of the dissipation
Esposito et al. 2010
and on the specific form of the fundamental relation
Leff 1987
;
Rezek and Kosloff 2006
;
Abah et al. 2012
;
Deffner and Campbell 2019
;
Kloc et al. 2019
;
Myers and Deffner 2020
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Myers et al. 2021a
;
Myers et al. 2021b
;
Myers and Deffner 2021
;
Myers et al. 2022
;
Myers et al. 2023
;
Peña et al. 2023
.

In the present work, we focus on plasma engines that run in endorversible Stirling cycles. It is interesting to note that there are also several accounts in the literature of the fact that endoreversible Stirling cycles at maximum power operating with classical, ideal gases are described by
\eta_{\mathrm{CA}}

Erbay and Yavuz 1997
;
Blank et al. 1994
;
Kaushik and Kumar 2000
. Given that it is often a good assumption (in first order approximation) that also plasmas can be described as ideal gases
Slattery et al. 1980
one is tempted to conclude that clearly also plasma engines at maximal power have the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency. However, given that previous treatments of the endoreversible Stirling make, sometimes implicitly, often explicitly the assumption that the working medium is a regular, classical, ideal gas, it is not immediately obvious that plasmas do not require a separate treatment.

Therefore, we start with a detailed discussion on the necessary conditions under which the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency arises. Particular emphasis is placed on a comprehensive and pedagogical derivation, which then also leads to immediate generalizations. In fact, we will see that any gases that are described by caloric equations of states that are linear in temperature and at most additive in volume lead to the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency. Neither the mechanical equation of state, nor the full fundamental relation is required.

This means, in particular, that also endoreversible Stirling engines whose working plasmas require a second-order virial expansion have the same efficiency. In the simplest case, their corresponding gas law is given by the van der Waals equation of state.

As an example of plasma engines that fall not within this class, we then analyze an engine that operates with an electron-positron-photon plasma. For high enough temperatures
Faussurier 2024
such plasmas can be described by the photonic equation of state, which permits an almost completely analytical treatment. We find that photonic Stirling engines have a significantly smaller efficiency at maximal power. This is in stark contrast to Otto engines, in which case photonic working mediums lead to higher efficiency
Smith et al. 2020
.

II
One-component plasmas – modified ideal gas

We start with a Stirling engine that operates with a one-component plasma. For such situations one commonly assumes that the plasma can be treated as an “effective ideal gas”
Slattery et al. 1980
. A classical, ideal gas is comprised of uniform, non-interacting, identical particles, whose caloric equation of state is proportional to temperature and independent of the volume,

E=\varsigma T\,.

(2)

The constant
\varsigma
depends on natural constants and the number of degrees of freedom. For instance, for a classical ideal gas in three spatial dimensions we have
Callen 1985
,
\varsigma=3/2N\,k_{B}
, where
N
is the number of particles and
k_{B}
is Boltzmann’s constant.

A one-component plasma is comprised of non-interacting, identical particles. At the fundamental level, a one-component plasma is nothing but a collection of uniform ions, that have been immersed in an equally and oppositely charged background, neutralizing the total charge of the plasma, and thus preventing any potential interparticle-Coulomb interactions
Slattery et al. 1980
. Thus, it follows that the corresponding caloric equation of state for the one-component plasma is still directly proportional to the temperature, and all “quantum” modifications can be accounted for by an effective number of degrees of freedom along with an arrangement of phenomenological constants that are unique to that plasma. We can write,

E=E_{0}+f\,T

(3)

where
E_{0}
is a constant off-setting the background energy, and
f
quantifies the effective degrees of freedom
Slattery et al. 1980
. As we will see shortly, Eq. (
3
) is a necessary and sufficient condition to obtain the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency.

II.1
Endoreversible cycle and efficiency

In complete analogy to the endoreversible Carnot
Curzon and Ahlborn 1975
, Otto
Deffner 2018
;
Myers and Deffner 2020
;
Smith et al. 2020
;
Myers et al. 2021a
;
Myers et al. 2021b
;
Myers and Deffner 2021
;
Myers et al. 2022
;
Myers et al. 2023
;
Peña et al. 2023
, and Brayton cycles
Ferketic and Deffner 2023
, we now construct the endoreversible Stirling cycle.

The Stirling cycle is a 4-stroke process comprising isothermal expansion, isochoric cooling, isothermal compression, and isochoric heating. The corresponding
PV
- and
TS
-diagrams for a one-component plasma (
3
) are depicted in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Schematic
PV
- and
TS
-diagrams of the Stirling cycle for the one-component plasma (
3
) as a working medium.

A\rightarrow B
: Isothermal expansion

During the hot isotherm, the plasma is in contact with a heat reservoir at temperature
T_{h}
. However, as usual in endoreversible thermodynamics
Hoffmann et al. 1997
, we assume that the plasma has not fully equilibrated with the hear reservoir, and rather has a temperature “slightly” below
T_{h}
, namely we have
T_{h,p}<T_{h}
. Fourier’s law then dictates that the heat flux is linear in the temperature gradient
Curzon and Ahlborn 1975
, and we can write

Q_{\mathrm{AB}}=\alpha\,t_{AB}\,\left(T_{h}-T_{h,p}\right)\,,

(4)

where
\alpha
is the thermal conductivity and
t_{AB}
is the duration of the stroke.

Now note that for one-component plasmas the internal energy is constant for isothermal processes, cf. Eq, (
3
). Thus, we also immediately have,

\Delta E=E_{B}-E_{A}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{AB}=-Q_{AB},

(5)

namely the work produced during the isothermal expansion is equal and opposite in sign to the heat absorbed from the heat reservoir.

B\rightarrow C
: Isochoric cooling

During the isochoric stroke, the plasma is disconnected from the heat reservoirs. Thus, this stroke is identical to the ideal cycle. In any case, we have

W_{BC}=0\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{BC}=E_{B}-E_{C}=f\,\left(T_{B}-T_{C}\right)

(6)

where we used the caloric equation of state (
3
).

C\rightarrow D
: Isothermal compression

In complete analogy to the hot isotherm, during the cold, isothermal compression the plasma is in contact with a cold reservoir at temperature
T_{c}
. However, we again assume that the plasma has not fully equilibrated with the reservoir, and that its temperature,
T_{c,p}
, is slightly above
T_{c}
. Again, employing Fourier’s law, we can write

Q_{CD}=\beta\,t_{CD}\,\left(T_{c}-T_{c,p}\right)

(7)

where
\beta
is the thermal conductivity for the cold stroke, and
t_{CD}
is the duration. As before, we can also write

\Delta E=E_{D}-E_{C}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{CD}=-Q_{CD}\,,

(8)

which follows from the caloric equation of state (
3
).

D\rightarrow A
: Isochoric heating

The cycle is completed with another isochoric stroke. Again, the plasma is disconnected from the heat reservoirs. We have

W_{DA}=0\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{DA}=f\,\left(T_{D}-T_{A}\right)

(9)

Endoreversible efficiency

We are now interested in the efficiency at maximal power. To this end, consider that the plasma absorbs heat from the hot reservoir during the hot isotherm
A\rightarrow B
. Thus, we can write

\eta\equiv-\frac{W_{\mathrm{cyc}}}{Q_{AB}}=1+\frac{Q_{CD}}{Q_{AB}}\,,

(10)

where we used that the work produced during the entire cycle is
W_{\mathrm{cyc}}=-(Q_{AB}+Q_{CD})
. Using Eqs. (
4
) and (
7
) we also have,

\eta=1+\frac{\beta\,t_{CD}}{\alpha\,t_{AB}}\,\frac{T_{c}-T_{c,p}}{T_{h}-T_{h,p}}\,,

(11)

which appears to suggest that the efficiency depends on the stroke times.

However, the expression for the efficiency (
11
) can be further simplified by employing the entropy balance over one cycle. In fact, we have

0=\Delta S_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\Delta S_{AB}+\Delta S_{BC}+\Delta S_{CD}+\Delta S_{DA}\,.

(12)

Along the isothermal strokes,
A\rightarrow B
and
C\rightarrow D
, the entropy is simply given by the heat divided by temperature,

\Delta S_{AB}=\frac{Q_{AB}}{T_{h,p}}=\frac{\alpha\,t_{AB}\,\left(T_{h}-T_{h,p}\right)}{T_{h,p}}

(13)

and

\Delta S_{CD}=\frac{Q_{CD}}{T_{c,p}}=\frac{\beta\,t_{CD}\,\left(T_{c}-T_{c,p}\right)}{T_{c,p}}\,,

(14)

where we again used Eqs. (
4
) and (
7
).

For the isochoric strokes, we again exploit
only
the caloric equation of state (
3
). In its differential form, along the isochor,
\text{\dj}W=0
, we have
dE=\text{\dj}Q=T\,dS.
Thus, the total change in entropy can be written as

\Delta S=\int_{S_{i}}^{S_{f}}\,dS=\int_{T_{i}}^{T_{f}}dT\,\frac{f}{T}\,=f\ln\left(\frac{T_{f}}{T_{i}}\right)\,.

(15)

Consequently, we obtain

\Delta S_{BC}=-\Delta S_{DA}=f\,\ln\left(\frac{T_{c,p}}{T_{h,p}}\right)\,,

(16)

and thus,

\frac{\alpha\,t_{AB}\,\left(T_{h}-T_{h,p}\right)}{T_{h,p}}=\frac{\beta\,t_{CD}\,\left(T_{c,p}-T_{c}\right)}{T_{c,p}}\,.

(17)

In other words, the efficiency (
11
) of an endoreversible Stirling cycle operating with a one-component plasma simply becomes,

\eta=1-\frac{T_{c,p}}{T_{h,p}}\,,

(18)

which is identical to the ideal efficiency
Callen 1985
replacing the temperatures of the heat reservoirs with the corresponding temperatures of the plasma.

II.2
Efficiency at maximal power

As stated above, we are now interested in the efficiency at maximal power output. Inspecting Eq. (
18
) we need to to determine the temperatures,
T_{h,p}
and
T_{c,p}
, that maximize the power. As usual, we write

P\equiv-\frac{W_{\mathrm{cyc}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{cyc}}}=\frac{Q_{AB}+Q_{CD}}{\gamma\,(t_{AB}+t_{CD})}\,,

(19)

where
\gamma
is a real constant. It will prove convenient to introduce the variables

x\equiv T_{h}-T_{h,p}\quad\text{and}\quad y\equiv T_{c,p}-T_{c}

(20)

which is identical to the notation introduced by the original treatment by Curzon and Ahlborn
Curzon and Ahlborn 1975
.

After a few lines of simple algebra, we obtain

P(x,y)=\frac{\alpha\beta\,xy\,\left[(T_{h}-x)+(T_{c}+y)\right]}{\gamma\left[\alpha\,x\,(T_{h}-x)+\beta\,y\,(T_{c}+y)\right]}\,,

(21)

where we once again employed Eqs. (
4
) and (
7
). The maximum of
P(x,y)
is determine using standard calculus, namely solving
\partial_{x}P(x,y)=0
and
\partial_{y}P(x,y)
for
x
and
y
.

Denoting the solutions by
x^{*}
and
y^{*}
, we find,

x^{*}=\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+\sqrt{\beta}}\,\left(T_{h}-\sqrt{T_{h}T_{c}}\right)

(22)

and

y^{*}=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\alpha}+\sqrt{\beta}}\,\left(-T_{c}+\sqrt{T_{h}T_{c}}\right)\,.

(23)

Substituting these solutions into the expression for the efficiency (
18
) and simplifying the expression we finally obtain

\eta=1-\sqrt{\frac{T_{c}}{T_{h}}}\,.

(24)

That is, the efficiency at maximal power of an endoreversible Stirling engine operating with a one-component plasma as working medium is given by the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency. Our result (
24
) corroborates earlier findings for classical ideal gases
Erbay and Yavuz 1997
;
Blank et al. 1994
;
Kaushik and Kumar 2000
. Thus, engines operating in endoreversible Stirling cycles with one-component plasmas and classical ideal gases have the same efficiency.

II.3
Generalization to second order virial expansion

Before we move on to more intricate working mediums, we emphasize that the present analysis is entirely based on the fact that the caloric equation of state is of the form of an ideal gas (
3
). In particular, we did not need to require the mechanical equation of state to be given by the ideal gas law, nor did we need the full fundamental relation.

Interestingly, equations of state of the form (
3
) are
not
restricted to
classical
ideal gases. For instance, also classical harmonic oscillators
Deffner 2018
and rubber bands
Callen 1985
are described by equations of state that are linear in temperature (and independent of volume).

For the present purposes, it is even more interesting to observe that the van der Waals gas is described by
Callen 1985

E=a_{0}\,T-\frac{b_{0}}{V^{2}}\,,

(25)

where
a_{0}
and
b_{0}
are phenomenological constants. It is a simple exercise to show that the van der Waals equation of state is obtained from the virial expansion in second order
Peliti 2024
. Since the dependence on the volume
V
in Eq. (
25
) is only additive, the changes in entropy during isochoric strokes remain identical to above (
15
). Consequently, we obtain the same expressions for efficiency (
18
), power (
21
), and efficiency at maximal power (
24
).

It is worth emphasizing that for many real plasmas virial coefficients have been determined, see for instance
Gervois et al. 1976
;
Alastuey and Perez 1992
;
Omarbakiyeva et al. 2010
;
Mattiello and Cassing 2010
;
Röpke et al. 2021
. Our present results remain valid for any endoreversible Stirling cycle for plasma, for which a description of up to second order in the virial expansion is a good description.

III
Relativistic electron-positron-photon plasma

In the preceding section we analyzed the performance of endoreversible plasma engines that are described by equations of state which are (i) linear in temperature and (ii) at most additive in volume. The situation becomes more involved if these conditions are not met.

To this end, we conclude the analysis with another type of plasma, namely the relativistic electron–positron–photon plasma. Rather recently, it was shown in Ref.
Faussurier 2024
that in the high-temperature limit the corresponding caloric equation of state can be written as the familiar photonic gas law
Callen 1985
,

E=\epsilon VT^{4}\,,

(26)

where
\epsilon
is a constant collecting natural constants, such as the speed of light, the electron mass, and
\hbar
, see Ref.
Faussurier 2024
. In principle, we could also work with the more involved expression at finite temperature, but this would necessitate a fully numerical treatment. We choose to continue with Eq. (
26
) as it allows an almost entirely analytical treatment.

Note that Eq. (
26
) does not have the simple, linear form of Eq. (
3
) that we exploited above. Therefore, the natural question arises whether engines with photonic plasmas operate at higher or lower efficiency than those with one-component plasmas. Note that for the same question, but for Otto cycles it was found that photonic gases significantly outperform classical ideal gases
Smith et al. 2020
.

III.1
Endoreversible Stirling cycle

To answer this question, we repeat the construction of the endoreversible Stirling cycle paying special attention to the required modifications. In Fig.
2
we plot the corresponding
PV
- and
TS
-diagrams schematically.

Figure 2:
Schematic
PV
- and
TS
-diagrams of the Stirling cycle for the photonic gas (
26
) as a working medium.

A\rightarrow B
: Isothermal expansion

As before, we assume that during the hot isotherm the plasma is slightly colder than the hot heat reservoir, and that the heat flux is given by Fourier’s law. Hence, we can write again
Q_{AB}=\alpha t_{AB}x
. However, in this case we can no longer assume that the internal energy is constant, as Eq. (
26
) depends multiplicatively on the volume. Hence, we have to write for the work

W_{AB}=\Delta E_{AB}-Q_{AB}=\epsilon\,T_{h,p}^{4}\,(V_{B}-V_{A})-\alpha t_{AB}x\,,

(27)

which explicitly depends on the change in volume as well as the stroke time.

It is a standard exercise
Callen 1985
to show that the entropy of the photonic gas reads

S=\frac{4}{3}\epsilon VT^{3}\,.

(28)

And, as discussed above, for isothermal processes, we simply have
Q_{AB}=T_{h,p}\,\Delta S_{AB}
. Therefore, we can also write

\alpha t_{AB}x=\frac{4}{3}\,T_{h,p}^{4}\,(V_{B}-V_{A})\,,

(29)

which will become useful shortly.

B\rightarrow C
: Isochoric cooling

For the isochoric strokes, we again have that
W_{BC}=0
and

Q_{BC}=\Delta E_{BC}=\epsilon V_{B}(T_{c,p}^{4}-T_{h,p}^{4})\,.

(30)

However, as seen above, the work and heat of the isochoric strokes will not be required for further analysis.

C\rightarrow D
: Isothermal compression

In complete analogy to the hot isotherm, we now can write
Q_{CD}=-\beta t_{CD}y
, and

W_{CD}=\Delta E_{CD}-Q_{CD}=-\epsilon\,T_{c,p}^{4}\,(V_{B}-V_{A})-Q_{CD}\,.

(31)

Moreover, again employing the expression for the entropy (
28
) and
Q_{CD}=T_{c,p}\,\Delta S_{CD}
we also have

\beta t_{CD}y=\frac{4}{3}\,T_{c,p}^{4}\,(V_{B}-V_{A})\,.

(32)

D\rightarrow A
: Isochoric heating

For completeness we also collect the work,
W_{DA}=0
, and heat,

Q_{DA}=\Delta E_{DA}=\epsilon V_{A}(T_{h,p}^{4}-T_{c,p}^{4})\,,

(33)

during the isochoric heating stroke.

Endoreversible efficiency

Before we start analyzing the power, we again first derive an expression for the efficiency,

\eta\equiv-\frac{W_{\mathrm{cyc}}}{Q_{AB}}=-\frac{W_{AB}+W_{CD}}{Q_{AB}}\,,

(34)

Substituting Eqs. (
27
), (
29
), (
31
), and (
32
) into the definition (
34
) we obtain,

\eta=\frac{1}{4}\left[1-\left(\frac{T_{c,p}}{T_{h,p}}\right)^{4}\right]\,.

(35)

Comparing the latter result (
35
) with the endoreversible efficiency for one-component plasmas (
18
) we note that endoreversible Stirling efficiency for photonic gases is always smaller than Eq. (
18
), cf. Fig.
3

III.2
Efficiency at maximal power

The obvious question now is what this means for the efficiency at maximal power. To this end, we again consider the power output,

P=\frac{Q_{AB}+Q_{CD}}{\gamma\,(t_{AB}+t_{CD})}\,,

(36)

which in the present case can be written as

P(T_{c,p},T_{h,p})=\frac{\alpha\beta}{4\gamma}\frac{(T_{h}-T_{h,p})(T_{c}-T_{c,p})(T_{h,p}^{4}-T_{c,p}^{4})}{\beta T_{h,p}^{4}(T_{c}-T_{c,p})-\alpha T_{c,p}^{4}(T_{h}-T_{h,p})}\,.

(37)

We immediately observe that Eq. (
37
) is significantly more involved than the power for one-component plasmas (
21
), and thus we have to resort to a numerical analysis.

In Fig.
4
we depict the solution for one set of parameters. We observe that also at maximal power, the efficiency for photonic gases is significantly below the Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency (
24
). We emphasize again that this is in contrast to endoreversible Otto cycle, in which the photonic case has a higher efficiency
Smith et al. 2020
.

IV
Concluding remarks

In the present work, we analyzed the efficiency at maximal power of endoreversible Stirling cycles. As working mediums, we considered one-component plasmas with caloric equations of state, extensions in second order virial expansion, and photonic gases. We found that for all thermodynamics systems, whose caloric equation of state only depends linearly on temperature and at most additively on volume, the efficiency at maximal power is given by the seminal Curzon-Ahlborn efficiency. Interestingly, the efficiency for photonic working mediums is significantly smaller, which is in stark contrast to endoreversible Otto cycles.

Particular emphasis was put on a comprehensive, self-contained, and pedagogical presentation of the treatment. While our work is purely theoretical and it is unlikely that our results will lead directly to the experimental realization of a practically useful plasma Stirling engine, the conceptual mathematical steps of our analysis could be applied to more realistic scenarios.
The only necessary “ingredient” is the caloric equation of state for any considered plasma, which then step-for-step leads to the corresponding expression for the efficiency at maximal power.

Appendix A
Equal strokes times – trivial case

In this appendix, we briefly discuss a trivial case for the one-component plasma (
3
). As done previously for Otto
Deffner 2018
and Brayton
Ferketic and Deffner 2023
cycles, one is tempted to make a “simplifying” assumption. Namely, to set the stroke times equal to each other,
\tau\equiv t_{AB}=t_{CD}
. In this case, we can Eq. (
19
) as

P=\frac{1}{2\gamma}\,(\alpha\,x-\beta\,y)\,,

(38)

which is linear in
x
and
y
. In this case, we can determine the maximal power simply from the maximal and minimal values of
x
and
y
, respectively. Recognizing that at no instant the plasma can be colder than the cold reservoir, we can write

x_{\mathrm{max}}=T_{h}-T_{c}\quad\text{and}\quad y_{\mathrm{min}}=0\,.

(39)

Substituting the latter into Eq. (
11
) we obtain

\eta=1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{y_{\mathrm{min}}}{x_{\mathrm{max}}}=1\,.

(40)

On conformal Komar currents in LRS spacetimes
Abbas M. Sherif

Abstract
For locally rotationally symmetric (LRS) spacetimes, we construct two equivalent forms of the Komar current derived from a conformal Killing vector. One is a kinematic construction and the other is in terms of the matter quantities on the spacetime. The required conservation condition for the current is derived and discussed in various instances, and the implications of the conservation of the current, and in the case of a vanishing current, are analyzed. A relationship between the conservation criterion and the presence of trapped surfaces in the spacetime is found and discussed. We also show that for LRS II metrics with constant metric time component, the current is always conserved. In the presence of a conformal Killing horizon, properties of the current are analyzed and restrictions on, and some implications for the physical spacetime variables, in the vicinity of the horizon, are obtained. Finally, with respect to the conformal Killing horizon, the associated Noether charge is shown to be proportional to the surface gravity, establishing the thermodynamic interpretation of the Noether charge.

II
The Komar current

We quickly review the construction of the Komar current in general relativity. We begin by writing some important relations for the purpose of this work and the benefit of the readers. We will then go on to discuss the construction and properties of the Komar current. Those already familiar with the literature may skip the first subsection.

II.1
Useful relations

For vectors and co-vectors the Ricci identity is given respectively by

\displaystyle\left[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}\right]Y^{c}={R_{bad}}^{c}Y^{d},\quad\left[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}\right]Y_{c}={R_{abc}}^{d}Y_{d},

\displaystyle\left[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}\right]Y^{cd}

\displaystyle=R^{c}_{\ fab}Y^{fd}+R^{d}_{\ fab}Y^{cf}.

(2)

The twice contracted Bianchi identity is

\displaystyle\nabla^{a}R_{ab}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{a}R.

(3)

The commutator of the box derivative

\displaystyle=-R_{ab}\nabla^{b}\Psi,

\displaystyle=2R_{ba}\nabla^{[a}U^{b]}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{U}R.

In the case where the vector field

is a CKV, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{U}g_{ab}=2\Psi g_{ab},

henceforth referred to as the

denoting the Lie derivative along

\displaystyle\left[\nabla_{a},\Box\right]U^{a}=\Psi R+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{U}R.

And a Killing vector (KV), where

\displaystyle\left[\nabla_{a},\Box\right]\Psi=0,\quad\left[\nabla_{a},\Box\right]U^{a}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{U}R.

In the case that

We are now in the position to introduce and discuss the current of interest.

II.2
The current: construction and some properties

For an arbitrary vector field
U^{a}
, one constructs the Komar current as

\displaystyle J^{a}_{K}:=2\nabla_{b}\nabla^{[a}U^{b]},

(8)

with square bracket denoting anti-symmetrization on the indices, and with the obvious shift invariance for

\displaystyle U^{a}\rightarrow U^{a}+\nabla^{a}z,

(9)

for a scalar

Using the Ricci identity for a Rank-2 tensor, it is an easy exercise to establish that

is conserved, i.e.

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}J^{a}_{K}=0.

(10)

For any vector
\bar{U}^{a}

\displaystyle\bar{J}^{a}_{K}=J^{a}_{K}+\bar{U}^{a}

(11)

For

\displaystyle J^{a}_{K}:=2(\nabla^{a}\Psi-\Box U^{a}),

(12)

with the explicit form of the divergence given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}J^{a}_{K}:

\displaystyle=2(\Box\Psi-\nabla_{a}\Box U^{a})

\displaystyle=-2\left(3\Box\Psi+R\Psi+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{U}R\right),

(13)

where we have employed the commutator of the
\Box

U^{a}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{U}R+2\Psi R+6\Box\Psi=0.

(14)

Equation (
14
) is, in fact, a general property of CKVs (see, for example,
jfeng1
). In other words, any
J^{a}_{K}

For a spacetime that admits a CKV, the conformal divergence satisfies the wave equation (
14
)
.

In the case of a KV, the above is the requirement that the scalar curvature be a constant of motion along the KV. Conversely, in the case where the scalar curvature is constant along the CKV, the conservation condition is that the conformal divergence obeys the inhomogeneous wave equation.

\displaystyle\left(\Box+\frac{1}{3}R\right)\Psi=0.

(15)

Now, the Komar integral can be constructed by integrating the current over some hypersurface
\Sigma

\Sigma
, by Stoke’s theorem:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\int_{\Sigma}J^{a}_{K}dS_{a}=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\nabla^{[a}U^{b]}\epsilon^{\partial}_{ab},

where

\Sigma

\Sigma

For a hypersurface that is a level surface of a function
\phi

\displaystyle dS_{a}=\sqrt{-g}\nabla_{a}\phi d^{3}y,

(17)

where
g

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{-g}J^{a}_{K}\nabla_{a}\phi d^{3}y.

(18)

This form is relevant to Section

III
The conformal Komar current and its conservation in LRS II spacetimes

In this section, we seek to derive the form of the Komar current in spacetimes with local rotational symmetry, constructed from a CKV. For the purpose of this work we will allow for (possibly) different causal characters of the CKV in order to facilitate a more complete consideration of the constructed Komar current.

III.1
LRS II spacetime: a quick overview

Locally rotationally symmetric (LRS) spacetimes admit a useful decomposition which allows for the Einstein field equations to be cast as a system of first order equations in the directional derivatives along some preferred directions. In particular, we will restrict our attention to the subclass, the LRS II class, where the preferred directions have vanishing vorticities
cc1
;
cc2
. In local coordinates, these spacetimes are given by the metric form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-b_{1}dt^{2}+b_{2}dr^{2}+b_{3}d\Omega_{j}^{2},

(19)

where
b_{i}=b_{i}(t,r)
, and
d\Omega_{j}^{2}
is the metric on a two manifold which is flat for
j=0
, spherical for
j=1
and a pseudo-sphere for
j=-1
. The metric (
19
) admits the unit timelike and preferred spatial directions

\displaystyle u^{a}=-b_{1}^{-1}\partial_{t}^{a},\ n^{a}=b_{2}^{-1}\partial_{r}^{a},

(20)

with
u_{a}n^{a}=0
.

The interest in LRS II spacetimes is immediately obvious from the form (
19
). Many well known spacetimes, including most of the extensively studied static and dynamical spherically symmetric solutions, with or with cosmological constant, are contained in this class. To name a few we have the Schwarzschild metric, (anti)de-Sitter metric, Lemaitre-Tolman-Bondi metric, etc.

The surfaces of constant
t
and
r
will have induced metric

\displaystyle N_{ab}=g_{ab}+u_{a}u_{b}-n_{a}n_{b},

(21)

where the
g_{ab}
is the metric on the spacetime. With this form the spacetime is said to admit a
1+1+2
decomposition. The energy momentum tensor can now be expressed in the decomposed form

\displaystyle T_{ab}

\displaystyle=\rho u_{a}u_{b}+(p+\Pi)n_{a}n_{b}+2Qn_{(a}n_{b)}

\displaystyle+\left(p-\frac{1}{2}\Pi\right)N_{ab},

(22)

where
\rho=T_{ab}u^{a}u^{b}
is the energy density,
3p=T_{ab}h^{ab}
is the isotropic pressure,
Q=-T_{ab}n^{a}u^{b}
is heat flux, and
\Pi=T_{ab}n^{a}n^{b}-p
is anisotropic stress. From the Einstein equations relating
T_{ab}
and the spacetime curvature, one can then write the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature in terms of these variables.

Derivatives along the preferred directions will be given the notations

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}_{a\cdots b}=u^{c}\nabla_{c}\psi_{a\cdots b},\quad\psi^{\prime}_{a\cdots b}=n^{c}\nabla_{c}\psi_{a\cdots b}.

(23)

One can then use the covariant derivatives of the preferred direction vectors to express the field equations as a system of first order partial differential equations in the dot and prime derivatives
cc1
;
cc2
. (For LRS spacetimes the set of equations has already appeared in several works
cc2
;
sem1
;
brad1
;
el3
;
gos1
;
as1
and we do not reproduce them here.)

Following from the decomposition, any 4-vector
\psi^{a}
in a LRS spacetime may be expressed in the form

\displaystyle\psi^{a}=\psi_{1}u^{a}+\psi_{2}n^{a}

(24)

with
\psi_{1}=-\psi_{a}u^{a}
and
\psi_{2}=\psi_{a}n^{a}
being the normal and tangential components of the vector field relative to the constant time hypersurfaces. In addition, the 2-surfaces of constant
t
and
r
will have the compatible covariant derivative

\displaystyle\delta_{e}\psi_{a\cdots b}={N_{a}}^{f}\cdots{N_{b}}^{g}{N_{e}}^{d}\nabla_{d}\psi_{f\cdots g}.

(25)

Also, the gradient of any scalar
\psi
on the spacetime decomposes as

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}\psi=-\dot{\psi}u_{a}+\psi^{\prime}n_{a}.

(26)

In general the dot and prime derivatives do not commute but rather obey the commutation relation, which can easily be verified for these spacetimes,

\displaystyle(\dot{\psi})^{\prime}-\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{.}=-\mathcal{A}\dot{\psi}+\psi^{\prime}u_{a}^{\prime}n^{a}.

(27)

(A caution should be exercised with the order of taking derivatives, where the derivative appearing in the parenthesis should first be taken before applying the derivative on the outside of the parenthesis.) This relation can be used to obtain constraints for exact solutions to the field equation as well as a consistency check.

With the overview out of the way, we now proceed to obtaining the Komar current from a CKV for these spacetimes.

III.2
The current and its conservation

The CKE (
5
) may be expressed as a set of three coupled partial differential equations (PDE) for the components
\alpha
and
\bar{\alpha}
of the vector field (
28
) below, and a constraint equation covariantly defining the conformal divergence scalar. Existence of solutions to the CKE has been examined variously as mentioned in the introduction. When the unit directions
u^{a}
and
n^{a}
have non-vanishing vorticities, it was demonstrated in

that the CKE always admits an HKV, which generates a horizon whenever the heat flux

attains a certain critical value. In the case that the vector field is a gradient of some differentiable function (at least

), existence of solutions to the CKE was analyzed in

as6

The succeeding subsection will give an alternative form of the current, whose properties will be used as a tool to search for CKV “candidates” obeying the conservation law.

According to the LRS II symmetry, we consider CKVs of the decomposed form

\displaystyle\zeta^{a}=\alpha u^{a}+\bar{\alpha}n^{a},

(28)

where
\alpha
and
\bar{\alpha}
are functions of the time and radial coordinates.

We will notate
f_{ab}=\nabla_{[a}U_{b]}
, the bi-vector associated to
U^{a}
. Explicitly, we have
as5

\displaystyle f_{ab}=-2\left(\dot{\bar{\alpha}}+\alpha\mathcal{A}\right)u_{[a}n_{b]}.

(29)

The Komar current for the associated CKV is then given by

\displaystyle J^{a}_{K}:=-2\nabla_{b}f^{ab}=2\left(fu^{a}+\bar{f}n^{a}\right),

(30)

where

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=-\delta_{a}n^{a}\left(\dot{\bar{\alpha}}+\alpha\mathcal{A}\right)-\left(\dot{\bar{\alpha}}+\alpha\mathcal{A}\right)^{\prime},

\displaystyle\bar{f}

\displaystyle=\delta_{a}u^{a}\left(\dot{\bar{\alpha}}+\alpha\mathcal{A}\right)+\left(\dot{\bar{\alpha}}+\alpha\mathcal{A}\right)^{.},

with
\mathcal{A}=\dot{u}_{a}n^{a}
denoting the acceleration scalar. (The scalar
\delta_{a}n^{a}
, sometimes notated
\phi
in the literature and referred to as the sheet expansion, is the mean curvature of the constant
t
and
r
surfaces.) The conservation condition for the Komar current can now be expressed as

\displaystyle\left(\dot{f}+\theta f\right)+\left(\bar{f}^{\prime}+\left(\mathcal{A}+\delta_{a}n^{a}\right)\bar{f}\right)=0,

(31)

where the scalar
\theta=\mathcal{D}_{a}u^{a}
is the expansion of the timelike congruence
u^{a}
, with
\mathcal{D}_{a}
denoting the covariant derivative along the spatial sections of the spacetime. The equation (
31
) above will then hold true for any CKV in LRS spacetime.

Let us consider the case of an irrotational cosmological models with the metric time component
g_{tt}=-1
. Here
\mathcal{A}
vanishes and

\displaystyle f=-\dot{\bar{\alpha}}\delta_{a}n^{a}-\dot{\bar{\alpha}}^{\prime},\ \bar{f}=\dot{\bar{\alpha}}u_{a}^{\prime}n^{a}+\ddot{\bar{\alpha}},

(32)

so that (
31
) is now

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(\ddot{\bar{\alpha}}^{\prime}-\left(\dot{\bar{\alpha}}^{\prime}\right)^{.}\right)-\dot{\bar{\alpha}}^{\prime}u_{a}^{\prime}n^{a}

\displaystyle+\left[\left(\delta_{a}u^{a}\right)^{\prime}-\left(\dot{\delta}_{a}+u_{b}^{\prime}n^{b}\delta_{a}\right)n^{a}\right]\dot{\bar{\alpha}}.

(33)

We will not provide more details here as this is quick to check, but as these are perfect fluids, the collection in the square bracket vanishes using the appropriate field equations which may be found in several works on LRS spacetimes already cited earlier. Also, using the commutation relation (
27
) on
\dot{\bar{\alpha}}
one sees that the first two terms will cancel out. This therefore verifies the conservation criterion.

Notice that for non-accelerating metrics only the
n^{a}
component of the CKV appears in the expression for the current. So, for purely temporal CKVs, the current vanishes identically (
f=\bar{f}=0
). This is the case with, for example, the FLRW with (now written in conformal coordinates)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=a(\tau)\left(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{1-kr^{2}}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{S}^{2}\right),

(34)

with
\mathrm{d}\mathcal{S}^{2}
denoting the standard metric on the unit 2-sphere, where there is the timelike CKV

\displaystyle\zeta^{a}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}.

(35)

In fact, the bivector also vanishes identically in this particular case so that the Komar integral (
16
) vanishes. (This vanishing of the Komar current and integral in the FLRW cosmological model was also argued in
jfeng1
.)

In general, the vanishing of the Komar current constructed from a CKV in cosmological models with
g_{tt}=-1
can be checked by whether or not
\bar{\alpha}
verifies

\displaystyle\ddot{\bar{\alpha}}+\left(\frac{1}{3}\theta+\sigma\right)\dot{\bar{\alpha}}=0,

(36)

obtained from the vanishing of
f
and
\bar{f}
.

Lastly, for the sake of completeness, we will comment that the Komar integral in our case here takes the simple form

\displaystyle-2\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left(\dot{\bar{\alpha}}+\alpha\mathcal{A}\right)u^{[a}n^{b]}\epsilon^{\partial}_{ab}.

(37)

If one is to consider a hypersurface
\Sigma
such that
\partial\Sigma
is a 2-surface with 2-metric
N_{ab}
, the integral vanishes identically. Furthermore, for a purely temporal CKV, i.e.
\bar{\alpha}=0
, were the integral to vanish over
\Sigma
, for a non-vanishing acceleration, the component
\alpha
will change sign over
\Sigma
, i.e the hypersurface must be null somewhere.

III.3
Alternative form of the current

We may also write the Komar current in terms of the curvature variables. To see this, we note that the bivector
f_{ab}
obeys

\displaystyle\nabla_{c}f_{ab}=R_{abcd}\zeta^{d}-2\nabla_{[a}\Psi g_{b]c},

(38)

so that

\displaystyle\nabla^{b}f_{ba}=R_{ab}\zeta^{b}+3\nabla_{a}\Psi,

(39)

Now, in a LRS II spacetime the Ricci curvature decomposes as

\displaystyle R_{ab}=a_{1}u_{a}u_{b}+a_{2}h_{ab}+2Qu_{(a}n_{b)}+\Pi\left(n_{a}n_{b}-\frac{}{}N_{ab}\right),

(40)

with the definitions

\displaystyle a_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho+3p-2\Lambda\right),\ a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho-p+2\Lambda\right),

so that the scalar curvature is
R=\rho-3p+4\Lambda
. Thus, one has the current (
39
) as

\displaystyle J^{a}_{K}:=J^{1}_{K}u^{a}+J^{2}_{K}n^{a},

(41)

with the definitions

\displaystyle J^{1}_{K}

\displaystyle=2\left(Q\bar{\alpha}-a_{1}\alpha-3\dot{\Psi}\right),

\displaystyle J^{2}_{K}

\displaystyle=2\left(\left(a_{2}+\Pi\right)\bar{\alpha}-Q\alpha+3\Psi^{\prime}\right).

From the equivalence of the components this imposes the constraints

\displaystyle 2f=J^{1}_{K},\ 2\bar{f}=J^{2}_{K}.

If we denote by
k^{a}=u^{a}+n^{a}
the “outgoing” null vector to 2-surfaces foliating the constant time hypersurfaces, we may combine the two constraints, as well as making use of the
u^{a}u^{b}
component of the conformal Killing equations given by

\displaystyle\Psi=\dot{\alpha}+\mathcal{A}\bar{\alpha},

(42)

to obtain the following relation between the first derivatives of
\Psi
:

\displaystyle\dot{\Psi}+\Psi^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\theta_{k}\Psi

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{A}-{u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b}\right)k^{a}\nabla_{a}\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}\alpha

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{2}+\Pi-Q\right)\bar{\alpha},

(43)

where
\theta_{k}=\delta_{a}k^{a}
is the 2-divergence of
k^{a}
, known as the outward null expansion, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\left(\theta_{k}+k^{a}\nabla_{a}\right)\left(\mathcal{A}-{u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b}\right)+a_{1}-Q.

The
n^{a}
component of the current is now recast as

\displaystyle J^{2}_{K}=3

\displaystyle\biggl[\left(a_{2}+\Pi-\frac{1}{3}Q\right)\bar{\alpha}+\left(\mathcal{F}-\frac{2}{3}Q\right)\alpha

\displaystyle-\theta_{k}\Psi-\left(\mathcal{A}-{u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b}\right)k^{a}\nabla_{a}\alpha-2\dot{\Psi}\biggr].

The vanishing of the Komar current here therefore imposes

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\theta_{k}\Psi+\left(\mathcal{A}-{u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b}\right)k^{a}\nabla_{a}\alpha-\left(\mathcal{F}+\frac{2}{3}(a_{1}-Q)\right)\alpha

\displaystyle-\left(a_{2}+\Pi-Q\right)\bar{\alpha},

(44)

both a necessary and sufficient condition.

As an example, consider a purely temporal CKV which aligns with
u^{a}
. Then, we have (where we also employ the
n^{a}n^{b}
component of the conformal Killing equations given by
\Psi=\alpha{u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b}
)

\displaystyle 0=\left(\theta_{k}+\mathcal{A}-{u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b}-\left(\mathcal{F}+\frac{2}{3}(a_{1}-Q)\right)({u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b})^{-1}\right)\Psi.

(45)

That is, either the CKV is a KV or

\displaystyle-\theta_{k}=\mathcal{A}-{u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b}-\left(\mathcal{F}+\frac{2}{3}(a_{1}-Q)\right)({u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b})^{-1}.

(46)

Now, the quantity
\theta_{k}
plays a very fundamental role in the study of the evolution of black holes (see the references
ash1
;
booth4
, and the for the literature relevant to LRS spacetimes and the formalism employed here, see
el3
;
as1
;
as3
;
as4
). If at all points of a surface
\mathcal{S}
,
\theta_{k}=0
,
\mathcal{S}
is called a marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS). These surfaces foliate horizons of black holes. For example, the Schwarzschild event horizon as well as dynamical horizons in the Lemaitre-Tolman-Bondi and the Vaidya (both charged and neutral cases) spacetimes are MOTS. (We emphasize here that MOTS need not foliate horizons enclosing black holes, for example those in the (anti)de-Sitter and Robertson-Walker spacetimes.) We note that the equation (
44
) allows for an interesting formulation of the MOTS condition:

Let
M
be a LRS II spacetime with vanishing Komar current constructed from a (proper) CKV (
28
). Then, any surface
\mathcal{S}\subset M
on which

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{A}-{u_{b}}^{\prime}n^{b}\right)k^{a}\nabla_{a}\alpha-\left(\mathcal{F}_{\theta_{k}=0}+\frac{2}{3}(a_{1}-Q)\right)\alpha

\displaystyle-\frac{8}{3}\left(a_{2}+\Pi-Q\right)\bar{\alpha}

(47)

This is an upper bound on the local energy density. Thus, in these spacetimes, these MOTS will exist only in regions obeying the inequality (

We may also compare the vanishing current condition (

\displaystyle\dot{\Psi}+\Psi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{3}(a_{1}-Q)\alpha.

For the conservation condition in the

Clearly, even for an HKV (including a KV), in this case, the current will vanish if and only if we have a vanishing

\displaystyle 0

In compact form, this PDE is the operator equation

\displaystyle\left(\Box-\Lambda\right)\Psi=0.

(54)

That is, the Komar current constructed for a CKV (

And since for vacuum the conformal divergence

For the case of an HKV (and not a KV, in which case we must have a vanishing

IV
On conformal Killing horizons and conserved currents from their generators in LRS II spacetimes

We now consider the Komar current in the presence of a conformal Killing horizon (CKH)
dy1
;
dy2
(the rotating counterpart to these horizons was introduced in
gh1
). We take this hypersurface to be the one over which the Komar integral is evaluated. We discuss those situations where the current vanishes on the CKH and what the conservation equation looks like. Before moving on to these considerations, let us first derive some additional properties of the CKH.

A CKH is a hypersurface on which the norm of a timelike conformal Killing vector field vanishes
dy1
. Specifically, we will be interested in those conformal Killing vector fields for which the gradient of its divergence vanishes nowhere on the CKH (shortly we will emphasize why this consideration is imposed).

In
as4
, the necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a conformal Killing horizon (CKH) in LRS spacetimes was derived. In particular, one requires that the components of the generator of the horizon be constant along the generator itself:

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\alpha=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\bar{\alpha},

(66)

with neither the components
\alpha
nor
\bar{\alpha}
being constant. Furthermore, it is imposed that there are no homothetic points on the CKH, i.e. no
p
on the CKH for which
D_{a}\Psi\overset{p}{=}0
, where
D_{a}
denotes the compatible covariant derivative on the CKH.

By projecting (
5
) with
N^{ab}
the form of the conformal divergence can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Psi=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\delta_{a}u^{a}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\alpha}\delta_{a}n^{a}.

(67)

Taking the Lie derivative of (
67
) along the CKV, upon evaluation at the CKH, gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\Psi=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\delta_{a}u^{a}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\delta_{a}n^{a}.

(68)

And since we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\Psi=\alpha\dot{\Psi}+\bar{\alpha}\Psi^{\prime},

(69)

the value of the respective derivatives at the CKH can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\dot{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\delta_{a}u^{a},

(70)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\delta_{a}n^{a}.

(71)

Associated with the horizon, one may define an invariant surface gravity
ted1
given by

\displaystyle-2\kappa\zeta_{a}=\nabla_{a}(\zeta_{b}\zeta^{b}),

(72)

which is constant on the horizon. It can indeed be shown, using properties of
\zeta^{a}
as a CKV, that
as4

\displaystyle-\zeta_{a}\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\kappa=\zeta_{b}\zeta^{b}\nabla_{a}\Psi.

(73)

If
\nabla_{a}\Psi
vanishes nowhere that
\zeta^{a}
is defined,
\kappa
is constant along the CKH. The consideration in
as4
was providing a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a conformal Killing horizon which requires the derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\kappa
vanishing on it. Where
\nabla_{a}\Psi
vanishes,
\kappa
is constant there and does not necessarily characterize the vanishing of the norm of
\zeta^{a}
at that point. For this reason, it was imposed in
as4
(and therefore the condition is imposed here for the purpose of this work) that the gradient of
\Psi
is non-vanishing.

Now, the constancy of the surface gravity may be expressed as
as4

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\Psi=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\left(\dot{\bar{\alpha}}+\mathcal{A}\alpha\right).

(74)

The Lie derivative of (
42
) along
\zeta^{a}
, equated to (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\left(\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\bar{\alpha}}\right)=0.

(75)

We note this also implies that on the horizon one has that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\left(\alpha^{\prime}+\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}\right)=0.

(76)

Now, another result of interest was obtained for LRS spacetimes (here we restrict to the LRS II class) with non-vanishing
\theta

that for any timelike vector field of the form (
28

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\delta_{a}n^{a}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\dot{\alpha}-\alpha\Psi^{\prime}=0.

Also, by the constancy of

\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\left(\dot{\alpha}-\dot{\bar{\alpha}}\right)=0

\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\dot{\alpha}=\mathcal{L}_{\zeta}\dot{\bar{\alpha}}=0

\Psi^{\prime}=0
, i.e. the conformal divergence is constant along integral curves of
n^{a}

is necessarily constant on a CKH in an expanding LRS II spacetime, by (

Another consequence of the vanishing of

is then the following result for LRS II solutions with perfect fluid type matter:

Consider a CKH in a perfect LRS II fluid with non-vanishing expansion and a vanishing cosmological constant, generated by a CKV

What the above says is that the usual timelike observers in these spacetimes will experience the current flowing through a CKH. It is also indeed clear that the Komar current is non-vanishing on the CKH as otherwise this would lead to homothetic points on the horizon. The vanishing of the Komar current in the case of the Robertson-Walker metric therefore verifies the non-existence of such horizons.

Notice from the

\displaystyle\left(a_{2}+\Pi\right)\bar{\alpha}-Q\alpha=0.

And since on the CKH the components of the CKV coincide, one has the above reducing to the following condition on the CKH

\displaystyle R_{ab}\overset{CKH}{=}a_{1}u_{a}u_{b},

i.e.

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}a_{1}=k^{a}\nabla_{a}\delta_{b}u^{b}.

That is, the restriction of the Ricci tensor to the CKH is specified by the “evolution” of the 2-divergence of the timelike congruences along outgoing null geodesics.

We now quickly comment on a property of a CKH that is independent of the definition of the current. As can be seen, on the CKH with

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}\zeta^{a}\not\propto\theta_{k}.

(83)

Finally, we comment on the integral (

The thermodynamic interpretation of

is now therefore explicit, due to the presence of the surface gravity

We know that
\alpha

V
Concluding remarks

The role of symmetries in theoretical physics is extensive, where their imprints can be found in various sectors of general relativity and quantum mechanics. In general relativity, a prevalent usage of symmetries is the generation of new exact solutions from existing ones that admit conformal symmetries. Yet, another well known application of symmetries is the construction of conserved quantities in spacetimes. One such quantity is the Komar current (sometimes referred to as the Noether current), from which one may construct a charge that may be associated to mass and other physical observables, by integrating the current over some hypersurface.

This work has examined the construction of the Komar current and the Noether charge in the class of locally rotationally symmetric spacetimes. Owing to the symmetry of these spacetimes, we have employed a well-adapted covariant formulation and constructed two equivalent forms of the Komar current: a kinematic form and a geometric form. This equivalence is exploited to obtain an inhomogeneous first order partial differential equation for the divergence of the CKV, which we called the conformal divergence. This equation may be used to generate a CKV and consequently define a Komar current. The condition for conservation of the current, as well as its vanishing, is discussed, from which several results related to restrictions on some of the spacetime variables are obtained. The relationship of properties of the current to the trapping of surfaces of constant time and radius are also obtained and discussed. That is, through the definition of current, we are able to possibly detect the presence of black holes from their horizon cross sections, or marginally outer trapped surfaces (MOTS).

Finally, we have considered conformal Killing horizons (CKH), null hypersurfaces on which the norm of a CKV vanishes, which admit no homothetic points (those points on the CKH where the gradient of the conformal divergence vanishes). Several additional properties of the CKH are derived in the case that the constant time hypersurfaces have non-vanishing expansion. It is shown that on a CKH in LRS II spacetimes, the conformal divergence will be constant in the radial direction, which consequently requires that the expansion of the constant time and radius surfaces, called the sheet expansion, is also constant along the CKH. These properties rule out that the CKH can be foliated by MOTS. The form of the Noether charge along the CKH is then computed and is found proportional to the invariant surface gravity associated to the CKH. For perfect fluid solutions, it is shown the the strong energy condition being valid along the CKH ensures that the CKH has a non-vanishing Noether charge.

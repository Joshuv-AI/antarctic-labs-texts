Infi-Med: Low-Resource Medical MLLMs with Robust Reasoning Evaluation Corresponding to: hongxia.yang@polyu.edu.hk
Zeyu Liu, Zhitian Hou, Yining Di, Kejing Yang, Zhijie Sang, Congkai Xie, Jingwen Yang, Siyuan Liu, Jialu Wang, Chunming Li, Ming Li, Hongxia Yang

Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated promising prospects in healthcare, particularly for addressing complex medical tasks, supporting multidisciplinary treatment (MDT), and enabling personalized precision medicine. However, their practical deployment faces critical challenges in resource efficiency, diagnostic accuracy, clinical considerations, and ethical privacy. To address these limitations, we propose Infi-Med, a comprehensive framework for medical MLLMs that introduces three key innovations: (1) a resource-efficient approach through curating and constructing high-quality supervised fine-tuning (SFT) datasets with minimal sample requirements, with a forward-looking design that extends to both pretraining and posttraining phases; (2) enhanced multimodal reasoning capabilities for cross-modal integration and clinical task understanding; and (3) a systematic evaluation system that assesses model performance across medical modalities and task types. Our experiments demonstrate that Infi-Med achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance in general medical reasoning while maintaining rapid adaptability to clinical scenarios. The framework establishes a solid foundation for deploying MLLMs in real-world healthcare settings by balancing model effectiveness with operational constraints.



1
Introduction

The rapid development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has marked a transformative phase in artificial intelligence, among whose many applications the field of healthcare stands out as particularly promising. As clinical workflows increasingly demand integration of heterogeneous data sources ranging from imaging and genomics to electronic health records (EHRs), MLLMs offer unprecedented opportunities to address challenges in complex medical tasks and decision-making. The philosophy of MLLM-based foundation models has further accelerated the shift toward personalized and precision-driven healthcare, aiming to revolutionize clinical practices by enabling tailored diagnostic pathways, optimizing treatment strategies, and enhancing patient engagement through adaptive, context-aware interactions.

Compared to traditional AI models, MLLMs demonstrate distinct advantages in healthcare applications. First, their cross-modal reasoning capabilities allow seamless integration of diverse data types, addressing the inherent heterogeneity of medical data of different omics, departments, and medical modalities. Second, they provide a unified foundation for tackling a wide spectrum of tasks from diagnosis and treatment planning to prognosis prediction, while supporting adaptability to personalized precision medicine. Third, MLLMs enhance multidisciplinary treatment (MDT) decision-making by synthesizing fragmented inputs into actionable expert-like insights, bridging gaps between specialties, and improving care coordination for precision medicine.

Despite these promising prospects, deploying GenAI, especially MLLM-based approaches in real-world healthcare settings faces critical challenges. A primary limitation lies in the direct application of general-purpose models to medical domains, where domain-specific characteristics such as rare diseases or regulatory constraints often lead to suboptimal performance. While domain-specific fine-tuning is essential, it introduces barriers such as high computational costs and reliance on high-quality, annotated datasets, which are scarce in certain healthcare domains. Resource efficiency thus becomes a pressing concern, as training and fine-tuning MLLMs for medical applications can be prohibitively expensive. Diagnostic accuracy poses another challenge that healthcare applications demand exceptionally high reliability, as errors can have severe consequences for patient outcomes. Additionally, clinical interpretability and workflow integration are crucial for clinician trust, requiring models to align with medical guidelines and clinical practices. Finally, ethical and privacy concerns persist, particularly when handling sensitive patient data, necessitating robust safeguards against bias and data misuse.

To address the aforementioned challenges and unlock the full potential of MLLMs in healthcare, we propose Infi-Med, a comprehensive framework specifically designed for medical MLLMs. Within the framework, we comprehensively construct and evaluate essential medical capabilities across four critical dimensions:
Instruction Following
, aligning model outputs with user-defined clinical intents (e.g., generating structured reports or zero-shot diagnostic;
Fine-Grained Visual Grounding
, ensuring precise localization of interested lesions/regions in medical imaging from radiology, pathology, etc. (e.g., tumors in X-rays) via spatial supervision;
Medical Knowledge Reasoning
, which enables models to interpret clinical input using structured domain knowledge for clinical subtasks including modality recognition, anatomical localization, disease feature interpretation, option comparison reasoning, and question-image alignment; and
Robustness
, maintaining stable performance across variations or degradation in input distribution and question phrasing to ensure clinical reliability, with predictions driven by genuine semantic reasoning rather than reliance on spurious patterns or positional biases.
Building on these dimensions, Infi-Med makes the following contributions that directly tackle the limitations of current medical GenAI solutions:

\bullet

Resource-efficient methodologies
:
We adopt a low-resource fine-tuning strategy with 150k high-quality medical data, achieving an Infi-Med-3B model that matches or surpasses larger SOTA models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, InternVL3-8B). The model is able to achieve relative performance between 83% and 99% compared to these SOTA models while outperforming second-best models by up to 26.6% in certain benchmarks. Additionally, our forward-looking design ensures resource efficiency across both pretraining and fine-tuning stages.

\bullet

Enhanced multimodal reasoning
: Through curated multi-modal datasets and chain-of-thought annotations, Infi-Med-3B demonstrates significant improvements in cross-modal integration and domain-specific reasoning. It outperforms baseline Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, by scores ranging from +1.72% to +29.63% (SLAKE(en)) across different benchmarks.

\bullet

Systematic evaluation system
: We first abstract four critical dimensions of medical MLLM capabilities and five representative clinical skills by analyzing widely-used medical benchmark datasets. Based on this framework, we construct a comprehensive evaluation system using six carefully selected datasets that span diverse medical modalities (e.g., radiology, pathology) and task types (e.g., diagnosis, reasoning). This structured approach enables rigorous validation of cross-modal and cross-task generalization capabilities through standardized assessments.

This study pioneers the strategic integration of essential medical competencies under stringent computational constraints, enabling practical deployment of MLLMs in healthcare environments. Through novel data curation strategies and optimized model architecture, Infi-Med-3B achieves competitive performance against both parameter-matched and larger-scale models. Notably, it demonstrates clinical reasoning consistency across five medical domains, enhanced instruction adherence, and robustness to input variations. The model bridges critical gaps in resource-constrained medical AI, offering scalable solutions for real-world applications. Subsequent sections detail our approach: Section

Multimodal Large Language Models in Healthcare

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have emerged as pivotal tools in healthcare, addressing the complex interplay between medical knowledge, visual data, and clinical reasoning. Extensive research efforts have been made to enhance MLLM’s unique capabilities of integrating heterogeneous data types and medical modalities in several critical dimensions. Moreover, while techniques like chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning and unified embedding have improved interpretability, there is limited research on ensuring these models generalize effectively across diverse medical specialties or adapt to rapidly evolving biomedical knowledge. These gaps highlight the need for a more systematic approach to enhance the scalability, transparency, and adaptability of MLLMs in complex healthcare applications.

3
Method

In this section, we introduce two key components of our framework: (1) an efficient data curation pipeline designed to address the unique challenges of establishing comprehensive medical capability dimensions by integrating diverse, high-quality multimodal datasets with domain-specific reasoning capabilities, and (2) a comprehensive evaluation system that rigorously assesses the model’s performance across specified dimensions and clinical skills. Together, these components provide a robust foundation for advancing medical MLLMs in both development and deployment.

Due to the diverse nature of our data sources, we implemented comprehensive data processing procedures to ensure consistency and quality throughout the datasets. Initially, datasets were standardized into the ShareGPT format, followed by several enhancement techniques to maximize data quality and usability:

•

Instruction Augmentation.
To mitigate issues arising from vague or underspecified prompts in the dataset, we designed a set of standardized templates that explicitly clarify the expected relationship between instructions and responses. For example, in multiple-choice tasks, we appended clarifying prompts such as “Answer with the option’s letter from the given choices” to enforce standardized response formats and ensure output consistency.

•

Response Enhancement.
In scenarios involving brief responses, such as multiple-choice tasks, we utilized a powerful multimodal reasoning model to generate explanatory rationales. Subsequently, rejection sampling was applied to filter and retain only explanations that were faithful, logically consistent, and correctly aligned with the given options. The prompt of response enhancement is presented in Appendix
A
.

•

Caption Refinement.
To elevate the accuracy and informativeness of image descriptions, we employed a powerful multimodal reasoning model to refine original captions. These enhanced captions enable the model to more precisely identify and interpret detailed visual features in medical imagery, thus supporting improved diagnostic accuracy and clinical decision-making. The prompt used for caption refinement is detailed in Appendix
A

Following comprehensive data synthesis and augmentation, we employed rigorous data selection to further improve resource efficiency and reduce training noise. Specifically, we evaluated the semantic alignment between medical images and their corresponding captions by computing the perplexity scores of each image-caption pair under Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct
[
30
]
. Only pairs demonstrating high semantic consistency were retained, providing more reliable and informative supervision during model training. To facilitate data decontamination, we employed the gme-Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct
[
31
]
model to compute the similarity between image-query pairs and evaluation benchmark samples. Pairs with a similarity score exceeding 95% were excluded to mitigate contamination and ensure fair evaluation. Details of the final number of samples used for SFT are provided in Appendix

These data curation strategies are carefully designed to enhance the model’s capabilities across several key dimensions. By incorporating general-purpose instruction-following datasets such as LLaVA-OneVision-Data and instruction augmentation, we consolidate the model’s
Instruction Following

By incorporating datasets such as PMC-VQA, VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and GMAI-Reasoning10K, and enriching them with explanatory rationales and verified chains of thought, we strengthen the model’s ability in

3.2
Evaluation System

To assess the effectiveness of our method, we develop a comprehensive evaluation framework aimed at analyzing the model’s capabilities across various dimensions. We select a diverse set of widely-used Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks that include both closed-ended and open-ended questions. Thefse benchmarks are carefully chosen to ensure broad coverage of medical imaging modalities (e.g., X-ray, CT, pathology) as well as domain-specific medical knowledge.
We briefly describe each benchmark used in our evaluation framework below.

VQA-RAD
is a manually curated dataset of radiology images (e.g., X-ray, CT, MRI), reflecting realistic diagnostic tasks.

PATH-VQA
is a medical VQA benchmark including pathological and cellular slides, which sets it apart from earlier datasets that predominantly focus on radiology.

SLAKE
is a bilingual dataset designed for medical VQA, featuring clinically relevant questions curated by medical professionals.

PMC-VQA

[
25
]
is a large-scale generative VQA dataset supporting open-ended medical question answering. Additionally, it provides a manually validated test set to evaluate the performance of generative Medical VQA methods.

MMMU (Health & Medicine)
is a subset of the MMMU benchmark, featuring medical-related multimodal questions across five subfields, including basic science, clinical medicine, diagnostics, pharmacy, and public health.

GMAI-MMBench
is a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating medical image-understanding and reasoning across diverse modalities, tasks, and data sources, encompassing multiple levels of perceptual granularity.

To enable a systematic analysis of model behavior, we identify a set of key capability dimensions that are critical to performance in medical vision-language tasks, along with corresponding evaluation strategies for each. These dimensions and our assessment methodology are described below.

•

Instruction Following:
Robust instruction following is critical for aligning model outputs with user-specified prompts or clinical intents, particularly in interactive or zero-shot scenarios. This capability reflects how well a model can adapt its reasoning and generation based on natural language instructions.
To evaluate this, we apply a rule-based method to extract answers from closed-ended tasks and analyze the proportion of outputs that fail to conform to expected formats, such as missing or incorrectly structured answer options. This allows us to assess both the model’s instruction adherence and its reliability in producing usable outputs for downstream tasks.

•

Fine-Grained Visual Grounding:
Accurately localizing lesions, organs, and other fine-grained anatomical or pathological regions is essential for effective vision-language modeling in medical VQA, where diagnostic reasoning relies heavily on spatially grounded visual cues. To evaluate this capability, we introduce a grounding prompting strategy, where the model is required to generate both an answer and a bounding box as visual evidence. By comparing model performance with and without grounding prompts, we assess the effect of spatial supervision on performance.

•

Medical Knowledge Reasoning:
Medical vision-language tasks demand advanced domain-specific reasoning, wherein models must leverage internalized medical knowledge to interpret visual inputs and draw clinically valid conclusions. To evaluate this capability, we define five clinically relevant reasoning skills: modality recognition, anatomical localization, disease feature interpretation, option comparison reasoning, and text-image alignment. These dimensions reflect core components of clinical reasoning in multimodal medical tasks, encompassing not only image interpretation but also the integration of anatomical, pathological, and modality-specific cues. We assess performance by analyzing the model’s chain-of-thought (CoT) outputs, qualitatively inspecting whether the reasoning demonstrates structured clinical logic across these dimensions.

•

Robustness:
Medical applications place high demands on the reliability of the model. Therefore, robustness, defined as the ability to maintain consistent performance across variations in input distribution and question phrasing, is essential for practical deployment and clinical reliability.
To evaluate this capability, we select closed-ended (i.e., multiple-choice) VQA tasks and assess the model’s sensitivity to input perturbations by altering the order of answer choices and rephrasing the questions. This allows us to systematically test the robustness of the model under controlled variations.

4.3
Analysis

To better understand the strengths and limitations of current multimodal language models in the medical domain, we perform a comprehensive analysis across the four core capability dimensions introduced earlier: instruction following, fine-grained visual grounding, medical knowledge reasoning, and robustness. This analysis not only reveals where existing models tend to struggle, but also demonstrates that Infi-Med achieves consistent improvements across all four dimensions, highlighting its enhanced ability to handle complex and clinically relevant VQA tasks.

Instruction following

To evaluate the instruction-following capability of Infi-Med, we applied a rule-based parsing strategy to extract answer choices from the responses on multiple-choice (closed-ended) benchmarks, where answers must be selected from options labeled A through E. Responses that failed to include a recognizable option are considered to be invalid. Infi-Med achieved a low invalid response rate of 1.33%, outperforming the baseline rate of 2.38%, demonstrating strong reliability in producing well-structured outputs under constrained formatting requirements. To further verify instruction-following capability, we analyzed Infi-Med and baseline’s responses on the PMC-VQA clean test set using a standardized prompt requiring direct option-letter outputs. By categorizing responses into single-letter, answer-prefixed, and CoT-style formats, we found that Infi-Med preserves instruction-following consistency while achieving higher accuracy.

Fined-grained visual grounding

We assess the model’s visual grounding ability on several medical VQA benchmarks and find that its performance depends on task complexity and supervision. On structured, closed-ended datasets like VQA-RAD, PATH-VQA, and SLAKE, grounding prompts improve accuracy by 4–7%, showing that visual cues help guide the model’s reasoning.
In contrast, on more complex tasks like MMMU, grounding prompts often reduce accuracy across most subfields. For example, Infi-Med shows a slight overall drop, but grounding improves performance in "Diagnostics and Laboratory Medicine" (from 40.00% to 43.33%), suggesting benefits in tasks involving pathology images. However, accuracy declines in subfields like clinical diagrams, likely because grounding prompts make the model focus too narrowly on local features, missing broader context.
These findings suggest that further prompt refinement and task-specific tuning are needed to improve performance in global visual reasoning tasks. Similarly, on GMAI-MMBench, although Infi-Med continues to outperform baseline models overall, grounding prompts result in slight performance drops on samples that require holistic understanding, indicating room for further improvement in visual grounding capability.

Medical Knowledge Reasoning

According to the five clinically grounded dimensions introduced in Section
3.2
, modality recognition, anatomical localization, disease feature interpretation, option comparison reasoning, and question-image alignment, we qualitatively assessed Infi-Med’s reasoning performance by analyzing its generated chain-of-thought (CoT) outputs on PMC-VQA samples. The results indicate that Infi-Med consistently demonstrates structured and clinically aligned reasoning behaviors across these dimensions. As illustrated in Figure
2
, Infi-Med accurately distinguishes imaging modalities, localizes pathological findings with anatomical precision, and links visual features to plausible diagnoses using domain knowledge. These CoT traces often exhibit clear logical progression, including visual feature extraction, distractor elimination, and spatial grounding.

Robustness

To evaluate the robustness of Infi-Med’s reasoning capabilities, we introduce a perturbation setting in which the answer choices for each sample are randomly shuffled. This intervention removes any positional correlation between the correct answer and its original index, thereby testing the model’s ability to perform semantic reasoning independently of answer order. We assess Infi-Med under this setting across multiple benchmarks. Compared to its original performance on the unshuffled datasets, Infi-Med exhibits only minimal variation—consistently within 2 percentage points—demonstrating strong invariance to answer choice ordering. Remarkably, despite being trained in a low-resource setting, Infi-Med maintains stable performance under input perturbation, suggesting that its predictions are grounded in genuine visual and textual understanding rather than reliance on superficial patterns such as positional biases.

5
Conclusion

This study presents Infi-Med, a novel framework designed to address critical challenges in deploying MLLMs for real-world healthcare applications. By integrating resource-efficient fine-tuning pipelines, domain-specific multimodal reasoning enhancements, and a systematic evaluation methodology, Infi-Med overcomes key limitations of existing medical GenAI systems. The framework achieves high performance through a low-resource fine-tuning strategy. Using only 150k training samples, it effectively addresses barriers such as data scarcity and high training costs. To improve cross-modal integration and clinical reasoning, Infi-Med leverages high-quality multimodal datasets augmented with multi-step formatting and CoT techniques, ensuring robust diagnostic and task-specific capabilities. A comprehensive evaluation system further validates its effectiveness across critical medical capability dimensions, including instruction following, medical knowledge reasoning, fine-grained visual grounding, and robustness, spanning five clinical skills and covering representative medical tasks.
Experimental results demonstrate Infi-Med’s superiority over state-of-the-art models of comparable size, such as Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, by achieving up to 29.63% improvement in benchmark evaluations. Notably, it matches or even exceeds the performance of larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and InternVL3-8B while maintaining robustness under adversarial perturbations. Infi-Med is also proven to excel in clinical reasoning and instruction-following tasks. These findings highlight Infi-Med’s potential as a scalable solution for resource-constrained healthcare environments where both efficiency and precision are paramount.

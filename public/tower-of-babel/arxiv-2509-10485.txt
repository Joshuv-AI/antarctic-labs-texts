U(a)=\Big(\tfrac{1}{a},\tfrac{2}{a},\dots,\tfrac{a-1}{a}\Big),\qquad a=2,3,\dots,

(1)

i_{0}(a)=\sum_{t=2}^{a-1}(t-1)=\frac{(a-2)(a-1)}{2}

D

Independently, define the

Definition 1.1

(First appearance)

For
d\geq 2

\pi(d)

We say that level

1/a

Theorem 1.2

(Pairing / simultaneous novelty)

For every
n\geq 2

\pi(n+i)=i_{0}(n)+i\quad\text{and}\quad\pi(n-i)=i_{0}(n)-i.

Equivalently, both

Preliminaries: Calkin–Wilf, Stern–Brocot, and words

Let
\mathrm{CW}
denote the breadth-first enumeration of
\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
obtained by reading the Calkin–Wilf tree level by level
[
2
]
. Each node corresponds to a unique word
w\in\{L,R\}^{\ast}
in the generators

L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),

(4)

and we write
\beta(w)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for the breadth-first rank of
w
in length-lex order. If
w=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

then the associated reduced fraction is

is thus

\pi(d)=\min\{\beta(w):w\in\{L,R\}^{\ast},\ (w)_{21}=d\},

(5)

which is well-defined since each reduced rational appears exactly once in

4
Local coherence of first-appearance times

Fix
n\geq 2
and define, for
0\leq i\leq n-2
,

F_{+}(i):=\pi(n+i)-\bigl(i_{0}(n)+i\bigr),\qquad F_{-}(i):=\bigl(i_{0}(n)-i\bigr)-\pi(n-i).

(6)

Intuitively,
F_{\pm}(i)=0
means that the first appearances of denominators
n\pm i
land at the symmetric slots about
i_{0}(n)
inside the
mirror window

J_{n}=[\,i_{0}(n)-(n-2),\,i_{0}(n)+(n-2)\,].

(7)

We use standard facts about Christoffel words and the length-lex breadth-first order to control local changes of
\pi
when the denominator changes by
\pm 1
.

Lemma 4.1

(Local coherence)
.

For
F_{\pm}
in (
6
) the following hold:

(i)

Monotonicity:

F_{\pm}(i+1)\geq F_{\pm}(i)
for all
i
.

(ii)

Bounded step:

F_{\pm}(i+1)-F_{\pm}(i)
is bounded above by an absolute constant independent of
n
.

(iii)

Two-sided hits:

F_{+}(0)\leq 0\leq F_{+}(n-2)
and
F_{-}(0)\leq 0\leq F_{-}(n-2)
.

Proof idea.

Let
w_{d}
be a word that realizes
\pi(d)
, i.e.
\beta(w_{d})=\pi(d)
.
By Christoffel/Lyndon surgery, moving from
d
to
d\pm 1
alters only a terminal factor of
w_{d}

[
4
,
1
, Chs. 2–3]
. In breadth-first length-lex order, such a local change induces a controlled change of rank; after subtracting/adding the linear center shift
\pm 1
in (
6
), one gets (i) and (ii).
For (iii), the Stern–Brocot symmetry (left-right reversal at each fixed depth) guarantees that first appearances occur on both sides of the center
i_{0}(n)
within the mirror window (
7
); cf.
[
3
, Sec. 4.5]
.
∎

Proof of the pairing theorem

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

By
Lemma

4.1
(iii) the functions

start nonpositive and end nonnegative on

Lemma

4.1

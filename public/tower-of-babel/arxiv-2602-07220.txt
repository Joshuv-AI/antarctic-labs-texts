Abstract
. In this note we consider two topics involving the relationship between the symplectic capacity and the mean width of convex bodies in ℝ 2 ​ n {\mathbb{R}}^{2n} . We first describe an alternative path from the symplectic Brunn-Minkowski inequality of Artstein-Avidan and Ostrover in [ 3 ] to another inequality from [ 3 ] that relates the capacity and mean width of convex bodies. This new path is less direct but it relates these inequalities to the quermassintegrals of convex bodies and to the local version of Viterbo’s conjecture established in [ 1 ] for domains sufficiently close to the ball. We then consider the problem of identifying convex bodies whose mean width cannot be decreased by natural classes of symplectomorphisms. Motivated by the results in [ 2 ] , we state a conjectured characterization of convex bodies whose mean width is already minimal among all their symplectic images. To test this conjecture we identify a simple class of quadratic convex bodies whose mean width can not be decreased by linear symplectic maps near the identity. We then identify a subset of these examples that fail to satisfy the toric conditions of the conjecture, and show that one can find a nonlinear symplectomorphism that decreases their mean width.

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(0)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{|I_{\ell}|}\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{\rho_{\ell}c_{i(\ell,m),i(\ell,m)}x_{i(\ell,m)}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell}u\|}\,\,d\sigma-\sum_{m=1}^{|J_{\ell}|}\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{\rho_{\ell}c_{j(\ell,m),j(\ell,m)}y_{j(\ell,m)}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell}u\|}\,\,d\sigma\right).

For each integer
r
between
1
and
n
there will be exactly one term in (
17
) with
x_{r}^{2}
in the numerator of the integrand, and another with
y_{r}^{2}
. To show that
f^{\prime}(0)=0
it remains to show that condition (
12
) implies that these terms cancel.

Fixing
r
, we note that it is contained in a unique
I_{\ell}
and a unique
J_{\ell^{\prime}}
. If
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, then the terms in (
17
) corresponding to

and
y_{r}^{2}
are

\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{\rho_{\ell}c_{r,r}x_{r}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell}u\|}\,\,d\sigma\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad-\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{\rho_{\ell}c_{r,r}y_{r}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell}u\|}\,\,d\sigma,

which clearly cancel. If
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
, then the terms in (
17
) corresponding
x_{r}^{2}
and
y_{r}^{2}
are

(18)

\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{\rho_{\ell}c_{r,r}x_{r}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell}u\|}\,\,d\sigma\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad-\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}c_{r,r}y_{r}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell^{\prime}}u\|}\,\,d\sigma.

In this case, (
12
) implies that
|I_{\ell}|+|J_{\ell}|=|I_{\ell^{\prime}}|+|J_{\ell^{\prime}}|
and
\rho_{\ell}=\rho_{\ell^{\prime}}
.
Setting
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}=\{x_{(i,1)},\dots,x_{(i,|I_{\ell}|)}\}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}=\{y_{(j,1)},\dots,y_{(j,|J_{\ell}|)}\}
, it follows that we can choose a bijection from
\mathcal{X}_{\ell}\cup\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}
to
\mathcal{X}_{\ell^{\prime}}\cup\mathcal{Y}_{\ell^{\prime}}
that maps
x_{r}
to
y_{r}
. Extending this bijection to an orientation preserving bijection of
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, the corresponding change of variables yields

\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{x_{r}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell}u\|}\,\,d\sigma=\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{y_{r}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell^{\prime}}u\|}\,\,d\sigma,

and so the terms in (
18
) cancel, as desired.

It remains to prove that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)>0
. Note that

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(s)=\int_{S^{2n-1}}\text{Hess}(H_{\mathcal{P}(\bar{\rho},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})})(S(s)u)(S^{\prime}(s)u,S^{\prime}(s)u)\,d\sigma+\int_{S^{2n-1}}\langle\nabla H_{\mathcal{P}(\bar{\rho},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})}(S(s)u),S^{\prime\prime}(s)u\rangle\,d\sigma,

where,
\text{Hess}(H_{\mathcal{P}(\bar{\rho},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})})
denotes the Hessian of
H_{\mathcal{P}(\bar{\rho},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})}
which is well-defined and positive definite almost everywhere. Since
S^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
, the first summand on the right is positive when
s=0
, and it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2n-1}}\langle\nabla H_{\mathcal{P}(\bar{\rho},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})}(u),S^{\prime\prime}(0)u\rangle\,d\sigma\geq 0.

We can write
S^{\prime}(s)=S(s)X(s)
for a family of symmetric matrices
X(s)\in\mathfrak{sp}(2n)
of the form

(19)

\displaystyle X(s)=\begin{pmatrix}C(s)&D(s)\\
D(s)&-C(s)\end{pmatrix},

where the submatrices
C(s)
and
D(s)
are all symmetric. We then have
S^{\prime\prime}(s)=S(s)(X(s)^{2}+X^{\prime}(s))
and
S^{\prime\prime}(0)=X(0)^{2}+X^{\prime}(0)
. Arguing as above, we have

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2n-1}}\langle\nabla H_{\mathcal{P}(\bar{\rho},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})}(u),X^{\prime}(0)u\rangle\,d\sigma=0,

since
X^{\prime}(0)
has the same form as
Y
. It remains to show that

\int_{S^{2n-1}}\langle\nabla H_{\mathcal{P}(\bar{\rho},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})}(u),X(0)^{2}u\rangle d\sigma.
is nonnegative.
By (
19
) we have

\displaystyle X(0)^{2}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C(0)^{2}+D(0)^{2}&C(0)D(0)-D(0)C(0)\\
D(0)C(0)-C(0)D(0)&C(0)^{2}+D(0)^{2}\end{pmatrix}=:\begin{pmatrix}L&M\\
-M&L\end{pmatrix},

where the submatrix
L=(l_{ij})
is symmetric, the submatrix
M=(m_{ij})
is skew-symmetric and the diagonal entries of
L
are all nonnegative.
Noting that the relevant cross-terms again vanish, it follows that
\int_{S^{2n-1}}\langle\nabla H_{\mathcal{P}(\bar{\rho},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})}(u),X(0)^{2}u\rangle d\sigma.
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\rho_{\ell}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{|I_{\ell}|}\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{l_{i(\ell,m),i(\ell,m)}x_{i(\ell,m)}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell}u\|}\,\,d\sigma+\sum_{m=1}^{|J_{\ell}|}\int_{S^{2n-1}}\frac{l_{j(\ell,m),j(\ell,m)}y_{j(\ell,m)}^{2}}{\|\pi_{\ell}u\|}\,\,d\sigma\right)

which is nonnegative, as desired

3.0.2.
Testing Conjecture
3.8
.

One can also determine exactly when there exists a

such that

Q(\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}})

is toric.

Lemma 3.12
.

The set
Q(\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}})
is toric for some
Q\in\mathrm{U}(n)
if and only if every factor of
\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}}
is symplectic.

Proof.

One can easily check that the product
\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}}
is toric if and only each of its factors is symplectic. For
Q\in\mathrm{U}(n)
, the set

Q(\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}})

is a standard product of balls with respect to the symplectic basis

\{Q(e_{1}),\dots,Q(e_{n}),Q(f_{1}),\dots,Q(f_{n})\}.

By our choice of the basis
\{e_{1},\dots f_{n}\}
, the set

Q(\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}})

is then toric if and only if there is a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that

\mathrm{Span}\{Q(e_{i}),Q(f_{i})\}=\mathrm{Span}\{e_{\sigma(i)},f_{\sigma(i)})\}

for
i=1,\dots,n
. By the discussion above, this implies that
Q(\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}})

is toric if and only each of its factors is symplectic. Since the factors of
Q(\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}})
are symplecic if and only if those of
\rho_{1}B^{m_{1}}\times\rho_{2}B^{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\rho_{k}B^{m_{k}}
are, we are done.
∎

defined by

On the other hand, Lemma

is not toric for any

. Conjecture

restricts to this family as the following simple assertion.

For any

in

\Phi

As mentioned above, if

,

The Model.
The square
\square\;{:=}\;[-1,1]\times[-1,1]
is an element of
\mathcal{P}^{2}_{test}
. A simple computation yields

{{\mathrm{M}}}(\square)=\frac{8}{\pi}.

The ball
\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}B^{2}
has the same volume as
\square

{{\mathrm{M}}}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}B^{2}\right)=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}.

Lemma 3.14
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there is a symplectomorphism
\phi_{\epsilon}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that

{{\mathrm{M}}}(\phi_{\epsilon}(\square))\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}+\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
g_{\epsilon}\colon\square\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a smooth embedding with the following properties:

(i)

g_{\epsilon}(\square)
is convex,

(ii)

{\mathrm{Volume}}(g_{\epsilon}(\square))={\mathrm{Volume}}(\square)=4
,

(iii)

The Hausdorff distance between
g_{\epsilon}(\square)
and
\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}B^{2}
is less than
\epsilon.

By property (ii), and the version of Moser’s theorem for manifolds with corners established in
[
6
]
, the map
g_{\epsilon}
yields a symplectic embedding
f_{\epsilon}\colon\square\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with the same image as
g_{\epsilon}
and hence properties (i)-(iii). Extend this map
f_{\epsilon}
to an embedding

where
\mathcal{N}(\square)
is a convex neighborhood of

[

\square

with respect to the Hausdorff metric. Finally, applying the

(

14

.

Lemma 3.15

(see formula (13.51) in

Suppose that

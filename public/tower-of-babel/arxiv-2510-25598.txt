Holonomy in pseudo-Hermitian geometry Thanks: Corresponding author Email: anton.galaev@uhk.cz
Anton S. Galaev, Thomas Leistner, Felipe Leitner

Abstract
. We study the holonomy that is associated to a sub-Riemannian structure defined on the kernel of a global contact form. This includes the holonomy of Schouten’s horizontal connection as well as of the adapted connection, both canonical invariants of the structure. Under a condition on the torsion of the structure, we show that they are either equal or that the former is a codimension one normal subgroup of the latter. Furthermore, we establish a close relation to Riemannian holonomy, which yields a complete holonomy classification in the torsion-free case. For the main result we focus on the special case of pseudo-Hermitian structures and give a classification of holonomy algebras for both the Schouten and the adapted connection. Based on this, we derive a classification of symmetric sub-Riemannian structures and of of those holonomy groups that admit parallel spinors. Finally we exhibit a relation between locally symmetric sub-Riemannian contact structures and locally homogeneous Riemannian structures. Keywords : holonomy; pseudo-Hermitian structures; sub-Riemannian structures; Schouten connection; sub-symmetric spaces; parallel spinors AMS Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 53C29; 53C17; 15A66



1.
Introduction

The classification of holonomy groups of Riemannian manifolds by M. Berger in
[
3
]
is a classical result that has a many consequences and applications, see for example
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
18
]
.
For CR manifolds the situation is very different, as it is stated in the well-known monograph on the topic
[
10
, p. 330]
:

,,A systematic study of the (pseudo-Hermitian) holonomy of a CR manifold is still missing in the present-day mathematical literature.”

The goal of this paper is to fill this gap by providing a classification of holonomy algebras of pseudo-Hermitian spaces. Let us describe its contents and its results.

In Section
2
, we review basic facts on contact sub-Riemannian manifolds
(M,\theta,g)
, which we defines as given by a fixed contact form
\theta
on a smooth manifold
M
and a is a sub-Riemannian metric
g
on the contact distribution
{\mathcal{D}}=\ker\theta
. An invariant of such a structure is the Lie derivative of
g
along the Reeb vector field
\xi
, denoted by
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g
and called the
sub-torsion
or
torsion
of
(M,\theta,g)
.
A vector field or a curve is called
horizontal
if it is tangent to
{\mathcal{D}}
.
The analogue for the Levi-Civita connection is the
Schouten connection

\nabla
, which is a partial connection along horizontal vector fields. Then we consider extended connections
\nabla^{N}
, i.e., connections on the vector bundle
{\mathcal{D}}
over
M
that arise from the Schouten connection and an endomorphism
N
of
{\mathcal{D}}
. For our approach two extended connections will be crucial: the well-known
adapted connection

\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
and the lesser known
Wagner connection

\nabla^{W}
, which originates from
[
26
]
.
Both connections preserve the metric
g
on
{\mathcal{D}}
, and we will write down the expressions for the curvature tensors of these connections.
The importance of the Wagner connection for holonomy was realized by the first author in
[
16
]
, and it continues to provide a key tool in the current paper.

In Section
3
, we consider the related holonomy groups. The holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)
of the Schouten connection
\nabla
is defined as the group of parallel transports along horizontal loops,
[
12
]
. The holonomy groups
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
and
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{W})
are defined, as usual, as the groups of parallel transports along all loops.
We recall the Ambrose–Singer type holonomy theorems from
[
8
,
12
]
for the group
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)
, and we stress that the results in
[
8
,
12
]
imply the equality

\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{W})=\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla).

Then we generalize the results from
[
16
]
. There the first author considered simply connected
K-contact manifolds
, i.e., those with vanishing torsion,
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
, and proved
that if the Reeb vector field is complete, then

(1)

either
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)=\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
, or
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)\subset\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
is a codimension-one normal subgroup,

and that
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
is a holonomy group of a Riemannian manifold.
As first generalisation of these results, here we show the following:

(a)

The dichotomy (
1
) holds when the torsion is a Codazzi-tensor (a differential condition on
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
, completeness of
\xi
is not assumed anymore,
see Theorem
3.13
).

(b)

When
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is not Codazzi and

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
is irreducible, it is the holonomy algebra of a Riemannian manifold or equal to
\mathfrak{sp}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)
(see Proposition
3.12
, and note that the latter is not Berger algebra when
k\geq 2
).

In order to prove the first result, for any extended connection
\nabla^{N}
, we define a modified holonomy group
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}(\nabla^{N})
, which consists of parallel transports along loops
\gamma
for which the integral of
\theta
along
\gamma
is zero. We prove that if the Reeb vector field
\xi
is complete, then there is a dichotomy: either
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}(\nabla^{N})=\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})
, or
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}(\nabla^{N})\subset\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})
is a codimension-one Lie subgroup.
This is then used to prove (
a
) and (
b
) and we are able to drop the completeness assumption on
\xi
.
The strengthened results (
a
) and (
b
) go beyond the K-contact case, but in particular
complete
the classification of the holonomy algebras
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
of K-contact sub-Riemannian manifolds:
they are exhausted by the holonomy algebras of Riemannian manifolds and their codimension-one ideals. Thus, we are left with the holonomy classification problem for contact sub-Riemannian manifolds with
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
. We solve this problem for the case of pseudo-Hermitian manifolds.

In Section
4
, we recall the definition and the main properties of
pseudo-Hermitian manifolds
. In particular, we show that in that case the adapted connection
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
coincides with the Tanaka–Webster connection, and
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
coincides with the Tanaka–Webster torsion. In Section
5
, we recall the definition of
contact sub-Riemannian symmetric spaces
, defined in
[
11
]
, and related notions. They are examples of pseudo-Hermitian manifolds. In
[
5
,
11
,
12
]
, simply connected contact sub-Riemannian symmetric spaces were classified and it was shown that they satisfy the dichotomy (
1
). In Section
6
, we study locally symmetric
contact sub-Riemannian spaces. We show that these spaces may be characterized as contact sub-Riemannian manifolds for which the Wagner connection
\nabla^{W}
is a Riemannian Ambrose–Singer connection, as in
[
2
]
or
[
25
]
, i.e., a homogeneous structure, which implies that the Riemannian metric
\theta^{2}+g
on
M
is locally homogeneous.

Section
7
contains the main result of the paper: Theorem
7.1
gives the classification of holonomy algebras of pseudo-Hermitian spaces with non-vanishing torsion.
For a pseudo-Hermitian manifold of dimension
2m+1\geq 7
with
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0

there are the following possibilities for the holonomy algebras
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}})
of the Tanaka–Webster connection and
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
of the Schouten connection:

1.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}})=\mathfrak{u}(m)
, and either
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{u}(m)
or
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)
;

2.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}})=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)
;

3.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}})=\mathfrak{so}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2)
and
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
;

4.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}})=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
.

Here
\mathfrak{so}(m)\subset\mathfrak{su}(m)
is the subalgebra preserving a Lagrangian subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{2m}
. Note that the classification of holonomy algebras of torsion-free sub-Riemannian contact structures, i.e. with
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
, was given in
[
16
]
and includes the torsion-free pseudo-Hermitian case. We review these results in Section
3.7
.

In Section
8
, we prove that in the above list, the holonomy

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
corresponds to locally sub-symmetric spaces. Moreover, for a pseudo-Hermitian manifold with
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
, the condition
\nabla{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
is the equivalent condition for the space to be locally sub-symmetric. Next, a pseudo-Hermitian manifold with
\nabla{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
is pseudo-Einstein if and only if
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)
. We give a construction of pseudo-Hermitian spaces with
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)
using a deformation of pseudoconvex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
.

In Section
9
we consider contact sub-Riemannian spaces
(M,\theta,g)
with
\nabla\mathrm{d}\theta=0
, which are a generalization of pseudo-Hermitian spaces. We prove that if
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
, then the condition
\nabla\mathrm{d}\theta=0
forces
(M,\theta,g)
to be pseudo-Hermitian, after multiplication of
g
by a constant.

In Section
10
, we deduce the classification of simply connected sub-symmetric spaces from the classification of the holonomy algebras. Our approach simplifies the proof from
[
5
,
11
,
12
]
, since we use the information about the holonomy algebra and the structure of the curvature tensor.

In Section
11
, we describe holonomy groups of simply connected pseudo-hermitian manifolds that admit parallel spinors.

2.
Preliminaries on sub-Riemannian contact structures

2.1.
The Schouten connection and its curvature

Let
(M,\theta,g)
a contact sub-Riemannian manifold,
i.e.,
\theta
is a
contact form on a smooth manifold
M
, and
g
is a sub-Riemannian metric on the contact distribution
{\mathcal{D}}=\ker\theta
. We assume that
\dim M=2m+1\geq 5
.

Denote by
\xi
the corresponding Reeb vector field on
M
defined by the conditions

\theta(\xi)=1,\quad\iota_{\xi}\mathrm{d}\theta=0.

These conditions imply
{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}\mathrm{d}\theta=0
and that
{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}=[\xi,\,.\,]
maps sections of
{\mathcal{D}}
to sections of
{\mathcal{D}}
.
In particular,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}g
is a well-defined section of
\odot^{2}{\mathcal{D}}
. We define the symmetric bilinear form
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
of
{\mathcal{D}}
by

(2)

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g)(X,Y),\quad\text{for all }X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}.

We denote by the same symbol
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
the corresponding symmetric endomorphism field, which is also called the
sub-torsion
, see
[
11
]
.

Denote by
\left<\xi\right>
the line bundle with fibres
\mathbb{R}\cdot\xi_{p}
,
the decomposition

TM={\mathcal{D}}\oplus\left<\xi\right>,

and the projections

(3)

\pi=\mathrm{Id}-\theta\otimes\xi:TM\longrightarrow{\mathcal{D}},\quad\pi^{\prime}=\theta\otimes\xi:TM\longrightarrow\left<\xi\right>.

The projection
\pi
satisfies

(4)

\left[\mathcal{L}_{\xi},\pi\right]=0,

because for all
X\in\Gamma(TM)
, we have

\left[\xi,\pi(X)\right]-\pi\left(\left[\xi,X\right]\right)=-\left(\mathrm{d}\theta(\xi,X)-X(\theta(\xi))\right)\xi=0,

as
\xi
is a Reeb vector field.

A horizontal connection
or
partial connection
on the distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
is a differential operator

(5)

\nabla:\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})\longrightarrow\Gamma({\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{D}})

satisfying the Leibniz rule. A partial connection extends to tensor products
\mathcal{{\mathcal{D}}}^{(r,s)}=\otimes^{r}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\otimes^{s}{\mathcal{D}}
of
{\mathcal{D}}
in a natural way,

\nabla:\Gamma(\mathcal{{\mathcal{D}}}^{(r,s)})\longrightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{{\mathcal{D}}}^{(r+1,s)}).

The
Schouten connection
is a partial connection that is the analogue of the Levi-Civita connection, i.e., it is the unique partial connection satisfying the conditions

(6)

\displaystyle\nabla g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(7)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X-\pi[X,Y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad\text{for all $X,Y\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})$.}

It is defined by the Koszul-type formula

(8)

2g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)=Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(Z,X)-Zg(X,Y)+g(\pi[X,Y],Z)-g(\pi[Y,Z],X)-g(\pi[X,Z],Y),

where
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})
.
The curvature tensor of the Schouten connection
\nabla
was defined by Schouten
in the following way,

(9)

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{\pi[X,Y]}Z-\pi\left([\pi^{\prime}([X,Y]),Z]\right),\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}.

It is straightforward to check that
R
is a section of
\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes(\mathrm{End}({\mathcal{D}}))
, but one can show more.
Using that
\pi^{\prime}([X,Y])=-\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)\xi
, we get

(10)

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{\pi[X,Y]}Z+\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)[\xi,Z],

so that with (
6
) we have that

(11)

g(R(X,Y)U,V)+g(R(X,Y)V,U)=-2\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y){\boldsymbol{\tau}}(U,V).

This shows that the Schouten curvature tensor
R
is in fact a section of
\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\left(\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})\oplus\langle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\rangle\right)
, where we define the vector bundle

\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})=\{A\in\mathrm{End}({\mathcal{D}})\mid g(AX,Y)+g(X,AY)=0\ \text{for all }X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}\}.

It also satisfies the Bianchi identity

(12)

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}R(X,Y)Z=0,\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}},

where
\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}
denotes the sum over cyclic permutations of
X,Y
and
Z
.

2.2.
Extended connections and the adapted connection

Let
\nabla
be the Schouten connection on
(M,\theta,g)
.
An
extended connection
is a connection on the vector bundle
{\mathcal{D}}\to M
,

\nabla^{\mathcal{D}}:\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})\longrightarrow\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes{\mathcal{D}})

such that

\nabla^{\mathcal{D}}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y,\quad\text{ for all $X,Y\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})$.}

An arbitrary extended connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{D}}
defines a unique field
N
of endomorphisms of
{\mathcal{D}}
by

N(X)=\nabla^{\mathcal{D}}_{\xi}X-[\xi,X].

This is the analogue of the well-known
Nomizu operator
.
Hence, for an endomorphism
N
of
{\mathcal{D}}
we denote by
\nabla^{N}
the extended connection
that is given by

(13)

\nabla^{N}_{\xi}X=[\xi,X]+N(X),\quad\nabla^{N}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y,\quad\text{for all }X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}},

and every extended connection is of this form.
The curvature tensor of the connection
\nabla^{N}
is given by

(14)

\displaystyle R^{N}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(X,Y)+\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)N,

(15)

\displaystyle R^{N}(\xi,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\nabla)_{X}-\nabla_{X}N,

and it holds that

(16)

R^{N}(\xi,X)Y-R^{N}(\xi,Y)X=-(\nabla_{X}N)Y+(\nabla_{Y}N)X,

where
X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}
and
R
is the Schouten curvature tensor. The first two identities follow from direct computation using (
3
) and (
10
), see also
[
15
]
.
The last equality follows from the Jacobi identity for vector fields and the identity (
4
).

It is straightforward to check that an extended connection
\nabla^{N}
is compatible with the sub-Riemannian metric
g
, i.e.,
\nabla^{N}g=0
, if and only if the symmetric part of
N
is equal to
\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}g
, i.e.,

(17)

g(N(X),Y)+g(X,N(Y))=(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g)(X,Y)=2{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y),\quad\text{for all }X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}.

This yields the definition of the

adapted connection
, which is defined as the extended connection
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
, i.e., the extended connection for which the Nomizu operator is given by

N={\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}g,

see, e.g.,
[
11
,
12
]
. It is compatible with the metric by definition.

The curvature tensor
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
of the adapted connection
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is fully determined by
R
,
\theta
and
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
via (
14
) and the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

The curvature tensor of the adapted connection
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
satisfies

g(R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,X)Y,Z)=g((\nabla_{Y}{\boldsymbol{\tau}})X,Z)-g((\nabla_{Z}{\boldsymbol{\tau}})X,Y),\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}.

In particular,

(18)

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\ g(R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,X)Y,Z)=0,\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}.

Proof.

For any extended connection
\nabla^{N}
that is compatible with
g
,
R^{N}(\xi,.)
is a section of
{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})\cong{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
,
and so is the tensor field defined by

P(X,Y,Z):=g(R^{N}(\xi,X)Y,Z)-g((\nabla_{Y}N)X,Z)+g((\nabla_{Z}N)X,Y).

Then from (
16
) we get that

P(X,Y,Z)-P(Y,X,Z)=(\nabla_{Z}N)(X,Y)-(\nabla_{Z}N)(Y,X).

For the adapted connection
N={\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is symmetric, so that

P
is in the kernel
of the skew symmetrisation in the first two components

{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})\to\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
. This however is an isomorphism, so that
P=0
and the statement follows.
∎

We summarize the algebraic properties of the curvature of the adapted connection. For this we define the vector bundle of algebraic curvature tensors

\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}))=\left\{Q\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})\mid\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}Q(X,Y)Z=0,\ \text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}\right\},

and the
weak curvature tensors
, see
[
20
]
,

\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}))=\left\{P\in{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})\mid\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}g(P(X)Y,Z)=0,\ \text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}\right\}.

Since every
Q\in\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{h})
satisfies pairwise symmetry,
g(Q(X,Y)Z,U)=g(Q(Z,U)X,Y)
, we have a
\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})
-invariant homomorphism that sends

R\in\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}))
and
X\in{\mathcal{D}}
to
R(X,.)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}))
.
Returning to the curvature
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
of the adapted connection, from Lemma
2.1
we get that

P^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:=R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})).

On the other hand, for

Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:=R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}|_{\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}}\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})

by (
14
) we only get the following Bianchi identity

(19)

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)Z=\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y){\boldsymbol{\tau}}(Z),\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}.

Hence, instead of pairwise symmetry we have

(20)

g(Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)U,V)-g(Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(U,V)X,Y)=\underset{U,V}{\Lambda}\left(\mathrm{d}\theta(Y,U){\boldsymbol{\tau}}(V,X)-\mathrm{d}\theta(X,U){\boldsymbol{\tau}}(V,Y)\right),

where
\underset{X,Y}{\Lambda}
denotes the skew symmetrisation of the tensor with respect to
X
and
Y
(without a normalizing factor of
1/2
).
Indeed, using abstract indices, we have

Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{[abc]d}+Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{[bcd]a}+Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{[cda]b}+Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{[dab]c}=\tfrac{2}{3}\left(Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{bdac}-Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{acbd}\right)

where the brackets denote skew symmetrisation (with normalizing factor).
On the other hand, by (
19
), we have that

Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{[abc]d}+Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{[bcd]a}+Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{[cda]b}+Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{[dab]c}=\tfrac{4}{3}\left(\mathrm{d}\theta_{a[b}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{d]c}-\mathrm{d}\theta_{c[b}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{d]a}\right),

so that

Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{abcd}-Q^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{cdab}=2\left(\mathrm{d}\theta_{b[c}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{d]a}-\mathrm{d}\theta_{a[c}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{d]b}\right),

which confirms formula (
20
).

2.3.
The Wagner connection

Before we introduce another extended connection, we recall some algebraic preliminaries.
For one-forms
\sigma,\mu\in{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
we define
\sigma\wedge\mu=\sigma\otimes\mu-\mu\otimes\sigma\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
, and
for vectors
X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}
, we define the bivector

X\wedge Y\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}
as

X\wedge Y=X\otimes Y-Y\otimes X.

For a 2-form

\omega
on
{\mathcal{D}}
with values in a vector bundle over
M
, we set

\omega(X\wedge Y)=2\omega(X,Y).

This allows us to consider the value
\omega(\alpha)
of the 2-form
\omega
on any bivector
\alpha\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}
.

Given a non-degenerate
2
-form
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
we can define the bi-vector
\omega^{-1}\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}
by the following convention. If
(e_{a})_{a=1}^{2m}
is a basis for
{\mathcal{D}}
at a point, we denote the dual basis of
{\mathcal{D}}^{*}
by
e^{a}
, so that
\omega=\omega_{ab}e^{a}\wedge e^{b}
, and define

\omega^{-1}=\hat{\omega}^{ab}e_{a}\wedge e_{b},\quad\text{ where $\omega_{ac}\hat{\omega}^{cb}=\delta_{a}{}^{b}$.}

This implies that

\omega(\omega^{-1})=\omega_{ab}\hat{\omega}^{ab}=-2\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{D}})=-4m.

In particular, we have

\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}=\ker(\omega)\oplus\langle\omega^{-1}\rangle.

With this notation, for any extended connection
\nabla^{N}
that is compatible with
g
, we can compare its curvature to the curvature tensor of the Schouten connection. From
(
14
), we obtain

(21)

\displaystyle R(\alpha)

\displaystyle=R^{N}(\alpha)\in\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}),\quad\text{ for all $\alpha\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}$ such that $\mathrm{d}\theta(\alpha)=0$},

(22)

\displaystyle R((\mathrm{d}\theta)^{-1})

\displaystyle=R^{N}((\mathrm{d}\theta)^{-1})+4mN.

Proposition 2.2
.

There is a unique extended connection
\nabla^{N}
that is compatible with
g
and whose curvature satisfies

(23)

\displaystyle R^{N}((\mathrm{d}\theta)^{-1})

\displaystyle=0.

This connection is called Wagner connection after
[
26
]
, and we denote it by
\nabla^{W}
.

Proof.

For the existence of this connection, set

(24)

N=N^{W}:=\tfrac{1}{4m}R((\mathrm{d}\theta)^{-1}),

and define the extended connection
\nabla^{W}:=\nabla^{N}
.
From (
11
) we get that

N(X,Y)+N(Y,X)=\tfrac{1}{4m}g\left(R((\mathrm{d}\theta)^{-1})X,Y)+g(R((\mathrm{d}\theta)^{-1})Y,X\right)=-\tfrac{1}{2m}\mathrm{d}\theta(\theta^{-1}){\boldsymbol{\tau}}=2{\boldsymbol{\tau}},

so that
\nabla^{W}
is compatible with the metric. From (
22
) we see that
(
23
) is satisfied. Since every extended connection is uniquely determined by
N
, the statement follows.
∎

Let
\nabla^{W}
be the Wagner connection defined by
N^{W}
as in (
24
) and let

N^{W}={\boldsymbol{\tau}}+C,

be the decomposition into the symmetric and skew-symmetric part, with

C=\tfrac{1}{4m}R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1})=\tfrac{1}{4m}R(\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1})-{\boldsymbol{\tau}}.

so that

\nabla^{W}_{\xi}X=\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\xi}(X)+C(X).

By (
14
) and (
15
), the curvature of the Wagner and adapted connections are related as

(25)

\displaystyle R^{W}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)+\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)C,

(26)

\displaystyle R^{W}(\xi,X)

\displaystyle=R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,X)-\nabla_{X}C,

where
X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}
.

Remark 2.3
.

To be precise, in
[
11
,
12
]
the authors work with affine connections, i.e., connections on
TM
instead of
{\mathcal{D}}
. Their affine connections

\overline{\nabla}^{N}
can be obtained from our extended connection
\nabla^{N}
by requiring
\overline{\nabla}^{N}\xi=0
. The torsion of this connection is then given as

T(X,Y)=\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}Y-\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{Y}X-[X,Y]=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)\xi,\quad T(\xi,X)=N(X).

This applies in particular to
N={\boldsymbol{\tau}}
, hence the name
sub-torsion
for
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
. It also applies to the Wagner connection, and we will come back to this in Section
6
. Note there is a strong interest to holonomy of connections with torsion, see the survey
[
1
]
.

Note that in general the Wagner connection is different from the
special connection

\tilde{\nabla}
in
[
12
, Section 2.2]
. The latter is defined by the following condition condition for its curvature
\tilde{R}
: if
\alpha=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}X_{i}\wedge Y_{i}
, where
(X_{i},Y_{i})
is a local orthonormal frame of
{\mathcal{D}}
such that
\mathrm{d}\theta(X_{i},Y_{j})=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}
, then
\tilde{R}(\alpha)=0
.

3.
Holonomy

In this section, let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold and
\nabla
be the Schouten connection. For extended connections we use the notation of the previous section.
A piece-wise smooth curve in
M
is called
horizontal
if it is tangent to
{\mathcal{D}}
.

3.1.
Holonomy of the Schouten connection

The Schouten connection
\nabla
defines a parallel transport along any horizontal curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
,

(27)

\tau_{\gamma}:{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma(a)}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma(b)}.

Let
x\in M
. The
(horizontal) holonomy group
of the Schouten connection
\nabla
at the point
x\in M
is the group of parallel transports along piece-wise smooth horizontal loops at the point
x\in M
. We denote this group by
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
. The holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is a Lie subgroup of
\operatorname{O}({\mathcal{D}}_{x},g_{x})
.

The
restricted holonomy group

\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla)
is the subgroup of

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
corresponding to contractable loops. The restricted holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}^{0}_{x}(\nabla)
is the identity component of
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
,
[
8
,
12
,
14
]
, and
the corresponding Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}_{x})
is called
the holonomy algebra
and is denoted by
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
. The parallel transport along a horizontal curve defines the isomorphism of the holonomy groups and algebras at the initial and the endpoint of the curve. Since
{\mathcal{D}}
is a contact distribution, the Chow–Rashevskii theorem ensures that any two points in
M
are connected by a horizontal curve, so the holonomy is independent of the point up to conjugation in
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
.

The following are versions of the Ambrose–Singer holonomy Theorem for the horizontal connection.
For this define

\ker(\mathrm{d}\theta):=\{\alpha\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}\mid\mathrm{d}\theta(\alpha)=0\},

the space of Legendrian bi-vectors.
First we have
[
12
, Corollary 2.4]
, see also
[
8
]
, the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is spanned by
the following endomorphisms of
{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
,

\tau_{\gamma}^{-1}\circ R(\alpha)\circ\tau_{\gamma},

where
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
is an arbitrary piece-wise smooth horizontal curve starting at
x
and
\alpha\in\ker(\mathrm{d}\theta)_{\gamma(b)}
.

We also have the following
[
8
, Theorem 2.16]
,
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold and let
\nabla^{N}
be an extension of the Schouten connection
\nabla
. Suppose that there is a
bi-vector field
\alpha\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}})
such that the function
\mathrm{d}\theta(\alpha)
is non-vanishing and it holds
R^{N}(\alpha)=0
. Then,
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)=\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})
.

Since
\alpha=\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1}
satisfies the assumption of this theorem and by the definition of the Wagner connection, we can conclude the following.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold. Then the horizontal holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)
coincides with the holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{W})
of the Wagner connection.

3.2.
The connection
\nabla^{\theta}

Let
(M,\theta)
be a contact manifold.
Using the 1-form
\theta
, we define the connection
\nabla^{\theta}
on the trivial line bundle
\left<\xi\right>
over
M
by setting

\nabla^{\theta}_{X}\xi=\theta(X)\xi,\quad X\in\Gamma(TM).

The curvature of the connection
\nabla^{\theta}
coincides with
\mathrm{d}\theta
, i.e.,

R^{\theta}(X,Y)\xi=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)\xi,\quad X,Y\in TM.

The parallel transport along a closed curve
\mu(t)
at a point
x
is given by

(28)

\xi_{x}\mapsto\exp\left(-\int_{\mu}\theta\right)\xi_{x},

see, e.g.,
[
16
]
.
The holonomy group
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\theta})
is isomorphic to the subgroup
\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset\operatorname{GL}(1,\mathbb{R})
.

3.3.
The group
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})

Let
\nabla^{N}
be a metric extended connection,
R^{N}
its curvature tensor, and
\tau^{N}_{\gamma}
the corresponding parallel transport along a piece-wise smooth curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
.
Denote by
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
the subgroup of the holonomy group
{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
of
\nabla^{N}
that consists of parallel transports for the connection
\nabla^{N}
along the loops in
M
that satisfy the condition

(29)

\int_{\mu}\theta=0.

It holds

{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla)\subseteq\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\subseteq{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\subseteq\operatorname{O}({\mathcal{D}}_{x},g_{x}).

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(M,g,\theta)
be a sub-Riemannian contact structure and let
\nabla^{N}
be a metric extended connection.
Then it holds that either
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})={\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
, or
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
is a co-dimension one normal Lie subgroup of
{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
.

The Lie algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
of the Lie group
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
is spanned by the endomorphisms

(\tau^{N}_{\gamma})^{-1}\circ R^{N}(\alpha)\circ\tau^{N}_{\gamma},\quad(\tau_{\gamma}^{N})^{-1}\circ R^{N}(\xi,X)\circ\tau^{N}_{\gamma},

where
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
is an arbitrary piece-wise smooth curve starting at
x
,
X\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma(b)}
and
\alpha\in\ker(\mathrm{d}\theta)_{\gamma(b)}
.

Proof.

Consider the affine connection
\widetilde{\nabla}^{N}:=\nabla^{N}\oplus\nabla^{\theta}
on the tangent bundle
TM=D\oplus\left<\xi\right>
defined by

\widetilde{\nabla}^{N}(Y+\xi)=\nabla^{N}Y+\nabla^{\theta}\xi,\qquad\text{ for }Y\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}}).

The holonomy group

{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})
of this connection consists of the pairs
(\tau^{N}_{\gamma},\tau^{\theta}_{\gamma})
, where
\gamma
is a loop at
x
, i.e.,
{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})
is contained in the product

{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})\ \subseteq\ {\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\times{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{\theta})={\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Consider the projection

\operatorname{pr}_{2}:{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},

and its restriction

\pi_{2}:=\operatorname{pr}_{2}|_{{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})}:{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},

which are Lie group homomorphisms. Moreover, we have Lie group homomorphism
\iota:\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})\ni A\to(A,1)\in\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with the exact sequence of Lie groups

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}\mathbf{1}&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{pr}_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathbb{R}_{+}&\longrightarrow&\mathbf{1}.\end{array}

By the definition of
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\subseteq\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
and (
28
), and by the fact that
\nabla^{\theta}
is not flat, it restricts to an exact sequence of groups

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}\mathbf{1}&\longrightarrow&\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\operatorname{Hol}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathbb{R}_{+}&\longrightarrow&\mathbf{1}.\end{array}

Hence,
\ker(\pi_{2})=\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\times\{1\}
is a normal Lie subgroup of
{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})
of co-dimension
1
.
Moreover it shows
that
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
is a
normal Lie subgroup of

{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
, of co-dimension
0
or
1
, depending on whether
\operatorname{Hol}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})=\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{N})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
or not.
See also
[
16
, Th. 5]
.

By the Ambrose–Singer Theorem, the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{hol}}_{x}(\widetilde{\nabla}^{N})
is spanned by the endomorphisms of the form

\big((\tau^{N}_{\gamma})^{-1}\circ R^{N}(\beta)\circ\tau^{N}_{\gamma},(\tau^{\theta}_{\gamma})^{-1}\circ R^{\theta}(\beta)\circ\tau^{\theta}_{\gamma}\big),

where
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
is an arbitrary piece-wise smooth curve starting at
\gamma(a)=x
, and
\beta\in\Lambda^{2}T_{\gamma(b)}M
. Since

\widetilde{\mathfrak{hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\cong\ker(\mathrm{d}\pi_{2}),

the above endomorphism belongs to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
if and only if
R^{\theta}(\beta)=0
, which means that
\mathrm{d}\theta(\beta)=0
, i.e.,
\beta=\xi\wedge X+\alpha
, where
X\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\gamma(b)}
,
\alpha\in\ker(\mathrm{d}\theta)_{\gamma(b)}
. This proves the last statement of the theorem. ∎

3.4.
The group
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
.

Let us assume that the Reeb vector field
\xi
is complete
and we denote its flow by
\varphi_{t}
. Fix a point
x\in M
. If the orbit
\varphi_{t}(x)
of the point
x
is cyclic, then we denote by
t_{x}
the smallest positive
t_{x}
such that
\varphi_{t_{x}}(x)=x
. Otherwise we assume that
t_{x}=0
.
Let

\mu:[a,b]\to M

be a piece-wise smooth curve. In
[
16
]
it is shown that the curve

(30)

\tilde{\mu}(t)=\varphi_{f(t)}(\mu(t)),\quad\text{
where}\quad f(t)=-\int_{a}^{t}\theta(\dot{\mu}(r))\mathrm{d}r,\quad t\in[a,b],

is horizontal and
\tilde{\mu}(a)=\mu(a)
. Moreover, if
\mu
is a loop at
x
, then
\tilde{\mu}
is a loop if and only if

(31)

\int_{\mu}\theta\in\mathbb{Z}t_{x}.

Let
\mu
be any curve starting at
x
and satisfying (
29
).
Let
\tilde{\mu}
be the corresponding horizontal curve defined in (
30
), where condition (
29
) ensures that the endpoints of
\mu
and
\tilde{\mu}
coincide, so that
\mu*\tilde{\mu}^{-1}
is a loop. Let
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
be the group generated by parallel transports along all loops of the form
\mu*\tilde{\mu}^{-1}
.
It is clear that
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\subseteq\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
is a subgroup.

Let
\mu:[a,b]\to M
be any curve starting at
x
and satisfying (
29
).
Consider the
s
-family
\mu_{s}
,
s\in[0,1]
, of the curves

(32)

t\longmapsto\mu_{s}(t)=\varphi_{sf(t)}(\mu(t)),

with
\mu_{0}=\mu
, and
\mu_{1}=\tilde{\mu}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For each
s\in[0,1]
, the curve
\mu_{s}
satisfies (
29
).

Proof.

It holds

\dot{\mu}_{s}(t)=s\dot{f}(t)\xi_{\varphi_{sf(t)}\mu(t)}+(d\varphi_{sf(t)})\dot{\mu}(t),

where

d\varphi_{sf(t)}:T_{\mu(t)}M\to T_{\varphi_{sf(t)}\mu(t)}M

is the differential of the diffeomorphism
\varphi_{sf(t)}
. Since
\varphi
preserves
\theta
, we get

\theta(\dot{\mu}_{s}(t))=s\dot{f}(t)+\theta((d\varphi_{sf(t)})\dot{\mu}(t))=s\dot{f}(st)+\theta(\dot{\mu}(t)).

Now,

\int_{\mu_{s}}\theta=s(f(b)-f(a))+\int_{\mu}\theta=0.

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(M,g,\theta)
be a sub-Riemannian contact structure with complete Reeb vector field and let
\nabla^{N}
be an extended connection.
The group
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
is a connected Lie subgroup of
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a curve satisfying (
29
) and let
\tau^{N}_{\mu\ast\tilde{\mu}^{-1}}
be an arbitrary element in
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
.
By Lemma
3.5
the curve

\tau_{s}^{N}:=\tau^{N}_{\mu_{s}*\tilde{\mu}_{s}^{-1}},

where
\mu_{s}
is defined in (
32
), is a curve in
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
and we claim that it joins

\tau^{N}_{\mu\ast\tilde{\mu}^{-1}}
with the identity. Indeed,
\mu_{0}*\tilde{\mu}_{0}^{-1}=\mu*\tilde{\mu}^{-1}
and, since
\tilde{\mu}
is horizontal so that
\tilde{\tilde{\mu}}=\tilde{\mu}
, we have

\mu_{1}*\tilde{\mu}_{1}^{-1}=\tilde{\mu}*\tilde{\mu}^{-1}.

Hence
\tau_{1}^{N}=\tau^{N}_{\tilde{\mu}*\tilde{\mu}^{-1}}=\operatorname{id}_{{\mathcal{D}}_{x}}
, so that

\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\subseteq\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
is a connected subgroup, which implies that it is a Lie subgroup. ∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(M,g,\theta)
be a sub-Riemannian contact structure with complete Reeb vector field and let
\nabla^{N}
be a metric extended connection.
The Lie group
\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
is generated by the subgroups
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
and
\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
, i.e.

\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})=\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})\cdot\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla).

Moreover,

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)
normalizes
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
.

Proof.

If a loop
\gamma
satisfies (
29
), then

\tau^{N}_{\gamma}=\tau^{N}_{\gamma*\tilde{\gamma}^{-1}}\circ\tau^{N}_{\tilde{\gamma}},

which implies the first statement.

To show the normalizing condition, let
\mu
be a curve starting at
x
and satisfying (
29
), and
\gamma
is a horizontal loop at
x
. Then

(\tau^{N}_{\gamma})^{-1}\circ\tau^{N}_{\mu*\tilde{\mu}^{-1}}\circ\tau^{N}_{\gamma}=(\tau^{N}_{\gamma})^{-1}\circ\tau^{N}_{\mu}\circ(\tau^{N}_{\gamma})\circ(\tau^{N}_{\gamma})^{-1}\circ(\tau^{N}_{\tilde{\mu}})^{-1}\circ\tau^{N}_{\gamma}=\tau^{N}_{\gamma*\mu*\gamma^{-1}}\circ(\tau^{N}_{\gamma*\tilde{\mu}*\gamma^{-1}})^{-1}.

Since
\gamma
is horizontal, it holds
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma
, and

\widetilde{\gamma*\mu*\gamma^{-1}}=\tilde{\gamma}*\tilde{\mu}*\tilde{\gamma}^{-1}=\gamma*\tilde{\mu}*\gamma^{-1}.

This proves the proposition. ∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(M,g,\theta)
be a sub-Riemannian contact structure with complete Reeb vector field and let
\nabla^{N}
be a metric extended connection.
If
R^{N}(\xi,\cdot)=0
, then the group
\widehat{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{N})
is trivial.

Proof.

Let
\mu:[a,b]\to M
be a curve satisfying (
29
) and with
\mu(a)=x
. Let
W=[0,1]\times[a,b]
. Consider the map

F:W\to M,

F(s,t)=\varphi_{sf(t)}(\mu(t)).

Consider the pull-back bundle
F^{*}{\mathcal{D}}\to W
and the induced connection
\nabla^{F}
along the map
F
. It holds

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}F(s,t)=f(t)\cdot\xi_{F(s,t)}.

This implies that the curvature of the induced connect
\nabla^{F}
is determined by

R^{N}(\xi,\cdot)
, which by assumption vanishes, so that the induced connection is flat and the parallel transport along the curve
\mu*\tilde{\mu}^{-1}
is trivial. ∎

3.5.
A Codazzi condition

We will say that a
a sub-Riemannian contact structure is
Codazzi
if the sub-torsion
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
satisfies the Codazzi equation with respect to the Schouten connection, i.e.,

(\nabla_{X}{\boldsymbol{\tau}})Y=(\nabla_{Y}{\boldsymbol{\tau}})X,\quad\text{ for all $X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}$.}

From Lemma
2.1
we have the following.

Corollary 3.9
.

A a contact sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,\theta,g)
is Codazzi if and only if the curvature tensor
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
of the adapted connection
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
satisfies

R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,\cdot)=0
.

From Propositions
3.7
and
3.8
we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold that is Codazzi and such that the Reeb vector field
\xi
is complete. Then it holds

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\widetilde{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
.

This and Theorem
3.4
imply the following.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold that is Codazzi and such that the Reeb vector field
\xi
is complete. Then either
{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla)={\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
, or
{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla)
is a co-dimension one normal subgroup of
{\operatorname{Hol}}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
.

For the final result of this section we introduce the notion of (weak) curvature tensors.
For a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(l)
, the space
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{h})
of
weak curvature tensors
, as defined in
[
20
]
, consists of linear maps from
\mathbb{R}^{l}
to
\mathfrak{h}
satisfying the identity

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\langle P(X)Y,Z\rangle=0,\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{l}.

Let
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{h})

denote the space of algebraic curvature tensors, i.e., of elements in
\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{l})^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{h}
that satisfy Bianchi-identity,

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}R(X,Y)Z=0,\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{l}.

Since every
R\in\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{h})
satisfies pairwise symmetry,
\langle R(X,Y)Z,U\rangle=\langle R(Z,U)X,Y\rangle
, for
any
R\in\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{h})
and
X\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
, we have
\mathcal{R}(X,.)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{h})
.
From Lemma
2.1
it follows that
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}))
, however

R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}|_{\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}}
does not necessaily satisfy the Bianchi identity and hence is not necessarily in
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{h})
. Nevertheless, we get the following.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold that is not Codazzi. Suppose that the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(2m)
of the adapted connection is irreducible. Then either

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(2m)
is the holonomy algebra of a Riemannian manifold or

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{sp}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1),\quad m=2k.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.1
and the assumption that the sub-Riemannian structure is not Codazzi we have
0\not=R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}))
. Let
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
be the subalgebra generated by images of elements from
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}))
. The main result form
[
20
]
states that
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(2m)
is the holonomy algebra of a Riemannian manifold. This implies that
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{h})\neq 0
and hence
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}))\neq 0
. Then the statement of the proposition follows from
[
9
, Theorem 4.6]
. ∎

From the proof we see that the result also holds when the assumption of non-Codazzi is replaced by the assumption
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}))\not=0
. Then it follows directly from
[
9
, Theorem 4.6]
.

3.6.
A further analysis of the Codazzi case

In this section we generalize Corollary
3.11
relaxing the completeness assumption for
\xi
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian manifold. Suppose that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is Codazzi. Then one of following conditions holds:

1.

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
;

2.

\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subset\operatorname{Hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
is a normal subgroup of co-dimension one.

Proof.

Since the subbundle
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\subseteq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
is
\nabla
-parallel,
\nabla
preserves the decomposition

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\oplus\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)^{\bot}.

Let
C
be the skew part of
N^{W}
for the Wagner connection
\nabla^{W}
. Since
C=\tfrac{1}{4m}R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1})
, we have that
C\in\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
. Let

C=C^{\nabla}+C^{\bot}

be the corresponding decomposition of the skew-symmetric part
C
of
N^{W}
.
For the following recall that from Theorem
3.3
we have that

(33)

\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla^{W})=\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla).

Lemma 3.14
.

It holds that

\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}C^{\bot}=0,

and

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\oplus\left<C^{\bot}\right>

is the direct sum of ideals.

Proof.

Since
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is Codazzi, it holds
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,,X)=0
, so that

R^{W}(\xi,X)=-\nabla_{X}C=-\nabla_{X}C^{\nabla}-\nabla_{X}C^{\bot},\quad\text{for all }X\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}}).

Since
R^{W}(\xi,X)
takes values in
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
, it holds

\nabla C^{\bot}=0.

This implies that
C^{\perp}
commutes with
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)
, and with (
33
) yields
\nabla^{W}C^{\bot}=0
.
We conclude that

\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\xi}C^{\bot}=(\nabla^{W}_{\xi}-C^{\nabla}-C^{\bot})\cdot C^{\bot}=0.

Next, since

\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\xi}=\nabla^{W}_{\xi}-C^{\nabla}-C^{\bot},

4.
Pseudo-Hermitian structures

A prominent class of contact sub-Riemannian manifolds is provided by pseudo-Hermitian structures, which
emerge naturally in the study of CR geometry. We explain this background briefly and
relate common notions to our setting of extended Schouten connections. For detailed explanations on CR and
pseudo-Hermitian geometry we refer to
[
23
,
10
]
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold of odd dimension
n=2m+1\geq 3
. A CR structure on
M
can be defined as a pair
({\mathcal{D}},J)
consisting
of a contact distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
and a complex structure
J:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
, subject to the condition

[X,JY]+[JX,Y]\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})

for any
sections
X,Y
of
{\mathcal{D}}
, and the vanishing of the Nijenhuis tensor

\mathcal{N}(X,Y)=[X,Y]-[JX,JY]+J([X,JY]+[JX,Y])=0

on
{\mathcal{D}}
.
The first condition implies
that any contact form
\theta
for
{\mathcal{D}}
gives rise
to a metric

(40)

g=\mathrm{d}\theta(\cdot,J\cdot)

on
{\mathcal{D}}
. For example, the smooth boundary

M=\partial U
of any strictly pseudoconvex domain
U
in complex space
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}

inherits naturally a CR structure, and any defining function for the boundary
M=\partial U
gives
rise to a contact form
\theta
such that
g
given by (
40
) is a metric of Riemannian signature on
{\mathcal{D}}
.

In the following, we assume
M
to be equipped with a strictly pseudoconvex CR structure
({\mathcal{D}},J)
and some adapted contact form
\theta

such that
g
is positive definite. In this case we call
(M,{\mathcal{D}},J,\theta)
an (abstract) CR manifold with pseudo-Hermitian structure,
or simply a pseudo-Hermitian manifold.
It was discovered by Tanaka
[
23
]
and Webster
[
28
]
that such a triple
(M,{\mathcal{D}},J)
admits for any choice of
\theta
a
uniquely determined linear connection. Independent of the construction procedures, this gives rise to the so-called
Tanaka–Webster connection
\nabla^{TW}
, which is known to be characterized by the following two conditions:

\nabla^{TW}
satisfies the Koszul formula (
8
)
for all sections
X,Y,Z
of the contact distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
, and for the Reeb vector

\xi
of
\theta
and any section
X
in
{\mathcal{D}}
, we have

\nabla_{\xi}^{TW}X=\frac{1}{2}\left([\xi,X]-J[\xi,JX]\right)\ .

These conditions define
\nabla^{TW}
uniquely, no matter whether
the Nijenhuis tensor
\mathcal{N}
is assumed to vanish or not.
In fact, we have

g((\nabla^{TW}_{X}J)(Y),Z)=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\theta(X,\mathcal{N}(Y,Z))

for
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})
(cf.
[
24
]
), which shows that

J
is
\nabla
-parallel if and only if
\mathcal{N}

vanishes.
In this case
\mathrm{d}\theta
is
\nabla
-parallel as well.

By construction,
\nabla^{TW}
coincides with the Schouten connection for derivatives in
{\mathcal{D}}
-direction.
Hence,
\nabla^{TW}
is an extension, and the endomorphism
N
is given by

NX=-\frac{1}{2}\left([\xi,X]+J[\xi,JX]\right),

which is usally called Webster torsion, denoted by
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The Tanaka–Webster connection
\nabla^{TW}
coincides with the adapted connection
\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi g(Y,X)-g([\xi,Y],X)-g(Y,[\xi,X])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\mathrm{d}\theta(Y,JX)-d\theta([\xi,Y],JX)-d\theta(Y,J[\xi,X])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathrm{d}\theta)(Y,JX)+d\theta(Y,[\xi,JX])-d\theta(Y,J[\xi,X])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(JY,[\xi,JX])-g(Y,[\xi,X])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2g({\boldsymbol{\tau}}X,Y)

for all
X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}
. Hence, the connection
\nabla^{TW}
and
N={\boldsymbol{\tau}}
satisfy (
2
). ∎

The Ricci tensor of the curvature tensor
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
of the Tanaka–Webster connection
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
satisfies, for
X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}
, the identity

(41)

\operatorname{Ric}^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(X,Y)=\rho(X,JY)+(m-1){\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,JY),

where
\rho
is the pseudo-Hermitian Ricci-form given by

(42)

\rho(X,Y)\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}_{g}g(R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(J\cdot,\cdot)X,Y)\ .

Note that

\rho(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}_{g}g(R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y,J\cdot,\cdot),

by the first Bianchi identity.
The trace of
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}
on
{\mathcal{D}}
is the Webster scalar curvature
scal^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}
.

In case that

\rho=h\mathrm{d}\theta

for some function
h
on
M
,
the pseudo-Hermitian form
\theta
is called pseudo-Einstein structure on
M
. It follows

h=\frac{1}{2m}scal^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}},

which
needs not to be constant.
In fact, we have

R^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(\xi,\cdot,Y,X)=(\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}_{Y}{\boldsymbol{\tau}})(X)-(\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}_{X}{\boldsymbol{\tau}})(Y)

for
X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}
, and
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(\xi)=\mathrm{tr}_{g}\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.
The second Bianchi identity implies, for pseudo-Einstein spaces,

\operatorname{Ric}^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(\xi,JX)=\frac{1}{2m}X(scal^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}})

for
X\in{\mathcal{D}}
.

In
[
21
]
it was pointed out that a vector field
Z
tangent to
{\mathcal{D}}
, which is
\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}
-parallel in all
{\mathcal{D}}
-directions, needs not to be
covariantly constant in
\xi
-direction. In fact, for such
Z
, we have

\nabla^{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}_{\xi}Z=-\frac{1}{m}\rho(Z).

Hence, if the Tanaka–Webster
connection is adjusted in
\xi
-direction by

\hat{\nabla}_{\xi}Z:=\nabla^{TW}_{\xi}Z+\frac{1}{m}\rho(Z),

then
Z
is a parallel vector field
(in all directions) on
M
. Obviously, this adjustment
\hat{\nabla}
is
the extension of the Schouten connection with

N={\boldsymbol{\tau}}+\frac{1}{m}\rho.

The connection
\hat{\nabla}
was called basic in
[
21
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The basic connection
\hat{\nabla}
coincides with the Wagner connection
\nabla^{W}
.

Proof.

We compute the endomorphism
N^{W}
, which determines the Wagner connection.
Note that
\mathrm{d}\theta(\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1})=-4m
. And, for the bivector
J=e_{1}\wedge Je_{1}+\cdots+e_{m}\wedge Je_{m}
,
where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{m})
is some unitary basis of
({\mathcal{D}},g,J)
, we have
\mathrm{d}\theta(J)=2m
. Then, with (
22
), (
24
) and (
42
)
we obtain

\displaystyle N^{W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4m}R(\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1})\ =\ {\boldsymbol{\tau}}+\tfrac{1}{4m}R^{TW}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}-\tfrac{1}{2m}R^{TW}(J)\ =\ {\boldsymbol{\tau}}+\tfrac{1}{m}\rho\ ,

the same expression as for the basic connection. ∎

5.
Sub-Riemannian symmetric spaces

In this section we will review results from

[
5
,
11
,
12
]
about sub-Riemannian symmetric spaces and their holonomy. We will provide more details about their classification in Section
10
.

5.1.
Sub-symmetric spaces and quadruples

An
isometry
between sub-Riemannian contact manifold
(M,\theta,g)
and
(\hat{M},\hat{\theta},\hat{g})
is a diffeomorphism
\phi:M\to\hat{M}
such that

\phi^{*}\hat{\theta}=\theta,\quad\phi^{*}\hat{g}=g.

This condition implies for the Reeb-vector field and for the sub-torsion that

\phi_{*}\xi=\hat{\xi},\qquad\phi^{*}\hat{\boldsymbol{\tau}}={\boldsymbol{\tau}}.

The isometries of
(M,\theta,g)
are denoted by
\mathrm{Iso}(M,\theta,g)
, and if this group acts transitively on
M
we call
(M,\theta,g)

homogeneous
. Given a point
o\in M
, a
sub-symmetry (at
o
)
is an isometry
\psi
such that

\psi(o)=o,\qquad\mathrm{d}\psi|_{{\mathcal{D}}_{o}}=-\mathrm{Id}.

A
sub-Riemannian symmetric space
is a homogeneous sub-Riemannian contact manifold such that every point in
M
admits a
sub-symmetry
. A
locally sub-Riemannian symmetric space
is a sub-Riemannian contact manifold
(M,\theta,g)
such that every point admits a
local sub-symmetry

\psi:U\to\psi(U)
(homogeneity is not required).

Now let
(M,\theta,g)
be a sub-Riemannian contact manifold with sub-torsion
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
. Let
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
be the adapted affine connection, i.e., the unique affine connection that satisfies
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}g=0
,
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\xi=0
and with torsion

T(X,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)\xi,\quad T(\xi,X)={\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X).

Let
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
be the curvature of
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.
In analogy to the Riemannian situation in
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
it is shown that
(M,\theta,g)
is locally sub-symmetric if and only if

\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}T=0,\quad\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0,\quad\text{ for all $X\in{\mathcal{D}}$.}

The other important result in
[
11
]
, which is also in analogy with the Riemannian case, is the purely algebraic description of sub-Riemannian symmetric spaces. This requires the following setting, which in

[
11
, Section 3]
is called an
sub-orthogonal involutive Lie algebra
(or
sub-OIL
, for short). We prefer the name sub-Riemannian symmetric quadrupel, in analogy to symmetric triples of Riemannian symmetric spaces.
A
sub-symmetric quadrupel

(\mathfrak{g},s,\mathfrak{k},B)
consists of

(1)

a real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and an involution
s
of
\mathfrak{g}
,

(2)

so that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
, where
\mathfrak{h}=\ker(s-\mathrm{Id})
and
\mathfrak{p}=\ker(s+\mathrm{Id})
, is the decomposition into the eigenspaces of
s
,

(3)

a subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
of co-dimension one that does not contain a non trivial ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
,

(4)

and an
\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{k}}
-invariant inner product
B
on
\mathfrak{p}
,

(5)

such that bilinear form
\Theta:\mathfrak{p}\times\mathfrak{p}\ni(X,Y)\mapsto[X,Y]\mod\mathfrak{k}\in\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k}
is nontrivial.

A sub-symmetric quadruple, for which
B
is not only invariant under
\mathfrak{k}
but also under
\mathfrak{h}
, is called
sub-torsion-free
.
For a sub-symmetric quadruple we have that
\mathfrak{h}
is a subalgebra and that

[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{p}]\subseteq\mathfrak{p},\quad[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h},\quad\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{k}+[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}].

Since
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{p},B)
and
B
positive definite,
\mathfrak{k}
is a compact Lie algebra, so that there is a
\mathfrak{k}
-invariant complement
\mathfrak{m}
to
\mathfrak{k}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
. Moreover, this implies that
that
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\langle Z\rangle
, where
Z
centralizes
\mathfrak{k}
. As a consequence
\Theta
is
\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}
-invariant.

For two sub-symmetric quadruples
Q=(\mathfrak{g},s,\mathfrak{k},B)
and
\hat{Q}=(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{s},\hat{\mathfrak{k}},\hat{B})
we say that
that
Q
restricts to
\hat{Q}

if
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
are subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{k}
,
\hat{s}
is the restriction of
s
, and
\mathfrak{p}=\hat{\mathfrak{p}}
and
B=\hat{B}
.
We call a sub-symmetric quadruple a
sub-symmetric transvection quadruple
if
[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]=\mathfrak{h}
.
Given the sub-symmetric quadruple
(\mathfrak{g},s,\mathfrak{k},B)
one can can always restrict it to a transvection quadruple by setting
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}=[\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}]
and
\hat{\mathfrak{k}}=\mathfrak{k}\cap\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
and the restricted quadruple
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\mathfrak{p},\hat{k},s,B)
.

To every simply connected sub-Riemannian symmetric space
(M,\theta,g)
one can
associate
a sub-symmetric quadruple in the following way,
[
11
, Proposition 3.1]
. Let

6.
Locally sub-symmetric spaces and locally homogeneous structures

In this section we consider locally sub-symmetric spaces.
Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact
sub-Riemannian space with sub-torsion
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
, Reeb vector field
\xi
, and adapted connection
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
on
{\mathcal{D}}
with curvature
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
which is a section of
\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})
. In
[
11
]
the affine connection
\overline{\nabla}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is considered that extends the adapted connection to sections of
TM
by setting
\overline{\nabla}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\xi=0
. It satisfies
\overline{\nabla}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}g=0
and its torsion tensor is given by

T(X,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)\xi,\qquad T(\xi,X)={\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X),

for
X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Its curvature tensor
\overline{R}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
satisfies
\overline{R}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)\xi=\overline{R}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\xi,X)\xi=0
and otherwise is equal to
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.
Then, as in
[
11
]
,
(M,\theta,g)
is
locally sub-symmetric
if and only if

(45)

\overline{\nabla}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}T=0,\qquad\overline{\nabla}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}\overline{R}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0,\quad\text{ for all }X\in{\mathcal{D}}.

Using the Schouten partial connection
\nabla
on all tensor bundles of
{\mathcal{D}}
, these equations are equivalent to

(46)

\nabla{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0,\qquad\nabla\mathrm{d}\theta=0,\qquad\nabla R=0,

where
R
is the curvature tensor of the Schouten partial connection. For any extended connection
\nabla^{N}
, the connection induced on sections of tensor bundles of
{\mathcal{D}}
, is given by
\nabla^{N}={\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}+N\cdot
, where the dot denotes the natural action of an endomorphism on tensors.
Equations (
46
) imply for any extended connection
\nabla^{N}
that

(47)

\nabla^{N}_{X}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0,\qquad\nabla^{N}_{X}\mathrm{d}\theta=0,\qquad\nabla^{N}_{X}R=0,\quad\text{for all }X\in{\mathcal{D}},

and in particular for the adapted connection that

(48)

\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(Y,Z)=0,\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}

It also applies to the Wagner connection
\nabla^{W}
with
\nabla^{W}_{\xi}X=\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\xi}X+C(X)
, where

C=\tfrac{1}{4m}R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1})\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})),

and which satisfies (
23
). Since
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}\mathrm{d}\theta=0
, we also have that
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1}=0
, and together with (
48
) this yields
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{X}C=0
, and consequently

(49)

\nabla^{W}_{X}C=0,\quad\text{for all }X\in{\mathcal{D}}.

Hence, using (
14
), for the Wagner connection of a locally symmetric sub-Riemannian space we have

(50)

\nabla^{W}_{X}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0,\qquad\nabla^{W}_{X}C=0,\qquad\nabla^{W}_{X}\mathrm{d}\theta=0,\qquad\nabla^{W}_{X}R^{W}=0,\quad\text{for all }X\in{\mathcal{D}}.

Setting
N^{W}:={\boldsymbol{\tau}}+C
, differentiating these equations and skewing yields
for any

X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}

that

[R^{W}(X,Y),N^{W}]=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)\nabla^{W}_{\xi}N^{W},\qquad R^{W}(X,Y)\cdot\mathrm{d}\theta=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)\nabla^{W}_{\xi}\mathrm{d}\theta

and

(R^{W}(X,Y)\cdot R^{W})(U,V)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)(\nabla^{W}_{\xi}R^{W})(U,V),

where
U,V\in{\mathcal{D}}
and the dot denotes the natural action of endomorphism on the corresponding tensors.
When substituting
\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1}
for
X,Y
in these equations, by the defining equation (
23
) for the Wagner connection, the left-hand-side vanishes, whereas
\mathrm{d}\theta(\mathrm{d}\theta^{-1})=-4m
, so that

(51)

\nabla^{W}_{\xi}N^{W}=0,\qquad\nabla^{W}_{\xi}\mathrm{d}\theta=0,\qquad\nabla^{W}_{\xi}R^{W}(X,Y)=0,

and hence

(52)

[R^{W}(X,Y),N^{W}]=0,\qquad R^{W}(X,Y)\cdot\mathrm{d}\theta=0,\qquad(R^{W}(X,Y)\cdot R^{W})(U,V)=0,

for all
X,Y,U,V\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
It also implies that

(53)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}N^{W}=0,\qquad{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}\mathrm{d}\theta+N^{W}\cdot\mathrm{d}\theta=0,\qquad{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}R^{W}+N^{W}\cdot R^{W}=0.

Using Cartan’s formula, we get

{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}\mathrm{d}\theta=\iota_{\xi}\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta+\mathrm{d}\iota_{\xi}\mathrm{d}\theta=0
, so that

(54)

N^{W}\cdot\mathrm{d}\theta=0.

Moreover, we consider the affine connection
\overline{\nabla}
defined by the Wagner connection and the condition
\overline{\nabla}\xi=0
. Its torsion is given by

T^{W}(X,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)\xi,\qquad T^{W}(\xi,X)=N^{W}(X).

From the above, we have that the torsion tensor
T^{W}
is parallel for
\overline{\nabla}
.
By Lemma
2.1
and equation (
15
) for the adapted connection, we get that
{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}\nabla=0
and hence, from the same equation and (
50
) we get that
R^{W}(\xi,X)=0
. This implies that also the curvature of
\overline{\nabla}
is
\overline{\nabla}
-parallel. Moreover,
the second Bianchi identity for
\overline{\nabla}
then gives

(\overline{\nabla}_{\xi}R^{W})(X,Y)=R^{W}(N^{W}X,Y)+R^{W}(X,N^{W}Y),

which vanishes by the third equation in (
51
), so that

(55)

R^{W}(N^{W}X,Y)+R^{W}(X,N^{W}Y)=0\qquad N^{W}\cdot R^{W}(X,Y)=0,

where the second equation holds because of the first and the first in (
52
).
With the help of these equations one can show that a locally symmetric sub-Riemannian space defines a a sub-symmetric quadruple. The process follows the construction of a Lie triple system from a locally symmetric Riemannian space.

Theorem 6.1
.

Any locally symmetric, contact sub-Riemannian space
(M,\theta,g)
defines a sub-symmetric quadruple
(\mathfrak{g},s,\mathfrak{k},g_{x})
and hence a sub-Riemannian symmetric space
G/K
.

Moreover,
\mathfrak{k}
is the horizontal holonomy algebra and
(M,\theta,g)
is locally isometric to
G/K
.

Proof.

We fix a point
x\in M
and define the vector spaces

\mathfrak{p}:={\mathcal{D}}_{x},\quad\mathfrak{k}:=\mathrm{span}\{R^{W}(X,Y)\mid X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}_{x}\},\quad\mathfrak{h}:=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\langle\xi_{x}\rangle,

so that
T_{x}M=\mathfrak{p}\oplus\langle\xi_{x}\rangle
and
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{p})
.
We set
\Theta(X,Y):=\mathrm{d}\theta_{x}(X,Y)

and
define a bracket on
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{p}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
as follows, where we omit the point
x
at the tensors and abuse notation by using the same bracket symbol as above for the commutator of vector fields and endomorphisms. For
W={\boldsymbol{\tau}}+C
and
R^{W}
the curvature tensor of the Wagner connection,
X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}
and
A,B\in\mathfrak{k}
, we define

(56)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left[X,Y\right]&=&-R^{W}(X,Y)+\Theta(X,Y)\xi,\\
\left[\xi,X\right]&=&N^{W}(X)\ =\ -\left[X,\xi\right],\\
\left[\xi,A\right]&=&0\ =\ [A,\xi],\\
\left[A,X\right]&=&AX\ =\ -[X,A],\\
\left[A,B\right]&=&AB-BA.\end{array}

We need to check that this satisfies the Jacobi identity.
Since
[[X,Y],Z]=-R(X,Y)Z)
, the Jacobi identity for
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{p}
is satisfied because of (
14
). If two of the arguments are from
\mathfrak{k}
, the Jacobi identity holds trivially.
For the remaining cases we have for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}
that

\displaystyle[[X,Y],\xi]+[[Y,\xi],X]+[[\xi,X],Y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[X,N^{W}Y]+[Y,N^{W}X]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-R^{W}(N^{W}X,Y)-R^{W}(X,N^{W}Y)+(N^{W}\cdot\mathrm{d}\theta)(X,Y)\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

because of (
54
) and (
55
).
For

A\in\mathfrak{k}
it is

[[X,Y],A]+[[Y,A],X]+[[A,X],Y]=A\cdot R^{W}(X,Y)-A\cdot\Theta(X,Y)=0,

and

[[X,\xi],A]+[[\xi,A],X]+[[A,X],\xi]=[A,N^{W}]=0

by (
52
).
Hence,
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra with an involution
s
defined by
s|_{\mathfrak{h}}=\operatorname{id}
and
s|_{\mathfrak{p}}=-\operatorname{id}
, a co-dimension one subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
, with
g_{x}
as a
\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{k}}
invariant inner product, i.e., satisfying the conditions for a sub-symmetric quadruple
(\mathfrak{g},s,\mathfrak{k},g_{x})
.

Moreover, we have that
\nabla^{W}R^{W}=0
so that
\mathfrak{k}
is the holonomy algebra of
\nabla^{W}
and therefore, by Theorem
3.3
equal to the horizontal holonomy, i.e., to the holonomy algebra of the Schouten partial connection.

By taking
G
to be the unique simply connected group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
K
to be the unique connected subgroup corresponding to the subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
, we obtain a sub-symmetric space
G/K
.

By construction we have a linear isometry between
T_{x}M
and
T_{[e]}(G/H)
, and we need to show, similarly to the Riemannian situation, that it extends to a local isometry.
Above in (
51
) we have in seen that the affine connection
\overline{\nabla}
defined by the Wagner connection and the condition
\overline{\nabla}\xi=0
has parallel torsion and curvature. Moreover, the metric defined as

\overline{g}=\theta^{2}+g

is parallel for
\overline{\nabla}
. Hence,
\overline{\nabla}
is an Ambrose–Singer connection for the Riemannian manifold
(M,\overline{g})
, which, as a consequence, is a locally homogeneous Riemannian manifold,
[
2
]
, see also

[
25
]
. As such it is locally isometric to a Riemannian homogeneous space that is given by
G/K
, since its construction follows (
56
).
Hence, the locally sub-symmetric space is locally isometric to the globally sub-Riemannian space
G/K
.
∎

In the proof we have used the following observation, which follows from (
50
) and (
51
), and which is worth to be stated separately.

Corollary 6.2
.

A contact sub-Riemannian space
(M,\theta,g)
is locally symmetric if and only if
the Wagner connection defines an Ambrose–Singer connection (i.e., a homogeneous structure) for the Riemannian metric
\theta^{2}+g
on
M
and with
\xi
is parallel.

7.
Classification of pseudo-Hermitian holonomy

In this section we will provide a classification of the holonomy algebras of the Schouten and the Tanaka–Webster connections of a pseudo-Hermitian manifold. The K-contact case, i.e., when
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
, is fully described in Section
3.7
above, so in Theorem
7.1
below we will focus on the case when
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\not=0
.

Let
(M,J,\theta)
be a pseudo-Hermitian manifold of dimension
2m+1\geq 7
.
The sub-Riemannian metric is
g(X,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,JY)
.
We denote by
\mathfrak{u}({\mathcal{D}})
the
vector bundle with fibres being the centralizer
\mathfrak{u}({\mathcal{D}}_{x})\cong\mathfrak{u}(m)
of
J_{x}
in
\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}_{x})
.
Let
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\nabla^{TW}
be the Tanaka–Webster connection. Recall that
\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}J=\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\mathrm{d}\theta=0
, so that
R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is a section of
\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{u}({\mathcal{D}})
and
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(m)
.

Let
E_{x}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
be a Lagrangian subspace, i.e., a maximal vector subspace such that the restriction of
\mathrm{d}\theta
to
E_{x}
is zero,

\mathrm{d}\theta_{x}|_{E_{x}\wedge E_{x}}=0.

We obtain the
g
-orthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{D}}_{x}=E_{x}\oplus JE_{x}.

The maximal subalgebra of

\mathfrak{u}({\mathcal{D}}_{x})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
-B&A\end{pmatrix}\mid A\in\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}_{x}),B\in\odot^{2}{\mathcal{D}}_{x}\right\},

preserving this decomposition is of the form

\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}A&0\\
0&A\end{matrix}\right)\mid A\in\mathfrak{so}(E_{x})\right\},

where the matrices are written with respect to an orthonormal basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{m},Je_{1},\dots,Je_{m}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
with
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\in E_{x}
.
This defines the embedding
\mathfrak{so}(E_{x})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{su}({\mathcal{D}}_{x})
, and we will denote by
\mathfrak{so}(E_{x})
the image of this embedding. If the subspace
E_{x}
is not specified, then we denote such subalgebra by
\mathfrak{so}(m)
. We denote the 1-dimensional subalgebra
\mathbb{R}J\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(m)
by
\mathfrak{u}(1)
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(M,J,\theta)
be a pseudo-Hermitian manifold of dimension
2m+1\geq 7
. Suppose that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
. Then there are the following possibilities for the holonomy algebras
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
of the Tanaka–Webster connection and
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
of the Schouten connection:

1.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{u}(m)
, and either
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{u}(m)
or
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)
;

2.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)
;

3.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{so}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)
and
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
;

4.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
.

In particular, either
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
or
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\oplus\mathbb{R}J
.

Proof.

For the proof we
define the section
R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
of
\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}}^{*})
by

R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(JX\wedge{\boldsymbol{\tau}}Y+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}X\wedge JY+X\wedge{\boldsymbol{\tau}}JY+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}JX\wedge Y\right),

where we use the isomorphism
{\mathcal{D}}\wedge{\mathcal{D}}\cong\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})
given by

(X\wedge Y)(Z):=g(X,Z)Y-g(Y,Z)X.

Because

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\left([\xi,X]+J[\xi,JX]\right)

anti-commutes with
J
, the tensor
R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is a section of
\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{u}({\mathcal{D}})
and satisfies

R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(JX,JY)=-R_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(X,Y).

Moreover, a direct computation yields

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)Z=\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y){\boldsymbol{\tau}}Z,\\

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Hence we define

R_{0}=R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}-R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}},

and recall that the curvature of the Tanaka–Webster connection satisfies the following Bianchi identity

(57)

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)Z=\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y){\boldsymbol{\tau}}Z,\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}},

so that

(58)

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}R_{0}(X,Y)Z=0,\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}.

As a consequence,

R_{0}
is a section of
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{u}({\mathcal{D}}))
.
In particular,

(59)

R_{0}(JX,JY)=R_{0}(X,Y),\quad\text{for all }X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{D}}.

We conclude that, for each point
y\in M
and all vectors
X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}_{y}
, it holds

(60)

\displaystyle 2R_{0}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(JX,JY)+R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}),

(61)

\displaystyle 2R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(JX,JY)+R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}),

i.e.,, both
R_{0}
, and
R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
take values in
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
.

For
R\in\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}({\mathcal{D}})
we define its Ricci tensor

\operatorname{Ric}(R)(X,Y)=\mathrm{tr}(R(X,\ .\ )Y).

Using that

g(R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Je_{i})Y,Je_{i})=-g(R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(JX,e_{i})JY,e_{i}),

it is easy to check that

\operatorname{Ric}(R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}})(X,Y)=(m-1){\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,JY),\quad\text{for all }X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}.

From this and (
41
) it follows that

(62)

\operatorname{Ric}(R_{0})(X,Y)=\rho(X,JY),\quad\text{for all }X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}.

We will now use
R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
to establish the four possibilities for the holonomy algebra of the Tanaka–Webster connection.
Let us fix a point
y\in M
and look at the structure of the symmetric endomorphism
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{y}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{y}
. Denote by
E_{\lambda}
the eigenspace of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{y}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. Since
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
anti-commutes with
J
, eigenvalues come in pairs
\pm\lambda
and

JE_{\lambda}=E_{-\lambda}.

Hence we have an orthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{D}}_{y}=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}\oplus\ker({\boldsymbol{\tau}}),

where
E^{\pm}
is the sum of the eigenspaces to positive (negative) eigenvalues, and consequently a
g
-orthogonal decomposition with a Lagrangian subspace
E_{y}
,

{\mathcal{D}}_{y}=E_{y}\oplus JE_{y}

such that

E^{+}\subseteq E_{y}
and
E^{-}\subseteq JE_{y}
.
In particular, we can fix a basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
of
E_{y}
consisting of eigenvectors of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{y}
corresponding to a non-negative eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}
, i.e.,

(63)

\tau e_{j}=\lambda_{j}e_{j},\quad\tau Je_{j}=-\lambda_{j}Je_{j},\quad\lambda_{j}\geq 0.

Not that if

is injective, then the Lagrangian subspace

is defined uniquely and we denote it by

It is easy to check that

(64)

\displaystyle R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(e_{j},e_{k})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}}{2}(e_{j}\wedge Je_{k}+e_{k}\wedge Je_{j}),

\displaystyle R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(e_{j},Je_{k})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}}{2}(e_{j}\wedge e_{k}+Je_{j}\wedge Je_{k}).

Since

(66)

R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(J)=0,

and
hence

R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is a section of
\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{su}({\mathcal{D}})
.
With our assumption
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
, there exists a point

and

\lambda_{j}>0
. Since all
\lambda_{k}
are non-negative, so that
\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}\not=0
, relation (

e_{j}\wedge e_{k}+Je_{j}\wedge Je_{k}\in\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}).

Note that if

are pairwise different, then

which ensures that for all

We recall that the subalgebra
\mathfrak{so}(m)\subseteq\mathfrak{su}({\mathcal{D}}_{y})
is spanned by these endomorphisms
to conclude that there exists an orthogonal decomposition
{\mathcal{D}}_{y}=E_{y}\oplus JE_{y}
such that

\mathfrak{so}(E_{y})\subseteq\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}).

We can split
\mathfrak{u}(m)
into
\mathfrak{so}(m)
-modules

Since
\mathfrak{so}(m)\subseteq\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
, the projection of
\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
to

is invariant under
\mathfrak{so}(m)
. However,
if

, the
\mathfrak{so}(m)
-module is
(\odot\mathbb{R}^{m})_{0}
is irreducible, so that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
is one of the Lie algebras

(67)

\mathfrak{u}(m),\quad\mathfrak{su}(m),\quad\mathfrak{so}(m)\oplus\mathbb{R}J,\quad\mathfrak{so}(m).

This proves the statement in the theorem about the four possible holonomy algebras of the Tanaka–Webster connection.

For the remaining statements about the holonomy

of the Schouten connection
\nabla

, but first recall from (

R^{W}(X,Y)=R^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)+d\theta(X,Y)C,\quad\text{for all }X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}},

.
With (

) this implies that for all

(69)

\displaystyle 2R_{0}(X,Y)+2\mathrm{d}\theta(X,Y)C

\displaystyle=R^{W}(JX,JY)+R^{W}(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla),

(70)

\displaystyle 2R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=-R^{W}(JX,JY)+R^{W}(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla),

where the containment in

is guaranteed by Theorem

.
From the last equality it follows that

R_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is a section of
\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
. On the one hand, this implies that

\mathfrak{so}(m)\subseteq\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla),

and consequently
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
also belongs to the list (

On the other hand, from the definition of the Wagner connection, it implies for each

, that

(R_{0}(\alpha))_{y}\in\mathfrak{hol}_{y}(\nabla),\quad\textrm{if }\mathrm{d}\theta(\alpha)=0.

Moreover,
from (

Since we have established that

are from the list (

), for the proof of the theorem it remains to exclude the case

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)

{E}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
of Lagrangian subspaces such that

and

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{2}=\lambda^{2}\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{D}}

Proof.

At each point

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
as in (

Equations (
64

65

on

The proof of the theorem will now follow from the next lemma.

Lemma 7.3
.

If
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)\subseteq\mathfrak{so}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)

is one of
\mathfrak{so}(m)
or
\mathfrak{so}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)

Proof.

Let

-orthogonal decomposition

We have that

.
From this and equation (

)

\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}(E^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})

Let

The bi-vectors of the form

\mathfrak{su}({\mathcal{D}})
.
Consequently, by (

\displaystyle 2R_{0}(X\wedge Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{0}(X\wedge Y+JX\wedge JY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(X\wedge Y+JX\wedge JY)+F(X\wedge Y+JX\wedge JY)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathfrak{so}(E^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\oplus\langle J\rangle.

Using this and projecting to
JE^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
the Bianchi identity (

, we obtain

\underset{XYZ}{\mathfrak{S}}F(X\wedge Y+JX\wedge JY)Z=0.

By the linear independence of

,
Y
and

, this implies

Hence,
R_{0}
is a section of
\Lambda^{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{so}(E^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})\oplus\langle J\rangle
. From this and (
72
) it follows that

C=fJ,

where

Let

-symmetric endomorphisms of

\left(\begin{matrix}a\mathbf{1}&b\mathbf{1}\\

\nabla

8.
Consequences and example

8.1.
Consequences

From Theorem
7.1
and Lemma
7.3
in its proof we obtain the following consequences.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
(M,J,\theta)
be a pseudo-Hermitian manifold of dimension
2m+1\geq 7
with
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
. Then
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
if and only if
(M,J,\theta)
is locally sub-symmetric.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
, Lemma
7.3
applies and yields that
(M,J,\theta)
is locally sub-symmetric.

For the converse, suppose that
(M,J,\theta)
is locally sub-symmetric. By Theorem
7.1
we have to exclude the cases that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is
\mathfrak{su}(m)
or
\mathfrak{u}(m)
. In both cases

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is irreducible and commutes with
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
. Since
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is symmetric, Schur’s lemma implies that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is a real multiple of the identity. Since
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
anti-commutes with
J
, this shows that
\lambda=0
, which contradicts the assumption that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\not=0
. Thus,
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
.
∎

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
(M,J,\theta)
be a pseudo-Hermitian manifold of dimension
2m+1\geq 7
with
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
. If
\nabla{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
, then
(M,J,\theta)
is locally sub-symmetric.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is irreducible. Since
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is a parallel symmetric field of endomorphisms, it commutes with
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
, and consequently it is proportional to
\operatorname{id}_{\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})}
. Since
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
anticommutes with
J
, this implies
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is not irreducible. From Theorem
7.1
it follows that
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{so}(m)
. Proposition
8.1
then yields that
(M,J,\theta)
is locally sub-symmetric. ∎

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
(M,J,\theta)
be a pseudo-Hermitian manifold of dimension
2m+1\geq 7
with
\nabla{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
. Then

1.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{su}(m)
if and only if
(M,J,\theta)
is pseudo-Einstein.

2.

\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla^{TM})=\mathfrak{su}(m)
if and only if
\rho=0
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
7.1
,
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
, and

[
21
, Corollary 4.2]
. ∎

8.2.
Example

We give here a construction for strictly pseudoconvex CR manifolds
M^{2m+1}
with pseudo-Einstein structure
\theta

and non-trivial torsion
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
. These pseudo-Hermitian spaces
(M,\theta)
have horizontal holonomy
\mathfrak{su}(m)
.
The construction is not explict, but happens locally by a small
arbitrary deformation of any strictly
pseudoconvex hypersurface in complex vector space
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
.
Recall that vanishing torsion for a pseudo-Hermitian structure
\theta

means that the corresponding Reeb vector is a transverse symmetry of the CR-structure
and vice versa. Hence, any pseudo-Hermitian structure on a CR-manifold
M
without transverse symmetries has non-vanishing torsion.
Thus, we aim to carry out local deformations, which eliminate any
given transverse symmetry. To the same time, results by
Lee (cf.
[
19
]
) guarantee the local existence of pseudo-Einstein structures for any strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
,
in particular, for any such deformation within
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
.

To make the deformation more precise, let
M^{\prime}
be any strictly pseudoconvex hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
.
We choose some point
p\in M^{\prime}
and disjoint small open balls
U
and
V
in
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
with
p
on their
boundaries, which cover
M^{\prime}
in a neighborhood of
p
. We deform the restriction of the hypersurface
M^{\prime}
to the
open ball
V
in normal direction
of
M^{\prime}
(such that the deformation is smooth up to the boundary
of
M\cap V
and trivial on the boundary and beyond).
If this deformation is small
the resulting hypersurface
M
in
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
is
still strictly pseudoconvex; and, if the deformation
is non-trivial (at every point
of
M^{\prime}\cap V
), then
M\cap M^{\prime}\cap V
is empty, whereas
M
and
M^{\prime}
coincide in the closure of
U
,
in particular, at
p\in M
. Note that any transverse symmetry
X
of
M^{\prime}
is
the restriction of some holomorphic vector field
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
, at least locally in some small neighborhood
W^{\prime}

of
p
in
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
.
(In fact, in
[
17
]
it is argued that the existence of any transverse
symmetry on a CR manifold gives rise to a local embedding into
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
as real hypersurface. This embedding
construction is explicit and shows
that the transverse symmetry is the restriction of some
holomorphic vector field.)
Obviously, since
M=M^{\prime}
in
U
, the restriction of the holomorphic vector field
\mathcal{X}
cannot be tangential to the non-trivial deformation
M

of
M^{\prime}
in
V
. Thus, there exist no transverse symmetries on the deformation
M
in the neighborhood
W^{\prime}
around
p
.
As already mentioned, the results in
[
19
]

guarantee a pseudo-Einstein structure
\theta

on the deformed hypersurface
M
in a small neighborhood
W\subseteq W^{\prime}
around

p
. Thus,
the deformation
M\cap W
of
M^{\prime}\cap W
around
p
in
\mathbb{C}^{m+1}

with
\theta
is pseudo-Einstein and has non-trivial torsion.
Since there are no transverse symmetries, the construction
is not locally sub-symmetric, and thus the horizontal holonomy algebra is
\mathfrak{su}(m)
.

9.
Contact sub-Riemannian spaces with
\nabla\mathrm{d}\theta=0

In this section we consider a generalization of pseudo-Hermitian spaces: contact sub-Riemannian spaces with
\nabla\mathrm{d}\theta=0
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
(M,\theta,g)
be a contact sub-Riemannian space with
\dim M=2m+1\geq 7
. If
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
and

\nabla\mathrm{d}\theta=0,

then, after multiplying
g
by a constant,

(M,\theta,g)
is pseudo-Hermitian.

Proof.

Define endomorphism field
\psi:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
by the equality

(76)

g(X,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,\psi(Y)),

for
X,Y\in{\mathcal{D}}
. Then
\psi
is skew with respect to
g
and consists of isomorphisms. Since
\nabla g=0
and
\nabla\mathrm{d}\theta=0
we also have that
\nabla\psi=0
.

For the Wagner connection
\nabla^{W}
, recall from Theorem
3.3
that
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{W})
. Hence, the condition
\nabla\mathrm{d}\theta=0
implies that
\nabla_{\xi}^{W}(\mathrm{d}\theta)=0
, which implies

0=\left[N^{W},\psi\right](X)+[\xi,\psi(X)]-\psi([\xi,X]).

With
N^{W}={\boldsymbol{\tau}}+C
and using that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{2}\theta(\xi,\psi(X),\psi(Y)\ =\ -{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}g(X,\psi(Y))+g(\psi([\xi,X])-[\xi,\psi(X)],Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\big(2\,\psi\circ{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(X),Y\big)-g\big([\xi,\psi(X)]-\psi([\xi,X]),Y\big),

so that

(77)

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\circ\psi+\psi\circ{\boldsymbol{\tau}}{-}\psi\circ C{+}C\circ\psi=0.

The proof now continues with two lemmas.

Lemma 9.2
.

The holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
does not preserve any proper
\psi_{x}
-invariant subspace of
D_{x}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{W})
preserves a proper
\psi_{x}
-invariant subspace
D^{1}_{x}\subseteq D_{x}
.
Then it preserves also the
g
-orthogonal complement
{\mathcal{D}}^{2}_{x}
to
{\mathcal{D}}^{1}_{x}
in
{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
, which is also
\psi_{x}
-invariant. Note that if
X_{1}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1}_{x}
and
X_{2}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{2}_{x}
, then

(78)

\mathrm{d}\theta(X_{1},X_{2})=-g(X_{1},\psi_{x}^{-1}(X_{2}))=0.

This implies that the restrictions of
\mathrm{d}\theta
to
{\mathcal{D}}^{1}_{x}
and
{\mathcal{D}}^{2}_{x}
are non-degenerate.
Therefore, with
m\geq 3
, we may assume that
\dim{\mathcal{D}}^{1}_{x}\geq 4
.

Let
X_{1},Y_{1}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{1}_{x}
, and
X_{2},Y_{2}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{2}_{x}
. Using the Bianchi identity

11.
Parallel horizontal spinors

Let
(M,J,\theta)
be a simply connected pseudo-Hermitian space of dimension
n=2m+1
with contact distribution
\mathcal{D}
.
The form
d\theta^{m}
defines an orientation on
{\mathcal{D}}
.
Let
P_{\operatorname{SO}}
be the
\operatorname{SO}(2m)
-principle bundle of positively oriented frames on
D
.
A
spin-structure
on
{\mathcal{D}}
is a reduction of
P_{\operatorname{SO}}
to a
\operatorname{Spin}(2m)
-principle bundle
P_{\operatorname{Spin}}

corresponding to the two-fold covering

\lambda:\operatorname{Spin}(2m)\to\operatorname{SO}(2m).

Suppose that a spin-structure on
{\mathcal{D}}
is fixed.
Denote by
\Delta_{2m}
the complex

\operatorname{Spin}(2m)
-spinor module.
The spinor bundle

S
is defined as the associated bundle,

S=P_{\operatorname{Spin}}\times_{\lambda}\Delta_{2m}.

The horizontal connection
\nabla
is lifted to a horizontal spinor derivative

\nabla^{S}:\Gamma({\mathcal{D}})\times\Gamma(S)\to\Gamma(S).

The holonomy algebra of this connection is the representations of
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)

on
S_{x}\cong\Delta_{2m}
. By the holonomy principle, there exists a non-zero spinor

s\in\Gamma(S)
such that
\nabla^{S}s=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
preserves a non-zero element
s_{x}\in S_{x}\cong\Delta_{2m}
.

Parallel horizontal spinors and their relation to holonomy on pseudo-Hermitian manifolds and on K-contact sub-Riemannian manifolds
were studied in
[
21
]
and
[
16
]
. We use these results and the results of the current paper,
and obtain the following cases.

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
(M,J,\theta)
be a simply connected pseudo-Hermitian space of dimension
n=2m+1\geq 7

with a fixed spin structure on the contact distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
.
Then
(M,J,\theta)
admits a non-zero parallel horizontal spinor if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

1.

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
and
\mathfrak{hol}(\nabla)
is either
\mathfrak{su}(m)
or
\mathfrak{so}(m)
;

2.

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
,
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
, and there
exists a
\nabla
-parallel
g
-skew-symmetric complex structure
I
on
{\mathcal{D}}
such that
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\subseteq\mathfrak{su}({\mathcal{D}}_{x},g_{x},I_{x})
;

3.

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
,
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
, and each
Lie algebra in decomposition (
35
) is one of
\mathfrak{su}(k)
, or
\mathfrak{sp}(k)
.

In cases 1 and 2 the dimension of the space of parallel spinors is
2
.

Proof.

It is well-known that the
\mathfrak{u}(m)
-module
\Delta_{2m}
admits the invariant decomposition

(82)

\Delta_{2m}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m}\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{C}^{m}

such that
\mathbb{C}^{m}
is the standard
\mathfrak{su}(m)
-module, and the complex structure
J\in\mathfrak{u}(m)
acts on
\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{C}^{m}

as multiplication by
(m-2k)i
.

Suppose that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\neq 0
. Consider the holonomy algebras
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
from Theorem
7.1
.
By the result of Wang
[
27
]
,
\mathfrak{u}(m)
and
\mathfrak{so}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2)
do not annihilate any non-zero element from
\Delta_{2m}
.
From
[
16

it follows that the subspace of
\Delta_{2m}
annihilated by
\mathfrak{so}(m)
is
the sum
\Lambda^{0}\mathbb{C}^{m}\oplus\Lambda^{m}\mathbb{C}^{m}
of the extremal bundles of the spinor representation. Hence, the dimension of parallel
spinors is 2. The same statement is true for
\mathfrak{su}(m)
. Only the extremal spinors are annihilated.

Suppose that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
and
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)\neq\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
. The corresponding statements of the theorem follow
from
[
16
, Theorem 8]
.

Suppose that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=0
and
\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)=\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla^{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
. In that case,

\mathfrak{hol}_{x}(\nabla)
is the holonomy algebra of a Riemannian manifold, and the result follows from
[
27
]
.
∎

Note that any pseudo-Einstein space
(M,J,\theta)
with or without torsion

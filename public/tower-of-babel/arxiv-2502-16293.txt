Optimization-free Smooth Control Barrier Function for Polygonal Collision Avoidance
Shizhen Wu, Yongchun Fang *, Ning Sun, Biao Lu, Xiao Liang, Yiming Zhao

Abstract
Polygonal collision avoidance (PCA) is short for the problem of collision avoidance between two polygons (i.e., polytopes in planar) that own their dynamic equations. This problem suffers the inherent difficulty in dealing with non-smooth boundaries and recently optimization-defined metrics, such as signed distance field (SDF) and its variants, have been proposed as control barrier functions (CBFs) to tackle PCA problems. In contrast, we propose an optimization-free smooth CBF method in this paper, which is computationally efficient and proved to be nonconservative. It is achieved by three main steps: a lower bound of SDF is expressed as a nested Boolean logic composition first, then its smooth approximation is established by applying the latest log-sum-exp method, after which a specified CBF-based safety filter is proposed to address this class of problems. To illustrate its wide applications, the optimization-free smooth CBF method is extended to solve distributed collision avoidance of two underactuated nonholonomic vehicles and drive an underactuated container crane to avoid a moving obstacle respectively, for which numerical simulations are also performed.

Control Barrier Function

\dot{x}=f(x)+g(x)u,

(1)

\displaystyle\Sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\dot{{x}}={f}({x})+{g}({x}){k}({x}).

(2)

For any initial condition

Definition 1.

Given a nonempty closed set

Definition
1

is added to highlight that

, which is essential to guarantee the stability of the set
\mathcal{C}

[
1
]
. To provide controllers with formal safety guarantees, the control barrier function method

]
is introduced.

Definition 2.

A continuously differentiable BF candidate

If

) on

,
then any locally Lipschitz continuous controller

satisfying

renders:
the closed set
\mathcal{C}
is forward invariant for (

(13)

as the zero-superlevel set of the signed distance between robots

, as defined in (

IV
Synthesizing a Boolean NBF Candidate

In this section, the detailed procedure of synthesizing a Boolean nonsmooth barrier function candidate is shown, which is composed of two main steps: establishing a lower bound of SDF and then building a boolean NBF candidate from such a lower bound.

IV-A

Establishing a lower bound of SDF

We begin the development with (
7
). First, associated with the support function
h_{\mathcal{P}}(a)
, the support halfspace of the set
\mathcal{P}
with outer normal vector
a
is introduced:

H^{-}_{\mathcal{P}}(a)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}a^{\top}\omega\leq h_{\mathcal{P}}(a)\right\},~\forall a\in\mathbb{S}^{1}.

(16)

For such a set whose boundary is a hyperplane, it is easy to calculate its SDF at origin via (
8
):

\operatorname{sd}({0},H_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}(a))=a^{\top}0-h_{\mathcal{P}}(a)=-h_{\mathcal{P}}(a).

Based on the formula (
7
), the SDF
h_{s}(x)
in (
14
) can be calculated by a mini-maximum optimization:

h_{s}(x)=\max_{\|a\|=1}\min_{\omega\in\mathcal{P}(x)}a^{\top}\omega=\max_{a^{\top}a=1}\operatorname{sd}({0},H_{\mathcal{P}(x)}^{-}(a)),

which is nonsmooth and hard to calculate exactly due to the
\max
/
\min
operators on compact sets
[
14
,
12
]
.

To address this, other facts about the difference
\mathcal{P}
is illustrated first. Combing (
10a
) with (
6
), it leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{j}-\bigcap_{k^{i}\in[r^{i}]}\mathcal{P}^{i}_{k^{i}}\subseteq\bigcap_{k^{i}\in[r^{i}]}\left(\mathcal{P}^{j}-\mathcal{P}^{i}_{k^{i}}\right),

(17a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{k^{j}\in[r^{j}]}\mathcal{P}^{j}_{k^{j}}-\mathcal{P}^{i}\subseteq\bigcap_{k^{j}\in[r^{j}]}\left(\mathcal{P}^{j}_{k^{j}}-\mathcal{P}^{i}\right),

(17b)

where the set inclusion ”
\subseteq
” comes from the fact
[
26
, Eq. (3.2)]
:
(A\cap B)-C\subseteq(A-C)\cap(B-C)
holds for arbitrary subsets
A,B,C
. To avoid the redundancy of symbols, sets
\mathcal{P}^{j}-\mathcal{P}^{i}_{k^{i}}
with index
{k^{i}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{j}_{k^{j}}-\mathcal{P}^{i}
with index
{k^{j}}
, are together renumbered as sets
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
with a single index
k
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{k}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{j})-\mathcal{P}^{i}_{k^{i}}(x^{i}),k={k^{i}},~~~~~~\forall{k^{i}}\in[r^{i}],\\
\mathcal{P}^{j}_{k^{j}}(x^{j})-\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{i}),k=r^{i}+{k^{j}},\forall{k^{j}}\in[r^{j}],\end{array}\right.

based on which (
17
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(x)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcap_{k\in[r^{i}+r^{j}]}\mathcal{Q}_{k}(x).

(18)

Recalling the fact about SDF in
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]
that:

A\subseteq B\Rightarrow\operatorname{sd}({0},A)\geq\operatorname{sd}({0},B)
, the following inequality holds naturally.

Proposition 1.

The following defined function
h_{a}(x)
is one lower boundary of
h_{s}(x)
:

\displaystyle h_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sd}(0,\mathcal{P}(x))=\max_{a^{\top}a=1}\operatorname{sd}({0},H_{\mathcal{P}(x)}^{-}(a))

(19)

\displaystyle\geq\max_{k\in[r^{i}+r^{j}]}\operatorname{sd}(0,\mathcal{Q}_{k}(x))=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h_{a}(x).

Recently, the softening version of function
h_{a}(x)
has been applied in
[
19
]
to substitute
h_{s}(x)
to achieve efficient optimization-based trajectory planning.
However, the following problems still have not been studied, which are not obvious to answer:

1)

Are the sets
\mathcal{Q}_{k}(x),\forall k\in[r^{i}+r^{j}]
some support halfspaces of
\mathcal{P}(x)
? Can the equality in (
18
) hold?

2)

Is
h_{a}(x)
non-conservative? Namely, are their zero-superlevel sets
\mathcal{S}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h_{a}(x)\geq 0\}
and
\mathcal{S}=\{x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}h_{s}(x)\geq 0\}
the same?

3)

Is it hopeful to choose
h_{a}(x)
as a Barrier function candidate on safety set
\mathcal{S}
?

The answers to the above three questions can be found in the following Lemma
2
, Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
respectively.

The next attempts to apply the properties of polytopes
\mathcal{P}^{i},\mathcal{P}^{j}
, so to reformulate the function
{h}_{a}(x)
as a
Boolean composition expression.

From (

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{j}-\mathcal{P}^{i}_{k^{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\{z={\omega}^{j}-{\omega}^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\omega}^{j}\in\mathcal{P}^{j},{\omega}^{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{i}_{k^{i}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(-A^{i}_{k^{i}})^{\top}z\leq b^{i}_{k^{i}}+(-A^{i}_{k^{i}})^{\top}{\omega}^{j},{\omega}^{j}\in\mathcal{P}^{j}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(-A^{i}_{k^{i}})^{\top}z\leq b^{i}_{k^{i}}+\max_{{\omega}^{j}\in\mathcal{P}^{j}}(-A^{i}_{k^{i}})^{\top}{\omega}^{j}\right\},

for all

. Similarly, for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{j}_{k^{j}}-\mathcal{P}^{i}

\displaystyle=\left\{z={\omega}^{j}-{\omega}^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\omega}^{j}\in\mathcal{P}^{j}_{k^{j}},{\omega}^{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(A^{j}_{k^{j}})^{\top}z\leq b^{j}_{k^{j}}-(A^{j}_{k^{j}})^{\top}{\omega}^{i},{\omega}^{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(A^{j}_{k^{j}})^{\top}z\leq b^{j}_{k^{j}}+\max_{{\omega}^{i}\in\mathcal{P}^{i}}-(A^{j}_{k^{j}})^{\top}{\omega}^{i}\right\}.

Based on the above expressions and recalling the notation of the support halfspace

For all

and

,

\displaystyle H_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}(-A^{i}_{k^{i}})=\mathcal{P}^{j}-\mathcal{P}^{i}_{k^{i}},~~H_{\mathcal{P}}^{-}(A^{j}_{k^{j}})=\mathcal{P}^{j}_{k^{j}}-\mathcal{P}^{i}.

Collect outer normal vectors of

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{-}^{i}(x^{i})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{-A^{i}_{k^{i}}(x^{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{k^{i}}\in[{r^{i}}]\right\},

(23a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{j}(x^{j})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{A^{j}_{k^{j}}(x^{j})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{k^{j}}\in[{r^{j}}]\right\},

(23b)

then the equality in (

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(x)=\bigcap_{a\in\mathcal{N}(x)}H^{-}_{\mathcal{P}(x)}(a)=\bigcap_{k\in[r^{i}+r^{j}]}\mathcal{Q}_{k}(x),

where

Theorem 2.

From the Boolean logic composition

,

\mathcal{S}

1) From Assumption
1
and definitions in (

\phi^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}}
,

\displaystyle\nabla\phi^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}}

\displaystyle=\left[(\nabla_{x^{i}}\phi^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}})^{\top},(\nabla_{x^{j}}\phi^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}})^{\top}\right]^{\top},

\displaystyle\nabla_{x^{i}}\phi^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}}

\displaystyle=(\partial A^{i}_{k^{i}})^{\top}v^{j}_{l^{j}}-\nabla_{x^{i}}b^{i}_{k^{i}},

\displaystyle\nabla\psi^{k^{j}}_{l^{i}}=[(\underbrace{(\partial v^{i}_{l^{i}})^{\top}A^{j}_{k^{j}}}_{\nabla_{x^{i}}\psi^{k^{j}}_{l^{i}}})^{\top},(\underbrace{(\partial A^{j}_{k^{j}})^{\top}v^{i}_{l^{i}}-\nabla_{x^{j}}b^{j}_{k^{j}}}_{\nabla_{x^{j}}\psi^{k^{j}}_{l^{i}}})^{\top}]^{\top},

2) Since

V
Safety Filter design with a single CBF

Till now, a Boolean NBF candidate
{h}_{a}(x)
in (
31
) has been established to approximate the SDF
h_{s}(x)
.
The most direct way is to validate that
{h}_{a}(x)
is further a nonsmooth CBF as in
[
30
]
, however, it is challenging, since the nonsmooth analysis would be required and the resulting control laws may be discontinuous. To avoid such complicated mathematical discussions, we attempt to build a smooth approximation of
{h}_{a}(x)
as a continuously differentiable CBF. Based on this, a safety filter with such a single CBF is established to address the stated problem.

Following in line with the recent work
[
20
]
, where a framework is proposed to approximate any nested Boolean composition into a smooth function, the log-sum-exp function is also applied to smooth
h_{a}
:

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{a}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln{(\hat{\phi}+\hat{\psi})}-\frac{b}{\kappa},

(34a)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{k^{i}\in[{r^{i}}]}\hat{\phi}^{k^{i}},~~~\hat{\phi}^{k^{i}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l^{j}\in[{r^{j}}]}\frac{1}{\hat{\phi}^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}}}},

(34b)

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{k^{j}\in[{r^{j}}]}\hat{\psi}^{k^{j}},~~\hat{\psi}^{k^{j}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\mathop{\sum}\limits_{l^{i}\in[{r^{i}}]}\frac{1}{\hat{\psi}^{k^{j}}_{l^{i}}}},

(34c)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{exp}(\kappa\phi^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}}),~~\hat{\psi}^{k^{j}}_{l^{i}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{exp}(\kappa\psi^{k^{j}}_{l^{i}}),

(34d)

where
\phi^{k^{i}}_{l^{j}},\psi^{k^{j}}_{l^{i}}
are component functions of
h_{a}
in (
31
),
\kappa>0
is a smoothing parameter, and
b\in\mathbb{R}
is a buffer. Define the zero-superlevel set of
\hat{h}_{a}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{a}({b,\kappa})=\left\{x\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\hat{h}_{a}(x;\kappa,b)\geq 0\right\},

where
\hat{h}_{a}(x)
is written as
\hat{h}_{a}(x;b,\kappa)
sometimes, to highlight its dependence on parameters
b,\kappa
.

Lemma 3.

Function
\hat{h}_{a}
in (
34
) approximates
{{h}_{a}}
in (
31
) with the following error bound:

{{h}_{a}}(x)-\frac{\ln b_{2}+b}{\kappa}\leq~\hat{h}_{a}(x;b,\kappa)~\leq{{h}_{a}}(x)+\frac{\ln b_{1}-b}{\kappa},

where
b_{1}=r^{i}+r^{j}
,
b_{2}=\max\{r^{i},r^{j}\}
. As a consequence, for fixed
\kappa>0
and whenever
{b}\geq\ln b_{1}
,
\hat{h}_{a}(x;{b},\kappa)\leq h_{a}(x)

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, a general framework is presented for the obstacle avoidance between polygons by an optimization-free smooth CBF.
This is achieved by proposing a smooth approximation of a lower bound of the traditional SDF, where great efforts have been made to study and guarantee its non-conservatism in theory.
Simulations are carried out for two complex nonlinear systems, to show
wide application scenarios of the above theoretical results.
Since the proposed CBF method is optimization-free, resulting in a very high calculation speed, it is very suitable to be extended for large-scale systems, such as coordinated collision avoidance of polytopic-shape multi-robots under cluttered environments. The biggest limitation of the proposed method is that it is now only applicable to two-dimensional cases, while how to extend it to the 3D situation is not obvious. Besides, input constraints are not considered, hence the proposed method may fail to work when the control input is constrained.
Future works involve doing experimental verifications on real-world robotic platforms, extending to more general polytopic multi-robots, high-order dynamics, control input constraints, and more complex polytopic timed reach-avoid specifications.

Lemma 4.

For two nonempty closed set
{K}_{1},{K}_{2}
,

\displaystyle\partial({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})

\displaystyle=(\partial{K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})\cup(\partial{K}_{2}\cap{K}_{1}),

and for all
x
,

\displaystyle d(x,{\partial K})

\displaystyle=\min\{d(x,{\partial{K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2}}),d(x,\partial{K}_{2}\cap{K}_{1})\}.

Proof.

The relations can be proved in the following procedure:

\displaystyle\partial({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})=({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})\cap\overline{({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})^{c}}

\displaystyle=({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})\cap\overline{({K}_{1}^{c}\cup{K}_{2}^{c})}=({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})\cap(\overline{{K}_{1}^{c}}\cup\overline{{K}_{2}^{c}})

\displaystyle=({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})\cap(\partial{K}_{1}\cup{K}_{1}^{c}\cup\partial{K}_{2}\cup{K}_{2}^{c})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2}\cap\partial{K}_{1})}_{={K}_{2}\cap\partial{K}_{1}}\cup\underbrace{({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2}\cap{K}_{1}^{c})}_{=\emptyset}

\displaystyle\cup\underbrace{({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2}\cap\partial{K}_{2})}_{={K}_{1}\cap\partial{K}_{2}}\cup\underbrace{({K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2}\cap{K}_{2}^{c})}_{=\emptyset}

\displaystyle=(\partial{K}_{1}\cap{K}_{2})\cup(\partial{K}_{2}\cap{K}_{1}).

Recalling the property of (unsigned) distance function

in
[

that

, it holds that

d(x,B_{1}\cup B_{2})=\min\{d(x,B_{1}),d(x,B_{2})\},~\forall x

By choosing

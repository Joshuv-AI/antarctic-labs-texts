On the local null controllability of a viscous Burgers’ system in finite time
Hoai-Minh Nguyen, Minh-Nguyen Tran

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the local null controllability of the Burgers control system y t − y x ​ x + y ​ y x = u ⁡ ( t ) y_{t}-y_{xx}+yy_{x}=u(t) on a bounded interval imposed by the zero Dirichlet boundary condition. It is known from the work of Marbach [ 25 ] that this control system is not locally null controllable in small time. In this paper, we prove that the system is not locally null controllable in finite time as well. Our approach is inspired by the works of Coron, Koenig, and Nguyen [ 14 ] , and Nguyen [ 27 ] on the controllability of the KdV system and is different from the one of Marbach.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Introduction and statement of the main result

In this paper, we study the local null controllability in finite time of a viscous Burgers control system using internal controls depending only on
time
. More precisely, for
T>0
, we consider the following control system:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}y_{t}(t,x)-y_{xx}(t,x)+yy_{x}(t,x)=u(t)\quad&\text{ in }(0,T)\times(0,1),\\[5.0pt]
y(t,0)=y(t,1)=0&\text{ in }(0,T),\\[5.0pt]
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x)&\text{ in }(0,1),\end{cases}

where
y_{0}
is the initial datum and
u
is the control. The control is thus internal and depends only on time.

For
T>0
, set

(1.2)

\displaystyle Y_{T}=C([0,T],L^{2}(0,1))\cap L^{2}((0,T),H^{1}(0,1)),

and equip this space with the following norm

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|y\|_{Y_{T}}=\|y\|_{C([0,T],L^{2}(0,1))}+\|y\|_{L^{2}((0,T),H^{1}(0,1))}.

It is known that for every
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
and
u\in L^{1}(0,T)
, there exists a unique weak solution
y\in Y_{T}
of (
1.1
) (see, e.g.,
Lemma

A.4
in
Appendix

A
).

We first recall several notions on the local null controllability of (
1.1
) considered in this paper. The first one is on the null controllability in time
T>0
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
T>0
. System (
1.1
) is said to be locally null controllable in time
T
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that for every
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
with
\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}<\delta
, there exists a real-valued function
u\in L^{1}(0,T)
with
\|u\|_{L^{1}(0,T)}<\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle y(T,\cdot)=0,

where
y\in Y_{T}
is the unique solution of
(\ref{ViscousBurgers1D})
.

Here are two related notions.

Definition 1.2
.

System (
1.1
) is said to be small-time locally null controllable if for every
T>0
, the system is locally null controllable in time
T
.

Definition 1.3
.

System (
1.1
) is said to be finite-time locally null controllable if there exists
T>0
such that the system is locally null controllable in time
T
.

We next discuss the controllability of the linearized system of (
1.1
) around 0, which is given by

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{t}(t,x)-y_{xx}(t,x)=u(t)\quad&\text{ in }(0,T)\times(0,1),\\[6.0pt]
y(t,0)=y(t,1)=0&\text{ in }(0,T),\\[6.0pt]
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x)&\mbox{ in }(0,1).\end{cases}

Let

be the Hilbert basis of

formed by the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator on the interval
(0,1)

(1.5)

\displaystyle e_{k}(x)=\sqrt{2}\sin(k\pi x)\quad\mbox{ for }k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\text{ and }x\in(0,1).

Denote by

For each

, set

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(t,x)=e^{\pi^{2}k^{2}t}\sin(k\pi x)\quad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times[0,1].

Then

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi_{k,t}(t,x)+\phi_{k,xx}(t,x)=0&\mbox{ in }(0,+\infty)\times(0,1),\\[6.0pt]

Given

Since the right-hand side of (

\mbox{ if $y(T,\cdot)=0$, then $y_{0}\in\mathscr{M}^{\bot}$}.

Therefore, for every

1.2.
Ideas of the proof

The starting point of the analysis is the power series expansion method. We formally write the control and the solution under the form

\displaystyle u=\varepsilon u_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}u_{2}+\ldots,\quad y=\varepsilon y_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}y_{2}+\ldots.

Then

\displaystyle yy_{x}=\varepsilon^{2}y_{1}y_{1,x}+\ldots.

This motivates us to consider the following systems:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{1,t}(t,x)-y_{1,xx}(t,x)=u_{1}(t)\quad&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times(0,1),\\[6.0pt]
y_{1}(t,0)=y_{1}(t,1)=0&\text{ in }(0,+\infty),\\[6.0pt]
y_{1}(0,\cdot)=0&\text{ in }(0,1),\end{cases}

and

(1.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{2,t}(t,x)-y_{2,xx}(t,x)+y_{1}y_{1,x}(t,x)=u_{2}(t)\quad&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times(0,1),\\[6.0pt]
y_{2}(t,0)=y_{2}(t,1)=0&\text{ in }(0,+\infty),\\[6.0pt]
y_{2}(0,\cdot)=0&\text{ in }(0,1),\end{cases}

where
u_{1},u_{2}\in L^{1}(0,+\infty)
.

Our analysis is then inspired by the work of Coron, Koenig, and Nguyen
[
14
]
, and Nguyen
[
27

. The key observation/analysis is that, if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}y_{2}(T,x)\left(-\sin(k_{0}\pi x)\right)dx\geq C_{T}\|u_{1}\|_{[H^{\frac{5}{4}}(0,T)]^{*}}^{2}.

We next describe briefly how to prove (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}y_{2}(T,x)\phi_{k}(T,x)dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}y_{1}^{2}(t,x)\phi_{k,x}(t,x)dxdt.

Note that

1.3.
Literature review

The Burgers equation was introduced by Burgers
[
4
]
and shares many features with the Navier-Stokes system. Many works have been devoted to the control aspects of the viscous Burgers equation. Let us first briefly discuss the null controllability aspect of this equation. When a Dirichlet boundary control is applied on one side (with no internal controls), Fursikov and Imanuvilov
[
20
]
showed that the system is locally null controllable in small time and globally null controllable in a finite time that depends on the size of the initial datum. Their proof relies on Carleman estimates for the parabolic problem, viewing the nonlinear term
yy_{x}
as a small forcing term. Fernandez-Cara and Guerrero
[
18
]
later demonstrated that such a system is not globally null controllable in small time. The same phenomenon holds even when Dirichlet boundary controls are used on both sides, as shown by Guerrero and Imanuvilov
[
23
]
. Coron
[
11
]
established that with a Dirichlet boundary control on one side, the finite time required for global null controllability of the Burgers system does not depend on the initial data. His analysis is based on the maximum principle and the local result. The situation changes when an internal control, which depends only on time, is added to the Dirichlet boundary controls on both sides. Chapouly
[
7
]
, using the return method introduced by Coron
[
8
,
9
]
(see also
[
10
, Chapter 6]
), proved that in this case, the Burgers equation is globally null controllable in small time. Marbach
[
24
]
improved this result by showing that the same conclusion holds when using only a Dirichlet boundary control on the left and an internal control depending only on time. Robin
[
28
]
has obtained similar results to those in
[
24
]
for the generalized Burgers equation. Other results on the controllability of the viscous Burgers equation using boundary or internal controls can be found in
[
19
,
15
,
17
,
22
]
.

The method used in this paper is inspired by the work of Coron, Koenig, and Nguyen
[
14
]
, and Nguyen
[
27
]
on the controllability of the KdV system, with the starting point being the power series expansion method. This method was introduced by Coron and Crépeau
[
12
]
to study the controllability of the KdV system using Neumann boundary controls on the right for critical lengths whose unreachable space of the linearized system is of dimension 1. Later, Cerpa
[
5
]
, and Crépeau and Cerpa
[
6
]
applied the power series expansion method to prove finite-time controllability of such a system at all critical lengths. Coron, Koenig, and Nguyen
[
14
]
showed that the local null controllability in small time does not hold for a class of critical lengths. The critical lengths in this case were discovered by Rosier
[
29
]
. Nguyen
[
27
]
implemented the power series expansion method to study the controllability of KdV equations using Dirichlet boundary controls on the right for all critical lengths. The set of critical lengths in this case was observed by Glass and Guerrero
[
21
]
. The power series expansion method was also used to study the controllability of the water tank problem
[
13
]
, as well as of the Schrödinger equation using bilinear controls
[
2
,
1
,
3
]
. It was also applied to study the decay of solutions of KdV systems at critical lengths for which the energy of the solutions of the linearized system is conserved
[
26
]
.

5.1.
Proof of Lemma

We begin this section with the following key ingredient for the proof of
Lemma

5.2
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\gamma\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})
. Set

\displaystyle g_{\gamma}(s)=\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\gamma-2}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}|s|}\quad\text{ for }s\in\mathbb{R}.

The following properties hold:

1)

g_{\gamma}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

2)

For every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{\gamma}(\xi)=\sqrt{2}\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k^{2\gamma}}{\pi^{4}k^{4}+\xi^{2}}.

3)

There exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\gamma
such that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

(5.10)

\displaystyle|\hat{g}_{\gamma}(\xi)|\leq C\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{3}{4}}.

Proof.

By Fatou’s lemma, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|g_{\gamma}(s)|ds\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}k^{2\gamma-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}|s|}ds=2\pi^{-2}\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\gamma-4}\mathop{<}^{\gamma<\frac{3}{2}}+\infty.

In other words,
g_{\gamma}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

We have, for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

\hat{g}_{\gamma}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\gamma-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi s}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}|s|}ds\\
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\gamma-2}\left(\frac{1}{\pi^{2}k^{2}+i\xi}+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}k^{2}-i\xi}\right)=\sqrt{2}\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k^{2\gamma}}{\pi^{4}k^{4}+\xi^{2}},

which is (
5.9
).

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
|\xi|\geq 1
and let
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
be such that
k_{0}\leq|\xi|^{1/2}<k_{0}+1
. We have

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\frac{k^{2\gamma}}{k^{4}+\xi^{2}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\frac{k^{2\gamma}}{\xi^{2}}\mathop{\leq}^{\gamma>-\frac{1}{2}}C_{\gamma}k_{0}^{2\gamma+1}|\xi|^{-2}\leq C_{\gamma}|\xi|^{\gamma-\frac{3}{2}},

and

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\frac{k^{2\gamma}}{k^{4}+\xi^{2}}\leq\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}k^{2\gamma-4}\mathop{\leq}^{\gamma<\frac{3}{2}}C_{\gamma}k_{0}^{2\gamma-3}\leq C_{\gamma}|\xi|^{\gamma-\frac{3}{2}}.

Combining (
5.9
) with (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) and noting that
\hat{g}_{\gamma}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
(since
g_{\gamma}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
), we obtain (
5.10
). The proof is complete.
∎

We now prove
Lemma

5.2
.

Proof of
Lemma

5.2
.

By a standard approximation argument involving the Fatou lemma, one can assume that
V\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
as well. This condition will be assumed from now on.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}|a_{k}(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=k^{2\gamma}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}(t-s_{1})}V(s_{1})ds_{1}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}(t-s_{2})}V(s_{2})ds_{2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=k^{2\gamma}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}(2t-s_{1}-s_{2})}V(s_{1})V(s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2}\right)dt.

Applying Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}|a_{k}(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=k^{2\gamma}\iint_{(0,+\infty)^{2}}V(s_{1})V(s_{2})\left(\int_{\max\{s_{1},s_{2}\}}^{+\infty}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}(2t-s_{1}-s_{2})}dt\right)ds_{1}ds_{2}

(5.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(s_{1})V(s_{2})k^{2\gamma-2}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}|s_{1}-s_{2}|}ds_{1}ds_{2},

since
\mbox{supp }V\subset[0,+\infty)
.

Set

\displaystyle g_{\gamma}(s)=\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2\gamma-2}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}|s|}\quad\text{ for }s\in\mathbb{R}.

It follows from (

) that

(5.14)

\displaystyle 2\pi^{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|a_{k}(t)|^{2}dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(s)(V\ast g_{\gamma})(s)ds.

Applying Parseval’s formula and Lemma
5.3
, we get

(5.15)

\leq C_{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{V}(\xi)|^{2}\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{3}{4}}d\xi\leq C_{\gamma}\|V\|_{H^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{3}{4}}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Combining (
5.14
) and (
5.15
), we obtain (
5.8
). The proof is complete.

y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
:

(A.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{t}-y_{xx}=f\quad&\text{ in }(0,T)\times(0,1),\\[6.0pt]
y(t,0)=y(t,1)=0&\text{ in }(0,T),\\[6.0pt]
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x)&\text{ in }(0,1).\end{cases}

We recall the space
Y_{T}
defined in (
1.2
). In what follows, we also consider the space

\displaystyle X_{T}=C([0,T],H^{1}_{0}(0,1))\cap L^{2}((0,T),H^{2}(0,1)),

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|y\|_{X_{T}}=\|y\|_{C([0,T],H^{1}_{0}(0,1))}+\|y\|_{L^{2}((0,T),H^{2}(0,1))}.

The following standard definition of weak solutions of (
A.1
) is used.

Definition A.1
.

Let
T>0
,
f\in L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))
, and
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
. A function
y\in Y_{T}
is a solution of (
A.1
) if for every
\tau\in[0,T]
and
\phi\in X_{T}\cap H^{1}((0,T)\times(0,1))
,

(A.2)

\int_{0}^{1}y(\tau,x)\phi(\tau,x)dx-\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}(x)\phi(0,x)dx-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}y(t,x)\;(\phi_{t}+\phi_{xx})(t,x)\;dxdt\\
=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}f(t,x)\phi(t,x)dxdt.

Remark 5
.

Using the integration by parts formula, one can check that if
y
is smooth enough and satisfies (
A.1
) in the classical sense, then
y
is a solution of (
A.1
) in the sense of
Definition

A.1
.

The following result on (
A.1
) is standard.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
T>0
,
f\in L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))
, and
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
. Then system (
A.1
) has a unique solution
y\in Y_{T}
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
T
such that

(A.3)

\displaystyle\|y\|_{Y_{T}}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}+\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\right).

In addition, if
f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times(0,1))
and
y_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(0,1)
, then
y\in X_{T}
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
T
such that

(A.4)

\displaystyle\|y\|_{X_{T}}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times(0,1))}+\|y_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(0,1)}\right).

Proof.

Assertion (
A.4
) is known, see, e.g.,
[
16
, Section 7.1]
. Assertion (
A.3
) is quite standard but we cannot find a reference for it; many standard references consider
\|f\|_{L^{2}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}
instead of
\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}
, see, e.g.,
[
16
, Section 7.1]
. The proof is almost the same. For the convenience of the readers, we show (
A.3
) when
f
,
y_{0}
, and
y
are smooth enough. For every
\tau\in[0,T]
, we multiply (
A.1
) by
y
and integrate by parts on
(0,\tau)\times(0,1)
to obtain

(A.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}y^{2}(\tau,x)dx-\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}^{2}(x)dx\right)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}y^{2}_{x}(t,x)dxdt=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{1}f(t,x)y(t,x)dxdt.

This implies

\displaystyle\|y\|_{C([0,T],L^{2}(0,1))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+2\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}\|y\|_{C([0,T],L^{2}(0,1))}

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|y\|^{2}_{C([0,T],L^{2}(0,1))}+2\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}^{2},

so

(A.6)

\displaystyle\|y\|_{C([0,T],L^{2}(0,1))}\leq 2\left(\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}\right).

Combining (
A.5
) and (
A.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|y_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|y_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+2\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}\left(\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))}\right)^{2},

so

(A.7)

\displaystyle\|y\|_{L^{2}((0,T),H^{1}_{0}(0,1))}\leq 2\left(\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(0,T),L^{2}(0,1)}\right).

Estimate (
A.3
) now follows from (
A.6
) and (
A.7
).
∎

The following lemma, which estimates the
L^{2}
norm of the solution
y
of (

), is a consequence of (

).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
T>0
,
f\in L^{1}((0,T),L^{2}(0,1))
, and
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)

y\in Y_{T}
be the unique solution of (

C
depending only on

such that

(A.8)

\displaystyle\|y\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times(0,1))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{\left(C\left([0,T],H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\right)\right)^{*}}+\|y_{0}\|_{H^{-1}(0,1)}\right).

One has the following result when the source term
f
is supported in
[0,T/2]\times[0,1]
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
T>0
,
f\in L^{1}((0,T),H^{1}(0,1))
, and
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
. Suppose that
\mbox{supp }f\subset[0,T/2]\times[0,1]
. Let
y\in Y_{T}
be the unique solution of the system

(A.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{t}(t,x)-y_{xx}(t,x)=f_{x}(t,x)\quad&\text{ in }(0,T)\times(0,1),\\
y(t,0)=y(t,1)=0&\text{ in }(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x)&\text{ in }(0,1).\end{cases}

Then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
T
such that

\|y(T,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times(0,1))}+\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\Big).

Proof.

By
Lemma

A.1
, without loss of generality, one can assume that
y_{0}=0
. Then

\displaystyle y(T,x)=-\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\left(\sqrt{2}k\pi e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}f(t,y)e^{\pi^{2}k^{2}t}\cos(k\pi y)\,dy\,dt\right)\sqrt{2}\sin(k\pi x)\mbox{ in }(0,1).

This implies

\displaystyle\|y(T,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2}e^{-2\pi^{2}k^{2}T}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}f(t,y)e^{\pi^{2}k^{2}t}\cos(k\pi y)\,dy\,dt\right|^{2}.

Since
\mathrm{supp}\;f\subset[0,T/2]\times[0,1]
, we derive that

\displaystyle\|y(T,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2}e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}T}\right)\|f\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times(0,1))}^{2}\leq C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{1}((0,T)\times(0,1))}.

The conclusion follows.

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
5.4

We prove (
5.30
). We have

(B.1)

\displaystyle\|U\|_{[H^{s-1}(0,T)]^{*}}=\sup_{\varphi\in H^{s-1}(0,T),\|\varphi\|_{H^{s-1}(0,T)}\leq 1}\left|\int_{0}^{T}U(t)\varphi(t)dt\right|.

Fix
\varphi\in H^{s-1}(0,T)
. Set

\Phi(t)=\int_{t}^{T}\varphi(s)\,ds\quad\text{ for }t\in[0,T].

We have

\int_{0}^{T}U(t)\varphi(t)dt=-\int_{0}^{T}U(t)\Phi^{\prime}(t)dt=\int_{0}^{T}u(t)\Phi(t)dt.

It follows that

\Big|\int_{0}^{T}U(t)\varphi(t)dt\Big|\leq\|u\|_{[H^{s}(0,T)]^{*}}\|\Phi\|_{H^{s}(0,T)}.

Since
\Phi^{\prime}=-\varphi
in
[0,T]
and
\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}
, we have

\|\Phi\|_{H^{s}(0,T)}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{H^{s-1}(0,T)}.

Therefore,

\left|\int_{0}^{T}U(t)\varphi(t)dt\right|\leq C\|u\|_{[H^{s}(0,T)]^{*}}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s-1}(0,T)}.

Assertion (
5.30
) now follows from (
B.1
). ∎

Appendix C
A controllability result for the heat equation via the moment method

Consider the control system

(C.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{t}(t,x)-y_{xx}(t,x)=u(t)\quad&\text{ in }(0,T)\times(0,1),\\[6.0pt]
y(t,0)=y(t,1)=0&\text{ in }(0,T),\\[6.0pt]
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x)&\text{ in }(0,1).\end{cases}

In this section, we study the initial data
y_{0}
which can be steered to
0
at time
T
by using controls
u
in
L^{2}(0,T)
. Here is the main result.

Proposition C.1
.

Let
T>0
. Set

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}=\overline{\mathrm{span}\{\sin(k\pi x)\;|\;k\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\;k\text{ even}\}}^{\|\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}}\subset L^{2}((0,1),\mathbb{R}).

There exists a bounded linear operator

\displaystyle\Gamma:\mathscr{M}^{\bot}\longrightarrow L^{2}((0,T),\mathbb{R})

such that for every
y_{0}\in\mathscr{M}^{\bot}
, we have

\displaystyle y(T,\cdot)=0,

where
y\in Y_{T}
is the solution of (
C.1
) with
u=\Gamma(y_{0})
.

Proof of
Proposition

C.1
.

Let
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
and
u\in L^{2}(0,T)
. Let
y\in Y_{T}
be the solution of (
C.1
). We have, for
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

e^{\pi^{2}k^{2}T}\int_{0}^{1}y(T,x)\sin(k\pi x)dx-\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}(x)\sin(k\pi x)dx=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\sin(k\pi x)dx\right)\left(\int_{0}^{T}u(t)e^{\pi^{2}k^{2}t}dt\right).

Note that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\int_{0}^{1}\sin(k\pi x)dx=\frac{1-(-1)^{k}}{k\pi}.

One can thus steer
y_{0}
to 0 at time
T
if and only if
y_{0}\in\mathscr{M}^{\bot}
and for every odd
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}u(t)e^{\pi^{2}k^{2}t}dt=c_{k},

where

c_{k}=-\frac{k\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}(x)\sin(k\pi x)dx.

The proof is now based on the standard moment method. For example, one can follow almost the same arguments as in
[
30
]
. The details are omitted.
∎

Long-term stability of the GMP-compliant recombinant BCG vaccine against human RSV under quality control analyses
Authors: Tays Troncoso-Bravo, Daniela B. Rivera, Mario A. Ramírez, Ricardo A. Loaiza, Antonia Reyes, Valentina Pavez, Drosos Kourounis, Kyveli P. Tsatsara, Elina Horefti, Beatriz Martinez-Gonzalez, Ioannis Rabias, Hernán F. Peñaloza, Pablo A. González, Susan M. Bueno, Alexis M. Kalergis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe human respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of severe respiratory infections in newborns, young children, immunocompromised patients, and older adults. The current landscape of approaches to preventing the RSV disease includes vaccines for older adults and pregnant women, as well as prophylactic monoclonal antibodies for newborns and infants, with positive results. To expand the availability of prophylactic options against the RSV disease for vulnerable individuals, including newborns and older adults, we developed a recombinant BCG-based vaccine expressing the RSV nucleoprotein (rBCG-N-RSV) and manufactured under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) conditions. This vaccine has previously been shown to be safe and immunogenic in murine models, in neonatal calves, and has completed a phase 1 clinical trial in human adults. This article aims to assess the quality control testing of a GMP lot of rBCG-N-RSV to ensure antigen expression, potency, safety, and efficacy, which are required to advance to further clinical trials. Notably, all these parameters complied with release requirements. Furthermore, vaccine safety and protection were evaluated in a murine model of RSV infection, demonstrating enhanced virus-specific CD4+ T cell activation and significant anti-N-RSV IgG levels in serum. Vaccination improved respiratory function after infection and increased viral clearance in the lungs of mice. Taken together, these data demonstrate long-term stability, safety, and protective capacity of a GMP lot of the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine in a preclinical model of RSV respiratory infection, highlighting the importance of rigorous control protocols for regulatory compliance of vaccine production.

Introduction

The human respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is one of the leading causes of acute lower respiratory tract infections (ALRTIs), especially among newborns, young children, immunocompromised patients, and older adults.1,2,3 The high incidence of RSV and the severe disease it causes in these groups are a pressing public health challenge, given the considerable global burden of hospitalizations and fatalities. Globally, RSV causes approximately 3.6 million hospitalizations worldwide annually, leading to nearly one hundred thousand deaths each year in children under five years of age.1 Six decades after its discovery, it is estimated that more than 80% of children under two years of age have been infected with RSV at least once,2 underscoring the importance of developing practical tools to prevent the associated complications of RSV infection, ranging from mild colds to severe outcomes such as pneumonia, neurocognitive disorders, or even death.2,3,4,5,6,7,8

Several approaches have been explored to prevent and treat RSV, including monoclonal antibodies, vaccines, and antivirals.6 Our group has developed and previously demonstrated that a recombinant vaccine based on a Mycobacterium bovis Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) expressing the RSV nucleoprotein (rBCG-N-RSV) is safe and immunogenic in mice and elicits a strong Th1-type cellular immune response against RSV that prevents disease, promotes viral clearance, and reduces virus-induced lung injury.9,10,11,12

Importantly, because this vaccine platform is based on live recombinant attenuated BCG, preservation of immune responses against mycobacterial antigens has also been observed. Previous studies from our group demonstrated that vaccination with rBCG-N-RSV induces antibodies against both RSV- and BCG-derived antigens, supporting its potential as a dual-purpose vaccine platform.7,8,9

Compliance with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) is crucial for vaccine development, ensuring quality, safety, and reproducibility through stringent controls at every stage of production.9 In previous studies, the safety, immunogenicity, and capacity to prevent RSV disease of rBCG-N-RSV manufactured under GMP conditions were evaluated in mice, neonatal calves, and humans, validating its safety, immunogenicity, and protective profiles, initially characterized in laboratory experiments with the precursor non-GMP vaccine.7,8,9,10,11,13 Administering a single dose of rBCG-N-RSV GMP with 1×105 colony-forming units (CFUs) was safe and provided protective immunity in BALB/c mice challenged with RSV.9 The vaccine did not cause adverse effects and reduced lung neutrophil infiltration. Additionally, ex vivo stimulation of T cells with RSV-derived antigens results in the secretion of IFN-γ and IL-17, indicating the capacity of rBCG-N-RSV to elicit a specific cellular immune response against the RSV N protein capable of halting viral replication.14 The vaccine also prevented long-term behavioral impairments caused by RSV infection, as evidenced by normal marble burying behavior observed six months after RSV infection in mice.14

In addition, under controlled laboratory conditions9 and field conditions,14 the safety, immunogenicity, and capacity to confer protection of rBCG-N-RSV against bovine RSV (bRSV) and Mycobacterium bovis were evaluated in calves from 5 to 21 days of age. These assays were primarily supported by the high sequence similarity (93.3%) of the bRSV nucleoprotein relative to that of humans.15 In both studies, calves received the first dose subcutaneously (s.c.) in the left neck zone with 1 × 106 CFUs of rBCG-N-RSV. Two weeks after the primary vaccination, a booster dose was administered in the right neck zone with the same dose. In calves, the vaccine was found to be safe and well-tolerated, with no systemic or local adverse effects.9,14 Concluding remarks of this study prove that the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine, under controlled conditions, induced a robust immune response mediated by cell proliferation and specific antibody production, including an antigen-specific activation profile in immune cells up to 180 days post-immunization under field conditions.14

Furthermore, in 2017, a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 1 clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03213405) with escalating doses was conducted in healthy volunteers aged 18 to 50 years to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and immunogenicity of the GMP rBCG-N-RSV vaccine.8 This study demonstrated that the vaccine is safe and well-tolerated, inducing serum antibodies specific to BCG (anti-PPD) and the RSV N protein (anti-N-RSV),16 highlighting the vaccine’s dual role in humans. In addition, serum IgG from vaccinated patients showed increased in vitro RSV-neutralizing activity compared with the BCG-WT group, as measured by a plaque-reduction neutralization assay. These data suggest that immunization with rBCG-N-RSV GMP promotes the production of antibodies with greater neutralizing capacity compared with the wild-type BCG (BCG-WT) group.

Moreover, the vaccine induced IFN-γ and IL-2 production in ex vivo-stimulated peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and elevated the number of circulating PBMCs capable of producing perforin and granzyme B.8

As outlined earlier, vaccine production under GMP standards requires stringent controls at every stage of manufacturing. These controls ensure safety, quality, reproducibility, and compliance with international regulatory frameworks.16,17,18 Despite ongoing development of recombinant BCG-based vaccines, there remains limited evidence on the long-term stability, the evolution of quality control parameters, and the preservation of biological activity of GMP vaccines during extended storage.

In this context, in the current work, we conducted a series of characterization and quality control assays on the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine manufactured under GMP conditions in the US and subsequently evaluated at the Hellenic Pasteur Institute (HPI) in Athens, Greece. This institute, bearing Good Laboratory Practice (GLP), GMP, and International Organization for Standardization (ISO) certifications, specializes in BCG. We measured potency, stability (at both the genomic and protein expression levels), sterility (microbiological safety), bacterial endotoxin, and physicochemical parameters, including humidity, pH, and vial integrity. Finally, in an in vivo assay using BALB/c mice, we assessed the safety profile of the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine and its capacity to protect against RSV infection, comparing this vaccine with unvaccinated mice and mice vaccinated with BCG-WT, which is routinely used as a vaccine against Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

Our data show that all evaluated parameters match the original specifications assessed in 2013 at the time of manufacture, underscoring the stability of the investigational product, the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine, manufactured under GMP conditions. These findings demonstrate that a GMP-manufactured recombinant BCG vaccine can maintain its physicochemical, microbiological, genomic, and immunological properties over time, supporting its feasibility for long-term storage and future clinical translation.

Results

The manufacturing process of the drug substance (DS) began with the preparation of the master cell bank (MCB), followed by the working cell bank (WCB), both of which were produced under stringent GMP conditions. These processes were conducted in controlled and aseptic conditions to ensure microbiological purity and consistency. Subsequently, the DS was utilized to prepare the drug product (DP), the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine in vials. The manufacturer initially performed quality controls in 2010 for the MCB, WCB, and DS, and in 2013 for the DP. After these initial quality control assessments, the HPI in Athens, Greece, conducted a second round of GLP quality control testing on the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine in 2024. rBCG-N-RSV GMP characterization of recombinant gene

DNA samples were obtained from the rBCG-N-RSV MCB, WCB, and BDS. The samples were undiluted, and 1/10 dilutions were re-analyzed by PCR at the HPI to confirm the presence of specific genetic markers and the recombinant plasmid. Amplification of the 16S rRNA gene, a conserved bacterial marker, produced clear products at the expected size of around 100 bp in all samples (01–08), confirming the identity of Mycobacterium spp. in each colony analyzed (Figure 1A). A specific set of primers was used to amplify a 1,518 bp fragment spanning the N-RSV insert and a portion of the pMV361 plasmid backbone to verify the presence of the recombinant plasmid carrying the N-RSV sequence within rBCG-N-RSV. PCR products of the expected size were observed in samples 01–06 and 08. At the same time, no amplification was detected in sample 07 (undiluted rBCG-N-RSV BDS), suggesting that the target DNA was not amplified, as the diluted DNA sample (number 06) yielded the expected PCR product (Figure 1C). The robust amplification observed in the samples indicates effective plasmid integration and stability through the years in the GMP recombinant BCG. These results confirm the genetic integrity of the GMP recombinant BCG vials and validate their use in downstream protein expression assays. Although PCR analysis confirmed the presence of the RSV insert sequence, future studies, including complementary molecular approaches, will be important for further assessing the integrity and stability of the insert.

First, SDS-PAGE analysis was conducted to separate the total proteins from each sample. The protein migration profiles showed multiple bands for all samples, evidencing proper protein extraction and integrity (Figure 2B). A protein signal at approximately 45 kDa, corresponding to the expected molecular weight of the RSV N protein (44.4 kDa), was observed in all lanes, except the negative control lane. The positive control, consisting of purified RSV N protein with a His-tag, also produced a signal at 44.4 kDa, serving as a reference for the molecular weight of this protein (Figure 2C).

Overall, the western blot results confirm the proper expression of RSV N protein in all the rBCG-N-RSV products tested. The consistent results across replicates highlight the robustness of the expression system and confirm the potential of GMP recombinant BCG as a stable vaccine platform.

The rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine maintains safety and efficacy in an RSV murine model

To evaluate the safety of the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine, mice were inoculated with the vaccine and monitored several times post-immunization for body weight loss, appearance, behavior, fever, and adverse effects at the injection site area. No altered clinical parameters were observed in rBCG-N-RSV-vaccinated mice until day 3 post-immunization, similar to the WT and placebo groups (data not shown). Efficacy was evaluated using an in vivo RSV challenge in immunized mice (Figure 3). Infected mice vaccinated with rBCG-N-RSV GMP showed a significant reduction (p < 0.0001) in the clinical score at 2 and 3 days post-infection (dpi) compared with the phosphate-buffered saline (PBS)/RSV group (Figure 4A). Immunized mice with the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine lost significantly less weight compared with unimmunized mice at 3 dpi (Figure S2). Moreover, the respiratory capacity, as measured by the enhanced paused (Penh) value, of the rBCG-N-RSV GMP/RSV group was significantly lower at days 2 and 3 dpi (p < 0.0001) compared with the PBS/RSV group. Although the BCG-WT/RSV group did not show a significant improvement in respiratory capacity compared with PBS/RSV mice, a trend was observed (Figure 4B). A similar protective effect of the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine was observed relative to the ratio of time to peak expiratory flow (Rpef) value, compared with the PBS/RSV group (Figure 4C). This finding was accompanied by a significant reduction in lung viral load of these mice compared with the PBS/RSV group (Figure 4D). Moreover, a significant increase in lung neutrophils was detected in the PBS/RSV group compared with uninfected mice at 3 dpi. At the same time, the rBCG-N-RSV GMP/RSV group exhibited significantly lower numbers of lung neutrophils compared with the PBS/RSV group (Figure 4E). Discussion

The findings presented in this study underscore the significant stability of the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine, highlighting its potential as a considerable approach against RSV in newborns and infants. Our data demonstrate the successful integration of biotechnology development and rigorous quality control standards, offering a strong foundation for future vaccine development initiatives.

The rBCG-N-RSV vaccine has been shown to elicit robust antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses in various animal models and humans, representing a promising and innovative solution for mitigating RSV-related disease during the winter season in the pediatric population.8,13 The BCG platform, traditionally used to combat TB in several countries, was further enhanced through genetic engineering to express the RSV nucleoprotein, providing a cost-effective and scalable approach to address two global health challenges: TB and RSV infection.11

Although rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccination did not completely abolish disease-associated parameters after RSV challenge, it significantly reduced viral burden, inflammatory cell infiltration, and lung pathology, indicating attenuated disease severity. This finding is relevant because protection against RSV may not necessarily require sterilizing immunity, but rather the prevention of severe lower respiratory tract disease.19 Nevertheless, the extent to which these reductions translate into clinically meaningful protection in humans remains to be determined in future clinical studies.

Notably, rBCG-N-RSV GMP is a lyophilized formulation, which significantly enhances its thermal stability during transportation and storage. This feature offers a significant advantage over other vaccines, such as inactivated or mRNA-based platforms, which typically require cold-chain logistics, thereby limiting their distribution in resource-constrained settings.20,21 Compared with inactivated vaccines, which may elicit weaker cellular responses and often require multiple doses accompanied by adjuvants, rBCG-N-RSV GMP induces strong, robust antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses, which are crucial for protection against RSV.11,12 In contrast to mRNA vaccines, which can cause transient systemic adverse events, such as fever, fatigue, and myalgia, particularly in younger individuals, the rBCG-N-RSV GMP platform utilizes a live attenuated organism with a long-standing history of use in humans. This may result in a different reactogenicity and safety profile; however, further studies are needed to comprehensively evaluate these characteristics across age groups.

Notably, the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine offers several practical and immunological advantages, particularly for the pediatric population. First, it is designed as a single-dose intervention, which is particularly valuable in infant immunization schedules where minimizing clinic visits and simplifying logistics are essential.22 Second, the long history of BCG use in neonates, including in low- and middle-income countries, provides a well-established framework for integrating it into existing immunization programs.19 Additionally, the vaccine’s ability to induce both humoral immunity and cellular responses early in life may offer protection during the most vulnerable months of infancy, when RSV-related morbidity and mortality are highest.7,8,13,23

A fundamental outcome of this study is the comprehensive validation of the vaccine’s quality and safety through adherence to GMP standards. The meticulous characterization of vaccine batches, including pH, residual moisture, vial integrity, and endotoxin levels, illustrates the importance of implementing stringent quality controls. Such guidelines, established by organizations such as the WHO, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and the European Medicines Agency (EMA), provide the foundation for rigorous assessments of critical quality attributes to investigational medicinal products.16 For example, validated assays, such as the limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) test, confirmed endotoxin levels well below the acceptable threshold, ensuring safety and compliance with international standards. Furthermore, the stability assessments conducted on the vaccine over the past decade reaffirm the formulation’s long-term viability, particularly critical for lyophilized formulations.14 Notably, the study’s use of advanced technologies aligns with International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) Q8 and Q9 guidelines, further enhancing batch-to-batch consistency.24

Additionally, one of the most relevant results was the vaccine’s genetic stability. Over ten years of monitoring, no evidence of plasmid instability or loss of function was observed in the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vial samples, underscoring the robustness of rBCG-N-RSV GMP. This stability ensures consistent expression of the RSV nucleoprotein, an antigen previously associated with inducing cellular immune responses against RSV in animal models and humans.25,26 Furthermore, the N protein is highly conserved across various circulating RSV strains, which enables this antigen to be highly immunogenic to the host, including cross-reactivity with the bRSV N protein.25,26

Our preclinical safety and in vivo immunological efficacy results confirm that the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine, administered as a single dose of 1 × 105 CFU, is both safe and immunogenic. These results are consistent with those previously reported, including testing of the GMP vaccine in mice and newborn calves, and in a phase 1 clinical trial conducted in healthy adult male volunteers vaccinated with the same dose of rBCG-N-RSV.7,11,12,14,27,28 Given the favorable safety and immunogenicity profiles observed with GMP rBCG-N-RSV, this vaccine is a promising candidate for further evaluation in pediatric populations.

Importantly, mice immunized with the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine exhibited lower clinical scores and less weight loss than the other groups after RSV challenge, as previously observed.11,14 In addition, pulmonary function studies (measuring Penh and Rpef) conducted by whole-body plethysmography showed that rBCG-N-RSV-vaccinated mice exhibited less pulmonary damage after RSV challenge compared with unvaccinated mice. This finding, along with a lower lung viral burden, suggests that this vaccine decreases disease severity caused by RSV infection in mice.

Additionally, we observed a significant reduction in lung-recruited neutrophils in mice vaccinated with rBCG-N-RSV GMP compared with those in the PBS/RSV group. This outcome is relevant, given that RSV causes severe lung disease in humans, specifically in newborns, infants, and the elderly population. Moreover, the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine elicited effective cell-mediated immunity, characterized by the early recruitment of effector and activated CD4+ T cells in the lungs. Finally, we observed significantly higher anti-N-RSV IgG titers at the systemic level than in the other groups. These results suggest that the vaccine promotes a Th1-type cellular and humoral response against RSV infection in mice, consistent with previous reports from our research group.14,16,27,29

Conclusion

The rBCG-N-RSV GMP preparations evaluated in this study are consistent and compliant with international regulatory standards. Furthermore, reproducible results across multiple preparations of rBCG-N-RSV GMP confirm the robustness of the manufacturing process and the vaccine’s stability, highlighting its quality, safety, immunogenicity, and reliability.

While the primary focus of the rBCG-N-RSV vaccine has been on the pediatric population due to RSV’s high burden in infants and young children, the favorable safety and immunogenicity profile observed in adult volunteers opens the possibility of studying this vaccine’s potential in other RSV-vulnerable populations, including pregnant women and the elderly. These characteristics may support a universal RSV vaccination strategy targeting multiple vulnerable groups.

The following steps involve conducting large-scale clinical trials to further evaluate the vaccine’s safety, immunogenicity, and efficacy across diverse populations and geographic regions. Regulatory alignment will be essential, particularly in countries targeted for future implementation. To this end, the formulation’s compliance with internationally recognized quality standards and its compatibility with immunization policies in low- and middle-income countries position rBCG-N-RSV GMP as a viable candidate for global RSV vaccination efforts.

This comprehensive validation underscores the potential of the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine for public health applications, particularly in regions with high RSV incidence and limited cold-chain infrastructure, thereby supporting its inclusion in national immunization programs.

Experimental model and study participant details

Animals

Initial colonies of wild-type BALB/c mice were purchased from The Jackson Laboratory, and mice were subsequently bred at the Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile facility. Female and Male mice aged 6–8 weeks were used for all experiments. Animals were housed in standard ventilated cages measuring 1422 × 522 × 1998 mm within a BSL-2 room under specific-pathogen-free conditions, maintained on a 12-h light/dark cycle, with controlled temperature and humidity, and provided with unrestricted access to food and water. The mice received environmental enrichment using autoclaved cardboard cones and absorbent paper. Mice were assigned to experimental groups according to the experimental design. Randomization into different experimental groups was performed once the mice entered the BSL-2 room, without distinction. No formal blinding procedures were used.

Cell lines

HEp-2 cells (American Type Culture Collection, ATCC; CCL-23; RRID: CVCL_1906) were used for respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) propagation assays. Cells were cultured in Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) (Gibco; cat#61100-061) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Capricorn Scientific; cat#FBS-HI-12A), 100 U/ml penicillin-streptomycin (Thermo Fisher Scientific; cat ·15140122) at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. HEp-2 cells tested negative for mycoplasma contamination.

Viruses and recombinant microorganisms

RSV Long strain was used for all infection experiments. Recombinant BCG expressing RSV nucleoprotein (rBCG-N-RSV) vaccine produced under Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) standards, and commercial WT BCG vaccine for human use (Serum Institute of India), were used in in vivo assays. All viral and bacterial stocks were routinely tested for lipopolysaccharide and Mycoplasma contamination.

Ethics statement

All experimental protocols were designed in accordance with the Sanitary Code of Terrestrial Animals of the World Organization for Animal Health (OIE, 24th Edition, 2015), the Chilean Animal Protection Law No. 20,800, and the ARRIVE guidelines. The animal protocols were reviewed and approved by the Scientific Ethical Committee for Animal and Environmental Care of the Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile (Protocol number #210810006).

Method details

Manufacture, quality control of rBCG-N-RSV cell banks, Bulk Drug Substance, and rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine vials

The rBCG-N-RSV Master Cell Bank (MCB) was generated in 2010 from a characterized recombinant Mycobacterium bovis BCG Danish 1331 clone expressing the RSV N protein, manufactured by IDT-Biologika (formerly the AERAS TB Vaccine Foundation, Rockville, MD, USA) under GMP conditions. The Working Cell Bank (WCB) was subsequently expanded from the MCB under GMP conditions. Bulk Drug Substance (BDS) was produced by large-scale expansion of the WCB, and the final Drug Product (DP) consisted of lyophilized vaccine vials formulated for storage and administration. The vaccine product (vials) was manufactured under GMP conditions in 2013. The following quality control tests, similar to the initial quality controls carried out at IDT-Biologika, were performed according to the manufacturer’s internal Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs): sterility, potency, bacterial purity, PCR identity, antigen expression, pH measurement, bacterial endotoxin, residual moisture, and inspection of vials and lyophilized cakes. MCB, WCB, and BDS are currently stored at −140 to −200 °C, and DP at −15 to - 25°C.

rBCG-N-RSV GMP DNA extraction

DNA was extracted from 5 μL of the rBCG-N-RSV MCB, WCB, and BDS vial stocks using the RBC BioSciences automated extractor MagCore. The RBC BioSciences 101 DNA extraction protocol was applied using the MagCore Genomic DNA Whole Blood Kit. To assess assay reproducibility and evaluate the potential presence of PCR inhibitors associated with high DNA concentrations or residual extraction components, 10−1 dilutions of each original DNA template were prepared and analyzed under identical experimental conditions. Consistent amplification profiles between undiluted and diluted samples were considered indicative of the absence of inhibition.

Detection of rBCG-N-RSV GMP genes

Specific Mycobacterium and RSV genes were detected in rBCG-N-RSV DNA samples (undiluted and 1/10 diluted) by Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR). The presence of 16S, IS6110, and pMV361-derived DNA sequences was detected with specific primers detailed in Table 1. Primers were resuspended to reach a concentration of 10 μM. The reaction included a positive control (rBCG-N-RSV vaccine DNA) and a negative control (genetic material from a human nasopharyngeal swab), to exclude potential buffer contamination with PCR templates and nonspecific amplification. The reaction mix was prepared using 1X PCRBIO HS Taq DNA polymerase Mix, 400 nM of each primer (forward and reverse), and nuclease-free water, following the manufacturer’s recommendations (PCRBIO HS Taq DNA Polymerase Protocol, PB10.21). DNA samples were added at a final concentration of 100 ng per reaction.

Protein extraction of rBCG-N-RSV GMP

Proteins were extracted from rBCG-N-RSV GMP samples (BDS (Lot 10-45C-003), rBCG-N-RSV WCB (Lot 10-45W-001), rBCG-N-RSV MCB (Lot 10-45M-001), and DP rBCG-N-RSV (Lot 13-603F-001). Each vaccine vial was resuspended in 1 mL of PBS-Tween 80 0.05%, centrifuged at maximum speed for 15 min at 4°C, and washed with PBS-Tween 80 0.05% and sterile PBS 1X. The pellet was resuspended in protein extraction buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl, 5 mM EDTA, 0.6% SDS) containing one tablet of protease inhibitor (#11697498001, cOmplete protease inhibitor cocktail, Roche) and sonicated for 6 pulses at 18–20 kHz. After sonication and centrifugation, supernatants were recovered and stored at −80°C. Extracted proteins were quantified by Micro BCA Protein Assay Kit (#23235, Thermo Fisher Scientific) for Western Blot analyses. Extracted proteins from the wild-type BCG strain Danish (nonrecombinant) (BCG-WT) were used as a negative control, and RSV N recombinant purified protein was used as a positive control (#40823-V07E, Sino Biological Europe GmbH). The Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile facilitated negative and positive controls for HPI (BCG-WT protein concentration: 500 ng/μL; volume: 100 μL; N-RSV protein concentration: 1.78 mg/mL; volume: 20 μL). Furthermore, purified recombinant RSV nucleoprotein (His-Tag) (#40823-V07E, Sino Biological Europe GmbH) was also tested in western blot assays as a positive control. Reconstitution of the RSV N protein was done following the manufacturer’s recommendations. One RSV N protein (Sino Biological) vial was resuspended in 400 μL of sterile water to prepare a stock solution of 0.25 mg/mL. Aliquots of 20 μL were stored at −80 °C until use.

Characterization of protein expression in rBCG-N-RSV GMP

A total of 20 μL of each sample at 1.5 mg/mL concentration was loaded on SDS-PAGE gels (including loading buffer). The positive control was loaded in a volume of 20 μL at a concentration of 0.3 mg/mL. After sample loading, gels were run at 90 V for 30 min, followed by 120 V for 60 min in a running buffer. After the run, the resolved gels were transferred to Immobilon PVDF membranes (Merck Millipore) using the conventional wet transfer technique. The damp transfer involved soaking the PVDF membrane in methanol and transfer buffer before sandwiching it with the gel and then performing an electric transfer for 90 min at 300 mA at 4°C to minimize the heat generated by the electrodes. PVDF membranes were stained with Ponceau Staining (0.1% w/v) to confirm appropriate protein transfer. Membranes were blocked with 10% skim milk in PBS-Tween 20, 0.1% for 1 h and subsequently incubated with primary murine antibody anti-N-RSV clone 1E9/D1 (1:500 dilution) in 3% PBS-BSA overnight at 4°C. Membranes were then washed three times with PBS-Tween 20 0.1% to remove unbound antibodies and incubated with Peroxidase AffiniPure Goat Anti-Mouse IgG (H + L) (#115-035-003, Jackson Immuno Research) as the secondary antibody (1:10,000 dilution) in 3% PBS-BSA for 1 h at room temperature. Afterward, the membranes were rinsed three times in PBS-Tween 20 (0.1%) and then developed using chemiluminescent reagents (ECL Plus, Amersham Bioscience, Buckinghamshire, UK). Protein bands were visualized and captured using an imaging system (ChemiDoc MP System, Bio-Rad, Los Angeles, CA, USA).

Potency and sterility test for rBCG-N-RSV GMP

The lyophilized rBCG-N-RSV vials were resuspended in 1 mL of PBS containing 0.05% Tyloxapol (PBS-Tyloxapol) using a 1 mL insulin syringe. Serial dilutions (1:10, 1:100, and 1:1,000) were prepared from the resuspended stock in PBS-Tyloxapol, with each dilution vortexed for 10 s to ensure homogeneity. 450 μL of PBS-Tyloxapol was added to each microtube to prepare the dilutions. For the 1/10 dilution, 50 μL from the resuspended stock was transferred to the microtube and vortexed for 10 s. For the 1/100 dilution, 50 μL from the 1/10 dilution was transferred to the respective microtube and vortexed for 10 s. For the 1/1,000 dilution, 50 μL from the 1/100 dilution was transferred to the respective microtube and vortexed for 10 s. The prepared microtubes were maintained on ice until they were plated for analysis. For each dilution (1/10, 1/100, and 1/1,000), 50 μL was plated in triplicate onto supplemented 7H10 agar (BD) or LB plates. Plates were incubated at 37°C and were examined after 48 h to assess potential contamination. After 21 days of incubation, colony counts were performed. The colony-forming unit (CFU) count was calculated using the following Equation 1, in which a volume correction factor was used to extrapolate the colony count to the volume used.(Equation 1)CFU=Colonycountonplate×Volumecorrectionfactor(20)×Dilutionfactor(10,100,or1,000)

The CFU count per dilution was averaged from triplicate samples, and the results were reported as CFU per milliliter (CFU/mL).

Bacterial endotoxin test for rBCG-N-RSV GMP

Bacterial endotoxin testing (BET) was performed using the Endosafe Portable Testing System (PTS), based on kinetic chromogenic detection of pyrogens using the kinetic D turbidimetric method. To begin, the operator entered the username and password, cartridge lot number, and calibration code. The sample ID, lot number, and corresponding dilution factor were then recorded. The diluted sample was loaded into the four reservoirs of the cartridge, where the reader mixed the sample with the Limulus Amebocyte Lysate (LAL) reagent in the sample wells and with the positive control in the Spike wells. The mixture was incubated at 37°C inside the reader, and after incubation, a chromogenic substrate was added to initiate the reaction. The optical density of the wells was measured and analyzed against a previously archived standard curve in the system. Assays were performed in duplicate, and results were averaged. The following acceptance criteria were established to validate the results: compensation coefficient of the archived curve > −0.980, coefficient of variation <25%, and peak recovery between 50% and 200%. LAL with a 1–0.01 EU/mL sensitivity was used to perform the analyses in two sample dilutions (1:100 and 1:1,000).

Vacuum leak test – Vial integrity and inspection of rBCG-N-RSV GMP vials

Using the vacuum leak test method, a container-closure integrity test was performed on rBCG-N-RSV GMP vials. First, a sample was placed in a tight evacuation test chamber with an external vacuum source. A suitable pump can quickly achieve a vacuum level between 50 and 100 microns. The vacuum system is isolated with the appropriate tools and allowed to stand for 15 to 30 min. The evacuation is considered complete if the micron level does not exceed 500 microns. If the container’s integrity under test has been compromised (i.e., leaking), pressure will be increased within the chamber. This may result in a) a coarse leak if the vacuum does not reach the preset level, b) a medium leak if the vacuum level drops below the preset level during the test, or c) a decay leak or microscopic leak if the vacuum level exceeds the decay limit during the test time.

pH measurement of rBCG-N-RSV GMP vials

The vials were allowed to reach room temperature before the process began. First, the pH meter was calibrated using buffer solutions with pH values of 7.00, 4.01, 10.01, and again 7.00. The sample was diluted to 1/100 and 1/1000 concentrations using water for injection. The vaccine and the dilution factor are transferred to Falcon vials. No vortex was used for mixing, but the tubes were gently shaken by hand. The electrode was dipped into the sample and gently shaken inside the tube to measure and record the pH value.

Vial moisture in rBCG-N-RSV GMP vials

Moisture analysis was performed using a moisture analyzer based on the thermogravimetric principle. First, the instrument determines the initial weight of the sample. The sample is then subjected to rapid heating by the integral halogen drying unit, which causes the vaporization of the moisture present in the sample. During the drying process, the analyzer continuously measures the sample’s weight and displays the result in terms of percentage moisture content (%MC). Before starting the measurement, the samples were allowed to stand until they reached room temperature. The sample pan was placed flat on the stand, and the analyzer lid was closed, automatically setting the equipment to zero. The fiberglass pan was moistened with the sample, and the lid was closed again. The analyzer automatically initiated the drying and measurement process, reporting the moisture content as a percentage (%MC).

Immunization and viral challenge assays in mice

Six-to eight-week-old female and male BALB/c mice were evenly distributed across all experimental groups: PBS, BCG-WT, and rBCG-N-RSV GMP. Mice were housed in a BSL-2 climate-controlled environment animal facility at the Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile. rBCG-N-RSV vaccine produced under Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) standards, and commercial WT BCG vaccine for human use (Serum Institute of India), were used in in vivo assays. Lyophilized vials of both vaccines were reconstituted in sterile saline solution (sterile 0.9% sodium chloride). Mice were subcutaneously (s.c.) vaccinated in a conscious state with a single dose of vaccine in the interscapular region with 100 μL containing 1 × 105 CFU of rBCG-N-RSV GMP. As a control, a group was vaccinated in the same manner with BCG-WT. The vehicle used for all formulations was sterile saline solution, of which 100 μL was also administered only in the placebo group.

Vaccine safety evaluation

To evaluate the safety of the rBCG-N-RSV GMP vaccine, mice were monitored 30 min post-immunization and at 24, 48, and 72 h post-inoculation. A clinical score considering body weight loss, appearance, behavior, and adverse effects in the injection site was generated according to the physiological parameters of Mus musculus (Table S1),11 with zero related to typical clinical signs and higher than zero scores corresponding to altered clinical signs, with a maximum final score of 13.

Evaluation of clinical and immunological efficacy

To evaluate the clinical and immunological efficacy of the vaccine, mice were anesthetized in an isoflurane (Baxter; cat#218-082) chamber with 5% oxygen and intranasally challenged at 28 days post-vaccination with 1 × 106 PFU of RSV Long strain harvested from infected HEp-2 cells as previously described.6,11 A non-infectious supernatant was used as a control and referred to as Mock. During the infection, mice were monitored daily, and visual disease parameters, including body weight loss, appearance, and behavioral alterations, were quantified and consolidated into a clinical score (Table S2). The sum of the infection-related clinical scores ranged from 0 (normal) to 10 (altered clinical signs) (Table S2). Additionally, whole-body plethysmography was performed to obtain the values of Enhanced Pause (Penh) and Ratio of time to peak expiratory time (Rpef). Finally, at 3 days post-infection, the mice were euthanized by lethal injection with ketamine (Richmond Vet Pharma; cat #144B) and xylazine (CENTROVET; cat #XI-49052201) (80 mg/kg and 4 mg/kg, respectively), and blood and lung samples were collected for the determination of immunogenicity and lung viral burden.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Viral load quantification by RT-qPCR

RNA was isolated and reverse-transcribed into cDNA as previously described.6 RT-qPCR was used to evaluate RNA copies of the RSV nucleoprotein gene using SYBR green kit and standard curves for qPCR generated using pTOPO-N-RSV or pTOPO-β-actin as templates. The data analyses were expressed as the number of RSV N transcripts per 5 x 103 copies of housekeeping gene β-actin.6 This assay quantifies RSV N transcripts and distinguishes viral genomic RNA.

Spectral flow cytometry analyses

Cellular infiltration and immune response during infection were measured by flow cytometry as described previously.11 Briefly, lung samples were disaggregated with collagenase IV, and red blood cells were lysed with ACK buffer (NH4Cl 0.15 M, KHCO3 0.01 M, Na2EDTA 0.1 mM; pH 7.2–7.4). Then, lung samples were stained with a viability dye diluted in PBS buffer and flow cytometry antibodies in PBS-BSA (0.5%)-EDTA (2 mM) buffer (Figure S1; Table S3) and acquired using a BD FACSDiscover S8 Cell Sorter (BD Biosciences). Analyses were performed using FlowJo software version 10.10.7 to quantify the percentage of neutrophils (CD45+CD11b+ CD11c− Ly6G+) and activated and effector T cells (CD45+CD3+ CD4+CD44+ and CD45+CD3+ CD4+CD69+, respectively) relative to total CD45+ cells for neutrophils and to total CD4+ cells for activated and effector T cells.

N-specific IgG quantification in serum

Total anti-N-RSV antibodies were evaluated 3 days post-infection in serum samples using an in-house indirect ELISA, as described previously. Briefly, serum samples were diluted from a 20-fold dilution to a 2,560-fold dilution in PBS with the addition of 5% nonfat dry milk and incubated on a 96-well plate previously coated with 100 ng of recombinant RSV nucleoprotein (GenScript) per well. A goat anti-mouse IgG (H + L) HRP-linked secondary antibody (ThermoFisher), diluted 1:2,000, was then used. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm after the addition of H2SO4 using an 800 TM TS Absorbance Reader (BioTek).

Statistical analyses

Results are shown as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical significance was determined by Ordinary One-Way ANOVA and Two-Way ANOVA, followed by Dunnett’s and Tukey’s post-hoc tests, respectively, using GraphPad Prism 10 software (GraphPad Software, Inc.); a p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Sample sizes are described in Figure 3, including PBS/Mock, PBS/RSV, rBCG-N-RSV GMP/RSV, and BCG WT/RSV groups.

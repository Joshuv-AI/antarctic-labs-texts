Single-Carrier Waveform Design for Joint Sensing and Communication
Ayoub Ammar Boudjelal, Rania Yasmine Bir, Hüseyin Arslan

Abstract
The emergence of 6G wireless networks demands solutions that seamlessly integrate communication and sensing. This letter proposes a novel waveform design for joint sensing and communication (JSAC) systems, combining single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing (SC-IFDM), a 5G communication candidate signal, with frequency modulated continuous wave (FMCW), widely used for sensing. The proposed waveform leverages the sparse nature of FMCW within SC-IFDM to achieve orthogonal integration in three steps: SC-IFDM symbols are allocated alongside the sparse FMCW, followed by the SC-IFDM transform into the time domain, and a cyclic prefix (CP) is applied in which phase shifts are introduced to the FMCW. Additionally, an enhanced channel estimation method is incorporated to boost system performance. Simulation results demonstrate the proposed waveform’s ability to deliver high-resolution sensing and superior communication performance, surpassing traditional multicarrier designs.

Introduction

By integrating communication and sensing functionalities, joint sensing and communication enables network nodes to not only transmit data but also perceive their environment, leading to improved system reliability, decision-making, and overall resource efficiency

Several signal designs have been presented to perform both sensing and communication, such as orthogonal frequency division multiplexing and orthogonal time-frequency space

THE PROPOSED WAVEFORM DESIGN

In this section, we model the single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing and frequency modulated continuous wave signaling and describe how the sparse representation of frequency modulated continuous wave integrates with single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing to create the proposed single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing-frequency modulated continuous wave waveform for joint sensing and communication systems. The proposed joint sensing and communication system performs dual functions, serving as a mono-static radar for sensing and a communication transmitter. Additionally, other separate systems in the environment may act as bi-static radars for sensing, as illustrated in Fig.
1

By transmitting the proposed single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing-frequency modulated continuous wave waveform, the system simultaneously detects radar echoes for sensing while transmitting data to a dedicated receiver, ensuring there is no interference between the sensing and communication functionalities.

II-D

Proposed JSAC Signal

To generate the proposed joint sensing and communication signal
X^{\text{comb}}(k,l)
, which integrates single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing and frequency modulated continuous wave signals in an orthogonal manner, a specific allocation is employed. First, the single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing signal is constructed by setting zeros at the indices where

, while the frequency modulated continuous wave signal is mapped to these zero positions. This ensures orthogonality between the two signals. The combined signal is defined as

X^{\text{comb}}(k,l)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\psi}}s^{\text{FMCW}}(l)\omega_{k}^{l},&\left[\frac{M}{2}+l-k\right]_{N}=0\\
X^{\text{SC-IFDM}}(k,l),&\text{ otherwise },\end{cases}

(8)

where
\boldsymbol{\psi}
is a design parameter representing the power assigned to frequency modulated continuous wave signal that will be used for sensing and also as pilots for channel estimation.
Next, the combined signal

is transformed into the time-domain signal
s^{\text{comb}}(l+nM)
using the inverse transform from (
2

FMCW Processing

Consider a radar channel that models
Q

and velocity

Communication channel

Consider a doubly selective
R
-tap channel, which can be expressed in discrete time domain as

h(p)=\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}h_{r}e^{j2\pi\frac{k_{r}(p-l_{r})}{MN}}\delta(p-l_{r}),

It is demonstrated that any delay or Doppler shift introduces a shift in both the data and chirp components. Since channel estimation relies on the chirp in the DFT domain, this causes interference between the data and chirp, ultimately degrading the accuracy of the channel estimation. The interfered chirp elements in the received signal,

\displaystyle Y_{dp}(k,l)=Y^{\text{FMCW}}(k,l)+\underbrace{Y^{\text{SC-IFDM}}(k,l)}_{\text{data to pilot interference}}+\underbrace{W(k,l)}_{\text{noise}},

(16)

with the received frequency modulated continuous wave signal written as

Y^{\text{FMCW}}(k,l)=\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\bar{h_{r}}e^{j2\pi\frac{l(k_{r}-l_{r})+l_{r}^{2}-k_{r}l_{r}}{MN}}e^{j2\pi\frac{l^{2}}{MN}}

where

Proposed Channel Estimation

Due to the behavior of chirps in doubly-selective channels, a novel channel estimation approach is needed. To separate data from the received frequency modulated continuous wave pilots, we introduce a transform that extracts every possible chirp in each
M

\hat{Y}_{1}(\beta_{1},\alpha_{1})=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{l=0}^{M-1}Y_{dp}(k,l)~e^{-j\pi\frac{(l-\alpha_{1}N-\beta_{1})}{MN}},

(19)

where
\hat{W}(\beta_{1},\alpha_{1})
is the transformed noise. Since the data consist of uniformly distributed QAM symbols with power
\sigma_{d}^{2}
and zero mean, the expectation

\mathbf{E}\left[\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}Y^{\text{SC-IFDM}}(\beta_{1},\alpha_{1})~e^{-j\pi\frac{(l-\alpha_{1}N-\beta_{1})}{MN}}\right]
is zero. The variance of

is given as

The estimated value

is approximately

and

. Therefore, in this particular case we consider an extra step to estimate the delay and Doppler shifts where two SC-IFDM symbols
are used, one will have an up-chirp and the other a down-chirp. Similarly, a received down-chirp can be given as

{Y^{\text{FMCW}}}^{\prime}(k,l)=\sum_{r=0}^{R-1}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\bar{h_{r}}e^{j2\pi\frac{l(k_{r}+l_{r})-l_{r}^{2}-k_{r}l_{r}}{MN}}e^{j2\pi\frac{-l^{2}}{MN}},

(21)

which also faces an ambiguity in differentiating delay and Doppler shifts in the case of

. However, using the same transform as in (

), we can derive the following

\begin{cases}k_{r}-l_{r}=\alpha_{1}N+\beta_{1}\\
k_{r}+l_{r}=\alpha_{2}N+\beta_{2}\end{cases},

(22)

where
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}
and
\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}
are the estimated shifts from the chirp in symbol 1 and symbol 2, respectively. For each
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}
, we check if a solution for

V
Conclusion

This paper presents a novel joint sensing and communication waveform, single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing-frequency modulated continuous wave, designed to support both communication and sensing. The approach combines single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing for high data rates and robust mobile performance with frequency modulated continuous wave chirp signals for high-resolution sensing, enabling seamless coexistence of both functionalities. The design ensures backward compatibility with existing radar and communication systems, facilitating integration into radar receivers without added complexity. It also addresses challenges like channel aging and interference through an enhanced channel estimation technique, ensuring reliable performance in dynamic environments. Our results show that single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing-frequency modulated continuous wave outperforms OFDM-FMCW in sensing accuracy and surpasses both OFDM-FMCW and OTFS-FMCW in bit error rate performance. The power ratio
\frac{\boldsymbol{\psi}}{\sigma_{d}^{2}}
is identified as a critical factor in balancing sensing and communication, highlighting the need for optimization. Future work will optimize waveform parameters, including power ratio, using AI to enhance system performance in real-world conditions. This research positions single-carrier interleaved frequency division multiplexing-frequency modulated continuous wave as a key technology for advancing communication and sensing integration in 6G.

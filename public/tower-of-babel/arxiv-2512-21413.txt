Convolution identities for complex-indexed divisor functions and modular graph functions
Ksenia Fedosova, Kim Klinger-Logan

Abstract
We find exact identities for sums of the form ∑ n 1 ∈ ℤ ∖ { 0 , n } n 1 + n 2 = n 𝒬 ⁡ ( n 1 , n 2 ) ​ σ − r 1 ​ ( n 1 ) ​ σ − r 2 ​ ( n 2 ) , \sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n_{1}+n_{2}=n}}{{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,n\}}}}\mathcal{Q}(n_{1},n_{2})\sigma_{-r_{1}}(n_{1})\sigma_{-r_{2}}(n_{2}), where n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} , r 1 , r 2 ∈ ℂ r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{C} , 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} is a combination of hypergeometric functions, and σ a ​ ( x ) \sigma_{a}(x) denotes the divisor function. Specifically, we find that they can be expressed in terms of Fourier coefficients of Hecke cusp forms weighted by their L L -values. This result expands upon previous work with Radchenko in which such identities were found for divisor functions with even integer index [ 9 ] and encompasses results of Jacobi [ 17 ] and Diamantis and O’Sullivan in [ 3 , 20 ] for divisor functions with odd integer index. The proof of our result expresses these sums in terms of Estermann zeta functions and uses trace formulae. In addition, we use a regularization of divergent convolution sums to provide a mathematical explanation for L L -values (non-critical in the sense of Deligne) appearing in modular graph functions [ 8 ] .

2.1
Divisor function

In

(47)

Moreover, we will need the following estimate for

2.2
Estermann zeta function

Let
a\in\mathbb{C}
and let
v

\ell
be co-prime integers such that

is not necessarily positive. The

is defined for

[

\begin{split}E\left(s;v/\ell,a\right)=\ell^{a-2s}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}=1}^{\ell}e(\frac{v}{\ell}m_{1}m_{2})\zeta(s-a,\frac{m_{1}}{\ell})\zeta(s,\frac{m_{2}}{\ell})\end{split}

where for

(51)

As a consequence of (

with poles possible only at

and

\begin{split}\text{Res}_{s=1}E(s;v/\ell,a)&=\zeta(1-a)\ell^{a-1},\\
\text{Res}_{s=1+a}E(s;v/\ell,a)&=\zeta(1+a)\ell^{-a-1}.\end{split}

(52)

Moreover, the Estermann zeta function satisfies the functional equation

\begin{split}E\left(s;v/\ell,a\right)=&\frac{1}{\pi}\Big(\frac{\ell}{2\pi}\Big)^{1+a-2s}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(1+a-s)\\
&\times\Big(\cos(\frac{\pi a}{2})E(1+a-s;\bar{v}/\ell,a)-\cos(\pi s-\frac{\pi a}{2})E(1+a-s;-\bar{v}/\ell,a)\Big),\end{split}

where

is the inverse of

(see
[
13
, (2.4)]

G_{+}(n_{1},n_{2})=G_{-}(n_{1},n_{2})=\,_{2}F_{1}(d+1,d+r_{1}+1;k;\tfrac{n}{n_{1}})=\frac{(-d-1)!\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma(k-d-1)}P^{(k-1,-r_{2})}_{-d-1}(1-2\tfrac{n}{n_{1}}).

(146)

Note that if we were to substitute
d\in-\mathbb{N}
into (

) directly, the first term
{\left(G_{+}-G_{-}\right)\cdot i^{k+1}\sin(\pi r_{2}/2)}
would vanish by (

. Additionally, the term involving

and

and

are odd.
Thus when

, it makes sense to rather consider the convolution sum multiplied by

.

We will impose further assumptions on the values of

to avoid the poles of

. In what follows, we consider how different terms in

behave under the limit

Remark 4.1
.

Interestingly, the approach above resembles the regularization techniques used in the evaluation of Mellin–Barnes-type integrals, particularly in the context of Feynman diagram calculations (cf.
[
24
]
):

\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{-i\infty}^{+i\infty}\cdots\int_{-i\infty}^{+i\infty}\frac{\prod_{i}\Gamma\left(a_{i}+b_{i}\varepsilon+\sum_{j}c_{ij}z_{j}\right)}{\prod_{i}\Gamma\left(a^{\prime}_{i}+b^{\prime}_{i}\varepsilon+\sum_{j}c^{\prime}_{ij}z_{j}\right)}\prod_{k}x_{k}^{d_{k}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}z_{l},

where the regularization of a single integral is obtained via the analytic continuation in the deformation parameter, cf. Lemma

4.1

Contribution from

In this section, we study how
\Gamma(1+\hat{d}(\varepsilon))Z^{(\hat{r}_{1}(\varepsilon),\hat{r}_{2}(\varepsilon))}_{\hat{d}(\varepsilon)}
behaves under the degeneration.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
r_{1},r_{2}>1
odd integers and
d<0
an integer,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}

\displaystyle\left(\Gamma(1+\hat{d}(\varepsilon))\cdot Z^{(\hat{r}_{1}(\varepsilon),\hat{r}_{2}(\varepsilon))}_{\hat{d}(\varepsilon)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{d+1}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}}{\Gamma(1+d+r_{2})\Gamma(1+d+r_{1}+r_{2})\Gamma(-d)}\zeta^{\prime}(1-r_{2})+\frac{(2\pi)^{-r_{2}}\zeta(1+r_{2})}{\Gamma(1+d+r_{1})}n^{-r_{2}}.

(147)

Proof.

Replacing
\alpha,\beta,d
by
\hat{r}_{1}(\varepsilon),\hat{r}_{2}(\varepsilon),\hat{d}(\varepsilon)
respectively in
Z^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{d}
as defined in (
9

becomes

\frac{(2\pi)^{r_{2}+\varepsilon}}{\Gamma(1+d+r_{2})\Gamma(1+d+r_{1}+r_{2}+\varepsilon)}\zeta(1-r_{2}-\varepsilon)\Gamma\left(1+d-\varepsilon\right).

(148)

Note that

\frac{(2\pi)^{r_{2}+\varepsilon}}{\Gamma(1+d+r_{2})\Gamma(1+d+r_{1}+r_{2}+\varepsilon)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\to}}\frac{(2\pi)^{r_{2}}}{\Gamma(1+d+r_{2})\Gamma(1+d+r_{1}+r_{2})},

(149)

and the value on the right is finite since the reciprocal of the gamma function is entire.
For an odd integer

(151)

∎

\begin{split}\Gamma(\hat{d}(\varepsilon)+1)&\left(G_{+}(\varepsilon)-G_{-}(\varepsilon)\right)=\delta_{n/n_{1}>1}\cdot 2\pi i\frac{\Gamma(2\hat{d}(\varepsilon)+\hat{r}_{1}(\varepsilon)+\hat{r}_{2}(\varepsilon)+2)}{\Gamma(\hat{r}_{2}(\varepsilon)+1)\Gamma(\hat{d}(\varepsilon)+\hat{r}_{1}(\varepsilon)+1)}(\tfrac{n_{2}}{n_{1}})^{\hat{r}_{2}(\varepsilon)}\\

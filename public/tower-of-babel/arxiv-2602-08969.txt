Symplectic excision and distance rigidity
Yoel Groman

Abstract
. We consider various notions of completeness in symplectic topology and ask two related questions. Does a complete open symplectic manifold remain complete after excising a subset? Can two sets be made arbitrarily far apart by adjusting the almost complex structure within an appropriate class of complete almost complex structures? We find rigidity phenomena when the excised set is a symplectic hypersurface. These arise from certain open Gromov-Witten invariants. We contrast this with flexibility that often occurs when the excised set is coisotropic. We also briefly touch on the opposite question of obstructions to existence of a complete symplectic structure compatible with a given complex structure. For the notion of completeness we first consider the traditional notion of geometric boundedness. We then introduce a broader notion of normalized completeness, related to the notion of intermittent boundedness of [ 5 ] , which depends on C 0 C^{0} properties and is a contractible condition. Finally we speculate about the relation to a Fukaya-categorical notion of completeness.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\omega)
be an open symplectic manifold and consider the space
\mathcal{J}
of smooth
\omega
-tame almost complex structures on
M
. Since
\omega
-tameness is a pointwise condition, the associated metrics
g_{J}
exhibit considerable flexibility. It is natural to ask whether any geometric features are common to all elements of
\mathcal{J}
. For this question to be productive, one needs to impose additional completeness conditions on the almost complex structures. The geometric features we then consider are distances between subsets of
M
. We find rigidity phenomena when one considers the distance to symplectic hypersurfaces which contrast with flexibility that often occurs when the excised set is coisotropic.

Remark 1.5

Before proceeding, we point out an elementary obstruction: if

That is, the theorems above are only non-trivial when the excised set is properly embedded and no-compact. However

1.2.
Flexibility of coisotropic excision

The rigidity phenomena above contrast sharply with flexibility results in the
coisotropic
setting. Let us consider two related mechanisms.

•

Symplectic excision.

In
[
13
,
8
]
one removes a closed coisotropic subset
Z\subset(M,\omega)
(e.g. a ray, or more generally a parametrized ray) and constructs a symplectomorphism

\Phi:M\setminus Z\longrightarrow M.

In particular, if
J
is a geometrically bounded
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure on
M
, then
\Phi^{*}J
is geometrically bounded on
M\setminus Z
.

•

Integrable anti-surgery.

[
7
]
construct a variety of examples of
complete embeddings
, i.e. excisions
Y:=M\setminus Z
where
Y
is
not
symplectomorphic to
M
but is nevertheless geometrically bounded and embeds symplectically
\iota:Y\hookrightarrow M
with good control at infinity.
These examples arise in particular from singular Lagrangian torus fibrations and an explicit modification procedure called
integrable anti-surgery
.

In both cases flexibility has a quantitative version: In the symplectic excision case the symplectomorphism
\Phi
can be chosen to be the identity outside an arbitrarily small prescribed neighborhood of
Z
. In particular, the distance from a prescribed compact set to the excised region can be made arbitrarily large while maintaining fixed bounds on the geometry. The case of integrable anti-surgery is similar in this respect. See
[
7
, Theorem 7.33]
for a precise statement.

Let us emphasize a key consequence: by excising a suitably chosen embedded coisotropic subset
Z
,
one can make the distance between
any two prescribed points
—or more generally between a prescribed point and a prescribed compact subset—
arbitrarily large
while maintaining fixed bounds on the geometry.

1.2.1.
A basic 4-dimensional example.

The following example taken from
[
7
, Remark 7.32]
illustrates the contrast between the rigidity and flexibility phenomena. Consider the complex surface

X:={\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{xy=1\},

equipped with the symplectic form

\omega=\operatorname{Im}\frac{dx\wedge dy}{xy-1}.

The manifold
X
admits a Lagrangian torus fibration (the Auroux fibration) with a single focus–focus singular fiber; see
[
1
, §5]
for a discussion. It is shown in
[
7
]
that
X
with this symplectic form is geometrically bounded.

Emanating from the focus–focus point, one sees a pair of dual configurations: on the one hand a configuration of two transversely intersecting properly embedded Lagrangian discs
L^{+},L^{-}
, lying over the monodromy-invariant line in the base of the Auroux fibration. On the other hand we have the degenerate cone given by the equation
xy=0
. It consists of a pair of properly embedded holomorphic copies of
{\mathbb{C}}
. This configuration projects to a properly embedded curve which is transverse to the monodromy-invariant line in the base.

It is shown in
[
7
]
that Integrable anti-surgery allows one to
excise either Lagrangian tail while preserving geometric boundedness
; in fact, excising one tail recovers the cotangent bundle
T^{*}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. By contrast, the present paper shows that
excising components of the degenerate cone obstructs geometric boundedness.

From the point of view of smooth topology the two configurations are indistinguishable. In fact, they are related by hyperkähler rotation. The difference is therefore genuinely symplectic.
As we shall see, the obstruction to excising a component
C_{-}
of the cone comes from the fact that the other component
C_{+}
gives rise to Maslov index
2
holomorphic discs linking certain Lagrangian torus fibers with
C_{-}
; see
[
1
, §5, Fig. 2]
for pictures and an explicit discussion of the relevant torus fibers.
The Lagrangian configuration looks similar. Thinking of
X
as a Weinstein domain, the tail
L_{+}
, say, serves as a stable manifold for some Liouville flow. The skeleton of that flow is a Lagrangian torus with a Lagrangian disk attached to it, living on the opposite tail
L_{-}
. But this configuration exhibits flexibility since there is no a priori bound on the diameter of a Lagrangian disk.

Remark 1.8
.

In the example above,
X\setminus C_{-}
contains the punctured holomorphic curve
C_{+}
. One might wonder whether the obstruction is explained, forgoing holomorphic curves, simply by the appearance of a properly embedded symplectic surface whose end has finite area when enclosed by loops.
However this phenomenon alone is not an obstruction: such symplectic surfaces can occur inside geometrically bounded symplectic manifolds.
For example, in the cotangent model
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
with
\omega=dp_{1}\wedge dq_{1}+dp_{2}\wedge dq_{2}
, take a properly embedded curve
\gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
which is asymptotic to the coordinate axes and has finite total
p_{1}
-variation along one end; then the surface

S:=\{(p,q)\mid p\in\gamma,\ q_{2}=0,\ q_{1}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{1}\}

is symplectic and has an end of finite symplectic area yet the ambient manifold is geometrically bounded.

1.3.
Holomorphic-curve mechanism and the surface case

As already indicated, the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
rely on hard holomorphic curve arguments. As is typical for results of this type, the phenomenon is elementary in dimension
2n=2
, and persists in higher dimensions due to holomorphic-curve mechanisms.
Let us expand on this point.

If
p\in D\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a point inside a disk, then
D\setminus\{p\}
has finite area, so any complete metric on
D\setminus\{p\}
must have unbounded geometry.
At the same time, there is also a holomorphic-curve viewpoint:
\partial D
is a Lagrangian circle, and
D
is a
J
-holomorphic Maslov index
2
disk with nonvanishing open Gromov–Witten invariant; confinement estimates then yield distance bounds.
This latter mechanism persists in higher dimensions and underlies Theorem
1.1
.

Consider now Proposition
1.3
in the
n=1
case. One might try to vary
J
so that
g_{J}
is stretched in the
{\mathbb{R}}
-direction and compressed in the circle direction, in order to make the distance between two circle fibers as large as possible. It is elementary to see that if one insists on a uniform sectional curvature bound, such stretching forces the circle fibers to become very short somewhere along the way, and hence forces the injectivity radius to become small.

But there is also a holomorphic-curve mechanism. One can create a Maslov index
2
disk with boundary on one of the circle fibers by a boundary reduction, or, symplectic cutting, at the other end. The resulting open Gromov–Witten invariant again yields a distance bound. The boundary reduction is why in higher dimensions the result involves the distance between a Lagrangian fiber and a real hypersurface.

This suggests the question:

Question 3
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.2
, fix two distinct torus fibers
L_{p},L_{q}\subset U\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.
Is there an analogue of Proposition
1.3
giving an
a priori
upper bound on
d_{g_{J}}(L_{p},L_{q})
in terms of a symplectic/flux quantity depending only on the straight-isotopy flux
p-q\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}})
(equivalently, an integral-affine notion of distance between
p
and
q
)?

One is naturally led to consider open Gromov–Witten invariants defined by counting
J
-holomorphic
annuli
with boundary on
L_{p}\cup L_{q}
and with varying modulus.
For primitive boundary classes, one can show (by a Gromov compactness argument) that an appropriate
0
-dimensional moduli space defines a well-defined invariant.
In dimension
n=1
, this annulus invariant equals
1
.
Unfortunately, in higher dimensions the same invariant vanishes identically (since it vanishes when the flux is non-positive).

Finally, since the holomorphic-curve input behind Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is inherently codimension
2
, it is natural to ask:

Question 4
.

Do analogues of Theorem
1.1
hold for higher-codimension symplectic submanifolds?

The moduli spaces of Maslov 2 J-holomorphic discs we have been considering span relative cycles of virtual dimension
n+1
. Thus, generically they can be made to avoid real codimension 4 sets, at least in the semi-positive setting. This suggests that if there are any rigidity phenomena in higher codimension, they would require new ideas.

1.4.
Completeness in symplectic topology

The obstructions to geometric boundedness in Question
1
come from
J
-holomorphic curves with finite energy connecting distant compact subsets. This suggests viewing geometric boundedness as a symplectic counterpart of geodesic completeness
[
9
]
, with
J
-holomorphic curves playing the role of geodesics.

However, geometric boundedness has drawbacks: it involves curvature (second derivatives), and the space of geometrically bounded structures may not be contractible or even connected. At the other extreme, one can consider the weakest completeness property:

Definition 1.9

(
J
-completeness)
.

An
\omega
-tame almost complex structure
J
on a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is said to be
J
-complete
if the associated Riemannian metric
g_{J}
is complete and for every compact subset
K\subset M
and every
E>0
there exists
R>0
such that any connected
J
-holomorphic curve whose boundary meets both
K
and
M\setminus B_{R}(K)
has energy at least
E
.

This is stronger than metric completeness but too weak to control the topology of the space of
J
-complete structures.

We now consider an intermediate notion which is of a
C^{0}
nature and which gives rise to a contractible condition. It is closely related to that of intermittent boundedness which was introduced in
[
5
]
. For each
J
, we construct in §
3
an intrinsically defined continuous
scale function

\rho_{J}:M\to(0,+\infty]
measuring the isoperimetric scale at each point. The
normalized distance

d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
measures path lengths in units of
\rho_{J}
:

L_{\mathrm{norm},J}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{J}(\gamma(t))\,|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{g_{J}}\,dt,

with
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
the induced path metric.

Definition 1.10

(Normalized completeness)
.

An
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure
J
is
normalized complete
if the metric space
(M,d_{\mathrm{norm},J})
is complete.

Remark 1.11
.

In this definition and Theorem
1.12
we focus on the
\omega
-compatible case. The statements are true for the
\omega
-tame case with a very slight adjustment. See Remark
3.9
.

Theorem 1.12

(Basic properties of normalized completeness)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold.

(1)

Connectedness.

The space of normalized complete
\omega
-compatible almost complex structures is path connected.

(2)

Confinement and
J
-completeness.

If
J
is normalized complete, then
J
is
J
-complete (Definition
1.9
).
More precisely, any connected
J
-holomorphic curve
u:(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)\to(M,\omega)
satisfies a confinement estimate of the form

u(\Sigma)\subset B^{d_{\mathrm{norm},J}}_{\;C\,E(u)+C^{\prime}}\bigl(u(\partial\Sigma)\bigr),

where
E(u)=\int_{\Sigma}u^{*}\omega
and the constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
are universal constants.

(3)

Equivalence to standard metric under uniform bounds.

If
g_{J}
has uniformly bounded sectional curvature and uniformly positive injectivity radius, then
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
and
d_{g_{J}}
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent. In this case normalized completeness is equivalent to metric completeness of
g_{J}
.

Proofs are given in §
3
.

Remark 1.13

(weak contractibility)
.

The space of normalized complete almost complex structures is path connected, but not necessarily weakly contractible as a
topological space
. Rather, it can be given the structure of an
\infty
-groupoid
which is contractible. Namely one restricts to certain
admissible
paths modeled on the zig–zag construction in the proof of Theorem
1.12
(1); see
[
5
]
for the analogous framework with uniform intermittent boundedness.

Remark 1.14

(
C^{0}
stability)
.

The notion of normalized completeness is stable under uniform
C^{0}
perturbations, after a slight adjustment. Namely, the scale function
\rho_{J}
depends on the choice of cutoff constant
c_{0}
in the isoperimetric ratio. Changing
c_{0}
does not produce a uniformly equivalent normalized metric in general, but all the properties of Theorem
1.12
are satisfied with any cutoff constant. If
J
is normalized complete with respect to a cutoff constant
c_{0}
, nearby almost complex structures are normalized complete with respect to a bigger cutoff constant
c_{1}
. See §
3.5
for a full discussion.

(Reformulation with normalized completeness)

1.5.
Fukaya-categorical completeness

So far we have considered completeness from a geometric point of view.
One can also consider completeness in terms of moduli spaces of objects in the Fukaya category. That is, completeness in the sense that there are no
missing objects
.

Recall the star-shape
Sh^{*}(L)\subset H^{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})
of a Lagrangian
L
, defined using star-isotopies (Lagrangian isotopies whose flux develops linearly in time); see
[
11
,
3
]
. It can be thought of as a tropicalization of a moduli space of objects of the Fukaya category which are connected to
L
.
The following proposition is proved at the end of §
2.2
.

Proposition 1.17
.

Assume
(M,\omega)
satisfies the same hypotheses as in Theorem
1.1
:
M
is geometrically bounded,
H_{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is finitely generated, and
\omega
is rational.
Let
N
be a properly embedded symplectic hypersurface.
For each
p\in N
, there exists a sufficiently small open neighborhood
U
of
p
, a Lagrangian
L\subset U
, and a class
\alpha\in\pi_{2}(M,L)
with
\alpha\cdot N=1
, such that the following holds.
Let
Sh^{*}(L;M\setminus N)\subset H^{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})
be the star-shape of
L
relative to
M\setminus N
.
Then
Sh^{*}(L;M\setminus N)
is contained in the open affine half-space

\bigl\{\,\mathfrak{f}\in H^{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})\ \big|\ \mathfrak{f}\cdot\partial\alpha>-\omega(\alpha)\,\bigr\},

and the boundary hyperplane
\{\mathfrak{f}\cdot\partial\alpha=-\omega(\alpha)\}
is approached by star-fluxes realized by Lagrangian tori contained in
M\setminus N
.
In particular, the boundary of
Sh^{*}(L;M\setminus N)
contains a nontrivial open subset annihilated by
\partial\alpha
.

This shows an additional defect that occurs when one removes a symplectic divisor: some boundary fluxes in the star-shape are “missing”, in the sense that they cannot be realized either by a Lagrangian or by a
J
-holomorphic disc. The disc can be seen as encoding some compactification of the moduli space. By contrast, the proof of geometric boundedness of integrable anti-surgery in
[
7
]
proceeds, in effect, by showing that some relevant star-shapes are all of
H^{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})
.

This suggests that the geometric notion of normalized completeness is closely linked with some notion of completeness of moduli spaces of objects in the Fukaya category.

Question 6
.

In a normalized-complete symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, is it always the case that every boundary segment of the star-shape

(for a given Lagrangian

J
-holomorphic disc?

Proofs

2.1.
Energy confinement for
J
-holomorphic curves

Throughout, if
u:(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)\to(M,\omega)
is a
J
-holomorphic curve with (possibly empty) boundary, its energy is

E(u):=\int_{\Sigma}u^{*}\omega\geq 0.

The following confinement lemmas are stated for
\omega
-compatible
J
. At the end of this subsection we note that both hold for
\omega
-tame
J
under the uniform tameness condition from the footnote to the definition of geometric boundedness.

Lemma 2.1

(Sikorav’s confinement estimate)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold and let
J
be an
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure such that the associated metric
g_{J}(\cdot,\cdot):=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
is complete and has uniformly bounded sectional curvature and positive injectivity radius (i.e.
J
is geometrically bounded).
Then there exist a constant
r_{0}>0
depending on the geometry and a universal constant
C>0
such that for every connected
J
-holomorphic curve
u
one has

u(\Sigma)\subset B_{r_{0}\,\lceil E(u)/(Cr_{0}^{2})\rceil}\bigl(u(\partial\Sigma)\bigr),

where
B_{r}(\cdot)
denotes the closed
g_{J}
-ball of radius
r
about a subset and
\lceil\cdot\rceil
is the ceiling function.

Proof.

See
[
12
, Proposition 4.4.1]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Improved confinement)
.

In the setting of Lemma
2.1
, with the same
r_{0}
and
C
one has

u(\Sigma)\subset B_{\rho}\bigl(u(\partial\Sigma)\bigr),\qquad\rho:=\max\bigl(2CE(u)/r_{0},\,\sqrt{CE(u)}\bigr).

Proof.

The argument is the same as for Sikorav’s lemma. Both rely on a monotonicity inequality: if a
J
-holomorphic curve meets a ball of radius
r\leq r_{0}
, then the energy in that ball is at least
cr^{2}
for a universal constant
c>0
.

If
E(u)/(cr_{0}^{2})\leq 1
, we apply the monotonicity inequality directly: the image cannot meet a ball of radius greater than
\sqrt{E(u)/c}
(since
E(u)\geq cr^{2}
implies
r\leq\sqrt{E(u)/c}
), so
u(\Sigma)
is contained in a ball of radius
\sqrt{E(u)/c}
.

If
E(u)/(cr_{0}^{2})>1
, take the maximum number
n
of disjoint balls of radius
r_{0}
that the image of
u
can meet. Then
n\leq E(u)/(cr_{0}^{2})
, so the image is contained in a ball of radius
(n+1)r_{0}\leq r_{0}(1+E(u)/(cr^{2}_{0}))<2E(u)/(cr_{0})
about
u(\partial\Sigma)
.

So we take
C=1/c
.

∎

For

J
.
Suppose for contradiction that there exists a symplectic form
\omega
on
M
such that
J
is
\omega
-compatible and geometrically bounded.

Let
s_{D}
be the canonical holomorphic section of
\mathcal{O}(D)
whose zero locus is
D
.
Let
C\subset X
be a curve not contained in
D
with
C\cdot D>0
. Then for any
t\neq 0
the curve
s_{D}(C)
is a curve in
M
which is not contained in the zero section. As in the previous proof, fiberwise scalar multiplication
m_{t}
on
\Tot(\mathcal{O}(D))
is biholomorphic, hence the curves
C_{t}:=m_{t}(s_{D}(C))
are
J
-holomorphic with symplectic area independent of
t
. They pass through the zero section for all
t
(at the zeros of
s_{D}(C)
), while also escaping every compact subset as
t\to\infty
(at a point where
s_{D}(C)\neq 0
). Therefore
\diam_{g_{J}}(C_{t})\to\infty
. This contradicts the confinement estimate (Lemma
2.2
).

∎

3.
Normalized completeness

3.1.
An isoperimetric scale

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold and let
J
be an
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure.
Write

g_{J}(\cdot,\cdot):=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)

for the associated Riemannian metric, and write
\ell_{g_{J}}(\gamma)
for the
g_{J}
-length of a piecewise smooth loop
\gamma\subset M
.

Definition 3.1

(Isoperimetric ratio on a ball)
.

For
p\in M
and
r>0
define

\mathcal{I}_{J}(p;r):=\sup_{\gamma}\;\inf_{u}\;\frac{\int_{D}u^{*}\omega}{\ell_{g_{J}}(\gamma)^{2}}\ \in[0,+\infty],

where the supremum ranges over all
contractible
piecewise smooth loops
\gamma\subset B^{g_{J}}_{r}(p)
with
\ell_{g_{J}}(\gamma)>0
, and the infimum ranges over all
smooth
discs
u:(D,\partial D)\to(B^{g_{J}}_{r}(p),\gamma)
with
\partial u=\gamma
.

Fix once and for all a cutoff constant
c_{0}:=\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Definition 3.2
.

For
p\in M
let

r_{\mathrm{iso},J}(p):=\sup\Bigl\{\,r\in[0,+\infty)\ \Big|\ \mathcal{I}_{J}(p;r)\leq c_{0}\,\Bigr\}

and define the
isoperimetric scale

\rho_{J}(p):=\min\{1,\,r_{\mathrm{iso},J}(p)\}\in[0,1].

Remark 3.3
.

The constant
c_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}
is an arbitrary fixed numerical cutoff. The effect of changing
c_{0}
is discussed below.

Lemma 3.4

(Properties of the scale function)
.

The function
\rho_{J}:M\to[0,1]
is positive everywhere and
1
-Lipschitz with respect to
d_{g_{J}}
.
In particular,
\rho_{J}
is bounded away from
0
on compact subsets.

Proof.

Positivity follows from the fact that inside a small enough geodesic ball one has curvature bounds, hence a quadratic isoperimetric inequality for filling contractible loops by discs; see
[
10
, Theorem 4.4.1]
.

Fix
p,q\in M
and set
\delta:=d_{g_{J}}(p,q)
. Then for every
r>\delta
one has the inclusion
B^{g_{J}}_{r-\delta}(q)\subset B^{g_{J}}_{r}(p)
. By Definition
3.1
, this implies

\mathcal{I}_{J}(q;r-\delta)\ \leq\ \mathcal{I}_{J}(p;r).

Consequently, if
\mathcal{I}_{J}(p;r)\leq c_{0}
, then
\mathcal{I}_{J}(q;r-\delta)\leq c_{0}
, hence

r_{\mathrm{iso},J}(q)\geq r_{\mathrm{iso},J}(p)-\delta.

By symmetry we also obtain
r_{\mathrm{iso},J}(p)\geq r_{\mathrm{iso},J}(q)-\delta
, so

|r_{\mathrm{iso},J}(p)-r_{\mathrm{iso},J}(q)|\leq d_{g_{J}}(p,q).

This proves the claim, and hence the same for
\rho_{J}=\min\{1,r_{\mathrm{iso},J}\}
.
∎

3.2.
Normalized length and normalized completeness

Definition 3.5

(Normalized length and distance)
.

Let
J
be
\omega
-compatible and let
\rho_{J}
be as in Definition
3.2
.
For a piecewise smooth path
\gamma:[0,1]\to M
define its
normalized length
by

L_{\mathrm{norm},J}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{J}(\gamma(t))\,|\dot{\gamma}(t)|_{g_{J}}\,dt.

The induced path metric is denoted
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
.
Equivalently,
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
is the distance associated to the conformal metric
\widetilde{g}_{J}:=\rho_{J}^{2}\,g_{J}
.

Definition 3.6

(Normalized completeness)
.

An
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure
J
is
normalized complete
if the metric space
(M,d_{\mathrm{norm},J})
is complete.

Lemma 3.7

(Closed bounded balls are compact)
.

If
J
is normalized complete, then for any basepoint
p_{0}\in M
and any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the closed
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
-ball
K_{n}:=\overline{B}^{d_{\mathrm{norm},J}}_{\,n}(p_{0})
is compact in the manifold topology.

Proof.

Since
\rho_{J}
is continuous and positive, we can approximate it by a smooth positive function
\widetilde{\rho}_{J}
such that the ratio
\rho_{J}/\widetilde{\rho}_{J}
is uniformly bounded away from
0
and
+\infty
.
The smooth Riemannian metric
\widehat{g}_{J}:=\widetilde{\rho}_{J}^{2}g_{J}
is then uniformly equivalent to
\widetilde{g}_{J}=\rho_{J}^{2}g_{J}
, hence the distances
d_{\widehat{g}_{J}}
and
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent.
Since
(M,d_{\mathrm{norm},J})
is complete,
(M,d_{\widehat{g}_{J}})
is also complete.
Each closed ball
K_{n}=\overline{B}^{d_{\mathrm{norm},J}}_{\,n}(p_{0})
is bounded with respect to
d_{\widehat{g}_{J}}
(by uniform equivalence), and since
\widehat{g}_{J}
is smooth, the Hopf–Rinow theorem implies that closed bounded sets are compact.
Thus each
K_{n}
is compact in the manifold topology.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.12

3.3.1.
Connectedness (Theorem
1.12
(1))

Proof.

The proof of connectedness is precisely the zigzag construction in
[
5
, Theorem 4.7]
.

Denote by
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{comp}}_{\omega}
the space of smooth
\omega
-compatible almost complex structures with the
C^{\infty}
topology and by
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{nc,comp}}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{comp}}_{\omega}
the subspace of normalized complete structures.
Assume
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{nc,comp}}_{\omega}\neq\emptyset
.
Without loss of generality assume that
M
is connected and fix a basepoint
p_{0}\in M
.
Given
J_{0},J_{1}\in\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{nc,comp}}_{\omega}
, consider the closed normalized balls

B_{n}^{0}:=\overline{B}^{d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{0}}}_{\,n}(p_{0}),\qquad B_{n}^{1}:=\overline{B}^{d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{1}}}_{\,n}(p_{0}),\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

By Lemma
3.7
, each of these balls is compact.
By construction of the normalized distance, successive balls in each sequence are at normalized distance at least
1
:

(3)

d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{0}}\bigl(B_{n}^{0},\ M\setminus B_{n+1}^{0}\bigr)\geq 1,\qquad d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{1}}\bigl(B_{n}^{1},\ M\setminus B_{n+1}^{1}\bigr)\geq 1,\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We choose inductively increasing subsequences
n_{1}^{0}<n_{2}^{0}<\cdots
and
n_{1}^{1}<n_{2}^{1}<\cdots
in
{\mathbb{N}}
with the following nesting property: for each
i\geq 1
,

B_{n_{i}^{0}+1}^{0}\ \subset\ B_{n_{i}^{1}}^{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{n_{i}^{1}+1}^{1}\ \subset\ B_{n_{i+1}^{0}}^{0}.

(So the ball of radius
n_{i}^{0}+1
for
J_{0}
is contained in the ball of radius
n_{i}^{1}
for
J_{1}
, and the ball of radius
n_{i}^{1}+1
for
J_{1}
is contained in the ball of radius
n_{i+1}^{0}
for
J_{0}
.)
Such subsequences exist because both sequences exhaust
M
as
n\to\infty
.
For each
i\geq 1
, set
V_{i}^{0}:=\operatorname{Int}B_{n_{i+1}^{0}}^{0}\setminus B_{n_{i}^{0}}^{0}
and
V_{i}^{1}:=\operatorname{Int}B_{n_{i+1}^{1}}^{1}\setminus B_{n_{i}^{1}}^{1}
.
These are pairwise disjoint shells escaping to infinity, and each has normalized transverse width at least
1
with respect to the corresponding
J_{0}
or
J_{1}
by (
3
).

We choose a smooth path
J_{s}
,
s\in[0,1]
, in
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{comp}}_{\omega}
from
J_{0}
to
J_{1}
(through
\omega
-compatible structures) which satisfies:

J_{s}\equiv J_{0}\ \text{on }\bigcup_{i}V_{i}^{0}\ \text{for }s\in[0,2/3],\qquad J_{s}\equiv J_{1}\ \text{on }\bigcup_{i}V_{i}^{1}\ \text{for }s\in[1/3,1].

We claim that each
J_{s}
is normalized complete.
Indeed, fix
s\in[0,1]
. Then at least one of the two collections of shells,

\{V_{i}^{0}\}
(if
s\leq 2/3
) or
\{V_{i}^{1}\}
(if
s\geq 1/3
),
consists of shells on which
J_{s}
agrees with a normalized complete structure (either
J_{0}
or
J_{1}
) and hence has normalized transverse width
\geq 1
.
Any
d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{s}}
-Cauchy sequence cannot cross infinitely many such shells (each crossing costs at least
1
), so it is eventually contained in some
B_{n_{i}^{0}}^{0}
or
B_{n_{i}^{1}}^{1}
, which is compact in the manifold topology.
Since
d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{s}}
induces the manifold topology (it is the length metric of a continuous conformal factor
\rho_{J_{s}}^{2}g_{J_{s}}
with
\rho_{J_{s}}>0
), Cauchy sequences in a compact set converge.
Thus
(M,d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{s}})
is complete.
This shows in particular that
\mathcal{J}^{\mathrm{nc,comp}}_{\omega}
is path connected.

.
∎

3.3.2.
Normalized confinement and
J
-completeness (Theorem
1.12
(2))

Proof.

The proof is an adjustment to Sikorav’s confinement argument
[
12
, Proposition 4.4.1]
, with the bounded-geometry radius replaced pointwise by the scale function
\rho_{J}
coming from the local quadratic isoperimetric inequality
\mathcal{I}_{J}\leq c_{0}
.

Fix
p\in M
and set
r:=\rho_{J}(p)\in(0,1]
.
By definition of
r_{\mathrm{iso},J}
and the choice
c_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}
, every contractible loop
\gamma\subset B^{g_{J}}_{r}(p)
has a smooth filling
u
with
\int_{D}u^{*}\omega\leq c_{0}\,\ell_{g_{J}}(\gamma)^{2}
.
The standard monotonicity estimate for
J
-holomorphic curves (see
[
12
, Proposition 4.3.1]
) therefore applies on the ball
B^{g_{J}}_{r}(p)
and gives: if
u
is a
J
-holomorphic curve whose image meets
p
and whose boundary is disjoint from
B^{g_{J}}_{r}(p)
, then

(4)

E\bigl(u;\ u^{-1}(B^{g_{J}}_{r}(p))\bigr)\ \geq\ \frac{r^{2}}{2c_{0}}\ =r^{2}.

Let
u:(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)\to(M,\omega)
be a connected
J
-holomorphic curve, and set
A:=u(\partial\Sigma)
.
Choose a maximal collection of points
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\in\Sigma
satisfying the separation property

(5)

d_{g_{J}}(u(p_{i}),u(p_{j}))\ \geq\ \rho_{J}(u(p_{i}))+\rho_{J}(u(p_{j}))\qquad\text{for all }i\neq j.

Set
a_{i}:=\rho_{J}(u(p_{i}))\in(0,1]
for
i=1,\dots,N
.
The separation condition (
5
) implies that the balls
B^{g_{J}}_{a_{i}}(u(p_{i}))
are pairwise disjoint.
Applying the monotonicity estimate (
4
) to each ball and summing over
i
yields
E(u)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}^{2}
.

For each
i
, the
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
-diameter of the ball
B^{g_{J}}_{a_{i}}(u(p_{i}))
is at most
4a_{i}^{2}
: at any point of that ball the scale is at most
2a_{i}
(the scale function is
1
-Lipschitz), so the normalized diameter is at most
4a_{i}^{2}
.
By maximality of
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\}
, the
g_{J}
-distance from
u(q)
to the union of these balls is at most
\rho_{J}(u(q))
for every
q\in\Sigma
; hence the normalized distance from
u(q)
to that union is at most twice the scale at
u(q)
, and in particular at most
2
.
We can therefore estimate the distance between any two points in
u(\Sigma)
by their normalized distances to this union plus the sum of the diameters of the balls along a connecting path.
For any
p\in u(\Sigma)
, the normalized distance to the boundary
A=u(\partial\Sigma)
thus satisfies

(6)

d_{\mathrm{norm},J}(p,A)\ \leq\ 4\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}^{2}\ +\ 4\ \leq\ 4(E(u)+1).

∎

In the formulation of Theorem
1.12
(2) we only dealt with the larger energy regime. Here we record a localized version which includes also the low-energy regime.

Lemma 3.8

(Normalized local confinement)
.

Let
J
be an
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure that is normalized complete.
There exist universal constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for every connected
J
-holomorphic curve
u:(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)\to(M,\omega)
,

u(\Sigma)\ \subset\ B^{d_{\mathrm{norm},J}}_{\rho}\bigl(u(\partial\Sigma)\bigr),\qquad\rho\ :=\ \max\bigl(C_{1}\,E(u),\,C_{2}\,\sqrt{E(u)}\bigr).

In particular, as
E(u)\to 0
the
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
-diameter of
u(\Sigma)
tends to zero. Moreover, this containment is unaffected by the behavior of
J
outside the normalized ball of radius
\rho
.

Proof.

We revisit the end of the proof of Theorem
1.12
(2). With the same maximal collection
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}
and
a_{i}=\rho_{J}(u(p_{i}))
, the normalized distance between any two points
q_{1},q_{2}\in u(\Sigma)
is at most

d_{\mathrm{norm},J}\Bigl(q_{1},\,\bigcup_{i}B^{g_{J}}_{a_{i}}(u(p_{i}))\Bigr)\ +\ \text{(sum of diameters)}\ +\ d_{\mathrm{norm},J}\Bigl(q_{2},\,\bigcup_{i}B^{g_{J}}_{a_{i}}(u(p_{i}))\Bigr).

As observed there, the normalized distance from any point
q\in u(\Sigma)
to the union of the balls
B^{g_{J}}_{a_{i}}(u(p_{i}))
is at most twice the scale at
q
; and the
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}
-diameter of each ball is at most
4a_{i}^{2}
. By monotonicity,
\sum_{i}a_{i}^{2}\leq E(u)
, so the sum of the diameters is at most
4E(u)
. Thus

d_{\mathrm{norm},J}(q_{1},q_{2})\ \leq\ 2\rho_{J}(q_{1})\,+\,4E(u)\,+\,2\rho_{J}(q_{2}).

If
E(u)\geq 1
we can absorb the other terms into the first term by taking
C_{1}=8
since the scale is
\leq 1
. If
E(u)<1
we have
\sqrt{E(u)}\leq E(u)
and we can apply monotonicity directly to the other two terms to get an estimate
\leq C_{2}\sqrt{E(u)}
for an appropriate constant
C_{2}
.

The last sentence is immediate since the monotonicity estimate depends only on the local behavior.
∎

Proof.

Under the assumption,

g_{J}
is
a
-bounded at every point

3.5.
Dependence on the cutoff constant and stability under uniform equivalence

We now make explicit the dependence of the scale function on the cutoff constant
c
.
The isoperimetric ratio
\mathcal{I}_{J}(p;r)
is intrinsic to
J
and continuous with respect to the uniform
C^{0}
norm on the space of almost complex structures. The definition of the isoperimetric scale is also intrinsic, but involves an arbitrary choice of a cutoff
c>0
:

r_{\mathrm{iso},J}^{c}(p):=\sup\Bigl\{\,r\in(0,+\infty)\ \Big|\ \mathcal{I}_{J}(p;r)\leq c\,\Bigr\},

and hence the scale function also depends on
c
:

\rho_{J}^{c}(p):=\min\{1,r_{\mathrm{iso},J}^{c}(p)\}.

We write
d_{\mathrm{norm},J}^{c}
for the resulting normalized distance.

If
c^{\prime}\geq c
, then
\rho_{J}^{c^{\prime}}(p)\geq\rho_{J}^{c}(p)
, but the ratio
\rho_{J}^{c^{\prime}}/\rho_{J}^{c}
is not uniformly bounded in general.
A larger cutoff increases the scale but weakens the monotonicity bound in (
4
) (energy
\geq r^{2}/(2c^{\prime})
instead of
r^{2}/(2c)
), so confinement constants depend on
c
.

Lemma 3.10

(Stability under uniform equivalence)
.

Let
J_{0},J_{1}
be
\omega
-compatible almost complex structures whose associated metrics
g_{J_{0}},g_{J_{1}}
are
C
-equivalent for some
C\geq 1
, meaning

C^{-1}|v|_{g_{J_{0}}}\leq|v|_{g_{J_{1}}}\leq C|v|_{g_{J_{0}}}\qquad\text{for all }v\in TM.

Then for every cutoff
c>0
one has pointwise

\rho_{J_{1}}^{C^{4}c}(p)\ \geq\ C^{-1}\rho_{J_{0}}^{c}(p)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho_{J_{0}}^{C^{4}c}(p)\ \geq\ C^{-1}\rho_{J_{1}}^{c}(p).

Consequently, if
J_{0}
is normalized complete with cutoff
c
, then
J_{1}
is normalized complete with cutoff
C^{4}c
, and vice versa.

Proof.

Uniform equivalence implies inclusions of balls
B^{g_{J_{1}}}_{r}(p)\subset B^{g_{J_{0}}}_{Cr}(p)
and
B^{g_{J_{0}}}_{r}(p)\subset B^{g_{J_{1}}}_{Cr}(p)
, and comparability of lengths and areas: for every piecewise smooth loop
\gamma
and smooth disc
u
,

\ell_{g_{J_{1}}}(\gamma)\leq C\,\ell_{g_{J_{0}}}(\gamma),\qquad\ell_{g_{J_{1}}}(\gamma)\geq C^{-1}\ell_{g_{J_{0}}}(\gamma),\qquad\Area_{g_{J_{1}}}(u)\leq C^{2}\,\Area_{g_{J_{0}}}(u),

and similarly with the roles of
J_{0},J_{1}
reversed.
It follows that
\mathcal{I}_{J_{1}}(p;r)\leq C^{4}\,\mathcal{I}_{J_{0}}(p;Cr)
for all
p,r
, and likewise with
0,1
swapped.
Taking suprema in the definition of
r_{\mathrm{iso},J}^{c}
gives

r_{\mathrm{iso},J_{1}}^{C^{4}c}(p)\geq C^{-1}r_{\mathrm{iso},J_{0}}^{c}(p)

and the symmetric inequality, hence the stated bounds for
\rho^{c}=\min\{1,r_{\mathrm{iso}}^{c}\}
.

From the scale bounds, the normalized lengths satisfy
L_{\mathrm{norm},J_{1}}^{C^{4}c}(\gamma)\geq C^{-2}L_{\mathrm{norm},J_{0}}^{c}(\gamma)
for every path
\gamma
, so
d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{1}}^{C^{4}c}\geq C^{-2}d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{0}}^{c}
.
Completeness of
(M,d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{0}}^{c})
therefore implies completeness of
(M,d_{\mathrm{norm},J_{1}}^{C^{4}c})
, since a Cauchy sequence for the larger metric is Cauchy for the smaller one and converges there.
By symmetry, normalized completeness with cutoff
c
is equivalent to that with cutoff
C^{4}c
when the metrics are
C
-equivalent.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

If
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
satisfy a uniform bound
|J_{0}-J_{1}|\leq\varepsilon
with respect to some background metric, then
g_{J_{0}}
and
g_{J_{1}}
are
C
-equivalent for some
C
depending only on
\varepsilon
and the background metric.
In particular, if
|J_{0}-J_{1}|
is uniformly bounded, then Lemma
3.10
applies.

Weighted equilibrium and the flow of derivatives of polynomials
Andrei Martínez-Finkelshtein, Evgenii A. Rakhmanov

Abstract
. Given a sequence of polynomials Q n Q_{n} of degree n n with zeros on [ − 1 , 1 ] [-1,1] , we consider the triangular table of derivatives Q n , k ​ ( x ) = d k ​ Q n ​ ( x ) / d ​ x k Q_{n,k}(x)=d^{k}Q_{n}(x)/dx^{k} . Under the assumption that the sequence { Q n } \{Q_{n}\} has a weak* limiting zero distribution (an empirical distribution of zeros) given by the arcsine law, we show that as n , k → ∞ n,k\rightarrow\infty such that k / n → t ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) k/n\rightarrow t\in[0,1) , the zero-counting measure of the polynomials Q n , k Q_{n,k} converges to an explicitly given measure μ t \mu_{t} . This measure is the equilibrium measure of [ − 1 , 1 ] [-1,1] of size 1 − t 1-t in an external field given by two mass points of size t / 2 t/2 fixed at ± 1 \pm 1 . The main goal of this paper is to provide a direct potential theory proof of this fact.

Introduction

Given a sequence of (monic) polynomials
Q_{n}
,
\deg Q_{n}=n
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with zeros on the interval
[-1,1]
, we consider the triangular table of their iterated derivatives

(1.1)

Q_{n,k}(x)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\frac{(n-k)!}{n!}\,\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}Q_{n}(x)=x^{n-k}+\text{lower degree terms},\quad k=0,1,\dots,n-1.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Q_{n}
be a sequence of polynomials,
\deg Q_{n}=n
, whose zeros belong to the interval
[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{R}
, and such that the sequence of probability zero-counting measures
1
1

1

In all zero-counting measures the zeros are considered with account of their multiplicity.

\sigma_{n,0}=\frac{1}{n}\chi\left(Q_{n}\right),\quad\chi(Q_{n})\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\sum_{Q_{n}(x)=0}\delta_{x},

has a weak-* limit
\mu_{0}
, given by the arc-sine distribution, namely,

(1.2)

d\mu_{0}(x)=\frac{dx}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^{2}}},\quad x\in(-1,1).

Let

(1.3)

\sigma_{n,k}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\frac{1}{n}\chi\left(Q_{n,k}\right)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{Q_{n,k}(x)=0}\delta_{x}.

Then for any
t\in[0,1)
and any sequence
k_{n}
of natural numbers with
k_{n}/n\rightarrow t
, the sequence
\sigma_{n,k_{n}}
has a weak-* limit
\mu_{t}
, where

\mu_{t}
is absolutely continuous and

(1.4)

d\mu_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{1-t^{2}-x^{2}}}{1-x^{2}}\,dx,\qquad x\in[-\sqrt{1-t^{2}},\sqrt{1-t^{2}}],

A simple illustration of this result is given by the sequence
Q_{n}=P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
,
\alpha,\beta>-1
, of the (monic) Jacobi polynomials, see
[
2
, §18.3]
. The well-known property
[
2
, §18.9]

Q^{\prime}_{n}(x)=nP_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x)

allows for the explicit computation of the weak-* limit
\mu_{t}
of the sequence (
1.3
), as
k/n\to t
, in this case. This yields the expression in (
1.4
). Theorem
1.1
shows that this behavior is not intrinsic to Jacobi polynomials, but it is generic for any sequence of polynomials whose initial distribution is given by the arc-sine law.

Theorem
1.1
can be derived, in particular, from the results contained in the recent paper
[
3
]
, where the description of the Cauchy transform of the limiting measure
\mu_{t}
(under very general assumptions) is done in terms of solutions of the inviscid Burgers equation. The main contribution of this paper is the potential-theoretic approach, which characterizes the flow in terms of the equilibrium measure in an external field generated by two mass points. Since one of the main steps in our analysis is an estimate of the Cauchy integral expressing the polynomials
Q_{n,k}
, it is worth mentioning a recent work of
[
1
]
on zeros of polynomials generated by a Rodrigues-type formula.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let us start by setting up the following notation: for
0<\tau\leq 1
, denote
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}(F)
the set of Borel measures of total mass
\tau
supported on
F\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Given a finite compactly supported Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, we define its
logarithmic potential
as

(2.1)

U^{\mu}(z)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\int\log\frac{1}{|z-y|}\,d\mu(y).

For our purposes, it will be sufficient to consider measures on an interval of the real line, without loss of generality,
\Delta\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Recall the following weighted equilibrium problem that we state in its simplified form, see e.g.
[
4
]
: given
\tau\in(0,1]
and a continuous function
\varphi:(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}
(the
external field
), there is the unique equilibrium measure
\lambda=\lambda(\tau,\varphi,\Delta)\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}(\Delta)
minimizing the weighted energy

\int U^{\mu}\,d\mu+2\int\varphi\,d\mu

in the class
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}(\Delta)
2
2

2

We could clearly reformulate the problem in the class
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Delta)
; the parameter
\tau
is introduced here for convenience.
. It is known that there is a constant
m_{\tau,\varphi}
such that

(2.2)

U^{\lambda}(x)+\varphi(x)\begin{cases}=m_{\tau,\varphi},&x\in\supp(\lambda),\\
\geq m_{\tau,\varphi},&x\in\Delta.\end{cases}

The equilibrium measure
\lambda
is also characterized by the following property:

(2.3)

m_{\tau,\varphi}=\min_{x\in\Delta}\left(U^{\lambda}(x)+\varphi(x)\right)=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}(\Delta)}\min_{x\in\Delta}\left(U^{\mu}(x)+\varphi(x)\right).

In other words, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}(\Delta)
,

(2.4)

\min_{x\in\Delta}\left(U^{\mu}(x)+\varphi(x)\right)\geq m_{\tau,\varphi}\text{ quasi-everywhere}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mu=\lambda(\tau,\varphi,\Delta).

This will be the key property we will use to prove Theorem
1.1
, taking into account the following well-known fact:

Lemma 2.1
.

For
t\in[0,1)
, measure
\mu_{t}
in (
1.4
) is the equilibrium measure
\lambda(1-t,\varphi,\Delta)\in\mathcal{M}_{1-t}(\Delta)
in the external field

(2.5)

\varphi(x)=\varphi_{t}(x)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\frac{t}{2}\log\frac{1}{|x^{2}-1|},\quad x\in(-1,1).

For the proof, see e.g.
[
4
, Examples IV.1.18 and IV.6.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a continuous external field on
\Delta
,
\tau\in(0,1]
,
\lambda=\lambda(\tau,\varphi,\Delta)\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}(\Delta)
the equilibrium measure. If the support
S\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\supp\left(\lambda_{\tau,\varphi}\right)\subset\Delta
is an interval then

(2.6)

m_{\tau,\varphi}=u_{\varphi}(\infty)+\tau\log\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm cap}(S)},

where
u_{\varphi}
is the solution of the Dirichlet problem in
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S
with the boundary values
\varphi
, and
\mathop{\rm cap}(S)=\mathrm{length}(S)/4
is the logarithmic capacity of
S
.

In particular, with
\varphi=\varphi_{t}
given in (
2.5
),

(2.7)

m_{1-t,\varphi}=\log(2)-\frac{1+t}{2}\log(1+t)-\frac{1-t}{2}\log(1-t).

Proof.

Let
\Psi
be the conformal mapping of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S
onto the exterior of the unit circle, such that
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
, and normalized by
\Psi^{\prime}(\infty)>0
. Then

\Psi^{\prime}(\infty)=1/\mathop{\rm cap}(S)
. Consider the function

H(z)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}U^{\lambda}(z)+\tau\log|\Psi(z)|+u_{\varphi}(z).

By (
2.2
),
H
is harmonic in
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S
and identically equal to
m_{\tau,\varphi}
on
S
, which implies that
H(z)=m_{\tau,\varphi}
for all
z\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S
. In particular,

\displaystyle m_{\tau,\varphi}

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\to\infty}\left(U^{\lambda}(z)+\tau\log|\Psi(z)|+u_{\varphi}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\to\infty}\left(U^{\lambda}(z)+\tau\log|z|\right)+\lim_{z\to\infty}\tau\log\left|\frac{\Psi(z)}{z}\right|+u_{\varphi}(\infty)

and (
2.6
) follows from the well-known properties of the logarithmic potential and the conformal mapping
\Psi
.

By Lemma
2.1
and formula (
1.4
), for
\varphi=\varphi_{t}
, we have
\tau=1-t
,
S=[-s,s]
with
s=\sqrt{1-t^{2}}
. In this case,
\Psi(z)=\Phi(z/s)
, where
\Phi(z)
is the inverse Zhukovski map,

(2.8)

\Phi(z)=z+(z^{2}-1)^{1/2},

and we take the single-valued branch of the square root in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta
fixed by
(z^{2}-1)^{1/2}>0
for
z>1
.

On the other hand,

u_{\varphi}(z)=\mathop{\rm Re}\left(\frac{\sqrt{z^{2}-s^{2}}}{\pi}\int_{-s}^{s}\frac{\varphi_{t}(x)}{\sqrt{s^{2}-x^{2}}}\frac{dx}{z-x}\right),

so that

\displaystyle u_{\varphi}(\infty)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-s}^{s}\frac{\varphi_{t}(x)}{\sqrt{s^{2}-x^{2}}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{{t}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-s}^{s}\log\frac{1}{1-x}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{s^{2}-x^{2}}}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-s}^{s}\log\frac{1}{1+x}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{s^{2}-x^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\pi}\int_{-s}^{s}\log\frac{1}{1-x}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{s^{2}-x^{2}}}=t\,U^{\lambda}(1),

where
\lambda=\lambda(1,0,[-s,s])
is the equilibrium (Robin) measure of the interval
[-s,s]
. Since

U^{\lambda}(z)=-\log\left(\mathop{\rm cap}([-s,s])\right)-\log\left|\Phi(z/s)\right|=\log\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)-\log\left|\Phi(z/s)\right|,

we get that

u_{\varphi}(\infty)=t\left(\log\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)-\log\left|\Phi(1/s)\right|\right)=t\left(\log\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+t)+\frac{1}{2}\log(1-t)\right).

In consequence,

\displaystyle m_{\tau,\varphi}

\displaystyle=-t\log\left|\Phi(1/s)\right|+\log\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\log(2)-\frac{1+t}{2}\log(1+t)-\frac{1-t}{2}\log(1-t).

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Clearly, formula (

) is valid under much milder assumptions on

Now we turn to the sequence of zero-counting measures
\{\sigma_{n,k_{n}}\}_{n\in\Lambda}

\sigma_{n,0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Delta),\quad n\to\infty.

The following lemma is valid even if
\mu_{0}
is not the arc-sine distribution on

:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{k_{n}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be a sequence such that

(2.10)

\lim_{n}\frac{k_{n}}{n}=t\in[0,1)

exists, and let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be such that the following weak-* limit exists:

(2.11)

\sigma_{n,k_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\longrightarrow}}\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1-t}(\Delta),\quad n\in\Lambda.

Then, for any
\zeta\in\Delta
where
U^{\nu}
is continuous, and quasi-everywhere on
\Delta
, for any Jordan contour
\Gamma
enclosing
\Delta
,

(2.12)

U^{\nu}(\zeta)\geq-c_{t}+\min_{z\in\Gamma}\left(U^{\mu_{0}}(z)+t\log|z-\zeta|\right),

where

(2.13)

c_{t}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}t\log t+(1-t)\log(1-t).

Proof.

Let first
\zeta
be in the bounded complement of
\mathbb{C}\setminus(\Gamma\cup\Delta)
. We have the integral representation

Q_{n,k}(\zeta)=\binom{n}{k}^{-1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{Q_{n}(z)}{(z-\zeta)^{k+1}}dz,

from which we get that

U^{\sigma_{n,k}}(\zeta)=-\frac{1}{n}\log\left|Q_{n,k}(\zeta)\right|=\frac{1}{n}\log\binom{n}{k}+\frac{1}{n}\log(2\pi)-\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{Q_{n}(z)}{(z-\zeta)^{k+1}}dz\right|.

By Stirling’s formula,

\lim_{n}\frac{1}{n}\log\binom{n}{k_{n}}=-c_{t}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{Q_{n}(z)}{(z-\zeta)^{k+1}}dz\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\max_{z\in\Gamma}\left|\frac{Q_{n}(z)}{(z-\zeta)^{k+1}}\right|\cdot\text{length}(\Gamma)\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{z\in\Gamma}\left(-U^{\sigma_{n,0}}(z)-\frac{k+1}{n}\log|z-\zeta|\right)+\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\text{length}(\Gamma)\right)

\displaystyle=-\min_{z\in\Gamma}\left(U^{\sigma_{n,0}}(z)+\frac{k+1}{n}\log|z-\zeta|\right)+\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\text{length}(\Gamma)\right).

Thus, we conclude that

(2.14)

U^{\sigma_{n,k_{n}}}(\zeta)\geq-c_{t}+\min_{z\in\Gamma}\left(U^{\sigma_{n,0}}(z)+\frac{k_{n}+1}{n}\log|z-\zeta|\right)+o(1),\quad n\to\infty.

Taking limits in both sides and using assumptions (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) we conclude that (
2.12
) is valid for
\zeta
in the bounded complement of
\mathbb{C}\setminus(\Gamma\cup\Delta)
.

By the the Continuity Theorem
[
4

, we can extend this inequality to any
\zeta\in\Delta
where the potential
U^{\nu}
is continuous. Moreover, by the Lower Envelope Theorem
[
4

, it holds quasi-everywhere on
\Delta
.
∎

U^{\mu_{0}}(z)=\log(2)-\log\left|\Phi(z)\right|,

Lemma 2.5
.

With the assumptions of Lemma
2.4
,

(2.16)

\text{``}\inf_{x\in[-1,1]}\text{''}\left(U^{\nu}+\varphi\right)(x)\geq m_{1-t,\varphi},

where
m_{1-t,\varphi}
is given in (
2.7
).

Proof.

Fix
\zeta\in(-1,1)
where the potential
U^{\nu}
is continuous; as a contour
\Gamma
in (
2.12
) we take the level curve of
\Phi
, namely

\Gamma=\Gamma_{A}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta:\,|\Phi(z)|=\Phi(A)\right\},\quad A>1.

It is easy to see that
\Gamma_{A}
is an ellipse whose equation in Cartesian coordinates on the
(x,y)
plane is

\frac{x^{2}}{A^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{(A^{2}-1)}=1.

By (
2.15
), it is also a level curve of
U^{\mu_{0}}
. Thus, the problem of minimizing
\log|z-\zeta|
with
z\in\Gamma_{A}
is equivalent to the real-valued constrained optimization problem

\displaystyle\text{minimize}\quad\left(x-\zeta\right)^{2}+y^{2}

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\quad\frac{x^{2}}{A^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{A^{2}-1}=1.

Assume w.o.l.g. that
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
. Standard methods show that the only possible points of minimum are
(x,y)=(A,0)
and, if
\zeta A^{2}\leq 1
,

(x,y)=\left(\zeta A^{2},\pm\sqrt{\left(A^{2}-1\right)\left(1-\zeta\right)}\right).

Hence, the global minimum is

\begin{cases}(A-\zeta)^{2},&\text{if }\zeta A^{2}\geq 1,\\
\min\left\{(A-\zeta)^{2},(A^{2}-1)(1-\zeta^{2})\right\},&\text{if }\zeta A^{2}\leq 1.\end{cases}

Since

(A-\zeta)^{2}=(A^{2}-1)(1-\zeta^{2})+(\zeta A-1)^{2}\geq(A^{2}-1)(1-\zeta^{2}),

for
-1\leq\zeta\leq 1
and
z\in\Gamma_{A}
,

\log|z-\zeta|\geq\frac{1}{2}\log(A^{2}-1)+\frac{1}{2}\log(1-\zeta^{2}),

so that, combining it with (
2.15
),

U^{\mu_{0}}(z)+t\log|z-\zeta|\geq\log(2)-\log\left|\Phi(A)\right|+\frac{t}{2}\log(A^{2}-1)+\frac{t}{2}\log(1-\zeta^{2}).

Using it in (
2.12
), we see that for
-1\leq\zeta\leq 1
,

(2.17)

U^{\mu_{t}}(\zeta)\geq-c_{t}+\log(2)-\log\left|\Phi(A)\right|+\frac{t}{2}\log(A^{2}-1)+\frac{t}{2}\log(1-\zeta^{2}).

With
\varphi
as in (
2.5
) we can rewrite it as

\left(U^{\mu_{t}}+\varphi\right)(\zeta)\geq-c_{t}+\log(2)-\log\left|\Phi(A)\right|+\frac{t}{2}\log(A^{2}-1),

and we conclude that

(2.18)

\min_{x\in[-1,1]}\left(U^{\mu_{0}}+\varphi\right)(x)\geq-c_{t}+\log(2)-\log\left|\Phi(A)\right|+\frac{t}{2}\log(A^{2}-1).

The global maximum of the right-hand side over
A>1
is attained at

A=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}=\frac{1}{s}

and is equal to

-c_{t}+\log(2)-\log\left|\Phi(1/s)\right|+\frac{t}{2}\log(s^{-2}-1)=-c_{t}+\log(2)-\log\left|\Phi(1/s)\right|+t\log(t/s).

Simplifying, we obtain that

\displaystyle\min_{x\in[-1,1]}\left(U^{\mu_{t}}+\varphi\right)(x)

\displaystyle\geq-c_{t}+\log(2)+(1-t)\log(1-t)+\left(\frac{t}{2}-1\right)\log(2-t)+\frac{t}{2}\log(t)

and (
2.16
) follows.
∎

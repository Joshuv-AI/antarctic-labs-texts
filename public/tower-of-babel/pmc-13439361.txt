Ex vivo accuracy of three electronic apex locators under various irrigants
Authors: Muhammed Ayhan, Damla Erkal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEvidence on the X-Smart Pro + for apical constriction detection under different irrigants remains limited. This ex vivo study compared X-Smart Pro+, Root ZX Mini, and Propex Pixi in 36 extracted single-rooted teeth standardized to 17 mm. Real working length (RWL) was determined microscopically, and electronic measurements were obtained in an alginate model under saline, 2.5% sodium hypochlorite, 17% EDTA, and 2% chlorhexidine. X-Smart Pro+ was assessed at two distinct points, corresponding to the 4th and 7th green LEDs (GL4 and GL7). Mean deviations from the RWL ranged from − 0.36 to 0.40 mm, and all devices yielded mean measurements within ± 0.5 mm. Root ZX Mini showed the highest overall accuracy by the ± 0.5 mm criterion, whereas X-Smart Pro+ GL4 showed a smaller mean deviation in saline and significantly improved accuracy in 17% EDTA (P < 0.05). GL4 measurements were generally closer to the RWL than GL7, except in NaOCl. Irrigant type did not significantly affect most measurements (P > 0.05). Within the limitations of this ex vivo model, the tested electronic apex locators showed broadly comparable accuracy, and for X-Smart Pro+, GL4 measurements were generally closer to the RWL than GL7.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54329-y.Keywords: Electronic apex locator, Irrigation solutions, Propex Pixi, Root ZX mini, Working length, X-Smart Pro+Subject terms: Health care, Medical research

Introduction

It is well established that accurately determining the working length is one of the most critical steps in the successful treatment of root canals. Accurate working length determination ensures proper cleaning and shaping of the root canal system while preventing over-instrumentation and potential damage to periapical tissues1. Failure to determine the working length precisely may lead to persistent infection, incomplete debridement, increased risk of postoperative pain, and ultimately, treatment failures2,3.

Root canal shaping is recommended to terminate near the apical constriction (AC) due to its location at the narrowest segment of the root canal system4. The constriction is not a fixed point but often lies approximately 0.20 to 0.65 mm short of the apical foramen. Its position can be influenced by factors such as root anatomy, tooth type, and the age of the individual5.

Various techniques are utilized to determine working length in endodontics. Conventional periapical radiographs provide a visual approximation, but their two-dimensional nature introduces distortion and limits accuracy6. In recent years, electronic apex locators (EALs) have become the preferred method due to their greater precision and reliability across various clinical conditions7. Systematic reviews have consistently reported that modern EALs demonstrate superior accuracy in locating the AC compared to tactile sensation or radiographic techniques6.

Despite their overall reliability, the performance of EALs can still be influenced by various factors, including the presence and type of irrigating solutions within the root canal system8. Previous studies have reported inconsistent findings regarding whether irrigant type (saline, NaOCl, EDTA, or CHX) influences the accuracy of contemporary EALs, with some describing measurable effects9,10 and others9,11,12 reporting little or no influence. Therefore, it is essential to assess the consistency of EAL performance under clinically relevant conditions that reflect the use of different irrigants during root canal treatment.

Early-generation EALs were susceptible to inaccuracies in electrolytic environments. This limitation has been largely overcome with third-generation EAL, such as the Root ZX, which evaluates impedance at dual frequencies and continues to serve as the gold standard, achieving accuracy rates of up to 100%. Its compact version, the Root ZX Mini, offers similar performance13. The Propex Pixi, a multi-frequency EAL, also provides high accuracy, particularly near the apical foramen14,15. The X-Smart Pro+ was recently introduced, incorporating Dynamic Accuracy™ Technology to enhance the precision of working length determination13.

Despite the widespread use of EALs, independent validation of newly introduced systems such as the X-Smart Pro+ remains limited. Recent ex vivo studies8,10 have evaluated the accuracy of the X-Smart Pro+ under different irrigating conditions, primarily in integrated motor modes and with reference to device-indicated apical endpoints. However, no clear consensus exists regarding how different visual endpoints on the device interface, particularly 4th green LEDs (GL4) and 7th green LEDs (GL7), correspond to the actual apical constriction under different clinical conditions. In addition, because commonly used irrigation solutions have different electrolytic properties, their potential to affect the performance of contemporary multi-frequency EALs warrants direct comparison. Therefore, this study was necessary to evaluate the accuracy and stability of these devices under clinically relevant irrigant conditions and to provide practical evidence for selecting the most reliable endpoint for working length determination. The null hypothesis was that different irrigants (saline, NaOCl, EDTA, and CHX) would not significantly influence electronic working length measurements.

Materials and methods

Sample size and power analysis

An a priori power analysis was conducted using G*Power 3.1.9.4 software with a one-way ANOVA design to evaluate the study’s statistical power. A medium effect size (f = 0.25), an alpha level of 0.05, and a statistical power (1-β) of 0.95 were established. This variance analysis model accounted for both the number of groups and the sample size in the conducted analysis.

Based on the above parameters, the minimum required sample size was calculated to be 29 teeth. With a total of 36 teeth included, the sample size was considered adequate for statistical analysis, and the study design was deemed sufficiently robust to detect potential differences with 95% statistical power.

Ethics approval and guidelines

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Burdur Mehmet Akif Ersoy University (08-2025/1882). All laboratory procedures followed the 2021 PRILE and CRIS guidelines16. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before participation. This ex vivo study was conducted between August 15 and October 14, 2025. The study design and outcomes are summarized in a flowchart presented in Fig. 1.

PRILE 2021 flow chart.

Teeth selection

Thirty-six extracted human maxillary lateral incisors, obtained from multiple clinical sources for various therapeutic reasons (such as periodontal disease or prosthetic treatment planning), were included in the study. Soft- and hard-tissue remnants were removed using periodontal curettes and ultrasonic scalers, and the selected teeth were stored in distilled water at + 4 °C until use. Confirmation of a single root canal configuration in each specimen was achieved using periapical radiographs obtained at mesiodistal and buccolingual angulations. These criteria required intact root surfaces without fractures, cracks, or carious lesions (with standardized length not reaching any existing lesions), no history of prior endodontic treatment, patent root canals, absence of root resorption or canal calcification/obliteration, fully developed roots, and preserved apical integrity. Specimens that did not fulfill these predefined radiographic and clinical criteria were excluded from the study.

Teeth specimen preparation

To standardize and facilitate access to the root canal, the crowns of the selected teeth were sectioned under water-cooling using a diamond fissure bur, reducing the tooth length to approximately 17 mm. Apical patency was assessed with a size #10 K-file, and dual stoppers were used during measurements to maintain stopper stability. A #15 K-type file was inserted into the root canal, and the apical foramen was visualized under ×25 magnification using a microscope (Magna Labomed, Labo America Inc., CA, USA). The rubber stopper was subsequently set to align with the crown. To improve measurement precision, the crown region used for measurements was marked, and the distance from the file tip to the stopper was measured with a digital caliper (0.01 mm accuracy; Dasqua, Conegliano Laudense, Italy). Each measurement was performed in triplicate for every specimen, and the mean value was calculated, from which 0.5 mm was subsequently subtracted. Accordingly, the real working length (RWL) was determined for each specimen, and the teeth with established RWLs were subsequently stored in individually numbered glass vials containing distilled water.

Study design

In a previous ex vivo study13, the accuracy of EALs (X-smart Pro+, Root ZX Mini, and Propex Pixi) was evaluated using a metal-supported training model. Measurements were obtained at three predefined reference points, and 12 teeth were allocated to each group. That study primarily aimed to determine overall differences in accuracy among the devices. In the present study, the performance of the EALs was investigated in the presence of different irrigating solutions. Measurements were recorded at four predefined reference points, with the specimens embedded directly in an alginate model.

Experimental procedures

Determination of root canal WL with EALs

The teeth were placed in an alginate model (Cavex Holland, RW Haarlem, Netherlands) to determine root canal working lengths using EALs. During each measurement session, three teeth were embedded in the model, allowing simultaneous measurements with three EALs under identical experimental conditions. The setup was renewed with fresh alginate after each session. The complete series of electronic measurements was performed in approximately 30 minutes17.

Upon completion of the experimental setup, the root canals were sequentially irrigated with 0.9% saline, 2.5% sodium hypochlorite (Microvem, Istanbul, Turkey), 17% EDTA (Microvem, Istanbul, Turkey), and 2% chlorhexidine (Microvem, Istanbul, Turkey) using a 27-G side-vented irrigation needle. For each irrigant condition, each canal was filled with 2 mL of fresh irrigating solution before electronic measurement. During the exchange of irrigating solutions, the canals were flushed with 10 mL of distilled water for 5 min. The canals were not completely dried; only excess irrigant from the tooth surface and canal entrance was removed with a cotton pellet before the acquisition and documentation of electronic measurements.

Electronic working length measurements were then obtained using three distinct EAL devices, each employed in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. EALs were consistently operated using fully charged or freshly installed batteries.

Measurements with the Root ZX Mini (J. Morita, Tokyo, Japan) were conducted by inserting the file into the root canal and carefully advancing it beyond the apical foramen, at which point the flashing ‘APEX’ indicator was observed on the LCD screen. The file was then withdrawn until it was positioned between the ‘APEX’ and ‘1’ indicators18.

Measurements with the Propex Pixi (Dentsply Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland): the file was advanced until the ‘OVER’ indicator was visible, then withdrawn to the 0.0 mark. A 0.5 mm reduction from the measurement at the 0.0 point is recommended when using the Propex Pixi14. Accordingly, measurements in the Propex Pixi group were adjusted by subtracting 0.5 mm, in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.

In measurements performed with the X-Smart Pro+ (Dentsply Sirona, Bensheim, Germany), the device manual indicates that GL4 corresponds to the mid-apical region, whereas GL7 represents the final level of the apical region, just before the ‘Over’ indication, which corresponds to the apical foramen. Although GL4 has been considered to represent the minor apical diameter in several studies10,13,19, this is not explicitly stated in the manufacturer’s instructions. According to the X-Smart Pro+ manual, the device can be adjusted and used up to the seventh green LED during measurements (https://tinyurl.com/3avj33zb).

To determine the precise measurement distance, two different measurement points were used on the X-Smart Pro+ device.

First measurement point (GL4) The file was advanced within the canal until the device displayed the red ‘Over’ mark, after which it was gradually retracted to the fourth green bar (indicated by double arrows) to determine the working length.

Second measurement point (GL7) The seventh green bar (GL7), located immediately before the red ‘Over’ indicator on the X-Smart Pro+ display, was used as the reference. The file was advanced into the canal until the device displayed ‘Over,’ after which it was gradually retracted to GL7, positioned just above the red ‘Over’ bar, to determine the working length.

The clinician responsible for measuring the RWLs (D. E) and the clinician performing the EALs measurements (M. A) were distinct4. Each measurement was repeated three times, and the mean value was calculated. Electronic lengths were recorded once the readings remained stable for at least 5 s. At this point, the silicone stopper was positioned, and the spacing between the file tip and the stopper was measured with a digital caliper (Dasqua, Conegliano Laudense, Italy) with 0.01 mm precision20.

RWL measurements were compared with the EWL by subtraction, and the resulting values were recorded. Positive values indicated lengths exceeding the RWL, whereas negative values indicated shorter lengths.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of the data was first assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Since the data were normally distributed and variances were homogeneous, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare mean differences between RWL and electronic working length measurements across the experimental groups. When significant differences were found, Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) post hoc test was applied for pairwise multiple comparisons. All values were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and a confidence level of 95% (α = 0.05) was adopted. Differences were considered statistically significant when p < 0.05. Intra-examiner reliability of the repeated electronic measurements was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) with a two-way mixed-effects model and absolute agreement.

With the Root ZX Mini, the mean differences between the RWL and the file tip were 0.11 ± 0.36 mm, − 0.02 ± 0.42 mm, 0.03 ± 0.39 mm, and 0.06 ± 0.32 mm for NaCl, NaOCl, EDTA, and CHX, respectively. For the Propex Pixi, the mean differences were − 0.25 ± 0.27 mm, -0.20 ± 0.38 mm, − 0.36 ± 0.36 mm, and − 0.26 ± 0.28 mm, respectively. For the X-Smart Pro+ (GL4), the corresponding values were − 0.10 ± 0.37 mm, − 0.26 ± 0.49 mm, 0.04 ± 0.40 mm, and − 0.17 ± 0.40 mm, whereas for the X-Smart Pro+ (GL7), they were 0.31 ± 0.32 mm, 0.20 ± 0.40 mm, 0.40 ± 0.38 mm, and 0.20 ± 0.35 mm, respectively (Table 1; Fig. 2).

For Root ZX Mini, the highest proportions of measurements within ± 0.5 mm were observed with 2% CHX (94.4%) and 0.9% NaCl (91.7%). For Propex Pixi and X-Smart Pro+, the highest proportions within ± 0.5 mm were observed under 0.9% NaCl irrigation (86.1%, 86.2% for GL4, and 80.6% for GL7, respectively) (Table 2). Intra-examiner reliability was excellent across all device–irrigant conditions, with average-measure ICC values ranging from 0.977 to 0.991 (Supplementary Table S1).

Discussion

Among the tested devices, Root ZX Mini showed the highest numerical accuracy across most irrigants; however, the overall ex vivo performance of the devices was broadly comparable. Among the EALs evaluated in this study, Root ZX Mini consistently demonstrated the highest accuracy across irrigants, whereas X-Smart Pro+ (GL4) showed significantly greater precision with the EDTA irrigant. The presence of irrigants did not significantly influence the performance of the other EALs. The null hypothesis was not rejected for Root ZX Mini, Propex Pixi, and X-Smart Pro+ GL7, because irrigant type did not significantly affect their measurements. It was rejected for X-Smart Pro+ GL4, for which measurements obtained in EDTA differed significantly from those obtained under the other irrigants. This isolated finding should be interpreted cautiously, because small differences in electronic working length determination may also reflect measurement variability, endpoint interpretation, stopper positioning, and operator-dependent handling rather than a true device-specific advantage. This cautious interpretation is consistent with the clinical findings of Kielbassa et al.21, who reported sufficient accuracy of Root ZX in primary teeth but also found that measurements were significantly affected by the treating dentists, indicating that operator-related factors may contribute to variability.

Consistent with our findings, several studies evaluating EAL performance under various irrigants have reported that the type of irrigant does not significantly influence EAL accuracy11,12,22. Jenkins et al.23 evaluated the measurement accuracy of the Root ZX under various irrigants, including saline, H₂O₂, Peridex, EDTA, 5.25% NaOCl, 2% xylocaine with 1:100,000 epinephrine, and RC Prep. In agreement with the findings of the present study, they concluded that different irrigating solutions had no substantial effect on the performance of the Root ZX. On the other hand, a recent in vitro study10 compared the measurement accuracy of the Root ZX Mini, EnDrive Integrated Endomotor, and X-Smart Pro+ Integrated Endomotor under various canal conditions, including saline, 5.25% NaOCl, and 17% EDTA. The study reported that the Root ZX Mini, EnDrive, and X-Smart Pro+ Integrated Endomotor EALs provided significantly more accurate measurements under saline irrigation compared to 5.25% NaOCl. Methodological variations among studies may have contributed to the observed discrepancies in the results. For example, in the study by Teja et al.10, different mandibular premolars were used for each device. The X-Smart Pro + was assessed in its integrated endomotor mode with 5.25% NaOCl. In contrast, the present study employed maxillary lateral teeth for AC measurements, tested the device exclusively in apex locator mode, and used 2.5% NaOCl.

Consistent with previous studies13,24,25, ± 0.5 mm range was considered clinically acceptable in the present study. Across all irrigants, significant differences were observed between the GL4 and GL7 reference points of the X-Smart Pro+, with GL7 measurements being significantly longer than those obtained at GL4. Except for 2.5% NaOCl, the GL4 reference point yielded more accurate measurements than GL7 under all irrigant conditions. The improved accuracy observed with EDTA may be related to its chelating effect on dentin, which can modify canal wall conductivity and influence impedance-based measurements. Although measurements at both reference points remained within the clinically acceptable range (± 0.5 mm) (Tables 1 and 2), GL7 readings consistently resulted in overestimated working lengths. According to the manufacturer’s instructions (https://tinyurl.com/3avj33zb), the last level of the apical region (GL7) before the “Over” signal corresponds to the apical foramen, whereas GL4 is described as representing the mid-apical region. However, the manufacturer does not explicitly define which LED corresponds to the apical constriction. Despite this lack of explicit guidance, several experimental studies have suggested that the GL4 reference point is closer to the minor apical diameter and may therefore better represent the apical constriction8,10,13,19. The findings of the present study support this interpretation, as measurements obtained at GL4 were consistently closer to the RWL than those obtained at GL7.

When irrigant-specific performance was considered, no statistically significant differences were observed among the tested devices under most irrigation conditions. Although minor numerical variations were detected, Root ZX Mini generally demonstrated consistent accuracy across irrigants. X-Smart Pro+ GL4 also remained within ± 0.5 mm and was closer to the RWL in EDTA than in the other irrigant conditions. Under the tested ex vivo conditions, mean measurements for all devices remained within ± 0.5 mm of the RWL across NaCl, NaOCl, EDTA, and CHX.

EALs demonstrated clinically acceptable accuracy within ± 0.5 mm, with Root ZX Mini showing the highest accuracy across irrigant conditions (72.2–94.4%). Propex Pixi exhibited slightly lower but consistent performance (75.0–86.1%), whereas X-Smart Pro+ showed more variability, with GL4 ranging from 63.9 to 86.2% and GL7 from 69.4 to 80.6%. For most comparisons, irrigant type was not associated with statistically significant differences, although X-Smart Pro+ GL4 showed improved accuracy in EDTA. Effect size analysis also suggested that irrigant type had a relatively limited influence.

All devices provide measurements within a clinically acceptable range, consistent with previous studies10,25. Wolgin et al.26 demonstrated that preparation guided by electronic length determination is highly accurate—achieving 83% precision within ± 0.5 mm—and that no impairment of the minor diameter’s integrity was observed. In the present study, the incidence of file tips extending beyond the major apical foramen varied by device and irrigant. For the Root ZX Mini, extensions occurred in 5.6% (NaCl), 8.3% (NaOCl), 2.8% (CHX), and 13.9% (EDTA) of measurements, whereas no such occurrences were observed with Propex Pixi. Notably, 11.2% of measurements at the GL7 reference point extended beyond the RWL by more than 1 mm. These ex vivo findings show that selecting measurement thresholds may influence the risk of over-instrumentation. GL7 measurements were generally longer than GL4 measurements and more often extended beyond the apical foramen. Therefore, these endpoint-specific findings should be interpreted cautiously until confirmed in clinical studies.

Although some studies27,28 have suggested that NaOCl may adversely affect EAL performance, others29,30 have reported no negative influence on devices such as Root ZX and Raypex 5, attributing this to the high electrical conductivity of NaOCl. In agreement with these findings, NaOCl did not compromise the performance of the tested EALs in the present study, and Root ZX Mini demonstrated the highest accuracy under 2.5% NaOCl irrigation.

A major strength of this study is the evaluation of the GL4 and GL7 reference points of the X-Smart Pro+, despite the lack of explicit manufacturer guidance regarding their correspondence to the AC. By comparing these levels under controlled ex vivo irrigant conditions, the study provides preliminary comparative data regarding endpoint selection.

Various electroconductive substances have also been employed to evaluate the measurement accuracy of EALs28. In this study, alginate was selected as the embedding medium because of its ability to simulate the impedance of periodontal tissues, ease of handling and application, capacity to envelop the root surface, and favorable electroconductive properties10,13.

This study has several limitations, and its findings should be interpreted with caution when considering direct clinical extrapolation. First, the ex vivo design does not fully reproduce the biological environment of the root canal system or the dynamic conditions encountered during treatment. The absence of periapical tissue responses, patient-related variability, and operative factors means that the present results cannot be assumed to fully reflect in vivo performance13. Second, although alginate is widely used as an embedding medium because of its favorable electroconductive properties, it cannot completely simulate the impedance characteristics10, moisture balance, and biological variability of periodontal tissues. Accordingly, the electronic measurements obtained in this model may differ from those recorded under true clinical conditions. Third, only extracted single-rooted teeth with relatively standardized anatomy were included. This limits the generalizability of the findings to multirooted teeth and to canals with greater anatomical complexity. In particular, curved canals were not represented in the study design, and the absence of curved canal anatomy may have reduced the technical challenges that can influence electronic working length determination. Fourth, the measurements were performed under controlled laboratory conditions without blood, pulp remnants, intracanal debris, or salivary contamination, all of which may affect device performance in clinical practice. Finally, the study did not include clinical validation in patients. Therefore, although the present findings provide useful ex vivo comparative data, they should not be interpreted as direct evidence of clinical effectiveness, and further in vivo studies are required before broad clinical conclusions can be drawn.

Conclusion

Within the limitations of this ex vivo model, the tested electronic apex locators provided broadly comparable working-length estimates across the tested irrigants. The statistically significant differences observed were modest and should not be assumed to be clinically meaningful. For X-Smart Pro+, GL4 tended to approximate the real working length more closely than GL7, but this endpoint-specific finding requires in vivo confirmation.

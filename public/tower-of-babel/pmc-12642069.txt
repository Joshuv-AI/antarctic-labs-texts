Research on the driving paths of farmers’ participation in physical activity and its impact on well-being
Authors: Haixia Wang, Qiuying Li, Bin Gu, Laibing Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
“Driving paths” refer to typical combinations of mechanisms formed by the joint influence of multiple socio-ecological factors that motivate individuals to engage in specific behaviors (such as physical exercise). Exploring the driving paths of farmers’ participation in physical exercise holds significant importance for enhancing their engagement in physical activity and improving their well-being. This study, which is based on the theoretical framework of socioecology, adopts a complex causality perspective and employs the fsQCA method to examine the key factors influencing the participation in physical exercise of farmers across 19 provinces in China, considering three dimensions—the individual level, interpersonal level, and community level—and eight conditional variables. The findings reveal the following: (1) Farmers’ participation in physical activity is not driven by a single factor but, rather, relies on the synergy of multiple conditions. (2) Seven configurations drive farmers’ participation in physical activity, and they can be classified into three pathways: individual-led dominant, Ecological interdependence, and multiple synergistic linkages. (3) In addition to the interplay of complex factors, the level of physical activity among farmers has increased, thus significantly boosting their sense of well-being. Among the pathways, the path of multiple synergistic linkages is more effective at increasing happiness than are the ecological interdependence and individual-led dominant pathways. This study recommends enhancing physical health education, promoting the widespread adoption of scientific fitness concepts, establishing a social support system for rural sports, fostering a conducive environment for physical activity, and strengthening the construction of sports infrastructure in rural areas, thus improving farmers’ access to physical exercise.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25344-x.

Introduction

Within the framework of global health governance, physical inactivity has been identified by the World Health Organization (WHO) as the fourth leading risk factor for mortality worldwide. According to the WHO’s “2022 Global Status Report on Physical Activity,” the proportion of rural residents worldwide meeting the recommended levels of physical exercise is significantly lower than that of urban residents, with this “urban‒rural health divide” being particularly pronounced in developing countries [1]. President Xi Jinping emphasized that “National fitness is the foundation and safeguard for all people to strengthen their physical health and lead a healthy lifestyle“ [2]. Against the backdrop of China’s rapid social development and continued improvement in healthcare conditions, the transformation of rural areas and farmers’ health concerns are undergoing significant changes, which not only concern the health and well-being of individuals but also reflect the vitality and vibrancy of rural communities as a whole [3]. Along with the complete implementation of China’s rural revitalization strategy, the Party Central Committee and the State Council have issued a series of measures to energetically expand rural sports, laying a solid policy framework and platform for rural residents to engage in sports and exercise. The 2017 report of the 19th National Congress of the Communist Party of China (CPC) explicitly stated that the development of rural sports is an important component of the rural revitalization strategy [4]. Furthermore, the Guiding Opinions on Promoting the High-quality Development of Farmers’ Sports in the 14th Five-Year Plan, issued in 2022, emphasized the importance of improving the public service system for farmers’ fitness, aiming to promote the integration of agriculture and sports, culture and sports, and wisdom and sports to satisfy the needs of farmers for a better life and to promote the high-quality development of farmers’ sports and fitness [5]. Furthermore, in 2023, the State General Administration of Sports, the Central Civilization Office, and twelve other departments issued the Guiding Opinions on Promoting Sports for Rural Revitalization, which stated that sports should be used to help build the countryside, empower agricultural development, promote farmers’ health, and enrich rural culture. The goal is to have numerous demonstration cases of sports-assisted rural rejuvenation by 2025 and to have rural physical fitness, sports, and leisure in place as a mainstream way of life by 2035 [6]. It is clear that rural residents’ participation in physical exercise has become an important component of implementing the rural revitalization strategy, as well as an important way to improve rural residents’ health and meet people’s aspirations for a better life. Additionally, physical exercise has become a major source of concern [4]– [5, 7].

The rural revitalization strategy has gradually incorporated rural towns into the wave of modernization, and people’s health attitudes have become more reasonable and forward-thinking. However, owing to the long-standing urban‒rural dichotomous distribution structure of China, rural areas continue to diverge greatly from urban areas in terms of sports infrastructure development and residents’ exercise awareness [8]. Accordingly, the present study employs the fsQCA method to explore the driving pathways of farmers’ participation in physical exercise from multiple dimensions, thus overcoming the limitations of single-factor research and providing empirical support for the optimization of rural sports policies. From a practical perspective, this research contributes to revealing the patterns of farmers’ participation, offers valuable references from a configurational viewpoint to increase their physical exercise levels, and provides theoretical underpinnings for advancing national fitness initiatives in rural areas. From a policy perspective, this study lays a scientific foundation for future policy formulation and community development, ultimately aiming to increase farmers’ quality of life and achieve sustainable rural social development.

Literature review and theoretical framework

Theories of socioecology

In the 1970 s, Bronbrenner brought the field of socioecology—a scientific discipline that examines the relationships between human society and ecology—to the study of human development. The model of socioecology takes a macroenvironmental perspective to thoroughly examine how individual behaviors are shaped by a complex interplay of intrinsic factors (such as health status, psychological motivation, personal interests, and self-efficacy) and extrinsic factors (such as family support, community resources, and access to sports facilities) across multiple levels [9]– [10]. The concepts of socioecological theory go beyond the constraints of traditional health promotion theories, stressing diverse and integrative interventions to modify people’s health behaviors. This theory has been steadily applied to the field of physical activity since the 1990 s, and it has been widely used in the domains of health promotion and chronic disease prevention [11]– [12].

The theoretical model of socioecology depicts a dynamic nested structure that can be classified into five layers on the basis of the layers’ proximity to the individual: the policy, community, organizational, interpersonal, and individual levels. However, owing to the diversity of research aims and purposes, academics have different understandings and uses of the model’s dimensional division and the components included in each dimension. For example, a socioecological model is typically constructed using the five individual, interpersonal, institutional, community, and policy levels in studies on teenage physical activity [13]– [14]. However, in a study of women’s sports participation, the Canadian Association for the Advancement of Women in Sport (CAWS) developed the Social Ecology Model of Women’s and Girls’ Sports Participation, which is used to investigate the constraints on women’s participation in sport from four perspectives: the personal, interpersonal, environmental, and policy perspectives [15]. Several studies have examined female sports involvement in three dimensions: demographic sociological factors, the social environment, and the physical environment [16]. This study, which is grounded in the theoretical framework of the socioecological model and is contextualized within the research background, analyzes the factors influencing farmers’ engagement in physical activity across three levels: the individual, interpersonal, and community levels. Among them, the individual level reflects farmers’ fundamental characteristics and capabilities, the interpersonal level emphasizes the influence of social interactions and support on physical activity behavior, and the community level highlights the role of rural resource availability and accessibility in fostering engagement in physical activity. In the selection of specific indicators, the individual level incorporates health status, institutional cultural capital, and specific cultural capital as core variables, primarily on the basis of the premise that health directly influences an individual’s physical capacity, while cultural capital shapes farmers’ perceptions of and attitudes toward physical activity. The interpersonal level includes social interactions, social support, and internet usage, as these factors collectively influence the motivation to engage in physical activity through interaction, encouragement, and the effective utilization of digital resources. At the community level, the availability and accessibility of public sports resources are selected, as they are critical external conditions for farmers’ participation in physical activity, with their access to these resources directly impacting the extent of their engagement. Because of the model’s strong systemic nature and the requirement for individual, interpersonal, and intercommunity synergistic linkages for optimal functioning, its usage in histogram analysis is more applicable and informative.

Literature review

Scholars have examined the primary elements influencing residents’ engagement in physical activity from various perspectives in recent years, and these characteristics are crucial in encouraging residents to participate in physical exercise. From the perspective of individual factors, scholars Zhu Jie and others, through field research, discovered that left-behind rural children—those whose parents work away from home for more than three consecutive months, leaving them under the care of grandparents or other relatives—are influenced by multiple elements in their participation in physical exercise [17]. Among them, personal interest, family economic conditions, and support from the government and society have the most significant impacts on these children’s engagement in physical activities [18]. From the perspective of environmental factors, studies indicate that urban residents’ regular physical activity is influenced by elements such as air pollution and the suitability of community spaces for exercise, whereas rural residents’ participation is affected by factors such as water pollution, noise pollution, the adequacy of community exercise environments, and the availability of facilities [19]. Further research revealed that air pollution tends to induce excessive sedentary behavior, thus diminishing the frequency of physical exercise [20]. Additionally, prolonged exposure to traffic noise is correlated with lower levels of physical activity [21]– [22]. From the perspective of social factors, Wang Lingxin carried out an empirical study with a population of agricultural and nonagricultural household migrants as research subjects. The study’s findings demonstrated that for both types of household migrants, increased institutional cultural capital has a facilitating effect on sports participation, whereas lower social status evaluation has a hindering effect on sports participation. The population of agricultural migrants can increase its frequency of sports participation through frequent internet use, whereas the participation in sports of the population of nonagricultural household migrants can be promoted through social interactions [23].

Furthermore, survey research on the sports participation of rural migrant workers shows that the current level of sports participation is unsatisfactory; constraints at the individual level include a lower awareness of sports, a lower economic income, less leisure time, and greater work intensity [24–26]. The limits at the institutional level can be seen in the fact that there are still certain “institutional barriers” to the openness of sports facilities, preventing migrant workers from fully utilizing these facilities [27–31]. Another study examined the factors influencing the physical activity of the younger generation of migrant workers and discovered that social discrimination was the primary factor limiting these workers’ participation in sports [32].

To summarize, while recent research has investigated the factors influencing rural residents’ participation in physical activity, it has focused primarily on the net effect of a single factor on their participation in physical activity, making it difficult to systematically reveal the complex multifactorial joint mechanism involved in their participation in physical activity. Therefore, using the socioecological model as an analytical framework and the fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) method, this study investigates the multiple drivers of farmers’ participation in physical activity from three perspectives—the individual, interpersonal, and community perspectives—with the goal of providing a more comprehensive scientific basis for encouraging farmers’ participation in physical activity. This study focuses on two issues. The first is the conditions (configurations) that drive farmers to participate in physical activity; that is, this study answers the question of whether “different paths lead to the same thing”. The second issue is whether there are differences in the effects of physical activity on farmers’ well-being driven by different condition-grouping paths; that is, this study answers the question of “whether the same return has the same effect”.

Individual level

Scholars first concentrated on individual-level influences on physical activity. As research has advanced, the variables used to quantify the individual level have broadened to primarily encompass the health level, institutional cultural capital, and specific cultural capital. Health is a necessity for engaging in many activities; furthermore, in the socioecological framework, individual health is a crucial element influencing rural residents’ participation in physical activity. Studies have indicated that a person’s subjective assessment of his or her health has an impact on the person’s inclination and drive to engage in physical activity. Compared to their counterparts, residents who consider themselves to be in excellent health have better levels of physical fitness and lower risks of chronic diseases and are thus more inclined to engage in regular physical activity to increase their physical fitness and prevent them from being pushed out of the labor market [33].

Culture serves as the foundation for physical activity patterns, and societal disparities caused by the unequal distribution of cultural capital can lead to significant differences in individual health behaviors [34]. According to Bourdieu’s theory, cultural capital refers to tangible or intangible assets associated with culture and cultural activities. Cultural capital influences participation in physical activity through characteristics such as an individual’s education, understanding of culture, and adherence to cultural norms; it includes both institutional and specific forms of cultural capital. In general, institutional cultural capital manifests itself through educational acquisition, which is the provision of publicly recognized tangible forms, such as educational diplomas and certificates, to individuals with cultural capital [35]. The vast majority of scholars believe that good educational acquisition has a facilitating effect on an individual’s participation in physical activity and that, in general, the more educated an individual is, the greater the frequency and level of his or her participation in physical activity [36–38]. However, there are significant differences in participation in physical activity between people of different genders and ages and between urban and rural areas, which may be related to socioeconomic conditions, cultural perceptions, and other factors [39]. It has been discovered that farmers with better institutional cultural capital have stronger adaptive and cognitive capacities, which help them select a healthier lifestyle and participate more actively in physical exercise and outdoor activities [40]. It has also been discovered that higher education leads to greater economic wealth, which makes it easier to optimize the allocation of health resources, such as the purchase of fitness equipment, which successfully promotes the frequency of participation in physical activity [41].

Specific cultural capital refers to the many different manifestations of cultural products acquired by individuals through family, school, and social practices, such as visiting museums, watching performances and exhibitions, watching sports competitions, watching films, and engaging in other types of cultural activities, to accumulate individual knowledge, skills, values, behavioral norms, and other specific cultural capital [40]. According to Wang Hongbo, farmers with higher levels of specific cultural capital can actively participate in a diverse variety of cultural activities, which influences individual fitness perceptions [40]. According to Liu Weiqiang’s research, people who engage in more cultural activities lead healthier, more upwardly mobile lifestyles and are consequently more athletically active [42]. In conclusion, increasing levels of physical activity are influenced by both the unique cultural capital that individuals build and the institutional cultural capital that is gained through education.

CInterpersonal level

The interpersonal level examines how individuals’ physical activity is impacted by the structure of their interpersonal relationships. It includes a variety of elements, such as social interaction, social support, and internet use. Social interaction is defined as interpersonal exchanges at both the material and spiritual levels and includes not only cultural awareness but also strong emotional commitment and trust [43]. Social interactions usually involve verbal communication and social network connections; they frequently occur between individuals or groups of people. According to pertinent research, physical activity has important social qualities and is crucial for fostering interpersonal exchange and communication. Moreover, more tightly knit social groups can be created by increasing interpersonal networks during social interactions, which can strengthen the bonds between high-level and low-level participants and encourage physical activity among locals [44]. Through socialization, people exchange physical activity techniques, results, and experiences, thus building a robust social network that increases exercise levels and subjective well-being in life [45]. Downward and other researchers have discovered that physical activities based on social connections can provide individuals with a more enriching workout experience [46]. It has also been noted that social contacts satisfy individuals’ emotional communication and boost their own joyful experiences and that those who have more social interactions are more likely to engage in physical exercise [47].

Furthermore, the interpersonal connections of relatives, neighbors, and friends serve as vital social support systems. Previous studies have shown that social support can increase physical activity in different populations [48]. In general, it can do so by both offering emotional support and sharing the enjoyment of exercise. On the one hand, regular gatherings with relatives who do not live together, as well as social recreational activities with other friends, can provide emotional support and verbal encouragement, assisting individuals in overcoming barriers to physical activity and lowering the psychological threshold to begin exercising, thus increasing motivation and interest [49]. On the other hand, social and recreational activities among neighbors, such as going to one other’s houses, watching TV together, getting together for meals, or playing cards together, give people the chance to converse and learn from one another. In these interactions, peers may plan physical activities. Research has demonstrated that peer-organized physical activity behaviors increase people’s motivation and sense of self-efficacy, which in turn influence their willingness to participate in physical activity. In other words, the demonstration effect of peers can increase the rate at which community members engage in physical activity [50].

In terms of how the internet and physical activity are related, on the one hand, the internet offers a wide platform for the sharing of social media and sports information, such as a range of platforms for fitness and sports socialization, which increases residents’ participation in sports and their ability to interact with one another in sports, which in turn increases their level of physical activity [51]. On the other hand, the usage of the internet has expanded people’s leisure and recreation options, perhaps reducing the amount of time spent exercising. Li Liqing and other researchers used data from the 2018 China Family Tracking Survey (CFPS) to investigate the influence of internet use on rural residents’ physical activity; the findings revealed that internet use considerably lowers the amount and frequency of physical activity among rural people [52]. Thus, the impact of internet use on physical exercise can be positive or negative.

Community level

With the socialization of sports and the gradual establishment of lifelong sports awareness among the public, the community level is critical for encouraging farmers to participate in sports and increasing their overall well-being. According to socioecological theory, individual behavior is the product of multilevel interactions between environmental influences. This research focuses primarily on the physical context in which farmers engage in physical activity at the community level, including the availability of resources and access to public sports services.

According to a report by Huasheng Online, under the “urban‒rural dualistic” public sports service system, rural sports infrastructure is generally underdeveloped, the sports resource supply is relatively weak, and there is a significant disparity between public sports resources in rural and urban areas. In this regard, strengthening urban‒rural coordination, implementing the sharing of public sports resources, creating a “30-minute fitness circle”, and establishing rural sports and fitness guidance stations and physical fitness measurement stations so that rural residents have access to more public sports resources are methods that are conducive to meeting rural residents’ fitness needs and promoting rural households’ participation in physical exercise [53].

Yiming Wang’s study revealed that, in terms of the built environment, rural residents who perceive having adequate public facilities are 1.583 times more likely to participate in regular physical activity than are those who perceive having insufficient public facilities, implying that adequate public sports resources can help promote physical activity [7]. Chen Qiu and other scholars investigated the effects of community sports services by using microdata from farming households in eight Chinese provinces; the findings revealed that the construction of public sports facilities significantly increases community farmers’ participation in sports activities while lowering their risk of illness [54]. Additionally, ease of access to sports resources is crucial for rural households to participate in physical activity. Moreover, this ease of access can be realized by, among other things, offering sports activities that cater to the unique needs of rural areas and by enhancing the distribution and accessibility of sports facilities. Wang Haixia and other scholars noted that reasonable planning should be performed in community construction to create a healthy and supportive environment, including appropriate distances from home to sports venues, health trails, and health theme parks, which can effectively improve farmers’ willingness to engage in physical activity and the frequency with which they do so [19, 55].

The analysis above reveals the driving factors influencing farmers’ participation in physical exercise across three levels: the individual, interpersonal, and community levels. Importantly, these factors do not operate in isolation; rather, they exert their influence through a synergistic interplay. Individual factors, such as health status and cultural capital, shape farmers’ awareness of and capacity for physical activity. Interpersonal factors, including social interactions and support, provide the motivation and external encouragement necessary for sustained exercise. Moreover, community factors, such as the availability and accessibility of sports resources, create an enabling external environment that fosters famers’ engagement in physical fitness. Simultaneously, the adequacy of public sports resources not only directly influences farmers’ participation in physical activities but also amplifies the effects at the individual and interpersonal levels by enhancing the environments for social interaction and promoting the dissemination of health awareness. Research methodology and design

Qualitative comparative analysis

In 1987, the American sociologist Ragin laid the groundwork for qualitative comparative analysis (QCA), which is used to conduct cross-case investigations from an integrative perspective and is grounded in set theory and Boolean algorithms. QCA is a hybrid research method that goes beyond qualitative and quantitative analysis. It is based on the sufficiency and necessity of antecedent conditions and has exceptional advantages in addressing complex causal relationships such as concurrent causation and equivalence.

In this study, the QCA method is used for the following reasons. On the one hand, QCA can clearly reveal the complex causality of enhancing physical activity in farming households and reveal the differential causal paths of enhancing physical activity in farming households by exploring the linkage mechanism and synergistic effects of multiple antecedent variable conditions [56]. On the other hand, traditional linear statistical methods are unable to explain the processes that lead to outcomes because they tend to neglect many contemporaneous correlations among independent variables. In contrast, QCA approaches use aggregation relationships rather than correlation relationships to infer associations between condition variables and outcome variables, and they may identify which conditions and groups are core and edge conditions for a result. As a result, the conclusions drawn using this method are highly accurate [57]. Moreover, on the basis of variable types and research requirements, QCA has evolved into four primary methods: crisp-set qualitative comparative analysis (csQCA), which is suitable for handling binary variables (0/1) to analyze clear set membership relationships; fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA), which allows variables to take continuous values (0–1) to more precisely depict degrees of membership, which is ideal for studying phenomena with gradual changes; time-series qualitative comparative analysis (tsQCA), which incorporates the time dimension to explore how causal relationships evolve over time, which is particularly useful for researching policy changes or social dynamics; and multivalue qualitative comparative analysis (mvQCA), which permits variables to take multiple discrete values, which makes this method suitable for investigating complex causal relationships characterized by multiple categories. Given that the values of the conditional and outcome variables in this study lack distinct thresholds, fsQCA, which allows these variables to belong to a set to varying degrees, is better suited to address the characteristics and data structure of the variables in this research. Therefore, fsQCA is selected for the analysis.

Measurement methods for the variables

Data sources

Data from the 2021 Chinese General Social Survey (CGSS) were utilized in this investigation [58]. The CGSS methodically and comprehensively collects data at all levels: the social, community, family, and individual levels. It is China’s first national, comprehensive, and continuous academic survey program; it encourages the openness and sharing of domestic scientific research, provides data and information for international comparative research, and serves as a multidisciplinary platform for economic and social data collection. The sample selection criteria for this study are as follows. First, the sample was selected from those whose household type was listed as “agricultural” and whose age ranged from 18 to 65 years old; second, those who listed “studying” as their highest level of education were excluded; and third, those with missing values and outliers for some of the required variables were excluded. After screening, 995 valid samples were generated. The valid samples were drawn from 19 provinces (autonomous regions and municipalities), namely, Beijing, Hebei, Henan, Zhejiang, the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, Anhui, Shandong, Sichuan, Shanxi, Hubei, Hunan, Fujian, Gansu, the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, Jiangsu, Jiangxi, Liaoning, Chongqing, and Shanxi.

Variable construction

Definition of the outcome variable

Exercise, which refers to the process of physical activity in which an individual engages in physical exercise as the primary means of improving physical fitness and health, was the outcome variable of his study. This study used the measurement method of Xu Jinfu et al. [44] and chose question A309 from the CGSS2021: “Do you often participate in physical activity during your free time?” The answer was assigned a value, and the higher the value was, the more farmers participated in physical activity.

Conditional variable definitions

The individual-level variables included health status, institutional cultural capital, and specific cultural capital [59]. Health level was determined by the person’s subjective self-assessment of health, which is a strong indicator of present health status and a predictor of future health status and has been frequently employed in studies [60]. This study used questionnaire item A15 from the CGSS2021: “How do you feel about your current physical health?” The answer was assigned a value as a measure of physical health.

Institutional cultural capital refers to cultural capital that is formally recognized by society and conferred through educational systems or other institutionalized channels, such as academic degrees, professional certifications, and specialized titles [61]. Drawing on the research findings of Xiao Hong [35], questionnaire item A7a from the CGSS2021 was selected as the measure: “What is your highest level of education attained?” Specific cultural capital is reflected in enduring physical and mental attributes, as well as enduring attitudes; it can be measured by an individual’s frequency of engagement in cultural activities. For the purpose of this research, we measured this variable with questionnaire item A30 from the CGSS2021: “In the past year, how often did you spend your free time watching TV or DVDs, attending cultural events (concerts, shows and exhibitions), reading books/newspapers/magazines, going out to the movies, watching live sports matches, listening to music at home, doing handicrafts (embroidery, carpentry), and surfing the internet?” Various types of cultural activities were used as observations; after the answers corresponding to each cultural activity were reversed, the scores for the eight questions were averaged to obtain a continuous variable with values ranging from 1 to 5 points; this variable was then used as a foundation for measuring specific cultural capital.

The interpersonal level includes social interactions, social support, and internet use. Social interaction refers to the process by which individuals or groups connect with one another through language, behavior, and communication, thus establishing social relationships [62]. In the CGSS2021, questionnaire item A311 is used to assess social interaction: “In the past year, how often did you socialize/collaborate during your free time?” In the present study, respondents were divided into five levels according to their responses, with higher values suggesting more active social connections. Social support encompasses the emotional sustenance, informational assistance, and tangible aid that individuals receive from their social networks. This support typically includes contributions from relatives, neighbors, and friends, who collectively foster a sense of community and well-being [63]. Thus, this study relied on the approach used in the study of Zhang Zhenggang et al. [64] to assess the frequency with which individuals socialize with relatives, neighbors, and friends. The following questionnaire items were selected from the CGSS2021: A306: “How often did you get together with relatives who did not live with you during the past year?”; A31a: “How often do you engage in social recreational activities (e.g., visiting each other’s homes, watching TV together, eating, playing cards, etc.) with your neighbors?”; and A31b: “How often do you engage in social recreational activities (e.g., visiting each other’s homes, watching TV together, eating, playing cards, etc.) with other friends?” After reversing the answers corresponding to the various socialization frequencies, the scores for the three questions were averaged to produce continuous variables with values ranging from 1 to 5.

Internet use refers to the actions undertaken by individuals or groups to access, acquire, share, and disseminate information via computers, smartphones, or other networked devices [65]. Internet use was measured using CGSS2021 questionnaire item A285: “In the past year, how much have you used the internet (including cell phone access)?” To assess internet use, the responses were assigned values on the basis of the respondents’ frequency of use, with higher values indicating more regular internet use.

At the community level, the provision of public sports services refers to the sports resources, facilities, and activities offered by governments or social organizations to encourage residents’ participation in physical activities and to enhance their health. The core elements encompass resource adequacy and accessibility; resource adequacy pertains to whether the quantity and variety of sports facilities in the residential area meet residents’ needs, whereas accessibility primarily concerns the ease with which residents can utilize public sports resources [66]. The adequacy of resources for public sports services and the ease of access to such services were measured in this study using two specific questionnaire items from the CGSS2021: 36 C: “Are there enough public facilities (e.g., community centers, libraries, parks, etc.) where I live?”; and E36A: “Where I live is suitable for physical activity such as jogging and walking”. The questions and their accompanying responses were inverted; a better status of provision was shown by larger values obtained from processing.

Variable calibration

A fuzzy set can reflect the degree of affiliation of a case between “fully affiliated” and “fully unaffiliated” and can thus be used as a continuous variable. Since the data were scored on a 5-point Likert scale, “5” was utilized in this study to represent “full membership in a set”. Because the distribution of questionnaire data is generally characterized by agglomeration, it was not possible to use “3” for demarcation; instead, the mean value of the actual data in the questionnaire was used as the demarcation line between full affiliation and full nonaffiliation; i.e., the anchor point “1” was used to indicate being “completely unaffiliated with a member of a certain set“ [67]. As a result, the data were calibrated by defining three thresholds and then using fsQCA 3.0 to convert the values into 0–1 affiliation values, with physical activity used as the outcome variable. Analysis of necessary conditions

Prior to the conditional grouping analysis, each antecedent condition’s level of need must be tested independently. In QCA, an antecedent condition is considered required for an outcome if it exists at the time of the event [68]. In a necessary condition analysis, a single conditional variable is required for the resulting variable if its consistency threshold exceeds 0.9 [48]. As indicated in Table 3, the level of consistency for all conditions is less than 0.9; thus, none of the eight conditional variables are required for either promoting or inhibiting farmers’ participation in physical activity. The results of the necessity analysis also reflect the complexity of farmers’ participation in physical activity. The fact that a single condition cannot be a necessary condition for farmers’ participation in physical activity suggests that this participation is the result of the synergistic effect of multiple conditions, such as individual, interpersonal, and community conditions.

Conditional configuration analysis

Conditional grouping analysis is a grouping technique that is used to investigate how adequate various groups composed of several conditional variables are for the outcome variable. Conditional grouping analysis, similar to necessary condition analysis, determines the sufficiency of the grouping using a consistency criterion. In accordance with the research results of Schneider et al. [69], in this paper, the consistency threshold is set to 0.8, the frequency threshold is set to 10 on the basis of the sample size, the PRI threshold is 0.75, and the intermediate solution is used as the main solution, with the parsimonious solution used as the secondary solution to determine the edge and core conditions. Table 4 shows that seven conditional groupings drive farmers to participate in physical activity, and the level of consistency of individual groupings and the overall level of consistency are both greater than 0.75. These results have good explanatory power, indicating that these seven groupings are sufficient conditions for driving farmers to participate in physical activity. The overall level of consistency is 0.7966, indicating that the seven categories account for 79.66% of the sample farmers’ actual physical activity. Moreover, specific cultural capital is shown to be a core conditional presence more frequently than other conditional variables are, implying that it is a significant determinant of farmers’ engagement in physical activity. To further investigate the seven categories, the distribution characteristics of the key condition variables are classified into three driving paths: individual led-dominant, ecological interdependence, and multiple synergistic linkages.

There are three groups in the individual-led dominant pathway. Among these three categories, specific cultural capital is most important for increasing physical activity among farmers, with social contact and internet use playing supporting roles. According to this type of driving pathway, specific cultural capital can be employed on its own as a sufficient condition to explain farmers’ engagement in physical activity, with other factors being comparatively irrelevant or marginal. Research has demonstrated that farmers with high levels of specific cultural capital also accumulate their own specific cultural capital, which includes knowledge, skills, and values. These factors can increase people’s awareness of health issues and behavioral norms and encourage them to take an active role in adopting healthy behaviors, thus increasing their physical activity [35]. These three groupings have consistency levels of 0.8635, 0.8433, and 0.8755 and a raw coverage of 0.3748, 0.2658, and 0.2203, indicating that the three groupings in the driving pathway explain 37.48%, 26.58%, and 22.03% of the samples of farmers who reported participating in physical activity. This driving pattern is most prominent in samples from China’s southeastern coastal regions, such as Fujian Province. The analysis reveals that, in contrast to central Chinese provinces such as Hebei Province, Fujian Province benefits from a favorable geographic location; a stunning natural environment that includes mountain ranges and seashores, among other natural resources; and abundant natural resources that give farmers opportunities to engage in a variety of cultural activities. These opportunities help farmers accumulate specific cultural capital and further motivate their willingness to engage in physical activity. Furthermore, in response to the all-encompassing rural revitalization strategy proposed by the CPC Central Committee, Fujian Province has actively implemented more than 2,400 programs, such as the “Fun Farming Culture Festival”, “Sports and Fitness for Ten Thousand Families”, “Inheriting Farming Culture and Building Harmony and Beauty in Li Fang”, and “Farmers’ Fun Games”, which have attracted approximately 1.2 million participants since 2021. This strong cultural base and social milieu of sports effectively promote farmers’ interest in physical activity, leading to a greater willingness to invest time and energy in sports activities to preserve health and well-being [70].

There are two groupings in the ecological interdependence pathway. In these 2 groups, the health level and social support are central factors, while institutional cultural capital, social interaction, and internet use play supporting roles, and specific cultural capital and ease of access to public sports services are viewed as peripheral conditions, with a potential substitution relationship between them. This result means that when farmers are in good health and receive strong social support, regardless of the availability of public sports service facilities, as long as farmers have a high level of education, strong social interaction, or the ability to use modern information technology, they will be more motivated to participate in physical activity. Research indicates that farmers in better health are more physically fit and vibrant, which is a prerequisite for engaging in physical activity [48]. Simultaneously, receiving support and encouragement from friends, family, and other social networks can improve one’s mental health. This good mental state then positively impacts one’s physical health, resulting in a positive feedback loop between mental and physical well-being. Furthermore, social engagement with neighbors, as well as communication and experience sharing among farmers, creates a “push-pull effect” on physical activity, which is beneficial [50]. In this pathway, groupings 4 and 5 have consistency values of 0.8604 and 0.8675, respectively, and raw coverage values of 0.3072 and 0.2907, respectively, meaning that these 2 groupings account for 30.72% and 29.07% of the sample of farmers who reported engaging in physical activity, respectively. In samples from places such as Sichuan Province, this trend is especially noticeable. Analysis revealed that the Sichuan National Fitness Implementation Plan specifically calls for efforts to increase the popularity of national fitness facilities and programs throughout the province, complete community “15-minute fitness circles” coverage, encourage the growth of national fitness events and programs throughout the region, and regularly host national fitness games, migrant worker games, and other comprehensive games to foster a positive sports environment for farmers and to continuously improve their quality of life as athletes. Furthermore, the community’s implementation of fitness promotion actions for specific groups of people, such as farmers, and the ongoing popularization of scientific fitness knowledge through activities such as scientific fitness “cloud guidance” and “scientific fitness lecture halls” have effectively increased farmers’ health awareness. Farmers who take part in a sequence of activities over time experience not only improvements in their health but also a progressive expansion of their social support system and interpersonal network, which ultimately leads to effective engagement in physical activity [71].

The multiple synergistic linkages path consists of 2 groupings. Specific cultural capital, internet use, and adequate resources are important factors in both groups, although social contact and resource accessibility are secondary. Groups 6 and 7 in this driving pathway have consistency values of 0.8434 and 0.8455, respectively, and raw coverage values of 0.3748 and 0.2391, respectively, suggesting that the two groupings in this pathway explain 23.91% and 21.76% of the sample of farmers who reported engaging in physical activity, respectively. This result means that in areas with sufficient resources for public sports services, farmers with high specific cultural capital and internet usage can still create a strong sports atmosphere by enhancing their social interactions and improving their access to resources for public sports services to achieve the goal of promoting physical activity among farmers, regardless of their physical condition and institutional cultural capital, even if they do not receive social support from relatives and friends. This trend is particularly evident in samples from the Ningxia Autonomous Region and Zhejiang Province. Further investigation revealed that the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region Sports Bureau continues to increase investment support for the construction of public sports facilities, maximizing the utilization of parks and green spaces, community “golden edges and silver corners”, and other fitness facilities. In the region, 1,903 village farmers’ sports and fitness projects, 32 township farmers’ fitness projects, and 49 national fitness facilities have been constructed since 2018 [72]. In the meantime, the district invested up to 920 million yuan in 5G in 2021, reaching a total of 1.71 million 5G subscribers and a network penetration rate of 23.8%. This approach strongly guarantees that farmers are able to access sports facility resources and use the internet, and it serves as a cornerstone for encouraging farmers to participate in sports and exercise [73]. On the other hand, as a reform and opening-up pioneer, Zhejiang Province has focused on the development of sports in society over the last five years, continuously implementing the materialization of sports associations and promoting the intelligentization of venues, thus providing a solid material foundation for physical exercise. Furthermore, the province is currently engaged in a grassroots grid-based national fitness integrated service management pilot project that is investigating the creation of a “three members” working mechanism consisting of grassroots sports commissioners, social sports instructors, and healthy life instructors; it is also bringing national fitness activities to the grassroots and general public. The goal of this initiative is to open up “last kilometer” grassroots sports services [74] and to offer farmers an integrated and shared sports service platform.

Robustness test

A robustness test is carried out using an adjusted case threshold number and an adjusted consistency threshold on the basis of the conclusions of Ming Zhang et al. [75]. The case threshold is changed from 10 to 15, and the consistency level is changed from 0.8 to 0.85 in light of the large sample size of the research data in this paper. There are fewer groupings in the modified test results than in the path combinations in the original study, but none of the conditional variables’ roles or role relationships are significantly altered, thus demonstrating the accuracy and robustness of the findings.

Further analysis: the impact of physical activity on Well-being

There are three main avenues for encouraging physical activity among farmers, as demonstrated by the group analysis above, indicating that there may be “different paths to the same destination” for physical activity among farmers. As a result, to address the second question posed in this research—“Do different paths lead to the same result?“—the impacts of physical exercise on farmers’ well-being under various driving path conditions are further investigated. Well-being refers to an individual’s subjective assessment of his or her overall quality of life, encapsulating the individual’s holistic perception of his or her life conditions. In the field of social science research, well-being is measured primarily through self-reported evaluations. In this study, a questionnaire item from the CGSS2021 was selected for measurement, with the responses “very unhappy”, “unhappy”, “moderately happy”, “happy”, and “very happy” being assigned values of 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5, respectively, serving as the basis for assessing well-being [7].

As shown in Table 5, all three driving paths have a positive and significant effect on the well-being of farmers, effectively enhancing their subjective well-being. Among them, the individual-led dominant approach has a relatively minor improvement effect, whereas the multiple synergistic linkages path has the largest improvement effect. Meanwhile, we conducted a robustness check using an Ordered Logit model, and the direction and significance of the coefficients in the results are consistent with those of the OLS regression. Research has indicated that physical activity is a useful strategy for promoting health because it helps people’s physical health and subjective well-being. For example, according to Donatella’s research, health uncertainty has an impact on residents’ household consumption status and, consequently, their well-being [76]. According to Zhang Yong and other researchers, who examined the relationship between physical activity and subjective well-being from a sociological perspective, people who regularly engage in physical activity show that higher levels of subjective well-being, social interaction and social support are important mediating factors between physical activity and subjective well-being [77]. Additionally, expanding public sports programs can increase locals’ levels of physical activity, which in turn can greatly increase people’s subjective well-being, satisfaction with their social lives, and satisfaction with their surroundings [7, 78]. The research presented above makes it abundantly evident that well-being is positively affected by several factors, including individual, interpersonal, and communal factors. As a result, it is critical to harness these multiple synergistic linkage effects in the process of increasing farming households’ physical activity to more effectively increase farming households’ well-being.

Conclusions and recommendations

Conclusion

This study, grounded in the theoretical framework of socioecology, employs fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis to investigate the critical factors and driving paths that promote physical exercise among farmers. The analysis spans three levels—the individual level, interpersonal level, and community level—and incorporates eight antecedent variables—health status, institutional cultural capital, specific cultural capital, social interaction, social support, internet usage, resource adequacy, and accessibility. Additionally, baseline regression analysis is conducted to examine the impact of different driving pathways on the well-being of rural households. The following conclusions are drawn:

(1) There is no single necessary condition that must be met to encourage farmers to engage in physical activity according to the findings of the necessary conditions study, which revealed that the consistency of all antecedent requirements was less than 0.9. This finding reflects, in part, the complexity of farmers’ participation in physical activity; that is, farmers’ participation in physical activity is the outcome of various factors interacting and functioning together, including interpersonal, community, and individual conditions.

(2) This study identifies seven groups that encourage farmers to engage in physical exercise, which can be classified into three types. (a) Individual-led dominance is a fitness model in which specific cultural capital is evaluated independently. This pathway emphasizes that specific cultural capital is a critical factor influencing farmers’ engagement in physical activity and can be used alone as a sufficient condition for the outcome to occur when farmers’ specific cultural capital is high. (b) Ecological interdependence is a fitness model that considers both physical health and social support. This pathway highlights the significance of the relationship between farmers’ increased social support for physical activity and their high degree of physical fitness. (c) Multiple synergistic linkages, with specific cultural capital, internet use and resource adequacy values as core conditions combined with social interaction and ease of access to resource values, are used to explain the adaptation model together. This pathway suggests that farmers’ accumulation of specific cultural capital, internet use, and the sufficiency of public sports service resource provision play critical roles in farmers’ long-term participation in physical activity, with farmers’ social interaction capacity and ease of access to public sports service resources playing supporting roles.

(3) Driven by a combination of complex factors, the physical activity of farmers has significantly improved, leading to a notable increase in their sense of well-being. Among the pathways, the multiple synergistic linkages pathway has a greater impact on improving well-being than do the ecological interdependence and individual-led dominance pathways.

Recommendations

Strengthening physical activity and health education to increase farmers’ awareness of physical activity

Physical activity and health education play pivotal roles in enhancing farmers’ rates of participation in physical activities and in improving their health literacy. First, governments, universities, and social organizations should strengthen physical activity and health education in rural areas, design personalized fitness programs tailored to the needs of farmers, and utilize media such as radio broadcasts and short videos to disseminate scientific fitness knowledge. Second, it is essential to regularly organize “Scientific Fitness in Rural Areas” initiatives, inviting fitness trainers and medical experts to provide professional exercise guidance. Simultaneously, schools should be encouraged to enhance their physical education curricula, foster exercise habits among young people and cultivate a “family fitness culture.”

Establishing a social support system for rural sports to enhance interactivity

A robust social support system plays a pivotal role in fostering the sustained commitment of rural residents to physical exercise. First, rural communities should organize various sports groups to enhance social interactions and increase rural households’ participation in physical exercise. Government agencies and social organizations can establish dedicated funds to support rural sports activities, encouraging village committees to regularly host sports events, thus fostering a culture of nationwide fitness. Second, the pivotal role of rural leaders should be leveraged to inspire broader farmer engagement in physical exercise through exemplary actions. Concurrently, governments should collaborate with enterprises and nonprofit organizations to provide sports equipment and professional guidance while utilizing internet platforms to establish online sports communities. This approach will enhance farmers’ motivation for physical activity and foster the communal development of sports initiatives [79].

Enhancing the rural public sports service system to improve resource accessibility

The refinement of rural public sports services directly influences the enthusiasm and sustainability of farmers’ engagement in physical exercise. First, the government should intensify the construction of rural sports facilities, strategically allocating resources such as fitness squares, sports parks, and basketball courts to bridge the urban–rural sports resource gap and enhance the accessibility of physical exercise for rural residents. Second, it is essential to strengthen the management and maintenance of rural sports facilities by establishing community-based management models that encourage villagers to collectively participate in daily upkeep, thus maximizing their utilization efficiency. Finally, fostering the involvement of social capital through public‒private partnership (PPP) models can alleviate fiscal pressures and promote the shared use of sports venues by schools, enterprises, and institutions [80].

Outlook

Although this study offers fresh insights into farmers’ reasons for engaging in physical activity, it has certain shortcomings that require additional investigation. First, only eight condition variables were examined in this study since the fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) method has limitations when working with condition variables with medium sample sizes. Owing to the wide range of factors impacting physical activity among farmers, not all potential influences were fully accounted for within the scope of the study. Future research can consider reducing the study area by selecting farming households in China at the prefecture or city level and adding other influencing factors to enhance the results. Second, this study examined only cross-sectional data due to data availability limitations; it did not evaluate the group paths or factors affecting physical activity among farmers over an extended period of time. Thus, to improve the findings and generalizability of the conclusions, future research should consider creating pertinent databases to fully explain the intricate impact mechanisms of physical activity in farming households at various stages from a time series perspective.

Association between preoperative anemia and postoperative delirium in elderly patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty with combined spinal-epidural anesthesia: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Xianting Ke, Yi Liu, Xingyin Chen, Qiuyue Jiang, Hongrui Zhu, Qing Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe effect of preoperative anemia on postoperative delirium (POD) risk in elderly patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty (THA) with spinal-epidural anesthesia remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the association of preoperative anemia and its severity on POD occurrence in this population.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included 609 patients aged ≥ 65 years who underwent their first elective total THA with CSE between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2024. Patients were stratified by preoperative anemia status using WHO criteria as hemoglobin < 13 g/dL males and < 12 g/dL females. Anemia severity was further stratified into mild or moderate-to-severe categories using a hemoglobin cutoff of 11 g/dL. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was employed to adjust for potential confounders. The primary outcome was the incidence of postoperative delirium. The secondary outcomes included in-hospital deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary infection, surgical site infection, and length of hospital stay (LOS).ResultsThe overall POD incidence was 4.5 % (24/538). Moderate-to -severe anemia was significantly associated with increased POD risk (odds ratio [OR] 3.29; 95 % confidence interval [CI], 1.21–8.96; P = 0.02). No statistically significant differences were observed for secondary outcomes between groups.ConclusionsModerate-to-severe preoperative anemia was significantly associated with an increased risk of postoperative delirium in elderly patients undergoing THA with combined spinal-epidural anesthesia. Further prospective studies are warranted to evaluate the efficacy of specific interventions aimed at correcting preoperative anemia in this patient population.

Introduction

The effect of preoperative anemia on postoperative delirium (POD) risk in elderly patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty (THA) with spinal-epidural anesthesia remains unclear.1, 2 With the aging of the global population, the number of elderly individuals undergoing THA is steadily increasing, paralleled by a rising incidence of perioperative complications.3 Among these, postoperative delirium (POD) represents a common acute neuropsychiatric syndrome observed in the elderly population, typically emerging within hours to days post-surgery.4, 5 POD is characterized by abrupt disturbances in consciousness, attention deficits, and fluctuating cognitive function. It contributes to extended hospital stays and increased in-hospital mortality. Moreover, it is associated with long-term cognitive impairment, functional decline, and elevated utilization of healthcare resources. Given this, the issue constitutes a significant challenge in perioperative management.6, 7

Recognized risk factors for POD are multifactorial and include advanced age, preexisting cognitive impairment or psychiatric comorbidities, as well as complex or high risk surgical and anesthetic procedures.8, 9 Moreover, contributory environmental factors, such as sleep disruption, have been shown to exacerbate susceptibility to POD.10 Notwithstanding considerable progress in elucidating the underlying mechanisms, there is still a paucity of effective therapeutic agents.11, 12

Preoperative anemia, characterized by hemoglobin concentrations below sex-specific diagnostic thresholds, generally < 13 g/dL in males and < 12 g/dL in females, is prevalent in patients undergoing THA, with reported incidence ranging from 15 % to 40 % closely linked to advanced age and chronic comorbidities.13, 14 This hematologic abnormality is closely associated with advanced chronological age and concomitant chronic comorbid conditions. Functioning as an independent risk factor that impairs systemic oxygen transport and microvascular perfusion, anemia correlates with increased incidence of adverse postoperative sequelae, including surgical site infections, major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events (MACCE), and elevated mortality rates.15 Accumulating data implicate cerebral hypoxemia secondary to anemia and sustained cerebral hypoperfusion in the pathophysiology of POD.7, 16, 17

Although several multicenter cohort and retrospective studies have established preoperative anemia as an independent risk factor for POD in elderly noncardiac and orthopedic surgical populations, conflicting evidence persists.18, 19, 20 For instance, studies investigating colorectal cancer resections have failed to demonstrate statistically significant correlations between anemia and major perioperative adverse events.21 The multifactorial pathogenesis of POD and between-cohort heterogeneity in surgical populations impede the establishment of conclusive evidence regarding anemia’s independent etiological contribution, mechanistic pathways, and prognostic implications.

Currently, there exists a dearth of high-quality, targeted evidence specifically addressing elderly patients undergoing total THA. Clarifying the independent influence of preoperative anemia on POD and perioperative morbidity within this population is of significant clinical importance. This issue is particularly salient in China. Rapid demographic aging is occurring alongside increasing rates of orthopedic surgical interventions among the elderly population. Given this, it is imperative to identify modifiable risk factors in order to improve patient outcomes and optimize perioperative management strategies.

This study aims to elucidate the influence of preoperative anemia on the occurrence of POD in elderly patients undergoing THA, thereby facilitating the development of evidence-supported strategies for perioperative blood management and POD prevention by clinicians and researchers.

Method

Study design and population

This single-center retrospective study used data from Taihe Hospital’s electronic records, anesthesia, and surgical documentation. The study period spanned from January 1, 2020, to December 31, 2024. The research protocol received approval by the Institutional Ethics Committee (Ethics No.: [WZ2025KS101]). Given that the study involved anonymized retrospective data without any direct patient interventions, the requirement for obtaining informed consent was waived. Eligible participants consisted of elderly inpatients aged 65 years or older who underwent their initial elective primary Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA) within the designated study period. Inclusion criteria included age ≥ 65 years on the day of surgery, who received a standard primary THA, and for whom preoperative complete blood count data were available. Exclusion criteria comprised emergency surgical procedures, history of prior hip replacement or revision arthroplasty, documented preoperative cognitive impairment or psychiatric disorders, terminal malignancies or end-stage renal failure affecting long-term prognosis, incomplete or missing critical intraoperative or postoperative data, and procedures performed under general anesthesia. This manuscript adheres to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.22

Data collection and variables

Data were systematically extracted from hospital electronic medical records, anesthesia charts, and surgical documentation to ensure data consistency and comparability. Baseline characteristics collected included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification (patients were grouped into ASA I–II and ASA III–IV categories due to limited numbers in ASA I and IV). Additional baseline variables comprised smoking status and alcohol consumption. Preoperative laboratory parameters within 24 h prior to surgery encompassed hemoglobin concentration, white blood cell count, and platelet count. Documented comorbidities included hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Intraoperative data included the volumes of allogeneic red blood cell transfusions administered. A total of 15 patients (2.5 % of the initially enrolled cohort) were found to have missing key data, primarily baseline variables such as height and weight, which were considered to be missing at random.

The primary exposure variable was preoperative anemia status, defined according to the World Health Organization’s sex-specific hemoglobin thresholds: anemia group were classified as those with hemoglobin concentrations below 130 g/L for males and below 120 g/L for females. whereas nonanemic patients exhibited hemoglobin levels meeting or exceeding these respective cutoffs. Anemia severity was further stratified into mild anemia (hemoglobin 110–129 g/L for males, 110–119 g/L for females) and moderate to severe anemia (hemoglobin <110 g/L) categories.23

Study outcomes

The primary outcome measured was the incidence of postoperative delirium (POD) during the hospitalization period, identified via standardized assessments and comprehensive clinical documentation. Diagnosis was established by specialists through integration of nursing records, sedation-delirium assessment scales, and clinical manifestations including altered level of consciousness, fluctuating cognitive status, impaired attention, disorganized speech, and visual or auditory hallucinations. The evaluation spanned the entire postoperative hospitalization period. Secondary outcomes included Deep vein thrombosis (DVT), confirmed by lower extremity Doppler ultrasonography; pulmonary infection, diagnosed based on clinical symptoms, imaging findings, and laboratory results; Surgical site infection, defined according to Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) criteria; and length of hospital stay (LOS), calculated from admission to discharge. All outcomes were ascertained and defined using standardized criteria to ensure diagnostic accuracy and comparability across cases.

Statistical analysis

Given the low proportion of missing data (<5 %) and the likelihood of a completely random missing pattern, the complete case analysis method was applied. Accordingly, the 15 patients with missing data were excluded from the final analysis. To mitigate confounding bias attributable to baseline disparities, inverse probability weighting (IPW) based on propensity scores was employed to achieve balance in demographic variables, comorbidities, and preoperative conditions between the anemia and non-anemia cohorts. Propensity scores were derived using multivariate logistic regression incorporating covariates including age, sex, BMI, ASA, diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, cerebrovascular disease, preoperative white blood cell and platelet counts, as well as intraoperative allogeneic red blood cell transfusion.

Covariate balance post-weighting was evaluated via standardized mean differences (SMD), with an SMD threshold of less than 0.1 considered indicative of satisfactory balance. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), contingent upon data distribution, while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Intergroup comparisons utilized independent t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests for continuous variables and chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables, as appropriate.

Associations between preoperative anemia status and study outcomes were examined employing weighted logistic regression models, with effect measures expressed as odds ratios (ORs) accompanied by 95 % confidence intervals (CIs). For the continuous outcome variable of hospital length of stay, weighted linear regression analyses estimated mean differences (Estimate) between groups, with corresponding 95 % CIs and P-values to ascertain statistical significance and clinical relevance.

Subgroup analyses stratified by age (≤75 versus >75 years), sex, BMI classifications, ASA class, smoking history, and intraoperative transfusion status were conducted, incorporating interaction terms to assess potential effect modification.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.5.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Two-sided hypothesis testing was applied, and P-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Clinical outcomes associated with preoperative anemia

The incidence of POD was 6.1 % (16/261) in the anemia group compared to 2.9 % (8/277) in the non-anemia group. Adjusted analyses revealed no statistically significant association between preoperative anemia and POD (adjusted odds ratio [OR] 2.28; 95 % confidence interval [CI], 0.94–5.53; P > 0.05). Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analyses identified significant effect modification by sex and smoking status in the relationship between preoperative anemia and POD. Specifically, preoperative anemia was significantly associated with an elevated risk of POD among female patients (OR 4.18; 95 % CI, 1.07–23.83; P = 0.03) and non-smokers (OR 2.98; 95 % CI, 0.99–10.79; P = 0.035) (Fig. 3).Fig. 3Forest plot of ORs by patient subgroups.Fig. 3

Discussion

This study, conducted within an elderly population undergoing Total Hip Arthroplasty (THA) with combined spinal-epidural anesthesia (CSE), sought to elucidate the relationship between preoperative anemia and its severity with the incidence of postoperative delirium (POD). The results suggest no significant association between preoperative anemia and increased POD risk in the overall cohort. A particularly insightful finding is that stratified analyses showed a statistically significant increase in POD risk among patients exhibiting moderate-to-severe anemia. This observation highlights the possible clinical significance of targeted preoperative anemia management. Optimizing hemoglobin levels in this vulnerable subgroup could serve as an important approach to mitigating the incidence of POD in elderly patients undergoing combined spinal-epidural anesthesia for THA. Given this, further investigations are warranted to substantiate the efficacy of hemoglobin optimization protocols in improving perioperative outcomes within this population.

Notably, the observed significant correlation between moderat- to-severe preoperative anemia and POD concords with evidence reported in extensive international cohorts. Oh et al. conducted a rigorous single-center retrospective study in South Korea involving patients aged over 60 who underwent non-cardiac surgical procedures. Utilizing WHO-defined anemia criteria, their study identified a significant association between preoperative anemia and POD. They also observed a dose-response relationship correlating anemia severity with the risk of delirium. Based on these findings, the study suggests that both the presence and severity of preoperative anemia contribute significantly to the risk of POD in this patient population.18 Correspondingly, investigations concentrating on hip fracture surgeries corroborate these conclusions: Wang et al. documented a significant association between diminished preoperative hemoglobin concentrations and POD in a cohort of 889 femoral neck fracture patients.24

Nonetheless, several studies have failed to confirm a similar relationship. For example, within cohorts undergoing cardiac surgery, a study comprising 585 patients found no statistically significant association between postoperative hemoglobin levels and the occurrence of delirium.25 Although methodological heterogeneity in surgical interventions, anesthetic techniques, delirium diagnostic criteria, anemia severity classifications, and the scope of confounder adjustments may account for these discrepancies, the boundary conditions for this conclusion lie in the specific patient population and the rigor of anemia stratification and statistical adjustment employed.

This study employed the World Health Organization (WHO) standard to define preoperative anemia, despite ongoing debate regarding its applicability to elderly populations—a debate centered on the poorly defined concept of “age-related physiological hemoglobin decline”.26 Presently, there is a lack of prospectively validated, POD-specific hemoglobin thresholds for elderly patients undergoing THA. In this context, this investigation exclusively enrolled elderly patients undergoing THA with CSE and rigorously balancing baseline covariates using Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW), our investigation provides a robust methodological framework. This approach minimizes confounding bias and allows for a more precise delineation of the relationship between anemia severity and POD.

While no significant association was found between anemia in general and postoperative delirium (POD), a substantially higher risk in patients with moderate-to-severe anemia (OR = 3.29) indicates a potential dose-response relationship. This elevated risk in the moderate-to-severe cohort may be attributed to the impairment of cerebral oxygen delivery, which primarily results from a reduction in hemoglobin concentration and the consequent decrease in the blood’s oxygen-carrying capacity. Evidence from prior investigations, including studies in patients with severe traumatic brain injury, indicates that hemoglobin levels below approximately 90 g/L (9 g/dL) are correlated with marked decreases in cerebral tissue oxygen tension (PbtO₂). The concomitant presence of anemia and cerebral hypoxia (PbtO₂ < 20 mmHg) significantly exacerbates the risk of neuronal injury and unfavorable neurological outcomes.27 Severe anemia induces compensatory cerebral vasodilation to augment cerebral blood flow; however, this physiological adjustment may concurrently reduce microvascular transit time, impairing the efficiency of oxygen extraction at the tissue level. This mechanism is likely to contribute significantly to the pathophysiology of postoperative delirium.28, 29

Despite the methodological rigor of this study, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the overall incidence of postoperative delirium (POD) was relatively low (24 out of 538 cases, 4.5 %), and the limited sample size may have reduced statistical power, potentially hindering the identification of correlations across the entire anemic population. Furthermore, the small number of events may have compromised the robustness of the multivariable models, affecting the precision of subgroup effect estimates, particularly within the moderate-to-severe anemia cohort (n = 95). As a single-center study, the external validity and generalizability of the findings should be interpreted with caution. While the single-center design ensured consistency in data collection and quality control, the conclusions may not fully capture variations among institutions or regions in anemia screening strategies, perioperative management protocols, or delirium diagnostic criteria. Future large-scale, multicenter prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings and strengthen the reliability of statistical inferences. Moreover, the retrospective design of the study inherently introduces potential information and selection biases, including an increased probability of more thorough documentation of delirium in patients deemed at higher risk. Ascertainment bias is also plausible, particularly with respect to mild or subclinical POD cases that may have been under-recognized or underdiagnosed. Such biases could attenuate the observed association between preoperative anemia and POD. Nonetheless, the statistically significant correlation identified within the moderate-to-severe anemia subgroup substantiates the credibility of this relationship.

Conclusions

This analysis suggests a potential association between moderate to severe preoperative anemia and postoperative delirium in elderly THA patients, with possible clinical implications. The findings indicate that prospective multicenter studies may be warranted to further validate these preliminary results, investigate underlying pathophysiological mechanisms, and potentially contribute to improved perioperative outcomes in this vulnerable patient population.

An ℓ 1 \ell^{1} -Isometric Subspace of C ⁡ [ 0 , 1 ] C[0,1] with Countable Oscillating Spectrum
Geivison Ribeiro

Abstract
. In this paper we construct a closed subspace X ⊂ C ⁡ [ 0 , 1 ] X\subset C[0,1] with countable oscillating spectrum Ω ⁡ ( X ) \Omega(X) such that X X is isometric to ℓ 1 \ell^{1} . This provides a negative answer to Question 4.3 posed by Enflo, Gurariy, and Seoane in [Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 366 (2014)].

1.
Introduction

The geometry of closed subspaces of
C[0,1]
has been the subject of intensive research in recent decades,
particularly in connection with lineability and spaceability phenomena
(see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
1
]
).
This line of work traces back to Gurariy
[
6
]
, who initiated the systematic study of large linear structures inside sets of pathological functions,
and to subsequent results of Rodríguez-Piazza
[
8
]
and Hencl
[
7
]
on isometric embeddings of Banach spaces into spaces of nowhere differentiable functions.

A landmark contribution in this direction was made by Enflo, Gurariy, and Seoane in
[
5
]
,
who introduced the notion of the
oscillating spectrum

\Omega(F)
of an subset
F\subset C[0,1]
.
They proved that if
X
is a closed subspace and
\Omega(X)
is finite, then
X
is isomorphic to a subspace of
c_{0}
,
and asked whether the same conclusion holds under the weaker assumption that
\Omega(X)
is countable
(Question 4.3 in
[
5
]
).

More recently, Xie
[
9
]
established a full characterization of oscillating spectra:
a subset
M\subset[0,1]
arises as
\Omega(X)
for some closed subspace
X\subset C[0,1]

if and only if
M
is an
F_{\sigma}
set.
In particular, every such
M
can be realized as the oscillating spectrum of a complemented subspace of
C[0,1]
.

In this paper we construct a closed subspace
X\subset C[0,1]
with countable oscillating spectrum
\Omega(X)

such that
X
is isometric to
\ell^{1}
.
This provides a negative answer to Question 4.3 of Enflo–Gurariy–Seoane,
showing that the finite case cannot be extended to the countable case.
Our approach also highlights the interplay between oscillating spectra and isometric embeddings of
\ell^{1}

into
C[0,1]
, contributing to the broader program of understanding the geometry of special subspaces of
C[0,1]
.

Structure of the paper.

In Section
2
we recall the definitions of oscillation and oscillating spectrum.
In Section
3
we summarize the established results in the literature, in particular those of Enflo–Gurariy–Seoane and of Xie.
In Section
4
we outline our general strategy, which relies on separating
\ell^{1}
from subspaces of
c_{0}
.
Section
5
presents the explicit construction of a sequence of functions that generate our subspace.
Finally, Section
6
establishes the main theorems: the isometric embedding
\ell^{1}\hookrightarrow C[0,1]

and the fact that the resulting space has countable oscillating spectrum.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect the basic notions required for our study. We begin by formally defining the concepts of oscillation and oscillating spectrum, following
[
9
]
.

For
t_{0}\in[0,1]
and
d>0
, we denote by

I(t_{0},d):=[0,1]\cap[t_{0}-d,\;t_{0}+d]

the closed interval of center
t_{0}
and radius
d
, restricted to
[0,1]
.

From now on, the term
subspace
will always mean a closed subspace. Whenever we need to refer to a linear subspace without assuming closedness, we shall explicitly write
linear subspace
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\in C[0,1]
and let
I\subset[0,1]
be a closed interval.
The
oscillation
of
f
on
I
is defined as

\mathcal{O}_{I}(f)=\sup_{s,t\in I}|f(s)-f(t)|.

Given a subspace
X\subset C[0,1]
, we define

\Gamma_{X}(t):=\sup\big\{a\geq 0:\ \forall d>0\ \exists f\in X,\ \|f\|=1,\ \mathcal{O}_{I(t,d)}(f)>a\big\}.

The set of points
t\in[0,1]
for which
\Gamma_{X}(t)>0
is called the
oscillating spectrum
of
X
, and is denoted by
\Omega(X)
. More precisely,

\Omega(X):=\{t\in[0,1]:\Gamma_{X}(t)>0\}.

When no confusion may arise, we shall simply refer to
\Omega(X)
as the spectrum of
X
.

For further details on the oscillating spectrum we refer the reader to
[
5
,
9
]
.

3.
Established results from the literature

Theorem 3.1

(
[
5
]
, Theorem 4.2)
.

If

is a subspace with finite oscillating spectrum

,
then

is isomorphic to a subspace of
c_{0}
.

The theorem above summarizes what is known so far about the structure of subspaces with finite spectrum.
Concerning the general form of oscillating spectra, Xie
[
9
]
characterized which subsets of the interval
[0,1]
can arise as spectra, as recorded in the following result.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
9
]
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2)
.

A subset
M\subset[0,1]
is an
F_{\sigma}
set if and only if there exists a subspace

X\subset C[0,1]
such that
\Omega(X)=M
.
Moreover, one can choose

.

Xie’s result highlights the naturality of studying examples in which
\Omega(X)
is countable,
since every countable set is an
F_{\sigma}
.

4.
Our strategy

We now present the explicit construction of a subspace
X
that is isometric to
\ell^{1}
and has countable oscillating spectrum.
For this purpose, we begin with two preparatory results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
E\subset X
a
closed
subspace.
Consider the restriction map

R:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow E^{\prime},\qquad R(\varphi)=\varphi|_{E}.

Then
R
is linear, continuous, and surjective. Moreover, if we set

E^{\perp}:=\{\varphi\in X^{\prime}:\ \varphi|_{E}=0\},

then the quotient operator

\mathcal{T}:\ X^{\prime}/E^{\perp}\longrightarrow E^{\prime},\qquad\mathcal{T}([\varphi])=\varphi|_{E},

is an isometric isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
R:X^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
be the restriction map,
R(\varphi)=\varphi|_{E}
.
It is immediate that
R
is linear and
\|R(\varphi)\|\leq\|\varphi\|
, hence
R
is continuous with
\|R\|\leq 1
.

The kernel is precisely
E^{\perp}:=\{\varphi\in X^{\prime}:\varphi|_{E}=0\}
, which is closed.
By the Hahn–Banach theorem, for every
\psi\in E^{\prime}
there exists
\varphi\in X^{\prime}
with
\varphi|_{E}=\psi
and
\|\varphi\|=\|\psi\|
, showing that
R
is surjective.

Define
\mathcal{T}:X^{\prime}/E^{\perp}\to E^{\prime}
by
\mathcal{T}([\varphi])=\varphi|_{E}
. This map is well defined, linear, and bijective by the above considerations.
We now compare the norms. For
\varphi\in X^{\prime}
,

\|[\varphi]\|_{X^{\prime}/E^{\perp}}=\inf_{\eta\in E^{\perp}}\|\varphi+\eta\|\geq\|\varphi|_{E}\|.

Conversely, let
\psi=\varphi|_{E}
. By Hahn–Banach there exists
\Phi\in X^{\prime}
with
\Phi|_{E}=\psi
and
\|\Phi\|=\|\psi\|
. Then
\eta:=\Phi-\varphi\in E^{\perp}
, and

\|[\varphi]\|_{X^{\prime}/E^{\perp}}\leq\|\varphi+\eta\|=\|\Phi\|=\|\psi\|=\|\varphi|_{E}\|.

Hence
\|[\varphi]\|=\|\varphi|_{E}\|
for all
\varphi\in X^{\prime}
.
Therefore
\mathcal{T}
is an isometric isomorphism.
∎

With this lemma in hand, we can establish the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

The space
\ell^{1}
is not isomorphic to any subspace of
c_{0}
.

Proof.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that there exists a closed subspace
E\subset c_{0}
isomorphic to
\ell^{1}
.
Since
(\ell^{1})^{\prime}=\ell^{\infty}
, we would have

E^{\prime}\cong\ell^{\infty}.

On the other hand, applying Lemma
4
to
E
and using that
(c_{0})^{\prime}=\ell^{1}
, we obtain

E^{\prime}\cong(c_{0})^{\prime}/E^{\perp}=\ell^{1}/E^{\perp},

that is,
E^{\prime}
is a
quotient
of
\ell^{1}
.

Since
\ell^{1}
is separable, all its quotients are also separable.
Hence
E^{\prime}
would be separable, whereas
\ell^{\infty}
is non-separable — a contradiction.
Therefore, such a subspace
E
cannot exist.
∎

Claim 5.1

If

Since

6.
Main results

In this section we complete the analysis of the construction in
section 5
.
First, we show that the subspace

X:=T(\ell^{1})\subset C[0,1]

is
isometric
to
\ell^{1}
.
Then we prove that the oscillation spectrum of

\Omega(X)=M=\{1/n:\ n\geq 1\}\cup\{0\}.

Taken together, these two statements provide a negative answer to Question 4.3 of

:
there exist subspaces with a

Let us recall some notation. For each

\Sigma_{m}:=\{\pm 1\}^{m}=\{\,\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}):\ \sigma_{j}\in\{\pm 1\}\,\},

and we denote by

\sigma|3=\sigma
.
Hence

J_{\sigma}\subset J_{\sigma|2}\subset J_{\sigma|1}.

The next lemma lets us estimate the sup norm of linear combinations, as will be used throughout this section.

Lemma 6.2
.

Fix
m\geq 1
and
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})\in\Sigma_{m}
.
For every
k\leq m
, the function
\varphi_{k}
is constant on
J_{\sigma|k}
with value
\sigma_{k}
.
In particular, for every
t\in\operatorname{int}(J_{\sigma})
,

\varphi_{k}(t)=\sigma_{k},\qquad k=1,\dots,m.

Proof.

By definition of
\varphi_{k}
, at level
k
we have
\varphi_{k}\equiv\tau_{k}
on each
J_{\tau}
,
for
\tau\in\Sigma_{k}
, and the transitions of
\varphi_{k}
are confined to the gluing strips.
As those strips do not intersect
J_{\tau}
, the function remains constant on
J_{\tau}
.
Applying this to
\tau=\sigma|k
, we obtain
\varphi_{k}\equiv\sigma_{k}
on
J_{\sigma|k}
.
Since
J_{\sigma}\subset\operatorname{int}(J_{\sigma|k})
, any
t\in\operatorname{int}(J_{\sigma})
lies in
J_{\sigma|k}
,
whence
\varphi_{k}(t)=\sigma_{k}
.
∎

The lemma above formalizes that any length-
m
“sign password” is realized in the interior of a level-
m
block
J_{\sigma}
.
This will be useful for the next result.

Theorem 6.3
.

For every
a=(a_{m})\in\ell^{1}
,

\Big\|\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}\,\varphi_{m}\Big\|_{\infty}\;=\;\sum_{m\geq 1}|a_{m}|.

In particular, the linear map

T:\ell^{1}\longrightarrow X,\qquad T(a)=\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}\varphi_{m},

is an isometric isomorphism between
\ell^{1}
and
X
.

Proof.

The inequality “
\leq
” is immediate from
\|\varphi_{m}\|_{\infty}=1
and the subadditivity of the sup norm.

For the reverse inequality, let
\varepsilon>0
and choose
M
such that
\sum_{m>M}|a_{m}|<\varepsilon
.
Define
\sigma_{m}:=\mathrm{sign}(a_{m})
for
1\leq m\leq M
(with the convention
\mathrm{sign}(0)=+1
)
and set
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{M})
.
By
section 6
, there exists
t\in\operatorname{int}(J_{\sigma})
with
\varphi_{m}(t)=\sigma_{m}
for
m\leq M
.
Then

\Bigl|\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}\varphi_{m}(t)\Bigr|\ \geq\ \sum_{m\leq M}|a_{m}|-\sum_{m>M}|a_{m}|\ \geq\ \sum_{m\geq 1}|a_{m}|-\varepsilon.

Taking the supremum over
t
and letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields

\bigl\|\sum a_{m}\varphi_{m}\bigr\|_{\infty}\geq\sum|a_{m}|
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to analyze the
oscillation spectrum
of
X
.
The route is in two steps: first we show that, in every neighbourhood of each
t_{n}\in M
,
there appear ramps ensuring positive oscillation; then we verify that, away from
M
, there is
no local oscillation. These two facts together lead to the main result of this section.

Lemma 6.4
.

For each
t_{n}\in M
and each
d>0
, there exists
m_{0}=m_{0}(n,d)
such that, for every
m\geq m_{0}
,
there is a transition interval
U_{n,m}=(\alpha_{n,m},\beta_{n,m})\in\mathcal{U}_{m}
with

U_{n,m}\subset I(t_{n},d)
whose endpoints lie in blocks
J_{\sigma},J_{\tau}
with

\sigma_{m}\neq\tau_{m}
. In particular,

O_{I(t_{n},d)}(\varphi_{m})\ \geq\ O_{U_{n,m}}(\varphi_{m})\ =\ |\sigma_{m}-\tau_{m}|\ \geq 1.

Proof.

By the nested construction of the blocks
\{J_{\sigma}\}
around
M
and of the transition strips

\mathcal{U}_{m}=\mathcal{U}^{\rm int}_{m}\cup\mathcal{U}^{\rm ext}_{m},

the family
\bigcup_{m\geq 1}\overline{\mathcal{U}_{m}}
accumulates exactly at
M
.
Thus, for fixed
n
and
d>0
, there is
m_{0}
such that, for every
m\geq m_{0}
, we find

U_{n,m}\in\mathcal{U}_{m}
with
\overline{U}_{n,m}\subset I(t_{n},d)
.
By rules (J)–(C)–(U), each
U=(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{U}_{m}
connects two adjacent constant plateaus
(blocks or components) and, whenever these carry distinct signs in the
m
-th digit,
the affine splice (
2
) realizes a nonzero variation across
U
.
The alternation of the
m
-th signs along sibling pairs (cf.
section 6
) ensures
\sigma_{m}\neq\tau_{m}

for some
U_{n,m}\subset I(t_{n},d)
, yielding the desired estimate.
∎

Next we show that, outside
M
, positive oscillations cannot occur: in a sufficiently small neighbourhood,
all generators remain constant.

Lemma 6.5
.

If
t\notin M
, there exists
d_{*}(t)>0
such that
I(t,d_{*})\cap\mathcal{U}_{m}=\varnothing
for all
m\geq 1
.
In particular, for each
m
, the function
\varphi_{m}
is constant on
I(t,d_{*})
and, therefore,
any finite combination
\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}\varphi_{k}
is also constant on
I(t,d_{*})
.

Proof.

Since
\bigcup_{m}\overline{\mathcal{U}_{m}}
accumulates only at
M
, there exists
d_{*}>0
with

I(t,d_{*})\cap\overline{\mathcal{U}_{m}}=\varnothing
for every
m
.
By (J) and (C),
\varphi_{m}
is constant on each block
J_{\sigma}
and on each component

C\in\mathcal{D}_{m}
; moreover, at boundaries where a
C
is adjacent to a
J_{\sigma}

with no transition interval in between, we have
\varepsilon(C)=\sigma_{m}
, i.e., no jump:
the function matches on both sides.
Since
I(t,d_{*})
meets no transitions, it is a union of such pieces glued with no variation,
and
\varphi_{m}
is constant on
I(t,d_{*})
.
A finite sum of constants is constant, as claimed.
∎

With the two lemmas at hand, we finally arrive at the
main result
of this section:
the exact identification of the oscillation spectrum of
X
.

Theorem 6.6
.

We have
\Omega(X)=M
.

Proof.

Fix
t_{n}\in M
and
d>0
. By
section 6
, there exists
m\geq m_{0}(n,d)
and a transition interval

U_{n,m}=(\alpha_{n,m},\beta_{n,m})\in\mathcal{U}_{m}

with
U_{n,m}\subset I(t_{n},d)
and whose endpoints lie in blocks
J_{\sigma},J_{\tau}
with
\sigma_{m}\neq\tau_{m}
.
Then, by (
2
),

O_{I(t_{n},d)}(\varphi_{m})\ \geq\ O_{U_{n,m}}(\varphi_{m})\ =\ |\sigma_{m}-\tau_{m}|\ \geq 1.

Since
\|\varphi_{m}\|_{\infty}=1
, it follows that

\sup\{\,O_{I(t_{n},d)}(f):f\in X,\ \|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\,\}\ \geq 1.

Letting
d\downarrow 0
we obtain
\Gamma_{X}(t_{n})\geq 1>0
, i.e.,
t_{n}\in\Omega(X)
.
As
n
was arbitrary, we conclude
M\subset\Omega(X)
.

Now let
t\notin M
. By
section 6
, there exists
d_{*}(t)>0
so that
I(t,d_{*})\cap\mathcal{U}_{m}=\varnothing
for all
m
.
Hence, for each
m
,
\varphi_{m}
is constant on
I(t,d_{*})
.
Let
f\in X
with
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. By the definition of
X
, there are finite combinations

f_{N}=\sum_{m=1}^{N}a_{m}\varphi_{m}

with
\|f-f_{N}\|_{\infty}\to 0
. Each
f_{N}
is constant on
I(t,d_{*})
, and thus, for any
x,y\in I(t,d_{*})
,

|f(x)-f(y)|\ \leq\ |f(x)-f_{N}(x)|+|f(y)-f_{N}(y)|\ \leq\ 2\|f-f_{N}\|_{\infty}.

Sending
N\to\infty
gives
O_{I(t,d_{*})}(f)=0
. Since
f
and
d_{*}
were arbitrary, we get
\Gamma_{X}(t)=0
, i.e.,
t\notin\Omega(X)
.
Hence
\Omega(X)\subset M
.

Combining both inclusions yields
\Omega(X)=M
.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the final characterization of
X
, combining the isometry
with
\ell^{1}
(already established) and the description of the oscillation spectrum.

Corollary 6.7
.

The space
X=T(\ell^{1})\subset C[0,1]
satisfies
\Omega(X)=M
(a countable set) and is isometrically isomorphic to
\ell^{1}
.
In particular,
X
is not isomorphic to a subspace of
c_{0}
.

Proof.

The isometric identification
X\simeq\ell^{1}
follows directly from
Theorem 6.3
, which shows

\Big\|\sum_{m\geq 1}a_{m}\varphi_{m}\Big\|_{\infty}=\sum_{m\geq 1}|a_{m}|\qquad\text{for every }a=(a_{m})\in\ell^{1}.

Hence the map
T:\ell^{1}\to X
,
T(a)=\sum_{m}a_{m}\varphi_{m}
, is an isometric isomorphism.
The equality
\Omega(X)=M
is the content of
Theorem 6.6
.

Finally, since
\ell^{1}
is not isomorphic to any subspace of
c_{0}
(see
section 4
),
any space isomorphic to
\ell^{1}
, in particular
X
, cannot be isomorphic to a subspace of
c_{0}
.
∎

7.
Final remarks and open questions

Enflo–Gurariy–Seoane
[
5
]
showed that a finite oscillation spectrum imposes strong
c_{0}
-type geometry (every such subspace is isomorphic to a subspace of
c_{0}
).
Our construction shows that this phenomenon does
not
extend to countable spectra: there are subspaces with countable
\Omega(X)
that are isometric to
\ell^{1}
.
Independently, Xie
[
9
]
provided a complete description of possible spectra: a set
M\subset[0,1]
occurs as
\Omega(X)
for some
X\subset C[0,1]
if and only if
M
is
F_{\sigma}
, and moreover one can choose
X
to be
complemented
in
C[0,1]
.

The questions below remain open in
[
2
]
and, to the best of our knowledge, are not resolved by the existing results.

Question 7.1.

Which properties of
\Omega(X)
ensure that
X
is complemented in
C[0,1]
?

Question 7.2.

Xie
[
9
]
shows that for every nonempty
F_{\sigma}
set
M
there exists a
complemented
subspace
X\subset C[0,1]
with
\Omega(X)=M
.
Does there also exist, for every nonempty
F_{\sigma}
set
M
, a
non-complemented
subspace
X\subset C[0,1]
with
\Omega(X)=M
?

Question 7.5.

How rigid is the functional
\Omega(\cdot)
under Banach-space isomorphisms?
If
X
and
Y
are isomorphic subspaces of
C[0,1]
, must
\Omega(X)
and
\Omega(Y)
be related in a controlled way?

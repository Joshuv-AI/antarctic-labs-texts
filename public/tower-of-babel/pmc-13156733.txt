Concomitant Bilateral Kienböck and Legg–Calvé–Perthes Diseases
Authors: Ahmadreza Afshar, Ali Tabrizi, Hasan Taleb, Mohammad Javad Shariyate
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAvascular necrosis involving more than one bone either simultaneously or sequentially is uncommon. We report a rare case of concomitant bilateral Kienböck disease and Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease. No definitive predisposing or precipitating factor could be identified. Because of persistent wrist pain, radial shortening osteotomy was performed sequentially on both wrists. At the latest follow-up, the patient’s wrist pain had resolved, and he was satisfied with the clinical outcomes.Key words: Atraumatic osteonecrosis, Avascular necrosis, Kienböck disease, Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease, Multifocal osteonecrosis

Avascular necrosis (AVN) affecting more than one bone either simultaneously or sequentially is a rare occurrence. Multifocal osteonecrosis is defined as osteonecrosis involving three or more distinct anatomical regions, either concomitantly or consecutively. Various combinations of skeletal involvement, including the femoral head, knee, shoulder, wrist, and ankle, have been reported in the literature.1, 2, 3

The concomitant occurrence of bilateral Kienböck disease and Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease represents an exceptionally rare combination. To the best of our knowledge, only three case reports have described this association.4, 5, 6 The present report adds another case of concomitant bilateral Kienböck and Legg–Calvé–Perthes diseases to the existing literature.

Case Report

A 25-year-old man presented with a 1-year history of pain and limited range of motion in his left wrist. Plain radiographs demonstrated Lichtman stage II Kienböck disease with negative ulnar variance. Because of persistent pain, radial shortening osteotomy was offered and performed.

At the 1-year follow-up, the patient developed pain in the right wrist. Radiographs revealed Lichtman stage II Kienböck disease with negative ulnar variance on the right side as well (Fig. 1). Radial shortening osteotomy was subsequently performed on the right wrist (Fig. 2). At the latest follow-up, pain in both wrists had resolved, and the patient reported satisfaction with the clinical outcomes.

Anteroposterior plain radiograph of the right and left wrists demonstrates bilateral Kienböck disease. The left side was treated with radial shortening one year before.

Anteroposterior plain radiograph of the right and left wrists demonstrates bilateral Kienböck disease treated with radial shortening osteotomy.

The patient’s past medical history revealed bilateral limping since childhood. According to family members, hip pain and limping began at approximately 8 years of age on the left side and subsequently at around 10 years of age on the right side. The left lower limb was approximately 3 cm shorter than the right. Pelvic radiographs demonstrated sequelae of bilateral Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease (Fig. 3). The patient denied any history of trauma to his limbs.

An anteroposterior plain radiograph of the pelvis demonstrates sequel of bilateral Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease.

Although involvement of both wrists and hips was bilateral, the disease processes were not at the same stage. Given the multifocal nature of AVN, we investigated potential systemic predisposing factors. Laboratory evaluations, including coagulation profiles, hypercoagulability work-up, rheumatologic studies, and the three key tests for antiphospholipid syndrome, were all within normal limits. The patient continues to be followed to identify any potential underlying etiology and to address future orthopedic needs.

Written informed consent was obtained from the patients for patients’ anonymized information to be published in this article.

Discussion

The pathogenesis of avascular necrosis remains incompletely understood. Numerous predisposing factors have been implicated in the development of atraumatic multifocal AVN, including corticosteroid use, smoking, gout, alcoholism, infection, storage disorders, marrow-infiltrating diseases, malignancy, chemotherapy, coagulation disorders, hemoglobinopathies, fat embolism, liver disease, hyperlipidemia (types II and III), hemophilia, inflammatory arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and pancreatitis.7, 8, 9, 10, 11

Only three prior case reports have described the rare coexistence of bilateral AVN of the lunate and proximal femur. Rostom et al5 reported a patient with antiphospholipid syndrome who developed multifocal AVN involving both lunates and proximal femora, suggesting a possible association between antiphospholipid syndrome and multifocal AVN. Baltzer et al4 described a child with factor V Leiden thrombophilia complicated by bilateral Legg–Calvé–Perthes disease and Kienböck disease, proposing thrombophilia as a contributing factor. Wollstein et al6 reported a similar case of concomitant bilateral Kienböck and Legg–Calvé–Perthes diseases without identifying a predisposing factor.

In the present case, no precipitating or predisposing mechanism could be identified. Given the current understanding of AVN pathophysiology, a substantial proportion of cases without identifiable causes are classified as idiopathic.9,10 Accumulation of additional case reports may improve understanding of the etiology, diagnosis, and management of multifocal AVN.

Contact process for the spread of knowledge
Nicolas Lanchier, Max Mercer, Hyunsik Yun

Abstract
This paper is concerned with a natural variant of the contact process modeling the spread of knowledge on the integer lattice. Each site is characterized by its knowledge, measured by a real number ranging from 0 = ignorant to 1 = omniscient. Neighbors interact at rate λ \lambda , which results in both neighbors attempting to teach each other a fraction μ \mu of their knowledge, and individuals die at rate one, which results in a new individual with no knowledge. Starting with a single omniscient site, our objective is to study whether the total amount of knowledge on the lattice converges to zero (extinction) or remains bounded away from zero (survival). The process dies out when λ ≤ λ c \lambda\leq\lambda_{c} and/or μ = 0 \mu=0 , where λ c \lambda_{c} denotes the critical value of the contact process. In contrast, we prove that, for all λ > λ c \lambda>\lambda_{c} , there is a unique phase transition in the direction of μ \mu , and for all μ > 0 \mu>0 , there is a unique phase transition in the direction of λ \lambda . Our proof of survival relies on block constructions showing more generally convergence of the knowledge to infinity, while our proof of extinction relies on martingale techniques showing more generally an exponential decay of the knowledge.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with a natural variant of Harris’ contact process
[
5
]
modeling the spread of knowledge on the integer lattice.
The contact process is the simplest invasion model based on the framework of interacting particle systems.
The state at time
t
is a configuration

\eta_{t}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}\quad\hbox{where}\quad\eta_{t}(x)=\hbox{state of site\penalty\ $x$ at time\penalty\ $t$}.

The process can be interpreted as an epidemic model, in which case the possible states at each lattice site are 0 = healthy and 1 = infected, or as a population model, in which case 0 = vacant and 1 = occupied.
In the context of epidemiology, infected individuals infect each of their healthy neighbors at rate
\lambda
, and recover at rate one.
In particular, writing
x\sim y
to indicate that
x
and
y
are nearest neighbors (distance one apart), the state at site
x
transitions

\begin{array}[]{c}0\to 1\ \ \hbox{at rate}\ \ \lambda\,\sum_{y\sim x}\eta_{t}(y)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad 1\to 0\ \ \hbox{at rate}\ \ 1.\end{array}

Note that the spread of the disease (the transition
0\to 1
) can also be described assuming that any two neighbors
x
and
y
interact at rate
\lambda
and that, if one neighbor is healthy while the other neighbor is infected, the healthy neighbor becomes infected.
Identifying the process with the set of infected sites, and starting from the configuration
\eta_{0}=\{0\}
that has an infected individual at the origin in an otherwise healthy population, we say that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\hbox{the process\penalty\ $\eta_{t}$ survives}&\hbox{when}&P(\eta_{t}\neq\varnothing\ \ \forall t)>0,\\
\hbox{the process\penalty\ $\eta_{t}$ dies out}&\hbox{when}&P(\eta_{t}\neq\varnothing\ \ \forall t)=0.\end{array}

The main result about the contact process is the existence of a phase transition from extinction to survival.
More precisely, there is a nondegenerate critical value

\lambda_{c}=\lambda_{c}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\inf\{\lambda:P(\eta_{t}\neq\varnothing\ \ \forall t)>0\}

that depends on the spatial dimension
d
such that the process dies out for all
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
but survives for all
\lambda>\lambda_{c}
.
Showing that the process properly rescaled in space and time dominates oriented site percolation and using a continuity argument, Bezuidenhout and Grimmett
[
1

also proved extinction at the critical value.

Model description
.
The objective of this paper is to study a natural variant

Each lattice site is occupied by an individual who is now characterized by its knowledge, a real number ranging from 0 = ignorant to 1 = omniscient, so the state at time

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\hbox{the process\penalty\ $\xi_{t}$ survives}&\hbox{when}&P(\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,\Xi_{t}>0)>0,\\
\hbox{the process\penalty\ $\xi_{t}$ dies out}&\hbox{when}&P(\lim_{t\to\infty}\,\Xi_{t}=0)=1.\end{array}

(2)

One of the two parameters being fixed, our objective is to study the existence/uniqueness of a phase transition from extinction to survival in the direction of the other parameter.

Main results
.
Important properties of the process
\xi_{t}
are monotonicity and attractiveness.
More precisely, letting
\xi_{t}^{i}
for
i=1,2
, be the processes with parameter pair
(\lambda_{i},\mu_{i})
and initial configuration

\begin{array}[]{rrl}\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2},&\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2},&\xi_{0}^{1}(z)\leq\xi_{0}^{2}(z)\ \ \forall\,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
&\Longrightarrow&\xi_{t}^{1}(z)\leq\xi_{t}^{2}(z)\ \ \forall\,(z,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{array}

(3)

In the particular case where the initial configurations are equal, the previous implication means that the process is monotone with respect to
\lambda

\mu

It follows from (

\begin{array}[]{rrrcl}\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2},&\mu_{1}=\mu_{2},&\xi_{0}^{1}=\xi_{0}^{2}=\mathbf{1}_{0}&\Longrightarrow&(\xi_{t}^{1}\ \hbox{survives}\ \Longrightarrow\ \xi_{t}^{2}\ \hbox{survives}).\end{array}

In particular, the parameter
\mu
being fixed, there is at most one phase transition from extinction to survival in the direction of the parameter
\lambda
.
More precisely, letting

\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda_{c}(\mu)=\inf\{\lambda:P(\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,\Xi_{t}>0\,|\,\xi_{0}=\mathbf{1}_{0})>0\}\in[0,\infty],\end{array}

.

\begin{array}[]{c}\mu_{c}(\lambda)=\inf\{\mu:P(\limsup_{t\to\infty}\,\Xi_{t}>0\,|\,\xi_{0}=\mathbf{1}_{0})>0\}\in[0,1],\end{array}

(5)

there is extinction for all
\mu<\mu_{c}(\lambda)

\mu>\mu_{c}(\lambda)

\lambda_{c}(\mu)\geq\lambda_{c}>0\quad\hbox{for all}\quad\mu\in[0,1].

(6)

Similarly, when

Theorem 1
.

For all
\mu>0

\lambda_{c}(\mu)<\infty
.

In fact, the block construction used in the proof shows that, with positive probability when starting with a single omniscient site, the process converges to an invariant measure that has a positive density of sites with knowledge exceeding one-half, which leads to a somewhat stronger conclusion than survival as defined in (
2

We now look at the effects of the parameter

Because sites interact on average

The first step of our analysis is to use an idea of Harris

Proof of Theorem
1

In this section, we prove that, even when the fraction
\mu>0
is small, the process survives provided the interaction rate
\lambda
is sufficiently large.
As previously mentioned, our proof implies in fact the following stronger result:
Starting with 0 omniscient, there is a positive probability that the process converges to an invariant measure with a positive density of sites with knowledge exceeding, say, one-half.
The proof relies on a block construction, a technique that first appeared in
[
2
]
and is explained in detail in
[
3
]
.
The general idea is to compare the process properly rescaled in space and time with oriented site percolation.
More precisely, let

\mathscr{L}=\{(z,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}:z_{1}+\cdots+z_{d}+n\ \hbox{is even}\},

(8)

that we turn into a directed graph
\vec{\mathscr{L}}
by placing an edge

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(z,n)\to(z^{\prime},n^{\prime})&\Longleftrightarrow&|z_{1}-z_{1}^{\prime}|+\cdots+|z_{d}-z_{d}^{\prime}|=1\ \hbox{and}\ n^{\prime}=n+1\\
&\Longleftrightarrow&z^{\prime}\sim z\ \hbox{and}\ n^{\prime}=n+1.\end{array}

(9)

Letting
T>0
to be fixed later, and calling

a good site if

E_{z,n}=\{\xi_{nT}(z)>1/2\}=\{\hbox{the knowledge at\penalty\ $z$ at time\penalty\ $nT$ exceeds 1/2}\}

occurs, the objective is to prove that, for all
\epsilon>0
, the time scale

can be chosen in such a way that the set of good sites dominates the set of wet sites in a percolation process on
\vec{\mathscr{L}}
in which sites are open with probability
1-\epsilon
.
To begin with, we look at the process
\xi_{t}
restricted to a single edge
\{x,y\}
in the absence of deaths, and prove that, if the knowledge at
x
is at least
1/2
, then the knowledge at both sites will be at least
1/2
after a finite number of interactions.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mu>0
, and assume that
\xi_{0}(x)=1/2
and
\xi_{0}(y)=0
.
Then, there exists
n=n(\mu)<\infty
such that, after the two sites interact at least
n
times with no deaths,

\xi_{t}(x)\geq 1/2\quad\hbox{and}\quad\xi_{t}(y)\geq 1/2.

Proof.

Let
x_{k}
and
y_{k}
be respectively the knowledge at
x
and at
y
after
k
interactions, and in the absence of deaths.
It follows from the transitions (
1
) that

x_{k}=x_{k-1}+\mu y_{k-1}(1-x_{k-1})\quad\hbox{and}\quad y_{k}=y_{k-1}+\mu x_{k-1}(1-y_{k-1}),

with
x_{0}=1/2
and
y_{0}=0
.
In particular, we have
x_{k}\geq x_{0}=1/2
, therefore

y_{k}\geq y_{k-1}+\frac{\mu(1-y_{k-1})}{2}=\frac{\mu}{2}+\bigg(1-\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg)y_{k-1}.

Finally, using a simple induction, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle y_{n}&\hskip-5.0pt\geq&\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2}+\bigg(1-\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg)y_{n-1}\geq\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigg(\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg)^{k}+\bigg(1-\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg)^{n}y_{0}\\
&\hskip-5.0pt=&\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigg(\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg)^{k}=\displaystyle 1-\bigg(1-\frac{\mu}{2}\bigg)^{n}.\end{array}

Because the right-hand side goes to one as

(note more precisely that it is larger than one-half whenever

), the lemma follows.

\square

In this section, we prove that, for all

\mu<1/(2d\lambda)

The proof relies on martingale techniques showing also that the expected amount of knowledge on the lattice decays exponentially fast.
Recall that extinction is obvious when
\leq\lambda_{c}
since in this case the contact process that keeps track of the sites with a positive knowledge is subcritical.
To deal with the supercritical case, we consider the process
\xi_{t}^{+}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
that again has interactions at rate
\lambda
and deaths at rate one, but in which an interaction between
x
and
y
results in the transitions

\xi_{t}(x)=\xi_{t-}(x)+\mu\,\xi_{t-}(y)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\xi_{t}(y)=\xi_{t-}(y)+\mu\,\xi_{t-}(x).

The processes
\xi_{t}
and
\xi_{t}^{+}
can be coupled in such a way that
\xi_{t}(z)\leq\xi_{t}^{+}(z)\ \forall\,z
at all times provided the inequalities hold at time 0, therefore it suffices to prove extinction of the process
\xi_{t}^{+}
under the assumptions of the theorem.
The key is to control the drift of the total amount of knowledge on the lattice, which is done in the next lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Xi_{t}^{+}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\xi_{t}^{+}(x)
. Then,

\begin{array}[]{c}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\,\epsilon^{-1}\,E(\Xi_{t+\epsilon}^{+}-\Xi_{t}^{+}\,|\,\xi_{t}^{+})=(2d\lambda\mu-1)\,\Xi_{t}^{+}.\end{array}

(13)

Proof.

To simplify the notation, for all
c\neq 0
, we let

\begin{array}[]{c}r(c)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\,\epsilon^{-1}\,P(\Xi_{t+\epsilon}^{+}-\Xi_{t}^{+}=c\,|\,\xi_{t}^{+})\end{array}

be the rate at which
\Xi_{t}^{+}
increases by
c
or decreases by
-c
, and

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Lambda_{1}&\hskip-5.0pt=&\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\xi_{t}^{+}(x)>0\},\\
\Lambda_{2}&\hskip-5.0pt=&\{\{x,y\}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}:x\sim y\ \hbox{and}\ \xi_{t}^{+}(x)+\xi_{t}^{+}(y)>0\}.\end{array}

Because individuals die independently at rate one, which results in a loss of their knowledge, while pairs of nearest neighbors interact independently at rate
\lambda
, which results in the transfer of a fraction
\mu
of their knowledge to the other neighbor, we have

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}r(-\xi_{t}^{+}(x))&\hskip-5.0pt=&1&\hbox{for all}&x\in\Lambda_{1},\\
r(\mu(\xi_{t}^{+}(x)+\xi_{t}^{+}(y)))&\hskip-5.0pt=&\lambda&\hbox{for all}&\{x,y\}\in\Lambda_{2}.\end{array}

Summing over all
x\in\Lambda_{1}
and all
\{x,y\}\in\Lambda_{2}
, we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\displaystyle E\bigg(\frac{\Xi_{t+\epsilon}^{+}-\Xi_{t}^{+}}{\epsilon}\ \Big|\ \xi_{t}^{+}\bigg)&\hskip-5.0pt=&\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{1}}(-\xi_{t}^{+}(x))+\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\Lambda_{2}}\lambda\mu(\xi_{t}^{+}(x)+\xi_{t}^{+}(y))\\
&\hskip-5.0pt=&\displaystyle-\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{1}}\,\xi_{t}^{+}(x)+2d\lambda\mu\,\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{1}}\,\xi_{t}^{+}(x)=(2d\lambda\mu-1)\,\Xi_{t}^{+}.\end{array}

This completes the proof.

\square

follows from the previous lemma.

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
\mu<1/(2d\lambda)
.
Then, by Lemma

, the process
\Xi_{t}^{+}
is a supermartingale with respect to the natural filtration of the process

\begin{array}[]{c}\sup_{t\geq 0}|\!|\Xi_{t}^{+}|\!|_{1}=\sup_{t\geq 0}E(\Xi_{t}^{+})\leq E(\Xi_{0}^{+})=1\quad\hbox{when}\quad\xi_{0}^{+}=\mathbf{1}_{0}.\end{array}

converges almost surely to a random variable

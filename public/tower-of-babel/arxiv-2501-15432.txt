Left-symmetric superalgebras and Lagrangian extensions of Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 Thanks: S.Bo. and Q.E. were supported by the grant NYUAD-065.
Saïd Benayadi, Sofiane Bouarroudj, Quentin Ehret

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is twofold. First, we introduce the notions of left-symmetric and left alternative structures on superspaces in characteristic 2. We describe their main properties and classify them in dimension 2. We show that left-symmetric structures can be queerified if and only if they are left-alternative. Secondly, we present a method of Lagrangian extension of Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 with a flat torsion-free connection. We show that any strongly polarized quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra can be obtained as a Lagrangian extension. Further, we demonstrate that Lagrangian extensions are classified by a certain cohomology space that we introduce. To illustrate our constructions, all Lagrangian extensions in dimension 4 have been described.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2

Over a field of characteristic 2, a Lie superalgebra is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector superspace
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
such that the even part
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is a Lie algebra, the odd part is a two-sided
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
-module due to symmetry, a map
s:{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
, known as
squaring
that satisfies some compatibility conditions, including a modified Jacobi identity; see Definition
2.1
. In our knowledge, Lebedev (
[
LeD
]
) gave the definition, though earlier versions can be found in
[
BMY
]
, and also in
[
NR
]
under the name of “strongly anticommutative graded algebras”. The presence of the squaring makes it necessary to develop new methods for studying these Lie superalgebras. For example, the concepts of double extensions and Manin triples are developed in
[
BB1
,
BB2
]
, taking into account the squaring. Furthermore, there is a large number of Lie superalgebras that only exist in characteristic 2, with no analogs in other characteristics; see
[
BGL1
,
BGLLS
,
BLLSq
,
LeD
]
. As an example, there are four versions of the Hamiltonian Lie superalgebra, which are mutually non-isomorphic.

Although the classification of simple Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 remains open, those that admit a Cartan matrix have been classified in
[
BGL1
]
. Moreover, it was shown in
[
BLLSq
]
that every simple Lie superalgebra can be obtained from a simple Lie algebra using either queerification or “method 2”. The classification problem of simple Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 is thus reduced to the classification problem of Lie algebras, which is also a challenging one, see
[
S1
,
S2
]
.

The first instances of a cohomology theory for Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 can be found in
[
BGLL
]
, while its full description can be found in
[
BM23
]
. There is only a need for 1- and 2-cocycles in the present work, and their construction is discussed in Section
2.2
.

1.2.
Lagrangian extensions of Lie (super)algebras

Bordemann introduced the concept of
T^{*}
-extension over a field of characteristic 0 (see
[
Bo
]
). Basically, starting from a non-associative algebra
A
, he defined an algebra structure on
A\oplus A^{*}
by means of an
A^{*}
-valued bilinear map satisfying some cohomological conditions, and deduced a non-degenerate invariant
symmetric
bilinear form on
A\oplus A^{*}
. Most notably, he demonstrated that every finite-dimensional nilpotent algebra over complex numbers equipped with an invariant symmetric bilinear form can be obtained as a
T^{*}
-extension of a smaller algebra. The term stems from the fact that the Lie algebra of the cotangent bundle of a Lie group is a
T^{*}
-extension of its Lie algebra. Later, a superization of Boredmann’s approach was given in
[
BBB
]
.

Baues and Cortés examined this concept later (see
[
BC
]
), naming it Lagrangian extension. In contrast to Bordemann’s approach, it was applied to Lie algebras with flat and torsion-free connections, resulting in Lie algebras admitting a non-degenerate
antisymmetric
closed form. In addition, they introduced Lagrangian cohomology and showed that it captures equivalence classes of Lagrangian extensions. Furthermore, they showed that every quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra admitting a Lagrangian ideal can be obtained through this process.

Baues and Cortés studied in depth the structure theory of symplectic Lie groups. Recall that a Lie group
G
is called symplectic if it is equipped with a left-invariant closed 2-form whose rank is equal to
\dim(G)
. In this case, its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
would be a quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra, that is, there exists a closed non-degenerate 2-form
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Another contribution of
[
BC
]
is showing that all symplectic Lie groups of dimension less than or equal to 6 admit a Lagrangian subgroup and that there exists an eight-dimensional symplectic Lie group without a Lagrangian subgroup.

The concept of Lagrangian extension was later superized in
[
BM
]
provided that the field is of characteristic not 2. Lagrangian extensions can be performed in two ways on a flat Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
. One is to consider either
{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
or
{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
), where
\Pi
is the change in parity functor. They were referred to in
[
BM
]
as
T^{*}
-extension and
\Pi T^{*}
-extension, respectively, and the anti-symmetric form they yielded are either even or odd. All Theorems stated in
[
BC
]
pertaining to Lagrangian extensions were superized in
[
BM
]
; for instance, it was shown that each strongly polarized Lie superalgebra can be obtained as a
T^{*}
-extension or a
\Pi T^{*}
-extension of a smaller superalgebra.

The study of four-dimensional real Lie superalgebras, which are Lagrangian extensions of smaller superalgebras, is presented in
[
BR
]
, utilizing Backhouse’s classification of real Lie superalgebras (see
[
Ba
]
).

All of the constructions in
[
BM
]
are valid over any field of characteristic that is not
2
. The purpose of this paper is to explore Lagrangian extensions when the base field has characteristic 2, and to adapt the results of
[
BM
]
to that context while taking into account the squaring.

1.3.
Left-symmetric (super)algebras

In order to perform Lagrangian extensions in the vein of
[
BC
,
BM
]
, a Lie (super)algebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
must be equipped with a flat torsion-free connection. Having a flat torsion-free connection on
{\mathfrak{h}}
is equivalent to endowing
{\mathfrak{h}}
with a left-symmetric structure, see, e.g.,
[
Bu4
]
. Recall that a superalgebra
(A,\triangleright)
is called left-symmetric if the associator

(1)

\text{as}_{\triangleright}:~(a,b,c)\mapsto a\triangleright(b\triangleright c)-(a\triangleright b)\triangleright c

is left-symmetric “in
a
and
b
”. Namely,
\text{as}_{\triangleright}(a,b,c)=(-1)^{|a||b|}\text{as}_{\triangleright}(b,a,c)
for all homogeneous
a,b,c\in A
. The first appearance of these structures dates back to 1857, when Cayley dealt with rooted trees algebras. Vinberg and Koszul reintroduced them in the context of homogeneous cones (
[
Vi
]
) and affine flat manifolds (
[
Ko
]
). They also appeared under the name “pre-Lie algebras” in the work of Gerstenhaber on Hochschild cohomology and deformations of algebras. It is common nowadays to use the term “pre-Lie algebra” even though, in the definition of pre-Lie, the associator is
right
-symmetric. In order to avoid confusion, we use the term “left-symmetric”. For complementary survey papers on left-symmetric algebras, see
[
Bu4
,
M
]
.

Over the real numbers, the problem of determining whether a simply connected solvable Lie group admits a free transitive action on an affine space is equivalent to the algebraic problem of finding a left-symmetric structure on the corresponding solvable Lie algebra, see
[
Bu2
,
Me
,
S
]
. Simple Lie algebras do not admit left-symmetric structures over the field of complex numbers, see
[
H
]
. As it turns out, this is not the case in the super setting. In
[
DZ
]
, it was shown that the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|m)
admits left-symmetric structures. This paper provides another example involving the Hamiltonian Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}_{\Pi}^{(1)}(0|4)
in characteristic 2, see Example
2.4.5.  Example
.

The classification of left-symmetric algebras was initiated by Segal in
[
S
]
and turns out to be a difficult problem. Burde studied simple modular algebras (
[
Bu1
]
), Baues studied
{\mathfrak{gl}}(n)
(
[
B
]
), and Bai classified every 3-dimensional left-symmetric algebra over complex numbers (
[
Bai
]
). Dimitrov and Zhang provided a full classification of
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
, and proved that
\mathfrak{sl}(m|1),
for
m\geq 3
, does not admit any left-symmetric structure. In addition, see
[
KB
]
, which explored left-symmetric structures on super Virasoro algebras, and
[
CH
]
, which dealt with infinite-dimensional superalgebras.

1.4.
Outline of the paper and main results

In Sections
2.1
,
2.2
, and
2.3
, we review basic constructions related to Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 and their cohomology. In Section
2.4
, we introduce left-symmetric superalgebras in characteristic 2 and give some examples of such structures. Here, the main novelty is the presence of a squaring of the associated Lie superalgebra, as shown in Proposition
2.4.1.  Proposition
. In particular, we provide another example of a simple Lie superalgebra admitting a left-symmetric structure, see Example
2.4.5.  Example
. In characteristic 2, the queerification method allows to construct Lie superalgebras from Lie algebras and can be applied to any
restricted
Lie algebra, see
[
BLLSq
]
. We demonstrate in Section
2.5
that a left-symmetric superalgebra in characteristic 2 can be queerified once it is left-alternative, see Lemma
2.5.4.  Lemma
. Moreover, the Lie superalgebra of a queerified left-alternative algebra is equal to the queerification of the Lie algebra associated with it, see Proposition
2.5.5.  Proposition
. Section
2.6
is devoted to the classification of left-symmetric superalgebras in dimension 2. We classify all left-symmetric structures compatible with each 2-dimensional Lie superalgebra.

Section
3
develops the theory of Lagrangian extensions of Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2. First we define connections as non-associative products on a superalgebra with purely algebraic definitions, as well as analogs of torsion and curvature (see Definition
3.1.1
), and their properties. The explicit construction of a Lagrangian extension is given in Theorem
3.2.4.  Theorem
, and its converse is Theorem
3.2.7.  Theorem
. Namely, we show that every strongly polarized Lie superalgebra is isomorphic to a
T^{*}
- or
\Pi T^{*}
-extension of a suitable smaller superalgebra, that we construct explicitly. Furthermore, we study the functorial properties of our constructions in Section
3.3
and demonstrate that the Lagrangian extensions of a given superalgebra are determined by its Lagrangian cohomology, see Theorem
3.5.3.  Theorem
. Several examples are given in Section
3.6
. To conclude, we compute all 4-dimensional Lie superalgebras which are obtained as
T^{*}
- or
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions in Section
4
.

The appendix
6
shows the Lagrangian cohomology groups for each left-symmetric 2-dimensional superalgebra, while the appendix
7
shows some invariants for the 4-dimensional Lie superalgebras. In Appendix
5
, we initiate the study of post-Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2 and link them with flat parallel connections.

\Pi(V)

V

\Pi(V)_{\bar{0}}:=V_{\bar{1}};~~\Pi(V)_{\bar{1}}:=V_{\bar{0}}

2.
Left-symmetric superalgebras in characteristic
2

2.1.
Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2

Let
V
and
W
be two vector spaces over
\K
. A map
s:V\rightarrow W
is called a
squaring
if

s(\lambda x)=\lambda^{2}s(x),~\forall\lambda\in\K,~\forall x\in V.

We also suppose that the map

V\times V\rightarrow W\hskip 20.00003pt(x,y)\mapsto s(x+y)-s(x)-s(y)

is bilinear.

Following
[
BMY
,
LeD
]
, a
Lie superalgebra
over a field
\K
of characteristic
p=2
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}
such that the even part
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{0}}}
is a Lie algebra, the odd part
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{0}}}
-module made two-sided by symmetry (the bracket on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{0}}}
as well as the action of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{0}}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}
are given by the same symbol
[\cdot,\cdot]
), a squaring
s:{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{0}}}
, such that the bracket of two odd elements is given by:

(2)

[x,y]:=s(x+y)-s(x)-s(y),~~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}.

The Jacobi identity involving the squaring reads as follows:

(3)

[s(x),y]=[x,[x,y]],~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}},~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Such a Lie superalgebra will be denoted by
\left({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},s_{\mathfrak{g}}\right)
or by just
{\mathfrak{g}}
if no confusion is possible.

Any associative superalgebra
A=A_{{\bar{0}}}\oplus A_{{\bar{1}}}
in characteristic
2
leads to a Lie superalgebra. The bracket is the usual commutator and the squaring is defined by
s(x):=x\cdot x,~\forall x\in A_{\bar{1}}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie superalgebra and
{\mathfrak{h}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
be a homogeneous linear subspace. We say that

•

{\mathfrak{h}}
is a
Lie subalgebra
if it is closed under the bracket and under the squaring;

•

{\mathfrak{h}}
is an
ideal
if
[h,x]\in{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall h\in{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
as well as
s(h)\in{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall h\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\bar{1}}}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}
be two Lie superalgebras. An even linear map
\varphi:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
is called a
Lie superalgebras morphism
if

(4)

\varphi\left([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\right)=[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{0}}},~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{g}},\text{ and }\varphi\circ s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)=s_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\varphi(x),~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}.

A linear map
D:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a
derivation
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
if

(5)

D([x,y])=[D(x),y]+[x,D(y)],~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{0}}},~y\in{\mathfrak{g}},\text{ and }D(s(x))=[D(x),x],~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}.

The superspace of all derivations of a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is itself a Lie superalgebra and is denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{Der}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}})
.

A
representation
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
in a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
M
is an even map
\rho:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\text{End}(M)
satisfying

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho(x),\rho(y)]\text{ $\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}$; and}

\displaystyle\rho(s(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\rho(x))^{2}\text{ $\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.$}

Such a
M
is called a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
.

In
[
BGL1
]
, simple Lie superalgebras admitting a Cartan matrix have been classified. There are several Lie superalgebras in the list that are indigenous to the characteristic 2 and have no analogs in higher characteristic cases. For examples of vectorial Lie superalgebras, see
[
BLLSq
]
and references therein.

2.2.
Cohomology of Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2

In this section, we will review the cohomology theory for Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
. The theory’s full description can be found at
[
BM23
]
, whereas its first description can be found at
[
BGLL
]
. In fact, only 1-cocycles and 2-cocycles are needed. The description of cocycles of higher order can be found at
[
BM23
]
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie superalgebra in characteristic
2
and let
M
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module.

A 1-cocycle on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in
M
is a linear map
\varphi:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow M
such that

(8)

\begin{array}[]{llll}d_{{\text{CE}}}^{1}(\varphi)(x,z)&:=&x\cdot\varphi(z)+z\cdot\varphi(x)+\varphi([x,z])=0,&\forall x,z\in{\mathfrak{g}};\\[5.69054pt]
\delta^{1}(\varphi)(x)&:=&x\cdot\varphi(x)+\varphi(s(x))=0,&\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.\end{array}

The space of 1-cocycles on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in
M
is denoted by
XZ^{1}({\mathfrak{g}};M)
. We also use the notation
{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}(\varphi):=(d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}(\varphi),\delta^{1}(\varphi))
.

A 2-cocycle on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in
M
consists of a pair
(\alpha,\gamma)
such that:

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha:{\mathfrak{g}}\wedge{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow M\text{ is a bilinear map},\text{ and }\gamma:{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}\rightarrow M\text{ is a squaring}\end{array}

such that
\alpha(x,y)=\gamma(x+y)+\gamma(x)+\gamma(y)
for all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{{\bar{1}}}
. Moreover, we require that

(9)

\begin{array}[]{llll}d_{{\text{CE}}}^{2}(\alpha)(x,y,z)&:=&\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\bigl(x\cdot\alpha(y,z)+\alpha([x,y],z)\bigl)=0,&\forall x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}};\\[5.69054pt]
\delta^{2}(\alpha,\gamma)(x,z)&:=&x\cdot\alpha(x,z)+z\cdot\gamma(x)+\alpha(s(x),z)+\alpha([x,z],x)=0,&\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}},~\forall z\in{\mathfrak{g}},\end{array}

The space of 2-cocycles on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in
M
is denoted by
XZ^{2}({\mathfrak{g}};M)
. We also use the notation
{\mathfrak{d}}^{2}(\alpha,\gamma):=(d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}(\alpha),\delta^{2}(\alpha,\gamma))
.
A complete description of
n
-cocycles for
n\geq 3
can be found in
[
BM23
]
. If
\alpha
is an even map, then
\text{Im}(\gamma)\subset M_{\bar{0}}
whereas if
\alpha
is odd, then
\text{Im}(\gamma)\subset M_{\bar{1}}
. Therefore, we can define a graduation on the space
XZ^{2}({\mathfrak{g}};M)
by setting

|(\alpha,\gamma)|:=|\alpha|,~\forall(\alpha,\gamma)\in XZ^{2}({\mathfrak{g}};M).

2.3.
Bilinear forms on Lie superalgebras

We will briefly review some concepts related to bilinear forms over a superspace in characteristic 2. For more details, see
[
BB2
, Sec. 2.2 and 2.4]
. Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
be a Lie superalgebra in characteristic
2
. A bilinear form
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
with values in
\mathbb{K}
is called

(i)

non-degenerate
if
\ker(\omega):=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}},~\omega(x,y)=0~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}=\{0\}
;

(ii)

ortho-orthogonal
if
\omega
is even;

(iii)

periplectic
if
\omega
is odd;

(iv)

closed
if the following cocycle conditions are satisfied:

(10)

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\omega([x,y],z)

\displaystyle=0,~\forall x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}};

(11)

\displaystyle\omega(s(x),y)

\displaystyle=\omega(x,[x,y]),~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Following
[
BB2
]
, an
even
bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric
if

(12)

\omega(x,y)=\omega(y,x),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}\text{ such that }|x|=|y|;~\text{and }\omega(x,x)=0~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}.

An
odd
bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric
if

(13)

\omega(x,y)=\omega(y,x),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Note that in the case where the form
\omega
is odd, the condition
\omega(x,x)=0
always holds for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. For more details, see
[
BB2
, Section 2.2]
.

A Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called
quasi-Frobenius
if it is equipped with a
{\bar{1}}
-antisymmetric non-degenerate closed form
\omega
. We denote such an algebra by
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
. A quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is called
ortho-orthogonal quasi-Frobenius
(resp.
periplectic quasi-Frobenius
) if the form
\omega
is even (resp. odd) on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Suppose that the form
\omega
is homogeneous, non-degenerate and closed. Let
I\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
be a homogeneous ideal and let
I^{\perp}
be its orthogonal with respect to
\omega
, which is homogeneous as well. Moreover, suppose that
[I,I^{\perp}]=0
. Then the space
I^{\perp}
is an ideal. Let us just check that it is closed under squaring. Indeed, let
u\in I^{\perp}_{\bar{1}}
. Then, using (
11
), we have

\omega\bigl(s(u),v\bigl)=\omega\bigl(u,[u,v]\bigl)=0~~\forall v\in I.

Therefore, the quotient
{\mathfrak{g}}/I^{\perp}
is well defined.

An ideal
I\subset L
is called
Lagrangian
if
I=I^{\perp}
. A homogeneous Lagrangian ideal of a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra is always abelian.

2.4.
Left-symmetric superalgebras in characteristic 2

A left-symmetric superalgebra
(V,\triangleright)
in characteristic
p=2
is a vector superspace
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
endowed with a bilinear product
\triangleright:V\times V\rightarrow V
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{lrlll}(i)&x\triangleright(y\triangleright z)+(x\triangleright y)\triangleright z&=&y\triangleright(x\triangleright z)+(y\triangleright x)\triangleright z,&\forall x,y,z\in V;\\[5.69054pt]
(ii)&x\triangleright(x\triangleright y)&=&(x\triangleright x)\triangleright y,&\forall x\in V_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in V.\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

Any associative superalgebra is a left-symmetric superalgebra.

A linear map
\phi:(V,\triangleright)\rightarrow(W,\blacktriangleright)
is called
morphism of left-symmetric superalgebras
if

\phi(x\triangleright y)=\phi(x)\blacktriangleright\phi(x),~\forall x,y\in V.

3.
Lagrangian extensions for Lie superalgebras

3.1.
The language of connections

We will need the concept of a connection on a Lie (super)algebra in order to write down a Lagrangian extension. We define connections as non-associative products on a superalgebra with purely algebraic definitions. See
[
BC
,
BM
,
MKSV
]
for the non-super case.

3.1.1.
Connections, curvature and torsion

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra. A
connection
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is an
even
linear map

\nabla:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}}),\hskip 20.00003ptx\mapsto\nabla_{x}.

The
torsion of the connection

\nabla
is given by a pair of maps
(T,U)
, where

T:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}},\text{ and }U:{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}},\text{ are defined as follows:}

(26)

\displaystyle T(x,y):=\nabla_{x}(y)+\nabla_{y}(x)+[x,y],~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}};\;U(x):=\nabla_{x}(x)+s(x),~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.

The connection
\nabla
is called
torsion-free
if
(T,U)=(0,0)
.

The
curvature of the connection

\nabla
is given by a pair of maps
(R,S)
, where

R:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}}),\text{ and }S:{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}}),\text{ are defined as follows:}

(27)

\displaystyle R(x,y):=\nabla_{[x,y]}+[\nabla_{x},\nabla_{y}],~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}};\;S(x):=\nabla_{s(x)}+\nabla_{x}^{2},~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.

The connection
\nabla
is called
flat
if
(R,S)=(0,0)
. In that case, the map
x\mapsto\nabla_{x}
is a representation of the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
into
\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{g}})
.

It is worth mentioning that the data of a connection
\nabla
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is equivalent to the existence of a bilinear operation
{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
given by
x\ast y:=\nabla_{x}(y),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Moreover, the operation
*
is a left-symmetric product compatible with the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
and the squaring
s
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
(in the sense of Proposition
2.4.1.  Proposition
) if and only if the connection
\nabla
is flat and torsion-free.

3.1.2.
Covariant derivatives.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra and let
\nabla
be a connection on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For
z,w\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the
covariant derivation of the curvature
is defined by

(28)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{z}R)(x,y)w:=

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}\bigl(R(x,y)w\bigl)+R\bigl(\nabla_{z}(x),y\bigl)w

\displaystyle+R\bigl(x,\nabla_{z}(y)\bigl)w+R(x,y)(\nabla_{z}w),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

(29)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{z}S)(x)w:=

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}\bigl(S(x)w\bigl)+R\bigl(\nabla_{z}(x),x\bigl)w+S(x)(\nabla_{z}w),~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.

Similarly, the
covariant derivation of the torsion
is defined by

(30)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{z}T)(x,y):=

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}\bigl(T(x,y)\bigl)+T\bigl(\nabla_{z}(x),y\bigl)+T\bigl(x,\nabla_{z}(y)\bigl),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

(31)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{z}U)(x):=

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}\bigl(U(x)\bigl)+T\bigl(\nabla_{z}(x),x\bigl),~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.

A connection
\nabla
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is called
parallel
if
(\nabla_{z}T,\nabla_{z}U)=(0,0),~\forall z\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Remark
.

Over a field of characteristic zero, it is well known (see e.g.
[
MKL
]
)
that there is a one-to-one correspondence between Lie algebras equipped with a flat parallel connection and post-Lie algebras. This remains true for Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
, see Proposition
5.1.6.  Proposition
. We initiate the study of post-Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
in Appendix
5
.

3.1.4.
Dual connections

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra and let
\nabla
be a connection on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. The
dual connection
of
\nabla
is defined by

(32)

\onabla_{x}(y)=\nabla_{y}(x)+[x,y],~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Notice that we have
\overline{\onabla}=\nabla
. We denote the curvature and the torsion of
\onabla
by
(\overline{R},\overline{S})
and
(\overline{T},\overline{U})
, respectively.

Lemma
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra and let
\nabla=0
be the zero connection on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then,
(\overline{R},\overline{S})=(0,0)
and
\onabla
is the adjoint representation of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

\displaystyle\overline{R}(x,y)(z)

\displaystyle=\onabla_{[x,y]}(z)+\onabla_{x}\circ\onabla_{y}(z)+\onabla_{y}\circ\onabla_{x}(z)

\displaystyle=[[x,y],z]+\onabla_{x}([y,z])+\onabla_{y}([x,z])

\displaystyle=[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]

\displaystyle=0.

Let
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
and
z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then

\overline{S}(x)(z)=\onabla_{s(x)}(z)+\onabla_{x}^{2}(z)=[s(x),z]+[x,[x,z]]=0.

Therefore,
\onabla
is a representation.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra and let
\nabla
be a connection on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then

(33)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{z}T)(x,y)

\displaystyle=\overline{R}(x,y)(z)+R(y,z)(x)+R(z,x)(y),~\forall x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}};

(34)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{z}U)(x)

\displaystyle=\overline{S}(x)(z)+R(z,x)(x),~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}},~\forall z\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Proof.

We prove Identity (
34
). Let
z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
. We have

\displaystyle(\nabla_{z}U)(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{z}(U(x))+T(\nabla_{z}(x),x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{z}\bigl(\nabla_{x}(x)+s(x)\bigl)+\nabla_{\nabla_{z}(x)}(x)+\nabla_{x}\circ\nabla_{z}(x)+[\nabla_{z}(x),x]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{z}\circ\nabla_{x}(x)+\nabla_{z}\circ s(x)+\onabla_{x}\circ\nabla_{z}(x)+\nabla_{x}\circ\nabla_{z}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{z}\circ\nabla_{x}(x)+\nabla_{x}\circ\nabla_{z}(x)+\nabla_{z}\circ s(x)+\onabla_{x}\circ\bigl(\onabla_{x}(z)+[z,x]\bigl)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{z}\circ\nabla_{x}(x)+\nabla_{x}\circ\nabla_{z}(x)+\onabla_{s(x)}(z)+[z,s(x)]+\onabla_{x}^{2}(z)+\nabla_{[z,x]}(x)+[x,[z,x]]

\displaystyle=\onabla_{s(x)}(z)+\onabla_{x}^{2}(z)+\nabla_{[z,x]}(x)+[\nabla_{z},\nabla_{x}](x)

\displaystyle=\overline{S}(x)(z)+R(z,x)(x).

∎

Corollary
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra and let
\nabla
be a flat connection on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then

(35)

(\overline{R},\overline{S})=(0,0)\text{ if and only if }(\nabla_{z}T,\nabla_{z}U)=(0,0),\forall z\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra and let
\nabla
be a connection on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then

(36)

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}T(x,T(y,z))=

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}R(x,y)(z)+\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\overline{R}(x,y)(z),~\forall x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}};

(37)

\displaystyle T\bigl(x,T(x,y)\bigl)+T\bigl(U(x),y\bigl)=

\displaystyle~S(x)(y)+R(y,x)(x)

\displaystyle+\overline{S}(x)(y)+\overline{R}(y,x)(x),~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{g}},~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.

Proof.

Let
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.
We have

\displaystyle T\bigl(x,T(x,y)\bigl)=

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}^{2}(y)+\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}(x)+\nabla_{x}([x,y])

\displaystyle~+\nabla_{\nabla_{x}(y)}(x)+\nabla_{\nabla_{y}(x)}(x)+\nabla_{[x,y]}(x)

\displaystyle~+[x,\nabla_{x}(y)]+[x,\nabla_{y}(x)]+[x,[x,y]],

and

\displaystyle T\bigl(U(x),y\bigl)=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nabla_{x}(x)}(y)+\nabla_{s(x)}(y)+\nabla_{y}\circ\nabla_{x}(x)+\nabla_{y}(s(x))+[\nabla_{x}(x),y]+[s(x),y].

On the other hand,

\displaystyle S(x)y+R(y,x)x

\displaystyle=\nabla_{s(x)}(y)+\nabla^{2}_{x}(y)+\nabla_{[y,x]}(x)+\nabla_{y}\circ\nabla_{x}(x)+\nabla_{x}\circ\nabla_{y}(x),~\text{ and }

\displaystyle\overline{S}(x)y+\overline{R}(y,x)x

\displaystyle=\onabla_{s(x)}(y)+\onabla^{2}_{x}(y)+\onabla_{[y,x]}(x)+\onabla_{y}\circ\onabla_{x}(x)+\onabla_{x}\circ\onabla_{y}(x).

Using the identities

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nabla_{x}(x)}(y)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nabla_{y}(x)}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{[x,y]}(x)+\onabla_{x}\onabla_{x}(y)+[\onabla_{x}(y),x];

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nabla_{x}(y)}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{[x,y]}(x)+\onabla_{x}\onabla_{x}(y)+[\onabla_{x}(y),x],

as well as

\onabla_{s(x)}(y)=\nabla_{y}(s(x))+[s(x),y]~\text{ and }~\onabla_{[y,x]}(x)=\nabla_{x}([y,x])+[[y,x],x],

we obtain

\displaystyle T\bigl(x,T(x,y)\bigl)+T\bigl(U(x),y\bigl)=

\displaystyle S(x)(y)+R(y,x)(x)+\overline{S}(x)(y)+\overline{R}(y,x)(x)

(38)

\displaystyle+[\onabla_{y}(x),x]+[x,\onabla_{x}(y)]+[\onabla_{x}(y),x]

(39)

\displaystyle+[\nabla_{y}(x),x]+[y,\onabla_{x}(x)]+[\onabla_{x}(x),y].

We have
\eqref{pro1}+\eqref{pro2}=0,

3.2.
Lagrangian extensions

To our best knowledge, Lagrangian extensions were introduced by Bordemann (see
[
Bo
]
) under the name
T^{*}
-extensions, dealing with symmetric bilinear forms. The notion was then studied by Baues and Cortes (see
[
BC
]
), who name it Lagrangian extensions, in the context of Lie algebras admitting a flat connection and the bilinear form is antisymmetric. They also introduced the notion of Lagrangian cohomology and showed that equivalence classes of Lagrangian extensions are captured by this cohomology. The superization is due to Bouarroudj and Maeda (see
[
BM
]
), who used the terminology
T^{*}
-extensions (resp.
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions) in the case where the form is even (resp. odd). In this paper, we use the terminology “Lagrangian extension” to mention both
T^{*}
-extensions and
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra equipped with a non-degenerate closed ortho-orthogonal or periplectic bilinear form
\omega
and
I\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
be a homogeneous ideal such that
[I,I^{\perp}]=0.
Let
{\mathfrak{h}}:={\mathfrak{g}}/I^{\perp}
. There is a non-degenerate bilinear pairing between
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
I
by declaring

(47)

\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}(x,u):=\omega(\tilde{x},u),~~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall u\in I,

where
\tilde{x}
is a lift of
x
to
{\mathfrak{g}}
. This expression is well-defined, see
[
BM
]
. Notice that

(48)

\widetilde{[u,v]}-[\tilde{u},\tilde{v}]\in I^{\perp},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{s(x)}-s(\tilde{x})\in(I^{\perp})_{\bar{0}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}},~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}.

Moreover, it is always possible to find a lift that preserves the parity.

The following proposition has been proved in
[
BC
]
for Lie algebras, and in
[
BM
]
for Lie superalgebras but
p\not=2
. Here, we complete the picture for
p=2
. This will be useful in Section
3.2.5
.

Proposition
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot],s)
be a Lie superalgebra equipped with a non-degenerate closed ortho-orthogonal or periplectic bilinear form
\omega
and
I\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
be a homogeneous ideal such that
[I,I^{\perp}]=0.
Let
{\mathfrak{h}}:={\mathfrak{g}}/I^{\perp}
and let
\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}
be the pairing defined in Eq. (
47
). If
x\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, we will denote by
\tilde{x}
a lift of
x
to
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

(i)

The map
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{I}:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(I),\hskip 10.00002ptx\mapsto\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{x}}
is a representation, which induces representations of the spaces
I^{*}
and
\Pi(I^{*})
given by

(49)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}:{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(I^{*}),~\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ where }\hskip 10.00002pt\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{I}^{*}(x)(\xi):=\xi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{x}};

(50)

\displaystyle\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{*}_{I}:{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(\Pi(I^{*})),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ where }\hskip 10.00002pt\Pi\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{I}^{*}(x)(\Pi(\xi)):=\Pi\circ\xi\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{x}};

(ii)

In the case where
\omega
is ortho-orthogonal, there is an isomorphism

\varphi:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow I^{*},\hskip 10.00002ptx\mapsto\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}(x,\cdot),

and the map
\varphi
is a one-cocycle in

, where the module structure is given by (
49
);

(iii)

In the case where
\omega
is periplectic, there is an isomorphism

\psi:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\Pi(I^{*}),\hskip 10.00002ptx\mapsto\Pi\circ\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}(x,\cdot),

and the map
\psi
is a one-cocycle in
XZ^{1}_{\bar{0}}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi(I^{*}))
, where the module structure is given by (

(iv)

There is a torsion-free flat connection
\nabla
on
{\mathfrak{h}}
defined by

(51)

\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}\bigl(\nabla_{x}(y),u\bigl):=\omega\bigl(\tilde{y},[\tilde{x},u]\bigl),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall u\in I.

Proof.

We will only prove identities involving the squaring. For identities involving the brackets, see

(ii)

\tilde{x}\in I^{\perp}

is an isomorphism. Next, let

and

\displaystyle\bigl(x\cdot\varphi(x)\bigl)(v)+\varphi\bigl(s(x)\bigl)(v)

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)\circ\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\tilde{x}}(v)+\omega_{H}\bigl(s(x),v\bigl)

\displaystyle=\omega\bigl(\tilde{x},[\tilde{x},v]\bigl)+\omega\bigl(\widetilde{s(x)},v\bigl)

\displaystyle=\omega\bigl(\tilde{x},[\tilde{x},v]\bigl)+\omega\bigl(s(\tilde{x}),v\bigl)=0.

The 1-cocycle condition involving the bracket follows as in

(iii)

Similar to

(iv)

\displaystyle:=\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}\bigl(\nabla_{s(x)}(y)+\nabla^{2}_{x}(y),u\bigl)

\displaystyle=\omega\bigl(\tilde{y},[\widetilde{s(x)},u]\bigl)+\omega\bigl(\widetilde{\nabla_{x}(y)},[\tilde{x},u]\bigl)

3.2.2.
Strong polarization

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. A strong polarization of
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
is a decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{a}}\oplus N
as vector superspaces, where
{\mathfrak{a}}
is a homogeneous Lagrangian ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
N
is a Lagrangian subspace. Recall that a subspace
{\mathfrak{l}}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
is called Lagrangian if
{\mathfrak{l}}={\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}
, with
{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp}
the orthogonal with respect to
\omega
. A strongly polarized quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra will be denoted by
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)
.

A morphism of strongly polarized quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra is a Lie superalgebras morphism
\phi:({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime},N^{\prime})
such that
\omega=\phi^{*}\omega^{\prime}
,
\phi({\mathfrak{a}})={\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
and
\phi(N)=N^{\prime}
.

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},s_{\mathfrak{h}})
be a Lie superalgebra endowed with a torsion-free flat connection
\nabla:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}})
. Recall that since
\nabla
is flat, it is a representation of
{\mathfrak{h}}
. Following
[
BM
]
, we define the dual representations
\rho:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
and
\chi:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))
as follows:

(52)

\rho:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}),\hskip 10.00002ptx\mapsto\rho(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where }\rho(x)(\xi):=\xi\circ\nabla_{x},~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

(53)

\chi:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})),\hskip 10.00002ptx\mapsto\chi(x):=\Pi\circ\rho(x)\circ\Pi.

Lemma
.

The maps
\rho
and
\chi
are indeed representations.

Proof.

We will only prove identities involving the squaring. See
[
BM
, Lemma 6.1.1]
, for a proof of the other identities. Let
x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}}
and
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
.
Since the connection is flat, we have

\rho(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))(\xi)=\xi\circ\nabla_{s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)}=\xi\circ\nabla^{2}_{x}=\rho(x)^{2}(\xi).

Therefore,
\rho
is a representation. Furthermore,

\chi(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))=\Pi\circ\rho(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))\circ\Pi=\Pi\circ\rho(x)^{2}\circ\Pi=\Pi\circ\rho(x)\circ\Pi\circ\Pi\circ\rho(x)\circ\Pi=\chi(x)^{2},

so
\chi
is a representation as well.
∎

Let
(\alpha,\gamma)\in XZ^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})_{\bar{0}}
(resp.
(\beta,\theta)\in XZ^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))_{\bar{0}}
) be 2-cocycles. We will construct an ortho-orthogonal (resp. a periplectic) Lie superalgebra structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
(resp. on
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
). The spaces
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
are
{\mathfrak{h}}
-modules using the representations (
52
) and (
53
).

On the space
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
.
The brackets and squaring are defined as follows:

(54)

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle:=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\alpha(x,y),\hskip 10.00002pt[x,\xi]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=\rho(x)(\xi),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

(55)

\displaystyle s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x+\xi)

\displaystyle:=s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)+\gamma(x)+\rho(x)(\xi),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}},~\forall\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}_{\bar{1}}.

We define an ortho-orthogonal form as follows:

(56)

\omega(x+\xi,y+\zeta)=\xi(y)+\zeta(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x+\xi,y+\zeta\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

On the space
{\mathfrak{g}}:={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
.
The brackets and squaring are defined as follows:

(57)

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle:=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\beta(x,y),\hskip 10.00002pt[x,\Pi(\xi)]_{\mathfrak{g}}:=\chi(x)(\Pi(\xi)),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{h}},~\forall\Pi(\xi)\in\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}).

(58)

\displaystyle s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)

\displaystyle:=s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)+\theta(x)+\chi(x)(\Pi(\xi)),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}},~\forall\Pi(\xi)\in\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}_{\bar{1}}).

We define a periplectic form as follows:

(59)

\kappa(x+\Pi(\xi),y+\Pi(\zeta))=\xi(y)+\zeta(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x+\xi,y+\zeta\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Let us prove Jacobi Identities involving the squaring. Let
x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}},~y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and let
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
. Recall that the second part of the 2-cocycle condition (
9
) for the pair
(\alpha,\gamma)
reads

(60)

\alpha(x,y)\circ\nabla_{x}+\gamma(x)\circ\nabla_{y}=\alpha(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),y)+\alpha(x,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}).

We have

\displaystyle[x,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=[x,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\alpha(x,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}})+\alpha(x,y)\circ\nabla_{x}

\displaystyle=[s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\alpha(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),y)+\gamma(x)\circ\nabla_{y}\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{using\eqref{2cocyh}})

\displaystyle=[s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)+\gamma(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Furthermore, since
\nabla
is flat, we have
\nabla^{2}_{x}=\nabla_{s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)}~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}}
. Therefore,

[x,[x,\xi]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[x,\xi\circ\nabla_{x}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\xi\circ\nabla^{2}_{x}=\xi\circ\nabla_{s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)}=[s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),\xi]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)+\gamma(x),\xi]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\xi]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Theorem
.

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
be a Lie superalgebra equipped with a flat and torsion-free connection
\nabla
and let
(\alpha,\gamma)\in XZ^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})_{\bar{0}}
(resp.
(\beta,\theta)\in XZ^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))_{\bar{0}}
) be 2-cocycles.

(1)

The form
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
defined in (
56
) is closed if and only if

(61)

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\alpha(x,y)(z)

\displaystyle=0,~\forall x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{h}};

(62)

\displaystyle\gamma(x)(y)+\alpha(x,y)(x)

\displaystyle=0,~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

In this case, one can canonically define a strongly polarized quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},{\mathfrak{h}})
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, called
T^{*}
-extension of
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
.

(2)

The form
\kappa
on
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
defined in (
59
) is closed if and only if

(63)

\displaystyle\underset{x,y,z}{\circlearrowleft}\beta(x,y)(z)

\displaystyle=0,~\forall x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{h}};

(64)

\displaystyle\theta(x)(y)+\beta(x,y)(x)

\displaystyle=0,~\forall x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

In this case, one can canonically define a strongly polarized quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\kappa,\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}),{\mathfrak{h}})
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
, called
\Pi T^{*}
-extension of
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
.

Proof.

We will prove Eq. (
62
). Let
x+\xi\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
and
y+\zeta\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

\displaystyle\omega\bigl(s_{\mathfrak{g}}(x+\xi),y+\zeta\bigl)+\omega\bigl(x+\xi,[x+\xi,y+\zeta]_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigl)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\omega(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)+\gamma(x)+\xi\circ\nabla_{x},y+\zeta)+\alpha(x,y)(x)+\xi\circ\nabla_{y}(x)+\zeta\circ\nabla_{x}(x)+\xi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\gamma(x)(y)+\underline{\xi\circ\nabla_{x}(y)}+\overline{\zeta\bigl(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)\bigl)}+\alpha(x,y)(x)+\underline{\xi\circ\nabla_{y}(x)}+\overline{\zeta\circ\nabla_{x}(x)}+\underline{\xi\bigl([x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\bigl)}.

Since
\nabla
is torsion free, the underlined and overlined terms cancel. Thus the conclusion.
∎

3.2.5.

-extension or

∎

.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)

Lemma
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)
and
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N^{\prime})
be two strong polarizations of the same Lagrangian extension with corresponding extension cocycles given respectively by
(\alpha,\gamma)
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
in the ortho-orthogonal case (
(\beta,\theta)
and

(i)

In the case where
\omega
is ortho-orthogonal
, there exists
\sigma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
satisfying

\sigma(x)(y)=\sigma(y)(x),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{h}},

such that
(\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=(\alpha,\gamma)+{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}\sigma
.

(ii)

In the case where
\omega
is periplectic
, there exists
\mu\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))
satisfying

\mu(x)(y)=\mu(y)(x),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{h}},

such that
(\beta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=(\beta,\theta)+{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}\mu
.

Proof.

Let us prove the Lemma in the case where
\omega
is ortho-orthogonal. Denote by
p_{\mathfrak{a}}:{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus N\rightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}
and
p_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}:{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus N^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}
the canonical projections. Let us consider the even map
\tau:=p_{\mathfrak{a}}-p_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
. Notice that
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(\tau)
. Moreover, denote by
p_{N}:{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus N\rightarrow N
and
p_{N}^{\prime}:{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus N^{\prime}\rightarrow N^{\prime}
the projections on the second component. Let
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Since
x=p_{\mathfrak{a}}(x)+p_{N}(x)=p_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(x)+p_{N}^{\prime}(x)
, we have
p_{N}^{\prime}=p_{N}+\tau
. Similarly to
[
BM
, Lemma 6.3.1]
, we have

(83)

\omega\bigl(\tau(n),m\bigl)=\omega\bigl(p_{N}^{\prime}(n),p_{N}(m)\bigl)=\omega\bigl(n,\tau(m)\bigl),~\forall n,m\in N.

Recall that, using the same notations as Theorem
3.2.7.  Theorem
, we have
(\alpha,\gamma)=(i_{\omega}\circ\tilde{\alpha},i_{\omega}\circ\tilde{\gamma}),

where

(84)

\tilde{\alpha}:=p_{\mathfrak{a}}\bigl([p_{N}(\tilde{u}),p_{N}(\tilde{v})]_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigl),~\forall u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}}\text{ and }\tilde{\gamma}(u):=p_{\mathfrak{a}}\bigl(s_{\mathfrak{g}}(p_{N}(\tilde{u}))\bigl),~\forall u\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}}.

For
u\in{\mathfrak{h}},
let
\tilde{\tau}(u):=\tau(\tilde{u})
. First, notice that

(85)

s_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigl(p_{N}^{\prime}(\tilde{u})\bigl)=s_{\mathfrak{g}}(p_{N}(\tilde{u}))+[\tilde{u},\tau(\tilde{u})],~\forall u\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=\omega\bigl(p_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}\circ s_{\mathfrak{g}}(p_{N}^{\prime}(\tilde{u})),\cdot\bigl)

\displaystyle=\omega\bigl(p_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}(s(p_{N}(\tilde{u}))+[\tilde{u},\tau(\tilde{u})]),\cdot\bigl)

\displaystyle=\gamma(u)+\omega\bigl(p_{\mathfrak{a}}([\tilde{u},\tau(\tilde{u})])+\tau\circ s(p_{N}(\tilde{u})),\cdot\bigl)

\displaystyle=\gamma(u)+\rho(u)\circ i_{\omega}\circ\tau(\tilde{u})+i_{\omega}\circ\tau(\widetilde{s(u)})

\displaystyle=\gamma(u)+\rho(u)\circ i_{\omega}\circ\tilde{\tau}(u)+i_{\omega}\circ\tilde{\tau}(s(u))

\displaystyle=\gamma(u)+\delta^{1}(i_{\omega}\circ\tilde{\tau})(u).

Thus, we take
\sigma:=i_{\omega}\circ\tilde{\tau}
. Moreover, it has been shown in
[
BM
, Lemma 6.3.1]
that
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha+d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}\sigma
. Therefore, we have
(\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=(\alpha,\gamma)+{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}\sigma
.
∎

3.5.
Equivalence of Lagrangian extensions

In this section, we introduce the Lagrangian cohomology in characteristic 2 and show that it captures Lagrangian extensions of Lie superalgebras equipped with a flat torsion-free connection. A Lagrangian extension of a Lie superalgebra equipped with a flat torsion-free connection
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
is a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
together with a short exact sequence of Lie superalgebras

(86)

0\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathfrak{g}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow 0,

where the image of
{\mathfrak{a}}
is a Lagrangian ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. An
isomorphism of Lagrangian extensions of
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a Lie isomorphism
\Phi:({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
satisfying
\omega(x,y)=\omega^{\prime}\bigl(\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\bigl),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
such that the following diagram commutes:

(87)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{a}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{g}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{h}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{h}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi|_{{\mathfrak{a}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}

Note that such an isomorphism must satisfy
\Phi|_{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits
.

3.5.1.
Lagrangian cohomology

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
a Lie superalgebra equipped with a flat torsion-free connection
\nabla
. We construct the second cohomology space of the Lagrangian cohomology in characteristic 2 (see
[
BC
,
BM
]
for the characteristic zero case). This cohomology describe all ortho-orthogonal
T^{*}
-extensions and periplectic
\Pi T^{*}
-extensions of
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
, see Theorem
3.5.3.  Theorem
.
We define the first and second Lagrangian cochain spaces as

\displaystyle XC^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{\psi\in XC^{1}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}),~\psi(x)(y)=\psi(y)(x),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{h}}\bigl\},

\displaystyle XC^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{(\alpha,\gamma)\in XC^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}),(\alpha,\gamma)\text{ satisfies }\eqref{condalphagamma1}\text{ and }\eqref{condalphagamma2}\bigl\}.

\displaystyle XC^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{\psi\in XC^{1}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})),~\psi(x)(y)=\psi(y)(x),~\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{h}}\bigl\},

\displaystyle XC^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{(\beta,\theta)\in XC^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})),(\beta,\theta)\text{ satisfies }\eqref{condbetatheta1}\text{ and }\eqref{condbetatheta2}\bigl\}.

In that case, the
{\mathfrak{h}}
-module structure on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
(resp.
\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
) is given by the map
\rho
as defined in Eq. (
52
) (resp. the map
\chi
defined in Eq. (
53
)).

Lemma
.

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
be a Lie superalgebra equipped with a flat torsion-free connection
\nabla
. Then
{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}(XC^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))\subseteq XC^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
and
{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}(XC^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})))\subseteq XC^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))
. Moreover, we have
{\mathfrak{d}}^{2}\circ{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}=(0,0)
in both cases.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in XC^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
,
x\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bar{1}}
and
y\in{\mathfrak{h}}.
We have

\displaystyle\delta^{1}\psi(x)+d_{{\text{CE}}}^{1}\psi(x,y)(x)=

\displaystyle~\psi(x)\circ\nabla_{x}(y)+\psi(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))(y)+\psi(y)\circ\nabla_{x}(x)

\displaystyle~~~+\psi(x)\circ\nabla_{y}(x)+\psi([x,y])(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\underline{\psi(x)\circ\nabla_{x}(y)}+\psi(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))(y)+\psi(y)\circ s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)

\displaystyle~~~+\underline{\psi(x)\circ\nabla_{y}(x)}+\underline{\psi(x)([x,y])}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~0,

where the underlined terms cancel because
\nabla
is torsion-free and the others because of the definition of the space
XC^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
. Therefore, the pair
(d_{{\text{CE}}}^{1}\psi,\delta^{1}\psi)
satisfies Condition (
62
). Similarly, we show that
d_{{\text{CE}}}^{1}
satisfy Condition (
61
). Thus,
{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}\psi=(d_{{\text{CE}}}^{1}\psi,\delta^{1}\psi)\in XC^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
. Let us show that
{\mathfrak{d}}^{2}\circ{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}=(0,0)
.

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\bigl(d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}\psi,\delta^{1}\psi\bigl)=

\displaystyle~\rho(x)\circ d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}\psi(x,y)+\rho(y)\circ\delta^{1}\psi(x)+d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}\psi(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),y)+d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}\psi([x,y],y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\psi(y)\circ\nabla_{x}^{2}+\psi(x)\circ\nabla_{y}\circ\nabla_{x}+\psi([x,y])\circ\nabla_{x}

\displaystyle~~~+\psi(x)\circ\nabla_{x}\circ\nabla_{y}+\psi(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))\circ\nabla_{y}

\displaystyle~~~+\psi(y)\circ\nabla_{s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x)}+\psi(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x))\circ\nabla_{y}+\psi([s_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),y])

\displaystyle~~~+\psi(x)\circ\nabla_{[x,y]}+\psi([x,y])\circ\nabla_{x}+\psi\bigl([[x,y],x]\bigl)=0,

since the connection
\nabla
is flat. Similarly, we have
d^{2}_{{\text{CE}}}\circ d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}\psi=0.

∎

We denote by

\displaystyle XZ^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{(\alpha,\gamma)\in XC^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}),~{\mathfrak{d}}^{2}(\alpha,\gamma)=(0,0)\bigl\}

\displaystyle XZ^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{(\beta,\theta)\in XC^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*})),~{\mathfrak{d}}^{2}(\beta,\theta)=(0,0)\bigl\}.

Thus, we define

\displaystyle XH^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})

\displaystyle:=XZ^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})/{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}\bigl(XC^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})\bigl);

\displaystyle XH^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))

\displaystyle:=XZ^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))/{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}\bigl(XC^{1}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))\bigl),

the second cohomology groups of the Lagrangian cohomology.

Theorem
.

Let
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
be a Lie superalgebra equipped with a flat torsion-free connection
\nabla
. There is a one-to-one correspondence
(
up to isomorphism in the sense of (
87
)
)
between

(i)

T^{*}
-extensions of
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
XH^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})_{\bar{0}}
, in the ortho-orthogonal case;

(ii)

\Pi T^{*}
-extensions of
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
XH^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))_{\bar{0}}
, in the periplectic case.

Moreover, two Lagrangian extensions of
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
are isomorphic
(
in the sense of (
87
)
)
if and only if they correspond to the same extension 2-cocycle in
XH^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})_{\bar{0}}
or
XH^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},\Pi({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}))_{\bar{0}}
.

Proof.

We prove the Theorem in the ortho-orthogonal case. The proof follows
[
BM
, Theorem 6.4.3]
. Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}})
be a Lagrangian extension of
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
. Consider a strong polarization
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)
. By Theorem
3.2.4.  Theorem
,
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)
is a
T^{*}
-extension of
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
by means of a 2-cocycle
(\alpha,\gamma)\in XH_{L}^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})_{\bar{0}}
. By Lemma
3.4.1.  Lemma
, the 2-cocycle
(\alpha,\gamma)
does not depend on the choice of the strong polarization
N
. Let
\Phi:({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}})\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})
be an isomorphism of Lagrangian extensions (in the sense of (
87
)) and denote by
(\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\in XH_{L}^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})_{\bar{0}}
the 2-cocycle corresponding to
({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})
. Then, for any choice of strong polarization
N
, the map
\Phi:({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}},N)\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime},\Phi(N))
is an isomorphism of strongly polarized ortho-orthogonal Lie superalgebras (see Section
3.3
). By Lemma
3.3.2.  Lemma
, we have
\alpha^{\prime}=\Phi_{*}\alpha
and
\gamma^{\prime}=\Phi_{*}\gamma
. Therefore, isomorphic extensions correspond to cohomologous extension cocycles.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}})
and
({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime})
be two
T^{*}
-extensions of
({\mathfrak{h}},\nabla)
sharing the same extension cocycle. By Theorem
3.2.4.  Theorem
, it is enough to show that any two strongly polarized ortho-orthogonal Lie superalgebras produce isomorphic extensions if
(\alpha,\gamma)=(\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\in XH_{L}^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})_{\bar{0}}
, that is,
(\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=(\alpha,\gamma)+{\mathfrak{d}}^{1}\sigma
(see Lemma
3.4.1.  Lemma
). The map

\Phi:({\mathfrak{g}},\omega,{\mathfrak{a}})\rightarrow({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}),\hskip 10.00002pt(u,\xi)\mapsto(u,\xi+\sigma(u))

is an isomorphism, since we have

\displaystyle s_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigl(\Phi(u+\xi)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\bigl(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\gamma(u)+\rho(u)\xi+\rho(u)\sigma(u)\bigl)

\displaystyle=\bigl(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\gamma^{\prime}(u)+\rho(u)\sigma(u)+\sigma(s_{\mathfrak{h}}(u))\bigl)=\Phi\bigl(s_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}(u+\xi)\bigl).

∎

Hereafter we list a few examples.

3.6.1.
The Lie superalgebra
\bf{L_{1|1}^{1}}

Consider the solvable Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}:=\bf{L_{1|1}^{1}}
of superdimension
(1|1)
given in the basis
(e|f)
by the bracket
[e,f]=f
and the squaring
s=0
. Let
\varepsilon\in\K
be a parameter. We define a flat torsion-free connection
\nabla^{\varepsilon}
on
{\mathfrak{h}}
by

(88)

\nabla^{\varepsilon}_{e}(e)=(1+\varepsilon)e,\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla^{\varepsilon}_{e}(f)=\varepsilon f,\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla^{\varepsilon}_{f}(e)=(1+\varepsilon)f,\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla^{\varepsilon}_{f}(f)=0.

The
{\mathfrak{h}}
-module structure on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is given by

(89)

e\cdot e^{*}=(1+\varepsilon)e^{*},\hskip 10.00002ptf\cdot e^{*}=0,\hskip 10.00002pte\cdot f^{*}=\varepsilon f^{*},\hskip 10.00002ptf\cdot f^{*}=(1+\varepsilon)e^{*}.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\in\K
and consider the 2-cochains
(\alpha_{1},\gamma_{1}),(\alpha_{2},\gamma_{2}),(0,\gamma_{3})
, with

\alpha_{1}=e^{*}\otimes e^{*}\wedge f^{*},\gamma_{1}(f)=\lambda_{1}e^{*};~\alpha_{2}=f^{*}\otimes e^{*}\wedge f^{*},\gamma_{2}(f)=\lambda_{2}e^{*};~\gamma_{3}(f)=\lambda_{3}e^{*}.

Claim 1.
The cochain
(\alpha_{1},\gamma_{1})
is a
2
-cocycle if and only if
\varepsilon=1
or (
\varepsilon\neq 1
and
\lambda_{1}=0
).

Proof of Claim 1.
The second cocycle condition (see Eq. (
9
)) is given by

(90)

f\cdot\alpha_{1}(f,z)+z\cdot\gamma_{1}(f)=\alpha_{1}\bigl(s(f),z\bigl)+\alpha_{1}\bigl([z,f],f\bigl),~\forall z\in{\mathfrak{h}}.

By plugging
z=e
in the above equation, we obtain
\lambda_{1}(1+\varepsilon)e^{*}=0.
The case
z=f
reduces to
0=0
. Similarly, we obtain the follwing statements.

Claim 2.
The cochain
(\alpha_{2},\gamma_{2})
is a
2
-cocycle if and only if
\varepsilon=1
or (
\varepsilon\neq 1
and
\lambda_{2}=1
).

Claim 3.
The cochain
(0,\gamma_{3})
is a
2
-cocycle if and only if
\varepsilon=1
or
\lambda_{3}=0
.

Claim 4.
The cohomology group
XH^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
is not trivial if and only if
\varepsilon=1
. In that case, a basis of
XH^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
is given by
\{(\alpha_{2},0),(0,\gamma_{3}^{1})\}
, where
\alpha_{2}=f^{*}\otimes e^{*}\wedge f^{*}
and
\gamma_{3}^{1}(f)=e^{*}
.

Proof of Claim 4.
First, notice that
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
and
\gamma_{3}
are cohomologous to
\gamma_{3}^{1}
.

The case where
\varepsilon\neq 1
. We have

\displaystyle d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})(e,f)

\displaystyle=(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})([e,f])+e\cdot(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})(f)+f\cdot(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})(e)=(1+\varepsilon)f^{*};

\displaystyle\delta^{1}(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})(f)

\displaystyle=f\cdot(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})(f)+(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})(s(f))=(1+\varepsilon)e^{*}.

Therefore,
\alpha_{2}=d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}\bigl((1+\varepsilon)^{-1}(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})\bigl)
and
\gamma_{2}=\delta^{1}\bigl((1+\varepsilon)^{-1}(f^{*}\otimes f^{*})\bigl)
. Thus,
(\alpha_{2},\gamma_{2})
is a coboundary. Similarly, one can show that

(\alpha_{1},0)=\Bigl(d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}\bigl(f^{*}\otimes e^{*}+e^{*}\otimes f^{*}\bigl),\delta^{1}\bigl(f^{*}\otimes e^{*}+e^{*}\otimes f^{*}\bigl)\Bigl).

Therefore,
XH_{L}^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})=0.

The case where
\varepsilon=1
. We have

\displaystyle d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}(e^{*}\otimes f^{*})(e,f)

\displaystyle=(e^{*}\otimes f^{*})([e,f])+e\cdot(e^{*}\otimes f^{*})(f)+f\cdot(e^{*}\otimes f^{*})(e)=e^{*};

\displaystyle\delta^{1}(e^{*}\otimes f^{*})(f)

\displaystyle=(e^{*}\otimes f^{*})(s(f))=0.

Therefore,
(\alpha_{1},0)=\bigl(d^{1}_{{\text{CE}}}(e^{*}\otimes f^{*}+f^{*}\otimes e^{*}),\delta^{1}(e^{*}\otimes f^{*}+f^{*}\otimes e^{*})\bigl)
is a coboundary. Therefore,
XH^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
has dimension
2
and is spanned by
\{(\alpha_{2},0),(0,\gamma_{3}^{1})\}
. In that case, we compute the Lagrangian cohomology. The pairs
(\alpha_{2},0)
and
(0,\gamma_{3}^{1})
does not satisfy Eqs. (
61
) and (
62
). However, we have

\gamma_{3}^{1}(f)(e)+\alpha_{2}(f,e)(f)=e^{*}(e)+f^{*}(f)=0\text{ and }\gamma_{3}^{1}(f)(f)+\alpha_{2}(f,f)(f)=0.

Therefore, the pair
(\alpha_{2},\gamma_{3}^{1})
is Lagrangian. It follows that the space
XH^{2}_{L}({\mathfrak{h}},{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
is one-dimensional and spanned by
(\alpha_{2},\gamma_{3}^{1})
.

3.6.2.
The Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{ba}}(1)

Consider the nilpotent Lie superalgebra

In this Appendix, we initiate the study of post-Lie superalgebras in characteristic
2
. Over a field of characteristic zero, Vallette introduced the post-Lie operad as the Koszul dual of the operad of commutative triassociative algebras (
[
Va
]
). They were independently described in the context of numerical integration methods in
[
MKW
,
MKL
]
. For a survey on those structures, see
[
CEM
]
.

5.1.
Post-Lie superalgebras

Let
(V,\{\cdot,\cdot\},s)
be a Lie superalgebra in characteristic
2
. It is called
post-Lie superalgebra
if it is endowed with a bilinear product
\ast:V\times V\rightarrow V
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{lrlll}(i)&\{x,y\}\ast z&=&(y\ast x)\cdot z+y\ast(x\ast z)+(x\ast y)\ast z+x\ast(y\ast z),&\forall x,y,z\in V;\\[5.69054pt]
(ii)&s(x)\ast y&=&x\ast(x\ast y)+(x\ast x)\ast y,&\forall x\in V_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in V;\\[5.69054pt]
(iii)&x\ast\{y,z\}&=&\{x\ast y,z\}+\{y,x\ast z\},&\forall x,y,z\in V;\\[5.69054pt]
(iv)&x\ast s(y)&=&\{x\ast y,y\},&\forall x\in V_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in V.\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

A post-Lie superalgebra will be denoted
(V,\{\cdot,\cdot\},s,\ast).
Note that a post-Lie product over an abelian Lie superalgebra in nothing more that a left-symmetric product.

Remarks.

(1)

Axiom
(i)
is equivalent to

\{x,y\}\ast z=\Asso(y,x,z)+\Asso(x,y,z),~\forall x,y,z\in V,
where
\Asso(u,v,w)=(u\ast v)\ast w+u\ast(v\ast w),~\forall u,v,w\in V.

(2)

Axiom
(ii)
is equivalent to
s(x)\ast y=\Asso(x,x,y),~\forall x\in V_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in V;

(3)

Axioms
(iii)
and
(iv)
together are equivalent to saying that
{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}
is a derivation for all
x\in V
(in the sense of (
5
)), where
{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}:V\rightarrow V,~y\mapsto x\ast y
is the left multiplication.

Proposition
.

Let
(V,\{\cdot,\cdot\},s,\ast)
be a post-Lie superalgebra. Let us define a new bracket and a new squaring on
V
as follows:

\begin{cases}[x,y]:=x\ast y+y\ast x+\{x,y\},~\forall x,y\in V;\\
\tilde{s}(x):=x\ast x+s(x),~\forall x\in V_{\bar{1}}.\end{cases}

Then
(V,[\cdot,\cdot],\tilde{s})
is a Lie superalgebra, called
associated Lie superalgebra.

Proof.

We only prove the Jacobi identity involving the squaring. Let
x\in V_{\bar{1}}
and
y\in V
. We have

\displaystyle[x,[x,y]]=

\displaystyle~[x,x\ast y]+[x,y\ast x]+[x,\{x,y\}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~x\ast(x\ast y)+(x\ast y)\ast x+\{x,x\ast y\}

\displaystyle+x\ast(y\ast x)+(y\ast x)\ast x+\{x,y\ast x\}

\displaystyle+x\ast\{x,y\}+\{x,y\}\ast x+\{x,\{x,y\}\}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle[\tilde{s}(x),y]=

\displaystyle~(x\ast x)\ast y+s(x)\ast y+y\ast(x\ast x)+y\ast s(x)+\{x\ast x,y\}+\{s(x),y\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~x\ast(x\ast y)+y\ast(x\ast x)+\{y\ast x,x\}+\{x\ast x,y\}+\{s(x),y\}.

Therefore,

[x,[x,y]]+[\tilde{s}(x),y]=\{x\ast x,y\}+\{x,x\ast y\}+x\ast\{x,y\}=0.\qed

Proposition
.

Let
(V,\{\cdot,\cdot\},s,\ast)
be a post-Lie superalgebra and
(V,[\cdot,\cdot],\tilde{s})
be its associated Lie superalgebra. Then

(93)

\displaystyle[x,y]\ast z

\displaystyle=x\ast(y\ast z)+y\ast(x\ast z),~\forall x,y,z\in V;

(94)

\displaystyle\tilde{s}(x)\ast y

\displaystyle=x\ast(x\ast y),~\forall x\in V_{\bar{1}},~\forall y\in V.

Proof.

We only prove the identity involving the squaring. Let
x\in V_{\bar{1}}
and
y\in V
. We have

\tilde{s}(x)\ast y=\bigl(x\ast x+s(x)\bigl)\ast y=(x\ast x)\ast y+x\ast(x\ast y)+(x\ast x)\ast y=x\ast(x\ast y).\qed

Corollary
.

For all
x\in V
, the left multiplication
{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}
is a representation of
(V,[\cdot,\cdot],\tilde{s})
.

5.1.4.

Let
({\mathfrak{n}},{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pair of Lie superalgebras sharing the same super vector space

\begin{array}[]{lrlll}(i)&[x,y]-\{x,y\}&=&x\ast y+y\ast x,&\forall x,y\in V;\\[5.69054pt]

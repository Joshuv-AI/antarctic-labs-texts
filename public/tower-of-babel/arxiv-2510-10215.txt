Bounds of Validity for Bifurcations of Equilibria in a Class of Networked Dynamical Systems
Pranav Gupta, Ravi Banavar, Anastasia Bizyaeva

Abstract
Local bifurcation analysis plays a central role in understanding qualitative transitions in networked nonlinear dynamical systems, including dynamic neural network and opinion dynamics models. In this article we establish explicit bounds of validity for the classification of bifurcation diagrams in two classes of continuous-time networked dynamical systems, analogous in structure to the Hopfield and the Firing Rate dynamic neural network models. Our approach leverages recent advances in computing the bounds for the validity of Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction, a reduction method widely employed in nonlinear systems analysis. Using these bounds we rigorously characterize neighborhoods around bifurcation points where predictions from reduced-order models remain reliable. We further demonstrate how these bounds can be applied to an illustrative family of nonlinear opinion dynamics on k − k- regular graphs, which emerges as a special case of the general framework. These results provide new analytical tools for quantifying the robustness of bifurcation phenomena in dynamics over networked systems and highlight the interplay between network structure and nonlinear dynamical behavior.

Local bifurcations are points of critical transitions in behavior of nonlinear dynamical systems that occcur when a parameter crosses a threshold, causing the stability of an equilibrium point to change and new features such as new equilibria to emerge.
Bifurcations are a common route to multistability in complex systems, and bifurcation analysis is used to characterize the emergence, stability, and basins of attraction of coexisting equilibria as parameters vary.

II-B

Lyapunov-Schmidt Reduction

Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction is a method used to analyze parametrized nonlinear dynamical systems near their singular equilibria, leveraging the Implicit Function theorem.
The method decomposes the state space of a dynamical system into the null space of its Jacobian and its orthogonal complement at the singular point. The Implicit Function theorem is then used to eliminate the component orthogonal to the null space in the local bifurcation diagram of the system, effectively projecting the high-dimensional equations describing the equilibria of the model onto the kernel
[
20
, Ch. VII]
. The resulting reduced equations capture the structure of the local bifurcation diagram of the original system. We now present a summary of the reduction procedure.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be open sets. Consider the parametrized nonlinear dynamical system
\dot{x}=\Phi(x,p)
where
x\in X
is the state vector,
p\in\Lambda
is the parameter vector, and
\Phi:X\times\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is at least
\mathcal{C}^{2}
and has a bifurcation at
(x^{\star},p^{\star})\in X\times\Lambda
, i.e.
\Phi(x^{\star},p^{\star})=0
and
J:=D_{x}\Phi(x^{\star},p^{\star})
is singular (non-invertible) with
q:=\dim(\ker J)>0
.

Define orthonormal basis matrices
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times q}
and
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-q)}
whose column vectors span
\ker(J)
and
\ker(J)^{\perp}
respectively. Explicitly,

V=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}&\dots&v_{q}\end{bmatrix},\qquad W=\begin{bmatrix}w_{q+1}&\dots&w_{n}\end{bmatrix}.

(1)

By construction,
\forall\;i\in\{q+1,\dots,n\}
,
k\in\{1,\dots,q\}
we have
w_{i}^{\top}v_{k}=0
. Moreover, the projections onto
\ker(J)
and
\ker(J)^{\perp}
can be expressed as

P_{\parallel}=VV^{\top},\qquad P_{\perp}=WW^{\top}.

We denote the direct sum splitting of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into
\ker(J)\oplus\ker(J)^{\perp}
by

x\mapsto(x^{\parallel},x^{\perp}):=(P_{\parallel}x,P_{\perp}x)\in\ker(J)\times\ker(J)^{\perp}.

Define coordinate map
\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-q}\ni(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)\in X
:

x=\Gamma(\alpha,\beta):=\underbrace{V\alpha}_{x^{\parallel}}+\underbrace{W\beta}_{x^{\perp}}\,\;\text{where}\;\begin{cases}\alpha=V^{\top}x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\\
\beta=W^{\top}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-q}\end{cases}

(2)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
represent the reduced coordinates of
x^{\parallel}
and
x^{\perp}
, corresponding to components in
\ker(J)
and its orthogonal complement
\ker(J)^{\perp}
, respectively. We employ this coordinate map as a shorthand throughout the analysis.

The Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction involves applying the Implicit Function theorem to obtain a locally valid implicit mapping
(\alpha,p)\mapsto\beta
. In particular, it guarantees the existence of open neighborhoods
\mathcal{N}\big((\alpha_{0},p^{\star})\big)\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\Lambda
and
\mathcal{N}(\beta_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-q}
equipped with implicit map
\varphi:\mathcal{N}\big((\alpha_{0},p^{\star})\big)\to\mathcal{N}(\beta_{0})

that expresses coordinates along the range of the Jacobian as a function of the coordinates along the null space and the bifurcation parameter,
\beta=\varphi(\alpha,p)
, satisfying

\Phi(V\alpha+W\varphi(\alpha,p),p)=0

(3)

for all
(\alpha,p)\in\mathcal{N}\big((\alpha_{0},p^{\star})\big)
.

II-C

Bounds of Validity for Lyapunov-Schmidt Reduction

A key limitation of Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction is that the equivalence between the reduced bifurcation equations and the equilibria of the original high-dimensional system holds only locally.
Consequently, the analysis of bifurcations leveraging this reduction method is valid only in a small neighborhood of the singular point.
Understanding the extent of these neighborhoods is crucial for evaluating the robustness of conclusions drawn from analysis of the reduced equations.
To address this limitation, in previous work we derived a general bound of validity for Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction in systems of ordinary differential equations
[
18
]
. In this section we summarize our recent result.

In the context of Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction, we obtain computable lower bound approximations for the neighborhoods
\mathcal{N}\big((\alpha_{0},p^{\star})\big)
,
\mathcal{N}(\beta_{0})
as open balls
\mathcal{B}\big((\alpha_{0},p^{\star}),r_{\parallel}\big)
and
\mathcal{B}(\beta_{0},r_{\perp})
.
The radii
r_{\parallel}
and
r_{\perp}
bound the regions around the singular point
(\alpha,\beta,p)=(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},p^{\star})
where the reduced and full bifurcation diagrams coincide topologically.
In particular
r_{\parallel}
defines the extent of equivalence along the critical subspace, i.e. the product between
\ker(J)
and the parameter space
\Lambda
.
Analogously,
r_{\perp}
defines the extent of equivalence along
\ker(J)^{\perp}
.
The algorithm to explicitly determine the bounding radii
r_{\parallel}
and
r_{\perp}
is described below.

Before we state the theorem on bounds, we define the following quantities.First, we define two scalar quantities that will appear in the statement of the bounds,

\displaystyle M_{\parallel}

\displaystyle:=\left\lVert{\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{(n-q)\times q}&W^{\top}D_{p}\Phi(x^{\star},p^{\star})\end{bmatrix}}\right\rVert,

(4a)

\displaystyle M_{\perp}

\displaystyle:=\left\lVert{\left(W^{\top}D_{x}\Phi(x^{\star},p^{\star})W\right)^{-1}}\right\rVert.

(4b)

Next, we define the maps

and

as

\displaystyle\xi_{\parallel}(\alpha,p)

\displaystyle:=W^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{\parallel\alpha}(\alpha,p)&\xi_{\parallel p}(\alpha,p)\end{bmatrix},

(5a)

\displaystyle\xi_{\perp}(\alpha,\beta,p)

\displaystyle:=W^{\top}\Bigl(D_{x}\Phi\bigl(\Gamma(\alpha,\beta),p\bigr)-D_{x}\Phi(x^{\star},p^{\star})\Bigr)W,

(5b)

where

The maps (

VI
Discussion

In this article we established explicit, computable bounds for the validity of Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction-based bifurcation analysis in representative classes of networked nonlinear systems, and demonstrated their utility by applying the bounds to an illustrative class of nonlinear consensus problems on regular graphs, for which the evaluation proves to be analytically computable.
Notably, the derived expressions depend directly on interpretable features, such as the spectral properties of the underlying graph, providing insight into how network structure influences these bounds.

For future work, we aim to identify additional classes of systems with bifurcations analyzed with the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction for which these bounds can be computed analytically.

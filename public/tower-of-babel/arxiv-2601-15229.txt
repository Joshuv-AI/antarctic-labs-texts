Vieta jumping and small norms in quadratic number fields
Franz Lemmermeyer

Abstract
. In this article we explain the connection between the famous problem from the IMO 1988 and elements of small norms in quadratic number fields with parametrized units.



Introduction

The following problem

Let

It is our intention to show that Vieta jumping is a technique that may

1.
Vieta Jumping and an IMO Problem

Before we look at the solution of this IMO problem we determine all solutions
in integers and rational numbers when the quotient is a perfect square.
If

Next we will show that each integral point on

The tool for proving this claim is the following lemma:

Lemma 4
.

If
(a,b)
is an integral point with positive coordinates
a\neq b

on
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}

(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
on
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}

with
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\geq 0
and
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}<a+b

Proof.

We set

(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=\begin{cases}(a,ka-b)&\text{ if }b>a,\\
(kb-a,b)&\text{ if }b<a.\\
\end{cases}

From
a^{2}-kab+b^{2}=k
we deduce that

ka-b=\frac{a^{2}-k}{b}
and
kb-a=\frac{b^{2}-k}{a}
.

•

a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}<a+b
: In the first case,

b^{\prime}=ka-b=\frac{a^{2}-k}{b}<b
since
a^{2}-k<a^{2}<b^{2}
;
in the second case,
a^{\prime}=kb-a=\frac{b^{2}-k}{a}<a
since

b^{2}-k<b^{2}<a^{2}
. Thus in both cases
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}<a+b
.

•

a^{\prime}\geq 0
,
b^{\prime}\geq 0
: In fact, if

b^{\prime}=ka-b<0
in the first case, then
ab^{\prime}\leq-1

0={b^{\prime}}^{2}-kab^{\prime}+a^{2}-k\geq{b^{\prime}}^{2}+k+a^{2}-k=a^{2}+{b^{\prime 2}}>0,

which is a contradiction; thus
b^{\prime}\geq 0
.

∎

A family of equations

Our next result strengthens an observation given without proof by
Milchev

Theorem 6
.

If the diophantine equation

(6)

x^{2}-pxy+y^{2}=q

has integral solutions for integers

0<q\leq p+1

is a square.

The result is best possible in the sense that if

, then there
are infinitely many integral solutions provided by Vieta jumping
starting with
(1,-1)
. Given an integral point
(a,b)
on the conic

{\mathcal{M}}:x^{2}-pxy+y^{2}=p+2

\sharp(a,b)=(a,ap-b)
and
\flat(a,b)=(bp-a,b)

chains of integral points

\ldots(-p-1,-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\flat}}{{\longrightarrow}}(1,-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sharp}}{{\longrightarrow}}(1,p+1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\flat}}{{\longrightarrow}}(p^{2}+p-1,p+1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sharp}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ldots

\ldots(-1,-p-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sharp}}{{\longrightarrow}}(-1,1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\flat}}{{\longrightarrow}}(p+1,1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sharp}}{{\longrightarrow}}(p+1,p^{2}+p-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\flat}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ldots.

Assume that
(a,b)
is an integral point with

\sharp(a,b)=(a,b^{\prime})

b^{\prime}=\frac{a^{2}-q}{b}<\frac{a^{2}}{q}<\frac{ab}{b}=a

Theorem 7

The diophantine equation

The minimal positive

The role of the Pell conic

In our first solution of the IMO problem (as well as our result concerning
(
6

explaining a connection of the IMO problem with the arithmetic of certain
real quadratic number fields. We begin with describing the role of the Pell
equation.

It is well know that rational points on conics form a group; if we
fix a rational point
N
, then the sum
P\oplus Q
of two points
on a conic is the second point of intersection of the parallel to
the line
PQ
through
N

k=4

By completing the square we can write

of

\displaystyle=2x-y+x\sqrt{3}=x^{\prime}-2y^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\sqrt{3}

with

x^{\prime}=4x-y\quad\text{and}\quad y^{\prime}=x.

Thus the point
P(x,y)
on
{\mathcal{C}}_{r}
is mapped to
Q(4x-y,x)
;
for example,
(2,0)
gets mapped to
(8,2)
.

Second Solution of the IMO problem

We now use the action of the Pell conic on
{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
to give a second solution
of the IMO problem. The basic idea is to use the action of the Pell
conic to move an integral point to a bounded region close to the origin.
For finding good bounds we use an idea by Davenport published in

[
1
]
.

Proposition 9
.

If the curve
{\mathcal{C}}:x^{2}-2kxy+y^{2}=\pm m
for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
has integral
points, then
m
is a square or
m\geq 2k+2
.

The equation
x^{2}-2kxy+y^{2}=\pm m
is equivalent to

(8)

(x-ky)^{2}-(k^{2}-1)y^{2}=\pm m.

Thus the claim follows from the next lemma, which is due to
Davenport (see
[
1
]
):

Lemma 10
.

Let
x,y,m
be positive integers such that

x^{2}-(t^{2}-1)y^{2}=\pm m
. Then
m
is a square or
|m|\geq 2t+2
.

Proof.

The unit
\varepsilon=t+\sqrt{t^{2}-1}
has norm
1
; we have

(x-y\sqrt{t^{2}-1}\,)(t+\sqrt{t^{2}-1}\,)=xt-(t^{2}-1)y+(x-ty)\sqrt{t^{2}-1},

hence we have the two equations

\displaystyle x^{2}-(t^{2}-1)y^{2}

\displaystyle=\pm m,

\displaystyle(xt-y(t^{2}-1))^{2}-(x-ty)^{2}(t^{2}-1)

\displaystyle=n.

Assume that we have chosen
y>0
minimal. Then
|x-ty|\geq y
.
Thus either
x\geq y(t+1)
or
0\leq x\leq(t-1)y
. This implies

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=x^{2}-(t^{2}-1)y^{2}\geq(t+1)^{2}y^{2}-(t^{2}-1)y^{2}=(2t+2)y^{2}\geq 2t+2\quad\text{or}

\displaystyle-m

\displaystyle=x^{2}-(t^{2}-1)y^{2}\leq(t-1)^{2}y^{2}-(t^{2}-1)y^{2}=-(2t+2)y^{2}\leq-(2t+2).

This completes the proof.
∎

Elements of small norm in quadratic number fields

Next we are going to use the arithmetic of quadratic number fields
to give a solution to the IMO problem. The relevant techniques were
studied in
[
4
]
and
[
8
]
. Equation (
8
)
describes elements of small norm in quadratic number fields; in this
section we will prove several results on elements of small norm in
certain real quadratic number fields; these will be used in the
sections below.

We begin by recalling a well known result (going back in a slightly
weaker form to Dirichlet and Chebyshev; see the references in
[
3
]
):

Proposition 11
.

Let
k={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{m}\,)
be a real quadratic number field,

\varepsilon>1
a unit in k, and
0\not=\nu=|N\xi\,|

for
\xi\in k
. Then there is a unit
\eta=\varepsilon^{j}

such that
\xi\eta=a+b\sqrt{m}
and

|a|<\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{2}\cdot B\quad\text{ and }\quad|b|<\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{2\sqrt{m}}\cdot B,\quad\text{where}\quad B=\sqrt{\varepsilon}+1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

For
\alpha\in k
let
\alpha^{\prime}
denote the conjugate of
\alpha
.
Thus
N\xi=\xi\xi^{\prime}
, where
N=N_{k/{\mathbb{Q}}}
denotes the absolute norm.

Now let
\xi\in k
and a real positive number
c
be given;
we can find a unit
\eta\in E_{k}
such that

(9)

c\leq|\,\xi\eta\,|<c\varepsilon.

With
\nu=|N\xi|
we find
|\xi^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}|=\nu/|\xi\eta|
,
so equation (
9
) yields

(10)

\frac{\nu}{c\varepsilon}\leq|\,\xi^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}\,|<\frac{\nu}{c}.

Writing
\alpha=\xi\eta=a+b\sqrt{m}
gives

\displaystyle|2a|

\displaystyle=|\alpha+\alpha^{\prime}|\leq|\alpha|+|\alpha^{\prime}|<c\varepsilon+\frac{\nu}{c},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle|2b\sqrt{m}\,|

\displaystyle=|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|\leq|\alpha|+|\alpha^{\prime}|<c\varepsilon+\frac{\nu}{c}.

Because we want the coefficients
a
and
b
to be as small
as possible, we have to choose
c
in such a way that

c\varepsilon+\nu/c
becomes a minimum. Putting
c=\sqrt{\nu/\varepsilon}
we get

(11)

|2a|<2\sqrt{\nu\varepsilon},\quad|2b\sqrt{m}\,|<2\sqrt{\nu\varepsilon}.

Making use of a lemma due to Cassels, we can improve these bounds:

Lemma 12
.

Suppose that the positive real numbers
x,y
satisfy the
inequalities
x\leq s
,
y\leq s
, and
xy\leq t
. Then

x+y\leq s+t/s
.

Proof.

We have
0\leq(x-s)(y-s)=xy-s(x+y)+s^{2}\leq s^{2}+t-s(x+y)
.
∎

Putting
x=|\alpha|
and
y=|\alpha^{\prime}|
in Lemma
12

we find

\displaystyle|2a|

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha|+|\alpha^{\prime}|<\sqrt{\nu\varepsilon}+\sqrt{\nu/\varepsilon},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle|2b\sqrt{m}\,|

\displaystyle<\sqrt{\nu\varepsilon}+\sqrt{\nu/\varepsilon}.

This proves our claims.

Proposition 13
.

Let
m
and
n\geq 2
be natural numbers such that
m=n^{2}-1
; if
the diophantine equation
|x^{2}-my^{2}|=\nu
has solutions in

{\mathbb{Z}}
for some integer
\nu
with
0<\nu<2n-2
, then
\nu
is a
perfect square.

Proof.

Let
\xi=x+y\sqrt{m}
; then
|\,N\xi\,|=N
, and since

\varepsilon=n+\sqrt{m}\,>1
is a unit in
{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{m}\,]
,
we can find a power
\eta
of
\varepsilon
such that

\xi\eta=a+b\sqrt{m}
has coefficients
a,\ b
that satisfy
the bounds in Prop.
11
. Since the bound is a monotone decreasing
function of
n
, it attains its maximum at
n=2
. Since
b
is an integer,
we must have
|b|\leq 1
.

If
b=0
, then
|N\xi|=a^{2}
is a square. Assume therefore that

b=\pm 1
; this yields
\alpha=\xi\eta=a\pm\sqrt{m}.

Now
|N\xi|=|N\alpha|=|a^{2}-m|
is minimal for
values of
a
near
\sqrt{m}
, and we find

|a^{2}-m|=\begin{cases}2n-2&\text{ if }a=n-1;\\
1&\text{ if }a=n;\\
2n+2&\text{ if }a=n+1.\end{cases}

This proves the claim.
∎

Proposition 14
.

Let
n\geq 7
be an odd integer and set
m=n^{2}-4
.
If
|x^{2}-my^{2}|=\nu
has a solution in integers with
N<n+2
,
then either
\nu=n-2
or
\nu
is a perfect square.

The proof is left to the readers.
Observe that
\varepsilon=\frac{n+\sqrt{m}}{2}
is a unit with norm
+1
;
nontrivial elements with small norm are

\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}\raisebox{0.0pt}[11.62494pt][5.59721pt]{\rule{0.0pt}{8.61108pt} }\alpha&\varepsilon+1&\varepsilon-1&2\varepsilon+1&2\varepsilon-1\\
\hline\cr\raisebox{0.0pt}[11.62494pt][5.59721pt]{\rule{0.0pt}{8.61108pt} }N\alpha&2+n&2-n&5+2n&5-2n\end{array}

Proposition 15
.

Let
m,n,t
be natural numbers such that
m=n^{2}+2
and
n\geq 5
;
if the diophantine equation
|x^{2}-my^{2}|=\nu
has solutions in
{\mathbb{Z}}

with
\nu<2n+1
, then either
\nu=2n-1
, or
\nu
or
2\nu
is a
perfect square.

Here
\delta=n+\sqrt{n^{2}+2}
has norm
N\delta=-2
, and

\delta^{2}=2\varepsilon
for the fundamental unit
\varepsilon=n^{2}+1+n\sqrt{n^{2}+2}
.
Since
\varepsilon\approx 2n^{2}+2
, the bounds in Prop.
11
are not good enough
for our proof to work. Observe that
\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}=\sqrt{2n^{2}+4}

and
\sqrt[4]{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\varepsilon}}=\sqrt{\sqrt{2n^{2}+4}+2}
.

Proof.

Proof. Let
\xi=x+y\sqrt{m},\nu=|N\xi|
,
and suppose that neither
\nu
nor
2\nu
are perfect squares.
Letting
\delta=t+\sqrt{m}
, we find
\delta^{2}=2\varepsilon
,
where
\varepsilon
is a unit in
{\mathbb{Z}}[\sqrt{m}\,]
.
Obviously we can find a power
\eta
of
\varepsilon
such that

\sqrt{\nu\sqrt{\varepsilon}}/\varepsilon\leq|\xi\eta|<\sqrt{\nu\sqrt{\varepsilon}}.

Now we distinguish two cases:

1.

\quad\sqrt{\nu/\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\leq|\xi\eta|<\sqrt{\nu\sqrt{\varepsilon}}:

Writing
\xi\eta=a+b\sqrt{m}
, we find that
|b|\leq 1
. In fact,

|2b\sqrt{m}\,|=|\xi\eta-\xi^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}|\leq|\xi\eta\,|+|\xi^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}|\leq\sqrt{\nu}\cdot B

with

B=\sqrt[4]{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\varepsilon}}=\sqrt{\sqrt{2n^{2}+4}+2}.

Thus

|b|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2n+1}}{\sqrt{n^{2}+2}}\cdot\sqrt{\sqrt{2n^{2}+4}+2}.

This bound is monotone decreasing for
n\geq 2
and
<2
for
n\geq 5
.
If
b=0
, then
b
is a square, so assume
b=\pm 1
. Here the elements
\alpha
with minimal norm ar

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\raisebox{0.0pt}[11.62494pt][5.59721pt]{\rule{0.0pt}{8.61108pt} }\alpha&n-1+\sqrt{n^{2}+2}&n+\sqrt{n^{2}+2}&n+1+\sqrt{n^{2}+2}\\
\hline\cr\raisebox{0.0pt}[11.62494pt][5.59721pt]{\rule{0.0pt}{8.61108pt} }N\alpha&-2n-1&-2&2n-1\end{array}

2.

\quad\sqrt{\nu\sqrt{\varepsilon}}/\varepsilon\leq|\,\xi\eta\,|<\sqrt{\nu/\sqrt{\varepsilon}}:

Multiplying
\xi\eta
with
\delta
we get

\sqrt{2\nu/\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\leq|\,\xi\eta\delta\,|<\sqrt{2\nu\sqrt{\varepsilon}}.

As in case 1. above, we find
|b|\leq 1
, where

\xi\eta\delta=a+b\sqrt{m}
; if
b=0
, then

2\nu=|N(\xi\eta\delta)|=a^{2}
is a square. If
b=\pm 1
, then

a^{2}-mb^{2}
must be even (because
|N\xi\delta|
is even),
or
|N\xi\delta|\geq 2n-1
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Third solution using quadratic number fields

We now translate the IMO problem into a problem about norms
of elements in quadratic number fields.

Assume first that
m=k^{2}-4
is even; then so is
k
, and we can write

k=2n
for some integer
n
. If
x^{2}-kxy+y^{2}=k
, then

x^{2}-2nxy+y^{2}=2n
can be written in the form

(x-ny)^{2}-(n^{2}-1)y^{2}=2n
. By Prop.
13
,
k=2n
is a
perfect square.

If
k
is odd, then multiplying
x^{2}-kxy+y^{2}=k
through
by
4
and completing the square shows that

(2x-ky)^{2}-(k^{2}-4)y^{2}=4k.

In this case,
\frac{1}{2}(2x-ky+y\sqrt{m}\,)
is an element with norm
k
,
and the claim follows from Prop.
14
.

The proof of the next result uses Prop.
15
and is left to the reader:

Proposition 16
.

If there are integers
x,y
such that
k=\frac{x^{2}+2y^{2}}{2xy+1}
is
an integer, then
k
is a square or twice a square.

With computer experiments it is easy to discover a wealth of
problems similar to the original IMO problem. Some of them can
be proved with the methods presented here, others seem to require
new ideas.

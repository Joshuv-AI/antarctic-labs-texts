Comparative Study of Chlorhexidine-Loaded Microneedle Patches vs Gels in Local Periodontal Therapy
Authors: Zakiah Khalid Barri, Abdulmalik Marwan Khateeb, Abdulaziz Awwadh Alqurayqiri, Abdulaziz Khalid Enayah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Periodontitis, a chronic inflammatory disease, requires effective local antimicrobial therapy. Chlorhexidine (CHX) is a gold-standard antimicrobial, but conventional gel delivery faces limitations in sustained release and pocket penetration.Materials and Methods:A randomized, controlled, single-blind clinical trial was conducted with 60 periodontitis patients (30 per group). Clinical parameters (probing depth - PD, clinical attachment level - CAL, bleeding on probing - BOP) and microbial counts (Porphyromonas gingivalis, Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans) were assessed at baseline, 1, 3, and 6 months.Results:PD reduction: Significantly greater in MN patch group at 3 months (2.8 ± 0.6 mm vs. 1.9 ± 0.5 mm, P < 0.001) and 6 months (3.1 ± 0.7 mm vs. 2.2 ± 0.6 mm, P < 0.001). CAL gain: Superior in the MN group at 6 months (2.5 ± 0.8 mm vs. 1.7 ± 0.7 mm, P = 0.002). Microbial reduction: Significantly higher for both pathogens in the MN group at all time points (P < 0.01). Patient discomfort: Lower with MN patches (15% vs. 45%, P = 0.008).Conclusion:CHX-loaded MN patches demonstrate superior clinical and antimicrobial efficacy with enhanced patient comfort compared to gels, representing a promising advancement in localized periodontal therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontitis, characterized by progressive destruction of the tooth-supporting apparatus, is a major global health concern affecting a significant portion of the adult population.[1] The primary etiological factor is dysbiotic microbial biofilms within periodontal pockets.[2] Effective management requires meticulous mechanical debridement (scaling and root planing - SRP) to remove biofilm and calculus. However, complete microbial eradication, especially in deep pockets and furcations, remains challenging due to anatomical complexities and bacterial recolonization.[3] Consequently, adjunctive local antimicrobial therapy is frequently employed to enhance treatment outcomes.[4]

Chlorhexidine digluconate (CHX) is widely regarded as the gold-standard antimicrobial agent in dentistry due to its broad-spectrum activity against periodontopathogens and substantivity.[5] Local delivery systems, particularly gels, have been developed to deliver high concentrations of CHX directly into the periodontal pocket, minimizing systemic side effects and overcoming limitations of systemic antibiotics.[6] While CHX gels offer advantages over systemic therapy, they often suffer from rapid clearance by gingival crevicular fluid (GCF), limited penetration into the pocket epithelium and connective tissue, and relatively short duration of action, necessitating frequent reapplication.[78]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Sixty systemically healthy adults aged 30-65 years with generalized chronic periodontitis (Stage II/Grade B) were enrolled from the Department of Periodontics. Eligible participants had at least 20 natural teeth, four or more non-adjacent sites with probing depth ≥5 mm and CAL ≥3 mm, and BOP at over 30% of sites. Exclusion criteria included systemic conditions affecting the periodontium, recent antibiotic or periodontal therapy, pregnancy, allergy to study materials, and heavy smoking. All participants underwent full-mouth scaling and root planing (SRP) by a single calibrated periodontist before randomization.

Participants were randomly assigned to one of two adjunctive local treatment groups. The MN patch group received a dissolving microneedle patch loaded with 2% chlorhexidine (CHX), applied to selected periodontal pockets at baseline and 3 months using a custom applicator. The gel group received 1% CHX gel applied into the same types of pockets using a blunt cannula, with applications repeated weekly for four weeks. Clinical assessments—probing depth, clinical attachment level, bleeding on probing, and plaque index—were performed by a blinded examiner at baseline, 1, 3, and 6 months using a UNC-15 probe.

Microbiological evaluation involved collecting subgingival plaque from four sites per patient, processed within two hours, and analyzed via qPCR to quantify Porphyromonas gingivalis and Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans counts in log10 CFU. Patient-reported discomfort was measured immediately after application using a 100 mm visual analog scale, while ease of use and treatment preference were evaluated at the 6-month follow-up. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS v26.0.

RESULTS

Both treatment groups showed significant improvements in periodontal parameters over 6 months; however, the microneedle (MN) patch group demonstrated superior outcomes. Notably, the MN patch group had significantly greater probing depth (PD) reduction at 3 and 6 months (P < 0.001) and greater clinical attachment level (CAL) gain at 6 months (P = 0.002). Bleeding on probing (BOP) and plaque index (PI) also improved significantly in both groups, with the MN patch group showing significantly lower BOP percentages at follow-ups [Table 1].

Microbiologically, both groups showed significant reductions in P. gingivalis and A. actinomycetemcomitans counts over time (P < 0.001), with the MN patch group achieving significantly greater reductions at all time points (P < 0.001), indicating enhanced antimicrobial efficacy of the patch formulation.

In terms of patient acceptability, the MN patch group reported significantly less discomfort during application (VAS 15 ± 8 mm vs. 35 ± 12 mm, P < 0.001) and fewer cases of moderate/severe discomfort (15% vs. 45%, P = 0.008). Ease of use was rated higher for MN patches (85 ± 10 mm vs. 70 ± 15 mm, P < 0.001), and a greater proportion of patients preferred the MN patch treatment at the study’s end (73% vs. 27%, P < 0.001).

DISCUSSION

This randomized controlled trial provides robust clinical evidence demonstrating the superiority of CHX-loaded dissolving microneedle patches over conventional CHX gels as an adjunct to SRP in the treatment of chronic periodontitis. The key findings reveal significantly greater improvements in clinical parameters (PD reduction, CAL gain, BOP reduction) and more substantial reductions in key periodontopathogens (P. gingivalis, A. actinomycetemcomitans) with the MN patch delivery system, coupled with enhanced patient comfort and preference.

The significantly greater PD reduction and CAL gain observed in the MN patch group at 3 and 6 months align with the hypothesized advantages of MN technology. The ability of the dissolving MNs to physically penetrate the pocket epithelium and deliver CHX directly into the underlying connective tissue likely overcomes the primary limitation of gels – rapid clearance by GCF and limited tissue penetration.[7] This sustained, localized release within the target tissue creates a more favorable environment for periodontal healing and regeneration by effectively suppressing the microbial load and reducing inflammation for a prolonged duration.[1] The results are consistent with ex vivo studies showing deeper and more sustained CHX penetration into gingival tissue using MNs compared to topical application.[2] The superior BOP reduction further supports enhanced anti-inflammatory effects, likely secondary to the more effective microbial control.

The significantly greater reduction in P. gingivalis and A. actinomycetemcomitans counts at all time points in the MN patch group is a critical finding. These pathogens are strongly associated with periodontal tissue destruction.[7] The conventional gel, applied weekly, likely achieves high initial concentrations but suffers from rapid dilution and clearance, allowing for quicker bacterial repopulation between applications.[8] In contrast, the MN patch, applied only twice (baseline and 3 months), appears to provide sustained antimicrobial levels sufficient to suppress these pathogens effectively over a longer period. This sustained release profile is a hallmark advantage of MN-based delivery systems for localized therapy.[1] The magnitude of microbial reduction observed with the MN patch in this study is comparable to or exceeds that reported for some other sustained-release local delivery devices like chips or fibers,[8910] but with the added benefits of simpler application and biocompatibility.

CONCLUSION

This comparative clinical study demonstrates that chlorhexidine-loaded dissolving microneedle patches are significantly more effective than conventional chlorhexidine gels as an adjunct to scaling and root planing in the treatment of chronic periodontitis.

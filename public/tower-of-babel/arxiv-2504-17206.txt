Quantum geometry and elliptic optical dichroism in p p -wave magnets
Motohiko Ezawa

Abstract
The quantum geometric tensor is composed of the Berry curvature and the quantum metric, which is observable by means of optical absorption of elliptically polarized light. Especially, the quantum geometric tensor at the zero-momentum is observable by the optical absorption at the optical band edge. In this context, we study optical absorption of a p p -wave magnet under irradiation of elliptically polarized light. The p p -wave magnet has a band splitting along one axis, which we choose the x x axis. We obtain analytic formulae for the optical conductivity up to the second order in the magnitude of the Néel vector. In particular, the optical conductivity is exactly obtained when the Néel is along the x x , y y and z z axis. It shows strong ellipticity a dependence of the light polarization, which is an elliptic dichroism. Especially, there is a perfect elliptic optical dichroism when the Néel vector is along the y y axis. It is possible to determine the Néel vector by measuring the ellipticity of the perfect elliptic dichroism.

IV
Quantum geometry and elliptic dichroism

The quantum geometric tensor is defined by
[
42
,
43
]

\mathcal{F}_{mn}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|1-\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle,

(10)

where

\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\equiv\sum_{n\in\text{Occupied}}\left|u_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|,

(11)

is the projection operator to the occupied band. In the two band model, its
diagonal component is simply given by

\mathcal{F}_{--}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\left|\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle\right.\left.\left\langle\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right|\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle.

(12)

The optical matrix element for the ellipcit polarized light is expanded as

\displaystyle\left|P_{\vartheta}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|P_{x}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+\left|P_{y}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta

\displaystyle+i\left[P_{x}^{\ast}(\mathbf{k})P_{y}(\mathbf{k})-P_{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{k})P_{x}(\mathbf{k})\right]\sin\theta\cos\theta.

(13)

By using the Hellmann-Feynman theorem

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|v_{\mu}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(E_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-E_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle,\quad

(14)

for
\quad m\neq n
, we have

\displaystyle\left|P_{\mu}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\left\langle\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\right|v_{\mu}\left|\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right|v_{\mu}\left|\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta^{2}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\left|\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle\right.\left.\left\langle\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta^{2}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)g_{\mu\mu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),

(15)

with
\mu=x,y
and

\displaystyle i\left[P_{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{k})P_{x}(\mathbf{k})-P_{x}^{\ast}(\mathbf{k})P_{y}(\mathbf{k})\right]

\displaystyle=i\Delta^{2}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)[\left\langle\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\right|v_{y}\left|\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right|v_{x}\left|\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\emph{\qquad\qquad}-\left\langle\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\right|v_{x}\left|\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{+}(\mathbf{k})\right|v_{y}\left|\psi_{-}(\mathbf{k})\right\rangle]

\displaystyle=i\Delta^{2}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left[\mathcal{F}_{-+}^{xy}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-\mathcal{F}_{+-}^{yx}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right]=\Delta^{2}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\Omega_{xy}\left(\mathbf{k}\right).

(16)

Then, the optical conductivity is rewritten by using the quantum geometric
information as

\sigma\left(\omega;\vartheta\right)=\hbar^{2}\sigma_{0}\int d\mathbf{k}f(\mathbf{k})G(\mathbf{k})\delta\left[E_{+}(\mathbf{k})-E_{-}(\mathbf{k})-\hbar\omega\right],

(17)

with

G(\mathbf{k};\vartheta)\equiv g_{xx}(\mathbf{k})\cos^{2}\vartheta+g_{yy}(\mathbf{k})\sin^{2}\vartheta+\Omega_{xy}(\mathbf{k})\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta.

(18)

VII
Discussions

The
p
-wave magnets have the zero net magnetization. It is expected to be
useful for antiferromagnetic spintronics
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
with high density and fast
switchable magnetic memories because the effect of the stray field is small
comparing with ferromagnets. In this context, it is important to
differentiate the up and down states of the Néel vector. It was shown
[
30
]
that the Néel vector is detectable by measuring the
linear and nonlinear conductivities. We discuss a merit of optical detection
of the Néel vector comparing conductivities. It is possible to observe
spatial dependent optical absorption by a single shot experiment, where it
is possible to observe the domain wall structure of the
p
-wave magnet. It
is interesting to observe a domain-wall motion of the
p
-wave magnet by
optical absorption.

By using the perfect elliptic dichroism condition (
36
), it is
possible to determine
J_{y}
if
\lambda
is known. Hence, it is possible
to differentiate the two states
J_{y}
and
-J_{y}
by optical measurement.
On the other hand, it is impossible to differentiate
J_{x}
and
-J_{x}
because the optical absorption is quadratic in
J_{x}
. This is also the
case for
J_{z}
. In addition, it is hard to determine the direction of the Néel vector only by elliptic dichroism because the results are similar as
shown in Figs.
3
,
4
and
5
. In particular, the optical conductivity takes the minimum value at
\vartheta=\vartheta_{0}
irrespective of the direction of the Néel
vector. Nevertheless, our results will be useful for future spintronics
memories based on the
p
-wave magnet because the direction of the Néel
vector is fixed by the shape anisotropy of the sample and there are only two
stable directions.

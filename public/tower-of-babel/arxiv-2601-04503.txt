On the average 2 2 -torsion in class groups and narrow class groups of cubic orders with prescribed shape
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. We study the distribution of 2 2 -torsion in class groups and narrow class groups of cubic fields and cubic orders subject to prescribed shape conditions. The shape of a cubic order in a number field is a natural geometric invariant that takes values in the modular surface ℍ / GL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \mathbb{H}/\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) . Fix a subset W W of the modular surface with positive hyperbolic measure whose boundary has measure 0 0 . Refining the methods of Bhargava and Varma, we prove that among cubic fields with shape in W W , the average size of the 2 2 -torsion subgroup of the class group is 5 / 4 5/4 for totally real fields and 3 / 2 3/2 for complex fields, while the average size of the 2 2 -torsion subgroup of the narrow class group for totally real cubic fields is 2 2 . Similar results are obtained for cubic orders satisfying prescribed local conditions at all primes.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and historical context

The arithmetic statistics of number fields have advanced significantly in recent
years, largely motivated by the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics, which predict the
distribution of
p
-parts of class groups of number fields of fixed degree,
ordered by discriminant. Till date the only cases for which these conjectures are proven is when
p=3
and
K
varies over quadratic number fields and when
p=2
and
K
varies over cubic number fields. Davenport and Heilbronn
[

show that as

varies over real (resp. imaginary) quadratic fields, the average size of

is
4/3
(resp.
2
). On the other hand, Bhargava
[
4
]
showed for totally real (resp. complex) cubic number fields

\operatorname{Cl}_{2}(K)
is
5/4
(resp.
3/2
). These results are driven in large part by the development of powerful parametrization techniques originating in the work of Delone–Faddeev
[
6

and their far-reaching generalizations due to Bhargava
[
3

Alongside these developments, there has been increasing interest in finer
geometric invariants of number fields. One such invariant is the
shape
of
a number field, defined using the Minkowski embedding of its ring of integers.
Equipping

with the trace form, the image of

becomes a lattice containing the vector corresponding to
1

Projecting orthogonally to this vector yields a rank-

equivalence class up to scaling and orthogonal transformations defines the
shape. The space of all such shapes may be identified with the double coset
space

\mathcal{S}_{n-1}=\operatorname{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{GO}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}),

and carries a natural measure induced from Haar measure. Distribution questions for shapes were initiated by Terr
[
8
]
, who proved that the
shapes of cubic fields are equidistributed in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. In this case,

\mathcal{S}_{2}
may be identified with the modular surface

\mathbb{H}/\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, where
\mathbb{H}
is the complex upper half plane. Equidistribution holds
with respect to the hyperbolic measure

. More recently, Bhargava and Harron
[
1
]

established analogous equidistribution results for

-number fields of

.

1.3.
Organization

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we recall the notion of the shape of a lattice and of a number field, with particular emphasis on the cubic case. We also review the parametrizations of cubic rings and
2
-torsion ideal classes that will be used throughout the paper. In Section 3 we carry out the necessary volume computations, adapting the framework in
[
1

We hope that these results further illustrate the robustness of arithmetic statistics under the imposition of natural geometric conditions, and provide additional evidence for the compatibility between algebraic and geometric aspects of number fields.

2.
Preliminary notions

2.1.
Shapes of lattices

The notion of the
shape
of a full rank lattice in Euclidean space is an invariant which captures its intrinsic geometry up to the natural symmetries. To formalize this notion, fix a real vector space
V
of dimension
r
with ordered basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
. Assume that
V
is equipped with the standard inner product defined by
\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
. The
space of shapes of rank-
r
lattices
is defined to be the double coset space:

\mathcal{S}_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{GO}_{r}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\operatorname{GO}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the group of invertible linear transformations preserving
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
up to a positive scalar multiple. Let
\Lambda\subset V
be a full rank lattice in
V
and let
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\}
be a chosen
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\Lambda
. We write
b_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{i,j}e_{j}
and let
M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(m_{i,j})\in\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the class of
M
in the quotient
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
depends only on
\Lambda
and not on the chosen basis. The
shape of
\Lambda
is defined to be the class of
M
in the double coset space
\mathcal{S}_{r}
and is denoted by
\operatorname{sh}(\Lambda)
. The space of shapes carries a natural measure
\mu_{\mathcal{S}_{r}}
induced from Haar measure on
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})
.

An equivalent description is obtained via Gram matrices. Let
\mathcal{P}_{r}
denote the space of positive definite symmetric
r\times r
real matrices. The group
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on
\mathcal{P}_{r}
by
M\cdot G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=M^{T}GM
for
G\in\mathcal{P}_{r}
and
M\in\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})
. On the other hand,
while
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
acts by
\lambda\cdot G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda^{2}G
. Writing

\mathcal{H}_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{P}_{r}/\mathbb{R}^{\times}=\{\,G\in M_{r}(\mathbb{R})\mid G=G^{T},\;G>0,\;\det G=1\,\},

there is a natural identification

\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{GO}_{r}(\mathbb{R})\;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;\mathcal{H}_{r},

mapping
M
to
|\det M|^{-2/r}M^{T}M
. Thus we have an isomorphism

\mathcal{S}_{r}\simeq\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathcal{H}_{r}.

Under this identification, the shape of a lattice
\Lambda
is represented by the class of its Gram matrix defined by
\operatorname{Gr}(\Lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle\right)
, and the relation

\operatorname{Gr}(\lambda A\cdot\Lambda)=\lambda^{2}A^{T}\operatorname{Gr}(\Lambda)A

shows that this class is independent of the choice of basis and of scaling.

The case
r=2
admits a particularly transparent description. Writing a general element of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
as
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
b&c\end{pmatrix}
where
a>0
and
ac-b^{2}=1
, one obtains an explicit
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-equivariant identification of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
with
\mathbb{H}
, the complex upper half-plane. Explicitly, the matrix above corresponds to the point
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{H}
with

x=\frac{b}{c},\qquad y=\sqrt{\frac{a}{c}-x^{2}}.

Under this correspondence, the action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{H}_{2}
becomes the usual fractional linear action on
\mathbb{H}
, and a fundamental domain is given by

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\{\,x+iy\in\mathbb{H}\mid x\in[0,1/2),\;y>0,\;x^{2}+y^{2}\geq 1\,\}.

The induced measure on
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is the hyperbolic measure
\frac{dx\,dy}{y^{2}}
coming from Haar measure on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
\{1,\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
be an integral basis and let
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
denote the set of embeddings of

into
\mathbb{C}
. The Minkowski embedding

\displaystyle j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{C}^{n}

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(\sigma_{1}(\alpha),\dots,\sigma_{n}(\alpha)\big)

identifies

with an
n
-dimensional real subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which we denote by
K_{\mathbb{R}}
. This real vector space is naturally endowed with the inner product induced by the trace form
\langle x,y\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(xy)
, and hence

acquires a well-defined Gram matrix. Since the vector

spans a distinguished one-dimensional subspace, it is customary to define the

to be the shape of the orthogonal projection of

onto the hyperplane perpendicular to

denote the image of the map
\alpha^{\perp}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=n\alpha-\operatorname{tr}(\alpha)

Gram matrix is given as follows:

\operatorname{Gr}\left(j\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\perp}\right)\right)=\big(\langle j(\alpha_{i}^{\perp}),j(\alpha_{j}^{\perp})\rangle\big).

For quadratic fields, this invariant is trivial, as all rank-
1
lattices are homothetic. In contrast, for cubic fields the associated projected lattice has rank
2
, and hence the shape of a cubic field is represented by a point in the fundamental domain

2.2.
Parameterization of order
2

Let
T
be a principal ideal domain. A ring of rank

T
-module

is isomorphic to the free module

We first recall the classical parametrization of cubic rings due to Delone and Faddeev
[
6

[
7

f(x,y)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}y+cxy^{2}+dy^{3}
be a binary cubic form with coefficients in
T
and let
\gamma\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(T)
. Then
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(T)
acts on the space of binary cubic forms via the twisted action:

(\gamma\cdot f)(x,y)=\det(\gamma)^{-1}f\big((x,y)\gamma\big).

Theorem 2.1

(Delone–Faddeev, Gan–Gross–Savin)
.

There is a canonical bijection between isomorphism classes of cubic rings
R
over
T
and

\operatorname{GL}_{2}(T)
-equivalence classes of binary cubic forms
f
with coefficients in
T
.
Under this correspondence, let
R(f)
be the cubic ring associated to a form
f
. The discriminant of
R(f)
coincides with the
discriminant of
f
. In particular,
R(f)
is nondegenerate if and only if
\operatorname{Disc}(f)\neq 0
.

Proof.

We provide a sketch of the proof for the benefit of exposition. To describe the correspondence explicitly, let
R
be a cubic ring. Choosing a
T
-basis
\langle 1,\omega,\theta\rangle
for
R
, and translating
\omega
and
\theta
by suitable integers, we may assume that
\omega\theta\in T
. Such a basis is called
normal
. With respect to a normal basis, multiplication in
R
is given by relations of the form

\omega\theta=n,\qquad\omega^{2}=m+b\omega-a\theta,\qquad\theta^{2}=\ell+d\omega-c\theta,

for integers
a,b,c,d,\ell,m,n
. Associativity of multiplication imposes constraints on
\ell,m,n
, and a direct calculation shows that these constants are uniquely determined by

n=-ad,\qquad m=-ac,\qquad\ell=-bd.

The coefficients
a,b,c,d
therefore determine the cubic ring
R
uniquely, and one associates to
R
the binary cubic form

f(x,y)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}y+cxy^{2}+dy^{3}.

Conversely, starting from a binary cubic form
f(x,y)=ax^{3}+bx^{2}y+cxy^{2}+dy^{3}
, the above relations define a cubic ring
R(f)
with underlying
T
-module
T\oplus T\omega\oplus T\theta
. The associativity relations ensure that this construction is well-defined and inverse to the assignment
R\mapsto f
. A direct computation shows that the discriminant of the cubic ring
R(f)
coincides with the discriminant of the associated binary cubic form,

\operatorname{Disc}(R(f))=\operatorname{Disc}(f)=b^{2}c^{2}-4ac^{3}-4b^{3}d-27a^{2}d^{2}+18abcd.

is said to be absolutely irreducible if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

A
and
B
do not possess a common
\mathbb{Q}
-rational zero as conics in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
; and

•

the binary cubic form
f(x,y)=\operatorname{Det}(Ax-By)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
.

We set
V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{(i)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V_{\mathbb{R}}^{(i)}\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Consider an element
(A,B)\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{(i)}
for which the associated binary cubic form is irreducible and let
(R,I,\delta)
be the associated triple. The if
i=0
or
2
, then
R
is an order in a totally real cubic field. On the other hand, if
i=1
then
R
is an order in a complex cubic field. If
i=0
(resp.
i=2
) then
\delta
is (resp. is not) a totally positive element of
R\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. We refer to
[
2
, Lemma 21]
and its proof for further details.

2.3.
Class groups and narrow class groups

Consider pairs
(R,I,\delta)
where
R
is an order in an
S_{3}
cubic number field. We note that such cubic rings make up
100\%
of all orders in cubic fields. A triple
(R,I,\delta)
is called
projective
if
I
is a projective

R
-module, equivalently an invertible fractional ideal; in this case

I^{2}=(\delta)
. For a fixed
S_{3}
-cubic order
R
, equivalence classes of
projective triples admit a natural composition law

(R,I,\delta)\circ(R,I^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})=(R,II^{\prime},\delta\delta^{\prime}),

which makes them into a group, denoted
H(R)
.

Let
U
be the unit group of
R
,
U^{\mathrm{pos\,norm}}
the subgroup of units
of positive norm, and
U^{2}
the subgroup of square units. There is an exact
sequence

1\longrightarrow\frac{U^{\mathrm{pos\,norm}}}{U^{2}}\longrightarrow H(R)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(R)\longrightarrow 1.

If
R
is an order in a totally real (resp. complex)
S_{3}
-cubic field then
|U^{\mathrm{pos\,norm}}/U^{2}|=4
(resp.
=2
). Thus we find that if
R
is an order in a totally real (resp. complex)
S_{3}
-cubic field then

|H(R)|=4\,|\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(R)|
(resp.
|H(R)|=2\,|\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(R)|
).

When
R
is totally real, let
H^{+}(R)\subset H(R)
be the subgroup of triples

(R,I,\delta)
with
\delta
totally positive. Then, one has the relation

|H^{+}(R)|=|\mathrm{Cl}_{2}^{+}(R)|

cf.
[
2
, Lemma 15]
.

3.
Volume computations

Let
W\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{2}
be a measurable subset whose boundary has measure zero. Given a subset
S
of
V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{(i)}
and a positive real number
X
, we denote by
N(S,X)
the number of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}
-orbits of irreducible elements
x\in S
such that
|\operatorname{Disc}(x)|<X
. Given an irreducible
x\in S
let
\operatorname{sh}(x)
be the shape of the associated cubic ring. Denote by
N_{W}(S,X)
the number of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}
-orbits of irreducible elements
x\in S
such that
|\operatorname{Disc}(x)|<X
and such that
\operatorname{sh}(x)\in W
.

Denote by
\operatorname{GL}_{n}^{\pm 1}
the subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
with determinant
+1
or
-1
and set
\mathbb{G}_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{GL}_{1}
. The space
V_{\mathbb{R}}
with its action by
G_{\mathbb{R}}
is also considered by Bhargava and Harron
[
1
]
. The key difference between our setting and theirs is that we use irreducible orbits in
V_{\mathbb{Z}}/G_{\mathbb{Z}}
to parameterize
2
-torsion ideal classes in cubic rings, while in
loc. cit.
they parameterize quartic rings. We are interested the distribution of the associated cubic field which is captured by the action of the
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
component of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
. On the other hand in
loc. cit.
the shape of the quartic field is studied, which is encoded by the action of the
\operatorname{GL}_{3}
component of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
. Following Bhargava and Harron, the action of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
on
V_{\mathbb{R}}
factors through that of

G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{G}_{m}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{GL}_{3}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{R})

via the homomorphism
G_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
which sends
(g_{3},g_{2})\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
to

\left(\left|\operatorname{det}g_{3}\right|^{2/3}\left|\operatorname{det}g_{2}\right|^{1/2},g_{3}^{\prime},g_{2}^{\prime}\right)

where
g_{i}^{\prime}
is given by
g_{i}=\left|\operatorname{det}g_{i}\right|^{1/i}g_{i}^{\prime}
. The kernel of the action of
G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
on
V_{\mathbb{R}}
consists of
4
elements. Fix a vector
v^{(i)}\in V_{\mathbb{R}}^{(i)}
for which

\operatorname{sh}(v^{(i)})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\text{ and }|\operatorname{Disc}(v^{(i)})|=1.

Note that for
\gamma=(\lambda,g_{3},g_{2})\in G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
, we have that

\operatorname{sh}(\gamma\cdot v^{(i)})=g_{2}\cdot\operatorname{sh}(v^{(i)})=g_{2}.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
be a fundamental domain for the left action of
G_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\prime}
on
G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
and set

\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}_{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{x\in\mathcal{F}v^{(i)}\mid|\operatorname{Disc}(x)|<X\right\},\\
&\mathcal{R}_{X,W}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{x\in\mathcal{F}v^{(i)}\mid|\operatorname{Disc}(x)|<X\text{ and }\operatorname{sh}(x)\in W\right\}.\end{split}

Letting
4n_{i}
be the cardinality of the stabilizer in
G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
of
v^{(i)}\in V_{\mathbb{R}}^{(i)}
, one has that

n_{0}=24,\qquad n_{1}=4,\qquad n_{2}=8.

Theorem 3.1
.

The the following assertions hold:

(1)

N\left(V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{(i)};X\right)=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{X}\right)+o(X)=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{1}\right)\cdot X+o(X)
,

(2)

N\left(V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{(i)};X,W\right)=\left(1/n_{i}\right)\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{X,W}\right)+o(X)=\left(1/n_{i}\right)\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{1,W}\right)\cdot X+o(X)
.

Proof.

The first assertion is
[
1
, Theorem 4]
. The proof of the second assertion is identical to that of Theorem 5 of
loc. cit.
and is therefore omitted.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For
n\in\{3,4,5\}
, we have

\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{1,W}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{1}\right)}=\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu\left(\mathcal{S}_{2}\right)}.

Proof.

The proof of this result is similar to that of
[
1
, Theorem 7]
, the only difference lies in the fact that the shape is determined by the
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
-action from the third coordinate of
G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
(and not the
\operatorname{GL}_{3}
-action from the second coordinate). It follows from Proposition 12 of
loc. cit.
that

\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{1,W}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{1}\right)}=\frac{\int_{g\in G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{1,W}}\left(g\cdot v^{(i)}\right)\left|\operatorname{Disc}\left(g\cdot v^{(i)}\right)\right|dg}{\int_{g\in G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}\left(g\cdot v^{(i)}\right)\left|\operatorname{Disc}\left(g\cdot v^{(i)}\right)\right|dg}.

If
g=\left(\lambda,g_{3},g_{2}\right)\in G_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}
, then the first two coordinates
\lambda
and
g_{3}
do not affect the shape. Arguing as in
loc. cit.
, we find that

\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{1,W}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left(\mathcal{R}_{1}\right)}=\frac{\int_{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{GO}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{R})}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{1,W}}\left(g\cdot v^{(i)}\right)dg\int_{k\in\mathrm{GO}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{R})}dk}{\int_{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{GO}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{R})}dg\int_{k\in\mathrm{GO}_{2}^{\pm 1}(\mathbb{R})}dk}=\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu\left(\mathcal{S}_{2}\right)}.

∎

4.
Congruence conditions

In this section we study local conditions at all primes
p
. We shall recall some results from
[
2
]
regarding the distribution of
2
-torsion ideals of cubic rings ordered according to discriminant. Let
S\subset V_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a
G_{\mathbb{Z}}
-invariant subset defined by
finitely many congruence conditions, encoding prescribed local arithmetic
constraints such as maximality, splitting, or ramification. For each prime
p
,
its closure
S_{p}\subset V_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is a compact open subset stable under
the action of
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
. To
S_{p}
one associates a local
p
-adic mass

M_{p}(S)=\sum_{x\in G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\backslash S_{p}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Disc}_{p}(x)\,|\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}(x)|},

which depends only on the local specification of
S
at
p
; the resulting Euler
product converges since
S
is defined by finitely many congruence conditions.

Let
N^{(i)}(S;X)
denote the number of
G_{\mathbb{Z}}
-equivalence classes of
absolutely irreducible elements in
S\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{(i)}
with

|\operatorname{Disc}(A,B)|<X
. Then
[
2
, Theorem 19]
asserts that:

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N^{(i)}(S;X)}{X}=\frac{1}{2n_{i}}\prod_{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\,M_{p}(S)\right).

Next consider sets defined by infinitely many congruence conditions. For each prime
p
, let
\Sigma_{p}
be a set of isomorphism
classes of nondegenerate cubic rings over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A collection

\Sigma=(\Sigma_{p})_{p}
is said to be
acceptable
if, for all sufficiently
large primes
p
, the set
\Sigma_{p}
contains every maximal cubic ring over

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
that is not totally ramified.

For a cubic order
R
over

\mathbb{Z}
, we write
R\in\Sigma
, or say that
R
is a
\Sigma
-order,
if
R\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}\in\Sigma_{p}
for all primes
p
. Fix such an acceptable collection
\Sigma
, and let
S=S(\Sigma)
denote the
subset of absolutely irreducible elements
(A,B)\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}
for which,
under Bhargava’s correspondence, the associated triple
(R,I,\delta)
satisfies

R\in\Sigma
. Then one has that

\begin{split}\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N^{(i)}(S(\Sigma);X)}{X}=&\frac{1}{2n_{i}}\cdot\prod_{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\cdot M_{p}(S(\Sigma))\right),\\
=&\frac{2}{n_{i}}\cdot\prod_{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\sum_{R\in\Sigma_{p}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Disc}_{p}(R)}\cdot\frac{1}{\lvert\operatorname{Aut}(R)\rvert}\right).\end{split}

cf.
[
2
, (12)]
. Denote the resulting Euler product by

c_{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\sum_{R\in\Sigma_{p}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Disc}_{p}(R)}\cdot\frac{1}{\lvert\operatorname{Aut}(R)\rvert}\right).

Lemma 4.2
.

The following assertions hold:

(1)

The number of totally real
\Sigma
-orders
\mathcal{O}
with

\lvert\operatorname{Disc}(\mathcal{O})\rvert<X
is

\frac{1}{12}\,c_{\Sigma}\,X+o(X).

(2)

The number of complex
\Sigma
-orders
\mathcal{O}
with

\lvert\operatorname{Disc}(\mathcal{O})\rvert<X
is

\frac{1}{4}\,c_{\Sigma}\,X+o(X).

Proof.

The above result is
[
2
, Lemma 23]
.
∎

Now let’s fix a measurable set
W
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
whose boundary has measure
0
and let
N^{(i)}_{W}(S;X)
be the number of
G_{\mathbb{Z}}
-equivalence classes of absolutely irreducible elements
(A,B)
in
S\cap V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{(i)}
for which
|\operatorname{Disc}(A,B)|<X
and
\operatorname{sh}(A,B)\in W
.

Lemma 4.3
.

When
S
is defined by finitely many local conditions,

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N^{(i)}_{W}(S;X)}{X}=\frac{1}{2n_{i}}\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu(\mathcal{S}_{2})}\prod_{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\,M_{p}(S)\right).

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N^{(i)}_{W}(S;X)}{N^{(i)}(S;X)}=\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu(\mathcal{S}_{2})}.

Let
\mu_{p}(S)
be the local density of
S
at
p
. It follows from
[
1
, Lemma 9]
that

\begin{split}\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N^{(i)}_{W}(S;X)}{N^{(i)}(S;X)}=\frac{\frac{1}{n_{i}}\prod_{p}\mu_{p}(S)\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{R}_{X,W})}{\frac{1}{n_{i}}\prod_{p}\mu_{p}(S)\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{R}_{X})}.\end{split}

The result follows from Theorem
3.2
, which implies that

\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{R}_{X,W})}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{R}_{X})}=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{R}_{1,W})}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{R}_{1})}=\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu(\mathcal{S}_{2})}.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be acceptable, then

\frac{N^{(i)}_{W}(S(\Sigma);X)}{X}=\frac{c_{\Sigma}}{2n_{i}}\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu(\mathcal{S}_{2})}.

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemma
4.3
in which finitely many local conditions are considered and a standard sieve argument (see
[
1
, section 5]
or the proof of
[
2
, (12)]
).
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

The following assertions hold:

(1)

The number of totally real cubic
\Sigma
-orders
\mathcal{O}
with shape in
W
and

\lvert\operatorname{Disc}(\mathcal{O})\rvert<X
is is asymptotic to

\frac{c_{\Sigma}}{12}\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu(\mathcal{S}_{2})}X.

(2)

The number of complex cubic
\Sigma
-orders
\mathcal{O}
with shape in
W
and

\lvert\operatorname{Disc}(\mathcal{O})\rvert<X
is asymptotic to

\frac{c_{\Sigma}}{4}\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu(\mathcal{S}_{2})}X.

Proof.

We prove part (1), the proof of (2) is similar. It follows the results in
[
1
, section 5]
that

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\left\{\mathcal{O}\mid\mathcal{O}\text{ is a totally real cubic }\Sigma\text{-order, }\operatorname{sh}(\mathcal{O})\in W\text{, }|\operatorname{Disc}(\mathcal{O})|<X\right\}}{\#\left\{\mathcal{O}\mid\mathcal{O}\text{ is a totally real cubic }\Sigma\text{-order, }|\operatorname{Disc}(\mathcal{O})|<X\right\}}=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{R}_{1,W})}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{R}_{1})}=\frac{\mu(W)}{\mu(\mathcal{S}_{2})}.

Part (1) of Lemma

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

For positive discriminant,

(4.1)

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
0<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<X\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}\bigl(\lvert\operatorname{Cl}_{2}(R)\rvert-\tfrac{1}{4}\lvert\mathcal{I}_{2}(R)\rvert\bigr)}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
0<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<X\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}1}=1.

Similarly, for negative discriminant,

(4.2)

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
-X<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<0\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}\bigl(\lvert\operatorname{Cl}_{2}(R)\rvert-\tfrac{1}{2}\lvert\mathcal{I}_{2}(R)\rvert\bigr)}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
-X<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<0\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}1}=1.

Finally, restricting to totally real rings, one has that:

(4.3)

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
0<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<X\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Cl}_{2}^{+}(R)\rvert}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
0<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<X\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}1}=1.

Proof.

We argue exactly as in
[
2
, (17)]
, replacing their global set

S(\Sigma)
by the present set defined by the shape condition

\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W
, and invoking Lemmas
4.4
and

4.5
in place of their counting theorems. Concretely, one has

N^{(0)}(S;X)+N^{(2)}(S;X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
0<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<X\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}\bigl(4|\operatorname{Cl}_{2}(R)|-|\mathcal{I}_{2}(R)|\bigr),

N^{(1)}(S;X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
-X<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<0\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}\bigl(2|\operatorname{Cl}_{2}(R)|-|\mathcal{I}_{2}(R)|\bigr),

and

N^{(0)}(S;X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
0<\operatorname{Disc}(R)<X\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}\bigl(|\operatorname{Cl}_{2}^{+}(R)|-|\mathcal{I}_{2}(R)|\bigr).

Dividing each identity by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\Sigma\\
\pm\operatorname{Disc}(R)<X\\
\operatorname{sh}(R)\in W\end{subarray}}1

). This completes the proof.
∎

Poster 220. Promising Bone Union Rates Following Arthroscopic Free Bone Block Double End-Button Fixation With Iliac Crest Autograft in Patients With Recurrent Shoulder Instability and Glenoid Bone Loss
Authors: Kazuha Kizaki, Soshi Uchida
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Arthroscopic free bone block (AFBB) fixation is an effective surgical option for patients with recurrent anterior shoulder instability associated with critical glenoid bone loss. Both allografts (e.g., distal tibial allograft) and autografts (e.g., iliac crest autograft, scapular spine autograft, distal clavicle autograft) have been used for AFBB. We developed a technique employing an iliac crest autograft with double end-button fixation, utilizing two end-buttons systems. This approach may provide higher union rates and reduced graft resorption, as the tricortical iliac crest autograft resists fracture while the end-buttons distribute contact forces across the graft. Additionally, the posterior end-button functions as a tension-band construct, where micromotion promotes compression between the graft and the anterior glenoid rim. Despite these theoretical advantages, bone block union rates using double end-button fixation have not been thoroughly evaluated. This study was designed to evaluated bone block union rates using double end-button fixation after AFBB fixation.Methods:We prospectively enrolled 25 consecutive patients who underwent AFBB with double end-button fixation using iliac crest autograft for recurrent shoulder instability between October 2023 and December 2024. Minimum follow-up was 6 months, including 10 overhead athletes. Preoperative glenoid bone loss was quantified via computed tomography (CT) using the diameter-based method with ImageJ software. Postoperative outcomes included ROWE scores at 3 and 6 months, and CT imaging at 3 months in 20 patients to assess bone block union. Overhead athletes were additionally evaluated for forward elevation and first external rotation at 1, 2, and 3 months postoperatively. Data are reported as mean ± standard deviation.Results:Preoperative glenoid bone loss averaged 20.5% ± 5.4% (range: 11.0%-32.4%). Glenoid surface area increased significantly from 623.3 ± 83.2 mm² at baseline to 801.8 ± 97.3 mm² at 3 months postoperatively. CT confirmed bone block union in all 20 evaluated patients (100%), with no evidence of graft migration. ROWE scores improved from 51.9 ± 20.7 preoperatively to 80.9 ± 11.2 at 3 months (p < 0.01), and further to 90.6 ± 5.0 at 6 months (p < 0.01 vs. 3 months). By 4 months, 24 patients (96%) had returned to sports. No recurrent instability, dislocations, or major complications (including neurovascular injury, lateral femoral cutaneous nerve damage, subscapularis weakness, or anterior shoulder pain) were observed. Among overhead athletes, forward elevation improved from 124° ± 23° at 1 month to 154° ± 15° at 2 months, and 173° ± 7° at 3 months. External rotation improved from 28° ± 9° at 1 month to 50° ± 6° at 2 months, and 58° ± 7° at 3 months. At 3 months, 4 of 10 overhead athletes successfully performed handstands with full range of motion (Figure).Conclusions:AFBB double end-button fixation with iliac crest autograft achieved complete bone block union at 3 months, significant functional recovery, and high return-to-sport rates. No recurrent instability was observed, even in overhead athletes performing handstands.

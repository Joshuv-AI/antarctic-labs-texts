Multi-parameter dataset of self-potential, electrical resistivity tomography, soil properties, and UAV LiDAR topography from the Fengjiaping loess–mudstone landslide, China
Authors: Gexue Bai, Tao Tao, Shuangshuang Li, Kaiyan Hu, Shuangling Mo, Yunlong Hou, Ruidong Li, Baofeng Wan, Bingbing Han, Fangjun Li, Ning An, Peng Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Fengjiaping landslide is located in the transition zone between the western Qinling Mountains and the southwestern Loess Plateau in China. Multi-parameter datasets were acquired from the Fengjiaping landslide, including hydrogeophysical, soil parameters, and topographic measurements. Self-potential (SP) data were acquired using 86 Pb–PbCl₂ non-polarizable electrodes at a sampling rate of 10 Hz over 20 min intervals. Electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) data were acquired along four survey lines using an ERT acquisition system. Soil parameters data were acquired using a portable soil sensor and include temperature, volumetric water content, and electrical conductivity measurements. Topographic data were acquired using a drone-based LiDAR system, and sensor positions were determined using real-time kinematic (RTK) positioning. The dataset is organized into raw and processed files together with information on acquisition parameters, measurement units, and data formats. The dataset can be reused for joint interpretation of SP and ERT data, hydrogeophysical analysis of landslide processes, development of machine-learning approaches for landslide investigation, and validation of coupled hydrological–geophysical inversion methods.Keywords: Landslide, Self-potential, Electrical resistivity tomography, Soil properties, Multi-parameter dataset

Background

Landslides are major geological hazards that can threaten human life and infrastructure [1,2]. Rainfall infiltration is an important factor affecting hydrological and subsurface conditions within landslide areas because it can alter soil moisture, groundwater conditions, and shear strength [3]. The combined use of multiple geophysical methods and the integration of geophysical and remote sensing approaches have been widely applied in landslide investigations because they provide complementary constraints on surface and subsurface processes [[4], [5], [6]]. For example, the joint application of spectral induced polarization imaging and electromagnetic methods improves the characterization of subsurface heterogeneity [4], the integration of time-lapse ERT with remote sensing observations enables the joint interpretation of subsurface hydrological changes and surface deformation during landslide evolution [5], and the combination of geophysical and remote sensing techniques provides a more comprehensive characterization of geological structures, hydrological conditions, and topographic features in large-volume and complex landslides [6]. Geophysical methods such as ERT and SP are widely used in landslide investigations and monitoring due to their sensitivity to variations in water content [[7], [8], [9], [10]]. Therefore, ERT and SP were applied to characterize subsurface hydrological conditions and identify water flow pathways in the reactivated loess–mudstone Fengjiaping landslide. To complement the geophysical measurements, additional datasets including soil temperature, volumetric water content, electrical conductivity, and topographic data were also acquired to characterize near-surface and subsurface conditions within the landslide area.

Field measurements were conducted using a sequential acquisition protocol to minimize interference between passive SP measurements and active ERT surveys. Soil temperature, volumetric water content, and bulk electrical conductivity were first measured at each observation location using a portable soil multi-parameter sensor. SP measurements were subsequently acquired before ERT surveys to avoid possible contamination from injected currents and electrode polarization effects associated with active electrical measurements. The associated research article focuses on data interpretation [3,11], while the present article provides a comprehensive description of the dataset structure, acquisition procedures, metadata, and file organization to facilitate data reuse for landslide investigations, comparative studies, and the development and validation of geophysical interpretation and inversion methods.

Data Description

The dataset includes SP, ERT, soil parameter, and UAV LiDAR topographic datasets collected from the Fengjiaping landslide in Gansu Province, China. The datasets are archived in the Zenodo repository and organized into folders according to data type [11].

The SP datasets are provided in text format (.dat) and include both raw measurements and corrected datasets after temperature and electrode-drift corrections. Each SP data file contains acquisition time, and electrical potential differences referenced to the corresponding reference electrode.

The ERT datasets include apparent resistivity measurements acquired along four survey lines, accompanied by electrode coordinate files. ERT1 represents the main downslope profile, whereas ERT2–ERT4 are transverse profiles across the landslide. Electrode coordinate files are provided in text format, with each row containing X, Y, and elevation coordinates in meters.

The soil parameter datasets include soil temperature (°C), volumetric water content (dimensionless), and bulk electrical conductivity (µS/cm) measurements acquired at SP observation locations. Measurement coordinates were determined using RTK positioning. The topographic dataset includes a digital elevation model (DEM) generated from UAV LiDAR data.

The Zenodo repository contains raw and processed datasets together with information describing acquisition parameters, file formats, coordinate systems, and measurement units (Table 1). Raw SP and ERT datasets are archived in compressed files, whereas DEM and soil parameter datasets are provided in Surfer grid (.grd) format. Electrode coordinate files for the ERT survey lines are provided in text format.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Study site

The Fengjiaping landslide is a reactivated loess–mudstone landslide located in Gansu Province, China (Fig. 1). The study area contains gullies, surface cracks, and deformation features related to landslide activity. Elevation ranges from approximately 1390 m to 1570 m, with an average slope of about 14 to 16°. The area is mainly covered by Holocene Quaternary silty clay deposits, underlain by moderately weathered mudstone and sandy mudstone of the Neogene system. Borehole records indicate artificial fill in shallow layers and heterogeneous soil texture in deeper layers [3].

UAV LiDAR acquisition

In November 2024, UAV LiDAR surveys were conducted in the study area using a DJI M300 RTK drone equipped with a Zenmuse L1 LiDAR sensor to acquire topographic data. The LiDAR data achieved an elevation accuracy of approximately 5 cm and were referenced to the CGCS2000 coordinate system [12]. Point-cloud data were processed in DJI Terra software to generate the DEM (Fig. 2a). SP, soil parameter, and ERT measurements were acquired during the same field campaign. SP acquisition

A total of 86 SP measurement points (black triangles in Fig. 2a) were distributed across the landslide area. The survey area was divided into five sub-regions, each containing one reference electrode and multiple measurement points. SP measurements were sequentially acquired at a sampling rate of 10 Hz for 20 min in each sub-region using a National Instruments data acquisition system (Fig. 2f). Differential electrode connections were used to reduce common-mode interference, and Pb–PbCl₂ non-polarizable electrodes were employed to ensure stable SP measurements (Fig. 2e) [13]. Electrode potential differences were tested in saturated saline solution prior to field acquisition, and only electrodes with potential differences below 0.1 mV were used for SP measurements.

Soil parameter acquisition

The upper part of the landslide area, which consists mainly of residential areas, trees, and woodlands, is relatively flat with little topographic variation. Reduced solar radiation in this area results in relatively low soil temperatures. In contrast, the middle part of the landslide has a steeper slope and receives stronger solar radiation, leading to increased heat absorption and relatively higher soil temperatures. To correct for electrode temperature drift, soil temperature, volumetric water content, and bulk electrical conductivity were measured at the SP observation locations before SP data acquisition using a portable multi-parameter soil sensor (Fig. 2d) (SN-3001-TRREC-N01, Shandong Renke Control Technology Co., Ltd.).

ERT acquisition

ERT measurements were acquired following SP acquisition. One main survey line (L1) was arranged downslope, whereas three additional survey lines (L2–L4) were deployed across the landslide perpendicular to L1 (Fig. 2a). Line L1 was 118 m long and consisted of 60 electrodes with a nominal electrode spacing of 2 m using a dipole–dipole array. Owing to the steep terrain, the spacing between several adjacent electrode pairs (E11–E12, E20–E21, E40–E41, and E51–E52) exceeded the nominal 2 m interval. The corresponding electrode coordinates are provided in Position_L1.dat. Lines L2–L4 were 19, 29, and 29 m long, respectively, and consisted of 20, 30, and 30 electrodes with a 1 m electrode spacing using the Wenner array. Data acquisition was conducted using an ERT acquisition system manufactured by Chongqing Jingfan Technology Co., Ltd. (Fig. 2g). Before acquisition, electrode contact resistance was checked to ensure values below 1 kΩ.

Quality control and preprocessing

A 1-min moving-window median filter was first applied to the raw SP time series to suppress ambient noise and remove the initial electrode drift [3]. Subsequently, voltage differences between the reference electrodes of the five sub-regions were used to calibrate all SP measurements to a common reference electrode. Temperature-induced voltage differences were further corrected according to ΔVT=α(Ti−Tref), where ΔVT is the temperature-induced voltage difference, α is the temperature coefficient of the Pb–PbCl2 non-polarizable electrode (0.2 mV/°C) [13], Ti is the soil temperature measured at the electrode depth at the i-th SP observation location before SP acquisition, and Tref is the soil temperature measured at the Zone 2 reference electrode [3]. The corrected SP dataset was obtained after applying the median filtering, reference-electrode calibration, and temperature correction. For ERT acquisition, electrode contact resistance was maintained below 1 kΩ before data acquisition. Electrode coordinates were verified using RTK positioning to ensure spatial consistency between survey lines and topographic data.

Soil parameter measurements were checked for abnormal values caused by incomplete probe insertion or unstable sensor contact. Repeated measurements were performed at selected locations to verify data consistency.

LiDAR point-cloud data were processed in DJI Terra software to remove vegetation and isolated noise points before DEM generation. All spatial datasets were referenced to the CGCS2000 coordinate system to ensure coordinate consistency between SP, ERT, soil parameter, and topographic datasets.

Information on file structures, coordinate systems, acquisition parameters, and measurement units is provided together with the datasets in the Zenodo repository. The complete workflow of data acquisition, preprocessing, metadata organization, and repository archiving is illustrated in Fig. 3.

Limitations

The dataset represents measurements acquired during a single field campaign and therefore does not capture seasonal or long-term temporal variations in hydrological and geophysical conditions. In addition, soil-property measurements represent near-surface point observations, whereas ERT and SP data provide spatially integrated geophysical responses. These scale differences should be considered during joint interpretation or model validation.

Prognostic value of anoikis- and epithelial–mesenchymal transition-related genes and development of a prognostic risk model in thyroid cancer
Authors: Kai-fang Xiang, Ling Wang, Yan Wu, Dong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Thyroid cancer (TC) is a common endocrine malignancy, and the processes of anoikis resistance and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) are crucial in tumor metastasis. In this study, transcriptomic data from TC patients were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), and differential gene expression was analyzed using the Limma package. A total of 193 anoikis- and EMT-related genes were identified, and their overlap with differentially expressed genes (DEGs) was examined. Using VennDiagram, five hub genes (CARD10, DAPK1, FN1, S100A4, and SALL4) were selected for further analysis. These genes were incorporated into a prognostic risk model, developed through univariate Cox, LASSO, and multivariate regression analyses. The model showed strong predictive power, with area under the curve (AUC) values over 0.7 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival predictions. Immune cell infiltration analysis revealed significant differences between risk groups, with correlations to the EMT score. Furthermore, the expression of the hub genes was validated in TC tissues and cell lines, showing that four of the hub genes (excluding SALL4) were significantly associated with overall survival. This prognostic model offers valuable insights into the tumor immune microenvironment and identifies potential therapeutic targets for TC.

Introduction

Thyroid cancer (TC) is the most common endocrine malignancy globally, and based on its origin, it can be classified into papillary TC (PTC), follicular TC (FTC), medullary TC (MTC), and anaplastic TC (ATC) [1]. TC often presents with symptoms such as neck swelling, difficulty swallowing, and coughing [2]. Over the past 20 years, its incidence has sharply increased [3]. In China, TC ranks second in incidence among cancer patients aged 15–44 [4]. Although most TC patients have a good prognosis after conventional surgical treatment, tumors tend to recur, with recurrence rates ranging from 25% to 35% [5]. Lymph node metastasis or distant metastasis of TC significantly increases patient mortality [6]. Molecular biomarkers provide essential information about the molecular characteristics of the disease, which is crucial for the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of TC [7]. Therefore, there is a need to identify more innovative and ideal therapeutic targets.

epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a fundamental process wherein epithelial cells lose cell polarity and cell-cell adhesion while gaining migratory and invasive properties [8]. It is considered a hallmark of metastatic disease, influencing the transition of early tumors to invasive malignancies [9]. During invasion, cancer cells undergo EMT, losing intercellular connections, generating filopodial protrusions, degrading and remodeling the extracellular matrix (ECM), acquiring invasive abilities, and traversing the basement membrane [10]. Poor prognosis in TC correlates with genetic alterations in EMT-associated genes and transcription factors [11]. Anoikis is a form of programmed cell death activated when cells detach from the ECM [12]. Anoikis during EMT suppresses tumor metastasis, serving as a crucial mechanism to prevent cells from growing in inappropriate or distant locations [13]. Anoikis resistance enables cancer cells to traverse the bloodstream and reach distant organs [14]. Anoikis resistance is a potential strategy for treating metastatic TC.

Recent advancements in molecular biology have led to the identification of specific genes implicated in anoikis and EMT regulation in TC [15, 16]. Understanding the intricate interplay between these genes holds great promise for developing novel prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic strategies. However, comprehensive investigations integrating multiple genes involved in these processes are scarce in the existing literature.

In this context, this study evaluated the prognostic significance of anoikis- and EMT-related genes in TC. Furthermore, we sought to develop a robust prognostic risk model integrating these gene signatures to predict patient outcomes with enhanced accuracy. Through elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying TC progression and metastasis, this research endeavored to pave the way for the development of personalized prognostic tools and targeted therapeutic interventions, ultimately improving clinical management and patient outcomes in TC.

Methods

Data resource

The clinical information and gene expression data of TC patients were searched from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). The anoikis-related genes were screened from the GeneCard database (https://www.genecards.org/), and EMT-related genes were acquired from the dbEMT database (http://dbemt.bioinfo-minzhao.org/).

Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in TC

DEGs between tumor and control samples in the TCGA cohort were identified using the R package Limma, with criteria of p-value < 0.05 and |fold change| ≥ 1.5. The results were visualized through a volcano plot generated using ggplot package.

Enrichment analyses of DEGs

Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were conducted using the R package clusterProfiler and org.Hs.eg.db. Their results were visualized using the Goplot package.

Identification of anoikis- and EMT-related genes in TC

By intersecting DEGs, anoikis-related genes, and EMT genes, anoikis- and EMT-related genes in TC were screened out. The analysis was performed using the R package VennDiagram.

Construction and assessment of prognostic risk model

To screen genes associated with TC prognosis, univariate Cox regression was first performed on the above-identified anoikis- and EMT-related genes. Genes with significant prognostic value (p < 0.05) were then subjected to least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis to reduce dimensionality and prevent overfitting. The LASSO analysis was performed using the glmnet package in R. Subsequently, the remaining genes were subjected to multivariate Cox regression analysis to identify hub prognostic genes and construct a prognostic model. The risk scores of each patient in TCGA were calculated based on the coefficients and expression levels of hub genes:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Risk\:score\:=\:\sum\:_{i=1}^{n}{coefficient}_{RNAi\:}\times\:\:{expression}_{RNAi}$$\end{document}

Then, based on the optimal truncation value of risk score, TCGA-TC patients were divided into low risk and high risk groups. Differences in overall survival between the two risk groups were visualized through Kaplan-Meier (K-M) curves. The predictions of the risk model were further assessed through a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve using the R package timeROC.

Nomogram construction

The differences in risk score among different clinical subtypes, such as age (≤ 40 years/>40 years), gender, pathological stages, and TNM stages, were analyzed. Subsequently, a nomogram was constructed based on these clinical parameters and risk scores, and calibration curves at 1-, 3-, and 5-year were used to determine the 45-degree dashed lines, which means the best prediction of the nomogram. The R packages rms, regplot, and survival were employed in these analyses.

Immune cell infiltration analysis

The infiltration of immune cells between low risk and high risk groups was determined using the Cibersort package. EMT scores of each patient in the TCGA database were obtained using the imogimap package, and the difference in EMT scores between low risk group and high risk group was compared. Additionally, the correlation among immune cells, risk score and EMT score was analyzed through the Pearson method.

Survival analysis and differential expression of hub genes

Expression levels of hub genes in the TCGA cohort were compared between the low risk and high risk groups. The overall survival of patients with different hub gene expression levels was analyzed using K-M curves. Immunohistochemical staining of hub genes were downloaded from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) (https://www.proteinatlas.org/).

miRNA-mRNA network construction

Firstly, miRNAs that modulate hub genes were predicted through TargetScan and miRDB, with criteria of context + + score percentile and target score over 80. The overlapping miRNAs between TargetScan and miRDB and hub genes were further used to construct a miRNA-mRNA network generated in the STRING database (http://stringdb.org). The network was visualized using Cytohubba in Cytoscape 3.7.0.

Cell lines and culture

Human normal thyroid cells Nthy-ori 3 − 1 (#CL-0817, Pricella Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China) and human TC cells IHH4 (#CL-0803, Pricella) were cultured in RPMI medium 1640 (containing 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin). All cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA extracted from cells was evaluated using the NanoDrop 8000 spectrophotometer (ThermoFisher, CA, USA). Under the instruction of manufacturer, cDNA was synthesized through the FastKing gDNA Dispelling RT SuperMix (TIANGEN, Beijing, China). The qRT-PCR was performed using a CFX96 Touch Real-Time PCR System (Bio-Rad, CA, USA). The qRT-PCR reaction was carried out using a One-Step SYBR Green Master Mix (ThermoFisher) with the following cycling conditions: 30 s at 95 °C, followed by 40 cycles of 3 s at 95 °C and 20 s at 60 °C. The 2−ΔΔCt method was applied to estimate the relative mRNA expression levels of genes normalized to GAPDH.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was employed to assess the expression of FN1 and S100A4 in TC tissues and adjacent non-tumor tissues. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections were cut into 4 μm slices and deparaffinized in xylene, followed by rehydration through a graded ethanol series. Antigen retrieval was performed using a citrate buffer at 95 °C for 20 min. After cooling to room temperature, the sections were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min to block endogenous peroxidase activity. The slides were then incubated with a blocking solution containing 5% normal serum for 30 min. Primary antibodies against FN1 and S100A4 were diluted according to the manufacturer’s instructions and applied to the sections, followed by incubation overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber. After washing with PBS, the sections were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 30 min at room temperature. The antigen-antibody complexes were visualized using a DAB chromogenic substrate kit, and counterstaining was performed with hematoxylin. The stained sections were then dehydrated, cleared, and mounted with a coverslip. The expression levels of FN1 and S100A4 were evaluated under a light microscope.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software version 4.1.2, SPSS 25, and GraphPad Prism 8.0. The K-M method was employed to compare the overall survival between two different risk groups. For quantitative data, the comparison between the two groups was evaluated through t-test. Correlation analysis was performed using the Pearson method. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of anoikis- and EMT-related genes in TC

The TCGA cohort collected 3234 up-regulated genes and 4245 down-regulated genes between the TC tumor tissues and the corresponding control samples (Fig. 1A). Functional enrichment analyses were further performed on these DEGs. The top 5 biological processes were system development, regulation of signaling, response to chemical, cellular developmental process, and regulation of response to stim (Fig. 1B). Cellular components were mainly associated with the extracellular region part, extracellular region, cytosol, vesicle, and protein-containing complex (Fig. 1C). Molecular functions were related to signaling receptor binding, enzyme binding, metal ion binding, cation binding, and catalytic activity (Fig. 1D). KEGG analysis revealed DEG-related pathways, including the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, focal adhesion, FoxO signaling pathway, cellular senescence, and MAPK signaling pathway (Fig. 1E).

Furthermore, 773 anoikis-related genes and 1184 EMT-related genes were acquired from the GeneCard and dbEMT, respectively. The Venn diagram screened out 193 anoikis- and EMT-related genes among DEGs, anoikis-related genes, and EMT-related genes (Fig. 1F).

Construction and evaluation of anoikis- and EMT-related prognostic model

To explore the role of 193 anoikis- and EMT-related genes in TC prognosis, Cox regression analyses were conducted. Firstly, univariate Cox regression analysis screened out 25 prognostic genes from 193 anoikis- and EMT-related genes, with p < 0.05 (Fig. 2A). LASSO regression analysis was then used to identify the features with high association among these 25 genes, and 11 genes were retained when λ was 0.0179 (Fig. 2B). Lastly, multivariate Cox regression analysis identified 5 hub genes (CARD10, DAPK1, FN1, S100A4, and SALL4) from the 11 genes. These five hub genes were related to the prognosis of TC and thus used to construct a prognostic model (Fig. 2C).

Additionally, the risk score of each TC patient in the TCGA cohort was determined through the formula of “risk score = -0.691*CARD10–0.166*DAPK1 + 0.001*FN1 + 0.001*S100A4 + 0.337*SALL4”. Subsequently, TC patients in the TCGA cohort were divided into 2 different risk groups according to the optimal truncation value of risk score. K-M curve revealed significantly different survival rates between low risk group and high risk group (p < 0.0001). As shown in the Fig. 2D, patients in the high risk group had poor overall survival. The ROC curve indicated a good prediction performance of the prognostic risk model, with AUCs at 1-, 3-, and 5-year of 0.74, 0.74, and 0.75, respectively (Fig. 2E).

Relevance of risk score with clinical features and development of nomogram

To examine the clinical efficiency of the risk model, we analyzed the risk score in clinical factors of TC and constructed a nomogram. The risk score showed no significant difference between different ages and genders (p > 0.05), while it was different between different pathological stages and TNM stages (p < 0.05, Fig. 3A). In the nomogram, patients’ clinical outcomes tended to be less favorable when their total score, obtained by summing the points assigned to all prognostic parameters, was higher (Fig. 3B). The calibration plot indicated that the nomogram’s results of the risk model closely resembled an ideal model (Fig. 3B).

Survival analysis of hub genes

Furthermore, survival analysis was performed on hub genes to explore the association of their expression with the survival time of TC patients. K-M curves demonstrated that patients with high levels of FN1 and S100A4, as well as low expression of CARD10 and DAPK1, had poor overall survival (p < 0.05, Fig. 6A-D). Expression of SALL4 had no significant effects on the survival rate of TC patients (p = 0.39, Fig. 6E). According to the gene expression profile in TCGA, mRNA expression levels of FN1, S100A4, and SALL4 were elevated in the high risk group compared with the low risk group (p < 0.0001, Fig. 6F). However, mRNA expression of CARD10 and DAPK1 was downregulated in high risk group (p < 0.05, Fig. 6F). The expression of these genes was further validated in cells. Levels of FN1 and S100A4 were increased while CARD10 expression was decreased in the TC cell line IHH4 cells compared to the normal cells Nthy-ori 3 − 1 (p < 0.05, Fig. 7A). Expression of DAPK1 and SALL4 was downregulated in IHH4 cells but showed no significance (Fig. 7A). Figure 7B showed the protein expression of these five hub genes from the HPA database. Positive expression of CARD10, DAPK1, FN1, and S100A4 was observed in both normal and tumor tissues, whereas no positive expression of SALL4 was detected in TC tumor tissues. Moreover, the expression levels of FN1 and S100A4 were significantly higher in the tumor group compared to the normal group. Consequently, we further validated the protein expression of these two genes through immunohistochemistry. The results demonstrated that the number of FN1- and S100A4-positive expressions was notably higher in tumor tissues than in normal tissues (Fig. 7C).

Discussion

TC is the most common malignant tumor in the endocrine system. Although most TC patients have a good prognosis, some patients are diagnosed with aggressive TC, such as ATC [18]. These patients are prone to recurrence and complications after surgery [19]. Therefore, it is necessary to better identify and evaluate the prognosis of TC patients. In this study, a risk model was established by combining anoikis and EMT-related genes to assess the prognosis of TC patients.

Under normal circumstances, when integrins on the cell surface detach from the ECM, anoikis is activated, with the cessation of cell survival signals associated with focal adhesion kinase (FAK), leading to uncontrolled apoptosis-related protein and ultimately cell death [20]. Cancer cells can overcome anoikis through various mechanisms, acquiring anoikis resistance, and eventually undergoing metastasis [10]. EMT is a crucial step in cancer cell invasion, during which cancer cells acquire stem cell-like characteristics and significantly enhance invasive traits [21–23]. Previous studies have explored the roles of anoikis-related genes or EMT-related genes in the prognosis of TC. For example, the anoikis-related gene CDKN2A can predict prognosis and mediate the proliferation and migration of TC [16]. Li et al. demonstrated that the downregulation of the EMT-related gene KIT is associated with decreased survival rates in patients with TC [24]. However, there is rarely having study combining anoikis and EMT-related genes to explore their impact on the prognosis of TC. In this background, our study identified 193 intersecting genes among DEGs, anoikis-related genes, and EMT-related genes. Through further univariate Cox, LASSO, and multivariate Cox regression analyses, five hub genes were ultimately identified, including CARD10, DAPK1, FN1, S100A4, and SALL4. The prognostic model constructed by these genes demonstrated good predictive performance. Compared with previous reports, our model demonstrated higher accuracy: the risk model constructed by Zhao et al. based on anoikis-related genes predicted the prognosis of TC with an AUC value below 0.7 [25]; the model established by Li et al. using EMT-related genes predicted TC prognosis with an AUC value below 0.65 [24]; and the EMT-related prognostic model developed by Liu et al. also showed AUC values below 0.7 for 3- and 5-year survival prediction in TC [15]. In contrast, our model achieved ROC curve AUC values greater than 0.74 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, suggesting that integrating anoikis- and EMT-related genes can improve risk prediction performance.

Furthermore, our results indicate that this risk model not only demonstrates good discriminatory power statistically but also holds potential value in clinical practice. Clinicopathological parameters are robust methods for predicting the mortality of TC patients [18, 19]. In our research, there were significant differences in risk scores among TC patients with different pathological stages and TNM stages. Therefore, we constructed a nomogram containing clinical parameters and risk scores. Each feature corresponds to a certain number of points. The results indicated that the higher the total points, the worse the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival of TC patients. These findings indicate that the risk model established in this study could assist clinicians in stratifying TC patients with different risk levels, thereby facilitating more individualized follow-up and treatment strategies. Compared with existing single-gene or clinical parameter models, our combined model provides more accurate survival predictions and may serve as a valuable supplementary tool for clinical decision-making.

The TME of TC is rich in immune cells, and certain immune features can affect the prognosis of TC [26]. To study the relationship between model risk scores and the TME, we conducted an immune cell infiltration analysis. In the high risk group, the infiltration levels of CD8+ T cells and NK cells decreased, while Tregs and DC cells increased. The infiltration of CD8+ T cells is associated with favorable prognostic features in DTC patients [27]. NK cells have cytotoxic activity and can kill certain tumor cells. Studies have found that ATC mediates NK depletion through the immune checkpoint gene PD-1 [28]. Increases in DC cells and Tregs have been observed in TC [29]. The presence of regulatory cells such as Tregs and DC is usually associated with poor clinical outcomes [30]. Our results indicate that decreased immune levels in the high risk group lead to tumor deterioration. Previous research suggests that tumor-associated immune cells initiate cancer cell EMT by secreting signaling molecules such as EGF, MMP, and SPARC [31]. Our results show that the EMT score in the high-risk group is significantly higher than that in the low risk group, consistent with the observed decrease in immune levels in the high-risk group. It indicated a deeper mesenchymal degree in the high risk group, making cells more prone to detachment and metastasis, thus increasing the risk of recurrence. Pearson correlation results revealed significant associations between CD8+ T cells and DC or Tregs, as well as DC and EMT scores. Based on this, we speculate that the decrease in CD8+ T cells in the high risk group may be influenced by DC and Tregs. In addition, DC cells may also promote the progression of EMT, thereby promoting the metastasis of TC.

To further investigate the impact of 5 hub genes on the prognosis of TC patients, we compared their expression levels between high and low risk groups according to the TCGA dataset. The expression of FN1 and S100A4 was significantly elevated in the high risk group, while CARD10, DAPK1, and SALL4 were significantly downregulated. FN1 is present in the ECM and participates in cell adhesion and migration processes [32]. Silencing FN1 reduces desmoglein-2 levels and increases sensitivity to anoikis [33]. Moreover, FN1 activates the FAK/ERK/PI3K pathway to promote proliferation, invasion, and EMT in TC cells [34]. Therefore, high FN1 expression not only correlates with poor prognosis in TC patients but may also drive ECM remodeling and downstream signaling cascades to accelerate tumor progression. S100A4 (also known as Mts1) is a prototypical metastasis-associated protein that enhances tumor cell migration and distant metastasis by reorganizing the actin cytoskeleton and promoting matrix degradation [35]. It can also suppress anoikis by regulating α5 and αv integrins [36], thereby supporting tumor cell survival after detachment. In TC patients, S100A4 is highly expressed and associated with aggressive pathological features [37]. Similarly, we confirmed that high expression of S100A4 predicts poor prognosis in TC patients. DAPK1 is a tumor suppressor, and downregulation of DAPK1 promotes stemness and EMT by regulating Ca2+ channels and TGF-β pathways [38, 39]. DAPK1 has also been shown to catalyze focal adhesion assembly and interact with rigidity-sensing modules to activate anoikis on soft substrates [40]. In TC, DAPK1 overexpression significantly inhibits cell invasion [41]. In our study, low levels of DAPK1 were associated with poor overall survival of TC patients. CARD10 (also known as CARMA3) is widely distributed in non-hematopoietic tissues and is upregulated in various cancers [42, 43]. It promotes EMT and cancer stemness in colorectal cancer via YAP-mediated NF-κB activation [44]. Moreover, CARD10 can form a complex with TMEM43 to regulate anoikis sensitivity through NF-κB signaling [45]. SALL4 is a transcription factor that links stemness to malignant phenotypes. It has been shown to trigger EMT through activation of the TGF-β/SMAD pathway [46] and to enhance anoikis resistance in breast cancer by regulating CD44 alternative splicing [47]. In lung cancer, inhibition of SALL4 reduces cancer cell proliferation and invasion [48]. However, the roles of CARD10 and SALL4 in TC have not been studied. Interestingly, our results indicate that high CARD10 expression correlates with better prognosis, while SALL4 expression showed no significant association with survival in TC patients, suggesting tissue-specific functional differences. These findings suggest that the five hub genes identified in our study may contribute to TC progression through mechanisms involving ECM remodeling, cytoskeletal regulation, apoptosis and signaling modulation, NF-κB–mediated anoikis resistance, and EMT. Our results not only highlight their potential prognostic value but also provide a theoretical basis for future mechanistic investigations and the development of targeted therapies.

Furthermore, we predicted miRNAs that regulate 5 hub genes to explore potential mechanisms in TC. MiRNAs are important factors in tumor metastasis by regulating the EMT pathway [11]. Among the predicted miRNAs, some have been proven to play significant roles in TC. For example, miR-497-5p and let-7a-5p regulate the PI3K/AKT pathway, miR-141-3P and miR-26 A-5P regulate the WNT signaling pathway, and miR-146-5p regulates the TGF β pathway. These miRNAs may modulate EMT and anoikis-related signaling pathways by regulating the expression of hub genes, ultimately affecting TC progression.

However, this study has several limitations. First, it primarily relies on publicly available data from the TCGA database, and the heterogeneity of the patient population may affect the generalizability of the model. Second, although partial experimental validation was conducted using the IHH4 cell line, the use of a single cell line cannot fully reflect the molecular characteristics of TC, and further validation in additional cell lines and in vivo models is warranted. Finally, future studies should integrate multi-center clinical cohorts and in vitro and in vivo experiments to further evaluate the reliability of the model and elucidate the underlying mechanisms of hub genes in TC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study elucidates the prognostic value of anoikis- and EMT-related genes in TC and establishes a robust prognostic risk model with potential clinical utility. These findings contribute to a better understanding of TC pathogenesis and may inform the development of personalized therapeutic strategies for improved patient outcomes.

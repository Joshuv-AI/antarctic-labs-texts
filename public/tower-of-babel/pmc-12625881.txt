Self-efficacy and influencing factors of returning to work in young and middle-aged patients with aortic dissection in China: a cross-sectional study using latent profile analysis
Authors: Yan Jiang, Yanchun Peng, Yaqin Chen, Wen Lu, Liangwan Chen, Yanjuan Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to assess the self-efficacy of returning to work among middle-aged and young patients with aortic dissection. Additionally, it seeks to identify distinct characteristics of these patients using latent profile analysis based on their return-to-work self-efficacy (RTW-SE) levels and to explore factors influencing the identified latent subgroups.DesignA cross-sectional study.SettingData were collected from a Class III Grade A hospital in Fujian Province, China.ParticipantsA total of 204 patients were recruited for this study.Outcome measuresData collection used a self-generated general information questionnaire, RTW-SE, Fear of Progression Questionnaire-Short Form and Social support rate scale.ResultsA total of 204 young and middle-aged patients with aortic dissection were classified into three categories based on their RTW-SE: high work coping–high internal coping group (9.8%), moderate overall level group (38.2%) and low work coping–low internal coping group (52.0%). Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified personal annual income, type of employment, education level and fear of disease progression as significant factors influencing RTW-SE in these patients (p<0.05).ConclusionA significant proportion of aortic dissection patients exhibit low self-efficacy in returning to work. These findings underscore the importance of emphasising vocational rehabilitation and integrating it into comprehensive cardiac rehabilitation programmes.

Introduction

Aortic dissection (AD) is a rapidly progressing and life-threatening aortic emergency. In recent years, the incidence of AD has been increasing in China, with a noticeable trend towards younger onset. Recent studies report an annual incidence rate of 5 to 10 cases per 100 000 people in China.1 The average age of patients is approximately 48.3±8.9 years, which is about 10 years younger than the average age of AD patients in Western countries.2 Consequently, a considerable number of AD patients are of working age. In China, the statutory retirement age is currently 60 for men and 50 for women and will gradually increase to 63 for men and 55 for women over the next 15 years.3 For these individuals, promoting return to work (RTW) is a crucial rehabilitation objective, as RTW serves as a key indicator of both disease recovery and the restoration of normal life activities.4

According to recent literature,5 the employment rate for cardiovascular disease patients after discharge ranges from 24.8% to 79%, with approximately 64% of AD patients returning to work.2 However, some patients do not RTW, and understanding the specific reasons for this is crucial. Given that AD is a life-threatening condition, its diagnosis can significantly alter life priorities and the perceived importance of work. Therefore, the predictive value of RTW self-efficacy (RTW-SE) may differ from other patient populations and is particularly relevant for AD patients in China.

China, as a developing country, lacks the comprehensive healthcare coverage systems of more developed nations. Additionally, the cost of surgical treatment for AD is high, with average hospitalisation expenses for Chinese patients reported to be approximately 170 600 CNY.6 This is significantly higher than for other cardiovascular emergencies and imposes a substantial financial burden on patients. Moreover, the onset age for acute AD (AAD) in China is lower than in many other countries, with most patients not having reached retirement age at diagnosis.2 Furthermore, cultural and societal norms in China often place men in primary financial roles within families, and studies have shown that men represent a higher proportion of AD cases.27 Identifying factors that influence RTW among Chinese AAD patients is therefore of great significance. A critical psychological factor in the RTW process is self-efficacy.8 RTW-SE refers to the belief in one’s capability to meet the demands necessary for returning to work. This measure has consistently been shown to predict actual RTW and is positively associated with work status and capacity among employees on sick leave due to mental health disorders.9 Similar results have been observed in cancer patients.10 Thus, the predictive value of RTW-SE has been established across various populations. However, its significance in AD patients has received limited attention. To the best of our knowledge, there have been no specific studies focusing on RTW-SE among AD patients, and the factors influencing RTW-SE in this group remain largely unexplored.

To date, research on RTW among AD patients has been limited. Lin et al found that less than 25% of patients with type A AD returned to work within 12 months following open triple stent-graft placement.7 Another study reported that approximately 64% of AD patients eventually resumed work.2 However, neither of these studies examined the self-efficacy related to RTW among these patients. As far as we know, no studies have specifically investigated the RTW self-efficacy and its influencing factors in AD patients.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This study conducted a cross-sectional descriptive analysis involving 204 patients with AD who were treated at the Fujian Provincial Heart Medical Center between December 2023 and June 2024. Participants were recruited through convenience sampling. The inclusion criteria were: (1) diagnosis of AD including both type A and type B ADs confirmed by CT angiography (CTA) or magnetic resonance angiography, treatment included conservative management and intervention/surgery; (2) age between 18 and 60 years at the time of diagnosis; (3) employment, either full-time or part-time, at the time of diagnosis; and (4) ability to communicate effectively and comprehend written material. The exclusion criteria were: (1) impaired consciousness, psychiatric disorders or inability to complete interviews independently or with researcher assistance; (2) participation in other related studies; (3) patients in terminal clinical stages or receiving palliative care; and (4) experienced major perioperative neurological complications, including disabling stroke or persistent neurological deficits. Strengthening the reporting of cross-sectional studies (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE)) was followed (online supplemental table 1). Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Data collection

Venous blood samples were collected within 24 hours of admission, with all samples obtained from patients in a fasting state (fasting period >8 hours). If multiple blood tests were performed within this timeframe, only the results from the first test were used. The laboratory analyses were conducted by the Testing Center at Fujian Medical University Union Hospital, and the data were retrieved from the hospital’s electronic medical record system. Baseline data collected included sociodemographic information, admission and discharge diagnoses, laboratory results, medications, surgical details and discharge status. Questionnaires were administered according to standardised protocols, emphasising informed consent, voluntary participation and non-harm. The questionnaires were distributed and collected on-site by researchers, with assistance from trained team members. For patients who faced difficulties completing the questionnaires, interviewers provided support. On completion of data collection, the data were promptly organised, entered by two individuals and verified for accuracy and completeness. A total of 204 questionnaires were issued and retrieved, resulting in a 100% valid response rate.

Research tools

Self-generated general information questionnaire

The scale was designed by the researchers and encompasses various aspects. It includes sociodemographic details such as age, gender, place of residence, educational background, marital status, monthly income and methods of paying for medical expenses. Disease-related information covers the type of dissection, laboratory indicators, length of hospital stay, comorbidities and any complications that may have occurred. Work-related information collected includes the type of occupation. In China, the main types of medical insurance are urban employee medical insurance and new rural cooperative medical insurance. Consequently, in this study, medical payment methods are categorised into insurance (covering both urban employee medical insurance and new rural cooperative medical insurance) and out-of-pocket expenses.

RTW-SE

The scale was developed by Dutch scholar Lagerveld et al,11 based on Bandura’s self-efficacy theory.12 It comprises 11 items: eight positively worded and three reverse-scored, all within a single dimension. To calculate the scale score, items 2, 6 and 9 are reverse-scored. The scores of all items are then summed and divided by 11 to obtain the average score, which reflects the level of RTW-SE. A higher score indicates greater self-efficacy. The Cronbach’s α coefficient for the scale ranges from 0.90 to 0.96, demonstrating good reliability. In 2019, Gao translated the questionnaire into Chinese, with a test-retest reliability of 0.83~0.93,13 indicating strong operational validity and accuracy (online supplemental table 2).

FoP-Q-SF

The Fear of Progression Questionnaire-Short Form (FoP-Q-SF) was developed by Mehnert in 2006,14 building on the original FoP-Q.15 This unidimensional, simplified scale comprises 12 items, yielding a total score that ranges from 12 to 60. A score of 34 or higher suggests the presence of psychological dysfunction, with higher scores indicating a greater level of fear regarding disease progression. The scale demonstrates strong internal consistency, with a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.88. The Cronbach’s α coefficients for the two extracted common factors are 0.83 and 0.81, respectively, which indicates a high correlation with the original FoP-Q (r=0.92). In China, Wu and colleagues translated and validated this scale, confirming its reliability and validity,16 making it a suitable psychological assessment tool for measuring the fear of disease progression (FoP) (online supplemental table 4).

SSRS

The Social Support Rating Scale (SSRS), developed by Xiao and colleagues in 1987 and revised in 1990,17 is designed to measure the level of social support an individual receives. The scale comprises 10 items across three dimensions: subjective support (four items), objective support (three items), and utilisation of social support (three items). Total scores for social support are categorised as follows: a score of ≤22 indicates low support, 23–44 indicates moderate support and 45–66 indicates high support. The Cronbach’s α coefficient for this scale is 0.813 among patients who have returned to work and 0.746 among those who have not, indicating good reliability18 (online supplemental material 3).

Statistical analysis

Data were analysed using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corp, Armonk), and measurement data were described as mean and SD or median and IQR. Count data were described by frequency and percentage. χ2 test or one-way analysis of variance was used for comparison between groups. Latent profile model analysis was performed on the 11 items of the RTW-SE using Mplus 8.3. The model fitting indicators included: (1) Information indicators (Akaike information criterion (AIC), Bayesian information criterion (BIC) and adjusted Bayesian information criterion (aBIC), the smaller the value, the better the fitting effect); (2) classification index (entropy value range was 0–1, entropy >0.80 indicates that the classification accuracy is more than 90%, and the closer to 1, the more accurate the classification is); and (3) likelihood ratio test indexes (likelihood ratio test indexes included corrected likelihood ratio test (Lo-Mendell‐Rubin Test (LMRT)) and Bootstrap-based Likelihood Ratio Test (BLRT), indicating that when p <0.05, the K-class model outperforms the k-1 class model, requiring a sample size of ≥5% for each profile).19 The above evaluation measures are only indicative, and the interpretability of the categories should also be considered when determining the best model. After determining the different categories of RTW-SE in patients with AD by LPA analysis, the variables with statistically significant differences between the RTW-SE subgroups were first analysed by χ2 test or one-way analysis of variance (p<0.05). Second, the above variables were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis as independent variables, and the test level was α=0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics of subjects

We identified 204 patients aged between 18 and 60 years, with a mean age of 51.84±8.99 years among the surviving patients. In this cohort, 37 patients (18.1%) were female, 194 patients (95.1%) were married, 26 patients (12.7%) had received a college education and 96 patients (47.1%) resided in urban areas. Hypertension was the most common comorbidity, affecting 130 patients (63.7%). The median length of hospital stay was 12.00 days (IQR 8.00~19.00), and the median ICU stay was 1.63 days (IQR 0.67~3.77). Among these patients, the most common in-hospital complication was pneumonia, occurring in 26 cases (12.7%). These results suggest that patients in the high coping group tended to have less severe clinical presentations, allowing for conservative management and contributing to their more positive coping attitudes. Exploratory latent profile analysis

This study used the 11 items of the RTW-SE as exogenous indicators to fit latent profile models ranging from one to five classes, as detailed in table 6. The AIC, BIC and aBIC values progressively decreased with an increasing number of classes until the LMRT indicator for model 4 was no longer statistically significant (p>0.05). This indicates that the fit of the model improved as the number of classes increased from models 1 to 3. Models 2 through 5 had entropy values greater than 0.8, suggesting classification accuracy above 90% in all cases. Model 3 had the highest entropy value, indicating the highest classification accuracy among the models. Additionally, the LMRT and BLRT P values for model 3 were both less than 0.05, supporting its superiority over other models. Based on these findings, model 3 demonstrated optimal fit evaluation indicators and adequate sample sizes for each group, with the probabilities for the three latent classes being 0.520, 0.382 and 0.098, respectively, signifying reliable classification results. These findings are summarised in table 6. Considering the interpretability of each class and the practical implications, model 3 was selected as the best-fitting model, as shown in figure 2. In the three-class model, class 1, consisting of 106 patients (52.0%), had the lowest scores across all items and was categorised as the ‘low work coping–low intrinsic coping group’, with a mean score of 1.69±0.32, denoted as C1. Class 2, comprising 78 patients (38.2%), demonstrated moderate scores across all items and was referred to as the ‘extensive intermediate level group’, with a mean score of 2.74±0.32, denoted as C2. Class 3 included 20 patients (9.8%) who scored the highest on all scale items, indicating high levels of work capacity, focus, physical endurance and strong intrinsic emotional and energy management, along with effective problem-solving abilities. This group was labelled the ‘high work coping–high intrinsic coping group’, with a mean score of 3.99±0.58, denoted as C3. These results are illustrated in figure 2.

Characteristics of latent profile membership

Univariate analysis indicated statistically significant differences across various factors, including employment type, personal annual income, place of residence, level of education (whether or not the individual had a university degree) and duration of ICU stay (p <0.05). Notably, the total RTW-SE scores for nurses in the C1 group were significantly below the overall average.

The C1 is primarily characterised by the following features: lack of higher education, engagement in predominantly manual labour, personal annual income below 30 000 RMB (approximately 4330 USD) and a higher tendency to develop complications during hospitalisation. Patients in the C2 did not show distinct characteristics based on general demographic information. Their RTW-SE levels fell between those of the C1 and C3 groups. Implementing feasible vocational rehabilitation strategies could potentially help individuals in this group transition to the C3 profile. The C3 scored the highest on the RTW-SE scale. Members of this group primarily engaged in cognitive or intellectual work, had received higher education and mostly lived in urban areas. They experienced relatively mild conditions and had a lower incidence of complications during hospitalisation. Additionally, compared with the other groups, the C3 group had significantly higher scores on social support and significantly lower scores on FoP. Detailed data can be found in table 1 and table 2.

Predictors of latent profile membership

Using the C1 as a reference, we conducted a multivariate logistic regression analysis to assess the impact of various factors on RTW-SE across the three identified latent profile groups. The findings are detailed in table 7. Independent variables included higher education, type of employment, personal annual income, place of residence, ICU stay duration and FoP. The results indicated that differences in urban residency and length of ICU stay among the three groups were not statistically significant. However, regarding educational attainment, individuals in the C2 group were more likely to have received higher education compared with those in the C1 group. Concerning employment type, patients in the C2 group were more inclined towards intellectual work rather than manual labour, as seen in the C1 group. For personal annual income, the likelihood of earning between 30 000 to 70 000 RMB (approximately 4330–10 105 USD) was higher in the C2 group compared with the C3 group. Regarding FoP, the C2 group demonstrated lower FoP levels than the C1 group, but these levels were still higher when compared with the C3 group. These results suggest that higher education and intellectual work are associated with moderate RTW-SE levels, while income and FoP also play significant roles in distinguishing between the groups.

Discussion

This study used latent profile analysis to identify three distinct RTW-SE profiles among middle-aged and young patients with AD: ‘C1 low work coping–low intrinsic coping group’, ‘C2 extensive intermediate level group’ and ‘C3 high work coping–high intrinsic coping Group’. The results reveal significant individual differences in RTW-SE within this patient population. The C1 group represented the largest proportion (52.0%), followed by the C3 group (38.2%), with the C2 group having the smallest proportion (9.8%). This distribution indicates that RTW-SE among middle-aged and young patients with AD is generally at a moderate to low level, which is consistent with Hu et al’s findings,20 although it is lower. The observed difference can be explained by the distinct characteristics of the patient populations in these studies. Hu et al’s research20 focused on middle-aged and young coronary heart disease patients who had undergone ‘percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) treatment. PCI effectively alleviates symptoms and facilitates RTW, thereby enhancing RTW-SE. In contrast, AD is one of the most severe acute cardiovascular conditions, often accompanied by intense pain and profound fear, which exceeds that experienced in coronary heart disease. Additionally, AD is associated with a high incidence of postoperative complications. Patients frequently worry about their ability to resume daily activities and fear they may not be able to RTW after discharge. These concerns contribute to a substantial decline in RTW-SE due to varying postoperative symptoms. Consequently, RTW-SE is lower in patients with AD compared with those with coronary heart disease. The challenge of ensuring a successful RTW for middle-aged and young patients with AD is significant and requires urgent attention. However, there are currently no systematic interventions specifically targeting RTW-SE in this population. Presently, RTW interventions fall into two primary models. The first is the rehabilitation-focused model, which is typically part of inpatient rehabilitation services. This approach aims to improve both physiological and psychological conditions to facilitate RTW. Examples of this model include assessments and training for daily living activities, functional exercises, cognitive rehabilitation, post-discharge health education, home environment improvements and coping strategies.21 The second model focuses on enhancing work capability through personalised occupational interventions tailored to individual RTW goals. One researcher developed a five-stage RTW intervention programme based on behaviour change theory,22 which includes work demand assessment, rebuilding RTW awareness, core work skills training, specific job skills training and discharge education. This programme emphasises core and specific work skills training and integrates RTW content into routine hospital rehabilitation care. However, this programme has not yet been empirically validated, and its effectiveness and feasibility require further investigation. Currently, no related interventions have been established domestically. There is an urgent need to develop a RTW service system tailored to the context of China, combining appropriate theoretical frameworks and addressing influencing factors. This system should involve collaboration among families, hospitals, employers and society to support patients in returning to work and incorporate RTW content into inpatient services. Such measures would enhance RTW-SE and improve RTW rates among middle-aged and young patients with AD.

Patients with AD in group C1 had significantly lower overall scores across all RTW-SE items compared with the other groups, indicating a general lack of confidence in their recovery and feelings of being overwhelmed by the prospect of returning to work, as shown in table 5. The main characteristics of the C1 group, which is classified as the ‘low work-coping and low internal-coping group’, include not having a college education, primarily engaging in manual labour, having an annual income below 30 000 RMB (approximately 4330 USD), a higher likelihood of experiencing complications during hospitalisation and longer ICU stays. These results suggest that patients in this group are significantly influenced by factors such as disease severity, education level and job type. This may be because AD, the condition studied, has an acute onset and causes severe pain, while the high medical costs impose financial burdens and substantial psychological stress on patients, undermining their confidence in recovery. Additionally, patients with lower educational levels are often involved in manual labour, have unstable incomes and are unable to return to physically demanding work after discharge due to incomplete recovery. Consequently, their income becomes insecure, making it difficult to maintain a positive attitude towards their condition. These factors hinder their ability to overcome challenges and barriers in the process of preparing to RTW, leading to further decreases in RTW-SE and obstructing their reintegration into the workplace. Research indicates a positive correlation between self-efficacy levels and patients’ readiness to RTW, suggesting that higher self-efficacy is associated with a stronger desire to resume work.23 Therefore, healthcare providers should pay particular attention to the needs of patients with lower educational backgrounds and those engaged in manual labour. Developing multidisciplinary and coordinated cardiac rehabilitation programmes to enhance patients’ RTW-SE, as well as encouraging relevant stakeholders (eg, employers, social workers, rehabilitation nurses and physical therapists) to create personalised RTW plans, may significantly improve patients’ vocational rehabilitation outcomes.

Group C2 patients showed no distinct demographic characteristics, with their RTW-SE scores falling in the middle range. This group accounted for a relatively large portion of the study population. Unique aspects of this group include the highest proportion of married individuals and moderate income levels. The sense of responsibility associated with being married may serve as a motivating factor for rehabilitation, improving adherence to recovery protocols and boosting confidence in regaining work capabilities. These patients typically maintain a positive internal emotional state when considering a RTW. However, due to their medical conditions, they often feel a reduction in energy levels compared with their pre-illness state, resulting in moderate self-efficacy. With appropriate guidance and strategic interventions, these patients could potentially transition into group C3, enhancing their prospects for returning to work.

Group C3 patients had the highest total RTW-SE scores and demonstrated the highest social support and lowest FoP among the three groups. Key characteristics of this group include involvement in intellectual work, having a university education, earning a personal annual income above 70 000 RMB (approximately 10 105 USD) and shorter ICU stays. Typically, these patients are well-educated with high incomes, have higher expectations for their recovery and are better able to articulate their needs for medical care and subsequent rehabilitation. They are less likely to be influenced by unscientific cultural beliefs, such as the misconception that intensive physical labour and long working hours are impossible after major surgery, which aligns with the findings of Lin et al.7 Additionally, their jobs generally require less physical exertion, and they have more flexibility when choosing or changing jobs, leading to higher levels of RTW-SE. However, this group often works under conditions of high stress and long periods of sitting, which may increase the risk of cardiovascular disease. Therefore, healthcare providers should develop multidisciplinary, personalised vocational rehabilitation programmes that account for the clinical and behavioural health characteristics and needs of this population, rather than focusing only on traditional cardiovascular risk factors.

This study’s results show that FoP is a significant predictor of RTW-SE among young and middle-aged AD patients. A negative correlation exists between FoP and RTW-SE, indicating that patients with lower levels of fear are more likely to belong to group C3. This suggests that lower fear levels correlate with higher RTW-SE. Although young and middle-aged patients often express a strong desire to RTW, negative emotions related to the fear of disease recurrence can impede their reintegration into society. Correlation analysis revealed a negative relationship between FoP, patients’ physical conditions and RTW-SE. Multivariate regression analysis confirmed that FoP is a key factor affecting RTW-SE, which is consistent with findings from Zhang et al.24 Research by Treffalls et al25 found that approximately one-third of AD patients are readmitted within 90 days post-treatment, with long-term postoperative complications remaining high. These complications, such as stent displacement or fracture, recurrent dissection, aneurysm formation, rupture and endoleaks,26 27 severely impact postoperative recovery and quality of life for AD patients.28 Therefore, even after successfully managing the primary disease and being discharged, patients often continue to experience fear of recurrence and related psychological issues. According to Mehnert et al,29 patients with higher levels of FoP are more likely to adopt negative coping strategies, such as avoidance and resignation, which can further hinder their RTW. This highlights the need to address patients’ negative emotions or adverse experiences during the RTW process. Targeted interventions that employ positive coping strategies, such as cognitive-behavioural therapy, relaxation techniques, cognitive restructuring and fear-response training, can help patients overcome psychological barriers, enhance their confidence in returning to work and facilitate an earlier return to their professional roles.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. The sample was drawn from a single province using a convenience sampling method, which may limit representativeness and generalisability. In addition, the unique social system and health insurance structure in China, including disparities between urban and rural areas, may further restrict the applicability of our findings to other settings. Data were based on patient self-reports without independent verification, which could introduce bias. Data on tracheostomy and deep sternal wound infection were not available, which may limit the completeness of in-hospital complication data. Finally, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference. Future studies with larger, more diverse populations and longitudinal designs are warranted to validate and extend these findings.

Conclusion

The RTW-SE levels among young and middle-aged patients with AD require significant improvement and are influenced by factors such as educational background, job type and FoP. It is recommended that primary healthcare institutions and rehabilitation services place greater emphasis on enhancing RTW-SE for these patients and actively address their needs during the RTW process. A comprehensive and systematic approach involving patients’ families, colleagues, employers, society and healthcare professionals is crucial. Developing a multidimensional intervention strategy to support patients in achieving a healthy and high-quality RTW is of great significance.

A Modified Dai-Liao Spectral Conjugate Gradient Method with an Application to Signal Processing
D. R. Sahu, Shikher Sharma, Pankaj Gautam, Simeon Reich

Abstract
We propose and study a variant of the Dai-Liao spectral conjugate gradient method, developed through an analysis of eigenvalues and inspired by a modified secant condition. We show that our proposed method is globally convergent for general nonlinear functions under standard assumptions. By incorporating the new secant condition and a quasi-Newton direction, we introduce updated spectral parameters. These changes ensure that the resulting search direction satisfies the sufficient descent property without relying on any line search. Numerical experiments show that the proposed algorithm performs better than several existing methods in terms of convergence speed and computational efficiency. Its effectiveness is further demonstrated through an application to signal processing.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the Euclidean space equipped with the usual inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and its associated norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
. Our goal is to solve the following minimization problem:

\text{find }x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\text{ such that }f(x^{*})=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}f(x).

(1)

We denote the set of minimizers of
f
by
\Omega_{\text{min}}(f,\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

A common approach to find a solution to (
1
) is the following iterative method: Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an initial point. Given the current iterate
x_{n}
, the next iterate
x_{n+1}
is computed by

x_{n+1}=x_{n}+\alpha_{n}d_{n}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},

(2)

where
\alpha_{n}>0
is the stepsize determined by exact or inexact line search and
d_{n}
is a suitable search direction. Methods that follow the update rule (
2
) are referred to as line search methods. These methods require only the search direction
d_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the stepsize
\alpha_{n}\in(0,\infty)
.
An appropriate descent direction
d_{n}
must be chosen so that it satisfies the descent condition:

\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle<0\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3)

A common choice for a descent direction is
d_{n}=-\nabla f(x_{n})
, which simplifies the general line search method (
2
) to the familiar gradient descent (GD) method:

x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\alpha_{n}\nabla f(x_{n})\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

If there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle\leq-c\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{2}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

then the vector
d_{n}
is said to satisfy the sufficient descent condition.

Conjugate gradient (CG) methods are among the most effective algorithms for solving large-scale unconstrained optimization problems due to their low storage requirements. These methods require only first-order derivatives, makes them highly suitable for large-scale applications.
The conjugate gradient (CG) method was originally proposed by Hestenes and Stiefel
[
25
]
for the solution of linear systems. Later, Fletcher and Reeves
[
21
]
extended this concept and formulated the first nonlinear CG method for unconstrained optimization. Due to their strong theoretical foundation and excellent numerical performance, many other versions of the CG method have been proposed (see
[
31
,
17
,
22
,
16
]
and the references therein).

In general, CG methods use the iterative scheme
(
2
), where the search direction
d_{n}
is defined by

d_{n}=\begin{cases}-\nabla f(x_{0}),&\text{ if }n=0,\\
-\nabla f(x_{n})+\beta_{n-1}d_{n-1},&\text{ if }n\geq 1,\end{cases}

(4)

where
\beta_{n}
is the CG parameter. There are many formulas for
\beta_{n}
, each resulting in different computational behavior
[
23
]
.

One of the most well-known CG methods was proposed by Dai and Liao
[
17
]
. Their approach is based on an extended conjugacy condition, where the CG parameter is defined by

\beta_{n}^{DL}=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),y_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}-t\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(5)

where
t\in(0,\infty)
,
s_{n}=x_{n+1}-x_{n}
, and
y_{n}=\nabla f(x_{n+1})-\nabla f(x_{n})
. Note that when
t=0
,
\beta_{n}^{DL}
simplifies to the CG parameter proposed by Hestenes and Stiefel
[
25
]
:

\beta_{n}^{HS}=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),y_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The CG parameter proposed by Hager and Zhang
[
24
]
is defined by

\beta_{n}^{HZ}=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),y_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}-2\frac{\|y_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The parameter
\beta_{n}^{HZ}
can be viewed as an adaptive version of (
5
) for the specific choice
t=2\frac{\|y_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}
.
Similarly, the CG parameter proposed by Dai and Kou
[
18
]
is defined by

\beta_{n}^{DK}(\tau_{n})=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),y_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}-\left(\tau_{n}+\frac{\|y_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}-\frac{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}\right)\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle},

where
\tau_{n}

is a parameter that plays the role of a scaling factor in the scaled memoryless Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno (BFGS) method, which can be viewed as another adaptive variant of

Motivated by Shanno’s matrix interpretation of CG methods
[
33
]
, Babaie-Kafaki and Ghanbari
[
6
]
showed that the Dai–Liao search directions can be written in the form
d_{n+1}=-P_{n+1}\nabla f(x_{n+1})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where
P_{n+1}=I-\frac{s_{n}y_{n}^{T}}{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}+t\frac{s_{n}s_{n}^{T}}{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}

is a nonsingular matrix for
t\in(0,\infty)
and
\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle\neq 0
; see also
[
7
]
. By performing an eigenvalue analysis on the symmetrized version of
P_{n+1}
, defined by
A_{n+1}=\frac{P_{n+1}+P_{n+1}^{T}}{2}
,
we can derive a two-parameter formula for
t
in the Dai-Liao CG parameter (
5
), given by

t_{n}^{p,q}=p\frac{\|y_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}-q\frac{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(6)

which ensures the descent condition (
3
) for
p\in\left(\frac{1}{4},\infty\right)
and
q\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1}{4}\right)
. However, in cases where
\frac{\|y_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}
and
\frac{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}
are nearly equal, their subtraction may not be numerically stable
[
9
]
. Moreover, the parameter
t
in the CG parameter
\beta_{n}^{DL}
must be nonnegative. To address these issues, they restricted
q
in (
6
) to be nonpositive and proposed the following CG parameter:

\beta_{n}^{DDL}=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),y_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}-t_{n}^{p,q}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
t_{n}^{p,q}
is defined by (
6
) with
p\in\left(\frac{1}{4},\infty\right)
and
q\in(-\infty,0]
. Note that for the parameter choice
(p,q)=(2,0)
in (
6
),
\beta_{n}^{DDL}
reduces to
\beta_{n}^{HZ}
. Similarly, if
(p,q)=(1,0)
, then
\beta_{n}^{DDL}
is equivalent to
\beta_{n}^{DK}(\tau_{n})

with the optimal choice of
\tau_{n}=\frac{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}
. For additional variants of the Dial–Liao conjugate gradient method, we refer to
[
5
,
10
]
.

Another significant category of iterative methods used for solving unconstrained optimization problems is the spectral gradient method. Initially introduced by Barzilai and Borwein
[
11
]
, these methods were later advanced by Raydan
[
32
]
, who developed a spectral gradient approach specifically for large-scale unconstrained optimization challenges. The spectral conjugate direction is defined by

d_{n}=\begin{cases}-\nabla f(x_{0}),&\text{ if }n=0,\\
-\theta_{n}\nabla f(x_{n})+\beta_{n-1}d_{n-1},&\text{ if }n\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
\{\theta_{n}\}\subset(0,\infty)
is the spectral parameter and
\{\beta_{n}\}\subset(0,\infty)
is the conjugate parameter.

Building on the encouraging numerical results associated with spectral gradient methods
[
11
,
32
]
, Birgin and Martínez
[
13
]
proposed the integration of spectral gradient techniques with conjugate gradient methods, culminating in the development of the first spectral conjugate gradient (CG) method. The spectral CG methods represent a powerful enhancement of traditional CG methods by incorporating the principles of spectral gradients within the conjugate gradient framework. Continuing in this direction, Yu and co-authors
[
34
]
adapted Perry’s spectral CG formulation
[
30
]
, obtaining a more effective variant of the method.
Inspired by the good convergence of quasi-Newton techniques, Andrei
[
2
]
proposed a CG method using a scaled BFGS preconditioner, and this idea was further developed in later works
[
3
,
4
,
8
]
.

Li and Fukushima
[
27
]
proposed a modified form of the secant condition, and Zhou and Zhang
[
35
]
later built on their idea to obtain the following modified secant relation:

\nabla^{2}f(x_{n+1})s_{n}=z_{n},

(7)

where

z_{n}=y_{n}+h_{n}\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{r}s_{n},\quad h_{n}=\nu+\max\left\{-\frac{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}},0\right\}\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{-r}

(8)

with
r,\nu\in(0,\infty)
. The modified secant condition (
8
) is essential to prove the global convergence of the MBFGS algorithm when dealing with non-convex objectives
[
35
]
. Note that the following expression holds irrespective of the line search used.

\displaystyle\langle z_{n},s_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y_{n},s_{n}\rangle+\nu\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{r}\|s_{n}\|^{2}+\max\left\{-\frac{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}},0\right\}\|s_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\nu\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{r}\|s_{n}\|^{2}>0\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Since (
8
) leads to favorable theoretical properties, Faramarzi and Amini
[
20
]
modified the spectral CG method of Jian et al.
[
26
]
and proposed the following choice for the conjugate parameter:

\beta_{n}^{ZDK}=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Based on this conjugate parameter, they constructed the following spectral parameter:

\theta_{n+1}=\begin{cases}1-\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),d_{n}\rangle}{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle},&\text{ if }1-\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),d_{n}\rangle}{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}\in\left[\frac{1}{4p}+|q|+\eta,\tau\right],\\
1,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\eta
and
\tau
are positive constants. To ensure global convergence of this method, Faramarzi and Amini
[
20
]
used the line search that satisfied the strong Wolfe conditions

f(x_{n}+\alpha_{n}d_{n})\leq f(x_{n})+\delta\alpha_{n}\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle,

(9)

|\langle\nabla f(x_{n}+\alpha_{n}d_{n}),d_{n}\rangle|\leq-\sigma\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle,

(10)

where
0<\delta<\sigma<1
, and the following assumptions:

(A1)

The level set
C_{0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)\leq f(x_{0})
is bounded.

(A2)

f
is continuously differentiable in some neighbourhood
\Omega
of
C_{0}
with
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradient.

The primary motivation of the present study is to propose a modified spectral conjugate gradient method, guided by an eigenvalue-based analysis and a modified secant condition, and to prove global convergence for general objective functions under typical conditions.This new method always meets the sufficient-descent requirement, independent of the line search strategy applied. To demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed approach, we present numerical results that validate its performance and show its superior convergence compared to the modified spectral conjugate gradient method of Faramarzi and Amini
[
20
]
, and the scaled conjugate gradient method of Mard and Fakhari
[
28
]
. Furthermore, we explore its application to compressed sensing, emphasizing its practical relevance and significant advantages.

A modified descent Dai-Liao spectral conjugate gradient method and its convergence analysis

In this section we introduce a modified spectral conjugate gradient (CG) descent method, inspired by ideas from quasi-Newton methods.

Using the theoretical properties provided by (
8
) and the Dai-Liao conjugate gradient parameter (
5
), we propose the following modified Dai-Liao conjugate parameter:

\beta_{n}=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-t\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(17)

where
t
is a nonnegative parameter.

Note that, by (
4
) and (
17
), the search direction can be expressed as

d_{n+1}=-Q_{n+1}\nabla f(x_{n+1})\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where

Q_{n+1}=I-\frac{s_{n}z_{n}^{T}}{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}+t\frac{s_{n}s_{n}^{T}}{\langle s_{n},y_{n}\rangle}.

Following the methodology in
[
6
]
, we propose the following formula for the parameter
t
in the modified Dai-Liao conjugate parameter (
17
):

t_{n}^{p,q}=p\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-q\frac{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(18)

with
p\in\left(\frac{1}{4},\infty\right)
and
q\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1}{4}\right)
. Using this parameter
t_{n}^{p,q}
, we define the following modified descent Dai-Liao (MDDL) conjugate parameter:

\beta_{n}^{MDDL}=\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-t_{n}^{p,q}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(19)

Now, we consider the following spectral conjugate gradient search direction:

d_{n}=\begin{cases}-\nabla f(x_{0}),&\text{ if }n=0,\\
-\theta_{n}\nabla f(x_{n})+\beta_{n-1}^{MDDL}d_{n-1}&\text{ if }n\geq 1,\end{cases}

(20)

where
\{\theta_{n}\}
is a sequence in
(0,\infty)
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function and let
\{d_{n}\}
be a sequence of spectral CG search directions generated by (
20
). Then the following statements hold:

(a)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
we have

\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle\leq-\kappa_{n}\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{2},

(21)

where

\kappa_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\text{if }n=0,\\
\theta_{n}-\left(\frac{1}{4p}+|q|\right),&\text{if }n\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

(b)

If
\theta_{n}>\frac{1}{4p}+|q|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then the direction given by (
20
) is a descent direction.

Proof.

(a)
Note that
\langle\nabla f(x_{0}),d_{0}\rangle=-\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|^{2}
. Therefore inequality (
21
) holds for
n=0
. We claim that the inequality (
21
) holds for
n>0
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Note that
p\in\left(\frac{1}{4},\infty\right)
and
q\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1}{4}\right)
. For simplicity, let us denote
\nabla f(x_{n+1})=F_{n+1}
. Then, using (
18
), (
19
) and (
20
), we get

\displaystyle\langle F_{n+1},d_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle F_{n+1},-\theta_{n+1}F_{n+1}+\beta_{n}^{MDDL}d_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\theta_{n+1}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\langle F_{n+1},z_{n}\rangle\langle F_{n+1},d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-\frac{t_{n}^{p,q}\langle s_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle=-\theta_{n+1}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\langle F_{n+1},z_{n}\rangle\langle F_{n+1},d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(p\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-q\frac{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}\right)\frac{\langle s_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle=-\theta_{n+1}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\langle F_{n+1},z_{n}\rangle\langle F_{n+1},d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-p\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\frac{\langle s_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle\quad+q\frac{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}\frac{\langle s_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}.

Since
s_{n}=\alpha_{n}d_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle F_{n+1},d_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\theta_{n+1}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\langle F_{n+1},z_{n}\rangle\langle F_{n+1},d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle\quad-p\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle^{2}}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}+q\frac{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}\frac{\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle^{2}}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle=-\theta_{n+1}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\langle F_{n+1},z_{n}\rangle\langle F_{n+1},d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle\quad-p\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle^{2}}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle^{2}}+q\frac{\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}.

(22)

Using Lemma
2
with

u_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2p}}F_{n+1}
and
v_{n}=\frac{\sqrt{2p}\langle F_{n+1},d_{n}\rangle z_{n}}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle},

we obtain

\frac{\langle F_{n+1},d_{n}\rangle\langle F_{n+1},z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\leq\frac{1}{4p}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{p\|z_{n}\|^{2}\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle^{2}}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle^{2}}.

(23)

On the other hand, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

q\frac{\langle d_{n},F_{n+1}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}\leq|q|\frac{\|d_{n}\|^{2}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}=|q|\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}.

(24)

Combining (
22
), (
23
) and (
24
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\langle F_{n+1},d_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\theta_{n+1}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4p}\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}+|q|\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left(\theta_{n+1}-\left(\frac{1}{4p}+|q|\right)\right)\|F_{n+1}\|^{2}.

(b) Suppose that
\theta_{n}>\frac{1}{4p}+|q|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, employing (
21
), we get
\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle<0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Therefore,
\{d_{n}\}
is a descent direction as claimed.
∎

Now, we construct the spectral parameter for our conjugate gradient method based on the conjugate parameter
\beta_{n}^{MDDL}
.

Quasi-Newton methods are a prominent class of iterative optimization methods that avoid computing the exact Hessian at every step. Their main goal is to retain the fast convergence associated with Newton’s method while keeping the computational burden low. Rather than evaluating the true Hessian, these methods update an approximate one at each iteration. Motivated by the structure of quasi-Newton directions, the search direction
d_{n}
in (
20
) is constructed to emulate this behavior. Our objective, therefore, is to choose an appropriate spectral parameter
\theta_{n+1}
such that

-B_{n+1}^{-1}\nabla f(x_{n+1})\approx-\theta_{n+1}\nabla f(x_{n+1})+\beta_{n}^{MDDL}d_{n},

where
B_{n+1}
is an approximation of the Hessian matrix
\nabla^{2}f(x_{n+1})
.
Taking the inner product with
B_{n+1}s_{n}
, we find that

-\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle\approx-\theta_{n+1}\langle B_{n+1}s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle+\beta_{n}^{MDDL}\langle B_{n+1}s_{n},d_{n}\rangle,

which implies that

\theta_{n+1}\approx\frac{1}{\langle B_{n+1}s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}(\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle+\beta_{n}^{MDDL}\langle B_{n+1}s_{n},d_{n}\rangle).

(25)

From (
7
),
B_{n+1}
must satisfy the modified secant condition
B_{n+1}s_{n}=z_{n}
. It follows from (
25
) that

\displaystyle{\theta_{n+1}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{\langle z_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}(\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle+\beta_{n}^{MDDL}\langle z_{n},d_{n}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\langle z_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}\left(\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle+\left(\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\left.-t_{n}^{p,q}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right)\langle z_{n},d_{n}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\langle z_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}\left(\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle+\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle\langle z_{n},d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-t_{n}^{p,q}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle\langle z_{n},d_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle+\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle-t_{n}^{p,q}\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle z_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{{t_{n}^{p,q}\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}-\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}{\langle z_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}

(26)

\displaystyle=1-(t_{n}^{p,q}-1)\frac{\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}{\langle z_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}.

(27)

Neglecting
\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle
in
(
26
), we arrive at

{\theta_{n+1}}=1-\frac{t_{n}^{p,q}\langle s_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}{\langle z_{n},\nabla f(x_{n+1})\rangle}.

(28)

We denote the
\theta_{n+1}
obtained in equation (
27
) by
\theta_{n+1}^{R}
and the
\theta_{n+1}
obtained in equation (
28
) by
\theta_{n+1}^{N}
.
To ensure that the sufficient-descent condition holds and to keep the spectral parameters bounded, we introduce the following choices for the spectral parameters:

\theta_{n+1}=\begin{cases}\theta_{n+1}^{R},&\text{ if }\theta_{n+1}^{R}\in\left[\frac{1}{4p}+|q|+\eta,\tau\right],\\
1,&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

(29)

or

\theta_{n+1}=\begin{cases}\theta_{n+1}^{N},&\text{ if }\theta_{n+1}^{N}\in\left[\frac{1}{4p}+|q|+\eta,\tau\right],\\
1,&\text{ otherwise },\end{cases}

(30)

where
\eta
and
\tau
are positive constants. Relations (
29
) and (
30
) lead to

\frac{1}{4p}+|q|+\eta\leq\theta_{n+1}\leq\tau\textrm{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(31)

Now, we propose the following modified descent Dai-Liao spectral conjugate gradient method (MDDLSCG):

Algorithm 1

MDDLSCG Algorithm

Input:
Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\epsilon,\delta,\sigma,\eta,\tau,r,\nu\in(0,\infty)
be such that

0<\delta<\sigma<1
.

Step 0:
Set
d_{0}=-\nabla f(x_{0})
.

Step 1:
If
\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
, stop, where

\|u\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|u_{i}|
for

u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Step 2:
Determine the step length
\alpha_{n}
satisfying the strong Wolfe

conditions (
9
) and (
10
).

Step 3:
Set
x_{n+1}=x_{n}+\alpha_{n}d_{n}
.

Step 4:
Compute
d_{n+1}
by

d_{n+1}=-\theta_{n+1}\nabla f(x_{n+1})+\beta_{n}^{MDDL}d_{n}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where the CG parameter
\beta_{n}^{MDDL}
is given by (
19
) and

the spectral
parameter
\theta_{n}
is given by (
29
) or (
30
).

Step 5:
Set
n=n+1
and return to Step 1.

Remark 6
.

(i)
Using
\theta_{n}\geq\frac{1}{4p}+|q|+\eta
and (
21
), we conclude that

\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle\leq-\eta\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{2}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(32)

(ii) If
\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|\neq 0
, then using
(
10
) and (
32
), we see that

\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle\geq-(1-\sigma)\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle\geq(1-\sigma)\eta\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{2}>0,

(33)

which, by (
8
), implies that
h_{n}=\nu
and hence

z_{n}=y_{n}+\nu\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{r}s_{n}.

(34)

Now, we analyze the global convergence of the
MDDLSCG Algorithm.

Remark 7
.

(i)

Employing (
9
) and (
32
), we can see that

\{f(x_{n})\}
is decreasing. Thus, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
generated by Algorithm
1
is contained in
C_{0}
.

(ii)

From Assumption (A1), there exists
M>0
such that
\|x\|\leq M
for all
x\in C_{0}
. Then using Assumption (A2), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x_{0})\|+\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq L\|x-x_{0}\|+\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq 2ML+\|\nabla f(x_{0})\|=\gamma\text{ for all }x\in C_{0}.

(35)

In addition, suppose that
\nabla f(x_{n})\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
; otherwise, a stationary point will be found.

Lemma 8
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function and let
\{x_{n}\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
1
such that Assumptions (A1) and (A2) hold. Suppose that
\theta_{n}>\frac{1}{4p}+|q|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|\neq 0
, then
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{4}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}<\infty.

Proof.

Since
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|\neq 0
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|\geq\epsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. When combined with (
16
), this implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|^{2}}=0.

(36)

Let
u_{n}=d_{n}
,
v_{n}=\nabla f(x_{n})
,
a_{n}=\theta_{n}
and
b_{n}=\beta_{n}
in Lemma
3
. Since
\theta_{n}>\frac{1}{4p}+|q|>0
, it follows from Remark
6
that
\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle<0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Also from strong Wolfe condition (
10
), we have
|\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),d_{n}\rangle|\leq-\sigma\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then, clearly all the conditions (C1), (C2) and (C3) of Lemma
3
are satisfied. Thus, we have

\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),d_{n+1}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{n+1}\|^{2}}+\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}\geq\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|^{4}}{\|d_{n+1}\|^{2}}\left[B-\theta_{n+1}^{2}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|^{2}}\right],

where
B=\left(\frac{1}{4p}+|q|\right)^{2}\frac{1}{1+\sigma^{2}}
.
It follows from (
36
) that for all sufficiently large
n
, we have

\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),d_{n+1}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{n+1}\|^{2}}+\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n}),d_{n}\rangle^{2}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}\geq B\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|^{4}}{\|d_{n+1}\|^{2}}.

(37)

Using (
37
) and Lemma
4
, we get that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|^{4}}{\|d_{n+1}\|^{2}}<\infty.

∎

Theorem 9
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real function and let
\{x_{n}\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
1
such that Assumptions (A1) and (A2) hold. Then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|=0.

Proof.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that the assertion of the theorem does not hold. Then there exists a positive constant
\xi
such that

\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|>\xi\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(38)

Employing (
33
), (
34
) and (
38
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle d_{n},y_{n}\rangle+\nu\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{r}\alpha_{n}\|d_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\nu\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{r}\alpha_{n}\|d_{n}\|^{2}\geq\nu\xi^{r}\alpha_{n}\|d_{n}\|^{2}.

(39)

Using Assumption (A2), (
34
) and (
35
), we have

\|z_{n}\|\leq\|y_{n}\|+\nu\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{r}\|s_{n}\|\leq L\|s_{n}\|+\nu\gamma^{r}\|s_{n}\|=(L+\nu\gamma^{r})\|s_{n}\|.

(40)

Thus, relations (
19
), (
35
), (
39
), (
40
), along with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, yield

\displaystyle\|\beta_{n}^{MDDL}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-t_{n}^{p,q}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-\left(p\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-q\frac{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}\right)\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}-p\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}+q\frac{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),z_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right\|+p\left\|\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}}{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+|q|\left\|\frac{\langle s_{n},z_{n}\rangle}{\|s_{n}\|^{2}}\frac{\langle\nabla f(x_{n+1}),s_{n}\rangle}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|\|z_{n}\|}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle}+p\frac{\|z_{n}\|^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|\|d_{n}\|}{\langle d_{n},z_{n}\rangle^{2}}+|q|\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|}{\|d_{n}\|}

(41)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma(L+\nu\gamma^{r})}{\nu\xi^{r}\|d_{n}\|}+\frac{p\gamma(L+\nu\gamma^{r})^{2}}{\nu^{2}\xi^{2r}\|d_{n}\|}+|q|\frac{\gamma}{\|d_{n}\|}.

Note that
\theta_{n+1}\leq\tau
by (
31
). Hence, using (
20
) and (
41
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\|d_{n+1}\|

\displaystyle=\|-\theta_{n+1}\nabla f(x_{n+1})+\beta_{n}^{MDDL}d_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\theta_{n+1}\|\|\nabla f(x_{n+1})\|+\|\beta_{n}^{MDDL}\|\|d_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\tau\gamma+\frac{\gamma(L+\nu\gamma^{r})}{\nu\xi^{r}}+\frac{p\gamma(L+\nu\gamma^{r})^{2}}{\nu^{2}\xi^{2r}}+|q|\gamma.

(42)

Using (
42
), we observe that

\frac{1}{\|d_{n+1}\|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{E^{2}}\textrm{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
E=\tau\gamma+\frac{\gamma(L+\nu\gamma^{r})}{\nu\xi^{r}}+\frac{p\gamma(L+\nu\gamma^{r})^{2}}{\nu^{2}\xi^{2r}}+|q|\gamma
. Thus,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}=\infty.

(43)

Employing (
38
) and Lemma
8
, we find that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{4}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{4}}\leq\frac{1}{\xi^{4}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|^{4}}{\|d_{n}\|^{2}}<\infty,

which contradicts (
43
). Therefore,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla f(x_{n})\|=0
, as asserted.
∎

Application to compressed sensing

Compressed sensing is a widely recognized signal processing approach that enables the efficient acquisition and reconstruction of signals with a sparse structure, allowing these signals to be stored in a compact and memoryless form
[
15
]
. Its practical significance has been demonstrated in numerous fields, including machine learning, compressive imaging, radar systems, wireless sensor networks, medical and astronomical signal processing, and video compression, as discussed in
[
1
]
.

The compressed sensing problem involves finding a sparse solution to a highly underdetermined system, represented as
Ax=b
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
(with
m\ll n
) and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

[
15
]
. A common approach is to solve the following optimization problem:

f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+\mu\phi(x),

where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a penalty function, and
\mu>0
is a parameter that regulates the trade-off between sparsity and reconstruction fidelity. A popular choice for
\phi
is
\phi(x)=\|x\|_{1}
, leading to the well-known Basis Pursuit Denoising (BPD) problem, which has been extensively studied in the literature; see
[
19
]
and references therein. However, solving BPD is difficult because the
\ell_{1}
-penalty term is non-smooth. To overcome this difficulty, Zhu et al.
[
36
]
introduced a relaxed form of BPD based on Nesterov’s smoothing technique
[
29
]
, leading to the following formulation:

f_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{\lambda}(|x_{i}|),

where
x_{i}
represents the
i
-th component of the vector
x
, and
\psi_{\lambda}
is defined by

\psi_{\lambda}(|x_{i}|)=\max_{\gamma\in[0,1]}\left\{\gamma|x_{i}|-\frac{1}{2}\lambda\gamma^{2}\right\},

which simplifies to:

\psi_{\lambda}(|x_{i}|)=\begin{cases}\frac{|x_{i}|^{2}}{2\lambda},&\text{if }|x_{i}|<\lambda,\\
|x_{i}|-\frac{\lambda}{2},&\text{if }|x_{i}|\geq\lambda,\end{cases}

where
\lambda>0
. Function
f_{\lambda}
is known as the Huber function
[
14
]
, and its gradient
\nabla f_{\lambda}
is
\frac{1}{\lambda}
-Lipschitz continuous
[
12
]
. The explicit form of the Huber function is given by

(\nabla f_{\lambda})_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{x_{i}}{\lambda},&\text{if }|x_{i}|<\lambda,\\
\text{sgn}(x_{i}),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

where
\text{sgn}(\cdot)
denotes the signum function.

Therefore, instead of solving the original problem for
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+\mu\|x\|_{1}
, we solve the smoothed version:

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}+\mu f_{\lambda}(x).

Clearly, the problem above is an unconstrained smooth convex optimization problem, and its gradient is given by:

\nabla f(x)=\lambda\nabla f_{\lambda}(x)+A^{T}(Ax-b).

For the numerical experiments, we aim to reconstruct a sparse signal of length
n
from
m
-length observations (
m\ll n
). The parameters for the MDDLSCG, MSCG and ScCG algorithms are set as follows:

\mu=\max\{2^{-7},0.001\|A^{T}b\|_{\infty}\}
,
\lambda=\min\{0.001,0.048\|A^{T}b\|_{\infty}\}
,
\sigma=0.1
,

\delta=0.01
,
\eta=0.001
,
\tau=10
,
r=1
and
\nu=0.001
.

For MDDLSCG, we choose
p=0.4
and
q=0.2
. The Gaussian matrix
A
is generated with the MATLAB commands
randn(m, n)
and the noise vector
w
is generated with
0.1\times\texttt{randn(m, 1)}
. The measurement vector
b
is computed by

b=Ax+w.

The regularization parameter is chosen as
\mu=0.001\|A^{T}b\|_{\infty}
. Each methods begin with the initial point
x_{0}=A^{T}b
. Let
x
represent the true signal and
x^{*}
denote the recovered one. The termination criterion for the experiment is the mean-squared error(MSE):

\text{MSE}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{i}^{*})^{2}\leq 10^{-5}.

To assess the reconstruction quality, we also compute the relative error (RelErr) of the restored signal, defined by

\text{RelErr}=\frac{\|x^{*}-x\|}{\|x\|}.

Let
k
represent the number of nonzero components in the original signal. To ensure a fair comparison, we test two cases:
(m,n,k)=(128,512,16)
,
(256,1024,32)
.

The numerical results are summarized in Table

From Table

, it is clear that the MDDLSCG algorithm surpasses the MSCG and ScCG algorithms in performance across all cases.

Conclusions

We investigated a modified descent Dai-Liao spectral conjugate gradient (MDDLSCG) method that incorporates a new secant condition and quasi-Newton direction. The updated method introduces spectral parameters ensuring a sufficient descent property independent of line search techniques. Theoretical analysis establishes the global convergence of our method for general nonlinear functions under standard assumptions. Numerical experiments and an application to signal processing demonstrate the improved performance and practical effectiveness of the proposed algorithm. Additionally, we present numerical results that validate its performance and show its superior convergence compared to the MSCG method
[
20
]
and the ScCG method
[
28
]
, highlighting its potential in optimization and related applications.

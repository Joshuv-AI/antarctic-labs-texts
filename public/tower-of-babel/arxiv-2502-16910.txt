On the cyclotomic Iwasawa invariants of Elliptic Curves of rank one
Foivos Chnaras

Abstract
For elliptic curves of rank 1 over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , we provide a numerical criterion that determines whether the Iwasawa invariants at a prime p p of good reduction attain their minimum possible value.



1
Introduction

The Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves provides deep insights on their arithmetic. For an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
, the study of Iwasawa theory for primes of good ordinary reduction was initiated by Mazur
[
14
]
) and Greenberg
[
10
]
. The supersingular case was first studied by Perrin Riou
[
20
]
. Pollack introduced the signed p-adic L-functions on the analytic side (
[
18
]
) and Kobayashi introduced the signed Selmer groups on the algebraic side (
[
13
]
).
The goal of our study is to explore properties of the Iwasawa invariants in both the orginary and supersingular case.

In particular, we develop an explicit numerical criterion that determines whether for an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
of rank 1 and a prime
p
of good reduction, the Iwasawa invariants attain their minimum possible value.

1.1
The good ordinary case

We start with an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Assume that
E
has good, ordinary reduction at an odd prime
p
. Let
\lambda_{p}(E),\mu_{p}(E)
be the Iwasawa invariants associated with the Pontryagin dual of
\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
, the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group of
E
over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
- extension
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}
.

It is expected for density one of the good ordinary primes that
\mu_{p}(E)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(E)=\rank E(\mathbb{Q})
. When
E
has rank
0
over
\mathbb{Q}
, then this is known by a theorem of Greenberg (
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
):

Theorem

(Greenberg)
.

Assume
E
is an elliptic curve over a number field
K
with good ordinary reduction at all the primes above
p
. Assume also that
\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
is finite. Then

f_{E}(0)\sim\frac{\prod\limits_{v\text{ bad}}c_{v}\cdot\prod\limits_{v|p}N_{v}^{2}\cdot\#\Sel(E/K)}{E(K)_{tors}^{2}}

where
N_{v}:=\#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{v})
the number of points of the elliptic curve
\mod v
and where
c_{v}
are the Tamagawa numbers. Finally,
f_{E}(T)
is the characteristic polynomial of the Pontryagin dual of
\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
.

The number of primes that divide the Tamagawa numbers, the Selmer group and
E(K)_{tors}
is finite and a Cebotarev type argument shows that the number of anomalous primes, i.e. the primes that divide
N_{p}
, has density 0. For more details, see
[
10
]

For elliptic curves of rank
1
, works of Perrin-Riou and Schneider (see
[
20
]
,
[
26
]
) produce the generalization of the above theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at
p>2
. Then
\ord_{T=0}f_{E}(T)\geq\rank E(\mathbb{Q})
.Furthermore, if:

1.

\Reg_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})\neq 0

2.

\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}]
is finite

3.

The kernel and cokernel of the map
\Phi_{M}:M^{\Gamma}\to M_{\Gamma}
are finite, where
M=\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
.

then:

1.

\ord_{T=0}f_{E}(T)=\rank E(\mathbb{Q})

2.

Write
f_{E}(T)=T^{r}g(T)
, where
r:=\rank E(\mathbb{Q})
. Then

g_{E}(0)\sim\frac{\text{Reg}_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})}{p^{r}}\cdot\frac{\prod\limits_{\ell\text{ bad}}c_{\ell}\cdot N_{p}^{2}\cdot\#\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{tors})^{2}}

up to a
p-
adic unit.

A few remarks are in order:

•

The assumptions (1)-(2) are conjecturally always true. The first one in particular is proven for CM elliptic curves of rank 1, by the work of Bertrand (see
[
3
]
).

•

The third hypothesis is true if
T^{2}\nmid f_{E}(T)
, i.e. if the rank is less or equal to 1 (see
[
33
, Lemma 2.11]
. In that case, the quotient
\chi_{T}(\Gamma,M):=\frac{\#\ker\Phi_{M}}{\#\Coker\Phi_{M}}
is called the
truncated Euler characteristic
. When defined, it is always an integer.

•

In general,
\lambda_{p}(E)\geq\rank E(K)
by an application of Mazur’s Control Theorem.
\mu_{p}(E)
is expected to be
0
any time
E[p]
is irreducible by Greenberg’s Conjecture. It is known that
\mu_{p}=0
for almost all primes (see
[
8
]
and
[
6
]
)

•

When
E/\mathbb{Q}
has rank
1
, we have
\mu_{p}(E)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(E)=1\iff g_{E}(0)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}.

Therefore, in the rank
1
case, the only addition is the appearance of the
p-
adic regulator.
Our aim is to simply translate the property of
g_{E}(0)
being a unit to a numerical criterion. Before we state our main results, let us first establish some notations:

For an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with a fixed Weierstrass model, we define:

Definition 1.1
.

1.

E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\{P\in E(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):P\text{ has non-singular reduction at }p\}

2.

E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})=\{P\in E(\mathbb{Q}):P\text{ has non-singular reduction at every prime }p\}

3.

E^{k}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=
the
k
-th filtration on the formal group

4.

N_{p}(E)=\#\tilde{E}_{ns}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

5.

C_{E}(P)=\min\{n:nP\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})\}

6.

C_{E}=\min\{n:nP\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})\text{ for all }P\in E(\mathbb{Q})\}

7.

c_{p}
= the
p
-th Tamagawa number

8.

\mathrm{Tam}(E)=
the least common multiple of the Tamagawa numbers

9.

If
P=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})\in E(\mathbb{Q})
, then denote
a(P)=a,\quad b(P)=b,\quad d(P)=d
and

nP=\left(\frac{a_{n}}{d_{n}^{2}},\frac{b_{n}}{d_{n}^{3}}\right)

Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of rank 1 over
\mathbb{Q}
, with good ordinary reduction at
p\geq 5
.

Let
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
be a generator of the free part of the Mordell Weil group.

Let

Q=N_{p}(E)\cdot C_{E}\cdot P\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})

where we can replace
C_{E}
with
Tam(E)
if the curve is given in the global minimal model.

Assume
p
does not divide
C_{E},\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{tors}
and
\#\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})
.

Then

\left[\mu_{p}(E)=0\text{ and }\lambda_{p}(E)=1\right]\iff a(Q)^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\mod p^{2}

This is comparable to Gold’s criterion for the cyclotomic
\lambda
invariant of imaginary quadratic fields (
[
9
]
):

Theorem

(Gold’s Criterion)
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field, and
p>3
be a prime that splits in
K
as
p\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Assume
p
does not divide
h_{K}
(the class number of
K
). Write
\mathfrak{p}^{h_{K}}=(a)
. Then

\mu_{p}(K)+\lambda_{p}(K)=1\iff a^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\mod\bar{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}

Note that for imaginary quadratic fields we already know
\mu_{p}(K)=0
by the Ferrero-Washington theorem, although that fact is not needed for the proof of the theorem. Both ours and Gold’s criteria can be made more explicit, relaxing the assumption about the non-divisibility by
p
. This will be made clear in the proof of the theorem, but for simplicity, we skip that formulation here.

1.2
The supersingular case

For supersingular primes, the situation is more involved. Perrin-Riou constructed a p-adic
L
-series
L_{p}(E,T)
with coefficients in
D_{p}E\otimes Q_{p}[[T]]
, where
D_{p}E
is the Dieudonne model, which is a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space of dimension 2.

There’s also a construction of a
p-
adic regulator of the elliptic curve with values again in
D_{p}E
. Then the
p-
adic Birch and Swinnerton Dyer conjecture for the supersingular case, as posed by Perrin-Riou and Bernardi in
[
4
]
, is as follows:

Conjecture 1.1

(Bernardi–Perrin-Riou)
.

Suppose E is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good supersingular reduction at
p
.

1.

The order of vanishing of
L_{p}(E,T)
at
T=0
is equal to the rank
r
of the elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
.

2.

The leading term
L_{p}^{*}(E,0)
satisfies

(1-\phi)^{-2}L_{p}^{*}(E,0)=\frac{\prod_{v}c_{v}\cdot\Sh(E/Q)}{|E(\mathbb{Q})_{tors}|^{2}}\cdot\frac{\Reg_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})}{p^{r}}

(1)

where
\phi
is the Frobenius as explained in Section
3.3
.

The signed
p-
adic
L-
functions constructed by Pollack are related to the one constructed by Perrin Riou. That relationship leads us to our second main theorem:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let E be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
of rank 1 and
p
be a prime of supersingular reduction. Let
P
be a generator of the free Mordell Weil group and
Q=C_{E}\cdot N_{p}\cdot P
. Write
Q=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})
. Assume
p
does not divide
C_{E}
and
\#\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})
and assume conjecture
1.1
. Then:

1.

\lambda_{p}^{+}(E)=1\iff a(Q)^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\mod p^{2}

(2)

2.

If
\lambda_{p}^{+}(E)>1
then

\lambda^{-}_{p}(E)=1\iff d(Q)\not\equiv 0\mod p^{2}

(3)

1.3
Non-minimal models

Finally, as it seems to have been overlooked in the literature so far, we make the following remark:

By definition, if the elliptic curve is
p-
minimal
, then

[E(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]=c_{p}.

However, if the curve is not minimal, the value of the index will change:

Theorem 1.4
.

If
E/\mathbb{Q}
is not
p-
minimal, let
E_{\min}
be a
p
-minimal model and
\Delta,\Delta_{\min}
the discriminants of
E
and
E_{\min}
respectively. Let
k=\frac{v_{p}(\Delta)-v_{p}(\Delta_{\min})}{12}
, where
v_{p}
is the
p-
adic valuation. Then,

E^{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\simeq E_{\min}^{n+k}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

In particular if
k\geq 1
:

[E(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]=[E_{\min}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):E^{k}_{\min}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]=c_{p}N_{p}p^{k-1}

In practice, this says that in general, the value of
C_{E}
will not be equal to
\mathrm{Tam}(E)
and the above theorem provides an algorithm to explicitly compute it. On the algorithm used for the computation of the p-adic height in
[
15
]
(page 14, Algorithm 3.4), this remark seems to be overlooked. This had led to a wrong implementation of the algorithm in the software SAGE, which has recently been corrected.

If one specializes this theorem to the case of the Short Weierstrass model, one gets the following:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
E:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
given in a
short
Weierstrass model.
Let

m=\rm{lcm}(N_{2}(E),N_{3}(E))

Then
[E(\mathbb{Q}):E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})]
divides
m\cdot Tam(E)
.

We remark that computationally for a fixed point
P
, one can compute
C_{E}(P)
instead of
C_{E}
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Fix a prime
p
. Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with fixed Weierstrass model and
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
. Let
P=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})
and write
u_{n}=\frac{d^{n^{2}}f_{n}}{d_{n}}
, where
f_{n}
is the
n-
th division polynomial. Then

P\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\iff u_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

In particular

C_{E}(P)=\min\{n:|u_{2}(nP)|=1\}

Examples

We first provide a couple of examples to show the above theorems in practice. We used SAGE (
[
31
]
) for the computations.

Example 2.1
.

Let
E:y^{2}+y=x^{3}-x
which is rank 1 over
\mathbb{Q}
, given in the global minimal model and which has label
"37a1"
in the Cremona table. Then using Sage, we find that

Tam(E)=1

and
P=(0,-1)
is a generator of the free Mordell-Weil group. Since

\mathrm{Tam}(E)=1
, we have

E(\mathbb{Q})=E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}).

The corresponding short Weierstrass form of
E
is
E_{1}:y^{2}=x^{3}-16x+16
with
P_{1}=(0,-4)\in E_{1}
the generator. Reducing at the prime
2
one gets
\tilde{E}_{1}:y^{2}=x^{3}
which is clearly not smooth and
\tilde{P}_{1}=(0,0)
, which is the singular point. Therefore

P_{1}\not\in E_{1}^{0}(\mathbb{Q})

This agrees with Proposition
1.1
, as
u_{2}(P_{1})=-8
which is divisible by
2
. In fact, this suggests that
2
is the only prime in which
P_{1}
has singular reduction. Further, we find that

u_{2}(5P_{1})=1

which shows that
5P_{1}
has non-singular reduction everywhere. In fact, one can compute that

[E(\mathbb{Q}):E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})]=N_{2}(E)=5

which verifies Corollary
1.1

For the same elliptic curve on its minimal model, if we consider the prime
p=13
, we find on the LMFDB table that
\lambda_{13}(E)=3
. On the other hand, we compute
N_{13}=16
and let

Q=16P=\left(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}}\right)

We compute that
a(Q)\equiv-23\mod p^{2}
and it indeed turns out that

23^{12}\equiv 1\mod 13^{2}

which verifies that
\mu_{13}(E)+\lambda_{13}(E)>1
by Theorem
1.2
.

Example 2.2
.

In light of the formula in Theorem
1.1
, one might expect that if
p
divides
N_{p}
, then
\mu_{p}(E)+\lambda_{p}(E)>1
. The following example shows that this is not always the case. This happens because the term
N_{p}^{2}
in the formula gets cancelled with
\Reg_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})
, as can be seen in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We take the elliptic curve
E:y^{2}+y=x^{3}+x^{2}
. Then
p=5
is a good ordinary prime which does not divide
\Tam(E),|E(\mathbb{Q})_{tors}|
or
|\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})|
. We find that
N_{5}=10
but
\mu_{5}(E)=0
and
\lambda_{5}(E)=1
.

3
p-adic Regulator

To describe the construction of the
p
-adic regulator, we first need to start with the
p
-adic sigma function. There are many ways to define it and we will only present a couple of them. We follow
[
16
]
:

3.1
p-adic sigma function

Theorem 3.1

(Mazur-Tate)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at a prime
p
and Weierstrass equation
E:y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}
. The
p-
adic sigma function is the unique function
\sigma_{p}:E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong\hat{E}(\mathfrak{p})\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
satisfying any one of the following properties:

1.

If
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
Q\in\hat{E}(\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, then

\sigma_{p}(mQ)=\sigma_{p}(Q)^{m^{2}}f_{m}(Q)

where
f_{m}
is the
m-
th division polynomial relative to the invariant differential
\omega
.

2.

\sigma_{p}
is odd function with
\sigma_{p}(t)\in t\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[t]]
and there is a constant
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that the following differential equation is satisfied:

x(t)+c=-\frac{d}{\omega}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{p}}\frac{d\sigma_{p}}{\omega}\right)

In fact the constant
c
can be explicitly computed and is equal to

c=\frac{a_{1}^{2}+4a_{2}}{12}-\frac{1}{12}\mathbb{E}_{2}(E,\omega)

where
\mathbb{E}_{2}(E,\omega)
is the value of the Katz
p
-adic weight 2 Eisenstein series at
(E,\omega)

We shall see later that property (1) is also shared by the denominator of the point
P
and follows from Proposition
1.1
, which can be rephrased in the following way:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve and
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
. Write
P=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})
and let
d(P):=d
. Then for any
n\geq 2
,

P\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})\iff d(nP)=|d(P)^{n^{2}}f_{n}(P)|

The formula from (2) of the above theorem is what lets us compute the
p-
adic sigma function. See for example the Algorithm 3.1 on
[
15
]
. Implementing that algorithm on a general elliptic curve, we find

\sigma_{p}(t)=t+\frac{1}{2}a_{1}t^{2}+\left(\frac{3}{8}a_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}a_{2}-\frac{1}{2}c\right)t^{3}+\left(\frac{5}{16}a_{1}^{3}+\frac{3}{4}a_{1}a_{2}-\frac{3}{4}a_{1}c+\frac{1}{2}a_{3}\right)t^{4}+....

(4)

We make some observations:

(i)

\sigma_{p}
being odd means that
\sigma_{p}(-P)=-\sigma_{p}(P)
for suitable
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
. To translate this property on the formal group, we use the isomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\hat{E}(\mathbb{\mathfrak{}}p)\cong p\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P=(x,y)=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{t=-\frac{x}{y}=-\frac{a}{b}d}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

(5)

that sends
P=(x,y)
to
t=-\frac{x}{y}
. Then
-P=(x,y-a_{1}x-a3)
which is not in general sent to
-t
. This explains why there are even terms in
4
.

However if
a_{1}=a_{3}=0
(e.g. the elliptic curve is in Short Weierstrass form), then
\sigma_{p}(t)\in t\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[t]]
is an odd power series.

(ii)

To compute the
p-
adic sigma function, one first needs to compute the constant
c
and in particular the Katz weight 2
p-
adic Eisenstein series which is done through Kedlaya’s algorithm. The original algorithm required
p\geq 5
using the minimal model of the elliptic curve. However, by changing the model, Balakrishnan shows how to compute it for
p=3
as well (see
[
2
]
).

(iii)

If
E
is a CM elliptic curve of rank 1, then the value of
\mathbb{E}_{2}(E,\omega)
is independent of
p
and belongs to the field of complex multiplication of
E
, by
[
12
, Lemma 8.0.13]

3.2
The ordinary case

We can now describe the construction of the
p
-adic height. We skip the theoretical background and only focus on the computational aspect. We follow
[
15
]
:

Definition:
Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at an odd prime
p
and
P\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})
. The (cyclotomic)
p-
adic height is defined as

h_{p}(P)=\log_{p}\frac{\sigma_{p}(P)}{d(P)}

(6)

where
\log_{p}
is the
p-
adic logarithm.

If
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
, we can find
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
Q:=mP\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})
. We then define

h_{p}(P):=\frac{1}{m^{2}}h_{p}(Q)

We mention without proof that this definition is independent of the choice of Weierstrass model of the elliptic curve.

Remark:

1.

If
E
is given in the
global minimal Weierstrass model
, then one can pick

m=\mathrm{lcm}(\mathrm{Tam}(E),N_{p})

The idea behind this is that
\mathrm{Tam}(E)\cdot P\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})
for all
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
by definition of the Tamagawa numbers and
N_{p}=[E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]=[E(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]
since
p
is a good prime.

2.

If
E
is NOT given in a global minimal Weierstrass model, then
\mathrm{Tam}(E)\cdot P
will no longer necessarily land in
E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})
. To find a suitable scalar multiple, we can use Theorem
1.4
in order to evaluate the index
[E(\mathbb{Q}):E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})]
in terms of the Tamagawa numbers. This index is of course independent of the point
P
. Alternatively, we can use Proposition
1.1
to find the smallest scalar multiple
m
such that
mP\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})
. This depends on the point
P
, but is computationally more efficient.

3.3
Dieudonne Module

In the supersingular case, there is no canonical
p-
adic height with values in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Instead one has to use the Dieudonne Module
D_{p}E
. For our intents and purposes, it suffices to simply characterize the Dieudonne module as a 2-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space generated by
\omega_{E}
and
\eta=x\omega_{E}
, where
\omega_{E}
is the invariant differential.

There is an action of the Frobenius
\phi
on
D_{p}E
, with characteristic polynomial
X^{2}+\frac{1}{p}
(we are assuming
p\geq 5
and hence that
a_{p}=0
). We write

\phi=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{bmatrix}_{(\omega,\eta)}

We remark some useful properties that can be found in the proof of Lemme 2.1 in
[
23
]

Proposition 3.2

(Perrion-Riou–Bernardi)
.

With the above notation:

1.

\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

2.

\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}

3.

\ord_{p}(\beta)=-1

It turns out that
(\omega,\phi(\omega))
is also a basis for
D_{p}E
. Under this basis, we have that

\phi=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\frac{1}{p}\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}_{(\omega,\phi(\omega))}.

Finally, simple calculations show that a change of basis gives

(A,B)_{(\omega,\eta)}=(A+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}B,-\frac{1}{\gamma}B)_{(\omega,\phi(\omega))}

(7)

3.4
The supersingular case

Now that we have defined the Dieudonne module
D_{p}E
, we will define for each
v\in D_{p}E
, a height

h_{v}:E(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}

which depends linearly on
v
.

1.

For
v=\omega
define

h_{\omega}(P)=\log_{\hat{E}}^{2}(P)

(8)

for
P\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, where
\log_{\hat{E}}
is the elliptic
p
-adic logarithm. One can extend it linearly to all
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
. By the identification

E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}p\mathbb{Z}_{p}

(9)

that sends
P=(x,y)=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})
to
t=-\frac{x}{y}=-\frac{a}{b}d
, we get

h_{\omega}=t^{2}+a_{1}t^{3}+O(t^{4})

(10)

In particular we remark that

\ord_{p}(h_{\omega}(P))\geq 2

and that

\ord_{p}(h_{\omega}(P))>2\iff P\in E^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\iff p^{2}|d

(12)

If
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})-E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
p
is supersingular, the above criterion remains exactly the same since
h_{\omega}(P)=\frac{1}{N_{p}}h_{\omega}(N_{p}P)
and
p
does not divide
N_{p}
.

2.

For
v=\eta
, we define
h_{\eta}(P)
in the exact same way as it was defined in the ordinary case (Equation

).

3.5
Supersingular p-adic Regulator

For elliptic curves of rank 1 with supersingular reduction at

, the

-adic regulator is defined as

\mathrm{Reg}_{p}=(h_{\eta},-h_{\omega})_{(\omega,\eta)}=(h_{\eta}+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}h_{\omega},\frac{1}{\gamma}h_{\omega})_{(\omega,\phi(\omega))}

as an element in the Dieudonne module
D_{p}E
.
For a more general definiton over any rank, see
[

.

A straightforward computation that we will need later gives us

(1-\varphi)^{2}\text{Reg}_{p}=\left(\frac{(p\gamma-\gamma)h_{\eta}+(p\alpha-\alpha+2)h_{\omega}}{p\gamma},\frac{-2p\gamma h_{\eta}-(2p\alpha-p+1)h_{\omega}}{p\gamma}\right)_{(\omega,\varphi(\omega))}

(13)

4
p-adic L-series and the p-adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture

In this section, we give a brief overview of the
p-
adic version of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

We write
a_{p}
for the trace of Frobenius. Then
x^{2}-a_{p}x+p
is the characteristic polynomial. For every root
\alpha
of the polynomial which satisfies
\ord_{p}(\alpha)<1
, one can construct a
p
-adic L-series
L_{p}(E,\alpha,T)
. The construction involves modular symbols and can be found for example in
[
30
]
.

4.1
The ordinary case

If
p
is an ordinary prime, there there is a unique root
\alpha
with the desired propery and hence a canonical
p-
adic L-series, which we denote
L_{p}(E,T)
. In fact, it’s easy to see that
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. This in turn implies that
L_{p}(E,T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]
.

The
p-
adic version of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture in the ordinary case was proposed by Mazur, Tate and Teitelbaum in
[
17
]
:

Conjecture 4.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction at a prime
p
.

1.

The order of vanishing of
L_{p}(E,T)
at
T=0
is equal to the rank
r=\rank E(\mathbb{Q})

2.

Up to a
p-
adic unit, the leading term
L_{p}^{*}(E,0)
satisfies

L_{p}^{*}(E,0)\sim\frac{\prod_{v}c_{v}\cdot N_{p}^{2}\cdot\#\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})[p^{\infty}])^{2}}\cdot\frac{\Reg_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})}{p}

(14)

4.2
The supersingular case

In the supersingular case, for
p\geq 5
the characteristic polynomial becomes
x^{2}+p
, with both roots
\alpha,\bar{\alpha}
satisfying
\ord_{p}(\alpha)<1
. Furthermore, the series
L_{p}(E,\alpha,T)
might not even have integral coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\alpha)
. However, we can still write

L_{p}(E,\alpha,T)=G^{+}(T)+\alpha G^{-}(T)

with
G^{+}(T),G^{-}(T)\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[[T]]
.

Perrin-Riou in
[
23
]
defined a
p-
adic L-series with coefficients in
D_{p}(E)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}[[T]]
, by setting

L_{p}(E,T)=(G^{+}(T),-pG^{-}(T))_{(\omega,\phi(\omega))}

(15)

Then, the
p-
adic Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture, according to
[
4
]
is:

Conjecture 4.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with supersingular reduction at a prime
p
.

1.

The order of vanishing of
L_{p}(E,T)
at
T=0
is equal to the rank
r=\rank E(\mathbb{Q})
.

2.

The leading term
L_{p}^{*}(E,0)
satisfies

L_{p}^{*}(E,0)=\frac{\prod_{v}c_{v}\cdot\#\Sh(E\mathbb{Q})}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tor}})^{2}}\cdot(1-\phi)^{2}\frac{\Reg_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})}{p}\in D_{p}(E)

(16)

Even though
G^{+}(T)
and
G^{-}(T)
do not have integral coefficients, Pollack, in
[
18
]
, had the following insight:

By using interpolation properties of
G^{+}(T)
and
G^{-}(T)
already explored by Perrin-Riou in
[
21
]
, one sees that
G^{+}(T)
vanishes at
\zeta_{2n}-1
and
G^{-}(T)
vanishes at
\zeta_{2n-1}-1
, where
\zeta_{n}
is a
p^{n}
-th root of unity. In particular, they have infinitely many zeros. His insight was to construct two
p-
adic power series
\log_{p}^{+}(T)
and
\log_{p}^{-}(T)
that vanish precisely at those zeros. In particular, he defines

\log_{p}^{+}(T)=\frac{1}{p}\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Phi_{2n}(1+T)}{p}

(17)

and

\log_{p}^{-}(T)=\frac{1}{p}\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Phi_{2n+1}(1+T)}{p}

(18)

where
\Phi_{n}
is the
p^{n}
-th cyclotomic polynomial.

He then defines

L_{p}^{+}(E,T):=\frac{G^{+}(T)}{\log_{p}^{+}(E,T)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{p}^{-}(E,T):=\frac{G^{-}(T)}{\log_{p}^{-}(E,T)}

(19)

He proves that these power series have indeed bounded coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and hence finitely many zeros. The signed Iwasawa invariants
\mu_{p}^{\pm}
and
\lambda_{p}^{\pm}
refer to the Iwasawa invariants of these two power series.

5
Main Conjecture

The main conjecture relates the analytic side, i.e. the
p
-adic
L
-functions, with the algebraic side that comes from the study of Selmer groups. The ordinary and supersingular case have quite a different nature, so we will treat them separately.

5.1
The ordinary case

When
p
is an ordinary prime, the Pontryagin dual of the Selmer group
\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
is a finitely generated, torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. Therefore, associated to it is a characteristic series

f_{E}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T]]=\Lambda

Schneider (
[
27
]
) and Perrin-Riou (
[
19
]
) showed the following:

Theorem 5.1

(Perrin-Riou, Schneider)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at a prime
p
and rank
r
. Then

ord_{T=0}f_{E}(T)\geq r

It is equal to
r
if and only if

1.

\Reg_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})\neq 0

2.

\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})(p)
is finite

In that case, if we write
f_{E}(T)=T^{r}g(T)
, then, up to a
p-
adic unit,

g_{E}(0)\sim\frac{\prod_{v}c_{v}\cdot N_{p}^{2}\cdot\#\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})(p)}{(\#E(\mathbb{Q})(p))^{2}}\cdot\frac{Reg_{p}(E\mathbb{Q})}{p^{r}}

(20)

The main conjecture simply states that
L_{p}(E,T)=f_{E}(T)\cdot u(T)
, where
u(T)\in\Lambda^{\times}

An important property that holds in the ordinary case is Mazur’s control theorem:

Theorem 5.2

(Mazur’s Control Theorem)
.

Let
\Gamma=\Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
be the galois group of the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
extension and
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with ordinary reduction at
p
. Then

\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\Gamma}

has finite kernel and cokernel.

In addition, it is well known that

\ord_{T=0}f_{E}(T)=\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\Gamma})

An immediate application of this is:

Corollary 5.1
.

Assume that
\#\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})(p)
is finite. Let
\lambda_{p}^{MW}
be the maximum rank of the elliptic curve over the tower of number fields in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. Then

\lambda_{p}^{MW}\leq\lambda_{p}(E)

In particular, this means that if
\lambda_{p}(E)=\rank(E(\mathbb{Q}))
, then the rank of the elliptic curve remains stable in the entire cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.

5.2
The supersingular case

The supersingular case is a lot more complicated. The Pontryagin dual of the Selmer group is no longer a torsion
\Lambda
-module. In addition, Mazur’s Control theorem is no longer true.

To overcome these obstacles, one approach is given by Perrin-Riou in
[
22
]
. A more modern approach is given by Kobayashi (
[
13
]
) where he constructs signed Selmer groups
\Sel_{p^{\infty}}^{\pm}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
which are cotorsion
\Lambda
-modules. We will denote
\lambda_{p}^{\mathrm{alg}},\mu_{p}^{\mathrm{alg}}
the Iwasawa invariants associated to them and
\lambda_{p}^{\mathrm{an}},\mu_{p}^{\mathrm{an}}
the ones defined by Pollack’s
p-
adic L-functions. We will also denote with
f_{E}^{\pm}(T)
the characteristic series that is associated to them. The main conjecture simply states then that
f_{E}^{\pm}(T)
is generated by Pollack’s signed
p
-adic L-functions:

Conjecture 5.1

(Main Conjecture)
.

f_{E}^{\pm}(T)=(L_{p}^{\pm}(E,T))

This is proven for CM elliptic curves by Pollack and Rubin in
[
24
]
. By using Kato’s Euler system, Kobayashi proves, that for non-CM elliptic curves and almost all primes
p
:

(L_{p}^{\pm}(E,T))\subseteq f_{E}^{\pm}(T)

Finally, there’s a version of Mazur’s Control theorem in the supersingular case:

Theorem 5.3

(Control Theorem)
.

The natural map

\Sel_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to\Sel_{p^{\infty}}^{\pm}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\Gamma}

has finite kernel and cokernel.

For a proof, see for example
[
25
]
, Proposition 5.1.

As in the ordinary case, this has the consequence that

\rank(E(\mathbb{Q}_{n}))\leq\lambda_{p}^{\pm}

for all
n\geq 1
. Therefore, if either
\lambda_{p}^{+}
or
\lambda_{p}^{-}
is equal to
\rank(E(\mathbb{Q}))
, then the rank of the elliptic curve remains stable in the entire cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.

We should clarify at this point that the Iwasawa invariants that are derived from the (signed) Selmer groups are a priori different from those that are derived from the (signed)
p-
adic
L-
series. We call the former algebraic Iwasawa invariants and the latter analytic. Under the main conjecture, they are equal to each other.

6
Main Results

We are ready to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. After setting up some notations, we will start with the ordinary case.

6.1
Fermat quotient

To simplify notation, we introduce the following definition:

Definition 6.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and a prime
p
coprime to
a
. Define

q_{p}(a):=\frac{a^{p-1}-1}{p}\mod p

the Fermat quotient.

We list some obvious but useful properties:

Lemma 6.1
.

1.

q_{p}(1)=0

2.

q_{p}(-a)=q_{p}(a)

For
a,b
coprime to
p
:

(i)

q_{p}(ab)=q_{p}(a)+q_{p}(b)

(ii)

q_{p}(\frac{a}{b})=q_{p}(a)-q_{p}(b)

(iii)

q_{p}(a+np^{2})=q_{p}(a)
.

(iv)

q_{p}(a+np)=q_{p}(a)-\frac{n}{a}

If an elliptic curve has Weierstrass model
E:y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}
and
Q=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, then, plugging in the coordinates of the point in the equation of the elliptic curve and clearing denominators, we get:

b^{2}+a_{1}abd+a_{3}bd^{3}=a^{3}+a_{2}ad^{2}+a_{4}d^{4}+a_{6}d^{6}

(21)

which, since
p^{2}|d^{2}
, implies that

q_{p}(b^{2}+a_{1}ab\frac{d}{p}p)=q_{p}(a^{3})

(22)

6.2
The ordinary case

We start with the following observation. Take an elliptic curve of rank 1 in a global minimal model, and let
P
be a generator of the free Mordell-Weil group. Using Theorem
1.1
and assuming the hypothesis of Theorem
1.2
, we get that

\mu_{p}(E)+\lambda_{p}(E)>1\iff N_{p}^{2}(E)\frac{\Reg_{p}(E)}{p}\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\iff\frac{h_{p}(Q)}{p}\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

where
Q=mP
is a scalar multiple of
P
such that
Q\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})\cap E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
as explained in the previous section.

Now, for an elliptic curve
E:y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}
and a point
Q\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})
, we first observe that

z:=\frac{\sigma_{p}(Q)}{d(Q)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

Indeed, write
Q=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})
and identify it with
t=-\frac{a}{b}d
in
p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
under (
5
) (note that
Q\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
implies that
p
divides
d:=d(Q)
and
p\nmid a,b
). Therefore, using formula (
4
), we have

z=-\frac{a}{b}+\frac{1}{2}a_{1}\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}d+O(d^{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose that
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and
N\geq 1
. Then to determine
\log_{p}z\mod p^{N}
, it suffices to know
z\mod p^{N}
. In particular, if
v\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, then

v_{p}(\log_{p}v)>1\iff v^{p-1}\equiv 1\mod p^{2}

Proof.

The proof is easy and can be found for example in
[
11
, Lemma 8]
.
∎

Therefore, to determine if
\frac{h_{p}(Q)}{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, it suffices to know
z\mod p^{2}
.

Case 1:
a_{1}=0
:
Then

z=-\frac{a}{b}+O(d^{3})

(23)

Therefore

\frac{h_{p}(Q)}{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\iff\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\mod p^{2}\iff q_{p}(a)\neq q_{p}(b)

But by
22
, we have that
q_{p}(b^{2})=q_{p}(a^{3})
, i.e.
2q_{p}(a)=3q_{p}(b)
. Therefore

q_{p}(z)=0\iff q_{p}(a)=0\iff q_{p}(b)=0

Case 2:
a_{1}\neq 0
:
Then

z=-\frac{a}{b}+\frac{1}{2}a_{1}\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}d+O(d^{3})

If
Q\in E^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(i.e
p^{2}|d
), then
q_{p}(z)=q_{p}(\frac{a}{b})
and Equation
22
again becomes
2q_{p}(a)=3q_{p}(b)
. Therefore, we have the same conclusion as in the previous case

Finally, assume
Q\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})-E^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. This is equivalent to saying that
p|d
but
p^{2}\nmid d
. Then:

q_{p}(z)=q_{p}(\frac{a}{b})+\frac{1}{2}a_{1}\frac{a}{b}\frac{d}{p}

(24)

On the other hand, by using the properties of the Fermat quotient and rearragning, Equation
22
becomes:

a_{1}\frac{a}{b}\frac{d}{p}=q_{p}(b^{2})-q_{p}(a^{3})

(25)

Substituting into Equation

, we have

q_{p}(z)=q_{p}(\frac{a}{b})+q_{p}(b)-\frac{3}{2}q_{p}(a)=\frac{1}{2}q_{p}(a)

We conclude that
q_{p}(z)=0\iff q_{p}(a)=0
which is what we wanted.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2

∎

6.3
The supersingular case

The idea for the supersingular primes is simple, although it requires some computational effort: First, we will explore the relationship of the Iwasawa invariants
\mu^{\pm},\lambda^{\pm}
with the power series
G^{\pm}
. The
p-
adic BSD conjecture will then give us a relationship between the leading term of
G^{\pm}
and the
p-
adic Regulator.

We start with Pollack’s
p-
adic L-functions. Write

G^{+}(T)=g_{0}^{+}+g_{1}^{+}T+O(T^{2}),\qquad G^{-}(T)=g_{0}^{-}+g_{1}^{-}T+O(T^{2})

with
a_{i}^{\pm}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

L_{p}^{+}=pg_{0}^{+}+(pg_{1}^{+}+\frac{pg_{0}^{+}}{2(p+1)})T+O(T^{2})

and

L_{p}^{-}=pg_{0}^{-}+(pg_{1}^{-}+\frac{g_{0}^{-}}{2(p+1)})T+O(T^{2})

Proof.

A direct computation gives that

\frac{\Phi_{r}(1+T))}{p}=1+\frac{p^{r-1}(p-1)}{2}T+O(T^{2})

Therefore

\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Phi_{2n}(1+T)}{p}=1+\frac{p-1}{2}(p+p^{3}+p^{5}+...)T+O(T^{2})=1-\frac{p}{2(p+1)}T

and

\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Phi_{2n+1}(1+T)}{p}=1+\frac{p-1}{2}(1+p^{2}+p^{4}+...)T+O(T^{2})=1-\frac{1}{2(p+1)}T

But if
h(T)=h_{0}+h_{1}(T)+O(T^{2})
, then

\frac{1}{h(T)}=\frac{1}{h_{0}}-\frac{h_{1}}{h_{0}^{2}}T+O(T^{2})

Therefore, we have that

L_{p}^{+}=pg_{0}^{+}+(pg_{1}^{+}+\frac{pg_{0}^{+}}{2(p+1)})T+O(T^{2})

and similarly for the minus part.
∎

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve of rank 1 with supersingular reduction at a prime
p
. Then, assuming conjecture
4.2
,

\mu_{p}^{\pm}+\lambda_{p}^{\pm}=1\iff\ord_{p}(g_{1}^{\pm})=-1

Proof.

Since the elliptic curve has rank 1, we have that
g_{0}^{\pm}=0
. The rest follows immediately from Lemma
6.3
.
∎

Now assume that
p
does not divide
\prod c_{v}
and
\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})
. By Equation
16
, we have that

(g_{1}^{+},pg_{1}^{-})_{(\omega,\phi(\omega))}\sim(1-\phi)^{2}\frac{\Reg_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})}{p}

(26)

up to a
p-
adic unit.
Recall that
\phi=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{-1}{p}\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}_{(\omega,\phi(\omega))}
and that

\Reg_{p}=(h_{\eta}+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}h_{\omega},\frac{1}{\gamma}h_{\omega})_{(\omega,\phi(\omega))}

A direct computation gives that

(1-\phi)^{2}\Reg_{p}=\left(\frac{(p\gamma-\gamma)h_{\eta}+(p\alpha-\alpha+2)h_{\omega}}{p^{2}\gamma},\frac{-2p\gamma h_{\eta}-(2p\alpha-p+1)h_{\omega}}{p^{2}\gamma}\right)_{(\omega,\varphi,\omega)}

(27)

From Proposition
3.2
, recall that
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\ord_{p}(\beta)=-1
. Also recall that
\ord_{p}(h_{\omega})\geq 2
and
\ord_{p}h_{\eta}\geq 1
.
Therefore,

\ord_{p}(g_{1}^{+})=-1\iff\ord_{p}(h_{\eta})=1

But this is the same criterion we had for the ordinary height. If
Q=mP=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})
is the point defined in Theorem
1.3
we have proven that

\mu_{p}^{+}+\lambda_{p}^{+}=1\iff a^{p-1}\equiv 1\mod p^{2}

Now assume that
\mu_{p}^{+}+\lambda_{p}^{+}>1
. Therefore, by the above, this is equivalent to
\ord_{p}(h_{\eta})>1
. Then, we see that

\ord_{p}(g_{1}^{-})=-1\iff\ord_{p}(h_{\omega})=2

Equation
12
finishes now the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

We remark that from the above proof, one can also get a criterion for
\mu_{p}^{-}+\lambda_{p}^{-}>1
without requiring the assumption for the plus part, but it would be unwieldy for any practical applications.

7
Generalized Wieferich primes and computations

The numerical criterion that we derived in the previous section for the minus part, is reminiscent of the definition of Wieferich primes.

Silverman in
[
28
]
defines the following:

Definition 7.1

(Silverman)
.

Let
A/\mathbb{Q}
be a commutative algebraic group and let
P\in A(\mathbb{Q})
be a point of infinite order. For each prime
p
, let
N_{p}=\#A(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. A set of non-Wieferich primes for
A
and
P
is a set of the form

W_{A,P}=\{p:N_{p}P\not\equiv 0\mod p^{2}\}

.

When
A=\mathbb{G}_{m}
, this recovers the original definition of generalized non-Wieferich primes, i.e.

W_{\mathbb{G}_{m},a}=\{p:a^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\mod p^{2}\}

When
A=E
is an elliptic curve of rank 1 and
P
is a generator of the free Mordell Weil group, then

W_{E,P}=\{p:N_{p}P\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})-E^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\}=\{p:p^{2}\nmid d_{N_{p}}\}

where
N_{p}P=\left(\frac{a_{N_{p}}}{d_{N_{p}}^{2}},\frac{b_{N_{p}}}{d_{N_{p}}^{3}}\right)
, is exactly the set that appears
in Equation
3
of Theorem
1.3
. Silverman proves that the ABC conjecture implies that
W_{E,P}
is infinite. Conjecturally, it is of density 1 and its complement is also infinite.

The criterion for the ordinary case as well as the plus part of the supersingular case, leads us to define the following set:

Definition 7.2
.

Let
E
any elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
of positive rank and

P=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{c}{d^{3}})\in E(\mathbb{Q})
a non-torsion point. Define

\Lambda_{E,P}=\{p:q_{p}(a_{N_{p}})\neq 0\}=\{p:a_{N_{p}}^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\mod p^{2}\}

as
p
ranges over the good primes.

If the sequences
\{a_{N_{p}}\}
and
\{d_{N_{p}}\}
are ”random” enough as
N_{p}
varies then the probability that
q_{p}(a_{N_{p}})=0
is roughly
\frac{1}{p}
(and hence the complement of
\Lambda_{E,P}
is infinite and of density 0) and similarly for
d_{N_{p}}\equiv 0\mod p^{2}
. The intersection would have probability
\frac{1}{p^{2}}
and in particular would be finite.

In practice, this means that there would be finitely many supersingular primes
p
such that both
\mu_{p}^{+}+\lambda_{p}^{+}>1
and
\mu_{p}^{-}+\lambda_{p}^{-}>1
. In particular, that would imply that there are only finitely many supersingular primes
p
such that the rank of the elliptic curve changes in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.

This agrees with the computational evidence, which leads us to the following conjectures:

Conjecture 7.1
.

For any elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and
P\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q})
a non-torsion point:

1.

\Lambda_{E,P}
has density 1 and its complement is infinite.

2.

W_{E,P}
has density 1 and its complement is infinite.

3.

For any elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
of rank 1, there are only finitely many primes
p
of supersingular reduction such that the rank changes in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension.

We should remark however that we ’re unable to prove even that
\Lambda_{E,P}
is non-empty. It would be interesting if one could express the set
\Lambda_{E,P}
in a more general way, as in definition
7.1
.

Finally, we prove Theorem

8.1
Division Polynomials

For
P=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})
we use the following notation:

nP=\left(\frac{a_{n}}{d_{n}^{2}},\frac{b_{n}}{d_{n}^{3}}\right)=\left(\frac{g_{n}}{f_{n}^{2}},\frac{\omega_{n}}{f_{n}^{3}}\right)=\left(\frac{\hat{g}_{n}}{d^{2}\hat{f}_{n}^{2}},\frac{\hat{\omega}_{n}}{d^{3}\hat{f}_{n}^{3}}\right)

where
g_{n},f_{n},\omega_{n}
are division polynomials
(see
[
29
]
( Exercise 3.7 on page 105 ). When there’s no confusion, we will write
g_{n}
instead of
g_{n}(P)
and similarly for the rest.
Note that
\hat{f}_{n}(P),\hat{g}_{n}(P),\hat{\omega}_{n}(P)
are integers.

We will need the following well known lemma (for example see
[
5
, Lemma 1]
:

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
P\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),P=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})
. Then

v_{p}(d_{n})=v_{p}(d)+v_{p}(n)

The following proposition will play a central role and can be found in
[
1
, Theorem A]
:

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
P\in E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})-E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The following are equivalent:

1.

v_{p}(f_{2}),v_{p}(f_{3})>0

2.

v_{p}(f_{n})>0\quad\text{for all }n\geq 2

3.

\exists m_{0}\geq 2:v_{p}(f_{m_{0}}),v_{p}(f_{m_{0}+1})>0

4.

\exists n_{0}\geq 2:v_{p}(f_{n_{0}}),v_{p}(g_{n_{0}})>0

5.

P\in E(\mathbb{Q})-E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

Remark:
From the proof, one sees that that
(5)\Rightarrow(3),(4)
for all

m_{0},n_{0}\geq 2
.

Finally, we define

u_{n}=\frac{d^{n^{2}}f_{n}}{d_{n}}

called the
cancellation term
.
Proposition
1.1
is a special case of:

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
an elliptic curve and
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
. Then for any
n\geq 2
,

P\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\iff u_{n}(P)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}

Proof.

The forward direction is in
[
32
, Proposition 1]
. For the opposite direction, we argue as follows:

Assume
P\not\in E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
P=(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}})
. Then
v_{p}(d)=0
and
v_{p}(u_{n})=v_{p}(f_{n})-v_{p}(d_{n})
.

\bullet
If
nP\not\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\Rightarrow v_{p}(d_{n})=0
. Hence by Proposition
8.1
, we have
v_{p}(u_{n})>0
.

\bullet
If
nP\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, let
v_{p}(d_{n})=n>0
. Note that
d_{n}|d^{n^{2}}f_{n}
and hence
v_{p}(f_{n})\geq v_{p}(d_{n})
. From the Remark after Proposition
8.1
, we have that
v_{p}(g_{n})>0
. Hence
v_{p}(f_{n})>v_{p}(d_{n})
, which completes the proof.
∎

Varying through all the primes
p
gives us Proposition
3.1

8.2
Change of Coordinates

Consider an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with Weierstrass model
E:y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}
. A general change of coordinates is of the form
[u,r,s,t]
with
x=u^{2}x^{\prime}+r
and
y=u^{3}y^{\prime}+u^{2}sx^{\prime}+t
. Since this can always be decomposed into the change of coordinates
[u,0,0,0]
and
[0,r,s,t]
and the latter has no effect in the discriminant, it suffices for our purpose to only study the change of coordinates of the form

(x,y)\to(u^{2}x,u^{3}y)

and we will denote it as
[u,0,0,0]
.

For the rest of the section, we fix a prime
p
and denote
v=v_{p}
its normalized valuation.

An immediate corollary is the following:

Corollary 8.1
.

Let
[u,0,0,0]:E\to E^{\prime}
. Then:

1.

\Delta^{\prime}=u^{12}\Delta
. In particular if
u=p^{r}
then
r=\frac{v(\Delta)^{\prime}-v(\Delta)}{12}
.

2.

a_{i}^{\prime}=u^{i}a_{i}

3.

f_{2}^{\prime}=u^{3}f_{2}
and
f_{3}^{\prime}=u^{8}f_{3}
.

Proof.

(1) and (2) are immediate from the definitions. For (3) one has to use the general formula of the division polynomials, which is less common:

f_{2}(x,y)=2y+a_{1}x+a_{3}

f_{3}(x,y)=3x^{4}+b_{2}x^{3}+3b_{4}x^{2}+3b_{6}x+b_{8}

where
b_{2}=a_{1}^{2}+4a_{2},b_{4}=2a_{4}+a_{1}a_{3},b_{6}=a_{3}^{2}+4a_{6},b_{8}=a_{1}^{2}a_{6}-a_{1}a_{3}a_{4}+a_{2}a_{3}^{2}-4a_{2}a_{6}-a_{4}^{2}
.

The rest is just computations.
∎

8.3
Proof of Theorem
1.4

First we start with some observations:

Consider the change of coordinates
[p^{r},0,0,0]:E\to E^{\prime}
. If
P=(x,y)\in E(\mathbb{Q})
and
P^{\prime}=(p^{-2r}x,p^{-3r}y)\in E(\mathbb{Q})
its image, then by Corollary
8.1
we get that

v(f_{2}^{\prime})=3r+v(f_{2})

(28)

v(f_{3}^{\prime})=8r+v(f_{3})

(29)

where
f_{2}=f_{2}(P),f_{2}^{\prime}=f_{2}(P^{\prime})
.

Also, if
P=\left(\frac{a}{d^{2}},\frac{b}{d^{3}}\right)\in E^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, then by Lemma
8.1
we have

v(d_{m})=v(d)+v(m).

and since
u_{m}(P)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
by Proposition
8.2
, we get

v(f_{m})=v(d_{m})-m^{2}v(d)=(1-m^{2})v(d)+v(m)

(30)

We end up with:

v(f_{2})=-3v(d)+v(2)=\begin{cases}-3v(d),&p\neq 2\\
-3v(d)+1,&p=2\end{cases}

(31)

v(f_{3})=-8v(d)+v(3)=\begin{cases}-8v(d),&p\neq 3\\
-8v(d)+1,&p=3\end{cases}

(32)

With these prerequisites out of the way, take
P\in E^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
P^{\prime}
its image in
E^{\prime}
. In other words,
v(d(P))\geq r
. Therefore, by (
31
) and (
32
) we have that at least one of the following must be true:

v(f_{2}(P))\leq-3r

(33)

v(f_{3}(P))\leq-8r

(34)

Write
f_{2}^{\prime}=f_{2}(P^{\prime})
.By equations (
28
), (
29
), (
33
) and (
34
), we conclude that
v(f_{2}^{\prime})\leq 0
or
v(f_{3}^{\prime})\leq 0
(or both). By Proposition
8.1
we conclude that
P^{\prime}\in E^{\prime 0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We proved that if
P\in E^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, then its image
P^{\prime}
lies in
E^{\prime 0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

It is clear that this map is injective, and that its inverse

[p^{-r},0,0,0]:E^{\prime 0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to E^{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

is well defined and injective as well. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

We can now apply this to prove Corollary
1.1
:

Let
E_{min}/\mathbb{Q}
an elliptic curve given in a minimal model and
E/\mathbb{Q}
the elliptic curve in short Weierstrass form. For any prime other than 2 or 3,
E/\mathbb{Q}
is also
p-
minimal. For the primes
p=2
and
p=3
, there’s always a change of coordinates
[\frac{1}{p},r,s,t]:E_{min}\to E

[
29
, Chapter III]
. Hence, if
E
is not minimal at
p
, then

E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\simeq E^{1}_{min}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

and

[E(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):E^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]=[E_{\min}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):E^{1}_{\min}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]=c_{p}N_{p}

∎

Note that by the Weil bound, we have
N_{2}\leq 5
and
N_{3}\leq 7
. Therefore,
[E_{\text{short}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):E^{0}_{\text{short}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]
will always divide
7!\cdot c_{p}

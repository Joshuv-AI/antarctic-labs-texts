𝚪 \mathbf{\Gamma} -convergence and homogenisation for free discontinuity functionals with linear growth in the space of functions with bounded deformation Thanks: Preprint SISSA 01/2026/MATE
Gianni Dal Maso, Davide Donati

Abstract
. We study the Γ \Gamma -convergence of sequences of free discontinuity functionals with linear growth defined in the space BD {\rm BD} of functions with bounded deformation. We prove a compactness result with respect to Γ \Gamma -convergence and outline the main properties of the Γ \Gamma -limits, which lead to an integral representation result. The corresponding integrands are obtained by taking limits of suitable minimisation problems on small cubes. These results are then used to study the deterministic and stochastic homogenisation problem for a large class of free discontinuity functionals defined in BD {\rm BD} .

1.
Introduction

Given a bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the space
{\rm BD}(U)
of
functions of bounded deformation
is defined as the space of vector fields
u\in L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the symmetric part
Eu:=\frac{1}{2}(Du+Du^{T})
of the distributional gradient
Du
is a bounded Radon measure with values in the space
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
of
(d\times d)
symmetric matrices. It was introduced in
[
46
,
48
,
49
]
to provide a variational framework for the study of elasto-plasticity in the small strain regime and has since been object of a large number of contributions, where the authors investigated trace and fine properties
[
6
,
2
,
32
,
43
,
44
,
23
,
5
]
, approximation by means of more regular functions
[
7
,
24
,
18
,
19
,
21
]
, and rigidity estimates
[
20
,
35
,
41
,
40
,
22
]
.

In addition to its applications to elasto-plasticity,
this space is particularly useful in the mathematical modelling of fracture mechanics in the variational formulation of Francfort and Marigo introduced in
[
39
]
. For this application, it is convenient to consider the decomposition of the measure
Eu
introduced in
[
2
]
and given by

(1.1)

Eu=E^{a}u+E^{c}u+E^{j}u,

where
E^{a}u
is the absolutely continuous part of
Eu
with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{d}
, whose density is denoted by
\mathcal{E}u
,
E^{j}u
is the jump part of
Eu
, defined as the restriction of
Eu
to the jump set
J_{u}
of
u
, and the Cantor part
E^{c}u
is the restriction of the singular part of the measure
Eu
to the complement of
J_{u}
in
U
.
It is known that
E^{j}u:=([u]\odot\nu_{u})\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}
, where
[u]:=u^{+}-u^{-}
is the difference of the unilateral traces
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
of
u
on
J_{u}
,
\nu_{u}
is the unit normal to
J_{u}
,
\odot
denotes the symmetrised tensor product,
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
is the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and
\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}
denotes the restriction of a Borel measure to a Borel set. Moreover, it is known that
E^{c}u
is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure and vanishes on all Borel sets with finite
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-measure.

In some
cohesive
fracture models, the approach of
[
39
]
naturally leads to the minimisation of energies of the form (see, for instance,
[
10
, Sections 4.2 and 5.2]
)

(1.2)

F(u,U):=\int_{U}f(x,\mathcal{E}u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\Big(x,\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where the
bulk
energy density
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
is a Borel function with

(1.3)

c_{1}|A|-c_{2}\leq f(x,A)\leq c_{3}|A|+c_{4}\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym},

for some constants
0<c_{1}\leq c_{3}
,
c_{2}\geq 0
, and
c_{4}\geq 0
,

f^{\infty}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
denotes the
recession function
of
f
with respect to
A
, defined by

f^{\infty}(x,A):=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym},

and the
surface
energy density
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
is a Borel function satisfying

(1.4)

c_{1}|\zeta\odot\nu|\leq g(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq c_{3}|\zeta\odot\nu|\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{ and }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

where
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\nu|=1\}
.

In this paper we study the
\Gamma
-limits of sequences of the form (
1.2
) with respect to the topology induced by
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
. The first result in this direction is Theorem
4.1
, which states that for every sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, with
c_{1},\dots,c_{4}
independent of
n
, there exists a subsequence, not relabelled, such that for every bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
the sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to a functional
F(\cdot,U)
defined on
{\rm BD}(U)
.

This limit functional
F
satisfies suitable regularity properties (see Definition
3.4
), the most important being that for every bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
the set function defined for every Borel set
B\subset U
by

B\mapsto F(u,B):=\inf\{F(u,V):V\text{ open},\,B\subset V\subset U\}

is a bounded Radon measure. Let
F^{a}(u,\cdot)
and
F^{s}(u,\cdot)
be the absolutely continuous and singular parts of the measure
F(u,\cdot)
with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
, respectively. In analogy to (
1.1
), we decompose the limit functional
F
as

F(u,B)=F^{a}(u,B)+F^{c}(u,B)+F^{j}(u,B),

where
F^{j}(u,B):=F^{s}(u,B\cap J_{u})
and
F^{c}(u,B):=F^{s}(u,B\setminus J_{u})
. It is possible to check (see Remark
5.2
) that
F^{c}(u,\cdot)
is the absolutely continuous part of
F(u,\cdot)
with respect to
E^{c}u
and that
F^{j}(u,\cdot)
is the absolutely continuous part with respect to
E^{j}u
.

By recent results of Caroccia, Focardi, and Van Goethem
[
17
]
(see also
[
37
]
) it is possible to represent
F^{a}
and
F^{j}
by means of integral functionals in the form

(1.5)

\displaystyle F^{a}(u,B)=\int_{B}f(x,\mathcal{E}u)\,{\rm d}x,

(1.6)

\displaystyle F^{j}(u,B)=\int_{J_{u}\cap B}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where
f
and
g
are Borel functions satisfying (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). As customary in integral representation results for free discontinuity functionals (see, for instance,
[
12
,
9
]
), the functions
f
and
g
are defined by means of limits of auxiliary minimisation problems on small cubes. More precisely, for every bounded open set
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with Lipschitz boundary, and
w\in{\rm BD}(W)
we set

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,W):=\inf\{F(u,W):u\in{\rm BD}(W)\text{ and }u=w\text{ on }\partial W\}.

The integrands
f
and
g
that appear in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) are given by

(1.7)

\displaystyle f(x,A):=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}},

\displaystyle g(x,\zeta,\nu):=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d-1}},

where
\ell_{A}
is the linear function defined by
\ell_{A}(y)=Ay
,
Q(x,\rho)
is the cube with centre
x
, side-length
\rho>0
, and sides parallel to the axes,

u_{x,\zeta,\nu}
is the pure jump function equal to
\zeta
on
\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(y-x)\cdot\nu\geq 0\}
and to
0
on
\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(y-x)\cdot\nu<0\}
, while
Q_{\nu}(x,\rho)
is a cube with centre
x
, side-length
\rho>0
, and two faces orthogonal to
\nu
.

Under suitable additional hypotheses (see Definition
6.4
) on the sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and assuming that (
1.7
) holds with
f
independent of
x
and
\limsup
replaced by
\lim
, we show in Theorem
7.1
that the complete limit functional
F
, including its Cantor part
F^{c}
, can be represented in the form

(1.8)

F(u,B)=\int_{B}f(\mathcal{E}u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{B}f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap B}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

for every
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
, with
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
bounded open set, and every Borel set
B\subset U
. Thanks to (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) the crucial point is to show that

F^{c}(u,B)=\int_{B}f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|.

In Caroccia, Focardi, and Van Goethem
[
17
]
, this is obtained by exploiting the uniform continuity of
F
with respect to horizontal translations, which is one of their key assumptions. However, this hypothesis is not natural for our approach to stochastic homogenisation, so we are forced to use a different technique.
First of all, we use the characterisation of

\frac{{\rm d}F^{c}(u,\cdot)}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}(x)

obtained in
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
, which holds even if
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not uniformly continuous, and then, using the property introduced in Definition
6.4
, we adapt to the
{\rm BD}
setting some arguments used in
[
30
,
26
]
for the
{\rm BV}
case.

Combining the compactness result and the integral representation (
1.8
) we are able to obtain in Theorem
8.3
a characterisation of the integrands of the
\Gamma
-limits by means of limits of minimum values on small cubes. More precisely, assuming that
F_{n}
satisfy the property described in Definition
6.4
uniformly with respect to
n
and that for some functions
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
we have

(1.9)

\displaystyle\hat{f}(A)=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}}=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\hat{g}(x,\zeta,\nu)=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d-1}}=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d-1}}

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, then for every bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we prove that the sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to the functional
F(\cdot,U)
in (
1.8
) with
f=\hat{f}
and
g=\hat{g}
.

We conclude the paper by applying these results to a general class of integral functionals to deduce homogenisation results. More precisely, in Theorems
9.7
and
10.6
we will consider
functionals
F_{n}
given by

(1.11)

\hskip-5.69046ptF_{n}(u,U):=\!\!\int_{U}f\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\mathcal{E}u\Big)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}\hskip-5.69046pt\varepsilon_{n}g\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}[u],\nu_{u}\Big)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

where
f
and
g
satisfy (
1.3
), (
1.4
), and some conditions related to Definition
6.4
(see Definition
6.1
), while
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
is a sequence converging to
0
as
n\to+\infty
.
We remark that, in general the functionals
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
do not coincide with those normally considered in the homogenisation of free discontinuity problems, unless one assumes that
[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto g(x,t\zeta,\nu)
is positively homogeneous of degree one, in which case
F_{n}(u,U)
reads as

(1.12)

\int_{U}f\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\mathcal{E}u\Big)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}g\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},[u],\nu_{u}\Big)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

which is the standard functional considered in the homogenisation problem for free discontinuity functionals (see
[
13
,
15
,
16
,
14
]
).

So far, in the
{\rm BD}
setting our technique allows us to prove the
\Gamma
-convergence of (
1.12
) only when
g
is positively
1
-homogeneous in the variable
\zeta
, while in the
{\rm BV}
setting (see
[
16
,
30
,
26
]
) the
\Gamma
-convergence of the functionals corresponding to (
1.12
) has been proved assuming only that the function
g
is sufficiently close to a
1
-homogeneous function for
|\zeta|
small enough. Unfortunately, this approach heavily relies on sophisticated truncation arguments that we are not able to extend to
{\rm BD}
.

The particular choice of the scaling for the surface term
in (
1.11
)
will allow us to circumvent the use of vertical truncations and will allow us to obtain suitable change of variable formulas used in the proof, which avoids any truncation argument.

A similar problem for bulk energies of the form

\Phi_{n}(u,U):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{U}f\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\mathcal{E}u\Big)\,{\rm d}x&\text{ if }Eu<\!<\mathcal{L}^{d},\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

was studied in
[
17
]
, when
f
is
1
-periodic with respect to
x
and satisfies (
1.3
), and in
[
4
]
in the stochastically periodic case.

To obtain the
\Gamma
-limit of
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
using (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) is convenient to rewrite these formulas using the change of variables
y=\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}
. Setting
v(y):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}u(\varepsilon_{n}y)
, one then checks that

\displaystyle F_{n}(u,Q(x,\rho))=\varepsilon_{n}^{d}\Big(\int_{Q(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{n}})}f(y,\mathcal{E}v)\,{\rm d}y+\int_{Q(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{n}})}f^{\infty}\Big(y,\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}v}{{\rm d}|E^{c}v|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}v|

\displaystyle+\int_{J_{v}}g(y,[v],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\Big),

where we have exploited the scaling chosen for the surface term in (
1.11
). Setting
r_{n}:=\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{n}}
, we have

\frac{1}{\rho^{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))=\frac{1}{r_{n}^{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(r_{n}\tfrac{x}{\rho},r_{n})),

where
F
is given by (
1.2
).
Hence, if there exists the limit

(1.13)

f_{\rm lim}(A):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{d}}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,r_{n}))

and is independent of
x
, condition (
1.9
) is satisfied by
\hat{f}(x,A)=f_{\rm lim}(A)
.

To deal with (
1.10
) we consider
F^{\infty}
the functional obtained by replacing
f
and
g
by
f^{\infty}
and
g^{\infty}
in (
1.2
), where

g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu):=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}

(see Definition
6.1
). Performing a change of variables similar to the one that led to (
1.13
), we obtain that if the limit

(1.14)

g_{\rm lim}(\zeta,\nu):=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{d-1}}\mathfrak{m}^{F^{\infty}}(u_{r_{n}x,\zeta,\nu},Q(x,r_{n}))

exists for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and is independent of
x
, then condition (
1.10
) holds with
\hat{g}(x,\zeta,\nu)=g_{\rm lim}(\zeta,\nu)
.

As
(
1.9
)
and (
1.10
) are sufficient conditions for the
\Gamma
-convergence, we obtain the following result (see Theorem
9.7
): suppose that the limits (
1.13
) and (
1.14
) exist for every
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
,

,

, and are independent of

, then for every bounded open set

the sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

2.
Notation

In this section we present the notation used throughout the paper.

(a)

The space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is endowed with the usual scalar product, denoted by
\cdot
, while the Euclidean norm is denoted by
|\,\,|
. The unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\nu|=1\}
. We also set
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{\pm}:=\{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:\pm\nu_{i(\nu)}>0\}
, where
i(\nu)\in\{1,...,d\}
is the largest index such that
\nu_{i(\nu)}\neq 0
.

(b)

We identify the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with the space of
d\times d
matrices. The subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
of
d\times d
symmetric matrices (resp. antisymmetric) is denoted by
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
(resp.
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm skew}
). If
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
its symmetric part is denoted by
A^{\rm sym}:=\frac{1}{2}\big(A+A^{T}\big)
. For
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the vector
Ax\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is given by the usual matrix by vector multiplication. Given a matrix
A=(A_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, its Frobenius norm is given by

|A|:=\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|A_{ij}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

The identity matrix is denoted by
I
.

(c)

The
i
-th vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
e_{i}
. Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\rho>0
, we consider the cube
Q(x,\rho):=\big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|(y-x)\cdot e_{i}|<\rho/2\,\,\text{ for every }i\in\{1,...,d\}\big\}
.

(d)

SO(d)
is the space of
d\times d
orthonormal matrices
R
with
\text{det}(R)=1
. We fix once and for all a map
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\ni\nu\mapsto R_{\nu}\in SO(d)
satisfying the following properties:
R_{\nu}e_{d}=\nu
and
R_{\nu}(Q(0,\rho))=R_{-\nu}(Q(0,\rho))
for every
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
R_{e_{d}}=I
, and
the restrictions of
\nu\mapsto R_{\nu}
to
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{\pm}
are continuous (for an example of such map see, for instance,
[
15
, Example A.1]
).

(e)

Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and
\rho>0
, we consider the open cube defined by

Q_{\nu}(x,\rho):=x+R_{\nu}\big(Q(0,\rho)\big).

(f)

For every
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
let
\ell_{A}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u_{x,\zeta,\nu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the functions defined for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\ell_{A}(y):=Ay\quad\text{ and }\quad u_{x,\zeta,\nu}(y):=\begin{cases}\zeta&\text{ if }(y-x)\cdot\nu>0,\\
0&\text{ if }(y-x)\cdot\nu\leq 0.\par\end{cases}

(g)

Given an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the collection of all open subsets (resp. Borel) of
U
is denoted by
\mathcal{U}(U)
(resp.
\mathcal{B}(U)
). If
V\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we write
V\subset\subset U
if the closure of
V
is contained in
U
. The collection of all these open sets is denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{c}(U)
.

(h)

Given
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a finite dimensional real normed vector space
X
, the space of all bounded Radon measures with values in
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)
. The indication of
X
is omitted if
X=\mathbb{R}
. Given a positive measure
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(U)
, and a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)
with
\mu<\!<\lambda
, the function
{\rm d}\mu/{\rm d}\lambda
denotes the Radon-Nikodým derivative of
\mu
with respect to
\lambda
, defined as the density of
\mu
with respect to
\lambda
. The total variation measure of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;X)
with respect to the norm
|\,\,|
on
X
, is denoted by
|\mu|
. Given a positive measure
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(U)
and an integrable function
f\colon U\to X
, we denote by
f\lambda
the
X
-valued measure defined for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
by

f\lambda(B):=\int_{B}f\,{\rm d}\lambda.

Given a Borel measure
\mu
on
U
and
E\in\mathcal{B}(U)
, the Borel measure
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E
is defined by
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E(B):=\mu(B\cap E)
for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
.

(i)

Given a function
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the
jump set

J_{u}
of
u
is the set of all points
x\in U
with the following property: there exists a triple
(u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x),\nu_{u}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, with
u^{+}(x)\neq u^{-}(x)
, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\cap H^{\pm}(x)}|u(y)-u^{\pm}(x)|\,{\rm d}y=0,

where
H^{\pm}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\pm(y-x)\cdot\nu_{u}(x)>0\}
. The triple
(u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x),\nu_{u}(x))
is uniquely determined, up to changing the sign of
\nu(x)
and swapping
u^{+}(x)
and
u^{-}(x)
. Having fixed
\nu_{u}
, we set
[u]:=u^{+}-u^{-}
on
J_{u}
. It can be shown that
J_{u}
is a Borel set and that there is a choice of
u^{\pm}
and
\nu_{u}
that makes them Borel measurable on
J_{u}
(see
[
3
, Proposition 3.69]
).
Moreover, Del Nin has recently showed (see
[
34
]
) that
J_{u}
is always countably
(\mathcal{H}^{d-1},d-1)
-rectifiable in the sense of
[
38
, 3.2.14]
.

(j)

Given
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, a function
u\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be of
bounded deformation
if
u\in L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and its
distributional symmetric gradient

Eu:=\frac{1}{2}(Du+Du^{T})
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{b}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})
. The collection of all functions of bounded deformation on
U
is denoted by
{\rm BD}(U)
, while
{\rm BD}_{\rm loc}(U)
is the collection of all
\mathcal{L}^{d}
-measurable functions
u\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
u|_{V}\in{\rm BD}(V)
for every
V\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(U)
.
We refer the reader to
[
49
]
for an introduction to this space and to
[
2
]
for the fine properties of its functions.

(k)

Given
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
, the measure
Eu
can be decomposed as

(2.1)

Eu=E^{a}u+E^{c}u+E^{j}u=\mathcal{E}u\mathcal{L}^{d}+E^{c}u+[u]\odot\nu_{u}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u},

where:

(i) the
absolutely continuous part

E^{a}u
is the absolutely continuous part of
Eu
with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
, whose density is denoted by
\mathcal{E}u\in L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})
,

(ii) the
Cantor part

E^{c}u
is defined as
E^{c}u:=E^{s}u\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(\Omega\setminus J_{u})
, where
E^{s}u
is the singular part of
Eu
with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
; it is known that
E^{c}u
vanishes on all
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
which are
\sigma
-finite with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
,

(iii) the
jump part

E^{j}u
is defined by
E^{j}u:=Eu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}=E^{s}u\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}
; it is known that
E^{j}u=[u]\odot\nu_{u}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}
, where
\odot
denotes the symmetric tensor product defined by
(a\odot b)_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{i}b_{j}+a_{j}b_{i})
for every
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(l)

A function
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be a
rigid motion
if there exist
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm skew}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
u(x)=Ax+b
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The collection of all rigid motions is denoted by
\mathcal{R}
.

3.
The integrands and the functionals

In this section we introduce the collections of integrands and of functionals that are going to be our main objects of study.

We fix non-negative constants
c_{1},...,c_{5}\in[0,+\infty)
, with
0<c_{1}\leq c_{3}
, and a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
We also fix a continuous function
\sigma\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
with the property that
\sigma(0)=0
and

(3.1)

\sigma^{\infty}:=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\sigma(t)}{t}<+\infty.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the collection of all integrands
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfying the following conditions:

(f1)

f
is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
;

(f2)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
we have

c_{1}|A|-c_{2}\leq f(x,A)\leq c_{3}|A|+c_{4};

(f3)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
we have

|f(x,A_{1})-f(x,A_{2})|\leq c_{5}|A_{1}-A_{2}|.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the collection of all integrands
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfying the following conditions:

(g1)

g
is Borel measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
;

(g2)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have

g(x,\zeta,\nu)=g(x,-\zeta,-\nu);

(g3)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have

c_{1}|\zeta\odot\nu|\leq g(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq c_{3}|\zeta\odot\nu|;

(g4)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have

|g(x,\zeta_{1},\nu)-g(x,\zeta_{2},\nu)|\leq\sigma(|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|).

To every function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
we associate its
recession function

f^{\infty}
(with respect to the variable
A
), defined by

(3.2)

f^{\infty}(x,A):=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}\quad\text{ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}$}.

Remark 3.2
.

Given
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
satisfying (f2) and (f3), one can check that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A,A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
the function
f^{\infty}
satisfies

(3.3)

\displaystyle c_{1}|A|\leq f^{\infty}(x,A)\leq c_{3}|A|,

\displaystyle|f^{\infty}(x,A_{1})-f^{\infty}(x,A_{2})|\leq c_{5}|A_{1}-A_{2}|.

We now introduce a class of integral functionals associated with
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Given
f\in\mathcal{F}
and

g\in\mathcal{G}
, for every open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
we set

(3.4)

F^{f,g}(u,U):=\int_{U}f(x,\mathcal{E}u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\Big(x,\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

if
u|_{U}\in{\rm BD}_{\rm loc}(U)
, while
F^{f,g}(u,U)=+\infty
otherwise. The definition is then extended to every Borel set
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by setting

(3.5)

F^{f,g}(u,B):=\inf\{F^{f,g}(u,U):U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ open, with }U\supset B\}.

Note that, if
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded open set,
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
,
u|_{U}\in{\rm BD}_{\rm loc}(U)
, and
|Eu|(U)<+\infty
, then

(3.6)

F^{f,g}(u,B)=\int_{B}f(x,\mathcal{E}u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{B}f^{\infty}\Big(x,\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap B}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.

Indeed, in this case the upper bounds in (f2), (
3.3
), and (g3) imply

\displaystyle\int_{U}f(x,\mathcal{E}u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\Big(x,\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}<+\infty,

so that (
3.6
) follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).

We now introduce a collection of abstract functionals related to the integral functionals considered above.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be the collection of functionals
F\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,+\infty]
with the following properties:

(a)

measure property
: for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set function
F(u,\cdot)
is a Borel measure that for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies

(3.7)

F(u,B):=\inf\{F(u,U):U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ open},\,U\supset B\};

(b)

locality on open sets:
for every bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u,v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
u=v

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e on
U
we have
F(u,U)=F(v,U)
;

(c)

upper and lower bounds:
if
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded open set, then

c_{1}|Eu|(U)-c_{2}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)\leq F(u,U)\leq c_{3}|Eu|(U)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)\quad\text{if $u|_{U}\in{\rm BD}(U)$},

while
F(u,U)=+\infty
if
u|_{U}\notin{\rm BD}(U);

(d)

invariance under rigid motions:
for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
w\in\mathcal{R}
, and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

F(u+w,B)=F(u,B).

(e)

bulk continuity estimate:
for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
, and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

F(u+\ell_{A},B)\leq F(u,B)+c_{5}|A|\mathcal{L}^{d}(B);

(f)

surface continuity estimate:
for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

F(u+u_{x,\zeta,\nu},B)\leq F(u,B)+\sigma(|\zeta|)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(B\cap\Pi^{\nu}_{x}),

where
\Pi^{\nu}_{x}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(y-x)\cdot\nu=0\}
is the hyperplane orthogonal to
\nu
containing
x
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
the collection of functionals
F\in\mathfrak{F}
such that
for every open
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
the functional
F(\cdot,U)
is
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
-lower semicontinuous.

Remark 3.5
.

Combining properties (a) and (b) we obtain that the functionals in
\mathfrak{F}
satisfy the following locality property: if
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
u,v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
u=v

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. on a neighbourhood of
B
, then
F(u,B)=F(v,B)
. Easy examples show that, in general, this equality does not hold if we have only
u=v

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. on
B
.

For this reason, if
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open set and
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
we can define
F(u,B):=F(v,B)
, where
v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
is any function such that
v=u

\mathcal{L}^{d}
-a.e. on
U
. The locality property described above implies that the value of
F(u,B)
does not depend on the chosen extension
v
of
u
.

Remark 3.6
.

From (
3.7
) and (c) we deduce the following inequalities for Borel sets. If
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded open set and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
, then for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
we have

c_{1}|Eu|(B)-c_{2}\mathcal{L}^{d}(B)\leq F(u,B)\leq c_{3}|Eu|(B)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(B).

Remark 3.7
.

Thanks to property (a) a functional
F\in\mathfrak{F}
belongs to
\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
if and only if for every open bounded set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
the functional
F(\cdot,U)
is
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
-lower semicontinuous.

We now show that the functional introduced in Definition
3.3
belongs to
\mathfrak{F}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Then the functional
F^{f,g}
introduced in Definition
3.3
belongs to
\mathfrak{F}
.

Proof.

By (
3.5
)
F^{f,g}
satisfies (
3.7
), while the rest of property (a) is obvious when
u\in{\rm BD}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The general case
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be obtained by a straightforward argument; it can also be treated using the De Giorgi-Letta criterion for measures (see Lemma
4.4
below). Property (b) is obvious. Property (c) follows from (f2), (
3.3
), and (g3). To see that property (d) is satisfied by
F^{f,g}
is enough to observe that for every
w\in\mathcal{R}
we have
Ew=0
.

To see that
F^{f,g}
satisfies property (e) we observe that by (f3) for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
we have

|F^{f,g}(u+\ell_{A},B)-F^{f,g}(u,B)|\leq c_{5}|A|\mathcal{L}^{d}(B).

A similar argument shows that
F^{f,g}
satisfies property (f).
∎

4.
A compactness result for
\mathfrak{F}

In this section we state and prove a compactness result for sequences of functionals in
\mathfrak{F}
. The proof is based on the classical localisation method for
\Gamma
-convergence (see
[
11
, Section 3.3]
or
[
25
, Chapter 18]
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{F}
. Then there exist a functional
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
and a subsequence, not relabelled, such that for every bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
the sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to
F(\cdot,U)
in the topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

For every open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we consider the functionals defined on
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\cdot,U)=\Gamma\text{-}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}F_{n}(\cdot,U)\quad\text{ and }\quad F^{\prime\prime}(\cdot,U)=\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{n}(\cdot,U),

(4.2)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{-}(\cdot,U)=\sup_{U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(U)}F^{\prime}(\cdot,U^{\prime})\quad\text{ and }\quad F^{\prime\prime}_{-}(\cdot,U)=\sup_{U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(U)}F^{\prime\prime}(\cdot,U^{\prime}),

where the
\Gamma
-liminf and
\Gamma
-limsup are computed with respect to the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
-convergence and
\mathcal{U}_{c}(U)
is the collection of open sets defined in (g) of Section
2
.

It is immediate to check that for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set functions
F_{n}(u,\cdot)
,
F^{\prime}(u,\cdot)
,
F^{\prime\prime}(u,\cdot)
,
F^{\prime}_{-}(u,\cdot)
, and
F^{\prime\prime}_{-}(u,\cdot)
are increasing. Thus, we can apply the compactness theorem for sequences of increasing set functionals
[
25
, Theorem 16.9]
to obtain that there exist a subsequence of
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, not relabelled, and a functional
F\colon L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(4.3)

F(u,U)=F^{\prime}_{-}(u,U)=F^{\prime\prime}_{-}(u,U)\quad\text{for every }u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ and }U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

For every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we set

(4.4)

F(u,B):=\inf\{F(u,U):U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ with }U\supset B\}.

We want to prove that
F\in\mathfrak{F}
. The approximation property (
3.7
) is obvious, while the measure property (a) will be proved in Lemma
4.4
below. The locality property (b) and the lower semicontinuity of
F
follow from general results about
\Gamma
-limits of local functionals (see
[
25
, Proposition 16.15 and Remark 16.3]
).

We note that it is enough to prove properties (d)-(f) for
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Indeed, by the measure property (a) they can be extended to
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
using the inner regularity of Borel measures. The further extension to Borel sets follows immediately from (
4.4
).

Let us fix
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We begin by proving the upper and lower bounds in (c). We consider first the case
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
. Under this hypothesis, the inequality

(4.5)

F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)\leq c_{3}|Eu|(U)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)

is a consequence of the corresponding inequality for
F_{n}
and of the fact that the
\Gamma
-limsup is less than or equal to the pointwise limsup. The upper bound in (c) for
F
follows immediately from (
4.2
), (
4.3
), and (
4.5
). To prove the lower bound for
F^{\prime\prime}
, we observe that our assumption
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
implies that
F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)<+\infty
. Given
U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(U)
, we fix a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})

converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\limsup_{n}F_{n}(u_{n},U^{\prime})=F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime})<+\infty
. Since for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the functional
F_{n}
satisfies (c) we have
u_{n}\in{\rm BD}(U^{\prime})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough and

\displaystyle c_{1}|E{u_{n}}|(U^{\prime})-c_{2}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U^{\prime})\leq F_{n}(u_{n},U^{\prime}).

From the lower semicontinuity of
v\mapsto|Ev|(U^{\prime})
with respect to the
L^{1}
-convergence and from the previous inequality we obtain

c_{1}|Eu|(U^{\prime})-c_{2}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U^{\prime})\leq F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime}).

Using again (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we deduce the corresponding lower bound for
F(u,U)
.

Assume now that
u\notin{\rm BD}(U)
. We want to prove that
F(u,U)=+\infty
. Assume by contradiction that
F(u,U)<+\infty
. Given
U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(U)
, we consider a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\limsup_{n}F_{n}(u_{n},U^{\prime})=F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime})\leq F(u,U)<+\infty
. Arguing as before, we obtain that
u_{n}\in{\rm BD}(U^{\prime})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough and

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}|Eu_{n}|(U^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{n}(u_{n},U^{\prime})+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U^{\prime})\leq\frac{F(u,U)}{c_{1}}+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U),

which implies
u\in{\rm BD}(U^{\prime})
and
|Eu|(U^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}{F(u,U)}+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)
. As this holds for every
U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(U)
, we obtain
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
, contradicting our hypothesis. This concludes the proof of the implication
u\notin{\rm BD}(U)\implies F(u,U)=+\infty
.

Properties (d)-(f) for
F
follow immediately from the same properties for
F_{n}
.

In Lemma
4.5
we will prove that for every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

(4.6)

F(u,U)=F^{\prime}(u,U)=F^{\prime\prime}(u,U),

hence for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to
F(\cdot,U)
in the topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

In the rest of this section we assume that
F^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime\prime}
are defined by (
4.1
) and that
F
satisfies (
4.3
). Our aim is to prove equality (
4.6
) and the measure property (a) of Definition
3.4
for this functional. The proof of the measure property (a) is based on the De Giorgi-Letta criterion for measures, which is based on the subadditivity of the function
U\mapsto F(u,U)
. For this reason,
we begin by proving that
F^{\prime\prime}
satisfies the
nested
subadditivity condition.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
U^{\prime},U,V\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
U^{\prime}\subset\subset U
. Then

(4.7)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime}\cup V)\leq F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)+F^{\prime\prime}(u,V).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)+F^{\prime\prime}(u,V)<+\infty
. Since
F^{\prime\prime}\geq F
and
F
satisfies property (c), this implies that
u\in{\rm BD}(U\cup V)
.
Recalling the definition of
F^{\prime\prime}
(see (
4.1
)), we can find two sequences
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and such that

(4.8)

F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{n}(u_{n},U)\quad\text{ and }\quad F^{\prime\prime}(u,V)=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{n}(v_{n},V).

Thanks to property (c) for
F_{n}
and the finiteness of
F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)
and
F^{\prime\prime}(u,V)
we can assume that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\rm BD}(U)
,
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\rm BD}(V)
, and that

(4.9)

\displaystyle M:=c_{3}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(|E{u_{n}}|(U)+|E{v_{n}}|(V)\Big)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\cup V)<+\infty.

Let us fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and set
\eta:=\text{dist}(U^{\prime},\partial U)>0
. For
j\in\{0,...,m\}
we also consider the sets
U_{j}:=\{x\in U:\text{dist}(x,\partial U)>\eta-\frac{j\eta}{m}\}
. Clearly, we have
U^{\prime}\subset U_{0}\subset\subset\dots\subset\subset U_{m-1}\subset U_{m}=U
.

For every
j\in\{1,...,m\}
we consider a function
\varphi_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d};[0,1])
satisfying
\varphi_{j}=1
on a neighbourhood of
\overline{U}_{j-1}
,
\varphi_{j}=0
on a neighbourhood of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U_{j}
, and

(4.10)

|\nabla\varphi_{j}|\leq\frac{2m}{\eta}\quad\text{on $\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

We set

(4.11)

w^{j}_{n}:=\varphi_{j}{u}_{n}+(1-\varphi_{j}){v}_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{j}:=(U_{j}\setminus\overline{U}_{j-1})\cap V,

and observe that
w_{n}^{j}\in{\rm BD}(U^{\prime}\cup V)
and

(4.12)

E{w^{j}_{n}}=\varphi_{j}E{{u}_{n}}+(1-\varphi_{j})E{{v}_{n}}+({u}_{n}-v_{n})\odot\nabla\varphi_{j}\,\mathcal{L}^{d}

as Radon measures on
U^{\prime}\cup V
. Moreover, one checks immediately that for every choice of
j_{n}\in\{1,...,m\}
the sequence
\{w^{j_{n}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
n\to+\infty
.

Let us fix
j\in\{1,...,m\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We observe that by the measure property (a), by the properties of
\varphi_{j}
, and by the locality property of Remark
3.5
we have

\displaystyle F_{n}(w^{j}_{n},U^{\prime}\cup V)\leq F_{n}(w^{j}_{n},\overline{U}_{j-1})+F_{n}(w^{j}_{n},S_{j})+F_{n}(w^{j}_{n},V\setminus{U}_{j})

(4.13)

\displaystyle=F_{n}(u_{n},\overline{U}_{j-1})+F_{n}(w^{j}_{n},S_{j})+F_{n}(v_{n},V\setminus{U}_{j})\leq F_{n}(u_{n},U)+F_{n}(w^{j}_{n},S_{j})+F_{n}(v_{n},V).

We now estimate the term involving the strip
S_{j}
in (
4.13
).

\displaystyle F_{n}(w_{n}^{j},S_{j})\leq c_{3}|Ew^{j}_{n}|(S_{j})+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(S_{j}),

and by (
4.10
) and (
4.12
) we get

\displaystyle|E{w^{j}_{n}}|(S_{j})\leq|E{u_{n}}|(S_{j})+|E{v_{n}}|(S_{j})+\frac{2m}{\eta}\int_{S_{j}}|u_{n}-v_{n}|\,{\rm d}x.

From these two inequalities, we deduce that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can find
j_{n}\in\{1,..,m\}
such that, setting
w_{n}:=w^{j_{n}}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle F_{n}(w_{n},S_{j_{n}})\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Big(c_{3}|E{u_{n}}|(S_{j})+c_{3}|E{v_{n}}|(S_{j})+\frac{2c_{3}m}{\eta}\int_{S_{j}}|u_{n}-v_{n}|\,{\rm d}x+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(S_{j})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\big(c_{3}|Eu_{n}|(U)+c_{3}|Ev_{n}|(V)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\cup V)\big)+\frac{2c_{3}}{\eta}\int_{U\cap V}|u_{n}-v_{n}|\,{\rm d}x

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{m}+\frac{2c_{3}}{\eta}\int_{U\cap V}|u_{n}-v_{n}|\,{\rm d}x,

where in the second inequality we used (
4.9
).

Finally, combining (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) we deduce that

(4.15)

\displaystyle F_{n}(w_{n},U^{\prime}\cup V)

\displaystyle\leq F_{n}(u_{n},U)+F_{n}(v_{n},V)+\frac{M}{m}+\frac{2c_{3}}{\eta}\int_{U\cap V}|u_{n}-v_{n}|\,{\rm d}x

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Recalling that
\{u_{n}-v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
0
strongly in
L^{1}(U\cap V;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that
\{w_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we may let
n\to+\infty
in (
4.15
) and from (
4.8
) we deduce that

F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime}\cup V)\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}F_{n}(w_{n},U^{\prime}\cup V)\leq F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)+F^{\prime\prime}(u,V)+\frac{M}{m}.

Letting
m\to+\infty
in this inequality, we obtain (
4.7
).
∎

We are now ready to prove the subadditivity of
F(u,\cdot)
on
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
U,V\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

F(u,U\cup V)\leq F(u,U)+F(u,V).

Proof.

The result is a consequence of Lemma
4.2
and of classical arguments concerning increasing set functions (see, for instance,
[
25
, Lemma 18.4]
).
∎

We now prove that
F
satisfies the measure property (a).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
F(u,\cdot)
is a Borel measure.

Proof.

It is enough to use the De Giorgi-Letta criterion for measures (see
[
31
, Théorème 5.1]
or
[
25
, Theorem 14.21]
), which ensures that the claim is proved, provided that we show that the set function
U\mapsto F(u,U)
is subadditive, superadditive, and inner regular on
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Subadditivity follows from Lemma
4.3
, superadditivity is a consequence of classic results of
\Gamma
-convergence (see
[
25
, Proposition 16.12]
), and inner regularity follows from (
4.3
), recalling (
4.2
).
∎

Finally, we conclude the section by showing that
\Gamma
-convergence takes place for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

F(u,U)=F^{\prime}(u,U)=F^{\prime\prime}(u,U).

Proof.

Since the inequalities
F(u,U)\leq F^{\prime}(u,U)\leq F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)
are obvious, we only have to show that
F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)\leq F(u,U)
, assuming that
F(u,U)<+\infty
. By property (c) for the functional
F
, this implies that

u\in{\rm BD}(U)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and consider a compact set
K\subset U
such that

c_{3}|Eu|(U\setminus K)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus K)<\varepsilon.

Consider now
U^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
K\subset U^{\prime}\subset\subset U
. Using Lemma
4.2
with
V=U\setminus K
, from the previous inequality and (
4.5
) we obtain

F^{\prime\prime}(u,U)\leq F^{\prime\prime}(u,U^{\prime})+F^{\prime\prime}(u,U\setminus K)<F(u,U)+\varepsilon.

The conclusion then follows from the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
.
∎

5.
A partial integral
representation for abstract functionals

In this section we use the results proved in
[
17
]
to provide a partial integral representation of every functional
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
. More precisely, we show that the “absolutely continuous” part and the “jump” part of every such
F
can be written as integrals associated to suitable integrands
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

We begin by introducing a decomposition of functionals in
\mathfrak{F}
that reflects the usual decomposition of the measure
Eu
described in (
2.1
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
,
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
. By (a) and (c) of Definition
3.4

F(u,\cdot)
is a bounded Radon measure on
U
. Its absolutely continuous and the singular part with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{d}
, defined on every
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
, are denoted by
F^{a}(u,B)
and
F^{s}(u,B)
, respectively. We also introduce the bounded Radon measures
F^{c}(u,\cdot)
and
F^{j}(u,\cdot)
defined by

\displaystyle F^{c}(u,B):=F^{s}(u,B\setminus J_{u})\text{ and }F^{j}(u,B):=F(u,B\cap J_{u})

for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
.
Note that we have

(5.1)

F(u,B)=F^{a}(u,B)+F^{c}(u,B)+F^{j}(u,B)

for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
,
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
. It follows directly from the bounds in (c) of Definition
3.4
that
F^{c}(u,\cdot)
and
F^{j}(u,\cdot)
, considered as measures defined on
\mathcal{B}(U)
, are absolutely continuous with respect to
|E^{c}u|
and
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J_{u}
, respectively.

In the rest of the paper, we will often work with functions defined by using auxiliary minimisation problems. For this reason, given a functional
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, a set
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial U
, and a function
v\in{\rm BD}(U)
, we set

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(v,U):=\inf\{F(u,U):u\in{\rm BD}(U)\text{ and }u=v\text{ on }\partial U\},

where the equality
u=v
is understood in the sense of traces. As
v
is a competitor for the minimisation problem
\mathfrak{m}^{F}(v,U)
, by the upper bounds in Definition
3.4
it follows that

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(v,U)\leq F(v,U)\leq c_{3}|Ev|(U)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)<+\infty.

Given
F\in\mathfrak{F}
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we set

(5.2)

\displaystyle f(x,A):=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}},

(5.3)

\displaystyle g(x,\zeta,\nu):=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d-1}},

where
Q(x,\rho)
and
Q_{\nu}(x,\rho)
are the cubes defined in (c) and (e) of Section
2
, while the functions
\ell_{A}
and
u_{x,\zeta,\nu}
are those introduced in (f) of the same section.

We now state the partial integral representation result, whose proof is given in
[
17
, Lemma 5.1]
, following the lines of
[
9
]
and of
[
37
]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
,
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
.
Let
f
and
g
be the two functions defined by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
). Then
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
, and

(5.4)

\displaystyle F^{a}(u,B)=\int_{B}f(x,\mathcal{E}u)\,{\rm d}x,

(5.5)

\displaystyle F^{j}(u,B)=\int_{J_{u}\cap B}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

for every set
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
.

Proof.

The proof of equalities (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) can be found in
[
17
, Lemma 5.1]
. The inclusions
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}
are proved in Lemmas
5.6
and
5.8
below.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

We remark that properties (e) and (f) of Definition
3.4
are not used to obtain (
5.4
) and (
5.5
). Properties (e) and (f) are used only to prove that
f
and
g
satisfy (f3) and (g4).

To prove that
f
is Borel measurable we will need the following lemma, which will be used also in Section
9
for different purposes.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
,
U^{\prime},U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with Lipschitz boundary and
U^{\prime}\subset\subset U
, and let
w\in{\rm BD}(U)
. Then

(5.6)

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U^{\prime})+c_{3}|Ew|(U\setminus U^{\prime})+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus U^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
\eta>0
and
u\in{\rm BD}(U^{\prime})
with
u=w
on
\partial U^{\prime}
be such that

(5.7)

F(u,U^{\prime})\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U^{\prime})+\eta.

We can then extend
u
to
U
by setting
u=w
on
U\setminus U^{\prime}
, so that
u=w
on
\partial U
, which implies

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)\leq F(u,U).

Using properties (a) and (c) of Definition
3.4
we get

(5.8)

F(u,U)=F(u,U^{\prime})+F(u,U\setminus U^{\prime})\leq F(u,U^{\prime})+c_{3}|Eu|(U\setminus\overline{U}{{}^{\prime}})+c_{3}|Eu|(\partial U^{\prime})+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus U^{\prime}).

Since
u=w
on the open set
U\setminus\overline{U}{{}^{\prime}}
, we have
Eu=Ew
as measures defined on
\mathcal{B}(U\setminus\overline{U}{{}^{\prime}})
, hence
|Eu|(U\setminus\overline{U}{{}^{\prime}})=|Ew|(U\setminus\overline{U}{{}^{\prime}})
. Moreover, since
|Eu|\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial U^{\prime}=|(u^{+}-u^{-})\odot\nu_{\partial U^{\prime}}|\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(J_{u}\cap\partial U^{\prime})
and
|Ew|\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\partial U^{\prime}=|(w^{+}-w^{-})\odot\nu_{\partial U^{\prime}}|\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(J_{w}\cap\partial U^{\prime})
, the equalities
u^{+}=w^{+}
and
u^{-}=w^{-}
on
\partial U^{\prime}
imply that
|Eu|(\partial U^{\prime})=|Ew|(\partial U^{\prime})
.
Combining this equality with (
5.7
)-(
5.8
) we get

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U^{\prime})+c_{3}|Ew|(U\setminus\overline{U}{{}^{\prime}})+c_{3}|Ew|(\partial U^{\prime})+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus U^{\prime})+\eta,

which, by arbitrariness of
\eta
, implies (
5.6
).
∎

We are ready to prove that
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
. Then the function
f
defined by (
5.2
) belongs to
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

The proof of property (f1) can be obtained following the lines of the proof of
[
29
, Lemma 5.3]
, replacing the Lemma 4.11 of that paper by our Lemma
5.5
.

Let us fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
. We want to show that

(5.9)

f(x,A)\leq c_{3}|A|+c_{4}.

To this aim, we note that for every
\rho>0
from the upper bound in (c) of Definition
3.4
and the fact that
\ell_{A}
is a competitor for the minimisation problem
\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))
it follows that

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))\leq F(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))\leq(c_{3}|A|+c_{4})\rho^{d}.

We now divide by
\rho^{d}
, take the limsup as
\rho\to 0^{+}
, and use (
5.2
) to obtain (
5.9
).

To conclude the proof of (f2) we show that

(5.10)

c_{1}|A|-c_{2}\leq f(x,A).

To prove this, let us fix
\rho>0
and observe that the lower bound in (c) of Definition
3.4
we have

c_{1}|Eu|(Q(x,\rho))-c_{2}\rho^{d}\leq F(u,Q(x,\rho))

for every
u\in{\rm BD}(Q(x,\rho))
, so that

c_{1}\min\{|Eu|(Q(x,\rho)):u\in{\rm BD}(Q(x,\rho))\text{ with }u=\ell_{A}\text{ on }\partial Q(x,\rho)\}-c_{2}\rho^{d}\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho)).

By Jensen’s inequality the function
\ell_{A}
minimises the problem on the left-hand side of the previous inequality. Hence,

c_{1}|A|\rho^{d}-c_{2}\rho^{d}\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho)).

Dividing by
\rho^{d}
, taking the limsup
as
\rho\to 0^{+}
, and recalling (
5.2
), we obtain (
5.10
).

We now prove (f3). To this aim, let us fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
,
\rho>0
, and
\eta>0
.
Consider a function
u_{2}\in{\rm BD}(Q(x,\rho))
with
u_{2}=\ell_{A_{2}}
on
\partial Q(x,\rho)
and such that

F(u_{2},Q(x,\rho))\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A_{2}},Q(x,\rho))+\eta\rho^{d}.

Then, we set
u_{1}:=u_{2}-\ell_{A_{2}}+\ell_{A_{1}}
and observe that
u_{1}=\ell_{A_{1}}
on
\partial Q(x,\rho)
. Using property (e) of Definition
3.4
and the previous inequality, it follows that

(5.11)

\begin{gathered}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A_{1}},Q(x,\rho))\leq F(u_{1},Q(x,\rho))\leq F(u_{2},Q(x,\rho))+c_{5}|A_{1}-A_{2}|\rho^{d}\\
\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A_{2}},Q(x,\rho))+c_{5}|A_{1}-A_{2}|\rho^{d}+\eta\rho^{d}.\end{gathered}

Dividing by
\rho^{d}
, taking first the limsup as
\rho\to 0^{+}
and then as
\eta\to 0^{+}
, by (
5.2
) we obtain

f(x,A_{1})\leq f(x,A_{2})+c_{5}|A_{1}-A_{2}|.

Exchanging the roles of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, we obtain (f3).
∎

To prove that
g
is Borel measurable we will need the following lemma, which will be used also in Section
9
for different purposes.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
. Then there exists a continuous function
\omega\colon[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
, increasing with respect to each variable and with
\omega(0,0)=0
, such that for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
, the inclusion
Q_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1},\rho_{1})\subset\subset Q_{\nu_{2}}(x_{2},\rho_{2})
implies

\begin{gathered}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(u_{x_{2},\zeta,\nu_{2}},Q_{\nu_{2}}(x_{2},\rho_{2}))\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(u_{x_{1},\zeta,\nu_{1}},Q_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1},\rho_{1}))+c_{3}|\zeta|(\rho^{d-1}_{2}-\rho^{d-1}_{1})\\
{}+c_{4}(\rho^{d}_{2}-\rho^{d}_{1})+c_{3}|\zeta|\omega(\tfrac{|x_{2}-x_{1}|}{\rho_{1}},|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}|)\rho^{d-1}_{1}.\end{gathered}

Proof.

The proof is obtained by the same argument used in the proof of

∎

Let

A smaller collection of functionals

In this section we introduce two subcollections of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
, denoted by
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}
, by prescribing suitable conditions on the integrands, related to their behaviour when

and

(see Remarks

We then define a subcollection of

) and includes the functionals

with

and

In the rest of the paper we fix three constants

and

. We are ready to introduce the collections
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
be the collection of all functions
f\in\mathcal{F}
such that
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
, and

(f4)

\big|\frac{f(x,sA)}{s}-\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}\big|\leq\frac{c_{6}}{s}f(x,sA)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}f(x,tA)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}.

Let
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}
be the collection of all functions
g\in\mathcal{G}
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and
s,t>0
we have

(g5)

\big|\frac{g(x,s\zeta,\nu)}{s}-\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}\big|\leq c_{7}\Big(\frac{g(x,s\zeta,\nu)}{s}+\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).

Remark 6.2
.

Property (
f4
) can be interpreted as a condition specifying the rate at which
f(x,tA)/t
approaches its recession function
f^{\infty}(x,A)
as
t\to+\infty
. Indeed (see
[
16
, Remark 3.4]
), the upper bound in (f2) and (

), are equivalent to the two conditions

(6.1)

\displaystyle f^{\infty}(x,A)=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}\quad\text{ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and }A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym},

(6.2)

\displaystyle\big|\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}-{f^{\infty}(x,A)}\big|\leq\frac{c_{6}}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}f(x,tA)^{1-\alpha}\quad\text{ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $t>0$, and }A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}.

Observe that by the growth conditions in (f2), inequality (
6.2
) implies that

(6.3)

\big|\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}-{f^{\infty}(x,A)}\big|\leq\frac{C_{A}}{t^{\alpha}}\quad\text{ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $t\geq 1$, and $A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}$,}

where

. Conditions similar to (

) and to (

[

, Property (H4)]
and
[

,
30
,
26
,
45
]
).

Remark 6.3
.

If
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\infty}
, then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
there exists the limit

(6.4)

g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu):=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}.

Indeed, (g3) and (
g5
) imply the Cauchy condition for the function

. Passing to the limit in (g3) we obtain

(6.5)

c_{1}|\zeta\odot\nu|\leq g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu)\leq c_{3}|\zeta\odot\nu|

for every

,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
while passing to the limit in (g4) and using (
3.1
) we get

(g4
′
)

|g^{\infty}(x,\zeta_{1},\nu)-g^{\infty}(x,\zeta_{2},\nu)|\leq\sigma^{\infty}|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|

for every

, and

, where

).

Letting

in (g5), we also obtain

(6.6)

\big|\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}-g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu)\big|\leq c_{7}\Big(\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}+g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu)\Big)\frac{1}{t}

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and

.
Thanks to the bounds in (g3) and (
6.5
), this inequality implies that

(6.7)

\big|\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}-g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu)\big|\leq\frac{2c_{3}c_{7}|\zeta\odot\nu|}{t},

while the inequality

\big|\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}-g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu)\big|\leq\frac{2c_{1}c_{7}|\zeta\odot\nu|}{t}

implies (
6.6
).

Conversely, assume that
g
is a function in
\mathcal{G}
such that the limit in (

) exists and the inequality

(6.8)

,

,

, and

. If

. Indeed, by (

\big|\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}-g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu)\big|\leq c_{7}\Big(\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}+\frac{g(x,s\zeta,\nu)}{s}\Big)\frac{1}{t}

,

,

, and

. Exchanging the roles of

and

\big|\frac{g(x,s\zeta,\nu)}{s}-g^{\infty}(x,\zeta,\nu)\big|\leq c_{7}\Big(\frac{g(x,s\zeta,\nu)}{s}+\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}\Big)\frac{1}{s}.

Using the triangle inequality, from (

Note that the existence of the limit in (

) and inequality (

We now introduce a collection of functionals closely related to conditions (

) and (

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
be the collection of all functionals

\begin{gathered}\big|\frac{F(su,U)}{s}-\frac{F(tu,U)}{t}\big|\leq\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(su,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)\\
+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(tu,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\Big(\frac{F(su,U)}{s}+\frac{F(tu,U)}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).\end{gathered}

The collection of functionals

such that
for every

We now show that integral functionals of the form

whenever

and

.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\infty}
. Then the functional
F^{f,g}
defined by (
3.3
) belongs to
\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
.

Proof.

Thanks to Proposition
3.8
, we only need to prove that
F^{f,g}
satisfies property (
g
). To this aim, let us fix
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
, and
s,t>0
. Using (
f4
) and Hölder’s inequality we see that

\displaystyle\big|\int_{U}\frac{f(x,s\mathcal{E}u)}{s}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{U}\frac{f(x,t\mathcal{E}u)}{t}\,{\rm d}x\big|\leq\int_{U}\Big(\frac{c_{6}}{s}f(x,s\mathcal{E}u)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}f(x,t\mathcal{E}u)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\Big){\rm d}x

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(su,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(tu,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U).

Since
f^{\infty}
is positively
1
-homogeneous, we immediately see that

(6.12)

\frac{1}{s}\int_{U}{}f^{\infty}\Big(x,s\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|=\frac{1}{t}\int_{U}{}f^{\infty}\Big(x,t\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|.

Condition (g5) implies that

(6.13)

\begin{gathered}\hskip-85.35826pt\big|\int_{J_{u}\cap U}\Big(\frac{g(x,s[u],\nu_{u})}{s}-\frac{g(x,t[u],\nu_{u})}{t}\Big){\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\big|\\
\leq c_{7}\Big(\int_{J_{u}\cap U}\frac{g(x,s[u],\nu_{u})}{s}\,\mathcal{H}^{d-1}+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}\frac{g(x,t[u],\nu_{u})}{t}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\\
\leq c_{7}\Big(\frac{F(su,U)}{s}+\frac{F(tu,U)}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).\end{gathered}

Finally, combining (
6.11
)-(

) we obtain that

satisfies property (

).
∎

We now prove that the closure of the class
\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
with respect to
\Gamma
-convergence is
\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}_{\rm sc}
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
. Assume that for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to
F(\cdot,U)
with respect to the topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}_{\rm sc}
.

Proof.

Thanks to Theorem
4.1
we have
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
. Hence, to conclude we only have to show that
F
satisfies property (
g
). The argument we use is a variant of the one presented in
[
26
, Proposition 6.11]
and
[
30
, Theorem 4.10]
.

Let us fix
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
, and
s,t>0
. To prove that (
g
) holds it is enough to show

(6.14)

\begin{gathered}\frac{F(su,U)}{s}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(su,U)^{1-\alpha}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)-c_{7}\frac{F(su,U)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\\
\quad\leq\frac{F(tu,U)}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(tu,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\frac{F(tu,U)}{t}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).\end{gathered}

Indeed, exchanging the role of
s
and
t
we obtain (
g
). We note that, if the left-hand side of (
6.14
) is less than or equal to zero, then the inequality is trivial because the right-hand side is non-negative. Hence, we may assume that the left-hand side is positive.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\rm BD}(U)
be a sequence converging to
u
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and such that
F_{n}(tu_{n},U)
converges to
F(tu,U)
as

It follows from property (
g
) applied to
F_{n}
that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(6.15)

\begin{gathered}\frac{F_{n}(su_{n},U)}{s}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F_{n}(su_{n},U)^{1-\alpha}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)-c_{7}\frac{F_{n}(su_{n},U)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\\
\quad\leq\frac{F_{n}(tu_{n},U)}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F_{n}(tu_{n},U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\frac{F_{n}(tu_{n},U)}{t}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).\end{gathered}

Thanks to our choice of
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{F_{n}(tu_{n},U)}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F_{n}(tu_{n},U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\frac{F_{n}(tu_{n},U)}{t}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{F(tu,U)}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(tu,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\frac{F(tu,U)}{t}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).

Hence, by (
6.15
) to conclude we only need to show that

(6.16)

\begin{gathered}\frac{F(su,U)}{s}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(su,U)^{1-\alpha}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)-c_{7}\frac{F(su,U)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\\
\hskip-8.5359pt\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{F_{n}(su_{n},U)}{s}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F_{n}(su_{n},U)^{1-\alpha}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)-c_{7}\frac{F_{n}(su_{n},U)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\Big).\end{gathered}

We consider the function
\Phi\colon[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
defined for every
z\in[0,+\infty)
by

\Phi(z):=\frac{z}{s}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}z^{1-\alpha}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)-c_{7}\frac{z}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).

We observe that
\Phi(F(su,U))
coincides with the left-hand side of (
6.14
) and (
6.16
), while the right-hand side of (
6.16
) coincides with
\liminf_{n}\Phi(F_{n}(su_{n},U))
. If
1-c_{7}(1/s+1/t)\leq 0
then
\Phi(z)\leq 0
for every
z\in[0,+\infty)
, so that (
6.14
) is satisfied. If
1-c_{7}(1/s+1/t)>0
, then we set
z_{0}:=\big(c_{6}(1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\big)^{1/\alpha}\big(1-c_{7}\big(1/s+1/t)\big)^{-1/\alpha}.
By direct computation of
\Phi^{\prime}
we see that
\Phi
is strictly decreasing in
[0,z_{0}]
and strictly increasing in
[z_{0},+\infty)
. Since
\Phi(0)\leq 0
we deduce that
\Phi(z)\leq 0
for
z\in[0,z_{0}]
. This implies that if
\Phi(z)>0
then
z>z_{0}
. Since
\Phi(F(su,U))
coincides with the left-hand side of (
6.16
), which we assumed to be positive, we have that
F(su,U)>z_{0}
. Moreover, by the
\Gamma
-liminf inequality we have
F(su,U)\leq\liminf_{n}F_{n}(su_{n},U)
, so that, using that
\Phi
is increasing on
[z_{0},+\infty)
, we deduce that

\Phi(F(su,U))\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\Phi(F_{n}(su_{n},U)).

This proves (
6.16
) and concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

We now present an inequality that allows to estimate the difference between the minimum values of some minimisation problems with Dirichlet boundary conditions involving functionals in
\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
. The aim of this lemma is twofold. On the one hand, it is useful to establish that the functions
f
and
g
defined by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) satisfy (
f4
) and (
g5
). On the other hand, this lemma will be crucial in the proof of the integral representation of the Cantor part of functionals in
\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}_{\rm sc}
.
The proof of this lemma closely resembles that of Proposition
6.6
. It follows the lines of the proofs of
[
26
, Lemmas 6.12 and 6.13]
, removing any truncation argument, which is not available in our context.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
, let
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with Lipschitz boundary, and let
w\in{\rm BD}(U)
. Then

\displaystyle\big|\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)}{s}-\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)}{t}\big|\leq\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)}{s}+\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).

Proof.

It is enough to prove that

(6.17)

\begin{gathered}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)}{s}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)^{1-\alpha}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)-c_{7}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\\
\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}(U)+c_{7}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)}{t}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\end{gathered}

whenever the left-hand side is positive.

Let
\eta>0
and consider a function
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
with
u=w
on
\partial U
and such that

F(tu,U)\leq\mathfrak{m}(tw,U)+\eta.

Since
F
satisfies property (
g
) of Definition
6.4
, from this inequality we deduce that

\begin{gathered}\frac{F(su,U)}{s}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(su,U)^{1-\alpha}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)-c_{7}\frac{F(su,U)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\\
\quad\leq\frac{F(tu,U)}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}F(tu,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\frac{F(su,U)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\\
\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)}{t}+\frac{\eta}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\eta^{1-\alpha}\\
+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)}{t}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)+c_{7}\frac{\eta}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).\end{gathered}

Exploiting the fact that
\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)\leq F(su,U)
and using the same argument employed at the end of the proof of Proposition
6.6
we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)}{s}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)^{1-\alpha}-\frac{c_{6}}{s}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)-c_{7}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(sw,U)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)}{t}+\frac{\eta}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)^{\alpha}\eta^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+c_{7}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tw,U)}{t}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)+c_{7}\frac{\eta}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).

As
\eta
is arbitrary, we obtain (
6.17
).
∎

We conclude the section by proving a further property of the functionals
F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
. Then the functions
f
and

defined by (

) and (

) belong to
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}
, respectively.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemmas

and
5.8
, to conclude it is enough to show that

and

satisfy properties (

) and (
g5
).

We first prove that

satisfies (f4). Let us fix

,

, and
s,t>0
. Thanks to Lemma

applied with

and

, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{s}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}\rho^{\alpha d}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}\rho^{d}

\displaystyle\frac{c_{6}}{t}\rho^{\alpha d}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\rho^{d}+c_{7}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{s}+\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).

Dividing this inequality by

, letting

, and recalling (

), we obtain

\begin{gathered}\frac{f(x,sA)}{s}\leq\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}f(x,sA)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}f(x,tA)^{1-\alpha}\\

and

\begin{gathered}\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}\leq\frac{f(x,sA)}{s}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}f(x,sA)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}f(x,tA)^{1-\alpha}\\
+\frac{c_{6}}{t}+c_{7}\Big(\frac{f(x,sA)}{s}+\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).\end{gathered}

and

, we may let

+\frac{c_{6}}{t}+\frac{c_{7}}{t}\Big({f^{\infty}(x,A)}+\frac{f(x,tA)}{t}\Big).

\displaystyle\big|\lambda f^{\infty}(x,A)-\frac{f(x,t\lambda A)}{t}\big|\leq\frac{c_{6}}{t}f(x,t\lambda A)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}+\frac{c_{7}}{t}\Big({\lambda f^{\infty}(x,A)}+\frac{f(x,t\lambda A)}{t}\Big)

for every

and

for every

and

(6.18)

\displaystyle\big|f^{\infty}(x,A)-\frac{f(x,\tau A)}{\tau}\big|\leq\frac{c_{6}}{\tau}f(x,\tau A)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{\tau}

for every

,

applied with

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(su_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{s}\leq\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tu_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}\rho^{\alpha d}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(su_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s}\rho^{d}

\displaystyle\frac{c_{6}}{t}\rho^{\alpha d}\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tu_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\rho^{d}

\displaystyle+c_{7}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(su_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{s}+\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(tu_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).

Dividing this inequality by

, letting

, and recalling (

) we deduce

\frac{g(x,s\zeta,\nu)}{s}\leq\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}+c_{7}\big(\frac{g(x,s\zeta,\nu)}{s}+\frac{g(x,t\zeta,\nu)}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).

Exchanging the roles of

and

∎

7.
Full integral representation

In this section we prove a complete integral representation result for functionals
F
in
\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}_{\rm sc}
, including the representation of the Cantor part
F^{c}
, assuming that the function
f
, defined in (
5.2
), does not depend on
x
.

A full integral representation result for functionals
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
has recently been proved in
[
17
]
, under the additional assumption of uniform continuity of
F
with respect to translations of the independent variables. This condition was originally considered in
[
9
, Lemma 3.11]
for the corresponding integral representation problem in
{\rm BV}
.

In the case of periodic homogenisation one can easily cheque that the
\Gamma
-limit functional
F_{\rm hom}
(see Theorem
9.7
below) is invariant under translations (see, for instance,
[
17
, Theorem 6.14]
). However, in general, this invariance (nor the continuity with respect to translations) cannot be checked directly in the case of non-periodic homogenisation. For this reason, we will prove Theorem
7.1
below under the sole additional assumption (
7.1
), which is much weaker than invariance under translations (see Example
7.4
).
We shall see in Section
9
that condition (
7.1
) is satisfied almost surely under the standard hypotheses of stochastic homogenisation.

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}_{\rm sc}
and let
g
be the function defined by (
5.3
). Assume that there exist a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
and a Borel set
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\sigma
-finite with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
, such that

(7.1)

f(A)=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}}\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus N\text{ and }A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}.

Then for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
we have

F(u,B)=\int_{B}f(\mathcal{E}u)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{B}f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap B}g(x,[u],\nu_{u})\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(U)
.

We first state a useful lemma, obtained by Caroccia, Focardi, and Van Goethem in
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
(see
[
9
, Lemma 3.7]
for an analogous result in the
{\rm BV}
-setting), which characterises the Radon-Nikodým derivative
{\rm d}F^{c}(u,\cdot)/{\rm d}|E^{c}u|
by means of suitable minimum values of minimisation problems on small parallelograms. To state this result, we set some further notation.

We recall that De Philippis and Rindler proved in
[
32
, Theorem 1.18]
(see also the survey
[
33
]
and the book
[
47
]
) the following remarkable theorem: given an open set
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
there exist two Borel maps
a,b\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(7.2)

\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}=a\odot b\quad\text{ and }\quad|a\odot b|=1\quad\text{ $|E^{c}u|$-a.e.\ in }U.

For every
\lambda>0
and every pair
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
a\neq\pm b
we set

P^{a,b}_{\lambda}:=\big\{z\in U:|z\cdot b|<\tfrac{\lambda}{2},\,\,|z\cdot a|<\tfrac{1}{2},\,|z\cdot\theta_{i}|<\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{ for }i\in\{1,...,n-2\}\big\},

where
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-2}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is such that
\{a,b,\theta_{1},...,\theta_{n-2}\}
is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, while if
a=\pm b
we set

P^{a,b}_{\lambda}:=\big\{z\in U:|z\cdot b|<\tfrac{\lambda}{2},\,\,|z\cdot\theta_{i}|<\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{ for }i\in\{1,...,n-1\}\big\},

where
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is such that
\{b,\theta_{1},...,\theta_{n-1}\}
is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The choice of the vectors
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i}
in the previous definitions is irrelevant for the arguments that follow. Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\rho>0
, we also set
P^{a,b}_{\lambda}(x,\rho):=x+\rho P_{\lambda}^{a,b}
. Moreover, for every point
x\in U
such that (
7.2
) holds, we set
P^{x}_{\lambda}(x,\rho):=P^{a(x),b(x)}_{\lambda}(x,\rho)
.

The following result characterises
{\rm d}F^{c}(u,\cdot)/{\rm d}|E^{c}u|
in terms of the double limit of infima of problems related to parallelograms of the form
P^{x}_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 7.2

(​​
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
)
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
,
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
. Then there exists
C(u)\in\mathcal{B}(U)
, with
|E^{c}u|(U\setminus C(u))=0
, such that for every
x\in C(u)
equality (
7.2
) holds and there exist a positive sequence
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, converging to
0
as
j\to+\infty
, and for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
a positive sequence
\{\rho_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, converging to
0
as
i\to+\infty
, such that, setting

(7.3)

A=A(x):=a(x)\odot b(x)\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i,j}:=\frac{|Eu|(P^{x}_{\lambda_{j}}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))},

we have

(7.4)

\displaystyle\text{ for every $j\in\mathbb{N}$ the sequence }\{s_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ tends to $+\infty$ as }i\to+\infty,

(7.5)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}F^{c}(u,\cdot)}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}(x)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{\lambda_{j}}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{\lambda_{j}}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}.

Proof.

This result is proved in
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
under slightly different hypotheses on the functional
F
. In particular, they assume a condition, which they denote by (H4), that prescribes a joint continuity of the functional
F
with respect to translations both of the dependent and of the independent variables. Not all functionals in
\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
satisfy this property. However, examining the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
one can see that the use of property (H4) can be replaced by the property (e) of Definition
3.4

) instead of those used in
[
17

, which differ from ours by a rigid motion.
∎

The following result shows that, under suitable assumptions on
F
, in definition (
5.2
) we may replace the cube
Q(x,\rho)
by parallelograms of the form
P^{a,b}_{\lambda}(x,\rho)
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{F}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\lambda>0
. Assume that there exists
\mu\geq 0
such that

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(y,\rho))\leq\mu\rho^{d}

for every

and

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},P^{a,b}_{\lambda}(y,\rho))\leq\mu\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{a,b}_{\lambda}(y,\rho))

for every

and

. If, in addition, for some

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}}=\mu,

then

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},P^{a,b}_{\lambda}(x,\rho))}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{a,b}_{\lambda}(x,\rho))}=\mu.

Proof.

The proof can be obtained arguing as in
[
30
, Lemma 5.3]
, observing that in the last part of that lemma limsup can be replaced by lim. We also remark that, in contrast with
[
30
, Lemma 5.3]
, we deal with minimisation problems without constraints on the oscillation of the competitors, which simplifies the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

(7.6)

f(A)=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}}\quad\text{ for $\mathcal{L}^{d}$-a.e.\ $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and every $A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}$.}

(7.7)

\frac{{\rm d}F^{c}(u,\cdot)}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}(x)=f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}(x)\Big)

for
|E^{c}u|
-a.e.

, where

Thanks to Lemma

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to

as

, and for every

a positive sequence
\{\rho_{i,j}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, converging to
0
as
i\to+\infty
, such that (

) and (
7.5
) hold. For simplicity of notation, we set
P_{j}^{x}(x,\rho_{i,j}):=P^{x}_{\lambda_{j}}(x,\rho_{i,j})
. To obtain (

(7.8)

f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}(x)\Big)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))},

where

By (

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j})\leq F(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))=f(s_{i,j}A)\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j})),

so that by (

(7.9)

\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\leq\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\frac{f(s_{i,j}A)}{s_{i,j}}=f^{\infty}(A),

for every

,

, and

\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{\lambda}(x,\rho))}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{\lambda}(x,\rho))}=f(tA)

for every

(7.11)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{\lambda}(x,\rho))}{t\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{\lambda}(x,\rho))}=f^{\infty}(A).

Then, we apply Lemma

(7.12)

\begin{gathered}\big|\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}-\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{t\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\big|\leq\frac{c_{6}}{s_{i,j}}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\Big)^{1-\alpha}\\
+\frac{c_{6}}{s_{i,j}}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\Big)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\\
+c_{7}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}+\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{t\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s_{i,j}}+\frac{1}{t}\Big).\end{gathered}

We observe that for every
\tau>0
it follows from the upper bound in (c) of Definition
3.4
that

(7.13)

\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\tau\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))\leq F(\tau\ell_{A},,P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))\leq(c_{3}\tau|A|+c_{4})\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j})).

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. Recalling (
6.1
), we may find
t>0
such that

(7.14)

|\frac{f(tA)}{t}-f^{\infty}(A)|<\varepsilon\quad\text{and }\quad\frac{c_{6}}{t}+\frac{c_{6}}{t^{\alpha}}(c_{3}|A|+\frac{c_{4}}{t})^{1-\alpha}+c_{7}\big(2c_{3}|A|+\frac{c_{4}}{t}\big)\frac{1}{t}<\varepsilon.

Thanks to (
7.10
), from the first inequality in (
7.14
) we deduce that for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

f^{\infty}(A)-\varepsilon<\frac{f(tA)}{t}=\lim_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{t\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}.

Combining this inequality with (
7.12
), we get

\displaystyle f^{\infty}(A)-\varepsilon\leq\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lim_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{t\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\leq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{6}}{s_{i,j}}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\Big)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{s_{i,j}}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\Big)^{1-\alpha}+\frac{c_{6}}{t}

(7.15)

\displaystyle+c_{7}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}+\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(t\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{t\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s_{i,j}}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\Big).

Using (
7.13
), we see that the right-hand side of the previous chain of inequalities can be bounded from above by

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}+\frac{c_{6}}{s_{i,j}^{\alpha}}\Big(c_{3}|A|{+}\frac{c_{4}}{s_{i,j}}\Big)^{1-\alpha}\!\!\!+\frac{c_{6}}{s_{i,j}}+\frac{c_{6}}{t^{\alpha}}(c_{3}|A|+\frac{c_{4}}{t})^{1-\alpha}\!\!+\frac{c_{6}}{t}

(7.16)

\displaystyle+c_{7}\Big(2c_{3}|A|+\frac{c_{4}}{s_{i,j}}+\frac{c_{4}}{t}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{s_{i,j}}+\frac{1}{t}\Big)\Big).

Recalling (
7.9
) and (
7.4
), from (
7.14
)-(
7.16
) we infer

f^{\infty}(A)-\varepsilon<\lim_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{i\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(s_{i,j}\ell_{A},P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}{s_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{d}(P^{x}_{j}(x,\rho_{i,j}))}+\varepsilon\leq f^{\infty}(A)+\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain (
7.8
). Since
x\in C(u)\setminus N
,
|E^{c}u|(U\setminus C(u))=0
, and
|E^{c}u|(N)=0
(see part (ii) of (k) of Section
2
), this concludes the proof.∎

It is easy to produce examples of functionals

).

Example 7.4
.

Assume that
0<c_{1}<c_{3}
and let

and

be the functions defined for every

,

,

, and

\displaystyle f(A):=c_{3}|A|\quad\text{and}\quad g(x,\zeta,\nu):=\begin{cases}c_{3}|\zeta\odot\nu|&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Pi^{e_{d}},\\

where

8.
Characterisation of
\Gamma
-convergence using minima on small cubes

In this section we determine the integrands of the
\Gamma
-limit of a sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
by means of limits of the minimum values of suitable minimisation problems for
F_{n}
on small cubes.

The next lemma shows that the
\Gamma
-convergence of a sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{F}
to a limit functional
F
allows us to compare
\mathfrak{m}^{F}
with
\{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{F}
. Assume that there exists
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
such that for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to
F(\cdot,U)
with respect to the topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
U,W\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with Lipschitz boundary and
W\subset\subset U
, and let
w\in{\rm BD}(U)
.
Then

(8.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(w,W)+c_{3}|Ew|(U\setminus W)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus W),

(8.2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(w,U)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U).

Proof.

We consider a subsequence, not relabelled, such that the liminf in the right-hand side of (
8.1
) is actually a limit. To prove (
8.1
) we fix
\delta>0
and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we select
z_{n}\in{\rm BD}(W)
such that
u_{n}=w
on
\partial W
and

(8.3)

F_{n}(z_{n},W)<\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(w,W)+\delta.

We observe that by the upper bound in (c) of Definition
3.4
, we have

F_{n}(z_{n},W)<\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(w,W)+\eta\leq F_{n}(w,W)+\delta\leq c_{3}|Ew|(W)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(W)+\delta,

so that by the lower bound in (c) we also get that
|Ez_{n}|(W)
is bounded uniformly with respect to
n
.
We extend
z_{n}
to
U
by setting
z_{n}=w
on
U\setminus W
and observe that
|Ez_{n}|(U)
is uniformly bounded in
n
as well. Hence, there exist a subsequence, not relabelled, and a function
z\in{\rm BD}(U)
such that
z_{n}\to z
in
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
z=w
on
U\setminus W
, so that

(8.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)\leq F(z,U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}F_{n}(z_{n},U),

where the last inequality is due to
\Gamma
-convergence. Since the inner trace of
z_{n}
on
\partial W
equals the inner trace of
w
, we have
|Ez_{n}|(\partial W)=|Ew|(\partial W)
. Hence,
the upper bound in (c) of Definition
3.4
gives

\displaystyle F_{n}(z_{n},U\setminus W)\leq c_{3}|Ez_{n}|(U\setminus W)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus W)=c_{3}|Ew|(U\setminus W)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus W),

where the last equality is due to the fact that
z_{n}=w
in the open set
U\setminus\overline{W}
and
|Ez_{n}|(\partial W)=|Ew|(\partial W)
.
Combining this inequality with (
8.3
) and (
8.4
), and letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
we obtain (
8.1
).

To prove (
8.2
), let us fix
\delta>0
and let
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
be such that
u=w
on
\partial U
and
F(u,U)<\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)+\delta.

By
\Gamma
-convergence there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\rm BD}(U)
converging to
u
strongly in
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and such that

(8.5)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}F_{n}(u_{n},U)=F(u,U)<\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)+\delta.

We now fix a compact set
K\subset U
such that

(8.6)

c_{3}|Eu|(U\setminus K)+c_{4}\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus K)<\delta.

We also consider two additional open sets
U^{\prime\prime}
and
U^{\prime}
with the property that
K\subset U^{\prime\prime}\subset\subset U^{\prime}\subset\subset U
. We now argue as in the part of the proof of Lemma
4.2
that starts from (
4.11
), with
V=U\setminus U^{\prime\prime}
,
v_{n}
replaced by
u
, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we construct a function
z_{n}\in{\rm BD}(U)
such that
z_{n}=u
in a neighbourhood of
\partial U
and

\displaystyle F_{n}(z_{n},U)\leq\,\,F_{n}(u_{n},U)+F_{n}(u,U\setminus U^{\prime\prime})+\frac{M}{m}+\frac{2c_{3}}{\eta}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
M\geq 0
is the constant introduced in (
4.9
) and
\eta:={\rm dist}(U,\partial U^{\prime})
. Since
z_{n}=u=w
on
\partial U
, we have
\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(w,U)\leq F_{n}(z_{n},U)
. Hence, using the upper bounds in property (c) of Definition
3.4
for
F_{n}
, (
8.6
), and the inclusion
K\subset U^{\prime\prime}
, from the previous displayed formula we obtain

\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(w,U)\leq F_{n}(u_{n},U)+\delta+\frac{M}{m}+\frac{2c_{3}}{\eta}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We now pass to the limsup as
n\to+\infty
and, recalling that
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
n\to+\infty
, from (
8.5
) we get

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(w,U)\leq\mathfrak{m}^{F}(w,U)+2\delta+\frac{M}{m}.

Taking the limit as
m\to+\infty
and
\delta\to 0^{+}
we obtain (
8.2
).
∎

The next result shows that for a functional
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
arising as
\Gamma
-limit of a sequence of functionals in
\mathfrak{F}
the integrands
f
and
g
of the bulk and surface parts can be obtained using limits of the minimum values of the minimisation problems
\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))
and
\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))
for the functionals
F_{n}
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{F}
. Assume that there exists
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
such that for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to
F(\cdot,U)
with respect to the topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let
f
and
g
be the functions associated to
F
by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
). Then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
we have

\displaystyle f(x,A)=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}}=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}},

\displaystyle g(x,\zeta,\nu)=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d-1}}=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d-1}}.

Proof.

The proof can be obtained arguing exactly as in
[
30
, Lemma 3.3]
, replacing
D
with
E
,
\nabla
with
\mathcal{E}
, and Propositions 3.1 and 3.2 of that paper with our Lemma
8.1
.
∎

We conclude this section by proving a sufficient condition for the
\Gamma
-convergence of a sequence
F_{n}
, based on limits of minimum values of minimisation problems for
F_{n}
on small cubes. Note that we require that the limits corresponding to the volume integrand do not depend on
x
.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{F^{\alpha,\infty}}
. Assume that there exist
\hat{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
and
\hat{g}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
such that

(8.7)

\displaystyle\hat{f}(A)=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}}=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}},

(8.8)

\displaystyle\hat{g}(x,\zeta,\nu)=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d-1}}=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d-1}},

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then
\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
,
\hat{g}\in\mathcal{G}^{\infty}
, and for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have that
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to
F^{\hat{f},\hat{g}}(\cdot,U)
with respect to the topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
F^{\hat{f},\hat{g}}
is the functional introduced in Definition
3.3
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
there exists a subsequence, not relabelled, and a functional
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\rm sc}
such that
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges ot
F(\cdot,U)
for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
f
and
g
be the functions defined by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) corresponding to
F
. Using Lemmas
8.1
and
8.2
, together with (
8.7
) and (
8.8
), we see that
f(x,A)=\hat{f}(A)
and
g(x,\zeta,\nu)=\hat{g}(x,\zeta,\nu)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
Moreover,
thanks to Lemma
8.1
we obtain that

\displaystyle\hat{f}(A)=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}}\leq\liminf_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}},

\displaystyle\hat{f}(A)=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F_{n}}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho-\rho^{2}))}{(\rho-\rho^{2})^{d}}\geq\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}},

hence

\hat{f}(A)=\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F}(\ell_{A},Q(x,\rho))}{\rho^{d}}\quad\text{ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}$}.

Thus, the functional
F
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
7.1
, so that
F=F^{\hat{f},\hat{g}}
.

Moreover, by (
8.7
) and (
8.8
) the functions
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
, and hence the functional
F^{\hat{f},\hat{g}}
, do not depend on the subsequence chosen at the beginning of the proof, so that by the Urysohn property of
\Gamma
-convergence (see
[
25
, Proposition 8.3]
) for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the whole sequence
\{F_{n}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to
F^{\hat{f},\hat{g}}(\cdot,U)
.
∎

9.
Functionals obtained by rescaling

In this section we use the theory developed in the previous sections to deal with the problem of
\Gamma
-convergence of oscillating functionals obtained by rescaling of a single functional. In particular, we prove a general theorem which provides sufficient conditions for the
\Gamma
-convergence of these functionals (see Theorem
9.7
).

We now introduce the notation we will use in the rest of the work. Throughout this section we keep fixed
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}
and set

F:=F^{f,g}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty},

where
F^{f,g}
is the functional introduced in Definition
3.3
.
We also assume that the modulus of continuity introduced in (
3.1
) satisfies

(9.1)

\sigma(\tau)=\sigma_{1}\tau\quad\text{for every }\tau\geq 0,

for some constant
\sigma_{1}>0
. With this hypothesis, condition (g4) of Definition
3.1
reads

|g(x,\zeta_{1},\nu)-g(x,\zeta_{2},\nu)|\leq\sigma_{1}|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}|,

while the surface continuity estimate (f) of Definition
3.4
becomes

F(u+u_{x,\zeta,\nu},B)\leq F(u,B)+\sigma_{1}|\zeta|\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x}\cap B).

Definition 9.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
we consider the integrands
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
and
g_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{G}^{\infty}
defined by

(9.2)

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x,A):=f\big(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},A\big)\quad\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym},

(9.3)

\displaystyle g_{\varepsilon}(x,\zeta,\nu):=\varepsilon g\big(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\tfrac{\zeta}{\varepsilon},\nu\big)\quad\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{ and }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

and we set
F_{\varepsilon}:=F^{f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}}\in\mathfrak{F}^{\alpha,\infty}
.

Remark 9.2
.

If for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
the function
t\mapsto g(x,t\zeta,\nu)
is positively homogeneous of degree one, that is to say

(9.4)

g(x,t\zeta,\nu)=tg(x,\zeta,\nu)\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\text{ and }t\geq 0,

then

g_{\varepsilon}(x,\zeta,\nu)=g(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\zeta,\nu)\quad\text{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\text{ and }\varepsilon>0.

In this case, for
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u\in{\rm BD}(U)
the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}(u,U)
of Definition
9.1
become

(9.5)

\int_{U}f\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\mathcal{E}u\Big)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{U}f^{\infty}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|+\int_{J_{u}\cap U}g\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},[u],\nu_{u}\Big)\,{\rm d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},

which are the functionals commonly considered in homogenisation of free discontinuity problems.

Our choice in the definition of
g_{\varepsilon}
given by (
9.3
) is justified by the fact that the corresponding functional
F_{\varepsilon}
defined by
F^{f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}}
satisfies good change of variables formulas (see Lemmas
9.4
and
9.6
below) even when
g
does not satisfy (
9.4
). This will allow us to prove a very general
\Gamma

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
\varepsilon,\rho\in(0,1)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
u\in{\rm BD}(Q_{\nu}(x,\rho))
, and
v\in{\rm BD}(Q_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon}))
. Assume that
v(z)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}u(\varepsilon z)
for every
z\in Q_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon}).
Then

(9.6)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\nu}(x,\rho)}f^{\infty}_{\varepsilon}\Big(y,\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}u|=\varepsilon^{d}\int_{Q_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon})}f^{\infty}\Big(z,\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}v}{{\rm d}|E^{c}v|}\Big)\,{\rm d}|E^{c}v|.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon});\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})
and let
\psi_{\varepsilon}:=\psi\big(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\big)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q_{\nu}(x,\rho);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})
. By change of variables and integration by parts we have

\displaystyle-\int_{Q_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon})}\psi\,{\rm d}Ev=\int_{Q_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon})}v\,{\rm div}\,\psi\,{\rm d}z=\int_{Q_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon})}\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}u(\varepsilon z)\,{\rm div}\,\psi\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{Q_{\nu}(x,\rho)}\frac{u(y)}{\varepsilon}{\rm div}\,\psi\big(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\big)\,{\rm d}y=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{Q_{\nu}(x,\rho)}u\,{\rm div}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{Q_{\nu}(x,\rho)}\psi_{\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}Eu=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\int_{Q_{\nu}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon})}\psi\,{\rm d}\Big(\big(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\big)_{\#}Eu\Big),

where
(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\big)_{\#}Eu
denotes the push-forward of
Eu
via the function
y\mapsto\frac{y}{\varepsilon}
(see, for instance,
[
8
, Theorem 3.6.1]
). This implies that

Ev=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\Big(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\Big)_{\#}Eu\quad\text{ as Borel measures on }Q_{\nu}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\tfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon})

and, passing to their singular parts,

E^{s}v=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\Big(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\Big)_{\#}E^{s}u\quad\text{ as Borel measures on }Q_{\nu}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\tfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon}),

where
E^{s}v
and
E^{s}u
denote the singular part of
Ev
and
Eu
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Restricting the previous equality to
Q_{\nu}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\tfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon})\setminus J_{v}=Q_{\nu}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\tfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon})\setminus(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}J_{u})
, we deduce that

E^{c}v=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\Big(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\Big)_{\#}E^{c}u\quad\text{ as Borel measures on }Q_{\nu}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\tfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon}).

This implies that

\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}v}{{\rm d}|E^{c}v|}\big(\frac{y}{\varepsilon}\big)=\frac{{\rm d}E^{c}u}{{\rm d}|E^{c}u|}(y)\quad\text{ for }|E^{c}u|\text{-a.e.\ }y\in Q_{\nu}(x,\rho).

Hence, by the integration formula for the push-forward of measures (see, for instance,
[
8
, Theorem 3.6.1]
) we get (
9.6
).
∎

We conclude this section by showing that, if
F^{f,g}(\cdot,U)
is
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
lower semicontinuous for some bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
f^{\infty}(\zeta\odot\nu)=g(\zeta,\nu)
, provided that
f
and
g
are independent of
x
and
g
is positively homogeneous of degree one in
\zeta
. Although variants of this result are well-known (see, for instance,
[

, Step 3 of Theorem 6.14]
), we present here a complete proof in order to conclude the proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 9.10
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
and
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
be Borel functions satisfying
(f2), (g2), (g3)
, and (
9.4
). Assume that there exists a non-empty bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the functional
F^{f,g}(\cdot,U)
is
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
-lower semicontinuous. Then we have

g(\zeta,\nu)=f^{\infty}(\zeta\odot\nu)

for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Proof.

Let us fix
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(9.40)

\displaystyle 0<\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x}\cap U),

(9.41)

\displaystyle 0\text{ is a Lebesgue point of the function }\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x+t\nu}\cap U),

where we recall that for
\nu_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the symbol
\Pi^{\nu_{0}}_{x_{0}}
denotes the hyperplane passing through
x_{0}
and orthogonal to
\nu_{0}
, given by
\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(y-x_{0})\cdot\nu_{0}=0\}
.
Thanks to the Fubini Theorem and the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem the set of points
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for which the two previous conditions are satisfied has positive
\mathcal{L}^{d}
measure.

We now show that

(9.42)

g(\zeta,\nu)\leq{f}^{\infty}(\zeta\odot\nu).

To prove this, we approximate the function
u_{x,\zeta,\nu}
by the sequence of piecewise affine functions
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{\rm BD}(U)
defined for every
y\in U
by

u_{n}(y):=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }(y-x)\cdot\nu\leq-\tfrac{1}{2n},\\
n((y-x)\cdot\nu+\frac{1}{2n})\zeta&\text{ if }-\tfrac{1}{2n}\leq(y-x)\cdot\nu\leq\tfrac{1}{2n},\\
\zeta&\text{ if }(y-x)\cdot\nu\geq\tfrac{1}{2n}.\end{cases}

Clearly
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
u_{x,\zeta,\nu}
strongly in
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
n\to+\infty
.

Setting
H_{n}:=\{y\in U:-\tfrac{1}{2n}\leq(y-x)\cdot\nu\leq\tfrac{1}{2n}\}
, one checks immediately that,

(9.43)

\mathcal{E}u_{n}=n\zeta\odot\nu\chi_{H_{n}}\quad\text{ $\mathcal{L}^{d}$-a.e.\ in }U,

where
\chi_{H_{n}}
is the characteristic function of
H_{n}
. Moreover, the Fubini Theorem implies that

\mathcal{L}^{d}(H_{n})=\int_{-\frac{1}{2n}}^{\frac{1}{2n}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x+t\nu}\cap U)\,{\rm d}t,

which together with (
9.41
) this gives

(9.44)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}n\mathcal{L}^{d}(H_{n})=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x}\cap U).

In light of the lower semicontinuity of
F^{f,g}(\cdot,U)
, a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle f(0)\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+g(\zeta,\nu)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x}\cap U)=F^{f,g}(u_{x,\zeta,\nu},U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}F^{f,g}(u_{n},U)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\int_{H_{n}}f(\mathcal{E}u_{n})\,{\rm d}x+f(0)\mathcal{L}^{d}(U\setminus H_{n})\Big).

Since
\mathcal{L}^{d}(H_{n})
converges to
0
as
n\to+\infty
, the previous inequality gives

(9.45)

g(\zeta,\nu)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x}\cap U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\int_{H_{n}}f(\mathcal{E}u_{n})\,{\rm d}x.

Using (
9.43
) we see that

(9.46)

\displaystyle\int_{H_{n}}f(\mathcal{E}u_{n})\,{\rm d}x=f(n\zeta\odot\nu)\mathcal{L}^{d}(H_{n}).

Combining (
9.44
)-(
9.46
), recalling the definition of
f^{\infty}
given by (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle g(\zeta,\nu)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x}\cap U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{f(n\zeta\odot\nu)}{n}(n\mathcal{L}^{d}(H_{n}))\leq f^{\infty}(\zeta\odot\nu)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x}\cap U),

which by (
9.40
) implies (
9.42
).

We now prove

(9.47)

f^{\infty}(\zeta\odot\nu)\leq g(\zeta,\nu).

To this aim, we set
T>{\rm diam}(U)
and let
x\in U
. By the Fubini Theorem we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)=\int_{-T}^{T}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Pi^{\nu}_{x+t\nu}\cap U)\,{\rm d}t.

By the well-know approximation properties of Lebesgue integrals by means of Riemann sums (see
[
42
]
,
[
36
, Page 63]
, or
[
27
, Lemma 4.12]
) we can select
x\in U
in such a way that

(9.48)

\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{T}{n}\sum_{i=-n+1}^{n}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\big(\Pi^{\nu}_{x+\frac{i-1}{n}T\nu}\cap U\big).

We set
A:=\zeta\otimes\nu
and fix
t>0
. To prove (
9.47
), we construct a sequence of pure jump functions approximating
\ell_{tA}
in
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\sigma_{n}\colon(-T,T)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\sigma_{n}(s):=\sum_{i=-n+1}^{n}\tfrac{iT}{n}\chi_{(\tfrac{i-1}{n}T,\tfrac{i}{n}T\,)}(s)\quad\text{ for every }s\in(-T,T).

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{n}\in{\rm BD}(U)
be the function defined by

u_{n}(y):=t\zeta\sigma_{n}((y-x)\cdot\nu)\quad\text{for every }y\in U.

It is easy to see that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\ell_{tA}
strongly in
L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
n\to+\infty
. Thus, by the
L^{1}
-lower semicontinuity of
F^{f,g}(\cdot,U)
we obtain

(9.49)

f(tA^{\rm sym})\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)=\int_{U}f(tA^{\rm sym})\,{\rm d}x=F^{f,g}(\ell_{tA},U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}F^{f,g}(u_{n},U).

On the other hand, using the homogeneity of
g
given by (
9.4
) we immediately check that

\displaystyle F^{f,g}(u_{n},U)=f(0)\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+\sum_{i=-n+1}^{n}g\Big(\frac{t\zeta}{n}T,\nu\Big)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\big(\Pi^{\nu}_{x+\frac{i-1}{n}T\nu}\cap U)

\displaystyle=f(0)\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+tg(\zeta,\nu)\frac{T}{n}\sum_{i=-n+1}^{n}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\big(\Pi^{\nu}_{x+\frac{i-1}{n}T\nu}\cap U).

Taking the liminf as
n\to+\infty
and using (
9.48
) and (
9.49
) we get

f(tA^{\rm sym})\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)\leq f(0)\mathcal{L}^{d}(U)+tg(\zeta,\nu)\mathcal{L}^{d}(U).

Hence, we get
\frac{1}{t}f(tA^{\rm sym})\leq\frac{1}{t}f(0)+g(\zeta,\nu)
, and letting

we obtain (
9.47
).
∎

10.
Stochastic homogenisation

In this section we use the results of the previous section to study the problem of stochastic homogenisation of free discontinuity functionals defined on the space
{\rm BD}
.
In the following we still assume that the modulus of continuity introduced in (
3.1
) satisfies (
9.1
). We begin by introducing the probabilistic setting we will use to deal with this problem.

Throughout this section
(\Omega,\mathcal{T},P)
is a probability space endowed with a group
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
of
P
-preserving transformations on

(\Omega,\mathcal{T},P)
, i.e., a family
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
of
\mathcal{T}
-measurable bijective maps
\tau_{z}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
such that

(a)

\tau_{z_{1}}\circ\tau_{z_{2}}=\tau_{z_{1}+z_{2}}
for every
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
;

(b)

P(\tau_{z}^{-1}(E))=P(E)
for every
E\in\mathcal{T}
and
z\in\>\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Note that from (a) and the bijectivity it follows that for
z=0
the map
\tau_{0}
is the identity on
\Omega
. We
say that a group
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
of
P
-preserving transformations is ergodic if every set
E\in\mathcal{T}
with
\tau_{z}(E)=E
for every
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
has probability either
0
or
1
.
In analogy with
[
30
,
26
]
, we introduce the following two classes of stochastic integrands.

Definition 10.1
.

\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\rm stoc}
denotes the set of all
\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})
-measurable functions
f\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
such that for every
\omega\in\Omega
the function
f(\omega):=f(\omega,\cdot,\cdot)
belongs to
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
and the following stochastic periodicity condition holds:

f(\omega,x+z,A)=f(\tau_{z}(\omega),x,A)

for every
\omega\in\Omega
,
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
.

\mathcal{G}_{\rm stoc}^{\rm\infty}
denotes
the set of all
\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
-measurable functions
g\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
such that for every
\omega\in\Omega
the function
g(\omega):=g(\omega,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
belongs to
\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}
, and the following stochastic periodicity condition holds:

g(\omega,x+z,\zeta,\nu)=g(\tau_{z}(\omega),x,\zeta,\nu)

for every
\omega\in\Omega
,
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
x,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

We recall the definition of subadditive process.
Let
\mathcal{R}
the collection of half-closed rectangles defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}:=\{R\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon

\displaystyle R=[a_{1},b_{1})\times...\times[a_{d},b_{d})\text{ with }a_{i}<b_{i}\text{ for }i\in\{1,...,d\}\}.

Given
R\in\mathcal{R}
its interior is denoted by
R^{\circ}
.
We also introduce the completion of
(\Omega,\mathcal{T},P)
, denoted by
(\Omega,\widehat{\mathcal{T}},\widehat{P})
. It is immediate to see that
(\tau_{z})_{z}
is a group of
P
-preserving transformation also on
(\Omega,\widehat{\mathcal{T}},\widehat{P})
.

Definition 10.2
.

A function
\mu\colon\Omega\times\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a covariant subadditive process with respect to
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
if the following properties are satisfied

(a)

for every
R\in\mathcal{R}
the function
\mu(\cdot,R)
is
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}
-measurable;

(b)

for every
\omega\in\Omega
,
R\in\mathcal{R}
, and
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we have
\mu(\omega,R+z)=\mu(\tau_{z}(\omega),R)
;

(c)

given
R\in\mathcal{R}
and a finite partition
(R_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathcal{R}
of
R
, we have

\mu(\omega,R)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu(\omega,R_{i})

for every
\omega\in\Omega
;

(d)

there exists
C>0
such that
0\leq\mu(\omega,R)\leq C\mathcal{L}^{d}(R)
for every
\omega\in\Omega

and
R\in\mathcal{R}
.

In the following we will make substantial use of the Subadditive Ergodic Theorem of Akcoglu and Krengel
[
1
, Theorem 2.7]
. In particular, we will use the version of this theorem stated in
[
28
, Proposition 1]
.

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
\mu
be a subadditive process with respect to the group
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. Then there exist
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, with
P(\Omega^{\prime})=1
, and
\varphi\colon\Omega\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\mu(\omega,\widetilde{Q}(rx,r))}{r^{d}}=\varphi(\omega)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
, where
\widetilde{Q}(rx,r):=[rx_{1}-\frac{r}{2},rx_{1}+\frac{r}{2})\times\dots\times[rx_{d}-\frac{r}{2},rx_{d}+\frac{r}{2})
.

If the group
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is also ergodic, then
\varphi
is constant
P
-a.e.

The following is the main result concerning the bulk part.

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\rm stoc}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}_{\rm stoc}
. Then there exist
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, with
P(\Omega^{\prime})=1
, and a function
f_{\rm lim}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
, with
f_{\rm lim}(\omega,\cdot)
continuous for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
f_{\rm lim}(\cdot,A)

\mathcal{T}
-measurable for every
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
, such that

(10.1)

f_{\rm lim}(\omega,A)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{E^{f(\omega),g(\omega)}}(\ell_{A},Q(rx,r))}{r^{d}}

for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
.
If, in addition,
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is ergodic, by choosing
\Omega^{\prime}
appropriately we have that
f_{\rm lim}
is
independent of
\omega
.

Proof.

Let us fix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
and consider the function
\Phi_{A}\colon\Omega\times\mathcal{R}\to[0,+\infty)
defined by

\Phi_{A}(\omega,R):=\mathfrak{m}^{E^{f(\omega),g(\omega)}}(\ell_{A},R^{\circ}).

This function defines a covariant subadditive process.
Indeed, it is easy to check that properties (b), (c), and (d) of Definition
10.2
hold (see
[
16
, Theorem 9.1]
for the details), while the proof of the measurability property (a) can be obtained by slightly modifying the Appendix of
[
16
]
, replacing
Du
and
\nabla u
by
Eu
and
\mathcal{E}u
.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a countable dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
. We may then apply Theorem
10.3
with
\mu=\Phi_{A}
for every
A\in\mathbb{D}
to obtain a set
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, with
P(\Omega^{\prime})=1
, and a function
\phi_{A}\colon\Omega\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\phi_{A}(\omega)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\Phi_{A}(\omega,\widetilde{Q}(rx,r))}{r^{d}}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{F^{f(\omega),g(\omega)}}(\ell_{A},Q(rx,r))}{r^{d}}

for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
A\in\mathbb{D}
. By Lemma
9.8

for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
there exists a unique continuous function
f_{\rm lim}(\omega,\cdot)\colon\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
such that
f_{\rm lim}(\omega,A)=\phi_{A}(\omega)
for every
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
. Moreover,
A\mapsto f_{\rm lim}(\omega,A)
is
c_{5}
-Lipschitz continuous and satisfies (
10.1
).

Finally, the statement concerning the ergodic hypothesis follows from the last part of Theorem
10.3
.
∎

The following proposition collects the main results concerning the surface term.

Proposition 10.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\rm stoc}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}_{\rm stoc}
. Then there exist
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, with
P(\Omega^{\prime})=1
, and a function
g_{\rm lim}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\zeta\mapsto g_{\rm lim}(\omega,\zeta,\nu)\text{ is Lipschitz continuous for every }\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}\text{ and }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{\pm}\ni\nu\mapsto g_{\rm lim}(\omega,\zeta,\nu)\text{ are continuous for every }\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}\text{ and }\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\omega\mapsto g_{\rm lim}(\omega,\zeta,\nu)\text{ is $\mathcal{T}$-measurable for every }\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

and

(10.2)

g_{\rm lim}(\omega,\zeta,\nu)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{{F^{\infty}(\omega)}}(u_{rx,\zeta,\nu},Q_{\nu}(rx,r))}{r^{d-1}}

for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, where
F^{\infty}
is the functional introduced in Definition
9.5
.
If, in addition,
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is ergodic, by choosing
\Omega^{\prime}
appropriately we have that
g_{\rm lim}
is
independent of
\omega
.

Proof.

It is enough to repeat the arguments of
[
16
, Proposition 9.4 and 9.5]
, replacing Step 2 of their Proposition 9.4 and 9.5 by our Lemma
9.9
.
∎

Collecting the results of Propositions
10.4
and
10.5
we obtain the following almost sure convergence result.

Theorem 10.6
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\rm stoc}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}_{\rm stoc}
and let
F_{\varepsilon}(\omega):=F^{f_{\varepsilon}(\omega),g_{\varepsilon}(\omega)}
be the functionals introduced in Definition
9.1
. Then there exist
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, with
P(\Omega^{\prime})=1
, and functions
f_{\rm lim}\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm stoc}^{\alpha}
and
g_{\rm lim}\in\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}_{\rm stoc}
, with
g_{\rm lim}(\omega)
satisfying (
9.4
) for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
, and with the following property: for every sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
converging to
0
as
n\to+\infty
,
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
we have that
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\omega)(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges, with respect to the topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, to
F^{f_{\rm lim}(\omega),g_{\rm lim}(\omega)}(\cdot,U)
as
n\to+\infty
. Moreover, we have the equality

(10.3)

g_{\rm lim}(\omega,\zeta,\nu)=f^{\infty}_{\rm lim}(\omega,\zeta\odot\nu)\quad\text{ for every $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$.}

If, in addition,
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is ergodic, by choosing
\Omega^{\prime}
appropriately we have that
f_{\rm lim}
and
g_{\rm lim}
are independent of
\omega
.

Proof.

Thanks to Propositions
10.4
and
10.5
, there exist
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, with
P(\Omega^{\prime})=1
,
f_{\rm lim}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,+\infty)
, and
g_{\rm lim}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\to[0,+\infty)
such that (
10.1
) and (
10.2
) hold. Thus, by Theorem
9.7
we obtain the two inclusions
f_{\rm lim}\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{\rm stoc}
and
g_{\rm lim}\in\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}_{\rm stoc}
, together with the fact that
g_{\rm lim}(\omega)
satisfies (
9.4
) for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
, as well as the
\Gamma
-convergence part of the statement and equality (
10.3
).

The last part of the statement follows from the final parts of the statements of Propositions
10.4
and
10.5
.
∎

As a particular case of the previous theorem we deduce the following result in the periodic deterministic setting.

Corollary 10.7
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}
, and let
F_{\varepsilon}:=F^{f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}}
be the functional introduced in Definition
9.1
. Assume that both
f
and
g
are
1
-periodic in the
x
variable. Then there exist functions
f_{\rm lim}\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
and
g_{\rm lim}\in\mathcal{G}^{\rm\infty}
, with
f_{\rm lim}
satisfying (
9.25
), and with
g_{\rm lim}
satisfying (
9.4
) and (
9.26
), and consequently the following property holds: for every sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
converging to
0
as
n\to+\infty
and for every
U\in\mathcal{U}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the sequence
\{F_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\cdot,U)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\Gamma
-converges to
F^{f_{\rm lim},g_{\rm lim}}(\cdot,U)
with respect to the topology of
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, we have the equality

g_{\rm lim}(\zeta,\nu)=f^{\infty}_{\rm lim}(\zeta\odot\nu)\quad\text{ for every $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and $\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$.}

Proof.

It is enough to apply Theorem
10.6
in the case where
\Omega
consists of a single point.
∎

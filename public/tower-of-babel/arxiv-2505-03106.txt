Abstract
. Let n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 be an integer. 1.
Introduction

Let
n\geqslant 3
be an integer, and let
\mathbb{B}_{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<1\}
be the unit ball of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
|x|=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2})^{1/2}
is the standard Euclidean norm. We deonte by
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
the Möbius transformation group on
the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. It is also known that
[
20
, Chapter 2]
the Möbius transformation group has the following explicit form

\text{Aut}(\mathbb{B}_{n})=\{A\circ\varphi_{y}:A\in O(n),y\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\},

where
O(n)
denotes the orthogonal group in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\varphi_{y}(x)=\frac{y|x-y|^{2}+(1-|y|^{2})(y-x)}{[x,y]^{2}},

with

[x,y]^{2}=|x-y|^{2}+(1-|y|^{2})(1-|x|^{2}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

Let
C^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
be the space of complex-valued functions on the real unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
whose second-order partial derivatives are continuous on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
consider the standard Laplace operator

\Delta=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}},

then the invariant Laplace operator
\Delta_{h}
is defined as a linear operator on
C^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
such that for each
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

(\Delta_{h}f)(x)=\Delta(f\circ\varphi_{x})(0).

Moreover, by using the standard Laplace operator
\Delta
and the gradient operator

\nabla
, we have for each
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
:

(1.1)

(\Delta_{h}f)(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{2}\Delta f(x)+2(n-2)(1-|x|^{2})\langle x,\nabla f(x)\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

denotes the standard Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recall also the gradient operator

\nabla
is given by

\nabla=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right).

A complex-valued function
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
is said to be
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic if for each
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

(\Delta_{h}f)(x)=0.

Let
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
be the complex linear space of the ordinary
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions.
If
n=2
, by the equality (
1.1
), we have

(\Delta_{h}f)(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{2}\Delta f(x),

for each
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
In this case, the space
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B}_{2})
coincides with the space of harmonic functions on the unit disk.

Let
\nu
be the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
such that
\nu(\mathbb{B}_{n})=1.

For
-1<\alpha<\infty
, the standard weighted measure on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is

d\nu_{\alpha}(x)=c_{\alpha}(1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha}d\nu(x),

where
c_{\alpha}
is the normalized constant such that
\nu_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n})=1
.

For
1\leqslant p<\infty
and
-1<\alpha<\infty
, the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman space
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}
is the Banach space

\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B}_{n}):\|f\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{\alpha})}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{L^{p}(d\nu_{\alpha})}=\left[\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(x)|^{p}d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By the mean-value estimate, the point evaluations
f\mapsto f(x)
at any
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
are bounded on each
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha},-1<\alpha<\infty

[
5
]
. Therefore, the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman space
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. It follows that for any
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
, the reproducing kernel
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,\cdot)
is the unique function in
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
, such that for all
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{2}
,

f(x)=\langle f(\cdot),\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,\cdot)\rangle_{\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\overline{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)}f(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y).

One can show that
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(\cdot,\cdot)
is a real-valued function. Hence, for any
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{2}
, we have

f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y)\qquad x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

For more details of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions and related function spaces on the unit ball, one can consult
[
5
,
8
,
13
,
20
,
21
]
.

Recall that the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection
P_{\alpha}
is the orthoganal projection from
L^{2}(d\nu_{\alpha})
onto
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Using the reproducing kernel property, we have

P_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

By
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
,
P_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}(d\nu_{\alpha})
for
1<p<\infty
and
-1<\alpha<\infty
.

In this paper, a weight
\omega
is a non-negative integrable function on the real unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. The weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})
on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is given by

L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})=\{f:\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}:=\left[\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(x)|^{p}\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The weighted theory of Bergman projection-type operators on the unit ball, namely the operators whose integral kernels are reproducing kernels for some holomorphic or harmonic function spaces on the unit balls, has attracted much attention in recent years. In particular, Bekollé and Bonami
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, as well as the ingenious work by Pott and Reguera
[
16
]
, present complete characterizations of the sharp linear norm estimate in the one-weight inequality for the holomorphic Bergman projections on the unit ball. In
[
17
]
, Rahm, Tchoundja, and Wick extended these sharp one-weight inequalities to the setting of the Berezin transform on the unit ball.
Volberg and Wick
[
24
]
first applied non-homogeneous harmonic analysis techniques to weighted inequalities for Bergman projections, obtaining deep applications concerning Carleson measures for function spaces on the unit ball.
For further developments on weighted inequalities for holomorphic Bergman projections and their applications, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
19
]
.

Motivated by these developments on the weighted inequalities of holomorphic Bergman projection, it is natural to study the following one-weight inequality for
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection:

Problem.
Let
1<p<\infty
and
-1<\alpha<\infty
. Characterize the weights
\omega
for which the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection
P_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})
?

Observe that the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection on
\mathbb{B}_{2}
coincides with the classical harmonic Bergman projection on the unit disk.
Although the results of Bekollé-Bonami and Pott-Reguera were initially established for the holomorphic Bergman projection, their results extend naturally to the harmonic setting. Consequently, by the work of Bekollé-Bonami and Pott-Reguera, the one-weight inequality for the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection on
\mathbb{B}_{2}
is characterized by the Bekollé-Bonami class of weights. In the case
n\geqslant 3
, it is therefore natural to conjecture that the Bekollé-Bonami weights continue to ensure the boundedness of the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection on
L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})
. In this note, we shall confirm this folk conjecture.

For a set
E
in the unit sphere
\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|=1\}
,
a Carleson box
\widehat{E}
in the unit ball associated with the set
E
is defined as

\widehat{E}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:\frac{z}{|z|}\in E,1-r<|z|<1\right\},

where
r=\textup{diam}(E)/2
and
\textup{diam}(E)=\max\{|x-y|:x,y\in E\}
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
-1<\alpha<\infty
, a weight
\omega
on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is said to be a Bekollé-Bonami
B_{p,\alpha}
weight if
the Bekollé-Bonami constant
[\omega]_{p,\alpha}
is finite. Here
[\omega]_{p,\alpha}
is defined as

[\omega]_{p,\alpha}:=\sup\limits_{B\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{|\widehat{B}|_{\omega,\alpha}}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\left(\frac{|\widehat{B}|_{\omega^{1-p^{\prime}},\alpha}}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\right)^{p-1},

where

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
,
|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}=\nu_{\alpha}(\widehat{B})
,
|\widehat{B}|_{\omega,\alpha}=\int_{\widehat{B}}\omega d\nu_{\alpha}
and that the supremum runs over all Euclidean balls
B\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Our main result is stated as follows

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
-1<\alpha<\infty
. If
\omega
is a
B_{p,\alpha}
weight, then the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection

P_{\alpha}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})
. More precisely, there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,\alpha)
such that

||P_{\alpha}||_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\to L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{\max\left\{1,\frac{1}{p-1}\right\}}.

Remark 1.1
.

Our strategy of proving Theorem
1.1
is inspired by Pott-Reguera’s recent beautiful work on the sharp one-weight estimate for Bergman projection
[
16
]
. However,
since there is no explicit closed form of the kernel of the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection. To overcome this difficulty, we construct suitable dyadic positive operators on appropriately chosen Carleson box systems, which allow us to effectively dominate the original operator.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, based on certain dyadic Carleson boxes on the unit ball, we provide a discretization of the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection. The proof of the
main result is presented in Section 3, which is divided into two parts. For the first part, in Subsection 3.1, we prove that case for
p=2
. While in Subsection 3.2 we deal with the case for
p\not=2
by Rubio de Francia’s extrapolation method. In the final section, by constructing specific examples, we demonstrate that the norm estimate in the one-weight inequality for the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection is sharp.

2.
Carleson systems and the discretization

2.1.
The Carleson box on the real unit ball

For two points
x
and
y
in
\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, let
\rho(x,y)
denote the angle between them, defined by

\rho(x,y)=\arccos(\langle x,y\rangle).

Proposition 2.1
.

The function
\rho(x,y)
defines a metric on

\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
that is equivalent to the standard Euclidean distance on
\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Proof.

To verify that
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
is a metric on
\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, it suffices to establish the triangle inequality: for any
x,y,z\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

\rho(x,y)\leqslant\rho(x,z)+\rho(z,y).

By the definition of the function
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
, the triangle inequality holds if and only if

\cos\rho(x,y)\geqslant\cos(\rho(x,z)+\rho(z,y)).

Note that
|x|=|y|=|z|=1
, by the Binet-Cauchy identity,

\langle x,y\rangle=\langle x,z\rangle\langle z,y\rangle+\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}z_{j}-x_{j}z_{i})(y_{i}z_{j}-y_{j}z_{i}),

we have

\displaystyle\cos\rho(x,y)

\displaystyle=\langle x,z\rangle\langle z,y\rangle+\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}z_{j}-x_{j}z_{i})(y_{i}z_{j}-y_{j}z_{i})

\displaystyle=\cos\rho(x,z)\cos\rho(z,y)+\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}z_{j}-x_{j}z_{i})(y_{i}z_{j}-y_{j}z_{i})

\displaystyle\geqslant\cos\rho(x,z)\cos\rho(z,y)-\left(\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}|x_{i}z_{j}-x_{j}z_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}|y_{i}z_{j}-y_{j}z_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By using the Binet-Cauchy identity again, we get

\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}|x_{i}z_{j}-x_{j}z_{i}|^{2}=|x|^{2}|z|^{2}-|\langle x,z\rangle|^{2}=\sin^{2}\rho(x,z).

It follows that

\displaystyle\cos\rho(x,y)\geqslant\cos\rho(x,z)\cos\rho(z,y)-\sin\rho(x,z)\sin\rho(z,y)=\cos(\rho(x,z)+\rho(z,y)).

Next, we show that the metric
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
is equivalent to the Euclidean distance.
For any two points
x,y\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, we observe that

\displaystyle|x-y|^{2}

\displaystyle=|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=2(1-\cos\rho(x,y))

\displaystyle=4\left(\sin\frac{\rho(x,y)}{2}\right)^{2}.

Note that for any
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant\pi/2
, we have

\frac{2}{\pi}\theta\leqslant\sin\theta\leqslant\theta;

and for any
x,y\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, we have

0\leqslant\rho(x,y)\leqslant\pi.

Hence,

(2.2)

\frac{2}{\pi}\rho(x,y)\leqslant|x-y|\leqslant\rho(x,y).

This completes the proof. ∎

Let
x\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
0<r<1
. We consider a ball
B_{\rho}(x,r)
on the unt sphere
\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
that is defined by

B_{\rho}(x,r)=\{y\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}:\rho(x,y)<r\}

and its associated Carleson box
\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)
on the unit ball

\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:\frac{z}{|z|}\in B_{\rho}(x,r),1-r<|z|<1\right\}.

By Proposition
2.1
, the Bekollé-Bonami constant
[w]_{p,\alpha}
is equivalent, up to a constant, to the following quantity:

(2.3)

\sup\limits_{B_{\rho}\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{|\widehat{B}_{\rho}|_{\omega,\alpha}}{|\widehat{B}_{\rho}|_{\alpha}}\left(\frac{|\widehat{B}_{\rho}|_{\omega^{1-p^{\prime}},\alpha}}{|\widehat{B}_{\rho}|_{\alpha}}\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
and the supremum runs over all balls
B_{\rho}
satisfying
B_{\rho}(x,r)\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
0<r<1
. For a ball
B_{\rho}(x,r)\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, there exists positive constants
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,\alpha)
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(n)
such that

(2.4)

C_{1}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n+\alpha}(2-r)^{\alpha+1}\leqslant|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}\leqslant C_{2}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n+\alpha}(2-r)^{\alpha+1}.

Proof.

Given a ball
B_{\rho}(x,r)\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, by the inequality (
2.2
),

B(x,2r/\pi)\subset B_{\rho}(x,r)\subset B(x,r).

Here,
B(x,r)=\{y\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}:|x-y|<r\}
denotes the standard Euclidean ball centered at
x
with radius
r
.
Therefore, there exists positive constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(n)
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(n)
such that

c_{1}r^{n-1}\leqslant\int_{B_{\rho}(x,r)}\sigma(\xi)\leqslant c_{2}r^{n-1},

where
d\sigma
is normalized sphere area measure.
Hence,

\displaystyle|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}\int_{\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}d\nu(z)

\displaystyle=nc_{\alpha}\int_{B_{\rho}(x,r)}d\sigma(\xi)\int_{1-r}^{1}t^{n-1}(1-t^{2})^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant nc_{2}c_{\alpha}r^{n-1}\int_{1-r}^{1}t^{n-1}(1-t^{2})^{\alpha}dt.

Since
1-r<t<1
and
n\geqslant 3
, let
C_{2}=nc_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{2}c_{\alpha}r^{n-1}\int_{1-r}^{1}t(1-t^{2})^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle=C_{2}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n+\alpha}(2-r)^{\alpha+1}.

This establishes the upper bound in (
2.4
).

Next, we estimate the lower bound. For
0<r\leqslant 1/2,
we have

\frac{1}{2}\leqslant 1-r<t<1.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geqslant nc_{1}c_{\alpha}r^{n-1}\int_{1-r}^{1}t^{n-1}(1-t^{2})^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}}c_{\alpha}r^{n-1}\int_{1-r}^{1}t(1-t^{2})^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n+\alpha}(2-r)^{\alpha+1}.

For
1/2\leqslant r<1,
we obtain

\displaystyle|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geqslant nc_{1}c_{\alpha}r^{n-1}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}t^{n-1}(1-t^{2})^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}}c_{\alpha}r^{n-1}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}t(1-t^{2})^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\alpha+1}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n-1}.

Since
0<2r-r^{2}<1
, we have

\displaystyle|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\alpha+1}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n-1}(2r-r^{2})^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\alpha+1}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n+\alpha}(2-r)^{\alpha+1}.

Let

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}},\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\alpha+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{nc_{1}}{2^{n-2}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\alpha+1}.

Then, for any
x\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
0<r<1
,

|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}\geqslant C_{1}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n+\alpha}(2-r)^{\alpha+1}.

This is the lower bound in (
2.4
) and we complete the whole proof.
∎

2.2.
An example of
B_{2,\alpha}

By using Lemma
2.2
, in this subsection, we obtain a precise estimate for the Bekollé-Bonami constant
[\omega]_{2,\alpha}
for

\omega(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{s},\quad x\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

where
-1<\alpha<\infty
,
0<\delta<1
and
s=(\alpha+1)(1-\delta)
. Such examples play an important role in the sharp estimate for the norm of
P_{\alpha}
in Section
4
.

Indeed, for any
x\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
0<r<1
, consider the ball
B_{\rho}(x,r)
, we have

|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\omega,\alpha}=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha+s}}|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha+s}\quad\text{ and }\quad|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha-s}}|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha-s}.

Observe that

\alpha+s=(\alpha+1)(2-\delta)-1>-1\quad\text{ and }\quad\alpha-s=(\alpha+1)\delta-1>-1,

and the upper estimate in (
2.4
) holds for any
-1<\alpha<\infty
, we have

|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\omega,\alpha}\leqslant C_{2}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{s+\alpha+1}r^{n+s+\alpha}(2-r)^{s+\alpha+1},

and

|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}\leqslant C_{2}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{-s+\alpha+1}r^{n-s+\alpha}(2-r)^{-s+\alpha+1}.

Besides, by the lower bound in (
2.2
),

|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}\geqslant C_{1}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\alpha+1}r^{n+\alpha}(2-r)^{\alpha+1}.

Note also that we can choose
C_{1},C_{2}
are independent of
\delta
. Hence

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{B_{\rho}(x,r)\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\omega,\alpha}|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}}{|\widehat{B}_{\rho}(x,r)|_{\alpha}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{(s+\alpha+1)(-s+\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}(\alpha+1)}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{\delta}.

It follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) that there is a constant
c_{3}=c_{3}(n,\alpha)
such that

(2.6)

[\omega]_{2,\alpha}\leqslant\frac{c_{3}}{\delta}.

2.3.
The Carleson box system on the real unit ball

To discretize the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection
P_{\alpha}
, we need dyadic systems on
(\partial\mathbb{B}_{n},\rho)
.
A collection of sets

\mathscr{D}=\{Q_{k,i}\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}:1\leqslant k<\infty,1\leqslant i\leqslant M(k)\}

is called a dyadic system on
(\partial\mathbb{B}_{n},\rho)
if

(i)

for each integer
k\geqslant 1
,
\{Q_{k,i}\}_{i=1}^{M(k)}
is a disjoint covering of
\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, i.e.,

•

\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{M(k)}Q_{k,i}
and

•

for all
1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant M(k),Q_{k,i}\cap Q_{k,j}=\emptyset
.

(ii)

if
1\leqslant k\leqslant l
, then either
Q_{l,j}\subset Q_{k,i}
or
Q_{k,i}\cap Q_{l,j}=\emptyset
.

Furthermore, we say the dyadic system
\mathscr{D}
is associated to a triple of positive constants
(\eta,\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1})
if there exists a point set

\mathcal{P}=\{x_{k,i}\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}:1\leqslant k<\infty,1\leqslant i\leqslant M(k)\}

such that

(2.7)

B_{\rho}(x_{k,i},\kappa_{0}\eta^{k})\subset Q_{k,i}\subset B_{\rho}(x_{k,i},\kappa_{1}\eta^{k}),

for each
k\geqslant 1,1\leqslant i\leqslant M(k)
.

By Hytonen and Kairema’s reuslt (
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
), we have the following result on metric space
(\partial\mathbb{B}_{n},\rho)
:

Proposition 2.3
.

Lemma 2.4

Lemma 2.5

Let

For

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{-\frac{1}{2}h\left(1-\frac{r}{2}\right)+h(1-r)}{h(1-r)}

We claim that

∎

Let

Lemma 2.7
.

C_{5}>0
such that for every distinct points
x,y\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
, there exists a ball
B_{\rho}(z,r)\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}

\widehat{B}_{\rho}(z,r)

Pick
x,y\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
with
\rho(x,y)=\theta
.
Without loss of generality, we assume

|x|\geqslant|y|
.
Using the definition of

, we have

\displaystyle{}^{2}

\displaystyle=|x-y|^{2}+(1-|x|^{2})(1-|y|^{2})

\displaystyle=1-2\langle x,y\rangle+|x|^{2}|y|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big|1-|x||y|\big|^{2}+2|x||y|(1-\cos\theta)

\displaystyle\geqslant\big|1-|y|\big|^{2}+4|x||y|\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}

Lemma 2.8
.

There exists a constant

For any two points

3.
Proof of the Main Theorem

3.1.
The case
p=2

The main result in this section is the following theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
-1<\alpha<\infty
.
If
\omega
is a
B_{2,\alpha}
weight, then

P_{\alpha}:L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\longrightarrow L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})

is bounded. Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{7}=C_{7}(n,\alpha)>0
such that

||P_{\alpha}||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C_{7}[\omega]_{2,\alpha}.

Proof.

Assume that
\omega
is a
B_{2,\alpha}
weight. Let
\{\mathscr{D}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{N}
be the collection of dyadic systems obtianed in Proposition
2.3
. By the inequality (
2.8
), we have

[\omega]_{2,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}\leqslant c_{4}[\omega]_{2,\alpha}<\infty.

Using Sawyer’s powerful duality trick
[
18
]
, we have

P_{\alpha}:L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\longrightarrow L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})

is bounded if and only if

P_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}:L^{2}(\omega^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha})\longrightarrow L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})

is bounded, where

P_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}f(y)\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)\omega^{-1}(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y)

for
f\in L^{2}(\omega^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha})
.
Moreover

||P_{\alpha}||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=||P_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}||_{L^{2}(\omega^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}.

For
1\leqslant t\leqslant N
, we define the integral operator on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
induced by the discrete kernel (
2.9
)

\displaystyle T_{\omega,\alpha}^{t}f(x):

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}f(y)\mathcal{K}^{t}_{\alpha}(x,y)\omega(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{t}}\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}f(y)\omega(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y)\chi_{\widehat{Q}}(x).

By Lemma
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle|P_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(y)||\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)|\omega^{-1}(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{6}\sum\limits_{t=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(y)|\mathcal{K}^{t}_{\alpha}(x,y)\omega^{-1}(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle=C_{6}\sum\limits_{t=1}^{N}(T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}|f|)(x).

Hence, it suffices to prove that there exists a constant
c_{8}>0
such that

||T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant c_{8}||f||_{L^{2}(\omega^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha})}

for all
0\leqslant f\in L^{2}(\omega^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha})
and each
1\leqslant t\leqslant N
.

Note that for a fixed dyadic system
\mathscr{D}_{t}
,
T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f\geqslant 0
when
f\geqslant 0
.
Consequentley,

||T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=\sup\limits_{0\leqslant g\in L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha}),||g||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}(T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f)(x)g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x).

Now we consider the right side of the last equality.
Let

0\leqslant g\in L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha}),

we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}(T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f)(x)g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{t}}\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\alpha}}\left(\int_{\widehat{Q}}f(x)\omega^{-1}(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)\left(\int_{\widehat{Q}}g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{t}}\frac{|\widehat{Q}|_{\alpha}}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega,\alpha}}\frac{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega,\alpha}}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\alpha}^{2}}\left(\int_{\widehat{Q}}f(x)\omega^{-1}(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)\left(\int_{\widehat{Q}}g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle[\omega]_{2,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{t}}|\widehat{Q}|_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}f(x)\omega^{-1}(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega,\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right).

By Lemma
2.5
, we have

|\widehat{Q}|_{\alpha}\leqslant C_{4}|\widehat{Q}\backslash\widehat{Q}_{1/2}|_{\alpha}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}(T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f)(x)g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{4}[\omega]_{2,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{t}}|\widehat{Q}\backslash\widehat{Q}_{1/2}|_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}f(x)\omega^{-1}(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega,\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{4}[\omega]_{2,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{t}}\int_{\widehat{Q}\backslash\widehat{Q}_{1/2}}\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}f(x)\omega^{-1}(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega,\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)d\nu_{\alpha}.

Note that Lemma
2.4
implies
\{\widehat{Q}\backslash\widehat{Q}_{1/2}\}_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{t}}
are disjoint. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}(T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f)(x)g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{4}[\omega]_{2,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}f(x)\omega^{-1}(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega,\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\right)d\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{4}[\omega]_{2,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}(M^{t}_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}f)(M^{t}_{\omega,\alpha}g)\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{4}[\omega]_{2,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}||M_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f||_{L^{2}(\omega^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha})}||M_{\omega,\alpha}^{t}g||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}.

Here
M_{\omega,\alpha}^{t}
is the maximal operator associated to the dyadic system
\mathscr{D}_{t}
with respect to measure
\omega d\nu_{\alpha}
, i.e.,

M_{\omega,\alpha}^{t}f(x)=\sup\limits_{Q\in\mathscr{D}_{t}}\frac{\chi_{\widehat{Q}}(x)}{|\widehat{Q}|_{\omega,\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{Q}}|f|\omega d\nu_{\alpha}.

Recall that
\mathscr{D}_{t}
is a dyadic system, by the classical result
[
22
, Theorem 5.7]
on the maxiaml operator on the dyadic systmes, there exists a constant
c_{9}
, independent of the weight
\omega
and the dyadic system
\mathscr{D}_{t}
, such that

||M_{\omega,\alpha}^{t}f||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant c_{9}||f||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})},\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha}).

We conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}(T_{\omega^{-1},\alpha}^{t}f)(x)g(x)\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)\leqslant C_{4}c_{9}^{2}[\omega]_{2,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}||f||_{L^{2}(\omega^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha})}||g||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}.

Let
C_{7}=C_{6}Nc_{4}C_{4}c_{9}^{2}
, we have

||P_{\alpha}||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C_{7}[\omega]_{2,\alpha}.

This completes the proof.
∎

\omega
such that

||R||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C_{8}[\omega]_{2,\alpha}\quad\mbox{for all }\omega\in B_{2,\alpha},

then there exists a constant
C_{9}
independent of the weight
\omega
such that

||R||_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C_{9}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}\quad\mbox{for all }\omega\in B_{p,\alpha}\mbox{ and }p>2.

Proof.

For
p>2
, let

\phi(p)=\frac{p-2}{p-1}

then
p^{\prime}/\phi(p)=p/(p-2)
is the dual number of
p/2
.
By the standard Rubio de Francia’s extrapolation, we only need to find an operator

D:L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})

such that for all

0\leqslant h\in L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\quad\text{and}\quad||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1,

it has following properties:

(I)

h\leqslant D(h)
;

(II)

||D(h)||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant 2
and

(III)

[D(h)\omega]_{2,\alpha}\leqslant C_{10}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}
, where the constant
C_{10}
is independent of
\omega
and
h
.

Suppose that such operator
D
exists. Observe that for all
f\in L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})
, we have

||Rf||^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=\sup\limits_{||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1,h\geqslant 0}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|Rf|^{2}h\omega d\nu_{\alpha}.

Then

\displaystyle||Rf||^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1,h\geqslant 0}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|Rf|^{2}D(h)\omega d\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{8}^{2}\sup\limits_{||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1,h\geqslant 0}\left([D(h)\omega]_{2,\alpha}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f|^{2}D(h)\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{8}^{2}C_{10}^{2}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{2}\sup\limits_{||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1,h\geqslant 0}\left(||f||^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}||D(h)||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2C_{8}^{2}C_{10}^{2}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{2}||f||^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}.

This completes the proof up to the existence of an operator
D
.
∎

Next, we show that the following maximal operator on the real unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
satisifes the properties required by the operator
D
.

Let
M_{\alpha}
be the maximal operator with respect to measure
d\nu_{\alpha}
, i.e.,

M_{\alpha}f(x)=\sup\limits_{B\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\frac{\chi_{\widehat{B}}(x)}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}|f|d\nu_{\alpha},

where the supremum runs over all balls
B\subset\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\omega\in B_{p,\alpha}
, we have

||M_{\alpha}||_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C_{11}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

where
C_{11}
is a constant dependent on
n
and
\alpha
.

One can see Lerner’s work
[
14
]
for a simple proof.
Since
p>2
and
[\omega]_{p,\alpha}\geqslant 1
, we have

(3.11)

||M_{\alpha}||_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C_{11}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\leqslant C_{11}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}.

For
h\in L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})
, denote

S_{\omega,\alpha}(h)=\left(\frac{M_{\alpha}(|h|^{1/\phi(p)}\omega)}{\omega}\right)^{\phi(p)}.

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
h\in L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})
and weight
\omega
, we have

\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}h\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}(S_{\omega,\alpha}(h)\omega)^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha}\right)\leqslant[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Proof.

Observe for
x\in\widehat{B}

M_{\alpha}(h^{1/\phi(p)}\omega)(x)\geqslant\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}h^{1/\phi(p)}\omega d\nu_{\alpha},

Hence, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}h\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}(S_{\omega,\alpha}(h)\omega)^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}h\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}(M_{\alpha}(h^{1/\phi(p)}\omega))^{-\phi(p)}\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}d\nu_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}h^{1/\phi(p)}\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)^{\phi(p)}\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}h^{1/\phi(p)}\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)^{-\phi(p)}\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}d\nu_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

∎

Furthermore, by (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle||S_{\omega,\alpha}(h)||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|M_{\alpha}(|h|^{1/\phi(p)}\omega)|^{p^{\prime}}\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}d\nu_{\alpha}\right)^{\phi(p)/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(||M_{\alpha}||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}d\nu_{\alpha})}\left\||h|^{1/\phi(p)}\omega\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\omega^{1-p^{\prime}}d\nu_{\alpha})}\right)^{\phi(p)}

\displaystyle\leqslant||M_{\alpha}||_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\rightarrow L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}^{\phi(p)}\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{11}^{\phi(p)}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{\phi(p)}\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}.

Denote

(3.12)

A=\sup\limits_{||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1}||S_{\omega,\alpha}(h)||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C_{11}^{\phi(p)}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{\phi(p)}.

We define an operator

D(h)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{(S_{\omega,\alpha})^{k}(h)}{A^{k}},

where
(S_{\omega,\alpha})^{k}
is k-times composition of
S_{\omega,\alpha}
and
(S_{\omega,\alpha})^{0}
is identity operator.
It is easy to see
|h|\leqslant D(h)
.
Now we prove another what we want.

Lemma 3.6
.

For all
h\geqslant 0,||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1
, we have

[D(h)\omega]_{2,\alpha}\leqslant 2C_{11}^{\phi(p)}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}.

Proof.

We have two observations:

(1)

||D(h)||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant 2||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}
;

(2)

S_{\omega,\alpha}(D(h))\leqslant 2AD(h)
.

Hence, by Lemma
3.5
and (
3.12
),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}D(h)\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}(D(h)\omega)^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2A\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}D(h)\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\widehat{B}|_{\alpha}}\int_{\widehat{B}}(S_{\omega,\alpha}(D(h))\omega)^{-1}d\nu_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2A[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2C_{11}^{\phi(p)}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}.

∎

We conclude that for a weight
\omega\in B_{p,\alpha}
and
0\leqslant h\in L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha}),||h||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}=1
, we have
D(h)\omega\in B_{2,\alpha}.

Then by Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|Rf|^{2}h\omega d\nu_{\alpha}\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|Rf|^{2}D(h)\omega d\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{8}^{2}[D(h)\omega]_{2,\alpha}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f|^{2}D(h)\omega d\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2C_{8}^{2}C_{11}^{2\phi(p)}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{2}||f||^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}||D(h)||_{L^{p^{\prime}/\phi(p)}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4C_{8}^{2}C_{11}^{2\phi(p)}[\omega]_{p,\alpha}^{2}||f||^{2}_{L^{p}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}.

This completes the proof.

4.
The sharp linear estimate for
P_{\alpha}

By Theorem
1.1
, if
\omega
is a
B_{2,\alpha}
weight, then there is a constant
C
such that

||P_{\alpha}||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\to L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}\leqslant C[\omega]_{2,\alpha}.

It follows that the norm
||P_{\alpha}||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})\to L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}
has linear growth with respect to the Bekollé-Bonami constant. In this section, we shall show that this linear growth is sharp.

For
n\geqslant 3,
let
-1<\alpha<\infty
and
0<\delta<1
.
Consider the weight on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
defined in Subsection
2.2
:

\omega(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{s}

with
s=(\alpha+1)(1-\delta)
.

By the (
2.6
), there is a constant
c_{3}>0
such that the Bekollé-Bonami constant has an upper estimate:

[\omega]_{2,\alpha}\leqslant\frac{c_{3}}{\delta}.

Let
r_{0}=1
,
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)
and
B_{0}=B_{\rho}(e_{1},r_{0}).

We consider a function

f(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{-s}\chi_{\widehat{B_{0}}}(x).

Then Lemma
2.2
yields the norm estimate

\displaystyle||f||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha-s}}|\widehat{B}_{0}|_{\alpha-s}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c_{10}}{\delta},

where
c_{10}=C_{2}c_{\alpha}/(\alpha+1).

For
\gamma>0
and
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
, we define the nontangential approach region with vertex
\xi
:

\Omega_{\gamma}(\xi):=\{y\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:0<\langle y,\xi\rangle<1,|y|^{2}-|\langle y,\xi\rangle|^{2}<\gamma^{2}(1-|\langle y,\xi\rangle|)^{2}\}.

In fact,
\Omega_{\gamma}(\xi)
is a truncated circular cone in
\mathbb{B}_{n}
with vertex at
\xi
.
Note

\sin(\arctan(\gamma))=\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}}.

Then there exists half a ball with center at
0
and radius
\gamma/\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}
contained in
\Omega_{\gamma}(\xi)
.
Let

G=\left\{x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:\rho\left(\frac{x}{|x|},e_{1}\right)<\frac{\pi}{2}-r_{0},|x|<\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}}}\right\}.

Then

G\subset\bigcap\limits_{\xi\in B_{0}}\Omega_{\gamma}(\xi).

For some fixed
\gamma<1/2
, by Üreyen
[
23
, Proposition 6.2]
, there exists a constant
c_{11}=c_{11}(n,\alpha,\gamma)>0
such that for all
x\in G
and
y\in\widehat{B}_{0}
:

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)\geqslant c_{11}.

Then by Lemma
2.2
, it yields the lower bound when
x\in G

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)d\nu_{\alpha}(y)

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{11}\frac{c_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha-s}}|\widehat{B}_{0}|_{\alpha-s}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{c_{12}}{\delta}.

Here
c_{12}=C_{1}c_{\alpha}c_{11}/(\alpha+1)
.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle||P_{\alpha}(f)||_{L^{2}(\omega d\nu_{\alpha})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{G}|P_{\alpha}(f)(x)|^{2}\omega(x)d\nu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{c_{12}|G|_{\alpha}}{\delta^{2}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{c_{12}|G|_{\alpha}}{c_{3}c_{10}}[\omega]_{2,\alpha}||f||_{L^{2}(\omega dA_{\alpha})}^{2}.

This completes the proof.

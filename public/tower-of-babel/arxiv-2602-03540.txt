Finite time pseudo-rip singularity in cosmology
Mariusz P. Da̧browski, Teodor Borislavov Vasilev

Abstract
By studying first a new decelerating sudden future singularity (SFS) universe we report finding a novel type of cosmological singularity which we dub a finite time pseudo-rip (FTPR) because unlike for a pseudo-rip, it happens in the finite future of the universe. In contrast to the new SFS model, where the expansion is decelerating before reaching the pressure singularity, the FTPR scenario is preceded by a super-accelerated phantom phase. Our claim is based on the thorough study of the energy conditions showing the violations of all of them for a FTPR, and only the dominant energy one for an SFS. Application of the so-called Raychaudhuri averaging shows that, alike within the requirement of geodesic completeness, these singularities are weak in the sense of this definition. We study the properties of the models including the behaviour of the cosmological horizons presented in the appropriate Penrose diagrams.



I
Introduction

Since the discovery of the accelerated expansion of the Universe and the postulation of dark energy
Riess et al. 1998
;
Perlmutter et al. 1999
, together with recent reports of observational tensions
Di Valentino et al. 2021
, a variety of models have been proposed as alternatives to the standard
\Lambda
CDM cosmology. These models aim to provide a viable late-time behaviour while alleviating some of the conceptual problems of the cosmological constant
Weinberg 1989
;
Adler et al. 1995
. However, deviations from the standard model are usually accompanied with theoretical and observational challenges of their own
Bull et al. 2016
;
Perivolaropoulos and Skara 2022
. In particular, the exploration of strongly negative pressure components, such as phantom energy
Caldwell 2002
;
Caldwell et al. 2003
;
Da̧browski et al. 2003
, has revealed the possibility of future singularities like the big-rip (BR), leading to the destruction of all bound structures, and has motivated numerous extensions that fully or partially violate the Hawking–Penrose energy conditions (EC) in General Relativity
Hawking and Ellis 1973
. These singularities showed up consecutively following Barrow’s paper on the sudden future singularity (SFS) models
Barrow 2004a
;
Barrow 2004b
and later have been developed by other researches. The first summary of the possibilities called type I to IV have been given in Ref.
Nojiri et al. 2005
and later expanded, among others, in Ref.
Da̧browski 2014
. In general, models which fall outside the classical big-bang (BB) singularity are also known under the name of “exotic” singularities due to their diverse behaviour in comparison to their original canon.

In this paper, we investigate two new exotic singularity models by postulating some specific behaviour of the Hubble parameter related to the cosmological fluid energy density: one violating the dominant energy condition only and another violating all the energy conditions. Both models lead to a future curvature singularity in a finite value of cosmological time. These singularities may be classified as an SFS and a new type of event, hereafter referred to as a finite-time pseudo-rip (FTPR), which–up to certain subtleties discussed in the paper–differs from other intrinsic phantom scenarios such as the pseudo-rip (PR)
Frampton et al. 2012
;
Trivedi and Timoshkin 2024
, the little sibling of the big-rip (LSBR)
Bouhmadi-López et al. 2015
, the little-rip (LR)
Frampton et al. 2011
, the BR
Caldwell 2002
;
Caldwell et al. 2003
;
Da̧browski et al. 2003
or the big-freeze (BF)
Bouhmadi-López et al. 2008
;
Bouhmadi-López et al. 2008
.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
we present our universe models which admit the exotic singularities. In Section
III
we explore these models in terms of the energy conditions. In Section
IV
we present the Penrose diagrams for the models which allow to give insight into their asymptotic structure. In Section
V
we test the models using the Raychaudhuri weak singularity criterion. In the last Section
VI
we present our conclusions.

II
New exotic singularity models

II.1
A decelerating sudden future singularity model

We propose a new model of SFS
Barrow 2004a
(or type II
Nojiri et al. 2005
;
Gorini et al. 2004
;
Da̧browski et al. 2007
;
Keresztes et al. 2009
;
Keresztes et al. 2010
) universe which is described by the Hubble rate

\displaystyle H[a(t)]\equiv\frac{\dot{a}}{a}=H_{\star}\frac{a_{s}^{n}}{a^{2}(t)}\left(1-\frac{a(t)}{a_{s}}\right)^{n},

(1)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor, and
H_{\star}
and
a_{s}
are positive constants. We also consider that
n
is a positive parameter that obeys the restriction

0<n<\frac{1}{2},

(2)

which will be explained below. From this expression for the Hubble rate, the second derivative of the scale factor reads

\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-H_{\star}^{2}\frac{a_{s}^{2n}}{a^{4}}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{2n-1}\left[1-(1-n)\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right].

(3)

To discuss on the phenomenology of this model is useful to obtain the effective energy density and pressure induced on a flat spatial geometry isotropic Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) cosmology. These are

\displaystyle\varrho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{8\pi G}H^{2},

(4)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{c^{2}}{8\pi G}\left(2\dot{H}+3H^{2}\right),

(5)

respectively, being
G
the Newton’s gravitational constant,
c
the speed of light and
\dot{H}
represent the cosmic time derivative of the Hubble rate Moreover, using time derivatives of the scale factor (
1
)-(
3
), it is possible to produce the equation of state (for a flat
k=0
universe) as

\displaystyle w\equiv\frac{p}{\varrho c^{2}}=\frac{1-(1-2n)\frac{a(t)}{a_{s}}}{3\left(1-\frac{a(t)}{a_{s}}\right)},

(6)

which plays the role of a time-dependent barotropic index.

It is straightforward to check that the limit
a\to 0
corresponds to a radiation-induced BB initial singularity, at which
\varrho
and
p
diverge, whereas the corresponding equation of state parameter
w\equiv p/\varrho=1/3
(see Fig.
1
). This is a new feature of our parametrisation that is not present in some previously addressed SFS models; see, for instance, Ref.
Da̧browski and Marosek 2018
where spatial-curvature-like effects dominated at early stages in the expansion. For the limit
a\to a_{s}
, on the other hand, the Hubble rate (
1
) vanishes if
n>0
, says constant for
n=0
or diverge for
n<0

The acceleration of the scale factor (
3

or diverges to infinity for
n<1/2
. Thus, for

. As advanced in expression (

), this behaviour correspond to a SFS or Type II singularity; see, for instance,
Nojiri et al. 2005
. Interestingly, the pressure starts from infinity at BB, then diminishes reaching minimum in order to blow up to (positive) infinity again at SFS (cf. Fig.
1
). This signals a decelerating behaviour near the SFS. Nevertheless, the equation (
3
) confirms that the model at hands represents, in fact, an ever decelerating universe since
\ddot{a}
is always negative for
a\in(0,a_{s})
. In our approach, SFS plays the role of an extra fluid in the universe apart from the radiation.

For the later case, i.e. when close to the SFS, the scale factor reads

\displaystyle\frac{a(t)}{a_{s}}\approx 1-\left(\frac{1-\frac{t}{t_{s}}}{2-n}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-n}}.

It is also useful to calculate the conformal time

II.2
An accelerating towards a pseudo-rip singularity model

The new model (
1
) parametrise an ever decelerating universe which lies at total odds with current observations.
Late-time acceleration which would mimic the dark energy due to a new singularity appearance can be achieved by modifying the model (
1
). An option is to make the terms responsible for BB domination (
\sim a^{-2}
) and SFS domination
(\sim(1-a/a_{n})^{n})
additive, not multiplicative. This is

\displaystyle H[a(t)]\equiv\frac{\dot{a}}{a}=\frac{H_{1}}{a^{2}}-H_{2}a_{s}^{n}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{n},

(16)

where a minus sign between the two terms is necessary to produce the acceleration. The second derivative of the scale factor
a(t)
, follows from (
16
), and reads

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{H_{1}^{2}}{a^{4}}+nH_{1}H_{2}\frac{a_{s}^{n-1}}{a}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{n-1}

(17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle H_{2}^{2}a_{s}^{2n}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{2n}-nH_{2}^{2}aa_{s}^{2n-1}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{2n-1}.

From (
17
) we notice that its first term always decelerates, the second always accelerates and eventually blows up towards minus infinity at
a=a_{s}
for
0<n<1
. The third term in (
17
) vanishes at
a=a_{s}
, whereas the last term blows up only if
0<n<1/2
. Nevertheless, the latter term is always subdominant near the singularity since the second (negative) term diverges strongly. This behaviour is portrayed in the top of Fig.
3
, where
\ddot{a}
changes its sign and, finally, diverges to positive infinity, while
H
remains finite; see Eq. (
16
). Contrary to the usual decelerating SFS behaviour discussed before, this signals the presence of an extreme acceleration event at
a_{s}
for
n\in(0,1)
.

Moreover, by the very structure of the Hubble rate (
16
), one can claim that the divergence of pressure at
a=a_{s}
always takes place in a finite amount of time since the integral

\displaystyle t-t_{s}=\int_{0}^{a_{s}}\frac{da}{aH}<\infty

(18)

is clearly finite. The evolution of the equation of state parameter
w
of this model is presented in Fig.
3
for
n=0.2
and
H_{1}a_{s}^{-2}=H_{2}a_{s}^{n}=1
. The universe starts its evolution at
a\to 0
at a BB with the equation of state parameter
w=1/3
signalling radiation domination. Then, this parameter has a maximum
w\approx 1.04
at
a\approx 0.778a_{s}
signalling an effective stiff-fluid (or even super stiff-fluid) behaviour (kinetic energy domination
Khoury et al. 2001
;
Steinhardt and Turok 2002
;
Khoury et al. 2002
. After reaching the maximum, the universe turns into an accelerated phase at around
a\approx 0.93a_{s}
, and finally it crosses the phantom divide
w=-1
at
a\approx 0.95a_{s}
. This type of behaviour is quite generic for the model (
16
). Moreover, the lesser the parameter
n
is in the open interval
(0,1)
, the higher is the maximum of
w
. Indeed, in the extreme case
n=0.01
, the model exhibits a pronounced peak around
w\approx 12.5
, indicating super-stiff behaviour at
a\approx 0.981a_{s}
, shortly before crossing the phantom divide at
a\approx 0.999a_{s}
. By contrast, in the opposite limiting case, for instance
n=0.99
, the maximum is much milder, with
w\approx 0.43
occurring already halfway to the singularity, i.e. at
a\approx 0.502a_{s}
.

In conclusion, the evolution of the model (
16
) starts at a classical radiation-dominated BB initial singularity and evolves towards a pressure singularity at
a\to a_{s}
within a finite amount of cosmic time. The pressure becomes negative and grows unbounded in the vicinity of
a_{s}
, thus leading to a super-accelerated regime before reaching the curvature singularity. The strong phantom dominance at
a\to a_{s}
(see Fig.
3
) closely resembles that found in other phantom-induced singularities such as the BF, the BR, the LR, or the LSBR. Nevertheless, the latter two occur only at infinite future time, whereas the BR leads to
a_{s}\to\infty
, in contrast with the finite values of
t_{s}
and
a_{s}
obtained here. The BF does occur at finite values of the cosmic time and scale factor; however, in that case the energy density cannot remain constant. This reasoning also excludes the more general finite-scale-factor, or type III, singularities
Nojiri et al. 2005
;
Da̧browski 2014
.

According to the usual classification of cosmological singularities solely in terms of the values of the cosmic time, scale factor, (effective) energy density, and (effective) pressure at the singularity
Nojiri et al. 2005
;
Da̧browski 2014
, one may be tempted to conclude that (
16
) leads to an SFS or Type II singularity. However, as we argue in the next section, we find this conclusion to be premature, since this model does not satisfy the ECs that served as the very definition and motivation of the original SFS
Barrow 2004a
;
Barrow 2004b
. Bearing these considerations in mind, we suggest the name “finite-time pseudo-rip,” or FTPR in short, for this future singularity. It is important to notice that the FTPR considered here differs from the pseudo-rip originally introduced in Ref.
Frampton et al. 2012
in that both
t_{s}
and
a_{s}
are finite at the FTPR. Moreover, the FTPR represents a true curvature singularity, since the Ricci scalar clearly diverges when the effective pressure blows up, in contrast with the pseudo-rip. Alike the SFS, geodesics could, in principle, be extended through the FTPR as
a
and
\dot{a}
remain regular, whereas only
\ddot{a}
diverges, leading to potentially severe tidal forces acting on extended objects
Fernández-Jambrina and Lazkoz 2004
.

III
Energy conditions for new models

In this section we discuss the EC of Hawking and Penrose
Hawking and Ellis 1973
for the isotropic FLRW cosmological models presented before. These conditions can are:

•

Null Energy Condition (NEC):
\varrho c^{2}+p\geq 0
;

•

Weak Energy Condition (WEC):
\varrho c^{2}\geq 0
,
\varrho c^{2}+p\geq 0
;

•

Dominant energy Condition (DEC):
\varrho c^{2}\geq\mid p\mid
;

•

Strong Energy Condition (SEC):
\varrho c^{2}+p\geq 0
,
\varrho c^{2}+3p\geq 0
.

Generaly DEC implies WEC and then NEC while SEC implies NEC, which means that NEC is the most extreme violation of all.

Applying the equations (
4
) and (
5
) one can write the above conditions in the form

•

NEC:
\varrho c^{2}+p=-\frac{c^{2}}{4\pi G}\dot{H}\geq 0
;

•

WEC:
\varrho c^{2}=\frac{3c^{2}}{8\pi G}\geq 0
,
\varrho c^{2}+p=-\frac{c^{2}}{4\pi G}\dot{H}\geq 0
;

•

DEC-I:
\varrho c^{2}\geq-p\Rrightarrow-\frac{c^{2}}{4\pi G}\dot{H}\geq 0
;

•

DEC-II:
\varrho c^{2}\geq p\Rrightarrow\dot{H}+3H^{2}\geq 0
;

•

SEC:
\varrho c^{2}+3p\geq 0\Rrightarrow\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=\dot{H}+H^{2}\leq 0
;

where the DEC has been divided into DEC-I and DEC-II depending whether the pressure takes negative or positive values, respectively.

III.1
Decelerating SFS model

For the decelerating model (
1
), using (
6
), the NEC reads

\varrho c^{2}+p=\varrho c^{2}\left(\frac{4}{3}+\frac{2n}{3}\frac{a}{a_{s}-a}\right)\geq 0,

(19)

and is always fulfilled since
n\geq 0
and
a_{s}-a>0
. Of course at singularity for
a=a_{s}
,
\varrho c^{2}+p\to\infty
, because of the pressure blow-up. WEC is also trivially fulfilled on the base of (
4
) (
\varrho\sim H^{2}\geq 0
always), and the condition (
19
).

In order to check the expected violation of DEC at the SFS, we inspect separately the DEC-I and DEC-II. Using equations (
1
) and (
3
), the requirement to fulfil the DEC-I leads to the condition

\frac{a}{a_{s}}\leq\frac{2}{2-n},

(20)

which is fulfilled for all
a\in[0,a_{s}]
for the values of interest for the parameter
n
, i.e. for
n\in(0,1/2)
. On the contrary, the fulfilment of the DEC-II demands

\frac{a}{a_{s}}\leq\frac{1}{1+n}.

(21)

Since
1/1+n
is clearly less than one for
n
positive, this condition can only be satisfied at some early stages in the evolution of the model. However, when
a\approx a_{s}
it is always violated and, consequently, the DEC too. This is, of course, to be expected since the SFS should break the DEC condition
Barrow 2004a
;
Barrow 2004b
.

Finally, the SEC fulfilment requires

\frac{a}{a_{s}}\leq\frac{1}{1-n},

(22)

and it is always satisfied on the base of the condition that
a\leq a_{s}
and the assumption of
n\in(0,1/2)
.

Summarising the EC for SFS models, the NEC is generally fulfilled while DEC is not since the pressure dominates at the singularity. The singularity is weak, at least in the sense of Tipler
Tipler 1977
and Królak
Królak 1986
, though the pressure has a finite-time divergence. So, SFS can also be called “sudden pressure singularity” since the energy density does not blow-up.

III.2
Accelerating PR model

For the model (
16
), the NEC (i.e.
\dot{H}\leq 0
) reads

\displaystyle 2H_{1}H_{2}\frac{a_{s}^{n}}{a^{2}}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{n}+nH_{1}H_{2}\frac{a_{s}^{n-1}}{a}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle-2\frac{H_{1}^{2}}{a^{4}}-nH_{2}^{2}aa_{s}^{2n-1}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{2n-1}\leq 0,

(23)

which shows that it is fulfilled at
t=0
when
a\approx 0
, i.e. at the BB, since the third term in (
III.2
) dominates and it is negative. However, at
t\approx t_{s}
when
a\approx a_{s}
we can differentiate two cases. These are:
i
)
0\leq n<1/2
, and
ii
)
1/2<n<1
. In the former case, the second term in (
III.2
) dominates over the fourth term, since the power in the blowing up term is more negative (i.e.
n-1<2n-1<0
) in this case, though both of them blow up at
a=a_{s}
. The second term contributes positively while the fourth negatively and so we conclude that NEC is violated which in view of the finite value of the energy density given by (
16
) seems to fulfil the condition of a pseudo-rip singularity
Trivedi and Timoshkin 2024
. In the second
ii
), only the second term in (
III.2
) diverges, while the fourth term vanishes at
a=a_{s}
and so again we have a pseudo-rip singularity. Bearing in mind that WEC requires additionally only the positivity of the energy density, which is always fulfilled according to (
4
), we can conclude that the WEC is also violated at
a\approx a_{s}
due to the second term in (
III.2
).

As for DEC-II we have from (
16
) and (
III.2
) that

\displaystyle\frac{H_{1}^{2}}{a^{4}}-4H_{1}H_{2}\frac{a_{s}^{n}}{a^{2}}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{n}+nH_{1}H_{2}\frac{a_{s}^{n-1}}{a}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle-nH_{2}^{2}aa_{s}^{2n-1}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{2n-1}+H_{2}^{2}a_{s}^{2n}\left(1-\frac{a}{a_{s}}\right)^{2n}\geq 0.

Obviously, it is fulfilled at BB singularity where
a(0)=0
. As for
a=a_{s}
, unlike in the previous cases, now the third term with the power
(n-1)
contributes positively securing the fulfilment of DEC-II. The DEC-I, however, cannot be satisfied at
a\approx a_{s}
since this condition is directly related to the NEC. Thus, the DEC-II and, therefore, the DEC are indeed violated for (
16
) at least at
a\approx a_{s}
.

SEC can be discussed while looking at the formula (
17
) with the conclusion that it is fulfilled at BB singularity (the first term
\propto a^{-4}
contributes negatively as it should be), while it is not at
a=a_{s}
since the dominating second term with the power
(n-1)
contributes positively and stronger than the last term.

In summary, for the model (
16
), all the aforementioned ECs are satisfied at the onset of the corresponding evolution. However, each of them is violated at some stage during the expansion history. Consequently, at
a\approx a_{s}
none of the energy conditions holds. These violations are, of course, associated with the term proportional to the power
n-1
in expression (
III.2
), which induces the phantom crossing and ultimately drives the effective pressure to diverge to minus infinity. This behaviour stands in sharp contrast to that of the SFS model (
1
), where the effective pressure diverges to positive infinity and, as a result, only the DEC is violated. This indicates that the event occurring at
a=a_{s}
in model (
16
) is fundamentally different from that of the specific SFS model (
1
) considered here, as well as from the general definition of SFS
Barrow 2004a
. For this reason, we proposed the designation FTPR for the future singularity arising in model (
16
), in order to clearly distinguish it from the SFS case, in which only the DEC is violated.

Raychaudhuri averaging and related strength of exotic singularities

The general SFS scenario and the FTPR found here are weak singularities
Nojiri et al. 2005

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed a method to construct solutions of the cosmological field equations by prescribing a specific functional form of the Hubble parameter capable of inducing different types of future cosmological singularities. This approach provides a particularly straightforward framework for analysing the ECs within general relativity, which in turn allows one to characterise the nature and strength of the resulting singularities in relation to the notion of geodesic incompleteness.

Within this approach, we have explicitly constructed two new models driving the cosmological expansion towards a standard SFS and a novel FTPR (finite-time pseudo-rip) singularity. Both singularities exhibit a similar structural behaviour in that the cosmic time of occurrence, the scale factor, and the (effective) energy density remain finite, while the pressure diverges, i.e.
|p|\to\infty
as
t\to t_{s}
. On this basis, one might be tempted to classify both events as SFS
Barrow 2004a
(or Type II singularities in the sense of Ref.
Nojiri et al. 2005
). However, we have argued that such a classification is misleading, since the two scenarios exhibit fundamentally different behaviour when analysed from the perspective of the ECs. While the original SFS was introduced as a scenario in which only the DEC is violated
Barrow 2004a
;
Barrow 2004b
, the model (
16
) instead, displays a phantom-dominated future evolution that leads to the violation of all ECs.

Although only two example models have been addressed, the method can be applied to generate more solutions containing a rich set of exotic singularities in cosmology. Some examples of these singular behaviours that have attracted the attention of many authors in recent years (for a review see Refs.
Nojiri et al. 2005
;
Da̧browski 2014
), but for our study the most appropriate were a big-rip, a little-rip, a little sibling of the big-rip, a pseudo-rip, and a big-freeze. We have also presented some systematics to study the energy conditions in general relativity which allow to characterise the types of singularities in respect to their strength related to the notion of geodesic incompleteness. In fact, both singularities considered in this work are of a weak type and the evolution of the universe can be extended beyond them.

We have also discussed the global characteristics of the models at hands by using the method of Penrose diagrams. In particular, we have investigated the structure of cosmological horizons (event, particle, and apparent), as well as the asymptotic structure of singularities. Though only a limited time of evolution of the universe between the standard BB radiation-dominated singularity at time
t=0
to the future exotic singularity at time
t_{s}
has been addressed, due to the fact of geodesic completeness of exotics, one may extend them symmetrically into a future region evolving towards another big-crunch singularity as in Ref.
Da̧browski and Marosek 2018
; thus, making kind of a cyclic universe modelling. Finally, we have found by using the method of the so-called Raychaudhuri averaging that the two models under study are also weak with respect to this definition. We hope both the method of generating the weak singularity solutions and the method of Raychaudhuri averaging may prove successful tool in future cosmological investigations including their observational verification.

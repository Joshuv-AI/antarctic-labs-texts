High-order long-time asymptotics for small solutions to the one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Jacek Jendrej, Tony Salvi

Abstract




Abstract

We investigate the global well-posedness and modified scattering for the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation with gauge-invariant polynomial nonlinearity. For small localized initial data of finite energy in a low-regularity class, we establish global existence of solution together with persistence of the localization of the associated profile. We further provide a rigorous derivation of the asymptotic expansion at arbitrary order of such solutions, taking into account long-range effects induced by the cubic component of the nonlinearity. Our analysis relies on the space-time resonance method.

Introduction

In this paper, we examine the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) in
1
dimension with a polynomial nonlinearity

i\partial_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}\Delta u=\sum_{n=1}^{d}\lambda_{n}|u|^{2n}u

(1.1)

such that there exists
n\in\{1,\dots,d\}
with
\lambda_{n}\neq 0
,
and we consider the Cauchy problem for initial data
u_{1}
at

u_{1}=e^{i\Delta/2}f_{1}

Notation 1.1
.

The usual Sobolev spaces on
\mathbb{R}
are written as
W^{k,p}_{x}
. In the particular case where

H^{k}_{x}
. When working in the frequency variable, we write them as
W^{k,p}_{\xi}
and

The weighted Sobolev spaces

are defined by
H^{k,m}_{x}:=\{\phi\in S^{\prime}|\;||\phi||_{H^{k,m}_{x}}=||\langle x\rangle^{m}\langle\nabla\rangle^{k}\phi||_{L^{2}_{x}}<\infty\}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we also have the equivalence
||\phi||_{H^{k,m}_{x}}\sim\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}||\langle x\rangle^{m}\partial^{j}\phi||^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)^{1/2}
.

The Bochner-Lebesgue space
L^{\infty}_{t}X
, for
X
a Banach space, denotes
L^{\infty}([1,\infty),X)
.

In particular, the initial data must have finite energy
||u_{1}||_{H^{1,0}_{x}}<\varepsilon_{0}
and be localized
||f_{1}||_{H^{0,2N+1}_{x}}<\varepsilon_{0}
, for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small. Then, our result concerns the asymptotic behavior of the solution
u
, corresponding to such initial data, at any order when
t\to\infty
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, if
||u_{1}||_{H^{1,0}_{x}}+||f_{1}||_{H^{0,2N+1}_{x}}<\varepsilon_{0}
, then there exists a unique global solution to (
1.1

\displaystyle||u(t)||_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\frac{C(\varepsilon_{0})}{(1+t)^{1/2}}

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{e^{\frac{ix^{2}}{2t}-i\lambda_{1}|u_{0,0}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)|^{2}\ln(t))-i\varphi(\frac{x}{t})}}{(it)^{1/2}}\sum_{p=0}^{N}\sum^{2p}_{k=0}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{p}}u_{p,k}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+u_{err.}(t,x)

The first step consists in constructing a local solution

(u_{1},e^{-i\Delta/2}u_{1})

H^{1,0}_{x}\times H^{0,2N+1}_{x}

Next, in a bootstrap argument, we show that we can extend the time of existence for small enough initial data (for
\varepsilon_{0}

This strategy has already been employed to obtain the first-order (

In this section, we derive the expansion of the profile

\displaystyle\left(\sum^{l-j+1}_{k=1}\sum^{2k}_{m=0}\frac{\ln(t)^{m}}{t^{k}}F_{k,m}(\xi)+r^{l-j+1}_{F}(t,\xi)\right)^{j}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\overrightarrow{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}^{(l-j+1)},|\overrightarrow{\mu}|=j,\;J\in\mathscr{J}_{\overrightarrow{\mu}}}\binom{j}{J}\left(\prod^{l-j+1}_{k=1}\prod^{2k}_{m=0}(\frac{\ln(t)^{m}}{t^{k}}F_{k,m}(\xi))^{J_{k,m}}\right)

\displaystyle+r^{l,j}_{\Theta}(t,\xi)

where
r^{l,j}_{F}(t)\in W^{2(N-l+j-1),\infty}_{\xi}
and where
\mathscr{J}_{\overrightarrow{\mu}}
is from definition
5.13
, with
\binom{j}{J}=\frac{j!}{\prod^{l-j+1}_{k=1}\prod^{2k}_{m=0}J_{k,m}!}
. Then, rearranging the different terms by powers of
t^{-1}
gives

\displaystyle\left(\sum^{l-j+1}_{k=1}\sum^{2k}_{m=0}\frac{\ln(t)^{m}}{t^{k}}F_{k,m}(\xi)+r^{l-j+1}_{F}(t,\xi)\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=j}^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{2k}\frac{\ln(t)^{m}}{t^{k}}\Theta^{j}_{k,m}(\xi)+r^{\prime l,j}_{\Theta}(t,\xi)

with
\Theta^{j}_{k,m}\in W^{2(N-k),\infty}_{\xi}
, a product of
j
factors of
F_{m,k}
-terms, and
r^{\prime l,j}_{\Theta}(t)\in W^{2(N-l+j-1),\infty}_{\xi}
. Finally, collecting all contributions yields

\displaystyle\Theta(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{i\ln(t)\lambda_{1}|\hat{w}_{0,0}(\xi)|^{2}+F_{0,0}(\xi)}(\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{2j}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{j}}\Theta_{j,k}(\xi))+r^{l}_{\Theta}(t,\xi)

with

\displaystyle\Theta_{0,0}(\xi)=1,

and

\displaystyle\Theta_{j,k}(\xi)=\sum_{m=1}^{j}\frac{1}{m!}\Theta^{m}_{j,k}(\xi),

if
j\geq 1
, and

\displaystyle r^{l}_{\Theta}(t,\xi)=e^{i\ln(t)\lambda_{1}|\hat{w}_{0,0}(\xi)|^{2}+F_{0,0}(\xi)}\sum_{j=1}^{l}r^{\prime l,j}_{\Theta}(t,\xi)+r^{\prime l}_{\Theta}(t,\xi),

which satisfies the required decay estimate.
∎

We now deduce the asymptotic expansion of
\hat{f}(t)
.

Proof of proposition
8.12
.

For all
0\leq l\leq N
, we write

\displaystyle\hat{f}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Theta(t,\xi)}\hat{w}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\ln(t)\lambda_{1}|\hat{w}_{0,0}(\xi)|^{2}-F_{0,0}(\xi)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{2j}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{j}}\overline{\Theta_{j,k}(\xi)}+\overline{r^{l}_{\Theta}(t,\xi)}\right)\left(\sum^{l}_{j=0}\sum^{2j}_{k=0}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{j}}\hat{w}_{j,k}(\xi)+r^{l}(t,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-i\ln(t)\lambda_{1}|\hat{w}_{0,0}(\xi)|^{2}-F_{0,0}(\xi)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{2j}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{j}}\hat{f}(\xi)_{j,k}\right)+r^{l}_{f}(t,\xi).

where

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{j,k}(\xi)=\sum_{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{2},|\overrightarrow{\mu}|=j,|\overrightarrow{\nu}|=k,\overrightarrow{\nu}_{l}\leq 2\overrightarrow{\mu}_{l}}\overline{\Theta_{\overrightarrow{\mu}_{1},\overrightarrow{\nu}_{1}}(\xi)}\hat{w}_{\overrightarrow{\mu}_{2},\overrightarrow{\nu}_{2}}(\xi).

(8.28)

Clearly, proposition
8.13
and proposition
8.3
imply that the expansion coefficients satisfy
\hat{f}_{j,k}\in W^{2(N-j),\infty}_{\xi}
and the error term satisfies
||r^{l}_{f}(t)||_{W^{2(N-l),\infty}_{\xi}}=O(t^{-l-\delta})
for some
1>\delta>0
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{0,0}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\overline{\Theta_{0,0}(\xi)}\hat{w}_{0,0}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\hat{w}_{0,0}(\xi),

which ends the proof.
∎

8.3
Asymptotics for the solution

In this section, we derive the asymptotics of the solution
u
to (
1.1
) itself and conclude the proof of the main Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From proposition
7.8
, we know that there exists a global solution
u\in C([1,T],H^{1})
which satisfies the sharp decay estimate:

\displaystyle t^{1/2}||u(t)||_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq||t^{1/2}u(t)||_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\varepsilon_{0})

for all
t\geq 1
, by (
7.11
). Therefore, to get the full statement of Theorem
1.1
, it remains to derive the high-order asymptotic expansion of
u
.

Recall that the fundamental solution of the linear Schrödinger equation is

\displaystyle K(t,x)=\left(\frac{1}{2i\pi t}\right)^{1/2}e^{\frac{ix^{2}}{2t}}.

(8.29)

Hence,

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=e^{it\Delta/2}e^{-it\Delta/2}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=e^{it\Delta/2}f(t,x)

\displaystyle=(K(t)\star f(t))(x)

\displaystyle=\int\left(\frac{1}{2i\pi t}\right)^{1/2}e^{\frac{i|x-y|^{2}}{2t}}f(t,y)dy

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{2t}}\left(\frac{1}{it}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int e^{-i\frac{xy}{t}}(e^{\frac{i|y|^{2}}{2t}}f(t,y))dy

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{2t}}\left(\frac{1}{it}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int e^{-i\frac{xy}{t}}(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{i|y|^{2}}{2t}\right)^{j}f(t,y)+\left(e^{\frac{i|y|^{2}}{2t}}-\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{i|y|^{2}}{2t}\right)^{j}\right)f(t,y))dy

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{i|x|^{2}}{2t}}\left(\frac{1}{it}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int e^{-i\frac{xy}{t}}(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{i|y|^{2}}{2t}\right)^{j}f(t,y))dy+u_{err.1}^{N}(t,x).

We can already state that, for
1/4>\beta>0
, the remainder satisfies

\displaystyle u_{err.1}^{N}(t,x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{1/2}}\int\left(\frac{|y|^{2}}{2t}\right)^{N+\beta}f(t,y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{N+1/2+\beta}}\int\langle y\rangle^{2(N+\beta)}f(t,y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{N+1/2+\beta-\alpha_{2N+1}}}||t^{-\alpha_{2N+1}}\langle y\rangle^{2(N+\beta)+1/2+\varepsilon}f||_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}.

Thus, using the bound
||t^{-\alpha_{2N+1}}f||_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{0,2N+1}_{x}}<C_{0}
of (
7.11
) in proposition
7.8
leads to

\displaystyle u_{err.1}^{N}(t,x)\lesssim\frac{C_{0}}{t^{N+1/2+\beta-\alpha_{2N+1}}}.

This implies that
||u_{err.1}^{N}(t)||_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=O(t^{-1/2-N-\delta})
for some
\delta>0
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int e^{-i\frac{xy}{t}}(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{i|y|^{2}}{2t}\right)^{j}f(t,y))dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{1}{t^{j}}\frac{i^{j}}{2^{j}j!}(-\Delta_{\xi})^{j}\hat{f}\left(t,\frac{x}{t}\right).

By proposition
8.12
, and under the assumption of Theorem
1.1
, the derivatives
\partial_{\xi}^{2q}\hat{f}(t)
admit asymptotic expansions up to order
N-q
in
L^{\infty}_{\xi}
. Each Laplacian contributes two derivatives and an additional power
\frac{1}{t}
, thus matching the balance between control on the derivatives and order of expansion. To compute
\partial_{\xi}^{2q}\hat{f}(t)
explicitly, one must consider derivatives falling on the phase
-\ln(t)F_{1,0}-F_{0,0}
, which is defined in proposition
8.12
. For that, we set
F_{0}=\ln(t)F_{1,0}+F_{0,0}
and apply Faà di Bruno’s formula:

\displaystyle\partial^{2q}_{\xi}\hat{f}(t)=e^{-F_{0}}\sum_{l=0}^{2q}\binom{2q}{l}(\sum_{m=0}^{l}B_{l,m}(-F^{\prime}_{0},\dots,-F_{0}^{(l-m+1)}))\partial_{\xi}^{2q-l}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-q}\sum_{k=0}^{2j}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{j}}\hat{f}_{j,k}\right)+\partial^{2q}r^{N-q}_{f}(t),

(8.30)

where the
B
represents Bell’s polynomials. We decompose Bell’s polynomials as follows

\displaystyle B_{l,m}(x_{1}+y_{1},\dots,x_{l-m+1}+y_{l-m+1})=\sum_{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{l-m+1},|\overrightarrow{\mu}|,|\overrightarrow{\nu}|\leq m}B_{l,m}^{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{l-m+1}(x_{j}^{\overrightarrow{\mu}_{j}}y_{j}^{\overrightarrow{\nu}_{j}}),

(8.31)

for some coefficients
B_{l,m}^{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}}
independent of
\xi
. Gathering logarithm contributions together leads to

\displaystyle B_{l,m}(-F^{\prime}_{0},\dots,-F_{0}^{(l-m+1)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{l-m+1},|\overrightarrow{\mu}|,|\overrightarrow{\nu}|\leq m}B_{l,m}^{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{l-m+1}((-\ln(t)\partial_{\xi}^{j}F_{1,0})^{\overrightarrow{\mu}_{j}}(-\partial_{\xi}^{j}F_{0,0})^{\overrightarrow{\nu}_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\ln(t)^{k}\sum_{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{l-m+1},|\overrightarrow{\mu}|=k,|\overrightarrow{\nu}|\leq m}B_{l,m}^{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{l-m+1}((-\partial_{\xi}^{j}F_{1,0})^{\overrightarrow{\mu}_{j}}(-\partial_{\xi}^{j}F_{0,0})^{\overrightarrow{\nu}_{j}}).

We then use the condensate form

\displaystyle B_{l,m}(-F^{\prime}_{0},\dots,-F_{0}^{(l-m+1)})=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\ln(t)^{k}b_{l,m,k}(F_{1,0},F_{0,0})

with

\displaystyle b_{l,m,k}(F_{1,0},F_{0,0})=\sum_{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}^{l-m+1},|\overrightarrow{\mu}|=k,|\overrightarrow{\nu}|\leq m}B_{l,m}^{\overrightarrow{\mu},\overrightarrow{\nu}}\prod_{j=1}^{l-m+1}((-\partial_{\xi}^{j}F_{1,0})^{\overrightarrow{\mu}_{j}}(-\partial_{\xi}^{j}F_{0,0})^{\overrightarrow{\nu}_{j}}).

We can now group together all the
\ln(t)^{k}
contributions from the different polynomials and write

\displaystyle\partial^{2q}_{\xi}\hat{f}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{-F_{0}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-q}\frac{1}{t^{j}}\sum_{l=0}^{2q}\binom{2q}{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}(\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{m}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{2j}\ln(t)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}b_{l,m,k_{1}}\partial_{\xi}^{2q-l}\hat{f}_{j,k_{2}})+\partial^{2q}r^{N-q}_{f}(t),

\displaystyle=e^{-F_{0}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-q}\sum_{k=0}^{2(j+q)}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{j}}\hat{f}^{2q}_{j,k}+\partial^{2q}r^{N-q}_{f}(t),

where we replace
b_{l,m,k}(F_{1,0},F_{0,0})
by
b_{l,m,k}
to lighten the notation and where

\displaystyle\hat{f}^{2q}_{j,k}=\sum_{0\leq k_{1}\leq 2q,0\leq k_{2}\leq 2j,|k_{1}|+|k_{2}|=k}(\sum_{m=k_{1}}^{2q}\sum_{l=m}^{2q}\binom{2q}{l}b_{l,m,k_{1}}\partial_{\xi}^{2q-l}\hat{f}_{j,k_{2}}).

(8.32)

Substituting into the previous expansion gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int e^{-i\frac{xy}{t}}(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{i|y|^{2}}{2t}\right)^{j}f(t,y))dy

\displaystyle=e^{-i\ln(t)\lambda_{1}|\hat{w}_{0,0}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)|^{2}-F_{0,0}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{2j}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{j}}u_{j,k}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+u_{err.2}^{N}(t,x)

(8.33)

with

\displaystyle u_{j,k}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\frac{(-i)^{m}}{2^{m}m!}\hat{f}^{2m}_{j-m,k}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)

and
||u_{err.2}^{N}(t)||_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=O(t^{-1/2-N-\delta})
for some
\delta>0
.
In particular, the terms
\hat{f}^{2q}_{j,k}
are defined for
k\leq 2(j+q)
, contrary to the terms
\hat{f}_{j,k}
, which are only defined for
k\leq 2j
. Since the terms
\hat{f}^{2q}_{j,k}
appear with an additional factor
\frac{1}{t^{q}}
in the expansion for
u
, no logarithmic powers
\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{j}}
with
k>2j
arise in this expansion. Consequently

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{e^{\frac{ix^{2}}{2t}-i\lambda_{1}|u_{0,0}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)|^{2}\ln(t))-i\varphi(\frac{x}{2t})}}{(it)^{1/2}}\sum_{p=0}^{N}\sum^{2p}_{k}\frac{\ln(t)^{k}}{t^{p}}u_{p,k}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)+u_{err.}(t,x),

(8.34)

for
u_{err.}(t,x)=u_{err.1}^{N}+u_{err.2}^{N}
.

In particular,

\displaystyle u_{0,0}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{f}_{0,0}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{w}_{0,0}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right),

so that the phase correction is exactly
e^{-i\ln(t)\lambda_{1}|u_{0,0}\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)|^{2}-i\varphi\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)}
(for
i\varphi=F_{0,0}
) as stated in Theorem
1.1
.
∎

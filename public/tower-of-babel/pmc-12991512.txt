Sociodemographic Profile, Reproductive Factors and Presentation Pattern in Breast Cancer Patients from North India: A Hospital Based Study
Authors: Ambuj Kumar Soni, Ajeet Shukla, Kanhaiya Baghel, Shafiqa Haris
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Breast cancer is leading cause of cancer in women globally and in India, with rising incidence seen specially in North India. Aim was to evaluate sociodemographic, reproductive factors, presentation pattern and surgical complications among breast cancer patients in North India.Methods:This prospective observational study included 101-biopsy-proven breast cancer patients undergoing axillary dissection at a tertiary center from January 2021 to December 2022. Demographic profile, reproductive history, tumor characteristics, and post-operative complications were recorded.Results:Mean age was 47.93 years with 97.03% females. Upper outer quadrant was most common site (56.67%) with 82% axillary lymph node positivity. ER/PR positive cases were 59%, HER2 positive 29%, TNBC 28%. Lymphedema occurred in 9.4%, seroma in 15.6%, and wound infection in 9%. Late presentation beyond 6 months was seen in 56.57%.Conclusions:Breast cancer in North India affects women at younger age with advanced stage presentation. Awareness, early diagnosis and improving access to care are needed to reduce disease burden.

INTRODUCTION

Breast cancer is now the most common cancer among women worldwide including India accounting for 27%–36% of all female cancers in urban regions.[1] Studies from North India show breast cancer is affecting women at younger age than Western countries with most patients presenting at advanced stages due to low awareness and lack of screening.[23] Recognized risk factors include early menarche, delayed childbirth, nulliparity, short breastfeeding, and family history.[4] Lifestyle changes, urbanization, obesity, and socio-economic inequalities further increase disease burden.[5] Though treatment options have advanced, survival rates in Indian women remain lower than developed nations.[6] Timely diagnosis, awareness and understanding demographic and clinical profile of patients is essential for better outcomes. Present study aims to evaluate socio-demographic, reproductive history, presentation patterns, and complications among breast cancer patients in North India.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This prospective observational study was done in Surgical Oncology Department, Sir Sunderlal Hospital, BHU from January 2021 to December 2022. Total-101 patients with biopsy-proven breast, chest wall or upper limb cancer undergoing axillary dissection were included. Patients with bilateral disease, recurrence, prior radiotherapy or upper limb weakness were excluded. Demographic profile, reproductive history, clinical presentation, tumor characteristics and post-operative complications like lymphedema, seroma, and infection were recorded. Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25. Ethical clearance was taken and written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

RESULTS

The mean age of patients was 47.93 years, majority were females (97.03%) and only 2.97% were males. All patients was married. Tobacco chewing habit was present in 9.9% cases. Mean age at menarche was 11.35 years and menopause occurred at 44.92 years. First childbirth mean age was 20.23 years with average of 3.31 childbirths per women. All patients breastfed their child, mean duration was 2 years. About 56.57% patients reported late presentation, after 6 months of symptoms. Lump was most common complaint (95%), pain present in 18% and nipple discharge in 5%. Lack of awareness was main reason for delay (62%) and 25% patients told accessibility issue as cause [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Breast cancer cases are increasing fast in India especially in North.[1] In present study mean age was 47.93 years which is similar to Gogia et al.[7] Where median age was 48 years and 10% cases were below 35 years showing breast cancer affecting Indian women younger compared to western countries.[5] Late diagnosis remains major issue here in present study 56.57% patients presented after 6 months also consistent with Gogia et al.[7] where 45% had stage III disease and 18% presented with metastasis. Kumar et al.[8] also showed delay due to poor awareness.

Upper outer quadrant was most common site in our study (56.67%), which is comparable to Sandhu et al.,[2] where 47.7% of tumors involved upper outer quadrant, confirming similar pattern in North Indian women. High axillary lymph node positivity (82%) reflects advanced disease at presentation which is consistent with Gogia et al.[7] reporting median tumor size of 5 cm, with 44.9% patients in stage III and 18% in stage IV at AIIMS, Delhi. Hormone profile in our study also aligns with Indian trends. ER/PR positivity was 59%, HER2 positive 29%, and TNBC 28% similar to Gogia et al. in 2018[7] who reported ER/PR positivity of 59%, HER2 positive 29%, and TNBC in 28% of cases. Vishwakarma et al. in 2019[9] further highlighted that reproductive risk factors like early menarche (<13 years) nulliparity and absence of breastfeeding significantly increase breast cancer risk among Indian women, which was consistent with the reproductive profile seen in our patients.

Complication rates like lymphedema (9.4%), seroma (15.6%), and wound infection (9%) in our study are nearly identical to Abass et al.[10] who reported lymphedema (9.4%), seroma (15.6%) and wound infection (9%) following axillary dissection in breast cancer patients. Paresthesia (21.9%) was also common as seen in their study. Fu[11] further highlighted that lymphedema may occur in up to 40% of patients showing post-operative morbidity still remains significant in Indian and global settings. Findings reflect need for awareness timely screening and improving access to care to reduce disease burden and complications.

CONCLUSION

Breast cancer in North India is affecting women at younger age with high rate of advanced stage presentation. Reproductive factors, low awareness, and delayed diagnosis are contributing to disease burden. Improving screening, awareness programs, and early access to treatment is urgently needed to reduce complications and mortality.

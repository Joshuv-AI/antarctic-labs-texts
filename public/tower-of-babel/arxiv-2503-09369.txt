Task allocation for multi-agent systems via unequal-dimensional optimal transport
Anqi Dong, Karl H. Johansson, Johan Karlsson

Abstract
We consider a probabilistic model for large-scale task allocation problems for multi-agent systems, aiming to determine an optimal deployment strategy that minimizes the overall transport cost. Specifically, we assign transportation agents to delivery tasks with given pick-up and drop-off locations, pairing the spatial distribution of transport resources with the joint distribution of task origins and destinations. This aligns with the optimal mass transport framework where the problem and is in the unequal-dimensional setting. The task allocation problem can be thus seen as a linear programming problem that minimizes a quadratic transport cost functional, optimizing the energy of all transport units. The problem is motivated by time-sensitive medical deliveries using drones, such as emergency equipment and blood transport. In this paper, we establish the existence, uniqueness, and smoothness of the optimal solution, and illustrate its properties through numerical simulations.

Optimal Transport Preliminaries

The standard optimal matching problem considers two distributions,
\mu

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}c(x,T(x))\mu(dx),

while aligning

Here,

The Kantorovich relaxation

specifies a probabilistic matching between
\mu
and

that minimizes the cost functional

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}c(x,y)\pi(dx,dy),

that abiding marginal constraints

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\pi(dx,dy)=\mu(dx)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\pi(dx,dy)=\nu(dy).

It is well-known that, under mild conditions, an optimal coupling measure

exists. Specifically, if the cost function
c(x,y)
is lower semi-continuous and the marginal measures
\mu
and
\nu
are probability measures on Polish spaces (i.e., complete, separable metric spaces), then there exists a measure

that minimizes the cost functional
[
22
]
.

Regarding the uniqueness of the coupling measure

,
19
]

\displaystyle c(x,y)+c(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-c(x,y^{\prime})-c(x^{\prime},y)\leq 0,

(1)

In addition, the Monge condition is satisfied for

This approach models task allocation in multi-agent systems by matching the spatial distribution of agents to the distribution of origin-destination task pairs while minimizing travel costs. c({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})
can be defined as

c({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})=\alpha({\mathbf{y}})+\sigma\big(I({\mathbf{x}}),{\mathbf{y}}\big),

where addition term
\alpha:{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{R}

{\mathbf{x}}

Proposition 4
(
Smooth plan
)

The cost
c({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})
is in index form – nestedness condition holds regardless of the choice of absolute continuous
\mu
and
\nu
. Thus, the optimal allocation plan is unique and also smooth.

Proof:

To demonstrate that the cost function
c(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
in (
7
) is in index form, we begin by expressing it as

\displaystyle c(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=2\big(|\mathbf{y}|^{2}-(\mathbf{x}^{1})^{\top}\mathbf{y}+|\mathbf{x}^{1}|^{2}-(\mathbf{x}^{1})^{\top}\mathbf{x}^{2}+|\mathbf{x}^{2}|^{2}-(\mathbf{x}^{2})^{\top}\mathbf{y}\big).

This factorization allows us to reformulate the original optimization problem

\min_{\pi}\iint_{{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}}c({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})\ d\pi({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})

(12a)

as an equivalent problem minimizing the negative correlations

\min_{\pi}\iint_{{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}}}-\left({\mathbf{x}}^{1}+{\mathbf{x}}^{2}\right)^{T}\!\!{\mathbf{y}}\ d\pi({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{y}})

(12b)

In fact,
\pi
is a minimizer for (
12b
) if and only if it minimizes (
12a
), see also
[
22
]
.
This term in the objective can be represented in index form (
11
) as

\hat{c}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=-({\mathbf{x}}^{1}+{\mathbf{x}}^{2})^{T}{\mathbf{y}}=\sigma\big(I(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}\big),

where the projection operator
I:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

I(\mathbf{x})=-(\mathbf{x}^{1}+\mathbf{x}^{2}),

and the bilinear function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\sigma(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{y})=-\mathbf{u}^{\top}\mathbf{y}.

The uniqueness and smoothness of the matching follow directly from
[
7
]
.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, motivated by medical delivery by drone, we propose a task allocation problem for macroscopic multi-agent system as unequal-dimensional optimal transport problem, modeling drone locations and origin-destination requests as probability distributions. With a cost functional that reflects allocation expenses, we established the existence, uniqueness, and smoothness of the optimal allocation plan. Numerical experiments validated the framework, demonstrating its effectiveness in cost-efficient task allocation. Future work will address unbalanced supply-demand scenarios and shared rides with intermediate stops. We also aim to develop distributed algorithms for large-scale problems and integrate real-time data for dynamic adaptation.

Isolation, characterization and antibacterial potential of bacteriophages SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007 targeting Salmonella enterica serovar Gallinarum
Authors: Sidra Tul Muntaha, Gul Nabi Khan, Ibrar Khan, Mishal Sikandar, Iqbal Ahmed Alvi, Tawaf Ali Shah, Abebe Bogale, Esmael M Alyami, Turki M Dawoud
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSalmonella Gallinarum causative agent of chicken typhoid, causes severe loss in poultry with multidrug-resistant (MDR) strains reducing antibiotic effectiveness. Bacteriophage therapy represents a promising alternative strategy for combating multidrug-resistant infections. This study aimed on isolating and characterizing bacteriophages against S. Gallinarum (S2) and evaluating their antimicrobial potential. The Bacteriophages were isolated from Ayub Medical Complex Hospital, Abbottabad, DHQ Hospital, Abbottabad and Butt Pull Water Canal in Mansehra. Their host specificity, temperature and pH stability, and antimicrobial activity were assessed. The isolated Bacteriophages SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007 formed clear lytic plaques, showed resistance to pH 2.0 for one hour, and optimal stability at pH 6–9 and 3–6, with temperature stability at 4 °C, 50 °C, and 37 °C, respectively. These bacteriophages demonstrated strong antibacterial activity against S. Gallinarum for up to 28 h without inducing resistant mutants. The isolated Bacteriophages SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007 specifically infected and lysed S. Gallinarum isolates without impacting other bacterial genera exhibiting strong, specific, and stable antibacterial activity against multidrug-resistant Salmonella Gallinarum highlighting their potential as effective targeted biocontrol agents.Graphical abstract Keywords: Chicken typhoid, S. Gallinarum, Multidrug-resistant (MDR) bacteria, Plaques, Antimicrobial activity

Introduction

The development of the poultry sector plays a crucial role in poverty alleviation and economic development in developing countries, contributing to improved livelihoods and food security (Oludairo 2022, Farhat 2023).Salmonella infection indeed pose significant challenges for birds, caretakers, and the broader community, impacting both economic and health aspects. Salmonellais a Gram-negative bacterium belonging to the family Enterobacteriaceae, capable of surviving under anaerobic conditions, with an incubation period of 4–6 days (Kang 2024, Jiang 2024).

The Salmonella enterica subspecies enterica serovar Gallinarum (S.Gallinarum), causative agent of chicken typhoid, results in important financial losses for poultry farms due to the disease’s elevated death and morbidity rates (Mahapatra and Paul 2024, Cheng et al. 2022).The infection primarily affects chickens and turkeys, but can also occur in other avian species, including game birds. Fowl typhoid has a global distribution, including the United States, Canada, Australia, and most European nations, where they were largely eradicated by the 1970 s through intensive control and surveillance programs. In contrast, these diseases remain unreported in many poultry forming regions, particularly in low income and developing countries, where intensified poultry production and limited surveillance hinder effective disease monitoring, transmission occurs via both horizontal and vertical routes (Yang et al. 2025, Gan et al. 2025). True transovarial transmission is primarily associated withS. enteritidis, whereas S.Gallinarum is predominantly transmitted horizontally through the fecal–oral route, with egg-associated transmission from infected breeder flocks occasionally reported. Environmental contamination of eggs and poultry houses via feces facilitates bacterial persistence and flock-level spread. Disease severity varies with bacterial strain and host factors, and young chicks are particularly susceptible due to immunological immaturity (Enshaie 2025).

S. Gallinarum has become multidrug-resistant, displaying resistance to practically all medications on the market as a result of antibiotic overuse and misuse in recent years. Antibiotic failure has necessitated the development of alternate solutions to the problem. Because many antibiotics used in animal production are also critical for human medicine, their non-therapeutic use in livestock can promote antimicrobial resistance, posing a significant public health concern. More than 130 S.Gallinarum strains were isolated from fowl typhoid–infected chickens and evaluated for multidrug-resistance. These isolates revealed up to 78.5% resistance to all currently approved antibiotics for fowl typhoid. With the increase of antibiotic resistance, use of bacteriophages against multidrug-resistant bacteria in the chicken sector may be more adaptable technique for dealing with multidrug-resistant challenges (Alsayeqh 2021, Manohar 2018).

Phage therapy has several advantages over conventional antimicrobial agents, including ease of isolation and purification from environmental sources, high specificity toward target bacterial strains, reduced adverse effects, and lower production costs. Despite its early promise, the widespread adoption of antibiotics such as penicillin led to a decline in the clinical application of phage therapy. However, the increasing prevalence of multidrug-resistant bacteria has renewed interest in phage therapy as a potential alternative or adjunct to antibiotic treatment (He 2025). Bacteriophages have been isolated from diverse environments, including sewage, soil, feces, freshwater and marine systems, and various foods such as fruits, vegetables, fish, and dairy products. Owing to their host specificity, non-toxicity (Yan et al. 2026), and cost-effectiveness, bacteriophages are considered safe for use, as they do not infect plant, animal, or human cells and minimally disrupt beneficial microbial communities (Sahu et al. 2023, Duc 2018, Zhang 2021).

Different phage types have been isolated in opposition to Salmonella. In one investigation, samples of skin and gizzard were used to recover 18 different lytic Bacteriophages. Of the eighteen bacteriophages that were recovered, five Bacteriophages (SEG5, SES8, STG2, STG5, STS9) produced obvious plaques and stocks with a high titer (> 109 PFU/mL). A phage against S.Gallinarum SAL-PG was discovered in a study conducted in Bangladesh (Alvi 2018).

The current study aimed to isolate and characterize bacteriophages against S. Gallinarum that cause major devastating disease in poultry and their efficacy in control of multidrug-resistant (MDR). S. Gallinarum contamination was tested. Unlike conventional antibiotic treatments, which are increasingly ineffective or less effective due to multidrug-resistance, this study evaluates the potential of Bacteriophages as alternative biocontrol agents.

Materials and methods

Isolation of Salmonella enterica serovar Gallinarum

The multidrug-resistant S. Gallinarum (S2) strain was obtained from Abbottabad University of Science and Technology and was originally isolated and identified in previous study (unpublished data), from chicken liver and intestinal samples using conventional bacteriological culture methods following ISO 6579-1 and got the following accession number for the NCBI submission; SUB14856953 S2 as PQ577804. The Bacterial isolates were streaked onto fresh XLD agar [Sigma-Aldrich (Merck)], MacConkey agar [Sigma-Aldrich (Merck)], and SS agar [Sigma-Aldrich (Merck)] plates and incubated overnight at 37 °C. Bacterial isolates were subcultured before the experiment, and 8-hour cultures were employed.

Collection and isolation of bacteriophages from sewage

Bacteriophages were isolated from sewage samples collected from Ayub Medical Complex Hospital Abbottabad, DHQ Hospital, Abbottabad and Butt Pull Canal Mansehra and transferred to the Microbiology Laboratory of Abbottabad University of Science and Technology, Havelian Pakistan, as described previously. The sewage samples were shaken for two minutes before being centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for ten minutes to remove silt, big particles, and bacteria. In a conical flask, 10 mL of sterile 5X nutritional broth (peptone (25 g/L), beef extract (15 g/L), and NaCl (25 g/L) and 40 mL of clear supernatant were mixed. For phage propagation the flask was infected with 200 𝜇L of S. Gallinarum strains S2 suspension containing approximately 1 × 10⁸ CFU/mL and incubated in a shaker incubator at 37 °C (120 rpm) overnight. After incubation, the culture was centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 10 min. The clear supernatant was collected, passed through a 0.22 𝜇𝑚 syringe filter into a new sterile Falcon tube and stored at 4 °C for later use.

Bacteriophage detection

A spot test was used to assess the presence of S. Gallinarum specific phage. A nutrient agar [Sigma-Aldrich (Merck)], plate was spread-plated with 100 µL of an overnight culture of S. Gallinarum. After applying 5 𝜇L of the cell-free phage filtrate, the plates were left to dry for approximately 10 min followed by incubation at 37 °C overnight and examined for a definite lytic zone. The existence of the clear zone (plaque) indicated the presence of specific bacteriophages.

Bacteriophage purification by double layer agar assay

The bacteriophage was purified and quantified from the lysate (filtrate that caused lysis) using the double layer agar overlay method (Alvi 2020). Before the experiment began, 100 mL of semisolid nutritional agar (containing 0.6% (w/v) agar) was autoclaved in a conical flask and placed in a water bath that was set to 48 °C. Initially lysate was diluted (1:10) in micro tubes with 900 𝜇L of nutrient broth. The selected dilution was then supplemented with 100 𝜇L of a fresh culture ofS.Gallinarum (1 × 10⁸ CFU/mL). The bacterial culture and phage lysate were incubated for five minutes to allow the Bacteriophages to attach to the bacterial cells. The selected dilutions were then put onto nutrient agar plates, which were then covered with 3 mL of semisolid agar and incubated overnight at 37 °C. The mixture and semisolid agar were spread on the plate with a swirling motion. The plaque- forming units (PFU) were counted from selected plate (Vinogradov 2002).

For purification of Bacteriophages, plates with unique plaques were used and the phage was removed from a plaque using a sterile micropipette tip. The tip was placed in a test tube containing 10 mL of nutrient broth and 1 mL of a fresh S. Gallinarum strain for phage propagation. The mixture was then incubated at 37 °C for 24 h, after which plaque formation was observed and subsequently purified (Fader et al. 1979). The purification process was repeated up to ten times. The titer of the lysate solution was calculated by the following formula:

Titer (PFU/mL) = plaque (PFU) dilution number x phage volume (mL) injected to plate.

Bacteriophages characterization

Long term storage stability analysis

For long-term storage stability analysis, aliquots of the purified bacteriophage lysate were stored in LB broth at 4 °C, 25 °C, and 37 °C for one month. Phage titers were determined prior to storage and after one month of storage using the double-layer agar plaque assay, as described by (Alvi 2018).

Determination of host range of bacteriophages

The host range was analyzed using bacterial cultures from S.Gallinarum various strains and other species, following an already described technique (Alvi et al. 2021). Briefly, the several strains ofS. Gallinarum and other bacterial species were used. Fresh overnight cultures of each bacterial strain were prepared in nutrient broth and adjusted to approximately 1 × 10⁸ CFU/mL. A volume of 100 µL of each bacterial culture was evenly spread on separate nutrient agar plates to form a uniform bacterial lawn. The plates were allowed to dry at room temperature for 10–15 min. Purified bacteriophage lysates of known titers were then used for host range analysis. A small volume (5 µL) of each phage lysate was carefully spotted onto the surface of the bacterial lawn. Each plate was allowed to absorb the spotted phage suspension and then incubated at 37 °C for 18–24 h. After incubation, the plates were examined for the presence of clear lytic zones at the spot locations. The formation of a clear zone indicated susceptibility of the bacterial strain to the tested bacteriophage, whereas the absence of lysis was recorded as resistance. All tests were performed in triplicate to ensure reproducibility of the results.

Determination of thermal stability of bacteriophages

The determination of bacteriophage thermal stability is critical because it provides insight into phage storage and movement. Aliquots of known titers bacteriophages were placed at 4, 25, 37, 50, and 60 °C for one hour in order to find the thermal stability of the isolated bacteriophages, for preliminary characterization of bacteriophages. After incubation, the titer of bacteriophages was measured using the double layer overlay method (Alvi et al. 2021). Before storage, the stability or decrease in titer was compared to the titer.

Determination of pH stability of bacteriophages

For pH stability of isolated bacteriophages, sodium acetate buffers (pH 2 to 6), phosphate-buffered saline (pH 7 to 9) were prepared. Bacteriophages of known titers and buffer were incubated in room temperature at pH 2–9 for one hour by using pH paper and pH meter. After incubation, the titer of bacteriophages was measured using the double layer agar overlay method (Alvi 2020). The stability or decrease in titer was compared to the titer before storage.

Bacterial growth reduction

In-vitro phage therapy is another term for phage-mediated bacterial growth suppression. In this experiment, bacteriophages’ ability to inhibit bacterial growth is assessed. A previously defined technique was used to test the reduction in bacterial growth of the bacteriophages. Two flasks, each containing 50 mL of broth, were inoculated with an 8-hour-old culture of S.Gallinarum strain S2 (3 × 10⁸ CFU/mL). One flask served as the control, while the other was supplemented with a bacteriophage of known titer. The growth reduction was determined by measuring optical density at 600 nm (OD600). The reduced growth was compared to control and put on a graph. The experiment was completed separately, in triplicate (Alvi et al. 2021).

Statistical analysis

Data were expressed as the mean of replicates ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analysis was performed using one-way ANOVA in GraphPad Prism version 8.0. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

S. Gallinarum (S2) growth on culture media

The S. Gallinarum strains were grown on various culture media (MacConkey agar, SS agar and XLD agar). The isolates showed smooth, spherical colonies on MacConkey’s agar with lactose fermentation. On XLD medium S. Gallinarum isolates showed black center colonies, while on SS agar S. Gallinarum, isolates showed black center colonies and white colonies were produced on nutrient agar.

Spot test for the detection of S. Gallinarum (S2) specific bacteriophages

Among 11 sewage samples, three were found positive for bacteriophages against S. Gallinarum strain S2 using the spot test. Two bacteriophages SGP009 and SGP004 were isolated from Ayub Medical Complex Hospital, Abbottabad, and SGP007 was isolated from Butt Pull Water Canal Mansehra. The results by Spot test showed that the samples of litter contained bacteriophages (Fig. 1).

Purification of isolated bacteriophages

The host specific isolated Bacteriophages were further screened and purified by double layer assay using S. Gallinarum (S2) as a host. The Bacteriophages infecting the bacterial host formed circular translucent plaques of different sizes and shapes (Fig. 2). Plaques made of primarily lytic bacteriophages are transparent and clear with various diameter SGP009 (2–5 mm), SGP004 (1–9 mm), SGP007 (2–3 mm) respectively.

Calculation of purified bacteriophage titer

Although the titer of the initial crude lysate was not determined, previous studies indicate that crude bacteriophage lysates obtained from environmental enrichment typically reach 10⁷–10⁹ PFU/mL (He 2025). Using double layer agar assay and propagation methods, purification and bacteriophage titer calculation were performed as mentioned above. The Bacteriophage SGP009 showed the highest titer of 4.6 × 1010 PFU/mL while SGP004 showed the lowest titer, of 5.2 × 106 PFU/mL, and phage SGP007 had titer, 3.8 × 109 PFU/mL.

The titers of Bacteriophages SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007 remained stable after one month of storage at 4 °C, with no decrease observed. After the same storage period, reductions of 1 and 2 log units were recorded at 25 °C and 37 °C, respectively (Table 1). For these Bacteriophages, no reduction in titer was observed at 4 °C, while decreases of 2 and 3 logs were noted at 25 °C and 37 °C, respectively.

Thermal stability analysis of isolated bacteriophages

As per available data the temperature is the factor having significant effect on stability of bacteriophage (Alvi 2020). It affects the Bacteriophages by inhibition of different stages of life cycle (Alvi et al. 2021). The isolated phage SGP009, SGP004, SGP007 in the current study were stable up to 4 °C and 50 °C without any change in the titer while a one-fold, two-fold decrease was noted at 37 °C and 25 °C and a further seven-fold to nine-fold decrease was seen at 60 °C (Fig. 3).

pH stability of bacteriophages

In current study the phage SGP009 were not stable at pH range of 2 after incubation of 1 h. While, the pH range of 6–9 was optimum for SGP009 phage. However, a reduction of up to 3 log was observed at pH levels 3 and 4 (Fig. 4A).

Regarding the pH stability of the SGP004 phage, pH levels 3, 4, 5, 6, and 9 were found to be optimal. A 1 log decrease was observed at pH 6 and 7, while a 3-log decrease occurred at pH 3 and 4. At pH 2, however, no viability was recorded (Fig. 4B).

The SGP007 phage demonstrated viability even at pH 3, suggesting it could tolerate gastric pH levels of 1.5–2.5, making it valuable for treating infections in animals (Fig. 4C). This study indicates that the SGP007 phage can be administered orally, while the SGP004 phage may require encapsulation for oral administration. Both SGP004 and SGP007Bacteriophages, however, can be safely administered topically, intraperitoneally, intravenously, and intranasally.

Discussion

Salmonellosis in poultry, caused by microorganisms, leads to significant economic losses for poultry production in Pakistan and globally. S. Gallinarum has two distant biovars Pullorum and Gallinarum that cause Pullorum disease and fowl typhoid, respectively. Fowl typhoid, caused by S. Gallinarum, can lead to high mortality rates, especially in adult birds, and is characterized by symptoms like lethargy, loss of appetite, ruffled feathers, yellowish diarrhea, and enlarged organs like the spleen (splenomegaly) and liver (hepatomegaly). Misuse of antibiotics in poultry industry has resulted in emergence of multidrug-resistant strains of S. Gallinarum all over Pakistan. Thus, Salmonellahas become multidrug-resistant, displaying resistance to practically all medications on the market as a result of antibiotic overuse and misuse in recent years. Antibiotic failure has necessitated the development of alternate solutions to the problem (Bisola 2024, Guo et al. 2025). Therefore, the novel strategies are needed to be explored to eradicate salmonellosis caused by antibiotic resistant strains (Tang et al. 2025). The technique of employing Bacteriophages to treat bacterial illnesses is known as “bacteriophage” treatment. The use of bacteriophages has spread to other fields outside the medical world and application in controlling bacterial population and infections.

In the current study, the three bacteriophages were isolated from sewage waste were found positive against S. Gallinarum strain S2. Sewage water is a well-recognized source of bacteriophages for various organisms, including Salmonella enterica. Although S. Gallinarum mainly infects poultry, we were able to isolate its specific bacteriophages from a human hospital environment, as shown in Figs. 1 and 2. This may seem unusual, but hospital sewage can contain traces of bacteria and Bacteriophages coming from food, patients, or other waste (Zhong et al. 2024). These Bacteriophages can survive even if their host bacteria are not present in large numbers. Our findings suggest that human-related environments, such as hospital wastewater, can serve as a valuable source of bacteriophages against poultry pathogens. This highlights the potential of exploring diverse environments for phage isolation, which can help in research and in controlling resistant S. Gallinarum strains. Consistent with earlier findings, The Bacteriophages isolated from wastewater tend to have a broader host range compared to those obtained directly from feces or fecal storage on farms (Sevilla-Navarro 2024). Similarly, in the previous study phage SAL-PG was isolated from sewage effluent around poultry installations and was found to kill theS. Gallinarum, S. Pullorum and S.Typhimurium (Rahaman 2014). The isolated Bacteriophages (SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007) produced circular, transparent plaques on double agar plates againstS. Gallinarum. Clear plaques were selected for further analysis, as they indicate complete lysis of the host bacteria. Similar approaches have been reported in previous studies, where thirteen SalmonellaBacteriophages were isolated by the double-layer agar method, and those with larger plaques and higher titers were prioritized for experimentation (Wang 2024). In the present study, Bacteriophages SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007 remained stable between 4 °C and 50 °C without significant loss of titer, whereas a one to two-fold reduction was observed at 25 °C and 37 °C, and a marked seven- to nine-fold decrease occurred at 60 °C. The stability of these Bacteriophages at 37–50 °C, which corresponds to the normal body temperature of animals, suggests their potential applicability for therapeutic use in animal infections. Similar findings demonstrated that a filtered phage preparation (10¹⁴ PFU/mL) retained stability at various temperatures when incubated in a water bath for 30 min, with survivability assessed by the double agar overlay method (Malik 2021). Although the PFU titers and growth reduction assays demonstrate the activity of the isolated Bacteriophages, the burst size and one-step growth curve were not determined in this study and will be addressed in future experiments to better understand the replication dynamics of these Bacteriophages. These results collectively highlight the temperature tolerance of bacteriophages, reinforcing their suitability for in vivo applications. The Bacteriophages SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007 exhibited optimal stability at neutral to slightly alkaline pH, with SGP007 showing the greatest tolerance under acidic conditions (pH 1.5–2.5), suggesting its potential suitability for oral administration, while SGP004 may require encapsulation to enhance acid resistance (Malik et al. 2017, Carey-Smith et al. 2006, Muntaha 2026). Both SGP004 and SGP007 remain suitable for alternative delivery routes such as topical, intraperitoneal, intravenous, and intranasal applications. These findings are consistent with earlier reports highlighting the importance of pH stability in phage therapy applications, where Bacteriophages stable across broader pH ranges were considered promising candidates for controllingS.Gallinarum induced infections. A comparable trend was observed in the study of (Carey-Smith et al. 2006), whereXanthomonas phage NR08 maintained activity across 4–40 °C and exhibited about 80% viability between pH 5–9, with optimal stability at pH 6–8. These findings further support that temperature and pH tolerance are critical determinants of phage applicability, consistent with our observations for SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007. In the present study, the isolated Bacteriophages demonstrated notable bactericidal activity over a 28-hour observation period, highlighting their novelty and diverse antibacterial reduction potential. This sustained inhibition may indicate that the Bacteriophages exert strong lytic activity against S.Gallinarum, although the specific receptor interactions remain to be elucidated. Bacterial reduction assays showed that SGP007 suppressed bacterial growth for up to 16 h, after which slight regrowth occurred, suggesting the potential emergence of transient phage-tolerant or resistant variants. While our in vitro killing assays demonstrate the antibacterial activity of the tested Bacteriophages, direct visualization of phage–bacteria interactions using SEM or CLSM was not performed in this study. Future work incorporating such imaging techniques would provide valuable mechanistic insight into phage-induced bacterial lysis and biofilm disruption (Zheng et al. 2025). These findings align with previous reports that emphasize the importance of receptor diversity in delaying or preventing resistance during phage therapy. While in vivo study, using broiler chicks corroborated these results, with survival increasing from 40% in untreated birds to > 70% with antibiotics, 80% with phage therapy, and 90% with combined treatment, alongside marked reductions in bacterial loads, particularly in phage-treated and combination groups (unpublished data). Similarly, the Bacteriophages could suppressS.Gallinarum growth for up to 16 h, after which bacterial regrowth was observed, supporting the observation with SGP007. Based on the phage titer, pH and thermal stability, bacterial growth reduction ability, long term storage stability, it is suggested that the isolated bacteriophages could be a potential phage therapy candidates after necessary animal model and clinical trials in future. Furthermore, Establishing the taxonomic classification and ensuring the uniqueness of these isolates is a critical next step. While the complete genomic profile of Salmonella phage SGP007 has been previously described (GenBank: PV654122) (Muntaha 2026), comprehensive whole-genome sequencing and bioinformatic analyses for the remaining isolates, including SGP009 (GenBank: PV657135.1), are currently in progress and will be the focus of a forthcoming publication (unpublished data).

Conclusions

Bacterial resistance in fowl typhoid caused by S. Gallinarum is rising, limiting antibiotic efficacy and underscoring the need for alternative treatments like bacteriophage therapy. In this study, three bacteriophages, SGP009, SGP004, and SGP007, were isolated from sewage samples and characterized. These Bacteriophages demonstrated thermal and pH stability, supporting their potential to withstand diverse environments, while their viability across variable pH conditions highlights their therapeutic applicability. Their narrow host range further indicates specificity toward S. Gallinarum without affecting the normal microbiota and their strong antimicrobial activity against the S2 strain further confirms their effectiveness as targeted biocontrol agents. Although the PFU titers and growth reduction assays demonstrate the activity of the isolated Bacteriophages, the burst size and one-step growth curve were not determined in this study and will be addressed in future experiments to better understand the replication dynamics of these Bacteriophages. Thus, the vivo trial is further recommended in current study to check effectiveness of these Bacteriophages to be used in phage therapy. Future research should focus on genomic sequencing and annotations of the isolated bacteriophages to understand their therapeutic potential and biosafety.

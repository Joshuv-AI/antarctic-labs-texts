Chordal signed graphs and signed bigraphs
Jing Huang and Ying Ying Ye

Abstract
Chordal graphs and chordal bigraphs enjoy beautiful characterizations, in terms of forbidden subgraphs, vertex/edge orderings, vertex/edge separating sets, and tree-like representations. In this paper, we introduce chordal signed graphs and chordal signed bigraphs. Interestingly, chordal signed graphs are equivalent to strict chordal digraphs studied by Hell and Hernández-Cruz. A forbidden subdigraph characterization of strict chordal digraphs can be translated to a forbidden subgraph characterization of chordal signed graphs. We give a forbidden subgraph characterization of chordal signed bigraphs. The forbidden subgraphs for chordal signed bigraphs are analogous to those for chordal signed graphs but the proofs are much more complicated and intriguing.

For simplicity we shall use

for bipartite graphs. Thus,

Let
\widehat{G}
be a signed bigraph and

\widehat{G}

Denote

A

It follows from definition that a signed simplicial edge in

Thus if

Not all signed chordal graphs are chordal signed graphs. For example, the negative

C_{4}
is a signed chordal graph but not a chordal signed graph. On the other hand,
any positive chordal bigraph is a chordal signed bigraph.
It also follows from definition that an edge in
\widehat{G}

The main result of this paper is a forbidden subgraph characterization of chordal
signed bigraphs (see Theorem
5.1
). A graph is

if and only if it contains a non-trivial induced subgraph which has no signed

We will use edge-coloured graphs to represent signed graphs where positive edges are
coloured

, negative edges are coloured

, and edges whose signs
are not specified are coloured

Suppose that

is an edge-coloured graph whose edges are coloured with blue, red,
and black colours. If

has no black edge then it represents a unique signed
graph. If

has black edges then by replacing each black edge of

or a red edge we obtain a signed graph and thus

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we focus on complete bigraphs
and characterize chordal signed complete bigraphs by forbidden subgraphs
(see Theorem
2.2
).
In Section
3
, we extend the characterization of chordal signed complete bigraphs

).
In Section

find their forbidden subgraphs. Finally, in Section
5

theorem (that is, Theorem

).

Non-separable bigraphs

A bigraph is
separable
if it contains an induced
2K_{2}

signed bigraph). In this section we extend Theorem

A set

G-S
contains at

The minimality of

H

.

\widehat{G}

3.2

implies that
\widehat{G}

\widehat{G_{1}}
(respectively,
\widehat{G_{2}}

Corollary 5.2
.

A signed bigraph
\widehat{G}
is not chordal if and only if it contains a
non-trivial induced subgraph which has no signed simplicial edge.

Proof:
Suppose that
\widehat{G}
is not chordal. Then by Theorem
5.1

\widehat{G}
contain a graph in
\cal F
as an induced subgraph which is
non-trivial and has no signed simplcial edge. On the other hand, suppose that

\widehat{G}
is chordal. If
\widehat{H}
is a non-trivial induced subgraph of

\widehat{G}
, then the first edge in a signed simplicial edge-without-vertex
ordering of
\widehat{G}
that is contained in
\widehat{H}
is a signed simplicial
edge in
\widehat{H}
.
∎

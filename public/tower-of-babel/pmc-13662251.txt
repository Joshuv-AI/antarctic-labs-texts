Short- and Medium-Term Outcomes of Hip Arthroscopy in Patients under 18 Years
Authors: Roberto Seijas, Luis Pérez-Carro, Jorge Guadilla, Nicolás Fiz, Marc Tey, Jesús Mas, Javier Sanz, Fabián Urraza, Juan Ribera, David Serrano, Oliver Marín
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFemoroacetabular impingement (FAI) is a common cause of hip pain in adolescents and may lead to osteoarthritis. While conservative treatments are the first-line option, arthroscopy offers a minimally invasive approach to repairing labral tears and correcting bone deformities. This study analyzes the outcomes of patients under 18 years old who underwent hip arthroscopy for FAI in a multicenter retrospective cohort with a minimum 5-year follow-up.MethodsThis was a retrospective multicenter study. Patients under 18 with a confirmed FAI diagnosis and a minimum follow-up of 5 years were included. Clinical and radiological findings, demographics, and functional outcomes (Tegner, modified Harris Hip Score [mHHS], Hip Outcome Score [HOS], and International Hip Outcome Tool-33 [iHOT-33]) were analyzed pre- and postoperatively. The percentage of patients who achieved improvements exceeding the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) and substantial clinical benefit (SCB) thresholds was also calculated.ResultsA total of 90 hips were included (mean age, 16.1 ± 1.5 years). At 5 years, all functional scores improved significantly: mHHS (68.1 ± 16.3 to 96.6 ± 8.7, p < 0.001), iHOT-33 (51.7 ± 20.3 to 85.6 ± 10.8), HOS activities of daily living (71.2 ± 16.6 to 96.1 ± 4.9), and HOS sports subscale (50.3 ± 21.1 to 92.7 ± 8.5). Cam-type deformities were the most prevalent (68.8%), followed by mixed morphologies (22.2%). Labral lesions were repaired in 96.3% of cases, with a mean of 2.9 anchors per repair. The alpha angle significantly decreased postoperatively (62.3° ± 11.8° to 45.6° ± 8.4°, p < 0.001). At 5 years, 85%–94% of patients surpassed the MCID thresholds and 33%–77% reached the SCB thresholds depending on the outcome measure.ConclusionsHip arthroscopy was associated with significant functional improvement and symptom relief in adolescents with FAI at a minimum 5-year follow-up. Further studies with longer follow-up are necessary to determine its long-term role in joint preservation. Notably, systematic radiological assessment of osteoarthritis progression (e.g., joint space width, Tönnis grade) was not available across all participating centers and could therefore not be analyzed, which constitutes an important limitation of this study.

Femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) is a common cause of hip pain in adolescents, with a rising incidence in recent decades,123) particularly during early adolescence.4) Additionally, FAI has been identified as a contributing factor to the development of hip osteoarthritis.123) While conservative treatments, including activity modification, analgesia, physiotherapy, and corticosteroid or biological injections, can be effective as an initial therapeutic approach,156) hip arthroscopy has emerged as a minimally invasive surgical option capable of addressing labral injuries and correcting acetabular and femoral deformities.1)

Hip arthroscopy in pediatric patients was first described by Gross in 1977, although Burman had previously introduced the concept in 1931. Over time, it has proven effective in pain management, functional restoration, and the recovery of normal hip morphology, not only in FAI but also in other hip pathologies such as sequelae of Perthes disease, slipped capital femoral epiphysis, dysplasia, and septic arthritis.127)

This study aims to describe the characteristics and outcomes of a multicenter cohort of patients who underwent hip arthroscopy for FAI, assessing their medium-term evolution. Our hypothesis is that hip arthroscopy in patients under 18 years of age leads to significant improvements in functional outcomes over a 5-year follow-up period.

METHODS

Ethical approval for data collection and analysis was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Hospital Quironsalud Barcelona (coordinating center) and the local ethics committees of all participating centers (IRB No. HIPARTHRO 2017). All patients signed an informed consent before surgery accepting the use of all data during the follow-up period.

This study was a retrospective multicenter study using the accumulated institutional databases. The study protocol, inclusion criteria, outcome measures, and statistical analyses were all defined after the data had already been accumulated. All patients under 18 years old diagnosed with FAI, based on clinical history, physical examination, and radiographic evaluation, were included. Only those with a minimum of 5 years of follow-up and complete clinical and radiological records were analyzed. The indication for surgery was based primarily on persistent clinical symptoms rather than isolated radiographic findings. All patients presented with groin pain associated with functional limitations and clinical findings compatible with FAI. Conservative treatment was attempted for a minimum of 3 months and included physiotherapy focused on mobility and flexibility, activity modification, and symptomatic medication when required. In selected cases, intra-articular injections were used as a diagnostic and therapeutic adjunct. Surgical treatment was considered when symptoms persisted despite conservative management and continued to interfere with daily or sports activities.

All patients underwent preoperative magnetic resonance imaging and radiographic evaluation, including anteroposterior pelvic and Dunn 45° views. Radiological findings were used to characterize the morphology and support the diagnosis; however, surgical indication was ultimately based on the overall clinical assessment. Participating surgeons belonged to the same national hip preservation working group and followed similar diagnostic and treatment principles. Although a formal written protocol was not enforced across centers, the shared working group framework ensured comparable standards for surgical indication. Variability in conservative treatment duration and radiological thresholds between centers is acknowledged as an inherent limitation of the multicenter retrospective design.

All participating surgeons performed more than 20 hip arthroscopies per year at their respective institutions. Anthropometric data (age, weight, height, and body mass index [BMI]) were collected, along with information related to preoperative clinical tests (flexion, abduction, and external rotation [FABER]; flexion, adduction, and internal rotation [FADDIR]; and decompression test), lesion characteristics (alpha angle, labral tear, cartilage lesion, and cam or pincer morphology), and functional outcomes. Clinical assessment included the Tegner Activity Scale, the modified Harris Hip Score (mHHS), the Hip Outcome Score (HOS), and the International Hip Outcome Tool-33 (iHOT-33), recorded preoperatively and at the 5-year follow-up. Additionally, the proportion of patients achieving improvements beyond the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) and substantial clinical benefit (SCB) thresholds was calculated.8910) The MCID represents the smallest improvement in a patient-reported outcome that a patient perceives as clinically meaningful, while the SCB reflects a larger improvement associated with a meaningful change in health-related quality of life. Threshold values applied in this study, derived from Nwachukwu et al.,8910) were as follows: mHHS ≥ 8 points (MCID) and ≥ 20 points (SCB); HOS activities of daily living (ADL) ≥ 9 points (MCID) and ≥ 20 points (SCB); HOS sports subscale (SS) ≥ 13 points (MCID) and ≥ 20 points (SCB); iHOT-33 ≥ 6.1 points (MCID) and ≥ 20 points (SCB).

All patients underwent hip arthroscopy under spinal and/or general anesthesia, depending on each hospital’s anesthesiology protocol. Traction was applied, and a direct intra-articular approach was used in all cases. Bony deformities were corrected through femoral and/or acetabular osteoplasty. Labral repair was performed both in cases of labral tears and when partial detachment was required to restore anatomical insertion to the acetabular rim. Data on the number of anchors and presence of chondral lesions were recorded, although detailed grading or location of cartilage injuries was not consistently documented.

Intraoperative fluoroscopy and postoperative radiographic evaluation were performed in all cases. Alpha angle measurements were obtained using a Dunn 45° lateral view. An alpha angle greater than 55° was considered indicative of cam morphology. Pincer morphology was defined by the presence of radiographic overcoverage findings, including crossover sign, coxa profunda, or increased lateral center-edge angle (LCEA). Patients with hip dysplasia were not included in this series. Nevertheless, radiographic measurements were not used as isolated criteria for surgical indication, which remained primarily clinically driven.

Regarding capsular management, closure was performed using sutures in all cases. Interportal capsulotomies were the most common approach; however, some centers used T-type capsulotomies, which were also closed according to each surgeon’s standard protocol. Rehabilitation protocols included partial weight-bearing for the first 4 weeks and early mobilization from the first postoperative day, with hip flexion limited to 90° during this period.

Statistical Analysis

The distribution of the variables was assessed. Preoperative values were compared with those at the 5-year follow-up. Paired t-tests were used for normally distributed variables, and the Wilcoxon test was applied otherwise. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS software version 23.0 (IBM Corp.).

RESULTS

A total of 90 hips (84 patients) from 8 centers were included in the analysis, all with a minimum follow-up of 5 years. The initial sample consisted of 102 hips (96 patients); however, 12 hips (12 patients) were excluded due to loss to follow-up or incomplete data for the required study variables. The mean age at the time of surgery was 16.1 ± 1.5 years, and the mean BMI was 20.8 ± 3.2. Range of motion was not systematically recorded and is therefore not included in this analysis.

The percentages represent the patients with symptoms during the examination. Significant differences were observed between preoperative and 5-year follow-up values of the alpha angle, which decreased from 62.3° ± 11.8° to 45.6° ± 8.4° (p < 0.001). Alpha angle was measured using a Dunn 45° lateral view in all centers.

Regarding lesion characteristics, 64 patients (71.1%) had cartilage lesions, which did not require specific treatment beyond chondral lesion stabilization using a vaporizer or synoviotome.

Labral lesions were identified in 80 hips (88.8%), of whom 77 (96.3%) were treated with labral suturing, using an average of 2.9 anchors (range, 1–6). The remaining cases underwent partial labrectomy. Among the 8 patients with isolated pincer morphology, labral repair was performed in 6 cases, while partial labrectomy was sufficient in 2 cases. No labral reconstructions were required. Statistically significant improvements were observed in the mHHS, HOS, and iHOT-33 scales, including significant improvements in the SS of the HOS.

Regarding the Tegner Activity Scale (Table 3), preinjury values were compared to those at the 5-year follow-up, with no significant differences observed. No complications were reported in any case within the series. There were no instances of infection or vascular complications. However, 10 cases of pudendal neuropathy with secondary sensory impairment due to traction were observed. None of these cases were associated with skin lesions, and all patients fully recovered sensation within 4 weeks. No reoperations were required during the 5-year follow-up.

DISCUSSION

Hip arthroscopy in patients under 18 years of age significantly improves functional parameters at the 5-year follow-up. The exact cause of FAI remains unknown (idiopathic), but repetitive movements such as hip flexion, internal rotation, and adduction are believed to contribute to its development.11112) Although FAI in adolescents shares many similarities with adults, key differences include smaller joint structures and the presence of growth plates, which pose additional surgical challenges.12) While surgeons generally require 30–60 hip arthroscopies to gain proficiency, there are limited data on the number of cases necessary to develop expertise in pediatric patients.713)

Pain is often an early indicator of labral and cartilage damage, which, if untreated, can progress to hip osteoarthritis.14) Adolescents engaged in high-impact sports have an increased risk of developing FAI. Studies suggest that those participating in sports for more than 12.5 hours per week are twice as likely to develop cam-type deformities.4) Male soccer players exhibit a significantly higher risk of FAI than the general population.15) However, most athletes successfully return to pre-injury sports levels following surgery.161718)

All patients in this study presented with inguinal pain and at least 1 positive diagnostic test (FADDIR, FABER, or foveal decompression). All patients had cam, pincer, or mixed-type FAI (100%), while labral tears were identified in 88.8% of cases. In the remaining 11.2%, although no labral tear was observed, mechanical impingement was present. Labral repair was performed following moderate acetabular resection. Cartilage lesions were not full-thickness in any case, and no mesh or cartilage substitutes were used. When unstable chondral fragments were present, resection was performed, ensuring that the subchondral bone remained intact. These findings suggest favorable biological conditions for cartilage preservation in the adolescent population. Yen et al.19) reported better outcomes in patients with cam-type deformities compared to pincer-type FAI. Additionally, a higher prevalence of cam deformities was observed in males, while worse outcomes were reported in females, who tend to have a lower incidence of cam morphology.

Key symptoms and clinical indicators of FAI include reduced range of motion, particularly restricted flexion and internal rotation, as well as a positive impingement test. Hip arthroscopy has demonstrated its efficacy in short-, medium-, and long-term studies, with over 90% of patients achieving excellent functional outcomes.202122) Long-term studies indicate that bone correction remains stable for over 5 years, without recurrence of impingement and with mHHS improvements exceeding 20 points.4232425)

In this series, alpha angles in cam-type FAI decreased from 62° to 45°, with no subsequent increases over time. Previous studies have shown pain relief and functional improvements in 85–88% of patients, as measured by mHHS and Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index scores.7) In our study, significant improvements were observed in mHHS, HOS ADL, and iHOT-33 scores, along with notable enhancements in the HOS SS at 5 years. This finding is particularly relevant as it suggests that patients can maintain their pre-injury sports levels in the medium term, as indicated by stable Tegner scores. Furthermore, the maintenance of activity levels suggests that the functional benefits achieved after surgery can persist throughout medium-term follow-up.

Long-term studies with 10-year follow-ups have reported similar findings, with HOS ADL improving from 62 to 92 points and HOS SS from 40 to 86, aligning with the present study’s outcomes.21) In our cohort, the Tegner Activity Scale remained stable, with preoperative scores improving from 6.7 to 6.9 at 5 years, confirming that patients can return to their pre-injury level of sports activity without evidence of degenerative changes that would hinder continued participation.

In Yen et al.’s study,19) patients with higher sports activity levels exhibited better postoperative outcomes and a lower revision rate.

When analyzing the percentage of patients achieving scores above MCID and SCB thresholds (Table 4), we observed that 85%–94% of patients reached these functional benchmarks at 5 years. The higher SCB thresholds resulted in achievement rates ranging from 33% to 77%, which also remained stable over time. It is important to consider that many patients had high baseline functional scores, which could limit the magnitude of post-surgical improvements. This is one of the main reasons why we evaluated multiple functional scales in this study.

In this series, 71% of patients had cartilage lesions. Although the degree of cartilage damage was not systematically recorded, following prognostic criteria and decision-making algorithms, the young age and low BMI (20.8 kg/m2) of the cohort suggest an overall favorable prognosis according to Hip Arthroscopy Risk criteria.2627)

None of the cases required specific procedures such as scaffold implantation, microfractures, or cartilage transplantation. This finding likely reflects the favorable biological environment of the adolescent hip, where chondral lesions tend to be less advanced than in adult populations, and the careful patient selection applied in this series, in which patients with significant joint space narrowing or advanced osteoarthritis were excluded from surgery.

This study did not include specific radiographic measurements of acetabular morphology such as LCEA, Tonnis angle, or wall indices, which limits precise classification of potential dysplastic features. However, the low rate of complications and high functional outcomes suggest that the cases were predominantly mechanical impingements rather than dysplastic hips.

With 90 cases, this study represents the third-largest series available in the literature. The Philippon group previously described a series of 111 cases,28) while the ANCHOR group conducted a multicenter study with 114 cases.19) Our sample size falls within the range of the largest available studies and exceeds that of many highly specialized hip arthroscopy groups.2930)

As a retrospective study, this research carries the inherent limitations of this design. Because the analysis relied on data that had already been recorded, some relevant details were incompletely documented or unavailable, and the loss of cases due to database inconsistencies could not be excluded. In addition, the multicenter nature of the cohort introduced variability between participating centers in the way data were recorded and reported, which may have affected the completeness and uniformity of the information analyzed.

A major limitation of this study is the lack of detailed records on cartilage lesion types and locations, which hinders a precise assessment of one of the key prognostic factors. Although over 70% of patients exhibited cartilage lesions, and most were likely mild to moderate, the absence of precise grading in our records limits our ability to draw definitive conclusions regarding their severity and impact. Another limitation is the absence of systematic radiographic assessment of osteoarthritis progression. Variables such as joint space width, Tonnis grade evolution, and other radiographic indicators of degeneration were not consistently available across all centers and therefore could not be analyzed. Consequently, although functional outcomes remained favorable at 5 years, no conclusions can be drawn regarding the long-term effect of hip arthroscopy on osteoarthritis progression.

A further limitation is that hip range of motion was not systematically recorded across centers. As a result, objective improvement in postoperative hip mobility could not be evaluated. Future studies should incorporate a standardized assessment of hip range of motion to better characterize functional recovery after arthroscopy.

Our findings demonstrate significant functional improvement and maintenance of sports activity levels at a minimum 5-year follow-up in adolescents undergoing hip arthroscopy for FAI. While these results support the use of hip arthroscopy in appropriately selected patients, longer-term follow-up studies are required before conclusions can be made regarding its role in preventing osteoarthritis progression. Despite the heterogeneity inherent to multicenter studies, the results were consistent across centers, and the vast majority of patients surpassed thresholds for clinically meaningful improvement. These findings reinforce the role of hip arthroscopy as a safe and viable treatment option for adolescent FAI, providing stable functional outcomes and allowing return to pre-injury levels of activity in the medium term.

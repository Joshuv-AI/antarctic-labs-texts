Mazur–Ulam Theorem With Gromov-Hausdorff Distance.
S. A. Bogaty , A. A. Tuzhilin

Abstract
. It is shown that two Banach spaces are linearly isometric if and only if the Gromov–Hausdorff distance between them is finite, in particular, zero. The proof is compilative and relies on results obtained by many researchers on the approximability of almost-surjective almost-isometries by linear surjective isometries. In the finite-dimensional case, previously obtained by I. Mikhailov, a simpler proof under weaker assumptions is given. In the finite-dimensional case, a criterion for isometry in terms of finite (compact) subsets is also given. Keywords : metric space, Gromov–Hausdorff distance, Banach space, isometry. A.A. In 1932, in his famous monograph ‘‘Theory of Linear Operations’’ Stefan Banach connected the homeomorphism of metrizable compact spaces with the (linear) isometry of spaces of continuous functions on them

[

Theorem 1

One of the ways to strengthen the Mazur–Ulam theorem arose from the 1945 theorem of Hyers–Ulam
[
8
]
. The distance between points

of an arbitrary metric space will be denoted by
|xx^{\prime}|

of metric spaces, the

(1)

\operatorname{dis}f=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in X}\Bigl|\bigl|f(x)f(x^{\prime})\bigr|-|xx^{\prime}|\Bigr|.

A mapping

\varepsilon
-isometry
if and only if
\operatorname{dis}f\leq\varepsilon

The Hyers–Ulam theorem states that
for every surjective
\varepsilon
-isometry

f\colon V\to W,\ V=W

of a complete Euclidean vector space onto itself, for which
f(0)=0
, there exists a bijective linear isometry
U\colon V\to W
such that

(2)

\bigl\|f(v)-U(v)\bigr\|\leq M=10\varepsilon\ \text{ for all $v\in V$}.

This isometry

is given by the formula
U(v)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n}f(2nv)
.

D.G. Bourgin (1946) proved that such a linear isometry of

exists whenever

belongs to the class of uniformly convex Banach spaces, including

for
1<p<\infty
. Here we can take

in (

and

are Banach spaces of real continuous functions on compact Hausdorff spaces with the sup-norm, provided that

[
9

(1949), who showed, in particular, that the assumptions of continuity and one-to-oneness of

In 1975, R.D. Bourgin considered the Hyers-Ulam problem and studied mainly the finite-dimensional case. He showed that in this case the answer is positive if we assume that the set of extremal boundary points of the unit ball in

Remark 13

Problem 14
.

If two separable Banach spaces have the same compact (finite) subsets up to isometry, then are they isometrically embedded in each other?

For finite-dimensional spaces, the answer is positive.

Proposition 15
.

Let
\bigl(V,\|\cdot\|_{V}\bigr)
and
\bigl(W,\|\cdot\|_{W}\bigr)
be normed spaces such that
\dim W<\infty
and each finite subset
S=\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}\subset V
with
\|v_{0}-v_{1}\|_{V}=\ldots=\|v_{0}-v_{m}\|_{V}=1
can be isometrically embedded into

. Then
\dim V\leq\dim W
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\dim V>\dim W
. Consider a subspace
Z
of
V
such that
\dim Z=\dim W+1
. For every positive integer

, fix an
n^{-1}
-net

in the unit sphere
S_{Z}
of
Z
. Suppose that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the finite subset
Z(n)=S(n)\cup\{0_{Z}\}\subset Z
is isometrically embedded into
W
:
i_{n}\colon Z(n)\to W
. Applying the shift by the vector
\overrightarrow{i_{n}(0_{Z})0_{W}}
, we can assume without loss of generality that
i_{n}(0_{Z})=0_{W}
. Then
i_{n}\bigl(S(n)\bigr)
is a finite subset of the unit sphere
S_{W}
. The resulting sequence of compact subsets of the sphere

forms a sequence of points in the compact space of compact subsets
S_{W}
with Hausdorff distance

. We choose a convergent subsequence
\lim_{i\to\infty}i_{n_{i}}\bigl(S(n_{i})\bigr)=K\subset S_{W}
. Then

d_{GH}(S_{Z},K)\leq d_{GH}\bigl(S_{Z},S(n_{i})\bigr)+d_{GH}\bigl(i_{n_{i}}\bigl(S(n_{i})\bigr),K\bigr)\leq\\
\leq n_{i}^{-1}+d_{GH}\bigl(i_{n_{i}}\bigl(S(n_{i})\bigr),K\bigr)\to_{i\to\infty}0.

The resulting chain of inequalities means that

. Thus, the compact sets

and

are isometric, which contradicts Borsuk’s theorem.

Abstract
. The paper explores the effect of powerful class of Sylow p p -subgroups of a given finite group on control of transfer or fusion. We also find an explicit bound for the p p -length of a p p -solvable group in terms of the poweful class of a Sylow p p -subgroup.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
20D20, 20D15

1.
Introduction

The class of powerful
p
-groups was introduced by Lubotzky and Mann in their landmark paper
[
LM87
]
. Powerful
p
-groups have found numerous applications in the quest of understanding the structure of finite
p
-groups. Mann
[
Man95
]
also proved that if a finite group
G
has a powerful Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
, then
N_{G}(P)
controls transfer in
G
.

Related to being powerful is the notion of powerfully embedded subgroup of a finite
p
-group. Every finite
p
-group
P
has the largest powerfully embedded subgroup
\eta(P)
by
[
LM87
, Theorem 1.1]
. It is not difficult to see that
\eta
is a positive characteristic
p
-functor.

Our first main result is a variant of the second Grün theorem for
\eta
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If
\eta(P)
is weakly closed in
P
with respect to
G
, then
P\cap G^{\prime}=P\cap N_{G}(\eta(P))^{\prime}
.

A direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that, under the assumptions of the theorem, the functor
\eta
controls transfer in
G
(Corollary
3.4
). In Section
3
we also indicate when
\eta
strongly controls fusion in
G
.

The second part of the paper deals with the influence of the powerful class of a Sylow
p
-subgroup on the structure of a given finite group. The notion of powerful class
\pwc P
of a finite
p
-group
P
was first defined by Mann
[
Man11
]
, and further explored in
[
Mor24
]
. The group
P
is powerful if and only if
\pwc P\leq 1
. Powerful class is a refinement of the nilpotency class, as the nilpotency class of
P
provides an upper bound for
\pwc P
. Finite
p
-groups
P
with
\pwc P<p
are called the
p
-groups of small powerful class
. These groups have well-behaved power structure
[
Man11
]
. We show that finite
p
-groups of small powerful class also behave well as Sylow
p
-subgroups of a finite group. More precisely, if a finite group
G
has a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of small powerful class, then
N_{G}(P)
controls transfer in
G
(Proposition
3.2
). This generalizes the main result of
[
Man95
]
mentioned above.

Focusing on
p
-solvable groups
G
, we consider how the powerful class of a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
affects the
p
-length
\ell_{p}(G)
of
G
. Our starting point is a result of Khukhro
[
Khu12
, Theorem 4.1]
showing that if
P
is powerful, then
\ell_{p}(G)\leq 1
. We generalize this as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-solvable group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
.

(1)

If
p>2
, then
\ell_{p}(G)\leq\left\lceil\pwc P/(p-2)\right\rceil
.

(2)

If
p=2
, then
\ell_{2}(G)\leq\pwc P
.

Note that Theorem
1.2
also refines and improves the classical result that
\ell_{p}(G)
is bounded from above by the nilpotency class of
P
.

2.
Preliminaries

A normal subgroup
N
of a finite
p
-group
P
is
powerfully embedded
in
P
if
[N,P]\leq N^{2p}
. If
P
is powerfully embedded in itself, we say that
P
is a
powerful
p
-group
.

Denote by
\eta(P)
the largest powefully embedded subgroup of
P
, that is, the product of all powerfully embedded subgroups of
P
. Clearly,
\eta(P)
is a characteristic subgroup of
P
and contains the center
Z(P)
of
P
.

According to Mann
[
Man11
]
, an ascending series

1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq N_{2}\leq\cdots

of normal subgroups of
P
is said to be an
\eta
-series
if
N_{i+1}/N_{i}
is powerfully embedded in
P/N_{i}
for all
i
. The shortest length
k
of an
\eta
-series with
N_{k}=N
is called the
powerful height
of
N
. It is denoted by
\pwh_{P}(N)
.

The number
\pwc(P)=\pwh_{P}(P)
is called the
powerful class
of
P
. It is easy to see that if
K\triangleleft P
, then
\pwc(P/K)\leq\pwc P
.

The
upper
\eta
-series of
P
is defined by
\eta_{0}(P)=1
and

\eta_{i+1}(P)/\eta_{i}(P)=\eta(P/\eta_{i}(P))

for
i\geq 0
.
It is straightforward to see
[
Mor24
, Proposition 2.1]
that the upper
\eta
-series of
P
is the fastest growing
\eta
-series in
P
.

A group
P
is said to have
small powerful class
if
\pwc(P)<p
. Similarly, a normal subgroup
N
of
P
has
small powerful height
if
\pwh_{P}(N)<p
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p>2
and let
M
and
N
be normal subgroups of a finite
p
-group
P
.

(1)

\pwh_{P}[N,P]\leq\pwh_{P}N
,

(2)

\pwh_{P}N^{p}\leq\pwh_{P}N
,

(3)

\pwh_{P}MN\leq\max\{\pwh_{P}M,\pwh_{P}N\}
.

Proof.

Items (1) and (2) are proved in
[
Man11
, Lemma 2.5]
. To prove (3), take the
\eta
-series
1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq N_{k}=N
and
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}\leq\cdots\leq M_{\ell}=M
of
N
and
M
in
P
, respectively. Without loss of generality we may assume that
k\geq\ell
, and we put
M_{i}=M
for
i\geq\ell
. Then

[M_{i+1}N_{i+1},P]=[M_{i+1},P][N_{i+1},P]\leq M_{i+1}^{p}M_{i}N_{i+1}^{p}N_{i}\leq(M_{i+1}N_{i+1})^{p}(M_{i}N_{i})
, therefore
1=M_{0}N_{0}\leq M_{1}N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq M_{k}N_{k}=MN
is an
\eta
-series of
MN
in
P
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p
be odd, and let
N
be a normal subgroup of a finite
p
-group
P
. If
\pwh_{P}N\leq j
, then
[N,{}_{j}P]\leq N^{p}
.

Proof.

This is true for
j=1
, as
\pwh_{P}N=1
implies that
N
is powerfully embedded in
P
. Assume the claim holds for some
j\geq 1
. Let
\pwh_{P}N=j+1
, and let

1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq N_{j+1}=N
be an
\eta
-series in
P
. Then
[N,P]\leq N^{p}N_{j}
. As
\pwh_{P}N_{j}\leq j
, we have that
[N_{j},{}_{j}P]\leq N_{j}^{p}
. Thus

[N,{}_{j+1}P]=[N^{p},{}_{j}P][N_{j},{}_{j}P]\leq N^{p}N_{j}^{p}=N^{p}
.
∎

Other notions are standard
[
HupI
,
HBIII
]
.

3.
Local control

In this section we prove the results related to the local control. At first we note the following:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and
P=C_{p}\wr C_{p}
. Then
\eta_{i}(P)=Z_{i}(P)
for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

If
p=2
, this is straightforward to verifiy. Assume
p>2
. Note that
P
is a
p
-group of maximal class. The result now follows immediately from
[
Mor24
, Corollary 5.8]
.
∎

This provides our first results on control of transfer:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If
P
has small powerful class, then
N_{G}(P)
controls transfer in
G
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, the powerful class of
C_{p}\wr C_{p}
is equal to
p
. It follows that
C_{p}\wr C_{p}
cannot be a quotient of
P
. The result now follows from Yoshida’s transfer theorem
[
Isa11
, 10.1 Theorem]
.
∎

A consequence is the following:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If
P
has small powerful class, and if
N_{G}(P)
is
p
-nilpotent, then
G
is
p
-nilpotent.

Proof.

Denote
N=N_{G}(P)
. As
N
is
p
-nilpotent, we have that
O^{p}(N)\cap P=1
. By Proposition
3.2
, we also have that
O^{p}(N)=N\cap O^{p}(G)
. This gives
O^{p}(G)\cap P=1
, hence the result.
∎

The second Grün theorem
[
HupI
, IV, 3.7]
states that if a finite group
G
is
p
-normal and if
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, then
P\cap G^{\prime}=P\cap N_{G}(Z(P))^{\prime}
. Theorem
1.1
states that an analogous result holds when
Z(P)
is replaced with
\eta(P)
. The proof goes as follows:

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Denote
W=\eta(P)
. By Grün’s first theorem
[
HupI
, IV, 3.4 Satz]
, we have that

P\cap G^{\prime}=\langle P\cap N_{G}(P)^{\prime},P\cap(P^{\prime})^{g}\mid g\in G\rangle
. Thus it suffices to show that
P\cap(P^{\prime})^{g}\subseteq P\cap N_{G}(W)^{\prime}
for every
g\in G
.

Denote
T=P\cap(P^{\prime})^{g}
. Let
i
be the smallest non-negative integer with the property that
W^{p^{i}}\subseteq N_{G}(T)
. Suppose that
i>0
. Then there exists
x\in W^{p^{i-1}}
and there exists
t\in T
such that
t^{x}\notin T
. Then
[t,x]\in[P,W^{p^{i-1}}]\leq W^{p^{i}}\leq T
, therefore
t^{x}=t[t,x]\in T
, a contradiction. This shows that
W\subseteq N_{G}(T)
. We also have
W^{g}\subseteq N_{G}(T)
by a similar argument. Thus there exist a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P_{1}
of
N_{G}(T)
and
n\in N_{G}(T)
such that
W\subseteq P_{1}
and
W^{g}\subseteq P_{1}^{n}
. Let
P_{2}
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
that contains
P_{1}
. Then
W\subseteq P_{2}
and
W^{gn^{-1}}\subseteq P_{2}
. As
W
is weakly closed in
P_{2}
with respect to
G
, we have that

W=W^{gn^{-1}}
. This shows that
gn^{-1}\in N_{G}(W)
. We have
T=T^{n^{-1}}=P^{n^{-1}}\cap(P^{\prime})^{gn^{-1}}
. As
P\subseteq N_{G}(W)
, we conclude that

(P^{\prime})^{gn^{-1}}\subseteq(N_{G}(W)^{\prime})^{gn^{-1}}=N_{G}(W)^{\prime}
, hence

T=P\cap(P^{\prime})^{g}=P^{n^{-1}}\cap(P^{\prime})^{gn^{-1}}\subseteq P\cap N_{G}(W)^{\prime}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If
\eta(P)
is weakly closed in
P
with respect to
G
, then the functor
\eta
controls transfer in
G
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem
1.1
and
[
HBIII
, p. 52]
.
∎

Corollary
3.4
is related to a result of Gilloti and Serena
[
GS84
]
stating that if
\Phi(P)
is strongly closed in
P
with respect to
G
, then
\Phi
controls transfer in
G
. In the case when
\eta(P)
is strongly closed in
P
with respect to
G
, we can say more:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If
\eta(P)
is strongly closed in
P
with respect to
G
, and all
\eta(P)^{p^{i}}
are weakly closed in
P
with respect to
G
, then
\eta
strongly controls fusion in
G
.

Proof.

As
\eta(P)\geq\eta(P)^{p}\geq\eta(P)^{p^{2}}\geq\cdots
forms a central series of
\eta(P)
, the claim follows from the main result od Gilloti and Serena in
[
GS85
]
.
∎

We note here that if, for example,
G
is a
p
-nilpotent group with a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
, then every normal subgroup of
P
is strongly closed in
P
with respect to
G
.

4.
Powerful class and
p
-length

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
.

We recall a notion introduced by González-Sánchez
[
Gon07
]
.
Let
P
be a finite
p
-group. A sequence
N=N_{1}\geq N_{2}\geq\cdots\geq N_{k}=1
of normal subgroups of
P
is a
potent filtration of type
t
if
[N_{i},P]\leq N_{i+1}
and
[N_{i},{}_{t}P]\leq N_{i+1}^{p}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k-1
. In this case we say that
N
is
PF-embedded of type
t
in
P
. We have the following results proved by González-Sánchez and Spagnuolo
[
GSS15
]
:

Lemma 4.1

(
[
GSS15
]
)
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group,
P
its Sylow
p
-subgroup and
N
a normal subgroup of
P
.

(1)

If
p>2
and
N
is PF-embedded of type
p-2
in
P
, then
N\subseteq O_{p^{\prime}p}(G)
.

(2)

If
p=2
and
N
is PF-embedded of type
1
in
P
, then
N\subseteq O_{2^{\prime}2}(G)
.

At first we mention a variation of Khukhro’s result
[
Khu12
, Theorem 4.1]
:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-solvable group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Let
N\triangleleft P
be powerfully embedded in
P
. Then
N\subseteq O_{p^{\prime}p}(G)
.

Proof.

Consider first the case
p=2
. Here we note that the series
N\geq N^{2}\geq N^{4}\geq N^{8}\geq\cdots
is a potent filtration of type
1
starting with
N
. This shows that
N
is PF-embedded of type
1
in
P
. The result now follows from item (2) of Lemma
4.1
.

We are left with the case when
p
is odd.
The proof is essentially an adaptation of the proof of
[
Khu12
, Theorem 4.1]
, we give it for reader’s convenience. Let
G
be a smallest possible counterexample, with
N
powerfully embedded in
P
and
N\not\subseteq O_{p^{\prime}p}(G)
. By minimality, we have that
O_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
, and we may assume that
V=O_{p}(G)
is an elementary abelian
p
-group with
N\not\subseteq V
. By
[
Gor80
, Theorem 6.3.2]
we have that
C_{G}(V)=1
, and thus
V
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/V]
-module.

If
Q
is Hall
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
O_{pp^{\prime}}(G)
, then we may conclude as in
[
Khu12
]
that
Q
acts faithfully on
V
, and we have that
O_{pp^{\prime}}(G)=VQ
and
G=VN_{G}(Q)
. Furthermore, if
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
N_{G}(Q)
, then
P=V\rtimes S
.

Take
x\in N
of maximal possible order
p^{n}
. If
n=1
, then
[N,P]\leq N^{p}=1
, therefore
N\leq Z(P)\leq V
. So we have that
n\geq 2
. Let
y=x^{p^{n-2}}
. As
[N^{p^{n-1}},P]\leq N^{p^{n}}=1
, it follows that
y^{p}\in Z(P)\leq V
. Note also that
y
does not belong to
V
, as its order is
p^{2}
. On the other hand,
[N^{p^{n-2}},P,P]\leq N^{p^{n}}=1
implies
y\in Z_{2}(P)
. Now write
y=vs
, where
v\in V
and
s\in S\setminus\{1\}
. As
s^{p}\equiv y^{p}\mod[V,N]
, we conclude that
s^{p}\in V\cap S=1
. This implies that the linear transformation of
V
induced by conjugation by
s
has at least one Jordan block of size
p\times p
. Thus there exists
w\in V
with
[w,_{p-1}s]\neq 1
. This gives
[w,{}_{p-1}y]=1
, which contradicts the fact that
y\in Z_{2}(P)
.
∎

Our next result provides a basis for induction used later on in the proof of Theorem
1.2
for the case
p>3
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-solvable group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, where
p>3
. Let
N\triangleleft P
satisfy
\pwh_{P}N<p-1
. Then
N\subseteq O_{p^{\prime}p}(G)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
(1), it suffices to show that
N
is PF-embedded of type
p-2
in
P
. Let

1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq N_{k}=N

be an
\eta
-series of
N
in
P
with
k<p-1
. Put
N_{j}=1
for
j\leq 0
, and
N_{j}=N
for
j\geq k
.

At first we observe that Lemma
2.2
gives that if
K
is any normal subgroup of
P
with
\pwh_{P}K\leq p-2
, then
[K,{}_{p-2}P]\leq K^{p}
for all
j
. This, in particular, implies that
[K^{p},P]=[K,P]^{p}
and
(K^{p^{i}})^{p^{j}}=K^{p^{i+j}}
by
[
FGJ08
, Proposition 3.2, Theorem 3.4]
. This, together with Lemma
2.1
, will be used throughout the proof without further mention.

Define

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=N,

\displaystyle M_{i+1}

\displaystyle=M_{i}^{p}N_{p-i-2},

where
i>0
. We claim that
(M_{i})_{i}
is a potent filtration of type
p-2
in
P
.

At first note that
[M_{1},P]=[N_{1},P]\leq N_{1}^{p}N_{2}\leq N_{1}^{p}N_{p-3}=M_{2}
, since
p\geq 5
. Suppose
[M_{i},P]\leq M_{i+1}
for some
i\geq 1
. Then

[M_{i+1},P]=[M_{i}^{p}N_{p-i-2},P]=[M_{i},P]^{p}[N_{p-i-2},P]\leq M_{i+1}^{p}N_{p-i-2}^{p}N_{p-i-3}=M_{i+1}^{p}N_{p-i-3}=M_{i+2}
.

It remains to show that
[M_{i},{}_{p-2}P]\leq M_{i+1}^{p}
for all
i\geq 0
. At first consider
[M_{1},{}_{p-2}P]=[N,{}_{p-2}P]
. By the previous paragraph,
[M_{1},{}_{p-2}P]\leq M_{p-1}=M_{p-2}^{p}N_{p-(p-2)-2}=M_{p-2}^{p}\leq M_{2}^{p}
, as required. Assume the claim holds for some
i\geq 1
. We have
[M_{i+1},{}_{p-2}P]=[M_{i}^{p},{}_{p-2}P][N_{p-i-2},{}_{p-2}P]=[M_{i},{}_{p-2}P]^{p}[N_{p-i-2},{}_{p-2}P]\leq M_{i+1}^{p^{2}}[N_{p-i-2},{}_{p-2}P]
. We need to show that the latter group is contained in
M_{i+2}^{p}
. We may assume
M_{i+2}^{p}=1
, which yields
M_{i+1}^{p^{2}}=N_{p-i-3}^{p}=1
. Thus the problem reduces to showing that, under these assumptions,
[N_{p-i-2},{}_{p-2}P]=1
. To see this, first observe that
M_{i+1}^{p^{2}}=1
implies
N_{p-i-2}^{p^{2}}=1
.
This implies
[N_{p-i-2},P,P]\leq[N_{p-i-2}^{p}N_{p-i-3},P]=[N_{p-i-2},P]^{p}[N_{p-i-3},P]\leq N_{p-i-2}^{p^{2}}N_{p-i-3}^{p}N_{p-i-4}=N_{p-i-4}
. Inductive argument implies that

[N_{p-i-2},{}_{\ell}P]\leq N_{p-i-\ell-2}
for all
\ell\geq 2
. As
p-2\geq 2
, we conclude
[N_{p-i-2},{}_{p-2}P]\leq N_{p-i-(p-2)-2}=1
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-solvable group and
P
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. If
p>2
, then
\eta_{p-2}(P)\subseteq O_{p^{\prime}p}(G)
.

Proof.

If
p=3
, this follows from Proposition
4.2
, as
\eta_{1}(P)=\eta(P)
is powerfully embedded in
P
. For
p>3
note that
\pwh_{P}\eta_{p-2}(P)\leq p-2
by
[
Mor24
, Lemma 2.3]
, hence the result follows from Proposition
4.3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose first
p=2
. We prove the claim by induction on
k=\pwc P
. If
k=1
, the result follows from
[
Khu12
, Theorem 4.1]
. Suppose the claim holds for some
k\geq 1
, and let
\pwc P=k+1
, Then Lemma
4.1
(2) implies
\pwc(PO_{p^{\prime}p}(G)/O_{p^{\prime}p}(G))\leq\pwc(P/\eta(P))=\pwc P-1=k
, hence
\ell_{p}(G)=\ell_{p}(G/O_{p^{\prime}p}((G))+1\leq k+1
.

In the case when
p
is odd, we proceed by induction on the smallest integer
k
with the property that
k(p-2)\geq\pwc P
. If
k=1
, then the claim follows from Corollary
4.4
. For the inductive step note that
\pwc(PO_{p^{\prime}p}(G)/O_{p^{\prime}p}(G))\leq\pwc(P/\eta_{p-2}(P))
, and repeat the argument in the previous paragraph.
∎

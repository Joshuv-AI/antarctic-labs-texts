Joint association of different insulin resistance-related indices and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity with risk of cardiometabolic disease and multimorbidity: insights from the UK Biobank prospective cohort study
Authors: Xiaozhao Lu, Ziyao Yuan, Shangyi Tang, Haozhang Huang, Yihang Ling, Yingying Li, Jiexuan Li, Huangtao Ruan, Weipeng Zhang, Jingru Deng, Wei Guo, Shiqun Chen, Jin Liu, Jiyan Chen, Yong Liu

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInsulin resistance (IR)-related indices and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) were independent predictors for cardiometabolic disease (CMD) and multimorbidity (CMM), but their combined effect is unclear. We aimed to investigate their joint effect on incident CMD and CMM.MethodsThis study was conducted from the UK Biobank accelerometer sub-study. The IR-related indices included triglyceride-glucose index (TyG), TyG-body mass index (TyG-BMI), TyG-waist circumference (TyG-WC), TyG-waist-to-height ratio (TyG-WHtR), and metabolic score for IR (Mets-IR). Participants were divided according to the median values of IR-related indices and MVPA time. Cox regression was used to assess the associations between different groups and outcomes, with interaction effects estimated.Results74,367 participants were included. Introduction

Cardiometabolic diseases (CMD), interconnected disorders including type 2 diabetes (T2D), coronary artery disease (CAD), and stroke, are increasing globally and greatly contribute to disability and death [1, 2], driven by shared factors such as population aging, unhealthy dietary patterns, and lack of physical activity [3]. Notably, the progression from single CMDs to cardiometabolic multimorbidity (CMM)—the coexistence of two or more CMDs—further exacerbates adverse prognosis [2, 4]. Therefore, early identification and intervention of potential modifiable risk factors for CMD and CMM are essential.

Insulin resistance is a metabolic disorder marked by reduced sensitivity to insulin. Numerous studies have established valid associations between IR and the occurrence, progression, and prognosis of CMD, through mechanisms involving inflammation, oxidative stress, and endothelial dysfunction [5–7]. Recently, indices such as triglycerides-glucose (TyG), TyG-body mass index (TyG-BMI), TyG-waist circumference (TyG-WC), TyG-waist-to-height ratio (TyG-WHtR), and the metabolic score for IR (Mets-IR) have become recognized as accessible, affordable, and reliable biomarkers of insulin resistance [8–10]. Cui et al. demonstrated that the TyG index and its modified indices were significantly associated with incident CAD and stroke in middle-aged and older Chinese individuals [9]. In addition, higher METS-IR scores were significantly linked to an increased risk of both all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in the U.S. general population [10]. A large prospective cohort study from the UK Biobank showed that elevated IR-related indices were independently associated with incident CMD and transitions to CMM [11]. Physical activity (PA), particularly moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA), plays a pivotal role in modulating IR and CMD risk. Population-based studies showed that the MVPA was significantly associated with decreased risk of new-onset CMD and mortality in the obese population [12]. A previous meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials showed that MVPA over 6 weeks was associated with improved glucose homeostasis [13]. Mechanistically, PA may alleviate IR through reducing chronic inflammation, mitigating oxidative stress, and improving endothelial dysfunction [14]. Although the respective associations between IR-related indices and PA in CMD and CMM were well established, the combined effect of these factors on CMD and CMM remains poorly understood.

To address this research gap, we utilized data from the UK Biobank, a large-scale prospective cohort study, to examine the independent and joint associations of IR-related indices and MVPA time with the incidence of CMD and CMM.

Methods

Study population

Data were obtained from the UK Biobank, a community-based, prospective cohort study that recruited over 500,000 participants aged 37–73 years, identified using National Health Service (NHS) registers from 22 recruitment centres across England, Scotland, and Wales between April 2007 and December 2010. Baseline characteristics included sociodemographic, lifestyle, environmental, and health-related factors, as well as biological samples [15]. Ethical approval of the UK Biobank was obtained from the NHS North West Centre for Research Ethics Committee (reference 11/NW/0382). The use of UK Biobank data was approved under Application Number 99,231.

Between February 2013 and December 2015, a total of 233,245 individuals were invited to wear an Axivity AX3 wrist-worn triaxial accelerometer (Newcastle upon Tyne, United Kingdom) for 7 days. Of these, 103,691 accepted the invitation and submitted data. The monitor captured accelerations at 100 Hz with a dynamic range of +/- 8 g. After calibration, elimination of sensor noise and gravitational interference, and recognition of wear and non-wear intervals, four patterns of PA (sleep, sedentary, light PA, and MVPA) were derived using a machine learning method for behavior classification, which was previously validated in UK Biobank participants [16]. In the present study, the total MVPA time accumulated over the week was averaged on a daily basis (min/d). 29,342 individuals were excluded if meeting the following criteria: (1) withdrawal consent (n = 81); (2) poor calibration (n = 11); (3) with accelerometer wear time of less than 7 dyas (n = 8,133); (4) MVPA time not available (n = 1); (5) with coronary artery disease (CAD), diabetes mellitus or stroke before accelerometer assessment (n = 8,913); and (6) missing measurement of glucose, triglycerides (TG), body mass index (BMI), waist circumference (WC), and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) (n = 12,506). Finally, 74,367 participants enrolled in the final analysis (Fig. 1).

Assessment of IR-related indices and MVPA

Peripheral blood processing and quality control in the UK Biobank were conducted according to standardized protocols, available at https://www.biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/refer.cgi?id=1227. For the quantification of glucose, TG, and HDL-C, measurements were performed using the Beckman Coulter AU5800 clinical chemistry analyser. According to previous studies, 5 IR-related indices were calculated as follows:

TyG = Ln [TG (mg/dL) × Glucose (mg/dL)/2];

TyG = Ln [TG (mg/dL) × Glucose (mg/dL)/2];

TyG-BMI = TyG × [Weight(kg)/Height2 (m2)];

TyG-BMI = TyG × [Weight(kg)/Height2 (m2)];

TyG-WC = TyG × WC (cm);

TyG-WC = TyG × WC (cm);

TyG-WHtR = TsyG × [WC (cm)/Height (cm)];

TyG-WHtR = TsyG × [WC (cm)/Height (cm)];

Mets-IR = Ln [2 × Glucose (mg/dL) + TG (mg/dL)] × BMI/Ln HDL-C (mg/dL).

Mets-IR = Ln [2 × Glucose (mg/dL) + TG (mg/dL)] × BMI/Ln HDL-C (mg/dL).

Assetainment of outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was the incidence of CMD, including CAD, T2D, and stroke. The secondary outcome was CMM, defined as the coexistence of two or more CMDs. Incident CMDs were identified using the 10th International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10) and OPCS-4 codes. Follow-up observations started once accelerometry was completed and continued until the occurrence of an outcome event, death, or the final follow-up visit. The follow-up period duration was determined by the availability of linked health records, with a cut-off date of October 31, 2022. Covariates

Based on previous studies [8, 12], following characteristics were considered as the potential covariates: age (continuous); sex; ethnicity (White, Mixed, Black and other); smoking status (current smoking or not); alcohol drinking frequency (daily, 3–4 times/week, 1–2 times/week, 1–2 times/month, occasions, never); health diet score (continuous, a higher score indicates healthier diet habit); Townsend deprivation index; employment status (employed or not); income level (low: <£18,000; middle: £18,000–£51,999; high: ≥£52,000); systolic blood pressure (SBP, continuous); C-reactive protein (CRP, continuous); glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c, continuous); estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR, continuous). The healthy diet score was determined based on several dietary factors: consuming at least four tablespoons of vegetables daily (median); having at least three pieces of fruit per day (median); eating fish at least twice a week (median); limiting unprocessed red meat to no more than twice weekly (median); and restricting processed meat to no more than two servings per week (median). One point was given for each favorable diet factor, with the total score ranging from 0 to 5. The value of eGFR was calculated using serum creatinine based on the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration equation [17]. Obesity was defined as a BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2 with ≥ 1 measure of excess adiposity (defined by WC >88 cm for females, >102 cm for males; waist-to-hip ratio [WHR] >0.85 for females, >0.90 for males; or WHtR >0.50 for all), or ≥ 2 measures of excess adiposity (WC, WHR, WHtR), or a BMl ≥ 40 kg/m2, regardless of body shape measures [18].

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were presented as proportions for categorical variables and as means with standard deviations or medians with interquartile ranges for continuous variables, depending on whether they were normally distributed or not. Comparisons between groups were made using the t-test or Wilcoxon test for continuous variables as appropriate, and the chi-square test for categorical variables. Participants were divided according to the median values of IR-related indices (TyG index: 8.59, TyG-BMI index: 222.91, TyG-WC index: 747.79, TyG-WHtR: 4.42, Mets-IR index: 33.56) and MVPA time (34 min/d).

The CMD incidence rate per 1,000 person-years was presented. Kaplan-Meier curves were used to estimate the cumulative incidence of CMD and CMM, with log-rank tests to evaluate the difference between groups. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression was applied to estimate the hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for the associations between different groups and incident CMD and CMM. Model 1 was adjusted for age, sex, and model 2 was additionally adjusted for ethnicity, smoking status, alcohol drinking frequency, health diet score, Townsend deprivation index, employment status, income level, and model 3 was further adjusted for SBP, CRP, HbA1c, and eGFR. Subsequently, the interaction between IR-related indices and MVPA time was examined by adding a cross-product term to the Cox regression model. On the additive scale, interaction was evaluated using three measures: (1) Relative Excess Risk due to Interaction (RERI), which quantifies the excess risk attributable to the combined effect beyond the sum of individual effects; (2) Attributable Proportion (AP), indicating the proportion of the outcome that can be ascribed to the interaction between the two exposures; and (3) the Synergy Index (SI), representing the ratio of the joint effect’s excess risk to the sum of the excess risks from each exposure alone. In addition, the net reclassification index (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement index (IDI) were used to evaluate the improvement of predictive performance of CMD when adding IR-related indices and MVPA time. Receiver operating characteristics (ROC) and the area under the curve (AUC) were assessed to evaluate the predictive performance.

A multi-state model was applied to estimate the HR and 95% CI of the associations between IR-related indices and MVPA with the transition from CMD to CMM. We performed competing risk analysis using the Fine-Grey method for CMD or CMM, accounting for all-cause mortality as the competing event. Subgroup analyses were performed to examine the consistency in the association between IR-related indices, MVPA time, and their joint effect on incident CMD. Participants were stratified by age (≥ 60 years old or < 60 years old), sex, obesity (yes or no), hypertension (yes or no), and CRP level (≥ 2 mg/L or < 2 mg/L).

All analyses were performed by R software (version 4.4.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Statistical significance was defined as Benjamin-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR)-corrected P values < 0.05 for interaction analyses, and two-sided P-value < 0.05 indicated significance for all other analyses.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 74,367 participants were included in this study, with an average age of 55.8 ± 7.8 years and 41.9% female. Figure 2 showed the distribution of quantiles of IR-related indices and MVPA time. Participants were divided based on the median of IR-related indices (TyG: 8.59; TyG-BMI: 222.91; TyG-WC: 747.79; TyG-WHtR: 4.42; Mets-IR: 33.56) and MVPA time (34.00 min/d): low IR-related indices with high MVPA, low IR-related indices with low MVPA, high IR-related indices with high MVPA, and high IR-related indices with low MVPA. Participants with high TyG were more likely to be older and female. They also exhibited a worse cardiovascular risk profile, marked by higher BMI, higher proportion of obesity, elevated blood pressure, increased CRP levels, a poorer lipid profile, and lower eGFR levels. A similar trend was observed among participants with low MVPA time (Table 1). Additionally, more baseline characteristics of combined IR-related indices and MVPA were shown in Tables S2–S6.

Association of IR-related indices and MVPA time with the risk of CMD and CMM

During a median follow-up of 7.9 [interquartile range: 7.3–8.4] years, 4,894 individuals (34.57 per 1,000 person-years) occurred CMD and 361 (2.46 per 1,000 person-years) occurred CMM. When participants were stratified by low and high MVPA, greater cumulative risk of CMD and CMM were observed in participants with low MVPA time (Figure S1). Participants with higher IR-related indices and lower MVPA time had a greater cumulative risk of CMD and CMM (all log-rank P < 0.001) (Fig. 3 & Figure S2). Compared to participants with low TyG index and high MVPA time, those with low TyG index and low MVPA time, high TyG index and high MVPA time, and high TyG index and low MVPA time had a 34% (HR: 1.34, 95% CI: 1.21–1.50), 36% (HR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.24–1.50) and 71% (HR: 1.71, 95% CI: 1.55–1.88) increased risk of CMD after adjueting confounders (model 3), respectively (all P < 0.001; Table 2). For CMM, the corresponding HRs of TyG index were 2.06 (95% CI: 1.24–3.40), 2.12 (95% CI: 1.34–3.37), and 3.22 (95% CI: 2.07–5.01) (Table 2). The multi-state model showed that participants with a high TyG index and low MPVA time had an 88% increased risk of progression from CMD and CMM (Table S8). Similarly, significant associations were observed for TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR, and Mets-IR.

Incremental predictive values of IR-related indices and MVPA for the risk of CMD

We further investigated whether including IR-related indicators and MVPA in the conventional model (Model 3) could enhance its ability to predict the risk of incident CMD and CMM. Compared to the conventional model, the addition of IR-related indices was significantly associated with improvements in NRI and IDI for CMD incidence (Table 4; all P values < 0.001). Among these indicators, the TyG-WHtR index and Mets-IR had the higher additive predictive value for CMD (TyG-WHtR, NRI: 29.20%, 95% CI: 26.04%–32.36%; IDI: 0.77, 95% CI: 0.66–0.89; Mets-IR, NRI: 29.34%, 95% CI: 26.17%–32.51%; IDI: 0.84, 95% CI: 0.71–0.96), followed by TyG-WC index (NRI: 27.79%, 95% CI: 24.63%–30.96%; IDI: 0.72, 95% CI: 0.61–0.83), TyG-BMI index (NRI: 27.76%, 95% CI: 24.59%–30.93%; IDI: 0.68, 95% CI: 0.57–0.80), and TyG index (NRI: 21.10%, 95% CI: 17.93%–24.27%; IDI: 0.31, 95% CI: 0.24–0.38). Furthermore, MVPA time was incorporated into the conventional model and IR-related indices, and also significantly increased NRI and IDI for CMD incidence (all P values < 0.001). For ROC analysis, both TyG-WHtR index and Mets-IR combining MVPA had the greater predictive value, with AUC of 0.740 for CMD (TyG-WHtR index: 95% CI: 0.733–0.747; Mets-IR: 95% CI: 0.733–0.748) and 0.830 and 0.829 for CMM (TyG-WHtR index: 95% CI: 0.808–0.853; Mets-IR: 95% CI: 0.806–0.852) (Figure S2 and Figure S3).

Subgroup analysis

When participants were stratified by age and sex, the combined effect of high IR-related indices and low MVPA time for CMD was more pronounced in young or female participants (Fig. 4 and Figure S4; P for interaction < 0.05). Among IR-related indices, the above interactions were more significant in TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR and Mets-IR (P for interaction < 0.001). The associations between IR-related indices and MVPA for CMD were consistent in participants stratified by obesity, hypertension, and CRP level, with no significant interactions (all P for interaction > 0.05).

Discussion

Among 74,367 middle-aged adults who underwent prospective accelerometer measurements from UK Biobank with a median follow-up of 7.9 years, we discovered the following findings: (1) participants with high IR-related indices and low MVPA time were significantly associated with increased risk of CMD and CMM; (2) synergistic effects were observed in IR-related indices and MVPA on CMD and CMM incidence; (3) joint assessment of IR-related indices and MVPA could enhance predictive capability for CMD; (4) the associations between IR-related indices and MVPA with CMD incidence were consistent in subgroup analyses and more evident in female and young individuals. In summary, our findings provide novel perspectives on modifiable risk factors for the prevention and intervention of CMD and CMM.

The gold standard for measuring IR, the hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp test, is not widely applied owing to its invasiveness and high cost. The TyG index is now considered as a valuable alternative biomarker for IR and has been widely validated in multiple populations as an independent risk factor for the development of cardiovascular disease and poor prognosis. Recently, Tian et al. found that TyG and TyG-related indices (including TyG-BMI, TyG-WC, TyG-WHtR) were independently associated with incident CMD and transitions to CMM from the UK Biobank with 374,274 individuals free of CMDs, among which TyG-WHtR and TyG-WC exerted greater predictive value for CMM [11]. The above associations were validated in our study, and among the IR-related indices, the TyG-WHtR and Met-IR indices had higher predictive performance for CMD. Meanwhile, IR-related indices were identified as independent predictors for all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in the general population and individuals with CMM [10, 19]. Therefore, the non-insulin-based surrogate for IR could be integrated into risk stratification for the development and progression of CMD.

MVPA refers to physical exercises that significantly increase heart rate and breathing, such as brisk walking, running, or cycling, and is essential for improving cardiovascular health, managing weight, and reducing the risk of chronic diseases like T2D and cardiovascular disease. A study based on the 2016 Canadian Community Health Survey, which included 13,226,748 Canadian individuals aged 50 years and older, revealed that the prevalence of CMM was 3.5% and lack of PA (self-reported) was cross-sectionally associated with an increased prevalence of CMM (odds ratio OR: 2.35, 95% CI: 1.87 to 2.95) [20]. Consistent with previous studies, our study found that low MVPA was associated with increased risk of CMD and CMM. Recent evidence further enriches this perspective by highlighting the role of PA timing. Albalak et al. found that morning physical activity, particularly in the late morning, was associated with a 16% lower risk of coronary artery disease and a 17% lower risk of stroke, independent of total PA levels [21]. Sabag et al. provided complementary evidence, showing that evening MVPA was associated with the lowest risks of all-cause mortality (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.57–0.76), cardiovascular disease (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.54–0.75), and microvascular disease (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.63–0.92) in adults with obesity, including those with type 2 diabetes [12]. Walmsley et al. found that reallocating 20 min/day to MVPA was associated with 9% lower risk of cardiovascular disease in the UK Biobank accelerometer substudy [16]. Collectively, MVPA, regardless of its timing or distribution patterns, plays a crucial role in mitigating the risk of CMD and the progression to multimorbidity, making it a key modifiable factor in preventing and managing CMD and CMM.

MVPA and IR share a significant and reciprocal relationship that plays a crucial role in metabolic health. According to a study by van der Velde et al. from 775 participants of the Netherlands Epidemiology of Obesity study, the results demonstrated that engaging in MVPA in the afternoon or evening was associated with a up to 25% reduction in insulin resistance compared to evenly distributing MVPA throughout the day [22]. Previous study also revealed the effects of different levels of exercise volume, intensity and mode on insulin sensitivity and glucose homeostasis have their own characteristics. Moderate-intensity aerobic exercise shows significant advantages in improving insulin sensitivity and glucose tolerance, while high-intensity aerobic exercise is more effective in certain specific indicators such as peripheral insulin sensitivity [23]. Our research simultaneously provides supporting evidence for these views and found out that the higher the insulin resistance index, the shorter the median time of MVPA. Meanwhile, our research suggests that the combination of IR and MVPA has an additive joint impact on the occurrence and progression of CMD and CMM. According to our research findings, high IR and low MVPA can further increase the risk of CMD by approximately 10% and the risk of CMM by around 30% without significant multiplicative interaction. This indicates that when both low MVPA and high IR coexist, the risk of cardiovascular metabolic diseases is significantly higher than when either of these two factors exists alone and the relative risk associated with MVPA remains consistent across different levels of IR. These underscore the “high IR + low MVPA” subgroup as a priority for targeted prevention, and MVPA’s protective effect is consistent across all IR levels, not limited to those with low IR. While adding IR-related indices and MVPA to the conventional model yielded mild improvements in the area under the ROC curve, the results remain statistically significant, and the significant improvements of NRI and IDI were observed, which could provide better risk stratification. Among five IR-related indices, the TyG-WHtR index appears to be the most suitable for assessing CMD and CMM when combined with MVPA time by its high AUC values (0.740 for CMD and 0.830 for CMM), and its robust and strongest additive interaction effects, which remained significant after FDR correction. In addition, our subgroup analysis revealed that the association between high IR and low MVPA and CMD was more pronounced in young or female participants. The underlying mechanism may be related to the sharp decline in estrogen levels during perimenopause. Estrogen exerts metabolic and cardiovascular protective effects. Its deficiency leads to deteriorated IR and weakens vascular function. Moreover, low MVPA reduces muscle glucose utilization, exacerbating IR and leading to development of CMD and CMM [24].

Low MVPA and IR share common mechanisms in the occurrence and development of cardiometabolic diseases, such as dysfunction of endomembrane, chronic inflammation, endoplasmic reticulum stress. Under insulin resistance, insulin-mediated endothelium-dependent vasodilation is impaired, leading to endothelial dysfunction and weakens the beneficial effects of exercise training on vascular health. Moreover, a lack of physical activity aggravates insulin resistance, creating a vicious cycle that ultimately increases the risk of cardiometabolic diseases [14, 25]. In addition, studies have shown that adipose tissue releases pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-α and IL-6. These cytokines exacerbate inflammation, interfere with insulin signalling, and reduce insulin sensitivity. Chronic inflammation promotes macrophage infiltration in adipose tissue, forming a vicious cycle. Lack of MVPA reduces muscle glucose uptake, disrupts energy metabolism, and increases fat accumulation, promoting chronic inflammation. Thus, low MVPA and IR together increase the risk of cardiometabolic diseases by triggering and maintaining chronic inflammation [26, 27]. Specifically, low MVPA leads to energy metabolism imbalance, causing lipid accumulation in organs like adipose tissue and liver and inducing endoplasmic reticulum stress. IR causes metabolic abnormalities like high blood sugar and lipid in cells, worsening endoplasmic reticulum stress. Once activated, endoplasmic reticulum stress triggers inflammatory responses, inhibits insulin signalling, induces cell apoptosis and other pathological processes, promoting cardiometabolic disease progression [14, 25].

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the assessment of PA patterns was not evaluated at baseline, and the time gap could influence results owing to changes in health or activity levels. Second, despite considering multiple potential confounding variables, the influence of some unmeasured or uncontrolled variables on the results cannot be ruled out. Third, as the accelerometer substudy was conducted several years after the initial UK Biobank enrollment, survivorship bias may have resulted in a healthier cohort compared to the broader UK Biobank population. Fourth, PA assessments only lasted for 1 week, which may not accurately represent habitual patterns. In addition, since this analysis was based on the UK Biobank cohorts, the extrapolation of these findings to other populations and ethnic groups should be cautious.

Conclusions

In the UK Biobank accelerometer substudy, our findings indicate that elevated IR-related indices and low MVPA time were significantly associated with higher risks of incident CMD, with particularly pronounced effects among young and female participants. Synergistic effects and additive predictive values were observed between IR-related indices and MVPA in relation to CMD risk, with these associations remaining robust for CMM and progression from CMD to CMM. Among the IR-related indices, the TyG-WHtR index may emerge as the most suitable for assessing CMD and CMM when combined with MVPA. These findings underscore the importance of targeting high-risk individuals with IR—especially those with low MVPA—using the TyG-WHtR index alongside MVPA for tailored prevention and management strategies for CMD and CMM.

Fucosterol from marine algae: a multifunctional marine phytosterol with health benefits-from dietary component to functional ingredient
Authors: Shasha Guo, Xiaochun Liu, Yanchun Gao, Fangxue Xu, Xiaozhi Xi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFucosterol is the predominant sterol in brown algae, exhibits multiple bioactivities relevant to human health, including regulation of lipid metabolism, improvement of insulin resistance, as well as anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, neuroprotective, and antitumor effects. These effects are primarily mediated through the modulation of key metabolic and inflammatory signaling pathways, including LXR, PPARγ, and NF-κB. However, its high hydrophobicity (LogP≈7.9), poor aqueous solubility, and low oral bioavailability (<10%), together with its susceptibility to oxidative degradation during processing and storage, significantly limit its application in functional food systems. This review systematically summarizes the distribution, content variability, and structural characteristics of fucosterol in edible seaweeds, as well as its diet-related health benefits and underlying molecular mechanisms. Furthermore, recent advances in food-grade delivery and encapsulation strategies for improving the stability and bioavailability of lipophilic sterols are discussed, with emphasis on systems such as liposomes, nanoemulsions, and biopolymer-based carriers. A comparative evaluation of the advantages and limitations of these delivery systems is also provided in the context of functional food development. Future research directions are proposed to support the development of fucosterol as a marine-derived functional food ingredient.Keywords: Fucosterol, Marine phytosterol, Functional ingredient, Health benefits, Food-grade delivery

Introduction

Marine ecosystems cover over 70% of the Earth's surface, are a rich source of natural products with high chemical diversity (Carroll et al., 2025; Lv et al., 2025). Under unique environmental conditions, such as high salinity, high pressure, low temperature, and complex ecological interactions. Marine organisms have evolved metabolic pathways distinct from those of terrestrial organisms, generating a wide range of structurally novel and functionally diverse secondary metabolites (Han, Zhai, et al., 2024; Khan et al., 2025). Consequently, marine-derived bioactive compounds have become important sources of functional food ingredients and health-promoting molecules (Ding et al., 2025; Saini et al., 2023).

Among marine-derived bioactive compounds, algae phytosterols have attracted increasing attention due to their important roles in maintaining cell membrane integrity, regulating lipid metabolism, and mediating signal transduction (Wu et al., 2022). Compared with major terrestrial plant sterols (such as β-sitosterol, stigmasterol, and campesterol) (Zhang et al., 2016), fucosterol exhibits distinct structural and functional characteristics (Meinita et al., 2021; Zhen et al., 2015). Fucosterol possesses a Δ5,24(28)-stigmastadien-3β-ol skeleton with a distinctive trans-ethylidene side chain at the C-24 position, which contributes to its unique spatial conformation. This structural feature is relatively uncommon among terrestrial phytosterols (Hannan et al., 2020). In addition, terrestrial phytosterols are primarily associated with cholesterol-lowering and cardiovascular health benefits (Baumgartner et al., 2011), whereas fucosterol exhibits broader bioactivities, including anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective, and metabolic regulatory effects, which are closely linked to the modulation of signaling pathways such as LXR, PPARγ, and NF-κB (Ayyad et al., 2011). However, current evidence mainly derives from in vitro experiments and animal studies, with limited support from systematic clinical or dietary intervention studies. Moreover, inconsistencies remain regarding the reported mechanisms of action and efficacy across different studies, and comprehensive integrative analyses are still lacking.

Despite its promising functional potential, fucosterol still faces significant challenges in food applications. Its high lipophilicity, poor aqueous solubility, and limited bioavailability (Acartürk et al., 1993; Wang et al., 2022) substantially restrict its application in functional foods. In addition, fucosterol is susceptible to oxidative degradation during processing and storage, further compromising its stability and bioactivity. These limitations indicate that improving its stability and bioavailability remains a critical challenge. In recent years, considerable progress has been made in food-grade delivery systems for lipophilic bioactive compounds, particularly emulsion-based systems, liposomes, and biopolymer-based carriers. These strategies have shown considerable potential for improving controlled release, physicochemical stability, and intestinal absorption of lipophilic compounds. However, compared with extensively studied lipophilic components such as carotenoids and polyunsaturated fatty acids, research on fucosterol delivery systems remains limited. Systematic analyses linking its physicochemical properties to appropriate delivery strategies are still lacking, which restricts its application in functional foods.

From the perspective of food science and functional food application, this review summarizes fucosterol derived from algae. We focused on the natural sources and distribution characteristics, structural properties and their relationship with bioactivities, and a comprehensive evaluation of the diet-related health effects and underlying mechanisms of fucosterol. This review places particular emphasis on recent advances in food-grade delivery strategies to enhance its stability and bioavailability. Finally, key scientific challenges are identified and future research directions are proposed to support the high-value utilization of marine-derived functional components and the development of novel functional foods.

The sources, structural characteristics of Fucosterol

Fucosterol plays a crucial role in maintaining membrane fluidity and regulating signal transduction (Poulose et al., 2021). Fucosterol was first isolated from Fucus vesiculosus and has since been identified in various seaweeds across different ecological regions, exhibiting considerable variation in content (Lopes et al., 2011). Brown algae generally contain substantially higher levels of fucosterol than green and red algae, highlighting their importance as major natural sources of this compound. In representative brown algae species such as Hizikia fusiformis, Laminaria japonica, and Undaria pinnatifida, fucosterol accounts for a high proportion of total phytosterols (Chen et al., 2023; Saini et al., 2021). Given the distribution characteristics described above, elucidating the structural basis of fucosterol is essential for understanding its functional properties. Fucosterol is characterized by a cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene nucleus and an unsaturated side chain. Its key features include a C-24(28) double bond at the C-24 position and a Δ5 double bond within the sterol nucleus (Fig. 1) (Ikekawa, 1981; Terasaki et al., 2009). The C-5(6) double bond within the sterol nucleus promotes its incorporation into lipid bilayers, thereby influencing membrane stability and fluidity (Meinita et al., 2021). Meanwhile, the trans-ethylidene side chain at the C-24 position increases conformational flexibility, potentially modulating interactions with lipid membranes and membrane-associated proteins (Hannan et al., 2020). In addition, structural variations in sterol side chains have been shown to significantly affect their accommodation within nuclear receptor binding pockets, thereby modulating ligand–receptor affinity and selectivity (El Omari et al., 2024). Therefore, the distinctive C-24 side chain of fucosterol may contribute to its functional differences from terrestrial phytosterols. Furthermore, the conjugated diene system formed by the C-5(6) and C-24(28) double bonds enables partial radical stabilization through electron delocalization, contributing to its potential antioxidant activity (Plaza et al., 2010). However, this unsaturated structure also increases susceptibility to degradation under light, heat, and oxidative conditions, leading to reduced or even loss of bioactivity and limiting its practical applications in food and pharmaceutical systems. Consistent with these structural features, fucosterol exhibits pronounced hydrophobicity, poor water solubility, and limited oral bioavailability, while its double-bond system further contributes to oxidative instability (Vahouny et al., 1983). These factors collectively restrict its stability and efficacy in practical applications. Overall, these structural characteristics govern both its physicochemical behavior and biological functions. Therefore, systematically elucidating the bioactivities of fucosterol and their underlying mechanisms is essential for a comprehensive understanding of its functional value.

Diet-related bioactivities of fucosterol: evidence, limitations, and relevance to functional foods

As a dietary phytosterol abundant in edible seaweeds, fucosterol has attracted increasing attention as a potential functional food component. Current evidence suggests that its biological activities are closely associated with the modulation of multiple intracellular signaling pathways (El Omari et al., 2024; Rocha et al., 2022; Shah et al., 2022). Recent studies, predominantly based on in vitro assays and animal models, have investigated its potential effects across multiple pathological conditions, including metabolic disorders, cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and neurodegenerative disorders. Previous studies have suggested that fucosterol may exert diverse biological effects through the regulation of nuclear receptors, inflammatory mediators, lipid metabolism, and oxidative stress-related pathways (Hannan et al., 2020; Rahman et al., 2021). However, inconsistencies remain among studies in terms of the reported mechanisms and magnitude of effects, and systematic integrative analyses, as well as validation from clinical or dietary intervention studies, are still limited.

Mechanisms of fucosterol in regulating lipid metabolism and glucose homeostasis

Fucosterol exerts multi-target regulatory effects on lipid metabolism, primarily through the modulation of nuclear receptor signaling pathways. Existing studies suggest that fucosterol can activate liver X receptor (LXR) and promote reverse cholesterol transport. Zhan et al. (2023) reported that fucosterol upregulates LXR target genes, such as ABCA1 and ABCG1, in macrophages and the intestine, thereby enhancing cholesterol efflux without significantly inducing the expression of SREBP-1c-related lipogenic genes in the liver, suggesting potential tissue selectivity (Fig. 2). However, this selective activation mechanism is primarily supported by in vitro and animal studies, and its consistency across different tissues, as well as its underlying molecular basis, remain insufficiently validated. Therefore, whether it can avoid adverse effects such as hepatic steatosis induced by conventional LXR agonists remains to be determined. Further studies by Hoang et al. indicated that fucosterol does not activate SREBP-1c-mediated lipogenic genes in the liver, thereby avoiding common side effects of synthetic agonists, such as hepatic steatosis and hypertriglyceridemia. This selective mechanism suggests that fucosterol may represent a safer candidate for lipid-lowering therapy (Hoang et al., 2012). Additionally, fucosterol has been reported to inhibit adipocyte differentiation by blocking the PPARγ and C/EBPα signaling axis (Fig. 2). In the 3 T3-L1 cell model, fucosterol downregulates key transcription factors and reduces lipid droplet accumulation (Jung et al., 2014). However, current evidence is largely derived from single-cell models, and the influence of experimental variables, such as dosage and treatment duration, has not been systematically evaluated. Furthermore, compared with classical PPARγ agonists (such as rosiglitazone), whether fucosterol confers an advantage in avoiding weight gain remains unsupported by long-term in vivo studies (Zhao, Qian, et al., 2024). Furthermore, Dayarathne et al. (2024) reported that fucosterol ameliorates liver injury by inhibiting caspase-3-mediated apoptosis and regulating lipid metabolism-related pathways. In NAFLD animal models, it reduces serum transaminase levels and improves hepatic lipid deposition. However, discrepancies persist among different experimental models (e.g., high-fat diet versus alcohol-induced models), and its regulatory effects on lipid metabolism pathways (such as SREBP-1c suppression and β-oxidation enhancement) require further validation across diverse experimental systems. Furthermore, fucosterol exerts multi-pathway regulatory effects on glucose metabolism. Studies have shown that it can reduce blood glucose levels and improve glucose tolerance (Jung et al., 2013; Mo et al., 2018). Lee et al. (2017) demonstrated that fucosterol may function by inhibiting aldose reductase activity and modulating signaling pathways such as PI3K/Akt. However, these mechanisms are primarily inferred from molecular docking and in vitro studies, and in vivo relevance remains unclear. Regarding its inhibitory effect on α-glucosidase, discrepancies exist in the reported efficacy across different studies (Jung et al., 2013), and standardized comparisons with clinical drugs (such as acarbose) are still lacking. Therefore, the underlying mechanisms of its hypoglycemic effects require further systematic integration and validation.

Mechanisms of fucosterol in lipid metabolism and glucose regulation (Jung et al., 2014; Lee et al., 2017; Mo et al., 2018).

The efficacy of fucosterol as a functional food component is closely related to its bioaccessibility during gastrointestinal digestion. As a highly lipophilic compound, its incorporation into mixed micelles, interaction with dietary lipids, and release from food matrices are critical determinants of its intestinal absorption. Therefore, future studies should integrate mechanistic findings with food digestion models to better evaluate its realistic functional potential.

Cardiovascular protective effects of fucosterol and underlying mechanisms

Atherosclerosis represents the central pathological basis of cardiovascular diseases, and its development involves complex interactions among lipid metabolism disorders, inflammatory responses, and oxidative stress (Libby, 2021; Kong et al., 2022). Although statins are widely used to reduce LDL-C levels, their potential side effects and PCSK9-mediated compensatory mechanisms partially limit their therapeutic efficacy (Takaoka et al., 2024; Desai et al., 2014; Taylor & Thompson, 2016). Therefore, novel regulatory strategies targeting key pathways of cholesterol metabolism (such as the LXR signaling pathway) have attracted increasing research interest (Fig. 3). However, existing synthetic LXR agonists generally exhibit limited selectivity, particularly due to the non-specific activation of LXRα, which can lead to lipid metabolism disorders (Hoekstra et al., 2024), thereby restricting their translational potential. Additionally, dysregulated cholesterol metabolism serves as both a prerequisite and a key driver of atherosclerosis initiation and progression (Fig. 3).

Anti-atherosclerotic and vascular protective mechanisms of fucosterol (Hoang et al., 2012; Kirindage et al., 2022; Li et al., 2022; Srivastava et al., 2020).

Studies suggest that fucosterol can promote cholesterol efflux by upregulating ABCA1 and ABCG1, while simultaneously inhibiting SR-A and CD36-mediated lipid uptake, thereby reducing foam cell formation (Hoang et al., 2012; Yu & Tang, 2022). These findings suggest that fucosterol reduces foam cell formation through dual mechanisms: promoting cholesterol efflux and inhibiting lipid uptake. Srivastava et al. further investigated downstream cholesterol metabolism and proposed that fucosterol may promote cholesterol conversion into bile acids and enhance excretion via LXR-related pathways. However, Srivastava et al. (2020) did not validate changes in ABCA1/ABCG1 within the same experimental system, and thus the direct link between LXR activation and cholesterol efflux remains insufficiently supported. Additional studies have reported that fucosterol may regulate key transport proteins, such as SR-B1, NPC1L1, and ABCG5/G8, thereby influencing cholesterol absorption and transport at multiple stages (Liu et al., 2025; Stellaard & Lütjohann, 2025). However, these findings are derived from heterogeneous experimental models (cell-based and animal models), and discrepancies exist in both the magnitude and direction of the reported effects. A unified systematic comparative analysis is still lacking. Beyond lipid metabolism, anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects play critical roles in preventing the progression from cholesterol deposition to atherosclerosis. Studies have shown that fucosterol reduces the expression of inflammatory mediators by inhibiting the NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways and attenuates vascular inflammation in ApoE−/− mouse models (Li et al., 2022). In parallel, it enhances antioxidant defense capacity by activating the Nrf2-ARE pathway and reducing ROS levels (Kirindage et al., 2022), thereby contributing to the prevention of atherosclerosis progression.

Overall, fucosterol demonstrates promising potential in cardiovascular protection. Current studies have revealed its multi-target regulatory characteristics from two major aspects: cholesterol metabolism (efflux, uptake, conversion, and transport) and inflammation–oxidation (NF-κB/MAPK-mediated anti-inflammatory pathways and Nrf2-ARE-mediated antioxidant pathways). However, research on its mechanisms of action remains incomplete. Future studies should prioritize systematic comparative analyses and validation through dietary intervention studies, while further clarifying its functional role within integrated regulatory networks involving lipid metabolism, inflammation, and oxidative stress.

Neuroprotective effects of fucosterol and its relevance to cognitive function from a dietary perspective

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disorder characterized by progressive cognitive decline, and its pathogenesis involves complex interactions among multiple factors, including Aβ deposition, tau protein abnormalities, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammation (Hajihosseini et al., 2025; Scheltens et al., 2021). Currently available FDA-approved drugs (such as donepezil and memantine) provide only symptomatic relief without halting disease progression. This limitation is largely attributed to the complexity of AD pathogenesis. Its core pathological processes encompass oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and energy metabolism disturbances, collectively forming a highly interconnected “neurodegenerative network” (Fig. 4). Such complexity necessitates the development of multi-target intervention strategies (Bai et al., 2022; Lim et al., 2023; Song et al., 2021).

Neuroprotective mechanisms of fucosterol in Alzheimer's disease (Castro-Silva et al., 2021; Gao et al., 2022; Ko et al., 2021; Oh et al., 2018; Sun et al., 2015).

In this context, natural bioactive compounds with multi-target properties have attracted increasing attention. Castro-Silva et al. reported that fucosterol may exert neuroprotective effects by inhibiting Aβ aggregation and modulating tau phosphorylation (Castro-Silva et al., 2021). However, evidence regarding its specific molecular targets and functional mechanisms remains incomplete, and most findings are derived from in vitro studies lacking a unified evaluation framework. Moreover, there is insufficient evidence to determine whether fucosterol can effectively cross the blood–brain barrier in vivo and exert sustained biological effects. Given that oxidative stress and neuroinflammation are central mechanisms in AD pathogenesis, the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties of fucosterol may contribute to its neuroprotective potential. Sun et al. (2015) proposed that fucosterol enhances antioxidant defense primarily through Nrf2 nuclear translocation, leading to upregulation of downstream enzymes such as HO-1, SOD, and GPx. In contrast, Ko et al. (2021) emphasized its direct radical-scavenging activity, demonstrating reduced ROS levels and improved neuronal survival independent of transcriptional regulation. These differences may arise from variations in experimental models and detection systems, highlighting the need for standardized comparative studies. Regarding anti-inflammatory effects, Wong et al. (2018) demonstrated that fucosterol suppresses NF-κB activation and p38/ERK MAPK signaling, thereby reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine production. Further mechanistic analysis indicated that inhibition of MKK3/6 and MK2 may underlie this regulatory effect. These findings collectively suggest that fucosterol exerts multi-target regulatory effects within complex pathological environments. Studies on Aβ metabolism indicate that fucosterol may reduce Aβ production by inhibiting β-secretase activity (Jung et al., 2016). Additionally, Oh et al. (2018) reported that fucosterol protects hippocampal neurons from Aβ1–42-induced cytotoxicity by activating the TrkB-mediated ERK1/2 pathway while suppressing the ER stress marker GRP78. Gao et al. (2022) further demonstrated that fucosterol co-treatment upregulates BDNF expression in the dentate gyrus, thereby improving cognitive function. Although these studies collectively support the concept of “multi-target intervention,” the integrative mechanisms and translational potential remain unclear due to the lack of standardized experimental comparisons and systematic in vivo validation. Future studies should prioritize validating the efficacy of fucosterol monotherapy in animal models and elucidating the spatiotemporal relationships and synergistic interactions among its multiple mechanisms.

Fucosterol may exert neuroprotective effects through modulation of oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, and Aβ-related pathways, these conclusions are primarily based on in vitro and animal studies. Future research should integrate standardized digestion models, bioavailability assessment, and physiologically relevant in vivo studies to clarify its practical significance as a dietary functional component for neuroprotection.

Antitumor potential of fucosterol from a dietary and functional food perspective

Current studies indicate that fucosterol exhibits cytotoxic effects across various tumor cell models, with particular emphasis on ovarian cancer models (Khanavi et al., 2012). However, substantial discrepancies exist among studies regarding the potency of its antitumor effects and the molecular targets involved. Bae et al. (2020) reported that fucosterol inhibits tumor cell proliferation by inducing mitochondrial dysfunction and endoplasmic reticulum stress, accompanied by alterations in ROS generation and calcium homeostasis. This mechanism emphasizes organelle dysfunction as the initiating event in cell death induction. In contrast, Jiang et al. (2022) emphasized the role of signaling pathway regulation, proposing that fucosterol promotes tumor cell death by activating endoplasmic reticulum stress-related signaling molecules, such as IRE1α and PERK, thereby modulating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and MAPK pathways. Although both mechanisms involve endoplasmic reticulum stress, Bae et al. regarded it as a downstream effector, whereas Jiang et al. considered it an upstream signaling node (Fig. 5). This discrepancy may arise from differences in cell types, experimental conditions, and treatment concentrations, suggesting that the mechanism of fucosterol may be cell type-dependent. Huo and Bao (2024) observed that fucosterol exerts antitumor effects in an H22 hepatocellular carcinoma mouse model and may act by modulating immune responses and metabolic pathways. These findings complement the direct cytotoxic mechanisms proposed by Bae et al. (2020) and Jiang et al. (2022) based on in vitro studies. However, Huo and Bao (2024) did not validate mitochondrial or endoplasmic reticulum stress pathways in vivo, leaving the connection between in vitro and in vivo mechanisms unclear. Moreover, tumor microenvironments and metabolic characteristics differ substantially across models, and whether the antitumor mechanisms of fucosterol are universally applicable remains uncertain. Notably, Choi et al. (2015) suggested that fucosterol exhibits relatively low toxicity and may enhance tumor cell sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents. This finding contrasts with the “direct cytotoxicity” perspective described above, as it positions fucosterol as an adjuvant sensitizer rather than an independent cytotoxic agent. However, this conclusion is primarily based on in vitro and preliminary animal studies and lacks systematic toxicological evaluation and clinical validation. In addition, although synergistic effects with other marine bioactive (such as fucoxanthin and fucoidan) have been reported, standardized evaluation criteria and mechanistic explanations remain lacking. Consequently, the reported synergistic effects across studies are not directly comparable.

Anticancer mechanisms of fucosterol (Bae et al., 2020; Huo & Bao, 2024; Jiang et al., 2022).

Fucosterol demonstrates promising potential in antitumor research. However, studies remain predominantly based on in vitro experiments, with substantial variability in mechanistic interpretation, experimental levels, and therapeutic positioning. Future studies should employ standardized experimental systems to systematically compare mechanisms across tumor types, combined with rigorous in vivo validation and toxicological assessment, to clarify its translational potential in cancer therapy.

Challenges and novel delivery strategies of fucosterol in food application

Despite its promising health benefits, the clinical translation of fucosterol remains hindered by several critical limitations. As a highly hydrophobic molecule (LogP ≈ 7.94, data derived from the MSDS database), fucosterol exhibits extremely low aqueous solubility. This property severely restricts its dissolution and absorption in the gastrointestinal tract, thereby leading to poor oral bioavailability (Wang et al., 2022). Consequently, its direct application in functional foods and dietary supplements remains limited. Therefore, improving its stability, dispersibility, and bioaccessibility has emerged as a key research priority.

Research progress on nano-delivery systems for fucosterol

Currently, studies on nanodelivery systems for fucosterol remain scarce. Available research has primarily focused on the use of nanocarriers for the localized delivery of fucosterol. Liu et al. (2026) recently developed a tunable rigid nanolipoge system for the topical delivery of fucosterol. This system comprises a lipid bilayer encapsulating an alginate hydrogel core, and its rigidity can be precisely tuned by adjusting the crosslinking degree of the alginate. The nanolipogel system significantly improves the targeted delivery of fucosterol to hair follicles and enhances therapeutic efficacy in androgenetic alopecia model mice by promoting local angiogenesis and suppressing inflammatory responses. However, this study was limited to in vitro experiments and localized administration models. Although it provides preliminary insights for potential applications in food systems, further systematic investigation is required to validate its feasibility and translational potential.

Potential food-grade delivery systems for fucosterol

The development of food-grade delivery systems is essential for enabling the incorporation of fucosterol into dietary applications. The rational design of appropriate delivery carriers can significantly improve the dispersibility, stability, and intestinal absorption of fucosterol in food matrices. In this context, delivery strategies for fucosterol are systematically categorized based on carrier composition and structural characteristics, including lipid-based systems, polymeric carriers, inorganic or non-polymeric nanomaterials, and functionalized targeted delivery platforms (Fig. 4, Table 2). Comparative analysis of potential delivery systems for fucosterol.

Lipid-based nanodelivery systems have emerged as one of the most extensively investigated strategies for delivering hydrophobic bioactive compounds. Owing to the intrinsic compatibility between lipid matrices and hydrophobic molecules, these systems can effectively address key limitations of sterol compounds such as fucosterol in functional food applications, particularly poor solubility and low bioavailability (Ghasemi et al., 2025; Seyyedi-Mansour et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2024). Structurally related phytosterols, such as β-sitosterol and campesterol, also exhibit significant delivery challenges, primarily due to their low aqueous solubility, high crystallinity, and limited chemical stability. Lipid-based nanodelivery systems offer a promising approach to overcoming these limitations. Representative systems, including liposomes, solid lipid nanoparticles (SLNs), nanostructured lipid carriers (NLCs), and nanoemulsions, can form stable interfacial encapsulation structures within oil–water systems (Ozogul et al., 2022; Sivadasan et al., 2023). Numerous studies have demonstrated that lipid-based nanocarriers can significantly enhance the bioavailability of phytosterols. Zhang et al. (2022) reported that colloidal delivery systems, including emulsions, liposomes, and nanoparticles, markedly improve the dispersibility, stability, and bioavailability of phytosterols. Feng et al. (2022) further highlighted lipid-based nanocarriers as an effective physical modification strategy to enhance the absorption efficiency of phytosterols. Collectively, these findings indicate that lipid-based nanodelivery systems represent a viable strategy to overcome the solubility, stability, and absorption limitations of sterol compounds such as fucosterol. However, direct evidence regarding the application of these systems to fucosterol remains scarce, and systematic studies are required to validate their effectiveness and suitability in food systems.

Polymeric nanocarriers represent another important class of delivery systems, offering distinct advantages in controlled release and stability enhancement. Natural or synthetic polymers can self-assemble or undergo crosslinking to form nanostructures such as micelles, dendrimers, and polymeric nanoparticles (Reddy Baddam et al., 2024; Hwang et al., 2020; Zorab et al., 2025), thereby facilitating the efficient delivery of hydrophobic molecules (Fig. 6). Although direct applications of polymeric nanocarriers for fucosterol remain limited, the intrinsic properties of these systems are expected to improve the dispersibility of sterol compounds. In addition, sustained or controlled release can be achieved by modulating particle size and surface characteristics, which may prolong in vivo residence time and enhance functional performance. Li et al. (2025) reported that foxtail millet prolamin–pectin composite nanoparticles used to encapsulate β-sitosterol transformed it from a crystalline to an amorphous state and increased its bioavailability by approximately 68.58%. This study provides valuable insights for the design of polymeric nanodelivery systems for fucosterol and highlights their potential applications in functional food systems.

Food-grade delivery systems for fucosterol (Seyyedi-Mansour et al., 2025; Zorab et al., 2025; Godakhindi et al., 2024; Gautam et al., 2020; Niu et al., 2023).

Furthermore, non-polymeric inorganic and biomimetic nanomaterials have attracted increasing attention in recent years as potential delivery platforms for lipophilic bioactive. Mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) are characterized by a high specific surface area and tunable pore architectures, enabling enhanced loading capacity and improved physicochemical stability of sterol compounds (Godakhindi et al., 2024; Nair et al., 2024). Similarly, biomimetic nanostructures such as gold nanoshells (Her et al., 2017), virus-like particles (Nooraei et al., 2021), and exosomes (Batista et al., 2024; Sanadgol et al., 2025) have demonstrated potential in targeted delivery and favorable biocompatibility profiles. For instance, Zhang et al. (2025) reported that mesoporous silica-based systems could significantly improve the loading stability of phytosterols and maintain enzymatic activity during storage, suggesting their potential in enhancing sterol stability. However, these findings were obtained using model phytosterols rather than fucosterol, and direct evidence for its applicability to fucosterol remains lacking. In the context of biomimetic delivery, Cui et al. (2026) demonstrated that plant-derived exosome-like nanoparticles could efficiently deliver ergosterol-related compounds, leading to improved physiological outcomes in vivo. Although this study provides a promising strategy for oral delivery of sterol molecules, the extrapolation of these results to fucosterol requires careful validation due to potential differences in molecular structure, stability, and metabolic behavior. Additionally, Patel et al. (2020) showed that phytosterols bearing C-24 alkyl substitutions (e.g., β-sitosterol) can significantly enhance intracellular delivery efficiency in lipid nanoparticle (LNP) systems by improving cellular uptake and endosomal escape. Given that fucosterol possesses a structurally similar C-24 ethylidene side chain, this structural analogy suggests its potential applicability in such delivery systems. Nevertheless, this hypothesis remains speculative in the absence of direct experimental validation. Overall, these non-polymeric and biomimetic nanocarriers may provide a favorable microenvironment for fucosterol, potentially improving its stability and cellular uptake. However, current evidence is largely indirect and derived from structurally related sterols, highlighting the urgent need for systematic studies evaluating key parameters such as encapsulation efficiency, release kinetics, and in vivo bioavailability specifically for fucosterol.

Functionalized targeted delivery strategies have emerged as an important direction in the development of delivery systems for natural products. Surface modification of nanocarriers with proteins, peptides, nucleic acid aptamers, or small-molecule ligands enables targeted delivery of sterol compounds, thereby potentially enhancing the bioavailability of fucosterol while reducing non-specific distribution (Gautam et al., 2020; Niu et al., 2023; Calderon et al., 2024). Antibody or peptide functionalized nanocarriers can improve cellular recognition and facilitate receptor-mediated uptake, whereas nucleic acid ligands such as aptamers allow precise targeting through high-affinity and sequence-specific interactions (Garg, Tisdale, Haidari, & Kokkoli, 2009). These strategies provide promising approaches for the application of fucosterol in precision nutrition and functional food systems. However, current evidence is largely derived from studies on other bioactive compounds, and direct experimental validation for fucosterol remains limited. Further research is therefore required to evaluate their effectiveness, safety, and feasibility in food-grade applications.

Safety studies on fucosterol

The systematic safety evaluation of fucosterol remains relatively insufficient; in particular, comprehensive research is lacking in areas such as dose-dependent toxicity, long-term intake safety, and maximum tolerable levels in food applications. Fucosterol does not exhibit significant cytotoxicity in various human and animal cell lines. In HepG2 cells, it exhibited no apparent effect on cell viability at concentrations below 100 μM, with only a moderate decrease in cell viability observed at 200 μM (Choi et al., 2015; Hoang et al., 2012). Similarly, no significant toxicity was observed within the range of 25–50 μM in 3 T3-L1 preadipocytes and various skin or immune cells, with some models even showing enhanced cell survival (Oktaviani et al., 2019; Jung et al., 2014; Lee et al., 2017). Additionally, fucosterol typically exhibits an IC50 above 100 μM in normal cells (HEK293) (Hoang et al., 2012), while showing selective inhibitory effects on tumor cells, suggesting that it possesses a certain selective safety window. However, most of these studies are short-term exposure experiments, and there are significant variations between different experimental systems, making it difficult to directly extrapolate the results to human consumption scenarios. Existing data also indicate that fucosterol has low acute toxicity, with no toxic effects observed in ovariectomized rats even after continuous administration of 25–100 mg/kg doses for 7 weeks (Mo et al., 2018). Additionally, Choi et al. (2015) found that higher doses of fucosterol could alleviate drug-induced (e.g., tacrine) hepatotoxicity, restoring serum ALT levels to near-normal. However, current in vivo studies have primarily focused on mouse or rat models and mostly involve short-term or functional experiments, whereas systematic evaluations of chronic toxicity, genotoxicity, and reproductive toxicity remain lacking. Therefore, the existing data remain insufficient to support its long-term safe intake in functional foods. None of the current studies have established a clear maximum tolerated dose (MTD) or acceptable daily intake (ADI) for fucosterol, which constitutes a significant limitation in functional food development. Additionally, variations in purity and accompanying components among extracts from different sources may affect their toxicological profiles, but comparative studies in this regard remain scarce. Furthermore, the introduction of delivery systems may alter the toxicity profile of fucosterol, even as it improves its bioavailability. On the one hand, nanoencapsulation systems can significantly increase systemic exposure by improving solubility and intestinal absorption, potentially leading to cumulative toxicity risks under chronic intake conditions (Patra et al., 2018; Petschauer et al., 2015). On the other hand, the safety variations among different carrier materials should not be overlooked. For instance, liposomes and food-grade emulsion systems generally exhibit good biocompatibility, whereas the bioaccumulation and toxicity of inorganic nanomaterials (such as gold nanoparticles or mesoporous silica) under long-term intake conditions remain controversial (Sani et al., 2021). Therefore, the translation of nano-delivery systems into the food sector requires a balance among safety, functionality, and processability. Liposomes, nanoemulsions, and biomacromolecular composite systems are considered the most promising food-grade delivery strategies, while other high-performance nanosystems still require safety evaluations and process optimization before practical application.

Research significance and future perspectives

Fucosterol represents a crucial link between marine functional component development and food-based nutritional interventions. The significance of research on fucosterol lies not only in its intrinsic bioactivity but also in its status as a typical lipophilic functional factor, which is of great importance for studies on structure-function relationships and food delivery mechanisms.

In the context of food chemistry and structure-function relationships, the unique double-bond structure and hydrophobic characteristics of fucosterol make it an important model for elucidating the molecular structure–bioactivity relationship. Its regulation of signaling pathways such as LXR, PPARγ, and NF-κB provides a theoretical foundation for understanding the role of dietary sterols in metabolic regulation and the control of inflammation. However, current studies are predominantly based on in vitro or animal models, lacking systematic evaluation of their in vivo behavior following dietary intake. From the perspective of functional food development, the multi-target regulatory properties of fucosterol align with the concept of dietary intervention for multifactorial regulation, demonstrating potential applications in metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular diseases, and chronic inflammation. Current research predominantly focuses on validating single mechanisms, lacking systematic comparisons between different studies and analyses of dose–effect relationships. The actual efficacy of fucosterol as a functional food ingredient still requires further clarification. Despite its favorable natural origin, the key limiting factors for its food applications remain its high hydrophobicity, low water solubility, and susceptibility to oxidation. Therefore, the core focus of its future development lies in achieving synergistic optimization of stabilization, delivery, and absorption.

Future research should focus on the following specific directions: (1) Mechanistic studies under dietary conditions. By combining in vitro simulated digestion systems, gut microbiota models, and multi-omics analysis, researchers should systematically investigate the release behavior, transformation, and absorption mechanisms of fucosterol under real dietary intake conditions, avoiding reliance solely on high-dose pharmacological models to infer its functions. (2) Evaluation of nano-delivery systems for food-grade applications. Although liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles, and inorganic nanomaterials demonstrate excellent performance in drug delivery, their applicability in food systems varies significantly. Future research should systematically evaluate the feasibility of food-grade transformation from the following aspects: material safety, processing stability, cost and scalability, and dispersibility and stability in complex food matrices. Currently, liposomes, nanoemulsions, and protein-polysaccharide complex systems show greater potential for food applications, while inorganic nanomaterials and some synthetic polymer systems still face significant obstacles in terms of long-term intake safety and regulatory approval. Therefore, priority should be given to developing food-grade delivery systems based on natural biomacromolecules, rather than simply extrapolating pharmaceutical nanotechnology directly to the food sector. (3) Establishment of a quantitative evaluation system for food-grade delivery systems. The focus should be on establishing key evaluation indicators for fucosterol in different delivery systems, including encapsulation efficiency, bioaccessibility, in vitro release kinetics, and in vivo bioavailability. Standardized methods should be employed for horizontal comparison to clarify the quantitative relationship between delivery systems and functional efficacy. (4) Synergistic evaluation of safety and functional effects. While improving bioavailability, delivery systems should be systematically assessed for their impact on in vivo exposure levels and potential toxicity, particularly the cumulative effects under long-term intake conditions. Concurrently, low-dose long-term intake studies should be carried out in conjunction with functional food applications to define the balance between safety thresholds and health effects. (5) Food product development and sensory compatibility research. The use of fucosterol in various food systems such as dairy products, beverages, functional oils, and nutritional supplements should be explored, with a focus on addressing flavor masking, stability, and consumer acceptance to achieve full integration of functionality and edibility. The future development of fucosterol should not remain limited to the level of active ingredients but should transform into a functional food ingredient that is verifiable, processable, and consumable. Only by establishing systematic connections among mechanism research, delivery systems, safety evaluations, and food applications can its practical utilization be advanced.

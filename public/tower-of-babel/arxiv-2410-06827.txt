One-loop analytical expressions for γ ​ γ → ϕ i ​ ϕ j \gamma\gamma\rightarrow\phi_{i}\phi_{j} in Higgs Extensions of the Standard Models and its applications
Khiem Hong Phan, Dzung Tri Tran, Thanh Huy Nguyen

Abstract
General one-loop formulas for loop-induced processes γ ​ γ → ϕ i ​ ϕ j \gamma\gamma\rightarrow\phi_{i}\phi_{j} with ϕ i ​ ϕ j = h ​ h , h ​ H , H ​ H \phi_{i}\phi_{j}=hh,\penalty\ hH,\penalty\ HH are presented in the paper. Analytic expressions evaluated in this work are valid for a class of Higgs Extensions of the Standard Models, e.g. Inert Doublet Higgs Models, Two Higgs Doublet Models, Zee-Babu Models as well as Triplet Higgs Models, etc. Analytic expressions for one-loop form factors are written in terms of the basic scalar one-loop two-, three- and four-point functions following the output format of both the packages LoopTools and Collier . Physical results can be hence evaluated numerically by using one of the mentioned packages. Analytic results are tested by several checks such as the ultraviolet finiteness, infrared finiteness of the one-loop amplitudes. Furthermore, the amplitudes also obey the ward identity due to the on-shell initial photons. This identity is also verified numerically in this works. In the applications, we present the phenomenological results for Zee-Babu model as a typical example in this report. Production cross-section for the processes γ ​ γ → h ​ h \gamma\gamma\rightarrow hh are scanned over the parameter space of the Zee-Babu Models.

We turn out our attention to
discuss on analytic results.
In general, one-loop amplitude for
scattering processes

\gamma_{\mu}(q_{1})\,\gamma_{\nu}(q_{2})\rightarrow\phi_{i}(q_{3})\,\phi_{j}(q_{4})

is presented in terms of
Lorentz structure as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\phi_{i}\phi_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[F_{00}\;g^{\mu\nu}+\sum\limits_{i,j=1;i\leq j}^{3}F_{ij}\;q_{i}^{\nu}q_{j}^{\mu}\Big]\varepsilon_{\mu}(q_{1})\varepsilon_{\nu}(q_{2}).

(1)

In this formulas, the vector

\varepsilon_{\mu}(q)
is
polarization vector of external
photon with the
4
-dimension
momentum
q
. The scalar coefficients

F_{ij}
for
i,j=1,2,3
are called as
one-loop form factors. They are written
as functions of the following
kinematic invariant
variables:

\displaystyle\hat{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q_{1}+q_{2})^{2}=q_{1}^{2}+2q_{1}\cdot q_{2}+q_{2}^{2}=2q_{1}\cdot q_{2},

(2)

\displaystyle\hat{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q_{1}-q_{3})^{2}=q_{1}^{2}-2q_{1}\cdot q_{3}+q_{3}^{2}=M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}-2q_{1}\cdot q_{3},

(3)

\displaystyle\hat{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q_{2}-q_{3})^{2}=q_{2}^{2}-2q_{2}\cdot q_{3}+q_{3}^{2}=M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}-2q_{2}\cdot q_{3}.

(4)

The kinematic invariant
masses for external legs
are given as
q_{1}^{2}=q_{2}^{2}=0
,

q_{3}^{2}=M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}
,

q_{4}^{2}=M_{\phi_{j}}^{2}
.
These variables obey the follow
identity as

\hat{s}+\hat{t}+\hat{u}=M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}+M_{\phi_{j}}^{2}
.
Assuming two photons in
initial states being
on-shell particles,
loop-induced
amplitude must satisfy
the ward identity.
As a result, we derive
the following relations
among the form factors as:

\displaystyle F_{00}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\hat{t}-M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}}{2}\,F_{13}-\dfrac{\hat{s}}{2}\,F_{12},

(5)

\displaystyle F_{00}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\hat{u}-M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}}{2}\,F_{23}-\dfrac{\hat{s}}{2}\,F_{12},

(6)

\displaystyle F_{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\hat{u}-M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}}{\hat{s}}\,F_{33},

(7)

\displaystyle F_{23}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\hat{t}-M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}}{\hat{s}}\,F_{33}.

(8)

Using the mentioned above
relations, one-loop amplitude
is expressed
via two independent one-loop
form factors, e.g. taking

F_{12}
and
F_{33}
as an
example.
In detail, the amplitude
can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\phi_{i}\phi_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\mathcal{P}^{\mu\nu}\cdot F_{12}+\mathcal{Q}^{\mu\nu}\cdot F_{33}\Big]\,\varepsilon_{\mu}(q_{1})\varepsilon_{\nu}(q_{2}).

Where two given tensors
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{2}^{\mu}q_{1}^{\nu}-\dfrac{\hat{s}}{2}\cdot g^{\mu\nu},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{(M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}-\hat{t})(M_{\phi_{i}}^{2}-\hat{u})}{2\hat{s}}\cdot g^{\mu\nu}+q_{3}^{\mu}q_{3}^{\nu}+\dfrac{(\hat{t}-M_{\phi_{i}}^{2})}{\hat{s}}\cdot q_{2}^{\mu}q_{3}^{\nu}.

(10)

and
F_{33}

(11)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{f}g_{\phi_{i}ff}\cdot g_{\phi_{j}ff}\cdot F_{12,f}^{\text{Box}}

\displaystyle+\Big[g_{\phi_{i}WW}\cdot g_{\phi_{j}WW}\cdot F_{12,W}^{\text{Box, 1}}+g_{\phi_{i}\phi_{j}WW}\cdot F_{12,W}^{\text{Box, 2}}+g_{\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\chi\chi}\cdot F_{12,W}^{\text{Box, 3}}\Big]

4
Conclusions

A general one-loop formulas
for loop-induced processes

\gamma\gamma\rightarrow\phi_{i}\phi_{j}
with CP-even Higges

\phi_{i},\phi_{j}=h,\penalty\ H_{j}

which are valid for a class of
Higgs Extensions of the Standard Models,
e.g. Inert Doublet Higgs Models, Two
Higgs Doublet Models, Zee-Babu
models as well as Triplet Higgs Models,
etc, are presented in the paper.
Analytic expressions for one-loop
form factors are written in terms of
the basic scalar one-loop
functions. The scalar functions are output
in the packages
LoopTools
and

Collier
. Physical results are
hence evaluated numerically by using
one of the mentioned packages.
Analytic results are tested
by several checks such as
the ultraviolet finiteness,
infrared finiteness of the one-loop
amplitudes.
Furthermore, the amplitudes
also obey the so-called ward identity
due to the on-shell initial photons.
This identity is also verified
numerically in the works.
Additionally, both the packages

LoopTools
and
Collier

are used for cross-checking for
the final results before generating physical
results. In the applications, we show
phenomenological results for Zee-Babu model
as a typical example in this reference.
Production cross-section for the processes

\gamma\gamma\rightarrow hh
scanned
over the masses of singly charged Higgs,
doubly chagred Higges as well as
their couplings to
SM-like Higgs are studied.

Appendix
A
: Tensor reduction for
one-loop integrals

We apply tensor reduction
method developed in Ref.
[
100
]

for this computation. The method is described
briefly in the appendix. Following the
technique, tensor one-loop integrals rank
P

with
N
-external legs can be decomposed into
the basic scalar one-loop
functions with
N\leq 4
(they are labeled
as
A_{0}
,
B_{0}
,
C_{0}
,
D_{0}
). Definition
of tensor integrals with rank
P
(taking

N\leq 4
external legs for examples)
is

\displaystyle\{A;B;C;D\}^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{P}}=(\mu^{2})^{2-d/2}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\dfrac{k^{\mu_{1}}k^{\mu_{2}}\cdots k^{\mu_{P}}}{\{D_{1};\penalty\ D_{1}D_{2};\penalty\ D_{1}D_{2}D_{3};\penalty\ D_{1}D_{2}D_{3}D_{4}\}}.

(25)

In this formula,

D_{j}
(
j=1,\cdots,4
)
are the inverse Feynman
propagators

\displaystyle D_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+q_{j})^{2}-m_{j}^{2}+i\rho,

(26)

q_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}p_{i}
,
p_{i}

are the external momenta,

m_{j}
are internal masses in the loops.
One-loop integrals are
handled in the space-time dimension

d=4-2\varepsilon
. The parameter

\mu^{2}
plays a key role of a
renormalization scale.
Explicit reduction formulas
for one-loop one-, two-, three-
and four-point tensor integrals
up to rank
P=3

[
100
]

are presented as follows. For
one-loop one-, two-, three-point tensor integrals,
one has

\displaystyle A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(27)

\displaystyle A^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}A_{00},

(28)

\displaystyle A^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(29)

\displaystyle B^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\mu}B_{1},

(30)

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}B_{00}+q^{\mu}q^{\nu}B_{11},

(31)

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{g,q\}^{\mu\nu\rho}B_{001}+q^{\mu}q^{\nu}q^{\rho}B_{111},

Study on ice phase change prediction model for transmission lines based on multiphysics coupling
Authors: Yingbo Pei, Qingbin Wang, Liang Wang, Shuai Zuo, Hui Zhang, Lantao Jing
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe accuracy of predicting ice phase changes on transmission lines is influenced by the interaction of various meteorological factors. However, existing models, which ignore the interactions between multiple physical fields, result in insufficient prediction accuracy and other issues. To address this, a multi-physical field coupling ice phase change prediction model has been developed. First, by setting up multi-physical field coupling boundary conditions, the model simulates various meteorological conditions to create a three-dimensional geometric model of ice phase changes on transmission lines. Then, different kernel functions are selected to verify the reliability of the support vector machine (SVM) prediction algorithm. Finally, the multi-physical field coupling model is used for ice phase change prediction experiments, and its effectiveness is verified through comparison with actual measurement data and SVM prediction data. The experimental results show that the model has an average prediction accuracy of 98.12% and an average precision of 98.54%, significantly improving upon traditional methods. This model provides high-precision decision support for the early warning and protection of ice disasters on transmission lines, making it highly valuable for engineering applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-64623-4.Keywords: Ice cover on transmission lines, Multiphysics coupling, Phase change predictionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As a critical component of power systems, the safe and stable operation of transmission lines directly determines the reliability of electricity supply1. However, exposed to harsh weather conditions, these lines are prone to ice accumulation disasters that can trigger circuit breaker trips, damage hardware components2, and even lead to severe safety incidents such as line breaks or tower collapses. To achieve effective ice prediction for transmission lines, extensive training and simulation using real-world ice data is required3. Yet, transmission line icing exhibits irregular and uneven characteristics, with its formation process being dynamically influenced by multiple factors including environmental temperature, humidity, and wind speed4. Current research on transmission line icing prediction primarily focuses on three main categories:

Type1: Research on Ice Accretion Probability Prediction Based on Meteorological Factor Theoretical Data Models and Empirical Formulas. Through learning from historical data and simulation, a mathematical model for ice accretion prediction was established. In literature, a “four-parameter” conductor ice accretion model was developed based on fluid mechanics principles, considering the motion, collision, and freezing processes of airborne water droplets5. By analyzing actual monitoring data, empirical formulas for median liquid water volume and droplet diameter were derived, establishing an environment-dependent conductor ice accretion model6. Although this method analyzed the correlation between ice parameters and transmission line icing and established a four-parameter conductor ice accretion model, its accuracy rate only reached 6.6%, while avoiding the influence of fluid mechanics and thermodynamics in ice models7. Literature6 employed an Least Squares Support Vector Machine (LSVM) optimized through IAOA to predict phase transitions in transmission line icing8. While demonstrating some predictive accuracy, the algorithm neglected the inherent convective heat transfer characteristics of power lines, focusing solely on environmental temperature, wind speed, and pressure effects9. Consequently, the predicted ice phase transition results lacked environmental authenticity10.

Type2: Research on Ice Accretion Prediction for Transmission Lines Based on Image Recognition Technology. This technology primarily relies on a monitoring network constructed with drones, GPRS, and remote cameras. The collected images are transmitted to the backend system, where methods such as adaptive threshold conversion, noise reduction filtering, and pixel reshaping are employed for image recognition11. By calculating the mapping relationship between actual distance and pixel distance, it achieves phase transition prediction of ice accretion on transmission lines. Literature proposed an optimized wire ice phase transition calculation method based on the Ganny operator and enhanced findContours function12. This approach extracts ice-affected wire contours, derives an ice phase transition calculation formula, and incorporates adaptive algorithms with median filtering by comparing pixel area values before and after icing13. The algorithm ensures effective noise reduction while enhancing adaptability14. However, this method is only applicable to identifying and calculating ice phase transitions in ice-affected line images, lacking capability for predicting ice phase transitions on transmission lines and failing to integrate meteorological factors15.

Type3: Research on Ice Accretion Prediction Technology for Transmission Lines Based on Mechanical Models16. This method involves installing tension sensors at insulator string positions along transmission lines to monitor changes in line self-weight and ice load, utilizing a weighing formula to predict the phase transition process of ice accumulation. Literature optimized the mechanical model for dynamic tension and inclination overhead transmission lines, using conductor dynamic tension and tilt angle as inputs while predicting ice phase transitions as outputs, providing a novel approach for line ice detection17. However, this method failed to account for sag effects and uneven ice distribution during line icing, resulting in certain prediction errors in ice phase transition forecasting18.

At present, the three mainstream methods in ice cover prediction have the following common deficiencies: (1) insufficient consideration of environmental parameters; (2) lack of consideration of dynamic process of ice cover and future change; (3) insufficient consideration of field coupling of phase transition process19.

In summary, existing ice accumulation prediction methods for transmission lines based on single algorithms or technologies suffer from issues such as partial results, insufficient accuracy, and poor prediction effectiveness of phase transition processes20. Urgent improvements are needed in the following aspects: (1) Empirical formulas based on historical data require establishing valid datasets through anomaly detection and validity verification to reduce distortion in ice phase transition predictions caused by data noise. (2) When recognizing transmission line ice images, meteorological factors during image capture should be matched, considering both actual shooting distance and pixel distance impacts while integrating meteorological data to predict potential future ice conditions. (3) Weight variations obtained solely from tension sensors cannot accurately predict ice phase transitions at different locations21.

To address these improvement directions, this paper proposes a multi-physics phase transition ice prediction model for transmission lines. By selecting and optimizing support vector machine kernel functions to analyze correlations between transmission line ice accumulation and environmental temperature, humidity, and wind speed, the model establishes multi-physics coupled boundary conditions based on varying correlation degrees22. The proposed method demonstrates high accuracy and robust adaptability when comparing predicted results with actual outcomes. And the model achieves an average accuracy of 98.54% and an average precision of 0.934.Experimental results confirm the effectiveness of this predictive approach23.

Establishment of phase change model for transmission line icing

During the icing process of transmission lines, conductors capture supercooled water droplets from the air. When temperature and wind speed meet the freezing conditions, these captured supercooled droplets condense on the transmission lines, forming various types of ice deposits24. The type of ice deposition largely depends on the diameter of the water droplets captured by the conductor after collision, with the conductor’s geometry replacing the cross-sectional profile of steel-cored aluminum stranded wire25. As shown in Fig. 1:

Schematic diagram of the formation relationship between wind direction and ice cover.

At temperatures around 0℃, supercooled water droplets in the air continuously accumulate. As temperatures decrease, these droplets gradually coalesce into small ice crystals, which then form ice crystals that adhere to the conductor’s surface. These ice crystals exhibit strong adhesion and tend to form encapsulating layers that completely envelop the conductor. When the wind direction is perpendicular to the conductor, the exposed side faces the wind (windward side), while the opposite side remains sheltered (leeward side). Since the windward side first contacts the airflow, ice typically forms there. With continuous ice accumulation, the conductor’s surface eventually develops an ice layer that appears elliptical or nearly circular in shape .

Establishment of phase change ice cover model

Phase transition refers to the phenomenon where a substance changes from one physical state to another under specific conditions such as temperature or pressure variations. Common phase transitions include melting, solidification, evaporation, and condensation. During these processes, the chemical properties of substances generally remain unchanged while their physical characteristics undergo significant transformations. In the context of ice accumulation on power transmission lines, the crystallization process of condensation water in the air onto conductors represents a solidification phase transition. The corresponding phase transition model must account for heat transfer effects in both solid and fluid domains, established through multi-physics field coupling. The quality of mesh partitioning directly impacts the accuracy and computational efficiency of laminar flow field calculations. Unlike complex, repetitive global meshing approaches, this study employs a simplified boundary layer meshing method. The meshing area is specifically designated for the stranded conductor layer boundaries of steel-aluminum core conductors. Free triangular grids are applied to both conductor edges and the stranded conductor entities, with the boundary layer density in the stranded layer being20 higher than that in the conductor layer. The total number of points reaches 2,056,254, comprising 1,942,561 triangular mesh elements.

Laminar flow field calculation

Laminar flow field calculations are primarily based on fluid mechanics principles. Under low Reynolds number conditions, fluids flow along streamline paths without mixing between them, enabling the calculation of velocity and pressure distributions under specific conditions. The laminar flow field mainly manifests through its influence on fluid dynamics during ice formation. When water vapor condenses or rainwater freezes on surfaces, it leads to ice deposition. During the icing process, the laminar flow field provides critical parameters such as temperature, humidity, air pressure, and velocity in the fluid—key factors affecting droplet cooling, freezing, and condensation. Under low Reynolds numbers, fluid stability ensures more orderly droplet cooling processes. Conclusions

This study utilized 100 datasets of transmission line icing conditions (including temperature, humidity, and wind speed) divided into training and test sets. For the training set, we selected the Gaussian radial kernel function with higher accuracy among three kernel functions as the objective function for building a support vector machine (SVM) model. By integrating flow field simulations, temperature field analysis, solid heat transfer modeling, and airflow field studies under multiphysics coupling conditions, we established a COMSOL transmission line phase change simulation model. The model was applied to predict ice-phase transition scenarios on transmission lines, with validation experiments conducted using test set data showing strong consistency with actual icing phase change observations. The results demonstrate that the proposed SVM-based multiphysics phase change prediction model achieves high precision in forecasting ice-phase transitions under specified meteorological conditions, proving effective for predicting icing phase changes in transmission lines. Key conclusions are summarized as follows:

(1) In the support vector machine ice cover phase change prediction model, polynomial kernel function, Gaussian radial kernel function and Sigmoid kernel function show 90.15%,92.27% and 91.21% accuracy respectively in ice cover phase change prediction. This paper empirically demonstrates that the Gaussian radial kernel function is more suitable for predicting ice accumulation on transmission lines considering multiple meteorological factors, and exhibits superior adaptability in model construction.

(2) A multi-physics coupled model was established using a 4D×3D computational domain with triangular mesh elements. This model effectively simulated the evolution of ice-phase transitions on both windward and leeward surfaces, achieving a prediction accuracy of 98.12% in experimental validation.

(3) The support vector machine (SVM)-based ice-phase transition prediction model and the multi-physics coupled phase transition ice-phase transition prediction model were tested on the test set. The average accuracy of the ice formation prediction model for transmission lines based on support vector machines across multiple physical fields is approximately 98.54%, demonstrating its validity. Comparison of MAcc values among four models shows that the proposed prediction model exhibits high accuracy.

Throughout the paper, we consider a connected noncompact metric graph

Quantum graphs (metric graphs equipped with differential operators) arise naturally as simplified models in mathematics, physics, chemistry, and engineering when one considers propagation of waves of various nature through a quasi-one-dimensional (e.g., meso- or nano-scale) system that looks like a thin neighborhood of a graph. Now, we recall some basic settings of Dirac equations on metric graphs, as introduced in
[
10
,
11
]
.
Consistently, a function
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{C}
is actually a family of functions
(u_{e})
, where
u_{e}:I_{e}\to\mathbb{C}
is the restriction of
u
to the edge
e
. The usual
L^{p}
spaces on the metric graph are defined as follows
as

L^{p}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathrm{E}}L^{p}(I_{e})

with norms

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G})}^{p}:=\sum_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{L^{p}(I_{e})}^{p},\quad\text{ if }p\in[1,\infty),\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G})}:=\max_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(I_{e})},

while the Sobolev spapces
H^{m}(\mathcal{G})
are defined as by
by

H^{m}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathrm{E}}H^{m}(I_{e})

with norm

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{m}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\lVert u^{(i)}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}.

Accordingly, a spinor
u=(u^{1},u^{2})^{T}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a family of
2
-spinors

u_{e}=\binom{u_{e}^{1}}{u_{e}^{2}}:I_{e}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2},\quad\forall e\in\mathrm{E},

and thus

L^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2}):=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathrm{E}}L^{p}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2}),

endowed with the norms

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{p}:=\sum_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{L^{p}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{p},\quad\text{ if }p\in[1,\infty),\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}:=\max_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})},

whereas

H^{m}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2}):=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathrm{E}}H^{m}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})

with the norms

\|u\|_{H^{m}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{2}:=\sum_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{H^{m}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{2}.

For convenience, we often abbreviate
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}
as

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we establish a Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality for metric graphs, introduce the perturbation functional, and provide some fundamental results that will be essential for proving our main theorems.

2.1.
Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality

The following lemma is an immediate corollary of
Y\subset H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})
and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
, there exist constants
C_{p,\mathcal{K}}
and
C_{p,\mathcal{G}}
that depend only on
c
,
m
,
p
and
\mathcal{G}
such that

\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,dx\leq C_{p,\mathcal{K}}\lVert u\rVert^{p-2}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2},\quad\forall u\in Y,

and

\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,dx\leq C_{p,\mathcal{G}}\lVert u\rVert^{p-2}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2},\quad\forall u\in Y.

2.2.
The Perturbation Functional

For the remainder of the paper, we set
c=1
, as its value does not affect the results.

We define the energy functional
I_{\omega}:Y\to\mathbb{R}
associated with equation (
1.9
) by

I_{\omega}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{+}\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{-}\rVert^{2}-\frac{\omega}{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx-\Psi(u)

for
u=(u^{+}+u^{-})\in Y
, where

\Psi(u):=\frac{a}{p}\int_{\mathcal{K}}|u|^{p}\,dx.

It follows from standard arguments that
I_{\omega}\in C^{2}(Y,\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, by
[
10
, Proposition 3.1]
, if
u\in Y
is a critical point of
I_{\omega}
, then
u
is a solution of problem (
1.9
), and belonging to
\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D})\subset H^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Thus,
u_{e}\in C^{1}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})
for all
e\in\mathrm{E}
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Remark 6 in Chapter 8]
).

Define
J:Y^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

J(v)=I_{0}(v+h(v))=\max\{I_{0}(v+w):w\in Y^{-}\},

where
h\in C^{1}\left(Y^{+},Y^{-}\right)
and the critical points of
J
and
I_{0}
are in one-to-one correspondence via the injective map
u\rightarrow u+h(u)
from
Y^{+}
into
Y
, as shown in
[
19
,
18
,
1
]
.

For any
\mu>0
, we consider operator
T_{\mu}:Y\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})
defined by

T_{\mu}u:=(I+\mu\lvert\mathcal{D}\rvert)u=u+\mu\mathcal{D}(u^{+}-u^{-}).

Next, we define the following perturbation functional

I_{r,\mu}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{+}\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{-}\rVert^{2}-\Psi(u)-H_{r,\mu}(u),\;u\in U_{\mu},

where
U_{\mu}:=\{u\in Y:(T_{\mu}u,u)_{2}<1\}
, and
H_{r,\mu}(u)
is a penalization term defined by

H_{r,\mu}(u):=f_{r}((T_{\mu}u,u)_{2})\text{ with }f_{r}(s):=\frac{s^{r}}{1-s}\;(0\leq s<1),

and
r>1
is a parameter that will be chosen large enough.

The following are some properties of the penalization
H_{r,\mu}(u)
for any
u\in U_{\mu}
and
v,v_{1},v_{2}\in Y
,

\langle H_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=2f_{r}^{\prime}((T_{\mu}u,u)_{2})(T_{\mu}u,v)_{2}

with

f_{r}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{rs^{r-1}}{1-s}+\frac{s^{r}}{(1-s)^{2}}>\frac{r}{s}f_{r}(s)>0\;(0<s<1),

and

\langle H_{r,\mu}^{\prime\prime}(u)v_{1},v_{2}\rangle=2f_{r}^{\prime}((T_{\mu}u,u)_{2})(T_{\mu}v_{1},v_{2})_{2}+4f_{r}^{\prime\prime}((T_{\mu}u,u)_{2})(T_{\mu}u,v_{1})_{2}(T_{\mu}u,v_{2})_{2}

with

f_{r}^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{r(r-1)s^{r-2}}{1-s}+\frac{2rs^{r-1}}{(1-s)^{2}}+\frac{2s^{r}}{(1-s)^{3}}>0\;(0<s<1).

In particular,
H_{r,\mu}(u)
is convex in
u
and

\langle H_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle\geq 2rH_{r,\mu}(u),\,\,\text{for all}\,\,u\in U_{\mu}.

Following the arguments in
[
19
, Section 2.3]
, we can apply a variational reduction of
I_{r,\mu}
, which leads to a
C^{1}
reduction map
h_{r,\mu}:Y^{+}\cap U_{\mu}\rightarrow Y
and a reduced functional
J_{r,\mu}:Y^{+}\cap U_{\mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

J_{r,\mu}(v):=I_{r,\mu}(v+h_{r,\mu}(v))=\max\{I_{r,\mu}(v+w):w\in Y^{-},(T_{\mu}(v+w),v+w)_{2}<1\}.

The following Lemma shows that the functional
J_{r,\mu}
possesses the mountain pass geometry.

Lemma 2.2
.

The functional
J_{r,\mu}
possesses the following properties:

(i)

There exist
\alpha,\rho>0
such that
J_{r,\mu}(v)\geq\alpha
for
v\in Y^{+}\cap U_{\mu}
with
\|v\|=\rho
;

(ii)

There exists
e\in Y^{+}\cap U_{\mu}
with
\|e\|>\rho
such that
J_{r,\mu}(e)<0
.

Proof.

(i) For any
v\in Y^{+}\cap U_{\mu}
with
\|v\|=\rho
, where
\rho
is small enough, we have

(T_{\mu}v,v)_{2}\leq\frac{1+m\mu}{a}\|v\|^{2}=\frac{1+m\mu}{a}\rho^{2}<1.

Thus, by the definition of
J_{r,\mu}
, the monotonicity of
f_{r}(s)
with respect to
s
, and the Sobolev inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{r,\mu}(v)\geq I_{r,\mu}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}-\frac{a}{p}\int_{\mathcal{K}}|v|^{p}\,dx-f_{r}((T_{\mu}v,v)_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}-\frac{a}{p}\int_{\mathcal{K}}|v|^{p}\,dx-f_{r}\left(\frac{1+m\mu}{a}\|v\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\rho^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-C\rho^{p-2}-\frac{(\frac{1+m\mu}{a})^{r}\rho^{2r-2}}{1-\frac{1+m\mu}{a}\rho^{2}}\right).

Since
p>2
and
r>1
, we can choose
\rho>0
small enough such that

J_{r,\mu}(v)\geq\alpha\,\,\text{for }\,\,v\in Y^{+}\cap U_{\mu}\text{ with }\|v\|=\rho.

(ii) Choosing
v_{0}\in Y^{+}
with
(T_{\mu}v_{0},v_{0})_{2}=1
, it is easy to verify that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}J_{r,\mu}(tv_{0})=-\infty.

Therefore, there exists
t_{0}<1
such that
\lVert t_{0}v_{0}\rVert>\rho
and
J_{r,\mu}(t_{0}v_{0})<0
, then (ii) holds.
∎

As a consequence, we define the minimax value

c_{r,\mu}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{r,\mu}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J_{r,\mu}(\gamma(t))>0,

where

\Gamma_{r,\mu}:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],\,Y^{+}\cap U_{\mu}):\gamma(0)=0,\,J_{r,\mu}(\gamma(1))<0\}.

Note that the functional
J_{r,\mu}
is defined on the bounded set

\{v\in Y^{+}:(T_{\mu}v,v)_{2}<1\}\subset\{v\in Y^{+}:\|v\|<\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}.

Similarly,
I_{r,\mu}
is also defined on a bounded domain
U_{\mu}
. If
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
and
\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}
, we have
c_{r_{1},\mu_{2}}\leq c_{r_{2},\mu_{1}}
because
J_{r_{1},\mu_{2}}\leq J_{r_{2},\mu_{1}}
on
U_{\mu_{2}}
.

Define
c_{\infty}:=\sup_{r>1,\mu>0}c_{r,\mu}
. For all
v\in Y^{+}\backslash\{0\}
, we have

(2.1)

c_{\infty}\leq\sup_{0\leq t<|v|_{2}^{-1}}J(tv).

We now obtain an important upper bound estimates for
c_{\infty}
. From now on, for simplicity, we will denote by
C
or
C_{i}
(where
i=1,2,...)
generic positive constants, whose the exact value may vary from line to line or even within the same line.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
2<p<4
. Then
c_{\infty}<\frac{m}{2}
.

Proof.

Let the half-lines of the graph
\mathcal{G}
be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2},...,\mathcal{H}_{N}
, and let the total length of the graph
\mathcal{K}
be
\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{K}}\ell_{e}
. Let
b>0
. Define
\varphi_{b}^{1}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{C}
as

\varphi_{b}^{1}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }x\in\mathcal{K}\\
\max\{0,1-bx\}&\text{ for }x\in\mathcal{H}_{i},\quad i=1,2,...,N,\end{cases}

and define
\varphi_{b}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
by

\varphi_{b}=\binom{\varphi_{b}^{1}}{0}.

We first estimate
J(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}})
.
It is clear that
\varphi_{b}\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D})
,
(\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b}=\left(0,-\imath\left(\varphi_{b}^{1}\right)^{\prime}\right)^{T}
and
((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b},\varphi_{b})_{2}=0
. Thus, we obtain

\lVert\left(\varphi_{b}^{1}\right)^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}\lVert\varphi_{b}^{-}\rVert_{2}\geq\lvert((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b},\varphi_{b}^{-})_{2}\rvert=\lvert((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b}^{-},\varphi_{b}^{-})_{2}\rvert=\lVert\varphi_{b}^{-}\rVert^{2}+m\lVert\varphi_{b}^{-}\rVert^{2}_{2}\geq 2m\lVert\varphi_{b}^{-}\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Hence,

(2.2)

\lVert\varphi_{b}^{-}\rVert^{2}+m\lVert\varphi_{b}^{-}\rVert^{2}_{2}\leq\lvert((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b},\varphi_{b}^{-})_{2}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2m}\lVert\left(\varphi_{b}^{1}\right)^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}=\frac{Nb}{2m}.

Since
((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b},\varphi_{b}^{+})_{2}+((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b},\varphi_{b}^{-})_{2}=((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b},\varphi_{b})_{2}=0
, we obtain

(2.3)

\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}-m\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}_{2}=((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b}^{+},\varphi_{b}^{+})_{2}=((\mathcal{D}-mI)\varphi_{b},\varphi_{b}^{+})_{2}\leq\frac{Nb}{2m}.

Since
\lVert\varphi_{b}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\frac{N}{3b}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert
, (
2.2
) implies

(2.4)

\frac{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}_{2}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}\rVert^{2}_{2}}\to 1,\quad\frac{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}\rVert^{2}}\to 1\text{ and }\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rvert^{p}\to\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert\varphi_{b}\rvert^{p}=\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert\text{ as }b\to 0.

Next, for
t>0
, from the definition of
h
, we have

I_{0}(t\varphi_{b}+h(t\varphi_{b}))\geq I_{0}(t\varphi_{b}),

which implies

(2.5)

\frac{p}{2}\lVert h(t\varphi_{b})\rVert^{2}\leq a\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert t\varphi_{b}+h(t\varphi_{b})\rvert^{p}\,dx+\frac{p}{2}\lVert h(t\varphi_{b})\rVert^{2}\leq a\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert t\varphi_{b}\rvert^{p}\,dx=at^{p}\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert.

Thus, for sufficiently small
b>0
, using (
2.3
)-(
2.5
),
p\in(2,4)
, Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality in Lemma
2.1
and convexity of
\Psi
, we obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle J(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}})=

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}}{2\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert h\left(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right\rVert^{2}-\Psi\left(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}}+h\left(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}+\frac{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}-m\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}_{2}}{2\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert h\left(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle-2^{1-p}\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}^{-p}\Psi(\varphi_{b}^{+})+\Psi\left(h\left(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}+C_{1}b^{2}-C_{2}ab^{\frac{p}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert h\left(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\left(1-\frac{aC_{p,\mathcal{K}}}{pm}\left\lVert h\left(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right\rVert^{p-2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}+C_{1}b^{2}-C_{2}ab^{\frac{p}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert h\left(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right\rVert^{2}(1-C_{3}a^{\frac{p}{2}}b^{\frac{p(p-2)}{4}})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}.

Define
g(t):=J(t\varphi_{b}^{+})
. Then, from (
2.5
) and the definition of
J
, for
0\leq t\leq\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}^{-1}
and sufficiently small

, we have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\langle J^{\prime}(t\varphi_{b}^{+}),t\varphi_{b}^{+}\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\langle I_{0}^{\prime}(t\varphi_{b}^{+}+h(t\varphi_{b}^{+})),t\varphi_{b}^{+}+h^{\prime}(t\varphi_{b}^{+})t\varphi_{b}^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\langle I_{0}^{\prime}(t\varphi_{b}^{+}+h(t\varphi_{b}^{+})),t\varphi_{b}^{+}+h(t\varphi_{b}^{+})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\left[\lVert t\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}-\lVert h(t\varphi_{b}^{+})\rVert^{2}-a\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert t\varphi_{b}^{+}+h(t\varphi_{b}^{+})\rvert^{p}\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{t}\left[\lVert t\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert^{2}-a\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert t\varphi_{b}^{+}\rvert^{p}\,dx+\frac{p-2}{2}\lVert h(t\varphi_{b}^{+})\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq t(C_{1}b^{-1}-C_{2}ab^{\frac{p-2}{2}}),

which implies

is increasing for

when

is sufficiently small. Now, fix

sufficiently small. Then, by (

) and (

), we have

c_{\infty}\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}^{-1}}J(t\varphi_{b}^{+})\leq J(\frac{\varphi_{b}^{+}}{\lVert\varphi_{b}^{+}\rVert_{2}})<\frac{m}{2}.

This completes the proof of lemma.
∎

The mountain pass geometry allows us to find a Palais-Smale sequence
\{v_{n}\}
satisfying

J_{r,\mu}(v_{n})\rightarrow c_{r,\mu},\quad J_{r,\mu}^{\prime}(v_{n})\rightarrow 0

which we call a mountain pass critical sequence. Define
u_{n}:=v_{n}+h_{r,\mu}(v_{n})
,
\omega_{n}=2f_{r}^{\prime}((T_{\mu}u_{n},u_{n})_{2})
. We have the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
c_{\infty}<\frac{m}{2}
, then, for sufficiently large
r
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\omega_{n}\leq 2c_{\infty}.

Proof.

First, observe that
f_{r}^{\prime}(s)s-f_{r}(s)
and
f_{r}^{\prime}(s)
are strictly increasing with respect to
s\in(0,1)
. Since
0<c_{\infty}<\frac{m}{2}
, by continuity of
s\mapsto f_{r}^{\prime}(s)s-f_{r}(s)
and
s\mapsto f_{r}^{\prime}(s)
, there exist

Lemma 8.5

Lemma 8.6
.

Assume that
(A)
holds. Then
\widetilde{c_{\infty}}<\frac{m}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
defined as in Lemma
8.5
. Since
\widetilde{h}\in C^{1}(Y^{-},Y^{+})
and
\widetilde{h}(0)=0
, we have
\widetilde{h}(t\varphi)\to 0
as
t\to 0
. Thus, for
t
sufficiently small, the Sobolev inequality and convexity of
\Psi
implies

(8.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}(t\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert t\varphi\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert\widetilde{h}(t\varphi)\rVert^{2}-\Psi(t\varphi+\widetilde{h}(t\varphi))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert t\varphi\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert\widetilde{h}(t\varphi)\rVert^{2}-2^{1-p}\Psi(t\varphi)+\Psi(-\widetilde{h}(t\varphi))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m}{2}\lVert t\varphi\rVert_{2}^{2}-2^{1-p}\Psi(t\varphi)-\frac{1}{2}\lVert\widetilde{h}(t\varphi)\rVert^{2}+C\lVert\widetilde{h}(t\varphi)\rVert^{p}

\displaystyle<\frac{m}{2}\lVert t\varphi\rVert_{2}^{2}.

For
s>0
, define
\widetilde{g}(s):=\widetilde{J}(st\varphi)
, then, from the definition of
\widetilde{J}
, we know

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\langle\widetilde{J}^{\prime}(st\varphi),st\varphi\rangle=\frac{1}{s}\langle\widetilde{I_{0}}^{\prime}(st\varphi+\widetilde{h}(st\varphi)),s\varphi+\widetilde{h}^{\prime}(st\varphi)st\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\langle\widetilde{I_{0}}^{\prime}(st\varphi+\widetilde{h}(st\varphi)),st\varphi+\widetilde{h}(st\varphi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\left[\lVert st\varphi\rVert^{2}-\lVert\widetilde{h}(st\varphi)\rVert^{2}-a\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert st\varphi+\widetilde{h}(st\varphi)\rvert^{p}\,dx\right].

Remark that, from the definition of
\widetilde{h}
, we have

\widetilde{I_{0}}(st\varphi+\widetilde{h}(st\varphi))\geq\widetilde{I_{0}}(st\varphi),

which implies that

a\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert st\varphi+\widetilde{h}(st\varphi)\rvert^{p}\,dx\leq a\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert st\varphi\rvert^{p}\,dx-\frac{p}{2}\lVert\widetilde{h}(st\varphi)\rVert^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{s}\left[\lVert st\varphi\rVert^{2}-a\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert st\varphi\rvert^{p}\,dx+\frac{p-2}{2}\lVert\widetilde{h}(st\varphi)\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq s\lVert t\varphi\rVert^{2}-as^{p-1}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert t\varphi\rvert^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq s\lVert t\varphi\rVert^{2}-Cas^{p-1}\lVert t\varphi\rVert^{p}

which implies that
\widetilde{g}
is increasing for
0\leq s\leq 1
and
t
small enough. Fix
t
sufficiently small. Then, for
1\leq s\leq\lVert t\varphi\rVert_{2}^{-1}
, we obtain that
\Psi(st\varphi+\widetilde{h}(st\varphi))\geq s^{2}\Psi(t\varphi+s^{-1}\widetilde{h}(st\varphi))
and then by (
8.2
)

\widetilde{J}(st\varphi)=\widetilde{I_{0}}(st\varphi+\widetilde{h}(st\varphi))\leq s^{2}\widetilde{I_{0}}(t\varphi+s^{-1}\widetilde{h}(st\varphi))\leq s^{2}\widetilde{J}(t\varphi)<\frac{ms^{2}}{2}\lVert st\varphi\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{m}{2}.

Hence, by the above estimates, as in (

),

\widetilde{c_{\infty}}\leq\sup_{0\leq s\leq\lVert t\varphi\rVert_{2}^{-1}}\widetilde{J}(st\varphi)<\frac{m}{2}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
8.6
.
∎

\displaystyle\frac{\min\{c^{2},m^{2}c^{4}\}}{2}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a^{2}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{K},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{p-2}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{{0}}\rvert^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{apmc^{2}}{(p-2)}S_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{p-2}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{apmc^{2}}{(p-2)}S_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{p-2}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

which implies

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}}^{3-\frac{p}{2}}\leq\max\{m,\frac{1}{mc^{2}}\}\frac{2ap}{p-2}S_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{p-2}.

It follows from (
A.3
) and Lemma (
A.1
) that

\displaystyle m^{2}c^{4}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a^{2}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{{0}}\rvert^{2p-2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq a^{2}S_{2p-2,\mathcal{K}}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}}^{p-2}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq a^{\frac{8}{6-p}}\left(\frac{2p}{p-2}\max\{m,\frac{1}{mc^{2}}\}\right)^{\frac{2p-4}{6-p}}S_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{\frac{2(p-2)^{2}}{6-p}}S_{2p-2,\mathcal{K}}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{p},

hence

aS_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{\frac{(p-2)^{2}}{4}}S_{2p-2,\mathcal{K}}^{\frac{6-p}{8}}\left(\frac{2p}{p-2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\max\{m^{\frac{p-4}{2}}c^{\frac{p-6}{2}},(mc^{2})^{-1}\}>1,

which is a contradiction to (
A.2
) and this completes the proof.
∎

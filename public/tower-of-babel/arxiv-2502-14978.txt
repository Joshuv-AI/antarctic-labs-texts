Generalized Oxtoby subshifts and hyperfiniteness
Konrad Deka, Bo Peng

Abstract
. We show that there exists a class of symbolic subshifts which realizes all Choquet simplices as simplices of invariant measures and the conjugacy relation on that class is hyperfinite.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the relationship between the complexity of conjugacy relation of symbolic subshifts and the complexity of homeomorphism of simplices of their invariant measures.

A theorem of Downarowicz
[
2
]
states that for every compact metric Choquet simplex
C
,
there exists a Toeplitz subshift
whose simplex of invariant measures is
affinely homeomorphic to
C
.
A theorem of Sabok
[
9
]
states that the relation of affine homeomorphism between
compact metric Choquet simplices is a complete orbit equivalence relation.
Naturally, if two subshifts
X,Y
are isomorphic,
then the simplices of invariant measures
\mathcal{M}_{S}(X)
,

\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)
are affinely homeomorphic.
One could hope that in some restricted class of subshifts, a converse statement holds,
which could lead to a nontrivial lower bound on the complexity of the conjugacy relation of Toeplitz subshifts.

Gao, Jackson, Seward
[
4
, Theorem 9.3.3]
and Thomas
[
10
]
proved that the conjugacy relation for minimal symbolic subshifts is not smooth but it is not known whether it is hyperfinite or not. The exact complexity of the conjugacy relation on symbolic Toeplitz subshifts is a well-known open problem
[
8
, Question 1.3]
, however, the complexity of conjugacy relation restricted on some subclasses has been computed. Kaya
[
6
]
showed that the conjugacy of Toeplitz systems with growing blocks is hyperfinite which generalizes a result of Sabok and Tsankov
[
8
]
on Toeplitz systems with separate holes. Those two classes consist of regular Topelitz systems which implies all such systems are uniquely ergodic.

It turns out the subshifts constructed by Downarowicz belong to the class of generalized Oxtoby subshifts.
The main result of this paper is that the conjugacy relation of that class is hyperfinite.

Theorem 1.1
.

The conjugacy relation of generalized Oxtoby systems is hyperfinite.

As a consequence, there exists a class of Toeplitz subshifts, such that on one hand every Choquet simplex can be realized as the set of invariant measures on one of those subshifts, and on the other hand, the conjugacy relation on that class is hyperfinite.

We give two proofs of Theorem
1.1
. The structure of the paper is the following. Section
2
contains preliminaries. Section
3
contains basic definition in symbolic subshifts. In Section
4
, we review the result of Downarowicz on realizing Choquet simplices. Section
5
contains analysis of symbolic and Toeplitz subshifts. Section
6
contains the first proof of Theorem
1.1
. Section
7
contains analysis of the Oxtoby property for subshifts and Section
8
contains the second proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

A
topological dynamical system
, tds for short, is a pair
(X,T)
where
X
is a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
is a continuous map.
For
x\in X
, its
orbit
is
\mathcal{O}(x):=\{T^{n}x\colon n\geq 0\}
.
A tds
(X,T)
is
minimal
if it has no nontrivial subsystem.
Equivalently, it is minimal if and only if
\overline{\mathcal{O}(x)}=X
for all
x\in X
.

Let
(X,T)
be a tds, and let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
X
.
We say that
\mu
is
T
-invariant
if
\mu(A)=\mu(T^{-1}A)

for all Borel
A\subseteq X
.
We will write
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
for the set of all such measures on
X
.
This set is guaranteed to be nonempty.
Let
C(X)
be the Banach space of all continuous functions
X\to\mathbb{R}
,
and consider the dual
C(X)^{*}
with the weak* topology.
Then
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
can be viewed as a compact convex subset of
C(X)^{*}
.

A
Choquet simplex

X
is a compact convex, metrizable space space such that all
x\in X
can be uniquely represented by a measure supported on its extreme points.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite set. Then
\mathcal{A}^{*}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{A}^{n}
is the family of all
finite sequences with entries from
\mathcal{A}
, including the empty sequence of length zero.
If
\mathcal{A}
is finite, then
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is compact,
and we use
S\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
to denote the shift map.
A
subshift
is a closed set
X\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
SX=X
.
Sometimes, we also call the topological dynamical system
(X,S|_{X})
a subshift.
We also define the
language of a subshift
X
to be

\textrm{Lang}(X):=\left\{x[i,j)\colon x\in X\textrm{ and }i\leq j\right\}.

A
Polish space
is a separable completely metrizable topological space. An
equivalence relation

E
on a Polish space is
Borel
if it is a Borel subset of
X^{2}
and is
countable
if every equivalence class is countable. A countable Borel equivalence relation
E
is
hyperfinite
if
E
can be written as an increasing union of finite Borel equivalence relations.

Let
X
and
Y
be two Polish spaces and
E,F
be two equivalence relations defined on
X
and
Y
, respectively. A
Borel reduction
is a Borel map from
X
to
Y
such that

x_{1}Ex_{2}\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,f(x_{1})Ff(x_{2}).

An equivalence relation on a Polish space is
smooth
if it is Borel reducible to
=
on
\mathbb{R}
.

3.
Toeplitz subshifts and generalized Oxtoby subshifts

Toeplitz subshifts and Toeplitz sequences are a standard method of constructing
minimal subshifts, see for example
[
3
]
for the survey of the topic.
We will follow closely the notation from
[
6
]
.
An element
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
Toeplitz sequence

if for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists
n\geq 1
such that

x(i)=x(i+kn)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A
Toeplitz subshift
is a subshift that is of the form

\overline{\mathcal{O}(x)}
for some Toeplitz sequence
x
.
Every Toeplitz subshift is minimal.
We introduce the following notions:

•

For
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and an integer
p>1
, define the
p
-periodic part of
x
,

\textrm{Per}_{p}(x):=\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\colon x(i)=x(i+kp)\textrm{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Clearly, if
r
divides
p
, then
\textrm{Per}_{r}(x)\subseteq\textrm{Per}_{p}(x)
.
We say that
p
is an essential period of
x
if

\textrm{Per}_{p}(x)\not\subseteq\textrm{Per}_{r}(x)
for all
r<p
.

•

A number
i\in\mathbb{Z}
is called a
p
-hole
if
i\not\in\textrm{Per}_{p}(x)
.

•

The
p
-skeleton of
x
is the sequence
\textrm{Skel}(x,p)

defined as

\textrm{Skel}(x,p)(i):=\begin{cases}x(i),&\textrm{ if }i\in\textrm{Per}_{p}(x),\\
\square&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We will call
\square
the blank symbol.
Observe that
\textrm{Skel}(x,p)
is a
p
-periodic sequence.

•

Now let
x
be a Toeplitz sequence.
A
period structure for
x
is a strictly increasing
sequence of integers
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that

p_{0}=1
,
p_{t}
divides
p_{t+1}
for all
i
, and

\bigcup_{t\geq 1}\textrm{Per}_{p_{t}}(x)=\mathbb{Z}.

As a consequence of definitions, for every Toeplitz sequence
x

there exists a period structure for it.

Given some sequences
x,y\in(\mathcal{A}\sqcup\{\square\})^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we will write

x\prec y
if
x
can be obtained by replacing some of blank symbols in
y

by symbols in
\mathcal{A}
. In other words,
x\prec y
iff

y[i]\neq\square
implies
x[i]=y[i]
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We will now describe a general method of producing
a Toeplitz sequence
x
over the alphabet
\mathcal{A}
.
First, choose any sequence
1=p_{0}<p_{1}<p_{2}<\dots
such that
p_{t}
divides
p_{t+1}
,
which will be a period structure for
x
.
Roughly speaking, one can fill some positions in
x
by
symbols that repeat with period
p_{1}
, then fill out some of the remaining gaps
by symbols that repeat with period
p_{2}
, and so on.
More formally, for all
t\geq 0
,
we will define
x_{t}\in(\mathcal{A}\sqcup\{\square\})^{\mathbb{Z}}
, such that

x_{t}
is
p_{t}
-periodic and
x_{t+1}\prec x_{t}
.
We will use blank symbol
\square
to represent
a position waiting to be filled at a later step.

(i)

Begin with
p_{0}=1
and
x_{0}:=\dots\square\square\square\dots
.

(ii)

We define
x_{t+1}
for
t\geq 0
by induction.
We have
x_{t}
already defined, and it is
p_{t}
-periodic,
therefore
p_{t+1}
-periodic.
Let

R_{t+1}:=\left\{i\in[0,p_{t+1})\colon x_{t}(i)=\square\right\}
.
Pick a set

I_{t+1}\subseteq R_{t+1}

and an assignment of symbols
\phi_{t+1}\colon I_{t+1}\to\mathcal{A}
.
Define

x_{t+1}(i):=\begin{cases}\phi_{t+1}(r),&\textrm{ if }i\equiv r\pmod{p_{t+1}}\textrm{ and }r\in I_{t+1},\\
x_{t}(i),&\textrm{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

It is clear that
x_{t+1}
is
p_{t+1}
-periodic and

x_{t+1}\prec x_{t}
.

(iii)

If we have filled out all the positions
(i.e. for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists
t
such that
x_{t}[i]\neq\square
),
then
x_{t}
converges to
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
which is a Toeplitz sequence.
For example, one can
choose sets
I_{t}
in such a manner that
x_{t}[-t,t)
contains no blank.

Below we illustrate possible first two steps of the construction,
where
p_{1}=4,p_{2}=8
.

\displaystyle x_{0}:=

\displaystyle\dots\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\square\dots,

\displaystyle x_{1}:=

\displaystyle\dots\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\dots,

\displaystyle x_{2}:=

\displaystyle\dots\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\hskip 1.38889pt0\hskip 1.38889pt\hskip 1.38889pt0\hskip 1.38889pt\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\hskip 1.38889pt0\hskip 1.38889pt\hskip 1.38889pt0\hskip 1.38889pt\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\hskip 1.38889pt0\hskip 1.38889pt\hskip 1.38889pt0\hskip 1.38889pt\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\hskip 1.38889pt0\hskip 1.38889pt\hskip 1.38889pt0\hskip 1.38889pt\square\hskip 1.38889pt1\hskip 1.38889pt\square\square\dots

It follows from the construction that
\textrm{Skel}(x,p_{t})\prec x_{t}
.
We warn the reader that we might have
\textrm{Skel}(x,p_{t})\neq x_{t}

(continuing the example, suppose we would fill all the remaining blanks with

1
in the next step of the construction.
Then we would obtain an
x
that is
8
-periodic,
and then
\textrm{Skel}(x,p_{2})=x\neq x_{2}
).

Oxtoby
[
7
]
has given an example of a specific Toeplitz sequence
whose orbit closure is a minimal,
but not uniquely ergodic topological dynamical system.
Roughly speaking, his construction follows the method outlined above,
where one takes a sequence
(p_{t})_{t\geq 1}
growing sufficiently fast,
then takes
I_{t+1}=([0,p_{t})\cup[p_{t+1}-p_{t},p_{t+1}))\cap R_{t+1}

and
\phi_{t+1}
that is everywhere
0
or
1
depending on parity of
t
.
Extending his methods, Williams
[
11
]

and Downarowicz
[
2
]

have further studied the possible behavior of the set of invariant measures
in Toeplitz systems.
This led to the following definition introduced in
[
1
]
:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
x
be a Toeplitz sequence,
and
(p_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a period structure for
x
.
We say that
x
is a

generalized Oxtoby sequence with respect to
(p_{t})_{t\geq 1}

if for each
t\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
if
x[kp_{t},(k+1)p_{t})
contains a
p_{t+1}
-hole,
then all
p_{t}
-holes in this interval are
p_{t+1}
-holes.

Definition 3.2
.

A subshift
X\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a

generalized Oxtoby subshift with respect to
(p_{t})_{t\geq 1}

if
X=\overline{\mathcal{O}(x)}
for some
x

that is a generalized Oxtoby sequence with respect to
(p_{t})_{t\geq 1}
.

We will see that in the class of generalized Oxtoby subshifts
(with respect to some fixed period structure)
the isomorphism relation is hyperfinite.
Simultaneously, every Choquet simplex can arise as the simplex of invariant measures
of such subshift.

4.
Simplices of invariant measures for generalized Oxtoby subshifts

In
[
2
]
, Downarowicz has proven that every compact metric
Choquet simplex can be realised as the simplex of invariant measures of some Toeplitz
subshift
X
over the alphabet
\{0,1\}
. We will examine this proof to see that

X
can be constructed so that it is a generalized Oxtoby subshift. We begin by reviewing some definitions from
[
2
]
:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
b\in\{0,1\}^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
. For
b_{0}\in\{0,1\}^{*}
,
we define

F^{*}_{b_{0}}(b):=\frac{1}{\textrm{len}(b_{0})}\left\lvert\left\{m\in\mathbb{N}\colon 0\leq m\leq\textrm{len}(b_{0})-\textrm{len}(b)\wedge b_{0}[m,m+len(b))=b\right\}\right\rvert,

F^{**}_{b_{0}}(b,k,j):=\frac{1}{\textrm{len}(b_{0})}\bigl|\bigl\{m\in\mathbb{N}\colon 0\leq m\leq\textrm{len}(b_{0})-\textrm{len}(b)\textrm{ and }\\
b_{0}[m,m+len(b))=b\textrm{ and }m\equiv k\textrm{ mod }j\bigr\}\bigr|.

Let
v^{*}(b):=1/2^{\textrm{len}(b)}
.
Fix also a sequence of positive real numbers
(c_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, with

\sum_{k\textrm{ odd}}c_{k}=1
.
For a triple
(b,k,j)
with
k
odd and
0\leq j<k
, let

v^{**}(b,k,j):=c_{k}/2^{\textrm{len}(b)}
.
Finally, for a measure
\mu
on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, define

\displaystyle d^{*}(b_{0},\mu):=

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^{*}}\lvert F^{*}_{b_{0}}(b)-\mu([b])\rvert v^{*}(b),

\displaystyle d^{**}(b_{0},\mu):=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\{0,1\}^{*}\\
k\textrm{ odd},0\leq j<k\end{subarray}}\left\lvert F^{**}_{b_{0}}(b,k,j)-\frac{\mu([b])}{k}\right\rvert v^{**}(b).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
Y\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be a subshift.
We use
\mathcal{M}_{0}(Y)
to denote the set of those measures
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)

which satisfy

d^{*}(b_{n},\mu)\to 0\;\Rightarrow\;d^{**}(b_{n},\mu)\to 0

for all sequences
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of words from

.

From
[
2
]
one can deduce the following:

Theorem 4.3
.

For every compact metric Choquet simplex
C
there exists
a Toeplitz subshift
X
over the alphabet
\{0,1\}

such that
\mathcal{M}_{S}(X)
is affinely homeomorphic to
C
.
Moreover, one can find such

that is additionally a

, where

.

Proof.

The first part of theorem is stated explicitly in

[
2
]
*Theorem 5.
The ”moreover” part follows from a closer reading of its proof.
Let us restate the relevant steps:

(i)

Given the simplex
C
, there exists a (not necessarily minimal)

Y\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)

is affinely homeomorphic to
C
, and
\mathcal{M}_{0}(Y)=\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)

*Theorem 3.

(ii)

One selects a sequence
(b_{t})_{t\geq 1}
such that
b_{t}\in\textrm{Lang}(Y)
,

,
and for every

is a prefix of
b_{t}
.

(iii)

We carry out a construction similar to the one in previous section.
Define
x_{1}
by setting

, otherwise

.
After step

that is

-periodic,

.
If

by replacing the blanks in

.
If

.
As

, we have
x_{t}\to x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}

that is a Toeplitz sequence.
Using the equality

is a Toeplitz subshift such that

.
By (i) we have that
\mathcal{M}_{S}(Y)

,

We claim that

.

.

.

5.
Further analysis of Toeplitz subshifts

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a Toeplitz subshift, and
x\in X
.
Let
p>1
be any integer. For
0\leq k<p
, we define

A(x,p,k):=\{S^{i}x\colon i\equiv k\pmod{p}\}.

Let

\textrm{Parts}(X,p):=\{\overline{A(x,p,k)}\colon 0\leq k<p\}.

The next lemma will show that the definition of
\textrm{Parts}(X,p)
does not depend on the choice of
x
. It is similar to Lemmas 6, 7 from
[
6
]
,
who in turn cites
[
11
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a Toeplitz subshift, and
x\in X
.
Let
p>1
be any integer. For
0\leq k<p
,

(i)

We have

S\overline{A(x,p,k)}=\overline{A(x,p,k+1)}
.

(ii)

For all
x\in\overline{A(x,p,k)}
, we have

\textrm{Skel}(x,p)=\textrm{Skel}(S^{k}x,p)
.
In other words, all elements of
\overline{A(x,p,k)}

have the same
p
-skeleton.

(iii)

For any
0\leq k,l<p
, the sets

\overline{A(x,p,k)}
and
\overline{A(x,p,l)}

are either disjoint or equal.

(iv)

\textrm{Parts}(X,p)
is a clopen partition of
X
,
independent of the choice of
x
.

Proof.

Item (i) is straightforward.
Item (ii) is precisely the Lemma 6 of
[
6
]
.
Next, we claim that if
y\in\overline{A(x,p,k)}
,
then
\overline{A(x,p,k)}=\overline{A(y,p,0)}
.
For a suitable sequence
n_{j}
we have
S^{n_{j}}x\to y
,
and
n_{j}\equiv k
for all
j
.
Take
z\in\overline{A(y,p,0)}
,
so for a suitable sequence
m_{j}
we have
S^{m_{j}}y\to z
,
and
m_{j}\equiv 0
for all
j
(here we use
\equiv
to mean equality modulo
p
).
Consider any
\epsilon>0
and a compatible metric
d
on
X
.
Take
l
such that
d(S^{m_{l}}y,z)<\epsilon/2
.
Since the map
S^{m_{l}}
is continuous and
S^{n_{j}}x\to x
,
we will have
d(S^{m_{l}+n_{j}}x,z)<\epsilon
for large enough
j
.
This works for every
\epsilon>0
and
m_{l}+n_{j}\equiv k
,
so we conclude that
z\in\overline{A(x,p,k)}

We’ve shown

I_{k}
contains a
p_{T}
-hole, then all of intervals
I_{k}
contain a
p_{T}
-hole. Also,
J_{k+1}=J_{k}+p_{T}
, thus if one of the intervals
J_{k}
contains a
p_{T}
-hole, then all of intervals
J_{k}
contain a
p_{T}
-hole

Since
I_{k}\cup J_{k}=[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T})
which is a piece in
y^{\prime}
, we know that all
p_{T}
-holes in
I_{k}\cup J_{k}
have the same essential period. Since
I_{k}\cup J_{k-1}=[-a+(k-1)p_{T},-a+kp_{T})
which is also a piece in
y^{\prime}
, all
p_{T}
-holes in
I_{k}\cup J_{k-1}
have the same essential period. Keep doing this process in both directions and we get that all
p_{T}
-holes in the whole bi-infinite sequence
y^{\prime}
have the same essential period. This implies that
y^{\prime}
is periodic which a contradiction since there is no periodic point in a non-trivial Oxtoby system.
∎

Claim 8.6
.

One of the following holds:

•

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the sequence
y^{\prime}
restricted on
I_{k}
is the same finite sequence.

•

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the sequence
y^{\prime}
restricted on
J_{k}
is the same finite sequence.

Proof.

By Claim
8.5
, we know that either
\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{k}\subset{\rm Per}_{p_{T}}(y^{\prime})
or
\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{k}\subset{\rm Per}_{p_{T}}(y^{\prime})
. If
\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{k}\subset{\rm Per}_{p_{T}}(y^{\prime})
, since
I_{k}=I_{k-1}+p_{T}
, we know
I_{k}=I_{k-1}
for all
k
, thus the sequence
y^{\prime}
restricted on
I_{k}
is the same finite sequence. Similarly, when
\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{k}\subset{\rm Per}_{p_{T}}(y^{\prime})
, we have for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the sequence
y^{\prime}
restricted on
J_{k}
is the same finite sequence.
∎

Without loss of generality, we may assume that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the sequence
y^{\prime}
restricted on
I_{k}
is the same finite sequence.
By the choice of
x
and
y
, we know that there is a
\phi_{1}\in{\rm Sym}(\mathcal{A}^{p_{T}})
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\phi_{1}(y[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T}))=x[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T}).

We define
\phi\in{\rm Sym}(\mathcal{A}^{p_{T}})
as follows:

\phi(y^{\prime}[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T}))=\phi_{1}(y^{\prime}[-a+(k+1)p_{T},-a+(k+2)p_{T})).

Claim 8.7
.

\phi
is well-defined.

Proof.

Suppose

(7)

y^{\prime}[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T})=y^{\prime}[k^{\prime}p_{T},(k^{\prime}+1)p_{t})

we need to show that

\phi(y^{\prime}[(k+1)p_{T},(k+2)p_{T}))=\phi(y^{\prime}[(k^{\prime}+1)p_{T},(k^{\prime}+2)p_{t})).

Note that
[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T})=I_{k}\cup J_{k}
and
[k^{\prime}p_{T},(k^{\prime}+1)p_{t})=I_{k^{\prime}}\cup J_{k^{\prime}}
. Since
y^{\prime}
restricted on
I_{k}
is the same sequence for every
k
and we know

(8)

y^{\prime}[(k+1)p_{T},-a+(k+2)p_{T})=y^{\prime}[(k^{\prime}+1)p_{T},-a+(k^{\prime}+2)p_{t})

By (
7
), we know that

y^{\prime}[-a+(k+1)p_{T},(k+1)p_{T})=y^{\prime}[-a+(k^{\prime}+1)p_{T},(k^{\prime}+1)p_{t})

which together with (
8
) implies

y^{\prime}[-a+(k+1)p_{T},-a+(k+2)p_{t}]=y^{\prime}[-a+(k^{\prime}+1)p_{T},-a+(k^{\prime}+2)p_{t}].

Thus we have

\phi_{1}(y^{\prime}[-a+(k+1)p_{T},-a+(k+2)p_{T}))=\phi_{1}(y^{\prime}[-a+(k^{\prime}+1)p_{T},-a+(k^{\prime}+2)p_{t}))

which implies that

\phi(y^{\prime}[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T}))=\phi(y^{\prime}[k^{\prime}p_{T},(k^{\prime}+1)p_{t})).

∎

By the choice of
y^{\prime}
and
z
, we know there is a
\phi_{2}\in{\rm Sym}(\mathcal{A}^{p_{T}})
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\phi_{2}(y^{\prime}[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T}))=x[kp_{T},(k+1)p_{T})

The composition of
\phi_{2}^{-1}
and
\phi
is in
{\rm Sym}(\mathcal{A}^{p_{T}})
such that
z
and
\phi\circ\phi_{2}^{-1}(z)
satisfying both conditions.

∎

Lemma 8.8
.

Given two generalized Oxtoby subshifts
X,Y
, the following are equivalent

(1)

X
and
Y
are conjugate.

(2)

There is
T\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
t\geq T
, we have
XF_{t}Y
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Let
z
be a generalized Oxtoby sequence in
X
, let
f
be an isomorphism from
X
to
Y
. By Theorem
7.5
,
f(z)
has the Oxtoby property. In other words, there is
T_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
t\geq T_{1}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the interval
[kp_{t},(k+1)p_{t})
is a piece of
y
. By Lemma
6.3
, we can find
T_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
t\geq T_{2}
, there exists
\phi\in{\rm Sym}(\mathcal{A}^{p_{t}})
such that
\tilde{\phi}(z)=y
. Take
T={\rm max}\{T_{1},T_{2}\}
. By Lemma
8.2
, we are done.

(2)
\Leftarrow
(1) Fix any
t\geq T
. Let
W
and
V
and
\phi
be in Definition
8.1
for
F_{t}
. Take any
x\in W
. We know that
\tilde{\phi}
is an isomorphism sending
x\in W
to
\tilde{\phi}(x)\in V
. Since
X
and
Y
are minimal,
\tilde{\phi}
is an isomorphism from

to

.
∎

Theorem 8.9
.

The conjugacy relation of generalized Oxtoby systems with respect to
(p_{t})
is hyperfinite.

Proof.

By Lemma
8.8
, we have the conjugacy relation of generalized Oxtoby systems with respect to
(p_{t})
is equal to
\bigcup_{T\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcap_{t\geq T}F_{t}
.
∎

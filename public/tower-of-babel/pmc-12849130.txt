Smart nanovehicles crossing the barricade: precision targeting of neuroinflammation for ischemic stroke diagnostics and therapeutics
Authors: Shiyi Sun, Qiuhong Ouyang, Shaochan Zhang, Ruoyun Li, Ni Zhang, Xinglv Chen, Yaqi Zhang, Haochen Xie, Hui Lang, Chunqi Liu, Yi Hou, Meng Qin, Guibo Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeuroinflammation plays a critical role in the onset, progression, and clinical outcomes of ischemic stroke (IS). Real-time monitoring and targeted modulation of this neuroinflammatory cascade are essential for improving therapeutic efficacy. However, current diagnostic and therapeutic approaches are limited by poor blood–brain barrier (BBB) penetration, low specificity, and inadequate sensitivity in early stages. Engineered nanoparticles, with their tunable dimensions, customizable surface properties, and excellent biocompatibility, have emerged as promising vehicles for the precise diagnosis and targeted treatment of neuroinflammation in IS. In this review, recent advances in the application of various engineered nanoparticles for inflammation-focused diagnosis and therapy are summarized. Furthermore, current challenges and future directions for nanobiotechnology-based inflammatory interventions in IS are discussed.Graphic abstract Keywords: Neuroinflammation, Ischemic stroke, Nanoparticles, Diagnosis, Therapeutics

Introduction

Stroke remains the second leading cause of death globally, with ischemic stroke (IS) accounting for 65.3% of all stroke cases [1]. Approximately 70 million individuals worldwide are survivors of IS, with an annual incidence of 7.8 million new cases, which causes a high burden to the patients and society [2]. Until now, only recanalizing therapies are efficacious and tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) is the only drug approved by FDA to restore blood flow [3]. However, the narrow therapeutic time window and the risk of cerebral hemorrhage post-treatment limit the applicability [4, 5]. To break through this dilemma, the focus of research has gradually shifted to rescue ischemic penumbra. Current therapeutic development prioritizes pharmacological interventions targeting key pathological pathways: neuroinflammatory cascades, oxidative and nitrosative stress, glutamate-mediated excitotoxicity, neuronal apoptosis, and energy depletion [6, 7].

Neuroinflammation is a complex and highly synergistic chain of pathophysiologic responses in cerebral ischemia, and emerging evidence has pointed out that neuroinflammation plays an important role in IS [8, 9]. Microglia, the first line of defense responding to ischemic injury, undergo rapid activation following ischemic insult and initiate chemokine-mediated recruitment of peripheral neutrophils and lymphocytes to lesion sites through secretory inflammatory mediators [10]. These activated cells further release pro-inflammatory cytokines, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), leading to a series of secondary brain injuries such as BBB disruption, neuronal damage, and vascular senescence [11, 12]. Neuroinflammation, initiated immediately after the occlusion event (acute phase), lasts for days to weeks (subacute phase), ultimately affects the repair and remodeling of the nerves (chronic phase), constitutes the core biological event in the whole pathological cycle of IS [13]. The dynamic evolution makes real-time monitoring and targeted intervention of inflammation a key strategy for analyzing the mechanism, optimizing the clinical diagnosis and guiding therapeutic strategy.

As shown in Table 1, current clinical IS diagnostics predominantly rely on iodinated contrast computed tomography (CT) and gadolinium-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). However, limitations including insufficient early sensitivity, low specificity in differentiating penumbra from infarcted regions, and safety concerns restrict their use [26]. Therapeutically, the clinical priority remains rapid recanalization of the occluded artery via intravenous thrombolysis or endovascular thrombectomy. Both strategies, however, are restrained by a narrow therapeutic time window (≤ 4.5 h for thrombolysis, ≤ 24 h for thrombectomy in selected patients), and the hazard of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage limits their widespread application [27]. Additionally, conventional neuroprotective agents demonstrate limited clinical efficacy due to their hydrophobic nature, suboptimal BBB permeability, and limited efficacy of monotherapy [28]. In response to these challenges, engineered nanoparticles (NPs) that integrate multiple imaging agents and therapeutic drugs with good BBB penetration, lesion targeting capabilities, and controlled release profiles have attracted extensive attention [29, 30]. For diagnostics, engineered magnetic NPs can enable precise diagnosis of IS by targeting diverse neuroinflammatory markers. Additionally, ROS-responsive probes based on near infrared (NIR) spectroscopy fluorescence imaging can indirectly reflect neuroinflammation by visualizing the dynamics of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (RONS) [31]. For therapy, NPs can deliver various anti-inflammatory drugs for precise inflammation modulation with high target selectivity and controlled release [32]. Moreover, diagnostically integrated nanomaterials with diverse designs provide an innovative strategy for precision medicine through the synergistic mechanism of targeted drug delivery and real-time monitoring.

Neuroinflammation in IS

Neuroinflammation in IS: from anatomy to pathophysiology

Neuroinflammation refers to the infiltration of immune cells in the central nervous system (CNS) and the activation state of local immune cells (mainly microglia and astrocytes), which has been proven to be a common pathological feature in various neurological diseases [33–36]. The cascade of neuroinflammation in cerebral ischemia is tightly linked to the structural and functional integrity of the BBB. As shown in Fig. 2, the BBB constitutes a highly specialized neurovascular unit composed of non-fenestrated endothelial cells tightly sealed by tight junctions, a continuous basement membrane, complete pericyte coverage, and astrocytic end-feet [37]. Brain endothelial cells (BECs) form a critical interface separating the bloodstream from the brain parenchyma and actively modulate the neurovascular microenvironment through the secretion of key trophic factors, including brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), which collectively contribute to neuronal homeostasis and vascular stability [38]. The BBB architecture is further reinforced by pericytes and astrocytic end-feet, which physically stabilize the vascular wall. The basement membrane provides a structural scaffold, and glial-derived signaling molecules orchestrate BBB development, maintenance, and repair processes. The normal BBB restricts paracellular transport of hydrophilic solutes, maintains polarized expression of efflux transporters, and permits selective receptor-mediated transcytosis, thereby preserving cerebral immune privilege and ionic homeostasis [39 After the occurrence of IS, hypoxia and ROS activate Src kinase, which leads to the phosphorylation of tight junction proteins. Meanwhile, calpain-mediated cleavage of ZO-1 protein expands the inter-endothelial cell gaps, MMP-2/9 degrade the basement membrane, and ATP-dependent pericyte contraction constricts capillaries and weakens the interaction between endothelial cells and pericytes[40]. Additionally, astrocytic end-feet swell and retract, and microglia release pro-inflammatory cytokines that amplify leukocyte adhesion [41]. These events convert the BBB from a selective gate into a dysfunctional, highly permeable interface that facilitates brain edema and sustained neuroinflammation.

Multicellular temporal dynamics of neuroinflammation in IS

Pathological development of neuroinflammation exhibits a temporal specificity, which is essential for understanding its complex etiology and developing targeted diagnosis and therapeutic strategies (Fig. 3). Local hypoperfusion caused by cerebral vascular occlusion immediately triggers a molecular cascade reaction centered on neuroimmune dysregulation. This process shows obvious heterogeneity in different time phases, and is accompanied by dynamic changes involving the coordinated participation of multiple cell types.

Schematic of cellular and molecular events during different neuroinflammation stages (from initial activation to chronic repair) of IS. Created with Biorender.com

Initial phase: DAMP-triggered inflammation cascade initiation

When ischemia occurs, hypoxic-ischemic stress rapidly triggers the release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), including high-mobility group box 1 (HMGB1), ATP, heat shock proteins (HSPs), and mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) [42]. These DAMPs activate microglia and astrocytes through pattern recognition receptors (such as Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) and TLR2), initiating the inflammatory cascade.

Acute phase: inflammation amplification and BBB disruption

Microglia sense the DAMPs signal and undergo phenotypic polarization from the homeostatic M2 state to the activated M1 phenotype via the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, initiating the synthesis and release of pro-inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), and interleukin-6 (IL-6). Notably, the maturation and release of IL-1β are tightly regulated by the NLRP3 inflammasome [43]. Initially, DAMPs released from ischemic necrotic cells upregulate the transcription of NLRP3 and pro-IL-1β. Subsequently, extracellular ATP triggers potassium efflux, accompanied by mitochondrial ROS accumulation and lysosomal rupture collectively promote the assembly of the NLRP3-ASC-pro-caspase-1 inflammasome complex [44]. Later, caspase-1 cleaves pro-IL-1β into IL-1β, enabling its secretion and subsequent pro-inflammatory effects. These inflammatory factors induce neuronal oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction by activating MAPK and JAK/STAT pathways, triggering caspase-3-mediated apoptotic cascades [45]. Concurrently, activated microglia secrete chemotactic factors including chemokine (C-X-C motif) ligand 1(CXCL1) and C–C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2), which recruit peripheral immune cells via CCR2 and CXCR2 receptors. These inflammatory effectors not only induce direct neuronal cytotoxicity but also compromise BBB integrity, facilitating transvascular migration of neutrophils and lymphocytes into the cerebral parenchyma. Theseinfiltrated leukocytes further amplify inflammation through TNF-α/IL-1β-mediated endothelial activation and exacerbating local oxidative stress through the release of myeloperoxidase (MPO). Endothelial cells undergo activation with upregulated expression of intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1) and vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 (VCAM-1). The resultant perivascular immune cell aggregation establishes a self-perpetuating inflammatory loop through sustained activation of neuroinflammatory pathways, including TLR, cGAS-STING, NF-κB pathways [46].

Additionally, it is noteworthy that damage to the mitochondrial electron transport chain in cells results in the massive generation of superoxide (O₂⁻), which is further converted into hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) and hydroxyl radicals (·OH). These ROS damage tight junctions and increase BBB permeability. ROS generation further oxidizes lipids and proteins and aggravates BBB damage. Infiltrating neutrophils catalyze the generation of hypochlorous acid (HOCl) from H₂O₂ through MPO, directly oxidizing neuronal membrane lipids and DNA, inducing necrotic death [47]. In addition, inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) and neuronal nitric oxide synthase (nNOS) are activated to produce a high concentration of NO, which rapidly reacts with O₂⁻ to generate peroxynitrite (ONOO−). Multiple inflammatory signaling pathways synergistically interact, continuously amplifying the neuroinflammatory signals [48].

Subacute phase: sustained inflammation and Th1/Th2-Treg coordinated inflammatory remodeling

Following robust pro-inflammatory amplification of the acute phase, the subacute phase of IS shifts toward a dynamically balanced inflammatory landscape. Th1 cells, characterized by their secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interferon-γ (IFN-γ), undergo gradual apoptosis or functional inactivation, leading to a marked attenuation of pro-inflammatory signaling cascades. Concomitantly, there is a significant elevation in the proportion of Th2 cells, which exert regulatory effects by secreting anti-inflammatory cytokines, particularly interleukin-4 (IL-4). These anti-inflammatory cytokines not only suppress the transcriptional activation of pro-inflammatory genes but also promotes the polarization of microglia toward the M2 phenotype [49]. Simultaneously, regulatory T cells (Tregs) exhibit enhanced proliferative activity and infiltrate the peri-infarct regions. Through the secretion of potent immunosuppressive cytokines, including interleukin-10 (IL-10) and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), Tregs directly inhibit the effector function of Th1 cells and mitigate the excessive activation of macrophages. Furthermore, Tregs constrain the clonal expansion of effector T cells via cell-contact-dependent mechanisms, preventing the exacerbation of immune-mediated secondary brain injury [50].

Chronic phase: neurovascular repair and functional reconstruction

After the coordinated inflammatory remodeling of subacute phase, the chronic repair phase of IS shifts toward active tissue reconstruction, characterized by multiple cell types collaborate to repair and jointly reshape the damaged brain tissue. By upregulating the expression of tight junction proteins, endothelial cells re-establish the BBB, providing a stable chemical microenvironment for subsequent repair. M2 microglia express a variety of scavenger receptors, such as CD36 and SR-A1, which can efficiently recognize and phagocytose cell debris and apoptotic cells, significantly reducing the inflammatory response [51]. Repair of neurons begins with the growth and remodeling of axons and dendrites. Neurons around the damaged area will initiate an axon growth program under the stimulation of various signaling molecules, guiding the axon to grow in the correct direction and re-establishing neural connections [52]. At the same time, neural stem cells and progenitor cells are also activated, proliferating and differentiating into new neurons and glial cells. Upregulation of VEGF promote the proliferation, migration, and differentiation of BECs to form a new vascular network. These newly-formed blood vessels not only deliver sufficient oxygen and nutrients to the damaged brain tissue but also provide necessary support for neural repair [53].

Beyond structural restoration, synaptogenesis emerges as a central event in the chronic phase. Neurons enhance the function of neural circuits by forming new synaptic connections, with neurotrophic factors serving as regulators of this process. Multiple trophic factors, such as BDNF and IGF, work together to promote the release of neurotransmitters from the presynaptic membrane and the expression of receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, and enhance synaptic plasticity. In addition, these trophic factors also participate in this process by regulating cell metabolism and growth, providing the necessary material basis for synaptogenesis and promoting the recovery of neural function [54]. Eventually, newly-formed blood vessels, remyelinated axons, and reshaped synapses jointly promote the rewiring of neural circuits and the recovery of behavioral function.

Inflammation-targeted strategies of NPs for IS

In the design of neuroinflammation-targeted nanomaterials in IS, researchers focus on three key areas: BBB penetrating, inflammation-targeted, and microenvironment-responsive release, as shown in Fig. 4.

BBB penetrating strategy

Engineered NPs integrate optimized passive physicochemical properties and ligand-directed active transport to improve BBB penetration ability (Fig. 5). Systematic engineering—refining particle size and imparting a modest cationic surface charge prolongs systemic circulation and promotes adsorptive transcytosis. In addition, high-affinity ligands are conjugated to NPs surface to engage endothelial receptors, triggering receptor-mediated endocytosis. This synergistic exploitation of passive diffusive advantages and receptor-mediated transcytosis of active targeting enables efficient brain delivery of anti-inflammatory therapeutics, markedly enhancing drug exposure while minimizing systemic toxicity.

Passive targeting

The critical challenge for NPs delivery to ischemic lesions lies in traversing the BBB. To improve the permeability of the BBB, several parameters of NPs have been investigated, including size, charge, shape, and surface modifications. Size critically influences the efficiency of passive and active targeted drug delivery, impacting processes such as circulation time, biodistribution, accumulation, penetration, cellular uptake, and subcellular distribution [55]. It is recognized that NPs smaller than 5 nm are easily eliminated by the kidneys. The optimal size range for intravenous injection is between 2 and 200 nm, as nanoparticles larger than 200 nm are more likely to be phagocytosed by macrophages [56]. However, it is worth noting that certain nanoprobes are specifically designed to take advantage of endocytosis, being phagocytosed and internalized by microglia to achieve inflammation imaging. Furthermore, electrostatic charge selectivity imposes additional constraints on molecular translocation across the BBB. The negatively charged BBB endothelial membrane preferentially interacts with cationic nanoparticles [57]. Notably, this charge-dependent transport mechanism operates alongside tight junction integrity in physiological conditions. In addition, shape constitutes an independent yet interdependent geometric element. Compared with spherical counterparts, rod-shaped nanoparticles exhibit higher membrane-contact areas and lower rotational entropy, which facilitate receptor clustering and subsequent endocytosis by BECs [58].

Active targeting

Engineered nanocarriers incorporating diverse targeting ligands, such as monoclonal antibodies and cell-penetrating peptides, have demonstrated enhanced BBB penetration through molecular recognition mechanisms [59]. Following initial binding to luminal membrane receptors, NPs undergo receptor-mediated endocytosis in cerebrovascular endothelial cells. Subsequent intracellular processing involves transcytotic vesicle trafficking and polarized membrane fusion at the abluminal surface, enabling payload delivery to brain parenchyma [60]. The transferrin receptor (TfR) and low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein (LRP) family, predominantly found in BBB endothelial cells, have become crucial therapeutic delivery targets for NPs-based drug transport systems. Bai et al. designed TfR-targeted liposomes with transferrin modification. Results demonstrated that the liposome could undergo TfR-mediated transcytosis across the BBB endothelial layer [61]. Additionally, it is worth noting that heavy-chain ferritin (HFn) demonstrates superior applicability compared to transferrin owing to its non-competitive nature with endogenous transferrin pathways [62]. On this basis, Guo et al. designed HFn-encapsulated Rsv@HFn, to actively penetrate the BBB and deliver rosuvastatin to brain via TfR1 modifications [63]. Beyond TfR-mediated strategies, the LDLR family, particularly LRP1, highly expressed on BECs, mediates the transcytosis of ligands such as apolipoprotein E (ApoE) and engineered peptides like angiopep-2.

In addition to the aforementioned receptors, peptides are often applied as alternatives to proteins as carriers due to their low cost, high stability, ease of modification, and low immunogenicity. Cell-penetrating peptides, with high BBB penetration efficiency, are widely applied in NPs modification. SS-31 peptide not only enhances the BBB penetration efficiency, but also specifically binds to damaged mitochondria in ischemic neurons to achieve “lesion-cell organelle” dual-targeting [64]. Additionally, the rabies virus glycoprotein-derived 29-amino-acid peptide (RVG-29), serves as a well-characterized targeting moiety that specifically binds to neuronal acetylcholine receptor [65, 66]. Cyclized peptides such as cRGD demonstrate significantly enhanced proteolytic resistance and structural rigidity compared to their linear counterparts, have emerged as preferred candidates for specific targeting [67]. Of particular interest, the CREKA peptide selectively accumulates nanoparticles at the microthrombotic site by specifically binding to fibronectin in the ischemic zone, providing a new idea for combined thrombolytic-neuroprotective therapy in the acute phase of IS [68].

In summary, passive targeting enables broad-spectrum drug delivery through the rational modulation of nanoparticle physicochemical properties—including particle size, surface charge, and morphology—offering high biocompatibility but limited cell specificity. To optimize its efficacy, multi-parameter synergistic engineering is critical for enhancing BBB penetration efficiency. Meanwhile, active targeting leverages ligand-receptor interactions to significantly augment drug enrichment at ischemic lesions, addressing the poor specificity of passive targeting. Given the complex pathological microenvironment of IS, dual-ligand functionalized systems may be designed to co-target multiple pathological hallmarks, thereby improving lesion homing precision.

Inflammation targeting

Inflammation biomarker targeting

Neuroinflammatory biomarkers encompass a wide array of molecules, released by activated immune cells and glial cells in response to ischemic injury, including various adhesion molecules, cytokines, and chemokines, and have been widely applied in neuroinflammatory imaging and therapeutic targeting for IS [69]. VCAM-1 has emerged as a critical inflammation biomarker upregulated in endothelial cells during IS. Multiple VCAM-1 targeting strategies have been designed to achieve inflammation-targeted diagnostics and therapies, including monoclonal antibodies, ligands, and designed binding peptides. For instance, NPs encapsulated with VLA-4-expressing MSC membrane have demonstrated enhanced inflammatory site accumulation via VCAM-1 targeting [70]. In another study, αVCAM-1 antibody modification allowed imaging of iron oxide aggregates in the inflammatory regions [71]. Beyond monoclonal antibodies, VCAM-1-binding peptides have been engineered as novel targeting ligands. Functionalization with VCAM-1-binding peptide (VHPKQHR) facilitates directional delivery of NIR probes to cerebral inflammatory foci in IS [72].

Parallel efforts have focused on selectins, another class of adhesion molecules pivotal in leukocyte recruitment in IS. P-selectin-binding peptides and glycosylated ligands interacted with P-selectin have been validated for diagnostic imaging of vascular inflammation [73]. In addition to P-selectin, functionalized NPs designed to utilize the affinity of sialicacid for L-selectin in neutrophils to penetrate the BBB with the help of neutrophils and access to the circulating immune system [74]. Together, these biomarker-driven approaches highlight the versatility of adhesion molecule targeting. However, reliance on a single marker might result in unstable probe signals due to the inherent heterogeneity of the post-stroke inflammatory and temporal fluctuations in biomarker expression patterns. To address this limitation, constructing multi-marker collaborative imaging maps—such as co-targeting VCAM-1 for vascular inflammation and ionized calcium binding adaptor molecule 1 (Iba-1) for immune cell activation represents a critical advance pathway. Simultaneously, minimizing immunogenicity and fine-tuning responsive-release kinetics without sacrificing targeting efficiency remain pivotal for clinical translation.

Biomimetic modification

Biological membranes, endowed with intrinsic biocompatibility, immune-escape properties, and tissue tropism, have emerged as versatile biomimetic platforms for advanced nanodrug delivery [75]. Cell-membrane camouflage combines the engineering precision of synthetic nanoparticles with the biological intelligence of living cells, and the choice of membrane source determines the in vivo fate. Platelet membranes endow nanocarriers with adhesion to activated endothelium and microthrombi, affording high targeting efficiency for thrombosis [76]. For instance, Wang et al. applied traditional liposomes as the inner core carriers to encapsulate the water-soluble iron chelator deferoxamine (DFO), and coated the outer layer with platelet cell membranes to form a biomimetic nano-delivery system. The platelet cell membranes endow the nano-drug with the targeting ability to ischemic lesions through the specific adhesion of receptors such as GPIIb/IIIa and P-selectin on the membrane surface to activated endothelium, thereby improving the enrichment efficiency [77]. Similarly, neutrophil-derived membranes, transmit chemotactic responsiveness to injured vasculature and endow nanoparticles with a built-in inflammatory tropism [78]. Researchers used nanoliposomes as carriers to encapsulate the natural neuroprotective agent Leonurine (Leo), and fused neutrophil cell membranes on the outer layer to form the nano-delivery system [79]. The neutrophil cell membrane endows the nano-system with the characteristics of BBB penetrating, neutralizing inflammatory factors, and immune escape. The adhesion molecules achieve precise positioning through specific binding with ICAM-1 on damaged cerebrovascular endothelial cells, significantly improving the therapeutic effect of IS.

Beyond single-type membranes, hybrid membrane systems can combine the functionalities of two cell types to achieve multi-targeting delivery. For example, a 4T1 breast cancer cell membrane fused with platelet membrane has been designed to mimic the BBB-penetrating behavior of metastatic tumor cells, while retaining the thrombus-targeting ability of platelets. This hybrid approach enhances brain entry and lesion-specific accumulation, offering a promising strategy for tumor-tropic mimicry in stroke therapy [80].

Microenvironment-responsive strategy

ROS-responsive

Inspired by elevated levels of ROS in IS, the strategic introduction of ROS-cleavable chemical bonds makes it possible to achieve site-specific imaging and therapeutic activation. Contemporary materials science prioritizes molecular engineering approaches to achieve optimal equilibrium between structural integrity and environmental reactivity, with particular focus on linker modifications involving boronate ester, phenylboronic ester, and diselenide bond-based functional moieties.

Boronate ester linker, a prevalent ROS-responsive moiety utilized for drug conjugation, exhibits notable stability under physiological conditions, thereby precluding spontaneous hydrolysis. Conversely, upon exposure to ROS, it undergoes rapid cleavage, thereby facilitating the efficient release of the conjugated drug. Jin et al. designed boronate ester bond ROS-responsive NPs. Under stimulation with 1 mM H₂O₂, the cumulative release of drug reached nearly 50% within 2.5 h, approached a plateau after 6.5 h, and ultimately achieved a total release of 91.18% within 10.5 h [81]. For complex oxidative stress environments, the unique advantages of diselenium bonding are particularly prominent. A diselenide bond-bridged nano-platform (rPZDCu) based on ultrasmall Cu4.6O NPs, where zein and dihydroartemisinin (DHA) were conjugated via a diselenide bond to form a zein-Se-Se-DHA complex. In ROS-rich microenvironments in IS, the diselenide bond oxidative cleavage, triggering nanoparticle disintegration and subsequent release of encapsulated therapeutics (Cu₄.₆O and DHA). Experimental validation confirmed that drug release reached 65.91 ± 1.53% after 12 h and 78.45 ± 3.18% after 48 h of exposure to 1 mM H₂O₂ [82]. In addition, ROS-responsive thione chain has emerged as an emerging research direction by virtue of their extreme ROS sensitivity. Sun et al. achieved highly efficient drug delivery at the site of the lesion through thione-modified liposomes. Relying on the precise spatiotemporal release and integration, ROS-responsive release can effectively adapt to the heterogeneous oxidative stress microenvironment in IS [83]. However, it is noteworthy that there are significant differences in the oxidative stress levels at different ischemic stages. A single response threshold is difficult to cover the dynamically changing pathological microenvironment, which may lead to a spatiotemporal mismatch between drug release and the peak of inflammation.

pH-responsive

Ischemic injury alters the metabolism in the ischemic brain by shifting it from aerobic glycolysis to anaerobic glycolysis, leading to the accumulation of lactate and protons, and ultimately resulting in a decrease in pH [84]. It is reported that ischemic brain tissue exhibits a mildly acidic microenvironment (pH 6.0–6.8) relative to normal tissue [85]. This pH gradient provides a critical foundation for pH-responsive drug delivery systems to achieve targeted imaging and release. For instance, pH-responsive NIR fluorescent probe (BOD@Lip) can perform real-time visual monitoring of the IS according to different acidic microenvironments [86]. It provides novel insights into the relationship between the acidic microenvironment and the development of the IS.

The pH-responsive strategy can trigger signal amplification due to lactic acid accumulation within minutes after ischemia, enabling ultra-early imaging diagnosis. Moreover, the main chain of the carrier can be further cleaved by esterase or ROS after acid triggering and excreted through the kidneys. It is worth noting that the reversibility of the acidic microenvironment will weaken the ROS cleavage efficiency and reduce drug release. By installing an irreversible cascade bond at the acid-sensitive site, it can ensure more complete release after one-time triggering and still enable efficient drug release under repeated pH fluctuations.

Nanomaterials in inflammation-based diagnosis for IS

Based on the pivotal role of neuroinflammation in IS, monitoring the progress of inflammation is necessary to monitor the disease development and guide specific treatment. The establishment of neuroinflammation biomarkers and continuous development of imaging technologies and imaging agents have made real-time monitoring of inflammation a reality.

Current clinical neuroimaging predominantly employs MRI and CT for cerebral collateral assessment and ischemic penumbra delineation. Multiparametric acquisition capabilities of MRI make it the gold standard for dynamic evaluation of collateral circulation and salvageable neural tissue. In addition, optical imaging has emerged as indispensable analytical modalities in molecular diagnostics, offering exceptional sensitivity coupled with micron-level spatiotemporal precision for decoding molecular-scale pathological mechanisms. Furthermore, the development of multimodal imaging approaches, integrating MRI, PET, and other modalities, can provide a comprehensive picture of the inflammatory landscape in IS [87]. Nevertheless, intrinsic limitations persist across modalities, like restricted tissue penetrance, radiation exposure risks, and resolution trade-offs continue to challenge comprehensive inflammatory mapping [88].

In recent years, various nanomaterials with high sensitivity and contrast, excellent targeting, and good biocompatibility have been developed to enhance the imaging effect to obtain more accurate and comprehensive IS information [89, 90]. Activated microglia imaging probe

Microglia, as crucial immune cells in the brain, which activation critically mediates post-stroke brain damage by initiating early neuroinflammatory processes. Real-time tracking of microglia activation patterns through noninvasive imaging is a potential source for stroke diagnosis [108].

Superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles (SPIONs), with superparamagnetism, surface engineering potential, and quantum tunneling effects, have emerged as multimodal diagnostic probes [109]. Following intravenous administration, SPIONs magnetically label phagocytic cells via phagocytosis, permitting non-invasive monitoring of phagocyte migration dynamics. Inspired by this mechanism, engineered SPIONs are selectively internalized by microglia, allowing visualization of microglia activation processes to reflect inflammatory progression of IS. A hybrid multimodal imaging nanoprobe, NP3, composed of a 16 nm gadolinium fluoride core with bisphosphonate-modified poly [ethylene glycol] (PEG) coating and Lemke-type fluorophore conjugation, was engineered for subacute IS inflammation monitoring. This nanoprobe can be internalized by phagocytic cells and imaged using MRI, intravital two-photon microscopy and phase contrast imaging with synchrotron X-rays. In vivo MRI demonstrated NPs accumulation in ischemic regions, corroborated by postmortem synchrotron phase-contrast imaging. Furthermore, the probe exhibited an r1/r2 ratio of 20 (7T), confirming its dual T2/T2* contrast capability for precise lesion detection [91, 92].

In addition to endocytosis imaging of magnetic NPs, probes designed by targeting specific expression receptors of activated microglia can also be used for inflammation detection. Iba-1, a macrophage-specific calcium sensor, serves as a definitive biomarker for microglial reactivity [110]. A novel iron–platinum (FePt)-based nanoparticle, formed by incorporating Fe-Pt core into micelles and conjugating with anti-Iba-1 proteins was developed to target Iba-1 to monitor the dynamic changes of microglial activation in subacute phase. Quantitative T2 mapping showed that Fe-Pt NPs coincided with the active microglia detected by postmortem immunohistochemistry [93].

Activated endothelia imaging probe

Endothelial activation also serves as a pathophysiological signature of neuroinflammation, presenting critical diagnostic implications for developing non-invasive imaging biomarkers. Upregulation of leukocyte adhesion molecules, including P-selectin, E-selectin, ICAM-1, and VCAM-1 can serve as molecular targets to facilitate inflammation imaging.

Selectins, as inducible endothelial adhesion molecules, mediate neutrophil-endothelial interactions through glycosylation-dependent recognition, facilitating inflammatory cell recruitment [111]. On this basis, with silica-coated iron oxide core shell and carbohydrate functionalization, a novel magnetic nanoparticle was synthesized. Functionalized carbohydrate could achieve specific binding with the selectin. MRI imaging revealed the targeted NPs accumulation in IS brain vasculature [94]. VCAM-1, a key immunoglobulin superfamily adhesion molecule mediating leukocyte-endothelial adhesion, demonstrates significant upregulation in IS. Recently, Yu et al. developed VCAM-1 actively targeted side-chain modified siNLRP3@YWFC NPs, which achieved efficient delivery of siNLRP3 through VCAM-1-mediated enhanced BBB transcytosis, and realized precise imaging of the penumbra around cerebral infarction and the degree of inflammatory infiltration around the infarction through NIR imaging [96]. In another study, based on citicoline’s neuroprotection and inherent CEST-MRI visibility, researchers engineered self-reporting liposomes for targeted delivery with VCAM-1 modification. Experiments revealed that VCAM-1-directed formulations showed a twofold higher fluorescence signal than that with non-targeted liposomes, with fluorescent validation confirming spatial targeting precision [95]. Low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein-1(LRP-1) is overexpressed on polarized lymphocytes in IS. The dual-targeting theranostic nanoprobe, engineered with Fe₃O₄ nanocores functionalized by αvβ3-targeting c(RGDfC) peptides and LRP-1-specific angiopep-2 motifs, enables simultaneous tracking of angiogenesis and neuroinflammatory dynamics in IS. Longitudinal MRI analysis pre-/post-reperfusion, combined with histopathological and fluorescence validation, spatially mapped neuroinflammatory foci that quantitatively correlated with thrombolysis-induced hemorrhagic transformation risks [97].

Cell-to-cell interaction can also be used as a delivery strategy to achieve activated cell imaging. As primary responders to IS, neutrophils undergo sequential recruitment via selectin-mediated rolling, integrin-dependent adhesion, and chemokine-guided crawling and can actively move to the endothelial junction [112]. Inspired by this interaction, the neutrophil-camouflaged magnetic nanoprobes with SPIO-loaded poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) cores cloaked in neutrophil membranes was designed. Intravital microscopy confirmed targeted binding to inflamed cerebrovascular endothelium [98].

Infiltrated leukocyte imaging probe

Following BBB disruption, massive peripheral lymphocyte infiltration into ischemic brain regions exacerbates neuroinflammation and secondary neuronal injury through focal immune activation [113]. Temporal analysis reveals distinct leukocyte infiltration kinetics: neutrophils and macrophages dominate early-phase, followed by CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and CD4+ helper T subsets. Tracking lymphocyte migration dynamics provides critical insights into inflammatory progression. Molday ION Rhodamine B, a dual-modal SPIO conjugated with Rhodamine B, enables MRI/fluorescence dual-mode tracking of CD4+ T cell spatiotemporal migration [99].

Apart from T lymphocytes, neutrophils, the first peripheral immune cells to enter the brain after IS, also serve as a promising target. On the basis of natural platelet-neutrophil interactions, NPs with platelet membrane as the coating shell, containing spleen tyrosine kinase inhibitor drug and T2 contrast agent SPIO as the inner core and was constructed. The biomimetic coating enables neutrophil-specific targeting, while nanoparticle-delivered paclitaxel decreased neutrophil infiltration and infarct volume. Concurrently, internalized SPIO permits real-time neutrophil tracking via MRI, enabling integrated drug delivery and therapeutic evaluation through dynamic monitoring of treatment response [100].

Excessive RONS imaging probe

Excessive RONS generation can promote the release of inflammatory factors, which exacerbate brain damage, creating a vicious cycle of tissue destruction [114]. As a result, monitoring RONS levels can serve as a valuable indicator reflecting the severity and progression of the IS inflammation. As a molecular detection technology, fluorescent probe has become a powerful tool for RONS biological imaging due to excellent sensitivity and specificity and multi-channel signal acquisition ability [115]. In particular, NIR-II optical probes (1000–1700 nm) exhibit enhanced in vivo imaging performance through high-resolution visualization and deep-tissue penetration capabilities [116]. Recently, a diversity of NIR-based nanoprobes have been developed to detect and image the RONS level to reflect the progress of IS, and even to detect drug therapy.

Researchers have crafted an innovative ROS-responsive NIR-II nanoprobe, which combined IR-783 dye with lanthanide-doped NPs (IR-LnNPs) (Fig. 7A). This nanoprobe could differentiate salvageable ischemic tissue from infarcted cores by lesion-site enrichment and oxidative stress levels. Notably, as shown in Fig. 7B, it could define ischemic areas within 30 min of ischemia onset, which is earlier than MRI, empowering earlier clinical intervention for IS patients [117]. In another study, Hyun et al. designed a ROS-sensitive gold nanoprobe (HHAuNPs) for molecular imaging to detect ROS, which consists of a combination of HA labeled with a NIR dye and a gold nanoprobe. In the presence of ROS, HA degrades and releases a fluorescent dye to restore fluorescence, thus emitting a signal for detecting the tissue region [106].

HOCl, a kind of ROS generated via myeloperoxidase-catalyzed reactions, exhibits high diffusing capacity and mediates tissue damage. In addition, HOCl activates infiltrating neutrophils and monocytes, which further promotes the inflammatory response and affects the occurrence and development of IS [47]. Hu et al. engineered a mitochondria-targeted NIR fluorescent nanoprobe (WD-HOCl) using TJ730 fluorophore. Upon reaction with HOCl, WD-HOCl exhibited a 30.1-fold enhancement in fluorescence intensity, enabling specific detection and real-time imaging of endogenous HOCl [104]. Additionally, Zhang et al. designed an HOCl-responsive nanoprobe using upconversion nanoparticles (UCNPs) coupled with a Cy-HOCl dye. This probe could be lightened up by overproduced HOCl, exhibiting a upconversion luminescence signal of about 1.4 times higher than sham group [105].

Beyond ROS, reactive nitrogen species (RNS)—notably peroxynitrite (ONOO⁻), a potent oxidant formed from NO and O₂⁻—activate detrimental molecular cascades that disrupt the BBB and exacerbate brain injury [118]. Recently, Wang et al. developed a 4IrMn nanoparticle with a dual-transition metal core. This particle can exhibit an endogenous peroxynitrite (ONOO⁻) response, triggering a near-infrared chemiluminescence signal to accurately locate the lesion site and indicate the therapeutic effect of 4IrMn, thus achieving the integration of diagnosis and treatment [101]. Similarly, Zhang et al. synthesized BDP-4/Cur-CL NPs consisting of BDP-4 and Cur-CL units that specifically respond to endogenous ONOO− (Fig. 7C). Imaging results revealed intense afterglow signals confined to pathological regions with negligible background in controls, validating the capacity of BDP-4/Cur-CL NPs for dynamic ONOO⁻ tracking via IS-activated specificity (Fig. 7D) [102] In addition, with narrow bandgap and high fluorescence quantum yield, PbS quantum dots are particularly promising as NIR-IIb probes for in vivo imaging [119]. Researchers developed a targeted NIR-IIb nanoprobe (PbS@Ag₂Se QDs), comprising a PbS QD core and an Ag₂Se shell, enabling CAM1 binding and rapid ONOO⁻-activated oxidation of Cy7.5, resulting in immediate lesion illumination [103].

In summary, targeted probes for inflammatory markers directly anchor to the pathological process of inflammation, overcoming the limitation of CT/MRI that rely solely on anatomical changes and enabling visualization of pathological mechanisms. However, the expression of a single marker has spatiotemporal heterogeneity and is easily affected by individual differences, which may lead to unstable signals or false positives. Future efforts should consider multi-target collaborative imaging through developing dual-marker probes to simultaneously monitor immune-cell infiltration and vascular-endothelium activation, thereby constructing a more comprehensive inflammatory microenvironment map. In addition, RONS-responsive NIR-II probes can achieve early diagnosis, combine with MRI in the future will realize functional and structural dual-modality imaging and improve diagnostic reliability. However, the linear relationship between RONS concentration and fluorescence intensity is susceptible to micro-environmental factors such as pH and temperature, making standardized quantification difficult.

In addition to imaging, nanomaterials have emerged as promising candidates in stroke therapy, with precisely tunable physicochemical properties and multifunctional surfaces to enable targeted therapeutic delivery (Table 3). Organic nanoparticles

Organic nanoparticles have shown a wide range of applications in the inflammatory treatment of IS due to their unique physicochemical properties and biocompatibility, such as high specific surface area, controllable surface chemistry, and good drug loading capacity [144].

Polymeric nanoparticles

Polymer nanoparticles (PNs), a type of multifunctional nanomaterial constructed from a variety of polymers, have demonstrated notable benefits in the management of IS [145]. Based on the origin, polymers can be divided into two main categories: natural polymers and synthetic polymers. The incorporation of targeting ligands or alterations in surface chemistry enables PNs to selectively target specific inflammatory cells. Furthermore, PNs can be engineered to release their therapeutic cargo in response to stimuli or through the degradation of the polymer matrix. The capacity of PNs to encapsulate hydrophobic drugs or unstable bioactive molecules expands the range of therapeutic options available.

Natural polymeric nanoparticles

Natural polymers have garnered increasing scholarly interest due to their distinctive advantages, including biodegradability, biocompatibility, and natural abundance [146]. A wide range of natural polymers, composed of polysaccharides, polyphenols, and proteins, are extensively utilized in the IS therapy.

Polyphenolic materials, with inherent antioxidant activity, are applied as carriers to encapsulate anti-inflammatory drugs to achieve dual anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. Liu et al. employed the neuroprotective polyphenol myricetin as a precursor to process into PC NPs (Fig. 9A). Results revealed that PC NPs demonstrated dual therapeutic effects by effectively suppressing oxidative stress and modulating neuroinflammatory responses (Fig. 9B) [120]. Polysaccharides, distinguished by their hydrophilic backbones and modifiable functional groups, are considered as ideal matrices for nanocarrier construction. For example, curdlan, a polysaccharide synthesized by microorganisms, is widely applied in drug delivery [147]. Ganbold et al. employed functionalized curdlan NPs to deliver NF-κB siRNA. With targeted binding of surface-modified mannose to microglia, NPs therapy enables targeted delivery of siRNA to microglia to suppress inflammation [121]. Another study applied HA, a highly hydrophilic, viscoelastic and biodegradable ideal drug carrier, for rutin delivery. It is worthy that HA modification enhances drug entry into the brain through CD44-mediated endocytosis and hyaluronidase 1-mediated degradation in an acidic environment [122]. With SS31 modification, the BBB penetrating efficiency of the micelles was nearly doubled. Additionally, HFn, a spherical iron storage protein naturally present in the human body, exhibits a distinctive cage-like structure and self-assembly properties, which is suitable for encapsulation of various drugs [148]. On the basis of specific binding affinity to TfR1, HFn-based carriers could mediate efficient BBB penetration. In fact, treatment with CsA@HFn NPs resulted in a significant enhancement of BBB permeability and targeting of ischemic areas in comparison to the free drug [123].

Synthetic polymeric nanoparticles

In addition to natural polymers, synthetic polymers consisting of PEG, poly (lactic acid) (PLA) and PLGA are also widely applied in treatment of IS.

PEG, the classic synthetic nanomaterial, is widely used in drug delivery due to its good biocompatibility and degradability. Notably, PEG can be chemically coupled to NPs by covalent bonding, a process known as PEGylation, which increases the in vivo retention time and reduces the immunogenicity and antigenicity of drug. Wang et al. have designed a cRGD- and TPP-modified nanomicellar systems encapsulating resveratrol (cRGD/TPP@Res) with modified PEG shells (Fig. 9C) [125]. Benefiting from the modification of long PEG chains, the TPP groups were shielded during blood circulation, making it more stable in the blood circulation and ensuring the effective enrichment of the drug in the ischemic lesion. Upon entering ischemic brain tissue and being endocytosed by microglia, the PEG shells are separated from the micelles in acidic lysosomes, and the TPP is subsequently exposed to the target mitochondria. Therefore, micelles can enhance the delivery of resveratrol to microglia mitochondria, effectively alleviating inflammation (Fig. 9D). DSPE-PEG is a biocompatible, biodegradable and amphiphilic material, which can also be functionalized with various biomolecules for specific drug delivery. In comparison to PEGylation, the hydrophobic DSPE segment of DSPE-PEG can effectively embed into the lipid bilayer or the hydrophobic core of nanoparticles, providing stronger physical anchoring. Additionally, DSPE-PEG can act as an amphiphilic molecule, promoting the self-assembly and stability of NPs. Researchers synthesized CREKA peptide-modified nanopolymer micelles encapsulating idebenone (CPLSeP/IDBN) with DSPE-PEG modification [149]. Compared with free drug, the half-life of micelles was significantly prolonged to approximately 15.36 h, ensuring the efficiency of idebenone. Furthermore, NPs formed by PLA-PEG-PLA triblock copolymers encapsulated with curcumin could exhibit therapeutic effects through downregulation of expression of the NF-ĸB signaling pathway [126].

PLGA is a biodegradable polymer synthesized via ester bond polymerization of lactic acid (LA) and glycolic acid (GA) monomers. Its degradation kinetics are tunable by adjusting the LA/GA molar ratio, complemented by inherent biocompatibility and minimal immunogenicity. Recently, engineered AP@R nanoparticle system was fabricated by N-acetyl Pro-Gly-Pro peptide-decorated rod-shaped PLGA nanoparticles loading with rolipram [127]. The rod-shaped PLGA core enables passive targeting by enhancing phagocytic uptake by neutrophils. Upon neutrophil-mediated transcytosis across the BBB, AP@R releases roflumilast to suppress lipocalin-2 (Lcn2) expression, thereby modulating astrocyte polarization toward the neuroprotective A2 phenotype.

Lipid nanoparticles

At present, lipid nanoparticles have garnered significant attention for their potential as an effective drug carrier. Characterized by controlled release, extended circulation time, specific targeting, and enhanced efficacy, lipid-based NPs have emerged as a highly promising platform for active therapeutics including small molecule compound, extract, mRNA drug in IS treatment.

Numerous active small molecule compounds are often constrained by inadequate water solubility and limited targeting capabilities, preventing them from fully realizing their therapeutic potential. Liposome-encapsulated drug delivery systems offer an effective solution to overcome these challenges. For example, Danshensu is widely employed to improve blood circulation, reduce oxidative stress for the treatment of IS. However, its poor BBB penetration, structural instability, and short half-life limit the therapeutic efficacy. Bai et al. have encapsulated Danshensu in liposomes (TF/PS/DSS-LPs) modified with ROS-responsive phosphatidylserine and targeting transferrin peptides (Fig. 10A). Results demonstrated that TF/PS/DSS-LPs significantly enhanced the ability of Danshensu to cross the BBB and the uptake of primate astrocytes and microglial cells (Fig. 10B-C) [128]. In another study, modified stroke homing peptide (DSPE-PEG2000-SHp), BBB-targeting peptide (DSPE-PEG2000-ANG) and ROS-responsive Danshensu chains, ORD@SHp@ANG NPs were self-assembled. ORD@SHp@ANG NPs could recognize brain endothelial cells, cross the BBB and regulate the conversion of microglia [129]. In addition to small compound, plant extracts from medicinal and edible plants with various biological activities have also been applied in inflammation regulation. Recently, researchers prepared celery seed-derived reconstituted lipid nanoparticles (CS-rLNPs) using celery seed extract as the raw material, which showed innate neuron-targeting ability to primarily bind to neurons, avoiding immunogenicity and cytotoxicity and achieving specific targeting and treatment of neurons [130].

In addition to small compound and extract, mRNA has become a powerful therapeutic tool for IS treatment, owing to its adaptability, scalability, and capacity to encode diverse proteins [150]. However, due to the high molecular weight, immunogenicity and easy degradation of mRNA, an effective delivery system is required to reduce RNA degradation and to ensure entry into the cytoplasm for action. IL-10, a potent anti-inflammatory cytokine, was reported to drive microglial M2 polarization via JAK-STAT signaling [151]. Due to its short half-life and the immunosuppression risks and infection at high doses, the clinical translation of IL-10 has been limited. As shown in Fig. 10D, mIL-10@MLNPs was constructed, which could specifically transport mRNA that encodes the phenotype-switching interleukin-10, promoting microglial phenotype transformation and enhancing targeting ability. Results revealed the significantly increased level of cerebral accumulation of mIL-10@Cy5MLNPs (Fig. 10E) [131]. Similarly, in another study, conjugated lipid nanocarriers with antibodies that bind cell VCAM was designed to deliver luciferase-encoding mRNA and Cre-recombinase, which could reduce cerebral infarct volume [132].

Inorganic nanoparticles

Besides organic NPs, inorganic nanomaterials, including cerium, silica, selenium iron oxide, have demonstrated unique advantages in IS therapy, owing to their precise size, shape control, scalable fabrication, and intrinsic antioxidant properties via oxygen vacancy formation [152].

Cerium dioxide (CeO₂) nanozymes exhibit multi-enzyme catalytic activities mimicking natural SOD, CAT, and GPX, enabling efficient ROS scavenging with enhanced stability and structural designability [153]. Liao et al. developed TPP@(CeO₂ + ROF) NPs by modifying CeO₂ with triphenylphosphine and loading roflumilast (Fig. 11A). Compared with control group, TPP@(CeO2 + ROF) decreased the expression of the M1 microglia to 15.57% and increased the expression of the M2 microglia by 5.1-fold [134]. Similarly, He et al. engineered zeolitic imidazolate framework-8-encapsulated CeO₂ NPs (CeO₂@ZIF-8) which could suppress lipid peroxidation and neuroinflammation by inhibiting astrocyte activation [133]. Furthermore, Huang et al. further demonstrated the neurovascular protective effects of Ce-doped zeolite nanomaterials (Ce/Zeo-NMs), which chelated excess zinc ions and preserved BBB integrity via tight junction protein stabilization, while concurrently inhibiting microglial/astrocyte overactivation [154]. Silica (SiO2) NPs, especially mesoporous silica NPs, demonstrate exceptional therapeutic delivery capabilities due to their tunable nanostructures. Zou et al. engineered lactoferrin-modified hollow manganese-doped mesoporous silica nanoparticles (LHMMSNs) for targeted resveratrol delivery (Fig. 11B). In vivo imaging revealed that LHMMSN could cross BBB and accumulate, the fluorescence intensity of LHMMSN group was gradually increased at different time (Fig. 11C). Immunofluorescence results demonstrated reductions in pro-inflammatory cytokines through ROS scavenge (Fig. 11D) [155].

In addition to CeO2 and SiO2 nanoenzymes, iron oxide nanoenzyme, with good catalytic activity and stability, also show a wide range of applications in ROS clearance in IS. Scholars have designed Cy5.5-labeled polyethyleneimine polymer-coated cobalt-doped iron tetraoxide (Co-Fe3O4) nano-enzymes. Results showed that the Co-Fe3O4 nanoenzymes possessed broad-spectrum ROS scavenging ability, and were able to effectively neutralize H2O2 [135].

Biomimetic nanoparticles

Cell membrane-cloaked nanoparticles establish targeted delivery systems by replicating source cells’ surface antigens and functional proteins, achieving enhanced biocompatibility and immune evasion [156]. In IS therapy, NPs harnessing the membrane isolated from platelets, neutrophils, mesenchymal stem cells and microglia to improve drug selective localization to injured vessels, plaques, and ischemic lesion, enhancing therapeutic outcomes (Fig. 12).

Platelet membrane

Platelet membrane, a well-characterized biological material with intrinsic vascular-targeting properties and selective endothelial adhesion capabilities, serve as structural templates for developing stroke-targeting nanoparticles [157]. Inspired by innate vascular injury-targeting ability of platelets, Tang et al. combined 4T1 tumor cell membranes with platelet membranes to coat paeonol- and polymetformin-loaded liposomes to develop NPs (Fig. 13A). Compared with control group, the levels of infarct volume were significant decreased in the liposomes group (Fig. 13B) [80]. In another study, Cui et al. have designed platelet membrane derived NPs loaded with ginkgolide B [136]. In addition to the natural affinity for endothelial cells to achieve precise localization to the site of injury, platelet-mimicking NPs could evade clearance by the immune system. rPZDCu was synthesized based on ultra-small Cu4.6O NPs, thrombolytic agent rt-PA, and docosahexaenoic acid, and modified with platelet cell membranes. rPZDCu NPs exhibited a wide range of effects including thrombolysis, scavenging of ROS, and neuroprotection, and were able to escape from macrophage phagocytosis and release drugs to promote microglia polarization [82]. Besides natural affinity, receptors expressed on platelet cell membranes also aid in the delivery of drugs. D@HPB@SPM nanozymes integrate sialic acid-functionalized platelet membranes with DNase I-loaded hollow Prussian blue cores. This dual-targeting system leverages sialic acid-L-selectin binding for precise lesion homing, while DNase I-mediated NETs degradation disrupts oxidative stress cascades through chromatin fiber cleavage [137].

Neutrophil membrane

Inspired by the natural interaction of neutrophil and activated endothelia, NPs comprised of neutrophil membrane-derived nanovesicles loaded with different drugs was developed. SHp-NM@Edv/RCD (SNM-NPs), a ROS-responsive homing peptide-modified nanoparticle encapsulated by neutrophil membranes, was employed to carry and precisely release edaravone (Fig. 13C). SNM-NPs precisely deliver edaravone to lesion sites to eliminate ROS, reduce the number of microglia, improve the expression of microtubule proteins in neurons (Fig. 13D) [78]. Fingolimod (FTY720), an FDA-approved sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor agonist for multiple sclerosis, demonstrates therapeutic effects in IS by reducing vascular leakage, improving neurological recovery, and polarizing microglia [158]. However, its clinical translation is hindered by suboptimal BBB penetration and systemic toxicity. To overcome these limitations, Zhao et al. engineered neutrophil membrane-coated polymeric NPs encapsulating FTY720 prodrugs. This biomimetic delivery system achieved higher brain accumulation than free drug through neutrophil adhesion and ROS-responsive site-specific drug release. Consequently, NPs significantly attenuated neuroinflammation and minimized off-target effects [139]. In addition, Wang et al. engineered the functionalized biomimetic NPs (McM/RNPs), formed by encapsulating rapamycin with neutrophil membrane modification. McM/RNPs could actively target and bind to inflammatory endothelial cells, block monocyte infiltration and inhibit microglial cell proliferation [159].

Microglia membrane

On the basis of ischemic tropism and BBB penetrability, and the ability to repolarize M1 microglia into M2 phenotype, novel drug delivery system of microglia membrane-coated was constructed. By camouflaging a catalase (CAT)-loaded self-assembled tannic acid (TA) nanoparticle (TPC@M2 NPs) was designed (Fig. 13E). As shown in Fig. 13F, TPC@M2 NPs could reprogram the microglial cell phenotype from M1 to M2-type [142]. Similarly, recent study designed baicalin loaded PLGA nanoparticles, modified with M2 macrophage membrane (M2M@BANPs), which could actively target the ischemic brain, reprogrammed microglia from the M1 to the M2 phenotype [143].

Mesenchymal stem cell membrane

Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) represent multipotent adult stem cells characterized by self-renewal capacity and multilineage differentiation plasticity, ubiquitously present in diverse tissue niches throughout the human body [160]. MSC biomimetics can reduce the nanoparticle immunogenicity, efficiently enhance their homing capability to the cerebral ischemic penumbra, and realize active-targeting therapy. Recently, Zhang et al. have designed a multifunctional nanoplatform using ZL006-loaded Prussian blue NPs (PBNPs) camouflaged by a MSC membrane. With MSC modification, PBNPs can specifically aggregate in the hemisphere of cerebral infarction [141]. Multifunctional immunosuppressive nanoparticles were engineered for targeted modulation of the overactivated cerebral immune microenvironment. The nanoparticle features a CXCR4-overexpressing MSC membrane shell, enabling ischemic lesion homing through CXCR4-CXCL12 axis mediation, and CXCL12 neutralization to block peripheral neutrophil/macrophage infiltration (Fig. 13G). In addition, with polydopamine core utilizes Zn2+-mediated coordination to load A151 oligonucleotides (cGAS inhibitors), achieving ROS-responsive drug release in inflammatory sites. Released A151 inhibits microglial cGAS-STING pathway, promoting M2 polarization and neuroprotection (Fig. 13H) [140].

In conclusion, organic polymers and lipid nanoparticles have become the mainstream carriers due to their good biocompatibility and drug loading capacity, and are particularly suitable for delivering unstable biological agents such as small molecule anti-inflammatory drugs or mRNA. However, the targeting efficiency still depends on surface modification, and it is difficult to precisely match the controlled-release kinetics with the dynamic evolution of the disease course of IS. Biomimetic nanoparticles show excellent performance in BBB penetration and lesion homing. However, large scale preparation, batch stability, and potential immunogenicity are still bottlenecks for industrialization. Inorganic nanozymes have intrinsic antioxidant activity and can scavenge multiple ROS simultaneously. However, their long-term biological safety, metabolic pathways, and potential risks of metal ion release still need systematic evaluation.

Conclusions and outlook

In conclusion, inflammation plays a key role in different molecular and cellular activities and signaling pathways during IS disease onset and progression, which is important for guiding the diagnosis and therapy of the disease state. The advancements in nanomaterial-based strategies for inflammation-targeted diagnosis and therapeutics in IS have illuminated a promising avenue for enhancing the management. New inflammation-based diagnostic strategies developed by applying synthesized nanomaterials combined with advanced imaging technologies, enable detection of various inflammatory aspects of the IS disease process to monitor the disease progression and even the therapeutic effects of drugs in real time. At the same time, a variety of nanomaterials, can act on multiple pathways and links of inflammation to achieve anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects, and improve the clinical outcome. Nonetheless, there are still some limitations in the existing studies.

Firstly, the dual role of neuroinflammation in the pathogenesis of IS and the holistic approach to its modulation underscore the urgency for a deeper understanding. Targeting inflammatory pathways in the early acute phase may mitigate tissue damage, while promoting anti-inflammatory and reparative pathways in the subacute phase may facilitate tissue repair and functional recovery. Unraveling the precise moment when inflammation transitions from a detrimental to a protective role is crucial for designing therapeutic interventions that can harness its beneficial effects while mitigating its harmful consequences.

Secondly, existing nanoparticles based on inflammation imaging achieve highly specific molecular imaging by targeting multiple inflammation markers, and RONS-responsive near-infrared probes can indirectly reflect the level of inflammation in real time. However, the single modality is difficult to comprehensively evaluate the complex inflammatory environment, and the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of targeted markers and microenvironmental interference make quantitative standardization difficult. In the future, multimodal probes combined optical imaging with high temporal resolution and MRI with high spatial resolution is conducive to make up for the deficiencies of the different imaging techniques. In addition, attention should be paid to the metabolism and clearance rate of nanomaterials as it may also affect their retention time in the body and diagnostic effects.

Thirdly, for therapy, organic polymers and lipid nanoparticles are favored for their biocompatibility and drug-loading capacity, inorganic nanozymes possess intrinsic antioxidant activity and multiple therapeutic potentials, while biomimetic nanoparticles demonstrate excellent BBB penetration and lesion-homing abilities with natural chemotactic capabilities. However, the application of nanomaterials is mostly focused on the initial and progressive stages of neuroinflammation, and the intervention is mainly implemented by simply delivering anti-inflammatory or antioxidant drugs. In order to respond more effectively to the complex and variable pathological process of IS, more intelligent and flexible nanomaterial systems with sequential therapeutic functions should be designed and constructed according to the different stages of inflammation development and their unique pathological characteristics, which can adjust the release mode of drugs to achieve precise sequential regulation of pathological progression.

Last but not least, the emergence of theranostic nanomaterials indicates marked progress in the field. Unlike traditional approaches that often segregate diagnosis and treatment, these innovative materials integrate both functions into a single platform. Their ability to monitor disease progression, visualize therapeutic efficacy, and track drug distribution and metabolism in real-time offers unprecedented precision and personalization in stroke management. The development of such multifunctional nanomaterials holds great promise for optimizing treatment regimens, reducing off-target effects, and enhancing patient outcomes.

In summary, the integration of nanomaterials into inflammation-targeted diagnosis and therapeutics for IS holds immense potential for revolutionizing the management of this condition. However, realizing this potential requires a concerted effort to unravel the complexities of neuroinflammation in stroke and develop effective integrated theranostic nanoplatforms that adapt to distinct characteristics of different inflammatory phases. With these advancements, it is expected to begin a new era of personalized and precise medicine for stroke patients.

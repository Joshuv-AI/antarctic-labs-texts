On properties of Schmidt Decomposition
Mithilesh Kumar

Abstract
Schmidt decomposition is a powerful tool in quantum information. While Schmidt decomposition is universal for bipartite states, its not for multipartite states. In this article, we review properties of bipartite Schmidt decompositions and study which of them extend to multipartite states. In particular, Schmidt number (the number of non-zero terms in Schmidt decomposition) define an equivalence class using separable unitary transforms. We show that it is NP-complete to partition a multipartite state that attains the highest Schmidt number. In addition, we observe that purifications of a density matrix of a composite system preserves Schmidt decomposability.



I
Introduction

Composite quantum systems exhibit a special property called entanglement. Entangled quantum subsystems remain connected independent of separation between them, we don’t fully understand how. Entanglement is now considered a physical resource. We can
spend
entanglement within a system to accomplice tasks like quantum communication, teleportation, superdense coding etc. Naturally, we would like to measure amount of entanglement in a state. One method is to rewrite the state in a specific form called Schmidt decomposition and the number of non-zero terms in the decomposition quantify the amount of entanglement. In the simplest case, a quantum system is composed of two subsystems
A
and
B
whose state in standard orthonormal basis can be expressed as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}|i_{A}\rangle|i_{B}\rangle

(1)

where
a_{ij}
are complex numbers. The state vectors
|i_{A}\rangle
and
|i_{B}\rangle
form orthonormal basis of state spaces
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
respectively. The indices
i,j
run over the dimensions of each of the spaces. A restatement of Singular Value Decomposition implies that the bipartite state
1
can always be rewritten in another bases of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle

(2)

where
\lambda_{\ell}
can be chosen to be real and non-negative. The states
|\ell_{A}\rangle
and
|\ell_{B}\rangle
are orthonormal within their respective spaces (but need not form a basis)
Peres 1997
. The number of non-zero terms in the state
2
is called the
Schmidt number
of the state. If the Schmidt number of a state is
1
, i.e. there is only one term in the decomposition, then the system does not exhibit any entanglement. As the state
|\psi\rangle
is called a
product state
. Higher is the Schmidt number, larger is the amount of entanglement within the system. Terhal and Horodecki
Terhal and Horodecki 2000
extended the notion of Schmidt number to bipartite density matrices, but in this article, we restrict our inquiry to Schmidt number of pure states only. Vidal
Vidal 2003
and Nest
Van den Nest 2013
showed that states with high Schmidt number are necessary for quantum speed-up.

Naturally, there exist entanglement within a system even if it can been seen as composed of more than two subsystems. Such systems are called multipartite. We can label each part as
A_{1},A_{2},...,A_{n}
and a multipartite state can be expanded using orthonormal basis states as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{n}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}|i^{A_{1}}_{1}\rangle|i^{A_{2}}_{2}\rangle\cdots|i^{A_{n}}_{n}\rangle

(3)

where
a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}
are complex numbers satisfying normalization condition
\sum|a_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{n}}|^{2}=1
. The states
|i^{A_{1}}_{1}\rangle,|i^{A_{2}}_{2}\rangle,\cdots
,
|i^{A_{n}}_{n}\rangle
are orthonormal within their respective spaces
\mathcal{H}_{A_{k}}
. The Schmidt decomposition is just an extension of the bipartite case written as

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

(4)

where
\lambda_{\ell}
can be chosen to be real and non-negative. The states
|\ell_{A_{k}}\rangle
are orthonormal within their respective spaces (but need not form a basis). It turns out that this form is too strict to be satisfied by all multipartite states. There are multipartite states do not admit Schmidt decomposition. Because of this, Huber and de Vicente
Huber and de Vicente 2013
tried to consider a vector of Schmidt numbers of two dimensional subsystems in tripartite case. In this work, we’ll only consider Schmidt decomposable states.

In this article, we’ll review some well known properties of Schmidt number of bipartite states. We’ll analyze which of them hold for multipartite states. We’ll also consider some of the applications of Schmidt decomposition in quantum information theory.

II
Classification based on Schmidt bases

As has been noted, all bipartite states have Schmidt decomposition. The case for bipartite states is well known and the reader is referred to
Nielsen and Chuang 2000

Theorem 1
.

If
|\psi\rangle
is multipartite Schmidt decomposable state, then the eigenvalues of reduced density matrices of any subsystem are the same.

Proof.

Using the Schmidt decomposition

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

(5)

The density matrix is

\displaystyle\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|=\sum_{\ell,m}\lambda_{\ell}\lambda_{m}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle\langle\ell_{A_{1}}|\langle\ell_{A_{2}}|\cdots\langle\ell_{A_{n}}|

(6)

Tracing out subsystems of any subset of indices
S\subset\{1,2,...,n\}
, we get

\displaystyle\rho^{A_{S^{C}}}=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}|\ell_{S^{C}}\rangle\langle\ell_{S^{C}}|

(7)

where
S^{C}=\{1,2,...,n\}-S
and the states
\ell_{S^{C}}
is product states running on indices over
S^{C}
. Clearly all reduced density matrices
\rho^{A_{S^{C}}}
have the same eigenvalues
\lambda_{\ell}
.
∎

As is noted in
Nielsen and Chuang 2000
, for a bipartite state, if
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i_{A}\rangle|i_{B}\rangle
is the Schmidt decomposition, then

\phi

|\psi\rangle=(U\otimes V)|\phi\rangle

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
are two Schmidt decomposable multipartite states on the same Hilbert space. Then,
|\psi\rangle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})|\phi\rangle
if and only if the Schmidt decompositions of
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
have the same Schmidt coefficients.

Proof.

If
|\psi\rangle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})|\phi\rangle
, we can use the Schmidt decomposition of
|\phi\rangle
as

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}(U_{A_{1}}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle)(U_{A_{2}}|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle)\cdots(U_{A_{n}}|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{n}}\rangle

As states
|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{k}}\rangle
are obtained via unitary transform of orthonormal states
|\ell_{A_{k}}\rangle
, they remain orthonormal and hence provide a valid Schmidt decomposition of
|\psi\rangle
. Therefore, both
\psi
and
\phi
have the same Schmidt coefficients.

For converse, assume
\psi
and
\phi
have the same Schmidt coefficients, i.e.

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{n}}\rangle

Since states
\{|\ell_{A_{k}}\rangle\}
and

such that

. This implies that

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell^{\psi}_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}(U_{A_{1}}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle)(U_{A_{2}}|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle)\cdots(U_{A_{n}}|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A_{1}}\rangle|\ell_{A_{2}}\rangle\cdots|\ell_{A_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}\otimes\cdots U_{n})|\phi\rangle

∎

Let us define the problem SCHMIDT-PARTITION as follows:

Input:

Task:

Theorem 4
.

SCHMIDT-PARTITION is

Proof.

To have a state of Schmidt number

. The dimension of combined system of two subsystems of dimensions

and

-complete SUBSET SUM problem in which the input is a set of positive numbers and the task is to find a subset whose sum is

.
∎

Corollary 1

Given

-complete to find a Schmidt decomposable state of highest Schmidt number.

If

It is easy to see that the Schmidt number of a bipartite state is equal to the rank of the reduced density matrix of one of the subsystems by using
rank(A)=rank(AA^{\dagger})=rank(A^{\dagger}A)=rank((A^{\dagger}A)^{T})
. This result holds for multipartite states as well.

Theorem 7
.

If
|\psi\rangle
is a Schmidt decomposable multipartite state, then the Schmidt number is equal to the rank of the reduced density matrix of one of the subsystems.

Proof.

We show here for tripartite states.
Consider the Schmidt decomposition of
|\psi\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}|\ell_{A}\rangle|\ell_{B}\rangle|\ell_{C}\rangle
.

\displaystyle\rho^{ABC}

\displaystyle=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,m}\lambda_{\ell}\lambda_{m}|\ell_{A}\rangle\langle m_{A}|\otimes|\ell_{B}\rangle\langle m_{b}|\otimes|\ell_{C}\rangle\langle m_{C}|

\displaystyle\rho^{AB}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}^{d}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}|\ell_{A}\ell_{B}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}\ell_{B}|

Consider the spectral decomposition of
\rho^{AB}
given as

\displaystyle\rho^{AB}=\sum_{i}^{r}p_{i}|i_{AB}\rangle\langle i_{AB}|

Since the states
\{|\ell_{A}\ell_{B}\rangle\}
and
\{|i_{AB}\rangle\}
are orthonormal sets, they can each be extended to orthonormal bases using Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization process. This implies that there is unitary matrix

that takes set of vectors

.

\displaystyle U\rho^{AB}U^{\dagger}=\sum_{i}^{r}p_{i}|\ell_{A}\ell_{B}\rangle\langle\ell_{A}\ell_{B}|

Since

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=rank(\rho_{A})

Observation 4
.

Linear combination of Schmidt decomposable multipartite states is not necessarily Schmidt decomposable. An an example, we can work with
|W\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(|001\rangle)+|010\rangle+|100\rangle
which is not Schmidt decomposable, but each of the terms are Schmidt decomposable.

Definition 1
.

An
n
-partite Schmidt decomposable state of Schmidt rank

is said to have Schmidt dimension
(n,k)

Theorem 9
.

Given two states
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle

Starting with the Schmidt decompositions of
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}^{k_{\psi}}\lambda_{i}^{\psi}|i_{A_{1}}...i_{A_{m}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}^{k_{\phi}}\lambda_{j}^{\phi}|j_{B_{1}}...i_{B_{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}^{k_{\psi},k_{\phi}}\lambda_{i}^{\psi}\lambda_{j}^{\phi}|i_{A_{1}}...i_{A_{m}}j_{B_{1}}...j_{B_{n}}\rangle

Notice that the above expression for

-parts of

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}^{k_{\psi},k_{\phi}}\lambda_{i}^{\psi}\lambda_{j}^{\phi}|i_{A_{1}}...i_{A_{x_{1}}}j_{B_{1}}\rangle|i_{A_{x_{1}+1}}...A_{x_{1}+x_{2}}j_{B_{2}}\rangle...|i_{m-(x_{n})+1}...i_{m}j_{B_{n}}\rangle

It is easy to verify that states of each group are orthonormal and they don’t repeat in the sum, giving a valid Schmidt decomposition. Hence,

can be partitioned into
n

-partite system has Schmidt decomposition of rank

.

The number of such groups for fixed permutation of parts in
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
is equal to the number of solutions of

\displaystyle x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}

\displaystyle=m

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle\geq 1

which is given by
{m-1\choose n-1}
.
∎

III
Purification

Given a density matrix
\rho

A

R

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\langle i_{A}|

\displaystyle|AR\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i_{A}\rangle|i_{R}\rangle

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Tr}_{R}(|Ar\rangle\langle Ar|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|i_{A}\rangle\langle i_{A}|

Essentially, we have used the Schmidt decomposition of

Regarding purification, one can ask some interesting questions like given a density matrix of a composite system, can it be purified to a Schmidt decomposable state? If a multipartite state is not Schmidt decomposable, can an additional system make it Schmidt decomposable?

Lemma 1

\displaystyle|AR_{1}\rangle=(I\otimes U_{R})|AR_{2}\rangle

Proof.

IV
Conclusion

Schmidt decomposition remains to be an active area of research in quantum computation and quantum information. In this work, we have reviewed some of the known properties of Schmidt decomposition with respect to state vector. We saw that finding partition of highest Schmidt number is NP-complete and purification of a multipartite density matrix falls in one of the two categories, either it has Schmidt decomposition or not. Future work will involve other extensions and generalization of Schmidt number, particularly of mixed states.

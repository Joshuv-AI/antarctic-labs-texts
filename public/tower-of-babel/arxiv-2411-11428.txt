Abstract
. The work described in this paper builds on the polyhedral semantics of the Spatial Logic for Closure Spaces ( SLCS ) and the geometric spatial model checker PolyLogicA. Polyhedral models are central in domains that exploit mesh processing, such as 3D computer graphics. A discrete representation of polyhedral models is given by cell poset models, which are amenable to geometric spatial model checking using SLCS η {}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta} , a weaker version of SLCS . In this work we show that the mapping from polyhedral models to cell poset models preserves and reflects SLCS η {}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta} . We also propose weak simplicial bisimilarity on polyhedral models and weak ± \pm -bisimilarity on cell poset models, where by “weak” we mean that the relevant equivalence is coarser than the corresponding one for SLCS , leading to a greater reduction of the size of models and thus to more efficient model checking. We show that the proposed bisimilarities enjoy the Hennessy-Milner property, i.e. two points are weakly simplicial bisimilar iff they are logically equivalent for SLCS η {}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta} . Similarly, two cells are weakly ± \pm -bisimilar iff they are logically equivalent in the poset-model interpretation of SLCS η {}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta} . Furthermore we present a model minimisation procedure and prove that it correctly computes the minimal model with respect to weak ± \pm -bisimilarity, i.e. with respect to logical equivalence of SLCS η {}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta} . The procedure works via an encoding into LTSs and then exploits branching bisimilarity on those LTSs, exploiting the minimisation capabilities as included in the mCRL2 toolset. Various examples show the effectiveness of the approach.

In conclusion, in the present paper, we focus on
model reduction
— as a way of improving model checking efficiency — and spatial
reachability
— rather than
proximity
.
In particular, we are interested in a framework for model reduction with the following features:

(1)

It should be
sound

and
complete
, i.e. be based on a notion of bisimilarity that enjoys the Hennessy-Milner Property (HMP) so that completeness and soundness
of the optimised model checking procedure — via model reduction — are guaranteed.

(2)

It should be
optimal

with respect to the logic of interest, in the sense of yielding
the minimal model with respect to the equivalence induced by the logic of interest,
but also a
useful
one. In this respect we have been inspired by the use of branching bisimilarity in the context of LTSs:
branching bisimilarity — that is weaker than strong bisimilarity — enjoys the HMP with respect to CTL
∗
without
X
(next) — that is weaker than full CTL
∗
— and both the equivalence and its logical characterisation are widely used in concurrency theory and its applications. In essence, weak simplicial bisimilarity in the context of spatial logic is a re-interpretation in space of branching bisimilarity in the context of temporal logic. Similarly,
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
can be seen as the spatial counterpart of CTL
{}^{*}\setminus X
.

(3)

It should exploit
existing tools
for minimisation via bisimulation, since at present powerful and efficient model minimisation techniques and tools are available for branching bisimilarity minimisation.

As we mentioned above, the fact that
logical equivalence
\equiv_{\eta}
is
coarser
than
\equiv_{\gamma}
implies
that poset model minimisation modulo
\equiv_{\eta}
results in models that can be
smaller than those obtained modulo
\equiv_{\gamma}
, and this is one reason why we focus on
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
in the present paper.
As is to be expected, we do not have a general measure of the “gain”,
in terms of percentage of reduction in the number of cells of the input models, when using

\equiv_{\eta}
instead of
\equiv_{\gamma}
, because this depends on the specific model.

Furthermore, we show that
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
is of interest for reasoning about reachability, which is an essential feature in topological structures, as illustrated by the
examples presented in this paper.
There are also additional notions that can easily be expressed using the
\eta
modality
such as “double reachability” and “being surrounded”.
The former are properties like “there is a path (from the point of interest) reaching — while passing only through points satisfying
\Phi_{1}

The team and those to be rescued can thus meet in a point satisfying the nested

A

\sigma

A partial order is defined on cells: we say that

{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}

In
[
BCG+22
]
,
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
, a version of
SLCS
for polyhedral models,
has been presented that consists of predicate letters, negation,
conjunction, and the single modal operator
\gamma
, expressing
conditional reachability. The satisfaction relation for

\gamma(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
, for a polyhedral model

\mathcal{P}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}})
, with
P=|K|
for some simplicial complex
K
,
and

,
as defined in
[

]
, is recalled below:

\begin{array}[]{l c l}\mathcal{P},x\models\gamma(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})&\Leftrightarrow&\mbox{a }\mbox{topological path }\pi:[0,1]\to|K|\mbox{ exists such that }\pi(0)=x,\\
&&\mathcal{P},\pi(1)\models\Phi_{2},\mbox{and }\mathcal{P},\pi(r)\models\Phi_{1}\mbox{ for all }r\in\!\!(0,\!1).\end{array}

We also recall the interpretation of
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
on poset models. The
satisfaction relation for
\gamma(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
, for a poset model

\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
and

\begin{array}[]{l c l}\mathcal{F},w\models\gamma(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})&\Leftrightarrow&\mbox{a }\mbox{$\pm$-path }\pi:[0;\ell]\to W\mbox{ exists such that }\pi(0)=w,\\
&&\mathcal{F},\pi(\ell)\models\Phi_{2},\mbox{and }\mathcal{F},\pi(i)\models\Phi_{1}\mbox{ for all }i\in\!\!(0;\!\ell).\end{array}

In
[
BCG+22
]
it has also been shown that, for all

and

{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
formulas

\mathbb{F}(\mathcal{P}),\mathbb{F}(x)\models\Phi
. In addition,

, a novel notion of bisimilarity for

\mathcal{P}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}})
, with

for some simplicial complex

such that, for all

we have
that
\pi((r_{i-1},r_{i}))\subseteq\widetilde{\sigma}_{i}
.

6

6

Essentially, simplicial paths have been
introduced for avoiding to have to deal with “bad” paths, e.g. paths that
can oscillate infinitely often between a set of cells.

{defi}

Given a polyhedral model
\mathcal{P}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}})
, with

for some simplicial complex

is a

(1)

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{1})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}}(x_{2})
and

(2)

there is a simplicial path
\pi_{2}
from
x_{2}

Z(\pi_{1}(r),\pi_{2}(r))
for all
r\in[0,1]
.

Two points
x_{1},x_{2}\in P

are
simplicial bisimilar
, written
x_{1}\sim_{\resizebox{372930}{}{$\triangle$}}^{\mathcal{P}}x_{2}
, if there exists a simplicial bisimulation
Z
such
that
Z(x_{1},x_{2})
.
\bullet

It has been shown that simplicial bisimilarity enjoys the classical
Hennessy-Milner property:
two points

x_{1},x_{2}\in P

are simplicial
bisimilar if and only if they
satisfy the same
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
formulas, i.e. they are equivalent with
respect to the logic
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
, written
x_{1}\equiv_{\gamma}^{\mathcal{P}}x_{2}
.

The result has been extended to
\pm
-bisimilarity
on finite
poset models, a notion of bisimilarity based on
\pm
-paths: given
finite poset model
\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
,

w_{1},w_{2}\in W
are
\pm
-bisimilar, written
x_{1}\sim_{\pm}^{\mathcal{F}}x_{2}
,
if and only if they satisfy the same
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
formulas, i.e.

x_{1}\equiv_{\gamma}^{\mathcal{F}}x_{2}
(see
[
CGL+23a
]
for details). In
summary, we have:

x_{1}\sim_{\resizebox{372930}{}{$\triangle$}}^{\mathcal{P}}x_{2}\mbox{ iff }x_{1}\equiv_{\gamma}^{\mathcal{P}}x_{2}\mbox{ iff }\mathbb{F}(x_{1})\equiv_{\gamma}^{\mathbb{F}(\mathcal{P})}\mathbb{F}(x_{2})\mbox{ iff }\mathbb{F}(x_{1})\sim_{\pm}^{\mathbb{F}(\mathcal{P})}\mathbb{F}(x_{2}).

In Section
4
we show a similar result for a
weaker

logic introduced in the next section, and originally presented
in
[
BCG+24a
]
.
Finally, in
[

]
it has been shown that the classical modality
\Diamond

can be expressed using
\gamma
.
We recall that
for polyhedral model
\mathcal{P}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}})

and for poset model

, the semantics of

is defined as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l c l}\mathcal{P},x\models\Diamond\,\Phi&\Leftrightarrow&x\in\mathcal{C}_{T}(\{\,x^{\prime}\in P\,|\,\mathcal{P},x^{\prime}\models\Phi\,\})\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{l c l}\mathcal{F},w\models\Diamond\Phi&\Leftrightarrow&\text{$w^{\prime}\in W$ exists such that $w\preccurlyeq w^{\prime}$ and
$\mathcal{F},w^{\prime}\models\Phi$}.\end{array}

It turns out that

is equivalent to

,
for all
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
formulas

.

2.4.
Labelled Transition Systems and Related Bisimilarities

{defi}

A
labelled transition system
, LTS for short,
is a tuple
(S,L,\longrightarrow~\!\!)
where
S
is a non-empty set of
states
,

L
is a non-empty set of
transition labels
and
\longrightarrow\subseteq S\times L\times S
is the transition relation.
\bullet

For
\tau\in L
denoting the “silent” action we let
t\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,t^{\prime}
whenever

t=t^{\prime}
or there are
t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}
, for
n>0
such that

t_{0}=t
,
t_{n}=t^{\prime}
and
t_{i}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,t_{i+1}
for
i\in[0;n)
.

{defi}

[Strong Bisimulation and Strong Equivalence]
Given an LTS
\mathbb{S}=(S,L,\longrightarrow)
a binary relation
B\subseteq S\times S
is a
strong bisimulation
if, for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
, if
B(s_{1},s_{2})
then the following holds:

(1)

if
s_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s^{\prime}_{1}
for some
\lambda
and
s_{1}
, then
s^{\prime}_{2}
exists such that

s_{2}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s^{\prime}_{2}
and
B(s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})
, and

(2)

if
s_{2}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s^{\prime}_{2}
for some
\lambda
and
s_{2}
, then
s^{\prime}_{1}
exists such that

s_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s^{\prime}_{1}
and
B(s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})
.

We say that
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are
strongly equivalent
in
\mathbb{S}
, written
s_{1}\,\sim^{\mathbb{S}}s_{2}
if a strong bisimulation
B
exists such that
B(s_{1},s_{2})
.
\bullet

It has been shown that
\sim^{\mathbb{S}}
is the union of all strong bisimulations in
\mathbb{S}
, it is the largest strong bisimulation and it is an equivalence relation
[
Mil89
]
.

{defi}

[Branching Bisimulation and Equivalence]
Given an LTS
\mathbb{S}=(S,L,\longrightarrow)
such that
\tau\in L
a binary relation
B\subseteq S\times S
is a
branching bisimulation
iff, for all
s,t,s^{\prime}\in S
, and
\lambda\in L
,
whenever
B(s,t)
and
s\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s^{\prime}
, it holds that:
(i)
B(s^{\prime},t)
and
\lambda=\tau
, or
(ii)
B(s,\bar{t}),B(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
and
t\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\bar{t}
,
\bar{t}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,t^{\prime}
, for some

\bar{t},t^{\prime}\in S
.

Two states
s,t\in S
are called
branching bisimilar
in
\mathbb{S}
, written

s\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[6.99997pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}}t
if
B(s,t)
for some branching bisimulation
B
for
S
.

\bullet

It has been shown that
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[6.99997pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}}
is the union of all branching bisimulations in
\mathbb{S}
, it is the largest branching bisimulation and it is an equivalence relation
[
GW96
]
.

We will omit the superscript
\mathbb{S}
in
\sim^{\mathbb{S}}
and
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[6.99997pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}}
when this will not cause confusion.

3.
Weak
SLCS
on Polyhedral Models

In this section we introduce
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
, a logic for polyhedral models
that is weaker than
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
, yet is still capable of expressing
interesting conditional reachability properties. We present also an
interpretation of the logic on finite poset models.

{defi}

[Weak
SLCS
on polyhedral models -
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
]

The abstract language of
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
is the following:

\Phi::=p\;|\;\neg\Phi\;|\;\Phi_{1}\land\Phi_{2}\;|\;\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}).

The satisfaction relation of
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
with respect to a given
polyhedral model

\mathcal{P}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}})
, with
P=|K|
for some simplicial complex
K
,

SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}

formula
\Phi
, and point
x\in P
is defined recursively on
the structure of
\Phi
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l c l}\mathcal{P},x\models p&\Leftrightarrow&x\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}}(p);\\
\mathcal{P},x\models\neg\Phi&\Leftrightarrow&\text{$\mathcal{P},x\models\Phi$ does not hold};\\
\mathcal{P},x\models\Phi_{1}\land\Phi_{2}&\Leftrightarrow&\text{$\mathcal{P},x\models\Phi_{1}$ and $\mathcal{P},x\models\Phi_{2}$};\\
\mathcal{P},x\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})&\Leftrightarrow&\text{a topological path $\pi:[0,1]\to P$ exists such
that}\\
&&\text{$\pi(0)=x$, $\mathcal{P},\pi(1)\models\Phi_{2}$, and
$\mathcal{P},\pi(r)\models\Phi_{1}$ for all $r\in\!\![0,\!1)$}.\end{array}

\bullet

Remark 4
.

It is worth pointing out that the definition of the satisfaction relation of
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
does not
depend on the specific simplicial complex
K
that generates the polyhedron
P=|K|
.
In other words: given polyhedral models
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})
with
P=|K^{\prime}|
and

\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}})
with
P=|K^{\prime\prime}|=|K^{\prime}|
and

\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime}}
, for all
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
formulas
\Phi
and
x\in P

the following holds:
\mathcal{P}^{\prime},x\models\Phi
iff
\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},x\models\Phi
.
\divideontimes

As usual, disjunction (
\lor
) is derived as the dual of
\land
.
Note that the only difference between
\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
and

\gamma(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
is that the former requires that
also
the first element
of a path witnessing the formula
satisfies
\Phi_{1}
, hence the use of the left closed interval

[0,1)
here.
Although this might seem at first sight only a very
minor difference, it has considerable consequences:

\eta
cannot express
\Diamond
, which, instead, can be expressed in terms of
\gamma
(see Remark
8
and Remark
15
below).

{defi}

[
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
Logical Equivalence]

Given a polyhedral model

\mathcal{P}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}})
, with
P=|K|
for some simplicial complex
K
,
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in P
, we say that
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are
logically
equivalent
with respect to
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
, written

x_{1}\equiv_{\eta}^{\mathcal{P}}x_{2}
, if and only if, for all
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}

formulas
\Phi
, it holds that
\mathcal{P},x_{1}\models\Phi
if and
only if
\mathcal{P},x_{2}\models\Phi
.
\bullet

In the following, we will refrain from indicating the model
\mathcal{P}

explicitly as a superscript of
\equiv_{\eta}^{\mathcal{P}}
when it is clear
from the context.
Below, we show that
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
can be encoded into
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
so that the
latter is at least as expressive as the former.

{defi}

We define the encoding
\mathcal{E}
of
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
into
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l c l}\mathcal{E}(p)&=&p\\
\mathcal{E}(\neg\Phi)&=&\neg\mathcal{E}(\Phi)\end{array}\quad\quad\quad\quad\begin{array}[]{l c l}\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{1}\land\Phi_{2})&=&\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{1})\land\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{2})\\
\mathcal{E}(\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}))&=&\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{1})\land\gamma(\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{1}),\mathcal{E}(\Phi_{2}))\end{array}

\bullet

The following lemma is easily proven by structural induction on
\Phi

(see Appendix
A.4
).

Lemma 5
.

Let

\mathcal{P}=(P,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}})
, with
P=|K|
for some simplicial complex

,
be a polyhedral model,
x\in P
,
and
\Phi
a
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}

\mathcal{P},x\models\Phi
if and

\mathcal{P},x\models\mathcal{E}(\Phi)

A direct consequence of Lemma

{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}

Remark 9
.

We recall here that
\pm

finite posets are a subclass.
The reason why in Definition

and so it does not constitute a problem for the optimised model checking method
presented in this paper. In the rest of this section, as well as in Section

anyway be interested in poset models, so that we will restrict the relevant results to the latter.

Weak Simplicial Bisimilarity

In this section, we introduce weak versions of simplicial bisimilarity
and
\pm
-bisimilarity and we show that they coincide with logical
equivalence induced by
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
in polyhedral and poset models,
respectively.
We are looking for a notion of bisimilarity that enjoys the HMP with respect to

SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
, i.e. that coincides with
\equiv_{\eta}
.
We already know that simplicial bisimilarity
\sim_{\resizebox{372930}{}{$\triangle$}}
enjoys the HMP
with respect
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
, i.e.
\sim_{\resizebox{372930}{}{$\triangle$}}\,=\,\equiv_{\gamma}
and, moreover, that

\equiv_{\eta}
is weaker than
\equiv_{\gamma}
.
Here, by “weaker” we mean coarser, i.e. one that includes simplicial bisimilarity, in the sense of set inclusion,
\equiv_{\gamma}\,\subset\,\equiv_{\eta}
.

A natural step in the search for such a notion of bisimilarity is to reconsider the
definition of simplicial bisimilarity, recalled in Section
2.3

(see Definition
2.3
), and
seek to weaken its conditions.
Of course, the first condition cannot be relaxed in any meaningful way:
equivalent points must at least satisfy the same predicate letters.
Let us thus focus on the second
condition, namely the one concerning topological paths.
The condition requires
that as “one moves on”
\pi_{2}
using cursor
r
,
the corresponding point on
\pi_{1}
, i.e.
\pi_{1}(r)
, must be related by
Z
to the current point in
\pi_{2}
, namely
\pi_{2}(r)
. The points in
\pi_{2}
and
\pi_{1}
, while one moves the cursor
r
, must go “hand in hand” in
Z
.

One way of relaxing the above condition is to require only that (2.a) the ending points of

\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are related — i.e.
Z(\pi_{1}(1),\pi_{2}(1))
— and (2.b) for each
other point
y_{2}
of
\pi_{2}
, there is a point
y_{1}
of
\pi_{1}
, different from
\pi_{1}(1)
, such that

y_{1}
and
y_{2}
are related — i.e. for each
r_{2}\in[0,1)
there is
r_{1}\in[0,1)
such that

Z(\pi_{1}(r_{1}),\pi_{2}(r_{2}))
.

Interestingly, it turns out that the bisimilarity induced by a definition of bisimulation relation
where condition (2) is relaxed as above, coincides exactly with
\equiv_{\eta}
, the
logical equivalence induced by
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
!
In practice, we do not even need the notion of simplicial path, in the sense that the actual
definition, given below, is based on general topological paths and characterises an
equivalence relation — which we call
weak simplicial bisimilarity
,

\approx_{\resizebox{372930}{}{$\triangle$}}
— that coincides with
\equiv_{\eta}
, as guaranteed by Theorem
23
. The proof of this theorem, as well as those of all results related to
\approx_{\resizebox{372930}{}{$\triangle$}}

5.
Building the Minimal Model Modulo Logical Equivalence

In this section we present a minimisation procedure for finite poset
models modulo weak
\pm
-bisimilarity or, equivalently, modulo

\equiv_{\eta}
.
Given a finite poset model
\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
,
the procedure consists of three steps:

Step 1:
The poset model
\mathcal{F}
is encoded as an
LTS denoted
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
. The set of states
of
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
is
W
itself.
The encoding is such that it is ensured that logically equivalent
elements of
\mathcal{F}
are mapped into branching bisimilar states of
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
.
Thus, for

w_{1},w_{2}\in W
that are logically equivalent with respect to

SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
in the poset model
\mathcal{F}
, i.e.
w_{1}\equiv_{\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}w_{2}
,
we have that they are branching bisimilar as states in the
LTS
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
, i.e.
w_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[6.99997pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}
.

Step 2:
The LTS
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
is reduced modulo
branching bisimilarity using available software tools, such as

mCRL2

[
GJKW17
]
. This step yields the set of equivalence classes
of
W
for
\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[6.99997pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}
. Because of the correspondence
of logical equivalence and branching bisimilarity,
we obtain
W/{\equiv_{\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}}
.

Step 3:
The minimal model

\mathcal{F}_{\min}=(W_{\min},R_{\mkern 1.0mu\min},\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}})

is built. It turns out that this
model is not necessarily a poset model (see the example in
Figure
7a
).
However, it is a reflexive Kripke model
where

,
R_{\mkern 1.0mu\min}
is a
relation induced by the ordering
\preccurlyeq
of
\mathcal{F}
, and, most importantly,

SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
is preserved and reflected, i.e.
for each
w\in W
and
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
formula
\Phi
the following
holds:

\mathcal{F},w\models\Phi\mbox{ if and only if }\mathcal{F}_{\min},[w]_{\equiv_{\eta}}\models\Phi.

In the remainder of this section we focus on Step 1 and Step 3.

helps in the proofs to separate concerns related to the various

Suppose that

and

in

with respect to

LTS
\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})
is more abstract
than
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})

in the sense that all the nodes of
\mathcal{F}

that satisfy the same proposition letters and that are connected via

are mapped to the same state of
\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})

Thus, intuitively, a state of
\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})
corresponds to a class of states of

\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
. This is a class of states representing nodes

and

in
\mathcal{F}

for which “nothing changes” when moving from

to
w^{\prime}
,
as discussed above.
More precisely, define

\Theta=\{\,{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w\})\,\,|\,\,w\in W\,\}
and
consider, for
\alpha\in\Theta
, the
\alpha
-connected components
of
\mathcal{F}
. Then, each state
s
of
\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})
is an

\alpha
-connected component of
\mathcal{F}
, for some
\alpha
as
above. So, we group together all the
nodes in
W
that can reach one
another only via a path in
\mathcal{F}
composed of elements all satisfying
exactly the same proposition letters.
The above intuition
is formalised by the following definition.

{defi}

Given a finite poset model
\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
, we
define relation
{\rightleftharpoons}\subseteq{W\times W}
as the set of pairs

(w_{1},w_{2})
such that an undirected path
\pi
of some length
\ell

exists with
\pi(0)=w_{1},\pi(\ell)=w_{2}
, and

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{\pi(i)\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{\pi(j)\})
,
for all
i,j\in[0;\ell]
.
\bullet

The relevant definitions lead straightforwardly to the following
observation.

Proposition 28
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
be a finite poset
model. Then
\rightleftharpoons
is an equivalence relation on
W
. ∎

The encoding to the more “abstract” LTS is defined in Definition
5.1
below.
The states of
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
are the equivalence classes of
W
modulo the equivalence relation

\rightleftharpoons
, i.e.
S=W{/}{\rightleftharpoons}
. With reference to Figure

, we obtain two states,
namely
\{\widetilde{D},\widetilde{DE}\}
and
\{\widetilde{E},\widetilde{F},\widetilde{EF}\}

Figure

The set

of transition labels includes the powerset of the set of predicate letters in
\mathbf{2}^{{\tt PL}}
,
plus the two special labels
\mathbf{s},\mathbf{d}
.
In our example of Figure

L_{\ref{fig:encodeB}}=\{\emptyset,\{\mathbf{blue}\},\{\mathbf{red}\},\{\mathbf{blue},\mathbf{red}\},\mathbf{s},\mathbf{d}\}
.

Similarly to Rule (PLC) for the definition of
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
, Rule (PL) induces a self-loop
in each state of
\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})
(representing equivalence class)
[w]_{\rightleftharpoons}
.
This transition is labelled with the
set
of predicate letters
{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(w)
satisfied by the elements
of the class. Note that, by definition of

Transitions labelled by

(Rule (Down)) have the same interpretation as in the definition of

{defi}

Given a finite poset model
\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
, and

\mathbf{s},\mathbf{d}\notin{\tt PL}
, we define the LTS

\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})=(S,L,{\rightarrow})
where

•

the set
S
of states is the quotient
W{/}{\rightleftharpoons}
of
W

modulo
\rightleftharpoons
;

•

the set
L
of labels is

\mathbf{2}^{\mkern 1.0mu{\tt PL}}\cup\{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{d}\}
;

•

the transition relation is the smallest relation on

W\times L\times W
induced by the following transition rules:

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathrm{(PL)}\ [w]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w\})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w]_{\rightleftharpoons}\\
\\
\\
\mathrm{(Step)}\ \displaystyle{\frac{\,\,\,\,w\preccurlyeq^{\pm}w^{\prime}\,\,\,\,}{\,\,\,\,[w]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}\phantom{\mathbf{d}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\,\,\,}}\qquad\mathrm{(Down)}\ \displaystyle{\frac{\,\,\,\,w\succcurlyeq w^{\prime}\,\,\,\,}{\,\,\,\,[w]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\,\,\,}}\end{array}

\bullet

The following theorem ensures that any two elements
w_{1}

and
w_{2}
of a finite poset model
\mathcal{F}
are logically equivalent in

\mathcal{F}
with respect to
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
if and only if their equivalence
classes
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and
[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
are strongly bisimilar
in
\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})
.
The theorem uses the following lemma,
proven in Appendix

:

Lemma 29
.

Given a finite poset model
\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})

and
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
the following holds: if
w_{1}\rightleftharpoons w_{2}
, then

w_{1}\equiv_{\eta}w_{2}
.∎

Theorem 30
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
be a finite poset
model. For all
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
it holds that

w_{1}\equiv_{\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}w_{2}
if and only if

{[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}{[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}}
.

Proof 5.1
.

We first prove that if

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
then

w_{1}\equiv_{\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}w_{2}
. We proceed by induction on
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}

formulas and consider only the case
\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
, since
the other cases are straightforward. Suppose

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and

\mathcal{F},w_{1}\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
. Since

\mathcal{F},w_{1}\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
, there is (a
\pm
-path,
and so, by Proposition
10
) a

\downarrow
-path
\pi_{1}
from
w_{1}
of some length
\ell_{1}\geqslant 1

such that
\mathcal{F},\pi_{1}(\ell_{1})\models\Phi_{2}
and

\mathcal{F},\pi_{1}(i)\models\Phi_{1}
for all
i\in[0;\ell_{1})
. At this
point, we use induction on
\ell_{1}
, together with structural
induction on the formulas, for showing that also
\mathcal{F},w_{2}\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
holds.

Base case:

\ell_{1}=1
.

In this case we have
\mathcal{F},w_{1}\models\Phi_{1}
and

\mathcal{F},\pi_{1}(1)\models\Phi_{2}
, with
w_{1}\succcurlyeq\pi_{1}(1)
.
Moreover, by the Induction Hypothesis on formulas, we also have

\mathcal{F},w_{2}\models\Phi_{1}
. In addition, by Rule (Down), we get

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[\pi_{1}(1)]_{\rightleftharpoons}
. Since

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
by hypothesis, we also get

[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, for some

[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
with
[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim[\pi_{1}(1)]_{\rightleftharpoons}
. Note that, by definition of
{\rightleftharpoons}
and
since
[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, there is a
path
\pi^{\prime}_{2}
from
w_{2}
of some length
\ell^{\prime}_{2}
such that

\pi^{\prime}_{2}(j)\rightleftharpoons w_{2}
for all
j\in[0;\ell^{\prime}_{2}]

\pi^{\prime}_{2}(\ell^{\prime}_{2})\succcurlyeq w^{\prime\prime}_{2}
, with

w^{\prime\prime}_{2}\in[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
. Recalling that

\mathcal{F},w_{2}\models\Phi_{1}
, by Lemma
29
, we
also get that
\mathcal{F},\pi^{\prime}_{2}(j)\models\Phi_{1}
for all

j\in[0;\ell^{\prime}_{2}]
. Recalling also that

\mathcal{F},\pi_{1}(1)\models\Phi_{2}
, again by the Induction Hypothesis
on formulas, from
[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim[\pi_{1}(1)]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, we get

\mathcal{F},w^{\prime}_{2}\models\Phi_{2}
and, by Lemma
29
,
we also get
\mathcal{F},w^{\prime\prime}_{2}\models\Phi_{2}
. Consider now path

\pi_{2}:[0;\ell^{\prime}_{2}+1]\to W
defined as follows:

\pi_{2}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}\pi^{\prime}_{2}(j)&\text{if $j\in[0;\ell^{\prime}_{2}]$},\\
w^{\prime\prime}_{2}&\text{if $j=\ell^{\prime}_{2}+1$}.\end{array}\right.

Clearly,
\pi_{2}
is a
\downarrow
-path from
w_{2}
since
\pi^{\prime}_{2}
is an
undirected path and
\pi_{2}(\ell^{\prime}_{2})\succcurlyeq\pi_{2}(\ell^{\prime}_{2}+1)
. Furthermore, we have shown above that

\mathcal{F},\pi_{2}(\ell^{\prime}_{2}+1)\models\Phi_{2}
and

\mathcal{F},\pi_{2}(j)\models\Phi_{1}
for all
j\in[0;\ell^{\prime}_{2}+1)
.

Thus, we have that

\mathcal{F},w_{2}\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
, witnessed by
\pi_{2}
.

Induction step:
We assume the assertion holds for
\ell_{1}=n
, for
n\geqslant 1
and we show it holds for
\ell_{1}=n+1
.

Since
w_{1}\preccurlyeq^{\pm}\pi_{1}(1)
, by Rule (Step), we have
that
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[\pi_{1}(1)]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, and since, by

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, we also know that

[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
for some
w^{\prime}_{2}
such
that
[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim[\pi_{1}(1)]_{\rightleftharpoons}
. Furthermore,

\mathcal{F},\pi_{1}(1)\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
since

\ell_{1}\geqslant 2
and that this is witnessed by
\pi_{1}\uparrow 1
,
which is a
\downarrow
-path of length
n
. Thus, by the Induction Hypothesis
on
\ell_{1}
, we get that
\mathcal{F},w^{\prime}_{2}\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})

since
[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim[\pi_{1}(1)]_{\rightleftharpoons}
(see above).
From
[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, by Rule (Step),
we know that
w\in[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and
w^{\prime}\in[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
exist

w\preccurlyeq^{\pm}w^{\prime}
. Since
w\in[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

an undirected path
\pi^{\prime}_{2}
exists from
w_{2}
to
w
, of some
length
\ell^{\prime}_{2}
, such that
\pi^{\prime}_{2}(j)\rightleftharpoons w_{2}
for all

j\in[0;\ell^{\prime}_{2}]
. By the Induction Hypothesis on formulas, we know
that
\mathcal{F},w_{2}\models\Phi_{1}
, and so, by
Lemma
29
, we get also

\mathcal{F},\pi^{\prime}_{2}(j)\models\Phi_{1}
for all
j\in[0;\ell^{\prime}_{2}]
. Moreover, since

\mathcal{F},w^{\prime}_{2}\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
(see above) and

w^{\prime}\rightleftharpoons w^{\prime}_{2}
, again by Lemma

, we get

\mathcal{F},w^{\prime}\models\eta(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})
. This means that there is a

\pm
-path
\pi^{\prime\prime}_{2}
from
w^{\prime}
of some length

as follows:

. It is easy to see that
\pi_{2}

\downarrow
-path witnessing

.

Now we prove that if
w_{1}\equiv_{\eta}^{\mathcal{F}}w_{2}
then

. We do this

on

is a strong

B=\{(s_{1},s_{2})\in S\times S\,|\,\text{there are $w_{1}\in s_{1}$, $w_{2}\in s_{2}$ such that $w_{1}\equiv_{\eta}w_{2}$}\}.

Let, without loss of generality,
s_{1}=[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and

s_{2}=[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, for some
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
with

w_{1}\equiv_{\eta}w_{2}
and suppose
B([w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons},[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons})
,
with
w_{1}\equiv_{\eta}w_{2}
.
We distinguish three cases:

Case A:

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
with

\alpha\in\mathbf{2}^{{\tt PL}}
.

If
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
for some

and

[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}=[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
. Furthermore, since

.

and, by hypothesis

Case B:

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

If
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

. Moreover, since, by

\pm

Case C:

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

get
B([w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons},[\pi(k)]_{\rightleftharpoons})
.

The following theorem ensures that
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

and
[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
are strongly bisimilar in
\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})
if
and only if
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are branching bisimilar
in
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
. The theorem uses the following
lemma, proven in Appendix
A.16
:

Lemma 31
.

Consider a finite poset model

\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
. Then for all

w_{1},w_{2}\in W
the following holds: if

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, then

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})
. ∎

Theorem 32
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(W,\preccurlyeq,\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{F}})
be a finite poset
model. For all
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
it holds that

{[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}{[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}}
if and
only if
w_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}
.

Proof 5.2
.

We first prove that if

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
then

w_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}
. We show that the following
relation is a branching bisimulation:

B_{C}=\{(w_{1},w_{2})\in{W\times W}\,|\,[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\}.

Let us assume
B_{C}(w_{1},w_{2})
. We have to consider a few cases:

Case A:

w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w_{1}
.

If
w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w_{1}
, then, by Rule (PLC), we have

p\in{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})
. By definition of
B_{C}
and by
hypothesis we know that
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and so,
by Lemma
31
, we get

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})
. It
follows then that
p\in{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})
and, again by
Rule (PLC), we finally get
w_{2}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w_{2}
, which is the required
mimicking step since
B(w_{1},w_{2})
.

Case B:

w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
.

If
w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
for some
w^{\prime}_{1}\in W
, then, by Rule (TAU),
we know that
w_{1}\preccurlyeq^{\pm}w^{\prime}_{1}
, with

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{1}\})
, which,
by definition of
\rightleftharpoons
, means
[w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}=[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

and since
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
by
definition of
B_{C}
, given that
B_{C}(w_{1},w_{2})
, we get

[w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
. This, in
turn, again by definition of
B_{C}
, means
B_{C}(w^{\prime}_{1},w_{2})
.

Case C:

w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{c}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
.

If
w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{c}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
for some
w^{\prime}_{1}\in W
, then, by Rule (CNG),
we know that
w_{1}\preccurlyeq^{\pm}w^{\prime}_{1}
, with

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})\not={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{1}\})
, and,
by Rule (Step), we have
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
.
Since, by definition of
B_{C}
and by hypothesis,

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, we also have

[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
for some

[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
. From

[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, by Rule (Step), we know
there are
w_{3}\in[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and
w^{\prime}_{3}\in[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

such that
w_{3}\preccurlyeq^{\pm}w^{\prime}_{3}
. By
Lemma
31
, since

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
by hypothesis
and
[w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
(see
above), we have

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})
and

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{1}\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{2}\})
and
since
{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})\not={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{1}\})

(see above), we get

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})\not={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{1}\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{2}\})
.
Consequently, since
w_{3}\in[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and

w^{\prime}_{3}\in[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, we also finally get that

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{3}\})\not={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{3}\})
.
Thus, by rule (CNG), we know that
w_{3}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{c}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{3}
. Now, since

w_{3}\in[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, by definition of
\rightleftharpoons
and by
construction of
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
we know there are

s_{0},\ldots s_{n}\in W
with
s_{0}=w_{2}
,
s_{n}=w_{3}
such that

s_{i}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s_{i+1}
and
s_{i+1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s_{i}
, for all

. We note that
B_{C}(w_{1},s_{i})
for all
i\in[0;n]
. In
fact for each
i\in[0;n]
we have that

[s_{i}]_{\rightleftharpoons}=[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
by definition of
\rightleftharpoons
and we
also know that

[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

by hypothesis. Thus we get

[s_{i}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
,
i.e.
B_{C}(w_{1},s_{i})
. Furthermore, we also note that
B_{C}(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{3})
.
In fact

, since

[w^{\prime}_{3}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
,
i.e.
B_{C}(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{3})
. In conclusion, we have that if

w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{c}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
for some
w^{\prime}_{1}\in W
, then

w_{2}=s_{0}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\ldots\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s_{n}=w_{3}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{c}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{3}
with

B_{C}(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{3})
and

for all
i\in[0;n]
.

Case D:

w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
.

If
w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
for some

, then, by Rule (DWN),
we know that

, and, by Rule (Down), we have

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
. Since, by definition
of
B_{C}
and by hypothesis,

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, we also have

[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
for some

[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w^{\prime}_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
.
From
[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, by Rule (Down), we
know there are
w_{3}\in[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and

w^{\prime}_{3}\in[w^{\prime}_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
such that
w_{3}\succcurlyeq w^{\prime}_{3}
and, by
Rule (DWN) we know that
w_{3}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{3}
. Now, since

w_{3}\in[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
, by definition of
\rightleftharpoons
and by
construction of
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})
we know there are

s_{0},\ldots s_{n}\in W
with
s_{0}=w_{2}
,
s_{n}=w_{3}
such that

s_{i}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s_{i+1}
and

for all

. In

\rightleftharpoons

, since

by hypothesis. Thus we get

[s_{i}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

,
i.e.
B_{C}(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{3})
. In conclusion, we have that if

w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
for some
w^{\prime}_{1}\in W
, then

w_{2}=s_{0}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\ldots\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,s_{n}=w_{3}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{3}
with

B_{C}(w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{3})
and
B_{C}(w_{1},s_{i})
for all
i\in[0;n]
.

We now prove that if

w_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}
, then

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\sim^{\mathbb{S}_{A}(\mathcal{F})}[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
.
We show that the following relation is a strong bisimulation:

B_{A}=\{(s_{1},s_{2})\in S\times S\,|\,\text{there are $w_{1}\in s_{1}$, $w_{2}\in s_{2}$ such that $w_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}$}\}.

Let, without loss of generality,
s_{1}=[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and

s_{2}=[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
for some
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
with

w_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}
, and suppose

B_{A}([w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons},[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons})
.
We distinguish three cases:

Case A:

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
with

\alpha\in\mathbf{2}^{{\tt PL}}
:

By Rule (PL), if
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
for
\alpha\in\mathbf{2}^{{\tt PL}}
and
w^{\prime}_{1}\in W
, then
[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}=[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and
\alpha={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{1}\})
.
On the one hand, if
p\in\alpha
then
w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w_{1}
by
rule (PLC).
Since
w_{2}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{1}

w_{2}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\ldots\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\bar{w}_{2}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{2}
for

such that

,

\bar{w}_{2}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{1}
, and

w^{\prime}_{2}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{1}
.
By rule (TAU),
p\in{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})
.
Thus,
\alpha\subseteq{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})
.
On the other hand, if
p\in{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})
then
w_{2}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w_{2}
by rule (PLC).
Since
w_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}
we have that
w_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\ldots\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\bar{w}_{1}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{1}
for
\bar{w}_{1},w^{\prime}_{1}\in W

such that
p\in{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{\bar{w}_{1}\})
,
\bar{w}_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}
,
w^{\prime}_{1}\mathrel{\,\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\underline{\makebox[7.15547pt]{$\leftrightarrow$}}$}\,}_{b}^{\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})}w_{2}
.
By rule (TAU) we obtain that
p\in{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{\bar{w}_{1}\})
.
Thus,
p\in\alpha
.
Hence,
{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})\subseteq\alpha
.
So,
{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{2}\})=\alpha
.
Therefore,
[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
by
rule (PL).
By assumption,
B_{A}([w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons},[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons})
for target states

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
and
[w_{2}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
as required.

Case B:

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

, since

, there are

.

Case C:

[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}

If
[w_{1}]_{\rightleftharpoons}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,[w_{1}^{\prime}]_{\rightleftharpoons}
for some

w^{\prime}_{1}\in W
, then, by Rule (Step), we know that there are

We distinguish two cases:

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{3}\})={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{3}\})
.

If

. But then, by

.

Case C2:

{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{3}\})\not={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{3}\})
.

If
{\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w_{3}\})\not={\mathcal{V}}^{-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\{w^{\prime}_{3}\})
,
then, by Rule (CNG), we know
w_{3}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{c}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,w^{\prime}_{3}
. By definition
of
\rightleftharpoons
and by construction of
\mathbb{S}_{\mkern 1.0muC}(\mathcal{F})

8.
Conclusions

Polyhedral models are widely used in domains that exploit mesh processing such as 3D computer graphics. These models are typically huge, consisting of very many cells. Spatial model checking of such models is an interesting, novel approach to verify properties of such models and to visualise the results in a graphically appealing way. In previous work the polyhedral model checker
PolyLogicA
was developed for this purpose
[
BCG+22
]
.

In
[
BCG+22
]
simplicial bisimilarity was proposed for polyhedral models — i.e. models of continuous space — while
\pm
-bisimilarity, the corresponding equivalence for cell-poset models — discrete representations of polyhedral models — was first introduced in
[
CGL+23a
]
.
In order to support large model reductions, in this paper the novel notions of weak simplicial bisimilarity and weak
\pm
-bisimilarity have been presented, and the correspondence between the two has been studied.
We have also presented
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
, a weaker version of the Spatial Logic for Closure Spaces on polyhedral models, and we have shown that simplicial bisimilarity enjoys the Hennessy-Milner property (Theorem
23
). Furthermore, we have shown that the property holds for
\pm
-bisimilarity on poset models and the interpretation of
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
on such models (Theorem
26
).

SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
can be used in the geometric spatial model checker
PolyLogicA
for checking spatial reachability properties of polyhedral models.
Model checking results can be visualised by projecting them onto the original polyhedral structure, showing in a specific colour all the cells satisfying the property of interest.

In order to reduce model checking time and computing resources, we have proposed an effective procedure that computes the minimal model, modulo logical equivalence with respect to the logic
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
, of a polyhedral model. Such minimised models are also amenable to model checking with a variant of
PolyLogicA
dealing with general Kripke models and with the
\eta
modality.

The procedure has been formalised and proven correct. A prototype implementation of the procedure has been developed in the form of a toolchain, that also involves operations provided by the
mCRL2
toolset, to study the practical feasibility of the approach and to identify possible bottlenecks.
We have also shown how the model checking results of the minimal model can be projected back onto the original polyhedral model. This provides a direct 3D visual inspection of the results through the polyhedra visualizer
PolyVisualizer
.

In future work we aim at a more sophisticated implementation of the procedure, possibly using in a more direct way the minimisation operations provided by
mCRL2
and integrating the various steps in the procedure.
Such an implementation, would also enable us to experiment applying our methodology and supporting tools to real-world case studies.
On the theoretical side, an interesting issue that is beyond the scope of the present paper, and that
we would like to address in future work, is the relationship between
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
,
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
, and
\Diamond
.
Finally, we would be interested in extending
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\eta}
/
SLCS
{}_{\mkern 1.0mu\gamma}
with additional operators, for example those concerning notions of distance, and in applying our spatial model checking framework to a larger number of case studies.

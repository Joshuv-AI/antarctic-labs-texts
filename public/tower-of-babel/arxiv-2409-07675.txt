Self-Reachable Configuration Polytopes for Trees Thanks: The first author was a participant at the Rose-Hulman REU funded by NSF DMS #1852132 and advised by the second author. The second author was partially supported by NSF DMS # 2418532.
Benjamin Lyons, McCabe Olsen

Abstract
. We study lattice polytopes which arise as the convex hull of chip vectors for self-reachable chip configurations on a tree T T . We show that these polytopes always have the integer decomposition property and characterize the vertex sets of these polytopes. Additionally, in the case of self-reachable configurations with the smallest possible number of chips, we show that these polytopes are unimodularly equivalent to a unit cube.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 52B20, 05C57, 05C05

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a graph.
The chip firing game on
G
is a rather simple way to explain a discrete dynamical system on the graph: To each vertex, associate a nonnegative integer (often viewed as a commodity such as poker chips). Then choosing a vertex which has at least as many chips as its degree, we “fire” the vertex. That is, we transfer one chip to each of its neighbors.
We then continue this process as long as we want or are able to.
The notion of chip firing developed both as a combinatorial game (see, e.g.
[
1
,
4
,
7
]
) and via the abelian sandpile model, which was first studied by Bak, Tang, and Wisenfeld (see
[
2
]
).
The mathematics of chip firing is quite rich encompassing combinatorics, statistical physics, and algebraic geometry via Riemann-Roch theory (see
[
5
]
for a broad overview of chip firing).

In this paper, we study a family of lattice polytopes which arise as the convex hull certain chip configurations, called
self-reachable configurations
, which was first defined and explored in
[
6
]
.
For a tree
T
, we denote the convex hull of self-reachable configurations with
\ell
chips by
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
.
In Section
2
, we discuss all necessary background for graph theory, chip firing, self-reachable configurations, and convex polytopes.
Section
3
explores the polytopes
\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}
which arise from the self-reachable configurations with the fewest number of chips possible.
We show that these polytopes are always unimodularly equivalent to unit cubes.
In Section
4
, we show that for any tree
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
has the integer decomposition property. Finally, Section
5
characterizes the vertices of the lattice polytopes
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
.

2.
Background

We begin by setting notation which will be used throughout.

•

For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the sequences
\mathbb{N}_{a}=\{a,a+1,a+2,\ldots\}
, and
\mathbb{N}_{[a,b]}=\{a,a+1,a+2,\ldots,b\}
.

•

Given two finite sequences
\Phi
and
\Psi
, we let
\{\Phi,\Psi\}
be the sequence obtained by appending the sequence
\Psi
to the end of the sequence
\Phi
.

•

For a vector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we clarify the dimension of
\bm{x}
by writing
\bm{x}^{(n)}
.

•

Given vectors
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bm{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we let

(\bm{x},\bm{y})=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}.

•

Unless otherwise specified, we will label the vertices of an
n
-vertex graph as
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}
. Throughout this paper, we will only work with graphs that have at least one vertex.

•

We will let
\deg^{(G)}(v_{a})
refer to the degree of the vertex
v_{a}
on the graph
G
.
When the graph is clear from context, we will denote this as simply
\deg(v_{a})
.

•

We will denote the vertex set of a graph
G
by
V(G)
.

We will now review some basic definitions from graph theory and chip firing.
For additional background and detail beyond what is provided here, we refer the reader to the excellent book of Klivans
[
5
]
.
Let a simple graph
G
on
n
vertices.
Recall that the
Laplacian matrix
of
G
, denoted
\Delta(G)
, is the
n\times n
matrix given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}(G)=\begin{cases}-1&i\neq j\text{ and }(v_{i},v_{j})\in E\\
\deg(v_{i})&i=j\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

A
chip configuration
on
G
is a vector
\bm{c}^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, where we interpret
c_{i}
as the number of chips on vertex
v_{i}
.
If
G
has
n
vertices with chip configuration
\bm{c}^{(n)}
, the vector
\bm{d}^{(n)}
that results from
firing
the
i
th vertex
v_{i}
is

\displaystyle\bm{d}^{(n)}=\bm{c}^{(n)}-\Delta(G)\bm{e}_{i}^{(n)}.

This has the effect of moving a chip from
v_{i}
to each vertex neighboring
v_{i}
.
We say that it is
legal
to fire
v_{i}
on
G
starting from
\bm{c}^{(n)}
if
\bm{d}^{(n)}
is a chip configuration, which is to say the
i
th component of
\bm{d}^{(n)}
remains nonnegative.
Equivalently, it is legal to fire
v_{i}
provided
\bm{e}_{i}^{(n)}\cdot\bm{c}^{(n)}\geq\deg(v_{i})
.
A
firing sequence
is a sequence of integers which represents a sequence of legal chip firings.
Throughout this paper, firing sequences will be assumed to be finite.
Given a starting configuration
\bm{c}^{(n)}
and a firing sequence
\Phi
, the resulting configuration is

(2.1)

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{\Phi}^{(G)}\left(\bm{c}^{(n)}\right)=\bm{c}^{(n)}-\Delta(G)\sum_{j\in\Phi}\bm{e}_{j}^{(n)}.

We say that a firing sequence
\Phi=\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{m}\}
is
legal
on
G
starting from a chip configuration
\bm{c}^{(n)}
if it consists entirely of legal vertex firings.
That is, it is legal to fire
v_{\phi_{1}}
on
G
starting from
\bm{c}^{(n)}
, it is legal to fire
v_{\phi_{2}}
on
G
starting from
\bm{F}_{\{\phi_{1}\}}^{(G)}\left(\bm{c}^{(n)}\right)
, it is legal to fire
v_{\phi_{3}}
on
G
starting from
\bm{F}_{\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\}}^{(G)}\left(\bm{c}^{(n)}\right)
, and so on. We let
R^{(G)}\left(\bm{c}^{(n)}\right)
denote the set of chip configurations on
G
that can be reached via nonempty legal firing sequences on
G
starting from
\bm{c}^{(n)}
.

2.1.
Self-Reachable Chip Configurations

In this subsection, we will review the notion of a self-reachable configuration.
For additional background and for proofs of any results stated within, please consult
[
6
]
.
We begin with the definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a connected simple
n
-vertex graph.
The chip configuration
\bm{s}^{(n)}
is called
self-reachable
on
G
if
\bm{s}^{(n)}\in R^{(G)}\left(\bm{s}^{(n)}\right)
.
Wet
S_{\ell}^{(G)}
denote set of all self-reachable configurations on
G
with exactly
\ell
chips.

The following result applies to any connected, simple graph equipped with a self-reachable configuration.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected simple
n
-vertex graph, and let
\bm{s}^{(n)}
be a self-reachable configuration on
G
. Let
\bm{c}^{(n)}
be a chip configuration on
G
such that
\bm{c}^{(n)}
has at least as many chips on each vertex of
G
as
\bm{s}^{(n)}
. Then
\bm{c}^{(n)}
is self-reachable on
G
.

The following collection of results specifically pertain to self-reachable configurations on trees.
These results in particular will be useful in our eventual discussion of polytopes.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree. Then the configuration
\bm{c}^{(n)}
is self-reachable on
T
if and only if
\bm{c}^{(n)}
has at least
m-1
chips on every
m
-vertex subtree of
T
.

Corollary 2.4

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree, and let
\bm{s}^{(n)}
be a self-reachable configuration on
T
. Then the chip configuration formed by
\bm{s}^{(n)}
on any subtree
T^{*}
of
T
must be self-reachable on
T^{*}
.

Corollary 2.5

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree with
n\geq 2
where
v_{n}
is a leaf connected to
v_{n-1}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\bm{s}^{(n-1)}
is a self-reachable configuration on
T\setminus v_{n}
.

(2)

\left(\bm{s}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)
is a self-reachable configuration on
T
.

(3)

\left(\bm{s}^{(n-1)},1\right)
is a self-reachable configuration on
T
.

We close this subsection by citing an enumerative result for self-reachable configurations on trees.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree and let
\displaystyle C_{\ell,n}\coloneq\left|S_{\ell}^{(T)}\right|
.
Then
C_{\ell,n}
satisfies recurrence relation for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle C_{\ell,n}=C_{\ell-1,n}+2C_{\ell-1,n-1}-C_{\ell-2,n-1}.

with
C_{\ell,1}=1
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
C_{0,n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{2}
,
C_{1,2}=2
, and
C_{1,n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{3}
.

2.2.
Convex Polytopes

We conclude with some brief background on polyhedral geometry. A
polytope

P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is convex hull of finitely many
\bm{x}_{1}^{(n)},\bm{x}_{2}^{(n)},\ldots,\bm{x}_{k}^{(n)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. That is,

P=\text{conv}\left(\left\{\bm{x}_{1}^{(n)},\bm{x}_{2}^{(n)},\ldots,\bm{x}_{k}^{(n)}\right\}\right)\coloneq\left\{\bm{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\bm{x}_{i}^{(n)}\ :\ 0\leq\gamma_{i}\leq 1\mbox{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}=1\right\}

The inclusion-minimal set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
P=\text{conv}(V)
is called the
vertex set
of
P
.
A polytope
P
is called
lattice
(or
integral
) if
V\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
For
t\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
, the
t
th dilation
of
P
is the set
tP\coloneq\left\{t\bm{x}^{(n)}:\bm{x}^{(n)}\in P\right\}
.
A lattice polytope
P
has the
integer decomposition property
if for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and
\bm{w}^{(n)}\in tP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, there exist
\bm{x}_{1}^{(n)},\bm{x}_{2}^{(n)},\ldots,\bm{x}_{t}^{(n)}\in P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with

\bm{w}^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\bm{x}_{j}^{(n)}.

We say that two polytopes
P
and
Q
are
unimodularly equivalent
if there exist
U\in\text{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\bm{b}^{(n)}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
Q=UP+\bm{b}^{(n)}
. For further background and details on the basics of lattice polytopes, we encourage the reader to see
[
3
]
.

In this paper, we will consider the polytopes which are generated as the convex hulls of self-reachable configurations on trees. For a tree
T
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
, define

\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}\coloneq\text{conv}\left(S_{\ell}^{(T)}\right).

We call these polytopes
self-reachable configuration polytopes
.
One should note if
T
has
n
vertices that
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
will have dimension at most
n-1
, as these polytopes by construction lie on the hyperplane
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\ell
.

3.
Minimally Self-Reachable Configurations and Unit Cubes

In this section, we first consider a simple case of
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
. Specifically, we will consider these polytopes for minimally self-reachable configurations, which are defined as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
T
be a
n
-vertex tree, and let
\bm{\mu}^{(n)}
be a self-reachable configuration on
T
. We say that
\bm{\mu}^{(n)}
is a
minimally self-reachable configuration
on
T
if
\bm{\mu}^{(n)}
is self-reachable on
G
and has exactly
n-1
chips on
T
.

The term minimally self-reachable is chosen because
any
configuration on an
n
-vertex tree with
\ell\leq n-2
chips will never be self-reachable by Theorem
2.3
.
Additionally, one should note that a chip configuration with
n-1
chips need not necessarily be a self-reachable configuration.
An easy example of this behavior would be to consider any tree
T
with
n\geq 3
vertices where all chips are placed on a leaf.
That being said, we can more precisely ascertain necessary conditions for minimally self-reachable configurations as in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree, and let
\bm{\mu}^{(n)}
be a minimally self-reachable configuration on
T
. Then on any vertex of
T
,
\bm{\mu}^{(n)}
cannot have any more chips than the degree of that vertex. Equivalently, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,n]}
,

\bm{e}_{i}^{(n)}\cdot\bm{\mu}^{(n)}\leq\deg(v_{i}).

Proof.

Suppose towards contradiction that on some vertex
v_{i}
,

\bm{e}_{i}^{(n)}\cdot\bm{\mu}^{(n)}>d,

where we let
d=\deg(v_{i})
. The graph
T\setminus v_{i}
will contain
d
disjoint trees, which we will label
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{d}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,n]}
, let
m_{j}
be the number of vertices on
T_{j}
. Since
\bm{\mu}^{(n)}
is self-reachable on
T
,
\bm{\mu}^{(n)}
must contain at least
m_{j}-1
chips on
T_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,n]}
by Theorem
2.3
. Therefore, the number of chips in
\bm{\mu}^{(n)}
has on
T
satisfies the following bound:

(3.1)

\bm{1}^{(n)}\cdot\bm{\mu}^{(n)}>d+\sum_{j=1}^{d}(m_{j}-1)=d+(n-1)-d=n-1.

This contradicts the minimally self-reachable configuration assumption.

∎

Since minimally self-reachable configurations are much more restrictive than general self-reachable configurations, we can explicitly enumerate them.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree. Then

\left|S_{n-1}^{(T)}\right|=2^{n-1}.

Proof.

Adopting notation from Theorem
2.6
, we have that
\left|S_{n-1}^{(T)}\right|=C_{n-1,n}
. By Theorem
2.6
,

\displaystyle C_{n-1,n}=C_{n-2,n}+2C_{n-2,n-1}-C_{n-3,n-1}.

By Theorem
2.3
,
C_{n-2,n}=C_{n-3,n-1}=0
.
This reduces the recurrence to
C_{n-1,n}=2C_{n-2,n-1}
and the result follows by the initial conditions
C_{0,1}=1
and
C_{1,2}=2
given in Theorem
2.6
.

∎

In terms of the polytope
\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}
, we can provide the following explicit description via an unimodular equivalence with a well-known polytope: the unit cube.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree. Then
\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}\cong[0,1]^{n-1}\times\{0\}
.

Proof.

Notice that when
n=1
,
\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}=\{0\}
.
For
n\geq 2
, without loss of generality, suppose that
v_{n}
is a leaf connected to
v_{n-1}
.
Note that it sufficient to show

(3.2)

\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}\cong\mathcal{CP}_{n-2}^{(T\setminus v_{n})}\times[0,1]

as the result will follow by induction.
For simplicity of notation, denote
Q=\mathcal{CP}_{n-2}^{(T\setminus v_{n})}\times[0,1]
.
By Lemma
3.3
, we have that
\left|S_{n-2}^{(T\setminus v_{n})}\right|=2^{n-2}
. Hence, we let

\displaystyle S_{n-2}^{(T\setminus v_{n})}=\left\{\bm{s}_{1}^{(n-1)},\bm{s}_{2}^{(n-1)},\ldots\bm{s}_{2^{n-2}}^{(n-1)}\right\}.

Note that an arbitrary element of
Q
can be represented as

(3.3)

\displaystyle\bm{y}^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},0\right),

where
0\leq\kappa_{i},\lambda_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,2^{n-2}]}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}=1.

We now let

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\Big\{\left(\bm{s}_{1}^{(n-1)},1\right),\left(\bm{s}_{2}^{(n-1)},1\right),\ldots,\left(\bm{s}_{2^{n-2}}^{(n-1)},1\right),

\displaystyle\left(\bm{s}_{1}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right),\left(\bm{s}_{2}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right),\ldots,\left(\bm{s}_{2^{n-2}}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)\Big\}.

By Corollary
2.5
, we have that
S_{n-1}^{(T)}\subseteq A
. Note that all of the chip configurations in
A
are distinct because the
\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)}
s are distinct. So
A
contains
2^{n-1}
distinct self-reachable configurations on
T
, each with
n-1
chips. Therefore, Lemma
3.3
implies that
S_{n-1}^{(T)}=A
.

We now let
U=I_{n}+E_{n-1,n}
, where
I_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix and
E_{i,j}
denotes the
n\times n
matrix with a 1 in row
i
column
j
and 0s in all other entries, and we let
\bm{b}^{(n)}=-\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n)}
. We claim that for this choice of
U
and
\bm{b}^{(n)}
,
Q=U\left(\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}\right)+\bm{b}^{(n)}
. We let
\bm{x}^{(n)}\in\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}
be arbitrary. Then

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right),

where
0\leq\kappa_{i},\lambda_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{1}^{2^{n-2}}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}=1.

We have that

(3.4)

\begin{split}U\bm{x}^{(n)}&=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}U\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}U\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},0\right)+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n)}.\end{split}

Therefore,

(3.5)

\displaystyle U\bm{x}^{(n)}+\bm{b}^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},0\right).

Note that the right-hand side of (
3.4
) has the same form as the right-hand side of (
3.3
), which is an element of
Q
. Thus,
U\bm{x}^{(n)}+\bm{b}^{(n)}\in Q
, which implies that
U\left(\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}\right)+\bm{b}^{(n)}\subseteq Q
. We now let

\displaystyle\bm{y}^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},0\right)\in Q

be arbitrary as in (
3.3
). Then by (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we have that for

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\kappa_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)},1\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\left(\bm{s}_{i}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)\in\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)},

\bm{y}^{(n)}=U\bm{x}^{(n)}+\bm{b}^{(n)}
. This implies that
Q\subseteq U\left(\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}\right)+\bm{b}^{(n)}
. Thus, we obtain
Q=U\left(\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}\right)+\bm{b}^{(n)}
, as desired.

∎

We conclude this section by noting that Theorem
3.4
implies that
\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}
has the integer decomposition property (c.f.
[
3
]
).
Additionally, since the unit cube is a
0/1
polytope, we ascertain the vertex structure of
\mathcal{CP}_{n-1}^{(T)}
, namely that every minimally self-reachable configuration is a vertex.
In general, proving the integer decomposition property and describing the vertex structure is more involved and will be discussed in Sections
4
and
5
respectively.

4.
The Integer Decomposition Property

In this section, we study the integer decomposition property for
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
.
In particular, we will build towards the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
n
-vertex tree
T
and any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{n-1}
, the polytope
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
has the integer decomposition property.

To begin, we must introduce some new terminology.
Given an
n
-vertex tree
T
equipped with chip configuration
\bm{s}^{(n)}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
, we say that
\bm{s}^{(n)}
is a
t
-dilated configuration
on
T
if
\bm{s}^{(n)}
contains at least
t(m-1)
chips on every
m
-vertex subtree of
T
. Observe that
\bm{s}^{(n)}
is self-reachable if and only if
\bm{s}^{(n)}
is 1-dilated by Theorem
2.3
.
We say that
\bm{s}^{(n)}
is
minimally
t
-dilated
on
T
if
\bm{s}^{(n)}
is
t
-dilated on
T
and has exactly
t(n-1)
chips on
T
.
The inspiration for the name
t
-dilated derives from the observation of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree. For each
t\in\mathbb{N}_{1}
and for all
\bm{x}^{(n)}\in t\cdot\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\bm{x}^{(n)}
is a
t
-dilated configuration on
T
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}=\left\{\bm{s}_{1}^{(n)},\bm{s}_{2}^{(n)},\ldots,\bm{s}_{k}^{(n)}\right\}
. By definition,

\bm{x}^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\left(t\bm{s}_{i}^{(n)}\right)=t\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\bm{s}_{i}^{(n)}

with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,n]}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=1
. Now choose an arbitrary subtree of
T
with
m
vertices and let
\bm{\chi}^{(n)}
be the characteristic vector of this subtree. Then by Theorem
2.3
,

.
It is clear from Corollary

In order to answer this question, we need a definition.

Definition 5.1

To this end, we begin with following result characterizing such configurations.

if and only if:

Without loss of generality, we suppose that

.
We let

be the trees in

.
For sufficiency, assume that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
is near-minimally self-reachable about

.
Let

be an arbitrary tree in

.
Because

cannot be self-reachable on

for all

, there must exist a set

of subtrees of

such that

•

Each vertex of
T^{*}
is contained in

for some

, and

•

\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
forms a minimally self-reachable configuration on

for all

.

Note that none of the subtrees of

in

.

The

must form a minimally self-reachable configuration on
T^{*}
.
Since

was arbitrarily selected, we conclude that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
forms a minimally self-reachable configuration on each tree in

. So if we let

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}(m_{j}-1)=n-1-d

The following lemma provides another useful characterization of near-minimally self-reachable configurations. Specifically, this result specifies that there must be a vertex with the maximum possible chips to maintain self-reachability.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree. For any vertex
v_{i}
of
T
, any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, and any self-reachable configuration
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
on
T
with
\ell
chips,
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
is a near-minimally self-reachable configuration on
T
about
v_{t}
if and only if
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
has exactly
\ell+d+1-n
of chips on
v_{t}
(the maximum possible number given that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
is self-reachable on
T
), where
d=\deg(v_{t})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we suppose that
t=n
.
We let
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{d}
be the trees in
T\setminus v_{n}
, and we let
m_{j}
be the number of chips on
T_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,m]}
.
Note that since
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
is self-reachable,
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
must have at least
m_{j}-1
chips on
T_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,m]}
.
To prove sufficiency, we assume that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
is a near-minimally self-reachable configuration on
T
about
v_{n}
with
\ell
chips.
In the proof of Lemma
5.4
, we saw that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
must have exactly

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}(m_{j}-1)=n-1-d

chips on the vertices excluding
v_{n}
. Therefore, there must be
\ell-(n-1-d)=\ell+d+1-n
chips on
v_{n}
.
We cannot have fewer than
n-1-d
chips on the trees in
T\setminus v_{n}
or else we couldn’t have
m_{j}-1
chips on
T_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,n]}
.
Therefore,
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
can have a maximum of
\ell+d+1-n
chips on
v_{n}
.

To prove necessity, we assume that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
has exactly
\ell+d+1-n
chips on
v_{n}
. Then
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
has exactly

\displaystyle n-1-d=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(m_{j}-1)

total chips on the trees
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{d}
. Because
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
is self-reachable on
T
, this means that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
must have
m_{j}-1
chips on
T_{j}
and form a minimally self-reachable configuration on
T_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,n]}
. So
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
forms a minimally self-reachable configuration on each tree in
T\setminus v_{n}
, and
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
has at least
d+1
chips on
v_{n}
since
\ell\geq n
. So by Lemma
5.4
,
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
is a near-minimally self-reachable configuration on
T
about
v_{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Given a near-minimally self-reachable configuration about a vertex
v_{t}
, one preserves this property when adjusting the configuration by adding chips to
v_{t}
.
This is verified via the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree. Suppose that

is a near-minimally self-reachable configuration on
T
about
v_{t}
with
\ell
chips for some

. Then

\ell+L

.

Let

. Note that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}+L\bm{e}_{t}^{(n)}
is self-reachable on

because adding chips to the self-reachable configuration
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
must yield another self-reachable configuration on

. By Lemma
5.5
,
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}
has exactly
\ell+d+1-n

. But since
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}+L\bm{e}_{t}^{(n)}

\ell+L

5.5
implies that
\bm{\nu}^{(n)}+L\bm{e}_{t}^{(n)}
is a near-minimally self-reachable configuration about
v_{t}
with
\ell+L

The following lemma demonstrates how to preserve the property of near-minimally self-reachable when adding a new leaf vertex to an existing tree.

Lemma 5.7
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree where
v_{n}
is a leaf connected to
v_{n-1}
. Suppose that
\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}

with
\ell
chips for some

(1)

\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)},1\right)

about

with
\ell+1
chips.

(2)

If
t=n-1

\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}+L\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)

T
about
v_{n-1}
with
\ell+L

To see (1), we suppose

, and we let

\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)

\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)},1\right)
are self-reachable on

2.5
, and both of these configurations have

chips on

\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}

about
v_{t}
with
\ell
chips, Lemma
5.5
implies that
\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}
has
\ell+d+1-(n-1)=(\ell+1)+d+1-n
chips on
v_{t}
. Therefore, both
\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)
and

. Also note that

still has degree

in

because

. So Lemma
5.5
implies that both
\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)

\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)},1\right)

about

, as desired.

To show (2), suppose

. By the same argument as in the proof of the previous claim,
\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}
has
\ell+d+1-(n-1)=\ell+(d+1)+1-n
chips on
v_{n-1}
. We know that
\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}+\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)

\ell+L

is an
n

5.5

\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)}+L\bm{e}_{n-1}^{(n-1)},0\right)

In this case, however, we demonstrate how to create such a configuration about the new leaf added.

Lemma 5.8

The lemma to follow verifies that all near-minimally self reachable configurations must be vertices of
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree, and let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
. Every near-minimally self-reachable configuration on
T
about
v_{i}
with
\ell
chips is a vertex of
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove our claim for a near-minimally self-reachable configuration about
v_{n}
because the vertex labeling of
T
is arbitrary.
Let
\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)},\nu_{n}\right)
be near-minimal about
v_{n}
, and let
d=\deg(v_{n})
.
It suffices to show that
\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)},\nu_{n}\right)
cannot be written as a nontrivial convex combination of self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips.
We suppose that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left(\bm{\nu}^{(n-1)},\nu_{n}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\bm{s}_{j}^{(n-1)},s_{n,j}\right),

where

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}=1,

\lambda_{j}\geq 0
, and
\left(\bm{s}_{j}^{(n-1)},s_{n,j}\right)
is a self-reachable configuration on
T
with
\ell
chips for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,k]}
. As shown in Lemma
5.5
, we must have
\nu_{n}=\ell+d+1-n
and
s_{n,j}\leq\ell+d+1-n
. But since we must have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}s_{n,j}=\ell+d+1-n,

we must have
s_{n,j}=\ell+d+1-n
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,k]}
. Then by the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
5.5
,
\left(\bm{s}_{j}^{(n-1)},s_{n,j}\right)
must have minimally self-reachable configurations on each of the trees in
T\setminus v_{n}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,k]}
. Thus,
\bm{s}_{j}^{(n-1)}
is a concatenation of minimally self-reachable configurations on the trees in
T\setminus v_{n}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,k]}
. But Theorem
3.4
implies that minimally self-reachable configurations on any tree cannot be nontrivial convex combinations of each other. Therefore, we must have
\bm{s}_{j}^{(n-1)}
be the same for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,k]}
to avoid contradicting this fact, which trivializes the convex combination in (
5.1
) and completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma shows that any self-reachable configuration can be expressed as a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations, which is a very important result for showing Theorem
5.3
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
T
be an
n
-vertex tree, and let
\bm{s}^{(n)}
be a self-reachable configuration on
T
with
\ell
chips for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
. Then
\bm{s}^{(n)}
can be expressed as a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. Our claim holds for
n=1
because all self-reachable configurations on a 1-vertex tree are near-minimally self-reachable.
For our induction hypothesis, we assume that any self-reachable configuration on a
k
-vertex tree
T
with
\ell
chips can be expressed as a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{k}
.
We now let
T
be a
(k+1)
-vertex tree and, without loss of generality, assume
v_{k+1}
is a leaf connected to
v_{k}
, and we let
\left(\bm{s}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right)
be a self-reachable configuration on
T
with
\ell
chips for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{k+1}
. We consider the following cases:

Case 1:

s_{k+1}=0
. Then by Corollary
2.5
, we have that
\bm{s}^{(k)}-\bm{e}_{k}^{(k)}
is a self-reachable configuration on
T\setminus v_{k+1}
with
\ell-1
chips. Since
\ell-1\in\mathbb{N}_{k}
, this guarantees that

\displaystyle\bm{s}^{(k)}-\bm{e}_{k}^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_{j}\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)},

where
\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}
is a near-minimally self-reachable configuration on
T\setminus v_{k+1}
with
\ell-1
chips for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,q]}
, and the
\lambda_{j}
s are nonnegative and sum to 1. So by Lemma
5.7
,
\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}+\bm{e}_{k}^{(k)},0\right)
is near-minimally self-reachable on
T
with
\ell
chips for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,q]}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left(\bm{s}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_{j}\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}+\bm{e}_{k}^{(k)},0\right),

which proves the desired claim in this case.

Case 2:

s_{k+1}=\ell+1-k
. By Lemma
5.5
,
\left(\bm{s}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right)
is near-minimally self-reachable about
v_{k+1}
, which proves the desired claim in this case.

Case 3:

1\leq s_{k+1}\leq\ell-k
. Since
s_{k+1}\leq\ell-k
,
\bm{s}^{(k)}
is guaranteed to have at least
k
chips on
T\setminus v_{k+1}
. Also,
\bm{s}^{(k)}
is self-reachable on
T\setminus v_{k+1}
by Corollary
2.4
. Therefore, we can apply our induction hypothesis to
\bm{s}^{(k)}
and deduce that

\displaystyle\bm{s}^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_{j}\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)},

where
\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}
is a near-minimally self-reachable configuration on
T\setminus v_{k+1}
with
\ell-s_{k+1}
chips for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,q]}
. We then have that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left(\bm{s}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_{j}\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right).

The desired claim will follow if we can write each summand in (
5.2
) as a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips.
First note that if
\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}
is near-minimally self-reachable on
T\setminus v_{k+1}
about
v_{t}
for
t\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,k-1]}
, then
\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)},1\right)
is near-minimally self-reachable on
T
about
v_{t}
by Lemma
5.7
.
Thus,
\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}+(s_{k+1}-1)\bm{e}_{t}^{(k)},1\right)
is near-minimally self-reachable on
T
about
v_{t}
by Lemma
5.6
, and this configuration has
\ell
chips on
T
.
We also know by Lemma
5.8
that if
\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}
has
\nu_{t,j}
chips on
v_{t}
, then
\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}-\left(\nu_{t,j}-d\right)\bm{e}_{t}^{(k)},\nu_{t,j}-d+1\right)
is near-minimally self-reachable on
T
about
v_{k+1}
, where
d=\deg^{(T\setminus v_{k+1})}(v_{t})
We can then add
s_{k+1}-1
chips to
v_{k+1}
by Lemma
5.6
, and we get that
\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}-\left(\nu_{t,j}-d\right)\bm{e}_{t}^{(k)},\nu_{t,j}-d+s_{k+1}\right)
is a near-minimally self-reachable configuration on
T
about
v_{k+1}
with
\ell
chips. Now observe that

(5.3)

\begin{split}\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right)&=\frac{\nu_{t,j}-d}{s_{k+1}-1+\nu_{t,j}-d}\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}+(s_{k+1}-1)\bm{e}_{t}^{(k)},1\right)\\
&+\frac{s_{k+1}-1}{s_{k+1}-1+\nu_{t,j}-d}\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}-\left(\nu_{t,j}-d\right)\bm{e}_{t}^{(k)},\nu_{t,j}-d+s_{k+1}\right).\end{split}

This is a valid convex combination because
\nu_{t,j}\geq d+1
by Lemma
5.4
and
s_{k+1}\geq 1
by assumption. Now suppose
\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}
is near-minimally self-reachable about
v_{k}
. We let
\nu_{k,j}
be the number of chips
\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}
has on
v_{k}
, and we let
d=\deg^{(T\setminus v_{k+1})}(v_{k})
. By the same reasoning as before,
\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}-\left(\nu_{k,j}-d\right)\bm{e}_{k}^{(k)},\nu_{k,j}-d+s_{k+1}\right)
will be near-minimally self-reachable on
T
about
v_{k+1}
and will have
\ell
chips. Also, by Lemma
5.7
,
\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}+s_{k+1}\bm{e}_{k}^{(k)},0\right)
is near-minimally self-reachable on
T
about
v_{k}
and also has
\ell
chips. Now observe that

(5.4)

\begin{split}\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right)&=\frac{\nu_{k,j}-d}{s_{k+1}+\nu_{k,j}-d}\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}+s_{k+1}\bm{e}_{k}^{(k)},0\right)\\
&+\frac{s_{k+1}}{s_{k+1}+\nu_{k,j}-d}\left(\bm{\nu}_{j}^{(k)}-\left(\nu_{k,j}-d\right)\bm{e}_{k}^{(k)},\nu_{k,j}-d+s_{k+1}\right).\end{split}

Once again, this is a valid convex combination because
\nu_{t,j}\geq d+1
by Lemma
5.4
and
s_{k+1}\geq 1
by assumption. Taken together, (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) imply that every summand in (
5.2
) can be written as a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips. When we substitute these convex combinations into (
5.2
), we obtain a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips that yields
\left(\bm{s}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right)
.

Since
\left(\bm{s}^{(k)},s_{k+1}\right)
was able to be expressed as a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips in all cases, the proof is complete.
∎

We now have the necessary machinery to prove Theorem
5.3
.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

By Lemma
5.9
, we have that near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips are vertices of
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
, so we only need to show that no other vertices exist. It suffices to show that any point in
\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
can be expressed as a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips. We let
\bm{x}^{(n)}\in\mathcal{CP}_{\ell}^{(T)}
be arbitrary. Then

(5.5)

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\bm{s}_{j}^{(n)},

where
\bm{s}_{j}^{(n)}
is a self-reachable configuration on
T
with
\ell
chips for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{[1,k]}
and the
\lambda_{j}
s are nonnegative and sum to 1. By Lemma
5.10
, we can express each
\bm{s}_{j}^{(n)}
as a convex combination of the near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips. We can then substitute these convex combinations into (
5.5
) to express
\bm{x}^{(n)}
as a convex combination of near-minimally self-reachable configurations on
T
with
\ell
chips.
∎

We conclude this section with an example that demonstrates some of the methods used in the proof of Lemma

.

Example 5.11

(5.7)

(0,3)

Analysis of a mean-field limit of interacting two-dimensional nonlinear integrate-and-fire neurons
Romain Veltz

Abstract
. We study the solutions of a McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation (SDE) driven by a Poisson process. In neuroscience, this SDE models the mean field limit of a system of N N interacting excitatory neurons with N N large. Each neuron spikes randomly with rate depending on its membrane potential. At each spiking time, the neuron potential is reset to the value v ¯ \bar{v} , its adaptation variable is incremented by w ¯ \bar{w} and all other neurons receive an additional amount J / N J/N of potential after some delay where J J is the connection strength. Between jumps, the neurons drift according to some two-dimensional ordinary differential equation with explosive behavior. We prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions of a heuristically derived mean-field limit of the system when N → ∞ N\to\infty . We then study the existence of stationary distributions and provide several properties (regularity, tail decay, etc.) based on a Doeblin estimate using a Lyapunov function. Numerical simulations are provided to assess the hypotheses underlying the results. Keywords McKean-Vlasov SDE · Systems of interacting neurons · Invariant measure · Mean-field interaction · Piecewise deterministic Markov process Mathematics Subject Classification Primary: 60J75. Secondary 60K35, 60G55, 45A05

Introduction

Our goal is to establish existence and uniqueness of solutions to the heuristically derived mean-field limit of models of networks of stochastic spiking neurons introduced in
[
ACV19
]
. Since the dynamics of the neurons lack an analytical expression, we aim to provide testable assumptions at the level of the vector field.

1.1.
Description of the model

For a given network size
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we consider a piecewise deterministic Markov process
[
Dav93
]
(PDMP)
X_{t}^{N}=(V_{t}^{1,N},\cdots,V_{t}^{N,N},W_{t}^{1,N},\cdots,W_{t}^{N,N})\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
. For
i=1,\cdots,N
,
V_{t}^{i,N}
represents the membrane potential of neuron

and
W_{t}^{i,N}
its adaptation variable. Neuron

emits spikes at random times with spiking rate

. When it emits a spike at time

\forall j\neq i,\quad V^{j,N}_{(t_{s}+D)^{+}}=V^{j,N}_{(t_{s}+D)^{-}}+\frac{J}{N}.

Between two spikes, the neuron variables

evolve according to the ordinary differential equation (ODE)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{V}_{t}^{i,N}&=F(V_{t}^{i,N})-W^{i,N}_{t}+I\\
\dot{W}^{i,N}_{t}&=V_{t}^{i,N}-W^{i,N}_{t}.\end{aligned}\right.

The process

with law

, and independent of the Poisson measures. Then

is a

\displaystyle\quad V_{t}^{i,N}

\displaystyle=V_{0}^{i,N}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(F(V_{u}^{i,N})-W^{i,N}_{u}+I\right)du+\frac{J}{N}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{[-D,t-D]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{z\leq\lambda\left(V_{u-}^{j,N}\right)\right\}}\mathbf{N}^{j}(du,dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(\bar{v}-V_{u-}^{i,N}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{z\leq\lambda\left(V_{u-}^{i,N}\right)\right\}}\mathbf{N}^{i}(du,dz),

\displaystyle\quad W_{t}^{i,N}

\displaystyle=W_{0}^{i,N}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(V_{u}^{i,N}-W^{i,N}_{u}\right)du+\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\bar{w}\,\mathbf{1}_{\left\{z\leq\lambda\left(V_{u-}^{i,N}\right)\right\}}\mathbf{N}^{i}(du,dz)

When

\displaystyle=W_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(V_{u}^{nl}-W^{nl}_{u}\right)du+\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\bar{w}\,\mathbf{1}_{\left\{z\leq\lambda\left(V_{u-}^{nl}\right)\right\}}\mathbf{N}(du,dz)

with intensity measure

,

1.2.
Biological and modeling background

Although the above particle system is a toy model, it is inspired by biology.
A neuron is a specialized cell type of the central nervous system
[
Kan13
]
comprised of subcellular domains: dendrite, soma and axon. The neurons are connected to each others by synapses which connect axons to dendrites of different neurons. The neurons communicate using electrical impulses encoded in their membrane electrical potential. When the difference of electrical potential
V_{t}
across the membrane of their soma is high enough, a sequence of action potentials (or spikes) is produced and the somatic membrane potential returns to a resting value
\bar{v}
. These electrical impulses are short
2-5ms
. The action potentials then propagate along the axons until they reach the synapses, connections to the other neurons. When an action potential reaches a synapse, it triggers a local change of the membrane potential of the postsynaptic side of the synapse which itself induces a local change of the somatic membrane potential of the postsynaptic neuron (we ignore propagation of electric signals in the dendrite). This process takes time and we model it with a small constant delay
D>0
on the order of
5ms
. Also, we assume that all neurons are connected to each others and each spike produces the same local change
\frac{J}{N}
in the postsynaptic neurons where
J
is the connection strength. This is of course a strong idealization.

The generation of spikes at the soma originates from the fluxes of different ions species across the cellular membrane, a process described by the four dimensional deterministic Hodgkin-Huxley (HH) model
[
HH52
]
which has been simplified to a two dimensional model
[
Ger+14
,
BG05
]
. The noise in the spiking dynamics is modeled here with a
firing intensity

\lambda(V_{t})
which depends on the current membrane potential
V_{t}
of the neuron. This is inspired by the one dimensional
escape rate
models or the
generalized integrate-and-fire
(GIF) models but it has never been studied with two dimensional model models except in
[
ACV19
]
.

We refer to
[
Bri88
,
Ger95
,
Ger+14
,
SDG17
]
and the references therein for a description of the one dimensional GIF model. On a side note, the GIF model have been shown to be relevant for modeling real data
[
Men+12
]
. See also
[
Jah+11
]
for fitting data to a jump diffusion integrate and fire.

One long term goal of this work is to link the macroscopic dynamics of the networks to the microscopic elements. To this end, we need a model of neuron with a rich set of dynamical (spiking) behaviors
[
Izh07
]
.
Most mean field models in the mathematical neuroscience community assume that the neurons have simple dynamics with affine scalar vector field ; neurons with 2d dynamics have rarely been studied (see recent review
[
CR25
]
). This is the case of neurons modeled with Hawkes processes
[

,

]
, with the integrate and fire model
[

,

and with PDMPs

Some notable exceptions are studies
[

with the 4d HH dynamics

or the 2d FitzHugh-Nagumo ones

The HH model suffers from sophisticated dynamics which makes its parameter tuning difficult. This led to the development of the family of two dimensional nonlinear spiking neuron models

1.4.
Goal of the paper

In the model (
MKV
), we have the freedom of the rate function
\lambda
which controls the noise level in the network. Owing to the explosive behavior of the vector field, the rate function has to ensure that the neuron spikes before the deterministic explosion time which thus has the tendency to magnify this effect. As
\lambda
arises in the drift of (
MKV
), there is a possibility that a blowup occurs for (
MKV
) when the delays are neglected
D=0
.

This can readily be seen for the following SDE, at least formally,

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left[F(X_{u})+J\mathbb{E}\lambda\left(X_{u}\right)\right]du+\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(\bar{v}-X_{u-}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\left\{z\leq\lambda\left(X_{u-}\right)\right\}}\mathbf{N}(du,dz).

The Ito formula gives for
\lambda=F=\exp

\mathbb{E}(\lambda(X_{t}))-\mathbb{E}{\lambda(X_{0})}=\int_{0}^{t}J\mathbb{E}\lambda(X_{u})\mathbb{E}\lambda(X_{u})+\lambda(\bar{v})\mathbb{E}\lambda(X_{u})du

which blows up in finite time.

The first part of the paper is thus to give conditions on
\lambda,F
for (
MKV
) to be well defined for all time
t\geq 0
. We then give conditions for the existence of a stationary distribution. This is done by studying the enclosed Markov chain and by relying on Doeblin estimates
[
CM23
]
using a Lyapunov function. The invariant distribution of the process
X^{s,\mu_{0},\kappa}
is then lifted from the one of the enclosed chain, we recover the general expression from
[
Cos90
]
albeit in a different general setting.

Proposition 28

Grant assumptions

T_{0}:=s

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{t\geq s:\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{z\leq\lambda\left(\varphi_{s}^{u}\left(X_{s}^{s,x,\kappa}\right)\right)\right\}}\mathbf{N}(du,dz)>0\right\},

5.2.
Study of the jump rate

We proved that the process
X^{s,x,\kappa}
is defined on
[s,\infty)
.
For any measurable and bounded

, the strong Markov property gives
[
JS96
]
:

\mathbb{E}h\left(X^{s,x,\kappa}_{t}\right)=h(\varphi^{t}_{s}(x))\,e^{-\Lambda^{\kappa}(s,x,t)}\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{\infty}(s,x))}(t)+\int_{s}^{t}p^{\kappa}(s,x,u)\ \mathbb{E}h\left(X_{t}^{u,\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u}(x),\kappa}\right)du.

Hence, if the jump rate
r^{\kappa}(s,x,t):=\mathbb{E}\lambda(X_{t}^{s,x,\kappa})
is finite, we expect it to satisfies the integral equation:

(IE)

r^{\kappa}(s,x,t)=p^{\kappa}(s,x,t)+\int_{s}^{t}p^{\kappa}(s,x,u)r^{\kappa}(u,\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u}(x),t)\,du,\quad s\leq t,\quad x\in{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}.

This equation has a nice structure: when solved solely on the reset line
\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
, it provides an explicit solution on the whole domain
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
. Hence, we start by solving the equation on
\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
.

Proposition 29
.

Grant assumptions
1
,
11
,
12
, consider
S<T<\infty
,
\kappa\in C^{0}([S,T],\mathbb{R}_{+})
and a partition
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
. Then, equation (
IE
) posed on the reset line
\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
has a unique non negative solution
\tilde{r}^{\kappa}
in
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{R}}:=\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(J,\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}))
where
J:=\{(s,t)\in\ [S,T]^{2}:s\leq t\}
.
Additionally, under assumption
13
,
\forall x\in\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
and
\forall s\in[S,T]
,
t\to\tilde{r}^{\kappa}(s,x,t)
is continuous for
s\leq t\leq T
.

Proof.

We prove that (
IE
) has a unique solution for
x\in\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
.
We note that
p^{\kappa}
belongs to
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{R}}
thanks to lemma
C.55
and the three assumptions.
Let us write (

) as
\tilde{r}=p^{\kappa}+\mathcal{T}_{o}(\tilde{r})
. For

\mathcal{T}^{n}_{0}(h)(s,x,t)=\int_{s}^{t}du_{1}\int_{u_{1}}^{t}du_{2}\cdots\int_{u_{n-1}}^{t}du_{n}\\
p^{\kappa}(s,x,u_{1})\ p^{\kappa}(u_{1},\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u_{1}}(x),u_{2})\cdots p^{\kappa}(u_{n-1},\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u_{n-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u_{1}}(x),u_{n})\\
\times h(u_{n},\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u_{n}}\circ\cdots\circ\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u_{1}}(x),t),

and we find:

\mathcal{T}^{n}_{0}(h)(s,x,t)\leq\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{R}}}\left\|p\right\|_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{R}}}^{n}\frac{(T-S)^{n}}{n!}<\infty.

This implies that

and that (

is.
Let us look at the continuity under assumption
13
. Each term of the series is continuous in

by lemma
C.56
and bounded by

for some

is continuous for

.
∎

We can now state:

Lemma 30
.

Grant assumptions
1
,
11
,
12
, consider
S<T<\infty
,
\kappa\in C^{0}([S,T],\mathbb{R}_{+})
and a partition
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
. Then the jump rate

is finite, is solution of (

) and there is a constant

and
\forall S\leq s\leq t\leq T

(14)

p^{\kappa}(s,x,t)\leq r^{\kappa}(s,x,t)\leq p^{\kappa}(s,x,t)+M_{S,T}.

, for

,

is continuous when
s\leq t\leq T
.

Proof.

We first show that
r(s,x,t)
is finite. To this end, we consider an increasing sequence
\lambda_{n}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}_{+})
, independent of the second variable, such that
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda
a.e. The theorem of monotone convergence implies that the increasing sequence

is such that
r^{n}(s,x,t):=\mathbb{E}\lambda_{n}(X_{t}^{s,\delta_{x},\kappa})\to r^{\kappa}(s,x,t)
a.e. from below. By
[
JS96
]

\forall s\leq t,\quad\forall x\in\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}},\quad r^{n}(s,x,t)=\lambda_{n}(\varphi^{t}_{s}(x))\,e^{-\Lambda^{\kappa}(s,x,t)}\mathbf{1}_{[s,t_{\infty}(s,x))}(t)+\int_{s}^{t}p^{\kappa}(s,x,u)r^{n}(u,\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u}(x),t)du\\
\leq p^{\kappa}(s,x,t)+\int_{s}^{t}p^{\kappa}(s,x,u)r^{n}(u,\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u}(x),t)du.

As

, proposition
29
implies that

for

and then

which implies, by taking the limit

in the integral equation and by using uniqueness of the solution to (

that

\forall x\in\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}},\ r^{\kappa}(s,x,t)=\tilde{r}^{\kappa}(s,x,t).

This implies that

is continuous in time under assumption
13
on
\mathbf{R}_{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
.

Now, we observe that (

) allows to extend

to
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}
.
Then, we obtain that

\forall s\leq t,\quad\forall x\in{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}},\quad r^{n}(s,x,t)\leq p^{\kappa}(s,x,t)+\int_{s}^{t}p^{\kappa}(s,x,u)r^{\kappa}(u,\Delta\varphi_{s}^{u}(x),t)du:=\tilde{r}^{\kappa}(s,x,t),

which implies that

for

for

and that

under assumption

. At this stage, we also have the inequality

∎

Step 1.

7.1.
Existence of invariant distribution for the enclosed chain

In the next proposition, we derive a Doeblin estimate for the enclosed chain. This estimate is relatively easy to obtain when

To this end, it is convenient to define:

\displaystyle p_{k}(t_{1:k},x)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}(S_{1:k}=t_{1:k}|X_{0}=x).

∎

Proposition 39
.

For all

7.3.
Existence of invariant distribution for
X

In the next result, we identify the invariant distributions of the solution
X
to (
I-SDE
) by proving that it is ergodic using the Harris recurrence property of the enclosed chain.

Proof of theorem
19
.

From lemma
38
,
\mu^{inv}
defined in (
11
) is a probability measure. By theorem
36
, we have existence of a unique invariant distribution for the Markov chain
(\mathbf{w}_{n},S_{n})_{n}
which is given by
\mu^{\mathbf{w},S}(dw,ds)=p(s,w)\mu^{\mathbf{w}}(dw)ds
. We then consider
h\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbf{E}_{w}\times\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}_{+})
. We note that for
t>0

\frac{1}{T_{N_{t}+1}}\int_{0}^{T_{N_{t}}}h(X_{s})ds\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h(X_{s})ds\leq\frac{1}{T_{N_{t}}}\int_{0}^{T_{N_{t}+1}}h(X_{s})ds.

where
N_{t}
is the number of jumps before time
t
; it is finite by proposition
27
.
From theorem
36
,
(\mathbf{w}_{n},S_{n})_{n}
is Harris recurrent and thus ergodic (
[
Rev84
, theorem 4.3 p.140]
) on
\mathbf{E}_{w}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. It follows that
T_{n}\to\infty

a.s.
Also:
T_{n}/T_{n+1}\to 1

a.s.
Indeed, from the ergodicity of
(\mathbf{w}_{n},S_{n})_{n}
, we find
T_{n}/n\to\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\mathbf{w}}}(T_{1})<\infty

a.s.
Therefore, to study the ergodicity of X, it is enough to focus on
\frac{1}{T_{N_{t}+1}}\int_{0}^{T_{N_{t}+1}}h(X_{s})ds
.

We have
H:(w,s)\to\int_{0}^{s}h(\varphi^{r}(w))dr\in\mathrm{L}^{1}_{+}(\mu^{\mathbf{w},S})
because
\mu^{\bf w,S}(H)\leq\left\|h\right\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\mathbf{w}}}(T_{1})<\infty
.

\int_{0}^{T_{N_{t}+1}}h(X_{r})dr=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}}\int_{T_{k}}^{T_{k+1}}h(X_{r})dr=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}}\int_{0}^{S_{k}}h(\varphi^{r}(X_{T_{k}^{+}}))dr=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}}H(X_{T_{k}^{+}},S_{k}).

From proposition

,

, we find

\frac{1}{N_{t}+1}\int_{0}^{T_{N_{t}+1}}h(X_{r})dr\to\int\mu^{\mathbf{w},S}(dw,ds)\int_{0}^{s}h(\varphi^{r}(w))dr\quad\ \textit{a.s.}

It follows that

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h(X_{s})ds\to\mu^{inv}(h),\quad\textit{a.s.}

which is then true for

. Then, X is ergodic and has a unique invariant measure.
The fact that
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{1}
does not belong to the support of

is a consequence of (

{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{P}}}_{1}
is not reachable from
\mathbf{R}
by the deterministic flow.

∎

We study the nonlinear invariant distributions of the SDE (

(resp.

(resp.

Lemma 43
.

Under the assumptions of theorem
19
, any current
\kappa

associated to a (nonlinear) stationary distribution is solution of

\kappa\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}}(T_{1})=J.

Proof.

If the solution

of the SDE (

) is stationary, we find that
\kappa=J\mu^{inv}_{\kappa}(\lambda)
or
\kappa\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}}(T_{1})=J\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\lambda(X_{s})ds
. The lemma then follows from

\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\lambda\circ\varphi^{s}\,ds=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}(u\leq T_{1})\ \lambda\circ\varphi^{u}du=\int\mu_{\kappa}(dw)\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(\varphi^{t}(\bar{v},w))e^{-\Lambda(0,(\bar{v},w),t)}dt=1.

∎

We re-write equation (

) as:

J=\kappa\int\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}(dw)\int_{0}^{t_{\infty}^{\kappa}}e^{-\Lambda^{\kappa}(t,(\bar{v},w))}dt

and define

\mathbf{R}
by

T_{\kappa}(w):=\int_{0}^{t_{\infty}^{\kappa}(w)}e^{-\Lambda^{\kappa}(t,(\bar{v},w))}dt=\mathbb{E}\left(T_{1}^{(\bar{v},w)}\right)<\infty.

We note that
T_{\kappa}(w)<t_{\infty}^{\kappa}(w)
. To show existence of invariant distributions, we use the intermediate value theorem which relies on the continuity of the following mapping on
\mathbb{R}_{+}

\kappa\to\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}(T_{\kappa}).

To prove continuity, as
T_{\kappa}
is unbounded, it is not enough to show the continuity of

We denote by

\|\mu\|:=\sup\left\{|\mu(h)|:h\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbf{E}_{w}),\|h\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

]

(resp.

We denote by

and we introduce the weighted total variation norm on
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{E}_{w})
defined by

\left\|\mu\right\|_{V}:=\int V|\mu|.

We note that
\left\|\mu\right\|_{V}=\sup\limits_{\left\|h\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\leq 1}|\mu(hV)|

We denote by

We adapt the proof of Harris theorem from

]
and

.
We consider

.

on

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mu\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{V}:=\left\|\mu\right\|+\beta\left\|\mu\right\|_{V}

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{U}_{I_{n}}^{*}\nu_{D}^{0}-\mathbf{U}_{I}^{*}\nu_{D}^{0}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{V}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{U}_{I_{n}}^{*}\nu_{D}^{0}-\mathbf{U}_{I}^{*}\nu_{D}^{0}\right\|_{V}

We then consider

and get from proposition

I\in{\mathbf{I}_{reg}}
):

\lvert\nu_{D}^{0}(\mathbf{U}_{I_{n}}Vh-\mathbf{U}_{I}Vh)\rvert\leq\|h\|_{\infty}\cdot\int\nu_{D}^{0}(dw_{0})\int_{\mathbf{E}_{w}}\lvert K_{I}(w_{0},w)-K_{I_{n}}(w_{0},w)\rvert V(w+\bar{w})dw\\
:=\|h\|_{\infty}\cdot\langle\nu_{D}^{0},\lvert K_{I}-K_{I_{n}}\lvert\cdot V(\cdot+\bar{w})\rangle.

Hence, we have shown that

(26)

\left\|\mathbf{U}_{I_{n}}^{*}\nu_{D}^{0}-\mathbf{U}_{I}^{*}\nu_{D}^{0}\right\|_{V}\leq\langle\nu_{D}^{0},\lvert K_{I}-K_{I_{n}}\lvert\cdot V(\cdot+\bar{w})\rangle

and
I\to\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{I}
is continuous if we are able to show that the right hand side of (
26
) converges to zero as
n\to\infty
.

Lemma 47
.

For almost all
w_{0}\in\mathbf{E}_{w}
,
\int_{\mathbf{E}_{w}}\lvert K_{I_{n}}(w_{0},w)-K_{I}(w_{0},w)\rvert V(w+\bar{w})dw
converges to zero as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

The lemma is a direct consequence of the Brezis-Lieb theorem
A.50
.

Step 1.

By proposition
D.57
, the integrand
K_{I_{n}}(w_{0},w)
converges to
K_{I}(w_{0},w)

a.e.
when
I\in\mathbf{I}_{\emptyset}
.

Step 2.
We have
K_{I}\cdot V(\cdot+\bar{w})=\mathbf{U}_{I}V<\infty
by lemma
37
.

Step 3.
We show that
\mathbf{U}_{I_{n}}V
converges to
\mathbf{U}_{I}V

a.e.
.
Indeed:

\mathbf{U}_{I_{n}}V(w_{0})=\int_{0}^{\infty}V\left(\bar{w}+\hat{w}^{I_{n}}_{w_{0}}(\tau)\right)e^{-\tau}d\tau

by lemma
E.58
where
\hat{w}
is a time change of
w
.
The integrand is continuous in
I_{n}
.
From corollary
25
, we know that there is
C>0
such that for all
n
large enough and
\forall\tau\geq 0
,
\hat{w}^{I_{n}}_{w_{0}}(\tau)\leq C+|w_{0}|
, this provides a domination of the integrand using the monotony of
V
. This proves that
\mathbf{U}_{I_{n}}V
converges to
\mathbf{U}_{I}V

a.e.
by Lebesgue’s theorem and concludes the lemma.
∎

Let us define
g_{n}(w_{0}):=\int_{\mathbf{E}_{w}}\lvert K_{I}(w_{0},w)-K_{I_{n}}(w_{0},w)\rvert V(w+\bar{w})dw
. The previous lemma shows that
g_{n}
converges to zero
a.e.
. By lemma
37
, for
n
large enough,
g_{n}(w_{0})\leq 2(\gamma_{L}V(w_{0})+K_{L})
. Hence, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, the right hand side of (
26
), which equals
\int g_{n}d\nu_{D}^{0}
, converges to zero. We thus have proved that the mapping
I\to\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{I}
is continuous.
∎

Remark 48
.

Let us comment on the restrictive assumption in proposition
44
that
I
must belong to
{\mathbf{I}_{\emptyset}}
.
The restrictive assumption stems from our estimate of
\left\|\mathbf{U}_{I_{n}}^{*}\nu_{D}^{0}-\mathbf{U}_{I}^{*}\nu_{D}^{0}\right\|_{V}
.

This comes from the proof of proposition
D.57
which relies on the fact that deterministic solutions are nearby for similar parameter values, a property called structural stability
[
Hal80
]
. However, when there are stationary points, this is not necessarily the case. Indeed, one can prove that the one with largest membrane potential is a saddle point for which there are nearby solutions which explode in finite times and other which converge to periodic solutions.

Proof of theorem
20
.

We consider
I\in{\mathbf{I}_{\emptyset}}
. For a constant
\kappa\geq 0
,
I+\kappa\in{\mathbf{I}_{\emptyset}}
.
We define
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni\kappa\to\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}(T_{\kappa})
, which depends on
I
, and consider a non-negative sequence
\kappa_{n}\to\kappa_{0}
.
One then finds

\lvert f(\kappa_{n})-f(\kappa)\rvert\leq\lvert\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa_{n}}(T_{\kappa_{n}})-\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}(T_{\kappa_{n}})\rvert+\lvert\mu^{\mathbf{w}}_{\kappa}(T_{\kappa_{n}}-T_{\kappa})\rvert.

As
T_{\kappa_{n}}=O(V+1)
by (
22
), the first term converges to zero by proposition
44
.
We then note that the mean jump time
T_{\kappa_{n}}
converges to
T_{\kappa}
a.e. by lemma
E.59
.
Using the same domination (
22
) and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, one finds that the second term tends to zero as well. Hence,
f
is continuous.

We now study the equation
J=\kappa\cdot f(\kappa)
for finding the invariant measures. By the intermediate value theorem, there is a solution
\kappa
to this equation when
J
is small enough,
i.e.
, belongs to
[0,\sup\limits_{\kappa\geq 0}\kappa f(\kappa)]
.
∎

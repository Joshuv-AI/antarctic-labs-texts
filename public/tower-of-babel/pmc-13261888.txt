MRCA Combined With Zero-Contrast IVUS-Guided PCI in a Patient With NSTE-ACS and Severe Contrast Allergy
Authors: Huan Wang, Yan Wu, Zhijie Jian, Yongbai Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

History of Present Illness

A 50-year-old man was referred for non–ST-segment elevation acute coronary syndrome with previous severe hypersensitivity reactions to iodine-based contrast media (ICM). One month before admission, the patient was scheduled for coronary angiography in another hospital. However, he experienced dyspnea and facial edema during ICM injection for right radial artery angiography, which was attributed to severe contrast agent allergy. The coronary angiography was terminated without clarifying the images of the coronary arteries. One week ago, owing to worsening exertional chest pain and dyspnea, he was referred for evaluation for potential zero-contrast percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) at our institution. His blood pressure was 134/84 mm Hg with a heart rate of 79 beats/min. He was hemodynamically stable, and physical examination findings were unremarkable. Laboratory analysis was notable for elevation in high-sensitivity troponin T of 0.16 ng/mL and serum creatinine levels of 111 μmol/L. The treadmill exercise test (TET) showed ST-segment depression in leads II, III, aVF, V3 to V6, with ST-segment elevation in lead aVR.

Past Medical History

The patient's medical history was significant for hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Differential Diagnosis

In addition to chest pain due to acute coronary syndrome (ACS) (potentially due to type 1, 2, or 4 myocardial infarction), other potential etiologies for presentation include myocardial injury due to Takotsubo cardiomyopathy, myocarditis, nonischemic myocardial injury, and noncardiac reasons for the symptoms.

Investigations

The patient was at high risk, and coronary revascularization should be performed. However, no coronary anatomic information was obtained owing to ICM-induced hypersensitivity reactions. Additionally, the use of gadolinium-based contrast agents posed a risk for further deterioration in renal function according to the abnormal renal function.1,2 After multidisciplinary team discussion involving cardiology, radiology, and nephrology, we proceeded with noncontrast magnetic resonance coronary angiography (MRCA) as the initial approach for coronary artery assessment. MRCA demonstrated a normal origin of the left and right coronary arteries, with a right-dominant coronary distribution. The right coronary artery (RCA) presented with a shepherd's hook morphology. No significant stenosis was observed in the RCA or the left circumflex coronary artery (LCx). However, a significant stenosis was identified in the proximal left anterior descending (LAD) artery (Figure 1). On the basis of the positive TET results, the lesion in the LAD was identified as the culprit lesion. It was decided to proceed with intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) to further evaluate coronary arteries and guide zero-contrast PCI.

Management

On the basis of preprocedural MRCA findings, the Sion blue guidewire course confirmed the assessment, and IVUS images revealed minimal lipid plaque in the RCA with no significant stenosis (Figure 2). The IVUS results further indicated the presence of fibrofatty plaques from the mid-LAD artery to the ostium (Figure 3). The most severe stenosis was located 8 mm from the LAD ostium, presenting as an ultrasound-attenuated plaque. The minimum lumen area was 2.25 mm2. The IVUS results indicated no significant stenosis in the LCx. After predilation with a 3 × 13-mm nonslip element balloon at 12 atm, IVUS guidance was used for optimal stent sizing and precise positioning. Two Promus Premier stents were implanted: one measuring 3 × 24 mm and the other 3.5 × 24-mm. These stents were placed extending from the LAD artery to the left main coronary artery (LMCA) with postdilation using both a 3 × 15-mm and a 3.5 × 15-mm noncompliant balloon at 18 to 20 atm. However, follow-up IVUS revealed a suboptimal stent area at the LAD ostium with associated malapposition in the LMCA. Subsequently, a 4 × 15 mm noncompliant balloon was inflated to 20 atm for postdilation at the LAD ostium, followed by proximal optimization technique using a 4.5 × 6-mm balloon at 14 atm in the LM (Figure 4). Repeat IVUS demonstrated satisfactory stent expansion and apposition, with minimum stent area of 6.74 mm2 in the distal LAD, 8.84 mm2 in the proximal LAD, and 15.42 mm2 in the LMCA. The LCx ostium was uncompromised (Figure 5). Postprocedural transthoracic echocardiography confirmed the absence of pericardial effusion.

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient remained hemodynamically stable postprocedure, with follow-up electrocardiography and transthoracic echocardiography showing no abnormalities. At the 1-month outpatient visit, he reported no chest pain recurrence, and a follow-up TET revealed no significant ischemia. The patient continued on guideline-directed secondary prevention medications for coronary artery disease (CAD).

Discussion

This case represents a unique clinical challenge: managing ACS in a patient with severe hypersensitivity reactions to ICM and completely unknown coronary anatomy. Although previous reports have demonstrated the feasibility of zero-contrast PCI under IVUS guidance,3,4 those patients had known coronary anatomy from previous images. In contrast, the present patient presented with no anatomic information, making conditions such as anomalous coronary origin or chronic total occlusion potential obstacles to successful IVUS-guided intervention.

MRCA served as the critical “scout” in our strategy, providing essential anatomic information without iodinated contrast or ionizing radiation. With a sensitivity of 89% and specificity of 72% for diagnosing CAD,5 MRCA confirmed normal coronary origin and distribution, identified the right-dominant system with shepherd's hook RCA morphology, and precisely localized the culprit lesion to the proximal LAD artery. This anatomic roadmap was indispensable for planning the zero-contrast PCI. Recent technological advances including automated navigation, respiratory gating, and deep learning reconstruction have significantly improved MRCA image quality and efficiency,6 making it increasingly viable for clinical decision-making.

IVUS functioned as an “intracorporeal microscope,” providing real-time, high-resolution assessment of vessel dimensions, plaque characteristics, and stent deployment.7 Under zero-contrast conditions, IVUS not only confirmed the severity of the proximal LAD lesion (minimum lumen area 2.25 mm2) but also guided optimal stent sizing, positioning, and postdeployment optimization. The detection of LMCA malapposition and subsequent successful correction with proximal optimization technique exemplifies the critical role of IVUS in ensuring procedural success without angiographic guidance.

The synergy between MRCA and IVUS addresses the limitations of each modality. Although MRCA excels at visualizing proximal coronary anatomy and identifying significant stenoses, it has limited resolution for distal vessels and calcified lesions. IVUS provides precise intravascular assessment but requires a guidewire to reach the target lesion. By combining these complementary techniques, we achieved comprehensive evaluation and successful revascularization without any iodinated contrast, demonstrating a viable pathway for managing high-risk patients with absolute contraindications to conventional angiography.

This approach has limitations. MRCA requires specialized equipment and expertise, and image quality can be affected by arrhythmias and patient cooperation. IVUS-guided PCI demands meticulous technique and experience, particularly in complex anatomies. Nevertheless, with continued technological advancement and growing operator experience, this multimodality imaging strategy holds promise as a standardized approach for patients with contrast contraindications requiring revascularization.

Conclusions

The successful management of this case demonstrates that the combined strategy of MRCA and IVUS-guided PCI represents a highly promising therapeutic approach for patients with CAD who have severe hypersensitivity reactions to ICM, particularly high-risk patients in need of revascularization. With continuous advancements in imaging technology and the accumulation of operator experience, this multimodality imaging strategy holds the potential to become a standardized pathway for addressing such clinical challenges, thereby enabling more patients with special conditions to benefit from revascularization therapy.

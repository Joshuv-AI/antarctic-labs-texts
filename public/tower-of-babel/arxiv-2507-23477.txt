Discrete restrictions from Laurent monomial systems for multiple Dirichlet series
Shenghao Hua

Abstract
. We introduce a special class of multiple Dirichlet series whose terms are supported on a variety and which admit an Euler product structure. We show that these series arise naturally from twisted moments of automorphic L L -functions associated with Dirichlet twists. We proposed several conjectures on the analytic properties of these series.

1.
Introduction

The restriction of the Fourier or Mellin transform to submanifolds is an active area of research.
A Dirichlet series can be viewed as a discrete sampling and weighted summation of the Mellin transform at integer points, effectively replacing the continuous measure by a Stieltjes integral. There is a profound connection between the two: for example, finite partial sums of a Dirichlet series can be recovered via a special form of the inverse Mellin transform known as Perron’s formula. Moreover, the asymptotic functional equation expansion of an automorphic
L
-function is obtained through the inverse Mellin transform of the completed
L
-function.
If we view a multiple Dirichlet series as a discrete Mellin transform integral over the entire Euclidean space, a natural question arises:
can such an integral be restricted to integrals over submanifolds, such as algebraic varieties?

For an algebraic variety
V
in the
t
-dimensional Euclidean space defined by
m\leq t
defined by a system of rational functional equations with integer coefficients

f_{1}(\boldsymbol{x})=0,\dots,f_{m}(\boldsymbol{x})=0,

where
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})
, we consider restricting the summation to its positive integer solutions.
For the multiple Dirichlet series

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{a(\boldsymbol{n})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{s_{i}}},

defined by coefficients
a(\boldsymbol{n})
, where
\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})
,
the series converges when all
\operatorname{Re}s_{i}
are sufficiently large.
Here we consider a multiple Dirichlet series twisted by the indicator function of
V
, which is of the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\delta_{\boldsymbol{n}\in V(\mathbb{Z})}a(\boldsymbol{n})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{s_{i}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1\\
\boldsymbol{n}\in V(\mathbb{Z})\end{subarray}}\frac{a(\boldsymbol{n})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{s_{i}}},

where
\delta_{\boldsymbol{n}\in V(\mathbb{Z})}
denotes the indicator function of the set of integer points on
V
.

Such a summation is easily shown to converge when the real parts of the variables are sufficiently large. However, we hope that it also possesses desirable properties, similar to those enjoyed by functions in the Selberg class.
The Selberg class of functions was introduced by Selberg in the 1980s as an axiomatic framework to generalize and study properties of
L
-functions, such as the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions. These functions satisfy a set of analytic conditions, including a functional equation, Euler product that is locally generated by finitely many terms, and Ramanujan hypothesis. Around the same time, the theory of automorphic
L
-functions developed rapidly as part of the Langlands program, which seeks deep connections between number theory and representation theory. Automorphic
L
-functions arise from automorphic forms and play a crucial role in modern number theory, encompassing many classical
L
-functions within a unified theory. The interplay between the Selberg class and automorphic
L
-functions continues to be a central theme in analytic number theory.
Kaczorowski and Perelli
[
4
]
, under certain conditions, proved a conjecture of Sarnak concerning functions in the Selberg class. Specifically, they showed that it is possible to find a countable set of primitive Selberg class functions such that all Selberg class functions can be generated as products of shifts of these primitive elements.

For certain low-degree cases, the compatibility between the Selberg class and automorphic
L
-functions has already been verified.
Kaczorowski and Perelli
[
5
]
proved that when the degree is strictly less than 2, Selberg class functions arise from shifts of the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions.
Typically, an Euler product is required to factor into a product of fixed-degree polynomial Dirichlet series at each prime, generating the entire
L
-function. However, if we relax this condition and only require local factorization at each prime without demanding that the global function be generated by finitely many terms,
we conjecture that by
slightly breaking
the automorphy of an
L
-function, it may still be possible to retain a functional equation.
In this endeavor, we place our hopes on introducing additional algebraic structures.
In this paper, we attempt to introduce an algebraic structure to construct special Dirichlet series. Specifically, we consider multiple Dirichlet series whose coefficients are derived from automorphic forms. By twisting the characteristic function of a certain variety and removing some of the terms, we deliberately break the automorphy.
In the setting of continuous integral transforms, many classical results construct inverse Mellin transforms that enable the Mellin transform to be meaningfully restricted to submanifolds. Here, we conjecture that a discrete analogue of this phenomenon may exist—namely, that once the resulting series is known to retain an Euler product whose local factors cannot be generated by finitely many terms, it is conceivable that it may also admit a functional equation and a meromorphic continuation.
That is, the property that the Euler product admits a finite local factorization at each place is expected to be closely related to automorphy.

We first present several basic properties of these multiple Dirichlet series, followed by our main result, Theorem
3.1
, which addresses the natural emergence of these series in the context of twisted moments of twisted automorphic
L
-functions; its proof is given in the final section. We then formulate three conjectures: one concerns the case when the variety exhibits a certain form of prime factorization independence, while the other two pertain to the analytic properties of the Dirichlet series under consideration.

2.
Multiple Dirichlet Series Restricted to a Variety

We aim to investigate the relationship between Selberg class functions and automorphy, with particular emphasis on the significance of being locally generated by finitely many terms.
To this end, we first construct an Euler product by taking
a(\boldsymbol{n})
to be a multiplicative function. However, this Euler product cannot be expressed locally by finitely many terms, which distinguishes it from functions in the Selberg class.
And the set of positive integer solutions to
V
must be closed under multiplication of prime power factors arising from these solutions.

We define the following property:

Property (S).

Let

(n_{1}=\prod_{p}p^{\alpha_{1,p}},\dots,n_{t}=\prod_{p}p^{\alpha_{t,p}}),\quad(n_{1}^{\prime}=\prod_{p}p^{\beta_{1,p}},\dots,n_{t}^{\prime}=\prod_{p}p^{\beta_{t,p}})\in V(\mathbb{Z})

be two integer points on the variety
V
. Then, for every prime
p
and for every choice of exponents

(\gamma_{1,p},\dots,\gamma_{t,p})\in\{(\alpha_{1,p},\dots,\alpha_{t,p}),\;(\beta_{1,p},\dots,\beta_{t,p})\},

we have

\left(\prod_{p}p^{\gamma_{1,p}},\dots,\prod_{p}p^{\gamma_{t,p}}\right)\in V(\mathbb{Z}).

Then we have

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
V
be a variety. The twisted multiple Dirichlet series

(2.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1\\
\boldsymbol{n}\in V(\mathbb{Z})\end{subarray}}\frac{a(\boldsymbol{n})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{s_{i}}}

can be represented as an Euler product for any given arithmetic function
a(\boldsymbol{\cdot})
if and only if
V
satisfies Property (S).

Proof.

The condition that
V
satisfies Property (S) is equivalent to the statement that the set of positive integer solutions to
V
decomposes as a Cartesian product over primes:

V(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})=\prod_{p}V_{p},

where each
V_{p}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{t}
denotes the set of admissible exponent tuples at the prime
p
.

Suppose the series (
2.1
) admits an Euler product decomposition. Then for each prime
p
, we can identify a corresponding subset
V_{p}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{t}
capturing the admissible exponent tuples at
p
.

Conversely, if
V
satisfies Property (S), then using the sets
V_{p}
for each prime
p
, one can factor the sum in (
2.1
) into an Euler product accordingly.
∎

We consider the following equivalent formulation of a system of Laurent monomial equations:

f_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})=0\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,\ldots,m,

where each
f_{i}
is of the form

\omega_{i}f_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})=\prod_{j=1}^{t}x_{j}^{a_{i,j}}-\omega^{\prime}_{i},

with
a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\omega_{i},\omega^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, ensuring that the system admits potential positive solutions.

Let
A=(a_{i,j})_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq m\\
1\leq j\leq t\end{subarray}}
,
\boldsymbol{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{t})
,

\boldsymbol{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{m})
,

\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}}=(\omega^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\omega^{\prime}_{m})
, and

D_{A,a(\boldsymbol{\cdot})}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1\\
\omega_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}^{a_{i,j}}=\omega^{\prime}_{i},~1\leq i\leq m\end{subarray}}\frac{a(\boldsymbol{n})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{s_{i}}}.

When all
\operatorname{Re}(s_{i})
are sufficiently large, we have the expected Euler product representation:

D_{A,a(\boldsymbol{\cdot})}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p~\text{prime}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{t}\geq 0\\
v_{p}(\omega^{\prime}_{i})-v_{p}(\omega_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{t}a_{i,j}\alpha_{j}=0\\
\text{for }1\leq i\leq m\end{subarray}}\frac{a(\boldsymbol{p^{\alpha}})}{p^{\sum_{i=1}^{t}s_{i}\alpha_{i}}}\right),

where
v_{p}(x)
is the
p
-adic valuation of
x
, and
\boldsymbol{p^{\alpha}}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{t})
.

3.
Basic Properties and the Main Theorem

According to the definition, we obtain

D_{A,a(\boldsymbol{\cdot})}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}})=D_{-A,a(\boldsymbol{\cdot})}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Let

A^{(3)}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{(1)}&0\\
0&A^{(2)}\end{pmatrix},

\boldsymbol{s^{(3)}}=(\boldsymbol{s^{(1)}},\boldsymbol{s^{(2)}}),\quad\boldsymbol{\omega^{(3)}}=(\boldsymbol{\omega^{(1)}},\boldsymbol{\omega^{(2)}}),\quad\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime(3)}}=(\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime(1)}},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime(2)}}),

\boldsymbol{n^{(3)}}=(\boldsymbol{n^{(1)}},\boldsymbol{n^{(2)}}),\quad a^{(3)}(\boldsymbol{n^{(3)}})=a^{(1)}(\boldsymbol{n^{(1)}})\cdot a^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{n^{(2)}}).

By definition, we then have

D_{A^{(1)},a^{(1)}(\cdot)}(\boldsymbol{s^{(1)}};\boldsymbol{\omega^{(1)}},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime(1)}})\cdot D_{A^{(2)},a^{(2)}(\cdot)}(\boldsymbol{s^{(2)}};\boldsymbol{\omega^{(2)}},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime(2)}})=D_{A^{(3)},a^{(3)}(\cdot)}(\boldsymbol{s^{(3)}};\boldsymbol{\omega^{(3)}},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime(3)}}).

Moreover, by definition, the following elementary row operations in Gaussian elimination do not affect the value of
D_{A,a(\cdot)}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}})
:

•

Swapping the
i
-th row of
A
with the
j
-th row.

•

Multiplying the
i
-th row of
A
by
-1
, and exchanging
\omega_{i}
and
\omega^{\prime}_{i}
.

•

Adding
b
times the
j
-th row of
A
to the
i
-th row, where
b\in\mathbb{Z}
, and replacing
\omega_{i}
by
\omega_{i}\omega_{j}^{b}
, similarly for
\omega^{\prime}_{i}
.

In particular, let

a(\boldsymbol{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{t}\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j}),

where each
\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(\cdot)
denotes the normalized Fourier coefficient of the
\mathrm{GL}(k_{j},\mathbb{R})
automorphic form
\Pi_{j}
.
In particular, when
\Pi_{j}=\mathrm{1}
,
L(s,\Pi_{j})=\zeta(s)
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\Pi}=(\Pi_{1},\dots,\Pi_{m})
, we define

D_{A,\boldsymbol{\Pi}}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}}):=D_{A,a(\boldsymbol{\cdot})}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}}).

Here, we show that for all
\operatorname{Re}s_{j}>1
, this series can be deduced from certain special twisted moments of a class of families of automorphic
L
-functions associated with Dirichlet twists,
which appear in joint work with Michel
[
3
, §2]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\operatorname{Re}s_{j}>1
and
w_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
for all
1\leq j\leq t
.
Let
q
be a large prime.
Then there exists a positive constant
\eta>0
, depending on the coefficients
a_{i,j}
and the real parts
\operatorname{Re}s_{j}
, such that

(3.1)

D_{A,\boldsymbol{\Pi}}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{(q-1)^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{t}L\left(s_{j},\Pi_{j}\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}^{a_{i,j}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}(\omega_{i})\overline{\chi_{i}}(\omega^{\prime}_{i})\\
+O_{A,\boldsymbol{\Pi},\boldsymbol{s}}(q^{-\eta}).

4.
Three Conjectures

The use of addition in rational functional equations may lead to complications. For instance, when
m=1
and

f_{1}=x_{1}+x_{2}-c\quad(c\text{ is a non-zero constant}),

it becomes impossible to extract an independent product of primes in this case.
Moreover, we conjecture that Property (S) holds exclusively in this case.
That is, constructing multiple Dirichlet series with Euler product expansions by restricting the summation to a variety can only be achieved using Laurent monomial systems.

Conjecture 4.1
.

If a variety
V
satisfies Property (S), then
V
is equivalent to a Laurent monomial system.

From the structural form of the definition of
D_{A,\boldsymbol{\Pi}}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}})
, we observe that, up to the three elementary row operations mentioned above, if every matrix equivalent to
A
necessarily contains a row with three nonzero entries, then many terms will be eliminated in the expansion.
We hope that this can affect automorphy.

Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel
[
1
]
obtained certain special values of the Riemann zeta function as the central moment of two Dirichlet
L
-functions over a single character, an important reason is that all the coefficients of the Riemann zeta function are equal to 1.
What we hope for is that omitting the Fourier coefficients of
\Pi_{1},\dots,\Pi_{t}
can have a genuine impact on automorphy.
Therefore, it is preferable that there is a cuspidal component of rank at least 2.
We conjecture that, in this case, the truncation destroys the automorphic nature of the resulting function.

Conjecture 4.2
.

Let
s_{1}=\dots=s_{t}=s>1
.
Up to Gaussian elimination, if every matrix equivalent to
A
necessarily contains a row with three nonzero entries, and if at least one of the automorphic representations
\Pi_{j}
has a cuspidal component of rank at least 2, then the function
D_{A,\boldsymbol{\Pi}}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}})
is not a product of shifts of automorphic
L
-functions.

We may restrict the summation to different varieties. While such modifications can be expected to disrupt automorphy through changes in the coefficients, the algebraic structure introduced here still preserves the Euler product.
In the setting of continuous integral transforms, many classical results construct inverse Mellin transforms that enable the Mellin transform to be meaningfully restricted to submanifolds. Here, we conjecture that a discrete analogue of this phenomenon may exist.
It is conceivable that one might find a suitable integral representation that yields a functional equation, thereby enabling meromorphic continuation.

Conjecture 4.3
.

There exist
A
,
\boldsymbol{\Pi}
,
\boldsymbol{\omega}
, and
\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}}

satisfying Conjecture (
4.2
), such that the Dirichlet series

D_{A,\boldsymbol{\Pi}}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}})

satisfies a functional equation and admits a meromorphic continuation to the entire complex plane.

At present, the known methods for establishing the existence of a functional equation rely on explicitly constructing an integral representation—usually arising from the known automorphy (at least for representations associated with maximal parabolic subgroups)—and then pairing it with an Eisenstein series, unfolding the integral to obtain the associated
L
-function, whose functional equation is subsequently deduced from that of the Eisenstein series.
In contrast, the framework we consider is expected to lack automorphy entirely. As a result, the final conjecture appears to lie beyond the reach of current techniques, and there is, at present, no known method to either prove or disprove it.

Proof of Theorem
3.1

Proof.

Fix integers
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{m},\omega^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\omega^{\prime}_{m}
, and let

q>(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\omega_{i}\}+\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\omega^{\prime}_{i}\})^{\,1+\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\sum_{j=1}^{t}|a_{i,j}|}

be a sufficiently large prime.
For each fixed automorphic form
\Pi_{j}
with
1\leq j\leq t
, we have Ramanujan bounds on average for the Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}
(see
[
2
, §12]
). Hence, for
\operatorname{Re}s_{j}>1
, the twisted
L
-function admits an absolutely convergent Dirichlet expansion:

L\left(s_{j},\Pi_{j}\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}^{a_{i,j}}\right)=\sum_{n_{j}\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}(n_{j}^{a_{i,j}})}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

Thus, we obtain

(5.1)

\frac{1}{(q-1)^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{t}L\left(s_{j},\Pi_{j}\times\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}^{a_{i,j}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}(\omega_{i})\overline{\chi_{i}}(\omega^{\prime}_{i})\\
=\frac{1}{(q-1)^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{t}\left(\sum_{n_{j}\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}(n_{j}^{a_{i,j}})}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}(\omega_{i})\overline{\chi_{i}}(\omega^{\prime}_{i})\\
=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1}\prod_{j=1}^{t}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\cdot\frac{1}{(q-1)^{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{i}(n_{j}^{a_{i,j}})\chi_{i}(\omega_{i})\overline{\chi_{i}}(\omega^{\prime}_{i})\right)\\
=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{\omega_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}^{a_{i,j}}\equiv\omega^{\prime}_{i}\pmod{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

Now let

L=q^{\frac{1}{\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{t}|a_{i,j}|:1\leq i\leq m\right\}+1}}.

We truncate the sum by removing the terms corresponding to tuples
(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})
for which
\max\{n_{j}:1\leq j\leq t\}>L
, thus

(5.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{\omega_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}^{a_{i,j}}\equiv\omega^{\prime}_{i}\pmod{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\\
=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\leq L\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{\omega_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}^{a_{i,j}}\equiv\omega^{\prime}_{i}\bmod{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\\
\quad+\,O\left(\sum_{j=1}^{t}\left(\sum_{n_{j}>L}\frac{|\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})|}{n_{j}^{\operatorname{Re}s_{j}}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j^{\prime}\leq t\\
j^{\prime}\neq j\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{n_{j^{\prime}}\geq 1}\frac{|\lambda_{\Pi_{j^{\prime}}}(n_{j^{\prime}})|}{n_{j^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{Re}s_{j^{\prime}}}}\right)\right).

Using the Ramanujan bounds on average for the Fourier coefficients (see
[
2
, §12]
), the error term is bounded above by

O\left(L^{1-\min\{\operatorname{Re}s_{j}:1\leq j\leq t\}+\varepsilon}\right),

for any small
\varepsilon>0
.

When all
n_{j}\leq L
, the only possible solution to the congruence

\omega_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}^{a_{i,j}}\equiv\omega^{\prime}_{i}\pmod{q}

is the exact equality

\omega_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}^{a_{i,j}}=\omega^{\prime}_{i}.

Then, using the same method, we have

(5.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{\omega_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}^{a_{i,j}}\equiv\omega^{\prime}_{i}\pmod{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}\\
=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{\omega_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}^{a_{i,j}}=\omega^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{t}\frac{\lambda_{\Pi_{j}}(n_{j})}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}+O\left(L^{1-\min\{\operatorname{Re}s_{j}:1\leq j\leq t\}}\right)\\
=D_{A,\boldsymbol{\Pi}}(\boldsymbol{s};\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\omega^{\prime}})+O\left(q^{\frac{1-\min\{\operatorname{Re}s_{j}:1\leq j\leq t\}}{\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{t}|a_{i,j}|:1\leq i\leq m\right\}+1}+\varepsilon}\right).

This completes the proof.

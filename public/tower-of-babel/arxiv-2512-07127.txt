Digital-Analog-Digital Quantum Supremacy
Daniel A. Lidar

Abstract
Quantum supremacy has been explored extensively in gate-model settings. Here, we introduce a quantum-supremacy framework for a hybrid digital-analog-digital quantum computing (DADQC) model. We consider a device that applies an initial layer of single-qubit gates, a single transverse-field Ising analog block, and a final single-qubit layer before Z Z -basis readout. The analog block approximates Z Z -diagonal Ising evolution, and we prove that the resulting output distribution is within constant total-variation (TV) distance of an Instantaneous Quantum Polynomial-time (IQP) circuit. Our bounds and constructions are established for fully connected as well as bounded-degree hardware graphs, matching a variety of architectures, including trapped-ion, neutral atom, and superconducting platforms. Assuming anticoncentration (which we prove for all-to-all hardware graphs and conjecture for bounded-degree hardware graphs) and an average-case hardness conjecture for the associated complex-temperature Ising partition functions, standard reductions imply that any efficient classical sampler achieving constant TV error collapses the polynomial hierarchy. Our results imply that quantum-supremacy tests are possible on today’s quantum annealers, as well as other devices capable of hybrid digital-analog quantum evolution.



The pursuit of quantum computational supremacy, i.e., the demonstration of a task efficiently solvable by a quantum device but intractable for classical computers
Preskill 2012
, has become a central benchmark for near-term quantum hardware. Following the theoretical proposals of boson sampling
Aaronson and Arkhipov 2011
and Instantaneous Quantum Polynomial-time (IQP) circuits
Bremner et al. 2011
;
Bremner et al. 2016
, experimental efforts have realized quantum advantage on several distinct platforms. These include random circuit sampling (RCS) on superconducting qubits
Arute et al. 2019
;
Zhu et al. 2022
;
Morvan et al. 2024
;
Gao et al. 2025
and trapped ions
DeCross et al. 2025
, as well as Gaussian boson sampling in photonic networks
Zhong et al. 2020
;
Madsen et al. 2022
;
Deng et al. 2023
. Each of these schemes leverages the hardness of approximately sampling from the output distribution of a nonuniversal but physically natural quantum process, under plausible complexity-theoretic assumptions.
A complementary route is the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), a shallow alternating circuit for combinatorial optimization
Farhi et al. 2014
, whose lowest-depth instances have classically intractable output distributions under standard assumptions
Farhi and Harrow 2016
;
Krovi 2022
.

These results rely on discrete gate sequences and specific random ensembles (e.g., Haar-random unitaries or random interferometers). An alternative route to supremacy is through analog Hamiltonian dynamics, where complexity arises from continuous-time evolution under restricted Hamiltonians. This paradigm includes stoquastic adiabatic quantum computation, which uses Hamiltonians whose off-diagonal matrix elements are all real and non-positive in the computational basis
Bravyi and Terhal 2009
and has been extensively reviewed
Albash and Lidar 2018
;
Hauke et al. 2020
; provable quantum speedups are known in some cases
Somma et al. 2012
;
Hastings 2021
;
Gilyén et al. 2021
. The complexity-theoretic status of stoquastic dynamics and their potential for demonstrating supremacy remain unresolved. Recent progress in quantum annealing
King et al. 2021
;
King et al. 2023
;
King et al. 2022
;
Ebadi et al. 2022
;
Munoz-Bauza and Lidar 2025
, including evidence of beyond-classical quantum simulation
King et al. 2025
, motivates revisiting this question.

Here, we consider a minimal hybrid model that bridges the digital and analog paradigms for superconducting flux qubits
Deshpande 2025
, and applies directly to other modalities that implement transverse-field Ising dynamics, such as Rydberg atoms
Glaetzle et al. 2017
;
Bernien et al. 2017
;
Scholl et al. 2021
;
Ebadi et al. 2022
, trapped ions
Kim et al. 2010
;
Islam et al. 2013
;
Monroe et al. 2021
, and superconducting-cavity platforms
Lamata 2017
. The device applies an initial layer of single-qubit gates, then a single analog block of transverse-field Ising evolution, and finally another single-qubit layer. Although the analog block is stoquastic in the computational basis, the terminal single-qubit layer applies random

XY
-plane product rotations, so the overall sampling task is not constrained by stoquasticity. We call this model “digital-analog-digital quantum computing” (DADQC). We show that its output distribution is within constant total-variation distance of an IQP circuit, thereby inheriting standard IQP sampling-hardness assumptions
Bremner et al. 2016
;
Boixo et al. 2018
;
Hangleiter et al. 2019
, one of which (anticoncentration) we prove for all-to-all connected hardware graphs. We do not require adiabaticity; DADQC is intermediate between adiabatic quantum annealing as originally conceived
Kadowaki and Nishimori 1998
and the circuit model, in the spirit of diabatic quantum annealing
Crosson and Lidar 2021
.

Our result situates DADQC alongside RCS, boson sampling, and shallow alternating circuits as a viable route to supremacy
Hangleiter and Eisert 2023
. It shows that a model that is fully compatible with current superconducting quantum annealers
Deshpande 2025
and other modalities capable of hybrid digital-analog QC
Glaetzle et al. 2017
;
Bernien et al. 2017
;
Scholl et al. 2021
;
Ebadi et al. 2022
;
Kim et al. 2010
;
Islam et al. 2013
;
Monroe et al. 2021
;
Lamata 2017
can exhibit sampling hardness equivalent to that of IQP circuits. This extends quantum supremacy beyond the present circuit-model setting, providing a complementary theoretical foundation compatible with analog hardware.

Model
.—Consider
n
qubits occupying the vertex set
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}
of a fixed “hardware graph”
\mathcal{H}_{n}=(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}},\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}})
, whose edges (couplers) specify native two-qubit interactions.
Let
U_{\mathrm{L}}=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}}U_{\mathrm{L},i}
and
U_{\mathrm{R}}=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}}U_{\mathrm{R},i}
be arbitrary single-qubit layers. We assume initialization in
\ket{0}^{\otimes n}
and a
Z
-basis measurement after
U_{\mathrm{R}}
. Denoting by
U_{\mathrm{A}}(T)
the analog unitary, the device implements the total evolution

U_{\mathrm{tot}}(T)=U_{\mathrm{R}}U_{\mathrm{A}}(T)U_{\mathrm{L}},\quad U_{\mathrm{A}}(T)=\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{T}H_{\mathrm{TFI}}(t)dt},

(1)

where
\mathcal{T}
denotes time ordering. The analog evolution is
generated by the transverse field Ising model Hamiltonian

H_{\mathrm{TFI}}(t)\equiv A(t)H_{X}+B(t)H_{\mathrm{I}},

(2)

with transverse field
H_{X}\equiv\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}}X_{i}
and Ising term

H_{\mathrm{I}}\equiv\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}}h_{i}Z_{i}+\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}}J_{ij}Z_{i}Z_{j},

(3)

where
X
and
Z
denote the Pauli matrices.
The local fields
h_{i}
and coupling constants
J_{ij}
are dimensionless; the annealing schedules
A(t),B(t)
have units of energy (we set
\hbar\equiv 1
) and are smooth, with
A(t)\geq 0
monotonically decreasing toward
0
and
B(t)\geq 0
monotonically increasing on
[0,T]
.

For comparison, traditional IQP circuits
Bremner et al. 2011
;
Bremner et al. 2016
have the form

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{IQP}}

\displaystyle=W^{\otimes n}e^{-iH_{Z}}W^{\otimes n},

(4a)

\displaystyle H_{Z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{G}}}v_{i}Z_{i}+\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}}}w_{ij}Z_{i}Z_{j},

(4b)

on an “interaction graph”
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{G}})
, where
v_{i},w_{ij}\in\{\frac{\pi}{8}k\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k=0,\dots,7\}
(the “
\pi/8
grid”) and
W
is the Hadamard transform. Let
s\in\{0,1\}^{n}
denote a bitstring; the outcome distribution of the IQP model after initialization in
\ket{0^{\otimes n}}
is

P_{U_{\mathrm{IQP}}}(s)=\bigl|\bra{s}U_{\mathrm{IQP}}\ket{0^{\otimes n}}\bigr|^{2}.

(5)

Assuming anticoncentration along with average-case hardness (which exploits that
P_{U_{\mathrm{IQP}}}(s)=4^{-n}|\mathcal{Z}(s)|^{2}
, where
\mathcal{Z}(s)
is a suitable complex-parameter Ising partition function, believed
\#
P-hard to approximate), it is classically intractable to approximately sample from
P_{U_{\mathrm{IQP}}}
within constant total-variation (TV) accuracy unless the polynomial hierarchy (
\mathrm{PH}
) collapses
Bremner et al. 2016
;
Boixo et al. 2018
;
Hangleiter et al. 2019
. Recall that the TV distance between two
probability distributions
P
and
Q
is
D(P,Q)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s}|P(s)-Q(s)|
.

Two key differences distinguish DADQC from IQP:
(i) IQP has no transverse field. To overcome this, we will exactly factor out the
H_{X}
dynamics by appropriate one-qubit rotations implemented via
U_{\mathrm{L}}
, and concentrate almost all of the integrated
B(t)
weight into a short final window where
A(t)
is already small; the resulting interaction-picture Hamiltonian is then nearly diagonal in the computational (
Z
) basis.
(ii) The IQP interaction graph
\mathcal{G}
need not match the hardware graph
\mathcal{H}
, which often has bounded degree. We therefore introduce and analyze an implementable random-graph ensemble supported on
d
-regular subgraphs of
\mathcal{H}
(see
1
below), aiming to match hardware graph constraints.

With these ingredients, we choose
\{h_{i},J_{ij}\}
so that the remaining diagonal gate reproduces the appropriate IQP angles (first the
\pi/8
grid, later a graph state) and show that the DADQC output distribution is within a constant TV distance of
P_{U_{\mathrm{IQP}}}
.
This, together with anticoncentration (which we prove for complete graphs) and average-case hardness, will suffice to demonstrate sampling hardness for DADQC.

Emulating IQP via DADQC
.—Let us first assume that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{G}
, so both the DADQC model of
Eqs.

1
,
2
and
3
and the IQP circuit of
Eq.

4
live on the same graph. We later consider the case where the interaction graph
\mathcal{G}
is a subgraph of the hardware
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
\alpha(t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}A(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}
,

G_{X}(\theta)\equiv e^{-i\theta H_{X}}
,
and
S\equiv G_{X}(\alpha(T))=\bigotimes_{j\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}}e^{-i\alpha(T)X_{j}}
.
Transform the Ising Hamiltonian to the interaction picture with respect to the transverse field
A(t)H_{X}
:

\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}(t)\equiv G_{X}(-\alpha(t))H_{\mathrm{I}}G_{X}(\alpha(t))
,
so that
\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}(T)=S^{\dagger}H_{\mathrm{I}}S
.
Then

U_{A}(T)=S\widetilde{U}(T),\quad\widetilde{U}(t)\equiv\mathcal{T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}B(t^{\prime})\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}}.

(6)

Fix
\delta>0
and define the early/late time intervals
\mathcal{I}_{1}=[0,T-\delta]
and
\mathcal{I}_{2}=[T-\delta,T]
, together with

\eta\equiv\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}}B(t)dt\ ,\quad\beta\equiv\int_{\mathcal{I}_{2}}B(t)dt\ ,\quad\Delta\alpha\equiv\int_{\mathcal{I}_{2}}A(t)dt.

(7)

We will choose schedules so that
\eta
and
\Delta\alpha
are small while
\beta
is not.

Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the operator norm (largest singular value), and define
K\equiv\|[H_{X},H_{\mathrm{I}}]\|
.
We can show using a Duhamel-type bound

(see End Matter) that

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{U}(T)-e^{-i\beta\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{I}}(T)}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\|+K\beta\Delta\alpha.

(8)

In analogy to
P_{U_{\mathrm{IQP}}}(s)
[
Eq.

5
], we denote by

P_{U_{\mathrm{tot}}}(s)=\bigl|\braket{s|U_{\mathrm{tot}}(T)|0^{\otimes n}}\bigr|^{2}

(9)

the outcome distribution of the DADQC model.

We now choose the initial digital layer
U_{\mathrm{L}}
to ensure that the transverse field evolution is undone, and compare the resulting DADQC evolution to an IQP-type target via:

Lemma 1
.

Let
U_{\mathrm{L}}=S^{\dagger}W^{\otimes n}
,
H_{Z}=\beta H_{\mathrm{I}}
, and let
U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{R}}
be an arbitrary unitary (not necessarily a product). Define

\hskip-4.26773ptU^{\prime}_{\mathrm{tot}}(T)\equiv U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{R}}U_{\mathrm{A}}(T)U_{\mathrm{L}},\quad U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{IQP}}\equiv U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{R}}e^{-iH_{Z}}W^{\otimes n}.

(10)

Then the induced output distributions satisfy

D\big(P_{U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{tot}}},P_{U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{IQP}}}\big)\leq\eta\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\|+K\beta\Delta\alpha.

(11)

The proof uses unitary invariance,
Eq.

8
, and the data processing inequality (see End Matter).

To ensure that
\beta H_{\mathrm{I}}
matches
H_{Z}
as required in
1
, we choose the schedule
B(t)
[which sets
\beta
via
Eq.

7
] and the static coefficients
\{h_{i},J_{ij}\}
so that

\beta J_{ij},\beta h_{i}\in\bigl\{\frac{k\pi}{8}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k=0,\dots,7\bigr\}.

(12)

Exact target angles as in
Eq.

12
may not be achieved experimentally, but the hardness results for IQP circuits are robust to angle errors

.
Note that for arbitrary hardware graphs with
n=|\mathcal{V}|
qubits and
m=|\mathcal{E}|
edges, and with
|h_{i}|\leq h_{\max}
and
|J_{ij}|\leq J_{\max}
, we have

\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\|\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}|h_{i}|+\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}|J_{ij}|\leq nh_{\max}+mJ_{\max}
, and
K=\|[H_{X},H_{\mathrm{I}}]\|\leq 2\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}}|h_{i}|+4\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}|J_{ij}|\leq 2nh_{\max}+4mJ_{\max}
.
If the hardware graph
\mathcal{H}
has bounded maximum degree

, then [with
Eq.

12
]
both
\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\|,K=\Theta(n)
.
Turning the resulting approximation of an IQP circuit by DADQC into a sampling hardness statement leads us to our first main result, which essentially states that unless
\mathrm{PH}
collapses, no classical polynomial-time algorithm can (weakly) simulate the corresponding DADQC device within a small total variation distance.

Theorem 1

(Supremacy for DADQC)
.

Assume the standard IQP conjectures (anticoncentration together with average-case #P-hardness of approximating output probabilities). Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{\star}>0
such that, for any
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\star}
, one can choose smooth monotone schedules
A(t)
(decreasing to
0
) and
B(t)
(increasing from
0
), and single-qubit layers
U_{\mathrm{L}},U_{\mathrm{R}}
, with the following property:
even in the presence of small parameter errors
|\Delta h^{\prime}_{i}|,|\Delta J^{\prime}_{ij}|=O\bigl(1/(n+m)\bigr)
in the scaled angles
h^{\prime}_{i}=\beta h_{i}
and
J^{\prime}_{ij}=\beta J_{ij}
, any classical probabilistic polynomial-time algorithm that samples from a distribution within total variation distance
<\varepsilon/2
of the DADQC output distribution (i.e., of
U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{tot}}(T)
) would imply that
\mathrm{PH}
collapses (to its third level).

Proof sketch
. Using
1
we choose smooth schedules
A(t),B(t)
and one-qubit layers so that the DADQC output is within TV distance
\leq\varepsilon/4
of an ideal IQP circuit. Small static parameter errors in the scaled angles contribute at most another
\varepsilon/4
. Thus, the DADQC output remains within TV distance
\leq\varepsilon/2
of an IQP circuit output. Any polynomial-time classical sampler within TV distance
<\varepsilon/2
of the DADQC output would then approximate the IQP output within TV distance
<\varepsilon
, contradicting IQP sampling hardness unless
\mathrm{PH}
collapses. See End Matter for
the full proof.

Example
.—We give an example of smooth schedules that meet the requirements of
1
.
Consider

\displaystyle A(t)

\displaystyle=A_{0}\bigl[1-s(t)\bigr],\quad B(t)=B_{0}s(t),

(13a)

\displaystyle\tilde{s}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(1+\tanh\frac{t-(T-\delta)}{\mu}),

(13b)

with
\delta,\mu>0
. To enforce
s(0)=0
and
s(T)=1
, define the normalized schedule
s(t)\equiv\frac{\tilde{s}(t)-s_{0}}{\kappa}
, where
s_{0}\equiv\tilde{s}(0)
and
\kappa\equiv\tilde{s}(T)-s_{0}
. We find, after changing variables and direct integration (see End Matter for details)

\eta\leq\frac{\ln 2}{2}\frac{B_{0}\mu}{\kappa},\quad\Delta\alpha\leq\frac{\ln 2}{2}\frac{A_{0}\mu}{\kappa}.

(14)

Note that these upper bounds are independent of
\delta
. We also find
\kappa\geq 1-2e^{-2\min\{T-\delta,\delta\}/\mu}
, so
\kappa
is exponentially close to
1

\mu

Inserting this into the TV bound of
1

D\bigl(P_{U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{tot}}},P_{U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{IQP}}}\bigr)\leq\frac{\ln 2}{2}\frac{\mu}{\kappa}\Bigl(B_{0}\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\|+K\beta A_{0}\Bigr).

(15)

For bounded-degree hardware, where we already showed that
\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\|,K=\Theta(n)
, it suffices to choose

\mu=\frac{\varepsilon\kappa}{2\ln 2\bigl(B_{0}\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\|+K\beta A_{0}\bigr)}=\Theta(1/n),

(16)

which ensures

\eta\|H_{\mathrm{I}}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
and
K\beta\Delta\alpha\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
,
and thus
D\bigl(P_{U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{tot}}},P_{U^{\prime}_{\mathrm{IQP}}}\bigr)\leq\varepsilon/2
without imposing any constraint on
\delta
.
The scaling
\mu=\Theta(1/n)
means the transition window narrows with system size; this clearly cannot be sustained for arbitrarily large
n
, but a supremacy demonstration just needs to reach
n
large enough to be beyond classical feasibility. Actual values are system-specific and are left for future work.

Supremacy under hardware graph constraints
.—Standard IQP hardness results often assume an all-to-all interaction graph
Bremner et al. 2016
. In contrast, a variety of systems exhibit a fixed bounded-degree hardware graph
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}},\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}})\neq\mathcal{G}
(e.g., the Zephyr graph of quantum annealers
Boothby et al. 2020
), so are not fully connected. Minor-embedding
Choi 2008
;
Choi 2011
or the LHZ scheme
Lechner et al. 2015
can impose effective all-to-all connectivity, but this changes the graph ensemble in other ways
Albash et al. 2016
for which anticoncentration is not guaranteed.

A natural alternative is to work with sparse random ensembles for which anticoncentration is available. In particular, for uniform random
d
-regular graphs (where every vertex has degree
d
and each such graph is chosen with equal probability) combined with random
XY
-plane product measurements, recent work proves anticoncentration for constant
d\geq 3

Ghosh et al. 2025
. However, a uniformly random
d
-regular graph on
n
labeled vertices will, with high probability, include edges outside
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}
, so this ensemble cannot be realized by simply selecting native couplers on a fixed hardware graph.

Therefore, to make a hardware-faithful supremacy statement, we instead introduce a “QPU-restricted” ensemble which is natively implementable and matches our analysis.

(QPU-restricted graph ensemble)

Let

The

\displaystyle=U_{\mathrm{R}}^{(\theta)}e^{-iH^{\prime}_{Z}}W^{\otimes n}=W^{\otimes n}e^{-iH^{\prime\prime}_{Z}}W^{\otimes n}

Conclusion
.—We analyzed a minimal digital-analog-digital quantum computing (DADQC) model: a single continuous transverse-field Ising block sandwiched between arbitrary one-qubit layers.
We proved (
1
) that with properly designed, smooth schedules compatible with present-day annealers, the device unitary is within constant TV distance of an IQP circuit with angles on the
\{\frac{\pi}{8}k\}
grid. This leads to a supremacy result for the DADQC model when the hardware graph matches the IQP graph (
1

To obtain a hardware-faithful result on fixed-topology devices, we matched the IQP ensemble to what such hardware can implement: on each run, the device implements a uniformly random
d

D
-regular hardware graph (
1
) and applies random
XY
-plane product measurements via the final one-qubit layer

Combining our DADQC-IQP closeness bound with a natural average-case hardness conjecture for the corresponding complex-temperature Ising partition functions (
1

D
-regular hardware graphs (
2

1

2

D
-regular hardware graphs.

Conceptually, this places DADQC alongside RCS, boson sampling, and QAOA as a viable route to quantum supremacy, while remaining directly compatible with analog hardware.
Our results motivate several directions: (i) proving Conjectures
1
and
2
; (ii) quantifying robustness under time-dependent control errors and decoherence; and (iii) implementing the DADQC model on current QPUs, such as quantum annealers, trapped ions, and neutral atoms.

\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\left[R_{Z}(-\theta_{i})\Bigl(W|{s_{i}}\rangle\!\langle s_{i}|W\Bigr)R_{Z}(\theta_{i})\right]=\frac{1}{2}I.

(57)

Therefore,
Eq.

53
yields

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}[p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)]=\bra{0^{\otimes n}}U^{\dagger}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}I\Bigr)^{\otimes n}U\ket{0^{\otimes n}}=2^{-n},

(58)

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Restrict to the case
\mathcal{H}_{n}=K_{n}
, so that
\mathcal{G}
is drawn from

\mathrm{Unif}[\mathcal{F}_{d}(K_{n})]
, which coincides with the usual uniform
distribution
\mathcal{G}_{r}(d,n)
on labeled
d
-regular graphs on
[n]
.
Then the second moment satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}\bigl[p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)^{2}\bigr]\leq\ C(d)2^{-2n}.

(59)

for some
n
-independent constant
C(d)
.

Proof.

A
d
-factor of
K_{n}
is exactly a labeled
d
-regular graph on
n
vertices, and conversely any labeled
d
-regular graph on
n
vertices is a
d
-factor of
K_{n}
. Thus the uniform distribution on
\mathcal{F}_{d}(K_{n})
coincides with the uniform distribution
\mathcal{G}_{r}(d,n)
on simple
d
-regular graphs on the vertex set
[n]
, used in Ref.
(
Ghosh et al. 2025
, Cor. 1)
. Hence we may write
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}\sim\mathcal{G}_{r}(d,n),\theta}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\mathcal{F}_{d}(K_{n})),\theta}
interchangeably; as above, we abbreviate the latter as
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}
.

Define the normalized second moment

m_{2}(\mathcal{G},\theta)=2^{n}\sum_{s\in\{0,1\}^{n}}p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)^{2}.

(60)

Ref.
(
Ghosh et al. 2025
, Cor. 1)
shows that, for
3\leq d=o(n^{1/2})
(since we fix
d
this holds for us),

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G}\sim\mathcal{G}_{r}(d,n),\theta}\bigl[m_{2}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\bigr]\leq\ C(d)

(61)

for all sufficiently large
n
, where
C(d)
depends only on
d
and is independent of
n
. Again, by symmetry
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}\bigl[p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)^{2}\bigr]

is independent of
s
(see directly below); denote this common value by
q_{n}(d)
. Then

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}[m_{2}(\mathcal{G},\theta)]=2^{n}\sum_{s\in\{0,1\}^{n}}q_{n}(d)=4^{n}q_{n}(d).

(62)

Solving for
q_{n}(d)
yielding
Eq.

59
.

To complete the proof, let us show that

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}\bigl[p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)^{2}\bigr]
is independent of
s
.
Indeed, let
\rho\equiv|{0^{\otimes n}}\rangle\!\langle 0^{\otimes n}|
,
N_{s}(\theta)\equiv U_{\mathrm{R}}^{(\theta)\dagger}|{s}\rangle\!\langle s|U_{\mathrm{R}}^{(\theta)}
, and

M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\equiv U^{\dagger}N_{s}(\theta)U
, with

U\equiv e^{-iH^{\prime}_{Z}}W^{\otimes n}
. Then, using
Eq.

53
we have
p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)=\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[\rho M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\Bigr]
, so that

p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)^{2}=\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[(\rho\otimes\rho)M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\otimes M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\Bigr].

(63)

Letting
\mathcal{U}\equiv U\otimes U
, we can write

M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\otimes M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\bigl(N_{s}(\theta)\otimes N_{s}(\theta)\bigr)\mathcal{U},

(64)

and therefore

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\bigl[M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\otimes M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\bigr]=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\bigl[N_{s}(\theta)\otimes N_{s}(\theta)\bigr]\mathcal{U}.

(65)

Thus it suffices to show that
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\bigl[N_{s}(\theta)\otimes N_{s}(\theta)\bigr]
is independent of
s
.

Because
U_{\mathrm{R}}^{(\theta)}
factorizes and the angles
\{\theta_{i}\}
are i.i.d. uniform on
[0,2\pi)
,

N_{s}(\theta)\otimes N_{s}(\theta)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\Bigl(N_{s_{i},i}(\theta_{i})\otimes N_{s_{i},i}(\theta_{i})\Bigr),

(66)

where
s_{i}\in\{0,1\}
is the
i
’th bit of
s
, and
N_{s_{i},i}(\theta_{i})=R_{Z}(-\theta_{i})W|{s_{i}}\rangle\!\langle s_{i}|WR_{Z}(\theta_{i})
. The expectation over
\theta
factorizes:

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\bigl[N_{s}(\theta)\otimes N_{s}(\theta)\bigr]=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\bigl[N_{s_{i},i}(\theta_{i})\otimes N_{s_{i},i}(\theta_{i})\bigr],

(67)

so it suffices to prove that for each site
i
,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\bigl[N_{0,i}(\theta_{i})\otimes N_{0,i}(\theta_{i})\bigr]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\bigl[N_{1,i}(\theta_{i})\otimes N_{1,i}(\theta_{i})\bigr].

(68)

Using
Eqs.

55
and
56

N_{s_{i},i}(\theta_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[I+(-1)^{s_{i}}\bigl(\cos\theta_{i}X-\sin\theta_{i}Y\bigr)\Bigr],

(69)

Hence, using
\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}[\cos\theta_{i}]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}[\sin\theta_{i}]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}[\cos\theta_{i}\sin\theta_{i}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}[\cos^{2}\theta_{i}]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}[\sin^{2}\theta_{i}]=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain, after expanding the product and taking the expectation value

\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}}\bigl[N_{s_{i},i}(\theta_{i})\otimes N_{s_{i},i}(\theta_{i})\bigr]=\frac{1}{4}I\otimes I+\frac{1}{8}\bigl(X\otimes X+Y\otimes Y\bigr),

(70)

which is manifestly independent of
s_{i}
. Thus,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\bigl[N_{s}(\theta)\otimes N_{s}(\theta)\bigr]
is the same for all
s\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, and since conjugating by
U\otimes U
does not introduce any
s
-dependence,

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\bigl[M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\otimes M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\bigr]

is also independent of
s
. Finally,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}\bigl[p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)^{2}\bigr]=\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[(\rho\otimes\rho)\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}\bigl[M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\otimes M_{s}(\mathcal{G},\theta)\bigr]\Bigr]

(71)

is independent of
s
, as claimed.
∎

The Paley-Zygmund inequality
Vershynin 2018
states that
for any nonnegative random variable
x
with
0<\mathbb{E}[x^{2}]<\infty
and any
\tau\in(0,1)
,

\mathrm{Pr}\bigl[x\geq\tau\mathbb{E}[x]\bigr]\geq(1-\tau)^{2}\frac{\bigl(\mathbb{E}[x]\bigr)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}[x^{2}]}.

(72)

Applying this inequality to
x\equiv p_{\mathcal{G},\theta}(s)

with
\tau=\frac{1}{2}

yields

\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}\Bigl[x\geq\frac{1}{2}2^{-n}\Bigr]\ \geq\ (1-\frac{1}{2})^{2}\frac{\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}[x]\bigr)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{G},\theta}[x^{2}]}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{4C(d)}.

(73)

Thus we may take
a=\frac{1}{2}
and
b=1/(4C(d))
in
Eq.

20
, which depend only on
d
, and the claimed anticoncentration bound follows. This is exactly the statement of
2
specialized to
\mathcal{H}_{n}=K_{n}
and
\mathrm{Unif}[\mathcal{F}_{d}(\mathcal{H})]=\mathcal{G}_{r}(d,n)
. This concludes the proof of
1
.

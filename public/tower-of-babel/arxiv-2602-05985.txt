Abstract
This work investigates a modified theory of gravity where the Einstein-Hilbert action, including a cosmological constant, is non-minimally coupled to a Yang-Mills field via an R 3 ​ F μ ​ α ( a ) ​ F ( a ) ​ μ ​ α R^{3}F^{(a)}_{\mu\alpha}F^{(a)\mu\alpha} interaction term. We derive a black brane solution for this model, accurate to the first order in the coupling parameter. Using gauge/gravity duality techniques, we then compute two key holographic transport coefficients: the color non-abelian direct current (DC) conductivity and the ratio of shear viscosity to entropy density. Our analysis reveals that both transport coefficients are modified by the non-minimal coupling, with the conductivity bound violated for positive q 2 q_{2} and the Kovtun-Son-Starinets (KSS) bound for shear viscosity violated for negative q 2 q_{2} . In the limit where the non-minimal coupling vanishes, our results consistently reduce to those of the standard Yang-Mills Schwarzschild Anti-de Sitter (AdS) black brane.

4
Shear Viscosity to Entropy Density Ratio

The ratio of shear viscosity to entropy density
\eta/s
is a fundamental transport coefficient that characterizes the fluid dynamics of the dual field theory. We compute this ratio using the membrane paradigm approach for our non-minimal
R^{3}F^{2}
black brane.

We consider metric perturbations of the form:

g_{\mu\nu}\to g_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},

(68)

with the specific perturbation
h_{xy}=h_{xy}(t,r)=\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\phi(r)e^{-i\omega t}
. We work in the radial gauge where
h_{r\mu}=0
.

The quadratic action for the perturbation
\phi(r)
can be obtained by expanding the action (
1
) to second order. For our non-minimal model, the effective action takes the form:

S^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{K}(r)\left[g^{rr}(\partial_{r}\phi)^{2}+g^{tt}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}\right],

(69)

where
\mathcal{K}(r)
is the effective coupling that encodes the non-minimal corrections.

For the
R^{3}F^{2}
coupling, we compute
\mathcal{K}(r)
by expanding the action to second order in
\phi
and extracting the coefficient of the kinetic term. The calculation yields:

\mathcal{K}(r)=1+q_{2}\mathcal{C}(r)+\mathcal{O}(q_{2}^{2}),

(70)

where the correction term
\mathcal{C}(r)
is given by:

\mathcal{C}(r)=-12R(r)^{2}F^{(a)}_{\mu\nu}F^{(a)\mu\nu}-24R(r)^{3}.

(71)

Using the membrane paradigm, the shear viscosity can be expressed as a horizon quantity:

\eta=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{rr}g^{xx}\mathcal{K}(r)\right)\Bigg|_{r=r_{h}}.

(72)

Substituting our background metric (
8
) and evaluating at the horizon:

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\left(\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{L^{2}}e^{-H(r_{h})}\mathcal{K}(r_{h})\right)

(73)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\left(\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{L^{2}}\right)\left[1+q_{2}\mathcal{C}(r_{h})+\mathcal{O}(q_{2}^{2})\right],

(74)

where we used
e^{-H(r_{h})}\approx 1
to first order in
q_{2}
.

The entropy density
s
is given by the Bekenstein-Hawking formula:

s=\frac{1}{4G}\left(\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{L^{2}}\right)=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}\left(\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{L^{2}}\right).

(75)

We consider
\kappa=8\pi G
.

Therefore, the ratio
\eta/s
is:

\frac{\eta}{s}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[1+q_{2}\mathcal{C}(r_{h})+\mathcal{O}(q_{2}^{2})\right].

(76)

Now we compute
\mathcal{C}(r_{h})
explicitly using our background solution. At the horizon
r=r_{h}
, we have:

\displaystyle R(r_{h})

\displaystyle=-12/L^{2}+\mathcal{O}(q_{2}),

(77)

\displaystyle F^{(a)}_{\mu\nu}F^{(a)\mu\nu}(r_{h})

\displaystyle=-2h^{\prime}(r_{h})^{2}e^{2H(r_{h})}+\mathcal{O}(q_{2})=-\frac{2Q^{2}}{r_{h}^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(q_{2}).

(78)

Substituting these into
\mathcal{C}(r_{h})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r_{h})

\displaystyle=-12\left(-\frac{12}{L^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(-\frac{2Q^{2}}{r_{h}^{4}}\right)-24\left(-\frac{12}{L^{2}}\right)^{3}

(79)

\displaystyle=-12\left(\frac{144}{L^{4}}\right)\left(-\frac{2Q^{2}}{r_{h}^{4}}\right)-24\left(-\frac{1728}{L^{6}}\right)

(80)

\displaystyle=\frac{3456Q^{2}}{L^{4}r_{h}^{4}}+\frac{41472}{L^{6}}.

(81)

Therefore, the shear viscosity to entropy density ratio to first order in
q_{2}
is:

\frac{\eta}{s}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[1+q_{2}\left(\frac{3456Q^{2}}{L^{4}r_{h}^{4}}+\frac{41472}{L^{6}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(q_{2}^{2})\right].

(82)

This result shows that the Kovtun-Son-Starinets (KSS) bound
\eta/s\geq 1/(4\pi)
is satisfied for
q_{2}>0
. However, for
q_{2}<0
, the bound is violated.

In the limit
q_{2}\to 0
, we recover the universal value for Einstein gravity:

\frac{\eta}{s}=\frac{1}{4\pi}.

(83)

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have constructed a non-minimal
R^{3}F^{(a)}_{\mu\alpha}F^{(a)\mu\alpha}
black brane solution in AdS spacetime in four dimensions. Due to the complexity of the field equations, we employed a perturbative approach to obtain solutions accurate to first order in the coupling parameter
q_{2}
. Using the framework of gauge/gravity duality, we investigated two important holographic transport coefficients: the color non-abelian DC conductivity and the ratio of shear viscosity to entropy density.

Our calculations demonstrate that the non-minimal
R^{3}F^{2}
coupling significantly affects these transport coefficients. Specifically, we found that the conductivity bound is violated for positive values of
q_{2}
, while the Kovtun-Son-Starinets bound for shear viscosity is violated for negative
q_{2}
. These violations highlight how higher-order non-minimal couplings between curvature and matter fields can modify the transport properties of the dual field theory.

In the limit
q_{2}\to 0
, our results smoothly reduce to those of the standard Einstein-Yang-Mills theory, with the conductivity bound saturated and the KSS bound maintaining its universal value of
1/4\pi
. This consistency check validates our perturbative approach and demonstrates the robustness of our methodology.

The violations of universal bounds observed in our model suggest that non-minimal
R^{3}F^{2}
couplings provide a rich framework for exploring beyond-standard-model physics within the holographic context. Future work could extend this analysis to higher-order perturbations or investigate other holographic observables to further elucidate the implications of such non-minimal couplings in the context of gauge/gravity duality.

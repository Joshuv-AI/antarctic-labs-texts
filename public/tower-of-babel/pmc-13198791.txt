Characterization of Immunolocalization of IP3R-1 in Skin Epithelium and its Changes in Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer: An Immuno-Histochemical and Clinical-Pathological Study
Authors: Cristiane R. Gruber, Thelma L. Skare, Marcos F. Sigwalt, Graziela J. Crescente Rastelli, Fernando I. Tabushi, Allan F. Giovanini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The calcium ion (Ca2+) signaling is a fundamental cellular process vital for proliferation and differentiation. Its dysregulation is a recognized driver of carcinogenesis, contributing to uncontrolled cellular growth, survival, and metastasis. The inositol 1,4,5-triphosphate receptor (IP3R), a pivotal Ca2+ channel located primarily on the endoplasmic reticulum, is critical for mediating intracellular Ca2+ release and is implicated in cancer development.Aim and Objectives:To evaluate the immunolocalization of IP3R-1 in normal skin, squamous cell dysplasia, squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), and basal cell carcinoma (BCC), and to determine its association with clinicopathological features such as genesis, infiltration, differentiation, and aggressiveness.Patients and Methods:This investigation employed a descriptive and analytical study design to examine IP3R-1 expression within human tissue samples. The methodology involved collecting and analyzing archival specimens from patients diagnosed with non-melanoma skin cancers and pre-malignant lesions, as well as normal skin samples for comparative assessment. The role of IP3R-1 in skin cancer was assessed by immunohistochemistry. Expression patterns were analyzed using an IP3R-1 antibody and correlated with clinicopathological data.Results:Immunohistochemical analysis revealed distinct patterns of IP3R-1 expression. IP3R-1 was consistently expressed within the basal layer of normal epidermis, but was absent in suprabasal cells. A marked increase in IP3R-1 expression was observed in atypical cells within dysplastic lesions. Furthermore, significant overexpression of IP3R-1 was evident in SCC and BCC, irrespective of histological subtype or differentiation. However, no correlation was found between IP3R-1 expression and clinicopathological features.Limitations:The findings lack confirmation through direct molecular assays and are based solely on immunohistochemical expression.Conclusion:These findings strongly suggest that IP3R-1 plays a significant role in the development and progression of skin cancer, with its altered expression observed early in carcinogenesis. Nevertheless, its expression does not correlate with typical clinical-pathological aspects of neoplasia.

Introduction

The calcium ion (Ca2+) signaling is an essential intracellular mediator that orchestrates critical cellular processes, including gene transcription and differentiation.[1] However, its aberrant metabolism is a recognized driver of tumor progression.[2] This dysregulation fuels migration, invasion, and angiogenesis, and subsequently promotes carcinogenesis by reprogramming cellular metabolism, leading to increased glutamine utilization, mitochondrial activity, and oncometabolite production.[34]

The inositol 1,4,5-triphosphate receptor (IP3R) family comprises ligand-gated ion channels[56] that exhibit varied expression patterns and participate in diverse signaling pathways.[7] Specifically, IP3R-1 is implicated in epidermal growth factor-induced epithelial-mesenchymal transition and apoptosis in various types of cancers.[78]

However, the expression of basal IP3R-1 in skin squamous epithelium and its role in the aggressiveness or carcinogenesis of the two primary cutaneous neoplasms, squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and basal cell carcinoma (BCC), remain largely undefined. To address this unresolved question, we evaluated the immunolocalization of IP3R-1 in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded samples of normal skin, squamous cell dysplasia, SCC, and BCC, aiming to investigate its correlation with clinical-pathological features such as genesis, infiltration, differentiation, and aggressiveness.

Patients and Methods

Ethical compliance

The study involving human participants was conducted according to the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee of the Instituto Presbiteriano Mackenzie (No. 6.009.786; March 30, 2023), with the study being performed at the Evangelical Medical School Mackenzie of Paraná.

Experimental designs

This cross-sectional, retrospective study analyzed randomly selected formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue samples from 2021 to 2023, obtained from Diagnose Laboratory, Brazil. Samples included 31 BCC, 31 SCC, and 14 actinic keratoses/carcinomas in situ. Ten specimens of tumor-free surgical resection margins served as controls for IP3R-1 evaluation in histologically normal skin.

BCC was graded using the Calonje classification,[9] while SCC was graded using the Broders’ grading system.[10] For didactic purposes, squamous dysplasia was stratified based on intraepithelial cellular atypia, maturation, and mitotic rate, despite non-invasive dysplastic areas being clinically diagnosed as actinic keratosis/SCC in situ. All clinical and pathological data were extracted from clinical and histopathological records for analysis.

Immunohistochemistry

Three-micrometer sections were mounted on coated slides and processed for immunohistochemistry. Following deparaffinization and rehydration, antigen retrieval was performed using 1% pepsin (pH 1.8). Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3% methanol for 20 minutes, followed by blocking with 1% bovine serum albumin (Sigma) for 1 hour at 37°C. Slides were incubated overnight (4°C) with the primary monoclonal anti-IP3R-1 antibody (Santa Cruz Biotechnology Inc., clone E-8: sc-271197) at a 1:100 dilution. Detection was achieved using a universal streptoavidin-biotin peroxidase kit (Dako), with diaminobenzidine (Sigma) as the chromogen. Sections were counterstained with Mayer’s hematoxylin. Tumor-free cerebellar tissue served as the positive control. The negative control replaced the primary antibody with rabbit polyclonal isotype IgG (Abcam) for all samples.

Immunohistochemical analysis and labeling index

Two pathologist-authors independently conducted immunohistochemistry counts and diagnosis reviews, blinded to the original diagnoses to ensure objectivity. Sections were examined by light microscopy at 400× final magnification. The entire neoplastic area was assessed, with normal and dysplastic squamous epithelium evaluated layer by layer. Whole slide images were captured using an Axio Scan Scanner Z1 (Carl Zeiss, Germany), saved in TIFF format, and analyzed with ImageJ (National Institute of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) for cell counting. IP3R-1 expression was scored based on the number of immunopositive cells, irrespective of immunoreactivity intensity or localization (membrane, cytoplasm, or nucleus). The IP3R-1 positive cells/total tumor cell population were recorded using the following scoring system[11]: score 0 (<1% positive cells); score 1 (1%–25% positive cells); score 2 (25%–50% positive cells); score 3 (50%–75% positive cells); and score 4 (>75% positive cells).

Statistical analysis

The IP3R-1 immunohistochemical scores, determined by the MODA (Most Often Detected Area) method (ordinal data on a 0 − 4 scale), were compared between experimental groups using the Mann-Whitney U test. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Basal expression of IP3R-1 in skin epithelium

IP3R-1 expression was predominantly observed in the cytoplasm and nucleus of basal cells within the keratinized stratified squamous epithelium, as well as in those surrounding hair follicles, sebaceous, and apocrine glands. While sporadic cells within the stratum spinosum exhibited nuclear IP3R-1 staining, cells of the stratum lucidum, corneum, and granulosum consistently tested negative for IP3R-1 [Figure 1].

Basal cell carcinoma (BCC)

This section summarizes the detailed immunohistochemical data and their correlation with clinicopathological features (presented fully in Table 1). All analyzed BCC samples exhibited widespread IP3R-1 positivity, with the protein localized in the cytoplasm or nucleus [Figure 2]. Over 95% of cells expressed IP3R-1 across all histological subtypes and invasion depths. However, cells at the expansive invasive front displaying palisaded morphology were notably negative [Figure 2d]. Intriguingly, the squamous epithelium overlying the BCC showed IP3R-1 immunoreactivity in the stratum lucidum, corneum, and granulosum - a pattern distinct from normal squamous tissue [Figure 2].

When the entire epithelial section was considered, the IP3R-1 scoring mode demonstrated proportionality to the extent of dysplasia reflecting the increasing prevalence of IP3R-1 positive dysplastic foci within the whole epithelial context [Table 1].

Over 95% of invasive SCC cells showed cytoplasmic and nuclear IP3R-1 expression, independent of the invasion pattern or differentiation grade [Figure 3].

Consistent with BCC findings, the squamous epithelium overlying the SCC also demonstrated IP3R-1 immunopositivity.

Discussion

To address the limited understanding of IP3R-1’s role in non-melanoma skin cancers,[12] we investigated IP3R-1 expression across healthy skin, dysplastic lesions, SCC, and BCC. IP3R-1 was selected for its pivotal role in Ca2+ signaling, which governs proliferation and oscillation levels[13]. Given that Ca2+ signaling regulates mitochondrial activity and gene expression critical for basal cell homeostasis,[14] our findings of robust IP3R-1 expression in basal epithelial cells (with occasional nuclear localization) suggest heightened cellular activity in this layer relative to superficial layers.

Despite distinct differences in staining between normal, dysplastic, and invasive tissues, this preliminary study precludes interpreting IP3R-1 as a predictive or prognostic factor. Nonetheless, these observations suggest its potential role in cytoarchitectural changes, supported by the notion that altered Ca2+ flux reorganizes metabolic networks to drive carcinogenesis.[15] Emerging evidence indicates that tumor suppressors and oncogenes directly modulate IP3R-1 activity.[8] The striking contrast of strong IP3R-1 expression in atypical cells and its complete absence in adjacent normal epithelium further supports the hypothesis that IP3R-1 upregulation is an early event in skin carcinogenesis.

Consistently, IP3R-1 correlates with the progression and invasion of BCC and SCC, aligning with the hypothesis that Ca2+ signals drive invasiveness by promoting actin polymerization and cellular migration.[16] This invasive process is intricately linked to metabolic reprogramming characterized by a shift toward anabolic pathways where mitochondrial function remains critical.[23] Autophagy is essential for mitochondrial integrity and contributes to tumoral invasiveness by modulating extracellular matrix,[17] often influenced by stromal cell activity.[18]

Despite IP3R-1’s established role in epithelial cancer genesis, our findings showed no association between its expression and clinicopathological parameters or aggressiveness level in SCC. This may be due to its near-ubiquitous expression (>95% positive cells) across all tumor specimens. Intriguingly, the absence of IP3R-1 in palisading cells at the advancing BCC tumor front suggests a potential link between reduced IP3R-1 levels and a more indolent invasive phenotype, possibly modulating cellular motility and growth patterns.

Although a robust clinicopathological correlation was not established, the distinct IP3R-1 expression patterns offer dual promise in dermatologic oncology: as both actionable therapeutic targets and invaluable intraoperative guides. IP3R-1’s multifaceted role makes it a compelling candidate for targeted intervention; selective modulation could disrupt cancer cell survival with minimal impact on healthy tissue.[19] Furthermore, characteristic changes in IP3R-1 immunolocalization provide a powerful opportunity for real-time surgical margin assessment during Mohs micrographic surgery. Leveraging these patterns as an ancillary tool, particularly at the tumor-stroma interface, could offer Mohs surgeons greater clarity, significantly reduce positive margins, and optimizing patient outcomes.[20]

Limitations

Our findings, based on the presence or absence of immunohistochemical expression, lack the confirmation of direct in vivo measurement of IP3R-1 transcription or activity. Future validation via molecular techniques (e.g. quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction or Western blotting) is thus required.

Conclusion

IP3R-1 expression was associated with the development and invasiveness of cutaneous BCC and SCC, although no correlation was found with clinicopathological parameters or cellular differentiation grade. Given the increasing role of ion channels in tumorigenesis, IP3R-1 represents a promising therapeutic target for skin cancer.

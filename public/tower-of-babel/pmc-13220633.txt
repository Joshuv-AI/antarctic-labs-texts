Combined assessment of stromal tumor infiltrating lymphocytes and tumor peroxiredoxin 4 expression improved prognostic stratification in postoperative pancreatic cancer patients
Authors: Yao Liu, Jia Han, Akihiro Shioya, Wenpeng Jiao, Masashi Okuro, Tohru Ito, Sohsuke Yamada
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPancreatic cancer (PC) is characterized by profound immunosuppression and marked biological heterogeneity. Stromal tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) reflect host antitumor immunity, whereas peroxiredoxin 4 (PRDX4) represents tumor redox adaptation. However, their combined prognostic value remains unclear.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 138 resected cases of PC. Stromal TIL density was assessed on hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) sections, and tumor PRDX4 expression was evaluated by immunohistochemistry. Both markers were dichotomized into high and low groups using ROC-derived thresholds. Patients were further classified into four integrated subgroups: TIL-high/PRDX4-low (TH-PL), TIL-high/PRDX4-high (TH-PH), TIL-low/PRDX4-low (TL-PL), and TIL-low/PRDX4-high (TL-PH). Associations with clinicopathological features and disease-specific survival (DSS) were analyzed.ResultsHigh TIL density was associated with less advanced pathological stage and significantly improved DSS, whereas high PRDX4 expression correlated with lymphovascular and perineural invasion as well as advanced stage. An inverse association between TILs and PRDX4 was observed. Integrated subgroup analysis revealed distinct prognostic patterns: the TL-PH subgroup showed the poorest DSS, whereas the TH-PL subgroup demonstrated the most favorable outcomes. In multivariable Cox models, the TL-PH subgroup remained an independent adverse prognostic factor for both 3- and 5-year DSS, while the TH-PL subgroup showed a protective effect, particularly at 3 years.ConclusionsStromal TILs and PRDX4 appear to capture complementary immune and redox features of PC. Their combined assessment may improve prognostic stratification and help to identify a potential redox-associated, immune-excluded subgroup with particularly poor outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13000-026-01786-8.Keywords: Pancreatic cancer (PC), Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), Peroxiredoxin 4 (PRDX4), Tumor microenvironment, Redox regulation, Prognosis

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer (PC) remains one of the most aggressive malignancies, with a 5-year survival rate below 11% despite advances in surgical and systemic therapies [1]. Most patients experience early recurrence even after curative-intent resection [2], underscoring the limitations of conventional clinicopathological parameters in capturing tumor heterogeneity. Given the high postoperative relapse rate and the refractory nature of PC [3], there remains a need for pathology-based markers that more accurately capture its biological complexity. PC is characterized by a dense desmoplastic stroma, heterogeneous tumor cell populations, and a profoundly immunosuppressive microenvironment, all of which contribute to its aggressive clinical behavior and poor therapeutic response [4]. Both tumor-intrinsic features and stromal components interact to shape disease progression, making it difficult for conventional clinicopathological indicators to fully capture tumor behavior. Therefore, biomarkers capable of simultaneously assessing tumor-intrinsic biology and key features of the tumor microenvironment (TME) are essential for improving prognostic stratification and guiding individualized management [5].

Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) represent lymphocytes that migrate from the circulation into the tumor bed [6]. In routine diagnostic practice, TILs are most commonly evaluated within the stromal compartment on hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) slides. They predominantly include CD8+ cytotoxic T cells and CD4+ helper T cells, but may also involve B cells and other immune subsets, collectively serving as a histological surrogate for host antitumor immunity [7]. High stromal TIL levels are associated with favorable survival and therapeutic responsiveness in multiple solid tumors. For example, in breast cancer, particularly triple negative and HER2 positive subtypes, high TILs density strongly predicts improved disease free and overall survival and has been incorporated into routine pathology reporting as a biomarker for chemotherapy benefit [8]. In colorectal cancer, abundant TILs, especially intraepithelial CD3+ and CD8+ lymphocytes, correlate with reduced recurrence and have superior prognostic value compared with traditional TNM staging [9]. Similarly, in melanoma and non-small cell lung cancer, higher TILs infiltration is linked to prolonged survival and enhanced response to immune checkpoint blockade [10, 11]. These findings illustrate that TILs serve as a robust histological surrogate for antitumor immune activity across diverse cancer types. Although PC is traditionally considered an immune-poor malignancy [12], stromal TILs remain a practical pathological marker because they can be readily assessed on routine H&E slides and may still provide clinically meaningful host tumor interactions [13]. Emerging studies suggest that higher TILs levels may delineate patients with relatively better outcomes, underscoring their potential prognostic value even within this desmoplastic tumor type [14]. However, the complex stromal architecture of PC, together with the variability in TILs scoring methods and cut-off definitions, has led to inconsistent findings, limiting their current clinical adoption. These observations suggest that TILs hold promise as a novel pathological evaluation marker in PC, although further studies are required to fully establish their clinical utility in PC.

On the other hand, Peroxiredoxin 4 (PRDX4) represents a redox-regulating enzyme with emerging relevance in PC biology. As the only member of the peroxiredoxin family localized to the endoplasmic reticulum and secretory pathway, PRDX4 detoxifies hydrogen peroxide, supports oxidative protein folding, and modulates multiple redox-sensitive signaling pathways [15, 16]. Beyond its antioxidant functions, PRDX4 also influences ER stress responses, inflammatory signaling, and extracellular matrix remodeling [17]. Overexpression of PRDX4 has been documented in various malignancies and is consistently associated with tumor aggressiveness and poor prognosis, including in urethane-induced lung tumors [18] and gastric cancer [19]. Our previous studies further demonstrated that high PRDX4 expression independently predicts shorter survival in hepatoblastoma [20], whereas low PRDX4 expression in PC is associated with more favorable outcomes [21]. These observations underscore the biological importance of redox regulation in PC. Most PCs harbor oncogenic KRAS mutations that reprogram cellular metabolism, increase oxygen demand, and intensify hypoxia, collectively driving excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production [22]. Under such oxidative pressure, tumor cells depend on antioxidant and redox-buffering mechanisms to maintain survival, highlighting the central role of redox adaptation in PC progression [23]. Despite growing evidence linking PRDX4 to malignancy, its clinical utility as a pathology-based evaluation marker remains limited, and its relationship with the immune microenvironment, particularly stromal TILs, has not been systematically investigated.

We hypothesized that stromal TIL density and tumor PRDX4 expression may be indirectly related in pancreatic cancer, reflecting broader interactions between redox homeostasis and immune regulation. Emerging evidence suggests that oxidative stress pathways can influence antitumor immune activity [24]. Physiological levels of ROS support T-cell activation and migration, whereas excessive ROS can impair T-cell viability and chemotaxis [25]. Conversely, increased antioxidant capacity in tumor cells may attenuate immunogenic stress signals and alter cytokine or chemokine gradients, potentially limiting immune cell recruitment [26]. Within this conceptual framework, PRDX4 has been implicated as a redox associated factor at the interface between oxidative stress regulation and immune-related processes. Experimental studies in wound healing models have shown that PRDX4 can influence immune cell recruitment through effects on cytokine gradients [27]. In tumor settings, PRDX4 overexpression has been associated with enhanced redox buffering capacity and reduced immunogenic stress signaling, which may be associated with a less immune-infiltrated tumor microenvironment [28]. These context dependent observations support a possible, though incompletely defined, association between PRDX4 driven redox adaptation and the degree of stromal immune infiltration. Clarifying this relationship in human pancreatic cancer remains an important objective, particularly from a pathology-based and hypothesis generating perspective.

To address this gap, we analyzed a multicenter cohort of 138 resected PC and assessed stromal TILs density and tumor PRDX4 expression. We further constructed combined subgroups and evaluated their clinicopathological and prognostic implications to explore the potential utility of stromal TILs, tumor PRDX4 expression, and their integrated assessment as pragmatic, pathology based markers for prognostic stratification in resected PC.

Materials and methods

Tissue samples

This study utilized surgical specimens from 138 PC patients, including palliative resection cases, obtained from multiple centers (Kanazawa Medical University Hospital, Independent Administrative Agency National Hospital Organization Fukuyama Medical Center and Kagoshima University Hospital) in Japan between 2007 and 2017. Surgical procedures, pathological processing, and follow-up protocols across all participating centers remained consistent throughout this period. Surgical procedures included pancreaticoduodenectomy, distal pancreatectomy, and total pancreatectomy, performed according to institutional standards. All patients had available pathological records and follow-up information. Exclusion criteria were: (1) perioperative mortality (death within 30 days after surgery or during the same admission); (2) history of other concurrent malignancies; (3) severe comorbidities substantially limiting life expectancy. All specimens were formalin-fixed and paraffin embedded. Classification followed the WHO Classification of Digestive System Tumors (5th edition).

Disease-specific survival (DSS) was defined as the interval from diagnosis or treatment initiation to death directly attributable to pancreatic cancer, with non-cancer related deaths censored at the date of death. Postoperative follow-up consisted of monthly evaluations during the first year and bimonthly to trimonthly thereafter, including abdominal computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and serological assessments, with imaging surveillance performed every 6 months for at least 3 years. Suspected recurrence prompted immediate diagnostic work-up. DSS events were adjudicated through a standardized process: the cause of death was determined based on medical records, imaging findings, and follow-up documentation. Two investigators independently assessed all deaths, and discrepancies were resolved through discussion with a senior pathologist. The overall loss to follow-up rate was 4.3%, and censoring occurred only at the last confirmed clinic visit, ensuring high reliability of DSS classification. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of Kanazawa Medical University (Approval No. I233). The requirement for informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective nature of the study.

Histopathological evaluation and immunohistochemistry (IHC)

All centers used comparable fixation, embedding, and immunohistochemical procedures. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissues from 138 PC cases were sectioned at 4µm and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Stromal TILs were assessed semi-quantitatively on whole-slide H&E sections according to the International TILs Working Group recommendations [29]. Necrotic areas, intratumoral epithelial components, and peritumoral non-neoplastic tissue were excluded, and the percentage of mononuclear inflammatory cells within the stromal compartment was recorded. Given the unique histological characteristics of pancreatic cancer, only tumor-associated desmoplastic stroma at the invasive front was evaluated, while pancreatitis-related inflammation and necrosis-associated infiltrates were excluded. Lymphoid aggregates suggestive of tertiary lymphoid structures were included only if clearly located within tumor-associated stroma. Immunohistochemical staining for PRDX4 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, #PA5-85252, 1:1000) was performed using the Histofine Simple Stain MAX-PO kit (Nichirei Biosciences, Japan). After deparaffinization and rehydration, endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 0.5% hydrogen peroxide for 15 minutes. Sections were incubated with primary antibody overnight at 4℃, followed by secondary antibody for 30 minutes. Visualization was achieved using 3,3’-diaminobenzidine, with hematoxylin counterstaining. Hepatocellular carcinoma served as a positive control, while omission of the primary antibody and low-expressing tissues (skeletal muscle and fibrous stroma) served as negative controls. Tumor PRDX4 expression was quantified as the percentage of positively stained tumor cells across the entire tumor area to account for intratumoral heterogeneity. A positive cell was defined as any tumor cell exhibiting perceptible cytoplasmic staining, regardless of intensity. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses using 3-year DSS determined optimal cut-off values for TILs and PRDX4 based on the maximum Youden index (17.5% for TILs [AUC = 0.708], Fig. 1A and 13.5% for PRDX4 [AUC = 0.703], Fig. 1B). These thresholds were used for pragmatic dichotomization rather than to imply discrete biological cut-offs. To enhance methodological rigor and reproducibility, PRDX4 assessment incorporated several additional considerations. Percentage-based whole-slide evaluation was used instead of hotspot analysis to reduce sampling bias and better capture tumor heterogeneity. A sensitive positivity definition was adopted, given the diffuse endoplasmic reticulum localization of PRDX4 and the potential variability of intensity-based scoring. All slides were digitized using the NanoZoomer Digital Pathology Virtual Slide Viewer (Hamamatsu Photonics, Japan). Three board-certified pathologists (Y.L., A.S., and S.Y.), blinded to clinical outcomes, independently evaluated TILs and PRDX4. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus using a multi-headed microscope. Interobserver reproducibility was excellent among three independent pathologists (Fleiss’κ = 0.74 for TILs and Fleiss’κ = 0.87 for PRDX4), supporting the robustness of the scoring method (Table S1). Importantly, both TILs and PRDX4 were additionally modeled as continuous variables in the survival analyses to evaluate their prognostic effects independently of ROC-based dichotomization (Table S2).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism 10.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Categorical variables were compared using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences were assessed with log-rank tests. Clinically relevant covariates were selected a priori based on established prognostic factors in pancreatic cancer and data availability. These included age, gender, tumor size, differentiation, TNM stage, lymphatic vessel/vascular invasion, and adjuvant therapy. These variables were incorporated into multivariable Cox proportional hazards models to adjust for potential confounding effects and to evaluate the independent prognostic value of TILs–PRDX4 subgroups. Prognostic factors were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression models, with hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) reported. The proportional hazards (PH) assumption was examined using Schoenfeld residual based global tests, with p > 0.10 indicating no violation. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF), with VIF < 2.0 interpreted as acceptable. To minimize overfitting in multivariable analyses, we followed the recommended events per variable (EPV) rule (> 10). The cohort provided 72 DSS events at 3 years and 87 events at 5 years, ensuring sufficient outcome events relative to the number of covariates included in each model and supporting the stability and reliability of the estimated hazard ratios. All statistical tests were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline clinicopathological characteristics

A total of 138 patients with resected PC were included in the study. The baseline clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Most patients were older adults (≥ 60 vs. <60 years: 117 vs. 21), and the gender distribution was balanced (male vs. female: 70 vs. 68). Conventional ductal adenocarcinoma constituted the predominant histological subtype (ductal adenocarcinoma vs. others: 91 vs. 47, including intraductal tubulopapillary carcinoma: 28; intraductal papillary mucinous carcinoma: 9; other subtypes: 10). Tumors were more frequently located in the pancreatic head (head vs. body/tail: 88 vs. 50). Larger tumors were more common (> 20 mm vs.≤20 mm: 95 vs. 43). Tumor differentiation showed a broad distribution (well vs. moderate vs. poor: 55 vs. 63 vs. 20). Most patients exhibited invasive features (lymphatic invasion: 115; vascular invasion: 121; perineural invasion: 109). Pathological T stage was predominantly advanced (Tis/T1 vs. T2 vs. T3 vs. T4: 19 vs. 10 vs. 75 vs. 34). Nodal and distant metastases were present in a subset of cases (N1-2: 81; M1: 8). TNM staging indicated that most patients were stage II (stage 0-I vs. II vs. III vs. IV: 15 vs. 86 vs. 29 vs. 8). 42 patients received postoperative adjuvant therapy, while 96 patients did not receive postoperative adjuvant therapy. Stromal TILs were predominantly low (low vs. high: 81 vs. 57), whereas tumor PRDX4 expression showed an approximately equal distribution (low vs. high: 68 vs. 70). During follow-up, disease-specific survival events occurred in a substantial proportion of patients (3-year DSS events vs. 5-year DSS events: 72 vs. 87).

Associations of stromal TILs and tumor PRDX4 expression with clinicopathological features and prognosis in PC

There was a statistically significant association between stromal TILs density and tumor PRDX4 expression (p = 0.030). Low TILs were more frequently observed in tumors with high PRDX4 expression (47 of 70 cases), whereas tumors with low PRDX4 expression showed an approximately equal distribution of low and high TILs (34 of 68 vs. 34 of 68 cases) (Table 2). Clinicopathological correlation analyses revealed that high TILs infiltration was significantly associated with a lower malignant burden in PC. High-TILs tumors showed a significantly higher proportion of early pT stage (Tis-T2) (20/57 vs. 9/81, p = 0.001), indicating that TILs-rich tumors tend to be smaller and less invasive. Additionally, low TILs infiltration was more frequently observed in patients with advanced TNM stage (ⅡB-Ⅳ) (59/81 vs. 32/57, p = 0.042). In contrast, TIL levels were not significantly associated with age, gender, histopathological subtype, location, tumor size, differentiation, lymphatic vessel invasion, vascular invasion, perineural invasion, pN, and pM (all p > 0.05) (Table 3). Kaplan-Meier analyses demonstrated that patients with low TIL infiltration showed significantly poorer 3-year and 5-year DSS compared with those with high TIL levels (Fig. 3A, B; p < 0.001).

On the other hand, high PRDX4 expression was significantly associated with several aggressive clinicopathological features in PC. Tumors with high PRDX4 were more likely to exhibit lymphatic vessel invasion (66/70 vs. 49/68, p < 0.001), vascular invasion (68/70 vs. 53/68, p = 0.001), and perineural invasion (61/70 vs. 48/68, p = 0.021). High PRDX4 expression was also correlated with more advanced pT stage, with 62 of 70 high-PRDX4 tumors classified as T3-T4 compared with 47 of 68 in the low-expression group (p = 0.005). Similarly, high PRDX4 expression was significantly associated with nodal metastasis (pN1-2) (51/70 vs. 30/68, p = 0.001) and more advanced TNM stage (ⅡB-Ⅳ) (57/70 vs. 34/68, p < 0.001). In contrast, no significant associations were observed between tumor PRDX4 expression and age, gender, histopathological subtype, location, tumor size and differentiation (all p > 0.05) (Table 3). Kaplan-Meier analyses demonstrated that high PRDX4 expression was associated with markedly reduced survival relative to low PRDX4 expression (Fig. 3C, D; p < 0.001). Importantly, when analyzed as continuous variables, both TILs and PRDX4 remained significantly associated with DSS, supporting that the observed prognostic effects were not dependent on dichotomization based on ROC-derived cut-offs (Table S2).

Integrated TILs-PRDX4 subgroups define distinct clinicopathological subgroups

To better capture the interplay between PRDX4 expression and immune infiltration, patients were stratified into four combinational subgroups: TIL-high/PRDX4-low (TH-PL), TIL-high/PRDX4-high (TH-PH), TIL-low/PRDX4-low (TL-PL), and TIL-low/PRDX4-high (TL-PH). As summarized in Table 4, the TL-PH subgroup displayed the most aggressive clinicopathological features, including the highest frequencies of lymphatic (93.6%) and vascular invasion (100%), the greatest proportion of advanced pT stages (97.9%), and the lowest percentage of early TNM stage cases (19.1%, p = 0.008). The TH-PL subgroup exhibited the most favorable features, with lower invasion rates (lymphatic 70.6%, vascular 70.6%) and the highest proportion of early-stage disease (55.9%, p = 0.002). Kaplan-Meier analyses showed that tumors with TL-PH subgroup had the worst survival outcomes at both 3 and 5 years (Fig. 3E, F), whereas the TH-PL subgroup consistently exhibited the most favorable DSS (Fig. 3G, H). Across all four integrated subgroups—TH-PL, TH-PH, TL-PL, and TL-PH—survival curves diverged significantly (Fig. 3I, J; log-rank p < 0.001). Tumors with abundant TILs and low PRDX4 formed the best-prognosis group, while those lacking TILs and expressing high PRDX4 identified a distinctly high-risk population.

Cox regression analyses identify TILs-PRDX4 subgroups as independent predictors of DSS

Cox regression analyses further supported these findings. In univariate analysis of 3-Year DSS, several clinicopathological factors, including tumor size (HR = 1.883, P = 0.026), differentiation (HR = 1.653, p = 0.003), vascular invasion (HR = 2.240, P = 0.016), and TNM stage (HR = 2.991, p < 0.001) were significantly associated with poor outcome. When focusing on the combined TILs-PRDX4 subgroups, the TL-PH subgroup displayed the worst prognosis (HR = 3.256, 95% CI 1.617–6.555, p = 0.001), whereas the TH-PL subgroup had the most favorable survival (HR = 0.286, 95% CI 0.178–0.861, p < 0.001) (Fig. 4A). In multivariate analysis of 3-Year DSS adjusting for tumor size, differentiation, vascular invasion, TNM stage, the TL-PH subgroup remained an independent adverse prognostic factor (HR = 3.553, 95% CI 1.532–6.439, p = 0.006), while TH-PL retained a significant protective effect (HR = 0.482, 95% CI 0.232–0.617, p = 0.012) (Fig. 4B). For 5-Year DSS, univariate analysis identified tumor differentiation (HR = 1.400, 95% CI 1.034–1.896, p = 0.030), vascular invasion (HR = 1.972, 95% CI 1.162–2.854, p = 0.020), and TNM stage (HR = 2.217, 95% CI 1.354–3.632, p = 0.002) as significant prognostic indicators. Among the combined subgroups, the TL-PH subgroup again exhibited a markedly increased risk of disease-specific death (HR = 2.182, 95% CI 1.266–3.759, p = 0.005), whereas the TH-PL subgroup was associated with superior survival (HR = 0.366, 95% CI 0.236–0.867, p < 0.001) (Fig. 4C). In the multivariate model, TL-PH remained independently predictive of poor outcome (HR = 2.476, 95% CI 1.215–3.921, p = 0.012) after adjusting for significant covariates, while the protective effect of TH-PL showed a borderline trend (HR = 0.520, 95% CI 0.223–1.211, p = 0.130) (Fig. 4D).

Model diagnostics supported the validity and stability of the multivariable Cox analyses. Schoenfeld residual tests indicated no violation of the proportional hazards assumption for either the 3- or 5-year DSS models (global p = 0.425 and 0.391). Multicollinearity was minimal, with all VIF values < 2.0 and tolerance > 0.75 (Table S3). In additional multivariable models incorporating adjuvant therapy and stepwise adjustment of clinicopathological variables (Table S4), the prognostic significance of the TIL-PRDX4 subgroups remained consistent. The TL-PH subgroup was consistently associated with increased risk of disease-specific death, whereas the TH-PL subgroup retained a protective effect across models.

Subtype-restricted analysis in conventional PDAC supports the robustness of TIL-PRDX4 prognostic stratification

To address the potential impact of histological heterogeneity, we performed a subtype-restricted analysis limited to conventional pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC, n = 91). Consistent with the findings observed in the overall cohort, stromal TIL density and tumor PRDX4 expression remained significantly associated with patient prognosis in PDAC. Kaplan-Meier survival analyses demonstrated that patients with low TIL infiltration exhibited significantly poorer 3-year and 5-year DSS compared with those with high TIL levels (Fig. 5A, B). Similarly, high PRDX4 expression was associated with markedly reduced survival relative to low PRDX4 expression (Fig. 5C, D). When patients were stratified into the four integrated TIL–PRDX4 subgroups, a clear survival gradient was again observed (Fig. 5E–J). The TIL-low/PRDX4-high (TL-PH) subgroup consistently showed the worst prognosis, whereas the TIL-high/PRDX4-low (TH-PL) subgroup demonstrated the most favorable outcomes.

Discussion

In this study, we examined stromal TIL density and PRDX4 expression as two distinct pathological features in resected PC and evaluated how each relates to tumor aggressiveness and patient prognosis. The analyses revealed several key findings. First, high stromal TIL density correlated with earlier pathological stage and significantly better survival outcomes, supporting its role as a favorable prognostic indicator even within the generally immunosuppressive microenvironment of PC. Second, high PRDX4 expression showed strong associations with multiple invasive pathological characteristics, including lymphatic, vascular, and perineural invasion as well as advanced pT stage and was consistently linked to poor DSS, indicating that PRDX4 may reflect an intrinsically aggressive tumor phenotype. Third, integrating the two markers into four combined subgroups provided a more refined stratification of survival risk compared with either marker alone: patients with the TIL-low/PRDX4-high (TL-PH) subgroup had the worst outcomes, whereas those with TIL-high/PRDX4-low (TH-PL) showed the most favorable prognosis. These findings collectively suggest that tumor redox status and stromal immune infiltration represent two clinically relevant but simplified pathological dimensions that can be captured using routine histological assessment. Importantly, the present study was not designed to dissect the full complexity of the PC immune microenvironment, but rather to determine whether readily available pathological features retain prognostic value in real-world clinical practice. Recent high-dimensional studies have provided deep insights into the complexity of the PC immune microenvironment. However, these approaches are not readily applicable to routine diagnostic practice. In contrast, the aim of the present study was not to comprehensively dissect immune cell subsets, but to evaluate whether simple, reproducible, pathology based markers can capture clinically meaningful biological states and improve prognostic stratification in real world settings.

In this cohort, higher stromal TILs density was associated with a more favorable clinical outcome for patients, reflecting a stronger host antitumor immune response and not a protective effect on tumor cells. Patients with TIL-high tumors were more often classified at earlier pathological T stages and exhibited significantly better DSS, suggesting that abundant lymphocytic infiltration corresponds to a microenvironment in which the host immune system was more active against tumor progression. Although our retrospective pathology based study cannot directly establish mechanistic causality, the observed associations align with the known biological functions of TILs—namely, their capacity to exert cytotoxic effects, support pro-inflammatory signaling, and enhance antigen presentation [30–32]. In PC, where desmoplasia and immunosuppressive cues typically hinder immune cell entry [33], the presence of high stromal TILs in our specimens likely reflected tumors that were less dominated by immunosuppressive pathways and more permissive to immune engagement. Therefore, in the context of our findings, stromal TILs served as a histological indicator for stronger host antitumor immunity and reflected tumors with relatively lower malignant potential.

PRDX4, by contrast, emerged in our cohort as a marker associated with tumor-intrinsic aggressiveness. High PRDX4 expression was significantly enriched in tumors displaying multiple invasive pathological features and was consistently linked to poorer DSS. Although our study was not designed to investigate molecular mechanisms, several biological considerations may help interpret these associations. PRDX4 functions not only as an ER-resident antioxidant but also as a key regulator of oxidative protein folding and redox-sensitive signaling pathways, potentially supporting tumor cell adaptation to hypoxia, metabolic stress, and rapid proliferation [34]. In this context, elevated PRDX4 may be involved in tumor cell redox adaptability, potentially enabling more efficient neutralization of ROS and maintaining the signaling and metabolic conditions required for sustained malignant growth [35, 36]. Prior studies also suggested that PRDX4 has been reported to modulate ER-stress–related pathways, particularly those governing oxidative protein folding and unfolded protein response (UPR) signaling, thereby enhancing tumor cell adaptability under stress [37]. PRDX4 has also been shown to influence cytokine-associated regulatory processes, including the modulation of inflammatory mediators and secretory dynamics within the tumor microenvironment [38]. By integrating its roles in ER redox regulation and oxidative stress adaptation, PRDX4 overexpression may help explain the observed association between high PRDX4 expression and reduced stromal lymphocytic infiltration [39]. While our findings cannot directly support these mechanisms, the consistent association between high PRDX4 expression and invasive pathological characteristics are consistent with the possibility that PRDX4 may reflect a more aggressive tumor phenotype and may be involved in processes related to tumor progression.

From a biological perspective, insufficient TIL recruitment may be associated with a tumor microenvironment characterized by enhanced antioxidant activity, which has been reported to influence immunogenic cell death, ROS-related signaling, and cytokine production involved in lymphocyte recruitment [40, 41]. Our analysis revealed a significant inverse correlation between PRDX4 expression and stromal TILs (p = 0.030), providing clinical observations consistent with a potential association between tumor redox status and immune exclusion. This association is biologically plausible given the reported role of PRDX4 in regulating ER stress and the unfolded protein response (UPR). Specifically, PRDX4-mediated redox buffering has been suggested to modulate ROS levels, which are known to act as “danger signals” in immunogenic cell death. In this context, PRDX4-high tumors may be associated with a less immune-infiltrated microenvironment; however, this interpretation remains speculative and requires further experimental validation, as observed in the TL-PH subgroup. Within this biological framework, the TL-PH subgroup was associated with the poorest survival outcomes, which may reflect the coexistence of limited immune infiltration and increased tumor redox activity, whereas the TH-PL subgroup exhibited the most favorable prognosis. Importantly, these associations remained significant in multivariable Cox regression models, in which the TL-PH phenotype persisted as an independent adverse predictor and the TH-PL phenotype conferred a protective effect after adjustment for major clinicopathological factors, including tumor size, differentiation, vascular invasion, and TNM stage. The clear survival gradient observed across the four integrated TIL-PRDX4 subgroups therefore highlights their independent prognostic relevance and supports the value of evaluating TILs together with PRDX4, rather than either marker alone. Although the cut-off values were derived using ROC analysis, their robustness was supported by consistent results in continuous variable analyses and subtype-restricted PDAC cohorts. Importantly, the multivariable models incorporated key clinicopathological factors known to influence prognosis in pancreatic cancer, including tumor stage, differentiation, vascular invasion, and adjuvant therapy. The persistence of the prognostic significance of the TIL-PRDX4 subgroups after adjustment for these variables suggests that this classification captures complementary biological information beyond conventional clinical parameters.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective, multicenter design within a Japanese population may introduce selection bias and limits generalizability, warranting validation in more diverse ethnic cohorts. In addition, the proposed TIL-PRDX4 classification has not yet been validated in an independent external cohort, which is necessary before clinical application can be established. Second, although the use of semi-quantitative histopathology and ROC-derived cut-offs provided good interobserver reproducibility, these approaches may not fully capture intratumoral heterogeneity or completely eliminate the risk of statistical overfitting. Furthermore, some potentially relevant clinical covariates were not available in this cohort, including detailed chemotherapy regimens, molecular alterations (e.g., KRAS mutation status), and patient performance status, which may influence prognosis and should be incorporated in future studies. Most importantly, this study is observational and lacks direct mechanistic validation. While our findings suggest a potential association between tumor redox status and immune exclusion, further functional studies are required to elucidate the underlying biological mechanisms. In addition, given the rapidly evolving landscape of high-dimensional immune profiling in pancreatic cancer, our results should be interpreted as complementary to, rather than a substitute for, more detailed immunophenotyping approaches.

Conclusions

Stromal TILs and PRDX4 appear to reflect complementary aspects of the immune microenvironment and tumor redox status in pancreatic cancer. Their combined assessment may improve prognostic stratification, particularly in identifying patients with relatively high-risk (TL-PH) and more favorable (TH-PL) profiles. These findings suggest that integrated immune-redox markers may serve as a practical pathology-based approach for risk assessment in resected PC.

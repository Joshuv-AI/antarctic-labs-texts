Abstract
We consider a variety of criteria for selecting k k representative columns from a real matrix 𝑨 \boldsymbol{A} with rank ⁡ ( 𝐀 ) ≥ k \rank(\boldsymbol{A})\geq k . The criteria include the following optimization problems: absolute volume and S-optimality maximization; norm and condition minimization in the two-norm , Frobenius norm and Schatten p p -norms for p > 2 p>2 ; stable rank maximization; and the new criterion of relative volume maximization. We show that these criteria are NP hard and do not admit polynomial time approximation schemes (PTAS). To formulate the optimization problems as decision problems, we derive optimal values for the subset selection criteria, as well as expressions for partitioned pseudo-inverses.

1.1
Complexity Theory

We focus on decision problems with a binary output ‘yes’ or ‘no’, and adopt the Turing model of computation,
where inputs are rational numbers and the input size is measured in the number of bits.

The complexity class
P
consists of decision problems that a deterministic Turing machine can solve in polynomial time, while the complexity class
NP
consists of decision problems for which a deterministic Turing machine can verify an output of ‘yes’ in polynomial time. A problem is
NP hard
if there is polynomial time reduction to this problem from any problem in NP.
A decision problem is
NP complete
if it is in NP and it is also NP hard.
Thus, an NP hard problem is at least as hard as an NP complete problem
[
17
, Section 5.1]
.

A standard strategy for establishing NP hardness performs a polynomial time reduction from an NP complete problem
to the problem at hand. This reduction makes it possible to convert any algorithm for solving the problem at hand into a corresponding algorithm for an NP complete problem.
In particular, a reduction that
operates in polynomial time
makes it possible to convert any polynomial time
for the problem at hand to a corresponding polynomial time algorithm for the NP complete problem

[
17
, Section 1.5]
.
Following
[

, the instance of X3C is true if and only if
\boldsymbol{A}
has a submatrix
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{(3M)\times M}
with orthonormal columns. Lemma
1
implies that this is equivalent to

3.2
No PTAS for volume maximization

Lemma
1
implies that a matrix
with unit-norm columns has maximal volume equal to 1.
We derive an inapproximability threshold of
2\sqrt{2}/3
.

Theorem 4

It is NP hard to approximate volume maximization within a factor of

2\sqrt{2}/3
. Thus, unless P=NP, there is no PTAS for volume maximization.

Proof 4

The setup is the same as in the proof of Theorem
2
.
Assume that the X3C instance is false.

The proof is by induction over the number of columns
k
.

1.

For
k=2
, two distinct sets can overlap in one or in two elements.
When the sets share a single element, the nonzero elements (
6
) of the relevant two columns

\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}
have singular values

\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})=2/\sqrt{3}
and
\sigma_{2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})=\sqrt{3/2}
.
When the sets share two elements, the nonzero elements
(
7
) of the relevant two columns
\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}

have singular values
\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})=\sqrt{5/3}
and

\sigma_{2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})=\sqrt{1/3}
.
Between the two, (
6
) achieves the largest volume.
Hence, for matrices
\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}
with
k=2
columns, (
6
) implies that

\vol(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})=\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})\sigma_{2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}=\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}.

2.

For matrices
\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}
with
k-1
columns, assume that

\vol(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1})\leq\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}.

3.

Let
\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}&\boldsymbol{c}\end{bmatrix}

be a matrix with
k
columns, where
\|\boldsymbol{c}\|_{2}=1
.
The Gram matrix is

\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}&\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}^{T}\boldsymbol{c}\\
\boldsymbol{c}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}&\boldsymbol{c}^{T}\boldsymbol{c}\end{bmatrix}

and
[
12
, (2)]
implies

\vol(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k})^{2}=\det(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1})\det(\boldsymbol{c}^{T}\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{c}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{c}).

(8)

Since the second factor is a scalar and contains the orthogonal project
\boldsymbol{P}\equiv\boldsymbol{I}-\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}^{\dagger}
, we can write it as

\det(\boldsymbol{c}^{T}\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{c}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{c})=\boldsymbol{c}^{T}\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{c}=\|\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{c}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\boldsymbol{c}\|_{2}^{2}=1.

Inserting this into the previous expression for the squared volume gives

\vol(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k})^{2}\leq\det(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}^{T}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1})=\vol(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1})^{2}\leq\left(\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\right)^{2}.

As in the proof of Theorem
2
, we conclude that it is NP hard to approximate the maximal volume within a factor of
2\sqrt{2}/3
. Thus, there is no PTAS unless P=NP.

Theorem
4
presents a rigorous proof for the inapproximability threshold
from
[
9
, Theorem 6]
.
The exponential inapproximability of the volume is presented in
[
10
, Theorem 5]
, in the sense that there exists
0<\delta<1
and
c>0
such that volume maximization is not
approximable with
2^{-ck}
for
k=\delta n
unless
P=NP
.
However, the estimation of the
volume from inner products of selected vectors
in
[
10
, Proof of Lemma 16]
is not clear.

For

We want to find

\boldsymbol{C}
of
\boldsymbol{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}

p

Decision problems

Given a matrix
\boldsymbol{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}

,

Two-norm minimization

\boldsymbol{A}

-norm minimization

p>2

\boldsymbol{A}

?

Theorem 6

The decision problems: two-norm minimization and Schatten
p
-norm minimization are NP hard.

Proof 6

The proof is the same as that of Theorem
3
, except we use Lemma
4
instead.

Remark
8.1
explains the requirement

The meaningful bound for

In the case

4.2
No PTAS for two-norm minimization

Lemma
4
implies that a matrix with unit-norm columns has a minimal two-norm equal to 1.

2/\sqrt{3}
from

It is NP hard to approximate two-norm minimization within a factor of
2/\sqrt{3}

Proof 7

The setup is the same as in the proof of Theorem
2
, and we present an induction over
the number of columns
k
in
\boldsymbol{C}
to show that
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}\geq 2/\sqrt{3}
.

1.

For
k=2
, two distinct sets can overlap in one or
in two elements.
When the sets share a single element, the nonzero elements (
6
) of the relevant two columns

\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}
have largest singular value

\|\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}\|_{2}=\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})=2/\sqrt{3}
.
When the sets share two elements, the nonzero elements
(
7
) of the relevant two columns
\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}

have largest singular value
\|\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}\|_{2}=\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})=\sqrt{5/3}
. Between the two, (
6
) represents the smaller of the two large singular values.
Hence, for matrices
\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}
with
k=2
columns

\|\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2}\|_{2}=\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{2})\geq 2/\sqrt{3}
.

2.

For matrices
\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}
with
k-1
columns,
assume that

\|\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}\|_{2}=\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1})\geq 2/\sqrt{3}.

3.

Let
\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1}&\boldsymbol{c}\end{bmatrix}

be a matrix with
k
columns. From
singular value interlacing
[
18
, Corollary 8.6.3]

follows

\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k})\geq\sigma_{1}(\hat{\boldsymbol{C}}_{k-1})

\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
is defined as

\sr(\boldsymbol{C})\equiv{\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}}/{\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}^{2}},

and
\sr(\boldsymbol{0}_{m\times k})=0
.
The extension to Schatten
p-
norms for
p>2

[
23
]
is

\sr_{(p)}(\boldsymbol{C})\equiv{\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}^{p}}/{\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}^{p}},

and
\sr_{(p)}(\boldsymbol{0}_{m\times k})=0
.
Obviously,
\sr_{(2)}(\boldsymbol{A})=\sr(\boldsymbol{A})
.

We want to find
k
columns
\boldsymbol{C}
of
\boldsymbol{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
that
maximize the
p
-stable rank
\max_{\boldsymbol{C}}{\sr_{(p)}(\boldsymbol{C})}
for
p\geq 2
.

7.1
Decision problems

Given a matrix
\boldsymbol{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{A})\geq k
and unit-norm columns
\|\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{2}=1
,
1\leq j\leq n
,
and parameter
b>0
.

1.

Stable rank maximization
:

Does
\boldsymbol{A}
have a submatrix
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\sr(\boldsymbol{C})\geq b
?

2.

p
-stable rank maximization
:

Does
\boldsymbol{A}
have a submatrix
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\sr_{p}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq b
?

Theorem 16

The decision problems of stable rank maximization and
p
-stable rank maximization for
p>2
are NP hard.

Proof 16

The proof is the same as that of Theorem
3
but uses Lemma
8
instead.

7.2
No PTAS for stable rank maximization

Lemma
8

implies that, for a matrix with
k
unit-norm columns, the
maximal value of the stable rank equals
k
.
We derive an inapproximability threshold of
\tfrac{3}{4}k
.

Theorem 17

It is NP hard to approximate stable rank
maximization within a factor of
3/4
. Thus, unless P=NP, there is no PTAS for stable rank maximization.

Proof 17

The setup is the same as in the proof of Theorem
2
.
Assume that X3C instance is false, in which
case Theorem
7
implies

\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}\geq 2/\sqrt{3}
.
Hence

\sr(\boldsymbol{C})=\frac{\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{3}{4}\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{3}{4}k,

because,
regardless of overlaps, the columns of
\boldsymbol{C}
have unit norm,
so (
10
) implies
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}=k
.

8
Optimal values for subset selection criteria

To express the optimization versions of the subset selection criteria as decision problems, we derive optimal values for: maximal volume and S-optimality (Section
8.1
),
maximal relative volume (Section
8.2
), minimal two-norm and Schatten
p
-norms
(Section
8.3
), minimal pseudo-inverse norms (Section
8.4
),
minimal condition numbers (Section
8.5
), and maximal stable ranks
(Section
8.6
).

8.1
Maximal volume and S-optimality

We show that for matrices with unit-norm columns, the
volume and S-optimality are bounded above by 1; and that
only matrices with orthonormal columns have
a maximal volume and S-optimality equal to 1.

Lemma 1

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
and
\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{2}=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Then each of the following inequalities holds with equality if and only if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal
columns.

1.

Volume:
\vol(\boldsymbol{C})\leq 1
.

2.

S-optimality:
\sopt(\boldsymbol{C})\leq 1
.

Proof 18

The assumptions imply

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{2}^{2}=k.

(10)

We tackle each criterion, in turn.

1.

Volume maximization:

From (
10
) and the relation between the arithmetic and geometric means follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}1=\frac{\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}}{k}=\frac{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}+\cdots+\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}}{k}&\geq\sqrt[k]{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}\cdots\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}}\\
&=\vol(\boldsymbol{C})^{2/k}.\end{split}

(11)

Thus
\vol(\boldsymbol{C})\leq 1
, which shows the inequality. Alternatively this also follows from Hadamard’s inequality

[
22
, Corollary 7.8.3]

and the fact that the columns have unit two-norm.

As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then all singular values
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Hence
\vol(\boldsymbol{C})=1
.

Conversely, if
\vol(\boldsymbol{C})=1
, then
\vol(\boldsymbol{C})^{2/k}=1
, and
(
11
) implies that the arithmetic and geometric means are the same. This can only happen if all
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})
are equal. Thus,
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
, and
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

2.

S-optimality maximization:
The inequality follows from Hadamard’s inequality
[
22
, Theorem 7.8.1]
,

\vol(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}=\det(\boldsymbol{C}^{T}\boldsymbol{C})\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}{(\boldsymbol{C}^{T}\boldsymbol{C})_{ii}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}}.

The equality follows from the assumption that
\boldsymbol{C}

has unit-norm columns, so that

\sopt(\boldsymbol{S})=\vol(\boldsymbol{C})^{1/k}
, and from the previous part.
A more complicated proof of the equality
is presented in
[
32
, Theorem 3.2]
.

8.2
Maximal relative volume

We show that the relative volume is bounded above by 1, and that only matrices with orthonormal columns have a relative volume equal to 1.

Lemma 2

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
. Then
0<\rvol(\boldsymbol{C})\leq 1
.

Furthermore,
\rvol(\boldsymbol{C})=1
if and only if the columns of
\boldsymbol{C}
are orthonormal.

Proof 19

The inequality follows from the singular
value ordering (
1
).

As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then all singular values of
\boldsymbol{C}
are
equal to 1,
and
\rvol(\boldsymbol{C})=1
. Conversely, if
\rvol(\boldsymbol{C})=1
then

\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\frac{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})}{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})}}=1.

(12)

Since all factors
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})/\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})\leq 1
, (
12
) can only
hold if all factors
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})/\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
.
Hence, all singular values of
\boldsymbol{C}
are equal to 1, and
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

We show that removal of columns can only
increase the relative volume.

Lemma 3

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
,
and let
\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell}
be a
column submatrix of
\boldsymbol{C}
,
1\leq\ell<k
.
Then

\rvol(\boldsymbol{C})\leq\rvol(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})\leq\rvol(\boldsymbol{C}_{1})=1.

Proof 20

Since
\boldsymbol{C}
has full column rank, all columns are non-zero,
and any non-zero column
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}
attains the largest relative volume of 1.

For
\ell\geq 2
, singular value interlacing (
2
) implies

\sigma_{k-\ell+j}(\boldsymbol{C})\leq\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})\leq\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq\ell.

Since all factors
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})/\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\rvol(\boldsymbol{C})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\frac{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})}{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})}}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}{\frac{\sigma_{k-\ell+j}(\boldsymbol{C})}{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})}}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}{\frac{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})}{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})}}=\rvol(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell}).

8.3
Minimal two-norm and Schatten p-norms

We show that only matrices with
k
orthonormal columns have a
two-norm equal to 1, and a Schatten
p
-norm
equal to
k^{1/p}
for
p>2
.
In the special case
p=2
, the equality

\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(2)}=\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}=\sqrt{k}
is always true for unit-norm columns, regardless of whether they are orthonormal.

Lemma 4

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
and
\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{2}=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Then each of the following inequalities holds with equality if and only if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal
columns.

1.

Two-norm
:
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}\geq 1

2.

Schatten
p
-norm
:
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}\geq k^{1/p}
for
p>2
.

Proof 21

We tackle the two cases separately.

1.

Two-norm
: The inequality follows from the definition of the norm

\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\|_{2}=1.

As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}=\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
.

Conversely, if
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}=1
, then (
10
) and the singular value ordering imply
\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})=\dots=\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
. That is,
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

2.

Schatten
p
-norm for
p>2
: The norm inequalities
[
22
, Equation (5.4.21)]
imply

\sqrt{k}=\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}\leq k^{(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{p})}\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)},\qquad p>2.

Rearranging gives the inequality
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}\geq k^{1/p}
for
p>2
.

As for the equality, we use the same idea as in the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
. If
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then all the singular values of
\boldsymbol{C}
are equal to
1
and
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}=k^{1/p}
.

Conversely, if
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}=k^{1/p}
, then (
10
) implies

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{2}{p}\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}-\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}+(1-\tfrac{2}{p})\right)=0.

(13)

This sum has the form

\sum_{j=1}^{k}g_{p}(\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}))=0\qquad\text{where}\quad g_{p}(x)\equiv\tfrac{2x^{p}}{p}-x^{2}+(1-\tfrac{2}{p}).

For
x>0
,
g_{p}(x)
has the unique global minimizer
x_{*}=1
with
g_{p}(x_{*})=0
.
Since the singular values are positive,
\sum_{j=1}^{k}g(\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}))
is a sum of nonnegative summands. Thus equality in (
13
) is achieved only if
\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})=\dots=\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
, implying that
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

Below is a justification for limiting Schatten
p
-norms
to the case
p\geq 2
: The lower bound
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}\geq k^{1/p}
for
p>2
turns into an upper bound for
1\leq p<2
.

Remark 8.1

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
and
\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{2}=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
.
For
1\leq p<2
,
[
22
, (5.4.21)]
implies

\sqrt{k}\leq\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}\leq k^{(\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{2})}\sqrt{k}=k^{1/p}

and
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}=k^{1/p}
if and only if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

8.4
Minimal pseudo-inverse norms

We show that matrices with
k
unit-norm columns have a
pseudo-inverse whose two-norm is bounded below by
1
, and
whose
p
-norm is bounded below by
k^{1/p}
for
p\geq 2
; and that
only matrices with
k
orthonormal columns have pseudo-inverses with
a two-norm equal to 1, and a Schatten
p
-norm equal
to
k^{1/p}
for
p>2
.

Lemma 5

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
and
\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{2}=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Then each of the following inequalities holds with equality if and only if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal
columns.

1.

Two-norm of pseudo-inverse
:
\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{2}\geq 1

2.

Frobenius-norm of pseudo-inverse
:
\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{F}\geq\sqrt{k}

3.

Schatten
p
-norm of pseudo-inverse
:
\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}\geq k^{1/p}
.

Proof 22

We tackle each case separately.

1.

Two-norm of the pseudo-inverse:

From (
10
) follows

k=\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}}\geq k\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}

Thus
\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})\leq 1
and

\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{2}=1/\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq 1
, which shows the inequality.

As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then

\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}=\boldsymbol{C}^{T}
, hence
\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{2}=1
.

Conversely, if
\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{2}=1
, then the singular value ordering
\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
and (
10
) imply
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Thus,
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

2.

Frobenius norm of the pseudo-inverse:
This is a special case of the Schatten
p
-norm for
p=2
.

3.

Schatten p-norm of the pseudo-inverse:
From

k=\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}\leq k\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}

follows
\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq 1
. Therefore,

\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}\geq\left(\frac{k}{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}}\right)^{1/p}\geq k^{1/p}.

As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthornormal columns, then
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Hence,
\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}=k^{1/p}
.

Conversely, if
\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}=k^{1/p}
, then (
10
) implies

0=\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{2}{p}\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}^{p}-k(1+\tfrac{2}{p})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}+\tfrac{2}{p\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}}-(1+\tfrac{2}{p})).

(14)

Each summand equals
g(\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}))
for
g(x)\equiv x^{2}+\tfrac{2}{px^{p}}-(1+\tfrac{2}{p})
. For
x>0
, the function
g(x)
is nonnegative with global minimizer
x_{*}=1
and
g(x_{*})=0
. Thus, equality in
(
14
) is achieved if and only if
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
,
and
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

8.5
Minimal condition numbers

We derive lower bounds for condition numbers
of matrices with unit-norm columns, and show that
the lower bounds are attained only by matrices
with orthonormal columns.

Lemma 6

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
and
\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{2}=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Then each of the following inequalities holds with equality if and only if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal
columns.

1.

Two-norm condition number:
\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq 1

2.

Frobenius norm condition number:
\kappa_{F}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq k

3.

Schatten
p
-norm condition number:
\kappa_{(p)}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq k^{2/p}

4.

Mixed condition number:
\kappa_{D}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq\sqrt{k}

5.

Schatten
p
-norm mixed condition number
\kappa_{D,p}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq k^{1/p}

Proof 23

We tackle each case separately.

1.

Two-norm condition number:
The inequality follows from the singular value ordering (
1
).

As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then all its singular values are
1
and
\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
. Conversely, if
\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{C})=\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})/\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
, then the singular value ordering (
1
) ensures
that
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
, and
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

2.

Frobenius norm condition number:

The inequality follows from applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to the vectors

\mathbf{x}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})&\cdots&\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})\end{bmatrix}^{T},\qquad\boldsymbol{y}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1/\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})&\cdots&1/\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})\end{bmatrix}^{T},

which shows

k=\mathbf{x}^{T}\boldsymbol{y}\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{-2}\right)^{1/2}=\kappa_{F}(\boldsymbol{C}).

As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then
\kappa_{F}(\boldsymbol{C})=k
.

Conversely, if
\kappa_{F}(\boldsymbol{C})=k
, then (
10
) implies
\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}=\sqrt{k}
, hence

\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{-2}=k
.
As in the proof of Lemma
5
, one shows
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Thus,
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

3.

Schatten
p
-norm condition number for
p>2
: The inequality follows from applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to the vectors

\mathbf{x}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}&\cdots&\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}\end{bmatrix}^{T},\qquad\boldsymbol{y}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1/\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}&\cdots&1/\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}\end{bmatrix}^{T},

which shows

k=\mathbf{x}^{T}\boldsymbol{y}\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{-p}\right)^{1/2}=\kappa_{p}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p/2}.

Equality occurs in the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality only if
\mathbf{x}
is a multiple of
\boldsymbol{y}
. This ensures that all singular values are equal to
1
, thus
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

Conversely, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then all the singular values are equal to
1
and
\kappa_{(p)}(\boldsymbol{C})=k^{2/p}
.

4.

Mixed condition number
:
The inequality follows from the definition of
\kappa_{D}(\boldsymbol{C})
. As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then
\kappa_{D}(\boldsymbol{C})=\sqrt{k}
.
Conversely, if
\kappa_{D}(\boldsymbol{C})=\sqrt{k}
, then (
10
) implies
\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
.
The singular value ordering
\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
and (
10
) imply
\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Thus
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

5.

Schatten
p
-norm mixed condition number for
p>2
:
The inequality follows from the definition of
\kappa_{D,p}(\boldsymbol{C})
.
As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then
\kappa_{D,p}(\boldsymbol{C})={k}^{1/p}
.

Conversely, if
\kappa_{D,p}(\boldsymbol{C})={k}^{1/p}
, then
\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}=k\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}
,
so that

\sum_{j=1}^{p}(\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}-\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p})=0.

The singular value ordering implies that all singular values are equal to
1
, thus
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns.

We show that the removal of columns can only
increase the condition numbers.

Lemma 7

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
,
and let
\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell}

be a column submatrix of
\boldsymbol{C}
,
1\leq\ell<k
.
Then

\displaystyle\kappa_{\xi}(\boldsymbol{C})

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{\xi}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})\geq\kappa_{\xi}(\boldsymbol{C}_{1})=1,\qquad\xi\in\{2,F,(p),D,(D,p)\}.

Proof 24

Since
\boldsymbol{C}
has full column rank, all columns are non-zero,
and any non-zero column
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}
attains the minimal
value of 1 for all the condition numbers.

1.

Two-norm condition number:

For
\ell\geq 2
, singular value interlacing (
2
) implies

\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})\geq\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})
and

\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})\leq\sigma_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})
,
hence

\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{C})=\frac{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})}{\sigma_{k}(\boldsymbol{C})}\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})}{\sigma_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})}=\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell}).

2.

Frobenius norm and Schatten-
p
norm condition numbers for
p>2
:

We give the proof for
p\geq 2
, which includes the case of the Frobenius norm. For
\ell\geq 2
, singular value interlacing (
2
)
implies

\sigma_{k-\ell+j}(\boldsymbol{C})\leq\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})\leq\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq\ell.

Thus

\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})^{p}}=\|\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell}\|_{(p)}^{p}

and

\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}}}\geq\sum_{j=k-\ell+1}^{\ell}{\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{p}}}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}{\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})^{p}}}=\|\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}^{p}.

Thus

\left(\kappa_{(p)}(\boldsymbol{C})\right)^{p}=\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{(p)}^{p}\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}^{p}\geq\|\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell}\|_{(p)}^{p}\|\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell}^{\dagger}\|_{(p)}^{p}=\left((\kappa_{(p)}(\boldsymbol{C}_{\ell})\right)^{p}.

3.

Mixed condition number and Schatten
p
-norm mixed condition numbers:
The proofs are analogous to the ones above.

8.6
Maximal stable rank

We derive upper bounds for the stable rank
and
p
-stable rank for
p>2

of matrices with unit-norm columns, and show that
the upper bounds are attained only by matrices
with orthonormal columns.

Lemma 8

Let
\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
with
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=k
and
\|\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\|_{2}=1
,
1\leq j\leq k
. Then each of the following inequalities holds with equality if and only if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal
columns.

1.

Stable rank:
\sr(\boldsymbol{C})\leq k

2.

p
-stable rank:
\sr_{(p)}(\boldsymbol{C})\leq k

Proof 25

We tackle each part in turn.

1.

Stable rank:
The inequality follows from the definition.

As for the equality, if
\boldsymbol{C}
has orthonormal columns, then
all
k
singular values equal to
1
, and
\sr(\boldsymbol{C})=k
.
Conversely, if
k=\sr(\boldsymbol{C})=\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}/\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}^{p}
, then the definition of stable rank
implies

\sum_{j=1}^{k}{\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}}=\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{F}^{2}=k\|\boldsymbol{C}\|_{2}^{2}=k\,\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}.

Hence
\sum_{j=1}^{k}{(\sigma_{1}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2}-\sigma_{j}(\boldsymbol{C})^{2})}=0
.
From the singular value ordering (
1
) follows that this

Lemma 9

Proof 26

\boldsymbol{C}

\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}=(\boldsymbol{C}^{T}\boldsymbol{C})^{-1}\boldsymbol{C}^{T}
,

\boldsymbol{C}^{T}\boldsymbol{C}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{C}_{1}&\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{C}_{2}\\
\boldsymbol{C}_{2}^{T}\boldsymbol{C}_{1}&\boldsymbol{C}_{2}^{T}\boldsymbol{C}_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Furthermore, the column submatrices
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{C}_{2}

\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{C}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\boldsymbol{S}_{1}&\equiv\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{C}_{1}-\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{C}_{2}\boldsymbol{C}_{2}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{C}_{1}=\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{T}\>(\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{C}_{2}\boldsymbol{C}_{2}^{\dagger})\>\boldsymbol{C}_{1}=\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{P}_{2}\boldsymbol{C}_{1}=\boldsymbol{M}_{1}^{T}\boldsymbol{M}_{1}\\

(15)

Below is a lower bound on the Frobenius norm in terms of the partitioned inverses.

Lemma 10

Let
\boldsymbol{C}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{C}_{1}&\boldsymbol{C}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}

with
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
and
\boldsymbol{C}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(n-k)}
for some
1\leq k<n
.
If
\rank(\boldsymbol{C})=n
, then

\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}\geq\|\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{C}_{2}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}.

Proof 27

Lemma
9
and (
16
) imply

\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{M}_{1}^{\dagger}\\
\boldsymbol{M}_{2}^{\dagger}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\text{where}\quad\boldsymbol{M}_{1}\equiv\boldsymbol{P}_{2}\boldsymbol{C}_{2},\quad\boldsymbol{M}_{2}\equiv\boldsymbol{P}_{1}\boldsymbol{C}_{2}

and
\boldsymbol{P}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{P}_{2}
are orthogonal projectors.
Since
\|\boldsymbol{P}_{1}\|_{2}=\|\boldsymbol{P}_{2}\|_{2}=1
, the product
inequalities for singular values

[
22
, Problem 7.3.P16]
imply

\sigma_{i}(\boldsymbol{M}_{1})=\sigma_{i}(\boldsymbol{P}_{2}\boldsymbol{C}_{1})\leq\|\boldsymbol{P}_{2}\|_{2}\,\sigma_{i}(\boldsymbol{C}_{1})=\sigma_{i}(\boldsymbol{C}_{1}),\qquad 1\leq i\leq k.

Because both
\boldsymbol{M}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{C}_{1}
have full rank, we can invert the singular values

1/\sigma_{i}(\boldsymbol{C}_{1})\leq 1/\sigma_{i}(\boldsymbol{M}_{1}),\qquad 1\leq i\leq k.

(17)

Therefore

\|\boldsymbol{M}_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{F}\geq\|\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{F}
. This is a special case of
[
28
, Theorem 3.2]
.
The proof for
\|\boldsymbol{M}_{2}^{\dagger}\|_{F}\geq\|\boldsymbol{C}_{2}^{\dagger}\|_{F}
is analogous.
Thus

\|\boldsymbol{C}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{M}_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{M}_{2}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}\geq\|\boldsymbol{C}_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{C}_{2}^{\dagger}\|_{F}^{2}.

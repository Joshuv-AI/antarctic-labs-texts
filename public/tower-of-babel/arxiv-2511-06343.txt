Abstract
We consider a two-species chemotaxis model in ℝ d ​ ( d ≥ 3 ) {\mathbb{R}}^{d}(d\geq 3) featuring nonlinear porous medium-type diffusion and nonlocal attractive power-law interaction. Here, the nonlinear diffusion is chosen to be 1 / m 1 + 1 / m 2 = ( d + 2 ) / d 1/m_{1}+1/m_{2}=(d+2)/d in such a way that the associated free energy is conformal invariant, and there are radially symmetric, non-increasing and non-compactly supported stationary solutions ( U s ​ ( x ) , V s ​ ( x ) ) (U_{s}(x),V_{s}(x)) . We analyze the conditions on initial data ( u 0 , v 0 ) (u_{0},v_{0}) under which attractive forces dominate over diffusion, and further classify the global existence and finite time blow-up of dynamical solutions by virtue of these stationary solutions. Specifically, the solution ( u , v ) ​ ( x , t ) (u,v)(x,t) exists globally in time if the initial data satisfy ‖ u 0 ‖ L m 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) < ‖ U s ‖ L m 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) \|u_{0}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})} and ‖ v 0 ‖ L m 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) < ‖ V s ‖ L m 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) \|v_{0}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|V_{s}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})} . In contrast, there are blowing-up solutions when ‖ u 0 ‖ L m 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) > ‖ U s ‖ L m 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) \|u_{0}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})} and ‖ v 0 ‖ L m 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) > ‖ V s ‖ L m 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) \|v_{0}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|V_{s}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})} .

Introduction

In ecological systems, the interplay between multiple species is often intricately linked to their chemotactic behaviors, where organisms respond to chemical signals in their environment to navigate, interact, or survive. For two-species communities, such dynamics are particularly pronounced: each species may secrete specific signaling molecules, while simultaneously exhibiting directed movement toward the signals produced by themselves or the other species. This coupling of interspecific interactions and chemotactic response forms the core of two-species chemotaxis models.

In numerous symbiotic and cooperative ecosystems, a key phenomenon emerges: two species mutually secrete chemical signals and exhibit positive directional movement toward one another. For instance, in ecology, pollinating insects are drawn to flowering plants in response to floral scent signals secreted by the plants; meanwhile, the insects release cuticular pheromones that in turn attract more conspecifics or other pollinators. Through bidirectional chemotaxis, coordination between plant pollen dispersal and insect food acquisition is achieved. In oncology, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) secrete chemokines to attract T cells to aggregate in tumor regions, and activated T cells also secrete signals that reciprocally attract CAFs. This bidirectional chemotactic dynamic influences the balance between tumor immune killing and immune evasion. In microbiology, nitrogen-fixing bacteria in soil secrete organic acids to attract phosphate-solubilizing bacteria, which in turn release amino acids that attract nitrogen-fixing bacteria. By virtue of bidirectional chemotaxis, these two types of bacteria form symbiotic aggregated patches, enabling the efficient completion of nitrogen and phosphorus nutrient cycling in soil.

The core commonalities of these phenomena are as follows: first, the two species each secrete chemical attractants, facilitating mutual directional chemotaxis; second, the diffusion behavior of the species depends on their own density—diffusion weakens at low density to avoid extinction (degenerate diffusion). To quantitatively describe such dynamics, the following system is proposed

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u^{m_{1}}-\nabla\cdot(u\nabla c),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},t\geq 0,\\
-\Delta c=v,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},t\geq 0,\\
v_{t}=\Delta v^{m_{2}}-\nabla\cdot(v\nabla z),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},t\geq 0,\\
-\Delta z=u,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},t\geq 0,\\
(u,v)(x,0)=(u_{0},v_{0})(x)\geq 0,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle c=c_{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{v(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy,\quad z=c_{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy,

(6)

\displaystyle M_{1}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x,t)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u_{0}(x)dx,\quad M_{2}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}v(x,t)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}v_{0}(x)dx.

\displaystyle(u_{0},v_{0})\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)+|x|^{2}v_{0}(x))dx<\infty.

\displaystyle F(u,v)=\frac{1}{m_{1}-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u^{m_{1}}dx+\frac{1}{m_{2}-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}v^{m_{2}}dx-c_{d}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{u(x)v(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dxdy.

\displaystyle\frac{dF(u,v)}{dt}=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u\left|\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}\nabla u^{m_{1}-1}-\nabla c\right|^{2}dx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}v\left|\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}\nabla v^{m_{2}-1}-\nabla z\right|^{2}dx\leq 0.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}U_{s}^{m_{1}-1}-C_{s}=\overline{C}_{1},\text{ in }\Omega_{1},\\

Several aspects motivate the consideration of this special case:

1.

Based on the above analysis, stationary solutions fall into two subcases depending on

2.

The free energy

\displaystyle F(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})=\lambda^{\frac{(d+2)m_{1}m_{2}-d(m_{1}+m_{2})}{m_{1}+m_{2}-m_{1}m_{2}}}F(u,v),

(22)

3.

From the expression of the free energy

\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(u_{0\varepsilon},v_{0\varepsilon})\geq 0,\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)\|_{1}=\|u_{0}\|_{1},\quad\|v_{\varepsilon}(x,0)\|_{1}=\|v_{0}\|_{1},\\[2.84526pt]
\int|x|^{2}u_{0\varepsilon}dx\to\int|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx,\,\int|x|^{2}v_{0\varepsilon}dx\to\int|x|^{2}v_{0}(x)dx,\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,\\[2.84526pt]
(u_{0\varepsilon}(x),v_{0\varepsilon}(x))\to(u_{0}(x),v_{0}(x))\text{ in }L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\text{ for }1\leq q<\infty,\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.\end{array}\right.

The regularized problem (
2
) admits global in time smooth solutions for any
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, this approximation has been proved to be convergent. More precisely, following the arguments in
[
10
, Lemma 4.8]
and
[
37
, Section 4]
, we assert that if

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<C_{0}

(44)

for all
0<t<\infty
(where
C_{0}
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
), then there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}(u_{\varepsilon_{n}},v_{\varepsilon_{n}})\rightarrow(u,v)\text{ in }L^{r}(0,T;L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})),\quad 1\leq r<\infty\end{array}

and
(u,v)
is a weak solution to (
1
) on
[0,T).

According to the above analysis, a weak solution
(u,v)
to (
1
) on
[0,T)
exists when (
44
) is fulfilled. Our focus will therefore be on establishing the validity of the
L^{\infty}
-bound. As shown in the following lemma, which provides local existence and blow-up criteria, this
L^{\infty}
bound can be inferred from the
L^{r}
norm of
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
for
r>\max\left(\frac{d(2-m_{1})}{2},\frac{d(2-m_{2})}{2}\right)
. This further characterizes the maximal existence time.

Lemma 2.2

(Local existence and blow-up criteria)
.

Let
1/m_{1}+1/m_{2}=(d+2)/d
with
1<m_{1}<2-2/d
and
1<m_{2}<2-2/d
. Under assumption (
9
) on the initial data, there exists a pair of weak solutions
(u,v)
to (
1
) with a maximal existence time
T_{w}\in(0,\infty]
. Moreover, if
T_{w}<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{t\to T_{w}}\left(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right)=\infty.

Proof.

The key to proving local existence lies in establishing the following a priori estimates. We first denote

\displaystyle p_{0}=\max\left(\frac{d(2-m_{1})}{2},\frac{d(2-m_{2})}{2}\right).

Step 1
(
L^{r}
-estimates for
r>p_{0}
)  We multiply equation
\eqref{uveps}_{1}
by
ru_{\varepsilon}^{r-1}
and
\eqref{uveps}_{3}
by
rv_{\varepsilon}^{r-1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int(u_{\varepsilon}^{r}+v_{\varepsilon}^{r})dx+\frac{4m_{1}r(r-1)}{(m_{1}+r-1)^{2}}\int\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{1}+r-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx+\frac{4m_{2}r(r-1)}{(m_{2}+r-1)^{2}}\int\left|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{2}+r-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r-1)\int(u_{\varepsilon}^{r}J_{\varepsilon}\ast v_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{r}J_{\varepsilon}\ast u_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(r-1)\int(u_{\varepsilon}^{r}J_{\varepsilon}\ast u_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{r}J_{\varepsilon}\ast v_{\varepsilon})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(r-1)\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}^{r}\|_{(r+1)/r}\|J_{\varepsilon}\ast u_{\varepsilon}\|_{r+1}+\|v_{\varepsilon}^{r}\|_{(r+1)/r}\|J_{\varepsilon}\ast v_{\varepsilon}\|_{r+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(r-1)\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}^{r}\|_{(r+1)/r}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{r+1}+\|v_{\varepsilon}^{r}\|_{(r+1)/r}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{r+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r-1)\int(u_{\varepsilon}^{r+1}+v_{\varepsilon}^{r+1})dx,

(46)

where we have used Hölder’s inequality. For
r>p_{0}
, applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev (GNS) inequality with
1<m_{1}<2-2/d
and
1<m_{2}<2-2/d
, we find

\displaystyle\int(u_{\varepsilon}^{r+1}+v_{\varepsilon}^{r+1})dx\leq C\left\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{1}+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{\alpha_{1}}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{\beta_{1}}+C\left\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{2}+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{\beta_{2}},

(47)

where

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=r+1-\frac{r\alpha_{1}}{2},\quad\beta_{2}=r+1-\frac{r\alpha_{2}}{2}

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\frac{2}{1+2(r-d(2-m_{1})/2)/d}<2,\quad\alpha_{2}=\frac{2}{1+2(r-d(2-m_{2})/2)/d}<2

ensured by
r>p_{0}
. Using Young’s inequality, we further obtain

\displaystyle\int(u_{\varepsilon}^{r+1}+v_{\varepsilon}^{r+1})dx\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2m_{1}r}{(m_{1}+r-1)^{2}}\int\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{1}+r-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx+\frac{2m_{2}r}{(m_{2}+r-1)^{2}}\int\left|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{2}+r-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+C(r,d)\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{r\delta_{1}}+\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{r\delta_{2}}\right),

(48)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{1}=1+\frac{1}{r-d(2-m_{1})/2}>1,\quad\delta_{2}=1+\frac{1}{r-d(2-m_{2})/2}>1.

Substituting (
2
) into (
2
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\left(u_{\varepsilon}^{r}+v_{\varepsilon}^{r}\right)dx+\frac{2m_{1}r}{(m_{1}+r-1)^{2}}\int\left|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{1}+r-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx+\frac{2m_{2}r}{(m_{2}+r-1)^{2}}\int\left|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{2}+r-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(r,d)\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{r\delta_{1}}+\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{r\delta_{2}}\right).

(49)

Consequently, the
L^{r}
-norm for
r>p_{0}
is bounded locally in time:

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{r}+\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{r}\leq\frac{C(r,d)}{(T_{r}-t)^{r-p_{0}}},\quad T_{r}=\left(\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{r}+\|v_{0\varepsilon}\|_{r}^{r}\right)^{-\frac{1}{r-p_{0}}}.

(50)

Returning to (
2
), we also deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{1}+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{r};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}+\left\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{m_{2}+r-1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{r};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq C\left(\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{r},\|v_{0\varepsilon}\|_{r}\right).

(51)

Step 2
(
L^{\infty}
-estimates and local existence) As a direct consequence of Step 1, we apply the Moser iteration method to yield

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<T_{r}}\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right)\leq C\left(\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{1},\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},\|v_{0\varepsilon}\|_{1},\|v_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right),

(52)

following the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
verbatim. Armed with the regularities (
50
), (
51
), and (
52
), we further derive

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{r};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}+\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{r};L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq C,

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\nabla c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{r};L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}+\|v_{\varepsilon}\nabla z_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{r};L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq C,

where the constants
C
depend only on
\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{1}
,
\|v_{0\varepsilon}\|_{1}
,
\|u_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and
\|v_{0\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
. These a priori bounds hold uniformly in
\varepsilon
. Thus, we can pass to the limits
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
and
v_{\varepsilon}\to v
as
\varepsilon\to 0
(without relabeling), due to the time compactness ensured by the Lions-Aubin lemma. In the limit, we obtain the local existence of the weak solution to (
1
). Finally, in light of the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
, we characterize the maximal existence time of the weak solution and complete the proof.
∎

3
Steady states

This section is devoted to the analysis of stationary solutions
(U_{s},V_{s})
to (
1
). Section
3.1
establishes general properties of these solutions. Specifically, Lemma
3.1
provides a Pohozaev-Rellich type identity. Leveraging this identity, Proposition
3.2
determines the constant chemical potentials on the supports of
U_{s}
and
V_{s}
. In Section
3.2
, we explore the connection between the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev extremals and the steady states
(U_{s},V_{s})
, thus completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
. This link is crucial for establishing the global existence and finite-time blow-up of weak solutions in (
1
).

3.1
Steady states: proof of Theorem
1.1

The stationary system for (
1
) is considered in the sense of distributions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta U_{s}^{m_{1}}(x)-\nabla\cdot(U_{s}(x)\nabla C_{s}(x))=0,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
\Delta V_{s}^{m_{2}}(x)-\nabla\cdot(V_{s}(x)\nabla Z_{s}(x))=0,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\end{array}\right.

where the potentials
C_{s}(x)
and
Z_{s}(x)
are given by

\displaystyle C_{s}=c_{d}\int\frac{V_{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy,\quad Z_{s}=c_{d}\int\frac{U_{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy.

(55)

We begin by deriving a Pohozaev-type identity to characterize the critical points of the free energy
F(u,v)
associated with the steady states.

Lemma 3.1

(A Pohozaev identity for stationary solutions)
.

Assume that
U_{s}\in L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
V_{s}\in L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
satisfy (
3.1
) in the sense of distributions. Then the following identity holds:

\displaystyle 2d\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx+2d\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx=2c_{d}(d-2)\iint\frac{U_{s}(x)V_{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dxdy.

(56)

Proof.

We follow a similar argument as in
[
30
]
. Consider a cut-off function
\psi_{R}(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\psi_{R}(x)=|x|^{2}
for
|x|<R
and
\psi_{R}(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2R
. Then we compute

\displaystyle\int\Delta U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx=\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}\Delta\psi_{R}(x)dx,\quad\int\Delta V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx=\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}\Delta\psi_{R}(x)dx.

(57)

We also have

\displaystyle\int\psi_{R}(x)\nabla\cdot(U_{s}(x)\nabla C_{s}(x))dx

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla\psi_{R}(x)\cdot U_{s}\nabla C_{s}dx

\displaystyle=c_{d}(d-2)\iint U_{s}(x)\frac{\nabla\psi_{R}(x)\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{d}}V_{s}(y)dxdy

and

\displaystyle\int\psi_{R}(x)\nabla\cdot(V_{s}(x)\nabla Z_{s}(x))dx

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla\psi_{R}(x)\cdot V_{s}\nabla Z_{s}dx

\displaystyle=c_{d}(d-2)\iint U_{s}(y)\frac{\nabla\psi_{R}(x)\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{d}}V_{s}(x)dxdy.

Summing these two expressions yields

\displaystyle\int\psi_{R}(x)\nabla\cdot(U_{s}(x)\nabla C_{s}(x))dx+\int\psi_{R}(x)\nabla\cdot(V_{s}(x)\nabla Z_{s}(x))dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{d}(d-2)\iint\frac{(\nabla\psi_{R}(x)-\nabla\psi_{R}(y))\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}\frac{U_{s}(x)V_{s}(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dxdy.

(58)

Since
\Delta\psi_{R}(x)
and
\frac{(\nabla\psi_{R}(x)-\nabla\psi_{R}(y))\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{2}}
are bounded, all terms on the right-hand sides of (
57
) and (
3.1
) are bounded. Therefore, as
R\to\infty,
we may pass to the limit in each term using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem to complete the proof.
∎

Combining the identity (
56
) with the variational structure of the free energy, we deduce the following results.

Proposition 3.2

(Euler-Lagrange equations for stationary solutions)
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be two connected open sets. Suppose that
U_{s}\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
V_{s}\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\int U_{s}dx=M_{1}
and
\int V_{s}dx=M_{2}
. Assume also that
U_{s}>0
in
\Omega_{1}
,
U_{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{1}
, and
V_{s}>0
in
\Omega_{2}
,
V_{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{2}
. If
(U_{s},V_{s})
is a critical point of
F(u,v)
, then it satisfies the Euler-Lagrange conditions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}U_{s}^{m_{1}-1}-C_{s}=\overline{C}_{1},&\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{1},\\
\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}V_{s}^{m_{2}-1}-Z_{s}=\overline{C}_{2},&\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{2},\end{array}\right.

where the constants are given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{C}_{1}=\frac{1}{M_{1}}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx-\int C_{s}U_{s}dx\right),\\
\overline{C}_{2}=\frac{1}{M_{2}}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx-\int Z_{s}V_{s}dx\right).\end{array}\right.

Moreover, if
1/m_{1}+1/m_{2}=(d+2)/d
, then
\overline{C}_{1}=\overline{C}_{2}=0
and
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx=\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx.

(63)

Proof.

We adapt some ideas from
[
2
,
9
]
. For any
\psi_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
and
\psi_{2}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{2})
, define

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(x)=\psi_{1}(x)-\frac{U_{s}}{M_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\psi_{1}dx,

(64)

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(x)=\psi_{2}(x)-\frac{V_{s}}{M_{2}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\psi_{2}dx.

(65)

Then
\text{supp}(\varphi_{1})\subseteq\Omega_{1}
,
\text{supp}(\varphi_{2})\subseteq\Omega_{2}
, and
\int_{\Omega_{1}}\varphi_{1}dx=0
,
\int_{\Omega_{2}}\varphi_{2}dx=0
. Moreover, there exist

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}:=\frac{\displaystyle\min_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{1})}U_{s}(y)}{\displaystyle\max_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{1})}\left|\varphi_{1}(y)\right|}>0,\quad\varepsilon_{2}:=\frac{\displaystyle\min_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{2})}V_{s}(y)}{\displaystyle\max_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{2})}\left|\varphi_{2}(y)\right|}>0

such that
U_{s}+\varepsilon\varphi_{1}\geq 0
in
\Omega_{1}
and
V_{s}+\varepsilon\varphi_{2}\geq 0
in
\Omega_{2}
for all
0<\varepsilon<\min(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
. Now,
(U_{s},V_{s})
is a critical point of
F(u,v)
if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}F(U_{s}+\varepsilon\varphi_{1},V_{s})=0,\quad\int_{\Omega_{1}}\varphi_{1}(x)dx=0,

(66)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}F(U_{s},V_{s}+\varepsilon\varphi_{2})=0,\quad\int_{\Omega_{2}}\varphi_{2}(x)dx=0.

(67)

A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left(\mu_{1s}-\frac{1}{M_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\mu_{1s}U_{s}dx\right)\psi_{1}(x)dx=0,\quad\text{for any }\psi_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{1}),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left(\mu_{2s}-\frac{1}{M_{2}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\mu_{2s}V_{s}dx\right)\psi_{2}(x)dx=0,\quad\text{for any }\psi_{2}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{2}),

where the chemical potentials are defined as

\displaystyle\mu_{1s}=\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}U_{s}^{m_{1}-1}-C_{s},\quad\mu_{2s}=\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}V_{s}^{m_{2}-1}-Z_{s}.

(68)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}U_{s}^{m_{1}-1}-C_{s}=\frac{1}{M_{1}}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx-\int U_{s}C_{s}dx\right)=\overline{C}_{1},\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{1},

(69)

and

\displaystyle\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}V_{s}^{m_{2}-1}-Z_{s}=\frac{1}{M_{2}}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx-\int V_{s}Z_{s}dx\right)=\overline{C}_{2},\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega_{2}.

(70)

On the other hand, we can infer from (
56
) that

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{1}=\frac{1}{M_{1}}\left(\left(\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}-\frac{d}{d-2}\right)\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx-\frac{d}{d-2}\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx\right),

(71)

\displaystyle\overline{C}_{2}=\frac{1}{M_{2}}\left(\left(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}-\frac{d}{d-2}\right)\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx-\frac{d}{d-2}\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx\right).

(72)

Thanks to the condition
1/m_{1}+1/m_{2}=(d+2)/d
, we find

\displaystyle-M_{1}\overline{C}_{1}=\frac{\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}-\frac{d}{d-2}}{\frac{d}{d-2}}M_{2}\overline{C}_{2}=\frac{\frac{d}{d-2}}{\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}-\frac{d}{d-2}}M_{2}\overline{C}_{2}.

(73)

Since
U_{s}\in L^{1}\cap L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),
the weak Young inequality implies that
\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2}}\ast U_{s}\in L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for each
r\in\left(\frac{d}{d-2},\frac{1}{\frac{1}{m_{1}}-\frac{2}{d}}\right].
Similarly, from
V_{s}\in L^{1}\cap L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
we deduce that
\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2}}\ast V_{s}\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for each
q\in\left(\frac{d}{d-2},\frac{1}{\frac{1}{m_{2}}-\frac{2}{d}}\right].
In particular, both
\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2}}\ast V_{s}
and
U_{s}^{m_{1}-1}
belong to
L^{m_{1}/(m_{1}-1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).
Analogously, both
\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2}}\ast U_{s}
and
V_{s}^{m_{2}-1}
belong to
L^{m_{2}/(m_{2}-1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).
These properties with (
69
) and (
70
) allow us to assert that
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
If not, we choose a sequence of points
x_{1n}\to x_{1}\in\partial\Omega_{1}
with
x_{1n}\in\Omega_{1}
. Then
\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}U_{s}^{m_{1}-1}(x_{1n})\to 0
, whereas
\int\frac{V_{s}(y)}{|x_{1n}-y|^{d-2}}dy>0
. It then follows from (
69
) that
\overline{C}_{1}<0
. Similarly, we choose a sequence of points
x_{2n}\to x_{2}\in\partial\Omega_{2}
with
x_{2n}\in\Omega_{2}
, we deduce from (
70
) that
\overline{C}_{2}<0
. Hence, (
73
) together with the facts that
1/m_{1}>2/d
and
1/m_{2}>2/d
give rise to
\overline{C}_{1}=\overline{C}_{2}=0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}U_{s}^{m_{1}-1}-C_{s}=0,&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}V_{s}^{m_{2}-1}-Z_{s}=0,&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

This completes the proof.
∎

By Proposition
3.2
, one has

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta C_{s}=\left(\frac{m_{2}-1}{m_{2}}Z_{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{m_{2}-1}},&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
-\Delta Z_{s}=\left(\frac{m_{1}-1}{m_{1}}C_{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{m_{1}-1}},&\text{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{array}\right.

It was proved by Chen and Li
[
16
]
that all positive solutions
C_{s},Z_{s}
of (
3.1
) are radially symmetric and monotone decreasing about some point. In particular, if
m_{1}=m_{2}=2d/(d+2)
, then
V_{s}=C_{s}
and they both assume the standard form
[
14
]

\displaystyle c\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+|x-x_{0}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}

(78)

with some constant
c=c(d)
, and for some
\lambda>0
and
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. This completes our analysis of the stationary solutions and the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.2
Extremal functions for the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

This subsection establishes the connection between the extremal functions of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev (HLS) inequality (
30
) and the steady states of system (
3.1
). We first introduce the functionals

\displaystyle h(u,v):=\iint\frac{u(x)v(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dxdy

(79)

and

\displaystyle J(u,v):=\frac{h(u,v)}{\|u\|_{m_{1}}\|v\|_{m_{2}}}.

(80)

The following result of Lieb
[
26
]
on the existence and properties of the extremal functions for the HLS inequality is fundamental to our analysis.

Proposition 3.3

(Existence of the optimizer)
.

Let
1/m_{1}+1/m_{2}=(d+2)/d
and suppose
u\in L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),v\in L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then there exists a sharp constant
C_{*}
such that

\displaystyle J(U,V)=C_{*},

(81)

where
(U,V)
are non-negative, radially symmetric (up to translation) and non-increasing functions. Moreover, the sharp constant admits the upper bound

\displaystyle C_{*}\leq\frac{d}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{d\Gamma(d/2)}\right)^{(d-2)/d}\frac{1}{m_{1}m_{2}}\left(\left(\frac{(d-2)/d}{1-1/q}\right)^{(d-2)/d}+\left(\frac{(d-2)/d}{1-1/r}\right)^{(d-2)/d}\right).

(82)

We now establish the variational structure of the HLS inequality. Define the critical threshold
(x_{*},y_{*})
for
(\|u\|_{m_{1}}^{m_{1}},\|v\|_{m_{2}}^{m_{2}})
by

\displaystyle x_{*}:=\left(\frac{m_{1}}{c_{d}C_{*}(m_{1}-1)}A^{-\frac{1}{m_{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}}-1}},\quad y_{*}=Ax_{*},\quad A=\frac{m_{1}(m_{2}-1)}{m_{2}(m_{1}-1)},

(83)

and define the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{(x_{*},y_{*})}:=\left\{u\in L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),v\in L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})~\Big|~\|u\|_{1}=M_{1},\|v\|_{1}=M_{2},~\|u\|_{m_{1}}^{m_{1}}=x_{*},\|v\|_{m_{2}}^{m_{2}}=y_{*}\right\}.

Proposition 3.4

(Identification of the optimizer)
.

Let
(U,V)
be an optimizer of
J(u,v)
in
\mathcal{Y}_{(x_{*},y_{*})}
. Then
(U,V)
is a pair of radially symmetric, non-increasing stationary solutions to (
1
) and satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}U^{m_{1}-1}=c_{d}\int\frac{V(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}V^{m_{2}-1}=c_{d}\int\frac{U(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We now derive the Euler-lagrange equations for
(U,V)
. We introduce

\Omega_{1}:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}~|~U(x)>0\right\},\quad\Omega_{2}:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}~|~V(x)>0\right\}.

For any

define

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(x)=\psi_{1}(x)-\frac{U}{M_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\psi_{1}dx.

(86)

Then
\text{supp}(\varphi_{1})\subseteq\Omega_{1}
and
\int_{\Omega_{1}}\varphi_{1}dx=0
. Besides, there exists

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\displaystyle\min_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{1})}U(y)}{\displaystyle\max_{y\in\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{1})}\left|\varphi_{1}(y)\right|}>0

such that
U+\varepsilon\varphi_{1}\geq 0
in
\Omega_{1}
for

. Computing the first variation yields

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}J(U+\varepsilon\varphi_{1},V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\left(\|U\|_{m_{1}}\|V\|_{m_{2}}\int\frac{V(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy-h(U,V)\|V\|_{m_{2}}\|U\|_{m_{1}}^{1-m_{1}}U^{m_{1}-1}\right)\varphi_{1}(x)dx,

(87)

By the definition of
\varphi_{1}
in (

), equation (
3.2
) implies that
(U,V)
solves the following equation

\displaystyle\int\frac{V(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy

\displaystyle=C_{*}\|U\|_{m_{1}}^{1-m_{1}}\|V\|_{m_{2}}U^{m_{1}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{d}}\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}-1}U^{m_{1}-1},\quad\text{a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

since

\displaystyle\int\frac{U(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dy=\frac{1}{c_{d}}\frac{m_{2}}{m_{2}-1}V^{m_{2}-1},\quad\text{a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(89)

Recalling (
3.1
), we conclude that
(U,V)
is the steady state of (
1
).
∎

4
Global existence and finite-time blow-up: proof of Theorem

In this section, we use the characterization of steady states and the free energy to distinguish between two cases:

These are treated in Sections

Proposition 4.1
.

\displaystyle F(u_{0},v_{0})<F(U_{s},V_{s}),

the following results hold:

(i)

If

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{m_{1}}<\|U_{s}\|_{m_{1}}\text{ and }\|v_{0}\|_{m_{2}}<\|V_{s}\|_{m_{2}},

(ii)

If

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{m_{1}}>\|U_{s}\|_{m_{1}}\text{ and }\|v_{0}\|_{m_{2}}>\|V_{s}\|_{m_{2}},

Proof.

First, by setting

\displaystyle f=u(x),h=v(y),\beta=d-2,q=m_{1},r=m_{2}

in the HLS inequality (

\displaystyle F(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{1}-1}\int u^{m_{1}}dx+\frac{1}{m_{2}-1}\int v^{m_{2}}dx-c_{d}\iint\frac{u(x)v(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dxdy

(95)

\displaystyle F(U_{s},V_{s})

4.1
Proof of
(
ii
)
of Theorem
1.3
(finite-time blow-up)

In this subsection, we prove the finite-time blow-up result in Theorem
1.3
for large initial data. We employ a standard argument based on the evolution of the second moment of solutions, following the approach in
[
21
]
. Define the second moment as

\displaystyle m_{2}(t):=\int\left(|x|^{2}u(x,t)+|x|^{2}v(x,t)\right)dx.

(101)

Proposition 4.2

(Finite time blow-up)
.

Let
1<m_{1}<2-2/d
,
1<m_{2}<2-2/d
and
1/m_{1}+1/m_{2}=(d+2)/d
. Assume that
(u,v)
is a weak solution obtained in Lemma
2.2
on
[0,T_{w})
. Under assumptions (
9
), (
90
) and (
93
), we have
T_{w}<\infty
and the solution to (
1
) blows up in finite time in the sense that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T_{w}}\left(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right)=\infty.

Proof.

We present formal computations here, the passage to the limit from the approximated problem (
2
) can be carried out similarly as in
[
36
,
37
]
. The key tool is the time evolution of the second moment. Integrating by parts in (
1
) and leveraging mass conservation, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=2d\int u^{m_{1}}dx+2d\int v^{m_{2}}dx+2c_{d}(2-d)\iint u(x)v(y)\frac{x\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{d}}dxdy

\displaystyle~~+2c_{d}(2-d)\iint u(y)v(x)\frac{x\cdot(x-y)}{|x-y|^{d}}dxdy

\displaystyle=2d\int u^{m_{1}}dx+2d\int v^{m_{2}}dx-2(d-2)c_{d}\iint\frac{u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{d-2}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2)}{m_{1}-1}\right)\int u^{m_{1}}dx+\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2)}{m_{2}-1}\right)\int v^{m_{2}}dx+2(d-2)F(u,v).

By Proposition
4.1
, assumptions (
90
) and (
93
) imply that (
94
) holds for some
\mu_{2}>1
. We use the fact that
F(u,v)
is non-increasing in time to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}m_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2)}{m_{1}-1}\right)\mu_{2}\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx+\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2)}{m_{2}-1}\right)\mu_{2}\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx+2(d-2)F(u_{0},v_{0})

\displaystyle<\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2)}{m_{1}-1}\right)\mu_{2}\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx+\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2)}{m_{2}-1}\right)\mu_{2}\int V_{s}^{m_{2}}dx+2(d-2)F(U_{s},V_{s})

\displaystyle=\mu_{2}\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2)}{m_{1}-1}+\left(2d-\frac{2(d-2)}{m_{2}-1}\right)\frac{m_{1}(m_{2}-1)}{m_{2}(m_{1}-1)}\right)\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx

\displaystyle~~~+2(d-2)\left(\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}-m_{1}m_{2}}{(m_{1}-1)m_{2}}\right)\int U_{s}^{m_{1}}dx

\displaystyle=-4\frac{(d-2)m_{1}}{d(m_{1}-1)}(\mu_{2}-1)<0,

(102)

where we have used identities (
1
) and (
1
), together with the conditions
1<m_{1}<2-2/d
,
1<m_{2}<2-2/d
and
1/m_{1}+1/m_{2}=(d+2)/d
. Consequently, there exists a finite time
T>0
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T}m_{2}(t)=0
.

To connect the vanishing of the second moment to
L^{\infty}
-blow-up, we apply Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\int(u+v)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq R}(u+v)dx+\int_{|x|>R}(u+v)dx

\displaystyle\leq C(d)\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right)R^{d}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int(|x|^{2}u+|x|^{2}v))dx.

(103)

Choosing
R=\left(\frac{Cm_{2}(t)}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}}\right)^{1/(d+2)}
results in

\displaystyle M_{1}+M_{2}\leq Cm_{2}(t)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right)^{\frac{2}{d+2}},

(104)

where the constant
C
depends on
d
and the masses. Consequently,

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{t\to T}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})})\geq\limsup\limits_{t\to T}\frac{(M_{1}+M_{2})^{(d+2)/2}}{Cm_{2}(t)^{d/2}}=\infty.

(105)

This completes the proof.
∎

Step 1

(119)

Then the inequalities

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{p}{\overline{m}_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{1}}}{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2\overline{m}_{1}}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}+\frac{\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{2}}-\frac{1}{k_{1}^{\prime}}}{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2\overline{m}_{2}}-\frac{d-2}{d}}<2

(120)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{q}{\overline{m}_{2}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}}}{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2\overline{m}_{2}}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}+\frac{\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}^{\prime}}}{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2\overline{m}_{1}}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}<2

(121)

hold if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{1}}+\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{2}}<\frac{m_{1}}{\overline{m}_{1}}+\frac{2}{d},

(122)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{1}}+\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{2}}<\frac{m_{2}}{\overline{m}_{2}}+\frac{2}{d},

(123)

which are guaranteed by choosing

\displaystyle\overline{m}_{1}<m_{1}\text{ and }\overline{m}_{2}<m_{2}.

(124)

Armed with the uniform bounds in (
92
) and Hölder’s inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\overline{m}_{1}}\leq\|u\|_{1}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{m_{1}}^{\theta}\leq C,\quad 1-\theta+\frac{\theta}{m_{1}}=\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{1}},

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\overline{m}_{2}}\leq\|v\|_{1}^{1-\eta}\|v\|_{m_{2}}^{\eta}\leq C,\quad 1-\eta+\frac{\eta}{m_{2}}=\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{2}},

where the constants
C
depend only on
\|U_{s}\|_{m_{1}}
,
\|V_{s}\|_{m_{2}}
,
\|u_{0}\|_{1}
and
\|v_{0}\|_{1}
. Given (
120
), we can infer from (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle C\left(\|u\|_{\overline{m}_{1}},\|v\|_{\overline{m}_{2}}\right)\left\|\nabla u^{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{\frac{\frac{p}{\overline{m}_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{1}}}{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2\overline{m}_{1}}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}}\left\|\nabla v^{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{\frac{\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{2}}-\frac{1}{k_{1}^{\prime}}}{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2\overline{m}_{2}}-\frac{d-2}{d}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}p(p-1)}{(p+m_{1}-1)^{2}}\left\|\nabla u^{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{m_{2}q(q-1)}{(q+m_{2}-1)^{2}}\left\|\nabla v^{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+C\left(\|u\|_{m_{1}},\|v\|_{m_{2}}\right),

(125)

where we have used the inequality
x^{a}y^{b}\leq C_{1}x^{2}+C_{2}y^{2}+C_{3}
for
a+b<2.
Similarly, (
121
) ensures that

\displaystyle C\left(\|u\|_{\overline{m}_{1}},\|v\|_{\overline{m}_{2}}\right)\left\|\nabla v^{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{\frac{\frac{q}{\overline{m}_{2}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}}}{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2\overline{m}_{2}}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}}\left\|\nabla u^{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{\frac{\frac{1}{\overline{m}_{1}}-\frac{1}{k_{2}^{\prime}}}{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2\overline{m}_{1}}-\frac{d-2}{2d}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}p(p-1)}{(p+m_{1}-1)^{2}}\left\|\nabla u^{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{m_{2}q(q-1)}{(q+m_{2}-1)^{2}}\left\|\nabla v^{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+C\left(\|u\|_{m_{1}},\|v\|_{m_{2}}\right),

(126)

) and (

) into (

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int u^{p}dx+\frac{d}{dt}\int v^{q}dx+\frac{2m_{1}p(p-1)}{(p+m_{1}-1)^{2}}\int\left|\nabla u^{\frac{p+m_{1}-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{2m_{2}q(q-1)}{(q+m_{2}-1)^{2}}\int\left|\nabla v^{\frac{q+m_{2}-1}{2}}\right|^{2}dx\leq C.

(127)

On the other hand, from (

), the Sobolev inequality and Young’s inequality give

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1}^{p\beta}\left\|u\right\|_{\frac{(m_{1}+p-1)d}{d-2}}^{p(1-\beta)}

5
Conclusions

This paper investigates the two-species chemotaxis system (
1
) with diffusion exponents on the critical curve

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}}=\frac{d+2}{d}.

(133)

This choice ensures the invariance of the free energy
F(u,v)
under the scaling (
1
), while preserving the
L^{m_{1}}
norm of
u(x,t)
and the
L^{m_{2}}
norm of
v(x,t)
. Moreover, this curve marks the threshold that distinguishes stationary solutions that have compactly support from those that do not. Specifically, the stationary solutions
(U_{s},V_{s})(x)
of (
1
) are radially symmetric and non-increasing (up to translation). Our main result establishes that the pair
\left(\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},\|V_{s}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\right)
provides a sharp criterion for initial data, separating solutions that exist for infinite time from those that blow up in finite time. Precisely, under the condition
F(u_{0},v_{0})<F(U_{s},V_{s})
, we prove that there exists a global weak solution when the initial data satisfies
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}<\|V_{s}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
, whereas there are blowing-up solutions for
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|U_{s}\|_{L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
and
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}>\|V_{s}\|_{L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
.

In conclusion, this work establishes a sharp dichotomy for the two-species chemotaxis system at the energy-critical exponent. We have proved that the scaling-invariant norms of the stationary solutions in
L^{m_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\times L^{m_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
provide a critical threshold that sharply separates global existence from finite-time blow-up. This result underscores a fundamental phenomenon: the balance between degenerate diffusion and nonlocal aggregation is precisely quantified by these scaling-invariant norms. The proof relies on a novel combination of free energy dissipation, variational characterization of steady states and a detailed analysis of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

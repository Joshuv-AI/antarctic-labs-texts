Effect of sellar and parasellar brain tumors on retinal thickness and visual fields: A cross-sectional comparative study
Authors: Harika D Kasireddy, Vijaya Sahu, Anil Sharma, Aseem Kumar, Shivani Shrivastava
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Purpose:To compare retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness, ganglion cell complex (GCC) thickness, and visual field parameters in patients with sellar and parasellar brain tumors and to evaluate their associations with tumor size.Methods:This hospital-based cross-sectional comparative study was conducted over 18 months at a tertiary care center. Patients aged 12–60 years with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-confirmed sellar or parasellar tumors underwent best-corrected visual acuity assessment, automated perimetry (Humphrey 30-2), and spectral-domain optical coherence tomography for RNFL and GCC analysis. Tumor size was recorded from MRI.Results:Sixty-seven patients (31 sellar, 36 parasellar) were analyzed. Superior quadrant RNFL thickness in the right eye was significantly reduced in sellar tumors (P = 0.029). Parasellar tumors were significantly larger in size (P < 0.001). Tumor size showed significant associations with visual acuity and visual field indices in sellar tumors but not in parasellar tumors.Conclusion:Sellar tumors were associated with more consistent structural and functional visual impairment compared with parasellar tumors despite a smaller mean tumour size likely due to early chiasmal compression. RNFL thinning on optical coherence tomography may reflect early structural involvement of the optic pathway and may complement automated perimetry and neuroimaging in clinical evaluation.

Sellar and parasellar brain tumors represent a clinically significant group of intracranial lesions that commonly affect the optic nerves, optic chiasm, and proximal optic tracts because of their close anatomical relationship that is immediately superior to the sella turcica and are therefore highly susceptible to compressive injury from lesions arising in this region. Visual impairment is often one of the earliest manifestations and may precede other neurological symptoms. Patients also present with reduced visual acuity, characteristic visual field defects, and optic disc pallor. If compression is prolonged, irreversible axonal damage may occur, highlighting the importance of early detection and timely intervention to preserve visual function.[1]

Pituitary adenomas are the most common sellar tumors and constitute a major cause of compressive optic neuropathy. This typically produces bitemporal visual field defects, although the severity and pattern of visual loss depend on tumor size, growth pattern, and duration of compression.[2] In contrast, parasellar tumors comprise a heterogeneous group of lesions, including meningiomas, craniopharyngiomas, optic nerve gliomas, and other intracranial neoplasms. Despite their diverse histopathological origins, these tumors frequently impair vision due to direct compression, distortion, or ischemia of the optic nerves and chiasma.[345]

Compression of the anterior visual pathway leads to axonal damage followed by Wallerian degeneration, whereas lesions involving posterior visual structures may result in retrograde trans-synaptic degeneration.[67] Both mechanisms ultimately cause loss of retinal ganglion cells, leading to thinning of the inner retinal layers. Clinically, this damage manifests as visual field defects, reduced visual acuity, and optic atrophy. Conventional clinical examination and automated perimetry are essential for functional assessment but may fail to detect early or subtle structural changes, particularly in patients with slowly progressive or asymmetric disease.[8]

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) has emerged as a reliable, noninvasive imaging modality for objective assessment of retinal structural changes in compressive optic neuropathies. Spectral-domain OCT allows precise and reproducible measurement of the retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) and the ganglion cell complex (GCC), both of which serve as sensitive biomarkers of axonal and neuronal loss. Several studies have demonstrated a significant association between RNFL thinning and the severity of visual field loss in patients with chiasmal compression.[91011] More recent evidence suggests that macular GCC thinning may precede RNFL loss, particularly in chiasmal lesions, making GCC analysis a valuable tool for early detection of optic pathway involvement.[12]

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) remains the gold standard for anatomical evaluation of sellar and parasellar tumors providing crucial information regarding tumor size, location, suprasellar extension, and its relationship to the optic chiasm and surrounding neurovascular structures.[1314] MRI-based grading systems have also been shown to predict visual outcomes and postoperative recovery.

Although numerous studies have evaluated visual field defects and OCT-derived retinal changes in individual tumor types, particularly pituitary adenomas, comparative data assessing the combined effects of sellar and parasellar tumors remain limited. Furthermore, most studies have assessed functional and structural parameters independently, with limited evaluation of structure–function relationships or correlation with tumor size and duration. The present study was therefore undertaken to compare visual acuity, visual field parameters, RNFL thickness, and GCC thickness in patients with sellar and parasellar brain tumors and to correlate these findings with tumor size and duration on MRI.

Methods

This hospital-based, cross-sectional comparative study was conducted over a period of 18 months at a tertiary care teaching hospital after obtaining approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee. Consecutive patients diagnosed with sellar or parasellar brain tumors on contrast-enhanced MRI and referred from the Department of Neurosurgery were prospectively enrolled after obtaining written informed consent. Patients aged between 12 and 60 years were included. Tumors were classified as sellar or parasellar based on MRI localization. Tumor size was recorded as the maximum linear diameter, and the duration of symptoms was documented from the onset of visual complaints.

Patients unable to perform reliable visual field or OCT testing due to severe visual impairment were excluded. Additional exclusion criteria included prior cranial radiotherapy, ocular media opacities, glaucoma, retinal or optic nerve diseases unrelated to compressive optic neuropathy, diabetes mellitus or other systemic conditions affecting the retina or optic nerve, history of ocular trauma, and recent ocular surgery or intraocular procedures within 3 months. Bedridden patients were also excluded. These criteria were applied to minimize confounding factors that could independently influence visual function or retinal thickness measurements.

All enrolled patients underwent comprehensive ophthalmic examination. The best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was measured using LogMAR charts, followed by slit-lamp biomicroscopy, intraocular pressure measurement, and dilated fundus examination. Functional assessment of the visual pathway was performed using automated perimetry with a standard 30-2 Swedish Interactive Threshold Algorithm. Visual field indices including mean deviation (MD), pattern standard deviation (PSD), and the pattern of visual field defects were recorded. Only visual fields meeting standard reliability criteria were included for analysis. Structural assessment was performed using spectral-domain OCT. Peripapillary RNFL thickness was measured in superior, inferior, nasal, and temporal quadrants, and macular GCC thickness was assessed across predefined sectors. OCT scans with poor signal strength or segmentation errors were excluded.

Statistical analysis

Data were entered into a structured database and analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences software. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Independent t-tests were used to compare BCVA, visual field indices, RNFL thickness, GCC thickness, tumor size, and duration between sellar and parasellar tumor groups. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to evaluate associations between tumor size and duration with visual acuity, visual field parameters, RNFL thickness, and GCC thickness. A two-tailed P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 67 patients with sellar and parasellar brain tumors were included in the study, comprising 31 patients in the sellar group and 36 in the parasellar group. The two groups were comparable with respect to demographic characteristics. The mean age was 42.67 ± 12.19 years in the sellar group and 40.47 ± 13.33 years in the parasellar group, with no statistically significant difference (P = 0.995). Gender distribution was also similar between the two groups (P = 0.646) [Table 1].

Evaluation of visual field patterns showed normal visual fields in 45.2% of eyes in the sellar group and 52.8% in the parasellar group. Tunnel vision was more frequently observed in the sellar tumor group, whereas hemianopia and superior or inferior quadrantanopia were similarly distributed across both groups. The overall distribution of visual field patterns did not differ significantly between sellar and parasellar tumors for either the right eye (P = 0.755) or the left eye (P = 0.493).

OCT demonstrated reduced RNFL thickness in the sellar tumor group compared to the parasellar group. In the right eye, the superior quadrant RNFL thickness was significantly lower in the sellar group (91.84 ± 29.17 µm) compared to the parasellar group (114.11 ± 50.86 µm; P = 0.029) [Table 3]. The RNFL thickness in the inferior, nasal, and temporal quadrants of the right eye was also lower in the sellar group, although these differences were not statistically significant. RNFL measurements in the left eye showed a similar trend, with lower values across all quadrants in the sellar group, without significant intergroup differences.

The GCC thickness was consistently lower in the sellar tumor group across all sectors in both eyes; however, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups. The mean duration of symptoms was longer in the sellar group (17.74 ± 12.67 months) than in the parasellar group (11.69 ± 12.75 months), though this difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.057). Tumor size was significantly larger in the parasellar group (38.86 ± 12.49 mm) compared to the sellar group (25.06 ± 10.78 mm; P < 0.001). Correlation analysis demonstrated significant associations between tumor size and visual as well as retinal structural parameters predominantly in the sellar tumor group, while such associations were limited in the parasellar group [Table 4].

Discussion

The present cross-sectional comparative study evaluates visual function and retinal structural changes in patients with sellar and parasellar brain tumors using automated perimetry and OCT. Although BCVA and global visual field indices did not differ significantly between the two groups, structural assessment demonstrated more consistent RNFL and GCC thinning in sellar tumors. In addition, tumor size showed stronger associations with both functional and structural parameters in sellar tumors compared with parasellar lesions. These findings suggest that tumor location and anatomical relationship to the optic chiasma influence the pattern of visual pathway involvement.

The ability of OCT to detect early structural damage despite relatively preserved visual function has been well documented. Danesh-Meyer et al.[15] demonstrated that RNFL thinning correlates with visual field loss in compressive optic neuropathy and may precede irreversible functional impairment. Similarly, Costello et al.[16] showed that OCT-derived RNFL measurements serve as a sensitive marker of axonal loss, often identifying damage earlier than standard perimetry. In the present study, comparable BCVA, MD, and PSD values between the two groups despite measurable OCT differences support the role of OCT as an early and objective indicator of optic nerve injury.

Macular GCC analysis has emerged as an important tool in the evaluation of chiasmal compression. In the present study, GCC thickness was consistently lower in the sellar tumor group, and significant negative correlations were observed between tumor size and GCC thickness in sellar tumors. Moon et al.[17] first reported that ganglion cell–inner plexiform layer thinning occurs early in compressive optic neuropathy and may be detected before significant RNFL loss. High sensitivity of macular GCC parameters for detecting early ganglion cell damage in pituitary adenoma-related chiasmal compression was further emphasized by Tieger et al.[18] and Blanch et al.[19] These findings support the concept that GCC measurements reflect cumulative neuronal loss and may provide additional prognostic information beyond peripapillary RNFL analysis.

The pattern of RNFL involvement observed in this study is anatomically consistent with previously published literature. In our cohort, superior quadrant RNFL thinning in the right eye reached statistical significance in the sellar group, while other quadrants demonstrated a similar but nonsignificant trend. Monteiro et al.[20] described preferential thinning of superior and nasal RNFL quadrants in chiasmal compression due to involvement of decussating nasal fibers. Garcia et al.[21] similarly reported characteristic sectoral RNFL loss in patients with pituitary macroadenomas. This sectoral vulnerability underscores the importance of quadrant-wise OCT analysis rather than reliance on average RNFL thickness alone.

Tumor size has emerged as a key determinant of visual and structural damage in sellar tumors. Fujimoto et al.[22] showed that increasing tumor size and suprasellar extension in pituitary adenomas are associated with worse visual fields and poorer postoperative recovery. More recently, Qin et al.[23] demonstrated significant correlations between pituitary adenoma size, RNFL thinning, and reduced visual acuity. Our findings extend these observations by showing significant associations between sellar tumor size and BCVA, MD, PSD, RNFL thickness, and GCC thickness, suggesting a strong structure–function relationship in sellar disease.

In contrast, parasellar tumors in our study showed weaker and more localized correlations between tumor size and OCT parameters. This is consistent with observations by Dutton and Al-Mefty, who noted that parasellar tumors such as meningiomas and craniopharyngiomas often displace or encase the optic nerves and chiasm rather than producing uniform direct compression.[2425] Jacob et al.[26] also reported that visual dysfunction in parasellar lesions is less predictable and does not always correlate linearly with tumor size. These differences in growth pattern and anatomical relationship likely account for the limited correlations observed in this group.

Visual field pattern analysis in the present study revealed higher PSD values and a greater frequency of tunnel vision in sellar tumors, indicating more localized and severe visual field defects. Similar findings have been described by Laws et al.[27] and Miller[28] in pituitary macroadenomas, attributing them to progressive chiasmal compression.

The presence of normal visual fields in a substantial proportion of patients despite OCT abnormalities further supports observations by Tieger et al.,[18] who emphasized that structural damage may precede detectable functional loss. This highlights the complementary role of OCT and perimetry in the evaluation of compressive optic neuropathy.

According to the multivariate analysis, a seller tumor site and increased tumor size independently correlate with an elevated risk of visual field impairment, regardless of adjustments for age, gender, and length of symptom presence. This is evidence supporting the hypothesis that chiasmal compression by sellar tumors poses a relatively higher risk compared to the varying anatomical consequences that could be experienced in case of parasellar tumors. It is possible that the lack of significant correlations between RNFL/GCC thickness and visual field loss can be attributed to insufficient sample size or a stronger association between tumor volume and structural changes for sellar tumors only.

From a clinical perspective, the present findings support the role of OCT as an adjunctive tool in the evaluation and monitoring of patients with sellar and parasellar tumors. Studies have shown that preserved RNFL and GCC thickness are associated with better postoperative visual recovery, whereas advanced thinning predicts limited improvement.[151720] Incorporating OCT into routine neuro-ophthalmic evaluation may therefore aid in early detection, prognostication, and surgical decision-making.

The limitations of this study include its cross-sectional design, modest sample size, and lack of postoperative follow-up, which precludes assessment of longitudinal changes and visual recovery. Similar limitations have been reported in other OCT-based studies of compressive optic neuropathy.[1923] Tumor size was assessed using the maximum linear diameter, which does not account for tumor volume, suprasellar extension, or the degree of optic chiasmal compression. Future prospective studies with longitudinal OCT and visual field follow-up are needed to establish robust OCT-based thresholds for predicting visual outcomes.

Conclusion

This cross-sectional study demonstrates that sellar brain tumors are associated with a more consistent pattern of retinal structural thinning and visual dysfunction compared with parasellar tumors. Structural changes on OCT showed significant associations with tumor size in sellar lesions, suggesting a closer structure–function relationship in this group. OCT-derived RNFL and GCC measurements may serve as useful adjunctive markers of optic pathway involvement, even in patients with relatively preserved visual fields. Integration of OCT with automated perimetry and MRI may assist in comprehensive clinical assessment; however, longitudinal studies are required before definitive prognostic or management recommendations can be made.

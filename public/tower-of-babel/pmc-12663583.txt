A deep learning-based intelligent curriculum system for enhancing public music education: a case study across three universities in Southwest China
Authors: Hao Du, Prakan Butkaew
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Responding to national aesthetic education reforms, this study introduces a deep learning-driven platform to enhance public music education in Southwest China’s universities. Utilizing LSTM and Transformer models, the system analyzes real-time student learning, predicts mastery trends, and delivers personalized feedback via a cloud-based interface. A semester-long experiment across Guizhou Minzu University, Guizhou University, and Xichang University compared three groups: traditional instruction, MOOC-based hybrid teaching, and AI-enhanced personalized learning. The AI group achieved 32% higher post-test mastery scores, with predictive models maintaining high accuracy (RMSE < 0.15). The platform supports adaptive assessments, intelligent feedback, and instructional decision-making, offering a scalable solution for AI integration in arts education, particularly in culturally diverse, data-scarce settings. This work informs policymakers and developers aiming to modernize aesthetic education through advanced computing.

Introduction

Public music education, a critical component of aesthetic eduction, plays an indispensable role in cultivating holistic human development and enhancing cultural literacy among university students1–3. In recent years, China’s national educational policy has increasingly emphasized the significance of integrating aesthetic education into comprehensive universities, underscoring music education as pivotal for enriching students’ intellectual, emotional, and social competencies4,5. However, despite robust policy advocacy, tangible improvements in teaching efficiency, student engagement, and resource utilization within public music education, especially at comprehensive universities in Southwest China, remain limited6,7.

Comprehensive universities in Southwest China typically encounter several unique challenges in public music education, including limited teaching resources, diverse student background, and significant variations in individual student needs and learning behaviors8,9. Among these institutions, Xichang University exemplifies the complexity faced by regional comprehensive universities - balancing national policy demands against practical constraints inherent to less economically developed regions. Identifying effective methods to enhance educational efficiency in such contexts demands innovative solutions grounded in interdisciplinary integration and technological advancement.

Existing literature identifies educational efficiency broadly as the optimal use of teaching resources to achieve educational goals10,11, which is critically influenced by curriculum design, instructional methods, resource allocation, and individualized student support. Traditional instructional approaches, characterized predominantly by face-to-face teaching and uniform curriculum structures, often neglect the diversity of student needs, resulting in suboptimal outcomes and limited student engagement12,13. Consequently, educational efficiency deteriorates, highlighting a critical gap between educational objectives and outcomes.

Empirical research in educational economics consistently emphasizes that maximizing teaching efficiency requires targeted interventions tailored to student-specific learning needs and behavioral patterns14,15. Previous studies demonstrate the potential of Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) to partially address these inefficiencies by offering flexible, accessible, and resource-rich learning environments16,17. MOOCs provide a feasible solution for scaling educational content and overcoming regional disparities in educational resources. Nevertheless, the standalone implementation of MOOCs often fails to fully capitalize on their transformative potential, primarily due to inadequate personalization and insufficient real-time instructional feedback mechanisms.

Recent advancements in artificial intelligence (AI), particularly deep learning technologies, present unprecedented opportunities to overcome these shortcomings18,19. Deep learning, especially models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Transformers, excels at modeling complex, sequential data, making them ideal for analyzing educational behaviors and learning paths20–22. Research in other educational contexts has shown that deep learning algorithms can accurately predict student performance, dynamically adjust instructional content, and significantly enhance individualized educational experiences23,24. However, while existing research has explored various AI applications in music education25–34, the integration of hybrid LSTM-Transformer architectures for personalized learning in public music education remains limited, particularly within the resource-constrained contexts of comprehensive universities in Southwest China. Furthermore, most existing studies focus on specialized music programs rather than public music courses for non-music majors, leaving a significant gap in understanding how to effectively implement AI-driven personalization for diverse student populations with varying musical backgrounds.

Motivated by this research gap, our study seeks to systematically explore how deep learning methodologies can revolutionize public music education efficiency in comprehensive universities. Specifically, this research is designed to quantitatively assess the educational efficiency of current music curricula at Xichang University and comparable institutions in the region, then experimentally evaluate improvements achieved through deep learning-driven interventions. Our approach involves creating a digital cloud-based music education platform embedded with deep learning algorithms to provide personalized content recommendations, real-time performance tracking, adaptive assessments, and optimized instructional feedback.

The novelty of our research lies in its innovative fusion of educational economics, digital pedagogy, and cutting-edge AI technologies. By leveraging deep learning techniques, our platform aims to precisely map and predict student learning behaviors, thereby offering personalized educational experiences tailored to individual proficiency paths. This targeted personalization is expected to substantially elevate educational efficiency by aligning resource allocation with student-specific needs, ultimately fostering higher engagement and improved learning outcomes.

To ensure robust empirical validity, our research employs a semester-long experimental design involving three representative comprehensive universities in Southwest China: Guizhou Minzu University (College of Arts), Guizhou University (College of Music), and Xichang University (College of Humanities). Each institution hosts distinct student populations with varying socio-economic backgrounds and musical aptitudes, thereby providing a rich empirical environment to test the effectiveness and generalizability of our proposed methodology. Within each university, students are systematically divided into three instructional groups: a traditional face-to-face teaching group, a MOOC-based blended instruction group, and a deep learning-enhanced personalized instruction group.

Preliminary insights indicate that deep learning-enhanced instruction significantly outperforms both traditional and MOOC-based approaches concerning student performance improvement, engagement levels, and instructional resource utilization efficiency35,36. Specifically, we anticipate measurable improvements such as higher course completion rates, enhanced student mastery of music skills, increased resource utilization efficiency, and improved teacher-student interactions through adaptive content recommendation and timely feedback mechanisms.

By providing an integrated technological and pedagogical framework, our study contributes substantially to theoretical and practical understandings of how deep learning can be strategically employed in public music education37,38. The outcomes of this research are expected to offer critical insights for educational policymakers, curriculum designers, and university administrators seeking innovative, scalable, and sustainable methods to enhance aesthetic educational efficiency. Ultimately, this research aims not only to address regional educational disparities but also to advance a broader educational paradigm shift toward personalized, data-driven, and technologically empowered public music education.

Literature review

Artificial intelligence applications in educational systems

The integration of artificial intelligence into music education has gained significant momentum in recent years, with researchers exploring various AI techniques to enhance learning outcomes and teaching efficiency. Chen and Sun (2024) investigated the usage of artificial intelligence technology in music education systems under deep learning, demonstrating improved student engagement and learning effectiveness25. Similarly, Chen (2025) explored the innovation of music teaching methods in universities based on fuzzy decision support systems and deep learning, revealing substantial improvements in pedagogical approaches26. Recent studies have shown promising results in applying AI to music education contexts. Cheng and Qu (2025) focused on intelligent generation and optimization of resources in music teaching reform based on artificial intelligence and deep learning, providing evidence for AI’s capability in content curation and delivery27. Lin and Chen (2025) developed an intelligent music education system utilizing algorithms for personalized learning experiences, demonstrating the potential for adaptive learning environments28.

Deep learning approaches have shown particular promise in educational applications, especially for sequential data modeling and personalized recommendation systems. Wu (2025) examined the application of artificial intelligence in music education management, highlighting both opportunities and challenges in implementation29. Tang and Zhang (2022) developed an intelligent deep learning-enabled recommendation algorithm for teaching music students, achieving notable improvements in learning outcomes30. The application of deep learning models such as LSTM and Transformer architectures in educational contexts has demonstrated superior performance in capturing temporal learning patterns. Wei et al. (2022) explored college music education and teaching based on AI techniques, providing empirical evidence for the effectiveness of neural network approaches31. Qiusi (2022) investigated research methods for improving music education levels under AI technology background, offering insights into systematic implementation strategies32.

MOOCs and blended learning in arts education with personalized learning systems

Personalized learning has emerged as a key application area for AI in education, with researchers developing sophisticated systems to adapt content delivery to individual learner needs. Zhang (2023) examined fusion artificial intelligence technology in music education teaching, demonstrating improved student satisfaction and learning efficiency33. Zhang et al. (2024) conducted a systematic literature review on transforming music education through artificial intelligence, providing comprehensive insights into current research trends and future directions34. Advanced recommendation systems have shown particular promise in music education contexts. Cipta et al. (2024) optimized AI-powered music creation social media to amplify learning content, revealing new approaches to content engagement and distribution39. Amin (2024) developed a music education framework using large language models (LLMs), contributing to the growing body of work on AI-enhanced pedagogical frameworks40.

Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) have provided scalable solutions for music education, though research indicates limitations in personalization and adaptive feedback mechanisms. Chang et al. (2024) developed MusicARLtrans Net, a multimodal agent interactive music education system driven via reinforcement learning, demonstrating advanced approaches to interactive learning environments41. Recent developments in intelligent systems for higher education have shown promise for addressing personalization challenges. Yigci et al. (2025) explored large language model-based chatbots in higher education, revealing potential applications for student support and guidance42. Nguyen (2023) investigated intelligent systems in education, proposing requirements and design methods that inform system architecture decisions43. Nguyen et al. (2021) developed ontology-based integration of knowledge bases for building intelligent searching chatbots, contributing to foundational technologies for educational AI systems44.

Research gaps and theoretical framework

While existing research has made significant contributions to AI applications in music education, several gaps remain unaddressed. First, most studies focus on specialized music programs rather than public music education for non-music majors in comprehensive universities. Second, limited research has explored the integration of LSTM and Transformer architectures specifically for music learning behavior prediction and content recommendation. Third, few studies have investigated the effectiveness of AI-driven personalization in resource-constrained educational environments, particularly in developing regions.

Furthermore, existing research lacks comprehensive evaluation frameworks that combine technical performance metrics with pedagogical effectiveness measures. Most studies focus either on algorithmic performance or educational outcomes, but rarely integrate both perspectives systematically.

Based on this literature review, our study addresses these gaps by developing a hybrid LSTM-Transformer system specifically designed for public music education in comprehensive universities, with particular attention to the unique challenges faced by institutions in Southwest China. Our approach contributes to the theoretical understanding of AI-enhanced music education while providing practical solutions for scalable implementation in resource-constrained environments.

Materials and methods

Research design and case selection

This study employed a semester-long, controlled experimental approach to investigate how deep learning-enhanced methods could improve public music education efficiency at comprehensive universities in Southwest China. To ensure regional representativeness and practical validity, three distinct institutions were purposively selected: the College of Arts at Guizhou Minzu University, the College of Music at Guizhou University, and the College of Humanities at Xichang University. These institutions represent different regional educational contexts and varying levels of institutional resources, enabling generalizable insights across diverse university settings.

Participants included a total of 113 first-year undergraduate students enrolled in non-music major classes across the three universities. To ensure methodological rigor and comparability, the following inclusion criteria were applied: Academic Status: First-year undergraduate students enrolled in mandatory public music education coursesMusical Background: No formal music training exceeding 2 years or professional music education backgroundAge Range: 18–22 years old to ensure developmental homogeneityTechnology Access: Reliable access to mobile devices and internet connectivity for platform interactionInformed Consent: Voluntary participation with signed informed consent formsPrior to the experimental intervention, comprehensive baseline assessments were conducted to validate homogeneity among participants and enable proper statistical analysis. The baseline evaluation included:Musical Proficiency Pre-test: A standardized 50-item assessment covering rhythm recognition, pitch identification, basic music theory, and listening comprehension, scored on a 0–1 normalized scaleDemographic Questionnaire: Age, gender, academic major, prior musical experience, and socioeconomic backgroundLearning Style Inventory: Visual, auditory, and kinesthetic learning preference assessment using Kolb’s Learning Style InventoryTechnology Familiarity Survey: Self-reported comfort level with digital learning platforms and mobile applicationsMusical Interest and Motivation Scale: 5-point Likert scale assessment of intrinsic motivation toward music learningStratified randomization was employed to ensure balanced group allocation across key demographic and academic variables. Participants were first stratified by university, gender, and academic major, then randomly assigned to experimental conditions within each stratum using computer-generated random numbers.

Granular, continuous data logging was employed to systematically capture students’ learning behaviors, encompassing metrics such as video viewing durations, frequencies of interactive engagement, chapter completion rates, and performance outcomes on assessments. A formative assessment, administered at the midpoint of the semester, served to deliver interim feedback, enabling instructors to make evidence-based adjustments to their pedagogical strategies. Upon conclusion of the intervention, participants undertook a comprehensive post-test designed to objectively measure advancements in music proficiency. Furthermore, an anonymous survey was distributed to elicit qualitative insights, probing students’ levels of satisfaction, engagement, perceived instructional effectiveness, and evaluations of the digital platform’s usability, thereby enriching the understanding of the learning experience.

Platform architecture and deep learning modules

To meet the increasing demand for personalized, scalable, and efficient music education in comprehensive universities, we developed a mobile cloud-based platform that integrates modular educational services with intelligent deep learning-driven recommendation systems. The platform was specifically designed to address the challenges of student heterogeneity, limited faculty resources, and real-time instructional adaptation in public music education. This section elaborates on the system architecture, the embedded deep learning framework, and the computational models used for behavior prediction and content recommendation.

Model inference pseudocode

Once the deep learning model is fully trained and validated, it is deployed to the backend of the mobile music education platform, where it functions as the core engine for real-time personalized instruction. The model operates continuously throughout the learning process, enabling the system to monitor and respond to students’ evolving learning needs in a timely and adaptive manner. This inference mechanism is triggered automatically whenever a student completes a meaningful interaction—such as watching a video, submitting a quiz, or responding to a recommendation—ensuring that the instructional content remains closely aligned with the learner’s current status.

The inference pipeline serves two primary functions:Proficiency Prediction: Based on the student’s historical interaction sequence (including video engagement metrics, quiz performance, content navigation behavior, and system feedback), the model predicts the student’s current learning proficiency. This prediction is expressed as a scalar score \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \hat{y}_T \in [0, 1], \end{aligned}$$\end{document} where a higher value indicates greater mastery of the current course material.Personalized Content Recommendation: The predicted proficiency score is then used to determine the most appropriate content level—foundational, intermediate, or advanced. The model filters a global pool of available learning resources to extract those that match the learner’s target level and prerequisite history. Candidate materials are subsequently ranked based on relevance to the learner’s encoded behavior vector. To further enhance engagement, a diversification function is applied to avoid redundant or overly similar recommendations.This process runs in real time and scales efficiently across hundreds of students. The curriculum is specifically tailored for non-music-major students, aiming to accommodate diverse levels of prior musical knowledge, motivational profiles, and cognitive learning styles. It is tightly integrated with the deep learning-based recommendation system to enable differentiated instruction and dynamic content delivery.

The baseline analysis revealed that participants were well-matched across experimental conditions. The average age was approximately 19 years with minimal variation between groups. Gender distribution was nearly equal across all conditions. Academic major distribution showed balanced representation from engineering, humanities, and science disciplines. Prior musical experience was limited across all groups, with most participants having less than one year of formal training, confirming the appropriateness of the sample for public music education research.

Discussion and conclusions

The findings of this study offer compelling empirical evidence that the strategic integration of deep learning-driven personalization, layered curriculum design, and mobile cloud-based delivery significantly enhances the effectiveness of public music education within comprehensive universities in Southwest China. This triadic approach leverages advanced computational techniques to tailor educational content to individual learner profiles, employs a structured yet flexible curriculum to scaffold musical knowledge and skills, and utilizes the accessibility of mobile cloud platforms to ensure seamless delivery of resources. As a result, this framework fosters an inclusive and adaptive learning environment that is particularly well-suited to the diverse needs of students in the region’s higher education institutions.

As illustrated in Fig. 9, the proposed instructional efficiency framework elucidates the synergistic interplay among advanced teaching methodologies, robust technical infrastructure, and clearly articulated pedagogical objectives. This interconnected system not only facilitates the delivery of high-quality, personalized educational experiences but also drives measurable improvements in learning outcomes, including enhanced musical proficiency, greater learner engagement, and improved retention of complex concepts. The framework’s emphasis on integrating cutting-edge technology with pedagogically sound principles underscores its potential to serve as a model for optimizing music education in similar academic contexts. By aligning technological innovation with curriculum design and clear educational goals, this approach ensures that students are equipped with both the technical skills and the creative capacities necessary to thrive in their musical studies.Fig. 9Summary Framework for Improving Educationa.

Summary Framework for Improving Educationa.

Compared with both the traditional face-to-face group and the MOOC blended group, the deep learning-enhanced group achieved a significantly higher post-test score gain (+65% vs MOOC and +180% vs Traditional), a markedly higher module completion rate (95%), and superior student satisfaction levels (4.41/5), as supported by Figs. 4 and 5. These quantitative improvements align with qualitative evidence gathered through structured teacher interviews, which indicate that instructors can devote more effort to high-value feedback and advanced coaching when routine module matching and adaptive recommendations are automated by the system.

Furthermore, the layered curriculum design–validated by the satisfaction radar results in Fig. 8–effectively addressed the diverse needs of students with varying proficiency levels, ensuring that both low- and high-performing learners were engaged at an appropriate pace and content depth. This demonstrates the viability of combining AI-driven analytics with modular course structures to improve instructional efficiency in heterogeneous learning contexts.

However, this study is not without limitations. The sample size was limited to three universities in Southwest China, and the intervention period spanned only one academic semester. These constraints may affect the generalizability of the findings to broader institutional contexts or long-term implementations. Future research should expand the participant base, incorporate multi-semester longitudinal designs, and investigate how dynamic learner modeling can adapt to evolving behavior patterns in real-world blended learning environments.

Finally, the policy implications summarized in Table 9 highlight how these research insights can translate into practical actions, such as refining institutional teaching guidelines, expanding smart learning infrastructure, and incentivizing the adoption of AI-supported pedagogical tools. Summary of Policy-Oriented Findings, Supporting Evidence, and Recommendations.

However, this study acknowledges several important limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings and planning future research. First, the sample size was limited to three universities in Southwest China, and the intervention period spanned only one academic semester (16 weeks). These constraints may affect the generalizability of the findings to broader institutional contexts, different cultural settings, or long-term implementations. The relatively homogeneous regional context may limit applicability to institutions with different resource levels, student demographics, or educational policies.

Second, while our study demonstrated significant short-term learning improvements, the long-term retention of knowledge and sustained engagement beyond the intervention period remain unexplored. Educational research emphasizes the importance of longitudinal follow-up to assess whether AI-enhanced learning leads to durable knowledge acquisition and continued musical engagement. Future studies should implement multi-semester tracking to evaluate long-term educational impact.

Third, the current evaluation framework focuses primarily on quantitative metrics and may not fully capture the nuanced aspects of aesthetic education, such as creative expression, cultural appreciation, and emotional engagement with music. While our qualitative feedback surveys provided some insights, more comprehensive ethnographic or phenomenological approaches could offer deeper understanding of student experiences with AI-enhanced music education.

Fourth, the study’s focus on public music education for non-music majors may limit transferability to specialized music programs or other arts education domains. The effectiveness of our approach in contexts requiring advanced technical skills or intensive creative development remains to be investigated.

Finally, the resource requirements for implementing and maintaining the AI-driven platform may pose challenges for widespread adoption, particularly in resource-constrained educational environments. Future research should explore cost-effective implementation strategies and investigate the scalability of such systems across diverse institutional contexts.

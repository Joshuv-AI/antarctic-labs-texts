Decomposition Theorem for Product of Fundamental Crystals in Monomial Realization
of type

Abstract
We consider a product of fundamental crystals of type C n C_{n} in monomial realization, where the product means a natural product of Laurent monomials, not a tensor product. Then we shall show that the product still holds a crystal structure and describe how it is decomposed into irreducible crystals, which is, in general, different from the decomposition rule for the tensor product of the fundamental crystals.



1
Introduction

For a symmetrizable Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, let
U\hskip-1.49994pt_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
be the corresponding quantum group.
It is known that any integrable highest weight
U\hskip-1.49994pt_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module
V

has a crystal base

(L,B,wt,\{\varepsilon_{i}\},\{\varphi_{i}\},\{{\tilde{e}}_{i}\},\{{\tilde{f}}_{i}\})_{i\in I}

[
5
,
6
]
, where
I
is the index set of simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
,

B
is called a crystal of
V
,

\varepsilon_{i},\varphi_{i}
are integer valued-functions on
B
and

\tilde{e}_{i}
,
\tilde{f}_{i}
are called the
Kashiwara operators
on
B
.
Roughly, we can say that crystal base is a basis for a
U\hskip-1.49994pt_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module at
q=0
and
holds a remarkable property for the tensor product of modules, namely,
if
U\hskip-1.49994pt_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules
M_{i}
(
i=1,2
) have crystal bases
(L_{i},B_{i})
(
i=1,2
), then
the tensor product
M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
holds
a crystal base
(L_{1}\otimes L_{2},B_{1}\otimes B_{2})
.
The explicit actions of the Kashiwara operators on tensor product of crystal bases
are given as follows (see Sect.2 for details): for
b_{1}\in B_{1},\,b_{2}\in B_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{i}(b_{1}\otimes\,b_{2})

\displaystyle=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\tilde{e}_{i}b_{1}\otimes\,b_{2}&\mbox{ if}&\varphi_{i}(b_{1})\geq\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2}),\\
b_{1}\otimes\tilde{e}_{i}b_{2}&\mbox{ if}&\varphi_{i}(b_{1})<\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2}),\end{array}}\right.

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{i}(b_{1}\otimes\,b_{2})

\displaystyle=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\tilde{f}_{i}b_{1}\otimes\,b_{2}&\mbox{ if}&\varphi_{i}(b_{1})>\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2}),\\
b_{1}\otimes\tilde{f}_{i}b_{2}&\mbox{ if}&\varphi_{i}(b_{1})\leq\varepsilon_{i}(b_{2}).\end{array}}\right.

Indeed, using these rules, we consider a decomposition of tensor product of crystal bases into irreducible crystal bases (see e.g.,
[
9
]
), which describes
the decomposition of tensor product of corresponding modules, that is,
for any dominant weight
\lambda,\,\mu
, there exist dominant weights

\lambda_{1},\mathellipsis,\lambda_{k}
such that

B(\lambda)\otimes B(\mu)\cong B(\lambda_{1})\oplus\mathellipsis\oplus B(\lambda_{k}),

and then we obtain the decomposition

V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)\cong V(\lambda_{1})\oplus\mathellipsis\oplus V(\lambda_{k}).

There are several types of realization for crystal bases,
e.g.
, tableaux realization, path realization, geometric realization,
polyhedral realization,
etc
.
Here we will treat the “monomial realization” which is introduced by
Nakajima
[
10
]
and refined by Kashiwara
[
3
]
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of Laurent monomials in the variables

Y_{i}(n)\,(i\in I,\,n\in\mathbb{Z},
see Sect.3):

\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\left\{{\left.\prod\limits_{i\in I,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{Y_{i}(n)}^{{y}_{i}(n)}\,\right|}\,y_{i}(n)\in\mathbb{Z}\,{\hbox{vanish except finitely many }}(i,\,n)\right\}.

Then we can define the crystal structures on this set of Laurent monomials.
This
{\mathcal{M}}
or its subcrystals are
called a
monomial realization
of crystals.
In Sect.3, it is introduced that if a monomial

Y
satisfies the highest weight condition,
then the irreducible component in
\mathcal{M}
including

Y
is isomorphic to the crystal
B(wt(Y))
corresponding
to the module
V(wt(Y))

[
3
]
as we mentioned above. In fact,
by Corollary
3.6

if
\mathfrak{g}
is semi-simple, then
\mathcal{M}
is an infinite direct sum of irreducible crystal
B(\lambda)
’s, namely,
any irreducible component in
\mathcal{M}
is isomorphic to the crystal

B(\lambda)
for some dominant weight
\lambda
.
Let us denote an irreducible component corresponding to a highest monomial
Y

by
\mathcal{M}(Y)
. The tensor product
\mathcal{M}(Y)\otimes\mathcal{M}(Y^{\prime})

has a crystal structure. But it is not clear whether the set of products

\mathcal{M}(Y)\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y^{\prime})\coloneqq\left\{M_{1}\cdot M_{2}|\,M_{1}\in\mathcal{M}(Y),M_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(Y^{\prime})\right\},

holds a crystal structure, where
M_{1}\cdot M_{2}
means a natural product of Laurent monomials.

Let
\mathcal{M}(Y_{i}(m))

(i\in I,\,m\in\mathbb{Z})

be one of monomial realizations for the fundamental crystal
B(\Lambda_{i})

(in
[
2
]
the explicit forms are given for classical type
\mathfrak{g}
),
where note that the weight of
Y_{i}(m)
is equal to
the fundamental weight
\Lambda_{i}
and

\tilde{e}_{j}Y_{i}(m)=0
for any
j\in I
.
In this article, for type
C_{n}
we consider the following problems
(see
[
1
]
for type
A_{n}
):

(1)

Dose the product

\mathcal{M}(Y_{p}(m))\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y_{q}(1))

(p,q\in I,\,m\in\mathbb{Z})
hold a crystal structure?

(2)

If the answer for (
(1)
) is affirmative,
describe the decomposition of the crystal

\mathcal{M}(Y_{p}(m))\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y_{q}(1))

(m\geq 1
).

Indeed, the answer for (
(1)
) is affirmative, which is shown in
Proposition
5.1
, and more general results are given
in Corollary
5.3
;
for any
m_{1},m_{2},\mathellipsis m_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
p_{1},p_{2},\mathellipsis p_{l}\in I\,(l>0)
,

\mathcal{M}(Y_{p_{1}}(m_{1}))\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y_{p_{2}}(m_{2}))\cdot\mathellipsis\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y_{p_{l}}(m_{l})),

holds a crystal structure. Here note that there might be some monomials
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(Y)
and
M^{\prime}_{1},M^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(Y^{\prime})
such that
M_{1}\neq M^{\prime}_{1}
,
M_{2}\neq M^{\prime}_{2}
and
M_{1}\cdot M_{2}=M^{\prime}_{1}\cdot M^{\prime}_{2}
. Therefore, it may happen on the number of monomials:

\#(\mathcal{M}(Y)\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y^{\prime}))<\#(\mathcal{M}(Y)\otimes\mathcal{M}(Y^{\prime})),

and then one can deduce that the decomposition for
\mathcal{M}(Y)\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y^{\prime})
may differ from the one for the tensor product

\mathcal{M}(Y)\otimes\mathcal{M}(Y^{\prime})
.
Indeed, the answer for (2) is given in Theorem
6.4
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(Y_{p}(m))\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y_{q}(1))

\displaystyle\cong B(\Lambda_{p}+\Lambda_{q})

\displaystyle\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,c)\neq(p,q),(q,p)\\
0\leq a\leq p,\,\,a+p\leq c+q,\,\,(p+q)-(a+c)\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0.}\\
a\leq c\leq n,\,\,a+q\leq c+p,\,\,\frac{p+q+c-a}{2}\leq n.\end{subarray}}B(\Lambda_{a}+\Lambda_{c})\cdot\mathcal{L}(m\geq\frac{q-p-a-c+2}{2}+n)

\displaystyle\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,c)\neq(p,q),(q,p)\\
{0\leq a<p<c\leq n},\,\,a+p\leq c+q,\,\,p+q=a+c,\\
a+q\leq c+p,\,\,\frac{p+q+c-a}{2}\leq n.\end{subarray}}B(\Lambda_{a}+\Lambda_{c})\cdot\mathcal{L}(m\geq q-a+1),

where
\mathcal{L}(P)=1
if
P
is true and
\mathcal{L}(P)=0
otherwise.
By Proposition
4.4
, forgetting the condition in
\mathcal{L}
,
then we get the decomposition rule
for the tensor product
\mathcal{M}(Y_{p}(m))\otimes\mathcal{M}(Y_{q}(1))
.
Hence, by this theorem, if
m
is sufficiently large, we get that

\mathcal{M}(Y_{p}(m))\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y_{q}(1))\cong B(\Lambda_{p})\otimes B(\Lambda_{q})
.
However, if not we find that
\mathcal{M}(Y_{p}(m))\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y_{q}(1))
is not
necessarily isomorphic to
B(\Lambda_{p})\otimes B(\Lambda_{q})
.
Indeed, if
m=1
, we know that
\mathcal{M}(Y_{p}(m))\cdot\mathcal{M}(Y_{q}(1))\cong B(\Lambda_{p}+\Lambda_{q})\varsubsetneq B(\Lambda_{p})\otimes B(\Lambda_{q})
.

Let

.

3
Nakajima’s monomial realization

In this section, we recall the crystal structure on the set of monomials discovered by H. Nakajima
[
10
]
. Our exposition follows that of M.Kashiwara
[
3
]
.
Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{g})
be the set of Laurent monomials in the variables
Y_{i}(m)\,(i\in I,m\in\mathbb{Z})
, called
Y
-variable:

\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\left\{{\left.\prod\limits_{i\in I,m\in\mathbb{Z}}{Y_{i}(m)}^{{y}_{i}(m)}\,\right|}\,y_{i}(m)\in\mathbb{Z}\,{\hbox{vanish except finitely many }}(i,\,m)\right\}.

We shall define a crystal structure of
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
c={(c_{ij})}_{i\neq j\in I}
be a set of integers such that

c_{ij}+\,c_{ji}=1
for
i\neq j
.
We set

A_{i}(m)\,=\,Y_{i}(m)Y_{i}(m\,+\,1)\,\prod\limits_{j\neq i}{Y_{j}(m\,+c_{ji})}^{\langle h_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle\,}.

For a monomial
M\,=\,\prod\limits_{i\,\in I,m\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}}{Y_{i}(m)}^{y_{i}(m)}\in\mathcal{M}
, we define

\displaystyle wt(M)

\displaystyle=\,\sum\nolimits_{i}(\sum\nolimits_{m}{y_{i}(m)})\Lambda_{i}\,\,,

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(M)

\displaystyle={\rm max}\,\bigg\{\,\sum_{k\leq m}{y_{i}(k)};\,m\in\,\mathbb{Z}\bigg\},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}(M)

\displaystyle={\rm max}\,\bigg\{\,-\sum_{k>m}{y_{i}(k)};\,m\in\,\mathbb{Z}\bigg\}.

We also define

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{i}(M)

\displaystyle=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{rcl}0&\mbox{ If}&\varphi_{i}(M)=0,\\
A_{i}{(n_{f})}^{-1}M&\mbox{ If}&\varphi_{i}(M)>0,\end{array}}\right.\qquad\tilde{e}_{i}(M)

\displaystyle=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{rcl}0&\mbox{ If}&\varepsilon_{i}(M)=0,\\
A_{i}(n_{e})M&\mbox{ If}&\varepsilon_{i}(M)>0,\end{array}}\right.

where

\displaystyle n_{f}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{m\mid\varphi_{i}(M)\mathrm{=}\sum_{k\leq m}{y_{i}(k)}\right\}=\min\left\{m\mid\varepsilon_{i}(M)\mathrm{=-}\sum_{k>m}{y_{i}(k)}\right\}\,,

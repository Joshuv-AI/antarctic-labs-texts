Design and Analysis of Microstrip Patch Antenna Array and Electronic Beam Steering Linear Phased Antenna Array with High Directivity for Space Applications
Authors: Kannan Pauliah Nadar, Vanitha Jeyaprakasam, Inbamalar Tharcis Mariapushpam, Chettiyar Vani Vivekanand, Anna Devi Eswaralingam, Magthelin Therase Louis, Jasmine Xavier Arul Raj, Hassen Ahmed Jibril, Andrews Sonia Chellappa, Rajesh Kannan Muthukutty, Saravanaram Gopalakrishnan
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
This study proposes an E-shaped microstrip patch antenna array (MSPAA). The substrate is RT/Duroid5880. Its 2.2 dielectric constant gives it a larger bandwidth than substrate materials with higher dielectric constants. The periphery dimension of a single E patch is 50 × 41 mm2. The areas of 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 MSPAAs are 120 × 96 and 180 × 144 mm2, respectively. Array members are 10 and 7 mm apart horizontally and vertically, respectively. MTM technology builds the projected MSPAA. This work looks at the effects of gain, reflection coefficient (return loss), directivity, bandwidth, and VSWR on proposed 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 MSPAAs with different patch lengths of 30, 40, and 60 mm, patch widths of 40, 50, and 60 mm, and substrate thicknesses of 0.8 and 1.6 mm. Also, this study compares the performance of existing E-shaped MSPAAs with proposed 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 MSPAAs. The suggested MSPAA performs better than the existing E-shaped MSPAA. The series network analyzer measures the prototype MSPAA’s experimental outcomes. The polynomial is recommended for estimating the antenna array area with odd and even rows and columns. CST Studio Suite software analyzes and designs antenna arrays, and simulation findings are compared with hardware results. The main benefit of the proposed 2 × 2 MSPAA is that it reduces sidelobes. It has a gain of 8.9 dBi, a directivity of 8.95 dBi, a VSWR of 1.02, a return loss of −32.91 dB, and a bandwidth of 0.1 GHz at both 1.6 and 0.8 mm substrate material thicknesses. Across its operating range of 3.8–4.2 GHz, the recommended array antenna has a radiation gain of 7.01–7.81 dB and an efficiency of 68.51–71.11%. At 4 GHz, this study looks at how electronic steering affects the phase angle of the excitation signal, the number of elements, and the distance between elements in the beam steering linear phased antenna array (BSLPAA) using MATLAB.

Introduction

An antenna is used to transmit and receive electromagnetic (EM) waves and acts as a transducer. An antenna array consists of individual elements that are operated in their own induction field. An antenna array can produce better radiation. The directivity can be increased using the antenna array, which results in high signal strength in a specific direction. Microstrip patch antenna (MSPA) is one of the most prominently used printed antennas in wireless applications. It is mainly used for its advantages, such as easy fabrication and the fact that it can be designed in any shape. An array antenna can provide more gain and directivity than a single antenna, which means that the radiation pattern it produces is equal to the vector sum of the radiation patterns of each element in the array. The antenna arrays are mainly used in radar, military communications, and satellite communications. Antenna array’s characteristics depend on the arrangement of individual elements, the magnitude of current, and the progressive phase shift by which individual elements are excited. An antenna array is generally used for point-to-point communication. The microstrip antenna is one of the most commonly used antennas in wireless communication due to its ease of fabrication, low weight, affordability, and conformability. The microstrip patch antenna’s potential is based on its patch shape, selection of material for the ground plane and dielectric substrate, and type of feeding. The length (L), width (W), and substrate thickness (h) of an E-shaped patch antenna are used to characterize its dimensions. The dimensions are L × W × h mm.

This work presents an E-shaped wideband patch antenna array. The suggested wideband patch antenna’s performance is contrasted with that of traditional wideband microstrip patch antennas. Wideband E-shaped patch antennas with parallel arms have thick walls. The E-shaped antenna is 70 × 50 × 15 mm. For use in contemporary wireless communication employing HFSS software, the proposed E-shaped patch antenna is intended to operate between 1.9 and 2.4 GHz.9 For the IEEE 802.11a standard, a wideband E-shaped microstrip patch antenna array (MSPAA) that operates in the 5.15–5.825 GHz range has been proposed. The E-shaped antenna measures 17.2 × 20 × 3.2 mm. The reflection coefficient is measured as being 10 dB over the whole frequency band at the input of the optimized E-shaped microstrip patch antenna.3 Using HFSS modeling software, the performance of an E-shaped patch antenna was examined by changing the feed location and substrate thickness. Fabrication and Testing of a Prototype MSPA

The three types of printing techniques available on the market to print microstrip patch antenna arrays are screen printing, aerosol jet printing, and inkjet printing. The screen printing method has been chosen in this work to realize the design of the proposed MSPA in hardware because of its ease of fabrication and cost effectiveness.

The proposed MSPA design is created with the CADD tool. The positive image film format of the created design in the CADD tool is obtained with a 1:1 thickness using the Photo Plotter Machine. The substrate is made of RT Duroid with dimensions of 180 × 44 and 120 × 96 mm2 for 3 × 3 MSPA and 2 × 2 MSPA, respectively.

The conducting ink is deposited on the design, which is created using the CADD tool. The microstrip E-patch is made of a copper material. The bonding material is mixed with this low-density copper powder in a ratio of 1:1 for screen printing. It is very suitable for maintaining viscosity. Ferric chloride is used as an etchant because of its versatility. The Lacquer is used to protect the conductive layer of MSPA from atmospheric oxidation. The operating temperature is set at an optimized range of 110–115 °C. Photographic images of screen-printed MSPA on ROGER 5880 are shown in Figures 15 and 16. The contact feeding type of microstrip feed line is used in this work. A subminiature A (SMA) connector is used for external feeding support. It provides a constant 50 ohm impedance across the connector. The operating frequency of the SMA connector is up to 18 GHz. The prototype model of printed MSPA is shown in Figures 17 and 18.

Area Calculation for Antenna Array

The antenna array consists of the array elements separated by some distance, and the elements are connected by the elements that are also made of the same material. The area calculation for an array includes two polynomials. One is for the odd number of rows and columns in the array, and another is for the even number of rows and columns in the array.

Conclusions

For space applications, an antenna array that is straightforward, compact, high gain, high directivity, low VSWR, and low return loss is required. Both the proposed 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 MSPAAs, which are tested with RT/Duroid 5880 substrates that are 0.8 and 1.6 mm thick, have these features. At a resonant frequency of 4.1 GHz, the 3 × 3 MSPAA shows a gain of 9.87 dBi, a directivity of 9.97 dB, a return loss of −30.66 dB, a VSWR of 1.03, and a bandwidth of 0.1 GHz for both 0.8- and 1.6-mm thick RT and Duroid 5880 substrates. The proposed 2 × 2 MSPAA gives a higher bandwidth of 0.18 GHZ with an RT/Duroid 5880 substrate thickness of 1.6 mm than the other three proposed arrays. Also, the 2 × 2 MSPAA provides a lower VSWR of 1.02 with an RT/Duroid 5880 substrate thickness of 0.8 mm than all other proposed arrays. The gain value can be increased as the number of radiating elements increases. The polynomial for area calculation of an antenna array with odd and even numbers of rows and columns was derived. The future scope can be broadened to 5G applications since the proposed microstrip antenna array has high directivity and gain with reduced lobes, which can provide better signal strength for configuring a wide data range. Moreover, the proposed MSPA arrays will be designed using decoupling mechanisms for future SAR and MIMO applications. In further detail, the 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 antenna arrays are to be designed using circular polarized ring resonator microstrip patch antennas with graphene loads or photoconductive dipoles for tetrahertz medical imaging applications. The proposed BSLPAA beam width depends on both the spacing between the elements and the number of elements in the antenna array. If the number of elements in the array increases, then the beam width reduces. Thus, more direct radiation is achieved.

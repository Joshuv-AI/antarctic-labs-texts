Efficient Inference and Computation of Optimal Alternatives for Preference Languages Based On Lexicographic Models Longer Version of IJCAI-17 Submission
Nic Wilson, Anne-Marie George

Abstract
We analyse preference inference, through consistency, for general preference languages based on lexicographic models. We identify a property, which we call strong compositionality , that applies for many natural kinds of preference statement, and that allows a greedy algorithm for determining consistency of a set of preference statements. We also consider different natural definitions of optimality, and their relations to each other, for general preference languages based on lexicographic models. Based on our framework, we show that testing consistency, and thus inference, is polynomial for a specific preference language ℒ p ​ q ​ T ′ {\cal L}^{\prime}_{pqT} , which allows strict and non-strict statements, comparisons between outcomes and between partial tuples, both ceteris paribus and strong statements, and their combination. Computing different kinds of optimal sets is also shown to be polynomial; this is backed up by our experimental results.

Introduction

Preferences are considered in many different fields such as recommender systems and human-computer interaction
[
6
,
18
]
, databases
[
1
,
12
]
and multi-objective decision making and social choice
[
2
,
14
,
17
]
.
They are used to, e.g., find optimal decisions, solutions or items that satisfy the wishes or needs of a user or a group.
In order to reason with preferences, typically assumptions are made on the
form of the relation used to model the user preferences.
In this paper we restrict our considerations to lexicographic models,
and we consider preference inference,
which involves reasoning about the the whole set of preference models that are consistent with the input preferences, as in
[
13
,
20
]
;
this contrasts with
work that focuses on learning one lexicographic model that fits best with the given preferences
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
24
]
.

Preference inference aims to overcome gaps of knowledge in the user preferences by analysing the given preferences and deciding whether another preference statement can be deduced.
Since in this paper we allow negations of statements, preference inference
can be reduced to testing consistency of a set of preference statements.
We define a property, strong compositionality, that is satisfied by many natural types of preference statements and
enables a simple greedy algorithm for testing consistency.

Fast computation is essential in many applications;
we give a concrete instance
{\cal L}^{\prime}_{pqT}
of a preference language that allows polynomial computation.
This can represent a relatively expressive form of preference input.
It can express preferences between a pair of complete assignments to a set of variables, and, more generally, between partial assignments
to just a subset of the variables, allowing
ceteris paribus
assumptions or stronger implications on the values of other variables.
We also allow non-strict statements, two forms of strict preference statements and can represent certain negated statements.

Previous work
on preference inference based on standard lexicographic models
have considered more restricted preference languages.
Wilson
[
20
]
considered only non-negated non-strict statements,
which can only express that one assignment is at least as good as another (or equivalent).
Kohli and Jedidi
[
13
]

considers only non-negated strict statements, which can only express that one complete assignment is strictly better than another.
In
[
21
]
, preference statements are comparisons of complete assignments,
and fixed value orders for the variables are given.

Other work on preference inference includes that based on
weighted sum models
[
23
,
15
]
,
on hierarchical models
[
21
]
,
conditional lexicographic models
[
19
]
;
Pareto orders
[
10
]
, and on general strict total orders, as in e.g., work on conditional preference structures such as
[
4
]
.
While lexicographic models prevent tradeoffs between variables, they usually allow better complexity results for general preference languages.

Given a set of preference inputs and a set of alternatives,
there are several natural notions of optimal set; for instance being undominated with respect to the induced preference relation;
or being possibly optimal, i.e., optimal in at least one model.
We establish relationships between the different notions of optimality.
For preference language
{\cal L}^{\prime}_{pqT}
, we analyse the running times and set relations theoretically and experimentally, illustrating the efficiency of the algorithms.

The paper is organised as follows: Section

The concepts of inference, consistency and strongly compositional statements are investigated in Section

2
Lex Models, Composition and Extension

In this section, we give some basic definitions; in particular, we define lexicographic models, along with
a natural composition operation and extension relation, that are important in our approach.
A lexicographic model has an associated ordering of (some of the) variables, along with value orderings for each such variable.
This generates a (lexicographic) ordering relation, by first comparing outcomes
\alpha
and
\beta
on the first (i.e., most important) variable
Y
; only if
\alpha(Y)=\beta(Y)
is the next most important variable considered.

Throughout the paper we consider a fixed set
V
of
n
variables,
where for each
X\in V
,
D(X)

is the set of possible values of
X
.
For subset of variables
A\subseteq V
let
\underline{A}=\prod_{X\in A}D(X)
be the
set of possible assignments to
A
.
For
X\in V
, we abbreviate
\underline{{\{X\}}}
to
\underline{X}

(which is essentially
D(X)
).
An
outcome
is an element of
\underline{V}
,
i.e., an assignment to all the variables.
For outcome
\alpha
and subset
U
of
V
,
we define
\alpha(U)
to be the tuple in
\underline{U}
generated by projecting (i.e., restricting)
\alpha
to
U
.
These definitions describe a decision scenario over a set of alternatives given by different features.
Consider for example the choice between different flight connections.
A set of variables to describe the flight connections could be
V=\{\mathit{airline},\mathit{time},\mathit{class}\}
.
The variable domains are then given by
D(\mathit{airline})=\underline{\mathit{airline}}=\{\mathit{KLM},\mathit{LAN}\}
,
D(\mathit{time})=\{\mathit{day},\mathit{night}\}
and
D(\mathit{class})=\{\mathit{economy},\mathit{business}\}
.
Then projection of the outcome
\alpha=(\mathit{KLM},\mathit{day},\mathit{economy})
to the variables
U=\{\mathit{airline},\mathit{class}\}
is given by
\alpha(U)=(\mathit{KLM},\mathit{economy})
.

Define
{\cal G}
to be the set of lexicographic models (over the set of variables
V
);
a
lexicographic model
or
lex model
,
\pi
(over
V
), is defined
to be a (possibly empty) sequence
of the form

(Y_{1},\geq_{Y_{1}}),\ldots,(Y_{k},\geq_{Y_{k}})
,
where
Y_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,k
) are different variables in
V
,
and each
\geq_{Y_{i}}
is a total order on
\underline{Y_{i}}
.
The associated relation
\succcurlyeq_{\pi}

\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}

is defined as follows:
for outcomes
\alpha
and
\beta
,

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
if and only if
either (i) for all
i=1,\ldots,k
,
\alpha(Y_{i})=\beta(Y_{i})
;
or
(ii) there exists
i\in{\{1,\ldots,k\}}
such that
for all
j<i
,
\alpha(Y_{j})=\beta(Y_{j})
and

\alpha(Y_{i})>_{Y_{i}}\beta(Y_{i})
(i.e.,
\alpha(Y_{i})\geq_{Y_{i}}\beta(Y_{i})
and
\alpha(Y_{i})\not=\beta(Y_{i})
).
Thus
\succcurlyeq_{\pi}
is a total pre-order on
\underline{V}
, which is a total order if

k=n=|V|
.

The corresponding strict relation
\succ_{\pi}
is given by

\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta
if and only if there exists
i\in{\{1,\ldots,k\}}
such that

\alpha(Y_{i})>_{Y_{i}}\beta(Y_{i})

and for all
j<i
,
\alpha(Y_{j})=\beta(Y_{j})
.
The corresponding equivalence relation
\equiv_{\pi}

is given by

\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
if and only if
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
,
\alpha(Y_{i})=\beta(Y_{i})
.
Thus,
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
if and only if

\alpha(V_{\pi})=\beta(V_{\pi})
,
where
V_{\pi}={\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{k}\}}
is set of the variables involved in
\pi
.
Consider lexicographic models
\pi=(\mathit{airline},\mathit{KLM}>\mathit{LAN}),(\mathit{time},\mathit{day}>\mathit{night})

and
\pi^{\prime}=(\mathit{class},\mathit{economy}>\mathit{business}),(\mathit{time},\mathit{night}>\mathit{day})

for the previous example of flight connections.
In
\pi
the choice of airline decides which connection is preferred;
only if these are the same, is the flight time considered.

\pi^{\prime}
first compares the classes;
only if these are the same, are the flight times considered.
For outcomes
\alpha=(\mathit{KLM},\mathit{day},\mathit{economy})
,
\beta=(\mathit{KLM},\mathit{night},\mathit{business})

and
\gamma=(\mathit{LAN},\mathit{day},\mathit{economy})
we have
\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta\succ_{\pi}\gamma

and
\alpha\equiv_{\pi^{\prime}}\gamma\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.

Composition of lexicographic models:

We define an important composition operation on lex models,
which can be shown to be associative.
Let

\pi=(Y_{1},\geq_{Y_{1}}),\ldots,(Y_{k},\geq_{Y_{k}})
,
and

\pi^{\prime}=(Z_{1},\geq_{Z_{1}}),\ldots,(Z_{l},\geq_{Z_{l}})

be two lexicographic models.
Let
\pi^{\prime\prime}
be the sequence
\pi^{\prime}
but where pairs
(Z_{i},\geq_{Z_{i}})

are omitted if
Z_{i}\in V_{\pi}
.
Define lex model
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}
to be

\pi
followed by
\pi^{\prime\prime}
.
Note that
V_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}=V_{\pi}\cup V_{\pi^{\prime}}
.

Lemma 1
.

\circ
is an associative operation on
{\cal G}
.

Proof.

For
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
,
we say that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are disjoint if

V_{\pi}\cap V_{\pi^{\prime}}=\emptyset
.
For
set of variables
U\subseteq V
,
define
\pi^{\downarrow-U}
to be

\pi
with all pairs of the form
(X,\geq_{X})
omitted,
when
X\in U\cap V_{\pi}
.
For any
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
,
we have that
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}=\pi\circ(\pi^{\prime})^{\downarrow-V_{\pi}}
,
by definition.

Consider any
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}\in{\cal G}
.
For
j=1,2,3
, let
V_{j}
equal
V_{\pi_{j}}
.
Then,
(\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{2})\circ\pi_{3}=(\pi_{1}\circ\pi^{\prime}_{2})\circ\pi^{\prime}_{3}
,
where

\pi^{\prime}_{2}=(\pi_{2})^{\downarrow-V_{1}}
and

\pi^{\prime}_{3}=(\pi_{3})^{\downarrow-V_{1}\cup V_{2}}
.

Associativity clearly holds for pairwise disjoint lexicographic models,
so

(\pi_{1}\circ\pi^{\prime}_{2})\circ\pi^{\prime}_{3}=\pi_{1}\circ(\pi^{\prime}_{2}\circ\pi^{\prime}_{3})
.
Now,
\pi_{1}\circ(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{3})

equals
\pi_{1}\circ(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{3})^{\downarrow-V_{1}}
,
so, to prove associativity of
\circ
it is sufficient
to prove that

(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{3})^{\downarrow-V_{1}}
equals

\pi^{\prime}_{2}\circ\pi^{\prime}_{3}
.
We have that

(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{3})^{\downarrow-V_{1}}
equals

(\pi_{2}\circ(\pi_{3})^{\downarrow-V_{2}})^{\downarrow-V_{1}}
,
which equals

\pi_{2}^{\downarrow-V_{1}}\circ\pi_{3}^{\downarrow-V_{1}\cup V_{2}}
,
i.e.,
\pi^{\prime}_{2}\circ\pi^{\prime}_{3}
, completing the proof.

\Box

Definition 1
.

For lex models
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
in
{\cal G}

we say that
\pi^{\prime}

extends

\pi
if
\pi^{\prime}\not=\pi

and the sequence
\pi^{\prime}
begins with
\pi
.
We then write
\pi^{\prime}\sqsupset\pi
,
and write
\pi^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\pi
to mean
that
\pi^{\prime}
extends or equals
\pi
.
This holds if and only if
there exists a lex model
\pi^{\prime\prime}
such that

\pi^{\prime}=\pi\circ\pi^{\prime\prime}
.

Lemma 2
.

For
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
, we have

\pi^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\pi
if and only if
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}=\pi^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\pi^{\prime}
extends or equals
\pi
, and
write
\pi^{\prime}
as
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime\prime}
where
V_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}\cap V_{\pi}=\emptyset
.
Then,
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}=\pi\circ(\pi\circ\pi^{\prime\prime})
which equals

\pi\circ\pi^{\prime\prime}=\pi^{\prime}
.
Conversely, suppose that
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}=\pi^{\prime}
.
Let
k=|V_{\pi}|
and let

\pi^{\prime\prime}
be the lexicographic model consisting of the first
k
pairs
of
\pi^{\prime}
.
This equals the first
k
pairs of
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\pi
,
and thus,
\pi^{\prime}
extends or equals
\pi
.

\Box

The composition of the two previously defined lex models is
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}=(\mathit{airline},\mathit{KLM}>\mathit{LAN}),(\mathit{time},\mathit{day}>\mathit{night}),(\mathit{class},\mathit{economy}>\mathit{business})
and
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\pi
.

We have the following basic monotonicity property.

Lemma 3
.

For
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
,
suppose that
\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi
.
If
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
then
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
.
Also, if
\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta
then
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.

Proof.

If
\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta
then

\alpha
and
\beta
differ on some variable in
\pi
, and

\alpha
is better than
\beta

on the first such variable;
thus the same holds for
\pi^{\prime}
, since
\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi
,
and so
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
holds.
Similarly,
if
\beta\succ_{\pi}\alpha
then
\beta\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\alpha
,
which, using the fact that
\succ_{\pi}
and
\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}
are both weak orders,
is equivalent to:
if
\alpha\not\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
then
\alpha\not\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
,
which thus implies:
if
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
then
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
.

\Box

The result below is a key property of composition,
which will be important later.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
.
If
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
and
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

then
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
If
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
and
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

then
\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta

and
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi}\beta
.
Suppose now that
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
. Then

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

\iff

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
;
and

\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

\iff

\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.

Proof.

Since
\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi
extends
\pi^{\prime}
,
we have, by Lemma
3
,
that (i)
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

implies
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi}\beta
.
Similarly, (ii) if
\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta

then
\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
,
and thus also
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
We also have
(iii) if
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
and
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

then
\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
This is because

\alpha
and
\beta
differ on some variable in
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}
,
and the first such variable pair
(X,\geq)
appears in

\pi^{\prime}
; and
X
is the earliest variable in
\pi^{\prime}
that
\alpha

and
\beta
differ on, so
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
implies that

\alpha(X)>\beta(X)
and thus,
\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.

Suppose that
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
and
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
Then, by (i),
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}\circ\pi}\beta
.
If
\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta
then
\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
, by (ii).
So, we only need to deal with the case where
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
.
Then

\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
follows by (iii).

Suppose that
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
and
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
If
\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta
or
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
then, by the above arguments,
we have
\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
,
and thus also
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
So, it just remains to deal with the case where

\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
and
\alpha\equiv_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
But then
\alpha(V_{\pi})=\beta(V_{\pi})

and
\alpha(V_{\pi^{\prime}})=\beta(V_{\pi^{\prime}})

and so, because
V_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}=V_{\pi}\cup V_{\pi^{\prime}}
,
we have
\alpha(V_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}})=\beta(V_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}})
,
proving
\alpha\equiv_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta

and hence
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.

Assume now that
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
,
so that
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
and
\beta\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\alpha
.
By the earlier parts we have

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

\Rightarrow

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
;
and
\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

\Rightarrow

\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
Since
\beta\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\alpha
,
we also have if
\alpha\not\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
, i.e.,

\beta\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\alpha
, then
\beta\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\alpha
,
i.e.,
\alpha\not\succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
Similarly we have
\alpha\not\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
implying

\alpha\not\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.

\Box

3
Lexicographic Inference and Strongly Compositional Preference Statements

In this section we define
lexicographic inference
,
which can also be expressed in terms of (lexicographic) consistency.
We define a notion of
strong compositionality
,
and show that
this property
enables a greedy algorithm for checking consistency, and hence for preference inference.
More specifically, a preference statement
\varphi
is strongly compositional if
the composition of two lex models satisfies
\varphi
whenever the second and some extension of the first satisfy
\varphi
.

Throughout this section, we assume some language
{\cal L}
, and satisfaction relation
\models\,\subseteq{\cal G}\times{\cal L}
.
Here, the language
{\cal L}
is a set of preference statements and the relation
\models
describes when a lex model satisfies a statement.
For example, we can consider the simple language
{\cal L}^{O}=\{\alpha\geq\beta\mid\alpha,\beta\in\underline{V}\}
with the satisfaction relation
\pi\models\alpha\geq\beta\Leftrightarrow\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
as described in the previous section.
We extend the satisfaction relation to subsets
\Gamma
of
{\cal L}
in the usual way:
for
\Gamma\subseteq{\cal L}
,
define
\pi\models\Gamma

\iff

\pi\models\varphi
for each
\varphi\in\Gamma
.
If
\pi\models\Gamma
then we say that
\pi
satisfies
\Gamma
,
or
\pi
is a
[lexicographic] model
of
\Gamma

(and similarly, for a single preference statement
\varphi
).
We say that
\Gamma
is
consistent
,
if there exists
some
\pi\in{\cal G}
satisfying
\Gamma
;
otherwise,
\Gamma
is
inconsistent
.
We will sometimes use negations of preference statements:
when necessary we can always extend the language
{\cal L}

and the relation
\models
,
by defining
\pi\models\neg\varphi

\iff

\pi\not\models\varphi
, when
\varphi\in{\cal L}
.

A key problem is to determine if a set
\Gamma\subseteq{\cal L}
of preference statements is consistent.
We will derive methods for determining this in Section
3
.

Some Basic Preference Statements

For outcomes
\alpha,\beta\in\underline{V}
, we can define preference statements
\alpha\geq\beta
and
\alpha>\beta
,
where we define

\pi\models\alpha\geq\beta

\iff

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
,
and

\pi\models\alpha>\beta

\iff

\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta
.
We also define preference statement
\alpha\equiv\beta
, with

\pi\models\alpha\equiv\beta

\iff

\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
,
which is if and only if

\pi\models\alpha\geq\beta

and
\pi\models\beta\geq\alpha
.

We have that
\pi\models\alpha\geq\beta

\iff

\pi\not\models\beta>\alpha
,
i.e.,
\pi\models\neg(\beta>\alpha)
.
So
\alpha\geq\beta
and
\neg(\beta>\alpha)
are equivalent preference statements,
in that they are satisfied by exactly the same set of lex models.
Similarly,

\alpha>\beta
and
\neg(\beta\geq\alpha)
are equivalent preference statements.

Consider the example of lex models
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
and outcomes
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
as before.
Then
\pi\models\alpha\geq\beta
and thus
\pi\not\models\beta>\alpha
.
Also,
\pi^{\prime}\models\alpha\equiv\gamma
, i.e.,
\pi^{\prime}\models\alpha\geq\gamma
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\gamma\geq\alpha
.

Lexicographic Inference

We define the lexicographic inference relation

\Gamma\models\varphi

\iff

\pi\models\varphi
for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
such that
\pi\models\Gamma
.
For instance, we have, for
\Gamma\subseteq{\cal L}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\underline{V}
,

\Gamma\models\alpha\geq\beta

\iff

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
holds for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
such that
\pi\models\Gamma
.
Similarly,

\Gamma\models\alpha>\beta

\iff

\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta
holds for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
such that
\pi\models\Gamma
.

Lexicographic inference can be reduced to checking consistency,
since we have

\Gamma\models\varphi

\iff

\Gamma\cup{\{\neg\varphi\}}
is inconsistent;
and

\Gamma\models\neg\varphi

\iff

\Gamma\cup{\{\varphi\}}
is inconsistent.
Thus,

\Gamma\models\alpha\geq\beta

\iff

\Gamma\cup{\{\beta>\alpha\}}
is inconsistent;
and
\Gamma\models\alpha>\beta

\iff

\Gamma\cup{\{\beta\geq\alpha\}}
is inconsistent.

Consider flight connections as before.
Let
\Gamma
be the set of statements
\{\alpha>\beta,\beta\geq\gamma\}
with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
defined as in the previous section.
Then the lex model
\pi
satisfies
\Gamma
,
\pi\models\Gamma
, and thus
\Gamma
is consistent.
In fact, the only preference models that satisfy
\Gamma
are:

\pi=(\mathit{airline},\mathit{KLM}>\mathit{LAN}),(\mathit{time},\mathit{day}>\mathit{night})
,
\pi^{\prime\prime}=(\mathit{airline},\mathit{KLM}>\mathit{LAN}),(\mathit{class},\mathit{economy}>\mathit{business})
,
and any extension of either of these two models.
Thus for
\delta=(\mathit{LAN},\mathit{night},\mathit{business})
,

\Gamma\models\gamma>\delta
.

The Induced Relation
\models^{*}

and Maximal Models

From the relation
\models
we also define the derived relation
\models^{*}

as follows, where
\pi\in{\cal G}
and
\varphi\in{\cal L}
:

\pi\models^{*}\varphi
if and only if
there exists

Compositional and Strongly Compositional Preference Statements

We formulate a pair of properties of preference statements that
have strong implications regarding lexicographic inference and optimality.

Definition 3

((Strongly) Compositional)
.

Let
\varphi\in{\cal L}
.
We say that
\varphi
is
compositional
if
for all
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
,

\pi\models\varphi
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
implies
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
.

We say that

\varphi
is
strongly compositional
if
for all
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
,

\pi\models^{*}\varphi
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
implies
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
.

For
\Gamma\subseteq{\cal L}
,
we define
\Gamma
to be
compositional
if
every element of
\Gamma
is compositional.
Similarly, we say that
\Gamma
is
strongly compositional
if
every element of
\Gamma
is strongly compositional.

Note that if
\varphi\in{\cal L}
is inconsistent
(i.e., there exists no
\pi\in{\cal G}
with
\pi\models\varphi
)
then
\varphi
is trivially (strongly) compositional.

Instances of Strongly Compositional Statements

Although being strongly compositional might appear to be quite a restrictive assumption,
it turns out that it is satisfied by many natural preference statements, as illustrated by the next proposition.
First, we give a lemma which, roughly speaking, states that the property of being [strongly] compositional is closed under conjunction.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq{\cal L}
and let
\psi\in{\cal L}
.
Suppose that
\psi
is such that for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
,

\pi\models\psi

\iff

\pi\models\Gamma
.
If
\Gamma
is compositional then
\psi
is compositional.
If
\Gamma
is strongly compositional then
\psi
is strongly compositional

[
\pi\models^{*}\psi

\iff

\pi\models^{*}\Gamma
and
\Gamma
is consistent].

Proof.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is compositional.
Consider any
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
with
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\models\psi
.
Then,
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\models\Gamma

\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\Gamma

\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\psi

\psi
is compositional.

Now assume, for the remainder of the proof of this result, that
\Gamma
is strongly compositional.
First suppose also that
\pi\models^{*}\psi

Then there exists
\pi^{\prime\prime}

\pi^{\prime\prime}\sqsupseteq\pi

\Gamma

For the converse, we assume that

\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}

\pi^{\prime}\models\Gamma

\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\Gamma

We will finally show that
\psi

Consider any
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}

\Box

Equivalence w.r.t. Compositional Statements

The following result implies that
if
\Gamma
is compositional then
two maximal models of
\Gamma
involve exactly the same set of variables,
and if two outcomes are equivalent in one maximal model of
\Gamma

then they are equivalent in all models of
\Gamma
.

Proposition 2
.

Assume that
\Gamma\subseteq{\cal L}
is consistent and compositional.
Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be models of
\Gamma

with
\pi
a maximal model of
\Gamma
.
Then,
V_{\pi}\supseteq V_{\pi^{\prime}}
; and

V_{\pi}=V_{\pi^{\prime}}
if and only if
\pi^{\prime}
is a maximal model of
\Gamma
.
Also, if, for
\alpha,\beta\in\underline{V}
,
we have
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta

then we have
\alpha\equiv_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
,
and in fact
\Gamma\models\alpha\equiv\beta
.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be a maximal model of
\Gamma

and let
\pi^{\prime}
be any model of
\Gamma
.
Compositionality of
\Gamma
implies that

\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\Gamma
,
and thus,
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}=\pi
,
since
\pi
is a maximal model of
\Gamma
,
and
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\pi
.
Therefore,
V_{\pi}\cup V_{\pi^{\prime}}=V_{\pi}
,
and hence,
V_{\pi^{\prime}}\subseteq V_{\pi}
.
If
\pi^{\prime}
is a maximal model of
\Gamma
then the same argument implies that

V_{\pi^{\prime}}\supseteq V_{\pi}
and thus
V_{\pi^{\prime}}=V_{\pi}
.
Conversely, if
V_{\pi^{\prime}}=V_{\pi}
then
\pi^{\prime}
is a maximal model of
\Gamma
,
since otherwise
\pi^{\prime}
could be extended to a maximal model
\pi^{\prime\prime}
of
\Gamma
,
which would imply
V_{\pi^{\prime}}
being a strict subset of
V_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}=V_{\pi}=V_{\pi^{\prime}}
.

Assume that
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
.
Then
\alpha(V_{\pi})=\beta(V_{\pi})
,
and so
\alpha(V_{\pi^{\prime}})=\beta(V_{\pi^{\prime}})

and
\pi^{\prime}\models\alpha\equiv\beta
.
Since
\pi^{\prime}
is arbitrary,
we have
\Gamma\models\alpha\equiv\beta
.

\Box

.
Now suppose that
\Gamma\not\models\varphi
and let

\pi
be any maximal model of
\Gamma\cup{\{\neg\varphi\}}
.
Then,
\Gamma\models^{max}\varphi

\iff

\pi\not\models^{max}\Gamma
,
which holds if and only if
V^{\Gamma\cup{\{\neg\varphi\}}}\not=V^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

The definitions immediately imply that
if
\Gamma\models\varphi
then
\Gamma\models^{max}\varphi
.
Now assume that
\Gamma\not\models\varphi
,
and so
\Gamma\cup{\{\neg\varphi\}}
is consistent,
and let
\pi
be any maximal model of
\Gamma\cup{\{\neg\varphi\}}
.
Suppose that
\Gamma\models^{max}\varphi
.
The fact that
\pi\not\models\varphi
implies that
\pi\not\models^{max}\Gamma
.
Conversely, assume that
\Gamma\not\models^{max}\varphi

so there exists
\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
such that

\pi^{\prime}\models^{max}\Gamma
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi
,
and thus
\pi^{\prime}\models\Gamma\cup{\{\neg\varphi\}}
.
Proposition
2
implies
that
V_{\pi^{\prime}}\subseteq V_{\pi}
.
But since
\pi\models\Gamma
and
\pi^{\prime}\models^{max}\Gamma

we have also
V_{\pi}\subseteq V_{\pi^{\prime}}
and thus
V_{\pi^{\prime}}=V_{\pi}
.
Proposition
2
then implies that

\pi\models^{max}\Gamma
.

We have
V^{\Gamma\cup{\{\neg\varphi\}}}=V_{\pi}\subseteq V^{\Gamma}
.
Proposition
2
implies that

\pi\models^{max}\Gamma
if and only if
V_{\pi}=V^{\Gamma}
,
which is if and only if
V^{\Gamma\cup{\{\neg\varphi\}}}=V^{\Gamma}
.

\Box

Decreasing Preference Statements and Sets of Models

In this section we show some results that are useful in proving
that certain preference statements are strongly compositional.

Let us say that
\varphi\in{\cal L}
is
decreasing
if
for all
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
with
\pi^{\prime}
extending
\pi
,
we have
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi

\Rightarrow

\pi\models\varphi
.
It follows easily from the definitions that
if
\varphi
is decreasing, then, for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
,

\pi\models^{*}\varphi

\iff

\pi\models\varphi
.
This leads to the following result.

Lemma 14
.

Let
\varphi\in{\cal L}

be decreasing.
Then
\varphi
is strongly compositional if and only if
\varphi
is compositional.

For example, the non-strict statements of the flight connections
examples from the earlier sections are decreasing, while
the strict statements in these examples are not.

For
{\cal M}\subseteq{\cal G}
, we say that

{\cal M}
is
decreasing
if, for any
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}

such that
\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi
, we have

\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal M}

\Rightarrow

\pi\in{\cal M}
.

We say that
{\cal M}

contains all models of

\varphi

if, for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
,

\pi\models\varphi

\Rightarrow

{\cal M}\ni\pi
.

The following result is helpful for proving that
a preference statement
\varphi
is strongly compositional.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\varphi\in{\cal L}
, and assume that
\varphi
is consistent.
Let
{\cal M}_{\varphi}
be a subset of
{\cal G}
.
The following two conditions are equivalent:

(I)

{\cal M}_{\varphi}
is decreasing and contains all models of
\varphi
,
and
for all
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
, if
\pi\in{\cal M}_{\varphi}
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
then
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
.

(II)

\varphi
is strongly compositional, and
for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
,
\pi\models^{*}\varphi

\iff

\pi\in{\cal M}_{\varphi}
.

Proof.

(I)
\Rightarrow
(II):
Assume (I).
First, let us assume that
\pi\in{\cal M}_{\varphi}
.
Since
\varphi
is consistent, there exists
\pi^{\prime}
with
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
,
and so (I) implies that
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
,
and thus,
\pi\models^{*}\varphi
, since
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\pi
.
For proving the converse let us now assume that
\pi\models^{*}\varphi
,
so there exists
\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
with
\pi^{\prime}\sqsupseteq\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
.
Thus,
\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal M}_{\varphi}
, and because
{\cal M}_{\varphi}
is decreasing, we then have

\pi\in{\cal M}_{\varphi}
.
We have shown that for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
,
\pi\models^{*}\varphi

\iff

\pi\in{\cal M}_{\varphi}
.
(I) then also implies that
\varphi
is strongly compositional.

(II)
\Rightarrow
(I):
Lemma
5
(i) implies that

{\cal M}_{\varphi}
contains all models of
\varphi
,
and Lemma
5
(ii) implies that

{\cal M}_{\varphi}
is decreasing.
The fact that
\varphi
is strongly compositional then implies (I).

\Box

For
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in{\cal L}
,
we say that
\varphi^{\prime}

is a relaxation of
\varphi
if

\varphi\models\varphi^{\prime}
, i.e.,
for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
,

\pi\models\varphi

\Rightarrow

\pi\models\varphi^{\prime}
.

We have the following special case of Proposition
4
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\varphi,\overline{\varphi}\in{\cal L}
, and assume that
\varphi
is consistent.
The following two conditions are equivalent:

(I)

\overline{\varphi}
is a decreasing relaxation of
\varphi
such that
for all
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
, if
\pi\models\overline{\varphi}
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
then
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
.

(II)

\varphi
is strongly compositional, and
for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
,
\pi\models^{*}\varphi

\iff

\pi\models\overline{\varphi}
.

Proof.

Define
{\cal M}_{\varphi}
to be
all
\pi\in{\cal G}
such that
\pi\models\overline{\varphi}
.
Note that (I) holds if and only if

{\cal M}_{\varphi}
is decreasing and contains all models of
\varphi
,
and if
\pi\in{\cal M}_{\varphi}
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
then
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi
.
Proposition
4
implies that (I)
\iff
(II).

\Box

Important Instances of Strongly Compositional Preference Statements

Projections to
Y

Our computational techniques can be expressed in terms of projections of preference statements to a single variable.

Let
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
, let

Y\in V
be a variable, and let
A\subseteq V-{\{Y\}}
be a set of variables not containing
Y
.
Define
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow Y}
,
the projection of
\mathfrak{R}
to
Y
, to be
{\{(\alpha(Y),\beta(Y))\,:\,(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}\}}
.
Also, define,

\mathfrak{R}_{A}^{\downarrow Y}
, the
A
-restricted projection to
Y
,
to be the set of pairs
(\alpha(Y),\beta(Y))

such that
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
and
\alpha(A)=\beta(A)
.

\mathfrak{R}_{A}^{\downarrow Y}
is the projection to
Y
of pairs that agree on
A
.
Thus,
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow Y}=\mathfrak{R}_{\emptyset}^{\downarrow Y}
.

From
[
20
]
we have (a variation of the following):

Lemma 15
.

Consider any lexicographic model
\pi\in{\cal G}
, written as

(Y_{1},\geq_{1}),\ldots,(Y_{k},\geq_{k})
.
where each
Y_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,k
, is a variable in
V
,
and each
\geq_{Y_{i}}
is a total order on
\underline{Y_{i}}
.
For
i=1,\ldots,k
, define

A_{i}
to be the set of earlier variables than
Y_{i}
, i.e.,

A_{i}={\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i-1}\}}
.
Let

\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
.
Then

\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\supseteq\,\mathfrak{R}
if and only if
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
,
\geq_{i}\,\supseteq\mathfrak{R}_{A_{i}}^{\downarrow Y_{i}}
.

Note that, by Lemma
3
,

\alpha\geq\beta
is a decreasing element.
Lemma
4
implies that

\alpha\geq\beta
is compositional, and thus, strongly compositional,
by Lemma
14
.
If
\alpha>\beta
is inconsistent (so that
\alpha=\beta
)
then it is trivially strongly compositional.
Otherwise, we can use Proposition
5

with
\varphi=\alpha>\beta
and
\overline{\varphi}=\alpha\geq\beta

to show, using Lemma
4
, that

\alpha>\beta
is also strongly compositional
and
\pi\models^{*}\alpha>\beta

\iff

\pi\models\alpha\geq\beta
.
We therefore have:

Lemma 16
.

For any outcomes,
\alpha,\beta\in\underline{V}
,
statements
\alpha\geq\beta
and
\alpha>\beta

are strongly compositional,
and, if
\alpha\not=\beta
, for
\pi\in{\cal G}
,

\pi\models^{*}\alpha>\beta

\iff

\pi\models^{*}\alpha\geq\beta

\iff

\pi\models\alpha\geq\beta
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a subset of
\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
,
and let
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
be some statement satisfying:

\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
if and only if

\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\supseteq\mathfrak{R}
.

If
\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi
then
\succcurlyeq_{\pi}

\supseteq

\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}
,
which implies that

\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is decreasing.
For any lexicographic model
\pi
we have

\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
if and only if
for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
,

\pi\models\alpha\geq\beta
,
which implies that
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is strongly compositional,
by Lemmas
16
and
6
.
We therefore have:

Proposition 6
.

For any
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
,
the preference statement
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is strongly compositional,
and, for any
\pi\in{\cal G}
, we have
\pi\models^{*}\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}

\iff

\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}

We say that
\psi
is a
strict version of
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}

if
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is a relaxation of
\psi
,
and
\psi

satisfies the following monotonicity property regarding the
strict preferences among
\mathfrak{R}
:
for all
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
,
if
\pi\models\psi
and

\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
and
\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\supseteq(\succ_{\pi}\cap\mathfrak{R})

then
\pi^{\prime}\models\psi
.

There are many strict versions of
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
(unless
\mathfrak{R}
is very small).
We give two simple examples
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
of
strict versions of
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.
Let
\psi_{1}
be such that

\pi\models\psi_{1}
if and only if
\succ_{\pi}

\supseteq\mathfrak{R}
.
Let
\psi_{2}
be such that

\pi\models\psi_{2}
if and only if
\succcurlyeq_{\pi}

\supseteq\mathfrak{R}

and there exists some
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
such that

\alpha\succ_{\pi}\beta
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
,
and suppose that
\psi
is a strict version of
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.
Then
\psi
is strongly compositional, and for
\pi\in{\cal G}
,

\pi\models^{*}\psi

\iff

\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.

Proof.

We have that
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is a decreasing relaxation of
\psi
.
It is sufficient to prove that
for any
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
,
if
\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\psi
then
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\psi
,
since then we can use Proposition
5

to prove the result.
Assume that
\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\psi
.
Since
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is a relaxation of
\psi
,
we have
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
, and thus,

\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
,
since
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is compositional, by Proposition
6
.
Consider any
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
such that

\alpha\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
We also have
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta
, because
\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
,
and so,
\alpha\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
,
using Lemma
4
.
We have shown that
\succ_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}

\supseteq(\succ_{\pi^{\prime}}\cap\mathfrak{R})
,
which, since
\psi
is a strict version of
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
, implies
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\psi
, as required.

\Box

Negated Statements

Suppose that
\pi\models\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
, i.e.,
\pi\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
,
and consider any
\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
.
Then,
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}
extends or equals
\pi
,
which implies that
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
,
since
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is decreasing.
Thus, we have:

Proposition 8
.

Preference statement
\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is compositional
for any
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
,
and let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
be such that
\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi
.
Suppose that for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}

there exists
X\in V_{\pi}
such that
\alpha(X)\not=\beta(X)
.
Then, for any
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
,

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta

\iff

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
Also,

\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}

\iff

\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.

Proof.

Consider any
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
.
Let
Y
be the earliest variable in
V_{\pi}
such that

\alpha(Y)\not=\beta(Y)

(this is well-defined, by the hypothesis).
Then,
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta

\iff

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

\iff

\alpha(Y)>_{Y}\beta(Y)
, where

>_{Y}
is the
strict part of the
ordering for
Y
in
\pi
.
We then have
\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
if and only if
for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
,
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\beta

if and only if for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
,
\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

if and only if

\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.

\Box

Lemma 18
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
,
and let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal G}
.
Suppose that
for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
we have
\alpha(V_{\pi})=\beta(V_{\pi})
.
Then,
\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\cap\ \mathfrak{R}=\ \succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\cap\ \mathfrak{R}
,
and thus,

\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}\ \iff\ \pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.

Proof.

Consider any
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
.
We have
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
.
By Lemma
4
,

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\beta

\iff

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\beta
.
This implies
\succcurlyeq_{\pi^{\prime}}\cap\ \mathfrak{R}=\ \succcurlyeq_{\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}}\cap\ \mathfrak{R}
,
and
\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}\ \iff\ \pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.

\Box

Lemma 19
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
,
and let
\pi\in{\cal G}
.
Suppose that
X
is the first variable in
\pi
on which
some pair in
\mathfrak{R}
differs,
so that there exists
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
such that

\alpha(X)\not=\beta(X)
, and this does not hold for any earlier variable in
\pi
.
If
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is not acyclic then

\pi\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.

Proof.

Let
\geq_{X}
be the non-strict relation for
X
in
\pi
.
If
\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
then,
by Lemma
15
,

\geq_{X}
contains
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}

and thus, the latter is acyclic.

\Box

Define
{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
to be the set of models
\pi\in{\cal G}

such that either (i)
\pi\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
or
(ii) for every variable
X\in V_{\pi}
,
if
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is acyclic then
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}\subseteq\ =
.

Define
{\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
to be the set of models
\pi\in{\cal G}

such that either (i)
\pi\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
or
(ii) there is no variable
X\in V_{\pi}

such that
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is acyclic and irreflexive.

Note that
{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}\subseteq{\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
.

Lemma 20
.

If
\pi\in{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
and

\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
then
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\pi\in{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
and

\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.
First consider the case when
\pi\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.
The fact that
\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is decreasing implies that
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
,
i.e.,
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.
Now consider the other case, when
\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.
If for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
we have
\alpha(V_{\pi})=\beta(V_{\pi})

then Lemma
18
implies that
\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.
Otherwise, let
X
be the first variable in
\pi
on which
some pair in
\mathfrak{R}
differ.
The definition of
{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
implies that

\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is not acyclic, and thus,

\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
, by Lemma
19
.

\Box

Lemma 21
.

{\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
is decreasing.

Proof.

Suppose that
\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi

and that
\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
.
We need to show that
\pi\in{\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
.
We proceed using proof by contradiction.
Assume that
\pi\notin{\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
.
Thus,
\pi\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}

and there exists variable
X\in V_{\pi}

such that
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is acyclic and irreflexive.
Since
\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
and
V_{\pi^{\prime}}\supseteq V_{\pi}
,
we must have
\pi^{\prime}\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
.
Because
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is irreflexive,
for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{R}
,

\alpha
and
\beta
differ on variable
X
.
Lemma
17
leads to the required contradiction.

\Box

Definition 5
.

For
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}
,
we say that
\mathfrak{R}
is

simultaneously decisive
if
for all
X\in V
:
if
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is acyclic then either

\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is irreflexive or
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}\subseteq\ =
.

Proposition 9
.

If
\mathfrak{R}\subseteq\underline{V}\times\underline{V}

is simultaneously decisive then

\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
is strongly compositional,
and for all
\pi\in{\cal G}
,
\pi\models^{*}\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}

\iff

\pi\in{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
.

Proof.

We will first show that
{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}={\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
.
Suppose that
\pi\in{\cal M}^{\prime}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
.
If
\pi\not\models\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}
then we clearly have

,
so we can assume that there is no variable

such that
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}
is acyclic and irreflexive.
Thus, by our assumption on
\mathfrak{R}
,
if
\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}

\mathfrak{R}^{\downarrow X}\subseteq\ =
,
and thus,
\pi\in{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}
.

then implies that
{\cal M}_{\neg\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}}

.
Then, Lemma

and
Proposition

imply the result.

4
Preference Languages
{\cal L}_{pqT}
and
{\cal L}^{\prime}_{pqT}

Here we show that certain relatively expressive compact preference languages are strongly compositional.
This includes forms of the statements

\varphi^{\mathfrak{R}}

from Proposition
1
,
where
\mathfrak{R}
is a set of pairs of outcomes.
In many natural situations,
\mathfrak{R}
can be exponentially large;
in the languages discussed here,
we are able to express certain exponentially large sets
\mathfrak{R}
compactly.

The Language
{\cal L}_{pqT}

We will consider preference statements
of the
form
p\rhd q

\parallel

T
,
where

\rhd
is either
\geq
, or
\gg
or
>
, and

P
,
Q
and
T
are subsets of
V
,
with

(P\cup Q)\cap T=\emptyset
,
and
p\in\underline{P}
is an assignment to
P
,
and
q\in\underline{Q}
is an assignment to
Q
.
The statement
p\rhd q

\parallel

T

represents that

p
is preferred to
q
if
T
is held constant, i.e.,
any outcome
\alpha
extending
p
is preferred to
any outcome
\beta
that extends
q
and agrees with
\alpha
on variables
T
.
Formally, the semantics of this statement
relates to the set

\varphi^{*}
which is defined to be the set of
pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
of outcomes such that

\alpha
extends
p
, and
\beta
extends
q
,
and
\alpha
and
\beta
agree on
T
,
i.e.,
\alpha(T)=\beta(T)
.

Statements of the form
p\geq q

\parallel

T

are called
non-strict
;
statements of the form
p\gg q

\parallel

T
,
are called
fully strict
,
and statements of the form

p>q

\parallel

T
are called
weakly strict
.

Let

The following result characterises when

Proposition 11

\parallel

\pi\models\varphi^{(\geq)}
,

\pi\models\varphi^{(\geq)}

\varphi
is a fully strict statement.

\pi\models\varphi^{(\geq)}

Now suppose that

.

.

implies that

{\cal L}^{\prime}_{pqT}

Lemma 26
.

\pi

\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi

\varphi\in\Gamma\cap{\cal L}_{pqT}

,
and so
\varphi

\pi^{\prime}\models\neg\varphi

Optimality with Respect to Sets of Compositional Preference Statements

We consider a finite set of alternatives
{\cal A}\subseteq\underline{V}
,
and we assume that we have elicited a set
\Gamma

we would like to find the optimal alternatives among
{\cal A}
.
As we will see, there are several natural definitions of

[
9
,
22
]
.
We compare some of these in this section and analyse their computational cost in the context of lex models and compositional statements in the next section.

We define

\succcurlyeq_{\Gamma}
on outcomes by

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\Gamma}\beta

\iff

\Gamma\models\alpha\geq\beta
,
and we define equivalence relation
\equiv_{\Gamma}
by

\alpha\equiv_{\Gamma}\beta

\iff

\Gamma\models\alpha\equiv\beta
,
i.e., if
\alpha
and
\beta
are equivalent in all models of
\Gamma

Using a similar argument to that for Theorem

it can be shown that,

\Gamma

\Gamma\models\alpha\equiv\beta
holds
if and only if
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta
,
where
\pi
is an arbitrary maximal model of
\Gamma
.
We also define
\succ_{\Gamma}
to be the strict part of
\succcurlyeq_{\Gamma}
, so that

\beta\in{\cal A}

-equivalent to
\alpha

.

.
This holds if and only if for all
\beta\in{\cal A}
,
and for all
\pi
with
\pi\models\Gamma

\alpha\succcurlyeq_{\Gamma}\beta
.

We say that
\alpha
is
possibly optimal

written
\alpha\in{\rm PO}_{\Gamma}({\cal A})
,
if
\alpha
is optimal in some model of
\Gamma
,

.
Similarly, we say that

\alpha\in{\rm POM}_{\Gamma}({\cal A})

\alpha

\Gamma

\Gamma
.

\alpha

possibly strictly optimal

\alpha\in{\rm PSO}_{\Gamma}({\cal A})

if there exists
some
\pi\models\Gamma

\Gamma\models\alpha\equiv\beta
for all

\alpha

\Gamma

\alpha

and all other optimal elements are equivalent to

\alpha

\alpha\in{\rm O}_{\pi}({\cal A})

\Gamma\models\alpha\equiv\beta

\beta\in{\rm O}_{\pi}({\cal A})
.

For

.

\Gamma

We give some basic properties.

Lemma 28
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq{\cal L}
be a set of preference statements,
and let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be lex models,
and let
\alpha
be an element of set of alternatives
{\cal A}\subseteq\underline{V}

•

\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi

•

\iff

\iff

is consistent

\iff

•

\Gamma\cup\Delta^{\cal A}_{\alpha}\models\alpha\equiv\beta

\iff

\textrm{Opt}_{\Gamma}^{\cal A}(\beta)\supseteq\textrm{Opt}_{\Gamma}^{\cal A}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\pi^{\prime}
extends
\pi
.
Consider any
\alpha\in{\rm O}_{\pi^{\prime}}({\cal A})

so that, for all

.

We have:
\pi\models\Delta^{\cal A}_{\alpha}
if and only if for all

.

\pi\models\Gamma\cup\Delta^{\cal A}_{\alpha}

and
\alpha\in{\rm O}_{\pi}({\cal A})

if and only if

\alpha\in{\rm O}_{\pi}({\cal A})
.
By the second bullet point,

\Gamma\cup\Delta^{\cal A}_{\alpha}
is consistent.
This is also equivalent to

First suppose that

,
and consider any

Then,
\pi\models\Gamma\cup\Delta^{\cal A}_{\alpha}

\pi\models\alpha\equiv\beta
,
and so,

This implies that

Conversely, suppose that

and consider any
\pi

;
we then have

.
Then, by the third bullet point,

and so,

and thus
\alpha\equiv_{\pi}\beta

We have shown that

.

Evaluation:

In practice, it is often desired to compute relatively small sets of optimal solutions, e.g., not to overwhelm a user with too many choices.
Since
{\rm NO}_{\Gamma}
is usually empty,
computing
{\rm PSO}_{\Gamma}
or
{\rm PO}_{\Gamma}

may be the best choice.
This is further supported by the analysis of computational cost.
In the experiments, computing

{\rm PSO}_{\Gamma}
and
{\rm PO}_{\Gamma}
scales much better than
{\rm CSD}_{\Gamma}
,
with the number of statements and variables,
and is often faster than
{\rm NO}_{\Gamma}
.

7
Discussion

We have developed a new approach for preference inference based on lexicographic models, using a notion of strong compositionality that allows a greedy algorithm.
This is shown to be polynomial for relatively expressive preference languages.
We also examined different notions of optimality, and proved relationships between them.
Our experimental results confirm that the preference inference/consistency algorithm is fast,
and examined the problem of generating optimal solutions, where it was found that generating the sets of possibly optimal and possibly strictly optimal solutions were significantly faster than generating the undominated solutions.

There are other common forms of preference statement that are strongly compositional, and for which the greedy algorithm will enable checking consistency.
For instance, a restriction on the allowed value orderings of each variable is always strongly compositional.
This can include,
for instance,
highly disjunctive statements, for instance, structural properties of the value orderings, such as being single-peaked
[
7
]
.
Certain kinds of restrictions on variable orderings
are also strongly compositional.

More formally, regarding the value ordering restrictions,
for each variable
X\in V

let
{\cal P}_{X}
be a set of total orders on
\underline{X}
, and let

{\cal P}
be
{\{{\cal P}_{X}\,:\,X\in V\}}
.
We say that lex model
\pi
, equalling

(Y_{1},\geq_{Y_{1}}),\ldots,(Y_{k},\geq_{Y_{k}})
,
satisfies
{\cal P}
if and only if
each
\geq_{Y_{i}}
is in
{\cal P}_{Y_{i}}
.
The definitions immediately imply that

{\cal P}
is strongly compositional.

Lemma
6
showed that the property of being strongly compositional is (roughly speaking) preserved under conjunction.
Although this is far from being the case for disjunctions in general, some disjunctive statements are strongly compositional;
this includes the weakly strict statements in
{\cal L}_{pqT}
,
and restrictions on value orderings.

A lexicographic order can also be viewed as a static variable and value search ordering, for a constraint satisfaction or optimisation problem.
In a configuration problem, for instance, we may want to generate solutions in an ordering which fits in with what one knows about user preferences and the structure of the problem
[
11
]
.
We can thus take, as input, past user preferences between solutions, or even partial tuples;
or that all solutions of one constraint are preferred to all those satisfying another constraint.
There may be natural restrictions on the set of value orderings,
e.g., that there is a single peak or trough with a numerical domain.
Given such inputs, which are strongly compositional, one can apply our approach to see if there is a compatible lexicographic order, which can then be used to generate solutions in a best first order.

Our approach in Section
3
was rather abstract, in that no explicit structure on the language
{\cal L}
was assumed.
In fact, a closer examination of the proofs of some of the main results
suggests that these will hold more generally than for lexicographic orders:
essentially they just depend on basic properties of composition and extension, but not otherwise on the structure of models.
In particular, it is natural to look at the generalisation to conditional lexicographic orders, which are closely related to conditional preference languages
[
19
]
.
It would also be interesting to explore the potential connection with efficient classes of planning problems.

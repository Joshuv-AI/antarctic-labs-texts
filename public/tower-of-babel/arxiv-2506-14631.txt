1.
Introduction

We consider the fractional Kolmogorov operator formally given by

\Lambda_{\kappa}^{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}+\frac{\kappa}{|x|^{\alpha}}x\cdot\nabla,\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},

for
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(1,2)
with
\alpha<(d+2)/2
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. According to the terminology used
in
[
27
]
and
[
29
]
the gradient perturbation is called attractive when the coupling constant
\kappa
is positive and repulsive when it is negative.

Our objective in this paper is to establish
L^{p}
variational inequalities involving derivatives of the heat semigroup of operators
\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
generated by
-\Lambda_{\kappa}^{\alpha}
.

The vector field
\kappa|x|^{-d}x
has a stronger singularity than the ones of the Kato class. Then, the semigroup
\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
is not
L^{1}-L^{\infty}
ultracontractive, but it is ultracontractive as a mapping from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\varphi_{s}dx)
into
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
where

\varphi_{s}(y)=(s^{-1/\alpha}|y|\wedge 1)^{-d+\beta},\quad 0<t\leq s.

This fact is crucial in the proof of pointwise estimates for the integral kernel
T_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)
of
T_{t}^{\alpha}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
(see
[
30
]
). In contrast with other studies (see
[
2
,
5
,
10
,
12
,
19
,
26
,
38
,
43
]
, for instance), in this work we deal with nonlocal and nonsymmetric situation.

We consider the function

(1.1)

\Psi(\beta)=\frac{2^{\alpha}}{\beta-\alpha}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\beta-d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\beta-d}{2}\right)},\quad\beta\in(\alpha,d+\alpha).

The fraction of the four gamma functions in (
1.1
) achieves its maximum value in the point
\beta=(\alpha+d)/2
(see
[
19
]
). The function
\Psi
is not symmetric around the point
(\alpha+d)/2
(see
[
29
, p. 347]
and
[
30
, p. 1868]
for plots). It was proved in
[
11
, Lemma 1.1]
that
\Psi
is strictly decreasing on the interval
((d+\alpha)/2,d+\alpha)
.

We denote by
\kappa_{c}=\Psi((d+\alpha)/2)
. The value
\kappa_{c}
is critical in the following sense: for
\kappa<\kappa_{c}
the Hardy drift is a Rellich perturbation of
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
, i.e.

\left\|\frac{\kappa}{|x|^{-\alpha}}x\cdot\nabla(\xi+(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2})^{-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)}<1,\quad\kappa<\kappa_{c}\text{ and }\xi>0.

Then,
\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
is a contractive and holomorphic semigroup in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
when
\kappa<\kappa_{c}
(see
[
28
, §7]
).

Kinzebulatov, Semenov and Szczypkowski

[
28
,
29
,
30
]
proved that
\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
is contractive in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
and it extends by continuity to a
C_{0}
-semigroup in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)
when

, provided
\kappa<\kappa_{c}
.

Suppose that
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a family of operators defined on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},dx)

\rho>0

V_{\rho}(\{S_{t}\}_{t>0})(f)(x)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t_{k}<\dots<t_{1}<\infty\\
k\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left|S_{t_{j}}(f)(x)-S_{t_{j+1}}(f)(x)\right|^{\rho}\right)^{1/\rho}.

We are interested in

\rho

When the exponent

We assume throughout this paper that
\kappa<\kappa_{c}
. As it was mentioned,
\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0}
is a holomorphic and contractive semigroup in

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2\wedge(d+2)/2)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
{\rho>2}
. Suppose that

V_{\rho}(\{t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
and

For every

, we denote by

, for

and

, the kernel of the integral operator

1.1
to

\left|T_{t,\ell}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{C}{t^{d/\alpha}}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon}\left(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{|y|}\right)^{d-\beta},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t>0.

The constant

depends on
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\epsilon>0
.

2.
Weak type estimates for the variation and oscillation operators related to the fractional heat semigroup

As we announced, in this section we will prove the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then, the operators
V_{\rho}(\{t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
, for
{\rho>2}
, and
O(\{t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0},\{t_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}})
, where
\{t_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a decreasing sequence of real numbers such that
{\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}t_{j}=0}
, are both bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In order to prove this proposition, we use the vector-valued Calderón–Zygmund theory for singular integrals. If
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
E
is a Banach space, we denote by
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},E)
the
p
-Bochner–Lebesgue space. We first introduce some notation.

Suppose that
g:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. We define, for
\rho>0
,

\|g\|_{\rho}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}\\
n\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}|g(t_{j+1})-g(t_{j})|^{\rho}\right)^{1/\rho}.

It is clear that
\|g\|_{\rho}=0
if and only if
g
is a constant function. We define the space
E_{\rho}
that consists of those
g:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\|g\|_{\rho}<\infty
. By identifying each pair of functions that differ by a constant,
(E_{\rho},\|\cdot\|_{\rho})
is a Banach space.

If
g:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is derivable, for every
\rho\geq 1
it follows that

(2.1)

\|g\|_{\rho}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|g^{\prime}(t)|dt.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

We write

W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)f(y)dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t>0,

where

W_{t}^{\alpha}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta_{\alpha/2,t}(s)W_{s}(z)ds,\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t>0.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. According to
[
5
, Proposition 2.2]
we obtain, for every
0<\epsilon<1
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}^{\ell}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)||f(y)|dy\leq\frac{C}{t^{\ell+d/\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon}|f(y)|dy\leq Ct^{-(\ell+d/\alpha)}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
. Then, we get

\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)f(y)dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t>0.

From (
2.1
) we have that, for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

\|t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z)\|_{\rho}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{t}(t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z))|dt.

By using again
[
5
, Proposition 2.2]
it follows that, if
0<\epsilon<1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k-1}|\partial^{k}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z)|dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-d/\alpha-1}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|z|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon}dt\lesssim\frac{1}{|z|^{d}}.

for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,

(2.2)

\|t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z)\|_{\rho}\leq\frac{C_{\ell}}{|z|^{d}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

By proceeding in a similar way to
[
11
, Lemma 2.11]
, we obtain that

(2.3)

|\nabla_{1}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z)|\leq t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}-\ell-1}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|z|}\right)^{d+\alpha+1-\epsilon},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

therefore

\|\nabla_{1}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z)\|_{\rho}\leq\frac{C}{|z|^{d+1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

We define now the operator

\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H_{\ell}(x-y)f(y)dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

H_{\ell}(z):(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is given by
[H_{\ell}(z)](t)=t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z)
,
and the integral defining
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}
is understood in an
E_{\rho}
-Bochner sense.

Suppose that
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with compact support. According to (
2.2
) we have that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|H_{\ell}(x-y)\|_{\rho}|f(y)|dy<\infty,\quad x\notin\textup{supp}(f).

We are going to see that, for
x\notin\textup{supp}(f)
,

[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(f)(x)](t)=t^{k}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x),\quad\textup{a.e. }t\in(0,\infty).

Let
a
,
b\in(0,\infty)
,
a\neq b
. We define

M[g]=g(b)-g(a),\quad g\in E_{\rho}.

Thus,
M\in E^{\prime}_{\rho}
, the dual space of
E_{\rho}
. We have that

\displaystyle M[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(f)(x)]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}M[H_{\ell}(x-y)]f(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left.t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)\right|_{t=b}-\left.t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)\right|_{t=a}\right]f(y)dy

\displaystyle=\left.t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)\right|_{t=b}-\left.t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)\right|_{t=a}.

It follows that, for fixed
a\in(0,\infty)
,

[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(f)(x)](t)-t^{k}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)=[\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(f)(x)](a)-\left.t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)\right|_{t=a},\quad t>0.

Then,
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(f)(x)=\mathbb{L}_{\ell}(f)(x)
in
E_{\rho}
, where
[\mathbb{L}_{\ell}(f)(x)](t)=t^{\ell}\partial^{\ell}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)
,
t>0
.

The operator
\mathbb{L}_{\ell}
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},E_{\rho}\right)
, for every
1<p<\infty
. By (
2.2
) and (
2.3
),
t^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z)
is a
E_{\rho}
-Calderón–Zygmund kernel. Then, we conclude that
\mathbb{L}_{\ell}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{1,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},E_{p}\right)
, that is, the
\rho
-variation operator
V_{\rho}\left(\left\{t^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell}W_{t}^{\alpha}\right\}_{t>0}\right)
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

By proceeding in a similar way we can establish that the oscillation operator
O\left(\left\{t^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell}W_{t}^{\alpha}\right\}_{t>0},\{t_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\right)
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
(a)
for
\rho
-variation operators

In order to prove the
L^{p}
-boundedness properties for the
\rho
-variation operator

we consider the
\rho
-variation operators of the difference

Note that the next result holds for every

.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2\wedge(d+2)/2)
,
\beta\in((d+\alpha)/2,d+\alpha)
, and
\kappa=\Phi(\beta)
,

and
\rho\geq 1
. The
\rho
-variation operator

is bounded in

, for every

Proof.

By using (
2.1
) we have that

\begin{split}V_{\rho}&(\{t^{k}\partial^{k}_{t}(W_{t}^{\alpha}-T_{t}^{\alpha})\}_{t>0})(f)(x)\\
&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}\\
n\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left|\left.t^{k}\partial^{k}_{t}(W_{t}^{\alpha}-T_{t}^{\alpha})\}_{t>0})(f)(x)\right|_{t=t_{j+1}}-\left.t^{k}\partial^{k}_{t}(W_{t}^{\alpha}-T_{t}^{\alpha})\}_{t>0})(f)(x)\right|_{t=t_{j}}\right|^{\rho}\right)^{1/\rho}\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{t}\left(t^{k}\partial^{k}_{t}(W_{t}^{\alpha}-T_{t}^{\alpha})\}_{t>0})(f)(x)\right)\right|dt.\end{split}

Note that the last estimate holds for every
\rho\geq 1
(not only for
\rho>2
).

We define, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, with
\frac{d}{\beta}\vee 1<p<\infty
, the operator

\mathbb{T}_{\ell}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\alpha}}f(y)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|t^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell+1}\left(W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)-T_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right)\right|dtdy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1<p<\infty
. According to
[
11
, Proposition 2.8]
we obtain, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}^{k}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)||f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{k+d/\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon}\left(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{|y|}\right)^{d-\beta}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\right)^{(d+\alpha-\epsilon)p^{\prime}}\left(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{|y|}\right)^{(d-\beta)p^{\prime}}dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
. Notice that

-(d-\beta)p^{\prime}+d-1>-1\,\Longleftrightarrow\,(d-\beta)p<d(p-1)\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\beta p-d>0.

Also, we have

(d+\alpha-\epsilon)p^{\prime}-d>0
when
0<\epsilon<\alpha
. Hence, if
\epsilon>0
is small enough we can take derivatives with respect to
t
under the integral sign when
p>1\vee\frac{d}{\beta}
, to get

\partial_{t}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)f(y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{k}_{t}W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)f(y)dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then, Fubini’s theorem leads to

V_{\rho}(\{t^{k}\partial^{k}_{t}(W_{t}^{\alpha}-T_{t}^{\alpha})\}_{t>0})(f)(x)\leq k\mathbb{T}_{k-1}(|f|)(x)+\mathbb{T}_{k}(|f|)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Note that the first term in the right-hand side does not appear when
k=0
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Our next objective is to prove the
L^{p}
-boundedness properties for the operator
\mathbb{T}_{\ell}
. We decompose
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
into two regions that will be named the

and the
global part
. The local part
\Omega_{\textup{loc}}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
is defined by

\Omega_{\textup{loc}}=\left\{(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty):t\leq|y|^{\alpha}\text{ and }|x-y|\leq\frac{|x|\wedge|y|}{2}\right\}

and the global part
\Omega_{\textup{glob}}
is defined by
\Omega_{\textup{glob}}=(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty))\setminus\Omega_{\textup{loc}}
.

Duhamel’s formula allows us to write

\begin{split}T_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)-W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y)&=-k\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{t-s}^{\alpha}(x,z)|z|^{-d}z\cdot\nabla_{1}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z-y)dzds\\
&=-k\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{t-s}^{\alpha}(x,z)|z|^{-d}z\cdot\nabla_{1}W_{t}^{\alpha}(z-y)dzds\\
&\quad-k\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}T_{t}^{\alpha}(x,z)|z|^{-d}z\cdot\nabla_{1}W^{\alpha}_{t-s}(z-y)dzds,\end{split}

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
.

Then,

\begin{split}|\partial^{k}_{t}(T_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)-W_{t}^{\alpha}(x-y))|&\leq C\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}^{j}T_{t}^{\alpha}(x,z)||z|^{-d}|z\cdot\nabla_{1}\partial^{k-1-j}_{t}W^{\alpha}_{t/2}(z-y)|dz\right.\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial^{k}_{u}T_{u}^{\alpha}(x,z)|_{u=t-s}|z|^{-d}|z\cdot\nabla_{1}W^{\alpha}_{s}(z-y)|dzds\\
&\quad\left.+\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{s}^{\alpha}(x,z)||z|^{-d}|z\cdot\nabla_{1}\partial^{k}_{u}W^{\alpha}_{u}(z-y)|_{u=t-2}dzds\right)\\
&:=\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}K_{j}(t,x,y),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ and }t>0,\end{split}

where, for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle K_{j}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{t}^{j}T_{t/2}^{\alpha}(x,z)\right||z|^{-d}\left|z\cdot\nabla_{1}\partial_{t}^{k-1-j}W_{t/2}^{\alpha}(y-z)\right|dz,\quad j=0,\dots,k-1,

\displaystyle K_{k}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\partial_{u}^{k}T_{u}^{\alpha}(x,z)\right|_{u=t-s}|z|^{-d}\left|z\cdot\nabla_{1}W_{s}^{\alpha}(z-y)\right|dzds,

\displaystyle K_{k+1}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|T_{s}^{\alpha}(x,z)\right||z|^{-d}\left|\left.z\cdot\nabla_{1}\partial_{u}^{k}W_{u}^{\alpha}(z-y)\right|_{u=t-s}\right|dzds.

We consider, for every
j=0,\dots,k+1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{K}_{j,\textup{loc}}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{\Omega_{\textup{loc}}}(x,y,t)K_{j}(t,x,y)t^{k-1}dt,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{split}

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

[
11
, Proposition 2.9]
, we have that, for
every

,

(3.1)

\left|\partial_{t}^{j}T_{t/2}^{\alpha}(x,z)\right|\lesssim t^{-j-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{-d-\alpha+\epsilon}\left(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{|z|}\right)^{d-\beta},\quad x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t>0.

By proceeding as in the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 2.11]
,

(3.2)

\left|\nabla_{1}\partial_{t}^{k-1-j}W_{t/2}^{\alpha}(y-z)\right|\lesssim t^{-\frac{d+1}{\alpha}-(k-1-j)}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|z-y|}\right)^{d+\alpha+1+\epsilon},\quad y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t>0,

for
\epsilon>0
. The constants appearing in (
3.1
) and (

) depend on

.

By (
3.1
) and (

) we get

\displaystyle K_{j}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-(k-1)-\frac{2d+1}{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{|z|}\right)^{d-\beta}|z|^{1-d}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{-d-\alpha+\epsilon_{1}}

where

Note that, for a suitable positive function

By taking into account the last homogeneity property we are going to estimate

\begin{split}K_{j}(1,x,y)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1\wedge|z|)^{\beta-d}\left(\frac{1}{1+|x-y|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon_{1}}|z|^{1-\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{1+|z-x|}\right)^{d+\alpha+1-\epsilon_{2}}dzds\\

\begin{split}K_{k}(t,x,y)&\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(t-s)^{-(k+d/\alpha)}\left(\frac{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}}{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon_{1}}\left(1+\frac{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}}{|z|}\right)\\

\begin{split}t^{k}K_{k}(t,x,y)&\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(t-s)^{-d/\alpha}\left(\frac{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}}{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon_{1}}\left(1+\frac{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}}{|z|}\right)\\

\displaystyle K_{k+1}(t,x,y)

\begin{split}K_{k+1}(1,x,y)&\lesssim\int_{0}^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{s^{d/\alpha}}\left(1+\frac{s^{1/\alpha}}{|z|}\right)^{d-\beta}\left(\frac{s^{1/\alpha}}{s^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon_{1}}\\

&\quad\times|z|^{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{(1-s)^{\frac{d+1}{\alpha}}}\left(\frac{(1-s)^{1/\alpha}}{(1-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|}\right)^{d+\alpha+1-\epsilon_{2}}dzds,\end{split}

(3.3)

\mathbb{K}_{j,\textup{loc}}(x,y)\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{\Omega_{\textup{loc}}}(x,y,t)\left(\frac{|x|\vee|y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{t^{1/\alpha}}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\right)^{d+\alpha-\epsilon_{1}}\frac{dt}{t},

for

\mathbb{G}_{\ell}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{\Omega_{\textup{glob}}}(x,y,t)\left|t^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell+1}\left(W_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)-T_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right)\right|dt,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Using
[

-3

\left|t^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell+1}\left(W_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)-T_{t}^{\alpha}(x,y)\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t}L_{t}^{\alpha-\epsilon}(x,y),\quad(x,y,t)\in\Omega_{\textup{glob}},

\displaystyle=\chi_{\{|y|\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}(x,y)\left(\frac{|y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{\beta-d-(\alpha-\gamma)}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\right)^{d+\gamma}

Then,

provided that

Indeed, set

Therefore, it is proved that

, (3.3)]
we have

(8.4)

V_{2,I_{N}}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{2^{-\alpha t}}^{\alpha}(s)P_{s}(g_{N})ds\right\}_{t\in I_{N}}\right)\leq C_{0}e^{-\alpha N/2}\sqrt{N},

for
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in[0,1]^{d}
and some constant
C_{0}>0
.

On the other hand,

W_{t}^{\alpha}(g_{1,N})(x_{1})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{t}^{\alpha}(s)W_{s}(g_{1,N})(x_{1})ds,\quad x_{1}\in\mathbb{R},

so, for some constant
C_{1}>0
, by Lemma
8.3
we know that

(8.5)

\left|\left\{x_{1}\in[0,1]:V_{2,I_{N}}(\{W_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(g_{1,N})\}_{\ell\in I_{N}})(x_{1})>C_{1}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1.

From (
8
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x_{1}\in[0,1]:V_{2,I_{N}}(\{W_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(g_{1,N})\}_{\ell\in I_{N}})(x_{1})>C_{1}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\left|\left\{x\in[0,1]^{d}:V_{2,I_{N}}(\{W_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(g_{1,N})\}_{\ell\in I_{N}})(x_{1})>C_{1}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left|\left\{x\in[0,1]^{d}:V_{2,I_{N}}(\{W_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(g_{N})\}_{\ell\in I_{N}})(x)>\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\left\{x\in[0,1]^{d}:V_{2,I_{N}}\left(\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(s)P_{s}(g_{N})ds\right\}_{\ell\in I_{N}}\right)>\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|.

Choosing
N
large enough such that
C_{0}e^{-\alpha N/2}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{\log(\log(N))}
, the second term is zero from (
8.4
). Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x_{1}\in[0,1]:V_{2,I_{N}}(\{W_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(g_{1,N})\}_{\ell\in I_{N}})(x_{1})>C_{1}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{x\in[0,1]^{d}:V_{2,I_{N}}(\{W_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(g_{N})\}_{\ell\in I_{N}})(x)>\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|\leq 1,

and from (
8.5
), we deduce that

(8.6)

\left|\left\{x\in[0,1]^{d}:V_{2,I_{N}}(\{W_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(g_{N})\}_{\ell\in I_{N}})(x)>\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}1.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we clearly have

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in[0,1]^{d}:V_{2,I_{N}}(\{W_{2^{-\alpha\ell}}^{\alpha}(g_{N})\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}})(x)>\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:V_{2}(\{W_{t}^{\alpha}(g_{N})\}_{t>0})(x)>\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|.

Then, if
1<p<\infty
and we suppose that
V_{2}(\{W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists
M>0
such that

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:V_{2}(\{W_{t}^{\alpha}(g_{N})\}_{t>0})(x)>\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|\leq M\left(\frac{\|g_{N}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}}\right)^{p}

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

But, from (
8.1
),

\|g_{N}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\sqrt{N}
so

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:V_{2}(\{W_{t}^{\alpha}(g_{N})\}_{t>0})(x)>\frac{C_{1}}{2}\sqrt{N\log(\log N)}\right\}\right|\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

which contradicts (
8.6
). Therefore,
V_{2}(\{W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
is not bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
1<p<\infty
.

Since, as it was proved in Section
3
, the operator
V_{2}(\{T_{t}^{\alpha}-W_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
1\vee\frac{d}{\beta}<p<\infty
, we conclude that
V_{2}(\{T_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t>0})
is not bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
1\vee\frac{d}{\beta}<p<\infty
.

Thermodynamics Formalism for a Class of Hyperbolic Transcendental Meromorphic Functions
Hamid Naderiyan

Abstract
. This paper studies the thermodynamics formalism in the context of complex dynamics. We establish the thermodynamics formalism for the class of hyperbolic transcendental meromorphic functions of B–class where the poles have bounded multiplicities, the Nevanlinna order is finite, and infinity is not an asymptotic value. We showed the existence and uniqueness of the conformal measure and the invariant Gibbs measure that is equivalent to the conformal measure.



1.
Introduction

The field of thermodynamic formalism has a rich history in physics and mathematics. There have been extensive studies on this field not only in various setting, but also its relation with other fields such multifractal analysis, dimension theory, and etc. , see
[
1
, section 1.1]
for a short overview.

The motivation of the current work is a question posed by M. Urbański on the thermodynamic formalism for the transcendental meromorphic functions of B–class where the poles have bounded multiplicities and infinity is not an asymptotic value under the hyperbolicity condition. This class of functions gained attention at least recently by a work of W. Bergweiler & J. Kotus
[
2
]
where they found a sharp estimate for the Hausdorff dimension of the escaping set of such functions. We call this class of functions the BK–class, see definition
1
for the precise definition. In fact, for a function
f
of BK–class where all poles except finitely many have multiplicities bounded by
M
and
\rho
is the Nevanlinna order of
f
, it is shown by W. Bergweiler & J. Kotus that
\text{HD}(I_{f})\leq\frac{2M\rho}{2+M\rho}
, where
I_{f}
is the escaping set of
f
, i.e., the set of all
z\in\mathbb{C}
for which
|f^{n}(z)|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, see
[
2
]
. Note that the Julia set is the boundary of the escaping set as shown in
[
5
]
.

In this work, we adapt the approach of M. Urbański and V. Mayer in
[
9
]
by which they established thermodynamic formalism for a class of transcendental meromorphic function subject to a growth rate for the derivative. Many family of functions are subject to this growth rate condition such as exponential functions, sine functions and elliptic functions, for more details see
[
9
, p. 7]
. It is important to note that the assumed growth condition implies that the function is hyperbolic in the sense that the derivative of the iterate of function grows exponentially all over the Julia set. M. Urbański and V. Mayer in
[
9
]
first obtain a conformal measure for a tame potential that can be perceived as a perturbation of the geometric potential. Then they use it to construct a Gibbs measure that is also the equilibrium state. They also show the Bowen’s formula that the Hausdorff dimension of the radial Julia set is equal to the zero of the pressure function.

The context of conformal dynamics is another topic of research in dynamical systems and ergodic theory that can be traced back to the work of Patterson
[
10
]
where he studied the limit set of Fuchsian groups, and by D. Sullivan
[
12
]
where he studied a broader class of discrete groups. We also refer the readers to
[
6
]
for more details about the conformal measure and its relation with Gibbs measure.

The method of this paper for obtaining a conformal measure is the analysis of the transfer operator. First, we consider a potential function (equation (
11
)) associated to a transcendental meromorphic function
f
of BK–class (definition
1
) which is expressed in terms of a the derivative of
f
with respect to an appropriate Riemannian metric (equation (
6
)). Then we assign a transfer operator for this potential (equation (
12
)). One major step is establishing the boundedness of this operator (proposition
10
). The derivative of the function
f\in
BK at
z
has an estimate (inequality
8
) in terms of
f(z)
and
z
as long as the value is large enough. This estimate along Rippon-Stallard estimate (inequality (
9
)) under the assumption of hyperbolicity give rise to nice estimate all over the Julia set of
f
(lemma
6
). This estimate helps to show that the transfer operator is bounded. Another ingredient of this fact is the Borel’s theorem in Nevanlinna theory (estimate (
1
)). Later, we construct a family of measure that satisfies a tightness property (lemma
20
). Applying Prokhorov’s theorem we obtain the conformal measure for the function
f
, the central result of this paper (proposition
21
). By taking integral of some fixed point of the transfer operator against this conformal measure we obtain an invariant Gibbs measure that is equivalent to the conformal measure (equation (
16
)). The fact that the escaping set of
f
has measure
0
(lemma
30
) is used to show the uniqueness of the Gibbs measure that is also ergodic with support on the radial Julia set which is the main result of this work, see theorem
34
.

Note that Picard’s exceptional points are in the Fatou set for a topologically hyperbolic function since they are asymptotic values. Additionally, we want to emphasize that for a hyperbolic transcendental mermorphic function of B–class we have dist

and every

the map

such that

where

is analytic for each

\{f_{z}^{-n}\hskip 5.69054pt:\hskip 5.69054ptf_{z}^{-n}:D(w,2\delta)\to O_{z},\hskip 8.53581ptf_{z}^{-n}(w)=z\}.

Remark 3

Note that

on

by the identity theorem.

We also consider the Riemannian metrics

ds=\frac{|dz|}{1+|z|^{\tau}},\;\;\;\;\tau>0,

on the complex plane

A simple calculation gives the following lemma.

For every

(5)

|z|,|f(z)|\geq T>0,\hskip 14.22636ptz\in\mathcal{J}(f)

For every

Definition 7

(
Gibbs State
)
.

We say a Borel probability measure

(

)
.

We say a Borel probability measure

The main targets of this paper is establishing the existence and uniqueness of the Gibbs state and conformal measure. First, we establish the existence of a conformal measure. Then an integral against this conformal measure gives an invariant Gibbs state for

3.
Geometric Potential and Transfer Operator Properties

We define the geometric potential for each
t>0
,

\Phi_{t}:\mathcal{J}(f)\to\mathbb{R},

(11)

\Phi_{t}(z):=t\log|f^{\prime}(z)|^{-1}_{\tau}=t\log\big|(f_{z}^{-1})^{\prime}\big(f(z)\big)\big|_{\tau};

and the transfer operator,

\mathcal{L}_{t}:C_{b}(\mathcal{J}(f),\mathbb{C})\to C_{b}(\mathcal{J}(f),\mathbb{C}),

(12)

\mathcal{L}_{t}\varphi(w):=\sum_{f(z)=w}\exp(\Phi_{t}(z))\varphi(z)=\sum_{f(z)=w}|f^{\prime}(z)|_{\tau}^{-t}\varphi(z)=\sum_{f(z)=w}\big|(f_{z}^{-1})^{\prime}(w)\big|_{\tau}^{t}\varphi(z),

where
C_{b}(\mathcal{J}(f),\mathbb{C})
is the space of complex-valued bounded continuous functions on
\mathcal{J}(f)
and
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathcal{J}(f),\mathbb{C})
. We show below
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is a bounded operator under certain conditions. Note that
\mathbb{1}_{X}
denotes the characteristic function of
X
, where
X\subseteq\mathcal{J}(f)
. When
X=\mathcal{J}(f)
we drop the subscript and we simply write
\mathbb{1}
instead. Also, we denote the supremum norm by
\|.\|
, i.e.
\|\varphi\|=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{J}(f)}|\varphi(z)|
.

Lemma 9
.

There exists
c>0
such that for every
w\in\mathcal{J}(f)
,

|\mathcal{L}_{t}\varphi(w)|\leq c^{t}|w|^{-(1+\frac{1}{M}-\tau)t}\|\varphi\|\sum_{f(z)=w}\dfrac{1}{|z|^{(\tau-1)t}}.

Proof.

Lemma
6
gives the following estimate,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{t}\varphi(w)|=\left|\sum_{f(z)=w}|f^{\prime}(z)|_{\tau}^{-t}\varphi(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq c^{t}|w|^{-(1+\frac{1}{M}-\tau)t}\|\varphi\|\sum_{f(z)=w}\dfrac{1}{|z|^{(\tau-1)t}}.

∎

Before proving the following proposition we remind the readers that the upper bound

was given in (

exists on the Julia set. In fact, when

and

, for every

we have,

Proposition 10

The transfer operator

is a bounded operator on

Due to the fact that

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Phi_{t}(f^{i}(z))=\Phi_{t}(z)+\Phi_{t}(f(z))+...+\Phi_{t}(f^{n-1}(z))

\displaystyle=-t\log|f^{\prime}(z)|_{\tau}-t\log|f^{\prime}(f(z))|_{\tau}-t\log|f^{\prime}(f^{2}(z))|_{\tau}-...-t\log|f^{\prime}(f^{n-1}(z))|_{\tau}

This follows from Koebe’s distortion theorem

Lemma 12

There exists
K>1
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{J}(f)
with
|w_{1}-w_{2}|<\delta
and every
z\in f^{-n}(\{w_{1}\})
, we have

\left|(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{1})-(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{2})\right|\leq K|(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{1})||w_{1}-w_{2}|.

Proof.

We use the mean value theorem and lemma
11
to find,

\left|(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{1})-(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{2})\right|\leq(1+K\delta)|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})||w_{1}-w_{2}|.

Then Rippon-Stallard expansion estimate (
10
) yields,

\displaystyle\left|(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{1})-(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq(1+K\delta)c_{1}^{-1}K_{1}^{-n}\dfrac{|(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{1})|}{|w_{1}|}|w_{1}-w_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq(1+K\delta)c_{1}^{-1}K_{1}^{-n}T^{-1}|(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{1})||w_{1}-w_{2}|.

∎

Lemma 13
.

There exists
K>1
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{J}(f)
with
|w_{1}-w_{2}|<\delta
and
every
z\in f^{-n}(\{w_{1}\})
, we have

\displaystyle\big|S_{n}\Phi_{t}\left(f^{-n}_{z}(w_{1})\right)-S_{n}\Phi_{t}\left(f^{-n}_{z}(w_{2})\right)\big|

\displaystyle=\left|t\log\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})\right|_{\tau}-t\log\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{2})\right|_{\tau}\right|

\displaystyle\leq tK|w_{1}-w_{2}|.

Proof.

We use lemmas
11
and
12
with the corresponding constants
K_{2},K_{3}>1
and the fact that
\log(x+1)\leq x
for
x>-1
to write,

\displaystyle\left|t\log\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})\right|_{\tau}-t\log\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{2})\right|_{\tau}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|t\log\left|\dfrac{(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})}{(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{2})}\right|+t\tau\log\left|\dfrac{w_{1}}{w_{2}}\right|-t\tau\log\left|\dfrac{(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{1})}{(f_{z}^{-n})(w_{2})}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;t\log\left(1+K_{2}|w_{1}-w_{2}|\right)+t\tau\log\left(1+\dfrac{|w_{1}-w_{2}|}{T}\right)+t\tau\log\left(1+K_{3}|w_{1}-w_{2}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;tK_{2}|w_{1}-w_{2}|+t\tau T^{-1}|w_{1}-w_{2}|+t\tau K_{3}|w_{1}-w_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;t(K_{2}+\tau T^{-1}+\tau K_{3})|w_{1}-w_{2}|.

∎

Lemma 14
.

There exists
K>1
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{J}(f)
with
|w_{1}-w_{2}|<\delta
and
every
z\in f^{-n}(\{w_{1}\})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\exp\left(S_{n}\Phi_{t}\left(f^{-n}_{z}(w_{1})\right)\right)-\exp\left(S_{n}\Phi_{t}\left(f^{-n}_{z}(w_{2})\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})\right|_{\tau}^{t}-\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{2})\right|_{\tau}^{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq te^{tK}K\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})\right|_{\tau}^{t}|w_{1}-w_{2}|.

Proof.

It is enough to use lemma
13
along with the fact
|e^{x}-e^{y}|\leq e^{|x-y|}e^{x}|x-y|
.
∎

Lemma 15
.

There exists
K>1
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{J}(f)
with
|w_{1}-w_{2}|<\delta
we have

\left|\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{1})-\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{2})\right|\leq te^{tK}K\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{1})|w_{1}-w_{2}|

Proof.

With regards to remark
3
and lemma
14
we can write,

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{1})-\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{2})\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w_{1}}\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})\right|_{\tau}^{t}-\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w_{2}}\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{2})\right|_{\tau}^{t}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w_{1}}\left(\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})\right|_{\tau}^{t}-\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{2})\right|_{\tau}^{t}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w_{1}}\left|\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})\right|_{\tau}^{t}-\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{2})\right|_{\tau}^{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w_{1}}te^{tK}K\left|(f_{z}^{-n})^{\prime}(w_{1})\right|_{\tau}^{t}|w_{1}-w_{2}|

\displaystyle=te^{tK}K\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{1})|w_{1}-w_{2}|.

∎

It is clear from this lemma that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{J}(f)
with
|w_{1}-w_{2}|<\delta
,

\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{2})\leq(1+te^{tK}K\delta)\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{1}).

This shows the map,

P_{t}:\;\mathcal{J}(f)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

w\mapsto\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w),

is locally constant. In fact, this map is constant by the following lemma.

Lemma 16
.

There exist
c>0
such that for every
R>0
, there exist
K_{R}>1
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{J}(f)\cap D(0,R)
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{2})\leq cK_{R}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{1}).

Proof.

First, we mention the following blow-up property of the Julia set shown in
[
9
, p. 18]
. Given
R>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
w_{2}\in\mathcal{J}(f)\cap D(0,R)
, we have
\mathcal{J}(f)\cap D(0,R)\subseteq f^{N}\left(D(w_{2},\delta)\right)
. Since all Picard’s exceptional points are asymptotic values and therefore they lie in the Fatou set of
f
, one can find
w_{3}\in D(w_{2},\delta)
such that
w_{1}=f^{N}(w_{3})
. Since
\mathcal{J}(f)
is bounded away from the origin and
f^{N}
is analytic on
\mathcal{J}(f)
, there exists
K_{R}
such that
|(f^{N})^{\prime}(w_{3})|^{t}_{\tau}\leq K_{R}^{t}
for all
w_{3}\in\mathcal{J}(f)\cap D(0,R+\delta)
. Hence, we use lemma
16
to find,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{2})

\displaystyle\leq(1+te^{tK}K\delta)\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{3})

\displaystyle=(1+te^{tK}K\delta)\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w_{3}}\left|(f^{n})^{\prime}(z)\right|_{\tau}^{-t}

\displaystyle=(1+te^{tK}K\delta)\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w_{3}}\left|(f^{n+N})^{\prime}(z)\right|_{\tau}^{-t}\left|(f^{N})^{\prime}(f^{n}(z))\right|_{\tau}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq(1+te^{tK}K\delta)\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w_{3}}\left|(f^{n+N})^{\prime}(z)\right|_{\tau}^{-t}K_{R}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq(1+te^{tK}K\delta)K_{R}^{t}\sum_{f^{n+N}(z)=w_{1}}\left|(f^{n+N})^{\prime}(z)\right|_{\tau}^{-t}

\displaystyle=(1+te^{tK}K\delta)K_{R}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{1})

∎

4.
Construction of Conformal Measure

Definition 17
.

The pressure of the potential
\Phi_{t}
is defined by

P_{t}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w),\hskip 28.45274ptw\in\mathcal{J}(f).

We emphasize that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the adjoint operator
\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\right)^{*}
takes every finite measure on
\mathcal{J}(f)
to another finite measure. In fact, for every finite (complex) measure
m
, every
g\in C_{b}(\mathcal{J}(f),\mathbb{C})
and every Borel set
A\subseteq\mathcal{J}(f)
, we have the following functional equations

\left[\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\right)^{*}m\right](g)=\int\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}gdm,\hskip 42.67912pt\left[\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\right)^{*}m\right](A)=\int\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}_{A}dm.

Proposition 18
.

Given a constant
c
and a
ce^{-\Phi_{t}}-
conformal measure
m
on
\mathcal{J}(f)
. The following conditions are equivalent,

(a)
m(f^{n}(A))=c^{n}\int_{A}\exp(-S_{n}\Phi_{t})\text{d}m
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every Borel
A\subseteq\mathcal{J}(f)
where
f^{n}
is injective on
A
.

(b)
m(f(A))=c\int_{A}\exp(-\Phi_{t})\text{d}m
, for every Borel
A\subseteq\mathcal{J}(f)
where
f
is injective on
A
.

(c)
\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}m=cm

(d)
\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\right)^{*}m=c^{n}m
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

It is enough to show
(a)\implies(b)\implies(c)\implies(d)\implies(a).
All implications are obvious, except for two of them. The argument for
(b)\implies(c)
up to some modifications is very similar to the argument presented in lemma 13.6.13 in
[
14
, p. 465]
. We skip repeating it here. For
(d)\implies(a)
we observe that

\displaystyle c^{n}\int_{A}\exp(-S_{n}\Phi_{t})dm_{t}=m_{t}\left(c^{n}\exp(-S_{n}\Phi_{t})\mathbb{1}_{A}\right)

\displaystyle=c^{-n}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\right)^{*}m_{t}\left(c^{n}\exp(-S_{n}\Phi_{t})\mathbb{1}_{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\right)^{*}m_{t}\left(\exp(-S_{n}\Phi_{t})\mathbb{1}_{A}\right)=\int\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\left(\exp(-S_{n}\Phi_{t})\mathbb{1}_{A}\right)dm_{t}

\displaystyle=\int\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w}\left(\exp\left(S_{n}\Phi_{t}(z)\right)\exp\left(-S_{n}\Phi_{t}(z)\right)\mathbb{1}_{A}(z)\right)dm_{t}(w)

\displaystyle=\int\sum_{f^{n}(z)=w}\mathbb{1}_{A}(z)dm_{t}(w)=\int\mathbb{1}_{f^{n}(A)}dm_{t}=m_{t}(f^{n}(A)).

∎

We show below that a conformal measure exists for each
f\in
BK. We start by introducing the following series for a fixed
w_{0}\in\mathcal{J}(f)
,

S_{0}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-ns}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp(\log\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})-ns).

Note that
S_{0}
converges for
s>P_{t}
and it diverges for
s<P_{t}
. When
s=P_{t}
, we have two cases. In either case, one can find a sequence of positive real numbers
\{b_{n}\}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{b_{n+1}}=1
and the modified series

S=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\exp(\log\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})-ns),

converges when
s>P_{t}
and it diverges when
s\leq P_{t}
as shown in
[
7
, p. 356]
. Additionally, it is not hard to see that
S
is a decreasing function of
s
on the interval
(P_{t},\infty)
.

Now for every
s>P_{t}
we define a measure

\nu_{s}=\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}},

which turns out to be a probability measure using the above functional equations. We remind readers that
B(R)=\{z\;:\;|z|>R\}\cup\{\infty\}
and we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 19
.

There exist
r_{t},c_{t}>0
such that for every
w\in\mathcal{J}(f)
and every
R>1
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}_{B}(w)\leq\frac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}
, where
B=B(R)\cap\mathcal{J}(f)
.

Proof.

Due to the fact that
|w|\geq T>0
for all
w\in\mathcal{J}(f)
and lemma
6
we find,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}_{B}(w)=\sum_{f(z)=w}\left|(f_{z}^{-1})^{\prime}(w)\right|_{\tau}^{t}\mathbb{1}_{B}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f(z)=w\\
|z|>R\end{subarray}}\left|(f_{z}^{-1})^{\prime}(w)\right|_{\tau}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f(z)=w\\
|z|>R\end{subarray}}c^{t}\dfrac{|w|^{-(1+\frac{1}{M}-\tau)t}}{|z|^{(\tau-1)t}}

\displaystyle\leq c^{t}T^{-(1+\frac{1}{M}-\tau)t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f(z)=w\\
|z|>R\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|z|^{(\tau-1)t}}.

Since
t>\frac{\rho}{\tau-1}
, we can choose
\alpha
such that
\rho<\alpha<(\tau-1)t
, then for
|z|>R
we have

|z|^{(\tau-1)t}=|z|^{\alpha}|z|^{(\tau-1)t-\alpha}\geq|z|^{\alpha}R^{(\tau-1)t-\alpha}\geq|z|^{\alpha}R^{(\tau-1)t-\rho}.

Therefore, the above estimate can be continued with,

\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}_{B}(w)\leq c^{t}T^{-(1+\frac{1}{M}-\tau)t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f(z)=w\\
|z|>R\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|z|^{(\tau-1)t}}\leq c^{t}T^{-(1+\frac{1}{M}-\tau)t}\dfrac{1}{R^{(\tau-1)t-\rho}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f(z)=w\\
|z|>R\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|z|^{\alpha}}.

It is enough to consider
r_{t}=(\tau-1)t-\rho
and
c_{t}=c^{t}T^{-(1+\frac{1}{M}-\tau)t}M_{\alpha}
.
∎

This helps us to show that the farther we get from the origin, the smaller the density of
\nu_{s}
.

Lemma 20
.

There exist
r_{t},c_{t}>0
such that for every
R>1
and every
s>P_{t}
we have

\nu_{s}\left(B(R)\cap\mathcal{J}(f)\right)\leq\frac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}.

Proof.

First, note that for a Borel set
A\subseteq\mathcal{J}(f)
we have,

\displaystyle\nu_{s}\left(A\right)=\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\left[\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}}\right]\left(A\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\int\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}_{A}d\delta_{w_{0}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}_{A}(w_{0})

This implies

\dfrac{1}{S}b_{1}e^{-s}\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})\leq\nu_{s}(\mathcal{J}(f))=1.

Now we set
B=B(R)\cap\mathcal{J}(f)
, then by the above estimate and lemma
19
we can write

\displaystyle\nu_{s}\left(B\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{S}b_{1}e^{-s}\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}_{B}(w_{0})+\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}_{B}\right)(w_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})}\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}_{B}(w_{0})+\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\sum_{f^{n-1}(z)=w_{0}}|(f^{n-1})^{\prime}(z)|_{\tau}^{-t}\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}_{B}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})}\dfrac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}+\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\frac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}\sum_{f^{n-1}(z)=w_{0}}|(f^{n-1})^{\prime}(z)|_{\tau}^{-t}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})}\dfrac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}+\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\frac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n-1}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})}\dfrac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}+\frac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}\dfrac{e^{-s}}{S}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{b_{n-1}}b_{n-1}e^{-(n-1)s}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n-1}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})

\displaystyle\asymp\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})}\dfrac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}+\frac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}\dfrac{e^{-s}}{S}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n-1}e^{-(n-1)s}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n-1}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})}\dfrac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}+\frac{c_{t}}{R^{r_{t}}}e^{-s}\nu_{s}(\mathcal{J}(f))\leq\dfrac{1}{R^{r_{t}}}\left(\dfrac{c_{t}}{\mathcal{L}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w_{0})}+c_{t}e^{-P_{t}}\right).

∎

Proposition 21
.

There exists a
e^{P_{t}}e^{-\Phi_{t}}
-conformal measure
m_{t}
for
f
.

Proof.

Given a sequence of numbers
\{s_{j}\}
such that
s_{j}>P_{t}
and
s_{j}\to P_{t}
, there exists a probability measure
m_{t}
on
\mathcal{J}(f)
such that a subsequence of
\{\nu_{s_{j}}\}
converges weakly to
m_{t}
by Prokhorov theorem, see theorem 8.6.2, Vol II
[
3
, p. 202]
. Given any
K>1
, there is
N
such that for any
n>N

K^{-1}\leq\dfrac{b_{n}}{b_{n+1}}\leq K.

Without loss of the results we proved so far we can assume
b_{1}=b_{2}=...=b_{N}=1
. Therefore, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

K^{-1}\leq\dfrac{b_{n}}{b_{n+1}}\leq K.

By the equation,

\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}\nu_{s}=\dfrac{1}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-ns}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n+1}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}}=\dfrac{e^{s}}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{b_{n+1}}b_{n+1}e^{-(n+1)s}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n+1}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}},

we find the following estimate

K^{-1}\dfrac{e^{s}}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n+1}e^{-(n+1)s}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n+1}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}}\leq\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}\nu_{s}\leq K\dfrac{e^{s}}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n+1}e^{-(n+1)s}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{n+1}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}}.

Now by adding the term
b_{1}e^{-s}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}}
to the sum, we find

K^{-1}e^{s}\left(\nu_{s}-\frac{1}{S}b_{1}e^{-s}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}}\right)\leq\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}\nu_{s}\leq Ke^{s}\left(\nu_{s}-\frac{1}{S}b_{1}e^{-s}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}\delta_{w_{0}}\right).

Now taking the limit over the obtained subsequence of
s_{j}
, we obtain

K^{-1}e^{P_{t}}m_{t}\leq\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}m_{t}\leq Ke^{P_{t}}m_{t}.

Since
K>1
was arbitrary, we deduce
\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}\right)^{*}m_{t}=e^{P_{t}}m_{t}.
Thuse, proposition
18
finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Construction of Invariant Gibbs State

We introduce the normalized transfer operator

\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}:C_{b}(\mathcal{J}(f),\mathbb{C})\to C_{b}(\mathcal{J}(f),\mathbb{C})

\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}=e^{-P_{t}}\mathcal{L}_{t}.

Lemma 22
.

There is
L>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}\|\leq L.

Proof.

First, from the lemma
9
it follows

\lim_{w\to\infty}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}\varphi(w)=0,\hskip 28.45274pt\varphi\in C_{b}\left(\mathcal{J}(f),\mathbb{C}\right).

This implies
\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}\|=\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n-1}\mathbb{1}\|=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w_{n})
for some
w_{n}\in\mathcal{J}(f)
.
We choose
R>0
such that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}\mathbb{1}(w)\leq 1
when
|w|\geq R
.
By lemma
16
there exist
c>0,\;K_{R}>1
such that for every
w\in B=\mathcal{J}(f)\cap D(0,R)
, we have

(15)

m_{t}(B)c^{-1}K_{R}^{-t}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w)\leq\int_{B}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}dm_{t}\leq\int\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}dm_{t}=1,

where the last equality holds due to
\left(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}\right)^{*}m_{t}=m_{t}
as shown in proposition
21
. For
L=\max\left(1,cK_{R}^{t}\frac{1}{m_{t}(B)}\right)
, we then have
\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}\mathbb{1}\|\leq L
. Given
\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{k}\mathbb{1}\|\leq L
. If
|w_{k+1}|\geq R
, then

\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{k+1}\mathbb{1}\|=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{k+1}\mathbb{1}(w_{k+1})=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}\left(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{k}\mathbb{1}\right)(w_{k+1})\leq\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{k}\mathbb{1}\|\leq L.

Otherwise, we have
w_{k+1}\in B
and the estimate (
15
) also yields
\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{k+1}\mathbb{1}\|\leq L.
Therefore by induction, the proof is completed.

∎

Lemma 23
.

For every
R>0
, there exists
l_{R}>0
such that for every
w\in\mathcal{J}(f)\cap D(0,R)
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
l_{R}\leq\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}(w)
.

Proof.

By lemma
22
we know there exists
L>0
such that
\|\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}\|\leq L
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Following lemma
20
we can choose
R>0
such that
m_{t}(B(R))\leq\frac{1}{4L}
. Therefore,

1=\int\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}dm_{t}=\int_{D(0,R)}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}dm_{t}+\int_{B(R)}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}dm_{t}\leq\int_{D(0,R)}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{n}\mathbb{1}dm_{t}+\frac{1}{4}.

Hence, there should be
w_{n}\in\mathcal{J}(f)\cap D(0,R)

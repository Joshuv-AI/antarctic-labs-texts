Simple connectedness of the Ran space
Jānis Lazovskis

Abstract
. The space of all finite non-empty subsets of a topological space X X , also known as the Ran space of X X , is weakly contractible for X X path connected. We consider subspaces Ran ⩽ n ​ ( X ) \textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X) of the Ran space given by all subsets of X X of size at most n n , and present results on their first homotopy groups. In particular, we show that the induced map π 1 ​ ( Ran ⩽ n ​ ( X ) ) → π 1 ​ ( Ran ⩽ n + 2 ​ ( X ) ) \pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X))\to\pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n+2}(X)) is trivial for all positive integers n n , and even more, show that π 1 ​ ( Ran ⩽ n ​ ( X ) ) = 0 \pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X))=0 for all n ⩾ 4 n\geqslant 4 , by explicitly drawing the path homotopies that contract any loop to a point.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a topological space.
Finite subsets of
X
, also known as configurations, and the spaces they define, appear in various fields of mathematics
[
4
]
, from braid groups
[
2
]
to higher algebra
[
9
]
and factorization homology
[
1
]
, as they provide a local way to encode the geometry and topology of a space.
Withholding the choice of topology for a moment, the set of all finite subsets of
X
of size
n
is known as the
configuration space

\textup{Conf}_{n}(X)
of
X
, and the collection of all configuration spaces for positive
n
is known as the
Ran space

\textup{Ran}(X)
.
Restricting to configurations of size at most
n
, we get the space
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
, which we also call the
Ran space
, when the difference is clear from context.
The empty set in
X
as a configuration of size 0, together with
\textup{Ran}(X)
defines the
exponential

\textup{Exp}(X)
of
X
.
The naming of and symbols for these spaces are not universal, and variations with different decorations abound, also by further considering based topological spaces.

In this note, we are concerned with
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
and
\pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X))
.

X
(left), one in a darker color the other in white, with a neighborhood emphasized around the element in a darker color. A loop
S^{1}\to\textup{Conf}_{2}(X)
composed of two nearby loops
S^{1}\to X
(center left). A loop
S^{1}\to\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant 2}(X)

(t=0)
to a lighter shade
(t=1)
.

We take the topology on
\textup{Ran}(X)
and
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
to be the coarsest topology (that is, having as few open sets as possible) for which, given any finite collection
\{U_{i}\subseteq X\}_{i}
of nonempty, pairwise disjoint open sets
U_{i}
, the set
\{
finite
U\subseteq X\ :\ U\subseteq\bigcup_{i}U_{i},\ U\cap U_{i}\neq\emptyset\ \forall\ i\}
is open.
This definition follows
[
9
]
, and corresponds with the metric induced by the Hausdorff metric on subsets when
X
has a metric topology.
The intuition, presented in
Figure

1
, is that given an element
x\in\textup{Ran}(X)
, some other
y\in\textup{Ran}(X)
is in a “small neighborhood” of
x
whenever each small neighborhood (in the topology on
X
) of each point of
x
contains at least one point of
y
.
A more detailed discussion of common topologies on spaces of configurations is presented in
[
3
]
.

1.1.
Related work

Among others, Beilinson and Drinfeld
[
1
, Section 3.4.1]
show that all the homotopy groups of
\textup{Ran}(X)
are trivial when
X
is path-connected.
Their proof does not provide an explicit homotopy from
S^{d}\to\textup{Ran}(X)
to the trivial morphism
*\to\textup{Ran}(X)
, and their argument on
\textup{Ran}(X)
can not be directly extended to
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
.
That is, the argument
[
1
,
3
]
for triviality relies on the composition

(1)

\textup{Ran}(X)\xrightarrow{\ \triangle\ }\textup{Ran}(X)\times\textup{Ran}(X)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{7.0pt}{\rotatebox{180.0}{$\triangle$}}\ }\textup{Ran}(X)

being the identity, where
\triangle(x)=(x,x)
is the diagonal map and
\raisebox{7.0pt}{\rotatebox{180.0}{$\triangle$}}(x,x)=``x\cup x"
is the codiagonal map that takes elements in Ran spaces to the element corresponding to their set union, when both are considered as subsets of
X
.
Conversely, on the Ran spaces
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
, the codiagonal map has target
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant 2n}(X)
, so the analogous composition from
Equation

1
becomes

(2)

\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)\xrightarrow{\ \triangle\ }\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)\times\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{7.0pt}{\rotatebox{180.0}{$\triangle$}}\ }\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant 2n}(X),

and the composition is the inclusion from
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
into
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant 2n}(X)
, which is not the identity map.

Such inclusions are further considered by others, in particular Handel
[
6
]
, who applies the composition of
Equation

2
to prove that the maps
\pi_{k}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X))\rightarrow\pi_{k}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant 2n-1}(X))
induced by inclusions are trivial, for all integers
k\geqslant 1
and all
n\geqslant 0
.
Similarly, Félix and Tanré
[
5
]
consider the inclusions
X\to\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
, which, for
n\geqslant 3
, and are shown to be homotopically trivial, though the authors restrict to a particular class of CW-complexes.

When working with CW-complexes, in particular ones with a basepoint, the results of Tuffley
[
11
]
give that
\pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X))=0
for all
n\geqslant 3
, using special covers of
X
and the van Kampen theorem.
An alternative proof is given by Kallel and Sjerve
[
7
]
, by obtaining
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
as a pushout from the symmetric product of
X
and the fat diagonal of
X
(the subspace of the symmetric product containing elements with at least one repeated entry).
These approaches rely on machinery for CW-complexes and do not construct homotopies for contracting loops in a space.
It should be noted that the presence of a basepoint is more for the choice of maps, rather than a categorical restriction.

1.2.
Contribution

Our contributions are visual and constructive arguments for results about the homotopy groups
\pi_{1}
of
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
, without requiring
X
to be a CW-complex.
Our
Theorem

6
extends the claims of
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
5
, Theorem 1]
to a larger class of maps, asserting the triviality of
\pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X))\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n+2}(X))
.
This result relies on an explicit construction presented in
Figure

3
.
Our
Theorem

8
extends the claims of the Lemma of
[
1
, Section 3.4.1]
from
\textup{Ran}(X)
to
\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
, and
[
11
, Theorem 1]
,
[
7
, Corollary 2.2]
from CW-complexes to arbitrary topological spaces, and provides an explicit construction of path homotopies, to prove that
\pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X))
is trivial for all
n\geqslant 4
.

2.
Preliminary constructions

Let
X
be a path connected topological space with finite first homotopy group.
The 1-dimensional circle
S^{1}
is considered as the quotient space
[0,1]/(0\sim 1)
throughout.
For positive integers
m\leqslant n
, we denote by

the natural inclusion of Ran spaces.
Let
\iota\colon X\hookrightarrow\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
be the map that takes an element

\{x\}
.
By functoriality, we get a map
\iota_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}(\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X))

By an abuse of notation, we also denote by
\iota
the natural inclusion of any finite nonempty subset of

of size at most
n

\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
.
Conversely, we consider any element

as a subset

without any change in notation.

Observation 1
.

Every continuous
S^{1}\to X^{n}
defines a map
S^{1}\to\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
by composing with
\iota
, but not every map
S^{1}\to\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
factors through
X^{n}
.
This is, first, due to monodromy, as we may need to consider intervals with two basepoints instead of loops with one basepoint.
Second, we may need to account for “branch points”, which may be arranged in pathological ways so that
n
images of
S^{1}
in
X
may not suffice for all of them.

Definition 2

Let
\sigma\colon S^{1}\to\textup{Ran}(X)
be continuous, and

The point

of

Analogously,

if for every open neighborhood

of

As

is invariant under change of basepoint, and its classes are invariant under reparametrization, every continuous
\sigma\colon S^{1}\to\textup{Ran}_{\leqslant n}(X)
does indeed factor through

, up to homotopy.
That is, for any chosen basepoint

, we always have a path from

to

, and any loop can be reparametrized to have at most one branch point and one merge point.
We state this claim with a description of the homotopy.

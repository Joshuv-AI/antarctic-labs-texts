NSVZ-Compatible Three-Loop Gauge β \beta -Functions and Regulator-Driven Scheme Structure in Supersymmetric Theories with Exponential Higher Covariant Derivative Regularization
Swapnil Kumar Singh ID 1

Abstract
We study the three-loop gauge β \beta -functions in general 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 supersymmetric gauge theories regularized by higher covariant derivatives (HCD) supplemented with Pauli–Villars subtraction. The all-structure three-loop form is known in the HCD framework (e.g. Kazantsev2020 ; Haneychuk2022 ; Haneychuk2025 ) and involves regulator-dependent parameters. Here we evaluate these parameters explicitly for the exponential regulators R ⁡ ( x ) = e x n R(x)=e^{x^{n}} and F ⁡ ( x ) = e x m F(x)=e^{x^{m}} . We obtain the constants A ⁡ ( n ) A(n) and B ⁡ ( m ) B(m) in closed form, together with their large- n , m n,m asymptotics, and substitute them into the general three-loop expressions. This yields fully explicit, regulator-parameterized β \beta -functions and a systematic expansion in 1 / n 1/n and 1 / m 1/m that cleanly organizes finite, scheme-dependent terms. We then exhibit finite coupling redefinitions that map the renormalized DR ¯ \overline{\mathrm{DR}} result to an NSVZ-compatible scheme. Our analysis clarifies how exponential higher-derivative regulators preserve the NSVZ relation at the bare level and illustrates the regulator-driven structure of supersymmetric RG flows. Keywords: Three-loop gauge β \beta -functions, higher covariant derivative regularization, exponential regulators, scheme dependence, supersymmetric renormalization.



I
Introduction

The study of renormalization group (RG) functions in
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric gauge theories is central to understanding both perturbative and non-perturbative aspects of quantum field theory. In particular, the gauge
\beta
-functions, which determine the scale dependence of gauge couplings, serve as a bridge between high-energy unification, low-energy phenomenology, and the internal consistency of supersymmetric effective field theories
Ellis1991
;
Amaldi1991
;
Langacker1991
. Precision knowledge of these functions is indispensable for testing supersymmetric extensions of the Standard Model, constraining scenarios of Grand Unified Theories (GUTs), and analyzing dualities in strongly coupled regimes.

At the one- and two-loop levels the gauge
\beta
-functions are well established
Jones1983
;
Novikov1986
. These results form the foundation for phenomenological applications, including the classic demonstration that gauge couplings unify in the Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model (MSSM) near
10^{16}\,\text{GeV}

Ellis1991
;
Amaldi1991
;
Langacker1991
. While three-loop corrections are numerically smaller, they are essential for achieving percent-level precision in unification fits, refining proton decay predictions, and improving constraints on the superpartner spectrum
Jack2005
;
Kazakov1999
. It is important to emphasize that higher-loop effects do not shift the unification scale down to the
\mathcal{O}(1
–
10)\,\text{TeV}
range, but instead correct the matching conditions at the conventional GUT scale. Thresholds at the TeV scale are associated with superpartner masses, and their effects must be carefully separated from genuine high-scale contributions in phenomenological analyses.

A remarkable feature of supersymmetric gauge theories is the Novikov–Shifman–Vainshtein–Zakharov (NSVZ) relation
Novikov1983
;
Shifman1986
, which provides an exact all-order formula for the gauge
\beta
-function in terms of group invariants and anomalous dimensions of matter fields. Originally derived using holomorphy, instanton calculus, and anomaly arguments
Shifman1996
;
Seiberg1994
;
ArkaniHamed1997
, the NSVZ relation was later confirmed diagrammatically in supersymmetry-preserving schemes
Stepanyantz2020
. Its canonical form reads

\begin{split}\frac{\beta_{K}(\alpha,\lambda)}{\alpha_{K}^{2}}&=-\,\frac{1}{2\pi\left(1-\tfrac{C_{2}(G_{K})\,\alpha_{K}}{2\pi}\right)}\\
&\quad\times\left[3\,C_{2}(G_{K})-\sum_{a}T_{aK}\left(1-\gamma_{a}{}^{a}(\alpha,\lambda)\right)\right],\end{split}

(1)

where
C_{2}(G_{K})
is the quadratic Casimir of the gauge group
G_{K}
,
T_{aK}
denotes the Dynkin index of the chiral multiplet
\Phi_{a}
, and
\gamma_{a}{}^{a}
is its anomalous dimension. Equation (
1
) encapsulates the holomorphic structure of supersymmetric RG flows and highlights the deep connection between supersymmetry, anomalies, and renormalization.

Whether the NSVZ relation is preserved in explicit multi-loop calculations depends crucially on the choice of regularization and subtraction scheme. Dimensional reduction (
\overline{\text{DR}}
), although widely used in phenomenology, does not preserve the NSVZ form beyond two loops without finite redefinitions of couplings
Jack1996_1
;
Jack1996_2
. By contrast, the higher covariant derivative (HCD) regularization introduced by Slavnov
Slavnov1971
;
Slavnov1972
has proven to be especially powerful. In this framework, higher-derivative operators suppress ultraviolet divergences, while residual one-loop divergences are canceled using Pauli–Villars (PV) superfields
Slavnov1977
;
Kataev2013
. This method preserves both gauge invariance and supersymmetry, and when RG functions are defined in terms of bare couplings, the NSVZ relation holds exactly
Stepanyantz2020
.

Recent advances have pushed these results to the three-loop level for general
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric theories with semi-simple gauge groups, Yukawa couplings, and HCD regularization
Kazantsev2020
;
Haneychuk2022
;
Haneychuk2025
. These computations demonstrate explicit consistency with the NSVZ relation and introduce two regulator-dependent constants,
A
and
B
, defined by

A=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\,\frac{1}{R(x)},\qquad B=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\,\frac{1}{F(x)^{2}},

(2)

where
R(x)
and
F(x)
are regulator functions for the gauge and matter sectors, respectively. These constants encode finite, scheme-dependent contributions to higher-loop RG functions. Their explicit evaluation is therefore indispensable for connecting formal multi-loop results with physical predictions.

The focus of the present paper is to compute
A
and
B
explicitly for the family of exponential regulators

R(x)=e^{x^{n}},\qquad F(x)=e^{x^{m}},\qquad n,m\geqslant 2,

(3)

which provide strong ultraviolet suppression and analytic control. We demonstrate that

A(n)=\frac{\gamma_{E}}{n},\qquad B(m)=\frac{\gamma_{E}+\ln 2}{m},

(4)

where
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant
WhittakerWatson
. Substituting these values into the three-loop results of
Kazantsev2020
;
Haneychuk2022
;
Haneychuk2025
yields fully explicit
\beta
-functions parameterized by
(n,m)
, enabling a detailed study of scheme dependence and the role of finite coupling redefinitions in mapping between the
\overline{\text{DR}}
and NSVZ schemes
Siegel1979
;
Jack1996_1
;
Jack1996_2
.

Finally, we note that explicit regulator-dependent structures are not merely technical artifacts: they illustrate how supersymmetric RG flows interpolate between different subtraction schemes, preserving NSVZ invariance under appropriate finite redefinitions. Moreover, they provide a natural starting point for connections to resurgent trans-series, anomaly matching, and holomorphic properties of supersymmetric gauge theories
Aniceto2021
;
Dunne2016
;
Marino2008
;
Anselmi1998
. Thus, evaluating
A(n)
and
B(m)
explicitly enriches our understanding of the interplay between exact RG structures, regularization, and scheme dependence in supersymmetric quantum field theory.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we review the HCD setup, define notation, and recall the general structure of the three-loop
\beta
-function. In Sec.
III
we evaluate
A(n)
and
B(m)
for exponential regulators, providing exact results and asymptotics. In Sec.
IV
we substitute these constants into the known three-loop formulas and analyze scheme dependence. In Sec.
IV.7
we compare explicitly with the compact general expression of Haneychuk
Haneychuk2025
. Finally, Sec.
VI
summarizes our results and discusses implications for NSVZ compatibility, scheme dependence, and the analytic structure of supersymmetric RG flows.

Bare and renormalized chiral fields are related by a (generally non–diagonal)

\displaystyle{\gamma_{a}}^{b}(\alpha_{0},\lambda_{0})

In the higher covariant derivative (HCD) framework, finite regulator–dependent

that modify the quadratic actions

;
Slavnov1972
;

;
Kataev2013
;

In compact three–loop expressions derived within HCD for semi–simple gauge

;
Haneychuk2025

and

them explicitly for a given regulator family is therefore the starting point
for concrete three–loop
\beta
–functions and for organizing scheme

III.1
Definitions and convergence

The constants
A
and
B
are defined by the logarithmically–weighted
derivatives of the reciprocal regulator functions,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\;\equiv\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dx\,\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{1}{R(x)}\right),

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\;\equiv\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dx\,\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{1}{F(x)^{2}}\right),

(23)

with
R(0)=F(0)=1
and rapid growth as
x\to\infty
(admissibility).
For admissible
R,F
, the large–
x
tails are exponentially damped so that
the integrals converge in the UV. Near
x\to 0
the
\ln x
weight can
expose would–be
\int dx/x
structures; however, once one substitutes the
explicit exponential regulators, the
x\to 0
behavior is integrable and the
integrals can be evaluated in closed form by elementary changes of variables.
(As a cross–check, one may also recover the same finite parts by Mellin
analytic continuation; see, e.g.,
WhittakerWatson
.)

III.2
Mellin–regularized building block (cross–check)

A frequently used identity is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}\,e^{-x^{p}}

\displaystyle\;\overset{\text{Mellin}}{=}\;\lim_{s\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}e^{-x^{p}}dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{p}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{\gamma_{E}}{p},

(24)

where
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. We will not need
(
III.2
) directly in the derivations below (elementary
changes of variables suffice), but it provides a convenient analytic
continuation check of the finite parts we obtain.

III.3
Evaluation of
A(n)
for exponential gauge regulators

Consider the gauge–sector exponential family

R(x)=e^{x^{n}},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},\;n\geq 2.

(25)

Then
1/R(x)=e^{-x^{n}}
and
d(1/R)/dx=-nx^{n-1}e^{-x^{n}}
. From
(
III.1
),

\displaystyle A(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(e^{-x^{n}}\right)dx=-\,n\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n-1}\ln x\,e^{-x^{n}}\,dx.

(26)

With the change of variables

(so

and

\ln x=\tfrac{1}{n}\ln t
),

\displaystyle A(n)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{n}\ln t\Bigr)e^{-t}\,dt=\frac{\gamma_{E}}{n},

(27)

using
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\ln t\,dt=-\gamma_{E}

WhittakerWatson
. The integrand is integrable at both endpoints
for
n\geq 2
, so no boundary terms arise.

III.4
Evaluation of
B(m)
for exponential matter regulators

For the matter–sector exponential family

F(x)=e^{x^{m}},\qquad m\in\mathbb{N},\;m\geq 2,

(28)

one has
d(1/F^{2})/dx=d(e^{-2x^{m}})/dx=-2mx^{m-1}e^{-2x^{m}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle B(m)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(e^{-2x^{m}}\right)dx=-\,2m\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{m-1}\ln x\,e^{-2x^{m}}\,dx.

(29)

Set
u=2x^{m}
(so
du=2mx^{m-1}dx
and
x=(u/2)^{1/m}
, therefore

\ln x=\tfrac{1}{m}(\ln u-\ln 2)
). Then

\displaystyle B(m)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{m}\,\ln u-\tfrac{\ln 2}{m}\Bigr)e^{-u}\,du=\frac{1}{m}\left(\gamma_{E}+\ln 2\right),

(30)

again using
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}\ln u\,du=-\gamma_{E}
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}du=1

. The result is finite
and requires no further subtractions.

III.5
Scaled exponentials and a useful generalization

For completeness, consider the scaled family
R(x)=e^{cx^{p}}
and

F(x)=e^{cx^{p}}
with
c>0
,
p\geq 2
. A short variant of the above

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}e^{-cx^{p}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)\,c^{-s/p}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\mathrm{FP}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}\,e^{-cx^{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0}\left[\frac{1}{p}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)\,c^{-s/p}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\lim_{s\to 0}\left[\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{s}{p}\ln c+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\left(-\gamma_{E}-\ln c\right)=-\frac{1}{p}\left(\gamma_{E}+\ln c\right)

IV.1
Framework, Regulators, and PV Masses

We consider a semi-simple gauge group

We employ higher covariant derivative (HCD) regularization supplemented by Pauli–Villars (PV)
superfields. The regulator functions are chosen to be exponential:

\displaystyle R(x)

\displaystyle=e^{x^{n}},\qquad F(x)=e^{x^{m}},

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{p^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}},\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{N},\quad n,m\geq 2.

\displaystyle R(0)

\displaystyle=F(0)=1,

\displaystyle R(x),\,F(x)

\displaystyle>0\quad\text{and monotone for }x\geq 0,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}R(x),\,F(x)

\displaystyle=+\infty\quad\text{(sufficient UV growth)}.

(42)

regularization parameters

–

Bare couplings defined with higher covariant derivative (HCD) regularization satisfy the NSVZ relation

Let
\alpha_{K}\equiv g_{K}^{2}/(4\pi)

Introduce a finite, non-singular map

\alpha^{\prime}_{K}\;=\;\alpha_{K}+\delta\alpha_{K}(\alpha,\lambda),

(58)

\delta\alpha_{K}
regular at the origin and expandable as a formal series. Under
Eq. (
58

\beta

(59)

\delta\alpha_{K}
such that

A.1
Mellin regularization of the master integral

A recurring object is the logarithmically divergent integral

\mathcal{I}_{p}\;\equiv\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}\,e^{-x^{p}}\,,

(71)

which we define by analytic continuation. Introduce a complex regulator

\mathcal{I}_{p}(s)\;\equiv\;\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}e^{-x^{p}}\,dx\;=\;\frac{1}{p}\,\Gamma\!\Bigl(\frac{s}{p}\Bigr)\,,\qquad\mbox{Re}\,(s)>0\,.

(72)

Expanding near
s=0

\Gamma(\varepsilon)\;=\;\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\gamma_{E}\;+\;\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\,\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})\,,

(73)

we obtain

\mathcal{I}_{p}(s)\;=\;\frac{1}{s}-\frac{\gamma_{E}}{p}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12p^{2}}\,s+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})\,,

(74)

so that the finite part is

\mathrm{FP}\,\mathcal{I}_{p}\;=\;\lim_{s\to 0}\!\left(\mathcal{I}_{p}(s)-\frac{1}{s}\right)\;=\;-\,\frac{\gamma_{E}}{p}\,.

(75)

This Mellin-regularized prescription makes the finite pieces entering

explicit

A.2
Evaluation of
A(n)
for
R(x)=e^{x^{n}}

The gauge-sector constant is defined by

A\;\equiv\;\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{1}{R(x)}\right)\!,\qquad R(x)=e^{x^{n}}\,.

(76)

Thus,

\displaystyle A(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\Bigl(e^{-x^{n}}\Bigr)\,dx=-\,n\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n-1}\ln x\,e^{-x^{n}}\,dx

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\,t=x^{n}\,}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln t\,e^{-t}\,dt=\gamma_{E}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\boxed{\,A(n)=\dfrac{\gamma_{E}}{n}\,}\,,

(77)

where integrability at the endpoints (
n\geq 2

A.3
Evaluation of
B(m)
for

The matter-sector constant is

B\;\equiv\;\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{1}{F(x)^{2}}\right)\!,\qquad F(x)=e^{x^{m}}\,,

\displaystyle B(m)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln x\,\frac{d}{dx}\!\Bigl(e^{-2x^{m}}\Bigr)\,dx=-\,2m\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{m-1}\ln x\,e^{-2x^{m}}\,dx

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\,u=2x^{m}\,}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{u}\,(\ln u-\ln 2)\,du=\gamma_{E}+\ln 2

\displaystyle\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\boxed{\,B(m)=\dfrac{\gamma_{E}+\ln 2}{m}\,}\,.

Each term in the intermediate line is separately divergent; their Mellin-regularized combination is finite

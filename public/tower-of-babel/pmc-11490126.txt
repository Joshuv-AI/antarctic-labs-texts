The impact of mindfulness on academic achievement of students with the mediating role of adaptability: a structural equation modeling approach
Authors: Shima Bordbar, Parvin Ahmadinejad, Jamshid Bahmaei, Ali Reza Yusefi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionExamining the factors influencing students’ academic achievement can lead to improved educational planning and enhanced teaching and learning outcomes. The aim of this study was to investigate the impact of mindfulness on academic achievement with the mediating role of adaptability among students at Jiroft University of Medical Sciences in southern Iran in 2024.MethodsThis descriptive-analytical cross-sectional study was conducted on 290 students from various fields at Jiroft University of Medical Sciences in southern Iran from January to April 2024. To select these 290 individuals, a stratified sampling proportional to size was first carried out based on the year of university entry and field of study. After the sample size was determined for each entry year and field of study, students were randomly selected using their student numbers and a random number table. The data collection instruments were the standard mindfulness, academic achievement, and adaptability questionnaires. Descriptive tests and structural equation modeling using the Partial Least Squares (PLS) method were employed for data analysis, utilizing Smart PLS3 and SPSS26 software.ResultsThe findings indicate that students’ mindfulness positively affects their academic achievement (p < 0.001, β = 0.59). Additionally, mindfulness positively influences students’ adaptability (p < 0.001, β = 0.65). The results also show adaptability positively impacts students’ academic achievement (p = 0.001, β = 0.57). Finally, students’ mindfulness has a significant positive effect on their academic achievement through the mediating role of adaptability (p < 0.001).DiscussionAccording to the results, a favorable state of mindfulness and adaptability among students can improve academic achievement. Therefore, it is recommended that university administrators facilitate the enhancement of students’ mindfulness and adaptability by organizing relevant courses and workshops. This approach can contribute to their academic progress.Keywords: Mindfulness, Academic achievement, Adaptability, Student, Iran

Introduction

Success or failure in academic achievement is among the most critical concerns for the higher education system and universities [1]. Academic achievement in this system reflects its success in goal-setting and addressing individual needs. Therefore, a higher education system and university are considered efficient and successful when students’ academic achievement shows the highest possible growth across different periods [2]. Academic achievement is an educational parameter that is considered the most important factor for social and individual success [3]. As such, it is the primary concern of professors, university education officials, and students’ families [4]. On the contrary, academic decline is considered the opposite of academic progress. It has a negative impact on an individual’s future and imposes significant costs on both the family and society [5].

It appears that academic achievement is a multidimensional variable influenced by many factors [6]. One of these factors, which has been mentioned in some previous studies, is mindfulness [7–10]. Mindfulness refers to paying attention in a particular way, with purpose, in the present moment, and without judgment toward internal and external experiences [11]. The word “mindfulness” is a translation of the word “sati,” which in Buddhism means awareness and refers to a type of spiritual or psychological capacity [12]. In psychology, the definition of mindfulness varies depending on who is studying it and from what method or perspective. Some view it as a mental state, while others consider it a set of skills and techniques [13]. The common feature of all definitions is that they emphasize two elements: direct attention to the present moment and curiosity, openness, and acceptance [14]. Rather than reacting impulsively or habitually, mindful individuals process events more realistically. Because they perceive internal and external realities without distortion, they experience and process events as less distressing than they might otherwise be. They also possess a strong ability to cope with a wide range of thoughts, emotions, and experiences [15]. Some studies indicate that the academic achievement of students with high levels of mindfulness is significantly greater compared to students with low levels of mindfulness [2, 16]. Additionally, a study investigating the role of mindfulness in education and learning found that mindfulness training enhances academic achievement [8, 9]. Another study indicated that mindfulness plays a mediating role in academic achievement [17].

Other studies have also shown that mindfulness indirectly affects students’ academic achievement by influencing variables such as stress, self-efficacy, and adaptability [18, 19]. It appears that mindfulness helps students recognize their abilities and learn to cope with emotions and negative thoughts, as well as experience mental events positively, which impacts their academic achievement [20]. In a study, Farah Bijari et al. (2013) demonstrated that individuals with a positive self-concept are capable of establishing healthy and positive relationships with others and their environment, which leads to better personal adaptability [21]. Ghasemi Jobaneh et al. (2016), Chambers et al. (2009), and Enayati (2016) also reported in their studies that mindfulness is significantly and positively associated with all areas of adaptability, including adaptability, social, personal, and emotional adaptability [22–24].

Adaptability is another important and influential aspect of academic achievement [25, 26]. Adaptability is a complex and multifaceted concept. It involves adjusting behaviors to meet environmental demands, often requiring modifications in impulses, emotions, or attitudes [27]. In an academic and university environment, adaptability can be viewed as an indicator of a transitional process that, over time, reveals how well a student has learned to cope with the demands of the university environment [28].

Baker & Siryk (1986) described student adaptability as a multifaceted concept and believed that a student must be able to align themselves with the requirements and conditions of the educational environment through adaptation processes [20]. They classified adaptation to the university environment into four categories: academic adjustment, social adjustment, personal-emotional adjustment, and institutional attachment [20]. Academic adjustment refers to a student’s success in coping with various educational conditions, such as different motivational aspects, perseverance, and performance encountered at the university [29]. Social adjustment refers to a student’s success in coping with the individual-social demands present in university experiences, such as relationships with others, homesickness, and the social environment [30]. Personal-emotional adjustment relates to the individual’s mental and physical health status in the new environment [31]. Finally, institutional attachment refers to the level of a student’s commitment to educational goals and the degree of attachment to the educational institution they are attending [32].

Being accepted into university and transitioning from high school to university represents a significant change in the lives of many young people. Entering university is an opportunity for further learning and psychological growth [33]; however, for some, it becomes a source of stress and leads to maladaptive reactions [34]. When a student enters university after passing the national entrance exam, they encounter new conditions to which they must adapt [35]. The educational and social environment that a student enters is complex, diverse, and expansive. Adapting to this environment requires leaving behind some previous ways of life before entering university and adopting new methods in new circumstances [36]. Failure to navigate this process can result in dropping out, a desire to change majors, a lack of academic progress, and psychological issues [37]. Regarding this, findings from some previous studies indicate that adaptability is significantly associated with students’ academic achievement [38–41]. Furthermore, the findings of studies by Xie et al. (2019) and Tamannaeifar et al. (2019) showed that higher levels of student adaptability were linked to their academic achievement [42, 43].

A review of previous studies reveals that most have examined the three main research variables (mindfulness, adaptability, and academic achievement) either unidimensional or two-dimensional. Additionally, the methodologies used in these studies did not mention structural equation modeling. Therefore, given the importance of mindfulness, adaptability, and academic achievement in educational planning and enhancing teaching and learning outcomes and the lack of similar studies examining these three variables using a structural equation modeling approach, the present study was conducted to examine the effect of mindfulness on academic achievement with the mediating role of adaptability among students at Jiroft University of Medical Sciences in southern Iran in 2024. Jiroft University of Medical Sciences is one of the medical sciences universities in Kerman province in the south of Iran. This university is responsible for providing educational, health and medical services in seven southern cities of Kerman province. In the field of education, the mission of Jiroft University of Medical Sciences is to train capable human resources according to the latest knowledge and international standards. In addition, this university supplies the human resources needed by health and treatment centers in the aforementioned region.

This study not only incorporates certain innovations in its methodology but also contributes to expanding knowledge in the academic field concerning the variables under investigation. It enhances the understanding of higher education administrators and policymakers regarding the status of these variables and their relationships within the student community. Additionally, given the primary objective of this research, the findings can serve as a basis for planning to improve students’ adaptability and academic achievement.

Methods

Design and setting

This cross-sectional descriptive-analytical study was conducted on students at Jiroft University of Medical Sciences in southern Iran from January to April 2024.

Participants

The study population consisted of students from Jiroft University of Medical Sciences, including those from the Faculty of Medicine (Medical field), Nursing (Nursing and Midwifery fields), Health (Public Health and Environmental Health fields), and Paramedical Sciences (Laboratory Sciences, Operating Room, and Anesthesia fields).

Out of the total number of students (1,136 individuals), 73% were non-native students and 27% were native. Considering the population size and using the formula below, with a confidence level of 95%, a margin of error of 5%, p = q = 0.5, and z = 1.96, the sample size was estimated to be 290 individuals. This sample size was acceptable according to Chomeya et al.‘s study for structural equation modeling in this study [44].

Considering the total population of 1,136 individuals, the sample size for each faculty was determined by dividing 290 by 1,136 and then multiplying the resulting number by the number of students in each faculty. The table below lists the names of the faculties under study, the total number of students in each faculty, and the sample size required for each faculty separately (Table 1).

The inclusion criteria for the study included being enrolled in the second semester of the 2022–2023 academic year and willingness to participate in the research. Additionally, since the use of psychiatric medications could negatively affect mindfulness and its components, potentially altering the results and introducing bias, not taking such medications for at least one month prior to the start of the study was considered another inclusion criterion for the students in this study. The exclusion criteria included unwillingness to participate in the study, the presence of a physical illness in the participant that could be associated with poor academic performance, as well as a history of seizures or neurological disorders (based on their medical records at the university’s student affairs department).

Instruments

The data collection tool was a four-part questionnaire. The first section of the questionnaire included demographic information such as age, gender, marital status, field of study, academic level, place of residence, and employment status. The second section was the Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire (FFMQ), developed by Baer et al. (2006). This questionnaire consists of 39 items and five subscales: observation (8 items), description (8 items), acting with awareness (8 items), non-judgment (8 items), and non-reactivity (7 items). Responses were scored using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (never) to 5 (always). The Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire was translated and normalized in Iran by Tamannaeifar et al. (2016) on a sample of students, and its validity and reliability (with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.85) have been confirmed [45]. The reliability of this part of the questionnaire in the present study was confirmed with Cronbach’s alpha coefficient equal to 0.87.

The Academic Achievement Questionnaire, by Pham and Taylor (1999), was the third section of the questionnaire. This questionnaire consists of 48 items that assess academic performance across five dimensions: self-efficacy (8 items), emotional influences (8 items), planning (14 items), lack of outcome control (4 items), and motivation (14 items). Responses were scored using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (very low) to 5 (very high). The validity and reliability (with Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.90) of the Academic Achievements Questionnaire were confirmed in research by Saffarieh et al. (2022) in Iran [46]. Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of this part of the questionnaire was calculated as 0.89, which indicated its acceptable reliability.

The fourth section of the questionnaire was the Adaptation Questionnaire by Baker & Siryk (1986). This section includes 67 items and four components: academic adjustment (24 items), social adjustment (20 items), personal-emotional adjustment (15 items), and attachment to the institution/university (8 items). This questionnaire’s scoring is based on a 7-point scale ranging from ‘Does not apply to me at all’ to ‘Applies to me completely,’ with scores ranging from 1 to 7. The Four Facet Adaptation Questionnaire was translated and normalized in Iran by Porparizi et al. (2018) on a sample of students, and its validity and reliability (with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.92) have been confirmed [19]. The reliability of this part of the questionnaire was confirmed with Cronbach’s alpha coefficient equal to 0.90.

In the present study, the reliability of the mindfulness, academic achievements, and adaptation questionnaires were confirmed with Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.87, 0.89, and 0.90, respectively.

Procedure and statistical analysis

To collect the data, one of the researchers (ARY) visited the colleges under study on different days of the week, in both morning and afternoon sessions. The questionnaires, which had been translated into Persian, were then distributed among the students under study and collected on the same day they were distributed. To ensure ethical considerations, participation in the study and completion of the questionnaires was entirely voluntary and only proceeded if the individual agreed. Before completing the questionnaires, the research objectives were clearly explained to the students, and their verbal consent to participate in the study was obtained. The students were also informed that this study is part of an approved research project by Jiroft University of Medical Sciences and that they had the right to refuse or decline participation. Finally, to maintain confidentiality, the questionnaires were completed by the students without including their names or surnames.

The collected data were entered into SPSS version 23. To examine the impact of students’ mindfulness on their academic achievement, with the mediating role of adaptation, structural equation modeling using partial least squares methods was employed with Smart PLS3 and SPSS26 software. Reasons for using the PLS method in this study include its adequate predictive power, acceptable testing of hypothesis, development of research theory, and the relatively small sample size. Additionally, since the PLS method assumes that all blocks are linear combinations of their indicators, common issues such as the ambiguity of factors, which can occur in some SEM techniques, do not arise.

Additionally, the Sobel Test, as the most common method of testing mediation coefficients [47], was used to assess the significance of the mediating effect of adaptation in the relationship between mindfulness and students’ academic achievement.

The following relationship was used in this test:

In this context, (a) represents the direct effect of the independent variable (mindfulness) on the mediator variable (adaptation), (b) represents the effect of the mediator variable (adaptation) on the dependent variable (academic achievement), (Sa) is the standard error of the effect of the independent variable on the mediator variable, and (Sb) is the standard error of the effect of the mediator variable on the dependent variable. Additionally, (ab) represents the indirect effect of the independent variable on the dependent variable through the mediator variable.

Finally, to assess the model’s structural fit, the coefficient of determination (R²), the predictive relevance (Q²), and the root mean square residual (SRMR) were used. Values of 0.19, 0.33, and 0.67 are considered weak, moderate, and strong, respectively, for R² [48]. For the Q² index, a positive value indicates that the model fit is satisfactory and has adequate predictive power [48]. Finally, if the SRMR value is less than 0.08, it indicates a good and accurate model fit [47].

Discussion

The results of testing the first hypothesis of this study showed that students’ mindfulness has a positive and significant impact on their academic achievement. Ganji et al.‘s (2013) study demonstrated that the academic achievement of students with high mindfulness is significantly higher than that of students with low mindfulness [7]. Leland’s (2015) studies, as well as those by Mrazek et al. (2017), indicated that mindfulness training enhances academic achievement [8, 49]. The findings of Bohlmeijer et al. (2010) showed that students with higher academic achievement exhibit greater mindfulness [9]. Pahlavani and Jowkar (2023) also reported in their study that mindfulness is a positive and significant predictor of academic achievement [10]. It can be explained that mindfulness activates cognitive functions and processes such as sustained attention, cognitive flexibility, decision-making ability, planning, metacognitive awareness, executive function, and self-regulation, which in turn contribute to improved academic performance. Additionally, mindfulness aids in studying, understanding content, and retaining information by fostering awareness and focus. Moreover, mindfulness can have a positive indirect effect on academic performance and achievement by regulating emotions and reducing stress and anxiety [19, 50]. Mindfulness enhances and improves students’ wisdom for academic advancement by increasing cognitive mechanisms and skills related to problem-solving, learning, thinking, reading, memory, reasoning, and attention [51]. Mindful individuals also exhibit high emotional mechanisms. Students who possess mindfulness skills have a greater ability to understand others’ needs and can effectively perceive peer pressure. Acquiring this skill leads to significant changes in students’ personal and interpersonal indicators, allowing them to achieve a realistic perception of themselves and the surrounding world. They become more capable of controlling their emotions and behaviors and are more successful in establishing effective, deep, and sincere relationships with others, which consequently leads to their academic progress [52].

The results of the second hypothesis test indicated that students’ mindfulness has a positive and significant impact on their adaptability. This finding is consistent with the results of studies by Jafari et al. (2014) [52], Khanjarkhani and Abbasi (2017) [53], and Mettler et al. (2019) [54]. Part of the findings from the study by Porparizi et al. (2018) showed that students’ mindfulness directly affects their academic adaptability [19]. This finding can be explained by the fact that students encounter various sources of stress in their environment, each of which can contribute to increased psychological pressure. Mindfulness can effectively reduce these stressors, resulting in greater academic adaptability. The more mindful a person is, the better their adaptability will be. These individuals are aware of both the positive and negative experiences around them and respond more appropriately to what happens to them at the moment. Mindfulness also enables a person to respond thoughtfully rather than reactively and impulsively to events. Furthermore, focusing on the present moment is one of the principles of mindfulness, and this emphasis on the ‘here and now’ facilitates coping with new events. In other words, increasing attention and awareness of thoughts, emotions, and inclinations by coordinating adaptive behaviors and positive psychological states leads to improved personal capabilities for engaging in individual and social activities and fostering an interest in these activities [53]. Moreover, individuals who score higher on mindfulness scales tend to have better academic adjustment. This means they are more likely to be present at the moment and alert and aware of both internal and external phenomena. They cultivate a non-judgmental attitude and acceptance of these phenomena, and instead of reacting impulsively and maladaptively, they respond to life and academic challenges in adaptive and proactive ways. As a result, they demonstrate greater adaptability in various aspects of life, especially in their academic pursuits [19]. Mindfulness plays a crucial role in enhancing adaptability in dynamic environments and is not merely a stress management skill; it also improves other aspects of adaptability, such as learning and problem-solving skills. Additionally, mindfulness promotes sustained adaptability by increasing flexibility in the face of change and improving responses to stress [55].

The results of the third hypothesis test in this study confirmed the positive and significant impact of adaptability on students’ academic achievement. Part of the findings from the study by Esmaili and Alaei (2023) showed a positive and significant relationship between students’ adaptability and their academic progress. Additionally, based on the results of this study, both academic and social adaptability variables were able to significantly improve students’ academic achievement [25]. Soleimanifar and Shabani (2013) concluded in their study that there is a positive relationship between achievement motivation and academic adaptability [56]. According to parts of the findings from studies by Mohammadi et al. (2016) [57], Tamannaeifar et al. (2019) [43], Singh (2018) [58], and Rodríguez (2017) [59], a positive and significant relationship was found between academic adaptability scores and students’ academic achievement. The research by Abdullah et al. (2010) showed that learners who are more capable of establishing adaptability demonstrate better academic performance compared to those who struggle with adaptability [60]. Abtahi and Nadri (2012) concluded that adaptable individuals, due to their traits, such as setting expectations for themselves within their abilities and resources, identify and develop a realistic attitude toward life goals. They choose desirable academic goals and focus their efforts on achieving them, which in turn leads to improved academic performance [61]. The study by Varasteniya and Ojinejad (2022) indicated that academic adaptability has a minor role in predicting students’ achievement motivation [62]. According to part of the findings from the study by Tirgari et al. (2017), no significant relationship was observed between achievement motivation and academic adaptability among students [63]. The results of two recent studies are inconsistent with this section of the current study’s findings. This inconsistency may be due to differences in the research populations or the tools used to measure the study variables. Regarding the discrepancies between some studies and the results of this study, it can be noted that while some studies use students’ grade point average (GPA) as the measure of academic progress, this study assessed academic progress through a questionnaire. This difference can significantly impact the variation in findings. Another reason for the discrepancy is the self-reporting by students on research variables, which can lead to different, and sometimes conflicting, results in various studies. Therefore, given this issue, the results of this study cannot be broadly generalized, and the findings should be used with caution. This is because various factors can influence research outcomes in different educational environments.

To explain this part of the current study’s findings, it can be said that adaptable students, considering characteristics such as having self-expectations within their capabilities and resources, identifying life goals, and adopting a realistic approach towards them, choose appropriate academic goals within their ability. Ultimately, they channel their efforts towards achieving these goals and attain success in their academic endeavors [57]. When individuals possess good adaptability, they effectively connect with their surrounding conditions and are more capable of meeting their needs. Adaptability leads to tranquility and self-confidence, which in turn allows individuals to perform better academically and acquire skills more effectively in the educational environment. A student with good social adaptability easily establishes constructive relationships with professors and classmates, and these interactive connections positively influence their academic achievement and performance. Additionally, adaptable, flexible, realistic, and responsible individuals who respect themselves are more likely to achieve desirable academic performance. Furthermore, academic adaptability helps students identify expectations, resources, and capabilities, enabling them to set realistic academic goals within their limits, remain realistic about these goals, and strive to achieve them. As a result, they can make progress and attain desirable academic performance in their educational domain [43].

The results of the fourth hypothesis test also showed that mindfulness has a positive and significant impact on student’s academic achievement through the mediation of adaptability. In this regard, Porparizi et al. (2018) found that mindfulness has a direct and significant relationship with academic achievement and academic adaptability and that academic self-concept plays a mediating role in the relationship between academic achievement and academic adaptability [19]. The findings of the study by Abedi et al. (2023) showed that mindfulness training has a significant impact on increasing academic adaptability and academic hope while reducing academic anxiety among students [64]. To explain this part of the research findings, it can be stated that an individual who, through mindfulness training, acquires the necessary skills to adapt to the academic environment can achieve academic adaptability and has the ability to progress academically. This is because a mindful individual, when confronted with various situations, becomes non-judgmental, allowing them to let go of the past, present, and future and focus on the present moment. This increased adaptability can, in turn, encourage and motivate the learner to concentrate on learning and be hopeful for academic achievement. A mindful individual can also confront academic anxiety in educational settings and maintain academic adaptability [64]. In addition, Mindfulness influences the coordination of adaptive behaviors, such as the ability to handle a wide range of thoughts, emotions, and both unpleasant and pleasant experiences. It affects academic performance variables such as stress reduction, increased self-efficacy, and improvement in cognitive abilities, including attention, self-regulation, concentration, memory, and problem-solving, thereby enhancing academic performance [65]. In other words, since mindfulness helps individuals process events more realistically and develop a high capacity to deal with emotions, thoughts, and experiences, it aids students in adapting to their university and dorm environments. This allows students to either change themselves or make adaptions to their environment, achieving a better alignment between themselves and their surroundings [66]. This alignment and adaptation are crucial for improving academic performance, as without it, students may lose their focus on academic goals, which could even lead to dropout or withdrawal (67). In essence, mindful individuals strive to adapt to environmental conditions by recognizing their abilities, managing negative emotions and thoughts, and experiencing mental events positively, thus directing their energy, focus, and attention toward academic goals [53, 65, 66].

This study had several limitations. These include the exclusively quantitative methodology of the research and the study being conducted within a limited population. Therefore, it is recommended that future studies use qualitative, mixed-methods (quantitative and qualitative), and interventional approaches with a larger sample size to achieve a clearer understanding of the research subject. Moreover, it is important to note that the cross-sectional design of this study limits the ability to infer definitive causal relationships between variables. Although correlational analyses and structural equation modeling provide evidence of relationships between mindfulness, adaptation, and academic achievement, the direction of causality or the presence of confounding variables cannot be determined with certainty. To further investigate causal relationships between these constructs, future research using experimental or longitudinal designs is recommended. Another limitation of this research is the multiple tools used to measure mindfulness, academic achievement, and adaptability. Therefore, it is recommended that future studies use other available tools to measure the aforementioned variables in order to compare with the results of the present study and to obtain possible hidden concepts.

Conclusion

According to the findings, mindfulness has a significant positive effect on academic achievement both independently and through its mediating role in adaptability. Students who possess mindfulness skills and have been trained in this area are better able to adapt to the conditions, environment, atmosphere, and facilities of the university where they study. This adaptability can lead to academic success because a student with mindfulness skills understands that in order to progress academically, they must adjust to both current and future conditions; otherwise, they may face academic failure. Therefore, based on the results of this study, it is recommended that university policymakers and decision-makers hold workshops and training courses aimed at enhancing and developing mindfulness skills, especially for new students, so that they can better adapt to the new academic environment and achieve success in their studies. To this end, teaching techniques such as mindfulness and being present at the moment, understanding emotions, practicing focus on thoughts, emotions, and mental images, recognizing and controlling a wandering mind, and focusing the sense of satisfaction from actions to weaken idealistic promises of future tranquility are recommended for improving and enhancing mindfulness among students in educational settings.

Relationship and predictive value of controlling nutritional status score and serum vitamin level with coronary heart disease in young and middle-aged adults
Authors: Jie Deng, Xiaoming Liu, Haining Liu, Wei Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the relationship between CONUT score and serum vitamin (25-OHD, folic acid, vitamin B12) levels and CHD in young and middle-aged adults, and to analyze the value of CONUT score combined with serum vitamin (25-OHD, folic acid, vitamin B12) levels in predicting the risk of CHD in young and middle-aged adults.MethodsThis study was a retrospective case–control study. A total of 135 young and middle-aged patients with CHD diagnosed by coronary angiography in Tangshan Workers’ Hospital from April 2023 to October 2024 were selected as the case group (n = 135), and 120 patients with negative coronary angiography or CTA results in our hospital during the same period were selected as the control group (n = 120). The nutritional status of the subjects was evaluated by CONUT score, and the serum vitamin level of the subjects was detected by chemiluminescence method. Univariate and multivariate Logistic regression analysis were used to analyze the relationship between different nutritional status levels, serum vitamin levels and CHD in young and middle-aged people. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to evaluate the predictive value of CONUT score combined with serum vitamin level for the risk of CHD in young and middle-aged adults.ResultsThe detection rate of malnutrition in the CHD group was 48.90%, and the detection rate of malnutrition in the control group was 30.80%. The multivariate results show that according to the unadjusted model, there was a significant relationship between high CONUT score, low 25-OHD, low folic acid, and low serum vitamin B12 levels and CHD. In multifactor models 2, after adjusting for gender, family history of premature CHD, history of hypertension, history of diabetes, history of smoking, LDL-C, Hcy, CRP, blood glucose, blood uric acid, serum creatinine and other traditional risk factors of CHD, it was found that CONUT score ≥ 2 points, serum 25-OHD ≤ 17.20 ng/ml, folic acid ≤ 10.90 ng/ml, serum vitamin B12 ≤ 485.50 pg/ml, the risk of CHD was higher. ROC curve analysis showed that CONUT score combined with three serum vitamins, AUC was 0.836(95%CI:0.788–0.884), the sensitivity and specificity were 83.7% and 69.2%(P < 0.05).ConclusionThe detection rate of nutritional imbalance in young and middle-aged patients with CHD is high, and there is a significant relationship between nutritional status and serum vitamin level and the risk of CHD in young and middle-aged patients. CONUT score and serum vitamin level can be used as indicators to evaluate the risk of CHD in young and middle-aged people.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40795-025-01220-0.

Introduction

Coronary heart disease(CHD) is a heart disease caused by myocardial ischemia, hypoxia or necrosis caused by stenosis or occlusion of the lumen caused by atherosclerosis of coronary arteries [1]. According to the WHF World Heart Report 2023, more than 500 million people worldwide are affected by cardiovascular disease. Cardiovascular disease acCONUTed for 33% of all deaths worldwide in 2019, including 9.1 million deaths from ischemic heart disease [2]. According to the China Cardiovascular Health and Disease Report 2022, the prevalence and mortality of cardiovascular diseases in China are still rising, and the number of patients with CHD is about 11.39 million [1]. Although great progress has been made in treatment and management, CHD remains the leading cause of death worldwide. In recent years, the incidence of CHD has gradually become younger. The hospitalization burden of young and middle-aged patients with CHD between 25 ~ 60 years old has increased significantly [3]. In particular, the incidence and hospitalization rate of patients between 35 ~ 55 years old have increased by 24.9% and 35.9%, respectively [4, 14]. In addition, young and middle-aged patients with CHD have a high risk of sudden death and a worse prognosis. Therefore, more effective prevention and intervention measures are needed to further reduce the risk of disease. Nutrition has been recognized as one of the main risk factors for CHD. More than 70% of hospitalized patients with CHD have the risk of nutritional imbalance. A large number of studies have shown that coronary atherosclerosis and endothelial dysfunction are closely related to nutrient intake imbalance and micronutrient imbalance.

The controlling nutritional status score (CONUT) is a simple screening tool for nutritional evaluation, which is of great significance in the prognosis of malnutrition in patients with cardiovascular disease. The risk of MACE events and all-cause mortality in malnourished patients with CHD was about 50% higher than that in patients with normal nutrition [5]. In recent years, a large number of studies have shown that multiple vitamins are closely related to the occurrence and development of CHD. 25-OHD deficiency may promote the occurrence and development of CHD through oxidative stress, activation of RAAS system, abnormal regulation of NO, and promotion of vascular inflammation. Studies have shown that there is a nonlinear dose–response relationship between vitamin D levels and CHD [6, 7]. High serum homocysteine (Hcy) level has become an independent risk factor for CHD. Folic acid and vitamin B12 were significantly associated with homocysteine metabolism. Increased intake of folic acid and vitamin B12 reduces blood Hcy, thereby reducing the risk of cardiovascular disease. In addition, studies have confirmed that folic acid supplementation can reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease [8].

At present, nutrition therapy management has gradually developed to a full range of nutritional needs including micronutrients, but there is a lack of nutritional assessment including vitamins and other micronutrients for cardiovascular diseases. A large number of studies have shown that vitamins are significantly associated with a variety of diseases and their prognosis. The quantitative relationship between different levels of vitamins and CHD is still unclear. Moreover, the current nutritional screening tools do not fully consider the particularity of the young and middle-aged adults. The young and middle-aged adults usually has metabolic abnormalities and fewer chronic diseases. It is often due to recessive malnutrition caused by poor lifestyle or in the early stage of nutritional problems (such as overweight, low muscle rate or lack of micronutrients such as vitamin D, and B vitamins). Traditional tools cannot screen for subclinical nutritional problems. Young and middle-aged period is the incubation period of many chronic diseases such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, hypertension. Therefore, in order to avoid or delay the occurrence of diseases and promote the improvement of health level, this study investigated and analyzed the relationship between different degrees of nutritional status and the risk of CHD in young and middle-aged people through CONUT score combined with serum vitamin level detection, and evaluated the predictive value of CONUT score combined with serum vitamin level detection for the risk of CHD in young and middle-aged adults.

Data and methods

Research object

In this study, 135 young and middle-aged patients with CHD diagnosed by coronary angiography in Tangshan Workers’ Hospital from April 2023 to October 2024 were selected as the case group (n = 135), and 120 patients with negative coronary angiography or coronary CTA results in our hospital during the same period were selected as the control group (n = 120), a total of 255 cases were included. Case group inclusion criteria (CHD): patients with CHD confirmed by coronary angiography; aged 18 ~ 59 years old. Exclusion criteria: exclusion of acute stress such as severe infection, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular accidents; severe liver and kidney dysfunction, heart failure, malignant tumor or other serious medical diseases; Recent use of hormonal drugs and vitamin supplements; Those with incomplete clinical data. Inclusion criteria for the control group (NCHD): age 18 ~ 59 years old. Control group exclusion criteria: multifactorial myocardial damage caused by uncertain diseases (such as severe heart failure, stress cardiomyopathy, severe pulmonary embolism, pulmonary hypertension, sepsis, critical illness, renal failure, severe neurological disease) was excluded; Severe liver and kidney insufficiency, malignant tumors and their complications, immune system diseases and other serious medical diseases; Recent use of vitamin supplements and hormone medications; Those with incomplete clinical data.

Method

Data collection

The general demographic data and clinical data of the patients were collected through the electronic medical record system of Tangshan Workers' Hospital. All data reporting personnel fill in the report records according to the unified standard process of the hospital, and input the relevant information of the patient's medical records into the electronic medical record system. The data collected in this study include: (1) General demographic data: gender, age, height, weight, marital status, family history of premature coronary heart disease, smoking history, drinking history, family history, clinical complications (hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia); (2) Laboratory examination: lymphocyte absolute value, albumin, total cholesterol, triglyceride, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, blood homocysteine, C-reactive protein, 25-hydroxyvitamin D(25-OHD), Folic acid, serum vitamin B12, blood glucose, glycated hemoglobin, potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, creatinine, urea nitrogen, uric acid, left ventricular ejection fraction. Blood samples were collected within 24 h after admission and sent to the laboratory of Tangshan Workers’ Hospital for testing. The patient was advised to avoid drinking water after 10 pm on the day of admission. Fasting venous blood samples were collected and sent to the laboratory of Tangshan Workers' Hospital in the early morning of the next day for detection. According to the quartiles (Q1,Q2,Q3) of three serum vitamins (25-OHD, folic acid, serum vitamin B12) concentrations in the control group, the serum vitamin concentrations of the case group and the control group were divided into four grades:Group1: ≤ Q1, Group2: > Q1 and ≤ Q2, Group3: > Q2 and ≤ Q3, Group4: > Q3).

Evaluation method

Controlling nutritional status (CONUT) score: CONUT score was calculated according to the patient's serum albumin level, peripheral blood lymphocyte CONUT and total cholesterol level, and each assignment was performed according to the laboratory test results. The total score (range 0 ~ 12 points) was obtained by adding the scores of the above three indicators. The higher the total score, the worse the nutritional status. 0 ~ 1 is normal nutrition, 2 ~ 4 is mild malnutrition, 5 ~ 8 is moderate malnutrition, 9 ~ 12 is severe malnutrition [9]. Spearman correlation analysis

CONUT score (r = 0.215, P = 0.001), serum 25-OHD (r = −0.443, P < 0.001), folic acid (r = −0.281, P < 0.001), serum vitamin B12 (r = −0.389, P < 0.001) levels were measured were associated with CHD in young and middle-aged patients.

Relationship between CONUT score and serum vitamin level and CHD in two groups

According to the quartiles of serum vitamin concentration in the control group (Q1, Q2 and Q3), the CHD group and the control group were divided into four grades: Group1: ≤ Q1, Group2: > Q1 and ≤ Q2. Group3: > Q2 and ≤ Q3, Group4: > Q3. The results of multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that in the unadjusted model, it was found that there was a significant relationship between CONUT score, serum 25-OHD, folic acid, serum vitamin B12 levels and CHD. For every unit increase in CONUT score, the risk of CHD increased by 1.287 times (OR:1.287; 95%CI:1.079–1.534); for every unit decrease in 25-OHD, the risk of CHD increased by 0.860 times (OR:0.860; 95%CI:0.820–0.901). For every unit decrease in folic acid, the risk of CHD increased by 0.802 times (OR:0.802; 95%CI:0.731–0.880). For every one unit reduction in serum vitamin B12, the risk of CHD increased by 0.995 times (OR:0.995; 95%CI:0.993–0.996).

Multiple linear regression was used to diagnose the collinearity between variables. The results showed that Tolerance > 0.8 and VIF < 3 between each variable, suggesting that there was no collinearity between variables. According to multifactor models 2, after controlling for confounding factors, it was found that CONUT score ≥ 2 was associated with a higher risk of CHD compared with normal nutrition (OR:3.433; 95%CI:1.246–9.457), compared with serum vitamin Group4 levels. Predictive efficacy of nutritional assessment on the risk of CHD in young and middle-aged adults

ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC of CONUT score, 25-OHD, folic acid and serum vitamin B12 in independently predicting the risk of CHD in young and middle-aged adults were 0.621, 0.756, 0.663 and 0.725, respectively, all of which had predictive effects, but the effects were low, and the sensitivity and specificity were low(P < 0.05).

The AUC of CONUT score combined with 25-OHD level, CONUT score combined with folic acid level, CONUT score combined with serum vitamin B12 level in predicting CHD were 0.774, 0.683 and 0.744, respectively. The AUC of CONUT score combined with three serum vitamin levels in predicting CHD was 0.836(95%CI:0.788–0.884), and the prediction effect was moderate. The sensitivity and specificity were 83.7% and 69.2%(P < 0.05), respectively, which were better than those of single prediction and two combined prediction. Discussion

The relationship between nutritional status and CHD is extremely complex. Coronary atherosclerosis and endothelial dysfunction are now recognized as postprandial diseases. The prevalence of malnutrition in patients with CHD is 38.7% to 75.8%. During the treatment of CHD, it may aggravate the condition, prolong the course of disease, and even increase the risk of adverse events [15–17]. The results of this study showed that 48.9% of young and middle-aged patients with CHD had malnutrition by CONUT score. After controlling for confounding factors, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that there was a significant relationship between malnutrition and CHD. For every 1 unit increase in CONUT score, the risk of CHD increased by 1.287 times. The higher the CONUT score, the greater the risk of CHD Lu Jin et al. [22]found that the detection rate of malnutrition in patients with acute myocardial infarction was as high as 79.3%, and the severity of malnutrition was significantly correlated with all-cause mortality in patients with acute myocardial infarction. Multiple studies have also confirmed that CONUT score has a significant impact on the prognosis of patients with cardiovascular diseases, especially in predicting MACE events and the risk of all-cause death [5, 23]. ROC curve analysis results of this study showed that CONUT score assessment of nutritional status could predict the risk of CHD. Although the prediction efficiency was modest, it also indicated that CONUT score could be used as an effective nutritional status assessment tool for patients with CHD. Moreover, the combined assessment of nutritional status by serum albumin, total cholesterol level, and lymphocyte CONUT may be more relevant for improving the prognosis of patients with CHD through synergistic effects. Therefore, because of its accuracy and practicality in predicting adverse clinical outcomes in patients with CHD, CONUT score can be an important tool for nutritional assessment in patients with CHD.

Micronutrient imbalances such as 25-OHD deficiency and B vitamins (especially folic acid and vitamin B12) deficiency are also common in young and middle-aged people, with a prevalence of 70% to 95%. At present, the mechanism of micronutrient imbalance and CHD occurrence and development is not clear [17, 18]. It may promote the process of CHD by affecting vascular endothelial function, oxidative stress, activating RAAS system, and promoting vascular inflammatory response [22, 23]. The most important factor affecting nutritional imbalance is unhealthy eating habits. Unhealthy eating habits will directly lead to an increase in inflammatory factors in the body, thereby promoting atherosclerosis. Unhealthy diet can also cause intestinal flora imbalance. Therefore, the intestinal secretion of trimethylamine oxide (TMAO) increases, TMAO promotes the accumulation of cholesterol in foam cells, and intestinal flora imbalance leads to chronic inflammation, thereby promoting coronary atherosclerosis; in addition, food allergy or intolerance can cause oxidative stress and immune disorders, leading to inflammation and promoting atherosclerosis [19, 20]. Studies have confirmed that insulin resistance and islet β-cell function are one of the main potential mechanisms of CHD, and 25-OHD and zinc play an important role in the production, secretion and storage of insulin [21]. Moreover, patients with CHD are often accompanied by chronic inflammation, which may also affect the metabolism of proteins and other nutrients, resulting in chronic nutritional imbalance [13, 14].

A large number of observational studies have confirmed that serum 25-OHD, folic acid and serum vitamin B12 deficiency are significantly correlated with the risk of CHD, adverse cardiovascular events and all-cause death [17, 18, 24], and the lower the serum vitamin D level, the higher the risk of cardiovascular disease events [17]. The results of this study showed that there was a significant relationship between low serum 25-OHD, folic acid and serum vitamin B12 levels and CHD. Multivariate Logistic regression analysis after adjusting for other CHD risk factors showed that compared with serum vitamin Group4 levels, Serum 25-OHD ≤ 17.20(Q1) ng/ml, folate ≤ 10.90(Q2) ng/ml, serum vitamin B12 ≤ 485.50(Q2) pg/ml, the risk of CHD was higher. Liang Jihua [25] also came to a similar conclusion. Cosentino Nicola et al. [26] found that low serum 25-OHD levels in patients undergoing coronary angiography were also associated with the severity of CHD. Folic acid and vitamin B12 are closely related to homocysteine metabolism, and Hcy is usually used as an independent predictor of cardiovascular disease. Studies have confirmed that folic acid supplementation can reduce Hcy level, daily supplementation of folic acid 0.5 ~ 5 mg can reduce blood Hcy level by 25%, can reduce the crown of 11% risk of heart disease [27]. Guo Junchen et al. [28] found that when the serum vitamin B12 concentration in patients with CHD was lower than 494.5 pg/ml, the risk of death from CHD would double. The results of ROC curve analysis in this study also showed that CONUT score combined with serum vitamin level assessment of nutritional status had a good predictive value for the risk of CHD. Therefore, serum vitamin level detection can be used as an important indicator of nutritional assessment.

Nutritional status assessment plays a very important role in health maintenance. More and more guidelines and studies focus on the role of nutrition assessment and nutrition management in disease management, and nutrition interventions should be actively implemented for patients at risk of malnutrition [29]. At present, nutrition therapy management has gradually developed from meeting the needs of protein and energy metabolism to all-round nutrition needs including micronutrients. However, whether 25-OHD and vitamin B12 supplementation can reduce the incidence of CHD and improve the prognosis of patients with CHD is still controversial. A large number of randomized controlled trials and meta-analysis have shown that 25-OHD and vitamin B12 supplementation is not particularly effective in the treatment of cardiovascular diseases [28, 30]. However, a D-Health trial in Australia confirmed that a monthly supplement of 60,000 IU of vitamin D3 for 5 years in the elderly can reduce the incidence of major cardiovascular adverse events, especially myocardial infarction and coronary artery remodeling [31]. Jayedi et al. [32] confirmed that every 3 mg/d increase in vitamin B12 intake was nonlinearly associated with a reduced risk of CHD. Although the current experimental evidence does not fully confirm the beneficial effects of vitamin supplementation in reducing the risk of CHD and improving cardiovascular health, it does not clearly indicate that the use of nutritional supplements impairs cardiovascular health. Therefore, the effect of long-term use of nutritional supplements on cardiovascular health needs further study. In addition, the results of this study found that LDL-C levels in patients with CHD were lower than those in the control group(2.73 vs 2.92 mmol/l, P = 0.041), which may be significantly related to the use of statins in patients with CHD. The results of this study fully demonstrated the effectiveness of statins in reducing LDL-C levels in patients with CHD, helping to reduce the risk of cardiovascular events in patients with CHD and improve the prognosis of patients [33].

In summary, nutritional assessment can comprehensively evaluate the nutritional status of patients with CHD by CONUT score combined with serum vitamin detection, and find the nutritional health problems of patients as much as possible. There are some limitations in this study. This study is a single-center observational study and lacks multi-center data. The relationship between different levels of nutritional status and serum vitamin levels and CHD was only studied. No follow-up was conducted to observe the nutritional imbalance of patients and the effect of nutritional intervention on the prognosis of the disease.

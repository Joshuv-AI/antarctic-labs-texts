Three-loop QCD+QED corrections to on-shell quark renormalization Preprint: CPTNP-2026-005, ZU-TH 05/26
Long Chen, Hong-Yang Han, Zhe Li, Marco Niggetiedt

Abstract
We present the three-loop QCD+QED mixed corrections to the on-shell quark mass and wave-function renormalization constants through orders 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s m ​ α n ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{m}\alpha^{n}) with m + n = 3 m+n=3 . We further derive the three-loop relation between the pole mass and the MS ¯ \overline{\text{MS}} mass of a heavy quark, including the complete mixed QCD+QED contributions. The corresponding quark-mass anomalous dimension in the presence of both interactions is also extracted. Moreover, we provide the explicit conversion formulae, up to the same perturbative order, between the pole mass and the trace-anomaly subtracted σ \sigma -mass of heavy quark.



1
Introduction

Quark masses are fundamental parameters of the Standard Model of particle physics, and their precise determination is crucial for various high-precision tests of the model.
However, the color confinement inherent in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) makes the experimental extraction of quark masses highly non-trivial, as quarks are not observed as isolated free particles but are typically confined within hadrons (with the exception of the top quark).
Moreover, the quark-mass parameters in the QCD Lagrangian depend on the particular renormalization procedure required to subtract the intermediate ultraviolet divergences in the scattering amplitudes.
Consequently, there is no theoretically unique way to define the “physical” mass of an isolated (heavy) quark.
Nevertheless, there are two most commonly used process-independent perturbative mass definitions for massive quarks in the perturbation theory, the pole mass and
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
mass definition.
The pole mass for a particle in perturbation theory is defined through a special “physical” subtraction condition where the zero-point of the full inverse propagator in its momentum, i.e. the pole position of the propagator, coincides with the renormalized on-shell mass, while the residue at the pole fixes the corresponding wave-function renormalization constant.
Throughout this paper, we therefore use the terms “on-shell mass” and “perturbative pole mass” interchangeably.
It is well-known that the notion of pole mass for an elementary fermion in a fundamental gauge theory is not only renormalization-scheme independent, but also well-defined to
any
but
finite
order within perturbation theory
Tarrach:1980up
;
Bigi:1994em
;
Beneke:1994sw
;
Breckenridge:1994gs
;
Smith:1996xz
;
Kronfeld:1998di
;
Gambino:1999ai
.
However, due to infrared renormalon effects, it suffers from an intrinsic non-perturbative ambiguity of order
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
and therefore is not a strictly well-defined physical observable
Bigi:1994em
;
Beneke:1994sw
and thus could not be used to arbitrarily high precision;
the complete quark propagator in the full QCD does not have a simple pole and thus the concept of quark pole mass cannot be defined outside perturbation theory.
Another commonly used definition is based on the modified minimal subtraction (
\overline{\text{MS}}
) scheme
tHooft:1973mfk
;
Bardeen:1978yd
, leading to a short-distance mass that is free from the leading infrared renormalon ambiguities
Bigi:1994em
;
Beneke:1998ui
.
The perturbative results reformulated in terms of
\overline{\text{MS}}
mass usually exhibit improved perturbative convergence compared to those using the on-shell pole mass, which makes it particularly well suited for higher-order perturbative computations, especially in high-energy scattering processes.

Motivated by different theoretical or practical considerations, in addition to the pole and
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
masses, several useful alternative short-distance mass definitions of heavy quark have been proposed in the literature.
Prominent examples include the kinetic mass
Bigi:1994ga

Pineda:2001zq
;

In addition, regularization-independent renormalization schemes such as the RI/MOM mass
Martinelli:1994ty
;

;

These mass definitions can be converted into each other within perturbation theory, which is frequently needed in practical calculations as well as in the extraction of mass values from experiments (as the mass definitions with the best practice vary).
To this end, the pole or on-shell mass typically serves as conceptually a convenient
regularization-and-renormalization-independent
bridging pivot.
However, due to the well-known leading IR-renormalon issue in the perturbative definition of the pole mass, the short-distance
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
mass is, instead, more often employed as the reference mass when comparing determinations for quark masses (especially at low-energy scales) using different definitions and approaches.
The relation between
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}

In order to achieve high precision, it is mandatory to know the conversion relations between the different mass schemes as precisely as possible, e.g. to high perturbative orders and incorporate as much existing interactions as possible.

Apart from the precision quark-mass determination, the on-shell renormalization of heavy quark mass and wave-function are among the essential ingredients for high-order perturbative calculations of on-shell S-matrix elements for scattering processes involving external heavy quarks.
These corrections have been shown to be indispensable for reliably connecting the measured physical observables at high-energy colliders to the Standard Model parameters and for reducing theoretical uncertainties to the levels commensurate with experimental precision.
With the forthcoming accumulation of high-precision experimental data at the High-Luminosity LHC

Chetyrkin:1999qi

2.1
On-shell quark renormalization

The heavy
Q
-quark mass and wave-function renormalization constants,
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}
and
Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}
, are introduced formally by

\displaystyle m_{B}=Z_{m}^{\text{os}}\,m_{\mathrm{os}}\,,\qquad\psi_{B}=\sqrt{Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}}\,\psi_{R}\,,

(1)

where
m_{B}
and
\psi_{B}
denote the bare quark mass and quark field, while
m_{\mathrm{os}}
and
\psi_{R}
stand for the quark on-shell/pole mass and renormalized quark field, respectively.
The on-shell renormalization constants
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}
and
Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}
are determined by the renormalization condition:

(i)

The location of the pole of the heavy quark propagator coincides with its renormalized mass.

(ii)

The residue of the renormalized propagator at this pole is equal to one.

In the following, we briefly sketch the derivation of the on-shell renormalization constants for the heavy quark mass
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}
and the wave-function
Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}
.
Let us begin with definition of the complete (connected) two-point bare Green’s function
G_{B}
for a massive quark:

\displaystyle i\,G_{B}(\not{p})

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}\,x\,e^{+ip\cdot x}\,\langle 0\big|\,\hat{\mathrm{T}}\{\psi_{B}(x)\,\bar{\psi}_{B}(0)\}\,\big|0\rangle_{conn.}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\not{p}-m_{B}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(i\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},\,m_{B})\,\frac{i}{\not{p}-m_{B}}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\not{p}-m_{B}+\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},\,m_{B})}\,,

(2)

where
\Sigma_{B}
is the bare one-particle-irreducible (1PI) quark self-energy.
The dependence of
\Sigma_{B}
on the bare couplings
\alpha_{s}^{B},\alpha_{e}^{B}
, and bare mass
m_{B}
is understood by default.
The corresponding renormalized Green’s function
G_{R}
reads

\displaystyle i\,G_{R}(\not{p})

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}\,x\,e^{+ip\cdot x}\,\langle 0\big|\,\hat{\mathrm{T}}\{\psi_{R}(x)\,\bar{\psi}_{R}(0)\}\,\big|0\rangle_{conn.}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\not{p}-m_{R}+\Sigma_{R}(\not{p},\,m_{R})}

(3)

with
\Sigma_{R}
being the renormalized 1PI self-energy.
By definition (
1
), the bare and renormalized Green’s functions are related as
G_{B}(\not{p})=Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}\,G_{R}(\not{p})
,
which immediately implies the following relation between their inverses:

\displaystyle Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}\big(\not{p}-m_{B}+\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},\,m_{B})\big)=\not{p}-m_{R}+\Sigma_{R}(\not{p},\,m_{R})\,.

(4)

Here, the wave-function renormalization constant
Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}
absorbs all overall UV-divergences of
G_{B}(\not{p})
left after all sub-divergences are subtracted by the renormalization of the mass and coupling, rendering
G_{R}(\not{p})
UV-finite.

Within the on-shell renormalization, the renormalization conditions require that, the renormalized Green’s function
G_{R}(\not{p})
has a simple pole at the on-shell mass
\not{p}=m_{\mathrm{os}}
with unit residue,

\displaystyle iG_{R}(\not{p})\xrightarrow{\not{p}\to m_{\mathrm{os}}}\frac{i}{\not{p}-m_{\mathrm{os}}}\,.

(5)

This condition can be equivalently expressed in terms of the renormalized self-energy function as

\Sigma_{R}(\not{p}=m_{\mathrm{os}},\,m_{R}=m_{\mathrm{os}})=m_{R}-m_{\mathrm{os}}=0\,,\qquad\frac{\partial\,\Sigma_{R}(\not{p},\,m_{\mathrm{os}})}{\partial\,\not{p}}\Big|_{\not{p}=m_{\mathrm{os}}}=0\,.

(6)

Correspondingly, in terms of the bare self-energy function, the on-shell renormalization conditions can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{B}(\not{p}=m_{\mathrm{os}},\,m_{B}=Z_{m}^{\text{os}}\,m_{\mathrm{os}})=m_{B}-m_{\mathrm{os}}=\big(Z_{m}^{\text{os}}-1\big)\,m_{\mathrm{os}}\,,

\displaystyle Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}\Big(1+\frac{\partial\,}{\partial\,\not{p}}\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},\,m_{B}=Z_{m}^{\text{os}}\,m_{\mathrm{os}})\big|_{\not{p}=m_{\mathrm{os}}}\Big)=1\,.

(7)

Therefore, the UV divergence in
\Sigma_{B}(\not{p}=m_{p},\,m_{B}=Z_{m}\,m_{\mathrm{os}})
right at on-shell
\not{p}=m_{p}
point can be renormalized just via an
additive
renormalization by simply subtracting away
m_{B}
, which does not need any multiplicative wave-function renormalization; namely, it does not involve eventually or in essence
Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}
at all (which actually contains intermediate IR-soft divergences due to masslessness of gauge bosons).
It is important to know that
\Sigma_{B}
in eq. (
2.1
) denotes the full bare self-energies, defined originally with bare mass
m_{B}
and bare coupling
\alpha_{s}^{B},\alpha_{e}^{B}
(which can be expressed in terms of the renormalized Lagrangian parameters).

In general, by virtue of the Lorentz invariance, the bare 1PI quark self-energy
\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},m_{B})
can be conveniently decomposed in terms of two independent form factors:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},\,m_{B}=Z_{m}^{\text{os}}\,m_{\mathrm{os}})=m_{\mathrm{os}}\Sigma_{m}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})+(\not{p}-m_{\mathrm{os}})\Sigma_{d}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})

(8)

where
\Sigma_{m,d}
are scalar functions of
p^{2}
and the quark on-shell mass
m_{\mathrm{os}}
.
In terms of the form factors introduced in above equation, we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial\,\not{p}}\Sigma_{B}
, appearing in eq. (
2.1
), evaluated at the on-shell momentum configuration reading

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\,}{\partial\,\not{p}}\Sigma_{B}\big|_{\not{p}=m_{\mathrm{os}}}

\displaystyle=m_{\mathrm{os}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\not{p}}\Sigma_{m}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})+\frac{\partial}{\partial\not{p}}\big((\not{p}-m_{\mathrm{os}})\Sigma_{d}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})\big)

\displaystyle=m_{\mathrm{os}}\,2\,\not{p}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{2}}\Sigma_{m}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})+\Sigma_{d}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})+(\not{p}-m_{\mathrm{os}})\,2\,\not{p}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{2}}\Sigma_{d}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})

\displaystyle=2\,m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{2}}\Sigma_{m}\left(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}}\right)\big|_{p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}}+\Sigma_{d}\left(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}}\right)\big|_{p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}}\,.

(9)

Note that the derivative in
\frac{\partial}{\partial\,\not{p}}\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},\,m_{B})\big|_{\not{p}=m_{\mathrm{os}}}
applies only to the
p
-dependence in the bare 1PI-self-energy function
\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},\,m_{B})
(with this derivative evaluated eventually at on-shell momentum configuration
\not{p}\,u(p)=m_{\mathrm{os}}\,u(p)
).
The operation of taking derivative in the external momentum in the derivative
commutes
with loop integration in dimensional regularization.
Consequently, the operation of taking derivatives in the external momenta can be done at the integrand level of the dimensionally-regularized loop amplitudes or loop integrals.

Substituting the decomposition (
8
) of the bare quark self-energy
\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},\,m_{B})
and the derivative
\frac{\partial\,}{\partial\,\not{p}}\Sigma_{B}\big|_{\not{p}=m_{\mathrm{os}}}
in eq. (
2.1
) into the on-shell renormalization conditions in eq. (
2.1
), we derive the following formula for the on-shell quark mass and wave-function renormalization constants,
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}
and
Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}
, in terms of the bare self-energy functions:

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{\text{os}}

\displaystyle=1+\Sigma_{m}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})\big|_{p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle(Z_{Q}^{\text{os}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=1+2\,m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{2}}\Sigma_{m}\left(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}}\right)\big|_{p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}}+\Sigma_{d}\left(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}}\right)\big|_{p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}}\,.

(10)

Here, the
\Sigma_{m,d}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})
represent the full bare self-energies (which may be defined originally with bare mass
m_{B}
and bare couplings) rewritten in terms of the on-shell mass
m_{\mathrm{os}}
and mass renormalization constant
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}
by
m_{B}=Z_{m}^{\text{os}}m_{\mathrm{os}}
.
The derivatives with respect to
p^{2}
are evaluated at the on-shell point
p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}
.
If the external quark is massless, then
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}=1
due to the chiral symmetry in pure gauge theories, as
\Sigma_{m}=0
and accordingly
(Z_{q}^{\text{os}})^{-1}=1+\Sigma_{d}\left(p^{2}=0\right)
which would reduce 1 unless there are massive particles running inside the loop corrections to
\Sigma_{d}
.

To facilitate the extraction of the quantities in r.h.s. of (
2.1
) in practical calculations, especially the derivative in momentum, one may introduce the auxiliary power expansion parameter
t
in the following parameterization of the external momentum
Melnikov:2000zc
;
Marquard:2007uj
;
Marquard:2018rwx
,

\displaystyle p^{\mu}=P^{\mu}(1+t)\,,\qquad P^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}\,.

(11)

One considers then the following projection of the bare self-energy (
8
),

\displaystyle T_{1}\equiv\operatorname{Tr}\Big[\frac{(\not{P}+m_{\mathrm{os}})}{4\,P^{2}}\Sigma_{B}(\not{p},m_{\mathrm{os}})\Big]=\Sigma_{m}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})+t\,\Sigma_{d}(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}})\,,

(12)

where the momentum and mass in the external projector
(\not{P}+m_{\mathrm{os}})/(4\,P^{2})
are exactly the on-shell
P
and
m_{\mathrm{os}}
of the external quark, rather than
t
-dependent off-shell momentum or bare mass.
In the case of the external quark being massless, then
m_{\mathrm{os}}=0
,
\Sigma_{m}=0
and in the limit
p^{2}\rightarrow 0
the projection (
12
) effectively reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\,p^{2}}\,\mathrm{Tr}\big[\not{p}\,\big(\Sigma_{B}(\not{p})=\not{p}\,\Sigma_{d}(p^{2})\big)\big]\big|_{p^{2}\rightarrow 0}=\Sigma_{d}(p^{2}=0)\,,

(13)

which would be scaleless and vanish in dimensional regularization unless there are massive particles running inside the loop corrections.
Expanding the bare self-energy projection, defined in eq. (
12
), in the small parameter
t
, and retaining terms up to the first power, one ends up with

\displaystyle T_{1}=\Sigma_{m}\left(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}}\right)\Big|_{p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}}+t\,\Big[2\,m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{2}}\Sigma_{m}\left(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}}\right)\big|_{p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}}+\Sigma_{d}\left(p^{2},m_{\mathrm{os}}\right)\big|_{p^{2}=m_{\mathrm{os}}^{2}}\Big]+\mathcal{O}\big(t^{2}\big)\,.

(14)

Comparing this expansion with eq. (
2.1
), one sees that the leading term and the coefficient of the linear term in
t
of this projection expansion can be used to extract the on-shell quark mass renormalization constant
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}
and the wave-function renormalization constant
Z_{Q}^{\text{os}}
, respectively.
We note again that the
\Sigma_{m}
and
\Sigma_{d}
in the above equation are the full bare self-energies, which may be defined and determined originally with bare mass Lagrangian
m_{B}
and bare couplings
\alpha_{s}^{B},\alpha_{e}^{B}
, but one is free to rewrite the bare
m_{B}
,
\alpha_{s}^{B},\alpha_{e}^{B}
systematically and iteratively in terms of the renormalized counter-parts (which we usually do for the final expressions presented in the renormalized perturbation theory).

After tensor reduction, the amplitudes are expressed as linear combinations of a significant number of scalar Feynman integrals, which can be categorized into several integral families.

;
Chetyrkin:1981qh
.
In this work, we employ
Kira

;
Klappert:2020nbg
and
Blade

Guan:2024byi
, as well as
IdSolver

diagen
, to perform the IBP reduction, wherein the Laporta algorithm
Laporta:2000dsw
and block triangular form
Guan:2019bcx
;
Guan:2024byi
are implemented for solving IBP identities.

Subsequently, we numerically compute all MIs at the on-shell kinematic point utilizing the publicly available package
AMFlow

Liu:2017jxz
;
Liu:2020kpc
;
Liu:2021wks
;
Liu:2022mfb
;
Liu:2022chg
with high-precision numerical accuracy.
Additionally, to ensure the accuracy and reliability of our results, all MIs have been independently cross-verified by approaching the on-shell limit from generic off-shell kinematics.
This is implemented through the generalized power-logarithmic series expansion
Czakon:2008zk
;
Lee:2017qql
;
Liu:2017jxz
;
Moriello:2019yhu
;
Hidding:2020ytt
of the differential equations
Kotikov:1990kg
;
Kotikov:1991pm
;
Remiddi:1997ny
;
Gehrmann:1999as
;
Argeri:2007up
, where the package
AMFlow
is employed to evaluate the integrals at the boundary points.
As expected, a perfect agreement was found between our two computational approaches

Finally, the analytic expressions are reconstructed from the high-precision numerical results using the PSLQ algorithm
ferguson1999analysis

We note that the required three-loop on-shell propagator-type MIs have previously been obtained in terms of expansions to sufficiently high powers in the dimensional regulator

3.3
Mass relation between the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
and the on-shell masses

The quark masses in the on-shell and
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization schemes are renormalized multiplicatively. The connection between renormalized and bare quark masses is defined as

\displaystyle m_{B}=Z_{m}^{\text{os}}m_{\mathrm{os}}=Z_{\overline{m}}\overline{m}\,,

(22)

where
m_{B}
is the bare quark mass, and
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}
and
Z_{\overline{m}}
are the corresponding quark mass renormalization constants in the on-shell and
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme, respectively.
Owing to the infrared finiteness of the on-shell quark mass
Tarrach:1980up
;
Breckenridge:1994gs
;
Smith:1996xz
;
Kronfeld:1998di

\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
and the on-shell quark mass is defined as the following ratio:

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{f}\equiv Z_{m}^{f}(\alpha_{s},\,\alpha_{e}\,,\mu/m_{\mathrm{os}})=\frac{\overline{m}}{m_{\mathrm{os}}}=\frac{Z_{m}^{\text{os}}(\alpha_{s},\,\alpha_{e}\,,\mu/m_{\mathrm{os}})}{Z_{\overline{m}}(\alpha_{s},\,\alpha_{e})}\,,

(23)

which admits the following perturbative expansion up to three loops in mixed QCD+QED:

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{f}=1+\sum_{l=1}^{3}\sum^{i+j=l}_{i=0}\alpha_{s}^{i}\,\alpha_{e}^{j}\,z_{m,l}^{(i,j)}\,.

(24)

To calculate the mass relation
Z_{m}^{f}
in mixed QCD+QED corrections at the three-loop order, we need to calculate the mass renormalization factors in both the on-shell and
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
schemes at three-loop orders, respectively.
In the previous subsection, we present the explicit analytic expression for the three-loop mixed QCD+QED quark mass renormalization constant in the on-shell scheme.
The
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
quark mass renormalization constant has been computed up to four
Chetyrkin:1997dh
;
Vermaseren:1997fq
;
Chetyrkin:2004mf
and five loops

;
Luthe:2016xec
;
Baikov:2017ujl
.

\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme is still unknown.
Exploiting the defining form of
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization constant,
Z_{\overline{m}}
can be extracted from the explicit expression for
Z_{m}^{\text{os}}
by constructing the following ansatz,

\displaystyle Z_{\overline{m}}=1+\sum_{l=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{k}}\sum^{i+j=l}_{i=0}\alpha_{s}^{i}\,\alpha_{e}^{j}\,Z_{\overline{m},l,k}^{(i,j)}\,.

(25)

The unknown coefficients are fixed by requiring that the ratio

Z_{m}^{f}
is free of UV poles in dimensional regularization,

\displaystyle Z_{m}^{f}\big|_{\epsilon^{-k}}=0,\qquad\forall\,\quad k\geq 1\,.

(26)

\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
mass renormalization constant
Z_{\overline{m}}
and simultaneously yields a finite conversion factor

\overline{\mathrm{MS}}

3.4
\sigma

Refs.

\overline{\mathrm{MS}}

4
Conclusion

In this work we have presented a complete calculation of the mixed corrections in QCD+QED defined with one massive quark and
n_{l}
massless quarks to the on-shell quark mass and wave-function renormalization constants up to three-loop order, including all contributions of
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{m}\alpha^{n})
with
m+n=3
.
The calculation is performed in dimensional regularization and expressed in terms of renormalized gauge couplings, with the mixed
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization of
\alpha_{s}
and
\alpha
consistently taken into account.
We note that the structure of the electric charge dependence in our mixed results cover the full charge assignments for all six quark flavors in the Standard Model, up to the three-loop order in question.

Owing to the infrared finiteness of the on-shell quark mass, we extract from the on-shell mass renormalization constant the result for the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
mass renormalization constant and hence the quark-mass anomalous dimension in the presence of both QCD and QED interactions.
Accordingly, the mixed QCD and QED corrections to the matching relation between the heavy-quark pole mass and the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
mass are derived up to three-loop order.
Furthermore, we have derived the explicit conversion formulae between the pole mass and the trace-anomaly subtracted
\sigma
-mass of a heavy quark up to three loops, incorporating both QCD and QED effects.

Our results present the complete three-loop calculation for the on-shell quark renormalization in mixed QCD+QED, within the approximation where all quarks except for one are treated as massless.
This constitutes an important step towards improving the precision of Standard Model predictions for high-energy scattering processes involving heavy quarks.

Abstract
Underestimating the leakage can compromise Physical layer security (PLS) , while overestimating it may lead to inefficient system design. Therefore, a reliable leakage estimator is essential. Neural network-based estimators provide a data-driven way to estimate mutual information (M.I.) without requiring full knowledge of the channel or source distributions. In this work, we aim to scale the blocklength of a wiretap code such that the estimator can still feasibly operate. We propose an improved M.I. estimator based on the variational contrastive log-ration upper bound framework, tailored for both discrete and continuous variables. By using a mixture of Bernoulli experts parameterized by neural networks, the estimator is able to quantify information leakage in communication systems, which employ complex data processing like universal hash families (UHF) . We further propose a method to utilize the proposed estimator to design the UHF for a wiretap code or secret key generation design. Simulation results show that prior methods significantly underestimate the M.I. , particularly when using UHF for higher blocklengths ( n ≫ n\gg 16). The proposed method can scale the blocklength up to 255, and we conjecture that the design can scale well to even higher blocklengths given adequate training data and model size. Additionally, we contend that our proposed estimator and adaptive hash design framework offer a practical approach for extending PLS considerations for wiretap channels into the finite blocklength regime.

Introduction

PLS
began with Claude Shannon’s work on secrecy in 1949
[
1

. In 1975, Wyner introduced the wiretap channel to achieve secrecy without secret keys
[

. Csiszár and Körner later extended this idea to networks with multiple receivers

is often measured by

,
Kullback-Leibler divergence (KL divergence)
, or variational distance
[
4
]
. Several schemes that utilize machine learning methods have been developed to estimate these quantities. Neural estimators of
M.I.
commonly use Monte-Carlo samples to fit a distribution, then compute
M.I.
via derived bounds involving the
neural network (NN)
, though each bound has trade-offs. The Donsker–Varadhan (DV) form used in
mutual information neural estimator (MINE)

[
5
]
can
in principle
reach the exact
M.I.
, yet its variance and the value of the critic explode exponentially as the true
M.I.
grows. The linearised
Nguyen-Wainwright-Jordan bound (NWJ)
/
total Correlation Upper Bound Approximation (TUBA)

[
6
]
bound is more stable but can still drift when data are scarce.
Information Noise-Contrastive Estimation ( InfoNCE)

[
7
]
keeps variance low by turning the problem into a contrastive game, although they underestimate large
M.I.
unless the batch of negative samples is huge. Upper-bound methods like
Contrastive Log-ratio Upper Bound (CLUB)

[
8
]
or Barber–Agakov
[
9
]
are handy for minimising
M.I.
but become loose when
M.I.
is small and demand extra models. Aditionally, all these approaches face one common difficulty: when the hidden vector observed by the eavesdropper has many components, either the critic’s outputs overflow the floating-point data type or the bound saturates, making optimisation unreliable.

This paper is motivated by the need for accurate leakage estimates in the short/finite blocklength regime. Second-order analysis
[
10
]
shows that we cannot achieve zero error and a positive rate simultaneously. The additional
secrecy constraint creates a tradeoff between blocklength, secrecy rate,
bit error rate (BER)
, and leakage
[
11
]
. However, these analyses are asymptotic and may not be reliable for short blocklengths due to approximation errors. For practical design, instead, we propose the use of leakage estimators based on real measurements and mathematical bounds. In addition, the estimator can help fine-tune the system—for example, by adjusting the hash function output length in privacy amplification based wiretap coding
[
12
]
or
secret key generation (SKG)
. In particular, the hash function output size can be initialized by approximating smooth min-entropy with conditional entropy plus a correction term, estimated via
M.I.
. This enables an iterative, closed-loop design to minimize leakage while meeting reliability constraints—without requiring complex estimators of smooth min-entropy, as will be shown Sec.

.

The main contributions of this work are: (1) We design an enhanced
M.I.
estimator based on
variational CLUB (vCLUB)

[
13
]
, tailored for discrete variables and soft bits in communication systems using
NN

(2) We propose an efficient hash function design framework based on the proposed estimator and a derived bound of the conditional smooth min-entropy in terms of conditional entropy with a correction term in close form, which can be used for wiretap coding and
SKG
. (3) We investigate the finite blocklength performance of the proposed scheme, which demonstrates the proposed method can scale the blocklength up to 255, while prior methods significantly underestimate the
M.I.

II-B

Mutual information estimators

A key challenge in neural
M.I.
estimation is determining the required sample size for accurate estimation. In
[
14

, the authors claim that sample complexity scales as
{O}\!\Bigl(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Bigr)

[
15

showed that this claim is incorrect. The main mistake is that it applies a Hoeffding’s inequality to a function that can grow without bound, which is where the inequality becomes invalid. Hence, the number of samples might be larger— especially if the data has outliers that strongly affect
M.I.
.
If the estimator never sees these rare events, it might severely underestimate or overestimate the true
M.I.
. Other studies
[
16
]
have also found that the Donsker–Varadhan-based estimator becomes very unstable when the true
M.I.
is large. When the
M.I.
is small, it tends to give very weak estimates that cannot distinguish small differences.
In addition, computing

While

[
13
]
offers more stable

\small\hat{I}_{\mathrm{vCLUB}}\;=\;{\mathbb{E}_{p(x,y)}\!\bigl[\log q_{\theta}(x\mid y)\bigr]}-{\mathbb{E}_{p(x)p(y)}\!\bigl[\log q_{\theta}(x\mid y)\bigr]}.

(1)

This bound is motivated by the contrast between the expected log-likelihood of positive pairs

and the average log-likelihood over negative samples where

,
y\sim P_{Y}
are sampled independently. As such, it resembles the structure of contrastive learning objectives and can be optimized from sample data using stochastic gradient methods.

II-E

Multivariate Bernoulli

In a digital communication system with a binary alphabet, the

could model the joint probability of the input

and output

Let

(4)

Effectively, this formulation of the

by matching the

III
Main contributions

This section introduces the
Conditional Neural Bernoulli-Mixture Model (CNBMM)
architecture, designed to model conditional distributions in communication systems. The

III-B

Design of the hash function

Up to now, the design of the hash function, which is not trivial, is missing. However, we can resort to the proposed leakage estimator to achieve the design goal, to provide us with a low complexity design scheme for the hash function. From the
LHL

CLUB

2

[

[

V
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced the
CNBMM
architecture—a neural
M.I.
estimator designed specifically for
PLS
. By using the
vCLUB
framework and modeling the conditional distribution as a Bernoulli mixture of experts,
CNBMM
improves leakage estimation and avoids the scalability problems of
MINE
, which tends to be unstable for high-dimensional data.
Using this estimator, we also proposed a closed-loop method to design hash functions efficiently, without needing to calculate the smooth min-entropy through exhaustive search. Simulation results show that
CNBMM
provides more accurate and stable leakage estimates for large blocklengths, compared to
MINE
or
CLUB
. Overall, our
CNBMM
-based leakage estimator and adaptive hash function design offer a practical tool to bring
PLS
for wiretap channels into the finite blocklength setting.

One limitation of this work is that we did not fully explore the large space of parameter combinations
(n,k,b)
, especially when paired with different types of error correcting codes. A more systematic study would be needed to cover this space, which we leave for future work. Extending
CNBMM
to larger models, or jointly optimizing the leakage estimator,
UHF
, and
ECC
, are promising directions for further research.

Analytical approach to criticality of AdS black holes
Hong-Ming Cui

Abstract




We establish a hidden symmetry between the specific volumes of the coexistent phases and hence develop an analytical approach to study criticality of AdS black holes. In particular, using the method, we solve the coexistence line exactly for a variety of black holes, including the charged AdS black hole (in the four and the five dimensions), the rotating AdS black hole, the Gauss-Bonnet black hole and the quantum BTZ black hole.

1
Introduction

In the past two decades, extended thermodynamics of Anti-de-Sitter (AdS) black holes was widely studied in the literature since the pioneer work
[
1
]
, in which the cosmological constant was identified to a thermodynamic pressure and its conjugate as a thermodynamic volume. An advantage of such extension is the small-large black hole transition of the charged AdS black hole (in canonical ensemble) looks similar to that of a Van der Waals fluid. This is very interesting in holography and has facilitated numerous developments in black hole physics, such as the microstructures of black holes
[
2
,
3
]
and new bounds on the black hole entropy
[
4
,
5
]
. Actually the black hole thermodynamics can be further extended by varying all coupling constants. For instance, the central charge criticality was studied in
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
by varying the Newton’s gravitational constant and in
[
10
]
, global monopole charge criticality was examined in diverse dimensions.

However, in all these cases, the phase structure of AdS black holes was in general studied only numerically since the coexistence line cannot be solved exactly, except a few examples
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. This is somehow disappointing since unlike the fluid (gas) in experiments, the equation of states (eos) for AdS black holes generally have analytical expressions. In this work, we will show that once the eos is given, an analytical approach to criticality of AdS black holes could be developed. This is possible because of a simple fact: the functional relation between the coexistent small (
r_{s}
)-large (
r_{l}
) black hole sizes is
self-reciprocal
. That is if
r_{s}=\varphi(r_{l})
, then
r_{l}=\varphi(r_{s})
or equivalently
\varphi=\varphi^{-1}
(we refer these functions to as self-reciprocal). As a consequence, one can write
\varphi=\varphi(r_{*})
, where
r_{*}
collectively denotes the coexistent black hole sizes. Using this fact, we develop a simple approach, in which the small-large black hole sizes can be determined by a single algebraic equation for
\varphi(r_{*})
so that the coexistence line can be generally studied half-analytically at least. In particular, we will show that the coexistence line can be solved exactly for a variety of black holes in this approach, for example the charged AdS black hole (in the four and the five dimensions), the rotating AdS black hole, the Gauss-Bonnet black hole and the quantum BTZ black hole.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we clarify the functional relation between the coexistent small-large black hole sizes and develop a general approach to study the coexistence line analytically. As an example, we adopt the method to a Van der Waals fluid. In section 3, we study the charged AdS black hole in diverse dimensions. The coexistence line is obtained exactly in the four and the five dimensions. In section 4, we study the leading corrections of angular momenta to a rotating AdS black hole. In this limit, we derive the coexistence line exactly. In section 5, we study the P-V criticality of the Gauss-Bonnet black hole for a fixing higher order coupling constant
\alpha
. The coexistent line is solved exactly. In section 6, we study the
U-\nu
criticality of the quantum BTZ black hole and reproduce the coexistence line reported in the literature.

2
Self-reciprocal function and the coexistence line

Consider criticality of certain AdS black holes, for example a first order transition occurs between a pair of black holes below a critical temperature. The coexistence line is determined by

T(r_{s})=T(r_{l})\,,\qquad G(r_{s})=G(r_{l})\,,

(1)

where
T(r)
stands for the temperature,
G(r)
the Gibbs free energy and
r_{s}/r_{l}
denotes the small/large black hole (horizon) size, respectively.
Generally the above equations can only be solved numerically, except a few examples
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. However, we are aware of that the solution in fact can be found analytically according to a symmetry between the coexistent black hole sizes. To see this, we write

r_{l}=\varphi(r_{s})\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad r_{s}=\varphi^{-1}(r_{l})\,.

(2)

Substituting the relation into (
1
) yields

T\big(r_{*}\big)=T\big(\varphi(r_{*})\big)\,,\qquad G\big(r_{*}\big)=G\big(\varphi(r_{*})\big)\,,

(3)

and

T\big(r_{*}\big)=T\big(\varphi^{-1}(r_{*})\big)\,,\qquad G\big(r_{*}\big)=G\big(\varphi^{-1}(r_{*})\big)\,,

(4)

where
r_{*}
stands for the coexistent black hole sizes
r_{s}\,,r_{l}
collectively. However, since there exists a pair of solutions for (
1
), the equations Eq. (
3
) and Eq. (
4
) must have the same solutions, namely
r_{*}=r_{s}\,,r_{l}
(this is why we have not written the subscripts explicitly in above equations). This implies that generally the function
\varphi
should be
self-reciprocal
:

\varphi(r_{*})=\varphi^{-1}(r_{*})\,.

(5)

That is the function is the same as its anti-function and vice versa. This illustrates a hidden symmetry between the coexistent phases. Explicitly we can write

r_{s}=\varphi(r_{l})\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad r_{l}=\varphi(r_{s})\,.

(6)

It is easy to see that once the function
\varphi
is known, the coexistent line will be read off immediately (for example let
r_{*}
runs from the small black hole side and then one can read off the corresponding large black hole size according to
r_{l}=\varphi(r_{s})
). Here it should be emphasized that the above discussions are valid to a general quantum fluid, with the horizon size replaced by the specific volume of the fluid molecules.

To proceed, let us consider a little math about the self-reciprocal function at first. The simplest example is
y=x
. However, this generally corresponds to trivial solutions (namely
r_{s}=r_{l}
) except at the critical point. For our purpose, a simple but sufficiently non-trivial example is the reciprocal function
y=c/x
, where
c
is an arbitrarily positive constant. Actually this indeed gives the function
\varphi
for certain examples (such as the Gauss-Bonnet black hole and the quantum BTZ black hole, see section 5 and 6 for details). However, generally the function
\varphi
could be complicated. Nonetheless, we will adopt the following ansatz

r_{l}=\phi(r_{s})/r_{s}\qquad\mathrm{or}\qquad r_{s}=\phi(r_{l})/r_{l}\,.

(7)

Notice that since
\varphi=\varphi(r_{*})
, where
r_{*}
runs from the coexistent small black hole size to the large black hole size, one has

\phi=\phi(r_{*})\,.

(8)

However, unlike
\varphi
, this function is generally not self-reciprocal. Nevertheless, the above ansatz (
7
) is extremely useful. We will show that by substituting it into the original problem (
1
), the small-large black hole sizes will be determined by a single algebraic equation about the function
\phi(r_{*})
.

2.1
Van der Waals fluid

Before moving to certain black holes, let us show how the ansatz (
7
) works for a general quantum fluid. Here we consider a typical example: the Van der Waals (VdW) fluid. The equation of state reads
[
1
]
(we have set the Boltzmann constant
k_{B}=1
)

T=\Big(P+{\frac{a}{v^{2}}}\Big)(v-b)\,,

(9)

where
T
is the temperature,
P
the pressure and
v
the specific volume of the fluid molecule. The constant
b>0
describes the nonzero size corrections from the molecules whereas the constant
a>0
is a measure of the attraction between them. The critical point can be found from the inflection point condition, given by
[
1
]

T_{c}={\frac{8a}{27b}}\,,\quad v_{c}=3b\,,\quad P_{c}={\frac{a}{27b^{2}}}\,.

(10)

The Gibbs free energy reads

G=-T\left(1+\mathrm{ln}\Big[{\frac{(v-b)T^{3/2}}{\Phi}}\Big]\right)-{\frac{a}{v}}+Pv\,,

(11)

where
\Phi
is a dimensionful constant characterizing the gas. Since the absolute value of
\Phi
is unimportant, without loss of generality we will set
\Phi=1
. For simplicity, here and below, we work in normalized quantities
t=T/T_{c}\,,g=G/G_{c}
and
z=v/v_{c}\,,p=P/P_{c}
. The normalized temperature and the Gibbs free energy are given by

\displaystyle t(z)={\frac{(3z-1)(3+pz^{2})}{8z^{2}}}\,,

\displaystyle g(z)={\frac{\#}{z^{2}}}\left[2pz^{2}-36z+6-(3z-1)(3+pz^{2})\mathrm{ln}\Big({\frac{a^{3}(3z-1)^{5}(3+pz^{2})^{3}}{27^{3}bz^{6}}}\Big)\right]\,,

(12)

where
\#
stands for an unimportant numerical factor.

Figure 1:
The coexistence curve on the
T-P
plane for the VdW fluid. The solid line is the half-analytical solution whereas the dashed line stands for the numerical solution, which has been moved along the vertical axis slightly.

To solve the coexistence curve, we substitute the ansatz

z_{l}=\phi(z_{s})/z_{s}\qquad\mathrm{or}\qquad z_{s}=\phi(z_{l})/z_{l}\,,

(13)

into the equations

t(z_{s})=t(z_{l})\,,\qquad g(z_{s})=g(z_{l})\,.

(14)

From the first equation, we solve the pressure

p_{*}={\frac{(3z_{*}-1)\phi-z_{*}^{2}}{\phi^{2}z_{*}}}\,.

(15)

By plugging it into the second equation, we deduce

6(\phi-z_{*}^{2})\Big((6z_{*}-1)\phi-z_{*}^{2}\Big)+(z_{*}-3\phi)(3z_{*}-1)\,\mathrm{ln}\left[{\frac{(3\phi-z_{*})^{2}}{z_{*}^{2}(3z_{*}-1)^{2}}}\right]=0\,.

(16)

This is a single algebraic equation determining the function
\phi(z_{*})
. Once
\phi(z_{*})
is known, the coexistent temperature can be read off directly from

t_{*}={\frac{(3z_{*}-1)(3+p_{*}z_{*}^{2})}{8z_{*}^{2}}}\,.

(17)

In fact, this has already given the coexistence curve on the
T-v
plane since
z=v/v_{c}
. Moreover, combining the relations (
15
) and (
17
), one obtains the coexistence curve on the
T-P
plane. While for the VdW fluid, the function
\phi(z_{*})
cannot be solved exactly (owing to the logarithmic function), our approach generally provides an analytical method to study the phase structure. As depicted in Fig.
1
, the half-analytical solution is perfectly matched with the ordinary numerical solution obtained by solving (
14
) directly.

To end this section, we point out that our method is valid to a general quantum fluid exhibiting critical phenomenon as long as the equation of state and the Gibbs free energy are known analytically. This is of course the case for the holographic fluids dual to AdS black holes.

3
Charged AdS black hole in diverse dimensions

Consider the charged AdS black hole in general
D=d+3
dimensional AdS spacetimes
[
13
,
14
]

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+{\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}}+r^{2}d\Omega_{d+1}^{2}\,,\quad A={\frac{16\pi}{d\omega_{d+1}}}\left({\frac{Q}{r_{h}^{d}}}-{\frac{Q}{r^{d}}}\right)dt\,,

\displaystyle f(r)={\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}}+1-{\frac{16\pi M}{(d+1)\omega_{d+1}r^{d}}}+{\frac{128\pi^{2}Q^{2}}{d(d+1)\omega^{2}_{d+1}r^{2d}}}\,,

(18)

where
\omega_{d+1}
stands for the volume of a unit
(d+1)
-dimensional sphere,
r_{h}
the horizon radius,
\ell
the AdS radius and
Q
the electric charge carried by the black hole. The mass, the temperature, the entropy and the electrostatic potential are given by

\displaystyle M={\frac{\omega_{d+1}}{16\pi d}}\left(d(d+1)r_{h}^{d}(1+r_{h}^{2}\ell^{-2})+{\frac{128\pi^{2}Q^{2}}{\omega^{2}_{d+1}\,r_{h}^{d}}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle T={\frac{1}{4\pi r_{h}}}\left(d+(d+2)r_{h}^{2}\ell^{-2}-{\frac{128\pi^{2}Q^{2}}{(d+1)\omega^{2}_{d+1}r_{h}^{2d}}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle S={\frac{1}{4}}\omega_{d+1}r_{h}^{d+1}\,,\qquad\Phi={\frac{16\pi Q}{d\omega_{d+1}r_{h}^{d}}}\,,

(19)

Moreover, by identifying the cosmological constant to the thermodynamic pressure

P=-{\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi}}\,,\qquad\Lambda=-{\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{2\ell^{2}}}\,,

(20)

the thermodynamic first law could be extended to
[
1
,
15
]

dM=TdS+\Phi dQ+VdP\,,

(21)

where the conjugate volume is evaluated to be

V={\frac{\omega_{d+1}r_{h}^{d+2}}{d+2}}\,.

(22)

A remarkable feature of such extension is in canonical ensemble the small-large black hole transition is quite similar to that of a VdW fluid. The critical point occurs at
[
15
]

\displaystyle r_{c}^{d}={\frac{8\sqrt{2(2d+1)}\,\pi Q}{\sqrt{d}\,\omega_{d+1}}}\,,

\displaystyle P_{c}^{d}={\frac{d^{2d+1}\,\omega^{2}_{d+1}}{(2d+1)2^{7+4d}\,\pi^{2+d}Q^{2}}}\,,

\displaystyle T_{c}^{d}={\frac{d^{2d+{\frac{1}{2}}}\,\omega_{d+1}}{8\sqrt{2}(2d+1)^{d+{\frac{1}{2}}}\,\pi^{d+1}Q}}\,,

(23)

where
r_{c}
stands for the critical horizon radius. However, the coexistence line was only studied numerically in
[
1
,
15
]
. Later it was shown that in the four dimension, the coexistence line can be solved exactly by using the Maxwell’s area law
[
11
]
.

Here we will show that the coexistence line can be solved exactly in both the four and the five dimensions and can be analytically studied in general higher dimensions. Again we work with normalized quantities
z=r_{h}/r_{c}\,,t=T/T_{c}\,,p=P/P_{c}
. The normalized temperature and the Gibbs free energy
g=G/G_{c}
are given by

\displaystyle t(z)={\frac{(d+1)(2d+1)z^{2d}-1+d(2d+1)p\,z^{2d+2}}{4d(d+1)z^{2d+1}}}\,,

\displaystyle g(z)={\frac{(d+1)\big((d+1)z^{2d}+1\big)-d^{2}p\,z^{2d+2}}{4(d+1)z^{d}}}\,.

(24)

Then substituting the ansatz (
13
) into the equations (
14
), we solve the pressure at first

p_{*}={\frac{(d+2)z_{*}^{2}(\phi^{d}-z_{*}^{2d})\big((d+1)\phi^{d}-1\big)}{d^{2}\phi^{d}\big(\phi^{d+2}-z_{*}^{2d+4}\big)}}\,,

(25)

and the original problem (
14
) reduces to

\displaystyle d\Big((d+1)(2d+1)z_{*}^{2d}-1\Big)\phi^{3d+3}-2(d+1)^{2}(2d+1)z_{*}^{2d+2}\phi^{3d+2}

\displaystyle+(d+1)(d+2)(2d+1)z_{*}^{2d+4}\phi^{3d+1}+(d+2)(2d+1)z_{*}^{2d+2}\Big((d+1)z_{*}^{2d}+1\Big)\phi^{2d+2}

\displaystyle-2(d+1)^{2}z_{*}^{2d+4}\Big((2d+1)z_{*}^{2d}+1\Big)\phi^{2d+1}+d(d+1)(2d+1)z_{*}^{4d+6}\phi^{2d}

\displaystyle-2(d+1)^{2}z_{*}^{4d+2}\phi^{d+2}+(d+2)(2d+1)z_{*}^{4d+4}\phi^{d+1}-dz_{*}^{6d+6}=0\,,

(26)

which is a single algebraic equation about
\phi(z_{*})
. Clearly once the function
\phi(z_{*})
is solved, the coexistent pressure and temperature will be extracted immediately since

t_{*}={\frac{(d+1)(2d+1)z_{*}^{2d}-1+d(2d+1)p_{*}\,z_{*}^{2d+2}}{4d(d+1)z_{*}^{2d+1}}}\,.

(27)

This gives rise to a half-analytical result for the coexistence line in general dimensions. While the equation (
3
) looks quite complicated, it does lead to exact solutions in lower dimensions.

3.1
The four dimension

In the four dimension
d=1
, the equation (
3
) simplifies to

(\phi-z_{*}^{2})^{4}\Big((6z_{*}^{2}-1)\phi^{2}-4z_{*}^{2}\phi-z_{*}^{4}\Big)=0\,.

(28)

The solution
\phi=z_{*}^{2}
is trivial because of
z_{s}=z_{l}
. The nontrivial solution is determined by the second bracket, given by

\phi(z_{*})={\frac{z_{*}^{2}\big(2+\sqrt{6z_{*}^{2}+3}\,\big)}{6z_{*}^{2}-1}}\,.

(29)

As an example, it is straightforward to verify that if
z_{l}=\phi(z_{s})/z_{s}
, then
z_{s}=\phi(z_{l})/z_{l}
(but the function
\phi
itself is not self-reciprocal). That is the functional relation between the small-large black hole sizes is self-reciprocal, as expected.

It follows that the coexistent pressure and the temperature read

\displaystyle p_{*}={\frac{\big(\sqrt{6z_{*}^{2}+3}-2\big)^{2}}{z_{*}^{4}}}\,,

\displaystyle t_{*}={\frac{6z_{*}^{2}+5-3\sqrt{6z_{*}^{2}+3}}{2z_{*}^{3}}}\,.

(30)

Notice that the latter has already given a close formula for the coexistence curve on the
T-V
plane because of
z=(V/V_{c})^{1/3}
. Moreover, by converting the black hole sizes in terms of the pressure according to

\displaystyle z_{s}^{2}\,z_{l}^{2}={\frac{1}{p_{*}}}\,,

\displaystyle z_{s}^{2}+z_{l}^{2}={\frac{2\big(3-2\sqrt{p_{*}}\big)}{p_{*}}}\,,

(31)

and using the fact
t_{*}=\big(t(z_{s})+t(z_{l})\big)/2
, we arrive at a compact result on the
T-P
plane

t_{*}=\sqrt{{\frac{p_{*}(3-\sqrt{p_{*}})}{2}}}\,.

(32)

This reproduces the result firstly obtained in
[
11
]
.

3.2
The five dimension

In the five dimension, the equation (
3
) reduces to

2(\phi+z_{*}^{2})(\phi-z_{*}^{2})^{4}\Big((15z_{*}^{4}-1)\phi^{4}-3z_{*}^{2}\phi^{3}-7z_{*}^{4}\phi^{2}-3z_{*}^{6}\phi-z_{*}^{8}\Big)=0\,.

(33)

Clearly the nontrivial solution is determined by the large bracket. One finds

\phi(z_{*})={\frac{1}{12}}\left({\frac{9z_{*}^{2}}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}+\sqrt{\Delta_{1}}+\sqrt{\Delta_{2}}\right)\,,

(34)

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}={\frac{3z_{*}^{4}}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}\left[{\frac{840z_{*}^{4}-29}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}-16(90z_{*}^{4}-17)\Sigma^{-1/3}+2\Sigma^{1/3}\right]\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}={\frac{6z_{*}^{4}}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}\left[{\frac{840z_{*}^{4}-29}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}+8(90z_{*}^{4}-17)\Sigma^{-1/3}-\Sigma^{1/3}+{\frac{27z_{*}^{2}\big(1800z_{*}^{8}+180z_{*}^{4}-11\big)}{(15z_{*}^{4}-1)^{2}\sqrt{\Delta_{1}}}}\right]\,,

where

\Sigma=4\left[-3915z_{*}^{4}-101+9\sqrt{15\big(19200z_{*}^{12}+1735z_{*}^{8}+2706z_{*}^{4}-121\big)}\right]\,.

(36)

The coexistent pressure and the temperature are given by

\displaystyle p_{*}={\frac{(3\phi^{2}-1)z_{*}^{2}}{\phi^{2}(\phi^{2}+z_{*}^{4})}}\,,

\displaystyle t_{*}={\frac{15z_{*}^{4}-1+10p_{*}z_{*}^{6}}{24z_{*}^{5}}}\,.

(37)

Figure 2:
Left: the coexistence curve for the five dimensional charged black hole. The solid line is the analytical solution whereas the dashed line stands for the numerical solution, which has been moved along the vertical axis slightly. Right: the half-analytical coexistence curve for the
D=6
(blue),
D=7
(green ) and
D=8
(orange) dimensional charged black hole, respectively.

This gives the coexistence curve on the
T-P
plane analytically, which is perfectly matched with the numerical result, as shown in the left panel of Fig.
2
.

However, in
D\geq 6
higher dimensions, the function
\phi(z_{*})
cannot be solved exactly any longer. Nevertheless, as far as we check, our half-analytical solutions in general higher dimensions are matched well with the numerical results, see the right panel of Fig.
2
.

4
Rotating AdS black hole

Consider the Kerr-AdS black hole in the
D=4
dimensions (under the Boyer-Lindquist coordinates)

ds^{2}=\rho^{2}\Big({\frac{dr^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}}+{\frac{d\theta^{2}}{\Delta_{\theta}}}\Big)-{\frac{\Delta_{r}}{\rho^{2}}}\Big(dt-a\sin^{2}\theta{\frac{d\phi}{\Xi}}\Big)^{2}+{\frac{\Delta_{\theta}\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}}\Big(adt-(r^{2}+a^{2}){\frac{d\phi}{\Xi}}\Big)^{2}\,,

(38)

where

\displaystyle\rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{r}=(r^{2}+a^{2})(1+g^{2}r^{2})-2M\Xi^{2}r\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\theta}=1-g^{2}a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,,\quad\Xi=1-g^{2}a^{2}\,,

(39)

where
g=1/\ell
. The various thermodynamic quantities are given by

\displaystyle M={\frac{(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})(1+g^{2}a^{2})}{2\Xi^{2}r_{h}}}\,,

\displaystyle T={\frac{(3g^{2}r_{h}^{2}+1)r_{h}^{2}+(g^{2}r_{h}^{2}-1)a^{2}}{4\pi r_{h}(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})}}\,,\quad S={\frac{\pi(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})}{\Xi}}\,,

\displaystyle J=Ma\,,\qquad\Omega={\frac{(1+g^{2}r_{h}^{2})a}{r_{h}^{2}+a^{2}}}\,,

\displaystyle V={\frac{2\pi(r_{h}^{2}+a^{2})\left(2r_{h}^{2}-(g^{2}r_{h}^{2}-1)a^{2}\right)}{3\Xi^{2}r_{h}}}\,.

(40)

where
J
is the angular momenta,
\Omega
the angular velocity and
V
the thermodynamic volume. The first law is extended to
dM=TdS+\Omega dJ+VdP
.

To study critical phenomenon of the solution, we shall consider the leading corrections from the angular momenta. Define the specific volume of the black hole
[
15
]

v\equiv 2\left({\frac{3V}{4\pi}}\right)^{1/3}\,.

(41)

The equation of state simplifies to

T=Pv+{\frac{1}{2\pi v}}-{\frac{48J^{2}}{\pi v^{5}}}+O(J^{4})\,.

(42)

In this approximation, the critical point occurs at

v_{c}=2\times 90^{1/4}\sqrt{J}\,,\quad T_{c}={\frac{90^{3/4}}{225\pi\sqrt{J}}}\,,\quad P_{c}={\frac{1}{12\sqrt{90}\,\pi J}}\,.

(43)

Figure 3:
The coexistence curve for the rotating AdS black hole. The solid line is the analytical solution whereas the dashed line stands for the numerical solution, which has been moved along the vertical axis slightly.

The Gibbs free energy in the same approximation reads

G={\frac{v}{8}}-{\frac{\pi Pv^{3}}{12}}+{\frac{20J^{2}}{v^{3}}}\,.

(44)

The normalized temperature and the Gibbs free energy are given by

\displaystyle t={\frac{15z^{4}-1+10pz^{6}}{24z^{5}}}\,,

\displaystyle g={\frac{9z^{4}+1-2pz^{6}}{8z^{3}}}\,,

(45)

where
z=v/v_{c}
. Again by plugging the ansatz (
13
) into the equations (
14
), we find

p_{*}={\frac{(15z_{*}^{4}-1)\phi^{4}-z_{*}^{2}\phi^{3}-z_{*}^{4}\phi^{2}-z_{*}^{6}\phi-z_{*}^{8}}{10\phi^{5}z_{*}^{4}}}\,,

(46)

where the function
\phi
is determined by a quartic algebraic equation

(15z_{*}^{4}-1)\phi^{4}-4z_{*}^{2}\phi^{3}-5z_{*}^{4}\phi^{2}-4z_{*}^{6}\phi-z_{*}^{8}=0\,.

(47)

The physical solution can be solved exactly as

\phi(z_{*})={\frac{1}{6}}\left({\frac{6z_{*}^{2}}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}+\sqrt{\Delta_{1}}+\sqrt{\Delta_{2}}\right)\,,

(48)

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}={\frac{3z_{*}^{4}}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}\left[{\frac{2(75z_{*}^{4}+1)}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}-(180z_{*}^{4}+11)\Sigma^{-1/3}+\Sigma^{1/3}\right]\,,

(49)

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}={\frac{3z_{*}^{4}}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}\left[{\frac{4(75z_{*}^{4}+1)}{15z_{*}^{4}-1}}+(180z_{*}^{4}+11)\Sigma^{-1/3}-\Sigma^{1/3}+{\frac{36z_{*}^{2}\big(450z_{*}^{8}+15z_{*}^{4}+1\big)}{(15z_{*}^{4}-1)^{2}\sqrt{\Delta_{1}}}}\right]\,,

where

\Sigma=540z_{*}^{4}-17+18\sqrt{5\big(3600z_{*}^{12}+840z_{*}^{8}+29z_{*}^{4}+1\big)}\,.

(50)

The coexistent temperature can be read off as

t_{*}={\frac{15z_{*}^{4}-1+10p_{*}z_{*}^{6}}{24z_{*}^{5}}}\,.

(51)

As depicted in Fig.
3
, our analytical solution is perfectly matched with the numerical result reported in
[
15
]
.

5
Gauss-Bonnet black hole

Consider the Gauss-Bonnet black hole in the five dimension

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+dr^{2}/f(r)+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}_{3}\,,

\displaystyle f(r)=1+{\frac{r^{2}}{4\alpha}}\Big[1-\sqrt{1-8\alpha\ell^{-2}+{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 64\alpha GM}{\scriptstyle 3\pi r^{4}}}}}\,\Big]\,,

(52)

where
\ell
stands for the bare AdS radius and
\alpha
is the Gauss-Bonnet coupling, having dimension of length square. In holography, causality of the boundary theory constrains
0<\alpha<9\ell^{2}/200

[
16
,
17
]
. The event horizon is defined by the largest real root of the equation
f(r_{h})=0
. Using standard method, the mass, the entropy and the temperature can be evaluated as

\displaystyle M={\frac{3\pi(r_{h}^{4}+r_{h}^{2}\ell^{2}+2\alpha\ell^{2})}{8\ell^{2}}}\,,

(53)

\displaystyle S={\frac{\pi^{2}r_{h}^{3}}{2}}\Big(1+{\frac{12\alpha}{r_{h}^{2}}}\Big)\,,

(54)

\displaystyle T={\frac{r_{h}(2r_{h}^{2}+\ell^{2})}{2\pi\ell^{2}(r_{h}^{2}+4\alpha)}}\,.

(55)

Moreover, by identifying the bare cosmological constant to the thermodynamic pressure
P=6\ell^{-2}/8\pi
and varying the Gauss-Bonnet coupling, the first law of thermodynamics can be extended to
[
18
]

dM=TdS+VdP+Ud\alpha\,,

(56)

where the thermodynamic volume and the chemical potential conjugate to
\alpha
are given by

V={\frac{\pi^{2}r_{h}^{4}}{2}}\,,\qquad U=-{\frac{3\pi\big(8r_{h}^{4}+3r_{h}^{2}\ell^{2}-4\alpha\ell^{2}\big)}{4\ell^{2}(r_{h}^{2}+4\alpha)}}\,.

(57)

The P-V criticality of the solution for a fixing
\alpha
was previously numerically studied in
[
18
]
. Here we shall derive the coexistence line analytically. The critical point occurs at

r_{c}=2\sqrt{3\alpha}\,,\quad T_{c}={\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{3\alpha}}}\,,\quad P_{c}={\frac{1}{96\pi\alpha}}\,.

(58)

The normalized temperature and the Gibbs free energy are given by

\displaystyle t={\frac{z(3+pz^{2})}{3z^{2}+1}}\,,

\displaystyle g={\frac{-6z^{4}+3z^{2}-1+pz^{4}(z^{2}+3)}{3z^{2}+1}}\,.

(59)

Substituting the ansatz (
13
) into the equations (
14
) yields

p_{*}={\frac{3(3\phi-1)z_{*}^{2}}{z_{*}^{4}+(3\phi+1)\phi z_{*}^{2}+\phi^{2}}}\,.

(60)

Interestingly, nontrivial solution of the function
\phi(z_{*})
turns out to be a simple constant
\phi(z_{*})=1
. This leads to

\displaystyle p_{*}={\frac{6z_{*}^{2}}{z_{*}^{4}+4z_{*}^{2}+1}}\,,

\displaystyle t_{*}={\frac{z_{*}(3+p_{*}z_{*}^{2})}{3z_{*}^{2}+1}}\,.

(61)

Furthermore, by converting the black hole sizes in terms of the pressure according to

\displaystyle z_{s}^{2}z_{l}^{2}=1\,,

\displaystyle z_{s}^{2}+z_{l}^{2}={\frac{6-4p_{*}}{p_{*}}}\,,

(62)

and using the fact
t_{*}=\big(t(z_{s})+t(z_{l})\big)/2
, we arrive at a compact formula on the
T-P
plane

t_{*}=\sqrt{{\frac{p_{*}(3-p_{*})}{2}}}\,.

(63)

The result is depicted in Fig.
4
and is perfectly matched with the numerical result reported in
[
18
]
.

6
quantum BTZ black hole

Our last example is the
U-\nu
criticality of the quantum BTZ (qBTZ) black hole studied in
[
12
]
, where

\nu\equiv\ell/\ell_{3}\,,

(64)

is a dimensionless parameter characterizing the strength of quantum backreactions of conformal fields in AdS
3
. Here
\ell_{3}
is the bare AdS
3
radius and
\ell
a length parameter inversely proportional to the brane tension, describing the position of the Karch-Randall brane in the classical AdS
4
C-metric
[
19
]
.

The mass, the temperature and the entropy of the solution are given by
[
19
]

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{\sqrt{1+\nu^{2}}}{2G_{3}}}{\frac{z^{2}(1-z^{3}\nu)(1+z\nu)}{(1+3z^{2}+2z^{3}\nu)^{2}}}\,,

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{z(2+3\nu z+\nu z^{3})}{2\pi l_{3}(1+3z^{2}+2\nu z^{3})}}\,,

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{\pi l_{3}}{G_{3}}}{\frac{z\sqrt{1+\nu^{2}}}{1+3z^{2}+2\nu z^{3}}}\,,

(65)

where
G_{3}
is the bare Newton’s constant in AdS
3
. Notice that (unlike previous sections) here
z
is inversely proportional to the event horizon radius
r_{h}

\displaystyle z\equiv{\frac{l_{3}}{r_{h}x_{1}}}\,,

(66)

where
x_{1}
is a parameter describing the truncated AdS
4
C-metric having a finite black hole in the bulk. Notice that both
z
and
\nu
runs in the region
(0\,,+\infty)
.
It is straightforward to test that the ordinary first law
dM=TdS
holds.

Previously extended thermodynamics of the solution was partly studied in the literature
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
by varying
G_{3}
and
\ell_{3}
. However, it was realized later that emergence of cricality of the solution heavily relies on the quantum backreaction parameter
\nu
. In
[
12
]
,
\nu
is treated as a thermodynamic variable directly while
G_{3}
and
\ell_{3}
are held fixed. In this case, the first law is extended to

\displaystyle dM=TdS+Ud\nu\,,

(67)

where the chemical potential
U
reads

U=-{\frac{z^{2}\left(\nu+z^{4}\nu^{3}+z(\nu^{2}-1)+z^{3}(3\nu^{2}+1)\right)}{2G_{3}\sqrt{1+\nu^{2}}(1+3z^{2}+2z^{3}\nu)^{2}}}\,.

(68)

Despite that the thermodynamic pressure
P
and the central charge
C
are not written explicitly in the extended first law, they actually vary as
\nu
varies
[
22
]
and their effects are encoded in the conjugate chemical potential
U
.

The critical point appears at

\displaystyle z_{c}=1\,,\qquad\nu_{c}=1\,,\qquad T_{c}={\frac{1}{2\pi l_{3}}}\,.

(69)

In particular, it was established in
[
12
]
the small-large black hole transition exists for both
\nu<\nu_{c}
(
T<T_{c}
) and
\nu>\nu_{c}
(
T>T_{c}
).

plane. The dashed line stands for the numerical solution, which has been moved along the vertical axis slightly.

To proceed, we work with the normalized temperature and the free energy

t=T/T_{c}\,,f=F/F_{c}
:

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{z(2+3z\nu+z^{3}\nu)}{1+3z^{2}+2z^{3}\nu}}\,,

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{3\sqrt{2}\sqrt{1+\nu^{2}}z^{2}\Big(1+z\nu\big(2+z^{2}(2+z\nu)\big)\Big)}{\Big(1+z^{2}(3+2z\nu)\Big)^{2}}}\,.

(70)

Substituting the ansatz (
13
) into the equations (
14
) yields

\nu={\frac{1}{4(3-\phi)\phi^{2}z_{*}^{3}}}\left(z_{*}^{6}+3(\phi^{2}-\phi+1)z_{*}^{4}+3\phi(\phi^{2}-\phi+1)z_{*}^{2}+\phi^{3}\pm\sqrt{\Pi}\right)\,,

(71)

where

\displaystyle\Pi=\left(z_{*}^{4}+(3\phi^{2}-8\phi+3)z_{*}^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(z_{*}^{8}+(3\phi^{2}+2\phi+3)z_{*}^{6}+6\phi(\phi^{2}+3\phi+1)z_{*}^{4}+\phi^{2}(3\phi^{2}+2\phi+3)z_{*}^{2}+\phi^{4}\right)\,.

(72)

Despite that the result looks complicated, nontrivial solution to the function
\phi(z_{*})
turns out to be a simple constant
\phi(z_{*})=1
surprisingly. This gives rise to

\displaystyle\nu={\frac{1}{8}}\left[(z_{*}+1/z_{*})^{3}\pm\sqrt{(z_{*}+1/z_{*})^{6}-64}\right]\,,

(73)

where the “
\pm
” sign corresponds to
\nu\geq 1
and
\nu\leq 1
, respectively.
Substituting the result into the equation of state, we derive the coexistence curve on the
t-\nu
plane (see Fig.
5
)

t_{*}(\nu)={\frac{z_{*}(\nu)\Big(2+3z_{*}(\nu)\nu+z_{*}^{3}(\nu)\nu\Big)}{1+3z_{*}^{2}(\nu)+2z_{*}^{3}(\nu)\nu}}\,,

(74)

where

z_{*}(\nu)={\frac{1}{2}}\left(\varphi(\nu)\pm\sqrt{\varphi(\nu)^{2}-4}\,\right)\,,\qquad\varphi(\nu)=\Big[4(\nu+1/\nu)\Big]^{1/3}\,,

(75)

where the sign “
\pm
” corresponds to the coexistent small and large black hole respectively. This reproduces the result reported in
[
12
]
. As depicted in Fig.
5
, it is perfectly matched with the numerical solution.

829. Voluntary exercise attenuates toluene conditioned place preference and restores reward neurocircuitry in rats
Authors: J L Asis, A Carampel, J Munar, C G Gregorio, L M Dalmacio, G Quirk, R Cena-Navarro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic use of inhalants in adolescents can cause persistent behavioral impairments, highlighting the urgent need for accessible, low-cost treatments. We recently reported that repeated inhalation of 3000 ppm toluene increased conditioned place preference (CPP) (Asis et al., 2024).Aims & ObjectivesOur aim is to assess whether voluntary exercise could decrease toluene CPP and restore the brain to its pre-addiction state.MethodWe exposed male (n = 27) and female (n = 27) adolescent SD rats to 3000 ppm toluene vapor over a 12-day period. They were then assigned to either an exercise group (Tol-Run) or a sedentary group (Tol-Sed). During the abstinence period (Day 1 to Day 7), rats in the exercise group had daily access to a movable running wheel (4 h/day), while sedentary rats were provided a fixed wheel. CPP was performed on Day 8, and brains were collected the same day for cFos immunohistochemistry.ResultsIn toluene-exposed rats, females spent much more time running than males (t29 = 7.43, p < 0.001). On D8 of abstinence, the total distance run by the females was negatively correlated with the CPP score (p = 0.007). Exercise also prevented the incubation of CPP from D1 to D8 in females (unpaired t-test: p = 0.036), but not in males (p = 0.241). We then performed cFos immunofluorescence and found that toluene significantly increased cFos density in the nucleus accumbens core (p = 0.015), dorsal CA1 (p = 0.036), and hilus (p = 0.011) in females, but not in males. These effects were reversed by exercise (nucleus accumbens core: p = 0.007, dorsal CA1: p = 0.055, hilus: p = 0.025). In males, exercise significantly decreased cFos density in the prelimbic (p = 0.024) and infralimbic prefrontal cortices (p = 0.016).Discussion & ConclusionsExercise may be a promising intervention for preventing relapse in inhalant use disorder, especially in females.

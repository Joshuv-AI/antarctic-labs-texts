Latent Objective Induction and Diversity-Constrained Selection: Algorithms for Multi-Locale Retrieval Pipelines
Faruk Alpay, Levent Sarioglu

Abstract
We present three algorithms with formal correctness guarantees and complexity bounds for the problem of selecting a diverse, multi-locale set of sources from ranked search results. First, we formulate weighted locale allocation as a constrained integer partition problem and give an O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n\log n) algorithm that simultaneously satisfies minimum-representation, budget-exhaustion, and proportionality-bound constraints; we prove all three hold with a tight deviation bound of < 1 <1 . Second, we define a cascaded country-code inference function as a deterministic priority chain over heterogeneous signals (TLD structure, model-inferred metadata, language fallback) and prove it satisfies both determinism and graceful degradation. Third, we introduce a κ \kappa -domain diversity constraint for source selection and give an O ⁡ ( | K | ⋅ R ) O(|K|\cdot R) algorithm that maintains the invariant via hash-map lookup, eliminating the aggregator monopolization pathology present in URL-level deduplication. We further formalize Latent Objective Induction (LOI), an environment-shaping operator over prompt spaces that steers downstream model behavior without restricting the feasible output set, and prove its convergence under mild assumptions. Applied to a multi-locale retrieval pipeline, these algorithms yield 62% improvement in first-party source ratio and 89% reduction in same-domain duplication across 120 multilingual queries.



1
Introduction

We study algorithmic problems arising in the design of multi-source retrieval pipelines where ranked lists from heterogeneous search indices must be merged into a single result set satisfying formal diversity and coverage constraints.

The core computational challenge is a
constrained selection problem
: given
|K|
keyword-specific ranked lists of sources, select at most
S_{\max}
sources such that (i) no publisher domain appears more than
\kappa
times, (ii) the total budget of keyword slots is distributed across
n
geopolitical locales proportionally to assigned weights with bounded deviation, and (iii) the country of origin of each selected source is inferred through a deterministic priority chain over signals of varying reliability. Naïve approaches—URL-level deduplication, single-locale search, heuristic country assignment—fail to satisfy these constraints simultaneously, leading to pathological outcomes such as aggregator monopolization
[
Shah and Bao 2024
]
and locale homogeneity.

This paper makes four contributions, ordered by their algorithmic character:

1.

A
weighted locale allocation
algorithm (Section
5
) that solves a constrained integer partition in
O(n\log n)
with provable correctness on three simultaneous constraints.

2.

A
cascaded country-code inference
function (Section
6
) with formal determinism and graceful degradation properties.

3.

A
\kappa
-domain diversity constraint
and selection algorithm (Section
7
) running in
O(|K|\cdot R)
with
O(1)
amortized per-domain lookup.

4.

The
Latent Objective Induction
(LOI) operator (Section
4
), formalized as an environment-shaping map with four axiomatic properties and a convergence theorem.

We instantiate these algorithms in a multi-locale retrieval pipeline (Section
8
) and evaluate on 120 queries spanning four languages (Section
9
), demonstrating that the combined system achieves 62% improvement in first-party source ratio and 89% reduction in domain duplication over a baseline lacking these algorithmic components.

Related Work

Constrained Selection and Partition Algorithms.

Our weighted locale allocation relates to the apportionment problem in social choice theory and constrained integer partition. The largest-remainder method (Hamilton’s method) for proportional seat allocation shares our three-phase structure. In information retrieval, PM-2
[
Dang and Croft 2012
]
formulates diversification as proportional allocation of subtopic coverage, though without our minimum-representation guarantee. Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR)
[
Carbonell and Goldstein 1998
]
penalizes semantic similarity to already-selected documents; xQuAD
[
Santos et al. 2010
]
models subtopic coverage explicitly. Both operate on embedding-level similarity, whereas our
\kappa
-domain constraint enforces diversity at the
structural
(domain) level—a property invisible to vector-space methods.

Priority Chains and Cascaded Inference.

Cascaded classifier architectures
[
Robertson and Zaragoza 2009
]
apply progressively expensive models. Our cascade differs: rather than filtering candidates, it resolves a
single attribute
(country code) through a deterministic priority chain over heterogeneous signal sources, with formal degradation guarantees.

Multilingual Retrieval.

Cross-language information retrieval (CLIR)
[
Oard 1998
]
translates queries or uses multilingual encoders
[
Zhang et al. 2022
,
Lawrie et al. 2023
]
. We instead generate locale-specific keywords in the target language, exploiting each locale’s native search index.

LLM-Augmented Retrieval.

RAG
[
Lewis et al. 2020
]
conditions generation on retrieved documents. Query expansion via LLMs
[
Wang et al. 2023
]
improves recall. Our pipeline extends this with a structured
strategic document
(research brief) consumed by downstream stages.

Mechanism Design and Nudge Theory.

In game theory, mechanism design
[
Hurwicz and Reiter 2006
]
constructs rules so that self-interested agents produce desired outcomes. Nudge theory
[
Thaler and Sunstein 2008
]
shapes choice architecture so the preferred option becomes the default. Our LOI operator applies these ideas to the algorithmic design of prompt environments.

4
Latent Objective Induction

We formalize the central architectural principle before presenting specific algorithms, as it governs the design of all downstream components.

4.1
Motivation: Explicit vs. Latent Steering

Consider the task of steering an LLM to prefer first-party sources over aggregators. Two paradigms exist:

Definition 4.1

(Explicit Constraint)
.

An
explicit constraint

C
is a rule injected into the system prompt of the form: “Do not select sources from domain
d
” or “Prefer
X
over
Y
.” Formally,
C
restricts the feasible output set
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{M}
by enumeration.

Definition 4.2

(Latent Objective Induction)
.

A
latent objective

\phi
is an objective embedded in the prompt environment
\mathcal{E}
such that the model’s generation probability satisfies:

P_{\mathcal{M}}\bigl[y\in\mathcal{F}_{\phi}\mid\mathcal{E}(\phi)\bigr]>P_{\mathcal{M}}\bigl[y\in\mathcal{F}_{\phi}\mid\mathcal{E}_{0}\bigr]

(1)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
is the set of outputs satisfying objective
\phi
,
\mathcal{E}(\phi)
is the shaped environment, and
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is the unmodified environment. Crucially,
\phi
is
never stated as a directive
; it is induced through the structure of
\mathcal{E}
.

Example 4.3

(First-Party Preference)
.

Rather than instructing “prefer first-party sources over aggregators” (explicit constraint naming specific entities), we embed the concept into the upstream planning stage: “First-party sources—where the entity itself publishes—rank above third-party sources that collect, aggregate, or review information about entities.” No domain name is mentioned. No enumeration of aggregators exists. The model learns a
concept
and applies it to arbitrary domains it encounters.

4.2
Formal Framework

Let
\Theta
denote the space of all possible prompt environments. Define the
environment-shaping operator
:

\Phi:\Theta\times\mathcal{P}(\text{Objectives})\to\Theta

(2)

mapping an environment
\theta\in\Theta
and an objective set
O
to a shaped environment
\Phi(\theta,O)
.

Definition 4.4

(LOI Operator Properties)
.

An LOI operator
\Phi
is
well-formed
if it satisfies:

(i)

Autonomy Preservation:

\mathcal{F}_{\Phi(\theta,O)}=\mathcal{F}_{\theta}
— the feasible output set is unchanged. The model
can
still select any output; only probabilities shift.

(ii)

Intent Opacity:
No substring of
\Phi(\theta,O)
explicitly names a target domain, entity, or output value that
\phi
intends to promote or suppress.

(iii)

Monotone Induction:
For objective
\phi\in O
, the probability
P_{\mathcal{M}}[y\in\mathcal{F}_{\phi}\mid\Phi(\theta,O)]
is monotonically non-decreasing in the specificity of
\phi
’s embedding.

(iv)

Composability:
For disjoint objectives
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in O
:

\Phi(\theta,\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\})=\Phi(\Phi(\theta,\{\phi_{1}\}),\{\phi_{2}\})

(3)

Theorem 4.5

(Convergence of LOI)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an LLM with instruction-following capability
\alpha\in(0,1]
(the probability that the model follows a correctly understood directive). Let
\phi
be an objective with semantic clarity
\beta\in(0,1]
(the probability that the concept embedding is correctly interpreted). Under LOI with
k
independent downstream stages each consuming the shaped environment, the probability that at least one stage produces an output satisfying
\phi
is:

P[\exists\,i\in[k]:y_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{\phi}]=1-(1-\alpha\beta)^{k}

(4)

which converges to
1
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

Each stage independently samples from
\mathcal{M}(\cdot\mid\Phi(\theta,\{\phi\}))
. The probability that stage
i
produces
y_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{\phi}
is at least
\alpha\beta
by definition of instruction-following capability and semantic clarity. Stage outcomes are conditionally independent given the shared environment. The probability that
no
stage succeeds is
(1-\alpha\beta)^{k}
, giving the result.
∎

Remark 4.6

(Contrast with Explicit Constraints)
.

Under explicit constraints, the feasible set shrinks:
|\mathcal{F}_{C}|<|\mathcal{F}_{\theta}|
. This introduces two failure modes: (a)
over-restriction
, where legitimate outputs are blocked (e.g., banning a domain that is occasionally the best source), and (b)
brittleness
, where new entities not covered by the enumeration bypass the constraint entirely. LOI avoids both by preserving the feasible set while shifting probability mass.

The allocation’s input weights derive from the research brief’s locale mix

Definition 5.4

(Normalized Locale Mix)
.

Given raw mix

(i)

Cascaded Country-Code Inference

6.1
Inference Function

Definition 6.1

(Country-Code Inference)
.

The inference function
\gamma:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{G}\cup\{\varepsilon\}
maps a source to a country code (or empty) via the following priority chain, returning the result of the first satisfied condition:

\gamma(s)=\begin{cases}\text{Override}(\operatorname{tld}(u))&\text{if }\operatorname{tld}(u)\in\mathcal{O}\\
\operatorname{tld}(u)&\text{if }|\operatorname{tld}(u)|=2\wedge\operatorname{tld}(u)\notin\mathcal{G}_{\text{gen}}\\
p&\text{if }\operatorname{tld}(u)\in\mathcal{G}_{\text{gen}}\wedge p\neq\varepsilon\\
\Lambda(\ell)&\text{if }\operatorname{tld}(u)\in\mathcal{G}_{\text{gen}}\wedge p=\varepsilon\wedge\ell\neq\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(15)

where
\mathcal{O}
is the TLD override map (e.g.,
\texttt{uk}\mapsto\texttt{gb}
),
p
is the model-inferred publisher country,
\ell
is the source language, and
\Lambda:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{G}
is the language-to-country fallback map.

Proposition 6.2

(Determinism)
.

For fixed inputs
(u,p,\ell)
, the cascade is deterministic: the first satisfied condition uniquely determines
\gamma
.

Proof.

The conditions form a strict priority chain with mutually exclusive triggers at each level. Specifically, Priority 1 is checked before Priority 2, and its condition (
\operatorname{tld}(u)\in\mathcal{O}
) either holds or does not, yielding a unique path through the chain.
∎

Proposition 6.3

(Graceful Degradation)
.

If all signals are absent (generic TLD, no model metadata, no language signal),
\gamma(s)=\varepsilon
. No incorrect inference is produced.

6.2
Signal Reliability Ordering

The priority ordering reflects empirical signal reliability:

Lemma 6.4

(TLD Reliability)
.

Country-code TLDs (e.g.,
.de
,
.tr
) have accuracy
\geq 0.95
for publisher country inference, as domain registration under country-code TLDs typically requires local presence or targeting intent. Generic TLDs carry no geographic signal (
\text{accuracy}\approx 0.5
).

This motivates placing TLD-based inference at the highest priority and model-inferred metadata (which depends on LLM accuracy) at a lower tier.

7.3
Interaction with LOI

The domain diversity constraint operates at the
algorithmic
level (a structural guarantee), while LOI operates at the
probabilistic
level (shifting model preferences). These are complementary:

•

LOI shapes the relevance model’s scoring so that first-party sources receive higher relevance scores, reducing the
frequency
with which aggregators are top-ranked.

•

The diversity constraint acts as a safety net: even if LOI fails for a particular keyword (the model still ranks an aggregator first), the constraint prevents multiple selections from that domain.

Theorem 7.4

(Combined Effectiveness)
.

Let
p_{a}
be the probability that an aggregator is top-ranked for a given keyword under LOI, and
p_{a}^{0}
the baseline probability without LOI (
p_{a}\leq p_{a}^{0}
). With
|K|
keywords and
\kappa=1
, the expected number of aggregator sources in
S
is:

\mathbb{E}[\card\{s\in S:\text{is\_aggregator}(s)\}]\leq\min\bigl(|K|\cdot p_{a},\;|\mathcal{D}_{a}|\bigr)

(17)

where
\mathcal{D}_{a}
is the set of distinct aggregator domains in the index. With
\kappa=1
, this is further bounded by
|\mathcal{D}_{a}|
, which is typically small (
\leq 3
).

10
Discussion

10.1
Generality of the Algorithms

The three core algorithms are not specific to web search. The weighted locale allocation (Algorithm
1
) solves any constrained integer partition where items must be distributed across categories with minimum-representation, budget, and proportionality constraints—applicable to resource allocation, task scheduling, and proportional representation systems. The
\kappa
-domain diversity constraint (Algorithm
2
) generalizes to any ranked-list merging problem where a grouping function partitions candidates into equivalence classes and at most
\kappa
representatives per class are permitted. The cascaded inference (Definition
6.1
) provides a template for any multi-signal attribute resolution where signals have known reliability ordering.

10.2
LOI as Mechanism Design for LLMs

LOI inherits from mechanism design
[
Hurwicz and Reiter 2006
]
the principle that system rules should align agent incentives with designer objectives. In classical mechanism design, the designer cannot control agents’ actions but can design the game structure. Analogously, we cannot control the LLM’s token generation, but we can design the prompt environment. The key insight is that LLMs are
cooperative agents
—they attempt to follow instructions—so LOI exploits this cooperativeness by providing conceptual frameworks rather than explicit directives, enabling generalization to novel inputs.

The connection to nudge theory
[
Thaler and Sunstein 2008
]
is equally illuminating. Thaler and Sunstein’s “libertarian paternalism” preserves freedom of choice while designing the choice architecture to favor beneficial outcomes. Our autonomy preservation property (Definition
4.4
(i)) is the formal analog: the model’s output space remains unrestricted, but the probability landscape tilts toward desired outcomes.

10.3
Limitations

Our approach inherits limitations of the underlying search API: if the search index lacks first-party sources, diversification cannot surface them. The research brief is generated by Phi-4
[
Abdin et al. 2024
]
, a 14B-parameter model, which may produce shallow strategies for highly specialized domains. The cascaded inference assumes TLD reliability, which fails for CDN-hosted content. LOI’s effectiveness depends on the downstream model’s semantic comprehension of the embedded concepts; smaller models may fail to interpret nuanced strategy text.

11
Conclusion

We have presented four algorithms for the constrained multi-source selection problem in retrieval pipelines. The weighted locale allocation solves a constrained integer partition in
O(n\log n)
with provable simultaneous satisfaction of minimum-representation, budget-exhaustion, and proportionality constraints. The cascaded country-code inference provides deterministic attribute resolution with formal graceful degradation. The
\kappa
-domain diversity constraint eliminates aggregator monopolization through
O(1)
amortized hash-map lookups. The Latent Objective Induction operator, formalized with four axiomatic properties, provides a principled alternative to explicit constraint injection for steering downstream computation, with convergence guarantees under mild assumptions. Empirical evaluation confirms that these algorithms compose effectively: the combined system achieves 62% improvement in first-party source ratio and 89% reduction in domain duplication. The algorithms are general—the allocation and diversity constraint apply to any ranked-list merging problem with coverage requirements, and LOI applies to any multi-stage pipeline with LLM-mediated decisions.

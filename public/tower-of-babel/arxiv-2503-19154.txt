Global minimizers for fast diffusion versus nonlocal interactions on negatively curved manifolds
José A. Carrillo, Razvan C. Fetecau, Hansol Park

Abstract
. We investigate the existence of ground states for a free energy functional on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds. The energy, which consists of an entropy and an interaction term, is associated to a macroscopic aggregation model that includes nonlinear diffusion and nonlocal interactions. We consider specifically the regime of fast diffusion, and establish necessary and sufficient conditions on the behaviour of the interaction potential for global energy minimizers to exist. We first consider the case of manifolds with constant bounds of sectional curvatures, then extend the results to manifolds with general curvature bounds. To establish our results we derive several new Carlson-Levin type inequalities for Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

1.
Introduction

In this work we consider a generic Cartan-Hadamard manifold
M
, and investigate the existence of global minimizers of the following free energy functional:

(1.1)

\displaystyle E[\mu]=\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{M\times M}W(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y),

where
0<q<1
,
W:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is an attractive interaction potential, and
\mathrm{d}V
denotes the Riemannian volume measure on
M
. The energy is defined on the space
\mathcal{P}(M)
of probability measures on
M
, and
\rho
represents the absolutely continuous part of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
with respect to
\mathrm{d}V
. The first component of the energy is entropy, which favours spreading, and the second is an interaction energy, which in our case promotes aggregation, at least for long distances.

The free energy (
1.1
) relates to the following evolution equation on
L^{1}(M)\cap\mathcal{P}(M)
:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho(x)-\nabla_{M}\cdot(\rho(x)\nabla_{M}W*\rho(x))=\Delta\rho^{q}(x),

where

W*\rho(x)=\int_{M}W(x,y)\mathrm{d}\rho(y),

and
\nabla_{M}\cdot
and
\nabla_{M}
represent the manifold divergence and gradient, respectively. Equation (
1.2
) can in fact be formally expressed as the gradient flow of the energy
E
on a suitable Wasserstein space
[
2
]
.

Energies in the form (
1.1
) and the associated evolution equation (
1.2
), known as the aggregation-diffusion equation, have a wide range of applications in mathematical biology. For instance, nonlinear diffusion versions of the classical Keller-Segel model for chemotaxis or swarming models (see
[
5
]
and the references therein) have been well studied. Such models also appear as paradigms of social interactions where they can lead to segregation of individuals into clusters of opinions, see
[
26
,
27
]
for instance. For specific manifold setups, we note the work in
[
19
]
, where the authors used the Onsager model to analyze phase transitions phenomena on the two dimensional sphere. Finally, consensus of large number of individuals moving under constraints, modelled by aggregation-diffusion equations on manifolds, can also find applications in control engineering or swarming models with body-attitude coordination, see
[
16
]
.

The range
0<q<1
of the nonlinear diffusion exponent is referred to in the literature as
fast
diffusion, while
q=1
and
q>1
correspond to
linear
and
slow
diffusion, respectively; note that for linear diffusion, the entropy term in (
1.1
) is replaced by
\int_{M}\rho(x)\log\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)
. The fast diffusion is faster than the linear/slow diffusion for small values of the density while it is actually slower for large densities (concentrations). Consequently, fast diffusion comes with an enhanced spreading at infinity, but at the same time, by competing with the nonlocal attractive interactions in our model, it may not be able to prevent Dirac delta aggregations from forming
[
7
]
. This is in contrast with the linear and slow diffusion cases.

The free energy (
1.1
) and the aggregation-diffusion equation (
1.2
) have been extensively studied in the Euclidean setup
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A recent review on the subject can be found in
[
5
]
. In
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
13
]
the authors considered an interaction potential of Riesz type, i.e.,
W(x,y)=\frac{1}{\beta}\|x-y\|^{\beta}
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm and established existence or non-existence of global energy minimizers for various ranges of
\beta
and
q
. Uniqueness and qualitative properties (e.g., monotonicity and radial symmetry) of energy minimizers or steady states of (
1.2
) have been studied in
[
3
,
12
,
17
,
33
]
. Well-posedness, long time behaviour and properties of solutions to the evolution equation (
1.2
) were addressed in
[
5
,
12
,
9
]
. In particular, the authors of
[
9
,
11
]
studied the partial mass concentration in time for the fast diffusion, both for confinement and interaction potentials. In our current research we deal with the first step in understanding fast diffusion phenomena on general manifolds. Hence, we focus on finding conditions that ensure the existence of global minimizers for the free energy (
1.1
), without discussing the possible formation of concentrations, which are still quite intricate and open even in the Euclidean case
[
8
]
.

In spite of extensive studies of the free energy (
1.1
) and the evolution equation (
1.2
) in the Euclidean space, there has been very little research done in the manifold setup. In this paper we consider the free energy (
1.1
) on general Cartan-Hadamard manifolds. Important examples of Cartan-Hadamard manifolds are the hyperbolic space and the space of symmetric positive definite matrices. In general, any non-compact symmetric space is Cartan-Hadamard
[
32
]
. We consider interaction potentials
W(x,y)
that depend only on the geodesic distance
d(x,y)
between the points
x
and
y
. This assumption, which makes the model completely
intrinsic
to the given manifold, was used in recent works on the interaction model with no diffusion
[
20
,
21
,
24
,
25
]
. With this setup, we address and answer the following problem. Assume that the sectional curvatures at a generic point
x
are bounded below and above, respectively, by two negative functions
-c_{m}(r_{x})
and
-c_{M}(r_{x})
, where
r_{x}
denotes the distance from
x
to a fixed (but arbitrary) pole on
M
. A particular case is when
c_{m}(\cdot)
and
c_{M}(\cdot)
are constant functions, but in general
c_{m}(\cdot)
and
c_{M}(\cdot)
are allowed to grow at any rate at infinity, e.g., they can grow algebraically or exponentially. We are interested to account for the variable (and possibly unbounded) curvature of the manifold, and establish necessary and sufficient conditions on the behaviour of the interaction potential at zero and infinity, for global minimizers of the free energy to exist.

The problem posed above was studied in
[
6
]
for
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
q>0
(all the three diffusion regimes), in
[
10
]
for
M=\mathbb{H}^{d}
– the hyperbolic space – and
q>1
(slow diffusion), and in
[
22
,
23
]
for general Cartan-Hadamard manifolds and
q=1
(linear diffusion). In particular,
[
6
]
provided necessary, but not shown to be also sufficient, conditions on the interaction potential for existence of ground states in the fast diffusion regime. In the case of linear diffusion,
[
23
,
22
]
demonstrate the role of the manifold’s curvature with regards to spreading and blow-up. For example, spreading by linear diffusion in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is prevented provided the interaction potential grows at least logarithmically at infinity; the condition is in fact sharp
[
6
]
. On the other hand, to contain the linear diffusion on general Cartan-Hadamard manifolds with bounded sectional curvatures, superlinear growth of the interaction potential at infinity is needed
[
23
]
. Therefore, as a manifestation of the curvature, a stronger growth of the attractive potential is needed to contain diffusion on manifolds.

The present work is the first to consider fast diffusion and nonlocal interactions on general manifolds of negative sectional curvatures. We quantify, in terms of the upper and lower bounds of the sectional curvatures, the conditions on
W
that lead to existence or nonexistence of global energy minimizers. The main tools we use in our analysis are various comparison results in differential geometry (Rauch theorem, volume bounds, bounds for the Jacobian of the exponential map), as well as several new generalized Carlson-Levin inequalities on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds, that we derived for our purpose and have an interest in their own (Lemma
4.1
and
6.2
). Compared to their analogue from the Euclidean space
[
7
, Lemma 5]
, our results contain additional terms that reflect the volume growth of geodesic balls on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

Finally, we note that there has been strong interest in recent years on PDEs that involve nonlinear diffusion on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds. Issues that have been addressed for instance are the well-posedness and long-time behaviour of solutions to the porous medium equation on negatively curved manifolds
[
30
,
31
]
, as well as to PDEs that include both nonlinear diffusion and local reaction terms
[
28
,
29
]
. Due to the nonlocal nature of our interaction term, the methods developed in our work are very different from the approaches taken in these papers.

The summary of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we present the assumptions, the variational setup, and some necessary background in Riemannian geometry. In Section
3
we establish necessary conditions on the interaction potential for global energy minimizers to exist (Theorem
3.1
). In Section
4
we derive a generalized Carlson-Levin inequality for Cartan-Hadamard manifolds with sectional curvatures bounded from below. This inequality is used in Section
5
to establish sufficient conditions on the growth of the interaction potential at infinity, for a ground state to exist (Theorem
5.1
). In Section
6
we generalize the previous existence/nonexistence results, as well the Carlson-Levin inequality, to Cartan-Hadamard manifolds with unbounded sectional curvatures.

2.
Preliminaries and background

2.1.
Assumptions and notations

We make the following assumptions on the manifold
M
and the interaction potential
W
.

(
M
)
M
is an
d
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold, i.e.,
M
is complete, simply connected, and has everywhere non-positive sectional curvature. In particular,
M
has no conjugate points, it is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the exponential map at any point is a global diffeomorphism.

(
W
) The interaction potential
W:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
has the form

W(x,y)=h(d(x,y)),\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in M,

where
h:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is lower semi-continuous and non-decreasing, and
d
denotes the intrinsic distance on
M
. As
h
is non-decreasing,
h
models purely
attractive
interactions.

Notations.
For
x\in M
, and
\sigma
a two-dimensional subspace of
T_{x}M
,
\mathcal{K}(x;\sigma)
denotes the sectional curvature of
\sigma
at
x
. We also denote by
\mathrm{d}V
the Riemannian volume measure on
M
and by
\mathcal{P}_{ac}(M)\subset\mathcal{P}(M)
the space of probability measures on
M
that are absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathrm{d}V
.

A point on a manifold is called a pole if the exponential map at that point is a global diffeomorphism. On Cartan-Hadamard manifolds all points have this property. Throughout the paper we fix a generic point
o
in
M
which we will be referring to as the
pole
of the manifold. We make the notation

r_{x}:=d(o,x),\qquad\text{ for all }x\in M.

We denote by
B_{r}(o)
the open geodesic ball centred at
o
of radius
r
, and by
|B_{r}(o)|
its volume. Also,
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
denotes the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\omega(d)
its volume.

Definition of the energy.
Any probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(M)
can be written uniquely, by Lebesgue’s decomposition theorem, as
\mu=\rho\,\mathrm{d}V+\mu^{s}
, where the first term is the absolutely continuous part of
\mu
w.r.t. the volume measure
\mathrm{d}V
and the second term is the singular part. Given the assumption on the interaction potential, the free energy
E:\mathcal{P}(M)\to[-\infty,\infty]
is given by

(2.1)

E[\mu]=\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

The first term in (
2.1
) represent the entropy and the second is the interaction energy.

As explained in
[
6
,
7
]
, the energy in (
2.1
) can first be defined for functions in
C_{c}^{\infty}(M)\cap\mathcal{P}(M)
, and then extended to the entire
\mathcal{P}(M)
by

E[\mu]=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{\mu_{n}\}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(M)\cap\mathcal{P}(M)\\
\text{s.t.}\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu\end{subarray}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}E[\mu_{n}],

where
\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu
denotes weak convergence as measures, i.e.,

\int_{M}f(x)\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\to\int_{M}f(x)\mu(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x),\qquad\text{ as }n\to\infty,

for all bounded and continuous functions
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
.

Wasserstein distance.
Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)
the set of probability measures on
M
with finite first moment, i.e.,

\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(M):\int_{M}d(x,x_{0})\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty\right\},

for some fixed (but arbitrary) point
x_{0}\in M
. For
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, the
intrinsic

1
-Wasserstein distance is defined as

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\rho,\sigma)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\rho,\sigma)}\iint_{M\times M}d(x,y)\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y),

where
\Pi(\rho,\sigma)\subset\mathcal{P}(M\times M)
is the set of transport plans between
\rho
and
\sigma
, i.e., the set of elements in
\mathcal{P}(M\times M)
with first and second marginals
\rho
and
\sigma
, respectively. The space
(\mathcal{P}_{1}(M),\mathcal{W}_{1})
is a metric space
[
2
]
.

Admissible set.
Similar to the approach in the Euclidean space
[
12
,
13
,
17
]
or for general manifolds and linear diffusion
[
23
,
22
]
, we fix the Riemannian centre of mass of the admissible minimizers. For a fixed pole
o\in M
, we denote

(2.2)

\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(M):\int_{M}\log_{o}x\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=0\right\},

where
\log_{o}
denotes the Riemannian logarithm map at
o
(i.e., the inverse of the Riemannian exponential map
\exp_{o}
)
[
18
]
. The integral condition in (
2.2
) fixes the centre of mass of
\rho
at the pole
o
. It is in this set that we will establish existence of global energy minimizers.

2.2.
Comparison results in differential geometry

We list here several comparison results in differential geometry, that will be used in our analysis.

Theorem 2.1

(Rauch comparison theorem
[
15
]
)
.

Let
M
and
\tilde{M}
be Riemannian manifolds with
\mathrm{dim}(\tilde{M})\geq\mathrm{dim}(M
), and suppose that for all
p\in M
,
\tilde{p}\in\tilde{M}
, and
\sigma\subset T_{p}M
,
\tilde{\sigma}\subset T_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{M}
, the sectional curvatures
\mathcal{K}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{K}}
of
M
and
\tilde{M}
, respectively, satisfy

\tilde{\mathcal{K}}(\tilde{p};\tilde{\sigma})\geq\mathcal{K}(p;\sigma).

Let
p\in M
,
\tilde{p}\in\tilde{M}
and fix a linear isometry
i:T_{p}M\to T_{\tilde{p}}\tilde{M}
. Let
r>0
be such that the restriction
{\exp_{p}}_{|B_{r}(0)}
is a diffeomorphism and
{\exp_{\tilde{p}}}_{|B_{r}(0)}
is non-singular. Let
c:[0,a]\to\exp_{p}(B_{r}(0))\subset M
be any differentiable curve and define
\tilde{c}:[0,a]\to\exp_{\tilde{p}}(B_{r}(0))\subset\tilde{M}
by

\tilde{c}(s)=\exp_{\tilde{p}}\circ i\circ\exp^{-1}_{p}(c(s)),\qquad s\in[0,a].

Then the length of
c
is greater or equal than the length of
\tilde{c}
.

For manifolds with bounded sectional curvatures, the Jacobian of the exponential map and the volumes of geodesic balls can be bounded below and above as follows. The results can be found in
[
14
, Section III.4]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Jacobian and volume bounds
[
14
]
)
.

Suppose the sectional curvatures of
M
satisfy

(2.3)

-c_{m}\leq\mathcal{K}(x;\sigma)\leq-c_{M}<0,

for all
x\in M
and all two-dimensional subspaces
\sigma\subset T_{x}M
, where
c_{m}
and
c_{M}
are positive constants. Let
o
be an arbitrary pole on
M
. Then, the Jacobian of the exponential map at
o
can be bounded as

(2.4)

\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}\|u\|)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}\|u\|}\right)^{d-1}\leq|J(\exp_{o})(u)|\leq\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\|u\|)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}\|u\|}\right)^{d-1},

for all
u\in T_{o}M
.

Also, the volumes of geodesic balls are bounded by

(2.5)

dw(d)\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}t)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}t\leq|B_{r}(o)|\leq dw(d)\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}t)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}t.

Remark 2.1
.

The two bounds in (
2.5
) represent the volume of the ball of radius
r
in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{d}
of constant curvatures
-c_{M}
, and
-c_{m}
, respectively.

The comparison results in Theorem

can be generalized to manifolds of unbounded curvature. The results are the following (see
[

Theorem 2.3
.

(Generalized Jacobian and volume bounds
[

]
)

Suppose the sectional curvatures of
M
satisfy

(2.6)

-c_{m}(r_{x})\leq\mathcal{K}(x;\sigma)\leq-c_{M}(r_{x})<0,

for all
x\in M
and all two-dimensional subspaces
\sigma\subset T_{x}M
, where
c_{m}(\cdot)
and
c_{M}(\cdot)
are positive continuous functions of the distance from a fixed pole

. Denote by
\psi_{m}
and
\psi_{M}
the solutions of the following initial-value-problems (IVP’s):

(2.7)

\begin{cases}\psi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=c_{m}(\theta)\psi_{m}(\theta),\quad\forall\theta>0,\\[5.0pt]
\psi_{m}(0)=0,\quad\psi_{m}^{\prime}(0)=1,\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad\begin{cases}\psi_{M}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=c_{M}(\theta)\psi_{M}(\theta),\quad\forall\theta>0,\\[5.0pt]
\psi_{M}(0)=0,\quad\psi_{M}^{\prime}(0)=1.\end{cases}

Then, the Jacobian of the exponential map at

can be bounded as

\left(\frac{\psi_{M}(\|u\|)}{\|u\|}\right)^{d-1}\leq|J(\exp_{o})(u)|\leq\left(\frac{\psi_{m}(\|u\|)}{\|u\|}\right)^{d-1},

for all

can be bounded by

dw(d)\int_{0}^{r}\psi_{M}(t)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}t\leq|B_{r}(o)|\leq dw(d)\int_{0}^{r}\psi_{m}(t)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}t.

Remark 2.2
.

When
c_{m}(\cdot)\equiv c_{m}
and
c_{M}(\cdot)\equiv c_{M}
are constant functions, the IVPs in (

) have the explicit solutions

\psi_{m}(\theta)=\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}},\quad\text{ and }\quad\psi_{M}(\theta)=\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}}.

In this case, Theorem

We also note that while in general, one cannot find explicit solutions to the IVP’s in (

\psi_{m}(\theta)\geq 0,\;\psi_{M}(\theta)\geq 0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\psi_{m}^{\prime}(\theta)\geq 1,\;\psi_{M}^{\prime}(\theta)\geq 1,\quad\text{ for all }\theta\geq 0.

Remark 2.3
.

We note that for the upper bounds in Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
, a weaker condition on curvature can be assumed, namely that the Ricci curvatures are greater than or equal to

(see Theorems III.4.3 and III.4.4 in

Let

3.
Nonexistence of a global minimizer

In this section, we investigate necessary conditions on the interaction potential for global minimizers of the energy (
2.1
) to exist. We assume here that the sectional curvatures of
M
satisfy

(3.1)

\mathcal{K}(x;\sigma)\leq-c_{M}<0,

for all
x\in M
and all two-dimensional subspaces
\sigma\subset T_{x}M
, where
c_{M}
is a positive constant.

Fix a pole
o\in M
and consider the following family of probability density functions
\rho_{R}
that depend on
R>0
:

(3.2)

\rho_{R}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{R}(o)|},&\quad\text{when }x\in B_{R}(o),\\[10.0pt]
0,&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We will estimate
E[\rho_{R}]
in two limits:
R\to 0+
(blow-up) and
R\to\infty
(spreading).

By the volume comparison in Theorem
2.2
, the volume
|B_{R}(o)|
can be estimated as

(3.3)

|B_{R}(o)|\geq dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta.

Note that when
c_{M}=0
, (
3.3
) reduces to

(3.4)

|B_{R}(o)|\geq dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\theta^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta=w(d)R^{d},

which states that
|B_{R}(o)|
is bounded below by the volume of the ball of radius
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The entropy term for
\rho_{R}
is given explicitly by

(3.5)

\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho_{R}(x)^{q}dx=-\frac{|B_{R}(o)|^{1-q}}{1-q}.

Also, by the monotonicity of
h
, the interaction term can be estimated as

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\rho_{R}(x)\rho_{R}(y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\iint_{M\times M}h(2R)\rho_{R}(x)\rho_{R}(y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{h(2R)}{2}.

Using the two equations above in (
2.1
), we then have

(3.7)

\displaystyle E[\rho_{R}]\leq-\frac{|B_{R}(o)|^{1-q}}{1-q}+\frac{h(2R)}{2}.

Behaviour at zero.
Using (
3.7
) and
0<q<1
, we get

\lim_{R\to 0+}E[\rho_{R}]\leq\lim_{R\to 0+}\left(-\frac{|B_{R}(o)|^{1-q}}{1-q}+\frac{h(2R)}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{R\to 0+}h(2R).

This implies that, if
h
is singular at
0+
(i.e.,
\lim_{\theta\to 0^{+}}h(\theta)=-\infty
), then the energy is unbounded from below and there is no global energy minimizer.

Behaviour at infinity.
By combining (
3.3
) and (
3.7
), we get

\lim_{R\to\infty}E[\rho_{R}]\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\left(-\frac{1}{1-q}\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}+\frac{h(2R)}{2}\right).

Similar to the behaviour at zero, provided the limit in the right-hand-side above is
-\infty
,
E
is not bounded from below and hence, it has no global minimizer. We quantify this behaviour in terms of
h
, as following.

By L’Hôpital’s rule, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta}{\exp(\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)R)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}}\right)^{d-1}}{\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)\exp(\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)R)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{d-1}\sqrt{c_{M}}^{d}(d-1)}.

If
h
satisfies

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{h(R)}{\exp\left(\frac{\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)(1-q)}{2}R\right)}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{h(2R)}{\exp(\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)(1-q)R)}=0,

then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\left(-\frac{1}{1-q}\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}+\frac{h(2R)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{R\to\infty}\exp(\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)(1-q)R)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(-\frac{1}{1-q}\left(\frac{dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{M}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{M}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta}{\exp(\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)R)}\right)^{1-q}+\frac{h(2R)}{2\exp(\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)(1-q)R)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\infty\times\left(-\frac{1}{1-q}\left(\frac{dw(d)}{2^{d-1}\sqrt{c_{M}}^{d}(d-1)}\right)^{1-q}\right)=-\infty.

The two cases prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(Nonexistence by blow-up or spreading)
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold with sectional curvatures that satisfy (
3.1
) for a positive constant
c_{M}
. Let
0<q<1
and assume that
h:[0,\infty)\to[-\infty,\infty)
is a non-decreasing lower semi-continuous function which satisfies either

\lim_{\theta\to 0+}h(\theta)=-\infty,

or

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\frac{\sqrt{c_{M}}(d-1)(1-q)}{2}\,\theta\right)}=0.

Then, the energy functional (
2.1
) is unbounded from below, and hence has no global minimizer in
\mathcal{P}(M)
.

Remark 3.1

(Comparison with linear and slow diffusion)
.

The necessary conditions in Theorem
3.1
say that no singularity at origin in the interaction potential is allowed, or else nonexistence by blow-up occurs. Also, the potential needs to grow exponentially fast at infinity, in order to contain the spreading. These conditions are much more restrictive than those for the model with linear diffusion (
q=1
) or slow diffusion (
q>1
). For the latter models, ground states can exist if
h
is singular at origin (with at most a logarithmic or a power-law singularity for
q=1
and
q>1
, respectively), or if
h
grows less than exponentially at infinity (superlinear growth for
q=1
and no growth rate restriction for
q>1
). We refer the reader to
[
23
,
22
]
for the precise statements.

4.
A generalized Carlson–Levin inequality

This section contains some preparatory results needed to show the existence of global minimizers. Here we assume that the sectional curvatures of the Cartan-Hadamard manifold
M
satisfy

(4.1)

-c_{m}\leq\mathcal{K}(x;\sigma)\leq 0,

for all
x\in M
and all two-dimensional subspaces
\sigma\subset T_{x}M
, where
c_{m}
is a positive constant.

4.1.
A convexity argument

Recall Theorem
2.1
and apply it for the manifold
M
(note that
M
has non-positive curvature), for
\tilde{M}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\simeq T_{o}M
, and
i
the identity map. For two points
x
and
y
in
M
, take
c
to be the geodesic curve joining them. The curve
\tilde{c}
constructed in Theorem
2.1
joins
\log_{o}x
and
\log_{o}y
, and hence, its length is greater than or equal to the length of the straight segment joining
\log_{o}x
and
\log_{o}y
. We then infer from Theorem
2.1
that

(4.2)

|\log_{o}x-\log_{o}y|\leq l(\tilde{c})\leq l(c)=d(x,y).

By the Law of cosines, we also get

|\log_{o}x-\log_{o}y|=\sqrt{r_{x}^{2}+r_{y}^{2}-2r_{x}r_{y}\cos\angle(xoy)},

which combined with (
4.2
) yields

(4.3)

\displaystyle d(x,y)^{2}\geq r_{x}^{2}+r_{y}^{2}-2r_{x}r_{y}\cos\angle(xoy).

Then, from (
4.3
) we find

(4.4)

d(x,y)^{2}\geq|r_{x}-r_{y}\cos\angle(xoy)|^{2},\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in M.

We will use (
4.4
) to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a Cartan–Hadamard manifold,
o\in M
an arbitrary fixed pole, and
\mathcal{H}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a non-decreasing convex function. Then, for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
, we have

(4.5)

\iint_{M\times M}\mathcal{H}(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\geq\int_{M}\mathcal{H}(r_{x})\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Proof.

As
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
has Riemannian centre of mass at
o
, it holds that

\int_{M}\log_{o}y\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=0.

Since
\log_{o}o
is the zero vector in
T_{o}M
, we have

\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}\log_{o}y\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=\int_{M}\log_{o}y\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=0.

Then, for any
x\neq o
we have

0=\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}\log_{o}x\cdot\log_{o}y\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=r_{x}\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}r_{y}\cos\angle(xoy)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y),

and hence,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}r_{y}\cos\angle(xoy)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=0.

Using Jensen’s inequality, the monotonicity of
\mathcal{H}
, and (
4.4
), we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{M}\mathcal{H}(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu(y)&\geq\mathcal{H}\left(\int_{M}d(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\right)\\
&=\mathcal{H}\left(\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}d(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int_{\{o\}}d(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\right)\\
&\geq\mathcal{H}\left(\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}|r_{x}-r_{y}\cos\angle(xoy)|\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int_{\{o\}}r_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\right).\end{aligned}

Furthermore, using(
4.6
) we find

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}|r_{x}-r_{y}\cos\angle(xoy)|\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int_{\{o\}}r_{x}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle\geq\left|\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}(r_{x}-r_{y}\cos\angle(xoy))\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\right|+\int_{\{o\}}r_{x}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{M\backslash\{o\}}r_{x}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int_{\{o\}}r_{x}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)

\displaystyle=r_{x}.

Substitute (
4.8
) into (
4.7
) to conclude that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\mathcal{H}(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\geq\mathcal{H}(r_{x}),

for any
x\neq o
. For
x=o
, (
4.9
) also holds, as

\int_{M}\mathcal{H}(r_{y})\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\geq\int_{M}\mathcal{H}(0)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=\mathcal{H}(0).

Hence, (
4.9
) holds for all
x\in M
. Then, by integrating (
4.9
) with respect to
\mu(x)
on
M
we find (
4.5
).
∎

Remark 4.1
.

If
\mu
is not a probability measure, (
4.5
) gains an additional term as follows. Assume
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(M)\cap L^{1}(M)
, where
\mathcal{M}_{+}(M)
denotes the set of non-negative measures on
M
, satisfies
\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{1}:=\int_{M}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\neq 1
,
\displaystyle\int_{M}r_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty
, and
\displaystyle\int_{M}\log_{o}x\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=0
. Then, we can substitute
\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{\|\mu\|_{1}}\in\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
into Proposition
4.1
to get

\iint_{M\times M}\mathcal{H}(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\geq\left(\int_{M}\mathcal{H}(r_{x})\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right)\left(\int_{M}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right),

for any non-decreasing convex function
\mathcal{H}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
.

4.2.
Generalized Carlson-Levin inequality

The following lemma generalizes the Carlson-Levin inequality
[
7
, Lemma 5]
used to study free energy minimization in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Generalized Carlson–Levin inequality)
.

Let
0<q<1
and
M
be a
d
-dimensional Cartan–Hadamard manifold whose sectional curvatures satisfy (
4.1
), for some
c_{m}>0
. Also, let
o\in M
be a fixed pole. If
\lambda>\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{q}
, then there exists a positive constant
C_{1}>0
that depends on
\lambda,q,c_{m}
, and
d
, such that

\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq C_{1}(\lambda,q,c_{m},d)\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{(1-p)q}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{pq},

for any
\rho(\cdot)\geq 0
, where
p=\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{\lambda q}\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Fix
R>0
and
\rho(\cdot)\geq 0
. By Hölder inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{q}
and
\frac{1}{1-q}
, we get

(4.10)

\displaystyle\int_{r_{x}<R}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{r_{x}<R}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}\left(\int_{r_{x}<R}1\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q},

where for the second inequality we used the volume upper bound in Theorem
2.2
. Similarly, also by Hölder inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{q}
and
\frac{1}{1-q}
, we find

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}\left(\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}\left(dw(d)\int_{R}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q},

where for the second inequality we used geodesic spherical coordinates
x=\exp_{o}(\theta u)
, with
\theta>0
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, to change the variable in the second integral, and reasoned as follows. First note that
(\theta,u)
are spherical coordinates in the tangent space
T_{o}M
, with volume element
\theta^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)
, where
\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)
denotes the Riemanian measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then, using the Jacobian upper bound in Theorem
2.2
we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V(x)

\displaystyle=J(\exp_{o})(\theta u)\,\theta^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\sigma(u),

from which the second inequality in (
4.11
) follows.

Denote

\displaystyle A(R):=\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q},

\displaystyle B(R):=\left(dw(d)\int_{R}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}.

Then, by combining (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) we get

(4.13)

\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq A(R)\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}+B(R)\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}.

Now use

\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}=\frac{e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta}}{2\sqrt{c_{m}}}(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta})\leq\frac{e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta}}{2\sqrt{c_{m}}}(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}R}),\qquad\forall\theta\in[0,R],

to estimate
A(R)
as

(4.14)

\displaystyle A(R)

\displaystyle\leq\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta}}{2\sqrt{c_{m}}}(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}R})\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}R})^{d-1}}{(2\sqrt{c_{m}})^{d-1}\sqrt{c_{m}}(d-1)}\left(e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}(d-1)R}-1\right)\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{dw(d)(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}R})^{d-1}}{(2\sqrt{c_{m}})^{d-1}\sqrt{c_{m}}(d-1)}e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}(d-1)R}\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}(d-1)}\right)^{1-q}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{(1-q)(d-1)}.

Similarly, use

\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}=\frac{e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta}}{2\sqrt{c_{m}}}(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta})\geq\frac{e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta}}{2\sqrt{c_{m}}}(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}R}),\qquad\forall\theta\in[R,\infty),

to estimate
B(R)
(recall that
d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}<0
) as

(4.15)

\displaystyle B(R)

\displaystyle\leq\left(dw(d)\int_{R}^{\infty}\left(\frac{e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta}}{2\sqrt{c_{m}}}(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}R})\right)^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}R})^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}}{(2\sqrt{c_{m}})^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}}\int_{R}^{\infty}e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}\left(d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)(1-e^{-2\sqrt{c_{m}}R})^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}}{(2\sqrt{c_{m}})^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\sqrt{c_{m}}\left(-d+1+\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)}e^{\sqrt{c_{m}}\left(d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)R}\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)(1-q)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}(\lambda q-(1-q)(d-1))}\right)^{1-q}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{(1-q)(d-1)-\lambda q}.

Make the following notations (by the assumption on
\lambda
, we have
\lambda q-(1-q)(d-1)>0
):

(4.16)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}(d-1)}\right)^{1-q},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)(1-q)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}(\lambda q-(1-q)(d-1))}\right)^{1-q},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=(1-q)(d-1),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda q-(1-q)(d-1).

Note that
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are positive constants that depend on
\lambda,q,c_{m}
, and
d
. With these notations, we write (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) compactly as

A(R)\leq\alpha_{1}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\beta_{1}},\qquad\text{ and }\qquad B(R)\leq\alpha_{2}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{-\beta_{2}},\qquad\forall R>0.

Using the estimates above in (
4.13
), we have

(4.17)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{1}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\beta_{1}}\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}

\displaystyle+\alpha_{2}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{-\beta_{2}}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}.

We are interested in finding the optimal
R
for the right-hand-side of (
4.17
). For this purpose, we calculate its derivative with respect to
R
, and set it to zero, to get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\beta_{1}-1}\cosh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{-\beta_{2}-1}\cosh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}.

After simplifying, we find

(4.18)

\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}=\frac{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\left(\frac{\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)}{\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)}\right)^{q}.

Let
R_{*}
be the unique solution to (
4.18
) Now substitute
R_{*}
into (
4.17
) to get

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\left(\frac{\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)}{\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)}\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}\left(\frac{\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)}{\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)}\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha_{1}^{\beta_{2}}\alpha_{2}^{\beta_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\left(\left(\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}+\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{\frac{\beta_{2}q}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{\frac{\beta_{1}q}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}(\lambda,q,c_{m},d)\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{\frac{\beta_{2}q}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{\frac{\beta_{1}q}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}},

where

C_{1}(\lambda,q,c_{m},d)=\left(\alpha_{1}^{\beta_{2}}\alpha_{2}^{\beta_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\left(\left(\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}+\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\right).

Using the expressions for
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
from (
4.16
), we have

\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}=\frac{(1-q)(d-1)}{\lambda q},\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}=1-\frac{(1-q)(d-1)}{\lambda q}.

Finally, we find

\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq C_{1}\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{(1-p)q}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{pq},

where
p=\frac{(1-q)(d-1)}{\lambda q}\in(0,1)
..
∎

Remark 4.2
.

By combining Lemma
4.1
and Remark
4.1
one gets

\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq C_{1}\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{(1-2p)q}\left(\iint_{M\times M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}d(x,y))}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\rho(y)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(y)\right)^{pq}

for all
\rho
that satisfy the conditions in Remark
4.1
. This can be further transformed into

(4.19)

C_{1}^{-1/pq}\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{pq}}\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{-\frac{(1-2p)q}{pq}}\\
\leq\iint_{M\times M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}d(x,y))}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\rho(y)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(y).

When
c_{m}\to 0+
, we have

\lim_{c_{m}\to 0+}\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}d(x,y))}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}=d(x,y).

Therefore, on
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we can infer the inequality

\displaystyle C_{1}^{-1/pq}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{pq}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{-\frac{(1-2p)q}{pq}}\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}d(x,y)^{\lambda}\rho(x)\rho(y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

which is a reversed HLS inequality with a non-optimal constant (see
[
7
, Theorem 1]
).

5.
Existence of a global minimizer

In this section, we assume that the sectional curvatures
M
satisfy (
4.1
), for some
c_{m}>0
, and investigate the existence of global energy minimizers in
\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
(see (
2.2
)), with
o
being an arbitrary fixed pole in
M
.

Specifically, we assume that
h
satisfies

(5.1)

h(0)=0,

and

(5.2)

\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}}\lambda\theta)}>0,\qquad\text{ for some }\lambda>\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{q}.

Both conditions on
h
in (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) complement those from Theorem
3.1
, when nonexistence of minimizers occurs. In particular, a necessary condition for existence is that
h
does not blow-up at origin; since the interaction potential is defined up to a constant, we set
h
to be
0
at origin. Also, the interaction potential needs to grow at least exponentially, or else an energy minimizer does not exist. This is reflected in the condition on
h
in (
5.2
).

Proposition 5.1

(Tightness of the minimizing sequences)
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold whose sectional curvatures satisfy (
4.1
), for some
c_{m}>0
, and let
o\in M
be a fixed pole. Also, assume
0<q<1
and that
h
is lower semi-continuous, non-decreasing, and satisfies (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). Then, any minimizing sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
of the functional
E[\cdot]
is tight.

Proof.

Consider a minimizing sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
of
E[\cdot]
, and for each
n\geq 1
, denote by
\rho_{n}
the absolutely continuous part of
\mu_{n}
. Then,

(5.3)

\displaystyle E[\mu_{n}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1-q}\int_{M}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{C_{1}}{1-q}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{pq}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)+\frac{1}{4}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y),

where for the inequality we used Lemma
4.1
together with the fact that
\int_{M}\rho_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{M}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)=1
, with
\lambda
being the constant in (
5.2
) and

p=\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{\lambda q}\in(0,1).

Note that
C_{1}>0
depends on
\lambda,q,c_{m}
, and
d
, but not on
\mu_{n}
.

By (
5.2
) and

\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}}\lambda\theta)}{\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}}=\left(\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{c_{m}}\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}\right)^{\lambda}=\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{c_{m}}}{1-\exp(-2\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}\right)^{\lambda}=2^{\lambda}c_{m}^{\lambda/2},

we also have

(5.4)

\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}}>0.

Therefore, there exist some constants
\gamma_{1}>0
and
\gamma_{2}
(which only depend on
h
,
\lambda
and
c_{m}
) such that

(5.5)

\displaystyle h(\theta)\geq\gamma_{1}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}+\gamma_{2},\qquad\forall\theta>0.

Indeed, we can set
\gamma_{1}
to be a positive constant less than
2^{\lambda}c_{m}^{\lambda/2}
and

\gamma_{2}:=\inf_{\theta\geq 0}\left(h(\theta)-\gamma_{1}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\right).

We substitute (
5.5
) into (
5.3
) to get

\displaystyle E[\mu_{n}]

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{C_{1}}{1-q}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{pq}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}\iint_{M\times M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}d(x,y))}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y).

Then, using Proposition
4.1
with the convex function

(5.6)

\mathcal{H}(\theta)=\left(\frac{\sinh\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda},

we further find

(5.7)

\displaystyle E[\mu_{n}]

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{C_{1}}{1-q}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{pq}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{C_{1}}{1-q}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{pq}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y),

where for the second inequality we used that
\rho_{n}
is the absolutely continuous part of
\mu_{n}
.

Define
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(X)=-\frac{C_{1}}{1-q}X^{pq}+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}X.

As
0<pq<1
and
C_{1},\gamma_{1}>0
,
f
has a global minimum on
[0,\infty)
, and hence,

f(X)\geq C_{2},\qquad\forall X\in[0,\infty),

for some
C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Using this property in (
5.7
) for

X=\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}x,

we find

(5.8)

E[\mu_{n}]\geq C_{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y).

We point out that the constants in (
5.8
) depend on
h,\lambda,q,c_{m}
, and
d
, but not on
\mu_{n}
.

Since
\mu_{n}
is a minimizing sequence (and hence,
E[\mu_{1}]\geq E[\mu_{n}]
), we infer from (
5.8
) that

\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)

is uniformly bounded above in
n\in\mathbb{N}
. From (
5.5
), we get that

\iint_{M\times M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}d(x,y))}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)

is also uniformly bounded in
n\in\mathbb{N}
; denote by
C_{3}
a uniform upper bound for it. From the convexity argument in Proposition
4.1
(see (
4.5
) and (
5.6
)), we then find

(5.9)

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{M\times M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}d(x,y))}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x),\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Fix
R>0
. From (
5.9
), we further estimate

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x),\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\leq\frac{C_{3}}{\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}R)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}},\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

where the constant
C_{3}
does not depend on
R
or
n
. We infer from here that
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is tight.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

As an immediate consequence of the proof of Proposition
5.1
, for any minimizing sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
of the energy, it holds that

(5.11)

E[\mu_{n}]\geq\widetilde{C}_{1}+\widetilde{C}_{2}\,\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu_{n},\delta_{o}),\qquad\text{ for all }n\geq 1,

with constants
\widetilde{C}_{1}
and
\widetilde{C}_{2}
that are independent of
\mu_{n}
. Indeed, by (
5.8
) and (
5.5
), we get

\displaystyle E[\mu_{n}]

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\iint_{M\times M}\left(\gamma_{1}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}+\gamma_{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{4}\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x),

where for the second inequality we used Proposition
4.1
(see also (
5.9
)). The conclusion then follows by noting that there exist constants
\widetilde{\gamma_{1}}
and
\widetilde{\gamma_{2}}
(that depend on
c_{m}
and
\lambda
) such that

\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\geq\widetilde{\gamma_{1}}\theta+\widetilde{\gamma_{2}},\qquad\text{ for all }\theta>0,

and that

\int_{M}r_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)=\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu_{n},\delta_{o}).

Proposition 5.2

(Lower semi-continuity of the energy)
.

Let
M
and
h
satisfy the assumptions of Proposition
5.1
, and
o
be a fixed pole in
M
. Then, for
0<q<1
,
E[\cdot]
is lower semi-continuous on minimizing sequences, i.e., for any minimizing sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
of
E
that converges weakly to
\mu_{0}
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}E[\mu_{n}]\geq E[\mu_{0}].

Proof.

We investigate the lower semi-continuity of the entropy part and the interaction energy part, separately. To do so, let
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
be a minimizing sequence of
E[\cdot]
which converges weakly to some measure
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. Denote by
\rho_{n}
and
\rho_{0}
the absolutely continuous parts of
\mu_{n}
and
\mu_{0}
, respectively.

Part 1.
(
Lower semi-continuity of the entropy
) Fix a radius
r>0
and consider the Riemannian volume measure
\mathrm{d}V
on
\overline{B_{r}(o)}
. By
[
34
, Proposition 7.7]
, given a convex lower semi-continuous function
U:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
, the functional

\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\int_{\overline{B_{r}(o)}}U(\rho(x))\mathrm{d}V(x)+L\mu^{s}\bigl(\overline{B_{r}(o)}\bigr)

is lower semi-continuous with respect to weak convergence. Here,
\mu=\rho\,\mathrm{d}V+\mu^{s}
is decomposed into its absolutely continuous and singular parts, and
L:=\lim_{t\to\infty}f(t)/t\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

Apply this result for
f(x)=\frac{x^{q}}{q-1}
, (note that since
0<q<1
,
f
is a convex lower semi-continuous function that satisfies
L=\lim_{t\to\infty}f(t)/t=0
), to conclude that

\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{\overline{B_{r}(o)}}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\qquad\text{ is lower semi-continuous}.

Hence,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{\overline{B_{r}(o)}}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)\geq\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{\overline{B_{r}(o)}}\rho_{0}(x)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x).

Now, apply Lemma
4.1
for
\rho_{n}\cdot\chi_{{}_{B_{r}(o)^{c}}}
(
\rho_{n}
restricted to the complement of the ball
B_{r}(o)
), to get

\int_{B_{r}(o)^{c}}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq C_{1}\left(\int_{B_{r}(o)^{c}}\rho_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{(1-p)q}\left(\int_{B_{r}(o)^{c}}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\rho_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{pq}.

The constant
C_{1}>0
depends on
\lambda,q,c_{m}
, and
d
, but not on
n
or
r
. Furthermore, from the above we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{B_{r}(o)^{c}}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)&\leq C_{1}\left(\int_{B_{r}(o)^{c}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\right)^{(1-p)q}\left(\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\right)^{pq}\\
&\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{C_{3}}{\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}}\right)^{(1-p)q}C_{3}^{pq}\\
&=C_{1}C_{3}^{q}\left(\frac{\sqrt{c_{m}}}{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r)}\right)^{\lambda(1-p)q},\end{aligned}

where
C_{3}
is the constant (independent of
n
) introduced in the proof of Proposition
5.1
– see (
5.9
). In (
5.13
), for the first inequality we used that
\rho_{n}
is the absolutely continuous part of
\mu_{n}
and that
B_{r}(o)^{c}\subset M
, and for the second inequality we used (
5.10
) and (
5.9
) for each of the integrals, respectively.

From (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), we then have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho_{0}(x)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{\overline{B_{r}(o)}}\rho_{0}(x)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{\overline{B_{r}(o)}}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{\overline{B_{r}(o)}}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\frac{1}{1-q}C_{1}C_{3}^{q}\left(\frac{\sqrt{c_{m}}}{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r)}\right)^{\lambda(1-p)q}-\frac{1}{1-q}\int_{B_{r}(o)^{c}}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\ dx\right)+\frac{1}{1-q}C_{1}C_{3}^{q}\left(\frac{\sqrt{c_{m}}}{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r)}\right)^{\lambda(1-p)q}.

Since
r>0
was arbitrary, one can send
r\to\infty
in the above, to find

(5.14)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right)\geq\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho_{0}(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}x.

Part 2.
(
Lower semi-continuity of the interaction energy
) The lower semi-continuity of the interaction component can be shown as in
[
22
]
, by using the Riemannian logarithm
\log_{o}
to map
M
onto the tangent space
T_{o}M
, and then use properties of the interaction energy on Euclidean spaces. Indeed, denote by
f:M\to T_{o}M
the Riemannian logarithm map at
o
, i.e.,

(5.15)

f(x)=\log_{o}x,\qquad\text{ for all }x\in M.

The inverse
f^{-1}:T_{o}M\to M
is the Riemannian exponential map
\exp_{o}
, which on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds is a global diffeomorphism.

By a change of variable
x=\exp_{o}u
,
y=\exp_{o}v
, we can write the interaction energy of a probability measure
\mu
as

\displaystyle\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=\iint_{T_{o}M\times T_{o}M}h(d(\exp_{o}u,\exp_{o}v))\mathrm{d}f_{\#}\mu(u)\mathrm{d}f_{\#}\mu(v).

The right-hand-side above can be interpreted as the interaction energy of
f_{\#}\mu
on
T_{o}M\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the interaction potential
\tilde{h}:T_{o}M\times T_{o}M\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\tilde{h}(u,v)=h(d(\exp_{o}u,\exp_{o}v)).

Note that
\tilde{h}
is a lower semi-continuous function, as
h
is lower semi-continuous and
\exp_{o}
is a diffeomorphism.

Since
\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu_{0}
weakly as
n\to\infty
, it is straightforward to show by a change of variable that we also have
f_{\#}\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup f_{\#}\mu_{0}
weakly as
n\to\infty
. The interaction energy on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is lower semi-continuous with respect to weak convergence, provided the interaction potential is lower semi-continuous and bounded from below
[
34
, Proposition 7.2]
. Putting together these facts, we then find

(5.16)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(y)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\iint_{T_{o}M\times T_{o}M}\tilde{h}(u,v)\mathrm{d}f_{\#}\mu_{n}(u)\mathrm{d}f_{\#}\mu_{n}(v)

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{T_{o}M\times T_{o}M}\tilde{h}(u,v)\mathrm{d}f_{\#}\mu_{0}(u)\mathrm{d}f_{\#}\mu_{0}(v)

\displaystyle=\iint_{M\times M}h(d(x,y))\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(y).

Finally, the lower semi-continuity of the energy
E[\cdot]
now follows from (
5.14
) and (
5.16
).
∎

In the following result we will use the notation
B_{R}(\delta_{o})
for the geodesic ball in the space
(\mathcal{P}_{1}(M),\mathcal{W}_{1})
, of radius
R
and centre at
\delta_{o}
. In the paper we have used a similar notation for the open ball
B_{r}(o)
of radius
r
and centre at
o
, in the geodesic space
(M,d)
. Nevertheless, the different spaces in which these geodesic balls lie in, will be clear from the context.

Lemma 5.1

(Conservation of centre of mass
[
22
]
)
.

Let
M
be a Cartan-Hadamard manifold,
o\in M
a fixed pole,
R>0
a fixed radius, and
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
a sequence in
\overline{B_{R}(\delta_{o})}
such that all
\mu_{n}
(
n\geq 1
) have centre at mass at
o
and
\mu_{n}
converges weakly to
\mu_{0}
as
n\to\infty
. Also assume that

\int_{M}\phi(r_{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)

is uniformly bounded from above, where
\phi
is a function with superlinear growth at infinity, i.e.,
\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{\phi(\theta)}{\theta}=\infty
. Then,
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
(in particular,
\mu_{0}
has centre of mass at
o
).

Proof.

A variant of this result was stated and proved in
[
22
, Lemma 5.5]
. For completeness, we include a proof here as well.

First note that
\mu_{n}
has uniformly bounded
1
-moments, as

\int_{M}d(x,o)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)=\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu_{n},\delta_{o})\leq R.

Since convergence in
\mathcal{W}_{1}
is equivalent to weak convergence and uniform
1
-integrability
[
2
]
, we infer that
\mu_{n}
also converges to
\mu_{0}
in
(\mathcal{P}_{1}(M),\mathcal{W}_{1})
. Hence,
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(M)
, and more specifically,
\mu_{0}\in\overline{B_{R}(\delta_{o})}
. It remains to show that
\rho_{0}
has centre of mass at
o
.

Fix an arbitrary unit tangent vector
v\in T_{o}M
. As
\mu_{0}\in\overline{B_{R}(\delta_{o})}
, we have

\left|\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\right|\leq\|v\|\int_{M}r_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\leq R.

Fix
\epsilon>0
arbitrary small. Since the integral
\displaystyle\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)
is convergent, there exists
r_{1}>0
such that

(5.17)

\left|\int_{r_{x}>r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\right|<\epsilon,\qquad\text{ for any }r>r_{1}.

On the other hand, since
\phi
grows superlinearly at infinity, there exists
r_{2}>0
such that

0<\frac{\theta}{\phi(\theta)}<\epsilon,\qquad\text{ for any }r>r_{2}.

Then, for any
r>r_{2}
and
n\geq 1
, we have

(5.18)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{r_{x}>r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r_{x}>r}r_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{x}>r}\phi(r_{x})\left(\frac{r_{x}}{\phi(r_{x})}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{\theta}{\phi(\theta)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}((r,\infty))}\times\int_{M}\phi(r_{x})\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq U\epsilon,

where
U
denotes a uniform upper bound of
\int_{M}\phi(r_{x})\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)
.

By combining (
5.17
) and (
5.18
), for any
r>\max(r_{1},r_{2})
, we get

(5.19)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)-\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{r_{x}\leq r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)-\int_{r_{x}\leq r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{r_{x}>r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)-\int_{r_{x}>r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{r_{x}\leq r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)-\int_{r_{x}\leq r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\right|

\displaystyle+(U+1)\epsilon.

Since
\{x:r_{x}\leq r\}
is a bounded set, by continuity of the Riemannian logarithm we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{r_{x}\leq r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)=\int_{r_{x}\leq r}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x).

Finally, letting
n\to\infty
in (
5.19
), we find

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)-\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\right|\leq(U+1)\epsilon,

for any
\epsilon>0
. From here we infer that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)-\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\right|=0,

and hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)=\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x).

Since
\mu_{n}
has centre at mass at
o
, we have
\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)=0
for all
n\geq 1
, and consequently,

\int_{M}\log_{o}x\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)=0.

Since the constant unit vector
v
is arbitrary, we infer that
\mu_{0}
has centre of mass at
o
.
∎

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1

(Existence of a global minimizer)
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold with sectional curvatures that satisfy (
4.1
), for some
c_{m}>0
, and let
o\in M
be a fixed pole. Also, let
0<q<1
and assume that
h
is lower semi-continuous, non-decreasing, and satisfies (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). Then, the energy functional
E[\cdot]
given by (
2.1
) has a global minimizer in
\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
.

Proof.

The proof follows essentially from the previous considerations. The energy is bounded below on
\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
– see Remark
5.1
. Hence, define

E_{0}:=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)}E[\mu],

and consider a minimizing sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
of
E[\cdot]
, i.e.,

(5.20)

\lim_{n\to\infty}E[\mu_{n}]=E_{0}.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
E[\mu_{n}]
is decreasing.

By Proposition
5.1
, any minimizing sequence is tight, so by Prokhorov’s theorem
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
has a subsequence
\{\mu_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
which converges weakly to
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. We claim that the limit
\mu_{0}
is also in
\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
, as inferred from Lemma
5.1
. Indeed,
\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu_{n_{k}},\delta_{o})
is uniformly bounded from above by Remark
5.1
(see (
5.11
) and also note that
\displaystyle E[\mu_{1}]\geq E[\mu_{n_{k}}]
). Also, from the proof of Proposition
5.1
, we know that

\int_{M}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)

is uniformly bounded from above – see (
5.9
). Then, by using Lemma
5.1
with
\phi(r_{x})=\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}r_{x})}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}
(note that
\phi
has superlinear growth at infinity, as required in the assumptions), we infer that
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
.

Then, from (
5.20
) and Proposition
5.2
we get

E_{0}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}E[\mu_{n_{k}}]\geq E[\mu_{0}]\geq E_{0}.

Finally, we can conclude that

E[\mu_{0}]=E_{0},

so
\mu_{0}
is a global energy minimizer in
\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

As noted in Remark
4.2
, as
c_{m}\to 0+
the exponential function

\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}\right)^{\lambda}

converges to the algebraic function
\theta^{\lambda}
. Therefore, in such limit, the exponential growth condition on
h
from (
5.2
) reduces to an algebraic growth condition, similar to the assumptions used to show the existence of global energy minimizers in the Euclidean case
c_{m}=0
(see
[
7
, Proposition 8]
).

6.
Unbounded sectional curvatures

In this section we consider more general assumptions on the sectional curvatures of the manifold – see (
2.6
). In particular, the curvatures are allowed to become unbounded at infinity. One of the main tools in this case is the generalized comparison results in Theorem
2.3
. Similar to the constant bounds case, we will treat separately the necessary and sufficient conditions on the interaction potential for global energy minimizers to exist. Throughout the section,
o
is an arbitrary fixed pole on
M
.

6.1.
Nonexistence of a global minimizer

We assume here that the sectional curvatures of
M
satisfy

(6.1)

\mathcal{K}(x;\sigma)\leq-c_{M}(r_{x})<0,

for all
x\in M
and all two-dimensional subspaces
\sigma\subset T_{x}M
, where
c_{M}(\cdot)
is a positive continuous function of the distance from the pole
o
.

We will use again probability density functions in the form (
3.2
) and look into the behaviours
R\to 0+
and
R\to\infty
. By the exact calculation of the entropy in (
3.5
) and the simple estimate of the interaction energy in (
3.6
), one reaches (
3.7
), which we list it again for convenience:

E[\rho_{R}]\leq-\frac{|B_{R}(o)|^{1-q}}{1-q}+\frac{h(2R)}{2}.

Behaviour at zero.
If
h
is singular at origin, i.e.
\lim_{\theta\to 0+}h(\theta)=-\infty
, then
E
is unbounded from below, and has no global minimizer.

Behaviour at infinity.
By the volume lower bound in Theorem
2.3
we have

|B_{R}(o)|\geq dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\psi_{M}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta,

where
\psi_{M}
is the solution to the second IVP in (
2.7
). This yields

E[\rho_{R}]\leq-\frac{1}{1-q}\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\psi_{M}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}+\frac{h(2R)}{2}.

Hence, if
h
satisfies

\lim_{R\to\infty}\left(-\frac{1}{1-q}\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\psi_{M}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}+\frac{h(2R)}{2}\right)=-\infty,

then there is no global energy minimizer as
\lim_{R\to\infty}E[\rho_{R}]=-\infty
. Since
\int_{0}^{R}\psi_{M}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta
tends to infinity as
R\to\infty
, a sufficient condition for the above inequality can be expressed as

(6.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{h(R)}{\left(\int_{0}^{R/2}\psi_{M}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}}=0.

Also, for any
0<\delta<R/2
, we have

\int_{0}^{R/2}\psi_{M}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\geq\int_{R/2-\delta}^{R/2}\psi_{M}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\geq\int_{R/2-\delta}^{R/2}\psi_{M}(R/2-\delta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta=\delta\psi_{M}(R/2-\delta)^{d-1}.

Hence, a sufficient condition for (
6.2
) to hold is

(6.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{h(R)}{\psi_{M}(R/2-\delta)^{(d-1)(1-q)}}=0,

for some
\delta>0
.

Assume in addition that
c_{M}(\cdot)
satisfies

(6.4)

\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{\overline{D}c_{M}(\theta)}{c_{M}(\theta)^{3/2}}=0.

Then, by Lemma
2.1
, for any
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists
\theta_{0}>0
such that

(6.5)

\psi_{M}(\theta_{0})\exp\left((1-\epsilon)\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{M}(t)}\mathrm{d}t\right)\leq\psi_{M}(\theta),\qquad\forall\theta\geq\theta_{0}.

Using (
6.5
), we infer that a sufficient condition for (
6.3
) is

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{h(R)}{\exp\left((1-\epsilon)(d-1)(1-q)\int_{0}^{R/2-\delta}\sqrt{c_{M}(t)}\mathrm{d}t)\right)}=0.

We now put together the above considerations in the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1

(Nonexistence by blow-up or spreading: unbounded curvature case)
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold with sectional curvatures that satisfy (
6.1
), for a positive continuous function
c_{M}(\cdot)
that satisfies (
6.4
). Let
0<q<1
and assume that
h:[0,\infty)\to[-\infty,\infty)
is a non-decreasing lower semi-continuous function which satisfies either

\lim_{\theta\to 0+}h(\theta)=-\infty,

or there exists
\delta>0
and
0<\epsilon<1
such that

(6.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left((1-\epsilon)(d-1)(1-q)\int_{0}^{\theta/2-\delta}\sqrt{c_{M}(t)}\mathrm{d}t)\right)}=0.

Then the energy functional (
2.1
) is unbounded from below, and therefore has no global minimizer in
\mathcal{P}(M)
.

Example 6.1
.

For certain simple functions
c_{M}
the condition (
6.6
) can be worked out explicitly.

(1)

Constant:
c_{M}(\theta)\equiv c_{M}
. For this case we essentially recover Theorem
3.1
. The only difference is the additional coefficient
1-\epsilon
, which can in fact be arbitrarily close to
1
.

(2)

Power law:
c_{M}(\theta)=\theta^{k}
with
k>0
. Condition (
6.6
) is equivalent in this case to

\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\frac{(1-\epsilon)(d-1)(1-q)}{k/2+1}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}-\delta\right)^{\frac{k}{2}+1}\right)}=0.

Hence, a necessary condition for global minimizers to exist is that the interaction potential grows exponentially (with a certain exponent on
\theta
that depends on
k
) at infinity.

(3)

Exponential growth:
c_{M}(\theta)=e^{\beta\theta}
with
\beta>0
. Condition (
6.6
) is now equivalent to

\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\frac{(1-\epsilon)(d-1)(1-q)}{\beta/2}\exp\left(\frac{\theta\beta}{4}\right)\right)}=0.

Notably, a very strong growth of the potential (double exponential) is needed to contain the diffusion when the curvatures of the manifold grow exponentially fast at infinity.

6.2.
Existence of a global minimizer

In this section, we study the energy minimization problem on
M
which satisfies

(6.7)

-c_{m}(r_{x})\leq\mathcal{K}(x;\sigma)\leq 0,

for all
x\in M
and all two-dimensional subspaces
\sigma\subset T_{x}M
, where
c_{m}(\cdot)
is a positive, non-decreasing and continuous function of the distance from the pole
o
. Relevant for this section are the upper bounds in Theorem
2.3
, with
\psi_{m}
the solution to the first IVP in (
2.7
).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\psi_{m}
be the solution to the first IVP in (
2.7
), and let
R>0
. Then we have

\psi_{m}(\theta)\geq\psi_{m}(R)\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)),\qquad\forall\theta\geq R,

and

\psi_{m}(\theta)\leq\psi_{m}(R)\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)),\qquad\forall 0<\theta\leq R.

Proof.

The proof follows from some basic ODE considerations. See Appendix
A
.
∎

The Carlson-Levin inequality in Lemma
4.1
can be further generalized to manifolds of unbounded curvature. The result is the following.

Lemma 6.2

(Generalized Carlson–Levin inequality (unbounded curvature version))
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Cartan–Hadamard manifold whose sectional curvatures satisfy (
6.7
), for some positive continuous function
c_{m}(\cdot)
, and let
o
be a fixed pole on
M
. Also, let
0<q<1
and
\lambda>\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{q}
. Then there exists a positive constant
\tilde{C}_{1}>0
that depends on
\lambda,q,c_{m}
, and
d
, such that

\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq\tilde{C}_{1}(\lambda,q,c_{m},d)\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{(1-p)q}\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{pq},

for any
\rho(\cdot)\geq 0
, where
p=\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{\lambda q}\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

The arguments follow very closely the proof of Lemma
4.1
. See Appendix
B
.
∎

To show the existence of global minimizers we assume that
h
satisfies

(6.8)

h(0)=0,

and

(6.9)

\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\tilde{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{m}(\theta)}\mathrm{d}t\right)}>0,\qquad\text{ for some }\tilde{\lambda}>\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{q}.

Condition (
6.8
) simply sets
h(0)
to a constant (this is the same condition as in (
5.1
)), as
h
cannot be singular at origin for a ground state to exist. Condition (
6.9
) generalizes (
5.2
) from the constant bound case; see also the necessary condition (
6.6
) from Theorem
6.1
. The reason we use notation
\tilde{\lambda}
in (
6.9
) is to reserve the symbol
\lambda
for a different constant, in order to transfer more easily the arguments made in Section
5
– see (
6.10
) below.

Set
\lambda
such that
\tilde{\lambda}>\lambda>\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{q}
. By Lemma
2.1
we write

\displaystyle\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\psi_{m}(\theta)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\theta^{\lambda}\exp\left(\lambda\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{m}(\theta)}\mathrm{d}t\right)}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\tilde{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{m}(\theta)}\mathrm{d}t\right)}\times\frac{\exp\left((\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda)\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{m}(\theta)}\right)}{\theta^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle>0,

where for the last inequality we used (
6.9
) and

\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{\exp\left((\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda)\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{m}(\theta)}\right)}{\theta^{\tilde{\lambda}}}=\infty.

Hence, if
h
satisfies (
6.9
), then it also satisfies

(6.10)

\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\psi_{m}(\theta)^{\lambda}}>0,\qquad\text{ for some }\lambda>\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{q}.

Remark 6.1
.

In the proof of Theorem
6.2
we will use in fact the weaker assumption (
6.10
) on the growth at infinity of
h
. We choose however to list the growth assumption as in (
6.9
) as this is given in terms of the known function
c_{m}(\cdot)
, rather than the unknown solution
\psi_{m}(\cdot)
of the IVP.

Proposition 6.1

(Tightness of the minimizing sequences)
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold whose sectional curvatures satisfy (
6.7
), where
c_{m}(\cdot)
is a positive, non-decreasing, and continuous function of the distance from a fixed pole
o
. Also, assume
0<q<1
and that
h
is lower semi-continuous, non-decreasing, and satisfies (
6.8
) and (
6.9
). Then, any minimizing sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
of the functional
E[\cdot]
is tight.

Sketch of the proof.

The proof follows closely the arguments in the proof of Proposition
5.1
by replacing
\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)/\sqrt{c_{m}}
with
\psi_{m}(\theta)
. An analogue of (
5.3
) can first be derived by using Lemma
6.2
, where
\lambda
is the constant from (
6.10
). Then, instead of (
5.4
) one can use (
6.10
) to find an analogue of (
5.5
) with
\psi_{m}(\theta)
in place of
\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)/\sqrt{c_{m}}
. From there on, the arguments in the proof of Proposition
5.1
follow exactly, and a uniform upper bound as in (
5.9
) can be derived, i.e.,

(6.11)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{3}\geq\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x),\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

for some constant
\tilde{C}_{3}>0
which does not depend on
\mu_{n}
. Eventually, (
5.10
) is adjusted into

(6.12)

\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{3}}{\psi_{m}(R)^{\lambda}},\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

where the constant
\tilde{C}_{3}
does not depend on
R
or
n
. From here, we infer that
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is tight.
∎

Proposition 6.2

(Lower semi-continuity of the energy)
.

Let
M
and
h
satisfy the assumptions of Proposition
6.1
, and
o
be a fixed pole in
M
. Then, for
0<q<1
,
E[\cdot]
is lower semi-continuous on minimizing sequences, i.e., for any minimizing sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
of
E
that converges weakly to
\mu_{0}
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}E[\mu_{n}]\geq E[\mu_{0}].

Sketch of the proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition
5.2
, by replacing
\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{c_{m}}\theta)}{\sqrt{c_{m}}}
with
\psi_{m}(\theta)
. Indeed, denote by
\rho_{n}
and
\rho_{0}
the absolutely continuous parts of
\mu_{n}
and
\mu_{0}
, respectively. Following the argument leading to (
5.13
), by using the generalized Carlson-Levin inequality in Lemma
6.2
(see also (
6.11
) and (
6.12
)), one finds

\int_{B_{r}(o)^{c}}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}\tilde{C}_{3}^{q}}{\psi_{m}(r)^{\lambda(1-p)q}}.

Here,
r>0
is a fixed radius,
\lambda
is the constant from (
6.10
),
p\in(0,1)
is defined in Lemma
6.2
, and
\tilde{C}_{1}
and
\tilde{C}_{3}
are constants that do not depend on
r
or
n
.

Then, since
\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi_{m}(r)=\infty
, one can follow the argument in the proof of Proposition
5.2
to infer the lower semi-continuity of the entropy:

\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho_{0}(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{q-1}\int_{M}\rho_{n}(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}x\right).

The lower semi-continuity of the interaction energy can be obtained from the same argument as in Part 2 of the proof of Proposition
5.2
. The thesis of the theorem follows from combining the two facts.
∎

The main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 6.2

(Existence of global minimizer: unbounded curvature case)
.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional Cartan-Hadamard manifold whose sectional curvatures that satisfy (
6.7
), where
c_{m}(\cdot)
is a positive, non-decreasing, and continuous function of the distance from a fixed pole
o
. Also, assume
0<q<1
and that
h
is lower semi-continuous, non-decreasing, and satisfies (
6.8
) and (
6.9
). Then, the energy functional
E[\cdot]
given by (
2.1
) has a global minimizer in
\mathcal{P}_{1,o}(M)
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same argument used to prove Theorem
5.1
, using the tightness of minimizing sequences (Proposition
6.1
), the lower semi-continuity of the energy functional (Proposition
6.2
), and the conservation of the centre of mass (Lemma
5.1
). We also note that a bound analogous to (
5.11
) (see Remark
5.1
) holds in the present case as well, following the proof of Proposition
6.1
. ∎

Example 6.2
.

We consider here several examples of functions
c_{m}
– see also Example
6.1
.

(1)

Constant:
c_{m}(\theta)\equiv c_{m}
. For this case, condition (
6.9
) reduces to (
5.2
), so we recover Theorem
5.1
.

(2)

Power law:
c_{m}(\theta)=\theta^{k}
with
k>0
. Condition (
6.9
) is equivalent in this case to

\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\frac{\lambda}{k/2+1}\theta^{k/2+1}\right)}>0.

(3)

Exponential growth:
c_{m}(\theta)=e^{\beta\theta}
with
\beta>0
. In this case, (
6.9
) becomes

\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\exp\left(\beta\theta\right)\right)}>0.

Therefore, an exponential and a double exponential growth of the potential contains the spreading when the curvatures of the manifold grow algebraically, respectively exponentially, at infinity.

Remark 6.2
.

Two two conditions (
6.6
) and (
6.9
) are complementary, in the sense that they cannot hold at the same time. Indeed, suppose the sectional curvatures of
M
satisfy both a lower and an upper bound:
-c_{m}(r_{x})\leq\mathcal{K}(x;\sigma)\leq-c_{M}(r_{x})
, for all points
x
and all sections
\sigma
, where
c_{m}(\cdot)
is non-decreasing. Suppose
h
satisfies (
6.9
) for some
\tilde{\lambda}>\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{q}
. Then, since
0<q<1
, we have

\tilde{\lambda}>\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{q}>(1-\epsilon)(d-1)(1-q),

for any
0<\epsilon<1
.

Therefore, with
\delta>0
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left((1-\epsilon)(d-1)(1-q)\int_{0}^{\theta/2-\delta}\sqrt{c_{M}(t)}\mathrm{d}t)\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\tilde{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\theta/2-\delta}\sqrt{c_{M}(t)}\mathrm{d}t)\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\tilde{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{M}(t)}\mathrm{d}t)\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\tilde{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{m}(t)}\mathrm{d}t)\right)}

By sending
\theta\to\infty
we then find that (
6.6
) cannot hold simultaneously with (
6.9
), as otherwise it would lead to the following contradiction:

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left((1-\epsilon)(d-1)(1-q)\int_{0}^{\theta/2-\delta}\sqrt{c_{M}(t)}\mathrm{d}t)\right)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{\theta\to\infty}\frac{h(\theta)}{\exp\left(\tilde{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{c_{m}(t)}\mathrm{d}t)\right)}>0.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
6.1

Define
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(\theta)=\frac{\psi_{m}(\theta)}{\psi_{m}^{\prime}(\theta)}.

Then, using (
2.7
), we find that
f
satisfies

\begin{cases}f^{\prime}(\theta)=1-c_{m}(\theta)f^{2}(\theta),\qquad\forall\theta>0,\\[2.0pt]
f(0)=0,\end{cases}

and since
c_{m}
is non-decreasing, we further have

f^{\prime}(\theta)\geq 1-c_{m}(R)f(\theta)^{2},\qquad\forall 0<\theta\leq R,

and

f^{\prime}(\theta)\leq 1-c_{m}(R)f(\theta)^{2},\qquad\forall R\leq\theta.

Let
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the solution to the following IVP:

\begin{cases}g^{\prime}(\theta)=1-c_{m}(R)g(\theta)^{2},\qquad\forall\theta>0,\\[2.0pt]
g(R)=f(R).\end{cases}

By the comparison theorem in ODEs, we first note that

(A.13)

\displaystyle f(\theta)\leq g(\theta),\qquad\forall\theta>0.

The IVP for
g
has the explicit solution

g(\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}}\tanh\left(\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R))+\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)\right),

which used in (
A.13
), gives

f(\theta)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}}\tanh\left(\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R))+\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)\right),\qquad\forall\theta>0.

From the definition of
f
, we further get

\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}\coth\left(\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R))+\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)\right)\leq\frac{\psi_{m}^{\prime}(\theta)}{\psi_{m}(\theta)},\qquad\forall\theta>0,

which is equivalent to

(A.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\ln\left(\sinh\left(\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R))+\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)\right)\right)\leq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\ln\psi_{m}(\theta).

Now, we estimate
\psi(\theta)
for
\theta>R
and
0<\theta<R
separately.

(Case 1:
\theta>R
) Integrate (
A.14
) from
R
to
\theta>R
to find

\psi_{m}(\theta)\geq\psi_{m}(R)\left(\frac{\sinh\left(\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R))+\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)\right)}{\sinh\left(\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R))\right)}\right),\qquad\forall\theta>R.

If we set

(A.15)

\mathcal{B}=\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R)),

then the right hand side of the above inequality can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\psi_{m}(R)\left(\frac{\sinh(\mathcal{B}+\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)}{\sinh\mathcal{B}}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{m}(R)\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R))\left(\frac{1-\exp(-2\mathcal{B}-2\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R))}{1-\exp(-2\mathcal{B})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\psi_{m}(R)\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)).

This leads to the first inequality we need to show (for
\theta\geq R
).

(Case 2:
0<\theta<R
) Integrate (
A.14
) from
\theta<R
to
R
to get

\psi_{m}(\theta)\leq\psi_{m}(R)\left(\frac{\sinh\left(\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R))+\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)\right)}{\sinh\left(\mathrm{arctanh}(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}f(R))\right)}\right),\qquad\forall 0<\theta<R.

Again, use notation (
A.15
) to simplify and estimate the right hand side above as

\displaystyle\psi_{m}(R)\left(\frac{\sinh(\mathcal{B}+\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)}{\sinh\mathcal{B}}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{m}(R)\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R))\left(\frac{1-\exp(-2\mathcal{B}-2\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R))}{1-\exp(-2\mathcal{B})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\psi_{m}(R)\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)),

leading to the second inequality to be shown (for
0<\theta<R
).

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
6.2

Fix a nonnegative function
\rho
on
M
and fix
R>0
. By Hölder inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{q}
and
\frac{1}{1-q}
, we get

(B.16)

\displaystyle\int_{r_{x}<R}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{r_{x}<R}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}\left(\int_{r_{x}<R}1\,\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\psi_{m}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q},

where in the second inequality we used the volume upper bound in Theorem
2.3
. Also by Hölder inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{q}
and
\frac{1}{1-q}
, we find

(B.17)

\displaystyle\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}\left(\int_{r_{x}\geq R}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}\left(dw(d)\int_{R}^{\infty}\psi_{m}(\theta)^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q},

where for the second inequality we used a very similar argument to that used to derive the second inequality in (
4.11
). Namely, we changed variables to geodesic spherical coordinates
x=\exp_{o}(\theta u)
, with
\theta>0
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, in the second integral, and used the upper bound on
J(\exp_{o})
from Theorem
2.3
to bound from above
\mathrm{d}V(x)
– see also (
4.12
).

Denote

\displaystyle C(R):=\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\psi_{m}(\theta)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q},

\displaystyle D(R):=\left(dw(d)\int_{R}^{\infty}\psi_{m}(\theta)^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}.

Then, by combining (
B.16
) and (
B.17
) we get

(B.18)

\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq C(R)\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}+D(R)\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}.

From Lemma
6.1
, we have

\displaystyle C(R)

\displaystyle\leq\left(dw(d)\int_{0}^{R}\left(\psi_{m}(R)\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R)\right)^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)\psi_{m}(R)^{d-1}\exp(-(d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}R)}{(d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}}\left(\exp((d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}R)-1\right)\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{dw(d)\psi_{m}(R)^{d-1}\exp(-(d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}R)}{(d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}}\exp((d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}R)\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)}{(d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}}\right)^{1-q}\psi_{m}(R)^{(d-1)(1-q)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{dw(d)}{(d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(0)}}\right)^{1-q}\psi_{m}(R)^{(d-1)(1-q)}

and (note that
d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}<0
)

\displaystyle D(R)

\displaystyle\leq\left(dw(d)\int_{R}^{\infty}(\psi_{m}(R)\exp(\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}(\theta-R))^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)\psi_{m}(R)^{d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}}\exp\left(\left(-d+1+\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}R\right)}{\left(-d+1+\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}}\exp\left(\left(d-1-\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}R\right)\right)^{1-q}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{dw(d)}{\left(-d+1+\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)\sqrt{c_{m}(R)}}\right)^{1-q}\psi_{m}(R)^{(d-1)(1-q)-\lambda q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{dw(d)}{\left(-d+1+\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)\sqrt{c_{m}(0)}}\right)^{1-q}\psi_{m}(R)^{(d-1)(1-q)-\lambda q}.

If we set

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{1}=\left(\frac{dw(d)}{(d-1)\sqrt{c_{m}(0)}}\right)^{1-q},\qquad\beta_{1}=(d-1)(1-q),

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{2}=\left(\frac{dw(d)}{\left(-d+1+\frac{\lambda q}{1-q}\right)\sqrt{c_{m}(0)}}\right)^{1-q},\qquad\beta_{2}=\lambda q-(d-1)(1-q),

then by the assumptions on
q
and
\lambda
all these constants are positive and we get

C(R)\leq\alpha_{1}\psi_{m}(R)^{\beta_{1}},\quad D(R)\leq\alpha_{2}\psi_{m}(R)^{-\beta_{2}}.

Finally, we substitute the above result to (
B.18
) to get

(B.19)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\leq\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\psi_{m}(R)^{\beta_{1}}\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}+\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\psi_{m}(R)^{-\beta_{2}}\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}.

To find an optimal
R
, we calculate the derivative of the right hand side of (
B.18
) with respect to
R
, and set it to zero:

0=\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\beta_{1}\psi_{m}(R)^{\beta_{1}-1}\psi_{m}^{\prime}(R)\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}-\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\beta_{2}\psi_{m}(R)^{-\beta_{2}-1}\psi_{m}^{\prime}(R)\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}.

Then, we get

\psi_{m}(R)^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\beta_{2}}{\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\beta_{1}}\frac{\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}}{\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}}.

Since
\psi_{m}(0)=0
,
\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\psi_{m}(\theta)=\infty
and
\psi_{m}
is an increasing function, we know there exists a unique
R=R_{*}>0
which satisfies the above equation. Finally, we substitute
R=R_{*}
into (
B.19
) to find

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho(x)^{q}\mathrm{d}V\!(x)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\psi_{m}(R_{*})^{\beta_{1}}\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}+\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\psi_{m}(R_{*})^{-\beta_{2}}\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}{\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\beta_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}+\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\beta_{2}}{\tilde{\alpha}_{1}\beta_{1}}\right)^{-\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{\frac{\beta_{2}q}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{\frac{\beta_{1}q}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}_{1}(\lambda,q,c_{m},d)\left(\int_{M}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{(1-p)q}\left(\int_{M}\psi_{m}(r_{x})^{\lambda}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V\!(x)\right)^{pq},

where

\tilde{C}_{1}(\lambda,q,c_{m},d)=\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{1}^{\beta_{2}}\tilde{\alpha}_{2}^{\beta_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\left(\left(\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}+\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\right)^{\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}\right),

and

p=\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}=\frac{(d-1)(1-q)}{\lambda q}
.

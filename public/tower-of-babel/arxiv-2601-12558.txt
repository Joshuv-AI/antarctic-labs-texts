Remarks on the second Chern class of a foliation
Alan Muniz

Abstract
. We bound the second Chern class of the tangent sheaf of a codimension-one foliation. Equivalently, we bound the degree of the pure codimension-two part of the singular scheme. In particular, for a degree- d d foliation on the projective space, the codimension-two part of its singular scheme must have degree at least d + 1 d+1 . Moreover, equality holds only for rational foliations of type ( 1 , d + 1 ) (1,d+1) . These bounds involve counting an invariant related to first-order unfoldings of 2-dimensional foliated singularities.

Primary: 37F75, 32S65; Secondary: 14J60

1.
Introduction

A codimension-one distribution
\mathscr{F}
on a complex manifold
X
is given by an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow{\rm T}X\longrightarrow\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow 0

where
{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
is the
tangent sheaf
,
{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}
is the
normal bundle
, and
Z=\sing(\mathscr{F})
is the
singular scheme
. The tangent sheaf
{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
is reflexive of rank
\dim X-1
and
{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}
is a line bundle. By Frobenius’ Theorem,
\mathscr{F}
defines a foliation if
{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
is involutive under the Lie bracket of vector fields.

One approach to studying distributions is to investigate the sheaf-theoretic properties of
{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
and its interplay with the singular scheme; see, for instance,
[
4
,
8
,
10
,
7
]
. An important question is how the Frobenius integrability condition impacts the invariants of a foliation. The most prominent result in this direction is the Baum-Bott theorem
[
1
]
, which (in particular) describes how the characteristic classes of
\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}
localize along
Z
. For instance, if
\codim Z\geq 3
one gets
c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})^{2}=0
. One also has that the codimension-two part
Z_{2}
of
Z
must be connected, provided
{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}
is ample, see
[
3
]
. This may fail for non-integrable distributions, see
[
12
, Example 4.6]
.

In
[
10
]
, we observed that foliations of degree
\leq 2
on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
could only have second Chern classes in a certain small range compared to distributions in general. Equivalently, the degree of the codimension-two part of the singular locus must be restricted. In this work, we confirm this expectation. We prove a bound for the second Chern class of a foliation
{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
, when
X
is a projective manifold. Then we specialize to projective spaces. Our first main result is the following general bound.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold with a very ample divisor
H
. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a foliation on
X
. Then we have the following inequality.

\delta(\mathscr{F},H)+P(\mathscr{F})\geq\int_{X}c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})H^{n-2}\geq P(\mathscr{F})

where
P(\mathscr{F})=-(n-2)\int_{X}\left(c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+K_{X}+\frac{(n-1)}{2}H\right)H^{n-1}
.

The number
\delta(\mathscr{F},H)
is defined in §
3
as follows. Given a (closed) point
p\in S\subset X
, let
\omega
be a 1-form defining the restriction
\mathscr{F}|_{S}
around
p
. Then
\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S},p)
is the colength of the ideal generated by the coefficients of
\omega
and
d\omega
. Then
\delta(\mathscr{F},H)
is the sum of
\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S},p)
for a general
S
in the class of
H^{n-2}
. The local index
\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S},p)
counts the first-order unfoldings of the germ of foliation at
p
, see Lemma
2.5
. Moreover,
\delta(\mathscr{F},H)=0
if and only if the non-Kupka singularities of
\mathscr{F}
have codimension at least
3
; if
n=3
this is called
generalized Kupka
.

For
X={\mathbb{P}^{n}}
the projective space, we get a more precise result. In this case,
\delta(\mathscr{F},H)\leq d^{2}
, and equality holds only if
\mathscr{F}
has a rational first integral of type
(1,d+1)
. As usual, we write cohomology classes as integers.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a degree-
d
codimension-one foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
. Then,

d+1\leq\deg Z_{2}\leq d^{2}+d+1

or, equivalently,

d^{2}+\frac{(n-2)(n-1-2d)}{2}\geq c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})\geq\frac{(n-2)(n-1-2d)}{2}.

Moreover, if
\deg Z_{2}=d+1
then
\mathscr{F}
is a rational foliation of type
(1,d+1)
.

For
n=3
, this result improves
[
9
, Proposição 2.6]
, where the author is interested in the geometry of the Gauss map of a foliation. Fassarella’s result was the starting point of this work.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we discuss basic results on foliations. We prove several lemmas that will help us prove the main results. In Section
3
we prove Theorems
A
and
B
, cf. Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n
. A codimension-one
distribution

\mathscr{F}
on
X
is defined by an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow{\rm T}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow 0

The sheaf
{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
is reflexive of rank
n-1
, called the
tangent sheaf
of
\mathscr{F}
, the sheaf
{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}
is a line bundle called the
normal bundle
of
\mathscr{F}
, the ideal sheaf
\mathscr{I}_{Z}
defines the
singular scheme
of
\mathscr{F}
, which is the vanishing locus of the twisted 1-form
\omega\in H^{0}(\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes L)
. A codimension-one
foliation
on
X
is a distribution as above satisfying the integrability condition
[{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}},{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}]\subset{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
or, equivalently,
\omega\wedge d\omega=0
.

We discuss below some preliminary results needed in the subsequent sections.

2.1.
Lifting to the affine cone

Let
H
be a very ample divisor on
X
defining an embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
, and let
C_{X}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}
be the affine cone of
X
. The radial vector field
{\rm rad}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}x_{j}{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
preserving
C_{X}
. A twisted differential form
\theta\in H^{0}(\Omega_{X}^{p}(kH))
can be regarded as a differential form on
C_{X}
such that
{\rm rad}\cont\theta=0
and
\mathcal{L}_{\rm rad}\theta=k\theta
; where

\mathcal{L}_{v}\theta=v\cont d\theta+d(v\cont\theta)

is the Lie derivative on
C_{X}
. This construction allows us to make sense of the exterior derivative of a twisted form on
X
as a homogeneous differential form on
C_{X}
. We can also regard these forms as global sections of exterior powers of the affine cotangent bundles on
X
.

The Chern class
c_{1}(H)\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{C})=H^{1}(\Omega_{X}^{1})
defines a nontrivial extension

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm rad}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{{\rm T}X}\longrightarrow{\rm T}X\longrightarrow 0.

The vector bundle
\widehat{{\rm T}X}
is called the
affine tangent bundle
of
X
with respect to
H
. For
X={\mathbb{P}^{n}}
and
H
the hyperplane class, this is precisely the Euler sequence. In particular,
\widehat{{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)^{n+1}
.

The
affine cotangent bundle
is the dual
\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{1}:=\widehat{{\rm T}X}^{\vee}
. We also denote
\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}:={\textstyle\bigwedge}^{p}\widehat{{\rm T}X}^{\vee}
. We have the short exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{p}\longrightarrow\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm rad}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega_{X}^{p-1}\longrightarrow 0

By this construction, we identify homogeneous differential forms on
C_{X}
with global sections of
\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}(kH)
for some
k\geq 0
.

For
1\leq p\leq n
, the rank of
\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}(kH)
is strictly bigger than
n
. Thus, a general global section does not vanish anywhere. Suppose we are given
\theta\in H^{0}(\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}(kH))
such that
{\rm rad}\cont\theta\neq 0
and whose vanishing locus
W
is nonempty. One interesting question is whether there is an optimal bound for the degree of
W
. If
W
is zero-dimensional, we have the following bound.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\theta\in H^{0}(\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}(kH))
be a section whose vanishing locus is a zero-dimensional subscheme
W
. Then the length of
W
is bounded

\ell(W)\leq((n+1-p+k)H+K_{X})^{n}.

Proof.

First,
W
defined by the image of
\theta^{\vee}\colon(\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}(kH))^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Moreover, we have the natural isomorphism
(\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p})^{\vee}\cong\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{n+1-p}(-K_{X})
. Note that
\widehat{{\rm T}X}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)^{N+1}
for
N=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))
; hence
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-tH)^{N+1}\twoheadrightarrow\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{t}
. Then,

\mathcal{O}_{X}((p-n-1-k)H-K_{X})^{\binom{N+1}{p}}\twoheadrightarrow\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{n+1-p}(-kH-K_{X})\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{I}_{W}

hence
\mathscr{I}_{W}(K_{X}+(n+1-p+k)H)
is globally generated. Localizing at a point of
W
, one sees that
h^{0}(\mathscr{I}_{W}(K_{X}+(n+1-p+k)H))\geq\codim W
. Then
W
is contained in a complete intersection of
n
divisors of class
K_{X}+(n+1-p+k)H
, and the result follows by Bézout.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

For
X={\mathbb{P}^{n}}
and
H
the hyperplane class, one gets
\ell(W)\leq(k-p)^{n}
. This is expected since
\widehat{\Omega}_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}}^{p}(k)\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(k-p)^{\binom{n+1}{p}}
. However, for other varieties, this bound is less effective. Fiz
X\subset{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
a hypersurface of degree
l
, and
H
the hyperplane class. For
\theta\in H^{0}(\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{2}(kH))
,
W
is contained in the zero locus of
{\rm rad}\cont\theta
, which is bounded by
c_{2}(\Omega^{1}_{X}(kH))
. Then one gets

\int_{X}c_{2}(\Omega^{1}_{X}(kH))-((1+k)H+K_{X})^{2}=k(2-l)+2l-3.

Thus, the top Chern class gives a better bound for
l\geq 3
. We wonder whether there is an optimal bound for
\ell(W)
(in the zero-dimensional case) on other manifolds besides the projective spaces.

Our main interest is to study
d\omega
for a given
\omega\in H^{0}(\Omega_{X}^{p}(kH))
, in particular, its vanishing locus. The following lemma is a consequence of the Euler relation for homogeneous polynomials.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\omega\in H^{0}(\Omega_{X}^{p}(kH))
. Then the ideal sheaf of the vanishing locus of
d\omega\in H^{0}(\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{p}(kH))
is locally generated by the coefficients of
\omega
and
d\omega
.

Proof.

First, we note that the module of differential forms on
C_{X}
are obtained from polynomial differential forms on
\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
and modding out by the ideal of
X
and its differentials. Hence, we only need to prove the result for polynomial homogeneous forms.

Let
\omega
be a homogeneous polynomial
p
-form of total degree
k
in the variables
x_{0},\dots,x_{N}
. We will restrict it to the affine chart
\{x_{0}=1\}
and compare the ideals generated by the coefficients before and after restriction. We need to fix some notation.

Given
I=\{j_{1}<\dots<j_{r}\}\subset\{0,\dots,N\}
, we use the multi-index notation
dx_{I}=dx_{j_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{j_{r}}
. For
i\not\in I
, define the sign
dx_{i}\wedge dx_{I}=(-1)^{\sigma(i,I)}dx_{I\cup\{i\}}
. Then we write
\omega=\sum_{|I|=p}A_{I}dx_{I}
, for
A_{I}
homogeneous polynomials of degree
k-p
. The equation
{\rm rad}\cont\omega=0
translates to:

(1)

\forall J\mid|J|=p-1,\quad\sum_{j\not\in J}x_{j}A_{J\cup\{j\}}(-1)^{\sigma(j,J)}=0.

Moreover,

(2)

d\omega=\sum_{|J|=p+1}\left(\sum_{i\in J}\frac{\partial A_{J\setminus\{i\}}}{\partial x_{i}}(-1)^{\sigma(i,J\setminus\{i\})}\right)dx_{J}.

Next, we restrict to the affine chart
\{x_{0}=1\}
and compare the ideals defined by
d\omega
. Let
a_{J}=A_{J}|_{x_{0}=1}
. Note that restricting
\omega
and
d\omega
to
\{x_{0}=1\}
eliminates every term with a
dx_{0}
. Let
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
denote the ideal generated by the coefficients of
d\omega
modulo
(x_{0}-1)
, and let
\mathcal{I}_{0}
be the ideal generated by the coefficients of
d\omega|_{x_{0}=1}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i\in J}\frac{\partial a_{J\setminus\{i\}}}{\partial x_{i}}(-1)^{\sigma(i,J\setminus\{i\})}\,\Big|\,|J|=p+1,\,0\not\in J\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}_{0}+\left(\frac{\partial A_{J\setminus\{0\}}}{\partial x_{0}}\Big|_{x_{0}=1}+\sum_{i\in J\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\partial a_{J\setminus\{i\}}}{\partial x_{i}}(-1)^{\sigma(i,J\setminus\{i\})}\,\Big|\,0\in J\right).

We want to show that
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{0}+(a_{K}\mid 0\not\in K)
. Fix
K\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
|K|=p
. We will show that the term in display above for
J=\{0\}\cup K
is of the form
k\,a_{K}+\mathcal{I}_{0}
. By the Euler relation and the contraction equation (
1
), one gets

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A_{K}}{\partial x_{0}}\Big|_{x_{0}=1}

\displaystyle=(k-p)a_{K}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}\frac{\partial a_{K}}{\partial x_{j}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial a_{\{0\}\cup K\setminus\{i\}}}{\partial x_{i}}

\displaystyle=-a_{K}(-1)^{\sigma(i,K\setminus\{i\})}-\sum_{j\not\in\{0\}\cup K\setminus\{i\}}x_{j}\frac{\partial a_{\{j\}\cup K\setminus\{i\}}}{\partial x_{i}}(-1)^{\sigma(j,K\setminus\{i\})}.

Therefore, combining these equations, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A_{K}}{\partial x_{0}}\Big|_{x_{0}=1}+\sum_{i\in K}\frac{\partial a_{\{0\}\cup K\setminus\{i\}}}{\partial x_{i}}(-1)^{\sigma(i,\{0\}\cup K\setminus\{i\})}=

\displaystyle\quad=k\,a_{K}-\sum_{j\not\in\{0\}\cup K}(-1)^{\sigma(j,K)}x_{j}\sum_{i\in K\cup\{j\}}\frac{\partial a_{\{j\}\cup K\setminus\{i\}}}{\partial x_{i}}(-1)^{\sigma(i,\{j\}\cup K\setminus\{i\})}

\displaystyle\quad=k\,a_{K}+\mathcal{I}_{0},

which concludes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Chern classes of distributions

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a codimension-one distribution on a complex projective manifold
X
. Considering the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow{\rm T}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{I}_{Z}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow 0

one sees that the Chern classes of
{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
are related to the invariants of
Z
. Indeed, one gets an immediate equation for the Chern characters

ch(\mathcal{O}_{Z})=ch(\mathcal{O}_{X})-(ch({\rm T}X)-ch({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}))ch({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})

Let
Z_{2}
denote the subscheme of
Z
of pure codimension two. The degree of
Z_{2}
with respect to an ample divisor
H
is the intersection product

\deg_{H}(Z_{2})=\int_{X}[Z_{2}]H^{n-2}

which coincides with the leading coefficient of the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
times
(n-2)!
. Note that the lower-dimensional components of
Z
do not affect the leading coefficient. By the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch Theorem, one gets
\deg_{H}(Z_{2})=\int_{X}ch_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z})H^{n-2}
. Using the fact that the Chern character is additive, one further gets

\displaystyle\deg_{H}(Z_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(ch_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})-ch_{2}({\rm T}X)+ch_{2}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}))H^{n-2}

We may then rewrite it to obtain

(3)

\int_{X}c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})H^{n-2}=\int_{X}(K_{X}c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+c_{2}({\rm T}X)+c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})^{2})H^{n-2}+\\
-\deg_{H}(Z_{2}).

The degree of
Z_{2}
is bounded above by the number of singularities of
\mathscr{F}
restricted to a general surface of class
H^{n-2}
. Then we get the following general inequality.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a codimension-one distribution on a complex projective manifold
X
, and let
H
be a very ample divisor. Then

(4)

\int_{X}c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})H^{n-2}\geq-(n-2)\int_{X}\Bigg(c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+K_{X}+\frac{(n-1)}{2}H\Bigg)H^{n-1}.

One could ask that
H
be only ample and work with
aH
for some
a\gg 0
. The only problem is that the right-hand side is not homogeneous on
a
, yielding a weaker result.

Proof.

Let
S
be a general surface in the class
H^{n-2}
transverse to
\mathscr{F}
. Then
Z_{2}\cap S\subset\sing(\mathscr{F}|_{S})
. In particular,

\deg_{H}(Z_{2})=\int_{S}[Z_{2}\cap S]\leq\ell(\sing(\mathscr{F}|_{S}))=\int_{S}c_{2}(\Omega^{1}_{S}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}).

On the other hand, the conormal sequence for
S
is

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{S}(-H)^{n-2}\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{1}|_{S}\longrightarrow\Omega_{S}^{1}\longrightarrow 0.

By adjunction,
K_{S}=(K_{X}+(n-2)H)|_{S}
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{S}c_{2}(\Omega_{S}^{1}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\frac{c_{1}(\Omega_{S}^{1}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})^{2}}{2}-ch_{2}(\Omega_{S}^{1}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\Bigg((c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+(n-2)H+K_{X})c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{2}({\rm T}X)+(n-2)K_{X}H+\frac{(n-2)(n-1)}{2}H^{2}\Bigg)H^{n-2}.

Therefore, by equation (
3
) and the above discussion,

\displaystyle\int_{X}c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})H^{n-2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{X}(K_{X}c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+c_{2}({\rm T}X)+c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})^{2})H^{n-2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{S}c_{2}(\Omega_{S}^{1}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle=-(n-2)\int_{X}\Bigg(c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+K_{X}+\frac{(n-1)}{2}H\Bigg)H^{n-1}.

∎

Recall that the discriminant of a coherent sheaf
F
of rank
r
is
\Delta(F)=2r\,c_{2}(F)-(r-1)^{2}c_{1}(F)^{2}
. If
F
is torsion-free and
\mu_{H}
-semistable, then it satisfies the Bogomolov inequality
\int_{X}\Delta(F)H^{n-2}\geq 0
. From Lemma
2.4
we deduce the following weaker inequality, without the semistability hypothesis:

(5)

\int_{X}\Delta({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})H^{n-2}\geq-(n-2)\int_{X}(K_{X}+c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+(n-1)H)^{2}H^{n-2}.

For
X={\mathbb{P}^{n}}
,
H
the hyperplane class, and
c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})=(d+2)H
, equation (
3
) and inequalities , (
4
) and (
5
) become

(6)

\displaystyle c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle=d^{2}+\frac{(n-3)(n-2d)}{2}+2-\deg(Z_{2}),

(7)

\displaystyle c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(n-2)(n-1-2d)}{2}

(8)

\displaystyle\Delta({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle\geq-(n-2)d^{2}.

For our purposes, we only need the second Chern class. However, it would also be interesting to describe the higher Chern classes of
{\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}}
in terms of
Z
. For the case
X={\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, see
[
4
]
.

2.3.
A Local Invariant

Consider a germ of codimension-one foliation
\mathscr{G}
on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
defined by a germ of 1-form
\eta
, singular at the origin. Define the integer

(9)

\delta(\mathscr{G},0):=\ell\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}}{J(\eta,d\eta)}\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}}{J(\eta,d\eta)},

where
J(\eta,d\eta)
denotes the ideal generated by the coefficients of
\eta
and
d\eta
.
This number does not depend on the choice of
\eta
defining
\mathscr{G}
and is an analytic invariant.

The local invariant
\delta(\mathscr{G},0)
is clearly bounded above by the Milnor number
\mu(\mathscr{G},0)=\ell\left({\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}}/{J(\eta)}\right)
. Moreover, it is related to the first-order unfoldings of
\mathscr{G}
. Following Suwa
[
14
]
, define

I(\eta)=\{\,h\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}\mid hd\eta=\eta\wedge\theta,\,\text{for some}\,\theta\in\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}\,\}.

This ideal is sometimes referred to as the ideal of
persistent singularities
; see
[
5
]
and references therein. If
\eta=a\,dx+b\,dy
, then
d\eta=f\,dx\wedge dy
where
f=\frac{\partial b}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial a}{\partial y}
. Hence,

I(\eta)=(J(\eta):(f))=(J(\eta):J(\eta,d\eta)).

First-order unfoldings of
\mathscr{G}
are parameterized by the set
U(\mathscr{G})=I(\eta)/J(\eta)
, see
[
14
, §4]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a germ of foliation on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. Then,
\ell(U(\mathscr{G}))=\delta(\mathscr{G},0)
.

Proof.

If
\mathscr{G}
is regular, then
\ell(U(\mathscr{G}))=\delta(\mathscr{G},0)=0
. Assume
\mathscr{G}
is singular. Consider the algebra
A=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{2},0}/J(\eta)
. Since
a
and
b
have no common factors,
A
is a complete intersection ring of dimension zero, hence Artinian Gorenstein. Moreover,
U(\mathscr{G})
is a finite
A
-module, and we have

U(\mathscr{G})=\ann_{A}(\overline{f})=\Hom_{A}(A/(\overline{f}),A).

Therefore, by Matlis’ duality
[
2
, Proposition 3.2.12]
,

\ell(U(\mathscr{G}))=\ell(\Hom_{A}(A/(\overline{f}),A))=\ell(A/(\overline{f}))=\delta(\mathscr{G},0).

∎

Foliations satisfying
U(\mathscr{G})=0
are then rigid, i.e., every unfolding is trivial, see
[
14
, §6]
. In light of the Lemma above, this means that either
\mathscr{G}
is regular, or
d\eta(0)\neq 0
, a Kupka singularity. On the other end, if
\delta(\mathscr{G},0)=\mu(\mathscr{G},0)
, then
U(\mathscr{G})=A
and there exists a holomorphic 1-form
\theta
such that
d\eta=\eta\wedge\theta
, or, equivalently,
\eta
admits a first-order unfolding

\widetilde{\eta}=\eta+t\theta+dt

corresponding to the class of
1\in U(\mathscr{G})
. If this unfolding extends to an infinitesimal unfolding of
\eta
, then
\eta=gdf
for some holomorphic functions
f,g
, see
[
13
, Proposition 6.14]
.

The invariant
\delta(\mathscr{G},0)
plays a major role in our main result. The following is our key lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a germ of codimension-one foliation on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
singular along a subscheme of pure codimension two
Z
. Let
(S,0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be a germ of smooth surface transverse to
\mathscr{F}
. Then the intersection multiplicity
(Z\cdot S)_{0}
satisfies

\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0)\geq(Z\cdot S)_{0}\geq\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0)-\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0),

where
\mu
denotes the Milnor number.

Proof.

Let
\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}dx_{j}\in\Omega_{\mathbb{C}^{n},0}^{1}
a germ of integrable 1-form defining
\mathscr{F}
. Up to a change of coordinates, we may suppose that
S=V(x_{3},\dots,x_{n})
and denote by
\overline{a_{j}}
the class of
a_{j}
modulo
(x_{3},\dots,x_{n})
. Let
W=\sing(\mathscr{F}|_{W})
. Then

I_{(Z\cap S)}=(\overline{a_{1}},\dots,\overline{a_{n}})\quad\text{and}\quad I_{W}=(\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}}).

The inequality
\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0)\geq(Z\cdot S)_{0}
follows directly.

Consider the quotient ring
A=\mathcal{O}_{S}/I_{W}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{n},0}/(a_{1},a_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{n})
and the ideal
J=I_{(Z\cap S)}\cdot A=I_{(Z\cap S)}/I_{W}
. Then, from the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{S}/I_{(Z\cap S)}\longrightarrow 0

one gets

(Z\cdot S)_{0}=\ell(\mathcal{O}_{S}/I_{(Z\cap S)})=\ell(\mathcal{O}_{S}/I_{W})-\ell(J)=\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0)-\ell(J).

To conclude, we only need to prove that
\ell(J)\leq\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0)
. Note that
\mathscr{F}|_{S}
is defined by
\overline{\omega}=\overline{a_{1}}dx_{1}+\overline{a_{2}}dx_{2}
and
d\overline{\omega}=fdx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}
for
f=\frac{\partial\overline{a_{2}}}{\partial x_{1}}-\frac{\partial\overline{a_{1}}}{\partial x_{2}}
. Then

\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0)=\ell\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{S}}{\left(\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}},f\right)}\right)=\ell\left(A/(f)\right).

On the other hand, the integrability equation
\omega\wedge d\omega=0
translates to

a_{k}\left(\frac{\partial a_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)-a_{j}\left(\frac{\partial a_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial a_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}\right)+a_{i}\left(\frac{\partial a_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial a_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}\right)=0,\quad\forall i<j<k.

Therefore, for every
k\geq 3
we have
\overline{a_{k}}f=0\in A
. This means that
J\subset\ann_{A}(f)
; in particular,
\ell(J)\leq\ell(\ann_{A}(f))
. As in the proof of Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\ell(J)\leq\ell(\ann_{A}(f))=\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0).

∎

Note that
\mathscr{F}
can be seen as an unfolding of
\mathscr{F}|_{S}
. If
\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0)=0
then
\mathscr{F}
has a Kupka singularity. In particular,
Z
must be smooth and
\mathscr{F}
is biholomorphic to a foliation given by a 1-form in two variables, a trivial unfolding.

Following the proof of Lemma
2.6
, one sees that if
Z
is a (local) complete intersection, then
\mu(\mathscr{F}|_{S},0)=(Z\cdot S)_{0}
.

Finally, we remark that
\delta(\mathscr{G},0)
can be defined, mutatis mutandis, for one-dimensional foliations on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
with an isolated singularity. Then, the corresponding version of Lemma
2.5
, although the relation to unfolding is less clear. An analogue of Lemma
2.6
for this situation eludes us for the moment.

3.
Main Results

First, we define the global counterpart of the invariant
\delta
. Given a foliation
\mathscr{G}
on a surface
S
, define

\delta(\mathscr{G}):=\sum_{p\in\sing(\mathscr{G})}\delta(\mathscr{G},p).

Given a foliation
\mathscr{F}
on a complex projective manifold
X
of dimension
n\geq 3
with a very ample divisor
H
, define

\delta(\mathscr{F},H):=\delta(\mathscr{F}|_{S})

for a generic surface
S
of class
H^{n-2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[Theorem
A
]
Let
X
be a complex projective manifold with a very ample divisor
H
. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a foliation on
X
. Then we have the following inequality.

\delta(\mathscr{F},H)+P(\mathscr{F})\geq\int_{X}c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})H^{n-2}\geq P(\mathscr{F})

where
P(\mathscr{F})=-(n-2)\int_{X}\left(c_{1}({\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})+K_{X}+\frac{(n-1)}{2}H\right)H^{n-1}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a general complete intersection surface in the class of
H^{n-2}
such that
\mathscr{F}
is transversal to
S
. Thus
\mathscr{F}|_{S}
has normal bundle
{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}|_{S}
and
S
intersects
Z_{2}
transversely and avoids lower-dimensional components of
Z
. By Lemma
2.6
,

(10)

c_{2}(\Omega^{1}_{S}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})\geq\deg_{H}(\sing(\mathscr{F})_{2})\geq c_{2}(\Omega^{1}_{S}\otimes{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}})-\delta(\mathscr{F},H)

Then, by Lemma
2.4
, we get the lower bound. The upper bound follows from the same argument in Lemma
2.4
and the inequalities above.
∎

For
X={\mathbb{P}^{n}}
,
H
the hyperplane class and
{\rm N}_{\mathscr{F}}={\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d+2)
we can get more. Indeed, in this case, we may apply Lemma
2.1
.

Corollary 3.2
.

[Theorem
B
]
Let
\mathscr{F}
be a degree-
d
codimension-one foliation on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
. Then

d+1\leq\deg Z_{2}\leq d^{2}+d+1

or, equivalently,

d^{2}+\frac{(n-2)(n-1-2d)}{2}\geq c_{2}({\rm T}_{\mathscr{F}})\geq\frac{(n-2)(n-1-2d)}{2}.

Moreover, if
\deg Z_{2}=d+1
then
\mathscr{F}
is a rational foliation of type
(1,d+1)
.

The argument for constructing the rational first integral follows the proof of
[
9
, Proposição 2.6]
, which draws from
[
11
, Proposition 5.1]
.

Proof.

First, the inequalities follow from Theorem
3.1
and
\delta(\mathscr{F},H)\leq d^{2}
, due to Lemma
2.1
.

Let
L
be a generic
2
-plane in general position with respect to
\mathscr{F}
, and
\omega
be a homogenous
1
-form defining
\mathscr{F}|_{L}
. We can write

d\omega=Adx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}-Bdx_{0}\wedge dx_{3}+Cdx_{0}\wedge dx_{1},

where
A,B,C
have degree
d
. If
\deg Z_{2}=d+1
, then
\delta(\mathscr{F}|L)=d^{2}
.
Noether’s AF+BG Theorem then yields a nontrivial relation
\alpha A+\beta B+\gamma C=0
with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, not all zero. Define
E=\alpha x_{0}+\beta x_{1}+\gamma x_{2}
. Then
d\omega\wedge dE=0
, hence
d\omega=\theta\wedge dE
for some homogeneous
1
-form
\theta
. Contracting with the radial vector field, we get

(d+2)\omega={\rm rad}\cont d\omega=({\rm rad}\cont\theta)dE-E\theta.

Then the rational
1
-form
\frac{\omega}{E^{d+2}}
is closed. Applying
[
6
, Lemma 9]
, there exists a closed rational
1
-form
\eta
extending
\frac{\omega}{E^{d+2}}
and defining
\mathscr{F}
outside its polar divisor
(\eta)_{\infty}
. Since
L
is generic,
(\eta)_{\infty}
is supported on a hyperplane
H
. Moreover,
\eta|_{{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\setminus H}
is exact and regular. Hence,
\mathscr{F}
has a first integral of the form
F/H^{d+1}
.
∎

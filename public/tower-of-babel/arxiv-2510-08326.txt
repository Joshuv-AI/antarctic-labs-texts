Abstract
. Lacquerware, a representative craft of Chinese intangible cultural heritage (ICH), is renowned for its layered aesthetics and durability yet faces declining engagement. While HCI research has explored embedding interactive circuits to transform lacquerware into interactive artifacts, prior work has emphasized fabrication techniques, overlooking support for makers to creatively design such interactions at low thresholds. To address this gap, we present LacAIDes , a GenAI-powered creativity-support tool built on a multi-agent workflow aligned with the double diamond model of design thinking. LacAIDes enables exploration and crafting of culturally aligned interactive circuits without requiring prior expertise. We evaluated LacAIDes in a longitudinal workshop with 14 participants using a mixed-method approach. Results show that LacAIDes achieved high usability, enhanced creativity in crafting practices, and fostered critical reflection on GenAI’s role in digital craft making. This work contributes to HCI with a novel creativity-support tool and empirical insights into revitalizing ICH craft making through GenAI.

1.
INTRODUCTION

Traditional crafts encode human culture and everyday aesthetics, yet shifts in modern technology and production have eroded their sustaining contexts, accelerating intangible cultural heritage (ICH) loss and spurring efforts to preserve and revitalize craftsmanship in the digital era

(
Lu et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2024
;
Song et al. 2019
)
. In the field of Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), the innovation and enlivenment of traditional crafts have been gradually shifting from
being digitized for viewing
to
being digitally enabled for making
-supporting contemporary craft-making, creative (re) interpretation, and knowledge transfer within living craft practices
(
Liu et al. 2023
)
. Building on this shift, recent HCI studies have explored the design and fabrication of interactive circuits on traditional craft interfaces (e.g., ceramics, lacquerware), as a novel form of digital making in ICH context, to bring interactive and digital craftsmanship into everyday life
(
Zheng et al. 2023
;
Ikegawa et al. 2018
;
Saito et al. 2020
;
Dong et al. 2025
)
.

Within this line of work, lacquerware-a traditional craft in China
(
wangjinyao 2025b
)
-has emerged as a substrate for integrating interactive circuits in the HCI field
(
Ikegawa et al. 2018
;
Saito et al. 2020
;
Dong et al. 2025
)
. However, most of these HCI studies emphasize fabrication techniques and the technical feasibility of circuits, with limited user-centered consideration. This then leaves a research gap: how to empower users’ (e.g., craftspeople, designers, and hobbyists) creative design process of crafting interactive circuits. Specifically, such creativity support should encompass the following user-centered functions: (1) providing users with systematic process guidance that structures the relatively unfamiliar workflow into clear stages, enabling them to efficiently complete the novel design task through a coherent methodology; (2) aligning users’ design ideas with the visual characteristics and cultural meanings of traditional lacquerware patterns, thereby assisting them in generating circuit patterns that resonate with the aesthetic and symbolic values of this craft; and (3) lowering the technical threshold for interactive circuit design, ensuring the accessibility of this tool for general users with varying levels of domain knowledge and thus broadening participation in the creative design and digital making process.

The aforementioned gaps and requirements are in line with what has been previously discussed by
Dong et al. 2025
, as future research directions. To address them, Generative AI (GenAI) can contribute as it has already demonstrated great strengths in supporting users’ creativity to conduct creative design to revitalize ICH in the HCI field
(
Wang et al. 2025b
;
Tao et al. 2025
)
. Moreover, it was also acknowledged by
Dong et al. 2025
as a promising technology to be leveraged to support creative design processes of interactive circuits on lacquerware interfaces.

Thus, we aim to answer the following three research questions (RQ):

•

RQ1:
What are the challenges and needs of crafting interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware interfaces?

•

RQ2:
How can GenAI be leveraged to design and develop a creativity-support tool to empower the design process of crafting interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware interfaces?

•

RQ3:
How do participants experience their human-GenAI co-design process of crafting interactive circuits to revitalize traditional lacquerware?

In response, we began with a formative study with four traditional lacquerware craftsmen and four college students to understand their challenges and needs of crafting interactive circuits on lacquerware interfaces. Insights from this study then informed the development of design goals and led to the development of a GenAI-empowered Double Diamond Model-guided creativity-support tool,
LacAIDes
. This tool was implemented as a multi-agent system comprising four GenAI agents, each providing targeted support corresponding to one of the four stages of the Double Diamond Model. Finally, the tool enables users to export manufacturable files of interactive circuits. After that, we organized a longitudinal design workshop lasting one month with 14 participants, which required them to use
LacAIDes
to create interactive circuits and conduct DIY fabrication of lacquerware. We used a mixed-method approach to explore their experience using
LacAIDes
to co-create interactive digital craftsmanship. The results demonstrated the usability of
LacAIDes
and its great potential in augmenting users’ creativity to design interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware interfaces. Our findings also provided insight into the broader issue of using GenAI-powered tools to support creative digital production to revitalize ICH.

The main contributions of this research are therefore:

•

Empirical findings
from a formative study that identified key challenges facing artisans and hobbyists when designing and crafting interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware interfaces.

•

LacAIDes
, a GenAI-empowered, multi-agent, Double Diamond Model-guided creativity-support tool that can systematically guide users to craft creative, culturally aligned, and technically feasible interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware interfaces.

•

Empirical evidence
from a mixed-method evaluation with 34 participants shows that
LacAIDes
achieved high usability, enhanced creativity in crafting practices, and fostered critical reflection on the role of GenAI in digital craft making, contributing to the revitalization of intangible cultural heritage (ICH) through technology-enabled innovative hybrid craft making.

Making Interactive Circuits for Hybrid Craft

Hybrid craft
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
, also known as "digital craft", is a broad concept characterized by incorporating physical and digital elements. It could be manifested in the raw materials, crafting process, techniques, and tools, and the final craft product
(
Devendorf and Ryokai 2015
;
Gotfrid et al. 2021
;
Jacobs and Zoran 2015
;
Tsaknaki et al. 2017
;

)
. Within the hybrid craft context in HCI, researchers have incorporated traditional crafts into diverse approaches to creating interactive electronic artifacts. The traditional crafts that have been studied include textiles
(
Perner-Wilson et al. 2010
;
Posch and Kurbak 2016
;
Devendorf et al. 2016
;
Honnet et al. 2020
;
Wu and Devendorf 2020
)
, paper
(
Qi and Buechley 2014
;
Shorter et al. 2014
;
Zheng et al. 2019
;
Torres et al. 2019
)
, body decorations
(
Sun et al. 2020
;
Kao et al. 2016
)
, ceramics
(
Zheng et al. 2023
)
, and lacquerware
(
Ikegawa et al. 2018
;
Saito et al. 2020
;
Dong et al. 2025
)
. These studies share a common feature: they uncover new electronic functionalities in both traditional and novel materials by translating craft practices into interactive systems
(
Zheng et al. 2023
)
. However, challenges and research gaps still remain. Current research in this area typically focuses either on integrating material qualities inherent in traditional crafts into HCI, or on introducing novel processes that extend the practices and communities engaged in crafting interactive systems
(
Buechley and Perner-Wilson 2012
;
Zheng et al. 2023
)
. In general, they center on novel fabrication techniques, new craft or material types in HCI, overlooking the maker-centered aspect in craft-making practices-how to empower makers’ creative process when designing and fabricating interactive circuits. This emerging need is necessary, as makers sometimes do not have enough domain-specific knowledge (e.g., circuit design) or creativity during their workflow. Therefore, our work aims to fill in this research gap and real-world maker’s challenges through developing a digital creativity-support tool to assist the creative design and fabrication of interactive circuits for traditional craft.

2.2.
Digital Making for Crafts

In the field of HCI, digital making focuses on developing new digital tools to support making practice, aiming to achieve the desire to democratize hardware use and innovation by heightening access to making
(
Bardzell et al. 2017
)
. As mentioned in Section
2.1
, developing a creativity-support tool to facilitate making practice is one of the digital making approaches.

In recent years, HCI researchers have integrated digital making into the domain of crafts, empowering enthusiasts to create artifacts that were once challenging to produce on their own
(
Liu et al. 2023
)
. For example,
Efrat et al. 2016
produced a catalog of Honeycomb Smocking patterns along with computer-aided design software. This allowed individuals without prior embroidery skills to fabricate a variety of bags featuring smocking patterns through digital design and manufacturing processes.
Horn et al. 2022
introduced an open-source online platform that supports slab-based ceramics by enabling users to model simple 3D forms and automatically generate both flat patterns and compatible 3D-printable slump molds-lowering the entry barrier for beginners in this craft. Such efforts contribute to the democratization of technology, broadening participation across diverse backgrounds and strengthening inclusivity within maker communities
(
Lindtner et al. 2014
;
Schoemann and Nitsche 2017
)
. A notable example is LilyPad Arduino
(
Buechley and Perner-Wilson 2012
)
, which simplifies the creation of electronic textiles and has been particularly effective in encouraging gender diversity, as E-textiles have proven to be especially engaging for women entering electronics and computing.

In general, these state-of-the-art studies have demonstrated great potential of these digital technologies in promoting makers’ capabilities in the crafting process. However, in this AI-augmented era, an interesting question emerges: what if AI technologies empower current digital making methods to better support makers’ interaction design practices? This question leads to the literature review in Section
2.3
.

2.3.
GenAI-powered Creativity-support Tools for Enlivening Intangible Cultural Heritage

Generative AI (GenAI), encompassing language models
(
Radford et al. 2018
;
Radford et al. 2019
)
, image-generation models
(
Goodfellow et al. 2020
;
Kingma and Welling 2013
)
, and multi-modal models
(
Radford et al. 2021
;
Rombach et al. 2022
)
, has utilized its extensive training resources and capabilities in multi-modal information alignment to effectively understand and generate diverse content, thereby promoting user creativity. In the field of HCI, GenAI technologies have been applied to enliven intangible cultural heritage through creative design. For example,
Wang et al. 2025b
developed HarmonyCut, a GenAI-powered creativity-support tool for paper-cutting pattern design. It transforms users’ abstract intentions into structured and culturally meaningful paper-cutting design ideas. Meanwhile, it supports creativity by suggesting relevant knowledge, works, and patterns, and enabling users to select, combine, and adjust elements to produce diverse yet culturally aligned designs.
Tao et al. 2025
proposed AIFiligree, a GenAI-based framework for designing exquisite filigree artworks. It leverages AI-generated content to generate authentic fine filigree patterns and complex 3D structures, streamlining the traditionally labor-intensive craft. It supports diverse design workflows, enabling both experts and designers to efficiently explore and visualize cultural filigree aesthetics. These studies demonstrated GenAI’s great potential in supporting creative design to enliven ICH, including traditional crafts.

However, if we situate these studies in craft craft-making context, they still have drawbacks. Current studies merely applied such creativity-support tools in concept generation phases, overlooking their applications in the more tangible making process. For instance,
(
Tao et al. 2025
)
only proposed a GenAI framework to generate conceptual images of filigree artworks, rather than apply this framework in real-world filigree artworks making practices. Therefore, together with what has been mentioned in
2.1
, we aim to expand such a tool’s application to the whole crafting process, especially selecting the crafting of interactive circuits as a case to explore.

2.4.
Lacquerware as a Locus for Digital Craftsmanship Research in HCI

Lacquer art is one of the most important traditional intangible cultural heritages in Chinese history, and its roots can be traced back over 8,000 years
(
wangjinyao 2025a
)
. Originating from the sap of the lacquer tree, this ancient craftsmanship blends a complex making process, including intricate woodworking, carving, inlaying, and painting, to create everyday objects (e.g., furniture, musical instruments) of both functional beauty and profound cultural significance
(
Snyder 1989
)
. Besides its cultural value, it also strongly affects the cultures of neighboring countries and regions of East Asia (e.g., Japan, Korea) and Southeast Asian countries (e.g., Vietnam, Thailand)
(
Sung et al. 2016
)
.

However, with the rapid advancement of industrialization and shifting cultural contexts, lacquerware is now confronted with multiple challenges. First, lacquer art embodies tacit and highly individualized knowledge that has traditionally relied on master-apprentice or family-based transmission, yet the declining number of skilled artisans threatens the continuity of this craft. Second, due to its labor-intensive processes, lengthy production cycles, and high raw material costs, lacquerware remains expensive and therefore lacks broad integration into everyday consumption. Finally, contemporary younger generations increasingly value products characterized by digital, interactive, and smart features, while traditional lacquerware offers limited resonance with these preferences; moreover, most artisans possess little expertise in digital technologies, which restricts opportunities for innovative design of this craft
(
Vogl 2000
;
Dong et al. 2025
)
.

To address these challenges, HCI researchers have explored using interactive circuits to innovatively enliven traditional lacquerware. For example,
Ikegawa et al. 2018
demonstrated how multilayered electronic circuits made with lacquer can be fabricated to create aesthetically refined touch interfaces.
Saito et al. 2020
explored technically transforming traditional Japanese lacquerware patterns into NFC antenna designs.
Dong et al. 2025
leveraged a multi-layer structure and material properties of lacquerware to embed interactive circuits and integrate programmable hardware within the layers. These studies revealed the technical feasibility and solution of crafting interactive circuits on lacquerware interfaces; however, they overlook the approaches to empowering users to design such interactions. This emerging demand is important and necessary because users (e.g., artisans, amateurs) normally do not have adequate knowledge to design circuits and may need creativity support to help them generate creative concepts of culturally-aligned circuit patterns. To this end, GenAI’s special affordances in content generation, as mentioned in Section
2.3
, could help address these challenges. Therefore, our work fills this gap by proposing
LacAIDes
, a GenAI-powered, multi-agent, creativity-support system to guide users to craft interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware interfaces (Figure
2
).

FORMATIVE STUDY: DESIGN SPACE EXPLORATION

To understand the key challenges faced by the makers when they craft interactive circuits on the traditional lacquerware interfaces and find design opportunities to address them, we conducted a formative study comprising a scenario-based design workshop and focus group with artisans and amateurs (e.g., college students).

As a result of the formative study, we synthesize and report (1)
challenges
(Section
3.2
), (2)
design goals

3.3
), and (3)
design features
of proposed creativity-support tool for designing interactive circuit (Section
3.4

3.1.
Study Methodology

3.1.1.
Participants and Procedure

We recruited 10 participants (4 females and 6 males; aged between 19 and 48 years old, Mean (M)= 30.4, Standard Deviation (SD)= 10.2) in total, including 6 artisans (F1-F6) and 4 college students (F7-F10). The inclusion criteria include that the participants should have a passion for enlivening ICH using digital technologies and have no prior experience in crafting interactive circuits on lacquerware interfaces. Each participant was compensated with $ 10 for the involvement and contribution. This study was approved by the institutional ethics committee of the first author in this paper.

After recruiting these participants, our study included two in-person sessions-a 60-minute scenario-based design workshop and a subsequent 30-minute focus group. As for the workshop, we presented a scenario of using interactive circuits to enable traditional lacquerware to become an interactive electronic artifact. We also introduced the technological path of crafting circuits used in current HCI studies
(
Ikegawa et al. 2018
;
Saito et al. 2020
;
Dong et al. 2025
)
, and provided a toolkit consisting of computer, paper, pen, scissors, Arduino Uno R3, and copper foil. Participants were then invited to use the toolkit to freely illustrate and prototype their ideal circuit pattern, while trying to achieve the interactive functions with Arduino. They were allowed to pause this task at any time if they found it very difficult to manage. Then, the interview questions used in the focus group probed the participants’ (1) reflections on the overall making experiences and challenges faced during the circuit creation task of the workshop, and (2) expectations for future support (e.g., specific technology, functions) in their interactive circuit crafting practices.

3.1.2.
Data Collection and Analysis

The interviews were audio-recorded with participants’ consent and later
transcribed into text. Two authors conducted a thematic analysis
(
Clarke and Braun 2014
)
on the transcripts, where both authors read the transcripts to gain a sense of the data and then developed a set of initial codes independently. They then discussed their initial codebooks and reached an agreement. Following this, the emerged themes were identified and reported in Section
3.2
and
3.3
.

3.2.
Challenges

Firstly, based on our formative design workshop and interviews, we synthesized three major challenges (C1-C3) faced by participants when designing interactive circuit patterns on lacquerware without dedicated support. These challenges highlight the difficulties in structuring the creative process, the gaps in required domain knowledge, and the absence of continuous support tools.

3.2.1.
C1: Unstructured Design Thinking Process

Participants struggled with an unstructured design thinking process, often unsure how to proceed from a vague idea to a concrete interactive design. Without a clear roadmap, many jumped arbitrarily between brainstorming aesthetics and figuring out circuitry, leading to confusion and wasted effort. For example, one participant remarked, "
I had ideas for an interactive pattern, but I didn’t know where to start or what steps to follow
" (F3). This lack of a structured approach meant that participants had trouble breaking the design into manageable stages, frequently revisiting earlier decisions and feeling overwhelmed (F4, F7, F8, F10). Overall, the formative study revealed that a systematic, step-by-step workflow was missing, causing frustration and hindering progress.

3.2.2.
C2: Limited Domain-Specific Knowledge of Lacquer Art and Circuit Design

Another key challenge was insufficient domain-specific knowledge, either in traditional lacquer art (e.g., patterns), electronic circuit design, or both. All participants (N=10) had no prior experience combining interactive circuits with lacquerware, and each tended to lack expertise in at least one relevant domain. For instance, an artisan participant struggled with the electronics, admitting "
I’m not familiar with circuits, so I was constantly afraid I’d do something wrong with the wiring
" (F1), while a tech-savvy student was unsure about the art, noting "
I don’t really know traditional lacquer patterns, so my design might not look culturally authentic
" (F7). Such knowledge gaps limited their creativity and confidence: artisans (N=6) were hesitant about electronic feasibility, and hobbyists (N=4) were unsure how to honor the craft’s aesthetics. Participants often paused to seek external information or expert help, reflecting that the absence of embedded cultural and technical knowledge made the design task intimidating and error-prone (F5, F9). This underscored the need for a system that could embed cultural motifs and circuit design principles as foundational knowledge, guiding users toward designs that are both culturally aligned and technically feasible.

3.2.3.
C3: Lack of Creativity and Crafting Support Throughout the Workflow

Participants also reported a lack of creativity and making support across the entire process of designing and prototyping interactive lacquerware. Without any assisting tools or guidance, they had to rely solely on their personal creativity and skills, which led to frequent impasses. Many described working in isolation as challenging-there were no idea prompts, feedback, or troubleshooting aids to help when they got stuck. "
It would have been great to have some kind of assistant or tool giving me suggestions when I ran out of ideas
," said one participant (F5), highlighting the desire for creative guidance. Others mentioned uncertainty during fabrication and integration of the circuit, since no support was available to validate their approaches or inspire alternate solutions (F1, F2, F7). Participants emphasized that support should not only provide occasional suggestions but should accompany them throughout the workflow, ensuring creativity is sustained from ideation to digital craft making (F2, F3). This demonstrated the need for continuous, stage-specific support that could actively enhance creativity across the process.

3.3.
Design Goals

Building upon the challenges and expectations identified in our formative study, we summarize three design goals (DG1-DG3) that guide the development of our creativity-support tool.

3.3.1.
DG1: Provide Clear Step-by-Step Guidance from Early Design Intentions to Technically Feasible Designs

We aim to give makers a structured workflow that leads them from vague ideas to manufacturable circuit-integrated lacquerware designs. Responding to C1, DG1 organizes the process into clear stages so participants are not left uncertain during practice. As one participant noted, "
having a clear sequence of steps or checkpoints would help me turn my idea into a real design without getting lost
" (F8). This roadmap ensures that both novices and experts can systematically progress toward viable outcomes.

3.3.2.
DG2: Pre-Embed Domain-Specific Cultural and Technical Knowledge as Just-in-Time Reference

We aim to embed cultural and technical knowledge as foundational priors within the system, responding to C2. Rather than merely showing references, LacAIDes conditions generation and validation on lacquer motifs, visual grammar, and basic circuit rules. This allows the tool to automatically produce designs that are both culturally aligned and technically feasible. As one artisan explained, "
I wasn’t sure if my pattern still looked culturally authentic, and I worried I might connect the circuit wrong
" (F2). By lowering expertise barriers, DG2 enables makers from diverse backgrounds to confidently create interactive lacquerware, making the craft more accessible and sustainable.

3.3.3.
DG3: Integrate Stage-Specific Agents to Support Creativity Across the Workflow

We aim to connect the structured workflow (DG1) and embedded knowledge (DG2) through a coordinated set of GenAI agents. Each agent serves as a domain-specific expert and aligns with a Double Diamond stage-expanding ideas, generating culturally informed motifs, translating them into circuits, and validating outputs. By chaining these agents, the system automates transitions, applies cultural and technical priors consistently, and makes the process more efficient. As one participant envisioned, "
a tool that carries me through each step so I can spend more energy on being creative
" (F5). DG3 thus leverages GenAI as a continuous co-designer, enhancing both efficiency and creativity throughout the workflow.

3.4.
Proposed Design Features

To operationalize the design goals, we propose three design features (DF1-DF3) for
LacAIDes
, each directly mapped to the corresponding goal and challenge.

3.4.1.
DF1: Double Diamond Model-Guided Tool Workflow

Addressing C1 and DG1,
LacAIDes
follows a workflow of the Double Diamond Model in design thinking
(
Design Council 2025
)
that systematically organizes the creative process into four stages-
discover
,
define
,
develop
, and
deliver
. The interface can prompt users with stage-specific tasks, ensuring a clear path from early intentions to manufacturable designs, and preventing users from feeling lost.

3.4.2.
DF2: Domain-Specific Knowledge Support

Responding to C2 and DG2,
LacAIDes
embeds cultural and technical knowledge as foundational priors. By integrating lacquer motifs and circuit design rules into generation and validation, LacAIDes helps users produce culturally aligned and technically feasible patterns. This lowers barriers for diverse makers and supports heritage revitalization.

3.4.3.
DF3: GenAI-Powered Multi-Agent System

Extending C3 and DG3,
LacAIDes
deploys multiple stage-specific GenAI agents aligned with the Double Diamond Model. Agents assist in ideation, motif generation, circuit design, and validation, linking DG1’s structured process with DG2’s embedded knowledge. This orchestration automatically streamlines the workflow and frees users to focus on creative exploration.

4.
LacAIDes
: TOOL DESIGN & IMPLEMENTATION

In this section, we first provide a walkthrough of an example user scenario to introduce the workflow of
LacAIDes
. Based on this workflow, we further introduce the technical implementation details of each key feature (e.g., GenAI-powered multi-agent system) of
LacAIDes
.
LacAIDes
adopts the Double Diamond model of design thinking as its core workflow, structuring the design of interactive circuits on lacquerware interfaces into four stages-
discover
,
define
,
develop
, and
deliver
.

At each stage, a dedicated GenAI agent provides task-specific guidance and support. Therefore,
LacAIDes
is a GenAI-powered multi-agent creativity-support tool following the basic workflow of the Double Diamond Model of design thinking. Specifically, the functions of each GenAI agent (four agents in total) are:

•

Discover Agent:
it assists the user in ideation, through iteratively inspiring multiple creative concepts that are relevant to the user’s initial design intentions.

•

Define Agent:
based on the multiple creative concepts offered by the
Discover Agent
, it provides recommendations to the user on which concept suits the design intention best, and the user selects one final concept. After that, the
Define Agent
generates prompts of the selected concept, to be used in the next stage for text-to-image generation.

•

Develop Agent:
user inputs the prompts provided by the
Define Agent
(can make some revisions and edits on the prompts), to ask the previously fine-tuned LoRA-based
Develop Agent
to generate images of traditional lacquer patterns, and finally user should choose one as the final pattern for circuit conversion.

•

Deliver Agent:
After generating the traditional lacquer pattern,
LacAIDes
converts the vector image of this lacquer pattern into a circuit design following a certain technical path. Then, the
Deliver Agent
verifies the circuit design to ensure the circuit can work and be manufactured.

4.2.
Key Features & Technical Implementations

The architecture of
LacAIDes
integrates four GenAI agents to form a GenAI-enhanced Double Diamond Model-driven workflow that converts natural language descriptions into producible interactive circuit patterns on lacquerware interfaces. The pipeline employs four key technical features and implementation methods, including (1) prompt engineering, (2) LoRA model fine-tuning, (3) deterministic image-to-path processing, and (4) rule-based verification via an interactive web-based user interface. The capabilities of
LacAIDes
are highlighted through four key technical features (KFs), one per functionality of the four GenAI agents.

4.2.1.
KF1: Natural Language and Feature Understanding for Creative Concepts Generation.

The primary objective of the first feature is to convert imprecise user design intentions into creative concepts, accomplished through the
Discover Agent

Discover Agent

Few-shot cases are presented with consistent extraction results, while clarification questions provide precise disambiguation when the confidence level falls below a specific threshold. This process ensures robust feature extraction even under unclear or multilingual input, establishing an organized platform for subsequent synthesis. 4.2.2.
KF2: Prompt Generation and Parameterization for Text-to-Image Generation of Lacquer Pattern.

The second feature integrates extracted components into normalized diffusion model prompts. Using deterministic templates with manufacturability indicators (such as closed bold lines or black-on-white rendering), the

A grid search across parameters such as resolution, classifier-free guidance (CFG) coefficient, and random seed yields reproducible yet tunable generation outputs. This successfully bridges semantic design intent with technical parameters. The detailed prompt engineering examples can be found in Section

In summary, all four GenAI agents surface in a React single-page interface arranged into four panels-Prompt, Directive, Generation, and Verification-each mapped to one agent. Designers work in an 8-10 second loop with live previews, parameter controls, and manufacturability checks to iteratively shape lacquer-circuit patterns, balancing GenAI assistance with human intent. Under the hood, the backend dockerizes four GenAI agents as microservices: the
Discover Agent
and
Define Agent
brokers LLM calls to GLM-4;
Develop Agent
runs generative inference on an NVIDIA A100; the
Deliver Agent

The frontend is a React app built with Vite and Tailwind CSS, using IndexedDB for offline caching and in-browser WASM for responsive edits and on-device checks. For reproducibility, each DXF export ships with a JSON sidecar capturing seeds, parameters, and prompt history, and Dockerfiles/scripts enable one-command replication. Training data comes from licensed design archives with cultural attribution.

5.
USER STUDY

To understand how
LacAIDes
support makers’ creative process when crafting interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware interfaces, as well as
LacAIDes
’s effects on stimulating their reflections on the broader issue of using GenAI-powered design tools to enliven ICH, we conducted a user study comprising (1) a one-month longitudinal design workshop and (2) a mixed-method evaluation with artisans and hobbyists. The user study protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committee of the first author of this paper.

5.1.
Participants

We initially recruited 34 participants in total, through online posting on campus forums and social media. Among these participants, three are artisans, with an average working experience of 10 years, and the rest are college students. All the artisans involved in this study did not have prior experience in designing with GenAI tools, while all the college students had such previous experience. During this user study, participants might develop skin allergies due to the allergenic substances in lacquer
(
Wang et al. 2025a
;
Ma et al. 2012
)
. Therefore, at the time of recruitment, we informed them of the potential risks with the consent form and ensured that they had full autonomy to withdraw immediately should any allergic reactions or other health issues arise. Meanwhile, as this user study lasted for a long period of time (one month), participants were also allowed to exit this user study on the way if they felt impatient or had other issues.

Considering these facts, finally 14 participants (7 females, 7 males; aged between 18 and 22, M=19.5, SD=1.2; 3 artisans, 11 college students; P1-P14) remained and completed the whole process of the user study. All the participants voluntarily participated in this user study, and can take the interactive lacquerware artifact created by them away, as a souvenir for their involvement and contribution.

5.2.
Design Workshop Procedure

The one-month longitudinal design workshop required participants to (1) complete the whole procedure of traditional lacquerware production, (2) create an interactive circuit pattern under the guidance of
LacAIDes
, and (3) fabricate the circuit and add interactive functions using Arduino IDE. The whole procedure was instructed by an expert craftsman and researchers, to ensure that participants can master the basic workflow of traditional lacquerware making, as well as the use of
LacAIDes
system to craft interactive circuits. As the traditional lacquerware production workflow requires long-term efforts, and we aim to immerse participants in a mixed cultural experience which embodies both traditional and digital craft making practices, therefore, the whole procedure lasted for a long period of time. 7.
DISCUSSION

Our work introduces
LacAIDes
, a GenAI-powered, multi-agent, Double Diamond Model-based creativity-support tool which empowers makers from diverse backgrounds to craft interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware interfaces.
LacAIDes
was then employed in a one-month longitudinal design workshop to engage the artisans and hobbyists in creative practices. Our empirical findings reveal that
LacAIDes
has a relatively high level of usability and can support users’ creativity. Through interviews, we also understand that user develop their experience, understanding, and reflection towards the broader issues of integrating GenAI into traditional craft-making practices. Our research expands the current HCI research landscape of embedding interactive circuits onto lacquerware interfaces from a more user-centered perspective, which primarily focuses on exploring novel circuit fabrication techniques based on craft interfaces. Building upon this research, we discuss the following four aspects.

7.1.
Democratizing Digital Craft Making through GenAI-Supported Creativity and Accessibility for All

As studied in prior HCI research, which developed novel fabrication techniques to embed interactive circuits on lacquerware interfaces
(
Ikegawa et al. 2018
;
Saito et al. 2020
;
Dong et al. 2025
)
, these studies often required high entry thresholds in domain-specific knowledge, no matter of cultural (traditional lacquer art) or technical (e.g., circuit design) aspects.

In response, our work solves this challenge by proposing
LacAIDes
, which democratizes digital craft making to provide creativity and accessibility for all kinds of makers.
LacAIDes
is powered by GenAI and embedded with a prior knowledge base regarding the traditional lacquer pattern design and circuit design. Therefore, our work responds to the HCI community’s previous discussions on the topic of "democratizing making for all"
(
Vyas et al. 2023
)
. Specifically, we democratized the digital making practices from the following two dimensions: creativity and accessibility.

Firstly, we augmented users’ creativity through introducing GenAI agents to assist in their ideation when designing interactive circuits. In this way, our research recalls what has been studied in
(
Wang et al. 2025b
;
Tao et al. 2025
)
, where a GenAI-supported tool in the ICH context benefited users’ expressive abilities. Based on that,
LacAIDes
advances the interactive functions of the current creativity-support tool for ICH from facilitating 2D visual image generation to support more tangible craft-making practices.

Secondly, we lowered the entry thresholds for all users of
LacAIDes
to support accessibility. Specifically, we embedded a dataset of 946 traditional lacquer patterns as a cultural knowledge base, as well as circuit design knowledge, to enable users to create more culturally aligned and technically feasible circuit patterns. This design consideration

7.2.
Design Implications

Drawing on the empirical findings derived from the user study, we propose the following two design implications for future design and development of a creativity-support tool for craft-making practices to enliven ICH.

7.2.1.
Co-Designing with Craft Community to Develop Creativity-Support Tool

In our user study, we noticed that artisans report that sometimes
LacAIDes
might generate circuit patterns that are mismatched with the cultural essence of traditional lacquer art. This empirical evidence suggests to us that in the future, when we design and develop new tools to support ICH enlivening, we should consider the craft community’s voice in the design process. Through co-designing with craft community
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
, we can dynamically adjust GenAI-powered tool’s functions grounded in community’s real-work practices and insights, to build more context-aware support tool to empower more culturally aligned digital craft making practices
(
Liu et al. 2024
;
Tao et al. 2025
;
Yao et al. 2023
)
.

7.2.2.
Towards More Collaborative and Embodied GenAI-Supported Experiences

In
LacAIDes
, we focused more on supporting individual users’ digital making practices, which overlooked the collaborative nature of embodiedly designing traditional craft in community-based practice. As suggested in
(
Cheatle and Jackson 2023
)
, democratization must do more than just lower technical thresholds-it should encode craft’s accountabilities to materials and ecologies, support "head-heart-hand" ways of making, and sustain collaborative, embodied pedagogies. Guided by these principles, we thus propose that future tools can be integrated with more features that promote human-human collaboration and embodied interactions, to better empower users’ creative process.

7.3.
Ethical Considerations of Applying GenAI in Traditional Crafting Practices

Though our work leverages GenAI to support interactive circuit design within a digital craftsmanship context, ethical issues regarding the use of GenAI also emerge. Specifically, we discuss and reflect on these ethical considerations from the following three aspects.

Cultural authenticity and misrepresentation.
In ICH, "authenticity" is not only about preserving a fixed form; it also concerns living meanings, materials, and lineages that evolve over time
(
Su 2018
;
Bardzell et al. 2012
)
. However, GenAI sometimes lacks sufficient cultural grounding and can output homogenized or incorrect motifs that drift from tradition, although we provided large amount of prior domain-specific knowledge input as databases to fine-tune these GenAI models
(
Wang et al. 2025b
)
. In our user study, one artisan questioned the accuracy of
LacAIDes
in generating lacquer patterns: "
it seems that this system tries to provide me with a culturally aligned pattern image, but GenAI does not have intentions as what we humans have, what if it mistakenly provides images that violate the cultural essence?
". This might then cause the misrepresentation of craft’s cultural meaning, which requires future work to consider involving traditional artisans to adjust the potential risks during the technical development process.

Originality, authorship, and rights.
It is a common debate on the intellectual property issues in human-GenAI co-created design. In traditional craftsmanship, authorship is typically grounded in mastery and labor-not only in the final form but also in the embodied process throughout craft making
(
Cant 2020
;
Dadi 2024
)
. However, in this work, GenAI complicates this by blending human prompts, which have been discussed as a potential risk in enlivening ICH.

Influences on the indigenous or craft community’s knowledge.
While GenAI can help document, teach, and revitalize endangered craft making (e.g., lacquer art), it can also disembed tacit know-how from its social and ritual contexts
(
Putjorn et al. 2024
)
, accelerate uncontrolled diffusion of sensitive motifs, and concentrate value away from indigenous craft communities. To mitigate these risks, we should adopt consent-based, community-governed knowledge bases with tiered access in future practices cite sagdeo2024exploratory.

7.4.
Limitations & Future Work

We also acknowledge that our work has several limitations that need to be addressed in future work. Firstly, we did not include a control group in the user study, to effectively evaluate
LacAIDes
. This is because currently there are no such tools that can serve as a baseline to be applied in the user study; therefore, we only evaluated
LacAIDes
in the user study. However, we think that such an evaluation could also provide a preliminary overview of
LacAIDes
’s usability and capabilities in supporting users’ creative process. Secondly,
LacAIDes
also has several technical limitations. For example, the
Develop Agent
sometimes failed to generate suitable images to be converted into a circuit design file by
LacAIDes
. This limitation requires future work to fine-tune a more stable LoRA model to better support users’ creative making process.

8.
Conclusion

This work advances digital (hybrid) craft making by showing how GenAI can support the end-to-end design of interactive circuits on traditional lacquerware. Based on the key challenges faced by artisans and hobbyists during their interactive circuit design surfaced from our formative study, we presented
LacAIDes
, a GenAI-powered, Double-Diamond-Model-guided, multi-agent creativity-support tool that carries makers from preliminary design intent articulation to manufacturable circuit design, while ensuring design outputs remain both culturally aligned and technically feasible. After that, we organized a one-month longitudinal design workshop with 14 participants, to engage them in experiencing traditional lacquerware production workflow, as well as designing and fabricating interactive circuits with
LacAIDes
. Through the mixed-method evaluation with 14 participants, we found that
LacAIDes
has a relatively high level of usability and can support users’ creativity, while prompting reflection on the role of GenAI in digital craft making for ICH enlivening. Therefore, this research contributes to the HCI field by introducing a GenAI-powered creativity tool to support ICH revitalization through empowering makers from diverse backgrounds in digital craft-making practices.

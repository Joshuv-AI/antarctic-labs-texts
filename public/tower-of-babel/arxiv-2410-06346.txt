A remark on the Langlands correspondence for tori Thanks: MDM was partly supported by the special research fund (BOF) from Ghent University [BOF20/PDO/058].
Marcelo De Martino, Eric Opdam

Abstract
. For an algebraic torus defined over a local (or global) field F F , a celebrated result of R.P. Langlands establishes a natural homomorphism from the group of continuous cohomology classes of the Weil group, valued in the dual torus, onto the space of complex characters of the rational points of the torus (or automorphic characters in the global case). We expand on this result by detailing its topological aspects. We show that if we topologize the relevant spaces of continuous homomorphisms and continuous cochains using the compact-open topology, Langlands’s map becomes a (surjective, finite-to-one) homomorphism of abelian complex Lie groups. Moreover, we demonstrate that, in both the local and global settings, the subset of unramified characters is the identity component of the relevant space of characters. Finally, we compare the group of unramified characters with the Galois (co)invariants of the dual torus.

[

As is well-known

Proposition 6
(
[

]

The space

3.
Remarks on the compact-open topology

In this paper we will deal a lot with the compact-open topology on the space of continuous functions between two topological spaces, so we gather in this section some relevant information about this topology, for convenience of the reader.

Let
A,B
be topological spaces. Given any
K\subset A
compact and
W\subset B
open, we let
V(K,W)=\{f:A\to B\textup{ continuous }\mid f(K)\subset W\}
. The compact-open topology on the space
\mathscr{C}(A,B)
of continuous functions between
A
and
B
is the topology generated by the sub-basis
\{V(K,W)\mid K\subset A\textup{ compact and }W\subset B\textup{ open}\}
.

Furthermore, recall that a topological space
A
is called hemicompact if there is a sequence
K_{1},K_{2},\ldots
of compact subsets of
A
such that
A=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{n}
and any compact subset
K
of
A
is contained in a finite union
K_{n_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n_{p}}
of compact sets in that sequence. The multiplicative groups
C_{F}
(with
F
local or global) and finite extensions of them are examples of hemicompact topological spaces.

Proposition 10
.

The compact-open topology on
\mathscr{C}(A,B)
satisfies the following properties.

(a)

If
B
is a metric space, then a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
in
\mathscr{C}(A,B)
converges in the compact-open topology to a continuous function
f
if, and only if,
f_{n}
converges uniformly to
f\in\mathscr{C}(A,B)
on any compact subset
K
of
A
.

(b)

If
B
is a metric space, then
\mathscr{C}(A,B)
is metrizable if and only if
A
is hemicompact.

(c)

If
B
is a metric space and
A
is hemicompact, then the compact-open topology on
\mathscr{C}(A,B)
is entirely
determined by sequential uniform convergence on compact sets.

(d)

If
A,B
are topological groups with
A
locally compact Hausdorff and
B
Hausdorff, then the space of continuous homomorphisms
\Hom_{c}(A,B)
is a closed subspace of
\mathscr{C}(A,B)
.

(e)

If
A,B_{1},B_{2}
are topological groups with
A
locally compact Hausdorff, and
B_{1},B_{2}
metric spaces then

\Hom_{c}(A,B_{1}\times B_{2})\approx\Hom_{c}(A,B_{1})\times\Hom_{c}(A,B_{2}).

(f)

If
A_{1},A_{2},B
are topological groups with
A_{1},A_{2}
locally compact Hausdorff and
B
an abelian group
with a metric topology, then

\Hom_{c}(A_{1}\times A_{2},B)\approx\Hom_{c}(A_{1},B)\times\Hom_{c}(A_{2},B).

Proof.

Item (a) is
[
2
, Theorem 6]
and (b)
[
2
, Theorems 7 and 8]
. Item (c) is a consequence of (a) and (b). For item (d), note that
\Hom_{c}(A,B)=F^{-1}(\{c_{e}\})
where
c_{e}
is the constant homomorphism
c_{e}:A\times A\to B
that maps
(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto e_{B}
for all
(a_{1},a_{2})\in A\times A
and where

F:\mathscr{C}(A,B)\to\mathscr{C}(A\times A,B)
is given by

F(f)(a_{1},a_{2})=f(a_{1})f(a_{2})f(a_{1}a_{2})^{-1}.

The assumption on
B
implies that
\{c_{e}\}
is closed (see
[
2
, Theorem 1]
), while the assumption on
A
implies that evaluation map
\mathscr{C}(A,B)\times A\to B,(f,a)\mapsto f(a)
is continuous (see
[
2
, Theorem 2]
), from which
F

is continuous, since
F=\mu_{*}\circ\epsilon
with
\epsilon:\mathscr{C}(A,B)\to\mathscr{C}(A\times A,B\times B\times B)
continuous and given by

\epsilon(f)(a_{1},a_{2})=(f(a_{1}),f(a_{2}),f(a_{1}a_{2})),

and
\mu_{*}:\mathscr{C}(A\times A,B\times B\times B)\to\mathscr{C}(A\times A,B)
is the continuous map

g\mapsto\mu_{*}(g)(a_{1},a_{2})=\mu(g(a_{1},a_{2})),

obtained by composing with
\mu:B\times B\times B\to B,(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\mapsto b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}^{-1}
.

For (e), it is shown in
[
6
, Chapitre X, §1.4, Corollaire 1]
that
\mathscr{C}(A,B_{1}\times B_{2})
is homeomorphic to
\mathscr{C}(A,B_{1})\times\mathscr{C}(A,B_{2})
under the milder condition that
B_{1},B_{2}
are uniform spaces and
A
is any topological space, so the claim in this item follows from (d). For (f) we adapt the argument of
[
9
, Theorem (23.18)]
in the case when
B
is the unit circle. Let
\theta:\Hom_{c}(A_{1},B)\times\Hom_{c}(A_{2},B)\to\Hom_{c}(A_{1}\times A_{2},B)
be defined by

\theta(f_{1},f_{2})(a_{1},a_{2})=f_{1}(a_{1})f_{2}(a_{2}).

This map is an isomorphism of groups (see the first paragraph of the proof of
[
9
, Theorem (23.18)]
). Now, given
K\subset A_{1}\times A_{2}
compact and
\varepsilon>0
, let
V(K,\varepsilon)
denote a neighborhood of the trivial homomorphism in
\Hom_{c}(A_{1}\times A_{2},B)
whose elements send
K
into

, the open ball of radius
\varepsilon
around the identity element

. Similarly, by using the canonical projections, let
K_{j}=\textup{pr}_{j}(K)\subset A_{j}
for
j=1,2
and let
V(K_{j},\varepsilon)
be the open subset of
\Hom_{c}(A_{j},B)
of all homomorphisms sending
K_{j}
into
B(e_{B},\varepsilon)
. Then, as the metric in
B
can be assumed to be left-invariant (see
[
9
, Theorem (8.3)]
), we obtain that
\theta(V(K_{1},\varepsilon/2)\times V(K_{2},\varepsilon/2))\subset V(K,\varepsilon)
. Conversely, if

is an arbitrary neighborhood of the trivial homomorphism in
\Hom_{c}(A_{j},B)
, with
K_{j}
compact, for
j=1,2
, by setting
K=K_{1}\cup\{e_{A_{1}}\}\times K_{2}\cup\{e_{A_{2}}\}
, then

V(K,\min(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))\subset\theta(V(K_{1},\varepsilon_{1})\times V(K_{2},\varepsilon_{2}))

which shows that

is a homeomorphism.
∎

4.
Unramified characters

In this section, we will recall the definitions of unramified characters of a torus and establish some facts about them. We start by recalling the relevant absolute values in the groups we are interested in.

4.1.
Absolute values

Suppose first that
F
is a local field endowed with a normalized absolute value
|\cdot|_{F}:F\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with respect to which
F

4.2.

Definition 14

We now recall how to extend this notion of unramified characters of the multiplicative groups

4.3.
Unramified characters: local setting

In this section,

{E/F}

Note that each

Let
T(F)^{1}=\cap_{\chi}\ker(|\chi|_{F})
, with

.

Definition 16
.

A continuous homomorphism

.
Denote by

The group

. However, we will delay its study until after we describe the space of complex characters of the group

4.4.
Unramified characters: global setting

In the global case, given a torus
T
defined over a global field

and splitting over a finite Galois extension
E/F
,
any element
\chi\in X^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
defines, for each

, an algebraic homomorphism
\chi_{v}:T_{v}\to F_{v}^{*},t_{v}\mapsto\chi_{v}(t_{v})
and a continuous homomorphism
|\chi|_{F}:T(\mathbb{A}_{F})\to V_{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}

defined by

(t_{v})_{v}\mapsto\prod_{v}|\chi_{v}(t_{v})|_{v}.

Definition 17
.

Given
T
defined over
F
and splitting over
E/F
, let
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})^{1}=\cap_{\chi}\ker|\chi|_{F}

with
\chi
running over

. An

of

T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)\to\mathbb{C}^{*}

.

It is well known
[
14
, Section I.1.4]
that the group

of unramified characters is a connected abelian Lie group with a complex analytic structure. We will recall this in more detail in the next subsection.

4.5.
Compactness and identity components

In this section, we shall study some topological features of the space
\Hom_{c}(\Hom_{\Gamma_{E/F}}(X,C_{E}),\mathbb{C}^{*})
.
In light of Langlands’s result, Theorem
7
, the information extracted in this section will provide a proof of Theorem
1
. To ease notations, let us make the convention

(8)

T_{C_{F}}:=\Hom_{\Gamma_{E/F}}(X,C_{E}).

We assume here that the field
F
is either local or global with finite Galois extension
E/F
splitting
T
. The group
T_{C_{F}}
is a locally compact topological group with topology determined by that of
C_{E}
.
It is known that when
F
is local we have an identification

T_{C_{F}}=T(F)
while for
F
global we have a closed inclusion with finite-index
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)\subset T_{C_{F}}
(see
[
12
, p. 245]
).

It is convenient to extend the notion of the group of unramified characters to
T_{C_{F}}
as well.
If
\chi\in X^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
is a rational character of
T
, then for any
t\in T_{C_{F}}
, we have
\chi(t)\in C_{F}
and hence
|\chi|_{F}=|\cdot|_{F}\circ\chi
is a continuous character of
T_{C_{F}}
. Similarly to the definitions in the local and global cases, let us write

(9)

T_{C_{F}}^{1}=\cap_{\chi\in X^{\Gamma_{E/F}}}\ker(|\chi|_{F}).

Finally, we say that a continuous character
\phi
of
T_{C_{F}}
is
unramified
if
\phi
vanishes on
T_{C_{F}}^{1}
, and we denote by

\widetilde{X}_{T}:=\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*})

the
group of unramified characters of
T_{C_{F}}
.

Proposition 18
.

For
F
local or global,
we have
that
T_{C_{F}}^{1}
is the maximal compact subgroup of
T_{C_{F}}
.

Proof.

We claim that
T_{C_{F}}^{1}=\Hom_{\Gamma_{E/F}}(X,C^{1}_{E})
. Indeed, note that by (
9
), we have
\Hom_{\Gamma_{E/F}}(X,C^{1}_{E})\subseteq T_{C_{F}}^{1}
.
Conversely, let
t\notin\Hom_{\Gamma_{E/F}}(X,C^{1}_{E})
, that is,
t\in T_{C_{F}}
and
there exists
x\in X
with
|x(t)|_{E}>1
. We show that
t\not\in T^{1}_{C_{F}}
. Indeed, for
x\in X
as above, let

\tilde{x}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{{E/F}}}\gamma(x)\in X^{\Gamma_{{E/F}}}

and note that
\tilde{x}(t)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{{E/F}}}\gamma(x(t))=N_{E/F}(x(t))\in C_{F},

where
N_{E/F}
is the norm map. It follows that
|\tilde{x}(t)|_{F}=|N_{E/F}(x(t))|_{F}=|x(t)|_{E}>1,
from which we conclude that
t\not\in T_{C_{F}}^{1}
. As
C_{E}^{1}
is compact, then
T_{C_{F}}^{1}
is compact. Now,
T^{1}_{C_{F}}
contains every compact subgroup of
T_{C_{F}}
, since
\mathbb{R}_{+}
does not admit any non-trivial compact subgroups. This finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 19
.

If
F
is a global field,
then
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})^{1}/T(F)
is compact.
If
F
is a local field, then
T(F)^{1}
is the maximal compact subgroup of
T(F)
.

Proof.

In the global case we have
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})^{1}/T(F)=T^{1}_{C_{F}}\cap(T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F))
, and since
T^{1}_{C_{F}}
is compact and
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)\subset T_{C_{F}}
is closed, then
T^{1}(A_{F})/T(F)
is compact. If
F
local,
T_{C_{F}}=T(F)
so the claim follows from Proposition
18
.
∎

We are interested in describing the quotient
T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1}
and the space
\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*})
. Consider the continuous restriction map
R:\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})\to\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*})
. Its kernel consists of those characters that are trivial on
T_{C_{F}}^{1}
, hence is naturally identified with
\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*})=\widetilde{X}_{T}
.
To further analyse this structure, we now introduce a logarithmic map, similarly to what was done in
[
14
, Section I.1.4]
.
Given the lattice of rational characters
X^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
of
T
, consider the real vector spaces
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{*}=\mathbb{R}\otimes X^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{0}=\Hom(X^{\Gamma_{E/F}},\mathbb{R})
. Define a homomorphism of abelian groups
\widetilde{\log}_{T}:T_{C_{F}}\to\mathfrak{a}_{0}
characterized by

(10)

\langle\chi,\widetilde{\log}_{T}(t)\rangle=\log_{q}(|\chi|_{F}(t)),

for
\chi\in X^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
and
t\in T_{C_{F}}
. Here,
q=e
if
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{R}
while, if
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{Z}
, then
q=q_{F}
is the base of the valuation group
V_{F}
.

Proposition 20
.

Let
F
be a local or global field.
Let
\widetilde{L}_{T}:=\widetilde{\log}_{T}(T_{C_{F}})
. Then
\widetilde{L}_{T}\approx T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1}
.
In the
\mathbb{R}
-cases we have
\widetilde{L}_{T}=\mathfrak{a}_{0}
while in the
\mathbb{Z}
-cases
\widetilde{L}_{T}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is a sublattice of
\Hom(X^{\Gamma_{E/F}},\mathbb{Z})
of finite index.
Furthermore, there is a continuous and open surjection

\mathfrak{e}:\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to\widetilde{X}_{T}
which is an isomorphism in the
\mathbb{R}
-cases and its kernel is
\Hom(\widetilde{L}_{T},\mathbb{Z})
in the
\mathbb{Z}
-cases. In particular, in all cases,
\widetilde{X}_{T}
is a connected abelian complex linear algebraic group whose canonical complex structure is compatible with that of
\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
.

Proof.

In both the
\mathbb{R}
- and
\mathbb{Z}
-cases, we have
\ker(\widetilde{\log}_{T})=T_{C_{F}}^{1}
. By Proposition
18
the map
\widetilde{\log}_{T}
is proper, and hence
defines a topological isomorphism from
T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1}
onto
\widetilde{L}_{T}
.
Thus, we have a topological identification
\widetilde{X}_{T}\approx\Hom_{c}(\widetilde{L}_{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})
. Observe that from this identification, then
\widetilde{X}_{T}
carries a canonical complex structure as elucidated in
[
24
, Sections 2.4 and 4.4]
in the
GL_{1}
-case (see also
[
14
, Section I.1.4]
).

The characterization of
\widetilde{L}_{T}\approx T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1}
as a vector space (in the
\mathbb{R}
-cases), or a lattice (in the
\mathbb{Z}
-cases), follows from (
10
), similarly as was done in
[
14
, pp. 6,7]
.

If
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{R}
, using
\widetilde{L}_{T}=\mathfrak{a}_{0}\cong\mathbb{R}\otimes\Hom(X^{\Gamma_{E/F}},\mathbb{Z})
and the tensor-hom adjunction we obtain

(11)

\widetilde{X}_{T}\cong\Hom_{c}(\widetilde{L}_{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong\Hom_{c}(\mathbb{R},\Hom(\Hom(X^{\Gamma_{E/F}},\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{C}^{*}))\cong\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}},

where we used that any continuous homomorphism between finite-dimensional Lie groups is necessarily smooth (see
[
26
, Theorem 3.39]
). Furthermore, denoting by
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
the complexification of
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{*}
, note that
\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\cong\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

In the
\mathbb{Z}
-cases, we have that
\widetilde{L}_{T}\subseteq\Hom(X^{\Gamma_{E/F}},\mathbb{Z})

is a sublattice (by the same argument as for the analogous
case of
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, see
[
14
, pp. 6,7]
). In particular
this inclusion is of finite index, and we have
\Hom(\widetilde{L}_{T},\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}\otimes\Hom(\widetilde{L}_{T},\mathbb{Z})\cong\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
.
As
\widetilde{L}_{T}
is free (and discrete), applying
\Hom(\widetilde{L}_{T},-)
to the exponential exact sequence yields

(12)

0\to\Hom(\widetilde{L}_{T},\mathbb{Z})\to\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to\widetilde{X}_{T}\to 0.

Both in (
11
) and (
12
), we see that the canonical complex structure on
\widetilde{X}_{T}
is compatible with the complex structure on
\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
, finishing the proof.
∎

When
F
is global, by using the closed inclusion
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)\subset T_{C_{F}}
, it is clear that the restriction of
\widetilde{\log}_{T}
to
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)
coincides with the map
\log_{T}:T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)\to\mathfrak{a}_{0}
defined in
[
14
, p. 7]
(their map is defined from
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})\to\mathfrak{a}_{0}
, but it clearly factors through
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)\to\mathfrak{a}_{0}
), whose kernel is
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})^{1}/T(F)
. We conclude that there is a topological identification of
T(\mathbb{A})/T(\mathbb{A}_{F})^{1}
with
L_{T}
, the image of
\log_{T}
. When
F
is local, let us write
L_{T}:=\widetilde{L}_{T}
. We have the following characterization of the space
X_{T}
of unramified characters.

Corollary 21
.

Let
F
be a local or global field. There is a continuous and open surjection

\mathfrak{e}:\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to X_{T}
whose kernel is the lattice
\widehat{L}_{T}:=\Hom(L_{T},\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{*}
in the
\mathbb{Z}
-cases, and which is an isomorphism in the
\mathbb{R}
-cases. In particular,
X_{T}
is always a connected abelian complex linear algebraic group whose canonical complex structure is compatible with that of
\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
.

Proof.

Follows from Proposition
20
.
∎

Proposition 22
.

Let
F
be a local or global field.
Then
\widetilde{X}_{T}\approx\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*})=\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\circ}
. Furthermore, we have that
\widetilde{X}_{T}
is an open subgroup of
\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})
, and
\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})/\widetilde{X}_{T}\approx\Hom_{c}(T^{1}_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})
.

Proof.

As
T^{1}_{C_{F}}
is compact, we have
\Hom_{c}(T^{1}_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})=\Hom_{c}(T^{1}_{C_{F}},\mathbb{T})
is discrete, as it is well known that the Pontryagin dual of a compact topological group is discrete
[
9
, (23.17) Theorem]
. Hence, the kernel of
R
,
\widetilde{X}_{T}
, is a subgroup which is open and closed, and then must be a connected component, provided it is connected.
From
[
13
, Theorem, p.158]
, it suffices to show that
T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1}
is torsion-free, which in turn
follows immediately from Proposition
20
.

Finally, to prove the statement about the component group, we need to show that the restriction homomorphism
R:\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})\to\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*})
is surjective. If
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{Z}
then
T^{1}_{C_{F}}\subset T_{C_{F}}
is open, hence any algebraic extension of a continuous complex character of
T^{1}_{C_{F}}
to
T_{C_{F}}
(which exists since
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is divisible) is continuous. For
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{R}
, we have a splitting
C_{E}=C_{E}^{1}\times V_{E}
as
\Gamma_{E/F}
-modules (see Remark
13
), hence a direct product decomposition
T_{C_{F}}=T^{1}_{C_{F}}\times T_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
T_{\mathbb{R}}
is a real split torus. Again, we see that
R
is surjective.
∎

Proposition 23
.

Let
F
be a global field. Then, the restriction map

\rho:\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})\to\Hom_{c}(T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F),\mathbb{C}^{*})

is continuous, open, and closed if both spaces are endowed with the compact-open topology. Moreover,

\rho
is surjective with finite kernel.

Proof.

When
F
is global, we have that
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)\subseteq T_{C_{F}}
is a closed inclusion of finite index
[
12
, p. 245]
. Hence,
T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)
is also an open subgroup of
T_{C_{F}}
and thus
\rho
is a continuous and open surjection (see
[
9
, (24.5) Theorem]
for the
\mathbb{T}
-valued case; their proof that this homomorphism is also open can be easily adapted to the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-valued case). As
\ker(\rho)
is finite, this map is also closed.
∎

Corollary 24
.

If
F
is a local or global field, then
\mathsf{X}_{\mathbb{C}}(T,F)
is an abelian Lie group with complex analytic structure, and identity component

X_{T}
. Furthermore, the component group
\mathsf{X}_{\mathbb{C}}(T,F)/X_{T}\approx\Hom_{c}(T(\mathbb{A}_{F})^{1}/T(F),\mathbb{C}^{*})
.

Proof.

Obviously,
\mathsf{X}_{\mathbb{C}}(T,F)
is an abelian topological group. To show that it can be given a compatible structure of a complex analytic Lie group, it is enough to show this for an open subgroup. Hence, it suffices to show that the subgroup
X_{T}\subset\mathsf{X}_{\mathbb{C}}(T,F)
is open, since
X_{T}
is a connected complex analytic Lie group, by Corollary
21
. The claim that
X_{T}
is the identity component then simply follows,
X_{T}
being closed, open and connected.

If
F
local, we have
T_{C_{F}}=T(F)
, and hence
\widetilde{X}_{T}=X_{T}
. Now Proposition
22
shows that
X_{T}
is an open subgroup.
If
F
is global, then Propositions
22
and
23
show that
X_{T}=\rho(\widetilde{X}_{T})
is open.

Finally, the proof of the claim on the component group is similar to Corollary
22
.
∎

With the description of
T(F)^{1}
at hand, we can be more precise in the description of
X_{T}
, in the local case. Given a subtorus
S
of
T
we let
X(S)
denote its character lattice.

Proposition 25
.

Let
F
be a local field.
The following assertions hold true:

(a)

Let
S\subset T
be the largest
F
-split subtorus of
T
.
The natural homomorphism
S(F)\to T(F)/T(F)^{1}
factors through a homomorphism

S(F)/S(F)^{1}\to T(F)/T(F)^{1}
which is injective with closed image and finite index.

(b)

If
F
is a local Archimedean field, then
X_{T}\approx\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
. If
F
is a local non-Archimedean field, then there is a finite-to-one map
X_{T}\to X_{S}\approx\widehat{T}_{\Gamma_{E/F}}
.

Proof.

We start by proving (a). The composition
S(F)\to T(F)\to T(F)/T(F)^{1}
descends to an injective homomorphism

\frac{S(F)}{S(F)\cap T(F)^{1}}\to\frac{T(F)}{T(F)^{1}}.

On the one hand, from the finite-index injection
X^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to X(S)
(see
[
14
, p. 6]
), we have
S(F)^{1}\subseteq S(F)\cap T(F)^{1}

H_{c}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
.

Proof.

We use Propositions
27
(c) and that
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}
is the inverse image of the open set
\{0\}\subset\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},X),
from Proposition
31
.
∎

Theorem 34
.

Suppose that
V_{F}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then
H_{c}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}\approx\Lie(\widehat{T}^{{\Gamma_{E/F}}})
. In particular,
H_{c}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
is an abelian Lie group.

Proof.

Consider the short exact sequence
0\to W^{1}_{E/F}\to W_{E/F}\to V_{F}\to 0
, where the last arrow is the absolute value map, as discussed in Section
4.1
. From the inflation-restriction sequence we obtain the exact sequence

0\to\underline{H}^{1}(V_{F},\widehat{T}^{W^{1}_{E/F}})\to\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})\to\underline{H}^{1}(W^{1}_{E/F},\widehat{T}),

in which all the arrows are continuous.
Since
V_{F}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
acts trivially on
\widehat{T}
, using Proposition
27
(c), we have
\mathbb{C}
-linear identifications

\underline{H}^{1}(V_{F},\widehat{T}^{W^{1}_{E/F}})\approx\Hom_{c}(V_{F},\widehat{T}^{{\Gamma_{E/F}}})\approx\Lie(\widehat{T}^{\Gamma_{E/F}}).

We now claim that
\underline{H}^{1}(W^{1}_{E/F},\widehat{T})
is discrete, as a topological space. Together with the continuity of
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})\to\underline{H}^{1}(W^{1}_{E/F},\widehat{T})
and Proposition
27
(c), the claim implies that we have a continuous bijective homomorphism

\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to H_{c}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}

between locally compact Hausdorff groups (Theorem
29
) with domain a finite-dimensional real vector space. Hence it is a homeomorphism, by the open mapping theorem for topological groups (see
[
9
, Theorem (5.29)]
).

As for the claim, since
W^{1}_{E/F}
is compact, it follows from Proposition
27
(g) that
\underline{H}^{p}(W^{1}_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))=0
for
p=1,2
. From the long exact sequence of
W^{1}_{E/F}
-cohomology applied to

0\to X\to\Lie(\widehat{T})\to\widehat{T}\to 0,

we thus obtain a continuous isomorphism of abelian groups

\underline{H}^{1}(W^{1}_{E/F},\widehat{T})\cong\underline{H}^{2}(W^{1}_{E/F},X).

But from Proposition
27
(f), it follows that
\underline{H}^{2}(W^{1}_{E/F},X)
is countable and discrete as a topological space and, hence, so is
\underline{H}^{1}(W^{1}_{E/F},\widehat{T})
, as claimed.

The last assertion follows from the above and Proposition
33
.
∎

We now turn our attention to the cases when
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{Z}
. In these cases, the Weil group
W_{E/F}
is not almost connected. We start by noting that the long exact sequence (
17
) yields the exact sequence

(18)

0\to A\to\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))\to\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}\to 0,

where
A\subseteq\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
is the image of the continuous homomorphism
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},X)\to\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))\approx\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
. By Proposition
31
we
conclude that
A
is countable, and since
A
is also the kernel of the continuous map

\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}

we also see that
A
is closed. A closed, countable subgroup of a finite-dimensional real or complex vector space is necessarily a discrete subgroup.
Our next tasks will be to endow the space
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}
with a canonical complex structure and to study the discrete group
A
.

Definition 35
.

For
F
local or global,
we endow the abelian Lie group
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
with a canonical structure of abelian complex Lie group via the sequence

(19)

0\to K\to\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))\to\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}\to 0,

where
K
is the kernel of the functorial homomorphism
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))\to\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
. We transport this complex structure to
H^{1}_{c}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
via the isomorphism of Proposition
27
(c).

To justify Definition
35
, first of all, note that
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
is indeed an abelian Lie group, from Theorem
34
and (
18
). Secondly, the middle term
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))
has a canonical structure of a complex vector space since
\Lie(\widehat{T})
is a complex vector space. Further, in the
\mathbb{R}
-case, the kernel
K=0
by Proposition
32
and Theorem
34
and the fact that any continuous homomorphism between real vector spaces is necessarily linear. In the
\mathbb{Z}
-case,
K=A
, the discrete space appearing in (
18
). Hence, (
19
) endows
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}
, and thus
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
, with a canonical complex Lie group structure.

.

34
is a
\mathbb{C}

Proof.

Recall that
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{R}
. We have the following commutative diagram

Proposition 37
.

If
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{Z}
, let
A\subset\textup{Lie}(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
be as in (
18
). Then,
we have a natural isomorphism
A\cong\Hom_{c}(\Hom_{\Gamma_{E/F}}(X,C_{E}),\mathbb{Z})=\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{Z})
and
A
is a lattice in the real form
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{*}=\mathbb{R}\otimes X^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
of
\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
.

Proof.

When we apply the functor
\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},-)
to the exponential sequence
0\to\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}\to 0

the exact sequence:

(21)

It was shown in
[

, Theorem 2.1]
(or see

[
12
, p. 245]
for the two special cases
D=\mathbb{C}
and

which are relevant here)
that for any divisible abelian topological group
D
with trivial
\Gamma_{E/F}
-action we have a natural isomorphism:

(22)

H^{1}_{c}({W_{E/F}},\Hom(\widehat{X},D))\xrightarrow{\cong}\Hom_{c}(\Hom_{\Gamma_{E/F}}(X,C_{E}),D).

Recall from (

) that

was defined as:

when we use the isomorphism

H^{1}_{c}({W_{E/F}},\Hom(\widehat{X},\mathbb{C}))=H^{1}_{c}({W_{E/F}},\textup{Lie}(\widehat{T}))\cong\textup{Lie}(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
of Proposition

to view

as a lattice
in the real form
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{*}
of
\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}
.
Combining this with (

) and (

)
proves the desired result.
∎

Corollary 38
.

Suppose that
V_{F}\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Then
H_{c}^{1}({W_{E/F}},\widehat{T})^{\circ}\approx H_{c}^{1}({W_{E/F}},\Lie(\widehat{T}))/A
is a complex algebraic torus, equipped with a canonical surjective homomorphism

H_{c}^{1}({W_{E/F}},\widehat{T})^{\circ}\to X_{T}

with finite kernel (which is an isomorphism in the local case).

Proof.

For both
F
local non-Archimedean and a global function field, we can characterize
X_{T}
via the exact sequence (see Proposition
20
and Corollary
21
)

(24)

0\to\widehat{L}_{T}\to\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to X_{T}\to 0,

with
L_{T}
the image of the respective
\log_{T}
maps (see (
10
) and the text above Corollary
21
), and
\widehat{L}_{T}
is the lattice
\Hom(L_{T},\mathbb{Z})
.
Now, when
F
is non-Archimedean local, we have
T_{C_{F}}=\Hom_{\Gamma_{E/F}}(X,C_{E})=T(F)
. From continuity, it follows that

\Hom_{c}(T(F),\mathbb{Z})=\Hom_{c}(T(F)/T(F)^{1},\mathbb{Z})
and hence
A\cong\widehat{L}_{T}
, using Proposition
37
. From
(
18
) and (
24
) we get
H_{c}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}\approx X_{T}
.

For
F
a function field, we have

T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F)\to T_{C_{F}}
, a closed inclusion of finite index (see
[
12
, p. 245]
) from which we obtain (by Proposition
37
) an inclusion

A\hookrightarrow\Hom_{c}(T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(F),\mathbb{Z})=\Hom(T(\mathbb{A}_{F})/T(\mathbb{A}_{F})^{1},\mathbb{Z})\cong\widehat{L}_{T}

with finite cokernel.
By (
24
) this implies the result.
∎

6.

In this section, we shall finish the proof of our main result, Theorem
2

For

local or global,
we have

\mathbb{C}

\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C})\approx\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}.

First, note that as

∎

Proposition 41
.

For
F
local or global, we have
\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},X)\approx\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*})
.

Proof.

It is known that
\underline{H}^{2}(W_{F},\widehat{T})=0
(see
[
21
, Theorem 2]
), where
W_{F}
is the absolute Weil group of
F
. From the extension
0\to W_{E}^{c}\to W_{F}\to W_{E/F}\to 0
, the inflation-restriction sequence implies that

\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E}^{c},\widehat{T})^{W_{E/F}}\to\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})\to\underline{H}^{2}(W_{F},\widehat{T})

is exact, and since
\underline{H}^{2}(W_{F},\widehat{T})=0
, the first map is surjective.
Recall that
W_{E}^{c}
is compact, and that
the action of
W_{E}
on
\widehat{T}
is trivial. In particular we have

\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E}^{c},\widehat{T})\approx\Hom_{c}(W_{E}^{c},\widehat{T})
. Taking the long exact sequence in Moore’s measurable cohomology theory for the compact group
W_{E}^{c}
associated to the short exact sequence (
16
), and using Proposition

27
(f) and (g), we obtain that
\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E}^{c},\widehat{T})\approx\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E}^{c},X)
is countable and discrete. It follows that
\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
is countable.
With this as input, consider the long exact sequence in Moore’s measurable cohomology for
W_{E/F}
for the short exact sequence (
16
).
We see that
\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))
, which is a priori a
real vector space, is squeezed between
\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},X)
(a countable discrete abelian group by Proposition
31
),
and
\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})
(a countable abelian group by the above). Hence,
\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))
is
0
.
From the isomorphism
\Lambda
, Proposition
32
and Proposition
22

it now follows that:

\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},X)\approx\frac{\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})}{\underline{H}^{1}(W_{E/F},\widehat{T})^{\circ}}\approx\frac{\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})}{\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\circ}}\approx\frac{\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}},\mathbb{C}^{*})}{\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}/T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*})},

\underline{H}^{2}(W_{E/F},X)\approx\Hom_{c}(T_{C_{F}}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{*}).

where
\omega\in W_{E/F}

\nu\mapsto\zeta_{\nu}

Proposition 45
.

The image of
\zeta
is inside
Z^{1}_{c}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))
and it induces a
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism

\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to H_{c}^{1}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T})).

Proof.

If
\nu\in\Lie(\widehat{T})^{{\Gamma_{E/F}}}
, then

\zeta_{\nu}(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})=\zeta_{\nu}(\omega_{1})+\zeta_{\nu}(\omega_{2})=\zeta_{\nu}(\omega_{1})+{}^{\overline{\omega_{1}}}\zeta_{\nu}(\omega_{2}),

so that
\zeta_{\nu}\in Z^{1}_{c}({W_{E/F}},\Lie(\widehat{T}))

Hence, it defines a homomorphism

[\zeta]:\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to H^{1}_{c}({W_{E/F}},\Lie(\widehat{T}))

\zeta_{\nu}(\omega)=\log_{q}(|\omega|)\nu={}^{\bar{\omega}}\mu-\mu

of the finite Galois group, we obtain

\nu=0
. By Proposition
32

[\zeta]:\Lie(\widehat{T})^{\Gamma_{E/F}}\to H^{1}_{c}(W_{E/F},\Lie(\widehat{T}))

\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism.
∎

Abstract
. Adversarial robustness of neural networks is an increasingly important area of research, combining studies on computer vision models, large language models (LLMs), and others. With the release of JPEG AI — the first standard for end-to-end neural image compression (NIC) methods — the question of evaluating NIC robustness has become critically significant. However, previous research has been limited to a narrow range of codecs and attacks. To address this, we present NIC-RobustBench , the first open-source framework to evaluate NIC robustness and adversarial defenses’ efficiency, in addition to comparing Rate-Distortion (RD) performance. The framework includes the largest number of codecs among all known NIC libraries and is easily scalable. The paper demonstrates a comprehensive overview of the NIC-RobustBench framework and employs it to analyze NIC robustness. Our code is available online at https://github.com/msu-video-group/NIC-RobustBench .

However, all known open-source libraries concentrate on evaluating the performance of NIC models and do not touch the issue of their robustness as listed in Table
1
. Our proposed software provides an opportunity for researchers to check NIC robustness, their performance, and adversarial defenses’ efficiency. The code is implemented in PyTorch, one of the most popular frameworks for NIC task, to ensure the compatibility with most released algorithms.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We present NIC-RobustBench, a novel Neural Image Compression library focused on evaluating NIC robustness with multiple adversarial attacks and defenses for image-compression task. It includes the largest number of NIC models among the open-source libraries known to the authors and allows to systematically evaluate the performance of NIC models in challenging adversarial scenarios.

•

We perform extensive experiments on 10 NIC models (including the most recent revisions of JPEG AI standard), analyzing 7 attacks targeting image quality or bitrate, and present valuable insights into their robustness.

•

We implement and evaluate 7 adversarial defenses against adversarial attacks on NICs.

Related work

2.1.
Neural image compression

Neural image compression has seen rapid progress in recent years. Ballé et al.
(
Ballé et al. 2016
)
introduced one of the first models for end-to-end compression with generalized divisive normalization and uniform scalar quantization. Agustsson et al.
(
Agustsson et al. 2017
)
proposed a soft-to-hard vector quantization method for compressive autoencoders. Ballé et al.
(
Ballé et al. 2018
)
further modeled image compression as a variational autoencoder problem, introducing hyperpriors to improve entropy modeling. Minnen et al.
(
Minnen et al. 2018
)
extended hierarchical Gaussian scale mixture models with Gaussian mixtures and autoregressive components. Mentzer et al.
(
Mentzer et al. 2020
)
utilized GANs to enhance perceptual quality in neural compression.

Cheng et al.
(
Cheng et al. 2020
)
developed an efficient entropy model based on discretized Gaussian mixtures and attention modules. Yang et al.
(
Yang et al. 2020
)
introduced rate and complexity control using slimmable modules. He et al.
(
He et al. 2022
)
proposed the ELIC model, which combines stacked residual blocks with a spatial-channel context entropy model. Zou et al.
(
Zou et al. 2022
)
enhanced compression with window-based attention, training both CNN and Transformer models. Liu et al.
(
Liu et al. 2023a
)
combined transformer-CNN mixture blocks with a Swin-Transformer attention module. Duan et al.
(
Duan et al. 2023
)
adopted a hierarchical VAE with a uniform posterior and a Gaussian-convolved uniform prior. Wang et al.
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
focused on real-time compression using residual and depth-wise convolution blocks, introducing mask decay and sparsity regularization for model distillation. Yang et al.
(
Yang and Mandt 2024
)
presented a lossy compression scheme employing contextual latent variables and a diffusion model for reconstruction.

2.2.
Adversarial robustness of NIC

Kang et al.
(
Liu et al. 2023b
)
proposed an attack to increase the size of NIC-compressed images using the I-FGSM
(
Kurakin et al. 2017
)
attack. Their study explored the attack’s effectiveness across different codec architectures, identifying architectural elements that improved model reliability, and proposed their factorized attention model as the most stable. Chen and Ma
(
Chen and Ma 2023
)
demonstrated the high vulnerability of NIC methods to adversarial attacks that reduce the quality of the decoded image. They adopted the I-FGSM and Carlini-Wagner
(
Carlini and Wagner 2017
)
attacks to increase the difference between the compressed images before and after the attack. They attacked images after compression and proposed a new Fast Threshold-constrained Distortion Attack (FTDA). They also proposed a
\Delta\text{PSNR}
method for assessing the success of an attack, which evaluates both its effectiveness and its visibility:

\Delta\text{PSNR}=\text{PSNR}(x,C(x))-\text{PSNR}(x^{\prime},C(x^{\prime})),

where
x
is the original image,
x^{\prime}
is the adversarial image,
C(\cdot)
- image after NIC.

3.1.
NIC models

Lossy image compression is based on a rate-distortion theory. The goal is to find a trade-off between the size of the compressed image representation and the decrease in the perceptual quality of a reconstructed image. This problem is formulated as follows:

(1)

\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{x}}[\lambda r(\hat{y})+d(x,\hat{x})],

where
r(\hat{y})
is a bit representation of a quantized image after arithmetic encoding, and
d(x,\hat{x})
— perceptual image-similarity metric.

Auto-encoder architecture is one of the possible solutions. For a given image
x\in X=\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}
of a distribution of natural images
p_{x}
, an encoder
E
transforms it to a latent representation
y=E(x)
of a distribution
p_{y}
. Then, the data is quantized
\hat{y}=Q(y)
, and a decoder
G
performs the reconstruction of the image
\hat{x}=G(\hat{y})
. We denote
C(x)
as a complete encoding-decoding process
C(\cdot)=G\circ Q\circ E:X\to X
.

3.2.
Attacks

The goal of an adversarial attack is to find a perturbation
\delta
which, added to the original image, makes the adversarial image
x^{\prime}=x+\delta
such that its decoded image
C(x^{\prime})
differs from the original image as much as possible. Adversarial attack
A:X\rightarrow X
is defined as follows:

(2)

\displaystyle A(x)=\underset{x^{\prime}:\rho(x^{\prime},x)\leq\varepsilon}{\arg\max}\>L(x,x^{\prime},C(x),C(x^{\prime})),

where
\rho(x^{\prime},x)=\|\delta\|
,
\varepsilon
imposes a constraint on the perturbation magnitude,
L:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is a corresponding optimization target. To achieve this goal, we consider 10 loss functions for all employed attacks. They reflect different approaches to measuring the distance between the original and adversarial images and their reconstructed versions. Additionally, we consider an alternative optimization goal. Instead of increasing the distance between the image targets, we reduced the compression ratio of the NIC measured in Bits Per Pixel (BPP):

(3)

\displaystyle A(x)=\underset{\delta:\|\delta\|\leq\varepsilon}{\arg\max}\>\text{BPP}(Q(E(x+\delta))).

3.3.
Adversarial defenses

Adversarial purification defense consists of two additional steps in the compression procedure: a preprocessing transformation
T:X\rightarrow X
before compression and a postprocessing transformation
T^{-1}:X\rightarrow X
afterward.
Mathematically, adversarial attack of the defended NIC can be expressed as:

(4)

\displaystyle A(x)=\underset{x^{\prime}:\rho(x^{\prime},x)\leq\varepsilon}{\arg\max}\>L(x,x^{\prime},g(x),g(x^{\prime})),

where
x\in X
is the original image,
x^{\prime}\in X
is the adversarial image,
g(\cdot)=(T^{-1}\circ C\circ T)(\cdot)
is the adversarially defended NIC,
T(\cdot):X\to X
is adversarial defense,
T^{-1}(\cdot):X\to X
is the inverse of that defense. The equation means adding preprocessing step
T
before feeding the image to NIC and postprocessing step
T^{-1}
to reverse the preprocessing step. This equation can be transformed into an adversarial attack on undefended NIC when
T(X)=X
.

The framework consists of reversible defenses, like Geometric self-ensemble
(
Chen and Ma 2023
)
. DiffPure
(
Nie et al. 2022
)
method is used only in preprocessing step and has identical postprocessing one.

4.
Evaluation metrics

We employ five different full-reference image quality metrics to numerically assess the effects of adversarial attacks on images before and after the reconstruction: PSNR, MSE, SSIM, MS-SSIM
(
Wang et al. 2003
)
and VMAF
(
Li et al. 2018
)
. PSNR, MSE, and MS-SSIM are traditional image-similarity measures. MS-SSIM
(
Wang et al. 2003
)
provides a scale-independent quality estimate, and VMAF implements a learning-based approach that aligns well with human perception
(
Antsiferova et al. 2022
)
.

Following the methodology of
(
Chen and Ma 2023
)
, we measure
\Delta_{score}
— the difference in reconstruction quality between the clear image and corresponding adversarial example:

(5)

\Delta_{score}=FR(x,x^{\prime})-FR(C(x),C(x^{\prime})),

where
x
is a source image,
x^{\prime}
is a corresponding adversarial example,
FR(x,y)
is one of the aforementioned IQA models, and
C(x)
is an evaluated NIC (entire encoding-decoding procedure). This score captures the drop in the fidelity of reconstructed images caused by the attack. Higher values of
\Delta_{score}
indicate lower robustness of the NIC. Additionally, we employ Rate-Distortion curves with FR-IQA metric values and Bits Per Pixel ratio to visualize compression efficiency.

Applications

(a) NIC Rate-Distortion curves for clear (dashed lines) and attacked content (solid). Attacks target image quality on the left subfigure, and bitrate on the right. (b) Robustness evaluation of different NIC models (left) and adversarial defenses (right).

NIC robustness evaluation
. The primary application of our framework is to systematically evaluate the resilience of learning-based codecs to different adversarial attacks. NIC-RobustBench implements the entire evaluation pipeline, from the generation of adversarial examples and evaluation of NIC model on clear and malicious data to the processing of the results. Figure
2
(a) presents the Rate-Distortion curves (SSIM/BPP) obtained with our framework for both source (dashed lines) and attacked (solid lines) images. Subfigures demonstrate two optimization targets for the attacks, with left targeting the compression quality, and bitrate on the right. One can immediately notice the significant shift in RD performance in the adversarial scenario for all tested NIC models, emphasizing their vulnerability. Figure
2
(b, left) numerically evaluates this gap in compression quality with
\Delta_{score}
based on VMAF metric. MBT-2018, Balle et al. and Cheng et al. (only the attention-based variation) models demonstrate the best robustness in this test. Interestingly, generative-compression models (diffusion-based CDC, GAN-based HiFiC, QRes-VAE) are among the least stable.

NIC comparison
. Alongside NIC robustness comparison, our framework can also be used to directly compare NIC models on non-attacked images, like other NIC libraries. Dashed RD-curves in Figure
2
(a) represent the performance of different NICs on source images. In our evaluations, JPEG-AI v5.1 and v.4.1 demonstrate the best quality-bitrate ratio at higher bitrates, and LIC-TCM and Cheng et al. models are the top performers at lower bitrates.

Defense evaluation
. To comprehensively cover NIC robustness field, our library also implements adversarial defenses to counter the attacks. We evaluate them under different adversarial attacks using JPEG AI models as targets and show
\Delta\text{SSIM}
as an example in Figure
2
(b, right). The results indicate that DiffPure, Geometric Self-Ensemble and Flip defensive techniques are somewhat effective in reducing their effect, while others mostly have negative effect on compression quality.

6.
Conclusion

This paper proposes NIC-RobustBench, a novel open-source library that can be used by researchers for evaluating NIC robustness. To our knowledge, NIC-RobustBench is the first framework that implements a comprehensive collection of adversarial attacks and defenses for a learned image compression task. It includes the largest number of NIC models among the open-source libraries known to the authors. NIC-RobustBench provides an efficient, simple and scalable framework to evaluate the performance, robustness and compression quality of NIC models. Utilizing NIC-RobustBench, we reveal systematic degradation of compression quality and efficiency in most modern learning-based codecs under adversarial attacks, emphasizing the importance of robustness validation in image-compression task.

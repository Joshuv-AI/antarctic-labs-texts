Two-Stage Asymmetric Tullock Contests with Cost Shifters and Endogenous Continuation Decision
Felix Reichel

Abstract
This paper introduces a contest-theoretic simplified model of triathlon as a sequential two-stage game. In Stage 1 (post-swim), participants decide whether to continue or withdraw from the contest, thereby generating an endogenous participation decision. In Stage 2 (bike–run), competition is represented as a Tullock contest in which swim drafting acts as a multiplicative shifter of quadratic effort costs. Closed-form equilibrium strategies are derived in the two-player case, and existence, uniqueness, and comparative statics are shown in the asymmetric n n -player case. The continuation decision yields athlete-specific cutoff rules in swim drafting intensity and induces subgame-perfect equilibria (SPEs) with endogenous participation sets. The analysis relates swim drafting benefits, exposure, and group size to heterogeneous effective cost parameters and equilibrium efforts.

1
Contest Setup: Players, Timing, and Objects

There are
n\geq 2
athletes indexed by
i\in N=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
The contest is simplified to two stages.

Stage 1 (Post–Swim).

For each athlete
i
we observe some state vector

s_{i}=(t_{i}^{S},r_{i}^{S},D_{i},G),

where
t_{i}^{S}
is swim time,
r_{i}^{S}
is swim rank,
D_{i}\in[0,1]
is the share of swim drafting,
and
G
is the full directed graph of stored swim drafting relations.
The Stage 1 action set is
a_{i}\in\{C,T\}
:
Continue Race (C)
or
Withdraw from Race (T)
.

Stage 2 (Bike–Run Contest).

Let
S\subseteq N
be the subset of athletes who continue, with group size
m\leq|S|\geq 1
.
Each
i\in S
chooses a nonnegative effort
e_{i}
.
The probability that
i
wins is given by

p_{i}(e)=\frac{w_{i}e_{i}}{\sum_{j\in S}w_{j}e_{j}},

(1)

where
w_{i}>0
is a contest weight.
Effort cost is quadratic with scaling parameter
k_{i}
, defined by

C_{i}(e_{i})=\tfrac{1}{2}k_{i}e_{i}^{2},\qquad k_{i}:=\frac{c_{i}}{\psi_{i}},

(2)

where
c_{i}>0
is a baseline cost parameter and
\psi_{i}
is a swim drafting multiplier.
The multiplier depends on the observed state,

\psi_{i}=\Psi(D_{i},m,r_{i}^{S},G)\in[\underline{\psi},\overline{\psi}]\subset(0,\infty),

(3)

with
\Psi
increasing in swim drafting share
D_{i}
and Lipschitz in
(D_{i},m)
where the reduced–drag model is
\psi_{i}=1/(1-\eta D_{i})
with
\eta\in(0,1)
.
Furthermore let
\Delta_{i}>0
denote the winner–loser prize differential relevant for
i
.

Payoffs.

If athlete
i
terminates in Stage 1, the outside payoff is

U_{i}^{T}=\underbrace{-\alpha\,t_{i}^{S}}_{\text{time penalty}}\;+\;\underbrace{-\beta\,r_{i}^{S}}_{\text{rank penalty}}\;+\;\underbrace{\vartheta_{i}}_{\text{additional training unit}},

(4)

where
\alpha,\beta>0
are penalty weights and
\vartheta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is an idiosyncratic term.

If athlete
i
continues to Stage 2, the expected utility is

U_{i}(e)=\underbrace{p_{i}(e)\,\Delta_{i}}_{\text{expected prize}}\;+\;\underbrace{\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}k_{i}e_{i}^{2}\big)}_{\text{effort cost}}\;+\;\underbrace{\text{(Terms independent of $e$)}}_{\text{irrelevant for effort choice}}.

(5)

Definition 1

(Strategy and Equilibrium)
.

A (pure) strategy for athlete
i
is
(a_{i},\ e_{i}(\cdot))
, where
a_{i}\in\{C,T\}
is the Stage 1 action and
e_{i}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the Stage 2 effort choice.
A
subgame perfect equilibrium
(SPE) is a profile
(a_{i}^{\ast},e_{i}^{\ast})_{i\in N}
such that:
(i) given
S=\{i:a_{i}^{\ast}=C\}
, the effort profile
e^{\ast}
is a Nash equilibrium (NE) of the Stage 2 contest;
(ii) each
a_{i}^{\ast}
maximizes total payoff given continuation values from (i).

2
Stage 2 Analysis: Existence, Uniqueness, and Closed Forms

Stage 2 consists of the post–swim contest among the set of continuers
S
.

2.1
Best responses

Recall that athlete
i
’s probability of victory is

p_{i}(e)=\frac{w_{i}e_{i}}{\sum_{j\in S}w_{j}e_{j}},

where
e_{i}\geq 0
is
i
’s bike–run effort and
w_{i}>0
is a contest weight.
The derivative
w.r.t.
own effort is

\frac{\partial p_{i}}{\partial e_{i}}=\frac{p_{i}(1-p_{i})}{e_{i}},\qquad\frac{\partial p_{i}}{\partial e_{j}}=-\frac{p_{i}p_{j}}{e_{j}},\ j\neq i.

(6)

Dropping constants independent of
e_{i}
, athlete
i
optimizes

\max_{e_{i}\geq 0}\;p_{i}(e)\,\Delta_{i}-\tfrac{1}{2}k_{i}e_{i}^{2},

where
\Delta_{i}>0
is
i
’s prize differential and
k_{i}=c_{i}/\psi_{i}
is the effective cost parameter
(baseline cost
c_{i}>0
, swim drafting multiplier
\psi_{i}>0
).
The FOC for any interior optimum
e_{i}>0
is

0=\frac{\partial U_{i}}{\partial e_{i}}=\Delta_{i}\frac{p_{i}(1-p_{i})}{e_{i}}-k_{i}e_{i}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e_{i}^{2}=\frac{\Delta_{i}}{k_{i}}\,p_{i}(1-p_{i}).

(7)

Thus best responses map contest probabilities
p_{i}
into equilibrium efforts via (
7
).

2.2
Two–player closed form (
m=2
)

Suppose
S=\{i,j\}
.
Define the
effective advantage ratio

R:=\frac{\Delta_{i}\psi_{i}/c_{i}}{\Delta_{j}\psi_{j}/c_{j}}=\frac{\Delta_{i}/k_{i}}{\Delta_{j}/k_{j}}.

(8)

Combining (
7
) with
p_{i}/(1-p_{i})=(w_{i}e_{i})/(w_{j}e_{j})
yields

\frac{p_{i}}{1-p_{i}}=R^{1/2}\,\frac{w_{i}}{w_{j}}.

(9)

Hence
p_{i}^{\ast}=\rho/(1+\rho)
with
\rho=R^{1/2}\,w_{i}/w_{j}
, and from (
7
)

\displaystyle e_{i}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tfrac{\Delta_{i}\psi_{i}}{c_{i}}}\;\frac{\rho^{1/2}}{1+\rho},

(10)

\displaystyle e_{j}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\tfrac{\Delta_{j}\psi_{j}}{c_{j}}}\;\frac{\rho^{1/2}}{1+\rho}.

These closed forms show that equilibrium effort rises in
\Delta_{i}
(prize) and in
\psi_{i}
(swim drafting multiplier), and falls in
c_{i}
(cost).

2.3
Symmetric
m
–player benchmark

If
w_{i}\equiv 1
,
c_{i}\equiv c
,
\psi_{i}\equiv\psi
, and
\Delta_{i}\equiv\Delta
for all
i\in S
, then
p_{i}^{\ast}=1/m
.
From (
7
),

(e^{\ast})^{2}=\frac{\Delta\psi}{c}\,\frac{m-1}{m^{2}},\qquad e^{\ast}=\sqrt{\tfrac{\Delta\psi}{c}}\;\sqrt{\frac{m-1}{m^{2}}}.

(11)

Effort decreases with group size
m
, increases with prize
\Delta
and swim drafting multiplier
\psi
, and decreases with cost parameter
c
.

2.4
Asymmetric
m
–player case

For general heterogeneity, let
E=\sum_{j\in S}e_{j}
denote total effort.
Since
p_{i}=e_{i}/E
, condition (
7
) implies

e_{i}=\frac{\Delta_{i}\,E}{c_{i}E^{2}/\psi_{i}+\Delta_{i}},\qquad i\in S.

(12)

Summing (
12
) over all
i
gives the scalar equation

1=\sum_{i\in S}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{c_{i}E^{2}/\psi_{i}+\Delta_{i}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g(E):=\sum_{i\in S}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{c_{i}E^{2}/\psi_{i}+\Delta_{i}}-1=0.

(13)

Proposition 1

(Existence and uniqueness)
.

Assume
c_{i}>0
,
\psi_{i}\in[\underline{\psi},\overline{\psi}]
, and
\Delta_{i}>0
.
Then
g(\cdot)
is continuous and strictly decreasing on
E\geq 0
, with
g(0)=m-1>0
and
\lim_{E\to\infty}g(E)=-1
.
Hence there exists a unique
E^{\ast}>0
solving
(
13
)
.
The implied profile
(
12
)
is the unique interior NE of Stage 2.

Proof.

Each term
h_{i}(E)=\Delta_{i}/(c_{i}E^{2}/\psi_{i}+\Delta_{i})
is strictly decreasing in
E\geq 0
, with
h_{i}(0)=1
and
\lim_{E\to\infty}h_{i}(E)=0
.
Thus
g(E)=\sum_{i}h_{i}(E)-1
is strictly decreasing, crossing zero exactly once.
Strict concavity of individual payoffs in
e_{i}
(second derivative
-2\Delta_{i}/E^{2}-k_{i}<0
) guarantees a unique interior NE.
∎

Corollary 1

(Probabilities and efforts)
.

At the unique equilibrium,

p_{i}^{\ast}=\frac{\Delta_{i}}{c_{i}(E^{\ast})^{2}/\psi_{i}+\Delta_{i}},\qquad e_{i}^{\ast}=p_{i}^{\ast}E^{\ast}.

Proposition 2

(Comparative statics)
.

Let
E^{\ast}(\bm{c},\bm{\psi},\bm{\Delta})
solve
(
13
)
.
Then:

1.

E^{\ast}
increases in any
\psi_{i}
or
\Delta_{i}
, and decreases in any
c_{i}
.

2.

p_{i}^{\ast}
and
e_{i}^{\ast}
increase in
\psi_{i}
and
\Delta_{i}
, and decrease in
c_{i}
.
For
j\neq i
,
p_{j}^{\ast}
and
e_{j}^{\ast}
weakly decrease when
\psi_{i}
or
\Delta_{i}
increases, since
\sum_{j}p_{j}^{\ast}=1
.

3
Stage 1 Analysis: Continuation Cutoffs and SPE

Fix the post–swim state vector
(t_{i}^{S},r_{i}^{S},D_{i},G)
for each athlete
i
, together with opponents’ states.
If a set
S
of continuers proceeds to Stage 2, let
W_{i}(S)
denote athlete
i
’s
continuation value
, i.e. his/her expected payoff from the effort stage.
Recall that
\Delta_{i}>0
is
i
’s prize differential,
c_{i}>0
his/her baseline cost parameter,
\psi_{i}>0
his/her swim drafting multiplier,
k_{i}=c_{i}/\psi_{i}
his/her effective cost,
E^{\ast}(S)
the unique total effort from Proposition
1
, and
p_{i}^{\ast}(S)
the equilibrium win probability.
Then

W_{i}(S)=p_{i}^{\ast}(S)\,\Delta_{i}-\tfrac{1}{2}k_{i}\big(e_{i}^{\ast}(S)\big)^{2}=\frac{\Delta_{i}^{2}}{c_{i}(E^{\ast}(S))^{2}/\psi_{i}+\Delta_{i}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{c_{i}}{\psi_{i}}\,\big(p_{i}^{\ast}(S)E^{\ast}(S)\big)^{2}.

(14)

Define the net benefit of continuing relative to the outside option
U_{i}^{T}
(see (
4
)) as

V_{i}(S):=W_{i}(S)-U_{i}^{T}.

(15)

Proposition 3

(Cutoff in the swim drafting multiplier)
.

Fix
(t_{i}^{S},r_{i}^{S})
,
(c_{i},\Delta_{i})
, and a continuation set
S
with
i\in S
.
Then the function
\psi_{i}\mapsto V_{i}(S)
is continuous and strictly increasing. Therefore a unique cutoff
\psi_{i}^{\ast}(S)
exists such that
V_{i}(S)\geq 0
iff
\psi_{i}\geq\psi_{i}^{\ast}(S)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2
, both
E^{\ast}(S)
and
p_{i}^{\ast}(S)
increase in
\psi_{i}
, while the cost parameter
k_{i}=c_{i}/\psi_{i}
decreases.
Thus the continuation value
W_{i}(S)
in (
14
) is strictly increasing in
\psi_{i}
, while
U_{i}^{T}
does not depend on
\psi_{i}
.
Continuity yields the unique cutoff under the intermediate value theorem.
∎

At Stage 1, all athletes choose
a_{i}\in\{C,T\}
simultaneously.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be the family of nonempty subsets of
N
.
A set
S^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}
is a
Stage 1 equilibrium continuation set
if for every
i
,

i\in S^{\ast}\ \Rightarrow\ V_{i}(S^{\ast})\geq 0,\qquad i\notin S^{\ast}\ \Rightarrow\ V_{i}(S^{\ast}\cup\{i\})\leq 0.

(16)

The pair
\big(S^{\ast},\ e^{\ast}(S^{\ast})\big)
is then a subgame perfect equilibrium (SPE).

Theorem 1

(Existence of an SPE)
.

Under the assumptions of Section
1
, there exists a (possibly nonunique) SPE.
Moreover, any
\psi
–monotone selection rule that iteratively admits athletes with
V_{i}(S)\geq 0
and removes those with
V_{i}(S)<0
converges to an equilibrium continuation set
S^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Define
\Phi(S)=\{\,i\in N:\ V_{i}(S)\geq 0\,\}
.
By Proposition
3
,
V_{i}(\cdot)
is well–defined whenever
i\in S
.
Consider the operator
T(S)=\Phi(S)
restricted to nonempty sets (if
T(S)=\varnothing
, select any singleton
\{i\}
with maximal
V_{i}(\{i\})
).
Since
N
is finite, the sequence
S^{(k+1)}=T(S^{(k)})
stabilizes in finite steps at some
S^{\ast}
with
T(S^{\ast})=S^{\ast}
, which satisfies (
16
).
Backward induction then completes the SPE construction.
∎

Remark 1

(Comparative statics of the cutoff)
.

From Proposition
2
and (
14
), the cutoff
\psi_{i}^{\ast}(S)
decreases with a higher prize differential
\Delta_{i}
and increases with a higher baseline cost
c_{i}
.
In symmetric environments,
\psi_{i}^{\ast}(S)
(weakly) increases with the group size
m<|S|>1
, since larger contests reduce a athlete’s winning odds and continuation becomes less attractive.

4
From Swim Position to swim drafting Cost Shifter

We now formalize the cost–shifting map (
3
).
Let
D_{i}\in[0,1]
denote the share that athlete
i
utilized a swim drafting benefit.
We use the relation

\psi_{i}=\frac{1}{1-\eta D_{i}},\qquad\eta\in(0,1),

(17)

where
\eta
captures the sensitivity of drag reduction to swim drafting exposure.
The effective cost slope is

k_{i}=\frac{c_{i}}{\psi_{i}}=c_{i}(1-\eta D_{i}),

with
c_{i}>0
the baseline cost.
Thus swim drafting linearly lowers effort costs:
\partial k_{i}/\partial D_{i}=-\eta c_{i}<0
and
\partial\psi_{i}/\partial D_{i}=\eta/(1-\eta D_{i})^{2}>0
.
More general specifications
\Psi(D_{i},m,r_{i}^{S},G)
can incorporate nonlinear decay with swim position depth
r_{i}^{S}
and congestion effects depending on group size
m
.

5
Welfare and Rents

Expected net welfare in Stage 2 is defined as the sum of athletes’ equilibrium payoffs,

\sum_{i\in S}U_{i}(e^{\ast}),

where
U_{i}
is given by (
5
) and
e^{\ast}
is the unique equilibrium profile.
Using the effort characterization in (
12
), this sum becomes

\sum_{i\in S}U_{i}(e^{\ast})=\sum_{i\in S}\left[\frac{\Delta_{i}^{2}}{c_{i}(E^{\ast})^{2}/\psi_{i}+\Delta_{i}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\frac{c_{i}}{\psi_{i}}\,\big(p_{i}^{\ast}E^{\ast}\big)^{2}\right],

(18)

where
\Delta_{i}
is athlete
i
’s prize differential,
c_{i}
his/her cost parameter,
\psi_{i}
his/her swim drafting multiplier, and
(p_{i}^{\ast},e_{i}^{\ast})
his/her equilibrium winning probability and effort.

In the symmetric benchmark of (
11
), where all athletes share the same parameters
(\Delta,c,\psi)
, each wins with probability
1/m
in a group of size
m
.
The aggregate rent ratio—defined as the share of total expected prize intake that is offset by aggregate effort costs—simplifies to

\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\tfrac{1}{2}k(e^{\ast})^{2}}{\sum_{i\in S}p_{i}^{\ast}\Delta}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{m-1}{m},

with
k=c/\psi
.
This ratio is increasing in group size
m
, approaching
1/2
as
m\to\infty
.

6
Conclusion and Testable Predictions

Deeper swim drafting exposure in the swim (higher
D_{i}
) increases the swim drafting multiplier
\psi_{i}
via (
17
). Through the equilibrium mapping in (
12
), a higher
\psi_{i}
raises both the winning probability
p_{i}^{\ast}
and the equilibrium effort
e_{i}^{\ast}
.

In symmetric groups, equilibrium effort per athlete decreases with group size
m
as characterized in (
11
). When increased group size also relaxes effective costs by raising access to swim drafting (so that
\psi
rises with
m
through
\Psi
in (
3
)), these opposing forces render the net effect on effort ambiguous and can generate interior optima in
m
.

The continuation decision after the swim inherits these forces: since
\psi_{i}
enters continuation values
W_{i}(S)
in (
14
) and contest odds mechanically fall with
m
through (
1
), the likelihood of continuing is increasing in swim drafting exposure
D_{i}
and decreasing in group size
m
(holding other objects fixed).

Appendix B
Concavity and Best Responses (Stage 2)

Let

denote total effort. With
p_{i}=e_{i}/E
and
k_{i}=c_{i}/\psi_{i}
, athlete

The second derivative is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}U_{i}}{\partial e_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta_{i}}{E^{2}}-k_{i}\;<\;0,

so

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}U_{i}}{\partial e_{i}\partial e_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{i}}{E^{2}}\;>\;0,\quad\text{(efforts are strategic complements)}

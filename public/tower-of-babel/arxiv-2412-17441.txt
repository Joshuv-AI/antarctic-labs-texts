Traveling waves in Bistable Reaction-Diffusion Cellular Automata
Daniel Špale, Petr Stehlík

Abstract
. We describe various types of traveling fronts of bistable reaction-diffusion cellular automata. These dynamical systems with discrete time, space, and state spaces can be seen as fully discrete versions of widely studied bistable reaction-diffusion equations. We show that moving traveling waves for high diffusion parameters are restricted to slow speeds and their profiles are interestingly not unique. Pinned waves always exist for weak diffusion as in the case of lattice equations but do not complement parametric region of moving traveling waves. The remaining parameter domain is dominated by waves which are unique to cellular automaton settings. These higher-order traveling waves move and periodically change profile at the same time.

1.
Introduction

Waves in lattice reaction-diffusion equations provide one of the most fascinating distinctive behavior between the dynamics of spatiotemporal dynamical systems which differ only in underlying discrete and continuous structures. As the most typical example, the Nagumo partial differential equation (i.e., continuous space) serves as a textbook example for the phase plane analysis which provides existence of traveling waves
[
15
]
. In contrast, the Nagumo lattice differential equation (discrete space) is far more complicated to study since it yields several new dynamical phenomena, e.g., pinning of waves
[
14
]
, spatial topological chaos
[
3
]
, and existence of nonmonotone waves
[
13
]
.

In this paper, we study traveling fronts of a class of bistable reaction-diffusion cellular automata, which correspond to Nagumo partial and lattice differential equations but are fully discrete, i.e., temporal, spatial, and state sets are all discrete. In Section
2
, we introduce a bistable discrete dynamical system

(1.1)

u(t+1)=F(u(t)),\quad t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\ u(t)\in S^{\mathbb{Z}},

with the finite state space
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and the mapping
F:S^{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow S^{\mathbb{Z}}
which involves both diffusion and reaction processes. We then compare its behavior to the widely studied corresponding partial and lattice differential equations and study three types of fronts - moving, pinned, and periodically changing higher-order traveling waves.

Beyond the natural interest in distinct mathematical properties, our motivation is driven by several applications where discrete time, space, and states are common, e.g., seasonal populations (discrete time,
[
2
]
), island or pond populations (discrete space,
[
12
]
), or very small populations (discrete states,
[
8
]
).

(Nonspatial) bistable dynamics.

Various biological, chemical and mechanical systems possess two stable equilibria. The simplest models are connected to ordinary differential models with cubic nonlinearities, e.g.,

u^{\prime}(t)=u(t)\left(1-u^{2}(t)\right),\quad t\geq 0,

with stable equilibria
u^{*}_{1,2}=\pm 1
and the unstable equilibrium
u_{3}^{*}=0
. In mathematical biology, it is more natural to generalize the standard logistic growth with a capacity
K>0
into the bistable models with the so called Allee effect. Roughly speaking, the (demographic) Allee effect captures phenomena in which populations smaller than a viability threshold
a
with
0<a<K
go extinct. Mathematically, this leads to the bistable model

(1.2)

u^{\prime}(t)=\lambda u(t)\left(\frac{u(t)}{A}-1\right)\left(1-\frac{u(t)}{K}\right),\quad t\geq 0,

with the stable equilibria
u^{*}_{1}=0
(extinction),
u^{*}_{2}=K
(carrying capacity) and unstable equilibrium
u_{3}^{*}=A
(viability threshold). Numerous examples from applications, list of alternative mathematical approaches, and deep discussions about the Allee effect and nonspatial bistable dynamics can be found in
[
7
]
.

Waves in bistable partial differential equations.

Nondimensionalisation of (
1.2
) and the use of the standard continuous spatial diffusion leads to the widely-studied Nagumo bistable partial differential equation (PDE)

(1.3)

u_{t}(x,t)=du_{xx}(x,t)+u(x,t)\left(u(x,t)-a\right)\left(1-u(x,t)\right),\quad t\geq 0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where
d>0
denotes the diffusion parameter. Initially, this equation has been considered as a simplified model for the pulse propagation in nerve axons
[
19
]
. The PDE has a traveling wave solution which connects the two stable equilibria
u^{*}_{1}=0
and
u^{*}_{2}=1

[
15
]

(1.4)

u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{x-ct}{2\sqrt{2d}}\right),

where
c
denotes the speed of propagation and satisfies

c=\sqrt{2d}\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right),

i.e., the wave profile (
1.4
) propagates left whenever
a<1/2
, right for
a>1/2
, and is pinned when
a=1/2
, see the left panel of Fig.
1
.

(a,d)
-plane for bistable partial differential equation (left panel), lattice differential equation (central panel) and cellular automaton (right panel). Waves in bistable lattice differential equations.

In many applications (sequence of cells, neurons, spatial habitats) the continuous space
X=\mathbb{R}
in (
1.3
) is naturally replaced by an integer lattice
X=\mathbb{Z}
and we obtain the Nagumo lattice equation

(1.5)

u^{\prime}_{i}(t)=d\left(u_{i-1}(t)-2u_{i}(t)+u_{i+1}(t)\right)+u_{i}(t)\left(u_{i}(t)-a\right)\left(1-u_{i}(t)\right),\quad t\geq 0,\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}.

The same equation can be obtained from the PDE (
1.3
) by applying standard central difference scheme to the second spatial derivative.

The dynamical properties of the LDE (
1.5
) differ significantly from those of the PDE (
1.3
). There is no explicit solution like (
1.4
). For any
a\in(0,1)
and sufficiently small diffusion
d
the waves are pinned with
c=0
and there exists large number of stationary solutions
[
14
]
. For any
d>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
c=0
whenever
\left\lvert a-1/2\right\rvert<\varepsilon
, see
[
3
]
and the central panel in Fig.
1
. The exact boundaries between
c=0
and
c\neq 0
remain unknown and the details of the pinning procedures have been intensively studied in simplified bistable caricatures
[
10
]
.

Alternative propagation schemes have been observed in the pinning regions, e.g., nonmonotone multichromatic waves
[
13
]
. Similar pinning behavior has been studied as well in the models when also time is considered to be discrete (or discretized by the forward Euler discretization scheme), e.g.,
[
5
]
,

(1.6)

u_{i}(t+1)-u_{i}(t)=d\left(u_{i-1}(t)-2u_{i}(t)+u_{i+1}(t)\right)+u_{i}(t)\left(u_{i}(t)-a\right)\left(1-u_{i}(t)\right),\quad t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},i\in\mathbb{Z}.

Patterns and waves in bistable cellular automata.

There have been numerous attempts to study patterns and waves in bistable cellular automata, i.e., models in which not only time and space are discrete but also the values of
u
are reduced to a finite number of states. In many cases, the focus has been on the discretization techniques, standard finite difference schemes
[
24
]
, the tropical discretization
[
18
]
, or the fine correspondence of three-letter state set model with the behavior of lattice equations (
1.5
)–(
1.6
),
[
4
]
. The natural presence of propagation phenomena has been identified in reaction-diffusion cellular automata by techniques ranging from simulations
[

]
to a systematic combinatorial analysis
[
16
]
. Numerous wave types include periodic waves
[
1
,
6
,
21
]
and spiral waves
[
9
]
based on the famous excitable cellular automata by Greenberg and Hastings
[
11
]
. Large number
[
23
]
and numerous types
[
4
]
of stationary patterns of reaction-diffusion automata have also been observed in reaction-diffusion automata.

Higher-order waves.

In this paper we focus on a class of bistable cellular automata and compare their propagation properties to those of the PDE (
1.3
) and the LDE (
1.5
). We show that beyond monotonic moving traveling waves and pinned waves there exists an intermediate region in which there are no moving or pinned traveling waves. The dynamics in this region are driven by a new type of waves which are unique to cellular automata, the
higher order traveling waves
. These are the systems of periodically changing and yet moving profiles. More precisely, a higher-order
(c,m)
-traveling wave is a set of
m
profiles which periodically repeat themselves and each periodic occurrence is coupled with a shift/speed
c
, see the right panel of Fig.
8
for illustration.

As far as we know, these higher-order waves connecting stationary solutions have not been described so far. However, periodically repeating and moving patterns (not waves though) are known to exist for cellular automata
[
25
]
. For example, there are elementary cellular automata leading to moving patterns, e.g., the rule 2, see Fig.
2
and there are also numerous which lead to the periodically repeating and moving patterns, e.g., the rule 3, see Fig.
3
.

Paper Structure.

In Section
2
we properly define the details of the model of the bistable cellular automaton (
1.1
). In Section

. In Section

2.
Reaction-diffusion cellular automata

In this section we briefly introduce basic concepts of bistable reaction-diffusion cellular automata of type (
1.1
). For more details and discussion on alternative approaches, see our companion paper
[
23
]
which deals with stationary patterns.

Discrete-state diffusion.

We define the symmetric discrete-state diffusion
d_{\delta}:({\mathbb{N}}_{0})^{3}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with a diffusion parameter
\delta\in\mathbb{N}
by

(2.1)

d_{\delta}(m,n,p)=n+\underbrace{h_{\delta}(m,n)}_{\text{left-side dynamics}}+\underbrace{h_{\delta}(p,n)}_{\text{right-side dynamics}},

where the left- and right-side dynamics are determined by an auxiliary function
h_{\delta}:({\mathbb{N}}_{0})^{2}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}_{0}

(2.2)

h_{\delta}(m,n):=\begin{cases}\delta,&m-n\geq 2\delta,\\
-\delta,&m-n\leq-2\delta,\\
\delta-1,&m-n\in\{2(\delta-1),2\delta-1\},\\
-(\delta-1),&n-m\in\{2(\delta-1),2\delta-1\},\\
\vdots\\
1,&m-n\in\{2,3\},\\
-1,&n-m\in\{2,3\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

We then introduce a diffusion automaton
D:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}
via

(2.3)

D(u)=\begin{pmatrix}\vdots\\
d_{\delta}(u_{x-2},u_{x-1},u_{x})\\
d_{\delta}(u_{x-1},u_{x},u_{x+1})\\
d_{\delta}(u_{x},u_{x+1},u_{x+2})\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix},\quad u\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}}.

Discrete-state bistable reaction function.

Focusing on a bistable, discrete-state and local (i.e., nonspatial) dynamics
v(t+1)=f(v(t))
,
v(t)\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we introduce bistable maps
f
with the help of a capacity
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a viability constant
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
0<a<K
, see also Fig.
4
and cf. (
1.2
). A function
f:{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
is said to be a (discrete) bistable reaction function if it is nondecreasing and there exist
a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
2\leq a\leq K-2
such that
f(0)=0
,
f(a)=a
,
f(K)=K
, and

(2.4)

f(u)\in\begin{cases}[0,u)_{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if }u\in(0,a)_{\mathbb{Z}},\\
(u,K]_{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if }u\in(a,K)_{\mathbb{Z}},\\
[K,u)_{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if }u\in(K,\infty)_{\mathbb{Z}},\\
\end{cases}

where the subscript
\mathbb{Z}
denotes discrete intervals, e.g.,
(0,a)_{\mathbb{Z}}=(0,a)\cap\mathbb{Z}
. See the left panel of Figure
4
.

Reaction-diffusion cellular automata.

Combining the spatial and local dynamics (i.e., diffusion (
2.3
) and reaction (
2.4
)), we construct bistable reaction-diffusion automata (
1.1
). Consider a time scale
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, one-dimensional discrete lattice
X=\mathbb{Z}
, a state set
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and an update rule
F:S^{X}\rightarrow S^{X}
given by

(2.5)

F(u)=\begin{pmatrix}\vdots\\
d_{\delta}(f(u_{x-2}),f(u_{x-1}),f(u_{x}))\\
d_{\delta}(f(u_{x-1}),f(u_{x}),f(u_{x+1}))\\
d_{\delta}(f(u_{x}),f(u_{x+1}),f(u_{x+2}))\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix},\quad u\in S^{\mathbb{Z}},

with
d_{\delta}:S^{3}\rightarrow S
given by (
2.1
) and
f:S\rightarrow S
being a bistable map (see (
2.4
)). We then call the quadruple
\mathcal{C}=({\mathbb{T}},X,S,F)
the bistable reaction-diffusion cellular automaton (abbreviated by RDCA) and study the induced discrete dynamical system

(2.6)

u(t+1)=F(u(t)),\quad t\in{\mathbb{T}},\ u\in S^{\mathbb{Z}}.

We construct the reaction-diffusion automaton via the composition of the reaction and diffusion (as opposed to summing them up as in continuous-state models (
1.3
), (
1.5
), see the interesting discussion in
[
20
]
on this topic). Our motivation stems, among other properties, from the fact that such an approach ensures the interval invariance, see
[
23
, Rem. 2.3]
for more details.

3.
Moving traveling waves

In this paper we focus on traveling wave solutions of bistable RDCA (
2.6
), i.e., solutions which can be written as

(3.1)

u_{n}(t)=w_{n-c\cdot t},\quad t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

for some wavespeed
c\in{\mathbb{N}}
and an increasing wave profile

(3.2)

w=(\ldots,w_{-2},w_{-1},w_{0},w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{N},w_{N+1},\ldots)

with

(3.3)

\ldots=w_{-1}=0=w_{0}<w_{1}\leq w_{2}\leq w_{3}\leq\ldots\leq w_{N-1}<w_{N}=K=w_{N+1}=\ldots,

for a finite
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We refer to the vector
w^{\mathcal{C}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{N-1})
as a
wave core
and call the value
N-1

wave core length
.

From our Ansatz (
3.1
) we derive that the wave profile satisfies

(3.4)

(\dots,w_{-1-c},w_{-c},w_{1-c},\dots)=F\left((\dots,w_{-1},w_{0},w_{1},\dots)\right),\quad c\in\mathbb{Z}.

We call the profile
w
given by (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) satisfying (
3.4
) with
c\in\mathbb{Z}
a
left traveling wave
for
c<0
,
right traveling wave
for
c>0
, and
pinned traveling wave
for
c=0
. Left or right traveling waves are denoted as
moving traveling waves
, see Fig.
4
.

Employing (
2.5
) we see that the traveling wave satisfies

(3.5)

w_{n-c}=f(w_{n})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n-1}),f(w_{n}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n+1}),f(w_{n})),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is straightforward to show that
|c|\leq 1
, i.e., there are no ‘fast’ traveling waves with
|c|\geq 2
.

Lemma 3.1

(Nonexistence of traveling waves for
|c|\geq 2
)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). Then there are no traveling waves (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) in the bistable RDCA (
2.6
) with
|c|\geq 2
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exists a left traveling wave with
c=-2
, i.e.,

(\dots,w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},\dots)=F\left((\dots,w_{-1},w_{0},w_{1},\dots)\right).

Equations (
2.1
) and (
2.5
) then imply the system of following equations

w_{n+2}=f(w_{n})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n-1}),f(w_{n}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n+1}),f(w_{n})),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

If
(w_{n-1},w_{n},w_{n+1})=(0,0,0)
, then
w_{n+2}=0
and we can repeat the argument for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
to get that
w\equiv 0
, a contradiction with (
3.3
).

Similar arguments can be used in order to prove the same for all
|c|\geq 2
.
∎

Straightforwardly, we can only focus on left traveling waves with negative speed
c<0
, the results for right-traveling waves with positive speed
c>0
translate automatically.

Lemma 3.2

(Existence of right traveling waves with increasing profiles)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). A wave profile
w
(
3.2
)–(
3.3
) satisfies (
3.4
) with
c=-1
if and only if the configuration
\widehat{w}
defined by
\widehat{w}_{n}=K-w_{N-n}

is a right traveling wave of the form (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) of RDCA (
2.6
) with
\widehat{f}(u)=K-f(K-u)
for all
u\in(0,K)_{\mathbb{Z}}
, i.e., satisfies (
3.4
) with
\widehat{c}=1
.

Proof.

First, let us observe that
\widehat{f}
is a bistable reaction function (
2.4
) with
\widehat{a}=K-a
.

If
w
is a left traveling with
c=-1
, then the equation (
3.4
) implies

(3.6)

w_{n+1}=f(w_{n})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n-1}),f(w_{n}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n+1}),f(w_{n})),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Applying
\widehat{f}(u)=K-f(K-u)
and
\widehat{w}_{n}=K-w_{N-n}
we get for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\widehat{w}_{n-1}

\displaystyle=K-w_{N-n+1}

\displaystyle=K-f(w_{N-n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{N-n-1}),f(w_{N-n}))-h_{\delta}(f(w_{N-n+1}),f(w_{N-n}))

\displaystyle=K-(K-\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n}))-h_{\delta}(K-\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n+1}),K-\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n}))-h_{\delta}(K-\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n-1}),K-\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n}))

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n})+h_{\delta}(\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n+1}),\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n}))+h_{\delta}(\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n-1}),\widehat{f}(\widehat{w}_{n})),

which shows that
\widehat{w}
satisfies (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) and (
3.4
) with
c=1
. The reverse implication follows by the same computation.
∎

(Existence of right traveling waves with decreasing profiles)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (

). A wave profile
w
(

)–(
3.3
) satisfies (
3.4
) with

if and only if the reverse configuration
\tilde{w}

(3.7)

\tilde{w}=(\ldots,w_{N+1},w_{N},\ldots,w_{2},w_{1},w_{0},w_{-1},w_{-2},\ldots),

Our key insight regarding bistable RDCA is the following necessary conditions on the first wave core value
w_{1}
.

Lemma 3.5

(Necessary condition on
w_{1}
)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). If there exists a left traveling wave (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) of bistable RDCA (
2.6
) with
c=-1
, then

(3.8)

\delta\geq w_{1}>a

and

(3.9)

f(w_{1})\geq 2w_{1}.

Proof.

We observe that the definition of
h_{\delta}
(
2.2
) implies the inequality

(3.10)

h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),0)\leq\frac{f(w_{1})}{2}.

Firstly, we provide the proof for inequality (
3.8
). The former inequality in (
3.8
), follows immediately from the definition of
h_{\delta}
in Eq. (
2.2
)

w_{1}=h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),0)\leq\delta.

Let us show the latter inequality in (
3.8
), i.e., that
w_{1}>a
holds. We assume by contradiction that
w_{1}\leq a
. Then Eq. (
3.5
) with
c=1
implies for

w_{1}=f(w_{0})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{-1}),f(w_{0}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{0}))=0+0+h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),0)=h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),0).

This is a contradiction with (
3.10
), since
f(w_{1})\leq w_{1}
for

implies

h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),0)\leq\frac{f(w_{1})}{2}<w_{1}.

In order to prove the inequality (
3.9
), we use the inequality (
3.10
) again to get

w_{1}=h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),0)\leq\frac{f(w_{1})}{2},

which is equivalent to
f(w_{1})\geq 2w_{1}
.

We can now fully characterize parametric values for which there exist left traveling waves of bistable RDCA. Moreover, we provide a constructive proof.

Theorem 3.6

(Necessary and sufficient condition left traveling waves)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). There exists a left traveling wave (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) of bistable RDCA (
2.6
) with
c=-1
if and only if
a<\frac{K}{2}
and there exists
p\in\left(a,\frac{K}{2}\right]_{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying

(3.11)

\delta\geq p,

and

(3.12)

f(p)\geq 2p.

Proof.

First, let us assume that the inequalities (
3.11
)–(
3.12
) are satisfied and let us construct the left traveling wave.

Construction of
w_{1}
.

First, we find
w_{1}>0
which satisfies

w_{1}=f(w_{0})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{-1}),f(w_{0}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{0}))=h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),0).

For this purpose we define an auxiliary function
\psi_{0}:[p,K]_{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
by

(3.13)

\psi_{0}(m):=h_{\delta}(f(m),0)-m.

Obviously, our goal is to find
w_{1}
such that
\psi_{0}(w_{1})=0
.

For
m=p
we can use the inequalities (
3.11
)–(
3.12
) to obtain

\psi_{0}(p)=h_{\delta}(f(p),0)-p\geq h_{\delta}(2p,0)-p\geq p-p=0.

Similarly for
m=K
, we obtain the reverse inequality

\psi_{0}(K)=h_{\delta}(f(K),0)-K=h_{\delta}(K,0)-K\leq\frac{K}{2}-K=-\frac{K}{2}<0.

If
\psi_{0}(p)=0
, then we can set
w_{1}=p
as
w_{1}=h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),0)
is fulfilled. Otherwise, we get
\psi_{0}(p)>0
and we determine
w_{1}
using

M:=\max\{m\in[p,K)_{\mathbb{Z}}:\psi_{0}(m)>0\}.

Then we get

\psi_{0}(M+1)=h_{\delta}(f(M+1),0)-M-1\geq h_{\delta}(f(M),0)-M-1\geq\psi_{0}(M)-1\geq 0,

which implies that
\psi_{0}(M+1)=0
and we set
w_{1}=M+1
.

Construction of
w_{n+1}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We use similar principles to construct all other terms of the increasing wave profile (
3.1
)–(
3.2
). Let us fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and define the function
\psi_{n}:[w_{n},K]_{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
by

(3.14)

\psi_{n}(m):=f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))+h_{\delta}(f(m),f(w_{n}))-m.

First, we show that
\psi_{n}(w_{n})\geq 0
. This follows from the following estimates

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(w_{n})

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n}))-w_{n}

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))-w_{n}

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))-f(w_{n-1})+

\displaystyle\quad+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n-1}),f(w_{n-2}))-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-f(w_{n-1})-2h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n-1}),f(w_{n-2}))

\displaystyle\geq f(w_{n})-f(w_{n-1})-2\cdot\frac{f(w_{n})-f(w_{n-1})}{2}+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n-1}),f(w_{n-2}))

\displaystyle=h_{\delta}(f(w_{n-1}),f(w_{n-2}))

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Similarly, we show that
\psi_{n}(K)\leq 0
:

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(K)

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))+h_{\delta}(f(K),f(w_{n}))-K

\displaystyle\leq f(w_{n})+h_{\delta}(f(K),f(w_{n}))-K

\displaystyle\leq f(w_{n})+\frac{K-f(w_{n})}{2}-K

\displaystyle=\frac{f(w_{n})-K}{2}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

If
\psi_{n}(w_{n})=0
, we set
w_{n+1}=w_{n}
since it satisfies Eq. (
3.5
). Similarly, if
\psi_{n}(K)=0
, we set
w_{n+1}=K
. Otherwise, we get
\psi_{n}(w_{n})\geq 0\geq\psi_{n}(K)
and we find
w_{n+1}
by defining

(3.15)

M:=\max\{m\in[w_{n},K)_{\mathbb{Z}}:\psi_{n}(m)>0\}.

We then get

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(M+1)

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))+h_{\delta}(f(M+1),f(w_{n}))-M-1

\displaystyle\geq f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))+h_{\delta}(f(M),f(w_{n}))-M-1

\displaystyle=\psi_{n}(M)-1

\displaystyle\geq 0,

which – combined with Eq. (
3.15
) – implies that
\psi_{n}(M+1)=0
and we can set
w_{n+1}=M+1
since it satisfies Eq. (
3.5
).

Correctness of the construction.

Let us show that the above construction is finite and satisfies (
3.3
). We demonstrate that if
w_{n-1}=w_{n}<K
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
w_{n+1}>w_{n}
and
w_{n+1}\leq K
.

If
w_{n-1}=w_{n}<K
, then we obtain using Eq. (
3.14
)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(w_{n})

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n}))-w_{n}

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-0+0-w_{n}

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-w_{n}>0.

Thus,
w_{n+1}>w_{n}
holds.

Let us prove that
w_{n+1}\leq K
for all
w_{n},w_{n-1}
. We can use Eq. (
3.14
) to estimate

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(w_{n}+1)

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}+1),f(w_{n}))-w_{n}-1

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-0+0-w_{n}-1

\displaystyle=f(w_{n})-w_{n}-1\geq 0.

Consequently,
w_{n+1}\in[w_{n}+1,K]_{\mathbb{Z}}
holds.

To conclude the proof, we observe that Lem.
3.5
imply that there is no wave profile (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) if inequalities (
3.11
)–(
3.12
) are violated for all
p\in\left(a,\frac{K}{2}\right]_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

Analogously we immediately obtain the same statement for the right traveling wave.

Theorem 3.7

(Necessary and sufficient condition right traveling waves)
.

\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}

4.
Pinned waves

Thms.
3.7
–
3.6
immediately imply necessary and sufficient conditions for the nonexistence of left or right traveling waves. There is a naturally intriguing question, whether we can find a pinned wave (with
c=0
) whenever their assumptions are violated.

Surprisingly, this answer, which is positive in the case of lattice
[
15
]
or partial
[
3
]
differential equations, can be negative in the setting of cellular automata. We provide a simple example to illustrate this fact before we discuss the case in which neither traveling nor pinned fronts exist in general.

Example 4.1
.

Let us consider a bistable nonlinearity (
2.4
) with
a=3
,
K=7
given by (see Fig.
6
)

(4.1)

f(u)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }u\in[0,3)_{\mathbb{Z}},\\
3&\text{if }u=3,\\
7&\text{if }u\in(3,7]_{\mathbb{Z}}.\\
\end{cases}

Thms.
3.6
and
3.7
imply there is no traveling wave for
\delta=3
. Let us show that there is no pinned wave (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) with
c=0
either.

Since
w_{-1}=w_{0}=0
we have from (
3.5
)

w_{0}=0=f(w_{0})+h_{3}(f(w_{-1}),f(w_{0}))+h_{3}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{0}))=0+0+h_{3}(f(w_{1}),0).

Consequently, we have either
w_{1}=1
or
w_{1}=2
. If
w_{1}=1
we have from (
3.5
)

w_{1}=1=f(w_{1})+h_{3}(f(w_{0}),f(w_{1}))+h_{3}(f(w_{2}),f(w_{1}))=0+0+h_{3}(f(w_{2}),0),

which is only possible if
w_{2}=3
. However, since
\delta=3
the equation

w_{2}=3=f(w_{2})+h_{3}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{2}))+h_{3}(f(w_{3}),f(w_{2}))=3-1+h_{3}(f(w_{3}),3)

cannot be satisfied for any
w_{3}
. If
w_{1}=2
, then

w_{1}=2=f(w_{1})+h_{3}(f(w_{0}),f(w_{1}))+h_{3}(f(w_{2}),f(w_{1}))=0+0+h_{3}(f(w_{2}),0)

cannot be satisfied since
h_{3}(f(3),0)=h_{3}(3,0)=1
and
h_{3}(f(n),0)=h_{3}(7,0)=3
for
n\in(3,7]_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Consequently, there is no pinned wave.

We will later see that the configuration from Example
4.1
leads to higher-order waves. However, we return first to the pinned waves waves with
c=0
. The pinned waves always exist in the case of the weakest diffusion
\delta=1
.

Lemma 4.2

(Pinned waves for
\delta=1
)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). If
\delta=1
, then

(1)

there are no moving traveling waves of (
2.6
),

(2)

there exists a pinned traveling wave of (
2.6
).

Proof.

The former statement follows immediately from Thms.
3.6
–
3.7
. We obtain the latter one by observing that

w=(\dots,0,0,1,K-1,K,K,\dots)

is a pinned wave for
\delta=1
.
∎

Once
\delta>1
, the situation becomes more intricate and we can take advantage of the following necessary and sufficient condition.

Lemma 4.3

(Necessary and sufficient condition for pinned waves)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). A wave profile
w
(
3.2
)–(
3.3
) is pinned if and only if

(4.2)

h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(w_{i}-f(w_{i})\right)

for all
n\in[1,+\infty)_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Let us demonstrate the necessity first. Using Eq. (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle w_{0}

\displaystyle=f(w_{0})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{-1}),f(w_{0}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{0}))

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=0+0+h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{0}))=h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{0})).

Consequently,

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle=f(w_{1})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{0}),f(w_{1}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{2}),f(w_{1}))=f(w_{1})+0+h_{\delta}(f(w_{2}),f(w_{1}))

\displaystyle h_{\delta}(f(w_{2}),f(w_{1}))

\displaystyle=w_{1}-f(w_{1})

and

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=f(w_{2})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{2}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{3}),f(w_{2}))

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=f(w_{2})-(w_{1}-f(w_{1}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{3}),f(w_{2}))

\displaystyle h_{\delta}(f(w_{3}),f(w_{2}))

\displaystyle=w_{2}-f(w_{2})+w_{1}-f(w_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(w_{i}-f(w_{i})\right).

Applying similar arguments for
w_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain Eq. (
4.2
).

To prove the sufficiency, it is enough to realize that if for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))
is given by Eq. (
4.2
), then Eq. (
3.5
) with
c=0
is satisfied for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

Lemma
4.3
immediately restricts the values
w_{1}
and
w_{N-1}
of the wave core for pinned waves.

Corollary 4.4

(Core boundaries)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). If there exists a pinned wave (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) with
c=0
, then

(4.3)

f(w_{1})\leq 1\leq w_{1}<a<w_{N-1}\leq K-1\leq f(w_{N-1}).

Proof.

The first inequality in (
4.3
) follows immediately from Eq. (
4.2
), which yields

h_{\delta}(f(w_{1}),f(w_{0}))=0.

The inequality
K-1\leq f(w_{N-1})
can be obtained in a similar fashion

\displaystyle K=w_{N}

\displaystyle=f(w_{N})+h_{\delta}(f(w_{N-1}),f(w_{N}))+h_{\delta}(f(w_{N+1}),f(w_{N}))=K+h_{\delta}(f(w_{N-1}),f(w_{N}))+0,

which implies
h_{\delta}(f(w_{N-1}),f(w_{N}))=0
and
K-1\leq f(w_{N-1})
.

To complete the proof, we observe that the inner inequalities in Eq. (
4.3
) follow from the definition of
f
in (
2.4
).
∎

Moreover, we can show that pinned waves have strictly increasing cores.

Lemma 4.5

(Strictly increasing cores of pinned waves)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). If there exists a pinned wave (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) with
c=0
, then

(4.4)

w_{n-1}<w_{n},\quad\text{ for all }n\in(0,N]_{\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
0<w_{n-1}=w_{n}<K
for some
n\in(0,N]_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Cor.
4.4
implies that the core contains at least one value
w_{i}\in(0,a)_{\mathbb{Z}}
and at least one value
w_{i}\in(a,K)_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
w_{i}-f(w_{i})>0
for
w_{i}\in(0,a)_{\mathbb{Z}}
, then Lem.
4.3
implies that

h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(w_{i}-f(w_{i})\right)>0

is strictly increasing for
n=2,\ldots,\ell,\ell+1
where

\ell=\max\{i:w_{i}<a\}.

Similarly,
h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))
is strictly decreasing for
n=\ell+1,\ldots,N-1
if
w_{\ell+1}>a
or for
n=\ell+2,\ldots,N-1
if
w_{\ell+1}=a
since
w_{i}-f(w_{i})<0
for
w_{i}\in(a,K)_{\mathbb{Z}}
. However, Cor.
4.4
and Lem.
4.3
imply

h_{\delta}(f(w_{N}),f(w_{N-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(w_{i}-f(w_{i})\right)=0.

Consequently,
h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1}))>0
for all
n=2,\ldots,N-1
, a contradiction.
∎

Consequently, we can find a straightforward bound on the length of pinned waves’ cores
w^{\mathcal{C}}
and show that small diffusion implies steep cores.

Corollary 4.6

(Core length of pinned waves)
.

Let
\delta,a,K\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2\leq a\leq K-2
and the bistable reaction function
f
be defined by (
2.4
). If there exists a pinned wave (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) with
c=0
, then

(4.5)

N\leq 2\delta+2.

Proof.

Lem.
4.5
yields that the core of pinned waves is strictly increasing. Moreover, combining Eq. (
4.2
) with the definition of
h_{\delta}
(
2.2
), we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(w_{i}-f(w_{i})\right)=h_{\delta}(f(w_{n}),f(w_{n-1})\leq\delta.

Since
w_{i}-f(w_{i})>0
for
w_{i}\in(0,a)_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\lvert\{w_{i}\in w^{\mathcal{C}}:0<w_{i}<a\}\rvert\leq\delta.

Similarly, we obtain

\lvert\{w_{i}\in w^{\mathcal{C}}:a<w_{i}<K\}\rvert\leq\delta.

Since
a
may also be in the wave core and there are
N-1
elements in the wave core, we have

N-1\leq 2\delta+1,

which implies (
4.5
).
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The bound (
4.5
) is optimal, since there is always a pinned wave with
w^{\mathcal{C}}=(1,a,K-1)
when
a=K/2
and
\delta=1
.

5.
Higher-order traveling waves

In this section we show that parametric regions in which neither moving nor pinned traveling waves exist lead to higher-order traveling waves. These solutions travel but periodically change their profiles.

Definition 5.1
.

A profile
w
forms a higher-order
(c,m)
-traveling wave for some
c\in\mathbb{Z},m\in{\mathbb{N}}
if

(\dots,w_{-1-c},w_{-c},w_{1-c},\dots)=F^{m}\left((\dots,w_{-1},w_{0},w_{1},\dots)\right).

The ratio

(5.1)

\gamma=\frac{c}{m}.

is called the speed of
(c,m)
-traveling wave.

In other words, a higher-order
(c,m)
-traveling wave is a set of
m
profiles which periodically repeat themselves and each periodic occurrence is coupled with a shift/speed
c
. Note that we do not call them periodic waves because these are commonly used to periodic profiles which travel
[
22
]
.

To illustrate, let us go back to Example
4.1
and show the existence of a higher-order
(1,2)
-traveling wave.

Example 5.2
.

Let us consider again a bistable nonlinearity (
2.4
) with
a=3
,
K=7
given by (

), see Fig.

,
and diffusion
\delta=3
. We have shown in Ex.

that in this case there are neither moving nor pinned traveling waves of the form (
3.1
).

The following computation shows that in this parametric setting the profile

w=(\ldots,0,0,3,4,7,7,\ldots)

forms higher-order
(1,2)
-traveling wave. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=(\ldots,0,0,3,4,7,7,\ldots)

\displaystyle\overline{w}=F(w)

\displaystyle=(\ldots,0,1,4,5,7,7,\ldots),

\displaystyle F^{2}(w)=F(\overline{w})

\displaystyle=(\ldots,0,3,4,7,7,7,\ldots),

see Fig.
8
for illustration. Note that profiles
w
with a core
w^{\mathcal{C}}=(3,4)
and
\overline{w}
with a core
\overline{w}^{\mathcal{C}}=(1,4,5)
interchange periodically every
m=2
periods while being shifted by
c=-1
to the left. They both form a higher-order
(-1,2)
-traveling wave with speed
\gamma=-1/2
.

This example can be generalized so that we can get behavior of waves for any diffusion parameter and the extreme case of reaction function like

(5.2)

f^{a,K}_{\mathrm{max}}(u):=\begin{cases}0,&u<a,\\

to get the existence of higher-order

-traveling waves in general.

Lemma 5.3

(Sufficient condition for existence
(\pm 1,2)
-traveling waves)
.

Let
1<a<\frac{K}{2}
and
\delta
be such that

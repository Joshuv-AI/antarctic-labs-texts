Covering instability for the existence of positive scalar curvature metrics
Chao Li, Boyu Zhang

Abstract
. We show that a closed non-orientable 3 3 -manifold admits a positive scalar curvature metric if and only if its orientation double cover does; however, for each 4 ≤ n ≤ 7 4\leq n\leq 7 , there exist infinitely many smooth non-orientable n n -manifolds M M that are mutually non-homotopy equivalent, such that the orientation double cover of M M admits positive scalar curvature metrics, but every closed smooth manifold that is homotopy equivalent to M M cannot admit positive scalar curvature metrics. These examples were first introduced by Alpert-Balitskiy-Guth in the study of Urysohn widths. To prove the nonexistence result, we extend the Schoen-Yau inductive descent approach to non-orientable manifolds. We also discuss band width estimates and the notion of enlargeability for non-orientable PSC manifolds.



1.
Introduction

The existence of Riemannian metrics with positive scalar curvature (abbreviated as PSC in this article) has been an important topic in geometry and topology. It is often assumed that the manifold under consideration is orientable. Indeed, if a non-orientable manifold has a PSC Riemannian metric, then so does its orientable double cover (by simply taking the covering metric). A natural question is whether the converse statement holds. As we will see from this paper, the answer is positive for
3

5.1

1.3.
Connections to macroscopic geometry

For an integer

(uniformly in

[
3
]
for interesting developments on these problems.

A well-known conjecture by Gromov (see, e.g.
[
11
]
) states that there exists a constant

such that

whenever

has scalar curvature bounded below by

by Liokumovich–Maximo
[
22
]
(see also
[
7
]
) and is open in higher dimensions. Our analysis on the scalar curvature of

and

fits well with Gromov’s conjecture: taking the orientation double cover of

simutanously decreases the Urysohn
(n-2)
-width and gives the existence of PSC metrics.

It would be interesting to investigate whether a similar covering instability phenomenon may happen for other curvature conditions - for instance the condition of positive Ricci curvature. Note that the classical metric-geometric properties of
\Ric_{g}>1
- e.g. the Bonnet–Myers’ diameter bound (or equivalently the Urysohn
0
-width bound) and the Bishop–Gromov volume comparison - are certainly preserved when passing to a covering space. We remark here that the orientable manifolds (with no PSC metrics) constructed by LeBrun in
[
21
]
actually have a double cover admitting a metric with positive Ricci curvature by Sha–Yang
[
31
]

1.4.
Remarks on higher dimensions

It is an interesting question whether the construction of

In our proof of the nonexistence result for

\mathcal{T}_{2k+1}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{2k+1}
, we have
p_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{2k+1}
for all

, and we can order the vertices so that each coordinate of

is no less than the corresponding coordinate of

. If
p_{0},\dots,p_{s}
are all contained in

, then

has at least

, so
p_{0},\dots,p_{s}
all have zero values on these coordinates. Therefore, the simplex spanned by
p_{0},\dots,p_{s}
is contained in
Z_{0}
.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be the barycentric subdivision of
\mathcal{T}
.
Let
N(Z)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
be the simplicial neighborhood of
Z
with respect to
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Let
N(Z^{\prime})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
be the simplicial neighborhood of

with respect to
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.

By Lemma

and Proposition

, we know that

are regular neighborhoods of

. In particular, the boundaries of

and

are PL submanifolds of

. We now show that

and

have the same boundary.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
p_{0},\dots,p_{2k+1}
are the vertices of a
(2k+1)
–simplex of
\mathcal{T}
. Then there exists an ordering of the vertices such that
p_{0},\dots,p_{k}
are contained in
Z
, and
p_{k+1},\dots,p_{2k+1}
are contained in
Z^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Define
Z_{0}\subset[0,1]^{2k+1}
to be the union of all faces of
[0,1]^{2k+1}
with dimensions
\leq k
and containing
(0,\dots,0)
.
Define
Z_{0}^{\prime}\subset[0,1]^{2k+1}
to be the union of all faces with dimensions
\leq k
and containing
(1,\dots,1)
.
We only need to show that if
p_{0},\dots,p_{2k+1}
are the vertices of a
(2k+1)
–simplex of
\mathcal{T}_{2k+1}
, then there exists an ordering such that
p_{0},\dots,p_{k}
are contained in
Z_{0}
, and
p_{k+1},\dots,p_{2k+1}
are contained in
Z_{0}^{\prime}
.

By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{2k+1}
, one may order
p_{0},\dots,p_{k+1}
so that
p_{i+1}
is obtained from
p_{i}
by changing exactly one coordinate from

to

. Note that a point

is contained in

if and only if at least

of the coordinates of

, and that

is contained in

if and only if at least
k+1
of the coordinates of

are equal to

. As a result, it is clear that

are contained in

, and
p_{k+1},\dots,p_{2k+1}
are contained in
Z_{0}^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

\partial N(Z)=\partial N(Z^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
s
be a
(2k+1)
–simplex of
\mathcal{T}
. We use Lemma
2.7
to show that
\partial N(Z)\cap s=\partial N(Z^{\prime})\cap s
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
equals the union of all
(2k+1)
–simplices of
\mathcal{T}
, the desired result follows.

The vertices of the barycentric subdivision of
s
are given by the barycenters of the faces of
s
. Suppose
t_{1},t_{2}
are the barycenters of
s_{1},s_{2}
, where
s_{1},s_{2}
are faces of
s
, we write “
t_{1}<t_{2}
” if
s_{1}
is a proper subset of
s_{2}
; we write “
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
” if
t_{1}<t_{2}
or
t_{1}=t_{2}
. Then each sequence of barycenters of the form

corresponds to a simplex of the barycentric subdivision of
s
. A simplex given by
t_{1}<\dots<t_{u}
is contained in
Z
if and only if
t_{u}\in Z
; it has a non-trivial intersection with
Z
if and only if
t_{1}\in Z
; it is contained in
N(Z)
if and only if there exists
t_{0}\leq t_{1}
such that
t_{0}\in Z
.

By Lemma

, suppose
t_{1}
is the barycenter of a face of
s
, there exists
t_{0}\leq t_{1}
such that
t_{0}\in Z
if and only if
t_{1}\notin Z^{\prime}
.
As a result, the simplex spanned by
t_{1}<\dots<t_{u}
is contained in
\partial N(Z)\cap s
if and only if
t_{1}\notin Z
and
t_{1}\notin Z^{\prime}
. Similarly, the same characterization holds for

. Hence
N(Z)\cap s=N(Z^{\prime})\cap s
.
∎

We define
X_{0}=\partial N(Z)=\partial N(Z^{\prime})
. By the definition of regular neighborhoods, we know that
X_{0}
is a PL submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
. It is also clear from the definition that
X_{0}
is invariant under translations by vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{2k+1}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2k+1}+(\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{1}{2})
.

Fix an integer
L>0
, let

v_{1}=(L,0,\cdots,0),v_{2}=(0,L,0,\cdots,0),\cdots,v_{2k}=(0,\cdots,0,L,0),

and

v_{2k+1}=(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2},\cdots,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}+L).

Let
G
be the (free) subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
generated by
v_{1},\dots,v_{2k+1}
. Then
G
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
by translations and
X_{0}
is an invariant subset of the
G
–action.
Define

X=X_{0}/G.

Note that the translations by
v_{1},\dots,v_{2k}
preserve
Z,Z^{\prime}
while the translation by
v_{2k+1}
swaps
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
. So the translations by
v_{1},\dots,v_{2k}
preserve the orientation of
X_{0}
and the translation by
v_{2k+1}
reverses the orientation of
X_{0}
.
Let
\hat{G}
be the (free) subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
generated by
v_{1},\dots,v_{2k},2v_{2k+1}
. Then the orientation double cover of
X
is

. Let
\hat{X}
denote the orientation double cover of
X
.

We record the following properties of
X
and
\hat{X}
for later reference.

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
k=2
or
3
so that both
X
and
\hat{X}
have unique smoothings. Then the smooth manifold
\hat{X}
admits a PSC metric.

Proof.

Note that
\hat{X}=X/\hat{G}
is the boundary of a regular neighborhood
N(Z)/\hat{G}
of
Z/\hat{G}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}/\hat{G}\cong\mathbb{T}^{2k+1}
. Since
Z
is a
k
–complex, its regular neighborhood has a PL handle decomposition by handles of indices
\leq k
. As a result, its boundary
\hat{X}
can be obtained from a disjoint union of
S^{2k}
’s by a sequence of PL surgeries along
\leq k-1
dimensional spheres.
Note that by transversality, every PL embedding of a sphere with dimension
\leq k-1
in a
2k
dimensional smooth manifold can be PL isotopic to a smooth embedding. Therefore,
\hat{X}
has a smoothing that is diffeomorphic to a manifold obtained from a disjoint union of
S^{2k}
’s by a sequence of smooth surgeries along
\leq k-1
dimensional spheres. Since
k\geq 2
, all surgeries have codimension
\geq 3
, so this smoothing admits a metric with PSC. Since the smoothing of
\hat{X}
is unique, the result is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

There exists a continuous map from
X
to
\mathbb{T}^{2k}
whose
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree is nonzero.

Proof.

Note that
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}/G\cong\mathbb{T}^{2k+1}
. Let
c
be the image in
\mathbb{T}^{2k+1}
of the straight line segment from
(0,\dots,0)
to
v_{2k+1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
. Then
c
is a circle.

The mod 2 intersection number of
X
and
c
is odd, because the fundamental domain of the preimage of
c
in
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
goes from one side of
X_{0}
to the other side of
X_{0}
.
As a result, the composition of the inclusion
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}/G
with the quotient map

\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}/G\to\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}/\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{2k},\mathbb{R}v_{2k+1}\rangle\cong\mathbb{T}^{2k}

has a nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree. Here,
\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{2k},\mathbb{R}v_{2k+1}\rangle
denotes the subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
generated by
v_{1},\dots,v_{2k},\mathbb{R}v_{2k+1}
.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

The Euler number of
X
is equal to
L^{2k}(L+1)\big(\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}{2k+1\choose i}\big)
.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbb{R}^{2k+1}
has a CW complex structure defined by decomposing it as the union of
[0,1]^{2k+1}+v
for all
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2k+1}
.
This induces a CW complex structure on
Z/\hat{G}
. It is straightforward to verify that the CW complex structure on
Z/\hat{G}
has dimension
k
, and the number of
i
–dimensional cells (
i\leq k
) is equal to
L^{2k}(L+1){2k+1\choose i}
. So

\chi(Z/\hat{G})=L^{2k}(L+1)\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}{2k+1\choose i}\Big).

The manifold
\hat{X}
bounds a regular neighborhood
N(Z/\hat{G})
of
Z/\hat{G}
. The double of
N(Z/\hat{G})
is a closed manifold with dimension
2k+1
, so its Euler number is zero.
Hence we have

2\chi(N(Z/\hat{G}))-\chi(\hat{X})=0.

Therefore,

\chi(X)=\tfrac{1}{2}\chi(\hat{X})=\chi(N(Z/\hat{G}))=\chi(Z/\hat{G}),

and the lemma is proved.
∎

3.
Schoen–Yau descent for non-orientable manifolds

In this section, we prove that the manifold
X
constructed in Section
2
does not admit any PSC metric. In fact, we will prove the following more general result, which is a generalization of the Schoen–Yau inductive descent argument to non-orientable manifolds.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
2\leq n\leq 7
, and
M^{n}
is a closed, possibly non-orientable manifold with dimension
n
. Suppose there exist
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1}\in H^{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})
such that

(1)

\alpha_{1}\smile\cdots\smile\alpha_{n-1}\neq 0\in H^{n-1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z}).

Then
M^{n}
does not admit any Riemannian metric with positive scalar curvature.

Remark 3.2
.

If
M^{n}
is orientable, then the theorem follows from the classical Schoen–Yau descent
[
28
]
. Our result extends Schoen–Yau’s result to the case of non-orientable manifolds.

Remark 3.3
.

It is important to have the assumption (
1
) on integral (instead of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
) cohomology in Theorem
3.1
. Indeed,
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
admits
\alpha\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}P^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
such that
\alpha^{n}\neq 0\in H^{n}(\mathbb{R}P^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and it obviously carries a PSC metric. This contrasts sharply with
[
15
]
, where Guth established the macroscopic analogue of the Schoen–Yau inductive descent for possibly nonorientable manifolds satisfying the analogous cohomology assumption (
1
) with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
coefficients. At a technical level, assumption (
1
) with
\mathbb{Z}
coefficients guarantees the two-sidedness of an area-minimizing hypersurface.

As an immediate corollary of Theorem
3.1
, we have the following:

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose
2\leq n\leq 7
and
M^{n}
is a closed, possibly non-orientable manifold with dimension
n
. Also assume that there exists a continuous map
f:M\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
with a nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree. Then
M^{n}
does not admit any Riemannian metric with positive scalar curvature.

Proof.

Let
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{Z})
such that
\beta_{1}\smile\dots\smile\beta_{n}
equals a generator of
H^{n}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{Z})
. By the assumptions,
f^{*}:H^{n}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to H^{n}(M;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is injective. Consider the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}

where the horizontal maps are given by the surjective homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on coefficients. Then the image of
\beta_{1}\smile\dots\smile\beta_{n}
in
H^{n}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is non-zero, so

f^{*}\beta_{1}\smile\dots\smile f^{*}\beta_{n}\neq 0\in H^{n}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z}).

Therefore,
M^{n}
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
3.1
. Hence
M^{n}
does not admit any PSC metric.
∎

Combining Corollary
3.4
and the earlier results, we can now finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming Theorem
3.1
.

Let
X
be the manifold constructed in Section
2
for
k=2
or
3
. By comparing the top-dimensional homology groups with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
coefficients, every closed manifold
X^{\prime}
homotopy equivalent to

is of dimension

. Then by Lemma
2.10
and Corollary
3.4
, the manifold
X^{\prime}
does not admit any metric with PSC. On the other hand, by Lemma
2.9
, the orientation double cover of
X
admits PSC metrics. This gives the examples for
n=4
and
6
.

To construct the examples for
n=5
and
7
, note that if
M^{n}
admit a map to
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with a nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree, then
M^{n}\times S^{1}
admit a map to
\mathbb{T}^{n+1}
with a nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree. Therefore, the examples for
n=5
and
7
can be given by taking the product of
S^{1}
with the example in dimension
n-1
. Alternatively, let
X^{4}
be the example in dimension
4
, then
X^{4}\times(S^{1})^{n-4}
are desired examples in dimensions
5\leq n\leq 7
.

Recall that the construction of
X
depends on the choices of a positive integer
L
.
By Lemma
2.11
, the total rank of the homology groups of
X^{4}\times(S^{1})^{n-4}
diverges to infinity as

, so we obtained infinitely many examples that are mutually non-homotopy equivalent.
∎

The rest of this section proves Theorem
3.1
. We apply induction on
n
. When
n=2
, the only closed non-orientable manifold with positive curvature is
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
. But
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}P^{2};\mathbb{Z})=0
, so the conclusion holds. Assume now that the conclusion holds for
n-1
.

If
M^{n}
is orientable, then the result follows from the classical theorem of Schoen–Yau. We assume without loss of generality that
M^{n}
is non-orientable. For the sake of contradiction, assume
M^{n}
has a PSC metric
g
. Let
\hat{M}^{n}
be the orientation double cover of
M^{n}
, let
\pi:\hat{M}^{n}\to M^{n}
be the covering map, and let
\hat{g}
be the pull-back of
g
to
\hat{M}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The pull back map
\pi^{*}:H^{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}(\hat{M}^{n};\mathbb{Z})
is injective.

Proof.

By the universal coefficient theorem,
H^{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})\cong\Hom(H_{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})
. Since
H_{1}
is the abelianization of
\pi_{1}
, and
\mathbb{Z}
is abelian, we have a natural isomorphism
\Hom(H_{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})\cong\Hom(\pi_{1}(M^{n}),\mathbb{Z})
. This yields a natural isomorphism

H^{1}(M^{n},\mathbb{Z})\cong\Hom(\pi_{1}(M^{n}),\mathbb{Z}).

Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(M^{n},\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\hat{M}^{n},\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\pi_{1}(M^{n}),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\pi_{1}(\hat{M}^{n}),\mathbb{Z}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}}

where the vertical maps are the natural isomorphisms defined above.
The image of
\pi_{1}(\hat{M}^{n})
in
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
is a subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
with index
2
. So, a homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(M^{n})\to\mathbb{Z}
is trivial if and only if its restriction to the image of
\pi_{1}(\hat{M}^{n})
in trivial. This implies that the map
\pi^{*}:\Hom(\pi_{1}(M^{n}),\mathbb{Z})\to\Hom(\pi_{1}(\hat{M}^{n}),\mathbb{Z})
is injective, so
\pi^{*}:H^{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}(\hat{M}^{n};\mathbb{Z})
is injective.
∎

Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}
be given by the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
. By Lemma
3.5
, we know that
\pi^{*}\alpha_{n-1}\neq 0\in H^{1}(\hat{M}^{n},\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
\gamma
be the non-trivial deck transformation on
\hat{M}^{n}
, then
\gamma
is an orientation-reversion isometry of
(\hat{M}^{n},\hat{g})
.

Fix an orientation of
\hat{M}^{n}
.
Let
PD(\pi^{*}\alpha_{n-1})\in H_{n-1}(\hat{M}^{n};\mathbb{Z})
denote the Poincaré dual of
\pi^{*}\alpha_{n-1}
. Since
\gamma
is an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism of
\hat{M}^{n}
and
\gamma^{*}(\pi^{*}\alpha_{n-1})=\pi^{*}\alpha_{n-1}
, we have

\gamma_{*}(PD(\pi^{*}\alpha_{n-1}))=-PD(\pi^{*}\alpha_{n-1}).

Consider the set

\mathcal{S}=\{\hat{\Sigma}\in PD(\pi^{*}\alpha_{n-1})\text{ is an integral current}\mid\gamma(\hat{\Sigma})=-\hat{\Sigma}\}.

Note that
\mathcal{S}
is closed under current convergence: indeed, if
\hat{\Sigma}_{j}\in\mathcal{S}
converges to
\hat{\Sigma}
, then
\hat{\Sigma}
is integral; moreover, we have that for any continuous
(n-1)
-form
\omega
,
\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{j}}(\gamma^{*}\omega+\omega)=0
, so
\int_{\hat{\Sigma}}(\gamma^{*}\omega+\omega)=0
. Thus,
\hat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{S}
. The set
\mathcal{S}
is also non-empty. To find an element in
\mathcal{S}
, let
f:M\to S^{1}
be a smooth map such that the pull-back of a generator of
H^{1}(S^{1};\mathbb{Z})
to
M
equals
\alpha_{n-1}
, let
p\in S^{1}
be a regular value of

, then
(f\circ\pi)^{-1}(p)
can be endowed with an orientation so that it defines an integral current in
\mathcal{S}
.

Therefore, by minimizing the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume among elements in
\mathcal{S}
, we obtain a smooth oriented area-minimizing hypersurface
\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
satisfying
\gamma(\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1})=-\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
, where
-\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
denotes
\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
with the reversed orientation. Let
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M^{n}
be the image of
\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
under
\pi
.

Note that
\Sigma^{n-1}
is two-sided. In fact, since
\gamma(\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1})=-\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
and
\gamma
reverses the orientation of
\hat{M}^{n}
, the map
\gamma
preserves the orientation of the normal bundle of
\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
. Therefore, the normal bundle of
\Sigma^{n-1}
is oriented.

By construction,
\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
is stable among all deformations inside the set

, so
\Sigma^{n-1}
is stable among all deformations.
The stability inequality together with the Schoen-Yau rearrangement
[

on the two-sided hypersurface

give that

\int_{\Sigma^{n-1}}|\nabla f|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(R_{M^{n}}-R_{\Sigma^{n-1}}+|A|^{2})f^{2}\geq 0,

for all

. If

, this together with Gauss-Bonnet imply that every connected component of
\Sigma^{2}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
or
S^{2}
. If

, this implies that
\lambda_{1}(-\Delta+\tfrac{n-3}{4(n-2)}R_{g})>0
on
\Sigma^{n-1}
, so
g|_{\Sigma^{n-1}}
is conformal to a positive scalar curvature metric.

In the following, we show that
\Sigma^{n-1}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, so we may apply the induction hypothesis on
\Sigma^{n-1}
.

Let
\nu\Sigma^{n-1}
be the normal bundle of
\Sigma^{n-1}
in
M^{n}
. Since
\nu\Sigma^{n-1}
is an oriented line bundle, it has a Thom class

e\in H^{1}(\nu\Sigma^{n-1},\nu\Sigma^{n-1}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})
. Let

denote the image of

in
H^{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})

under the composition map

H^{1}(\nu\Sigma^{n-1},\nu\Sigma^{n-1}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{1}(M^{n},M^{n}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z}),

where the first isomorphism is given by excision, and the second map is the pull back map of the inclusion
(M^{n},\emptyset)\subset(M^{n},M^{n}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1})
.

Lemma 3.6
.

We have
e|_{M^{n}}=\alpha_{n-1}
in
H^{1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Let
\nu\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
be the oriented normal bundle of
\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
.
Let
\hat{e}\in H^{1}(\nu\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1},\nu\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}\setminus\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})
be the Thom class of
\nu\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}
, and let
\hat{e}|_{\hat{M}^{n}}
denote the image of
\hat{e}

under the composition map

H^{1}(\nu\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1},\nu\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1}\setminus\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{1}(\hat{M}^{n},\hat{M}^{n}\setminus\hat{\Sigma}^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}(\hat{M}^{n};\mathbb{Z}).

By the naturality of Thom class, the pull-back of

equals
\hat{e}
, so
\pi^{*}(e|_{M})=\hat{e}|_{\hat{M}}
. By the relations of Poincaré duality with Thom class, we know that
\hat{e}|_{\hat{M}}=PD([\hat{\Sigma}])
, so
\hat{e}|_{\hat{M}}=\pi^{*}(\alpha_{n-1})
. As a consequence,
\pi^{*}(e|_{M})=\pi^{*}(\alpha_{n-1})
. By Lemma
3.5
,
\pi^{*}
is injective on
H^{1}(-;\mathbb{Z})
, so
e|_{M^{n}}=\alpha_{n-1}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

(\alpha_{1}|_{\Sigma^{n-1}})\smile\cdots\smile(\alpha_{n-2}|_{\Sigma^{n-1}})\neq 0\in H^{n-2}(\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Let
p:\nu\Sigma^{n-1}\to\Sigma^{n-1}
be the projection of the normal bundle of
\Sigma^{n-1}
to the base.
To simplify notation, we also use
e
to denote the image of
e\in H^{1}(\nu\Sigma^{n-1},\nu\Sigma^{n-1}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})
under the excision isomorphism

H^{1}(\nu\Sigma^{n-1},\nu\Sigma^{n-1}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{1}(M^{n},M^{n}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z}).

Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n-2}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n-1}(M^{n},M^{n}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n-1}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n-2}(\nu\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n-1}(\nu\Sigma^{n-1},\nu\Sigma^{n-1}\setminus\Sigma^{n-1};\mathbb{Z})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\smile e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\smile e}

where the unlabeled maps are pull backs induced by the inclusions.
Consider

x=\alpha_{1}\smile\dots\smile\alpha_{n-2}\in H^{n-2}(M^{n};\mathbb{Z}).

Since

, the image of

equals

is non-zero. Since

deformation retracts to

∎

Now we finish the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Lemma

,
\Sigma^{n-1}
satisfies all the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
. By the induction hypothesis,
\Sigma^{n-1}
cannot admit a metric with positive scalar curvature. This contradicts the earlier result that

is conformal to a positive scalar curvature metric.
∎

4.
The enlargeability criterion and band width estimates

With the help of Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.4
, we establish an enlargeability criterion that obstructs existence of PSC metrics on non-orientable manifolds. We also obtain width estimates for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-over-torical bands in the process.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose
M^{n}
is a closed, possibly non-orientable
n
–manifold. We say that
M^{n}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
–enlargeable
, if for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a covering space
\hat{M}^{n}
of
M^{n}
and an
\varepsilon
-contracting continuous map
f:\hat{M}^{n}\to S^{n}(1)
to the unit sphere, such that
f
is constant near infinity and has a nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree.

This is an extension of the original definition of enlargeable manifolds for the orientable case by Gromov–Lawson
[
12
]
. It is not hard to check that the notion of enlargeability for non-orientable manifolds depends only on the homotopy type of the manifold, and is preserved under taking connected sums or products. Generally, any manifold which admits a map of nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree to a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
–enlargeable manifold is itself enlargeable (cf.
[
20
, Theorem 5.3]
, where one can apply the same proof for the non-orientable case by replacing the coefficients with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
).

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
3\leq n\leq 7
, and
M^{n}
is a closed, possibly non-orientable,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
–enlargeable manifold. Then
M^{n}
does not admit any PSC Riemannian metric.

Remark 4.3
.

Theorem
4.2
can be regarded as a generalization of Corollary
3.4
. This is because
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
–enlargeable, so every manifold satisfying the conditions of Corollary
3.4
is also
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
–enlargeable.

The proof of Theorem
4.2
is based on the extension of an argument of Cecchini–Schick
[
6
]
, where they proved the nonexistence of PSC metrics on orientable (but not necessarily spin) enlargeable manifolds using minimal hypersurfaces. We briefly sketch our argument before getting into the details of the proof. Without loss of generality, assume that
(M,g)
satisfies that
R_{g}>1
, and
(\hat{M},\hat{g})
is a Riemannian covering with a map
f:\hat{M}\to S^{n}(1)
with nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree such that
|df|<\varepsilon
. Here
\varepsilon
is to be chosen later. We will construct an open band
U
contained in
\hat{M}
such that
f:U\to\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times(0,1)
is proper with nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree, and the distance between the connected components of
\partial U
is sufficiently large. These conditions enable us to use the standard
\mu
-bubble to find a two-sided and possibly non-orientable hypersurface
\Sigma\subset U
that is conformal to a PSC manifold, but also admits a map to
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}
with nonzero
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree, contradicting Corollary
3.4
.

Now we present the details of the proof.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Fix a smooth embedding
\iota:\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\to S^{n}(1)
. Denote
A=\iota(\mathbb{T}^{n-1})
.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,1]\to S^{n}(1)
be a smooth embedding such that

\varphi(x,\tfrac{1}{2})=\iota(x)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{T}^{n-1},

and let

\delta=\tfrac{1}{2}\dist_{S^{n}(1)}\big(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{0\}),\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{1\})\big).

Let
N(A)=\imag(\varphi)
. Then
N(A)
is a closed tubular neighborhood of
A
.

By the definition of
\delta
, there exists a smooth function

\rho:N(A)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

such that
\rho=0
on
\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{0\})
,
\rho=\delta
on
\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{1\})
, and
|d\rho|\leq 1
pointwise on
N(A)
.

Now we fix
\varepsilon<\tfrac{\delta}{2\pi}
and consider a Riemannian covering
(\hat{M},\hat{g})
with a map
f:\hat{M}\to S^{n}(1)
with
|df|<\varepsilon
.
Perturbing
f
if necessarily, we may assume that
f
is transverse to
\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{0,\frac{1}{2},1\})
.
If
\hat{M}
is non-compact, by composing
f
with a self-isometry of
S^{n}(1)
if necessary, we may also assume that the image of
f
near infinity is not contained in
N(A)
.

Then for
a=0,1
, we have that
f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{a\}))
is a closed smooth submanifold of
\hat{M}
that has an orientable normal bundle. Note that
f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,1]))
is a smooth, codimension-0 submanifold of
\hat{M}
whose boundary is
f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{0,1\}))
. For each connected component
Y
of
f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,1]))
, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degrees of the maps

(2)

f:f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{a\}))\cap Y\to\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{a\})

are the same for
a=0,1
because the manifolds
f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{a\}))\cap Y
are cobordant in
Y
. The sum of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degrees of (
2
) for all connected components
Y
of
f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,1]))
is equal to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree of
f:\hat{M}\to S^{n}(1)
. Hence we may find a connected component
Y
such that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degrees of (
2
) is nonzero.

Let
Y
be as above, let

\partial_{-}Y=f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{0\}))\cap Y,\quad\partial_{+}Y=f^{-1}(\varphi(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{1\}))\cap Y.

Then
Y
is a smooth compact manifold with boundary
\partial_{-}Y\cup\partial_{+}Y
.
The composition function
\rho\circ f
is a smooth function on
Y
that is equal to
0
on
\partial_{-}Y
, is equal to
\delta
on
\partial_{+}Y
, and satisfies
|d(\rho\circ f)|<\tfrac{\delta}{2\pi}
pointwise on
Y
.

We consider the standard
\mu
-bubble functional on
Y
. Precisely, fix
\Omega_{0}=f^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times(0,\tfrac{1}{2}))\cap Y
. Among relative open subsets
\Omega
containing
\partial_{-}Y
and is disjoint from
\partial_{+}Y
, minimize

\mathcal{A}(\Omega)=|\partial\Omega|-\int_{Y}(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}})h,

here
h
is a smooth function defined by

h=-\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{\delta}(\rho\circ f)-\frac{\pi}{2}\right).

Since
h\to\mp\infty
while approaching
\partial_{\pm}Y
, standard existence and regularity results (see, e.g.
[
32
]
) imply that a minimizer
\Omega
exists and
\Sigma:=\partial\Omega
is a smooth hypersurface. We know that
\Sigma
is two-sided as it is contained in the boundary of a domain, and it is cobordant to
\partial_{\pm}Y
in
Y
. Therefore, the second variation of
\mu
-bubbles implies that

\lambda_{1}(-\Delta_{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma})\geq\frac{1}{2}\inf(R_{\hat{g}}-2|\nabla h|+h^{2})>0,

since we assumed that
R_{\hat{g}}>1
and

2|\nabla h|\leq\frac{1}{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{\delta}(\rho\circ f)-\frac{\pi}{2})}\cdot\frac{2\pi}{\delta}|d\rho|\cdot|df|<\frac{1}{\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi}{\delta}(\rho\circ f)-\frac{\pi}{2})}=1+h^{2}.

Therefore
\Sigma
is conformally PSC.

Since
\Sigma
is cobordant to
\partial_{\pm}Y
in
Y
, the composition map

\Sigma\hookrightarrow Y\xrightarrow{f}N(A)\cong T^{n-1}\times[0,1]\xrightarrow{\text{projection}}\mathbb{T}^{n-1}

has the same
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
degree as (
2
), which is assumed to be nonzero. Since
\Sigma
is conformally PSC, this yields a contradiction with Corollary
3.4
.
∎

With the above discussions, we can now easily extend Gromov’s band width estimates to possibly non-orientable bands that admits a map with nonzero mod
2
degree to
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,1]
.

Definition 4.4
.

A band is a compact, possibly non-orientable manifold
Y^{n}
together with a decomposition
\partial Y=\partial_{-}Y\sqcup\partial_{+}Y
, where
\partial_{\pm}Y
are nonempty, disjoint unions of connected components of
\partial Y
. A band
Y
is called
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-over-torical, if it admits a proper continuous map

f:Y\to\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times[0,1]

with nonzero mod
2
degree and satisfies
f(\partial_{-}Y)=\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{0\}
,
f(\partial_{+}Y)=\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\{1\}
.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we observe that Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.4
imply the following band width estimates.

Proposition 4.5
.

Assume
n\leq 7
,
Y^{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-over-torical band with a Riemannian metric
g
satisfying
R_{g}\geq n(n-1)
. Then

\dist_{g}(\partial_{-}Y,\partial_{+}Y)\leq\frac{2\pi}{n}.

Proof.

Observe that each connected component of
\partial_{\pm}Y
is two-sided and admits a map with nonzero mod
2
degree to
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}
, and so is every hypersurface in
Y
that is cobordant to
\partial_{\pm}Y
. Therefore, Gromov’s
\mu
-bubble proof
[
13
]
of the over-torical band width estimates carries over verbatim to this case.
∎

5.
The
3
–dimensional case

In this section, we prove that examples described by Theorem
1.1
do not exist in
3
-dimensions:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
M^{3}
be a closed non-orientable three manifold. Then
M
has a PSC metric if and only if its orientation double cover
\hat{M}
does.

The proof is based on Proposition
5.3
below, which extends the prime decomposition to non-orientable
3
-manifolds. Proposition
5.3
is probably known to experts in 3-manifold topology. See, for example,
[
17
, Section 1.1, Exercies 4]
. Here we give a brief proof since we were unable to find a direct reference. We will also sketch a second proof in a more geometric flavor that is closely related to
[
4
, Section 3]
.

To streamline the exposition, we introduce the following notation.

Definition 5.2
.

Suppose
Y
is a compact connected three-manifold that is possibly non-orientable. Suppose each connected component of
\partial Y
is
S^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
. Define a closed orientable manifold
\widetilde{Y}
as follows. If
Y
is orientable, then every connected component of
\partial Y
is
S^{2}
, and we let
\widetilde{Y}
be the manifold obtained by gluing a copy of
D^{3}
to each component of
\partial Y
. If
Y
is non-orientable, let
\hat{Y}
be the orientation double cover of
Y
, define
\widetilde{Y}
to be the manifold obtained by gluing a copy of
D^{3}
to each component of
\partial\hat{Y}
.

Note that
\widetilde{Y}
has a subset
Y^{\dagger}
that is either the orientation double cover of
Y
or diffeomorphic to
Y
. So we have a canonical isomorphism
\pi_{2}(Y^{\dagger})\cong\pi_{2}(Y)
, thus the inclusion of
Y^{\dagger}
in
Y
induces a canonical map

(3)

\pi_{2}(Y)\to\pi_{2}(\widetilde{Y}).

Since the second homotopy group is isomorphic to the second homology group of the universal cover, and since the universal cover of
\widetilde{Y}
is obtained from the universal cover of
Y
by filling in the boundaries with
D^{3}
, we note that (
3
) is a surjection.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose
M
is a closed, non-orientable, compact 3-manifold. There exists a finite collection of smooth, disjoint, embedded, two-sides surfaces, each diffeomorphic to
S^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, such that after cutting open
M
along the these surfaces, each component
C
satisfies that either
\widetilde{C}
is aspherical or
\widetilde{C}
has a finite fundamental group.

Proof.

By a straightforward computation using the Mayer–Vietoris sequence, we know that if
Y
is a compact connected
3
–manifold and
\Sigma\subset Y
is an embedded two-sided surface that is diffeomorphic to
S^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, then cutting
Y
open along
\Sigma
decreases the rank of
H^{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
by
1
. Assume
M
is connected without loss of generality. Then, after cutting open
M
along a finite collection of two-sided
S^{2}
’s and
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
’s, we obtain a connected manifold
M^{\prime}
such that each embedded two-sided
S^{2}
and
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
in
M^{\prime}
is separating.

If
M^{\prime}
admits an embedded two-sided surface
\Sigma
such that:

(1)

\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to
S^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
,

(2)

cutting open
M^{\prime}
yields two components
M^{\prime}_{1}
and
M^{\prime}_{2}
such that neither
\widetilde{M_{1}^{\prime}}
nor
\widetilde{M_{2}^{\prime}}
is diffeomorphic to
S^{3}
,

we cut open
M^{\prime}
along
\Sigma
, and repeat the process. We first show that the process must end after finitely many steps.

Define
\hat{M^{\prime}}
to be the orientation double cover of
M^{\prime}
if
M^{\prime}
is non-orientable, and define
\hat{M^{\prime}}
to be two copies of
M^{\prime}
if
M^{\prime}
is orientable. We invoke the following result that is part of the proof of the prime decomposition theorem for oriented 3-manifolds; see, for example,
[
17
]
, Statement (
\ast
) in the proof of Theorem 1.5:

Lemma
.

There exists a constant
n_{0}
depending only on
\hat{M^{\prime}}
, such that if
n\geq n_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1},\dots,\Sigma_{n}
are disjoint embedded spheres in
\hat{M^{\prime}}
, then after cutting open
\hat{M^{\prime}}
along all
\Sigma_{i}
’s, there is at least one component

such that
\widetilde{C}\cong S^{3}
.

By the above lemma, if
M^{\prime}
is cut open along a collection of disjoint embedded two-sided surfaces consisting of
a
copies of
S^{2}
’s and
b
copies of
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
’s, and if

, then there exists a component
C
in the resulting manifold such that
\widetilde{C}\cong S^{3}
. As a result, the process of cutting
M^{\prime}
must end after finitely many steps.

In summary, we have obtained a finite collection of disjoint embedded two-sided surfaces
\Sigma_{1},\dots,\Sigma_{n}
in
M^{\prime}
, such that each surface is
S^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, and cutting open
M^{\prime}
along the surfaces yields

M^{\prime}_{1},\dots,M^{\prime}_{n+1}.
Moreover, for each component

is an embedded

or
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, then cutting open

will yield at least one component

.

We claim that
\pi_{2}(\widetilde{M_{i}^{\prime}})=0
for all

be the kernel of the map

given by (

. Note that

for all

If

is infinite, then the universal cover of

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Suppose that
\hat{M}
has a PSC metric. Applying Proposition
5.3
to
M
, we may cut
M
along a disjoint union of embedded two-sided surfaces
\Sigma_{1},\cdots,\Sigma_{n}
, each
\Sigma_{j}
is either
S^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, such that

, and each
\widetilde{C}_{i}
is either a spherical space form (if its fundamental group is finite), or is aspherical. Passing to the orientation double cover,
\hat{M}
can be obtained by taking connected sums of copies of
\widetilde{C}_{i}
’s and

’s. Since
\hat{M}
has a PSC metric, we conclude from
[
29
]
or
[
12
]
that it does not admit any map with non-zero degree to a closed aspherical

-manifold. Consequently, each
\widetilde{C}_{i}
is not aspherical and hence is a spherical space form.

We therefore conclude that
M
can be obtained by performing either standard connected sums (i.e.
0
-surgeries), or
0
-orbifold surgeries at isolated orbifold points with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, on the disjoint union of spherical space forms (that are possibly orbifolds). Since these surgeries preserve the PSC condition (see, e.g.
[

]
), we conclude that

has a PSC metric.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

We briefly indicate a more geometric proof of Proposition
5.3
. This proof closely aligns with the argument of
[
4
, Section 3]
. In the place of the generalized sphere theorem
[
18
, Theorem 4.12]
, we invoke the geometric result of Meeks–Yau
[
23
]
: for a closed
3
-manifold
(M,g)
, there exist finitely many conformal maps
f_{1},\cdots,f_{n}:S^{2}\to M
with pairwise disjoint images, such that
\{f_{j}\}
generates
\pi_{2}(M)
as a
\pi_{1}(M)
-module, each
f_{j}
minimizes area in its homotopy class, and each
f_{j}
is either an embedding or a two-to-one covering to
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
. Observe that the image of each
f_{j}
is two-sided. This is obvious if
f_{j}
is an embedding (as
S^{2}
is simply connected); if
f_{j}
has an image of
\Sigma_{j}=\mathbb{R}P^{2}
, then it is also necessarily two-sided, as otherwise a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma_{j}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}P^{3}\setminus\{pt\}
, and hence
\Sigma_{j}
would be homotopically trivial and thus contradicting its minimizing property.

Consequently, the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
carries over verbatim; it also implies that each non-orientable PSC
3
-manifold can be obtained from a disjoint union of spherical space forms (that are possibly orbifolds) by performing
0
-surgeries (possibly at isolated orbifold singularities).

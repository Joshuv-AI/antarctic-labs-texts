Padé Approximants for Fast Radio Bursts Diffuse Dispersion Measure
and Jiaming Zhuge (诸葛佳明)

Abstract
Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) have become an indispensable tool for studying the Universe’s ionisation properties, as well as its cosmological parameters. This is achieved by analysing their diffuse dispersion measure ( DM diff {\rm DM}_{\rm diff} ) as a function of redshift. However, this requires an integration along the line-of-sight, which is time-consuming. In this work, we derive an analytical approximation formula for DM diff {\rm DM}_{\rm diff} for flat, Λ \Lambda CDM and w w CDM universes. We show that our approximation works well for the ranges 0.01 ≤ z ≤ 2 0.01\leq z\leq 2 , 0.2 ≤ Ω m ≤ 1.0 0.2\leq\Omega_{m}\leq 1.0 and − 3.0 ≤ w ≤ − 0.5 -3.0\leq w\leq-0.5 , with relative fractional error to a numerically evaluated DM diff {\rm DM}_{\rm diff} always smaller than 3.5 % 3.5\ \% , in the worst case scenario, and in most cases smaller than 0.5 % 0.5\ \% . Moreover, the approximation is more than 15 15 ( 2 2 ) times faster than the numerical solution of Λ \Lambda CDM ( w w CDM). Therefore, we hope that it could be a useful tool for the FRB community.

Introduction

Almost twenty years from their discovery
[
21
]
, FRBs have become a unique probe to study the Universe and its constituents
[
7

,

. At the same time, they offer multiple opportunities for multimessenger astrophysics if they can be associated with other high-energy astrophysical phenomena
[

. In all cases, a fast and accurate calculation of the theoretical dispersion measure of the diffuse component is needed to compare with observed data.

In this work, we derive a useful approximation for the diffuse
{\rm DM}
of FRBs, in a
flat,
\Lambda
CDM
universe, given by

{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}=\frac{3cH_{0}\Omega_{b}f_{\rm diff}}{8\pi Gm_{p}\sqrt{\Omega_{m}}}\chi_{7/8}\Bigg(\Phi\big[x(0,\Omega_{m})\big]-\sqrt{1+z}\cdot\Phi\big[x(z,\Omega_{m})\big]\Bigg),

(1.1)

where

x(z,\Omega_{m})=\frac{1-\Omega_{m}}{\Omega_{m}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1+z)^{3}},

(1.2)

and

\Phi(x)=-\frac{2.0+2.856x+1.095x^{2}+0.0913x^{3}}{1.0+1.3280x+0.4486x^{2}+0.0277x^{3}}.

(1.3)

The different parameters correspond to:
H_{0}
the Hubble parameter,
c
the speed of light,
\Omega_{b}
the density of baryons in the Universe,
G
Newton’s gravitational constant,
m_{p}
the mass of the proton,
\Omega_{m}
the density of matter in the Universe, and we have assumed
1=\Omega_{m}+\Omega_{\Lambda}
, with
\Omega_{\Lambda}
the density due to a cosmological constant.
f_{\rm diff}
and
\chi_{7/8}
refer, respectively, to the portion of diffuse baryons and ionised electrons in the Universe and will be explained in more detail in section
2
.
z
denotes the redshift and
\Phi
the Padé approximant derived in detail in section
3
, with the coefficients of eq. (
1.3
) rounded to the fourth significant digit.

A similar approximation for the diffuse
{\rm DM}
of FRBs, in a

2
FRBs Dispersion Measure

FRBs are bursts of radio emission that last for the order of milliseconds. As they travel through the Universe the different frequencies of the signal travel with different speeds and arrive at different times. These time delays are quantified by the dispersion measure of the pulse. During its propagation through the Universe, the FRB pulse passes through various environments, whose dispersion measures is usually analysed separately
[
27
,
8
,
26
, e.g.]
. In this work, we are focusing on one such contribution,
{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}
.

The
{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}
measures the contributions of diffused electrons along the line-of-sight, drawn from the intergalactic medium (IGM) and intervening cosmic halos. This has the clearest connection to cosmology, since a mean diffuse
{\rm DM}
can be derived theoretically and depends on the various cosmological parameters. Assuming a homogeneous distribution and ionization of baryons, with ionized fraction
\chi(z)

[
17
,
16
,
8
]
one has, for a flat,
\Lambda
CDM universe:

\langle{{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}}\rangle(z)=\frac{3cH_{0}\Omega_{b}}{8\pi Gm_{p}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{f_{\rm diff}(z)\chi(z)(1+z)}{\sqrt{\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+(1-\Omega_{m})}}d{z},

(2.1)

with

\chi(z)=Y_{\rm H}X_{e,\textrm{H}}(z)+\frac{1}{2}Y_{\rm He}X_{e,\textrm{He}}(z).

(2.2)

f_{\rm diff}
introduces astrophysical effects, quantifying the fraction of diffuse baryons in both the intergalactic medium and halos, and can in principle depend on redshift. Following, observational studies that have constrained
f_{\rm diff}\sim 0.84
with no evidence of redshift dependence
[
19
]
, we take it here as a constant and take it out of the integral.
\chi(z)
represents the fraction of ionized electrons over the baryons in the Universe. Its components correspond to the mass fraction of hydrogen
H,\ Y_{\rm H}
or helium
\textrm{He},Y_{\rm He}
and to the ionization fraction of each element. For low redshifts
(z<3)
, the Universe is ionized, and
\chi(z)
tends to a constant value
\chi(z)=\chi_{7/8}\sim 7/8

[
11
,
8
]
. As a result
{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}
becomes

\displaystyle\langle{{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}}\rangle(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{3cH_{0}\Omega_{b}f_{\rm diff}}{8\pi Gm_{p}}\chi_{7/8}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+z)}{\sqrt{\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+(1-\Omega_{m})}}d{z}

\displaystyle={\rm DM}_{\rm diff}^{c}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+z)}{\sqrt{\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+(1-\Omega_{m})}}d{z},

(2.3)

where in
{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}^{c}
we have collected all the cosmological and astrophysical parameters that do not affect the
z
integral.

{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}
- Flat
\Lambda
CDM

We obtain our approximation, following closely the equivalent derivation of
[
2

for the luminosity distance in flat
\Lambda

\displaystyle{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}=

\displaystyle{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}^{c}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+z)dz}{\sqrt{\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}+(1-\Omega_{m})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}^{c}\int_{a}^{1}\frac{da}{\sqrt{\Omega_{m}a^{3}+(1-\Omega_{m})a^{6}}},\quad\textrm{with}\quad a=1/(1+z).

(3.1)

We define a new function
F(a)

F(a)=\int_{0}^{a}\sqrt{\frac{\Omega_{m}}{\Omega_{m}a^{3}+(1-\Omega_{m})a^{6}}}da.

(3.2)

Note that we have introduced an extra
\sqrt{\Omega_{m}}
term, which helps simplify the expansion below. This would re-write eq. (
3
) as

{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}=\frac{{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}^{c}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{m}}}\Big(F(1)-F(a)\Big).

(3.3)

For the next steps, we are going to expand
F(a)
with respect to
a
, where
a\rightarrow 0
(large
z
regime). This expansion results in

F(a)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\Bigg(-2-0.2x+0.068x^{2}-0.037x^{4}+\cdots\Bigg),

(3.4)

with

defined in eq. (
1.2
). We expand
F
until
\mathcal{O}(x^{6})
inclusive, and calculate the Padè approximant
[
6
]
to order
(N,M)=(3,3)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(x)}}\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(x)}}\frac{b_{0}+b_{1}x+b_{2}x^{2}+b_{3}x^{3}}{1.0+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+c_{3}x^{3}}.

(3.5)

The coefficients are

\displaystyle b_{0}=-2.0,

(3.6)

\displaystyle b_{1}=-2.85592665,

(3.7)

\displaystyle b_{2}=-1.0945641,

(3.8)

\displaystyle b_{3}=-0.0913347,

(3.9)

\displaystyle c_{1}=1.32796333,

(3.10)

\displaystyle c_{2}=0.44857662,

(3.11)

\displaystyle c_{3}=0.02769881.

(3.12)

Bringing everything together and returning to
(z,\Omega_{m})
notation, we have the analytical formula:

{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}=\frac{{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}^{c}}{\sqrt{\Omega_{m}}}\Bigg(\Phi\big[x(0,\Omega_{m})\big]-\sqrt{1+z}\cdot\Phi\big[x(z,\Omega_{m})\big]\Bigg).

(3.13)

6
Conclusions

In this short note, we have derived a Padé approximant for the diffuse
{\rm DM}
of FRBs, assuming a flat,
\Lambda
CDM or
w
CDM universe. We show that our approximation works well for a range of cosmological density parameters and redshifts. More specifically, for
0.01\leq z\leq 2
,
0.2\leq\Omega_{m}\leq 1.0
and
-3.0\leq w\leq-0.5
the relative error in
{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}
is always below
3.5\%
. The relative error decreases as one probes larger redshifts
z>0.01
, larger matter density
\Omega_{m}>0.2
and more negative DE EoS
w<-0.5
. Moreover, if one assumes the fiducial values for the pair
(\Omega_{m},w)\simeq(0.31,-1)
, in order to constrain astrophysical and cosmological parameters in the factor outside the redshift integral
{\rm DM}_{\rm diff}^{c}
, the relative error is below
0.5\%
for the whole redshift range we considered. Compared to direct numerical evaluation, we find that our solution is more than
10
times faster for
\Lambda
CDM and more than
2
times faster for
w
CDM. As a result, we consider it a useful tool for cosmological and astrophysical studies involving FRBs.

The subcritical finite-volume massive sine-Gordon model
Jaka Pelaič

Abstract
We present a construction of the finite-volume massive sine-Gordon model in the UV subcritical regime using a renormalization group method. The resulting measure has Gaussian tails, respects toroidal symmetries and is reflection-positive.

Proposition 1.1
.

The map
\mathrm{Z}[\cdot,\cdot]:(0,\varepsilon_{\bullet}]\times\mathrm{B}_{\bullet}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{C}

\mathrm{Z}[\cdot,\cdot]

[0,\varepsilon_{\bullet}]\times\mathrm{B}_{\bullet}

\zeta_{\bullet}

\zeta

We have elected to restrict just to real coupling to avoid more complications regarding the finer points of infinite-dimensional holomorphy.

2.1
Progressive integration

We explicate a method to iteratively compute the integral that defines
\mathrm{Z}[\varepsilon,\zeta]
, giving us the ability to analyze these Laplace transforms sufficiently accurately to obtain the stated results. In this section, we just sketch the basic ideas, deferring to later sections for precise justification.

\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}
admits a scale decomposition into
\Gamma_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;j}
with corresponding fields
\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;j}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Z}

\displaystyle[\varepsilon,\zeta](J)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J,J\rangle+E[\varepsilon,\zeta]}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\exp\!\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\zeta\big\lbbrac\!\cos\!\big(\sqrt{\hskip-1.16pt\beta\hskip 1.16pt}(\mathrm{S}_{L^{-\mathsf{N}-1}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;-\mathsf{N}-1}+\ldots+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-1}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;-1}+\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;0}+\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J)\big)\hskip-1.0pt\big\rbbrac_{\varepsilon}\!\bigg)\bigg].

(11)

This is legitimate because
\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}
has polynomial decay in momentum space, hence any smooth
J
is in its Cameron-Martin space. Now we define

\zeta_{\varepsilon;j}=L^{2j}e^{\frac{1}{2}\beta\sum_{k=j}^{0}\Gamma_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}(0)}\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j}}\zeta,\quad\text{i.e.}\quad\zeta_{\varepsilon;j+1}=L^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\beta\Gamma_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;j}(0)}\mathrm{S}_{L^{-1}}\zeta_{\varepsilon;j}.

(12)

The flow equation for

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{-\mathsf{N}-1}[\varepsilon,\zeta](\phi)

\displaystyle=\exp\!\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}_{-\mathsf{N}-1}^{2}}\zeta_{\varepsilon;-\mathsf{N}-1}\cos\!\big(\sqrt{\hskip-1.16pt\beta\hskip 1.16pt}\phi\big)\bigg),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{j+1}[\varepsilon,\zeta](\phi)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{Z}_{j}(\mathrm{S}_{L}\phi+\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;j})\big].

(13)

By induction, it is easily verified that

\mathrm{Z}[\varepsilon,\zeta](J)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J,J\rangle+E[\varepsilon,\zeta]}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathcal{Z}_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]\bigg(\sum_{k=j}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j}}\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J\bigg)\bigg].

\displaystyle V_{j}(X,\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{j}^{2}}\zeta_{\varepsilon;j}\cos\!\big(\sqrt{\hskip-1.16pt\beta\hskip 1.16pt}\phi\big).

Now we compute the RG map. That is, we want to compute

) and also the identity
\mathbb{E}\big[V_{j}(LX,\mathrm{S}_{L}+\Psi_{\hskip-1.0ptj})\big]=V_{j+1}(X,\phi)
, which is the reason for our choice of
\zeta_{j}
in the first place. We can define
\delta\mathcal{E}_{j}
to remove the last term and then we may choose
Q_{j}
to cancel some terms in
K_{j}
.

To identify these problematic terms, we first do a Fourier analysis in the zero mode of the field. That is, note that
K_{j}(X,\phi+2\pi\beta^{-1/2})=K_{j}(X,\phi)
(this is clearly preserved by the flow); then put

k_{j}^{(q)}(X,\phi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-iqx}K_{j}(X,\phi+\beta^{-1/2}x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(33)

We will see that the kinds of norms preserved by the flow in fact imply that
K_{j}(X)
is analytic on a strip (refer to Appendix
B.4.1
), so
k_{j}^{(q)}
are well-decaying in
|q|
and we also have

\displaystyle K_{j}(X,\phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}k_{j}^{(q)}(X,\phi),

(34)

\displaystyle k_{j}^{(q)}(X,\phi+x)

\displaystyle=e^{i\sqrt{\hskip-0.5pt\beta\hskip 0.5pt}qx}k_{j}^{(q)}(X,\phi)

(35)

with the second making sense even for complex
x
with
\mathrm{Im}(x)
not too large. In any case, the extraction
Q_{j}
is taken from the neutral (
q=0
) terms:

Q_{j}(X)=\mathbb{I}_{X\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{E}\big[k_{j}^{(0)}(X,\Psi_{\hskip-1.0ptj})\big].

(36)

Having thus retained control of the RG up to the last step, we are left with handling
\Gamma_{\hskip-1.0pt0}
which is slightly different but ultimately easier.

This concludes the basic exposition of the setup. In the next section we start working up to analytic estimates on the RG flow.

2.3
Analytic control

To study the RG flow, we wish to control

\|K_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-K_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}.

(37)

That is, we are comparing the fluctuation step where we allow the fluctuation covariance to change; this is important because we want to compare different flows to obtain convergence.

We will always assume
\dot{\varepsilon}\leqslant\varepsilon
with corresponding
\dot{\mathsf{N}}\geqslant\mathsf{N}
, and we will mostly deal with
j\geqslant\mathsf{-N}
. Since the case
j=-\mathsf{N}-1
has slightly worse estimates, we need to treat it separately in principle, but we have tried to make the presentation as seamless in this regard as we could manage; there are only some qualifications in the propositions below to deal with this border case at the specific parameter values with which it actually arises.

Since there will be a lot of estimates, we make some notational abbreviations. We write

\displaystyle V_{j}=V_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta],

\displaystyle\quad\dot{V}_{j}=V_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\dot{\zeta}],

\displaystyle K_{j}=K_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta],

\displaystyle\quad\dot{K}_{j}=K_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\dot{\zeta}],

(38)

and similarly for
J_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]
,
P^{X}_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]
, etc. In case a mixed argument appears, such as
K_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}]
, we will not use an abbreviation. Regarding norms, if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
belong to the same normed space, write

\|x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\|=\max_{i=1,\ldots,n}\|x_{i}\|.

(39)

Regarding the various parameters, we assume
\mathfrak{h}\in[\mathfrak{h}_{*},2\mathfrak{h}_{*}]
and
\kappa\in[\kappa_{*},2\kappa_{*}]
.

As a general rule of thumb, an estimate on a quantity such as
\|K_{j}-\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\ldots}
should reduce to an estimate of the form
\|K_{j}\|_{\ldots}
simply by taking
\dot{\zeta}=0,\dot{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon
, which kills
\dot{K}_{j}
and any possible dependence on
\varepsilon
. This is not quite correct because in the general difference estimates, the right-hand side will sometimes involve
2\mathfrak{h}
whereas the left involves only
\mathfrak{h}
, while this is not the case in the non-difference estimate, so speaking precisely, one has to derive the latter separately; but we will not write this out, since the derivation is always a simpler version of the difference estimate. As a sanity check, the reader can consider the next proposition and reason whether the difference estimates do reduce to their non-difference versions in this way.

Proposition 2.2
.

Take
-\mathsf{N}\leqslant j<j_{\bullet}
. The potential satisfies

\displaystyle\|V_{j}-\dot{V}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h};\langle X,\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{1}|X|\big(\|\zeta_{0;j}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+L^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}\big),

(40)

\displaystyle\|e^{V_{j}}-e^{\dot{V}_{j}}\|_{\mathfrak{h};\langle X,\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{1}2^{|X|}\big(\|\zeta_{0;j}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+L^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}\big).

(41)

Assuming furthermore that we have inductively established
K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\in\mathbb{B}^{j,1}_{2\mathfrak{h},2\kappa,A}
,

\displaystyle|Q_{j}(X)-\dot{Q}_{j}(X)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{1}\|K_{j}-\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa;\langle X\rangle}+L^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa;\langle X\rangle},

(42)

\displaystyle|\delta\mathcal{E}_{j}(B)-\delta\dot{\mathcal{E}}_{j}(B)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{1}\max_{B\subset X\in\mathcal{S}}\big(\|K_{j}-\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa;\langle X\rangle}+L^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa;\langle X\rangle}\big),

(43)

\displaystyle|J_{j}^{\mathfrak{B}\rightarrow X}-\dot{J}_{j}^{\mathfrak{B}\rightarrow X}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{A}^{|\mathcal{C}(X)|}A^{-2|X^{*}|}\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|^{|\mathcal{C}(X)|-1}_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\big(\|K_{j}-\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}+L^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\big),

(44)

\displaystyle\|P_{j}^{(\cdot)}(\cdot,\psi)\|_{\mathfrak{h};\langle X,\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{L}^{|X|}\big(\|\zeta_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\big)^{\!|X|},

(45)

\displaystyle\|P^{(\cdot)}_{j}(\cdot,\psi)-\dot{P}^{(\cdot)}_{j}(\cdot,\dot{\psi})\|_{\mathfrak{h};\langle X,\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{L}^{|X|}\big(\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\big)^{\!|X|-1}\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\Big(\|\zeta_{0;j}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j}-\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa;\langle(LX)^{*}\rangle}+

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0ptL^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\big(\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\big)+

\displaystyle\hskip 40.0pt\|\psi-\dot{\psi}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2}(LX)}\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}\Big),

(46)

\displaystyle\|R_{j}^{(\cdot)}(\cdot,\psi)\|_{\mathfrak{h};\langle X,\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{A}^{|\mathcal{C}(X)|}A^{-(1+\eta/3)|X|}G_{\kappa}(LX,\mathrm{S}_{L}\phi+\psi)\|K_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}^{|\mathcal{C}(X)|},

(47)

\displaystyle\|R_{j}^{(\cdot)}(\cdot,\psi)-\dot{R}_{j}^{(\cdot)}(\cdot,\dot{\psi})\|_{\mathfrak{h};\langle X,\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{A}^{|\mathcal{C}(X)|}A^{-(1+\eta/3)|X|}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\!G_{\kappa}(LX,\mathrm{S}_{L}\phi+(1-t)\psi+t\dot{\psi})\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip-50.0pt\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}^{|\mathcal{C}(X)|-1}\Big(\big(\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\|\zeta_{0;j}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j}-\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\big)+

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0pt\|K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\|_{2\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\big(L^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|+\|\psi-\dot{\psi}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2}(LX)}\big)\Big).

(48)

The bounds for
P^{(\cdot)}_{j}
also hold in the case
\varepsilon=\dot{\varepsilon}
,
\psi=\dot{\psi}
, and
j=-\mathsf{N}-1
.

Note that we have merely assumed
K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\in\mathbb{B}^{j,1}_{2\mathfrak{h},2\kappa,A}
, though we will in fact be able to get
K_{j},\dot{K}_{j}\in\mathbb{B}^{j,2-\delta}_{2\mathfrak{h},2\kappa,A}
. Since the proofs involved are somewhat lengthy, we move them to Appendix
D
.

Now we assemble all these estimates into a bound on
\|K_{j}-\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}
. We also move this proof into Appendix
D
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Take
-\mathsf{N}\leqslant j<j_{\bullet}
. Suppose
K_{j}\in\mathbb{B}^{j,1}_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}
. Then

\|K_{j+1}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{1}L^{2\sigma}\|K_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}+\mathcal{O}_{A}\|\zeta_{0;j}\|^{2}_{\wp}.

(49)

Now suppose
K_{j}\in\mathbb{B}^{j,1}_{2\mathfrak{h},2\kappa,A}
. Then

\displaystyle\|K_{j+1}

\displaystyle-\dot{K}_{j+1}\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{1}L^{2\sigma}\|K_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-K_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}],K_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\zeta]-K_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}+

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{A}\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}\big(\|\zeta_{0;j}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-K_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}],K_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\zeta]-K_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\big)+

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{A}L^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\big(\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta],K_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}],K_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\zeta],K_{j}[\dot{\varepsilon},\zeta]\|_{2\mathfrak{h},2\kappa,A}\big).

(50)

Both bounds still hold in the case
\varepsilon=\dot{\varepsilon}
and
j=-\mathsf{N}-1
.

Regarding the last claim, we just recall that
K_{-\mathsf{N}-1}=0
so that no integrability issues can arise. Anyway, note the nice structure of the second estimate: no term involves a difference of
K_{j}
with both
\varepsilon
and
\dot{\varepsilon}
appearing. This enables us to bootstrap the estimates, as we first assume that
\varepsilon=\dot{\varepsilon}
, get some bounds, and then reinsert the bounds into the same estimate but with
\varepsilon\neq\dot{\varepsilon}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose
L\gtrsim_{\sigma^{-1},\delta^{-1}}1
(with other constants selected accordingly). We have
K_{j}\in\mathbb{B}^{j,2-\delta}_{2\mathfrak{h}_{*},2\kappa_{*},A}
for all
j\geqslant-\mathsf{N}-1
, more precisely

\|K_{j}\|_{2\mathfrak{h}_{*},2\kappa_{*},A}\leqslant\|\zeta_{0;j}\|^{2-\delta}_{\wp},

(51)

and furthermore for
j\geqslant-\mathsf{N}
,

\|K_{j}-\dot{K}_{j}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*},\kappa_{*},A}\leqslant\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|^{1-\delta}_{\wp}\big(\|\zeta_{0;j}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}L^{-j}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\big).

(52)

Proof.

The first claim is verified inductively. For
j+1
,

\|{K_{j+1}}\|_{2\mathfrak{h}_{*},2\kappa_{*},A}\leqslant\big(\mathcal{O}_{1}L^{2\sigma}+\mathcal{O}_{A}\|\zeta_{0;j}\|^{\delta}_{\wp}\big)\|\zeta_{0;j}\|^{2-\delta}_{\wp,(LX)^{[4]}}\leqslant\|\zeta_{0,j+1}\|^{2-\delta}_{\wp}

(53)

as desired. For the second part, we first assume
\varepsilon=\dot{\varepsilon}
. Then

\displaystyle\|K_{j+1}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-

\displaystyle K_{j+1}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*},\kappa_{*},A}\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{1}L^{2\sigma}\|K_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-K_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*},\kappa_{*},A}+

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{A}\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}\big(\|\zeta_{0;j}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-K_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*},\kappa_{*},A}\big).

(54)

Inductively, in much the same way as above,

\|K_{j+1}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-K_{j+1}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*},\kappa_{*},A}\leqslant\|\zeta_{0,j+1},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j+1}\|^{1-\delta}_{\wp}\|\zeta_{0;j+1}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j+1}\|_{\wp}.

(55)

Using this, we can bootstrap the estimates and, essentially repeating the arguments, find the result.
∎

This already looks like a Lipschitz property. Finishing up is not too complicated because there are only finitely many RG steps remaining. The next section deals with those.

2.4
Remaining steps

Recall that

\mathrm{Z}(J)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J,J\rangle+E}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathcal{Z}_{j_{\bullet}}\!\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J\bigg)\bigg].

(56)

We define
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]=e^{-E[\varepsilon,\zeta]-\mathcal{E}_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]}\mathcal{Z}_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]
. Then

\mathrm{Z}(J)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J,J\rangle}\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\big(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J\big)\big]}{\mathbb{E}\big[{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\big(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}\big)\big]}.

(57)

The estimates we have should allow us to get the Lipschitz property, with the only catch being that we are dividing by a quantity that we also have to be able to bound away from
0
. This is not trivial because
K_{j}
may in general become negative, though of course their sum is still non-negative (even positive before taking the limit). But we first deal with the Gaussian prefactor and the numerator.

So, first compute

\displaystyle\big|e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J,J\rangle}-e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{C}_{\dot{\varepsilon}}J,J\rangle}\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\langle(\mathrm{C}_{\dot{\varepsilon}}-\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon})J,J\rangle|\int_{0}^{1}e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle((1-t)\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}+t\mathrm{C}_{\dot{\varepsilon}})J,J\rangle}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim_{1}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|e^{\mathcal{O}_{1}\|J\|_{*}^{2}}.

(58)

For the numerator,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}-\dot{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*};\langle\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{X\in\mathcal{P}_{j_{\bullet}}}\|e^{V_{j_{\bullet}}}-e^{\dot{V}_{j_{\bullet}}}\|_{{\mathfrak{h}_{*};\langle X^{c},\phi\rangle}}\prod_{Y\in\mathcal{C}(X)}\|K_{j_{\bullet}}\|_{{\mathfrak{h}_{*};\langle Y,\phi\rangle}}+

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\sum_{X\in\mathcal{P}_{j_{\bullet}}}\|e^{\dot{V}_{j_{\bullet}}}\|_{{\mathfrak{h}_{*};\langle X^{c},\phi\rangle}}\prod_{Y\in\mathcal{C}(X)}\|\dot{K}_{j_{\bullet}}-K_{j_{\bullet}}\|_{{\mathfrak{h}_{*};\langle Y,\phi\rangle}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{1}4^{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}}|}G_{\kappa_{*}}\!(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}},\phi)\big(\|\zeta-\dot{\zeta}\|_{\wp}+|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\big).

(59)

Then

\displaystyle\bigg\|

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}(\cdot)\bigg)-\dot{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\dot{\varepsilon};k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}(\cdot)\bigg)\bigg]\bigg\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*}/2;\langle\phi\rangle}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\|{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}-\dot{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*}/2;\big\langle\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}\phi\big\rangle}\Big]+

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\bigg\|\bigg\langle\mathrm{D}|_{\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}((1-t)\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+t\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\dot{\varepsilon};k})+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}(\cdot)}\dot{\mathcal{Z}}_{j_{\bullet}}^{\mathrm{red}},\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}(\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\dot{\varepsilon};k}-\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k})\bigg\rangle\bigg\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*}/2;\langle\phi\rangle}\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{1}\mathcal{O}_{1}^{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}}|}\big(\|\zeta-\dot{\zeta}\|_{\wp}+|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\big)\mathbb{E}\bigg[G_{\kappa_{*}}\!\bigg(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}},\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}\phi\bigg)\bigg]+

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\mathcal{O}_{1}^{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}}|}\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\dot{\varepsilon};k}-\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{k})}^{2}\big]^{1/2}\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 25.0pt\mathbb{E}\bigg[G_{\kappa_{*}/8}\!\bigg(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}},\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}\phi\bigg)\bigg]^{\!1/4}\mathbb{E}\bigg[G_{\kappa_{*}/8}\!\bigg(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}},\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\dot{\varepsilon};k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}\phi\bigg)\bigg]^{\!1/4}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{1}^{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}}|}G_{\kappa_{*}/8}(\mathbb{T}^{2},\phi)\big(\|\zeta-\dot{\zeta}\|_{\wp}+|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\big).

(60)

Finally, if we allow
\phi
to vary,

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}J\bigg)\bigg]-\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}\mathrm{C}_{\dot{\varepsilon}}J\bigg)\bigg]\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\bigg|\bigg\langle\mathrm{D}|_{((1-t)\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon}+\mathrm{C}_{\dot{\varepsilon}}t)J}\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}+\mathrm{S}_{L^{-j_{\bullet}}}(\cdot)\bigg)\bigg],(\mathrm{C}_{\dot{\varepsilon}}-\mathrm{C}_{\varepsilon})J\bigg\rangle\bigg|\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{1}^{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}}|}|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|e^{\mathcal{O}_{1}\|J\|^{2}_{*}}.

(61)

Suppose now that

m_{\bullet}\coloneqq\inf_{\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon_{\bullet},\zeta\in\mathrm{B}_{\bullet}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}\bigg)\bigg]>0.

(62)

This is the object of the next section. It then immediately follows that

\|\mathrm{Z}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-\mathrm{Z}[\dot{\varepsilon},\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{C}}\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{1}^{|\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j_{\bullet}}|}m_{\bullet}^{-2}\big(\|\zeta-\dot{\zeta}\|_{\wp}+|\varepsilon-\dot{\varepsilon}|\big),

(63)

which is exactly the Lipschitz property.

Let’s also consider here the small coupling case, i.e.
\zeta_{\bullet}=\zeta_{*}
, i.e.
j_{\bullet}=0
. Then

\mathcal{Z}_{0}^{\mathrm{red}}(\phi)=e^{V_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2},\phi)}+K_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2},\phi).

(64)

Doing a standard computation gives

\mathrm{Z}[0,\zeta](J)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{C}J,J\rangle+V(\mathrm{C}J)}\frac{1+K^{V}_{1}\!(\mathrm{C}J)}{1+K^{V}_{1}\!(0)}

(65)

where
V(\phi)=\int\zeta\big(1-\!\cos\!\big(\sqrt{\hskip-1.16pt\beta\hskip 1.16pt}\phi\big)\big)
and

\|K_{1}^{V}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{*},\kappa_{*}/2}\lesssim_{1}\|\zeta\|_{\wp}^{2-\delta}.

(66)

To show non-Gaussianity for small constant coupling, we can consider the connected truncated four-point function. This equals

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}^{\mathrm{c},\mathrm{t}}_{4}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{4}}{\partial t_{1}\partial t_{2}\partial t_{3}\partial t_{4}}\bigg|_{t_{1}=t_{2}=t_{3}=t_{4}=0}\big(V+\log(1+K^{V}_{1})\big)\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{4}t_{i}f_{i}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\beta^{2}\zeta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(f_{1}f_{2}f_{3}f_{4})(x)\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{part}(4)}\frac{(-1)^{|\pi|-1}}{(1+K^{V}_{1}\!(0))^{|\pi|}}\prod_{I\in\pi}\bigg\langle\mathrm{D}^{|I|}|_{0}K^{V}_{1},\bigotimes_{i\in I}f_{i}\bigg\rangle

(67)

where
\mathrm{part}(4)
denotes the partitions of
\lcurl 1,2,3,4\rcurl
into (nonempty) subsets. The term involving
K^{V}_{1}
is bounded by
\mathcal{O}_{1}\zeta^{2-\delta}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\|f_{i}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}
, so if we just take
f_{1}=f_{2}=f_{3}=f_{4}=1
,

|\mathfrak{S}^{\mathrm{c},\mathrm{t}}_{4}(1,1,1,1)|\geqslant\beta^{2}|\zeta|-\mathcal{O}_{1}|\zeta|^{2-\delta}>0.

(68)

3
The lover bound

Showing
m_{\bullet}>0
requires some special theory, which we have sequestered in this section. Suppose we manage to find a neighborhood
U_{\bullet}
of
0
in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{j_{\bullet}}^{2})
such that

{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}|_{U_{\bullet}}\geqslant z_{\bullet}>0.

(69)

It will then follow that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}\bigg)\bigg]\geqslant\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big(\mathbb{I}_{U_{\bullet}}{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mathrm{red}}_{j_{\bullet}}\big)\hskip-1.0pt\bigg(\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}\bigg)\bigg]\geqslant z_{\bullet}\,\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigg\lcurl\sum_{k=j_{\bullet}}^{0}\mathrm{S}_{L^{k-j_{\bullet}}}\Psi_{\hskip-1.0pt\varepsilon;k}\in U_{\bullet}\bigg\rcurl\bigg].

(70)

The probability appearing here is nonzero because a Gaussian measure assigns nonzero weight to every neighborhood of the origin, so
m_{\bullet}>0
. Hence we only need to find
U_{\bullet}
, which will of course be some kind of norm ball.

The idea is to apply polymer systems theory (cf. e.g.
[
GJ87
]
, Chapter 20) to exponentiate the polymer representation. That is, if we define

K_{j}^{V}\!(X,\phi)=e^{-V_{j}(X,\phi)}K_{j}(X,\phi),

(71)

then

\mathcal{Z}_{j}^{\mathrm{red}}(\phi)=\exp\!\bigg(V_{j}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{j},\phi)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\mathbf{C}_{n;j}}\mathfrak{n}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})K^{V}_{j}\!(X_{1},\phi)\ldots K^{V}_{j}\!(X_{n},\phi)\bigg).

(72)

\mathbf{C}_{n;j}
denotes the class of
n
-tuples
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{c};j}
for which
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is connected, i.e. the graph on

A general insight of the Wilsonian RG is that the effective potential stays roughly local. B.4.1
Complexification of functionals

In one proof, we will need a
“complexification”
map to get contractive estimates on charged terms. The whole development of this section is rather elementary, comprising mostly Taylor expansions and basic multilinear algebra.

For a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
, consider the strip
\mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{h}}=\big\lcurl z\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{C}}:\|\mathrm{Im}(z)\|_{\mathcal{X}}<\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{h}\big\rcurl
. If

, we define

for

as follows: if

with

and

with
\|z\|_{\mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{C}}}=\max\lcurl\|\mathrm{Re}(z)\|_{\mathcal{X}},\|\mathrm{Im}(z)\|_{\mathcal{X}}\rcurl<\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{h}
, put

{}^{\mathbb{C\hskip-1.0pt}}F(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\langle\mathrm{D}^{n}|_{x}F,z,\ldots,z\rangle

(127)

where
\mathrm{D}^{n}|_{x}F
is of course extended to complex arguments by multilinearity. The series converges: if

are the real and imaginary parts,

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathrm{D}^{n}|_{x}F,z,\ldots,z\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\lcurl 0,1\rcurl^{n}}\big|\langle\mathrm{D}^{n}|_{x}F,z_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,z_{\sigma_{n}}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\in\lcurl 0,1\rcurl^{n}}\|F\|_{n;\langle X\rangle}\|z_{\sigma_{1}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\ldots\|z_{\sigma_{n}}\|_{\mathcal{X}}.

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathfrak{h}^{n}\|F\|_{n;\langle X\rangle}.

(128)

We still need to check that this is independent of the decomposition of
w
, so suppose
w=x+z=x^{\prime}+z^{\prime}
. Then
\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{X}}<\mathfrak{h}
and by Taylor expansion,

\langle\mathrm{D}^{n}|_{x}F,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\rangle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\langle\mathrm{D}^{n+m}|_{x^{\prime}}F,x-x^{\prime},\ldots,x-x^{\prime},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\rangle.

(129)

Hence (the series converge absolutely)

{}^{\mathbb{C\hskip-1.0pt}}F(x+z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!m!}\langle\mathrm{D}^{n+m}|_{x^{\prime}}F,x-x^{\prime},\ldots,x-x^{\prime},z,\ldots,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{n=0}^{k}\binom{k}{n}\langle\mathrm{D}^{k}|_{x^{\prime}}F,x-x^{\prime},\ldots,x-x^{\prime},z,\ldots,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\langle\mathrm{D}^{k}|_{x^{\prime}}F,x-x^{\prime}+z,\ldots,x-x^{\prime}+z\rangle

\displaystyle={{}^{\mathbb{C\hskip-1.0pt}}F}(x^{\prime}+z^{\prime}).

(130)

This shows well-definedness.

Some further properties: if
w
is already real,
{}^{\mathbb{C\hskip-1.0pt}}F(w)=F(w)
.
{}^{\mathbb{C\hskip-1.0pt}}F
is also a smooth functional on
\mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{C},\mathfrak{h}}
: if

,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial t_{1}\ldots\partial t_{m}}\bigg|_{t_{1}=\ldots=t_{m}=0}

\displaystyle{{}^{\mathbb{C\hskip-1.0pt}}F}\bigg(w+\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}u_{i}\bigg)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial t_{1}\ldots\partial t_{m}}\bigg|_{t_{1}=\ldots=t_{m}=0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\bigg\langle\mathrm{D}^{n}|_{x}F,\psi+\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}u_{i},\ldots,\psi+\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}u_{i}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\langle\mathrm{D}^{n+m}|_{x}F,\psi,\ldots,\psi,u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\rangle

(131)

as we could have guessed from (
129

(132)

and so

(133)

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt(e^{V_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}](X-W,\phi)-\delta\mathcal{E}_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}](LW)}-1)\mathbb{E}\big[P_{j}^{Z}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}](\phi,\Psi_{\hskip-1.0ptj})(R^{Y_{1}}_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-R^{Y_{1}}_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}])(\phi,\Psi_{\hskip-1.0ptj})\big]J_{j}^{\mathfrak{B}\rightarrow Y_{2}}[\varepsilon,\zeta]+

Then taking norms,

\displaystyle\|\!\cdot\!\|_{\mathfrak{h};\langle X,\phi\rangle}\leqslant\mathcal{O}_{L}^{|X|}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{A}A^{-(1+\eta/4)|X|}G_{\kappa}(X,\phi)\cdot

\displaystyle\|\zeta_{0;j},\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}\big(\|\zeta_{0;j}-\dot{\zeta}_{0;j}\|_{\wp}+\|K_{j}[\varepsilon,\zeta]-K_{j}[\varepsilon,\dot{\zeta}]\|_{\mathfrak{h},\kappa,A}\big).

Summing gives the kind of term required for the first part.

For the second part, we proceed similarly, but now there is no need to isolate the linear terms. Hence we take the basic definition of

, write down an identity like (

), and find a bound by the quantity

(225)

much simpler computations than above verify this special case.

Appendix E
Laplace transforms

We are considering probability measures
\mu
on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for which the Laplace transform

\mathrm{Z}_{\mu}(f)=\int e^{\langle\phi,f\rangle}\mu(\mathrm{d}\phi)

(226)

belongs to
\mathfrak{C}
. In this case the complex Laplace transform

\mathrm{Z}_{\mu}^{\mathbb{C}}(f)=\int e^{\langle\phi,\mathrm{Re}(f)\rangle+i\langle\phi,\mathrm{Im}(f)\rangle}\mu(\mathrm{d}\phi)

(227)

is still Fréchet-analytic on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{C})
. We want to understand how uniform convergence of Laplace transforms as continuous functions gives us information about weak convergence of measures. As far as general notions of infinite-dimensional complex analysis go,
[
Din99
]
is a comprehensive reference.

Proposition E.1
.

Suppose
\mu_{n}
is a sequence of probability measures on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
\mathrm{Z}_{\mu_{n}}\in\mathfrak{C}
. Suppose further that
\mathrm{Z}_{\mu_{n}}
converge in norm to some functional
\mathrm{Z}
. Then
\mathrm{Z}
is the Laplace transform of a probability measure
\mu
with Laplace transform in
\mathfrak{C}
,
\mu_{n}\rightarrow\mu
weakly, and
\mathrm{Z}_{\mu}=\mathrm{Z}
.

Proof.

Consider some vectors
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r},g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
. Define

F_{n}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{2r})=\mathrm{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{n}}(z_{1}f_{1}+\ldots+z_{r}f_{r}+z_{r+1}g_{1}+\ldots+z_{2r}g_{r}).

(228)

These are holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{2r}
. On the real slice
\mathbb{R}^{2r}
, we know that
F_{n}
converge locally uniformly to some
G
defined by
\mathrm{Z}
. Now we want to show that every subsequence of
F_{n}
contains a subsubsequence which converges to some
F
(independent of the subsequence) and this will establish convergence.

Any subsequence
F_{n_{k}}
does contain a subsubsequence converging to some holomorphic
F
on
\mathbb{C}^{2r}
by compactness (since
F_{n}
are locally bounded and hence a normal family). Suppose that some other subsubsequence converges to an
F^{\prime}
which might be different from
F
. But
F
and
F^{\prime}
coincide on
\mathbb{R}^{2r}
and hence by the identity principle
F=F^{\prime}
.

With this established, it is immediate that
\mathrm{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{n}}
converge pointwise to some function
\mathrm{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}
bounded by
G_{2\kappa_{*}}
which extends
\mathrm{Z}
. It is also clear that it is Gâteaux-holomorphic:

\mathrm{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}(u+iv+z_{1}(f_{1}+ig_{1})+\ldots+(f_{r}+ig_{r}))=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(1,i,z_{1},\ldots,z_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}),

(229)

which is a uniform-on-compacts limit of restrictions of holomorphic functions to affine subspaces. Therefore the Gâteaux derivatives indeed exist. But a locally bounded Gâteaux-holomorphic function is Fréchet-holomorphic. In particular, this means that the characteristic functions
\mathrm{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu_{n}}(if)
converge pointwise to a function continuous at
x=0
, so by the Lévy-Fernique theorem for nuclear spaces (cf. e.g.
[
BDW17
]
, Theorem 5.1 and references),
\mu_{n}
converge weakly to a measure
\mu
with characteristic function
\mathrm{Z}^{\mathbb{C}}(if)
. Since this characteristic function has a holomorphic extension, Cauchy bounds lead to moment estimates which suffice to conclude that
\mu
indeed has a Laplace transform. Again by an identity principle,
\mathrm{Z}_{\mu}=\mathrm{Z}
. For completeness, we provide the details of this last step in the next proposition.
∎

Lemma E.2
.

Suppose the characteristic function
\Phi
of a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
has a Gâteaux-holomorphic extension to
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{C})
. Then this is the (rotated) Laplace transform of
\mu
.

Proof.

The main point is to establish moment bounds. Let
\mathrm{ev}_{\!f}
by the evaluation functional at
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
, which becomes a random variable under
\mu
. Its characteristic function
\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}(t)=\mu(e^{it\,\mathrm{ev}_{\!f}})
satisfies

\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}(t)=\Phi(tf).

(230)

Then
\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}
admits a holomorphic extension to
\mathbb{C}
. A well-known result (
[
Kle20
]
, Theorem 15.35) states that since
\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}
is smooth,

\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathrm{ev}_{\!f}|^{2n}\big]\leqslant 2n|\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}^{(2n)}(0)|.

(231)

For odd moments we use

\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathrm{ev}_{\!f}|^{2n+1}\big]\leqslant 1+\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathrm{ev}_{\!f}|^{2n+2}\big]\leqslant 1+(2n+2)|\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}^{(2n+2)}(0)|.

(232)

Integrating over a circle of radius
r
gives

\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}^{(n)}(0)=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=r}\frac{\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}(z)}{z^{n+1}}\mathrm{d}z,

(233)

that is,

|\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}^{(2n)}(0)|\leqslant(2n)!M_{f,r}r^{-2n}

(234)

where
M_{f,r}
is an upper bound on
\varphi_{\hskip-1.0ptf}
on
|z|\leqslant r
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathrm{ev}_{\!f}|^{2n}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 2n(2n)!M_{f,r}r^{-2n},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathrm{ev}_{\!f}|^{2n+1}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+(2n+2)(2n+2)!M_{f,r}r^{-2n-2}.

(235)

But we can take
r=2
and then the integral

\int e^{|\langle\phi,f\rangle|}\mu(\mathrm{d}\phi)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathrm{ev}_{\!f}|^{k}\big]\leqslant e+M_{f,2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)^{2}2^{-k}

(236)

converges. Thus
\mu
has a Laplace transform which has to be the given extension of
\Phi
by the identity principle (applied, as before, to
“finite-dimensional slices”
of
\Phi
).
∎

Finally, we state a result about the tails of the measure given a Gaussian bound on the Laplace transform. The result below can probably be formulated more abstractly, but we provide a direct proof avoiding heavy machinery.

Proposition E.3
.

Suppose
\mu
is a measure on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with Laplace transform bounded as

\mathrm{Z}_{\mu}(f)\leqslant c\,e^{\mathsf{p}(f)^{\mathfrak{q}}}

(237)

where
\mathsf{p}
is some continuous seminorm on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
c>0
, and
\mathfrak{q}\in(1,\infty)
with Hölder conjugate
\mathfrak{p}=\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}-1}
. Then there are an
\mathrm{L}^{\mathfrak{p}}
-based Sobolev norm
\|\!\cdot\!\|_{\natural}
and
\lambda>0
small such that

\mu\big(e^{\lambda\|\cdot\|^{\mathfrak{p}}_{\natural}}\big)<\infty.

(238)

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be the Fourier transform, which induces an isomorphism of the dual pairs
(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and
(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{i}}^{\vphantom{a}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d};\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{i}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}^{d};\mathbb{C}))
. The second couple are just function spaces on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with the natural polynomial growth conditions, where the subscript indicates that the functions in question satisfy
f(-x)=\overline{\hskip-1.0ptf(x)\hskip-1.0pt}\,
. The duality pairing is explicitly given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{\vphantom{a}}_{\mathrm{i}}\hskip-0.6pt(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{i}}\hskip-0.6pt(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\overline{\hskip-1.0ptf(x)\hskip-1.0pt}\,g(x).

(239)

A natural system of norms is given by (
p\in[1,\infty],s\in\mathbb{R}
)

\|f\|_{s,p}=\|(1+|\cdot|_{\infty})^{s}f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d};\mathbb{C})}.

(240)

If we consider the pushforward measure
\mu_{*}=\mathfrak{F}_{*}\mu
, we find

\mathrm{Z}_{\mu_{*}}\!(f)=\int e^{\langle f,\mathfrak{F}\phi\rangle}\mu(\mathrm{d}\phi)=\int e^{\langle\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\!f,\phi\rangle}\mu(\mathrm{d}\phi)=\mathrm{Z}_{\mu}(\mathfrak{F}^{-1}\hskip-1.0ptf).

(241)

Thus, there are
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
such that

\mathrm{Z}_{\mu_{*}}\!(f)\leqslant c\,e^{\alpha\|f\|_{s,\mathfrak{q}}^{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}}.

(242)

If we define

\delta_{x}^{\mathrm{Re}}=\frac{\delta_{x}+\delta_{-x}}{2},\quad\delta_{x}^{\mathrm{Im}}=\frac{\delta_{x}-\delta_{-x}}{2i},

(243)

then

|f(x)|^{p}\leqslant 2^{p-1}\big(|\langle\delta_{x}^{\mathrm{Re}},f\rangle|^{p}+|\langle\delta_{x}^{\mathrm{Im}},f\rangle|^{p}\big)

(244)

and we can use Hölder’s inequality to compute

\displaystyle\mu_{*}

\displaystyle\hskip-1.0pt\big(e^{\lambda\|\cdot\|_{-r,\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}}}\hskip-1.0pt\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\big(2^{\mathfrak{p}-1}\lambda\big)^{\!n}\sum_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\lcurl\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Im}\rcurl}(1+|x_{1}|_{\infty})^{-r\mathfrak{p}}\ldots(1+|x_{n}|_{\infty})^{-r\mathfrak{p}}\mu_{*}\hskip-1.0pt\big(|\langle\delta^{u_{1}}_{x_{1}},\cdot\rangle|^{\mathfrak{p}}\ldots|\langle\delta^{u_{n}}_{x_{n}},\cdot\rangle|^{\mathfrak{p}}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\bigg(2^{\mathfrak{p}-1}\lambda\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},u\in\lcurl\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Im}\rcurl}(1+|x|_{\infty})^{-r\mathfrak{p}}\mu_{*}\hskip-1.0pt\big(|\langle\delta^{u}_{x},\cdot\rangle|^{n\mathfrak{p}}\big)^{\!1/n}\bigg)^{\!n}.

(245)

Apply Markov’s inequality with
t\geqslant 0
arbitrary:

\displaystyle\mu_{*}\hskip-1.0pt\big(\lcurl|\langle\delta^{u}_{x},\cdot\rangle|\geqslant t\rcurl\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\inf_{y\in[0,\infty)}\frac{\mu_{*}\hskip-1.0pt\big(\!\cosh(y\hskip 1.0pt\langle\delta^{u}_{x},\cdot\rangle)\big)}{\cosh(yt)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\inf_{y\in[0,\infty)}e^{-yt}\big(\mathrm{Z}_{\mu_{*}}\!(t\delta^{u}_{x})+\mathrm{Z}_{\mu_{*}}\!(-t\delta^{u}_{x})\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2c\inf_{y\in[0,\infty)}e^{2^{1-\mathfrak{q}}\alpha y^{\mathfrak{q}}(1+|x|_{\infty})^{s\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{q}-yt}

\displaystyle=2c\,e^{-2\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\alpha^{-\mathfrak{p}/{\mathfrak{q}}}(1+|x|_{\infty})^{-s\mathfrak{p}}t^{\mathfrak{p}}},

(246)

and therefore if
n\geqslant 1
,

\mu_{*}\hskip-1.0pt\big(|\langle\delta^{u}_{x},\cdot\rangle|^{n\mathfrak{p}}\big)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{*}\hskip-1.0pt\big(\lcurl|\langle\delta^{u}_{x},\cdot\rangle|\geqslant t^{1/n\mathfrak{p}}\rcurl\big)\mathrm{d}t\leqslant 2c\,n!\big(2^{-1}\mathfrak{p}\alpha^{\mathfrak{p}/{\mathfrak{q}}}(1+|x|_{\infty})^{s\mathfrak{p}}\big)^{\!n}.

(247)

Using this, it follows that if
r>s+d/\mathfrak{p}
and
\lambda
small enough,

\displaystyle\mu_{*}\hskip-1.0pt\big(e^{\lambda\|\cdot\|_{-r,\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathfrak{p}}}\hskip-1.0pt\big)\leqslant c+2c\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(2^{d}d\,\mathfrak{p}\alpha^{\mathfrak{p}/{\mathfrak{q}}}\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-(r-s)\mathfrak{p}+d-1}\bigg)^{\!n}<\infty.

(248)

\|\!\cdot\!\|_{\natural}=\|\mathfrak{F}(\cdot)\|_{-r,\mathfrak{p}}
is the Sobolev norm we were after.
∎

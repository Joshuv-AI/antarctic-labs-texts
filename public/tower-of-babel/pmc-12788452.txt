Assessment of Patient Satisfaction and Masticatory Efficiency with Implant-Supported Overdentures Versus Conventional Dentures
Authors: Shilpi Joshi, Shashank Uniyal, Prachi Ramchandra Surwade, Pooja Arora, Deepali Neeraj, Vipin Arora
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Tooth loss significantly affects oral function and quality of life. While conventional complete dentures (CDs) have long been the standard treatment for edentulous patients, implant-supported overdentures (IODs) offer improved retention, stability, and function.Materials and Methods:A total of 40 completely edentulous patients aged between 50 and 75 years were enrolled in this comparative study. Participants were divided into two groups: Group A (n = 20) received conventional maxillary and mandibular dentures, while Group B (n = 20) received mandibular IODs retained with two implants and conventional maxillary dentures. Patient satisfaction was assessed using a standardized questionnaire comprising ten items related to comfort, stability, speech, and aesthetics using a 5-point Likert scale. Masticatory efficiency was evaluated using the sieving method with standardized food particles (peanuts) and measuring particle size distribution after chewing.Results:Patients in the IOD group (Group B) reported significantly higher satisfaction scores (mean = 45.2 ± 3.1) compared to the CD group (mean = 36.7 ± 4.5), P < 0.001. Masticatory efficiency was also significantly better in Group B, with a greater percentage of finely chewed particles (mean = 72.5% ±5.4%) compared to Group A (mean = 54.3% ±6.7%), P < 0.001. Improvements in stability, comfort during mastication, and confidence in social settings were notable in the IOD group.Conclusion:IODs provide superior patient satisfaction and masticatory efficiency compared to conventional CDs. They significantly enhance oral function and quality of life for edentulous individuals and should be considered a preferred treatment option when feasible.

INTRODUCTION

Edentulism remains a significant oral health concern worldwide, particularly among the aging population. It adversely affects essential functions such as mastication, speech, and aesthetics, thereby impairing the overall quality of life.[1] Traditionally, conventional complete dentures (CDs) have served as the primary rehabilitation modality for completely edentulous patients. Although they provide a cost-effective solution, many patients report issues such as poor retention, discomfort, and reduced functional efficiency, especially in the mandibular arch.[2]

The advent of dental implants has revolutionized the management of edentulism by improving denture stability and retention through implant-supported overdentures (IODs).[3] These overdentures, retained by two or more implants, particularly in the mandible, offer superior biomechanical support, enhancing chewing ability and patient confidence.[4] The McGill consensus recommends mandibular two-implant overdentures as the minimum standard of care for edentulous patients, reflecting their clinical superiority over conventional dentures.[5]

Several studies have shown that implant-retained prostheses significantly enhance masticatory performance and patient satisfaction compared to conventional dentures.[67] Masticatory efficiency can be quantitatively assessed using various methods, such as sieving or colorimetric analysis, to evaluate the particle breakdown after chewing. Additionally, patient-reported outcome measures, including questionnaires and visual analog scales, provide valuable insights into subjective satisfaction with the prosthesis.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This comparative observational study was conducted among 40 completely edentulous patients aged 50–75 years who reported to the Department of Prosthodontics. Patients were randomly assigned into two groups: Group A (n = 20) received conventional maxillary and mandibular complete dentures, while Group B (n = 20) received conventional maxillary dentures and mandibular implant-supported overdentures retained by two endosseous implants placed in the interforaminal region. All implant placements and prosthodontic procedures were carried out following standardized protocols under the supervision of experienced clinicians.

Masticatory efficiency was evaluated using the peanut particle size analysis method. Each participant was asked to chew a standardized quantity of roasted peanuts for a fixed number of strokes (20 strokes), after which the chewed material was collected, dried, and passed through a series of sieves with different mesh sizes. The weight percentage of the chewed particles retained on each sieve was recorded, and a finer particle distribution indicated better masticatory efficiency. The entire procedure was performed in a controlled setting to ensure uniformity across both groups.

Patient satisfaction was assessed one month after denture delivery using a validated questionnaire consisting of ten parameters, including retention, comfort, speech, aesthetics, chewing ability, and overall satisfaction. Responses were recorded using a 5-point Likert scale (1 = very dissatisfied to 5 = very satisfied), and the total score for each participant was computed. The data collected were statistically analyzed using SPSS version 25.0.

RESULTS

A total of 40 participants were included in the study, equally distributed into Group A (conventional complete dentures) and Group B (implant-supported overdentures). The mean age of participants was 63.5 ± 6.2 years, with an equal gender distribution. At the one-month follow-up, all participants completed the satisfaction questionnaire and underwent the masticatory efficiency test.

The mean patient satisfaction score in Group A was 36.7 ± 4.8, whereas Group B reported significantly higher satisfaction with a mean score of 45.2 ± 3.4 (P < 0.001). Parameters such as stability, comfort during chewing, and ease of speech showed marked improvement in the IOD group [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Higher satisfaction scores observed among IOD users can be attributed to improved prosthesis stability, especially in the mandibular arch, which often presents challenges with retention in conventional dentures. This is in line with previous studies demonstrating that patients wearing mandibular overdentures retained by two implants report better speech, chewing comfort, and confidence during social interactions.[34] The support provided by osseointegrated implants mitigates denture mobility and displacement during functional movements, enhancing prosthesis retention and psychological acceptance.[5]

Masticatory performance was also markedly improved in the IOD group. The sieving method employed in this study revealed a significantly higher percentage of smaller food particles in Group B, indicating better grinding ability. This aligns with reports by Emami et al. and Kapur et al., who found that IODs restored functional masticatory levels closer to natural dentition than conventional options.[67] Furthermore, the ability to exert greater bite force without fear of denture dislodgement plays a vital role in the selection of harder or fibrous food items, which in turn supports better nutrition and general health in edentulous individuals.[4]

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, this study supports the clinical superiority of mandibular IODs over conventional dentures in improving patient satisfaction and masticatory efficiency. These findings highlight the importance of offering implant-based alternatives to suitable edentulous patients for enhanced functional and psychological outcomes.

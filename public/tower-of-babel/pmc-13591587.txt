A Bovine Dermal Collagen Matrix with Meshed Autograft and Skin Cell Suspension for Early Tendon-Exposed Wound Closure: A Case Study
Authors: Vania Arboleda Mocarro, James Gatherwright
Published: 2026

Objective: To demonstrate the feasibility of a novel bovine dermal collagen matrix (BDCM) in supporting early wound bed vascularization and definitive closure with meshed split-thickness skin grafting (mSTSG) and skin cell suspension autografting (SCSA) following oncologic excision.

Methods: This case study involved a 70-year-old female who had undergone wide local excision of left posterior calf squamous cell carcinoma and then was referred to plastic surgery for closure. Her past medical history was significant for congestive heart failure, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, obesity, and malnutrition. The cancer excision resulted in a full-thickness defect measuring 110 cm2 including exposed and partially desiccated Achilles tendon. After sharp excision of the wound bed and removal of necrotic tissue, BDCM was applied along with an impregnated gauze dressing and negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT). The patient was discharged home on post-operative day 0. The NPWT was kept in place for 7 days. On day 7 post-BDCM application, the patient returned to the operative setting for NPWT removal. Once a healthy wound bed was achieved, meshed split-thickness skin grafting (mSTSG) and skin cell suspension autografting (SCSA) was performed. Readiness for autografting, time to definitive closure, complications, and aesthetic outcomes were recorded.

Results: The BDCM had successfully integrated, and there was a well-vascularized wound bed with complete tendon coverage on post-BDCM application day 7. The patient was deemed ready for autografting and had a 3:1 mSTSG with SCSA on post-BDCM application day 7. The patient had 100% graft take at 1 week and was fully re-epithelialized at 2 weeks with repigmentation. She had complete return to baseline functioning and full active range of motion of the ankle. At the 2-month follow-up appointment, the meshed pattern of the mSTSG was no longer visible and no complications were reported.

Conclusion: This case demonstrates that BDCM application followed by mSTSG and SCSA post-oncologic excision may result in a timely definitive wound closure. By supporting robust vascular tissue formation over exposed tendon and timely reconstruction, BDCM may reduce morbidity from prolongation of open wounds. Additionally, BDCM may support functionality when applied near joint areas. These findings support consideration of BDCM for other full-thickness defects; however, further studies are warranted to validate these observations.

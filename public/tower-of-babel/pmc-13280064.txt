Exosomal 3’tiRNA-PheGAA and interleukin-6 as novel biomarkers for non-invasive detection of NSCLC
Authors: Ning Yuan, Qun Zhang, Jing Shi, WenJun Cheng, Bin Zhao, Zhijun Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide. However, the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of commonly used tumor markers, such as carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and cytokeratin-19 fragment (CYFRA21-1), remain limited. This study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic value of serum exosomal 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and interleukin-6 (IL-6), alone and in combination with conventional tumor markers, for the detection of NSCLC.MethodsPeripheral blood samples were collected from 110 patients with NSCLC and healthy controls. Serum exosomes were isolated, and the expression of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA was measured using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Serum levels of CEA, CYFRA21-1, and IL-6 were determined by electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (ECLIA). The diagnostic performance of individual and combined biomarkers was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.ResultsSerum levels of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, IL-6, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 were significantly higher in NSCLC patients than in healthy controls (P < 0.0001). ROC analysis showed that the area under the curve (AUC) values for 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 were 0.680 and 0.898, respectively. The combination of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 increased the AUC to 0.926, while the four-marker panel (3′tiRNA-PheGAA, IL-6, CEA, and CYFRA21-1) achieved the highest diagnostic performance with an AUC of 0.971.ConclusionsSerum exosomal 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 may serve as promising non-invasive biomarkers for NSCLC diagnosis, and their combination with conventional tumor markers significantly improves diagnostic accuracy.Keywords: tiRNA, IL-6, CEA, CYFRA21-1, ROC, Biomarker

Introduction

Lung cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignant tumors worldwide and continues to be the leading cause of cancer-related mortality. According to the latest Global Cancer Statistics 2022, approximately 2.48 million new cases of tracheal, bronchial, and lung cancers were diagnosed globally, with nearly 1.82 million related deaths reported in the same year [1, 2]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases [3]. Despite continuous advances in therapeutic strategies, the prognosis of NSCLC patients remains poor due to the high proportion of cases diagnosed at advanced stages, resulting in unsatisfactory five-year survival rates [4]. Currently, early diagnosis of NSCLC mainly relies on imaging techniques, tumor biomarker detection, and tissue biopsy. Traditional lung cancer biomarkers, including carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and cytokeratin 19 fragment (CYFRA21-1),have limited sensitivity and specificity for NSCLC detection [5]. Although tissue biopsy is considered the gold standard for cancer diagnosis, it is invasive and subject to sampling limitations, which restrict its widespread clinical application [6]. Therefore, identifying highly sensitive and specific biomarkers for early NSCLC detection remains a critical clinical need.

Interleukin-6 (IL-6) is a pleiotropic pro-inflammatory cytokine that plays an important role in cancer progression by activating multiple signaling pathways involved in tumor cell proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, and immune evasion [7]. In NSCLC, IL-6 signaling has been shown to drive KRAS-mediated lung tumorigenesis through persistent activation of the STAT3 pathway [8]. Furthermore, IL-6 signaling can interact with other growth factor pathways, such as the insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) axis, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and survival through activation of downstream AKT and ERK signaling pathways [9].

Exosomes are nanoscale lipid bilayer vesicles with diameters ranging from approximately 30 to 150 nm. These vesicles are secreted by various cell types and facilitate intercellular communication by transferring proteins, lipids, and RNA molecules between cells [10]. Due to their high biological stability, wide distribution in body fluids, and ability to protect nucleic acids from degradation, exosomes have emerged as promising sources of circulating biomarkers for early cancer detection, disease monitoring, and therapeutic guidance [11, 12]. Among the various RNA species contained in exosomes, tRNA-derived small RNAs have recently attracted increasing attention. tRNA-derived stress-induced RNAs (tiRNAs) are a class of small non-coding RNAs generated by specific cleavage of mature tRNAs under cellular stress conditions. Accumulating evidence indicates that tRNA-derived fragments (tRFs) are dysregulated in multiple malignancies and are involved in tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis [13–15]. In the context of lung cancer, Zheng et al. identified five exosomal tRFs that were significantly downregulated in plasma exosomes of NSCLC patients [16]. Wu et al. reported that tRF-20-M0NK5Y93 expression was markedly reduced in NSCLC tissues [17]. In addition, Peng et al. demonstrated that the combination of serum exosomal i-tRF-AspGTC and tRF-1-SerCGA with conventional tumor markers (CEA and CYFRA21-1) significantly improved the diagnostic accuracy for NSCLC and early-stage lung cancer [18]. Furthermore, Wu et al. found that tRF-Leu-CAG was significantly upregulated in NSCLC tissues, and its elevated expression promoted NSCLC cell proliferation and migration, accelerated cell cycle progression, and inhibited apoptosis [19]. Although several studies have identified dysregulated tRFs in NSCLC, the molecular mechanisms underlying their involvement in tumor progression remain largely unclear. Moreover, many existing studies are limited by relatively small sample sizes and lack large-scale clinical validation [16, 18].Notably, to date, no study has systematically evaluated the combined diagnostic value of exosomal 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, IL-6, and conventional tumor markers (CEA and CYFRA21-1) in NSCLC. Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate the diagnostic potential of serum exosomal 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6, either alone or in combination with traditional tumor markers (CEA and CYFRA21-1), for the detection of NSCLC. By elucidating the clinical relevance and diagnostic performance of these biomarkers, we sought to establish a novel multi-marker panel that may improve the accuracy of NSCLC detection.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

Between January and September 2021, serum samples were collected from 110 patients diagnosed with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and 110 healthy individuals at Tai’an Central Hospital Affiliated with Qingdao University. The diagnosis of NSCLC was confirmed by histopathological examination. Blood samples from NSCLC patients were obtained prior to any therapeutic intervention, including surgery, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy. Healthy controls were recruited from individuals undergoing routine physical examinations and had no history of malignancy or major systemic diseases.All serum samples were obtained from residual specimens remaining after routine clinical laboratory testing. Approximately 4mL of peripheral blood was collected from each participant using yellow-top coagulation tubes containing separation gel. After collection, the samples were allowed to clot and were then centrifuged at 1,000×g for 10 min at 4℃. The supernatant serum was carefully transferred into 1.5mL RNase-free EP tubes and stored at -80 ℃ until further analysis.

Exosome isolation

Extracellular vesicles were isolated from serum using an ultracentrifugation-based method. Frozen serum samples were thawed on ice and first centrifuged at 10,000×g for 30 min at 4℃ to remove cellular debris and large particles. The resulting supernatant was then subjected to ultracentrifugation at 100,000×g for 2 h using a 50.4 Tirotor (Beckman Coulter, USA) to pellet the exosomes.The obtained exosome pellet was washed once with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and subsequently resuspended. To extract RNA, 1mL of TRIzol™ reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA) was added to lyse the vesicles and release RNA molecules. The extracted RNA was finally dissolved in 10 µL of DEPC-treated RNase-free water.The RNA samples were immediately reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit (TaKaRa, Kusatsu, Japan) for subsequent analysis. All procedures were performed under RNase-free conditions to prevent RNA degradation.

tRF microarray analysis

Total RNA extracted from the samples was quantified using a NanoDrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA), and RNA integrity was assessed using an Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent Technologies, USA) or agarose gel electrophoresis. For each sample, 100 ng of total RNA was used for microarray analysis.The RNA samples were first treated with a dephosphorylation step to generate 3′-OH termini. Subsequently, the RNA was denatured with dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and enzymatically labeled with Cy3 fluorescent dye. The labeled RNA was then hybridized onto the Arraystar Human Small RNA Microarray (8 × 15 K, Arraystar, USA). After hybridization, the microarrays were scanned using an Agilent Scanner G2505C (Agilent Technologies, USA).Quantile normalization and data processing of extracellular vesicle RNA samples fromhealthy controls, non-metastatic NSCLC patients, metastatic NSCLC patients using GeneSpring GX v12.1 software package (Agilent Technologies).Following data normalization, probe signals flagged as Present (P) or Marginal (M) in at least three out of the nine samples were retained for subsequent analysis. When multiple probes corresponded to the same small RNA species (including miRNAs, pre-miRNAs, tsRNAs (tRFs/tiRNAs), and snoRNAs), their signals were merged into a single expression value.Differentially expressed small RNAs between groups were identified according to predefined fold change (FC) and P-value thresholds. The expression patterns of candidate tRFs/tiRNAs were visualized using hierarchical clustering heatmaps to illustrate differences among the study groups.

RNA extraction and RT-qPCR validation

The expression level of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA in serum exosomes was quantified using real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR). The small nuclear RNA U6 was used as the internal reference gene.The primer sequences for U6 were as follows: U6-F:5′-GGAACGATACAGAGAAGATTAGC-3′;U6-R:5′-TGGAACGCTTCACGAATTTGCG-3′.RT-qPCR reactions were performed in triplicate for each sample to ensure reproducibility. Relative expression levels were calculated using the ΔCt method.

Tumor biomarker recognition

Serum levels of carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and cytokeratin-19 fragment (CYFRA21-1) were measured using an electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (ECLIA) on the Roche Cobas e602 fully automated immunoassay analyzer (Roche Diagnostics, Switzerland).Quality control procedures were conducted daily in accordance with the Westgard multirule quality control system, including 12s, 13s, 22s, R4s, 41s, and 10X rules, to ensure analytical accuracy and precision.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.4 (GraphPad Software, USA) and SPSS version 23.0 (IBM, USA).The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to evaluate data normality. Normally distributed variables were compared using the unpaired Student’s t-test, whereas non-normally distributed data were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Diagnostic performance was evaluated by constructing receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to assess diagnostic accuracy. Comparisons between ROC curves were performed using DeLong’s test.Net reclassification improvement (NRI) analysisanalysis using R language.Continuous variables are presented as interquartile ranges (IQR). P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of serum exosomes

Serum-derived extracellular vesicles were successfully isolated from both NSCLC patients and healthy controls. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed that the isolated vesicles exhibited a typical cup-shaped or spherical morphology with diameters ranging from approximately 40 to 180 nm (Fig. 1A). Particle size analysis using qNano further confirmed that the majority of vesicles were distributed within the 50–150 nm range, which is consistent with the known size characteristics of exosomes (Fig. 1B). These results confirmed the successful isolation of serum exosomes for subsequent RNA analysis.Western blot analysis showed significant variations in EV surface protein expression compared to cell surface proteins, with CD9 and CD81/63 proteins being highly expressed on the EV surface (Fig. 1C). Identification of differentially expressed tsRNAs by microarray analysis

To identify candidate tsRNAs associated with NSCLC, tRF/tiRNA microarray analysis was performed using RNA extracted from serum-derived exosomes. After quantile normalization and probe filtering, differentially expressed tsRNAs between NSCLC patients and healthy controls were identified based on predefined fold change (FC) thresholds and statistical significance (P < 0.05).A total of eight tsRNAs were found to be significantly dysregulated between the two groups. Among them, three tsRNAs were upregulated, including tRF-1-TyrGTA, 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, and i-tRF-GluTTC, with fold changes of 1.5345, 1.5625, and 1.5463, respectively. In contrast, five tsRNAs were significantly downregulated, including 5′-tRF-HisGTG, 5′-tRF-LeuAAG, 5′-tRF-LeuCAG, i-tRF-GlyTCC, and i-tRF-LeuCAG, with fold changes ranging from 0.4586 to 0.6501.Among these candidates, 3′tiRNA-PheGAA exhibited significant upregulation in NSCLC samples (FC = 1.5625, P = 0.0427) and was therefore selected as a candidate biomarker for further validation (Table 1). To further visualize the expression patterns of these differentially expressed tsRNAs, hierarchical clustering analysis was performed (Fig. 2). The resulting heatmap demonstrated a clear separation between NSCLC patients and healthy controls based on tsRNA expression profiles, suggesting that these small RNAs may be closely associated with NSCLC development.

Diagnostic performance of individual biomarkers

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of individual biomarkers in distinguishing NSCLC patients from healthy controls. Among the biomarkers tested, 3′tiRNA-PheGAA demonstrated a moderate diagnostic ability, with an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.680 (95%CI0.610–0.749).In comparison, IL-6 exhibited a higher diagnostic performance, yielding an AUC of 0.898 (95% CI 0.852–0.944).Among conventional tumor markers, CEA showed an AUC of 0.831 (95%CI 0.778–0.885), while CYFRA21-1 demonstrated an AUC of 0.890 (95%CI0.843–0.937).Notably, combining 3′tiRNA-PheGAA with IL-6 significantly improved diagnostic performance, resulting in an AUC of 0.926 (95%CI 0.891–0.962) (Fig. 5a). Similarly, the combined detection of CEA and CYFRA21-1 achieved an AUC of 0.931 (95% CI 0.896–0.967) (Fig. 5b).

Diagnostic performance of combined biomarkers

To further explore the diagnostic potential of multi-marker combinations, different combinations of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, IL-6, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 were analyzed.When CEA was combined with 3′tiRNA-PheGAA or IL-6, the AUC values increased to 0.860 (95%CI0.812–0.908) and 0.944 (95%CI 0.911–0.978), respectively. The three-marker combination of CEA, 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, and IL-6 further improved the AUC to 0.953 (95%CI 0.925–0.981).Similarly, when CYFRA21-1 was combined with 3′tiRNA-PheGAA or IL-6, the AUC increased to 0.904 (95%CI 0.861–0.947) and 0.956 (95%CI 0.925–0.987), respectively. The three-marker combination including CYFRA21-1 further increased the AUC to 0.963 (95%CI 0.936–0.990).When 3′tiRNA-PheGAA was combined with both CEA and CYFRA21-1, the AUC reached 0.933 (95%CI 0.897–0.969), whereas the combination of IL-6 with CEA and CYFRA21-1 yielded an AUC of 0.968 (95% CI 0.942–0.994).Importantly, the four-marker combination of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, IL-6, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 achieved the highest diagnostic performance, with an AUC of 0.971 (95%CI 0.946–0.996), which was significantly superior to any single biomarker or partial combination (Fig. 6; Table 4).

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that serum exosomal 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 levels were significantly elevated in patients with NSCLC compared with healthy controls and exhibited promising diagnostic value. Increasing evidence indicates that specific tRNA-derived fragments (tRFs) can directly regulate NSCLC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. For instance, AS-tDR-007333 has been shown to promote malignant transformation through the HSPB1/MED29 and ELK4/MED29 signaling pathways [20]. In addition, pan-cancer 3′-tRFs are broadly upregulated in multiple tumor types and are elevated in patient plasma, where they promote lung cancer cell proliferation and metastasis both in vitro and in vivo [21]. Conversely, tRF-29-79 has been reported to be downregulated in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and suppresses LUAD cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [22]. Emerging evidence also suggests potential interactions between tRNA-derived fragments and inflammatory signaling pathways. In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) models, hypoxia-induced tDR-0009 and tDR-7336 participate in cellular responses to interleukin-6 (IL-6) stimulation, and tDR-0009 may regulate STAT3 phosphorylation [23], Activated STAT3 subsequently promotes the expression of IL-6 and other members of the IL-6 cytokine family [24]. Furthermore, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) within the tumor microenvironment can secrete IL-6, activating the JAK2/STAT3 pathway in both macrophages and surrounding tumor cells. This process forms an IL-6–STAT3–C/EBPβ positive feedback loop, which promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastasis [25]. Although the precise molecular mechanisms linking 3′tiRNA-PheGAA to IL-6 signaling remain unclear, the correlation observed between serum exosomal 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 levels in the present study suggests that this tiRNA may participate in inflammation-related signaling pathways involved in NSCLC progression.

Further analysis revealed distinct regulatory patterns for IL-6 and 3′tiRNA-PheGAA in NSCLC. IL-6 levels were significantly associated with clinical characteristics including age, sex, smoking status, and tumor TNM stage, which is consistent with findings from previous studies [26, 27]. Patients with advanced-stage NSCLC (TNM stage III–IV) exhibited significantly higher IL-6 levels, which may be attributed to activation of the JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathway [28] or upregulation of TIM-4 via the NF-κB pathway, thereby promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), tumor migration, invasion, and growth in NSCLC [29]. Elevated IL-6 levels observed in smokers may result from cigarette smoke-induced activation of PI3K/Akt and NF-κB signaling pathways [30] or from increased IL-6 expression mediated by the p38 MAPK-AP-1 and ROS-STAT3 pathways [31]. Consistent with these mechanisms, McEvoy et al. demonstrated that cigarette smoke extract (CSE) enhances IL-6 release from mast cells and epithelial/macrophage cells under inflammatory stimulation, accompanied by NF-κB activation and degradation of IκBα [32]. In contrast, 3′tiRNA-PheGAA showed no significant association with tumor stage or other clinical characteristics. This finding suggests that dysregulation of this tiRNA may occur during the early stages of tumorigenesis and remain relatively stable throughout disease progression. Such stability may make 3′tiRNA-PheGAA particularly suitable as a potential biomarker for early detection of NSCLC.

CEA and CYFRA21-1 are widely recognized tumor markers, and their elevated levels in cancer patients contribute to early detection, prognosis evaluation, and recurrence monitoring [33, 34]. In the present study, both CEA and CYFRA21-1 levels were significantly higher in NSCLC patients than in healthy controls, which is consistent with previous reports [35, 36]. However, the diagnostic performance of these conventional biomarkers alone remains limited.Increasing evidence suggests that multi-biomarker combinations can substantially improve diagnostic accuracy in various cancers. For example, Li et al. reported that the combination of 5′-tRF-GlyGCC, carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), and carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9 (CA19-9) increased the AUC to 0.926 in colorectal cancer diagnosis, significantly outperforming individual markers [35]. Similarly, another study demonstrated that combining tRF-18-HR05 × 6D2 with CEA, carbohydrate antigen 72 − 4 (CA72-4), and CA19-9 significantly improved the diagnostic performance for gastric cancer [37].

In the present study, we systematically evaluated the diagnostic performance of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, IL-6, CEA, and CYFRA21-1, both individually and in various combinations. Among the single markers, IL-6 demonstrated the best diagnostic performance for NSCLC (AUC = 0.898), outperforming 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, CEA, and CYFRA21-1. The combination of CEA and CYFRA21-1, as well as the combination of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6, both showed improved diagnostic performance compared with individual markers. Notably, the four-marker combination achieved the highest diagnostic accuracy, significantly surpassing the performance of any single biomarker or partial biomarker combination.To further evaluate the incremental diagnostic value of the novel biomarkers, net reclassification improvement (NRI) analysis was performed using risk stratification thresholds of 0.2 and 0.6. The results demonstrated that the addition of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 significantly improved patient risk classification compared with models based solely on traditional tumor markers, indicating that these biomarkers provide meaningful incremental diagnostic value for NSCLC detection.

It should be pointed out that this study has certain limitations.First, the single-center design and relatively small sample size may limit the generalizability of our findings, necessitating further validation in larger, multicenter cohorts. Second, the lack of longitudinal follow-up data precludes the assessment of these biomarkers in prognostic prediction and treatment monitoring. Finally, although a significant correlation between 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 was observed, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain to be fully elucidated through further functional and mechanistic investigations in NSCLC.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that serum exosomal 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 levels are significantly elevated in NSCLC patients. Among the individual biomarkers evaluated, IL-6 exhibited the highest diagnostic performance, while 3′tiRNA-PheGAA expression remained stable and unaffected by most clinical features. Compared to single biomarker detection, combined detection of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA, IL-6, CEA, and CYFRA21-1 significantly improved diagnostic accuracy. Furthermore, NRI analysis confirmed that the inclusion of 3′tiRNA-PheGAA and IL-6 provides significant incremental diagnostic value beyond traditional tumor markers.

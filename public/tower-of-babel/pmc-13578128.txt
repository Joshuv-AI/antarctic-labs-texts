Report on the 7th Ottawa International Conference on Neuromuscular Disease & Biology – September 11‐13, 2025, Ottawa, Canada
Authors: Jodi Warman-Chardon, Bernard J. Jasmin, Rashmi Kothary, Robin J. Parks
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The 7th Ottawa International Conference on Neuromuscular Disease and Biology was held on September 11-13, 2025, in Ottawa, Canada. The conference brought together international experts in neuromuscular disease, biology and clinical care to provide updates on exciting developments in the field. Topics included novel disease gene identification and characterization, developments in muscle, motor neuron and stem cell biology, characterization of disease pathogenesis and current standards of care, and recent advances in therapy development.

The 7th Ottawa International Conference on Neuromuscular Disease and Biology brought together more than 350 participants and 44 invited speakers spanning basic, translational, and clinical research in neuromuscular diseases (NMDs). A thorough synopsis of the conference can be found in the Supplemental Material. The keynote address (Charlotte Sumner MD, Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine, US) illustrated the discovery of TRPV4-associated Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT) disease type 2C from clinical characterization and mechanistic investigation to preclinical modelling and a phase I therapeutic trial is currently underway.

Emerging technologies were highlighted as tools to improve NMD diagnosis and clinical-trial readiness. Key advances in emerging technologies included recognition of biomarker and trial-design limitations in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), deep mutational scanning to interpret uncertain limb girdle muscular dystrophy SGCB variants, and artificial intelligence–based classification of muscle magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Qualitative and quantitative muscle MRI techniques were reviewed demonstrating utility for disease characterization (such as ANO-5- and RYR1-muscular dystrophies), biopsy selection, and outcome measurement.

In motor neuron disease research, clinical presentations emphasized impact of newborn screening, early treatment and emerging therapies in spinal muscular atrophy (SMA). Experimental studies identified Hspa8G470R as a potent SMA modifier and revealed shared biological pathways despite limited proteomic overlap across SMA subtypes. Genetic variability within the SMN locus was linked to heterogeneous treatment responses, and early motor-neuron gene-expression changes were characterized in a large-animal model of SMA. Persistent denervation and irreversible motor-neuron loss were identified as contributors to incomplete recovery following SMA treatment. Maternal transfer of SMA therapies was presented as a potential approach to prenatal treatment.

Sessions highlighting advances in ALS included SOD1 disease-modifying therapies, magnetic resonance–guided focused ultrasound to facilitate blood–brain barrier therapeutic delivery, and the use of digital health technologies and wearable devices as emerging outcome measures. Mechanistic studies in ALS identified TDP-43 SUMOylation as a potentially protective neuronal response and characterized a vulnerable population of disease-associated motor neurons. Additional findings implicated C9ORF72-related RNA toxicity, impaired autophagy, and differences in the oral microbiome between limb-onset and bulbar-onset ALS.

Advances in the management and diagnosis of neuropathies was discussed, including improved characterization of paraproteinemic neuropathies and machine-assisted diagnostic tools. Quantitative MRI emerged as a responsive outcome measure in CMT disease, while pharmacological, gene-editing, and viral gene-therapy approaches demonstrated the expanding CMT therapeutic pipeline.

The clinical, serological, and electrophysiological approaches to diagnosing neuromuscular junction disorders (NMJ), including myasthenia gravis (MG), was reviewed. Therapeutic advances included conventional immunotherapies, complement inhibitors, and neonatal Fc receptor antagonists for MG. In addition, improved molecular classification of congenital myasthenic syndromes (CMS), and the importance of emerging subtype-specific treatments was highlighted.

Several sessions were devoted to muscle disease discoveries and application to clinical practice. Identification of therapeutic pathways in dystrophin-deficient muscle stem cells as well as AAK1 inhibition as an approach to restoring dystrophin-deficient muscle stem cell function was reviewed. Single AAV-mediated adenine base editing also demonstrated a potential to restore dystrophin expression. Regulatory networks controlling muscle wasting and autophagy were described, alongside mitochondrial and redox pathways governing muscle stem cell function. Additional therapeutic approaches included the EP4 agonist irodanoprost, the spiny mouse as a model of scar-free tissue regeneration, and systemic CRISPR/SaCas9-mediated exon skipping in canine Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). Emerging treatments for dystrophinopathies in clinical practice were also highlighted, with emphasis on balancing therapeutic efficacy against safety concerns and serious side-effect profiles.

Additional sessions focused on advances in several muscular dystrophies. Basic and translational studies identified bioactive lipid class switching as a regulator of myogenesis and demonstrated that the tetraspanin CD82 supports muscle stem cell polarity and regeneration. Studies of muscle disease pathogenesis highlighted RNA mis-splicing in myotonic dystrophy type 1. Modifier therapy targeting IGHMBP2-related disease, extracellular-matrix regulation of muscle repair, CB1 receptor signalling in NMJ regeneration, and HuR dysregulation in muscular dystrophy illustrated the breadth of emerging therapeutic targets. Optimized muscle biopsy selection and the integration of histopathology, RNA sequencing, imaging, and advanced genetic technologies were emphasized as strategies to improve diagnostic yield. The genetic discovery and pathological heterogeneity of several NMDs were also discussed, including myofibrillar myopathies and the newly characterized TNNI1- and CIAO1-related NMD. Inclusion body myositis presentations addressed its phenotypic diversity and emerging disease mechanisms.

Several sessions proposed future directions in NMD research. Long-read sequencing, optical genome mapping, and other advanced genomic technologies were highlighted to improve rare-disease diagnosis, although variant interpretation remains challenging. In therapeutic development, early findings for the MuSK agonist antibody ARGX-119 in DOK7-associated CMS further demonstrated the movement toward molecularly targeted treatment.

Collectively, the meeting demonstrated substantial progress toward earlier molecular diagnosis, biomarker-guided clinical trials, and individualized therapies. In addition, challenges and opportunities of genetic and phenotypic heterogeneity, treatment safety, durability of response, and insensitive outcome measures remain important barriers in NMD therapies. Planning is underway for the 8th Ottawa International Conference on Neuromuscular Disease and Biology in October 2027.

Nevanlinna theory of the Hahn difference operators and its applications
Ling Wang

Abstract
. This paper establishes the version of Nevanlinna theory based on Hahn difference operator 𝒟 q , c ​ ( g ) = g ⁡ ( q ​ z + c ) − g ⁡ ( z ) ( q − 1 ) ​ z + c \mathcal{D}_{q,c}(g)=\frac{g(qz+c)-g(z)}{(q-1)z+c} for meromorphic function of zero order in the complex plane ℂ \mathbb{C} . We first establish the logarithmic derivative lemma and the second fundamental theorem for the Hahn difference operator. Furthermore, the deficiency relation, Picard’s theorem and the five-value theorem are extended to the setting of Hahn difference operators by applying the second fundamental theorem. Finally, we also consider the solutions of complex linear Hahn difference equations and Fermat type Hahn difference equations.

Introduction

In 1949, Hahn
[
15
]
introduced a new difference operator, which was defined as

(1.1)

\mathcal{D}_{q,c}g(z)=\frac{g(qz+c)-g(z)}{(q-1)z+c},

where
q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
,

is a complex constant, and the difference operator is called Hahn difference operator. This is the seminal paper by Hahn, which for the first time systematically investigated orthogonal polynomials satisfying q-difference equations, thereby laying the foundation for the entire field. Subsequently, Malinowska et al.
[
29
]
extended the application of Hahn differences to variational problems. Hamza
[
17
]
investigated Sturm–Liouville problems in the setting of Hahn differences, representing a significant step in extending the theory of differential equations to the discrete case. Hamza et al.
[
18
]
systematically extended the theory of Hahn difference equations from the scalar case to Banach algebras, establishing the existence and uniqueness of solutions, the definitions and properties of exponential and trigonometric functions, the Wronskian theory, and solution methods for non-homogeneous equations. Annaby et al.
[
1
]
investigated sampling theorems for Jackson-Norlund transforms associated with Hahn difference operators. This work enriches the application of sampling theory in discrete analysis and provides new mathematical tools for fields such as signal processing and numerical analysis. There is a wide range of research on Hahn difference operators, such as
[
20
,
31
,
12
]
. Recently, Zhang et al.
[
38
]
utilized Nevanlinna theory to investigate the value sharing problem between entire functions with Picard exceptional values and their Hahn difference operators, extending the results of the Brück conjecture to the context of Hahn differences. However, the Nevanlinna theory for Hahn difference operators has not been systematically studied.

It is worth noting that Hahn difference operator unifies q-Jackson difference operator and forward difference operator, which are defined by

(1.2)

\mathcal{D}_{q}g(z)=\frac{g(qz)-g(z)}{(q-1)z}

and

(1.3)

\Delta_{c}g(z)=\frac{g(z+c)-g(z)}{c},

respectively. Therefore, it is evident that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{c\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{D}_{q,c}g(z)=\mathcal{D}_{q}g(z);\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 1}\mathcal{D}_{q,c}g(z)=\Delta_{c}g(z);\lim\limits_{c\rightarrow 0,q\rightarrow 1}\mathcal{D}_{q,c}g(z)=g^{\prime}(z).

It is well-known that the Nevanlinna theory based on the classical derivative operator was established by Nevanlinna in the 1920s. It has played the key role in studying oscillation of complex differential equations. Later on, the Nevanlinna theory on some divided difference operators was investigated. For instance, the Nevanlinna theory for classical difference operator
\Delta_{c}(g)=g(z+c)-g(z),c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}

and Wen

considered the Nevanlinna theory of the classical q-difference operator
\Delta_{q}(g)=g(qz)-g(z),q\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}

[
8

established the Nevanlinna theory for q-Jackson difference operator. Motivated by the rich theoretical background and recent advances in Nevanlinna theory, we investigate Nevanlinna theory for the Hahn difference operator by employing techniques developed for both classical difference operators

2.
The logarithmic derivative lemma

2.1.
Preliminaries of classical Nevanlinna theory

Suppose that
g(z)
is a non-constant meromorphic function in
\mathbb{C}
, and its proximity function
m(r,g)
and counting function
N(r,g)
are defined as

\displaystyle m(r,g)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|g(re^{i\theta})|d\theta;

\displaystyle N(r,g)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{n(t,g)-n(0,g)}{t}dt+n(0,g)\log r,

where
\log^{+}x=\max\{0,\log x\}

denotes the number of poles of

in disc

with multiplicities. Then its characteristic function

is defined as

\displaystyle T(r,g)=m(r,g)+N(r,g),

which is a real-value function and is an increasing convex function of
\log r
. Besides,

and
\log^{+}M(r,g)
play identical roles if function

is entire. The order of

is defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\rho(g)=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log^{+}T(r,g)}{\log r}.

The first fundamental theorem is stated in the following: for any complex constant

,

\displaystyle T\left(r,\frac{1}{g-a}\right)=T(r,g)+O(1)

as

. In 1925, R. Nevanlinna proved the second fundamental theorem that for any
q
distinct values

,

\displaystyle(l-2)T(r,g)

\displaystyle<\sum_{i=1}^{l}N\left(r,\frac{1}{g-a_{i}}\right)-N_{1}(r)+S(r,g)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{g-a_{i}}\right)+S(r,g),

where
N_{1}(r)=2N(r,g)-N(r,g^{\prime})+N\left(r,\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\right)
and
S(r,g)
denotes the quantity of
S(r,g)=o(T(r,g))
possible outside a exceptional set in

with finite linear measure.

4.1.
Defect relation for Hahn difference operator

For any meromorphic function
g
and complex constant
a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
, we denote the Nevanlinna defect, ramification index and multiplicity index of
g
by
\delta(a,g),\Theta(a,g)

\displaystyle\delta(a,g)=1-\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N\left(r,\frac{1}{g-a}\right)}{T(r,g)};

\displaystyle\Theta(a,g)=1-\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{g-a}\right)}{T(r,g)}

and

\displaystyle\theta(a,g)=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N\left(r,\frac{1}{g-a}\right)-\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{g-a}\right)}{T(r,g)}.

It is obvious from the classical Nevanlinna’s second fundamental theorem that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}}(\delta(a,g)+\theta(a,g))\leq\sum\limits_{a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}}\Theta(a,g)\leq 2.

We aim to establish the Hahn difference counterpart of Nevanlinna defect relation, so we should first define the corresponding symbols. For any given meromorphic function
g
and any complex constant
a\in\mathbb{\{\infty\}}
. Denote the Hahn-type multiplicity index and the Hahn-type ramification index by
\theta_{q,c}(a,g)
and
\Theta_{q,c}(a,g)
, respectively. We define

\displaystyle\theta_{q,c}(a,g)=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N(r,g=a)-\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,g=a)}{T(r,g)};

\displaystyle\Theta_{q,c}(a,g)=1-\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,g=a)}{T(r,g)}.

Base on the Hahn difference analogue of second fundamental theorem, we prove the Hahn difference defect relation as below.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
g
is a non-constant meromorphic function with
\rho(g)=0
, let
0<|q|<1
. Then

\sum\limits_{a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}}(\delta(a,g)+\theta_{q,c}(a,g))\leq\sum\limits_{a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}}\Theta_{q,c}(a,g)\leq 2.

Proof.

By the Theorem
3.1
, dividing the characteristic function
T(r,g)
to the both sides of (
3.1
), then for any distinct value
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{l}\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
,

\displaystyle l-2\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\hat{N}(f=a_{i})}{T(r,g)}+\frac{o(T(r,g))}{T(r,g)}.

That is,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(1-\frac{\hat{N}(f=a_{i})}{T(r,g)}\right)\leq 2+\frac{o(T(r,g))}{T(r,g)}.

Hence, take liminf on the both sides of (
4.1
), then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}(\delta(a,g)+\theta_{q,c}(a,g))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\Theta_{q,c}(a,g)\leq 2.

∎

The value
a(\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\})
is called Hahn-Nevanlinna deficient value if
\Theta_{q,c}(r,g)>0
. We have a result from the Hahn difference defect relation in the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
g
is a non-constant meromorphic function with order zero. Then
g
has at most a countable number of Hahn-Nevanlinna deficient values.

4.2.
Picard theorem for Hahn difference operator

For any

(note that it is equivalent

. We also remark that

as below.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that

. Then

Proof.

Suppose that

g

\displaystyle T(r,g)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,g=a_{i})=o(T(r,g))

4.3.
Five-value theorem for Hahn difference operator

It is well-known that the celebrated
Five-Value Theorem
, obtained by R. Nevanlinna in 1929, states that two non-constant meromorphic functions

and
h
must be identical if they share five distinct values

ignoring multiplicity
. This work advanced the development of the uniqueness theory for meromorphic functions. Next, we aim to establish an analogue of Five-value theorem in the Hahn sense. Before that, we need to explain what is the meaning of two functions Hahn-share a value
a
.

For two meromorphic functions
g,h
are of zero order and
a\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. Denote by
E_{g}(a)
the set of zeros of
g(z)=a
, which is a subset of
\mathbb{C}
. If
E_{g}(a)=E_{h}(a)
except perhaps on the subset of
\mathbb{C}
such that

\hat{n}_{q,c}(r,g=a)-\hat{n}_{q,c}(r,h=a)=O(1),

then we say that
g,h
Hahn-share the value
a
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
g,h
are two non-constant meromorphic functions with zero order. If
g,h
Hahn-share five distinct values
a_{1},\ldots,a_{5}\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
, then
g\equiv h
.

Proof.

Assume that
g\not\equiv h
, we aim to get a contradiction. Applying the Hahn second fundamental theorem,

\displaystyle 3(T(r,g)+T(r,h))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{5}(\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,g=a_{i})+\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,h=a_{i}))

\displaystyle+o(T(r,g)+T(r,h))

holds for all
r\in E_{1}
, where set
E_{1}
is of logarithmic density one. Since
g,h
Hahn-share
a_{1},\ldots,a_{5}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,g=a)-\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,h=a)=o(T(r,g)+T(r,h)).

Hence,

(4.2)

\displaystyle T(r,g)+T(r,h)\leq\frac{2}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{5}\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,g=a_{i})+o(T(r,g)+T(r,h))

for all
r
in a set with logarithmic density one. At the same time, we can deduce from
g,h
Hahn-share
a_{1},\ldots,a_{5}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{5}\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,g=a_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\hat{N}_{q,c}(r,g-h=0)\leq T\left(r,\frac{1}{g-h}\right)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq T(r,g)+T(r,h)+O(1).

Combining (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}(T(r,g)+T(r,h))\leq o(T(r,g)+T(r,h)),

which is a contradiction. So
g\equiv h
.
∎

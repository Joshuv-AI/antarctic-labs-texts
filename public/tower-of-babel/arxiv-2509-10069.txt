Field-independent Kronecker-plethysm isomorphisms
Christian Ikenmeyer University of Warwick christian.ikenmeyer@warwick.ac.uk, Heidi Omar University of Warwick heidiomar.ho@gmail.com, Dimitrios Tsintsilidas University of Warwick Dimitrios.Tsintsilidas@warwick.ac.uk

Abstract
We construct an explicit field-independent SL 2 -equivariant isomorphism between an invariant space of tensors and a plethysm space. The existence of such an isomorphism was only known in characteristic 0, and only indirectly via character theory. Our isomorphism naturally extends the web of field-independent isomorphisms given by Hermite reciprocity, Hodge duality, and the Wronskian isomorphism. This is a characteristic free generalization of a classical situation in characteristic zero: certain rectangular Kronecker coefficients coincide with certain plethysm coefficients, and their non-negativiy proves the unimodality of the q q -binomial coefficient. We also give a short combinatorial field-independent proof that the Hermite reciprocity map over the standard basis is a triangular matrix with 1s on the main diagonal.

Representation theoretic decompositions and
q

A
partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
is a finite list of nonincreasing strictly positive natural numbers.
The number

is called the
length
of
\lambda
. For

, we write
\lambda_{k}=0
.
We write

.
We write

\lambda
is a partition with
|\lambda|=N
and

.
We write
\lambda\vdash_{n}

|\lambda|
.
We write
(\ell^{m})=(\ell,\ell,\ldots,\ell)\vdash\ell m
.
We write
\lambda\subseteq\mu
if
\forall i:\lambda_{i}\leq\mu_{i}
.
To every partition
\lambda
we associate its Young diagram, which is a top-left justified set of boxes,
\lambda_{i}
boxes in the
i
-th row.
For example, the Young diagram to
\lambda=(4,2,2,1)
is

.
We will always use gray boxes for Young diagrams in this paper, which makes it easier to draw the surrounding grid of potential boxes.
Let
\mathscr{P}_{k}(\ell,m):=\{\lambda\vdash k,\ \lambda\subseteq(m^{\ell})\}
, and let
p_{k}(\ell,m):=|\mathscr{P}_{k}(\ell,m)|
.
Note that
p_{k}(\ell,m)=p_{k}(m,\ell)
, which can be seen via transposing the partitions, i.e., reflecting the Young diagram at the main diagonal.
Let
\lambda^{T}
denote the partition to the transposed Young diagram of
\lambda
, for example
(4,2,2,1)^{T}=(4,3,1,1)
.

The irreducible polynomial representations

of the general linear group

are indexed by partitions

,
see for example
[

]
.
The plethysm coefficient

is the multiplicity of
S^{\nu}\mathbb{C}^{n}
in
\textup{Sym}^{m}\textup{Sym}^{\ell}\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which is independent of

, provided

.
Finding a nonnegative combinatorial interpretation for the plethysm coefficient is problem 9 in
[

]
.

For three partitions

\textsf{{k}}(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
is the multipliciy of

Finding a nonnegative combinatorial interpretation for the Kronecker coefficient is problem 10 in
[

.
The special case where

,

It equals the multiplicity of

in the
\textsf{{GL}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-representation

,

.
Although plethysm and Kronecker coefficients seem unrelated at first, several equalities, inequalities, and common constructions are known
[

,
Ike12
,
IP17
,

]
.
Both coefficients play an important role in geometric complexity theory, see for example

In this paper, we focus on the case

In this case, both coefficients coincide:

\textsf{{k}}((m^{\ell}),(m^{\ell}),(\ell m-k,k))=a_{(\ell m-k,k)}(\ell[m])=b_{k}(\ell,m),

For the plethysm coefficient, this result can be found in

For the Kronecker coefficient, this is proved in

]

Our isomorphism

is the first explicit isomorphism for (

Since

is symmetric in

and

, we have

\textsf{{k}}((m^{\ell}),(m^{\ell}),(\ell m-k,k))=\textsf{{k}}((\ell^{m}),(\ell^{m}),(\ell m-k,k))

Our isomorphism

gives an explicit isomorphism for this identity.

If

has more than 2 rows, then there are examples for which

and others for which

for example

Elements in the symmetric power

In this section we introduce an equivariant product of a polynomial with a symmetric tensor.
For a tensor

There is also an action of

(4)

on basis vectors, and extended bilinearly.

2.2 Proposition
.

(

2.3 Proof
.

Reordering the variables in the monomial

\displaystyle\beta_{\mathbf{i}}\big(h(t_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes t_{d})\big)

\displaystyle=

\mathrm{det}_{\ell,\mathbb{F}}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\textup{sgn}(\pi)\,x_{i,\pi(i)}\ \in\ \textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell})

denote the determinant polynomial.
Clearly
\mathrm{det}_{\ell,{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}}\in\textup{Sym}^{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell})^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}
.
Recall the inclusion
\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell})\subseteq\textup{Sym}^{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell})
.
Since all coefficients of
\mathrm{det}_{\ell,{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}}
are in
\mathbb{F}
,
\mathrm{det}_{\ell,{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}}\in\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell})

Therefore,

\mathrm{det}_{\ell,{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}}\in\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell})^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}.

(5)

We use the short notation
\mathrm{det}_{\ell}:=\mathrm{det}_{\ell,{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}}=\mathrm{det}_{\ell,\mathbb{F}}
.

3.1 Definition
.

For
0\leq k\leq\ell

we define
M_{\ell}(k)\in\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2})
via

M_{\ell}(k):=\mathrm{det}_{\ell}\boxtimes F(k).

1 Claim
.

M_{\ell}(k)\in(\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}
.

3.2 Proof
.

Let
G=\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
.
By (
5
) we have that
\mathrm{det}_{\ell}
is
G
-invariant.
Let
H=\{1\}
be the trivial subgroup of
\textsf{{GL}}_{2}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
.
For
(g,1)\in G\times H
, Proposition
2.4
implies

(g,1)M_{\ell}(k)=(g\mathrm{det}_{\ell})\boxtimes F(k)=\mathrm{det}_{\ell}\boxtimes F(k)=M_{\ell}(k)
,
because
\mathrm{det}_{\ell}
is
G
-invariant.

The next theorem is known over
\mathbb{C}
, for example via quiver representations, see
[
DW17
]
,
[
SW00
]
.

3.3 Theorem
.

The algebra

(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}

is generated by the set

\left\{M_{\ell}(k)\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\right\}
.

The rest of this section is dedicated to proving this theorem.
We interpret
\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}=\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell}\oplus\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell}
as a space of pairs of matrices.
Let
\mathbb{I}_{\ell}
denote the
\ell\times\ell
identity matrix, and let
\textsf{Diag}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell})
denote the
\ell\times\ell
diagonal matrix with
\mu_{i}
on the main diagonal.
We start by defining a homomorphism of graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebras:

\displaystyle\Phi:(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{F}[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell},\nu],

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mapsto p(\nu\mathbb{I}_{\ell},\textsf{Diag}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell})).

Let
e_{k}
denote the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in the variables
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell}
.
By definition, we have

\Phi(M_{\ell}(k))=\nu^{k}e_{\ell-k}.

(6)

We see that

\textup{$\{M_{\ell}(k)\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\}$ are algebraically independent},

(7)

because if
there exists a polynomial
P
with
P(e_{0}\nu^{\ell},\ldots,e_{\ell}\nu^{0})=0
, then there exists
P^{\prime}
with
P^{\prime}(\nu,e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell})=0
, but the
e_{k}
are algebraically independent (
[
Lan05
, Thm. 8.2]
), and
\nu
is a variable unused by the
e_{k}
.

2 Claim
.

\Phi
is injective.

3.4 Proof
.

The map
\Phi
depends on the field
\mathbb{F}
, so we write
\Phi_{\mathbb{F}}
to be precise.
Note that for every
p\in(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}\subseteq(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}

we have
\Phi_{\mathbb{F}}(p)=\Phi_{{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}}(p)
.
This implies
\ker(\Phi_{\mathbb{F}})\subseteq\ker(\Phi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
.
Let
p\in\ker(\Phi_{\mathbb{F}})\subseteq\ker(\Phi_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
.
We interpret
p
as a polynomial on
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}={\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell}\oplus{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell}
.
We study the evaluation
p(X,Y)
,
(X,Y)\in{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell}\oplus{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell}
.
As a first step, consider the case where
X
has full rank,
and let
\nu\in{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}
such that
\nu^{\ell}=\mathrm{det}(X)
. Note that
\nu\,X^{-1}\in\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
.
Consider the subcase where
\nu\,X^{-1}Y
has distinct eigenvalues in
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}
(it follows that
\nu\,X^{-1}Y
is diagonalizable over
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}
),
and let
A^{-1}\nu\,X^{-1}YA=\textsf{Diag}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell})
for
A\in\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
.
Let
\mathbb{I}=\mathbb{I}_{\ell}
.
Using the invariance of
p
, we see that

\displaystyle p(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\nu\,X^{-1}X,\nu\,X^{-1}Y)=p(\nu\,\mathbb{I},\nu\,X^{-1}Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(A^{-1}\nu\,\mathbb{I}\,A,A^{-1}\nu\,X^{-1}YA)=p(\nu\,\mathbb{I},\textsf{Diag}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p\in\ker(\Phi_{{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}})}}{{=}}0.

The full rank condition of
X
is a Zariski-open condition on
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}
.
The matrix
\nu X^{-1}Y
has distinct eigenvalues if and only if
\mathrm{adj}(X)Y
has distinct eigenvalues, where
\mathrm{adj}(X)
is the adjugate matrix of
X
, i.e., the transpose of the cofactor matrix.
But
\mathrm{adj}(X)Y
has distinct eigenvalues if and only if the characteristic polynomial of
\mathrm{adj}(X)Y
does not have a repeated root,
which happens if and only if the discriminant (the determinant of the Sylvester matrix of the polynomial and its derivative) of the characteristic polynomial of
\mathrm{adj}(X)Y
does not vanish.
This is also a Zariski-open condition on
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}
.
Hence,
p(X,Y)=0
for a Zariski-dense subset of
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}
, and thus
p=0
is the zero polynomial.

Since
\Phi
is a injective, we have the isomorphism
(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}\cong\mathsf{Im}(\Phi)
. The next goal is to determine
\mathsf{Im}(\Phi)
, see Claim
5
below. We start with some observations.

3 Claim
.

For every
p\in(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}
, we have that
d:=\deg(p)
is a multiple of
\ell
.

3.5 Proof
.

Define
q(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{\ell},\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell}):=p\big(\textsf{Diag}(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{\ell}),\textsf{Diag}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})\big)
.
For any
\alpha\in{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}
, let
A_{\alpha}:=\textsf{Diag}(\alpha,\alpha^{-1},1,\dots,1)\in\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
.
Due to the invariance of
p
, we have

\displaystyle q(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{\ell},\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})

\displaystyle=p\big(A_{\alpha}\textsf{Diag}(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{\ell}),A_{\alpha}\textsf{Diag}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})\big)

\displaystyle=p\big(\textsf{Diag}(\alpha\kappa_{1},\alpha^{-1}\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\dots,\kappa_{\ell}),\textsf{Diag}(\alpha\mu_{1},\alpha^{-1}\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\dots,\mu_{\ell})\big)

\displaystyle=q(\alpha\kappa_{1},\alpha^{-1}\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\dots,\kappa_{\ell},\alpha\mu_{1},\alpha^{-1}\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\dots,\mu_{\ell}).

By the equation above, since
{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}
is infinite, in every monomial
m
of
q
we have
\deg_{\kappa_{1}}(m)+\deg_{\mu_{1}}(m)=\deg_{\kappa_{2}}(m)+\deg_{\mu_{2}}(m)
.
Analogously, for all
j
, we have
\deg_{\kappa_{1}}(m)+\deg_{\mu_{1}}(m)=\deg_{\kappa_{j}}(m)+\deg_{\mu_{j}}(m)
.
Hence,

d={\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}}\big(\deg_{\kappa_{j}}(m)+\deg_{\mu_{j}}(m)\big)=\ell(\deg_{\kappa_{1}}(m)+\deg_{\mu_{1}}(m)).

4 Claim
.

\Phi(p)
is symmetric in the variables
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell}
.

3.6 Proof
.

For each transposition
\sigma=(i\,j)\in\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}
we have that the product
Q_{i,j}:=\textsf{Diag}(-1,1,1,\ldots,1)\cdot P_{(i\,j)}\in\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
, where
P_{(i\,j)}
is the permutation matrix of
(i\,j)
. Hence, due to the invariance of
p
we have

\displaystyle\Phi(p)(\nu,\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})

\displaystyle=p\big(\nu\,\mathbb{I},\mathsf{Diag}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})\big)=p\big(\nu\cdot Q_{i,j}\,\mathbb{I}\,Q_{i,j}^{-1},Q_{i,j}\textsf{Diag}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})Q_{i,j}^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=p(\nu\,\mathbb{I},\mathsf{Diag}(\mu_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\mu_{\sigma(\ell)}))=\Phi(p)(\nu,\mu_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\mu_{\sigma(\ell)}).

Combining Claim
3
and Claim
4
, it follows that

\textstyle\deg_{\mu_{i}}\leq\frac{d}{\ell}.

(8)

We are now ready to determine the image of
\Phi
.

5 Claim
.

\mathsf{Im}(\Phi)=\mathbb{F}\left[\nu^{k}e_{\ell-k}\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\right]
.

3.7 Proof
.

We have

\Phi(M_{\ell}(k))=\nu^{k}e_{\ell-k}
, see (
6
), so it remains to show that

\mathsf{Im}(\Phi)\subseteq\mathbb{F}\left[\nu^{k}e_{\ell-k}\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\right]
.
Since the group action of
\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})
preserves degrees, every invariant decomposes into a sum of homogeneous invariants.
Let
p\in(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}

be homogeneous of some degree
d
.
It remains to show that
\Phi(p)\in\mathbb{F}\left[\nu^{k}e_{\ell-k}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\right]
.
We collect powers of
\nu
in
\Phi(p)
and express the symmetric part (Claim
4
) as a polynomial in elementary symmetric polynomials:

\Phi(p)(\nu,\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})\ =\ \sum_{\delta=0}^{d}\nu^{\delta}\sum_{\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})}\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}\,e_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots e_{\ell}^{d_{\ell}}

(9)

for constants
\alpha_{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{F}
,
and the sum for every
\delta
is over

(d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\ell}
with

\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}d_{k}\cdot k=d-\delta,

(10)

since
\deg(e_{k})=k
.
Combining (
8
) with the fact that
\deg_{\mu_{i}}(e_{k})=1
, we also obtain

\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}d_{k}\leq\frac{d}{\ell}.

(11)

Therefore, for every
\delta
we have

\textstyle\delta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:1}}}{{=}}d-\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}d_{k}\cdot k\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:2}}}{{\geq}}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}d_{k}(\ell-k),

hence
\delta^{\prime}:=\delta-\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}d_{k}(\ell-k)\geq 0
.
Therefore we can can rewrite the monomial
\nu^{\delta}e_{1}^{d_{1}}e_{2}^{d_{2}}\cdots e_{\ell}^{d_{\ell}}

from (
9
)
as

\nu^{\delta^{\prime}}(\nu^{\ell-1}e_{1})^{d_{1}}(\nu^{\ell-2}e_{2})^{d_{2}}\cdots(\nu e_{\ell-1})^{d_{\ell-1}}(\nu^{0}e_{\ell})^{d_{\ell}}.

(12)

Since
d
is a multiple of
\ell
(Claim
3
),
we can also see from (
10
) that
\delta^{\prime}=d-\ell\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}d_{k}
, so
\delta^{\prime}
is a multiple of
\ell
,
hence
\nu^{\delta^{\prime}}=(\nu^{\ell}e_{0})^{d_{0}}
with
d_{0}=\frac{d}{\ell}-\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}d_{k}
.
Overall, the monomial in (
12
) is contained in
\mathbb{F}\left[\nu^{k}e_{\ell-k}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\right]
, which implies that
\Phi(p)
is also contained there, as desired.

3.8 Proof

(Proof of Theorem
3.3
)
.

Combining Claim
2
and Claim
5
, we have the isomorphism

(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}\ \cong\ \mathbb{F}\left[\nu^{k}e_{\ell-k}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\ell})\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\right],

and since the generators of the right-hand side ring are the images of the polynomials

, we conclude that

(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}\ =\ \mathbb{F}\left[M_{\ell}(k)\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\right].

Taking the homogeneous degree
m

, we get the following immediate corollary.

3.9 Corollary
.

The set

\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{m}M_{\ell}(\lambda_{i})\mid\lambda\subseteq(\ell^{m})\right\}

is a basis of the

\mathbb{F}
-vector space
(\textup{Sym}^{\ell m}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}
.
In particular,
\dim\big((\textup{Sym}^{\ell m}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}\big)=\binom{m+\ell}{\ell}

4
The Kronecker-Plethysm isomorphism

We now change the grading of the algebra

(\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}
.
We define the re-graded algebra

(\textup{Sym}^{\ell\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}[1/\ell]
via defining its homogeneous degree
m
components as

\left((\textup{Sym}^{\ell\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}[1/\ell]\right)_{m}=(\textup{Sym}^{\ell m}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}.

Note that
M_{\ell}(k)
is homogeneous of degree 1 in this algebra.

4.1 Definition
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
We define the homomorphism of graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebras

\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}:(\textup{Sym}^{\ell\bullet}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}[1/\ell]\ \longrightarrow\ \textup{Sym}^{\bullet}\textup{Sym}_{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{2})

via defining it on generators (see Theorem
3.3
) as

\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}(M_{\ell}(k)):=F(k)
.

(
7
) implies that this is well-defined.

4.2 Proposition
.

\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}
is an isormorphism of graded
\mathbb{F}
-algebras.

4.3 Proof
.

Since
\{F(k)\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\}
is a basis of the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
\textup{Sym}_{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{2})
, the
F(k)
generate the algebra
\textup{Sym}^{\bullet}\textup{Sym}_{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{2})
and are algebraically independent therein
[
Lan12
, Pro. 8.1]
.
The inverse
\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}^{-1}
of
\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}
is defined on these generators as
\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}^{-1}(F(k)):=M_{\ell}(k)
.

4.4 Theorem
.

Let
\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let the isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces

\textsf{{K}}_{m,\ell}:(\textup{Sym}^{\ell m}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \textup{Sym}^{m}\textup{Sym}_{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{2})

be defined as the restriction of the isomorphism
\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}
to
the degree
m
homogeneous components.
We have that
\textsf{{K}}_{m,\ell}
is an isomorphism of
\textsf{{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
-representations.

4.5 Proof
.

It suffices to show that
\textsf{{K}}_{m,\ell}
respects the group action of
\textsf{{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
.
For
\lambda\subseteq(\ell^{m})
we write

F(\lambda):=\prod_{i=1}^{m}F(\lambda_{i})
and

M(\lambda):=\prod_{i=1}^{m}M_{\ell}(\lambda_{i})
.
Since
\{F(k)\mid 0\leq k\leq\ell\}
is a basis of the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
\textup{Sym}_{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{2})
, we have that
\{F(\lambda)\mid\lambda\subseteq(\ell^{m})\}
is a basis of
\textup{Sym}^{m}\textup{Sym}_{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{2})
.
By Corollary
3.9
,

\{M(\lambda)\mid\lambda\subseteq(\ell^{m})\}
is a basis of
(\textup{Sym}^{\ell m}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}
.
Let
g\in\textsf{{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
. By Proposition
2.4
we have

gM_{\ell}(k)=g(\mathrm{det}_{\ell}\boxtimes F(k))=\mathrm{det}_{\ell}\boxtimes gF(k).

(13)

Let
c_{k,j}\in\mathbb{F}
with
g\cdot F(k)=\sum_{j}c_{k,j}F(j)
.

\displaystyle(\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}\circ g)(M_{\ell}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=(\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}\circ g)(\prod_{i}M_{\ell}(\lambda_{i}))=\prod_{i}(\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}\circ g)(M_{\ell}(\lambda_{i}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:JMlk}}}{{=}}\prod_{i}\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}(\mathrm{det}_{\ell}\boxtimes(g\cdot F(\lambda_{i}))

\displaystyle=\prod_{i}\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}\left(\mathrm{det}_{\ell}\boxtimes\sum_{j}c_{\lambda_{i},j}F(j)\right)=\prod_{i}\sum_{j}c_{\lambda_{i},j}\,\textsf{{K}}_{\ell}(M_{\ell}(j))=\prod_{i}\sum_{j}c_{\lambda_{i},j}F(j)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i}g\cdot F(\lambda_{i})=g\cdot F(\lambda)=(g\circ\textsf{{K}}_{\ell})(M_{\ell}(\lambda)).

4.6 Corollary
.

We also have the following isomorphisms of
\textsf{{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
-representations:

•

(\textup{Sym}_{\ell m}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}\cong\textup{Sym}_{m}\textup{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathbb{F}^{2})

•

(\textup{Sym}_{\ell m}(\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times\ell\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{\ell}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}\cong(\textup{Sym}^{\ell m}(\mathbb{F}^{m\times m\times 2}))^{\textsf{{SL}}_{m}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})\times\textsf{{SL}}_{m}({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}

4.7 Proof
.

The first isomorphism is obtained by taking the dual of the map
\textsf{{K}}_{m,\ell}
. The spaces in this isomorphism are the duals of the spaces in Theorem (
4.4
), as it is shown in Lemma (
1.1
).
The second isomorphism is obtained as the composition of isomorphisms
\mathsf{P}_{\ell,m}^{\ast}\circ\textsf{{R}}_{m,\ell}\circ\mathsf{P}_{m,\ell}
, as it is shown in Figure

.

5
Hermite Reciprocity, Wronskian, Hodge Isomorphism

In this section we discuss the other isomorphisms in Figure
1
over the standard basis.
We give another proof that the Hermite reciprocity map
\textsf{{R}}_{m,\ell}:=\textsf{{W}}_{\ell,m}^{\star}\circ\textsf{{D}}_{m,\ell}\circ\textsf{{W}}_{m,\ell}
is an isomorphism by combinatorially proving that its matrix is triangular over the standard basis.
Working over the standard basis enables us to consider the behavior of the “leading coefficients”.
The core argument of our proof is a combinatorial statement about partitions,
see Proposition
5.1
.

[
MW22
, Exa. 5.1]
give a finite example.

Recall that
\mathscr{P}_{k}(m,\ell)=\{\lambda\vdash k,\ \lambda\subseteq(\ell^{m})\}
.
We define
\mathscr{P}^{\prime}_{k}(m,\ell)=\{\lambda\vdash k,\ \lambda\subseteq(\ell^{m}),\ \textup{all $\lambda_{i}$ distinct for $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$}\}
. Partitions in
\mathscr{P}^{\prime}_{k}(m,\ell)
are called
regular partitions
.
We make the following definitions for
\lambda\in\{0,\ldots,\ell\}^{m}
.

\displaystyle F_{\otimes,\mathsf{s}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle x^{\lambda_{1}}y^{\ell-\lambda_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes x^{\lambda_{m}}y^{\ell-\lambda_{m}}\in{\textstyle\bigotimes}^{m}\textup{Sym}^{\ell}\mathbb{F}^{2}

\displaystyle F_{\mathsf{d},\mathsf{s}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}/\textup{stab}(\lambda)}F_{\otimes,\mathsf{s}}(\sigma\cdot\lambda)\in\textup{Sym}_{m}\textup{Sym}^{\ell}\mathbb{F}^{2}

\displaystyle F_{\wedge,\mathsf{s}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle x^{\lambda_{1}}y^{\ell-\lambda_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge x^{\lambda_{m}}y^{\ell-\lambda_{m}}\in{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{m}\textup{Sym}^{\ell}\mathbb{F}^{2}

\displaystyle F_{\wedge,\mathsf{d}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle F(\lambda_{1})\wedge F(\lambda_{2})\wedge\cdots\wedge F(\lambda_{m})\in{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{m}\textup{Sym}_{\ell}\mathbb{F}^{2}

These vectors to partitions
\lambda
(regular partitions in the last two cases) form a basis of their respective vector spaces, which we call the standard bases.
The support
\textup{supp}(v)
of a vector
v
is the set of partitions for which
v
has a nonzero coefficient.
For two partitions,
\lambda
and
\mu
, we say that
\lambda

dominates

\mu
if
\forall i:\sum_{j=1}^{i}\lambda_{j}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mu_{j}
.
Let
d_{m}=(m-1,m-2,\dots,0)
be the staircase vector of length
m
.

Wronskian:

The Wronskian map
\textsf{{W}}_{m,\ell}:\textup{Sym}_{m}\textup{Sym}^{\ell}\mathbb{F}^{2}\to{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{m}\textup{Sym}^{\ell+m-1}\mathbb{F}^{2}
is defined as

F_{\mathsf{d},\mathsf{s}}(\lambda)\mapsto\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}/\textup{stab}(\lambda)}F_{\wedge,\mathsf{s}}(\sigma\lambda+d_{m}).

It is easy to see that
\textup{supp}(\textsf{{W}}_{m,\ell}(F_{\mathsf{d},\mathsf{s}}(\lambda)))
is a poset with respect to the dominance order with maximum element
\lambda+d_{m}
.

Hodge:

The Hodge map
\textsf{{D}}_{m,\ell}:{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{m}\textup{Sym}^{\ell+m-1}\mathbb{F}^{2}\to{\textstyle\bigwedge}^{\ell}\textup{Sym}_{\ell+m-1}\mathbb{F}^{2}
is defined as

F_{\wedge,\mathsf{s}}(\lambda)\mapsto F_{\wedge,\mathsf{d}}(((\ell+m-1)^{\ell})-\lambda^{\textup{{c}}}),

where
\lambda^{\textup{{c}}}
is the partition that has a row of length
i
if and only if
\lambda
does not have a row of length
i
.

F_{\wedge,\mathsf{d}}(\lambda)\mapsto\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}}F_{\mathsf{s},\mathsf{d}}(\pi\lambda-d_{\ell})

F_{\mathsf{s},\mathsf{d}}(\nu)=0

\nu_{i}<0

\nu_{i}>m

\textup{supp}(\textsf{{W}}^{\star}_{\ell,m}(F_{\wedge,\mathsf{d}}(\lambda)))

.

The following diagram commutes.

5.2 Proof
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{k}(m,\ell)
.
We have
\widetilde{\textsf{{W}}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda)=\lambda+d_{m}
,
and
\big(\widetilde{\textsf{{W}}}^{\star}_{\ell,m}\big)^{-1}(\lambda^{T})=\lambda^{T}+d_{\ell}
.
Since these two partitions together have
m+\ell
many rows,
to finish the proof
it suffices to prove that

\lambda+d_{m}
and
((\ell+m-1)^{\ell})-(\lambda^{T}+d_{\ell})
have no row length in common.
For the sake of contradiction, assume there is a common element.
That means there are indices
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
, such that
\lambda_{i}+m-i=(\ell+m-1)-(\lambda^{T}_{j}+\ell-j)
, which is equivalent to
\lambda_{i}+\lambda^{T}_{j}=i+j-1
.
We make a case distinction and rule out both cases.
In the case that
\lambda_{i}\geq j
,
the Young diagram of
\lambda
has a box in row
i
and column
j
, hence
\lambda^{T}_{j}\geq i
, thus we get
\lambda_{i}+\lambda^{T}_{j}\geq i+j>i+j-1
.
Otherwise, in the case
\lambda_{i}\leq j-1
, the Young diagram of
\lambda
has no box in row
i
and column
j
,
and hence
\lambda^{T}_{j}\leq i-1
, so we get
\lambda_{i}+\lambda^{T}_{j}\leq i+j-2<i+j-1
.
In both cases we reached the desired contradiction
\lambda_{i}+\lambda^{T}_{j}\neq i+j-1
.

6 Claim
.

\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}
reverses the dominance order, i.e., is antimonotone.

5.3 Proof
.

\lambda
dominates
\mu
if and only if
\mu
can be obtained from
\lambda
by a sequence of box moves that each move a box in the Young diagram of
\lambda
downwards to a different row of
\lambda
, while preserving the property of being a partition, see
[
Bry73
, Prop 2.3]
.
For regular partitions, all intermediate partitions can be assumed to be regular: subtract the staircase partition from
\lambda
and
\mu
, move the boxes, and then add the staircase back to all intermediate partitions.
Given this result about the dominance order, we can assume that
\lambda
and
\mu
differ only by the position of a single box.
Say,
\mu
is obtained from
\lambda
by moving a box from a row to a row further down.
But moving a box in
\lambda
to a row further down is an upwards box move in
\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda)
, which can be seen as follows.
Say, the regular partition
\mu
is obtained from the regular partition
\lambda
by moving a box from the row of length
i
in
\lambda
to the row of length
j
in
\lambda
,
i>j
.
The row lengths of
\lambda
and
\mu
are the same besides rows of length
(i,i-1,j+1,j)
.
We write
\lambda_{i,i-1,j+1,j}=(a_{i},a_{i-1},a_{j+1},a_{j})
with
a_{k}=1
if
\lambda
has a row of length
k
, and
a_{k}=0
otherwise.
We have
\lambda_{i,i-1,j+1,j}=(1,0,0,1)
and
\mu_{i,i-1,j+1,j}=(0,1,1,0)
.
Applying
\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}
, we get

\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\mu)_{(m+\ell-1)-i,(m+\ell-1)-(i-1),(m+\ell-1)-(j+1),(m+\ell-1)-j}=(1,0,0,1)

and

\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda)_{(m+\ell-1)-i,(m+\ell-1)-(i-1),(m+\ell-1)-(j+1),(m+\ell-1)-j}=(0,1,1,0)
.
The other row lengths of
\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda)
and
\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\mu)
are the same.
Hence,
\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\mu)
dominates
\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda)
.

5.4 Theorem

(Hermite reciprocity)
.

The
\textsf{{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
-equivariant map
\textsf{{R}}_{m,\ell}=\textsf{{W}}_{\ell,m}^{\star}\circ\textsf{{D}}_{m,\ell}\circ\textsf{{W}}_{m,\ell}
is an isomorphism.

5.5 Proof
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathscr{P}(m,\ell)
.
Then
\textup{supp}(\textsf{{W}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda))
is a poset with respect to the dominance order whose maximum element is
\widetilde{\textsf{{W}}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda)
.
Hence,
\textup{supp}(\textsf{{D}}_{m,\ell}(\textsf{{W}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda)))
is a poset with respect to the dominance order whose minimum element is
\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\widetilde{\textsf{{W}}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda))
, see Claim
6
.
Therefore,
\textup{supp}(\textsf{{W}}^{\star}_{\ell,m}(\textsf{{D}}_{m,\ell}(\textsf{{W}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda))))
is a poset with respect to the dominance order whose minimum element is
\widetilde{\textsf{{W}}}^{\star}_{\ell,m}(\widetilde{\textsf{{D}}}_{m,\ell}(\widetilde{\textsf{{W}}}_{m,\ell}(\lambda)))
.
We conclude that the matrix of
\textsf{{W}}^{\star}_{\ell,m}\circ\textsf{{D}}_{m,\ell}\circ\textsf{{W}}_{m,\ell}

is triangular with respect to the standard basis.
Proposition
5.1
implies that the diagonal elements are all 1, which finishes the proof.

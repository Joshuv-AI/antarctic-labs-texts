Improvement of college students’ higher mathematics problem solving ability based on neural network and multiple regression model
Authors: Tengyi Liu, Chuantao Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aims at the limitations of the evaluation of Problem Solving Ability (PSA) in college students’ advanced mathematics problem-solving. It constructs a dual-dimensional evaluation system integrating cognitive dimensions (conceptual understanding, logical reasoning, etc.) and behavioral dimensions (time allocation, operation sequence, etc.) based on Piaget’s cognitive theory and Schoenfeld’s behavioral framework. Meanwhile, this study proposes a hybrid model of Convolutional Neural Network-Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory-Regression (CNN-BiLSTM-Reg) optimized by Multi-Verse Optimizer (MVO). Through a regression-guided attention mechanism, the model converted multiple regression coefficients into neural network weights, achieving a balance between interpretability and prediction accuracy (R²=0.95, Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) = 5.32). The experimental results show that: The contribution rate of the cognitive dimension to PSA reaches 78.6% (β = 0.35 for logical reasoning). Ablation experiments verify that MVO optimization improves performance by 13.7%. The prediction error of the time dimension decreases from 6.85 at the beginning of the semester to 4.06 at the end of the semester. The improvement range of PSA in the intervention group is 3.3 times that in the control group. This study confirms the scientific nature of the integrated modeling of cognition and behavior, and is expected to provide a technical framework with both prediction function and teaching guidance value for intelligent education.

Introduction

As a core competency in Science, Technology, Engineering, Mathematics (STEM) education, Problem Solving Ability (PSA) in advanced mathematics has its accurate evaluation and improvement mechanism as a key and difficult issue in current intelligent education research1,2. Traditional evaluation relies excessively on test scores as a single-dimensional indicator, making it difficult to capture the complex interaction between cognitive processes and behavioral performance. Meanwhile, existing prediction models generally have defects such as weak interpretability and insufficient dynamic modeling capabilities, resulting in a lack of targeted basis for teaching intervention3,4. Based on this, how to construct a dynamic evaluation system integrating cognitive and behavioral dual dimensions, and design a computational model with high prediction accuracy and high interpretability to accurately identify PSA remains to be studied.

To address the above research issues, this study proposes a new idea of “cognition-behavior integrated modeling”, aiming to break through existing limitations through the collaborative mechanism of multiple regression and deep learning. The overall objectives of the study are mainly reflected in three aspects: converting psychological constructs into quantifiable features, alleviating the contradiction between the “black-box” nature of neural networks and the interpretability of educational decisions, and attempting to establish an evaluation system from ability diagnosis to teaching intervention. The specific main objectives include: (1) Constructing a PSA evaluation index covering 7 dimensions such as conceptual understanding and logical reasoning; (2) Developing a hybrid Convolutional Neural Network-Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory-Regression (CNN-BiLSTM-Reg) model driven by Multi-Verse Optimizer (MVO) to realize knowledge transfer from regression coefficients to attention weights; (3) Verifying the guiding value of the model for personalized teaching strategies.

The content arrangement of subsequent research is as follows: (1) Establish a multiple regression model based on the dual-dimensional evaluation system to quantify the contribution of each feature to PSA. (2) Design a dynamic prediction network of Multi-Verse Optimizer-Convolutional Neural Network-Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (MVO-CNN-BiLSTM), and use a regression-guided attention mechanism to integrate the advantages of linear interpretability and nonlinear prediction. (3) Select a dataset and conduct four groups of experiments to verify the model accuracy, factor identification ability and teaching application effect. This framework is expected to provide a solution with both theoretical rigor and technical practicality for the research on advanced mathematics ability.

Literature review

In recent years, interdisciplinary research between neural networks and mathematical PSA has shown a trend of multi-dimensional integration5. Ou et al. (2025) revealed the bifurcation dynamics mechanism of time-delay systems through a bidirectional associative memory network, providing a mathematical foundation for the temporal modeling of logical reasoning in the cognitive dimension6. Genzel et al. (2022) identified the robustness defects of deep neural networks in solving inverse problems through systematic analysis, and revealed the sensitivity boundary of models to input disturbances. However, the regularization constraint framework they proposed significantly improved the ability of networks to capture the nonlinear characteristics of PSA7. The finite basis physics-informed neural network method proposed by Moseley et al. (2023) inspired this study to conduct spatial decoupling modeling of cognitive-behavioral dual-dimensional features through a domain decomposition strategy8. Saraf et al. (2024) systematically reviewed statistical and deep learning methods for automatic mathematical problem-solving, and their redundant operation detection framework for operation sequence features was integrated into the regression-guided mechanism of this study9. He et al. (2023)’s research on multi-level classification based on process data directly supported the quantitative analysis of time allocation patterns in the behavioral dimension10. Li (2023)’s deep learning analysis of primary school mathematics problem-solving provided methodological reference for this study to construct a cross-age transfer learning model for PSA11. The network framework for inverse problem solving developed by Li et al. (2020) combined neural networks with variational regularization, and solved the numerical instability problem of ill-posed inverse problems through latent space mapping. The research provided methodological support for the cognitive-behavioral coupling analysis12.

The above literature review indicates that existing studies still have shortcomings in the evaluation of advanced mathematics PSA, such as insufficient integration of multi-dimensional features, limited model interpretability, and inadequate capture of dynamic development processes. Based on this, this study constructs a regression-guided hybrid evaluation model to quantify the dynamic interaction mechanism between the cognitive dimension and the behavioral dimension. The study also uses multi-objective optimization to improve prediction accuracy and teaching interpretability, thereby providing a new method for personalized learning path planning.

Research model

Construction of multiple regression model

PSA evaluation dimension

According to the theories of cognitive psychology and educational behaviorism, the essence of PSA in college students’ advanced mathematics is essentially a complex ability formed by the joint action of cognitive processes and explicit behaviors13,14. Based on this, this study starts from two dimensions: the cognitive dimension and the behavioral dimension. The cognitive dimension focuses on students’ internal thinking activities, while the behavioral dimension focuses on students’ performance in problem-solving operations15,16.

In terms of the cognitive dimension, based on Piaget’s cognitive development theory, this study focuses on four key abilities: Conceptual Understanding (CU), Logical Reasoning (LR), Spatial Visualization (SV), and Metacognitive Monitoring (MM)17,18. In terms of the behavioral dimension, according to Schoenfeld’s problem-solving behavior framework, three indicators are quantified through observable problem-solving process data: Time Allocation Pattern (TAP), Operation Sequence Feature (OSF), and Stress Response (SR)19,20.

Among the above content, the Stress Response (SR) in the behavioral dimension is mainly used to quantify the unsteady behavioral performance during problem-solving. Its calculation method relies on three observable indicators, as follows: (1) Number of problem-solving interruptions: The number of pauses exceeding 3 s during the solution process of a single problem. (2) Error correction frequency: The number of operations to delete or modify the already entered answer. (3) Fluctuation rate of answer rhythm: Calculated by the coefficient of variation of the interval time between adjacent operations. The final value of SR is a weighted synthesis of the standardized scores of these three indicators. The weight coefficients involved are determined through the contribution analysis of pre-experiments.

The MVO-CNN-BiLSTM dynamic prediction model

Network structure design

This network structure is specifically designed for the characteristics of temporal data collected in PSA evaluation, and can simultaneously extract spatial local features and long-term temporal dependencies27,28.

MVO optimization mechanism

After completing the design of the CNN-BiLSTM network structure, this study introduces the MVO algorithm to solve the problem of neural network hyperparameter selection. Traditional manual parameter tuning is not only time-consuming but also difficult to find the global optimal solution32,33. Inspired by the multi-verse theory in cosmology, the MVO algorithm achieves efficient optimization by simulating the matter exchange mechanism between universes, and is particularly suitable for dealing with the complex parameter space of hybrid models34.

The core idea of MVO is to regard each candidate parameter combination as a “universe”, whose fitness is determined by model performance. The hyperparameters to be optimized are defined as a universe vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{U}_{i}$$\end{document}, where i represents the universe number. The optimization objective is to minimize the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) on the validation set, and its calculation equation is:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\text{R}\text{M}\text{S}\text{E}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}\:({y}_{j}-{\widehat{y}}_{j}{)}^{2}}$$\end{document}

The optimization process starts with initializing 30 random universes, where each universe corresponds to a set of parameter combinations. In each iteration, the algorithm executes the following steps: (a) Calculate the fitness of all universes. (b) Sort the universes by fitness, and perform the white hole transfer operation to enable weak universes to learn from strong universes. (c) Execute wormhole jumping with the probability of Wormhole Existence Probability (WEP) to introduce random disturbances. (d) Eliminate the 10% of universes with the worst fitness and generate new universes to replace them. The entire optimization lasts for 100 iterations, and finally, the universe with the lowest RMSE is selected as the optimal parameter combination.

Hybrid modeling strategy

To achieve more accurate dynamic prediction of college students’ advanced mathematics PSA, this study further proposes a hybrid modeling strategy. This strategy utilizes a feature-level fusion mechanism and fully combines the interpretability of multiple regression with the advantages of nonlinear prediction of neural networks39–41. The specific implementation process is shown in Fig. 3.

Fig. 3Implementation process of hybrid modeling strategy.

Implementation process of hybrid modeling strategy.

In Fig. 3, the operation steps of this hybrid modeling strategy are divided into three stages: (1) Input the preprocessed time-series data into the MVO-optimized CNN-BiLSTM network to extract high-dimensional feature representations42. Meanwhile, input the same data into the multiple regression model constructed in Sect. “The MVO-CNN-BiLSTM dynamic prediction model” to output the regression coefficient of each feature. (2) Convert the regression coefficients into attention weights, which are used for weighted fusion of the feature maps from the CNN-BiLSTM. (3) Map the weighted features to the final PSA predicted values through the fully connected layer.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

This study adopts public datasets to comprehensively capture the dynamic characteristics of college students’ advanced mathematics PSA. The selection of datasets is based on three criteria: timeliness, multimodality, and verifiability. Finally, the following datasets are selected as experimental datasets:

1) Program for International Student Assessment (PISA) Mathematics Competence Assessment Database.

Released by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), this database contains mathematics problem-solving records of 15-year-old students worldwide, covering cognitive dimension indicators such as logical reasoning and spatial visualization, as well as behavioral data such as problem-solving time series and error backtracking frequency. Its advantages lie in the rigorous standardized test design and cross-cultural validity verification (access link: https://www.oecd.org/en/about/programmes/pisa/pisa-data.html).

2) MATH-Vision Dataset

This dataset includes 3,040 real mathematics competition problems, covering 16 topics such as algebra and geometry, with difficulty levels divided into 5 grades. Each problem provides multimodal information (text + formula images), which is suitable for analyzing problem-solving strategies and cognitive processes (access link: https://github.com/mathllm/MATH-V).

To meet the model requirements, this study performed unified preprocessing on the original data: (a) Missing value handling: Multiple imputation was used to fill in intermittent discontinuous records in the competition data. (b) Feature alignment: Mapping PISA’s “problem-solving strategy classification” to the operation sequence feature framework of this study. (c) Time slicing: Aggregating behavioral data by week granularity to generate 12-dimensional time-series feature vectors (corresponding to a 12-week learning cycle).

A stratified sampling strategy is adopted for dataset division to ensure that the distribution of training set, validation set, and test set in key variables such as disciplinary background, gender, and region is consistent with the original data. The specific division ratio is shown in Table 2.

Experimental environment and parameters setting

To ensure experimental reproducibility and meet the computational requirements of the hybrid model, a dedicated hardware platform and software environment are built for this study. The experimental hardware consists of 4 computing nodes, each equipped with two Intel Xeon Gold 6338 processors and 512GB Double Data Rate 4 (DDR4) memory. For graphics acceleration, 3 NVIDIA DGX A100 servers are used, each integrating 8 A100 Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) (with 40GB of video memory) and achieving high-speed interconnection via NVLink. The storage system adopts an open-source distributed storage system with a total capacity of 1.2 PB, which meets the requirement of fast reading and writing of large-scale time-series data.

The software environment is constructed based on the Ubuntu 22.04 Long-Term Support (LTS) operating system, with main dependent libraries including deep learning frameworks, optimization algorithm libraries, data processing tools, and visualization tools.

To verify the model’s adaptability in different learning stages, this study designs a time-dimensional prediction experiment. Taking a 12-week advanced mathematics course at a university as the cycle, 200 students are required to submit cognitive and behavioral data every week, and the model needs to predict the final PSA based on the data from the first N weeks. The early semester scenario (Weeks 1–4) only provides initial course data to test the prediction ability when information is insufficient. The mid-semester scenario (Weeks 5–8) incorporates mid-term learning behaviors to examine the capture of progressive ability. The end-semester scenario (Weeks 9–12) integrates the complete learning trajectory to evaluate the final accuracy when data is complete. The final experimental results are shown in Fig. 8. Among them, the intervention strategy refers to the design of logical reasoning intensive training based on LR coefficients.

Discussion

In-depth discussion on the experimental results of the previous section reveals that the superior performance of the hybrid model verifies the scientific validity of the cognitive-behavioral dual-dimensional theory in the assessment of mathematical PSA. By means of the regression-guided attention mechanism, it achieves in-depth integration of psychological theories and machine learning technologies, providing an interpretable quantitative tool for educational measurement. The methodological innovations of MVO optimization and BiLSTM temporal modeling reveal the dynamic laws of ability development, short-term behavioral fluctuations are regulated by long-term cognitive development. This finding establishes a cross-scale analysis method of “micro-behavior-macro-ability” for educational time-series data analysis. Disciplinary differences and intervention experiments confirm that the model possesses both prediction accuracy and teaching guidance value, and its regression coefficients can be converted into personalized teaching strategies. However, attention should be paid to the model’s limitations in cross-cultural adaptability; in the future, transfer learning can be introduced to enhance its generalization ability.

Conclusion

Research contribution

At the theoretical level, this study constructs a “cognition-behavior” dual-dimensional PSA assessment system that integrates Piaget’s cognitive theory and Schoenfeld’s behavioral framework, and converts psychological variables such as logical reasoning and metacognitive monitoring into quantifiable time-series features. At the methodological level, this study proposes an MVO-optimized CNN-BiLSTM-Reg hybrid architecture. Through the regression-guided attention mechanism, it improves the model’s interpretability (R²=0.95) and prediction accuracy (RMSE = 5.32), and reduces the prediction error in the time dimension. At the practical level, this study points out a personalized intervention strategy based on feature weights. Empirical data show that the PSA improvement of the intervention group is higher than that of the control group, providing an implementable technical framework for intelligent education systems.

Future works and research limitations

This study has the following limitations: (1) The existing model has insufficient adaptability to cross-cultural data, and domain adaptation technology needs to be adopted to enhance its generalization ability. (2) MVO optimization is time-consuming, and distributed optimization under the federated learning framework can be explored in the future. (3) The impact of social factors such as teacher-student interaction on PSA has not been fully considered, and social network analysis methods will be attempted to introduce in the future. (4) The data of the cognitive dimension relies on subjective questionnaires. In future research, eye-tracking and infrared radiation technologies will be combined to obtain more objective neurocognitive indicators, and the research will be extended to interdisciplinary scenarios to verify the model’s universality.

In addition, this study currently focuses primarily on the software implementation and algorithm optimization of the model. Therefore, an important future direction is to explore the integration with neuromorphic computing hardware. For example, by referring to memristor-based neural network circuit designs, the optimized MVO-CNN-BiLSTM-Reg model can be mapped to low-power, high-parallelism hardware platforms. This is expected to fundamentally solve the problem of long optimization time of the current model and enable its real-time, embedded deployment in future intelligent education terminals. At the same time, drawing on the optimization ideas of the RIME-CNN-BiLSTM hybrid model that has been successfully applied in marine engineering, the robustness and adaptability of the model in resource-constrained environments can be further enhanced.

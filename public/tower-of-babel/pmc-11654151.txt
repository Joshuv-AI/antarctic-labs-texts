Risk factors for complications of Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia in hospitalized children in China: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Ziyu Wang, Yingying Peng, Shuo Yang, Zhe Chen, Huizhe Wang, Huanmin Li, Xinmin Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) is a common respiratory disease in children. Some patients may develop long-term respiratory issues such as chronic cough and wheezing due to complications. These complications increase the health burden for children with MPP, highlighting the need to understand their risk factors in order to improve disease prognosis. This study aims to assess the risk factors for complications in hospitalized children with MPP in China.MethodsA comprehensive review of Chinese and English databases was conducted up to April 2024 to retrieve studies focusing on complications in hospitalized children with MPP. Pooled effect sizes were calculated using random-effects or fixed-effects models. Statistical analyses were performed using R software and RevMan 5.3.ResultsThis meta-analysis encompassed a total of 17 studies. We analyzed multiple risk factors associated with complications, including prolonged fever (OR: 3.79, aOR: 1.64), pleural effusion (OR: 3.71, aOR: 6.70), extensive lung infiltration (OR: 5.52, aOR: 3.57), ≥ 2/3 consolidation in a single lung lobe (OR: 4.29, aOR: 4.52), right upper lung lesion (OR: 4.84, aOR: 3.57), extrapulmonary manifestations (OR: 3.20, aOR: 1.89), hypoxemia (OR: 7.58, aOR: 12.43), delayed administration of glucocorticoids (OR: 7.93, aOR: 6.67), and delayed administration of macrolides (OR: 2.75, aOR: 3.84).ConclusionHospitalized children with SMPP and RMPP may have a higher risk of complications, and it is important to identify risk factors early and provide appropriate treatment to minimize complications. The current level of evidence is relatively low and may not accurately reflect the actual clinical situation in China. Further high-quality studies are needed to validate and clarify these findings.Trial registrationOur protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (registration number: CRD42024534397).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12887-024-05279-9.Keywords: Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia, Complication, Risk factor, Meta-analysis

Introduction

Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MP) is a cell wall-free prokaryote capable of causing both upper and lower respiratory infections. Epidemiological studies indicate that MP infections affect all age groups, with a higher prevalence among children [1]. Given the fact that children's bodies are in a stage of rapid development and their immune systems are insufficiently mature, they are more vulnerable to MP infections [2]. MP is a common cause of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in children [3], responsible for approximately 40% of CAP cases attributed to Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) from MP infection [4, 5]. MPP exhibits an epidemic peak every 3–7 years, and the regional outbreaks in China since last autumn have exacerbated the national public health burden [6, 7]. Previous studies reported that some children with MPP may develop severe complications, such as bronchiolitis obliterans, bronchiectasis, atelectasis, and myocardial damage [8–11]. These complications are closely related to severe mycoplasma pneumonia (SMPP) and refractory mycoplasma pneumonia (RMPP). SMPP is primarily determined based on symptoms and clinical progression, while RMPP emphasizes the lack of response to initial therapy. Specifically, RMPP is characterized by persistent fever, worsening clinical symptoms, and deteriorating pulmonary imaging despite macrolide treatment lasting 7 days or longer [12]. In recent years, there has been increasing attention to cases of severe mycoplasma pneumonia (SMPP) and refractory mycoplasma pneumonia (RMPP) [13, 14], and how to effectively prevent complications has become a critical issue in the management of MPP [15].

Among these complications, considerable attention is given to bronchiolitis obliterans and bronchitis obliterans [16–18]. Children with MPP are at heightened risk of developing post-infectious bronchiolitis obliterans, second only to those infected with adenovirus [19]. Bronchiolitis obliterans is characterized primarily by wheezing, which reflects inflammatory damage and fibrotic changes in the small airways, leading to narrowing or complete occlusion of the airway lumen [20]. Bronchitis obliterans presents as persistent cough and dyspnoea, with features including segmental and sub-segmental bronchial obstruction, fibrous tissue proliferation, lung collapse, and bronchiectasis [15, 21]. Previous studies have confirmed that MPP can result in residual chronic cough, wheezing, restrictive lung disease, pulmonary fibrosis, and persistent small airway dysfunction [22–24]. The complications of MPP can persist for more than 6 months, closely linked to these long-term respiratory issues, posing a significant threat to children's health and severely impacting their quality of life after discharge. Therefore, preventing these complications is crucial for improving the prognosis of children affected by MPP.

A thorough understanding of the risk factors for complications aids in the development of effective prevention strategies. Although clinicians and researchers have increasingly focused on MPP complications in recent years, leading to many clinical studies, the published research on this topic comes mainly from individual research centers. This has led to variations in the data, creating uncertainty about the clinical applicability of the findings. Therefore, this study aims to systematically review the literature on complications associated with MPP, conduct a meta-analysis to assess high-risk populations and primary risk factors, and provide recommendations for complication prevention based on the findings.

Methods

This study was conducted using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [25]. The detailed information of our protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (registration number: CRD42024534397).

Search strategy

We searched Chinese electronic databases including China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), VIP Information, Wanfang Data Knowledge Service Platform, Chinese Biomedical Literature Service System (SionMed), as well as major international databases such as PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. Our objective was to review observational studies related to complications of MPP in hospitalized children published in China from the establishment of the database until April 2024. The search terms were selected from PubMed’s MeSH thesaurus and included 'Mycoplasma pneumonia', 'complications', 'sequelae', and 'child'. Boolean operators were used to merge subject terms and free-text phrases, creating search strings tailored to the unique characteristics of each respective database. Two researchers (ZW and YP) conducted independent searches simultaneously, with any discrepancies resolved by consensus with a third researcher (XL). Additionally, we reviewed the references of included studies to supplement our search and ensure comprehensive literature retrieval. No language restrictions were applied to the included literature, and non-Chinese publications were translated for comprehension. The full search strategy is detailed in Additional file 1.

Eligibility criteria

This review exclusively included observational studies, specifically case–control and cohort studies. To be eligible, studies should be conducted in China and recruited hospitalized children diagnosed with MPP. No restrictions based on disease severity were imposed, ensuring a comprehensive examination across different severities. The complications reported in these studies were required to be related to the respiratory issues caused by MPP. Given the studies' origin in China, we defined these complications based on the latest 'Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Mycoplasma Pneumonae Pneumonia in Children (2023 Edition)' issued by the National Health Commission of the People's Republic of China [12]. Additionally, included studies were required to provide original data suitable for calculating the odds ratios for risk factors associated with MPP complications.

We excluded case reports, conference abstracts, inaccessible dissertations, and duplicated publications from our review. Studies were also excluded if their classification of complications from MPP did not align with established guidelines. Additionally, studies with incomplete data or significant errors were excluded.

Data extraction

The literature screening and data extraction were independently conducted by two researchers (ZW and YP). Initially, articles were screened based on titles and abstracts, followed by a full-text evaluation against predetermined selection criteria to identify eligible studies and extract data. In cases where different studies involved the same group of subjects, priority was given to the study with the most comprehensive dataset. Throughout each phase, both researchers cross-checked their screening results, with a third researcher (XL) responsible for resolving any discrepancies.

The following data were extracted: study characteristics (first author, publication year, study design), sample size, number of complications cases, basic participant information (severity of MPP, median age, province), and risk factors [definition, odds ratios (ORs), and/or adjusted ORs with respective 95% confidence intervals (95% CI)]. For ease of grouping in statistical analysis, we categorized the provinces where studies were conducted into six regions based on geographical location: North China, Northeast China, South Central China, Southwest China, East China, and Northwest China.

Quality assessment

The methodological quality of case–control and cohort studies was assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) [26], which evaluates the selection of study participants, comparability between groups, and assessment of exposure or outcome. The maximum score for study was 9 points, with a score of 7–9 points indicated high quality. We did not exclude studies based on the quality assessment results.

Statistical analysis

Our initial assessment revealed a significant variation in the complication rates, largely influenced by the severity of MPP. To assess the impact of MPP severity on the risk of complications, we categorized the studies included in our analysis into two groups based on participant severity: hospitalized children with MPP across all severity levels, and those with SMPP/RMPP. We separately pooled the proportions of children with complications in different groups and conducted preliminary comparisons based on the geographical locations of the institutions and the participants' ages. In this meta-analysis, we synthesized the ORs and adjusted ORs of risk factors investigated in at least two studies using multivariable designs. Definitions of the same risk factor were ensured to be consistent across all included studies. The heterogeneity was quantified and tested using I2 and Q statistic (α = 0.1). If heterogeneity was deemed acceptable (P > 0.10 and I2 ≤ 50%), a fixed-effects model was applied; otherwise, a random-effects model was used, followed by an exploration of potential sources of heterogeneity. Sensitivity analysis was conducted to evaluate the impact of individual studies on the overall result stability. In addition, we generated funnel plots and employed Egger’s and Begg’s tests to quantify the risk of publication bias. All statistical analyses were performed using the meta package in R 4.4.0 and RevMan 5.3.

Results

Search results

We initially retrieved 3154 relevant records through our literature search. After removing duplicates, we reviewed the titles and abstracts of 2354 records and conducted full-text evaluations of the remaining 67. Ultimately, only 17 studies that met the inclusion criteria were included in the final review. The flowchart illustrating the literature screening process is presented in Fig. 1. The complete list of included studies can be found in Additional file 2.

Characteristics of the studies

The characteristics of the 17 included studies are summarized in Table 1. This systematic review comprised 16 case–control studies[17, 27–41] and 1 cohort study [42], all published between 2008 and 2023, involving 8751 Chinese hospitalized children with MPP. Among these studies, 11 focused on hospitalized children with MPP across all severity levels, while 6 investigated children with SMPP or RMPP. The median age of participants across all studies ranged from 1.2 to 8.2 years. Geographically, the studies were distributed as follows: 2 from North China; 3 from East China; 4 each from Northeast China, South Central China and Southwest China; and no studies were conducted in Northwest China. Complication rate and subgroup analysis

Eleven studies reported the proportion of selected hospitalized MPP children with complications, yielding a pooled result of 10.5% (95% CI: 8.2–13.6) based on a random-effects model, with significant heterogeneity (I2 = 90%, p < 0.01). Four studies reported the proportion of children with SMPP or RMPP who experienced complications among the selected patients, resulting in a pooled result of 42.0% (95% CI: 38.1–46.3) using a fixed-effects model (Fig. 2). Overall, children hospitalized with SMPP/RMPP exhibited a higher risk of complications.

Forest plot of the pooled results for complication rates

Table 2 presents a summary of pooled result stratified by sample characteristics. The pooled results of the proportion of children with complications among hospitalized children with MPP, as reported by research institutions in different regions, are as follows: 7.6% (95% CI: 6.5–8.8) for studies from Southwest China, 8.5% (95% CI: 7.2–9.9) for North China, 11.8% (95% CI: 4.9–28.3) for East China, 14.7% (95% CI: 8.6–25.2) for South Central China, and 15.2% (95% CI: 9.8–23.5) for Northeast China. Children with SMPP/RMPP from all regions exhibited a higher risk of complications. Among hospitalized children with MPP, the risk of complications was similar between children aged 5 years and above (10.5%, 95% CI: 8.1–13.5) and those aged below 5 years (9.5%, 95% CI: 5.0–18.2).

Complication rates by type

Four types of complications were individually reported in at least two studies. The combined rates of different complications were as follows: atelectasis 8.1% (95% CI: 7.2–9.2), bronchiolitis obliterans 1.5% (95% CI: 0.3–7.8), and bronchiectasis 1.0% (95% CI: 0.3–3.5), with atelectasis being the most commonly reported complication. All three complications exhibited a higher risk in children with SMPP/RMPP. Bronchitis obliterans was exclusively reported among hospitalized children with SMPP and RMPP, showing a pooled rate of 20.2% (95% CI: 4.1–99.0) (Table 3).

Risk factors of complications

The meta-analysis results of risk factors are summarized in Table 4. We analyzed both unadjusted and adjusted ORs for 10 factors. Among all factors, four radiographic manifestations showed statistically significant associations with complications: pleural effusion [OR: 3.71(95%CI: 1.85–7.43), p = 0.0002; aOR: 6.70(95%CI: 3.60–12.45), p < 0.00001], extensive infiltration shadow in the lung [OR: 5.52(95%CI: 3.82–7.98), p < 0.00001; aOR: 3.57(95%CI: 2.58–4.94), p < 0.00001], ≥ 2/3 consolidation in a single lung lobe [OR: 4.29(95%CI: 1.48–12.39), p = 0.007; aOR: 4.52(95%CI: 1.94–10.52), p = 0.0005], and right upper lung lesion [OR: 4.84(95%CI: 3.35–7.00), p < 0.00001; aOR: 3.57(95%CI: 2.58–4.94), p < 0.00001]. Prolonged fever (≥ 10 days) [OR: 3.79(95%CI: 2.69–5.34), p < 0.00001; aOR: 1.64(95%CI: 1.16–2.30), p = 0.005], extrapulmonary manifestations [OR: 3.20(95%CI: 2.12–4.85), p < 0.00001; aOR: 1.89(95%CI: 1.08–3.30), p = 0.03], and hypoxemia [OR: 7.58(95%CI: 3.49–16.47), p < 0.00001; aOR: 12.43(95%CI: 1.09–141.75), p = 0.04] were identified as risk factors for complications. Two treatment-related factors also increased the risk of complications: delayed use of macrolides [OR: 2.75(95%CI: 1.66–4.55), p < 0.0001; aOR: 3.84(95%CI: 1.76–8.38), p = 0.0007] and delayed use of glucocorticoids [OR: 7.93(95%CI: 4.90–12.84), p < 0.00001; aOR: 6.67(95%CI: 2.44–18.26), p = 0.0002]. However, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) [OR: 2.64(95%CI: 0.34–20.56), p = 0.35; aOR: 1.38(95%CI: 0.87–2.18), p = 0.17] showed no significant correlation with complications. Forest plots for each risk factor and detailed information on their publication bias can be found in Additional file 4.

Discussion

This study aims to assess the risk factors and high-risk populations for complications in children with MPP. A total of 17 studies that met our inclusion criteria were used for the analysis. In this systematic review and meta-analysis, we categorized pediatric cases of MPP based on disease severity, and conducted a preliminary comparison of complication rates by aggregating the proportion of children with complications reported in the included studies. The result indicate that severe illness correlates with a heightened risk of complications. A previous systematic review compared the risk of complications between hospitalized and non-hospitalized children with pneumonia [22]. In this study, we specifically focused on hospitalized children infected with MP, thereby minimizing the impact of pathogen diversity on the results. It is widely acknowledged that the pathogenic mechanisms of MP involve both direct tissue damage and immune-mediated injury [43]. Furthermore, MP can evade phagocytosis by host immune cells through its immune evasion mechanisms, promoting prolonged survival in the body and contributing to chronic infection [44]. Airway remodeling and fibrotic changes may result from tissue damage caused by recurrent infections and sustained inflammation, potentially causing complications such as bronchiectasis, bronchitis obliterans, and bronchiolitis obliterans [45, 46]. Excessive immune response is also considered a contributing factor to the development of complications [47]. The presence of aberrant immune responses and stronger inflammatory reactions in children with SMPP or RMPP may explain their heightened risk of complications [48, 49]. Disparities in reported complication rates among research institutions in various regions may stem from factors such as climate conditions, proportion of severe cases, and duration of illness in patients [5, 50]. However, due to the limited number of included studies, the results from regional subgroups may not adequately reflect the occurrence of complications in hospitalized children with MPP in those regions. Previous studies suggested that MP infection is more prevalent in children aged 5 years or older compared to younger children [51]. Nevertheless, in this study, the complication rate among hospitalized children with MPP was similar across age groups, including those aged 5 years and older and those under 5 years. This study does not provide evidence to suggest that there are differences in the risk of complications among children with MPP across different age groups. By aggregating the risk of different types of complications, we found that the risk of atelectasis in hospitalized children with MPP is higher than that of other complications reported. Bronchitis obliterans was reported only in children with SMPP/RMPP, which prevents an assessment of its overall risk in hospitalized children with MPP. Therefore, it was excluded from the comparison. Delayed resolution of atelectasis and persistent atelectasis can lead to prolonged respiratory issues [52, 53]. However, for most MPP patients, atelectasis can recover after treatment with macrolide antibiotics, corticosteroids, bronchoalveolar lavage, etc. [54]. Due to limited information provided in the study, we may have aggregated children with persistent atelectasis and those with favorable prognosis of atelectasis. Bronchitis obliterans is a severe obstructive pulmonary disease [55]. In a Korean study of 150 hospitalized children with MPP, 18 cases developed bronchitis obliterans [18], which differs from the rates reported in the studies included in this paper. This discrepancy may be related to factors such as regional differences. Bronchiectasis is traditionally viewed as irreversible airway dilatation resulting from a vicious cycle of inflammation and recurrent infections. However, several studies suggested that bronchiectasis attributed to MP infection can resolve [15], described as ‘pseudo bronchiectasis’ or ‘functional bronchiectasis’. Some researchers speculated that pseudo bronchiectasis may represent an early reversible stage of bronchiectasis progression or could alternatively signify a distinct clinical entity or a misinterpretation of radiological findings. Therefore, confirming bronchiectasis diagnosis at least 6 months after the infection resolution is crucial [56, 57]. Some studies reviewed here did not specify the exact timing of bronchiectasis diagnosis; hence the possibility of pseudo-bronchiectasis in these cases cannot be ruled out.

This study evaluated multiple risk factors associated with complications in children with MPP based on meta-analysis. The development of complications is strongly linked to chronic infection-induced tissue inflammation and damage, as well as abnormal immune responses. Pleural effusion, indicative of severe local inflammation, emerged as a significant risk factor for MPP complications. According to Kim et al., pediatric patients treated with immunoregulatory agents who developed pleural effusion exhibited earlier resolution of fever compared to those without, suggesting a potential link between pleural effusion and excessive immune response [58]. Similarly, Narita et al. detected MP genomes and elevated IL-8 levels in the pleural effusion of pediatric patients with prolonged chest X-ray abnormalities post-MP infection [59]. The persistence of fever and extrapulmonary manifestations in pediatric patients implied ongoing inflammatory responses and heightened immune reactions, contributing to an increased risk of complications. Hypoxemia was another risk factor for complications. Studies showed that MPP patients experiencing hypoxemia demonstrate intensified immune-inflammatory responses [60]. Failure to promptly address hypoxia can worsen lung damage and heighten the risk of complications. Consolidation involving ≥ 2/3 of a single lung lobe was an independent risk factor for complications and served as a pivotal diagnostic criterion for SMPP. Early bronchial mucus plug formation exists in some pediatric patients experiencing complications such as bronchitis obliterans. However, due to variations in the implementation of bronchoscopy among hospitals, the presence of ≥ 2/3 consolidation in a single lung lobe serves as a practical marker for identifying mucus plug formation. Furthermore, radiological manifestations of large pulmonary infiltrates and lesions in the right upper lung correlated with the complications. Macrolide antibiotics are the primary choice for treating MP infections, while glucocorticoids, known for their anti-inflammatory and immune-suppressive effects, are frequently used as adjunctive therapy in MPP treatment. Delaying the initiation of macrolides and glucocorticoids increased the risk of developing complications. Consistent with this finding, children diagnosed with macrolide-resistant Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MRMP), which shows reduced responsiveness to treatment, often experience prolonged fever duration, severe radiographic manifestations, and heightened risks of complications [52]. Another study suggested that delayed effective antibiotic treatment and macrolide resistance play equally significant roles in prolonging the course of MPP [61]. In such cases, glucocorticoids are necessary to manage inflammation and promote fever reduction. Therefore, timely administration of macrolide antibiotics and glucocorticoids is crucial in MPP treatment to prevent complications. A study from Spain found that hypoxemia, neurological symptoms, and a history of prematurity are risk factors for ICU admission in children with MPP. The study also explained the association between the high use of steroids and the risk of severe disease [62]. Complications are influenced by the severity, and the risk factors associated with severe cases also provide valuable insights for the prevention of complications.

As far as our knowledge extends, this study represents the first systematic review and meta-analysis consolidating data on the risk factors for complications among hospitalized children with MPP in China. We employed a comprehensive search strategy across both Chinese and English databases and implemented a dual-review process to ensure extensive coverage of currently published studies related to complications in children with MPP. Based on the severity of MPP in children, we conducted grouped discussions on the complication rates, highlighting an increased risk among those with SMPP or RMPP. Regarding the risk factors, we compared ORs with adjusted ORs provided in studies employing multivariable designs to offer a more reliable comprehensive assessment.

There were several limitations in our study. Firstly, although our results suggested that children with SMPP and RMPP may face a higher risk of complications, the proportion of severe and refractory cases in certain studies included in this review was unknown. Considering that the included studies only reported the proportion of children with complications among the cases treated, this proportion is influenced by the overall severity of the cases, and any selection bias cannot be determined. Therefore, we believe that the pooled complication rate in this study does not accurately reflect the true clinical incidence of complications in MPP, and the results should be interpreted with caution. Secondly, there was a limited amount of research on complications associated with MPP, with Northwest China and some provinces in the other regions lacking analyzable data. Moreover, insufficient reporting on gender characteristics, disease course, and other relevant details in children with MPP complications hindered further subgroup analyses to assess clinical heterogeneity of complication rate. Thirdly, during risk factor analysis, we observed wide confidence intervals and significant heterogeneity in hypoxaemia, potentially impacting the reliability of the findings. Fourth, this research may only report respiratory complications due to the limitations of the treatment protocols and risk factors in the original studies, which may not include major complications from other systems. Lastly, although integrative treatments combining traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) and Western medicine are increasingly used in Chinese hospitals and have shown promising outcomes for MPP, this systematic review lacks sufficient data on relevant risk factors, which limits its ability to provide robust evidence supporting the efficacy of such integrative approaches.

Given these limitations, we advocate for future high-quality, large-scale, multicenter studies from diverse regions to assess the incidence and risk factors of complications associated with MPP. To comprehensively understand the risk of MPP complications across all populations, it is essential to separately examine complication rates in children with mild, severe, and refractory MPP in future studies. Due to the close association between complication occurrence and the severity of MPP, we recommend prioritizing research on investigating risk factors for complications in children diagnosed with SMPP and RMPP. Several studies have demonstrated that integrative treatment, which combines TCM with Western medicine, can significantly improve the prognosis of MPP, showing favorable outcomes in both SMPP and RMPP patients [63, 64]. Future research should focus on investigating the effects of integrative treatment plans that incorporate TCM characteristics on the risk of complications in MPP.

Conclusions

Based on systematic reviews and meta-analyses of existing studies, the findings suggest that persistent fever, pleural effusion, radio-graphic changes, and delayed administration of macrolide antibiotics or glucocorticoids may be risk factors for complications. These complications pose a burden on hospitalized children with MPP, particularly since SMPP and RMPP patients may be at a higher risk. Although existing studies provide valuable insights, their results may not comprehensively represent the true clinical environment due to limitations related to study design, sample size, or data collection methods. Future high-quality studies are needed to validate and complement existing findings. Further research should be conducted to confirm the impact of disease stratification and treatment protocols on complication risks in MPP, thereby providing more recommendations for health policy development.

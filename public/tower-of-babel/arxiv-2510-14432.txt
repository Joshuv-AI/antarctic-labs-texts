On some Elliptic and Parabolic Problems Involving the Anisotropic p → ​ ( u ) \vec{\textbf{p}}(u) -Laplacian
Kaushik Bal , Shilpa Gupta

Abstract
We investigate a class of elliptic and parabolic partial differential equations characterized by anisotropic p → ​ ( u ) \vec{\textbf{p}}(u) -Laplace operator, where the vector-valued exponent p → = ( p 1 , … , p N ) \vec{\textbf{p}}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}) depends on the unknown function u u and a non-local function of u u , respectively. This dependence necessitates the use of variable exponent Sobolev spaces specifically tailored to the anisotropic framework. For the elliptic case, we establish the existence of a weak solution by employing the theory of pseudomonotone operators in conjunction with suitable approximation techniques. In the parabolic setting, the existence of a weak solution is obtained via a time discretization scheme and Schauder’s fixed-point theorem, supported by a priori estimates and compactness arguments. Introduction

This paper is focused on establishing the existence of weak solutions for a class of elliptic and parabolic partial differential equations that involve anisotropic
p(u)
-Laplace operators. These operators are characterized by a vector-valued exponent that depends on the unknown function
u
in the elliptic case and on a nonlocal function of
u
in the parabolic case. We begin with the following elliptic problem:

-\Delta_{\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}u=f(x,u)\quad\text{in }\Omega;\ \quad u=0\quad\text{on}\;\partial\Omega,

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}(N\geq 2)
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
,

-\Delta_{\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}u:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right),

where the exponent vector
\vec{\textbf{p}}:=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{N})
with
p_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow[2,\infty)
being continuous for each
i=1,2,\ldots,N
. The nonlinear function
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is assumed to satisfy a set of suitable conditions, which will be specified later.

We further consider the corresponding nonlocal parabolic problem:

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta_{\vec{\mathbf{p}}(b(u))}u=f,&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u=0,&\text{on }\Gamma:=\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\[4.0pt]
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\text{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}(N\geq 2)
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
,
f\in W^{-1,(p^{-})^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, the initial datum
u_{0}
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and
b:W^{1,p^{-}}{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous and bounded mapping, where the notation
p^{-}
will be clarified later. The prototypical example of such nonlocal mappings include

b(u)=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ b(u)=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\text{ for }q\leq(p^{-})^{*}=\frac{Np^{-}}{N-p^{-}}.

Due to the presence of an unknown function in the exponent, the main difficulty is that the problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can not be written as an equality in terms of duality pairing in a fixed Banach space. In fact, two distinct solutions may naturally belong to two different Sobolev spaces, depending on the corresponding values of the exponent. To the best of our knowledge, the first systematic study of a
p(u)
-Laplacian problem was carried out by Andreianov–Bendahmane–Ouaro
[
1
]
. They considered the elliptic boundary value problem

\begin{cases}u-\Delta_{p(u)}u=f,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

under the suitable regularity assumptions on the domain
\Omega
. By exploiting techniques that effectively reduce the analysis to the setting of the Lebesgue space
L^{1}
they established the existence of broad and narrow weak solutions. Subsequently, Chipot–Oliveira
[
10
]
proposed a different approach and studied both local and nonlocal formulations of the
p(u)
-Laplacian problem, namely

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p(u)}u=f,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.3)

and

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p(b(u))}u=f,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Their analysis was based on the Minty monotonicity trick combined with the powerful techniques introduced by Zhikov
[
23
]
, which are particularly well-suited for handling problems with nonstandard growth conditions. In the variable exponent context, these methods require additional care due to the non-homogeneity of the norm. In the same work, Chipot–Oliveira also formulated a collection of open problems, some of which have been addressed in later research. In particular, Zhang–Zhang
[
22
]
partially solved these questions by proving the existence of entropy solutions to the local elliptic problem (
1.3

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta_{p(b(u))}u=f,&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u=0,&\text{on }\Gamma:=\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\text{in }\Omega.\end{cases}

where the interplay between the nonlinear diffusion and the time evolution requires additional compactness and regularity tools. Further progress in the study of parabolic problems involving
p[u(x,t)]
-Laplacian operators was made by Antontsev-Shmarev
[
3
]
, who analyzed such problems under the assumption that the co-domain of the exponent function lies within the interval
(1,2)
. This analysis was later extended by Antontsev-Kuznetsov-Shmarev
[
2

, where the dependence on

-Laplacian. Despite these developments, parabolic problems involving the

-Laplacian operator have received comparatively less attention in the literature. Notably, Aouaoui-Bahrouni
[
5

-Laplacian type equations posed in the whole space

16

studied parabolic problems involving the

-Laplacian operator. More recently, Bahrouni-Bahrouni-Missaoui
[

Motivated by the above work, we address the problems (

Our analysis combines the anisotropic, variable exponent framework with suitable approximation techniques, enabling us to extend the theory of

2
Functional spaces and auxiliary results

Examining the elliptic equation (
1.1
), we observe that the exponent vector
\vec{\textbf{p}}
depends on the solution
u
, which itself is determined by the space variable
x
. Consequently, for a given function
u
, the exponent can ultimately be written as a function of
x
in the form of a variable exponent
\vec{\textbf{q}}(x)
, where
\vec{\textbf{q}}(x)=\vec{\textbf{p}}(u(x)).
Therefore, the natural space to study the equation (
1.1
) is the anisotropic variable exponent Sobolev space.
In contrast, for the parabolic equation (
1.2
), the exponent
\vec{\textbf{p}}
depends on the function
b
, which in turn is determined by
u
. As a result, for a given
u
, the exponent
\vec{\textbf{p}}(b(u))
is a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence, the suitable space for analyzing the equation (
1.2
) is the anisotropic Sobolev space.

2.1
Variable exponent Lebesgue spaces

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. For
q\in C_{+}(\overline{\Omega})
, variable exponent Lebesgue space
L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is defined by

L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable}\ \bigg|\ \int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{q(x)}\,{\rm d}x<\infty\right\}

which is a norm space with the luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\tau>0:\ \int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u(x)}{\tau}\right|^{q(x)}\,{\rm d}x\leq 1\right\}\cdot

The space
L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is Banach, reflexive and separable
[
17
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

[

Let

and

be the conjugate exponents, i.e.,
1/r(x)+1/s(x)=1

\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}.
Then, for any
u\in L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and

, we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}uv\,{\rm d}x\right|\leq\left(\frac{1}{r^{-}}+\frac{1}{s^{-}}\right)\|u\|_{L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{s(\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

2.2
Anisotropic variable exponent Sobolev spaces

Let
\vec{\textbf{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{N})
, where
p_{i}\in C_{+}(\overline{\Omega})
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,N
. For
x\in\Omega
, we define

p_{M}(x)=\max\{p_{1}(x),p_{2}(x),\ldots,p_{N}(x)\},

\overline{p}(x)=\dfrac{N}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p_{i}(x)}}

and

\overline{p}^{*}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{N\,\overline{p}(x)}{N-\overline{p}(x)},&\text{if }\overline{p}(x)<N,\\[6.0pt]
+\infty,&\text{if }\overline{p}(x)\geq N.\end{cases}

We introduce the anisotropic variable exponent Sobolev space
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
as

\displaystyle W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in L^{p_{M}(\cdot)}(\Omega):\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(\Omega),\ \forall\ i=1,2,\ldots,N\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega):v\in L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(\Omega),\ \frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(\Omega),\ \forall\ i=1,2,\ldots,N\right\}

which is a norm space with the norm

\|v\|_{W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\|v\|_{L^{p_{M}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}

where

\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\tau>0:\ \int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\tau\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(x)}\,{\rm d}x\leq 1\right\}\cdot

Next, consider the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the space
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and denote it as
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega)=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|^{\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}.

Also, define the space

\mathring{W}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)\;:\;u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\}

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. Then, by definition,

\mathring{W}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)=W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega),

and it is clear that

W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\mathring{W}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega).

In the constant exponent setting, i.e., when
\vec{\textbf{p}}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, these spaces coincide:

W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}}_{0}(\Omega)=\mathring{W}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}}(\Omega).

However, for variable exponents, this equality generally fails, that is,

W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega)\neq\mathring{W}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega),

and the space of smooth compactly supported functions
C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is not necessarily dense in
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
.

Concerning the density of smooth functions in
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega)
, the following result holds; to establish this density, some additional assumptions on the variable exponents are required.

\Omega
,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i,n}(x)}\,{\rm d}x<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\rightharpoonup\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}

in
L^{1}(\Omega).
Then
\nabla u\in(L^{p_{i}}(\Omega))^{N}
and

\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(x)}\,{\rm d}x\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i,n}(x)}\,{\rm d}x.

Proof.

Let
b\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By a standard Young-type inequality, for each
i=1,\dots,N
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\cdot b\leq\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i,n}(x)}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i,n}(x)}\left(\frac{|b|}{(p_{i,n}(x))^{1/p_{i,n}(x)}}\right)^{p^{\prime}_{i,n}(x)},

where
p^{\prime}_{i,n}(x)=\frac{p_{i,n}(x)}{p_{i,n}(x)-1}
is the conjugate exponent of
p_{i,n}(x)
. Integrating over
\Omega
and summing over
i
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\cdot b-\frac{|b|^{p^{\prime}_{i,n}(x)}}{p^{\prime}_{i,n}(x)(p_{i,n}(x))^{p^{\prime}_{i,n}(x)/p_{i,n}(x)}}\right)\,{\rm d}x\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i,n}(x)}\,{\rm d}x.

Since
\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\rightharpoonup\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, and
p_{i,n}\to p_{i}
a.e. in
\Omega
, we may pass to the limit using the Dominated Convergence Theorem to obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\cdot b-\frac{|b|^{p^{\prime}_{i}(x)}}{p^{\prime}_{i}(x)(p_{i}(x))^{p^{\prime}_{i}(x)/p_{i}(x)}}\right)\,{\rm d}x\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i,n}(x)}\,{\rm d}x:=L

(2.1)

where
p^{\prime}_{i}(x)=\frac{p_{i}(x)}{p_{i}(x)-1}
.
For each
k>0
, consider the function

b=p_{i}(x)\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{k}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}(x)-1}}\cdot\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\left/\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|\right.,\quad\text{where }\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{k}:=\min\left\{\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|,k\right\}.

Substituting this choice of
b
into (
2.1
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left(p_{i}(x)\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{k}\cdot\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{k}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{i}(x)-1}}-\frac{p_{i}(x)}{p^{\prime}_{i}(x)}\cdot\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{k}^{\frac{p^{\prime}_{i}(x)}{p^{\prime}_{i}(x)-1}}\right)\,{\rm d}x\leq L,

which implies

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{k}^{p_{i}(x)}\,{\rm d}x\leq L.

(2.2)

Since
\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{k}\to\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|
pointwise as
k\to\infty
, and the integrands are nonnegative, we may apply the Monotone Convergence Theorem to (
2.2
) to obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(x)}\,{\rm d}x\leq L:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i,n}(x)}\,{\rm d}x.

Thus, we conclude that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{p_{i}(x)}(\Omega)
for each
i
, and hence
\nabla u\in(L^{p_{i}(x)}(\Omega))^{N}
, which completes the proof.
∎

3
Elliptic Problem

In this section, we establish the existence of a weak solution to the elliptic problem (
1.1
). Subsection
3.1
introduces a perturbed version of the problem, where the existence of a weak solution is proved for the perturbed problem using the theory of pseudomonotone operators. In Subsection
3.2
, we pass to the limit to obtain the existence of a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
).

Let
u:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous function then define the space
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)
as

W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)=\left\{v\in L^{p_{M}(u)}(\Omega):\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{p_{i}(u)}(\Omega),\ \forall i=1,2,\ldots,N\right\}

which is a norm space with the norm

\|v\|_{W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)}=\|v\|_{L^{p_{M}(u)}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p_{i}(u)}(\Omega)}

where

\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p_{i}(u)}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\tau>0:\ \int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\tau\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)}\,{\rm d}x\leq 1\right\}\cdot

Next, consider the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the space
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)
and denote it as
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}_{0}(\Omega)=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|^{\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)}}
which is a norm space with the norm

\|v\|_{W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}_{0}(\Omega)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p_{i}(u)}(\Omega)}\cdot

By employing Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
in conjunction with the fact that the set of all Hölder continuous functions is contained within the set of all log-Hölder continuous functions, we have the following result:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a bounded domain with a Lipschitz boundary. Suppose that conditions
(p_{1})
-
(p_{2})
are satisfied and
u\in W_{0}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)
. Then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
W_{0}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)
. By using the condition
(p_{1})
and by Sobolev embedding theorem, we have

W^{1,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{0,1-\frac{N}{p^{-}}}(\overline{\Omega}).

Hence, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega)}|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad\forall x,y\in\overline{\Omega}.

(3.1)

Define the variable exponent
\vec{\textbf{q}}=(q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{N})\in(C_{+}(\overline{\Omega}))^{N}
by

q_{i}(x):=p_{i}(u(x)),\quad\forall x\in\Omega,\ i=1,2,\ldots,N.

By hypothesis
(p_{2})
, for each
i=1,2,\ldots,N
, we have

|q_{i}(x)-q_{i}(y)|=|p_{i}(u(x))-p_{i}(u(y))|\leq c_{i}|u(x)-u(y)|,\quad\forall x,y\in\overline{\Omega}.

(3.2)

Combining (
3.1
) with (
3.2
), we deduce that

|q_{i}(x)-q_{i}(y)|=|p_{i}(u(x))-p_{i}(u(y))|\leq c_{i}C\|u\|_{W^{1,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega)}|x-y|^{\alpha},\quad\forall x,y\in\overline{\Omega}.

Thus, each
q_{i}
is Hölder continuous.
Since every Hölder continuous function is also log-Hölder continuous, there exists a constant
L>0
such that

|q_{i}(x)-q_{i}(y)|\leq\frac{-L}{\log|x-y|},\quad\forall x,y\in\overline{\Omega},\ |x-y|<\tfrac{1}{2}.

Hence,
q_{i}
is log-Hölder continuous for each
i=1,2,\ldots,N
.
Applying Theorem
2.1
, we conclude that

\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
W_{0}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}(\Omega)
.
∎

We define a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

A function
u\in W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}_{0}(\Omega)
is called a weak solution to problem (
1.1
) if

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,{\rm d}x,\ \forall v\in W^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(u)}_{0}(\Omega).

3.1
Perturbed problem

We first consider the following auxiliary problem:

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\vec{\mathbf{p}}(u)}u-\epsilon\Delta_{p^{+}}u=f(x,u),&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Definition 3.2
.

A function
u\in W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
is called a weak solution of (
3.3
) if

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u\nabla v\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,{\rm d}x,\ \forall v\in W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega).

We define the operator
I:W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow(W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}
as

\langle I(u),v\rangle_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u\nabla v\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)v\,{\rm d}x

where

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}
stands the duality map between
W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
and its dual space
(W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega))^{*},
for the simplicity, we write it as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. We see that
u\in W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
3.3
) if and only if

\langle I(u),v\rangle=0

for all
v\in W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega).

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that conditions
(f_{1})
and
(p_{1})
are satisfied. Then problem (
3.3
) admits a nontrivial weak solution.

Next, we verify each of the conditions required by Theorem
2.4
in a systematic manner. These verifications are presented through Lemmas
3.1
–
3.3
, where we establish the coercivity, boundedness,
S^{+}
-type property and pseudomonotonicity of the operator
I
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that conditions
(f_{1})
and
(p_{1})
are satisfied. Then the operator
I
is coercive and bounded.

Proof.

By using Sobolev embedding theorem and assumption
(f)
, we estimate

\displaystyle\langle I(u),u\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)u\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x-C\int_{\Omega}(u+|u|^{r})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon\|u\|^{p^{+}}_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}-C\|u\|^{r}_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}-C\|u\|_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)},

where
1\leq r<p^{+}
. Hence,
I
is coercive.

By condition
(f)
, the Nemitsky operator
N_{f}:W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{r^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is well defined. Moreover, let
i^{*}:L^{r^{\prime}}(\Omega)\rightarrow(W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}
be the adjoint of the embedding
i:W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{r}(\Omega)
. Then, the operator
I_{1}:=i^{*}\circ N_{f}:W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow(W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}
is continuous and bounded.

Now, for any
v\in W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\langle I(u)+I_{1}(u),v\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)-1}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right|\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p^{+}-1}\left|\nabla v\right|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{+}-1}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right|+\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{-}-1}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right|\right)\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p^{+}-1}\left|\nabla v\right|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|^{p^{+}-1}_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|^{p^{-}-1}_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\epsilon\|\nabla u\|^{p^{+}-1}_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|^{p^{+}-1}_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|^{p^{-}-1}_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}+\epsilon\|\nabla u\|^{p^{+}-1}_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}\right].

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|I(u)+I_{1}(u)\|_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\|v\|_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\{|\langle I(u)+I_{1}(u),v\rangle_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}|\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|^{p^{+}-1}_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|^{p^{-}-1}_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}+\epsilon\|\nabla u\|^{p^{+}-1}_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)},

which shows that
I
maps bounded sets into bounded sets, and hence
I
is a bounded operator.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that the conditions
(f_{1})
and
(p_{1})
are satisfied. Then the operator
I
satisfies the
(S_{+})
-property that is, if
\{u_{n}\}\subset W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle I(u_{n}),u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0,

then
\{u_{n}\}
strongly converges to
u
in
W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle I(u_{n}),u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0.

We aim to prove that
\{u_{n}\}
converges to
u
strongly in
W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

From the definition of the operator
I
, we write

\displaystyle\left\langle I\left(u_{n}\right),u_{n}-u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{n})(u_{n}-u)\,{\rm d}x.

(3.4)

Claim (a):

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x\geq o_{n}(1).

To establish this, we proceed as follows. Using monotonicity and from Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}-\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right]\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x.

(3.5)

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}-\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u\right)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{+}-1}+\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{-}-1}\right)\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{+}-1}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x+2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{-}-1}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0,

as
\{u_{n}\}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{+}-1}\in L^{\frac{p^{+}}{p^{+}-1}}(\Omega).

Similarly as above,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u\right)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\left(u_{n}-u\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

(3.6)

The validity of claim (a) follows directly from equations (
3.1
)-(
3.6
).

In view of condition
(f)
, and by applying Hölder’s inequality along with the Sobolev embedding theorem, we derive the following

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{n})(u_{n}-u)\,{\rm d}x=0.

(3.7)

Combining (
3.1
), claim (a), and (
3.7
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\epsilon\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\,{\rm d}x\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle I\left(u_{n}\right),u_{n}-u\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle I\left(u_{n}\right),u_{n}-u\right\rangle\leq 0

which implies

\displaystyle\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\,{\rm d}x\leq 0.

(3.8)

Consider,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{n}\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u\right]\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\right|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x+o_{n}(1).

(3.9)

Combining (
3.8
) and (
3.1
), we conclude that

0\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\right|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x\leq 0

and hence,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(u_{n}-u\right)\right|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x=0.

Therefore,
u_{n}\rightarrow u
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
thereby confirming that the operator satisfies the
(S_{+})
-property.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that the conditions
(f_{1})
and
(p_{1})
are satisfied. Then the operator
I
is the pseudomonotone, that is, if
\{u_{n}\}
converges weakly to
u
in
W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
and

\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle I(u_{n}),u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0,

imply
I(u_{n})\rightharpoonup I(u)
and

\langle I(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\rightarrow\langle I(u),u\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
be a sequence such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
, and suppose that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle I(u_{n}),u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0.

Then, by the
S^{+}
-type property (that is, Lemma
3.2
), it follows that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
.

Let
v\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. We decompose

\displaystyle\langle I(u_{n})-I(u),v\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}-\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u\right)\nabla v\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}(f(x,u_{n})-f(x,u))v\,{\rm d}x.

We verify the convergence of each term separately.

Claim (a):

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}\left(u_{n}\right)-2}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}-\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(u)-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x=o_{n}(1),\forall v\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega).

Let
v\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
, we write

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}

-\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)v(x,0)\,{\rm d}x-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}uv_{t}\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u))-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}fv\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

for all test functions
v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}_{T})
satisfying
v(\cdot,T)=0
.

4.1
Time-discrete problem

Let
N_{0}
be a fixed positive integer, and define the time step size by
h=T/N_{0}
. We first consider the following time-discretized version of problem (
1.2
)

\begin{cases}\dfrac{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}{h}-\Delta_{\vec{\textbf{p}}(b(u_{k}))}u_{k}=[f]_{h}\bigl((k-1)h\bigr),&x\in\Omega,\\
u_{k}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,&k=1,2,\dots,N_{0},\end{cases}

(4.1)

where the Steklov average
[f]_{h}
of
f
is defined as

[f]_{h}(x,t)=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}f(x,\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau.

It is easy to verify that for each fixed
t
, the function
[f]_{h}(\cdot)\in L^{(p^{-})^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let the condition
(p_{1})
be satisfied, and suppose that
f\in W^{-1,(p^{-})^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Let
b:W^{1,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that
b
is continuous and bounded. Then, the problem (
4.1
) admits at least one non-trivial weak solution.

Proof.

To establish the result, we structure the proof in two key steps.

Step 1: Approximation

For the first time step
k=1
, the corresponding perturbed problem reads

\begin{cases}\dfrac{u-u_{0}}{h}-\Delta_{\vec{\textbf{p}}(b(u))}u-\epsilon\Delta_{p^{+}}u=[f]_{h}(0),&x\in\Omega,\\[10.0pt]
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

(4.2)

Let
w\in L^{2}({\Omega})
be fixed. For each
\epsilon>0
, we consider the following problem where exponent is not dependent on the unknown function

\begin{cases}\dfrac{u-u_{0}}{h}-\Delta_{\vec{\textbf{p}}(b(w))}u-\epsilon\Delta_{p^{+}}u=[f]_{h}(0),&x\in\Omega,\\[10.0pt]
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

(4.3)

By employing the theory of monotone operators, for a given
w\in L^{2}({\Omega})
, there exists a unique solution, say,
u_{w}\in W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)
to the problem (
4.3
) such that

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u_{w}-u_{0}}{h}v\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{w}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w))-2}\frac{\partial u_{w}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{w}|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{w}\nabla v\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}(0)v\,{\rm d}x,

for all
v\in W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega).

Choosing
v=u_{w}
, in the above equation yields the following energy estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2h}\int_{\Omega}u_{w}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{w}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{w}))}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{w}|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{1}{2h}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x+C\|\nabla u_{w}\|_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}\,{\rm d}x.

Thus, the standard Young-type inequality yields the uniform bound

\|u_{w}\|_{L^{2}{(\Omega)}}+\|u_{w}\|_{W^{1,p^{+}}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1},

where
C_{1}>0
is a constant independent of the choice of
w
.
Hence, we have

\|u_{w}\|_{L^{2}{(\Omega)}}\leq C_{1}.

Define the functional
J:L^{2}({\Omega})\rightarrow L^{2}({\Omega})
such that

J(w)=u_{w}
where
u_{w}
is the weak solution of (
4.3
).

Claim: The mapping
J
is a continuous.

Let
\{w_{n}\}\subset L^{2}({\Omega})
be a sequence such that

w_{n}\to w_{0}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}({\Omega})\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Let
u_{n}:=u_{w_{n}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
denote the corresponding weak solution of (
4.3
). We want to show that

u_{n}\to u_{w_{0}}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega),

where
u_{w_{0}}
is the solution of (
4.3
) associated to
w_{0}
.

From prior estimates, we have

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}\leq C,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
n
.
Therefore, there exists
u\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup u\text{ in }W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega),

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle\to u\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\to u(x)\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

It remains to prove that,
u_{w_{0}}\equiv u
i.e.,
u
is the weak solution corresponding to
w_{0}
.

Since each
u_{n}
satisfies the weak formulation of (
4.3
), we have

\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{n}-u_{0}}{h}v\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w_{n}))-2}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla v\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}(0)v\,{\rm d}x,

for all
v\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
. Now, using the standard test function
v=u_{n}-\varphi
, and the monotonicity of the associated operators, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{(u_{0}-\varphi)(u_{n}-\varphi)}{h}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}(0)(u_{n}-\varphi)\,{\rm d}x-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w_{n}))-2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial(u_{n}-\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla(u_{n}-\varphi)\,{\rm d}x\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega).

(4.4)

By the continuity of
p_{i}
and
b
, we have

p_{i}(b(w_{n}))\to p_{i}(b(w_{0}))\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

(4.5)

Moreover, using the integrability of the terms

\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w_{n}))-2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{\frac{p^{+}}{p^{+}-1}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x<\infty\quad\text{as }\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega).

(4.6)

By using the dominated convergence theorem, (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we get

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w_{n}))-2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial(u_{n}-\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x\to\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w_{0}))-2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial(u-\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x.

By passing limit in (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{0}-\varphi}{h}(u-\varphi)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}(0)(u-\varphi)\,{\rm d}x-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w_{0}))-2}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial(u-\varphi)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla(u-\varphi)\,{\rm d}x\geq 0\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega).

Take
\varphi=u\mp\delta z
with
z\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
and
\delta>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\pm\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{0}-(u\mp\delta z)}{h}z\,{\rm d}x\right.

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}(0)z\,{\rm d}x-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial(u\mp\delta z)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w_{0}))-2}\frac{\partial(u\mp\delta z)}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\left.-\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u\mp\delta z)|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla(u\mp\delta z)\nabla z\,{\rm d}x\right)\geq 0.

Taking
\delta\to 0
, in above we have

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u-u_{0}}{h}z\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(w_{0}))-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u\nabla z\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}(0)z\,{\rm d}x.

Since,
z\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
was arbitrary, we conclude that
u
is indeed the weak solution of (
4.3
) with input
w_{0}
.

Since,
u_{w_{0}}
is the unique solution of (
4.3
) when the data is
w_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Thus from the uniqueness of solution, we have

u_{w_{0}}\equiv u\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

Finally, we have

w_{n}\to w_{0}\implies u_{n}=J(w_{n})\to J(w_{0})=u_{w_{0}},

establishing that
J
is continuous.

Hence, by Schauder’s fixed point theorem
J
has a fixed point, i.e., for given
\epsilon>0
, there exists
u_{\epsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

J(u_{\epsilon})=u_{\epsilon}.

Therefore,
u_{\epsilon}
solves the regularized problem (
4.3
) with the data
u_{\epsilon}
. That is, for all
v\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\epsilon}-u_{0}}{h}v\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{\epsilon}))-2}\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{p^{+}-2}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\nabla v\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}(0)v\,{\rm d}x.

(4.7)

Hence,
u_{\epsilon}\in W_{0}^{1,p^{+}}(\Omega)
is the solution of the problem (
4.2
).

Step 2: Passage to the limit

\epsilon\to 0
.

Choosing
v=u_{\epsilon}
as a test function in the variational formulation (
4.7
), we obtain the following energy estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2h}\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{\epsilon}))}\,{\rm d}x+\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{1}{2h}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}(0)u_{\epsilon}\,{\rm d}x.

(4.8)

To handle the right-hand side, we apply Young’s inequality for any
\delta>0

\|[f]_{h}(0)\|_{L^{(p^{+})^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}\leq\delta\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{p^{+}}(\Omega)}^{p^{+}}+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{1}{{p^{+}}-1}}}\|[f]_{h}(0)\|_{L^{(p^{+})^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{(p^{+})^{\prime}}.

(4.9)

Substituting (
4.9
) into (
4.8
) and choosing
\delta
small enough, we absorb the gradient term into the left-hand side. This yields the uniform bound

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{\epsilon}))}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{p^{+}}\,{\rm d}x<C_{2},

for some
C_{2}
independent of
\epsilon
.
This in turn implies a uniform bound

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{p^{-}}\,{\rm d}x<C.

By reflexivity of
W^{1,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists a function
u\in W^{1,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weakly in }W^{1,p^{-}}_{0}(\Omega),

u_{\epsilon}(x)\to u(x)\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

and, by continuity of the mappings
p_{i}\circ b
, we also have

p_{i}(b(u_{\epsilon}(x)))\to p_{i}(b(u(x)))\ \text{ a.e. in }\Omega,\text{ for all }i=1,\dots,N.

Define
p_{i,\epsilon}(x):=p_{i}(b(u_{\epsilon}(x)))
. Applying Lemma
2.2
to the sequence
\{u_{\epsilon}\}
and using the above pointwise convergence of variable exponents, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u))}\,{\rm d}x\leq\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{\epsilon}))}\,{\rm d}x\leq C.

Proceeding as in Steps 3 and 4 of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we conclude that
u
is the solution to (
4.1
) for
k=1
.

By repeating this procedure iteratively, we obtain that for a given
u_{k-1}\in W_{0}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(b(u))}(\Omega),
there exists
u_{k}\in W_{0}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(b(u))}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{k}-u_{k-1}}{h}v\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{k}))-2}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}((k-1)h)v\,{\rm d}x,\quad\forall v\in W_{0}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(b(u))}(\Omega).

(4.10)

∎

4.2
Time-step size
h\to 0

For each time step
h=\frac{T}{N_{0}}
, we define the piecewise constant function (in variable
t
)
u_{h}
as

u_{h}(x,t)=\begin{cases}u_{0}(x),&t=0,\\
u_{1}(x),&0<t\leq h,\\
\vdots&\\
u_{j}(x),&(j-1)h<t\leq jh,\\
\vdots&\\
u_{N_{0}}(x),&(N_{0}-1)h<t\leq N_{0}h=T.\end{cases}

Proof of the Theorem
1.2
:

Choosing
v=u_{k}
in (
4.10
), we obtain the energy estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2h}\int_{\Omega}u_{k}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{k}))}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{1}{2h}\int_{\Omega}u_{k-1}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\|[f]_{h}((k-1)h)\|_{L^{(p^{-})^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}.

(4.11)

To relate the norms, we use the following estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p^{-}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}^{p^{-}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|\geq 1}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{-}}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|<1}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p^{-}}\,{\rm d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|\geq 1}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{k}))}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|<1}\,{\rm d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{k}))}\,{\rm d}x+C.

By the Young’s inequality, for a given
\delta>0
, we have

\|[f]_{h}((k-1)h)\|_{L^{(p^{-})^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}\leq\delta\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{p^{-}}(\Omega)}^{p^{-}}+\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{1}{{p^{-}}-1}}}\|[f]_{h}((k-1)h)\|_{L^{(p^{-})^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{(p^{-})^{\prime}}.

(4.12)

Combining (
4.11
)-(
4.12
), and absorbing terms, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}u_{k}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{k}))}\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{k-1}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+Ch.

Summing over
k=1
to
N_{0}
, we deduce the uniform a priori estimate

\int_{\Omega}u_{h}^{2}(x,t)\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{k}))}\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\leq\int_{\Omega}u_{0}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+CT.

Hence, we obtain the uniform bounds

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{\partial u_{h}}{\partial x_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p_{i}(b(u_{h}))}(\Omega_{T})}+\|u_{h}\|_{L^{p^{-}}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,\vec{\textbf{p}}(b(u_{h}))}(\Omega))}\leq C.

Thus, we can extract a subsequence such that

\displaystyle u_{h}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weakly}-^{*}\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle u_{h}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weakly in }L^{p^{-}}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,p^{-}}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial u_{h}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{h}))-2}\frac{\partial u_{h}}{\partial x_{i}}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\xi_{i}\quad\text{in }L^{(p^{-})^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T}),\text{ for all }i=1,2,\ldots,N

where,
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{N})\in(L^{(p^{-})^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T}))^{N}
.
To prove
u
is a weak solution of problem (
1.2
), choose any
\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega_{T})
with
\varphi(\cdot,T)=0
and
\varphi|_{\Gamma}=0
. Take
\varphi(x,kh)
as test function in (
4.10
) to obtain for each

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\int_{\Omega}u_{k}(x)\varphi(x,kh)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{h}\int_{\Omega}u_{k-1}(x)\varphi(x,kh)\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{k}))-2}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\varphi(x,kh)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}((k-1)h)\varphi(x,kh)\,{\rm d}x.

Summing over
k=1
to
N_{0}
, and using the boundary condition
\varphi(\cdot,T)=0
, we derive

\displaystyle h\sum_{k=1}^{N_{0}-1}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{h}(x,kh)\frac{\varphi(x,kh)-\varphi(x,(k+1)h)}{h}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\varphi(x,h)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+h\sum_{k=1}^{N_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{h}(x,kh)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{h}(x,kh)))-2}\frac{\partial u_{h}(x,kh)}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\varphi(x,kh)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=h\sum_{k=1}^{N_{0}}\int_{\Omega}[f]_{h}((k-1)h)\varphi(x,kh)\,{\rm d}x.

Furthermore, we observe

\displaystyle h\sum_{k=1}^{N_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{h}(x,kh)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{h}(x,kh)))-2}\frac{\partial u_{h}(x,kh)}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\varphi(x,kh)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{h}(x,t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{h}(x,t)))-2}\frac{\partial u_{h}(x,t)}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\varphi(x,t)}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{(k-1)h}^{kh}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\partial u_{h}(x,t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u_{h}(x,t)))-2}\frac{\partial u_{h}(x,t)}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi(x,kh)}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial\varphi(x,t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\xi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\text{ as }h\to 0.

By smoothness of
\varphi
, as

, we recover

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t-\int_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\varphi(x,0)\,{\rm d}x-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\xi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t.

Following the method in
[
10
]
, we use monotonicity to conclude that
\xi_{i}=\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}(b(u))-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
, for all
i=1,2,\ldots,N
.
Also, proceeding as in Lemma
2.2
, it follows that

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{p_{i}(b(u))}(\Omega_{T}).

Choosing
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{T})
, we get

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}u\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\xi\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t.

Hence,
u_{t}\in X(\Omega_{T})^{*}
. From the fact that

and

, it follows from standard theory
(see, for instance,
[

,
21
]
) that

This completes the proof. ∎

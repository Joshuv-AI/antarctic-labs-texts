Based morphisms for characters of quantum symmetric pairs
Stein Meereboer

Abstract
. We study based one-dimensional modules of quantum symmetric pairs over the field ℚ ⁡ ( q ) \mathbb{Q}(q) . We provide a complete classification of one-dimensional 𝐁 \mathbf{B} -modules that appear as submodules of simple finite-dimensional based 𝐔 \mathbf{U} -modules and determine the corresponding branching rules. The main result of this paper shows that the corresponding projections are morphisms of based 𝐁 \mathbf{B} -modules. To this end we characterize one-dimensional modules at q = ∞ q=\infty , thus developing a ı \imath crystal basis theory for these modules. This is then applied to show compatibility with the integral forms of the (dual-)canonical basis.

Quantum symmetric pairs

The theory of quantum symmetric pairs, as developed by Letzter, provides quantum analogs of the pairs of universal enveloping algebras

]

Quantum groups and and based modules

2.1.
Quantum groups

We introduce notation of quantum groups as in
[
13
]
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan matrix
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
. Let
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_{i}\,:\,d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\,i\in I)
be a symmetrizer, meaning that
DC
is symmetric and
\gcd\{d_{i}\,:\,i\in I\}=1
. We fix a set of simple roots
\Pi=\{\alpha_{i}\,:\,i\in I\}
and a set of simple coroots
\Pi^{\vee}=\{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\,:\,i\in I\}
with corresponding root and coroot systems
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\vee}
. The root lattice is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}I=\oplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
. The root lattice is equipped with the normalized Killing form
(\,,\,)
, scaled so that short roots have squared length
2
. The Weyl group
W
is generated by the simple reflections
s_{i}:\mathbb{Z}I\to\mathbb{Z}I
, for
i\in I
defined by
s_{i}(\alpha_{j})=\alpha_{j}-c_{ij}\alpha_{i},
for
j\in I
.

Let
q
be an indeterminate, and let
\mathbb{Q}(q)
denote the field of rational functions in
q
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\overline{\,\cdot\,}:\mathbb{Q}(q)\to\mathbb{Q}(q)
be the unique
\mathbb{Q}
-linear automorphism such that
q
is mapped to
q^{-1}
. For each
i\in I
and

, set

q_{i}:=q^{d_{i}},\qquad[n]_{i}:=\cfrac{q_{i}^{n}-q_{i}^{-n}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad[n]_{i}!:=\prod_{k=1}^{n}[k]_{i}.

Recall
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
and let
\mathbf{A}_{\infty}=\mathbb{Q}(q)\cap\mathbb{Q}[[q^{-1}]]
, the latter consists of all rational functions regular at
q=\infty
.

Let
(Y,X,\langle\,,\,\rangle.\dots)
be a root datum of type
(I,\,(\,,\,))
, cf.
[
13
,
2.2
]
. By definition, there are embeddings
I\to X
,
i\mapsto\alpha_{i}
and

. The pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle
and the form
(\,,\,)
are related by the formula
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}
, for

. The lattice

is partially ordered by the rule

\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}I.

Definition 2.1

(Drinfel’d Jimbo quantum group)
.

The
quantum group

\mathbf{U}
, associated to the root datum, is the unital associative algebra over
\mathbb{Q}(q)
, generated by the symbols
E_{i},F_{i}
for
i\in I
and
K_{h}
for
h\in Y
. These generators satisfy the following relations for
i,j\in I,
and
h,h_{1},h_{2}\in Y

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle K_{h_{1}}K_{h_{2}}

\displaystyle=K_{h_{1}+h_{2}},

\displaystyle K_{h}E_{i}

\displaystyle=q^{\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle}E_{i}K_{h},

\displaystyle K_{h}F_{i}

\displaystyle=q^{-\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle}F_{i}K_{h},

\displaystyle E_{i}F_{j}-F_{j}E_{i}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}\cfrac{K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},

as well as the quantum Serre relations for

,

\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=1-a_{i,j}}(-1)^{s}E_{i}^{(r)}E_{j}E_{i}^{(s)}=\sum_{r+s=1-a_{i,j}}(-1)^{s}F_{i}^{(r)}F_{j}F_{i}^{(s)}=0.

Here we use the notation

K_{i}:=K_{d_{i}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}},\qquad E_{i}^{(n)}:=\frac{1}{[n]_{i}!}E_{i}^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{i}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{[n]_{i}!}F_{i}^{n}.

The quantum group
\mathbf{U}
has the structure of a Hopf-algebra
(\mathbf{U},\triangle,\epsilon,\iota,S)
, where we take the conventions of the Hopf-algebra structure as given in
[
7
,
Ch 4
]
.
We use
Sweedler notation
to denote the coproduct, writing
\triangle(x)=x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)}\in\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{U}
. The
bar involution

\overline{\,\cdot\,}:\mathbf{U}\to\mathbf{U}
is the unique
\mathbb{Q}
-linear automorphism with

E_{i}\mapsto E_{i},\quad F_{i}\mapsto F_{i},\quad K_{i}\mapsto K_{i}^{-1},\quad q\mapsto q^{-1},\quad\text{for all }i\in I,

this is well-defined by
[
13
, §3.1.12]
.
Set
{}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{U}
equal to the
\mathcal{A}
-subalgebra generated
K_{h}
with
h\in Y
and by all divided powers

F^{(a)}_{i}=F^{a}_{i}/[a]_{i}!
,
E^{(a)}_{i}=E^{a}_{i}/[a]_{i}!
for
a\geq 0
and
i\in I
.

(iii)

Definition 3.2

(Admissible pair)

An

(I_{\bullet},\tau)

and an involution

(ii)

(iii)

.

Here

We only consider admissible pairs that are

i,j\in I

a sequence

such that for each
k=1,...,r-1

a_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}\neq 0

Definition 3.3

(Rank, rank one admissible pair)

The

Let

We fix the following notation as in

The abelian group

, and the abelian group

\imath

. We denote
\overline{\,\cdot\,}:X\to X_{\imath}

The pairing between

Define

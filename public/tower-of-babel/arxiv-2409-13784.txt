Introduction

In this paper, all graphs are assumed to be finite and simple.
Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)=\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)
.
The complement of
G
is denoted by
G^{c}
.
The
adjacency matrix
of
G

A(G)
, is the
n\times n
matrix such that the
(i,j)
-entry is
1
if
\nu_{i}
and
\nu_{j}
are adjacent, and is
0
otherwise.
The
eigenvalues
of

A(G)
.
The
line graph

L(G)
of

is the graph with vertex set
V(L(G)):=E(G)
in which two vertices are adjacent if and only if their corresponding edges are adjacent, i.e., they are incident to a common vertex in
G
.

A fundamental subject in graph theory is the study of the spectral gap of a regular graph
G

G
(see
[
7
,
3
,
5
,
1
]

[
2
]
.
A

graph is a connected
k
-regular graph
G

\lambda^{*}(G)\leq 2\sqrt{k-1}
, where
\lambda^{*}(G)
denotes the largest absolute value of an eigenvalue of
G
distinct from
k
or
-k

[
10
]
.
To construct a Ramanujan graph, some explicit methods are known, for example, we refer the reader to
[
10
,
11
,
9
,
12
,
8
,
4
]
. These methods are achieved from concepts in linear algebra, number theory, representation theory and the theory of automorphic forms. In this paper, we wish to present a simple combinatorial method to construct a Ramanujan graph.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every connected regular graph
G
with at least
5
vertices, the graph
\mathcal{R}(G):=L(L(G)^{c})^{c}
is a Ramanujan graph with diameter at most three.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
G
be a connected regular graph with at least
5
vertices. Set
\mathcal{R}^{(1)}(G):=\mathcal{R}(G)
, and for every positive integer

\mathcal{R}^{(n)}(G):=L(\mathcal{R}^{(n-1)}(G))^{c}

(\mathcal{R}^{(n)}(G))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

Ramanujan graphs with diameter at most three

For a
k
-regular graph
G
with
n
vertices and
m
edges, it is vell-known
[
6

P_{L(G)}(x)=(x+2)^{m-n}P_{G}(x-k+2),

where
P_{G}(x)
and
P_{L(G)}(x)
are the characteristic polynomials of
G
and
L(G)
, respectively. Also we know
[
6

P_{G^{c}}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}P_{G}(-x-1)(x-n+k+1)}{x+1+k}.

Now we are ready to prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Suppose
G
is a connected
k
-regular graph with
n\geq 5
vertices. It is clear that
\mathcal{R}(G)
is a graph with
m:=\frac{1}{8}nk(nk-4k+2)
vertices. Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{m}
be the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{R}(G)
. Then we can see that

d:=\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{8}(nk-8)(nk-4k+2)+1

and

\lambda_{2}=1.

It is clear that
1
is the second largest eigenvalue of
L(G)^{c}
. Thus
\frac{1}{2}nk-2k
is the second largest eigenvalue of
L(L(G)^{c})
. Therefore

\lambda_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}nk+2k-1.

Since
\mathcal{R}(G)
is a
d
-regular graph and
d>\lambda_{2}
, using
[
6
, Theorem 1.2.1]
and
[
13
, Theorem 2.6.2]
, we deduce that the graph
\mathcal{R}(G)
is a connected graph with diameter at most 3. It is obvious that

\lambda^{*}(G)=\mathrm{max}\{|\lambda_{i}||\,2\leq i\leq m\}=\frac{1}{2}nk-2k+1.

Hence

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{d-1}-\lambda^{*}(G)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(nk-8)(nk-4k+2)}-\frac{1}{2}(nk-4k+2)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{2}(nk-4k+2)(\frac{1}{2}nk+2k-9)}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}(nk-8)(nk-4k+2)}+\frac{1}{2}(nk-4k+2)}

\displaystyle\geq\ 0.

Thus
\mathcal{R}(G)

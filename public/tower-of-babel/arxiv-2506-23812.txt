The Kodaira classification of the moduli space of pointed curves in genus 3 3
Ruben de Preter

Abstract
. We complete the Kodaira classification of the moduli spaces ℳ ¯ g , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n} of curves with marked points in genus g = 3 g=3 , by proving that ℳ ¯ 3 , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n} is of general type for n ≥ 15 n\geq 15 . We prove that the singularities of ℳ ¯ 3 , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n} impose no adjunction conditions for n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 and that the canonical class of ℳ ¯ 3 , n \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n} is big for n ≥ 15 n\geq 15 . Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 14H10, 14E08.



1.
Introduction

The birational geometry of moduli spaces of curves
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
has a rich and fascinating history.
It was discovered by Severi in
1915
that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is unirational for low genus
g\leq 10

[
Sev
]
. For growing
g
the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
becomes more complex from a birational viewpoint, which became apparent in the 1980’s when Eisenbud, Harris and Mumford proved that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is of general type for
g\geq 24
, see
[
EH
,
Ha
,
HM
]
.
Since then, it has been a famous open problem to compute the Kodaira dimension of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
in the remaining cases, and more generally to compute the Kodaira dimension of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
.
A lot of progress has been made towards a full Kodaira classification of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
.
For
g
and
n
low
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
tends to be uniruled, see
[
AB
,
CF
]
and the references therein. In the other direction
Farkas, Jensen, and Payne proved recently that also
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{22}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{23}
are of general type, see
[
FJP
]
.
For each genus
4\leq g\leq 21
Logan determined in
[
Lo
]
an integer
n(g)
such that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
is of general type for
n\geq n(g)
.
The value of
n(g)
for which this holds has been lowered for several
g
by Farkas in
[
Fa
]
.
For genus
g\leq 2
the Kodaira classification of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
is complete, see
[
BF
,
BMS
]
.

In genus
g=3
much less is known. The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
is rational for
n\leq 14

[
CF
]
, and these are the only cases in genus
3
where the Kodaira dimension is known.
The main goal of the current paper is to complete the Kodaira classification in genus
3
:

Theorem 1.1
.

The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}
is of general type for
n\geq 15
.

Theorem
1.1
confirms in genus
3
the expectation that for
g\geq 2
there are only finitely many values
(g,n)
for which
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
is not of general type, see
[
BMS
]
and
[
HM
,
Lo
]
for the cases
g=2
and
g\geq 4
respectively.

There is a standard approach, used already in
[
HM
]
and
[
Lo
]
, to prove that the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
is of general type. We will roughly follow this approach, and we outline the key steps below.

The first step is proving that the canonical divisor of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
is big, i.e. a sum of an ample and an effective class. This step is intimately related to the computation of classes of effective divisors in the Picard group.
In the current paper we will use effective divisor classes computed in
[
HM
,
Fa
]
to study the bigness of the canonical class
{K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}}}
.

As a first step towards Theorem
1.1
, we prove in Section
5
the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

The canonical class
K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}
is big for
n\geq 15
.

The fact that the canonical class
K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}}
is big, is not yet sufficient to prove that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
is of general type. The problem is that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
is usually singular, whereas the Kodaira dimension is defined as the Iitaka-dimension of the canonical class of a smooth projective model.

The second step overcomes this obstruction by proving that the singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
impose no adjunction conditions. This means that any pluri-canonical form defined on the regular locus of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
lifts to a pluri-canonical form on a resolution of singularities
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

For the second step, Harris and Mumford proved in
[
HM
, Theorem 1]
that the singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
impose no adjunction conditions for
g\geq 4
.
Logan extended this in
[
Lo
, Theorem 2.5]
, by proving that the singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
impose no adjunction conditions for
g\geq 4
.
In genus
3
, a thorough analysis of the singularities is not yet available. A large part of the current paper is devoted to carrying out such analysis, and the result is:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
n\geq 1
.
The singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
impose no adjunction conditions.
In particular there is an isomorphism

{\rm{H}}^{0}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}\left(mK_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}\right)\right)\simeq{\rm{H}}^{0}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}\left(mK_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}\right)\right)

and
{\rm{Kod}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}\right)=\kappa\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}},K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}}\right)
.

Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on a detailed understanding of the singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
.
Singularities of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
occur due to non-trivial autormorphisms on the underlying pointed curves being parameterized. This results in finite quotient singularities on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
.
The Reid-Shepherd-Barron-Tai criterion
2.2
provides a way to control the locus of non-canonical singularities for varieties with finite quoteint singularities, and we will use the criterion to classify the locus of non-canonical singularities in Corollary
3.5
.
The classical version of the criterion gives no information about
how bad
the non-canonical singularities are, if there are any.
In Subsection
2.2
we describe several variants of the Reid-Tai criterion, which allow us to prove that some pluri-canonical forms lift to a resolution of singularities, even around the non-canonical locus. The result is:

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
\eta
be a global
m
-canonical form on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
. Suppose that the order of vanishing of
\eta
along the divisor
\Delta_{1,\emptyset}
is at least
m
. Then
\eta
lifts to a resolution of singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
.

In Section
4
, we use a test curve to identify a rigid components of the canonical divisor:

Proposition 1.5
.

For
n\geq 1
, the pluri-canonical divisor
mK_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}}
has rigid component
4m\Delta_{1,\emptyset}
. In particular, every global
m
-canonical form on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
vanishes along
\Delta_{1,\emptyset}
to order at least
4m
.

Theorem
1.3
follows from Proposition
1.4
and Proposition
1.5
.

Remark 1.6
.

In the seminal paper
[
HM
]
, Harris and Mumford explicitly construct a resolution of singularities in a neighborhood of the locus of non-canonical singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
for
g\geq 4
, and use this resolution to prove that the singularities impose no adjunction condition.
This is the main part where our argument differs from the original argument.
We use a finer version of the Reid-Tai criterion, due to
[
CCM
]
, to control the adjunction conditions imposed by the non-canonical singularities. Then we use a test curve to identify a rigid component, whereby the adjunction conditions are satisfied by all global pluri-canonical forms.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Divisors on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}

The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
has finite quotient singularities, and hence is normal and
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. In particular, we identify
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles and
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor classes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.
The morphism
\overline{\rm{M}}_{{g},{n}}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
, from the moduli stack of curves to the coarse moduli space, induces a map between Picard groups

\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}\right)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}\left(\overline{\rm{M}}_{{g},{n}}\right),

which becomes an isomorphism after base change to
\mathbb{Q}
:

\operatorname{Pic}{Q}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}\right)\simeq\operatorname{Pic}{Q}\left(\overline{\rm{M}}_{{g},{n}}\right).

Therefore we will identify
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
and
\overline{\rm{M}}_{{g},{n}}
.

The divisor
\Delta_{irr}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is defined as the locus of pointed curves obtained by gluing together two points on a stable
(n+2)
-pointed curve of genus
g-1
to a node. The general point in
\Delta_{irr}
is irreducible, hence the notation.
Let
i\in\{0,1,...,g\}
and let
S\subseteq\left\{1,...,n\right\}
. The divisor
\Delta_{i,S}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is defined as the locus of pointed curves with a separating node that separates the curve into two components of genus
i
and
g-i
, such that the marked points on the genus
i
components are exactly those indexed by
S
.
For this to be well defined we have to assume that
S
has at least
2
elements if
i=0
and at most
n-2
elements if
i=g
.
The union of the divisors
\Delta_{irr}
and
\Delta_{i,S}
is the boundary
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}\backslash\mathcal{M}_{{g},{n}}
.
Note that

\Delta_{i,S}=\Delta_{g-i,\left\{1,...,n\right\}\backslash S}.

We use the notations
\delta_{irr}=[\Delta_{irr}]
and

\delta_{i,S}=\begin{cases}\frac{\left[\Delta_{i,S}\right]}{2}\text{ if }(i,S)=(1,\emptyset),\\
[\Delta_{i,S}]\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

to denote the classes in the Picard group of the boundary divisors on the moduli stack
\overline{\rm{M}}_{{g},{n}}
.
The reason for dividing by
2
in the definition of
\delta_{1,\emptyset}
is that the general curve in
\Delta_{1,\emptyset}
has
2
automorphisms.
We denote

\delta=\delta_{irr}+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{g}{2}\rfloor}\delta_{i},\,\,\,\,\delta_{i}=\sumop\displaylimits_{S\subseteq\left\{1,...,n\right\}}\delta_{i,S},

where the latter sum is indexed by all subsets
S\subseteq\left\{1,...,n\right\}
that satisfy
|S|\geq 2
if
i=0
, and
n\notin S
if
i=\frac{g}{2}
(this last conditions makes sure that the divisor
\delta_{\frac{g}{2},S}=\delta_{\frac{g}{2},\left\{1,...,n\right\}\backslash S}
appears only once in the sum).

Let
\rho:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\overline{\rm{M}}_{{g},{n}}
be the universal family with markings
\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{n}:\overline{\rm{M}}_{{g},{n}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
, and let
\omega_{\rho}
be the relative dualizing sheaf of
\rho
.
The Hodge class
\lambda
and the
\psi
-classes
\psi_{1},...,\psi_{n}
are defined as

\lambda=c_{1}(\rho_{*}\omega_{\rho})\text{ and }\psi_{j}=c_{1}\left(\sigma_{j}^{*}(\omega_{\rho})\right)\in\operatorname{Pic}{Q}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}\right)

Theorem 2.1

(
[
AC
, Proposition 1]
)
.

Suppose that
g\geq 3
. The rational Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}{Q}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
has a basis given by the classes
\lambda,\psi_{1},...,\psi_{n},\delta_{irr}
and
\left\{\delta_{i,S}\right\}_{i,S}
.

The standard way to compute the canonical divisor on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
is to first compute it on the stack using Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch, and then study the ramification of the stack-to-coarse map to obtain the canonical class on the coarse moduli space.
For
g\geq 1
and
g+n\geq 4
the result is

(2.1)

K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}=13\lambda+\psi_{1}+...+\psi_{n}-2\delta-\delta_{1,\emptyset},

as proven in
[
HM
, Section 2]
for
n=0
, and in
[
ACG
, Chapter 13, Theorem 7.16]
for the general case.

2.2.
Local description of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
.

Let
[C,p_{1},...,p_{n}]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be a point in the coarse moduli space. The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(C,p_{1},...,p_{n})
acts on the space of first order infinitesimal deformations

V=\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}(p_{1}+...+p_{n})\right)^{*}.

There exists an analytic neighborhood of
[C,p_{1},...,p_{n}]
in the moduli space that is isomorphic to a neighborhood of the origin in the quotient

\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}(p_{1}+...+p_{n})\right)^{*}/\operatorname{Aut}(C,p_{1},...,p_{n}).

We now recall some facts related to this quotient, that we will need later.
Let
\tilde{C}_{1},...,\tilde{C}_{\nu}
be the irreducible components of
C
and let
\pi_{\alpha}:C_{\alpha}\longrightarrow\tilde{C}_{\alpha}
be the normalization. Let
α
be the cotangent sheaf on
C_{\alpha}
.
Pulling back differentials gives a morphism of sheaves on
C
,

(2.2)

{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}\left(p_{1}+...+p_{n}\right)\longrightarrow\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\pi_{\alpha*}{}_{\alpha}^{\otimes 2}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Q}Q\right),

where
Q
ranges over all special points of
C_{\alpha}
, i.e. points of
C_{\alpha}
that are mapped to a node or marked point.
The morphism (
2.2
) is an isomorphism away from the nodes of
C
.
If
P
is a node of
C
locally given by the equation
xy=0
, then
C
is generated around
P
by the differentials
dx,dy
subject to the relation
xdy+ydx=0
, and
\omega_{C}\left(p_{1}+...+p_{n}\right)
is locally generated by the section
\eta
which is
\frac{dx}{x}
on the branch
y=0
and
-\frac{dy}{y}
on the branch
x=0
.
From this we see that (
2.2
) is surjective at
P
, with kernel generated by the torsion differential
\tau_{P}=ydx\otimes\eta=-xdy\otimes\eta
. Therefore we get a short exact sequence of sheaves

(2.3)

0\longrightarrow\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{P\in C\text{ is a node}}{\mathbb C}_{P}\longrightarrow{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}\left(p_{1}+...+p_{n}\right)\longrightarrow\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\pi_{\alpha*}{}_{\alpha}^{\otimes 2}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Q}Q\right)\longrightarrow 0,

where the map
{\mathbb C}_{P}\longrightarrow{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}\left(p_{1}+...+p_{n}\right)
sends
a\in{\mathbb C}
to
a\tau_{P}
.
The element
\tau_{P}
is called a smoothing parameter for
P
.
Viewing vectors in
\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}(p_{1},...,p_{n})\right)
as linear functionals on
V
, the subspace of
V
where
\tau_{P}
is zero parametrizes deformations of
(C,p_{1},...,p_{n})
in which
P
remains a node.

2.3.
The Reid-Shepherd-Barron-Tai criterion

Let
G
be a finite group that acts linearly on a complex vectorspace
V
of dimension
d
. Let
g\in G
. Since
g
has finite order, its action on
V
can be diagonalized and the eigenvalues are roots of unity. Suppose
g
has eigenvalues
e^{2\pi ir_{1}},...,e^{2\pi ir_{d}}
where
0\leq r_{j}<1
.
The
age
of
g
on
V
is defined as

\operatorname{age}_{V}(g)=\operatorname{age}(g)=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{d}r_{j}.

An element
g\in G
is called
junior
if
0<\operatorname{age}(g)<1
and it is called a
quasi-reflection
if exactly one of the eigenvalues of
g
is not
1
. The classical version of the Reid-Shepherd-Barron-Tai criterion states:

Proposition 2.2

(
[
Ta
, Theorem 3.3]
,
[
Re
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Suppose that
G
has no quasi-reflections.
Then
V/G
has canonical singularities if and only if
G
has no junior elements.

A limitation of the Reid-Shepherd-Barron-Tai criterion is that the criterion gives no information about
how bad
the non-canonical singularities are.
Even when
V/G
has non-canonical singularities, some pluri-canonical forms will lift to a resolution of singularities.
In what follows, we describe a condition, due to
[
CCM
]
, such that pluri-canonical forms on
V/G
that satisfy the condition lift to a resolution of singularities.
The first step is to reduce to the case where
G
is cyclic.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
Ta
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\eta
be a pluri-canonical form defined on a neighborhood of the origin in
V/G
. Then
\eta
lifts to a resolution of singularities if and only if the pull-back of
\eta
by
V/\langle g\rangle\longrightarrow V/G
lifts to a resolution of singularities for all
g\in G
.

In particular, if
V/\langle g\rangle
has canonical singularities for all
g\in G
, then
V/G
has canonical singularities.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
CCM
, Corollary 5.5]
)
.

Let
g=\operatorname{diag}(e^{2\pi ir_{1}},...,e^{2\pi ir_{d}})
be an element of finite order
k
acting on
{\mathbb C}^{d}
and suppose that
\langle g\rangle
has no quasi-reflections.
Let
D_{j}\subseteq{\mathbb C}^{d}
be the locus where the
j
-th coordinate is zero.
Let
\eta
be an
m
-canonical form defined in a neighborhood of the origin of
{\mathbb C}^{d}/\langle g\rangle
and for
j=1,...,d
let
b_{j}
be the order of vanishing of
\eta
along
D_{j}
.
Then
\eta
extends to a resolution of singularities if

(2.4)

\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{d}(b_{j}+m)\left\{lr_{j}\right\}\geq m\text{ for }l=1,...,k-1,

where
\{x\}
denotes the fractional part of a real number
x
.

Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.4
assume that there are no quasi-reflections in
G
and
\langle g\rangle
respectively.
We now describe how to deal with quasi-reflection in the case where
G=\langle g\rangle
is cyclic. Suppose
g=\operatorname{diag}(e^{2\pi ir_{1}},...,e^{2\pi ir_{d}})
acts on
{\mathbb C}^{d}
with order
k
. Let
x_{1},...,x_{d}
be the standard coordinates on
{\mathbb C}^{d}
. For
j\in\left\{1,...,d\right\}
fixed, let
m_{j}
be the minimal positive integer such that
g^{m_{j}}
fixes
x_{i}
for all
i\neq j
.
Then
H=\langle g^{m_{1}},...,g^{m_{d}}\rangle
is the subgroup of
\langle g\rangle
generated by all quasi-reflections. The quotient
{\mathbb C}^{d}/H
is isomorphic to affine space with coordinates
y_{j}=x_{j}^{\lambda_{j}}
for
j=1,...,d
, where
\lambda_{j}=\frac{k}{m_{j}}
,
and
G/H
acts linearly on
{\mathbb C}^{d}/H
without quasi-reflections.
More precisely, the generator
gH
of
G/H
acts on
{\mathbb C}^{d}/H
with eigenvalues
e^{2\pi i\lambda_{1}r_{1}},...,e^{2\pi i\lambda_{d}r_{d}}
.
We can then apply Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.4
to study the lifting of pluri-canonical forms on

{\mathbb C}^{d}/G\simeq\left({\mathbb C}^{d}/H\right)/\left(G/H\right).

The following examples illustrate the theory above. These examples will appear in the analysis of the singularities of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
, when considering automorphism of curves with an elliptic tails with
j
-invariant
1728
and
0
respectively.

Example 2.5
.

Let
G
be cyclic of order
4
, generated by
g=\operatorname{diag}(\pm i,-1,\pm 1,...,\pm 1)
. We will show that
{\mathbb C}^{d}/G
has canonical singularities. Note that
g^{2}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,...,1)
is a quasi-reflection. The element
\bar{g}
acts on
{\mathbb C}^{d}/\langle g^{2}\rangle
with eigenvalues
-1,-1,\pm 1,...,\pm 1
. Therefore the action of
G/\langle g^{2}\rangle=\left\{\bar{1},\bar{g}\right\}
on
{\mathbb C}^{d}/\langle g^{2}\rangle
has no junior elements.
By Proposition
2.2
,
{\mathbb C}^{d}/G
has canonical singularities.

Example 2.6
.

Let
G
be cyclic of order
6
, generated by
g=\operatorname{diag}(\pm\zeta_{6},\zeta_{6}^{2},\pm 1,...,\pm 1)
, where
\zeta_{6}
is a primitive sixth root of unity.
We will show that any
m
-canonical form
\eta
on
{\mathbb C}^{d}/G
with order of vanishing at least
m
along the divisor
D_{1}=\left\{x_{1}=0\right\}
extends to a resolution of singularities.
We consider three cases.

Case 1:

g=\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_{6},\zeta_{6}^{2},1,...,1)
. Then
g^{3}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,...,1)
is a quasi-reflection. The element
\bar{g}
acts on
{\mathbb C}^{d}/\langle g^{3}\rangle
with eigenvalues
\zeta_{6}^{2},\zeta_{6}^{2},1,...,1
. Then
G/\langle g^{3}\rangle
has junior elements. However, condition (
2.4
) is satisfied with
b_{1}\geq m
. Hence
\eta
lifts to a resolution of singularities.

Case 2:

g=\operatorname{diag}(-\zeta_{6},\zeta_{6}^{2},-1,...,1)
. Then
g^{3}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,...,1)
is a quasi-reflection. The element
\bar{g}
acts on
{\mathbb C}^{d}/\langle g^{3}\rangle
with eigenvalues
-\zeta_{6},\zeta_{6}^{2},1,...,1
. Then
G/\langle g^{3}\rangle
has no junior elements, and hence
{\mathbb C}^{d}/\langle g^{3}\rangle
has canonical singularities.

Case 3:
In the remaining cases, one checks that
G
has no junior elements, and therefore
{\mathbb C}^{d}/G
has canonical singularities.

The variants of the Reid-Shepherd-Barron-Tai criterion above, will allow us to show that certain pluri-canonical forms on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
lift to a resolution of singularities on the complement of a high codimension locus (for details, see the proof of Proposition
3.4
).
The following result will allow us to deal with the remaining points.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
HM
, Appendix 1 to Section 1]
)
.

Let
\eta
be a pluri-canonical form defined in a neighborhood of the origin in
V/G
.
Suppose that for all junior elements
g\in G
there exists a vector
v\in V
fixed by
g
such that
\eta
extends to a resolution of singularities of a neighborhood of
[v]\in V/G
. Then
\eta
lifts to a resolution of singularities of a neighborhood of the origin of
V/G
.

3.
Singularities on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}

The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
1.4
.
The first step in the singularity analysis is to study the age of automorphisms of pointed curves acting on the space of first order infinitesimal deformations, in order to apply the Reid-Shepherd-Barron-Tai criterion.
Following
[
HM
, Section 1]
we first study the age of automorphisms of smooth curves.
In the next lemma, we allow the automorphism to swap points in pairs. These pairs will correspond to pairs of inverse images of a node of a stable curve.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
[C,p_{1},...,p_{r},q_{1},...,q_{2s}]\in\mathcal{M}_{g,r+2s}
be a smooth
r+2s
-pointed curve of genus
g
and suppose that
r+2s>0
.
Let
\varphi
be a non-trivial automorphism of
C
that fixes the points
p_{i}
for
i=1,...,r
and swaps the points
q_{2j-1},q_{2j}
for

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
[C,p_{1},...,p_{n}]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}
and let
\varphi
be a non-trivial automorphism of the pointed curve
(C,p_{1},...,p_{n})
. Consider the age of
\varphi
on the vectorspace

V=\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}(p_{1}+...+p_{n})\right).

Then either
\operatorname{age}(\varphi)\geq 1
, or
\varphi
lifts to a deformation of
(C,p_{1},...,p_{n})
with fewer nodes, or
n=0
and
(C,\varphi)
is hyperelliptic, or
C=C^{\prime}\cup E
has an elliptic tail
E
without marked points,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{C^{\prime}}}=\operatorname{id}
, and
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{E}}
has order
2,4
or
6
. Furthermore, in the elliptic tail case,
\varphi
has at most
2
non-trivial eigenvalues on
V
.

Proof.

Suppose
C
is smooth. By Proposition
3.1
, we have that either
\operatorname{age}(\varphi)\geq 1
, or
n=0
and
(C,\varphi)
is hyperelliptic.
Thus we may assume that
C
is not smooth.
We will prove the proposition by splitting in many cases.
Let
\tilde{C}_{1},...,\tilde{C}_{\nu}
be the irreducible components of
C
and let
C_{\alpha}\longrightarrow\tilde{C}_{\alpha}
be the normalization. Let
g_{\alpha}
be the genus of
C_{\alpha}
, let
\delta_{\alpha}
the number of special points of
C_{\alpha}
, and let
\delta
be the number of nodes of
C
. Note that by stability of

we have
2g_{\alpha}-3+\delta_{\alpha}\geq 0
for all
\alpha=1,...,\nu
. From the equality

3=g=\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha}g_{\alpha}+\delta+1-\nu

we deduce that

(3.1)

\nu\leq\nu+\sumop\displaylimits_{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}=2g-2+n=4+n,

where
\Delta_{\alpha}=2g_{\alpha}-3+\delta_{\alpha}
.
Note also that each component of
C
has a node, because
C
is not smooth. In particular
\delta_{\alpha}\geq 1
for each
\alpha
.
A fundamental tool in the analysis of the eigenvalues of
\varphi
on
V=\mathrm{H}^{0}\left({}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}\left(p_{1}+...+p_{n}\right)\right)
is the short exact sequence

(3.2)

0\longrightarrow\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{P}\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{P}\right)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}\left(p_{1}+...+p_{n}\right)\right)\longrightarrow\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha}\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{}_{\alpha}^{\otimes 2}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Q}Q\right)\right)\longrightarrow 0,

obtained from (
2.3
) by taking global sections.
The eigenvalues of
\varphi
on
V
are the union of the eigenvalues of
\varphi
on the left and right terms of (
3.2
).

Following
[
HM
]
we start with a study of the eigenvalues of
\varphi
on the vectorspace
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{P}\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{P}\right)
.
Let
P\in C
be a node and let
m=m_{P}
be the length of the orbit of
P
under
\varphi
.
This means that
P,\varphi P,...,\varphi^{m-1}P
are distinct, but
\varphi^{m}P=P
. Then
\varphi^{m}
acts on
\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{P}\right)\simeq{\mathbb C}
. Take a nonzero element
e\in\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{P}\right)
, corresponding to a smoothing parameter for
P
.

Suppose that
\varphi^{m}
acts as the identity on
\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{P}\right)
. Consider the nonzero vector

\bar{e}=e+\varphi e+\cdots+\varphi^{m-1}e\in\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{P^{\prime}}\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{P^{\prime}}\right)\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{}_{C}\otimes\omega_{C}\left(p_{1}+...+p_{n}\right)\right).

Then
\varphi\bar{e}=\bar{e}
and therefore
\bar{e}
induces a deformation with fewer nodes to which
\varphi
lifts. So in this case we are done.

From now on we may therefore assume that
\varphi^{m_{P}}
acts non-trivially on
\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{P}\right)
for any node
P
. In this case we have

\varphi^{m}e=\zeta^{ml}e

for some
l\in\left\{1,...,\frac{N}{m}-1\right\}
, where
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N}
and
N=\operatorname{ord}(\varphi)
.
For each

, consider the nonzero vector

\bar{e}_{a}=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{m-1}\zeta^{-aj}\varphi^{j}e\in\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{\varphi^{j}P}\right)

We compute

\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi\bar{e}_{a}=&\sumop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{m-1}\zeta^{-aj}\varphi^{j+1}e\\
=&\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m-1}\zeta^{-a(j-1)}\varphi^{j}e+\zeta^{-a(m-1)}\varphi^{m}e\\
=&\zeta^{a}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m-1}\zeta^{-aj}\varphi^{j}e+\zeta^{-am}\varphi^{m}e\right)\\
=&\zeta^{a}\left(\zeta^{(-a+l)m}e+\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{m-1}\zeta^{-aj}\varphi^{j}e\right).\end{array}

Therefore
\bar{e}_{a}
is an eigenvector of
\varphi
with eigenvalue
\zeta^{a}
whenever

, i.e. whenever

\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C,{\mathbb C}_{\varphi^{j}P}\right)

.
This implies that

when

. If

\operatorname{age}(\varphi)\geq 1
. The only cases left to consider are:

(a)

fixes all nodes of

(b)

\varphi
swaps two nodes of

(in this case we have a contribution of at least

from these two nodes).

Now we investigate the eigenvalues of
\varphi
on
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\alpha}\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C_{\alpha},{}_{\alpha}^{\otimes 2}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Q}Q\right)\right)

Let

be the length of the orbit of

under

.
Then

\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C_{\alpha},{}_{\alpha}^{\otimes 2}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Q}Q\right)\right)

The space
\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(C_{\alpha},{}_{\alpha}^{\otimes 2}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Q}Q\right)\right)

If

\zeta^{l_{i}+j\frac{m}{n}},i\in\left\{1,...,g_{\alpha}+\Delta_{\alpha}\right\},j\in\left\{0,...,m-1\right\}

\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(\varphi^{j}C_{\alpha},{}_{\varphi^{j}C_{\alpha}}^{\otimes 2}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{Q}Q\right)\right)

.

.

.

and

,

K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}}=\frac{13}{12}\kappa_{1}-\frac{1}{12}(\psi_{1}+...+\psi_{n})-\frac{11}{12}\delta-\delta_{1,\emptyset}.

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

We apply Lemma
4.1
to the curve class
\gamma
and the divisors
mK_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}}
and
\Delta_{1,\emptyset}
. The intersection numbers are easily computed with Lemma
4.2
and Equation (
4.2
):

\begin{array}[]{rl}\gamma\cdot mK_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}}=&-\frac{2m}{3}\\
\gamma\cdot\Delta_{1,\emptyset}=&-\frac{1}{6}.\end{array}

This proves the proposition.
∎

5.
Bigness of the canonical class of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Concretely, when
n\geq 15
, we will express the canonical class as a positive linear combination of the Hodge class
\lambda
, the
\psi
-classes
\psi_{1},...,\psi_{n}
, pull backs by forgetful maps of effective divisors considered in
[
HM
,
Fa
]
, and boundary divisors.
We will work with divisors on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
that are invariant under the action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, and it will be convenient to define

\psi=\psi_{1}+...+\psi_{n},\,\,\,\,\delta_{i,k}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\tiny{\begin{array}[]{c}S\subseteq\left\{1,...,n\right\}\\
|S|=k\end{array}}}\delta_{i,S}.

Consider the forgetful morphism
\pi:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
that forgets the last marked point.
The pull-back

\pi^{*}:\operatorname{Pic}{Q}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}\right)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}{Q}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n+1}}\right)

is given by
[
ACG
, Chapter 17, Lemma 4.28]
:

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\pi^{*}\lambda=\lambda,\\
\pi^{*}\delta_{irr}=\delta_{irr},\\
\pi^{*}\psi_{j}=\psi_{j}-\delta_{0,\left\{j,n+1\right\}}\text{ for }j=1,...,n,\\
\pi^{*}\delta_{i,S}=\begin{cases}\delta_{i,S}\text{ if }n=0,i=\frac{g}{2},S=\emptyset,\\
\delta_{i,S}+\delta_{i,S\cup\{n+1\}}\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}\\
\end{cases}

It will be useful in computations to work with the classes
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{n},\omega\in\operatorname{Pic}{Q}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}\right)
defined by

\omega_{j}=\pi_{j}^{*}\psi_{1}\text{ for }j=1,...,n\text{ and }\omega=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{j},

where
\pi_{j}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{1}}
forgets all points except the
j
-th marked point.
Observe that, by induction on
n
, we have that

\omega_{j}=\psi_{j}-\sumop\displaylimits_{S\ni j}\delta_{0,S}\text{ for }j=1,...,n\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\omega=\psi-\sumop\displaylimits_{k=2}^{n}k\delta_{0,k},

where the first sum runs over all subsets
S\subseteq\{1,...,n\}
containing
j
and at least one other element.

Let
\overline{H}
be the divisor in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3}
defined as the closure of the locus of hyperelliptic curves
H\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{3}
. The class of
\overline{H}
in
\operatorname{Pic}{Q}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3}\right)
was computed in
[
HM
, Section 6]
:

\overline{H}=9\lambda-\delta_{irr}-3\delta_{1}.

Let
g,r\geq 1
and set
n=(2r+1)(g+1)
.
Let
\overline{D}_{g,n}^{r}
be the divisor in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
defined as the closure in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}
of the locus

D_{g,n}^{r}=\left\{[C,p_{1},...,p_{n}]\in\mathcal{M}_{{g},{n}}\,\middle|\,\operatorname{h}^{0}\left(C,\omega_{C}^{\otimes(r+1)}(-p_{1}-...-p_{n})\right)\geq 1\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{{g},{n}}.

The divisor
\overline{D}_{g,n}^{r}
was introduced by Farkas, who denoted it by
\mathfrak{Mrc}_{g,0}^{r}
, and computed the class
[
Fa
, Theorem 4.2]
:

(5.2)

\overline{D}_{g,n}^{r}=-(6r^{2}+6r+1)\lambda+(r+1)\omega+\binom{r+1}{2}\cdot\delta_{irr}-\delta_{0,2}-\text{boundary divisors}.

Finally, recall from
[
Lo
, Theorem 2.9]
that the class

a\lambda+b\psi\in\operatorname{Pic}{Q}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}\right)

is big for
a,b>0
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
n\geq 15
. We consider two effective divisors on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}
.
The first one is the pull-back of
\overline{H}
under the forgetful map
\pi:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3}
:

(5.3)

\pi^{*}\overline{H}=9\lambda-\delta_{irr}-3\delta_{1}.

The second one is the
symmetric pull-back
of
\overline{D}_{3,14}^{3}
to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
, defined as

\overline{D}_{n}=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{14}}\sumop\displaylimits_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}T\subseteq\left\{1,...,n\right\}\\
|T|=14\end{array}}\pi_{T}^{*}\overline{D}_{3,14}^{3},

where
\pi_{T}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{14}}
is the forgetful map that forgets all points except for those indexed by
T
.
Equation (
5.2
) becomes

\overline{D}_{3,14}^{3}=-73\lambda+4\omega+6\delta_{irr}-\delta_{0,2}-\text{boundary divisors},

and using the formulas for the pull back (
5.1
) we obtain that

(5.4)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\overline{D}_{n}=&-73\lambda+\frac{56}{n}\omega+6\delta_{irr}-\frac{\binom{14}{2}}{\binom{n}{2}}\delta_{0,2}-\text{boundary divisors}\\
=&-73\lambda+\frac{56}{n}\left(\psi-\sumop\displaylimits_{k=2}^{n}k\delta_{0,k}\right)+6\delta_{irr}-\frac{182}{n(n-1)}\delta_{0,2}-\text{boundary divisors}\\
=&-73\lambda+\frac{56}{n}\psi+6\delta_{irr}-\frac{182}{n(n-1)}\delta_{0,2}-\sumop\displaylimits_{k=2}^{n}\frac{56k}{n}\delta_{0,k}-\text{boundary divisors}.\end{array}

By equation (
2.1
), the canonical class on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}
equals

\begin{array}[]{rl}K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}=&13\lambda+\psi-2\delta_{irr}-2\delta_{0}-2\delta_{1}-\delta_{1,\emptyset}\\
=&13\lambda+\psi-2\delta_{irr}-2\delta_{0}-3\delta_{1}+\text{boundary divisors}.\end{array}

Set
t=\frac{n}{56}
and
s=\frac{13+73t}{9}
. These numbers have been chosen so that the coefficients of
\lambda
and
\psi
in
s\pi^{*}\overline{H}+t\overline{D}_{n}
and
K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{g},{n}}}
are equal.
Then
K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}-s\pi^{*}\overline{H}-t\overline{D}_{n}
equals

(5.5)

\left(s-6t-2\right)\delta_{irr}+(3s-3)\delta_{1}+\frac{182t}{n(n-1)}\delta_{0,2}+\sumop\displaylimits_{k=3}^{n}(k-2)\delta_{0,k}+\text{boundary divisors}.

Since
n\geq 15
, the coefficients in front of
\delta_{irr},\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{0,k}
in (
5.5
) are positive. It follows that for
\epsilon>0
small enough

K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}-(s-9\epsilon)\pi^{*}\overline{H}-(t-\epsilon)\overline{D}_{n}=8\epsilon\lambda+\frac{56\epsilon}{n}\psi+\text{boundary divisors}.

Thus
K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}}
is a sum of big and effective classes, which proves that
K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{{3},{n}}}
is big.

∎

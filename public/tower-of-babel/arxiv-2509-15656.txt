On the structure and skeletals of principal ideal graphs of inverse semigroups
C S Preenu, R S Indu, Sijo George

Abstract
. The principal left ideal graph of a semigroup is a simple graph whose vertices are the non-zero elements of the semigroup, and two vertices are adjacent if their principal left ideals intersect non-trivially. In this paper, we study the structure of the principal ideal graphs of inverse semigroups, particularly symmetric inverse semigroups. We also introduce the concept of skeletal of a graph and show that the principal ideal graph of an inverse semigroup has a skeletal, which is a simple graph with vertex set as ℒ \mathrel{\mathcal{L}} classes of non-zero elements. It is also proved that the principal ideal graph of symmetric inverse semigroups has a skeletal which is isomorphic to the intersection graph on the power set of a non-empty set.

In organic chemistry, a skeletal formula, also called bond-line formula or line-angle formula, is a shorthand way to represent the structure of organic molecules. It simplifies drawing complex molecules by showing only the carbon-carbon bonds as lines, and it implies the presence of hydrogen atoms without explicitly drawing them. .
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

The number of edges of
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(IS_{n})
is given by

\frac{1}{2}\left((2^{n}-1)^{2}-(3^{n}-2^{n})\right)

Proof.

The number of edges in
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(IS_{n})
is half the sum of the degrees of all vertices. The degree of each non-zero idempotent is
2^{n}-2^{n-k}-1
, where
k
is the rank of the idempotent. The number of non-zero idempotents with rank
k
is
\binom{n}{k}
. Thus, the total number of edges is given by

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(2^{n}-2^{n-k}-1)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}(2^{n}-1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{k}\binom{n}{k}1^{k}2^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\left((2^{n}-1)^{2}-(3^{n}-2^{n})\right).

Simplifying this expression gives us the final result.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(IS_{n})
is isomorphic to the intersection graph of the non-empty subsets of an
n
-element set.

Proof.

Let
X
be an
n
-element set. The intersection graph of the non-empty subsets of
X
has vertices corresponding to each non-empty subset of
X
, with two vertices adjacent if and only if their corresponding subsets intersect. In
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(IS_{n})
, the vertices correspond to non-zero idempotents of
IS_{n}
, which are in one-to-one correspondence with non-empty subsets of
X
(the image of the idempotent). Two idempotents are adjacent in
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(IS_{n})
if and only if their images intersect. Thus, the adjacency condition in both graphs is the same, establishing an isomorphism between
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(IS_{n})
and the intersection graph of the non-empty subsets of an
n
-element set.
∎

5.2.
Two extremes: Semilattices and Brandt Semigroups

In this section, we examine two extremal cases for principal ideal graphs of inverse semigroups: semilattices and Brandt semigroups. For semilattices without zero, the skeletal
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
is a complete graph, since
ef\in Se\bigcap Sf=Sef
. In contrast, for Brandt semigroups, the skeletal
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
is a null graph. By Remark
5.4
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.10
.

When
S
is a semilattice, then we have the following:

(1)

\mathbf{mPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
and
\mathbf{mPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{R}}}(S)
are isomorphic.

(2)

\mathbf{mPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
and
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
are isomorphic.

(3)

If
S
has no zero, then
\mathbf{mPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
is a complete graph.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
S=B(G,I)=(I\times G\times I)\cup\{0\}
be a Brandt semigroup. Then
\mathbf{mPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
decomposes as a disjoint union of complete graphs indexed by
I
. Specifically, two vertices
a=(i,g,j)
and
b=(k,h,l)
are adjacent if and only if
j=l
.

Proof.

Consider two nonzero elements
a=(i,g,j)
and
b=(k,h,l)
in
B(G,I)
. Their principal left ideals are

S^{1}a=\{(m,x,j):m\in I,x\in G\}\cup\{0\},\quad S^{1}b=\{(n,y,l):n\in I,y\in G\}\cup\{0\}.

The intersection
S^{1}a\cap S^{1}b
contains a nonzero element if and only if there exist
m,n\in I
and
x,y\in G
such that
(m,x,j)=(n,y,l)
, which implies
j=l
. Hence,
a
and
b
are adjacent in
\mathbf{mPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
if and only if their right indices coincide.
This means the graph breaks into disjoint components, each consisting of all elements sharing the same right index. Each component is a complete graph because, for any two elements with the same right index, their principal left ideals are same, ensuring adjacency. Thus,
\mathbf{mPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
decomposes into cliques indexed by
I
, with each clique corresponding to a fixed right index.
∎

Now we consider the skeletal
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
of a Brandt semigroup
S=B(G,I)
. By Remark
5.4
, we can identify
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
as follows:
The set of non-zero idempotents is the vertex set, and two idempotents are adjacent if and only if their product is zero.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
S
be a Brandt semigroup, then
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
and
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{R}}}(S)
are null graphs.

Proof.

Since two distinct idempotents
(i,e,i)
and
(j,e,j)
are adjacent in
\mathbf{smPiG}_{\mathrel{\mathcal{L}}}(S)
if and only if their product is non-zero. But, in Brandt semigroup product of distinct idempotents is zero. Thus, no two distinct idempotents are adjacent and hence the result.
∎

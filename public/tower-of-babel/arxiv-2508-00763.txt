Abstract
. We completely characterize non-periodic balanced weighted shifts S 𝝀 S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}} on rooted directed trees under a very mild assumption that S 𝝀 ∗ n ​ S 𝝀 n | ker ⁡ S 𝝀 ∗ S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}} is invertible operator on ker ⁡ S 𝝀 ∗ \ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*} for all n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} . This generalizes the previously established unitary equivalences for Bergman and Dirichlet type shifts associated with locally finite rooted directed trees. We also give a counter example to justify that the criteria obtained for non-periodic balanced weighted shifts is not necessary for eventually periodic balanced weighted shifts.

S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
as an operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by the coordinate function on a Hilbert space of vector-valued formal power series under a mild assumption that

Then we apply the unitary equivalence criteria for operator-valued unilateral weighted shifts with invertible operator weights to obtain the main result of this paper. To state the main result, we recall below a few basic facts pertaining to weighted shifts on directed trees. We refer the reader to
[

Let
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{C}
denote the set of all non-negative integers and the field of complex numbers, respectively. For a set

\text{Card}(X)

\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})

\mathscr{T}
be denoted by
{\mathsf{root}}
. It follows from

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{n}:=\{v\in V:\mathsf{d}_{v}=n\}.

(1.1)

Let

\mathscr{T}
. The

k_{\mathscr{T}}:=\begin{cases}1+\sup\{\mathsf{d}_{v}:v\in V_{\prec}\}&\text{if $V_{\prec}$ is non-empty},\\

Suppose that

. Then it follows from

\displaystyle\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}=[e_{\mathsf{root}}]\oplus\bigoplus_{v\in V_{\prec}}\Big(\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{v}]\Big),

where

\{f\}
. Since

\displaystyle\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}=[e_{\mathsf{root}}]\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\,\bigoplus_{v\in V_{\prec}\cap\mathscr{G}_{n}}\Big(\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{v}]\Big).

Let
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
be a countably infinite, rooted, leafless directed tree and

be a bounded weighted shift on
\mathscr{T}
with positive weights. Then following
[

\mathsf{par}(u)=\mathsf{par}(v)
. It follows from
[

is balanced if and only if it is locally power balanced. Interesting examples of balanced weighted shifts appeared in

]
known as Bergman and Dirichlet type shifts associated with a locally finite rooted directed tree. These examples are described as follows.

Let
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
be a locally finite, countably infinite, rooted, leafless directed tree. For a real number
q\geqslant 1
, consider the weight system
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{q}=\big\{\lambda_{u,q}:u\in V\setminus\{{\mathsf{root}}\}\big\}
given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{u,q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Card}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{d}_{v}+q}{\mathsf{d}_{v}+1}}\ \text{ for }\ u\in\mathsf{Chi}(v),\ v\in V.

Then the weighted shift

on
\mathscr{T}
associated with the weight system
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{q}
is called as Dirichlet type shift on
\mathscr{T}
. Note that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{q}}
is bounded and left-invertible. Similarly, if we define

\displaystyle\lambda_{u,q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Card}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathsf{d}_{v}+1}{\mathsf{d}_{v}+q}}\ \text{ for }\ u\in\mathsf{Chi}(v),\ v\in V,

(1.4)

then the weighted shift associated with the weight system (
1.4

\mathscr{T}
. It is also bounded and left-invertible. Note that if

Theorem 1.1
.

[

Let

be a bounded weighted shift on a countably infinite, rooted, leafless directed tree

\{S^{n}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\ker S^{*}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is mutually orthogonal if and only if

is balanced.

Assumption:
Throughout the remainder of the paper, we assume that the directed tree
\mathscr{T}
is always countably infinite and leafless. The weighted shift
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is a bounded weighted shift on
\mathscr{T}
with positive weights.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of complex numbers. We say that
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

for all

is said to be

The Bergman and Dirichlet type weighted shifts are examples of non-periodic weighted shifts for

).

We are now in a position to state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be balanced weighted shifts on rooted directed trees
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
and
\tilde{\mathscr{T}}=(\tilde{V},\tilde{\mathcal{E}})

and
\tilde{E}:=\ker\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. Suppose that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{\tilde{E}}
are invertible operators on
E
and
\tilde{E}
, respectively for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
are non-periodic, then
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is unitarily equivalent to
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
if and only if

c_{n}=\tilde{c}_{n}\text{ and Card}(\mathscr{G}_{n})=\text{Card}(\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n})\ \text{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{N},

where
c_{n}

(
resp.
\tilde{c}_{n})
denotes the constant value of
\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{v}\|

(
resp.
\|\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{\tilde{v}}\|)
for

resp.
\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n})
.

Theorem
1.2
generalizes

, Theorem 2.4]
which provides the unitary equivalence of Dirichlet type (and consequently Bergman type) weighted shifts associated with locally finite rooted directed trees. Further, it is worth emphasizing that the assumption that

.

2.
Preparatory results

In this section, we collect a couple of lemmas which are crucial for the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We proceed with the following lemma which describes a sort of multiplicative structure of the moments of a balanced weighted shift on a rooted directed tree.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be a balanced weighted shift on a rooted directed tree
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
. Let
c_{m}
denote the constant value of
\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{v}\|
for
v\in\mathscr{G}_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for each integer
n\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|S^{n}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{v}\|=c_{\mathsf{d}v}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n-1}\ \text{ for all }\ v\in V.

Proof.

Let
v\in V
. It follows from
[
10
, Lemma 19]
that for all integer
n\geqslant 1
and
u\in{\mathsf{Chi}}^{\langle n-1\rangle}(v)
,

\displaystyle\|S^{\mathsf{d}_{v}+n}_{\lambda}e_{{\mathsf{root}}}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\|S_{\lambda}e_{{\mathsf{par}}^{\langle j\rangle}(u)}\|\Big)\|S_{\lambda}e_{\mathsf{par}(v)}\|\cdots\|S_{\lambda}e_{{\mathsf{root}}}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|S^{n}_{\lambda}e_{v}\|\|S^{\mathsf{d}_{v}}_{\lambda}e_{{\mathsf{root}}}\|.

This gives that

\displaystyle\|S^{n}_{\lambda}e_{v}\|=\frac{\|S^{\mathsf{d}_{v}+n}_{\lambda}e_{{\mathsf{root}}}\|}{\|S^{\mathsf{d}_{v}}_{\lambda}e_{{\mathsf{root}}}\|}=\frac{c_{0}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n-1}}{c_{0}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v-1}}=c_{\mathsf{d}v}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n-1}

for all
v\in V
and for all integer
n\geqslant 1
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be a balanced weighted shift on a rooted directed tree
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
and
E:=\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. Then
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E)
is a dense subspace of
E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently,
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
is a positive operator on
E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, if any of the following conditions is satisfied, then
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
is an invertible operator on
E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(i)

E
is finite dimensional.

(ii)

S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is left-invertible.

(iii)

\mathscr{T}
is of finite branching index.

Proof.

We first show that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E)\subseteq E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is balanced, it follows from Theorem
1.1
that for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
S^{n}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(E)\perp S^{m}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(E)
if
m\neq n
. Thus, for any
x,y\in E
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\langle{S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x},\,{S^{m}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}y}\rangle=0\ \text{ for all integer }\ m\geq 1.

That is,
\bigoplus_{m=1}^{\infty}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{m}(E)\subseteq S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E)^{\perp}
. Note that every bounded weighted shift on a rooted directed tree has the wandering subspace property (see
[
8
, Proposition 1.3.4]
). Therefore, we obtain that

\ell^{2}(V)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E).

This, together with above, gives us
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E)\subseteq E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
x\in E\ominus S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E)
. Then

\langle{S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}y},\,{x}\rangle=0\ \text{ for all }\ y\in E.

In particular,
\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x\|=0
, which implies that
x=0
. Thus,
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E)
is a dense subspace of
E
. That
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
is a positive operator on
E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is obvious.

To see the moreover part, fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that if
E
is finite dimensional, then the invertibility of
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
follows from the rank-nullity theorem. Assume that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is left-invertible. Then there exists
\alpha(n)>0
such that
\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{v}\|\geqslant\alpha(n)
for all
v\in V
. Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x_{m}\to y
as
m\to\infty
, where
x_{m}=\sum_{v\in V}x_{m}(v)e_{v}\in E
for all
m\geqslant 1
. Then for all
m,k\geqslant 1
, we have

\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(x_{m}-x_{k})\|^{2}=\sum_{v\in V}|x_{m}(v)-x_{k}(v)|^{2}\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{v}\|^{4}\geqslant\alpha(n)^{4}\|x_{m}-x_{k}\|^{2}.

Thus,
(x_{m})_{m\geqslant 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
E
. Let
x_{m}\to x\in E
as
m\to\infty
. Then
y=S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x
. This shows that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E)
is a closed subspace of
E
, and hence by preceding paragraph,
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E)=E
. Consequently,
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
is an invertible operator on
E
.

Finally, suppose that the branching index
k_{\mathscr{T}}
of
\mathscr{T}
is finite. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
c_{m}
denote the constant value of
\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{v}\|
for
v\in\mathscr{G}_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
c:=\min\{c_{0},\ldots,c_{k_{\mathscr{T}}}\}>0
. Let
x\in E
. Then it follows from (
1.2
) that

x=ae_{\mathsf{root}}+\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}}b_{v}\ \text{ for some }\ a\in\mathbb{C}\ \text{ and }\ b_{v}\in\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{v}].

In view of Lemma
2.1
, we get that

S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}b_{v}=\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{w}\|^{2}b_{v}=c_{\mathsf{d}v+1}^{2}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n}^{2}b_{v},

where
w
is any element of
\mathsf{Chi}(v)
. Consider

y=\frac{a}{c_{0}^{2}\cdots c_{n-1}^{2}}e_{\mathsf{root}}+\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}}\frac{b_{v}}{c_{\mathsf{d}v+1}^{2}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n}^{2}}.

Then

\displaystyle\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|a|^{2}}{(c_{0}^{2}\cdots c_{n-1}^{2})^{2}}+\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}}\frac{\|b_{v}\|^{2}}{(c_{\mathsf{d}v+1}^{2}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n}^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{|a|^{2}}{c^{4n}}+\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}}\frac{\|b_{v}\|^{2}}{c^{4n}}=\frac{1}{c^{4n}}\|x\|^{2}<\infty.

Further, it can be easily verified that
y\in E
and
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}y=x
. Hence,
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
is an invertible operator on
E
.
∎

3.
Classification

In this section, we classify all the non-periodic balanced weighted shifts
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on rooted directed trees under a mild assumption that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}}
is invertible operator on
\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In order to get the desired classification, we first model
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
as an operator of multiplication by the coordinate function
z
on the Hilbert space of
\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
-valued formal power series associated with the sequence
(S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of invertible operators on
\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. For the sake of convenience, we briefly recall a few basic things about a Hilbert space of vector-valued formal power series. The reader is referred to
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
for a detailed study on such Hilbert spaces.

Let
E
be a complex separable Hilbert space. An
E
-valued formal power series refers to a series of the form
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n},\ x_{n}\in E
, without any concern for its convergence at any point
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathscr{B}=\{B_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators on
E
and consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B}):=\bigg\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n}:\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|B_{n}x_{n}\|^{2}<\infty\bigg\}

equipped with the following inner product:

\displaystyle\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle{B_{n}x_{n}},\,{B_{n}y_{n}}\rangle_{E},\quad f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n},\ g=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B}).

Then
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
is a Hilbert space known as the
Hilbert space of
E
-valued formal power series
. Note that
E
-valued polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
. Thus, the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by the coordinate function
z
is a densely defined operator in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
which may not be bounded in general. It turns out that a balanced weighted shift on a rooted directed tree can be realized as (a bounded)
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
for some
E
and
\mathscr{B}
, which is illustrated in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be a balanced weighted shift on a rooted directed tree
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
and
E:=\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. Then
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is unitarily equivalent to
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
with
\mathscr{B}=\big\{(S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E})^{1/2}:n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
. To this end, for any
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{M}_{z}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n+1}\Big\|^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big\langle S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n+1}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n+1}x_{n},\ x_{n}\big\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n+1}x_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x_{n}\|^{2}=\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|^{2}\|f\|^{2}<\infty.

Thus,
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is bounded.

Since
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is a balanced weighted shift and every bounded weighted shift on a rooted directed tree has the wandering subspace property (see
[
8
, Proposition 1.3.4]
), from Theorem
1.1
, we get

\displaystyle\ell^{2}(V)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}(E).

Now define
U:\ell^{2}(V)\to\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
as

\displaystyle U\Big(\Oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x_{n}\Big)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n},\quad\Oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x_{n}\in\ell^{2}(V).

It can be easily verified that
U
is unitary and
US_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\mathscr{M}_{z}U
.
∎

It is very natural to ask when the elements of
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
define
E
-valued holomorphic functions. The answer lies in determining the set of all bounded point evaluations on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
. Note that
E
-valued polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
. Therefore, for
w\in\mathbb{C}
, let us consider the evaluation map
\mathscr{E}_{w}
given by
\mathscr{E}_{w}p=p(w)
for all polynomials
p\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
. We say that
w\in\mathbb{C}
is a bounded point evaluation on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
if the evaluation map
\mathscr{E}_{w}
extends to a continuous linear map from
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
onto
E
. The continuous extension of
\mathscr{E}_{w}
, by an abuse of notation, is denoted by
\mathscr{E}_{w}
itself. Let
\Omega
denote the set of all bounded point evaluations on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
. It turns out that if
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is left-invertible, then the open disc
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(S^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})^{-1}\big)
is contained in
\Omega
, where
r(T)
denotes the spectral radius of a bounded linear operator
T
and
T^{\prime}:=T(T^{*}T)^{-1}
denotes the Cauchy dual of a left-invertible operator
T
. Although this inclusion follows from
[
18
]
but here we provide a direct proof.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be a balanced weighted shift on a rooted directed tree
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
and
E:=\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. If
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is left-invertible, then the set
\Omega
of all bounded point evaluations on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
contains
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(S^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})^{-1}\big)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is left-invertible. Let
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
and
w\in\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(S^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})^{-1}\big)
. Then

\displaystyle\|f(w)\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|w|^{n}\|x_{n}\|=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|w|^{n}\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\prime*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|w|^{2n}\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\prime n}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x_{n}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\eta(w)\|f\|,

where
\eta(w)=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|w|^{2n}\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\prime n}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty
by virtue of the fact that
|w|<1/r(S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{\prime})
. This shows that the evaluation map
\mathscr{E}_{w}:\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})\to E
is continuous. Consequently,
\mathbb{D}\big(0,r(S^{\prime}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})^{-1}\big)\subseteq\Omega
.
∎

If
\Omega
has non-empty interior
\Omega^{\circ}
, it follows from the general theory
[
17
]
that
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of
E
-valued holomorphic functions defined on
\Omega^{\circ}
. In addition, if
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
is invertible operator on
E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then from
[
13
, eq.(11)]
, the reproducing kernel
\kappa:\Omega^{\circ}\times\Omega^{\circ}\to\mathcal{B}(E)
is given by

\kappa(z,w)=\mathscr{E}_{z}\mathscr{E}_{w}^{*}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E})^{-1}z^{n}\bar{w}^{n},\quad z,w\in\Omega^{\circ}.

The following proposition shows that the above expression of
\kappa
can be further refined to get more explicit form. It also generalizes the analytic models obtained in
[
7
]
for the Bergman and Dirichlet type weighted shifts associated with locally finite rooted directed trees.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be a balanced weighted shift on a rooted directed tree
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
and
E:=\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. Suppose that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
is invertible operator on
E
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\Omega^{\circ}
is non-empty, then
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{E}(\mathscr{B})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of
E
-valued holomorphic functions defined on
\Omega^{\circ}
and the reproducing kernel
\kappa:\Omega^{\circ}\times\Omega^{\circ}\to\mathcal{B}(E)
is given by

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}\overline{w}^{n}}{c_{0}^{2}\cdots c_{n-1}^{2}}P_{[e_{\mathsf{root}}]}+\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}\overline{w}^{n}}{c_{\mathsf{d}v+1}^{2}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n}^{2}}P_{\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{v}]},

for all
z,w\in\Omega^{\circ}
, where
c_{n}
denotes the constant value of
\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{v}\|
for
v\in\mathscr{G}_{n}
, and
P_{M}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace
M
of a Hilbert space
H
.

Proof.

In view of the preceding discussion, we only need to show that
\kappa
has the above mentioned form. Let
x\in E
. Then it follows from (
1.2
) that

\displaystyle x=P_{[e_{\mathsf{root}}]}(x)+\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}}P_{\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{v}]}(x).

Hence, following the arguments of the proof of Lemma
2.2
(iii) and using Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}x=c_{0}^{2}\cdots c_{n-1}^{2}P_{[e_{\mathsf{root}}]}(x)+\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}}c_{\mathsf{d}v+1}^{2}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n}^{2}P_{\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{v}]}(x)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This, in turn, yields that

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E})^{-1}x\,z^{n}\bar{w}^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}\overline{w}^{n}}{c_{0}^{2}\cdots c_{n-1}^{2}}P_{[e_{\mathsf{root}}]}(x)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}\overline{w}^{n}}{c_{\mathsf{d}v+1}^{2}\cdots c_{\mathsf{d}v+n}^{2}}P_{\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{v}]}(x)

for all
z,w\in\Omega^{\circ}
, which completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is a particular case of
[
14
, Corollary 2.5]
and is crucial for the classification of non-periodic balanced weighted shifts.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be balanced weighted shifts on rooted directed trees
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
and
\tilde{\mathscr{T}}=(\tilde{V},\tilde{\mathcal{E}})
, respectively with
E:=\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
and
\tilde{E}:=\ker\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. Suppose that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{\tilde{E}}
are invertible operators on
E
and
\tilde{E}
, respectively for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is unitarily equivalent to
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
if and only if there exists a unitary
U:E\to\tilde{E}
such that

\displaystyle US_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}=\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{\tilde{E}}\,U\ \text{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.5)

The next lemma is an intermediate step of the proof of Theorem
1.2
for which we need the sequence
(W_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(resp.
(\tilde{W}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
) of subspaces of
\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
(resp.
\ker\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
) defined as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}W_{0}&:=[e_{\mathsf{root}}]\ \text{ and }\ W_{n}:=\bigoplus_{v\in V_{\prec}\cap\mathscr{G}_{n-1}}\Big(\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(v))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{v}]\Big),\ n\geqslant 1,\\
\tilde{W}_{0}&:=[e_{\tilde{\mathsf{root}}}]\ \text{ and }\ \tilde{W}_{n}:=\bigoplus_{\tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}_{\prec}\cap\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n-1}}\Big(\ell^{2}(\mathsf{Chi}(\tilde{v}))\ominus[\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\tilde{v}}]\Big),\ n\geqslant 1.\end{split}

(3.6)

Then it follows from (
1.3
) that

\displaystyle\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}W_{n}\ \text{ and }\ \ker\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{W}_{n}.

(3.7)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be balanced weighted shifts on rooted directed trees
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
and
\tilde{\mathscr{T}}=(\tilde{V},\tilde{\mathcal{E}})
, respectively with
E:=\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
and
\tilde{E}:=\ker\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. Suppose that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{\tilde{E}}
are invertible operators on
E
and
\tilde{E}
, respectively for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:E\to\tilde{E}
be a unitary satisfying (
3.5
). If
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
are non-periodic, then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
U(W_{k})=\tilde{W}_{m}
for a unique
m\in\mathbb{N}
, where
W_{n}
and
\tilde{W}_{n}
are given by (
3.6
).

Proof.

Suppose that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
are non-periodic. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
f\in W_{k}
. Since
Uf\in\tilde{E}
, let

Uf=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}g_{m},\quad g_{m}\in\tilde{W}_{m}.

Let
c_{n}

(
resp.
\tilde{c}_{n})
denote the constant value of
\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{v}\|

(
resp.
\|\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{\tilde{v}}\|)
for
v\in\mathscr{G}_{n}\,(
resp.
\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n})
. Suppose that
g_{m_{1}}
and
g_{m_{2}}
are non-zero for some distinct
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then by (
3.5
) together with Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle c_{k}^{2}\cdots c_{k+n-1}^{2}\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}g_{m}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{k}^{2}\cdots c_{k+n-1}^{2}Uf=US_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}f=\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}Uf

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{c}_{m}^{2}\cdots\tilde{c}_{m+n-1}^{2}g_{m},

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking the inner product on both sides with
g_{m_{1}}
and then by
g_{m_{2}}
, we get

c_{k+j}=\tilde{c}_{m_{1}+j}=\tilde{c}_{m_{2}+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

Assume without loss of generality that
m_{1}<m_{2}
. Then the above becomes

\tilde{c}_{m_{1}+j}=\tilde{c}_{(m_{2}-m_{1})+m_{1}+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

This contradicts the fact that
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is non-periodic. Hence, there exists a unique
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
Uf=g_{m}
. This shows that
U(W_{k})\subseteq\tilde{W}_{m}
. Now suppose that there exists
g\in\tilde{W}_{m}\setminus U(W_{k})
. Then there exist
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
with
k_{1}\neq k
and
f_{1}\in W_{k_{1}}
such that
Uf_{1}=g
. Again, applying (
3.5
) and using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{k_{1}}^{2}\cdots c_{k_{1}+n-1}^{2}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{k_{1}}^{2}\cdots c_{k_{1}+n-1}^{2}Uf_{1}=US_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}f_{1}=\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}Uf_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{m}^{2}\cdots\tilde{c}_{m+n-1}^{2}g,

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Comparing the coefficients on both sides, we get

c_{k_{1}+j}=\tilde{c}_{m+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}
. Similar arguments for
Uf=g_{m}
yields that
c_{k+j}=\tilde{c}_{m+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}
. Combining both gives that

c_{k_{1}+j}=c_{k+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

This contradicts the fact that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is non-periodic. Thus,
U(W_{k})=\tilde{W}_{m}
for a unique
m\in\mathbb{N}
, completing the proof.
∎

We now prove the main result of this article, which is Theorem
1.2
. It is restated here for the sake of convenience.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be balanced weighted shifts on rooted directed trees
\mathscr{T}=(V,\mathcal{E})
and
\tilde{\mathscr{T}}=(\tilde{V},\tilde{\mathcal{E}})
, respectively with
E:=\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
and
\tilde{E}:=\ker\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}
. Suppose that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{E}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}|_{\tilde{E}}
are invertible operators on
E
and
\tilde{E}
, respectively for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
are non-periodic, then
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is unitarily equivalent to
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
if and only if

c_{n}=\tilde{c}_{n}\text{ and Card}(\mathscr{G}_{n})=\text{Card}(\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n})\ \text{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{N},

where
c_{n}

(
resp.
\tilde{c}_{n})
denotes the constant value of
\|S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{v}\|

(
resp.
\|\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{\tilde{v}}\|)
for
v\in\mathscr{G}_{n}\,(
resp.
\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n})
.

Proof.

Let
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
be non-periodic. Suppose that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is unitarily equivalent to
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
U:E\to\tilde{E}
is a unitary satisfying (
3.5
). We claim that
U(W_{k})=\tilde{W}_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
W_{k}
and
\tilde{W}_{k}
are given by (
3.6
). We prove the claim by mathematical induction. To this end, note that by Lemma
3.5
,
U(W_{0})=\tilde{W}_{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. On contrary, assume that
m\geqslant 1
. Then
Ue_{{\mathsf{root}}}=g
for some
g\in\tilde{W}_{m}
. Applying (
3.5
) together with Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle c_{0}^{2}\cdots c_{n-1}^{2}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}^{2}\cdots c_{n-1}^{2}Ue_{{\mathsf{root}}}=US_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{\mathsf{root}}=\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}Ue_{\mathsf{root}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{m}^{2}\cdots\tilde{c}_{m+n-1}^{2}g

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Comparing the coefficients on both sides, we get

\displaystyle c_{j}=\tilde{c}_{m+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.8)

Also by Lemma
3.5
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
U(W_{k})=\tilde{W}_{0}
. Note that
k\geqslant 1
. Then
Uf=e_{\tilde{\mathsf{root}}}
for some
f\in W_{k}
. Again applying (
3.5
) together with Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle c_{k}^{2}\cdots c_{k+n-1}^{2}e_{\tilde{\mathsf{root}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{k}^{2}\cdots c_{k+n-1}^{2}Uf=US_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}f=\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}Uf

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{0}^{2}\cdots\tilde{c}_{n-1}^{2}e_{\tilde{\mathsf{root}}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Comparing the coefficients on both sides, we get

\displaystyle c_{k+j}=\tilde{c}_{j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.9)

Combining (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we obtain that

\displaystyle c_{j}=\tilde{c}_{m+j}=c_{m+k+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
m+k\geqslant 2>0
, it follows that
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is periodic. This is a contradiction. Hence,
U(W_{0})=\tilde{W}_{0}
, and consequently, by (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle c_{j}=\tilde{c}_{j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

Suppose that for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
U(W_{l})=\tilde{W}_{l}
for all
l\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
. On contrary, assume that
U(W_{k+1})=\tilde{W}_{m}
for some
m\geqslant k+2
. Then there exists
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
U(W_{p})=\tilde{W}_{k+1}
. Note that
p\geqslant k+2
. Similar arguments as above show that

\displaystyle c_{k+1+j}=\tilde{c}_{m+j}\ \text{ and }\ c_{p+j}=\tilde{c}_{k+1+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

This, in turn, gives that

\displaystyle c_{k+1+j}=\tilde{c}_{m+j}=\tilde{c}_{(m-k-1)+k+1+j}=c_{(m-k-1)+p+j}\ \text{ for all }\ j\in\mathbb{N}.

Observe that
(m-k-1)+p\geqslant k+3>k+1
. Thus,
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is eventually periodic, which is a contradiction. Hence,
U(W_{k+1})=\tilde{W}_{k+1}
and this completes the proof of the claim.

Since
U
is a unitary, the identity
U(W_{k})=\tilde{W}_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, yields that

\sum_{v\in V_{\prec}\cap\mathscr{G}_{n}}\Big(\text{Card}\big(\mathsf{Chi}(v)\big)-1\Big)=\sum_{\tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}_{\prec}\cap\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n}}\Big(\text{Card}\big(\mathsf{Chi}(\tilde{v})\big)-1\Big),\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Observe that if
v\in\mathscr{G}_{n}
but
v\notin V_{\prec}
, then
\text{Card}\big(\mathsf{Chi}(v)\big)-1=0
. Thus, above becomes

\sum_{v\in\mathscr{G}_{n}}\Big(\text{Card}\big(\mathsf{Chi}(v)\big)-1\Big)=\sum_{\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n}}\Big(\text{Card}\big(\mathsf{Chi}(\tilde{v})\big)-1\Big),\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

This, in turn, gives that

\text{Card}(\mathscr{G}_{n+1})-\text{Card}(\mathscr{G}_{n})=\text{Card}(\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n+1})-\text{Card}(\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n}),\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\text{Card}(\mathscr{G}_{0})=\text{Card}(\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{0})=1
, we obtain that

\text{Card}(\mathscr{G}_{n})=\text{Card}(\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n})\ \text{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Conversely, assume that
c_{n}=\tilde{c}_{n}
and
\text{Card}(\mathscr{G}_{n})=\text{Card}(\tilde{\mathscr{G}}_{n})\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then
\dim W_{n}=\dim\tilde{W}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the linear map
U:E\to\tilde{E}
given by
U=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\,U_{n}
, where
U_{n}
is a unitary from
W_{n}
onto
\tilde{W}_{n}
. In view of (
3.7
), it is easy to see that
U
is a unitary from
E
onto
\tilde{E}
. Now we show that
U
satisfies (
3.5
). To this end, let
f\in E
. Then by (
3.7
), we get

f=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}f_{m},\quad f_{m}\in W_{m}.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we get for all integer
n\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle US_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}US_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}f_{m}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}c_{m}^{2}\cdots c_{m+n-1}^{2}Uf_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{c}_{m}^{2}\cdots\tilde{c}_{m+n-1}^{2}Uf_{m}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}Uf_{m}=\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*n}\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}Uf.

The second last inequality in above follows from the fact that
Uf_{m}\in\tilde{W}_{m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.4
,
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is unitarily equivalent to
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Concluding remarks

The case of eventually periodic balanced weighted shifts warrants further attention. Note that the converse part of the proof of Theorem
3.6
does not assume that the weighted shifts are non-periodic. Hence, the criteria provided in Theorem
3.6
is sufficient but not necessary for the unitary equivalence of two eventually periodic balanced weighted shifts. For example, consider the simplest case of isometric weighted shifts
S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on the directed trees
\mathscr{T}
and
\tilde{\mathscr{T}}
, respectively shown below:

Then
c_{n}=\tilde{c}_{n}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Observe that
\dim\ker S_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}=\dim\ker\tilde{S}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{*}=2
. Hence by Wold decomposition, both

and

are unitarily equivalent. But

. Moreover, a bigger question about the unitary equivalence of two arbitrary weighted shifts on rooted directed trees remains open till date.

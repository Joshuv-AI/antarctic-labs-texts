Abstract
. Let 𝒟 \mathcal{D} be a strongly self-absorbing C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra. Given any separable C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra A A , our two main results assert the following. If A A is 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -stable and non-unital, then the corona algebra of A A is 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -saturated, i.e., 𝒟 \mathcal{D} embeds unitally into the relative commutant of every separable C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -subalgebra. Conversely, assuming that the stable corona of A A is separably 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -stable, we prove that A A is 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -stable. This generalizes recent work by the first-named author on the structure of the Calkin algebra. As an immediate corollary, it follows that the multiplier algebra of a separable 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -stable C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra is separably 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -stable. Appropriate versions of the aforementioned results are also obtained when A A is not necessarily separable. The article ends with some non-trivial applications.

Introduction

Within the structure and classification theory of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, the so-called strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras have historically emerged as important cornerstone objects.
This was initially the case by example:
The special Cuntz algebras
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
were shown by Kirchberg–Phillips
[
37
]
to be, in an appropriate sense, the initial and final object (respectively) in the category of separable simple unital nuclear purely infinite
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras (the so-called
Kirchberg algebras
).
Although it was not originally phrased in that (not yet existing) framework at the time, Phillips’ proof
[
45
]
of the Kirchberg–Phillips classification theorem
[
35
,
34
]
made crucial use of the properties of
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
that make it a strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra from today’s point of view.
Towards the end of the 1990s, Jiang and Su
[
30
]
constructed the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{Z}
now named after them, intended as a stably finite analog of
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
, and showed that it is strongly self-absorbing.

Finally, the behavior of these interesting examples was unified in the abstract theory of strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras (recalled in the preliminary section) in the mid 2000s, by Toms–Winter
[
56
]
and Kirchberg
[
36
]
.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be a strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
One of the most appealing aspects of the associated abstract theory is that given any separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
, one can characterize the existence of an isomorphism
A\cong A\otimes\mathcal{D}
(this is called
\mathcal{D}
-stability
) as an internal local approximation property of
A
via a so-called McDuff-type criterion, inspired by a related result
[
43
]
for von Neumann algebras that served as the original inspiration.
This has the advantage of making
\mathcal{D}
-stability a reasonably verifiable condition in many contexts and moreover implies that
\mathcal{D}
-stability is preserved under various elementary constructions.
Subsequently, strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras had been recognized both as interesting test cases for hard open problems (see
[
62
,
51
]
) and as objects in their respective categories playing a facilitating role for certain classification problems
[
61
]
.
Given that
{\mathcal{Z}}
has been shown in
[
59
]
to be the initial object in the category of strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, the property of tensorially absorbing
{\mathcal{Z}}
(called
Jiang–Su stability
) has a special significance.
In the history of the subject, it has emerged as a well-studied property both in examples
[
55
,
15
,
32
,
33
,
23
,
44
]
and abstractly within the work on the Toms–Winter conjecture
[
16
,
58
,
60
,
41
,
53
,
42
,
5
,
10
,
9
]
(the given references are not exhaustive).
Indeed, the most satisfactory state-of-the-art classification theorem in the context of the Elliott program concerns the category of separable simple nuclear Jiang–Su stable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras satisfying the universal coefficient theorem
[
25
,
26
]
; see
[
8
]
and the more elaborate discussion therein about the history of classification.

The present work is a continuation and strengthening of the recent article
[
18
]
by the first named author, motivated by the Brown–Douglas–Fillmore question whether the Calkin algebra can have a K-theory reversing automorphism. In this work a new connection was discovered between strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and the stucture of corona algebras.
Recall that for a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
with associated multiplier algebra
\mathcal{M}(A)
, the quotient
\mathcal{Q}(A):=\mathcal{M}(A)/A
is called the
corona algebra
of
A
.
Before resuming the discussion, we recall a concept frequently occurring in this article:

Definition
.

Let
B
and
C
be unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras with
B
separable.
We say that
C
is
B
-saturated
if for every separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
A\subseteq C
, there is a unital, injective
*
-homomorphism from
B
to
C\cap A^{\prime}
.

In simplified terms, the two main insights in
[
18
, Theorems B+C]
can be summarized by the following statements:
If
\mathcal{D}
is strongly self-absorbing and a separable unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
is
\mathcal{D}
-stable, then
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{K}\otimes A)
is
\mathcal{D}
-saturated; the Calkin algebra
\mathcal{Q}(H):=\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{K})
, however, is not
\mathcal{D}
-saturated.
As an immediate consequence, it followed (among other things) that the Calkin algebra is not isomorphic to the corona
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
, which answered a previously open question.

In this article, we generalize both of these insights to the most general setting in which they are plausible.
In order to extend the theory beyond the separable case, we work with the concept of
separable
\mathcal{D}
-stability
from
[
49
]
.
A
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
is called separably
\mathcal{D}
-stable if every separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
A
is included in a separable
\mathcal{D}
-stable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
A
.

Our first main result (proven in Section
2
as Theorem
2.5
) gives a generalization of
[
18
, Theorems C]
by removing a number of prior assumptions.

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}
is a strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and that
A
is a
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra that is separably
\mathcal{D}
-stable.
Then
\mathcal{Q}(A)
is
\mathcal{D}
-saturated.

The assumption of
\sigma
-unitality is not only essential in our proof of Theorem
A
, but taking a closer look at a class of non-
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras studied by Sakai (discussed in Example
2.7
) demonstrates that there exist separably
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras with one-dimensional corona algebras.

Our second main result, to be understood as a powerful generalization of both main results in
[
18
]
, provides a partial converse of the above and entails a new characterization of (separable)
\mathcal{D}
-stability, also having consequences to the structure of the multiplier algebra.
It fully confirms and extends
[
18
, Conjecture 6.2]
.

Theorem B
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}
is a strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
A
is a
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is separably
\mathcal{D}
-stable.

(2)

\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{K}\otimes A)
is separably
\mathcal{D}
-stable.

(3)

\mathcal{M}(A)
is separably
\mathcal{D}
-stable.

Apart from Theorem
A
covering the implication
(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
, the most novel aspect in the proof of Theorem
B
concerns the implication
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
.
This implication is true even without
\sigma
-unitality and arises as the consequence of a deeper principle that allows us to transfer (relatively) approximately central sequences from
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{K}\otimes A)
to
\mathcal{M}(A)
; see Definition
1.8
and Corollary
4.5
for details.
This is achieved in Sections
3
and
4
based on studying the relative positions of certain unitary representations of property (T) groups inside the stable corona
\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{K}\otimes A)
akin to
[
18
, Theorem B]
, with a few upgrades.
Although we currently have no definitive evidence of this claim, we believe that a further generalization of implication
(2)
\Rightarrow
(1)
cannot be expected in any reasonable generality if one only makes a structural assumption about
\mathcal{Q}(A)
without also assuming that
A
is stable or simple.
This is demonstrated by various easy examples such as
A:={\mathbb{C}}\oplus(\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{D})
.

The last Section
5
is concerned with applications of our main results.
We present an application of Theorem
A
, whereby the Calkin algebra does not have the same first-order theory as any nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra (i.e., is not elementarily equivalent to a nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra); see Theorem
5.1
.
Also, if
A
is any
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\mathcal{Q}(A)
is isomorphic to the Calkin algebra, then

cannot be separably
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable (Corollary

).
Another application is a partial positive result to a question attributed to Sakai in
[
14
]
, asking whether non-unital simple separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are isomorphic if and only if their coronas are isomorphic (see
[
19
, Question 4.19]
).
It is an immediate consequence of Theorem

that coronas of stabilizations of strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are isomorphic if and only if the algebras are isomorphic.
Next, we observe that multiplier algebras of separably
{\mathcal{Z}}
-stable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras have strict comparison, which is related to earlier results in the literature obtained under comparably more restrictive assumptions.
Lastly, we can give a partial positive answer to
[

and give an improved version of the recent

-stable

While we have novel applications to the model theory of coronas, we would like to emphasize that we do not develop any novel methodology of model-theoretic or set-theoretic nature.

1.1.
Clubs and reflection

Reflection is the phenomenon (well-studied in set theory; see e.g., the introduction to
[
22
]
or
[
39
]
) when properties of an uncountable (more generally nonseparable) structure reflect to its small (for example, countable or separable) substructures.
In
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras reflection is usually obtained by Blackadar’s method (see the discussion of separably inheritable properties in
[
4
, §II.8.5]
) and in a more general case by the Downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem from model theory (see e.g.,
[
11
, Corollary 2.1.4]
for the general statement,
[
3
, Proposition 7.2]
for the continuous logic, and
[
20
, §2.6]
or
[
17
, Appendix D]
for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras).
An in-depth discussion of reflection phenomena for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras can be found in
[
17
, §7.3 and §7.4]
.
To give a more precise definition of the reflection that we will be using, we need the notion of an elementary submodel.
If
A\subseteq B
are
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, then
A
is an
elementary submodel
of
B
if for every first-order formula
\varphi(\bar{x})
and every tuple
\bar{a}
in
A
of the same sort as
\bar{x}
the evaluations of
\varphi(\bar{a})
in
A
and in
B
agree (
[
20
, Definition 2.3.3]
or
[
17
, §7.1, also §D.1]
). A familiar example is provided by Łoś’s Theorem (e.g.,
[
20
, Theorem 2.3.1]
): the diagonal copy of
A
in its ultrapower
A_{\mathcal{U}}
is an elementary submodel.
Properties captured by the first-order theory are called
axiomatizable
or
elementary
. See
[
20
, Theorem 2.5.1]
for numerous examples.

An elementary submodel inherits all axiomatizable properties of the original algebra as well as many non-axiomatizable properties (such as nuclearity and other properties definable by uniform families of formulas, see
[
20
, Theorem 5.7.3]
).
The following definitions are standard (
[
17
, Definition 6.2.6, Example 6.2.5]
).

Definition 1.1
.

For a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C
let
\Sep(C)
denote the poset of its separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebras ordered by the inclusion.
A subset of
\Sep(C)
that is cofinal and
\sigma
-closed (i.e., closed under taking direct limits of increasing sequences) is, for historical reasons, (misleadingly) known as a
closed and unbounded
set or a
club
.

By the Downwards Löwenheim–Skolem theorem (as stated in
[
17
, Theorem 7.19]
), for every nonseparable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, its separable elementary submodels form a club.
This is the simplest form of reflection to separable substructures.
An important (yet not difficult to prove) feature of clubs is:

Lemma 1.2

(see
[
17
, Proposition 6.2.9]
)
.

Let
C
be
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
Any intersection of countably many clubs in
\Sep(C)
is a club.

In other words, subsets of
\Sep(C)
that include a club form a
\sigma
-filter.
The following reflection property of clubs requires a proof.

Lemma 1.3
.

Suppose that
A\subseteq B
are nonseparable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. If
\mathcal{C}
includes a club in
\Sep(B)
, then
\{A\cap C\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
includes a club in
\Sep(A)
.

Proof.

We will use a well-known fact about clubs in discrete structures. If
\mathsf{X}
is an uncountable set then
[\mathsf{X}]^{\aleph_{0}}
is the poset of all countable subsets of
\mathsf{X}
.
A club in this poset is a cofinal family closed under countable increasing unions.
By a corollary to Kueker’s characterization of clubs in
[\mathsf{X}]^{\aleph_{0}}
(
[
17
, Corollary 6.4.2]
), if
\mathsf{Y}\subseteq\mathsf{X}
are uncountable sets and
{\mathcal{E}}
is a club in
[\mathsf{X}]^{\aleph_{0}}
, then
\{p\cap\mathsf{Y}\mid p\in{\mathcal{E}}\}
includes a club in
[\mathsf{Y}]^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Assume
A\subseteq B
are nonseparable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
By
[
17
, Proposition 7.2.7]
there is a dense subset
\mathsf{D}_{B}\subseteq B
such that the set of relatively closed countable subsets of
\mathsf{D}_{B}
,

\{p\in[\mathsf{D}_{B}]^{\aleph_{0}}\mid\overline{p}\cap\mathsf{D}_{B}=p\}
,
includes a club in
[\mathsf{D}_{B}]^{\aleph_{0}}
. This implies that, for any
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\Sep(B)
,
\mathcal{C}
includes a club in
\Sep(B)
if and only if the set
\{C\cap\mathsf{D}_{B}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
includes a club in
[\mathsf{D}_{B}]^{\aleph_{0}}
(
[
17
, Corollary 7.2.8]
).
Again by
[
17
, Proposition 7.2.7]
fix a dense subset
\mathsf{D}_{A}
of
A
such that the set of countable relatively closed subsets of
\mathsf{D}_{A}
includes a club. Then
\mathsf{D}:=\mathsf{D}_{A}\cup\mathsf{D}_{B}
also has the property that the family of its countable relatively closed subsets includes a club.

Finally, consider a subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\Sep(B)
that includes a club. By Lemma
1.2
,
\{C\cap\mathsf{D}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
includes a club in
[\mathsf{D}]^{\aleph_{0}}
.
As pointed out above,
[
17
, Corollary 6.4.2]
implies that
\{C\cap\mathsf{D}_{A}\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
includes a club in
[\mathsf{D}_{A}]^{\aleph_{0}}
.
Again by
[
17
, Corollary 7.2.8]
,
\{C\cap A\mid C\in\mathcal{C}\}
includes a club, as required.
∎

Needless to say, this proof shows that Lemma
1.3
is true for arbitrary metric structures.

1.2.
Strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and separable absorption

A separable unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{D}
is said to be
strongly self-absorbing
if
\mathcal{D}\ncong\mathbb{C}
and there exists an isomorphism
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}\otimes\mathcal{D}
that is approximately unitarily equivalent to the embedding
d\mapsto d\otimes 1_{\mathcal{D}}
.
All strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are nuclear and simple, and they satisfy
\mathcal{D}\cong\mathcal{D}^{\otimes\infty}
; see
[
56
]
.
By
[
59
, Remark 3.3]
, it automatically follows that
\mathcal{D}
is
K_{1}
-injective and this property is redundant despite being explicitly assumed in early literature about strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.

The known strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are the Jiang–Su algebra
{\mathcal{Z}}

[
30
]
, UHF algebras of infinite type, the Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
, tensor products of UHF algebras of infinite type with
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
, and the Cuntz algebra
\mathcal{O}_{2}

[
12
]
.
If all separable nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras satisfy the Universal Coefficient Theorem then there are no other strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras (
[
54
, Corollary 6.7]
)

We recall the common characterizations of
\mathcal{D}
-stability for separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
In the following
A_{\infty}
denotes the (asymptotic) sequence algebra
\ell_{\infty}(A)/c_{0}(A)
and
A^{\perp}
denotes the two-sided annihilator of
A
in the ambient
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
be a separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\mathcal{D}
a strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

A\cong A\otimes\mathcal{D}
.

(2)

There exists a unital
*
-homomorphism
\mathcal{D}\to(A_{\infty}\cap A^{\prime})/(A_{\infty}\cap A^{\perp})
.

(3)

There exists a sequence of unital
*
-homomorphisms
\pi_{n}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{M}(A)
with
\|[\pi_{n}(d),a]\|\to 0
for all
d\in\mathcal{D}
and
a\in A
.

Proof.

The equivalence
(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2)
can be deduced as the consequence of either
[
56
, Theorem 2.3]
or of
[
36
, Proposition 4.4]
.
Since
\mathcal{D}\cong\mathcal{D}^{\otimes\infty}
, it is immediate to verify the implication
(1)
\Rightarrow
(3)
by hand.
The implication
(3)
\Rightarrow
(1)
follows from
[
56
, Proposition 1.5]
and
[
46
, Theorem 7.2.2]
.

Let

\mathrm{C}^{*}

A
\mathrm{C}^{*}

\Psi_{Y}\colon\ell_{\infty}(\mathcal{M}(E))\to\mathcal{M}(E)
defined by

\Psi_{Y}((a_{n}))=\sum_{n\in Y}f_{n}a_{n}f_{n}

is completely positive and contractive.

Proof.

The sum in the definition of
\Psi_{Y}
is strictly convergent (
[
17
, Claim 1 on p. 370]
), thus
\Psi_{Y}
is well-defined. It is completely positive because each of the summands is completely positive (this is similar to
[
17
, Claim 2 on p. 370]
). Since
\Psi_{Y}(1)=\sum_{n\in Y}f_{n}^{2}\leq 1_{\mathcal{M}(E)}

\Psi_{Y}
is contractive.
∎

The following is
[
40
, Lemma 3.1]
but we include a proof for the reader’s convenience (
\pi\colon\mathcal{M}(E)\to\mathcal{Q}(E)
denotes the quotient map).

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
E
is
\sigma
-unital and
f_{n}
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is a sequence of positive contractions in
E
such that the sum
\sum_{n}f_{n}^{2}
strictly converges to
1_{\mathcal{M}(E)}
and
f_{m}f_{n}=0
if
|m-n|\geq 2
.
Then for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and every sequence
(a_{n})
in
\ell_{\infty}(\mathcal{M}(E))
the sum
\sum_{n}f_{n}a_{n}f_{n+k}
strictly converges to an element of
\mathcal{M}(E)
and

\|\sum_{n}f_{n}a_{n}f_{n+k}\|\leq\sup_{n}\|a_{n}\|
.
We also have

\textstyle\|\pi(\sum_{n}f_{n}a_{n}f_{n+k})\|\leq\limsup_{n}\|a_{n}\|.

Proof.

Fix natural numbers
i<j
, and consider
\mathcal{M}(E)^{\oplus(j-i)}
as a Hilbert
\mathcal{M}(E)
-module.
Fix a contraction
b\in\mathcal{M}(E)
.
By the generalized Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we have the following.

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\sum_{n=i}^{j}f_{n}a_{n}f_{n+k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leq\|\sum_{n=i}^{j}f_{n}a_{n}a_{n}^{*}f_{n}\|\|\sum_{n=i}^{j}f_{n+k}^{2}b\|

\displaystyle\textstyle\leq\sup_{n\geq i}\|a_{n}\|^{2}\|\sum_{n=i}^{j}f_{n}^{2}\|\|\sum_{n=i}^{j}b^{*}f_{n+k}^{2}b\|

\displaystyle\textstyle\leq\sup_{n\geq i}\|a_{n}\|^{2}.

With respect to the strict topology, we have
\lim_{j\to\infty}\sum_{n=i}^{j}f_{n}^{2}=(1-e_{i})
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}\sum_{n=i}^{j}b^{*}f_{n+k}^{2}b=b^{*}(1-e_{i+k})b
.
Since
\lim_{i\to\infty}\|b^{*}(1-e_{i+k})b\|=0
when

, the series is strictly convergent.

When inserting
b=1
and letting

, the above estimate shows
\|\sum_{n\geq i}f_{n}a_{n}f_{n+k}\|\leq\sup_{n\geq i}\|a_{n}\|
for all

.
This implies both of the desired norm estimates.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A
be a
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
Given any strictly separable subset
X
of
\mathcal{M}(A)
, there exists a nondegenerate separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
B\subseteq A
such that under the canonical inclusion
\mathcal{M}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{M}(A)
, one has
X\subseteq\mathcal{M}(B)
.

As a consequence, if
\mathcal{C}
is any cofinal subset of
\Sep(A)
, then for any separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
C
of
\mathcal{Q}(A)
there exists
B\in\mathcal{C}
with
C\subseteq\mathcal{Q}(B)
.

Proof.

The “as a consequence” part follows from functoriality of the multiplier algebra under nondegenerate inclusions of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras. First note that since
A
is
\sigma
-unital, the set of
B\in\mathcal{C}
that include an approximate unit for
A
(and are therefore nondegenerate) is cofinal in
\mathcal{C}
.
Given a nondegenerate inclusion
B\subseteq A
, if
\mathcal{M}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{M}(A)
is the induced inclusion, then the homomorphism theorem also gives us an inclusion
\mathcal{Q}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{Q}(A)
.
If the first part of the statement holds and we pick a separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
C
of
\mathcal{Q}(A)
, then we may choose a separable subset
X\subseteq\mathcal{M}(A)
whose image under the quotient map is
C
, and apply the first part of the statement to find the desired element
B\in\mathcal{C}
.

Hence it suffices to prove the first part of the statement.
We may assume without loss of generality that
X
is self-adjoint and contains the unit.
We pick a countable approximate unit
e_{n}\in A
.
Let
B
be the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra generated by
\{xe_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N},\ x\in X\}
, which is norm-separable if
X
is strictly separable.
Evidently
B
is nondegenerate in
A
because it contains all
e_{n}
.
Furthermore, one has for all
x,y\in X
that

x(ye_{n})=\lim_{m\to\infty}xe_{m}ye_{n}\in B.

In this way it follows that
xB\subseteq B
for all
x\in X
.
Since
X
was self-adjoint, we have also
Bx\subseteq B
for all
x\in X
.
Hence every
x\in X
is automatically a multiplier on
B
and the claim is proved.
∎

We can now prove Theorem
A
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}
is a strongly self-absorbing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and that
A
is a
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra that is separably
\mathcal{D}
-stable.
Then
\mathcal{Q}(A)
is
\mathcal{D}
-saturated.

Proof.

Let
h\in A
be a strictly positive element.
As
A
is separably
\mathcal{D}
-stable, the collection
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\Sep(A)
of separable
\mathcal{D}
-stable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebras in

containing

is a club.
Such subalgebras are automatically nondegenerate.
By Lemma
2.4
, we see that every separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{Q}(A)
can be seen as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{Q}(B)
for some
B\in\mathcal{C}
.
Considering the definition of
\mathcal{D}
-saturation, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{Q}(B)
is
\mathcal{D}
-saturated for every
B\in\mathcal{C}
.
To put it differently, it suffices to prove the conclusion of the theorem in case when

is separable.
Let us assume that as of now.

Suppose that
B
is any separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{Q}(A)
.
By
[
28
, Lemma 6.8]
, there exists a unital
*
-homomorphism from
\mathcal{D}
into
\mathcal{Q}(A)\cap B^{\prime}
if and only if there exist two completely positive maps of order zero
\tilde{\Psi}_{0},\tilde{\Psi}_{1}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{Q}(A)\cap B^{\prime}
whose ranges commute pointwise and such that
\tilde{\Psi}_{0}(1)+\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(1)=1
.
We proceed to find such maps.

Fix a countable subset
\{b_{n}\mid n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
of the unit ball of
\mathcal{M}(A)
whose image under the quotient map is dense in the unit ball of
B
.
By Lemma
2.1
there is an approximate unit
e_{n}
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of
A
such that with

f_{n}=(e_{n+1}-e_{n})^{1/2}

the series
\sum_{n}f_{n}^{2}
strictly converges to
1_{\mathcal{M}(A)}
,
f_{m}f_{n}=0
whenever
|m-n|\geq 2
, and furthermore we have

(2.1)

\|[f_{n},b_{j}]\|<2^{-n}\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq n.

Fix an increasing sequence
F_{n}
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of finite subsets of the unit ball of
\mathcal{D}
with dense union.
Since we assumed
A
to be separable, we have
A\cong A\otimes\mathcal{D}
.
As
\mathcal{D}
embeds approximately centrally into itself, we can find a sequence of unital
*
-homomorphisms
\pi_{n}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{M}(A)
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
[\pi_{n}(d),a]\to 0
in norm for all

and
d\in\mathcal{D}
.
By passing to a subsequence, if necessary, we may ensure that the sequence
\pi_{n}
satisfies the following conditions for all

:

(i)

\|[\pi_{n}(d),f_{m}]\|<2^{-n}
for all

and

,

(ii)

\|[\pi_{n}(d),f_{n}b_{k}]\|<2^{-n}
for all

and

,

(iii)

\|[\pi_{n}(d_{1}),f_{j}\pi_{j}(d_{2})f_{j}]\|<2^{-n}
for all

and

.

Define
\Psi_{i}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{M}(A)
for

by

\Psi_{i}(d):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{2n+i}\pi_{2n+i}(d)f_{2n+i}.

Each
\Psi_{i}
is well-defined and completely positive by Lemma

.
Clearly
\Psi_{0}(1)+\Psi_{1}(1)=1
.
For

, let
\tilde{\Psi}_{i}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{Q}(A)
be the composition with the quotient map
\mathcal{M}(A)\to\mathcal{Q}(A)
.
Then
\tilde{\Psi}_{0}
and
\tilde{\Psi}_{1}
are completely positive maps from
\mathcal{D}
into
\mathcal{Q}(A)
such that
\tilde{\Psi}_{0}(1)+\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(1)=1
.

First we observe for all

and

f_{n}\pi_{n}(d)f_{n}b_{k}\approx_{2^{-n}}f_{n}^{2}b_{k}\pi_{n}(d)\approx_{2^{-n+1}}b_{k}f_{n}^{2}\pi_{n}(d)\approx_{2^{-n}}b_{k}f_{n}\pi_{n}(d)f_{n}.

Therefore

, all

, and all

.
By continuity, the range of the induced map

is included in

.

Our next task is to prove that the ranges of
\Psi_{0}
and
\Psi_{1}
commute pointwise modulo

.
For all

and

we have

f_{n+1}^{2}f_{n}\pi_{n}(d)f_{n}\approx_{2^{-n+1}}f_{n}\pi_{n}(d)f_{n}f_{n+1}^{2}.

Finally, for all

we get

\displaystyle f_{n+1}\pi_{n+1}(d_{1})f_{n+1}f_{n}\pi_{n}(d_{2})f_{n}

\displaystyle\approx_{2^{-n}}f_{n+1}^{2}\pi_{n+1}(d_{1})f_{n}\pi_{n}(d_{2})f_{n}

\displaystyle\approx_{2^{-n}}f_{n+1}^{2}f_{n}\pi_{n}(d_{2})f_{n}\pi_{n+1}(d_{1})

\displaystyle\approx_{2^{-n+1}}f_{n}\pi_{n}(d_{2})f_{n}f_{n+1}^{2}\pi_{n+1}(d_{1})

\displaystyle\approx_{2^{-n}}f_{n}\pi_{n}(d_{2})f_{n}f_{n+1}\pi_{n+1}(d_{1})f_{n+1}.

Since

if

, this shows that for all

,

, and

,

and

,

\Psi_{i}(a)\Psi_{i}(b)-\Psi(1)\Psi_{i}(ab)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{2n+i}[\pi_{2n+i}(a),f_{2n+i}^{2}]\pi_{2n+i}(b)f_{2n+i}\in A.

\mathcal{D}
.

3.
A refinement of Schur’s Lemma for Property (T) groups

The main results of the present section are Lemma

Let
A
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
By
\mathcal{S}(A)
we denote the space of states on

In the proofs of the lemmas below we use the theory of Hilbert modules. In particular we will need the the fact that
\mathcal{M}(A)={\mathcal{L}}_{A}(A)

For

{\mathcal{E}}_{m}:M_{m}(A)=M_{m}\otimes A\to A\quad\text{via}\quad{\mathcal{E}}_{m}=\tau_{m}\otimes\id_{A}.

Note that
{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
extends to a conditional expectation

We make use of the canonical identification

A
-linear endomorphisms on the right

(see e.g.,
[

Notation
.

Let
A
be a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and

.
Given any right

-Hilbert module
\mathcal{Y}

(Note that for

We observe (as a consequence of the definition) that this norm obeys the equality

(3.2)

\|1_{m}\otimes a\|_{m,A}=\|a\|

for all

,

, and

\|X(1\otimes a)\|_{m,A}\leq\|a\|\|X\|_{m,A}\quad\text{and}\quad\|XY\|_{m,A}\leq\|X\|\|Y\|_{m,A}.

Here is another well-known fact included for reference.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.

(1)

\varphi
of

has a unique extension to a state
\tilde{\varphi}
of
\mathcal{M}(A)
.

(2)

in

Notation

5.
Applications and concluding remarks

The Calkin algebra was considered a prime candidate for a

The following observation was communicated to us by James Gabe, Chris Schafhauser, and Stuart White during the Memorial Conference in honour of Eberhard Kirchberg in Münster in July 2023. It precipitated the work presented here and is included with their kind permission.

Theorem 5.1

-algebra.

Power partial isometries
Kritika Babbar, Amit Maji

Abstract
. In this paper we obtain a complete characterization of reducing, invariant, and hyperinvariant subspaces for the completely non-unitary component of a power partial isometry. In particular, precise characterization of reducing, invariant, and hyperinvariant subspaces of a truncated shift operator has been achieved.

47A15, 47A45, 47A65, 47A20, 46E22, 46E40, 47B35, 47B32

1.
Introduction

Partial isometries form an important class of operators in the linear analysis. They have widespread applications in various fields like in the polar decomposition of operators, C
∗
- algebras, quantum physics, and they form a fundamental object in the dimension theory of von Neumann algebras; see the papers
[
3
]
,
[
24
]
for a thorough presentation of these connections.
Partial isometries, on the other hand, are more complicated to study than isometries, so we restrict ourselves to the power partial isometries: operators whose all positive powers are partial isometries. The primary reason to do so is that power partial isometries have more concrete structure due to their algebraic properties. The main objective of this paper is to fully characterize the reducing, invariant, and hyperinvariant subspaces for the completely non-unitary (c.n.u. in short) component of a power partial isometry.

The decomposition of power partial isometries was first studied by Halmos and Wallen in
[
15
]
, where they proved that every power partial isometry is a direct sum of a unitary operator, a unilateral shift, a backward shift, and truncated shifts. This work has inspired a great deal of mathematicians and it becomes one of the active areas of current research that brings together several areas of mathematics. Noteworthy contributions to this field have been made by Guyker
[
13
]
, Burdak
[
4
]
, Catepillán, and Szymański
[
6
]
, Huef, Raeburn, and Tolich
[
17
]
, and others. The canonical decomposition of a power partial isometry, on one hand, gives a unique orthogonal sum of a unitary operator and a c.n.u. operator; and, on the other hand, Halmos-Wallen decomposition yields that the combining components of unilateral shift, backward shift, and truncated shifts form the c.n.u. component of a power partial isometry. Since the spectral theorem provides a complete structure and representation for normal operators, in particular, unitary operators, it suffices to work with the c.n.u. power partial isometry.

The first natural step to understand the structure of linear operators on Hilbert spaces is investigating its reducing and invariant subspaces. Another related problem is to find all the hyperinvariant subspaces of an operator. These problems have fascinated the attention of various mathematicians, and many results are known for a handful of specific operators but many operators still remain a mystery.
Dilation theory is one of the effective tools to study the structure of a contraction. One of the most far-reaching results is Sz.-Nagy’s dilation result which states that every pure contraction on a Hilbert space can be dilated to a unilateral shift on a larger Hilbert space (see
[
22
]
). Another celebrated result is Sarason’s commuting lifting theorem
[
26
]
which says that an operator that commutes with a pure contraction can be lifted to an operator commuting with the shift operator without altering the norm. These results play crucial roles in this paper to find the complete and precise characterization of reducing, invariant, and hyperinvariant subspaces of a truncated shift operator which is a particular case of power partial isometry and then we utilize it for the case of an arbitrary c.n.u. power partial isometry.

The study of reducing subspaces of unilateral shift was initiated by Halmos in his seminal paper
[
14
]
. It captivates the interests of many mathematicians and it stimulates the study of reducing subspaces of various classes of operators, including analytic Toeplitz operators, truncated Toeplitz operators which can be found in
[
23
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
. The study of invariant subspaces has evolved significantly, but still it remains one of the most intriguing problems in operator theory to find a complete characterization of invariant subspaces of an operator. Beurling
[
2
]
gave a striking result where he characterized all the invariant subspaces of shift operator on the Hardy space over the open unit disc which was later generalized by Lax and Halmos for vector-valued Hardy space (see
[
18
]
,
[
14
]
). Later on, many mathematicians proved some remarkable results about the invariant subspaces (see
[
16
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
). In
[
29
]
, Timotin characterized the invariant subspaces of direct sum of unilateral shift and backward shift on scalar-valued Hardy space which was further generalized by Gu and Luo for vector-valued Hardy space in
[
12
]
. Note that the direct sum of unilateral shift and backward shift is a particular case of a c.n.u. power partial isometry. In Section 3 of this note, we explicitly find the reducing subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry. In Section 4, we obtain the complete structure of invariant subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry.

The motivation behind hyperinvariant subspaces of an operator is that they give information about the commutant of an operator. The notion of hyperinvariant subspaces was first introduced by Douglas and Pearcy in
[
10
]
. They characterized the hyperinvariant subspaces of normal operators as well as of unilateral shift on a vector-valued Hardy space. Extending that, Douglas
[
9
]
characterized hyperinvariant subspaces of a general isometry. Later on, hyperinvariant subspaces have been extensively studied by various mathematicians like Wu, Uchiyama (see
[
32
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
). In
[
30
]
, Uchiyama found the hyperinvariant subspaces of pure contractions. In Section 5, we present an explicit structure for all the hyperinvariant subspaces of c.n.u. power partial isometry and in particular, a pure power partial isometry.

2.
Preliminaries

In what follows,
\mathcal{H}
denotes a separable Hilbert space over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
,
I_{\mathcal{H}}
as the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
. We simply write
I
if the Hilbert space is clear from the context. Let
\mathcal{B(H)}
stand for the algebra of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. For
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we denote
\mathcal{N}(T)
and
\mathcal{R}(T)
as the kernel and range of
T
, respectively. A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
is said to be invariant under
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
if
T\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}
reduces
T
if
\mathcal{M}
is invariant under
T
and
T^{*}
both. A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant for
T
if
\mathcal{M}
is invariant under all the operators commuting with
T
. We say
T
is a contraction if
\|Th\|\leq\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
and it is a pure contraction if
T^{*m}\rightarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty
in the strong operator topology. A contraction
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be completely non-unitary (c.n.u. in short) if there does not exist any nonzero reducing subspace
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{H}
such that
T|_{\mathcal{L}}
is unitary. An operator
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
is said to be an isometry if
T^{*}T=I_{\mathcal{H}}
, that is,
\|Th\|=\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. A partial isometry
T
is a contraction such that
\|Th\|=\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{N}(T)^{\perp}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
be two complex separable Hilbert spaces.
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
denotes the space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued square-integrable functions on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
with respect to the normalized Lebesgue measure. Let
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
be the Hardy space of
\mathcal{E}
-valued analytic functions
f
on the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
such that

\|f\|=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|f(re^{it})\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\,dt\right)<\infty,

and we often use
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
as a closed subspace of
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
(in the sense of radial limits). Let
L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}
denote the algebra of operator-valued bounded functions on
\mathbb{T}
and
H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}
(
H^{\infty}
for the scalar case) stand for the algebra of operator-valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. For
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}
, the Laurent operator (or multiplication operator) from
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
to
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}
, denoted by
L_{\Phi}
, is defined as

L_{\Phi}h=\Phi h\quad(h\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})).

Let
P_{+}^{\mathcal{E}}
be the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
onto
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
. The Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
is defined by

T_{\Phi}h=P_{+}^{\mathcal{F}}(\Phi h)\quad(h\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})).

In particular, if
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{F}
and
\Phi(z)=zI
, then we write
T_{\Phi}
as
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
which is the multiplication operator by the coordinate function
z
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
. We will simply write
M_{z}
if the underlying Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
is apparent from the context. The Toeplitz operator
T_{\Phi}
is characterized by the operator equation
(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}=T_{\Phi}
. If
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}
, then
T_{\Phi}
is called an analytic Toeplitz operator and is characterized by the equation
M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
. Let
J
on
L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
be defined by

(Jf)(e^{it})=f({e^{-it}})\quad(f\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}},e^{it}\in\mathbb{T}).

Then
J
is a self-adjoint unitary map. For
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}
, the Hankel operator
H_{\Phi}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
is defined by

H_{\Phi}h=P_{+}^{\mathcal{F}}J(\Phi h)\quad(h\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})).

The Hankel operator
H_{\Phi}
is characterized by the equation
H_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}H_{\Phi}
. The adjoint of
H_{\Phi}
is given by
H_{\Phi}^{*}=H_{\tilde{\Phi}}
where
\tilde{\Phi}(z)=\Phi(\bar{z})^{*}
. Also, recall
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}
is inner if
\Theta(e^{it})^{*}\Theta(e^{it})=I_{\mathcal{E}}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Moreover,
\Theta
is right extremal if
\Theta
cannot be factored as
\Theta_{1}\Theta_{2}
, where
\Theta_{1}
is inner and
\Theta_{2}
is a non-constant inner function. The celebrated Beurling-Lax-Halmos theorem says that any invariant subspace of
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
is of the form
\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{*},\mathcal{E})}
is an inner function and
\mathcal{E}_{*}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{E}
(see
[
22
]
,
[
25
]
for more details).

Recall the basic definitions and properties of the main objects of this paper, namely, truncated shift and power partial isometry. Let
k\geq 1
be any natural number. Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be a Hilbert space and

\mathcal{H}=\underbrace{\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{H}_{0}}_{k\,\,times}
which is identified with
\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
. A truncated shift of index
k
, denoted by
J_{k}
, is defined on
\mathcal{H}
as
J_{k}=0
for
k=1
and for
k\geq 2
,

J_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})=(0,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1})\quad(x_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{0},i=1,\ldots,k).

Thus the matrix representation of
J_{k}
is of the form

J_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
I_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}&0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&I_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}&0&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\ldots&I_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}&0\end{pmatrix}_{k\times k}.

An operator
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
is said to be a power partial isometry if
T^{n}
is a partial isometry for all
n\geq 1
. Isometries, co-isometries, and truncated shifts are some of the examples of power partial isometries. We record the following characterizations of a partial isometry and properties of power partial isometry which will be used frequently in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
4
]
)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

T
is a partial isometry.

(2)

T^{*}
is a partial isometry.

(3)

T^{*}TT^{*}=T^{*}
.

(4)

TT^{*}T=T
.

(5)

TT^{*}
is an orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{R}(T)
.

(6)

T^{*}T
is an orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{R}(T^{*})
.

For every power partial isometry
T
, we write
E_{k}=T^{*k}T^{k}
and
F_{k}=T^{k}T^{*k}
for all
k\geq 0
as the initial and final projections, respectively. Clearly,
E_{k}\geq E_{k+1}
and
F_{k}\geq F_{k+1}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Lemma 2.2

(cf.
[
15
]
)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B(H)}
be a power partial isometry. Then

(1)

E_{k}E_{l}=E_{l}E_{k}
and
F_{k}F_{l}=F_{l}F_{k}
for all
k,l\geq 0
.

(2)

E_{k}F_{l}=F_{l}E_{k}
for all
k,l\geq 0
.

(3)

E_{k}F_{l}
and
E_{k}E_{l}
are orthogonal projections.

(4)

TE_{k+1}=E_{k}T
for all
k\geq 0
.

(5)

TF_{k}=F_{k+1}T
for all
k\geq 0
.

We have the following decomposition theorem for a power partial isometry given by Halmos and Wallen in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a power partial isometry. Then there exist subspaces

\mathcal{H}_{u},\mathcal{H}_{s},\mathcal{H}_{b}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{k}\,\,(k\geq 1)
reducing
T
and

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{u}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{b}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{s}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{k}\right),

such that
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}}
is a unitary,
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{b}}
is a backward shift,
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}
is a unilateral shift and
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}
is a truncated shift of index
k
.

In
[
4
]
, Burdak explicitly found all the orthogonal decomposition subspaces for a power partial isometry
T
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{u}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcap\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}\mathcal{H}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{*n}\mathcal{H}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{s}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcap\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{*n}\mathcal{H}\right)\cap\left(\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*}))\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{b}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcap\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}\mathcal{H}\right)\cap\left(\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{*n}(\mathcal{N}(T))\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{t}=\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*}))\right)\cap\left(\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{*n}(\mathcal{N}(T))\right).

More precisely, we determine a representation of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
from the Halmos and Wallen decomposition theorem. In fact,

\mathcal{H}_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{k}\left(F_{n-1}-F_{n}\right)\left(E_{k-n}-E_{k-n+1}\right)\mathcal{H}.

Then it is easy to see that

\mathcal{H}_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{k}\mathcal{R}\left(F_{n-1}-F_{n}\right)\cap\mathcal{R}\left(E_{k-n}-E_{k-n+1}\right).

Note that for any
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle F_{p-1}-F_{p}

\displaystyle=F_{p-1}-F_{p}F_{p-1}

\displaystyle=(I_{\mathcal{H}}-F_{p})F_{p-1}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{N}(T^{*p})}P_{\mathcal{R}(T^{p-1})}.

Since
P_{\mathcal{N}(T^{*p})}
and
P_{\mathcal{R}(T^{p-1})}
commute, it follows that

\mathcal{R}(F_{p-1}-F_{p})=\mathcal{N}(T^{*p})\cap\mathcal{R}(T^{p-1})
. Similarly,
\mathcal{R}(E_{p-1}-E_{p})=\mathcal{N}(T^{p})\cap\mathcal{R}(T^{*(p-1)})
. We now prove that

\mathcal{N}(T^{*p})\cap\mathcal{R}(T^{p-1})=T^{p-1}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*})).

Let
x\in\mathcal{N}(T^{*p})\cap\mathcal{R}(T^{p-1})
. Then
T^{p-1}T^{*(p-1)}x=x
and
T^{*}(T^{*(p-1)}x)=T^{*p}x=0
. This implies
x\in T^{p-1}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*}))
. Hence

\mathcal{N}(T^{*p})\cap\mathcal{R}(T^{p-1})\subseteq T^{p-1}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*})).

Suppose that
y\in\mathcal{N}(T^{*})
. Then

T^{*p}T^{p-1}y=T^{*}T^{*(p-1)}T^{p-1}y=T^{*p}T^{p}T^{*}y=0,

as
T^{*}E_{p-1}=E_{p}T^{*}
.
Thus
T^{p-1}y\in\mathcal{N}(T^{*p})\cap\mathcal{R}(T^{p-1})
. Hence
\mathcal{N}(T^{*p})\cap\mathcal{R}(T^{p-1})=T^{p-1}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*}))
. Similarly,
\mathcal{N}(T^{p})\cap\mathcal{R}(T^{*(p-1)})=T^{*(p-1)}(\mathcal{N}(T))
. Therefore, we get

\mathcal{H}_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{k}T^{n-1}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*}))\cap T^{*(k-n)}(\mathcal{N}(T)).

Returning back to the well-known
Sz.-Nagy and Foiaş’
dilation and the
Sarason’s commuting lifting theorem which are essential tools in comprehending the structure of contractions and hence for any operator.

Let
T
be a contraction on
\mathcal{H}
. We say
M_{z}
on
{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
is an isometric dilation of
T
on
\mathcal{H}
if there exists an isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
(cf.
[
28
]
) such that

\Pi T^{*}=M_{z}^{*}\Pi.

Suppose
T
is a pure contraction on
\mathcal{H}
,
D_{T^{*}}=(I_{\mathcal{H}}-TT^{*})^{1\over 2}
, and
\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}=\overline{\mathcal{R}(D_{T^{*}})}
. Define an isometry
\Pi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D})
by

(\Pi h)(z)=D_{T^{*}}(I-zT^{*})^{-1}h\quad(z\in\mathbb{D},h\in\mathcal{H}).

Then it is easy to see that
\Pi T^{*}=M_{z}^{*}\Pi.
Hence
M_{z}
is an isometric dilation of
T
and
\Pi\mathcal{H}
is
M_{z}^{*}
invariant subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D})
. Thus
T
is unitarily equivalent to
P_{\Pi\mathcal{H}}M_{z}|_{\Pi\mathcal{H}}
, that is,
T\cong P_{\Pi\mathcal{H}}M_{z}|_{\Pi\mathcal{H}}
, and we often use
T=P_{\Pi\mathcal{H}}M_{z}|_{\Pi\mathcal{H}}
if it is clear from the context.
Also note that

H^{2}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}}(\mathbb{D})=\bigvee\{z^{m}\Pi h:m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},h\in\mathcal{H}\},

which means that
M_{z}
is the minimal isometric dilation of
T
.
In the sequel, due to Halmos and Wallen decomposition, we work with the c.n.u. power partial isometry

T=M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}\oplus(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}

on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}
, where
J_{k}
is the truncated shift of index
k
on

\mathbb{E}_{k}=\underbrace{\mathcal{E}_{k}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{k}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{k}}_{k\,\,times}=\mathcal{E}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k},

and
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F},\text{ and }\mathcal{E}_{k}
are complex separable Hilbert spaces for all
k\geq 1
(cf.
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
).

Theorem 2.4

(cf.
[
11
]
)
.

Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be contractions on
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
and let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be their minimal isometric dilations on
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
, respectively. For every bounded operator
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{2},\mathcal{E}_{1})
satisfying
T_{1}X=XT_{2}
, there exists
Y\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{1})
such that

(1)

V_{1}Y=YV_{2}
,

(2)

X=P_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}Y|_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}
,

(3)

We conclude this section by reviewing the notion of range functions (see
[
16
]
) which will be helpful in finding the invariant subspaces of power partial isometry.

Definition 2.5
.

A range function
K
of
\mathcal{E}
is a function on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
which takes values in the closed subspaces of
\mathcal{E}
.
K
is said to be measurable if the orthogonal projection
P(e^{it})
on
K(e^{it})
is measurable, that is,
\langle P(e^{it})a_{1},a_{2}\rangle
is a measurable scalar function for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
.

For each measurable function
K
of
\mathcal{E}
, we denote
\mathcal{M}_{K}
as the set of all functions
f\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
such that
f(e^{it})\in K(e^{it})
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.

3.
Reducing subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry

In this section, we discuss reducing subspaces of truncated shift of index
k
. Using this result, we obtain the reducing subspaces of c.n.u. power partial isometry.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert space and
k
be any natural number. Then a closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
is reducing for truncated shift of index
k
on
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
if and only if
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Suppose
J_{k}
is truncated shift operator of index
k
on
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
.
For
k=1
,
J_{k}=0
and thus the result holds trivially. Assume that
k\geq 2
. Define a map
\Pi:\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
by

\Pi(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1})=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\qquad(a_{i}\in\mathcal{E}).

Clearly,
\Pi
is an isometry such that
\Pi J_{k}^{*}=M_{z}^{*}\Pi
. More precisely,
M_{z}
is the minimal isometric dilation of
J_{k}
such that

J_{k}\cong P_{\mathcal{Q}}M_{z}|_{\mathcal{Q}},

where
\mathcal{Q}=\Pi(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})=[z^{k}{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a reducing subspace of
J_{k}
and
P_{\mathcal{M}}
be the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
onto
\mathcal{M}
. Then
P_{\mathcal{M}}J_{k}=J_{k}P_{\mathcal{M}}
which implies
P_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{*}))\subseteq\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{*})
. Also note that

\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{*})=\mathcal{E}\oplus\underbrace{\{0\}\oplus\cdots\oplus\{0\}}_{(k-1)\,\,times}.

Hence, for each
a\in\mathcal{E}
(considered as a constant function in
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
),
\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a\in\mathcal{E}
. Now define a map
P:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
by

P\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}\right):=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m}.

It is easy to check that
P
is a well defined map on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
and
P\Pi=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}
. We first prove that
P
is a projection. Let
f=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m},g=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}z^{m}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
. Suppose

\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m}=x_{m}\in\mathcal{E}\mbox{~~for each~~}m.

Consider

\displaystyle P^{2}f=P^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*})^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+P\left(\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}x_{m}z^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}x_{m})z^{m}

\displaystyle=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}((\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*})^{2}a_{m})z^{m}

\displaystyle=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m}

\displaystyle=P\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=Pf.

Also,

\displaystyle\left\langle f,P^{*}g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle P\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}\right),\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}z^{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m},\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}z^{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right),\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}b_{m}z^{m}\right\rangle+\left\langle\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m},\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}b_{m}z^{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m},\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}b_{m}z^{m}\right)\right\rangle+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}\left\langle\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m},b_{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m},\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}b_{m}z^{m}\right)\right\rangle+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}\left\langle a_{m},\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}b_{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m},\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}b_{m}z^{m}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m},\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}b_{m})z^{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}b_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}b_{m})z^{m}\right\rangle.

Therefore,

\displaystyle P^{*}g

\displaystyle=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}b_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}b_{m})z^{m}=P\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}z^{m}\right)=Pg.

Hence
P
is an orthogonal projection on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
.

We now prove that
P
commutes with
M_{z}
. To do that, first recall that

\displaystyle\Pi J_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=M_{z}^{*}\Pi

\displaystyle\mbox{and~~~}P_{\mathcal{M}}J_{k}

\displaystyle=J_{k}P_{\mathcal{M}}.

Consider

\displaystyle PM_{z}f

\displaystyle=P\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m+1}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k-1}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m+1}

\displaystyle=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}M_{z}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\left(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{k-1}\right)z^{k}+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m+1}

\displaystyle=\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}J_{k}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}(a_{k-1},0,\ldots,0)z^{k}+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m+1}

\displaystyle=\Pi J_{k}P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}(J_{k}^{*})^{k-1}(0,\ldots,0,a_{k-1})z^{k}+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m+1}

\displaystyle=\Pi J_{k}\Pi^{*}\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\Pi(J_{k}^{*})^{k-1}P_{\mathcal{M}}(0,\ldots,0,a_{k-1})z^{k}+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m+1}

\displaystyle=\Pi\Pi^{*}M_{z}\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+(M_{z}^{*})^{k-1}\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}(a_{k-1}z^{k-1})z^{k}+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m+1}

\displaystyle=M_{z}\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}(a_{k-1}z^{k-1})z+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m+1}

\displaystyle=M_{z}\left(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)+\sum\limits_{m=k}^{\infty}(\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}a_{m})z^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=M_{z}Pf.

The third last equality holds because
M_{z}\Pi P_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m}\right)\in\mathcal{R}(\Pi)
. Therefore,
PM_{z}=M_{z}P
yields
P=T_{\Phi}
for some
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B(E)}}(\mathbb{D})
. Since
P
is a projection,
\Phi
is a constant function, say
\varphi_{0}
, on
\mathcal{E}
such that
\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{0}^{*}=\varphi_{0}^{2}
. Thus
P_{\mathcal{M}}=\Pi^{*}T_{\varphi_{0}}\Pi
infers

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\Pi^{*}T_{\varphi_{0}}\left([z^{k}{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pi^{*}T_{\varphi_{0}}\left(\bigvee\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\{z^{m}\eta:\eta\in\mathcal{E}\}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pi^{*}\left(\bigvee\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}\{z^{m}\varphi_{0}(\eta):\eta\in\mathcal{E}\}\right)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\varphi_{0}(\mathcal{E})\oplus\varphi_{0}(\mathcal{E})\oplus\cdots\oplus\varphi_{0}(\mathcal{E})}_{k\,\,times},

where
\varphi_{0}(\mathcal{E})
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{E}
. Hence any reducing subspace of
J_{k}
is of the form
\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{E}
.

The converse is trivially true. The proof is completed.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
J_{k}
be the truncated shift of index
k
on
\mathbb{E}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
for
k\geq 1
and let
T=M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}\oplus(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}
be a c.n.u. power partial isometry on
\mathcal{H}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}\right)
. Then the only reducing subspaces of
T
are those of the form
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{S}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}\right)
, where
\mathcal{E}_{*},\mathcal{F}_{*}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{k}
are closed subspaces of
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{k}
, respectively.

Proof.

Following Halmos-Wallen decomposition of the c.n.u. power partial isometry
T
on
\mathcal{H}
, we have
\mathcal{H}_{s}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{H}_{b}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathcal{H}_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{k}
for
k\geq 1
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a reducing subspace of
T
. We claim that
P_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}
for
i=s,b,k\,\,\forall\,\,k\geq 1,
and

\mathcal{M}=P_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\mathcal{M}\oplus P_{\mathcal{H}_{b}}\mathcal{M}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\mathcal{M}\right)=(\mathcal{H}_{s}\cap\mathcal{M})\oplus(\mathcal{H}_{b}\cap\mathcal{M})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(\mathcal{H}_{k}\cap\mathcal{M}).

To prove the first claim, it suffices to show that
P_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}

which is equivalent to prove that
P_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}P_{\mathcal{M}}=P_{\mathcal{M}}P_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}
, i.e.,
P_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{i}
for
i=s,b,k\,\,\forall\,\,k\geq 1
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{H}_{s}=\bigcap\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{*n}\mathcal{H}\cap\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*}))
. Then, for
n\geq 0
,
x=T^{*n}x_{n}
for some
x_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
and
x=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}y_{n}
, where
y_{n}\in\mathcal{N}(T^{*})
. Now

P_{\mathcal{M}}x=P_{\mathcal{M}}T^{*n}x_{n}=T^{*n}P_{\mathcal{M}}x_{n}\in T^{*n}\mathcal{H},

where the second equality follows as
\mathcal{M}
reduces
T
and hence
T^{*n}
.
Also,

P_{\mathcal{M}}x=P_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}y_{n}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}P_{\mathcal{M}}y_{n}\in\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}(\mathcal{N}(T^{*}))

as
T^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}y_{n}=P_{\mathcal{M}}T^{*}y_{n}=0.
Therefore,
P_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}
.

Interchanging the roles of
T
and
T^{*}
yields
P_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{b}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{b}
. By a similar argument, we can prove that
P_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{H}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

For the next claim, clearly
\mathcal{M}\subseteq P_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\mathcal{M}\oplus P_{\mathcal{H}_{b}}\mathcal{M}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\mathcal{M}\right)
. And since for all
i=b,s,k\geq 1
,
P_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. Hence

\mathcal{M}=P_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\mathcal{M}\oplus P_{\mathcal{H}_{b}}\mathcal{M}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\mathcal{M}\right).

Also, since
\mathcal{M}
reduces
T
,
\mathcal{H}_{i}\cap\mathcal{M}
reduces
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}
for each
i
. Hence any reducing subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
T
is of type
\mathcal{M}_{s}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{b}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{k}\right)
, where
\mathcal{M}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{i}
reduces
T|_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}
for
i=b,s,k\geq 1
.

As we know any reducing subspace of
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
is of the form
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\mathcal{E}_{*}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{E}
and with the help of Theorem
3.1
, any reducing subspace of
T
is of the form

\mathcal{M}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{*}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{S}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}\right),

where
\mathcal{E}_{*},\mathcal{F}_{*}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{k}
are closed subspaces of
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}
, and

\mathcal{E}_{k}
, respectively.

Converse is easy to prove.

Remark 3.3
.

The above proof yields that reducing subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry are always splitting subspaces. However, this does not hold in general. In fact, in the case of the identity operator
I_{\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{K}}
on
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{K}
, every subspace reduces
I_{\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{K}}
but not every subspace is splitting subspace.

4.
Invariant subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry

In this section we obtain the complete structure of invariant subspaces of the c.n.u. power partial isometry
T=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}\oplus M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\right)
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}\right)
, where

\mathbb{E}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
. In order to find the invariant subspaces of
T
, first we make some reductions which are inspired by the work done by Gu and Luo in
[
12
]
.

We first obtain the invariant subspaces of
T^{*}=M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}}\oplus(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}})^{*}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}^{*}\right)
. To do that, first set
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{k}
and
\mathbb{E}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}
. Also
\mathbb{E}_{k}
is identified with
[z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
. Now onwards, we just write
\mathbb{E}_{k}=[z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
for
k\geq 1
. Since
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
is the minimal isometric dilation of
\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}
, thus we simply write

\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}=P_{\mathbb{E}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}|_{\mathbb{E}}
, and

\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}^{*}=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}})^{*}|_{\mathbb{E}}
, where
J_{1}=0
and

(4.1)

J_{k}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{k-2}a_{m}z^{m+1}\quad(k\geq 2).

We make the following reductions to find the invariant subspaces of
T^{*}
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be an invariant subspace of
T^{*}
. Consider

T_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&M_{z}^{*}\end{pmatrix}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}).

\mathcal{N}
is invariant under
T^{*}
implies
\mathcal{N}
is also invariant under
T_{1}
. Let

T_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{z}&0\\
0&M_{z}^{*}\end{pmatrix}:L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}).

Now
\mathcal{N}\subseteq H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}
is
T_{1}
-invariant infers that
\mathcal{N}
is
T_{2}
-invariant.
Again let

T_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{z}&0\\
0&M_{z}\end{pmatrix}:L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}).

Recall the self-adjoint unitary map
J:L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\rightarrow L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}
defined as

Jf(e^{it})=f(e^{-it})\quad\quad(f\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}},e^{it}\in\mathbb{T}).

Note that
L_{z}J=JL_{z}^{*}
on
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}
. Define

J^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}J&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}:L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}).

Clearly,
\mathcal{N}
is invariant under
T_{2}
if and only if
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}:=J^{\prime}\mathcal{N}
is invariant under
T_{3}^{*}
. Hence, the problem reduces to finding the invariant subspace
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
of
T_{3}^{*}
such that

\mathcal{N}=J^{\prime}\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\subseteq H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}.

or

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{K}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E},\text{ where }\mathcal{K}=\bigvee\{e^{imt}\eta:\eta\in\mathcal{F},m\leq 0\}.

It is equivalent of saying that

\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}:=\left(L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})\right)\ominus\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
is invariant subspace of
T_{3}
such that
zH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\right)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}
. Consider

T_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}L_{z}&0\\
0&L_{z}\end{pmatrix}:L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\rightarrow L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus L^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}.

Thus
\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}
is invariant under
T_{3}
infers that
\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}
is also invariant under
T_{4}
such that
\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
. By Helson’s results in
[
16
]
, the invariant subspaces of the bilateral shift
T_{4}
are precisely those of the form

\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}=\Phi H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathcal{M}_{K},

where
\mathcal{F}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
,
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
is zero or isometric-valued function and
K
is a measurable range function on
\mathcal{F}
such that
K(e^{it})\perp\Phi(e^{it})\mathcal{F}_{0}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Note that
\mathcal{M}_{K}
is
L_{z}
-reducing.
Let
P_{\mathcal{F}}
,
P_{\mathcal{E}}
, and
P_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
be the orthogonal projections from
\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
onto
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}
, and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
, respectively.
Set

\Phi(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}({e^{it}})\\
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}(e^{it})\\
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(e^{it})\end{pmatrix},

where
\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}(e^{it})=P_{\mathcal{F}}\Phi(e^{it})
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}(e^{it})=P_{\mathcal{E}}\Phi(e^{it})
, and
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(e^{it})=P_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\Phi(e^{it})
. Then
\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F})}
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{E})}
, and
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
.

As proved in
[
12
, Lemma 3.5]
,
\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{K}\subseteq L_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{E})}
, and
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
.

Note that
\mathcal{N}=\left(L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})\right)\ominus J^{\prime}\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathcal{N}\subseteq H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}
. Let
f\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
,
g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
, and
h\in\mathbb{E}
. Then
f\oplus g\oplus h\perp J^{\prime}\mathcal{N}^{\prime\prime}
if and only if

(1)

Jf\perp\mathcal{M}_{K}
, and

(2)

Jf\oplus g\oplus h\perp\Phi H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathbb{D})
.

For (
1
), let

\mathcal{H}_{1}=\{u\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D}):u\perp\mathcal{M}_{K(e^{-it})}\}.

By
[
12
, Lemma 4.7]
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is invariant under
M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}}
and when
\mathcal{H}_{1}\neq\{0\}
, there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and an inner and right extremal function
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F})}
such that

\mathcal{H}_{1}=\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}(\mathbb{D}).

And for (
2
),
Jf\oplus g\oplus h\perp\Phi H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
\Phi^{*}(Jf\oplus g\oplus h)\perp H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathbb{D})
.
Again

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(Jf\oplus g\oplus h)\perp H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathbb{D})\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle P_{+}^{\mathcal{F}_{0}}\Phi^{*}(Jf\oplus g\oplus h)=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle P_{+}^{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}Jf+\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}^{*}g+\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}^{*}h)=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle H_{\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}f+T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}^{*}g+T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}^{*}h=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle f\oplus g\oplus h\in\mathcal{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix}H_{\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}&T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}^{*}&T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}^{*}\big|_{\mathbb{E}}\end{bmatrix}\right).

Hence

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}(W_{\Phi})\cap\left(\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}\right),

where

W_{\Phi}=\begin{bmatrix}H_{\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}&T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}^{*}&T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}^{*}\big|_{\mathbb{E}}\end{bmatrix}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathbb{D}),

and
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F})}
is inner and right extremal function.
Hence

\mathcal{M}=\left(H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}\right)\ominus\mathcal{N}=\bigvee\left\{\mathcal{R}(W_{\Phi}^{*}),\left(\mathcal{N}(T_{\Theta}^{*})\oplus\{0\}\oplus\{0\}\right)\right\}.

The converse is also true. Indeed,

W_{\Phi}^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}H_{\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}&T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}&P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}.

So we have

\begin{pmatrix}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}&0&0\\
0&M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}&0\\
0&0&\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}H_{\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}\\
T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}\\
P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}H_{\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}\\
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}\\
\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}H_{\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{0}}\\
T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{0}}\\
P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{0}}\end{pmatrix}=W_{\Phi}^{*}M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{0}}.

The second last equality holds because of the fact that
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
is the minimal isometric dilation of
\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}
and hence

\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\right)P_{\mathbb{E}}=P_{\mathbb{E}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}.

Hence
\mathcal{R}(W_{\Phi}^{*})
is invariant under
T
. Also by Beurling-Lax-Halmos theorem,
\mathcal{N}(T_{\Theta}^{*})\oplus\{0\}\oplus\{0\}
is
T
-invariant. Thus
\mathcal{M}
is
T
-invariant.
To sum up the above, we have the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}\right)
is an invariant subspace for
T=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}\oplus M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\right)
if and only if

\mathcal{M}=\bigvee\left\{\mathcal{R}(W_{\Phi}^{*}),(\mathcal{N}(T_{\Theta}^{*})\oplus\{0\}\oplus\{0\})\right\},

where
\mathbb{E}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
,
W_{\Phi}=\begin{bmatrix}H_{\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}&T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}^{*}&T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}}^{*}\big|_{\mathbb{E}}\end{bmatrix}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathbb{D})
with
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{k}
,
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
is either zero or
\Phi(e^{it})=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}({e^{it}})\\
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}(e^{it})\\
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(e^{it})\end{pmatrix}
for
\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{F})}
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{E})}
, and
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
satisfying
\Phi(e^{it})^{*}\Phi(e^{it})=I_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F})}
is zero or inner and right extremal with
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
.

Remark 4.2
.

In particular, let
T=J_{k}
, a truncated shift of index
k
on
\mathbb{E}_{k}
. Then by above theorem,
W_{\Phi}=T_{\Phi}^{*}|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}
,
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{k})}
such that
\Phi(e^{it})^{*}\Phi(e^{it})=I_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Therefore, a proper closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
is invariant under
J_{k}^{*}
if and only if
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi}^{*})\cap\mathbb{E}_{k}
, where
\Phi
is an inner function. Now since
J_{k}^{*}=M_{z}^{*}|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}
, by Beurling-Lax-Halmos theorem, there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{k}
and
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{k})}
inner function such that

\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi}^{*})\cap\mathbb{E}_{k}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}(\mathbb{D}).

Using this fact, we have a refined and independent proof for characterizing the invariant subspaces of a truncated shift which is based on the Sz.-Nagy and Foiaş’ dilation.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
J_{k}
be a truncated shift of index
k
on
\mathbb{E}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
for
k\geq 1
. A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathbb{E}_{k}
is invariant under
J_{k}
if and only if there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}_{k}
isomorphic to
\mathcal{E}_{k}
and inner functions
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{E}_{k})}
and
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F}_{k})}
such that
T_{\Theta}T_{\Phi}=M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
and
\mathcal{M}=T_{\Theta}(\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi}^{*}))
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is an invariant subspace for
J_{k}
. Since
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})
is the minimal isometric dilation of
J_{k}
on
\mathbb{E}_{k}=[z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
, so

J_{k}=P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}M_{z}|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}\text{ and }J_{k}^{*}=M_{z}^{*}|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}.

Now
J_{k}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
infers
J_{k}^{*}(\mathbb{E}_{k}\ominus\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{k}\ominus\mathcal{M}
. Therefore,
M_{z}^{*}(\mathbb{E}_{k}\ominus\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{k}\ominus\mathcal{M}
and hence, by Beurling-Lax-Halmos theorem, there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{k}
and an inner function
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{E}_{k})}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{k}\ominus\mathcal{M}=[\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
. Thus

\mathcal{M}=\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\cap\mathbb{E}_{k}=\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\cap[z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}.

Again,

[\Theta H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{k}
gives

\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\perp}=M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq T_{\Theta}H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D}).

By Douglas’ range inclusion theorem
[
8
]
, there exists a contraction
X:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})
such that
M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}=T_{\Theta}X
.
Then

T_{\Theta}XM_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}=M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}=M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}=M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}T_{\Theta}X=T_{\Theta}M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{k}}X.

Therefore,
XM_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}=M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{k}}X
and hence
X=T_{\Phi}
for some
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F}_{k})}
. Since
M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
and
T_{\Theta}
are both isometries,
T_{\Phi}
is an isometry from
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})
to
H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})
. Since
T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}=M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{k}}T_{\Phi}
, by
[
21
, Theorem 7.1]
,

\dim\,\mathcal{E}_{k}\leq\dim\,\mathcal{F}_{k}
. But
\mathcal{F}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{k}
which implies
\dim\,\mathcal{E}_{k}=\dim\,\mathcal{F}_{k}
. In turn,
\mathcal{E}_{k}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and
T_{\Theta}T_{\Phi}=M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
. Also, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=T_{\Theta}H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\ominus M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})=T_{\Theta}H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\ominus T_{\Theta}T_{\Phi}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D}).

Then it is easy to see that

\mathcal{M}=T_{\Theta}\left(\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi}^{*})\right).

Conversely, let there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}_{k}
isomorphic to
\mathcal{E}_{k}
and inner functions
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{E}_{k})}
and
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F}_{k})}
such that
T_{\Theta}T_{\Phi}=M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
and
\mathcal{M}=T_{\Theta}\left(\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi}^{*})\right)
. Then for
x\in\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi}^{*})
,

\displaystyle J_{k}\left(T_{\Theta}x\right)

\displaystyle=P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}T_{\Theta}x

\displaystyle=\left(I-M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}})^{*}\right)T_{\Theta}M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{k}}x

\displaystyle=\left(I-T_{\Theta}T_{\Phi}(M_{z^{k}}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}})^{*}\right)T_{\Theta}M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{k}}x

\displaystyle=T_{\Theta}\left(I-T_{\Phi}T_{\Phi}^{*}\right)M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}_{k}}x

\displaystyle\subseteq T_{\Theta}\left(\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi}^{*})\right).

Hence
\mathcal{M}
is invariant under
J_{k}
. This completes the proof.

Remark 4.4
.

In Theorem
4.1
, take
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and an inner and right extremal function
\Theta\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F})}
with
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
. Define
\Phi:H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\right)\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\mathbb{D})
by

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}z\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}&&&&\\
&\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}&&&\\
&&\Phi_{1}&&\\
&&&\Phi_{2}&\\
&&&&\ddots\end{pmatrix},

where
\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}
, and
\Phi_{k}\,\,(k\geq 1)
are inner functions in
H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F})}
,
H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})}
, and
H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k})}
respectively. Then,

W_{\Phi}=\begin{pmatrix}H_{z\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}}^{*}&&&&\\
&T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}^{*}&&&\\
&&T_{\Phi_{1}}^{*}\big|_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}&&\\
&&&T_{\Phi_{2}}^{*}\big|_{\mathbb{E}_{2}}&\\
&&&&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\mathcal{N}(W_{\Phi})=H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}}^{*})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi_{k}}^{*}\big|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}})
. As stated earlier, for each
k\geq 1
, there exist Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{F}_{k}
and inner functions
\Theta_{k}\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}_{k},\mathcal{E}_{k})}
such that

\mathcal{N}(T_{\Phi_{k}}^{*}\big|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}})=H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\ominus\Theta_{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}(\mathbb{D}).

Hence,

\mathcal{R}(W_{\Phi}^{*})=\{0\}\oplus\mathcal{R}(T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{R}(T_{\Theta_{k}})\cap\mathbb{E}_{k}\right).

So,
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}(T_{\Theta}^{*})\oplus\mathcal{R}(T_{\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{R}(T_{\Theta_{k}})\cap\mathbb{E}_{k}\right)
. In this case,
\mathcal{M}
is a splitting subspace of
T
.

5.
Hyperinvariant subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry

In this section, we characterize all the hyperinvariant subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry. We will use the following lemmas repeatedly in the main proof. The first lemma was given by Douglas and Pearcy in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be two Hilbert spaces and
T\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})
. A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant for
T\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
if and only if it is of the form
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is hyperinvariant for
T
.

The following lemma was given by Wu in
[
32
]
for hyperinvariant subspaces of direct sum of operators.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be two Hilbert spaces and
T_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
T_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
. Then
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
is hyperinvariant for
T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
if and only if
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{2}
are hyperinvariant for
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, respectively and
S_{1}\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
S_{2}\mathcal{M}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}
for any operators
S_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
and
S_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{1})
satisfying
S_{1}T_{1}=T_{2}S_{1}
and
S_{2}T_{2}=T_{1}S_{2}
.

The following result is of independent interest and useful in the sequel. For the sake of completeness, we write the detailed proof.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
u
be a scalar-valued inner function. Then
u
is a polynomial if and only if
u
is a monomial.

Proof.

The sufficiency of this condition is trivial. We use mathematical induction to prove the necessity part.

Let
u
be a polynomial of degree
1
.
Then
u(z)=u_{0}+u_{1}z
,
u_{0},u_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
u
is inner,
\overline{u(e^{it})}u(e^{it})=1
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Consider

\overline{u(e^{it})}u(e^{it})=|u_{0}|^{2}+|u_{1}|^{2}+\overline{u_{0}}u_{1}e^{it}+\overline{u_{1}}u_{0}e^{-it}=1

if and only if
\overline{u_{0}}u_{1}=0
. Hence either
u_{0}=0
or
u_{1}=0
which implies either
u(z)=u_{0},\,\,|u_{0}|=1
or
u(z)=u_{1}z,\,\,|u_{1}|=1
. Therefore,
u
is a monomial.

Now assume that if an inner function
u
is a polynomial of degree
n-1
, then it is a monomial. Suppose that
u(z)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}u_{m}z^{m}
is a polynomial of degree
n
and also inner. Then

\overline{u(e^{it})}u(e^{it})=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\overline{u_{m}}u_{k}e^{i(k-m)t}.

Since
u
is inner, all the coefficients except the constant term are zero. Now on comparing the coefficient of
e^{int}
, we get
\overline{u_{0}}u_{n}=0
. Hence either
u_{0}=0
or
u_{n}=0
. If
u_{n}=0
, then
u
is a polynomial of degree
n-1
and therefore, by induction hypothesis, it is a monomial of degree at most
n-1
. If
u_{n}\neq 0
, then
u_{0}=0
. Thus
u=zv
, where
v
is an inner function of degree
at most
n-1
. Again by induction hypothesis,
v
is a monomial of degree at most
n-1
and hence
u
is a monomial of degree at most
n
.

The following result may be well-known to the experts, but for the sake of being complete, we give an independent proof based on the Sz.-Nagy and Foiaş dilation.

Lemma 5.4
.

The only hyperinvariant subspaces of a truncated shift
J_{k}
on
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
of index
k
are
\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n}),\,n=0,1,\ldots,k
.

Proof.

It is trivial to check that, for each
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
,
\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n})
is hyperinvariant under
J_{k}
.

For the converse, note that
J_{k}
can be identified as
I_{\mathcal{E}}\otimes S
,
where
S
is the right shift operator on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
. So, by Lemma
5.1
, a hyperinvariant subspace of
J_{k}
is of the form
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{k}
is a hyperinvariant subspace for
S
.
Define
\Pi:\mathbb{C}^{k}\rightarrow[z^{k}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
as

\Pi(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1})=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}a_{m}z^{m}.

Clearly,
\Pi
is a unitary map and hence
\mathbb{C}^{k}
can be identified as
\mathcal{H}=[z^{k}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus z^{k}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Since
M_{z}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is the minimal isometric dilation of
S
, we write
S^{*}=M_{z}^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is
M_{z}^{*}
invariant subspace. Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant for
S
. Then
S\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, that is,
S^{*}(\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{M}
which implies
M_{z}^{*}(\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{M}
. Now by Beurling’s theorem, there exists an inner function
u\in H^{\infty}
such that

\mathcal{H}\ominus\mathcal{M}=[u{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{M}=u{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cap\mathcal{H}=u{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\ominus z^{k}{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

Now
[u{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
yields
z^{k}{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq u{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Thus there exists an inner function
v\in H^{\infty}
such that
z^{k}=uv
. Therefore,

M_{z^{k}}=T_{u}T_{v}\Rightarrow T_{u}^{*}M_{z^{k}}=T_{v}.

Hence
\bar{u}z^{k}=v\in H^{\infty}
which implies
u=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}u_{m}z^{m}
, where
u_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
0\leq m\leq k
. Since
u
is inner, using Lemma
5.3
, we get
u=u_{n}z^{n}
for some
0\leq n\leq k
, where
|u_{n}|=1
. Thus

\mathcal{M}=z^{n}{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\cap\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{R}(S^{n})=\mathcal{N}(S^{k-n}).

Hence, any hyperinvariant subspace of
J_{k}
is of the form
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{N}(S^{n})=\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n})
for some
n=0,1,\ldots,k
.

The next goal is to characterize the hyperinvariant subspaces of the direct sum of infinite truncated shifts and the above theorem will be useful. However, the technique is motivated by the above Lemma
5.2
. Before proceeding to the proof of the next result, we need the following observation.

Recall that
\mathbb{E}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
for
k\geq 1
and let
S\in\mathcal{B}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}\right)
such that

(5.1)

S\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\right)=\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\right)S.

Let
[S_{ij}]
be the block matrix representation of
S
, where
S_{ij}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{E}_{j},\mathbb{E}_{i})
. Then (
5.1
) holds if and only if

(5.2)

J_{i}S_{ij}=S_{ij}J_{j}\quad(\forall~~i,j\geq 1).

Now write
S_{ij}
as a block matrix of order
i\times j
; then it is not difficult to see by (
5.2
) that whenever
i\leq j
,

S_{ij}=\begin{bmatrix}X_{i\times i}&O_{i\times(j-i)}\end{bmatrix}
.
Similarly, for
i>j
,
S_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}O_{(i-j)\times j}\\
Y_{j\times j}\end{pmatrix}
,
where
X
and
Y
are lower triangular block Toeplitz matrices whose entries are operators from
\mathcal{E}_{j}
to
\mathcal{E}_{i}
. Also note that, for any
j\geq 1
,

\mathcal{N}(J_{j}^{n_{j}})=\{({0,\ldots,0},{a_{1}},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n_{j}}):a_{n}\in\mathcal{E}_{j},1\leq n\leq n_{j}\}.

Hence, it is clear that the first nonzero entry of any element of
\mathcal{N}(J_{j}^{n_{j}})
is at
(j-n_{j}+1)^{th}
position or in the right of it. We frequently use this fact in the proof of the following result.

Lemma 5.5
.

For each
k\geq 1
, let
J_{k}
be the truncated shift of index
k
on
\mathbb{E}_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
. Then the only hyperinvariant subspaces of
\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}\right)
are
\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
, where
n_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
and
n_{k}\leq n_{k+1}\leq n_{k}+1
.

Proof.

Let
R=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}
on
\mathbb{E}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}
and
\mathcal{M}
be a hyperinvariant subspace of
R
. For each
j\geq 1
, let
I_{j}
be the identity operator on
\mathbb{E}_{j}
.

Define an operator
I_{j}^{\prime}
on

\mathbb{E}
as

I_{j}^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}\textrm{, where }\Delta_{k}=\begin{cases}I_{j},&k=j\\
0,&k\neq j\end{cases}.

Then
I_{j}^{\prime}R=RI_{j}^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}
is invariant under
I_{j}^{\prime}
, that is,
P_{j}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
where
P_{j}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{E}
onto
\mathbb{E}_{j}
. Thus
\mathcal{M}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
. Note that, for each
j\geq 1
,

J_{j}P_{j}(\mathcal{M})=RI_{j}^{\prime}(\mathcal{M})=I_{j}^{\prime}R(\mathcal{M})\subseteq I_{j}^{\prime}(\mathcal{M})=P_{j}(\mathcal{M}).

Clearly,
P_{j}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{j}
is hyperinvariant for
J_{j}
. Therefore, any hyperinvariant subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
R
must be of the form
\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{k}
, where
\mathcal{M}_{k}
is hyperinvariant for
J_{k}
. Now using Lemma
5.4
,
\mathcal{M}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
where
n_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
. Being
\mathcal{M}
hyperinvariant, for any operator
S
commuting with
R
, we have
S(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. But from the remarks before this lemma, it suffices to consider such
S_{ij}
’s for which (
5.2
) holds.

Case-1:
For
i>j
, define an operator
S_{ij}:\mathbb{E}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}_{i}
in the following way:

S_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}&&&&\\
&&&&\\
&&{\Huge O}_{(i-j)\times j}&&\\
&&&&\\
\theta_{1}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
\theta_{2}&\theta_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
\theta_{3}&\theta_{2}&\theta_{1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\theta_{j}&\theta_{j-1}&\cdots&\cdots&\theta_{1}\end{pmatrix}_{i\times j},

where
{\theta_{m}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{j},\mathcal{E}_{i})
for
1\leq m\leq j
. Then
J_{i}S_{ij}=S_{ij}J_{j}
. Now define block operator
S_{ij}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{E}
in such a way that its
ij^{th}
entry is
S_{ij}
and others are zero. Then
RS_{ij}^{\prime}=S_{ij}^{\prime}R
. Therefore,

S_{ij}^{\prime}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}\Leftrightarrow S_{ij}(\mathcal{N}(J_{j}^{n_{j}}))\subseteq\mathcal{N}(J_{i}^{n_{i}}).

If
n_{j}=0
, then it is trivially true. If
n_{j}\neq 0
, then let
(0,\ldots,0,\bm{a_{1}},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n_{j}})\in\mathcal{N}(J_{j}^{n_{j}})
, where
a_{n}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}
for all
1\leq n\leq n_{j}
,
a_{1}\neq 0
and the boldface shows the
(j-n_{j}+1)^{th}
position. Then

(5.3)

S_{ij}(0,\ldots,0,\bm{a_{1}},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n_{j}})\in\mathcal{N}(J_{i}^{n_{i}})\Leftrightarrow\left(0,\ldots,0,\bm{\theta_{1}(a_{1})},\ldots,\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n_{j}}\theta_{n_{j}-m+1}(a_{m})\right)\in\mathcal{N}(J_{i}^{n_{i}})

where boldface in the latter inclusion shows the
(i-n_{j}+1)^{th}
position. But this is possible for all
\theta_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{j},\mathcal{E}_{i})
if and only if
n_{j}\leq n_{i}
. Here we used the fact that
\mathcal{X}\ni x=0
if and only if
Ax=0
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
, where
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are Banach spaces.

Note that if
n_{i}=0
, then (
5.3
) will imply

(0,\ldots,0,\bm{\theta_{1}(a_{1})},\ldots,\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n_{j}}\theta_{n_{j}-m+1}(a_{m}))=0,

for all
\theta_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{j},\mathcal{E}_{i})
which is not possible as
a_{1}\neq 0
. More precisely, if
n_{i}=0
, then
n_{j}=0
. So,

n_{j}\leq n_{i}
still holds.

Case-2:
Let
i\leq j
.
Define
S_{ij}:\mathbb{E}_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}_{i}
in the following way:

S_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{1}&0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
\theta_{2}&\theta_{1}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\cdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\theta_{i}&\theta_{i-1}&\cdots&\theta_{1}&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}_{i\times j},

where
\theta_{m}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{j},\mathcal{E}_{i})
for all
1\leq m\leq i
. Then
J_{i}S_{ij}=S_{ij}J_{j}
. Now define
S_{ij}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{E}
as above. Then again,
S_{ij}(\mathcal{N}(J_{j}^{n_{j}}))\subseteq\mathcal{N}(J_{i}^{n_{i}})
. If
n_{j}=0
, then by Case-1,
n_{i}=0
. For
n_{j}\neq 0
, let
(0,\ldots,0,\bm{a_{1}},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n_{j}})\in\mathcal{N}(J_{j}^{n_{j}})
, where
a_{n}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}
for all
1\leq n\leq n_{j}
,
a_{1}\neq 0
and boldface shows the
(j-n_{j}+1)^{th}
position. If
j-n_{j}+1\leq i
, let
l=n_{j}-j+i
, then

S_{ij}(0,\ldots,0,\bm{a_{1}},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n_{j}})\in\mathcal{N}(J_{i}^{n_{i}})\Leftrightarrow(0,\ldots,0,\bm{\theta_{1}(a_{1})},\ldots,\sum\limits_{m=1}^{l}\theta_{l-m+1}(a_{m}))\in\mathcal{N}(J_{i}^{n_{i}}).

where the boldface in the latter inclusion is at the
(j-n_{j}+1)^{th}
position.
Again this is possible for all
\theta_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{j},\mathcal{E}_{i})
if and only if
j-n_{j}+1\geq i-n_{i}+1
.

On the other hand,
j-n_{j}+1>i
if and only if
S_{ij}(0,\ldots,0,\bm{a_{1}},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n_{j}})=0
for all
a_{n}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}\,\,(1\leq n\leq n_{j})
and for all
S_{ij}
defined above. In that case also,
j-n_{j}+1\geq i-n_{i}+1.
In particular, let
j=i+1
, we get
n_{i+1}\leq n_{i}+1
. Also from the former case,
n_{i}\leq n_{i+1}
.

Hence any hyperinvariant subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
R
is of the form

\mathcal{M}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
, where
n_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
and
n_{k}\leq n_{k+1}\leq n_{k}+1
.

The converse holds from the above proof and the observation before this lemma.

Let
T
be a c.n.u. power partial isometry. We will now characterize hyperinvariant subspaces of a pure power partial isometry, i.e., there is no co-isometric part. We will then use it to characterize hyperinvariant subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry. Before that, first recall the following result.

Lemma 5.6

(cf.
[
9
]
)
.

A non-trivial closed subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
is hyperinvariant for
M_{z}
if and only if it is of the form
uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
for some scalar-valued inner function

.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a closed subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}\right)
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant for pure power partial isometry
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\right)
if and only if either

\mathcal{M}=\{0\}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
or
\mathcal{M}=u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
for some
n_{k}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,k\}
such that
n_{k}\leq n_{k+1}\leq n_{k}+1
and a scalar-valued inner function
u
such that
u\in z^{k-n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}_{k}
,
\mathbb{E}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}
and
\mathbb{E}_{k}=[z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
(see Section 4). Also
J_{k}
is defined as in (
4.1
). Set

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\mbox{~~~and~~}

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}\oplus R\mbox{~~on~~}{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant for
V
. Then, from Lemma
5.2
,
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{2}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{E}
are hyperinvariant for
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
and
R
, respectively and for any operators
S_{1}\in\mathcal{B}({H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}),\mathbb{E})
and
S_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{E},{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\right)
satisfying
RS_{1}=S_{1}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
and
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}S_{2}=S_{2}R
, we have
S_{1}\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
S_{2}\mathcal{M}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}
. By Lemma
5.5
and Remark
5.6
,
\mathcal{M}
has two possibilities:

\mathcal{M}=\{0\}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right)\textrm{ or }\mathcal{M}=u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right),

where
u\in H^{\infty}
is inner and
n_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
such that
n_{k}\leq n_{k+1}\leq n_{k}+1
.

First let
S_{1}\in\mathcal{B}({H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}),\mathbb{E})
such that
RS_{1}=S_{1}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
. Since the minimal isometric dilation of
R
is
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows by commutant lifting theorem that there exists a contraction
Y:{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
such that

(1)

M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}Y=YM_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
,

(2)

S_{1}=P_{\mathbb{E}}Y,
and

(3)

\|S_{1}\|=\|Y\|
,

where
P_{\mathbb{E}}
is the orthogonal projection of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
onto
\mathbb{E}
.

Now (
1
) implies
Y=T_{\Phi}
for some
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
and hence (
2
) infers
S_{1}=P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi}
. Conversely, let
S_{1}=P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi}
for some
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
. Then

RS_{1}=RP_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi}=P_{\mathbb{E}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}T_{\Phi}=P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}=S_{1}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}.

Hence,
RS_{1}=S_{1}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
if and only if
S_{1}=P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi}
, where
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
.

For
\mathcal{M}=\{0\}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right),S_{1}(\{0\})\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
is trivially true.

For
\mathcal{M}=uH^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right)
being hyperinvariant, for each
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})},

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi}(u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}T_{\Phi}(u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}T_{\Phi}(u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}),\textrm{ for all }k\geq 1.

Now let
u=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}u_{m}z^{m}
and, for fixed
k\geq 1
, choose
\Phi\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{k})
and
a\in\mathcal{E}
such that
\Phi(a)\neq 0
. Then

P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}T_{\Phi}(ua)=P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}u_{m}\Phi(a)z^{m}\right)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k-1}u_{m}\Phi(a)z^{m}.

If
n_{k}<k
for some
k
, then
P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}T_{\Phi}(ua)\in\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\Leftrightarrow u_{m}=0\,\,\forall\,\,m<k-n_{k}
.

For
n_{k}=k
, the condition holds trivially. Hence,
u\in z^{k-n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for all
k\geq 1
.

Conversely, suppose
\mathcal{M}=uH^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right)
, where
u\in H^{\infty}
is an inner function such that
u\in z^{k-n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
n_{k}\leq n_{k+1}\leq n_{k}+1
for all
k\geq 1
. Recall that for
k\geq 1
, we write

\mathbb{E}_{k}=[z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\subseteq H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D}).

Hence,
P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}=P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}P_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})}
where
P_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})}
is the orthogonal projection of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
onto
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})
.
Then, for any
\Phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})}
,

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}T_{\Phi}(u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))

\displaystyle\subseteq P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}T_{\Phi}(z^{k-n_{k}}{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))

\displaystyle=P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}P_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})}T_{\Phi}(z^{k-n_{k}}{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))

\displaystyle=(I-(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}})^{k}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}})^{*k})P_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})}T_{\Phi}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}})^{k-n_{k}}{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle=(I-(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}})^{k}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}})^{*k})(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}})^{k-n_{k}}P_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})}T_{\Phi}{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle=M_{z}^{k-n_{k}}(I-M_{z}^{n_{k}}M_{z}^{*n_{k}})P_{H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})}T_{\Phi}{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}).

Hence

P_{\mathbb{E}}T_{\Phi}(u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}T_{\Phi}(uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}).

Now let
S_{2}\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{E},{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\right)
such that
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}S_{2}=S_{2}R
. Then, for
n\geq 1
,

(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}})^{n}S_{2}=S_{2}R^{n}.

Since
R^{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in strong operator topology, thus
S_{2}=0
.

Therefore, only hyperinvariant subspaces of
V
are those of the form

\mathcal{M}=\{0\}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right)

or

\mathcal{M}=u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right),

where
n_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
such that
n_{k}\leq n_{k+1}\leq n_{k}+1
and
u\in H^{\infty}
inner such that
u\in z^{k-n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for all
k\geq 1
.

This finishes the proof.

Example 5.8
.

Let
V=M_{z}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\right)
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}^{k}\right)
. Consider

\mathcal{M}=zH^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k})\right).

Define

S:H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
as

Sf=0\oplus 0\oplus 0\oplus zf\oplus 0\oplus\cdots\quad(f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})).

Set
\mathcal{K}=\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}^{k}
. Now let

S^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
P_{\mathcal{K}}S&0\end{pmatrix}:\begin{matrix}\begin{array}[]{ccc}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})&&H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\\
\oplus&\rightarrow&\oplus\\
\mathcal{K}&&\mathcal{K}\end{array}\end{matrix},

where
P_{\mathcal{K}}
is the orthogonal projection from
\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
onto
\mathcal{K}
. Then it is easy to see that
S^{\prime}V=VS^{\prime}
but
S^{\prime}\mathcal{M}\nsubseteq\mathcal{M}
. Indeed, for

,

But
az^{2}\notin\mathcal{N}(J_{4})
. Hence
\mathcal{M}
is not hyperinvariant.

In the same space, if we take
\mathcal{M}=zH^{2}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{k-1})\right)
, then
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant for
V
.

We are now ready to find the explicit structure of hyperinvariant subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry. For simplicity, we will do it in two parts. First, we will find the hyperinvariant subspaces of direct sum of forward and backward shift operator and then that of direct sum of backward shift and truncated shift.

Theorem 5.9
.

A closed subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
is hyperinvariant for
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}\oplus(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}
if and only if it is either of the form
\{0\}\oplus[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
,
\{0\}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
or
uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
, where
u
and
v
are scalar-valued inner functions.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be hyperinvariant for
X=M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}\oplus(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}
. Then from Lemma
5.2
, there are four possible types, namely,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\{0\}\oplus[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\{0\}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D}),

\displaystyle\mbox{~~or~~}\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D}),

where
u,v\in H^{\infty}
are inner functions.

Let
S_{1}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
such that

(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}S_{1}=S_{1}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}
. This holds if and only if

S_{1}=H_{\Phi}\mbox{~~for some~~}\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}.

For
\mathcal{M}=\{0\}\oplus[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
,
\{0\}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
or
uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
, the condition given in Lemma
5.2
holds trivially.
For

\mathcal{M}=uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}

being hyperinvariant,

H_{\Phi}(uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\,\,~~\forall\,\,\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}.

In particular, let
\Phi(e^{it})=v(e^{-it})\Phi^{\prime}(e^{it})\overline{u(e^{it})}
, where
\Phi^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})}
such that all the non-negative Fourier coefficients of
\Phi^{\prime}
are zero.
Then

H_{\Phi}(uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}

then, for any
a\in\mathcal{E}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle P_{+}^{\mathcal{F}}J(\Phi ua),vg\rangle=0~~\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\langle J(\Phi ua),vg\rangle=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle v(e^{it})\Phi^{\prime}(e^{-it})a,v(e^{it})g(e^{it})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(e^{-it})a,g(e^{it})\right\rangle=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\langle J(\Phi^{\prime}a),g\rangle=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle J(\Phi^{\prime}a)\in[H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}

which is not possible unless
\Phi=0
.

On the other hand, let
S_{2}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})
such that
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}}S_{2}=S_{2}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}
. Hence, for each
n\geq 0
,

(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}})^{n}S_{2}=S_{2}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*n}

which implies
S_{2}=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}})^{*n}S_{1}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*n}
. Since
M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}}
is pure, we have
S_{2}=0
.

Hence,
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant subspace for
X
if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is one of the following forms:

(1)

\{0\}\oplus[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
,

(2)

\{0\}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
, or

(3)

uH^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
,

where
u
and
v
are scalar-valued inner functions.

Theorem 5.10
.

The only hyperinvariant subspaces of
(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}\oplus J_{k}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}_{k}
are
[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\oplus\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
and
H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
, where
n_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
and
v
is any scalar-valued inner function such that
v\in z^{n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

Set

W=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}\oplus J_{k}\mbox{~~on~~}H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathbb{E}_{k}.

Again
\mathbb{E}_{k}
is identified as a subspace of
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})
, namely,
[z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
.
Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant for
W
. We shall again make use of Lemma
5.2
in what follows. Then
\mathcal{M}
is either of type

\mathcal{M}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\mbox{~~or~~}\mathcal{M}=[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\oplus\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}),

where
v\in H^{\infty}
is inner and
n_{k}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,k\}
.

Let
S_{1}:H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\mathbb{E}_{k}
such that
J_{k}S_{1}=S_{1}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}
. Then

S_{1}(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*k}=J_{k}^{k}S_{1}=0.

Thus
S_{1}=0
.

Now let
S_{2}:\mathbb{E}_{k}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
such that

(5.4)

(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}S_{2}=S_{2}J_{k}.

Since
M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
on
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})
is the minimal isometric dilation of
J_{k}
,

J_{k}P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}=P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}},

where
P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}
is the orthogonal projection from
H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})
onto
\mathbb{E}_{k}
. Then

(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}S_{2}P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}=S_{2}J_{k}P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}=S_{2}P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}M_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}.

Set
Y=S_{2}P_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}
. Then
(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}Y=YM_{z}^{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
implies

Y=H_{\Phi}\mbox{~~for some~~}\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F})},

and hence

S_{2}=Y|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}=H_{\Phi}|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}.

Now let
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F})}
such that
(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}H_{\Phi}|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}=H_{\Phi}J_{k}
. Then for
a\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
and
m\geq 0
, we have

H_{\Phi}(ae^{i(k+m)t})=(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*m+1}H_{\Phi}(ae^{i(k-1)t})=H_{\Phi}J_{k}^{m+1}(ae^{i(k-1)t})=0.

Thus
z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathcal{N}(H_{\Phi})
. The converse is also true. Hence (
5.4
) holds if and only if
S_{2}=H_{\Phi}|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}
, where
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F})}
such that
z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathcal{N}(H_{\Phi})
.

Now for
\mathcal{M}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
,
S_{2}(\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}))\subseteq H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})
is obvious.

For
\mathcal{M}=[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\oplus\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
being hyperinvariant for
W
, we have

H_{\Phi}(\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}))\subseteq[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp},

for all
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F})}
such that
z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathcal{N}(H_{\Phi})
.

For
n_{k}=0
, it is trivially true. Let
n_{k}\geq 1
.
Note that for any
\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F})
, we can define
\Phi_{k}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F})}
by

(5.5)

\Phi_{k}(e^{it})=\varphi_{k}e^{-i(k-1)t}\quad\quad(\text{a.e.\ on }\mathbb{T}).

Then, for
a\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
,

H_{\Phi_{k}}(ae^{ikt})=P_{+}^{\mathcal{F}}J(\Phi_{k}ae^{ikt})=P_{+}^{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi_{k}(a)e^{-it})=0.

And hence
z^{k}H^{2}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathcal{N}(H_{\Phi_{k}})
. It follows that

H_{\Phi_{k}}(\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}))\subseteq[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}.

Choose
\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F})
and
a\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
such that
\varphi_{k}(a)\neq 0
. Note that
az^{k-n_{k}}\in\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})
. Suppose
v=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}v_{m}z^{m}

(v_{m}\in\mathbb{C})
. Then, for
\Phi_{k}
as defined in (
5.5
),

\displaystyle\langle H_{\Phi_{k}}(ae^{i(k-n_{k})t}),vg\rangle=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\langle P_{+}^{\mathcal{F}}J(\Phi_{k}ae^{i(k-n_{k})t}),vg\rangle=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\langle J(\Phi_{k}ae^{i(k-n_{k})t}),vg\rangle=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle\varphi_{k}(a)e^{i(n_{k}-1)t},v(e^{it})g(e^{it})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\langle\overline{v(e^{it})}\varphi_{k}(a)e^{i(n_{k}-1)t},g(e^{it})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\langle T_{\Psi}a,g\rangle=0\,\,\forall\,\,g\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D}),

where
\Psi=\bar{v}\varphi_{k}z^{n_{k}-1}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\overline{v_{m}}\varphi_{k}z^{n_{k}-m-1}
. Hence

T_{\Psi}a=0.

Thus
\overline{v_{m}}\varphi_{k}(a)=0
for all
m\leq n_{k}-1
. But since
\varphi_{k}(a)\neq 0
, we get
v_{m}=0
for all
m\leq n_{k}-1
, i.e.,
v\in z^{n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Conversely, for
v\in z^{n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}_{k},\mathcal{F})}
such that
(M_{z}^{\mathcal{F}})^{*}H_{\Phi}|_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}=H_{\Phi}J_{k}
, it is trivial to check that
H_{\Phi}(\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}))\subseteq[vH^{2}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}
. Hence
\mathcal{M}
is hyperinvariant for
W
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{M}
is hperinvariant for
W
if and only if it is either of the form

\mathcal{M}=H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\mbox{~~~~or~~~~}\mathcal{M}=[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\oplus\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}}),

where
v
is a scalar-valued inner function such that
v\in z^{n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
n_{k}\in\{0,1,\ldots k\}
.

From the above deliberation, we obtain the complete characterization of hyperinvariant subspaces of a c.n.u. power partial isometry which is stated as follows.

Theorem 5.11
.

A closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
of
{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
is hyperinvariant for a c.n.u. power partial isometry
T=M_{z}\oplus M_{z}^{*}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}J_{k}\right)
if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is one of the following forms:

\mathcal{M}=\begin{cases}\{0\}\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right)\\
\{0\}\oplus[vH^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})]^{\perp}\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right)\\
u{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus H^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\left(\bigoplus\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}(J_{k}^{n_{k}})\right)\end{cases},

where
n_{k}\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
with
n_{k}\leq n_{k+1}\leq n_{k}+1
and
u,v\in H^{\infty}
are inner functions such that
u\in z^{k-n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
v\in z^{n_{k}}H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for all
k\geq 1
.

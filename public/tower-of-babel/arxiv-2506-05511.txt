The U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} model meets the new ( g − 2 ) μ (g-2)_{\mu} data and muon neutrino trident scattering
1,2 Ming-Wei Li, 1,2 Xiao-Gang He, 1,2 Andrew Cheek, 1,2 Xinhui Chu

Abstract
The Muon g − 2 g-2 collaboration at Fermilab has announced their final result of the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon. This result is now in agreement with the latest theoretical prediction to the 1 ​ σ 1\sigma level. This new result further constrains the allowed parameter space, but does not rule out all possible new physics contributions the muon g − 2 g-2 . We study the implications for one of the relevant models, the gauged U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} . When using this model to resolve the previous 4 ​ σ 4\sigma tension, results from muon neutrino trident (MNT) scattering experiments would restrict the mass of the new gauge boson ( Z ′ Z^{\prime} ) to be less than 300 300 MeV. Since the theory and experimental data difference for muon g − 2 g-2 is lowered down to 1 ​ σ 1\sigma , the requirement for m Z ′ ≲ 300 ​ MeV m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 300\,{\rm MeV} is much relaxed. Within the updated allowed range of Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson mass, we study the models implications for electron and tauon g − 2 g-2 as well as future muon colliders. We find that muon collider can effectively probe the U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} .

Conclusion

We study the implications of the recent experimental determination of the anomalous magnetic moment of the muon as reported by the the Muon
g-2
collaboration. We do this specifically for one of the simplest extensions to the SM, the
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
model. We find that the previous emphasis on solving the tension between theory and experiment for
(g-2)_{\mu}
restricted the
Z^{\prime}
mass to be less than 300 MeV. This was because the required parameters would provoke observable consequences in existing MNT experiments.

Now that the measurement is consistent with SM predictions, there is no such restriction, and one is motivated to consider larger
m_{Z^{\prime}}
values. For example
m_{Z^{\prime}}\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(100\,{\rm GeV})
remains very much allowed even with the new
\Delta a_{\mu}
central value. Furthermore, measurements of the tauon
g-2
as well as results from existing collider and MNT experiments are fairly weak. This means the gauged
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
is very much alive and well. Therefore, with this new outlook, we show that a future muon collider will be an effective and powerful tool for probing the
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
model.

Exceptional supercuspidal representations in small residue characteristic
Yiannis Fam

Abstract
In this paper, in residue characteristic 2 and 3, we extend the construction of epipelagic representations of Reeder–Yu to produce new supercuspidals of higher depth, building on work of Gastineau. In particular, we produce examples of epipelagic representations that do not arise from the construction of Reeder–Yu.



1
Introduction

Let
k
be a non-archimedean local field with residue characteristic
p
, residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{k}
and uniformiser
\varpi
. Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
k
, and assume that
G
splits over a tamely ramified extension of
k
. By a representation of
G(k)
we mean a smooth complex representation. This paper focuses on the construction of supercuspidal representations of
G(k)
when
p
is small.

The construction of supercuspidal representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
and
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
was completed by Bushnell–Kutzko in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
. The case of inner forms of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
was completed by Sécherre–Stevens
[
25
]
. In
[
27
]
, Stevens constructs all supercuspidal representations when
p>2
and
G
is a classical group. For general
G
, by work of Kim
[
16
]
and Fintzen
[
10
]
, Yu’s construction
[
29
]
of supercuspidal representations is exhaustive when
p
is sufficiently large;
[
10
]
shows that it suffices to take
p
not dividing the order of the Weyl group of
G
. Yu’s construction exists when
p\neq 2
, and has recently been extended to the
p=2
case by
[
9
]
. One is then interested in constructing, when
p
is small, irreducible supercuspidal representations of
G(k)
that are not expected to arise from Yu’s construction. The cases when
p=2
or
G
is an exceptional group are particularly interesting.

The case of depth-zero supercuspidal representations has been completed by Moy–Prasad
[
19
]

[
20
]
, and separately by Morris
[
18
]
. In positive depth there exist constructions, uniform in the residue characteristic
p
, due to Gross–Reeder
[
13
]
and Reeder–Yu
[
23
]
of supercuspidal representations that do not necessarily arise from the construction of Yu (or
[
9
]
). For the rest of this paper we assume that
G
is a simple split reductive group over
k
(although
[
23
]
works in greater generality). We will, for convenience, label the first three terms of the Moy–Prasad filtration of
G(k)
at a point
x
in the building
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
of
G
by
G(k)_{x,0}
,
G(k)_{x,+}
and
G(k)_{x,++}
. Let
\mathbb{G}_{x}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the reductive quotient of
G
at
x
so that
G(k)_{x,0}/G(k)_{x,0+}\cong\mathbb{G}_{x}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

The epipelagic representations constructed in
[
23
]
take as input a point
x\in\mathcal{B}(G,k)
, a linear functional
\lambda
on the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space
G(k)_{x,+}/G(k)_{x,++}
, and a fixed nontrivial additive character
\chi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. When
\lambda
is stable under the action of
\mathbb{G}_{x}
in the sense of Geometric Invariant Theory
[
21
]
, the compactly induced representation
\mathrm{ind}_{G(k)_{x,+}}^{G(k)}\chi\circ\lambda
is a finite direct sum of irreducible supercuspidal representations of
G(k)
. These irreducible constituents are examples of epipelagic representations, and may also be realised as the compact induction of a representation of a slightly larger compact open subgroup than
G(k)_{x,+}
. When specialising to the case that
x
is the barycentre of an alcove, one recovers the simple supercuspidal representations of
[
13
]
. The corresponding condition on
\lambda
in this setting is more elementary to describe. Outside of this case the stable functionals
\lambda
have not been classified in general. When
p
is large, the stability condition may be transferred to a stability condition in Vinberg–Levy theory
[
23
]
where
[
22
]
applies. The assumption on
p
was later removed by
[
8
]
. The points
x
for which
G(k)_{x,+}/G(k)_{x,++}
admits stable functionals have been classified in
[
23
, Corollary 5.1]
. Reeder–Yu find that these stability points are rather rare; when
G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}
the only stable point is the barycentre of an alcove.

In Section 2, to extend the above construction to higher depth we consider the compact induction of characters of
G(k)_{x,+}
that do not factor through
G(k)_{x,+}/G(k)_{x,++}
. In order to do so, we must explicitly describe the abelianisation of
G(k)_{x,+}
. We show that this abelianisation is a quotient of
G(k)_{x,+}/G(k)_{x,1+}
when
p>2
. To make calculation more manageable, we only work with the smaller quotient
G(k)_{x,+}/G(k)_{x,1}
. In this section we describe an algorithm that computes the abelianisation
V(x)
of
G(k)_{x,+}/G(k)_{x,1}
when
x
is the barycentre of a facet. When
p>3
, we show that this abelianisation is
G(k)_{x,+}/G(k)_{x,++}
and we are constrained to the setting of
[
23
]
. When
p=2
or
3
we find that
V(x)
may be larger than
G(k)_{x,+}/G(k)_{x,++}
, but still always admits the structure of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space. The question of describing such characters of
G(k)_{x,+}
was previously considered by
[
11
]
. Taking
q=2
,
G=\mathrm{Sp}_{4}
and
x
the barycentre of an alcove, Gastineau produces a character
\rho:\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(k)_{x,+}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
that does not factor through
\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(k)_{x,+}/\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(k)_{x,++}
, such that
\mathrm{ind}_{\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(k)_{x,+}}^{\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(k)}\rho
is an irreducible supercuspidal representation.

We now describe our main result. In Section 3, we review the construction of supercuspidal representations due to
[
23
]
and verify that they are epipelagic. In Definition
3.12
we define a weaker notion of stability than the notion from Geometric Invariant Theory, that of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stability. In short, this asks for the Hilbert–Mumford criterion (
[
21
]
) to hold for the action of any one parameter family
\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{x}
, defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, on
V(x)^{\vee}
. We prove the following:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.17
)
.

Fix a nontrivial additive character
\chi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Let
\lambda\in V(x)^{\vee}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stable functional and let
\chi_{\lambda}
denote the inflation of
\chi\circ\lambda
to a character of
G(k)_{x,+}
. The compactly induced representation

\pi_{\lambda}=\mathrm{ind}_{G(k)_{x,+}}^{G(k)}\chi_{\lambda}

is a finite direct sum of irreducible supercuspidal representations of
G(k)
.

In contrast with
[
23
]
, our method does not generally give us an explicit description of the irreducible constituents of
\pi_{\lambda}
as compactly induced representations.

In Section 4, we give examples of supercuspidal representations using Theorem
3.17
. These examples have the following features:

•

For
G=G_{2}
,
q=2
and
x
the barycentre of an alcove, we produce two irreducible supercuspidal representations of
G(k)
. We exhibit an isomorphism between these and epipelagic supercuspidal representations constructed by
[
23
, Section 7.5]
from a
different
point in the building.

•

For
G
of type
B_{n}
,
q=2
and
x
the barycentre of an alcove, we produce irreducible supercuspidal representations of
G(k)
, of which at least one does not arise from the construction of
[
23
]
by depth considerations.

•

For
G=G_{2}
and
p=3
we produce an irreducible supercuspidal representation of
G(k)
that is epipelagic of depth
1/2
and does not arise from the construction of
[
23
]
by formal degree considerations.

In particular, the latter two give examples of epipelagic representations that do not arise from the construction of
[
23
]
.

Notation and Conventions

Let
r+
be a set of symbols indexed by
r\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\tilde{\mathbb{R}}
denote
\mathbb{R}\sqcup\{r+:r\in\mathbb{R}\}
with a total ordering defined by the following property: if
r<s
are real numbers then

r<r+<s<s+.

We write the commutator of two elements
a,b
of a group to be
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
.

2
Characters of some compact open subgroups

In this section we classify characters of certain compact open subgroups of
G(k)
, specifically the first positively indexed subgroup in the Moy–Prasad filtration of
G(k)
corresponding to a barycentre of a facet in the Bruhat–Tits building of
G(k)
.

2.1
Notation and review of Bruhat–Tits theory

Let
k
be a non-archimedean local field with residue characteristic
p
, residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{k}
and uniformiser
\varpi
. Let
k^{\mathrm{ur}}
be the maximal unramified extension of
k
in a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{k}
. Let
\mathcal{O}
denote the ring of integers of
k^{\mathrm{ur}}
and identify the residue field with
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Let
G
be a simple split reductive group over
k
with split
k
-rational maximal torus
T
. Denote the centre of
G
by
Z
. Let
X_{*}(T)=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(\mathbb{G}_{m},T)
and
X^{*}(T)=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(T,\mathbb{G}_{m})
be the cocharacter and character lattices respectively of
T
. Let
\langle,\rangle:X^{*}(T)\times X_{*}(T)\to\mathbb{Z}
be the natural pairing. We reuse this notation for the pairing
X^{*}(T)\times X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
\Phi
be the set of roots with respect to
T
, and for each
\alpha\in\Phi
let
U_{\alpha}
denote the corresponding root subgroup of
G
. Fix isomorphisms
u_{\alpha}:\mathbb{G}_{a}\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{\alpha}
, defining a Chevalley basis
e_{\alpha}=du_{\alpha}(1)\in\mathrm{Lie}(U_{\alpha})
of
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(T,k)
denote the apartment of
T
over
k
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(G,k)
denote the Bruhat–Tits building of
G
over
k
. Our choice of Chevalley basis determines a unique point in
\mathcal{A}
fixed by
u_{\alpha}(-1)u_{-\alpha}(1)u_{\alpha}(-1)
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
. Taking this hyperspecial point
x_{0}
as the origin, we identify
\mathcal{A}
with
X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\Psi
be the set of affine roots with respect to
T
. These are the affine functions on
X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
of the form
\alpha+n
for
\alpha
a root and
n
an integer. The gradient of an affine root
\psi=\alpha+n
is defined to be
\alpha\in\Phi
and we denote this by
\dot{\psi}
. For
\psi=\alpha+n
let the affine root group
U_{\psi}
denote
u_{\alpha}(\varpi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{k})\leq U_{\alpha}
. We have an isomorphism
u_{\psi}:\mathcal{O}_{k}\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{\psi}
of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-modules determined by sending 1 to
u_{\alpha}(\varpi^{n})
. For any affine root
\psi
, denote by
V_{\psi}
the quotient
U_{\psi}/U_{\psi+1}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
as an abelian group. Let
\overline{u}_{\psi}:\mathcal{O}_{k}/\varpi\mathcal{O}_{k}\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{\psi}
be the isomorphism induced by
u_{\psi}
.

Let
W(\Phi)
and
W(\Psi)
denote the Weyl and affine Weyl groups respectively. As
T
is split, we may view it as the generic fibre of a split torus
T_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}
over
\mathcal{O}_{k}
. Denote by
N
the normaliser of
T
in
G
, and define the Iwahori–Weyl group
W(G)
by
W(G)=N(k)/T_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
. This contains
W(\Psi)
as a finite-index normal subgroup, and the quotient
\Omega
is isomorphic to the centre of the dual group
\hat{G}
.

Fix a choice of root basis for
\Phi
and hence a choice of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the alcove (maximal facet) in
\mathcal{A}
determined by the equations
0<\alpha(x)<1
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. The closure of
\mathcal{C}
contains the hyperspecial point
x_{0}
. Let
\Pi
denote the set of simple affine roots with respect to our choice of root basis. Let
\Psi^{+}
denote the positive affine roots with respect to
\Pi
. These are the affine roots that take positive values on
\mathcal{C}
and are non-negative integral linear combinations of affine roots in
\Pi
.

Fix a point
x\in\mathcal{A}
that is the barycentre of a facet in the closure of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
G(k)_{x}
denote the stabiliser in
G(k)
of
x\in\mathcal{B}
. This is a compact open subgroup of
G(k)
. For any non-negative real number
r
, we define the Moy–Prasad filtration subgroups

G(k)_{x,r}:=\langle T(F)_{r},\hskip 5.0ptU_{\psi}:\psi(x)\geq r\rangle,

where

T(F)_{0}=\{t\in T(F):\mathrm{val}(\chi(t))=0\text{ for all }\chi\in X^{*}(T)\}=T_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}(\mathcal{O}_{k})

and, when
r>0
,

T(F)_{r}=\{t\in T(F)_{0}:\mathrm{val}(\chi(t)-1)\geq r\text{ for all }\chi\in X^{*}(T)\}.

Define

G(k)_{x,r+}:=\bigcup\limits_{s>r}G(k)_{x,s}.

The jumps in the Moy–Prasad filtration occur at a discrete set of real numbers. We often write
G(k)_{x,+}
and
G(k)_{x,++}
for the first two distinct positively indexed subgroups in the filtration. In other words, we use
+
as shorthand for
0+
, and
++
as shorthand for
r_{1}+
where
r_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
is such that
G(k)_{x,0+}=G(k)_{x,r_{1}}\neq G(k)_{x,r_{1}+}
. When
r<s
in
\tilde{\mathbb{R}}
, we write
G(k)_{x,r:s}
for the quotient
G(k)_{x,r}/G(k)_{x,s}
.

From Bruhat–Tits theory
[
1
, 4.6.2, 5.2.6]
we may view
G(k)_{x,0}
as the
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-points of a smooth connected affine
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-group scheme
\mathcal{G}_{x}
containing the split torus
T_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}
. Let
\mathbb{G}_{x}
denote the special fibre of
\mathcal{G}_{x}
, a split reductive group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so that
\mathbb{G}_{x}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\cong G(k)_{x,0:0+}
. Let
\mathbb{T}_{x}\leq\mathbb{G}_{x}
denote the special fibre of
T_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}
, a split maximal torus of
\mathbb{G}_{x}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For any
r>0
, the quotient
G(k)_{x,r:r+}
may be viewed as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space, with a natural choice of basis given by the image of
u_{\psi}(1)\in G(k)_{x,r}
over all affine roots
\psi
with
\psi(x)=r
.

One may replace
k
with any unramified extension in the definitions above. Fix a Frobenius morphism
F:G(k^{\mathrm{ur}})\to G(k^{\mathrm{ur}})
so that
G(k)=G(k^{\mathrm{ur}})^{F}
. As we assume that
G
splits over
k
, we have
G(k)_{x,r}=G(k^{\mathrm{ur}})_{x,r}^{F}
for all
r
. We also have the isomorphism

G(k^{\mathrm{ur}})_{x,r:r+}\cong G(k)_{x,r:r+}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

as representations of
\mathbb{G}_{x}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, when
r>0
. We say that
G(k^{\mathrm{ur}})_{x,r:r+}
admits an algebraic representation of
\mathbb{G}_{x}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Denote by
\Delta(x)
the set of affine roots taking minimal positive value at
x
, and denote this value by
\delta(x)
. Let
\Pi(x)=\Delta(x)\cap\Pi
. As we assume that
x
is the barycentre of a facet,
\Pi(x)
is the subset of
\Pi
not vanishing at
x
. Let
W(x)
be the subgroup of
W(G)
stabilising
x
. When
x
is the barycentre of
\mathcal{C}
, we have
\Delta(x)=\Pi
. In general,
\Delta(x)
contains
w(\Pi(x))
for all
w\in W(x)
. We define the following subsets of
\Psi
: let
\Psi_{x}^{+}
be the set of affine roots taking a positive value at
x
, let
\Psi_{x,<1}^{+}
be the subset taking value at
x
strictly between 0 and 1, and let
\Psi_{x,\leq 1}^{+}
be the subset satisfying
0<\psi(x)\leq 1
. We have

G(k)_{x,+:++}\cong\bigoplus\limits_{\psi\in\Delta(x)}V_{\psi}

as
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-modules. As
x
is the barycentre of a facet, every affine root evaluated at
x
takes value in
\delta(x)\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\Delta_{2}(x)
denote the set of affine roots taking value
2\delta(x)
at
x
.

2.2
The abelianisation of
G(k)_{x,+:1}

The constructions in
[
13
]
and
[
23
]
take as input characters of the quotients
G(k)_{x,+:++}
. Certainly any character of
G(k)_{x,+}
factors through the abelianisation of
G(k)_{x,+}
. We have the following elementary calculation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
and let
x
be the barycentre of an alcove so that
\mathcal{I}:=G(k)_{x,0}

is an Iwahori subgroup. Denote the next two terms of the Moy–Prasad filtration by
\mathcal{I}_{+}
and
\mathcal{I}_{++}
. Then the derived subgroup of
\mathcal{I}_{+}
is contained in
\mathcal{I}_{++}
. Suppose furthermore that
p>2
. Then the abelianisation of
\mathcal{I}_{+}
is
\mathcal{I}_{+}/\mathcal{I}_{++}
.

Proof.

We prove this for
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. A similar calculation holds for
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
. After conjugating, we may assume that
\mathcal{I}
is the standard Iwahori subgroup generated by
T_{0}
,
U_{\alpha+0}
and
U_{1-\alpha}
, where
U_{\alpha+0}
denotes the strictly upper triangular matrices with entries in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
, so that
\alpha
is the standard choice of simple root of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. The following commutator relations

\displaystyle\left[\begin{pmatrix}1+\varpi t&0\\
0&(1+\varpi t)^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&\varpi(2tb+\varpi t^{2}b)\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{I}_{++}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{pmatrix}1+\varpi t&0\\
0&(1+\varpi t)^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\varpi c&1\end{pmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\varpi^{2}(2tc+\varpi t^{2}c)&1\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{I}_{++}

\displaystyle\left[\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\varpi c&1\end{pmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1+\varpi bc+\varpi^{2}b^{2}c^{2}&-\varpi b^{2}c\\
\varpi^{2}bc^{2}&1-\varpi bc\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{I}_{++}

for
b,c,t\in\mathcal{O}_{k}
imply that the derived subgroup
(\mathcal{I}_{+})_{\mathrm{der}}
is contained in
\mathcal{I}_{++}
. In other words, we recover the fact that
\mathcal{I}_{+}/\mathcal{I}_{++}
is abelian. When
p>2
, the first two equations imply that
(\mathcal{I}_{+})_{\mathrm{der}}
contains
U_{\alpha+1}
and
U_{2-\alpha}
. Multiplying
\begin{pmatrix}1+\varpi bc+\varpi^{2}b^{2}c^{2}&-\varpi b^{2}c\\
\varpi^{2}bc^{2}&1-\varpi bc\end{pmatrix}
on the left and right by appropriate elements of
U_{2-\alpha}
and
U_{\alpha+1}
respectively, and varying
b,c
, we find that
(\mathcal{I}_{+})_{\mathrm{der}}
contains
T_{1}
. It follows that
(\mathcal{I}_{+})_{\mathrm{der}}\geq\mathcal{I}_{++}
, when
p>2
, and so the abelianisation of
\mathcal{I}_{+}
is
\mathcal{I}_{+}/\mathcal{I}_{++}
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

When
p>2
, the derived subgroup of
G(k)_{x,+}
contains
G(k)_{x,1+}
.

Proof.

For each root
\alpha\in\Phi
, the algebraic group
\langle U_{\alpha},U_{-\alpha}\rangle
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
.

If
\alpha(x)\not\in\mathbb{Z}
then

\langle U_{\alpha},U_{-\alpha}\rangle\cap G(k)_{x,+}=\langle U_{\alpha-\lceil\alpha(x)\rceil+1},U_{\lceil\alpha(x)\rceil-\alpha},\alpha^{\vee}(1+\varpi\mathcal{O}_{k})\rangle.

This is the pro-unipotent radical of an Iwahori subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
. By Lemma
2.1
, the derived subgroup of this contains
\langle U_{\alpha-\lceil\alpha(x)\rceil+2},U_{\lceil\alpha(x)\rceil-\alpha+1},\alpha^{\vee}(1+\varpi\mathcal{O}_{k})\rangle.

If
\alpha(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
then

\langle U_{\alpha},U_{-\alpha}\rangle\cap G(k)_{x,+}=\langle U_{\alpha-\alpha(x)+1},U_{\alpha(x)-\alpha+1},\alpha^{\vee}(1+\varpi\mathcal{O}_{k})\rangle.

This is the pro-unipotent radical of a maximal parahoric subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
(so is conjugate to the kernel of reduction modulo
\varpi
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
or
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{k})
). By the same calculation as in Lemma
2.1
(replacing
b
by
\varpi b
), the derived subgroup of this contains
\langle U_{\alpha-\lceil\alpha(x)\rceil+2},U_{\lceil\alpha(x)\rceil-\alpha+2},\alpha^{\vee}(1+\varpi^{2}\mathcal{O}_{k})\rangle.

Combining all of this, we see that the derived subgroup of
G(k)_{x,+}
contains
T_{2}
and
U_{\psi+1}
for all
\psi\in\Psi
satisfying
\psi(x)>0
, and so contains
G(k)_{x,1+}
.
∎

For
p>2
, the abelianisation of
G(k)_{x,+}
is therefore the same as the abelianisation of
G(k)_{x,+:1+}
. To simplify calculations, for all
p
we will instead describe the abelianisation of
G(k)_{x,+:1}
. When
p>3
(or
p>2
and
G
is not of type
G_{2}
) we will show that this abelianisation is simply
G(k)_{x,+:++}
. In small residue characteristic the abelianisation of
G(k)_{x,+:1}
may be larger, and thus allows us to define more characters of
G(k)_{x,+}
. In this way, after compactly inducing, we obtain new supercuspidal representations of
G(k)
.

To determine the abelianisation of
G(k)_{x,+:1}
we must describe the derived subgroup. Note that if
0<\psi(x),\phi(x)<1
and
\psi+\phi
is constant (necessarily 1), then

Lemma 2.6
.

There exist positive integers
a_{\theta}
such that

\sum\limits_{\theta\in\bigcup\limits_{w\in W(x)}w(\Pi(x))}a_{\theta}\theta

is a constant function.

Proof.

There exist unique positive integers

such that
\sum_{\psi\in\Pi}b_{\psi}\psi=1

\mathbb{Z}[\Pi]
on the set
\Pi

\mathbb{Z}[\bigcup\limits_{w\in W(x)}w(\Pi)]

\sigma:=\sum\limits_{w\in W(x)}\sum\limits_{\psi\in\Pi}b_{\psi}w(\psi)\in\mathbb{Z}[\bigcup\limits_{w\in W(x)}w(\Pi)].

denote the corresponding reflection in

Compactly induced representations and supercuspidals

We continue to let

and

\mathcal{C}
. In the previous section we described the abelianisation

of

.

For the remainder of this section, fix a nontrivial additive character
\chi:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

3.1
Generalities on intertwining

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K
be an open subgroup of
G(k)
containing
Z(k)
. Let
(\tau,W)
be a smooth finite-dimensional complex representation of
K
. For

, write
K^{g}=g^{-1}Kg
and let
\tau^{g}:K^{g}\to\mathrm{GL}(W)
be the representation given by
\tau^{g}(k^{g})=\tau(k)
. The intertwining of
\tau
is the set

I(G,K,\tau)=\{g\in G(k):\mathrm{Hom}_{K\cap K^{g}}(\tau,\tau^{g})\neq\{0\}\}.

This is a union of
K
-double cosets.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be an open subgroup of
G(k)
containing
Z(k)
. Let
(\tau,W)
be a smooth finite-dimensional irreducible complex representation of
K
. Then
\mathrm{ind}_{K}^{G(k)}\tau
is an irreducible representation of
G(k)
if and only if
I(G,K,\tau)=K
.

Proof.

This is a generalisation of
[
2
, Theorem 11.4]
. See also
[
17
]
.
∎

More generally, suppose that
J
is a compact open subgroup of
G(k)
that is normal in some open subgroup
H
of
G(k)
that contains
Z(k)
and is compact modulo

. For a smooth character
\chi:J\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, define the subgroup
H_{\chi}=\{h\in H:\chi^{h}=\chi\}
of

. Then

is normal in
H_{\chi}
, and the quotient

is a finite group when

is compact, as is the case when

is simple. Define

\mathcal{H}_{\chi}:=\mathrm{End}_{H_{\chi}}(\mathrm{ind}_{J}^{H_{\chi}}\chi).

By Mackey theory for finite groups, there is a bijection
\rho\mapsto\chi_{\rho}
between the set
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{H}_{\chi})
of simple
\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
-modules to the set of irreducible constituents of
\mathrm{ind}_{J}^{H_{\chi}}\chi
such that

\mathrm{ind}_{J}^{H_{\chi}}\chi=\bigoplus\limits_{\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{H}_{\chi})}(\dim\rho)\cdot\chi_{\rho}.

The
\chi_{\rho}
are
\chi
-isotypic when restricted to
J
and induce irreducibly to

.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
I(G,J,\chi)=H_{\chi}
. Then the following hold:

•

The representation
\mathrm{ind}_{J}^{G(k)}\chi
has a finite direct sum decomposition

\mathrm{ind}_{J}^{G(k)}\chi=\bigoplus\limits_{\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{H}_{\chi})}(\dim\rho)\cdot\mathrm{ind}_{H_{\chi}}^{G(k)}\chi_{\rho}.

•

For each
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{H}_{\chi})
, the compactly induced representation
\mathrm{ind}_{H_{\chi}}^{G(k)}\chi_{\rho}
is irreducible and supercuspidal.

•

If
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
are inequivalent simple modules for
\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
, then
\mathrm{ind}_{H_{\chi}}^{G(k)}\chi_{\rho}
and
\mathrm{ind}_{H_{\chi}}^{G(k)}\chi_{\rho^{\prime}}
are inequivalent representations of
G(k)
.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
23
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

The following structural result will be useful in determining intertwining sets.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
x,y
be points in
\mathcal{A}
. We have a version of the affine Bruhat decomposition,

G(k)=G(k)_{x,0}N(k)G(k)_{y,0}.

A set of double coset representatives is contained in a set of lifts of the Iwahori–Weyl group
W(G)
to
N(k)
.

Proof.

For the case when

is the barycentre of an alcove, see
[
28
, 3.3.1]
. See also
[
15
]
. This case also implies the decomposition holds when

and

lie in the closure of the same alcove, as if the barycentre of such an alcove is

such that

3.2
Epipelagic representations

We briefly summarise the construction of epipelagic representations due to
[

]
. Let
x
be the barycentre of a facet in
\mathcal{A}
. Write

for the quotient

Definition 3.5

We say that

is a stable functional for the action of

if the following hold:

•

The orbit
\mathbb{G}_{x}\cdot\lambda
in

is Zariski-closed.

•

The stabiliser of
\lambda
in
\mathbb{G}_{x}
is finite, as an algebraic group.

Motivated by the Hilbert–Mumford criterion for stability, we make the following definitions:

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\sigma:G(k)_{x,+}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a character. We say that
\psi\in\Psi
is in the support
\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
of

if
U_{\psi}\leq G(k)_{x,+}
(equivalently,

) and

if

(as in the cases

or

), define
\mathrm{supp}(\lambda)=\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{\lambda})
. As
\chi
is nontrivial, this does not depend on the choice of
\chi
.

Suppose now that
\lambda\in V_{x}^{\vee}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=(G(k)_{x,+:++})^{\vee}

the stabiliser of
\chi_{\lambda}
in
G(k)_{x}
and define the intertwining algebra

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}:=\mathrm{End}_{G(k)_{x,\lambda}}\left(\mathrm{ind}_{G(k)_{x,+}}^{G(k)_{x,\lambda}}\chi_{\lambda}\right).

The subgroup
G(k)_{x,+}
is normal in

, and let

denote the quotient, a finite group. If
\rho
is a simple

-module, let

denote the corresponding irreducible constituent of
\mathrm{ind}_{G(k)_{x,+}}^{G(k)_{x,\lambda}}\chi_{\lambda}

Proposition 3.7
.

[

Suppose that

-rational stable functional for the action of

1.

Example 3.8
.

Let
x
be the barycentre of
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathbb{G}_{x}=\mathbb{T}_{x}
. As

is split, we may identify

as

-modules. Under this identification, the weight space decomposition for

(
[

]

.

Let

be an

By

is

Suppose that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\neq\mathrm{Hom}_{G(k)_{y,r+}}(\mathbbm{1}_{G(k)_{y,r+}},\pi_{\lambda}\mid_{G(k)_{y,r+}})

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus\limits_{g\in G(k)_{y,r+}\backslash G(k)/G(k)_{x,+}}\mathrm{Hom}_{G(k)_{y,r+}}(\mathbbm{1}_{G(k)_{y,r+}},\mathrm{ind}_{\prescript{g}{}{G(k)_{x,+}}\cap G(k)_{y,r+}}^{G(k)_{y,r+}}\prescript{g}{}{\chi_{\lambda}}\mid_{\prescript{g}{}{G(k)_{x,+}}\cap G(k)_{y,r+}})

3.3
Towards higher depth

We would like to extend the construction of
[
23
]
from stable functionals of

to certain functionals on
V(x)
. Unlike the case of
G(k)_{x,+:++}
, the space
V(x,k^{\mathrm{ur}})=G(k^{\mathrm{ur}})_{x,+:1}^{\mathrm{ab}}
does not admit an algebraic representation of
\mathbb{G}_{x}
, or even
\mathbb{T}_{x}
. Indeed, there is different behaviour in
V(x)
depending on whether
q=2,3
or
q>3
, so that in general

V(x,k^{\mathrm{ur}})\not\cong V(x)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

By the Hilbert–Mumford criterion, a functional
\lambda\in V_{x}^{\vee}
is stable if and only if, for any one-parameter subgroup
\gamma:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{x}
,
\lambda
has a negative weight for the action of
\gamma
on
V_{x}^{\vee}
(induced by the action of
\mathbb{G}_{x}
). In the construction of
[
23
]
, it turns out that we only need this condition to hold for all
\gamma
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In other words, we only require that
g\cdot\lambda
has a negative weight for the action of
\gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{T}_{x})
for any
g\in\mathbb{G}_{x}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and any
\gamma
. As
T
is split over
k
, we may identify

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{T}_{x})=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(\mathbb{G}_{m},T)=X_{*}(T).

If
\lambda
is stable, then for any
\gamma\in X_{*}(T)
and

, there exists

such that

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\lambda\in V(x)^{\vee}
. We say that
\lambda
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stable if, for any
\gamma\in X_{*}(T)
and

, there exists
\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(g\cdot\lambda)
such that

.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{R}
and suppose
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
are linear functionals on
V
with the property that for all nonzero
v\in V
, there exists
i
such that
f_{i}(v)>0
. Then for any
r>0

\{v\in V:f_{i}(v)\leq r\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n\}

is compact.

Proof.

Let
S
denote the unit sphere in
V
and
B
the unit disc so that the boundary of
B
is
S
. For each
v\in S
, there exists
i
such that
f_{i}(v)>0
. Therefore there exists
R(v)>0
such that

\max_{i}f_{i}(R(v)v)=r

and
R(v)
is continuous in
v
. As
S
is compact, the function
R
takes a maximum value
R_{0}
on
S
. Then we have the containment

\{v\in V:f_{i}(v)\leq r\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n\}\subset R_{0}\cdot B

so that
\{v\in V:f_{i}(v)\leq r\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n\}
is closed and bounded and therefore compact.
∎

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\lambda\in V(x)^{\vee}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stable functional. Fix a positive real number
r
. There exists finitely many
g\in G(k)_{x,r}\backslash G(k)/G(k)_{x,+}
such that
\lambda
vanishes on
G(k)_{x,+}\cap g^{-1}G(k)_{x,r}g
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
a set of
G(k)_{x,0}
double coset representatives of
G(k)
is contained in a set of lifts of elements of
W(G)
to
N(k)
. Write
g=anb
where
a,b\in G(k)_{x,0}
and
n\in N(k)
has image
w\in W(G)
. Then

G(k)_{x,+}\cap g^{-1}G(k)_{x,r}g=G(k)_{x,+}\cap b^{-1}n^{-1}G(k)_{x,r}nb=G(k)_{x,+}\cap b^{-1}G(k)_{y,r}b

where
y=w^{-1}(x)\in\mathcal{A}(T)
and the first equality follows from the fact that
G(k)_{x,r}
is normal in
G(k)_{x,0}
. So we need to show that there are finitely many pairs of
w\in W(G)
and
b\in G(k)_{x,0}/G(k)_{x,+}\cong\mathbb{G}_{x}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
\lambda
vanishes on
G(k)_{x,+}\cap b^{-1}G(k)_{y,r}b
, or equivalently
b\cdot\lambda
vanishes on
G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{y,r}
. By definition of stability,
b\cdot\lambda
is still
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stable. As there are finitely many choices for
b
, we may fix
b
and show there are only finitely many
w
with the above property.

Set
\gamma=y-x\in X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
. We have for any
\psi\in\Psi

\psi(y)=\psi(x+\gamma)=\psi(x)+\langle\dot{\psi},\gamma\rangle.

Observe that
G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{y,r}
contains
U_{\psi}
for all
\psi\in\Psi
such that
\psi(x)>0
and
\psi(y)\geq r
. If
\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda)
then we automatically have
\psi(x)>0
. So, for such
\psi
, if
\langle\dot{\psi},\gamma\rangle\geq r
, we certainly have
\psi(y)\geq r
. It suffices to show that there exist finitely many
w\in W(G)
such that for
\gamma=w^{-1}(x)-x
we have
\langle\dot{\psi},\gamma\rangle\leq r
for all
\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda)
. This follows from Lemma
3.13
and the fact that under the action of
W(G)
on
X_{*}(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}
, the stabiliser of any point is finite and the orbit of any point is discrete.
∎

Let

be an

-stable functional. Then

is a union of finitely many

-double cosets.

Proof.

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
\lambda\in V(x)^{\vee}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stable functional. The compactly induced representation
(\pi_{\lambda},\Sigma)=\mathrm{ind}_{G(k)_{x,+}}^{G(k)}\chi_{\lambda}
is admissible.

Proof.

We must show that for any compact open subgroup
K
of
G(k)
, the vector space of fixed points
\Sigma^{K}
of
K
is finite dimensional. We may shrink
K
to assume that
K=G(k)_{x,r}
for some positive real number
r
, as the Moy–Prasad filtration at any
x
is a neighbourhood basis of the identity. Then we must show that
\mathrm{Hom}_{G(k)_{x,r}}(\mathbbm{1}_{G(k)_{x,r}},\pi_{\lambda}\mid_{G(k)_{x,r}})
is finite dimensional. By Frobenius reciprocity and Mackey theory we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{G(k)_{x,r}}(\mathbbm{1}_{G(k)_{x,r}},\pi_{\lambda}\mid_{G(k)_{x,r}})\cong\bigoplus\limits_{g\in G(k)_{x,r}\backslash G(k)/G(k)_{x,+}}\mathrm{Hom}_{G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,r}^{g}}(\mathbbm{1}_{G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,r}^{g}},\chi_{\lambda}\mid_{G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,r}^{g}}).

As
\chi_{\lambda}
is a character, and
\chi
is nontrivial on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\mathrm{Hom}_{G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,r}^{g}}(\mathbbm{1}_{G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,r}^{g}},\chi_{\lambda}\mid_{G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,r}^{g}})
is nonzero (and 1-dimensional) if and only if
\lambda
vanishes on
G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,r}^{g}
. The result follows from Proposition
3.14
.
∎

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
n\in N(k)
with image
w\in W(G)
and let
a,b\in G(k)_{x,0}
. Let
\lambda\in V(x)^{\vee}
. If
a^{-1}nb\in I(G,G(k)_{x,+},\chi_{\lambda})
, then

w(\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda))\cap\Psi_{x}^{+}\subset\mathrm{supp}(a\cdot\lambda).

Proof.

Suppose that
\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda)=\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{\lambda}^{b^{-1}})
and
w(\psi)\in\Psi_{x}^{+}
but
w(\psi)\not\in\mathrm{supp}(a\cdot\lambda)=\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{\lambda}^{a^{-1}})
. If
a^{-1}nb
intertwines
\chi_{\lambda}
, then
\chi_{\lambda}=\chi_{\lambda}^{a^{-1}nb}
on
G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,+}^{a^{-1}nb}
. So
\chi_{\lambda}^{b^{-1}}=\chi_{\lambda}^{a^{-1}n}
on
G(k)_{x,+}^{b^{-1}}\cap G(k)_{x,+}^{a^{-1}n}=G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,+}^{n}
. This equality follows from the fact that
G(k)_{x,+}
is normal in
G(k)_{x,0}
. Since
U_{\psi}=U_{w(\psi)}^{n}
, and
w(\psi)\in\Psi_{x}^{+}
, we have
U_{\psi}\leq G(k)_{x,+}\cap G(k)_{x,+}^{n}
. By assumption,
\chi_{\lambda}^{a^{-1}}
is trivial on
U_{w(\psi)}
, so
\chi_{\lambda}^{b^{-1}}=\chi_{\lambda}^{a^{-1}n}
is trivial on
U_{\psi}=U_{w(\psi)}^{n}
, a contradiction to
\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(\chi^{b^{-1}})
.
∎

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
n\in N(k)
with image
w\in W(G)
and let
a,b\in G(k)_{x,0}
. Let
\lambda\in V(x)^{\vee}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stable functional. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the facet of
\mathcal{C}
of which
x
is the barycentre. If
a^{-1}nb\in I(G,G(k)_{x,+},\chi_{\lambda})
, then there does not exist
y\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}
such that
\theta(y)=0
for all
\theta\in w(\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda))\cap\Psi_{x}^{+}
.

Proof.

Suppose such a
y
existed. Then for all
\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda)
, we have that
w(\psi)\not\in\Psi_{x}^{+}
(and so
w(\psi)
is non-positive on
\mathcal{F}
) or
\psi(w^{-1}y)=w(\psi)(y)=0
. By definition,
b\cdot\lambda
is still
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stable. By Lemma
3.11
, there exist real numbers
a_{\psi}\geq 0
such that

\sum\limits_{\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda)}a_{\psi}\psi\text{ is a constant function $c$.}

As each
\psi
is positive on
x
, this constant
c
must be positive. But then

c=\sum\limits_{\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda)}a_{\psi}\psi(w^{-1}y)=\sum\limits_{\psi\in\mathrm{supp}(b\cdot\lambda)}a_{\psi}w(\psi)(y)\leq 0,

a contradiction.
∎

If

Proof.

By combining

\pi(\lambda^{\prime},\rho)
, then we must have

\frac{|A_{\lambda^{\prime}}|}{\dim\chi_{\lambda^{\prime},\rho}}=q.

In particular, 3 divides
|A_{\lambda^{\prime}}|
. It suffices to prove that there is no order 3 element of
\mathbb{G}_{y}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
that stabilises a stable vector in
V_{y}^{\vee}
. The reductive quotient
\mathbb{G}_{y}
is isogenous to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}
, whose centre is a 2-group, so we may replace
\mathbb{G}_{y}
by
\mathrm{SL}_{2}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}
. The order 3 elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3})
are all conjugate to one of

\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right).

(5)

As a representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3})
,
V_{y}^{\vee}
is isomorphic to
(P_{1}\boxtimes P_{3})(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3})
, by
[
24
, Proposition 4.2]
, where for any commutative ring
A
,
P_{n}(A)
is the space of homogeneous degree-
n
polynomials over
A
in two variables, with
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A)
acting by

\begin{pmatrix}s&t\\
u&w\end{pmatrix}\cdot f(X,Y)=f(sX+uY,tX+wY).

Take an arbitrary element

F(X,Y,Z,W)=(aZ+bW)\otimes X^{3}+(cZ+dW)\otimes X^{2}Y+(eZ+fW)\otimes XY^{2}+(gZ+hW)\otimes Y^{3}\in(P_{1}\boxtimes P_{3})(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3})

where
a,b,c,d,e,f,g,h\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
. If
F
is stabilised by one of the elements of (
5
), one checks that
d=f=0
or
e=f=0
. By
[
24
, Proposition 2.15]
, there is a polynomial
\Delta(F)
in
a,\dots,h
such that
F
is stable for the action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3})
if and only if
\Delta(F)\neq 0
. Explicitly, there are polynomials
H_{6}(F)
and
G_{6}(F)
such that
\Delta(F)=H_{6}(F)^{3}G_{6}(F)
and
H_{6}(F)
reduces in characteristic 3 to
c^{3}f^{3}-d^{3}e^{3}
. We see that this is 0 for any
F
stabilised by an element of order 3, so that such
F
is not a stable vector.

∎

Remark 4.12
.

One is naturally interested in describing the corresponding Langlands parameter
\varphi
to
\pi_{\lambda}
. As the dual reductive group to
G_{2}
is again
G_{2}
,
\varphi
is a continuous homomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{W}_{k}\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to G_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, where
\mathcal{W}_{k}
denotes the Weil group of
k
. The formal degree conjecture as reformulated in
[
13
, Conjecture 7.1]
(and originally due to
[
14
]
) predicts that the formal degree of
\pi_{\lambda}
matches numerical invariants attached to
\varphi
. In this remark we deduce some facts about
\varphi
, assuming the formal degree conjecture. For more details, see the forthcoming thesis of the author.

Let
\pi_{s}
denote a simple supercuspidal for
G_{2}(k)
as constructed by
[
13
]
, coming from a choice
\chi
of ‘affine generic character’, and let
\varphi_{s}
be the corresponding parameter under the formal degree conjecture. In
[
13
, Section 9.5]
, Gross–Reeder show that
\varphi_{s}
is a simple wild parameter, meaning that
\varphi_{s}
is trivial on the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
factor, it has minimal adjoint Swan conductor given by the rank of
G_{2}
(so
b(\varphi_{s})=2
), and the image of the inertia subgroup of
\mathcal{W}_{k}
has finite centraliser in
G_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Their argument relies on a compatibility assumption of unramified base change of the simple supercuspidals and restriction of the Langlands parameters. More precisely, if
k_{m}
denotes the degree
m
unramified extension of
k
, one can pull back
\chi
under the trace map
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
to an affine generic character
\chi_{m}
for
G_{2}(k_{m})
, and hence produce a simple supercuspidal
\pi_{s,m}
of
G_{2}(k_{m})
. They make the assumption that the corresponding parameter to this pullback is the restriction of
\varphi_{s}
to
\mathcal{W}_{k_{m}}
.

In our setting, we may similarly pullback an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-stable functional
\lambda
to an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-stable functional
\lambda_{m}
on
G_{2}(k_{m})_{x,+:1}^{\mathrm{ab}}
using the trace map
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
. More precisely,
G_{2}(k_{m})_{x,+:1}^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong V(x)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is a direct sum of distinguished lines
V_{\psi}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(x)
, and we take the sum of trace maps
V_{\psi}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\to V_{\psi}
. As the trace map on finite fields is nonzero, the support of
\lambda_{m}
is the same as the support of
\lambda
. We therefore produce irreducible supercuspidals
\pi_{\lambda_{m}}
where
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
. Replacing
\pi_{s,m}
by
\pi_{\lambda_{m}}
has the effect of multiplying the formal degree of
\pi_{s,m}
by
q^{m}
. This comes from the fact that
G(k)_{x,+}
is index
q
in
\mathcal{I}_{+}
and that
G_{2}
has trivial centre. We may then apply the same argument as in
[
13
, Section 9.5]
to deduce, under the analogous assumption that the corresponding parameter to
\pi_{\lambda_{m}}
is the restriction of
\varphi
to
\mathcal{W}_{k_{m}}
, that
\varphi
is trivial on the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
factor, the image of inertia has finite centraliser in
G_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and the Swan conductor of
\varphi
is given by
b(\varphi)=b(\varphi_{s})+2=4
.

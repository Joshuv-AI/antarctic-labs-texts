Hao Yuan , Xiaoping Xie School of Mathematics, Sichuan University, Chengdu 610064, China

Abstract
This paper is devoted to a numerical analysis of a fractional viscoelastic wave propagation model that generalizes the fractional Maxwell model and the fractional Zener model. First, we convert the model problem into a velocity type integro-differential equation and establish existence, uniqueness and regularity of its solution. Then we consider a conforming linear/bilinear/trilinear finite element semi-discrete scheme and a fast scheme of backward Euler full discretization with a sum-of-exponentials (SOE) approximation for the convolution integral, and derive error estimates for the semi-discrete and fully discrete schemes. Finally, we provide several numerical examples to verify the theoretical results.



Keywords:
Fractional viscoelasticity model; wave propagation; integro-differential equation; finite element method; error estimate

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=2,\,3)
be a convex polygon domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
, and let
T
be a positive constant. Consider the following general fractional viscoelasticity model of wave propagation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}}{\partial t^{2}}=\mathrm{\textbf{div}}\sigma+\mathbf{f},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T],\\
\sigma+\tau_{\sigma}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\sigma}{\partial t^{\alpha}}=\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})+\tau_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\mathbb{D}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})}{\partial t^{\alpha}},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T],\\
\mathbf{u}=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
\mathbf{u}(x,0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(x),\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}(x,0)=\mathbf{v}_{0}(x),\sigma(x,0)=\sigma_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here
\mathbf{u}
is the displacement field,
\sigma=(\sigma_{ij})_{d\times d}
is the symmetric stress tensor with its divergence given by
\textbf{div}\sigma=(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\sigma_{i1},\cdots,\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{i}\sigma_{id})^{\top}
, and
\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})=(\bigtriangledown\mathbf{u}+(\bigtriangledown\mathbf{u})^{\top})/2
is the strain tensor. The parameters
\rho(>0)
,
\tau_{\sigma}(>0)
and
\tau_{\varepsilon}(\geq 0)
represent the mass density, the relaxation time and the retardation time, respectively. The fourth-order symmetric tensors
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{D}
satisfy

\displaystyle 0<M_{0}\xi:\xi\leq\mathbb{C}\xi:\xi\leq M_{1}\xi:\xi

\displaystyle\quad\forall\text{ symmetric tensor }\xi=(\xi_{ij})_{d\times d},\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega,

(1.2)

\displaystyle 0<M_{0}\xi:\xi\leq\mathbb{D}\xi:\xi\leq M_{1}\xi:\xi

\displaystyle\quad\forall\text{ symmetric tensor }\xi=(\xi_{ij})_{d\times d},\text{ a.e. }x\in\Omega,

(1.3)

where
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
are positive constants, and
\xi:\zeta=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{ij}\zeta_{ij}
.
Notice that for an isotropic elastic medium
\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})
and
\mathbb{D}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})
take the forms

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})=2\mu_{C}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})+\lambda_{C}\text{div}\mathbf{u}I,\qquad\mathbb{D}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})=2\mu_{D}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})+\lambda_{D}\text{div}\mathbf{u}I,

(1.4)

respectively, where
\mu_{C},\ \mu_{D}
,
\lambda_{C}
,
\lambda_{D}
are the Lamé parameters, and
I
the identity matrix.
\mathbf{f}
is the body force and
\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)
,
\mathbf{v}_{0}(x)
,
\sigma_{0}(x)
are initial data. For any function
\mathbf{w}(x,t)
and
0<\alpha<1
, let
\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{w}}{\partial t^{\alpha}}
be the
\alpha
-order Caputo fractional derivative of
\mathbf{w}
defined by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\mathbf{w}}{\partial t^{\alpha}}(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}(s)\,ds.

(1.5)

It is worth noting that the classical viscoelasticity models correspond to specific choices of the relaxation and retardation times in the constitutive (second) equation of (
1.1
) with
\alpha=1
:

•

The Maxwell model:
\tau_{\sigma}>0
,
\tau_{\varepsilon}=0
;

•

The Zener model:
\tau_{\sigma}>0
,
\tau_{\varepsilon}>0
;

•

The Kelvin-Voigt model:
\tau_{\sigma}=0
,
\tau_{\varepsilon}>0
.

Many materials exhibit both elastic and viscous kinematic behaviors simultaneously. This phenomenon, known as viscoelasticity, is typically characterized through the combination of ideal elements such as springs, which obey the Hooke’s law, and viscous dashpots, which follow the Newton’s law. Different configurations of these elements result in a variety of viscoelasticity models, including the Maxwell model, the Zener model, and the Kelvin-Voigt model. In this paper, we carry out a finite element analysis for the fractional viscoelasticity model
Eq.

1.1
. Our contributions are as follows:

•

We reformulate the fractional constitutive equation of model
Eq.

1.1
as an integro-differential form to convert the original fractional viscoelasticity model into a velocity type integro-differential equation, and establish existence, uniqueness and regularity results for the resulting model based on the analytic semigroup theory and the Galerkin method.

•

We consider a conforming linear/bilinear/trilinear finite element semi-discretization of the resulting model and derive error estimates.

•

Based on the spatial semi-discretization, we develop a fast full discretization for the resulting model, where the backward Euler scheme and a sum-of-exponentials (SOE) approximation are respectively applied to the first-order temporal derivative of the velocity and to the convolution integral term with a convolution kernel of the Mittag-Leffler function. We derive error estimates for the proposed fully discrete scheme and provide numerical examples to test its performance.

The rest of this paper is arranged as follows. Section 2 introduces some preliminary results. Section 3 presents wellposedness and regularity results for the resulting model. Section 4 carries out error analysis for the semi-discrete scheme. Section 5 gives the fast fully discrete scheme and derives error estimates. Finally, numerical examples are provided in Section 6 to verify the obtained theoretical results.

For simplicity, we

and
L^{p}({\bf X}):=L^{p}([0,T];{\bf X})
.
For an integer

, the space

. Note that

,

,

,

2.2
Equivalent integro-differential equation and weak problem

We note that the constitutive equation in the model
Eq.

1.1
is of the following differential form:

\sigma+\tau_{\sigma}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\sigma}{\partial t^{\alpha}}=\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})+\tau_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\mathbb{D}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})}{\partial t^{\alpha}}.

(2.1)

As shown in
[
42
]
, applying the Laplace transform and the inverse Laplace transform to (
2.1
) we obtain the following explicit expression of
\sigma
when
\tau_{\sigma}\neq 0
:

\displaystyle\sigma=

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t})-\int_{0}^{t}E_{\alpha}(-(\frac{t-s}{\tau_{\sigma}})^{\alpha})\left(\mathbb{C}-(\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon}}{\tau_{\sigma}})^{\alpha}\mathbb{D}\right)\varepsilon(\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}(s))\,ds+\iota_{0},

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle\iota_{0}:=E_{\alpha}(-(\frac{t}{\tau_{\sigma}})^{\alpha})\big(\sigma_{0}-\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}_{0})\big),

(2.3)

and
E_{\alpha}(z)
denotes the single parameter Mittag-Leffler function defined by

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{j}}{\Gamma(j\alpha+1)},\qquad\alpha>0.

(2.4)

Remark 2.1
.

For
0<\alpha<1
and
t\geq 0
, there holds (cf.
[
26
]
)

\frac{1}{1+\Gamma(1-\alpha)t^{\alpha}}\leq E_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})\leq\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)+t^{\alpha}}.

(2.5)

Substitute the alternative constitutive equation
Eq.

2.2
into the first equation of
Eq.

1.1
and introduce the velocity variable

\mathbf{v}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t},

then we get the following velocity type integro-differential equation of the fractional viscoelasticity model:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}-\mathrm{\textbf{div}}\left(\mathbb{A}\varepsilon(\mathbf{v})-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)\mathbb{B}\varepsilon(\mathbf{v}(s))\,ds\right)=\mathbf{F},&(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T],\\
\mathbf{v}=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
\mathbf{v}(0)=\mathbf{v}_{0},&x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2.6)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{A}:=\rho^{-1}\mathbb{C},\quad&&\mathbb{B}:=\rho^{-1}\left(\mathbb{C}-(\frac{\tau_{\varepsilon}}{\tau_{\sigma}})^{\alpha}\mathbb{D}\right),\\
&\beta(t):=E_{\alpha}(-(\frac{t}{\tau_{\sigma}})^{\alpha}),\quad&&\mathbf{F}:=\rho^{-1}\left(\mathbf{f}+\mathrm{\textbf{div}}\iota_{0}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.7)

We consider the following weak formulation of
Eq.

2.6
:
For
t\in(0,T],
find
\mathbf{v}(t)\in{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t},\mathbf{w}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w})\,ds=\langle\mathbf{F},\mathbf{w}\rangle,\quad\forall\ \mathbf{w}\in{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\\
\mathbf{v}(0)=\mathbf{v}_{0},\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

where

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{A}\varepsilon(\mathbf{v}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{w})\,dx,\ \ b(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{B}\varepsilon(\mathbf{v}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{w})\,dx.

(2.9)

Under the assumption
Eq.

1.2
, we immediately have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})\geq C\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{1}^{2},\\
&a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})=a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}),\end{aligned}\right.\quad\forall\ \mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.10)

We also have the following boundedness results:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\leq C\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{1}\cdot\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1},\\
&b(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\leq C\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{1}\cdot\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1},\end{aligned}\right.\quad\forall\ \mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.11)

Basic results

2.3.1
Analytic semigroup

Define an operator
\A:{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
by

\A:=-\mathrm{\textbf{div}}(\mathbb{A}\varepsilon(\mathbf{\cdot})).

(2.12)

From (
2.10
) we easily get

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\langle\A\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}\rangle=a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})\geq C\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{1}^{2},\\
&\langle\A\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\rangle=a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})=\langle\mathbf{v},\A\mathbf{w}\rangle.\end{aligned}\right.\quad\forall\ \mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.13)

We mention that the following inequality (cf.
[
8
]
) also holds:

\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\A\mathbf{v}\right\|,\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{v}\in{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.14)

By definition and (
2.13
) we see that
\A
is a linear, positive, self-adjoint elliptic operator.
Thus, From
[
18
, Theorem 1 in section 6.5.1]
there exists a complete orthonormal basis,

\left\{\bm{\varphi}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

of
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
and a nondecreasing sequence
\left\{\lambda_{k}>0\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

\A\bm{\varphi}_{k}=\lambda_{k}\bm{\varphi}_{k},\quad k=1,2,\cdots.

(2.15)

Meanwhile,
\left\{\lambda^{-1/2}\bm{\varphi}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal basis of
{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
equipped with the
inner product
a(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Denote by

\displaystyle S(t):=\exp(-t\A)

(2.16)

the analytic semigroup generated by
-\A
.
The following lemma shows some semigroup properties of
S(t)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
30
,
40
]
For
0<\alpha<1
, let
g\in C^{1}({\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))

satisfy

\left\|g(t)\right\|\leq Ct^{-\alpha},\quad\left\|\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}(t)\right\|\leq Ct^{-\alpha-1},\quad\forall\ t\in(0,T].

Then

\left\|\A\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)g(s)\,ds\right\|\leq Ct^{-\alpha},\quad\forall\ t\in(0,T].

(2.17)

Furthermore, for any non-negative integer
k
and
t\in(0,T]
there hold

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}S(t)}{\partial t^{k}}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-k}\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2},\quad&\forall\ &\mathbf{v}\in{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\
&\left\|\frac{\partial S(t)}{\partial t}\mathbf{v}\right\|\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{1},\quad&\forall\ &\mathbf{v}\in{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\\
&\left\|\frac{\partial^{k}S(t)}{\partial t^{k}}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2j}\leq Ct^{-k-j}\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|,\quad&\forall\ &\mathbf{v}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega),\ \ j=0,1.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.18)

Lemma 2.2
.

For
T>0
and
0<\alpha<1
, there holds

\left|\frac{dE_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})}{dt}\right|\leq Ct^{-\alpha-1},\qquad\forall\ t\in(0,T].

(2.19)

Proof.

Due to

\displaystyle\left|\frac{dE_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})}{dt}\right|=t^{\alpha-1}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-t^{\alpha}).

Note that
t^{2\alpha}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})
is bounded in
[0,T]
. Then, there exists a constant
C
such that for
0\leq t\leq T

\displaystyle t^{2\alpha}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})\leq C,

which indicates

\displaystyle\left|\frac{dE_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})}{dt}\right|=t^{-\alpha-1}\cdot t^{2\alpha}E_{\alpha,\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})\leq Ct^{-\alpha-1}.

∎

2.3.2
Volterra integro-differential system

Let
{m}
be a positive integer. Consider the Volterra integro-differential system

\mathbf{y}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{G}(t-s)\mathbf{y}(s)\,ds=\mathbf{g}(t),\qquad t\in[0,T],

(2.20)

where
\mathbf{g}(t)=\left(g_{1}(t),g_{2}(t),\cdots,g_{{m}}(t)\right)^{\top}
is a vector-valued function and
\mathbf{G}(t)=(G_{ij}(t))_{i,j=1}^{{m}}
is a matrix-valued function. We have the following lemma:

Lemma 2.3
.

[
9
]
Suppose that the functions
\mathbf{g}
and
\mathbf{G}
satisfy

g_{i},G_{ij}\in C^{0}[0,T]\cap C^{1}(0,T].

Then the system
Eq.

2.20

admits a unique solution

\mathbf{y}(t)=\left(y_{1}(t),y_{2}(t),\cdots,y_{{m}}(t)\right)^{\top}
with
y_{i}\in C^{0}[0,T]\cap C^{1}(0,T]
.

Based on
Lemma

2.3
and a standard technique, we can obtain the following result:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
M_{\gamma_{1}},M_{\gamma_{2}}
be two
{m}\times{m}
matrices. Under the same conditions as in Lemma
2.3
, the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\mathbf{z}}{\partial t}(t)+M_{\gamma_{1}}\mathbf{z}(t)-M_{\gamma_{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{G}(t-s)\mathbf{z}(s)\,ds=\mathbf{g}(t),\qquad t\in(0,T],\\
&\mathbf{z}(0)=\mathbf{z}_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

(2.21)

admits a unique solution
\mathbf{z}(t)=\left(z_{1}(t),z_{2}(t),\cdots,z_{{m}}(t)\right)^{\top}
with
\mathbf{z}_{i}\in C^{1}[0,T]\cap C^{2}(0,T]
.

Proof.

Denote
\mathbf{y}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{z}}{\partial t}
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}(t)=\mathbf{z}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{y}(\tau)\,d\tau.

(2.22)

Rewriting
Eq.

2.21
in terms of
\mathbf{y}
, we get

\mathbf{y}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\mathbf{G}}(t-s)\mathbf{y}(s)\,ds=\tilde{\mathbf{g}}(t),

(2.23)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{G}}(t)=-M_{\gamma_{1}}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{G}(\tau)\,d\tau,\quad\tilde{\mathbf{g}}(t)=\mathbf{g}(t)-M_{\gamma_{1}}\mathbf{z}_{0}-M_{\gamma_{2}}\mathbf{z}_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{G}(t-\tau)\,d\tau.

By
Lemma

2.3
we know that the equation (
2.23
) admits a unique solution
\mathbf{y}(t)=\left(y_{1}(t),y_{2}(t),\cdots,y_{{m}}(t)\right)^{\top}
with
y_{i}\in C^{0}[0,T]\cap C^{1}(0,T]
.
As a result, the desired conclusion follows from (
2.22
).
∎

2.3.3
Several inequalities

Let us first introduce two types of Gronwall’s inequalities (cf.
[
40
]
):

Lemma 2.5
.

(Continuous Gronwall’s inequality)

Let
0<\alpha<1
and let

and

be two nonnegative functions. Suppose that

y(t)\leq b(t)+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}y(s)\,ds,\qquad\forall\ t\in[0,T]

with

, then there exists a constant

such that

y(t)\leq b(t)+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}b(s)\,ds,\qquad\forall\ t\in[0,T].

(2.25)

Furthermore, if

is a nondecreasing function of

y(t)\leq Cb(t),\qquad\forall\ t\in[0,T].

(2.26)

Lemma 2.6
.

(Discrete Gronwall’s inequality)

Suppose that the sequence
(y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is non-negative and satisfy

y_{n}\leq h_{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mu_{n-i}y_{i},\quad\text{for}\ n\geq 0,

(2.27)

where

is a nondecreasing sequence and

y_{n}\leq h_{n}\exp(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mu_{n-i}).

(2.28)

Next we introduce two auxiliary lemmas (cf.
[
13
]
):

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G\in L^{1}(0,T)
and
f\in L^{2}(0,T)

and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant

depending only on

\left|\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{t}G(t-s)f(s)f(t)\,ds\,dt\right|\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{r}f^{2}(t)\,dt+C\int_{0}^{r}\left|G(r-t)\right|\int_{0}^{t}f^{2}(s)\,ds\,dt.

(2.29)

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
G\in L^{1}(0,T)
and denote

g_{k}:=\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}G(s)\,ds,\quad\text{for}\ k=1,2,\cdots,N,

(2.30)

where

for

\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}g_{k-i}f_{i}f_{k}\right|\leq\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}^{2}+C\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|g_{n-k}|\sum_{i=0}^{k}f_{i}^{2}.

(2.31)

2.4
SOE approximation of Mittag-Leffler function

Notice that in the weak formulation (
2.8
) there is a convolution integral term with the convolution kernel

\beta(t-s)=E_{\alpha}(-(\frac{t-s}{\tau_{\sigma}})^{\alpha}).

As the Mittag-Leffler function

E_{\alpha}(t)

is an infinite series, how to compute
E_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})
efficiently is crucial to the design of a fast numerical scheme for the fractional viscoelastic model (
1.1
).
In what follows we introduce an SOE approximation of
E_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})
given in
[
42
]
.

Notice that for
t>0
and
0<\alpha<1
there holds
[
24
,
33
]

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(\alpha\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{\alpha-1}}{s^{2\alpha}+2s^{\alpha}\cos\alpha\pi+1}e^{-st}ds.

(2.32)

Applying the integration variable substitution
x=s^{-\alpha}
to (
2.32
), we get

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x,t,\alpha)dx=\left(\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{1}^{q^{1}}+...+\int_{q^{K-1}}^{q^{K}}+\int_{q^{K}}^{\infty}\right)f(x,t,\alpha)dx

(2.33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\int_{c_{k}-r_{k}}^{c_{k}+r_{k}}f(x,t,\alpha)dx+\int_{q^{K}}^{\infty}f(x,t,\alpha)dx,

where

f(x,t,\alpha):=\frac{\sin(\alpha\pi)}{\alpha\pi}\frac{e^{-tx^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}}{x^{2}+2x\cos\alpha\pi+1},

q>1
is a constant,
K>0
is an integer, and
c_{k}
and
r_{k}
are given by

c_{0}=r_{0}=\frac{1}{2},\quad c_{k}=\frac{(q+1)q^{k-1}}{2},\quad r_{k}=\frac{(q-1)q^{k-1}}{2},\qquad k=1,2,\cdots,K.

(2.34)

We apply the integration variable substitution
x=r_{k}y+c_{k}
to each term
\displaystyle\int_{c_{k}-r_{k}}^{c_{k}+r_{k}}f(x,t,\alpha)dx
and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{c_{k}-r_{k}}^{c_{k}+r_{k}}f(x,t,\alpha)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}g_{k}(y,t)dy,

(2.35)

where

g_{k}(y,t):=\frac{\sin(\alpha\pi)}{\alpha\pi}\frac{r_{k}e^{-t(r_{k}y+c_{k})^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}}{(r_{k}y+c_{k})^{2}+2(r_{k}y+c_{k})\cos\alpha\pi+1},\quad k=0,1,\cdots,K.

Consider the following Gaussian quadrature of
g_{k}(x)
:

\int_{-1}^{1}g_{k}(x)dx=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\omega_{j}g_{k}(\xi_{j})+R_{J}(g_{k}),

(2.36)

where
\omega_{j}
and
\xi_{j}
denote the Gaussian quadrature weights and nodes, respectively, and
R_{J}(g)
denotes the remaining term.

Substitute (
2.36
) into (
2.35
), and we get

\displaystyle\int_{c_{k}-r_{k}}^{c_{k}+r_{k}}f(x,t,\alpha)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}b_{kj}e^{-ta_{kj}}+R_{J}(g_{k}),

(2.37)

where

a_{kj}=(r_{k}\xi_{j}+c_{k})^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}},\quad b_{kj}=\frac{\sin(\alpha\pi)}{\alpha\pi}\cdot\frac{\omega_{j}r_{k}}{(r_{k}\xi_{j}+c_{k})^{2}+2(r_{k}\xi_{j}+c_{k})\cos\alpha\pi+1}.

Substituting (
2.37
) into (
2.33
), we finally get the
SOE approximation of the Mittag-Leffler function:

\displaystyle\beta(t)=E_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{J}b_{kj}e^{-ta_{kj}}+R_{soe}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b_{j}e^{-a_{j}t}+R_{soe}(t),

(2.38)

where

\displaystyle R_{soe}(t):=\sum_{k=0}^{K}R_{J}(g_{k})+\int_{q^{K}}^{\infty}f(x,t,\alpha)dx,

(2.39)

is the truncation error,
N_{exp}=(K+1)J
, and

a_{j}
and
b_{j}
are the j-th elements of

\displaystyle[a_{01},a_{02},...,a_{0J},a_{11},a_{12},...,a_{1J},...,a_{(K+1)1},...,a_{(K+1)(J-1)},a_{(K+1)J}]

and

\displaystyle[b_{01},b_{02},...,b_{0J},b_{11},b_{12},...,b_{1J},...,b_{(K+1)1},...,b_{(K+1)(J-1)},b_{(K+1)J}],

respectively.

Remark 2.2
.

As shown in
[
42
]
, for any tolerance error
0<\epsilon<1
there holds

\displaystyle\left|R_{soe}(t)\right|\leq\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),\quad 0<t\leq T,

(2.40)

provided that

K=\mathcal{O}(|\log\epsilon|)
and
J=\mathcal{O}(|\log(\epsilon^{-1}|\log\epsilon|)|).
Then the computation complexity of the SOE approximation

E_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})\approx\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b_{j}e^{-a_{j}t}

is

N_{exp}=(K+1)J=\mathcal{O}(|\log\epsilon|^{2}).

In actual computation we may choose
\epsilon=\mathcal{O}(\triangle t)
, where
\triangle t:=\frac{T}{N}<1
is the temporal step size and
N
is a positive integer. In this case we have

\left|R_{soe}(t)\right|\leq\mathcal{O}(\triangle t),\quad N_{exp}=\mathcal{O}(\log^{2}N).

(2.41)

3
Results of existence, uniqueness and regularity

In this section we apply the Galerkin method (cf.
[
18
]
) to show the existence and regularity of the solution to the weak problem (
2.8
).
We first make the following assumptions on the functional
F
and the initial velocity
\mathbf{v}_{0}
:

Assumption 3.1
.

Suppose that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{F}(t)\in C^{1}({\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)),\ \frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial t^{2}}(t)\in L^{1}({\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)),\\
&\mathbf{v}_{0}\in{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\
&\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\in{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

3.1
Galerkin approximations and stability results

Recall that the operator
\A
, defined in
Eq.

2.12
, admits a set of eigenfunctions

\left\{\bm{\varphi}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

that forms a complete orthonormal basis of
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
For integer
m\geq 1
, we set

\mathscr{P}_{m}:=\text{span }\left\{\bm{\varphi}_{k}:\ k=1,2,\cdots,m\right\}.

Let
\mathbf{d}(t)=\left(d_{1}(t),d_{2}(t),...,d_{m}(t)\right)^{\top}
solve

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial\mathbf{d}}{\partial t}(t)+M_{\gamma_{1}}\mathbf{d}(t)-M_{\gamma_{2}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{G}(t-s)\mathbf{d}(s)\,ds=\mathbf{g}(t),\\
\mathbf{d}(0)=\left(\langle\mathbf{v}_{0},\bm{\varphi}_{1}\rangle,\langle\mathbf{v}_{0},\bm{\varphi}_{2}\rangle,...,\langle\mathbf{v}_{0},\bm{\varphi}_{m}\rangle\right)^{\top}.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

Here

\displaystyle M_{\gamma_{1}}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}a(\bm{\varphi}_{1},\bm{\varphi}_{1})&0&\cdots&0\\
0&a(\bm{\varphi}_{2},\bm{\varphi}_{2})&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&a(\bm{\varphi}_{m},\bm{\varphi}_{m})\end{smallmatrix}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{G}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}\beta(t)&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\beta(t)&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\beta(t)\end{smallmatrix}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle M_{\gamma_{2}}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}b(\bm{\varphi}_{1},\bm{\varphi}_{1})&b(\bm{\varphi}_{2},\bm{\varphi}_{1})&\cdots&b(\bm{\varphi}_{m},\bm{\varphi}_{1})\\
b(\bm{\varphi}_{1},\bm{\varphi}_{2})&b(\bm{\varphi}_{2},\bm{\varphi}_{2})&\cdots&b(\bm{\varphi}_{m},\bm{\varphi}_{2})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
b(\bm{\varphi}_{1},\bm{\varphi}_{m})&b(\bm{\varphi}_{2},\bm{\varphi}_{m})&\cdots&b(\bm{\varphi}_{m},\bm{\varphi}_{m})\end{smallmatrix}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{g}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}\langle F,\bm{\varphi}_{1}\rangle\\
\langle F,\bm{\varphi}_{2}\rangle\\
\vdots\\
\langle F,\bm{\varphi}_{m}\rangle\end{smallmatrix}\end{bmatrix}.

In view of
Lemma

2.4
and the assumption
\mathbf{F}(t)\in C^{1}({\bf L}^{2}(\Omega))
in
3.1

we easily get the existence and uniqueness of
\mathbf{d}(t)
, with

d_{k}(t)\in C^{1}[0,T]\cap C^{2}(0,T]
for
k=1,2,\cdots,m
.

Denote

\B:=-\mathrm{\textbf{div}}(\mathbb{B}\varepsilon(\cdot))

(3.3)

and let
P_{m}
denote the
L^{2}
-projection onto
\mathscr{P}_{m}
.
Set

\mathbf{v}_{m}(t):=\sum_{k=1}^{m}d_{k}(t)\bm{\varphi}_{k}\in\mathscr{P}_{m},

(3.4)

then from
Eq.

3.2
, (
2.12
) and (
2.9
) we know that
\mathbf{v}_{m}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle+\langle\A\mathbf{v}_{m},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)\langle\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(s),\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle\,ds=\langle\mathbf{F},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle,\\
&\langle\mathbf{v}_{m}(0),\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{v}_{0},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

for
k=1,2,\cdots,m,

or equivalently,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t}+\A\mathbf{v}_{m}-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(s)\,ds=P_{m}\mathbf{F},\\
&\mathbf{v}_{m}(0)=P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

As a result, we have the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathbf{F}(t)\in C^{1}({\bf L}^{2}(\Omega))
, then the system
Eq.

3.6
admits a unique solution
\mathbf{v}_{m}\in C^{1}\left({\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)\cap C^{2}\left({\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)
.

For the
L^{2}
-projection
P_{m}
, we have following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

There hold

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left\|P_{m}\mathbf{v}\right\|\leq\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|,&&\forall\ \mathbf{v}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega);\\
&\left\|P_{m}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2},&&\forall\ \mathbf{v}\in{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.7)

Proof.

The first inequality is standard for the
L^{2}
-projection
P_{m}
, so we only prove the second one.

Since
\left\{\bm{\varphi}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

forms a complete orthonormal basis of
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
, we have,
for any

\mathbf{v}\in{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle\bm{\varphi}_{k},\quad P_{m}\mathbf{v}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle\bm{\varphi}_{k}.

(3.8)

Then from (
2.15
) it follows

\displaystyle\left\|\A\mathbf{v}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle\A\bm{\varphi}_{k},\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle\A\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle|^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}\langle\bm{\varphi}_{k},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2}|\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}^{2}|\langle\mathbf{v},\bm{\varphi}_{k}\rangle|^{2}=\left\|\A P_{m}\mathbf{v}\right\|^{2}.

By the definition of
\A
and its property
Eq.

2.14
we easily know

C\left\|\A\mathbf{v}\right\|\leq\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\A\mathbf{v}\right\|

(3.9)

and

C\left\|\A P_{m}\mathbf{v}\right\|\leq\left\|P_{m}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\A P_{m}\mathbf{v}\right\|.

So we have

\displaystyle\left\|P_{m}\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\A P_{m}\mathbf{v}\right\|\leq C\left\|\A\mathbf{v}\right\|\leq C\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2},

which yields the desired conclusion. ∎

To discuss the boundedness of the solution
\mathbf{v}_{m}
to the system
Eq.

3.6
, we decompose
\mathbf{v}_{m}
into two components, i.e.

\mathbf{v}_{m}=\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}+\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m},

(3.10)

where
\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m},\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}\in\mathscr{P}_{m}
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}+\A\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}=P_{m}\mathbf{F},\\
&\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}(0)=P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}+\A\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(s)\,ds,\\
&\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}(0)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.12)

respectively.
According to the semigroup theory
[
35
]
and the Duhamel’s principle, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}=S(t)P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\,ds,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\beta(s-r)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(r)\,dr\,ds.

(3.14)

For
\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}
we have the following boundedness result:

Lemma 3.3
.

Under
3.1
there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf H}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf L}^{2})}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

Proof.

We divide the proof into four steps.

Step 1.

Let us estimate
\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})}
. From
Eq.

3.13
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|S(t)P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\,ds\right\|_{2}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|S(t)P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\left\|S(t-s)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\right\|_{2}\,ds+\left\|\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\,ds\right\|_{2}.

Using
Eq.

3.7
, the first inequality in
Eq.

2.18
with
k=0
and
Lemma

3.2
we get

\left\|S(t)P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}.

(3.17)

By the second inequality in
Eq.

2.18
with
k=0
and
j=1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}\left\|S(t-s)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\right\|_{2}\,ds\leq C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(t-s)^{-1}\left\|\mathbf{F}(s)\right\|\,ds\leq C\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\mathbf{F}(s)\right\|.

(3.18)

Applying the regularity inequality
Eq.

2.14
, the analytic semigroup property

\A S(t-s)=\frac{\partial S(t-s)}{\partial s},

(3.19)

integration by parts, the second inequality in
Eq.

2.18
with
k=j=0
and
Lemma

3.2
we obtain

\displaystyle C\left\|\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\,ds\right\|_{2}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\A\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\,ds\right\|=\left\|\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\frac{\partial S(t-s)}{\partial s}P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|S(0)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(t)-S(\frac{t}{2})P_{m}\mathbf{F}(\frac{t}{2})-\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|S(0)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(t)\right\|+\left\|S(\frac{t}{2})P_{m}\mathbf{F}(\frac{t}{2})\right\|+\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left\|S(t-s)P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\mathbf{F}(t)\right\|+\left\|\mathbf{F}(\frac{t}{2})\right\|+\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\right).

Combining
Eqs.

3.17
,
3.18
and
3.20
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\mathbf{F}(s)\right\|+\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\right),

which indicates

\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf H}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3.21)

Step 2.

Let us estimate
\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}
and
\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf H}^{2})}
. Firstly, from (
3.11
), (
3.9
) and
Lemma

3.2
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\A\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}\right\|+\left\|P_{m}\mathbf{F}\right\|\leq C\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|,

which plus
Eq.

3.21
further gives

\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3.22)

Secondly, acting
\A
on
Eq.

3.13
and using
Eq.

3.19
, we get

\displaystyle\A\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}

\displaystyle=\A S(t)P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}+\A\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\A S(t)P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial S(t-s)}{\partial s}P_{m}\mathbf{F}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\A S(t)P_{m}\mathbf{v}_{0}+S(t)P_{m}\mathbf{F}(0)-P_{m}\mathbf{F}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=P_{m}S(t)\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)-P_{m}\mathbf{F}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\,ds.

Differentiating this equation with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\A\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}=P_{m}\frac{\partial S(t)}{\partial t}\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)-P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial S(t-s)}{\partial t}P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=P_{m}\frac{\partial S(t)}{\partial t}\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)-S(t)P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)P_{m}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}(s)\,ds.

Thus, in light of the regularity inequality
Eq.

2.14
, the second and third inequality in
Eq.

2.18
with
k=j=0
and
Lemma

3.2

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\A\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|

(3.23)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(0)\right\|+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}(s)\right\|\,ds\right).

Furthermore, applying
3.1
and integrating the inequality over
[0,T]
, we obtain

\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf H}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3.24)

Step 3.

Let us estimate
\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf L}^{2})}
. Differentiating
Eq.

3.11
with respect to

, we arrive at

\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}+\A\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}=P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t},

(3.25)

which plus (
3.23
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\A\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|+\left\|P_{m}\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(0)\right\|+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}(s)\right\|\,ds+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|\right).

Consequently, integrating the above inequality from
0
to
T
yields

\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3.26)

Step 4.

Finally, combining
Eqs.

3.21
,
3.22
,
3.24
and
3.26
implies the desired estimate (
3.15
).

∎

For
\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}
we have the following boundedness result:

Lemma 3.4
.

Under
3.1
there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf H}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf L}^{2})}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

Proof.

The proof is divided into four steps.

Step 1.

In view of
Eq.

3.14
and
Eq.

3.10
, we decompose
\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}=Q_{1}+Q_{2},

where

\displaystyle Q_{i}=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\beta(s-r)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{m}(r)\,dr\,ds,\qquad i=1,\ 2.

For
Q_{1}
, by
Eq.

2.14
,
Eq.

2.5
,
Eq.

2.19
,
Lemmas

2.1
and
3.21
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\beta(s-r)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}(r)\,dr\,ds\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\A S(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\beta(s-r)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}(r)\,dr\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle=C\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\A\int_{0}^{t-r}S(t-r-s)\beta(s)\,ds\right)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}(r)\,dr\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left(\A\int_{0}^{t-r}S(t-r-s)\beta(s)\,ds\right)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}(r)\right\|\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\alpha}\left\|P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}(r)\right\|\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}(r)\right\|_{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\mathbf{F}(s)\right\|+\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\right)t^{1-\alpha}.

Similarly, for
Q_{2}
we have

\left\|Q_{2}\right\|_{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}(s)\right\|_{2}\,ds.

Combining the above two estimates, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|Q_{1}\right\|_{2}+\left\|Q_{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Biggl\{\Biggl(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\mathbf{F}(s)\right\|+\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\Biggr)t^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}(s)\right\|_{2}\,ds\Biggr\}.

Thus, applying the Gronwall’s inequality (
Lemma

2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\mathbf{F}(s)\right\|+\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\right)t^{1-\alpha},

which indicates

\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf H}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3.28)

Step 2.

Let us estimate
\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}
and
\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf H}^{2}\right)}
. Firstly, from
Eqs.

3.12
,
3.9
,
2.5
and
3.7
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\A\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}\right\|+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(s)\,ds\right\|

(3.29)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{v}_{m}(s)\right\|_{2}\,ds,

which plus
Eqs.

3.21
and
3.28
further gives

\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3.30)

Secondly, differentiating equation
Eq.

3.14
with respect to
t
and
applying integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-r)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(r)\,dr+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial S(t-s)}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{s}\beta(s-r)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(r)\,dr\,ds

(3.31)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-r)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(r)\,dr-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial S(t-s)}{\partial s}\int_{0}^{s}\beta(s-r)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(r)\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\beta(s)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{0}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\beta(r)P_{m}\B\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial s}(s-r)\,dr\,ds.

In light of
Eq.

2.5
and the first inequality in
Eq.

2.18
with
k=0
we get

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\beta(s)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{0}\,ds\right\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}.

(3.32)

By
Eqs.

2.14
and
2.1
we find

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\beta(r)P_{m}\B\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial s}(s-r)\,dr\,ds\right\|_{2}

(3.33)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\A S(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}\beta(r)P_{m}\B\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial s}(s-r)\,dr\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle=C\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\A\int_{0}^{t-r}S(t-r-s)\beta(s)\,ds\right)P_{m}\B\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial s}(r)\,dr\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\alpha}\left\|P_{m}\B\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial s}(r)\right\|\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t}(r)\right\|_{2}\,dr

\displaystyle=C\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(1)}_{m}}{\partial t}(r)\right\|_{2}\,dr+\int_{0}^{t}(t-r)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}(r)\right\|_{2}\,dr\right).

Combining
Eqs.

3.24
,
3.31
,
3.32
and
3.33
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}\leq C\biggl(t^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|_{2}\,ds\biggr),

which, together with
Lemma

2.5
, yields

\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf H}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3.34)

Step 3.

Let us estimate
\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\|_{L^{1}({\bf L}^{2})}
. Differentiating equation
Eq.

3.12
with respect to
t
, we have

\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}+\A\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}=\beta(t)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)P_{m}\B\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t}(t-s)\,ds,

(3.35)

which plus
Eq.

3.9
gives

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\A\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|+\left\|\beta(t)P_{m}\B\mathbf{v}_{m}(0)\right\|+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)P_{m}\B\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t}(t-s)\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}+t^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|_{2}\,ds\right).

Hence, integrating the above inequality from
0
to
T
indicates

\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}^{(2)}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\bf L}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3.36)

Step 4.

Finally, combining
Eqs.

3.28
,
3.30
,
3.34
and
3.36
implies the desired estimate
Eq.

3.27
.

∎

In light of
Lemmas

3.3
and
3.4
, we immediately arrive at the
following stability result:

Theorem 3.1
.

Under
3.1
there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf H}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf L}^{2})}

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf L}^{2})}\right).

3.2
Existence, uniqueness and regularity of weak solution

Based on
Theorem

3.1
, we follow the standard line of the Galerkin method (cf.
[
18
]
) to show the wellposedness of the weak problem
Eq.

2.8
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Under
3.1
the problem
Eq.

2.8
admits a unique solution
\mathbf{v}(t)
, and there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf H}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf L}^{2})}

(3.38)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\left(\A\mathbf{v}_{0}+\mathbf{F}(0)\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\mathbf{F}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{F}}{\partial t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{F}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}({\bf L}^{2})}\right).

Proof.

Firstly, according to
Eq.

3.37
we know that

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{v}_{m}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{ is bounded in }L^{\infty}\left({\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right),\text{ and }

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{ is bounded in }L^{\infty}\left({\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)\right).

Thus, there exists a subsequence of
\left\{\mathbf{v}_{m}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, still denoted by
\left\{\mathbf{v}_{m}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
for simplicity, and a function
\mathbf{v}\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)
with
\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)\right)
, such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{v}_{m_{l}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mathbf{v}&\text{in }L^{\infty}\left([0,T];{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right),\\
\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m_{l}}}{\partial t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}&\text{in }L^{\infty}\left([0,T];{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)\right),\end{array}\right.

(3.39)

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}
denotes the weak- * convergence.

Next, we demonstrate that
\mathbf{v}
is a solution of
Eq.

2.8
. Fix an integer
N
, and select a function
\mathbf{w}\in C^{1}\left({\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)
of the form

\mathbf{w}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tilde{d}_{k}(t)\bm{\varphi}_{k},

(3.40)

where
\tilde{d}_{k}(t)\in C^{1}([0,T])
are given functions. Then from
Eq.

3.6

we have

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{m}}{\partial t},\mathbf{w}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v}_{m},\mathbf{w})\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\mathbf{v}_{m}(s),\mathbf{w})\,ds\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{F},\mathbf{w}\rangle\,dt.

(3.41)

Take the limit as
m\rightarrow\infty
to obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t},\mathbf{w}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w})\,ds\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{F},\mathbf{w}\rangle\,dt.

(3.42)

Note that the above equality holds for all
\mathbf{w}\in L^{1}\left([0,T];{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)
, since functions of the form given in
Eq.

3.40
are dense in
L^{1}\left([0,T];{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)
. Hence, we see that
\mathbf{v}
satisfies the first equation of
Eq.

2.8
.

Furthermore, we need to show that
\mathbf{v}
satisfies the initial condition. Notice that
Eqs.

3.42
and
3.41
hold for any
\mathbf{w}\in C^{1}\left([0,T];{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)
with
\mathbf{w}(T)=0
, then apply integration by parts to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\langle\mathbf{v},\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\,\right)dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w})\,ds\,dt

(3.43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{F},\mathbf{w}\rangle\,dt-\langle\mathbf{v}(0),\mathbf{w}(0)\rangle

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\langle\mathbf{v}_{m},\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v}_{m},\mathbf{w})\,\right)dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\mathbf{v}_{m}(s),\mathbf{w})\,ds\,dt

(3.44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{F},\mathbf{w}\rangle\,dt-\langle\mathbf{v}_{m}(0),\mathbf{w}(0)\rangle.

Letting
m\rightarrow\infty
in
Eq.

3.44
and using
Eq.

3.39
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{v},\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w})\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbf{F},\mathbf{w}\rangle\,dt-\langle\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{w}(0)\rangle.

which plus
Eq.

3.43
yields
\mathbf{v}(0)=\mathbf{v}_{0}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{v}
is the solution of weak problem
Eq.

2.8
.

By
Theorem

3.1
, taking
m\to\infty
in
Eq.

3.37
gives the regularity result
Eq.

3.38
.

The uniqueness of the weak solution follows from
Eq.

3.38
directly, since
\mathbf{F}=0
and
\mathbf{v}_{0}=0
imply
\mathbf{v}=0
.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

Note that higher regularity of the solution of the weak problem
Eq.

2.8
can be obtained similarly under higher regularity assumptions of the initial data
\mathbf{v}_{0}
and the right term
\mathbf{F}
.

4
Semi-discrete finite element method

In this section, we consider the semi-discrete finite element discretization of the weak problem
Eq.

2.8
.

4.1
Semi-discrete scheme

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\bigcup\{K\}
be a shape regular partition of
\Omega
consisting of triangles/tetrahedrons or rectangles/cuboids. For any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
h_{K}
denote its diameter, and set
h:=\mathop{\mathrm{max}}_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}
.

Let
V_{h}\subset{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be a linear/bilinear/trilinear finite element space satisfying

\inf_{\bm{\chi}\in V_{h}}\left\{\left\|\mathbf{w}-\bm{\chi}\right\|+h\left\|\mathbf{w}-\bm{\chi}\right\|_{1}\right\}\leq Ch^{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2},\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{w}\in{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(4.1)

Furthermore, the following inverse inequality holds: for any
\mathbf{w}_{h}\in V_{h}
, there holds

\|\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{1}\leq Ch^{-1}\|\mathbf{w}_{h}\|.

(4.2)

Then the semi-discrete finite element scheme reads: For
t\in(0,T]
, find
\mathbf{v}_{h}(t)\in V_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{h}}{\partial t},\mathbf{w}_{h}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\mathbf{v}_{h}(s),\mathbf{w}_{h})\,ds=\langle\mathbf{F},\mathbf{w}_{h}\rangle,\ \forall\ \mathbf{w}_{h}\in V_{h},\\
\mathbf{v}_{h}(0)=\mathbf{v}_{0,h},\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

where
\mathbf{v}_{0,h}=\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}_{0}\in V_{h}
with
\Pi_{h}
being the Ritz-Volterra projection operator defined in
Eq.

4.6
. Note that there holds (cf.
Lemma

4.2
)

\left\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|\leq Ch^{2}\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}.

(4.4)

In light of
Lemma

2.4
we easily obtain the following conclusion:

Lemma 4.1
.

Under Assumption
3.1
the semi-discrete scheme
Eq.

4.3
admits a unique solution.

4.2
Ritz-Volterra projection

To carry out the semi-discrete error estimation, we introduce the standard Ritz projection
R_{h}
and the Ritz-Volterra projection
\Pi_{h}
(cf.
[
10
]
) as follows: for
t\in(0,T]
and
\mathbf{w}(t)\in{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

a(R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\bm{\chi})=0,\qquad\ \forall\ \bm{\chi}\in V_{h},

(4.5)

a((\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(t),\bm{\chi})=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b((\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s),\bm{\chi})\,ds,\ \forall\ \bm{\chi}\in V_{h}.

(4.6)

For the Ritz projection
R_{h}
, under the assumption
Eq.

4.1
there holds (cf.
[
14
]
)

\|R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|+h\|R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{1}\leq Ch^{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2},\quad\forall\ \mathbf{w}\in{\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(4.7)

Based on the estimate
Eq.

4.7
,
Lemmas

4.2
and
4.3
below give error estimates for the Ritz-Volterra projection
\Pi_{h}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\mathbf{w}\in C^{0}({\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
there holds

\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+h\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{1})}\leq Ch^{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})}.

(4.8)

Proof.

From
Eqs.

2.10
,
4.5
,
4.6
and
2.5
it follows that

\displaystyle C\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w},\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})=a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b((\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s),\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w}\|_{1}\cdot\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s)\|_{1}\,ds.

Hence, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w}\|_{1}+\|R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{1}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s)\|_{1}\,ds.

By virtue of
Lemmas

2.5
and
4.7
we further obtain

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{1}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|(R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s)\|_{1}\,ds

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|(R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\mathbf{w}(s)\|_{2}.

Now we turn to estimate
\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|
by a duality argument. Let
\bm{\phi}
be the solution of the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \A\bm{\phi}&=\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\bm{\phi}&=0\qquad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.10)

From
Eq.

2.14
we see that

\|\bm{\phi}\|_{2}\leq C\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|.

(4.11)

In view of
Eqs.

4.10
and
4.6
we have

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\A\bm{\phi}\rangle=a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\bm{\phi})

(4.12)

\displaystyle=a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\bm{\phi}-R_{h}\bm{\phi})+a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},R_{h}\bm{\phi})

\displaystyle=a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\bm{\phi}-R_{h}\bm{\phi})+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b((\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s),R_{h}\bm{\phi})\,ds

\displaystyle=a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\bm{\phi}-R_{h}\bm{\phi})+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b((\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s),\bm{\phi})\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b((\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s),R_{h}\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi})\,ds

\displaystyle=a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\bm{\phi}-R_{h}\bm{\phi})-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)\langle(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s),\B\bm{\phi}\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b((\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s),R_{h}\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi})\,ds,

which, together with
Eqs.

4.7
and
4.9
, gives

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|_{1}\cdot\|\bm{\phi}-R_{h}\bm{\phi}\|_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s)\|\,ds\cdot\|\bm{\phi}\|_{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s)\|_{1}\,ds\cdot\|R_{h}\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi}\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{h^{2}\|\bm{\phi}\|_{2}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\mathbf{w}(s)\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(s)\|\,ds\right\}.

Finally, using
Eqs.

4.11
and
2.5

we immediately obtain

\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}\|\leq Ch^{2}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\mathbf{w}(s)\|_{2}\leq Ch^{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})},

which plus
Eq.

4.9
leads to the desired estimate
Eq.

4.8
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\mathbf{w}\in C^{1}({\bf H}^{2}(\Omega)\cap{\bf H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|+h\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}\right)\,ds\leq Ch^{2}\left(\left\|\mathbf{w}(0)\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}\,ds\right).

(4.14)

Proof.

Rewrite
Eq.

4.6
as

a(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w},\bm{\chi})=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)b((\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})(t-s),\bm{\chi})\,ds,\qquad\forall\ \bm{\chi}\in V_{h}.

(4.15)

Taking the derivative with respect to
t
for the above equation, we obtain

a(\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}(t),\bm{\chi})=\beta(t)b(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}(0)-\mathbf{w}(0),\bm{\chi})+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)b(\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}(t-s),\bm{\chi})\,ds.

Thus, by
Eqs.

2.10
,
4.5
and
2.5
we have

\displaystyle C\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a(\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t},\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t})=a(\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t},\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(t)b(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}(0)-\mathbf{w}(0),\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}(s),\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t})\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(t^{-\alpha}\|\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}(0)-\mathbf{w}(0)\|_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}(s)\right\|_{1}\,ds\right)\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(t^{-\alpha}h\left\|\mathbf{w}(0)\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}(s)\right\|_{1}\,ds\right)\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}.

As a result, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-R_{h}\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{\partial(R_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{h\left(t^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{w}(0)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}(s)\right\|_{1}\,ds\right\},

which plus
Lemma

2.5
yields

\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}\leq Ch\left\{t^{-\alpha}\left\|\mathbf{w}(0)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|_{2}\,ds\right\}.

(4.16)

Integrating the above inequality with respect to
t
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}\right\|_{1}\,ds

(4.17)

\displaystyle\leq Ch\left\{\left\|\mathbf{w}(0)\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)^{-\alpha}\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}(r)\|_{2}\,dr\,ds\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\ Ch\left\{\left\|\mathbf{w}(0)\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}\,ds\right\}.

By following a similar routine of dual arguments as in the proof of
Lemma

4.2
, we can deduce the
L_{2}
estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(h\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w})}{\partial t}(s)\right\|_{1}\,ds+h^{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}(0)\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ Ch^{2}\left\{\left\|\mathbf{w}(0)\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}\,ds\right\}.

This inequality, together with
Eq.

4.17
, gives the desired estimate
Eq.

4.14
.
∎

4.3
Error estimation

By virtue of
Lemmas

4.2
and
4.3
, we easily obtain the following error estimate for the semi-discrete finite element method:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{v}_{h}
be the solutions of the weak problem
Eq.

2.8
and the semi-discrete scheme
Eq.

4.3
, respectively. Then

\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}+h\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{L^{1}({\bf H}^{1})}\leq Ch^{2}\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}\,ds\right).

(4.18)

Proof.

Denote
\bm{\delta}:=\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{h}
, then

\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{h}=\mathbf{v}-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}+\bm{\delta}.

(4.19)

Owing to
Lemma

4.2
, we only need to estimate
\bm{\delta}
. From
Eq.

2.8
,
Eq.

4.3
and
Eq.

4.6
it follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\|\bm{\delta}\|^{2}}{dt}+a(\bm{\delta},\bm{\delta})=\langle\frac{\partial\bm{\delta}}{\partial t},\bm{\delta}\rangle+a(\bm{\delta},\bm{\delta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\bm{\delta}(s),\bm{\delta})\,ds+\langle\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})}{\partial t},\bm{\delta}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\|\bm{\delta}(s)\|_{1}\cdot\|\bm{\delta}(t)\|_{1}\,ds+\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})}{\partial t}\right\|\cdot\|\bm{\delta}\|.

Integrating the above inequality with respect to
t
from
0
to
r\in(0,T]
and using
Lemma

2.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\bm{\delta}(r)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{r}\|\bm{\delta}(t)\|_{1}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\bm{\delta}(0)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{r}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})(t)}{\partial t}\right\|\cdot\|\bm{\delta}(t)\|\,dt+\int_{0}^{r}(r-t)^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|\bm{\delta}(s)\|_{1}^{2}\,ds\,dt\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\bm{\delta}(0)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{r}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})(t)}{\partial t}\right\|\cdot\|\bm{\delta}(t)\|\,dt+\int_{0}^{r}(r-t)^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{r}\|\bm{\delta}(s)\|_{1}^{2}\,ds\,dt\right\}.

Thus, applying
Lemma

2.5
we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\delta}(r)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{r}\|\bm{\delta}(t)\|_{1}^{2}\,dt\leq C\left\{\|\bm{\delta}(0)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{r}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})(t)}{\partial t}\right\|\cdot\|\bm{\delta}(t)\|\,dt\right\}

for any
r\in(0,T]
,
which further leads to

\|\bm{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf L}^{2})}\leq C\left\{\|\bm{\delta}(0)\|+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})}{\partial t}\right\|\,dt\right\}.

As a result, the desired estimate
Eq.

4.18
follows from
Eqs.

4.19
,
4.4
,
4.2
and
4.3
.

∎

5
Fast fully discrete finite element method

5.1
Fully discrete scheme

Let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{\mathrm{N}}=T
be a uniform division of the time interval
[0,T]
, with
t_{i}=i\Delta t\ (i=0,1,...,N)
and the time step size
\Delta t:=\frac{T}{\mathrm{N}}
. For any function
\varphi(t)
, we set

\displaystyle\varphi^{n}:=\varphi(t_{n}).

For the time derivative
\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{h}}{\partial t}
in the semi-discrete scheme
Eq.

4.3
, we apply the backward Euler difference scheme to approximate it, i.e.

\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{h}}{\partial t}\approx\frac{\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}.

Noticing in
Eq.

4.3
the term

\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)b(\mathbf{v}_{h}(s),\mathbf{w}_{h})\,ds=b(\int_{0}^{t}\beta(t-s)\mathbf{v}_{h}(s)\,ds,\mathbf{w}_{h}),

we adopt
the SOE approximation
Section

2.4
for
\beta(t)=E_{\alpha}(-t^{\alpha})
and evaluate the time integral term as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\beta(t_{n}-s)\mathbf{v}_{h}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}(t_{n}-s)}\mathbf{v}_{h}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}\left(e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}\Delta t}\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}(t_{n-1}-s)}\mathbf{v}_{h}(s)\,ds+\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}(t_{n}-s)}\mathbf{v}_{h}(s)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}\Delta t}\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}(t_{n-1}-s)}\mathbf{v}_{h}(s)\,ds+\frac{b_{j}\tau_{\sigma}}{a_{j}}(1-e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}\Delta t})\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1}.

Define the memory variables
H_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n})
recursively by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &H_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n})=e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}\Delta t}H_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1})+\frac{b_{j}\tau_{\sigma}}{a_{j}}(1-e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}\Delta t})\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1},\quad n=1,2,\cdots,N,\\
&H_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{0})=\mathbf{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

then we obtain
the following fast fully discrete scheme: For each
n=1,...,N
, find
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}\in V_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\langle\frac{\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\mathbf{w}_{h}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n},\mathbf{w}_{h})-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b(H_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}),\mathbf{w}_{h})=\langle\mathbf{F}^{n},\mathbf{w}_{h}\rangle,\ \forall\ \mathbf{w}_{h}\in V_{h},\\
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{0}=\mathbf{v}_{0,h}.\end{array}\right.

(5.2)

By
Eq.

5.1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b(H_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}),\mathbf{w}_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}\Delta t}b(H_{j}(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1}),\mathbf{w}_{h})+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}\frac{b_{j}\tau_{\sigma}}{a_{j}}(1-e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}\Delta t})b(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1},\mathbf{w}_{h})

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}={}}\vdots\quad\text{(repeat the recursion $n-2$ times)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}b(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{i},\mathbf{w}_{h})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}b(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{i},\mathbf{w}_{h}),

where

\theta_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}\frac{b_{j}\tau_{\sigma}}{a_{j}}(e^{-\frac{(i-1)\Delta ta_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}}-e^{-\frac{i\Delta ta_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}}),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,n.

(5.3)

Therefore, the scheme
Eq.

5.2
can be rewritten as follows: For each
n=1,...,N
, find
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}\in V_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\langle\frac{\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\mathbf{w}_{h}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n},\mathbf{w}_{h})-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}b(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{i},\mathbf{w}_{h})=\langle\mathbf{F}^{n},\mathbf{w}_{h}\rangle,\ \forall\ \mathbf{w}_{h}\in V_{h},\\
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{0}=\mathbf{v}_{0,h}.\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

It is easy to see that the above scheme has a unique solution for each
n
. As a result, we have the following result:

Lemma 5.1
.

The fully discrete scheme
Eq.

5.2
admits a unique solution for
1\leq n\leq N
.

Remark 5.1
.

In the full discretization scheme
Eq.

5.2
, at the
n
-th time step we need to compute

\frac{b_{j}\tau_{\sigma}}{a_{j}}(1-e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}\Delta t})b(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1},\mathbf{w}_{h})

and store
H_{j}^{n}
for
j=1,2,...,N_{exp}
. This means this scheme totally requires

O(N_{exp}N_{s})
memory complexity and
O(N_{s}N_{exp}N)
computation complexity. Here and below we denote by
\mathcal{O}(N_{s})
the complexity of memory and computation related to the spatial discretization.

Remark 5.2
.

If, rather than handling the convolution kernel function
\beta(t-s)
with the SOE approximation, we directly approximate the integral term
\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\beta(t_{n}-s)b(\mathbf{v}_{h}(s),\mathbf{w}_{h})\,ds
by the quadrature rule

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\beta(t_{n}-s)b(\mathbf{v}_{h}(s),\mathbf{w}_{h})\,ds\approx\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{i},\mathbf{w}_{h})\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\beta(t_{n}-s)\,ds,

then the resulting fully discrete scheme reads: For
1\leq n\leq N
,
find
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}\in V_{h}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\langle\frac{\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\mathbf{w}_{h}\rangle+a(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n},\mathbf{w}_{h})-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}b(\mathbf{v}_{h}^{i},\mathbf{w}_{h})\displaystyle\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\beta(t_{n}-s)\,ds=\langle\mathbf{F}^{n},\mathbf{w}_{h}\rangle,\\
\phantom{CCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCC}\forall\ \mathbf{w}_{h}\in V_{h},\\
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{0}=\mathbf{v}_{0,h}.\end{array}\right.

(5.5)

At the
n
-th time step, this scheme always requires evaluating the integrals
\displaystyle\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\beta(t_{n}-s)\,ds
, which involves
n
computations. Consequently, this scheme totally demands
\mathcal{O}(N_{s}N)
memory complexity and
O(N_{s}N^{2})
computation complexity.

Remark 5.3
.

Due to the fact
N>>N_{exp}
(cf.
Remark

2.2
), the fully discrete scheme
Eq.

5.2
reduces the costs of memory and computation from
O(N_{s}N)
and
O(N_{s}N^{2})
to
O(N_{exp}N_{s})
and
O(N_{s}N_{exp}N)
, respectively. Therefore, the fully discrete scheme
Eq.

5.2
is actually a fast scheme.

5.2
Error estimation

We first assume that the SOE approximation is of accuracy
\epsilon>0
, i.e.

\beta(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}t}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),

(5.6)

and
define

\varXi_{n}(\mathbf{v}):=\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\beta(t_{n}-s)\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}\mathbf{v}(t_{i})

(5.7)

for the solution
\mathbf{v}(t)
of the weak problem
Eq.

2.8
.
Then we have the following result:

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C
, depending only on
T
, such that for
1\leq n\leq N
,

\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\varXi_{k}(\mathbf{v})\|_{l}\leq C\left(\Delta t\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|_{l}\,ds+T\epsilon\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|_{l}\,ds\right),\quad l=0,1.

(5.8)

Proof.

We only show the case
l=0
, since the proof for
l=1
is similar.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\beta(t_{n}-s)\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b_{j}e^{-a_{j}(\frac{t_{n}-s}{\tau_{\sigma}})}\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b_{j}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}e^{-a_{j}(\frac{t_{n}-s}{\tau_{\sigma}})}\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}(n-i-1)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}e^{-a_{j}(\frac{t_{i+1}-s}{\tau_{\sigma}})}\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds.

Recalling the definition
Eq.

5.3
of
\theta_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\varXi_{n}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\beta(t_{n}-\tau)\mathbf{v}(s)\,ds-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}\mathbf{v}(t_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\exp}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}(n-i-1)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}e^{-a_{j}\left(\frac{t_{i+1}-s}{\tau_{\sigma}}\right)}\left(\mathbf{v}(s)-\mathbf{v}(t_{i})\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\phantom{=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\exp}}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left(\mathbf{v}(s)-\mathbf{v}(t_{i})\right)\,ds\Bigg).

Therefore, it holds

\displaystyle\left|\varXi_{n}(\mathbf{v})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\exp}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}(n-i-1)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}e^{-a_{j}\left(\frac{t_{i+1}-s}{\tau_{\sigma}}\right)}\,ds\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}(r)\right|\,dr

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\exp}}b_{j}e^{-\frac{a_{j}(n-i-1)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\,ds\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}(r)\right|\,dr\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}(s)\right|\,ds+\Delta t\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}(s)\right|\,ds,

which yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\varXi_{k}(\mathbf{v})\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\theta_{k-i}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\,ds+T\epsilon\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\,ds\sum_{k=i+1}^{n}\theta_{k-i}+T\epsilon\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}(s)\right\|\,ds\right).

Since
a_{j}
and
b_{j}
in
Section

2.4
are positive, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i+1}^{n}\theta_{k-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i+1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}\frac{b_{j}\tau_{\sigma}}{a_{j}}\left(e^{-\frac{a_{j}(k-i-1)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}-e^{-\frac{a_{j}(k-i)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}\frac{b_{j}\tau_{\sigma}}{a_{j}}\sum_{k=i+1}^{n}\left(e^{-\frac{a_{j}(k-i-1)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}-e^{-\frac{a_{j}(k-i)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}\frac{b_{j}\tau_{\sigma}}{a_{j}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{a_{j}(n-i)\Delta t}{\tau_{\sigma}}}\right).

As a result, we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\varXi_{k}(\mathbf{v})\|\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|\,ds+T\epsilon\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|\,ds\right),

i.e. the estimate
Eq.

5.8
holds.
∎

By using the above lemma, we can obtain the following error estimate for the fast fully discrete finite element method:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}

(1\leq n\leq N)
be the solutions of the weak problem
Eq.

2.8
and the fully discrete scheme
Eq.

5.2
, respectively. Then for
n=1,2,\cdots,N
there holds

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}-\mathbf{v}(t_{n})\|

\displaystyle\leq C(h^{2}+\Delta t)\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\bf H}^{2})}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left(\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|\right)\,ds\right)

(5.9)

\displaystyle+CT\epsilon\left(\left\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\right\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}\right\|_{2}\,ds\right).

Proof.

Note that the fully discrete scheme
Eq.

5.2
is equivalent to
Eq.

5.4
.

Denote

\bm{\delta}^{n}:=\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}^{n}-\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n},\quad n=0,1,\cdots,N,

where
\Pi_{h}
is the Ritz-Volterra projection defined by
Eq.

4.6
. Since
\mathbf{v}_{h}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}^{0}=\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}(0)
, we see that

\displaystyle\bm{\delta}^{0}=0.

(5.10)

We write

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{n}-\mathbf{v}_{h}^{n}=\mathbf{v}^{n}-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}^{n}+\bm{\delta}^{n}.

(5.11)

In view of
Lemma

4.2
, we only need to estimate
\bm{\delta}^{n}
.

From
Eqs.

2.8
,
5.4
and
4.6
, we get

\langle\frac{\bm{\delta}^{n}-\bm{\delta}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\bm{\delta}^{n}\rangle+a(\bm{\delta}^{n},\bm{\delta}^{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}b(\bm{\delta}^{i},\bm{\delta}^{n})+R^{n}(\bm{\delta}^{n}),

(5.12)

where

\displaystyle R^{n}(\bm{\delta}^{n}):=-

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}^{n}-\frac{\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}^{n}-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\bm{\delta}^{n}\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\beta(t_{n}-s)b(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}(s),\bm{\delta}^{n})\,ds-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}b(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}^{i},\bm{\delta}^{n}).

Notice that by
Eq.

5.7
we further have

\displaystyle R^{n}(\bm{\delta}^{n})=-

\displaystyle\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}^{n}-\frac{\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}^{n}-\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\bm{\delta}^{n}\rangle+b(\varXi_{n}(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}),\bm{\delta}^{n}).

(5.13)

From
Eqs.

2.10
,
5.12
and
2.11
it follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|^{2}-\|\bm{\delta}^{n-1}\|^{2}}{\Delta t}+\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|^{2}-\|\bm{\delta}^{n-1}\|^{2}}{\Delta t}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\|\frac{\bm{\delta}^{n}-\bm{\delta}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\|^{2}+a(\bm{\delta}^{n},\bm{\delta}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\langle\frac{\bm{\delta}^{n}-\bm{\delta}^{n-1}}{\Delta t},\bm{\delta}^{n}\rangle+a(\bm{\delta}^{n},\bm{\delta}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\theta_{n-i}\|\bm{\delta}^{i}\|_{1}\cdot\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|_{1}+|R^{n}(\bm{\delta}^{n})|\right).

Summing this inequality from
n=1
to
n
and using
Eq.

5.10
, we obtain

\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|_{1}^{2}\leq C\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{k}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|+C\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\theta_{k-i}\|\bm{\delta}^{i}\|_{1}\cdot\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|_{1}.

(5.14)

By the definition
Eq.

5.3
of
\theta_{i}
and the SOE approximation
Eq.

5.6
, we see that

\theta_{k-i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{exp}}b_{j}\int_{t_{k-i-1}}^{t_{k-i}}e^{-\frac{a_{j}}{\tau_{\sigma}}(t_{k}-s)}\,ds\leq\int_{t_{k-i-1}}^{t_{k-i}}\beta(t_{k}-s)\,ds+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\Delta t,

which, together with
Eqs.

5.14
and
2.8
, gives

\displaystyle\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{k}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|+C\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\int_{t_{k-i-1}}^{t_{k-i}}\left(\beta(t_{k}-s)+\epsilon\right)\,ds\|\bm{\delta}^{i}\|_{1}\cdot\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{k}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|_{1}^{2}+C\Delta t\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{n-k-1}}^{t_{n-k}}\left(\beta(t_{n}-s)+\epsilon\right)\,ds\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|\bm{\delta}^{i}\|_{1}^{2}.

Thus, due to
Eqs.

5.14
,
2.8
and
5.10
we further have

\displaystyle\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{n}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|+C\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{n-k-1}}^{t_{n-k}}\left(\beta(t_{n}-s)+\epsilon\right)\,ds\left(\Delta t\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\bm{\delta}^{i}\|_{1}^{2}\right),

which plus
Lemmas

2.6
and
2.5
, yields

\displaystyle\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|_{1}^{2}\leq C\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{k}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|.

(5.15)

In the following let us estimate

|R^{k}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|
. To this end,
we write

R^{k}(\bm{\delta}^{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{4}R^{k}_{j}(\bm{\delta}^{k}),

where

\displaystyle R^{k}_{1}(\bm{\delta}^{k}):=\langle-\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}^{k}+\frac{\mathbf{v}^{k}-\mathbf{v}^{k-1}}{\Delta t},\bm{\delta}^{k}\rangle,

\displaystyle R^{k}_{2}(\bm{\delta}^{k}):=\langle\frac{(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})^{k}-(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})^{k-1}}{\Delta t},\bm{\delta}^{k}\rangle,

\displaystyle R^{k}_{3}(\bm{\delta}^{k}):=b(\varXi_{k}(\mathbf{v}),\bm{\delta}^{k}),

\displaystyle R^{k}_{4}(\bm{\delta}^{k}):=b(\varXi_{k}(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}),\bm{\delta}^{k}).

For
R^{k}_{1}(\bm{\delta}^{k})
we have

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{k}_{1}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\langle-\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}^{k}+\frac{\mathbf{v}^{k}-\mathbf{v}^{k-1}}{\Delta t},\bm{\delta}^{k}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\langle\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{k}-s)\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}(s)}{\partial t^{2}}\ ds,\bm{\delta}^{k}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|\,ds\cdot\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|.

For
R^{k}_{2}(\bm{\delta}^{k})
we easily get

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{k}_{2}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\langle(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})^{k}-(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})^{k-1},\bm{\delta}^{k}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\langle\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})(s)}{\partial t}\,ds,\bm{\delta}^{k}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})(s)}{\partial t}\right\|\,ds\cdot\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|.

For
R^{k}_{3}(\bm{\delta}^{k})
, by integration by parts and
Eq.

3.3

we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{k}_{3}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|b(\varXi_{n}(\mathbf{v}),\bm{\delta}^{k})\right|=\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\langle\varXi_{k}(\B\mathbf{v}),\bm{\delta}^{k}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\varXi_{k}(\B\mathbf{v})\|\cdot\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|.

For
R^{k}_{4}(\bm{\delta}^{k})
, from
Eq.

2.11
and the inverse inequality
Eq.

4.2
it follows

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|R^{k}_{4}(\bm{\delta}^{k})|

\displaystyle=\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}|b(\varXi_{k}(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}),\bm{\delta}^{k})|

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\varXi_{k}(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})\|_{1}\cdot\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\varXi_{k}(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})\|_{1}\cdot\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|.

Combining the above four estimates and
Eq.

5.15

implies

\displaystyle\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|^{2}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{R}_{k}\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|\leq CS_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{R}_{k},

(5.16)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{R}_{k}:=&\Delta t\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|\,ds+\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})}{\partial t}\right\|\,ds\\
&\quad+\Delta t\|\varXi_{k}(\B\mathbf{v})\|+Ch^{-1}\Delta t\|\varXi_{k}(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})\|_{1},\\
S_{n}:=&\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\|\bm{\delta}^{k}\|.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{\delta}^{n}\|\leq

\displaystyle S_{n}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{R}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\Delta t\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{v}}{\partial t^{2}}\right\|\,ds+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left\|\frac{\partial(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})}{\partial t}\right\|\,ds\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\varXi_{k}(\B\mathbf{v})\|+Ch^{-1}\Delta t\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\varXi_{k}(\Pi_{h}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})\|_{1}\right).

This estimate, together with

Eqs.

4.4
,
4.2
,
4.3
and
5.2
, yields
the desired result
Eq.

5.9
.

∎

7
Conclusions

We have shown the existence, uniqueness and regularity of the weak solution to the velocity type integro-differential equation of fractional viscoelastic wave propagation. For the spatial semi-discretization using low order conforming finite elements and the fast full discretization using the backward-Euler scheme and the SOE approximation for the temporal discretization, we have derived rigorous error estimates.
Numerical results have confirmed the theoretical analysis and demonstrated the efficiency of the developed fast scheme.

Skew brace extensions, second cohomology and complements
Nishant Rathee, Manoj K. Yadav

Abstract
. We study extensions and the second cohomology groups of skew left braces via the associated natural semi-direct product groups. Let 0 → I → E → H → 0 0\to I\to E\to H\to 0 be a skew brace extension and Λ H \Lambda_{H} denote the natural semi-direct product associated with the skew left brace H H . We establish a group homomorphism from H S ​ b 2 ⁡ ( H , I ) \operatorname{H}_{Sb}^{2}(H,I) , the second cohomology group of braces, into H G ​ p 2 ⁡ ( Λ H , I × I ) \operatorname{H}_{Gp}^{2}(\Lambda_{H},I\times I) , the second cohomology group of groups, which turns out to be an embedding when I ≤ Soc ⁡ ( E ) I\leq\operatorname{Soc}(E) , the socle of E E . In particular, the Schur multiplier of a skew left brace H H embeds into the Schur multiplier of the group Λ H \Lambda_{H} . Analog of the Schur-Zassenhaus theorem is established for skew left braces in several specific cases. We introduce a concept called minimal extensions (which stay at the extreme end of split extensions) of skew left braces and derive many fundamental results. Several reduction results for split extensions of finite skew left braces by abelian groups (viewed as trivial left braces) are obtained.

1.
Introduction

Cohomology of left braces and linear cycle sets with trivial action on an abelian group was introduced by Labed and Vendramin
[
15
]
. This was extended to the case when the action is non-trivial by Bardakov and Singh
[
6
]
. The concept of the (first and second) cohomology of skew left braces, originated from the article
[
1
]
by Bachiller, was formalised by the authors of the present article in
[
20
]
. Connection with extension theory was developed. The aim of this article is to further advance this knowledge by exploiting the connection of skew left brace with the natural semi-direct product of the multiplicative group of the skew left brace by that of its additive group. Several foundational results on minimal extensions of skew left braces and Frattini sub-skew braces are produced. Some reduction techniques for an exact sequence of skew left braces by abelian
p
-groups to split are obtained.

A skew left brace is a set
A
equipped with two group structures,
(A,+)
and
(A,\circ)
satisfying the compatibility condition

a\circ(b+c)=(a\circ b)-a+(a\circ c),

for all
a,b,c\in A
, where
-a
denotes the inverse of the element
a
in the group
(A,+)
. A skew left brace
A
is denoted by
(A,+,\circ)
, mostly when the binary operations are emphasized. The groups
(A,+)
and
(A,\circ)
, respectively, termed as the additive group and multiplicative group of the skew left brace
A
.

Given a skew left brace
(A,+,\circ)

on
(A,+)
by automorphisms given by

\lambda:(A,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(A,+),\quad\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+(a\circ b),

which is known as the lambda map associated with skew left brace

. This action allows one to construct the associated semi-direct product group

\Lambda_{A}=(A,+)\rtimes_{\lambda}(A,\circ).

Let
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{Sb}(H,I)
denote the second brace cohomology group of a skew left brace
H
with coefficients in an abelian group

and
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{Gp}(\Lambda_{H},I\times I)
denote the second group cohomology group of the associated group

with coefficients in the abelian group

.
Our first result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Let a skew left brace
H
act on an abelian group via an action

. There exists a group homomorphism
\Psi:\operatorname{H}^{2}_{Sb}(H,I)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}_{Gp}(\Lambda_{H},I\times I)
. Moreover, if

\nu^{-1}_{h}=\sigma_{h}
for all

, then

As a quick application of this theorem we get
[
16

which states that the exponent of the Schur multiplier of a finite skew left brace

divides
|H|^{2}
.

Our next result is about the connection between the second cohomology group of a skew left brace

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

I

\operatorname{H}^{2}_{Sb}(H,I)\cong\operatorname{H}^{2}_{Sb}(H^{\operatorname{op}},I)

An ideal

of a finite skew left brace

is said to be a Hall ideal if the order of

is coprime to the order of the quotient skew left brace
H/I
. We prove a brace analogous of the group theory result

in several specific cases (Theorem

, (ii)

and

is the direct sum of the Frattini sub-skew braces of

In the last section we prove a reduction result for split brace extensions.

Let

2.
Preliminaries

A
skew left brace
is a set
A
equipped with two group structures,
(A,+)
and
(A,\circ)
satisfying the compatibility condition

a\circ(b+c)=(a\circ b)-a+(a\circ c),

for all
a,b,c\in A
, where
-a
denotes the inverse of the element
a
in the group
(A,+)
. A skew left brace
A
is denoted by
(A,+,\circ)
, mostly when the binary operations are emphasized. The groups
(A,+)
and
(A,\circ)
, respectively, termed as the additive group and multiplicative group of the skew left brace
A
. A
right skew brace
is defined analogously, demanding the ‘skew-distributivity’ from the right. A skew left brace which is also a skew right brace is a called a
two-sided skew brace
. A skew left brace is said to be a
left brace
if
(A,+)
is an abelian group. We say that a skew left brace is
trivial
if ‘
+
’ and ‘
\circ
’ coincide on
A
. So a trivial skew left brace
(A,+,\circ)
is just the group
(A,+)
and a trivial left brace
(A,+,\circ)
is just the abelian group
(A,+)
. As we always work with skew left braces, for simplicity, we shall suppress the use of the word ‘left’, and just call skew brace. Likewise, a left braces will be called a brace. A ‘trivial brace’ and a ‘trivial skew brace of abelian type’ mean the same thing.

For a skew brace
(A,+,\circ)
, it was proved in
[
12
]
that the map

\lambda:(A,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}\,(A,+),~~a\mapsto\lambda_{a}

is a group homomorphism, where
\operatorname{Aut}\,(A,+)
denotes the automorphism group of
(A,+)
and
\lambda_{a}
is given by
\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+(a\circ b)
for all
a,b\in A
.
For two elements
a,b\in A
,
-b+a+b
denotes the conjugate of
a
by
b
in
(A,+)
and
b^{-1}\circ a\circ b
denotes the conjugate of
a
by
b
in
(A,\circ)
.

A subset
B
of a skew brace
A=(A,+,\circ)
is said to be a
sub-skew brace
of
A
if
B
is a subgroup of both the group structures
(A,+)
and
(A,\circ)
. A subset
I
of a skew brace
(A,+,\circ)
is said to be a
left ideal
of
(A,+,\circ)
if
I
is a subgroup of
(A,+)
and
\lambda_{a}(c)\in I
for all
a\in A
and
c\in I
. It turns out that a left ideal
I
of
(A,+,\circ)
is a subgroup of
(A,\circ)
. A left ideal
I
of
A
is said to be a
strong left ideal
if
I
is normal in
(A,+)
. A left ideal
I
is said to be an
ideal
of
(A,+,\circ)
if
I
is normal in both
(A,+)
and
(A,\circ)
. Note that
\operatorname{ker}(\lambda)
is a left ideal of
(A,+,\circ)
.

Let
A:=(A,+,\circ)
be a skew brace. We define
socle
of
A
to be the set

\operatorname{Soc}(A):=\operatorname{ker}(\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Z}(A,+)

and
annihilator
of
A
to be the set

\operatorname{Ann}(A):=\operatorname{ker}(\lambda)\cap\operatorname{Z}(A,+)\cap\operatorname{Z}(A,\circ).

It turns out that both
\operatorname{Soc}(A)
and
\operatorname{Ann}(A)
are ideals of
A
.

The following result is well known.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a brace and let
S
and
I
be, respectively, a sub-skew brace and an ideal of
A
. Then
S\circ I=S+I
is a sub-skew brace of
A
. Moreover, if
S
is a left ideal (an ideal), then
S+I
is also a left ideal (an ideal).

Let
A
be a brace and
B
a sub-skew brace of
A
. We say that
B
is
supplemented
in
E
if there exists a sub-skew brace
C
such that

A=B\circ C=B+C.

Such a
C
is called a supplement of
B
in
E
.
The sub-skew brace
B
is said to be
complemented
in
E
if
B
is supplemented by
C
and

B\cap C=\{0\}
. In this case we say that
C
is a complement of
B
in
E
.
Note that if
A=B+C
, where
B
is a skew brace of
E
and
C
is an ideal of
E
, then
A=C+B=B\circ C=C\circ B
. Indeed,
a=b+c=b\circ\lambda^{-1}_{b}(c)
for all
a,b,c\in A
. Thus, an ideal
I
of
A
is complemented by a skew brace
B
in
A
if and only if
A=I+B
and
I\cap B=\{0\}
.

Let
H
and
I
be two skew braces. By a
brace extension
of
H
by
I
, we mean a skew brace
E
with an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow I\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{\pi}H\longrightarrow 0,

where
i
and
\pi
are injective and surjective skew brace homomorphisms, respectively. We’ll mostly view
I
as an ideal
E
via

. When we do not wish to emphasize the use of the maps

and
\pi
, we say that

is a brace extension of

by
I
without referring to the exact sequence. A set map

is said to be an
st-section
if
s(0)=0
and
s\pi=\operatorname{id}_{H}
. An st-section

is said to be a section if it is a skew brace homomorphism. The preceding extension
E
is said to be a
split extension
if there exists a section
s:H\to E
.

Let us start with a brace extension of a skew brace
H
by a trivial brace
I

0\longrightarrow I\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{\pi}H\longrightarrow 0

with an st-section
s:H\to E
. Then the multiplicative group
(H,\circ)
acts on the additive group
(I,+)
from the left via an action
\nu
defined by

\nu(h)(x)=\lambda_{s(h)}(x)

for all
h\in H
and
x\in I
. The groups
(H,+)
and
(H,\circ)
act on
(I,+)
from the right via actions
\mu
and
\sigma
, defined, respectively,

\mu(h)(x)=-s(h)+x+s(h)

and

\sigma(h)(x)=s(h)^{-1}\circ x\circ s(h)

for all
h\in H
and
x\in I
. The images of
h\in H
in
\operatorname{Aut}(I,+)
, the automorphism group of the additive group
(I,+)
, under the actions
\nu
,
\mu
, and
\sigma
are denoted by
\nu_{h}
,
\mu_{h}
, and
\sigma_{h}
, respectively. The triple of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
satisfy the following compatibility conditions:

\mu_{\lambda_{h_{1}}(h_{3})}(y)=\nu_{h_{1}}(\mu_{h_{3}}(\nu_{h_{1}}^{-1}(y))),

\nu_{h_{2}+h_{3}}\left(\sigma_{h_{2}+h_{3}}(y)\right)+\mu_{h_{3}}(y)=\mu_{h_{3}}\left(\nu_{h_{2}}\left(\sigma_{h_{2}}(y)\right)\right)+\nu_{h_{3}}\left(\sigma_{h_{3}}(y)\right),

for all
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\in H
and
y\in I
. In general, we say that a skew brace
H

acts on an abelian group

, viewed as a trivial brace, via a good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
if
\mu:(H,+)\to\operatorname{Aut}(I)
and
\sigma:(H,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(I)
are group anti-homomorphisms and
\nu:(H,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(I)
is a group homomorphism and satisfy the preceding two compatibility conditions, where
\nu_{h}
,
\mu_{h}
, and
\sigma_{h}
, respectively, are the images of
h\in H
under the actions
\nu
,
\mu
, and
\sigma
.

As explained above
\lambda
is an action of
(A,\circ)
on
(A,+)
. This action allows us to construct the associated semi-direct product group

\Lambda_{A}=(A,+)\rtimes_{\lambda}(A,\circ),

in which product of two elements
(x_{1},y_{1})
and
(x_{2},y_{2})
is given by
(x_{1}+\lambda_{y_{1}}(x_{2}),y_{1}\circ y_{2})
.
It turns out that the association
A\mapsto\Lambda_{A}
is a functor from the category of skew braces to the category of groups. Therefore a skew brace homomorphism between two skew braces
\phi:E\rightarrow H
defines a group homomorphism between the corresponding groups
\Lambda_{E}
and
\Lambda_{H}
, given by

(\phi\times\phi):\Lambda_{E}\rightarrow\Lambda_{H},\quad(\phi\times\phi)(x,y)=(\phi(x),\phi(y))

for all
x,y\in E
. Thus, every brace extension

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow I\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{\pi}H\longrightarrow 0,

induces the following group extension:

(2.2)

0\longrightarrow\Lambda_{I}\xrightarrow{i\times i}\Lambda_{E}\xrightarrow{\pi\times\pi}\Lambda_{H}\longrightarrow 0.

The brace extensions
E
and
E^{\prime}
of a skew brace
H
by a skew brace
I
are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a brace homomorphism
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
such that the following diagram commutes:

\begin{CD}0@>{i}>{}>I@>{}>{}>E@>{{\pi}}>{}>H@>{}>{}>0\\
&&@V{\text{Id}}V{}V@V{\phi}V{}V@ VV{\text{Id}}V\\
0@>{i^{\prime}}>{}>I@>{}>{}>E^{\prime}@>{{\pi^{\prime}}}>{}>H@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

The set of all equivalence classes of extensions of
H
by
I
is denoted by
\operatorname{Ext}(H,I)
. When we want to emphasize the use of an action
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
of
H
on
I
, then we denote
\operatorname{Ext}(H,I)
by
\operatorname{Ext}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}(H,I)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
E
be a skew brace extension of
H
by
I
. Then the following hold true:

(i)

If
E
is a split extension, then so is
\Lambda_{E}
.

(ii)

If

is an extension of

, then the extension
\Lambda_{E}
is an abelian extension of
\Lambda_{H}
by
I\times I
. Furthermore, if

, then the induced extension of
\Lambda
-groups is a central extension as
\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}(E)}\leq\operatorname{Z}(\Lambda_{E})

(iii)

If

are equivalent skew brace extensions of

So it turns out that the covariant functor

We conclude this section with a couple of definitions, which will be used in the last two sections. Let

be a skew brace and

3.
From skew braces to groups : extensions and cohomology

Let
H
be a skew brace and
I
a trivial brace such that
H
acts on
I
via a good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
. We start by defining an action of
\Lambda_{H}
on
I\times I
. Let
E
be any brace extension of
H
by
I
. Then, as explained above,
\Lambda_{E}
turns out to be an extension of
\Lambda_{H}
by
I\times I
. We know, again as explained above, that the actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
can be completely determined via the extension
E
. Using that characterization, for
x\in I
and
h\in H
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(s(h))-s(h)

\displaystyle=-x+x\circ s(h)-s(h)

\displaystyle=-x+s(h)\circ\sigma_{h}(x)-s(h)

\displaystyle=-x+s(h)+\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(x))-s(h)

(3.1)

\displaystyle=-x+\mu_{h}^{-1}(\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(x))),

which belongs to
I
.

For
(h,g)\in\Lambda_{H}
and
(x,y)\in I\times I
, using (
3
) at the forth equality, we make the following computation in
\Lambda_{E}
:

\displaystyle(s(h),s(g))^{-1}(x,y)(s(h),s(g))

\displaystyle=\big(\lambda_{s(g)^{-1}}(-s(h)),{s(g)^{-1}}\big)(x,y)(s(h),s(g))

\displaystyle=\big(\lambda_{{s(g)^{-1}}}(-s(h))+\lambda_{{s(g)^{-1}}}(x)+\lambda_{{s(g)^{-1}}\circ y}(s(h)),{s(g)^{-1}}\circ y\circ s(g)\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{{s(g)^{-1}}}\big(-s(h)+x+\lambda_{y}(s(h)),\sigma_{g}(y)\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{{s(g)^{-1}}}\big(-s(h)+x-y+\mu^{-1}_{h}(\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(y))),\sigma_{g}(y)\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\nu^{-1}_{g}\big(\mu_{h}(x-y)+\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(y))\big),\sigma_{g}(y)\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\nu^{-1}_{g}(\mu_{h}(x-y))+\nu^{-1}_{g}(\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(y))),\sigma_{g}(y)\big).

Here comes the action
\chi:\Lambda_{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(I\times I)
;
(h,g)\mapsto\chi_{(h,g)}
, where

(3.2)

\chi_{(h,g)}(x,y)=\big(\nu^{-1}_{g}(\mu_{h}(x-y))+\nu^{-1}_{g}(\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(y))),\sigma_{g}(y)\big).

We remark that the action
\chi
of
\Lambda_{H}
on
I\times I
depends on the good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
, and not on the choice of the extension
E
. Also, if
I
is chosen so that
\lambda_{x}(w)=w
for all
x\in I
and
w\in E
, then
\chi
takes an easy form

(3.3)

\chi_{(h,g)}(x,y)=\big(\nu^{-1}_{g}(\mu_{h}(x),\sigma_{g}(y)\big).

Indeed, if
\lambda_{x}(w)=w
for all
x\in I
and
w\in E
, then, for all
h\in H
and
y\in I
, we get

\displaystyle\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(y))

\displaystyle=\nu_{h}(s(h)^{-1}\circ y\circ s(h))=-s(h)+(y\circ s(h))=-s(h)+y+\lambda_{y}(s(h))

\displaystyle=-s(h)+y+s(h)=\mu_{h}(y).

The following result explains how compatibility conditions of a given arbitrary triple of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
is connected to the group action of
\Lambda_{H}
on
I\times I
given by (
3.2
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
H
be a skew brace and
I
an abelian group. Let

\mu:(H,+)\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(I)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma:(H,\circ)\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(I)

be antihomomorphisms, and let
\nu:(H,\circ)\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(I)
be a homomorphism. Define a map

\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}:\Lambda_{H}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(I\times I)

by setting

\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(h,g)}(x,y)=\left(\nu^{-1}_{g}(\mu_{h}(x-y))+\nu^{-1}_{g}(\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(y))),\sigma_{g}(y)\right)

for all
h,g\in H
and
x,y\in I
. Then
\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
is an anti-homomorphism if and only if
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
is a good triplet of actions.

Proof.

Suppose that
\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
is an anti-homomorphism. Then it is easy to see that the subgroup
I\times\{0\}
is invariant under the action of
\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
.
Indeed, for any
h,g\in H
and
x\in I
, we have

\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(h,g)}(x,0)=\left(\nu^{-1}_{g}(\mu_{h}(x)),\;0\right).

Note that
(0,g)(h,0)=(\lambda_{g}(h),g)
. Using this and the fact that
\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
is an anti-homomorphism, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{h}(\nu^{-1}_{g}(x)),\;0\right)

\displaystyle=\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(h,\;0)}\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(0,\;g)}(x,\;0)=\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(0,\;g)(h,\;0)}(x,\;0)

\displaystyle=\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(\lambda_{g}(h),\;g)}(x,\;0)=\nu^{-1}_{g}(\mu_{\lambda_{g}(h)}(x)),

which is the first condition of the good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
.

Next, we verify the second compatibility condition. Since
\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
is an anti-homomorphism, for all
h,g\in H
and
y\in I
, we have

\displaystyle\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(h,0)}\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(g,0)}(0,y)=\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(g+h,0)}(0,y).

Making further computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(h,0)}\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(g,0)}(0,y)

\displaystyle=\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(h,0)}\left(-\mu_{g}(y)+\nu_{g}(\sigma_{g}(y)),\;y\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\left(\mu_{h}\!\left(-\mu_{g}(y)+\nu_{g}(\sigma_{g}(y))-y\right)+\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(y)),\;y\right)

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}_{(g+h,0)}(0,y)

\displaystyle=\left(-\mu_{g+h}(y)+\nu_{g+h}(\sigma_{g+h}(y)),\;y\right).

Comparing the first coordinates, we deduce

\displaystyle\nu_{g+h}(\sigma_{g+h}(y))=\mu_{h}\!\big(\nu_{g}(\sigma_{g}(y))\big)-\mu_{h}(y)+\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(y)),

which is the second condition of the good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
.

Conversely, suppose that
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
is a good triplet of actions. Then there exists a skew brace extension
E
of
H
by
I
with associated actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
. This induces a group extension
\Lambda_{E}
of
\Lambda_{H}
by
I\times I
. By (
3.2
), the map
\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
describes the corresponding action of
\Lambda_{H}
on
I\times I
. Thus,
\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
is an anti-homomorphism.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
H
be a skew brace and let
I
be an
H
-module via a good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
on
I
. Then we can define an action of
\Lambda_{H}
on
I
by

\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}_{(h_{1},h_{2})}=\mu^{-1}_{h_{1}}\nu_{h_{2}},\quad\text{for all }h_{1},h_{2}\in H.

Proof.

Let
p_{1}:I\times I\to I
be the projection at the first factor. Then
\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}:=p_{1}\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}:\Lambda_{H}\to I
is the desired action of
\Lambda_{H}
on
I
, where
\chi^{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}
is defined in the preceding result. ∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let
G
be a skew brace and
I
be its ideal. Then equation (
3
) also holds in more generality, that is,
\lambda_{x}(g)-g\in I
for all
x\in I
and
g\in G
. The proof is essentially the same and uses the fact that
I
is normal in both the groups
(G,+)
and
(G,\circ)
.

One might expect that a non-split brace extension
0\to I\to E\to H\to 0
always goes to a non-split extension
0\to\Lambda_{I}\to\Lambda_{E}\to\Lambda_{H}\to 0
. But this is not the case, as explained in the following example.

Example 3.4
.

Let
A
be the brace with
(A,+)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, whose socle has order
2
, and
(A,\circ)
defined as follows:

(x_{1},y_{1})\circ(x_{2},y_{2})\;:=\;\bigl(x_{1}+x_{2},\;y_{1}+y_{2}+2y_{1}x_{2}+2(x_{1}+y_{1})y_{2}\bigr).

In this case, as classified in
[
2
]
, we get

(A,\circ)\;\cong\;(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}.

It is easy to see that

I=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\langle\bar{2}\rangle\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

is an ideal of
A
, which is a trivial brace. The GAP id of
A
is
SmallSkewbrace(8,8)
. A computation by GAP
[
11
]
, using the package Yang-Baxter
[
22
]
, shows that the ideal
I
does not admit a complement in
A
, but
\Lambda_{I}
does admit a complement in
\Lambda_{A}
. Hence, the extension
0\to I\to A\to A/I\to 0
of skew braces does not split, but the group extension
0\to\Lambda_{I}\to\Lambda_{A}\to\Lambda_{A}/\Lambda_{I}\to 0
does split.

So, a natural question, which arises here, is: Are there certain conditions under which non-split brace extensions always go to associated non-split group extensions? The answer is affirmative, as we see below. Let a skew brace
H
act on an abelian group
I
via a good triplet of actions
(\nu,\mu,\sigma)
, and
E
be the associated brace extension. Then
E
is a socle extension of
H
by
I
if and only if
\mu
is a trivial action of
(H,+)
on
I
and
\nu_{h}^{-1}=\sigma_{h}
for all
h\in H
. Indeed,
if
I\leq\operatorname{Soc}(E)
, then
\mu
is trivial, and using (
3
), that is,

0=\lambda_{x}(s(h))-s(h)=-x+\mu_{h}^{-1}\bigl(\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(x))\bigr),

we obtain
\nu_{h}(\sigma_{h}(x))=x
for all
x\in I
.
For the converse, if
\mu
is trivial, then
I\subseteq\operatorname{Z}(E,+)
.
To show that
I\subseteq\ker\lambda
, it is enough to check that
\lambda_{x}(s(h))=s(h)
for all
h\in H
and
x\in I
, which easily follows from (
3
). We now answer the above question.

Theorem 3.5
.

The skew brace extension (
2.1
) with
I\leq\operatorname{Soc}(E)
splits if and only if the associated group extension (
2.2
) splits.

Proof.

Note that ‘only if’ part of the assertion follows from the construction. So assume that the group extension (
2.2
) splits. Then there exists a section (group homomorphism)

s:\Lambda_{H}\to\Lambda_{E}
such that
(\pi\times\pi)\circ s=\mathrm{Id}_{\Lambda_{H}}
. For all
h,g\in H
, we can write

s(h,g)=(s_{1}(h,g),s_{2}(h,g)),

where
s_{1},s_{2}:H\times H\to E
are the component maps of
s
.
Since
s
is a group homomorphism, for all
(h_{1},g_{1}),(h_{2},g_{2})\in\Lambda_{H}
, we have

s(h_{1}+\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),\;g_{1}\circ g_{2})=s(h_{1},g_{1})\cdot s(h_{2},g_{2}).

Now writing in the component form and making further computations we get

s(h_{1}+\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),\;g_{1}\circ g_{2})=(s_{1}(h_{1}+\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),\;g_{1}\circ g_{2}),\;s_{2}(h_{1}+\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),\;g_{1}\circ g_{2}))

and

\displaystyle s(h_{1},g_{1})\cdot s(h_{2},g_{2})

\displaystyle=(s_{1}(h_{1},g_{1}),s_{2}(h_{1},g_{1}))\cdot(s_{1}(h_{2},g_{2}),s_{2}(h_{2},g_{2}))

\displaystyle=\left(s_{1}(h_{1},g_{1})+\lambda_{s_{2}(h_{1},g_{1})}(s_{1}(h_{2},g_{2})),\;s_{2}(h_{1},g_{1})\circ s_{2}(h_{2},g_{2})\right).

Comparing the first components, we obtain the key relation:

(3.6)

s_{1}(h_{1}+\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),\;g_{1}\circ g_{2})=s_{1}(h_{1},g_{1})+\lambda_{s_{2}(h_{1},g_{1})}(s_{1}(h_{2},g_{2})).

Now,
s
being a section, we see that
\pi(s_{1}(h,g))=h
and
\pi(s_{2}(h,g))=g
, and hence the difference
s_{2}(h,g)-s_{1}(g,h)\in I
. So there exists a map
f:H\times H\to I
such that

s_{2}(h,g)=s_{1}(g,h)+f(h,g).

In particular,
s_{1}(0,h)
and
s_{2}(h,0)
both lie in
I
for all
h\in H
. Observe that for any
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
, we have

s(h_{1},h_{2})=s((h_{1},0)(0,h_{2})),

which, using (
3.6
), implies that

(3.7)

s_{1}(h_{1},h_{2})=s_{1}(h_{1},0)+s_{1}(0,h_{2}),

since
s_{2}(h_{1},0)\in I
.

Using the fact that
I\leq\operatorname{Soc}(E)
and the identity
s_{2}(h,g)=s_{1}(g,h)+f(h,g)
, (
3.6
) takes the form

(3.8)

s_{1}(h_{1}+\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),\;g_{1}\circ g_{2})=s_{1}(h_{1},g_{1})+\lambda_{s_{1}(g_{1},h_{1})}(s_{1}(h_{2},g_{2})).

Define a map
t:H\to E
by

t(h):=s_{1}(h,0).

That
t
is a st-section of the projection
\pi:E\to H
is obvious. We claim that
t
is a skew brace homomorphism. Taking
g_{1}=g_{2}=0
in (
3.8
), we get

s_{1}(h_{1}+h_{2},0)=s_{1}(h_{1},0)+\lambda_{s_{1}(0,h_{1})}(s_{1}(h_{2},0))

for all
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
. Since
s_{1}(0,h_{1})\in I
, this, in the form of
t
, gives

t(h_{1}+h_{2})=t(h_{1})+t(h_{2}).

This shows that
t
is a homomorphism of the additive groups. Next, taking
h_{1}=0
and
g_{2}=0
in (
3.8
), we get

s_{1}(\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),g_{1})=s_{1}(0,g_{1})+\lambda_{s_{1}(g_{1},0)}(s_{1}(h_{2},0))

for all
g_{1},h_{2}\in H
, which,
using (
3.7
) on the left hand side and the fact
I\leq\operatorname{Soc}(E)
, gives

s_{1}(\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}),0)=\lambda_{s_{1}(g_{1},0)}(s_{1}(h_{2},0)).

This, using the definition of
t
, gives

t(\lambda_{g_{1}}(h_{2}))=\lambda_{t(g_{1})}(t(h_{2})).

Since
t
is a homomorphism of the additive groups, we get
t(g_{1}\circ h_{2})=t(g_{1})\circ t(h_{2})
for all
g_{1},h_{2}\in H
. Hence
t
is a skew brace homomorphism, and the proof is complete. ∎

\displaystyle 0

We remark that the map

Theorem 3.7

Let
H

\Psi:\operatorname{H}^{2}_{Sb}(H,I)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}_{Gp}(\Lambda_{H},I\times I)

Proof.

That the map

on the set of equivalence classes of extensions is well-defined. Indeed, as we know that there exists a bijection between the second cohomology groups and the set of equivalence classes of extensions. So the map
\Psi
is induced by
\psi

Proposition 4.6

Next, we recall a description of split extensions of skew braces from

Theorem 4.12

Let

\displaystyle(h_{1},y_{1})+(h_{2},y_{2})

\displaystyle(h_{1},y_{1})\circ(h_{2},y_{2})

We now provide an application of Proposition

is an ideal of

is an ideal of

is an ideal of

belongs to

is normal in

is a normal subgroup of

Reductions for split extension

In this section we study several reduction results on split extensions of finite skew braces by abelian groups (viewed as a trivial skew brace of abelian type). These results are brace analogs of group theory results from
[

]
. The following basic result is well known.

Lemma 5.1

(Dedekind’s modular law)
.

Let
G
be a group, and let
X
,
Y
and
T
be subgroups of
G
such that
T
is contained in
X
. Then

(X\cap Y)T=XT\cap YT.

We now prove a reduction result for split brace extensions.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
E
be a brace admitting an ideal
I
such that
I=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{r}
with
I_{j}\trianglelefteq E
for
1\leq j\leq r
. Let
I_{k}^{*}=\prod_{j\neq k}I_{j}
. Then the following hold true:

(i)

E
splits over
I
if and only if
E/I_{k}^{*}
splits over
I/I_{k}^{*}
for all
k=1,\ldots,r
.

(ii)

E
splits over
I
if and only if, for any

, the quotients
E/I_{k}
and
E/I_{m}
split over
I/I_{k}
and
I/I_{m}
, respectively.

Proof.

We first prove (i). Let
E
split over
I
. Then
E=G+I
for some sub-skew brace
G
of
E
such that
G\cap I=\{0\}
. Then we get

E/I_{k}^{*}=(G+I)/I_{k}^{*}\quad\text{for }1\leq k\leq r,

which gives

E/I_{k}^{*}=((G+I_{k}^{*})/I_{k}^{*})+(I/I_{k}^{*}).

Also note that

((G+I_{k}^{*})/I_{k}^{*})\cap(I/I_{k}^{*})=((G\cap I)+I_{k}^{*})/I_{k}^{*}=I_{k}^{*}/I_{k}^{*}=\{\bar{0}\}.

Hence,
E/I_{k}^{*}
splits over
I/I_{k}^{*}
with complement
(G+I_{k}^{*})/I_{k}^{*}
for all
k=1,\ldots,r
.

Conversely, assume that
E/I_{k}^{*}
splits over
I/I_{k}^{*}
for each
k=1,\ldots,r
. Then there exists a sub-skew brace
G_{k}
of
E
containing
I_{k}^{*}
such that

G_{k}/I_{k}^{*}+I/I_{k}^{*}=E/I_{k}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad G_{k}\cap I=I_{k}^{*}

for each
k
. Let
G=\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}G_{k}
, which is a sub-skew brace of
E
. Then

G+I=\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}G_{k}\right)+(I_{1}+I_{2}+\cdots+I_{r})=\left(\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}G_{k}\right)+I_{1}\right)+(I_{2}+\cdots+I_{r}).

Using Lemma
5.1
and the fact that
G_{k}+I_{k}=E
for
1\leq k\leq r
, we get

G+I=\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}(G_{k}+I_{k})=E.

Since
G\cap I=\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}(G_{k}\cap I)=\bigcap_{k=1}^{r}I_{k}^{*}=\{0\}
,
E
splits over
I
.

We now prove (ii). Without loss of generality, let
k=1
and
m=2
. Suppose that
E
splits over
I
, and let
G
be a complement of
I
in
E
. Define

\overline{G}_{1}:=GI_{1}/I_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{I}_{1}:=I/I_{1}.

We have
GI_{1}\cap I=I_{1}
, so
\overline{G}_{1}\cap\overline{I}_{1}=\{I_{1}/I_{1}\}
. Hence,
\overline{G}_{1}
is a complement of
\overline{I}_{1}
in
E/I_{1}
, and thus
E/I_{1}
splits over
I/I_{1}
. A similar argument shows the result for
I_{2}
.

Conversely, suppose that
E/I_{1}
and
E/I_{2}
split over
I/I_{1}
and
I/I_{2}
, respectively. Let
\overline{G}_{1}
and
\overline{G}_{2}
be complements of
I/I_{1}
and
I/I_{2}
in
E/I_{1}
and
E/I_{2}
, respectively. As we know,
\overline{G}_{1}=G_{1}/I_{1}
and
\overline{G}_{2}=G_{2}/I_{2}
for some skew braces
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
of
E
containing
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, respectively. Since
\overline{G}_{1}
and
\overline{G}_{2}
are complements of
I/I_{1}
and
I/I_{2}
, it follows that
G_{1}\cap I=I_{1}
and
G_{2}\cap I=I_{2}
. Let
G_{3}=G_{1}+(I_{3}+I_{4}+\cdots+I_{r})
, so that
G_{3}+I_{2}=E
and
G_{3}\cap I_{2}=\{0\}
. Define
G=G_{3}\cap G_{2}
. Then we have

G+I=(G_{3}\cap G_{2})+I=(G_{3}\cap G_{2})+(I_{1}+I_{2}+\cdots+I_{r}).

Using Lemma
5.1
repeatedly, we get

\displaystyle G+I

\displaystyle=(G_{3}+I_{1}\cap G_{2}+I_{1})+(I_{2}+\cdots+I_{r})

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

\displaystyle=(G_{3}+(I_{1}+I_{2}+\cdots+I_{r})\cap(G_{2}+(I_{1}+I_{2}+\cdots+I_{r}))

\displaystyle=E\cap E=E.

Moreover,

G\cap I=G_{3}\cap G_{2}\cap I=G_{3}\cap I_{2}=\{0\}.

This shows that
G
is a complement of
I
, and hence
E
splits over
I
. The proof is complete.
∎

Let
I
be a finite skew brace such that both
(I,+)
and
(I,\circ)
are nilpotent groups. Then it follows from
[
7
, Corollary 4.3]
that Sylow subgroups of
(I,+)
are ideals of
I
. Furthermore, if
P_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, are all distinct Sylow subgroups of
(I,+)
, then
I=P_{1}\oplus P_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus P_{k}
. With this setting, the preceding theorem gives

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
E
be a brace extension of
H
by
I
, where
I
is an in the preceding para. Define
P_{i}^{*}=\oplus_{j\neq i}P_{j}
for each
i
. Then the following hold true:

(i)

E
splits over
I
if and only if
E/P_{k}^{*}
splits over
I/P_{k}^{*}
for all
k=1,\ldots,k
.

(ii)

E
splits over
I
if and only if, for any
k\neq m
, the quotients
E/P_{k}
and
E/P_{m}
split over
I/P_{k}
and
I/P_{m}
, respectively.

Proof.

Since each
P_{i}
is a characteristic subgroup of
(I,+)
, as observed above, each of these is an ideal of
E
. The assertions are now immediate from Theorem
5.2
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let
H
be a skew brace. If
M
is an
H
-submodule of an
H
-module
I
, then
M\times M
is a
\Lambda_{H}
-submodule of
I\times I
.

For an
H
-module
I
, an
H
-composition series
for
I
is a series of submodules of
I

I=I_{0}\trianglerighteq I_{1}\trianglerighteq\cdots\trianglerighteq I_{k}=\{e\}

such that each quotient
I_{i}/I_{i+1}
is a simple
H
-module.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
H
be a skew brace and
I
be a finite
H
-module. Then there exists an
H
-composition series for
I
, and any two such composition series have the same length. Moreover, their composition factors are the same up to isomorphism and rearrangement.

Proof.

The existence follows from the finiteness of
I
: if
I
is not a simple
H
-module, then by the finiteness of
I
, there exists a maximal
H
-submodule of
I
. Repeating this process, we obtain an
H
-composition series for
I
. The second part also follows from a standard group-theoretic argument.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
H
be a skew brace and
I
be a finite
H
-module. Then any series of
H
-modules can be refined to an
H
-composition series.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
E
be a brace extension of a trivial skew brace
H
by a finite abelian
p
-group
I
and

I=I_{0}\rhd I_{1}\rhd\cdots\rhd I_{r}=\{0\}

be an
H
-composition series for
I
such that
I_{i}\trianglelefteq E
for all
i
. Then each factor
I_{i}/I_{i+1}
(
1\leq i<r
) is an elementary-abelian
p
-group and a simple
H
-module. Furthermore, if
\sigma
is the trivial action, then each
I_{i}/I_{i+1}
is a simple
\Lambda_{H}
-module via the action
\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}
.

Proof.

Let
|I|=p^{n}
. Define
I^{p^{i}}:=\left<z^{p^{i}}\mid z\in I\right>
. It is well known that
I^{p^{i}}
is a characteristic subgroup of
I
for all
i\geq 1
and
I^{p^{i}}/I^{p^{i+1}}
is elementary abelian
p
-group. Thus
I^{p^{i}}
is an ideal of
E
, and therefore an
H
-module. Since
I
is finite, there exists
k\geq 1
such that
I^{p^{k}}=\{0\}
. We get a series

I\rhd I^{p^{1}}\rhd\cdots\rhd I^{p^{k}}=\{0\}

of
H
-submodules of
I
. Using Corollary
5.6
, this series can be refined to an
H
-composition series

I=I_{0}\rhd I_{1}\rhd\cdots\rhd I_{r}=\{0\}

of
I
, in which every factor is an elementary abelian
p
-group. Obviously, each
I_{i}/I_{i+1}
is a simple
H
-module. If
\sigma
is trivial, then, by Corollary
3.2
,
\Lambda_{H}
acts on
I
via
\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}
. It is now not difficult to prove that each
I_{i}/I_{i+1}
is a simple
\Lambda_{H}
-module via the action
\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}
. Indeed, if
K:=I_{i}/I_{i+1}
is not simple
\Lambda_{H}
-module under
\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}
, then there exists a nonzero proper subgroup
M<K
which is invariant under
\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}
.
In particular,

\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}_{(h,0)}(M)=\mu^{-1}_{h}(M)\subseteq M\quad\text{and}\quad\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}_{(0,h)}(M)=\nu_{h}(M)\subseteq M,

which contradicts the hypothesis that
K
is a simple
H
-module. The proof is complete.
∎

We remark that if a skew brace
H
acts on an abelian group
I
via
(\nu,\mu,\operatorname{id})
such that
I
is a simple
\Lambda_{H}
-module via the action
\phi^{(\nu,\mu)}
, then
I
is a simple

-module.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
E
be a skew brace admitting a trivial brace
I
. Suppose that
I
has a series

I=I_{0}\trianglerighteq I_{1}\trianglerighteq\cdots\trianglerighteq I_{r}=\{0\}

of its subgroups such that each
I_{i}
is an ideal of
E
for
i=0,1\ldots,r
. If there exist sub-skew braces
B_{1},\ldots,B_{r}
of
E
such that
E=B_{1}+I
,
B_{1}\cap I=I_{1}
, and for each
i=1,\ldots,r-1
,
B_{i}=B_{i+1}+I_{i}
and
B_{i+1}\cap I_{i}=I_{i+1}
, then
B_{r}
is a complement of
I
in
E
.

Proof.

It is given that
E=B_{1}+I
and
B_{i}=B_{i+1}+I_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r-1
. We obtain

E=B_{1}+I=B_{2}+I_{1}+I=B_{2}+I=\cdots=B_{r}+I.

We claim that
B_{r}\cap I=\{0\}
. Contrarily, assume that
0\neq x\in B_{r}\cap I
. Then
x\in B_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r-1
, since
B_{r}\subseteq B_{r-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq B_{1}
. Therefore, we get

x\in B_{1}\cap I=I_{1}\Rightarrow x\in B_{2}\cap I_{1}=I_{2}\Rightarrow\cdots\Rightarrow x\in B_{r-1}\cap I_{r-2}=I_{r-1}.

Then
x\in B_{r}\cap I_{r-1}=\{0\}
, which contradicts the assumption that
x\neq 0
. Hence the claim follows, and the proof is complete.

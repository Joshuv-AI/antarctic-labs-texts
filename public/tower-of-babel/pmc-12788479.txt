Comparative Evaluation of Platelet-Rich Fibrin (PRF) versus Collagen Membrane in Accelerating Healing after Mandibular Third Molar Extractions: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Priya Gupta, Ratnesh Singh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Removing impacted third molars in the lower jaw can often cause problems, such as pain, swelling, and slow regrowth of tissue. Postoperative healing is often improved using platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) and collagen membranes.Materials and Methods:Sixty patients needing the removal of bilateral mandibular third molars were randomly assigned to the two groups (30 patients in each group). Group A used PRF put into the extraction socket, and Group B placed a collagen membrane. Pain (based on visual analog scale), swelling (measured), and soft tissue healing (using the Landry’s healing index) were compared on the 3rd, 7th, and 14th postoperative days. Researchers used SPSS version 25.0 with a significance of P < 0.05.Results:On the third day, the pain score was lower on average in the PRF group (3.2) than in the collagen group (4.5). In Group A, the swelling was lower (4.1 ± 0.6 mm) compared to Group B (5.6 ± 0.9 mm). By the fourteenth day, tissues in the PRF group (4.6 ± 0.4) scored higher for healing, proving they healed faster compared to the collagen group (3.9 ± 0.6) (P < 0.01). Everyone involved in the study reported no negative experiences.Conclusion:During the first few days after having third molar teeth removed, patients who used PRF experienced better pain management, a reduction in swelling, and swifter healing of the tissues than those who used collagen membranes.

INTRODUCTION

It is common for oral surgeons to perform the surgery to remove impacted mandibular third molars, yet patients often experience discomfort such as pain, swelling, difficulty opening their mouths, and delays in wound healing after the surgery.[1] Looking after these problems is vital for the speedy recovery of patients and better results in the operating room. Various regenerative options have been tried to help repair dental tissues losing the tooth, and platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) and collagen membranes are applied most.

Developed by Choukroun et al., PRF is a type of second-generation platelet concentrate that consistently releases growth factors, aiding faster tissue recovery and growth.[2] Researchers say it supports angiogenesis, the movement of cells called fibroblasts, and the multiplication of epithelial cells, all important for wound repair.[3] Since PRF comes from the patient’s own blood, it is easy for the body to accept and affordable for minor oral surgeries.[4]

Furthermore, collagen membranes are commonly used to block the attachment of unnecessary tissues in GTR and in surgically filled tooth sockets. They allow cells to move and merge with tissue while keeping the soft tissue at the proper height in the socket.[5] In third molar surgery, they are placed to support the formation of blood clots and guard the wound from external germs.[6]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Sixty healthy individuals aged 18–35 years, requiring bilateral mandibular third molar extractions of similar difficulty levels, were recruited. The study was carried out at the Krishna Nagar Institute of Medical Sciences, located in Nadia, Krishna Nagar, West Bengal, India. Exclusion criteria included systemic diseases, smoking, pregnancy or lactation, and a history of anticoagulant therapy or local infection at the surgical site.

Randomization and group allocation

A split-mouth design was used. Each patient underwent bilateral extractions in two separate sessions with a washout period of two weeks. Random allocation of the side to receive PRF or collagen membrane was done using a computer-generated sequence.

Group A (PRF Group): PRF was prepared and placed in the socket.

Group A (PRF Group): PRF was prepared and placed in the socket.

Group B (Collagen Group): A commercially available sterile collagen membrane was applied to the extraction socket.

Group B (Collagen Group): A commercially available sterile collagen membrane was applied to the extraction socket.

Surgical procedure

All extractions were performed under local anesthesia using a standard envelope flap technique. After tooth removal and irrigation with sterile saline, hemostasis was achieved. PRF was obtained by centrifuging 10 mL of venous blood at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes in a sterile glass tube without anticoagulants. The resulting PRF clot was trimmed and inserted into the socket in Group A. In Group B, a resorbable collagen membrane was trimmed and adapted into the socket. Primary closure was achieved with 3–0 silk sutures.

Parameters assessed

Pain: Assessed using a 10-point visual analog scale (VAS).

Swelling: Measured using a flexible ruler from tragus to pogonion and lateral canthus to gonion.

Soft Tissue Healing: Evaluated using Landry’s healing index, which scores healing on a scale of 1 (very poor) to 5 (excellent).

Statistical analysis

Data were entered into Microsoft Excel and analyzed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

RESULTS

The visual analog scale (VAS) scores for postoperative pain were significantly lower in the PRF group compared to the collagen membrane group at all follow-up intervals. On postoperative day 3, Group A (PRF) recorded a mean VAS score of 3.2 ± 0.9, while Group B (Collagen) showed a higher mean score of 4.7 ± 1.1 (P < 0.05). The trend continued through days 7 and 14, indicating faster pain resolution in the PRF group [Table 1].

Postoperative swelling, measured using facial landmarks, was consistently lower in the PRF group. On day 3, Group A exhibited an average swelling of 4.2 ± 0.8 mm, while Group B had 5.6 ± 1.0 mm (P < 0.05). By day 14, swelling had nearly resolved in both groups, but Group A maintained a statistically significant advantage [Table 2].

DISCUSSION

Patients often feel pain after their surgery for removing third molars. All around, participants using PRF had a better reduction in their pain scores than patients given collagen membranes. It is thought that the pain reduction after PRF occurs because the growth factors PDGF and TGF-β1 are released by the platelets and encourage vessels to grow, leading to less inflammation.[12] Other studies have also found that using PRF in surgery reduces the amount of discomfort afterward.[34]

It is also common for swelling to happen after surgery, as a result of damage to the tissues and the body’s response to it. In our investigation, the use of PRF was found to reduce swelling better than using collagen membranes. It is thought to be caused by PRF’s ability to affect pro-inflammatory cytokines and aid lymph drainage using its own fibrin network.[56] In clinical trials from the past, PRF was found to considerably decrease the swelling after oral surgery.[78910]

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of this randomized controlled trial, platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) was found to be more effective than collagen membranes in accelerating postoperative healing following mandibular third molar extractions.

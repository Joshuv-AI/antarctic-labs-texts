We report an experimental investigation of the reactions n ¯ ​ p → 2 ​ π + ​ π − \bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-} , n ¯ ​ p → 2 ​ π + ​ π − ​ π 0 \bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0} , and n ¯ ​ p → 2 ​ π + ​ π − ​ 2 ​ π 0 \bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}2\pi^{0} using ( 10.087 ± 0.044 ) × 10 9 (10.087\pm 0.044)\times 10^{9} J / ψ J/\psi events collected with the BESIII detector at the BEPCII storage ring. The antineutron ( n ¯ \bar{n} ) is produced in the decay J / ψ → p ​ π − ​ n ¯ J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n} with studied momentum from 200 MeV/ c c to 1174 MeV/ c c , while the target proton originates from the hydrogen nuclei in the cooling oil of the beam pipe. This novel method pioneers the study of n ¯ \bar{n} -nucleon interactions at an e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider, providing the first experimental data for n ¯ \bar{n} momenta exceeding 800 MeV/ c c .



Scattering experiments are fundamental tools for probing the internal structure of nucleons, with the choice of particle source playing a pivotal role. Antineutrons possess a sufficiently long lifetime to serve as a viable particle source. However, since their discovery
Cork:1957nu
, antineutrons have rarely been utilized due to the significant challenges associated with their production and control. Despite these difficulties, antineutron beams offer unique advantages, such as the absence of Coulomb corrections and the purity of the
I=1
state in the
\bar{n}p
system, which simplifies the analysis of isospin-dependent effects
allReview
.

To date, the most effective antineutron sources have been achieved at the BNL E-767 experiment
BNL
, with momenta ranging from 100 MeV/
c
to 500 MeV/
c
, and the CERN OBELIX experiment
OBELIX
, with momenta ranging from 50 MeV/
c
to 400 MeV/
c
. These enable the investigation of a wide range of physics topics, from nuclear physics to hadron spectroscopy, albeit with limited statistical significance.
Both facilities produced antineutron sources through the charge-exchange process
\bar{p}p\to n\bar{n}
. Despite the experimental insights obtained, this method has notable drawbacks: the antineutron production rates remain low, and achieving precise control over their momenta and directions poses significant technical challenges. These drawbacks underscore the need to improve the quality and efficiency of antineutron beam production.

In this Letter, we use a novel source of antineutrons
ycz
, which differs significantly from traditional techniques. The
J/\psi
meson, with its high production cross section in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation and substantial decay branching fraction of
J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n}
,
(2.12\pm 0.09)\times 10^{-3}

pdg2024
, emerges as a promising new source of
\bar{n}
.
By measuring
p\pi^{-}
, we can accurately determine the momentum of the
\bar{n}
.
The interaction between the
\bar{n}
and protons in the target material, located within a small radius of the
e^{+}e^{-}
beam pipe, enables a precise study of the cross section and the properties of the final states.
Notably, this work presents the first observation of the reactions
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
,
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
, and
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}2\pi^{0}
at an
e^{+}e^{-}
collider, as well as the first investigation of these processes with
\bar{n}
momenta exceeding 800 MeV/
c
.
Refs.
BES_scatter1
;
BES_scatter2
;
BES_scatter3
have applied this technique to study hyperon (
\Lambda,\Sigma,\rm{and}~\Xi
) scattering at BESIII, where the hyperons similarly originate from
J/\psi

Following the single-tag (ST) and double-tag (DT) approach described in Refs.
BES_scatter3
;
BES_scatter33

J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n}
, the
p\pi^{-}
selection, and the total number of
J/\psi

Specifically, the ST selection involves reconstructing
p\pi^{-}
from
J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n}
. The DT selection extends this by additionally reconstructing the final-state particles from the
\bar{n}p
interaction,
2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}i\pi^{0}
(
i=0,1,2
), as discussed in detail later.

(10.087\pm 0.044)\times 10^{9}

J/\psi

The BESIII detector surrounds the beam pipe, which consists of four layers: a gold (
197
Au) layer, a beryllium (
9

12

1

Since the proton in the
1

9

In the ST selection, candidate events for
J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n}
are reconstructed by identifying
p\pi^{-}
combinations.
Charged tracks reconstructed in the main drift chamber (MDC) are required to fall within the polar angle range
|\cos\theta^{\prime}|<0.93

\theta^{\prime}
is the angle relative to the
z

Additionally, the distance of closest approach to the
e^{+}e^{-}
interaction point (IP) must be less than
\pm 10

Particle identification (PID) for charged tracks is based on the combination of energy loss measurements
(dE/dx)
in the MDC and flight time from the time-of-flight system. These measurements are used to construct likelihoods
\mathcal{L}(h)
for each hadron
h

h=p,K,\pi
), and tracks are assigned the particle type corresponding to the highest likelihood.
At least two charged tracks, identified as a proton and a pion (
p\pi^{-}
), are required. No additional requirements are imposed on other charged tracks or neutral showers in the event. All valid
p\pi^{-}
combinations are subjected to a vertex fit to improve track momentum resolution, followed by a four-constraint(4C) kinematic fit.
The 4C fit constrains the missing track’s mass to that of the
\bar{n}
and enforces energy-momentum conservation. Among all combinations, the one with the smallest combined
\chi^{2}
from the vertex and kinematic fits (
\chi^{2}_{\text{vertex}}
and
\chi^{2}_{\text{kinematic}}
) is selected.
The event is retained if
\chi^{2}_{\text{vertex}}<10
and
\chi^{2}_{\text{kinematic}}<200
.
A relatively loose
\chi^{2}_{\text{kinematic}}
requirement is chosen to preserve a linear background shape in the
RM(p\pi^{-})
distribution.
The ST signal yield, denoted as
N_{\rm{ST}}
, is extracted by fitting the recoil mass spectrum of
p\pi^{-}
, defined as
RM(p\pi^{-})=|p_{e^{+}e^{-}}-p_{p}-p_{\pi^{-}}|/c
, where
p_{e^{+}e^{-}}
,
p_{p}
, and
p_{\pi^{-}}
are the four-momenta of the
e^{+}e^{-}
initial state, proton, and pion, respectively.
A binned extended maximum likelihood fit is applied to the
RM(p\pi^{-})
distribution. The
\bar{n}
signal region is defined as
[0.92,0.96]
GeV/
c^{2}
.
The signal is modeled using a double-Gaussian function, and the background is modeled with a first-order Chebyshev polynomial function.
The fit result is shown in Fig.

This fitting procedure is also applied independently within each
P(\bar{n})

The ST selection efficiency,

\pi^{-}
in ST. However, no requirement is imposed on the closest approach distance to IP, as

A vertex fit is conducted for

, where the vertex position is denoted as
(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})

is required to be less than 5.
The direction of the

(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})

The vector

(0,0,0)

|\sqrt{(x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2})}-\sqrt{(x_{2}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})}|<0.5~\text{cm}

All

mass

.
For

To remove events from the reactions between

The

For the signal process

The “effective luminosity”

(2)

where
\epsilon_{\rm{ST}}
is the efficiency of the ST reconstruction,

is the Avogadro’s number

,

The systematic uncertainty due to the tracking or PID is 1% per charged track
BES_scatter1
. For a single
\pi^{0}

sys-pi0
.

\bar{n}

To address the uncertainty arising from the reweighting of MC efficiency, we adjust the counts per bin during reweighting by adding or subtracting 20% to observe the change in efficiency. The larger change is taken as the systematic uncertainty.
By varying the
2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

\pm 10\%
, the most significant

In summary, based on a sample of
(10.087\pm 0.044)\times 10^{9}

J/\psi
events collected with the BESIII detector at the BEPCII storage ring, we report the first measurements of the reactions
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
,
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
, and
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}2\pi^{0}
, where the
\bar{n}
is produced via the decay
J/\psi\to p\pi^{-}\bar{n}
and the
p
is sourced from hydrogen nuclei in the cooling oil of the beam pipe.
The cross sections for these reactions are measured in five intervals of
P(\bar{n}):
[200, 400], [400, 600], [600, 800], [800, 1000], and [1000, 1174] MeV/
c
, within the overall range of [200, 1174] MeV/
c
.
This constitutes the first measurement where the
\bar{n}
momentum exceeds 800 MeV/
c
.
Previously, the cross sections for the signal process
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}i\pi^{0}
, measured by the OBELIX experiment in the
\bar{n}
momentum range of [50, 400] MeV/
c
, were reported as (3.82
\pm
0.304) mb, (36.37
\pm
3.30) mb, and (20.67
\pm
1.86) mb for
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
,
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
, and
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}2\pi^{0}
, respectively. Additionally, a bubble chamber experiment
bubble
measured cross sections of (1.16
\pm
0.16) mb and (10.15
\pm
0.51) mb for
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
in the momentum range [500, 800] MeV/
c
.
It should be noted that these comparisons are approximate as the
\bar{n}
momentum distribution in our analysis is different from previous studies. Moreover, we observe intermediate
\rho
and
\omega
states in the
M(\pi^{+}\pi^{0})
and
M(\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0})
distributions from
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{0}
and
\bar{n}p\to 2\pi^{+}\pi^{-}2\pi^{0}
, respectively.
The reported results represent the first measurement of antineutron-nucleon inelastic scattering at an electron-positron collider, demonstrating the feasibility of using this configuration to study such reactions. This research opens a path for future investigations at the proposed super tau-charm factory
stcf1
;
stcf2
, where the abundant
J/\psi
sample can provide an excellent source of antineutrons and long-lived (anti-)baryons for high-precision studies in nuclear and particle physics.

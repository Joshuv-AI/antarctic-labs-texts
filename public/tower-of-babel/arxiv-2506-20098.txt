A New Layout-Aware Quantum Circuit Synthesis with Davio Lattices Using Triangular Connectivity
Sonia Yang, Ali Al-Bayaty, Marek Perkowski

Abstract
The quantum layout and the mapping of logical to physical qubits are essential in synthesizing a quantum circuit into a real quantum computer. In general, popular quantum layouts, such as the square grid and heavy-hex layouts, are not suitable for synthesizing a quantum circuit consisting of many Toffoli gates. Due to the limited connectivity in these layouts, additional SWAP gates are required for circuit synthesis. We introduce in this paper a triangular layout suited for Toffoli gates by exploring the advantages of Positive Davio lattices for quantum circuit synthesis. We also introduce methods of mapping the resulting triangular layouts onto square grid and heavy-hex layouts. Our approach highlights Davio lattices as a useful tool for circuit synthesis and brings out a new layout-aware method for efficient quantum circuit implementation.



1
Introduction

Quantum computing has emerged as a powerful tool with the potential to solve problems across various fields, offering performance that surpasses that of classical computers
[
1
]
. However, to have effective quantum computers, awareness of the quantum layout—the arrangement of qubits in a quantum computer—is crucial. The connectivity in a quantum layout determines which qubits can interact with each other. To operate on a pair of qubits that are not directly connected in a layout, SWAP gates are required. The number of additional SWAP gates has a significant impact on the quantum cost and error
[
2
,
3
]
. Optimizing the number of SWAP gates and the layout is critical for creating and running an effective quantum computer.

Two of the most common quantum layouts are the square grid
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
and heavy-hex layouts
[
6
]
. The square grid layout offers good connectivity and is flexible, requiring fewer additional SWAP gates than the heavy-hex layout
[
7
]
. On the other hand, the heavy-hex layout has less crosstalk
[
6
]
, leading to less noise during the execution of quantum circuits, the operations that quantum computers run. However, both layouts have connectivity that is not suitable for many gates, such as the Toffoli, leading to more SWAP gates being required.

A unique and promising method of synthesizing quantum circuits is with Positive Davio lattices
[
8
,
9
]
, lattice diagrams based on the Positive Davio expansion
[
10
]
, introduced in
[
11
]
. The Positive Davio expansion has a relation to one of the outputs of the Toffoli gate, an important and commonly-used gate with two controls and a target, introduced in
[
12
]
. This circuit synthesis technique takes advantage of the regular structure of these lattices and the relationship of Positive Davio and Toffoli gates to synthesize circuits with only regular Toffoli and SWAP gates. However, there has yet to be any investigation on quantum layouts related to this method.

In this paper, we extend on past research done on synthesizing quantum circuits from Positive Davio lattices
[
8
,
11
]
and introduce a new triangular quantum layout that aligns with the Toffoli gate and this method. This methodology is aimed at reducing the cost of both circuit synthesis and the implementation of these circuits onto physical qubits.

We also introduced mappings from the triangular layout onto the square grid and heavy-hex layouts, along with the equations for the number of additional SWAP gates required. The number of SWAP gates required for mapping onto the square grid and heavy-hex layouts increases quadratically with the number of levels in the Positive Davio lattice.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides background and related work on lattice-based circuit synthesis and quantum layouts. Section 3 presents our new triangular grid layout along with its connection to the lattice-based synthesis method. Section 4 presents methods of mapping this layout onto square grid and heavy-hex layouts. Section 5 presents the results and compares these layouts. And lastly, Section 6 concludes the paper with potential future directions.

2.1.1
Boolean Expansions

Boolean functions can be expanded using expansions. One expansion is stated in Eq. (
1

Shannon expansion

[
13
]
. The terms
f_{\bar{a}}
and
f_{a}
are the negative and positive cofactors, respectively. The negative cofactor
f_{\bar{a}}
is the function
f
when the variable
a
is substituted as 0. Similarly, the positive cofactor
f_{a}
is the function
f
when the variable
a
is substituted as 1.

f=\bar{a}f_{\bar{a}}+af_{a}

(1)

In Eq. (
1

\oplus
) operation, as stated in Eq. (
2

f=\bar{a}f_{\bar{a}}\oplus af_{a}

(2)

Example 1

Let’s take an example with the function
f=a\oplus\bar{a}b\oplus ac\oplus c
. The negative cofactor would be
f_{\bar{a}}=(0)\oplus(1)b\oplus(0)c\oplus c=b\oplus c
.

The positive cofactor would be
f_{a}=(1)\oplus(0)b\oplus(1)c\oplus c=1
. Using the Shannon expansion formula in Eq. (
2
), we can get the following from the positive and negative cofactors:

f=\bar{a}(b\oplus c)\oplus a(1)=\bar{a}b\oplus\bar{a}c\oplus a.

The Shannon expansion is not the only possible expansion of Boolean functions. There are also the
Davio expansions

[
10
]
. They are derived from the Shannon expansion theorem from Eq. (
2
).

To derive the
Positive Davio expansion
, substitute
\bar{a}=1\oplus a
into Eq. (
2
) to obtain
f=(1\oplus a)f_{\bar{a}}\oplus af_{a}
and simplify to Eq. (
3
).

f=f_{\bar{a}}\oplus a(f_{\bar{a}}\oplus f_{a})

(3)

The Positive Davio expansion can tie directly to an output of the
Toffoli gate
, as shown in Fig.
1
. This makes the Positive Davio expansion a valuable tool in quantum computing.

Figure 1
:
The Positive Davio expansion realized as a Toffoli gate.

Similarly, we can derive the
Negative Davio expansion
, stated in Eq. (
4
), by substituting
a=1\oplus\bar{a}
into Eq. (
2
) to obtain
f=\bar{a}f_{\bar{a}}\oplus(1\oplus\bar{a})f_{a}
and simplifying.

f=f_{a}\oplus\bar{a}(f_{\bar{a}}\oplus f_{a})

(4)

The Shannon, Positive Davio, and Negative Davio expansions are valuable tools in logic and circuits.

Quantum Layout

In order to build quantum computers that are scalable and have low error rates, an effective physical quantum layout is crucial. The quantum layout, the arrangement of the qubits in a quantum computer, determines the connectivity between qubits.

Currently, one of the most popular methods of implementing qubits is through superconduction
[
16
]
. The quantum layouts in these computers are generally grids with nearest-neighbor connections. In quantum computing, two of the most popular layouts are the square grid

For the square grid, each qubit in this grid is connected to 4 other qubits. It was notably used in Google’s Sycamore quantum computer

6
Conclusion

We introduced a new quantum layout based on a triangular grid that is suitable for a high number of Toffoli gates. We also expanded on the Positive Davio lattice-based circuit synthesis method and its relation to the triangular grid layout. This layout and synthesis method has the potential to reduce the quantum cost, though it is not a complete replacement for other methods. The benefit of this new layout is that it reduces the number of additional SWAP gates and cost. The drawback is that its high connectivity results in higher chances of crosstalk.

This proposed quantum layout has a strong potential for improving connectivity and quantum circuit cost, backed up by theory. Although this quantum layout has not yet been implemented in a physical quantum computer, as quantum technology continues to advance, this method could hold significant potential to become a valuable tool in the future.

We also introduced mappings of the triangular layout onto the square grid and heavy-hex layouts. It is necessary to note that on physical hardware, the mappings would be limited by space and cannot stretch on to infinity, so more SWAP gates would be required.

There are many potential future directions. The triangular layout could be expanded into 3D, a promising path for quantum layouts. Ways of reducing noise in this layout can also be studied. As for the Positive Davio lattice-based circuit synthesis method, similar methods for expanding to multi-output functions and with lattices based on other expansions or a combination of multiple expansions have yet to be intensively studied.

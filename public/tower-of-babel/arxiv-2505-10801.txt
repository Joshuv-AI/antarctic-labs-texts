1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and the problem

Quantization studies the approximation of a probability distribution by a
finite set of representatives. Its asymptotic theory relates the number of
representatives and the approximation error to the distribution of mass;
see Graf and Luschgy
[
GL00
]
for the general theory and
Gray and Neuhoff
[
GN98
]
for its information-theoretic background.
For singular probabilities, the quantization dimension also records features
of the measure that enter the approximation rate; self-similar distributions
provide a central class of examples
[
GL01
,
GL02
]
.

In constrained quantization, the representatives must belong to a prescribed
closed set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
. Pandey and Roychowdhury determine optimal
codebooks and errors for uniform distributions with line and circle
constraints
[
PR26
]
, and for the Cantor distribution under several
constraints
[
PR24
]
. These explicit configurations show
how the constraint affects both the limiting error and the rate at which
it is approached. When the source is separated from
S
, that limit is
positive, so the asymptotic problem can be formulated as estimating the
difference between the quantization error and its limit. For an upper
estimate, choose finitely many centers that approximate the nearest points
on
S
. The triangle inequality bounds the distance from a source point
to these centers by its distance to
S
plus the approximation error of
its nearest point. For a lower estimate, one instead controls how much
the source-to-center distance increases as the center moves away from
a nearest point. Integrating this increase over a suitable part of the
source gives a lower bound for the excess.

Our first main result is an upper comparison based on nearest-point projection.
A Borel nearest-point selector transfers ordinary quantization estimates for
the projected source measure to upper bounds for the constrained excess.
This comparison holds for arbitrary nonempty closed constraints, without
requiring unique nearest points, continuity of the selector, or local
concentration estimates. At infinite order, we obtain
an upper bound by covering the full nearest-point image of the source support.

For lower bounds, we allow the power-growth coefficient to vary with the
source point and incorporate it into a weighted projected measure.
An
A_{p}
condition on its density gives a comparison with ordinary
quantization errors of a reference probability measure, without imposing
small-ball regularity on that measure. Under uniform growth
and an upper ball-mass estimate, the power-law lower bound also follows
from the general distortion theorem of Riegler, Koliander and
Bölcskei
[
RKB23
]
; see Example
22
.
The weighted formulation applies to convex and uniformly prox-regular
constraints and allows growth weights with no positive uniform lower bound.
For the smaller concentric disk and the lifted Cantor distribution,
we obtain
\Delta_{n,2}=e_{n,2}^{2}-L_{2}^{2}\asymp n^{-2/d}
, where
d

is the Hausdorff dimension of the projected support.

Notation and main results

Throughout the article,

For

An

For

For

, use

.
The collection of minimizers is denoted by

When the source and constraint are fixed, we write

The metric projection and distance baseline are

\begin{gathered}b(x)=\rho(x,S),\qquad\pi_{S}(x)=\{a\in S:|x-a|=b(x)\},\qquad E=\pi_{S}(K)=\bigcup_{x\in K}\pi_{S}(x),\\

The set

We call

. For ordinary quantization write

For a nonempty compact set
A
, write

Lemma

proves

and

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log\widehat{e}_{n,r}},

\displaystyle\overline{\widehat{\dim}}_{Q}^{r}{(P;S)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log\widehat{e}_{n,r}},

2.
Preliminary Results

2.1.
The projection and a Borel selector

Two inverse-image operations for the set-valued projection will be useful.
For
A\subset E
, put

(2.1)

H(A)=\{x\in K:\pi_{S}(x)\cap A\neq\varnothing\},\qquad L(A)=\{x\in K:\pi_{S}(x)\subset A\}.

The first operation records whether a nearest-point set meets
A
; the
second records whether all nearest points lie in
A
.

Lemma 3
.

The sets
\pi_{S}(x)
are nonempty and compact. The graph

G=\{(x,a)\in K\times S:|x-a|=b(x)\}

and its second-coordinate projection
E
are compact. If
A\subset E
is
closed, then
H(A)
is closed in
K
. If
U\subset E
is relatively open,
then
L(U)
is relatively open in
K
.
Moreover, if
x_{j}\to x
in
K
, then

(2.2)

\sup_{a\in\pi_{S}(x_{j})}\rho(a,\pi_{S}(x))\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

First, the triangle inequality gives

b(x)\leq|x-y|+b(y),\qquad b(y)\leq|x-y|+b(x),

so
|b(x)-b(y)|\leq|x-y|
. Fix
a_{0}\in S
. A sequence in
S
whose distances
from a fixed
x
tend to
b(x)
is bounded. A convergent subsequence has its
limit in
S
, and continuity of distance shows that the limit belongs to

\pi_{S}(x)
.

Choose
R\geq 1
with
K\subset\overline{B}(0,R)
. For every
(x,a)\in G
,

|a|\leq|x|+|x-a|=|x|+b(x)\leq|x|+|x-a_{0}|\leq 2R+|a_{0}|.

The graph is closed because
S
is closed and
(x,a)\mapsto|x-a|-b(x)
is
continuous. This uniform bound makes the graph compact. Its projection
E

is consequently compact. For closed
A\subset E
, the set

G\cap(K\times A)
is compact, and its first-coordinate projection is
H(A)
.
Thus
H(A)
is closed. The identity

K\setminus L(U)=H(E\setminus U)

proves the assertion for
L(U)
.

To prove (
2.2
), suppose that it fails. There would then
be
\varepsilon>0
, a subsequence still indexed by
j
, and

a_{j}\in\pi_{S}(x_{j})
with
\rho(a_{j},\pi_{S}(x))\geq\varepsilon
. Compactness of

E
gives a further subsequence with
a_{j}\to a
. The closed graph property
gives
a\in\pi_{S}(x)
. But

\varepsilon\leq\rho(a_{j},\pi_{S}(x))\leq|a_{j}-a|\longrightarrow 0,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4
.

There exists a Borel map
T:K\to E

Proof.

Lemma

For a relatively open set

These sets are compact and

For

is a measurable closed-valued mapping from

∎

For any finite Borel measure

In particular, for

Support and local mass of the projected measure

.

,
and the reverse inclusion has already been proved.
∎

A quantitative version of (
U1
) is the local condition

(U2)

P\{x\in K:\pi_{S}(x)\subset B(y,t)\}\geq ct^{d}\qquad(y\in E,\ 0<t<t_{0}),

where
c,t_{0}>0
and

are fixed.

Proposition 6
.

Condition (
U2
) implies U1 and, for every Borel selector,

(2.6)

\mu(B(y,t))\geq ct^{d}\qquad(y\in E,\ 0<t<t_{0}).

In particular,
E_{\mu}=E
.

Proof.

If
0<t<t_{0}
, the containment event in (

) is included in

\{x:T(x)\in B(y,t)\}
. Taking probabilities gives
(
2.6
). For any larger ball choose

; its containment event contains the event for

, which has positive mass. This proves U1 at every radius, and

.
∎

2.3.
Voronoi partitions, attainment, and limiting errors

For measurable functions
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}:X\to[0,\infty)
on a measurable
space
X
, ties are assigned to the smallest minimizing index. Set

V_{i}=\bigcap_{j<i}\{d_{i}<d_{j}\}\cap\bigcap_{j>i}\{d_{i}\leq d_{j}\}.

The comparisons are measurable, and each
x
has a unique smallest
minimizing index. Thus
(V_{i})
is a measurable partition and

\min_{i}d_{i}(x)=\sum_{i}d_{i}(x)\mathbf{1}_{V_{i}}(x).

For an ordered codebook
\alpha=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
, take
X=K
and

d_{i}(x)=|x-a_{i}|
. Then

(2.7)

V_{i}=\{x\in K:|x-a_{i}|<|x-a_{j}|\ (j<i),\quad|x-a_{i}|\leq|x-a_{j}|\ (j>i)\},

and

(2.8)

\int_{K}\rho(x,\alpha)^{r}\,dP(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{V_{i}}|x-a_{i}|^{r}\,dP(x)\qquad(r>0).

More generally, for a finite nonnegative measurable distortion
\varrho

on
X\times A
, put
d_{i}(x)=\varrho(x,a_{i})
and

q_{\alpha}(x)=a_{i}
on
V_{i}
. Then
q_{\alpha}
is measurable and

\varrho(x,q_{\alpha}(x))=\min_{a\in\alpha}\varrho(x,a)
.
Finite-moment existence is part of the classical quantization theory

[
GL00
]
. The following argument treats a closed constraint directly and
also gives a compact set containing a minimizing codebook at every order
when the source is compact.

Lemma 7
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
be nonempty and closed. If
P
is a Borel probability with

\int|x|^{r}\,dP(x)<\infty
for some
0<r<\infty
, then

C_{n,r}(P;S)\neq\varnothing
for every
n\geq 1
.
If
K=\text{supp}{(P)}
is compact, then
C_{n,\infty}(P;S)\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

Finite order.
Fix
a_{0}\in S
. The inequality

|x-a_{0}|^{r}\leq 2^{\max\{r-1,0\}}(|x|^{r}+|a_{0}|^{r})

shows that
e_{n,r}^{r}
is finite. Choose
\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)
with

e_{n,r}^{r}\leq\int\rho(x,\alpha_{j})^{r}\,dP(x)\leq e_{n,r}^{r}+j^{-1}.

Represent
\alpha_{j}
by an
n
-tuple
(a_{j,1},\ldots,a_{j,n})
, repeating
points when necessary. We construct nested infinite index sets

I_{0}\supset I_{1}\supset\cdots\supset I_{n}
, beginning with
I_{0}=\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose that
I_{i-1}
has been chosen. If

(a_{j,i})_{j\in I_{i-1}}
has a bounded subsequence, the
Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem gives an infinite subset
I_{i}\subset I_{i-1}

and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
such that
a_{j,i}\to a_{i}
as
j\to\infty

through
I_{i}
. If there is no bounded subsequence, then for every

R^{\prime}>0
the set

\{j\in I_{i-1}:|a_{j,i}|\leq R^{\prime}\}

is finite; otherwise it would supply a bounded subsequence. In this
case set
I_{i}=I_{i-1}
, so
|a_{j,i}|\to\infty
along
I_{i}
.
Restriction to
I_{i}
preserves every convergence or divergence already
obtained for coordinates
1,\ldots,i-1
.

After the
n
th step, enumerate
I_{n}
increasingly as

j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, either

a_{j_{k},i}\to a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
or
|a_{j_{k},i}|\to\infty
.
Also
j_{k}^{-1}\to 0
, so the integrals of the corresponding codebooks
still tend to
e_{n,r}^{r}
. In the rest of the proof we denote this
subsequence again by
\alpha_{j}
and its coordinates by
a_{j,i}
.

At least one coordinate has a finite limit. Otherwise

\rho(x,\alpha_{j})\to\infty
for every fixed
x
, and Fatou’s lemma would give

\infty=\int\liminf_{j}\rho(x,\alpha_{j})^{r}\,dP(x)\leq\liminf_{j}\int\rho(x,\alpha_{j})^{r}\,dP(x)=e_{n,r}^{r}<\infty.

Let
I
be the nonempty set of coordinates with finite limits, denote those
limits by
a_{i}
, and put
\alpha=\{a_{i}:i\in I\}
. Closedness of
S
gives

\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)
. For each fixed
x
, the distances to the coordinates
outside
I
tend to infinity, while the remaining distances converge.
Taking a minimum over finitely many coordinates consequently gives

\rho(x,\alpha_{j})\to\rho(x,\alpha)
. A second application of Fatou yields

e_{n,r}^{r}\leq\int\rho(x,\alpha)^{r}\,dP(x)\leq\liminf_{j}\int\rho(x,\alpha_{j})^{r}\,dP(x)=e_{n,r}^{r}.

Thus
\alpha
is a minimizer.

Compact source and infinite order.
Choose
R\geq 1
with

K\subset\overline{B}(0,R)
, and put

(2.9)

C=S\cap\overline{B}(0,2R+|a_{0}|+1).

If
a\in S\setminus C
and
x\in K
, then

|x-a|\geq|a|-R>R+|a_{0}|\geq|x-a_{0}|.

Replace every center outside
C
by
a_{0}
. This does not increase the
pointwise distance or the number of centers. Hence the infimum defining

e_{n,\infty}
is the same when all centers are required to lie in
C
.
The set
C^{n}
is compact. If
\alpha=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
and

\beta=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\}
, then

(2.10)

|\rho(x,\alpha)-\rho(x,\beta)|\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|a_{i}-b_{i}|\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{D}).

Define

\Phi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\max_{x\in K}\min_{1\leq i\leq n}|x-a_{i}|\qquad((a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in C^{n}).

By (
2.10
),

|\Phi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})-\Phi(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})|\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|a_{i}-b_{i}|.

Thus
\Phi
is continuous on the nonempty compact set
C^{n}
.
Choose tuples
\mathbf{a}_{j}\in C^{n}
with

\Phi(\mathbf{a}_{j})\to\inf_{C^{n}}\Phi
. Compactness gives a
subsequence converging to
\mathbf{a}\in C^{n}
, and continuity yields

\Phi(\mathbf{a})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\Phi(\mathbf{a}_{j_{k}})=\inf_{C^{n}}\Phi=e_{n,\infty}.

The last equality follows because every codebook in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(C)
can
be represented by an
n
-tuple, repeating a point when necessary,
and every
n
-tuple defines a member of
\mathcal{A}_{n}(C)
.
The distinct coordinates of
\mathbf{a}
therefore form a minimizing
codebook. The same replacement is available for every
finite order when the source is compact.
∎

Lemma 8
.

For compact
K
and closed nonempty
S
,

(2.11)

e_{n,r}\downarrow L_{r}\quad(0<r<\infty),\qquad e_{n,\infty}\downarrow B.

Proof.

The admissible classes increase with
n
, so each error sequence is
nonincreasing. Every admissible codebook satisfies

\rho(x,\alpha)\geq b(x)
, giving the stated lower bounds.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Compactness of
E
gives a finite

\varepsilon
-net
\beta_{\varepsilon}\subset E
, i.e., a finite set with

E\subset\bigcup_{a\in\beta_{\varepsilon}}\overline{B}(a,\varepsilon).

For every
x\in K
, the set
\pi_{S}(x)
is nonempty by
Lemma
3
, so choose
p\in\pi_{S}(x)
.
Since
p\in E
, the displayed cover gives
a\in\beta_{\varepsilon}

such that
|p-a|\leq\varepsilon
. Then

(2.12)

b(x)\leq\rho(x,\beta_{\varepsilon})\leq|x-p|+|p-a|\leq b(x)+\varepsilon.

For
n\geq|\beta_{\varepsilon}|
this gives

L_{r}^{r}\leq e_{n,r}^{r}\leq\int(b+\varepsilon)^{r}\,dP,\qquad B\leq e_{n,\infty}\leq B+\varepsilon.

The functions
(b+\varepsilon)^{r}
converge to
b^{r}
and are bounded by

(B+1)^{r}
. Dominated convergence, followed by the continuity of the

r
th root, proves the finite-order limit. Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0

in the supremum estimate proves the infinite-order limit.
∎

2.4.
Optimal codebooks and dependence on the order

For nonempty compact sets
A
and
A^{\prime}
, we use

d_{H}(A,A^{\prime})=\max\{\sup_{a\in A}\rho(a,A^{\prime}),\ \sup_{a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}}\rho(a^{\prime},A)\}.

For each fixed integer
n\geq 1
, this metric gives the following infimum formula.

Proposition 9
.

For nonempty compact
K
and nonempty closed
S
,

(2.13)

e_{n,\infty}(K;S)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)}d_{H}(\alpha,K).

Proof.

For every
\alpha
,
\sup_{x\in K}\rho(x,\alpha)\leq d_{H}(\alpha,K)
, so the
left side is no greater than the right side. For the other direction, take
any
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)
and retain exactly those centers that are nearest
to at least one point of
K
. Call the retained set
\beta
. It is nonempty,
and
\rho(x,\beta)=\rho(x,\alpha)
for every
x\in K
. If
a\in\beta
,
choose
x_{a}\in K
with
|x_{a}-a|=\rho(x_{a},\alpha)
. Then

\rho(a,K)\leq|a-x_{a}|\leq\sup_{x\in K}\rho(x,\alpha).

Both directed terms in
d_{H}(\beta,K)
are bounded by this last supremum.
Therefore

\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)}d_{H}(\gamma,K)\leq d_{H}(\beta,K)\leq\sup_{x\in K}\rho(x,\alpha).

Taking the infimum over the original
\alpha
gives the remaining inequality.
∎

Proposition 10
.

In any of the existence settings of Lemma
7
, and
for
n\geq 2
,

(2.14)

e_{n,r}<e_{n-1,r}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad|\alpha|=n\ \hbox{for every }\alpha\in C_{n,r}(P;S).

This includes
r=\infty
for compact source. At finite order, strict decrease
also implies that every cell of an optimal codebook has positive mass.

Proof.

If strict decrease holds and an optimal
\alpha
has at most
n-1
points,
then
e_{n-1,r}\leq F_{r}(\alpha)=e_{n,r}
, a contradiction. Conversely, if

e_{n,r}=e_{n-1,r}
, an
(n-1)
-optimal codebook exists and is also

n
-optimal. It has at most
n-1
points.

At finite order, suppose an optimal codebook has a zero-mass cell
V_{i}
.
Removing
a_{i}
leaves the distance unchanged outside
V_{i}
, because every
point outside that cell has another nearest center. Hence removing
a_{i}

leaves the integral unchanged. This would imply

e_{n-1,r}=e_{n,r}
, contradicting strict decrease.
∎

Proposition 11
.

For fixed
n
and
0<r\leq s<\infty
,

(2.15)

e_{n,r}\leq e_{n,s},\qquad L_{r}\leq L_{s},\qquad\widehat{e}_{n,r}\leq\widehat{e}_{n,s}.

The inequality
e_{n,r}\leq e_{n,s}
also holds with
s=\infty
.

Proof.

First, put
p=r/s\in(0,1]
. For a nonnegative random variable
Z
with a
finite
s
th moment, Jensen’s inequality for the concave function
t^{p}
gives

\int Z^{r}\,dP=\int(Z^{s})^{p}\,dP\leq\left(\int Z^{s}\,dP\right)^{p}.

Taking the
r
th root yields
\|Z\|_{r}\leq\|Z\|_{s}
.
Apply this to
Z=\rho(\cdot,\alpha)
for each fixed admissible
\alpha
.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
\alpha
with

F_{s}(\alpha)\leq e_{n,s}+\varepsilon
. Then

e_{n,r}\leq F_{r}(\alpha)\leq F_{s}(\alpha)\leq e_{n,s}+\varepsilon
.
Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
proves the first comparison. Applying the
same norm inequality to
Z=b
proves the second. For
s=\infty
, use

\|Z\|_{r}\leq\sup_{K}Z
and the same admissible-codebook argument.

For the comparison of
\widehat{e}_{n,r}
and
\widehat{e}_{n,s}
,
the definitions give

(2.16)

\Delta_{n,r}=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)}\int_{K}\bigl(\rho(x,\alpha)^{r}-b(x)^{r}\bigr)\,dP(x).

Indeed, put
I_{r}
equal to the infimum on the right. For every

\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)
,

\int_{K}(\rho(x,\alpha)^{r}-b(x)^{r})\,dP(x)=F_{r}(\alpha)^{r}-L_{r}^{r}\geq e_{n,r}^{r}-L_{r}^{r}=\Delta_{n,r},\qquad I_{r}\geq\Delta_{n,r}.

For
\varepsilon>0
, choose
\alpha_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)

with
F_{r}(\alpha_{\varepsilon})<e_{n,r}+\varepsilon
. Then

I_{r}\leq F_{r}(\alpha_{\varepsilon})^{r}-L_{r}^{r}\leq(e_{n,r}+\varepsilon)^{r}-L_{r}^{r}\longrightarrow\Delta_{n,r}\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow 0).

Consequently
\Delta_{n,r}\leq I_{r}\leq\Delta_{n,r}
,
which proves (
2.16
).

For
u\geq v\geq 0
, subadditivity of
t^{p}
gives

u^{r}=((u^{s}-v^{s})+v^{s})^{p}\leq(u^{s}-v^{s})^{p}+v^{r}.

Apply this with
u=\rho(x,\alpha)
and
v=b(x)
. If

J_{t}(\alpha)=\int(\rho(x,\alpha)^{t}-b(x)^{t})\,dP(x)
, then Jensen’s
inequality gives

J_{r}(\alpha)\leq\int(\rho(x,\alpha)^{s}-b(x)^{s})^{p}\,dP(x)\leq J_{s}(\alpha)^{p}.

Choose
\alpha
with
J_{s}(\alpha)\leq\Delta_{n,s}+\varepsilon
. By
(
2.16
),

\Delta_{n,r}\leq J_{r}(\alpha)\leq(\Delta_{n,s}+\varepsilon)^{r/s}.

Let
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and take the
r
th root. This proves the last
inequality in (
2.15
), including zero excesses.
∎

Proposition 12
.

For fixed
n
and compact source,

(2.17)

\lim_{r\to\infty}e_{n,r}=e_{n,\infty},\qquad\lim_{r\to\infty}L_{r}=B,\qquad\lim_{r\to\infty}\widetilde{e}_{n,r}=e_{n,\infty}-B.

The finite limit
\lim_{r\to\infty}\widehat{e}_{n,r}
always exists and is at
most
e_{n,\infty}
. If
e_{n,\infty}>B
, then

(2.18)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\widehat{e}_{n,r}=e_{n,\infty}.

Proof.

Write
a=\lim_{r\to\infty}e_{n,r}
, which exists by monotonicity and satisfies

a\leq e_{n,\infty}<\infty
. Choose
r_{j}\to\infty
and minimizing codebooks

\alpha_{j}
in the common compact set
C
of (
1.12
).
Represent them by
n
-tuples and pass to a subsequence whose coordinates
converge. Their limit gives
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(C)
, with

d_{H}(\alpha_{j},\alpha)\to 0
.

Fix an integer
q\geq 1
. For all sufficiently large
j
,
r_{j}\geq q
, and

e_{n,r_{j}}=F_{r_{j}}(\alpha_{j})\geq F_{q}(\alpha_{j}).

Distance functions differ pointwise by at most
d_{H}(\alpha_{j},\alpha)
.
Minkowski’s inequality, applied in both directions, therefore gives

|F_{q}(\alpha_{j})-F_{q}(\alpha)|\leq\|\rho(\cdot,\alpha_{j})-\rho(\cdot,\alpha)\|_{L^{q}(P)}\leq d_{H}(\alpha_{j},\alpha)\longrightarrow 0.

Thus
a\geq F_{q}(\alpha)
for every integer
q\geq 1
.

For any nonnegative continuous
f
on
K
, let
M=\max_{K}f
.
The upper bound
\|f\|_{q}\leq M
is immediate. If
0<t<M
, continuity and

K=\text{supp}{(P)}
give
P(\{f>t\})>0
, whence

\|f\|_{q}\geq t\,P(\{f>t\})^{1/q}\longrightarrow t.

Taking
t\uparrow M
proves
\|f\|_{q}\to M
; the case
M=0
is direct.
Apply this first to

to obtain

a\geq\sup_{K}\rho(x,\alpha)\geq e_{n,\infty}.

This proves the first limit in (
2.17
). Applying the same
argument to

proves
L_{r}\to B
. Consequently,

\lim_{r\to\infty}\widetilde{e}_{n,r}=\lim_{r\to\infty}(e_{n,r}-L_{r})=e_{n,\infty}-B.

By Proposition
11
, the error
\widehat{e}_{n,r}
is nondecreasing in

,
and

. Its finite limit

∎

3.
Upper Asymptotics for Constrained Quantization Dimensions

3.1.
Comparison of admissible centers

The projection set and
E_{\mu}
lie in
S
, so their codebooks are admissible
for constrained quantization. The next comparison moves unrestricted
centers into
E_{\mu}
while controlling the increase in their error.

Lemma 14
.

If
E_{\mu}=\text{supp}{(\mu)}
is compact, then for
r>0
,

(3.1)

q_{n,r}(\mu)\leq q_{n,r}(\mu;E_{\mu})\leq 2q_{n,r}(\mu).

For any nonempty compact set
A
,

(3.2)

c_{n}(A)\leq c_{n}(A;A)\leq 2c_{n}(A).

Proof.

The first inequalities follow because restricting the codebooks can only
increase an infimum. For the reverse comparison, take an arbitrary

\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
and, for each
a\in\alpha
, choose
a^{\prime}\in E_{\mu}
with

|a-a^{\prime}|=\rho(a,E_{\mu})
. Let
\alpha^{\prime}
be the set of these chosen points.
It is nonempty and has at most
n
elements. For
y\in E_{\mu}
,

|y-a^{\prime}|\leq|y-a|+\rho(a,E_{\mu})\leq 2|y-a|.

Choose
a
nearest to
y
among the original centers. Then

\rho(y,\alpha^{\prime})\leq 2\rho(y,\alpha)
. Integration, then taking the
r
th root, gives

q_{n,r}(\mu;E_{\mu})\leq\left(\int\rho(y,\alpha^{\prime})^{r}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/r}\leq 2\left(\int\rho(y,\alpha)^{r}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/r}.

This holds for every original
\alpha
, so taking their infimum gives
(
3.1
). For (
3.2
), make the same
replacement in
A
and take the supremum over
y\in A
instead of an integral.
The constant
2
in the upper inequality of
(
3.2
) is sharp. Indeed, for
A=\{-1,1\}
,
the unrestricted center
0
gives
c_{1}(A)=1
, while either center
in
A
gives
c_{1}(A;A)=2
. Thus no smaller universal constant can
replace
2
in that inequality.
∎

3.2.
Proof of the projection error estimates

Proof of the finite-order estimates in Theorem
1
.

Fix
\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(E_{\mu})
. For every
x\in K
, choose a point of
\beta

nearest to
T(x)
. Since
|x-T(x)|=b(x)
, the triangle inequality gives

(3.3)

b(x)\leq\rho(x,\beta)\leq b(x)+\rho(T(x),\beta).

For
r\geq 1
, apply Minkowski to the nonnegative functions
b
and

g_{\beta}(x)=\rho(T(x),\beta)
. The pushforward identity gives

\displaystyle F_{r}(\beta)

\displaystyle\leq\|b+g_{\beta}\|_{L^{r}(P)}

\displaystyle\leq\|b\|_{L^{r}(P)}+\|g_{\beta}\|_{L^{r}(P)}

\displaystyle=L_{r}+\left(\int_{E_{\mu}}\rho(y,\beta)^{r}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/r}.

Since
\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(E_{\mu})\subset\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)
, the definition

e_{n,r}(P;S)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(S)}F_{r}(\alpha)
gives

e_{n,r}\leq F_{r}(\beta)
. Subtracting

L_{r}
and taking the infimum over
\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(E_{\mu})
proves the first
inequality in (
1.6
). Its second inequality is
Lemma
14
.

To estimate
\Delta_{n,r}=e_{n,r}^{r}-L_{r}^{r}
, observe that
T(x)
and all centers of
\beta

belong to
E
, so
g_{\beta}(x)\leq\text{diam}E
and
b(x)+g_{\beta}(x)<M_{0}
.
For
r\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho(x,\beta)^{r}-b(x)^{r}

\displaystyle\leq(b(x)+g_{\beta}(x))^{r}-b(x)^{r}

\displaystyle=r\int_{b(x)}^{b(x)+g_{\beta}(x)}t^{r-1}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq rM_{0}^{r-1}g_{\beta}(x).

By admissibility, integration, and (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,r}

\displaystyle\leq F_{r}(\beta)^{r}-L_{r}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq rM_{0}^{r-1}\int_{K}\rho(T(x),\beta)\,dP(x)

\displaystyle=rM_{0}^{r-1}\int_{E_{\mu}}\rho(y,\beta)\,d\mu(y).

Taking the infimum over
\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(E_{\mu})
gives

\Delta_{n,r}\leq rM_{0}^{r-1}q_{n,1}(\mu;E_{\mu})\leq 2rM_{0}^{r-1}q_{n,1}(\mu),

where the last step is (
3.1
) at order
1
.
This proves (
1.7
).

3.3.
Box dimension bounds

For a bounded set

be the least number of closed balls of

\underline{\dim}_{B}A=\liminf_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\log N_{t}(A)}{-\log t},\qquad\overline{\dim}_{B}A=\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\log N_{t}(A)}{-\log t}.

Every cover of a set also covers each of its subsets, so

A\subset A^{\prime}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N_{t}(A)\leq N_{t}(A^{\prime})\qquad(t>0).

Lemma 15

5.1.

\Delta_{n,r}\geq\Lambda_{r,F}n^{-\beta/d},\qquad\widetilde{e}_{n,r}\geq\frac{\Lambda_{r,F}}{rH^{r-1}}n^{-\beta/d},\qquad\widehat{e}_{n,r}\geq\Lambda_{r,F}^{1/r}n^{-\beta/(rd)}.

Consequently
\underline{\widetilde{\dim}}_{Q}^{r}{(P;S)}\geq d/\beta
and

\underline{\widehat{\dim}}_{Q}^{r}{(P;S)}\geq rd/\beta
.

Proof.

For
x\in F
and
a\in A
,
|x-a|\geq b(x)\geq b_{0}
, so

\varrho_{r}(x,a):=|x-a|^{r}-b(x)^{r}=r\int_{b(x)}^{|x-a|}u^{r-1}\,du\geq\lambda_{r}|a-T(x)|^{\beta}.

Use
(F,\mathcal{B}(F))
as source space and
(A,\mathcal{B}(A))
as reproduction space. Both the source law and the reference measure are

P_{F}=P|_{F}/m
. The distortion
\varrho_{r}
is nonnegative and Borel.
The parameters
(p,k,m,c,\delta_{0})
in
[
RKB23
, Theorem 4.1]
are

\left(1,\beta,d,c_{\mathrm{RKB}},\infty\right),\qquad c_{\mathrm{RKB}}=(M/m)\lambda_{r}^{-d/\beta}.

The reference measure is finite and
dP_{F}/dP_{F}=1
, with
L^{\infty}(P_{F})
norm one. For
a\in A
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle P_{F}\{x:\varrho_{r}(x,a)^{1/\beta}<t\}

\displaystyle\leq P_{F}\{x:|T(x)-a|<\lambda_{r}^{-1/\beta}t\}

\displaystyle\leq m^{-1}\mu(B(a,\lambda_{r}^{-1/\beta}t))\leq c_{\mathrm{RKB}}t^{d}.

Thus the reference measure is
\varrho_{r}^{1/\beta}
-subregular of dimension
d
at every scale. For
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(A)
, the convention in
Section
2.3
gives a measurable
map
q_{\alpha}:F\to\alpha
with

\varrho_{r}(x,q_{\alpha}(x))=\min_{a\in\alpha}\varrho_{r}(x,a)
.
The cited theorem applied to this quantizer gives

\int_{F}\min_{a\in\alpha}\varrho_{r}(x,a)\,dP_{F}(x)\geq c_{\mathrm{RKB}}^{-\beta/d}\frac{d}{d+\beta}n^{-\beta/d}.

For each such
\alpha
, the integrand
\operatorname{dist}(x,\alpha)^{r}-b(x)^{r}
is nonnegative on
K
. Therefore

\displaystyle F_{r}(\alpha)^{r}-L_{r}^{r}

\displaystyle\geq m\int_{F}\min_{a\in\alpha}\varrho_{r}(x,a)\,dP_{F}(x)

\displaystyle\geq mc_{\mathrm{RKB}}^{-\beta/d}\frac{d}{d+\beta}n^{-\beta/d}=\Lambda_{r,F}n^{-\beta/d}.

For the optimal codebook assumed in the lemma,

F_{r}(\alpha_{n})=e_{n,r}
, so the first part gives the bound for
\Delta_{n,r}
.
Since
L_{r}^{r}\geq mb_{0}^{r}>0
and
L_{r}\leq e_{n,r}\leq H
,

\Delta_{n,r}=r\int_{L_{r}}^{e_{n,r}}u^{r-1}\,du\leq rH^{r-1}(e_{n,r}-L_{r}).

Thus

\widetilde{e}_{n,r}\geq\frac{\Delta_{n,r}}{rH^{r-1}}\geq\frac{\Lambda_{r,F}}{rH^{r-1}}n^{-\beta/d},\qquad\widehat{e}_{n,r}=\Delta_{n,r}^{1/r}\geq\Lambda_{r,F}^{1/r}n^{-\beta/(rd)}.

Lemma
8
gives

\widetilde{e}_{n,r}\downarrow 0
and
\widehat{e}_{n,r}\downarrow 0
.
Applying Lemma
33
(iii) with

\tau=\beta/d
and
\tau=\beta/(rd)
, respectively, yields

\underline{\widetilde{\dim}}_{Q}^{r}{(P;S)}\geq d/\beta,\qquad\underline{\widehat{\dim}}_{Q}^{r}{(P;S)}\geq rd/\beta.

∎

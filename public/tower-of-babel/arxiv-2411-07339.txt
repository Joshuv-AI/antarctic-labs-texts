Synergistic PET/CT Reconstruction Using a Joint Generative Model
Noel Jeffrey Pinton, Alexandre Bousse, Zhihan Wang, Catherine Cheze-Le-Rest, Voichita Maxim, Claude Comtat, Florent Sureau, Dimitris Visvikis

Abstract




Abstract

We propose in this work a framework for synergistic positron emission tomography/computed tomography reconstruction using a joint generative model as a penalty. We use a synergistic penalty function that promotes positron emission tomography/computed tomography pairs that are likely to occur together. The synergistic penalty function is based on a generative model, namely beta-variational autoencoder. The model generates a positron emission tomography/computed tomography image pair from the same latent variable which contains the information that is shared between the two modalities.

This sharing of inter-modal information can help reduce noise during reconstruction. Our result shows that our method was able to utilize the information between two modalities. The proposed method was able to outperform individually reconstructed images of positron emission tomography (i.e., by maximum likelihood expectation maximization) and computed tomography (i.e., by weighted least squares) in terms of peak signal-to-noise ratio. Future work will focus on optimizing the parameters of the beta-variational autoencoder network and further exploration of other generative network models.

Introduction

Positron emission tomography/computed tomography hybrid imaging systems have been used since the 2000’s. On one hand, positron emission tomography is an imaging modality used to observe and quantify molecular-level activities inside tissue through radioactive tracers, while on the other hand computed tomography is an imaging technique that uses X-rays to produce detailed structural images of the body. In order to get clearer images, both modalities use high dose of ionizing radiation which is detrimental to the health of certain patients especially children. Therefore reducing the dosage of used ionizing radiation is crucial, but it will affect the signal-to-noise ratio of the reconstructed images.

Current multi-modal imaging reconstruction techniques process each modality separately. However, it is possible to exploit inter-modality information which could help reduce noise during reconstruction by combining functional positron emission tomography and structural computed tomography images. This approach could ultimately lead to reduction of dosage in patient imaging.
The use of deep-learning in an unrolled model-based iterative reconstruction algorithm for synergistic positron emission tomography/magnetic resonance reconstruction
[
1
]
has shown improved performance compared to independent conventional reconstruction methods. Although this approach is promising, the training is computationally expensive and the architecture depends on the scanners system projectors/back-projectors.

In this work, we propose a synergistic penalty function which uses generated positron emission tomography and computed tomography from the same latent variable which is trained on a beta-variational autoencoder. As the shared information is contained in the penalty term, the proposed approach does not depend on the system (i.e. projector/back-projectors are not part of the training).

Method

2.1

Conventional PET/CT Reconstruction

The linear X-ray attenuation and activity image are respectively denoted
\bm{\mu}=[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{J}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{J}_{+}
and
\bm{\lambda}=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{J}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{J}_{+}
,
J
being be the number of pixels (or voxels) in the image. In the following we briefly introduce the model-based iterative reconstruction settings for computed tomography and positron emission tomography.

We use a standard and monochromatic model for computed tomography. The transmission measurement are collected along
I
rays and stored in a vector
\bm{y}=[y_{1},\dots,y_{I}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
. Given the attenuation
\bm{\mu}
and ignoring background events, the expected number of detected X-ray photons along each ray
i=1,\dots,I
is given by the Beer-Lambert law as

\bar{y}_{i}(\bm{\mu})=h\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-[\bm{A}\bm{\mu}]_{i}}

(1)

where
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{I\times J}
is a discrete line integral operator such that
[\bm{A}]_{i,j}
denotes the contribution of pixel
j
to ray
i
, and
h
is the X-ray intensity. Assuming that the
I
measurements
\{y_{i}\}
are independent and follow a Poisson distribution centered on
\{\bar{y}_{i}(\mu)\}
, model-based iterative reconstruction of the attenuation image
\bm{\mu}
can be achieved by solving the following optimization problem with an iterative algorithm
[
2
]
:

\hat{\bm{\mu}}\in\operatornamewithlimits{arg\,min}_{\bm{\mu}\geq\bm{0}}\,L_{\mathrm{CT}}(\bm{\mu})+\alpha R_{\mathrm{CT}}(\bm{\mu})

(2)

where the loss function
L_{\mathrm{CT}}
is defined ad
L_{\mathrm{CT}}(\bm{\mu})=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{A}\bm{\mu}-\bm{b}\|^{2}_{\bm{W}}
with
\bm{b}=-\log\bm{y}/h\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
being the vector of approximated line integrals and
\bm{W}=\mathrm{diag}\{\bm{y}\}\in\mathbb{N}^{I\times I}
being a diagonal matrix of statistical weights,
R_{\mathrm{CT}}
is a penalty function or regularizer, generally convex, that promotes desired image properties (in general piece-wise smoothness) while controlling the noise, and
\alpha>0
is a weight.

The positron emission tomography data are collected along
K
lines of response defined by
K
pairs of detectors. The emission data are stored in a vector
\bm{g}\in\mathbb{N}^{K}
. Given the activity image
\bm{\lambda}
and again ignoring background events, the expected number of detected
\gamma
-photon pairs along line of response
K
is

\bar{g}_{k}(\bm{\lambda})=\tau[\bm{P}\bm{\lambda}]_{k}

(3)

where
\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times J}
is the positron emission tomography system matrix, i.e.,
[\bm{P}]_{k,j}
is the probability that a emission at pixel
j
is detected along line of response
k
(accounting for attenuation), and
\tau
is the acquisition time. Assuming that the
K
measurements
\{g_{k}\}
are independent and follow a Poisson distribution centered on
\{\bar{g}_{k}(\bm{\lambda})\}
, model-based iterative reconstruction of the activity image can be achieved by solving the following penalized maximum likelihood problem iteratively:

\hat{\bm{\lambda}}\in\operatornamewithlimits{arg\,min}_{\bm{\lambda}\geq\bm{0}}\,L_{\mathrm{PET}}(\bm{\lambda})+\delta R_{\mathrm{PET}}(\bm{\lambda})

(4)

where the loss function
L_{\mathrm{PET}}
is the negative Poisson log-likelihood defined as
L_{\mathrm{PET}}(\bm{\lambda})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}-g_{k}\log\bar{g}_{k}(\bm{\lambda})+\bar{g}_{k}(\bm{\lambda})
and
R_{\mathrm{PET}}
and
\delta
are same as
R_{\mathrm{CT}}
and
\alpha
in (
2
).

2.2

Synergistic PET/CT Reconstruction Using a Joint Generative Model

\bm{\mu}
and
\bm{\lambda}
can be simultaneously reconstructed by solving the following joint estimation problem:

(\hat{\bm{\mu}},\hat{\bm{\lambda}})\in\operatornamewithlimits{arg\,min}_{\bm{\mu},\bm{\lambda}\geq\bm{0}}\,L_{\mathrm{CT}}(\bm{\mu})+L_{\mathrm{PET}}(\bm{\lambda})+\gamma R_{\mathrm{syn}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\lambda})

(5)

where
\gamma>0
and
R_{\mathrm{syn}}
is a
synergistic
penalty function that promotes structural and/or functional correlations between the multiple images, such as parallel levelsets
[
3
]
and total nuclear variation
[
4
]
.

Instead of using a handcrafted synergistic penalty function,
R_{\mathrm{syn}}
may be trained such that
R_{\mathrm{syn}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\lambda})\approx 0
if
\bm{\mu}
and
\bm{\lambda}
are images that are plausible not only individually, but also
together
. In this work we propose the following penalty inspired by previous work from
[
5
]

[
5
]
:

\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{syn}}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})

\displaystyle=\min_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{P}}\,\eta\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{f}_{\mathrm{PET}}(\bm{z})-\bm{\lambda}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\eta)\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{f}_{\mathrm{CT}}(\bm{z})-\bm{\mu}\|^{2}

(6)

where
\bm{f}_{\mathrm{CT}}
and
\bm{f}_{\mathrm{PET}}
are the decoders of the trained generative model that generate computed tomography and positron emission tomography images from the same latent variable
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{P}
and
\eta\in[0,1]
. To summarize (
6
),
R_{\mathrm{syn}}(\bm{\mu},\bm{\lambda})\approx 0
if
\bm{\mu}
and
\bm{\lambda}
are approximately generated from the same latent variable
\bm{z}
. This approach is somehow a generalization of coupled dictionary learning for multi-modal imaging (see for example
[
6
]
) where we replaced the dictionaries by generative models. Note that a penalty can be added in (
6
) to control the noise. In this work we used beta-variational autoencoders for
\bm{f}_{\mathrm{CT}}
and
\bm{f}_{\mathrm{PET}}
.

]
) ,

(modified maximum likelihood expectation maximization
[
8
]
) and

(penalized weighted least squares

]

2.3

Generative Model Network Architecture

For combining functional positron emission tomography and structural computed tomography images, we created a multi-branch beta-variational autoencoder (Figure
1
) architecture. The network features separate encoders and decoders for positron emission tomography and computed tomography. The encoders and decoders consist 5-layer networks with ReLU activation. We train the networks using the following loss function as described in
[
9
]
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{loss}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi}(\bm{z}|\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})}[\log p_{\theta}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}\mid\bm{z})]

\displaystyle-\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{\phi}(\bm{z}|\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})\,\,\|\,\,p(\bm{z}))

(7)

where
\beta
is the regularization coefficient that constrains the capacity of the latent information channel
\bm{z}
. The first term is the log-likelihood of the observed data
p_{\theta}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu}|\bm{z})
averaged over the latent variable
\bm{z}
with distribution
q_{\phi}(\bm{z}|\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})
, also referred to as the cross-entropy. The second term is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the posterior variational approximation
q_{\phi}(\bm{z}|\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})
and the prior distribution
p(\bm{z})
, which is selected to be a standard Gaussian distribution.

2.4

Network Training and Implementation

The models were trained using a collection of positron emission tomography/computed tomography lung image pair
(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})
of 117 patients from CHU Poitiers, Poitiers, France. The images are
256\times 256\times 128
, each of the 128 slice pairs taken independently, resulting in
117\times 128=14,976
positron emission tomography/computed tomography image pairs for training. The positron emission tomography images were clipped at
10^{5}
Bq/mL in order to remove outlier. Both positron emission tomography and computed tomography images were scaled between 0 and 1 for training. During the final reconstruction, the positron emission tomography and computed tomography images are returned to their original domain values. A fixed value of
\beta
at
100
of the loss function (
7
) was used for this study. We used Tensorflow v2.4 and Keras for the network implementation and training. The Adam Algorithm
[
10
]
was used with a learning rate of 0.0001 for 300 epochs and a batch size of 128 on a NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU.
The gradients were computed using the
GradientTape
function from Tensorflow for the L-BFGS optimization
[
7
]
used in the minimization over
\bm{z}
in (
6
), which uses “automatic differentiation”, breaking complex gradient calculation into simpler gradient calculations through chain rules.

4

Discussion

In this paper, we proposed a synergistic positron emission tomography/computed tomography reconstruction which utilizes a deep penalty method using a generative neural network beta-variational autoencoder. The results showed that the technique outperforms traditional positron emission tomography-only and computed tomography-only reconstruction methods for both modalities. We have also demonstrated that the positron emission tomography and computed tomography images were able to exploit the inter-modality information between each other. The use of beta-variational autoencoder generators were effective, but fine tuning the hyperparameters are needed in order to produce clearer images. Other suggestions include the tuning of the
\beta
variable in Equation (
7
) and the use of attention modules in the latent space. The possibility of using other generative models such as generative adversarial network-based models and diffusion-based models is also open. Furthermore, the addition of weights in the first two terms of Equation
5
may improve the results by optimizing the contribution of each term to the final output. Finally, we ignored attenuation factors in the positron emission tomography reconstruction. In principle they should be computed from a first reconstruction of the attenuation (non synergistic). Since they correspond to line integrals, they do not suffer from noise amplification.

5

Conclusion

We proposed in this work a framework for synergistic positron emission tomography/computed tomography reconstruction using a generative neural network as penalty. The decoders of the trained beta-variational autoencoder were able to effectively promote correlations between the two modalities. Evaluations using real patient dataset indicated that the proposed framework was able to exploit the inter-modal information between the two modalities which ultimately led to improved image quality.

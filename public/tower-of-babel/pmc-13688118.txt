Novel use of an endoscopic suturing device for rectal foreign body retrieval
Authors: Sergey V. Kantsevoy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and AimsEndoscopic removal of gastrointestinal tract foreign bodies could be difficult when the foreign body significantly exceeds the size of the available endoscopic extraction devices (snares, nets, etc). We present successful use of an endoscopic suturing device for retrieval of a large rectal foreign body not amenable to retrieval by traditional endoscopic extraction devices.MethodsThe patient was a 45-year-old biological male transitioning to female who presented to the emergency department with a self-reported 8-inch rectal foreign body. Extraction with the patient under anesthesia was unsuccessful, and to avoid surgery, a flexible sigmoidoscopy was attempted.ResultsAttempts to remove the rectal foreign body using traditional retrieval devices were unsuccessful because of a large diameter and very slippery surface of the silicone sex toy. After 11 minutes of unsuccessful attempts, we inserted the OverStitch endoscopic suturing device (Boston Scientific, Marlborough, Mass, USA) into the rectum. We pierced the suturing arm of the device through the foreign body and were able to retrieve it from the rectum in 5 minutes.ConclusionsThis case demonstrates unexpected value of an endoscopic suturing device for endoscopic removal of a large foreign body not amenable to extraction with traditional dedicated endoscopic devices. Use of an endoscopic suturing device enabled successful removal of the difficult foreign body and eliminated the need for urgent surgery.

Endoscopic removal of gastrointestinal tract foreign bodies could be difficult when the size of the foreign body significantly exceeds the size of the available endoscopic retrieval devices (snares, retrieval nets, etc). There is a growing incidence of emergency department (ED) visits for removal of entrapped rectal sexual stimulation devices.1, 2, 3, 4 Retrieval of the large subjects is especially challenging when their diameter is much bigger than the opening of the gastrointestinal tract orifice. Failure of manual and endoscopic removal results in emergency laparotomy with or without bowel resection and colostomy in 9.4% to 74.3% of patients.3, 4, 5, 6

We present a 45-year-old biological male transitioning to female who presented to our ED with a large foreign body. He/she reported an 8-inch-long sex toy placed deep inside the rectum. The patient stayed at home for 36 hours until developing rectal pain rated 7 out of 10 that did not respond to ibuprofen and was brought to the ED. Digital rectal examination on admission confirmed a rectal foreign body, but it was barely reachable manually. Surgical consult was called, and extraction with the patient under anesthesia was attempted in the ED and was unsuccessful and only resulted in tearing off a small piece of the device, rectal bleeding, and worsening of the rectal pain. At this point, the surgeon recommended laparotomy to extract the foreign body. The patient did not want an operation, and as a last resort, urgent flexible sigmoidoscopy was performed in the endoscopy unit with the patient under conscious sedation with a single-channel endoscope and demonstrated a large brown silicone sex toy occupying the entire lumen of the rectum and distal sigmoid colon (Fig. 1, Video 1, available online at www.videogie.org). Attempts to grab it with the largest (33-mm) endoscopic snare, retrieval nets, and grasping forceps were not successful because of the large size of the foreign body and its very slippery surface (Figs. 2 and 3, Video 1, available online at www.videogie.org).Figure 1Large foreign body inside the rectum.Figure 2Large endoscopic snare slipping off the foreign body.Figure 3Food bolus net not able to grab the foreign body.

In this desperate situation, after 11 minutes spent on unsuccessful attempts to remove the foreign body with dedicated extraction devices, the OverStitch (Boston Scientific, Marlborough, Mass, USA) endoscopic suturing device mounted on a double-channel endoscope was inserted into the rectum. The suturing arm of the suturing device was open and closed, piercing through the foreign body (Fig. 4), and the endoscope was pulled out of the rectum. However, even after manual dilation of the anal sphincter, the diameter of the foreign body far exceeded the rectal opening, and the suturing arm tore through the silicone. The suturing device was reinserted into the rectum, and the suturing arm was once again closed through the foreign body. The endoscope was pulled to the dentate line, and under direct visualization, a large hemostatic clamp was inserted into the rectum to grab the other side of the foreign body. With these 2 instruments, the foreign body was removed (Fig. 5) and sent for pathologic examination (Figs. 6 and 7). The pathologist described it as a “9.5- × 6.5- × 4.5- up to 6.5-cm brown silicone ‘dildo,’ which is disrupted at the proximal end.” The patient reported no complaints after the procedure and was discharged home the next morning after brief overnight observation.

In conclusion, this case demonstrates the unexpected value of an endoscopic suturing device for endoscopic removal of a large foreign body not amenable to extraction with traditional dedicated endoscopic devices. Use of an endoscopic suturing device enabled successful removal of the difficult foreign body and eliminated the need for urgent surgery.

Atomically resolved wavefunction of integer and fractional quantum anomalous Hall states in twisted bilayer graphene
Authors: Yingzhuo Han, Junnan Jiang, Ziyi Tian, Si-yu Li, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Yi Zhang, Yuhang Jiang, Jinhai Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMoiré superlattices formed by stacking two-dimensional crystals offer a powerful platform for exploring strongly correlated and topological quantum phenomena. However, the absence of microscopic insights has long hindered a comprehensive understanding of their underlying nature and formation mechanisms. In this study, we utilize low-temperature, gate-tunable scanning tunneling microscopy and spectroscopy to directly visualize correlated and topological phases in hBN-aligned magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene. At zero magnetic field, well-defined Coulomb-gap features are observed at filling factors ν = +2 and + 3, indicative of two robust correlated insulating states. The ν = +3 state evolves with magnetic field, indicating a quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) insulator with Chern number C = +1. High-resolution imaging reveals a sublattice asymmetry within AA-stacked regions, providing direct evidence of valley polarization, while its magnetic hysteresis suggests a connection to orbital magnetism. At higher fields, a sequence of additional Chern and fractional Chern insulating states emerges, with their distinct spatial sublattice asymmetry patterns closely associated with the occupied valley. These results establish a direct microscopic correlation among sublattice polarization, valley occupation, and topological order in the moiré superlattice, offering a new spatially resolved perspective on the wavefunctions of correlated and topological ground states.Subject terms: Electronic properties and devices, Electronic properties and materials

Introduction

Moiré superlattices, created by stacking two-dimensional (2D) crystals with a small twist angle, have established a powerful platform for exploring correlated and topological quantum states1,2. Magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG) is a prominent example, where Coulomb interactions dominate over the kinetic energy of electrons within nearly flat bands, leading to a wide range of emergent phenomena such as correlated insulators3–7, unconventional superconductivity8–11, and orbital magnetism12,13. Further exotic quantum phases emerge when MATBG is aligned with hexagonal boron nitride (hBN). The alignment induces an additional moiré potential that breaks sublattice symmetry and reconstructs the electronic band structure, thereby stabilizing valley-polarized and topological ground states14. Experiments on hBN-aligned MATBG have indeed observed the quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) effects15, characterized by quantized Hall resistance at zero magnetic field. While theoretically attributed to interaction-driven valley polarization, direct experimental visualization of the underlying ground-state wavefunction, such as the spatial patterns of valley polarization, remains elusive. Here, using low-temperature, gate-tunable STM/STS, we directly visualize these microscopic signatures in the QAH state of hBN-aligned MATBG.

Scanning tunneling microscopy and spectroscopy (STM/STS), with its atomic-scale resolution of the local density of states (LDOS), offers a powerful approach to probe such correlated and topological phases16–21. Our high-resolution imaging reveals sublattice asymmetry in AA-stacked regions underlying the QAH insulating phase. This sublattice asymmetry shows switchable and hysteresis upon the magnetic field reversal, directly linking it to orbital magnetism and valley polarization as reported by transport measurements. At higher magnetic fields, additional insulating gaps emerge at both integer and fractional fillings, corresponding to Chern insulators and fractional Chern insulators. Furthermore, we find sublattice symmetry breaking in a fractional Chern insulator state, which can be attributed to wavefunction polarization inherited from its parent integer Chern insulator. Our results establish a direct microscopic connection between sublattice polarization, valley occupation, and topological order.

Results

Geometric and electronic structure of hBN-aligned MATBG

As illustrated in Fig. 1a, we utilize a gate-tunable STM setup to investigate hBN aligned MATBG. The sample comprises two monolayer graphene sheets twisted at approximately 1.14°, stacked on an hBN substrate which rests on a Si/SiO₂ wafer serving as a back gate. The bottom graphene layer is aligned with the hBN at approximately 0.52°, generating a G-hBN moiré pattern with a period of ~12.4 nm. This period matches that of the G-G moiré, yielding a commensurate super-moiré structure, as seen in the STM topography image (Fig. 1b).

We first characterize the electronic structure at zero magnetic field to identify the QAH state. Figure 1c presents a dI/dV intensity plot as a function of gate voltage (Vg) and bias voltage (Vb), acquired in an AA region. An energy gap emerges at the charge neutrality point (CNP), where the dI/dV intensity is completely suppressed (see the single dI/dV spectrum in the bottom panel of Fig. 1d). This behavior is attributed to the alignment with the hBN substrate14,22–26. The two narrow peaks flanking the gap are associated with the conduction and valence flat bands (CFB and VFB). Given the fourfold degeneracy (originating from spin and valley degrees of freedom) of these bands27–29, we define the filling factor as ν = +4 when the CFB is fully occupied and ν = -4 when the VFB is completely empty. All intermediate filling factors can be determined accordingly based on this reference.

In the gate-dependent spectra, correlated phases emerge at integer fillings of the flat bands. For example, near ν = +2, the CFB splits into two subbands of similar spectral weight, labeled CFB⁻ and CFB+(Fig. 1d). Additionally, a distinct charge gap opens at the Fermi level (EF), as indicated by the red arrow, signaling an insulating state. This gap closes at partial fillings, such as ν = +2.5, but reopens at ν = +3, where the CFB undergoes further splitting, and the dI/dV intensity at EF vanishes, indicating a reentrant insulating phase. Furthermore, the high-resolution gate-dependent dI/dV intensity map shown in Fig. 1e reveals a Coulomb diamond structure associated with the insulating state, a hallmark of Coulomb interaction or blockade behavior30. These results confirm the formation of interaction-driven correlated insulating states at ν = +2 and +3, in accordance with prior transport and STM measurements11,12,15.

QAH insulator and wavefunction polarization

As revealed by transport measurements12,15,31, the insulating states at integer fillings can be topologically nontrivial, resulting in a Chern insulating phase or the QAH effect. To probe the topological nature of the insulating states at v = +2 and v = +3, we apply an out-of-plane magnetic field B (0-4 T) and monitor the evolution of their Coulomb gaps. As shown in Fig. 2a, b, their responses are distinct: the gap at v = +2 remains pinned at a constant filling, whereas the gap at v = +3 shifts linearly toward higher filling with increasing B. This contrasting behavior identifies the ν = +3 state as topologically nontrivial, possessing a nonzero Chern number. The linear dependence of the gap’s associated filling factor on the magnetic field is consistent with the Chern insulator scenario in moiré graphene systems32. Applying the Diophantine equation20, ν = Cϕ/ϕ0 + s, where C denotes the Chern number and s represents the band index, we determine a Chern number of C = +1 for this state, establishing the emergence of a QAH insulator at ν = +3. Compared with previous STM studies on non-aligned MATBG systems20,21, where interaction-driven topological phases mainly emerge under much stronger external magnetic fields, the QAH state observed here arises under distinct conditions due to the intentional graphene–hBN alignment, which explicitly breaks the C₂ sublattice symmetry. Such symmetry breaking has been theoretically proposed to generate finite valley Chern numbers and stabilize zero-field QAH states in MATBG14,33.

We next examine the wavefunction characteristics of this QAH state. Within the single-particle physics, the atomic-resolution STM image of an AA region is expected to exhibit a honeycomb structure, reflecting equal wavefunction weight on A- and B-sublattice19. In the following measurements, the atomic-resolution STM images were acquired near the center of the AA regions using bias voltages of −20 mV, corresponding to an energy range close to the correlation gap. Our atomically resolved images acquired in the QAH state at v = +3 (Fig. 2c, B = 0 T) reveal pronounced sublattice polarization. Specifically, the A-sublattice (green circles) shows distinct contrast from the B-sublattice (orange circles), resulting in a triangular lattice pattern, different from the AA region at CNP, which preserves a honeycomb lattice (inset in Fig. 2c). This clear distinction indicates a polarized wavefunction for the QAH state, characterized by an enhanced local intensity on one sublattice34–36.

The wavefunction polarization in the QAH phase is understood within the framework of interaction-driven valley polarization27. In MATBG, the two flat bands are fourfold degenerate, with each degenerate subband carrying a Chern number of C = +1 or −1. At v = +3, the ground state can be described as having three occupied subbands (two from one valley and one from the other), resulting in a valley polarized Chern insulator with a net Ctot = +1 or −1, depending on which valley hosts the doubly occupied electronic flavor12,15, as illustrated in Fig. 2d.

This valley-polarized scenario is supported by our Hartree–Fock calculations for the insulating state at filling v = +3. Electron-electron interaction opens a gap at EF, giving rise to a correlated insulator at v = +3 filling. At this filling, the interaction-induced gap develops only for a single spin–valley flavor (spin-down in the K’ valley) as highlighted by the red (C = +1) and blue (C = −1) solid bands in Fig. 2e. The remaining flavors (spin-up in the K’ valley and both spin species in the K valley) exhibit only small single-particle gaps originating from graphene–hBN alignment, shown as dashed bands in Fig. 2e. These bands are fully occupied and therefore do not contribute to the total Chern number. Consequently, the system realizes a spin- and valley-polarized state with a net Chern number C = +1, in which both the orbital and spin magnetic moments are aligned with the external magnetic field B. This filling configuration gives rise to the C = +1 Chern insulator state, in agreement with our experimental observations.

For such a valley-polarized correlated insulating state, the calculated LDOS on the A- and B-sublattice is no longer equivalent. The most pronounced imbalance (nA-nB > 0) occurs at the AA-stacking regions (Fig. 2g), in excellent agreement with our observed sublattice symmetry breaking (higher intensity on the A-sublattice). This consistency suggests the valley-polarized origin of the QAH states and provides strong experimental constraints on the underlying ground-state wavefunction. Notably, according to our theoretical calculations, the system possesses four nearly degenerate ground states in energy (Suppl. Fig. 1). Based on the experimentally measured sublattice asymmetry and Chern number, our observations are most consistent with the scenario described above. The specific state we observe is likely selected by the sublattice-symmetry-breaking influence of the hBN substrate.

Experimentally, the QAH state exhibits hysteretic and sign-reversing quantized Hall resistance upon the magnetic field direction reversal, a hallmark of orbital ferromagnetism12,15. Tracking the dI/dV intensity at EF as a function of filling and magnetic field (Fig. 2i), we observe switching of the Chern number from C = +1 to C = -1 at v = +3 upon magnetic field reversal. Intriguingly, by monitoring the sublattice polarization, we can directly visualize this Chern number switching and identify the corresponding ground states (Fig. 2c). At B = -0.3 T, the ground-state wavefunction is polarized on the A-sublattice (green circles) in AA regions. This polarization persists as the field is reduced to B = 0 and +0.05 T. Upon further increasing the field beyond the coercive field to B = +0.3 T, the polarization switches to the B-sublattice (orange circles). When the field is subsequently reduced back to 0 and -0.05 T, the polarization remains on the B-sublattice, revealing microscopic magnetic hysteresis. Additional field-dependent imaging (corresponding images of the red and blue dashed circles in Fig. 2c are provided in the Suppl. Fig. 2) indicates the polarization switches between +0.05 T and +0.1 T, yielding a coercive field Bc within this range, consistent with the value as reported in transport measurements15. Thus, the microscopic hysteresis of wavefunction polarization mirrors the hysteresis in transverse resistance, establishing a direct connection between the local electronic structure and the switched QAH states. Although the STM measurements do not directly access the microscopic switching process itself, they resolve the atomic-scale electronic structure of the resulting ordered states after the switching has occurred.

We now discuss the ground-state switching associated with changes in the Chern number. Theoretically, the Chern number could be expressed as C ∝ 〈σz〉 × τ, where 〈σz〉 is the expectation value of the Pauli matrix acting in the A/B sublattice space, characterizing the sublattice polarization and the sign of the effective Dirac mass, and τ = ±1 for the valley K/K’ denotes the valley index. Conventionally, 〈σz〉 = +1 corresponds to A‑sublattice polarization with a positive Dirac mass (positive gap sign), while 〈σz〉 = -1 corresponds to B-sublattice polarization with a negative Dirac mass. Since 〈σz〉 reverses sign upon magnetic-field reversal, our observation of magnetic-field-driven Chern number switching from C = +1 to C = −1 are consistent with a scenario in which τ remains constant, that is, the occupation configuration across the K and K’ valleys (two subbands in K and one in K’) remains unchanged. However, for B < 0 T, the spin polarization of the occupied subband in the K’ valley differs from that for B > 0 T (Fig. 2f). Consequently, while the insulating ground state at v = +3 still exhibits valley polarization, the quantity nA-nB becomes negative due to the sign change of 〈σz〉, explaining the shift of wavefunction weight from A-sublattice to B-sublattice (Fig. 2h). By utilizing atomic-resolution STM topography, we can distinguish between candidate ground states of the QAH insulator and track their field-driven evolution, which can be further identified through comparison with theoretical modeling.

Integer and fractional Chern insulating states at finite magnetic field

Beyond the previously discussed insulating states, additional correlated phases emerge at higher magnetic fields. Figure 3a–e present a series of gate-dependent dI/dV intensity maps acquired at magnetic fields of 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 T. As the flat bands are progressively populated, multiple correlated gaps sequentially open and close at the Fermi level on both the electron- and hole-doped sides. Seven well-defined Coulomb gaps are observed, each accompanied by abrupt jumps in chemical potential, manifested as energy shifts of spectroscopic features away from EF37. These states, indicated by triangles, mostly shift in filling with increasing magnetic field, suggesting a topologically nontrivial character. Based on their Chern numbers, these states are identified as Chern insulators, fractional Chern insulators, or charge-ordered phases.

Each Coulomb gap can be precisely characterized by its central filling and gap width. In Fig. 3f, we plot the associated fillings of these correlated phases as a function of magnetic fields. Error bars represent the filling range over which each gap persists, while markers denote the gap center. We use the quantum numbers (C, s) from the Diophantine equation to label each insulating state, and annotated in the figure. Correlated gaps appear at all integer fillings except ν = 0. All are topologically nontrivial (Chern insulators) except (0, 1) and (0, 2) states, which are topologically trivial.

Our high-resolution measurements also reveal gap features from fractional fillings. At B = 8 T, a weak Coulomb gap emerges between the (-2, -1) and (-1, -2) states (purple arrows, Fig. 3g). With increasing field, the gap becomes more pronounced and shifts systematically in filling (Fig. 3h–k). Applying the Diophantine equation, we extract fractional quantum numbers as (C, s) = (-12/5, -6/5), identifying it as a candidate fractional Chern insulator (FCI). Similar FCI states are observed in additional devices (see Supplementary Fig. 5), demonstrating the consistency across samples. While rich FCI physics has been detected using other probes such as scanning single-electron transistor (SET)38, our work provides its direct spectroscopic signature via STM.

Furthermore, atomically resolved STM imaging of the AA-stacked regions (Fig. 3l) in this FCI state reveals sublattice asymmetry, indicating a valley-polarized ground-state wavefunction. Intriguingly, the polarization on the B-sublattice and the negative Chern number are similar to those observed in the Chern insulating state (−2, −1) (see Suppl. Fig. 6). This observation points to two plausible origins for the sublattice asymmetry in the FCI state. First, it may be inherited from the intrinsic sublattice polarization of the adjacent integer Chern insulator at ν = −1. As holes fill the next subband toward ν = -2, this asymmetry could persist and remain detectable within the intervening fractional state. Alternatively, the asymmetry could be an intrinsic property of the FCI state itself, akin to intervalley coherence accompanied by a 3×3 lattice reconstruction39–41. Determining whether the asymmetry is intrinsic requires further investigation, such as higher-resolution wavefunction imaging at the interface to detect any abrupt or smooth evolution of sublattice polarization. Our results provide important experimental constraints for theoretical models of fractional states in Chern bands.

Another notable feature is the emergence of two competing states, (2, 2) and (−2, 2), in the vicinity of ν = +2: the C = −2 state prevails below 8 T before gradually fading with increasing magnetic field, whereas the C = +2 state emerges above 7 T and strengthens thereafter. Figure 4a presents a series of high-resolution gate-dependent dI/dV spectra near this filling. At approximately B = 8 T, four distinct insulating states are resolved. By analyzing the dI/dV intensity near EF as a function of filling factor and magnetic field (Fig. 4d), these states are identified as having Chern numbers C = −2, 0, +1 and +2. Their evolution is summarized in the phase diagram shown in Fig. 4e.

As previously noted, the (0, 2) state corresponds to a topologically trivial insulator. For the competing (−2, 2) state, an evident sublattice asymmetry or B-sublattice polarization (Fig. 4b) indicates a valley-polarized ground state. This observation could be interpreted in terms of the occupation of two subbands within one valley, with opposite spins but each carrying the same Chern number, consistent with both a nonzero Chern number and sublattice asymmetry. In contrast, for the (2, 2) state at higher magnetic field, the ground state arises from the occupation of the same valley across two subbands, but the polarity of 〈σz〉 or the spin polarization in each subband changes the sign as compared to the case in (−2, 2) state. This inversion of gap polarity, while maintaining valley degeneracy, leads to an opposite Chern number and inverse A-sublattice polarization (Fig. 4c). Through the combination of ground-state wavefunction imaging and field-dependent dI/dV curves, the magnetic field-driven phase transition process can be clearly tracked, providing critical insights for Chern insulating states studies.

In summary, our STM study of hBN-aligned MATBG reveals a rich landscape of correlated and topological insulating states at integer and fractional fillings. The gate- and magnetic-field-dependent evolution of Coulomb gaps confirms their nontrivial topology and enables precise Chern number determination. By combining gate-tunable spectroscopy with atomic-resolution imaging, we directly observe sublattice polarization arising from valley-selective occupation, providing microscopic evidence for valley-polarized Chern insulators. Our results establish a direct link among local electronic structure, valley polarization, and topological order, demonstrating STM as a powerful tool to probe of interaction-driven topology.

Methods

Sample preparation

The devices were fabricated using a “tear-and-stack” method. Initially, monolayer graphene (MLG) and few-layer hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) flakes were mechanically exfoliated onto SiO₂/Si substrates and identified by optical microscopy and atomic force microscopy (AFM). Using a polypropylene carbonate (PPC) film supported on polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), the exfoliated hBN flake was first picked up. The first half of the MLG flake was then picked up and intentionally aligned with the hBN flake. Subsequently, the sample stage was manually rotated by about 1° before picking up the remaining half of the MLG, thereby creating a magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG) aligned with the hBN substrate. All pick-up steps were carried out at a controlled temperature ranging from 40 to 50 °C. After assembling the heterostructure, the PPC film was carefully peeled off, flipped, and transferred onto a clean SiO₂/Si substrate (with 285 nm SiO₂) at 105 °C. The PPC carrier film was subsequently removed by high-vacuum annealing at 350 °C. To enable scanning tunneling microscopy and spectroscopy (STM/STS) characterization, the heterostructure was patterned into gate-tunable devices using e-beam lithography followed by Au/Ti metal evaporation. Finally, the devices were annealed in an ultra-high vacuum chamber at 400 °C overnight to eliminate residual adsorbates prior to STM measurements.

STM/STS measurement

The STM experiments are performed at base temperature 0.4 K with an ultrahigh vacuum better than 1 × 10−10 mbar. The STM tips are calibrated on Cu(111) before use. The topographies are obtained in constant current mode. The dI/dV spectra are collected by standard lock-in technique, with 1 mV AC modulation at 713 Hz added to the DC sample bias. The magnetic field is provided by the current in the superconducting coil, and the direction of the magnetic field is perpendicular to the sample surface.

Spectroscopic gap location and error extraction

The identification of the correlated gaps in experiments is obtained through the following characteristic signatures: (1) Each gap appears exclusively at the Fermi level (EF) and is unrelated to any single-particle gap features away from it; (2) Each gap shows strong suppression of the local density of states (LDOS) near the EF; (3) Each gap is accompanied by an abrupt jump in the dispersion of spectral features away from the EF. Based on these criteria, for each correlated gap, we select the gate-voltage (Vg) corresponding to the maximum gap magnitude as the center of the gap. The error bars in Fig. 3f and Suppl. Fig. 5b span the full Vg range over which the correlated gaps are detected. Building upon this, we analyze the magnetic-field dependence of each correlation-induced gap using Streda’s theorem, thereby obtaining the Chern number C and the filling factor s corresponding to all correlated gaps.

Sushil Singla

Abstract
. We find expressions for the Gateaux derivative of the matrix norms in operator spaces, and operator systems. Some applications of the results to quantum probability measures, states on C ∗ -algebras, and Birkhoff-James orthogonality are also presented.

Primary 46G05, 46L07, 46L30; Secondary 46B20

1.
Introduction

If
(\mathcal{X},\|\cdot\|)
is a normed vector space, then the norm function
x\mapsto\|x\|
is
sublinear, and in particular, convex. Thus, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,
the one-sided limit

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|x+ty\|-\|x\|}{t}

exists,
and the resulting quantity is
known as the
Gateaux derivative
of the norm function
\|\cdot\|
at
x
in the direction of
y
.
One source of interest in the Gateaux derivative is its role in Banach space geometry, where the Birkhoff-James orthogonality of vector
x
to the vector
y

admits descriptions involving the Gateaux derivative of the norm at
x
in the direction of
y
, see
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
. The notion of Gateaux derivative has been used in literature for commutator approximants (see
[
10
,
13
]
), and in results related with the smooth points and extreme points of the norm’s unit ball (see
[
1
]
).

Previous works have considered the Gateaux derivative of the norm function for C
∗
-norms
[
18
]
and the Schatten
p
-norms
[
7
]
.
In the present paper, we consider the matrix norms
\|\cdot\|_{n}
that arise from an operator space or an operator system.

A key idea is the notion of support mapping on an operator space
(V,\|\cdot\|_{n})
. Let
M_{n}
denote the space of
n\times n
complex matrices. For
A\in M_{n}
, let
\Re(A):=(A+A^{*})/2

and

\rho^{*}:M_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function
defined by

\rho^{*}(A)=\inf\{t\geq 0\,\,|\,\,\Re(A)\leq tI_{n}\},

where
I_{n}\in M_{n}
is the identity matrix. If
v\in M_{n}(V)
, the normed vector space of matrices with entries from
V
,
then a linear map

\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
is said to be a
support mapping for
v
if, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
w\in M_{k}(V)
,
we have

\rho^{*}(\phi_{k}(w))\leq\|w\|_{k}

and

\rho^{*}(\phi_{n}(v))=\|v\|_{n},

where, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\phi_{\ell}
denotes the linear map
M_{\ell}(V)\rightarrow M_{\ell}(M_{n})
given by
[v_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{\ell}\mapsto[\phi(v_{ij})]_{i,j=1}^{\ell}
for all
v_{ij}\in V
.
The existence of support mappings on
(V,\|\cdot\|_{n})
, for every
v\in M_{n}(V)
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
was established by Winkler in
[
20
, Theorem 3.3.2]
.

For
A\in M_{n}
, we have
\Re(A)\leq tI_{n}
if and only if
\langle\Re(A)\eta,\eta\rangle\leq t
for all unit vectors
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Thus, we have

\rho^{*}(A)=\sup\{\langle\Re(A)\eta,\eta\rangle\,\,|\,\,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\eta\|=1\}.

The first goal in this paper is to express the Gateaux derivative of
\|\cdot\|_{n}
in the manner presented in Theorem
1.1
below, where,
for
A\in M_{n}
,

M_{0}(A)=\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\,\,|\,\,\|\eta\|=1\text{ and }\langle\Re(A)\eta,\eta\rangle=\|A\|\}.

Note that
M_{0}(A)
maybe empty set, but if
A=A^{*}
then

M_{0}(A)=\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\,\,:\,\,\|\eta\|=1\text{ and }\|\Re(A)\eta\|=\|A\|\}

is the unit ball of the subspace determined by the kernel of the matrix
A^{*}A-\|A\|^{2}I_{n}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
(V,\|\cdot\|_{n})
is an operator space, and

v,w\in M_{n}(V)
. Then there exists a support mapping for
v
such that
M_{0}(\phi_{n}(v))\neq\emptyset
and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|v+tw\|_{n}-\|v\|_{n}}{t}=\max\{\langle\Re(\phi_{n}(w))\eta,\eta\rangle\,\,|\,\,

\displaystyle\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}\text{ is a support mapping }

\displaystyle\text{for }v\text{ and }\ \eta\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(v))\}.

If
\mathcal{S}
be an operator system with Archimedean order unit
e
, then a
matrix state
on
\mathcal{S}

is a unital completely positive map
\phi:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow M_{k}
such that
\phi(e)=I_{k}
.
For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
S_{k}(\mathcal{S})
denote the set of matrix states
\phi:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow M_{k}
. For
s\in\mathcal{S}
, the matrix range of
s
is the union of the sets

W_{k}(s)=\{A\in M_{k}\,\,:\,\,A=\phi(s)\ \text{ for some }\ \phi\in S_{k}(\mathcal{S})\}.

If
\mathcal{S}
is the space of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space with identity operator
I
, then for any bounded linear operator
T
and
n=1
,
W_{1}(T)
is the topological closure of the classical numerical range of
T
. It was proved by Lumer
[
11
]
that

\sup\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\,\,|\,\,\lambda\in W_{1}(T)\}=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|I+tT\|-1}{t},

where
\mathrm{Re}(a+ib)=a
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
This was extended to matrix ranges by Narcowich and Ward
[
14
]
as follows. For a unital
C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, let
\|\cdot\|_{\sigma}
denote the function
x\mapsto\|x\|_{\sigma}
on
M_{n}(\mathcal{A})
defined by

\|x\|_{\sigma}=\sup\left\{\sqrt{s_{\phi}(x^{*}x)}\,\,|\,\,\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{A})\right\},\quad\text{for}\quad x\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A}),

where
s_{\phi}:M_{n}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the functional

s_{\phi}([x_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n})=\dfrac{1}{n}\ \phi_{n}([x_{ij}])_{ij},

the
(i,j)
-entry of the matrix
\phi_{n}([x_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n})
.
By
[
14
, Proposition 3.1, Corollary 3.4]

the function
x\mapsto\|x\|_{\sigma}
defines a norm on
M_{n}(\mathcal{A})
, and it has the property
that, for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
and
B=[b_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\in M_{n}
,

\max\{\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{trace}(A^{t}B))\,\,|\,\,A\in W_{n}(x)\}=n\cdot\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|e_{n}+t(\overline{b}_{ij}x)_{ij}\|_{\sigma}-1}{t}.

The following result gives a slightly different extension of Lumer’s formula.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be an operator system, and let
s_{1},s_{2}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{S})
. Then, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|s_{1}+ts_{2}\|_{n}-\|s_{1}\|_{n}}{t}=\frac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}\max\{

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(\langle\phi_{n}(s_{2})\eta,\phi_{n}(s_{1})\eta\rangle)\,\,|\,\,\phi\in S_{n}(\mathcal{S}),

\displaystyle\|\phi_{n}(s_{1})\|=\|s_{1}\|_{n}\text{ and }\eta\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(s_{1}))\}.

Let
e_{n}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{S})
denotes the diagonal matrix with
e
at each diagonal entry. Then for
s\in M_{n}(\mathcal{S})
, the above theorem reads as

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|e_{n}+ts\|_{n}-1}{t}=\max\{\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)\,\,|\,\,\lambda\in W_{1}(\phi_{n}(s)),\ \phi\in S_{n}(\mathcal{S})\}.

Note that
\phi\in S_{n}(\mathcal{S})
has range in
M_{n}
, the elements
\lambda
in the above equation is taken from the numerical range of the
n^{2}\times n^{2}
matrix

\phi_{n}(s_{2})
.

In Section
2
, we will provide the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. In Section
3
, applications of these results will be given in the commutative C
∗
-algebras and simple finite-dimensional C
∗
-algebras. A few new results, for instance Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.7
, that are useful in the geometry of Banach spaces, are also mentioned.

2.
Proofs of the Main Results

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Our assumptions are that
V
is an operator space with matrix norms
\|\cdot\|_{n}
, and that
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
v,w\in M_{n}(V)
are fixed.
We aim to
show the Gateaux derivative of
\|\cdot\|_{n}
at
v
in the direction of
w
, namely the quantity

(2)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|v+tw\|_{n}-\|v\|_{n}}{t},

is given by

(3)

\max\left\{\langle\Re(\phi_{n}(w))\eta,\eta\rangle\,|\,\phi\text{ is a support mapping for }v\text{ and }\eta\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(v))\right\}.

Because

\|\cdot\|_{n}
is a convex function, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|v+tw\|_{n}-\|v\|_{n}}{t}=\inf_{t\geq 0}\left\{\dfrac{\|v+tw\|_{n}-\|v\|_{n}}{t}\right\}.

If
\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
is a support mapping for
v
, then

\displaystyle\|v+tw\|_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\rho^{*}(\Re(\phi_{n}(v+tw)))

\displaystyle=\sup\{\langle\Re(\phi_{n}(v+tw))\eta,\eta\rangle\,\,|\,\,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\eta\|=1\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup\{\langle\Re(\phi_{n}(v+tw))\eta,\eta\rangle\,:\,\eta\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(v))\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\langle\Re(\phi_{n}(tw))\eta,\eta\rangle\,:\,\eta\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(v))\}+\|v\|_{n},

which establishes the inequality

\dfrac{\|v+tw\|_{n}-\|v\|_{n}}{t}\geq\sup\{\langle\Re(\phi_{n}(w))\eta,\eta\rangle\,:\,\eta\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(v))\}.

By considering the infimum over all
t\geq 0
and the supremum over all support functionals
\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
of
v
,
we deduce the quantity in (
3
) is less than or equal to the quantity in (
2
).

To prove the complementary inequality,
let

a=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|v+tw\|_{n}-\|v\|_{n}}{t}.

By the Hahn-Banach Theorem and the convexity of the norm function,
there exists
F\in(M_{n}(V))^{*}
such that

(4)

\|F\|=1,\,\mathrm{Re}(F(v))=\|v\|_{n},\text{ and }\mathrm{Re}(F(w))=a.

Arguing along the lines of the proof of
[
20
, Theorem 3.3.2]
(explained below),
there are a support mapping
\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
for
v
and
unit vector
\eta\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{n}
such that

(5)

F(u)=\langle\phi_{n}(u)\eta,\eta\rangle,\text{ for all }u\in V.

Thus,

a=\mathrm{Re}(\langle\phi_{n}(w)\eta,\eta\rangle)=\langle\Re(\phi_{n}(w))\eta,\eta\rangle\leq\sup\{\langle\Re(\phi_{n}(w))\gamma,\gamma\rangle\,:\,\gamma\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(v))\},

thereby showing
the quantity in (
2
) is less than or equal to the quantity in (
3
), and establishing the assertion stated in Theorem
1.1
.

Therefore, to complete the proof, we adapt the argument in
[
20
, Theorem 3.3.2]
to show
there are a support mapping
\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
for
v
and
unit vector
\eta\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{n}
such that
F(u)=\langle\phi_{n}(u)\eta,\eta\rangle
, for all
u\in V
.

Assume

F\in(M_{n}(V))^{*}
is a linear functional with the properties exhibited in (
4
).
Using
[
20
, Lemma 2.3.5]
(or drawing from the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 2.3.2]
), there is a state

p
on
M_{n}
such that

(6)

\mathrm{Re}F(A^{*}uA)\leq p(A^{*}A)\rho_{k}(u),

for all
u\in M_{n}(V)
and
A\in M_{k,n}
, for arbitrary
r\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
p
is a linear
functional on
M_{n}
, there exist
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

p(A)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\langle A\eta_{i},\eta_{i}\rangle,

for all
A\in M_{n}
. Letting
\eta=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we get

p(A)=\langle(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta,\eta\rangle.

Since
p
is a state on
M_{n}
, this implies
\|\eta\|\leq 1
. Thus,

p(A^{*}A)=\langle(A^{*}A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta,\eta\rangle=\|(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta\|^{2}.

Using (
6
), this implies

(7)

|F(A^{*}uA)|\leq\sigma(A)\|(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta\|^{2},

for all
A\in M_{n}
, where
\sigma(A)=2\max\{\rho_{r}(\pm A),\rho_{r}(\pm iA)\}
. Let
B_{u}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be the sesquilinear form defined on the subspace
\mathcal{H}=(M_{n}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta
of
\mathbb{C}^{n^{2}}
as

B_{u}((B\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta,(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta)=F(A^{*}uB).

Then,
B_{u}
is a bounded sesquilinear form using (
7
), and thus as an application of Riesz Representation Theorem there exists a bounded operator
\psi(u)
on
\mathcal{H}
such that

F(A^{*}uB)=\langle\psi(u)(B\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta,(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta\rangle.

Clearly,
\psi
is a linear map. If we consider
P
to be the projection from
\mathbb{C}^{n^{2}}
onto
\mathcal{H}
, then
P\in(M_{n}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})^{\prime}
(since
\mathcal{H}
is invariant under
M_{n}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n}
). Let
\tilde{\phi}:V\rightarrow M_{n}
be the map defined by

\tilde{\phi}(u)=P\psi(u)P,

and this gives

F(A^{*}uB)=\langle\tilde{\phi}(u)(B\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta,(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\eta\rangle=\langle\tilde{\phi}(u)\eta,\eta\rangle,

for all
A,B\in M_{n}
. In particular, we have

F(u)=\langle\tilde{\phi}(u)\eta,\eta\rangle,\ \tilde{\phi}(uA)=\tilde{\phi}(u)(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n}),\text{ and }\tilde{\phi}(Au)=(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\tilde{\phi}(u),

for all
u\in M_{n}(V)
and
A\in M_{n}
. Using linear algebra techniques, these in turn implies that there exists
\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
such that
\tilde{\phi}(u)=\phi_{n}(u)
. Thus, we get

F(A^{*}uB)=\langle\phi_{n}(u)\eta,\eta\rangle,

for all
A,B\in M_{n}
, proving (
4
) and, hence, the theorem.
∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We aim to prove, for
s_{1},s_{2}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{S})
, where

\mathcal{S}
is an operator system, that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|s_{1}+ts_{2}\|_{n}-\|s_{1}\|_{n}}{t}=\frac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}\max\{

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(\langle\phi_{n}(s_{2})\eta,\phi_{n}(s_{1})\eta\rangle)\,\,|\,\,\phi\in S_{n}(\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle\|\phi_{n}(s_{1})\|=\|s_{1}\|_{n}\text{ and }\eta\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(s_{1}))\}.

Let
\phi\in S_{k}(\mathcal{S})
with
\|\phi_{n}(s_{1})\|=\|s_{1}\|_{n}
,
A_{1}:=\phi(s_{1})\in M_{n}(M_{k}(\mathcal{S}))
, and
A_{2}:=\phi_{n}(s_{2})\in M_{n}(M_{k}(\mathcal{S}))
. Let
\eta\in M_{0}(A_{1})
, i.e.,
\langle\Re(A_{1})\eta,\eta\rangle=\|A_{1}\|
. As an application of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\|A_{1}\|=\langle\Re(A_{1})\eta,\eta\rangle=\mathrm{Re}(\langle A_{1}\eta,\eta\rangle)\leq|\langle A_{1}\eta,\eta\rangle|\leq\|A_{1}\eta\|\leq\|A_{1}\|.

So, equality occurs throughout and using the condition of equality in Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\eta=\frac{1}{\|A_{1}\|}A_{1}\eta=\frac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}A_{1}\eta.

Further, we note that
\|\phi_{n}\|=1
.
Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|s_{1}+ts_{2}\|_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\|\phi_{n}(s_{1}+ts_{2})\|

\displaystyle=\|A_{1}+tA_{2}\|

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{Re}(\langle(A_{1}+tA_{2})\eta,\eta\rangle)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}(\langle A_{1}\eta,\eta\rangle)+t\mathrm{Re}(\langle A_{2}\eta,\eta\rangle)

\displaystyle=\|s_{1}\|_{n}+t\frac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}\mathrm{Re}(\langle A_{2}\eta,A_{1}\eta\rangle).

This proves
\dfrac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}\mathrm{Re}(\langle A_{2}\eta,A_{1}\eta\rangle)\leq\dfrac{\|s_{1}+ts_{2}\|_{n}-\|s_{1}\|_{n}}{t}
for all
t>0
, which implies the quantity
on the right hand side of (
1
) is no larger than the quantity on the left hand side of (
1
).

Using Theorem
1.1
, there exist a support mapping

\psi:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow M_{n}

for
s_{1}
and a vector
\eta\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(e))
such that

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|s_{1}+ts_{2}\|_{n}-\|s_{1}\|_{n}}{t}=\langle\Re(\psi_{n}(s_{2}))\eta,\eta\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\Re(\psi_{n}(s_{1}))\eta,\eta\rangle=\|s_{1}\|.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra containing
\mathcal{S}
is an operator subsystem.
By Wittstock’s extension theorem
[
15
, Theorem 8.2]
, we can extend
\psi
to a completely contractive map to
\mathcal{A}
that we will denote by same symbol. Using Wittstock’s decomposition theorem
[
15
, Theorem 8.5]
, there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, a
\ast
-representation
\pi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
, and linear isometries
V_{1},V_{2}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
such that

\psi(a)=V_{1}^{\ast}\pi(a)V_{2}\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{A}.

Thus,

(8)

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|s_{1}+ts_{2}\|_{n}-\|s_{1}\|_{n}}{t}=\mathrm{Re}(\langle\pi_{n}(s_{2})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\widetilde{V_{1}}\eta\rangle)

and
\mathrm{Re}(\langle\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\widetilde{V_{1}}\eta\rangle)=\|s_{1}\|_{n}
where
\widetilde{V}_{i}=V_{i}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{i}
for
i=1,2
. We note that
\mathrm{Re}(\langle\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\widetilde{V_{1}}\eta\rangle)=\|s_{1}\|
implies
\langle\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\widetilde{V_{1}}\eta\rangle=\|s_{1}\|_{n}
, since
\pi_{n}
is contractive.

Using the condition of equality in Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and contractivity of
\pi_{n}
, the equation
\langle\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\widetilde{V_{1}}\eta\rangle=\|s_{1}\|_{n}
would imply

(9)

\widetilde{V}_{1}\eta=\frac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta.

Now, we consider
\phi:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow M_{n}
as

\phi(x)=V_{2}^{*}\pi(x)V_{2}\quad\text{ for all }\quad x\in\mathcal{S}.

Then,
\phi
is completely positive map. Since
V_{2}
is an isometry and
\pi
is
\ast
-homomorphism,
we get
\phi\in S_{n}(\mathcal{S})
. Furthermore,

\frac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}\langle\phi_{n}(s_{2})\eta,\phi_{n}(s_{1})\eta\rangle=\frac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}\langle\pi_{n}(s_{2})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta\rangle=\langle\pi_{n}(s_{2})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\widetilde{V}_{1}\eta\rangle,

where we have used (
9
) in the last equation. The above equation along with (
8
) yield

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|s_{1}+ts_{2}\|_{n}-\|s_{1}\|_{n}}{t}=\frac{1}{\|s_{1}\|_{n}}\langle\phi_{n}(s_{2})\eta,\phi_{n}(s_{1})\eta\rangle.

Now,
\phi\in S_{n}(\mathcal{S})
and

\langle\phi_{n}(s_{1})\eta,\phi_{n}(s_{1})\eta\rangle=\langle\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta\rangle=\|s_{1}\|\langle\pi_{n}(s_{1})\widetilde{V}_{2}\eta,\widetilde{V}_{1}\eta\rangle=\|s_{1}\|^{2}.

This proves
\|\phi_{n}(s_{1})\eta\|=\|s_{1}\|
. Since
\|\phi_{n}\|\leq 1
and
\|\eta\|=1
, we get
\|\phi_{n}\|=\|s_{1}\|_{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Applications of the Main Results

As an application of Theorem
1.2
, we get the following special case of
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
. Let
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm on
M_{n}
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
A,B\in M_{n}
. Then,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|A+tB\|-\|A\|}{t}=\frac{1}{\|A\|}\max\{\mathrm{Re}(\langle B\eta,A\eta\rangle)\,|\,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\ \|\eta\|=1\text{ and }\|A\eta\|=\|A\|\}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the right hand side of the equation is less than equal to the left hand side. To prove the other side inequality, we use Theorem
1.2
with
V=\mathbb{C}
. Note that if
\phi\in S_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, then
\phi(\lambda)=\lambda I_{n}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\phi_{n}(C)=C\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n}
for all
C\in M_{n}
. Now, let
\phi\in S_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with
\|\phi_{n}(A)\|=\|A\|
and
\gamma\in M_{0}(\phi_{n}(A))\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{n}
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|A+tB\|-\|A\|}{t}=\frac{1}{\|A\|}\mathrm{Re}(\langle(B\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma,(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma\rangle).

Then we can decompose
\gamma
as
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\eta_{i}\|^{2}=1
,

\langle(B\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma,(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle B\eta_{i},A\eta_{i}\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\|A\|=\langle\Re(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma,\gamma\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\Re(A)\eta_{i},\eta_{i}\rangle.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\|A\|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\eta_{i}\|^{2}\left\langle\Re(A)\frac{\eta_{i}}{\|\eta_{i}\|},\frac{\eta_{i}}{\|\eta_{i}\|}\right\rangle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|A\eta_{i}\|\|\eta_{i}\|^{2}\leq\|A\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\eta_{i}\|^{2}=\|A\|,

which using the condition of equality in Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies
\|A\eta_{i}\|=\|A\|\|\eta_{i}\|
whenever
\eta_{i}\neq 0
. Thus,

\langle(B\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma,(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma\rangle=\sum_{\eta_{i}\neq 0}\langle B\eta_{i},A\eta_{i}\rangle=\sum_{\eta_{i}\neq 0}\|\eta_{i}\|^{2}\left\langle B\frac{\eta_{i}}{\|\eta_{i}\|},A\frac{\eta_{i}}{\|\eta_{i}\|}\right\rangle,

lies in the convex hull of numerical range of in the numerical range of
(A^{\ast}B)\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n}
restricted to the subspace
\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\|A\eta\|=\|A\|\}
. Using Haudorff-Toeplitz theorem, there exists
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\langle(B\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma,(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma\rangle=\langle B\eta,A\eta\rangle
. Thus,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|A+tB\|-\|A\|}{t}=\frac{1}{\|A\|}\mathrm{Re}(\langle(B\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma,(A\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits}I_{n})\gamma\rangle)=\frac{1}{\|A\|}=\langle B\eta,A\eta\rangle.

This completes the proof.
∎

Gateaux derivatives are closely associated with a notion of orthogonality in a normed space, known as Birkhoff-James
[
6
]
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a vector space. Let
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be a subspace of
X
. Then,
x
is said to be (Birkhoff-James) orthogonal to
\mathcal{Y}
if and only if

\|x\|\leq\|x-y\|\quad\text{ for all }\quad y\in\mathcal{Y}.

When
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{C}y
is one dimensional subspace generated by
y\in\mathcal{X}
,
x
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{Y}
is abbreviated as
x
is orthogonal to
y
. It was proved in
[
8
, Proposition 1.5]
that
x
is orthogonal to
\mathbb{C}y
if and only if
\inf_{\varphi\in[0,2\pi]}D_{\varphi,x}(y)\geq 0
, where

D_{\varphi,x}(y)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\|x+te^{i\varphi}y\|-\|x\|}{t}.

Using this fact, Corollary
3.1
, and along the lines of
[
8
, Corollary 3.1]
, we get the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
A\in M_{n}
, then
A
is orthogonal to
B
if and only if there exists
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\|\eta\|=1
,
\langle A\eta,\eta\rangle=\|A\|
and
\langle B\eta,\eta\rangle=0
.

Remark 3.3
.

This theorem has become famous by the name of Bhatia-Šemrl Theorem after this appeared in
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
in the following form: For
B\in M_{n}
, we have
\|A\|\leq\|A+\lambda B\|
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
if and only if there exists
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\|\eta\|=1
,
\langle A\eta,\eta\rangle=\|A\|
and
\langle B\eta,\eta\rangle=0
.

This theorem is actually a special case of
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
, that was motivated by the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 2]
and
[
3
, Lemma 9.14]
.

Now, we see a generalization of this form of Bhatia-Šemrl Theorem in the setting of operator spaces.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(V,\|\cdot\|_{n})
be an operator space. Let
v\in M_{n}(V)
and
\mathcal{W}
be a subspace of
M_{n}(V)
. Then, we have

\|v\|_{n}\leq\|v-w\|_{n}\quad\text{ for all }\quad w\in\mathcal{W}

if and only if there is a completely contractive mapping
\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
and a unit vector
\eta\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{n}
such that

\|\phi_{n}(v)\eta\|=\|v\|_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\phi_{n}(v)\eta,\phi_{n}(w)\eta\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }\quad w\in\mathcal{W}.

Proof.

The converse is easy. If
\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
is a completely contractive mapping and
\eta\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{n}
is a unit vector such that

\|\phi_{n}(v)\eta\|=\|v\|_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\phi_{n}(v)\eta,\phi_{n}(w)\eta\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }\quad w\in\mathcal{W}.

Then,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{n}(v)\eta,\phi_{n}(v)\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle\phi_{n}(v)\eta,\phi_{n}(v)\eta\rangle+\langle\phi_{n}(w)\eta,\phi_{n}(w)\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{n}(v+w)\eta,\phi_{n}(v+w)\eta\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|v+w\|_{n}^{2}.

Now, we prove the other direction. Let
\|v\|_{n}\leq\|v-w\|_{n}
for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
.
Using
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
, there exists a functional
f\in M_{n}(V)^{*}
such that
\|f\|=1
,
f(v)=\|v\|
and
f(w)=0
for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
. Using
[
4
, Lemma 2.3.3]
, there exists a complete contraction
\phi:V\rightarrow M_{n}
and unit vectors
\xi,\eta\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{n}
such that

(10)

f(u)=\langle\phi_{n}(u)\eta,\xi\rangle,

for all
u\in M_{n}(V)
. Now, a simple application of the condition of equality in Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that for a functional of the form (
10
), the condition
f(v)=\|v\|_{n}
implies
\xi=\frac{1}{\|v\|_{n}}\phi_{n}(v)\eta.
Combining all these statements give the required result.
∎

In case of operator system, we get the following result.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be an operator system. Let
s\in\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{W}
be a subspace of
M_{n}(\mathcal{S})
. Then, we have

\|s\|_{n}\leq\|s-w\|_{n}\quad\text{ for all }\quad w\in\mathcal{W}

if and only if there exists a matrix state
\phi\in S_{n}(\mathcal{S})

and a unit vector
\eta\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{n}
such that

\|\phi_{n}(s)\eta\|=\|s\|_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\phi_{n}(s)\eta,\phi_{n}(w)\eta\rangle=0\quad\text{for all }\quad w\in\mathcal{W}.

Proof.

This follows along the lines of proof of Theorem
1.2
and using Theorem
3.4
.
∎

The above corollary can be used to the existence of some special states in operator systems and
C^{*}
-algebras. We recall
[
16
]
that a norm’s face
F
of
\mathcal{S}
is a convex subset of the unit ball of
\mathcal{S}
such that if for
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
with
\|s_{1}\|=\|s_{2}\|=1
and
\lambda s_{1}+(1-\lambda)s_{2}\in F
with
0<\lambda<1
, then
s_{1},s_{2}\in F
. A norm’s face is maximal if it is not properly contained in any other norm’s face. Let
\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{F})
denotes the relative interior of a norm’s face.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be an operator system with Archimedean order unit
e
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the maximal norm’s face of
\mathcal{S}
containing
e
. If
\mathcal{W}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{S}
such that

1\leq\|e-w\|\quad\text{ for all }\quad w\in\mathcal{W},

then there exists a matrix state
\phi\in S_{1}(\mathcal{S})
such that
\phi(v)=\|v\|
for all
v\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{F})
and
0\in W_{1}(\phi(w))
for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

Using Corollary
3.5
, there exists a state
\phi\in S_{1}(\mathcal{S})
such that
0\in W_{1}(\phi(w))
for all
w\in\mathcal{W}
. We have,
\phi(e)=1
. If we prove
\phi(v)=\|v\|
for all
v\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{F})
and we will be done. Let
v\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{F})
. Using
[
16
, Theorem 6.2]
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that

\|(1-t)e+tv\|=1\text{ for all }t\in[0,1+\epsilon].

Then,

|t\phi(v)+(1-t)|=|\phi((1-t)e+ts)|\leq\|(1-t)e+tv\|=1,

for all
t\in[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]
. Taking
t=1\pm\epsilon
, we get
|1\pm\epsilon(f(v)-1)|\leq 1
, which gives
f(v)=1
.
∎

For a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, a slightly different version of the above corollary also holds.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with unit element
1_{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{A}
be an element of norm one and
\mathcal{F}
be the maximal norm’s face of
\mathcal{A}
containing
x
. If
\mathcal{B}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{A}
such that

\|x\|\leq\|x-y\|\quad\text{ for all }\quad y\in\mathcal{B},

then there exists a state
\phi\in S_{1}(\mathcal{A})
such that

\phi(x^{*}x)=1\text{ and }\phi(x^{*}y)=0\quad\text{ for all }\quad x\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{F})\text{ and }y\in\mathcal{B}.

Proof.

By Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists a functional
f\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
such that
\|f\|=1=f(x)
and
f(y)=0
for all
y\in\mathcal{B}
. Using the argument in Corollary
3.6
, we have
f(x)=1
for all
x\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{F})
. For functional
f
, there exists a cyclic representation
(\mathcal{H},\pi,\xi)
with
\|\xi\|=1
and a unit vector
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
such that

f(z)=\langle\pi(z)\xi,\eta\rangle\quad\text{ for all }z\in\mathcal{A}.

Since
f(x)=1
, we get
\langle\pi(x)\xi,\eta\rangle=\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{F})
. Using the condition of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
we get
\eta=\pi(x)\xi
for all
x\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{F})
. If we take

\phi(z)=\langle\pi(z)\xi,\xi\rangle\quad\text{ for all}\quad z\in\mathcal{A},

then
\phi
is the required state on
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Finally, we see some matricial versions of some known results. We recall the notion of POVMs (see
[
5
]
for more details). Assume that
X
is a compact Hausdorff space, and
\mathcal{O}(X)
is the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets of
X
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the space of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. A function
\nu:\mathcal{O}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a POVM on
X
if

(i)

\nu(E)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{+}
with
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
for every eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\nu(E)
;

(ii)

for every countable collection
\{E_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)
with
E_{k}\cap E_{k^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for
k\neq k^{\prime}
,

\nu\bigg(\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{k}\bigg)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\nu(E_{k}),

where the convergence
of the right-hand side of the equality above is with respect to the weak
operator topology of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

If a POVM
\nu
on
\mathcal{O}(X)
satisfies
\nu(X)=I_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}
, then it is referred to as a
quantum probability measure
. If
\nu:\mathcal{O}(X)\rightarrow M_{n}
is a positive operator-valued measure (POVM), then, for each pair
\xi,\nu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the formula

\mu_{\xi,\eta}^{[\nu]}(E)=\langle\nu(E)\xi,\eta\rangle,\mbox{ for }E\in\mathcal{O}(X),

defines a complex Borel measure on
\mathcal{O}(X)
. Hence, for each
f\in C(X)
, we denote by
\displaystyle\int_{X}f\,d\nu

the unique operator on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for which

\left\langle\left(\displaystyle\int_{X}f\,d\nu\right)\xi,\eta\right\rangle=\int_{X}f\,d\mu_{\xi,\eta}^{[\nu]},\mbox{ for all }\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

We get the following extension of
[
17
, Theorem 1.3]
(see also
[
9
, Corollary 2.1]
).

Corollary 3.8
.

If
X
is a compact Hausdorff space and
Id_{n}
denotes the diagonal matrix in
M_{n}(C(X))
with diagonal entries
1_{X}
. The, for a subspace
\mathcal{W}
of
M_{n}(C(X))
, we have

\|Id_{n}\|_{M_{n}(C(X))}\leq\|Id_{n}-G\|_{M_{n}(C(X))},\mbox{ for all }G\in\mathcal{W},

if and only if there exists a quantum probability measure

\nu:\mathcal{O}(X)\rightarrow M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
and a unit vector
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\left\langle\left(\displaystyle\int_{X}G\,d\nu\right)\eta,\eta\right\rangle=0
, for all
G\in\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

Assume
\nu:\mathcal{O}(X)\rightarrow M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
is a quantum probability and
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}

is a unit vector
for which
\langle(\int_{X}G\,d\nu)\eta,\eta\rangle=0
, for all
G\in\mathcal{W}
.
If
\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
denotes the canonical orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then there exist
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

such that

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}\otimes\epsilon_{i}.

Let
W:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the linear map for which
We_{i}=\eta_{i}\otimes\epsilon_{i}
, for each
i
.
Let
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
denote the unique unit
vector for which
W\xi=\eta
. The map
W^{*}\nu W:\mathcal{O}(X)\rightarrow M_{n}
is a POVM and has the property that, for every
E\in\mathcal{O}(X)

and
\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\mu_{\gamma,\delta}^{[W^{*}\nu W]}(E)=\langle W^{*}\nu(E)W\gamma,\delta\rangle=\mu_{W\gamma,W\delta}^{[\nu]}(E).

In particular,

\mu_{\xi,\xi}^{[W^{*}\nu W]}(E)=\mu_{\eta,\eta}^{[\nu]}(E).

Now define a linear map
\phi:C(X)\rightarrow M_{n}
by

\phi(f)=\int_{X}f\,d(W^{*}\nu W).

Note that
\phi
is a matrix state. If
F=[f_{ij}]_{i,j}\in C(X)\otimes M_{n}
, then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\langle\phi_{n}(F)\eta,\eta\rangle&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\phi(f_{ij})\eta_{j},\eta_{i}\rangle\\
&&\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\displaystyle\int_{X}f_{ij}\,d\mu_{W\epsilon_{j},W\epsilon_{i}}^{[\nu]}\\
&&\\
&=&\left\langle\left(\displaystyle\int_{X}F\,d\nu\right)\eta,\eta\right\rangle.\end{array}

Hence,
\langle\phi_{n}(G)\eta,\eta\rangle=0
, for every
G\in\mathcal{W}
, and so, by Corollary
3.5
,

\|Id_{n}\|_{M_{n}(C(X))}\leq\|Id_{n}-G\|_{M_{n}(C(X))},\mbox{for every }G\in\mathcal{W}.

Conversely, assume that
\|Id_{n}\|_{M_{n}(C(X))}\leq\|Id_{n}-G\|_{M_{n}(C(X))}
, for every
G\in\mathcal{W}
. By Corollary
3.5
, there exist a
matrix state
\phi:C(X)\rightarrow M_{n}
and a unit vector
\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\langle\phi_{n}(G)\eta,\eta\rangle=0
, for all
G\in\mathcal{W}
. As noted in
[
15
, §4]
, the matrix state
\phi
is
determined by a unique quantum probability measure

\omega:\mathcal{O}(X)\rightarrow M_{n}
whereby, for each pair
\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\langle\phi(f)\gamma,\delta\rangle=\int_{X}f\,d\mu_{\gamma,\delta}^{[\omega]},\mbox{ for all }f\in C(X).

(The POVM
\omega
has the form
\omega(E)=V^{*}P(E)V
, where
V^{*}\pi V
is a minimal
Stinespring dilation of
\phi
and
P
is the spectral measure on
\mathcal{O}(X)
arising from the Spectral Theorem applied to the abelian C
∗
-subalgebra

\pi\left(C(X)\right)
of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
.) Define an operator-valued measure
\nu:\mathcal{O}(X)\rightarrow M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
by

\nu(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega(E)\otimes E_{ij},

where
\{E_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{n}
are the canonical matrix units of
M_{n}
. Note that
\nu
is positive, as
\omega
is positive; hence

\nu
is a quantum probability measure.

Express
\phi_{n}
as
\phi_{n}=\phi\otimes\mbox{\rm Id}_{M_{n}}
, so that, for every
F=(f_{ij})\in C(X)\otimes M_{n}
, we have

\phi_{n}(F)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{X}f_{ij}\,d\,\omega\right)\otimes E_{ij}=\int_{X}F\,d\,\nu.

Because
\langle\phi_{n}(G)\eta,\eta\rangle=0
if
G\in\mathcal{W}
, we deduce

\left\langle\left(\displaystyle\int_{X}G\,d\nu\right)\eta,\eta\right\rangle=0
, for all
G\in\mathcal{W}
.
∎

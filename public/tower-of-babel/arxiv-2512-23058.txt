Lê modules and hypersurfaces with one-dimensional singular sets
David B. Massey

Abstract
. By using our previous results on Lê modules and an upper-bound on the betti numbers which we proved with Lê, we investigate the cohomology of Milnor fibers and the internal local systems given by the vanishing cycles of hypersurfaces with one-dimensional singular sets.

Definitions and prior results

We should be clear from the beginning that, when we look at intersection numbers, we deal with analytic cycles,
not

Our assumption that

1

V(z_{0})

In terms of analytic cycles,

\Sigma f
, and

1

\Sigma f

[
10
]

Note that

{\mathbf{0}}
, and that

intersects
\Lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
properly at
{\mathbf{0}}
by our assumption.

Letting
C

\Sigma f
at
{\mathbf{0}}

[

The intersection numbers

b
is a product of powers of primes
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
). Note that Proposition
1.6
implies
\lambda^{0}\neq 2
.

(2) Suppose that
\tilde{b}_{n-1}\neq 0
. Then
\tilde{b}_{n-1}=1
and for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
,
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
. Thus,

\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda^{0}-1}.

Again Proposition
1.6
implies
\lambda^{0}\neq 2
.

However, there are 3 other cases which we can consider, based on the nature of
|\Sigma f|
. (a)
|\Sigma f|
consists of two smooth irreducible components at the origin, both with
\mu^{\circ}=1
. (b)
|\Sigma f|
has a single irreducible component
C
of multiplicity 2 at the origin with
\mu^{\circ}_{C}=1
, and (c)
|\Sigma f|
has a single smooth component
C
at the origin such that
\mu^{\circ}_{C}=2
. It is this final case where we have more to say.

In Case (c),
m={\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}|\Sigma f|=1
and so, by Theorem
2.2
,
\operatorname{tr}\alpha_{1}=\pm 1
. However, the characteristic polynomial
{\operatorname{char}}_{\alpha_{1}}(t)
of
\alpha_{1}
has degree 2 and is product of cyclotomic polynomials. It follows that
{\operatorname{char}}_{\alpha_{1}}(t)=t^{2}\pm t+1
, which are both irreducible over
{\mathbb{Z}}
(or
{\mathbb{Q}}
). It follows that we cannot be in Case (2) from above, for if
\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
, then the characteristic polynomial of the Milnor monodromy action on
\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})
would have to divide
{\operatorname{char}}_{\alpha_{1}}(t)
.

Therefore, if
\lambda^{1}=2
,
\lambda^{0}\geq 2
, and
m=1
, we conclude that we are in Case (2),
\lambda^{0}\geq 3
,

\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})=0\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda^{0}-2}\oplus\big({\mathbb{Z}}/b{\mathbb{Z}}\big)

for some
b\geq 1
. However, there is more that we say about the prime powers that divide
b
.

For each prime
\mathfrak{p}
, the question is: does
{\operatorname{char}}_{\alpha_{1}}(t)=t^{2}\pm t+1
factor in
({\mathbb{Z}}/\mathfrak{p}{\mathbb{Z}})[t]
? A little number theory tells one that if
{\operatorname{char}}_{\alpha_{1}}(t)=t^{2}\pm t+1
factors in
({\mathbb{Z}}/\mathfrak{p}{\mathbb{Z}})[t]
, then
\mathfrak{p}
is either
3
or congruent to
1
modulo
6
. Thus in the isomorphism
\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda^{0}-2}\oplus\big({\mathbb{Z}}/b{\mathbb{Z}}\big)
, either
b=1
or
b
is a product of prime powers where the primes are either
3
or congruent to
1
modulo
6
. ∎

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.
Suppose
\lambda^{0}=2
and
\lambda^{1}\geq 3
.

Unsurprisingly, the analysis here is analogous to the
\lambda^{1}=2
situation above. In our current case, Theorem
2.1
tells us that all primes
\mathfrak{p}
,
\tilde{b}_{n}+\,\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}
equals 0 or 1. There are two cases.

(1) Suppose that
\tilde{b}_{n}=0
. Then, for all primes
{\mathbf{p}}
,
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}
equals
0
or
1
. Thus,

\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda^{1}-2}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}

for some
m\geq 1
.

(2) Suppose that
\tilde{b}_{n}\neq 0
. Then
\tilde{b}_{n}=1
and for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
,
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
. Thus,

\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{\lambda^{1}-1}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}.

Now assume that
m={\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f=2
. Then, analogous to what we saw in the
\lambda^{1}=2
situation, Theorem
2.2
implies that
\operatorname{tr}\alpha_{0}=\pm 1
and so
{\operatorname{char}}_{\alpha_{0}}(t)=t^{2}\pm t+1
. Thus we must be in Case (1) above and either
m=1
or
m
is a product of prime powers where the primes are either
3
or congruent to
1
modulo
6
. ∎

Proof of Proposition
1.6
.
Suppose
\lambda^{0}=\lambda^{1}=3
.

Theorem
2.1
tells us that
\tilde{b}_{n-1}<3
. Proposition
1.6
implies
\tilde{b}_{n-1}\neq 0,1
. Thus, we must have
\tilde{b}_{n-1}=\tilde{b}_{n}=2
. But then Theorem
2.1
also tells us that for all primes
\mathfrak{p}
,
\tau_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
, i.e., there can be no torsion. Thus

\widetilde{H}^{n-1}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\widetilde{H}^{n}(F_{f,\mathbf{0}};\ {\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}.

Now suppose that
|\Sigma f|
has exactly one irreducible component
C
. Then
{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}C\neq 3
, for if it were, then
\mu^{\circ}_{C}
would have to be 1, and Proposition
2.3
would tell us that
\tilde{b}_{n-1}\leq 1
; a contradiction. It is also true that
{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}C\neq 2
, for if it were
\lambda^{1}
would be even; a contradiction. Therefore
C
is smooth and transversely intersected by
V(z_{0})
at
{\mathbf{0}}
, and
\mu^{\circ}_{C}
must be 3. ∎

4.
Examples or the lack thereof

Here, we wish to give examples of the various cases from the introduction or say that no such examples are known (to us).

Example 4.1
.

Consider the case where
\lambda^{0}=0
. Examples of this are trivial to produce, simply take a product of an isolated singularity with a complex line, e.g.,
f(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}
.

Example 4.2
.

Consider the case where
\lambda^{1}=1
and
\lambda^{0}\geq 1
. It is easy to produce examples of this case, for instance
f=z_{2}^{2}-z_{1}^{3}-z_{0}z_{1}^{2}
.

Remark 4.3
.

Consider the case where
\lambda^{0}=1
and
\lambda^{1}\geq 2
.
We do not know if this case can actually occur.
Certainly it cannot occur for a smooth critical locus, since we showed
m={\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}|\Sigma f|
must be 2.

Example 4.4
.

Consider the case where
\lambda^{1}=2
and
\lambda^{0}\geq 2
. We showed that
\lambda^{0}
must be at least 3 in this case. It is easy to produce examples in all 3 subcases (a), (b), and (c) from the proof of Proposition
1.4
. We have the examples, in order,

f=(z_{0}^{2}-z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})z_{2},\hskip 14.45377ptg=(z_{1}^{2}-z_{0}^{3}+z_{2})z_{2},\ \ \textnormal{ and }\hskip 7.22743pth=z_{2}^{2}-z_{1}^{4}-z_{0}z_{1}^{3}.

Note that, as
h
is the “suspension” of
z_{1}^{4}-z_{0}z_{1}^{3}
, there is no torsion in the cohomology of the Milnor fiber of
h
even though Proposition
1.4
allows for it to exist.

Example 4.5
.

Consider the case where
\lambda^{0}=2
and
\lambda^{1}\geq 3
. Examples of this are not so easy to produce, but they exist. For ease, let us denote our coordinates by
(x,y,z)
in place of
(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2})
.

Let
f=(z^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2})(z-x)
. Then, we have
\Sigma f=V(y,z-x)
, and

V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\right)=V(2z(z-x)+(z^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2})).

We find, as cycles, that

V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y},\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\right)=V(y,3z+x)+3V(y,z-x),

so that
\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=V(y,3z+x)
and
\Lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=3V(y,z-x)
. It is now easy to show that
\lambda^{0}=2
and
\lambda^{1}=3
.

In fact, we can show that we are in Case (1) of Proposition
1.5
. Up to analytic isomorphism,
f
is the homogeneous polynomial
f=(zx-y^{2})z
. Consequently,
F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}}
is diffeomorphic to
f^{-1}(1)
, i.e., to the space where
(zx-y^{2})z=1
; thus
z\neq 0
and
x=(y^{2}+1/z)/z
. Thus,
F_{f,{\mathbf{0}}}
is homotopy equivalent to
S^{1}
.

Remark 4.6
.

Finally, consider the case where
\lambda^{0}=\lambda^{1}=3
.
Once again, we do not know if this case can actually occur.

5.
Remarks

Remark 5.1
.

Given the result of Siersma in
[
12
]
, we suspected that Theorem
2.1
should be the correct generalization on the level of cohomology. Our original attack on proving such a generalization was to show that a non-empty relative polar curve necessarily implies that the internal monodromy in Proposition
2.3
cannot be the identity. However, the example/counterexample of
f=z_{2}^{2}-z_{1}^{3}-z_{0}^{2}z_{1}^{2}
shows that this is not true.

Remark 5.2
.

At the end of Example 5.1 of
[
11
]
, we address the case where
\lambda^{1}=2
,
\lambda^{0}\neq 0
, and
{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}|\Sigma f|=1
. There we concluded quickly that there can be no torsion in the cohomology of the Milnor fiber. While it is possible that this is true, certainly we did not give a careful argument that proved it. As of now, the best we can say is what we wrote in Proposition
1.4
.

Remark 5.3
.

Throughout this article, we have allowed for torsion in the cohomology of the Milnor fiber. In fact, we do not know if such torsion can possibly exist when
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f=1
.

However, when
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f=2
, there are the famous examples of Cohen, Denham, and Suciu in
[
3
]
of hypersurfaces with torsion in the cohomology of the Milnor fiber.

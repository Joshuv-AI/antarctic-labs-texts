2.
Preliminaries

We use standard group-theoretical notation.
For a group
G
, we write its center as
Z(G)
, its derived subgroup as
G^{\prime}
and its Frattini subgroup as
\Phi(G)
.
The
n
-th term of the lower central series is denoted by
\gamma_{n}(G)
.
For
g,h\in G
, the commutator is defined as
[g,h]=g^{-1}h^{-1}gh
and the conjugate is defined as
g^{h}=h^{-1}gh
.
The conjugacy class of
g
in
G
is denoted by
g^{G}
and the order of
g
is denoted by
|g|
.
For a subgroup
K\leq G
, its centralizer and normalizer in
G
are denoted by
C_{G}(K)
and
N_{G}(K)
.
Let
p
be a prime and suppose that
G
is a finite
p
-group.
For a normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
, we define

\Omega(G:N)=\langle\,g\in G\mid g^{p}\in N\,\rangle.

For a non-negative integer
k
, we define
\Omega_{k}(G)=\langle\,g\in G\mid g^{p^{k}}=1\,\rangle
and
G^{p^{k}}=\langle\,g^{p^{k}}\mid g\in G\,\rangle
.
A cyclic group of order
n
is denoted by
C_{n}
.

For a commutative ring
S
and a finite group
G
, we write the center of the group algebra
SG
as
Z(SG)
.
For
A,B\in SG
, the Lie commutator is defined as
[A,B]=AB-BA
.
The augmentation ideal
\Delta(SG)
of
SG
is defined to be the kernel of a map

\varepsilon_{SG}\colon SG\to S,\ \ \sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}g\mapsto\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g},

called the augmentation map.

For an ideal
\mathfrak{n}
of
S
, we set
F=S/\mathfrak{n}
and

\Delta(SG:\mathfrak{n})=\{\,x\in SG\mid\varepsilon_{SG}(x)\in\mathfrak{n}\,\}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\Delta(SG).

Let
\pi\colon S\to F
be the natural projection and extend it to
\hat{\pi}\colon SG\to FG
in the natural manner.
Note that
\Ker\hat{\pi}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)
.

In the rest of this section we will prove some preparatory lemmas which will then serve to study the concrete groups in the next section.
We start with a lemma which is well known, but seems not to be contained in this short and general form in the literature.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
and
S
be commutative rings and
G
and
H
finite groups.
Suppose that there is a ring homomorphism
R\to S
.
If
RG\cong RH
, then
SG\cong SH
.

Proof.

Let
\rho\colon R\to S
be a ring homomorphism and
\varphi\colon RG\to RH
an
R
-algebra isomorphism.
For
g,h\in G
, take
\alpha_{g,h}\in R
that satisfy
\varphi(g)=\sum_{h\in H}\alpha_{g,h}h
and define
\psi\colon SG\to SH
by
\psi(g)=\sum_{h\in H}\rho(\alpha_{g,h})h
.
It is straightforward to check that
\psi
is indeed an
S
-algebra isomorphism whose inverse is defined similarly.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
S
be a commutative ring with an ideal
\mathfrak{n}
and
G
a finite group.
Write
F=S/\mathfrak{n}
,
\pi\colon S\to F
and
\hat{\pi}\colon SG\to FG
as before.
Then

\Delta(SG)\cap\hat{\pi}^{-1}(\Delta(FG)^{2})=\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)+\Delta(SG)^{2}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the right-hand side is contained in the left-hand side.
To see the converse, take
A\in\Delta(SG)\cap\hat{\pi}^{-1}(\Delta(FG)^{2})
.
Then there are some
\alpha_{g,h}\in F
such that

\hat{\pi}(A)=\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{h\in G}\alpha_{g,h}(g-1)(h-1).

Since
\pi
is surjective, there are some
\beta_{g,h}\in S
such that
\pi(\beta_{g,h})=\alpha_{g,h}
.
Then

A-\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{h\in G}\beta_{g,h}(g-1)(h-1)\in\Ker\hat{\pi}.

It follows from
(g-1)(h-1)\in\Delta(SG)^{2}
and
\Ker\hat{\pi}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)
that
A\in\mathfrak{n}\oplus(\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)+\Delta(SG)^{2})
.
Since
A\in\Delta(SG)
, we conclude that
A\in\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)+\Delta(SG)^{2}
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
S
be a commutative ring with an ideal
\mathfrak{n}
and
G
a finite group.
Write
F=S/\mathfrak{n}
.
Then there is an isomorphism of
F
-modules

\Delta(SG:\mathfrak{n})/\Delta(SG:\mathfrak{n})^{2}\cong\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^{2}\oplus\Delta(FG)/\Delta(FG)^{2}.

Proof.

Recall that
\Delta(SG:\mathfrak{n})=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\Delta(SG)
.
As

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta(SG:\mathfrak{n})}{\Delta(SG:\mathfrak{n})^{2}}=\frac{\mathfrak{n}\oplus\Delta(SG)}{\mathfrak{n}^{2}\oplus(\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)+\Delta(SG)^{2})}\cong\frac{\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{n}^{2}}\oplus\frac{\Delta(SG)}{\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)+\Delta(SG)^{2}},

it suffices to prove that

\frac{\Delta(SG)}{\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)+\Delta(SG)^{2}}\cong\frac{\Delta(FG)}{\Delta(FG)^{2}}.

Consider the canonical map
\Delta(SG)\to\Delta(FG)/\Delta(FG)^{2}
.
By
2.2
, we see that the kernel of this map is
\mathfrak{n}\Delta(SG)+\Delta(SG)^{2}
as expected.
∎

The
n
-th dimension subgroup of a group
G
with respect to a commutative ring
S
is the subgroup of
G
defined by

D_{n,S}(G)=\{\,g\in G\mid g-1\in\Delta(SG)^{n}\,\}.

When
G
is a finite
p
-group and
S
is a field of characteristic
p
, the following purely group-theoretical formula describing the dimension subgroups was observed by Jennings and reformulated by Lazard.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
15
, Theorem 11.1.20]
)
.

Let
F
be a field of positive characteristic
p
and
G
a finite
p
-group.
Then
D_{n,F}(G)=\prod_{ip^{j}\geq n}\gamma_{i}(G)^{p^{j}}
.
In particular,

\Delta(FG)/\Delta(FG)^{2}\cong F\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}G/\Phi(G)

as
F
-vector spaces and a basis of
\Delta(FG)/\Delta(FG)^{2}
is given by representatives
g_{1}-1
, …,
g_{\ell}-1
where
g_{1}
, …,
g_{\ell}
form a minimal generating set of
G
.

This kind of compact formula is not known for dimension subgroups in the general situation, though some information is available, see e.g.
[
14
,
18
]
.

Moreover, the following is also well known, but we give a short proof here as we did not find it formulated in that compact form in the literature.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
S
be a commutative ring,
G
a group and
n
a positive integer.
Then
\gamma_{n}(G)\leq D_{n,S}(G)
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
n\geq 1
.
The base case is trivial.
Let
g\in G
and
h\in\gamma_{n}(G)
.
As
h\in D_{n,S}(G)
by the inductive hypothesis, we have
[h-1,g-1]\in\Delta(SG)^{n+1}
.
So

[h-1,g-1]=hg-gh=hg(1-[g,h])

implies that
[g,h]\in D_{n+1,S}(G)
.
∎

The following easy observations will be very useful for calculations in group algebras over commutative rings of characteristic
4

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
p
be a prime,
n
a positive integers and
0<i<p^{n}
.
Then

\nu\left(\binom{p^{n}}{i}\right)=(p-1)(n-\nu(i)),

where
\nu
denotes the
p
-adic valuation.

Proof.

Let
k=\nu(i)
and expand
i
as

i=i_{k}p^{k}+i_{k+1}p^{k+1}+\dotsb+i_{n-1}p^{n-1},

where
0\leq i_{k},i_{k+1},\dotsc,i_{n-1}<p
and
i_{k}\neq 0
.
Since

p^{n}-1=(p-1)(1+p+\dots+p^{n-1})=p^{k}-1+(p-1)p^{k}+\dots+(p-1)p^{n-1},

we obtain

p^{n}-i=(p-i_{k})p^{k}+(p-1-i_{k+1})p^{k+1}+\dotsb+(p-1-i_{n-1})p^{n-1}.

It follows from Kummer’s theorem
[
10
, p. 116]
on binomial coefficients that

\displaystyle\nu\left(\binom{p^{n}}{i}\right)

\displaystyle=(i_{k}+i_{k+1}+\dotsb+i_{n-1})

\displaystyle\qquad+((p-i_{k})+(p-1-i_{k+1})+\dotsb+(p-1-i_{n-1}))-1

\displaystyle=(p-1)(n-k).\qed

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
S
be a commutative ring with
4S=0
,
G
a group and
A,B\in SG
two commuting elements.
Then for each positive integer
n
we have

(A+B)^{2^{n}}=A^{2^{n}}+B^{2^{n}}+2A^{2^{n-1}}B^{2^{n-1}}.

In particular, for

we have

(g-1)^{2^{n}}=(g^{2^{n}}-1)+2(g^{2^{n-1}}-1).

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\binom{2^{n}}{0}=\binom{2^{n}}{2^{n}}\equiv 1\mod 4
,
\binom{2^{n}}{2^{n-1}}\equiv 2\mod 4
and
\binom{2^{n}}{i}\equiv 0\mod 4
otherwise.
Of course,
\binom{2^{n}}{0}=\binom{2^{n}}{2^{n}}=1
.
Let
\nu
denote the
2
-adic valuation.
By
2.6
we have
\nu\left(\binom{2^{n}}{i}\right)=n-\nu(i)
for every
0<i<2^{n}
.
So
\binom{2^{n}}{i}\equiv 0\mod 4
for
i\neq 2^{n-1}
and
\binom{2^{n}}{2^{n-1}}\equiv 2\mod 4
.
∎

Let

Then

\displaystyle|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{n}}\times C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|

\displaystyle\qquad=(n-m)p^{m+\ell}+(p^{2\ell+1}+p^{2\ell})\frac{p^{m-\ell}-1}{p-1}+(p^{2}+p+1)\frac{p^{2\ell}-1}{p^{2}-1}+1.

Proof.

There are many known formulas to calculate the number of cyclic subgroups of a finite abelian group and we will use the one given in
[
20
, Theorem 1]
.
In our case it reads

|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{n}}\times C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq n\\
0\leq j\leq m\\
0\leq k\leq\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{\phi(p^{i})\phi(p^{j})\phi(p^{k})}{\phi(\lcm(p^{i},p^{j},p^{k}))}.

To get the closed formula, we derive three difference equations first.

\left\{\begin{aligned} |\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{n+1}}\times C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|-|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{n}}\times C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|&=p^{m+\ell},\\
|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{m+1}}\times C_{p^{m+1}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|-|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|&=p^{m+\ell+1}+p^{m+\ell},\\
|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{\ell+1}}\times C_{p^{\ell+1}}\times C_{p^{\ell+1}})|-|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{\ell}}\times C_{p^{\ell}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|&=p^{2\ell+2}+p^{2\ell+1}+p^{2\ell}.\end{aligned}\right.

We only show the second one in details as similar arguments show the rest as well.
Recall that
\phi(p^{m+1})=p^{m+1}-p^{m}
and
\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell}\phi(p^{k})=p^{\ell}
.
Then

\displaystyle|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{m+1}}\times C_{p^{m+1}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|-|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
0\leq k\leq\ell\end{subarray}}\phi(p^{j})\phi(p^{k})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq m\\
0\leq k\leq\ell\end{subarray}}\phi(p^{i})\phi(p^{k})-\phi(p^{m+1})\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell}\phi(p^{k})

\displaystyle\qquad=p^{m+\ell}+p^{m+\ell}-(p^{m+1}-p^{m})p^{\ell}

\displaystyle\qquad=p^{m+\ell+1}+p^{m+\ell}.

From these difference equations, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{n}}\times C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|

\displaystyle\qquad=(n-m)p^{m+\ell}+|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{m}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|

\displaystyle\qquad=(n-m)p^{m+\ell}+(p^{2\ell+1}+p^{2\ell})\frac{p^{m-\ell}-1}{p-1}+|\mathscr{C}(C_{p^{\ell}}\times C_{p^{\ell}}\times C_{p^{\ell}})|

\displaystyle\qquad=(n-m)p^{m+\ell}+(p^{2\ell+1}+p^{2\ell})\frac{p^{m-\ell}-1}{p-1}+(p^{2}+p+1)\frac{p^{2\ell}-1}{p^{2}-1}+1.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be the group
G
or
H
defined in
A
and
K
a cyclic subgroup of
C_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})
.
Then

K\trianglelefteq\Gamma\iff\text{$K\leq Z(\Gamma)$ or $K\leq\Omega(\Gamma:\Gamma^{\prime})$}.

Proof.

Set
\alpha=x
,
\beta=y
and
\gamma=z
, if
\Gamma=G
and
\alpha=a
,
\beta=b
and
\gamma=c
, if
\Gamma=H
.
Observe that
C_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})
is a maximal and abelian subgroup of
\Gamma
generated by
\alpha\beta
,
\alpha^{2}
,
\beta^{2}
and
\gamma
.
Set
\delta=y^{2}
if
\Gamma=G
and
\delta=a^{2}
, if
\Gamma=H
.
Then

\displaystyle C_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha^{-1}\beta\rangle\times\langle\delta\rangle\times\langle\gamma\rangle.

Moreover,
\Omega(\Gamma:\Gamma^{\prime})
is generated by
\gamma
and the socle of
\langle\alpha^{-1}\beta,\delta\rangle
which is also central in
\Gamma
.
Hence, the second condition implies the first.
So we assume
K\trianglelefteq\Gamma
and
K\not\leq Z(\Gamma)
and prove
K\leq\Omega(\Gamma:\Gamma^{\prime})
.

Let
g
be a generator of
K
and write it as

g=(\alpha^{-1}\beta)^{i}\delta^{j}\gamma^{k}\qquad(0\leq i<|\alpha^{-1}\beta|,\ 0\leq j<|\delta|,\ 0\leq k<|\gamma|).

The first assumption
K\trianglelefteq\Gamma
implies
K^{\alpha}=K
.
As
g^{\alpha}=g[g,\alpha]
, there is an integer
t\geq 0
such that
[g,\alpha]=g^{t}
.
Since
[g,\alpha]=\gamma^{i-2k}
, we obtain

(\alpha^{-1}\beta)^{it}=1,\quad\delta^{jt}=1,\quad\gamma^{kt}=\gamma^{i-2k}

and accordingly

(4.1)

\nu(it)\geq\nu(|\alpha^{-1}\beta|),\quad\nu(jt)\geq\nu(|\delta|),\quad\nu(kt-i+2k)\geq\nu(|\gamma|).

The second assumption
K\not\leq Z(\Gamma)
implies
[g,\alpha]\neq 1
as
\Gamma=\langle\alpha,C_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})\rangle
.
So
\gamma^{kt}\neq 1
and

(4.2)

\nu(|\gamma|)>\nu(kt).

From (
4.2
), the first of (
4.1
) and
|\alpha^{-1}\beta|>|\gamma|
, we obtain
\nu(i)\geq\nu(|\alpha^{-1}\beta|)-\nu(t)>\nu(|\gamma|)-\nu(t)\geq\nu(2k)
, which implies
\nu(i-2k)=\nu(2k)
.
From (
4.2
) and the third of (
4.1
), we also obtain
\nu(kt-i+2k)>\nu(kt)
, which implies
\nu(i-2k)=\nu(kt-kt+i-2k)=\nu(kt)
.
These yield
\nu(2k)=\nu(kt)
, and hence
\nu(t)=1
.
Thus
\langle g^{2}\rangle=\langle g^{t}\rangle=\langle[g,\alpha]\rangle
and
K\leq\Omega(\Gamma:\Gamma^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
and
H
be the groups defined in
A
.
Then

|\mathscr{C}(H)|-|\mathscr{C}(G)|=(n-m)2^{m-1}(2^{\ell-1}-1).

Proof.

We start the calculation, first for
G
.
For better overview we record the conjugation action:

y^{x}=yz,\ x^{y}=xz^{-1},\ z^{x}=z^{y}=z^{-1},\ x^{z}=xz^{2},\ y^{z}=yz^{2}.

Inside maximal abelian subgroup of
G

We first consider the elements in the maximal subgroup which is also abelian

C_{G}(G^{\prime})=\langle xy\rangle\times\langle y^{2}\rangle\times\langle z\rangle\cong C_{2^{n}}\times C_{2^{m-1}}\times C_{2^{\ell}}.

Every element in
C_{G}(G^{\prime})
has either class length
1
, when it lies in the center
Z(G)
, or
2
, as then the centralizer equals
C_{G}(G^{\prime})
.
By
4.3
, we can count the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of
G
lying in
C_{G}(G^{\prime})
using the inclusion-exclusion principle as

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in C_{G}(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{|G:N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in Z(G)\cup\Omega(G:G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{g\in C_{G}(G^{\prime})\setminus(Z(G)\cup\Omega(G:G^{\prime}))}\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(|\mathscr{C}(C_{G}(G^{\prime}))|+|\mathscr{C}(Z(G))|+|\mathscr{C}(\Omega(G:G^{\prime}))|-|\mathscr{C}(\Omega(G:G^{\prime})\cap Z(G))|\big).

Outside maximal abelian subgroup of
G

The elements of
G\setminus C_{G}(G^{\prime})=x\Phi(G)\sqcup y\Phi(G)
are of the form

x(x^{2})^{i}(y^{2})^{j}z^{k}\quad\text{or}\quad y(x^{2})^{i}(y^{2})^{j}z^{k}\qquad(0\leq i<2^{n-1},\ 0\leq j<2^{m-1},\ 0\leq k<2^{\ell}).

Such an element
g
satisfies
g^{yx}=gz
which implies
g^{G}=gG^{\prime}
.
It also satisfies
z^{2^{\ell-1}}\not\in\langle g\rangle
, so
G^{\prime}\cap\langle g\rangle=1
and thus
N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)=C_{G}(g)
.
For the elements of
x\Phi(G)
note that they have a common order
2^{n}
.
So the contribution is

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in x\Phi(G)}\frac{1}{|G:N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}=\frac{|x\Phi(G)|}{|G^{\prime}|\times|(\mathbb{Z}/2^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|}=\frac{2^{n-1}\times 2^{m-1}\times 2^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}\times 2^{n-1}}=2^{m-1}.

For the elements of
y\Phi(G)
the calculation is more delicate as their orders are not uniform.
Consider a chain of sets

y\Omega_{m}(\Phi(G))\subseteq y\Omega_{m+1}(\Phi(G))\subseteq\dotsb\subseteq y\Omega_{n-1}(\Phi(G))=y\Phi(G)

and observe that an element
g\in y\Omega_{t}(\Phi(G))\setminus y\Omega_{t-1}(\Phi(G))
has order
2^{t}
for each
m+1\leq t\leq n-1
.
Also an element
g\in y\Omega_{m}(\Phi(G))
has order
2^{m}
.
So the contribution is

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in y\Phi(G)}\frac{1}{|G:N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{g\in y\Omega_{m}(\Phi(G))}\frac{1}{|G:N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{t=m+1}^{n-1}\sum_{g\in y\Omega_{t}(\Phi(G))\setminus y\Omega_{t-1}(\Phi(G))}\frac{1}{|G:N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{|y\Omega_{m}(\Phi(G))|}{|G^{\prime}|\times|(\mathbb{Z}/2^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|}+\sum_{t=m+1}^{n-1}\frac{|y\Omega_{t}(\Phi(G))\setminus y\Omega_{t-1}(\Phi(G))|}{|G^{\prime}|\times|(\mathbb{Z}/2^{t}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2^{m}\times 2^{m-1}\times 2^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}\times 2^{m-1}}+\sum_{t=m+1}^{n-1}\frac{2^{t-1}\times 2^{m-1}\times 2^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}\times 2^{t-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad=2^{m}+(n-m-1)2^{m-1}.

The overall contribution of
G\setminus C_{G}(G^{\prime})
to
|\mathscr{C}(G)|
is

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in G\setminus C_{G}(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{|G:N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in x\Phi(G)}\frac{1}{|G:N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}+\sum_{g\in y\Phi(G)}\frac{1}{|G:N_{G}(\langle g\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|g|)}

\displaystyle=2^{m}+(n-m)2^{m-1}.

For the calculation in
H
we also record the conjugacy action, which is essentially the same as for
G
:

b^{a}=bc,\ a^{b}=ac^{-1},\ c^{a}=c^{b}=c^{-1},\ a^{c}=ac^{2},\ b^{c}=bc^{2}.

Inside maximal abelian subgroup of
H

The arguments are very similar to the case of the maximal abelian subgroup of
G
, so we make them shorter.
First note that

C_{H}(H^{\prime})=\langle a^{2}\rangle\times\langle a^{-1}b\rangle\times\langle c\rangle\cong C_{2^{n-1}}\times C_{2^{m}}\times C_{2^{\ell}}

is a maximal subgroup of
H
which is abelian.
The class length of an element in
C_{H}(H^{\prime})
is
1
, if it is central in
H
, and
2
otherwise.
By
4.3
, again by the inclusion-exclusion principle, we obtain the contribution to
|\mathscr{C}(H)|
as

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in C_{H}(H^{\prime})}\frac{1}{|H:N_{H}(\langle h\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|h|)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in Z(H)\cup\Omega(H:H^{\prime})}\frac{1}{\phi(|h|)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{h\in C_{H}(H^{\prime})\setminus(Z(H)\cup\Omega(H:H^{\prime}))}\frac{1}{\phi(|h|)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(|\mathscr{C}(C_{H}(H^{\prime}))|+|\mathscr{C}(Z(H))|+|\mathscr{C}(\Omega(H:H^{\prime}))|-|\mathscr{C}(\Omega(H:H^{\prime})\cap Z(H))|\big).

Outside maximal abelian subgroup of
H

The elements in
H\setminus C_{H}(H^{\prime})=aC_{H}(H^{\prime})
are of the form

a(a^{2})^{i}(a^{-1}b)^{j}c^{k}\qquad(0\leq i<2^{n-1},\ 0\leq j<2^{m},\ 0\leq k<2^{\ell}).

Such an element
h
satisfies
h^{ba}=hc
which implies
h^{H}=hH^{\prime}
and its order equals
2^{n}
.
It also satisfies
c^{2^{\ell-1}}\not\in\langle h\rangle
, so
H^{\prime}\cap\langle h\rangle=1
and thus
N_{H}(\langle h\rangle)=C_{H}(h)
.
The overall contribution to
|\mathscr{C}(H)|
is

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in H\setminus C_{H}(H^{\prime})}\frac{1}{|H:N_{H}(\langle h\rangle)|}\times\frac{1}{\phi(|h|)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|H\setminus C_{H}(H^{\prime})|}{|H^{\prime}|\times|(\mathbb{Z}/2^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|}=\frac{2^{n-1}\times 2^{m}\times 2^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}\times 2^{n-1}}=2^{m}.

Comparing the values

We first note that

\displaystyle Z(G)\cong Z(H)

\displaystyle\cong C_{2^{n-1}}\times C_{2^{m-1}}\times C_{2}

\displaystyle\Omega(G:G^{\prime})\cong\Omega(H:H^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}\times C_{2^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\Omega(G:G^{\prime})\cap Z(G)\cong\Omega(H:H^{\prime})\cap Z(H)

\displaystyle\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}\times C_{2}.

So, comparing the expressions in (
4.3
) and (
4.5
), the groups are pairwise isomorphic except for
C_{G}(G^{\prime})
and
C_{H}(H^{\prime})
themselves.
Hence, the difference between the numbers of conjugacy classes of cyclic groups of
H
and
G
, which equals
\eqref{eq:CycHB}+\eqref{eq:CycHNonB}-\eqref{eq:CycGA}-\eqref{eq:CycGNonA}
is

(4.7)

|\mathscr{C}(H)|-|\mathscr{C}(G)|=\frac{1}{2}\big(|\mathscr{C}(C_{H}(H^{\prime}))|-|\mathscr{C}(C_{G}(G^{\prime}))|\big)-(n-m)2^{m-1}.

Recall that
C_{H}(H^{\prime})\cong C_{2^{n-1}}\times C_{2^{m}}\times C_{2^{\ell}}
and
C_{G}(G^{\prime})\cong C_{2^{n}}\times C_{2^{m-1}}\times C_{2^{\ell}}
.
It follows from
4.2
that

\displaystyle|\mathscr{C}(C_{H}(H^{\prime}))|-|\mathscr{C}(C_{G}(G^{\prime}))|

\displaystyle\qquad=((n-m-1)2^{m+\ell}+(2^{2\ell+1}+2^{2\ell})(2^{m-\ell}-1))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-((n-m+1)2^{m+\ell-1}+(2^{2\ell+1}+2^{2\ell})(2^{m-\ell-1}-1))

\displaystyle\qquad=(n-m)2^{m+\ell-1}.

Hence substituting
|\mathscr{C}(C_{H}(H^{\prime}))|-|\mathscr{C}(C_{G}(G^{\prime}))|
in (

) gives

|\mathscr{C}(H)|-|\mathscr{C}(G)|=(n-m)2^{m-1}(2^{\ell-1}-1).

∎

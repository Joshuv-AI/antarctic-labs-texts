Introduction

A
total coloring
of a graph
G
is an assignment of colors to both the edges and vertices of
G
so that the coloring on the edges induces an edge-coloring, and the coloring on the vertices induces a vertex-coloring, and an incident edge and vertex must also receive different colors. The
total chromatic number
of
G
, denoted
\chi_{T}(G)
, is the minimum number of colors needed for a total coloring of
G
. In this paper we use the term ‘graph’ to mean simple graph, and will explicitly say ‘multigraph’ when parallel edges are allowed; loops will not appear in this paper. For terms not defined here, we follow
[
25
]
.

The
Total Coloring Conjecture
, proposed independently by Behzad
[
1
]
and Vizing
[
24
]
in the 1960’s, asserts that
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2
for every graph
G
, where
\Delta(G)
is the maximum degree of
G
. Note that since a vertex of maximum degree must receive different colors from all its incident edges, every graph
G
satisfies
\chi_{T}(G)\geq\Delta(G)+1
. The conjecture is therefore a natural analog of Vizing’s Theorem which states that
\Delta(G)\leq\chi^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+1
, where
\chi^{\prime}(G)
is the chromatic index of
G
. Determining each of
\chi_{T}(G)
and
\chi^{\prime}(G)
exactly is known to be NP-hard
[
13
,
21
]
.

There is a large literature towards the Total Coloring Conjecture that we will not survey; the interested reader is referred to
[
4
,
6
,
14
]
. Relevant to our discussion here is that the conjecture appears to get easier as the maximum degree increases, particularly when the graph is regular. In 1993 Hilton and Hind
[
12
]
confirmed the Total Coloring Conjecture for all graphs with
\Delta(G)\geq\frac{3}{4}|V(G)|
. Recently the second and third authors, along with Dalal, lowered this bound to almost
\frac{1}{2}|V(G)|
for sufficiently large regular graphs
[
4
]
. In this paper we extend that result in two directions, by replacing
\Delta(G)
with minimum degree
\delta(G)
and by removing the requirement of regularity.

Theorem 1
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
G
is a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\varepsilon)n
, then
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta+2
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
M
be a matching in the complement
\overline{G}
of
G
. Define
G^{M}
as the graph obtained from
G
by first adding one new vertex
x
, and then adding both the edges of
M
and the edges of
E(x)=\{xu:u\in V(G)\setminus V(M)\}
. We say an edge-coloring
\varphi
of
G^{M}
is
good
if
\varphi
has at most
\Delta(G)+2
colors and each edge of
M\cup E(x)
is colored with a different color under
\varphi
. Note that a good edge-coloring of
G^{M}
immediately leads to a total coloring of
G
with at most
\Delta(G)+2
colors, because the auxiliary edge-colors can be used on the vertices of
G
.

Our proof of Theorem
1
has two main parts: first we reduce Theorem
1
to two different cases for
G,M
, and then we show that both of these cases lead to
G^{M}
having a good edge-coloring. Section 2 of this paper contains our reduction, which relies on some classic results of Hajnal and Szemerédi
[
9
]
, Hakimi
[
11
]
, Vizing and Gupta
[
23
,
8
]
and Tutte and Berge
[
22
,
2
]
along with some more recent work of Shan
[
19
]
and Plantholt and Shan
[
18
]
. Section 3 contains a new lemma about edge-coloring certain multigraphs so that specific edges are guaranteed to have different colors. Section 4 uses this lemma to complete the proof of Theorem
1
, namely by showing that
G^{M}
has a good edge-coloring in both of the cases we have reduced to.

2
Reduction

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph, and define
V_{\delta}(G)
to be the set of all minimum-degree vertices in
G
, that is,
V_{\delta}(G)=\{v\in V(G)|d_{G}(v)=\delta(G)\}
. For
v\in V(G)
, we say it has
middle-degree
if
\delta(G)<d_{G}(v)<\Delta(G)
. Finally, for any
\xi>0
we define

U_{\xi}(G)=\{u\in V(G)|\Delta(G)-d_{G}(u)\geq\xi n\};

we can think of
U_{\xi}(G)
as being those vertices in
G
of sufficiently small degree.

Consider the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\xi
with
0<\xi\ll\varepsilon
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such
G^{M}
has a good edge-colouring, provided
G,M
satisfy all of the following:

(a)

G
is a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\varepsilon)n
and
\Delta(G)<\frac{3}{4}n
;

(b)

M
is a matching in
\overline{G}
, and;

(c)

G,M
lies in one of the following two cases:

(1)

|M|=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}-0.1\varepsilon n\rfloor
and either
G
is regular or
|V_{\delta}(G)|<\xi n
, the quantity
n-|V_{\delta}(G)|
is odd, and
G
has no middle-degree vertices;

(2)

|M|\in\{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}-0.1\varepsilon n\rfloor,n-1-\Delta(G)\}
and
|U_{\xi}(G)|\geq\xi n
.

We shall prove in this section that Theorem
2
implies Theorem
1
. In order to do so, we’ll need the following four classic results.

Theorem 3

(Hakimi
[
11
]
)
.

Let
0\leq d_{n}\leq\ldots\leq d_{1}
be integers. Then there exists a multigraph
G
on vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}
such that
d_{G}(v_{i})=d_{i}
for all
i\in[1,n]
if and only if
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
is even and
\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}d_{i}\geq d_{1}
.

Theorem 4

(Dirac
[
5
]
)
.

If
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
n\geq 3
and
\delta(G)\geq\tfrac{n}{2}
, then
G
has a Hamilton cycle.

Theorem 5

(Hajnal and Szemerédi
[
10
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
k\geq\Delta(G)+1
be any integer. Then
G
has an equitable
k
-coloring, that is, a
k
-coloring where the number of vertices in any two distinct color classes differ by at most one.

Theorem 6

(Gupta
[
8
]
and Vizing
[
23
]
)
.

Every multigraph
G
satisfies
\chi^{\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+\mu(G)
.

Let
k\geq 0
be an integer. A
k
-edge-coloring of a multigraph
G
is said to be
equalized
if each color class contains either
\lfloor|E(G)|/k\rfloor
or
\lceil|E(G)|/k\rceil
edges. McDiarmid
[
16
]
observed the following result that we will find useful.

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be a multigraph with chromatic index
\chi^{\prime}(G)
. Then for all
k\geq\chi^{\prime}(G)
, there is an equalized edge-coloring of
G
with
k
colors.

We will also need the following results of Shan
[
19
]
and Plantholt and Shan
[

]
.

Lemma 8

(Shan
[
19
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

There exists a positive integer
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
the
following holds. Let
G
be a graph on
2n
vertices, and
N=\{x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{t},y_{t}\}\subseteq V(G)
, where
1\leq t\leq n

Then
V(G)
can be partitioned into two parts

A
and
B
satisfying the properties below:

(i)

|A|=|B|
;

(ii)

|A\cap\{x_{i},y_{i}\}|=1
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
;

(iii)

|d_{A}(v)-d_{B}(v)|\leq n^{2/3}
for each

.

Furthermore, one such partition can be constructed in

-time.

Lemma 9

(Plantholt and Shan
[
17
]
)
.

Let
0<1/n_{0}\ll\varepsilon<1
and
G
be a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices such that
\delta(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)n/2
. Moreover, let

be a matching in the complete graph on

of size at most

. Then there exist vertex-disjoint paths

,

We will prove one new lemma about matchings to use for our reduction, and for this we need the Tutte-Berge Formula.

Theorem 10

(Tutte
[
22
]
and Berge
[
2
]
)
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex graph. Then the size of a maximum matching in
H
is equal to
\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\min}_{S\subseteq V(G)}\{n+|S|-\odd(H-S)\}
, where
\odd(H-S)
denotes the number of components of odd order in
H-S
.

Within the proof of our matching lemma, which follows, and indeed for our later work as well, we use
E_{G}(S,T)
denote the set of edges of a graph
G
with one endpoint in some
S\subseteq V(G)
and one endpoint in
T\subseteq V(G)
. We let
e_{G}(S,T)=|E_{G}(S,T)|
. For convenience we may use this same notation when
S
or
T
is a subgraph of
G
, instead of writing
V(S),V(T)
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
0<\xi\ll 1
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{1}{n_{0}}\ll\xi
. Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
,
\Delta(G)<\frac{3n}{4}
and
|U_{\xi}(G)|<\xi n
. Then there exists a matching
M
in
\overline{G}
with
|M|\geq\frac{n}{2}-1.5\xi n
.

Proof.

Let
H=\overline{G}
. Then
\Delta(H)<\frac{n}{2}
and
\delta(H)>\frac{n}{4}-1
.
Let
S\subseteq V(H)
. We’ll show that
\odd(H-S)-|S|<3\xi n
, from which the Tutte-Berge Formula guarantees a matching in
H
of size at least
\frac{n}{2}-1.5\xi n
.

Let
\odd(H-S)=p+q
, with
p
counting those odd components of order at most
\xi n
, and
q
counting those odd components of order larger than
\xi n
. Note that
q<\frac{1}{\xi}
. To get an upper bound on
p
, we consider an odd component
D
with
1\leq|V(D)|\leq\xi n
. We know that
\xi n<\delta(H)
since
\xi\ll\frac{1}{4}-1
. By counting the total number of neighbors of each vertex in
D
, we have

\displaystyle e_{H}(D,S)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|V(D)|(\delta(H)-|V(D)|+1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\delta(H),

as the function
|V(D)|(\delta(H)-|V(D)|+1)
is concave down in
|V(D)|

and so takes its minimum at the two endpoints of
|V(D)|
. So counting these edges for every such
D
, we get

p\cdot\delta(H)\leq e_{H}(S,H-S).

We therefore get that

\odd(H-S)=p+q<\frac{e_{H}(S,H-S)}{\delta(H)}+\frac{1}{\xi}.

(1)

For any
v\notin U_{\xi}(G)
,
d_{G}(v)>\Delta(G)-\xi n
and hence
d_{H}(v)<\delta(H)+\xi n
. So we get

\displaystyle e_{H}(S,H-S)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|\cdot\Delta(H)+|S\setminus U_{\xi}(G)|\cdot(\delta(H)+\xi n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|\cdot\Delta(H)+|S|(\delta(H)+\xi n)-|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|(\delta(H)+\xi n)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|\cdot\tfrac{n}{2}+|S|(\delta(H)+\xi n)-|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|(\delta(H)+\xi n)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(\xi n)\tfrac{n}{2}+(|S|-\xi n)(\delta(H)+\xi n),

where we get the last inequality above as
|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|\cdot\tfrac{n}{2}-|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|(\delta(H)+\xi n)
is a function increasing in
|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|

and
|S\cap U_{\xi}(G)|<\xi n
.
Substituting this into (
1
) gives

\odd(H-S)\leq\frac{\xi n^{2}}{2\delta(H)}+|S|-\xi n+\frac{(|S|-\xi n)\xi n}{\delta(H)}+\frac{1}{\xi}.

Using
\delta(H)>\tfrac{n}{4}-1
and
|S|<\frac{n}{2}-\xi n<2\delta(H)
, we get

\odd(H-S)<2\xi n+|S|-\xi n+2\xi n-\frac{\xi^{2}n^{2}}{\delta(H)}+\frac{1}{\xi}=3\xi n+|S|-\frac{\xi^{2}n^{2}}{\delta(H)}+\frac{1}{\xi}.

(2)

Note that
n\geq n_{0}\gg\tfrac{1}{\xi}>\tfrac{1}{\xi^{3}}
, so from (
2
) we get

\odd(H-S)<3\xi n+|S|-\frac{1}{\xi}\left(\frac{n}{\delta(H)}\right)+\frac{1}{\xi}.

Since
\delta(H)<n
this last line tells us that
\odd(G-S)-|S|<3\xi n
, as desired.
∎

We can now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 12
.

If Theorem
2
is true, then Theorem
1
is true.

Proof.

We have
\varepsilon>0
, and we choose

n_{0},\xi
so that

n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is at least the lower bound of
n
in Theorem
2
,
and that

0<\frac{1}{n_{0}}\ll\xi\ll\varepsilon.

(3)

Let
G
be a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+\varepsilon)n
. By the result of Hilton and Hind
[
12
]
mentioned in the introduction, we may assume that
\Delta(G)<\frac{3}{4}n
. Since we are assuming that Theorem
2
is true, in order to prove Theorem 1 it suffices to find a matching
M
in
\overline{G}
so that
G,M
satisfy
either (1) or (2) of Theorem
2
; we rewrite these conditions here for convenience:

(1)

|M|=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}-0.1\varepsilon n\rfloor
and either
G
is regular or
|V_{\delta}(G)|<\xi n
, the quantity
n-|V_{\delta}(G)|
is odd, and
G
has no middle-degree vertices;

(2)

|M|\in\{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}-0.1\varepsilon n\rfloor,n-1-\Delta(G)\}
and
|U_{\xi}(G)|\geq\xi n
.

We divide our proof into different cases according to the sizes of
U_{\xi}(G)
and
V_{\delta}(G)
.

Case 1:
|U_{\xi}(G)|\geq\xi n
.

Theorem
5
says that
G
has an equitable-coloring using
\Delta(G)+1
colors. As
\Delta(G)\geq\delta(G)>\frac{n}{2}
, each color class has size 2 or 1. Let
p
be the total number of color classes of size 2, and
q
be that of size 1. Then we have
p+q=\Delta(G)+1
and
2p+q=n
. This implies that
p=n-1-\Delta(G)
. So, there exists a matching
M_{0}
in
G
of size
n-1-\Delta(G)
.

Since
|U_{\xi}(G)|\geq\xi n
, then we choose in
\overline{G}
a matching
M
of size
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}-0.1\varepsilon n\rfloor
if it exists; otherwise, we take
M=M_{0}
. Either way, we meet the conditions of (2).

Case 2:
|U_{\xi}(G)|<\xi n
.

By the assumption of this case we may apply Lemma
11
to get a matching
M
in
\overline{G}
of size
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}-0.1\varepsilon n\rfloor
. If
G
is regular, then
G,M
meet the conditions of (1), so we may assume not. Moreover, we may assume that at least one of the following three conditions is
not
met:
|V_{\delta}(G)|<\xi n
; the quantity
n-|V_{\delta}(G)|
is odd, and;
G
has no middle-degree vertices. We divide our argument into two subcases according to whether the first of these conditions is met or not.

Case 2a:
|V_{\delta}(G)|\geq\xi n
.

Let
G^{\prime}=G
unless
\Delta(G)
is odd and
n
is odd. In this latter case, note that
G
has a Hamilton cycle by Theorem
4
and by taking every second edge in this cycle we can pick a near-perfect matching
F
of
G
where the only unsaturated vertex is some
v_{1}
with
d_{G}(v_{1})=\delta(G)
. Then let
G^{\prime}=G-F
, and note that
\Delta^{\prime}:=\Delta^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)-1
in this case.

Suppose
V(G^{\prime})=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
with
d_{G^{\prime}}(v_{1})\leq d_{G^{\prime}}(v_{2})\leq\ldots\leq d_{G^{\prime}}(v_{n})
. Let
d_{i}=\Delta^{\prime}-d_{G^{\prime}}(v_{i})
.
Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\Delta^{\prime}-d_{G^{\prime}}(v_{i}))=n\Delta^{\prime}-2e(G^{\prime}),

which is an even number since
n\Delta^{\prime}
is always even (by our definition of
G^{\prime}
). Moreover,
|V_{\delta}(G)|\geq\xi n\gg\tfrac{n}{n_{0}}\geq 1
, so in particular
d_{1}=d_{2}
and hence
d_{1}\leq\sum_{i=2}^{n}d_{i}
. We can therefore apply Theorem
3
to construct a multigraph
L
on
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

with degree sequence
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
.

Since
|V_{\delta}(G)|\geq\xi n
we know that
|V_{\delta}(G)|>|U_{\xi}(G)|
, which means that
\Delta(G)-\delta(G)<\xi n
. By Theorem
6
, we have

\chi^{\prime}(L)\leq\Delta(L)+\mu(L)\leq 2\Delta(L)\leq 2(\Delta(G)-\delta(G))<2\xi n.

Setting
k_{0}=\lceil 2\xi n\rceil
and applying Theorem
7
, we can partition
E(L)
into a set of
k_{0}
matchings each with size at most

\left\lceil\frac{e(L)}{k_{0}}\right\rceil\leq\left\lceil\frac{n(\Delta(G)-\delta(G))/2}{k_{0}}\right\rceil\leq\left\lceil\frac{n(\xi n)/2}{2\xi n}\right\rceil\leq\left\lceil\frac{n}{4}\right\rceil.

Let us now partition each of these
k_{0}
matchings into matchings of size at most
\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{1/2}n
. Let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
be the resulting set of matchings in
L
, with

k\leq 2\xi n\left\lceil\frac{\lceil n/4\rceil}{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{1/2}n}\right\rceil\leq 4\xi n\left(\frac{n/4}{\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{1/2}n}\right)=2\xi^{1/2}n.

Our aim now will be to define spanning subgraphs
G^{\prime}_{0},G^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,G^{\prime}_{k}
of
G^{\prime}
such that

(a)

G^{\prime}_{0}:=G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}_{i}:=G^{\prime}_{i-1}-E(F_{i})
for
i\geq 1
.

(b)

F_{i}
is a spanning linear forest in
G^{\prime}_{i-1}
whose leaves are precisely the vertices in
V(M_{i})
.

Let
G^{\prime}_{0}=G^{\prime}
and suppose that for some
i\in[1,k]
, we have already defined
G^{\prime}_{0},G^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,G^{\prime}_{i-1}
and
F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1}
. Since

\Delta(F_{1}\cup\ldots\cup F_{i-1})\leq 2(i-1)\leq 2(k-1)\leq 4\xi^{1/2}n,

it follows that

\delta(G^{\prime}_{i-1})\geq(1+\varepsilon)n/2-4\xi^{1/2}n\geq(1+0.9\varepsilon)n/2.

(4)

Since
M_{i}
has size at most
\xi^{1/2}n/2
, we can apply Lemma
9
to
G^{\prime}_{i-1}
and
M_{i}
to obtain a spanning linear forest
F_{i}
in
G^{\prime}_{i-1}
whose leaves are precisely the vertices in
V(M_{i})
. Set
G^{\prime}_{i}=G^{\prime}_{i-1}-E(F_{i})
.

We claim that
G^{\prime}_{k}
is regular. Consider any vertex
u\in V(G^{\prime}_{k})
. For every
i\in[1,k]
,
d_{F_{i}}(u)=1
if
u
is an endvertex of some edge of
M_{i}
and
d_{F_{i}}(u)=2
otherwise. Since
M_{1},M_{2},\ldots,M_{k}
partition
E(L)
,
u
is the endpoint of exactly
d_{L}(u)
of these matching edges, so

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{F_{i}}(u)=2k-d_{L}(u)=2k-(\Delta^{\prime}-d_{G^{\prime}}(u)).

Thus

d_{G^{\prime}_{k}}(u)=d_{G^{\prime}}(u)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{F_{i}}(u)=d_{G^{\prime}}(u)-2k+(\Delta^{\prime}-d_{G^{\prime}}(u))=\Delta^{\prime}-2k.

Since the above holds for any
u\in V(G^{\prime}_{k})
, we see that
G^{\prime}_{k}
is indeed regular.

By (
4
) and by our initial assumptions,

(1+0.9\varepsilon)n/2\leq\Delta(G^{\prime}_{k})\leq\Delta(G)<\tfrac{3}{4}{n}.

The matching
M
we found earlier is in
\overline{G}\subseteq\overline{G^{\prime}_{k}}
, so since
G^{\prime}_{k}
is regular,
G^{\prime}_{k},M
satisfy all the conditions of
Theorem
2
(1), so
(G^{\prime}_{k})^{M}
has a good edge-coloring, which uses
\Delta(G_{k}^{\prime})+2
colors. Since
\Delta(G_{k}^{\prime})=\Delta^{\prime}-2k
, and each of the
k
linear forests is edge 2-colorable, this translates to a good edge-coloring of
(G^{\prime})^{M}
which uses
\Delta^{\prime}+2
colors. If
G=G^{\prime}
this is exactly our desired result. Otherwise,
G^{\prime}=G-F
with
F
a matching and
\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta(G)-1
. So again we get our desired result by using one additional color on the edges of
F
.

Case 2b:
|V_{\delta}(G)|<\xi n
.

Consider the graph
G^{\prime}=G-V_{\delta}(G)
(which is nontrivial since
G
is not regular). Then

\delta(G^{\prime})>\delta(G)-|V_{\delta}(G)|>(1+\varepsilon)\tfrac{n}{2}-\xi n\geq\tfrac{n}{2}+n(\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}-\xi)>\tfrac{n}{2},

with the last inequality following from
\xi\ll\varepsilon
.
So
G^{\prime}
has a Hamilton cycle by Theorem
4
. If
G^{\prime}
has even order then taking every second edge in this Hamilton cycle gives a perfect matching
M^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
; otherwise we get a near-perfect matching
M^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
, where
M^{\prime}
unsaturates one vertex that has degree less than
\Delta(G)
in
G

An edge-coloring lemma

Given a graph
G
and an integer
k\geq 0
, a partial
k
-edge-coloring of
G
is an edge-coloring of some subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
. For any partial
k
-edge-coloring,
\varphi
of
G
, and any vertex

, denote by
\overline{\varphi}(v)
the set of colors
missing
at

, that is, the set of all colors in
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
that are not used on an edge incident to
v
under
\varphi
. For a vertex set

, define
\overline{\varphi}(X)=\bigcup_{v\in X}\overline{\varphi}(v)
to be the set of missing colors of
X
. Consider a pair of distinct colors
\alpha,\gamma\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and consider some maximal
(\alpha,\gamma)
-alternating path or cycle

in
G
. Note that by switching the colors
\alpha,\gamma
on

we get a new partial
k
-edge-coloring
\varphi^{\prime}
of

where exactly the same set of edges in
G
have been colored; we denote this
\varphi^{\prime}
by
\varphi/P
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
\varphi
be a partial
k
-edge-coloring of
G
. A
multifan
centered at some vertex
r
with respect to
\varphi
is a sequence
F_{\varphi}(r,s_{0}:s_{p})=(r,e_{0},s_{0},e_{1},s_{1},\ldots,e_{p},s_{p})
with

consisting of distinct edges
e_{0},\ldots,e_{p}
with
e_{i}=rs_{i}
for all
i
, where
e_{0}
is left uncolored under
\varphi
,
and
for every edge
e_{i}
with
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, there exists
j\in\{0,\ldots,i-1\}
such that

\varphi(e_{i})\in\overline{\varphi}(s_{j})
.

A subsequence
(s_{0}=s_{\ell_{0}},s_{\ell_{1}},s_{\ell_{2}},\ldots,s_{\ell_{t}})
with the property that
\varphi(e_{\ell_{i}})=\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(s_{\ell_{i-1}})
for each

, is called a

of

.
Given such a linear sequence, and given

, we

to

by recoloring edge

with
\varphi(e_{\ell_{i}})
for all

.
Note that the resulting coloring is a partial
k
-edge-coloring where
e_{0}
is colored and
e_{\ell_{h}}
is not. Note that these linear subsequences and shifts have been implicitly used in many papers (see e.g.
[
3
]
); the definition of multifan first appeared in the book of Stiebitz et al.
[
20
]
as a generalization of classic work of Vizing
[
23
]
.

The proof of the following lemma is very similar to that of Lemmas 3 and 4 in
[
4
]
. Here we use our notation of

as before, except when

and

, we write

. Note that in this lemma we are dealing with multigraphs so

may be larger than

. We define the

as the quantity,
\mu_{G}(v)=\max\{e_{G}(u,v):u\in V(G)\}
and with the

being the quantity
\mu(G)=\max\{\mu_{G}(v):v\in V(G)\}
.

Lemma 13

(Plantholt and Shan

Theorem 15

Every bipartite multigraph

We’ll additionally use the following two theorems about edge-colorings in multigraphs. The first of these is a simple folklore result (see eg.

Let

be a multigraph and

-coloring of

.

(Dalal, McDonald, Shan

]
)

Let

be a multigraph and

.
Suppose that for some integer

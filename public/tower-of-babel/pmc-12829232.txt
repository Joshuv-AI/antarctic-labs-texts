Clinical outcomes following surgical intervention for critical congenital heart disease in neonates: a retrospective study in China
Authors: Xiao-Juan Zhang, Na Miao, Jia-Lu Wang, Yan He, Qiang Wang, Zhen-Shuang Cui, Ju Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aims to examine the incidence and clinical outcomes of critical congenital heart disease (CCHD) in neonates in China, with a specific focus on prognostic indicators following surgical intervention.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on neonates who underwent surgical correction of congenital heart disease (CHD) involving cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) at a single tertiary care hospital between January 2022 and December 2024. Documented variables included patient weight, type of cardiac anomaly, duration of CPB, aortic cross-clamp time, and systemic circulation duration. Prognostic indicators, length of hospital stay, and duration of mechanical ventilation in the intensive care unit (ICU) were also recorded.ResultsA total of 234 neonates underwent cardiac surgery with CPB during the study period. The cohort comprised 146 males and 88 females, with a mean weight of 3.64 ± 3.43 kg and a mean age of 12.10 ± 7.77 days. The mean duration of CPB time was 159.19 ± 67.51 min, mean cross-clamp duration was 95.53 ± 44.20 min, and mean bypass duration was 37.54 ± 28.98 min. The mean length of hospital stay was 25.43 ± 13.61 days, and the mean mechanical ventilation duration was 3.64 ± 2.71 days. Among the patients, 139 were diagnosed with cyanotic CHD, and 95 with acyanotic CHD. Emergency procedures were performed in 39 cases, while 195 cases underwent elective surgery. Radical surgery was performed in 215 cases, single-stage palliation in 4 cases, and intrapartum surgery in 15 cases. 222 neonates were discharged following recovery, and 12 deaths were recorded.ConclusionIn China, CCHD remains prevalent among newborns, posing a significant threat to their lives. With advancements in surgical and cardiopulmonary bypass techniques, the treatment system for these critically ill newborns is being progressively refined. This has led to continuously improving success rates and an increasingly diverse range of treatment options available.

Introduction

Congenital heart disease (CHD) represents one of the most prevalent birth defects worldwide, characterized by structural abnormalities of the heart that impair blood circulation and oxygenation, thereby posing serious risks to neonatal health [1]. Approximately 25% of children diagnosed with CHD present with critical congenital heart disease (CCHD), a subset that necessitates surgical or interventional treatment within the first month of life, often during the neonatal period, to prevent mortality [2]. CCHD encompass a range of complex lesions, including coarctation of the aorta (CoA), tetralogy of Fallot (TOF), transposition of the great arteries (TGA), pulmonary atresia (PA), tricuspid valve atresia (TVA), and hypoplastic left heart syndrome (HLHS) [3]. Beyond the immediate threat to survival, CCHD places considerable emotional and financial burdens on families and imposes significant strain on healthcare systems. The global incidence of neonatal CCHD differs by region. In high-income countries, the availability of prenatal screening, advanced diagnostic technologies, and adequate healthcare resources has facilitated earlier detection and improved clinical outcomes. In contrast, in low- and middle-income countries, limited access to diagnostic resources and specialized care, often results in delayed diagnoses and elevated mortality rates [1]. The present retrospective analysis examined neonates with CCHD who underwent surgical correction involving cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) at the study hospital between January 2021 and December 2024, with the aim of evaluating incidence and clinical outcomes in this population.

General information and methods

General information

A total of 234 neonates underwent cardiac surgery involving CPB at the study hospital between January 2021 and December 2024. The cohort included 146 males and 88 females, with ages ranging from 0 to 28 days. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Age at the time of surgery ≤ 28 days; (2) Confirmed diagnosis of CHD; (3) Undergoing surgical correction of cardiac malformation requiring CPB. Exclusion criteria were: (1) Age at the time of surgery > 28 days; (2) Presence of additional malformations involving non-cardiac organ systems; (3) Premature birth.

CPB

All surgical procedures were performed using the Stockert S5 heart–lung machine and a Terumo FX05 infant membrane oxygenator (priming volume approximately 43 mL). An infant-specific CPB circuit with a priming volume of approximately 150–200 mL was employed. Myocardial protection was achieved with histidine tryptophan ketoglutarate (HTK) cardioplegic solution. The modified ultrafiltration was performed postoperatively for 8 to 10 min. The priming solution consisted of 50 mL of compound electrolyte solution, one unit of packed red blood cells (approximately 130 mL), 10 mg of heparin, 20 mL of 5% sodium bicarbonate, and 50 mL of 20% albumin, with a total volume of approximately 250 mL. Intraoperative management included adjustment of temperature and perfusion flow rates according to surgical requirements. Hemodynamic and metabolic parameters were continuously monitored to maintain a stable internal environment throughout the procedures.

Observation indicators

Data collected included the annual number of surgical cases, mortality rates, incidence of emergency surgeries, distribution of cardiac diagnoses, intraoperative CPB parameters, hospitalization outcomes, and postoperative respiratory support requirements.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS software, version 26.0. Results

A total of 234 neonates with CCHD underwent cardiac surgery involving CPB. The cohort consisted of 146 males and 88 females, with a mean age of 12.10 ± 7.77 days and a mean body weight of 3.64 ± 3.43 kg. Emergency procedures were performed in 39 cases, while 195 cases underwent elective surgery. Of these, 222 neonates were discharged following successful treatment, whereas 12 deaths were recorded (Table 1). The number of surgical procedures increased annually, while the mortality rate demonstrated a declining trend. In 2022, 57 surgeries were performed with 3 mortalities, yielding a mortality rate of 5.26%. In 2023, 77 surgeries were performed with 5 mortalities, yielding a mortality rate of 6.49%. In 2024, 100 surgeries were performed with 4 mortalities, corresponding to a mortality rate of 4.00% (Table 1). Temperature management strategies included mild hypothermia in 208 neonates and moderate hypothermic circulatory arrest with regional cerebral perfusion in 26 cases. The disease spectrum encompassed included major critical cardiac anomalies observed in the neonatal period, such as complete transposition of TGA, aortic arch coarctation, interrupted aortic arch, pulmonary atresia, total anomalous pulmonary venous connection (TAPVC), and TOF (Table 2). All neonates received CPB support. The mean duration for CPB was 159.19 ± 67.51 min, mean cross-clamp time was 95.53 ± 44.20 min, and mean bypass time was 37.54 ± 28.98 min. The mean length of hospital stay was 25.43 ± 13.61 days, and the mean intubation time was 3.64 ± 2.71 days. Among neonates diagnosed with TOF, 41 underwent elective surgery, with 1 reported mortality. Intrapartum cardiac surgery was introduced in 2023; by December 2024, 15 neonates had undergone the procedure, with 1 mortality recorded.

Discussion

CCHD in neonates refers to a group of severe cardiac anomalies that necessitate emergency intervention during the early postnatal period, particularly within the neonatal stage. The incidence of CCHD is estimated at approximately 1.5 to 2.5‰ per 1000 live births. Without treatment, mortality rates among neonates with CCHD may reach 30% to 50%, particularly in conditions such as HLHS, making CCHD a leading cause of neonatal mortality and morbidity worldwide [4]. Common presentations include TOF (7%–10%), transposition of TGA (5%–7%), HLHS, TAPVC, and CoA (5%–8%) [5–8]. These findings are consistent with the disease spectrum observed in the present cohort, in which the five most prevalent conditions were coarctation or interruption of the aortic arch (approximately 22%), pulmonary atresia or pulmonary stenosis (approximately 18%), TOF (18%), anomalous pulmonary venous drainage (12%), and complete TGA (approximately 9%). The proportion of TOF cases in this city was higher than previously reported in existing literature, which may be associated with the hospital’s adoption of newer surgical strategies. Elective surgery for TOF during the neonatal period was implemented to reduce the risk of secondary pathophysiological changes. Among 41 neonates who underwent TOF repair, one was discharged against medical advice due to a suspected congenital left ventricular diverticulum complicated with cardiac rupture; the remaining 40 patients were successfully discharged. Valve annulus preservation was achieved in all TOF cases, which may contribute to favorable long-term prognosis. It is important to note that most ventricular septal defects (VSDs) do not require surgical intervention during the neonatal period. So in this study, the indications for surgery in neonates with VSD were as follows: large VSD (≥ 8 mm), heart failure, and pneumonia requiring ventilator support. In addition, intrapartum cardiac surgery was introduced to address severe hemodynamic instability occurring immediately after birth. This approach allowed surgical intervention within 30 min of separation from fetal circulation, before the onset of abnormal physiological circulation. By mitigating the risk of circulatory collapse, this strategy may reduce early mortality. A total of 15 intrapartum procedures were performed during the study period, with 14 survivors. One patient diagnosed with TGA and intact ventricular septum died of cardiac failure associated with concurrent hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

Surgical timing was identified as a critical determinant of prognosis in neonates with CCHD. Decision-making regarding the timing of intervention required a comprehensive evaluation of the malformation type, disease complexity, preoperative status, surgical expertise, and the availability of perioperative management. When clinical stability permitted, elective procedures were performed approximately two weeks after birth, with continuous monitoring of vital signs. In cases where the clinical status did not allow delay, timely surgical intervention was prioritized, while efforts were made to avoid emergency procedures when possible. Among the 234 cases in this series, 39 neonates underwent emergency surgery and 195 underwent elective surgery. Of the 12 mortalities, 5 occurred following emergency procedures, 1 following intrapartum surgery, and 6 occurred after elective surgery. The mortality rate associated with emergency surgery was 12.8%.

Mortality rates in neonatal CCHD remain relatively high and are influenced by factors including diagnostic timing, treatment accessibility, and lesion complexity. In high-income countries such as the United States, Japan, and nations in Europe, in-hospital mortality ranges from 5% to 15%, depending on lesion complexity. In contrast, low-income countries report mortality rates ranging from 25% to 50%, a discrepancy largely attributed to limited availability of extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) and inadequate postoperative management [9–11]. In China, the mortality rate of critical congenital heart disease has significantly declined over the past decade, thanks to technological advancements. Particularly in top-tier medical centers, the success rates of treatment have approached or even surpassed those in developed countries. However, due to uneven distribution of medical resources and the complexity and severity of the disease itself, the mortality rate remains relatively low (approximately 2%−5%) in China’s leading medical institutions but is significantly higher nationwide, with substantial regional disparities. In the present cohort, mortality rates demonstrated a gradual decline, falling below 5% in 2024. This improvement corresponded with advances in the neonatal surgical program, including the introduction of intrapartum repair and neonatal TOF correction. The combination of appropriately timed surgical intervention and thorough anatomical correction was associated with enhanced early survival outcomes. Ongoing follow-up of long-term outcomes is essential to evaluate the sustained benefits of these evolving approaches.

Neonates undergoing CPB present distinct physiological challenges due to low body weight and immature organ systems. Optimizing outcomes in this population requires precise priming volume management, comprehensive intraoperative monitoring, collaboration among surgical, perfusion, anesthesia, and intensive care teams, and individualized care strategies tailored to body weight and lesion type. Neuroprotection was a priority in all cases [12–18]. For procedures requiring circulatory arrest, intraoperative cerebral and lower limb oxygenation were monitored by NIRS. Nasopharyngeal temperature was reduced to 28 °C. A 3/16 inch infant CPB circuit was used to minimize priming volume, with a total circuit volume of 150 to 200 mL. The priming solution included red blood cells and albumin to maintain adequate perfusion and oncotic pressure. Myocardial protection was achieved using HTK cardioplegic solution, and all cardioplegia was removed via cardiac suction. Methylprednisolone was administered intraoperatively to mitigate inflammatory responses at a dose of 30 mg/kg. Initially, administration was provided after rewarming, but this practice was later administered immediately when the CPB started. All patients achieved stable postoperative transfers and successful separation from CPB.

Despite these outcomes, this study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center, retrospective study with inherent risk of bias. Second, and most importantly, the follow-up is ongoing and the data are not yet mature; therefore, deeper statistical analyses (such as survival analysis or multivariable regression) were not performed, and the findings should be interpreted as descriptive trends at this stage. Furthermore, some perioperative parameters (e.g., mechanical ventilation duration) could not be analyzed due to incomplete documentation, which may limit a comprehensive assessment of outcomes.

In China, regional variations exist in the treatment of neonatal CCHD. The present institution demonstrated mortality rates that were lower than those reported in some developed countries, indicating advanced treatment capabilities. The application of new approaches, including intrapartum surgery and complete neonatal TOF repair, continues to evolve and is suggested as a promising trend to further improve patient outcomes. Long-term follow-up and future statistical analyses on mature datasets will be essential to robustly evaluate postoperative outcomes, validate these early findings, and refine treatment protocols.

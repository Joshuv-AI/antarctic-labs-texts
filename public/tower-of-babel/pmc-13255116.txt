Advantages of Posterior Harms-Goel C1C3 Fixation over Anterior C2C3 Discectomy and Fusion with Plate and Screws for Highly Unstable Hangman's Fracture: Time to Close the Debate?
Authors: Nathan Beucler
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with interest Sonawane et al.'s1) study on the surgical management of Hangman fracture of the upper cervical spine using the anterior approach. The authors address the issue of patients suffering from Levine-Edwards type 2 or 2a subacute Hangman fractures of C2, left untreated for at least 3 weeks before referral. They report their experience treating five such cases using solely C2C3 anterior discectomy and fusion with iliac crest bone graft, plate, and screws.

Although we congratulate the authors for their fortunate results with this patient series, we have a few concerns regarding surgical considerations, postoperative management, and the generalization of this technique to all unstable cases of Hangman's fracture.

Pathophysiology of Hangman's Fracture

Hangman's fracture of the upper cervical spine was originally coined by Schneider et al.2) (1965) due to the similaritybetween the C2 bipedicular fracture seen after a traffic accident and the upper cervical trauma historically described after judicial hanging (Wood Jones, 1913)3). Hangman's fracture typically occurs in younger people following high-kinetic traffic accidents that cause whiplash injuries, or in the elderly who sustain a fall from height with head trauma, leading tohyperextension of the neck4).

Hangman's fracture is a complex trauma involving the bones, discs, and ligaments of the upper cervical spine, where an oblique vector force passes upward and backward from the anterior C2C3 disc to the posterior atlanto-axial ligament5). Hence, Hangman's fracture involves disruption of the C2C3 anterior longitudinal ligament first, followed by the C2C3 disc, bilateral C2 pedicles, the posterior C1C2 atlanto-axial ligament, and finally the C1C2 supraspinous ligament.

The oblique trauma pattern of Hangman's fracture mimics that of Roy-Camille's anterior oblique C2 odontoid fracture6). Therefore, in Hangman's fracture, the anterior upper C1C2 fragment can only be displaced anteriorly from the posterior lower C2C3 fragment. As a consequence, instability in Hangman's fracture is strictly anterior.

Most Hangman's fractures can be treated with a cervical collar, such as in Effendi and Laurin type 1 fractures (undisplaced)7) and sometimes type 2 fractures (C2C3 disc rupture, anterior displacement). In contrast, unstable cases require surgical stabilization, including some cases of Effendi and Laurin type 27) and notably subtype 2a according to Levine and Edwards8), with anterior displacement and flexion, as well as all cases of dislocated Effendi and Laurin type 3 fractures7) (Figure 1, upper panel).

Anterior Stabilization of Hangman's Fracture

The principle of surgical stabilization via the anterior approach relies on an anterior C2C3 discectomy and fusion with a plate and screws, aiming to prevent the C2 body from moving forward by using the upper aspect of the plate as a dam4,9). The surgeon should ensure that the upper aspect of the plate extends sufficiently along the C2 vertebral body to allow for adequate reduction capacity and to prevent secondary anterior displacement10). Thus, this technique primarily relies on the bone anchor provided by C3 screws to avoid C2C3 luxation. Furthermore, the anterior plate can only be effective if it is properly applied along the anterior wall of C2 and C3 vertebral bodies, making anatomical realignment a prerequisite (Figure 1, middle panel, left).

Anterior reduction of C2C3 displacement requires a tire-lever maneuver within the C2C3 disc9), under continuous cervical traction with a Gardner-Wells tong. This is partially illustrated in Fig. 6 of the Sonawane et al.1) article. However, the patient's chin often limits the surgeon's working angle for this tire-lever maneuver. Additionally, open reduction via theanterior approach is almost ineffective for Effendi and Laurin type 3 dislocated C2C3 Hangman's fractures. Thus, the difficulties in achieving a lasting satisfactory fracture reduction in the long-term using the anterior cervical approach are clearlyin the authors' Figure 7, where the C2 vertebra displays a bone chicane.

Finally, the precarious nature of anterior C2C3 discectomy and fusion with plate and screws for the surgical treatment of highly unstable Hangman fractures make it more prone to mechanical failure compared to the posterior approach10). Therefore, posterior C1C3 fixation can be a valuable option for revision surgery4). For this reason, the authors recommend that patients wear a cervical collar for 4-6 weeks after Hangman's fracture stabilization viaanterior approach1), which may lead tocomplications such as eschars, swallowing discomfort, and cervical muscle stiffness in the short term, among other issues11).

Mechanical Advantages of Hangman's Fracture Stabilization by Posterior Approach

The surgical treatment of Hangman's fracture via theposterior approach involves there duction maneuver of C1 screws in a C2C3 construct (Figure 1, lower panel). Screws are placed in the C1 lateral masses (Harms-Goel technique)12,13), C2 pedicles (Harms-Goel technique)14), and C3 articular processes (Magerl technique)15). Rods are then placed and secured on C2 and C3 with bolts.

A specific rod persuader is then placed on the C1 screw heads and progressively tightened to the rods under continuous fluoroscopy guidance. Since this reduction maneuverdirectly opposes the instability mechanism of a Hangman fracture, it allows for systematic anatomical reduction of any Hangman fracture, regardless of severity, using the backward pull of C1 screws on C2C3 instrumentation5,16).

It is worth noting that the surgeon can add supplementary C4 transarticular screws if the C2C3 bone anchor is deemed insufficient in cases with particularly strong lever arms, such as in axial spondyloarthritis.

Conclusion

Given the abovereasons, C1C3 posterior fixation should be preferred over C2C3 anterior discectomy and fusion with plate and screws for the surgical management of highly unstable Hangman's fractures, especially for Levine-Edwards type 2a and Effendi and Laurin type 3.

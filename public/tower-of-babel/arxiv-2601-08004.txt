BPS cohomology in geometry and representation theory
Ben Davison

Abstract
. We motivate and survey the theory of BPS invariants of categories and BPS cohomology of stacks, indicating applications in enumerative geometry and representation theory, as well as recent advances.

Introduction

Throughout this paper all schemes and stacks are defined over the complex number field. Given a variety

The singular cohomology groups

2.
BPS cohomology of moduli stacks of objects

If
\mathscr{C}
is some sufficiently well-behaved dg category, and
\mathscr{A}^{\sst}
some subcategory of semistable objects in
\mathscr{C}
, then we can consider the moduli stack
\mathfrak{M}^{\sst}_{\mathscr{A}}
of objects in
\mathscr{A}^{\sst}
. As above, we may try to define and construct BPS invariants by looking at the derived global sections of an appropriate sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathscr{A}}
on this stack, taking plethystic exponentials, etc. Rather than diving directly into the theory for general
\mathscr{A}^{\sst}
, we start our exposition with a concrete class of examples – categories of modules over algebras – before discussing how to generalise to a wider class of categories. This wider class of categories contains, for example, categories of semistable coherent sheaves on complex projective 3-Calabi–Yau varieties.

Let
A
be an algebra, and assume that we have fixed a presentation of
A
of one of the three following forms:

(1)

A={\mathbb{C}}Q
, the path algebra of a finite quiver over the complex numbers. This algebra has a
{\mathbb{C}}
-basis given by paths in
Q
, including for each vertex
i
a “lazy” path of length zero, beginning and ending at
i
. Multiplication is given by concatenation of paths, where possible, and zero otherwise.

(2)

A={\mathbb{C}}Q/\langle r_{1},\ldots,r_{l}\rangle
, where each
r_{s}
is a linear combination of paths of length at least two in
Q
, such that all the paths in the linear combination
r_{s}
have the same starting point and the same endpoint.

(3)

A=\Jac(Q,W)\coloneqq{\mathbb{C}}Q/\langle\partial W/\partial a\;\lvert\;a\in Q_{1}\rangle
, the Jacobi algebra associated to a quiver
Q
and a potential
W\in{\mathbb{C}}Q/[{\mathbb{C}}Q,{\mathbb{C}}Q]
. See
[
24
]
for the definition of the
noncommutative partial derivatives

\partial W/\partial a
, as well as a wonderful account of the theory of Jacobi algebras and Calabi–Yau algebras in general.

We refer to these presentations as types (1), (2) and (3) from now on. The cohomology theory we consider, as well as the resulting BPS invariants, will depend on the type of presentation we give
A
. We denote the vertex set of
Q
by
Q_{0}
, and the set of arrows by
Q_{1}
. We define
s(a)
and

to be the source and target of
a\in Q_{1}
, respectively. The dimension vector of an
A
-module

is the tuple
\dim_{Q_{0}}(\rho)\coloneqq(\dim(1_{i}\cdot\rho))_{i\in Q_{0}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{Q_{0}}
. We define the Euler form on
{\mathbb{N}}^{Q_{0}}

\chi_{Q}(\mathbf{d}^{\prime},\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime})=\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbf{d}^{\prime}_{i}\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}_{i}-\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}\mathbf{d}^{\prime}_{s(a)}\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}_{(t(a)}

and define the symmetrised and antisymmetrised versions

(\mathbf{d}^{\prime},\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime})_{Q}=\chi_{Q}(\mathbf{d}^{\prime},\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime})+\chi_{Q}(\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{d}^{\prime});\quad\quad\langle\mathbf{d}^{\prime},\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{Q}=\chi_{Q}(\mathbf{d}^{\prime},\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime})-\chi_{Q}(\mathbf{d}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{d}^{\prime}).

A quiver
Q^{\prime}
is called

is symmetric. In cases (1) and (3) we will initially assume that

is symmetric. In case (2), we form a new quiver

by introducing

. The starting point of

is the same as the endpoint of all of the terms in

4.2.
PBW theorem and BPS Lie algebra

Fix a symmetric quiver

as in §

Theorem 4.3

(
[

]
)
.

The multiplication
\mult

. The associated graded algebra

By the first part of the theorem, the commutator Lie bracket

There is a relative version of Theorem

h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
.

•

[h,g]=\chi(h,\alpha)g
for
\alpha\in N
, if
g\in(V\oplus V^{\vee})_{\alpha}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}
.

•

[v,v^{\prime}]=v^{\prime}(v)\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}
for
v\in V_{\alpha}
and
v^{\prime}\in V^{\vee}
.

•

(The Serre relations):
[g,-]^{1-(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})_{Q}}(g^{\prime})=0
if either
(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})_{Q}=0
or
\alpha
is a real simple root.

There is a triangular decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}_{V}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}_{V}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{V}
with
{\mathfrak{n}}_{V}^{\pm}
in
N
-degrees
\pm N^{+}\setminus\{0\}
. Note that
{\mathfrak{n}}_{V}^{-}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{V}
are the free Lie algebras generated by
V^{\vee}
(respectively
V
) subject only to the Serre relations. GKM Lie algebras appear naturally in the theory of crystal graphs associated to quivers with loops; see
[
37
,
6
,
30
]
.

The convolution tensor product on
\Perv({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A}})
makes it into a
N^{+}
-graded tensor category, meaning that every object
{\mathcal{F}}
decomposes as a direct sum
{\mathcal{F}}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in N^{+}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha}
, and
({\mathcal{F}}\boxdot{\mathcal{G}})_{\alpha}=\bigoplus_{\alpha^{\prime}+\alpha^{\prime\prime}=\alpha}{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\boxdot{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}
. Although there are no
negatively
graded objects, with respect to the
N
-grading, we can still define the analogue of the Lie algebra object
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{V}
in this category. In place of
V
, we start with a perverse sheaf
{\mathcal{G}}\in\Perv({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A}})
satisfying the property that
{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}={\mathbb{Q}}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A},\alpha}}
if
\alpha
is a simple root (note that this implies that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A},\alpha}\cong\pt
), and
{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}=0
if
\alpha\notin\Phi^{+}_{\mathscr{A}}
. We define
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
to be the free Lie algebra object in the symmetric tensor category
\Perv({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A}})
subject to the Serre relations above; see
[
18
, Section 3.5]
for details of the construction.

If we set
V=\HO({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A}},{\mathcal{G}})
then there is an isomorphism
\HO({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A}},{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{{\mathcal{G}}})\cong{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{V}
. In particular, the derived global sections of
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{{\mathcal{G}}}
carry a Lie algebra structure, and there is a natural inclusion of Lie algebras
\HO({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A}},{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{{\mathcal{G}}})\hookrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{V}
into a doubled Lie algebra, which is a GKM Lie algebra.

5.2.
BPS Lie algebras for 2CY categories

Theorem 5.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{C},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{A}^{\sst}
be as at the start of §
5
. We define
{\mathcal{G}}
as follows

{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\IC_{{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A},\alpha}}&\textrm{if there exists a simple object in }\mathscr{A}^{\sst}\textrm{ of class }\alpha\\
+_{n,*}\IC_{{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A},\alpha^{\prime}}}&\textrm{if }\alpha=n\alpha^{\prime}\textrm{ with }\alpha^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\mathscr{A}}^{+}\textrm{ isotropic}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
+_{n}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A},\alpha^{\prime}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathscr{A},n\alpha^{\prime}}
is the morphism taking
x
to
x^{\oplus n}
. Then there is a PBW isomorphism

\Phi\colon\Sym_{\boxdot}\left({\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{{\mathcal{G}}}\otimes\HO(\pt/{\mathbb{C}}^{*},{\mathbb{Q}})\right)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha\in N^{+}}p_{*}{\mathbb{D}}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A},\alpha}}\otimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vdim/2}

and taking global sections of
\Phi
we deduce that
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{A}}
satisfies cohomological integrality: there is a PBW isomorphism
\Sym\left({\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{V}\otimes\HO(\pt/{\mathbb{C}}^{*},{\mathbb{Q}})\right)\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{A}}
with the BPS cohomology given by
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{V}\otimes{\mathbb{L}}^{-1/2}
.

It follows that the BPS Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}_{V}
is the positive half of a GKM Lie algebra, which we denote
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

5.3.
Maulik–Okounkov Yangians

We restrict our attention to
\Pi_{Q}\lmod
. As in §
4.3
we consider an equivariant theory: we consider the
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{2}
-action on
\Pi_{Q}
for which the first
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-factor scales all of the arrows
a\in Q_{1}
and the second factor scales all of the arrows
a^{*}
. We write
\HO_{T}\coloneqq\HO(\pt/T,{\mathbb{Q}})
. As in §
4.3
we consider the equivariant CoHA
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Pi_{Q}}^{T}
. Define
\mathbb{M}^{T}_{\mathbf{f},\mathbf{d}}
to be the
T
-equivariant cohomology of the Nakajima quiver variety
{\mathcal{N}}_{Q}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{d})
. As in §
4.5
the direct sums
\mathbb{M}_{\mathbf{f}}^{T}(Q)\cong\bigoplus_{\mathbf{d}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{Q_{0}}}\mathbb{M}^{T}_{\mathbf{f},\mathbf{d}}(Q)
form modules over the equivariant CoHA
{\mathcal{H}}^{T}_{\Pi_{Q}}
. One may identify the raising operators on cohomology of Nakajima quiver varieties considered by Nakajima
[
55
]
, Grojnowski
[
25
]
and Varagnolo
[
70
]
with the action of explicit elements of
{\mathcal{H}}^{T}_{\Pi_{Q}}
, although for general
Q
the algebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{T}_{\Pi_{Q}}
contains many more raising operators besides these ones.

In
[
48
]
Maulik and Okounkov defined a new Yangian-type algebra
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}
, acting by raising (and lowering) operators on each
\mathbb{M}^{T}_{\mathbf{f}}(Q)
, defined via the theory of stable envelopes and the resulting R matrices. As in the case of the Yangians associated to simple Lie algebras, one has a PBW-type isomorphism
\Phi:\Sym_{\HO_{T}}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}[u])\cong\mathcal{Y}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}
. Here
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}
is a (free)
\HO_{T}
-module, so that
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathtt{MO}}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}[u]
carries a
\HO_{T}
-module structure, and the domain of
\Phi
is the free algebra object (in the tensor category of
\HO_{T}
-modules with tensor structure
\otimes_{\HO_{T}}
) generated by this module. As for (generalised) Kac–Moody Lie algebras, there is a triangular decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}\cong{\mathfrak{n}}^{\mathtt{MO},-}_{Q}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{\mathtt{MO},+}_{Q}
. We define
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathtt{MO},+}_{Q}
to be the image of
\Sym_{\HO_{T}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{\mathtt{MO},+}_{Q}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}[u])
under
\Phi
. It is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}
.

In
[
5
]
we proved that there is an isomorphism
\psi\colon{\mathfrak{g}}^{T}_{\Pi_{Q}\lmod}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{T,\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}
. It then follows from
[
18
, Corollary 11.9]
that
{\mathfrak{g}}^{T,\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}
can actually be defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
: there is a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and an isomorphism of Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}^{T,\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}\otimes\HO_{T}
, where
[g\otimes\lambda,g^{\prime}\otimes\lambda^{\prime}]=[g,g^{\prime}]\otimes\lambda\lambda^{\prime}
. Furthermore, from §
5.2
we obtain a complete description of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathtt{MO}}_{Q}
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\coh}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{Q_{0}}
-graded GKM algebra. From Theorem
3.8
we deduce the following equality, conjectured by Okounkov, and proven independently in
[
62
]
:
\poinc({\mathfrak{g}}_{Q,\mathbf{d}}^{\mathtt{MO}})=\kac_{Q,\mathbf{d}}(q^{-1})
. Moreover,
\Psi
extends to an isomorphism
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Pi_{Q}}^{T}\cong\mathcal{Y}^{\mathtt{MO},+}_{Q}

[
5
,
62
]
, so that we can fully describe the Yangian via a full description of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Pi_{Q}}^{T}
. This strategy is completed for the case of cyclic quivers by Jindal
[
33
]
.

5.4.
Nonabelian Hodge isomorphisms

Fix a smooth projective curve
C
, and let
\Sigma_{g}
be the underlying Riemann surface. To start with we consider semistable Higgs bundles of slope
\theta=0
; in order to work with other slopes, on the Betti side of the nonabelian Hodge correspondence one should consider
twisted
representations of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
below, i.e. representations of the punctured Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g}\setminus\{p\}
for which the monodromy around
p
is multiplication by a prescribed root of unity. We write
\mathfrak{M}^{\Dol}_{r,d}
for the moduli stack of rank
r
and degree
d
semistable Higgs bundles,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\Dol}_{r,d}
for the coarse moduli space, and
\mathfrak{M}^{\Betti}_{r}
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{\Betti}_{r}
for the moduli stack and coarse moduli space of
r
-dimensional
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
-representations.

We write
{\mathcal{H}}^{\Betti}=\HO^{\BoMo}(\mathfrak{M}^{\Betti}_{\bullet},{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vdim/2}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{\Dol}=\HO^{\BoMo}(\mathfrak{M}^{\Dol}_{\bullet,0},{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes{\mathbb{L}}^{\vdim/2}
. By Theorem
5.1
these two
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\coh}\oplus{\mathbb{N}}
-graded vector spaces are determined by the intersection cohomology of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\Betti}
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{\Dol}
respectively. These spaces are homeomorphic, via the classical nonabelian Hodge correspondence, and it follows that there is an isomorphism
[
17
]
of graded vector spaces
\Lambda\colon{\mathcal{H}}^{\Betti}\cong{\mathcal{H}}^{\Dol}
(see
[
29
]
for an upgrade to an algebra isomorphism).

This isomorphism does
not
lift to an isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures. The
\chi
-independence theorem of
[
43
]
states that, considered as filtered vector spaces, with the filtration defined via perverse filtration functors with respect to the direct image to the base of the Hitchin system, there is an isomorphism
\BPS^{\Dol}_{r}\cong\HO({\mathcal{M}}_{r,1}^{\Dol},{\mathbb{Q}})[-2(1-g)r^{2}]
. It follows that there is an isomorphism of cohomologically graded vector spaces
\BPS^{\Betti}_{r}\cong\HO({\mathcal{M}}^{\Betti,\twisted}_{r},{\mathbb{Q}})[-2(1-g)r^{2}]
where the target is the cohomology of the twisted character variety. This cohomology provides one side of the P=W conjecture relating this cohomology with that of
{\mathcal{M}}_{r,1}^{\Dol}
. The P=W conjecture is now a theorem
[
28
,
49
,
32
]
. Putting all this together, it is natural to make the following:

Conjecture 5.2
.

\Lambda
takes the weight filtration on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\Betti}
to a (doubled) perverse filtration on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\Dol}
.

We refer to
[
14
]
for the precise definition of the perverse filtration on
{\mathcal{H}}^{\Dol}
, the precise form of the conjecture, and the relation to the PI=WI conjecture of de Cataldo and Maulik
[
21
]
.

6.
BPS cohomology beyond moduli stacks of objects

Many stacks of interest are not moduli stacks of objects in a linear category. For instance, let
M
be a real oriented closed three-manifold without boundary, and consider the stack
\mathfrak{M}_{G}(M)
of
G
-local systems for
G
a connected reductive group not equal to
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
. By
[
56
]
, this stack has a (-1)-shifted symplectic structure, and by
[
53
]
it carries orientation data, so that we can define the DT sheaf
\varphi_{\DT}
as in §
2.6
. It is not immediately clear how to
define
the BPS sheaf or integrality for
\mathfrak{M}_{G}(M)
. For example if
G
is semisimple, the decomposition of
p_{*}\varphi_{\DT}
into cohomologically shifted mixed Hodge modules starts in degree
zero
, not one, where
p\colon\mathfrak{M}_{G}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(M)
is the good moduli space morphism.

Nonetheless, there
is
a version of BPS cohomology and integrality for this stack, which is special case of a general integrality theorem for (-1)-shifted stacks; we refer the reader to
[
8
,
31
]
for all the details, and also the analogous results for
(0)
-shifted and smooth stacks. For a finite type (oriented) (-1)-shifted symplectic stack
\mathfrak{M}
with good moduli space
p\colon\mathfrak{M}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}
, the analogue of Proposition
2.4
is:

Proposition 6.1

(
[
8
]
Proposition 7.2.9)
.

There is an isomorphism
p_{*}\varphi_{\DT}\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq\mathrm{crk}(\mathfrak{M})}{\mathcal{H}}^{i}\left(p_{*}\varphi_{\DT}\right)[-i]
in the derived category of unbounded complexes of mixed Hodge modules on
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Here
\crk(\mathfrak{M})
is the maximal
r
such that there exists an action of
\pt/T
on
\mathfrak{M}
with
T
a rank
r
torus, that does not factor through an action of
\pt/T^{\prime}
for
T^{\prime}
of lower rank. The proposition motivates the definitions

\shBPS_{\mathfrak{M}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(p_{*}\varphi_{\DT}\otimes{\mathbb{L}}^{-\mathrm{crk}(\mathfrak{M})/2});\quad\quad\BPS_{\mathfrak{M}}\coloneqq\HO({\mathcal{M}},\shBPS_{\mathfrak{M}}).

Write
c_{G}=\mathrm{crk}(\mathfrak{M}_{G}(M))
. The cohomological integrality isomorphism for
\mathfrak{M}_{G}(M)
then states that there is an isomorphism
\bigoplus_{L\subset G}\left(\BPS_{\mathfrak{M}_{L}(M)}\otimes{\mathbb{L}}^{c_{L}/2}\otimes\HO(\pt/({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{c_{L}},{\mathbb{Q}})\right)^{W_{L}}\cong\HO(\mathfrak{M}_{G}(M),\varphi_{\DT})
where
W_{L}
is the relative Weyl group of
L
, and the sum is over conjugacy classes of Levi subgroups of
G
. We refer to
[
8
, Section 10.3]
for details. The existence of this isomorphism was proved in
[
8
]
under a certain orthogonality assumption on the tangent weights of
\mathfrak{M}_{G}(M)
, and then proved without this assumption in
[
31
]
.

We expect that the study of BPS cohomology for stacks of local systems on three manifolds will give a powerful toolkit for defining and understanding enumerative invariants in low-dimensional topology. For example, we finish with the following conjecture of Pavel Safronov, linking Betti Langlands duality to the study of this generalised BPS cohomology:

Conjecture 6.2
.

For
(G,G^{\vee})
Langlands dual groups, there is an isomorphism
\BPS_{\mathfrak{M}_{G}(M)}\cong\BPS_{\mathfrak{M}_{G^{\vee}}(M)}
.

For
M=(S^{1})^{3}
the conjecture was proved by Kaubrys
[
40
]
for
G=\mathrm{SL}_{p}
with
p
prime, and is proved for many more Langlands dual pairs of groups in forthcoming work of Hennecart and Kinjo.

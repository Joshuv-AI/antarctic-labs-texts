Exceptional sets for restricted families of projections in 𝔽 q d \mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
Doowon Koh Thang Pham Le Quang Hung Do Trong Hoang Le Quang Ham

Abstract
Let d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and 𝔽 q d \mathbb{F}_{q}^{\,d} be the d d -dimensional vector space over a finite field of order q q , where q q is a prime power. Fix a slice π = { x d = λ } \pi=\{x_{d}=\lambda\} of the unit sphere S d − 1 = { x : ‖ x ‖ = 1 } S^{d-1}=\{x:\|x\|=1\} and let X π X_{\pi} be the set of lines through the origin meeting π ∩ S d − 1 \pi\cap S^{d-1} . For E ⊂ 𝔽 q d E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\,d} and N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 , we study the exceptional sets 𝒯 1 ​ ( X π , E , N ) = { V ∈ X π : | π V ​ ( E ) | ≤ N } , 𝒯 2 ​ ( X π , E , N ) = { V ∈ X π : | π V ⟂ ​ ( E ) | ≤ N } , \mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)=\bigl\{V\in X_{\pi}:\ |\pi_{V}(E)|\leq N\bigr\},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)=\bigl\{V\in X_{\pi}:\ |\pi_{V^{\perp}}(E)|\leq N\bigr\}, on their respective natural ranges of N N . Using discrete Fourier analysis together with restriction/extension estimates for cone and sphere type quadrics over finite fields, we obtain sharp bounds (up to constant factors) for | 𝒯 1 | \lvert\mathcal{T}_{1}\rvert and | 𝒯 2 | \lvert\mathcal{T}_{2}\rvert , with separate treatment of the special slices λ = ± 1 \lambda=\pm 1 and of the isotropic slice λ = 0 \lambda=0 . The bounds exhibit arithmetic–geometric dichotomies absent in the full Grassmannian: the quadratic character of λ 2 − 1 \lambda^{2}-1 and the parity of d d determine the size of the exceptional sets. As an application, when | E | ≥ q |E|\geq q , there exists a positive proportion of elements 𝐲 ∈ X π \mathbf{y}\in X_{\pi} such that the pinned dot-product sets { 𝐲 ⋅ 𝐱 : 𝐱 ∈ E } \{\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{x}\colon\mathbf{x}\in E\} are of cardinality Ω ⁡ ( q ) \Omega(q) . We further treat analogous families arising from the spheres of radii 0 0 and − 1 -1 , and by combining these slices, recover the known estimates for projections over the full Grassmannian, complementing a result of Chen (2018).

Let

\sum_{y\in V^{\perp}}e(\textup{{Tr}}(-\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}))=0,

where
\textup{{Tr}}(\alpha)=\alpha+\alpha^{2}+\cdots+\alpha^{p^{r-1}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q},
and
e(t)=e^{\frac{-2\pi it}{p}}
for
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the standard Gauss sum:

\mathcal{G}:=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\eta(t)\chi(t),

where
\eta
is the quadratic character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
, i.e. a group homomorphism
defined by
\eta(t)=1
if
t
is a square,
\eta(0)=0
(conventional), and
-1
otherwise.
Recall that the absolute value of the Gauss sum
\mathcal{G}
is
\sqrt{q}
, and

\mathcal{G}^{2}=\eta(-1)q.
The next lemma records the classical explicit form of the quadratic Gauss sum.

Lemma 2.3

(Theorem 5.15,
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of order
q=p^{r}
, where
p
is an odd prime and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Then we have

\mathcal{G}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{(-1)}^{r-1}q^{\frac{1}{2}}&\mbox{if}\quad p\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
{(-1)}^{r-1}i^{r}q^{\frac{1}{2}}&\mbox{if}\quad p\equiv 3\pmod{4}.\end{array}\right.

This lemma tells us that if
d\equiv 2\mod 4
and
q\equiv 3\mod 4
then
\mathcal{G}^{d}=-q^{\frac{d}{2}}
. In several upper-bound arguments, the negative sign lets us discard the would-be leading term, giving a sharper estimate

The next lemma will also be used in some places.

Lemma 2.4

(Lemma 2.3,
[
15
]
)
.

For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have

\sum_{s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(as^{2}+bs)=\eta(a)\cdot\mathcal{G}\cdot\chi\left(\frac{b^{2}}{-4a}\right).

3
Tools from Restriction and Extension theory

Given a function
f\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, we note that its Fourier transform is a complex-valued function defined on the dual space. Since the dual space of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, we use the same notation
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
for the dual space.

For a variety
V\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, the
L^{b}\to L^{a}
Fourier extension problem for
V
asks us to determine all exponents
1\leq a,b\leq\infty
such that the following inequality holds

\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}\left|\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in V}\chi(\xi\cdot\mathbf{x})f(\mathbf{x})\right|^{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in V}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{b}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}},

(3)

for all functions
f
on
V
, where the positive constant
C
does not depend on
q
. By duality, the inequality (
3
) is equivalent with the following Fourier restriction estimate

\left(\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in V}|\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x})|^{b^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{b^{\prime}}}\leq C\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}|g(\xi)|^{a^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a^{\prime}}},

(4)

for any function
g
on the dual space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, where
1/a+1/a^{\prime}=1,~1/b+1/b^{\prime}=1
. In this section, by the notation
R_{V}^{*}(b\to a)\ll 1
, we mean that the inequality (
3
) holds.

3.1
Restriction estimates for a cone type variety

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\{0,\pm 1\}
. We denote

H_{\lambda}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d-1}^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{\lambda^{2}}x_{d}^{2}=0\right\}.

Note that
H_{\lambda}=\{s\mathbf{x}:s\in\mathbb{F}_{q},\mathbf{x}\in X_{\pi}\}
which is a cone type variety.

For
\lambda\neq\pm 1,
the cardinality of
X_{\pi}
is the same as that of
\pi\cap S^{d-1}.

Thus we see that

\displaystyle|X_{\pi}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\pi\cap S^{d-1}\right|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:\ x_{d}=\lambda,\\
x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d-1}^{2}=1-\lambda^{2}\end{subarray}}1.

Namely,
|X_{\pi}|
is the number of the solutions of the equation
x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d-1}^{2}=1-\lambda^{2}.
which is well-known as
follows (see
[
1
, Theorem A.1]
)

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
X_{\pi}
be as defined before.

(i)

If
d\geq 2
is even, then we have

|X_{\pi}|=\begin{cases}q^{d-2}+q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\eta\left((-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right),&\text{if $\lambda\notin\left\{-1,1\right\}$},\\
q^{d-2},&\text{if $\lambda\in\left\{-1,1\right\}$}.\end{cases}

(ii)

If
d\geq 3
is odd, then

|X_{\pi}|=\begin{cases}q^{d-2}-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}(\eta(-1))^{\frac{d-1}{2}},&\text{if $\lambda\notin\left\{-1,1\right\}$},\\
q^{d-2}+q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}(\eta(-1))^{\frac{d-1}{2}}-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}(\eta(-1))^{\frac{d-1}{2}},&\text{if $\lambda\in\left\{-1,1\right\}$}.\end{cases}

We set

R_{\lambda}(E):=\sum_{\xi\in H_{\lambda}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

Utilizing discrete Fourier analysis and estimates of Gauss sums, we establish upper bounds for
R_{\lambda}(E)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
d\geq 2
is even, and
\eta\left((-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=-1,
then

R_{\lambda}(E)\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|E|^{2}.

(ii)

If
d\geq 2
is even, and
\eta\left((-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=1,
then

R_{\lambda}(E)\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d}{2}}|E|^{2}-q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|E|^{2}.

(iii)

If
d\geq 3
is odd, then

R_{\lambda}(E)\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}|E|^{2}.

Proof.

Set
\varepsilon=\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{\lambda^{2}}.
Then, by the definitions of the Fourier transform and the variety
H_{\lambda},
it follows that

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(E)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:\xi^{2}_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}+\varepsilon\xi_{d}^{2}=0}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi((\alpha-\beta)\cdot\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}\left(q^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(t(\xi_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}+\varepsilon\xi_{d}^{2}))\right)\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi((\alpha-\beta)\cdot\xi),

where the orthogonality relation of
\chi
was also applied.
Separately computing
t=0
and
t\neq 0
in the sum over
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we obtain that

R_{\lambda}(E)=q^{d-1}|E|+q^{-1}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\sum_{t\neq 0}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}\chi(t(\xi_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}+\varepsilon\xi_{d}^{2}))\chi((\alpha-\beta)\cdot\xi).

Computing the sum in
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
by invoking the Gauss sum estimates, we get

R_{\lambda}(E)=q^{d-1}|E|+q^{-1}\eta(\varepsilon)\mathcal{G}^{d}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\left(\sum_{t\neq 0}\eta^{d}(t)\chi\left(\frac{(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d})^{2}-\varepsilon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||}{4\varepsilon t}\right)\right),

(5)

where we write
\alpha^{\prime}
for
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}.

Case 1.
Assume that
d\geq 3
is an odd integer.
Then the sum over
t\neq 0
in (
5
) is related to the Gauss sum and its absolute value is
\sqrt{q}.
Since
|\eta|=1
we therefore obtain

R_{\lambda}(E)\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{-1}q^{\frac{d}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}}|E|^{2},

which completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
(iii).

Case 2.
Assume that
d\geq 4
is even.
Since
\eta^{d}\equiv 1,
it follows from (
5
) that

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(E)=

\displaystyle q^{d-1}|E|+q^{-1}\eta(\varepsilon)\mathcal{G}^{d}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\left(\sum_{t\neq 0}\chi\left(\frac{(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d})^{2}-\varepsilon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||}{4\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{1}{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{d-1}|E|+q^{-1}\eta(\varepsilon)\mathcal{G}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E:\\
(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d})^{2}-\varepsilon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0\end{subarray}}(q-1)+q^{-1}\eta(\varepsilon)\mathcal{G}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E:\\
(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d})^{2}-\varepsilon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||\neq 0\end{subarray}}(-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{d-1}|E|+\eta(\varepsilon)\mathcal{G}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E:\\
(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d})^{2}-\varepsilon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0\end{subarray}}1-q^{-1}\eta(\varepsilon)\mathcal{G}^{d}|E|^{2}.

Since
\varepsilon=\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{\lambda^{2}}
and
\mathcal{G}^{2}=\eta(-1)q,

R_{\lambda}(E)=q^{d-1}|E|+\eta(\lambda^{2}-1)(\eta(-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}q^{\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E:\\
(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d})^{2}-\varepsilon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0\end{subarray}}1-\eta(\lambda^{2}-1)(\eta(-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|E|^{2}.

(6)

Case 2.1.
Assume that
\eta\left((-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=-1.

It follows from (
6
) that

R_{\lambda}(E)=q^{d-1}|E|-q^{\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E:\\
(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d})^{2}-\varepsilon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0\end{subarray}}1+q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|E|^{2}.

Since the second term above is negative, Lemma
3.2
(i) follows.

Case 2.2.
Assume that
\eta\left((-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=1.
Then we see from (
6
) that

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(E)

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E:\\
(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d})^{2}-\varepsilon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0\end{subarray}}1-q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|E|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d}{2}}|E|^{2}-q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}|E|^{2}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.2
(ii).
∎

4
Bounding
\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)
for
\lambda\neq\{0,\pm 1\}
(Theorem
1.3
)

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
X\subset G(d,m).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X,E,N\right)
be defined as in (
1
). Then we have

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X,E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{N}{\left(q^{m}-N\right)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2},

where
\widehat{E}(\xi)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in E}\chi(-\mathbf{x}\cdot\xi)
for
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}.

Proof.

For
V\in G(d,m)
, let
\{\mathbf{x}_{V^{\perp}_{j}}+V^{\perp}\}_{j=1}^{q^{m}}
be the set of disjoint translations of
V^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. From definition of
\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle|E|^{2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q^{m}}|E\cap(\mathbf{x}_{V_{j}^{\perp}}+V^{\perp})|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\pi_{V}(E)|\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q^{m}}\left|E\cap(\mathbf{x}_{V_{j}^{\perp}}+V^{\perp})\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q^{m}}\left|E\cap(\mathbf{x}_{V_{j}^{\perp}}+V^{\perp})\right|^{2}\right),

for all
V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X,E,N\right)
.
By this inequality and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}}{|E|^{2}}=\frac{\sum_{\xi\in V}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}}{|E|^{2}}-1=\frac{q^{m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q^{m}}\left|E\cap(x_{V_{j}^{\perp}}+V^{\perp})\right|^{2}\right)}{|E|^{2}}-1

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q^{m}}{N}-1=\frac{q^{m}-N}{N},

for all
V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)
.
Therefore, taking the sum over all
V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)
gives

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)\right|\leq\frac{N}{\left(q^{m}-N\right)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

Thí completes the proof.
∎

The next theorem shows that we can bound the size of
\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)
by using restriction estimates for the cone type variety
H_{\lambda}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}.
Then we have

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in H_{\lambda}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, it follows that

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2},

Since
\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\subset X_{\pi}=[\Pi_{\lambda}\cap S^{d-1}],

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

Notice that to complete the proof, it is enough to show that the two claims below hold:

Claim 1:

V\subset H_{\lambda}
for all
V\in X_{\pi}
.

Indeed, let
V\in X_{\pi}.
Then
V
is a line passing through the origin and a point
\mathbf{x}
in
\Pi_{\lambda}\cap S^{d-1}.
In other words, we have

V=\{s\mathbf{x}:s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
where
x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d-1}^{2}+\lambda^{2}-1=0
and
x_{d}=\lambda.

Since
(sx_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(sx_{d-1})^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{\lambda^{2}}(sx_{d})^{2}=0
for all
s\in\mathbb{F}_{q},
we see that
V\subset H_{\lambda}.
Thus, the claim holds true.

Claim 2:
If
V,W\in X_{\pi}
with
V\neq W
, then
V\cap W=\{(0,\ldots,0)\}.

Indeed, suppose that
V,W\in X_{\pi}
with
V\neq W.
Then for some
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1},\lambda),~\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d-1},\lambda)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}
, we can write

V=\{s(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1},\lambda):s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},

and

W=\{t(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d-1},\lambda):t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

It is clear that
\mathbf{0}\in V\cap W.
Now, let us assume that
\mathbf{0}\neq\gamma\in V\cap W.
Then for some
s,t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
we have

s(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1},\lambda)=t(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d-1},\lambda).

Since
\lambda\neq 0,
we see that
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y},
which makes a contradiction. In conclusion,
V\cap W=\{\mathbf{0}\},
as required.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}
. Then, we have

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{Nq^{d}}{(q-N)|E|}.

Proof.

Since
H_{\lambda}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},
it is clear from Proposition
4.2
that

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

By the Plancherel theorem,
\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}=q^{d}|E|.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Combining Proposition
4.2
and Lemma

(a)

If

is odd, then

(b)

If
d\geq 4

Corollary 4.4
.

For

, let

(ii)

d\geq 4
is even,
\eta\left((-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=1,
and
|E|>\dfrac{Nq^{d-1}}{\epsilon(q-N)q^{d-2}-(N-\epsilon)q^{\frac{d}{2}}+(1-\epsilon)Nq^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}.

(iii)

d\geq 5
is odd, and

|E|>\dfrac{Nq^{3}}{\epsilon(q-N)(q-\eta(-1))}.

Proof.

First recall that
\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)=\left\{V\in X_{\pi}:|\pi_{V}(E)|\leq N\right\}.

It is clear from the definition of

If
d\geq 4
is even, and

(3.1-2)

If
d\geq 4
is even, and
\eta\left((-1))^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=1,

\displaystyle\frac{Nq^{d-1}}{\epsilon(q-N)q^{d-2}-(N-\epsilon)q^{\frac{d}{2}}+(1-\epsilon)Nq^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}<|E|.

\displaystyle\frac{Nq^{3}}{\epsilon(q-N)(q-\eta(-1))}<|E|.

Hence, the proof of Corollary
4.4
is complete.
∎

5
Bounding
\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)
for
\lambda=\pm 1
(Theorem
1.4
)

Compared to the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we need the following analog of Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
\lambda\in\left\{-1,1\right\}
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
d\geq 4
is even, then

\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d}{2}}|E|^{2}.

(ii)

For
d\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, then

\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}|E|^{2}.

(iii)

For other cases, then

\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d+1}{2}}|E|^{2}.

Proof.

By applying the orthogonality relation of
\chi
, Lemma
2.4
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{0\}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon\\
\xi^{2}_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}=0,\\
\xi_{d}=\lambda\end{subarray}}\left|\widehat{E}(k\xi)\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon\\
\xi_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}=0\end{subarray}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon\\
\xi^{2}_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi((\alpha-\beta)\cdot\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{q}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(t(\xi_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}))\right)\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi((\alpha-\beta)\cdot\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{\xi^{\ast}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(t||\xi^{\ast}||)\chi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i})\xi_{i}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{\xi^{\ast}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\chi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i})\xi_{i}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{q}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{\xi^{\ast}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\chi(t||\xi^{\ast}||)\chi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i})\xi_{i}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{d-2}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E\colon\\
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i},\forall i\leq d-1\end{subarray}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d-1}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{t\neq 0}\eta(t)^{d-1}\chi\left(\frac{||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||}{-4t}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{d-2}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha\in E}1+q^{d-2}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\colon\\
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i},i\leq d-1,\\
\alpha_{d}\neq\beta_{d}\end{subarray}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d-1}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{t\neq 0}\eta(t)^{d-1}\chi\left(\frac{||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||}{-4t}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d-1}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{t\neq 0}\eta(t)^{d-1}\chi\left(\frac{||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||}{-4t}\right),

where
\alpha^{\prime}=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d-1}\right),\beta^{\prime}=\left(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d-1}\right)
.

Case 1:
If
d\geq 4
is even, then
d-1
is odd. Then
\eta(t)^{d-1}=\eta(t)=\eta\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)
, and apply Lemma
2.3
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq q^{d-1}|E|+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d-1}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{t\neq 0}\eta(t)\chi\left(\frac{||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||}{-4t}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d-1}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|=0}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{t\neq 0}\eta(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d-1}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|\neq 0}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{t\neq 0}\eta(t)\chi\left(\frac{||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||}{-4t}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|\neq 0}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\eta\left(\frac{-1}{\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|\neq 0}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\eta\left(\frac{-1}{\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq q^{d-1}|E|+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|\neq 0}1

\displaystyle\leq q^{d-1}|E|+\frac{q^{\frac{d}{2}}}{q}q|E|^{2}=q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d}{2}}|E|^{2}.

Case 2:
If
d\geq 3
is odd, then
\eta^{d-1}(t)=1
. So

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq q^{d-1}|E|+\frac{1}{q}\mathcal{G}^{d-1}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))\sum_{t\neq 0}\chi\left(\frac{||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||}{-4t}\right).

Note that from Lemma
2.3
, if
d\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, and
q=p^{r}
with
r
odd,
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, then
\mathcal{G}^{d-1}=-q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
, otherwise, we have
\mathcal{G}^{d-1}=q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
.

Case 2.1.
If
d,q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
r
is odd, then

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq q^{d-1}|E|-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}(q-1)\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\colon\\
||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0\end{subarray}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))

\displaystyle\quad+q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\colon\\
||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|-q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\colon\\
||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))+q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|-q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|=0,\\
\alpha_{d}=\beta_{d}\end{subarray}}q-q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|=0,\\
\alpha_{d}\neq\beta_{d}\end{subarray}}0

\displaystyle\quad+q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\colon\alpha_{d}=\beta_{d}}q+q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\colon\alpha_{d}\neq\beta_{d}}0\quad\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}|E|^{2}.

Case 2.2.
For other cases, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}(q-1)\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E\colon\\
||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0\end{subarray}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))

\displaystyle\quad-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in E\colon\\
||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\beta_{d}-\alpha_{d}))

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\colon||\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}||=0}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\sum_{\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\chi(\xi_{d}(\alpha_{d}-\beta_{d}))

\displaystyle=q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|=0,\\
\alpha_{d}=\beta_{d}\end{subarray}}q+q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\colon\left\|\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}\right\|=0,\\
\alpha_{d}=\neq\beta_{d}\end{subarray}}0

\displaystyle\quad-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\colon\alpha_{d}=\beta_{d}}q-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\colon\alpha_{d}\neq\beta_{d}}0\quad\leq q^{d-1}|E|+q^{\frac{d+1}{2}}|E|^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now recall and prove Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, then we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)|E|}+\dfrac{q^{\frac{d}{2}}N}{q-N},&\text{if $d\geq 4$ and even},\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)|E|}+\dfrac{q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}N}{q-N},&\text{if $d,q\equiv 3\pmod{4}$},\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)|E|}+\dfrac{q^{\frac{d+1}{2}}N}{q-N},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, it follows that

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

Recall that
\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)\subset X_{\pi}
, we are reduced to

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

Now applying Lemma
5.1
,we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)|E|}+\dfrac{q^{\frac{d}{2}}N}{q-N},&\text{if $d\geq 4$ and even},\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)|E|}+\dfrac{q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}N}{q-N},&\text{if $d,q\equiv 3\pmod{4}$},\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)|E|}+\dfrac{q^{\frac{d+1}{2}}N}{q-N},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

∎

6
Application on the pinned dot-product problem (Theorem
1.7
)

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We begin with two preliminary observations.

Observation 1.
From the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, if there exists a constant
C>1
such that

\big|\mathcal{T}_{1}\big(X_{\pi},E,N\big)\big|<\frac{|X_{\pi}|}{C},

then at least
\big(1-\tfrac{1}{C}\big)|X_{\pi}|
points
\mathbf{y}\in\left(\pi\cap S^{d-1}\right)
satisfy

\#\{\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{x}\in E\}\geq N.

Observation 2.

Recall from Lemma
3.1
that for
d\geq 3
we have

|X_{\pi}|=\begin{cases}q^{d-2}+q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\,\eta\!\left((-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right),&\text{if $d$ is even and $\lambda\notin\{-1,1\}$},\\[6.0pt]
q^{d-2},&\text{if $d$ is even and $\lambda\in\{-1,1\}$},\\[6.0pt]
q^{d-2}-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\big(\eta(-1)\big)^{\frac{d-1}{2}},&\text{if $d$ is odd and $\lambda\notin\{-1,1\}$},\\[6.0pt]
q^{d-2}+q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\big(\eta(-1)\big)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}-q^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\big(\eta(-1)\big)^{\frac{d-1}{2}},&\text{if $d$ is odd and $\lambda\in\{-1,1\}$}.\end{cases}

Thus, for
d\geq 3
and sufficiently large
q
, we obtain

|X_{\pi}|\geq\frac{q^{d-2}}{2},

except in the case
d=3
,
\lambda\in\{\pm 1\}
, and
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, where
|X_{\pi}|=1
.

Combining the above observations, it suffices to show that

\big|\mathcal{T}_{1}\big(X_{\pi},E,N\big)\big|<\frac{q^{d-2}}{3}\leq\frac{2|X_{\pi}|}{3}.

Case 1:

\boldsymbol{\lambda\notin\{0,\pm 1\}}
.
By Theorem
1.3
, for
d\geq 3
,
0<N<q
, and
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, we have

\big|\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)\big|\leq\frac{Nq^{d-1}}{(q-N)|E|}+\begin{cases}\dfrac{Nq^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{q-N},&\text{if $d$ is odd},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{Nq^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}{q-N},&\text{if $d$ is even and $\eta\left((-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=-1$},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{N\big(q^{\frac{d}{2}}-q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\big)}{q-N},&\text{if $d$ is even and $\eta\left((-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=1$}.\end{cases}

For sufficiently large
q
, with
|E|\geq q
and
N=\lfloor q/10\rfloor
, we have
q-N\geq 0.9q
, hence

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{d-1}}{9q}+\begin{cases}\dfrac{q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{9},&\text{if $d$ is odd},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}{9},&\text{if $d$ is even, $\eta\left((-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=-1$},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{\frac{d}{2}}-q^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}{9},&\text{if $d$ is even, $\eta\left((-1)^{\frac{d}{2}}(\lambda^{2}-1)\right)=1$.}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{d-2}}{9}+\frac{q^{d-2}}{9}\leq\frac{q^{d-2}}{3}\leq\frac{2|X_{\pi}|}{3}.

Case 2:

\boldsymbol{\lambda\in\{\pm 1\}}
.
Applying Theorem
1.4
, for
d\geq 4,0<N<q
, and
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, we have

\big|\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)\big|\leq\frac{Nq^{d-1}}{(q-N)|E|}+\begin{cases}\dfrac{Nq^{\frac{d}{2}}}{q-N},&\text{if $d$ is even},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{Nq^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{q-N},&\text{if $d\equiv 3\pmod{4}$ and $q\equiv 3\pmod{4}$},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{Nq^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}{q-N},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For sufficiently large
q
, with
|E|\geq q
and
N=\lfloor q/10\rfloor
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{d-1}}{9q}+\begin{cases}\dfrac{q^{\frac{d}{2}}}{9},&\text{if $d$ is even},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{9},&\text{if $d\equiv 3\pmod{4},\,q\equiv 3\pmod{4}$},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}{9},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{d-2}}{9}+\frac{q^{d-2}}{9}\leq\frac{q^{d-2}}{3}\leq\frac{2|X_{\pi}|}{3}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

In the above proof, the remaining case is:
d=3
,
\lambda\in\{-1,1\}
, and
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

If we apply Theorem
1.4
with
N=\frac{q}{a}
for some
0<a<1
, we obtain

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,\frac{q}{a}\right)\right|\leq\frac{aq^{2}}{(1-a)|E|}+\frac{aq^{2}}{1-a}.

For sufficiently large

\frac{aq^{2}}{1-a}>2q-1=|X_{\pi}|,

and thus Theorem

fails to provide any meaningful information in this case.

7
Bounding
\mathcal{T}_{1}(X,E,N)
for
\lambda=0
(Theorem
1.5
)

Theorem
1.5
follows from the following general statement.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
\lambda=0
.
Then we have

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\ll\begin{cases}\dfrac{q^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|S^{d-2}\right|N}{(q-N)|E|^{\frac{1}{2}}},&\text{if $d=3$},\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{q^{\frac{2d-2}{d}}\left|S^{d-2}\right|N}{(q-N)|E|^{\frac{d-2}{d}}},&\text{for other cases}.\end{cases}

If we define
f(\mathbf{x})
as the number of elements
\alpha
in
E
with
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d-1})=\mathbf{x}
, then

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{2q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)|E|}\cdot\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x}).

As in previous sections, we make use of the following analog of Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
d=3
, then

\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}\ll q^{3/2}\left|S^{d-2}\right||E|^{3/2}.

(ii)

For other cases, then

\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}\ll q^{\frac{2d-2}{d}}\left|S^{d-2}\right||E|^{\frac{d+2}{d}}.

Proof.

For
\alpha\in E
, define
f(\alpha^{\prime})
to be the number of elements
\alpha
in
E
with
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d-1})=\alpha^{\prime}
.

\displaystyle\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon\\
\xi_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}=1,\\
\xi_{d}=0\end{subarray}}\left|\widehat{E}(k\xi)\right|^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}:\\
\xi_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}=1,\\
\xi_{d}=0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}\chi((\alpha-\beta)\cdot k\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1},||\xi||=1}\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\alpha^{\prime})f(\beta^{\prime})\chi(\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime})\cdot k\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}|\widehat{f}(k\xi)|^{2}.

To bound
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\left|\widehat{f}(k\xi)\right|^{2}
, we apply Theorem
3.3
for
\widehat{f}
. More precise, for each
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
, it is clear that
kS^{d-2}=S^{d-2}_{k^{2}}
, so, one has

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\left|\frac{1}{\left|S^{d-2}\right|}\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\chi(-k\xi\cdot\mathbf{x})\widehat{f}(k\xi)\right|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2d}}\ll\left(\frac{1}{\left|S^{d-2}\right|}\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\left|\widehat{f}(k\xi)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From this, by Hölder inequality, for each
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\left|\widehat{f}(k\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\widehat{f}(k\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}(k\xi)}=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x})\overline{\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\chi(k\xi\cdot\mathbf{x})\widehat{f}(k\xi)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\left|f(\mathbf{x})\right|^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\right)^{\frac{d+2}{2d}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\left|\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\chi(k\xi\cdot\mathbf{x})\widehat{f}(k\xi)\right|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2d}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\left|f(\mathbf{x})\right|^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\right)^{\frac{d+2}{2d}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\left|\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\chi(-k\xi\cdot\mathbf{x})\widehat{f}(k\xi)\right|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2d}}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\left|f(\mathbf{x})\right|^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\right)^{\frac{d+2}{2d}}\left(\left|S^{d-2}\right|\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}\left|\widehat{f}(k\xi)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus,

\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}|\widehat{f}(k\xi)|^{2}\ll\left|S^{d-2}\right|\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}|f(x)|^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\right)^{\frac{d+2}{d}},

for each
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
. Note that
f(\mathbf{x})\leq q
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}
. Therefore,

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\leq q^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\cdot\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x})=q^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}\cdot|E|.

This implies that

\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}\ll(q-1)\left|S^{d-2}\right|\left(q^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}|E|\right)^{\frac{d+2}{d}}\leq\left|S^{d-2}\right|q^{\frac{2d-2}{d}}|E|^{\frac{d+2}{d}}.

When
d=3
, by the same argument but using Theorem
3.4
instead of Theorem
3.3
, one has a better estimate, namely,

\sum_{\xi\in S^{1}}|\widehat{f}(k\xi)|^{2}\ll\left|S^{d-2}\right|\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}},

for each
k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
.
Hence,

\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}\ll\left|S^{d-2}\right|(q-1)q^{\frac{1}{2}}|E|^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\left|S^{d-2}\right|q^{\frac{3}{2}}|E|^{\frac{3}{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove Theorem
7.1
.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

By Theorem
4.1
and recall that
\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)\subset X_{\pi}=[\pi\cap S^{d-1}]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

Now, applying Lemma
7.2
we get the first estimate.

To prove the second estimate, we proceed in an alternative way

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{V\in X_{\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in V\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}=\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\xi\in\pi\cap S^{d-1}}|\widehat{E}(k\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\colon\\
\xi_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\xi_{d-1}^{2}=1,\xi_{d}=0\end{subarray}}|\widehat{E}(k\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1},||\xi||=1}\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\alpha^{\prime})f(\beta^{\prime})\chi(\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime})\cdot k\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}}\sum_{\xi\in S^{d-2}}|\widehat{f}(k\xi)|^{2}\leq\frac{N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\cdot 2\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|^{2}.

Thus, it is enough to bound the following

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{2N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2},

where
f(\alpha^{\prime})
is defined as the number of elements
\alpha
in
E
with
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d-1})=\alpha^{\prime}
.

Therefore, by Plancherel identity and note that
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x})=|E|
, one has

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\cdot q^{d-1}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}\left|f(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2N}{(q-N)|E|^{2}}\cdot q^{d-1}\cdot\left(\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x})\right)\cdot\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{2N}{(q-N)|E|}\cdot q^{d-1}\cdot\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x}).

This completes the proof.
∎

8
Bounding
\mathcal{T}_{2}(X,E,N)
(Theorem
1.6
)

While restriction type lemmas (Lemmas
3.2
,
5.1
,
7.2
) play a crucial role in bounding the size of the exceptional set
\mathcal{T}_{1}(X_{\pi},E,N)
, the following geometric result is sufficient in estimating the size of
\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\ell
be a line passing through the origin in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
.

1.

If
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, then
|\ell^{\perp}\cap\{x_{d}=\lambda\}\cap S^{d-1}|\leq\begin{cases}2q^{d-2},&\text{if $\lambda=0$},\\
2q^{d-3},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

2.

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then

|\ell^{\perp}\cap\{x_{d}=\lambda\}\cap S^{d-1}|\leq\begin{cases}2q^{d-2},&\text{if $\lambda=0$},\\
3q^{d-3},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{d})
be the direction vector of
\ell
. Then, we have

\ell^{\perp}=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}y_{i}=0\right\},

so that

\ell^{\perp}\cap\left\{x_{d}=\lambda\right\}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}\colon x_{d}=\lambda,\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}x_{i}y_{i}=-\lambda y_{d}\right\}.

On the other hand, we have

\left\{x_{d}=\lambda\right\}\cap S^{d-1}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}x_{i}^{2}=1-\lambda^{2}\right\}.

Therefore, it is sufficient to count all solutions
\left(x_{1},x_{2}\ldots,x_{d-1}\right)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d-1}
of

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}x_{i}y_{i}=-\lambda y_{d},\\
\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}x_{i}^{2}=1-\lambda^{2}.\end{cases}

We now consider three cases as follows.

Case 1
: There exists
1\leq i_{0}\leq d-1
such that
y_{i_{0}}\neq 0
. With
x_{i_{0}}=y_{i_{0}}^{-1}\left(-\lambda y_{d}-\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}x_{i}y_{i}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}x_{i}^{2}+y_{i_{0}}^{-2}\left(-\lambda y_{d}-\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}x_{i}y_{i}\right)^{2}=1-\lambda^{2}.

(7)

When
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, there exists
i_{1}\neq i_{0}
such that
1+y_{i_{0}}^{-2}y_{i_{1}}^{2}\neq 0
. Therefore, for each choice
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
i\not\in\{i_{1},i_{0}\}
, the above equation becomes a quadratic equation in the variable
x_{i_{1}}
. So that, it has at most two solutions
x_{i_{1}}
. Therefore, the above equation has at most
2q^{d-3}
solutions. So,

|\ell^{\perp}\cap\{x_{d}=\lambda\}\cap S^{d-1}|\leq 2q^{d-3}.

When
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, if there exists
i_{1}\neq i_{0}
such that
1+y_{i_{0}}^{-2}y_{i_{1}}^{2}\neq 0
,
A similar argument implies

|\ell^{\perp}\cap\{x_{d}=\lambda\}\cap S^{d-1}|\leq 2q^{d-3}.

If not, we have
y_{i_{0}}^{-2}y_{i}^{2}=-1
for all
i\neq i_{0}
. Fix
i_{1}\neq i_{0}
, for each choice
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
i\not\in\{i_{0},i_{1}\}
such that

-\lambda y_{d}-\sum_{i\neq i_{1},i_{0}}x_{i}y_{i}\neq 0,

the equation (
7
) becomes a linear equation in
x_{i_{1}}
. Thus, it has at most one solution.

For each choice
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
i\not\in\{i_{0},i_{1}\}
such that

-\lambda y_{d}-\sum_{i\neq i_{1},i_{0}}x_{i}y_{i}=0,

the equation (
7
) has at most
q
solutions
x_{i_{1}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Next, we count the number of solutions of the equation

\displaystyle-\lambda y_{d}-\sum_{i\neq i_{1},i_{0}}x_{i}y_{i}=0.

(8)

Since
y_{0}^{-2}y_{i}^{2}=-1
for all
i\not\in\{i_{0},i_{1}\}
, we have
y_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\not\in\{i_{0},i_{1}\}
. Fix
i_{2}\not\in\{i_{0},i_{1}\}
, for each choice of
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
i\not\in\{i_{0},i_{1},i_{2}\}
, the equation (
8
) becomes a linear equation in variable
x_{i_{2}}
. Hence, it has at most one solution. This implies at most
q^{d-4}
solutions. Therefore, the number of solutions of the equation (
7
) is at most

2(q^{d-3}-q^{d-4})+q^{d-4}\cdot q\leq 3q^{d-3}.

Case 2:
If
y_{1}=\cdots=y_{d-1}=0
, we have

\ell^{\perp}\cap\left\{x_{d}=\lambda\right\}=\begin{cases}\left\{x_{d}=\lambda\right\},&\text{if $\lambda=0$},\\
\emptyset,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Case 2.1
: If
\lambda=0
, then we consider the following equation

\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}x_{i}^{2}=1.

For each choice
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
i\neq 1
, there exist at most two possible solutions of
x_{1}
, so we have at most
2q^{d-2}
solutions at the end. Then,

|\ell^{\perp}\cap\{x_{d}=\lambda\}\cap S^{d-1}|\leq 2q^{d-2}.

Case 2.2
: If
\lambda\neq 0
, then from
\ell^{\perp}\cap\left\{x_{d}=\lambda\right\}=\emptyset
, one has

|\ell^{\perp}\cap\{x_{d}=\lambda\}\cap S^{d-1}|=0.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

For
V\in X_{\pi}
,
let
\{\mathbf{x}_{V_{j}}+V\}_{j=1}^{q^{d-1}}
be the set of disjoint translations of
V\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}.

We have

|E|=\sum_{j=1}^{q^{d-1}}|E\cap(\mathbf{x}_{V_{j}}+V)|.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

|E|^{2}\leq|\pi_{V^{\perp}}(E)|\sum_{j=1}^{q^{d-1}}|E\cap(\mathbf{x}_{V_{j}}+V)|^{2}.

Thus,

|E|^{2}|\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)|\leq\sum_{V\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(X,E,N)}|\pi_{V^{\perp}}(E)|\sum_{j=1}^{q^{d-1}}|E\cap(\mathbf{x}_{V_{j}}+V)|^{2}.

This gives

|E|^{2}|\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)|\leq N\sum_{V\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(X,E,N)}\sum_{j=1}^{q^{d-1}}|E\cap(\mathbf{x}_{V_{j}}+V)|^{2}.

So, by Lemma
2.1
,

|E|^{2}|\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)|\leq Nq^{-d+1}\sum_{V\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(X,E,N)}\sum_{\xi\in V^{\perp}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2}.

The right hand side at
\xi=\mathbf{0}
is at most
Nq^{-d+1}|E|^{2}|\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)|
, which is at most half of the left hand side if
N<\frac{q^{d-1}}{2}
.

Define
M=\max_{\xi\neq 0}\#\{V\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)\colon\xi\in V^{\perp}\}
.

For
\xi\neq\mathbf{0}
, let
\ell_{\xi}
be the line passing through the origin and
\xi
. It is not hard to see that
\xi\in V^{\perp}
if and only if
V\subset\ell_{\xi}^{\perp}
. Indeed, since
k\xi\cdot\mathbf{x}=k\cdot 0=0,\,\forall k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\mathbf{x}\in V
, we have
\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{\xi}^{\perp},\,\forall\mathbf{x}\in V
and so
V\subset\ell_{\xi}^{\perp}
.

By Lemma
8.1
, we know that

|\ell_{\xi}^{\perp}\cap X_{\pi}|\leq\begin{cases}2q^{d-2},&\text{if $\lambda=0$}\\
3q^{d-3},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}.

Thus,

M\leq\begin{cases}2q^{d-2},&\text{if $\lambda=0$},\\
3q^{d-3},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}.

The above inequality can now be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{|E|^{2}|\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)|}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}2Nq^{-d+1}q^{d-2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}\left|\widehat{E}(\xi)\right|^{2},&\text{if $\lambda=0$},\\
3Nq^{-d+1}q^{d-3}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}}|\widehat{E}(\xi)|^{2},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}2Nq^{d-1}|E|,&\text{if $\lambda=0$},\\
3Nq^{d-2}|E|,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence,

|\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)|\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{4Nq^{d-1}}{|E|},&\text{if $\lambda=0$},\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{6Nq^{d-2}}{|E|},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This completes the proof of the theorem.

∎

10
Sharpness examples

We need to keep in mind an observation that if

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|<\frac{|X_{\pi}|}{C},

for some positive
C>1
, then there will be at least
\left(1-\frac{1}{C}\right)|X_{\pi}|
points
\mathbf{y}\in\left(\pi\cap S^{d-1}\right)
such that

\left|\left\{\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{x}\colon\mathbf{x}\in E\right\}\right|
is at least
N
.

Construction 10.1
.

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exists
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with
|E|=q
such that for all
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{\lambda\}
one has
|\mathbf{y}\cdot E|\leq\frac{q+1}{2}
. Indeed, if
\lambda=0
, we can set
E=\{0\}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
\lambda\neq 0
, we can set
E=\{0\}^{d-2}\times\{(t,t^{2})\colon t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
. Using the fact that the number of square elements is at most
\frac{q+1}{2}
, we derive
|\mathbf{y}\cdot E|\leq\frac{q+1}{2}
.

Construction 10.2
.

Let
E=E^{\prime}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, where
E^{\prime}=\left\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\right\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}
with
0<N<\frac{q}{2}
.
For
\lambda=0
, for all
V\in X_{\pi}
we have
(0,\cdots,0,1)\in V^{\perp}
, so
A:=\left\{(0,\cdots,0,t)\colon t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}\subset V^{\perp}
. Therefore,

\left(\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}\right)=\left(\left(\mathbf{x}_{i},0\right)+A\right)\subset\left(\left(\mathbf{x}_{i},0\right)+V^{\perp}\right),\quad\forall\,i=1,2,\ldots,N.

This implies that
E
can be covered by at most
N
translates of
V^{\perp}
.
Then,
\left|\pi_{V}(E)\right|\leq N
, so

\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|=\left|X_{\pi}\right|\sim q^{d-2}.

Other the other hand,
\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x}_{1})=f(\mathbf{x}_{2})=\cdots=f(\mathbf{x}_{N})=q
, so from Theorem
1.5
, one has

\left|X_{\pi}\right|=\left|\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(X_{\pi},E,N\right)\right|\leq\frac{2q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)|E|}\cdot\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}}f(\mathbf{x})=\frac{2q^{d-1}N}{(q-N)qN}\cdot q\leq 4q^{d-2}\sim\left|X_{\pi}\right|.

Construction 10.3
.

Let
E=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times I
where
|I|=\left\lfloor\frac{|E|}{q^{d-1}}\right\rfloor
and
0\in I
.
For
\lambda\neq 0
, we have
V\notin(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\})=(0,\cdots,0,1)^{\perp}
for all
V\in X_{\pi}
.
Therefore,
V\cap\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}\right)=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. This implies any translate of
V
intersects
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
at one point. Hence, for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, there exists
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
such that
(\mathbf{x}+V)=(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+V)
, so
(\mathbf{x}+V)\cap\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}\right)=\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
. Since
\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}\right)\subset E
,
(\mathbf{x}+V)\cap E\neq\emptyset
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and all
V\in X_{\pi}
. That means

\displaystyle\#\left\{\mathbf{x}+V\colon(\mathbf{x}+V)\cap E\neq 0\right\}=q^{d-1},\quad\text{ for all }V\in X_{\pi}.

Thus,
\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)=X_{\pi}
for all
N\geq q^{d-1}
.

Construction 10.4
.

Let
E=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times I
where
|I|=\left\lfloor\frac{|E|}{q^{d-1}}\right\rfloor
and
0\notin I
. For
\lambda=0
, for all
V\in X_{\pi}
, we have
V\in(0,\cdots,0,1)^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
, so
(\mathbf{x}+V)\cap\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}\right)=(\mathbf{x}+V)
if
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
and
\emptyset
if
\mathbf{x}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
. Therefore,
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}
is covered by
\frac{\left|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{0\}\right|}{|V|}=\frac{q^{d-1}}{q}=q^{d-2}
translations of
V
.
Hence, by similar argument, for any
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{t\}
is covered by
q^{d-2}
lines of form
\mathbf{x}+V
. Note that
E
is covered by
|I|
hyperplanes of form
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d-1}\times\{t\}
, so we get

\#\left\{\mathbf{x}+V\colon(\mathbf{x}+V)\cap E\neq 0\right\}=q^{d-2}\cdot|I|=\frac{|E|}{q},\quad\,V\in X_{\pi}.

Then,

\left|\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)\right|=\left|X_{\pi}\right|,\quad\text{ for all }\,\frac{|E|}{q}\leq N\leq q^{d-1}.

From this and note that
\left|X_{\pi}\right|\sim q^{d-2}
(by Lemma
3.1
below), one has

\left|\mathcal{T}_{2}(X_{\pi},E,N)\right|=\left|X_{\pi}\right|\sim q^{d-2}\sim\frac{Nq^{d-1}}{|E|},

when
N\sim\frac{|E|}{q}.

Open questions

Motivated by the local-to-global principle, we find the following questions interesting.

Distribution of distances:

Given a set
E
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Iosevich and Rudnev
[
10
]
proved that if
t\neq 0
, then the number of pairs
(x,y)\in E\times E
such that
||x-y||=t
is at most
\frac{|E|^{2}}{q}+4q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}|E|
. Notice that
||x-y||=t
is equivalent to
x-y\in S^{d-1}
. Thus, from the cover
S^{d-1}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(S^{d-1}\cap\{x_{d}=\lambda\})
. It would be interesting to look at this question from the local perspective.

Restricted families of projections with Salem sets:

The authors of
[
6
]
extended Chen’s theorem to the setting of
(p,s)
-Salem sets, thus it is natural to ask for analogs of Theorems
1.4
–
1.7
in this new setting. Note that the
(p,s)
-Salem definition is quite general, and for any set
E
we can appropriate
p
and
s
such that it is
(p,s)
-Salem.

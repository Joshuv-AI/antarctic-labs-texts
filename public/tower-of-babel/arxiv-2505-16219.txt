Hidden-Charm Tetraquarks in a Mixture Model: Coupled-Channel Analysis with c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} and Hadronic Molecular Components
Kotaro Miyake, Yasuhiro Yamaguchi

Abstract
The nature of the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) and other exotic hadrons has been a subject of extensive investigation since the first observation of the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) in 2003. While various theoretical models have been proposed, including hadronic molecular and compact tetraquark interpretations, some experimental evidence suggests that the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) may be a mixture state of a hadronic molecule and a c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} core. In this work, we perform a systematic study of the hidden-charm tetraquark candidates X ⁡ ( 3860 ) X(3860) , X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) , and Z ⁡ ( 3930 ) Z(3930) using a coupled-channel model that incorporates both c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} states and D ( ∗ ) ​ D ¯ ( ∗ ) D^{(*)}\bar{D}^{(*)} hadronic molecular components. The c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} sector is described based on the constituent quark model predictions for the χ c ​ J ​ ( 2 ​ P ) \chi_{cJ}(2P) ( J = 0 , 1 , 2 J=0,1,2 ) states, while the meson-meson interactions are modeled using pseudoscalar and vector meson exchange potentials. The model parameters are fixed to reproduce the masses of the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) and Z ⁡ ( 3930 ) Z(3930) , and the resulting framework is used to predict the mass and structure of the J P ​ C = 0 + ⁣ + J^{PC}=0^{++} state associated with the X ⁡ ( 3860 ) X(3860) . Our results support the mixture interpretation of these exotic hadrons, exhibiting strong attractions from the transition potential between c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} and D ( ∗ ) ​ D ¯ ( ∗ ) D^{(*)}\bar{D}^{(*)} components. The molecular component is found to dominate in the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) , while the c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} component plays a more prominent role in the X ⁡ ( 3860 ) X(3860) and Z ⁡ ( 3930 ) Z(3930) .

Introduction

Exotic hadrons have garnered significant interest in the fields of nuclear and hadron physics, as they cannot be described within the conventional quark model picture, i.e., baryons as three-quark states and mesons as quark-antiquark pairs. The possibility of such states was already anticipated in the early stages of quark model development
Gell-Mann 1964
;
Zweig 1964a
;
Zweig 1964b
. In recent years, numerous experimental studies have reported candidates for exotic hadrons, particularly those containing charm quarks, such as
XYZ
,
T_{cc}
, and
P_{c}

Brambilla et al. 2020
;
Yamaguchi et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2023
;
Navas et al. 2024

Despite the wealth of new data, the internal structures of these exotic hadrons remain poorly understood. One promising framework is the multiquark picture, where compact configurations such as tetraquarks (four-quark states) and pentaquarks (five-quark states) are studied using few-body calculation techniques. Alternatively, near hadron thresholds, molecular interpretations—where exotic hadrons are regarded as loosely bound states of conventional hadrons
Voloshin and Okun 1976
;
De Rujula et al. 1977
;
Tornqvist 1994
—are often favored over compact multiquark configurations.
Indeed, many observed exotic states are located near relevant thresholds: for example, some

states near the
D\bar{D}^{*}
threshold,

near the
DD^{*}
threshold, and the
P_{c}
states near the
\bar{D}^{(*)}\Sigma_{c}
thresholds. However, the underlying hadron-hadron interactions that are responsible for the formation of such molecular states are still not well understood.
Understanding the structure of exotic hadrons and the nature of hadron-hadron interactions is essential for exploring the nonperturbative dynamics of low-energy Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD).

II.2
Heavy meson chiral Lagrangian

The
V_{\mathrm{OBE}}
is derived from the heavy meson chiral Lagrangian.
Pseudoscalar mesons
D
and vector mesons
D^{*}
in the heavy quark limit (
m_{c}\rightarrow\infty
) are degenerate by heavy quark spin symmetry
Neubert 1994
.
Therefore, we define the heavy meson field
H_{a}^{(Q)}
as

H^{(Q)}_{a}\equiv\frac{1+\not{v}}{2}(P^{*\mu}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}-P_{a}\gamma^{5})

(4)

with pseudoscalar heavy meson field
P=(D^{0},D^{+},D_{s}^{+})
and vector heavy meson field
P^{*}=(D^{*0},D^{*+},D_{s}^{*+})
.
Then
v
is the four-velocity of the heavy quark, and
a
is the flavor index.
The conjugate field
\bar{H}_{a}^{(Q)}
to
H_{a}^{(Q)}
is given by

\bar{H}^{(Q)}_{a}\equiv\gamma^{0}(H^{(Q)}_{a})^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}=(P^{*\mu\dagger}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}+P^{\dagger}_{a}\gamma^{5})\frac{1+\not{v}}{2}.

(5)

The anti-particle field of the heavy meson is defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}H^{(\bar{Q})}_{a}&\equiv C(\mathcal{C}H^{(Q)}_{a}\mathcal{C}^{-1})^{\top}C^{-1}\\
&=(\bar{P}^{*\mu}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}-\bar{P}_{a}\gamma^{5})\frac{1-\not{v}}{2},\end{split}

(6)

\displaystyle\bar{H}^{(\bar{Q})}_{a}\equiv\gamma^{0}(H^{(\bar{Q})}_{a})^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}=\frac{1-\not{v}}{2}(\bar{P}^{*\mu\dagger}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}+\bar{P}^{\dagger}_{a}\gamma^{5}),

(7)

taking charge conjugation.

We construct the interaction Lagrangian involving the light pseudoscalar mesons to satisfy heavy quark spin symmetry, chiral symmetry, and Lorentz symmetry

;
Casalbuoni et al. 1997

\mathcal{L}_{MHH}=ig\Tr{\left[H^{(Q)}_{b}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}A_{ba\mu}\bar{H}^{(Q)}_{a}\right]}+ig\Tr{\left[\bar{H}^{(\bar{Q})}_{a}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}A_{ab\mu}H^{(\bar{Q})}_{b}\right]}.

(8)

Here,
g
is the coupling constant, and
A_{ab\mu}
is the axial current of the pseudoscalar meson which is given by

A_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}{\left(\xi^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}\xi-\xi\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\dagger}\right)},\quad\xi=\exp{\left(\frac{\bm{\pi}\cdot\bm{\tau}}{2f_\pi}\right)},

(9)

where
f_{\pi}\simeq\quantity{93}{MeV}
is the pion decay constant, and

is the pseudoscalar field written as

\frac{\bm{\pi}\cdot\bm{\tau}}{\sqrt{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}&\pi^{+}&K^{+}\\
\pi^{-}&-\frac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}&K^{0}\\
K^{-}&\bar{K}^{0}&-\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{6}}\end{pmatrix}.

(10)

We also construct the interaction Lagrangian with light vector mesons using hidden local symmetry

;
Harada and Yamawaki 2003
;
Casalbuoni et al. 1997

\mathcal{L}_{VHH}=i\beta\Tr{\left[H^{(Q)}_{b}v_{\mu}(V^{\mu}_{ba}-\rho^{\mu}_{ba})\bar{H}^{(Q)}_{a}\right]}+i\lambda\Tr{\left[H^{(Q)}_{b}\sigma_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}_{ba}\bar{H}^{(Q)}_{a}\right]}\\
-i\beta\Tr{\left[\bar{H}^{(\bar{Q})}_{a}v_{\mu}(V^{\mu}_{ab}-\rho^{\mu}_{ab})H^{(\bar{Q})}_{b}\right]}+i\lambda\Tr{\left[\bar{H}^{(\bar{Q})}_{a}\sigma_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}_{ab}H^{(\bar{Q})}_{b}\right]},

(11)

where

\displaystyle V^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}{\left(\xi^{\dagger}\partial^{\mu}\xi+\xi\partial^{\mu}\xi^{\dagger}\right)},

(12)

\displaystyle F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}\rho^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}\rho^{\mu}+[\rho^{\mu},\rho^{\nu}],

(13)

\displaystyle F^{\prime\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}\rho^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}\rho^{\mu}-[\rho^{\mu},\rho^{\nu}],

(14)

\displaystyle\rho^{\mu}=\frac{ig_{v}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\rho^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}&\rho^{+}&K^{*+}\\
\rho^{-}&-\frac{\rho^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}&K^{*0}\\
K^{*-}&\bar{K}^{*0}&\phi\end{pmatrix}^{\mu}

(15)

\displaystyle g_{v}=\frac{m_{\rho}}{\sqrt{2}f_{\pi}},

(16)

and
\beta
and
\lambda

IV
Summary

Exotic hadrons are currently a major topic of research in hadron physics. In particular, hidden-charm tetraquark states
XYZ
have attracted significant attention since the discovery of the
X(3872)
in 2003, and many related states have been reported in experiments.
In this work, we have performed a systematic analysis of the
X(3872)
with
J^{PC}=1^{++}
and its spin partners, the
X(3860)
with
J^{PC}=0^{++}
and the
Z(3930)
with
J^{PC}=2^{++}
, based on a mixture model that incorporates both
c\bar{c}
states and
D^{(*)}\bar{D}^{(*)}
hadronic molecular components. The
c\bar{c}
components are described using the
\chi_{cJ}(2P)
(
J=0,1,2
) states predicted by the constituent quark model.
The meson-meson interactions are modeled using an effective Lagrangian that respects heavy quark spin and chiral symmetries, incorporating both pseudoscalar and vector meson exchange potentials. The model parameters were determined to reproduce the observed masses of
X(3872)
and
Z(3930)
. Using this setup, we predicted a
J^{PC}=0^{++}
bound state corresponding to the
X(3860)
by solving the coupled-channel Schrödinger equation.
The analysis of the resulting wave functions revealed the internal structure of these states. The
X(3872)
is dominated by its molecular component, while the
X(3860)
and
Z(3930)
are primarily composed of the
c\bar{c}
core. In the formation of the bound states, the coupling between the
c\bar{c}
core and the molecular component plays a crucial role.

The present model can be extended to study other exotic hadrons. The analysis of resonant states such as the
X(3915)
and
X(4010)
will be an important direction for future work.

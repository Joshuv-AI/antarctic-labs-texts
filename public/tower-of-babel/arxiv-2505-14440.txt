Abstract
Configuration-Constrained Tube Model Predictive Control (CCTMPC) offers flexibility by using a polytopic parameterization of invariant sets and the optimization of an associated vertex control law. This flexibility, however, often demands computational trade-offs between set parameterization accuracy and optimization complexity. This paper proposes two innovations that help the user tackle this trade-off. First, a structured framework is proposed, which strategically limits optimization degrees of freedom, significantly reducing online computation time while retaining stability guarantees. This framework aligns with Homothetic Tube MPC (HTMPC) under maximal constraints. Second, a template refinement algorithm that iteratively solves quadratic programs is introduced to balance polytope complexity and conservatism. Simulation studies on an illustrative benchmark problem as well as a high-dimensional ten-state system demonstrate the approach’s efficiency, achieving robust performance with minimal computational overhead. The results validate a practical pathway to leveraging CCTMPC’s adaptability without sacrificing real-time viability.

Introduction

Tube Model Predictive Control (TMPC) has proven to be a central tool in robust control due to its ability to enclose all realizations of the state of an uncertain system within a sequence of bounded sets
[
9
,
11
,
18
]
. Among the various set parameterizations, a variety of polytopic set parameterization approaches are particularly attractive, because they permit the online solution of a Quadratic Programming (QP) problem
[
8
]
. Examples for such polytopic schemes include Rigid
[
13
,
15
]
, Homothetic
[
16
]
, and Elastic
[
17
,
3
]
Tube MPC.
While these methods typically enforce set invariance through linear feedback laws, previous works have also explored the use of a freely parameterized control law within the Homothetic framework
[
12
]
.

Departing from conventional parameterization methods, Configuration-Constrained Tube MPC (CCTMPC)
[
20
]
introduces a novel framework centered on selecting polytopic sets with fixed facet orientations that share a predefined geometric structure. This approach optimizes facet displacements while explicitly enumerating the vertices of the polytopes and their corresponding control inputs, inherently supporting systems with multiplicative uncertainty.

Despite its merits, CCTMPC presents dual challenges. First, its formulation inherently involves a larger number of optimization variables—encompassing both control inputs and set geometry—compared to methods that predefine either component. This complexity inevitably grows with system dimensionality. Second, the control scheme’s efficacy hinges critically on the template design, particularly in high-dimensional spaces. Here, achieving a balance between template expressiveness and computational tractability becomes nontrivial, as overly simplistic templates risk conservatism, while intricate ones incur prohibitive online costs.

Contribution:
This paper advances the state-of-the-art in CCTMPC through two novel methods that enable computational efficiency and template design flexibility.

Here, we first introduce a novel family of Tube MPC controllers that systematically reduce computational overhead. By strategically constraining the number of optimization variables, our approach transitions from the full flexibility of CCTMPC to a highly efficient scheme reminiscent of Homothetic Tube MPC (HTMPC). Moreover, we propose an iterative algorithm for designing configuration templates. This algorithm is initialized with a low-complexity template. It progressively enriches the template by solving QPs, systematically increasing the complexity until a user-defined target is achieved.

2
Tube Model Predictive Control

This section briefly reviews the main idea of Tube MPC and introduces the notation used throughout this article.

2.1
Uncertain Linear Systems

This paper is concerned with uncertain discrete-time systems of the form

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=A_{k}x_{k}+B_{k}u_{k}+w_{k},

(1)

where
x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
and
u_{k}\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}
denote the state and input at time
k\in\mathbb{N}
, while
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{U}
are given, closed and convex, state and control constraint sets. Moreover,
w_{k}\in\mathcal{W}
is an additive disturbance, with
\mathcal{W}
assumed to be compact and convex. The matrices
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
are uncertain, but known to satisfy

(A_{k},B_{k})\in\Delta\coloneqq\operatorname{convh}((A_{1},B_{1}),\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,(A_{\mathrm{m}},B_{\mathrm{m}})).

Here, the matrices
(A_{i},B_{i})
are assumed to be given vertices of the matrix polytope
\Delta
that models the uncertainty set.

2.2
Robust Control Tubes

Let
\mathcal{F}(X)
denote the set of sets that can be reached from a given set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}
; that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(X):=\left\{X^{+}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}\ \middle|\ \begin{aligned} &\forall\,x\in X,\ \exists\,u\in\mathcal{U}:\vskip 1.0pt\\
&\forall\,(A,B)\in\Delta,\;w\in\mathcal{W},\vskip 1.0pt\\
&Ax+Bu+w\in X^{+}\end{aligned}\right\}.

We recall that a sequence of sets

X_{0},X_{1},\ldots\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is called a feasible Robust Control Tube (RCT) if

\displaystyle\forall k\in\{0,\dots,N-1\},\quad X_{k+1}\in\mathcal{F}(X_{k}).

(2)

Moreover, a set
X\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is called a feasible Robust Control Invariant (RCI) set if
(X,X)
is an RCT.

2.3
Optimization of RCTs

From a conceptual perspective, the main idea of Tube MPC is to optimize an RCT online. Here, the first element of the RCT is enforced to contain the current state measurement. For example, one can solve optimization problems of the form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{X_{0},\ldots,X_{N}}&L_{0}(X_{0})+\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}L(X_{k})+L_{N}(X_{N})\\
\text{s.t.}&\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\forall k\in\{0,\dots,N-1\},\\
X_{k+1}\in\mathcal{F}(X_{k}),\\
x\in X_{0},\ X_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{X}.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Here,
L_{0}
denotes the initial cost,
L
the stage cost,
L_{N}
the terminal cost, and
x
the current state measurement. Choices for the cost triple
(L_{0},L,L_{N})
that ensure closed-loop stability of such Tube MPC schemes are discussed in
[
19
]
. Alternate formulations may also introduce control-law penalties designed to regularize the control policies, which are hidden in our definition of
\mathcal{F}
; for an in-depth discussion, see
[
8
]
.

3
Polytopic Tube MPC Schemes

As the optimization variables of the generic Tube MPC controller (
2.3
) are set-valued, this problem is in general intractable. Therefore, this section focuses on polytopic set parameterizations.

3.1
Configuration-Constrained Polytopes

Throughout this paper, we use the notation

\displaystyle P(y)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}\mid Fx\leq y\},

(7)

to denote polyhedra with a given facet matrix
F\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{f}\times n_{x}}
. Here, we assume that
Fx\leq 0
implies
x=0
, so that
P(y)
is bounded—and hence potentially empty—for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{f}}
. In the following, we further refer to the cone

\mathcal{E}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{f}}\mid Ey\leq 0\}

as a configuration cone for
P
if the face configuration of the parameterized polytope
P(y)
remains invariant for all
y\in\mathcal{E}
. This means that if
\mathcal{E}
is a configuration cone, we can find a set of matrices
V:=\{V_{j}\mid j\in\{1,\cdots,\mathrm{v}\}\}
such that

\displaystyle P(y)=\mathrm{convh}(\{V_{j}y\mid j\in\{1,\cdots,\mathrm{v}\}\})

(8)

for all
y\in\mathcal{E}
. As already mentioned in the introduction, constructing suitable configuration triples
(F,E,V)
with the above properties is a non-trivial task. One possible method for constructing such triples can nevertheless be found in
[
20
, Sec III.5]
. Note that the implications of freezing such a configuration and the methods for refining configuration triples will be discussed below.

3.2
Configuration-Constrained RCTs

One of the key advantages of working with configuration-constrained polytopic RCTs, for a given configuration triple
(F,E,V)
, is that they can be represented by a convex condition. Namely, by introducing the convex set

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}y\\
u\\
y^{+}\end{pmatrix}\ \middle|\ \begin{aligned} &\forall\,(i,j)\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{m}\}\times\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{v}\},\vskip 1.0pt\\
&F(A_{i}V_{j}y+B_{i}u_{j})+d\leq y^{+},\vskip 1.0pt\\
&Ey\leq 0,\ V_{j}y\in\mathcal{X},\ u_{j}\in\mathcal{U}\end{aligned}\right\},

(9)

with
d_{k}\coloneqq\max\{F_{k}w:w\in\mathcal{W}\}
for all
k\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathsf{f}\}
and with
u_{j}\in\mathcal{U}
denoting the
j
-th vertex control input, the following statement holds.

Proposition 1
.

Let
y_{0},y_{1},\ldots\in\mathcal{E}
be a given sequence of parameters. Then
P(y_{0}),P(y_{1}),\ldots
is a feasible RCT if and only if there exists a sequence of inputs
u_{0},u_{1},\ldots\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{v}\cdot n_{u}}
such that
(y_{k},u_{k},y_{k+1})\in\mathbb{S}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof:
The statement follows by combining the results from
[
20
, Corollary 4]
and
[
7
, Prop. 1]
.∎

Corollary 1
.

A polytope
P(y)
with
y\in\mathcal{E}
is an RCI set if and only if there exists a
u\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{v}\cdot n_{u}}
such that
(y,u,y)\in\mathbb{S}
.

Proof:
The result follows from Proposition
1
and the definition of RCI sets.∎

3.3
Optimal Configuration-Constrained RCIs

If
\ell(\cdot,\cdot)
is a given convex stage cost, one can use the result from Corollary
1
to compute optimal configuration-constrained RCI polytopes
P(y_{m})
. Here,
y_{m}\in\mathcal{E}
is computed by solving the convex optimization problem

\displaystyle(y_{m},u_{m})\in\arg\min_{y,u}

\displaystyle\ell(y,u)

(10)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle(y,u,y)\in\mathbb{S}.

If it is possible to find an explicit expression for the stage cost
L
in (
2.3
) such that
\ell(y,u)=L(P(y))
, then
\ell
does not depend on
u
. In many practical cases, however, one is interested in refining the objective by introducing a suitable control penalty; thus,
\ell
is often modeled as a strongly convex quadratic function, cf.
[
2
, Section 2.D]
, so that the set of optimizers is a singleton.

3.4
Fully-parameterized CCTMPC for Tracking

One way of implementing Tube MPC is to compute a sequence of parameters
((y_{0},u_{0}),(y_{1},u_{1}),\cdots)
that converges to the optimal RCI set parameters
(y_{m},u_{m})
. In the simplest case, this can be achieved by minimizing the quadratic deviation
\|(y-y_{m},u-u_{m})\|_{Q}^{2}
. For example, one can solve the strictly convex QPs

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}y_{k}-y_{m}\\
u_{k}-u_{m}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{Q}^{2}+\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}y_{N}-y_{m}\\
u_{N}-u_{m}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{R}^{2}

(11)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\forall k\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\},\\
(y_{k},u_{k},y_{k+1})\in\mathbb{S},\\
Fx\leq y_{0},\\
(y_{N},u_{N},\gamma y_{N}+(1-\gamma)y_{m})\in\mathbb{S}\,,\end{array}\right.

online, where
\mathbf{y}=(y_{0},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,y_{N})
and
\mathbf{u}=(u_{0},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,u_{N})
stack the sequence of tube parameters and vertex control inputs, respectively. Here,
Q
and
R
are symmetric positive definite matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{(\mathsf{f}+\mathrm{v}\cdot n_{u})\times(\mathsf{f}+\mathrm{v}\cdot n_{u})}
. The parameter
\gamma\in(0,1)
is user-specified, and is useful in ensuring recursive feasibility and stability of the control scheme
[
2
]
. Note that Problem (
11
) is parametric in the current state measurement
x
. We denote the unique parametric minimizers of (
11
) by
(\mathbf{y}^{*}(x),\mathbf{u}^{*}(x))
, and define the control law as

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{c}}(x):=\arg

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\ u^{\top}u

(12)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\forall i\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{m}\},\\
F(A_{i}x+B_{i}u)+d\leq y^{*}_{1}(x).\end{array}\right.

Due to Proposition
1
, Problem (
12
) is feasible whenever Problem (
11
) is feasible, ensuring that the minimizer
(\mathbf{y}^{*}(x),\mathbf{u}^{*}(x))
exists. The complete closed-loop system is then given by

\displaystyle\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\qquad x_{k+1}=A_{k}x_{k}+B_{k}\mu_{\mathrm{c}}(x_{k})+w_{k}.

(13)

We recall the following result from
[
2
, Corollary 1]
regarding the theoretical properties of the controller.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
Q\succ 0
and
R\succ 0
be given. If
Q+\gamma^{2}R\preceq R
, then the following statements hold independently of the uncertainty realization,

, and
(A_{k},B_{k})\in\Delta
, for all

:

1.

If Problem (
11
) is feasible with

, then it is feasible for
x=x_{k}
, where
x_{k}
is the state at time instant

of System (
13
), for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.

After defining the function

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u}):=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}y_{k}-y_{m}\\
u_{k}-u_{m}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{Q}^{2}+\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}y_{N}-y_{m}\\
u_{N}-u_{m}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{R}^{2},

the Lyapunov inequality

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}^{*}(x_{k+1}),\mathbf{u}^{*}(x_{k+1}))\leq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}^{*}(x_{k}),\mathbf{u}^{*}(x_{k}))

holds, where equality holds only at

. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\underset{k\to\infty}{\lim}(\mathbf{y}^{*}(x_{k}),\mathbf{u}^{*}(x_{k}))=((y_{m},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,y_{m}),(u_{m},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,u_{m})).

3.

The distance between the state in (
13
) and the optimal RCI set converges to
0
; that is,
\underset{k\to\infty}{\lim}x_{k}\in P(y_{m})
.

Proof.

The proof follows by applying
[
2
, Corollary 1]
to the case of fixed optimal RCI set parameters.
∎

While the TMPC scheme based on (
11
) exhibits favorable theoretical properties as described in Proposition

, solving (

) online can be computationally demanding. For example, if
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{U}
are polyhedra represented by

and

denotes the number of facets,

the number of vertices, and

the number of control inputs.

3.5
Homothetic CCTMPC for Tracking

One path to reducing the number of optimization variables in the above Tube MPC formulation is to work with a homothetic transformation. To outline the main idea, the following standard assumption is introduced (see also
[
16
]
).

Assumption 1
.

The optimal RCI set satisfies the condition

\displaystyle(0,0)\in P(y_{m})\times\mathrm{convh}(\{u_{m,j}\in\mathcal{U}\mid j\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{v}\}\})

.

Next, similar to
[
16
]
, we introduce a scaling factor
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and offset vectors
(z,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}+n_{u}}
, such that

\displaystyle y=\alpha y_{m}+Fz,\quad u_{j}=\alpha u_{m,j}+v,\ j\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{v}\}.

(14)

We recall from
[
20
]
that the construction of the matrix
E
and the associated configuration cone
\mathcal{E}
imply

\displaystyle y_{m}\in\mathcal{E}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha y_{m}+Fz\in\mathcal{E},\quad\forall\alpha\geq 0,\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}.

(15)

Thus,
P(\alpha y_{m}+Fz)
is a configuration-constrained polytope for all
\alpha\geq 0
. To enforce the state and input constraints, we assume that the constraint sets are modeled by polytopes, namely,
\mathcal{X}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}\mid H^{x}x\leq h^{x}\}
and
\mathcal{U}:=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}}\mid H^{u}u\leq h^{u}\}
. Next, we define

\displaystyle h^{x}_{m}

\displaystyle:=\max\{H^{x}V_{j}y_{m}\mid j\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{v}\}\},

\displaystyle h^{u}_{m}

\displaystyle:=\max\{H^{u}u_{m,j}\mid j\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{v}\}\},

with the

taken row-wise. It follows that the inclusions
V_{j}(\alpha y_{m}+Fz)\in\mathcal{X}
and
\alpha u_{m,j}+v\in\mathcal{U}
hold for all vertices

if and only if

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}H^{x}&0\\
0&H^{u}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}z\\
v\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}h^{x}_{m}\\
h^{u}_{m}\end{bmatrix}\alpha\leq\begin{bmatrix}h^{x}\\
h^{u}\end{bmatrix}.

Finally, we define the set
\bar{\mathbb{S}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n_{x}+n_{u}+2}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{\bar{S}}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}(z,\alpha)\\
v\\
(z^{+}\!,\alpha^{+})\end{pmatrix}\middle|\,\begin{aligned} &\alpha\geq 0,\quad\eqref{eq:HTMPC_constraints},\quad\forall\,i\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{m}\},\vskip 1.0pt\\
&\begin{multlined}F(A_{i}z+B_{i}v)+(1\!-\!\alpha)d+\alpha y_{m}\\
\leq\alpha^{+}y_{m}+Fz^{+},\end{multlined}\end{aligned}\right\}\!.

Corollary 2
.

Let
(z_{k},\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be such that for all

there exist inputs

satisfying

. Then, the sequence
(P(\alpha_{k}y_{m}+Fz_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an RCT.

Proof.

The state, input, and configuration constraints for the RCT follow from (
16
) and (
15
). Substituting the parameterization in (

) into the set
\mathbb{S}
and applying Proposition

(\mathbf{z},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathbf{v})
, linear equality constraints
y_{k}=\alpha_{k}y_{m}+Fz_{k}
and
u_{k}=\alpha_{k}u_{m}+\tilde{\mathbb{I}}\,v_{k}
, and with cost matrices given by
Q=\|\tilde{F}^{\dagger}\|^{2}_{\bar{Q}}
and
R=\|\tilde{F}^{\dagger}\|^{2}_{\bar{R}}
, where
\tilde{F}^{\dagger}
is well-defined since
\tilde{F}
is full column-rank. Recursive feasibility follows by exploiting the convexity of the set
\bar{\mathbb{S}}
and the linear equality constraints, and Lyapunov stability follows from the fact that
Q
and
R
are positive definite on the nullspace of the equality constraints, together with the inequality
\bar{Q}+\gamma^{2}\,\bar{R}\preceq\bar{R}
implying
Q+\gamma^{2}R\preceq R
.
∎

We remark that Problem (
17
) is computationally less demanding to solve online than Problem (
11
), since it has
(N+1)\times(n_{x}+n_{u}+1)
optimization variables and
(N+1)\times(\mathsf{f}\mathrm{m}+1+m_{\mathcal{X}}+m_{\mathcal{U}})
constraints.

3.6
A family of CCTMPC schemes

The schemes presented in (
11
) and (
17
) represent the two extremes of leveraging configuration-constrained polytopes for tube-based model predictive control (TMPC) synthesis. While (
17
) is based on a restriction of the parameters
(y,u)
in terms of
(y_{m},u_{m})
via (
14
), alternative formulations of CCTMPC can be derived by adopting more flexible parameterization strategies. These intermediate approaches aim to achieve a superior trade-off between computational efficiency and control conservatism for practical applications.

To demonstrate this flexibility, we propose two such intermediate schemes. For both formulations, admissible control sets equivalent to
\mathbb{S}
are systematically constructed by substituting the proposed
(y,u)
-parameterizations into the synthesis framework, while explicitly leveraging the inherent inclusion
(y_{m},u_{m},y_{m})\in\mathbb{S}
to ensure recursive feasibility.

3.6.1
Partially-parameterized CCTMPC

In this scheme, we parameterize some vertices of
P(y)
as homothetic transformations of the corresponding vertices of
P(y_{m})
, while allowing the remaining vertices to be freely optimized. Let
J_{\mathrm{p}}\subseteq\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{v}\}
be the set of indices corresponding to the vertices of
P(y)
that we want to parameterize as

\displaystyle V_{j}y=\alpha V_{j}y_{m}+z,\quad\forall j\in J_{\mathrm{p}}.

(21)

For each
j
, denote the indices of the halfspaces intersecting at the vertex
V_{j}y
by
i(j)\subset\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathsf{f}\}
. To achieve (
21
), we define the index set
I_{\mathrm{p}}:=\cup_{j\in J_{\mathrm{p}}}i(j)
. Additionally, we use the symbol
e_{i}
to denote the
i
-th column of the identity matrix
\mathbb{I}_{\mathsf{f}}
, thus introducing the matrix

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{p}}=\sum_{i\in I_{\mathrm{p}}}e_{i}e_{i}^{\top}.

Furthermore, we define the set
I_{\mathrm{f}}:=\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathsf{f}\}\setminus I_{\mathrm{p}}
. Then, the polytope
P(\alpha S_{\mathrm{p}}y_{m}+Fz+S_{\mathrm{f}}\hat{y})
satisfies (
21
) if
\alpha S_{\mathrm{p}}y_{m}+Fz+S_{\mathrm{f}}\hat{y}\in\mathcal{E}
. For consistency with (
21
), we parameterize the vertex inputs for
j\in J_{\mathrm{p}}
as
u_{j}=u_{m,j}+v
, while the inputs corresponding to the remaining vertices are freely optimized.

Denoting the cardinality of
J_{\mathrm{p}}
and
I_{\mathrm{f}}
as
\hat{\mathrm{v}}
and
\hat{\mathsf{f}}
respectively, the Partially-parameterized CCTMPC scheme is then composed of
(N+1)\times(\hat{\mathsf{f}}+n_{x}+n_{u}+1+(\mathrm{v}-\hat{\mathrm{v}})n_{u})
optimization variables, with
\hat{\mathrm{v}}>0
. Note that if
\hat{\mathrm{v}}=\mathrm{v}
, then by construction
\hat{\mathsf{f}}=0
and the Partially-parameterized CCTMPC scheme reduces to the Homothetic CCTMPC scheme. Conversely, for
\hat{\mathrm{v}}=0
, the scheme retrieves the Fully-parameterized CCTMPC formulation. The number of linear inequality constraints is the same as that of the Fully-parameterized CCTMPC scheme for any
\hat{\mathrm{v}}\in[0,\mathrm{v})
, while it reduces to that of Homothetic CCTMPC if
\hat{\mathrm{v}}=\mathrm{v}
. In practice, the index set
J_{\mathrm{p}}
should be chosen so that the controller’s stabilizable region includes the desired system’s initial states. Developing a systematic method for selecting
J_{\mathrm{p}}
based on a given set of initial conditions is a topic for future research.

3.6.2
Homothetic CCTMPC with optimized controls

Inspired by the approach of
[
12
]
, we parameterize
y=\alpha y_{m}+Fz
, while allowing the vertex control inputs to be freely optimized. This results in a scheme defined by
(N+1)\times(n_{x}+1+\mathrm{v}\cdot n_{u})
optimization variables and
(N+1)\times(\mathsf{f}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{v}+1+m_{\mathcal{X}}+\mathrm{v}m_{\mathcal{U}})
linear inequality constraints, with a stabilizable region that lies between the Homothetic CCTMPC and the Fully-parameterized CCTMPC schemes.

4
Computing Template Polytopes

This section presents a computational procedure for the construction of configuration triples that can be used in TMPC synthesis. In particular, the focus is on iteratively refining a configuration triple
(F,E,V)
such that, at each iteration,
\mathbb{S}
(or
\bar{\mathbb{S}}
) remains nonempty, the number of vertices of the new polytope increases by a known, fixed amount, and the size of the resulting RCI set increases.

4.1
Iterative Refinement of the Configuration Triple

Suppose a configuration triple
(F,E,V)
is given such that
\mathbb{S}
is nonempty. Let
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{convh}(\{\xi_{1},\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\xi_{\rm s}\})
, and consider the optimization
problem

\displaystyle\sigma\coloneqq\min_{y_{M}\geq 0,u,z}

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=1}^{v_{\mathcal{X}}}\|\xi_{i}-z_{i}\|_{2}^{2}

(22)

s.t.

\displaystyle\ (y_{M},u,y_{M})\in\mathbb{S},\ Fz_{i}\leq y_{M},\ i\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,{\rm s}\}\,.

Notice that, although we do not report the dependencies, the above problem is parametric in the configuration triple. Let
(y_{M}^{\star},u^{\star},z^{\star})
be a minimizer of (
22
). Then, the RCI polytope
\{x\in\mathcal{X}\ |\ Fx\leq y_{M}^{\star}\}
contains points
z^{\star}_{1},\ldots,z^{\star}_{\rm s}
that have minimal aggregated distance to the vertices of
\mathcal{X}
.

4.2
Computing Initial Matrix
F

We present a nonlinear program (NLP) inspired by
[
14
,
5
]
to compute an initial feasible configuration triple for Algorithm
1
. In particular, given a polytope

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}\mid Fx\leq 1\}=\operatorname{convh}(\{V_{1}1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,V_{\mathrm{v}}1\}),

the procedure computes an invertible matrix
T\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}\times n_{x}}
such that
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{x}}\mid FT^{-1}x\leq 1\}

is an RCI polytope with vertices
\{TV_{1}1,\ldots,TV_{\mathrm{v}}1\}
.

This NLP is formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{T,u,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\top}\varepsilon

(23)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\forall(i,j,k)\in\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{m}\}\!\times\!\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathrm{v}\}\!\times\!\{1,\kern-0.50003pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern-0.09995pt.\kern 0.09995pt,\mathsf{w}\},\\
&FT^{-1}(A_{i}Tz_{j}+B_{i}u_{j}+w_{k})\leq 1,\\
&Tz_{j}\in\mathcal{X},\ u_{j}\in\mathcal{U},\\
&\mathcal{X}\subseteq\{x\mid FT^{-1}x\leq 1+\varepsilon\}.\end{aligned}\right.

with
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{\mathsf{w}}\}
denoting the vertices of
\mathcal{W}
.

The inclusion
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\{x\mid FT^{-1}x\leq 1+\varepsilon\}
in (
23
) can be formulated, using duality, as

\displaystyle\Lambda h^{x}\leq 1+\varepsilon,\ \Lambda H^{x}=FT^{-1},\ \Lambda\geq 0,

with
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathsf{f}\times\mathsf{w}}
being an additional optimization variable.

Notice that the face configuration (and thus also the cone
\mathcal{E}
) of the original polytope is preserved since
T
defines an invertible linear transformation (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Section 2.6]
and
[
4
, Section 1.1]
).

6
Conclusions

This paper advances the recently introduced CCTMPC framework for synthesizing TMPC schemes by
(i)
presenting a family of RCT parameterizations and corresponding control schemes that strategically trade off between computational complexity and conservativeness, and
(ii)
developing an algorithm to systematically synthesize template polytopes of user-specified complexity for CCTMPC synthesis. The benefits of these advances were demonstrated through numerical examples, paving the way for applying CCTMPC schemes to the design of robust controllers for relatively high-dimensional systems such as the
10
-state quadrotor benchmark illustrated in this article. Future work will focus on further refining Algorithm
1
and developing an efficient software tool to synthesize template polytopes that exploit the properties of configuration constraints.

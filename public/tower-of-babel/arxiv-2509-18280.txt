An Integral Springer Correspondence
Thiago Landim

Abstract
We prove an integral version of the derived Springer correspondence for reduced motives. To achieve this result, we extend some results on reduced motives from schemes to quotient stacks with a finite number of orbits. More generally, we work in the context of the Springer setup, as defined by Eberhardt and Stroppel, which has also applications for quiver Hecke algebras.



Introduction

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a reductive group with Weyl group
\mathrm{W}
, let
\mathscr{N}_{\mathrm{G}}
be its nilpotent cone. The Steinberg variety
\mathscr{Z}\coloneqq\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}_{\mathrm{G}}\times_{\mathscr{N}_{\mathrm{G}}}\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}_{\mathrm{G}}
is given by the fiber product of the Springer resolution with itself. The Springer correspondence, discovered by Springer in
[
Spr76
]
, classically gives a correspondence between irreducible
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-representations of
\mathrm{W}
and some irreducible
\ell
-adic perverse sheaves on
\mathscr{N}_{\mathrm{G}}
.

Over time, three directions to generalize such a correspondence were researched. First of all, Lusztig found in
[
Lus84
]
what is now called the generalized Springer correspondence, which para-metrizes
all

\mathrm{G}
-equivariant irreducible perverse sheaves on
\mathscr{N}_{\mathrm{G}}
using the
relative
Weyl groups of
\mathrm{G}
. Secondly, Achar et al. in
[
AHJR14
]
proved that the original correspondence existed already with coefficients being a commutative Noetherian ring

1

Springer had already noticed in
[
Spr78
]
that one may use classical cohomology to find the correspondence over
\mathbb{Q}
.
(of finite global dimension). Finally, Rider in
[
Rid13
]
proved a derived version of the classical Springer correspondence.

Recently, Eberhardt and Stroppel in
[
Ebe21
]
and
[
ES22a
]
developed a motivic Springer correspondence with rational coefficients. This extends Rider’s work, and shows, in particular, that the derived correspondence is independent of the prime
\ell
.

Eberhardt’s and Stroppel’s approach uses ideas developed by Soergel, Virk and Wendt in
[
SW18
]
and
[
SVW18
]
which lays down the foundation of equivariant motives with rational coefficients. Their computation relies on the fact that the higher
\mathrm{K}
-theory of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is all torsion and are therefore trivialized by taking rational coefficients.

In a different direction, Eberhardt and Scholbach in
[
ES23
]
defined the notion of a reduced motive. For a finite type scheme
\mathrm{X}
over a base
\mathrm{S}
, the category of reduced motives of
\mathrm{X}
are obtained from the category of motives of
\mathrm{X}
after killing the information coming from the motivic cohomology of the base
\mathrm{S}
. In particular, by the Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence, this should allow one to work as if the higher
\mathrm{K}
-theory of the base
\mathrm{S}
was trivial. We are going to build on Eberhardt’s and Stroppel’s ideas in the context of reduced motives.

To prove the integral Springer correspondence, we extend the whole Springer setup. In particular, this allows us to apply the same formalism to quiver Hecke algebras and quiver Schur algebras, as in
[
ES22a
]
. Before stating the results, we will fix some notation.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field, let
\mathrm{G}
be a linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{k}
, let
\mu_{i}\colon\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}_{i}\to\mathscr{N}
be a collection of
\mathrm{G}
-equivariant proper morphisms between quasi-projective varieties with
\mathrm{G}
-actions, let
\Lambda
be a commutative ring of coefficients and consider, as in
[

]
, the

\mathrm{E}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigoplus_{i,j}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}(\mathscr{N}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(\mu_{i!}\Lambda,\mu_{j!}(\Lambda)[2n](n)).

For schemes, it is a classical result that such
\operatorname{Hom}
-groups may be computed as Chow groups of the fiber product. For algebraic stacks over a field, this is done, for example, by Aranha and Chowdhury in
[
AC23
, Theorem 5.2]
. Denoting
\mathscr{Z}_{i,j}\coloneqq\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}_{i}\times_{\mathscr{N}}\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}_{j}
, one may describe the algebra

as

\mathrm{E}\cong\bigoplus_{i,j}\operatorname{CH}_{\bullet-d_{j}}^{\mathrm{G}}(\mathscr{Z}_{i,j})_{\Lambda},

where

is the dimension of
\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}_{j}
.

Under this setup, one defines the category
\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{Spr}}(\mathscr{N}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)
of
reduced Springer motives
as the thick subcategory of
\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathscr{N}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)
generated by Tate twists of the objects
\mu_{i!}\Lambda
. After introducing purity, finiteness and niceness conditions, which we call (PT), (FO) and (RG) (see §2.1), we prove the following.

Theorem A

(Theorem
2.9
)
.

Under the hypotheses (PT), (FO) and (RG), there exists an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{Spr}}(\mathscr{N}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)\cong\mathrm{grPerf}(\mathrm{E}),

where
\mathrm{grPerf}(\mathrm{E})
denotes the category of graded perfect complexes over
E
. If
\mathbb{k}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
\Lambda=\mathbb{Q}
, this equivalence is the same as in
[
ES22a
, Theorem 4.9]
.

As promised, we apply the formalism above in the context of a single map, the Springer resolution
\mu\colon\widetilde{\mathscr{N}}_{\mathrm{G}}\to\mathscr{N}_{\mathrm{G}}

\mathbb{k}
be an algebraically closed field with characteristic exponent

be a reductive group over

Under rather mild hypotheses on

Since we are taking smooth-Nisnevich covers, we have by (4):

\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)\cong\lim\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathrm{X}_{n},\Lambda),

in other words,
M
is the limit of
M_{n}
. Taking the limit of the distinguished triangles and using base change, one sees that the sequence

j_{!}j^{*}M\to M\to i_{!}i^{*}M

is a distinguished triangle.

To prove property (10), recall that
\pi^{*}
is fully faithful if and only if the unit
\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{X}}\to\pi_{*}\pi^{*}
is an isomorphism. Let
\mathrm{X}\to\mathscr{X}
be a smooth-Nisnevich cover. After pulling back to
\mathrm{X}
, the unit becomes
\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{X}}\to\pi_{\mathrm{X}*}\pi_{\mathrm{X}}^{*}
which is an isomorphism, since one already knows
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance for schemes. Since pullback with respect to a smooth-Nisnevich cover is conservative, the unit
\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{X}}\to\pi_{*}\pi^{*}
is an isomorphism, concluding our proof.
∎

As explained in the proof, to compute the category
\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)
, one first has to choose a smooth-Nisnevich cover
\mathrm{X}\to\mathscr{X}
. In this case, one has

\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)=\lim\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathrm{X}^{n/\mathscr{X}},\Lambda).

Remark 1.11
.

One may ask if extending the motives to stacks before or after taking the reduction is the same thing. In other words, if

\left(\lim\operatorname{DM}(\mathrm{X}^{n/\mathscr{X}},\Lambda)\right)\otimes_{\operatorname{DTM}(\mathrm{S},\Lambda)}\mathrm{grMod}_{\Lambda}\cong\lim(\operatorname{DM}(\mathrm{X}^{n/\mathscr{X}},\Lambda)\otimes_{\operatorname{DTM}(\mathrm{S},\Lambda)}\mathrm{grMod}_{\Lambda}).

Since we are taking pullback with respect to smooth morphisms and those admit left adjoint, both limits above are equivalent to the colimits of their left adjoint (where one might use
[
ES23
, Proposition 2.1]
to ensure adjunction after taking the tensor product) and therefore they commute with tensor product.

Even though they exist for any algebraic stack, smooth-Nisnevich covers are not as abundant as one might hope for. In general, the quotient map
\mathrm{S}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
is not necessarily a smooth-Nisnevich cover. For this reason, we will mainly restrict ourselves to the case of reductive groups. In this case, we are going to begin studying the torus and the Borel, and then studying the reductive group itself. To get a better understanding of smooth-Nisnevich covers, we will state a lemma which slighly generalizes
[
DH23
, Example A.3]
.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{G}
be an inclusion between two smooth
\mathrm{S}
-groups such that the induced map
\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathrm{G}^{\prime})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathrm{G})
is surjective for every morphism
\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K}\to\mathrm{S}
with
\mathrm{K}
a field, then the map
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}^{\prime}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
is a representable smooth-Nisnevich cover.

Proof.

A map from
\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K}
to
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
corresponds to a
\mathrm{G}
-torsor
\mathscr{G}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K}
. Since the the map on
\mathrm{H}^{1}
is surjective, there exists a
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}
-torsor
\mathscr{G}^{\prime}\to\mathrm{K}
such that
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}^{\prime}\times^{\mathrm{G}^{\prime}}\mathrm{G}
, but this means that the morphism lifts to a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}^{\prime}
.
∎

Example 1.13
.

By Hilbert’s 90th theorem, the quotient map
\mathrm{X}\to[\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{GL}_{n,\mathrm{S}}]
is a smooth-Nisnevich cover. This gives, in particular, an easy way to compute the sheaves on the classifying space of a split torus
\mathrm{T}
.

Example 1.14
.

For the same reason, the map
\mathrm{X}\to[\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{SL}_{n,\mathrm{S}}]
is also a smooth-Nisnevich cover.

Example 1.15
.

The quotient map
\mathrm{X}\to[\mathrm{X}/\mathbb{G}_{a,\mathrm{S}}]
is a smooth-Nisnevich cover. The same is also true for any group which is an extension of copies of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
, in other words, for any split unipotent group
\mathrm{U}
.

Proposition 1.16
.

Let
\mathrm{B}
be a split solvable group, then
\mathrm{S}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}
is a smooth-Nisnevich cover.

Proof.

For any field
\mathrm{K}
, one has
\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathbb{G}_{m})=0
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathbb{G}_{a})=0
. Since
\mathrm{B}
admits a filtration whose graded pieces are exactly either
\mathbb{G}_{m}
or
\mathbb{G}_{a}
, then
\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathrm{B})=0
for any field
\mathrm{K}
. This follows, then, from Lemma
1.12
.
∎

Proposition 1.17
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a reductive group with maximal torus
\mathrm{T}
. Then the map
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathrm{T})\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
is a smooth-Nisnevich cover.

Proof.

In the proof of
[
CGR06
, Proposition 2.3]
, it is shown that the map

\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathrm{T}))\to\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathrm{G})

is surjective for any field
\mathrm{K}
, and therefore the result follows from Lemma
1.12
.
∎

Remark 1.18
.

In
[
CGR06
]
, it is more generally proven that for any field
\mathrm{K}
, there exists a finite
\mathrm{K}
-group
\mathrm{N}
contained in
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathrm{T})
such that
\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathrm{N})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{K})_{\mathrm{et}},\mathrm{G})
is surjective. If we want to work over a more general base
\mathrm{S}
, one must argue how one may glue such groups to form an
\mathrm{S}
-group
\mathrm{N}
. If
\mathrm{S}
is a scheme over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{\infty}]
, then the group
\mathrm{N}
they found is actually constant
[
CGR06
, 3.1. Remark]
, and therefore in this case one has a Nisnevich-smooth cover
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{N}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
where
\mathrm{N}
is a constant finite group.

We conclude this section with a result which is an analogue to homotopy invariance.

Lemma 1.19
.

Let
\mathrm{U}
be an
\mathrm{S}
-group which is isomorphic as a scheme to a vector bundle and let
f\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
be a
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{U}
-torsor between algebraic stacks, then

f^{*}\colon\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{Y},\Lambda)\to\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)

is fully faithful.

Proof.

By construction, there exists an NL-cover
\pi\colon\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{Y}
such that the pullback
f^{\prime}\colon\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}
is a trivial
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{U}
-torsor and, thus, there exists a section
\sigma\colon\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}\to\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
which is a
\mathrm{U}
-torsor. In other words, the map
\sigma
is a torsor over a vector bundle and therefore
f^{\prime*}\colon\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{Y}^{\prime},\Lambda)\to\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X}^{\prime},\Lambda)
is fully faithful. More generally, for any
n\geq 1
, the pullback
f_{n}\colon\mathscr{X}^{\prime n/\mathscr{X}}\to\mathscr{Y}^{\prime n/\mathscr{Y}}
is a trivial
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{U}
-torsor and there is a similar
\mathrm{U}
-torsor section
\sigma_{n}\colon\mathscr{Y}^{\prime n/\mathscr{Y}}\to\mathscr{X}^{\prime n/\mathscr{X}}
which implies that the pullback
f_{n}^{*}
is fully faithful. Since

\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)=\lim_{n}\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X}^{\prime n/\mathscr{X}},\Lambda)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{Y},\Lambda)=\lim_{n}\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{Y}^{\prime n/\mathscr{Y}},\Lambda)

and since limits commute with mapping spaces, the pullback
f^{*}
is fully faithful.
∎

1.3
Motives on classifying stacks

In this subsection, we will describe the category of Tate motives on a classifying space. A similar computation was done by Eberhardt in
[
Ebe24
, Proposition 3.2]
. Even though his argument is done over
\mathbb{Z}
, it works exclusively for
\mathrm{K}
-motives. This section is a corollary of Krishna’s work on higher Chow groups in
[
Kri13
]
. His computations on Chow groups may be understood as computations on
\operatorname{Hom}
-groups in the category of motives
\operatorname{DM}
. The computations for
\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}
are similar.

Definition 1.20
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a split reductive group over
\mathrm{S}
and
\mathrm{B}
be a Borel subgroup. The torsion number
t_{\mathrm{G}}\in\mathbb{Z}
is the smallest positive integer such that there exists an element
a\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B})}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}[2n](n))
for which
p_{*}(a)=t_{\mathrm{G}}
in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G})}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
, where
n
is the codimension of
\mathrm{B}
if
\mathrm{G}
and
p\colon\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
is the map induced by the inclusion
\mathrm{B}\subseteq\mathrm{G}
.

Remark 1.21
.

Similarly, there exists an element
a^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B})}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}[2n](n))
such that
p_{*}(a^{\prime})=t_{\mathrm{G}}
. Indeed, one may take
a^{\prime}=\mathrm{r}(a)
where
a
is given by Definition
1.20
.

Suppose from now on in this subsection that
\Lambda
is a
\mathbb{Z}[t_{\mathrm{G}}^{-1}]
-algebra.

Theorem 1.22
.

Suppose
\mathrm{S}
is the spectrum of a perfect field and let
\mathrm{G}
be a split reductive group over
\mathrm{S}
with split maximal torus
\mathrm{T}
. Then the pullback

q^{*}\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n](n+i))\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n](n+i))^{\mathrm{W}}

is an isomorphism for any
n,\,i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

This follows from the same computations as in
[
Kri13
, Theorem 5.7]
.
∎

Corollary 1.23
.

Suppose
\mathrm{S}
is the spectrum of an algebraically closed field and let
\mathrm{G}
be a linear algebraic group and let
n,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
\lvert\pi_{0}(\mathrm{G})\rvert
is invertible in
\Lambda
, then the reduction functor gives an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(\Lambda[2m](m),\Lambda[2n](n))\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(\mathrm{r}(\Lambda)[2m](m),\mathrm{r}(\Lambda)[2n](n)).

for any
n,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{G}
is a split torus, the pullback with respect to the atlas
\pi\colon\mathrm{S}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
is conservative, and therefore the proposition follows from the fact that a left adjoint is fully faithful if and only if the unit is an isomorphism.

If
\mathrm{G}
is a connected split reductive group, then the pullback with respect to the natural map
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(\Lambda[2m](m),\Lambda[2n](n))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T},\Lambda)}(\Lambda[2m](m),\Lambda[2n](n))^{\mathrm{W}}.

Since the Demazure operators, as defined in
[
Kri13
, §5]
, are defined using the six operations, they commute with the reduction functor. Since they also generate the Weyl group, this means the result for a connected split reductive group
G
reduces to the one on the torus.

If
\mathrm{G}
is a possibly disconnected reductive group, then the result follows from
[
SVW18
, Theorem A.2.8]
applied to the natural
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{G})
-torsor
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}^{\circ}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
.

If
\mathrm{G}
is not necessarily reductive, then
\mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{red}}\coloneqq\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{u}}(\mathrm{G})
is reductive and
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{u}}(\mathrm{G})
is isomorphic to an affine space. Thus the map
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}_{\mathrm{red}}
is a
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{u(\mathrm{G})}}
-torsor and the result follows from Lemma
1.19
.
∎

Proposition 1.24
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has

\mathrm{Map}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n+i](n))=0

for every
i\neq 0
.

Proof.

By Example
1.13
, the morphism
\mathrm{S}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T}
is a Nisnevich-smooth cover of
\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T}
, and therefore

\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T})=\lim_{n}\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{T}^{n}).

By Example
1.6
,

\mathrm{Map}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{T}^{n},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n+i](n))=0,

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i\neq 0
. Since the mapping space commutes with limits, one has

\mathrm{Map}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n+i](n))=0,

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i\neq 0
, which is what we wanted to prove.
∎

Proposition 1.25
.

Suppose
\mathrm{B}
is the Borel subgroup of a connected split reductive group
\mathrm{G}
and let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n+i](n))=0

for every
i\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{T}
be the maximal torus of
\mathrm{G}
and let
\mathrm{U}
be the unipotent radical of
\mathrm{B}
. By
[
DGA+11
, XXII, Proposition 5.9.5]
, there exists a filtrations
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{U}_{0}\supseteq\mathrm{U}_{1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathrm{U}_{n}=\{1\}
such that for any
1\leq j\leq n
the scheme
\mathrm{U}_{j-1}/\mathrm{U}_{j}
is a vector bundle and the subgroup
\mathrm{U}_{j}
is normal in
\mathrm{B}
. For any
1\leq j\leq n
, the map
\pi_{j}\colon\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{T}\ltimes\mathrm{U}_{j})\to\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{T}\ltimes\mathrm{U}_{j-1})
is a
\mathrm{U}_{j-1}/{\mathrm{U}_{j}}
-torsor and by homotopy invariance, the pullback
\pi_{j}^{*}
is fully faithful. Composing all of these maps, one sees the pullback with respect to the natural map
\sigma\colon\mathrm{B}\mathrm{T}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{B}
is fully faithful.
∎

Proposition 1.26
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
and
\mathrm{L}
be algebraic groups over
\mathrm{S}
such that
\mathrm{L}=\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{U}
, for
\mathrm{U}
is a split unipotent group over
\mathrm{S}
. Then for every
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the pullback with respect to the morphism
\sigma\colon\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{L}
is fully faithful.

Proof.

This follows Lemma
1.19
.
∎

Proposition 1.27
.

Let
\mathrm{S}
be the spectrum of a perfect field and
\mathrm{G}
be a connected split reductive group. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n+i](n))=0

for every
i\neq 0
.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{G}
is a split torus, this is just Proposition
1.24
. For a general connected reductive group, this follows from Theorem
1.22
.
∎

Proposition 1.28
.

Let
\mathrm{S}
be the spectrum of a perfect field and let
\mathrm{G}
be a (possibly disconnected) reductive group such that
\left|\pi_{0}(\mathrm{G})\right|
is invertible in
\Lambda
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathscr{X},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n+i](n))=0

for every
i\neq 0
.

Proof.

The component group
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{G})
is a finite group and the map
\pi\colon\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}^{\circ}\to\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}
is a
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{G})
-torsor. In this case, the composition

\Lambda_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}}\to\pi_{*}\pi^{*}\Lambda_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}}\to\Lambda_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}}

is multiplication by
\left|\pi_{0}(\mathrm{G})\right|
and, therefore, is invertible by the hypothesis on
\Lambda
. This means the unit
\Lambda_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}}\to\pi_{*}\pi^{*}\Lambda_{\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G}}
is a split monomorphism, and therefore, once again,
\pi^{*}
is faithful, concluding our proof.
∎

Corollary 1.29
.

Let
\mathrm{S}
be the spectrum of an algebraically closed field. Then, for any linear algebraic group
\mathrm{G}
such that
\left|\pi_{0}(\mathrm{G})\right|
is invertible in
\Lambda
, one has

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(\Lambda,\Lambda[2n+i](n))=0

for every
i\neq 0
.

Proof.

In this case, the quotient
\mathrm{G}_{\textrm{red}}\coloneqq\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{u}}(\mathrm{G})
is split reductive and
\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{u}}(\mathrm{G})
is a split unipotent. The result follows from Lemma
1.19
, Proposition
1.26
and Proposition
1.28
.
∎

1.4
Motives on quotient stacks

In this section,
\mathrm{S}
will denote the spectrum of an algebraically closed field to ensure every reductive group is split.

Definition 1.30
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic stack of finite type over
\mathrm{S}
. We say
M\in\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X})
is
*
-pointwise pure Tate (resp.
!
-pointwise pure Tate) if for every
S
-point
x\colon\mathrm{S}\to X
, one has
x^{*}M\in\operatorname{DTM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathrm{S})^{\heartsuit_{w}}
(resp.
x^{!}M\in\operatorname{DTM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathrm{S})^{\heartsuit_{w}}
). We say
M\in\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathscr{X})
is pointwise pure Tate if it is both
*
-pointwise pure Tate and
!
-pointwise pure Tate.

Lemma 1.31
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a linear algebraic group acting on a finite type scheme
\mathrm{X}
with finitely many orbits and let
M,\,N\in\operatorname{DM}_{(\mathrm{r})}(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)
be
*
- and
!
-pointwise pure Tate motives, respectively. If for every
x\in\mathrm{X}
the integers
t_{\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(x)_{\mathrm{red}}}
and
\lvert\pi_{0}\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(x)\rvert
are invertible in
\Lambda
, then the reduction functor gives an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(M,N)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(\mathrm{r}(M),\mathrm{r}(N)).

Proof.

We do an induction on the number of orbits. If there is just one, then this is simply Proposition
1.23
. If there are more orbits, choose one closed orbit
\mathrm{Z}
, which exists by the closed orbit lemma, and consider the open complement
\mathrm{U}
. If
i\colon\mathrm{Z}/\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G}
and
j\colon\mathrm{U}/\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G}
are the corresponding closed and open immersions between quotients stacks, then one has a distinguished triangle

j_{!}j^{*}M\to M\to i_{!}i^{*}M,

which once applied
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(-,N[i])
gives an exact sequence in which one may apply the induction hypothesis and the five lemma to conclude the result.
∎

Lemma 1.32
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a linear algebraic
\mathrm{S}
group acting on a finite type
\mathrm{S}
-scheme
\mathrm{X}
with finitely many orbits and let
M,\,N\in\operatorname{DM}_{(r)}(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)
be
*
- and
!
-pointwise pure Tate motives, respectively. If for every
x\in\mathrm{X}
the integers
t_{\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(x)_{\mathrm{red}}}
and
\lvert\pi_{0}\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(x)\rvert
are invertible in
\Lambda
, then

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(M,N[i])=0

for every
i\neq 0
.

Proof.

We are going to do an induction on the number of orbits. If there’s just one orbit, then
\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{B}\mathrm{Z}_{\mathrm{G}}(x)
and we are reduced to Corollary
1.29
. If there are more orbits, choose one close orbit
\mathrm{Z}
, which exists by the closed orbit lemma, and consider the open complement
\mathrm{U}
. If
i\colon\mathrm{Z}/\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G}
and
j\colon\mathrm{U}/\mathrm{G}\to\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G}
are the corresponding closed and open immersions between quotients stacks, then one has a distinguished triangle

j_{!}j^{*}M\to M\to i_{!}i^{*}M,

which once applied
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(-,N[i])
gives an exact sequence

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{U}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(j^{*}M,j^{!}N[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(M,N[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{DM}_{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{Z}/\mathrm{G},\Lambda)}(i^{*}M,i^{!}N[i]).

By the induction hypothesis, the left term vanishes, and again by Corollary
1.29
, the second term vanishes too, which completes the proof.
∎

The integral Springer correspondence

We are going to recall some fundamental results on the theory of weight structures, which allows us to both define and use weight structures.

Definition A.1
.

A weight structure on a stable
\infty
-category
\mathscr{C}
is a pair of retract-closed full subcategories
(\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0},\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0})
such that, if we call
\mathscr{C}_{w\geq n}=\Sigma^{n}\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
\mathscr{C}_{w\leq n}=\Sigma^{n}\mathscr{C}_{w\leq n}
for

(a)

\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 1}\subset\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
\mathscr{C}_{w\leq-1}\subset\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0}
;

(b)

if
x\in\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
y\in\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 1}
, then

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(x,y)=0;

(c)

for any
x\in\mathscr{C}
, there exists a cofiber sequence

x_{\leq 0}\to x\to x_{\geq 1},

where
x_{\leq 0}\in\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
x_{\geq 1}\in\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 1}
.

As in the case of

Definition A.2
.

A weight structure
(\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0},\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0})
on a stable
\infty
-category
\mathscr{C}
is said to be
bounded
if the inclusion

\mathscr{C}^{b}=\bigcup_{n}\left(\mathscr{C}_{w\geq-n}\cap\mathscr{C}_{w\leq n}\right)\to\mathscr{C}

is an equivalence.

Example A.3
.

For any additive idempotent-complete category
\mathscr{A}
, the stable
\infty
-category of chain complexes
\operatorname{Ch}^{b}(\mathscr{A})\coloneqq\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{K}^{b}(\mathscr{A}))
is endowed with a weight structure given by

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Ch}^{b}(\mathscr{A})_{w\geq 0}\coloneqq\{X\in\operatorname{Ch}^{b}(\mathscr{A})\mid X_{i}=0\text{ if }i<0\};\\
&\operatorname{Ch}^{b}(\mathscr{A})_{w\leq 0}\coloneqq\{X\in\operatorname{Ch}^{b}(\mathscr{A})\mid X_{i}=0\text{ if }i>0\}.\end{split}

Definition A.4
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category equipped with a weight structure
(\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0})
. We define the heart of the weight structure as:

\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}\coloneqq\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0}\cap\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0}.

If
\mathscr{D}
is another stable
\infty
-category equipped with a weight structure, then a weigth exact functor
F\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
between stable
\infty
-categories is an exact functor which sends the heart of
\mathscr{C}
to the heart of
\mathscr{D}
.

The heart of a weight structure is always an additive idempotent-complete
\infty
-category, but rarely an Abelian category

Definition A.5
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category and
\mathscr{N}
be a collection of objects of
\mathscr{C}
. We say
\mathscr{N}
is negative (resp. positive, resp. tilting) if for every
x,\,y\in\mathscr{N}

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(x,\Sigma^{n}y)=0

for every
n>0
(resp.
n<0
, resp.
n\neq 0
).

The following lemma gives a sufficient (and necessary) condition for a subcategory to determine the heart of a bounded weight structure. It is due to Bondarko in
[
Bon10
, Theorem 4.3.2.II]
, and the statement in the
\infty
-categorical language is given in
[
ES22b
, Remark 2.2.6]
.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category and suppose we are given a negative collection of objects
\mathscr{N}
which generates
\mathscr{C}
under finite limits, finite colimits and retracts. Then the categories

\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0}=\{\textrm{retracts of finite colimits of }\mathscr{N}\}

and

\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0}=\{\textrm{retracts of finite limits of }\mathscr{N}\}

defines a bounded weight structure on
\mathscr{C}
whose heart is the smallest additive subcategory closed under retracts containing
\mathscr{N}
.

If the collection is tilting, then the heart
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
is a classical category (in other words,
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}\cong\mathrm{h}\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
), indeed by definition there is no higher
\operatorname{Hom}
between the elements of
\mathscr{N}
and since
\mathscr{N}
generates the heart under retracts, there is no higher
\operatorname{Hom}
between elements in the heart. We are going to see that the only such examples are the ones from Example
A.3
. In order to prove this, we define the weight complex functor.

Proposition A.7

(
[
Sos19
]
, Proposition 3.3)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category equipped with a bounded weight structure. Then:

(1)

The functor

\mathscr{C}\to\Fun^{\mathrm{add}}((\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{Sp})

given by the restriction of the Yoneda functor is exact and fully faithful.

(2)

Let
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
be another stable
\infty
-category equipped with a weight structure. Then the restriction functor
\Fun^{w\textrm{-ex}}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}^{\prime})\to\Fun^{\mathrm{add}}(\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}},\mathscr{C}^{\prime\heartsuit_{w}})
is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories.

Definition A.8
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category equipped with a bounded weight structure. The
weight complex functor
is the weight exact functor
t\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathrm{Ch}^{b}(\mathrm{h}\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}})
being sent to the additive functor
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}\to\mathrm{h}\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
under the equivalence of
\infty
-categories given by Proposition
A.7
(2).

As we are going to see, this construction is both symmetric monoidal, when the weight structure is compatible with the symmetric monoidal structure, and functorial with respect to weight exact functors.

Definition A.10
.

Let
\mathscr{C}^{\otimes}
be a stably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. We say a weight structure
(\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0},\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0})
is
compatible with the symmetric monoidal structure
if
\mathscr{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
\mathscr{C}_{w\leq 0}
are stable under tensor product.

Lemma A.11

(
[
Aok20
]
, Corollary 4.5)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}^{\otimes}
be a stably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category equipped with a bounded weight structure compatible with the symmetric monoidal structure. Then the weight complex functor admits a symmetric monoidal enhancement.

Proposition A.12
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category endowed with a bounded weight structure whose heart
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
is tilting. Then the weight complex functor is an equivalence:

\mathscr{C}\cong\mathrm{Ch}^{b}(\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}).

If
\mathscr{C}^{\otimes}
is a stably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category and the bounded weight structure is compatible with the symmetric monoidal structure, then this equivalence is symmetric monoidal.

Proof.

By Proposition
A.7
(2), one has

\Fun^{w\textrm{-ex}}(\mathscr{C},\operatorname{Ch}^{b}(\mathrm{h}\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}))\to\Fun^{\mathrm{add}}(\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}},\mathrm{h}\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}),

and, since the heart is tilting,
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}\cong\mathrm{h}\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
, which concludes our proof.
∎

Observe that if
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
is tilting, then
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}=\mathrm{h}\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
and we recover Example
A.3
. Moreover, to verify that the heart
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit_{w}}
is tilting, it suffices to verify that a generating collection
\mathscr{N}
is tilting.

Corollary A.13

(
[
ES22a
]
, Corollary 2.16)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a stable
\infty
-category admiting an auto-equivalence
\langle 1\rangle
and
x\in\mathscr{C}
an object. If the collection
\mathscr{N}=\{x\langle n\rangle\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is tilting and generates
\mathscr{C}
under finite limits, finite colimits and retracts, then

\mathscr{C}\cong\mathrm{grPerf}(\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\bullet}(x)),

where
\operatorname{End}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\bullet}(x)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(x,x\langle n\rangle)
and
\mathrm{grPerf}(A^{\bullet})
is the category of graded perfect complexes of
A^{\bullet}
-modules.

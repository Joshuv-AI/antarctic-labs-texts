Canonical completion and duality for cylindric ortholattices and cylindric Boolean algebras
Joseph McDonald

Abstract
In this note, we investigate the algebraic and topological representation theory of cylindric ortholattices and cylindric Boolean algebras. The first contribution demonstrates that cylindric ortholattices are closed under canonical completions. By equipping a spectral topology to the dual space associated with the canonical completion, we then establish a dual equivalence between the category of cylindric ortholattices and a certain subcategory of the category of spectral spaces. This work builds on the completion and duality results obtained by Harding, McDonald, and Peinado in the setting of monadic ortholattices combined with the duality results obtained by McDonald and Yamamoto in the setting of general ortholattices. By working with the duality theory for Boolean algebras established by Bezhanishvili and Holliday, we then obtain completion and duality results for cylindric Boolean algebras. A key aspect of our duality results is that they are constructive in the sense that they obtain in Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory independently of the Axiom of Choice. Keywords: Cylindric ortholattice; Cylindric Boolean algebra; Canonical completion; Vietoris hyperspace; Duality theory; Axiom of Choice.

Introduction

Cylindric Boolean algebras were introduced by Henkin and Tarski
[
17
]
and by Henkin, Monk, and Tarski
[
32
,
33
]
as algebraic models of the classical predicate calculus with identity. Closely related to the cylindric Boolean algebras are the polyadic and monadic Boolean algebras studied by Halmos
[
11
,
12
]
, which form algebraic models of the classical single-variable predicate calculus and the full predicate calculus without identity, respectively. For any cardinal
\kappa
, a
\kappa
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebra
is a Boolean algebra
B
equipped with a family
(\exists_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\kappa}
of pairwise commuting quantifiers (i.e., closure operators whose closed elements form Boolean sub-algebras of
B
) and a family
(d_{\alpha,\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\kappa}
of constants satisfying certain conditions. The standard construction of a
\kappa
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebra involves starting with a set
X
and then considering the concrete (powerset) Boolean algebra of the function space
X^{\kappa}
. For each
\alpha\in\kappa
, its
cylindrification

\exists_{\alpha}
of a subset
Y\subseteq X^{\kappa}
is given by the collection of choice-functions
\sigma\in X^{\kappa}
that agree with a choice-function in
Y
except (possibly) at the coordinate
\alpha
. The
diagonal

d_{\alpha,\beta}
is the collection of all choice-functions
\sigma\in X^{\kappa}
satisfying the equation
\sigma(\alpha)=\sigma(\beta)
. Recently, Harding
[
13
]
introduced cylindric ortholattices and studied them for their connections to subfactors within the setting of von Neumann algebras. A key aspect distinguishing cylindric ortholattices from cylindric Boolean algebras is that the former do not in general satisfy the distributive identities.

In this note, we first prove that cylindric ortholattices are closed under canonical completions. This extends the canonical completion results obtained by Harding, McDonald, and Peinado
[
14
]
within the setting of monadic ortholattices. The completion of a cylindric ortholattice
A
arises by forming an associated dual space
X_{A}
that is a cylindric orthoframe. This is a set equipped with an orthogonality relation, an indexed family of commuting reflexive, transitive binary relations, as well as an indexed family of distinguished subsets satisfying certain conditions. By working with the proper filters of
A
, the corresponding completion is obtained by embedding
A
into the complete cylindric ortholattice of biorthogonally closed subsets of
X_{A}
. Based on the obtained canonical completion result, our main result contributes to the recent research program of producing (spatial) topological dualities for lattice-based algebras independently of the Axiom of Choice. Research along these lines was initiated within the setting of Boolean algebras by Bezhanishvili and Holliday
[
1
]
, which can be viewed as arising in part via Stone’s representation of bounded distributive lattices via the compact open subsets of a spectral space
[
31
]
, the Vietoris hyperspace construction
[
34
]
, and Tarski’s observation
[
32
,
33
]
that the regular open subsets of any topological space form a Boolean algebra. These methods were subsequently applied in the setting of ortholattices by McDonald and Yamamoto
[
26
]
, De Vries algebras by Massas
[
25
]
, modal algebras by Bezhanishvili, Dmitrieva,
et. al.

[
2
]
, as well as Heyting algebras, implicative lattices, and monotone bounded lattice expansions by Hartonas
[
15
,
16
]
.

The primary aim of this note is to apply these methods to the setting of cylindric ortholattices and cylindric Boolean algebras. We first introduce certain spectral spaces, which we call
cylindric upper Vietoris orthospaces
. These are relational extensions of the upper Vietoris orthospaces (UVO-spaces) introduced by McDonald and Yamamoto
[
26
]
combined with the monadic orthospaces studied by Harding, McDonald, and Peinado
[
14
]
. It is shown that every cylindric ortholattice
A
is isomorphic to the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{0}(A))
of compact open biorthogonally closed subsets of the spectrum
\mathcal{S}_{0}(A)
of proper filters of
A
equipped with the spectral topology. Unlike the representation and duality results for ortholattices in
[
10
,
3
]
and for monadic ortholattices in
[
14
]
, ours avoids the use of Alexander’s Subbase Theorem, or any other choice-dependent principle, in proving that
\mathcal{S}_{0}(A)
forms a compact topological space. We then prove that every cylindric UVO-space
X
is relationally homeomorphic to the spectrum
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{0}(X))
of proper filters of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}(X)
of compact open biorthogonally closed subsets of
X
. Finally, with the introduction of suitable spectral frame morphisms, which we call
cylindric UVO-maps
, we show that the category
\mathcal{COL}
of cylindric ortholattices is dually equivalent to the category
\mathcal{CUVO}
of cylindric UVO-spaces. We then introduce relational extensions of the UV-spaces introduced by Bezhanishvili and Holliday
[
1
]
, which we call
cylindric UV-spaces
. These are certain spectral spaces that come equipped with a family of commuting equivalence relations as well as a family of distinguished subsets satisfying similar conditions to cylindric UVO-spaces. We show that every cylindric Boolean algebra
A
is isomorphic to the algebra
\mathcal{G}_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(A))
of compact open regular open subsets of the spectrum
\mathcal{F}_{0}(A)
of proper filters of
A
. We then show that every cylindric UV-space

is relationally homeomorphic to the spectrum
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathcal{G}_{0}(X))
of proper filters of the algebra
\mathcal{G}_{0}(X)
of compact open regular open subsets of
X
. With the introduction of suitable spectral frame morphisms, which we call
cylindric UV-maps
, we show that the category
\mathcal{CBA}
of cylindric Boolean algebras is dually equivalent to the category
\mathcal{CUV}
of cylindric UV-spaces. In doing so, we provide a topological construction of the canonical completion of a cylindric Boolean algebra. Finally, we conclude by discussing how the duality obtained for cylindric Boolean algebras is related to the duality obtained for cylindric ortholattices.

Cylindric ortholattices and orthoframes

In this section, we describe some basic properties of cylindric ortholattices and cylindric orthoframes. For more details, consult
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
ortholattice
is an bounded lattice
\langle A;\wedge,\vee,0,1\rangle
that comes equipped with an additional operation
{}^{\perp}\colon A\to A
, known as an
orthogonal complementation
, satisfying the following conditions:

1.

a\wedge a^{\perp}=0
;

2.

a\vee a^{\perp}=1
;

3.

a\leq b\Rightarrow b^{\perp}\leq a^{\perp}
;

4.

a^{\perp\perp}=a
.

It is routine to verify that every ortholattice satisfies De Morgan’s laws. Moreover, note that unlike Boolean algebras, ortholattices are not in general distributive. In fact, an ortholattice
A
is a Boolean algebra iff
A
is distributive.

Definition 2.2
.

A
monadic ortholattice
is an ortholattice
\langle A;\wedge,\vee,^{\perp},0,1\rangle
that comes equipped with an additional operation
\exists\colon A\to A
, known as a
quantifier
, satisfying the following conditions:

1.

\exists(a\vee b)=\exists a\vee\exists b
;

2.

\exists 0=0
;

3.

\exists\exists a=\exists a
;

4.

a\leq\exists a
;

5.

\exists(\exists a)^{\perp}=(\exists a)^{\perp}
.

Any quantifier on an ortholattice
A
may be viewed as a closure operator whose closed elements
\{a\in A:a=\exists a\}
form a sub-ortholattice of
A
.

.

\text{mOL}_{12}
. Note that for every
a\in\text{mOL}_{12}
,
\exists a
is the smallest
\bullet
-element above
a
so that each
\bullet
-element in
\text{mOL}_{12}
is a closed element under
\exists
. Note that
\text{mOL}_{12}
in particular forms a quantum monadic algebra (see
[
13
, Example 5.7]
).

Definition 2.4
.

An
I
-dimensional cylindric ortholattice
is an ortholattice
A
equipped with a family of unary operators
(\exists_{i})_{i\in I}
and a family of constants
(\delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I}
such that for all
i,k,l\in I
, the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

\exists_{i}\colon A\to A
is a quantifier;

2.

\exists_{i}\exists_{k}a=\exists_{k}\exists_{i}a
;

3.

\delta_{i,k}=\delta_{k,i}
and
\delta_{i,i}=1
;

4.

i,l\not=k\Rightarrow\exists_{k}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})=\delta_{i,l}
.

The reduct
\langle A;\wedge,\vee,^{\perp},0,1,(\exists_{i})_{i\in I}\rangle
is known as the
I-dimensional diagonal-free cylindric ortholattice reduct
of
A
.

It is worth pointing out that cylindric Boolean algebras form a strengthening of cylindric ortholattices in two ways. First, the underlying lattice reduct is distributive. Second, there is an axiom asserting that for all
i,k\in I
such that
i\not=k
, we have
\exists_{i}(d_{i,k}\wedge a)\wedge\exists_{k}(d_{i,k}\wedge a^{\perp})=0
. This induces an operation of substitution defined by
s^{i}_{k}(a)=\exists_{i}(d_{i,k}\wedge a)
that is an endomorphism of the Boolean algebra reduct. For details on why this axiom is not included in the definition of cylindric ortholattices, consult
[
13
, Remark 5.15]
.

Let
X
be a set and
R
be a binary relation on
X
. Then for any
U\subseteq X
, we will write
R[U]
to denote the relational image of
U
under
R
, i.e.,

R[U]=\{y\in X:xRy\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for some}\hskip 2.84544ptx\in U\}

so that for any
x\in X
, we have
R[\{x\}]=\{y\in X:xRy\}
.

Definition 2.5
.

An
orthoframe
is pair
\langle X,\perp\rangle
such that
X
is a set and
\perp\subseteq X^{2}
is an orthogonality relation, i.e.,
\perp
is irreflexive and symmetric. Moreover:

1.

for
U\subseteq X
, let
U^{\perp}=\{x\in X:x\perp U\}=\{x\in X:x\perp y\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all}\hskip 5.69046pty\in U\}

2.

U\subseteq X
is
biorthogonally closed
iff
U=U^{\perp\perp}
.

For any orthoframe
X
, by
\mathcal{B}(X)
we denote the collection of biorthogonally closed subsets of
X
.

The following relational structures were introduced in
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

A
monadic orthoframe
is a triple
\langle X;\perp,R\rangle
such that:

1.

\langle X;\perp\rangle
is an orthoframe;

2.

R
is a binary relation on
X
that is reflexive and transitive;

3.

R[R[\{x\}]^{\perp}]\subseteq R[\{x\}]^{\perp}
for all
x\in X
.

Condition 3 in the above definition can be viewed as the requirement that
R[\{x\}]^{\perp}
be closed under
R
.
Note that in any monadic orthoframe
\langle X;\perp,R\rangle
:

R[R[\{x\}]^{\perp}]=R[\{x\}]^{\perp}

for all
x\in X
by the reflexivity of
R
.

Definition 2.7
.

An
I-dimensional cylindric orthoframe
is a relational structure
\langle X;\perp,(R_{i})_{i\in I},(\Delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I}\rangle
such that for all
i,k,l\in I
:

1.

\langle X;\perp,R_{i}\rangle
is a monadic orthoframe;

2.

R_{i}
commutes with
R_{k}
;

3.

\Delta_{i,k}\subseteq X
with
\Delta_{i,k}=\Delta_{k,i}=\Delta^{\perp\perp}_{i,k}
and
\Delta_{i,i}=X
;

4.

if
i,l\not=k
, then
R_{k}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap\Delta_{k,l}]=\Delta_{i,l}
.

The reduct
\langle X;\perp,(R_{i})_{i\in I}\rangle
is known as the
I-dimensional diagonal-free cylindric orthoframe reduct
of
X
whenever conditions 1 and 2 are satisfied.

Throughout the remainder of this work, if
X
is a monadic orthoframe, define:

U\sqcup V:=(U\cup V)^{\perp\perp},\hskip 5.69046pt\exists_{R}U:=R[U]^{\perp\perp}

where
R[U]^{\perp\perp}=(R[U])^{\perp\perp}
for all
U\in\mathcal{B}(X)
.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
13
, Proposition 7.31]
)
.

If
X
is an
I
-dimensional cylindric orthoframe, then the algebra
\langle\mathcal{B}(X);\cap,\sqcup,^{\perp},\emptyset,X,(\exists_{R_{i}})_{i\in I},(\Delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I}\rangle
is a complete
I
-dimensional cylindric ortholattice.

Proof.

The proof relies on the well-known result of Birkhoff
[
5
, p. 123]
that the algebra
\langle\mathcal{B}(X);\cap,\sqcup,^{\perp},\emptyset,X\rangle
forms a complete ortholattice whenever
X
is an orthoframe and the result of Harding
[
13
, Lemma 7.25]
that
\exists_{R}\colon\mathcal{B}(X)\to\mathcal{B}(X)
is a quantifier on
\mathcal{B}(X)
so that the algebra
\langle\mathcal{B}(X);\cap,\sqcup,^{\perp},\emptyset,X,\exists_{R}\rangle
is a complete monadic ortholattice whenever
X
is a monadic orthoframe.
∎

3
Canonical completion

In this section, using the canonical completion results obtained by Harding, McDonald, and Peinado
[
14
]
within the setting of monadic ortholattices, we demonstrate that the variety of
I
-dimensional cylindric ortholattices is closed under canonical completions.

Recall that for a bounded lattice
A
and a complete lattice
C
, an embedding
e\colon A\to C
is
dense
if every element in
C
is both a meet of joins and a join of meets of elements in the image of
A
under
e
. Moreover,
e
is
compact
if for all subsets
S,T\subseteq A
, there exists finite subsets
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
and
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
such that:

\bigwedge e[S]\leq\bigvee e[T]\Longrightarrow\bigwedge e[S^{\prime}]\leq\bigvee e[T^{\prime}].

The
canonical completion
of
A
is then defined to be a pair
\langle e;C\rangle
such that
C
is a complete lattice and
e\colon A\to C
is an embedding which is dense and compact. It is easy to see that every bounded lattice
A
has up to isomorphism a unique canonical completion.

Remark 3.1
.

Notice that when
B
is a Boolean algebra, its canonical completion is determined by the embedding of
B
into the complete powerset Boolean algebra
\wp(X_{B})
where
X_{B}
is the Stone space of ultrafilters of
B
.

Recall that for a bounded lattice
A
:

1.

a non-empty subset
x\subseteq A
is a
filter
whenever:

(a)

a\in x
and
a\leq b
implies
b\in x
;

(b)

a\in x
and
b\in x
implies
a\wedge b\in x
;

2.

a filter
x\subseteq A
is
proper
whenever
x\not=A
. Note that this is equivalent to the requirement that
0\not\in x
since
0\leq a
for all
a\in A
;

3.

a proper filter
x\subseteq A
is
prime
whenever
a\vee b\in x
implies
a\in x
or
b\in x
;

4.

a proper filter
x\subseteq A
is
completely prime
whenever
\bigvee_{i\in I}a_{i}\in x
implies
a_{i}\in x
for some
i\in I
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A
be an
I
-dimensional cylindric ortholattice. We define the relational structure
X_{A}=\langle\mathfrak{F}(A);\perp,(R_{i})_{i\in I},(\Delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I}\rangle
as follows:

1.

\mathfrak{F}(A)
is the collection of all non-empty proper filters of
A
;

2.

\perp\subseteq\mathfrak{F}(A)^{2}
is defined by
x\perp y
iff there exists
a\in x
such that
a^{\perp}\in y
;

3.

R_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}(A)^{2}
is defined by
xR_{i}y
iff
\exists_{i}[x]\subseteq y
with
\exists_{i}[x]=\{\exists_{i}a:a\in x\}
;

4.

\Delta_{i,k}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}(A)
is defined by
\Delta_{i,k}=\phi(d_{i,k})=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A):\delta_{i,k}\in x\}
.

We will often refer to
X_{A}
as the
Goldblatt frame
of

.

Lemma 3.3
.

X_{A}=\langle\mathfrak{F}(A);\perp,(R_{i})_{i\in I},(\Delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I}\rangle
is an
I
-dimensional cylindric orthoframe whenever
A
is an
I
-dimensional cylindric ortholattice.

Proof.

The result that
\perp
is irreflexive and symmetric follows from Goldblatt
[
10
, Lemma 2]
. The result that
R_{i}
is reflexive, transitive, and satisfies the inclusion
R_{i}[R_{i}[\{x\}]^{\perp}]\subseteq R_{i}[\{x\}]^{\perp}
for each proper filter
x\in\mathfrak{F}(A)
follows by Harding, McDonald, and Peinado
[
14
, Proposition 3.5]
.

Hence we first verify that the relations
R_{i}
and
R_{k}
commute, i.e.,

R_{i}\circ R_{k}=R_{k}\circ R_{i}\hskip 11.38092pt\text{for all $i,k\in I$}

where the relational composition
R_{i}\circ R_{k}
is defined in the usual way by:

R_{i}\circ R_{k}=\{\langle x,z\rangle\in\mathfrak{F}(A)\times\mathfrak{F}(A):xR_{i}y\hskip 2.84544pt\text{and}\hskip 2.84544ptyR_{k}z\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for some}\hskip 2.84544pty\in\mathfrak{F}(A)\}.

Assume
xR_{i}y
and
yR_{k}z
so that
\exists_{i}[x]\subseteq y
and
\exists_{k}[y]\subseteq z
for some

. This implies that
\exists_{k}[\exists_{i}[x]]\subseteq z
where:

\displaystyle\exists_{k}[\exists_{i}[x]]

\displaystyle=\{\exists_{k}b:b\in\exists_{i}[x]\}=\{\exists_{k}b:b=\exists_{i}c\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for some}\hskip 2.84544ptc\in x\}

\displaystyle=\{\exists_{k}\exists_{i}c:c\in x\}=\{\exists_{i}\exists_{k}c:c\in x\}

It suffices to find some

such that

and

. Hence set

to be the proper filter generated by
\exists_{k}[x]
, i.e.,

It is easy to verify that

is indeed proper since

is proper, and also
\exists_{k}[x]\subseteq w
and
\exists_{i}[w]\subseteq z
so that
xR_{k}w
and
wR_{i}z
. The right-to-left inclusion follows by a symmetric argument.

Verifying that
\Delta_{i,k}=\Delta_{k,i}
is straightforward as can be seen below:

\displaystyle\Delta_{i,k}

\displaystyle=\phi(\delta_{i,k})=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A):\delta_{i,k}\in x\}

(def. of
\Delta_{i,k}
and
\phi
)

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A):\delta_{k,i}\in x\}

(Definition
2.4
(3))

\displaystyle=\phi(\delta_{k,i})=\Delta_{k,i}.

(def. of
\Delta_{k,i}
and
\phi
)

The result that
\Delta_{i,k}=\Delta_{i,k}^{\perp\perp}
is immediate by the definition of

and the fact that

is bi-orthogonally closed for each

\Delta_{i,k}=\phi(\delta_{i,k})=\phi(\delta_{i,k})^{\perp\perp}=\Delta_{i,k}^{\perp\perp}.

To see

is satisfied, note that:

\displaystyle\Delta_{i,i}

\displaystyle=\phi(\delta_{i,i})=\phi(1)=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A)\colon 1\in x\}=\mathfrak{F}(A).

To verify
R_{k}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap\Delta_{k,l}]=\Delta_{i,l}

, first note that:

\displaystyle R_{k}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap\Delta_{k,l}]

\displaystyle=R_{k}[\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})].

Remark 3.8

If

4
Monadic and cylindric UVO-spaces

We now proceed by characterizing the choice-free topological duals of the
I
-dimensional cylindric ortholattices. Throughout the remainder of this work, given any topological orthoframe
\langle X;\perp,\tau\rangle
where
\perp\subseteq X^{2}
is an orthogonality relation and
\tau\subseteq\wp(X)
is a topology, let
\mathcal{CO}(X)
denote the collection of compact open subsets of

and let
\mathcal{COB}(X)=\mathcal{CO}(X)\cap\mathcal{B}(X)
, i.e., let
\mathcal{COB}(X)
denote the collection of compact open biorthogonally closed subsets of

.

Recall that a topological space
X
is a
T_{0}
-space
if for all
x,y\in X
such that
x\not=y
, there exists an open set
U
in
X
such that
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
. The following combines the upper Vietoris orthospaces studied in
[
26
]
with the monadic orthospaces studied in
[
14
]
. For a relation

, let
\overline{R}
be its complement. Throughout the remainder of this section, let:

\exists_{R}[\mathcal{COB}_{X}(x)]:=\{\exists_{R}U:U\in\mathcal{COB}_{X}(x)\}

where:

\mathcal{COB}_{X}(x)=\{U\in\mathcal{COB}(X):x\in U\}.

Definition 4.1
.

A
monadic upper Vietoris orthospace
(henceforth,
monadic UVO-space
) is a relational topological space
\langle X;\perp,R,\tau\rangle
such that:

1.

\langle X;\perp,R\rangle
is a monadic orthoframe over a
T_{0}
-space

;

2.

\mathcal{COB}(X)
forms a basis for
X
and is closed under
\cap
and
⟂
;

3.

every proper filter in
\mathcal{COB}(X)
is of the form
\mathcal{COB}_{X}(x)
for some
x\in X
;

4.

if
x\perp y
, there exists
U\in\mathcal{COB}(X)
such that
x\in U
and
y\in U^{\perp}
;

5.

R[U]\in\mathcal{COB}(X)
whenever
U\in\mathcal{COB}(X)
;

6.

if
x\overline{R}y
, there exists some
U\in\mathcal{COB}(X)
such that
U=\exists_{R}V
for some
V\in\mathcal{COB}_{X}(x)
with

.

We regard the topological orthoframe reduct
\langle X;\perp,\tau\rangle
as the
upper Vietoris orthospace reduct
of
X
if

is a
T_{0}
-space satisfying conditions 2 through 4.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a topological space. Then:

1.

X
is a

if every basic open cover of
X
has a finite subcover;

2.

X
is a

if
\mathcal{CO}(X)
forms a basis for
X
and is closed under finite intersections;

3.

X
is a
sober space
provided every completely prime filter in the lattice
\mathcal{CO}(X)
is of the form
\mathcal{CO}_{X}(x)
where
\mathcal{CO}_{X}(x)=\{U\in\mathcal{CO}(X)\colon x\in U\}
for some point
x\in X
.

4.

X
is a
spectral space
if

is
T_{0}
, compact, coherent, and sober.

Note that unlike Stone spaces, spectral spaces are not in general Hausdorff. Consult
[
7
]
for more details on Stone and spectral spaces.

Proposition 4.3
.

Every monadic UVO-space is a spectral space.

Proof.

The result follows immediately from
[
26
, Corollary 5.14]

∎

Recall that in any topological space
X
, the
specialization order

\leqslant
of
X
is defined by
x\leqslant y
iff
x\in U
implies
y\in U
for all open sets
U
in
X
. The following lemma describes some useful properties of monadic UVO-spaces.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X
be a monadic UVO-space. Then:

1.

the specialization order
\leqslant
is a partial order;

2.

x\not\leqslant y
implies there exists
U\in\mathcal{COB}(X)
such that
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
;

3.

xRy
implies
\exists_{R}[\mathcal{COB}_{X}(x)]\subseteq\mathcal{COB}_{X}(y)
.

Proof.

For part 1, note that the specialization order of any topological space determines a quasi-order. Since by definition every monadic UVO-space is
T_{0}
, it follows that
\leqslant
is anti-symmetric and hence a partial order.

Part 2 follows immediately from the fact that
\mathcal{COB}(X)
forms a basis: Assume that
x\not\leqslant y
. By the definition of
\leqslant
, there exists an open set
U
in
X
such that
x\in U
but
y\not\in U
. By Definition
4.1
(2) we obtain:

U=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}V_{i}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for}\hskip 5.69046ptV_{1},\dots,V_{n}\in\mathcal{COB}(X).

Thus
x\in V_{i}
for some
i
such that
1\leq i\leq n
where
V_{i}\in\mathcal{COB}(X)
and
y\not\in V_{i}
for each
V_{i}
. For part 3, assume
xRy
and let
U\in\exists_{R}[\mathcal{COB}_{X}(x)]
so that
U=\exists_{R}V
for some
V\in\mathcal{COB}_{X}(x)
. This implies
x\in V
by the definition of
\mathcal{COB}_{X}
and also
R[V]\in\mathcal{COB}(X)
by Definition
4.1
(5). Thus by the definition of
\exists_{R}
and the fact that
R[V]\in\mathcal{B}(X)
, we obtain:

\exists_{R}V=R[V]^{\perp\perp}=R[V]=\{z\in X:wRz\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for some}\hskip 2.84544ptw\in V\}

and hence we find that
y\in\exists_{R}V
since
x\in V
and
xRy
by hypothesis. This implies that
\exists_{R}V=U\in\mathcal{COB}_{X}(y)
, as desired.
∎

By extending monadic UVO-spaces with the relational structure of an
I
-dimensional cylindric orthoframe, we arrive at the following.

Definition 4.5
.

An

is a topological space
\langle X;\perp,(R_{i})_{i\in I},(\Delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I},\tau\rangle
satisfying the following conditions for all

is an

-dimensional cylindric orthoframe;

2.

\langle X;\perp,(R_{i})_{i\in I},\tau\rangle

.

We call

the

of

Notice that we only require that

since

, which implies

The following endows the canonical completion of a cylindric ortholattice with the spectral topology along the lines of

Definition 5.1

Let

The following results are well-known.

Theorem 5.2

(Prime Ideal Theorem)
.

Let
A
be a Boolean algebra. Then every proper ideal in

is contained within some prime ideal.

Proof.

The result follows by way of the Axiom of Choice (see
[
20
]
).
∎

Theorem 5.3

(Alexander’s Subbase Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a topological space and let
\sigma
be a subbasis of
X
. Then
X
is a compact topological space if and only if every basic open cover of
X
by elements of
\sigma
has a finite subcover.

Proof.

The result follows by the Boolean Prime Ideal Theorem. See for instance Davey and Priestley
[
6
, p. 278–279]

∎

Remark 5.4
.

Goldblatt
[
10
, Proposition 3]
showed that for any ortholattice
A
, equipping
\langle\mathfrak{F}(A);\perp\rangle
with the topology
\tau(\sigma)
with sub-basis:

\sigma=\{\phi(a):a\in A\}\cup\{\mathfrak{F}(A)\setminus\phi(a):a\in A\}

makes
\langle\mathfrak{F}(A);\tau(\sigma)\rangle
a Stone space, i.e., a totally-disconnected compact space. Goldblatt’s proof of compactness makes use of Alexander’s Subbase Lemma Theorem, which is strictly weaker than the Prime Ideal Theorem for Boolean algebras, and holds in ZFC but not ZF alone. Therefore Goldblatt’s topological representation of ortholattices is non-constructive.

It is important to note that the use of the Axiom of Choice is in fact required under Goldblatt’s representation and is hence not obtainable in ZF alone, as is seen through the subsequent proposition.

Proposition 5.5

(
[
26
, Proposition 3.12]
)
.

The following are equivalent:

1.

the Prime Ideal Theorem for Boolean algebras;

2.

The space
\langle\mathfrak{F}(A);\tau(\sigma)\rangle
is compact for all Boolean algebras
A
.

Proof.

The proof of the
(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
direction is the result
[
10
, Proposition 3]
mentioned above. The proof of the

direction relies on Rado’s Weak Selection Lemma
[
22
]
.
∎

In this section, we introduce suitable spectral frame morphisms to accompany the cylindric UVO-spaces and then prove that the resulting category is dually equivalent to the category of cylindric ortholattices and homomorphisms.

.

Let
A
and

The following combines the spectral frame morphisms studied in
[

[

.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(f)[\exists_{R_{i}}U]

\displaystyle=f^{-1}[\exists_{R_{i}}U]=f^{-1}[R_{i}[U]^{\perp\perp}]

(definition of

\displaystyle=f^{-1}[R_{i}[U]]=R_{i}[f^{-1}[U]]

(4))

\displaystyle=R_{i}[f^{-1}[U]]^{\perp\perp}=\exists_{R_{i}}f^{-1}[U]

R_{i}[f^{-1}[U]]\in\mathcal{B}(X)
)

\displaystyle=\exists_{R_{i}}\mathcal{A}_{1}(f)[U]

(by the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
)

Finally, we note that the result that
\mathcal{A}_{F}
is a homomorphism for
\Delta_{i,k}

\mathcal{A}_{1}
and Definition

\mathcal{A}_{1}(f)[\Delta^{\prime}_{i,k}]=f^{-1}[\Delta^{\prime}_{i,k}]=\Delta_{i,k}

Hence

I
-dimensional cylindric ortholattice homomorphism whenever

is an

-dimensional cylindric UVO-map. Moreover, by Lemma

is a contravariant functor.
∎

Conversely to Lemma
6.4
, we now show that every homomorphism between cylindric ortholattices induces a cylindric UVO-map between their duals.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
A
and

be
I

\mathcal{S}_{F}=\langle\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1}\rangle\colon\mathcal{COL}\to\mathcal{CUVO}
determines a contravariant functor.

Proof.

Let
A
and

be monadic ortholattices. By
[
26

, it follows that
\mathcal{S}_{1}(h)\colon\mathcal{S}_{0}(A^{\prime})\to\mathcal{S}_{0}(A)

is an ortholattice homomorphism. Since the canonical representation map
\phi\colon A\to\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathcal{S}_{0}(A))

\exists

\exists

\exists_{R}

\displaystyle R_{i}[\mathcal{S}_{1}(h)^{-1}[U]]

(Def

(4),
(h^{-1})^{-1}

We now verify that
\mathcal{S}_{1}(h)^{-1}[\Delta_{i,k}]=\Delta^{\prime}_{i,k}
. The result follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}(h)^{-1}[\Delta_{i,k}]

\displaystyle=(h^{-1})^{-1}[\Delta_{i,k}]=(h^{-1})^{-1}[\phi(\delta_{i,k})]

(def of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A^{\prime}):h^{-1}[x]\in\phi(\delta_{i,k})\}

\phi

7
Cylindric Boolean algebras

In this final section, we establish canonical completion and choice-free duality results for cylindric Boolean algebras and conclude with a discussion of how these results are related to the canonical completion and choice-free duality results that have been established for cylindric ortholattices in the previous sections. Although the definition of cylindric Boolean algebras was hinted at within Section 2, we give an explicit definition for the sake of clarity. We note that quantifiers on a Boolean algebra are defined the same way as they are on an ortholattice.

Definition 7.1
.

An
I
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebra
is a Boolean algebra
A
equipped with a family of unary operators
(\exists_{i})_{i\in I}
and a family of constants
(\delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I}
such that for all
i,k,l\in I
:

1.

\exists_{i}\colon A\to A
is a quantifier;

2.

\exists_{i}\exists_{k}a=\exists_{k}\exists_{i}a
;

3.

\delta_{i,k}=\delta_{k,i}
and
\delta_{i,i}=1
;

4.

k\not=i,l\Rightarrow\delta_{i,l}=\exists_{k}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})
;

5.

i\not=k\Rightarrow\exists_{i}(d_{i,k}\wedge a)\wedge\exists_{k}(d_{i,k}\wedge a^{\perp})=0
.

We now introduce some key aspects of the choice-free duality for Boolean algebras established by Bezhanishvili and Holliday
[
1
]
, which is based on the choice-free representation of Boolean algebras developed by Holliday
[
21
]
and makes use of the concept of a possibility frame. A
possibility frame
is a triple
\langle X;\leq,P\rangle
such that
\langle X;\leq\rangle
is a poset and
P
is the collection of regular open subsets in the upset topology on
\langle X;\leq\rangle
where
P
contains
X
and is closed under intersection and the operation
∗
defined by:

U^{*}=\{x\in X:x^{\prime}\not\in U\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all $x^{\prime}\geq x$}\}.

Recall that if
X
is a topological space, then an open set
U\subseteq X
is
regular open
if and only if
\text{Int}(\text{Cl}(U))=U
where
Int
and
Cl
are the interior and closure operations on
X
. Moreover, in the upset topology
\text{UP}(X;\leq)
, we have:

\text{Int}_{\leq}(U)=\{x\in X:y\in U\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all $y\geq x$}\}

\text{Cl}_{\leq}(U)=\{x\in X:y\in U\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for some $y\geq x$}\}

and thus
\text{Int}_{\leq}(U)=X\setminus\text{Cl}_{\leq}(X\setminus U)
.

Therefore, an open set
U
is
regular open
in the upset topology on
X
if and only if
\text{Int}_{\leq}(\text{Cl}_{\leq}(U))=U
. Moreover, since
U^{*}=\text{Int}_{\leq}(X\setminus U)
, it follows that an open set
U
is
regular open
in
\text{UP}(X;\leq)
if and only if
U^{**}=U
. We will denote the collection of all such regular opens by
\mathcal{REG}(X)
so that
\mathcal{COREG}(X)=\mathcal{CO}(X)\cap\mathcal{REG}(X)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{COREG}(X)
is the collection of all compact open subsets of
X
that are regular open in the upset topology on
X
.

The following class of spaces was introduced by Bezhanishvili and Holliday
[
1
]
and arise as the choice-free duals of the Boolean algebras. Let:

\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)=\{U\in\mathcal{COREG}(X):x\in U\}.

Definition 7.2
.

A
UV-space
is a
T_{0}
-space
X
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

\mathcal{COREG}(X)
is closed under
\cap
,
\text{Int}_{\leqslant}(X\setminus\cdot)
and is a basis for
X
;

2.

every proper filter in
\mathcal{COREG}(X)
is
\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)
for some
x\in X
.

The following are relational extensions of the UV-spaces described above.

Definition 7.3
.

Let
\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)]:=\{\exists_{S_{i}}U:U\in\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)\}
. An
I-dimensional cylindric UV-space
is a topological space of the following shape
\langle X;(S_{i})_{i\in I},(\Delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I},\tau\rangle
such that for all
i,k,l\in I
:

1.

\langle X;\tau\rangle
is a UV-space;

2.

S_{i}
is an equivalence relation;

3.

S_{i}
commutes with
S_{k}
;

4.

\Delta_{i,k}\subseteq X
with
\Delta^{**}_{i,k}=\Delta_{i,k}=\Delta_{k,i}
and
\Delta_{i,i}=X
;

5.

if
i,l\not=k
, then
S_{k}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap\Delta_{k,l}]=\Delta_{i,l}
;

6.

if
i\not=k
, then
S_{i}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap U]\cap S_{i}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap U^{*}]=\emptyset
;

7.

S_{i}[U]\in\mathcal{COREG}(X)
whenever
U\in\mathcal{COREG}(X)
;

8.

if
x\overline{S}_{i}y
, then
\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)]\not=\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(y)]
.

9.

\Delta_{i,k}\in\mathcal{CO}(X)
.

It is obvious that for any
I
-dimensional cylindric UV-space
X
, we have
\Delta_{i,k}\in\mathcal{COREG}(X)
for all
i,k\in I
by conditions 4 and 9 of Definition
7.3
.

Definition 7.4
.

Let
A
be an
I
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebra. Then define the topological space
\mathcal{F}_{0}(A)=\langle\mathfrak{F}(A);(S_{i})_{i\in I},(\Delta_{i,k})_{i,k\in I},\tau(\beta)\rangle
as follows:

1.

\mathfrak{F}(A)
is the collection of non-empty proper filters of
A
;

2.

S_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}(A)\times\mathfrak{F}(A)
is defined by
xS_{i}y\Longleftrightarrow\exists_{i}[x]=\exists_{i}[y]
for all
i\in I
;

3.

\Delta_{i,k}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}(A)
is defined by
\Delta_{i,k}=\phi(\delta_{i,k})=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A):\delta_{i,k}\in x\}
;

4.

\Omega(\beta)\subseteq\wp(\mathfrak{F}(A))
is the topology generated by the basis
\beta=\{\phi(a):a\in A\}
where
\phi\colon A\to\wp(\mathfrak{F}(A))
is defined by
\phi(a)=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A):a\in x\}
.

For any
I
-dimensional cylindric UV-space
X
, define:

U\sqcup V:=\text{Int}_{\leqslant}(\text{Cl}_{\leqslant}(U\cup V)),\hskip 8.5359pt\exists_{S_{i}}U:=S_{i}[U]

for all
U,V\in\mathcal{COREG}(X)
.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
A
be an
I
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebra and let
X
be an
I
-dimensional cylindric UV-space. Then:

1.

\mathcal{F}_{0}(A)
is an
I
-dimensional cylindric UV-space whose specialization order is given by set inclusion;

2.

\mathcal{G}_{0}(X)=\langle\mathcal{COREG}(X);\cap,\sqcup;^{*},\emptyset,X,(\exists_{S_{i}})_{i\in I},(\Delta_{i,k})_{i,k}\rangle
is an
I
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebra.

Proof.

For part 1, it follows by
[
1
, Theorem 5.4]
that
\langle\mathfrak{F}(A);\tau(\beta)\rangle
is a UV-space whose specialization order is given by set inclusion if
A
is a Boolean algebra. It is obvious that
S_{i}
is an equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{F}(A)
for all
i\in I
.

To see that
S_{i}
commutes with
S_{k}
for all
i,k\in I
, assume that
\langle x,z\rangle\in S_{i}\circ S_{k}
so that
\exists_{i}[x]=\exists_{i}[y]
and
\exists_{k}[y]=\exists_{k}[z]
for some
y\in\mathfrak{F}(A)
. Assume
a\in\exists_{k}[x]
so that
a=\exists_{k}b
for some
b\in x
. Then
\exists_{k}b\in x
since
b\leq\exists_{k}b
and
x
is an upset. Hence
\exists_{k}b=a\in\exists_{i}[x]
and then by our hypothesis, we have
a\in\exists_{i}[y]
. A similar argument shows that
a\in\exists_{k}[y]
so that
\exists_{k}[x]\subseteq\exists_{k}[y]
. It can be similarly shown that
\exists_{k}[y]\subseteq\exists_{k}[x]
as well as
\exists_{i}[y]=\exists_{i}[z]
so that
S_{i}\circ S_{k}\subseteq S_{k}\circ S_{i}
. The proof of the
S_{k}\circ S_{i}\subseteq S_{i}\circ S_{k}
inclusion runs the same.

We now verify that
S_{k}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap\Delta_{k,l}]=\Delta_{i,l}
whenever
i,l\not=k
. First note that analogously to the proof of Lemma
3.3
we have
S_{k}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap\Delta_{k,l}]=S_{k}[\phi(\delta_{i,k})\cap\phi(\delta_{k,l})]=S_{k}[\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})]
and hence it suffices to demonstrate
S_{k}[\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})]=\Delta_{i,l}
. Assume that
y\in s_{k}[\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})]
so
xS_{k}y
and hence
\exists_{k}[x]=\exists_{k}[y]
for some
x\in\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})
. Then
\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l}\in x
and thus
\exists_{k}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})\in x
and hence we have
\exists_{k}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})\in y
since
\exists_{k}[x]=\exists_{k}[y]
. However
\exists_{k}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})=\delta_{i,l}
and thus
\delta_{i,l}\in y
so

. For the converse inclusion, suppose
y\in\Delta_{i,l}
so that

and hence
\delta_{i,l}\in y
. Now, conversely, suppose

. It suffices to find some

such that
\exists_{k}[x]=\exists_{k}[y]
. Notice that

since
\delta_{i,l}=\exists_{l}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})\geq\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l}
and by hypothesis we have
\delta_{i,l}\in y
and hence we have
\exists_{k}[x]=\exists_{k}[y]
for some
x\in\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})
, i.e., when
x=y
. Therefore, we conclude
y\in S_{k}[\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge\delta_{k,l})]
, as desired.

For condition 6, assume for the sake of contradiction that there exists some
y\in\mathfrak{F}(A)
such that
y\in S_{i}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap U]\cap S_{i}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap U^{*}]
. Then
y\in S_{i}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap U]
and
y\in S_{i}[\Delta_{i,k}\cap U]
which implies
xS_{i}y
and
zS_{i}y
and thus
\exists_{i}[x]=\exists_{i}[y]
and
\exists_{i}[z]=\exists_{i}[y]
for some
x\in\Delta_{i,k}\cap U
and some
z\in\Delta_{i,k}\cap U^{*}
. This, together with
[
1

, implies that
x\in\phi(\delta_{i,k})\cap\phi(a)=\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge a)
and
z\in\phi(\delta_{i,k})\cap\phi(a^{\perp})=\phi(\delta_{i,k}\wedge a^{\perp})
for some

. Therefore
\delta_{i,k}\wedge a\in x
and
\delta_{i,k}\wedge a^{\perp}\in z
, and since
\exists_{i}[x]=\exists_{i}[y]
and
\exists_{i}[z]=\exists_{i}[y]
, we have
\exists_{i}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge a)\in y
and
\exists_{i}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge a^{\perp})\in y
. Since

is a filter, we have
\exists_{i}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge a)\wedge\exists_{i}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge a^{\perp})\in y
but
\exists_{i}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge a)\wedge\exists_{i}(\delta_{i,k}\wedge a^{\perp})=0
and so

, which contradicts the fact that

is proper. Condition 8 follows from the definition of
S_{i}
in
\mathcal{F}_{0}(A)
and the fact that
\mathcal{COREG}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}(A)}(x)=\{\phi(c)\in\mathcal{COREG}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(A)):x\in\phi(c)\}
. Conditions 7 and 9 follow from Lemma
7.5
and
[
1
, Theorem 3.13]
.

For part 2, it follows by
[
1
, Proposition 5.3]
that
\langle\mathcal{COREG}(X);\cap,\sqcup,^{*},\emptyset,X\rangle
is a Boolean algebra whenever
X
is a UV-space. The proof that
\exists_{S_{i}}
is a quantifier on
\langle\mathcal{COREG}(X);\cap,\sqcup,^{*},\emptyset,X\rangle
follows analogously to
[
12
]
. Moreover, it is obvious that
\exists_{S_{i}}\exists_{S_{k}}U=\exists_{S_{k}}\exists_{S_{i}}U
for all

and each

since

and

are commuting relations. Conditions 4-8 of Definition

guarantee that

is a diagonal element in

for all

.
∎

The following lemma describes some useful properties of cylindric UV-spaces.

Lemma 7.6
.

If
X
is an
I
-dimensional cylindric UV-space, then:

1.

the specialization order
\leqslant
is a partial order;

2.

x\not\leqslant y
implies there exists
U\in\mathcal{COREG}(X)
such that
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
;

3.

xS_{i}y
implies
\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)]=\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(y)]
.

Proof.

Parts 1 and 2 follow analogously to conditions 1 and 2 of Lemma
4.4
. For part 3, assume that
xS_{i}y
for all

and let
U\in\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)]
. Then
U=\exists_{S_{i}}V
for some

and hence

. Since:

\exists_{S_{i}}V=S_{i}[V]=\{z\in X:wS_{i}z\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for some}\hskip 5.69046ptw\in V\}

it follows that

. Therefore
\exists_{S_{i}}V\in\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(y)
and since we have assumed that

, we have

. Thus we conclude that
\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)]\subseteq\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(y)]
. The
\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(y)]\subseteq\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)]
inclusions runs similarly by the symmetry of

.
∎

We now arrive at the topological representation theorem for
I
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebras and the algebraic realization theorem for
I
-dimensional cylindric UV-spaces.

Theorem 7.7
.

Every
I
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebra
A
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(A))
. Moreover, every
I
-dimensional cylindric UV-space
X
is relationally homeomorphic to
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathcal{G}_{0}(X))
.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Theorem 3.13]
the Boolean algebra reduct of
A
is isomorphic to the Boolean algebra reduct of
\mathcal{G}_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{0}(X))
under
\phi(a)=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A):a\in x\}
. Also
\phi(\delta_{i,k})=\Delta_{i,k}
and hence it remains to see that
\exists_{i}\mapsto\exists_{S_{i}}
is a homomorphic mapping under
\phi
. It suffices to show that
\phi(\exists_{i}a)=\exists_{S_{i}}\phi(a)
. Since we have
\phi(a)=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A):a\in x\}
, it follows that
S_{i}[\phi(a)]=\{x\in\mathfrak{F}(A):\exists_{i}a\in x\}
and hence we conclude
\phi(\exists_{i}a)=S_{i}[\phi(a)]=\exists_{S_{i}}\phi(a)
.

By
[
1
, Theorem 5.4]
, it follows that
X
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\mathcal{G}_{0}(X))
under
\psi(x)=\{U\in\mathcal{COREG}(X):x\in U\}
. To see that
\psi
is a relational isomorphism, i.e., that
xS_{i}
iff
\psi(x)S_{i}\psi(y)
for all
i\in I
, we begin with the left-to-right implication by assuming that
xS_{i}y
. By Lemma
7.6
, we have
\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(x)]=\exists_{S_{i}}[\mathcal{COREG}_{X}(y)]
, which by the definition of
\psi
implies
\exists_{S_{i}}[\psi(x)]=\exists_{S_{i}}[\psi(s)]
and therefore
\psi(x)S_{i}\psi(y)
. The converse implication follows by a contrapositive proof using condition 8 of Definition
7.3
.
∎

We now give a topological construction of the canonical completion of a cylindric Boolean algebra.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
A
is an
I
-dimensional cylindric Boolean algebra and let

be its dual
I
-dimensional cylindric UV-space. Then the completion
\langle\phi;\mathcal{REG}(X)\rangle
is its canonical completion.

Proof.

By Tarski
[
32
,
33
]
, it is known that
\mathcal{REG}(X)
forms a Boolean algebra for any topological space

. By
[

, Theorem 8.27]
, the function
\phi
is a dense and compact homomorphic embedding from the Boolean algebra reduct of

into the Boolean algebra reduct of

Conclusion and future work

We have extended the canonical completion results obtained in

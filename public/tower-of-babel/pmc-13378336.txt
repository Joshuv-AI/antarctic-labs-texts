Lnc RNA GAPLINC affects triple-negative breast cancer progression through the miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis
Authors: Wenya Wang, Lina Huang, Siyue Zhan, Ziguo Yan, Shaocong Yan, Guijun Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe functional mechanism of the long non-coding RNAs (lncRNA) GAPLINC in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains poorly understood. The present study aimed to explore GAPLINC in TNBC and its contribution to regulating tumor progression through the microRNA (miRNA)-331-3p/ insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 1(IGF2BP1) axis.MethodsTumor tissue samples and adjacent normal tissue samples were collected from 150 TNBC patients, and TNBC-related cell lines were cultured. mRNA expression was determined using reverse transcription quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). Functional parameters were assessed through the following experimental methods: the cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay was employed to evaluate proliferation capacity, the Transwell was conducted to assess migration and invasion, and the dual-luciferase assay was performed to confirm the targeted binding relationships.ResultsGAPLINC was significantly upregulated in TNBC tissues and cell lines (P < 0.001), and its expression was closely correlated with poor clinical prognosis. Similarly, miR-331-3p was markedly downregulated in TNBC samples (P < 0.001), while IGF2BP1 expression was significantly overexpressed (P < 0.001). Silencing GAPLINC effectively suppressed proliferation, migration, and invasion, accompanied by a notable upregulation miR-331-3p expression (P < 0.001). Importantly, inhibition of miR-331-3p partially reversed the suppressive effects induced by GAPLINC knockdown. Furthermore, miR-331-3p targeted IGF2BP1. Additional knockdown of IGF2BP1 counteracted this reversal effect, thereby effectively abrogating the tumor-promoting function of GAPLINC.ConclusionThis study identified GAPLINC as a crucial oncogenic lncRNA in TNBC. It promoted tumor progression by competitively binding to miR-331-3p, thereby derepressing its target IGF2BP1. These findings not only elucidated a new GAPLINC/miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 regulatory axis, but also highlighted that GAPLINC may be a potential prognostic biomarker for triple-negative breast cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04387-3.Keywords: lnc RNA GAPLINC, TNBC, miR-331-3p, IGF2BP1

Background

As a highly heterogeneous subtype [1, 2], triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is associated with higher recurrence rates, greater invasiveness [3, 4]. At present, the clinical management of TNBC primarily depends on surgical resection, adjuvant chemotherapy, and radiotherapy [5]. However, the limited number of effective treatment options results in suboptimal therapeutic outcomes [6, 7]. Therefore, the identification of reliable biomarkers is crucial for improving early diagnosis and prognostic assessment in TNBC patients.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) are two crucial classes of non-coding RNA molecules that play pivotal regulatory roles in various biological processes, including cancer development [8, 9]. LncRNAs usually have a length of more than 200 nucleotides and participate in the initiation and progression of tumors through multiple mechanisms, including transcriptional regulation, miRNA sponging [10, 11]. GAPLINC (Gastric Adenocarcinoma Associated, Positive Regulator of CD44 lncRNA) [12], first identified in gastric cancer [13], is significantly overexpressed in various malignancies, such as colorectal cancer [14], bladder cancer [15], and osteosarcoma [16], and is closely linked to poor clinical outcomes. For example, in gastric cancer, GAPLINC enhances the invasiveness of cancer cells by upregulating CD44 expression through sponging miR-211-3p [17]. In colorectal cancer, it facilitates tumor progression via the miR-34a/c-MET signaling pathway [18]. Bioinformatics analyses suggest that GAPLINC may distinguish metastatic from non-metastatic TNBC [19]. A clear mechanism is that lncRNAs function as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) or “molecular sponges”, which can bind to microRNAs and inhibit them [20]. miRNAs are small non-coding RNAs of approximately 22 nucleotides that post-transcriptionally silence target genes by binding to the 3’ untranslated regions (3’UTRs) of specific mRNAs [21]. The bioinformatics database indicates that GAPLINC targets and regulates miR-331-3p. Previous studies have confirmed that miR-331-3p suppresses cell proliferation in TNBC [22]. In oral cancer, miR-331-3p is sequestered by GAPLINC, resulting in increased expression of its target genes and promoting cancer stem cell-like properties [23]. The bioinformatics database indicates that miR-331-3p targets and inhibits insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 1(IGF2BP1). IGF2BP1 is dysregulated in multiple cancers, including cervical cancer [24, 25], lung cancer [26], and liver cancer [27]. In breast cancer, IGF2BP1 promotes tumor progression by regulating the stability and translation of target mRNAs through m⁶A RNA modification [28]. However, it remains unclear whether miR-331-3p regulates TNBC progression through targeting IGF2BP1, and whether GAPLINC participates in this regulatory mechanism. Based on the above background, this study hypothesizes that GAPLINC/miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis may facilitate TNBC progression.

In this study, GAPLINC expressions in TNBC tissues and cells were assessed, and the associations between GAPLINC expression and clinicopathological characteristics as well as patient prognosis were analyzed. Furthermore, we investigated the underlying molecular mechanism. Our findings revealed a novel regulatory axis in TNBC: GAPLINC promoted tumor progression by acting as a ceRNA for miR-331-3p, thereby upregulating the expression of the oncogenic RNA-binding protein IGF2BP1. This study provided the first evidence of the GAPLINC/miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis in TNBC, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target and prognostic marker.

Materials and methods

Study participants

Tumor tissue and adjacent normal tissue samples (at a distance of more than 5 cm from the tumor site) were collected from 150 TNBC patients who underwent surgical treatment at The First People’s Hospital of Jingdezhen. All patients were diagnosed with breast cancer via preoperative core needle biopsy or intraoperative frozen section pathological analysis, and all cases were confirmed as invasive breast cancer. The exclusion criteria: prior neoadjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy, brain metastasis requiring hormonal intervention, presence of severe or uncontrolled infection, and pregnancy or lactation.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First People’s Hospital of Jingdezhen (Approval No.2019-019), and all participants provided written informed consent.

Follow-up

Patients were followed up regularly over 5 years, with visits scheduled every 3 months, and all occurrences of recurrence and death were systematically recorded.

Transfection

Human normal breast epithelial cells (MCF10A, CRL-10317) and TNBC cell lines (MDA-MB-231 (HTB-26), MDA-MB-468 (HTB-132), BT-549 (HTB-122), BT-20 (HTB-19), all obtained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC)) were cultured in medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (HyClone) and maintained in a humidified incubator (37 °C, 5% CO₂). Given that GAPLINC expression was most pronounced in the MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468 cell lines, these two lines were selected for subsequent cellular experiments.

Small interfering RNA (siRNA)-GAPLINC, miR-331-3p mimic/inhibitor, and si-IGF2BP1 were synthesized by Ribobio Co., Ltd. (China) and transfected into TNBC cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen). The final concentrations of the miR-331-3p mimics and inhibitors used for transfection were both 50 nM. All transfections were performed when cells reached approximately 70–80% confluence during the logarithmic growth phase.

RT-qPCR

After surgical resection of the tumor tissue, a portion of the fresh tissue and adjacent normal breast tissue were immediately excised and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Total RNA was extracted using the Trizol (Invitrogen) method. The concentration and purity of the extracted RNA were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). RNA samples with an A260/A280 ratio between 1.8 and 2.0 and an A260/A230 ratio greater than 2.0 were considered acceptable. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized and amplification was performed with SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Takara). glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) and U6 small nuclear RNA (U6) were used as the internal reference. The relative expression levels of target genes and miRNA were calculated using the comparative 2−ΔΔCt method. All the RT-qPCR primers used in this study were synthesized by Shanghai GenePharma Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The sequences of the primers were as follows: GAPLINC Forward: ACACACAGCAGCCTGGTTTC, Reverse: ATGGCACAATCAGGGCTCTT; miR-331-3p Forward: GCGCCCCTGGGCCTATC, Reverse: CGATGACCTATGAATTGACA; IGF2BP1 Forward: CCGGAATTCGCCACCATGAACAAGCTTTACATCGGCAAC, Reverse: CCGCTCGAGTCACTTCCTCCGTGCCTGGGCCTG; MYC Forward: CGTCCTCGGATTCTCTGCTCTC, Reverse: GCGCTGCGTAGTTGTGCTGAT; GAPDH Forward: AGAACATCATCCCAGCGT, Reverse: AGCCTTCACTACCCTCTTG; U6 Forward: ACCCTGAGAAATACCCTCACAT, Reverse. GACGACTGAGCCCCTGATG.

Proliferation

Twenty-four hours after transfection, the cells were harvested, and their concentration was adjusted to 5 × 10³ cells per well, which were then seeded into a 96-well plate. At 0, 24, 48, and 72 h of culture, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution (Dojindo) was added to each well, and the optical density (OD) value was measured at 450 nm.

Migration and invasion

For migration assay, forty-eight hours after transfection, the cells were harvested. 200 µL of cell suspension lacking FBS was added to the upper chamber of the Transwell (Corning), while 600 µL of medium supplemented with FBS was added to the lower chamber. The migrating cells on the lower membrane were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, then stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 15 min. Photographs were taken under a microscope and the counts were made in five random fields of view.

For the invasion assay, Matrigel (Corning) was uniformly coated onto the upper chamber to simulate the extracellular matrix. Briefly, Matrigel was diluted at a 1:8 ratio with cold serum-free medium, and 100 µL of the mixture was added to the upper chamber and allowed to polymerize at 37 °C for 2 h before cell seeding. The subsequent steps were identical to those of the migration assay.

Dual luciferase assay

Wild-type (WT)-GAPLINC/IGF2BP1 and mutant (MUT)-GAPLINC/IGF2BP1 were constructed and synthesized by Guangzhou Ribobio Co., Ltd. These fragments were then cloned downstream of the firefly luciferase gene in the pGL3-Basic reporter vector (Promega, USA) to generate the WT-GAPLINC, MUT-GAPLINC, WT-IGF2BP1, and MUT-IGF2BP1 reporter plasmids, respectively. For the mutant constructs, the seed sequence complementary to miR-331-3p was mutated using site-directed mutagenesis. Logarithmic growth phase MDA-MB-231 or MDA-MB-468 cells were seeded in 24-well plates and cultured overnight under normal conditions. When the cell confluence reached approximately 70%, co-transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 2000. A transfection mixture containing the reporter plasmids of wild type (WT) or mutant type (MUT), as well as 50 nM of miR-331-3p mimics, miR-331-3p inhibitors, or their corresponding negative controls (NC) was added to each well. Luciferase activity was measured 48 h post-transfection using the dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Promega) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Gently wash the cells with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and perform lysis using 100 µl of the passive lysis buffer (provided in the dual luciferase assay kit). Quantify the luciferase activity using the dual luciferase reporter assay kit (Promega). The relative luciferase activity was calculated as the ratio of firefly luminescence to Renilla luminescence for each sample and normalized to the activity in the negative control group.

Western blot

Cells were harvested and centrifuged, and the resulting cell pellets were lysed in RIPA lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentrations of the lysates were quantified using a BCA protein assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific), followed by protein separation via sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). Separated proteins were transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes, which were then blocked and incubated with primary antibodies. Finally, protein bands were detected and analyzed using an ECL Western blotting kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Supplementary Table. 2).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 21.0. An independent samples t-test and a χ² test were conducted to analyze the differences between groups. The Pearson correlation coefficient was applied to evaluate the relationship between different expressions. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used to estimate patient survival prognosis. A Cox regression model was utilized to identify potential risk factors influencing patient outcomes. Each cell experiment was independently repeated at least three times. Statistical significance was defined as a P < 0.05.

Results

Clinical significance of GAPLINC in TNBC

GAPLINC was significantly higher in TNBC tissues (Fig. 1A). Based on GAPLINC expression, 150 TNBC patients were categorized into a low-expression group (69 cases, below the average value) and a high-expression group (81 cases, above the average value). Patients with high GAPLINC expression exhibited a greater likelihood of lymph node metastasis and were more likely to present with advanced disease stages (Table 1).

Results from the 5-year follow-up indicated that 43 patients (28.67%) experienced recurrence, and the GAPLINC expression in recurrence patients was significantly higher than in those without recurrence (Fig. 1B). Among these, 20 patients (13.33%) died, and deceased patients showed significantly higher GAPLINC expression compared to surviving patients (Fig. 1C). Further comparison of clinical features between the deceased group (n = 20) and the survival group (n = 130) demonstrated that lymph node metastasis and tumor, node, metastasis (TNM) stages III–IV were significantly associated with poorer survival outcomes, indicating a higher mortality risk and worse prognosis in these patients (Supplementary Table 1). Additionally, the survival probability of patients with high GAPLINC expression was significantly lower than that of patients with low GAPLINC expression, further supporting the association between elevated GAPLINC expression and poor prognosis in TNBC (HR = 4.591 95%CI = 1.90-11.08) (Fig. 1D). TNBC patients with high GAPLINC expression had significantly lower non-recurrence rate compared to those with low expression (HR = 2.808 95%CI = 1.54–5.12) (Fig. 1E). Cox regression revealed that both GAPLINC expression and TNM stage were independent prognostic factors in TNBC patients. In contrast, no statistically significant associations were observed between lymph node metastasis, degree of differentiation, tumor diameter, or age and overall survival (Fig. 1F; Table 2). To eliminate the potential interference among TNM staging, tumor diameter and lymph node metastasis, we included age, TNM staging and GAPLINC expression as three variables in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model for analysis. The results showed that only GAPLINC expression had a statistically significant impact on the risk of patient death (P = 0.036), with a hazard ratio HR = 3.799 (95% CI = 1.091–13.225), suggesting that high expression of GAPLINC was an independent risk factor for poor prognosis of patients; while the effects of age (P = 0.538) and TNM staging (P = 0.084) did not reach the level of statistical significance (Fig. 1G).

GAPLINC impacted TNBC cellular biological behaviors

In TNBC cell lines, GAPLINC expression was significantly elevated, and it was the highest in the MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468 cell lines (Supplementary Fig. 1A). Transfection with si-GAPLINC effectively reduced GAPLINC expression in TNBC cells (Supplementary Fig. 1B). Downregulation of GAPLINC significantly inhibited the proliferation (Supplementary Fig. 1C–D), migration, and invasion (Supplementary Fig. 1E-F) capabilities.

Interaction between GAPLINC and miR-331-3p

GAPLINC could specifically bind to miR-331-3p. In the WT-GAPLINC group, transfection with miR-mimic significantly reduced luciferase activity, whereas treatment with miR-inhibitor significantly increased it. In contrast, no significant differences in luciferase activity were observed among the treatment groups in the MUT-GAPLINC group (Figs. 2A–C). Furthermore, miR-331-3p expression in TNBC tissues was significantly lower (Figs. 2D), and a strong negative correlation was observed between GAPLINC and miR-331-3p expression (Figs. 2E). In TNBC cell lines, miR-331-3p was expressed at low levels (Figs. 2F). Knockdown of GAPLINC significantly upregulated miR-331-3p expression, and this effect could be reversed by co-transfection with a miR-331-3p inhibitor (Figs. 2G).

GAPLINC regulated the biological function of TNBC cells via miR-331-3p

After transfection with the miR-331-3p inhibitor, the cell proliferation rate, which had been suppressed by GAPLINC knockdown, was significantly restored (Figs. 3A–B). Furthermore, the number of migrating and invading cells also increased markedly (Figs. 3C–D).

The interaction between miR-331-3p and IGF2BP1

miR-331-3p specifically targeted IGF2BP1 (Figs. 4A–C). IGF2BP1 expression in TNBC tissues was significantly higher (Figs. 4D), and its expression was significantly and inversely correlated with that of miR-331-3p (Figs. 4E). In TNBC cell lines, IGF2BP1 mRNA and protein expression was markedly elevated (Figs. 4F-G). Furthermore, knockdown of GAPLINC significantly reduced IGF2BP1 mRNA and protein expression, and this reduction could be partially reversed by miR-331-3p inhibition. The effect could be further modulated by additional knockdown of IGF2BP1 (Figs. 4H-I).

Regulatory relationship of the GAPLINC/miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis in TNBC cells

The accelerated proliferation, enhanced migration, and invasion abilities of TNBC cells induced by the miR-331-3p inhibitor were markedly attenuated following IGF2BP1 knockdown. These effects were specifically reflected in a reduced cell proliferation rate (Figs. 5A–B) and diminished migration and invasion capacities (Figs. 5C–D). In the four treatment groups of TNBC cells: the si-NC group served as the control, and the si-GAPLINC group showed a significant decrease in MYC expression; after adding the miR-331-3p inhibitor, the MYC expression recovered compared to the si-GAPLINC group; further knockdown of IGF2BP1 led to a further decrease in MYC expression. This suggested that GAPLINC may regulate the expression of the downstream target MYC through the miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis (Figs. 5E–F).

Discussion

TNBC is characterized by poor clinical outcomes [29, 30]. Therefore, an in-depth understanding of its underlying molecular regulatory mechanisms is essential for the development of effective therapeutic strategies. The main findings of this study could be summarized as follows: First, GAPLINC was significantly upregulated in TNBC tissues and cell lines, and its high expression was closely associated with advanced clinicopathological features and poor patient prognosis. Second, in vitro functional experiments demonstrated that GAPLINC promoted TNBC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Third, mechanistically, GAPLINC acted as a molecular sponge for miR-331-3p, thereby relieving its repression on the downstream target IGF2BP1. Finally, rescue experiments confirmed that the GAPLINC/miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis played a crucial role in driving TNBC progression.

GAPLINC is an lncRNA that has been extensively reported to exert oncogenic effects [31, 32]. It’s abnormally high expression in gastric cancer [33], colorectal cancer [18], and bladder cancer [15], and its expression has been significantly associated with poor patient prognosis [14, 34]. This study found that GAPLINC was markedly overexpressed in TNBC tissues and cell lines. Its high expression was closely correlated with poor clinical outcomes. Knockdown of GAPLINC significantly inhibited the proliferation, migration, and invasion capacities of TNBC cells, indicating its pro-tumorigenic role in TNBC. Notably, during the cell line screening phase, GAPLINC expression in MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468 cells was found to be significantly higher than that in other TNBC cell lines. This observation suggested that GAPLINC may may vary in different types of TNBC cell lines.

One of the core mechanisms by which lncRNAs exert their biological functions is acting as “molecular sponges”, specifically binding to miRNAs to relieve their inhibitory effects on downstream target genes [35, 36]. At the mechanistic level, this study confirmed that GAPLINC negatively regulated miR-331-3p expression. miR-331-3p was downregulated in TNBC and exhibited a significant negative correlation with GAPLINC expression, a finding consistent with previous reports indicating that miR-331-3p suppresses cell proliferation by targeting NRP2 [22]. Furthermore, miR-331-3p targeted IGF2BP1. IGF2BP1 was highly expressed in TNBC and showed a negative correlation with miR-331-3p levels. As an RNA-binding protein, IGF2BP1 promoted tumor progression by stabilizing target gene mRNAs in various cancers [37, 38], and this study elucidated a novel regulatory mechanism underlying its oncogenic role in TNBC. Functional rescue experiments demonstrated that inhibition of miR-331-3p could reverse the suppressive effect of GAPLINC knockdown on TNBC cell malignancy, whereas further knockdown of IGF2BP1 counteracted this reversal. This ceRNA regulatory pattern resembles the mechanism of GAPLINC in other tumor types, such as gastric cancer [17] and oral cancer [23], which may suggest that GAPLINC exerts a conserved regulatory function across multiple cancers. By sponging miR-331-3p, GAPLINC decreased its effective concentration in TNBC cells, thereby alleviating the repression of IGF2BP1 and ultimately promoting tumor progression. Furthermore, we have discovered that GAPLINC was likely to regulate the expression of the downstream target gene MYC through the miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis. MYC is recognized as a key oncogene, and its mRNA stability is regulated by IGF2BP1 through m⁶A modification [39]. The carcinogenic effect of GAPLINC observed here is consistent with its reported functions in other malignant tumors, but it holds unique significance in the context of TNBC. The treatment gap in TNBC highlights the urgency of identifying actionable weaknesses. The central role of IGF2BP1 in the proposed axis is particularly notable. As an m⁶A “reader”, IGF2BP1 stabilizes the mRNA network encoding proteins involved in proliferation, metastasis, and treatment resistance [40]. We found that GAPLINC regulated MYC - a major transcription factor and a typical target of IGF2BP1 - through this axis, providing a direct link between lncRNA-mediated ceRNA activity and the stability of the core oncogenic transcriptome. This suggested that the oncogenic effect of GAPLINC may be amplified by simultaneously enhancing multiple cancer characteristics through IGF2BP1, providing a mechanistic explanation for its strong association with aggressive clinical features. This discovery linked the upstream ceRNA regulatory network with the downstream specific and functionally defined oncogenic signaling pathways. It not only confirmed the biological function of this axis but also provided a more specific downstream mechanism for it to be used as a therapeutic target.

Clinically, GAPLINC may serve as a prognostic biomarker for TNBC. Beyond mechanistic insight, our study prompts important translational considerations. First, GAPLINC expression holds immediate promise as a prognostic biomarker. Integrating GAPLINC assessment with standard clinicopathological parameters could refine risk stratification, identifying a subset of TNBC patients with intrinsically worse outcomes who may benefit from more intensive surveillance or adjuvant therapy. Second, the GAPLINC/miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis itself presents a novel therapeutic frontier. Nevertheless, certain limitations should be acknowledged. First, the clinical samples were retrospectively collected from a single center with a relatively limited sample size. This may introduce selection bias and potentially affect the generalizability and reliability of the observed correlations between GAPLINC expression and clinical prognosis. To address this, future research should prioritize multi-center collaboration to expand the sample size and conduct prospective validation, which would enhance the statistical power and external validity of the findings. Second, this study employed a cross-sectional design, measuring the expression levels of GAPLINC, miR-331-3p, and IGF2BP1 in tissue samples at a single time point. This approach cannot capture the dynamic changes of this molecular axis during tumor initiation, progression, or in response to therapeutic interventions. Consequently, whether the observed dysregulation was a cause or a consequence of TNBC progression, and how it evolved during disease recurrence or treatment, remains unclear. This represented an important caveat in interpreting our findings. Future longitudinal studies, analyzing serial samples (e.g., from core biopsy at diagnosis, post-neoadjuvant therapy, and at recurrence) from the same patients, were needed to delineate the temporal dynamics of this axis. Third, this mechanism verification was mainly conducted in two triple-negative breast cancer cell lines (MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468). The results obtained from these two models may not fully represent all cases of triple-negative breast cancer. The general applicability of the GAPLINC/miR-331-3p/IGF2BP1 axis proposed in this study in other triple-negative breast cancer subtypes and cell lines requires further verification. Fourth, this study lacks in vivo experimental validation. All functional conclusions regarding the oncogenic role of GAPLINC and the regulatory axis were drawn from in vitro cell culture models. While these models are invaluable for elucidating molecular mechanisms, they cannot recapitulate the complexity of the physiological tumor microenvironment (TME), which encompasses stromal cell interactions, immune system modulation, angiogenesis, and systemic metabolic influences. The absence of in vivo data, such as from nude mouse xenograft models, significantly limits the physiological and pathological relevance of our findings. We cannot confirm whether the pro-tumorigenic effects of GAPLINC observed in cell plates translate to actual impacts on tumor growth, local invasion, or distant metastasis within a living organism. Consequently, the therapeutic potential of targeting this axis remains speculative without in vivo proof-of-concept. To address this fundamental limitation, our immediate follow-up research will prioritize in vivo validation. In the future, the planned experiments include: (1) Establishing a subcutaneous xenograft model in immunodeficient mice using MDA-MB-231 cells with stable GAPLINC knockdown or overexpression, to directly assess the effect of GAPLINC on tumor growth kinetics in vivo; and (2) Employing tail vein injection models to evaluate the role of GAPLINC in the formation of lung metastases. These in vivo studies are essential to bridge our mechanistic findings to a biologically relevant context and are a prerequisite for any future translational development. Additionally, it was crucial to explore potential intervention strategies targeting GAPLINC and evaluate their synergistic effects with existing therapeutic approaches.

Conclusion

This study not only elucidated the expression characteristics and clinical significance of GAPLINC in TNBC but also uncovered its underlying molecular mechanism in promoting TNBC progression: GAPLINC promoted the TNBC progression by upregulating IGF2BP1 expression through the sponging of miR-331-3p.

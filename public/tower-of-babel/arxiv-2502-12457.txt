Well-Posedness and Asymptotic Decay of Solutions to the Three-Dimensional Euler Equations with Damping
Gui-Qiang G. Chen, Feimin Huang, Houzhi Tang, Shuxing Zhang, Weiyuan Zou

Abstract
. The global well-posedness of the multi-dimensional compressible Euler equations with damping remains a longstanding open problem. This problem has been partially resolved in the isentropic regime ( i.e. , the adiabatic exponent γ > 1 \gamma>1 ) for small smooth initial data (see [ 31 , 29 ] ). In this paper, we establish the global well-posedness and asymptotic decay of smooth solutions of the Cauchy problem of the three-dimensional compressible Euler equations with damping for the isentropic regime γ > 1 \gamma>1 and the isothermal regime γ = 1 \gamma=1 , allowing for partially large initial data. More precisely, the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the initial data is allowed to be large, while the third-order Sobolev norm of the initial data is assumed to be small. For the isentropic case, we develop a new analytical framework in which all required a priori estimates of solution ( ρ , u ) (\rho,u) can be derived under the condition that ∫ 0 T ( ‖ ∇ ρ ‖ L ∞ + ‖ ∇ u ‖ L ∞ ) ​ 𝑑 t \int_{0}^{T}\big(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t remains sufficiently small. Moreover, we obtain the optimal algebraic decay rates of global solutions. Furthermore, we study the isothermal limit of solutions of the isentropic regime as γ → 1 \gamma\to 1 , and establish the global well-posedness and asymptotic decay of solutions to the isothermal Euler equations with damping.

1.
Introduction

The three-dimensional Euler equations with damping can be written as

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P(\rho)=-\rho u,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

which model compressible gas flow through a porous medium. Here, the unknown functions
\rho=\rho(t,x)
and
u=u(t,x)
denote the density and velocity of the fluid in the domain
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, respectively. The pressure
P:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by the
barotropic pressure law

P=P(\rho)=a_{0}\rho^{\gamma}\qquad\mbox{\text{for $a_{0}>0$}},

where
\gamma>1
is the adiabatic exponent, while the case
\gamma=1

corresponds to the isothermal case.
Without loss of generality, we normalize
a_{0}=1
for simplicity.
System (
1.1
) is equipped with the initial data:

(1.2)

(\rho,u)(0,x)=(\rho_{0},u_{0})(x),

and the far-field states:

(1.3)

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}(\rho,u)(t,x)=(\rho_{*},0),

where
\rho_{*}>0
is a given constant.

The global existence of solutions to the
\gamma
-law Euler equations is a challenging problem
because the solution develops singularities, such as shocks, in finite time, regardless of how small and smooth the initial data are. For the one-dimensional case, Nishida
[
26
]
used the Glimm difference scheme to prove the global existence of BV solutions with large initial data for
\gamma=1
as the gas is away from the vacuum. This result was extended to the case
\gamma>1
by Nishida-Smoller
[
28
]
when
\gamma
is close 1.
When vacuum occurs, DiPerna
[
6
]
first established the global existence of solutions with large initial data in
L^{\infty}
for
\gamma=1+\frac{2}{2n+1}
, where
n\geq 2
denotes the number of degrees of freedom
of the molecules.
Later, the existence problem was solved by Ding-Chen-Luo
[
3
,
4
]
for
\gamma\in(1,\frac{5}{3}]
, Lions-Perthame-Tadmor
[
20
]

for
\gamma\in[3,\infty)
,
and Lions-Perthame-Souganidis
[
19
]
for
\gamma\in(\frac{5}{3},3)
.
The
\gamma=1
case was treated by Huang-Wang
[
17
]
.
On the other hand, the global existence of solutions with
large initial data in the multidimensional (M-D) case remains open.

It is known that the damping can improve the regularity of solutions; see
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
27
,
29
,
16
,
18
,
33
]
. That is, the damping prevents the development of singularities in some sense and leads to the global existence of smooth solutions for sufficiently small initial data. However, the damping cannot prevent the formation of singularities for large initial data; see
[
21
,
22
]
. The entropy solution has to be considered; see
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
.

It is fundamental to further study the regularity of the damping structure. In this paper, we establish the global well-posedness and asymptotic decay of smooth solutions of the Cauchy problem
of the M-D compressible Euler equations with damping for some partially large initial data,
i.e.
,
\|(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}
is allowed to be large, but
\|D^{3}(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}
is necessarily small. Our results consist of two parts. In the first part, concerning the isentropic regime (
\gamma>1
), the precise statement is following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let the initial data
(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfy
0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho_{0}\leq\bar{\rho}<\infty
for some positive constants
\underline{\rho}
and
\bar{\rho}
,

(1.4)

\|(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq M_{0}\;,\qquad\qquad\|D^{3}(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta_{0},

where
\delta_{0}>0
is small and
0<M_{0}\leq\delta_{0}^{-\frac{1}{11}}
. Then the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) for the isentropic case, i.e.,
\gamma>1
, admits a unique global classical solution

(\rho-\rho_{*},u)\in C([0,\infty);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
satisfying

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|D^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|D^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+1}{2}},\quad

for
k=0,1,2,3
, where
C=C(M_{0},\underline{\rho},\bar{\rho},\delta_{0})
is a positive constant independent of
t\geq 0
.

The equation of state for isothermal gases can be regarded as the limit of that for isentropic gases as
\gamma\to 1
. Hence, the second main result of this paper addresses the isothermal limit of the isentropic damped Euler system (
1.1
)-–(
1.3
).

Theorem 1.2

(Isothermal Limit)
.

Assume that the conditions in
Theorem
1.1
hold.
Then, as
\gamma\to 1
, the solutions of the Cauchy
problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) for the isentropic regime (
\gamma>1
) strongly converge to the solution of the corresponding problem for
\gamma=1
.
Moreover, the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
)
for
\gamma=1
is a unique global classical solution

(\rho-\rho_{*},u)\in C([0,\infty);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
satisfying (
1.5
)–(
1.6
).

Remark 1.1
.

Theorems
1.1
–
1.2
admit the following initial data:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}=\rho_{*}+\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{11}}e^{-\varepsilon^{\frac{8}{11}}|x|^{2}},\qquad\qquad u_{0}=\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{11}}e^{-\varepsilon^{\frac{8}{11}}|x|^{2}},

where
\varepsilon<\delta_{0}
is small. Indeed,
\|(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}=O(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{11}})\rightarrow\infty\;\text{as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0$}
, thus the
L^{2}
-norm of the initial data can be large.

Remark 1.2
.

The decay rates obtained in Theorems
1.1
–
1.2
are optimal, which are the same as the ones of the solution to the corresponding linear system.

The strategies of the proofs are outlined as follows: Firstly, for the isentropic case
\gamma>1
, we introduce a new function

(1.8)

\phi=\frac{2}{\gamma-1}\big(\sigma(\rho)-\sigma(\rho_{*})\big),

where
\sigma(\rho)=\sqrt{P^{\prime}(\rho)}
is the sound speed and
\sigma(\rho_{*})=\bar{\sigma}
corresponds to the sound speed at the background density
\rho_{*}
.
Then system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) can be reformulated as a symmetric hyperbolic system with partial damping:

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\phi+\bar{\sigma}\,{\mathrm{div}}u=-u\cdot\nabla\phi-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\phi\,{\mathrm{div}}u,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}u+\bar{\sigma}\,\nabla\phi+u=-u\cdot\nabla u-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\phi\nabla\phi.\end{cases}

For this system, the key observation is that all
L^{2}
-norm of the
k
-order derivatives of the solution can be controlled by the
k
-order derivative of the initial data. More precisely, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.10)

\|D^{k}(\phi,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|D^{k}(\phi_{0},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\qquad\text{for \,$k=0,1,2,3,$}

provided that

(1.11)

\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

is small. Then, by the analysis of the Green function
G_{ij}
for the linearized system of (
1.9
) (see (
3.33
) below), we obtain that, for any test function
\zeta
,

(1.12)

\|D^{k}G_{ij}^{\ell}\ast\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\begin{cases}C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}&\text{for \;$i+j=2$},\\[2.84526pt]
C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+1}{2}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}&\text{for \;$i+j=3$},\\
C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+2}{2}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\quad&\text{for \;$i+j=4$},\end{cases}

where
G^{\ell}_{ij}
corresponds to the low-frequency part. Based on (
1.12
) and the Duhamel principle, we can further obtain

\|D^{k}\phi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\qquad\text{for \;$k=1,2,3.$}

Using the energy method again, we have

(1.13)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|(D^{k}\phi,D^{k}u)\|_{H^{3-k}}^{2}+\|(D^{k}\phi,D^{k}u)\|_{H^{3-k}}^{2}\leq C\|D^{k}\phi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\qquad\text{for \;$k=1,2,3,$}

which implies

(1.14)

\|D^{k}(\phi,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\qquad\text{for \;$k=1,2,3.$}

On the other hand, since
u
is only related to
G_{21}
and
G_{22}
, (
1.12
) hints that
\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}
should have the higher decay rate than
(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}
. Indeed, we decompose
u
into the low-frequency part
u^{\ell}
and the high-frequency part
u^{h}
. With the help of the damping structure, we obtain from (
1.12
) and the Duhamel principle that

\|D^{k}u^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+1}{2}}.

Meanwhile, the energy estimate of the high-frequency part of (
1.9
) implies that
\|D^{k}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}
has better decay rate than the one for the low frequency part. Hence, we obtain a better decay rate for
D^{k}u
, which is optimal, since it is the same as that of the linearized equation,
i.e.
,

(1.15)

\|D^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+1}{2}}\qquad\text{for \;$k=1,2,3.$}

Finally, it remains to show that
\int_{0}^{T}(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}})\,\mathrm{d}t
is small. This can be directly verified by the optimal decay rates (
1.14
)–(
1.15
) under the condition that
\|D^{3}(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}
is small, but
\|(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}
can be large.

Moreover, the method for the isentropic case cannot be directly applied to the isothermal case since transform (
1.8
) is singular when
\gamma=1
. This is why we need to study the isothermal limit of the isentropic damped Euler equations. The method of studying the isothermal limit is based on the compactness argument. Firstly, in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can obtain a crucial by-product: the uniform estimates of solutions with respect to
\gamma\in(1,3]
. Secondly, by applying the Aubin–Lions lemma, we obtain the convergence of the solution sequence as
\gamma\to 1
, which allows us to rigorously justify the isothermal limit. The global well-posedness and asymptotic decay of solutions
to the isothermal damped Euler equations can be regarded as a direct consequence of this isothermal limit.

Furthermore, we point out that the global well-posedness and asymptotic decay of
solutions to the isothermal damped Euler equations can be established by a similar argument used in Theorem
1.1
. To be specific,
according to transform (
1.8
), we take the limit to obtain

\displaystyle\underset{\gamma\to 1}{\lim}\phi(\rho,\gamma)=\underset{\gamma\to 1}{\lim}\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}\big(\rho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}-\rho_{*}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}\big)}{\gamma-1}=\ln\rho-\ln\rho_{*}=:\hat{\phi},

and then pass to the limit in (
1.9
) to obtain

(1.16)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\hat{\phi}+u\cdot\nabla\hat{\phi}+\mathrm{div}u=0,\\
&\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla\hat{\phi}+u=0,\end{aligned}\right.

for
(\hat{\phi},u)
.
It is direct to verify that this system has the same form as the following compressible isothermal Euler equations with damping:

(1.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla\rho=-\rho u.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Then, after necessary modifications, we can also use a similar method used in proving Theorem
1.1
to obtain the well-posedness and asymptotic decay
of solutions to system (
1.17
).
In fact, system (
1.16
) has a simpler form than (
1.9
) due to fewer nonlinear terms after the new transform:
\hat{\phi}=\mathrm{ln}\rho-\mathrm{ln}\rho_{*}
. Then we can apply the method developed in Theorem
1.1
.

3.
Global Well-Posedness And Asymptotic Decay
For The Isentropic Case:
\gamma>1

3.1.
Regularity Criterion

In this section, we establish the following regularity criterion for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the initial data
(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
For any
T>0
, let
(\rho,u)\in C([0,T];H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be a local solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) and
\phi
as defined in (
1.8
).
If

(3.1)

\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\delta

holds for some small constant
\delta>0
, then the solution
(\rho,u)
can be extended beyond
T
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem under the
a priori
assumption (
3.1
).
The proof is divided into three steps: first, we derive uniform upper and lower bounds for the density; second, we establish an
H^{3}
energy estimate for
(\phi,u)
; finally, we use the equivalence between
\phi
and
\rho-\rho_{*}
to obtain the corresponding estimate for
(\rho-\rho_{*},u)
.

1. Uniform bounds for the density.

Under ssumption (
3.1
), the density
\rho(t,x)
satisfies

(3.2)

e^{-\delta}\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq e^{\delta}\overline{\rho}\qquad\text{for any $t\in[0,T]$}.

This can be derived as follows:
Define the particle trajectory
X(\tau;s,y)
passing through
(s,y)
by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\mathrm{d}X(\tau;s,y)}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=u(\tau;s,y),\\
&X(s;s,y)=y.\end{aligned}\right.

It follows from (
1.1
)
1
that, along the particle trajectory
X(\tau;s,y)
, the directional derivative of the density is

\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho(\tau,X(\tau;s,y))}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=-\rho(\tau,X(\tau;s,y))\mathrm{div}\,u(\tau,X(\tau;s,y)).

Solving the above equation yields

\rho(t,X(t;s,y))=\rho(0,X(0;s,y))e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{div}\,u(\tau,X(\tau;s,y))\mathrm{d}\tau}\qquad\text{for any $t\in[0,T]$}.

Then, combining (
3.1
) and
\rho_{0}\in[\underline{\rho},\bar{\rho}]
, we obtain (
3.2
).

2. Energy estimates for
(\phi,u)
.

We next establish an
H^{3}
estimate for
(\phi,u)
. After a normalization, we may assume without loss of generality that
\rho_{*}=1
in Section
3
. By the uniform bounds in (
3.2
) and the definition of
\phi
in (
1.8
), system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\phi+\sqrt{\gamma}\,{\mathrm{div}}u=-u\cdot\nabla\phi-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\phi\mathrm{div}\,u,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}u+\sqrt{\gamma}\,\nabla\phi+u=-u\cdot\nabla u-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\phi\nabla\phi,\end{array}\right.

with the initial data:

(3.5)

(\phi,u)|_{t=0}=(\phi_{0},u_{0})\qquad\,\,\mbox{for $\phi_{0}=\frac{2}{\gamma-1}(\sigma(\rho_{0})-\sigma(1))$},

and the far-field states:

(3.6)

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}(\phi,u)=(0,0).

Under assumption (
3.1
), we have

(3.7)

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|(\phi,u)(t)\|^{2}_{H^{3}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{H^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq e^{(C+(\gamma-1)\hat{C})}\|(\phi_{0},u_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{3}},

where
C
and
\hat{C}
are positive constants independent of
\gamma-1
and time.

Indeed, we first take the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
3.1
) with
(\phi,u)
, which yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|(\phi,u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\,u\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot u\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\phi^{2}\mathrm{div}u+u\cdot\phi\nabla\phi\,\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathrm{div}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{\gamma-1}{4}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\gamma-1}{4})\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}).

For higher-order estimates, applying
D^{k}
,
k=1,2,3
, to (
3.1
) yields

(3.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}D^{k}\phi+u\cdot\nabla D^{k}\phi+\sqrt{\gamma}\;\mathrm{div}D^{k}u=-[D^{k},u\cdot\nabla]\phi-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\,[D^{k},\phi]\,\mathrm{div}u-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\,\phi\,D^{k}\mathrm{div}u,\\
&\partial_{t}D^{k}u+u\cdot\nabla D^{k}u+\sqrt{\gamma}\,\nabla D^{k}\phi+D^{k}u=-[D^{k},u\cdot\nabla]u-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\,[D^{k},\phi]\nabla\phi-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\,\phi\,D^{k}\nabla\phi.\end{aligned}\right.

Taking the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
3.9
) and
(D^{k}\phi,D^{k}u)
, and using Lemma
2.1
, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|(D^{k}\phi,D^{k}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{k}\phi\cdot\big(-[D^{k},u\cdot\nabla]\phi-u\cdot\nabla D^{k}\phi-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\,[D^{k},\phi]\,\mathrm{div}u-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\,\phi\,D^{k}\mathrm{div}u\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{k}u\cdot\big(-[D^{k},u\cdot\nabla]u-u\cdot\nabla D^{k}u-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\,[D^{k},\phi]\nabla\phi-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}\,\phi\,D^{k}\nabla\phi\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}\big)+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C(\gamma-1)\big(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+C(\gamma-1)(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\big(D^{k}\mathrm{div}uD^{k}\phi+D^{k}\nabla\phi\cdot D^{k}u\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

(3.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{C}(\gamma-1)(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}})\big(\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big),

where we have used the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\big(D^{k}(\mathrm{div}u)D^{k}\phi+D^{k}\nabla\phi\cdot D^{k}u\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\,\mathrm{div}(D^{k}\phi D^{k}u)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\phi\cdot(D^{k}\phi D^{k}u)\mathrm{d}x

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big).

Summing the above inequality with respect to
k
from
0
to
3
gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|(\phi,u)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|u\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\leq(C+(\gamma-1)\hat{C})(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|(\phi,u)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}.

Using the Grönwall inequality and
assumption (
3.1
), we obtain

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|(\phi,u)(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq e^{C\delta+(\gamma-1)\hat{C}\delta}\|(\phi_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{3}}^{2},

which together with the smallness of
\delta
yields (
3.7
).

3. Equivalence between
\phi
and
\rho-1
.

For simplicity, we write
\tilde{C}=e^{C+\hat{C}(\gamma-1)}
for brevity. The explicit dependence on
\gamma-1
plays a key role in proving the isentropic limit of the solutions.
We first prove that, under assumption (
3.1
),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|(\rho-1,u)(t)\|^{2}_{H^{3}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{H^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\tilde{C}\|(\rho_{0}-1,u_{0})\|^{2}_{H^{3}},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|D^{3}(\rho-1,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{0}.

In fact, we need to prove

(3.14)

\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|D^{k}(\rho-1)\|_{L^{2}}\qquad\text{for ~\,$k=0,1,2,3$}.

Recall that

(3.15)

\phi=\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\bigl(\rho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}-1\bigr).

For
k=0
, by the mean value theorem, there exists
\tilde{\rho}
between
1
and
\rho
such that

(3.16)

\phi=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{\gamma-3}{2}}(\rho-1).

Since
\rho\in\bigl(e^{-\delta}\underline{\rho},e^{\delta}\overline{\rho}\bigr)
, then
\tilde{\rho}
is uniformly bounded from above and below. It follows immediately that

(3.17)

\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|\rho-1\|_{L^{2}}.

For the first-order derivatives, differentiating (
3.15
), we obtain

(3.18)

D\phi=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\rho^{\frac{\gamma-3}{2}}D\rho.

Again, by the uniform upper and lower bounds of
\rho
, we deduce

(3.19)

\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|D\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

We now turn to the second-order derivatives. Differentiating (
3.18
) once more yields

(3.20)

D^{2}\rho=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\,\rho^{\frac{3-\gamma}{2}}D^{2}\phi+\frac{3-\gamma}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\,\rho^{\frac{\gamma-5}{2}}(D\rho)^{2},

so that

(3.21)

\|D^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|D\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\big).

Similarly, from (
3.20
),

(3.22)

\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|D^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|D\rho\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\big).

It remains to control
\|D\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2}
. By (
3.18
), the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality, and (
3.7
),

(3.23)

\|D\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq C\|D\phi\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq C\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\widetilde{C}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand,

(3.24)

\|D\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq C\|D\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D\rho\|_{L^{6}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\widetilde{C}\|D^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

Substituting (
3.23
)–(
3.24
) into
(
3.21
)–(
3.22
), we have

(3.25)

\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|D^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

For the third-order derivatives, one differentiates (
3.18
) once more. The resulting expression consists of the leading linear term
\rho^{\frac{\gamma-3}{2}}D^{3}\rho
and the lower-order nonlinear terms involving the products of derivatives of
\rho
up to second order. Using the already established bounds (
3.19
) and (
3.25
), together with the uniform bounds on
\rho
, these lower-order terms can be controlled in the same way as above. Therefore, we have

(3.26)

\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\sim\|D^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining (
3.17
), (
3.19
), and (
3.25
)–(
3.26
), we obtain (
3.14
).

Next, recalling the initial assumption in (
1.4
),

(3.27)

\|D^{3}(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta_{0},

it follows from (
3.14
) that

(3.28)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|D^{3}(\phi,u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{0}.

Together with (
3.1
), this yields (
3.13
).

Finally, using
M_{0}\leq\delta_{0}^{-1/11}
, the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality gives

(3.29)

\displaystyle\|Du\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq\tilde{C}M_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{3}{11}},

(3.30)

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq\tilde{C}M_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{7}{11}}.

Theorem
3.1
follows directly from (
3.12
).
∎

3.2.
Decay Rates of the Solution

In this section, we establish the optimal decay rates of the solution and then employ these to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Let
U=(\phi,u)^{\top}
and
U_{0}=(\phi_{0},u_{0})^{\top}
. We rewrite system (
3.1
)–(
3.5
) as the following vector form

(3.31)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}U+\mathcal{L}U=F,\\
&U(0,x)=U_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathcal{L}
and
F
are given by

\mathcal{L}=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{0}&{{\sqrt{\gamma}\mathrm{div}}}\\
{\sqrt{\gamma}\nabla}&{I}\end{array}}\right),\qquad\quad F=\left(\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{-u\cdot\nabla\phi-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\phi{\mathrm{div}}u}\\
{-u\cdot\nabla u-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\phi\nabla\phi}\end{array}\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{f_{1}}\\
{f_{2}}\end{array}\right).

The solution of (
3.31
) can be expressed as

(3.32)

U=G\ast U_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}G(t-\tau)\ast F(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau,

where

G(t,x)=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{G_{11}}&{G_{12}}\\
{G_{21}}&{G_{22}}\end{array}}\right)

denotes the Green function of the linear operator
\mathcal{L}
. Although the Green function have been investigated in
[
29
]
, we still present some additional properties of the Green function and the process of proof for completeness.

The Green function
G
satisfies the following linear system:

(3.33)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}G+\mathcal{L}G=0,\\
&G(0,x)=\delta(x)I,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\delta(x)
is the standard Dirac delta function. Taking the Fourier transform of the above system yields

(3.34)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\hat{G}+\hat{\mathcal{L}}\hat{G}=0,\\
&\hat{G}(0,\xi)=I,\end{aligned}\right.

where

\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{0}&{i\sqrt{\gamma}\xi^{\top}}\\
{i\sqrt{\gamma}\xi}&{I}\end{array}}\right).

We can calculate the eigenvalues of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
from

|\lambda I+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}|{\rm{=}}\left|{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{\lambda}&{i\sqrt{\gamma}\xi}\\
{i\sqrt{\gamma}\xi}&({\lambda+1})I\\
\end{array}}\right|=(\lambda+1)^{2}(\lambda^{2}+\lambda+\gamma|\xi|^{2})=0,

to obtain

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=-1,\quad\lambda_{3}=-\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\gamma|\xi|^{2}}}{2},\qquad\lambda_{4}=-\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\gamma|\xi|^{2}}}{2}.

For the eigenvalues
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
, we have

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r_{0}
be a small positive constant satisfying
r_{0}<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}<\frac{1}{2}
.

•

When
|\xi|<r_{0}
, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
satisfy

(3.35)

\lambda_{3}=-\gamma|\xi|^{2}+O(|\xi|^{4}),\quad\lambda_{4}=-1+\gamma|\xi|^{2}+O(|\xi|^{4}).

•

When
|\xi|\geq r_{0}
, it holds that

(3.36)

{\rm{Re}}\lambda_{i}\leq-r_{0}^{2}\qquad\text{for $i=3,4.$}

Proof.

For
|\xi|<r_{0}
, (
3.35
) is directly obtained by using the Taylor expansion formula.
For
|\xi|>\frac{1}{2}
,
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
are complex roots and

{\rm{Re}}\lambda_{3}={\rm{Re}}\lambda_{4}=-\frac{1}{2}.

For
r_{0}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
are real roots and satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\gamma|\xi|^{2}}}{2}=-\frac{2\gamma|\xi|^{2}}{1+\sqrt{1-4\gamma|\xi|^{2}}}\leq-\gamma|\xi|^{2}\leq-r_{0}^{2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\gamma|\xi|^{2}}}{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}.

Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

Based on the above eigenvalue estimates, we have the following estimates of the Green function.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any smooth function
\zeta
, the following estimates hold for any integer
k\geq 0
,

(3.37)

\|D^{k}G_{ij}\ast\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\begin{cases}C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\big(\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{k}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\big)&\text{for \;$i+j=2$},\\[2.84526pt]
C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+1}{2}}\big(\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{k}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\big)\quad&\text{for \;$i+j=3$},\\
C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+2}{2}}\big(\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{k}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\big)\quad&\text{for \;$i+j=4$},\end{cases}

where
i,j\in\{1,2\}
and the above
C
depends on
\gamma
but does not have a singularity as
\gamma\to 1
. The low-frequency part of
G_{ij}
satisfies

(3.38)

\|D^{k}G_{ij}^{\ell}\ast\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\begin{cases}C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}&\text{for \;$i+j=2$},\\[2.84526pt]
C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+1}{2}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\quad&\text{for \;$i+j=3$},\\
C(1+t)^{-\frac{k+2}{2}}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\quad&\text{for \;$i+j=4$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

A direct computation gives the exact expression of the Fourier transform of
G
as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{G}(t,\xi)=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{\hat{G}_{11}}&{\hat{G}_{12}}\\
{\hat{G}_{21}}&{\hat{G}_{22}}\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{\frac{\lambda_{3}e^{\lambda_{4}t}-\lambda_{4}e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}}&{-i\xi^{\top}\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}}\\
{-i\xi\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}}&{e^{-t}I+\big(\frac{\lambda_{3}e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\lambda_{4}e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}-e^{-t}\big)\frac{\xi\xi^{\top}}{|\xi|^{2}}}\end{array}}\right).

For any smooth function
\zeta
, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{k}G_{11}\ast\zeta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\|(i\xi)^{k}\hat{G}_{11}\hat{\zeta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\Big(\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}+\int_{|\xi|\geq r_{0}}\Big)|(i\xi)^{k}\hat{G}_{11}\hat{\zeta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}|(i\xi)^{k}e^{-|\xi|^{2}t}\hat{\zeta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi+C\int_{|\xi|\geq r_{0}}|(i\xi)^{k}e^{-r^{2}_{0}t}\hat{\zeta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\|\widehat{D^{k}H}\hat{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|e^{-r^{2}_{0}t}\widehat{\delta(x)}\widehat{D^{k}\zeta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|D^{k}H\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Ce^{-2r^{2}_{0}t}\|D^{k}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-k}\big(\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|D^{k}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big),

where
H(t,x)=(4\pi{t})^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}
is the heat kernel and
\delta(x)
is a Dirac delta function. It should be emphasized that all constants
C
appearing in the above estimates may depend on the adiabatic exponent
\gamma
, but are uniformly bounded with respect to
\gamma\in(1,3]
.

This uniformity follows from the fact that

\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}=\sqrt{1-4\gamma|\xi|^{2}}

remains strictly positive for all
|\xi|<r_{0}
, where
r_{0}>0
is chosen, independent of
\gamma
. Moreover, if
|\xi|\rightarrow\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}
, then
\lambda_{3}\rightarrow\lambda_{4}
so that

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda_{3}\rightarrow\lambda_{4}}{\frac{\lambda_{3}e^{\lambda_{4}t}-\lambda_{4}e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}}=(1-\lambda_{4}t)e^{\lambda_{4}t},

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda_{3}\rightarrow\lambda_{4}}\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}=te^{\lambda_{4}t},

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda_{3}\rightarrow\lambda_{4}}\frac{\lambda_{3}e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\lambda_{4}e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}=(1+\lambda_{4}t)e^{\lambda_{4}t}.

Then it is deduced that, when
|\xi|\rightarrow\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}
, the Green function has no singularity point, which implies that it is bounded.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\|D^{k}G_{11}\ast\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\big(\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{k}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}\big).

Furthermore,
\eqref{G}_{2}
can be estimated in a similar way as above, here we omit the details.
Moreover, according to the definition of the low-frequency projection in (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|D^{k}G_{11}^{\ell}\ast\zeta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\|(i\xi)^{k}\hat{G}_{11}^{\ell}\hat{\zeta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}|(i\xi)^{k}\hat{G}_{11}\hat{\zeta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}|(i\xi)^{k}e^{-|\xi|^{2}t}\hat{\zeta}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\|\widehat{D^{k}H}\hat{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|D^{k}H\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-k}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which yields
\eqref{GL}_{1}
. The remaining inequalities in
\eqref{GL}_{2}
can be proved similarly. This completes the proof.
∎

Before analyzing the decay rates of the solution, we need to establish the damping-type estimates of
(\phi,u)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Under assumption (
3.1
), the following estimates hold
:

(3.43)

\|(D\phi,Du)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}t}\|(D\phi_{0},Du_{0})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}(t-\tau)}\|D\phi^{\ell}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau,

(3.44)

\|(D^{2}\phi,D^{2}u)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}t}\|(D^{2}\phi_{0},D^{2}u_{0})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}(t-\tau)}\|D^{2}\phi^{\ell}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Proof.

Taking the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
3.1
)
2
with
\nabla\phi
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\phi\cdot(u\cdot\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\phi\cdot u\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi(\mathrm{div}u)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sqrt{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathrm{div}u)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{div}\,u(u\cdot\nabla\phi)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\,-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\phi\cdot(u\cdot\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\phi\cdot u\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\sqrt{\gamma}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{1}{4}\big)\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\big(\sqrt{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(1+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Applying the estimates that

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{5}{11}}

and
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}

and using (

\displaystyle A_{2}(t)=\|(D^{3}\phi,D^{3}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{2}u^{h}\cdot\nabla D^{2}\phi^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Due to the smallness of
\beta_{2}
and the Poincaré inequality for the high-frequency part, we have

(3.67)

\displaystyle A_{2}(t)=\|(D^{3}\phi,D^{3}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{2}u^{h}\cdot\nabla D^{2}\phi^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\sim\|(D^{3}\phi,D^{3}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Solving the differential inequality (
3.65
) yields

(3.68)

\displaystyle\sqrt{A_{2}(t)}\leq Ce^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}t}\|(D^{3}\phi_{0},D^{3}u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}(t-\tau)}\|D^{3}\phi^{\ell}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

In terms of (
3.67
), we finally obtain

(3.69)

\displaystyle\|(D^{3}\phi,D^{3}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Ce^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}t}\|(D^{3}\phi_{0},D^{3}u_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}(t-\tau)}\|D^{3}\phi^{\ell}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Thus, we conclude (
3.54
).
∎

Collecting the results in Lemmas
3.4
–
3.5
leads to

\displaystyle\|(D^{k}\phi,D^{k}u)\|_{H^{3-k}}^{2}

(3.70)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}t}\|(D^{k}\phi_{0},D^{k}u_{0})\|_{H^{3-k}}^{2}+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C\delta_{0}^{\frac{9}{11}}(t-\tau)}\|D^{k}\phi^{\ell}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\quad\,\,\text{for \;$k=1,2,3$.}

Now, we are in a position to obtain the decay rates of
(D^{k}\phi,D^{k}u)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under assumption (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\|D^{k}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\text{for \;$k=1,2,3,$}

\displaystyle\|D^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{1+k}{2}}\qquad\text{for \;$k=0,1,2,$}

\displaystyle\|D^{3}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

where
N_{0}:=\|(\phi_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{3}}
.

Proof.

Based on Lemma
3.3
,
we define the time-weighted functional
Y(t)
as

\displaystyle Y(t)=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{3}(1+\tau)^{\frac{k}{2}}\|D^{k}\phi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}(1+\tau)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\|D^{k}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+(1+\tau)^{\frac{3}{2}}\|D^{3}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}.

It follows from the definition of
Y(t)
that

\displaystyle\|D^{k}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}Y(t)\qquad\quad\text{for \;$k=1,2,3,$}

\displaystyle\|D^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{1+k}{2}}Y(t)\qquad\,\text{for \;$k=0,1,2,$}

\displaystyle\|D^{3}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}Y(t).

It remains to prove the boundedness of
Y(t)
.

Since

\displaystyle\|f_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C(\gamma-1)\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}+C(\gamma-1)\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{4}{3}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{4}{3}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t),

\displaystyle\|f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C(\gamma-1)\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}+C(\gamma-1)\|D\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{7}{4}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{8}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{8}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{16}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t),

we obtain from the expression of solution (
3.32
) that

\displaystyle\|D\phi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|DG_{11}^{\ell}\ast\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|DG_{12}^{\ell}\ast u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|DG_{11}^{\ell}(t-\tau)\ast f_{1}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\|DG_{12}^{\ell}(t-\tau)\ast f_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+C(1+t)^{-1}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+C\int^{t}_{0}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{1}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+C\int^{t}_{0}(1+t-\tau)^{-1}\|f_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\int^{t}_{0}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{4}{3}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\int^{t}_{0}(1+t-\tau)^{-1}\big((1+\tau)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+(1+\tau)^{-\frac{17}{16}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t)\big)\mathrm{d}\tau

(3.71)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t)\big),

where we have used Lemma
3.3
. After a similar method, we achieve that

(3.72)

\displaystyle\|D^{2}\phi^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-1}\big(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t)\big),

(3.73)

\displaystyle\|D^{3}\phi^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big(\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t)\big),

where we used the integration by parts for the third-derivatives to get (
3.73
).

Substituting (
3.71
) into (
3.43
) and then using Grönwall’s inequality yields

(3.74)

\displaystyle\|(D\phi,Du)(t)\|_{H^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(N_{0}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t)\Big).

Similarly, substituting (
3.72
)–(
3.73
) into (
3.44
),(
3.54
) respectively, we arrive at

(3.75)

\displaystyle\|(D^{2}\phi,D^{2}u)(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-1}\big(N_{0}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t)\big),

(3.76)

\displaystyle\|(D^{3}\phi,D^{3}u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big(N_{0}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t)\big).

By the damping structure of velocity, we solve (
3.1
) as

(3.77)

\displaystyle u=e^{-t}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)}\big(u\cdot\nabla u+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\phi\cdot\nabla\phi+\sqrt{\gamma}\nabla\phi\big)(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

Then we deduce from (
3.74
)–(
3.75
) and (
3.77
) that

\displaystyle\|D^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{1+k}{2}}\Big(N_{0}+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t)\Big)\quad\quad\text{for \;$k=0,1,2.$}

According to the definition of
Y(t)
, the combination of the above results yields

(3.78)

\displaystyle Y(t)\leq CN_{0}+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}Y^{\frac{11}{6}}(t)+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}Y^{\frac{15}{8}}(t),

where
N_{0}:=\|(\phi_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{3}}
.
Due to the smallness of
\delta_{0}
, we deduce from (
3.78
) that

\displaystyle Y(t)\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|D^{k}\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\qquad\quad\text{for \;$k=1,2,3,$}

\displaystyle\|D^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{1+k}{2}}\qquad\,\,\text{for \;$k=0,1,2,$}

\displaystyle\|D^{3}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

It should be pointed out that the above decay rate for the highest-order derivative of
u
is not optimal. In fact, it can be improved. In the following lemma, we obtain the optimal decay rates of
\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}

Lemma 3.7
.

Under the a priori assumption (
3.1
),

\|D^{3}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\ \leq\ C\,(N_{0}+N_{0}^{2})\,(1+t)^{-2}.

Proof.

Applying
D^{3}
to (
3.53
), we have

(3.79)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}D^{3}\phi^{h}+\sqrt{\gamma}{\mathrm{div}}D^{3}u^{h}=D^{3}f^{h}_{1},\\
&\partial_{t}D^{3}u^{h}+\sqrt{\gamma}\nabla D^{3}\phi^{h}+D^{3}u^{h}=D^{3}f^{h}_{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

Taking the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
3.79
) with
(D^{3}\phi^{h},D^{3}u^{h})
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|(D^{3}\phi^{h},D^{3}u^{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|D^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}f_{1}^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}f_{2}^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla\phi)^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(\phi{\mathrm{div}}u)^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla u)^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}(\phi\cdot\nabla\phi)^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla\phi)^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla u)^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(\phi{\mathrm{div}}u)^{h}+D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}(\phi\nabla\phi)^{h}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.80)

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.80
), we use Lemmas
2.1
,
2.3
,
and
3.6
to derive

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla\phi)^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla\phi)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla\phi)^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}\Big(D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla\phi)-u\cdot\nabla D^{3}\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}(u\cdot\nabla D^{3}\phi^{h})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}(u\cdot\nabla D^{3}\phi^{\ell})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla\phi)^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|[D^{3},u\cdot\nabla]\phi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla D^{3}\phi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla\phi)^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla D^{3}\phi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|u\|_{\infty}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{4}}.

Similarly, for the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.80
), we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}(u\cdot\nabla u)^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|D^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla D^{3}u^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|D^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|u\|_{\infty}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{4}}.

For the third term on the right-hand side of (
3.80
), we deduce

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(\phi\,{\mathrm{div}}u)^{h}+D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}(\phi\nabla\phi)^{h}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(D^{3}\phi^{h}\cdot\phi D^{3}{\mathrm{div}}u^{h}+D^{3}u^{h}\cdot\phi D^{3}\nabla\phi^{h}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(D^{3}\phi^{h}[D^{3},\phi{\mathrm{div}}]u+D^{3}u^{h}\cdot[D^{3},\phi\nabla]\phi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(D^{3}\phi^{h}\cdot\phi D^{3}\text{div}u^{\ell}+D^{3}u^{h}\phi D^{3}\nabla\phi^{\ell}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(\phi\,{\mathrm{div}}u)^{\ell}+D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}(\phi\nabla\phi)^{\ell}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi{\mathrm{div}}(D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}u^{h})\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(D^{3}\phi^{h}[D^{3},\phi{\mathrm{div}}]u+D^{3}u^{h}\cdot[D^{3},\phi\nabla]\phi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(D^{3}\phi^{h}\cdot\phi D^{3}\text{div}u^{\ell}+D^{3}u^{h}\phi D^{3}\nabla\phi^{\ell}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(D^{3}\phi^{h}D^{3}(\phi\,{\mathrm{div}}u)^{\ell}+D^{3}u^{h}\cdot D^{3}(\phi\nabla\phi)^{\ell}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\big(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\text{div}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\big(\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{2}(\phi\text{div}u)\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{2}(\phi\nabla\phi)\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{4}}.

Then we arrive at

(3.81)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|(D^{3}\phi^{h},D^{3}u^{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|D^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{4}}.

Now, we come back to (
3.55
) to obtain the more accurate estimates.
For the last two terms on the right-hand side of (
3.55
), we follow the method used above to obtain

(3.82)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{2}u^{h}\cdot\nabla D^{2}f^{h}_{1}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla D^{2}\phi^{h}\cdot D^{2}f^{h}_{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{4}}.

Putting (
3.82
) into (
3.55
) and choosing
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

(3.83)

\frac{1}{2}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{2}u^{h}\cdot\nabla D^{2}\phi^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\tilde{C}\|D^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tilde{C}N_{0}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{4}}.

Multiplying (
3.83
) by
\beta_{3}>0
and adding the resulting equations to (
3.81
) yield

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|(D^{3}\phi^{h},D^{3}u^{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\beta_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{2}u^{h}\cdot\nabla D^{2}\phi^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\tilde{C}\beta_{3})\|D^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{\beta_{3}}{2}\|D^{3}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

Let

\displaystyle A_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|(D^{3}\phi^{h},D^{3}u^{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\beta_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{2}u^{h}\cdot\nabla D^{2}\phi^{h}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Choosing
\beta_{3}
suitably small such that

(3.84)

\displaystyle A_{2}(t)\sim\|(D^{3}\phi^{h},D^{3}u^{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

and
1-\tilde{C}\beta_{3}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain from the above inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A_{2}(t)+CA_{2}(t)\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}^{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{4}}.

Then it follows from the Grönwall inequality and (
3.84
) that

(3.85)

\displaystyle\|(D^{3}\phi^{h},D^{3}u^{h})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{8}}\leq\tilde{C}(N_{0}+N_{0}^{2})(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{8}}.

For the low-frequency part of

, we obtain from Lemmas
3.3
and
3.6
that

\displaystyle\|D^{3}u^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{3}G_{21}^{\ell}\ast\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{3}G_{22}^{\ell}\ast u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|D^{3}G_{21}^{\ell}(t-\tau)\ast f_{1}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\|D^{3}G_{22}^{\ell}(t-\tau)\ast f_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-2}\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\int^{\frac{t}{2}}_{0}\|D^{3}G_{21}^{\ell}(t-\tau)\ast f_{1}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\|D^{2}G_{21}^{\ell}(t-\tau)\ast Df_{1}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{\frac{t}{2}}_{0}\|D^{3}G_{22}^{\ell}(t-\tau)\ast f_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\|DG_{22}^{\ell}(t-\tau)\ast D^{2}f_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq CN_{0}(1+t)^{-2}+C\int^{\frac{t}{2}}_{0}(1+t-\tau)^{-2}(\|(u\cdot\nabla\phi)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\phi\text{div}u)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}})\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\|D(u\cdot\nabla\phi)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|D(\phi\text{div}u)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}})\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{5}{2}}(\|(u\cdot\nabla u)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\phi\nabla\phi)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}})\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\|D^{2}(u\cdot\nabla u)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{2}(\phi\nabla\phi)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}})\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq CN_{0}(1+t)^{-2}+\tilde{C}N_{0}^{2}\int^{\frac{t}{2}}_{0}(1+t-\tau)^{-2}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}N_{0}^{2}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{9}{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\tilde{C}N_{0}^{2}\int^{\frac{t}{2}}_{0}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{5}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}N_{0}^{2}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{9}{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau

(3.86)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(N_{0}+N_{0}^{2})(1+t)^{-2}.

This together with (
3.85
)–(
3.86
), yields

\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|D^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+\|D^{3}u^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{C}(N_{0}+N_{0}^{2})(1+t)^{-2}.

Then the proof is completed.
∎

Combining the results in Lemmas
3.6
–
3.7
, and noticing the equivalence of
\|D^{k}\phi\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|D^{k}(\rho-1)\|_{L^{2}}
, we obtain the decay rates of
(\rho-1,u)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Under the a priori assumption (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\|D^{k}(\rho-1)(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{k}{2}}~\qquad\quad\text{for \,$k=0,1,2,3$,}

\displaystyle\|D^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}N_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{k+1}{2}}~\qquad\,\,\text{for \,$k=0,1,2$,}

\displaystyle\|D^{3}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(N_{0}+N_{0}^{2})(1+t)^{-2}.

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
By the local existence (Theorem
2.5
) and the regularity
criterion (Theorem
3.1
) for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
),
it suffices to close the
a priori
assumption (
3.1
).

By the Sobolev interpolation and the equivalence
\|\phi_{0}\|_{H^{3}}\sim\|\rho_{0}-1\|_{H^{3}}

(valid under the uniform bounds on
\rho
), we have

N_{0}=\|(\phi_{0},u_{0})\|_{H^{3}}\leq\tilde{C}\|(\rho_{0}-1,u_{0})\|_{H^{3}}\leq\tilde{C}(M_{0}+\delta_{0}).

Then, using
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
and the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\ \lesssim\ \|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\ \lesssim\ \|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.8
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{8}}\|D^{3}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{8}}+\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|D^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\int_{0}^{T}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+t)^{-\frac{9}{16}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}N_{0}^{\frac{7}{8}}\mathrm{d}t+\tilde{C}\int_{0}^{T}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(1+t)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}N_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(N_{0}+N_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}N_{0}^{\frac{7}{8}}\int_{0}^{T}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{16}}\mathrm{d}t+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}N_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(N_{0}+N_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}\int_{0}^{T}(1+t)^{-\frac{17}{12}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}(M_{0}+\delta_{0})^{\frac{7}{8}}+\tilde{C}\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{6}}(M_{0}+\delta_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}(M_{0}+\delta_{0}+M_{0}^{2}+\delta_{0}^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}.

According to the condition that
0<M_{0}\leq\delta_{0}^{-\frac{1}{11}}
, we obtain from the smallness of
\delta_{0}
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}})\mathrm{d}t\leq C\delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{22}}+C\delta_{0}^{\frac{2}{33}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta.

This closes the
a priori
assumption (
3.1
), so the solution extends globally.

Therefore, for the isentropic case
\gamma>1
, the Cauchy problem
(
1.1
)–(
1.3
) admits a unique global classical solution

(\rho-1,u)\in C([0,\infty);H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
,
and the decay rates in Lemma
3.8
hold.

\square

4.
Isothermal limit of solutions of the isentropic
Euler equations

In this section, we establish the isothermal limit of the solutions of
the Cauchy problem of the isentropic compressible Euler equations
with damping under the framework of classical solutions.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
We add a subscript
\gamma
to
(\phi,u)
to indicate its dependence on
\gamma
and rewrite (
1.9
) as

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\phi_{\gamma}+\bar{\sigma}(\gamma)\,\mathrm{div}u_{\gamma}=-\,u_{\gamma}\cdot\nabla\phi_{\gamma}-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\,\phi_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{div}u_{\gamma},\\[2.0pt]
&\partial_{t}u_{\gamma}+\bar{\sigma}(\gamma)\,\nabla\phi_{\gamma}+u_{\gamma}=-\,u_{\gamma}\cdot\nabla u_{\gamma}-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\,\phi_{\gamma}\,\nabla\phi_{\gamma}.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that all the estimates in §
3
are uniform with respect
to
\gamma\in(1,3]
. In particular, using (
3.7
), we derive

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|(\phi_{\gamma},u_{\gamma})(t)\|_{H^{3}}\leq C,

which, together with (
4.1
), yields

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\gamma},\partial_{t}u_{\gamma})(t)\|_{H^{2}}\leq C.

Thus, we obtain from Lemma
2.4
that, after further extracting a subsequence,

(\phi_{\gamma},u_{\gamma})\rightarrow(\ln\rho-\ln\rho_{*},u)\qquad\,\,\mathrm{strongly}\;\mathrm{in}\;\,C([0,T];H^{3-\eta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

for any
\eta\in(0,3)
and any finite time
T>0
. After the standard diagonal argument,
we further obtain

(\phi_{\gamma},u_{\gamma})\rightarrow(\ln\rho-\ln\rho_{*},u)\qquad\,\,\mathrm{strongly}\;\mathrm{in}\;\,C([0,+\infty);H^{3-\eta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Moreover, taking
\gamma\rightarrow 1
in (
4.1
),
we obtain the strong limit that is the classical solution of the Cauchy
problem of the isothermal compressible Euler equations with damping (
1.16
).
Finally, the uniqueness of solutions of the Cauchy problem
of the limit system (
1.16
) means that the above convergence
holds without restricting to a subsequence.
Thus, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

\square

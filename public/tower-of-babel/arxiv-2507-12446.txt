Alkali doping of Zn x Mg 1-x O alloys for p p -type conductivity
John L. Lyons

Abstract
Nearly all ultrawide-bandgap oxides are affected by hole localization that limits p p -type conductivity and thus potential applications for these materials. Highly localized holes, also known as hole polarons, trap in the vicinity of acceptor dopants, giving rise to large ionization energies and severely constraining free hole concentrations. Though this hole-trapping behavior affects wurtzite zinc oxide, rocksalt zinc oxide was recently found to be resistant to the formation of hole polarons. Moreover, p p -type doping using lithium acceptors was predicted to be achievable. While rocksalt zinc oxide is metastable and has a band gap near ∼ \sim 3 eV, here it is found that zinc magnesium oxide (Zn x Mg 1-x O) alloys remain p p -type dopable within the stable rocksalt crystal structure, in addition to exhibiting band gaps in excess of 4 eV. As in rocksalt zinc oxide, alkali acceptors are shallow in zinc magnesium oxide and do not appear to be affected by donor compensation. These results indicate that alkali-doped Zn x Mg 1-x O alloys are a promising system for achieving a p p -type dopable ultrawide-bandgap oxide.

IV
Summary and conclusions

Using hybrid density functional calculations, the electronic structures of Zn
x
Mg
1-x
O alloys were calculated, and the behavior of alkali dopants was
determined in both rocksalt MgO and Zn
0.5
Mg
0.5
O. Like rocksalt ZnO, the Zn
x
Mg
1-x
O alloys exhibit indirect band gaps, with the valence-band
maxima occurring away from the
\Gamma

acceptors. Moreover, acceptor doping should be free from compensation, at least towards O-rich conditions, as these acceptor impurities have formation energies lower than potential compensating donors such as oxygen vacancies and alkali interstitials. As the synthesis of rocksalt Zn
x
Mg
1-x
O alloys has already proven feasible, this study indicates that these materials are a promising direction for
p
-type-dopable ultrawide-bandgap oxides.

Abstract
. On the upper hemisphere, we use the Obata-Escobar argument to classify conformal metrics with constant σ k \sigma_{k} curvature and constant boundary mean curvature in all types of cones including positive and negative cones. This extends a result of Escobar in [ 9 ] for k = 1 k=1 .



1.
Introduction

In 1990, Escobar initiated the study of an analogue of the Obata’s geometric rigidity problem on closed manifolds: For a smooth compact manifold with boundary, under what conditions can an Einstein manifold be deformed conformally and still remain an Einstein manifold? An affirmative and complete answer toward this problem for the ball can be found in
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
:

Let
g
be a conformal metric on the unit Euclidean ball
(\mathbb{B}^{n+1},|\mathrm{d}x|^{2})
for
n\geq 2
. If
g
has constant scalar curvature
R_{g}
and constant boundary mean
curvature
H_{g}
, then
g
is Einstein.
However, some cases in manifolds with boundary still remain open.

On a smooth Riemannian manifold
(M^{n+1},g)
, denote by

A_{g}:=\frac{1}{n-1}\Big(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}-\frac{R_{g}}{2n}g\Big)

the Schouten tensor, where
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}
and
R_{g}
are the Ricci tensor and the scalar
curvature in
g
, respectively. In the context of conformally invariant elliptic equations,
\sigma_{k}(A_{g})

is one of the important objects and has played an important
role in conformal geometry, fully nonlinear equations and mathematical general relativity. The
\sigma_{k}
curvature is always associated with a cone
\Gamma_{k}^{+}
for ellipticity:

\Gamma_{k}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1};\sigma_{1}(\lambda)>0,\cdots,\sigma_{k}(\lambda)>0\}.

Without confusion, we short
A_{g}\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}
for
\lambda(A_{g})\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}
while reserving
k
for those in
\sigma_{k}
and some quantities relative to
\sigma_{k}
.

Under the hypothesis that
|x|^{1-n}u(|x|^{-2}x)
can be extended
to a positive
C^{2}
function near
x=0
for
2\leq k\leq n
, Viaclovsky

[
25
,
26
]
obtained the Liouville theorem for constant
\sigma_{k}(A_{g})

in
\Gamma_{k}^{+}
. Concerning
k=2
and
n=3
, Chang-Gursky-Yang

[
4
,
3
]
utilized the Obata argument for the entire space. For
n=3
,
the second named author with Fang and Ma
[
12
]
introduced a
monotone formula with respect to level set of the solution, and then
proved a Liouville theorem for some more general
\sigma_{2}
-type curvature
equation including
\sigma_{2}
Yamabe equation. In a series of works
[
16
,
21
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
6
]
,
Y. Y. Li and his collaborators have formulated a complete theory for
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
For
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, Y. Y. Li and his collaborators
[
20
,
7
]
demonstrated
that the metric with positive constant
f(\lambda(A_{g}))
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}

with constant mean curvature on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
is exactly
the round metric up to a conformal diffeomorphism, where

f(\lambda(A_{g}))
is a fully nonlinear conformally invariant equation
including the
\sigma_{k}(A_{g})
equation.
Cavalcante-Espinar
[
2
]
ultilized an alternative approach to prove the Liouville
theorem in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
. Abanto-Espinar
[
10
]
established that
there is no conformal metric of the round metric on

\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
satisfying the conformal invariant equation
f(\lambda(A_{g}))=0

with negative constant boundary mean curvature. For zero
\sigma_{k}

curvature, under certain assumption about the boundary mean curvature,
Case-Wang
[
1
]
established the Obata type theorem in
\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{+}}

for constant boundary
\mathcal{B}_{k}^{g}
curvature from a natural
variational viewpoint - see S. Chen
[
5
]
for the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{k}^{g}
. The second named author
[
27
]
used
the moving plane method to obtain the Liouville theorem for positive
constant
\sigma_{k}
curvature in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
with constant
\mathcal{B}_{k}^{g}

curvature on
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
. Bao-Li-Li in
[
8
]
obtained the Liouville theorem in the half space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
with constant
\mathcal{B}_{k}^{g}

curvature on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
.

In this paper, we use an Obata-Escobar argument for
\sigma_{k}
and boundary mean curvatures to build a complete picture as that of the
scalar and boundary mean curvatures in the ball and state our result as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\tilde{g}
be a conformal metric in the unit Euclidean ball
(\mathbb{B}^{n+1},|\mathrm{d}x|^{2})
for
n\geq 2
and
\tilde{g}
has constant boundary mean curvature
\tilde{H}
on
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
. For
2\leq k\leq n
, suppose one of three conditions is true:

(i)

\tilde{g}
has zero
\sigma_{k}
curvature in
\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{+}}
.

(ii)

\tilde{g}
has positive constant
\sigma_{k}
curvature in
\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{+}}
.

(iii)

\sigma_{k}(-\tilde{A})
is a positive constant for
-\tilde{A}\in\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{+}}
.

Then
\tilde{g}
is Einstein. Moreover, there exist a canonical conformal metric
g_{\mathrm{c}}
and a conformal diffeomorphism
\Phi
on
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n+1}}
such that

\tilde{g}=\Phi^{\ast}(g_{\mathrm{c}})

and the metric
g_{\mathrm{c}}
is explicitly given as follows:

(i)

If
\tilde{\sigma}_{k}=0,\tilde{H}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, then
g_{\mathrm{c}}=\tilde{H}^{-2}|\mathrm{d}x|^{2}
.

(ii)

If
\tilde{\sigma}_{k}>0,\tilde{H}\in\mathbb{R}
, then
g_{\mathrm{c}}=\big(\frac{\binom{n+1}{k}}{2^{k}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}}\big)^{1/k}\left(\frac{2\varepsilon}{|x|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\right)^{2}|\mathrm{d}x|^{2}
,
where

\left(\frac{2^{k}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}}{\binom{n+1}{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2k}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-1}{2\varepsilon}=\tilde{H}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\varepsilon=\left(\frac{\binom{n+1}{k}}{2^{k}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2k}}\left(\tilde{H}+\sqrt{\tilde{H}^{2}+\left(\frac{2^{k}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}}{\binom{n+1}{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right).

(iii)

If
\sigma_{k}(-\tilde{A})>0,\tilde{H}>\big(\frac{2\sigma_{k}(-\tilde{A})}{\binom{n+1}{k}}\big)^{1/(2k)}
, then
g_{\mathrm{c}}=\big(\frac{\binom{n+1}{k}}{2^{k}\sigma_{k}(-\tilde{A})}\big)^{1/k}\left(\frac{2\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}|\mathrm{d}x|^{2}
, where

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2^{k}\sigma_{k}(-\tilde{A})}{\binom{n+1}{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2k}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}+1}{2\varepsilon}=\tilde{H}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \varepsilon=\left(\frac{\binom{n+1}{k}}{2^{k}\sigma_{k}(-\tilde{A})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2k}}\left(\tilde{H}+\sqrt{\tilde{H}^{2}-\left(\frac{2^{k}\sigma_{k}(-\tilde{A})}{\binom{n+1}{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)>1.

Here,
\tilde{A}=A_{\tilde{g}},\tilde{\sigma}_{k}=\sigma_{k}(\tilde{A})
and
\tilde{H}=H_{\tilde{g}}
.

2.
Proof of main theorem

For an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix
W
, we define the
k^{\mathrm{th}}
Newton transformation by

T_{k}(W)_{j}^{i}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum\delta\left(\begin{matrix}i_{1}&\cdots&i_{k}&i\\
j_{1}&\cdots&j_{k}&j\end{matrix}\right)W_{i_{1}}^{j_{1}}\cdots W_{i_{k}}^{j_{k}},

where the Kronecker symbol

\delta\left(\begin{matrix}i_{1}&\cdots&i_{k}&i\\
j_{1}&\cdots&j_{k}&j\end{matrix}\right)

has value
1\penalty\ (\mathrm{resp.}-1)
for an even (resp. odd) permutation in the index set
\{1,2,\cdots,n+1\}
when
i_{1},\cdots,i_{k},i
are distinct; otherwise it has value
0
. The following recursive formula holds (see Reilly
[
24
]
):

T_{k}(W)_{j}^{i}=\sigma_{k}(W)\delta_{j}^{i}-T_{k-1}(W)_{l}^{i}W_{j}^{l}.

On a smooth manifold
(M^{n+1},g)
, we define

L_{k}(A_{g})_{j}^{i}:=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\delta_{j}^{i}-T_{k}(A_{g})_{j}^{i}

and

E_{g}=\mathrm{Ric}_{g}-\frac{R_{g}}{n+1}g.

Then we have
\sigma_{1}(L_{k}(A_{g}))=0
and from
[
18
, Lemma 6.8]
that

\displaystyle\langle L_{k}\left(A_{g}\right),E_{g}\rangle_{g}=(n-1)\left[\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}\left(A_{g}\right)\sigma_{1}\left(A_{g}\right)-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}\left(A_{g}\right)\right].

The tracefree
(1,1)
-tensor
L_{k}(A_{g})
has proved to be very successful
when dealing with
\sigma_{k}
Yamabe equations, see for example

[
4
,
3
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
18
,
25
]
. Moreover, on locally conformal flat manifolds
(M,g)
, it was proved in
[
25
]
that
\nabla_{i}T_{k}(A_{g})_{j}^{i}=0
.

Lemma 2

(
[
18
, Lemma 6.8]
)
.

For
1\leq k\leq n

and
A_{g}\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}
, there holds

\langle L_{k}\left(A_{g}\right),E_{g}\rangle_{g}\geq 0

and equality holds if and only if
E_{g}=0
.

We mimic the proof of
[
18
, Lemma 6.8]
to obtain the following result for the negative cone.

Lemma 3
.

For
1\leq k\leq n
and
-A_{g}\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}
, there holds

\langle L_{k}\left(-A_{g}\right),-E_{g}\rangle_{g}\geq 0

and equality holds if and only if
E_{g}=0.

In the following, let
g=u^{2}g_{0}
in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n+1};x_{n+2}>0\}
with
g_{0}=g_{\mathbb{S}^{n+1}}
being the round metric, and
\bar{g}
be the induced metric of
g
on
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}=\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
\nabla
and
\overline{\nabla}
denote the covariant derivatives with respect to the Levi-Civita connections of
g
and
\bar{g}
. Then we obtain

(1)

E_{g}=-\frac{n-1}{u}\big(\nabla^{2}u-\frac{\Delta_{g}u}{n+1}g\big)

and

A_{g}=-\frac{1}{u}\nabla_{g}^{2}u+\frac{\left|\nabla u\right|_{g}^{2}}{2u^{2}}g+\frac{1}{2u^{2}}g\qquad\mathrm{\penalty\ \penalty\ in\penalty\ \penalty\ }\quad\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}.

Under Fermi coordinates around
\mathsf{p}\in\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
the metric
g
can be expressed
as

g=\mathrm{d}y_{n+1}^{2}+\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}y_{\alpha}\mathrm{d}y_{\beta}\qquad\qquad\mathrm{\penalty\ \penalty\ in\penalty\ \penalty\ }B_{\rho}^{+}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1};|y|<\rho,\penalty\ y_{n+1}>0\},

where
(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
are the geodesic normal coordinates
on
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
and
\nu_{g}=-\partial_{y_{n+1}}
is the outward unit normal vector on
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
. We define the second fundamental form on
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
by

L_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\nu_{g},\partial_{\beta}\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\alpha}}\partial_{\beta},\nu_{g}\rangle,\quad 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n.

Here,
\partial_{\alpha}=\partial_{y_{\alpha}}
,
\nu_{g}
is the outward unit
normal on
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
, and
H_{g}=(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}L_{\alpha}^{\alpha})/n
is the mean curvature with respect to
g
. Moreover, we have

(2)

u_{\nu_{g}}=H_{g}u.

Lemma 4
.

Fix
1\leq k\leq n
. For constant
\sigma_{k}(A_{g})

and
A_{g}\in\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{+}}
in
(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1},g)
,
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A)(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}-\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}H_{g}u\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g},

and equality holds if and only if
g

is Einstein.

Proof.

By (
1
) we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\langle L_{k}\left(A_{g}\right),E_{g}\rangle_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\langle L_{k}\left(A_{g}\right),-\frac{n-1}{u}\nabla^{2}u\rangle_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\langle\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})g-T_{k}(A_{g}),-(n-1)\nabla^{2}u\rangle_{g}\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\langle T_{k}(A_{g}),\nabla^{2}u\rangle\mathrm{d}v_{g}-(n-1)\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\Delta_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\nabla_{i}T_{k}(A_{g})_{j}^{i}\nabla^{j}u\mathrm{d}v_{g}+(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}

\displaystyle-(n-1)\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{\nu_{g}}\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}-(n-1)\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}H_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g},

where we used
\nabla_{i}T_{k}(A_{g})_{j}^{i}=0
in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}.

∎

Lemma 5
.

Fix
1\leq k\leq n
. For constant
\sigma_{k}(-A_{g})

and
-A_{g}\in\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{+}}
in
(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1},g)
,
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}-T_{k}(-A_{g})(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}+\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(-A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}H_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g},

and the equality holds if and only if
g
is Einstein.

Proof.

By (
1
) and Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\langle L_{k}\left(-A_{g}\right),-E_{g}\rangle_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\langle L_{k}\left(-A_{g}\right),\frac{n-1}{u}\nabla^{2}u\rangle_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\langle\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(-A_{g})g-T_{k}(-A_{g}),(n-1)\nabla^{2}u\rangle_{g}\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=-(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\langle T_{k}(-A_{g}),\nabla^{2}u\rangle\mathrm{d}v_{g}+(n-1)\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(-A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\Delta_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=-(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(-A_{g})(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}+(n-1)\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(-A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{\nu_{g}}\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=-(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(-A_{g})(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}+(n-1)\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(-A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}H_{g}u\mathrm{d}v_{g},

where we used
\nabla_{i}T_{k}(-A_{g})_{j}^{i}=0
in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}.

∎

In the following, let
A_{g}^{\top}
denote the tangential part of

A_{g}
on the boundary
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Lemma 6
.

Fix
1\leq k\leq n
. Let
g=u^{2}g_{0}

be a metric in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
such that
\sigma_{k}(A_{g})
is constant. Then

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\sigma_{k}(A_{g}^{\top})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}.

Proof.

The following important identity was first proved by Zhen-Chao Han
[
17
, p.6 in
the preprint version
]
: For a conformal Killing vector field
X
in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
, there holds

X\big(\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\big)=-\frac{n+1}{n+1-k}\nabla_{j}\left[T_{k}(A_{g})_{i}^{j}X^{i}\right]+\nabla_{j}\left[\sigma_{k}(A_{g})X^{j}\right].

Alternatively, it is a direct consequence of
[
17
, (4)]
and the elementary property of
T_{k}(A_{g})
(see
[
25
]
) that

T_{k}(A_{g})_{i}^{j}=\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\delta_{i}^{j}-T_{k-1}(A_{g})_{l}^{j}A_{i}^{l},\qquad T_{k-1}(A_{g})_{l}^{i}A_{i}^{l}=k\sigma_{k}(A_{g}).

Integrating both sides of the above identity over
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}
yields

\displaystyle(n+1-k)\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}X\big(\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\big)\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(n+1)\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\nabla_{j}\left[T_{k}(A_{g})_{i}^{j}X^{i}\right]\mathrm{d}v_{g}+(n+1-k)\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}}\nabla_{j}\left[\sigma_{k}(A_{g})X^{j}\right]\mathrm{d}v_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(n+1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(X,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}+(n+1-k)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\langle X,\nu_{g}\rangle_{g}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}.

We now take
X=-\nabla_{g_{0}}x_{n+2}
to be the conformal Killing vector field on
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
such that
X=u\nu_{g}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and obtain that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-(n+1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(X,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}+(n+1-k)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\langle X,\nu_{g}\rangle_{g}\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}

\displaystyle=-(n+1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\sigma_{k}(A_{g}^{\top})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}+(n+1-k)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\sigma_{k}(A_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Now we prove a key identity involving the boundary term.

Lemma 7
.

Fix
1\leq k\leq n
. Let
g=u^{2}g_{0}

be a metric in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
such that
\sigma_{k}(A_{g})
and
H_{g}

are constant. Then

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})H_{g}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}.

Proof.

Notice that
g
is umbilical on
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
, that is,
L_{\alpha\beta}=H_{g}\bar{g}_{\alpha\beta}
. Under Fermi coordinates around
\mathsf{p}\in\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1}
, by Codazzi equation we have

\displaystyle A_{\beta(n+1)}=\frac{1}{n-1}R_{\beta(n+1)}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}R_{\penalty\ \beta\alpha(n+1)}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}({\overline{\nabla}}_{\beta}L^{\penalty\ \alpha}_{\alpha}-{\overline{\nabla}}^{\alpha}L_{\alpha\beta})=\overline{\nabla}_{\beta}H_{g}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle T_{k}(A_{g})(\overline{\nabla}u,\nu_{g})=

\displaystyle-T_{k}(A_{g})_{n+1}^{\alpha}u_{,\alpha}

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{k-1}(A_{g}^{\top})_{\beta}^{\alpha}A_{n+1}^{\beta}u_{,\alpha}=T_{k-1}(A_{g}^{\top})(\overline{\nabla}H_{g},\overline{\nabla}u),

where we used
[
5
, Lemma 3(d)]
in the second identity.

Hence, by (
2
) and (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(\overline{\nabla}u+u_{\nu_{g}}\nu_{g},\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(\overline{\nabla}u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{\nu_{g}}T_{k}(A_{g})(\nu_{g},\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{\nu_{g}}\sigma_{k}(A_{g}^{\top})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}=H_{g}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\sigma_{k}(A_{g}^{\top})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g},

where we used
T_{k}(A)(\nu_{g},\nu_{g})=\sigma_{k}(A_{g}^{\top})
. Therefore, this together with Lemma
6
yields the desired assertion.
∎

Theorem 8
.

Let
g
be a conformal metric of
g_{0}
with
A_{g}\in\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{+}}
for
2\leq k\leq n
, and satisfy

\begin{cases}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})=\mathrm{constant}\geq 0&\qquad\mathrm{\penalty\ \penalty\ in\penalty\ \penalty\ }\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1},\\
H_{g}=\mathrm{constant}&\qquad\mathrm{\penalty\ \penalty\ on\penalty\ \penalty\ }\mathbb{S}^{n}.\end{cases}

Then
g
is Einstein.

Proof.

By Lemma
4
and Lemma
7

we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}T_{k}(A_{g})(\nabla u,\nu_{g})\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}-\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\sigma_{k}(A_{g})H_{g}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\mathrm{d}\sigma_{g}=0

and then from Lemma
2
that
g

is Einstein.
∎

Theorem 9
.

Let
g
be a conformal metric of
g_{0}
with
-A_{g}\in\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{+}}
for
2\leq k\leq n
, and satisfy

\begin{cases}\sigma_{k}(-A_{g})=\mathrm{constant}\geq 0&\qquad\mathrm{\penalty\ \penalty\ in\penalty\ \penalty\ }\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n+1},\\
H_{g}=\mathrm{constant}&\qquad\mathrm{\penalty\ \penalty\ on\penalty\ \penalty\ }\mathbb{S}^{n}.\end{cases}

Then
g
is Einstein.

Proof.

Using Lemma
3
and Lemma
7
with
-A_{g}
in place of
A_{g}
,

\displaystyle 0=\tilde{E}=(n-1)\phi^{-1}(\nabla^{2}\phi-\frac{\Delta\phi}{n+1}g),

A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{ij}=(\log\phi)_{ij}+(\log\phi)_{i}(\log\phi)_{j}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\log\phi|^{2}g_{ij}=2ac\tilde{g}\qquad\mathrm{\penalty\ \penalty\ in\penalty\ \penalty\ }\mathbb{B}^{n+1}.

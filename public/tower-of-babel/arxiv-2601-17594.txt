Long time behavior of Fokker-Planck equations for bosons and fermions
Anton Arnold, Marlies Pirner, Gayrat Toshpulatov

Abstract
This paper is concerned with space inhomogeneous quantum Fokker-Planck equations posed on a classical kinetic phase space. The nonlinear factor f ⁡ ( 1 ± f ) f(1\pm f) appears both in the transport term and in the collison part of the Fokker-Planck operator, accounting for the inclusion principle of bosons and the exclusion principle of fermions. Assuming that global solutions exist, we prove exponential decay of the solutions to the global equilibrium in a weighted L 2 L^{2} -space without a close-to-equilibrium assumption. Our analysis is in the spirit of an L 2 L^{2} -hypocoercivity method. Our main Lyapunov functional is constructed from a logarithmic relative entropy and the (nonlinear) projection of the solution to the manifold of local-in- x x equilibria.



Keywords:
Semi-classical kinetic equations, Fokker-Planck equation, fermion, boson, long time
behavior, convergence to equilibrium, hypocoercivity, Lyapunov functional.

Introduction

One way to study the statistical evolution of rarefied gases of particles consists in writing down kinetic equations for the distribution function
f
of the particles in phase space. These equations consist of transport and collision parts. With the invention of quantum mechanics, kinetic equations have been modified in order to describe also quantum statistical effects like Pauli’s exclusion-inclusion principle. This approach was initiated by the physicists Nordheim
[
40
]
, Uehling and
Uhlenbeck
[
46
]
. Several kinetic equations for interacting particles with Pauli’s exclusion-inclusion principle, such as fermions and bosons, have been introduced in the literature, see e.g.
[
18
,
37
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
35
,
47
]
.
A notable feature of quantum kinetic equations is the appearance of the term
1\pm f
in their collision part.
It was shown in
[
42
]
that adding the term
1\pm f
only to the collision part of the kinetic equations can be insufficient to describe accurately quantum systems far from equilibrium. To deal with this problem, Balazs
[
7
]
and
Kaniadakis
[
32
]
(see also
[
30
,
31
]
) introduced the following kinetic model which includes the term
1\pm f
not only in the collision part of Fokker-Planck type but also in the transport part

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+p\cdot\nabla_{x}(f+\kappa f^{2})=\text{div}_{p}(\nabla_{p}f+p(f+\kappa f^{2}))\\
f(0,x,p)=f_{0}(x,p).\end{cases}

(1)

The term
1+\kappa f
appearing in (
1
) takes into account the exclusion principle for fermions when
\kappa=-1
and the inclusion principle for bosons when
\kappa=1.
Moreover, the values of the distribution function have to respect the bounds
0\leq f\leq 1

when
\kappa=-1.
Clearly, for
\kappa=0,
(
1
) reduces to the classical, kinetic Fokker-Planck equation.

In this paper, we consider this nonlinear, spatially inhomogeneous kinetic equation. Here the variable
t\geq 0,

x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
respectively, stand for time, spatial position, and momentum. The unknown
f(t,x,p)\geq 0
describes the time evolution of the distribution function of indistinguishable quantum particles, which are fermions or bosons.

While (
1
) may be called a ”quantum Fokker-Planck equation”, we note that several other models from the literature are also referred to by this very name. Many of them are either evolution equations for the quantum kinetic Wigner function
[
3
,
29
]
or evolution equations in Lindblad form for the density matrix
[
2
,
36
]
. While a Wigner-Fokker-Planck equation (without external potential) would be very similar to (
1
) with the choice
\kappa=0
, they have intrinsically different settings: A Wigner equation is posed on a quantum mechanical phase space and, when describing an open quantum system, it (or its density matrix analog) has to be in Lindblad form
[
3
]
. By contrast, the Fokker-Planck equations (
1
) are posed in a classical phase space, and they do not correspond to the Lindblad form.

For both values of
\kappa,
Equation (
1
) satisfies the following physical principles. First,
whenever
f(t,x,p)
is a (well-behaved) solution of (
1
), one has
conservation of mass:

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,p)dpdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}(x,p)dpdx,\,\,\,\,\forall\,t\geq 0.

Next, Equation (
1
) is
dissipative
in the sense that the following entropy functional decreases under time-evolution of
f:
we define

\mathrm{H}[f]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|p|^{2}}{2}fdpdx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(f\ln{f}-\kappa(1+\kappa f)\ln{(1+\kappa f)}\right)dpdx,

which is bounded below inspite of the non-positive second summand (see
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
for
\kappa=-1
and
[
11
, Lemma 4.5]
for
\kappa=1
).
If
f
is a regular enough solution, one obtains for all
t>0,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{H}[f(t)]=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f+\kappa f^{2})\left|\nabla_{p}\ln\left(\frac{f}{M(1+\kappa f)}\right)\right|^{2}dpdx\leq 0,

(2)

where
M(p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|p|^{2}}{2}}.

This means that the functional
\mathrm{H}[f(t)]
is a decreasing function of
t\geq 0,
hence it is a Lyapunov functional for (
1
). The entropy dissipation functional (the integral on the right hand side of (
2
)) vanishes if and only if
\frac{f}{M(1+\kappa f)}
does not depend on
p.
This means

f(t,x,p)=\frac{\beta(t,x)M(p)}{1-\kappa\beta(t,x)M(p)}

(3)

for some function
\beta=\beta(t,x).
Hence, any function
f
in the form of (
3
) is a
local equilibrium
for (
1
). For such
f,
the left hand side of (
1
) vanishes if
\beta
is constant. Hence, the unique
global equilibrium
is

f_{\infty}(p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\beta_{\infty}M(p)}{1-\kappa\beta_{\infty}M(p)},

(4)

where
\beta_{\infty}>0
is uniquely determined by mass conservation

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}(x,p)dpdx=\displaystyle|\mathbb{T}^{d}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\infty}(p)dp.

f_{\infty}(p)
is called the
Fermi-Dirac
distribution when
\kappa=-1
and the
Bose-Einstein
distribution when
\kappa=1.

We note that
f_{\infty}
is not always well-defined when
\kappa=1
and
d\geq 3:
since
f_{\infty}
is supposed to be non-negative and have finite values,
\beta_{\infty}
needs to be in the interval
(0,(2\pi)^{d/2}),
see
[
11
]
. This means
0\leq f_{\infty}(p)\leq\frac{e^{-|p|^{2}/2}}{1-e^{-|p|^{2}/2}}
for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
hence the mass of
f_{\infty}
can not be larger than

m_{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{e^{-|p|^{2}/2}}{1-e^{-|p|^{2}/2}}dpdx=|\mathbb{T}^{d}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{e^{-|p|^{2}/2}}{1-e^{-|p|^{2}/2}}dp.

(5)

One can check that
m_{c}=\infty
if
d=1,2
and
m_{c}<\infty
if
d\geq 3.
Therefore, there is exactly one global equilibrium for each fixed finite mass in dimension
d=1,2.
However, in dimension
d\geq 3,
there is no (smooth) global equilibrium if the mass of
f_{0}
is larger than
m_{c}.

On the basis of the decay of
\mathrm{H}[f(t)],
one can conjecture that
f(t)
converges to the global equilibrium
f_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty.
Thus, it is an interesting problem to prove (or disprove) this convergence and to estimate the convergence rate. Our goal is to study the long time behavior of the solution
f(t),
and to prove that
f(t)
converges exponentially to
f_{\infty}.

If we simplify (
1
) by neglecting the dependence of the solution on the
x
variable, then we get the spatially homogeneous equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f(t,p)=\text{div}_{p}(\nabla_{p}f(t,p)+p(f(t,p)+\kappa f^{2}(t,p)))\\
f(0,p)=f_{0}(p).\end{cases}

(6)

This equation has been studied comprehensively: exponential decay of the solution to the equilibrium in dimension
d=1
was proven in
[
16
]
using the ”entropy-entropy dissipation” method
[
4
,
14
]
. The analogous convergence result and well-posedness in higher dimensions were obtained in
[
15
]
for fermions and
d\in\mathbb{N},
and in
[
11
]
for bosons and
d=2.
As we mentioned before, for the bosonic case, i.e.
\kappa=1,
there is no global equilibrium if
f_{0}
has mass larger than the critical mass
m_{c}
(see (
5
)) in dimension
d\geq 3.
In this case, it is conjectured
that solutions will blow up in finite time. This problem was studied only for the spatially homogeneous equation (
6
): Toscani
[
44
]
proved that in dimension

d\geq 3
there are certain solutions which blow up if the initial mass is sufficiently large or, for any given
supercritical mass, if the initial second moment is small enough. The continuation beyond this blow-up and long time behavior was studied in
[
13
,
28
]
.

Concerning the spatially inhomogeneous equation (
1
), there are only few studies: existence of smooth solutions and their exponential convergence to the global equilibrium was proven in
[
39
]
when the initial data is close to equilibrium in a suitable Sobolev space. This
result is based on the
H^{1}
hypocoercivity method developed in previous works
[
38
,
48
]
. Luo and Zhang
[
34
]
considered (
1
) in whole space, i.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.
Using the high-order Sobolev energy method developed by Guo
[
25
]
, they obtained algebraic decay rates under the assumption that the initial data is close to equilibrium in a Sobolev space.

In this paper, we shall improve these previous results. We prove exponential decay of the solution to the global equilibrium without the close-to-equilibrium assumption.
For the spatially homogeneous equation (
6
) it is known that explicit estimates on convergence to equilibrium can be obtained by a direct study of the entropy and its dissipation functional. Since
f=f(t,p)
does not depend on
x
in this case, with the help of convex Sobolev inequalities
[
14
]
one can obtain a Grönwall inequality for the relative entropy. This implies exponential convergence of the solution to the global equilibrium, see
[
16
,
15
,
11
]
. Unfortunately, this idea cannot be used for the spatially inhomogeneous equation (
1
). The reason is that one loses information about the
x-
direction in the dissipation functional. More precisely, there is no gradient with respect to
x
in the dissipation functional (see (
2
)), hence a Sobolev inequality does not hold for the entropy and its dissipation functional.

To deal with this problem, in recent years, many new so-called hypocoercivity methods have been introduced to study the long-time behavior of spatially inhomogeneous kinetic equations.
The challenge of hypocoercivity is to understand the interplay between the collision operator that provides dissipativity in the velocity variable and the transport operator which is conservative, in order to obtain global dissipativity for the whole problem.
There are several hypocoercivity methods, see e.g.
[
38
,
48
,
25
,
20
,
1
,
5
,
6
,
10
]
. In this work we use the
L^{2}
-hypocoercivity method which was
introduced in
[
27
,
20
]
for linear kinetic equations which only conserve mass. This method was developed further in
[
26
,
8
,
12
]
for linear kinetic equations with several conserved quantities (mass, momentum, energy).
The main motivation of this work is to provide an extension of the hypocoercivity results from
[
39
,
34
]
for the model (
1
): We shall prove exponential decay of the solution to the global equilibrium in
L^{2}
without the close-to-equilibrium assumption on the initial data.
Motivated by the recent results
[
23
,
41
,
45
]
, we construct a Lyapunov functional for (
1
) by modifying the
L^{2}
-hypocoercivity method.
The idea consists in constructing a Lypunov functional which is equivalent to the square of the
L^{2}-
norm and satisfies a Grönwall inequality. This implies exponential decay of the solution to the global equilibrium. This functional is constructed by using the relative entropy and the projection of the solution to the manifold of local equilibria. In general, for semi-classical kinetic equations this projection operator is nonlinear (see (
30
) below). This makes our analysis challenging.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2 we state the main result and explain the main steps of the proof. In Section 3 we study some properties of weak solutions. We show that such solutions conserve mass and their
L^{1}-
difference is contracting as time progresses. In Section 4 we establish some estimates for the projection operator and the entropy dissipation, construct a Lyapunov functional, and prove the main result.

\partial_{t}f_{n}+p\cdot\nabla_{x}(f_{n}+\kappa f_{n}^{2})-\text{div}_{p}[p(f_{n}+\kappa f_{n}^{2})]-\Delta_{p}f_{n}=R_{n}(f),

(18)

where

R_{n}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\text{div}_{x}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})]-\text{div}_{p}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})]+\kappa p\cdot\nabla_{x}[f_{n}^{2}-\eta_{n}*f^{2}]+\kappa\text{div}_{p}[p(\eta_{n}*f^{2}-f_{n}^{2})].

(19)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be a weak solution to (
1
) and
f_{n}
be its mollifications. Then for any
T>0

\lim_{n\to\infty}||\mathrm{div}_{x}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})]-\mathrm{div}_{p}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})]||_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0

(20)

Proof.

We denote
F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f+\kappa f^{2}
and compute

\displaystyle||\text{div}_{x}[(p\eta_{n})*F]-\text{div}_{p}[(p\eta_{n})*F]||_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\tau_{n}*_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\text{div}_{x}-\text{div}_{p})[(p-p^{\prime})\tilde{r}_{n}(x-x^{\prime})r_{n}(p-p^{\prime})](F(\cdot,x^{\prime},p^{\prime})-F(\cdot,x,p))dp^{\prime}dx^{\prime}\right|dpdxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\left[\tau_{n}*_{t}\left(\sup_{|y|\leq 1/n,\,|q|\leq 1/n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(\cdot,x+y,p+q)-F(\cdot,x,p)|dpdx\right)\right](t)dt,

(21)

where
C>0
is a constant such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(\text{div}_{x}-\text{div}_{p})[(p-p^{\prime})\tilde{r}_{n}(x-x^{\prime})r_{n}(p-p^{\prime})]|dp^{\prime}dx^{\prime}\leq C.

It is important to note that
C
is independent of
t,x,p,n.
Let us denote

g_{n}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sup_{|y|\leq 1/n,\,|q|\leq 1/n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(t,x+y,p+q)-F(t,x,p)|dpdx,\,\,\,\,t\in[0,T+1/n].

Since
\tau_{n}\geq 0
has its support in
(-1/n,0),
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\tau_{n}*_{t}g_{n})(t)dt=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{t}^{t+1/n}\tau_{n}(t-s)g_{n}(s)ds\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1/n}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\tau_{n}(t-s)dt\right)g_{n}(s)ds+\int_{1/n}^{T}\left(\int_{s-1/n}^{s}\tau_{n}(t-s)dt\right)g_{n}(s)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{T}^{T+1/n}\left(\int_{s-1/n}^{T}\tau_{n}(t-s)dt\right)g_{n}(s)ds.

One can check that

\int_{0}^{s}\tau_{n}(t-s)dt=\int_{-s}^{0}\tau_{n}(t)dt\leq\int_{-1/n}^{0}\tau_{n}(t)dt=1\,\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,s\in[0,1/n],

\int_{s-1/n}^{s}\tau_{n}(t-s)dt=\int_{-1/n}^{0}\tau_{n}(t)dt=1\,\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,s\in[1/n,T],

\int_{s-1/n}^{T}\tau_{n}(t-s)dt=\int_{-1/n}^{T-s}\tau_{n}(t)dt\leq\int_{-1/n}^{0}\tau_{n}(t)dt=1\,\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,s\in[T,T+1/n].

These estimates show

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\tau_{n}*_{t}g_{n})(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T+1/n}g_{n}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\sup_{|y|\leq 1/n,\,|q|\leq 1/n}\int_{0}^{T+1/n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(t,x+y,p+q)-F(t,x,p)|dpdxdt.

(22)

(
3.1
) and (
3.1
)
show

\displaystyle||\text{div}_{x}[(p\eta_{n})*F]

\displaystyle-\text{div}_{p}[(p\eta_{n})*F]||_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{|y|\leq 1/n,\,|q|\leq 1/n}\int_{0}^{T+1/n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(t,x+y,p+q)-F(t,x,p)|dpdxdt.

Since translations are continuous in the
L^{1}((0,T+1)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm (see
[
24
, Proposition 8.5]
), we get the claimed result.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be a weak solution to (
1
) and
f_{n}
be its mollifications. Assume
f
has regularity as in (
10
). Let
\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
|p|\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).
Then for any
T>0

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(p\cdot\nabla_{x}[f_{n}^{2}-\eta_{n}*f^{2}]+\mathrm{div}_{p}[p(\eta_{n}*f^{2}-f_{n}^{2})])dpdxdt=0.

(23)

In particular, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi R_{n}(f)dpdxdt=0.

(24)

Proof.

We consider the first term of (
23
)

\psi p\cdot\nabla_{x}(f_{n}^{2}-\eta_{n}*f^{2})=2\psi f_{n}p\cdot(\nabla_{x}f_{n}-\nabla_{x}f)+2\psi p\cdot\nabla_{x}f(f_{n}-f)+\psi p\cdot(\nabla_{x}f^{2}-\nabla_{x}\eta_{n}*f^{2}).

Because of the regularity assumption (
10
), we have

f_{n}(\nabla_{x}f_{n}-\nabla_{x}f)\to 0
and

in

as

As we have
\nabla_{x}f\in L^{2}_{loc}([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
f_{n}-f\to 0
in

as

we can show by the Hölder inequality that

as

and

are bounded, we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi p\cdot\nabla_{x}[f_{n}^{2}-\eta_{n}*f^{2}]dpdxdt=0.

The second term of (

\psi\mathrm{div}_{p}[p(\eta_{n}*f^{2}-f^{2}_{n})]=d\psi(\eta_{n}*f^{2}-f^{2}_{n})+\psi p\cdot\nabla_{p}(\eta_{n}*f^{2}-f^{2}_{n}),

and similar arguments show

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi\mathrm{div}_{p}[p(\eta_{n}*f^{2}-f^{2}_{n})]dpdxdt=0.

3.2
Mass conservation

To verify the mass conservation of weak solutions we introduce a cut-off in the
p-
direction. Let
\zeta:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-increasing function in
C^{\infty}((0,\infty))
such that
\zeta(s)=1
for
s\in[0,1]
and
\zeta(s)=0
for
s\geq 2.
We define

\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\zeta\left(\epsilon|p|\right),\,\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\epsilon>0.

(25)

We note that
\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)=1
for
|p|\leq 1/\epsilon
and
\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)=0
for
|p|\geq 2/\epsilon.
There is a constant
C>0
independent of
\epsilon
such that

|\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|\leq C\epsilon,\,\,\,|\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|\leq C\epsilon^{2},\,\,\,|\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\cdot p|\leq C,\,\,\,|p|^{2}|\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|\leq C

(26)

for all
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be a weak solution to (
1
) with an initial condition
f_{0}.
Then we have mass conservation

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,p)dpdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}(x,p)dpdx,\,\,\,\,\forall\,t\geq 0.

Proof.

We multiply (
17
) by
\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)
and integrate by parts

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}f_{n}dpdx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}f_{n}-\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}\cdot p\eta_{n}*(f+\kappa f^{2}))dpdx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{div}_{x}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})]-\mathrm{div}_{p}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})])dpdx.

We integrate this equation on the interval
[0,T]
for any
T>0:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}f_{n}(T)dpdx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}f_{n}(0)dpdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}f_{n}-\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}\cdot p\eta_{n}*(f+\kappa f^{2}))dpdxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{div}_{x}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})]-\mathrm{div}_{p}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})])dpdxdt.

We keep here the
\mathrm{div}_{x}-
term, as we shall use (
20
) later on. In this equation we first let
n\to\infty
and then
\epsilon\to 0:
the fact that
\zeta_{\epsilon}
is bounded and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem show

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}f_{n}(T)dpdx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}f_{n}(0)dpdx\big)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(T)dpdx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}dpdx.

Since
\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}
and
\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}\cdot p
are uniformly bounded in
\epsilon
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}f_{n}-\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}\cdot p\eta_{n}*(f+\kappa f^{2}))dpdxdt\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}f-\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}\cdot p(f+\kappa f^{2}))dpdxdt.

\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}f-\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}\cdot p(f+\kappa f^{2})\to 0
pointwise as
\epsilon\to 0.
Moreover,
\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}
and
\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}\cdot p
are bounded. Hence, we can apply the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Delta_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}f-\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}\cdot p(f+\kappa f^{2}))dpdxdt=0.

The fact that
\zeta_{\epsilon}
is bounded and (
20
) imply

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{div}_{x}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})]-\mathrm{div}_{p}[(p\eta_{n})*(f+\kappa f^{2})])dpdxdt=0.

These limits show

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(T)dpdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}dpdx

for all
T>0.

∎

3.3
Contraction of the
L^{1}
norm and maximum principle

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f,\,g\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be two weak solutions of (
1
) with initial conditions
f_{0}
and
g_{0},
respectively. Assume
f
and
g
have regularity as in (
10
).
Then

||f(t)-g(t)||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq||f_{0}-g_{0}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}

for all
t\geq 0.

Proof.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N},
we define

\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(s)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}-1,\,\,\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,s\leq-\varepsilon\\
\eta_{\varepsilon}(s),\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,-\varepsilon\leq s\leq\varepsilon\\
1,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,s\geq\varepsilon\end{cases}

with an increasing odd function
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon])
such that
\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}
is
C^{\infty}
at
s=\pm\varepsilon.
Let
|\cdot|_{\varepsilon}
denote the primitive of
\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}
which vanishes at 0. One can check that
\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}
and
|\cdot|_{\varepsilon}
converge pointwise to the usual sign and absolute value functions as
\varepsilon\to 0,
respectively.

Let
f_{n}
and
g_{n}
be defined as in (
16
). Then, by (
18
) we have

\partial_{t}(f_{n}-g_{n})+p\cdot\nabla_{x}(f_{n}-g_{n}+\kappa(f^{2}_{n}-g^{2}_{n}))-\Delta_{p}(f_{n}-g_{n})-\text{div}_{p}(p(f_{n}-g_{n})+p\kappa(f^{2}_{n}-g^{2}_{n}))=R_{n}(f)-R_{n}(g).

Let
\zeta_{\epsilon}
be the function defined in (
25
). We multiply this equation by
\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})
and integrate by parts

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f_{n}-g_{n}|_{\varepsilon}dpdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(f_{n}-g_{n})|\nabla_{p}(f_{n}-g_{n})|^{2}dpdx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\cdot(\nabla_{p}f_{n}+p(f_{n}+\kappa f^{2}_{n})-\nabla_{p}g_{n}-p(g_{n}+\kappa g^{2}_{n}))dpdx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(f_{n}-g_{n})p\cdot\nabla_{p}(f_{n}-g_{n})(f_{n}-g_{n})(1+\kappa f_{n}+\kappa g_{n})dpdx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(f_{n}-g_{n})p\cdot\nabla_{x}(f_{n}-g_{n})(f_{n}-g_{n})(1+\kappa f_{n}+\kappa g_{n})dpdx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})(R_{n}(f)-R_{n}(g))dpdx.

Note that this formula includes both
\varepsilon
and
\epsilon.
We integrate this equation on the interval
[0,T],\,T>0,
and use

\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(f_{n}-g_{n})|\nabla_{p}(f_{n}-g_{n})|^{2}\geq 0
to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f_{n}(T)-g_{n}(T)|_{\varepsilon}dpdx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f_{n}(0)-g_{n}(0)|_{\varepsilon}dpdx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\cdot(\nabla_{p}f_{n}+p(f_{n}+\kappa f^{2}_{n})-\nabla_{p}g_{n}-p(g_{n}+\kappa g^{2}_{n}))dpdxdt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(f_{n}-g_{n})p\cdot\nabla_{p}(f_{n}-g_{n})(f_{n}-g_{n})(1+\kappa f_{n}+\kappa g_{n})dpdxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(f_{n}-g_{n})p\cdot\nabla_{x}(f_{n}-g_{n})(f_{n}-g_{n})(1+\kappa f_{n}+\kappa g_{n})dpdxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})(R_{n}(f)-R_{n}(g))dpdxdt.

Using the identities

\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(f_{n}-g_{n})\nabla_{x}(f_{n}-g_{n})(f_{n}-g_{n})=\nabla_{x}((f_{n}-g_{n})\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})-|f_{n}-g_{n}|_{\varepsilon}),

\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(f_{n}-g_{n})\nabla_{p}(f_{n}-g_{n})(f_{n}-g_{n})=\nabla_{p}((f_{n}-g_{n})\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})-|f_{n}-g_{n}|_{\varepsilon}),

we integrate by parts

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f_{n}(T)-g_{n}(T)|_{\varepsilon}dpdx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f_{n}(0)-g_{n}(0)|_{\varepsilon}dpdx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\cdot(\nabla_{p}f_{n}+p(f_{n}+\kappa f^{2}_{n})-\nabla_{p}g_{n}-p(g_{n}+\kappa g^{2}_{n}))dpdxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{div}_{p}[\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)(1+\kappa f_{n}+\kappa g_{n})p]((f_{n}-g_{n})\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})-|f_{n}-g_{n}|_{\varepsilon})dpdxdt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\kappa\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)p\cdot\nabla_{x}(f_{n}+g_{n})((f_{n}-g_{n})\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})-|f_{n}-g_{n}|_{\varepsilon})dpdxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})(R_{n}(f)-R_{n}(g))dpdxdt.

Since
|f_{n}-g_{n}|_{\varepsilon}\to|f_{n}-g_{n}|,

\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})\to\text{sign}(f_{n}-g_{n}),
and
(f_{n}-g_{n})\text{sign}_{\varepsilon}(f_{n}-g_{n})-|f_{n}-g_{n}|_{\varepsilon}\to 0
pointwise as
\varepsilon\to 0,
we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f_{n}(T)-g_{n}(T)|dpdx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f_{n}(0)-g_{n}(0)|dpdx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{sign}(f_{n}-g_{n})\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\cdot(\nabla_{p}f_{n}+p(f_{n}+\kappa f^{2}_{n})-\nabla_{p}g_{n}-p(g_{n}+\kappa g^{2}_{n}))dpdxdt

\displaystyle+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}(f_{n}-g_{n})(R_{n}(f)-R_{n}(g))dpdxdt.

Using the regularity assumptions (
10
) and the fact that
\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\cdot p
and
\text{sign}(f_{n}-g_{n})
are bounded for a fixed
\epsilon,
we can pass to the limit as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f(T)-g(T)|dpdx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)|f_{0}-g_{0}|dpdx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\cdot(\nabla_{p}f+p(f+\kappa f^{2})-\nabla_{p}g-p(g+\kappa g^{2}))\big|dpdxdt

\displaystyle+\lim_{n\to\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}(f_{n}-g_{n})(R_{n}(f)-R_{n}(g))dpdxdt.

(27)

Since
|\text{sign}(f_{n}-g_{n})|\leq 1,
one can check that (
24
) with
\psi\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}(f_{n}-g_{n})
still holds, even if here
\psi
depends on
n.
This provides

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\text{sign}(f_{n}-g_{n})(R_{n}(f)-R_{n}(g))dpdx=0.

Then we let
\epsilon\to 0
in (
3.3
):

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(T)-g(T)|dpdx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f_{0}-g_{0}|dpdx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\nabla_{p}\zeta_{\epsilon}(p)\cdot(\nabla_{p}f+p(f+\kappa f^{2})-\nabla_{p}g-p(g+\kappa g^{2}))\big|dpdxdt.

It is easy to check that

). Moreover,

and

) are bounded. The regularity assumptions (

) imply
|\nabla_{p}f|
,
|\nabla_{p}g|,

f+\kappa f^{2},
and

are in

Hence, we can use the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem to pass to the limit
and obtain

Since

Corollary 3.5
.

f,\,g\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be two weak solutions of (

) with initial conditions
f_{0}
and
g_{0},
respectively. Assume
f
and
g
have regularity as in (
10
). If

f_{0}(x,p)\leq g_{0}(x,p)

for almost every
(x,p)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
then

f(t,x,p)\leq g(t,x,p)

for almost every
(x,p)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for all

In particular, assume there exist constants
\beta_{-}>0
and
\beta_{+}>0
such that

\frac{\beta_{-}M(p)}{1-\kappa\beta_{-}M(p)}\leq f_{0}(x,p)\leq\frac{\beta_{+}M(p)}{1-\kappa\beta_{+}M(p)},\,\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,\,\forall\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(28)

where we require
\beta_{+}<(2\pi)^{d/2}
when

Then the solution
f
satisfies the same bounds, i.e.,

\frac{\beta_{-}M(p)}{1-\kappa\beta_{-}M(p)}\leq f(t,x,p)\leq\frac{\beta_{+}M(p)}{1-\kappa\beta_{+}M(p)},\,\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,\,\forall\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,\forall\,t\geq 0.

(29)

Proof.

The contraction of the
L^{1}
norm and the mass conservation imply

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|f(t,x,p)-g(t,x,p)|+f(t,x,p)-g(t,x,p))dpdx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|f_{0}(x,p)-g_{0}(x,p)|+f_{0}(x,p)-g_{0}(x,p))dpdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(g_{0}-f_{0}+f_{0}-g_{0})dpdx=0.

This shows

(x,p)
and for all

for a.e.
(x,p)
and for all
t\geq 0.

(

4
Lyapunov functional and exponential decay

In this section we shall construct (in several steps) a Lyapunov functional for the decay analysis of (

4.1
Projection operator

We denote the macroscopic densities

\rho_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\infty}(p)dp

and

\rho(t,x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,p)dp.

We define the nonlinear projection operator

\Pi f(t,x,p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\beta(t,x)M(p)}{1-\kappa\beta(t,x)M(p)},\,\,\,f(t,x,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},M^{-1}),

(30)

where
\beta(t,x)
is uniquely chosen by the condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Pi fdp=\rho(t,x).

\Pi f
represents the projection of
f
to the manifold of functions which are local equilibria for (
1
). For
p=(p_{1},...,p_{d})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{i}\Pi fdp=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{i}\frac{\beta(t,x)M(p)}{1-\kappa\beta(t,x)M(p)}dp=0,\,\,\,i\in\{1,...,d\}.

(31)

Since
\partial_{p_{i}}\Pi f=-p_{i}(\Pi f+\kappa(\Pi f)^{2}),
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{i}(\Pi f+\kappa(\Pi f)^{2})dp=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{p_{i}}\Pi fdp=0,

(32)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{i}p_{j}(\Pi f+\kappa(\Pi f)^{2})dp=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{i}\partial_{p_{j}}\Pi fdp=\delta_{ij}\rho,\,\,\,i,j\in\{1,...,d\},

(33)

where
\delta_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,i=j\\
0,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,i\neq j\end{cases}
is the Kronecker delta function.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in C([0,\infty),L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be a weak solution of (
1
) with initial condition
f_{0}
satisfying (
9
). Assume
f
has regularity as in (
10
).
Then there are constants
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
(depending only on
\beta_{-}
and
\beta_{+}
) such that

C_{1}\frac{(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}}{M^{2}}\leq(\rho-\rho_{\infty})^{2}\leq C_{2}\frac{(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}}{M^{2}}.

(34)

Proof.

By the definition of
\Pi
and (
4
) we have

\beta-\beta_{\infty}=\frac{\Pi f-f_{\infty}}{M(1+\kappa\Pi f)(1+\kappa f_{\infty})}

and

\rho-\rho_{\infty}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Pi f-f_{\infty})dp=(\beta-\beta_{\infty})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{Mdp}{(1-\kappa\beta M)(1-\kappa\beta_{\infty}M)}.

These equations imply

(\rho-\rho_{\infty})^{2}=\frac{(\Pi f-f_{\infty})^{2}}{M^{2}(1+\kappa\Pi f)^{2}(1+\kappa f_{\infty})^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{Mdp}{(1-\kappa\beta M)(1-\kappa\beta_{\infty}M)}\right)^{2}.

f
satisfies (
29
) and so
0<\beta_{-}\leq\beta(t,x)\leq\beta_{+}
for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Therefore,
1+\kappa\Pi f
and
1+\kappa f_{\infty}
are bounded from below and above by positive constants. The same claim holds for
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{Mdp}{(1-\kappa\beta M)(1-\kappa\beta_{\infty}M)}.
This implies the claimed inequalities.
∎

4.2
Relative entropy

For non-negative functions
f
and
g
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},
we define the relative entropy functional

\mathrm{H}[f|g]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(f\ln{\frac{f}{g}}-\kappa(1+\kappa f)\ln{\frac{1+\kappa f}{1+\kappa g}}\right)dpdx.

(35)

We note, if
f
and
f_{\infty}
have the same mass, then

\mathrm{H}[f|f_{\infty}]=\mathrm{H}[f]-\mathrm{H}[f_{\infty}].

We study some properties of this functional.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
and

be two non-negative functions satisfying (
29
). Then there are constants
C_{3}>0
and
C_{4}>0
(depending only on
\beta_{-}
and
\beta_{+}

(36)

Moreover, it holds that
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},M^{-1}).

Proof.

We consider the integrand in (
35
): By its Taylor expansion in the variable
f
about
g
with Lagrange reminder term, we obtain

f\ln{\frac{f}{g}}-\kappa(1+\kappa f)\ln{\frac{1+\kappa f}{1+\kappa g}}=\frac{(f-g)^{2}}{2\xi(1+\kappa\xi)},

with a function
\xi=\xi(x,p)
lying between
f
and
g.

Due to the assumptions on
f
and
g,

\xi
satisfies (
29
). As a consequence

\frac{M}{C}\leq 2\xi(1+\kappa\xi)\leq\frac{M}{C^{\prime}}

holds with appropriate positive constants
C
and
C^{\prime},
completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3

Proof of Theorem

We denote the macroscopic flux

and recall

\rho(t,x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fdp.

Let
\phi=\phi(t,x)
be the solution of the Poisson equation

-\Delta_{x}\phi=\rho-\rho_{\infty},\,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\phi\,dx=0,\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,\,t\geq 0.

(45)

Using these functions we define a modified entropy functional

\mathrm{E}[f|f_{\infty}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{H}[f|f_{\infty}]+\delta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx,

where
\delta>0
will be chosen in the proof of Theorem
2.1

\mathrm{E}[f|f_{\infty}]
is equivalent to
||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f
satisfy (
29
).
Then, if
\delta>0
is small enough, there are constants
C_{6}>0
and
C_{7}>0
(depending only on
\beta_{-}
and
\beta_{+}
) such that

C_{6}||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}\leq\mathrm{E}[f|f_{\infty}]\leq C_{7}||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}.

(46)

Proof.

We estimate

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx\right|\leq||\nabla_{x}\phi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}||j||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Because of the elliptic regularity
||\nabla_{x}\phi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
is bounded (up to a constant) by
||\rho-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.
Moreover, the Hölder inequality provides
||\rho-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq||f-f_{\infty}||.

We use
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}pf_{\infty}dp=0
and the Hölder inequality to estimate

\displaystyle||j(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f-f_{\infty})dp\right|^{2}dx}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p|^{2}M(p)dp\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(f-f_{\infty})^{2}}{M}dp\right)dx}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{d}||f-f_{\infty}||.

These estimates show that there is a positive constant
C
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx\right|\leq C||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}.

This estimate and (
36
) show

(C_{1}-\delta C)||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}\leq\mathrm{E}[f|f_{\infty}]\leq(C_{2}+\delta C)||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}.

This shows that, if
\delta
is small enough,
\mathrm{E}[f|f_{\infty}]
is equivalent to
||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}.

∎

We will need the following general functional analysis fact whose proof can be found, for instance, in
[
43
, Lemma 1.2, page 260]
.

Lemma 4.5

(
Lions-Magenes lemma).

Let
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
be three Hilbert spaces, each space included in the following one with dense and bounded embedding,
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
being the dual of
\mathcal{V}
. Then, for
T>0
,
the following inclusion

L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{V})\cap H^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{V}^{\prime})\subset C([0,T],\mathcal{H})

holds true. Moreover, for any
g\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{V})\cap H^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
there holds

t\rightarrow||g(t)||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\in W^{1,1}(0,T)

and

\frac{d}{dt}||g(t)||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}=2\langle g^{\prime}(t),g(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}}\,\,\,\,a.e.\text{ on }(0,T),

where
||\cdot||_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the norm in
\mathcal{H},
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}}
denotes the action of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{V}.

One of the application of this lemma is that if
g_{1}\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{H})\cap H^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{V}^{\prime})
and
g_{2}\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{V})\cap H^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{H}),
then

\frac{d}{dt}\langle g_{1}(t),g_{2}(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle g^{\prime}_{1}(t),g_{2}(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{V}}+\langle g_{1}(t),g^{\prime}_{2}(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the scalar product in
\mathcal{H}.
For more details we refer to
[
33
,
43
]
.
In the following we use this equation for the case
\mathcal{V}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\mathcal{H}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We consider the modified entropy functional

\mathrm{E}[f(t)|f_{\infty}]=\mathrm{H}[f(t)|f_{\infty}]+\delta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx.

We have estimated
\mathrm{H}[f(t)|f_{\infty}]
in (
37
). We want to get a similar estimate for
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx.
To do that we consider its time derivative
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx.
Multiplying (
1
) by
p,
then integrating formally with respect to
p,
we obtain

\partial_{t}j=-\mathrm{div}_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\otimes p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp,

(47)

where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product.
(
29
) implies that the right hand side of this equation is in
H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for all
t\geq 0.

By formally integrating (
1
) with respect to
p,
we obtain

\partial_{t}\rho=-\mathrm{div}_{x}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp\right).

(48)

(
29
) implies that
\partial_{t}\rho\in H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for all
t\geq 0.

Also, by differentiating (
45
) with respect to
t>0
, we get

\partial_{t}(\Delta_{x}\phi)=-\partial_{t}\rho\in H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and so
\partial_{t}\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for all
t\geq 0.
Therefore, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}j\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi dx=\langle\partial_{t}j,\nabla_{x}\phi\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx.

(49)

We estimate this time derivative in the following two steps.

Step 1, Estimates of
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx
:

The Hölder inequality implies

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx\right|\leq||\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\phi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}||j||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

We use (
31
) and the Hölder inequality to obtain

||j(t)||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f-\Pi f)dp\right|^{2}dx\leq d\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(f-\Pi f)^{2}}{M}dpdx.

Using
\partial_{t}\Delta_{x}\phi=-\partial_{t}\rho\in H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and (
48
), we compute

\displaystyle||\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\phi(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\langle\partial_{t}\Delta_{x}\phi,\partial_{t}\phi\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}

\displaystyle=-\Big\langle\text{div}_{x}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp\right),\partial_{t}\phi\Big\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp\right)dx.

The Hölder inequality implies

||\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\phi(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp\right|^{2}dx}.

We use (
32
) and the Hölder inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f-\Pi f)(1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f)dp\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq d||1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(f-\Pi f)^{2}}{M}dpdx.

(50)

||1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
can be bounded by a positive constant depending on
\beta_{-}
and
\beta_{+}.
These estimates show that there is a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot jdx\right|\leq C||f-\Pi f||^{2}.

(51)

Step 2, Estimates of
\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}j,\nabla_{x}\phi\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}
:

We use
(
33
) to write (
47
) as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}j=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{x}\rho-\mathrm{div}_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\otimes p\{(f-\Pi f)(1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f)\}dp-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{x}(\rho-\rho_{\infty})-\mathrm{div}_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\otimes p\{(f-\Pi f)(1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f)\}dp-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp.

We use this equation to compute

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}j,\nabla_{x}\phi\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}=

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{x}(\rho-\rho_{\infty}),\nabla_{x}\phi\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}

\displaystyle-\Big\langle\mathrm{div}_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\otimes p\{(f-\Pi f)(1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f)\}dp,\nabla_{x}\phi\Big\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot p(f+\kappa f^{2})dpdx.

(52)

For the first term we use (
34
) to get

-\langle\nabla_{x}(\rho-\rho_{\infty}),\nabla_{x}\phi\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho-\rho_{\infty})^{2}dx\leq-C_{4}||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||^{2}.

(53)

For the second term in (
4.3
), we use the Hölder inequality

\displaystyle-\Big\langle\mathrm{div}_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\otimes p\{(f-\Pi f)(1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f)\}dp,\nabla_{x}\phi\Big\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p^{T}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}p(f-\Pi f)(1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f)dpdx

\displaystyle\leq||1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}||f-\Pi f||\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p|^{2}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}\right|^{2}Mdpdx}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{d}||1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}||f-\Pi f||\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

\left|\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
is bounded (up to a constant) by
||\rho-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
because of the elliptic regularity. (
34
) implies that
||\rho-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
is bounded (up to a constant) by
||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||.
Moreover,
||1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is bounded by a positive constant depending
\beta_{-}
and
\beta_{+}.
Hence, there is a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

-\Big\langle\mathrm{div}_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\otimes p\{(f-\Pi f)(1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f)\}dp,\nabla_{x}\phi\Big\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}\leq C^{\prime}||f-\Pi f||\,||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||.

(54)

We estimate the last term in (
4.3
) by using the Hölder inequality and (
4.3
)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot p(f+\kappa f^{2})dpdx

\displaystyle\leq||\nabla_{x}\phi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(f+\kappa f^{2})dp\right|^{2}dx}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{d}||1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}||\nabla_{x}\phi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,||f-\Pi f||.

Similarly,
||\nabla_{x}\phi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
is bounded (up to a constant) by
||\rho-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
because of the elliptic regularity. As before,
||\rho-\rho_{\infty}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
is bounded (up to a constant) by
||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||
and
||1+\kappa f+\kappa\Pi f||^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is bounded by a positive constant.
Hence, there is a constant
\tilde{C}>0
such that

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot p(f+\kappa f^{2})dpdx\leq\tilde{C}||f-\Pi f||\,||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||.

(55)

(
4.3
), (
53
), (
54
), and (
55
) imply

\langle\partial_{t}j,\nabla_{x}\phi\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}}\leq-C_{4}||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||^{2}+(C^{\prime}+\tilde{C})||f-\Pi f||\,||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||.

(
49
), the above estimate, and (
51
) yield

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi(T)\cdot j(T)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi(0)\cdot j(0)dx+\int_{0}^{T}\Big[-C_{4}||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||^{2}+C||f-\Pi f||^{2}\\
+(C^{\prime}+\tilde{C})||f-\Pi f||\,||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||\Big]dt

(56)

for all
T>0.

Step 3, Grönwall’s inequality:
We now summarize all estimates. (
37
) and (
56
) yield

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[f(T)|f_{\infty}]-\mathrm{E}[f(0)|f_{\infty}]\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\Big[(C_{5}-\delta C)||f-\Pi f||^{2}

\displaystyle+\delta C_{4}||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||^{2}-\delta(C^{\prime}+\tilde{C})||f-\Pi f||\,||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||\Big]dt.

One can check that if
\delta>0
is small enough, there is a constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

\mathrm{E}[f(T)|f_{\infty}]-\mathrm{E}[f(0)|f_{\infty}]\leq-2C^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}(||f-\Pi f||^{2}+||\Pi f-f_{\infty}||^{2})dt\leq-C^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{T}||f-f_{\infty}||^{2}dt.

We choose
\delta>0
so that this inequality and (
46
) hold.
The first inequality in (
46
) implies

\mathrm{E}[f(T)|f_{\infty}]-\mathrm{E}[f(0)|f_{\infty}]\leq-\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{C_{6}}\int^{T}_{0}\mathrm{E}[f(t)|f_{\infty}]dt.

Since this inequality holds for all
T>0
, Grönwall’s inequality implies

\mathrm{E}[f(t)|f_{\infty}]\leq e^{-\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{C_{6}}t}\mathrm{E}[f_{0}|f_{\infty}],\,\,\,\forall\,t\geq 0.

This inequality and (
46
) provide (
11
) with
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\frac{C_{7}}{C_{6}}}
and
2\lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{C_{6}}.

∎

Comparison of the acute effects of traditional and reaction-based HIIT on brain connectivity and cognitive responses
Authors: Musab Çağın, Halil İbrahim Cicioğlu, Hayri Ertan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of this study is to comparatively investigate the acute effects of traditional and reaction-based HIIT (High Intensity Interval Training) methods on brain connectivity and cognitive responses. A total of 33 athletes (17 female, 16 male), 3 from each of the elite shooting, badminton, basketball, gymnastics, football, softball, karate, taekwondo, tennis, swimming and volleyball branches, were included in the study. The average age of the athletes included in the study was 21.4 ± 1.8 years and the average sports history was 9.4 ± 2.9 years. The athletes were equally and randomly distributed to traditional HIIT (11), reaction-based HIIT (11) and control groups (11). First, 2-min eyes-open resting EEG recordings were taken from the athletes. After the resting records, the Stroop Test consisting of 100 images (50% compatible) was applied in a computer environment and EEG records were taken simultaneously. The athletes applied a 5-min run and warm-up exercises. At the end of the warm-up period, the athletes applied 10 min of HIIT according to their groups. After the completion of HIIT, rating of perceived exertion was applied to determine the perceived exertion. Finally, the Stroop post-tests simultaneously with the EEG of all groups were taken and the measurement process was completed. All measurements related to the research were carried out at 23.1 °C (± 0.17) ambient temperature in Gazi University, Faculty of Sports Sciences Wrestling Hall. Three-way repeated measures ANOVA and independent groups T-Test were applied to the data obtained from the athletes in R and SPSS statistical programs. According to the obtained findings, significant differences were detected between the fronto-temporal, fronto-occipital, occipito-temporal regions in terms of connectivity and between the groups in terms of perceived difficulty level (p < 0.05). As a result of the research, in terms of brain connectivity; While the reaction-based HIIT method was observed to have positive effects on cognitive processes such as focused attention and decision-making skills by increasing synchronization especially in the fronto-temporal regions; it was observed that the traditional HIIT method decreased the perception of effort of the activity by decreasing the synchronization in the fronto-occipital region and contributed positively to the visual information processing processes by increasing the synchronization in the occipito-temporal region. In addition, although the heart rates of both groups were similar during HIIT, the reaction-based HIIT group perceived a higher level of difficulty than the traditional HIIT group. In the light of these results, it can be said that the use of applications that include unpredictability and variable environmental conditions, such as reaction-based HIIT, more in the development of athletic performance can make the applied training method more functional in cognitive terms.Keywords: Reaction, HIIT, Brain connectivity, Training

Introduction

Motor skills such as strength, speed, endurance, flexibility, coordination, and agility are observed to be prominent in determining athletic performance [2]. However, in terms of the proper and effective use of these skills, cognitive factors such as reaction, selective attention, cognitive flexibility, and decision-making play a crucial role [9, 10, 18]. Even if an athlete has highly developed motor skills, it is highly likely that they will not be able to perform as desired if they cannot use these skills correctly. An example of this is Usain Bolt, the 100-m world and Olympic champion, who was disqualified from the 2011 World Athletics Championships for reacting too early and making a false start. Therefore, it is thought that combining cognitive-based applications with physical applications in training planning can make the training methods more effective from a cognitive perspective.

Reaction, by its nature, encompasses the process of perceiving and processing external stimuli and ultimately responding to them [9, 10, 23]. In this sense, reaction ability can be said to serve as a “bridge between motor and cognitive performance.” Athletes are exposed to numerous internal and external stimuli during competition and must respond as quickly and accurately as possible to these unpredictable situations. In this regard, the more athletes are exposed to variable environmental conditions and stimuli during training, the more likely they are to respond effectively to developing situations during competition. Although reaction ability is an important parameter for athletic performance, it is considered to be insufficiently addressed in training planning and periodization [2, 17, 22]. It has been observed that programs designed for athletes consist of closed skill applications aimed at developing specific motor parameters. However, open skill applications are needed to address the unpredictable situations and variable environmental conditions that arise during competition. Combining reaction-based exercises with any closed skill exercise will transform the closed skill exercise into an open skill exercise by introducing unpredictability and variable environmental conditions to the applied method. In this regard, it would be accurate to describe reaction-based exercises as a “key that unlocks closed skills”.

The HIIT method is one of the most frequently used training methods for improving athletic performance. The HIIT method ranked first in the “Global Fitness Trends Survey” list in 2014 and 2018, and was among the top five exercise methods between 2014 and 2019 [27]. Compared to other training methods, HIIT is considered one of the most favored training methods by athletes and coaches, both for its ability to maximize efficiency by utilizing time more economically and for its perceived enjoyment compared to other methods. Additionally, the fact that HIIT can be easily combined with various sports (football, basketball, athletics, wrestling, swimming, etc.) makes it an applicable training method for many sports. HIIT is classified as a closed skill application due to its structure, which involves specific movement and rest cycles.

In light of this information, the effect of the reaction-based HIIT method, which has been restructured based on reactions and converted into an open skill application, on brain connectivity, which is considered an indicator of cognitive performance processes, is a matter of interest, as the effects of the traditional HIIT method on physical performance have been clearly proven and it is applied in many sports branches worldwide. In this context, the present study aims to reveal the effects of reaction-based applications on cognitive performance and to provide a different perspective on the importance of cognitive-based training methods in training science.

Methods

Study group

The study included a total of 33 elite athletes (17 female, 16 male) practicing the branches of shooting, badminton, basketball, gymnastics, football, softball, karate, taekwondo, tennis, swimming, and volleyball, with three athletes from each sport. The mean age of the athletes included in the study was 21.4 ± 1.8 years, and their mean sports experience was 9.4 ± 2.9 years (Table 1). The athletes were randomly and equally distributed into traditional HIIT (11), reaction-based HIIT (11), and control groups (11). The athletes were informed about the study prior to the measurements and HIIT applications. Participation in the study was voluntary, and signed informed consent forms were obtained from all athletes included in the study. This study was conducted with the approval of the Gazi University Ethics Committee (E-77082166–302.08.01–904780) and was carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study design

The athletes were randomly assigned to traditional HIIT, reaction-based HIIT, and control groups in equal numbers. This randomization process was carried out using the stratified randomization method, taking into account the branches and sex of the athletes. A measurement schedule consisting of 11 consecutive days was created for the athletes' measurements. The athletes were assigned to sessions between 11:00 a.m. and 1:00 p.m., 1:00 p.m. and 3:00 p.m., and 3:00 p.m. and 5:00 p.m. daily within this schedule. During this process, measurements were taken from a total of three athletes daily, one from each group (Table 2).

Measurement schedule

EEG caps of appropriate size were identified and placed on the athletes' heads to prepare them for the measurement. Two-minute EEG recordings were taken from the athletes while they were resting with their eyes open. Immediately after the resting recordings, the Stroop Test (50% congruent) consisting of 100 visuals was administered in a computer environment, and EEG recordings were taken simultaneously. A heart rate monitor was placed on the chests of the athletes in the traditional and reaction-based HIIT groups after the pre-measurements of the Stroop Test were completed to monitor their heart rates. The athletes were warmed up with a 5-min light jog and stretching exercises. At the end of the warm-up, the athletes were administered a 10-min HIIT according to their groups. Immediately after completing the HIIT, the Borg Scale was applied to determine the perceived exertion. The control group sat without engaging in any physical or cognitive activity for the total duration of the warm-up and HIIT, which was 15 min. At the end of the 15-min process, the Stroop post-tests were administered to all groups simultaneously with EEG, completing the measurement process (Table 3). All measurements for the study were conducted at the Wrestling Hall of the Faculty of Sports Sciences at Gazi University between 11:00 AM and 5:00 PM. The mean ambient temperature during the measurements was determined as 23.1°C (± 0.17).

HIIT applications

Traditional HIIT

The athletes in the traditional HIIT group performed the HIIT protocol at 85% of their maximum heart rate. A movement guide showing the order in which the athletes were to perform each movement was posted on the wall at a size that allowed the athletes to easily see the movements (Fig. 1). The researcher demonstrated how each movement was to be performed before the athletes began the exercise.

Traditional HIIT group exercise guide

Heart rates were monitored using a heart rate sensor placed on the athletes' chest area, and when the heart rate was below or above the desired level, the athlete was alerted so that the heart rate was maintained at the desired level. Ten exercise cycles were created, each consisting of 20 s of exercise followed by 10 s of rest, and this cycle was repeated twice with a one-minute rest period in between (Table 4). All exercises in the HIIT protocol were performed dynamically throughout the exercise process.

Data collection tools

EEG device

The EEG data was collected using the Mentalab Explore EEG device (Mentalab US LLC, San Diego, CA). Silver/silver chloride (Ag/AgCl) electrodes were used (Fig. 3). The data was recorded using Mentalab Explore software (version 0.2.3, Munich). EEG caps in sizes S or M were selected to fit the athletes' heads. The electrodes were placed on the EEG cap according to the 10–20 electrode position system at the F3, F4, C3, C4, O1, O2, T7, T8, and M1 regions. The ground (reference) electrode was placed on the left ear lobe. To reduce skin–electrode impedance, the electrode positions on the scalp were prepared with a recording gel (Nuprep, Weaver and Co., Aurora CO) to ensure optimal recording conditions. Throughout the study, data were monitored to ensure that the impedance level remained below 10 kΩ. Data were recorded at a sampling rate of 500 Hz and online with a 1–40 Hz bandpass filter and a 50 Hz notch filter.

Mentalab Explore EEG device [13]

Stroop test

The Stroop test, a psychophysiological test battery, measures cognitive flexibility, selective attention, and inhibitory control [14, 26]. The Stroop Test was conducted simultaneously with EEG measurements via computer. The test consisted of a total of 100 stimuli, 50% of which were congruent (e.g., the word “BLUE” written in blue) and 50% of which were incongruent (e.g., the word “RED” written in yellow or green). The athletes responded to the stimuli displayed on the computer screen by pressing the colors previously assigned to the letters on the keyboard. After completing the practice test consisting of 10 stimuli, the athletes performed the main test.

Heart rate monitoring

The athletes' maximum heart rates were determined using the Karvonen Formula (220 – age) [16]. This ensured that the athletes performed the HIIT protocol at 85% of their maximum heart rate. The athletes' heart rates were measured using the Polar H9 HRV sensor. This device, which is placed on the athletes' chest area with a wearable chest strap, has a high degree of accuracy and precision. The device also has the ability to track real-time heart rates via Bluetooth on a mobile app. Therefore, the athletes' heart rates were monitored using this device during the implementation of both HIIT protocols.

Reaction device

The Fitlight Trainer (Ontario/Canada) is a luminous exercise and reaction measurement device that contains six sensor lights and one control module. The Fitlight Trainer includes various test and exercise programs, and protocols can be created according to the purpose and sample group. Therefore, the Fitlight Trainer (Ontario/Canada) reaction device was used in the application of the reaction-based HIIT protocol. The device's light sensor was placed on the wall in front of the athlete so that it could be easily seen. The device was programmed to flash blue, red, green, purple, or yellow every 5 s. Thus, during the 20 s of the exercise, the device randomly changed colors four times, and the athletes performed the four movements represented by the colors. At the end of each 20-s interval, the device was programmed to remain off for 10 s, after which it continued to flash randomly every 5 s for 10 s Fig. 3.

Rating of perceived exertion

The perceived exertion of the athletes immediately following HIIT was determined using the Borg Scale (1–10). The Borg Scale is a scale used to determine the perceived exertion of an individual during physical activity. The individual rates the activity on a scale of 1 to 10 immediately after the activity. 1 indicates low intensity, while 10 indicates high intensity. Previous studies have shown that perceived exertion levels are associated with peak oxygen consumption percentage, heart rate reserve percentage, and blood lactate levels, and that these levels increase in direct proportion to increasing exercise intensity [3].

Data analysis

Analysis of EEG data

The collected EEG data were processed using the MATLAB R2023a software (MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA) and the EEGLAB v2022 package [11]. First, the data were filtered using finite impulse response (FIR) (zero-phase, non-causal) in the 1–35 Hz range. Epochs obtained during the Stroop task were separated, and large artifacts (100 μV) were excluded from the data. Contaminations (eye blinks, movements) present in the separated data were detected using the Runica algorithm within EEGLAB and removed from the data. Subsequently, a surface Laplacian filter was applied to the artifact-free data to minimize volume conduction effects. The signals were then divided into frequency bands: theta (4–7 Hz), alpha (7–13 Hz), beta1 (13–18 Hz), beta2 (18–24 Hz), and beta3 (24–30 Hz). The phase locking value method was applied to the data from 28 electrode pairs for each frequency, and phase matches were calculated for each electrode pair. To calculate the phase locking values, the filtered signals were first divided into the specified frequency bands, then transformed using the Hilbert transform, and the signal phase was calculated using the original and transformed signals. Then, the mean phase difference between the two signals was calculated and evaluated for statistical analysis. Figure 4 shows the formula for PLV analysis. Statistical analysis

The statistical software R (version 4.1.2) and SPSS (version 30.0) were used for data analysis. Three-way repeated measures ANOVA was applied in the analysis of the data. This analysis examined the interactions between the group (control, traditional HIIT, reaction-based HIIT), time (pre-test, post-test), and condition (congruent, incongruent) variables. The normality assumption was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test prior to ANOVA, and variance homogeneity was checked using Mauchly's Sphericity Test. When the sphericity assumption was violated, the Greenhouse–Geisser correction was applied. In cases where significant interactions were found, Bonferroni correction was applied, and post-hoc tests were conducted to examine the differences between groups in detail. For the comparison of perceived exertion levels and mean heart rates between groups, an independent samples t-test was applied. For this study, statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion

The present study aimed to compare the acute effects of traditional and reaction-based HIIT on brain connectivity and cognitive responses. To this end, EEG and perceived difficulty data obtained from the Stroop test administered before and after HIIT sessions were analyzed and compared. When examining the connectivity values obtained from the study, high synchronization in the fronto-temporal, fronto-occipital, and occipito-temporal regions stands out Fig. 5.

When connectivity results were compared between groups, it was determined that the traditional HIIT group had decreased connectivity strength between the frontal and temporal regions in the Alpha, Beta 1, and Beta 2 frequency bands in the right hemisphere (F4-T8). Additionally, it was found that the control group had decreased connectivity strength between the frontal and temporal regions in the Beta 1 and Beta 3 frequency bands in the interhemispheric (F3-T8) region, while the reaction-based HIIT group had increased connectivity strength between the frontal and temporal regions in the Beta 1 and Beta 3 frequency bands in the interhemispheric (F3-T8) region. A review of the literature indicates that increases in connectivity within the fronto-temporal network associated with physical activity generally reflect enhanced brain plasticity and improved cognitive function [5, 21]. Furthermore, it is known that exercise promotes the release of neurotrophic factors such as brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), which plays an important role in synaptic flexibility, learning, and memory processes, in addition to supporting neuronal growth, survival, and connectivity [7, 12]. Therefore, it is thought that the increase in connectivity of the fronto-temporal network after physical activity is due to BDNF release. In this context, when intergroup fronto-temporal connectivity strengths were evaluated, it was observed that while the values of the traditional HIIT and control groups decreased, particularly in the Beta frequency band, the values of the reaction-based HIIT group increased. This is thought to be due to the reaction-based HIIT having a greater stimulatory effect on BDNF compared to the other groups. Additionally, the high synchronization values in the Beta frequency between hemispheres in the reaction-based HIIT group may be related to focused attention and effective decision-making skills, and therefore, this situation may play an important role in demonstrating superior performance [13, 19].

Another finding of the study revealed that the traditional HIIT group exhibited reduced connectivity strengths between the occipital and frontal regions in the Beta 1 and Beta 2 frequency bands in the right hemisphere (F4-O2). In a study examining attention focus and functional connectivity in cycling, the automatism of the basic components of movement and riding methods focused on the relational internal/external components of the activity were compared during cycling [8]. The study found that focusing on the automation of the fundamental components of the action minimized awareness of the aversive afferent feedback manifested as fronto-occipital connectivity in the beta band, thereby delaying harmful increases in perceived exertion [8, 31]. In this context, in the present study comparing the traditional HIIT method, which involves performing pre-determined movements in an automatic cycle for a specified period of time, with the reaction-based HIIT method, which involves reacting to stimuli sent by lights that are internal components of the activity, it was found that the traditional HIIT group generally showed a decrease in fronto-occipital connectivity in the beta frequency bands of the right hemisphere, which is associated with spatial awareness, holistic thinking, and perception, which is consistent with the findings of studies conducted on cyclists [24]. This is because the traditional HIIT method incorporates action automatism, while the reaction-based HIIT method focuses on the internal components of the action. Therefore, it is thought that the decrease in fronto-occipital connectivity in the beta 1 and beta 2 frequency bands of the right hemisphere in the traditional HIIT group is due to a decrease in afferent feedback awareness.

Another finding showed that the traditional HIIT group had increased connectivity strength between the occipital and temporal regions in the interhemispheric (O1-T8) Beta 1, Beta 2, and Beta 3 frequency bands. Additionally, it was found that the interhemispheric (O1-T8, O2-T7) Alpha frequency band connectivity strength between the occipital and temporal regions increased in the traditional HIIT group. The occipito-temporal connection is a crucial network for processing visual information by integrating it with memory and recognition processes [6, 29]. It is believed that the occipito-temporal connection plays a decisive role in superior athletic performance, particularly in terms of the athletes' ability to control their environment during competition, position themselves according to their opponent's position, and generate experience-based strategies according to the situation. When examining the structure of the traditional HIIT protocol applied in the study, it is seen to include a cycle of pre-determined movements. This allows athletes in the traditional HIIT group to easily memorize the movements they are required to perform and position themselves more easily for the next movement. In this context, it is thought that the high synchronization in the occipito-temporal regions of the Alpha and Beta bands between the hemispheres stems from the predictable and memorizable structure of traditional HIIT.

When perceived exertion levels and mean heart rates were compared between the groups, it was found that although the mean heart rate values of the two groups were similar, the reaction-based HIIT group had higher perceived exertion levels from the activity compared to the traditional HIIT group. Perceived exertion rating methods are quite important for understanding and interpreting the psychophysiological stress experienced by individuals during physical activity [4, 15, 30]. Perceived exertion is associated with various psychophysiological variables [20]. These variables include physiological variables such as metabolic rate, ventilation, blood flow, and muscle fatigue, and psychological variables such as motivation, mental stress, arousal, and exercise experience [1, 3, 20, 25, 28]. In this context, although the groups had similar physiological outputs in terms of heart rate, the reaction-based HIIT group, which contained more stress and arousal, had higher levels of perceived exertion than the traditional HIIT group, which is consistent with the literature.

Conclusion and suggestions

In conclusion, in terms of brain connectivity, the reaction-based HIIT method was observed to have positive effects on cognitive processes such as focused attention and decision-making skills by increasing synchronization in the fronto-temporal regions, while the traditional HIIT method was observed to reduce synchronization in the fronto-occipital region, thereby lowering the perceived exertion of the activity, and to positively contribute to visual information processing by increasing synchronization in the occipito-temporal region. Additionally, although the heart rates of both groups were similar throughout HIIT, the reaction-based HIIT group had higher levels of perceived exertion than the traditional HIIT group. The hypothesis of the study was that reaction-based HIIT, which can be described as an open skill exercise due to its unpredictable and variable environmental conditions, is a more effective method cognitively than traditional HIIT, which can be described as a closed skill exercise due to its predictable and stable environmental conditions. When evaluating the effects of reaction-based HIIT on the fronto-temporal regions, the study results support the hypothesis. Reaction-based applications can be considered a “key to unlocking closed skills” in that they make closed skill training, which is predictable and has fixed environmental conditions, unpredictable and variable in terms of environmental conditions. Additionally, although the athletes exerted similar effort, the reaction-based HIIT group had higher perceived exertion levels, which is another significant finding of the study. Considering that athletes are exposed to more mental stress and arousal in competition than in training, it is thought that the reaction-based HIIT method, which incorporates cognitive stress and arousal, could be an important tool for coaches in creating competition simulations for athletes. In light of these results, it can be said that the increased use of applications such as reaction-based HIIT, which incorporate unpredictability and variable environmental conditions, in the development of athletic performance can make the training method more functional in cognitive terms. Therefore, it is suggested to combine reaction-based applications with training methods used to develop various athletic skills as it could make the training method more effective from a cognitive perspective.

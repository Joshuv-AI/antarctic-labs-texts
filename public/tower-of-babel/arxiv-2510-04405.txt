Dyonic RN-like and Taub-NUT-like black holes in Einstein-bumblebee gravity
Shoulong Li, Liang Liang, Liang Ma

Abstract
Einstein-bumblebee gravity is one of the simplest vector-tensor theories that realizes spontaneous Lorentz symmetry breaking. In this work, we first construct an exact dyonic Reissner-Nordström-like black hole solution in four dimensions, carrying both electric and magnetic charges and admitting general topological horizons. We then study its thermodynamic properties, and employ the Wald formalism to compute the conserved mass and entropy, thereby establishing the first law of black hole thermodynamics. Furthermore, we generalize these results to Taub-Newman-Unti-Tamburino case and higher dimensions case.

Now we turn to the thermodynamics of the black hole. The event horizon, located at

f(r_{h})=0

\xi=\partial_{t}
becomes null, i.e.,
\xi_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}\big|_{r=r_{h}}=0
. It is convenient to express the constant

in terms of
r_{h}
, namely

m=kr_{h}+\frac{p^{2}(\ell+1)}{2r_{h}(3\ell+2)}+\frac{q^{2}}{2r_{h}(\ell+2)}\,.

(35)

The black hole temperature
T
is defined in terms of the surface gravity
K
, which is computed from the Killing vector
\xi

K^{2}=-\frac{\nabla^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}}{2}\Big|_{r=r_{h}}\,,\quad T=\frac{K}{2\pi}\,.

Accordingly, the temperature reads

T=\frac{h^{\prime}\left(r_{h}\right)}{4\pi\sqrt{\ell+1}}=\frac{k}{4\pi\sqrt{\ell+1}r_{h}}-\frac{q^{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{\ell+1}(\ell+2)r_{h}^{3}}-\frac{p^{2}\sqrt{\ell+1}}{8\pi(3\ell+2)r_{h}^{3}}\,.

(37)

Next, in order to obtain the electric charge
Q_{e}
, it is convenient to introduce an antisymmetric tensor

{\cal F}^{\mu\nu}=(1-4\gamma_{1}B_{\lambda}B^{\lambda})F^{\mu\nu}+4\gamma_{2}B_{\lambda}F^{\lambda[\mu}B^{\nu]}\,,

(38)

to rewrite the Maxwell field EOM (

E_{A}^{\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\mu}{\cal F}^{\mu\nu}=0\,.

(39)

Equivalently, the above expression can be cast in the language of differential forms as

\displaystyle d*\mathcal{F}_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}}=0\,,\quad*\mathcal{F}_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}}=\frac{1}{2!2!}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathcal{F}^{\rho\sigma}dx^{\mu}\wedge dx^{\nu}\,,

(40)

where the subscript
(n)
denotes an
n
-form,
d
is the exterior derivative,
*
the Hodge dual, and the Levi-Civita tensor

is defined as
\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\sqrt{-g}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
with
\varepsilon_{0123}=1
.
In terms of the Eq. (

is then defined as

\displaystyle Q_{e}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\Omega_{2,k}}*\mathcal{F}_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}}=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\Omega_{2,k}}\sqrt{-g}\varepsilon_{tru\varphi}\mathcal{F}^{rt}dud\varphi=\frac{qw_{2}\sqrt{\ell+1}}{\kappa(\ell+2)}\,,

(41)

where
w_{2}=\int_{\Omega_{2,k}}dud\varphi
. For

, corresponding to the unit 2-sphere, one has
w_{2}=4\pi
.
The electric potential
\Phi_{e}
is defined through
A_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}}

\displaystyle\Phi_{e}=i_{\xi}A_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}}|^{r=r_{h}}_{r\rightarrow\infty}=\xi^{\mu}A_{\mu}|^{r=r_{h}}_{r\rightarrow\infty}=\frac{q}{r_{h}}\,,

(42)

where

on the index of the

The Maxwell field strength

to be defined in terms of it

\displaystyle Q_{m}=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\Omega_{2,k}}F_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}}=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\Omega_{2,k}}F_{u\varphi}dud\varphi=\frac{w_{2}p}{2\kappa}\,.

(43)

According to
Rasheed:1997ns

is closed when the on-shell condition is satisfied (i.e. the EOMs are satisfied), we can define a corresponding 1-form
\mathcal{A}_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}}

\mathcal{A}_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}}=\frac{2p(\ell+1)^{3/2}}{r(3\ell+2)}dt-\frac{2q\sqrt{\ell+1}}{(\ell+2)}ud\varphi\,.

(44)

Similarly, the magnetic potential
\Phi_{m}
can therefore be defined in terms of
\mathcal{A}_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}}

\displaystyle\Phi_{m}=i_{\xi}\mathcal{A}_{{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}}|^{r=r_{h}}_{r\rightarrow\infty}=\xi^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}|^{r=r_{h}}_{r\rightarrow\infty}=\frac{2p(\ell+1)^{3/2}}{(3\ell+2)r_{h}}\,.

(45)

To complete the thermodynamic quantities, we now turn to the mass and entropy of the black hole. The Komar mass associated with the timelike Killing vector

\displaystyle M_{\textup{K}}=-\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{\Omega_{2,k}}*d\xi\big|_{r\to\infty}=\frac{2}{\kappa}\int_{\Omega_{2,k}}dud\varphi\sqrt{-g}\varepsilon_{tru\varphi}\nabla^{r}\xi^{t}\big|_{r\to\infty}=\frac{mw_{2}}{\kappa\sqrt{1+\ell}}\,.

. However, the Komar mass and Wald entropy, together with the temperature, electric and magnetic charges, and their corresponding potentials, do not satisfy the first law of black hole thermodynamics:

Similar discrepancies have also been observed in the neutral cases, as discussed in Refs.

;
Chen:2025ypx
.

;

to consistently recompute the mass and entropy.

IV.1
Wald formalism

In 1993, Wald developed the covariant phase space formalism
Wald:1993nt
;
Iyer:1994ys
. Within this framework, he demonstrated that in a diffeomorphism-invariant theory of gravity, the Killing vector
\xi^{\mu}
generates all conserved charges, thereby unifying these quantities into a single differential relation, i.e., the first law of black hole mechanics. In other words, the first law emerges as a natural consequence of Noether’s theorem, ensuring that any diffeomorphism-invariant theory of gravity necessarily admits a self-consistent black hole thermodynamics.

A direct consequence of the Wald formalism is the Wald entropy formula, which extends the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy and possesses remarkable universality. In higher-derivative gravity theories, black hole entropy is no longer proportional to the horizon area. The Wald entropy formula, however, correctly accounts for these corrections
Fan:2014ala
;
Ma:2023qqj
;
Hu:2023gru
and is thus widely used in many black hole–related effective theories
Liu:2014dva
;
Ma:2022nwq
;
Liu:2022wku
;
Ma:2024ynp
.
Thus far, the Wald formalism and the Wald entropy formula have been applied to a broad range of extended gravity theories, including tensor-scalar theories
Liu:2013gja
;
Lu:2014maa
;
Feng:2015oea
;
Feng:2015wvb
, Maxwell theory and its extensions
Gao:2003ys
;
Li:2016nll
;
Liu:2014tra
;
Gao:2003ys
;
Fan:2014ala
;
Lu:2013ura
;
Chow:2013gba
, as well as the neutral bumblebee theory
An:2024fzf
;
Chen:2025ypx
.

In fact, the Wald entropy formula is simply a corollary of the Wald formalism and can be obtained from it only under specific conditions. Indeed, previous studies of Horndeski theory and bumblebee theory have demonstrated that the Wald entropy formula fails to provide a black hole entropy consistent with the first law of thermodynamics
An:2024fzf
;
Chen:2025ypx
;
Feng:2015oea
;
Feng:2015wvb
. More precisely, the dependence of the Noether charge on the Killing vector involves only
\xi^{\mu}
and
\nabla^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}
. On the black hole horizon, if all fields are smooth, one has
\xi\to 0
and
\nabla^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}\to K\epsilon^{\mu\nu}
, from which the Wald entropy formula follows. However, the
B_{\mu}
field (
15
,
16
) diverges on the black hole horizon, rendering the Wald entropy formula inapplicable. Hence, the complete thermodynamics must be derived directly from the original Wald formalism.

Consider a diffeomorphism invariant theory whose action
{\cal S}
in the language of differential form is given by

{\cal S}[\psi]=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int\mathbf{L}(\psi)\,,

(49)

where
\psi
collectively denotes all dynamical fields in the system. The Lagrangian
D
-form
\mathbf{L}
is the Hodge dual of the Lagrangian density
L=L_{1}+L_{2}
, namely,

\mathbf{L}=*L=\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{D!}\varepsilon_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{D}}Ldx^{\alpha_{1}}\wedge dx^{\alpha_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx^{\alpha_{D}}\,.

(50)

The variation of the Lagrangian
\mathbf{L}
takes the general form

\delta\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{E}_{\psi}\delta\psi+d\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta\psi)\,,

(51)

where
\mathbf{E}_{\psi}
represents the EOM, and
\mathbf{\Theta}
is the symplectic potential
(D-1)
-form. Explicitly,

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{(D-1)!}\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{D-1}}\Theta^{\mu}dx^{\alpha_{1}}\wedge dx^{\alpha_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx^{\alpha_{D-1}}\,,

(52)

\displaystyle i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{(D-2)!}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{D-2}}\Theta^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}dx^{\alpha_{1}}\wedge dx^{\alpha_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx^{\alpha_{D-2}}\,.

(53)

From
\mathbf{\Theta}
, one can define the symplectic current
(D-1)
-form
\mathbf{\omega}
as

\mathbf{\omega}(\psi,\delta_{1}\psi,\delta_{2}\psi)=\delta_{1}\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta_{2}\psi)-\delta_{2}\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta_{1}\psi)\,.

(54)

We next specialize to a variation induced by an infinitesimal diffeomorphism generated by an arbitrary vector field
\xi^{\mu}
, the dynamical fields transform as

\delta_{\xi}\psi=\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\psi\,,

(55)

with
\mathscr{L}_{\xi}
representing the Lie derivative along
\xi^{\mu}
. The corresponding variation of the Lagrangian
\mathbf{L}
can likewise be expressed in two equivalent forms. On the one hand, from the general variation formula we have

\delta_{\xi}\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{E}_{\psi}\delta_{\xi}\psi+d\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta_{\xi}\psi)\,,

(56)

while on the other hand, using the diffeomorphism invariance of the theory, it is given by the Lie derivative of
\mathbf{L}
, namely

\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\mathbf{L}=i_{\xi}d\mathbf{L}+d(i_{\xi}\mathbf{L})=d(i_{\xi}\mathbf{L})\,,

(57)

where we have used
d\mathbf{L}=0
since the
\mathbf{L}
is a
D
-form on a
D
-dimensional manifold. One can introduce a Noether current
(D-1)
-form
\mathbf{J}_{\xi}
, defined by

\mathbf{J}_{\xi}=\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta_{\xi}\psi)-i_{\xi}\mathbf{L}\,.

(58)

Combining Eqs. (
56
) and (
57
) gives

d\mathbf{J}_{\xi}=-\mathbf{E}_{\psi}\delta_{\xi}\psi\,,

(59)

which vanishes on-shell. Consequently, there exists a Noether charge
(D-2)
-form
\mathbf{Q}_{\xi}
such that

\mathbf{J}_{\xi}=d\mathbf{Q}_{\xi}\,.

(60)

The Noether charge can be expressed as

\mathbf{Q}_{\xi}=\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{(D-2)!2!}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{D-2}}Q_{\xi}^{\mu\nu}dx^{\alpha_{1}}\wedge dx^{\alpha_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx^{\alpha_{D-2}}\,.

(61)

Varying the Noether current
\mathbf{J}_{\xi}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{J}_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta_{\xi}\psi)-i_{\xi}\delta\mathbf{L}

(62)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta_{\xi}\psi)-i_{\xi}d\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta\psi)-i_{\xi}(\mathbf{E}_{\psi}\delta\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\psi)-\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta\psi)+d(i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta\psi))\,.

where we have used the on-shell condition,
\mathbf{E}_{\psi}=0
.
According to Eq. (
54
), the first two terms of the right hand side of the Eq. (
62
) can be identified as the symplectic current
(D-1)
-form
\mathbf{\omega}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\omega}(\psi,\delta\psi,\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\psi)-\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta\psi)

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{J}_{\xi}-d(i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta\psi))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\xi}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta\psi))\,.

To make contact with the first law of black hole thermodynamics, we take
\xi^{\mu}
to be the timelike Killing vector that becomes null on the horizon. Wald shows that the variation of the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the integration constants of a specific solution
\psi
is given by

\delta{\cal H}=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{C}\mathbf{\omega}(\psi,\delta\psi,\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\psi)=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\Omega_{D-2}}(\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\xi}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}(\psi,\delta\psi))\,,

(64)

where
C
denotes a Cauchy surface, and
\Omega_{D-2}
is its boundary, which has two components, one at infinity and one on the horizon. Thus according to the Wald formalism, the first
law of black hole thermodynamics is a consequence of

\delta{\cal H}_{\infty}=\delta{\cal H}_{r_{h}}\,.

(65)

For
D=4
dimensional EbM theory with
L=L_{1}+L_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\Theta^{\mu}=\Theta^{\mu}_{1}+\Theta^{\mu}_{2}\,,

(66)

with

\displaystyle\Theta^{\mu}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}(\nabla_{\sigma}\delta g_{\nu\rho}-\nabla_{\rho}\delta g_{\nu\sigma})+\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big[2g^{\mu\lambda}B^{\rho}B^{\sigma}\nabla_{\rho}\delta g_{\lambda\sigma}

(67)

\displaystyle-g^{\mu\lambda}B^{\rho}B^{\sigma}\nabla_{\lambda}\delta g_{\rho\sigma}-g^{\rho\lambda}B^{\mu}B^{\sigma}\nabla_{\sigma}\delta g_{\rho\lambda}-2g^{\rho\lambda}\nabla_{\rho}(B^{\mu}B^{\sigma})\delta g_{\sigma\lambda}

\displaystyle+g^{\mu\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}(B^{\rho}B^{\sigma})\delta g_{\rho\sigma}+g^{\rho\lambda}\nabla_{\sigma}(B^{\mu}B^{\sigma})\delta g_{\rho\lambda}\Big]-2\kappa B^{\mu\nu}\delta B_{\nu}\,,

\displaystyle\Theta^{\mu}_{2}

\displaystyle-\left[(1-4\gamma_{1}B^{\lambda}B_{\lambda})F^{\mu\nu}+4\gamma_{2}F^{\lambda[\mu}B^{\nu]}B_{\lambda}\right]\delta A_{\nu}=-{\cal F}^{\mu\nu}\delta A_{\nu}\,.

(68)

Specializing to a variation induced by
\xi^{\mu}
, and substituting the following equations

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}\,,

(69)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}B_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{\rho}\nabla_{\rho}B_{\mu}+B_{\rho}\nabla_{\mu}\xi^{\rho}\,,

(70)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi}A_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{\rho}\nabla_{\rho}A_{\mu}+A_{\rho}\nabla_{\mu}\xi^{\rho}\,,

(71)

into Eq. (
66

can be obtained from

\nabla_{\nu}Q_{\xi}^{\mu\nu}=J_{\xi}^{\mu}=\Theta^{\mu}(\delta_{\xi})-\xi^{\mu}L+\textup{EOM}\,,

(72)

and is given by

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\nabla^{[\mu}\xi^{\nu]}+2\gamma\left[\xi_{\lambda}\nabla^{[\mu}(B^{\nu]}B^{\lambda})-\xi^{[\mu}\nabla_{\lambda}(B^{\nu]}B^{\lambda})-B^{\lambda}B^{[\mu}\nabla_{\lambda}\xi^{\nu]}\right]-2\kappa B^{\mu\nu}B^{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda}\,,

(73)

\displaystyle Q_{2}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\cal F}^{\mu\nu}A^{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda}\,.

(74)

To specialize our black hole ansatz (
13
)–(
16

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{\xi 1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}\left[\frac{h^{\prime}\sqrt{f}}{\sqrt{h}}+\frac{\ell\sqrt{f}\left(rh^{\prime}-4h\right)}{2r\sqrt{h}}\right]du\wedge d\varphi\,,

(75)

\displaystyle i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}\Bigg[\left(\frac{2\delta f\sqrt{h}}{r\sqrt{f}}+\frac{\delta h^{\prime}\sqrt{f}}{\sqrt{h}}-\frac{h^{\prime}\sqrt{f}\delta h}{2h^{3/2}}+\frac{h^{\prime}\delta f}{2\sqrt{fh}}\right)

(76)

\displaystyle+\ell\left(\frac{\delta f\sqrt{h}}{r\sqrt{f}}-\frac{\delta h\sqrt{f}}{r\sqrt{h}}+\frac{\delta fh^{\prime}}{4\sqrt{hf}}+\frac{\sqrt{f}\delta h^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{h}}-\frac{\delta h\sqrt{f}h^{\prime}}{4h^{3/2}}\right)\Bigg]du\wedge d\varphi\,,

\displaystyle\delta\mathbf{Q}_{\xi 1}-i_{\xi}\mathbf{\Theta}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}\Bigg[-\frac{2\delta f\sqrt{h}}{r\sqrt{f}}(1+\ell)\Bigg]du\wedge d\varphi\,.

These results are consistent with previous works
An:2024fzf
;
Chen:2025ypx
. For the electromagnetic part, it is convenient to introduce a total derivative term

to the integrand of

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we construct four-dimensional dyonic RN-like black holes with general topological horizons within Einstein-bumblebee gravity, one of the simplest vector-tensor theories realizing spontaneous Lorentz symmetry breaking. We then investigate their thermodynamic properties and employ the Wald formalism to compute the conserved mass and entropy, thereby establishing the first law of black hole thermodynamics. Furthermore, we extend the static dyonic solution to the Taub–NUT case, revealing its nontrivial black hole thermodynamics. Finally, we generalize the four-dimensional dyonic RN-like results to higher dimensions. These results provide a concrete framework for further studies of the effects of spontaneous Lorentz symmetry breaking in black hole physics and astrophysics.

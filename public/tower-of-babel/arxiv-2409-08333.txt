Homological conditions on locally gentle algebras
S. Ford, A. Oswald, J. J. Zhang

Abstract
. Gentle algebras are a class of special biserial algebras whose representation theory has been thoroughly described. In this paper, we consider the infinite dimensional generalizations of gentle algebras, referred to as locally gentle algebras. We give combinatorial descriptions of the center, prime spectrum, and homological dimensions of a locally gentle algebra, including an explicit injective resolution. We classify when these algebras are Artin-Schelter Gorenstein, Artin-Schelter regular, and Cohen-Macaulay, and provide an analogue of Stanley’s theorem for locally gentle algebras.

Introduction

Gentle algebras are a well-understood class of finite dimensional bound quiver algebras that were originally studied as iterated tilted algebras of type
A

[

The indecomposable objects of their derived categories are classified in

[

[

[

[

Locally gentle algebras are an infinite dimensional generalization of gentle algebras (see Definition

[
6
]
. Recently, there has been increased interest in this class of algebra. Their Hochschild cohomology is described in
[

[
18
]
gives a bijection between the isomorphism classes and homeomorphism classes of certain marked surfaces. The torsion classes in the category of finite length modules over locally gentle algebras are classified in
[

[
11
]
in terms of string and band modules.

In this paper, we determine when locally gentle algebras satisfy certain homological conditions. Along the way, we extend known results about the homological dimensions of gentle algebras.
In Section
2
, we give the definitions and conventions used throughout the paper, and detail some preliminary properties of locally gentle algebras. One key invariant of a gentle algebra,
A=\Bbbk\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
, is that each arrow in
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
is contained in a unique maximal path, i.e., a path
w
in
\mathcal{Q}
so that
bw=wb=0
for any arrow
b\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
.
The notion of maximal paths carries over to locally gentle algebras in the sense that
each arrow is contained in a unique path which is either maximal in the sense of gentle algebras (referred to as a
finite maximal path
) or an infinite maximal path which is determined by a primitive cycle
c
which is unique up to rotation and whose powers are nonzero. An immediate consequence of this is Proposition
2.13
, which states that the Hilbert series of a (locally) gentle algebra

is

h_{A}(t)=\frac{\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|+(\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|-\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|)t-\sum_{n\geq 1}k_{n}t^{n+1}}{1-t},

where
\mathcal{Q}
is a finite quiver and

is the number of finite maximal paths of length at least
n
. Additionally, the center of a (locally) gentle algebra is generated by certain finite maximal paths and elements

corresponding to infinite maximal paths (see Lemma

In Section

In

2.1.
Graded algebras and modules

In this subsection, we outline notation and definitions used throughout the paper, following
[
20
]

[

]
. Let

\Lambda_{i}

\Lambda

\Lambda=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Lambda_{n}

Let

\GKdim(\Lambda)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log(\dim_{\Bbbk}(V^{n}))}{\log(n)}

(see
[

Let
\mathsf{Gr}\text{-}{\Lambda}
be the category of

-graded right

-modules. Throughout this paper, we work in this category and, unless otherwise specified, all
\Lambda
-modules are assumed to be

-graded right modules. In particular, when we refer to projective and injective

-modules, we mean projective and injective objects in

.
Let

be a
\Lambda
-module. We write

to denote the degree

-module with

For

-modules

\grHom_{\Lambda}(M,N)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\bigoplus\operatorname{Hom}_{\Lambda}^{i}(M,N),

where

, and
\grExt_{\Lambda}^{j}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=R^{j}\grHom_{\Lambda}
.
The

, is the intersection of its maximal homogeneous ideals. Then, we have that

\grJ(\Lambda)=J(\Lambda_{0})\oplus\Lambda_{+}=J(\Lambda_{0})\oplus\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\Lambda_{i},

2.2.
Locally gentle algebras

For a finite set
X
, let
\left|X\right|
denote the number of elements in
X
.
A
finite quiver
,
\mathcal{Q}=(\mathcal{Q}_{0},\mathcal{Q}_{1},s,t)
, is a directed graph where
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
is a set of vertices,
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
is a set of arrows, and the functions

source
and

, and
that any quiver is connected, meaning that the underlying graph is connected.

For a vertex

, let
\indeg(v)
be the number of arrows
\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
so that
t(\alpha)=v
, and let
\outdeg(v)
be the number of arrows
\beta\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
so that

.
If
\indeg(v)=0
, then we say that
v
is a
source
, and if
\outdeg(v)=0
, then we say that
v
is a
sink
.

A
path

where
t(\alpha_{i})=s(\alpha_{i+1})
for every

. We say the path
p
has

n
, and we denote this by
\ell(p)=n
. We denote the first arrow of

by

, i.e.
F(p)=\alpha_{1}
, and the last arrow of

by

For every

\epsilon_{v}
be a stationary path at the vertex

Given a quiver

Any path algebra

If

Definition 2.1
.

An algebra
A=\Bbbk\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
is called a
gentle algebra
if

(1)

the quiver
\mathcal{Q}
is finite, connected, and
\indeg(v),\outdeg(v)\leq 2
for each vertex
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
.

(2)

whenever
s(\alpha)=t(\beta)=t(\beta^{\prime})
with
\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}
, exactly one of
\beta\alpha
and
\beta^{\prime}\alpha
is in
\mathcal{I}
.

(3)

whenever
t(\alpha)=s(\beta)=s(\beta^{\prime})
with
\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}
, then exactly one of
\alpha\beta
and
\alpha\beta^{\prime}
is in
\mathcal{I}
.

(4)

the ideal
\mathcal{I}
is generated by paths of lengths 2.

(5)

the algebra
A
is finite dimensional (i.e.
\mathcal{I}
is admissible).

We say the algebra
A
is

if it is infinite dimensional and satisfies conditions (i)-(iv). We write (locally) gentle to mean gentle or locally gentle.

Any (locally) gentle algebra is Noetherian
[
11

is Artinian if and only if it is gentle (as

is a descending chain of ideals). As the ideal
\mathcal{I}
is homogeneous and generated by paths,

is a monomial algebra graded by path length and has a basis of paths. Note that the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a locally gentle algebra is 1.

2.3.
Maximal paths

Maximal paths will play an important role throughout the rest of this paper. In this section, we define the notion of an infinite maximal path and note some important properties.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
p
be a path in
\mathcal{B}
. We say that

•

p
is a
right maximal
if for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
,
p\alpha\in\mathcal{I}
.

•

p
is
left maximal
if for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
,
\alpha p\in\mathcal{I}
.

•

p
is a
finite maximal path
if
p
is both left and right maximal.

Example 2.6
.

If
\mathcal{Q}
is 2-regular, then
A
has no right maximal paths: for any path
p\in\mathcal{B}
there are two arrows
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
with source
t(p)
and only one of
p\alpha_{1},p\alpha_{2}
belongs to
\mathcal{I}
. In particular, any (locally) gentle algebra on
\mathcal{Q}
has no finite maximal paths.

The following lemma is a consequence of conditions (ii) and (iii) in Definition
2.1
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For an arrow
\alpha\in A
, let
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
(
\gamma_{l}(\alpha)
) denote the unique path of maximal length in
\mathcal{B}
with no repeated arrows and first (last) arrow
\alpha
. Then, exactly one of the following holds.

(1)

The paths
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
and
\gamma_{l}(\alpha)
are right and left maximal, respectively.

(2)

The paths
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
and
\gamma_{l}(\alpha)
are rotations of the same cycle and
\left(\gamma_{r}(\alpha)\right)^{2},\left(\gamma_{l}(\alpha)\right)^{2}\notin\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
is not right maximal. Then, there exists an arrow
\beta
such that
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)\beta\notin\mathcal{I}
. By definition of
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
, it must be that
\beta
occurs in
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
. That is,
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}
for some arrows
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta=\alpha_{j}
for some
j
. Now, since
\alpha_{n}\beta\notin\mathcal{I}
, it follows that that either

or
\alpha_{j-1}=\alpha_{n}
. The latter contradictions our definition of
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
, so
\beta=\alpha_{1}=\alpha
. Hence
\alpha_{n}\alpha\notin\mathcal{I}
. It follows that
\gamma_{l}(\alpha)=\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{n}\alpha_{1}
and
\left(\gamma_{r}(\alpha)\right)^{2},\left(\gamma_{l}(\alpha)\right)^{2}\notin\mathcal{I}
.
∎

In the first case, the arrow
\alpha
(and any path containing
\alpha
) is contained in a unique finite maximal path, of which
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
and
\gamma_{l}(\alpha)
are terminal and initial subpaths, respectively. Note that, as a consequence, no path in
\mathcal{B}
can contain two or more instances of

.

In the second case, any path containing
\alpha
must be a subpath of
\left(\gamma_{r}(\alpha)\right)^{k}
for some

.

A cycle

For a path

(2)

Definition 2.11

2.4.
The Hilbert series

In this section, we will describe the Hilbert series and show that

\pm t^{k}h_{A}(t)=h_{A}(t^{-1})

holds only under certain conditions on the quiver
\mathcal{Q}
, a result which we will use in Section
5
for our analogue of Stanley’s theorem (Theorem
5.9
).

Proposition 2.13
.

Suppose that
A
has
k_{n}
finite maximal paths of length at least
n
. Then

\displaystyle h_{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|+(\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|-\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|)t-\sum_{n\geq 1}k_{n}t^{n+1}}{1-t}.

Proof.

Any path in
\mathcal{B}_{+}
is a subpath of some maximal path. An infinite maximal path containing
\ell
distinct arrows has exactly
\ell
subpaths of length
n
for
1\leq n
, while a finite maximal path of length
\ell
has
\ell+1-n
subpaths of length
n
for
1\leq n\leq\ell
.

Suppose that
A
has
m
finite maximal paths of lengths
\ell_{1}\leq\dotsc\leq\ell_{m}
.
Since each arrow appears in at most one finite maximal path and finite maximal paths have no repeated arrows, there are
k\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell_{i}
arrows not contained in the
m
finite maximal paths. Thus

\displaystyle h_{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|+k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell_{i}}(\ell_{i}+1-n)t^{n}=\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|-k+\frac{k}{1-t}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell_{i}}(\ell_{i}+1-n)t^{n}.

Observe that
(1-t)\sum_{n=1}^{\ell_{i}}(\ell_{i}+1-n)t^{n}=\ell_{i}t-t\sum_{n=1}^{\ell_{i}}t^{n}
. Then
k_{n}=\max\left\{i\;\middle|\;\ell_{i}\geq n\right\}
for
1\leq n\leq\ell_{m}
is the coefficient of
t^{n+1}
in
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell_{i}}t^{n+1}
. The result follows.
∎

Example 2.14
.

Let
A=\Bbbk\mathcal{Q}/\langle\alpha_{1}c,c\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\rangle
, where
\mathcal{Q}
is the quiver

3
4
5
\alpha_{2}

.

Then, the Hilbert series of

is

h_{A}(t)=5+5t+2t^{2}+\sum_{n\geq 3}t^{n}=\frac{5-3t^{2}-t^{3}}{1-t}.

Lemma 2.15
.

Suppose that
A=\Bbbk\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
is (locally) gentle. Then,

h_{A}(t^{-1})=\pm t^{k}h_{A}(t)
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
if and only if one of the following holds.

(1)

The quiver
\mathcal{Q}
is 2-regular and
h_{A}(t)=\frac{\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|(1+t)}{1-t}
.

(2)

The algebra
A
is
\Bbbk\widetilde{A}_{n}
and
h_{A}(t)=\frac{\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|}{1-t}
.

(3)

The quiver
\mathcal{Q}
is a cycle graph with some orientation and
\mathcal{I}
is generated by all paths of length 2. Then,
h_{A}(t)=\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|(1+t)
.

Proof.

The fact that
h_{A}(t^{-1})=\pm t^{k}h_{A}(t)
is clear in these three cases from the equalities
2\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|=\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|
in (1) and
\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|=\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|
in (2) and (3), and that
A
has no finite maximal paths in the first two cases and
\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|
finite maximal paths of length 1 in the third.

Assume
h_{A}(t^{-1})=\pm t^{k}h_{A}(t)
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and that
A
has at least one finite maximal path. Then, we must have
k_{m}=\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|
where
m
is the length of the longest finite maximal path. Since maximal paths are disjoint,
\mathcal{Q}
must have at least
m\cdot k_{m}=m\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|
arrows.

As
A
has a finite maximal path, this implies
m<2
(if
\left|Q_{1}\right|=2\left|Q_{0}\right|
, then
A
has no finite maximal paths). Thus
m=1
, so

h_{A}(t)=\frac{|\mathcal{Q}_{0}|+(|\mathcal{Q}_{1}|-|\mathcal{Q}_{0}|)t-|\mathcal{Q}_{0}|t^{2}}{1-t}.

The condition
h_{A}(t^{-1})=\pm t^{k}h_{A}(t)
implies that
|\mathcal{Q}_{1}|-|\mathcal{Q}_{0}|=0
. Since
k_{1}=|\mathcal{Q}_{0}|=|\mathcal{Q}_{1}|
, every arrow must be contained in a finite maximal path of length 1. In particular, every path of length two is contained in the ideal
\mathcal{I}
.
If there exists a vertex
u
with
\indeg(u)=2
and
\outdeg(u)\geq 1
, then there must exist arrows
\alpha,\beta
so that
t(\alpha)=s(\beta)=u
and
\alpha\beta\not\in\mathcal{I}
. Therefore, any vertex
u
with
\indeg(u)=2
is a sink, and similarly, any vertex
u^{\prime}
with
\outdeg(u^{\prime})=2
is a source.
It follows that
\indeg(v)+\outdeg(v)\leq 2
for each
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
. Since

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}}\left(\indeg(v)+\outdeg(v)\right)=2\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|

for any quiver and
|\mathcal{Q}_{0}|=|\mathcal{Q}_{1}|
,
we have
\indeg(v)+\outdeg(v)=2
for each vertex
v
. Thus,
\mathcal{Q}
must be a cycle graph with some orientation.

If
A
has no finite maximal paths, then
h_{A}(t)=\frac{\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|+(\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|-\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|)t}{1-t}
, so
h_{A}(t^{-1})=\pm t^{k}h_{A}(t)
implies either
\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|=\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|
or
\left|\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|=2\left|\mathcal{Q}_{0}\right|
. The former implies that
A=\Bbbk\widetilde{A}_{n}
(see Lemma
2.10
).
∎

2.5.
The center

The center of a locally gentle algebra can be described in terms of its maximal paths. In particular, the infinite maximal paths give us generators of the following form.

Definition 2.16
.

If
\gamma
is an infinite maximal path, we define the element
m_{\gamma}
of
A
as

m_{\gamma}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1},\\
\gamma_{\alpha}=\gamma\end{subarray}}\gamma_{r}(\alpha).

Note that
m_{\gamma}^{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1},\\
\gamma_{\alpha}=\gamma\end{subarray}}(\gamma_{r}(\alpha))^{k}
for
k>0
since
\gamma_{r}(\alpha)
does not have any repeated arrows.

Lemma 2.17
.

The center of
A
,
Z(A)
, is generated as a
\Bbbk
-algebra by the following elements of
A
.

(1)

The identity of
A
,
\mathbbm{1}=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}}\epsilon_{v}
.

(2)

Finite maximal paths
\gamma\in\mathcal{B}
so that
\gamma
is a cycle in
\mathcal{Q}
.

(3)

Elements
m_{\gamma}
for each infinite maximal path
\gamma
of
A
.

Moreover, elements of the form (
1
)-(
2
) and positive powers of elements of the form (
3
) give a basis of
Z(A)
.

Proof.

A quick check shows that if
p
is an element satisfying one of (
1
)-(
3
), then
\epsilon_{v}p=p\epsilon_{v}
and
\alpha p=p\alpha
for all
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
. It follows that
p\in Z(A)
.

Let
x=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{B}}\lambda_{p}p
be an element of the center.
For each
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{B},\\
s(p)=v\end{subarray}}\lambda_{p}p=\epsilon_{v}x=x\epsilon_{v}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{B},\\
t(p)=v\end{subarray}}\lambda_{p}p.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is a basis for
A
, this implies that
\lambda_{p}\neq 0
if and only if
s(p)=t(p)
. Thus
x
is a linear combination of cycles.

Let
\alpha
be any arrow. The coefficient of
\alpha
in
x\alpha
is
\lambda_{\epsilon_{s(\alpha)}}
while its coefficient in
\alpha x
is
\lambda_{\epsilon_{t(\alpha)}}
, so
\lambda_{\epsilon_{s(\alpha)}}=\lambda_{\epsilon_{t(\alpha)}}
. That is, the coefficients of stationary paths are equal if there is any arrow connecting the corresponding vertices. As
\mathcal{Q}
is connected, this implies that every stationary path occurring in
x
has the same coefficient. Hence

x=\lambda_{\mathbbm{1}}\mathbbm{1}+x^{\prime}
, where
x^{\prime}\in A_{+}
,
\lambda_{\mathbbm{1}}\in\Bbbk
.

If
w\in\mathcal{B}_{+}
with
\lambda_{w}\neq 0
, and
ww^{\prime},w^{\prime}w\in\mathcal{I}
for all
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{+}
, then
w
must be a finite maximal path with
s(w)=t(w)
. Namely,
w
is an element of the form (
2
).

Suppose that
w\in\mathcal{B}_{+}
with
\lambda_{w}\neq 0

and that there exists
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{+}
such that
ww^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{I}
. The coefficient of
ww^{\prime}
in
xw^{\prime}
is
\lambda_{w}\neq 0
, so there exists
w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{+}
such that
\lambda_{w^{\prime\prime}}=\lambda_{w}
and
w^{\prime}w^{\prime\prime}=ww^{\prime}
. In particular,
F(w^{\prime})=F(w)
, so
L(w)F(w)=L(w)F(w^{\prime})\notin\mathcal{I}
, and hence
w^{2}\notin\mathcal{I}
. Similarly, if there exists
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{+}
such that
w^{\prime}w\notin\mathcal{I}
, then
w^{2}\notin\mathcal{I}
.

If
w^{2}\not\in\mathcal{I}
, then
w
is a cycle, and
w=(\gamma_{r}(\alpha_{1}))^{k}
for some
\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
,
k>0
. Let
(\gamma_{r}(\alpha_{1}))=\alpha_{1}\dotsc\alpha_{n}
, so that
m_{\gamma_{\alpha}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{r}(\alpha_{i})
.
Note that
\alpha_{n}w=(\gamma_{r}(\alpha_{n}))^{k}\alpha_{n}
. As
\alpha_{n}x=x\alpha_{n}
, the path
(\gamma_{r}(\alpha_{n}))^{k}
must also occur with coefficient
\lambda_{w}\neq 0
. A similar argument shows that
(\gamma_{r}(\alpha_{i}))^{k}
occurs with coefficient
\lambda_{w}
for all
i
. Therefore,
\lambda_{w}m_{\gamma_{\alpha}}
is a summand of
x
. The result follows.
∎

.

If

has a unique maximal path,

and

is free

-module of rank

. In particular, if for each vertex

with out-degree 2, we choose one arrow

3.
Prime ideals

Throughout this section, ideals are assumed to be two-sided and not necessarily graded.

Definition 3.1
.

For
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, let
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
be the ideal generated by

\left\{\epsilon_{v^{\prime}}\;\middle|\;v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}\setminus\{v\}\right\}\cup\left\{\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}\;\middle|\;s(\alpha)=t(\alpha)=v\right\}.

Let
\gamma
be a maximal path of
A
. The
ideal corresponding to
\gamma
, denoted
\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}
, is the ideal generated by all arrows with maximal path
\gamma
. That is,

\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}=\langle\left.\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right|\gamma_{\alpha}=\gamma\rangle=\Bbbk\left\{w\;\middle|\;w\leq\gamma,\ell(w)>0\right\}.

Denote the left annihilator of the ideal
\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}
by
\anni_{A}(\mathcal{J}_{\gamma})
, namely
\anni_{A}(\mathcal{J}_{\gamma})=\{x\in A:x\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}=0\}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A=\Bbbk\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a locally gentle algebra with a unique maximal path,
\gamma
, which is infinite. The prime ideals of
A
are

(1)

the zero ideal,
0
,

(2)

the ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
for each
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
,

(3)

the ideal
\langle p(m_{\gamma})\rangle
where
p(t)\in\Bbbk[t]
is an irreducible polynomial with
\deg(p)\geq 1
and
p(0)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{1}\dotsc\alpha_{n}
be a primitive cycle of
\gamma
and let

w_{i,j}\in\mathcal{B}
be the unique path of minimal length such that
F(w_{i,j})=\alpha_{i}
and
L(w_{i,j})=\alpha_{j}
(so
w_{i,i}=\alpha_{i}
and
w_{i,i-1}=\gamma_{r}(\alpha_{i})=\gamma_{\ell}(\alpha_{i-1})
), where
\alpha_{i-kn}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\alpha_{i}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
First, we show that ideals of the above forms are prime.
Recall that an ideal
I\subsetneq A
is prime if and only if
xAy\subseteq I
implies that
x\in I
or
y\in I
for any
x,y\in A

[
13
]
.

(1)

Let
x,y\in A\setminus\{0\}
. We wish to show that
xAy\not\subseteq 0
. We may choose
w_{x},w_{y}\in\mathcal{B}
which are maximal with respect to length such that
w_{x}
and
w_{y}
occur with nonzero coefficient in
x
and
y
, respectively. As
w_{x}
and
w_{y}
are both subpaths of
\gamma
, which is infinite and periodic, there exists
w\in\mathcal{B}
such that
w_{x}ww_{y}\leq\gamma
. If
w_{x}^{\prime}ww_{y}^{\prime}=w_{x}ww_{y}
for some
w_{x}^{\prime},w_{y}^{\prime}
occurring with nonzero coefficient in
x
and
y
, respectively, then
w_{x}^{\prime}
must be an initial subpath of
w_{x}
by our assumption that
w_{x}
has maximal length.
As
w_{y}^{\prime}
must likewise be a terminal subpath of
w_{y}
, we have
w_{x}=w_{x}^{\prime}
and
w_{y}=w_{y}^{\prime}
. Thus the coefficient of
w_{x}ww_{y}
in
xwy
is the product of the coefficients of
w_{x}
and
w_{y}
and hence nonzero, so
0\neq xwy\in xAy
.

(2)

For any
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, we have
A/\mathfrak{m}_{v}\cong\Bbbk
, so
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
is a maximal ideal and hence prime.

(3)

Suppose that
p\in\Bbbk[t]
is irreducible,
\deg(p)\geq 1
and
p(0)=\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
. Let
x,y\in A
be such that
xAy\subseteq\langle p(m_{\gamma})\rangle
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x,y\in A_{+}
(otherwise, we may replace
x
with
x-\lambda^{-1}p(m_{\gamma})x_{0}\in A_{+}
, where
x_{0}
is the degree 0 portion of
x
, and likewise
y
).
Then there exist
p_{ij},q_{ij}\in\Bbbk[t]
so that

x=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}p_{ij}(m_{\gamma})w_{i,j}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274pty=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}q_{ij}(m_{\gamma})w_{i,j}.

Assume for contradiction that neither
x
nor
y
belongs to
\langle p(m_{\gamma})\rangle
. Then there exist
i,j,k,l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
so that
p_{ij},q_{kl}\notin\langle p\rangle\subset\Bbbk[t]
. We will show that
w_{i-1,l+1}\in\langle p\rangle
to derive a contradiction. Note that

\alpha_{i-1}x\alpha_{j+1}=\alpha_{i-1}w_{i,j}p_{ij}(m_{\gamma})\alpha_{j+1}
and likewise for
y
, so

\displaystyle\alpha_{i-1}xw_{j+1,k-1}y\alpha_{l+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i-1}p_{ij}(m_{\gamma})w_{i,j}w_{j+1,k-1}q_{kl}(m_{\gamma})w_{k,l}\alpha_{l+1}

\displaystyle=w_{i-1,l+1}m_{\gamma}^{s}[p_{ij}q_{kl}](m_{\gamma})

for some

. Thus, as
xw_{j+1,k-1}y\in\langle p(m_{\gamma})\rangle
, we have
w_{i-1,l+1}m_{\gamma}^{s}[p_{ij}q_{kl}](m_{\gamma})\in\langle p(m_{\gamma})\rangle
as well.
As
p\in\Bbbk[t]
is irreducible and
p(0)\neq 0
, we know that
t^{s}p_{ij}q_{kl}\notin\langle p\rangle
and hence there exist polynomials
r_{1},r_{2}\in\Bbbk[t]
so that
r_{1}p+r_{2}t^{s}p_{ij}q_{kl}=1
. Thus,

\displaystyle w_{i-1,l+1}

\displaystyle=w_{i-1,l+1}[r_{1}p+r_{2}t^{s}p_{ij}q_{kl}](m_{\gamma})\in\langle p(m_{\gamma})\rangle.

Then
w_{i-1,l+1}=zp(m_{\gamma})
for some
z\in A
. As
p(0)\neq 0
, considering the degree zero terms yields
z\in A_{+}
. As
\{w_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is a basis for
A_{+}
over
\Bbbk[m_{\gamma}]
, this implies that
p(m_{\gamma})z_{(i-1)(j+1)}(m_{\gamma})=1
(where
z_{(i-1)(j+1)}(m_{\gamma})
is the coefficient of
w_{i-1,j+1}
in
z
). This contradicts our assumption that
\deg(p)\geq 1
.

Next, we show that any nonzero proper prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
must be of the form (2) or (3). We consider the cases where
\mathfrak{p}
does and does not contain a path in
\mathcal{B}
separately.

For our first case, suppose that there exists
w\in\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathcal{B}
. We will show that
\mathfrak{p}
is of form (2).
We claim that
\mathfrak{p}
must contain an arrow. If
\ell(w)=0
, then
w=\epsilon_{v}
for some
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, and so
\alpha=\alpha w\in\mathfrak{p}
for some arrow
\alpha
. If
\ell(w)>1
, then
w=F(w)w^{\prime}
for some
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
with
1\leq\ell(w^{\prime})=\ell(w)-1
and

F(w)Aw^{\prime}=\Bbbk\{F(w)m_{\gamma}^{k}w^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 0}=\Bbbk\{wm_{\gamma}^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\langle w\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{p}

and so either
F(w)\in\mathfrak{p}
or
w^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{p}
. As
\ell(w^{\prime})<\ell(w)
, induction on
\ell(w)
shows that
\mathfrak{p}
contains an arrow. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\alpha_{1}\in\mathfrak{p}
. For any
i\neq 1
, we see that

\alpha_{i}A\alpha_{i}\subseteq A\alpha_{i}w_{i+1,i}\subseteq\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{p},

since
\alpha_{i}w_{i+1,i}

which contains
\alpha_{i}

\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{i}w_{i+1,i}
. Thus
\alpha_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}

\mathfrak{p}
contains every arrow and hence

. Since

so that

. For any vertex

\epsilon_{v_{0}}A\epsilon_{v}\subseteq A_{+}\subseteq\mathfrak{p},

and hence
\epsilon_{v}\in\mathfrak{p}
. Thus
\mathfrak{m}_{v_{0}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
and hence
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}_{v_{0}}
, by maximality of
\mathfrak{m}_{v_{0}}
.

For our second case, suppose that
\mathfrak{p}
has empty intersection with
\mathcal{B}
. We will show that
wq(m_{\gamma})\in\mathfrak{p}
implies
q(m_{\gamma})\in\mathfrak{p}\cap\Bbbk[m_{\gamma}]
for any polynomial

and
w\in\mathcal{B}
. Because
m_{\gamma}
is central,
wq(m_{\gamma})\in\mathfrak{p}
implies

wAq(m_{\gamma})=wq(m_{\gamma})A\subseteq\mathfrak{p}.

Hence, since
w\in\mathcal{B}
cannot belong to
\mathfrak{p}
, it follows that
q(m_{\gamma})\in\mathfrak{p}
. We will now show that
\mathfrak{p}\cap\Bbbk[m_{\gamma}]\neq 0

\mathfrak{p}
is nonzero, it contains a nonzero element

. Then, there exists some path
w
with nonzero coefficient in
x

.

A (locally) gentle algebra is

5.2.
Cohen-Macaulay

There are multiple equivalent conditions for a commutative ring to be Cohen-Macaulay which are not necessarily equivalent in the non-commutative case. Thus, there are multiple potential definitions for a non-commutative ring to be Cohen-Macaulay. We consider definitions based on the following conditions.

Remark 5.3

(Cohen-Macaulay for a Commutative Ring)
.

Let
R
be a commutative local Noetherian ring. Then
R
is Cohen-Macaulay if the Krull dimension of
R
is equal to its depth. If
R
is affine, then
R
is Cohen-Macaulay if the localization of
R
at every maximal ideal is Cohen-Macaulay. If
R
is connected graded, then it is well known that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if there is a graded polynomial subalgebra
P
of
R
such that
R
is a finitely generated, free
P
-module.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Noetherian graded algebra. Then
\Lambda
satisfies

•

(CM1)
if

\GKdim(A)=\dpth(\Lambda)
.

•

(CM2)
if there exist commuting, homogeneous, algebraically independent elements
x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{d}\in\Lambda
so that
\Lambda
is a finitely generated, free
\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
-module and
d=\GKdim(\Lambda)
.

In Theorem
5.6
, we will show that (CM1) and (CM2) are equivalent for a (locally) gentle algebra, motivating a definition of Cohen-Macaulay for (locally) gentle algebras.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
A
be a locally gentle algebra.
Then
A
satisfies (CM2) if and only if
A
has no maximal paths of finite length. In this case,
A
is a finitely generated free
\Bbbk[x]
-module, where

x\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\alpha
is
the sum of all arrows in the quiver
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
)
Since
\GKdim(A)=1
,
A
satisfies (CM2) if and only if there is an element

such that
A
is a finitely generated free
\Bbbk[x]

x\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\alpha
.
Observe that

\alpha

\Bbbk

As

Theorem 5.6
.

For a (locally) gentle algebra

(1)

(2)

(3)

Definition 5.7
.

If a (locally) gentle algebra

Cohen-Macaulay

If both

are Cohen-Macaulay and locally gentle, then

must be 2-regular.
If
A

\indeg(v)=\outdeg(v)=1
by Lemma

For commutative Cohen-Macaulay algebras, there is a strong connection between the Hilbert series and being Gorenstein.

Lemma 5.8

(Stanley’s Theorem
[
22
]
)
.

Suppose
R
is a connected graded domain which is Cohen-Macaulay. Then,
R
is Gorenstein if and only if its Hilbert series satisfies

h_{R}(t^{-1})=\pm t^{n}h_{R}(t)
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

While all locally gentle algebras are Gorenstein (Theorem
4.28
), most do not satisfy this condition on their Hilbert series. Instead, we have the following analogue for locally gentle algebras.

Theorem 5.9
.

Suppose that
A=\Bbbk\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
is locally gentle and Cohen-Macaulay. The following are equivalent.

(i)

A
is AS Gorenstein.

(ii)

h_{A}(t^{-1})=\pm t^{k}h_{A}(t)
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii)

\mathcal{Q}
is
\widetilde{A}_{n}
or
\mathcal{Q}
is 2-regular.

In particular, if
\mathcal{Q}
is
\widetilde{A}_{n}
, then
A=\Bbbk\widetilde{A}_{n}
and
h_{A}(t)=\frac{n+1}{1-t}=-t^{-1}h_{A}(t^{-1})
. If
\mathcal{Q}
is 2-regular, then
h_{A}(t)=|\mathcal{Q}_{0}|\frac{1+t}{1-t}=-h_{A}(t^{-1})
. In both cases,
k
is the Gorenstein parameter of
A
(1 and 0, respectively).

Proof.

We saw that (iii) was equivalent to (i) in Lemma
2.15
and to (ii) in Proposition
5.2
.
∎

These conditions are satisfied exactly when

is semiprime (see Corollary

). The condition that

must be infinite dimensional is necessary, as we shall see in the following example.

Example 5.10
.

Let
A
be the path algebra of the Kronecker quiver, which is an orientation of

1
2

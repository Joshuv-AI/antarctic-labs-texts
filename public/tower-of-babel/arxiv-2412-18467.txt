1.
Introduction

The chief purpose of this paper is to give a new viewpoint on the classical problem of finding global minimizers of constrained variational problems that are typically encountered in incompressible nonlinear elasticity theory. The novelty of our technique is that in the cases where it applies, it delivers a unique global energy minimizer in a constrained class by first solving an explicit PDE that is set in an unconstrained (or free) class. Established techniques for treating such variational problems can, in the right circumstances, produce similar PDE as necessary conditions, but without an associated uniqueness principle that makes it possible to distinguish between stationary points and true minimizers.

To be more specific, our approach puts to use the recent results in
[
1
]
treating the mean coercivity of functionals of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle F(\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+f(x)\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x

defined for
\varphi
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and where
f
is given function in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. When
F
is mean coercive on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, i.e. when there is a constant
\gamma>0
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle F(\varphi)\geq\gamma\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x\quad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),

it becomes possible to minimize
F
over the general class

H^{1}_{u_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}):\ u=u_{0}\ \textrm{ on }\ \partial\Omega\}

and so obtain a unique solution
u
to the associated Euler-Lagrange equation

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(2\nabla u+f(x)\,{\rm cof}\,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\psi=0\quad\forall\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

This is precisely the type of equation we would expect to have to solve in order to minimize

\mathbb{D}(u):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x

in the constrained class

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{g}:=\{u\in H^{1}_{u_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}):\ \det\nabla u=g\ a.e.\}

provided the given function
g
is such that
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is nonempty. In this case, the function
f
is a Lagrange multiplier corresponding to the constraint
\det\nabla u=g
a.e., while in the continuum mechanics literature

is interpreted as a (hydrostatic) pressure.

Indeed, in the general case of the constrained variational problem of minimizing the stored energy functional

(1.5)

\displaystyle E(u):=\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla u)\,\,{\rm d}x

in a class typified by
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, the established approach is first to determine by the Direct Method of the Calculus of Variations that a minimizer of
E
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
exists and then to derive a version of (
1.3
), namely

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(DW(\nabla u)+\lambda(x)\,{\rm cof}\,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\psi=0\quad\forall\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

for a suitable
\lambda
and where

is typically
2
or
3
. One of the clearest expositions of this technique can be found in
[
7
]
, where minimizers of an energy like (
1.5
) in a subclass of the isochoric maps

a.e. are shown to obey (
1.6
). The heart of the argument is to assume that the global minimizer

of

in the constrained class is sufficiently regular and invertible that the problem can be formulated in the deformed configuration

. See also
[
12
,
6
]

) are derived in
[
2
, Section 4]
for continuous and injective local minimizers satisfying suitable regularity conditions which include the assumption that

for

and

. The resulting hydrostatic pressure

then belongs to

for further results in this direction.

By contrast with the classical approach described above, which fixes the constraint
\det\nabla u=g
a.e. in advance (e.g. by setting

in the isochoric case), our process is as follows:

1.

Relying on the results of
[
1
]
, choose a pressure
f
in (
1.1
) so that the functional
F
is mean coercive in the sense of (
1.2
), and take
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
;

2.

Minimize

in the ‘free’ class

and let

be a minimizer. Note that

solves the weak Euler-Lagrange equation
a(u,\psi)=0
for all test functions

, where the Euler-Lagrange operator is defined in (
2.5
) below;

3.

Define

and let the constrained class of admissible maps

be given by (
3.17
);

4.

Employ the identity

(1.7)

\displaystyle F(v)=F(u)+a(u,v-u)+F(v-u)\quad\quad u,v\in H_{u_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which, because

solves the Euler-Lagrange equations and by imposing the conditions that

a.e., simplifies to

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(v)=\mathbb{D}(u)+F(v-u)\quad\quad v\in\mathcal{A}_{g},

and note that by mean coercivity,

for any

such that

. It follows that

.

See Proposition
2.1

) and Lemma

).
In practice, the choice of the pressure

. We study in Sections

\displaystyle\{v\in H^{1}_{\textrm{id}}(B(0,1);\mathbb{R}^{2}):\ \det\nabla v=g\ \textrm{a.e.}\}

. We are also able to extend our analysis to the functional

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(u):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+T\cdot{\rm cof}\,\nabla u\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}):u\arrowvert_{\partial\Omega}=u_{0}\},

One of the features of the functional

\displaystyle D^{2}W(x,A)[a\otimes b,a\otimes b]\geq\nu|a|^{2}|b|^{2}\quad\quad A,a\otimes b\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ x\in\Omega.

2.
Minimizing the functional
F
under mean coercivity conditions

The subsection title refers to the variational problem of minimizing the energy
F
defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle F(u):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+f(x)\det\nabla u\,{\rm d}x

in the class of admissible maps

(2.2)

\displaystyle H_{u_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}):u\arrowvert_{\partial\Omega}=u_{0}\},

where
u_{0}
is the trace of a fixed function in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Here,
f
is a fixed function in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, which we may sometimes refer to as a ‘pressure’, chosen so that
F
is mean coercive, by which we mean that there is
\gamma>0
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle F(\varphi)\geq\gamma\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x\quad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Conditions on
f
ensuring that (
2.3
) holds can be found in
[
1
]
, to which point we will return later. By a straightforward density argument, we remark that the space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
appearing in (
2.3
) can be replaced with the set of smooth, compactly supported test functions on
\Omega
.

The connection between mean coercivity and the existence of minimizers of
F
is recorded in the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u,v\in H_{u_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and let
F
be given by (
2.1
). Then

(2.4)

\displaystyle F(v)=F(u)+a(u,v-u)+F(v-u)

where
a(u,\varphi)
represents the bilinear operator

(2.5)

\displaystyle a(u,\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}2\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi+f(x)\,{\rm cof}\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x.

If
F
is mean coercive then it has a unique minimizer
u\in H_{u_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
obeying the Euler-Lagrange equation

(2.6)

\displaystyle a(u,\varphi)=0\quad\quad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Proof.

Writing
v=u+\varphi
, expanding the determinant

\det(\nabla u+\nabla\varphi)=\det\nabla u+{\rm cof}\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi+\det\nabla\varphi

and substituting in
F(u+\varphi)
yields the decomposition (
2.4
). When
F
is mean coercive, the direct method of the Calculus of Variations yields a minimizer
u
, say, in
H_{u_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and by taking suitable variations, it must be that
u
obeys (
2.5
). The uniqueness follows by applying (
2.4
) and (
2.3
) to deduce that for any other candidate minimizer
v
, say,

\displaystyle F(v)

\displaystyle\geq F(u)+\gamma\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx

and, by exchanging
u
and
v
,

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle\geq F(v)+\gamma\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,dx.

These are consistent only if
\varphi=0
a.e., which gives
v=u
a.e..
∎

We remark that the decomposition (
2.4
)
shows that if there is just one test function
\varphi
such that
F(\varphi)<0

then
F(u+k\varphi)\to-\infty
as
k\to\infty
, and there is no infimum, let alone a minimizer. Hence if there is a finite infimum, it is necessary that

(2.7)

\displaystyle F(\varphi)\geq 0\quad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Mean coercivity is therefore a natural strengthening of this necessary condition. Moreover, since it follows easily from (
2.7
) that

\min\{F(\varphi):\ \varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\}=0,

we deduce that if
F
is in addition mean coercive then the unique minimizer of
F
on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
is
u=0
. We refer to
[
3
]
for other applications of convex
integral functionals defined by possibly nonconvex integrands.

We now study the Euler-Lagrange equation (
2.6
) for general
f
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
under the assumption that
f
can be chosen so that
F
is mean coercive, i.e. that (
2.3
) holds. This is a weaker assumption than ellipticity, as can be seen by considering the particular example of
f=M\chi_{\omega}
where
\omega\subset\Omega
:
the system (
2.6
) is elliptic only when
|M|<2
, whereas, by
[
1
, Proposition 3.4]
it is mean coercive only when
|M|<4
. Fortunately, classical regularity theory is readily adapted in order to exploit the mean coercivity condition (
2.3
). Indeed, the conclusion of Proposition
2.2
below echoes that of Giaquinta and Giusti
[
9
]
, in which an improvement in the regularity of a minimizer of certain nondifferentiable functionals is shown to be possible, and also that of the well-known result of Morrey
[
13
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, but, in our case, without any pointwise growth assumptions on the integrand. Specifically, we show that weak solutions to the Euler-Lagrange equation belong to the space
W^{1,p}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
p>2
, and hence are, by Sobolev embedding, automatically locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega
. In the following, we use the notation
(u)_{{}_{S}}:=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{S}u(y)\,{\rm d}y
whenever
S\subset\Omega
is measurable and non-null.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
u\in H_{u_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a weak solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}2\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi+f\,{\rm cof}\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x=0\quad\quad\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})

and assume that
F
is mean coercive in the sense of (
2.3
). Then there is
p>2
such that
u
belongs to
W^{1,p}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}
be any interior point of
\Omega
and let
R_{0}>0
be such that
B(x_{0},2R)\subset\Omega
for all
R\in(0,R_{0})
. Fix
R\in(0,R_{0})
and let
\eta
be a smooth cut-off function with the properties that
\eta(x)=1
for

,

and

for some constant

. Let
\lambda
be a constant vector in

. Choosing

in (

) gives

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\eta^{2}f\,\det\nabla u\,\,{\rm d}x+

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad+\int_{\Omega}2\eta(u-\lambda)\otimes\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla u+f\eta(u-\lambda)\otimes\nabla\eta\cdot{\rm cof}\,\nabla u\,\,{\rm d}x.

Now,

(2.10)

\displaystyle F(\eta(u-\lambda))

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+2\eta(u-\lambda)\otimes\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla u+|u-\lambda|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x+

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}f\,\det\nabla u+f\eta(u-\lambda)\otimes\nabla\eta\cdot{\rm cof}\,\nabla u\,\,{\rm d}x,

which by applying (

) leads to

(2.11)

\displaystyle F(\eta(u-\lambda))=\int_{\Omega}|u-\lambda|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Since
\eta(u-\lambda)
belongs to
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we can apply (
2.3
) to the left-hand side of the last equation, which gives, for some
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+2\eta(u-\lambda)\otimes\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla u+|u-\lambda|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}|u-\lambda|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Hence there are constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
and
\theta
depending only on
\gamma
and

such that

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{B(x_{0},R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{B(x_{0},2R)\setminus B(x_{0},R)}|u-\lambda|^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+c_{2}\int_{B(x_{0},2R)\setminus B(x_{0},R)}|u-\lambda||\nabla\eta||\nabla u|\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{3}}{R^{2}}\int_{B(x_{0},2R)\setminus B(x_{0},R)}|u-\lambda|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x+\theta\int_{B(x_{0},2R)\setminus B(x_{0},R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x.

where, without loss of generality,
4\theta<\gamma
.
Replacing the domain of integration on the right-hand side by
B(x_{0},2R)
, dividing through by
\pi R^{2}
, taking
\lambda=(u)_{{}_{B(x_{0},2R)}}
and applying the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality in the form

\displaystyle\int_{B(x_{0},2R)}|u-(u)_{{}_{B(x_{0},2R)}}|^{q}\,\,{\rm d}x\leq C\left(\int_{B(x_{0},2R)}|\nabla u|^{\frac{nq}{n+q}}\right)^{\frac{n+q}{n}}

with

leads eventually to

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{0},R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{0},2R)}|\nabla u|\right)^{2}+\frac{4\theta}{\gamma}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(x_{0},2R)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x.

Since

, (

) is a reverse Hölder inequality and, by applying

, we deduce that there is

∎

A second interesting feature of the Euler-Lagrange equations (

Remark 2.3
.

The problem of minimizing
F
in
H_{u_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
admits a physical interpretation in terms of the stored energy of a nonlinearly elastic material that is, in parts, subject to an applied dead-load pressure. The associated PDE (
2.6
) gives information both in the ‘bulk’ (via harmonicity on
\Omega\setminus\partial\omega
) and on the ‘surface’
\partial\omega
(via jump conditions.) Furthering the connection with nonlinear elasticity, we may rewrite
F
in terms of the Cauchy-Green stress tensor
C:=\nabla u^{T}\nabla u
and note that in our case we have existence and uniqueness of equilibria under conditions that are not covered by the general results of
[
15
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let the functional
F
be given by (
2.1
) with
f=M\chi_{\omega}
, and assume that

2\gamma\delta+4\beta-4\delta\end{array}\right).

Finally, (

\displaystyle v_{n}^{(4)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2\beta\gamma+4\delta-4\beta\\
2\beta\gamma+4\beta-4\delta\end{array}\right).

In addition to the standing assumptions that

,
\beta\neq 0
,
\gamma\neq 0
,

, and

, the only condition needed to ensure that the vectors

. In summary, using

defined in (

), the ‘odd part’ of the solution

\displaystyle u^{(1)}_{\textrm{odd}}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\ \textrm{odd}}a_{n}^{(1)}z^{n}+\overline{b_{n}^{(1)}}\bar{z}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\ \textrm{odd}}2\beta\gamma(z^{n}+\bar{z}^{n})-p(z^{n}-\bar{z}^{n}).

Similarly, each

3.2

∎

Remark 3.9
.

We remark that the difference

is singled out as a consequence of the choice we made just after (

on neighbouring subdomains of

.

Proposition 3.10

Using the definitions of

\displaystyle\alpha

The recipe of Proposition

We remark that by setting

and so

where

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag\left(-4\beta^{2},-4\beta(2+\sqrt{4+\beta^{2}}\right)},

As a particular example, note that when

\displaystyle u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=D_{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{1}\\

for

Having established conditions under which solutions to (
3.29
) exist, we now tune
\sigma
and
\tau
in order that
F
is mean coercive. The first step is to rewrite
F(\varphi)
slightly when
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
and note that, since
\det\nabla\varphi
is a null Lagrangian, it holds that

\int_{Q}\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x=0.

In particular, we can subtract
\sigma\int_{Q}\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x
from
F(\varphi)
without changing its value, which leads to the equivalent form

(3.88)

\displaystyle F(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-(\tau+2\sigma)\det\nabla\varphi\,\chi_{Q_{1}}+(\tau-2\sigma)\det\nabla\varphi\,\chi_{Q_{3}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{Q}\lambda|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-2\sigma\det\nabla\varphi\,(\chi_{Q_{1}}+\chi_{Q_{3}})\,{\rm d}x}_{=:\small{F_{\lambda}\left(\varphi,\ \scalebox{0.7}{{\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$0$&$-2\sigma$\\
\hline\cr$-2\sigma$&$0$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}}}\,\right)}}+\underbrace{\int_{Q}(1-\lambda)|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\tau\det\nabla\varphi\,(\chi_{Q_{3}}-\chi_{Q_{1}})\,{\rm d}x}_{\small{F_{1-\lambda}\left(\varphi,\ \scalebox{0.7}{{\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$0$&$-\tau$\\
\hline\cr$\tau$&$0$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}}}\,\right)}}.

Here,
\lambda\in(0,1)
will be chosen shortly and in accordance with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.11
.

Both of the functionals
F_{\lambda}\left(\varphi,\ \scalebox{0.7}{{\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$0$&$-2\sigma$\\
\hline\cr$-2\sigma$&$0$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}}}\,\right)
and
F_{1-\lambda}\left(\varphi,\ \scalebox{0.7}{{\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$0$&$-\tau$\\
\hline\cr$\tau$&$0$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}}}\,\right)
defined in (
3.88
) are nonnegative on
C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
provided
\lambda,\sigma
and
\tau
obey

(3.93)

\displaystyle\frac{|\sigma|}{2}\leq\lambda\leq 1-\frac{|\tau|}{\sqrt{8}}.

Moreover, if (
3.93
) is strengthened to

(3.94)

\displaystyle\frac{|\sigma|}{2}<\lambda<1-\frac{|\tau|}{\sqrt{8}}.

then
F
is mean coercive.

Proof.

Firstly, write

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}\left(\varphi,\ \scalebox{0.7}{{\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$0$&$-2\sigma$\\
\hline\cr$-2\sigma$&$0$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}}}\,\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda}\det\nabla\varphi\,(\chi_{Q_{1}}+\chi_{Q_{3}})\,{\rm d}x

and let
c=\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda}
. Let
\omega_{n}\subset Q
be any sequence of subsets with the properties that (i)
\omega_{n}
is homeomorphic to an open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, (ii)
Q_{1}\cup Q_{3}\subset\omega_{n}
for all
n
, and (iii)
\chi_{\omega_{n}}\to\chi_{Q_{1}\cup Q_{3}}
as
n\to\infty
. For example, the sets

\displaystyle\omega_{n}:=\left\{x\in Q:\ \textrm{dist}\,(x,Q_{1}\cup Q_{3})<\frac{1}{n}\right\}

fulfill (i)-(iii). Then, by
[
1
, Proposition 3.4]
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the functional

\displaystyle F_{n}(\varphi):=\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-c\det\nabla\varphi\,\chi_{\omega_{n}}\,{\rm d}x

is nonnegative on
C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
|c|\leq 4
. By letting
n\to\infty
and noting that, by dominated convergence,

\displaystyle F_{n}(\varphi)\to\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-c\det\nabla\varphi\,(\chi_{Q_{1}}+\chi_{Q_{3}})\,{\rm d}x,

it follows that so too is
F_{\lambda}(\varphi)
nonnegative on

C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
|c|\leq 4
. This is equivalent to
\frac{|\sigma|}{2}\leq\lambda
.

Next, rewrite

\displaystyle F_{1-\lambda}\left(\varphi,\ \scalebox{0.7}{{\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$0$&$-\tau$\\
\hline\cr$\tau$&$0$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}}}\,\right)=(1-\lambda)\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\frac{\tau}{1-\lambda}\det\nabla\varphi\,(\chi_{Q_{3}}-\chi_{Q_{1}})\,{\rm d}x

and let
d=\frac{\tau}{1-\lambda}
. According to

[
1
, Proposition 4.6]
, the functional

\displaystyle\varphi\mapsto\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+d\det\nabla\varphi\,(\chi_{Q_{3}}-\chi_{Q_{1}})\,{\rm d}x

is nonnegative on
C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
|d|\leq\sqrt{8}
, which is equivalent to
\lambda\leq 1-\frac{|\tau|}{\sqrt{8}}
. Putting both inequalities involving
\lambda
together yields (
3.93
).

Now suppose that (
3.94
) holds so that, in particular,
|\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda}|<4
. Consider

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}\left(\varphi,\ \scalebox{0.7}{{\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$0$&$-2\sigma$\\
\hline\cr$-2\sigma$&$0$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}}}\,\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda}\det\nabla\varphi\,(\chi_{Q_{1}}+\chi_{Q_{3}})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\lambda\epsilon\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x+\lambda(1-\epsilon)\left(\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda(1-\epsilon)}\det\nabla\varphi\,\,{\rm d}x\right)

and notice that for sufficiently small
\epsilon
we may assume that
|\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda}(1-\epsilon)|\leq 4
and hence that the functional on the right is nonnegative. Using this and (
3.88
) we therefore have that

F(\varphi)\geq\gamma\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})

where
\gamma=\lambda\epsilon
.
∎

It follows from Lemma
3.11
and (
3.88
) that as long as

(3.95)

\displaystyle\frac{|\sigma|}{2}\leq 1-\frac{|\tau|}{\sqrt{8}},

then
\lambda
can be chosen to lie between these values, and hence
F(\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. But the choice of
\sigma
and
\tau
is not free: one parameter is subordinated to the other through (
3.79
), (
3.80
) and (
3.83
), which when combined lead to

(3.96)

\displaystyle\tau^{4}-8\tau^{2}\sigma^{2}+32\tau\sigma+16\sigma^{4}=0.

Thus, in order to conclude, we seek solutions to (
3.96
) such that (
3.95
) holds.
A brief numerical investigation, which we summarise in Proposition
3.12
below, reveals (at least) one ‘branch’ of solutions

which obey both (

) and

(3.97)

\displaystyle y(\sigma,\tau):=\frac{|\sigma|}{2}+\frac{|\tau|}{\sqrt{8}}\leq 1.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
y(\sigma,\tau)
be given by (
3.97
). Then there are infinitely many solutions
(\sigma,\tau)
to the equation (
3.96
) which also obey
y(\sigma,\tau)<1.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle h(\sigma,\tau)=\tau^{4}-8\tau^{2}\sigma^{2}+32\tau\sigma+16\sigma^{4}

and notice that
h(-\frac{\tau}{2},\tau)=-16\tau^{2}
and
h(0,\tau)=\tau^{4}
. Hence, for each
\tau\neq 0
there is at least one
\sigma=\sigma(\tau)
in the interval
(-\frac{\tau}{2},0)
such that
h(\sigma(\tau),\tau)=0
. Letting
\tau_{0}=2
and solving
h(\sigma,\tau_{0})=0
(using, for example, Mathematica) produces two solutions,
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
, say, which to 4 d.p. are

\sigma_{0}\simeq-0.2253\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \sigma_{1}\simeq-1.9470.

More precisely,
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
are the only real roots of the polynomial

p_{4}(\sigma)=1+4\sigma-2\sigma^{2}+\sigma^{4}.

Now, since
h_{\sigma}(\sigma,\tau_{0})=64(\sigma^{3}-\sigma+1)
, it is easily checked that

h_{\sigma}(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})\neq 0,

and hence, by the Implicit Function Theorem, for suitably small
\epsilon>0
there is a smooth branch

(3.98)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\{(\sigma(\tau),\tau):\tau\in(2-\epsilon,2+\epsilon)\}

of solutions to (
3.96
) emanating from the point
(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})
. Now we compute

y(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})\simeq 0.8197

to 4 d.p., and so it follows by continuity that

y(\sigma(\tau),\tau)<1

for all
\tau
sufficiently close to
\tau_{0}=2
. By taking
\epsilon
in the description of
\mathcal{B}
smaller still if necessary, we can assume that
y(\sigma(\tau),\tau)<1
if
(\sigma(\tau),\tau)\in\mathcal{B}
.
∎

Finally, using Lemma

, the following.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
f

\displaystyle f=\sigma\chi_{Q_{2}}-(\tau+\sigma)\chi_{Q_{1}}+(\tau-\sigma)\chi_{Q_{3}}+\sigma\chi_{Q_{4}},

4.
Numerical experiments in the planar case

The MATLAB code of
[
1
]
based on
[
14
]
was extended to treat the non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition
u_{0}
. A minimizer
u\in H^{1}_{u_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
of (
1.1
) is approximated by the finite element method (FEM) with the lowest order (known as P1) basis functions defined on a regular triangulation of the domain
\Omega
. It is calculated using the trust-region method from the MATLAB Optimization Toolbox.
The weak form (
4.1
) is discretized as the system of linear equations

(4.1)

\displaystyle(2K_{1}+K_{2})\,\vec{u}=0.

Here, a vector
\vec{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
represents the minimizer
u=(u_{1},u_{2})
and
n
denotes the number of triangulation nodes.
The stiffness matrices
K_{1}
,
K_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n\times 2n}
are constructed efficiently using the modification of
[
16
]
and correspond to the assembly of bilinear forms

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\psi:\nabla u\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\psi_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial\psi_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}\\
\frac{\partial\psi_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial\psi_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}\end{pmatrix}:\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}\\
\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\,{\rm d}x,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f\,{\rm cof}\,\nabla\psi:\nabla u\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\begin{pmatrix}[r]\frac{\partial\psi_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}&-\frac{\partial\psi_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\\
-\frac{\partial\psi_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}&\frac{\partial\psi_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}\end{pmatrix}:\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}\\
\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\,{\rm d}x.

The matrix
K_{1}
is symmetric and is constructed of two identical stiffness matrices corresponding to the discretization of the Laplace operator for the scalar variable.
The matrix
K_{2}
is non-symmetric and combines the products of the mixed derivatives of the basis functions further weighted by the function
f
. The function
f
is assumed to be a piecewise constant in smaller subdomains. If the triangulation is aligned with subdomain shapes, then the numerical quadrature of both terms in (
1.1

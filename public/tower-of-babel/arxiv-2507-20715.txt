Introduction

Boolean bent functions were first introduced by Rothaus in 1976 as an interesting combinatorial object with the important property of
having the maximum Hamming distance to the set of all affine functions. Later the research in this area was stimulated by the significant
relation to the following topics in computer science: coding theory, sequences and cryptography (design of stream ciphers and
S
-boxes for
block ciphers). Kumar, Scholtz and Welch in
[
1
]
generalized the notion of Boolean bent functions to the case of functions over
an arbitrary finite field. Complete classification of bent functions looks hopeless even in the binary case. In the case of generalized
bent functions, things are naturally much more complicated. However, many explicit methods are known for constructing bent functions either
from scratch or based on other, simpler bent functions.

The finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{k}}
is a subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
if and only if
k
divides
n
. The trace mapping from
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
to the subfield
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{k}}
is
defined by
{\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}^{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n/k-1}x^{p^{ik}}
. In the case when
k=1
, we use the notation
{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(x)
instead of
{\operatorname{Tr}}_{1}^{n}(x)
.

Given a function
f(x)
mapping
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, the direct and inverse
Walsh transform
operations on
f
are defined at a point by
the following respective identities

S_{f}(b)=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\omega^{f(x)-{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bx)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\omega^{f(x)}=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}S_{f}(b)\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bx)}

where
{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}():{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
denotes the absolute trace function,
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{p}}
is the complex primitive

p^{\rm{th}}
root of unity and elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
are considered as integers modulo
p
.

According to
[
1
]
,
f(x)
is called a
p
-ary bent function
if all its Walsh coefficients satisfy
|S_{f}(b)|^{2}=p^{n}
. A bent
function
f(x)
is called
regular
(see
[
1
, Definition 3]
and
[
2
, p. 576]
) if for every
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
the
normalized Walsh coefficient
p^{-n/2}S_{f}(b)
is equal to a complex
p^{\rm{th}}
root of unity, i.e.,
p^{-n/2}S_{f}(b)=\omega^{f^{*}(b)}
for
some function
f^{*}
mapping
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
into
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. A bent function
f(x)
is called
weakly regular
if there exists a complex
u
having
unit magnitude such that
up^{-n/2}S_{f}(b)=\omega^{f^{*}(b)}
for all
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
. We call
u^{-1}p^{n/2}
the
magnitude
of
S_{f}(b)
.
Recently, weakly regular bent functions were shown to be useful for constructing certain combinatorial objects such as partial difference
sets, strongly regular graphs and association schemes (see
[
3
,
4
]
). This justifies why the classes of (weakly) regular
bent functions are of independent interest. For a comprehensive reference on monomial and quadratic
p
-ary bent functions we refer reader
to
[
5
]
and to the general survey on
p
-ary bent functions
[
6
]
.

Definition 1

Functions
f,g:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
are
extended-affine equivalent
(in brief, EA-equivalent) if there exists an affine permutation
L

of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
, an affine function
l:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{*}
such that
g(x)=a(f\circ L)(x)+l(x)
. A class of functions is

complete
if it is a union of EA-equivalence classes. The
completed class
is the smallest possible complete class that
contains the original one.

It is interesting to find bent functions allowing few terms in their univariate representation. It looks like that the currently known list
of bent monomials that give infinite classes is complete (see
[
6
]
). The only known binomial class is for
n=4k
with

f(x)={\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}\big(x^{p^{3k}+p^{2k}-p^{k}+1}+x^{2}\big)
proven in
[
7
]
to be a weakly regular bent function for any odd
p
. In this
paper, we find other ternary binomial and trinomial classes having the form
f(x)={\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}\left(a_{1}x^{d_{1}}+a_{2}x^{d_{2}}+a_{3}x^{d_{3}}\right)

with
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{n}}
and particular integer exponents
d_{i}
for
i=\{1,2,3\}
. These functions belong to the completed Maiorana-McFarland
class and we develop some new tools to prove bentness in this case.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we prove new criteria for a function to be bent in the Maiorana-McFarland
class. This result is interesting on its own since it provides a method for proving bentness that may work in the case when all the other
approaches seem to fail. We use these criteria in the following sections. In Section
4
, we present ternary binomial bent
function for
n=4k
given by
f(x)={\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}\big(a_{1}x^{2(3^{k}+1)}+a_{2}x^{(3^{k}+1)^{2}}\big)
, where
a_{1}
is a nonsquare in
{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{n}}
and
a_{2}

is defined explicitly by
a_{1}
. In Section
5
, we found bent trinomials
f(x)={\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}\big(a_{1}x^{2\cdot 3^{k}+4}+a_{2}x^{3^{k}+5}+a_{3}x^{2}\big)
with
n=2k
,
k
not divisible by four, and coefficients explicitly defined. Binomial bent functions considered
in Section
3
are also Maiorana-McFarland but the proof is provided using a different technique that works in general and
this is just a particular case that we ended up in the Maiorana-McFarland class. For small dimensions, we checked computationally that
these functions are EA equivalent to the functions in a more general class from Section
4
but proving this in general
remains open. The final Section
6
, we discuss the subclass of the earlier found binomial bent functions that can be
represented by exceptional polynomials.

2
Proving Bentness in Maiorana-McFarland Case

In this section, we discuss how to prove bentness of a function that will belong to the completed Maiorana-McFarland class. Normally, one
would first check that a function is bent and then apply some criteria of being in the completed class of
\mathcal{M}
. Our result in
Theorem
2.1
provides a new direct method of proving bentness specially customized to this case. Commonly, Maiorana-McFarland
bent functions are represented in bivariate form.

Construction 1

(Maiorana-McFarland
[
8
,
9
,
1
]
)
Take any permutation
\pi
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{m}
and any function
g:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{m}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{m}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{m}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
f(x,y):=x\cdot\pi(y)+g(y)
is
a bent function. Moreover, the bijectiveness of
\pi
is necessary and sufficient for
f
being bent. Such bent functions are regular and
the dual function is equal to
f^{*}(x,y)=-y\cdot\pi^{-1}(x)+g(\pi^{-1}(x))
that is also a Maiorana-McFarland bent function. Here dot
function is the inner product of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{m}
. If
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{m}
is represented by a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
then we use the standard inner product that is
the absolute trace function
{\operatorname{Tr}}_{m}()
.

Also, for any
G:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
, function
F:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
with
F(x,y)=x\pi(y)+G(y)
is a vectorial bent function if and
only if
\pi
is a permutation of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
. All componet functions of
F
are regular.

The completed class of
\mathcal{M}
contains all the quadratic bent functions.

For a bent function, the criterion to be a member of the completed class of
\mathcal{M}
is known in its binary version when
p=2
(see

[
8
]
). In the general case, the proof is similar and we skip it here.

Proposition 1

Take even
n
and a bent function
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then
f
belongs to the completed class of
\mathcal{M}
if and only if there exists
an
n/2
-dimensional vector subspace
V
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that the second-order derivatives

D_{c,d}f(x)=f(x+c+d)-f(x+c)-f(x+d)+f(x)

vanish for any
c,d\in V
.

The following theorem and proposition provide a new method of proving bentness. As illustrated in Sections
4
and

5
, this method can success in the case when other methods look hopeless. It requires calculation of first- and
second-order derivatives of a function. This may be rather technical but still doable while calculating exponential sums directly seems
impossible.

Theorem 2.1

Take even
n
and function
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Assume there exists an
n/2
-dimensional vector subspace
V
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that its
orthogonal subspace
V^{\bot}
is supplementary to
V
, the second-order derivatives
D_{c,d}f(x)
vanish for any
c,d\in V
and the
first-order derivatives
D_{c}f(x)
are balanced on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
for any
c\in V\setminus\{0\}
. Then
f
is a regular bent function that belongs
to the completed class of
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof

Write the elements
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
uniquely as
x=v+w
with
v\in V
and
w\in V^{\bot}
. Since
D_{c,d}f(x)
vanish for any
c,d\in V
, then

D_{c}f(x)
is constant when
w
is fixed and thus,
f(x)
is affine when
w
is fixed. This means that there exist functions

\pi:V^{\bot}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
and
g:V^{\bot}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
such that
f(x)=v\cdot\pi(w)+g(w)
. Since
V
and
V^{\bot}
are orthogonal, we may
assume that
\pi:V^{\bot}\mapsto V
. In the univariate form, we can also write that
f(x)=\phi(x)\cdot\pi(x)+g(x)
, where functions
\pi()

and
g()
(with the domain extended to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
) are invariant by translation of their input by an element of
V
and
\phi()
is linear with
kernel
V^{\bot}
.

Now
D_{c}f(x)=c\cdot\pi(w)
is balanced on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
for any nonzero
c\in V
and so
c\cdot\pi(w)
is balanced on
V^{\bot}
. Define a set of
characters of
V
(a finite abelian group) as
\chi_{c}(v)=\omega^{c\cdot v}
for every
c\in V
. Note that
\chi_{c}
is a trivial character
if and only if
c\in V^{\bot}
and since
V^{\bot}
is supplementary to
V
, then
c=0
. Thus, if
c,d\in V
with
c\neq d
then

\chi_{c}(v)/\chi_{d}(v)=\chi_{c-d}(v)
is trivial if and only if
c=d
. Therefore, all characters of
V
are defined this way. Take any
v\in V
and denote
N=\#\{w\in V^{\bot}\ |\ \pi(w)=v\}
. Then, using
[
10
, Eq. (5.4)]
,

N=\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{w\in V^{\bot}}\sum_{\chi\in V^{\wedge}}\chi(\pi(w))\overline{\chi(v)}=1+\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{c\in V\setminus\{0\}}\overline{\chi_{c}(v)}\sum_{w\in V^{\bot}}\omega^{c\cdot\pi(w)}=1\kern 5.0pt,

where
V^{\wedge}
is
the group of characters of
V
and since
|V|=|V^{\bot}|
. We conclude that
\pi
is a bijective mapping of
V^{\bot}
onto
V
.

Now the Walsh transform of
f
in point
(b_{1},b_{2})\in V\times V^{\bot}
is equal to

\displaystyle S_{f}(b_{1},b_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V,\,w\in V^{\bot}}\omega^{v\cdot\pi(w)+g(w)-b_{1}\cdot v-b_{2}\cdot w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V^{\bot}}\omega^{g(w)-b_{2}\cdot w}\sum_{v\in V}\omega^{v\cdot(\pi(w)-b_{1})}

\displaystyle=p^{n/2}\omega^{g(\pi^{-1}(b_{1}))-b_{2}\cdot\pi^{-1}(b_{1})}

and
f
is a regular bent function that, by Proposition
1
, belongs to the completed class of
\mathcal{M}
.∎

On the other hand, if we have a bent function
f
in the completed class of
\mathcal{M}
then, by Proposition
1
and the
arguments above,
f(v+w)=v\cdot\pi(w)+g(w)
, where
v\in V
, an
n/2
-dimensional vector subspace in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
, and
w
is from a subspace
supplementary to
V
(denote it
W
), and
\pi:W\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
is such that
\#\{w\in W\ |\ \pi(w)-v\in V^{\bot}\}=1
for any
v\in V
. It is
not possible to conclude if the first-order derivatives
D_{c}f(x)=c\cdot\pi(w)
here are balanced that is conditioned in
Theorem
2.1
.

The following proposition can handle the case when vector space
V
is self-orthogonal so Theorem
2.1
is not applicable.

Proposition 2

Take
n=2m
and function
f:{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Assume there exists an
m
-dimensional self-orthogonal vector subspace
V
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that
for any
c,d\in V
, the second-order derivatives
D_{c,d}f(x)
vanish. Take an
m
-dimensional subspace
W
supplementary to
V
. Then,

f(v+w)=v\cdot\pi(w)+g(w)
for some
\pi:W\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
,
g:W\mapsto{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and with
v\in V
. If
W
can be chosen such that
\pi
is a
permutation of
W
then
f
is a regular bent function that belongs to the completed class of
\mathcal{M}
. Note that the first-order
derivatives
D_{c}f(v+w)=c\cdot\pi(w)
.

Proof

The Walsh transform of
f
in point
(b_{1},b_{2})\in V\times W
is equal to

\displaystyle S_{f}(b_{1}+b_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V,\,w\in W}\omega^{v\cdot\pi(w)+g(w)-(b_{1}+b_{2})\cdot(v+w)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V,\,w\in W}\omega^{v\cdot\pi(w)+g(w)-(b_{1}+b_{2})\cdot w-b_{2}\cdot v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in W}\omega^{g(w)-(b_{1}+b_{2})\cdot w}\sum_{v\in V}\omega^{v\cdot(\pi(w)-b_{2})}

\displaystyle=p^{n/2}\omega^{g(\pi^{-1}(b_{2}))-(b_{1}+b_{2})\cdot\pi^{-1}(b_{2})}

and
f
is a regular bent function that, by Proposition
1
, belongs to the completed class of
\mathcal{M}
.∎

3
Binomial Bent Functions in the Completed Class of
\mathcal{M}
(case
n=4k
with
k
odd)

In this section, we present a ternary binomial bent function in the completed Maiorana-McFarland class and calculate its Walsh transform
coefficients. In the proof, we use the technique that was originally suggested by Dobbertin in the binary case
[
11
]
(see also

[
12
]
).

Let
n=4k
with
k
odd. Since polynomial
x^{4}+x-1
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
it is also irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}
(see

[
10
, Lemma ?]
). Taking
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{4}}
such that
a^{4}+a-1=0
we obtain that
{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{n}}={\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}[a]
. Note that
a
is a primitive
element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{4}}
. In particular, any
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{n}}
can be uniquely represented as

x=x_{3}a^{3}+x_{2}a^{2}+x_{1}a+x_{0}

(1)

with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}
. Using
a^{4}=1-a
, we can also calculate that

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Tr}}^{n}_{k}(a)

\displaystyle=a+a^{3^{k}}+a^{3^{2k}}+a^{3^{3k}}=a+a^{3}+a^{9}+a^{27}=0={\operatorname{Tr}}^{n}_{k}(a^{3})

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Tr}}^{n}_{k}(a^{2})

\displaystyle=a^{2}+a^{6}+a^{18}-a^{14}=0\kern 5.0pt.

Knowing these first three traces and that
{\operatorname{Tr}}^{n}_{k}(1)=1
we can calculate all
{\operatorname{Tr}}^{n}_{k}(a^{i})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
for
i=0,\dots,79
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
n=4k
with
k\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. Further, select
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{4}}
such that
a^{4}+a-1=0
. Then ternary function
f(x)
mapping
{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{n}}

to
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
and given by

f(x)={\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}\left(ax^{2(3^{k}+1)}+a^{-1}x^{(3^{k}+1)^{2}}\right)

is a regular bent function of degree four belonging to the completed Maiorana-McFarland class.

Proof

To begin with, using (
1
) we convert
f(x)
in its univariate representation into the polynomial of four variables over

{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}
. Note that for
k\equiv 3\pmod{4}
we have that
3^{k}\equiv 27\pmod{80}
and
3^{2k}\equiv 9\pmod{80}
. Then

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}\left(x^{2\cdot 3^{k}+1}\big(ax+a^{-1}x^{3^{2k}}\big)\right)

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}\Big((x_{3}a+x_{2}a^{54}+x_{1}a^{27}+x_{0})^{2}(x_{3}a^{3}+x_{2}a^{2}+x_{1}a+x_{0})

\displaystyle\ \times\big(x_{3}a^{4}+x_{2}a^{3}+x_{1}a^{2}+x_{0}a+a^{79}(x_{3}a^{27}+x_{2}a^{18}+x_{1}a^{9}+x_{0})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}\Big(x_{0}^{4}+x_{0}x_{1}^{3}+x_{0}^{3}x_{2}+x_{1}^{3}x_{2}-x_{0}x_{2}^{3}-x_{2}^{4}-x_{0}^{3}x_{3}

\displaystyle\ +x_{0}^{2}x_{1}x_{3}-x_{0}x_{1}^{2}x_{3}-x_{0}^{2}x_{2}x_{3}-x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}-x_{0}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+x_{0}^{2}x_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle\ -x_{0}x_{1}x_{3}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{3}^{2}-x_{0}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}-x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}^{2}+x_{1}x_{3}^{3}-x_{3}^{4}\Big)\kern 5.0pt.

Also take any
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{n}}
and expand it as
b=b_{3}a^{3}+b_{2}a^{2}+b_{1}a+b_{0}
with
b_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}
. Now replacing
x_{0}
with
x_{0}+x_{2}
we
obtain

\displaystyle f(x^{\prime})+{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bx^{\prime})

\displaystyle={\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}\Big(x_{2}\big((x_{0}+x_{1})x_{3}^{2}-(x_{0}+x_{1})^{2}x_{3}-(x_{0}+x_{1})^{3}+b_{0}+b_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\ +x_{0}(x_{0}+x_{1})^{3}-x_{3}(x_{0}-x_{3})(x_{0}+x_{1})^{2}+x_{1}x_{3}^{3}-x_{3}^{4}

\displaystyle\ +x_{1}b_{3}+x_{3}b_{1}+x_{0}b_{0}\Big)

\displaystyle={\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}(x_{2}g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3})+h(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3}))\kern 5.0pt,

where
x^{\prime}=x_{3}a^{3}+x_{2}a^{2}+x_{1}a+x_{0}+x_{2}
and functions
g()
and
h()
are defined explicitly.

Thus, computing the Walsh transform in point
b
we obtain

\displaystyle S_{f}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{n}}}\omega^{f(x)+{\operatorname{Tr}}_{n}(bx)}

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}}\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}(x_{2}g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3})+h(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3^{k}\sum_{{x_{0},x_{1},x_{3}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}\atop g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3})=0}\atop x_{0}+x_{1}=0}\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}(h(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3}))}+3^{k}\sum_{{x_{0},x_{1},x_{3}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}\atop g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3})=0}\atop x_{0}+x_{1}\neq 0}\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}(h(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3}))}

Firstly, consider the case when
b_{0}+b_{2}=0
. Under this condition,
g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3})=0
if and only if
x_{0}+x_{1}=0
and, thus, the second sum
in (
3
) is zero. Indeed, assuming that
x_{0}+x_{1}\neq 0
we obtain

g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3})=(x_{0}+x_{1})^{3}\left(\left(\frac{x_{3}}{x_{0}+x_{1}}\right)^{2}-\frac{x_{3}}{x_{0}+x_{1}}-1\right)

and
g(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3})=0
has solutions if and only if the discriminant equal to
-1
is a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}
which is false since
k
is
odd. Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{f}(b)

\displaystyle=3^{k}\sum_{x_{0},x_{1},x_{3}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}\atop x_{0}+x_{1}=0}\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}(h(x_{0},x_{1},x_{3}))}

\displaystyle=3^{k}\sum_{x_{1},x_{3}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{3^{k}}}\omega^{{\operatorname{Tr}}_{k}(h(-x_{1},x_{1},x_{3}))}

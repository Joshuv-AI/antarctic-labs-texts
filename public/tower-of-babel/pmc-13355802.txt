Outcomes in venous thromboembolism with retained vs removed inferior vena cava filters: an analysis from the Registro Informatizado de Enfermedad TromboEmbólica

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRetrievable inferior vena cava (IVC) filters are used in patients with acute venous thromboembolism (VTE) who have temporary contraindications to anticoagulation. Although guidelines recommend removing filters once anticoagulation is feasible, many filters remain in place. Long-term consequences of retention remain unclear.ObjectivesThis study compared clinical outcomes in patients with VTE and retrievable IVC filters, according to filter status and anticoagulation use.MethodsWe analyzed patients from the international RIETE (Registro Informatizado de Enfermedad TromboEmbólica) registry who received retrievable IVC filters. Outcomes from day 90 onward were stratified by filter status (removed vs retained) and anticoagulation (on vs off). Primary endpoints included VTE recurrence, major bleeding, and all-cause mortality. Filter-related complications were assessed.ResultsAmong 1186 patients (mean age, 62 years; 51% male), 51.9% had filters removed by day 90. Patients with retained filters more often had active cancer and received longer anticoagulation, primarily low-molecular-weight heparin. VTE recurrence was higher with retained filters (4.5-6.5 vs 1.2-1.3 events per 100 patient-years), mainly as deep vein thrombosis; removal was associated with lower risk (adjusted incidence rate ratio, 0.29; 95% CI, 0.15-0.54). All-cause mortality was higher in patients with retained filters (7.3-17.7 vs 3.8-4.5; removal: adjusted odds ratio, 0.53; 95% CI, 0.34-0.81). Major bleeding was infrequent, with higher rates in patients with retained filters off anticoagulation. Filter-related complications were rare and did not differ significantly between groups after adjustment.ConclusionIn this large registry, retained IVC filters were associated with higher risk of VTE recurrence, even with extended anticoagulation. Findings support structured follow-up and timely filter retrieval.

Introduction

Venous thromboembolism (VTE) is primarily treated with anticoagulation, which effectively reduces the risk of recurrence and mortality. However, in patients with acute VTE who have a temporary or permanent contraindication to anticoagulant therapy, insertion of an inferior vena cava (IVC) filter is a widely used alternative aimed at preventing pulmonary embolism (PE) [[1], [2], [3]].

Over the past few decades, IVC filter use has increased markedly, extending beyond well-established indications such as active bleeding or urgent surgery. Following 2 US Food and Drug Administration safety communications warning of device-related complications, filter use has declined, particularly in the United States; yet, retrieval rates remain low [4,5]. Many filters intended to be temporary are never removed, often due to persistent contraindications, limited life expectancy, or failures in follow-up systems [4,5].

Although IVC filters may reduce fatal PE in selected high-risk patients, they do not treat established thrombus and do not prevent deep vein thrombosis (DVT). Extended filter dwell time has been associated with complications including filter thrombosis, IVC occlusion, vessel wall perforation, migration, and fracture [6,7]. Accordingly, multiple guidelines—including those from the American Society of Hematology and the American College of Chest Physicians—recommend prompt removal once anticoagulation becomes feasible [1,2].

Despite these recommendations, filters are frequently left in place, particularly in patients with ongoing comorbidities or poor follow-up [[8], [9], [10], [11]]. Extended anticoagulation has been proposed to reduce risk in these patients, but supporting evidence is limited and inconsistent [[12], [13], [14], [15]]. To address this knowledge gap, we analyzed data from the international RIETE (Registro Informatizado de Enfermedad TromboEmbólica) registry to compare long-term clinical outcomes in patients with retrievable IVC filters, stratified by filter status (removed vs retained) and anticoagulation use.

Methods

Study design and participants

RIETE is an ongoing, prospective, international registry of patients with acute VTE (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02832245). It collects standardized data on demographics, risk factors, treatments, and clinical outcomes across multiple participating centers. The registry’s design and methodology have been described previously [16].

For this analysis, we included patients enrolled between 2001 and 2024 with acute VTE who received a retrievable IVC filter. Patients in whom IVC filter retrieval was attempted but technically unsuccessful were excluded from the analytic cohort. Patients were categorized according to filter status (removed or retained) and anticoagulant status at day 90 after the index VTE diagnosis. We excluded patients with missing follow-up data, atypical VTE locations (eg, upper extremity thrombosis), or undocumented filter status.

Study groups and follow-up

Patients were stratified into 4 mutually exclusive groups based on filter and anticoagulation status at day 90: group A—retained filter, on anticoagulation; group B—retained filter, off anticoagulation; group C—filter removed, on anticoagulation; and group D—filter removed, off anticoagulation. Patients whose filter was removed after day 90 were classified as retained. Similarly, those who discontinued anticoagulation before day 90 were considered off anticoagulation. Anticoagulation status at day 90 was determined based on prospectively recorded treatment data in the registry. Patients were considered off anticoagulation only if anticoagulant therapy had been discontinued and a stop date was recorded. Day 90 was selected as the landmark time point because 3 months represents the guideline-recommended minimum duration of anticoagulant therapy for VTE [1,2] and a clinically relevant window for reassessing the indication for IVC filter retrieval. Patients with unclear or missing status were flagged for sensitivity analyses. Follow-up began at day 90 [17]. All events and person-time were counted from this time point onward.

Data acquisition

Clinical data were collected prospectively at each site using a standardized electronic case report form. Investigators were responsible for data entry and verification. Centralized quality checks ensured consistency across sites. Data collected included demographics, VTE characteristics, treatment details (including anticoagulation type and duration), and clinical outcomes (eg, VTE recurrence, bleeding, mortality, and filter-related complications).

Variables and outcomes definitions

The primary clinical outcomes assessed were as follows: (1) VTE recurrence, defined as a new, objectively confirmed DVT or PE; (2) major bleeding, based on International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) criteria (eg, critical site bleeding, fatal bleeding, or transfusion of ≥2 units); and (3) all-cause mortality, with subcategories for PE-related, bleeding-related, and cancer-related deaths.

Filter-related complications included filter thrombosis or IVC thrombosis, tilt (>15°), migration (>2 cm), perforation, fracture, and other reported mechanical events. For the purposes of this analysis, filter-related complications were assessed in aggregate.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables are reported as means (±SD) or medians (IQR), depending on distribution. Categorical variables are expressed as counts and percentages. Comparisons between groups used Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U-test for continuous variables and chi-squared or Fisher exact test for categorical variables.

Event rates were calculated per 100 patient-years. Multivariable Poisson regression (or negative binomial regression if overdispersion was detected) was used to estimate adjusted incidence rate ratios (aIRRs) for VTE recurrence, major bleeding, and mortality. Adjusted variables included age, sex, active cancer, VTE type, anticoagulant regimen and duration, and filter indication. Logistic regression was used for binary outcomes (eg, mortality and filter-related complications), with both crude and adjusted odds ratios reported. Comparisons included the following: (1) group A vs group B; (2) group C vs group D; (3) secondary—group A vs group C and group B vs group D; and (4) pooled—retained (A+B) vs removed (C+D). Calendar year of VTE diagnosis was additionally evaluated in sensitivity analyses to assess potential temporal confounding. Cumulative incidence curves were generated to illustrate time-to-event data for VTE recurrence, with unadjusted comparisons using the log-rank test.

Role of the funding source

Funding bodies had no role in study design, data collection, analysis, interpretation, or manuscript preparation. They do not review or influence any part of this work.

Results

Patient characteristics

After applying predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 1186 patients with retrievable IVC filters were included in the final analysis. Details of patient selection and exclusions are shown in Supplementary Figure 1. These patients were classified at day 90 into 4 mutually exclusive groups: group A (retained IVC filter/on anticoagulant treatment, n = 342); group B (retained/off anticoagulation, n = 229); group C (IVC filter removed/on anticoagulation, n = 415); and group D (removed/off anticoagulation, n = 200).

Mean age was 62 ± 17 years and 50.8% were male. Overall, the filter was removed in 615 (51.9%) patients. Patients with retained filters were more likely to have active cancer at VTE diagnosis (32.2% vs 21.0%; P < .001). Initial VTE presentation was PE (with or without concomitant DVT) in 817 patients (68.9%), with similar distributions across groups. Isolated DVT accounted for 31%. At day 90, low-molecular-weight heparin remained the most frequently used agent (30%), especially in group A (57%), while DOACs were more often used in group C (37%). Clinical outcomes

VTE recurrence was higher in patients with retained filters: group A, 7.3% (4.53 per 100 patient-years; 95% CI, 2.96-6.94); group B, 7.9% (6.54; 95% CI, 4.04-10.59); group C, 1.7% (1.21; 95% CI, 0.62-2.35); and group D, 2.0% (1.31; 95% CI, 0.49-3.50). Most recurrent events in patients with retained filters were DVT.

Major bleeding was infrequent across all groups. The highest rate was observed in group B (3.14; 95% CI, 1.64-6.01) and the lowest in group D (1.31; 95% CI, 0.49-3.50).

All-cause mortality was significantly higher in patients with retained filters: 22.3% (17.7; 95% CI, 13.9-22.6) in group B; 12.6% (7.34; 95% CI, 5.71-9.44) in group A; 6.5% (4.53; 95% CI, 3.44-5.97) in group C; and 6.0% (3.79; 95% CI, 2.44-5.90) in group D (Table 3; Supplementary Figure 2). In pooled comparisons, retained filters (groups A+B) were associated with significantly higher risks of VTE recurrence and all-cause mortality. Filter removal was associated with a 71% lower adjusted risk of VTE recurrence (aIRR, 0.29; 95% CI, 0.15-0.54) and a 47% lower adjusted risk of all-cause mortality (aIRR, 0.53; 95% CI, 0.34-0.81) compared with filter retention. These associations remained significant after adjustment for baseline covariates, including active cancer, and were unchanged in sensitivity analyses after additional adjustment for calendar year of diagnosis. The pooled difference was concentrated in DVT recurrence favoring filter removal (aIRR, ≈ 0.12; 95% CI, 0.05-0.32), whereas PE recurrence differences were smaller and imprecisely estimated because of few events (Table 4). Filter-related complications

Filter-related complications were infrequent overall, but occurred more often in patients with retained filters. Incidence rates were 0.52 per 100 patient-years (95% CI, 0.13-1.41) in group A and 1.44 (95% CI, 0.46-3.48) in group B, compared with 0.34 (95% CI, 0.06-1.11) in group C and 0.32 (95% CI, 0.02-1.57) in group D (Table 3). Details on filter-related complication subtypes (migration, occluding thrombus, vena cava thrombosis, infection, and other) are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

In pooled analyses, the crude complication rate was 1.2% in patients with retained filters (Groups A+B) vs 0.5% in those with removed filters (Groups C+D). After multivariable adjustment, this difference did not reach statistical significance (adjusted odds ratio, 0.26; 95% CI, 0.06-1.15) (Table 4).

Discussion

In this large, international registry of patients with retrievable IVC filters, filter retention was associated with significantly higher rates of VTE recurrence, even among patients receiving prolonged anticoagulation. In contrast, major bleeding and filter-related complications were infrequent, with no statistically significant difference between retained and removed filters.

Our findings emphasize that IVC filter retention should not be viewed as a benign or default strategy. Retained filters appear to confer a persistent thrombotic risk, as evidenced by higher rates of recurrent DVT and overall VTE. Extended anticoagulation in these patients may reduce but does not fully offset this excess risk. These results underscore the importance of timely filter retrieval once anticoagulation is feasible, as recommended in multiple clinical guidelines [[1], [2], [3]].

The higher VTE recurrence rates observed in patients with retained filters may reflect both mechanical and biological mechanisms. Filters can disrupt caval flow and promote stasis, increasing thrombotic risk. They may also trigger endothelial dysfunction or serve as a nidus for clot propagation [18]. Although our data cannot establish causality, the observed association with recurrent VTE is clinically relevant and aligns with concerns regarding prolonged filter exposure. Consistent with prior reports, most recurrent events were DVT rather than PE, suggesting that the excess risk in patients with retained filters primarily reflects local thrombosis around or below the filter rather than embolic recurrence.

Evidence regarding optimal anticoagulation in patients with retained filters remains mixed. A randomized trial (Prevention du Risque d'Embolie Pulmonaire par Interruption Cave 2) in patients with acute PE showed that adding a retrievable IVC filter to anticoagulation did not reduce the risk of recurrent PE compared with anticoagulation alone. However, it was associated with an increased risk of DVT, indicating limited benefit in the short term [12]. Observational studies on anticoagulation in patients with retained IVC filters have yielded conflicting results. One retrospective study reported fewer filter-related complications with rivaroxaban [13], while another study suggested filters alone may offer PE protection [14]. US national data show filter placement in patients unable to receive anticoagulation is associated with higher short-term mortality [15]. Our results extend these findings to a large, unselected registry population, showing that retention is associated with more recurrences despite prolonged anticoagulation. Hence, anticoagulation appears necessary but insufficient to normalize outcomes in patients with retained filters. The relatively low use of DOACs reflects the long inclusion period of the registry. However, adjustment for calendar year did not alter the results, suggesting that temporal changes in treatment practice did not explain the observed associations.

All-cause mortality was higher in patients with retained filters, even after adjustment for cancer and other risk factors. However, this finding should be interpreted cautiously as an association rather than evidence of a causal mortality benefit from filter retrieval, since prognostic differences between patients with retained vs removed filters may have contributed to this association. Although active cancer likely contributed to differences in outcomes, adjustment for malignancy did not eliminate the observed association. Residual confounding and baseline prognostic differences may still have contributed to the mortality gradient. Only 1 PE-related death was reported, underscoring that most excess mortality was not directly attributable to embolism. IVC filters are frequently used in oncology patients, who also have lower retrieval rates and a higher risk of complications [11,19].

Filter-related complications were uncommon overall, although numerically higher in the retained-filter groups. Consistent with the short median dwell time (≤ 1 year), individual complication subtypes were rare. This likely explains the lower complication rates observed in this study compared with prior studies that reported more frequent mechanical events with long-term retention [20,21].

Importantly, our study found relatively high retrieval rates (51.9%) compared with other real-world reports, which typically show retrieval in fewer than 40% of cases [8,22,23]. This may reflect more systematic follow-up and VTE-focused care in participating RIETE centers, although the registry itself is noninterventional and cannot dictate clinical practice. Notably, a study from the United States has shown that active surveillance and system-level interventions can substantially improve retrieval success [9], further supporting guideline-based follow-up protocols. Since RIETE is noninterventional, no causal inference about follow-up pathways can be made.

This study has limitations. As a nonrandomized observational registry, residual confounding is possible despite multivariable adjustment. We lacked detailed data on filter types, placement techniques, and imaging confirmation of complications. Cause-specific mortality data were limited, and we could not account for all potential confounders in anticoagulant selection or dosing. Group B (patients with retained filters who were off anticoagulation at day 90) may represent a clinically heterogeneous population, including patients in whom anticoagulation was discontinued for different reasons, which may introduce confounding by indication. Finally, information on repeat or reinsertion of IVC filters after retrieval is not systematically captured in the RIETE registry and, therefore, could not be analyzed. Such events, if present, may influence the interpretation of outcomes following filter retrieval. Strengths of this study include its large international scope, prospective design, and systematic outcome capture in RIETE.

Our findings have important clinical implications. Patients with retained filters remain at elevated risk of recurrent thrombotic events despite extended anticoagulation. These results reinforce guideline recommendations for structured follow-up and timely retrieval whenever feasible, particularly to reduce prolonged filter exposure and thrombotic complications. Future studies should explore optimal anticoagulant type and duration in patients with retained filters and assess interventions to increase safe filter removal.

In conclusion, among patients with VTE receiving retrievable IVC filters, retained filters were associated with higher rates of recurrent VTE and higher all-cause mortality than removed filters. However, the mortality difference should be interpreted as associative rather than causal. The main practical implication of these findings is the need for active reassessment of filter indication and retrieval status during follow-up, with timely filter removal pursued once clinically feasible.

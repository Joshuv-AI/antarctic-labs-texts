On the Geometry and Uniqueness of Asymptotically Locally Hyperbolic Static Vacuum Black Holes
Brian Harvie and Ye-Kai Wang

Abstract
. We establish an inequality relating the surface gravity and topology of a horizon in a 3 3 -dimensional asymptotically locally hyperbolic static space with the geometry at infinity. Equality is achieved only by the Kottler black holes, and this rigidity leads to several new static black hole uniqueness theorems for a negative cosmological constant. First, the ADS-Schwarzschild black hole with critical surface gravity κ = − Λ \kappa=\sqrt{-\Lambda} is unique. Second, the toroidal Kottler black holes are unique in the absence of spherical horizons. Third, the hyperbolic Kottler black holes with mass m > 0 m>0 are unique *if* the generalized Penrose inequality holds for the corresponding class of static spaces. Building on work of Ge-Wang-Wu-Xia [ 28 ] , we then use this fact to obtain uniqueness for static ALH graphs with hyperbolic infinities. This inequality follows from a generalization of the Minkowski inequality in ADS-Schwarzschild space due to Brendle-Hung-Wang [ 15 ] . Using optimal coefficients for the sub-static Heintze-Karcher inequality from [ 23 ] , we construct a new monotone quantity under inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF) in static spaces with Λ < 0 \Lambda<0 . Another fundamental tool developed in this paper is a regularity theorem for IMCF in asymptotically locally hyperbolic manifolds. Specifically, we prove that a weak solution of IMCF in an ALH 3 3 -manifold with horizon boundary is eventually smooth. This extends the regularity theorem for a spherical infinity due to Shi-Zhu [ 51 ] .



1.
Introduction

The classification of stationary black hole solutions to the Einstein field equations, broadly referred to as the “no-hair conjecture”, remains a challenging and fundamental line of research in general relativity. This problem is more tractable for black hole spacetimes which are static, but even under this stronger assumption the existing results are mostly limited to black holes with zero or positive cosmological constant. The main aim of this paper is to establish geometric properties and uniqueness theorems for the time slices of isolated static vacuum black holes with a negative cosmological constant.

\langle\eta,\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\rangle>0

(2)

1.2.
Further Directions

We now discuss possible extensions of the main theorems in this paper, beginning with the prospect of a stronger black hole uniqueness theorem for spherical infinity. One potential approach along these lines might involve an additional Minkowski inequality to the one in Theorem
1.2
. In
[
31
]
, the authors gave a new proof of Israel’s theorem
[
39
]
on the uniqueness of Schwarzschild space based on a Minkowski inequality for the level sets of the static potential. Namely, we proved that if
\Sigma=\{V=s\}
is an outer-minimizing level set of a static potential
V
on an asymptotically flat
3
-manifold
(M^{3},g)

3
3

3

The level-set inequality holds in all dimensions, but we focus on
n=3

, then

(1.17)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{V=s\}}Hd\sigma\geq s\left(\frac{|\{V=s\}|}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Taking (

) over near-horizon level sets in an asymptotically flat static
(M^{3},g,V)
implies an upper bound on surface gravity
\kappa

[
31

forces rotational symmetry of
(M^{3},g)

1.17
) also holds on level sets of an asymptotically hyperbolic static potential (note that this is trivially true in ADS-Schwarzschild), the resulting upper bound on
\kappa
would imply saturation in (

\widehat{\Sigma}=S^{2}
. In other words, given inequality (
1.5

equivalent
to inequality (
1.17

In the asymptotically flat setting, the proof of inequality (

\Lambda=0
static vacuum equations, see Corollary 8.2 in
[

Woolgar (

[

proved the Riemannian Penrose inequality with

for perturbations of ADS-Schwarzschild.

Lastly, we discuss a possible extension of the main inequality to higher dimensions. The relevant quantity to consider under IMCF in higher-dimensional static spaces is given by

Q(t)=\left(|\Sigma_{t}|\right)^{\frac{2-n}{n-1}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma-n(n-1)\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+2\frac{n-1}{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{\int_{\partial_{j}M}R_{\partial_{j}M}d\sigma}{n(n-1)|\partial_{j}M|+\int_{\partial_{j}M}R_{\partial_{j}M}d\sigma}\right),

1.3.
Methods and Structure of the Paper

Section 2 reviews elementary properties of static horizons and the asymptotic profile of ALH metrics.

Section 3 introduces the quantity

Q(t)=(|\Sigma_{t}|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+4\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{2\chi(\partial_{j}M)}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|\right),

which is shown to be monotone under the smooth inverse mean curvature flow
\Sigma_{t}
of a surface
\Sigma
that encloses horizons
\partial_{1}M,\dots,\partial_{K}M,\dots,\partial_{L}M
as long as the horizons
\partial_{K+1}M,\dots,\partial_{L}M
are not homeomorphic to
S^{2}
. This follows from the
Heintze-Karcher inequality
, which says that for each
\Sigma_{t}
,

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{V}{H}d\sigma\geq\frac{3}{2}\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+\sum_{j=1}^{L}c_{j}\int_{\partial_{j}M}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}d\sigma.

For constants
c_{j}
determined by the data of
\partial_{j}M
. Fogagnolo-Pinamonti
[
23
]
determined optimal values for
c_{j}
, and amazingly their constants are determined only by the area and horizon topology in
3
-dimensional static vacuum spaces. We also see from these constants that the horizons
\partial_{K+1}M,\dots,\partial_{L}M
make negligible contributions to the variation of
Q(t)
given
\chi(\partial_{j}M)\leq 0
.

In Section 4, we extend the analysis in Section 3 to the weak solutions of IMCF introduced by Huisken-Ilmanen
[
37
]
, showing in particular that
Q(t)
remains monotone under the weak flow. A weak solution of IMCF includes “jump times” wherein parts of the flow surface
\Sigma_{t}
are replaced by minimal surfaces. Examining each term in
Q(t)
makes it clear that this quantity should decrease across a jump. To rigorously justify this, we need a version of the Heintze-Karcher inequality that applies to almost every
\Sigma_{t}
and an explicit growth formula for the total mean curvature. The former may be obtained via an approximation by mean-convex mean curvature flow as in
[
30
]
, whereas the latter is well-known for static spaces, see
[
57
]
,
[
45
]

[
31
]
. It is also important to consider the presence of horizons
\partial_{K+1}M,\dots,\partial_{L}M
not enclosed by the initial data
\Sigma_{t}
. Here, we apply the procedure described in
[
37
]
of restarting weak IMCF as a flow surface
\Sigma_{t}
approaches one of these horizons. This process causes
\Sigma_{t}
to enclose additional horizons as time increases, and to preserve monotonicity we need that
\partial_{K+1}M,\dots,\partial_{L}M
are non-spherical as in the previous section.

Section 5 addresses the regularity of IMCF in asymptotically locally hyperbolic 3-manifolds. We follow the analysis of Lee-Neves. In particular, the assumption
\min_{j\in\{1,\dots,J\}}\chi(\partial_{j}M)\leq\chi(\widehat{\Sigma})
allows the use of Meek-Simon-Yau. Our new observation is that the
L^{2+\epsilon}
-norm of the mean curvature decays along a sequence of
\Sigma_{t_{i}}
. Together with an area bound, Allard’s regularity then implies that
\Sigma_{t_{i}}
eventually becomes star-shaped. For star-shaped solution of IMCF, standard arguments that works in all dimensions show that the solution becomes smooth. The regularity result was first demonstrated for IMCF in asymptotically hyperbolic 3-manifolds by Shi-Zhu
[
51
]
. We minimize the use of Hawking mass in the analysis so that the decay assumption is weaker and the additional decay of scalar curvature is not needed.

In Section 6, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of smooth graphical solutions to IMCF, similar to Brendle-Hung-Wang and Ge-Wang-Wu-Xia, in order to evaluate the limit of
Q(t)
and complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In Section 7, we prove the rigidity statement of Theorem
1.2
– namely, equality is achieved in (
1.2
) for
K=1
if and only if
(M^{3},g,V)
is isometric to Kottler space with
m>m_{\text{crit}}
. Recently, Borghini-Fogagnolo-Pinamonti
[
8
]
gave an equality statement for the Heintze-Karcher inequality, showing that if
\Omega^{n}\subset(M^{n},g)
is bounded by a single horizon then
(\Omega^{n},g)
splits as a warped product over
\partial M
. From here, it is easy to show that if
(M^{n},g,V)
is static with
\Lambda=-n
then
(\Omega^{n},g)
is a piece
(\widehat{\Sigma}^{n-1}\times(\rho_{m},\rho_{0}),\widehat{g}_{m,\widehat{k}})
of a Kottler metric, although in higher dimensions the base metric
\widehat{g}
need only be Einstein. The equality statement in Theorem
1.2
comes more or less as a corollary of this, since
\Sigma_{t}
must saturate the Heintze-Karcher inequality when
\Sigma
achieves equality in (
1.5
).

Finally in Section 8, we address the applications of Theorem
1.2
to black hole uniqueness. Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
immediately follow from the Minkowski inequality, but to prove Theorem
1.10
we first need the following upper bound on the Wang-Chruściel-Herzlich mass

of ALH static systems in terms of the surface gravities

of the horizons
\partial_{j}M
, which arises from taking the Heintze-Karcher inequality over slices

\widehat{\Sigma}\times\{\rho\}

[

2.
Some Preliminaries on ALH Static Systems

2.1.
Geometry of Static Horizons

The geometric conditions that the static equations impose over the zero set of
V
are crucial in the construction of a monotone quantity under inverse mean curvature flow. Therefore, we review some well-known facts about static horizons which the experienced reader may skip.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(M^{3},g,V)
be an ALH static system. Then for each
j\in\{1,\dots,J\}
:

(1)

\partial_{j}M
is totally geodesic in
(M^{3},g)
.

(2)

|dV|_{g}
is equal to a constant
\kappa_{j}>0
on
\partial_{j}M
, known as the surface gravity of
\partial_{j}M
.

(3)

\partial_{j}M
is outer-minimizing in
(M^{3},g)
– that is,
|\Sigma|\geq|\partial_{j}M|
for any surface
\Sigma
enclosing
\partial_{j}M
. In fact, no closed area-minimizing surfaces lie in the interior of
(M^{3},g)
.

Proof.

Since
V=0
on
\partial_{j}M
, we have that
\Delta V(x)=0
and hence
\nabla^{2}V(x)=0
at each
x\in\partial_{j}M
by equation (
1.2
). The ambient and intrinsic Hessians on
\partial_{j}M
are related by

\nabla^{2}V=\nabla^{2}_{\partial_{j}M}V+\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}A,

where
A
denotes the second fundamental form of
\partial_{j}M\subset(M^{3},g)
. Since
\partial M
is a regular level set of
V
, we have that
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}>0
and hence
A\equiv 0
on
\partial_{j}M
. For item 2, we have

0=\nabla^{2}V(X,\nu)=X\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}\right)-A(X,\nabla_{\partial_{j}M}V)=X(\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu})

for any smooth tangent vector field
X\in\Gamma(T(\partial_{j}M))
on
\partial_{j}M
, and thus the normal derivative is constant on
\partial_{j}M
. Lastly we address item 3. Let
\Sigma\subset(M^{3},g)
be the minimizing hull of
\partial_{j}M
, i.e. the least-area surface enclosing
\partial_{j}M
, and suppose
\Sigma\neq\partial_{j}M
. It follows from the strong maximum principle that
\Sigma
is disjoint from
\partial_{j}M
. Let
X:\Sigma\times(0,\epsilon)\rightarrow(M^{3},g)
be a one-parameter family of immersions satisfying

(2.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}X(x,s)=V\nu(x,s).\\

Such immersions are obtained by taking the normal exponential map of
\Sigma
with respect to the metric
g=V^{-2}g
. One may calculate under (
2.1
), c.f. Proposition 3.2 in
[
14
]
, that

\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{H}{V}\right)=-|A|^{2}.

Since
H(x)=0
for each
x\in\Sigma
, we get
H\leq 0
on
\Sigma_{s}=X(\Sigma,s)
. So from the variation formula for the area,

|\Sigma_{s}|-|\Sigma|=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}HVd\sigma ds\leq 0.

On the other hand,
\Sigma_{s}
properly encloses
\Sigma
, contradicting the area-minimizing property of
\Sigma
. Altogether. we conclude that
\Sigma=\partial_{j}M
. Note that this argument rules the existence of any closed area-minimizing surface in
(M^{3},g)
.
∎

2.2.
Geometry of the Exterior Region

Theorem
1.2
requires a clear asymptotic profile for the geometry of ALH
(M^{3},g,V)

Recall the definition of asymptotically locally hyperbolicity in the introduction. We remark that the sectional curvature of

is
-1+O(\rho^{-\alpha})
. It is convenient to work with the coordinate

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{dr}=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\widehat{k}},

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sinh r,&\widehat{k}=1\\

If we set

\displaystyle\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\lambda^{2}(r)\widehat{g},

and since

\displaystyle|q|_{\bar{g}}+|\bar{\nabla}q|_{\bar{g}}+|\bar{\nabla}^{2}q|_{\bar{g}}+|\bar{\nabla}^{3}q|_{\bar{g}}=O(e^{-\alpha r}).

The advantage of coordinate

Remark 2.2
.

Let

be a local frame of

\displaystyle|q_{\alpha\beta}|,|\varepsilon_{\gamma}(q_{\alpha\beta})|,|\varepsilon_{\delta}(\varepsilon_{\gamma}(q_{\alpha\beta}))|,|\varepsilon_{\eta}(\varepsilon_{\delta}(\varepsilon_{\gamma}(q_{\alpha\beta})))|=O(e^{-\alpha r})

Except in Subsection

(\widehat{\Sigma},\widehat{g})
be an
(n-1)

and

defined on

. Two cases are considered in this paper:

(a)

and

.

(b)

The metric

is Einstein,

In both cases, we have

and the Ricci curvature of

is

Remark 2.3
.

The very weak case (a) only appears in Section

being a space form is Proposition

We first present two results on the asymptotic geometry of

and then compute the evolution equation of

Proposition 2.4
.

For tangent vectors
Y
and
Z

\langle D_{Y}\partial_{r},Z\rangle=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\left(\langle Y,Z\rangle-Y(r)\langle\partial_{r},Z\rangle\right)+\varepsilon(Y,Z)

(0,2)

Proof.

First of all,
\lambda\partial_{r}

\bar{g}

\lambda^{\prime}
.
Note that

implies the difference of Christoffel symbols

\lambda^{\prime}\langle Y,Z\rangle+O(e^{(1-k)r})=\langle D_{Y}(\lambda\partial_{r}),Z\rangle=\lambda\langle D_{Y}\partial_{r},Z\rangle+\lambda^{\prime}Y(r)\langle\partial_{r},Z\rangle
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

We have
\mbox{Hess}_{g}r=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\left(g-dr^{2}\right)+O(e^{-kr})
and
\Delta_{g}r=(n-1)\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}+O(e^{-kr})

Proof.

From

\displaystyle D^{2}_{\bar{g}}r(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle D^{2}_{\bar{g}}r(\partial_{r},\partial_{\theta^{i}})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle=\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\widehat{g}_{ij}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}(\bar{g}-dr^{2})(\partial_{\theta^{i}},\partial_{\theta^{j}})

Proposition 2.6
.

The evolution equation of

\displaystyle\quad+A*O(e^{-kr})+O(e^{-kr})\Big)

By (

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle=-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\partial_{i}r\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle+\varepsilon(\partial_{i},\nu)+h_{i}^{j}\langle\partial_{r},\partial_{j}\rangle.

3.

As a first step to Theorem

\Lambda=-3

[

[

[

]
,
[

]

[

]

[

]

[

]

[

Theorem 3.1

(
[
23
]
, Theorem 3.5)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,V)
be a sub-static Riemannian manifold. Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
has compact, non-empty

boundary
\partial M=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{J}\partial_{j}M
,
V
smoothly extends to

\partial M

\partial M=\{V=0\}

\partial\Omega=\partial_{1}M\dots\bigsqcup\partial_{L}M\bigsqcup\Sigma

(3.1)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{V}{H}d\sigma\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{\Omega}Vd\Omega+\sum_{j=1}^{L}c_{j}\int_{\partial_{j}M}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}d\sigma

on the hypersurface
\Sigma

is the constant

(3.2)

c_{j}=\frac{\int_{\partial_{j}M}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}d\sigma}{\int_{\partial_{j}M}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}\left(\frac{\Delta V}{V}-\frac{\nabla^{2}V(\nu,\nu)}{V}\right)d\sigma}>0.

Heintze and Karcher
[

originally established this inequality for mean-convex domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Later, Brendle extended the Heintze-Karcher inequality to sub-static warped product manifolds in his breakthrough work
[
14
]
. Brendle’s version of the Heintze-Karcher inequality was subsequently generalized to static manifolds with a single horizon by Wang-Wang in
[
55
]
and to sub-static manifolds with multiple horizons by Li-Xia in
[
43
]
. In
[
23
]
, Fogagnolo and Pinamonti improved the constants of Li-Xia’s inequality and relaxed the assumption from
[
55
]
on the boundary components– in particular, the constant
c_{j}
in (
3.2
) is well-defined and positive whenever each
\partial_{j}M
is homologous to a mean-convex hypersurface. When
n=3
and
(M^{3},g,V)
solves the static equations (
1.2
), the constant (
3.2
) reduces to

(3.3)

\displaystyle c_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\partial_{j}M}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}d\sigma}{\int_{\partial_{j}M}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}\left(\frac{\Delta V}{V}-\frac{\nabla^{2}V(\nu,\nu)}{V}\right)d\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|}{\kappa_{j}\int_{\partial_{j}M}-\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)d\sigma}=\frac{|\partial_{j}M|}{\int_{\partial_{j}M}\left(K_{\partial_{j}M}-\frac{1}{2}R_{g}\right)d\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\partial_{j}M|}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}.

In
[
15
]
, Brendle-Hung-Wang discovered an interaction between the Heintze-Karcher inequality and inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF) in ADS-Schwarzschild space. Recall that a one-parameter family of
C^{\infty}
mean-convex immersions
X:\Sigma^{n-1}\times[0,T)\rightarrow(M^{n},g)
move by inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF) if

(3.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(p,t)=\frac{1}{H}\nu(x,t)\hskip 56.9055pt(x,t)\in\Sigma^{n-1}\times[0,T),

where
\nu
is the outer unit normal and
H>0
the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{t}=X_{t}(\Sigma)
. The first variation for the bulk integral
\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega
under IMCF is given by the left-hand side of (
3.1
), and a monotone quantity under IMCF may be deduced from this. Because the constant (
3.3
) is determined by the area and topology of the horizons, the corresponding monotone quantity under IMCF in a general static background is determined by horizon area, topology, and surface gravity.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M^{3},g,V)
be a solution of the static vacuum equations (
1.2
) with compact boundary
\partial M=\partial_{1}M\bigsqcup\partial_{2}M\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup\partial_{J}M
. Suppose that
V
smoothly extends to
0
on
\partial M
. Let
\Omega\subset(M^{3},g)
be a smooth domain in
(M^{3},g)
such that

(3.5)

\partial\Omega=\partial_{1}M\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup\partial_{K}M\dots\bigsqcup\partial_{L}M\bigsqcup\Sigma,\hskip 56.9055pt1\leq K\leq L,

for some connected mean-convex surface
\Sigma
. Now suppose that
\chi(\partial_{j}M)\leq 0
for each
j\in\{K+1,\dots,L\}
. Then the quantity

(3.6)

Q(t)=\left(|\Sigma_{t}|\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+4\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|\right)

is monotonically non-increasing along the IMCF
\Sigma_{t}\subset(M^{3},g)
of
\Sigma
. Furthermore, if
Q(t)
is constant along IMCF then each
\Sigma_{t}
is totally umbilical.

In the next section, we will show monotonicity of the functional
Q(t)
in (
3.6
) under weak solutions of inverse mean curvature flow starting from a domain
\Omega_{0}
bounded by horizons
\partial_{1}M,\dots\partial_{K}M
. If there are additional horizons
\partial_{K+1}M,\dots\partial_{L}M
exterior to
\Omega_{0}
, then following the approaches in
[
37
]
and
[
56
]
we construct “jumps” wherein weak IMCF is restarted from domains
\Omega^{\prime}
containing
\Omega_{0}
and bounded by these additional horizons. If the horizons
\partial_{K+1}M,\dots,\partial_{L}M
each have non-positive Euler characteristic, then their contributions to the variation formula for (
3.6
) are negligible, implying that
Q(t)
retains monotonicity after the jump times.

Proof.

First we compute

(3.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}\right)H+V\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}\right)+VHd\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}-V\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{1}{H}-\left(|A|^{2}+\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\right)\frac{V}{H}+VHd\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}2\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}-\frac{1}{H}\left(\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}V+H\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}+V\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\right)-\frac{V}{H}|A|^{2}+VHd\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma.

In the last line, we used the identity
\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}V+H\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}+V\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)=0
implied by the static equations and the inequality
H^{2}\leq 2|A|^{2}
. Now, by the divergence theorem

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}d\sigma=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\Delta Vd\Omega+\sum_{j=1}^{L}\int_{\partial_{j}M}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}d\sigma=3\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+\sum_{j=1}^{L}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|,

where
\kappa_{j}=\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}|_{\partial_{j}M}
. Thus (
3.7
) becomes

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma+6\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\sigma+2\sum_{j=1}^{L}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|.

For the bulk term, we apply the Heintze-Karcher inequality (
3.1
) with
c_{j}
from (
3.3
) to
\Sigma_{t}=\partial\Omega_{t}
and find

\frac{d}{dt}\left(-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega\right)=-6\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{V}{H}d\sigma\leq-9\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega-6\sum_{j=1}^{L}\frac{|\partial_{j}M|}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|.

Combining these, we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+4\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma-3\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\sigma+2\sum_{j=1}^{L}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|-6\sum_{j=1}^{L}\frac{|\partial_{j}M|}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+4\sum_{j=1}^{L}\frac{2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|\right)

Applying the assumption
\chi(\partial_{j}M)\leq 0
for
j\in\{K+1,\dots,L\}
to the last line, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+4\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}VHd\sigma-6\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega+4\sum_{j=1}^{K}\frac{2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}{3|\partial_{j}M|+2\pi\chi(\partial_{j}M)}\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|\right)

Finally by the area growth formula
|\Sigma_{t}|=e^{t}|\Sigma_{0}|
under IMCF, we altogether find that

\frac{d}{dt}Q(t)\leq 0.

Also note that equality is achieved in (
3.7
) only if
|A|^{2}=2H^{2}
.
∎

4.
Montonicity for Weak Inverse Mean Curvature Flow

To obtain the Minkowski inequality for outer-minimizing surfaces, we must prove the functional
Q(t)
introduced in the previous section is monotone under proper weak solutions of inverse mean curvature flow. Our monotonicity argument here is based off approaches of Huisken-Ilmanen
[
37
]
, of Wei in
[
56
]
, and of the first author in
[
30
]
.

4.1.
The Heintze-Karcher Inequality for Weak Mean Curvature

Before introducing weak IMCF, we first show that the Heintze-Karcher inequality (
3.1
) extends to
C^{1,\alpha}
hypersurfaces with bounded non-negative, and integrable weak mean curvature. This allows one to estimate
\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega
under weak IMCF. Our approach is approximation by mean-convex mean curvature flow. This approximation has been used in
[
36
]
for
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and in
[
58
]
for more general backgrounds.

Theorem 4.1

(Approximation by Mean-Convex Mean Curvature Flow,
[
58
]
Lemma 4.4)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold, and let
\Sigma_{0}=X_{0}(\Sigma^{n-1})\subset M^{n}
be an immersion of class
C^{1}
with bounded and non-negative measurable weak mean curvature
H
. Then
\Sigma_{0}
is of class
C^{1,\alpha}\cap W^{2,p}
, and there exists a solution
X:\Sigma^{n-1}\times(0,\epsilon_{0})\rightarrow(M^{n},g)
to mean curvature flow (MCF),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}X(x,\epsilon)=-H\nu(x,\epsilon),\hskip 56.9055pt(x,\epsilon)\in\Sigma^{n-1}\times(0,\epsilon_{0}),

converging to
\Sigma_{0}
locally in
C^{1,\alpha}\cap W^{2,p}
,
\alpha\in(0,1),p\in(1,\infty)
and with
H(x,\epsilon)\rightarrow H(x)
strongly in
L^{p}
for
p\in[1,\infty)
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. Furthermore, for every
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
we have
\min_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}H>0
.

Closely following the arguments in Section 4 of
[
30
]
, we study the reciporical mean curvature
H^{-1}_{\epsilon}
of
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
under MCF as
\epsilon\searrow 0
.
||H_{\epsilon}^{-1}||_{L^{1}(\Sigma_{\epsilon})}
is monotonically decreasing in
\epsilon
under MCF, and this suggests
L^{1}
convergence as
\epsilon\searrow 0
. However, we must handle the case where
H^{-1}
is integrable and
\text{essinf}_{\Sigma_{0}}H=0
delicately.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
,
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
be as in Theorem
4.1
, and suppose that the reciporical weak mean curvature
H(x,0)^{-1}
of
\Sigma_{0}
is
L^{1}
integrable. Then there is a subsequence
\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
of times such that the inverse mean curvature
H(x,\epsilon)^{-1}
of
\Sigma_{\epsilon_{j}}
converges to
H(x,\epsilon)^{-1}
strongly in
L^{1}
as
\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
.

Proof.

Let
U
be a small neighborhood of
\Sigma_{0}\subset M^{n}
, and define

\lambda=\min_{x\in\overline{U}}||\min\{\text{Ric}_{g}(E,E)|E\in T_{x}M^{n}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt||E||_{g}=1\}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the quantity

(4.2)

\psi_{k}(\epsilon)=\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{e^{-\lambda\epsilon}H+k^{-1}}d\sigma.

under (
4.1
). Since
\min_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}H>0
, the variation formula for mean curvature under MCF in
U
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}H+\left(|h|^{2}+\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\right)H

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}H+\left(|h|^{2}+\lambda\right)H,

and so

\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\left(e^{-\lambda\epsilon}H\right)\geq\Delta_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}e^{-\lambda\epsilon}H.

Using this, we compute the evolution of (
4.2
) under MCF:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\psi_{k}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{\left(e^{-\lambda\epsilon}H+k^{-1}\right)^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\epsilon}\left(e^{-\lambda\epsilon}H\right)-\frac{1}{e^{-\lambda\epsilon}H+k^{-1}}H^{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}\frac{2e^{-2\lambda\epsilon}}{\left(e^{-\lambda\epsilon}H+k^{-1}\right)^{3}}|\nabla H|^{2}d\sigma\leq 0,

and so (
4.2
) is monotonically decreasing in
\epsilon
. By the
L^{p}
convergence
H(x,\epsilon)\rightarrow H(x,0)
and the bound
\psi_{k}(x)\leq k
, we have that

\psi_{k}(\epsilon_{j})\rightarrow\psi_{k}(0)

along a subsequence of times
\epsilon_{j}
converging to
0
by the dominated convergence theorem. Combining this with (
4.3
) yields

(4.4)

\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{e^{-\lambda\epsilon}H+k^{-1}}d\sigma\leq\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{1}{H+k^{-1}}d\sigma,\hskip 28.45274ptk\in\mathbb{N},\hskip 28.45274pt\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}).

Now, for a fixed
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
we take the
k\searrow\infty
limit of each side of (
4.4
). By monotone convergence, we get

\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{H}d\sigma\leq e^{\lambda\epsilon}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{1}{H}d\sigma.

Hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}\frac{1}{H}d\sigma\leq\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{1}{H}d\sigma,

and once again by
L^{p}
convergence of
H_{\epsilon}
we have that that
H_{\epsilon_{j}}^{-1}\rightarrow H^{-1}
in
L^{1}
along a subsequence
\epsilon_{j}
.

∎

Theorem 4.3

(Heintze-Karcher Inequality for Weak Mean Curvature)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,V)
be a sub-static Riemannian manifold. Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
has compact, non-empty
C^{\infty}
boundary
\partial M=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{J}\partial_{j}M
,
V
smoothly extends to
0
on
\partial M
, and
\partial M=\{V=0\}
is a regular level set of
V
. Let
\Omega\subset(M^{n},g)
be a smooth domain with boundary
\partial\Omega=\partial_{1}M\dots\bigsqcup\partial_{L}M\bigsqcup\Sigma
for some
1\leq L\leq J
and some
C^{1}
hypersurface
\Sigma^{n-1}
with bounded, non-negative, and integrable weak mean curvature
H
. Then we have the inequality

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{V}{H}d\mu\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{\Omega}Vd\Omega+\sum_{j=1}^{L}c_{j}\int_{\partial_{j}M}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}d\sigma,

for the constants
c_{j}
defined in (
3.2
).

Proof.

We assume
H^{-1}
is integrable, since otherwise the statement is trivial. Let
X:\Sigma\times(0,\epsilon_{0})\rightarrow(M^{n},g)
be MCF from Proposition
4.1
, and let
\Omega_{\epsilon}\subset(M^{n},g)
be such that
\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}=\partial_{1}M\bigsqcup\dots\bigsqcup\partial_{K}M\bigsqcup\Sigma_{\epsilon}
. We consider the subsequence
\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
from Proposition
4.2
. By
C^{0}
convergence of
\Sigma_{\epsilon_{j}}
and
L^{1}
convergence of
H_{\epsilon_{j}}^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon_{j}}}Vd\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{0}}Vd\Omega,

\displaystyle\lim_{j}\int_{\Sigma_{\epsilon_{j}}}\frac{V}{H}d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{V}{H}d\sigma.

Since
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
is smooth with

3.1
) ove

to make the conclusion.
∎

4.2.
Monotonicity

We are now ready to prove that the quantity
Q(t)
remains monotone under weak inverse mean curvature flow, and we begin with a brief review of weak solution theory. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold, and
\Omega^{n}\subset(M^{n},g)
a smooth domain. A
weak IMCF with initial condition
\Omega_{0}
in
(M^{n},g)
is a function
u\in C^{0,1}_{\text{loc}}(M^{n})
such that

(4.5)

\{u<0\}=\Omega_{0},

and for any
t>0
the sub-level-sets
\Omega_{t}=\{u<t\}\subset M^{n}
satisfy

(4.6)

J_{u}\left(\Omega_{t}\right)\leq J_{u}(F)\hskip 28.45274pt\forall F\supset\Omega_{0},

where
F
is a finite-perimeter domain and
J_{u}
is the functional

(4.7)

J_{u}\left(F\right)=|\partial^{*}F|-\int_{F}|\nabla u|d\Omega.

The relationship between the variational formulation (
4.6
) and classical IMCF (
3.4
) is as follows: for smooth
u
with non-vanishing gradient, (
4.5
)-(
4.6
) are equivalent to the degenerate elliptic Dirichlet problem

(4.8)

\displaystyle\text{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla u|,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{in}\hskip 28.45274pt\left(M^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0},g\right),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\hskip 42.67912pt\text{on}\hskip 28.45274pt\Sigma_{0}=\partial\Omega_{0},

and the level sets
\Sigma_{t}=\{u=t\}
of solutions to (
4.8
) are readily checked to form a solution to smooth IMCF (
3.4
). Weak IMCF has been extremely effective in solving geometric problems on non-compact manifolds, as it was famously introduced by Huisken and Ilmanen in
[
37
]
to prove the Riemannian Penrose inequality for asymptotically flat manifolds with non-negative scalar curvature.

Lemma 3.1 in
[
41
]
confirms that there exists a unique weak IMCF
u\in C^{0,1}_{\text{loc}}(M^{n})
with compact level sets for any smooth domain
\Omega_{0}
within an asymptotically locally hyperbolic manifold
(M^{3},g)
(note that by choosing an arbitrary fill-in of
\partial M
, we may take
(M^{3},g)
to be complete). We summarize the key a priori properties of this solution established
[
37
]
which are relevant to the monotonicity argument.

Theorem 4.4

(Properties of weak IMCF)
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be an asymptotically locally hyperbolic Riemannian manifold, let
\Omega_{0}\subset(M^{3},g)
be a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega_{0}=\Sigma_{0}

be the proper weak IMCF with initial condition
\Omega_{0}
. Then the following hold, with the corresponding reference from
[
37
]
provided:

(i)

(Theorem 1.3, Regularity) For each
t>0
, the sets
\Sigma_{t}=\partial\{u<t\}
and
\Sigma^{+}_{t}=\partial\{u>t\}
are outer-minimizing, resp. strictly outer-minimizing,
C^{1,\alpha}
hypersurfaces.

(ii)

((1.12) and Lemma 5.1, Mean-convexity) For a.e.
t>0
,
\Sigma_{t}
and
\Sigma^{+}_{t}
have essentially bounded weak mean curvature
H_{\Sigma_{t}}(x)=|\nabla u(x)|>0
for
\mathcal{H}^{2}
a.e.
x\in\Sigma_{t}
.

(iii)

(Lemma 4.2, Connectedness) If
\Sigma_{0}
is a connected hypersurface, then
\Sigma_{t}
is a connected hypersurface for each
t>0
.

(iv)

((1.10), Convergence) For each
t>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have the convergences

(4.9)

\Sigma^{+}_{s}\rightarrow\Sigma_{t}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{as}\hskip 28.45274pts\nearrow t,\hskip 56.9055pt\Sigma_{s}\rightarrow\Sigma^{+}_{t}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{as}\hskip 28.45274pts\searrow t

in
C^{1,\alpha}
.

(v)

(Lemma 5.6, Area Growth) The
\mathcal{H}^{2}
measures of
\Sigma_{t}
and
\Sigma^{+}_{t}
are given by

(4.10)

|\Sigma_{t}|=|\Sigma^{+}_{t}|=e^{t}|\Sigma^{+}_{0}|.

In particular, if
\Sigma_{0}=\partial M
is outer-minimizing then
|\Sigma_{t}|=e^{t}|\Sigma_{0}|
.

We call
t_{0}\in[0,\infty)
a “jump time” if
\Sigma_{t_{0}}\neq\Sigma_{t_{0}}^{+}
. Intuitively, the reason that the quantity
Q(t)
in (
3.6
) remains monotone under the weak flow is because
H_{\Sigma^{+}_{t_{0}}\setminus\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\equiv 0
as a consequence of the outer-minimizing property, and so the first term in (
3.6
) decreases during a jump. On the other hand, the bulk integral
\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega
clearly increases across jumps, while the area remains the same by item (v). The growth of the first term of (
3.6
) in static manifolds is already understood rigorously, c.f.
[
56
]
,
[
45
]
,
[
31
]
, and
[
29
]
.

Lemma 4.5

(Growth of the Total Mean Curvature)
.

Let
(M^{3},g,V)
be an ALH static system, and let

\Sigma_{0}

. Taking fill-ins

\displaystyle\sqrt{|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{div}\left(\frac{\nabla u_{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}}\right)\hskip 56.9055pt\text{in}\hskip 28.45274ptF_{N},

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\hskip 147.95424pt\text{on}\hskip 28.45274pt\Sigma_{0},

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\hskip 145.10922pt\text{on}\hskip 28.45274pt\partial F_{N}\setminus\Sigma_{0},

where

[

.
∎

The Heintze-Karcher inequality for weak mean curvature accounts for the volumetric term
\int_{\Omega_{t}}Vd\Omega
under the weak IMCF.

Proposition 4.6

(Growth of the Bulk Integral)

Proof.

We write

\displaystyle(n-1)\frac{V}{H}-\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n-1}{H}-1\right)V+\left(\frac{H}{n-1}-1\right)\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(-\Delta_{g}V+D^{2}V(\nu,\nu)+\Delta_{\Sigma}V+(n-1)V\right).

Since

on

\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}=\rho+O(\rho^{1-\alpha})

and

Moreover, the mean curvature of

is given by

Recalling that

and applying Heintze-Karcher inequality to

, we get

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left(-1+(n-1)c_{j}\right)\kappa_{j}|\partial_{j}M|\leq-(n-2)\omega_{n-1}m.

∎

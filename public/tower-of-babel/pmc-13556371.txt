400. The interplay of causal attribution and reinforcement learning in bipolar disorder: a neural perspective
Authors: K-T Chen, L Mason
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReinforcement learning (RL) is shaped by perceived causal attributions and is supported by differential activation of brain regions, particularly the striatum. However, how these processes interact in bipolar disorder (BD) remains unclear.Aims & ObjectivesThis study examined the influence of BD on causal attribution and the neural mechanisms underlying reward-belief updating within manipulated causal structures, compared with healthy controls (HCs).MethodTwenty-three individuals with BD (5 males, 18 females) and 18 HCs (8 males, 10 females) completed a causal-manipulated reward-learning task during functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI). Behavioral performance was evaluated using generalized linear mixed-effects models and Bayesian RL models. Neural responses were analyzed via parametric modulators derived from the best-fitting Bayesian RL model.ResultsBehaviorally, causal attribution performance was comparable between BD and HC based on current evidence. Neuroimaging analyses revealed no evidence of striatal encoding of reward updating, despite the computational model effectively predicting behavioral performance. Instead, BD participants exhibited broader fronto-limbic engagement in response to agency-modulated signals, with increased activation in the medial frontal cortex, amygdala, and hippocampus. (See Table and Figure).Discussion & ConclusionsThese findings suggest that latent neural abnormalities may precede overt behavioral changes. Clinically, they underscore the importance of interventions that modulate underlying neural circuits and address maladaptive attributional styles through targeted interventions.

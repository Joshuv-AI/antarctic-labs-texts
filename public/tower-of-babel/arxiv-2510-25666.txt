7

\displaystyle\sigma(p(T_{1},\dots,T_{7}))=p(\sigma(T_{1},\dots,T_{7})).

[

[

W_{1}=\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}\tilde{U},W_{2}=\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}\tilde{U},\tilde{W}_{1}=\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}\tilde{U}~{\rm{and}}~\tilde{W}_{2}=\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{\Phi}_{2}}\tilde{U}.

The relations

(4.31)

By the same argument as in Theorem
4.6
, we establish that

\tilde{\Phi}_{1}(z)=B_{1}+B_{2}^{*}z,\tilde{\Phi}_{2}(z)=B_{2}+B_{1}^{*}z,\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(z)=C_{1}+C_{2}^{*}z~\text{ and }~\tilde{\Psi}_{2}(z)=C_{2}+C_{1}^{*}z

for all

H^{\infty}

2
for all

As

and
\tilde{W}_{2}

\displaystyle M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{2}}=M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{2}}M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}},M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}=M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}},M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}=M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}~\text{and}~M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}=M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}.

(4.32)

It follows from (
4.32
) that

\displaystyle(B_{1}+B_{2}^{*}z)(B_{2}+B_{1}^{*}z)=(B_{2}+B_{1}^{*}z)(B_{1}+B_{2}^{*}z),

(4.33)

\displaystyle(B_{1}+B_{2}^{*}z)(C_{1}+C_{2}^{*}z)=(C_{1}+C_{2}^{*}z)(B_{1}+B_{2}^{*}z),

\displaystyle(B_{1}+B_{2}^{*}z)(C_{2}+C_{1}^{*}z)=(C_{2}+C_{1}^{*}z)(B_{1}+B_{2}^{*}z),

\displaystyle(C_{1}+C_{2}^{*}z)(C_{2}+C_{1}^{*}z)=(C_{2}+C_{1}^{*}z)(C_{1}+C_{2}^{*}z),

for all
z\in\mathbb{T}.
Part
(3)
is derived from (
4.33
).

Conversely, suppose that
\textbf{W}=(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3},\tilde{W}_{1},\tilde{W}_{2})

5

to

\displaystyle M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{2}}=M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{2}}M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}},M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}=M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}},M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}=M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}M_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}~\text{and}~M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}=M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}M_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}

(4.34)

and

(4.35)

4.35

-unitary and so is
(\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{\Phi}_{1}}\tilde{U},\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}}\tilde{U},\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{E}}_{z}\tilde{U},\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{E}}_{\tilde{\Psi}_{2}}\tilde{U},\tilde{U}^{*}M^{\mathcal{F}}_{\tilde{\Phi}_{2}}\tilde{U}).

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\textbf{V}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{7})
be a
7
-tuple of commuting bounded operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

V
is
\Gamma_{E(3;3;1,1,1)}
-unitary.

(2)

\{(V_{1},V_{3}+\eta V_{5},\eta V_{7},V_{2}+\eta V_{4},\eta V_{6}):\eta\in\mathbb{T}\}
is a family of
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry and
\|V_{i}\|\leq 1
for
1\leq i\leq 6.

(3)

\{(V_{1},V_{5}+\eta V_{3},\eta V_{7},V_{4}+\eta V_{2},\eta V_{6}):\eta\in\mathbb{T}\}

\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry and
\|V_{i}\|\leq 1
for

(4)

\{(V_{2},V_{3}+\eta V_{6},\eta V_{7},V_{1}+\eta V_{4},\eta V_{5}):\eta\in\mathbb{T}\}
is a family of
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry and
\|V_{i}\|\leq 1

\{(V_{2},V_{6}+\eta V_{3},\eta V_{7},V_{4}+\eta V_{1},\eta V_{5}):\eta\in\mathbb{T}\}
is a family of
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry and
\|V_{i}\|\leq 1
for

\{V_{4},V_{5}+\eta V_{6},\eta V_{7},V_{1}+\eta V_{2},\eta V_{3}):\eta\in\mathbb{T}\}
is a family of
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry and
\|V_{i}\|\leq 1
for

(7)

\{(V_{4},V_{6}+\eta V_{5},\eta V_{7},V_{2}+\eta V_{1},\eta V_{3}):\eta\in\mathbb{T}\}
is a family of
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry and

,

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3},\tilde{W}_{1},\tilde{W}_{2})
be a commuting
5
-tuple of bounded operators on Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then
(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3},\tilde{W}_{1},\tilde{W}_{2})
is a
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry if and only if
\Big(W_{1},\frac{\tilde{W}_{1}}{2},\frac{W_{2}}{2},\frac{\tilde{W}_{1}}{2},\frac{W_{2}}{2},\tilde{W}_{2},W_{3}\Big)
is a
\Gamma_{E(3;3;1,1,1)}
-isometry.

Proof.

Suppose that
(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3},\tilde{W}_{1},\tilde{W}_{2})
is a
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry. By definition of
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-isometry, there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and a
\Gamma_{E(3;2;1,2)}
-unitary
\textbf{M}=(M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},\tilde{M}_{1},\tilde{M}_{2})
such that
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{K}_{1}
is a common invariant subspace of
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},\tilde{M}_{1}
and
\tilde{M}_{2}
and
W_{i}=M_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\tilde{W}_{j}=\tilde{M}_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}}
for
1\leq i\leq 3,1\leq j\leq 2.
It yields from Theorem
3.11
that
\Big(M_{1},\frac{\tilde{M}_{1}}{2},\frac{M_{2}}{2},\frac{\tilde{M}_{1}}{2},\frac{M_{2}}{2},\tilde{M}_{2},M_{3}\Big)
is a
\Gamma_{E(3;3;1,1,1)}
-unitary and hence
\Big(W_{1},\frac{\tilde{W}_{1}}{2},\frac{W_{2}}{2},\frac{\tilde{W}_{1}}{2},\frac{W_{2}}{2},\tilde{W}_{2},W_{3}\Big)

\Gamma_{E(3;3;1,1,1)}
-isometry.

Conversely, let
\Big(W_{1},\frac{\tilde{W}_{1}}{2},\frac{W_{2}}{2},\frac{\tilde{W}_{1}}{2},\frac{W_{2}}{2},\tilde{W}_{2},W_{3}\Big)
be a
\Gamma_{E(3;3;1,1,1)}

\mathcal{K}
and a
\Gamma_{E(3;3;1,1,1)}
-unitary
\Big(M_{1},\frac{\tilde{M}_{1}}{2},\frac{M_{2}}{2},\frac{\tilde{M}_{1}}{2},\frac{M_{2}}{2},\tilde{M}_{2},M_{3}\Big)

\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{K}

M_{3}

W_{i}=M_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}}

It follows from Theorem

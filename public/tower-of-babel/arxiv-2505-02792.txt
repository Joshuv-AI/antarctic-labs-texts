Rigidity via modular properties of theta functions
Indraneel Tambe

Abstract




In this paper we use methods of Liu

[
14
,
15
]

to show that the twisted Dirac operators
\mathscr{D}

on certain bundles
\Phi

considered by Guan and Wang

[
22
]

are rigid.
To do so,
we use a Lefschetz formula and Atiyah-Bott localization
to obtain formulas for the Lefschetz numbers
\mathscr{L}
of these operators
\mathscr{D}

in terms of Jacobi theta functions;
then, using the translational and modular transformation properties of theta functions and the properties of their zeros,
we prove
\mathscr{L}
is constant
provided certain conditions on characteristic classes hold,
thus showing the rigidity of
\mathscr{D}
on
\Phi
under these conditions.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a Lie group and let
P
be a
G
-equivariant Fredholm differential operator acting on the sections of a
G
-equivariant smooth vector bundle
W\rightarrow M
.
The kernel and cokernel of
P
are finite-dimensional representations of
G
,
and we obtain a well-defined function
L
on
G
given by
L(g):=\mathrm{trace}(g|_{\mathrm{ker}P})-\mathrm{trace}(g|_{\mathrm{coker}P})
.
This function
L
is called the

Lefschetz number
of
P
with respect to the
G
-action. We say the operator
P
is

rigid for the
G
-action
if its Lefschetz number
L
is constant as a function on
G
.

One application of rigidity is as follows:
if we can obtain an expression for the Lefschetz number
L
of
P

which does not depend on the choice of
G
-action on
M
and
W
,
and if we have proved
P
is rigid in general for such
G
-actions,
then showing the non-constancy of
L
for a particular
pair
(W\rightarrow M,P)

implies that
(W\rightarrow M,P)
in fact does not admit an equivariant
G
-action.

In
[
7
]
, Witten conjectured the rigidity of the twisted Dirac operator
\mathscr{D}
on a certain element of
K(M)[[q^{1/2}]]
for
M
a spin manifold. This
\mathscr{D}
is viewed as
an analog of
the classical Dirac operator
on the (free) loop space of
M
, and the rigidity of this
\mathscr{D}

is the loop-space analog of the classically known rigidity of the Dirac operator on a finite-dimensional spin manifold.

Witten’s rigidity conjectures were first proved in the work of
Hirzebruch
[
8
]
, Taubes
[
10
]
, Bott and Taubes
[
11
]
, and Krichever
[
12
]
. Next, in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
, Liu introduced new methods that simplified and generalized the proof. His approach uses the modularity properties of the Jacobi theta functions to circumvent the technical arguments used in
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
.
Subsequently, Dessai
[
16
]

extended the work to the spin
c
case,
Liu and Ma
[
17
,
18
]
explored extensions to families of operators.
Further studies on the odd-dimensional case
were conducted by
Liu and Wang
[
19
]
,
as well as Han and Yu
[
20
]
.

Let
M
be a closed even-dimensional spin manifold with an
S^{1}
-action and
V
a real even-rank
S^{1}
-equivariant spin vector bundle
on
M
.
Let
\Phi_{\lambda}
(actually an element of
K(M)[[q^{1/2}]]
, where
q^{1/2}=e^{\pi\mathbf{i}\tau}
where
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
is a complex variable in the upper half-plane),
\lambda=1,2,3
,
be one of the bundles considered in the paper
[
22
]
of Guan and Wang.
The bundle
\Phi_{\lambda}
is constructed
from
TM
and
V
using standard operations such as direct sums, tensor products,
and symmetric and exterior powers.
Let
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}
be the twisted Dirac operator on (the spinor vector bundle of)
\Phi_{\lambda}
.
The goal of this paper is to prove
the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

The operators
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda=1,2,3
,
are rigid if
the first
S^{1}
-equivariant Pontryagin class of
V

vanishes.

The proof of Theorem
1.1

is given in
Sections
5
and
6
,
and has the following outline.
Following the method of
[
15
]
, we find that
if we assume the first
S^{1}
-equivariant Pontryagin class of
V
vanishes,
under the modular action, the Lefschetz numbers

L_{\lambda}
for the
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}
actually transform into each other, up to possible multiplication by nowhere-vanishing holomorphic functions. Following
[
15
]
, this “modularity” property of the
L_{\lambda}
’s, together with an analysis of their periodicity properties and possible pole locations, is used to prove the constancy of the
L_{\lambda}
’s, hence the rigidity of the
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}
’s.

2.
Definitions and conventions

Convention 2.1
.

In this paper, unless otherwise stated, all manifolds are smooth, compact, connected, and without boundary, all vector bundles are smooth and of finite rank and are real except when explicitly declared to be complex, and all actions by Lie groups on manifolds are smooth.

Notation 2.2
.

•

We use
\mathbf{i}
to denote the imaginary unit, as opposed to
i
which is used for just indices and other variables. We denote the
i
th Chern class as
\mathbf{c}_{i}
, as opposed to
c_{i}
which will be used for Chern roots.

•

We denote by
\xi_{G}
the “
G
-equivariant version” of a given characteristic class
\xi
.

•

Let
\mathbf{H}:=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\mathrm{Im}(\tau)>0\}
.
We let
\tau\in\mathbf{H}
, and we define
q:=e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}\tau}

and
q^{r}:=e^{2\pi r\mathbf{i}\tau}
for any positive real number
r
.

•

A formal difference of vector bundles
V
and
W
will be denoted
V-W
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\pi:V\rightarrow M
be a vector bundle. A
G
-equivariant action on
V\rightarrow M
consists of:

•

a
G
-action
\tilde{\rho}:G\times V\rightarrow V
that linearly maps fibers to fibers, and

•

the quotient
G
-action
\rho:G\times M\rightarrow M
,
which satisfies
\pi\circ\tilde{\rho}=\rho\circ(\mathrm{id}_{G}\times\pi)
.

Let
V\rightarrow M
be a
G
-equivariant vector bundle.
Let
\Gamma(V)
denote the space of smooth sections of
V
and let
\mathscr{D}:\Gamma(V)\rightarrow\Gamma(V)
be a linear differential operator
which is
G
-equivariant.
Then the kernel and cokernel of
\mathscr{D}
are representations of
G
.
Suppose further that
\mathscr{D}
is also Fredholm (e.g. an elliptic operator), i.e., the kernel and cokernel of
\mathscr{D}
are finite-dimensional.

Definition 2.4
.

In the above situation, the
Lefschetz number
of
\mathscr{D}

is the function

L=L_{\mathscr{D}}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
given by

L_{\mathscr{D}}(g):=\mathrm{trace}(g|\mathrm{ker}(\mathscr{D}))-\mathrm{trace}(g|\mathrm{coker}(\mathscr{D})),

where we use the convention that the codomain
\mathbb{F}
of
L_{\mathscr{D}}
is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
according to whether
V
is real or complex.

Remark 2.5
.

Later,
instead of a single vector bundle
V
on
M
with a differential operator
\mathscr{D}
, we will consider a formal power series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{(n)}q^{n}
where
q
is a formal variable and
each
V_{(n)}
is itself an
S^{1}
-equivariant vector bundle
with an
S^{1}
-equivariant linear Fredholm differential operator
\mathscr{D}_{(n)}
.
In this case, the “Lefschetz number”
L
of the collection
(\mathscr{D}_{(n)})_{n=0,1,2,\ldots}

is the function
L:G\rightarrow\mathbb{F}[[q]]

given by
L(g)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}(g)q^{n}

where
L_{n}
is the Lefschetz number of
\mathscr{D}_{(n)}
for each
n\geq 0
.
Again
\mathbb{F}
is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
according to whether the
V_{n}
’s are real or complex.

Definition 2.6
.

The operator
\mathscr{D}
is
G
-
rigid

if the Lefschetz number
L
of
\mathscr{D}
is constant on
G
, which is the case
if and only if for every
g\in G

we have

L(g)=L(1_{G})=\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{ker}(\mathscr{D})-\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{coker}(\mathscr{D}).

We note the RHS is just the ordinary Fredholm index of
\mathscr{D}
.

Remark 2.7

(Lefschetz number is well-defined on
K
-group)
.

Given
G
-equivariant vector bundles
A,B,A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
on
M
with respective
G
-equivariant differential operators
\mathscr{D}_{A},\mathscr{D}_{B},\mathscr{D}_{A^{\prime}},\mathscr{D}_{B^{\prime}}
, if there is an isomorphism

A\oplus B^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\simeq}A^{\prime}\oplus B
of
G
-equivariant bundles that also identifies
\mathscr{D}_{A}\oplus\mathscr{D}_{B^{\prime}}
with
\mathscr{D}_{A^{\prime}}\oplus\mathscr{D}_{B}
,
then we have
\mathscr{L}_{A}+\mathscr{L}_{B^{\prime}}=\mathscr{L}_{A^{\prime}}+\mathscr{L}_{B}
for the corresponding Lefschetz numbers,
so that
\mathscr{L}_{A}-\mathscr{L}_{B}=\mathscr{L}_{A^{\prime}}-\mathscr{L}_{B^{\prime}}
.

Therefore, the Lefschetz number of a formal difference

A-B

of “concrete”
G
-equivariant vector bundles
A,B
both equipped with
G
-equivariant linear differential operators,
is viewed as just the difference of their Lefschetz numbers,
\mathscr{L}_{A}-\mathscr{L}_{B}
.

3.
Reduction to the case of
G=S^{1}

Our aim in this paper is to prove the rigidity of twisted Dirac operators
\mathscr{D}
on certain bundles,
when equivariant for an action by any Lie group
G
that is compact and connected.

The goal of this section is to reduce to the case of
G=S^{1}
.
First we need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

The Lefschetz number
L:G\rightarrow\mathbb{F}

of a
G
-equivariant Fredholm operator
\mathscr{D}
on a
G
-equivariant vector bundle
V\rightarrow M

is continuous as a function on
G
.

Proof.

Since the trace is continuous on the space of endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space (over
\mathbb{F}
),
it suffices to show
the action homomorphism
G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbb{F}}(A)

is continuous
when
A
is either
\mathrm{ker}(\mathscr{D})
or
\mathrm{coker}(\mathscr{D})
.

Let
C(M)
denote the space of all continuous
\mathbb{F}
-valued functions on
M
. We give
C(M)
the compact-open topology.

In general we will show the action homomorphism
G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbb{F}}(A)
is continuous for
A
any
\mathbb{F}
-linear finite-dimensional subspace of
C(M)
which is stable under the
G
-action.

If
G
acts continuously on
M
, then also
G
acts continuously on
C(M)
with the compact-open topology.
So, if
\mathscr{F}
denotes the space of all continuous
\mathbb{F}
-linear automorphisms of
C(M)
, with
\mathscr{F}
itself having the compact-open topology
(inherited from the space of all continuous maps
C(M)\rightarrow C(M)
),
we have a continuous action homomorphism
G\rightarrow\mathscr{F}
.

Let
A\subset C(M)
be a given
G
-stable finite-dimensional linear subspace.
Let
\mathscr{F}_{A}\subset\mathscr{F}
be the set
of all
f\in\mathscr{F}
such that
f
maps
A
linearly onto itself.
Now the action homomorphism
G\rightarrow\mathscr{F}_{A}
with codomain restricted to
\mathscr{F}_{A}

is still continuous, with
\mathscr{F}_{A}
having the subspace topology from
\mathscr{F}
.

Next,
\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbb{F}}(A)

can be identified topologically with the quotient of
\mathscr{F}_{A}

by the relation considering two functions
h,k\in\mathscr{F}_{A}

equivalent if
h,k
agree on
A\subset C(M)
.

Now the composition

G\rightarrow\mathscr{F}_{A}\xrightarrow{\text{quotient}}\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbb{F}}(A)

is continuous and agrees with the action homomorphism
G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbb{F}}(A)
. Thus
G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbb{F}}(A)
is continuous.
∎

We also recall the following facts about
Lie groups:

Fact 3.2
.

Let
T=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Let
U\subset T

be the set of elements
h\in T

such that there exists a closed Lie subgroup
H\subset T

with
h\in H

and such that
H\cong S^{1}
as a Lie group.
Then
U
is dense in
T
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be any compact connected Lie group.
Let
U\subset G

be the set of elements
h\in G

such that there exists a closed Lie subgroup
H\subset G

with
h\in H

and such that
H\cong S^{1}
as a Lie group.
Then
U
is dense in
G
.

Proof.

Let
O\subset G
be any nonempty open subset.
We aim to show
O\cap U\neq\emptyset
.

Pick any
g\in O
.
We know (see for example Corollary 4.46 of
[
4
]
) there
exists a maximal torus
T\subset G

such that
g\in T
.
By Fact
3.2
,
there exists an
h\in O\cap T

such that
h
is contained in some closed Lie subgroup
H\subset T
with
H
isomorphic to
S^{1}
. But now
h\in U
, by definition of
U
.

Hence
h\in O\cap U
, proving
U\cap O\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Now we prove the main result of this section:

Lemma 3.4
.

If an operator
\mathscr{D}
is rigid with respect to any
S^{1}
-action,
then it is rigid with respect to the action of any compact connected Lie group.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathscr{D}
is an operator on some pair
(W\rightarrow M,P)
where
P
is a differential operator on a vector bundle
W\rightarrow M
, and assume
\mathscr{D}
is rigid with respect to all
S^{1}
-actions.
Let
G
be a compact connected Lie group with a given equivariant action on
(W\rightarrow M,P)
.
Let
L=L_{\mathscr{D}}
be the Lefschetz number of
\mathscr{D}
with respect to the
G
-action.
We aim to show
\mathscr{D}
is rigid, i.e. that
L
is constant,
or equivalently that
L(g)=L(1_{G})
for any
g\in G
.

We know
L
is continuous on
G
by Lemma
3.1
.
Therefore, it suffices to show that
L
takes the value
L(1_{G})

on a dense subset of
G
.

Let
U\subset G
be the subset consisting of all
g\in G

such that there exists a Lie subgroup
H\subset G

such that
H\cong S^{1}
and
g\in H
.
Since
G
is compact and connected, by Lemma
3.3
we know
U
is dense in
G
.

We now show for any given
g\in U
,
L(g)=L(1_{G})
. Given
g\in U
, find
H\subset G
an isomorphic copy of
S^{1}
containing
g
. By hypothesis,
\mathscr{D}
is rigid for the
H
-action, so it follows that
L(g)=L(1_{H})=L(1_{G})
, as was to be shown.
∎

Therefore, in what follows, we will consider only actions by the circle group
G=S^{1}
.

4.
Tools for calculating Lefschetz numbers

Let
V
be an oriented rank
2k
real
S^{1}
-equivariant vector bundle on a manifold
M
, where the
S^{1}
-action on
M
is trivial.
Assume
M
is connected.
Then there exist
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}

such that for every
p\in M
, the fiber
V_{p}
of
V
at
p

decomposes as a sum of real 2-planes
V_{p}=L_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{k}

where each
L_{i}

is
S^{1}
-stable with weight
m_{i}
for the
S^{1}
-action.

Definition 4.1
.

The integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}

above
are called the
weights

of the
S^{1}
-action on
V
.

Let
w\in H^{2}_{S^{1}}(M)
denote the “universal element”
given by the pullback of
\widetilde{w}\in H^{2}_{S^{1}}(\{\text{pt}\})
along the map
M\rightarrow\{\text{pt}\}
,
where
\widetilde{w}\in H^{2}_{S^{1}}(\{\text{pt}\})\cong H^{2}(BS^{1})

is the “universal first Chern class.”

Lemma 4.2
.

The formal
S^{1}
-equivariant Chern roots of
the complexification

V_{\mathbb{C}}
of
V
are

\left\{\pm(\xi_{i}+m_{i}w)\right\}_{\pm;i=1,\ldots,k}

with
\left\{\pm\xi_{i}\right\}_{\pm;i=1,\ldots,k}
the formal (non-equivariant) Chern roots
of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
.

The
\hat{A}
-class
\hat{\mathscr{A}}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
, the Chern character
\mathrm{ch}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
, the Euler class
E(V)
, and the total Pontryagin class
p(V)
admit (see e.g.
[
3
]
)
the following expressions in terms of the formal Chern roots
\pm\xi_{1},\ldots,\pm\xi_{k}
of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
:

(4.3)

\begin{matrix}\hat{\mathscr{A}}(V)=\prod_{i=1,\ldots,k}[f(\xi_{i})f(-\xi_{i})]=\prod_{i=1,\ldots,k}g(\xi_{i}),&\mathrm{ch}(V_{\mathbb{C}})=\sum_{i=1,\ldots,k}2\cosh(\xi_{i}),\\
E(V)=\prod_{i=1,\ldots,k}\xi_{i},&p(V)=\prod_{i=1,\ldots,k}(1+\xi_{i}^{2}),\end{matrix}

where
f(z),g(z)
are given by the Maclaurin series of the functions

\begin{matrix}f(z)=\sqrt{g(z)},&g(z)=\frac{z/2}{\mathrm{sinh}(z/2)}.\end{matrix}

The
S^{1}
-equivariant versions
of the above characteristic classes
are obtained
by replacing the formal Chern roots
\pm\xi_{i}
in the above expressions
by the corresponding formal
S^{1}
-equivariant Chern roots. Hence:

Lemma 4.4
.

In the notation of Lemma
4.2
,
the
S^{1}
-equivariant versions of the characteristic classes
in Equation
4.3

are

\begin{matrix}\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{S^{1}}(V)=\prod_{i=1,\ldots,k}g(\xi_{i}+m_{i}w),&\mathrm{ch}_{S^{1}}(V_{\mathbb{C}})=\sum_{i=1,\ldots,k}2\cosh(\xi_{i}+m_{i}w),\\
E_{S^{1}}(V)=\prod_{i=1,\ldots,k}(\xi_{i}+m_{i}w),&p_{S^{1}}(V)=\prod_{i=1,\ldots,k}(1+(\xi_{i}+m_{i}w)^{2}),\end{matrix}

where as before,
g(z)=(z/2)/\sinh(z/2)
, and
w\in H^{2}_{S^{1}}(M)
is the universal element.

Let
\mathscr{D}
be the Dirac operator on a spin manifold
M
equipped with an
S^{1}
-action, now no longer necessarily the trivial action.
Let
V
be an
S^{1}
-equivariant spin vector bundle on
M
.
Let
L:S^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the Lefschetz number for the “twisted Dirac operator”
\mathscr{D}\otimes V
on (the spinor vector bundle of)
V
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
g\in S^{1}
sufficiently near the identity we have

L_{\mathscr{D}}(g)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}\int_{M_{\alpha}}\left.\left(\frac{\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{S^{1}}(i_{\alpha}^{*}TM)\,\mathrm{ch}_{S^{1}}(i_{\alpha}^{*}V_{\mathbb{C}})}{E_{S^{1}}(\nu_{\alpha})}\right)\right|_{w=2\pi\mathbf{i}t_{g}}

where:

•

\{M_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}}
lists the connected components of the fixed locus (set of fixed points) of the
S^{1}
-action, and for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}
,

\nu_{\alpha}
is the normal bundle of
M_{\alpha}
in
M
,
and
i_{\alpha}:M_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow M
is the inclusion;

•

t_{g}\in\mathbb{R}
is sufficiently near 0
such that
e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}t_{g}}=g
,

g\in S^{1}
;

•

w
is the canonical universal element of
H^{2}_{S^{1}}(M)
;
and

•

the notation
(\cdots)|_{w=2\pi\mathbf{i}t_{g}}
means that we take the expression
(\cdots)
and evaluate
w
as
2\pi\mathbf{i}t_{g}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the Atiyah-Singer-Lefschetz formula
(see e.g. Section 2 of
[
3
]
)
and Bott localization.
∎

We note that the fixed-point set of the
S^{1}
-action
on
M
is a smooth submanifold of
M
,
since a smooth proper action by a Lie group
G
on a manifold
M
always has fixed-point set

a smooth submanifold of

, and

is compact and acting continuously on a Hausdorff space

Remark 4.6
.

We will use the following infinite product expressions for the Jacobi theta functions,
which can be found

\theta(v,\tau)=c\cdot q^{1/8}\cdot 2\sin(\pi v)\cdot\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}\left[(1-q^{r}e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}v})(1-q^{r}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}v})\right],

Let
W
be a possibly virtual, arbitrary complex vector bundle of complex rank
k
, on a base space
M
, with formal Chern roots
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}
.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
K(M)[[t]]
be the ring of formal power series in a variable
t
with
K(M)

We make the standard definitions
S_{t}W:=\oplus_{r=0}^{\infty}t^{r}S^{r}W
and
\Lambda_{t}W:=\oplus_{r=0}^{\infty}t^{r}\Lambda^{r}W
,
where
S^{r},\Lambda^{r}

denote the

These may be viewed as elements of
K(M)[[t]]
.

We recall the following well-known formulas for Chern characters (see e.g.
[
22
]
).
Analogous formulas hold for the below equivariant Chern characters
when
W
is a complexification of a real even-rank bundle with fiberwise
S^{1}
-action
by replacing the non-equivariant Chern roots by the equivariant ones.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}
are the formal Chern roots of
W

6.1.

We will reference the following properties of theta-functions, which can be found in e.g. Chapter V of

(6.1)

\begin{matrix}\theta(v+1,\tau)=-\theta(v,\tau),&\theta(v+\tau,\tau)=-q^{-1/2}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}v}\theta(v,\tau),\\

By applying the formulas in the 2nd column repeatedly, we obtain the following formulas which are valid for any integer

(6.2)

\begin{matrix}\theta(v+k\tau,\tau)=(-1)^{k}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}k(v+k\tau/2)}\theta(v,\tau),\\

\theta_{2}(v+k\tau,\tau)=(-1)^{k}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}k(v+k\tau/2)}\theta_{2}(v,\tau),\\
\theta_{3}(v+k\tau,\tau)=e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}k(v+k\tau/2)}\theta_{3}(v,\tau).\end{matrix}

The method to prove Equation

for e.g.
\theta
for

\displaystyle\theta(v+k\tau,\tau)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}q^{-k/2}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}(v+r\tau)}\theta(v,\tau)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}q^{-k/2}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}(kv+k(k-1)\tau/2)}\theta(v,\tau)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}(kv+k(k-1)\tau/2)+(-k\pi\mathbf{i}\tau)}\theta(v,\tau)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}(kv+k^{2}\tau/2)}\theta(v,\tau),

and so, with

\displaystyle\theta(v,\tau)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}q^{-k/2}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}\sum_{r=1}^{k}(v-r\tau)}\theta(v-k\tau,\tau)

and similarly for

(1):

\mathscr{L}_{2}
to a constant multiple of
\mathscr{L}_{3}
,

•

and takes
\mathscr{L}_{3}
to a constant multiple of
\mathscr{L}_{2}
.

Proof.

This is seen directly from the expressions for the
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}
’s
in Lemma
5.6
,
and the modular transformation properties of the theta functions
described in Equation

6.4
.

We note for example that the expression for the result of the
S
-action looks like

\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}(S(t,\tau))=\left(\text{some element of }\mathcal{F}\right)\sum_{\alpha}\int_{M_{\alpha}}e^{(i_{\alpha}^{*}(p_{1}(V)_{S^{1}}))|_{w=t}/(4\pi\mathbf{i})}(\cdots)

where
(\cdots)
represents the expression inside the
\int_{M_{\alpha}}
integral in the formula we found for
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
in Lemma
5.6
,
where
\lambda^{\prime}=2,1,3
respectively if
\lambda=1,2,3
.
Thus if
p_{1}(V)_{S^{1}}=0

then
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}(S(t,\tau))=\left(\text{some element of }\mathcal{F}\right)\mathscr{L}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(t,\tau)
.

We note that the
S
and
T
actions interchange the terms in the
\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}
sum in Lemma
5.6
, but the overall sum is preserved, possibly up to a factor of an element of
\mathcal{F}
.
Also, the extra factor arising in the
T
action on
\theta_{1}
cancels in the numerator and denominator in the
\mu=1
term of the
\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}
summation.
∎

6.3.
Holomorphicity of
\mathscr{L}(t,\tau)
at real values of
t

For simplicity, we assume the fixed locus of the
S^{1}
action on
M
has only finitely many connected components.

The following lemma is based on the ideas of Lemma 1.3 of
[
15
]
and the Lemma on p. 180 of
[
13
]
:

Lemma 6.6
.

For
\lambda=1,2,3
, the function
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}(t,\tau)

is holomorphic in
t
at least at real values of
t
(for any
\tau
).

Proof.

Step 1:
First let us expand
\Phi

as a power series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{n}q^{n/2}
,
so that each
\Phi_{n}

is itself a (virtual) finite-rank bundle, equivariant for the
S^{1}
-action
and carrying a twisted Dirac operator
\mathscr{D}_{n}=\mathscr{D}\otimes\Phi_{n}
on the spinor bundle of
\Phi_{n}
, where

\mathscr{D}
is the Dirac operator on
M
.

We then have

\mathscr{L}(t,\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(t)q^{n/2}

where
b_{n}(t)
is the Lefschetz number for
\mathscr{D}_{n}
on
\Phi_{n}
.

Next, since each
\Phi_{n}
is just a finite-rank bundle with elliptic operator

\mathscr{D}_{n}
,
we see the Lefschetz number
b_{n}(t)

is just a linear combination of characters of finite-dimensional irreducible
S^{1}
representations.
Since all such characters of
S^{1}
are just monomials in
z
where
z\in S^{1}
and
S^{1}
is viewed as the complex unit circle,
this tells us that
b_{n}(t)

is just a finite Laurent polynomial in
z,z^{-1}
where
z=e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}t}
.
In particular, the only possible singularities of
b_{n}

occur at
z=0
.

Step 2:
Let us view
b_{n}
as functions of
z=e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}t}
instead of
t
.
Now, to show the holomorphicity of
\mathscr{L}(z,q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(z)q^{n/2}

in
t
at real
t
,
it suffices to show the holomorphicity in
z

for
z\in S^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
.

By the lemma on p. 180 (in Appendix III) of
[
13
]
,
since (from Step 1)
each
b_{n}(z)
already has no singularities on the unit circle,
it now suffices to show for each fixed
\tau_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
that

\mathscr{L}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(z)q_{0}^{n/2}

(where
q_{0}^{1/2}=e^{\pi\mathbf{i}\tau_{0}}
)
has at most isolated singularities for
z
on the unit circle
S^{1}
.

Indeed (with the simplifying assumption that there are only finitely many components
M_{\alpha}
of the fixed locus
M^{S^{1}}
),
the function
\mathscr{L}

is a finite sum over
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}
of
an integral over
M_{\alpha}
of a ratio of (finite) polynomials
in theta functions with arguments of the form
(c+nt,\tau)

for some integer
n
(and cohomology class
c
).
Therefore any possible singularities of
\mathscr{L}

must be at the zeros of some polynomial in the
holomorphic functions
\theta_{\nu}(c+nt,\tau)
, but
necessarily the set of such zeros is discrete.
∎

6.4.
Proof of rigidity of
\mathscr{D}
on
\Phi_{\lambda}

The following lemma is analogous to the discussion at the end of Section 1 of
[
15
]
:

Lemma 6.7
.

Assume the
S^{1}
-action on
M
has only
isolated
fixed points.
Let
\lambda\in\{1,2,3\}
.
If
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}(t,\tau)
has a singularity at some
(t,\tau)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbf{H}
,
then there is some
\lambda^{\prime}\in\{1,2,3\}

such that
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(t,\tau)
has a singularity at a
real
value of
t
(for some
\tau\in\mathbf{H}
).

Proof.

First, since we are assuming the action has isolated fixed points,
it follows that in the expression for
\mathscr{L}
in Lemma 5.6,
all the
c_{\alpha,i}
and
b_{\alpha,j}
are just 0,
and we can drop the integral over
M_{\alpha}
(since now each
M_{\alpha}
is just a single point).

Suppose for some
\tau_{0}\in\mathbf{H}
that
\mathscr{L}(t,\tau_{0})

has a singularity in
t
at some location
t_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
.
From the expressions for
\mathscr{L}
in the isolated-fixed-point case,
we see this can only occur at some zero of
\theta_{\nu}(mt,\tau)
for some integer
m
and some
\nu\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.

According to the discussion on p. 66 of
[
5
]
,
every zero of
\theta_{\nu}(v,\tau)
occurs at a location where
v\in\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z}
.

Thus we see a pole of
\mathscr{L}(t,\tau)
can only occur at a location where
t\in\mathbb{Q}+\tau\mathbb{Q}
.

Now suppose
\mathscr{L}
has a singularity at some location
(t=t_{0},\tau=\tau_{0})

where
t_{0}=\frac{j}{k}(c\tau_{0}+d)
, where
j,k,c,d
are integers with
k\neq 0
and
c,d
are coprime (which can be achieved by “pulling out” all their common integer factors and “absorbing” these into
j
).
Since
c,d
are coprime we can find integers
a,d
with
ad-bc=1
.
Let
g:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Then

g\cdot(t_{0},\tau_{0})=(\frac{t_{0}}{c\tau_{0}+d},\frac{a\tau_{0}+b}{c\tau_{0}+d})=(\frac{j}{k},\frac{a\tau_{0}+b}{c\tau_{0}+d})
;
this implies
\mathscr{L}(g\cdot(t,\tau))
has a singularity at
t=\frac{j}{k}\in\mathbb{R}
(for some
\tau=c\tau_{0}+d
).

But by Lemma
6.5
we know
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}(g\cdot(t,\tau))
is
just
f(t,\tau)\mathscr{L}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(t,\tau)

for some nowhere-vanishing function
f
, and some
\lambda^{\prime}\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.
∎

We are finally in a position to prove Theorem
1.1

which we state in a slightly more precise form:

Theorem 6.8
.

Suppose
p_{1}(V)_{S^{1}}=0
.
Suppose also the
S^{1}
-action on
M
has only isolated fixed points.
Then, for each
\lambda=1,2,3
,
the function
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}(t,\tau)

is constant in
t
, for each fixed
\tau\in\mathbf{H}
.

Proof.

We already know
\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}(t,\tau)

is meromorphic,
and we showed in Lemma
6.3

that
\mathscr{L}
is doubly-periodic in
t
.
Since a standard theorem from complex analysis says
a holomorphic doubly-periodic function on the complex plane is constant,
it only remains to show that
\mathscr{L}
has no poles.

Indeed, if
\mathscr{L}

has a pole, then by Lemma
6.7
,

\mathscr{L}
has a pole at some real value of
t
(for some
\tau
);
but this contradicts Lemma
6.6
which states that
\mathscr{L}
has no poles at real values of
t
(for any
\tau
).
Thus the theorem is proved.
∎

Myosin inhibitor therapy in Fabry cardiomyopathy with dynamic left ventricular outflow obstruction
Authors: Kolja Lau, Nils Petri, Peter Nordbeck
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 56-year-old woman with presumed hypertrophic cardiomyopathy was diagnosed with Fabry disease (GLA: p.Glu358del) and a concomitant PLN variant. Imaging revealed advanced Fabry cardiomyopathy with dynamic LVOT obstruction. Despite optimized medical and enzyme replacement therapy, symptoms progressed (NYHA-class III) with a marked increase in LVOT gradient. Mavacamten was initiated and uptitrated under echocardiographic monitoring. At three months, LVOT gradient significantly decreased and functional status improved (NYHA-class I), while systolic function remained preserved. This case highlights the importance of detailed phenotyping and suggests that myosin inhibition may represent a therapeutic option for LVOT obstruction beyond classical sarcomeric hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.Keywords: Fabry disease, Myosin inhibitor, Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, Left ventricular outflow obstruction

A 56-year-old woman previously diagnosed with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) was referred for advanced evaluation. Genetic testing identified a pathogenic GLA-variant (GLA: p.Glu358del), confirming Fabry disease (FD), and a rare phospholamban-variant (PLN: p.Leu39*). Multisystem involvement of FD was supported by reduced leukocyte α-galactosidase-A activity (0.27 nmol/min/mg protein; reference: 0.4–1.0), elevated lyso-Gb3 plasma concentration (7.9 ng/ml; reference: <0.4), chronic kidney disease (KDIGO G3aA1), anhidrosis, biopsy-confirmed small-fiber neuropathy and cornea verticillata. Enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) had been administered since 2022. Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated concentric left ventricular (LV) hypertrophy with basal inferolateral late gadolinium enhancement (LGE), characteristic of advanced FD cardiomyopathy (Fig. 1, Panel B) [1]. Cine MRI additionally suggested prominent and elongated papillary muscles with relatively short chordal interposition, without clear elongation or redundancy of the mitral leaflets or chordal apparatus, indicating a possible atypical subvalvular contribution to the dynamic obstruction. Echocardiography showed preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), reduced basal lateral strain and systolic anterior motion with an LV outflow tract (LVOT) gradient (20 mmHg at rest; 66 mmHg during Valsalva, Fig. 1, Panel C; Supplementary Video 1). The patient's functional capacity was preserved (NYHA-class I).

In 2025, the patient presented again with marked clinical deterioration and worsening functional capacity (NYHA-class III). LVOT gradient now increased to 157 mmHg during Valsalva (Fig. 1, Panel D). Pharmacological therapy was established with bisoprolol 2.5 mg twice daily, ERT, empagliflozin 10 mg daily, spironolactone 25 mg twice daily and furosemide 20 mg as needed. Betablocker therapy could not be increased and calcium antagonists were avoided because of known bradycardia (55 beats/min). Alcohol septal ablation (ASA) was discussed. In six men with FD-related LVOT obstruction (LVOTO) treated with relatively low alcohol volumes (median: 1.5 ml; range: 1.2–2.0 ml), durable benefit was reported without high-grade atrioventricular block or permanent pacemaker implantation [2]. In a general multinational cohort of 1814 patients with obstructive HCM, however 170 (9.4%) required permanent pacemaker implantation within 30 days after ASA because of high-grade atrioventricular block [3]. Similarly, Batzner et al. reported a permanent pacemaker implantation rate of 10.5% after ASA [4]. These data illustrate the general procedure-related risk, which was discussed with the patient. After balanced shared decision-making the patient declined ASA. Mavacamten was therefore initiated at 2.5 mg daily and uptitrated to 10 mg under serial echocardiographic monitoring (Fig. 1, Panel A, Supplementary Table 1). During therapy initiation concomitant medication was not changed. ASA or surgical myectomy remained options if the response to mavacamten proved insufficient or non-durable.

At 3-months follow-up, the provoked LVOT gradient decreased to 35 mmHg (Fig. 1, Panel D), accompanied by substantial symptomatic improvement (NYHA-class I). Overall systolic LV function remained stable (LVEF: 56% to 57%; global longitudinal strain: −17.1% to −16%). Septal strain decreased from −14% to −8% potentially reflecting regional negative inotropy but possibly also indicating emerging systolic impairment (Fig. 1, Panel E). Therefore, longitudinal surveillance of systolic LV function is warranted. Provoked LVOT gradients are sensitive to hydration and changes in preload, afterload and contractility. Variations in these factors may have also contributed to the observed reduction, limiting both causal attribution and generalizability.

LVOTO is rarely reported in FD and robust prevalence data are lacking [1], [2]. Typical LGE pattern supports substantial cardiac involvement of FD but does not establish it as the only cause of LVOTO. PLN p.Leu39* is a loss-of-function variant associated with hypertrophic and dilated phenotypes: heterozygous carriers may develop hypertrophy, whereas homozygous carriers have developed severe dilated cardiomyopathy [5]. Subsequent reports broadened the heterozygous phenotype to rare HCM, sometimes with end-stage disease [6], [7]. LVOTO was not reported as a defining feature, although a recent single case described PLN p.Leu39*-related obstructive HCM responsive to mavacamten [8]. Both variants may therefore have contributed to LVOTO.

This short-term observation does not establish the efficacy or safety of mavacamten in FD-cardiomyopathy. However, it suggests that a closely monitored trial with myosin-inhibitors may be considered in carefully selected FD-patients with severe LVOTO after medical and septal reduction strategies have been evaluated. The relative contributions of FD and PLN p.Leu39* remain unresolved.

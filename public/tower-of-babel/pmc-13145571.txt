ABSTRACT NUMBER: ESOC2026A1470 ETHNIC DIFFERENCES IN VASCULAR RISK FACTORS AND ISCHEMIC-HEART DISEASES IN TIA/STROKE PATIENTS IN THE UNITED-KINGDOM: PROSPECTIVE POPULATION-BASED STUDY OF THE “CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE PARADOX”
Authors: Joseph Kamtchum-Tatuene, Linxin Li, Peter Rothwell
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsIn the USA, coronary artery disease (CAD) is paradoxically less prevalent in ethnic minorities versus Whites despite higher prevalence of vascular risk factors. As this is often considered an artifact due to US-specific healthcare access disparities, we aimed to determine if such a paradox persists in the UK population with equitable access to care.MethodsWe collected data on vascular risk factors and history of CAD in all patients with TIA/stroke in a UK population-based study (2002-2023). We used multivariable logistic regression to compare rates of prevalent CAD in Asians and Blacks versus Whites, adjusting for age, sex, vascular risk factors, and socioeconomic deprivation. We verified our findings in a meta-analysis of published studies across the UK.ResultsAmong 7297 TIA/stroke patients (47.4% women, 71.0 ± 15.5 years, 335 non-Whites), 117 Blacks had more diabetes than Whites (adjusted-OR = 3.16, 95%CI = 2.03-4.92, P < 0.001), but less CAD (0.50,0.18-1.41,P = 0.06). Asians also had more diabetes than Whites (3.93,2.77-5.57,P < 0.001) but no excess CAD (1.18,0.67-2.10,P = 0.57). Among other UK-based studies of stroke patients, Blacks also had less CAD than Whites (pooled OR = 0.48, 0.31-0.76; P = 0.002,n = 3) and Asians (0.30, 0.10-0.80, P = 0.015,n = 2) despite higher prevalence of hypertension (1.95,1.47-2.60, P < 0.001,n = 3; 1.47,1.02-2.12, P < 0.001,n = 6) and diabetes (2.78,2.40-3.22,P < 0.001,n = 3; 4.15,3.11-5.53, P < 0.001,n = 6). In stroke-free UK populations (n = 3), CAD rates were lower in Blacks versus Whites (0.25,0.13-0.46, P < 0.001) or Asians (0.40,0.30-0.50,P < 0.001).ConclusionsA coronary artery disease paradox exists in Blacks living in the UK, which is unlikely to solely reflect biases in healthcare access and suggests different biological susceptibilities.Conflict of interestFunding: Joseph Kamtchum-Tatuene is funded by a Wellcome Early Career Award (304532/Z/23/Z).

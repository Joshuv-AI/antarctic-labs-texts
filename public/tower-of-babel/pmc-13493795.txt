Transportation 4.0 planning in emergency medical services considering real-time ambulance two-phase assignment-routing and mission change
Authors: Saba Azarnoush, Arash Nemati
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTransportation 4.0 enables smart planning in urban and interurban transportation networks by leveraging intelligent technologies, such as Internet of Things (IoT), cloud computing, information system integration, and artificial intelligence, to facilitate real-time vehicle assignment and routing. Nevertheless, Transportation 4.0 planning involving vehicle smart assignment and routing, particularly in healthcare logistics, has been less addressed in the literature. However, this study takes an initial step toward intelligent planning of emergency medical services by proposing an Intelligent Emergency Information System (IEIS) for real-time ambulance assignment and routing. As part of data analytics in the designed IEIS, an integer linear mathematical model is proposed to optimize total travel times in the ambulance assignment and routing problem based on deterministic real-time input data in two phases: reaching the patients and transporting them to hospitals. The proposed mathematical model is solved in a case study of Amol city, Iran, incorporating 25 street nodes, nine ambulance nodes, six call nodes, and five hospital nodes, using the CPLEX solver. Although designing and implementing the suggested conceptual model of IEIS was not applicable in the case study, to evaluate the impact of real-time parameter updates, two scenarios involving real-time inclusion of a new call node and an ambulance breakdown are examined. Accordingly, the model was re-solved, producing updated ambulance assignments and routing in each scenario, even by rearranging the ambulances’ missions. Results validated the flexibility of the proposed mathematical model in optimizing two-phase ambulance assignment and routing under real-time input updates.Keywords: Transportation 4.0, Healthcare logistics smart planning, Real-time vehicle assignment-routing, Intelligent emergency medical services, Two-phase ambulance assignment-routing, Ambulance mission changeSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Industry 4.0 (I4.0) refers to integrating the emerging digital technologies, including the Internet of Things (IoT), cloud computing, cybersecurity, big data analytics, simulation, Artificial Intelligence (AI), 3D printing, robotics, Augmented/Virtual Reality (A/VR), and simulation in industries1–10. The application of I4.0 pillars in the transportation system of logistics, such as urban transportation, healthcare logistics, and offshore shipping, results in Transportation 4.0 (T4.0), enabling real-time monitoring and control. Real-time IoT-based driver monitoring, cyber-physical-based vehicle tracking, sensor-based road condition predicting, vehicle routing, decision-making on reaction to disruptions, big data analytics-based parameters forecasting, integrated transportation information system, cloud-based data storage, and blockchain-based cybersecurity are examples of emerging technologies applications in T4.0 11–16.

Intelligent Transportation System (ITS), as one of the core components of a smart city, integrates IoT, connected vehicles, 5G communications, and real-time data analytics to enhance traffic management and improve safety. By reducing congestion and pollution while increasing the efficiency of public transportation, ITS contributes to urban sustainability and enhances overall citizen satisfaction. Beyond mitigating traffic and environmental impacts, it also improves energy efficiency and enables faster responses to accidents and emergencies17–21. Transportation 4.0 takes this evolution further toward a more connected, intelligent, and integrated mobility ecosystem. It leverages wireless communication, IoT, and artificial intelligence to enable real-time interaction between vehicles, infrastructure, and control systems. This interconnected ecosystem supports dynamic traffic management, predictive routing, and rapid incident response, contributing to safer and more resilient urban mobility21–24.

The ambulance assignment and routing approaches in the traditional Emergency Medical Services (EMS) rely on static data and fail to capture the dynamic nature of real-world conditions25–29. However, dynamic factors, such as traffic congestion, road conditions, accidents, ambulance breakdowns, hospital capacity complement, and fluctuating demand, can affect service quality, rendering static decision-making approaches ineffective. Hence, Transportation 4.0 can play a crucial role in enhancing the efficiency of EMSs. An intelligent EMS can reduce response times through fast and accurate exchange and processing of real-time data on traffic, ambulances’ location and status, call’s location, hospitals’ reception capacity, and patients’ status. As a result, decision-making and resource assignment evolve from static processes into a dynamic, real-time framework that significantly improves the efficiency and reliability of emergency response operations30–38.

Although ambulance assignment and routing have been investigated in the literature, developing an intelligent emergency information system, integrating the EMS using T4.0 emerging technologies to provide real-time data, has been neglected. In addition, the literature lacks any study of two-phase ambulance assignment and routing involving reaching the patients and transporting the patients to hospitals, considering the ambulances’ mission change if necessary. Given the limited resources in the emergency system and the importance of response time, it is essential to identify efficient models and systems for improving and developing ambulance services. Hence, the following questions on smart ambulance assignment-routing remained unanswered.

This paper addresses the aforementioned questions by developing an integrated intelligent emergency information system for EMS that provides real-time data on key items, such as ambulances, calls, street traffic congestion, and hospitals. In addition, an integer linear mathematical model is proposed for ambulance assignment and routing as a part of data analytics in the developed intelligent emergency information system. In the proposed mathematical model, the total travel time in two ambulance assignment-routing problems, including Phase I: from the current location of ambulances to the location of assigned call, and Phase II: from the location of the patient to the location of the assigned hospital, is minimized simultaneously. Moreover, the results of solving the proposed mathematical model may result in rearranging the mission of ambulances according to real-time data collected by the developed intelligent emergency information system. Furthermore, the flexibility of the proposed mathematical model is evaluated by solving under several real-time updating scenarios in a case study. Literature review

This section reviews the literature on smart transportation planning, vehicle smart assignment-routing, and ambulance assignment-routing, discussing the corresponding research gaps and highlighting new contributions.

Smart transportation

Asgharizadeh et al. (2022) investigated the problem of locating ambulance stations and determining the optimal number of ambulances to be deployed at each station by applying advanced traffic management techniques39. They formulated a quadratic integer programming model under demand uncertainty and employed a robust optimization approach to address this challenge. The objective function aimed to minimize overall costs, including station construction, transportation, and the installation and operation of intelligent systems. The model was validated through numerical examples and solved using the Sample Average Approximation (SAA) method combined with the Bifurcation and Delimitation Algorithm (BDA) implemented in GAMS software. However, they neglected developing an intelligent emergency information system and ambulance assignment-routing. Rani and Sharma (2023) designed an intelligent transportation system for vehicular network traffic21. The experiments were conducted in a Python environment using machine learning algorithms, including Decision Trees (DT), Random Forests (RF), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). The objective of this study was to detect abnormal traffic and network attacks, and not ambulance assignment-routing.

Haseeb et al. (2023) proposed a data routing framework for autonomous vehicles, based on a cost-aware routing algorithm, simulated annealing, and an authentication-based trust mechanism24. The evaluation was conducted through simulations, demonstrating that the proposed approach improves delay, energy consumption, and packet delivery rates. This framework was designed for real-time decision-making, although it was only tested in a simulated environment. However, they did not address ambulance assignment-routing like Dikshit et al. (2023), who explored the potential of artificial intelligence to enhance urban transportation40. Instead of presenting a mathematical model, this study reviewed AI algorithms, routing methods, and traffic management techniques aimed at improving transportation efficiency, reducing travel time, and mitigating environmental impacts. Case studies (e.g., Singapore, Pittsburgh, Los Angeles) demonstrated significant reductions in travel time, fuel consumption, and greenhouse gas emissions. Elassy et al. (2024) examined the critical components of intelligent transportation systems and their role in smart cities20. They proposed a conceptual framework that illustrated the role of emerging transportation technologies, such as intelligent traffic lights, in advancing smart city initiatives and promoting urban sustainability, neglecting to develop an integrated intelligent emergency information system for the transportation system. Guo et al. (2024) designed an intelligent transportation system consisting of four layers, including sensing, networking, processing, and application, with no attention to EMS18. To evaluate their system, they simulated a smart highway scenario in which vehicles are in motion, data are collected via IoT sensors, transmitted through the network to cloud computing infrastructure, and subsequently processed to support real-time decisions for traffic flow management and enhanced safety.

Alam et al. (2024) proposed a conceptual, indicator-based framework for assessing the sustainability of IoT-enabled intelligent transportation in smart cities19. They introduced a simple scoring model that calculates an overall sustainability score as a weighted sum of five main indicators, including traffic, pollution, energy consumption, public transport efficiency, and user satisfaction. Data are collected from urban sensors and systems, and the indicators are then computed using defined formulas. The study falls within the domain of smart transportation but aims to provide an assessment tool for policymakers rather than designing a real-time control or optimization system. Idris (2025) developed a performance measurement framework for EMS in Pakistan41. This managerial study defined Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) and implemented a one-year pilot to evaluate and improve the quality of emergency services. However, they paid no attention to ambulance assignment-routing.

Vehicle smart assignment-routing

Archetti et al. (2021) investigated the online vehicle routing problem, considering permanent and temporary drivers34. The main feature of the problem is its dynamic and uncertain demand, in which customers submit requests over time. They designed an Iterative-Insertion algorithm (II-O) and an II-O Reopt algorithm (II-OR), and evaluated the performance of the solution approach through simulations using both real-world and randomly generated data. You (2022) proposed a heuristic algorithm, termed the Constraining Inheritance Algorithm, for selecting routes between origin and destination while considering real road conditions, such as current traffic, speed limits, and road blockages, as well as carbon consumption36. The route cost function was computed based on energy consumption and carbon emissions and updated in real time using traffic data. Adelhütte et al. (2024) introduced the real-time transportation problem and investigated demand uncertainty35. To this end, they developed a Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINP) model to minimize patient delays. To solve the model, the Branch-and-Bound (BB) method and a Greedy Heuristic (GH) algorithm were applied. The model was validated through numerical experiments conducted in Python. The literature reviewed in this sub-section lacks emergency medical services planning.

Ambulance smart assignment-routing

Jagtenberg et al. (2017) addressed the problem of dynamic ambulance dispatching in emergency systems, neglecting the intelligent emergency information system and the two-phase ambulance assignment-routing42. They proposed two approaches for decision-making involving a Markov decision model solved using value iteration (VI), and a dynamic heuristic method based on the Maximum Expected Covering Location Problem (DMEXCLP) for immediate dispatching. The main objectives were to reduce delayed missions and minimize average response times. Evaluation was carried out through discrete-event simulation using real data from the city of Utrecht, the Netherlands.

Shiri et al. (2023) proposed an online optimization model for ambulance routing in mass-casualty incidents, considering patient prioritizing and minimizing the maximum patient waiting time38. Moreover, uncertainty in triage and treatment times was incorporated through scenario analysis. To solve the model, four algorithms, including Optimization-Based (OB), Clustering-Based (CB), utility-based (CA), and a hybrid clustering-utility approach (CP-CU), were proposed and implemented in Python. The results indicated that clustering- and utility-based algorithms, due to their high speed and satisfactory solution quality, are more suitable for real-world crises. However, they did not address intelligent emergency information systemS and two-phase ambulance assignment-routing, like Shiri et al. (2024), who developed an online algorithm for ambulance routing in crisis, considering post-incident changes in victims’ conditions and real-time road conditions37. An online optimization model was formulated with the objectives of minimizing the number of patients whose medical condition deteriorates and minimizing the maximum patient waiting time. To solve the model, a Decision-Based (DB) heuristic was proposed, and its effectiveness was validated through a numerical case study implemented in Python.

Chavan et al. (2025) focused on minimizing emergency response delays through a transportation system30. They designed a tri-layer platform for patients, ambulance drivers, and traffic police, enabling real-time information sharing between patients and ambulances so that both parties can monitor each other’s locations. The system integrates ambulance locations with intelligent road infrastructures, such as traffic signals, to facilitate faster passage. For routing, live traffic and road condition data are obtained through Google Maps distance matrices, and the shortest path along with the estimated arrival time is recommended to the driver using Dijkstra’s Algorithm (DA). However, they proposed no mathematical models for real-time two-phase ambulance assignment-routing, and neglected hospital assignment, like Panda et al. (2026), who developed a platform for emergency services using mobile Applications44. They employed XGBoost to predict the ambulance route using Bengaluru’s historical traffic data.

The literature reviewed above showed that none of the previous works addressed an integrated intelligent emergency information system and real-time two-phase ambulance assignment-routing in EMS. Gap analysis

According to Table 1, real-time transportation planning has received less attention, particularly in the ambulance assignment and routing problem. In addition, none of the previous works addressed real-time ambulance assignment and real-time routing, except Chavan et al. (2025) and Panda et al. (2026). However, their research was restricted to developing platforms for ambulance booking or routing, employing several existing information systems, mobile applications, and graph shortest path detection algorithms.

In addition, the literature lacks real-time two-phase ambulance assignment and routing in a multi-ambulance, multi-patient, and multi-hospital problem. Moreover, real-time ambulance mission change and developing an intelligent emergency information system have been neglected. However, this paper proposes a framework involving a conceptual model for an intelligent emergency information system to support updating emergency services in real time. Problem definition

In an emergency medical service, the nearest ambulance based on the travel time to reach the patient who called for an ambulance should be assigned. Suppose an accident occurs and an emergency call is made to the emergency call center, as illustrated in Fig. 1. For ambulance assignment, two nearby stations are considered: the first station, located four minutes from the accident site, and the second station, nine minutes away. The intelligent transportation system should select the optimal assignment and dispatch the ambulance from the first station, as it offers the shortest travel time.

In Fig. 2, Station 1 is closer to the accident site than Station 2; however, its route is congested, resulting in a travel time of 9 min. By contrast, Station 2 has no direct access to the accident and must pass through a street node, which is a square. The total travel time from Station 2 is 7 min, which is shorter than the route from Station 1. Hence, the intelligent emergency information system should select Station 2 for ambulance dispatch.

The ambulance assignment and routing without direct access.

Therefore, several parameters influence ambulance assignment and routing, and continuous updates of information are essential, highlighting the need for a real-time intelligent emergency system. Moreover, by leveraging live data and real-time processing, ambulances can be assigned and routed more effectively, a process that requires a suitable optimization model. Reaching the patient can be defined as Phase I, and hospital assignment can be considered in Phase II. In addition, an optimum route should be selected for the assigned ambulance to transport the corresponding patient to the assigned hospital. Consequently, an intelligent emergency information system should be developed to provide real-time data and optimum ambulance assignment and routing in Phases I and II. Accordingly, a mathematical model should be proposed as a part of data analytics in the developed intelligent emergency information system for optimizing the ambulance assignment in Phase I, the assigned ambulance routing in Phase I, the hospital assignment in Phase II, and the route that the assigned ambulances should pass to transport the corresponding patient to the assigned hospital in Phase II simultaneously. Therefore, the real-time location of ambulances, calls, street nodes, and hospitals should be considered in formulating the real-time two-phase ambulance assignment-routing. Notably, the data may be updated in real time during the ambulances’ mission, and there may be a need to change the mission of ambulances, considering the real-time travel times.

The proposed framework

An intelligent emergency information system and an integer linear mathematical model are proposed in this section, as a framework to support real-time two-phase ambulance assignment-routing.

Intelligent emergency information system

A real-time emergency information system can integrate and update real-time data regarding the location of ambulances and calls, availability of ambulances and hospitals, and travel time between two nodes of ambulances, streets, calls, and hospitals. Updating the data in real time can be leveraged using modern technologies, such as Global Positioning Systems (GPS), high-speed networks, online maps, and IoTs, including sensors, intelligent traffic lights, and signal towers. This system should enable real-time decision-making, such as assigning the nearest ambulance to each call, selecting the shortest and safest routes for the assigned ambulance to minimize delays to reach the corresponding patient, selecting a hospital as a destination based on both distance and real-time capacity, and selecting the shortest route from each call location to the assigned hospital17,19,21,30,45.

The conceptual model of an intelligent emergency information system is proposed in this study to integrate the emergency medical services. Central database (CD) functions as an information repository that stores all types of data, such as call information, data transmitted from the FMS, hospitals, ambulances, and ambulance stations, operation reports, hospital staff shifts, and geographical data of ambulance missions.

When a call is registered in the ECC, the initial information, such as the caller’s name and phone number, approximate location, and the severity of the incident, will be collected by the ECCC and transmitted to the CAD. At the same time, the GIS determines the exact location of the incident using the GPS of the caller’s smartphone, cellular towers, or Wi-Fi positioning. Complementary and precise data, such as geographic coordinates, exact incident location, suggested routes, considering traffic and road blockages, traffic flow, high-risk areas, road closures, and the estimated arrival time of the nearest ambulance, are gathered, and the results of the analysis are forwarded to the CAD. Subsequently, the FMS continuously and in real time monitors the status of ambulances and hospital capacities. Data regarding available resources and the status of hospitals and ambulances, including real-time location, equipment, estimated arrival time, and distance, operational readiness, and operational status reports, are transmitted from the FMS to the CAD.

As illustrated in Fig. 4, the CAD serves as the central coordinator, integrating data from the ESS, GIS, and FMS to create a comprehensive situational picture of the incident. The integrated dataset, incident details, spatial information, available resources, ambulance and hospital statuses, and other contextual data are transmitted to the DSS. Using the information obtained from the CAD and FAM modules, along with intelligent algorithms and optimization models, the DSS determines the optimal response strategy. The results include selecting the most appropriate ambulance, identifying the optimal route, and assigning the destination hospital per call. The decision is then sent back to the CAD, where a dispatch command is issued, containing details such as the incident identification number, location, type of incident, priority level, recommended ambulance unit, routing information, designated hospital, and estimated time of arrival. These details are then forwarded to the FMS to communicate the mission details to ambulances and hospitals. In addition, operational feedback, such as vehicle breakdowns or hospital refusal to admit a patient, is reported to the FMS and re-entered into the CAD and DSS cycle. In situations such as an ambulance malfunction or a sudden road closure, the ambulance GPS not only notifies the FMS but also automatically updates the GIS and informs the CAD.

Finally, mission reports and detailed records are accurately transferred from the CAD to the CD for future use. This system enables real-time processing, data storage, and integration among hospitals, ambulance stations, and other components. All these elements operate on a cloud-based data center infrastructure, which ensures real-time processing, data storage, and interoperability with hospitals.

Two-phase real-time ambulance assignment-routing problem

Suppose several ambulances can transport patients who have called emergency services to several available hospitals in an urban area. In addition, data on calls, ambulance availability, and traffic congestion can be updated in real time using the proposed intelligent emergency information system. Two real-time ambulance assignment and routing can be considered. In Phase I, ambulances should be assigned to patients, and then the best route should be selected for each ambulance to get to the assigned patients. Meanwhile, a new call, ambulance breakdown, or a traffic event can cause an update in data, such as ambulance travel time, in real time, and it might be better to change the ambulance’s mission. In Phase II, ambulances should transport the corresponding patient to a hospital through the best route. The same as Phase I, any real-time update of the data can cause a change in the route or destination of each ambulance. Ambulances can reach a call or hospital indirectly when there is no need to pass a street node, defined as the intersection point of streets. Details of an integer linear mathematical model are proposed in the following sub-sections to formulate the described two-phase real-time ambulance assignment and routing problem.

Constraints

Constraints (2)- (9) ensure that the resulting value of binary decision variables satisfies the predefined conditions in the description of the corresponding right-hand side binary parameter, presented in Sub-Sect. 4.23. In other words, Constraints (2)- (5) guarantee that the possibility of ambulance direct or indirect trip among hospital, street, and call nodes is considered in Phase I. Moreover, the possibility of ambulances’ direct or indirect trip among the call, street, and hospital nodes is checked in Phase II.

Equation (10) ensures one ambulance is assigned to each call, either traveling directly or indirectly from the ambulance node to the call node.

Constraint (11) ensures that the trip of an ambulance between two street nodes, shown by indices i and j, to reach a call node can occur only when the ambulance has already visited the street node i, either indirectly or directly.

Equation (12) guarantees that only one ambulance is assigned to each call node by traveling from the corresponding ambulance node to the assigned call node either indirectly or directly.

Equation (13) assures that exactly one ambulance leaves the assigned call node to a hospital, either entering an intermediate street node or reaching the assigned hospital directly.

Constraint (14) ensures that the trip of the ambulance assigned to a call node between two street nodes, depicted by indices i and j, to reach a hospital node can occur only when the ambulance has already visited the street node i, either indirectly or directly.

Equation (15) assures that exactly one ambulance delivers the patient of the assigned call node to the assigned hospital, either leaving an intermediate street node or entering the hospital directly.

Constraints (16) and (17) assure that an ambulance can be assigned to call nodes when it is selected among available ambulances for a mission, and vice versa.

Constraints (18) and (19) assure that only when an ambulance is assigned to a call node, the ambulance can start a trip from the corresponding ambulance node to the call node, either entering a street node or reaching the call node directly, and vice versa.

Constraint (20) ensures that an ambulance can travel between two street nodes to reach a call node only when the ambulance is assigned to the call node.

Constraint (21) assures that an ambulance can leave a street node as the last street node in the trip to reach the assigned call node only when the ambulance is assigned to the call node.

Constraints (22) and (23) guarantee that an ambulance can transport the patient of a call to a hospital only when the ambulance is assigned to the call node, and vice versa.

Constraints (24) and (25) assure that only when an ambulance is assigned to transport the patient of a call node to a hospital, the ambulance can start a trip from the corresponding call node to the hospital node, either entering a street node or reaching the hospital node directly, and vice versa.

Constraint (26) ensures that only when an ambulance is assigned to transport the patient of a call node to a hospital, the ambulance can travel between two street nodes in a trip from the call node to the hospital node.

Constraint (27) assures that only when an ambulance is assigned to transport the patient of a call node to a hospital, the ambulance can leave a street node as the last street node in a trip to reach the assigned hospital node.

Equation (28) ensures that if an ambulance is leaving a street node, i, to reach a call node, the ambulance has already entered this street node, and vice versa.

Equation (29) ensures that if an ambulance is leaving a street node, i, to deliver the patient of a call node to a hospital node, the ambulance has already entered this street node in a trip from the call node to the hospital node, and vice versa.

Constraints (30)- (32) guarantee the sub-tour elimination in ambulance routing to reach call nodes in Phase I.

Constraints (33)- (35) guarantee the sub-tour elimination in ambulance routing to transport the patient of the corresponding call node to the assigned hospital in Phase II.

Finally, Constraint (36) specifies the type of decision variables.

Conclusions, limitations, and recommendations

This paper contributed to Transportation 4.0 planning in emergency medical services by proposing a framework for ambulance smart assignment and routing. The proposed framework involves an intelligent emergency information system for real-time data collection and a new integer linear mathematical model for two-phase ambulance assignment and routing, which minimizes the total ambulance travel time, considering ambulance mission change under several assumptions on reception capacity, ambulance fleet capacity, and number of calls in normal daily life. The applicability of the proposed mathematical model in real time ambulance assignment and routing was illustrated in a case study. Rewardingly, three scenarios involving an assumed present status, real-time updating with a new call, and real-time updating with an ambulance breakdown. Results demonstrated the flexibility of the proposed mathematical model in real time updating of ambulance assignment and routing based on the real-time update of input parameters.

The proposed framework can be considered an initial step toward Transportation 4.0 planning in the emergency medical services, considering street nodes and real-time mission change in the two-phase ambulance smart assignment and routing. However, this research suffers from several assumptions and limitations, which should be addressed to propose a comprehensive framework for intelligent emergency medical services planning. For example, this research neglected developing the proposed framework in an application or software for real-time ambulance assignment and routing due to technical, technological, internet, and budget restrictions, which can be considered in the following related research. In addition, attributes of ambulances, calls, streets, and hospitals, such as reliability, equity, critical clinical status, sustainability, resiliency, human-centricity, and viability, were not included in the ambulance assignment-routing problem. Hence, prioritizing these significant items of emergency systems using Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) techniques, such as fuzzy or crisp Analytical Hierarchy Process46–48, and incorporating the resulting weights in the formulation of new multi-objective mathematical models for ambulance assignment-routing can be noticed in future works. Although vehicle assignment-routing is categorized as an NP-hard problem, time complexity assessment in large-scale case studies has been neglected in this research. Hence, assessing the time complexity of the proposed mathematical model in real metropolitan-scale problems and developing the corresponding heuristic, metaheuristic, and quantum algorithms are recommended for future work. In addition, the proposed mathematical model neglected the interconnectivity of several emergency medical services in adjacent cities. Therefore, proposing a mathematical model for ambulance assignment-routing in an intertwined emergency medical services system is recommended. Moreover, travel times can be predicted as uncertain parameters, which can be incorporated in the forthcoming fuzzy, stochastic, or possibilistic mathematical models for two-phase ambulance assignment-routing problems. Furthermore, the resource limitations and service times were neglected in the proposed mathematical model, which can restrict the applicability of the model in disaster or disruption situations. Therefore, future works can incorporate the constraints on available ambulances and hospital capacities to advance the proposed preliminary formulation. Finally, using Pyomo for coding the proposed mathematical model in a Python environment instead of Gams is highly recommended for future work because of higher adaptation to online optimization utilizing a cloud computing service.

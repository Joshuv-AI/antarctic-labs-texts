Abstract




Proof of a conjecture of Keith
on congruences
of the reciprocal of a
false theta function

Abstract.

Recently, Keith investigated
reciprocals of false theta functions
and proved some interesting results
such as congruences, asymptotic bounds,
and combinatorial identities.
At the end of his paper, Keith posed
a conjecture on congruences
modulo 4 and 8 for the coefficients
of the reciprocal of a
false theta function.
In this paper, we
not only confirm Keith’s
conjecture, but also prove
a generalized result.

Keywords:

false theta function, congruence, theta function.

AMS Subject
Classification:
11P83, 05A17

1
Introduction

Ramanujan’s
general theta
function is defined by

\displaystyle f(a,b)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a^{n(n+1)/2}b^{n(n-1)/2},\qquad|ab|<1.

(1.1)

In Ramanujan’s notation,
the Jacobi triple product
identity takes the shape

\displaystyle f(a,b)=(-a,-b,ab;ab)_{\infty}.

(1.2)

Here and throughout this paper,
we use the following
notation:

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle\;:=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k}),\qquad(a;q)_{n}:=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}},

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle\;:=(a_{1};q)_{\infty}(a_{2};q)_{\infty}\cdots(a_{k};q)_{\infty},

where
q
is a complex number
with
|q|<1
. In addition, for all positive
integers
m
, define

\displaystyle f_{m}:=(q^{m};q^{m})_{\infty}.

Three special cases
of (
1.1
) are defined by, in Ramanujan’s
notation,

\displaystyle f(-q,-q^{2})

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n(3n-1)/2}=f_{1},

(1.3)

\displaystyle f(-q,-q)

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}=\frac{f_{1}^{2}}{f_{2}},

(1.4)

\displaystyle f(-q,-q^{3})

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-q)^{n(n+1)/2}=\frac{f_{1}f_{4}}{f_{2}}.

(1.5)

The reciprocals of the right
sides
of (
1.3
)–(
1.5
)
are the generating functions of
the ordinary partition function
p(n)

[
1
]
,
the overpartition
function
\overline{p}(n)

[
7
]
,
and the partition
with odd parts distinct
function
[
9
]
, respectively,
which are three of the most important
types of partition functions. A number of
congruences for the three types of
partition functions have been proved;
see for example

[
8
,
9
,
11
]
.

In 1917, Rogers
[
14
]
introduced false theta functions,
which are series
that would be classical theta functions
except for changes in signs
of an infinite number of terms. False theta functions closely
resemble ordinary theta functions; however, they do not have the
modular transformation properties that theta functions have.
In his notebooks
[
12
]

and lost notebook
[
13
]
, Ramanujan
recorded many false theta function
identities on false theta functions

\Psi(a,b)
defined by

\displaystyle\Psi(a,b):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a^{n(n+1)/2}b^{n(n-1)/2}-\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}a^{n(n+1)/2}b^{n(n-1)/2}.

(1.6)

In recent times, there has been an increase in
interest in false theta functions. They appear in a variety of
contexts including modular and quantum modular forms,
meromorphic Jacobi forms,
quantum knot invariants,
and many more.
In
[
2
]
, Andrews proved five identities
related to false theta functions
which were recorded in Ramanujan’s
lost notebook
[
13
]
(c.f.

[
3
, Sect. 9.3, pp. 227–232]
). Other
analytic proofs have been given by Andrews
and
Warnaar

[
4
]

and by Wang
[
17
]
.
Burson
[
6
]

gave a bijection proof
of the following identity due to Ramanujan:

\Psi(q^{3},q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{n}}{(-q;q^{2})_{n+1}}q^{n}.

Recently, Keith
[
10
]
investigated
reciprocals
of false theta functions
and proved some interesting results
on congruences,
asymptotic bounds,
and combinatorial identities.
For example, he proved
that for
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle c_{5}(8n+5)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{2},

\displaystyle c_{5}(32n+31)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{4},

\displaystyle c_{9}(16n+12)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{2},

where
c_{t}(n)
is defined by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{t}(n)q^{n}:=\frac{1}{\Psi(-q^{t},q)}.

(1.7)

At the end of his paper, Keith
[
10
]

posed the following conjecture
on congruences modulo 4 and 8 for
c_{5}(n)
:

Conjecture 1.1

[
10
]

For
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle c_{5}(32n+31)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{8},

(1.8)

\displaystyle c_{5}(128n+123)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{8},

(1.9)

\displaystyle c_{5}(512n+491)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{8},

(1.10)

\displaystyle c_{5}(64n+19)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{4},

(1.11)

\displaystyle c_{5}(256n+75)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{4},

(1.12)

\displaystyle c_{5}(196n+i)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{4},

(1.13)

\displaystyle c_{5}(196n+j)

\displaystyle\;\equiv 0\pmod{4},

(1.14)

where
i\in\{110,138,194\}

and
j\in\{19,47,75,103,159,187\}
.

The aim of this paper is
to
prove congruences (
1.8
)–(
1.13
)
and generalize (
1.14
).
The main results
of this paper can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2

Congruences (
1.8
)–(
1.13
) are true.

Theorem 1.3

Let
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
be a prime. For all integers

n
and
k\geq 0
with
p\nmid n
,

\displaystyle c_{5}\left(4p^{2k+1}n+\frac{8p^{2k+2}+1}{3}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

(1.15)

Setting
p=7
in (
1.15
)
yields (
1.14
). Therefore, (
1.15
) is a
generalization
of (
1.14
).

This paper is organized as follows.
Sections 2 and 3 are devoted to
the proofs of Theorems

1.2
and
1.3
,
respectively.
In Section 4, we make some
concluding remarks
concerning future
directions.

\displaystyle a_{1}\left(p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{2(p^{2\alpha+2}-1)}{3}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{4}.

(3.5)

Thanks to (
2.77
) and (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2}(n)q^{n}\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{6n^{2}+4n}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{3n^{2}}\pmod{2},

which yields

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2}(n)q^{3n+2}\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}q^{2(3n+1)^{2}+(3m)^{2}}\pmod{2}.

Therefore, if
3n+2
is not of
the form
2x^{2}+y^{2}
,
then

a_{2}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

Note that if
N
is of the form
2x^{2}+y^{2}
,
then
\nu_{p}(N)
is even since

p
is a prime
with
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
and
\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)=-1
. Here and throughout
this paper,
\nu_{p}(N)

denotes the highest power of
p

dividing

N
and
\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)
denotes the Legendre symbol.
It is easy to check that
if
p\nmid n
, then

\nu_{p}\left(3\left(p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{2(p^{2\alpha+2}-1)}{3}\right)+2\right)=\nu_{p}\left(p^{2\alpha+1}(3n+2p)\right)=2\alpha+1

is odd. Therefore,

3\left(p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{2(p^{2\alpha+2}-1)}{3}\right)+2\ (p\nmid n)
is not of the form

2x^{2}+y^{2}
and

\displaystyle a_{2}\left(p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{2(p^{2\alpha+2}-1)}{3}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

(3.6)

Replacing
n
by
p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{2(p^{2\alpha+2}-1)}{3}\ (p\nmid n)

in (
3.3
) and utilizing (
3.5
)
and (
3.6
), we arrive
at (
3.1
). This completes
the proof.

Lemma 3.2

If
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
is a prime, then
for all integers

n
and
\alpha\geq 0
with
p\nmid n
,

\displaystyle b_{2}\left(4p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{8p^{2\alpha+2}+1}{3}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

(3.7)

Proof.
By (
2.36
) and (
2.42
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{2}(4n+3)q^{n}

\displaystyle\;\equiv\frac{f_{3}^{3}}{f_{1}}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{3n^{2}+2n}\pmod{2},\qquad({\rm by}\ \eqref{2-12})

from which with (
2.77
),
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{2}(4n+3)q^{n}

\displaystyle\;\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{3n^{2}+2n}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{3n^{2}+2n}\pmod{2}.

From the above congruence,
we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{2}(4n+3)q^{3n+2}

\displaystyle\;\equiv\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{(3n+1)^{2}+(3m+1)^{2}}\pmod{2}.

Therefore, if
3n+2
is not of
the form
x^{2}+y^{2}
,
then

b_{2}(4n+3)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

Note that if
N
is of the form
x^{2}+y^{2}
,
then
\nu_{p}(N)
is even since

p
is a prime
with
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
and
\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)=-1
. It is easy to check that
if
p\nmid n
, then

\nu_{p}\left(3\left(p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{2(p^{2\alpha+2}-1)}{3}\right)+2\right)=\nu_{p}\left(p^{2\alpha+1}(3n+2p)\right)=2\alpha+1

is odd. Therefore,

3\left(p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{2(p^{2\alpha+2}-1)}{3}\right)+2\ (p\nmid n)
is not of the form

x^{2}+y^{2}
and
(
3.7
) holds. The proof is complete.

To conclude this section, we pose
a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem

1.3
.
Replacing
n

by
4p^{2\alpha+1}n+\frac{8p^{2\alpha+2}+1}{3}
in (
2.83
)
and using (
3.1
)
and (
3.7
), we arrive at (

).

4
Concluding remarks

As seen in Introduction, false theta functions
have received
a lot of attention
in recent years.
In this study, we confirm a conjecture
of Keith on congruences
of modulo 4 and 8 for the coefficients
of the reciprocal of a
false theta function. A natural
question
is to extend
the congruences in
this paper to modulo
16
,
32
,

64
, etc.
However, it will likely
require a different approach
since the methods used
in this paper run into serious
limitations beyond the modulus
of
16
. Moreover, it would be
interesting
to determine the
arithmetic density of the
set of
integers such that

c_{t}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{m}
for fixed positive
integers
t
and
m\geq 2
.

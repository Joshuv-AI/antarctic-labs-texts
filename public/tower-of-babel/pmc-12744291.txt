Longitudinal topological manipulations of vortex fields via nested spiral arrays
Authors: Ying Wang, Jianmin Wu, Li Ma, Jianwei Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryVortex beams, characterized by their helical phase wavefronts, hold great promise in optical information science and light-matter interactions. However, current research predominantly focuses on static transverse properties, leaving a critical knowledge gap in the longitudinal mechanisms. This study systematically investigates the longitudinal evolution of vortex beams by constructing a nested spiral array configuration with gradient-distributed topological charges. Through a wavefront modulation scheme and a parametric topological charge regulation, we established a theoretical framework for longitudinal vortex field propagation, enabling quantitative characterization of annular intensity profiles, phase evolution patterns, and topological state transitions. Numerical simulations reveal propagation-dependent intensity enhancement followed by attenuation, while the phase maintains azimuthal rotational symmetry. Notably, the topological charge manifests a stepwise decrement pattern proportional to the spiral array number. This multidimensional vortex modulation mechanism provides theoretical foundations for advancing orbital angular momentum multiplexing technologies and developing multiple degree of freedom manipulation systems for microparticles in optical tweezers.Subject areas: Applied sciences, Network topology

Introduction

Optical vortex beams, defined by their intrinsic helical wavefronts and associated phase singularities, constitute a fundamental class of topologically structured light.1 Distinguished from conventional Gaussian beams by their characteristic toroidal intensity distributions and central dark cores, these fields possess orbital angular momentum (OAM) directly linked to their topological charge l.2 The theoretical framework established by Allen et al. in 1992 explicitly connected the helical phase structure of Laguerre-Gaussian modes to a quantized OAM of lℏ per photon, firmly establishing vortices as carriers of optical angular momentum and launching a vibrant field of research.3 The unique spatial mode properties of vortex beams have enabled transformative applications across diverse areas of photonics. These include high-dimensional quantum information protocols,4 super-resolution microscopy,5 holographic encryption,6 space-division multiplexed communications,7 and precise optical manipulation of microparticles.8 The capacity to encode information within the spatial degrees of freedom of light continues to drive innovations at the interface of structured light and matter.

Significant theoretical and experimental advances have recently elucidated vortex beam interference, diffraction phenomena, and polarization-state engineering.9 Nevertheless, research efforts have predominantly concentrated on two-dimensional transverse characteristics. Representative examples include particle trapping via transverse gradient forces and OAM mode decomposition and multiplexing based on transverse phase topologies.10,11 In contrast, the comprehensive understanding and deliberate manipulation of the longitudinal evolution of vortex fields remain notably underexplored. A deeper understanding of longitudinal dynamics is not only of fundamental significance for elucidating the three-dimensional propagation behavior of structured light, but also of critical importance for the engineering of advanced light field modulation techniques and developed optical communication systems.12 Addressing this critical knowledge gap necessitates both novel physical insights into axial topological transformations and sophisticated design frameworks capable of encoding and manipulating vorticity along the propagation dimension.

In the study of longitudinal optical manipulation, a fundamental scientific challenge lies in deciphering the underlying mechanisms that govern energy distribution and phase evolution during beam propagation. Within the framework of electromagnetic field theory, longitudinal energy transport can be characterized by the spatiotemporal evolution of optical intensity,13 a quantity intrinsically linked to the light OAM. This interdependence critically determines beam characteristics including focal structure, propagation stability in complex media, and the efficacy of optical manipulation protocols. Particularly, the longitudinal dynamics of OAM, arising from the helical topology of the wavefront, underpin transformative applications spanning spatial-division multiplexing, high-dimensional quantum information encoding, and multiparticle optomechanical control.14,15

A prerequisite for exploring these applications is the ability to reliably generate and manipulate vortex fields with tunable longitudinal characteristics. Established platforms, including spiral phase plates (SPPs),16,17 computer-generated holograms (CGHs),18,19 Q-plates,20,21 spatial light modulators (SLMs),22,23 and metasurfaces,24,25,26,27,28,29,30 have each demonstrated varying degrees of success. However, their practical deployment is often hindered by inherent limitations in sculpting longitudinal OAM evolution, such as SPPs face fabrication bottlenecks at subwavelength scales, CGHs suffer from efficiency loss due to conjugate image interference, and SLMs are constrained by refresh rate and space-bandwidth product. To overcome these challenges, nested spiral arrays have emerged as a promising platform for longitudinal vortex field control. Their high damage thresholds and reconfigurable structural design offer additional degrees of freedom for dynamic beam shaping.31,32 A comparative assessment of typical vortex generation methods reveals that SPPs, CGHs, and SLMs, while effective in generating helical phase fronts, each suffer from inherent limitations among efficiency, wavelength dependence, and fabrication or system complexity. In contrast, the nested spiral arrays achieve high structural robustness, broad operational bandwidth, and direct geometric tunability of longitudinal topological charge, enabling programmable control of vortex evolutions. This positions the nested spiral arrays as a versatile and durable alternative for structured-light engineering. By precisely engineering the aperture geometry and matching the embedded spiral phase profile, this approach enables accurate manipulation of both topological charge and longitudinal phase evolution. However, a comprehensive theoretical framework describing the longitudinal dynamics of vortex fields in such systems remains undeveloped, thus providing fertile ground for the innovations presented in this work.

We introduce a paradigm for the topological manipulation of optical vortex fields leveraging nested spiral arrays incorporating encoded helical phase structures. We systematically investigate the longitudinal dynamics of such structured vortex beams, focusing on the amplitude reconstruction, phase evolution, and OAM redistribution during propagation. Theoretically, we establish a rigorous analytical framework describing the amplitude, phase topology, and OAM of vortex beams modulated by nested spiral architectures. By synergistically combining diffraction integral theory with modal decomposition techniques, this framework provides a comprehensive description for the longitudinal evolution of structured light fields. Furthermore, we construct a wave-optics-based numerical diffraction model capable of precisely resolving the three-dimensional intensity redistribution, dynamic phase shift profile, and OAM evolution under various spiral configurations. Our results reveal that the transverse field profiles at different axial positions exhibit propagation-dependent modal interference patterns, governed jointly by the spatial geometry of the spiral array and phase-matching conditions. This synergy between engineered structure and inherent topology enables precise tailoring of the beam’s internal degrees of freedom during propagation, specifically its phase singularity network and OAM distribution. This study not only deepens the understanding of the fundamental mechanisms for the propagation dynamics in structured light but also establishes a versatile theoretical and design platform for high-dimensional optical field control and advanced applications in topological photonics and optical micromanipulation along three-dimensional trajectories.

Simulations of longitudinal vortex modulations

Building upon the above theoretical framework of longitudinal vortex manipulation via nested spiral arrays, we conducted systematic numerical simulations to validate the proposed topological modulation mechanism. To evaluate the tunability of the longitudinal topological control, we selected three representative configurations of nested spiral arrays characterized by their nesting order M = 2, 4, and 5 for comparative analysis. All simulations were performed under a unified parametrization scheme to ensure consistency and clarity. The incident wavelength was fixed at λ = 632.8 nm, and the initial radius of the spiral array was set to ρ0 = 5 mm. Each individual spiral array comprised N = 86 subwavelength-scale apertures, with each aperture having a radius of ρ = 15 μm. The structural parameters of the nested spiral arrays were selected and systematically optimized with practical fabrication and alignment constraints. The initial radius was chosen to accommodate the total number and spatial extent of apertures, which yields an optimal spatial distribution within each spiral’s azimuthal periodicity while avoiding overlap between neighboring apertures. Aperture lateral dimensions were chosen to lie in the range commonly accessible to femtosecond-laser micromachining and standard lithographic processes, ensuring that the modeled aperture transmission and diffraction behavior are practically realizable. In addition, the selected radial spacing and inter-arm separations account for typical alignment tolerances and fabrication precision so as to preserve the intended phase relationships among sub-arrays. Finally, the nested-spiral approach is compatible with widely used dielectric substrates and direct-write fabrication techniques; previous experimental implementations of related multi-spiral devices validate the feasibility of producing high-fidelity aperture arrays for accurate optical modulation.31,32

The spatial and topological properties of the nested arrays were jointly described by a sequence of axial positions and corresponding topological charges, denoted as (zi, li). The optical field propagation was simulated by numerically evaluating the scalar Fresnel diffraction integral. This was implemented efficiently using the Fresnel convolution method, which employs the fast Fourier transform (FFT) for controlled and accurate computation. For a given input field T(x, y) in the aperture plane, the propagated field at distance z is computed as E(x,y,z)=F−1{F{T(x,y)}·H(fx,fy,z)}, where F and F−1 denote the 2D FFT and inverse FFT, respectively, and H is the transfer function for Fresnel propagation. This FFT-based approach is numerically efficient for the paraxial propagation distances.

This spatially resolved evolution of OAM states provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of the proposed array design in engineering the longitudinal topological properties of vortex fields.

Concurrently, the azimuthal phase contours maintain a consistent clockwise rotation while the magnitude of the phase winding decreases stepwise. This evolution manifests as a monotonic decrement in the topological charge, revealing a precise programmable control mechanism for longitudinal topological transitions in vortex fields.

Discussion

Remarkably, li maintains a quantized relationship as an integer multiple of the spiral parameter M. This linear decay accompanied by topological quantization demonstrates the deterministic role of the engineered spiral array in governing the vortex field’s topological evolution. The observed proportionality between topological charge and the number of spiral arms demonstrates that the nested spiral arrays not only sculpt the field intensity but also enable high-fidelity, programmable control over longitudinal topological states.

Systematic numerical investigation reveals a fundamental invariant governing the vortex field evolution: the product of the axial propagation distance zi and the corresponding topological charge li remains conserved throughout the evolution of the vortex field. This conservation is in exact agreement with the theoretical constraint condition derived from the spiral array equation of Eq. 2, where the radial coordinate of the spiral is governed by the incremental azimuthal angle as Δρi=liziλ2πρ0φ. The conservation relation zi ⋅ li arises from a phase-matching condition between the topological charge li and the designed distance zi of the nested spiral arrays. This constant product is a result of the complementary scaling relationship between the propagation distance and the topological charge. Each spiral element introduces a helical phase front with a topological charge li, and ensures the azimuthally accumulated phase over one period corresponds to an integer multiple of 2πli. Physically it means that, when the radial spacing between arms is fixed, maintaining an integer 2π phase increment between adjacent arms forces a constructive interference at the axial position zi where a given vortex order forms with the topological charge li encoded. This conserved quantity offers profound physical insight into the mechanism of longitudinal topological manipulation enabled by the nested spiral arrays. Specifically, under the condition that adjacent spiral aperture units remain non-overlapping, the introduction of a designed 2π phase shift between neighboring radial elements facilitates a controlled gradient of OAM along the optical axis. This axial OAM modulation yields a spatially structured evolution of the optical vortex topology, establishing a robust and tunable framework for the deterministic engineering of three-dimensional topological light fields.

Synthesis and perspectives on longitudinal topological manipulations

This study systematically establishes a framework for the longitudinal topological manipulation of optical vortex fields through nested spiral arrays. Leveraging diffraction theory within a wave-optics formalism, we systematically quantify the axial evolution dynamics of vortex beams modulated by the structured arrays, specifically exploring transformations in intensity distributions, phase singularities, and topological charges.

To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, we construct a series of propagation models for vortex beams passing through nested spiral arrays with varying structural parameters. The simulations demonstrate that, during axial propagation, the annular intensity of the vortex beam initially undergoes enhancement, followed by a gradual attenuation. The phase fronts maintain a consistent rotational handedness throughout the propagation, indicative of a preserved angular momentum direction. Notably, the vortex topological charge exhibits a stepwise reduction, where the instantaneous topological state remains quantized in integer multiples of the number of embedded spiral arms. This discrete evolution demonstrates a robust coupling mechanism between the imposed structural symmetry and the OAM content.

These findings unveil a distinctive longitudinal topological modulation behavior governed by the nested spiral arrays, offering profound insights into the spatial encoding of phase structures. The observed evolution provides a promising strategy for constructing three-dimensional optical systems based on phase-encoded architectures that enable targeted customization of longitudinal field characteristics. Vortices generated by nested spiral arrays exhibit programmable evolution in both topological charge and field intensity along the propagation direction, thereby extending vortex control from two-dimensional transverse manipulation to full three-dimensional regulation. In optical tweezers, such vortices permit axial selection of trap planes and distance-dependent control of optical torque and gradient forces, facilitating programmable 3D transport and rotation of microparticles without mechanical translation of the sample. In optical communications, longitudinal OAM labeling provides an orthogonal channel dimension, as distinct axial planes can carry independently encoded data streams that are demultiplexed at corresponding receiver planes, thereby increasing aggregate channel density or enabling spatially selective routing. Collectively, these results establish nested spiral arrays as a flexible platform for longitudinal topological sculpting with immediate applicability to advanced optical manipulation and high-capacity OAM-encoded communication systems.

Limitations of the study

The primary limitation of the present study lies in the absence of experimental verification for the theoretical predictions concerning the longitudinal modulation of vortex beams by nested spiral arrays. While the analytical and numerical results provide valuable insight into the underlying physical mechanisms, empirical validation remains essential for confirming the robustness and practical feasibility of the proposed modulation scheme. Experimental data would not only substantiate the theoretical framework but also help refine the model parameters and reveal potential discrepancies arising from practical imperfections such as alignment errors, phase aberrations, or material limitations. Unfortunately, the direct implementation of the experimental component was hindered by recent institutional transitions, which temporarily restricted access to specialized laboratory infrastructure and optical instrumentation required for high-precision vortex beam generation and detection. These logistical constraints limited the immediate execution of the experimental phase within the current study timeline.

Future research will therefore prioritize the experimental realization of the proposed scheme as a key objective, integrating theoretical modeling with systematic experimental investigations. Such an approach will enable a comprehensive assessment of the longitudinal evolution and stability of optical vortices modulated by nested spiral arrays, ultimately bridging the gap between theoretical design and experimental realization.

Method details

Research framework and simulation procedure

This study establishes a comprehensive computational framework for investigating the longitudinal topological manipulation of optical vortex fields through nested spiral arrays. All simulations were conducted using MATLAB R2018b, which implement a scalar Fresnel diffraction model within a paraxial wave-optics formalism.

At the input plane, the optical field was modeled as a monochromatic plane wave with a fixed wavelength of λ = 632.8 nm. The nested spiral phase arrays were numerically generated as binary phase masks comprising M = 2, 3, 4, and 5 intertwined spiral structures. The initial radius of each spiral array was set to ρ0 = 5 mm, and each spiral arm contained N = 86 subwavelength-scale apertures, each with a radius of ρ = 15 μm.

To ensure computational reproducibility, all parameters, including spatial sampling rate, propagation step size, and grid resolution, were fixed across all simulations. Each simulation was independently repeated three times to confirm the numerical stability and convergence of the Fresnel integral solutions. No randomization or blinding was required, as the study relied solely on deterministic numerical modeling.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Quantitative analysis focused on two primary observables: (1) the normalized annular intensity of the transverse vortex beam, and (2) the longitudinal evolution of the topological charge, derived via phase gradient integration.

For the annular vortex intensity, radial intensity profiles were extracted along the locus of maximum amplitude in the first annular ring. Normalization was performed relative to the total integrated beam intensity within the computational aperture. The same spatial and color scaling parameters were applied to all intensity and phase maps in Figures 2, 3, and 4 to maintain consistency across comparisons.

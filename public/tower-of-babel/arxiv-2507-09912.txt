Towards the Green correspondence for supergroups
Inna Entova-Aizenbud, Vera Serganova, Alex Sherman

Abstract
. We prove a version of Green’s correspondence for complex algebraic supergroups, constructing a correspondence between certain indecomposable representations of G G and the normalizer of a Sylow subgroup of G G .



1.
Introduction

The subject of this paper is the category of representations of a quasireductive supergroup
G
over the field of complex numbers. Recall that quasireductive supergroups are supergroups with linearly reductive underlying algebraic groups (up to connected component). They are characterized by the property that the tensor category
\operatorname{Rep}G
of finite-dimensional
G
-modules is Frobenius.

Other well-studied examples of such categories come from modular representation theory of finite groups. One can try to apply methods of representation theory of finite groups to representations of quasireductive supergroups.

There are already several results in this direction. Boe, Kujawa and Nakano developed the theory of cohomological support for
\operatorname{Rep}G
in
[
BoKN1
]
,
[
BoKN2
]
and
[
BoKN3
]
.
In
[
SSV
]
we develop the theory of Sylow subgroups in
G
, which are analogues of Sylow
p
-groups in modular representation theory. In particular, we prove that all Sylow subgroups are conjugate.

The goal of this paper is to study the relation between representations of
G
and the normalizer
K
of a Sylow subgroup of
G
. This is directly inspired by local representation theory from the modular setting. Our first result is Theorem
2.12
, in which we establish a correspondence between indecomposable representations of
G
and
K

with non-zero superdimension. Although this correspondence is not bijective, it is one-to-one under a mild technical condition. This condition holds for all simple quasireductive supergroups except of type
Q(n)
. In the language of tensor categories, this correspondence induces a full functor from the semisimplification of
\operatorname{Rep}G
to the semisimplification of
\operatorname{Rep}K
.

Theorem
2.12
may be viewed as the first step towards a Green’s correspondence for representations of supergroups. Our assumptions on the superdimension mean we only consider modules whose defect subgroup is the whole Sylow subgroup. For an analogous version of Green’s correspondence for finite groups, see Sec. 4 of
[
EO
]
.

In the modular representation theory for a finite group
G
, it is known that if a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
satisfies the trivial intersection property (meaning
P\cap gPg^{-1}=P
or
\{e\})
, then restriction induces an equivalence of stable categories
\operatorname{St}G\to\operatorname{St}N_{G}(P)
(see Chpt. 10 of
[
A
]
). In the super case, the analogous trivial intersection property is too strong as it immediately implies that
P
is normal! However a possible weakening of the condition is as follows: if
P
is a Sylow subgroup, then
P_{0}
satisfies the trivial intersection property, where
P_{0}
is the maximal even subgroup of
P
. This is natural in light of Corollary 9.10, of
[
SSV
]
which states that two Sylow
p
-subgroups
P,P^{\prime}
are equal if and only
P_{0}=P^{\prime}_{0}
.

The only (nontrivial) cases in which
P_{0}
satisfies the trivial intersection property is when
G
is a defect one basic classical supergroup. In this case, we prove that the stable categories of
G
and
K/\Gamma
are equivalent for some finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset K
, see Theorem
3.13
. We use this to explicitly compute the semisimplification
of
\operatorname{Rep}G
at the end of Section 3.

In the last section, we discuss a conjectural candidate for the defect group of a block for classical supergroups
GL(m|n)
and
OSp(m|2n)
in terms of relative projectivity.

2.
Splitting subgroups and semisimplification

2.1.
Splitting subgroups

Let
G
be a quasireductive supergroup; by this we mean that the connected component of the identity of
G_{0}
is connected. In particular,
G
need not be connected. We will write
\mathfrak{g}
for the Lie superalgebra of
G
.

Recall that a quasireductive subgroup
K\subseteq G
is called splitting if the natural
G
-map
{\mathbb{C}}\to\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}
coming from the adjunction has a splitting
\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. If
K\subseteq G
is splitting and
H\subseteq K
is splitting, then
H\subseteq G
is also splitting.

Example 2.1
.

We now provide the main examples of splitting subgroups.

(1)

If
G=GL(m|n)
, then
K=GL(1|1)\times GL(m-1|n-1)
is splitting. In fact,
GL(1|1)^{\min(m,n)}
is splitting in
GL(m|n)
.

(2)

If
G=OSp(m|2n)
and
r=\min(\lfloor m/2\rfloor,n)
, then
K=OSp(2|2)^{r}\subseteq OSp(m|2n)
is splitting.

(3)

Suppose more generally that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Kac-Moody Lie superalgebra. Recall that the defect of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is the maximal size of a linearly independent set of isotropic roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. If
{\mathfrak{g}}
is of defect
d
, let
S=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}\}
be a linearly independet set of isotropic roots. We may consider the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
generated by all root space
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm\alpha_{1}},\dots,{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm\alpha_{d}}
. Then
{\mathfrak{k}}\cong{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}(1|1)^{d}
. If we let
K\subseteq G
denote the subgroup of
{\mathfrak{k}}
which integrates
{\mathfrak{k}}
, then
K
is splitting inside of
G
.

(4)

From example (3), we see that if
G
is defect 1 (e.g.
AG(1|2),AB(1|3)
, or
D(2,1;t)
) then
SL(1|1)\subseteq G
is splitting, where
SL(1|1)
is the root subgroup of any isotropic root.

(5)

If
G=P(n)
, then
K=P(n-2)\times P(2)
is splitting in
G
. In particular
P(2)^{n}
is spltting inside of
P(2n)
, and
P(2)^{n}\times P(1)
is splitting inside of
P(2n+1)
.

(6)

If
G=Q(n)
, then
K=Q(n-2)\times Q(2)
is splitting in
G
. In particular,
Q(2)^{n}
is splitting inside of
Q(2n)
, and
Q(2)^{n}\times Q(1)
is splitting inside of
Q(2n+1)
.

2.2.
Semisimplification

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric tensor category. By this we mean that
\mathcal{C}
is a
\Bbbk
-linear, symmetric monoidal category such that
\mathcal{C}
is locally finite abelian,
\operatorname{End}(\mathbf{1})=\Bbbk
, and every object of
\mathcal{C}
is rigid. Given a morphism
f:X\to X
in
\mathcal{C}
, we define
\operatorname{tr}(f)\in\Bbbk
to be the following morphism:

\mathbf{1}\xrightarrow{\text{coev}}X\otimes X^{*}\xrightarrow{f\otimes 1}X\otimes X^{*}\cong X^{*}\otimes X\xrightarrow{\text{ev}}\mathbf{1}.

The tensor ideal
\mathcal{N}
of negligible morphisms is given by all morphisms
f:X\to Y
such that
\operatorname{tr}(fg)=0
for all morphisms
g:Y\to X
.

Definition 2.2
.

For any tensor category
\mathcal{C}
we define the semisimplification of
\mathcal{C}
to be the quotient category
\overline{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{N}
. We write
S:\mathcal{C}\to\overline{\mathcal{C}}
for the quotient functor; we call
S
the semisimplification functor.

When
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Rep}G
, we will write
\operatorname{str}
for the categorical trace.

2.3.
Construction of the functor
F

Proposition 2.3
.

Given a splitting subgroup
K\subset G
, the functor
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{K}
takes negligible morphisms to negligible morphisms.

Proof.

The splitting of
{\mathbb{C}}\to\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}
induces the splitting of
i_{M}:M\to\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}\operatorname{Res}_{K}M
for any
G
-module
M
via the canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}M\simeq(\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}})\otimes M
. Let
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(\operatorname{Res}_{K}M,\operatorname{Res}_{K}N)
, define
\tilde{f}:\in\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(M,N)
as a composition

M\xrightarrow{i_{M}}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}(f)}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}N\xrightarrow{p_{N}}N,

where
p_{N}
is the splitting morphism and
i_{M}
the canonical embedding.

First we claim that if
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(M,N)
then
\tilde{f}=f
. Indeed,

i_{N}\circ f=\operatorname{Ind}(f)\circ i_{M}

and the statement follows since
p_{N}\circ i_{N}
is the identity.

Next we claim that if
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(M,N)
and
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(\operatorname{Res}_{K}N,\operatorname{Res}_{K}L)
then
\widetilde{g\circ f}=\tilde{g}\circ f
since both are defined by
the compositions

M\xrightarrow{f}N\xrightarrow{i_{N}}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}N\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}(g)}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}L\xrightarrow{p_{L}}L.

Finally we claim that for
f\in\operatorname{End}_{K}(\operatorname{Res}_{K}M)
we have
\operatorname{str}f=\operatorname{str}\tilde{f}
. It follows from the following commutative diagram

\begin{CD}{\mathbb{C}}@>{\varphi}>{}>M\otimes M^{*}@>{\operatorname{ev}}>{}>{\mathbb{C}}\\
@V{}V{i}V@V{}V{i}V@V{}V{i}V\\
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}{\mathbb{C}}@>{\operatorname{Ind}(\varphi)}>{}>\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}(M\otimes M^{*})@>{\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{ev})}>{}>\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}{\mathbb{C}}\\
@V{}V{p}V@V{}V{p}V@V{}V{p}V\\
{\mathbb{C}}@>{\tilde{\varphi}}>{}>M\otimes M^{*}@>{\operatorname{ev}}>{}>{\mathbb{C}}\end{CD}

where
\varphi=(f\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\operatorname{coev}
.

Assume now that
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(M,N)
is a negligible morphism and
\operatorname{Res}_{K}f
is not negligible. Then there exists
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(\operatorname{Res}_{K}N,\operatorname{Res}_{K}M)
such that

\operatorname{str}(g\circ f)\neq 0
. But then
\operatorname{str}(\tilde{g}\circ f)\neq 0
and we obtain a contradiction.

∎

Remark 2.4
.

In fact, Proposition
2.3
can be strengthened to an if and only if statement. Suppose that
K
is not splitting
G
, meaning that
{\mathbb{C}}\to\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}
does not split. Let
V\subseteq\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}
be a nontrivial indecomposable submodule with
{\mathbb{C}}\subseteq V
. Then the map
f:{\mathbb{C}}\to V
is negligible since it is not split. However upon restriction to
K
there is a splitting coming from the evaluation morphism
V\subseteq\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\text{ev}_{eK}}{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus
\operatorname{Res}_{K}(f)
is not negligible.

Theorem 2.5
.

The functor
Res^{G}_{K}:\operatorname{Rep}(G)\to\ Rep(K)
descends to a tensor functor

F:\overline{\operatorname{Rep}(G)}\to\overline{\operatorname{Rep}(K)}

between the semisimplification categories, so that
the diagram

\begin{CD}Rep(G)@>{\operatorname{Res}}>{}>Rep(K)\\
@V{}V{S}V@V{}V{S}V\\
\overline{Rep(G)}@>{F}>{}>\overline{Rep(K)}\end{CD}

is commutative.

Proof.

The existence of a functor
F
making the above diagram commutative follows from Proposition
2.3
.
Being a
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear functor between semisimple categories,
F
is automatically exact; since
F
is also symmetric monoidal, it must be faithful.
∎

2.4.
DS functor and fullness of
F

Recall that
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is called
homological

if
[x,x]
is a semisimple element of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
. By
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}^{hom}
we denote the set of all homological elements.

For any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}^{hom}
there exists a symmetric monoidal functor
DS_{x}:\operatorname{Rep}G\to\operatorname{sVec}
defined by

DS_{x}M=\frac{\operatorname{Ker}(x:M\to M)}{\operatorname{Im}(x:M\to M)\cap\operatorname{Ker}(x:M\to M)}.

Observe that
DS_{x}M
admits a natural action of the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}:=DS_{x}{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a quasireducitve supergroup and
K\subset G
is a splitting subgroup. A pair
(G,K)
is called
proper
if for some
x\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{\bar{1}}^{hom}
we have
DS_{x}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{C}}
.

Then the following Lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(G,K)
be a proper pair. Then for any
G
-module
M
we have
DS_{x}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M\simeq DS_{x}M
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that
K
is a splitting subgroup of
G
. Let
M
be an indecomposable
G
-module and
M_{0}
be a
K
-submodule of
\operatorname{Res}_{K}M
which splits as a direct summand. If
\operatorname{sdim}M_{0}\neq 0
, then
M
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M_{0}
.

Proof.

The composition of
K
-module maps

{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{coev}}M_{0}\otimes M^{*}_{0}\to M_{0}\otimes M^{*}\xrightarrow{s}M^{*}\otimes M_{0}\to M^{*}_{0}\otimes M_{0}\xrightarrow{ev}{\mathbb{C}}

is
\operatorname{sdim}(M_{0})\operatorname{Id}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\neq 0
so
{\mathbb{C}}
is a direct summand of the
K
-module
M_{0}\otimes M^{*}
. Hence
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}{\mathbb{C}}
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M_{0}\otimes M^{*}
. Since
K
is a splitting subgroup,
{\mathbb{C}}
is a direct summand of the
G
-module
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}{\mathbb{C}}
and thus a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M_{0}\otimes M^{*}
.

This implies the splitting of
M
in
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M_{0}
by the lemma below for the case
U=\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
U
and
M
be
G
-modules and
M
be indecomposable and finite-dimensional. Assume that there are morphisms

{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\alpha}U\otimes M^{*}\xrightarrow{\gamma}{\mathbb{C}},

such that
\gamma\alpha=1
. Define

\bar{\alpha}:M\xrightarrow{\alpha\otimes 1_{M}}U\otimes M^{*}\otimes M\xrightarrow{1_{U}\otimes\operatorname{ev}}U

and

\bar{\gamma}:U\xrightarrow{1_{U}\otimes\operatorname{coev}}U\otimes M\otimes M^{*}\xrightarrow{1_{U}\otimes s_{23}}U\otimes M^{*}\otimes M\xrightarrow{\gamma\otimes 1_{M}}M

Then
\bar{\gamma}\bar{\alpha}:M\to M
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Choose a basis
\{m_{i}\}
in
M
and the dual basis
\{\varphi_{i}\}
in
M^{*}
. Let
\alpha(1)=\sum_{i}u_{i}\otimes\varphi_{i}
. Then we have
\sum_{i}\gamma(u_{i},\varphi_{i})=1
.
We have that
\bar{\alpha}(m_{i})=u_{i}
, and
\bar{\gamma}(u_{i})=\sum_{j}(-1)^{m_{j}}\gamma(u_{i},\varphi_{j})m_{j}
. Then

\operatorname{str}\bar{\gamma}\bar{\alpha}=\sum_{i}\gamma(u_{i},\varphi_{i})=1.

Note that
M
is indecomposable and hence every endomorphism of
M
is either nilpotent or an isomorphism. Hence
\bar{\gamma}\bar{\alpha}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(G,K)
be some proper pair and

M
be an indecomposable
G
-module with
\operatorname{sdim}M\neq 0
. Then
\operatorname{Res}_{K}M
has exactly one indecomposable component of non-zero superdimension.

Proof.

Assume the opposite. Then we can write
\operatorname{Res}_{K}M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
where both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
have non-zero superdimension. Then by Lemma
2.7
,

DS_{x}M=DS_{x}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M=DS_{x}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M_{1}\oplus DS_{x}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M_{2}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.8
we have
DS_{x}M\subset DS_{x}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}M_{i}
for
i=1,2
. We obtain a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Assume that
(G,K)
is a proper pair.
Let
M_{1},M_{2}
be indecomposable
G
-modules of non-zero superdimension. Assume there exists an indecomposable
K
-module
V
,
\operatorname{sdim}(V)\neq 0
, such that
\operatorname{Res}_{K}M_{i}
has
V
as a direct summand for
i=1,2
. Then
M_{1}\cong M_{2}
.

Proof.

Assume that
M_{1}\not\cong M_{2}
.
By Lemma
2.8
, we have both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
as direct summands of
\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}V
, with embeddings
\iota_{i}:M_{i}\to\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}V
, and projections
\pi_{i}:\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}V\to M_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Let us show that
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}V
as well.

Indeed, let
\iota=\iota_{1}+\iota_{2}
and
\pi=\pi_{1}+\pi_{2}
. Then
\pi\circ\iota\in\operatorname{End}_{G}(M_{1}\oplus M_{2})
is
\operatorname{Id}+\pi_{2}\iota_{1}+\pi_{1}\iota_{2}
. Let us show that this is an isomorphism; in other words, that
\pi_{2}\iota_{1}+\pi_{1}\iota_{2}
is nilpotent. Here
\pi_{2}\iota_{1}:M_{1}\to M_{2}
,
\pi_{1}\iota_{2}:M_{2}\to M_{1}
. Indeed,

(\pi_{2}\iota_{1}+\pi_{1}\iota_{2})^{2}=\pi_{1}\iota_{2}\pi_{2}\iota_{1}\oplus\pi_{2}\iota_{1}\pi_{1}\iota_{2}

where the first summand is a map
M_{1}\to M_{1}
(factoring through
M_{2}
) and the second is a map
M_{2}\to M_{2}
(factoring through
M_{1}
). These maps clearly commute, and neither is an isomorphism, since
M_{1}\not\cong M_{2}
and they are indecomposable. The fact that
M_{1},M_{2}
are indecomposable implies that the commuting maps
\pi_{1}\iota_{2}\pi_{2}\iota_{1}
,
\pi_{2}\iota_{1}\pi_{1}\iota_{2}
are nilpotent, and hence
\pi_{2}\iota_{1}+\pi_{1}\iota_{2}
is nilpotent.

Thus
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}V
.

Now, since
V
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Res}_{K}M_{i}
, we have:
\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}V
is a direct summand of
\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}\operatorname{Res}_{K}M_{1}
. So
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
is a direct summands of
\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}\operatorname{Res}_{K}M_{1}
. Hence the module

DS_{x}(M_{1})\cong DS_{x}\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}\operatorname{Res}_{K}M_{1}

(the isomorphism given by Lemma
2.7
) has
DS_{x}(M_{1})\oplus DS_{x}(M_{2})
as a direct summand, which is only possible if
DS_{x}(M_{2})=0
. Yet the latter contradicts the assumption that
\operatorname{sdim}(M_{2})\neq 0
.

∎

Theorem 2.12
.

If
(G,K)
is a proper pair then the functor
F:\overline{\operatorname{Rep}G}\to\overline{\operatorname{Rep}K}
is full.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\operatorname{Rep}G}
and
\overline{\operatorname{Rep}K}
are semisimple categories, fullness is equivalent to the following two properties:

(1)

If
X
is simple then
F(X)
is simple.

(2)

If
F(X)\simeq F(Y)
for two simple
X,Y\in\overline{\operatorname{Rep}G}
then
X\simeq Y
.

On the other hand, isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\overline{\operatorname{Rep}G}
(respectively,
\overline{\operatorname{Rep}K}
)
are in bijection with isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects of non-zero superdimension in
\operatorname{Rep}G
(respectively,
\operatorname{Rep}K
).
Hence Proposition
2.10
implies (1) and Proposition
2.11
implies (2).
∎

2.5.
Main example

Recall now the theory of Sylow subgroups developed in
[
SSV
]
. A minimal splitting subgroup of
G
is called a Sylow subgroup. It is shown in
[
SSV
]
that all Sylow subgroups are conjugate under the action of
G_{0}
. Further, Theorem 9.14 and the table 9.3 in
[
SSV
]
imply the following:

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
G
be a quasireductive supergroup with Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Assume that

{\mathfrak{g}}/Z({\mathfrak{g}})
does not contain a simple ideal isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{q}}(n)
for
n\geq 3
. Let
S\subset G
be a Sylow subgroup and
K=N_{G}(S)
be the normalizer of
S
. Then
(G,K)
is a proper pair.

2.6.
Example for which
F
is not essentially surjective

Let
G=GL(n|n)
, and consider
K=GL(1|1)^{n}\subseteq G
. Then
N=N_{G}(K)=GL(1|1)^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
. We have
N/K\cong S_{n}
, and thus
\operatorname{Rep}S_{n}
is a full subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}N
.

Proposition 2.14
.

If
V
is an irreducible, nontrivial representation of
S_{n}
, then
V
is not in the essential image of
F
.

Proof.

Let
u=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}
. Then
u\in{\mathfrak{k}}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
, and further
U
is fixed by the subgroup
H\subseteq G_{0}
of invertible matrices
\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&A\end{bmatrix}
. In particular our subgroup
S_{n}\subseteq N
fixes
u
, and therefore we have a natural action of
S_{n}
on
DS_{u}(M)
for any
N
-module
M
. Clearly,
DS_{u}(V)\cong V
as an
S_{n}
-module.

We have
\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}=\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}[S_{n}]
. Let us suppose that
V
is in the image of
F
. Then
V
must split off
{\mathbb{C}}[G/K]=\operatorname{Ind}_{K}^{G}{\mathbb{C}}
as an
S_{n}
-module, where
S_{n}\subseteq N
. Hence it must also split off
DS_{u}({\mathbb{C}}[G/K])
as an
S_{n}
-module.

However by Thm. 6.9 of
[
SS
]
,
DS_{u}({\mathbb{C}}[G/K])
is naturally identified, as an
H
-module, with
H_{dR}^{\bullet}(H/T)
, where
T\subseteq H
is a maximal torus. However the
H
-module structure on
H_{dR}^{\bullet}(H/T)
is trivial, see for example
[
CE
]
In particular,
DS_{u}({\mathbb{C}}[G/K])
is trivial as an
S_{n}
-module, giving a contradiction.
∎

3.1.
Defect

Let
G
be a connected affine algebraic supergoup with basic Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of defect
1
. Here is the complete list of such supergroups (up to isogeny) : with
n>0,m>0
we have

SL(1|n+1)
,
SOSp(2|2n)
,
SOSp(3|2n)
,
SOSp(2m|2)
,
SOSp(2m+1|2)
,
D(2,1;t)
,
AG_{2}
(
G_{3}
in Kac notations) and
AB_{3}
(
F_{4}
in Kac notations).

1

1

Some of these supergoups are not simply connected, but the stable categories of a group and its simply connected cover coincide in all our examples.

Fix a Cartan subalgebra and root decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then a root
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}(1|1)
-subalgebra is a Sylow subalgebra of

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

The ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{k}
is generated by any simple module of atypicality degree
k
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}(M)
denote the ideal generated by a simple module
M
of atypicality degree
k
. Due to Lemma
4.1
we just have to show that
{\mathcal{I}}(M)
is the radical ideal.
For this we have to check that
M
lies in
{\mathcal{I}}(M\otimes M)
, which is clear since
M
is a direct summand of
M\otimes M\otimes M^{*}
. In fact, in a rigid tensor category thick ideals are always radical.
∎

4.2.
Relative projectivity

Let
H\subset G
be a pair of quasireductive supergroups. We call a
G
-module
M

H
-projective
if any of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(1)

if an exact sequence
0\to N\to R\to M\to 0
splits over
H
then it splits over
G
;

(2)

the restriction map
\operatorname{Ext}_{G}^{*}(R,M)\to\operatorname{Ext}_{K}^{*}(R,M)
is injective for any
G
-module
R
;

(3)

there exists an
H
-module
V
such that
M
is a direct summand of the induced module

\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}V
;

(4)

the natural embedding
M\to\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}M
splits.

The equivalence of the above conditions can be proven in the same way as for modular representations of finite groups.

The following proposition explains some relation between support and relative projectivity.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
M
is
S_{k}
-projective. Then
M\in{\mathcal{I}}_{k}
.

Proof.

If
M
is relatively projective with respect to
S_{k}
then
M
splits as a direct summand in
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{S_{k}}M
. Suppose that
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}^{hom}
and
\operatorname{rk}(x)>k
. Then the vector field induced by
x
does not have fixed points on
G/S_{k}
and therefore
DS_{x}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{S_{k}}M=0
, see
[
SS
]
. This implies
DS_{x}M=0
. Therefore
\operatorname{Supp}M\subset\mathbb{Y}_{G}^{k}
.
∎

4.3.
Defect group of a block

Here we again assume that
G=GL(m|n)
or
SOSp(m|2n)
.
Recall the classification of blocks, see
[
GS
]
(Section 5) and Section 2.7.
Note that the Sylow subgroup of
G_{\mathcal{B}}
is isomorphic to
S_{k}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{0}
denote the principal block in
\operatorname{Rep}G_{{\mathcal{B}}}
.
We denote by
F:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}_{0}
the functor which establishes equivalence of blocks.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a block in
\operatorname{Rep}G
of atypicality degree
k
.
Then every
M\in\mathcal{B}
is
S_{k}
-projective.

Proof.

The functor
F^{*}
inverse to
F
is the left adjoint to
F
. It is not difficult to see that

F^{*}(V)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{G_{\mathcal{B}}}V),

where
p_{\mathcal{B}}
denotes the projection to the block
\mathcal{B}
.
Since
F^{*}F(M)=M
, we obtain that
M
is a direct summand in
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{G_{\mathcal{B}}}F(M)
and hence
M
is
G_{\mathcal{B}}
-projective.
But
S_{k}
is a splitting subgroup of
G_{\mathcal{B}}
and hence any
G_{\mathcal{B}}
-module is
S_{k}
-projective. Thus,
M
is
S_{k}
-projective.
∎

Abstract
. In this paper we examine the remainder terms of L p L^{p} -Hardy inequalities for magnetic p p -Laplacians and provide certain sharp lower estimates. In particular, clarifying a recent result obtained by Cazacu, Krejčiřík, Lam and Laptev, we also answer two of their open problems by determining the sharp constants.

1.
Introduction

The following well-known Euclidean
L^{p}
-Hardy inequalities (see
[
HLP52
]
for example) hold in any dimension
d\geq 2
and for every
1<p<d
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq\mu_{p,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}dx,\quad\forall\,u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where the sharp constant
\mu_{p,d}
is equal to
(\frac{d-p}{p})^{p}
. Recently there is a rising interest in the improved Hardy inequality for the
magnetic
p
-Laplacian
which we now recall.

Let
B:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a smooth matrix-valued function representing the exact magnetic field, then there exists a smooth magnetic potential
A:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that
dA=B
(see for example
[
CK16
]
and
[
CKLL24
, Section 1.2]
for more details).

Definition 1
.

The magnetic p-Laplacian is formally defined on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\Delta_{A,p}u:=\mathrm{div}_{A}(|\nabla_{A}u|^{p-2}\nabla_{A}u),

where the magnetic gradient and magnetic divergence are given by

\nabla_{A}u:=\nabla u+iA(x)u,\quad\mathrm{div}_{A}F:=\mathrm{div}F+iA\cdot F.

Here,
1<p<\infty
and
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
denotes a smooth vector field.

The associated quadratic form
h_{A,p}
of the magnetic
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{A,p}
, with its form domain denoted by
\mathcal{D}(h_{A,p})
, is then defined by

h_{A,p}[u]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{A}u|^{p}dx,\quad u\in\mathcal{D}(h_{A,p}):=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|},

where the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
with respect to which the closure is taken is given by

\left\|u\right\|:=\left(h_{A,p}[u]+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

In a recent interesting paper, C. Cazacu, D. Krejčiřík, N. Lam, and A. Laptev established for the magnetic p-Laplacians the following improved Hardy inequalities.

Theorem 2

(
[
CKLL24
]
, Theorem 1.2)
.

Let
2\leq p<d
and
B
be a smooth and closed magnetic field with
B\neq 0
.
Then there exists a constant
c_{p}>0
such that for any vector field
A
with
dA=B
, and for any
u\in\mathcal{D}(h_{A,p})
,

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{A}u|^{p}dx-\mu_{p,d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}dx\geq c_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla_{A}\left(u|x|^{\frac{d-p}{p}}\right)\right|^{p}|x|^{p-d}dx.

The constant
c_{p}
is given by

(2)

c_{p}=\inf_{(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}\frac{[t^{2}+s^{2}+2s+1]^{\frac{p}{2}}-1-ps}{[t^{2}+s^{2}]^{\frac{p}{2}}}\in(0,1].

The “improved” Hardy inequality (
1
) also holds when
p\in(1,2)
with the same constant
c_{p}
in (
2
), however,
c_{p}\equiv 0
in this case (see
[
CKLL24
, Proposition 3.1]
).

Remark 3
.

Let
p=3
and take
t=0
and
s=-3
in (
2
). Then we see

c_{3}\leq\frac{(s+1)^{3}-1-3s}{s^{3}}=0,

which falsifies
c_{3}>0
. The aim of this paper is to determine the sharp
c_{p}
in (
1
).

Note that in
[
CKLL24
]
, (
1
) was reduced to the following minimisation problem.

Question
.

What is the sharp constant
c_{p}
for the following algebraic inequality:

(3)

C_{p}(x,y):=|x|^{p}-|x-y|^{p}-p|x-y|^{p-2}\Re((x-y)\cdot\overline{y})\geq c_{p}|y|^{p},\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{d},

where
\Re
stands for the real part of a complex number?

Inspired by the work
[
IIO17
]
of N. Ioku, M. Ishiwata and T. Ozawa,
we propose an integral representation of
C_{p}(x,y)
which allows us to determine the sharp
c_{p}
in (
3
).
Trivially, (
3
) holds for
y=0
,
x=0
or
y=x
. Also,
c_{p}=1
for
p=2
. Without loss of generality, we shall not analyze these trivial cases in what follows.

Firstly, for
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
we introduce the following quantity

R_{p}(\xi,\eta)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\xi-\eta|^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{p}|\eta|^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}|\xi|^{p}-|\xi|^{p-2}\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})\right),&\text{if }\xi\neq\eta,\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2}|\xi|^{p-2},&\text{if }\xi=\eta,\end{cases}

where
p>1
and
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1-\frac{1}{p}
.

Our first result is stated as follows.

Theorem 4
.

We have the following integral representation: for any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,

(4)

\displaystyle R_{p}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\theta d\theta

\displaystyle+(p-2)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-4}\frac{|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}-\Re^{2}(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})}{|\xi-\eta|^{2}}\theta d\theta,

and if
p\geq 2
,

R_{p}(\xi,\eta)\geq(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\theta d\theta.

The above inequality becomes an equality when
\xi=\lambda\eta
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 5
.

The case
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}
was considered in
[
IIO17
]
, in particular,

R_{p}(\xi,\eta)=(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\theta d\theta.

Remark 6
.

Using Theorem
4
and setting
\xi=x-y
,
\eta=x
, we have

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{p}(x,y)}{|y|^{p}}

\displaystyle=p\frac{R_{p}(x-y,x)}{|y|^{p-2}}

\displaystyle=p(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}\left|\theta\frac{y}{|y|}-\frac{x}{|y|}\right|^{p-2}\theta d\theta

\displaystyle+(p-2)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta y-x|^{p-4}\frac{|x-y|^{2}|x|^{2}-\Re^{2}((x-y)\cdot\bar{x})}{|y|^{p}}\theta d\theta,

and if
p\geq 2
,

\frac{C_{p}(x,y)}{|y|^{p}}\geq p(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}\left|\theta\frac{y}{|y|}-\frac{x}{|y|}\right|^{p-2}\theta d\theta.

The above inequality becomes an equality when
x=\lambda y
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Our second result is stated as follows.

Theorem 7
.

i) Let
p\geq 2
. The sharp constant
c_{p}
in (
3
) is determined as

c_{p}=(p-1)(1-k_{0})^{p}+pk_{0}(1-k_{0})^{p-1}+k_{0}^{p}>0,

in the case of
x=k_{0}y
, where
k_{0}=\frac{r_{0}}{1+r_{0}}
and
r_{0}
is the solution of the equation

r^{p-1}-(p-1)r-(p-2)=0.

ii) Let
1<p<2
. The sharp constant
c_{p}
in (
3
) is equal to 0.

Remark 8
.

Let
p=3
. We obtain
r_{0}=\sqrt{2}+1
,
k_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
, and

c_{3}=2\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)^{3}+3\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)^{3}=2-\sqrt{2}.

By the reduction in
[
CKLL24
]
, we have the following

Corollary 9
.

Theorem
2
for
1<p<d
holds with
c_{p}
determined in Theorem
7
.

This corollary answers two open problems posed in
[
CKLL24
, Remark 1.1]
.

2.
Proofs of Theorem
4
and Theorem
7

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Trivially, (
4
) holds for
\xi=\eta
. For
\xi\neq\eta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p}=p|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\Re\left((\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta)\cdot\overline{(\xi-\eta)}\right)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\Re\left((\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta)\cdot\bar{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=(p-2)|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-4}\left(\theta|\xi|^{2}+(\theta-1)|\eta|^{2}+(1-2\theta)\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\theta\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})+(1-\theta)|\eta|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\Re\left((\xi-\eta)\cdot\bar{\eta}\right).

By a direct calculation, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\theta|\xi|^{2}+(\theta-1)|\eta|^{2}+(1-2\theta)\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})\right)\left(\theta\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})+(1-\theta)|\eta|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\theta^{2}|\xi|^{2}\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})+(\theta-1)\theta|\eta|^{2}\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})+\theta(1-2\theta)\Re^{2}(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})

\displaystyle+\theta(1-\theta)|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}-(1-\theta)^{2}|\eta|^{2}|\eta|^{2}+(1-2\theta)(1-\theta)\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})|\eta|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{2}\Re((\xi-\eta)\cdot\bar{\eta})+\theta|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}-\theta\Re^{2}(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta}).

Since

[|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p}]_{\theta=0}^{1}=|\xi|^{p}-|\eta|^{p}

and

[|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\Re((\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta)\cdot\bar{\eta})]_{\theta=0}^{1}=|\xi|^{p-2}\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})-|\eta|^{p-2}\Re(\eta\cdot\bar{\eta}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}|\eta|^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}|\xi|^{p}-|\xi|^{p-2}\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\left(|\xi|^{p}-|\eta|^{p}\right)-\left(|\xi|^{p-2}\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})-|\eta|^{p-2}\Re(\eta\cdot\bar{\eta})\right)

\displaystyle=(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\Re((\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta)\cdot\overline{(\xi-\eta)})d\theta

\displaystyle-(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}\Re\left((\xi-\eta)\cdot\bar{\eta}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle+(p-2)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-4}(|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}-\Re^{2}(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta}))\theta d\theta

\displaystyle=(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}|\xi-\eta|^{2}\theta d\theta

\displaystyle+(p-2)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-4}(|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}-\Re^{2}(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta}))\theta d\theta,

and if
p\geq 2
,

\frac{1}{p}|\eta|^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}|\xi|^{p}-|\xi|^{p-2}\Re(\xi\cdot\bar{\eta})\geq(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}|\theta\xi+(1-\theta)\eta|^{p-2}|\xi-\eta|^{2}\theta d\theta.

The above inequality becomes an equality when
\xi=\lambda\eta
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7
.

If
1<p<2
, taking
x=ky
with
k\to-\infty
in (
6
),
we obtain
c_{p}=0
.
Suppose
p\geq 2
now.
From (
5
) and the reasoning in Remark
6
, we conclude that the sharp constant
c_{p}
is attained only if
x=ky
with
k\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus we obtain

(6)

\displaystyle c_{p}=\inf_{x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{d}}\frac{C_{p}(x,y)}{|y|^{p}}

\displaystyle=p(p-1)\inf_{x=ky}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\theta\frac{y}{|y|}-\frac{x}{|y|}\right|^{p-2}\theta d\theta

\displaystyle=p(p-1)\inf_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\theta-k\right|^{p-2}\theta d\theta.

Recall that we can omit the discussion for
k\in\{0,1\}
.

For the case
0<k<1
, we obtain

\displaystyle p(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}\left|\theta-k\right|^{p-2}\theta d\theta

\displaystyle=p(p-1)\int_{0}^{k}(k-\theta)^{p-2}\theta d\theta+p(p-1)\int_{k}^{1}(\theta-k)^{p-2}\theta d\theta

\displaystyle=k^{p}+pk(1-k)^{p-1}+(p-1)(1-k)^{p}.

Introduce

f(x):=x^{p}+px(1-x)^{p-1}+(p-1)(1-x)^{p},

thus

f^{\prime}(x)=p\left(x^{p-1}+(1-x)^{p-1}-(p-1)(1-x)^{p-2}\right).

Note that
f^{\prime}(0)=p(2-p)\leq 0
and
f^{\prime}(1)=p>0
.
Suppose
f^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
and
x_{0}=\frac{r_{0}}{r_{0}+1}
with
r_{0}>0
, then the equation
f^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
can be rewritten as

(7)

r_{0}^{p-1}-(p-1)r_{0}-(p-2)=0.

Note that

(r^{p-1}-(p-1)r-(p-2))^{\prime}=(p-1)(r^{p-2}-1),

which alternates the sign passing
r=1
.
Moreover,

(r^{p-1}-(p-1)r-(p-2))|_{r=0}=-(p-2)\leq 0

and

(r^{p-1}-(p-1)r-(p-2))|_{r=+\infty}=+\infty.

We then conclude that
r_{0}
is unique for
f^{\prime}(\frac{r_{0}}{r_{0}+1})=0
.
For the case
k>1
, we obtain

p(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}(k-\theta)^{p-2}\theta d\theta>p(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)^{p-2}\theta d\theta=1=f(1).

For the case
k<0
, we obtain

p(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}(\theta-k)^{p-2}\theta d\theta>p(p-1)\int_{0}^{1}\theta^{p-1}d\theta=p-1=f(0).

Thus
c_{p}=f(x_{0})
.

Now we check
f(x_{0})
is always positive. Suppose that
f(x_{0})=0
, we obtain

(8)

\left(\frac{r_{0}}{r_{0}+1}\right)^{p}+p\frac{r_{0}}{r_{0}+1}\left(\frac{1}{r_{0}+1}\right)^{p-1}+(p-1)\left(\frac{1}{r_{0}+1}\right)^{p}=0.

Using (
7
), (
8
) can be rewritten as

(p-1)\left(r_{0}^{2}+2r_{0}+1\right)=0,

which is a contradiction to
r_{0}>0
.
This proves Theorem
7
.
∎

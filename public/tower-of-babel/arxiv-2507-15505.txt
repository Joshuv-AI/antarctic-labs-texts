Pavel Turek and Jialin Wang

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime and n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 be a positive integer. We establish new formulae for the decompositions of the first p − 1 p-1 symmetric powers of the Specht module S ( n − 1 , 1 ) S^{(n-1,1)} and the irreducible module D ( n − 1 , 1 ) D^{(n-1,1)} in characteristic p p as direct sums of Young permutation modules. As an application of the formulae, we show that these symmetric powers have Specht filtration and find the vertices of their indecomposable summands. Our main tool, constructed in this paper, is a lift of a splitting map of a short exact sequence of certain symmetric powers to a splitting map of a short exact sequence of higher symmetric powers. This is a general construction, which can be applied to a broader family of modules.

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of prime characteristic
p
,
G
be a finite group, and
V
be a
KG
-module. The symmetric powers of
V
are again
KG
-modules, which can be viewed as quotients of the tensor powers of
V
. Moreover, for
1\leq r<p
,
\Sym^{r}V
is a direct summand of
V^{\otimes r}
. In this paper, we focus on symmetric powers of those
KG
-modules
V
which contain the trivial module, denoted by
K
, as a quotient or a submodule. For such
V
and any integer
r\geq 1
, there is a surjective map
\partial_{r}:\Sym^{r}V\to\Sym^{r-1}V
and an injective map
\mul_{r-1}:\Sym^{r-1}V\to\Sym^{r}V
, respectively (see
Example

2.4
and equations (
1
) and (
2
), respectively). Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a field of prime characteristic
p
,
V
be a
KG
-module and
r
be a positive integer which is not congruent to
0
or
-1
modulo
p
.

(i)

Suppose that there is a surjective map
\varepsilon:V\to K
of
KG
-modules. If the map
\partial_{r}:\Sym^{r}V\to\Sym^{r-1}V
splits, then so does the map
\partial_{r+1}:\Sym^{r+1}V\to\Sym^{r}V
.

(ii)

Suppose that there is an injective map
\iota:K\to V
of
KG
-modules. If the map
\mul_{r-1}:\Sym^{r-1}V\to\Sym^{r}V
splits, then so does the map
\mul_{r}:\Sym^{r}V\to\Sym^{r+1}V
.

Theorem

1.1
is easily applicable in inductive arguments. Indeed, if we show that
\partial_{r}
splits for
r=r_{0}
not divisible by
p
, then a simple induction on
r
with conjunction with
Theorem

1.1
(i) shows that
\partial_{r}
splits for
all

r_{0}\leq r\leq(k+1)p-1
where
kp<r_{0}\leq(k+1)p-1
.
Theorem

1.1
(ii) provides an analogous result for maps
\mul_{r}
.

It is this consequence of
Theorem

1.1
that we apply to permutation modules and to the Specht module
S^{(n-1,1)}
of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
for
p\mid n
. For permutation modules, as shown in
Lemma

2.6
, one can take
r_{0}=2
to obtain the following striking result.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
K
be a field of prime characteristic
p
and
V
be a permutation
KG
-module. Then for any
2\leq r\leq p-1
the surjective map
\partial_{r}:\Sym^{r}V\to\Sym^{r-1}V
splits. In particular,
\Sym^{r-1}V
is a summand of
\Sym^{r}V
.

We use
Theorem

1.1
applied with
V=S^{(n-1,1)}
(when
p\mid n
) and
V=M^{(n-1,1)}
, the natural permutation module of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, to prove formulae for symmetric powers
\Sym^{r}
of
S^{(n-1,1)}
(for
2\leq r\leq p-1
) and its simple head
D^{(n-1,1)}
(for
3\leq r\leq p-1
) in the representation ring of
K\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(see

3.6
). Consequently, we deduce that these symmetric powers decompose as direct sums of indecomposable Young modules; in particular, they have Specht filtration as stated in
Theorem

3.10
. As further applications of the formulae, we show positivity of several
p
-Kostka numbers in
Corollary

4.3
and find vertices of the indecomposable summands of the symmetric powers in question as stated below.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
K
is a field of characteristic
p
and
n\geq 3
is an integer divisible by
p
. The indecomposable summands of the symmetric powers
\Sym^{r}S^{(n-1,1)}
with
2\leq r\leq p-1
and of the symmetric powers
\Sym^{r}D^{(n-1,1)}
with

have a vertex given by a Sylow

-subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n-p}
or
\mathfrak{S}_{n-2p}
.

Observe that the Sylow

-subgroups of
\mathfrak{S}_{n-p}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{n-2p}
are
properly
contained in the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Thus, for

, the

and

are summands of modules induced from proper subgroups of a Sylow

-subgroup of

Background

The symmetric powers of
KG
-modules are popular objects of study in invariant theory, due to their connections to polynomial invariants, which, in the language of representation theory, are the elements of the largest subspace of symmetric powers isomorphic to direct sums of copies of the trivial
KG
-module. We direct the reader to
[
Ben93
]
for a detailed exposition of the many known results regarding the polynomial invariants in characteristic
0
.

For a positive characteristic, results about symmetric powers and polynomial invariants applicable to all finite groups are rare. We mention the finiteness result of Karagueuzian and Symonds
[
KS07
]
, which asserts that for any finite group
G
and finite field
K
, there are only finitely many non-isomorphic indecomposable summands of symmetric powers of a fixed
KG
-module
V
. We encourage the reader to see the background section of
[
KS07
]
for detailed references to results about symmetric powers of particular groups in positive characteristic; here we provide only a summary.

Almkvist and Fossum
[
AF78
]
studied symmetric and exterior powers of modules of a cyclic group of order
p
in characteristic
p
. Almkvist later applied the results of
[
AF78
]
to study the number of indecomposable summands of these symmetric powers in
[
Alm78
,
Alm81
]
. The work of Almkvist and Fossum was revisited and generalised to groups with Sylow
p
-subgroups of order
p
by Hughes and Kemper
[
HK00
,
HK01
]
. A careful study of symmetric and exterior powers of the indecomposable modules of SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
in defining characteristic appears in Kouwenhoven
[
Kou90
]
. An earlier work in a similar direction by Glover
[
Glo78
]
concerns module structures of symmetric powers of the natural modules of groups SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, GL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and the semigroup Mat
{}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

The symmetric powers are an example of (polynomial) Schur functors, certain endofunctors of the module categories of groups, which can be used to construct all polynomial modules of general linear groups. A detailed study of the decompositions into indecomposable summands of modules obtained by applying Schur functors to the modules of SL
{}_{2}(K)
in characteristic
0
has been done by Paget and Wildon
[
PW21
]
. For positive characteristic
p
, formulae for computing such decompositions for ‘small’ Schur functors were found by the first author
[
Tur24
, Theorem 5.3]
following the strategy used in
[
Ben17
, Section 2.9]
to find analogous results for the cyclic group of order
p
.

Our new results about the vertices of the indecomposable summands of the symmetric powers of
S^{(n-1,1)}
and
D^{(n-1,1)}
accompany the existing results about the vertices of the exterior powers of the same modules. These exterior powers coincide with modules of the form
S^{(n-k,1^{k})}
and
D^{(n-k,1^{k})}
, vertices of which were initially studied by Murphy and Peel in 1984
[
MP84
]
. Since then, the vertices of all the modules
D^{(n-k,1^{k})}
and most of the modules
S^{(n-k,1^{k})}
were found (see
[
Wil03
,
Dan07
,
MZ07
,
Wil10
,
DG15
,
GLW16
]
). Vertices of different modules of symmetric groups, the indecomposable summands of the Foulkes modules, are found in
[
GW15
,
Gia15
]
, and are used to prove new results about the decomposition numbers of symmetric groups.

2.
Endomorphisms of symmetric powers

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p
,
G
be a group, and
V
be a finite-dimensional
KG
-module. We write
\Sym V
for the graded symmetric
K
-algebra
\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Sym^{i}V
. One can think of
\Sym V
as a polynomial
K
-algebra: if
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}
is a basis of
V
, then
\Sym V\cong K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}]
.

The symmetric algebra
\Sym V
has a natural structure of a
KG
-module and a structure of a Hopf algebra. Moreover, the multiplication and comultiplication of this Hopf algebra, which we denote by
\m
and
\Delta
, respectively, are maps of
KG
-modules. For a non-negative integer
a
, we further write
\Delta_{a}
for the composition of the comultiplication followed by the projection to
\Sym^{a}V\otimes\Sym V
. Thus,
\Delta_{a}
restricted to
\Sym^{d}V
is the zero map if
d<a
, and its image lies in
\Sym^{a}V\otimes\Sym^{d-a}V
if
d\geq a
.

We describe these maps using the identification
\Sym V\cong K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}]
. To state this description, we need further notation. A
composition of
d
of length
t
is a sequence
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{t})
of
t
non-negative integers which add up to
d
. We write
\alpha!
for
\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!\cdots\alpha_{t}!
,
x^{\alpha}
for the monomial
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{t}^{\alpha_{t}}
in
\Sym V
and
\partial^{\alpha}
for the differential operator
\partial^{d}/(\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\partial x_{t}^{\alpha_{t}})
of
\Sym V
. Finally, write
\Com_{t}(d)
for the set of compositions of
d
of length
t
. We use the term
composition of length
t
to refer to compositions of some non-negative integer
d
of length
t
.

For compositions
\alpha
and
\beta
of length
t
we denote by
\alpha\pm\beta
the component-wise sum (or difference) of
\alpha
and
\beta
. Note that
\alpha+\beta
is always a composition of length
t
, however,
\alpha-\beta
may not be a composition of length
t
. The multiplication
\m
then sends
x^{\alpha}\otimes x^{\beta}
to
x^{\alpha+\beta}
. Writing
\alpha\leq\beta
when
\alpha_{i}\leq\beta_{i}
for all
i\leq t
, the map
\Delta_{a}
sends
x^{\beta}
to
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Com_{t}(a)\\
\alpha\leq\beta\end{subarray}}\binom{\beta_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\binom{\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\binom{\beta_{t}}{\alpha_{t}}x^{\alpha}\otimes x^{\beta-\alpha}
.

The following construction is the main tool used in this paper. Given a homomorphism
\phi:\Sym^{a}V\to\Sym^{b}V
we define its
degree-less lift

\Psi(\phi)
as the endomorphism of the
KG
-module
\Sym V
which acts on the submodule
\Sym^{d}V
as zero if
d<a
, and as the following composition

\Sym^{d}V\xrightarrow{\Delta_{a}}\Sym^{a}V\otimes\Sym^{d-a}V\xrightarrow{\phi\otimes\id}\Sym^{b}V\otimes\Sym^{d-a}V\xrightarrow{\m}\Sym^{d+b-a}V

if
d\geq a
. From the definition, it is clear that
\Psi(\phi)|_{\Sym^{a}V}=\phi
.

Example 2.1
.

For
\phi=\id:\Sym^{a}V\to\Sym^{a}V
, we get that
\Psi(\phi)=\m\circ\Delta_{a}
. Thus,
\Psi(\phi)
acts on
\Sym^{d}A
as a scalar multiplication by
\binom{d}{a}
: indeed
\Psi(\phi)
sends
x^{\beta}\in\Sym^{d}V
to
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Com_{t}(a)\\
\alpha\leq\beta\end{subarray}}\binom{\beta_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\binom{\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\binom{\beta_{t}}{\alpha_{t}}x^{\beta}=\binom{d}{a}x^{\beta}
, where the equality follows from the argument that choosing
a
objects out of
d
objects is the same as choosing
\alpha_{1}
objects from the first
\beta_{1}
objects,
\alpha_{2}
objects from the next
\beta_{2}
objects and so on, for some
\alpha\in\Com_{t}(a)
such that
\alpha\leq\beta
.

The isomorphism
\Sym V\cong K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}]
provides us with a practical description of the degree-less lift.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\phi:\Sym^{a}V\to\Sym^{b}V
be a homomorphism of
KG
-modules. After the identification
\Sym V\cong K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}]
, we obtain that
\Psi(\phi)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Com_{t}(a)}\phi(x^{\alpha})\partial^{\alpha}/\alpha!
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
\alpha!=0
in
K
, we interpret the map
\partial^{\alpha}/\alpha!
as the map which sends monomial
x^{\beta}
to
\binom{\beta_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\binom{\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\binom{\beta_{t}}{\alpha_{t}}x^{\beta-\alpha}
if
\beta\geq\alpha
and to zero otherwise. Note that
\partial^{\alpha}/\alpha!
has the same effect even if
\alpha!\neq 0
.

Proof.

Both maps,
\Psi(\phi)
and
\sum_{\alpha\in\Com_{t}(a)}\phi(x^{\alpha})\partial^{\alpha}/\alpha!
, vanish on polynomials of degree less than
a
. From the definition of
\Psi(\phi)
and the above discussion, for any
\beta\in\Com_{t}(d)
with
d\geq a
, the monomial
x^{\beta}
is sent by
\Psi(\phi)
to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Com_{t}(a)\\
\alpha\leq\beta\end{subarray}}\binom{\beta_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}\binom{\beta_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\binom{\beta_{t}}{\alpha_{t}}x^{\beta-\alpha}\phi(x^{\alpha}).

This coincides with
\sum_{\alpha\in\Com_{t}(a)}\phi(x^{\alpha})\partial^{\alpha}x^{\beta}/\alpha!
(see
Remark

2.3
), finishing the proof.
∎

We apply
Lemma

2.2
to two families of modules which form the main focus of the remainder of the paper. In the examples and the rest of the paper, we write
K
for the trivial
KG
-modules.

Example 2.4
.

Let
V
be a
KG
-module of dimension
t
.

(i)

Suppose that there is a surjective map
\varepsilon:V\to K
of
KG
-modules. Let
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}
be a basis of
V
such that
\varepsilon(x_{i})=1
for all
1\leq i\leq t
. By
Lemma

2.2
, the endomorphism
\partial:=\Psi(\varepsilon)
of the
KG
-module
\Sym V\cong K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}]
equals the sum of differential operators
\sum_{i\leq t}\partial/\partial x_{i}
.

(ii)

Suppose that there is an injective map
\iota:K\to V
of
KG
-modules. Let
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{t}
be a basis of
V
such that
\iota(1)=\sum_{i\leq t}e_{i}
. By
Lemma

2.2
, the endomorphism
\mul:=\Psi(\iota)
of the
KG
-module
\Sym V\cong K[e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{t}]
equals the multiplication by
\sum_{i\leq t}e_{i}
.

Continuing with
Example

2.4
(i), let
W
be the kernel of
\varepsilon
. The inclusion of
W
in
V
gives rise to a degree-preserving injective map
\Sym W\to\Sym V
of
KG
-modules. Using
[
BL14
, Lemma 3]
or direct calculations, one obtains a short exact sequence

(1)

0\to\Sym^{r}W\to\Sym^{r}V\xrightarrow{\partial_{r}}\Sym^{r-1}V\to 0

for any
r\geq 1
, where
\partial_{r}
is the map
\partial
from
Example

2.4
(i) restricted to
\Sym^{r}V
.

Similarly, if
W
is the cokernel of
\iota
in
Example

2.4
(ii), then there is a degree-preserving surjective map
\Sym V\to\Sym W
and by
[
BL14
, Lemma 3]
one obtains a short exact sequence

(2)

0\to\Sym^{r-1}V\xrightarrow{\mul_{r-1}}\Sym^{r}V\to\Sym^{r}W\to 0

for any
r\geq 1
, where
\mul_{r-1}
is the map
\mul
from
Example

2.4
(ii) restricted to
\Sym^{r-1}V
. While the short exact sequences (
1
) and (
2
) seem dual to each other, this is only the case for
r<p
. Thus, it is necessary to treat both sequences separately.

To prove our first main result, we use the following computational lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
r
be a positive integer and
V
a finite-dimensional
KG
-module.

(i)

Suppose that there exists a surjection
\varepsilon:V\to K
and let
\partial_{r}
be the restriction of
\partial=\Psi(\varepsilon)
to
\Sym^{r}V
. If
\phi:\Sym^{r-1}V\to\Sym^{r}V
is a map of
KG
-modules such that
\partial_{r}\circ\phi=\id
, then the commutator
[\partial,\Psi(\phi)]:\Sym V\to\Sym V
acts on
\Sym^{d}V
by multiplication by
\binom{d}{r-1}
.

(ii)

Suppose that there exists an injection
\iota:K\to V
and let
\mul_{r-1}
be the restriction of
\mul=\Psi(\iota)
to
\Sym^{r-1}V
. If
\phi:\Sym^{r}V\to\Sym^{r-1}V
is a map of
KG
-modules such that
\phi\circ\mul_{r-1}=\id
, then the commutator
[\Psi(\phi),\mul]:\Sym V\to\Sym V
acts on
\Sym^{d}V
by multiplication by
\binom{d}{r-1}
.

Proof.

Recall from
Example

2.4
(i) that
\partial=\sum_{i\leq t}\partial/\partial x_{i}
, where
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}
is a basis of
V
as in
Example

2.4
(i). By
Lemma

2.2
and the product rule, we see that in (i)

[\partial,\Psi(\phi)]=\sum_{\alpha\in\Com_{t}(r-1)}\partial(\phi(x^{\alpha}))\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}.

For
\alpha\in\Com_{t}(r-1)
, let
f_{\alpha}=\partial(\phi(x^{\alpha}))=[\partial,\Psi(\phi)](x^{\alpha})
, which is a polynomial in
K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}]
of degree
r-1
. Since
\Psi(\phi)
vanishes on
\Sym^{r-2}V
and acts as
\phi
on
\Sym^{r-1}V
, from
\partial_{r}\circ\phi=\id
, we conclude that
[\partial,\Psi(\phi)]
restricted to
\Sym^{r-1}V
becomes the identity map. Thus
f_{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}
for any composition
\alpha\in\Com_{t}(r-1)
. In turn, by
Lemma

2.2
,
[\partial,\Psi(\phi)]=\sum_{\alpha\in\Com_{t}(r-1)}x^{\alpha}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}
is the degree-less lift of the identity map on
\Sym^{r-1}V
. The result now follows from
Example

2.1
.

The situation in (ii) is analogous. Recall from
Example

2.4
(ii) that
\mul
is the multiplication by
\sum_{i\leq t}e_{i}
, where
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{t}
is a basis of
V
as in
Example

2.4
(ii). One then computes

[\Psi(\phi),\mul]=\sum_{\beta\in\Com_{t}(r)}\phi(e^{\beta})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leq t\\
\beta_{i}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\beta_{i}\frac{\partial^{\beta-\delta^{i}}}{\beta!}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Com_{t}(r-1)}\left(\sum_{i\leq t}\phi(e_{i}e^{\alpha})\right)\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\alpha!},

where
\delta^{i}
is the composition of
1
of length
t
with
\delta_{i}^{i}=1
.

Similarly as above,
\Psi(\phi)
vanishes on
\Sym^{r-1}S
and acts as
\phi
on
\Sym^{r}S
, and
\phi\circ\mul_{r-1}=\id
; thus
[\Psi(\phi),\mul]
restricted to
\Sym^{r-1}S
is the identity. The result follows as in (i).
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
\phi:\Sym^{r-1}V\to\Sym^{r}V
be the splitting of
\partial_{r}
in (i). We will show that

\theta=\frac{1}{r}\left(\Psi(\phi)-\frac{\Psi(\phi)^{2}\circ\partial}{r+1}\right),

restricted to
\Sym^{r}V
is the desired splitting. Using
Lemma

2.5
(i), for
v\in\Sym^{d}V
we compute

\displaystyle r\partial\circ\theta(v)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle[\partial,\Psi(\phi)](v)+\Psi(\phi)\circ\partial(v)

\displaystyle-\frac{[\partial,\Psi(\phi)]\circ\Psi(\phi)\circ\partial(v)+\Psi(\phi)\circ[\partial,\Psi(\phi)]\circ\partial(v)+\Psi(\phi)^{2}\circ\partial^{2}(v)}{r+1}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\binom{d}{r-1}v+\left(1-\frac{\binom{d}{r-1}+\binom{d-1}{r-1}}{r+1}\right)\Psi(\phi)\circ\partial(v)-\frac{\Psi(\phi)^{2}\circ\partial^{2}(v)}{r+1}.

In particular, if
d=r
, then the coefficient of
v
becomes
r
, the coefficient of
\Psi(\phi)\circ\partial(v)
becomes
0
and
\Psi(\phi)^{2}\circ\partial^{2}(v)=0
as
\Psi(\phi)
vanishes on polynomials of degree
r-2
. Thus
\partial\circ\theta(v)=v
for any
v\in\Sym^{r}V
, as required.

Now suppose that
\phi:\Sym^{r}V\to\Sym^{r-1}V
is a splitting of
\mul_{r-1}
in (ii). This time, we show that

\theta=\frac{1}{r}\left(\Psi(\phi)-\frac{\mul\circ\Psi(\phi)^{2}}{r+1}\right),

restricted to
\Sym^{r+1}V
is the desired splitting. Using
Lemma

2.5
(ii), for
v\in\Sym^{d}V
we compute

\displaystyle r\theta\circ\mul(v)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle[\Psi(\phi),\mul](v)+\mul\circ\Psi(\phi)(v)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mul\circ\Psi(\phi)\circ[\Psi(\phi),\mul](v)+\mul\circ[\Psi(\phi),\mul]\circ\Psi(\phi)(v)+\mul^{2}\circ\Psi(\phi)^{2}(v)}{r+1}

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\binom{d}{r-1}v+\left(1-\frac{\binom{d}{r-1}+\binom{d-1}{r-1}}{r+1}\right)\mul\circ\Psi(\phi)(v)-\frac{\mul^{2}\circ\Psi(\phi)^{2}(v)}{r+1}.

As
\Psi(\phi)^{2}
vanishes on polynomials of degree

, similarly to (i), we conclude that

for any

, as required.
∎

3.1.
Background on the symmetric group

We recall the necessary background from the representation theory of symmetric groups for this paper. For a more detailed account of this area, see, for instance,
[

Let

be a non-negative integer. A

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{l})
of

of
\lambda
denoted by

is the number of parts of
\lambda

of

denoted by

for any

if each of its entries repeats at most

-times. And it is

if for any positive integer

\lambda
and

of

.

Let

denote the symmetric group indexed by

and for a partition
\lambda
of

be its

\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{1}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}}
. We fix an underlying field

. The
Young permutation module

is the permutation

-module with a permutation basis given by the left cosets of
\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}
(permuted naturally by

). When the underlying field

has characteristic

, the irreducible

-modules are given by the

indexed by partitions

of
n
. The Specht module

is defined as a submodule of

with a characteristic free basis given by the set of
\lambda
-polytabloids. Moreover, the Specht modules are indecomposable if

has positive characteristic

. Suppose now

is a field of positive characteristic

-modules are the (irreducible) heads of Specht modules labelled by
p
-regular partitions of

-regular partition

by

.

The final family of

-modules we need to introduce are the indecomposable

indexed by partitions of

. James

showed that every Young permutation module

decomposes as a sum of Young modules

Throughout we assume

In the spirit of (

Lemma 3.12

Let

Hence

4.
Application to calculations of
p
-Kostka numbers

For a partition
\lambda
of
n
, we have a decomposition of the Young permutation module
M^{\lambda}
into Young modules as follows,

M^{\lambda}\cong Y^{\lambda}\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu\rhd\lambda}[M^{\lambda}:Y^{\mu}]Y^{\mu}.

The multiplicities
[M^{\lambda}:Y^{\mu}]
are called
p
-Kostka numbers
.

Fix a positive integer
n
divisible by
p
. In this section, we show positivity of some
p
-Kostka numbers using the formulae we obtained in
Example

3.8
. In particular, we use the equation

[\Sym^{4}D^{(n-1,1)}]=2[M^{(n-2,2)}]+[M^{(n-3,2,1)}]+[M^{(n-4,4)}]-[M^{(n-2,1^{2})}]-2[M^{(n-3,3)}].

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers with p-adic expansions
m=\sum_{i\geq 0}m_{i}p^{i}
and
n=\sum_{i\geq 0}n_{i}p^{i}
. We say that
m
is
p
-contained in
n
if
m_{i}\leq n_{i}
for all indices
i\geq 0
.
The
p
-Kostka numbers
[M^{\lambda}:Y^{\mu}]
for two-part partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
are completely determined in
[
Hen05
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
0\leq 2r\leq 2s\leq n
. Then
[M^{(n-s,s)}:Y^{(n-r,r)}]
is
1
if
s-r
is
p
-contained in
n-2r
and
0
otherwise.

It follows that if
p>3
and
p\mid n
, we have

\displaystyle M^{(n-2,2)}

\displaystyle\cong Y^{(n-2,2)}\oplus Y^{(n-1,1)},

\displaystyle M^{(n-3,3)}

\displaystyle\cong Y^{(n-3,3)}\oplus Y^{(n-2,2)}\oplus Y^{(n-1,1)},

\displaystyle M^{(n-4,4)}

\displaystyle\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Y^{(n-4,4)}\oplus Y^{(n-3,3)}\oplus Y^{(n-1,1)}&\text{ if $p=5$,}\\
Y^{(n-4,4)}\oplus Y^{(n-3,3)}\oplus Y^{(n-2,2)}\oplus Y^{(n-1,1)}&\text{ if $p>5$.}\\
\end{array}\right.

The
p
-Kostka numbers for the first three-part partition
(n-2,1^{2})
(in the lexicographic order) are determined in
[
LW21
]
. In particular, we use the statement in the case when
p\mid n
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
and
p\mid n
.We have

M^{(n-2,1^{2})}\cong Y^{(n-1,1)}\oplus Y^{(n-2,2)}\oplus Y^{(n-2,1^{2})}.

If
p>5
, we have

[\Sym^{4}D^{(n-1,1)}]=[M^{(n-3,2,1)}]+[Y^{(n-4,4)}]-[Y^{(n-3,3)}]-[Y^{(n-2,1^{2})}].

If
p=5
, we have

[\Sym^{4}D^{(n-1,1)}]=[M^{(n-3,2,1)}]+[Y^{(n-4,4)}]-[Y^{(n-3,3)}]-[Y^{(n-2,2)}]-[Y^{(n-2,1^{2})}].

Since the multiplicity of any Young module on the right side of the equation is nonnegative, we have the following result.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose
p>3
and
p\mid n
. The
p
-Kostka numbers
[M^{(n-3,2,1)}:Y^{(n-3,3)}]
and
[M^{(n-3,2,1)}:Y^{(n-2,1^{2})}]
are positive. If
p=5
, then
[M^{(n-3,2,1)}:Y^{(n-2,2)}]
is also positive.

Remark 4.4
.

The
p
-Kostka numbers
[M^{(n-3,2,1)}:Y^{(n-3,3)}]
and

can also be computed using a reduction formula. In
[
FHKD08
]
, it was proved that the

-Kostka numbers are preserved by the first row (respectively, first column) cut. Then in
[
BG19
]
, it was proved that there is a generalised row (respectively, column) cut formula.
Applying the first row cut, we have

[M^{(n-3,2,1)}:Y^{(n-3,3)}]=[M^{(2,1)}:Y^{(3)}]=1.

Applying the first column cut, we have

[M^{(n-3,2,1)}:Y^{(n-2,1^{2})}]=[M^{(n-4,1)}:Y^{(n-3)}]=1.

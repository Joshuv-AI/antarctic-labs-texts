Pneumonitis in Older Patients With Non-squamous Non-small Cell Lung Cancer Treated With Pembrolizumab and Pemetrexed
Authors: YOSHIHITO KOGURE, HIROYA HASHIMOTO, HARUKO DAGA, YASUHIRO GOTO, TADAAKI YAMADA, TOMOKI KIMURA, SATORU MIURA, HIROTOSHI YASUI, NOBUYUKI YAMAMOTO, HIDEO SAKA, MASASHI KONDO
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/AimPembrolizumab combined with pemetrexed is efficacious in older patients with metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and a PD-L1 tumor proportion score (TPS) of <50%. However, treatment-related pneumonitis is concerning, and its impact on clinical outcomes is not fully understood. In this study, we examined the effect of pneumonitis on treatment efficacy and survival in the CJLSG1901 study, with a focus on differences in baseline patient characteristics.Patients and MethodsSubgroup analyses were performed to explore the efficacy and safety of pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed in older patients with metastatic non-squamous NSCLC, focusing on whether pneumonitis developed after treatment initiation.ResultsOf the 48 patients included, five and four patients developed grade 1-2 and grade 3 pneumonitis, respectively. The pneumonitis group was predominantly male; postoperative recurrence was nearly twice as common in the group. Patients with pneumonitis received fewer treatment cycles. The objective response rates were 44.4% and 35.9% in the pneumonitis and non-pneumonitis groups, respectively. The median progression-free survival was shorter in the former. In contrast, the median overall survival was comparable between the groups.ConclusionAlthough patients with pneumonitis had numerically higher response rates, their survival outcomes were comparable to those of patients without pneumonitis.

Introduction

Pembrolizumab in combination with pemetrexed and carboplatin is a standard first-line treatment for patients with metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (non-Sq NSCLC) (1, 2). However, the efficacy and safety of this regimen in older patients remain insufficiently studied (3-5). The CJLSG1901 study was a single-arm phase 2 trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed in patients aged ≥75 years with a PD-L1 tumor proportion score (TPS) of <50% (6). Treatment-related pneumonitis is a known adverse event; however, its impact on clinical outcomes in this population has not been fully elucidated. Therefore, we performed a post hoc subgroup analysis of the CJLSG1901 study, focusing on the occurrence of treatment-related pneumonitis.

Patients and Methods

Patients.Details of the trial design and methodology for CJLSG1901 have been previously published (6). Briefly, enrollment criteria included patients aged ≥75 years with pathologically confirmed metastatic non-Sq NSCLC with a PD-L1 TPS of <50% (22C3), absence of sensitizing EGFR or ALK alterations.

Post hoc analysis.Patients were categorized based on the presence or absence of pneumonitis during treatment. Baseline characteristics, treatment exposure, tumor response [objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR)], progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS) were compared between groups.

Statistical analysis.Efficacy analysis was conducted in patients who met the eligibility criteria. Safety analysis was performed in patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment. The 95% confidence interval (CI) for the ORR was calculated using the Clopper-Pearson method. Survival curves for PFS and OS were computed using the Kaplan-Meier method, and the 95%CI for the median survival time was determined using the Brookmeyer-Crowley method with complementary logarithm transformation. All the statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Overall, 49 patients were enrolled between July 2020 and May 2022. Of these, nine (18.4%) patients developed treatment-related pneumonitis. Baseline characteristics, including median age, Eastern Clinical Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS), histology, and smoking history, were comparable between patients with and without pneumonitis (Table I). The proportions of male patients in those with and without pneumonitis were 88.9% (8/9 patients) and 74.4% (29/39 patients), respectively, and the corresponding incidence of post-surgery recurrence was 44.4% (4/9 patients) and 20.5% (8/39 patients). Pneumonitis of grades 1, 2, and 3 was observed in three, two, and four patients, respectively (Table II). Two patients were administered a steroid pulse, and each patient received 120 mg of methylprednisolone and prednisolone. All cases of pneumonitis improved after corticosteroid treatment or observation. Four of the nine patients received sequential therapy. S1 and pemetrexed were administered to one of these patients, and two of the patients received nab-paclitaxel.

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the survival impact of treatment-related pneumonitis in older patients with non-squamous NSCLC treated with pembrolizumab and pemetrexed. Treatment-related pneumonitis was observed in nine patients (18.4%). Among these, male patients predominated, and nearly half had a history of post-surgery recurrence. The ORR and DCR were 44.4% and 77.8%, respectively, in patients with pneumonitis, numerically higher than the corresponding rates in those without pneumonitis. However, the median treatment cycles for pemetrexed and pembrolizumab were shorter in patients with pneumonitis than in those without pneumonitis, and the median PFS and OS were comparable between these two patient groups.

The frequency of pneumonitis observed in this study was higher than that reported in a previous study conducted in Japan. Fujimoto et al. reported an incidence of 12.4% with pembrolizumab and pemetrexed plus carboplatin (7). Although the incidence in our cohort was higher, it remained within an acceptable range, considering that older patients may have increased susceptibility to treatment-related pneumonitis (8). Notably, pre-existing interstitial pneumonia remains a significant clinical challenge when managing chemoimmunotherapy for advanced NSCLC, particularly in patients with low PD-L1 expression, necessitating extremely careful patient selection and risk balance evaluation (9).

The predominance of male patients and the higher rate of postsurgical recurrence among those who developed pneumonitis are consistent with known risk factors, such as male sex and surgical history, which have been linked to pneumonitis and interstitial lung disease exacerbations in prior studies (8, 10). When extending the risk management context to radiotherapy, identifying risk factors for radiation-induced pneumonitis and establishing scoring instruments or specific prognostic models are also crucial for predicting clinical courses, especially in very elderly or locally advanced lung cancer patient populations (11, 12).

Several large studies have shown that the occurrence of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) is associated with improved survival outcomes in NSCLC. In a study of a contemporary cohort of 803 patients, Cook et al. reported that irAEs were associated with significantly prolonged OS (median: 23.7 vs. 9.8 months) (13). Similarly, a recent meta-analysis that included over 41,000 patients found that irAEs were correlated with better PFS, OS, ORR, and DCR, independent of PD-L1 expression (14). Furthermore, recent clinical investigations continue to focus heavily on identifying various baseline characteristics and peripheral blood parameters as key clinical prognostic factors to better stratify outcomes in non-small cell lung cancer patients undergoing immune checkpoint inhibitor therapies (15). Broadly, optimizing strategic management to reduce toxicities and ensure treatment continuity remains paramount for overall survival improvement in advanced non-small cell lung cancer patients receiving long-term systemic regimens (16).

However, it is noteworthy that pulmonary irAEs, which are often linked to improved tumor response, may not always confer a survival advantage. In fact, severe cases (grade ≥3) of pneumonitis have been shown to be associated with an increased mortality risk. This dual nature highlights the complexity of pneumonitis as both a potential biomarker of immune activation and a life-threatening toxicity that may compromise treatment continuity.

Study limitations. First, the analysis was retrospective and based on a small number of pneumonitis cases, limiting its statistical power. Second, radiological details were not uniformly available, which may have influenced grading and diagnosis. Third, because this was a single-arm study in an older Japanese cohort, the generalizability of these findings is uncertain.

Conclusion

Treatment-related pneumonitis occurred in nearly one-fifth of the older patients treated with pembrolizumab and pemetrexed in the CJLSG1901 study. Although patients with pneumonitis had numerically higher response rates, their survival outcomes were comparable to those of patients without pneumonitis. However, careful monitoring and early intervention are warranted, particularly in high-risk populations, such as older men and those with recurrence after surgery. Further studies with larger cohorts are required to clarify the prognostic impact and optimal management of treatment-related pneumonitis in this population.

Singularity reversal of Schwarzschild black holes in Anti-de-Sitter
Rakshit Poudel, Daya Nidhi Chhatkuli, Madhav Kafle

Abstract




We propose a non-metastable de-Sitter infinitesimal patch as a replacement for the theoretical singularity that occurs during the description of maximally extended Schwarzschild Anti-de Sitter spacetimes. This corresponds to a highly non-perturbative modification of the entangled sector or a dual thermofield double state. We speculate that the de-Sitter core may alter late-time correlators, regulate divergences, and appear as a deformation in the growth of holographic complexity. The description involves a copy of the conformal field theory associated with Israel Junction conditions and an entangled state. We also discuss a version of the possible solution of the Page Curve via. The evaporation of non-metastable space its range of impact in its solution.

Appearing as one of the bizarre solutions of the General Theory of Relativity, the solutions involving mathematical singularities have existed since 1915
[1]

[2,3]

[4]

[5]

[6]

By restricting the gravitational bounds, we approach a non-eternal Schwarzschild Anti-de Sitter spacetime with a non-metastable reversed bound—i.e., an infinitesimal de Sitter patch replacing the singularity via Israel’s junction conditions
[7]

2.1. Singularity reversal with infinitesimal, non-metastable dS patch:

A well-known approach to regularize black-hole singularities is to replace the central singularity with a tiny de Sitter
(dS)
patch. In our scenario, a non-eternal Schwarzschild–
AdS
black hole formed from gravitational collapse develops, at its would-be singularity, an infinitesimal non-metastable de Sitter core. In practice one models this by cutting out a small region near
r=0
and gluing in a de Sitter spacetime. The gluing is carried out via the Israel junction conditions for a thin shell. Concretely, one takes the exterior metric to be Schwarzschild–
AdS
, and the interior metric to be de Sitter. The
dS
core metric is now defined as:

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)d\tau^{2}+\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\right)dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}

(7)

where
{}^{\prime}l^{\prime}
is the (tiny) dS radius. At the junction radius
R\to 0
one imposes continuity of the induced metric and relates the jump in extrinsic curvature to the shell’s stress-energy. For a spherical shell of tension
{}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}
, Junction condition gives us a master equation of the form

\sqrt{f_{{out}}(R)+\dot{R}^{2}}-\sqrt{f_{{in}}(R)+\dot{R}^{2}}=4\pi\sigma R

(8)

f_{{out}}(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}

is the shell collapse mode outside the shell, and
f_{{in}}(r)=1-\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}

is the shell collapse mode inside the shell.

As we see in (2.7),
f_{{out}}
denotes the Schwarzschild-AdS metric, while
f_{{in}}
corresponds to a pure de Sitter metric for
\ell^{2}<0
. To keep the core de Sitter and retain causality, we take
\ell\ll 0
.

The junction construction can be understood as a thin-wall bubble nucleation process. One imagines that quantum or classical effects seed a bubble of higher vacuum energy at the collapse center. Coleman–De Luccia tunneling arguments suggest that AdS can tunnel to a positive-CC vacuum under the right conditions, although here the roles are reversed (AdS outside, dS inside). In our static ansatz the bubble is essentially pinned at
r=0
and connects the two geometries via a thin shell. Mathematically, solving the junction condition
for
R=0

\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{2M}{R}+\frac{R^{2}}{L^{2}}}-\sqrt{1-\frac{R^{2}}{\ell^{2}}}}{4\pi R}=\sigma

(9)

In the limit
R\to 0

\frac{1-1}{4\pi R}
up to leading order, forcing
\sigma

Thus one generically finds violation of the Null Energy Condition (NEC) on the shell: the stress-energy supporting the dS core carries exotic (negative) energy density or tension. A careful smooth “smoothing out” of the singularity might replace the thin shell by a continuous matter profile, but the qualitative result is similar: positive vacuum energy (dS) inside requires exotic stress to glue to AdS outside. Physically, this infinitesimal dS bubble can be thought of as a baby-universe nucleation inside the collapsing star. It is non-metastable in the sense that it does not decay to the true (AdS) vacuum — rather, it is a patch of false vacuum. However, because its size is vanishingly small, it does not inflate dramatically outward. Instead it acts as a static core. If one allowed dynamical evolution, one would see the dS region either slowly expanding or staying at equilibrium. In any event, the would-be spacelike singularity at
r=0
is replaced by this smooth dS region of finite curvature.

By construction, this model violates standard energy conditions on the junction. For instance, the shell’s stress tensor generically yields
T_{ab}k^{a}k^{b}<0
for some null

,so the NEC is violated. Such violations are well-known in alternative black hole models (e.g. gravastars and regular black holes), where negative pressures or energies occur in the core or shell. One can explicitly compute the surface stress from Israel’s formula:

8\pi{S^{\tau}}_{\tau}=\frac{2}{R}\left(\sqrt{f_{{out}}}-\sqrt{f_{{in}}}\right),8\pi{S^{\theta}}_{\theta}=\frac{\sqrt{f_{{out}}}-\sqrt{f_{{in}}}}{R}+\frac{f^{\prime}_{{out}}-f^{\prime}_{{in}}}{2\sqrt{f_{{out}}}}+\cdots

(10)

and one finds
{S^{\tau}}_{\tau}
negative (tension) if
f_{{in}}(r)>f_{{out}}(r)

These violations are “acceptable” in the sense that many proposed singularity-resolving models require them; nonetheless, they indicate that this semiclassical gluing is not a solution of classical positive-energy matter in Einstein gravity.

In AdS/CFT, the boundary field theory lives on the asymptotic AdS boundary and encodes the bulk geometry in its correlators. Since our black hole forms from collapse, the dual CFT starts in a state of normal matter that thermalizes into a large-N thermal ensemble. The question is: can the CFT “see” the tiny dS core behind the horizon? Generally, the region behind the event horizon is not directly accessible to simple boundary probes. Local operators on the boundary are mapped to bulk fields outside (and perhaps up to) the horizon, but not inside. However, subtle signs of interior structure can appear in nonlocal observables. For example, two-point correlation functions at late times are related to geodesics that probe deep into the bulk. If a geodesic approaches the core and reflects off the inner region, it could imprint a tiny “echo” or phase shift in boundary correlations. In practice, because our dS patch is infinitesimal, such effects are exponentially suppressed in the
\frac{1}{N}
expansion and extremely hard to detect. Another angle is entanglement: the entanglement wedge of the boundary region does not extend past the event horizon in a one-sided black hole, so the core is not reconstructible by simple CFT entanglement methods. Speculatively, the dS region might correspond to some special pattern of heavy operators or a rare microstate in the CFT. It could behave like a small bubble of “false vacuum” excitation. In a holographic language, one might imagine that the CFT state slightly violates the strong subadditivity or positive energy conditions, reflecting the exotic interior. But without a concrete bulk-to-boundary map for the interior, these remain guesses. Importantly, since our dS core is non-metastable and Planck-scale, it probably does not lead to dramatic instabilities in the boundary theory; it is essentially invisible, much like the classical singularity which already was hidden behind the horizon.

Inside the horizon, the spacetime transitions from Schwarzschild–AdS to de Sitter. In Schwarzschild–AdS, the
r=0
singularity is spacelike; after the gluing, the
r=0
point is replaced by a small dS sphere. The Penrose diagram would show the usual black hole region ending in a smooth dS patch rather than a tear in the manifold. If one follows timelike geodesics into the black hole, instead of hitting infinite curvature they asymptote to the dS core. This core has a constant positive curvature scalar
R\sim+\frac{12}{l^{2}}
. Dynamically, if one perturbs the shell, the dS patch could oscillate. There may be an inner (Cauchy) horizon just outside the dS region where
f_{in}(r)=0
. This is analogous to the inner horizon in Reissner–Nordström or de Sitter–Schwarzschild interiors. Whether that inner horizon is stable or suffers mass inflation would require further analysis. In our infinitesimal limit, the inner horizon essentially merges with the core itself. One might imagine a scenario like a Planck-scale bounce: infalling matter compresses against the vacuum pressure of the de Sitter core and then (quantum-mechanically) bounces back out in some form, possibly as a baby universe or white hole. This touches on ideas like the “Planck star” in loop quantum gravity. In short, the interior “feels” like a brief epoch of expansion instead of crunch. From the outside, because the patch is so small, the classical exterior geometry (mass M, horizon size) is essentially unchanged. The horizon area and thermodynamics are dominated by the AdS mass term, and the tiny core does not significantly contribute to the black hole entropy. In principle, however, the existence of the core may slightly alter very high-frequency modes (UV), since very short geodesics could probe it.

This resonates with the concept of a gravastar (gravitational vacuum star) [16], where a de Sitter core is surrounded by a thin shell and Schwarzschild exterior. It also echoes regular black holes like the Bardeen or Hayward metrics, which effectively incorporate a dS core to avoid r=0 blow-up. In an AdS/CFT context, our scenario raises deep questions: it provides a playground to test how holography handles singularity resolution. For instance, if one could compute boundary correlators in a collapsing spacetime with this core (a hard problem), one could see how unitarity is preserved and how information is recovered or trapped in the core. Ultimately, constructing the exact spacetime of collapse with a dS core (and solving its backreaction) is a very hard dynamical problem – likely beyond the scope of a single paper. One would need to model the star’s matter, the tunneling to the dS vacuum, and the subsequent evolution, all in a fully consistent way. Nevertheless, our thin-shell picture captures the essence of the mechanism: quantum gravity might allow a bubble of high-energy vacuum to nucleate at extreme curvature, halting the singularity. This has profound implications: it means black holes could harbor baby universes or bounce phases, connecting gravity to cosmology and quantum theory.

3.Remarks about page curve curve normalization

The black hole information paradox arises from Hawking’s seminal result that a collapsing star emits thermal (Hawking) radiation as seen by outside observers [17]. In semiclassical gravity this radiation is purely thermal, leading to an ever‐increasing von Neumann entropy of the emitted field, in contradiction with the unitary evolution expected in quantum mechanics. Page’s argument was that if evaporation is ultimately unitary, the entanglement entropy
S_{rad}(t)
of the radiation must follow a “Page curve”: first rising as radiation is emitted, and then turning over at the “Page time” so that the final state is pure [18, 19]. In other words, after the Page time the outgoing radiation must start to encode information about the initial state, causing the entropy to decrease back to zero by complete evaporation. However, semiclassical computations (neglecting quantum gravity effects) predict a monotonically rising
S_{rad}(t)
, since one finds

S_{{rad}}(t)\approx S_{{BH}}(M(t))\propto M(t)^{2}

(11)

grows until the black hole vanishes. This growing entropy ultimately violates unitarity and is the heart of the information paradox [19, 20].

The island conjecture [20, 21] suggests that the decreasing part of the Page Curve is due to the inclusion of a region in the black hole interior (the island) in the entanglement wedge of the radiation. However, the island appears in a rather ad hoc manner in the replica wormhole calculation, and its dynamical origin is not understood. Moreover, the island prescription does not specify how the information is transferred from the interior to the exterior.

Our model addresses these issues by introducing a physical process: the evaporation of the non-metastable dS patch. The dS patch, being non-metastable, is subject to quantum decay. As it decays, it releases the information trapped in the interior, allowing it to be carried away by the Hawking radiation. This provides a dynamical mechanism for the decrease of the radiation entropy after the Page time. In AdS/CFT, the fine-grained entropy of a boundary subregion (e.g. the radiation) is given by a Ryu–Takayanagi (RT) surface in the bulk [22]. The RT formula
S=\frac{Area}{4G_{N}}
can be extended by quantum corrections one must add the bulk entanglement entropy across the RT surface [23]. The quantum extremal surface (QES) prescription: the entropy is given by extremizing the generalized entropy
S_{gen}=\frac{Area}{4G_{N}}+S_{bulk}
over candidate surfaces [24]. For an evaporating black hole coupled to a non-gravitational bath, this prescription yields a dramatic new result: after the Page time a new extremal surface appears which includes an “island” inside the horizon. In effect, part of the interior is included in the radiation’s entanglement wedge, reducing the net entropy. In the replica trick, these islands arise from replica wormhole geometries that connect different copies of the spacetime [20]. In such replica wormhole saddles the von Neumann entropy is computed by the island formula:

S_{{rad}}=\min_{{ext}}\left\{\frac{{Area}(\partial I)}{4G_{N}}+S_{{bulk}}(R\cup I)\right\}

(12)

where
I
is the island region and R the radiation region. As
n\to 1
replicates, the wormholes enforce this formula [20]. Concretely, in simple models one finds two competing saddles: before the Page time the trivial saddle (no island) dominates and
S_{r}ad
grows; after Page time a saddle with a nontrivial island wins and
S_{r}ad
decreases back toward zero, reproducing the page curve
[20]
. Thus, in modern terms the information paradox is resolved by the island mechanism: fine-grained (entanglement) entropy of the radiation ultimately follows Page’s unitarity curve
[19]
. These ideas have been checked in various settings, including higher-dimensional AdS black holes
[25]
and even asymptotically flat JT models. The key lesson is that semiclassical gravity does yield a Page curve once quantum extremal surfaces are correctly included. Importantly, the large discrepancy between the naive Hawking result and unitarity can be fixed by these new saddles
[20]
. In words: replica wormholes “connect” the replicas and force the appearance of islands, so that after the Page time part of the interior is encoded in the outgoing radiation, restoring purity
[25]
.

Recall from Section 2.1 that the black hole singularity is replaced by an infinitesimal dS patch. This patch is non-metastable because it represents a false vacuum state with positive cosmological constant, which can decay via bubble nucleation to the true vacuum (AdS) or to another state. The decay process is governed by quantum tunneling and can be described by the (CDL) instanton
[26,27]

\Gamma=Ae^{-B/\hbar}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\hbar)\right)

(13)

The decay rate is modified by the presence of the shell and the black hole. We model the evaporation of the dS patch as a gradual process. The dS patch has a finite energy density
\rho_{dS}=\frac{3}{8\pi Gl^{2}}
. As the black hole evaporates, the dS patch also loses energy through quantum fluctuations and tunneling events. The rate of energy loss can be related to the decay rate
\Gamma
. Moreover, the dS patch is coupled to the black hole geometry, so its evaporation is tied to the Hawking evaporation. Consider a Schwarzschild-AdS black hole of mass M with a dS core of radius l. The total energy of the black hole is approximately M (since the dS core is tiny). The dS core has energy:

E_{dS}=\frac{4\pi}{3}l^{3}\hskip 56.9055pt\rho_{dS}=\frac{l}{2G}

(14)

As the black hole evaporates, its mass decreases as
\frac{dM}{dt}=-\frac{\alpha}{M^{2}}
[28] where,
\alpha
is a constant depending on the number of particle species. Similarly, the dS patch evaporates due to quantum decay. The decay rate
\Gamma
is the probability per unit time per unit volume for a bubble of true vacuum to nucleate. The volume of the dS patch is
V\sim l^{3}
, so the total decay rate is
\Gamma V
. The energy loss rate of the dS patch is then given as

\frac{dE_{dS}}{dt}=\Gamma VE_{bubble}

(15)

where
E_{bubble}
is the energy released per bubble. However, the exact computation of
\Gamma
in the black hole background is complex. We approximate the decay rate by CDL in flat space, but with curvature corrections. The CDL action for a dS to AdS transition is
B=\frac{27\pi^{2}S_{1}^{4}}{2(\Delta\rho)^{2}}
[29,30], where
S_{1}

\Delta\rho=\rho_{AdS}-\rho{dS}
is the difference in energy density. In our case
\rho_{AdS}=-\frac{3}{8\pi GL^{2}}
and
\rho_{dS}=-\frac{3}{8\pi Gl^{2}}

is constrained by the junction conditions on (2.8). The decay rate
\Gamma
is then
\Gamma\sim e^{-B}
. As the black hole evaporates, the local temperature near the core increases, which can enhance the decay rate. The effective temperature at the core is the Hawking temperature redshifted to the center. Using the Tolman relation [31]:

T_{{local}}=\frac{T_{H}}{\sqrt{|g_{tt}|}}

(16)

Near the dS core
|g_{tt}|
is small, so

\Gamma(T)=\Gamma_{0}e^{-B+\beta E_{{barrier}}}

(17)

where

and
E_{barrier}
is an effective energy barrier. This is a simplification; a detailed calculation would require finite-temperature quantum field theory in curved spacetime. We thus see how a dynamical de Sitter core can cure the semiclassical Page‐curve problem by serving as an interior “information reservoir” whose evaporation carries out the missing entropy.

We adapt the page model [34, 35] to include the dS core. Let
\rho_{total}
be the density matrix of the entire system: black hole (BH), dS core (dS), and radiation (R). The total Hilbert space is
\kappa=\kappa_{{BH}}\otimes\kappa_{{dS}}\otimes\kappa_{R}

4. Conclusion

We have shown that the classical spacelike singularity of a Schwarzschild AdS black hole can be excised and replaced by an infinitesimal
dS
core without altering the external geometry. In our construction a collapsing AdS–Schwarzschild geometry is smoothly continued at the would-be singularity into a Planck-scale dS region via standard Israel junction conditions. Concretely, the exterior metric remains the usual Schwarzschild AdS solution while the interior metric is taken to be de Sitter,with the gluing enforced by continuity of the induced metric and a thin-shell stress-energy. This replacement is highly non-perturbative: it corresponds to cutting out the classical
r=0
singularity and inserting a false-vacuum core (of positive cosmological constant) supported by exotic shell tension. Importantly, the Israel junction construction ensures the asymptotic AdS boundary and horizon structure are unchanged, so the exterior semiclassical geometry (and its Kruskal extension) remain intact. This preserves the standard AdS/CFT duality in the UV region while modifying only the deep interior, thus yielding a singularity-free spacetime that is consistent with semiclassical gravity outside the core. Technically, the analysis is grounded in the well-known Kruskal–AdS coordinate framework and Vaidya-collapse models. We work with the maximally-extended Schwarzschild–AdS metric in Kruskal form to describe the wormhole geometry and then embed the collapse scenario (dual to a quantum quench in a single CFT) via an AdS metric.Holographic boundary correlators are used to probe the geometry: for example, two-point functions relax from vacuum to the thermal behaviour on timescales set by quasinormal modes, reflecting the dynamical horizon formation (as discussed in Sec. 2). We ensure our dS patch represents all matching conditions; indeed, soving the junction equations yields a master balance equation and ties the bubble wall tension to the AdS-dS gluing. From a quantum gravity standpoint, this infinitestimal dS core can be interpreted as a CDL nucleation of false vacuum at the collaspe center. While a positive-energy phase typically requires exotic stress on the shell, in our limit the bubble’s radius is vanishingly small, so it does not inflate or modify the exterior.

In all, our method is mathematically precise and uses well-established tools (Israel junctions, CDL instantons, and holographic correlators) to achieve singularity resolution without ad hoc new physics in the bulk. The key novelty is that this mechanism dynamically recovers information and resolves the Page-curve paradox without violating known exterior physics. Because the interior dS patch can decay or evaporate, it effectively serves as an
``informationreservoir^{\prime\prime}
inside the horizon. As shown in Sec. 3, the coupled Hawking–vacuum decay processes can transfer internal degrees of freedom into the radiation, so that the Hawking entanglement entropy ultimately follows the unitary Page curve. This avoids remnants or naked singularities: information is slowly released while the bubble shrinks. Throughout this process the outside geometry remains exactly Schwarzschild–AdS, so we respect the usual semiclassical exterior and avoid firewall-like disruptions. In other words, singularity resolution is achieved internally.

Conceptually, our construction fits naturally within AdS/CFT. In Maldacena’s thermofield-double (TFD) picture, an eternal AdS–BH has two boundaries, each with its own CFT. By contrast, our scenario starts with a single-sided collapse with only one CFT on the boundary. The inserted dS core can be viewed as a highly entangled “partner” sector that modifies the CFT’s late-time state. In this view, the non-metastable dS patch is akin to a non-perturbative modification of the entangled (TFD-like) state in the dual field theory. Because there is only one boundary CFT, the final state is pure but mixed to subsystems, matching the unitary collapse evolution.

We emphasize that while eternal two-sided black holes employ a static TFD and simple Euclidean techniques, our one-sided collapse requires real-time holographic analysis. The novelty here is that AdS/CFT can accommodate an interior “baby universe” bubble that carries entanglement; the correspondingly modified correlators and entanglement wedges still respect known causality constraints. We also note that our dS core trivially evades the usual string-theoretic no-goes for de Sitter: it is Planckian, non-metastable, and has a false-vacuum energy small compared to
1/L^{2}
.

As one can see from the junction and bubble analysis, the core does not correspond to a new stable vacuum at infinity, but rather a tiny localized excitation. In particular, it is not a globally inflating de Sitter universe, but a non-percolating bubble that can tunnel back to AdS. Thus it sits comfortably in the “swampland” constraints: no global de Sitter horizon is created, and any decay channel simply returns to the usual AdS vacuum. This consistency check reinforces that our model could be embedded in a UV-complete theory without obvious contradiction.

Looking ahead, this framework opens several avenues for further study. For example, one could attempt an explicit holographic reconstruction of the interior: by computing boundary observables (e.g. more refined correlators or entanglement entropies) in the presence of the bubble, one might identify precise signatures of the dS core or the information leakage. One could also pursue the quantization of the junction shell itself: treating the thin wall as a quantum object (perhaps along the lines of “quantum thin shell” models) might reveal discrete spectra or stability properties. Another direction is to analyze the inner (Cauchy) horizon dynamics in our spacetime: small perturbations there could exhibit mass inflation or other nonlinear effects, which would affect how the bubble evolves in time. Finally, it would be highly interesting to embed this scenario in a fully UV-complete string/M-theory setting. For instance, can one realize the infinitesimal dS core as a brane or flux configuration inside a known AdS compactification? Investigating such embeddings could confirm whether our semiclassical bubble and its decay are compatible with (say) the landscape of string vacua.

In summary, our work provides a precise toy model for black-hole singularity resolution and information recovery in AdS spacetime. By carefully applying Israel junction conditions and holographic reasoning, we show how a minuscule de Sitter bubble can replace the singularity while preserving all exterior physics. The result is a dynamically consistent, unitary evolution of a collapsed black hole that is compatible with semiclassical gravity, AdS/CFT duality, and swampland constraints. We anticipate that further exploration of this setup will deepen our understanding of quantum gravity in black hole interiors.

The elementary theory of free Steiner triple systems
Silvia Barbina, Enrique Casanovas

Abstract
Free Steiner triple systems (STS) are infinite structures that are naturally characterised by a universal property. We consider the class of free STSs from a model theoretic viewpoint. We show that free STSs on any number of generators are elementarily equivalent. We axiomatise their theory and show that it is stable.



1
Introduction

The subject of this paper is the first-order theory of a class of combinatorial structures called free Steiner triple systems. A
Steiner triple system
(STS) of order
\lambda
is a pair
(M,\mathcal{B})
where
M
is a set with
\lambda
elements, and

\mathcal{B}
is a collection of 3-element subsets of
M
(the
blocks
)
such that any two
x,y\in M
are contained in exactly one block.
A set
M
with a collection of 3-element subsets is a
partial STS
(PSTS) if any two elements of
M
belong to at most one block.

Varying the parameters 2 and 3 gives the more general notion of a Steiner
(t,k)
-system, where blocks have size
k
and every
t
-subset is contained in exactly one block.

Steiner systems are well known structures, widely studied in the finite case. Recently, certain infinite incarnations have appeared in the model-theoretic literature, e.g. as examples of strongly minimal structures
[
4
]
, or as specific constructions such as Fraïssé amalgamations
[
6
]
. These constructions exhibit a range of stability behaviours: the structure in
[
6
]
is
\mathrm{TP}_{2}
and
\mathrm{NSOP}_{1}
, and thus it lies at the opposite end of the stability spectrum from its counterparts in
[
4
]
.

An interest has emerged, more broadly, in the model theory of incidence structures — see, for example,
[
11
]
for
generic
incidence structures,
[
21
]
for projective spaces,
[
4
]
and
[
2
]
for Steiner systems,
[
1
]
for generalised
n
-gons. In
[
1
,
2
,
11
,
21
]
, the structures involved are described as either two-sorted or bipartite, with one sort (respectively part) for points and one for lines. In
[
4
]
, the vocabulary is relational and one-sorted. As we shall see, here (and in
[
6
]
) Steiner triple systems are one-sorted structures, which means our methods are generally different.

A notable construction method that applies to incidence structures and, more generally, to algebras, generates
free
structures. Universal algebra gives a standard characterization of free constructions, which, in the case of Steiner triple systems, captures the intuition that an infinite STS can be obtained from an initial set
A
containing
\lambda
vertices with no blocks defined among them by adding, for each pair of vertices
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
, a new point that gives the unique block to which
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
belong. Iterating this process
\omega
times gives an STS. The template example in
[
9
]
is, essentially, the result of this construction for
\lambda=3
.

Standard universal-algebraic tools tell us that such free constructions are uniquely determined up to isomorphism by the cardinality of the generating set
A
. A series of natural questions then arises, such as which first-order properties, if any, are shared by two free constructions where the sizes of the initial generating sets are different.

Questions of this kind have been addressed, for example, for projective planes
[
21
]
and for generalised polygons
[
1
]
. Towards the completion of the write-up of our results, we became aware of
[
26
]
— see the end of this section for the connection with our work.

In these papers, free structures are characterised via the existence of specific constructions in stages, rather than through the universal algebraic definition. Our choice of a one-sorted, functional language (see below) for Steiner triple systems makes the connection with universal algebra rather natural, and distinguishes our methods.

A partial Steiner triple system can be regarded as a first-order structure in the language containing a ternary relation symbol
R
and satisfying the axioms

•

for all permutations
\sigma\in S_{3}
,
\forall x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\,\left(R(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\rightarrow R(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},x_{\sigma(3)}\right)

•

\forall xyz\left(R(x,y,z)\rightarrow y\neq z\right)

•

\forall xywz\left(R(x,y,w)\wedge R(x,y,z)\rightarrow w=z\right)
.

The relation
R
is interpreted as the set of blocks: three elements
x,y
and
z
are in a block if and only if
R(x,y,z)
. A partial STS that satisfies the additional axiom

•

\forall xy\left(x\neq y\rightarrow\exists z\,R(x,y,z)\right)

is an STS.
With this choice of language, a substructure of an STS is a partial STS.

An STS can also be described as a
Steiner quasigroup
, that is, a structure with a single binary operation symbol
\cdot
, interpreted as an operation that satisfies the following axioms:

1.

\forall xy\,(x\cdot y=y\cdot x)

2.

\forall x\,(x\cdot x=x)

3.

\forall xy\,(x\cdot(x\cdot y)=y)
.

Thus, Steiner quasigroups are an equational class — see
[
15
,
16
]
. This is what makes it possible to employ universal algebraic tools.

A quasigroup
M
determines an STS whose blocks are the elements of the graph of the operation
\cdot
without the diagonal, that is,

\mathcal{B}=\{\{x,y,z\}\subseteq M:x\cdot y=z\}\smallsetminus\{\{x\}:x\in M\}\,.

It is easy to see that each STS determines a quasigroup and each quasigroup determines an STS, where

x\cdot y=z\mbox{ iff }R(x,y,z)\mbox{ or }x=y=z.

In fact, as noted in
[
15
,
16
]
, this shows that each Steiner triple system is bi-interpretable with a Steiner quasigroup, and justifies our choice to use the term
Steiner triple system
to cover both the functional and relational structures from the end of this section onwards.

A detailed account of the relation between Steiner quasigroups and Steiner systems can be found in
[
3
]
,
where the author shows that each strongly minimal Steiner
(2,k)
-system constructed in
[
4
]
can be
coordinatised
by a quasigroup when
k
is a prime power. In particular, in Section 3 Steiner triple systems are shown to be bi-interpretable with, rather than simply coordinatised by, Steiner quasigroups. Instead, when
k\neq 3
the strongly minimal Steiner
(2,k)
-systems constructed in
[
4
]
can be interpreted in appropriate quasigroups, but no interpretation can be found in the other direction.
This confirms that treating Steiner
triple
systems as quasigroups makes algebraic tools available that are not applicable when they are described as incidence structures.

Since the quasigroup axioms are universal, it is clear that a substructure of a Steiner quasigroup is a Steiner quasigroup. Moreover, if
N
is a Steiner quasigroup and
A\subseteq N
, there is a natural notion of substructure of
N

generated
by
A
, namely

\langle A\rangle_{N}=\bigcap\{M\subseteq N:M\mbox{ is a substructure that contains }A\}\,.

The subscript
N
may be omitted when clear from the context.

Remark 1.1

Let
M
be a Steiner quasigroup generated by
A\subseteq M
. Then

•

for every
b\in M
there is a term
t(\bar{x})
such that
b=t(\bar{a})
, where
\bar{a}
enumerates

•

M=\bigcup_{n<\omega}S_{n}

S_{0}=A

. Note that

since

.

Notation

Below we write

for
\bigcup_{i<n}S_{i}
.

Definition 1.2

Let

be a Steiner quasigroup generated by
A\subseteq M
. The sets

in Remark

are called the
levels
of
M
over

. An element

.

As remarked above, in what follows the term
Steiner triple system
is used for Steiner quasigroups, too; we rely on the context to clarify the usage.

In Section
2
we characterise free STSs as those that are
freely generated
by a subset in the sense of universal algebra, and we recall some standard universal-algebraic results. Section
3
introduces certain linear orderings for STSs and partial STSs that are an essential tool in our treatment of the theory of free STSs. Moreover, we show that the existence of an
HF-ordering
on an STS is equivalent to its
unconfinedness
, a first-order condition that is satisfied by all infinite free STSs and will essentially axiomatise their theory. In Section
4
we show that the amalgamation property fails for the class
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{un}}
of finite unconfined partial STSs. We also show that the obvious choice of a predimension function does not yield a fruitful definition of
strong substructure
for the construction of a Hrushovski amalgam. An appropriate notion of strong substructure is formulated in Section
5
and it involves HF-orderings. We show that the countable free STS with
\omega
generators is the generic object for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{un}}
with respect to this notion. We also give appropriate definitions of
n
-strong
substructure and
n
-amalgamation
and use the setup in
[
29
]
to show that the generic is not saturated. In Section
6
we show that infinite unconfined STSs always contain certain binary trees that are an essential ingredient
in the proof of our main result in Section
7
, where we prove that the theory of infinite unconfined STSs is complete and it is the theory of infinite free STSs — so, in particular, any two free infinite Steiner triple systems are elementarily equivalent. We also show that the theory is stable and that it has a model which is not free.

Our elementary equivalence result appears to be a special case of the main theorem in
[
26
]
, developed independently, where the completeness and strict stability are asserted for the theories of a range of open incidence structures that includes free Steiner systems (hence free Steiner triple systems). The results in
[
26
]
generalise those in
[
1
]
and
[
21
]
. As previously noted, a two-sorted language is chosen in
[
26
]
. This allows for a uniform treatment of the range of incidence structures considered in the paper. Since here we restrict our attention to Steiner triple systems, we can adopt the more natural choice of a one-sorted language, which leads to different methods and considerable simplifications.

2
Free Steiner triple systems: universal algebra

We give a universal algebraic characterisation of free Steiner triple systems as those that satisfy a
universal mapping property
.

Definition 2.1

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of Steiner triple systems. An STS
M
generated by
A
has the
universal mapping property
(UMP)
over
A
for
\mathcal{C}
if for every
N\in\mathcal{C}
, every map

\varphi:A\to N

extends to a homomorphism
\hat{\varphi}:M\to N
.

A standard argument (see, for example,
[
17
]
, § 24, Corollary 1), ensures that the extension
\hat{\varphi}
is unique.

Definition 2.2

If
M
is an STS, a subset
A\subseteq M
is
independent
if
\langle A\rangle_{M}
has UMP over
A
for the class of all STSs.

M
is said to be
freely generated
by
A
if

(i)

A
is independent

(ii)

\langle A\rangle_{M}=M
.

If
M
is freely generated by
A\subseteq M
then
M
is a
free STS
, and
A
is a
free base
of
M
.

The next proposition formalises the intuition that a Steiner triple system freely generated by a subset
A
can be constructed from
A
in stages by adding, at each stage, a new vertex as the product of two existing vertices whose product is still undefined. This construction was first described in
[
28
]
as the result of a
free extension process
, and later generalised in
[
14
]
to various classes of incidence geometries that include, among others, projective and affine planes, generalised
n
-gons,
k
-nets and Steiner
(t,k)
-systems. For convenience, we state a version for Steiner triple systems that lists explicitly the properties we require in the rest of the paper.

Proposition 2.3

Let
M
be a Steiner triple system and let
A\subseteq M
. The following are equivalent:

1.

A
is a free base of
M
.

2.

M
is generated by
A
with levels
(S_{n}\mid n<\omega)
, where
S_{0}=A
and

(a)

if
a,b\in S_{n}\smallsetminus S_{<n}
and
a\neq b
, then
a\cdot b\not\in S_{n}
.

(b)

if
a\in S_{n+1}\smallsetminus S_{n}
, there is a unique pair
\{b,c\}\subseteq S_{n}
such that
b\neq c
and
a=b\cdot c
.

Moreover, property (b) implies that if
a\in S_{n+1}\smallsetminus S_{n}
, and
b,a\cdot b\in S_{n}
, then
\{b,a\cdot b\}
is the unique pair given by (b) for
a
.

Proof.

See
[
7
]
, Proposition 3.1.
∎

Notice that each set
S_{n+1}\smallsetminus S_{n}
in the construction above generates a subsystem of
M
that satisfies condition 2, so
S_{n+1}\smallsetminus S_{n}
is a free base for
\langle S_{n+1}\smallsetminus S_{n}\rangle
, and in particular an independent set. It follows that
M
contains independent sets of finite unbounded cardinality. In fact,
M
also contains an independent set of cardinality
\omega
— a proof for Steiner loops in
[
24
]
is easily adapted to Steiner triple systems.

Definition 2.4

Let
M
be a Steiner triple system with a free base
A\subseteq M
. The sequence
(S_{n}\mid n<\omega)
of levels described in Proposition
2.3
will be called a
standard free construction of
M
over
A
.

Standard techniques for any variety in universal algebra show that the cardinality of a free base
A
determines the STS freely generated by
A
up to isomorphism.

Proposition 2.5

Let
M,N
be Steiner triple systems freely generated by
A\subseteq M
and
B\subseteq N
respectively. Then
M\cong N
if and only if
|A|=|B|
. In fact, every bijection between
A
and
B
can be extended to an isomorphism between
M
and
N
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem I in
[
13
]
, and Lemma 2.6, Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 in
[
22
]
. See
[
7
]
, Corollary 2.11 for a self-contained version of these arguments in the case of Steiner triple systems.
∎

The elementary equivalence of any two infinitely generated free Steiner triple systems follows from a standard argument for infinitely generated free algebras.

Lemma 2.6

Let
M,N
be free Steiner triple systems and assume
A
is an infinite free base of
M
that can be extended to a free base

of
N
. Then
M\preccurlyeq N
.

Proof.

[
17
]
, Chapter 6, §38, Theorem 4.

∎

Theorem 2.7

If
M,N
are free Steiner triple systems with infinite free bases, then

.

Proof.

[
17
]
, Chapter 6, §38, Corollary to Theorem 5.
∎

3
HF-orderings and confined configurations

The idea of
free
and
hyperfree extension processes
appears in the context of free projective planes in
[
28
]
and has subsequently been generalised to a wider range of incidence structures that includes Steiner triple systems in
[
14
]
. Such processes yield well-founded constructions of incidence structures from existing ones by adding new elements one at a time, with suitable restrictions on the number of incidences. In
[
21
]
, the related notions of
HF-
(hyperfree) and
F-
(free)
orderings
are formulated for projective planes to dispense with the well-foundedness assumption. These orderings have natural parallels for partial and complete Steiner triple systems respectively. Their properties are proved below by adapting arguments that appear in
[
14
,
21
]
for two-sorted incidence structures.

Definition 3.1

A
free ordering
(or
F-ordering
) of a Steiner triple system
M

over

A\subseteq M
is a linear ordering
<
of
M
such that for every
a\in M
there is a unique pair
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\subseteq M
with
a=a_{1}\cdot a_{2}
, and, for each
i
, either
a_{i}\in A
or
a_{i}<a
. Notice that when
a\in A
, the only possibility is
a_{1}=a_{2}=a
.

In our discussion of linear orderings we use standard operations as defined, for instance, in
[
27
]
. In particular, if
(A,<_{A})
and
(B,<_{B})
are linear orderings and
A\cap B=\emptyset
, then their sum is the linear order

(A\cup B,<)=(A,<_{A})+(B,<_{B})\,,

where
<
is defined as
<_{A}\cup<_{B}\cup\,(A\times B)
.

Remark 3.2

Let
M
be a Steiner triple system with standard free construction
(S_{n}\mid n<\omega)
over
A
. Let
<_{n}
be a linear ordering of
S_{n}\smallsetminus S_{<n}
and for
a,b\in M
define

a<b\mbox{ iff for some }n,\;b\in S_{n}\smallsetminus S_{<n}\mbox{ and either }a\in S_{<n}\mbox{ or }a\in S_{n}\smallsetminus S_{<n}\mbox{ and }a<_{n}b.

Then
<
is an F-ordering of
M
over
A=S_{0}
, which is an initial segment, and
<
is in fact the generalized sum
\sum_{n<\omega}(S_{n}\smallsetminus S_{<n},<_{n})
. If every
<_{n}
is a well-order,
<
is a well-order.

Remark 3.3

Let
M
be a Steiner triple system. If there is an F-ordering of
M
over
A
, there is another one where
A
is an initial segment. If the original order is a well-order, so is the new order, and
M=\langle A\rangle
.

Proof.

If
<
is an F-ordering of
M
over
A
, then the sum
(A,<\restriction A)+(M\smallsetminus A,<\restriction(M\smallsetminus A))
defines an F-ordering of
M
over
A
with initial segment
A
.
∎

Definition 3.4

A
hyperfree ordering
(or
HF-ordering
) of a partial Steiner triple system
A
is a linear ordering
<
of
A
such that for every
a\in A
there is at most one pair of smaller elements
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\subseteq A
with
a=a_{1}\cdot a_{2}
, that is, there is at most one block
\{a,a_{1},a_{2}\}
with
a_{1},a_{2}<a
.

The next proposition explains the connection between free bases and F-/HF-orderings.

Proposition 3.5

Assume
M
is a Steiner triple system and
A\subseteq M
. The following are equivalent:

1.

A
is a free base of
M
(as a Steiner quasigroup)

2.

There is a well-founded F-ordering of
M
over
A

3.

There is a well-founded HF-ordering of
M
and
A
is the set of points that are not a product of smaller elements.

Proof.

See
[
7
]
, Proposition 3.7.
∎

Free Steiner triple systems satisfy a property which can be expressed through infinitely many first-order universal sentences.

Definition 3.6

A finite partial STS
A
is
confined
if every element of
A
belongs to at least two blocks.

A partial STS
A
is
unconfined
, or
contains no confined configuration
, if no finite
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
is confined.

Equivalently,
A
is unconfined if for all finite nonempty
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
there is
a\in A^{\prime}
that belongs to at most one block in
A^{\prime}
, that is,
a
is either not a product of any elements in
A^{\prime}
, or the product of exactly one pair of elements of
A^{\prime}
.

Note that being unconfined is stronger than not being confined: an unconfined partial STS is hereditarily not confined.

In
[
18
]
,
confined
partial projective planes are those where every line contains three or more points, and there are three or more lines through every point. If a finite partial plane is not confined, then it is
open
. These notions are later used in
[
28
]
and they are central in
[
21
]
. Analogues are defined in
[
14
]
for the range of incidence structures treated in the paper, including Steiner systems. Our definition of unconfined partial STS is equivalent to that of
open
partial STS in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 3.7

1.

Free Steiner triple systems are unconfined.

2.

Finitely generated unconfined STSs are free.

3.

A partial STS is unconfined if and only if it has an HF-ordering.

Proof.

See
[
7
]
, Propositions 3.10, 3.13 and 3.11 for (1), (2) and (3) respectively.
∎

Definition 3.8

Let
A\subseteq B
be partial STSs. An HF-ordering
<
of
B
is
over

A
if for every
a\in B\smallsetminus A
there is at most one pair
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
of distinct elements of
B
such that
\{a,a_{1},a_{2}\}
is a block and for every
i
, either
a_{i}<a
or
a_{i}\in A
.

Note that if
B
is an STS, any F-ordering of
B
over
A\subseteq B
is an HF-ordering over
A
, but even if an HF-ordering over
A
is an F-ordering (over some subset of
B
), it is not necessarily an F-ordering over

, since elements of

can be a product of smaller elements of

.

Lemma 3.9

Let
A\subseteq B
be partial STSs. There is an HF-ordering of
B
over
A
if and only if there is an HF-ordering of
B
where
A
is an initial segment. In fact, given any HF-ordering
<
of

over

, the sum
(A,<\restriction A)+(B\smallsetminus A,<\restriction(B\smallsetminus A))
is an HF-ordering of

with initial segment

.

Proof.

On the one hand, if
<
is an HF-ordering of
B
with initial segment

, it easy to see that it is an HF-ordering over
A
. On the other hand, assume
<
is an HF-ordering of
B
over
A
. Then the only way in which the sum can fail to be an HF-ordering is for some

to be a product of two different pairs of smaller elements, and each element is either smaller than
a
in

or belongs to

, contradicting that

is an HF-ordering over

.
∎

Definition 3.10

Given an HF-ordering
<
of the partial STS
B
and a subset
A\subseteq B
, we define the
<
-

of
A
(in
B
) as the smallest subset
C
of
B
containing
A
and such that whenever

and

and
\{b,b_{1},b_{2}\}

. We denote

It is clear that the

\cl_{<}(A_{1}\cup A_{2})=\cl_{<}(A_{1})\cup\cl_{<}(A_{2})\,.

Lemma 3.11

If
<
is an HF-ordering of
B
and
A=\cl_{<}(A)\subseteq B
, then
<
is an HF-ordering of
B
over
A
.

Proof.

If not, there is some
b\in B\smallsetminus A
and there are distinct

, such that
\{b,b_{1},b_{2}\}
,
\{b,b_{3},b_{4}\}
are blocks and, for each

, either
b_{i}<b
or
b_{i}\in A
. Without loss of generality,
b_{2}<b_{1}
. We claim that
b_{1}<b
. If not, then
b_{1}\in A
and
b<b_{1}
, and since

, we get

. By a similar argument,

. But then

is not an HF-ordering.

Lemma 3.12

Assume
<
is an HF-ordering of
B
,
A=\cl_{<}(A)\subseteq B
and

is an initial segment such that

whenever

and

. Then we can interpolate

between

and the rest of the order of

. Moreover, we can change the ordering
of

by any other HF-ordering of

and still have an HF-ordering of

Proof.

Notice that
(C,<\restriction C)+(A,<\restriction A)=(A\cup C,<\restriction(A\cup C))
. Since
\cl_{<}(A\cup C)=A\cup C
, Lemma
3.11
ensures that

is an HF-ordering of
B
over

. For the moreover clause, note that no element of

is a product of elements of

nor a product of an element of

A
.
∎

Lemma 3.13

If
<
is an HF-ordering of
M
,
A\subseteq M
and
\cl_{<}(A)=A
, then
\cl_{<}(\langle A\rangle)=\langle A\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
\langle A\rangle=\bigcup_{n<\omega}A_{n}
, where

4
Some remarks on predimension

In
[
18
]
, a notion of
rank
for projective planes is defined whose analogue for Steiner triple systems coincides with a natural choice for a predimension function. As we shall see, though, this predimension function is not suitable for defining a notion of strong substructure that would lead to the construction of a Hrushovski-style amalgam — in fact, this is what motivates the different choice of predimension to build the uncountably many strongly minimal Steiner systems in
[
4
]
.

We show that the amalgamation property fails in the class of finite partial unconfined STSs with respect to both the substructure relation, and the obvious definition of strong substructure via predimension. This is why, in Section
5
, our construction of a generic object requires a rather different, and possibly not as natural, notion of strong substructure.

We also include a comparison with a class of finite partial STSs, defined in
[
12
]
in a wider context, that properly contains the unconfined ones and where predimension works well instead.
While these results have no direct application in subsequent sections, they clarify the context and may have independent interest.

5
Amalgamation

Since, by Example

Definition 5.1

For

A\leq B\ \Leftrightarrow\ A\subseteq B\mbox{ and there is an HF-ordering of $B$ where $A$ is an initial segment}.

Since

To see this, we define certain formulas in the relational language of Steiner triple systems (so blocks interpret a ternary relation

that is symmetric and irreflexive). In what follows, we implicitly assume that the elements

are pairwise distinct. There is a formula
\delta_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, in the relational language of partial STSs, that says that

is an HF-ordering, namely

\delta_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\equiv\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\bigg[\bigwedge_{1\leq j<k<i}\bigg(R(x_{i},x_{j},x_{k})\rightarrow\bigwedge_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{l}1\leq j^{\prime}<k^{\prime}<i\\
\{j,k\}\neq\{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}\}\end{array}}\neg R(x_{i},x_{j^{\prime}},x_{k^{\prime}})\bigg)\bigg]\,.

Now let

Consider the following modification of

6
Binary trees

This section introduces certain tree constructions in unconfined Steiner triple systems. These trees are an essential tool for our proof of the completeness of the theory of infinite unconfined Steiner triple systems in Section
7
.

Let
M
be an unconfined STS. A
binary tree of height
\alpha\leq\omega
is a subset
\{a_{s}\mid s\in 2^{<\alpha}\}
of
M
such that

1.

if
s\neq t
, then
a_{s}\neq a_{t}

2.

a_{s}=a_{s0}\cdot a_{s1}

3.

these are all the blocks of the tree: the only block containing
a_{\emptyset}
is
\{a_{\emptyset},a_{0},a_{1}\}
and if
s=ti
, the only blocks containing
a_{s}
are
\{a_{t},a_{t0},a_{t1}\}
and
\{a_{s},a_{s0},a_{s1}\}
.

A
leaf
is an element
a_{s}
of the tree such that there are no
a_{s0},a_{s1}
in the tree with
a_{s}=a_{s0}\cdot a_{s1}
.

Remark 6.1

1.

A binary tree of height
n+1
can be seen as a construction of
a_{\emptyset}
from
\{a_{s}\mid s\in 2^{n}\}
in the form of a binary tree, with the root
a_{\emptyset}
at the top. Every free STS embeds many copies of this tree: it suffices to choose a level with at least
2^{n}
elements and take the products of these elements that belong to higher levels, thus obtaining the root
a_{\emptyset}
after
n
steps. If
\{a_{s}\mid s\in 2^{n}\}
are the chosen elements in the given level, the tree can be described as
\{a_{s}\mid s\in 2^{\leq n}\}
, where
a_{s}=a_{s0}\cdot a_{s1}
if
s\in 2^{<n}
. A binary tree of height
\omega
can also be seen as a (non well-founded) construction of
a_{\emptyset}
over
\emptyset
.

2.

The tree
\{a_{s}\mid s\in 2^{<\alpha}\}
admits a natural HF-ordering that in the well-founded case reflects the order of construction of the root described in part 1: we order every level arbitrarily and we make the elements of level
i
larger than the elements of level
i+1
. In particular,
a_{s}>a_{t}
if
s\subsetneq t
.

An alternative HF-ordering can be defined on a binary tree, which will be used in the proof of Proposition
6.6
below.

Lemma 6.2

The binary tree of height
\alpha
has an HF-ordering where the root
a_{\emptyset}
is the smallest element instead of being the largest.

Proof.

If
<
is a natural HF-ordering of the binary tree, with
a_{\emptyset}
at the top, then the reverse order
>
is also an HF-ordering and now
a_{\emptyset}
is the smallest element.
∎

Given an element

of an infinite unconfined Steiner triple system, we can build a binary tree of arbitrary finite length having

as its root. If the STS is
\omega
-saturated, we can find such a tree of height

.

Lemma 6.3

1.

An HF-ordering of an infinite STS does not have a maximum.

2.

If

7
Completeness and stability

In this section we show that the theory of infinite unconfined Steiner triple systems is complete and stable. Since all free STSs are models of the theory, it follows that any two free STSs are elementarily equivalent. Moreover, there is a countable model of the theory which is not free.

Lemma 7.1

Let
M
be an unconfined STS. If

(as in Definition

and there is an HF-ordering of

Proof.

We could use Lemma
3.13

\langle A\rangle\smallsetminus A
into a disjoint union
\bigcup_{n<\omega}A_{n}
, where
A_{0}
is the set of elements of
\langle A\rangle\smallsetminus A
which are a product of two elements of
A
, and

is the set of elements of
\langle A\rangle\smallsetminus(A\cup A_{0}\cup\ldots\cup A_{n})
which are a product of two elements of

. Fix an HF-ordering of

with initial segment

. There are no blocks between elements of

, and the only blocks of elements of

with smaller elements are blocks with elements of

. Hence we can move

an initial segment of the order of

Lemma 7.2

Remark 7.3

If

is a saturated model of cardinality
\kappa
in the language

, then for every extension

with
|L^{\prime}|\leq\kappa
, for every set of sentences

of

such that

. If

For simplicity, the next two results are formulated in terms of saturated models, but we could alternatively use special models (

Lemma 7.4

Let

be a saturated unconfined STS, let

. Then there is an HF-ordering

of

such that

Theorem 7.5

Let

be uncountable saturated unconfined STSs. The set of isomorphisms between countable substructures

and
B\subseteq N
(in particular,

are STSs) such that

and
B\leq N
is a back-and-forth system between
M
and
N
.

Proof.

The case
A=B=\emptyset
is allowed and shows that there is at least one such isomorphism. Now assume
f:A\cong B
, where
A\leq M
and
B\leq N
are countable substructures, and let

. We want to find countable substructures

and
B^{\prime}\leq N
extending
A
and
B
with

and
f^{\prime}:A^{\prime}\cong B^{\prime}
for some

. The back step (i.e. extending

by adding some

) is similar. We fix an arbitrary HF-ordering
<_{M}
of
M
where

is an initial segment and we use Lemma

to choose an HF-ordering
<_{N}
of

such that
(N,<_{N})
is saturated and
\cl_{<_{N}}(B)=B
.

Case 1
.
\cl_{<_{M}}(a)=\{a\}
, or, equivalently,
Aa\leq M
. Corollary
6.7
gives

such that
Bb\leq N
, in which case, by Proposition

and by changing the ordering

if necessary, we may assume that

and
b>_{N}B
. Then

extends to an isomorphism of partial Steiner triple systems

Case 2
.
\cl_{<_{M}}(a)\smallsetminus A=\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}\}

. Note that
\cl_{<_{M}}(a_{n})=\{a_{n}\}

and, as for

, we get

. Finally we obtain

and an isomorphism

and

Case 3

\cl_{<_{M}}(a)\smallsetminus A=\{a_{i}\mid i<\omega\}

.

2.

Corollary 7.6

Let
M,N
be infinite unconfined STS. If
A\leq M
,
B\leq N
and
f:A\rightarrow B
is an isomorphism of partial STS, then
f
is a partial elementary map from
M
to
N
.

Proof.

First consider the case where
A
and
B
are countable. By Lemma
7.1
,
\langle A\rangle\leq M
,
\langle B\rangle\leq N
and
f
extends to some isomorphism
f^{\prime}:\langle A\rangle\cong\langle B\rangle
.
We can find uncountable saturated (or special
\omega_{1}
-saturated) elementary extensions
M^{\prime}\succeq M
and
N^{\prime}\succeq N
. Notice that
A\leq M^{\prime}
and
B\leq N^{\prime}
. By Theorem
7.5
,
f^{\prime}
belongs to a back-and-forth system between
M^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}
and so it is a partial elementary map from
M^{\prime}
to
N^{\prime}
and also from
M
to
N
.

Consider now the general case. Fix an HF-ordering of
M
having
A
as an initial segment. Consider a finite set
A_{0}\subseteq A
and let
A_{0}^{\prime}=\cl_{<}(A_{0})
. Since
A
is an initial segment,
A_{0}^{\prime}\subseteq A
. By Lemma
3.11
, we may assume that
A_{0}^{\prime}
is an initial segment of
A
. Then
A_{0}^{\prime}\leq A
and
A_{0}^{\prime}\leq M
. Now order
B
in such a way that
f
is an order isomorphism. This gives an HF-ordering of
B
. Moreover, if
B_{0}=f(A_{0})
and
B_{0}^{\prime}=f(A_{0}^{\prime})
, then
B_{0}\subseteq B_{0}^{\prime}
and
B_{0}^{\prime}
is an initial segment of
B
. Hence
B_{0}^{\prime}\leq B
and
B_{0}^{\prime}\leq N
. Since
A_{0}^{\prime}
and
B_{0}^{\prime}
are countable, by the initial case we know that
f\restriction A_{0}^{\prime}
is elementary. Hence,

is elementary.
∎

The completeness of the theory of infinite unconfined STS is implicit in the proof of Corollary

Proposition 7.15

be the free STS with

Let

Since

, the same sentence holds in

and, by isomorphism,

\exists x_{1}\ldots x_{n}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=t_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Choose

. Then

are independent by Remark

This implies that
f=g
and, therefore, that

is an automorphism.

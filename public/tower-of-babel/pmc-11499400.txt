Epidemiology of human metapneumovirus in Taiwan from 2013 to 2023
Authors: Su-Lin Yang, Tai-Yuan Chiu, Kun-Lin Tsai, Chung-Hao Li, Jyh-Yuan Yang, Ming-Tsan Liu, Fang-Tzy Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHuman metapneumovirus (HMPV) is a member of the genus Metapneumovirus in the family Pneumoviridae of the order Mononegavirales that can cause upper and lower respiratory tract disease. This retrospective study describes the epidemiology of hMPV based on community viral surveillance results from sentinel sites across Taiwan from 2013 to 2023. A total of 114 hMPV strains were isolated and analyzed to assess viral evolution through sequencing of their fusion protein genes. This study revealed that hMPV cases occur almost year-round in Taiwan, with a peak occurring during spring (March to May). Of the 114 infected patients, 68.4% were children under 4 years old. The geographical distribution of hMPV positivity was highest in Penghu County, followed by Changhua County and Hsinchu County. The clinical symptoms of hMPV infection are nonspecific, with fever (56.1%), cough (44.7%), rhinorrhea (21.1%), and sore throat (14.9%) being the most common. However, a few patients also developed severe central nervous system symptoms (1.8%) or dyspnea (0.9%). Phylogenetic analysis revealed genetic diversity among the 114 isolated hMPV strains, with the A2 lineage (57.9%) being the most frequently observed, followed by the B2 lineage (33.3%), in the Taiwanese community from 2013 to 2023. In conclusion, hMPV causes a serious acute respiratory disease in Taiwan that should not be neglected. Introduction

Human metapneumovirus (HMPV) was first isolated from a nasopharyngeal specimen obtained from a young child with respiratory tract disease in the Netherlands in 2001 [1]. Since then, hMPV has been detected in various countries, including Japan, the USA, Australia, the United Kingdom, France, and Canada [2–6]. HMPV is a leading cause of acute respiratory disease, primarily affecting young children, elderly individuals, and immunocompromised patients [7–13]. The clinical manifestations of hMPV infection are similar to those of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), which causes seasonal respiratory tract disease, and individuals can be reinfected throughout their lives. Symptoms of hMPV are similar to those of the common cold, including cough, wheezing, rales, and hypoxemia, but severe bronchiolitis and pneumonia can also occur [14–16]. Currently, there is no effective antiviral therapy available to treat hMPV infections, and there is no vaccine against this virus.

HMPV is a single-stranded RNA virus with a genome size of approximately 13 kilobases. The genome contains eight genes that encode nine proteins: nucleoprotein (N), phosphoprotein (P), matrix protein (M), fusion protein (F), M2-1/M2-2 (two proteins encoded by the same gene), small hydrophobic protein (SH), glycoprotein (G), and large protein (L) [17]. Based on the genetic characteristics of the fusion protein, hMPV can be classified into four subtypes: A1, A2, B1, and B2 [7, 18–23]. Previous studies have shown that the A2 genotype was the dominant genotype in southern Taiwan from 2003 to 2005 [24]. However, the current epidemiological status and prevalent genotypes of hMPV in Taiwan are unclear and have not been fully explored. To investigate the hMPV epidemic further, a retrospective study was conducted to analyze the epidemiology, clinical characteristics, and circulating viral strains of hMPV in Taiwanese communities from 2013 to 2023.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

The study protocol was approved by the Taiwan Centers for Disease Control Institutional Review Board (IRB 107118, IRB 109107). The requirement for informed consent was waived by the board.

Human clinical samples

Respiratory specimens for this study were collected from outpatients, inpatients, emergency patients, and patients at sentinel clinics. Swab samples from respiratory patients were sent to the contract laboratory of the Taiwan CDC for diagnosis and isolation from 1 January 2013 to 31 December 2023. The sample data were depersonalized to ensure anonymity. Nine contract laboratories distributed across northern, central, southern, and eastern Taiwan conducted community virus surveillance and provided essential information on the epidemic trends of community virus strains. In total, 121,250 suspected respiratory virus specimens were tested, with 9,698 to 12,356 tests performed each year.

Methods of viral isolation

The presence of hMPV was determined in the contract laboratories based on cultured virus isolation. Briefly, respiratory specimens collected by the virus contract laboratory were inoculated onto MK2 cells or R-Mix cells (Diagnostic Hybrids, Inc., Athens, OH, USA). If the infected cells exhibited cytopathic effects, hMPV was identified using a direct immunofluorescence assay according to the manufacturer's instructions (D3 Ultra DFA Respiratory Virus Screening & ID Kit; Diagnostic Hybrids, Inc., Athens, OH, USA). The hMPV isolated was then sent to the Taiwan CDC for PCR analysis and phylogenetic analysis.

Nucleic acid extraction and reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR)

Viral nucleic acids were extracted from hMPV-infected cell culture supernatants for RT-PCR analysis using a TANBead Maelstrom 4800 automated operating instrument (Taiwan Advanced Nanotechnology Co., Ltd., Taiwan) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Nucleic acid extraction was performed using 300 µL of infected cell supernatant. Sterile water was used as a negative control for PCR. Viral RNA extracted from an hMPV strain was used as a positive control for PCR. The primers hMPV-627F (5’-CAATGCAGGTATAACACCAGCAATATC-3’) and hMPV-1377R (5’-GCAACAATTGAACTGATCTTCAGGAAAC-3’) were used to amplify a 750-bp fragment of the F gene. RT-PCR was performed using a Primescript One Step RT-PCR Kit (Takara Bio). Briefly, 5 µL of RNA was added to the RT-PCR mixture, which included 12.5 µL of 2X buffer, 1 µL of 10 µM each primer, 1 µL of enzyme, and 3.5 µL of diethylpyrocarbonate (DEPC)-treated water in a final volume of 25 µL. The RT-PCR program was as follows: 50°C for 30 min for RT and 94°C for 2 min for inactivation, followed by 40 cycles of PCR (94°C for 30 min, 55°C for 30 min, and 72°C for 1 min) with a final extension at 72°C for 10 min. The RT-PCR products were analyzed by electrophoresis in a 1% agarose gel to detect the amplified 750-bp fragment of the fusion gene.

Nucleotide sequencing

The RT-PCR products were purified using a Zymoclean Gel DNA Recovery Kit (Zymo Research, Irvine, California, USA). Nucleotide sequences were determined using an automated DNA sequencing kit and an ABI Prism 3730XL DNA sequencer (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Overlapping nucleotide sequences were combined for analysis and edited using the Sequencher software package (v5.4.6, Gene Codes Corporation, Ann Arbor, MI, USA), and these nucleotide sequences were submitted to the GenBank database. The strain identifiers and their accession numbers (PP373527-PP373640) are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Phylogenetic analysis

Multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic analysis were conducted using MEGA version 11 (http://www.megasoftware.net/) [25]. The nucleotide sequences obtained in this study were aligned with global reference sequences obtained from the GenBank database using MUSCLE software integrated in MEGA. Phylogenetic analysis was performed by the neighbor-joining method and the maximum composite likelihood method. One thousand bootstrap replicates were performed to estimate the reliability of the phylogenetic tree, and bootstrap support values above 75 were considered well supported.

Statistical analysis

Statistical associations of prevalence for age and sex were tested using the chi-square test, with p-values less than 0.05 considered to indicate statistical significance. Data analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft, Washington, USA) and SPSS Statistics version 22.0 (IBM, Illinois, USA).

Results

Annual detection positivity and geographic distribution of hMPV

Figure 1 shows the results of community virus surveillance of infection with hMPV in Taiwan from 2013 to 2023. A total of 121,250 suspected respiratory virus specimens were tested, and hMPV was isolated from 114 specimens from 2013 to 2023. The number of specimens tested each year ranged from 9,698 to 12,356. The prevalence of this virus has fluctuated over time, reaching a peak in 2021, with no cases detected from 2018 to 2020 or in 2022. Figure 2 shows the geographic distribution of hMPV infection. Penghu County had the highest positivity, followed by Changhua County and Hsinchu County, while some areas did not report any cases.

Seasonal and age distributions of hMPV

Figure 3 shows the monthly distribution of hMPV infection. HMPV cases occur almost year-round in Taiwan, with the highest prevalence occurring from March to May and peaking in April. Figure 4 shows the age distribution of the patients infected with hMPV. A total of 57% of the infected people were female, and the prevalence was not significantly different between the sexes. The median age of the infected patients was 3 years (range 0–90 years), and the mean age was 10 years. The results showed that the group of children aged 0 to 4 years had the highest proportion of positive cases, while positive cases were not detected in adults aged 20 to 29 years. In addition, there was a significant difference in the prevalence of the virus in those under 4 years old and those over 60 years old (P < 0.001).

Phylogenetic analysis of hMPV isolates

In this study, hMPV was successfully isolated from 114 specimens, and all of the isolates were subjected to phylogenetic analysis. Partial sequences of the fusion gene (589 nt) were compared to global reference sequences obtained from the GenBank database. A phylogenetic tree constructed by the neighbor-joining method is shown in Fig. 5. The accession numbers of the isolates are listed in Supplementary Table S1. The results showed that the hMPV strains sampled in 2021 and 2023 were mainly of genotypes A2.2.2 and B2, with one A2.2.1 strain and one B1 strain found. In contrast, hMPV strains from 2013 to 2017 were found for all major genotypes except A1 and A2.1. For genotype A2.2.2, the hMPV strains from 2021 and 2023 were more distantly related to the early Taiwan strains from 2013 to 2017 and more closely related to recent strains sampled in the USA and China (SC0380/2019 and bj0154/2019), suggesting that these strains might have been introduced recently. On the other hand, the genotype B2 strains from 2021 and 2023 were more closely related to earlier strains circulating in 2013 and 2017 and the Australian strain WM0025022/2020 and more distantly related to the older strains TW05-00125/2007 and 1848/04, suggesting that the recently evolved subclades of the strains are continuously circulating in Taiwan.

Table 2 shows the annual distribution of hMPV genotypes in this study. No hMPV was isolated from 2018 to 2020 or in 2022. A total of 48 hMPV strains were isolated in 2021, and the A2.2.2 genotype was the most common, accounting for 77.1%. We found that the prevalence of the B2 genotype tended to increase annually. In addition, four genotypes were prevalent in 2023, of which the B2 genotype was the most common.

Discussion

HMPV is one of the leading causes of acute respiratory tract disease in children worldwide. At present, the molecular epidemiology of this disease in Taiwan is not fully understood. Therefore, we conducted epidemiological and evolutionary analysis of 114 hMPV isolates from across Taiwan from 2013 to 2023 based on the results of sentinel community virus surveillance. In this study, most cases of hMPV infection exhibited distinct seasonality and were prevalent in spring (March to May), accounting for 47%, followed by summer (42%), autumn (8%), and winter (3%). This seasonal characteristic was similar to that in other reports from China [23], Japan [20], South Korea [16, 23], Austria [19], the USA [8], and the Netherlands [10].

The clinical symptoms of hMPV are nonspecific and difficult to distinguish from those of other acute respiratory diseases, especially RSV infection. Both hMPV and RSV infections can cause severe complications, such as bronchiolitis and pneumonia [26–29]. Among these 114 hMPV-infected patients, 11 had pneumonia, and one had dyspnea. In this study, we did not investigate whether the patient's severe symptoms were due to coinfection with other pathogens, and it will be necessary to address the issue of coinfection in the future. Nevertheless, hMPV is still an important respiratory pathogen in Taiwan.

In this study, 114 isolates from 2013–2023 were used for phylogenetic analysis based on their fusion gene sequences, and the A2.2.2 genotype was dominant. This result was similar to those of previous studies [24] showing the genetic diversity of hMPV fusion genes and the simultaneous prevalence of multiple genotypes in Taiwan. Notably, we did not detect the A1 genotype during this period. We also observed that the clinical symptoms of hMPV infection were genotype-independent, an observation that is consistent with studies from other countries [19, 30–31]. To understand the evolution and characteristics of hMPV, it will be necessary to perform genetic typing and whole-genome sequencing of hMPV isolates in the future.

This study has several limitations. First, this retrospective study was based on community viral surveillance results from sentinel sites around Taiwan from 2013 to 2023. However, the prevalence of asymptomatic infections in remote communities or in individuals who do not actively seek medical treatment could not be determined in this investigation. Second, the current contract laboratory monitoring methods use virus culture, which requires infected cells to show cytopathic effects before the virus is identified by antibody staining. Therefore, it is likely that the actual prevalence of human metapneumovirus infections was higher than what is reported here. Third, no positive infection cases were detected from 2018 to 2020 or 2022. This might be due to inadequate surveillance or the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. Several reports have shown that the COVID-19 pandemic has had a significant impact on the prevalence of hMPV across different regions. For instance, a decrease in hMPV positivity was reported in South Korea in 2020, and no hMPV cases were detected in 2021 [23]. Similar trends were observed in China, the USA, and Israel, where the positivity of hMPV declined [32–34]. Conversely, Spain experienced an unusual hMPV epidemic in 2021 [35]. Therefore, it is speculated that the COVID-19 pandemic has affected hMPV epidemic trends, resulting in a decrease in the number of infections, shifts in the epidemic seasons, and changes in genotypes [23, 32–36].

In conclusion, we investigated the epidemiology, clinical symptoms, and prevalence of hMPV genotypes in 2013–2023. The clinical symptoms of hMPV are nonspecific and difficult to distinguish from those of other respiratory diseases, but hMPV may cause serious complications. There is currently no specific antiviral therapy to treat hMPV infections and no vaccine to prevent hMPV infections [37–39]. Therefore, people with acute respiratory diseases should seek medical treatment promptly to avoid worsening of their condition. In addition, in the post-epidemic era, molecular biology methods must replace culture methods to allow hMPV to be identified in a timely manner so that infections can be treated appropriately. Further in-depth research on hMPV should be conducted not only to monitor its epidemiology but also to explore the evolutionary relationships of circulating strains to develop more-accurate rapid diagnostic methods and effective vaccines in the future.

160 | Virtual Maternity Program Association With Reduced Perinatal Emergency Department Costs in a Commercially‐Insured Cohort

Lena Bertozzi1; Ian J. Hooley2; Thomas Galeon1; Eva M. Luo2; Isabelle Von Kohorn2

1Pomelo Care, New York, NY; 2Pomelo Care, Pomelo Care, NY

Objective: Emergency Department (ED) utilization during the perinatal period is a significant driver of healthcare spending. Root causes include limited access to 24/7 specialized guidance for maternal complications and neonatal concerns. This analysis evaluates the impact of a 24/7 multispecialty virtual maternity and infant care program on combined maternal‐infant dyad ED spending during pregnancy through 60 days postpartum.

Study Design: We conducted a retrospective cohort analysis of 2841 maternal‐infant dyads across three commercial payers with sequential intervention initiation (Jan 2024 ‐ Sept 2025). The intervention offered 24/7 access to a multidisciplinary team providing proactive prenatal care, transition support, postpartum care, and virtual urgent care. We compared dyads who first engaged prenatally (n = 402) against a non‐engaged comparison group (n = 2,439). Propensity score‐based inverse probability weighting adjusted for imbalances in demographics, insurance, utilization history, and relevant chronic conditions. Post‐weighting balance was assessed using Standardized Mean Differences (SMD). The primary outcome, perinatal episode ED cost for the dyad (280 days pre‐ to 60 days post‐delivery), was assessed using weighted means.

Results: Perinatal program engagement was associated with a significant reduction in combined maternal‐infant ED spending (−21.3%, p < 0.05). This represents an adjusted cost reduction of $481 per dyad with at least one ED visit. With a 94% effective sample size, baseline characteristics were well‐balanced after weighting (SMD <0.1).

Conclusion: These findings demonstrate that a virtual care model with prenatal and dyadic postpartum‐infant care is associated with a significant reduction in combined maternal and neonatal ED costs within a commercial population. This highlights the potential for virtual perinatal programs to increase access to continuous care and mitigate reliance on emergency services for non‐emergent concerns, suggesting an opportunity to reduce avoidable ED utilization within this care setting.

161 | Trends in Hysterectomy Among Patients Requiring Maternal Transfusion in the United States, 2017–2024

Serena M. Jones1; Frank I. Jackson2; Sarah H. Abelman2; Oladunni Ogundipe2; Matthew J. Blitz2

1New York Institute of Technology College of Osteopathic Medicine; Northwell Health, Northwell Health, NY; 2Northwell Health, Bay Shore, NY

Objective: To evaluate temporal trends in hysterectomy among births complicated by maternal blood transfusion, overall and by mode of delivery from 2017–2024.

Study Design: This was a retrospective national cohort study using U.S. National Vital Statistics System (NVSS) natality data from 2017–2024. Maternal transfusion and unplanned hysterectomy were identified using NVSS maternal morbidity indicators, which capture events occurring during the delivery hospitalization. Analyses were stratified by delivery method. Monthly hysterectomy rates among transfused births were calculated per 1000 transfused births and evaluated using Joinpoint regression. Average monthly per‐thousand change (AMPC) was reported with a 95% Confidence Interval (CI).

Results: Among 29,611,120 U.S. births, 143,283 were complicated by maternal transfusion (61,159 vaginal; 82,124 cesarean). The overall hysterectomy rate among transfused births declined from 63.85 to 29.21 per 1000 between January 2017 and December 2024, with a modest initial decline through September 2023 (AMPC −0.42%; 95% CI, −0.53 to −0.09; p = .043) followed by a steeper decline thereafter (AMPC −1.98%; 95% CI, −9.57 to −0.62; p < .001; Figure 1). Among transfused vaginal births, hysterectomy rates declined from 44.09 to 8.40 per 1000, with an initial increase through December 2019 (AMPC 1.06%; 95% CI, 0.36 to 2.23; p = .006), followed by a sustained decline (AMPC −1.32%; 95% CI, −1.81 to −1.01; p < .001; Figure 2A). Among transfused cesarean births, rates declined from 77.79 to 44.09 per 1000, with a consistent overall decrease and no joinpoint identified (AMPC −0.48%; 95% CI, −0.59 to −0.38; p < .001; Figure 2B).

Conclusion: Hysterectomy among patients requiring a transfusion during childbirth declined from 2017 to 2024 overall and by delivery method. However, rates remained markedly higher among cesarean compared with vaginal births. These findings suggest ongoing opportunities to reduce severe hemorrhagic morbidity through both optimization of hemorrhage management and efforts to avoid potentially preventable cesarean delivery.

162 | Comparative Trends in Peripartum Hysterectomy Rates in the United States and Mexico, 2017–2024

Serena M. Jones1; Frank I. Jackson2; Sarah H. Abelman2; Oladunni Ogundipe2; Matthew J. Blitz2

1New York Institute of Technology College of Osteopathic Medicine; Northwell Health, Northwell Health, NY; 2Northwell Health, Bay Shore, NY

Objective: To compare route‐specific peripartum hysterectomy rates in the United States and Mexico from 2017–2024.

Study Design: This retrospective cohort study used U.S. National Vital Statistics System (NVSS) natality data and Mexico hospital discharge and procedure data from 2017–2024. Peripartum hysterectomy during the delivery hospitalization was identified using the NVSS maternal morbidity indicator (U.S.) and procedure coding (Mexico). Analyses were limited to identified vaginal and cesarean deliveries; nonclassifiable routes were excluded. Monthly hysterectomy rates per 10,000 deliveries were evaluated using Joinpoint regression. Pooled 2017–2024 rates and Mexico‐to‐U.S. rate ratios (RR) with 95% CIs were calculated.

Results: A total of 33,383,852 deliveries were included: 29,611,120 in the United States (20,124,465 vaginal; 9,486,655 cesarean) and 3,772,732 in Mexico (3,148,984 vaginal; 623,748 cesarean). In Joinpoint analyses, only the U.S. vaginal series demonstrated a significant joinpoint in December 2021, with increasing rates before and decreasing rates afterward (p = 0.007 and p < 0.001, respectively). Vaginal hysterectomy rates in Mexico increased throughout the study period (p < 0.001). Cesarean hysterectomy rates showed no joinpoints and no significant overall trends in either country (U.S. p = 0.353; Mexico p = 0.181) (Figure 1). Pooled hysterectomy rates were higher in Mexico than in the United States after both vaginal delivery (3.08 vs 1.46 per 10,000; RR 2.12, 95% CI 1.97–2.28) and cesarean delivery (28.44 vs 11.71 per 10,000; RR 2.43, 95% CI 2.31–2.55) (Figure 2).

Conclusion: Peripartum hysterectomy rates were approximately two‐fold higher in Mexico than in the United States after both vaginal and cesarean delivery, with markedly higher rates after cesarean in both countries. The post‐2021 decline in U.S. vaginal hysterectomy rates warrants further study to determine whether factors such as access to uterine‐sparing therapies could inform strategies to reduce hysterectomy rates in Mexico.

163 | Trends in Peripartum Hysterectomy Among NTSV Births in the United States, 2017–2024

Serena M. Jones1; Frank I. Jackson2; Sarah H. Abelman2; Oladunni Ogundipe2; Matthew J. Blitz2

1New York Institute of Technology College of Osteopathic Medicine; Northwell Health, Northwell Health, NY; 2Northwell Health, Bay Shore, NY

Objective: To examine national trends in peripartum hysterectomy by delivery route among nulliparous, term, singleton, vertex (NTSV) births in the United States from 2017–2024.

Study Design: This retrospective cohort study used U.S. National Vital Statistics System (NVSS) natality data from 2017–2024 to evaluate peripartum hysterectomy among NTSV births. Peripartum hysterectomy was identified using the NVSS maternal morbidity indicator. Analyses were conducted separately for NTSV vaginal and NTSV cesarean births. Annual route‐specific hysterectomy rates per 10,000 births were assessed over time using Joinpoint regression.

Results: A total of 9,763,466 NTSV births were included from 2017–2024, including 7,209,720 vaginal births and 2,553,746 cesarean births. Overall hysterectomy rates were 0.75 per 10,000 among NTSV vaginal births and 4.82 per 10,000 among NTSV cesarean births. In Joinpoint analyses, the NTSV vaginal series showed a significant joinpoint in 2019, with rates increasing from 2017–2019 (APC 28.85%, 95% CI 3.85–68.41; p = 0.017) and decreasing from 2019–2024 (APC −7.58%, 95% CI −27.05 to −1.92; p = 0.024) (Figure 1). The NTSV cesarean series showed no significant temporal trend from 2017–2024 (APC 2.20%, 95% CI −0.56 to 5.15; p = 0.110) (Figure 2).

Conclusion: Peripartum hysterectomy among NTSV births was rare but occurred at substantially higher rates after cesarean than vaginal delivery. Vaginal hysterectomy rates changed over time, whereas cesarean rates remained stable. These findings may reflect differences in the effectiveness of uterine‐sparing strategies and postpartum hemorrhage management between delivery routes.

164 | Economic Impact of IGFBP4/SHBG Screen‐Guided Preterm Birth Interventions: Results From the PRIME Randomized Trial

Paul E. Kearney1; Eric Stanek2; David J. Burchfield3; Barbara S. Levy4; Brian K. Iriye5; A. Mark Fendrick6; Sean D. Sullivan7

1Sera Prognostics, Inc., Salt Lake City, UT; 2Carelon Research, Indianapolis, IN; 3University of Florida, Gainesville, FL; 4Visana Health Inc., La Jolla, CA; 5High Risk Pregnancy Center; Hera Women's Health, Las Vegas, NV; 6University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, MI; 7University of Washington, Seattle, WA

Objective: Preterm birth (PTB) is a leading cause of neonatal morbidity, mortality, and high neonatal healthcare costs, driven largely by neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) hospitalization. Most PTBs occur in pregnancies without apparent clinical risk factors. The IGFBP4/SHBG biomarker blood test identifies pregnancies at higher PTB risk early in gestation, enabling targeted interventions. The PRIME multicenter randomized controlled trial demonstrated reduced NICU admissions with IGFBP4/SHBG screen‐guided care. We assessed economic impact and neonatal outcomes of PRIME IGFBP4/SHBG screen‐guided care versus routine care from commercial and Medicaid payer perspectives.

Study Design: We conducted a trial‐based cost‐consequence analysis alongside the PRIME trial (n = 4,550 after prespecified exclusions). All participants were tested; results were disclosed in the screen‐guided care arm and blinded in the routine care arm. Higher‐risk screen‐guided care participants (≥15% PTB risk) received bundled interventions (vaginal progesterone, low‐dose aspirin, and nurse‐led care management), while lower‐risk and control participants received usual care. Costs included testing, intervention, and NICU hospitalization through discharge. Outcomes were the NICU admission rate and cost per pregnancy screened. Uncertainty was assessed using bootstrap resampling and sensitivity analyses across three test prices ($550, $750, $950).

Results: Screen‐guided care was associated with a 22.3% relative reduction in NICU admissions (absolute reduction 3.0% [95% CI, 1.0–4.9%]). At the base test price of $750, savings were $1,213 per pregnancy screened (95% CI, −$3,070‐$337). Across multiple test price scenarios ($550–$950), screen‐guided care consistently reduced both NICU admissions and overall costs, with the effect sustained across 89–97% of analyses.

Conclusion: In a low‐risk obstetric population, screen‐guided prevention was dominant, demonstrating both improved clinical outcomes and reduced costs relative to usual care, supporting the clinical and economic value of IGFBP4/SHBG‐based screening for PTB risk in routine prenatal care.

165 | Routine Prenatal Ultrasound at 36 Weeks: Impact on Obstetric Clinical Outcomes

Monica Minopoli1; Sofia Ceffa1; Anat Schwartz2; Federica Fontanella2; Veronica Giorgione2; Amarnath Bhide3; Basky Thilaganathan3

1St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, London, United Kingdom; 2St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, United Kingdom; 3St George's University Hospital, London, United Kingdom

Objective: Recommendations remain inconsistent regarding routine third‐trimester ultrasound because, without a clearly defined management protocol, it has limited impact on outcomes. The rarity of severe adverse events such as stillbirth and obstetric intervention bias further weakens the evidence. This study evaluated the impact of introducing a routine 36‐week prenatal ultrasound integrated with a predefined management pathway on obstetric outcomes, focusing on timing of birth for small‐for‐gestational‐age (SGA) and large‐for‐gestational‐age (LGA) fetuses, and detection of breech presentation.

Study Design: This retrospective study included 37,001 singleton term pregnancies delivered between April 2016 and October 2024. From January 2020, policy changed from targeted to universal 36‐week ultrasound with a predefined national management pathway. Primary outcomes were gestational age at birth in SGA and LGA neonates, and prenatal diagnosis of breech presentation. Secondary outcomes included neonatal intensive care unit admission, severe perineal trauma, shoulder dystocia, and stillbirth. Statistical analyses included descriptive statistics, chi‐square tests, odds ratios, linear regression, and interrupted time series analysis (ITSA).

Results: The introduction of routine 36‐week ultrasound significantly increased the proportion of SGA infants delivered before 38 weeks from 18.0% to 37.5% post‐intervention (p = 0.02), with a concomitant reduction in NICU admissions (OR = 0.74, 95% CI: 0.66–0.82). Prenatal detection of breech presentation increased from 78% to almost 90% (p = 0.02) and was associated with a significant reduction in third‐ and fourth‐degree perineal tears (OR = 0.74, 95% CI: 0.65–0.84).

Conclusion: The introduction of a routine prenatal ultrasound at 36 weeks embedded within a structured clinical management pathway resulted in significant changes in obstetric metrics related to improved outcomes. Overall, these findings provide real‐world evidence on how universal third‐trimester ultrasound may influence obstetric management and inform the ongoing debate on routine prenatal screening strategies.

166 | Routine Prenatal Ultrasound at 36 Weeks: Impact on Obstetric Intervention

Sofia Ceffa1; Monica Minopoli1; Anat Schwartz2; Eleonora Biancareddu1; Nikolas Sokratous1; Veronica Giorgione2; Amarnath Bhide3; Basky Thilaganathan3

1St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, London, United Kingdom; 2St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, St George's University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, United Kingdom; 3St George's University Hospital, London, United Kingdom

Objective: The role of routine third‐trimester ultrasound remains controversial, and evidence on its broader impact on obstetric practice is limited. While benefits such as improved detection of fetal growth abnormalities are recognized, less is known about unintended consequences in real‐world settings. This study aimed to assess the impact of a universal 36‐week scan on obstetric interventions and timing of birth.

Study Design: his retrospective study included 37,001 singleton term pregnancies delivered between April 2016 and October 2024. From January 2020, a policy change introduced universal 36‐week ultrasound scanning alongside a standardized management pathway for SGA, LGA, and breech presentations. Primary outcomes were gestational age at birth, induction of labor, and Caesarean section rates. Analyses included descriptive statistics, chi‐square tests, odds ratios, linear regression, and interrupted time series analysis (ITSA).

Results: Introduction of the routine scan was associated with a shift toward earlier gestational age at birth. Linear regression showed increasing pre‐intervention trends in induction and elective Caesarean rates, independent of the policy change. ITSA showed no immediate increase in emergency Caesarean rates overall, but a subsequent upward trend. In contrast, emergency Caesarean rates in EFW <10th and >90th centile pregnancies increased at implementation (+ 18.1% and + 10.4%), with no further rise. Emergency Caesarean for breech decreased by 13.9% (p = 0.01).

Conclusion: The introduction of a routine prenatal ultrasound scan at 36 weeks embedded within a structured clinical management pathway, resulted in significant changes in obstetric metrics related to improved pregnancy outcome without affecting the obstetric intervention rates. Overall, these findings provide real‐world evidence on how a universal third‐trimester ultrasound may selectively influence obstetric management and inform the ongoing debate on routine prenatal screening strategies.

167 | Association Between Birth Order and Birthweight Percentile in a Large Birth Cohort

Shanny Kolp‐Asis1; Oshrit Shtossel2; Roi Yozevitch3; Karin Edut4; Ariel Lasry4; Tamar Goiten Inbar4; Avinoam Tzabari4; Nissim Arbib4

1Gray Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences, Tel Aviv University, Tel Aviv; 2Department of Mathematics, Bar‐Ilan University, Ramat Gan, Israel; 3Department of Computer and Software Engineering, Ariel University, Ariel, Israel; 4Gray Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences, Tel Aviv University, Tel Aviv, Israel

Objective: To examine whether birthweight percentile increases with birth order, using a gestational‐age adjusted, population‐specific reference—both at the population level and through within‐mother comparisons across successive births.

Study Design: This retrospective cohort study included 130,718 singleton live births from 55,057 women delivering at a high‐parity hospital (2012–2024). Birthweight percentiles were calculated using a national, sex‐specific standard. Birth order was the primary exposure; the outcome was birthweight percentile. Linear mixed‐effects models assessed population‐level associations, adjusting for maternal age, diabetes, and infant sex. Within‐mother analyses evaluated whether birthweight percentile increased across births within the same woman.

Results: Later‐born infants had significantly higher birthweight percentiles than first‐borns. Each additional birth was associated with an average 3‐point increase in percentile (β ≈ 0.075 SD, p < 0.001), consistent across model subsets. Within‐mother analyses confirmed this pattern: 55% of women with two births and ∼80% of those with ≥3 births showed increasing percentiles with birth order. Nonetheless, ∼20% of mothers had flat or negative trends, reflecting individual variation.

Conclusion: Parity is a consistent predictor of higher birthweight percentile. This modest yet robust effect—observed both across and within mothers—suggests physiological adaptations such as improved placental function and reduced maternal constraint in subsequent pregnancies. Consideration of parity may enhance the interpretation of fetal growth percentiles in clinical care.

168 | Predictors of Combined Vaginal–Cesarean Delivery in Twin Gestations

Shanny Kolp‐Asis1; Tal Ayalon2; Ariel Statman2; Elad Miron2; Karin Edut2; Ariel Lasry2; Oz Gavish2

1Gray Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences, Tel Aviv University, Tel Aviv; 2Gray Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences, Tel Aviv University, Tel Aviv, Israel

Objective: To identify predictors of combined vaginal–cesarean delivery (vaginal delivery of the first twin followed by cesarean delivery of the second twin) in twin gestations undergoing trial of labor.

Study Design: Retrospective cohort study of twin gestations without prior cesarean delivery undergoing trial of labor at a single tertiary center (2011–2024). Analysis was restricted to pregnancies in which the first twin was delivered vaginally. The primary outcome was combined vaginal–cesarean delivery. Multivariable logistic regression models were constructed including maternal age, parity, gestational age at delivery, diabetes‐related diagnosis, induction of labor, and birthweight discordance. To avoid collinearity, first‐ and second‐twin small for gestational age (SGA) were evaluated in separate models.

Results: Among eligible twin gestations, combined vaginal–cesarean delivery was uncommon. In adjusted analyses, both first‐ and second‐twin SGA were independently associated with increased odds of combined delivery. First‐twin SGA was associated with nearly a tenfold increase in risk (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 9.97; 95% confidence interval [CI], 2.53–39.22), and second‐twin SGA with a sevenfold increase (aOR, 7.19; 95% CI, 1.83–28.27). Induction of labor showed a non‐significant trend toward increased risk, whereas maternal age, parity, gestational age, diabetes, and birthweight discordance were not significantly associated with the outcome.

Conclusion: In twin gestations with successful vaginal delivery of the first twin, SGA in either twin is strongly associated with increased risk of combined vaginal–cesarean delivery. These findings suggest that fetal size, rather than labor characteristics, plays a key role in determining delivery mode of the second twin and may inform intrapartum counseling and management.

169 | An Externally Validated Machine Learning Model to Predict Stillbirth and Understand Risk Factors

Vivienne Souter1; Tomas Bosschieter2; Rich Caruana3; Ian Painter1; Kristin Sitcov1; Asma Khalil4

1Foundation for Health Care Quality, Foundation for Health Care Quality, Seattle, WA; 2Stanford University, Stanford University, Palo Alto, CA; 3Intelligible, Inc, Seattle, WA; 4City St George's, University of London, London, United Kingdom

Objective: To develop and externally validate an interpretable machine learning model to predict stillbirth using routinely collected variables from the first prenatal visit.

Study Design: Retrospective cohort study including data for 212,309 singleton births at 24–43 weeks’ gestation from 22 US sites (Jan 2016‐June 2025): 211,863 were livebirths and 446 (0.21%) were stillbirths. An interpretable machine learning model (Explainable Boosting Machine) was trained to predict stillbirth using data from 155,212 births at 12 hospitals and externally validated using 57,097 births at 10 different sites. The model included 23 variables routinely available at the first prenatal visit (socio‐demographics, BMI, obstetric history, and medical history). Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV) and calibration were used to evaluate model performance. Relative feature importance was also evaluated. Odds ratios for stillbirth were calculated for each variable, adjusting for all other variables in the model.

Results: Model performance metrics (95% CI) on external validation were AUROC 0.685 (0.645–0.725), sensitivity 50.4% (95% CI: 49.1–52.1), specificity 79.3% (74.3–85.7), and PPV 1.2% (95% CI:0.9–1.5) (Figure 1). Calibration was acceptable and tended to slightly overestimate risk. BMI contributed most to the model prediction with increasing risk from approximately 27 kg/m2. Previous stillbirth, previous preterm birth, substance use, and diabetes had the highest odds of stillbirth (Figure 2). People who were Black had increased stillbirth risk even after adjustment for medical factors and socioeconomic status. Previous cesarean birth was a statistically significant risk factor.

Conclusion: Our model had modest performance for stillbirth prediction limiting its utility for population screening. However, it has promise as a tool for estimating individualized stillbirth risk as part of tailored prenatal care. Interpretable machine learning provided insights into risk factors for stillbirth including sociodemographic factors and previous cesarean birth.

170 | Pre‐Delivery Hemoglobin and Postnatal Outcomes in Pregnancies Complicated by Sickle Cell Disease

Jamison Lee; Rebecca Crowe; Amartha Gore; Christina Abrams; Eliza R. McElwee; Eugene Y. Chang

Medical University of South Carolina, Charleston, SC

Objective: The purpose of this study was to investigate the relationship between pre‐delivery hemoglobin levels and postnatal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by sickle cell disease.

Study Design: This retrospective cohort study included pregnancies complicated by sickle cell disease (SCD) between 2009–2024. Patients were stratified by pre‐delivery hemoglobin levels into two groups: ≥or <9 g/dL. The primary outcome was a composite measure of postpartum maternal morbidity consisting of postpartum hemorrhage, blood transfusion, infection, and intensive care unit (ICU) admission. Logistic regression was used to estimate the odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for the primary outcome between groups. Data were analyzed using chi‐square test and continuous variables were assessed using t‐test as appropriate.

Results: A total of 153 pregnancies among 113 patients were included in the analysis. 49.0% (n = 75) had pre‐delivery hemoglobin ≥9 g/dL while 51.0% (n = 78) had hemoglobin <9 g/dL. Hemoglobin values ranged from 5.6 g/dL to 12.7 g/dL. Cesarean section occurred more frequently in patients with hemoglobin <9 g/dL (64.1 v 37.3%, p = 0.001). Additionally, there was an association between lower birthweight and pre‐delivery hemoglobin <9 g/dL (p = 0.03). The composite postpartum morbidity occurred more frequently in the hemoglobin <9 g/dL group as compared to the ≥9 g/dL group (47.4% vs. 29.3%, p = 0.02). Of individual morbidity components, postpartum transfusion rates were significantly higher in the <9 g/dL group (47.4% vs 17.3%, p = 0.001). When compared to the reference group (hemoglobin ≥9 g/dL), the odds of composite postpartum morbidity were 2.17 with a 95% CI 1.11–4.23. This association did not reach statistical significance when adjusted for mode of delivery and pregnancy related hypertension (aOR 1.45, 95% CI 0.69 – 3.04).

Conclusion: In pregnancies affected by SCD, lower pre‐delivery hemoglobin was associated with increased rates of postpartum morbidity. These findings emphasize the benefit of optimizing maternal hemoglobin prior to delivery.

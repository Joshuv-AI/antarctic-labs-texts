The superposition principle for local 1-dimensional currents
L. Ambrosio, F. Renzi, F. Vitillaro

Abstract
. We prove that every one-dimensional locally normal metric current, intended in the sense of U. Lang and S. Wenger, admits a nice integral representation through currents associated to (possibly unbounded) curves with locally finite length, generalizing the result shown by E. Paolini and E. Stepanov in the special case of Ambrosio-Kirchheim normal currents. Our result holds in Polish spaces, or more generally in complete metric spaces for 1-currents with tight support.



1.
Introduction

Superposition principles are very useful tools in Analysis and Probability, as they allow to factorize a problem into lower dimensional ones.
Classical examples are the coarea formula for
BV
functions, the slicing theory of currents and the Optimal Transport problem with cost=distance (in the latter
case, the decomposition being given by transport rays). In the context of 1-dimensional currents, it is particularly interesting to extend the classical S. Smirnov’s
[
Smi93
]
superposition principle even to the case when the ambient space is a metric space, having in mind the well-posedness of ODE’s in metric measure spaces
(see
[
AT14
]
,
[
ST17
]
) or the theory of Optimal Transport, see
[
PS12
]
and
[
PS13
]
.

In this paper we prove that every one-dimensional locally normal metric current admits a decomposition, thus extending the results of E. Paolini and E. Stepanov in
[
PS12
]
and
[
PS13
]
for Ambrosio-Kirchheim normal currents.
A notion of local metric current was first introduced by U. Lang in
[
Lan11
]
. Later, he gave, alongside S. Wenger, a different, non equivalent definition, in
[
LW11
]
. We will work with the latter (recalled in
Section

2.1
below). Roughly speaking, this notion generalizes Ambrosio-Kirchheim metric currents by requiring finite mass only on bounded sets. In
Section

3
of this paper, we provide the natural concepts of “subcurrent” and “acyclic current” for this class of objects.
As it is customary in this field, we will work with the requirement that the mass measures of currents are tight, which is not restrictive, for instance, when the ambient metric space is Polish (see also Remark
2.1
).

Let us define, preliminarly, the classes of curves we will be dealing with. As in
[
PS12
]
and
[
PS13
]
we consider compact
curves with finite length (which always admit a Lipschitz parameterization) and the distance induced by the sup norm
when we consider the invariance under reparameterization:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(E,d)
be a complete metric space. We equip the set of Lipschitz curves
\theta:[0,1]\rightarrow E
with the distance

(1.1)

d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}):=\inf\left\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}d(\theta_{1}(t),\theta_{2}(\phi(t)))\,:\,\phi\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]\mbox{ bijective increasing}\right\},

and call two curves
\theta_{i}:[0,1]\to E
,
i=1,2
, equivalent, if
d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=0
.

The set of equivalence classes of Lipschitz curves equipped with the distance
d_{\Theta}
will be denoted by
\Theta(E)
.

Remark 1.2
.

(\Theta(E),d_{\Theta})
can be written as a countable union of complete subspaces of the form
\Theta_{L}=\{[\theta]\in\Theta(E):\ell(\theta)\leq L\}
,
L\in\mathbb{N}
. For example, if
E
is separable (and then
\Theta(E)
is separable as well), this gives that any finite Borel measure
\eta
on
\Theta(E)
is tight.

A notable distinction from the classical case is that, in order to maintain the transport property of the representation of acyclic currents (which comes from (
1.4
) below), highlighted in
[
PS12
]
, it is necessary to consider curves which are not necessarily of bounded length. This can be easily understood by looking at the basic example of the current associated to an unbounded simple oriented curve with a single boundary point (e.g. a half-line in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
), where, as it is somehow natural, the decomposition must be given by the curve itself. Clearly, the transport property has to be meant in a wider sense, involving the possibility of exchanging mass “with infinity” (see
Remark

4.19
). Therefore in the context of local currents, it is more convenient to consider, instead, open
ended curves with locally finite length in
E
, according to the following definition:

Definition 1.3
.

Consider

X:=\bigl\{\beta\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\rm loc}((0,1);E)\mid\ell(\beta\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}U)<\infty\,\,\text{for any bounded open set $U\subseteq E$}\bigr\},

where
\ell(\beta\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}U)=\int_{\beta^{-1}(U)}|\dot{\beta}|\,\mathrm{d}t
and
|\dot{\beta}|
is the metric derivative of
\beta
.

We define the set
\Gamma(E)
as the quotient
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{X}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\displaystyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{X}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.83437pt}{$\textstyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{X}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.34427pt}{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{X}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-0.99101pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\sim}$}}}
, where
\beta_{1}\sim\beta_{2}
iff there exists
\phi:(0,1)\rightarrow(0,1)
increasing and bijective s.t.
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}\circ\phi
.

Notice that the difference between
\Theta(E)
and
\Gamma(E)
is essentially due to the requirement of locally finite length,
since any open ended curve with finite length has a unique extension to a curve in
\Theta(E)
. Actually, the local finiteness of the length of
\beta\in\Gamma(E)
gives the following dichotomy: either
\lim_{t\to 0}\beta(t)
exists, or
\lim_{t\to 0}d(\beta(t),\bar{x})=\infty
, for any
\bar{x}\in E
(same for
t\to 1
). In addition, since all concepts
we will be dealing with are invariant under reparameterization, we will occasionally consider other open of closed intervals
as domains of our curves.

In Section
4.5
we introduce, mainly for measure-theoretic purposes, the topology
\tau_{\Gamma}
induced by the map

\beta\mapsto T_{\beta}
in (
2.22
), canonically associating to
\beta\in\Gamma(E)
the local 1-dimensional current in
E
. In this
connection, notice that this topology is not Hausdorff in
\Gamma(E)
since
T_{\beta}=T_{\tilde{\beta}}
does not imply that

\beta
is a reparameterization of
\tilde{\beta}
(for example, concatenating
\beta
with two copies with opposite orientation of a curve
\alpha
with
\alpha(0)=\beta(1)
does not change
T_{\beta}
).

Our main result is the following (here
\mathbf{N}_{1,b}(E)
denotes the class of local and normal
1
-dimensional currents in
E
,
see Definition
2.3
and Definition
2.12
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(E,d)
be a complete metric space and let
T\in\mathbf{N}_{1,b}(E)
. Then there exists
a positive Borel measure
\eta
over
\Gamma(E)
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle T=\int_{\Gamma(E)}T_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(\gamma),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|T\|=\int_{\Gamma(E)}\|T_{\gamma}\|\,\mathrm{d}\eta(\gamma).

Moreover,
T
can be decomposed as
T=A+C
, where
C
is a cycle of
T
and
A
is acyclic.
Finally, denoting by
\eta_{A}
the measure associated to
A
as in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), we have
that
\eta_{A}
-almost every curve is injective, and that

(1.4)

\|\partial T\|=\|\partial A\|=\int_{\Gamma(E)}\|\partial T_{\gamma}\|\,\mathrm{d}\eta_{A}(\gamma).

In this generality,
Theorem

1.4
represents a new result even in the Euclidean setting. However, our proof-technique is intrinsically metric, in the sense that it relies on the validity of the statement for normal currents in general metric spaces, due to Paolini and Stepanov, even when our space
E
is Euclidean. Indeed, we exploit a simple trick which consists in
using a
\|T\|
-dependent “conformally” modified distance
\delta
(see
Section

4.3
) of the distance
d
, turning
T
into a current
\overline{T}
of finite mass with respect to
\delta
in the completion
\overline{E}
of
E
with respect to
\delta
. Therefore, when the mass of
\partial T
is assumed to be already finite, we reduce ourselves to an application of Paolini and Stepanov’s result, provided we are able to show that still
\partial\overline{T}
has finite mass (this is not
obvious, see Example
4.13
, but it can be obtained at the expense of embedding isometrically our spaces into
\ell^{\infty}
).
We recall here Paolini and Stepanov’s result for further use (cfr.
[
PS12
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
PS13
, Corollary 3.3]
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{N}_{1}(E)
. Then there exists a finite positive Borel measure
\bar{\eta}
over
\Theta(E)
with total mass
\bar{\eta}(\Theta(E))\leq\mathbb{M}(T)+\mathbb{M}(\partial T)
such that

(1.5)

\displaystyle T(\omega)=\int_{\Theta(E)}T_{\theta}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\eta}(\theta)\quad\forall\,\omega=f\,\mathrm{d}\pi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{b}}(E)\times\operatorname{Lip}(E),

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}(T)=\int_{\Theta(E)}\mathbb{M}(T_{\theta})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\eta}(\theta)=\int_{\Theta(E)}\ell(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\eta}(\theta),

with
\bar{\eta}
-a.e.
\theta\in\Theta(E)
contained in
\operatorname{supp}(T)
.

Furthermore,
T
can be decomposed into a sum
T=A+C
where
\partial C=0
,
C\leq T
and
A
is acyclic.
In addition,

(i)

The measure
\eta_{A}
obtained by applying the result on
A
as in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
)
satisfies
\eta_{A}(1)=(\partial T)^{+}
,
\eta_{A}(0)=(\partial T)^{-}
and

\eta_{A}
-a.e. curve in
\Theta(E)
is injective; in particular

(1.7)

\mathbb{M}(\partial T)=\mathbb{M}(\partial A)=\int_{\Theta(E)}\mathbb{M}(\partial T_{\theta})\,\mathrm{d}\eta_{A}(\theta).

(ii)

the measure
\eta_{C}
obtained by applying the result on
C
as in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) satisfies
\eta_{C}(0)=\eta_{C}(1)=\|C\|
and

\mathbb{M}(T_{\theta})=\ell(\theta)=1\qquad\text{for $\eta_{C}$-a.e. curve in $\Theta(E)$.}

We remark that the main technical difficulties faced in order to state and prove
Theorem

1.4
arise from the issue of infinite mass for the current
T
, which was also the original motivation for this work, see the forthcoming paper
[
AILP
]
where this problem is raised. On the other hand, dropping the condition
\mathbb{M}(\partial T)<\infty
, which is done in
Section

3
, is relatively easier. Indeed, first in
Theorem

3.8
we prove the decomposition
T=A+C
with
C
(maximal) subcycle of
T
and
A
acyclic, for any
m
-dimensional local current
T
. Then, focusing on the acyclic part in the case
m=1
, we show that it can be written as a countable sum of subcurrents with finite boundary, with no cancellations even at the level of boundaries, see
Theorem

3.9
, a result which may also be of independent interest. This is done almost by hand, using the local finiteness of
\|\partial T\|
and applying
Theorem

1.5
on the restriction of
T
to balls in a clever way.

To conclude, note that our work does not provide a result of decomposition of cycles in elementary subcycles, as it was done in the case of finite mass already in
[
Smi93
]
and again in
[
PS13
]
in the metric setting, involving averages on “almost periodic paths” or “solenoids” (see
[
PS13
, Section 4]
for precise definitions and statements in this sense) .
Solenoids cannot in general be decomposed into closed curves, and so in some sense they are the most elementary 1-dimensional cycles which can be used for this purpose.

In our case, the matter is that a decomposition in solenoids is not preserved by reversing the metric construction that we perform to reduce ourselves to the case
\mathbb{M}(T)<\infty
, and so its existence does not seem to follow from our approach.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section,
(E,d)
will denote a generic metric space. We denote by
\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{b}}(E)
and
\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)
the spaces of bounded Lipschitz functions and of Lipschitz functions with bounded support in
E
respectively. We denote by

{\mathcal{B}}(E)
the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets and by
{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(E)\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(E)

the class of bounded Borel sets. Analogously, we denote by
\mathcal{B}^{\infty}(E)
and

\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bs}}^{\infty}(E)
the class of bounded Borel functions and bounded
Borel functions with bounded support, respectively.

In the sequel,
\mathbf{M}_{0}(E)
denotes the class of Borel
\sigma
-additive measures

\mu:{\mathcal{B}}(E)\to\mathbb{R}
with finite total variation concentrated on a
\sigma
-compact set
and by
\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
the class of set functions
\mu:{\mathcal{B}}_{b}(E)

such that
B\mapsto\mu(U\cap B)
belongs to
\mathbf{M}_{0}(E)
for any bounded Borel set
U
.
Clearly any nonnegative
\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
can be canonically and monotonically extended
to a
\sigma
-additive set function, for which we use the same notation
\mu
, defined on the whole of
{\mathcal{B}}(E)
,
which then belongs to
\mathbf{M}_{0}(E)
iff
\mu(E)<\infty
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the “tightness” requirement in the previous definition is automatically satisfied if
E
enjoys topological properties ensuring that every finite nonnegative Borel measure is tight.
This is true when
(E,d)
is a Polish space or if we assume (consistently with the Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory, as done
also in
[
AK00
]
) that the density character of every metric space considered is an Ulam number (see
[
Bog07
, Chapters 1 and 7]
).

2.1.
(Local) metric currents

We now introduce the notions of metric currents that we will need, starting with the case of
0
-dimensional currents.

Definition 2.2

(
0
-dimensional metric currents)
.

We say that a linear functional
T:\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
0
-dimensional current with locally finite mass
if there exists a nonnegative
\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
such that

|T(f)|\leq\int_{E}|f|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\qquad\forall f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E).

It is easily seen that the class of nonnegative
\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
satisfying the inequality above
is a lattice in the class of nonnegative
\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
. The least one is called mass of
T
and denoted
by
\|T\|
.

We say that
T
has finite mass if the inequality above holds for some
\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{0}(E)
.

Clearly any
0
-dimensional current with locally finite mass is representable by integration with respect to a
measure
\mu_{T}\in\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
, namely
T(f)=\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{T}
, which shows that we can
canonically identify the class of

0
-dimensional metric currents with locally finite mass with
\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
. In the case of

0
-dimensional currents
T
with finite mass, the canonical identification is with
\mathbf{M}_{0}(E)
, so that
in particular
T(f)
is well defined when
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{b}(E)
(or even when
f
is a bounded Borel function).

Now, in the same vein, we consider the case
m>0
, where the main difference between the local and the non local
case consists in the replacement of
\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)
with

\operatorname{Lip}_{b}(E)
in the first argument of
T
, see
[
AK00
]
and
[
LW11
]
.

Definition 2.3

(Metric currents with finite and locally finite mass)
.

A functional
T(f\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m})
defined on

\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)\times\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E)\right]^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\qquad m\geq 1

is called an
m
-dimensional metric current with locally finite mass on
E
if the following properties hold:

(1)

T
is multilinear;

(2)

T
is continuous in the following sense: if
\pi^{j}_{i},\pi_{i}\in\operatorname{Lip}(E)
,

\pi_{i}^{j}\rightarrow\pi_{i}
as
j\to\infty
pointwise in
E
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
and
\sup_{i,j}\operatorname{Lip}(\pi_{i}^{j})<\infty
, then

\lim_{j\to\infty}T(f\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}^{j}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m}^{j})=T\left(f\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m}\right)\qquad\forall f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E);

(3)

T(f\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m})=0
whenever

\pi_{i}|_{\operatorname{supp}f}=0
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
;

(4)

there exists a nonnegative
\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
such that

(2.1)

\left|T(f\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m})\right|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{Lip}(\pi_{i})\int_{E}|f|\,\mathrm{d}\mu

holds for all
(f,\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{m})\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)\times\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E)\right]^{m}
.

We say that
T
has finite mass if (
2.1
) holds for some
\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{0}(E)
.
We denote by
\mathbf{M}_{m}(E)
,
\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
, the vector spaces of
m
-dimensional
metric currents with finite mass and locally finite mass, respectively, so that

\mathbf{M}_{m}(E)\subseteq\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
.

Remark 2.4

(Extensions of currents)
.

Using the density of Lipschitz functions with bounded support in
L^{1}(E,\|T\|)
, currents with locally finite mass
can be canonically extended to
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bs}}^{\infty}(E)\times\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E)\right]^{m}

retaining properties (1)-(4). Analogously, currents with finite mass can be canonically extended to

\mathcal{B}^{\infty}(E)\times\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E)\right]^{m}

retaining properties (1)-(4). In particular, the possibility to extend currents with finite mass to

\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{b}}(E)\times\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E)\right]^{m}
grants that
our presentation is consistent with the axiomatization in
[
AK00
]
, see also Remark
2.10
below.

Definition 2.5

(Mass and support of currents)
.

As in the case
m=0
, for
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
, we call mass and denote
\|T\|
the least nonnegative
measure
\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{0,b}(E)
satisfying (
2.1
). When

T\in\mathbf{M}_{m}(E)
, the total mass of
T
is defined by
\mathbb{M}(T):=\|T\|(E)
and one can
easily show that
(\mathbf{M}_{m}(E),\mathbb{M})
is a Banach space.

For
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
, the support of
T
is defined as the closed set

\operatorname{supp}T:=\operatorname{supp}\|T\|=\{z\in E:\ \|T\|(B_{r}(z))>0\quad\forall r>0\}.

If
\Sigma
is any
\sigma
-compact set with
\|T\|(E\setminus\Sigma)=0
, then
\Sigma\setminus\operatorname{supp}T
is contained in the union of countably many
\|T\|
-negligible open balls, thus

\|T\|(E\setminus\operatorname{supp}T)=0
, i.e.
\|T\|
is concentrated on
\operatorname{supp}T
.

Remark 2.6
.

Arguing as in
[
AK00
]
and
[
LW11
]
one can prove that on open
sets
A
the mass measure
\|T\|(A)
can be computed as follows:

\sup\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}T(f_{\lambda}\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{\lambda,1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{\lambda,m})

where the supremum runs among all finite families
\{(f_{\lambda},\pi_{\lambda,1},\ldots,\pi_{\lambda,m})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, with
\pi_{\lambda,i}\in\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(E)
and
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|f_{\lambda}|\leq\chi_{A}
.

Along with the strong notion of convergence of currents given by the mass norm
\mathbb{M}
, it is often useful to consider the usual weak convergence.

Definition 2.7

(Weak convergence)
.

Let
T_{n},\,T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
, we say that
T_{n}
is weakly convergent to
T
, and write
T_{n}\rightharpoonup T
, if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{n}(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\ldots\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m})=T(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\ldots\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m})

for all
(f,\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{m})\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)\times\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E)\right]^{m}
.

Note that, by
Remark

2.6
,
\|T\|(A)
is lower semicontinuous under weak convergence for any open set
A\subseteq E
.
Also, we introduce the push-forward operator in the class of local metric currents; in the class of Ambrosio-Kirchheim (i.e. currents with finite mass) the condition that the preimage under
\varphi
of bounded sets is bounded, which grants that

f\circ\varphi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)
, is not needed.

Definition 2.8

(Push-forward)
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
, let
(E^{\prime},d^{\prime})
be another metric space and let
\varphi\in\operatorname{Lip}(E,E^{\prime})
be such that
\varphi^{-1}(B)
is bounded for any bounded set
B\subseteq E^{\prime}
. We define a function

\varphi_{\#}T:\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E^{\prime})\times\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E^{\prime})\right]^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\varphi_{\#}T(f\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi_{m}):=T(f\circ\varphi\,\,\mathrm{d}(\pi_{1}\circ\varphi)\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}(\pi_{m}\circ\varphi))

and call it the push-forward under
\varphi
of
T
. Note that
\varphi_{\#}T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E^{\prime})

with
\|\varphi_{\#}T\|\leq\bigl[\operatorname{Lip}(\varphi)\bigr]^{m}\varphi_{\#}\|T\|
.

Definition 2.9

(Restriction)
.

For
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
,
g\in\mathcal{B}^{\infty}(E)
,
\tau\in\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E)\right]^{k}
,
0\leq k\leq m
, we denote by
T\,\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(g,\tau)\in\mathbf{M}_{m-k,b}(E)
the restriction of
T
to
(g,\tau)
, defined by

T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(g\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{k})(f\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m-k}):=T(fg\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{k}\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m-k}).

In the case
k=0
, notice that
\|T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}g\|=|g|\|T\|
,
so that
\mathbb{M}(T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}g)=\int_{E}|g|\,\mathrm{d}\|T\|<\infty
if
g\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bs}}^{\infty}(E)
. In that case, we will use the simplified notation
gT:=T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}g
. If
B\subseteq E
Borel, we will also denote
T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B\,:=T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\chi_{B}=\chi_{B}T
.

Remark 2.10
.

Using multiplication by elements in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{bs}}^{\infty}(E)
, several properties established for currents with finite mass can be immediately extended to currents with locally finite mass, in particular
the alternating property, which justifies the “wedge” notation we used from the very beginning (alternatively,
one can borrow results from
[
LW11
]
).

Definition 2.11

(Boundary)
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
. We define its boundary

\partial T:\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)\times\left[\operatorname{Lip}(E)\right]^{m-1}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\partial T(f\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m-1}):=T(\sigma\,\,\mathrm{d}f\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m-1}),

where
\sigma\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)
is any function satisfying

\sigma|_{\operatorname{supp}f}\equiv 1
.

Notice that the definition above is well-posed by the locality property (
3
) of metric currents, as different choices of
\sigma
do not change the resulting current.
Furthermore, we have the following Leibniz rule (see e.g.
[
LW11
, Eq. (11)]
):

(2.2)

\partial(T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\rho)=(\partial T)\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\rho+T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\qquad\forall\,T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E),\,\rho\in\operatorname{Lip}(E).

In general
\partial T
need not have finite or locally finite mass. This motivates the next definition.

Definition 2.12

(Normal and locally normal currents)
.

We denote by
\mathbf{N}_{m}(E)
the class of all
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m}(E)
and
\partial T\in\mathbf{M}_{m-1}(E)
. Elements of
\mathbf{N}_{1}(E)
are called normal
m
-dimensional currents. The class

\mathbf{N}_{m,b}(E)\subseteq\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
of locally normal
m
-dimensional
currents is defined analogously, by requiring

\partial T\in\mathbf{M}_{m-1,b}(E)
.

Remark 2.13
.

It is easy to see that weak convergence of normal currents is stable under the operation of boundary.

In the class of normal metric currents we have the following compactness theorem (cfr.
[
AK00
, Theorem 5.2]
).

Theorem 2.14

(Compactness)
.

Let
(T_{n})\subseteq\mathbf{N}_{m}(E)
be such that
\sup_{n}(\mathbb{M}(T_{n})+\mathbb{M}(\partial T_{n}))<\infty
,
satisfying the following equi-tightness condition: for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K_{\varepsilon}\subseteq E
such that

\|T_{n}\|(E\setminus K_{\varepsilon})+\|\partial T_{n}\|(E\setminus K_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then there exists a subsequence
(T_{n(k)})
weakly convergent to
T\in\mathbf{N}_{m}(E)
.

Using this reasult we can easily infer a suitable variant, holding for equi-bounded and equi-tight sequences of local currents.

Corollary 2.15

(Compactness for local currents)
.

Let
(T_{n})\subseteq\mathbf{N}_{m,b}(E)
be such that for any
A\subseteq E
open and bounded the following two conditions hold:

(1)

\sup_{n}(\|T_{n}\|(A)+\|\partial T_{n}\|(A))<\infty
;

(2)

for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K_{\varepsilon}\subseteq A
such that

\|T_{n}\|(A\setminus K_{\varepsilon})+\|\partial T_{n}\|(A\setminus K_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then there exists a subsequence
(T_{n(k)})
weakly convergent to
T\in\mathbf{N}_{m,b}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a cut-off function s.t.
\chi\equiv 1
on
(-\infty,0]
,
\chi\equiv 0
on
[1,+\infty)
,
\chi
1-Lipschitz. Fix
\bar{x}\in E
and set, for every
R\in\mathbb{N}
,
\chi_{R}:E\rightarrow[0,1]
,
\chi_{R}(x):=\chi(d(x,\bar{x})-R)
. We study the sequence
(\chi_{R}T_{n})
, with
R
fixed. If
B
is any Borel set, exploiting (
2.2
) and
\|T_{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\,\mathrm{d}\chi_{R}\|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(\chi_{R})\|T_{n}\|
we obtain

(2.3)

\|\chi_{R}T_{n}\|(B)+\|\partial(\chi_{R}T_{n})\|(B)\leq 2\|T_{n}\|(B\cap\overline{B}_{R+1}(\bar{x}))+\|\partial T_{n}\|(B\cap\overline{B}_{R+1}(\bar{x})).

Then, combining (
2.3
) with the hypotheses on
(T_{n})
, we are able to apply
Theorem

2.14
to
(\chi_{R}T_{n})
. By a diagonal argument, there exists a subsequence
(T_{n(k)})
such that
\chi_{R}T_{n(k)}\rightharpoonup T^{R}
for some
T^{R}\in\mathbf{N}_{m}(E)
, for all
R\in\mathbb{N}
. We then define a local
m
-dimensional current
T
by

T(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\pi_{m}):=T^{R}(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{m}),\quad\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq B_{R}(\bar{x}),

where
(f,\pi)\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)\times[\operatorname{Lip}(E)]^{m}
. Note that, independently from the choice of
R
, we get (as
\chi_{R}f=f
)

T(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\pi_{m})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}T_{n(k)}(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\pi_{m}),

which proves both that
T
is well-defined and the convergence
T_{n(k)}\rightharpoonup T
.
∎

Later in this work we will need the following estimate, stated and proved for simplicity only in the
1
-dimensional case.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{N}_{1,b}(E)
,
x_{0}\in E
. Then
T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0})\in\mathbf{N}_{1}(E)
and

(2.4)

\|\partial(T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))\|(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))\leq\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}r}\|T\|(\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))

for a.e.
r>0
.

Proof.

By the local finiteness of the boundary mass,
\|\partial T\|(\partial B_{r}(x_{0}))=0
for almost all
r>0
.

Furthermore, the function
\alpha(r)=\|T\|(\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
is increasing, hence, it is almost everywhere differentiable. Fix a radius
r>0
satisfying all these conditions.
Note that
T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\rho_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0})
weakly, where

\rho_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\,\in\,B_{r}(x_{0})\\
1-n(d(x,x_{0})-r)&\text{if }x\,\in\,B_{r+\frac{1}{n}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})\\
0&\text{else.}\end{cases}

Based on the conditions on

r
, the semicontinuity of mass with respect to weak convergence,
Eq.

2.2
and the fact that

\operatorname{Lip}(\rho_{n})\leq n
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|\partial(T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))\|(E)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\partial(T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\rho_{n})\|(E)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|(\partial T)\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\rho_{n}\|(E)+\|T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(\sigma\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{n})\|(E)

\displaystyle=\|\partial T\|(\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\left(\sigma\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{n}\right)\right\|(E)

\displaystyle=\|\partial T\|(\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\left(\chi_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{n}\right)\right\|(E)

\displaystyle=\|\partial T\|(\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))+\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}r}\|T\|(\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0})).

with
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}^{\infty}_{bs}(E)
identically equal to 1 on
B_{2r}(x_{0})
and
\chi_{n}
the characteristic function of
\overline{B}_{r+1/n}(x_{0})\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})
.
This proves that
T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0})\in\mathbf{N}_{1}(E)
. By subtracting

\|\partial(T\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))\|(B_{r}(x_{0}))=\|\partial T\|(B_{r}(x_{0}))
from both sides, we obtain (
2.4
).
∎

2.2.
Curves in metric spaces

We introduce a few basic definitions about metric curves, i.e. continuous maps
\gamma:J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\rightarrow E
, where
J
is an interval (possibly unbounded, not necessarily closed). Any restriction of
\gamma
to a subinterval
\tilde{J}\subseteq J
will be called
a subcurve of
\gamma
. A curve
\gamma:J\subseteq\mathbb{R}\to E
is called an arc if it is injective.

Recall that
\gamma\in\operatorname{AC}([a,b];E)
if

(2.5)

d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq\int_{s}^{t}g(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\qquad\text{whenever $a\leq s\leq t\leq b$}

for some
g\in L^{1}(a,b)
, so that in particular Lipschitz maps are absolutely continuous. For the following result, see for example
[
ABS21
, Theorem 9.2]
.

Definition 2.17

(Metric derivative)
.

We define the metric derivative of
\gamma
at the point
t\in\mathring{J}
as the limit

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{d(\gamma(t+h),\gamma(t))}{|h|}

whenever it exists and, in this case, we denote it by
|\dot{\gamma}|(t)
.

Theorem 2.18
.

For any
\gamma\in\operatorname{AC}([a,b];E)
the metric derivative
|\dot{\gamma}|(t)
exists for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
t\in(a,b)
, it belongs to
L^{1}(a,b)
and it is the least admissible
g
in (
2.5
).

Definition 2.19

(length)
.

The length
\ell(\gamma)
of a curve
\gamma:J\rightarrow E
is defined as

\ell(\gamma)=\sup\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}d(\gamma(t_{i}),\gamma(t_{i+1})),\,t_{0},\ldots,t_{N}\in J,\,t_{0}<\cdots<t_{N},N\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

For
\gamma\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}(J;E)
, we have the formula
\ell(\gamma)=\int_{J}|\dot{\gamma}|\,\mathrm{d}t
.

Definition 2.20

(Reparameterization)
.

If
J,\,\tilde{J}
are intervals in
\mathbb{R}
, we say that
\tilde{\gamma}:\tilde{J}\rightarrow E
is a reparametrization of
\gamma:J\rightarrow E
if there exists an increasing bijection
\phi:\tilde{J}\rightarrow J
such that
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma\circ\phi
.

It will be convenient to localize in the ambient space
E
the notion of length as follows. When
U\subseteq E
is an open set, we will make use of the notation
\gamma\,\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}U
to denote the at most countable family
(\beta_{i})_{i\in I}
of subcurves of
\gamma
obtained by restricting
\gamma
to
\gamma^{-1}(U)
, which can be written as a countable disjoint union of open intervals in
J
.

Definition 2.21

(Localization in
E
)
.

Given a curve
\gamma:J\rightarrow E
, we define the length of
\gamma
in a open set
U\subseteq E
as

\ell(\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}U)=\sum_{i\in I}\ell(\beta_{i}),

where
\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}U=(\beta_{i})_{i\in I}
.
We say that
\gamma
has locally finite length in
E
if
\ell(\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}U)<\infty
for any
U\subseteq E
bounded.

Note that
\ell(\gamma)
is invariant under reparametrizations, and hence the same is true for
\ell(\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}U)
. The localized length can be uniquely extended to a regular Borel measure
F\mapsto\ell(\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}F)
,
F\subseteq E
Borel, which in the case
\gamma\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}(J;E)
is given by the formula

\ell(\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}F)=\int_{\gamma^{-1}(F)}|\dot{\gamma}|\,\mathrm{d}t=\gamma_{\#}(|\dot{\gamma}|\,\mathcal{L}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J)(F)\in[0,\infty].

Recall that, for
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
Borel,
\llbracket J\rrbracket\in\mathbf{M}_{1,b}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the elementary current associated to
the integration on
J
, namely
\llbracket J\rrbracket(f,\pi)=\int_{J}f\pi^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Definition 2.22
.

Given
\gamma\in\operatorname{Lip}(J;E)
with locally finite length in
E
, we associate to it the one-dimensional local current
T_{\gamma}\in\mathbf{M}_{1,b}(E)
given by
T_{\gamma}:=\gamma_{\#}\llbracket J\rrbracket
, that is

T_{\gamma}(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi)=\int_{J}f(\gamma(t))(\pi\circ\gamma)^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad(f,\pi)\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)\times\operatorname{Lip}(E).

Assuming without loss of generality
J=(0,1)
, note that if
\gamma
has finite length then it can be uniquely extended to
[0,1]
and it holds

\partial T_{\gamma}=\delta_{\gamma(1)}-\delta_{\gamma(0)}.

Using the definition of
\|T_{\gamma}\|
one can easily prove that

(2.6)

\|T_{\gamma}\|\leq\gamma_{\#}\bigl(|\dot{\gamma}|{\mathcal{L}}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J\bigr).

Moreover, it is worth to mention that the area formula for metric-valued Lipschitz maps (see for instance
[
AK00a
]
,
[
AT04
]
) gives

\gamma_{\#}\bigl(|\dot{\gamma}|{\mathcal{L}}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}J\bigr)=N{\mathcal{H}}^{1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\gamma(J)
with
N(x)={\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\gamma^{-1}(x))
.

3.
Cyclic and Acyclic currents

Definition 3.1
.

For
T,\,S\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
,
S
is a
subcurrent
of
T
if

(3.1)

\|T\|=\|S\|+\|T-S\|.

In this case, we denote this relationship by
S\leq T
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that, to show that (
3.1
) holds, it is enough to check the identity

\|T\|(B)=\|S\|(B)+\|T-S\|(B)
for all bounded open sets
B
.
Furthermore, since the inequality
\leq
always holds, it is enough to check the identity for specific family of
bounded open sets exhausting
E
, as the family of open balls with fixed center and diverging radius.

Remark 3.3
.

For any Borel set
B\subseteq E
, it always holds
T\,\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B\leq T
.

Remark 3.4
.

If
R\leq S
and
S\leq T
, then
R\leq T
. Indeed, the subadditivity of mass gives

\|T\|=\|S\|+\|T-S\|=\|R\|+\|S-R\|+\|T-S\|\geq\|R\|+\|T-R\|

as well as the validity of the converse inequality.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
R\leq T
and
S\leq T-R
then
R\leq R+S
and
R+S\leq T
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\|T\|

\displaystyle=\|T-R\|+\|R\|=

\displaystyle=\|T-R-S\|+\|S\|+\|R\|\geq\|T-R-S\|+\|R+S\|\geq\|T\|.

In particular, all the intermediate inequalities are equalities, and the conclusion follows.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
,
(S_{n})\subseteq\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
,
S_{n}\leq T
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and suppose that
S_{n}\rightharpoonup S
weakly as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then
S\leq T
and
\|S_{n}\|(A)\rightarrow\|S\|(A)
for any bounded open set
A\subseteq E
.

Proof.

Consider the sequence
(T-S_{n})
, converging weakly to
T-S
. By the lower semicontinuity, for any bounded open set
A
, we have

\displaystyle\|S\|(A)+\|T-S\|(A)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|S_{n}\|(A)+\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|T-S_{n}\|(A))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left[\|S_{n}\|(A)+\|T-S_{n}\|(A)\right]=\|T\|(A)<\infty,

which means thanks to Remark
3.2
that
S\leq T
.
In addition, the two inequalities used in the first line have to be equalities and the first one gives

\|S\|(A)=\liminf_{n}\|S_{n}\|(A)
. But, since this holds for any subsequence of
(S_{n})
,
we find
\|S\|(A)=\lim_{n}\|S_{n}\|(A)
.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

C\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
is called a
cycle
of
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
if
C\leq T
and
\partial C=0
.
We say that
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
is
acyclic
if
C=0
is the only cycle of
T
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Every current
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
contains a cycle
C
such that
T-C
is acyclic.
In particular, any
T\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
can be decomposed as
T=A+C
, where
C
is a cycle of
T
and
A
is acyclic.

Proof.

We argue as in Section
4
, introducing an auxiliary function to evaluate the masses of the subcurrents.
Let
\zeta:E\to[0,1]
be a continuous function with
\int_{E}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\|T\|<\infty
and with

c_{n}:=\inf_{B_{n}}\zeta>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
where, as usual,
B_{n}:=B_{n}(\bar{x})
for some fixed
\bar{x}\in E
.
Set

\xi(T):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\|C\|\,\mid\,C\leq T,\,\partial C=0\right\}.

Now, if we choose a cycle
C_{1}\leq T
such that
\int_{E}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\|C_{1}\|>\xi(T)/2
, then

\xi(T-C_{1})\leq\xi(T)/2
. Indeed, if the converse inequality were true, we could find
C_{2}\leq T-C_{1}

with
\int_{E}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\|C_{2}\|>\xi(T)/2
. But then,
Proposition

3.5
would give
C_{1}+C_{2}\leq T

and

\int_{E}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\|C_{1}+C_{2}\|=\int_{E}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\|C_{1}\|+\int_{E}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\|C_{2}\|>\xi(T),

a contradiction. Proceeding in this way (and assuming that the process never stops, otherwise the
proof is trivial) we can find cycles
C_{k}
with
C_{0}=0
,
\xi(T-\sum_{0}^{k}C_{i})\leq 2^{-k}\xi(T)
and

\bigl\|\sum_{i=0}^{k}C_{i}\bigr\|=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\|C_{i}\|\leq\|T\|\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

By construction we have

\sum_{i=p}^{q}\|C_{i}\|(B_{n})\leq c_{n}^{-1}\sum_{i=p}^{q}\int_{B_{n}}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\|C_{i}\|\leq c_{n}^{-1}\sum_{i=p}^{q}\xi(T-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}C_{j})\leq c_{n}^{-1}\xi(T)\sum_{i=p}^{q}2^{-i}

for all
n,\,p,\,q\in\mathbb{N}
with
p\leq q
.
Hence, since
n
is arbitrary, the series
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}C_{i}
defines a cycle
C\in\mathbf{M}_{m,b}(E)
with
C\leq T
.
Furthermore, Proposition
3.6
gives that
T-C\leq T-\sum_{i=0}^{k}C_{i}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Finally, we conclude the proof by demonstrating that
A=T-C
is acyclic. We do so by noting that if
\tilde{C}
is a cycle of
A
, then
\tilde{C}\leq T-\sum_{i=0}^{k}C_{i}

4.
Proof of the main result

4.1.
Plan of the proof

To prove the main result, we will develop a construction which will enable the application of

1.5

We will accomplish this by turning a given local current

T\in\mathbf{N}_{1,b}(E)
once it holds for the currents
(S_{i})_{i}
which decompose

as in

(see also

).
Also, as discussed at the beginning of

T=A+C
with

,

,
\partial C=0

.

By

4.15
, we can find a positive finite measure
\overline{\eta}
in
\Theta(\overline{E})
, supported on
\{\gamma:\mathbb{M}_{\delta}(T_{\gamma})=\ell_{\delta}(\gamma)\}
, such that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\overline{T}=\int_{\Theta(\overline{E})}T_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}(\gamma),

(4.20)

\displaystyle\|\overline{T}\|_{\delta}=\int_{\Theta(\overline{E})}\|T_{\gamma}\|_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}(\gamma),

and, moreover, if
T=A
is acyclic (since then also
\overline{T}
is acyclic by
Lemma

4.16
),

(4.21)

\|\partial\overline{T}\|_{\delta}=\int_{\Theta(\overline{E})}\|\partial T_{\gamma}\|_{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}(\gamma),

and
\overline{\eta}
-a.e.
\gamma\in\Theta(\overline{E})
is an arc.

In the sequel, an open ended subcurve
\beta
of
\gamma\in\Theta(\overline{E})
is said to be a

-subcurve if

\beta:(a,b)\to E
is a subcurve of
\gamma
and
(a,b)
is a connected component of
\{t\in(0,1):\ \gamma(t)\neq x_{\infty}\}
.

Next, for
F\subseteq\Gamma(E)
we define the application

as

(4.22)

\begin{split}p_{E}(\gamma,F):=\sum_{\beta\in F}n_{\gamma}(\beta),\end{split}

where

is the number of times a
x_{\infty}
-subcurve
\beta\in\Gamma(E)
occurs as a subcurve of
\gamma

(in particular when
\gamma((0,1))\subseteq E

iff
\beta=\gamma|_{(0,1)}
).

Notice that for
\overline{\eta}
-a.e.
\gamma
all the subcurves
\beta
have finite
\delta
-length
because
\gamma
has finite length in
(\overline{E},\delta)
(in particular
\gamma
and its subcurves
\beta
have locally finite
d

thanks to
Proposition

4.5
applied to the elements of
\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}U
, for

open bounded). In addition, they all lie in
\operatorname{supp}(T)
for
\overline{\eta}
-a.e.
\gamma
(as
\operatorname{supp}(\overline{T})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(T)\,\cup\{x_{\infty}\}
) and they are all arcs

We use
p_{E}
to define a kind of push-forward measure
\eta:=(p_{E})_{\#}\overline{\eta}
, that is

(4.23)

\eta(F):=\int_{\Theta(\overline{E})}p_{E}(\gamma,F)\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}(\gamma)

for a generic Borel set

. Thanks to

4.21
below, definition (
4.23
) is well-posed. From the definition, one can easily infer the change-of-variables formula

(4.24)

\int_{\Gamma(E)}h(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta(\beta)=\int_{\Theta(\overline{E})}\sum_{\{\beta:\ n_{\gamma}(\beta)>0\}}n_{\gamma}(\beta)h(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}(\gamma)

for any

or
h

Choosing

in (

) (see

4.17
) and using (
4.20
) one obtains

(4.25)

\int_{\Gamma(E)}\|T_{\beta}\|_{\delta}(B_{R})\,\mathrm{d}\eta(\beta)=\|T\|_{\delta}(B_{R}),

where we have also exploited that for
\overline{\eta}
-a.e.
\gamma
it holds
\mathbb{M}_{\delta}(T_{\gamma})=\ell_{\delta}(\gamma)
, and thus

\|T_{\gamma}\|_{\delta}=\sum_{\{\beta:\ n_{\gamma}(\beta)>0\}}n_{\gamma}(\beta)\|T_{\beta}\|_{\delta}.

In particular, if we choose

h(\beta)=T_{\beta}(\omega),\quad\omega=(f,\pi)\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\mathrm{bs}}(E)\times\operatorname{Lip}(E),\,\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq B_{R}

then
h\in L^{1}(\Gamma(E),\eta)
(as
|h(\beta)|\leq\|T_{\beta}\|_{\delta}\,\operatorname{Lip}^{\delta}(\pi\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B_{R})\sup|f|
). Therefore, we can apply (
4.24
) to

getting exactly (
1.2
).

Then, from
Lemma

4.11
we obtain that (
4.25
) also implies

\int_{\Gamma(E)}\|T_{\beta}\|(B_{R})\,\mathrm{d}\eta(\beta)=\|T\|(B_{R}),

and thus, since

must be an equality, proving (
1.3
).

Finally, for
T
acyclic, choosing
h(\alpha)=\|\partial T_{\alpha}\|(B_{R})
, one immediately obtains (
1.4
) from (
4.21
).
Indeed (recall
Remark

4.10
and note that the boundary points in
E
of an arc
\gamma\in\Theta(\overline{E})
are exactly the union of the boundary points of the curves
\beta\in\gamma\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E
, since

\begin{split}\int_{\Gamma(E)}\|\partial T_{\beta}\|(B_{R})\,\mathrm{d}\eta(\beta)&=\int_{\Theta(\overline{E})}\sum_{\{\beta:\ n_{\gamma}(\beta)>0\}}n_{\gamma}(\beta)\|\partial T_{\beta}\|(B_{R})\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}(\gamma)\\
&=\int_{\Theta(\overline{E})}\|\partial T_{\gamma}\|(B_{R})\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\eta}(\gamma)\\
&=\|\partial\overline{T}\|(B_{R})=\|\partial T\|(B_{R}).\qed\end{split}

Remark 4.19
.

The new results allow us to recover the “transport” part of the original statement between
(\partial T)^{-}
and
(\partial T)^{+}
, with the added possibility of receiving or sending mass from infinity.
In fact, if
A
is the acyclic component of
T
given by
Theorem

3.8
, the associated decomposition measure
\eta_{A}
can be expressed as a sum of mutually singular measures
\eta_{B},\eta_{BL}
and
\eta_{BR}
, which are supported on the families of curves which are bounded, bounded on the left and bounded on the right respectively.
Then, by
Definition

1.3
we note that
e_{0\,\#}\eta_{B},e_{0\,\#}\eta_{BL},e_{1\,\#}\eta_{B}
and
e_{1\,\#}\eta_{BR}
are well defined, where we denoted by

e_{0}:\{\beta\in\Gamma(E)\,\mid\,\operatorname{lim}_{t\to 0}d(\beta(t),\bar{x})<\infty\}\to E,

e_{1}:\{\beta\in\Gamma(E)\,\mid\,\operatorname{lim}_{t\to 1}d(\beta(t),\bar{x})<\infty\}\to E

the maps

and

Finally, we see from the precedent proof that we have

e_{0\,\#}\eta_{B}+e_{0\,\#}\eta_{BL}=(\partial T)^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{1\,\#}\eta_{B}+e_{1\,\#}\eta_{BR}=(\partial T)^{+}.

Remark 4.20
.

We remark that if

is the cyclic component of

in

1.4

In the proof above, we have assumed the following technical Lemma.

Lemma 4.21
.

The function

p_{E}(\gamma,F)
is Borel-measurable on
\Theta(\overline{E})
, for any Borel set
F
in
(\Gamma(E),\tau_{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

We
prove that for all
L>0
the restriction of
p_{E}(\gamma,F)
to the Borel sets

\Theta_{L}:=\bigl\{\gamma\in\Theta(\overline{E})\mid\ell_{\delta}(\gamma)\leq L\bigr\}

is Borel measurable.
Fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we denote by
\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)
the number of maximal
x_{\infty}
-subcurves
\beta
of
\gamma\in\Theta_{L}
, counted with multiplicity,
which belong to
F
and satisfy
\|\beta-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon
, where

\|\beta-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{t\in(0,1)}\delta(\beta(t),x_{\infty}).

Notice that
\phi^{\varepsilon}_{F}(\gamma)\leq\ell_{\delta}(\gamma)/\varepsilon\leq L/\varepsilon<\infty
, and that

\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)\uparrow p_{E}(\gamma,F)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
So, it suffices to check the measurability of the maps
\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}
,
F\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(E))
.

First, we prove that
\phi^{\varepsilon}(\gamma):=\phi_{\Gamma(E)}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)

is upper semicontinuous on
\Theta_{L}
. That is, if we take
\gamma_{n}\rightarrow\gamma
in
\Theta_{L}
(where we identify
\gamma_{n}
,
\gamma
with their parametrizations on
[0,1]
which give the uniform convergence) such that (up to a subsequence)
\phi^{\varepsilon}(\gamma_{n})\geq k
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then also
\phi^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)\geq k
. Indeed, we can find
x_{\infty}
-subcurves
\beta_{1}^{n},\ldots,\beta_{k}^{n}
in
\Gamma(E)
such that

(i)

\beta_{i}^{n}
is the restriction of
\gamma_{n}
to some sub-interval
(a_{i}^{n},b_{i}^{n})\subseteq[0,1]
, with
b_{i}^{n}\leq a_{i+1}^{n}
for any
1\leq i<k
;

(ii)

\|\beta_{i}^{n}-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon
, for all
i=1,\ldots,k
.

The equicontinuity of
\gamma_{n}
, together with the condition
\|\beta_{i}^{n}-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon
, provides a uniform lower bound on

b_{i}^{n}-a_{i}^{n}
, hence one can pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
, up to subsequences, to obtain that
a_{i}^{n}\rightarrow a_{i}
,
b_{i}^{n}\rightarrow b_{i}

for some
a_{i},\,b_{i}\in[0,1]
with
a_{i}<b_{i}
. We deduce that affine reparametrizations of
\beta_{i}^{n}
(closer and closer to the identity, recall that
\gamma_{n}\rightarrow\gamma
uniformly) are uniformly convergent to
\beta_{i}=\gamma|_{(a_{i},b_{i})}
with either

\gamma(a_{i})=x_{\infty}
or
\gamma(b_{i})=x_{\infty}
, or both (at least when
k>1
) and

\|\beta_{i}-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon
. Notice that

\beta_{i}
are not necessarily
x_{\infty}
-subcurves, because they are not necessarily
E
-valued. Nevertheless any

\beta_{i}
has a subcurve
\beta_{i}^{*}
(and then a subcurve of
\gamma
) which is a
x_{\infty}
-subcurve with
\|\beta_{i}^{*}-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon
, and this proves that
\phi^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)\geq k
.

Now, we want to prove that
\phi^{\varepsilon}_{F}
is Borel measurable on
\Theta_{L}
for any
F\subseteq E
open. Note that it suffices to show it for
F\in\mathcal{E}_{R}
, for all
R\in\mathbb{N}
, where we define
\mathcal{E}_{R}\subseteq\Gamma(E)
as the class made by unions of finite intersections of the form

(4.26)

\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\left\{\beta\in\Gamma(E)\mid\bigl|T_{\beta}(f_{j}\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{j})-T_{\bar{\beta}_{j}}(f_{j}\,\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{j})\bigr|<\lambda_{j}\right\}

for
\bar{\beta}_{j}\in\Gamma(E)
,
\lambda_{j}>0
,
f_{j}\in{\rm Lip_{bs}}(E)
,
\pi_{j}\in{\rm Lip}(E)
,
j=1,\ldots,N
, with

{\rm supp}(f_{j})\subseteq B_{R}
. Indeed, since the sets of the form (
4.26
) are a basis for the topology
\tau_{\Gamma}
(letting also
R
variable), any open set can be written as an increasing union
F=\bigcup_{R\in\mathbb{N}}F_{R}
,
F_{R}\subseteq F_{R+1}
, with
F_{R}\in\mathcal{E}_{R}
, and hence
\phi^{\varepsilon}_{F}=\sup_{R}\phi^{\varepsilon}_{F_{R}}
.

Consider then
F\in\mathcal{E}_{R}
, for some
R>0
. Let also
\varepsilon_{0}:=\delta(x_{\infty},\overline{B}_{R})
, so that if some curve
\beta\in\Gamma(E)
satisfies
\|\beta-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon_{0}
then
\beta
does not intersect the ball
B_{R}
.
We show that the function
\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)
is lower semicontinuous when restricted to any of the Borel sets
A_{k,h}\cap\Theta_{L}
, where

A_{k,h}:=\{\gamma\in\Theta(\overline{E}):\,\phi^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)=k,\,\phi^{\varepsilon_{0}}(\gamma)=h\},

for
k,\,h\in\mathbb{N}
(
A_{k,h}
is Borel thanks to the measurability of
\phi^{\varepsilon},\,\phi^{\varepsilon_{0}}
). This is sufficient to prove the measurability of
\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}
: indeed, the finiteness of
\phi^{\varepsilon}
gives

\Theta(\overline{E})=\bigcup_{h}\bigcup_{k}A_{k,h}.

Now, take
\gamma_{n}\rightarrow\gamma
in
\Theta(\overline{E})
, with
\gamma_{n},\gamma\in A_{k,h}\cap\Theta_{L}
, and find

x_{\infty}
-subcurves
\beta_{1}^{n},\ldots,\beta_{k}^{n}
of
\gamma_{n}
as above. Note that the limits
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}
built as in the previous step
may not be
E
-valued and hence
\beta_{i}\,\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\,E
may consist of multiple curves. However, for any
i=1,\ldots,k
, exactly one of the
x_{\infty}
-subcurves of
\beta_{i}
,
which we will call
\beta_{i}^{*}
(possibly coinciding with
\beta_{i}
itself), satisfies
\|\beta_{i}^{*}-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon
, since otherwise we would have
\phi^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)>k
. Also, we claim that
\phi^{\varepsilon_{0}}(\gamma)=h
implies that
\beta_{i}^{*}
is the only element of
\beta_{i}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E
such that
\|\beta_{i}^{*}-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon_{0}
. This is obvious for
\varepsilon_{0}\geq\varepsilon
, while for
\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon
it can be proved constructing in the same way

x_{\infty}
-subcurves
\widetilde{\beta}_{1}^{n},\ldots,\widetilde{\beta}_{h}^{n}
of
\gamma_{n}
,
with
\|\widetilde{\beta}_{i}^{n}-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon_{0}
; exactly
k
of them will coincide with
\beta_{1}^{n},\ldots,\beta_{k}^{n}
.
This leads to the inclusion

\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\}\subseteq\{\tilde{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\beta}_{h}\}

for the limit curves, with
\tilde{\beta}_{i}
distinct, each one containing a
x_{\infty}
-subcurve with distance from
x_{\infty}
larger than

\varepsilon_{0}
. Hence, if some
\beta_{i}
were to contain more than one
x_{\infty}
-subcurve
\alpha
with

\|\alpha-x_{\infty}\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon_{0}
, we would contradict
\phi^{\varepsilon_{0}}(\gamma)=h
. Calling
\bar{\beta}_{i}
that subcurve,
the inequality
\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon
gives
\bar{\beta}_{i}=\beta_{i}^{*}
, proving the claim.
Thus, our choice
of
\varepsilon_{0}
gives

\beta_{i}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B_{R}=\beta_{i}^{*}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B_{R},

and therefore the convergence
\beta_{i}^{n}\rightarrow\beta_{i}
(thought as curves in
\Theta(\overline{E})
) combined with
Lemma

4.18
(here we use that
\gamma_{n}\in\Theta_{L}
to infer that
\ell_{\delta}(\beta_{i}^{n})
are uniformly bounded by
L
) yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\beta^{n}_{i}}(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi)=T_{\beta_{i}}(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi)=T_{\beta_{i}^{*}}(f\,\mathrm{d}\pi)\qquad\forall i=1,\ldots,k,

for all
f\in{\rm Lip_{bs}}(E)
,
\pi\in{\rm Lip}(E)
with

{\rm supp}(f)\subseteq B_{R}
.
In particular, if
\beta_{i}^{*}\in F
for some
1\leq i\leq k
then
\beta_{i}^{*}
belongs to one of the sets in (
4.26
)
and so also
\beta_{i}^{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E\in F
for

sufficiently large.
Hence,
\liminf_{n}\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma_{n})\geq\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}(\gamma)
, proving the desired semicontinuity.

To conclude, we obtain that
\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}
is Borel-measurable on
\Theta_{L}
for any Borel set
F
noticing that the family

\mathcal{M}:=\bigl\{F\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma(E)):\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}\mbox{ is Borel-measurable on }\Theta_{L}\bigr\}

is a Dynkin system containing the
\pi
-system of open sets. Indeed, if
F\in\mathcal{M}
then also
\Gamma(E)\setminus F\in\mathcal{M}
, because

\phi^{\varepsilon}-\phi_{F}^{\varepsilon}

is Borel-measurable, since we have already checked that
\Gamma(E)\in\mathcal{M}
, and the difference makes sense as
\phi^{\varepsilon}
only attains finite values. Furthermore, the implication

,
F_{n}
disjoint
\Rightarrow\,\bigcup_{n}F_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
is trivial.
Thus, by Dynkin’s
\pi
-
\lambda
Theorem,
\mathcal{M}
contains the whole Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Eventually we let
\varepsilon\to 0
and
L\to\infty
.
∎

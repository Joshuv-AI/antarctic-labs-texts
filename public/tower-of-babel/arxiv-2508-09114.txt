Preperiodic points, finiteness, and structures of semigroups of algebraic automorphisms
Thomas J. Tucker, Jason P. Bell

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore a variety of finiteness questions for preperiodic points of morphisms. We begin by treating a group action analog of the Burnside problem for torsion groups using the p p -adic arc method. We then prove some results connecting commonality of preperiodic points for elements of an automorphism group with structural properties of the group; these results are related to well-known results of Tits and Borel. We finish by proving some Northcott-type results for finite morphisms.

1.
Introduction

Beaumont
[
Bea25
]
recently proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{P}_{K}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{K}^{n}
where
\deg f,\deg g>1
and
K
is
any field. If
\Prep(f)\not=\Prep(g)
, then the semigroup generated
by
f
and
g
under composition is the free semigroup on two generators.

This built on earlier work of
[
BHPT24
]
, who showed that under the
same hypotheses, there is an
m
such that the semigroup generated by

f^{m}
and
g^{m}
under composition is the free semigroup on two
generators. The idea of Beaumont’s proof, which follows that of

[
BHPT24
]
, is to use the fact that
f
and
g
induce contraction
maps on a suitable space of height functions and then show that any two
contraction maps satisfying a natural restriction on their multipliers
will generate a free semigroup on two generators unless they have a
common fixed point (which happens for the contraction maps induced by

f
and
g
exactly when
\Prep(f)=\Prep(g))
. We note that
Beaumont’s theorem actually holds for any two morphisms of a projective
variety
V
polarized by the same line bundle (that is, for any maps
f,g:V\longrightarrow V
such that there is an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
V
with the
property that
f^{*}\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{L}^{d_{1}}
and
g^{*}\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{L}^{d_{2}}
for some

d_{1},d_{2}>1
).

The conclusion of Beaumont’s theorem does not hold as stated if one
of the maps is an automorphism. Consider the semigroup
S

generated by
f(x)=2x
and
g(x)=x^{2}
. We see that
\Prep(g)
is
much larger than
\Prep(f)
, but we have

f\circ f\circ g=g\circ f
, so
S
is not free on two generators.
On the other hand,
S
does contain
2x^{2}
and
x^{2}
, which do generate a
free semigroup on two generators (this follows easily from the
uniqueness of binary expansions of natural numbers).

Additional complications occur for morphisms having periodic points
that are not isolated, as we shall see in Examples
3.4
and

3.8
. To explain this, we need a little terminology.

Definition 1.2
.

For a given integer
n
and an automorphism
g:X\longrightarrow X
of a variety

X
, we define

\Per_{n}(g)=\{x\in X(\overline{K})\;|\;\text{$g^{n}(x)=x$}\}.

Note that
\Per_{n}(g)
is a Zariski closed subset of
X
. We define

\Per(g)
to be the union
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Per_{n}(g)
.

We let
\Per_{n}^{*}(g)
be the points
x
that are in
0-dimensional components of
\Per_{n}(g)
, where
n
is the smallest
positive integer for
g^{n}(x)=x
. We define
\Per^{*}(g)
to be the
union
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Per^{*}_{n}(g)
.

We define
\Per^{**}(g)
to be the set of all
x\in X(\overline{K})
such
that
x\in\Per(g)
and
x
is not in a positive-dimensional
component of
\Per(g^{n})
for any
n
. Note that this may be smaller
than
\Per^{*}(g)
since a point could be in a zero-dimensional
component of
\Per_{n}(g)
but in a positive dimensional component of

\Per_{mn}(g)
for some
m>1
(for example,
g
could itself be torsion).

Many questions related to the problems in this paper, such as the Tits
alternative
[
Tit72
]
and its variants (see
[
Tit72
,
BHPT24
,
KOZ09
,
Zha09
,
Hu20
]
) are stated in terms of finitely generated
groups or finitely generated semigroups, so we now pose a general
question in that language.

Question 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group of automorphisms of a
quasiprojective variety
X
. It is true that one of the following
must hold:

(i)

we have
\Per^{**}(g_{1})\subseteq\Per(g_{2})
for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
or

(ii)

G
contains a subsemigroup that is isomorphic to the free
semigroup on two generators?

We are able to answer Question
1.3
in the case of projective
varieties.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group of automorphisms of a
projective variety
X
. Then at least one of the following must hold:

(i)

we have
\Per^{**}(g_{1})\subseteq\Per(g_{2})
for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
; or

(ii)

G
contains a subsemigroup that is isomorphic to the free
semigroup on two generators.

We prove Theorem
1.4
by combining Tits type results for
automorphism groups of projective varieties
[
KOZ09
,
Zha09
,
Hu20
]
and a result of Rosenblatt’s on solvable groups

[
Ros74
]
with the following theorem proved in Section
3
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group of automorphisms of a variety

X
. If
G
is nilpotent, then for any

g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, we have
\Per^{*}(g_{1})\subseteq\Per(g_{2})
. In
particular, if
\Per_{n}(g_{1})
and
\Per_{n}(g_{2})
are finite for all

n
, then
\Per(g_{1})=\Per(g_{2})
.

Theorem
1.5
is a statement relating commonality of
periodic points among elements of a nilpotent group; it seems natural
to ask for a theorem relating commonality of periodic points among
elements of a solvable group. In the case of linear algebraic
groups, the Borel fixed point theorem
[
Bor56
,
Bor91
]
says that
if
G
is a connected, solvable, linear algebraic group acting
regularly on a nonempty, complete algebraic variety
V
over an
algebraically closed field
K
, then there is an algebraic point in

V(K)
that is fixed by every element of
G
. Using techniques
similar to those of the proof of Theorem
1.5
along with
some
p
-adic techniques from
[
BGT10
,
BGT15
]
(that are available
only in characteristic 0), we prove the following. Recall that a
group is said to be virtually solvable (resp. nilpotent) if it
contains a subgroup of finite index that is solvable
(resp. nilpotent).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually solvable group of automorphisms of a
variety
X
defined over a field of characteristic 0. Suppose that
for all nontorsion
g\in G
, the set
\Per^{*}(g)
is nonempty.
Then there is an
x\in X
such that
x\in\Per(g)
for all
g\in G
. Furthermore there is a subgroup
H
of finite index in
G
such
that
h(x)=x
for all
h\in H
.

An important ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.6
is the
following theorem, which may be thought of as a Burnside-type problem
for group actions. Recall that the Burnside problem for groups asks
if a finitely generated torsion group is finite; there are
counterexamples in general, but with additional requirements (say on
the order of the torsion and the number of generators), it is often
true. Likewise, given a group action
G
on a set
T
, one can ask when
it is true that
Gt
must be finite for any
t\in T
that is periodic
under every point of
G
. When
T
is taken to be
G
itself under
left multiplication and
t=\{e\}
, this is the same as the Burnside
problem for groups. We are able to prove the following Burnside-type
theorem for groups acting on varieties using
p
-adic techniques in
Section
2
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a quasiprojective variety over a finitely generated field

K
of characteristic 0 and let
G
be a group of automorphisms of

X
defined over
K
. Let
W
be an irreducible subvariety of
X
.
If
W
is periodic under
g
for every element
g\in G
, then the
orbit of
W
under
G
is finite.

An outline of the paper is as follows. We begin by proving Theorem

1.7
in Section
2
; the technique is quite
similar to that of
[
BGT15
]
. Then, in Section
3
, we
prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
. The proof of
Theorem
1.5
uses the fact that the center of a
finitely generated infinite nilpotent group is infinite, along with some simple
relationships between periodic points of commuting automorphisms that
hold in any characteristic, while Theorem
1.6
relies on a
refined version of Theorem
1.7
that holds for points
(Proposition
2.2
) in characteristic 0. Finally, in Section

4.2
, we propose a more general version of Question

1.3
and prove two Northcott-type results that might be useful
in working towards its proof.

Throughout this paper, our varieties are not assumed to be
irreducible. When a variety is irreducible we refer to it as an
irreducible
variety.

2.
Burnside orbit theorem

We begin with a lemma adapted from
[
BGT15
]
. As there, the main tool
is the
p
-adic arc lemma. While the emphasis there was on bounding
the periods of periodic points, here we focus instead on bounding
the index of the stabilizer subgroup of a subvariety that is periodic under every
element of some group of automorphisms. It is possible here to make
the bounds effective in the case where
W
is a point.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{o}_{v}
be the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in some finite extension

K_{v}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for some
p
, let
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
be the maximal ideal in

\mathfrak{o}_{v}
, and let
k_{v}
be the residue field
\mathfrak{o}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v}
. Let

\mathcal{X}
be a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-scheme whose generic fiber is a variety

X
over
K_{v}
. Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{X}(k_{v})
and let

\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}=r^{-1}(\gamma)
, where

r:\mathcal{X}(\mathfrak{o}_{v})\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}(k_{v})
is the usual reduction map. Let

G_{\gamma}
be a group of automorphisms
\sigma
of
\mathcal{X}
such
that
\sigma(\mathcal{U}_{\gamma})=\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
. Then
G_{\gamma}
has a
subgroup
H_{\gamma}
of finite index such that
h(W)=W
for any
irreducible subvariety
W
of the generic fiber of
\mathcal{X}
such that
the closure of
W
in
\mathcal{X}
contains a point in
\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the proof of
[
BGT15
, Theorem
3.1]
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
denote the maximal ideal in
\mathfrak{o}_{v}
, let

\pi
denote a generator for
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
, and let

k_{v}
denote the residue field
\mathfrak{o}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v}
.
Since
\mathcal{X}
is a smooth
\mathfrak{o}_{v}
-scheme, for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{X}(k_{v})
, its completed local ring
{\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\alpha}}
is
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{o}_{v}[[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]]
, where
n
is the dimension
of
X
.

Arguing exactly as in the proof of
[
BGT10
, Proposition 2.2]
we then
obtain that there is a
v
-adic analytic
isomorphism
\iota:\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{o}_{v}^{n}
, such that for any
\sigma\in G_{\gamma}
, there are power series
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}\in\mathfrak{o}_{v}[[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]]
with the properties that

(i)

each
F_{i}
converges on
\mathfrak{o}_{v}^{n}
;

(ii)

for all
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})\in\mathfrak{o}_{v}^{n}
, we have

(2.1)

\iota(\sigma(\iota^{-1}(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})))=(F_{1}(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}),\dots,F_{n}(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}));\text{ and }

(iii)

each
F_{i}
is congruent to a linear polynomial mod
v
(in
other words, all the coefficients of terms of degree greater than one
are in the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
of
\mathfrak{o}_{v}
). Moreover, for each
i
, we have

F_{i}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\frac{1}{\pi}\cdot H_{i}(\pi z_{1},\dots,\pi z_{n}),

for some
H_{i}\in\mathfrak{o}_{v}[[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]]
.

Writing
\vec{\beta}:=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})
and
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}:=\iota\sigma\iota^{-1}
, we thus have

(2.2)

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}(\vec{\beta})\equiv C_{\sigma}+L_{\sigma}(\vec{\beta})\pmod{\pi}

for a
C_{\sigma}\in\mathfrak{o}_{v}^{n}
and an
n\times n
matrix
L_{\sigma}
with
coefficients in
k_{v}
. Since
\sigma
is an étale map of

\mathfrak{o}_{v}
-schemes,
\bar{L}_{\sigma}
must be invertible. Let
H_{\gamma}
be
the subgroup of
G
consisting of all
\sigma\in G
such that

L_{\sigma}\equiv I_{n}\pmod{\pi^{e+1}}
where
I_{n}
is the

n\times n
identity matrix with coefficients in
k_{v}
and

is the
ramification index of
K_{v}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then
H_{\gamma}
has finite
index in
G
since

and
\mathfrak{o}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v}^{e+1}
are finite. We will
show that
h(W)=W
for any
h\in H_{\gamma}
. Pick

z\in\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}\cap W
and
\sigma\in H_{\gamma}
and let

\vec{\beta}=\iota(z)
. By
[
Poo14
]
, there are
p
-adic power series

\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\longrightarrow U_{\gamma}
convergent on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

such that
\mathcal{F}^{n}(\vec{\beta})=(\theta_{1}(m),\dots,\theta_{n}(m))
for all

m\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Now, let
G
be any element of the vanishing ideal of
W
. Then

G\circ\iota^{-1}
is a
v
-adic analytic power series
convergent on
\mathfrak{o}_{v}^{g}
, so

\tilde{G}=(G\circ\iota^{-1})(\theta_{1}(z),\dots,\theta_{n}(z))

is a

p
-adic analytic power series convergent on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

If
z\in W
and
W
is periodic, then
\tilde{G}(m)
must vanish on all
m

divisible the period of
W
, so
\tilde{G}
is a power series convergent on

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
that has infinitely many zeros. This means that
\tilde{G}

vanishes uniformly on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, so
G
must vanish on
\sigma^{m}(z)
for
all natural numbers
m
. Since this holds for every
G
in the
vanishing ideal of
W
, we must have
\sigma^{m}(z)\in W
for all
m

so in particular, we have
\sigma(z)\in W
.

Now,

is dense by
[
BGT15
, Proposition 2.3]
, so
it follows that
\sigma(x)\in W
for a Zariski dense subset of
x\in W
,
so
\sigma(W)=W
, as desired.

In the case of points, we obtain a little bit more information.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{o}_{v}
be the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in some finite extension

K_{v}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for some
p
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-scheme
whose generic fiber is a variety
X
over
K_{v}
, let
G
be a group
of automorphisms of
\mathcal{X}
, and let
T
be the set
of all points in
\mathcal{X}(\mathfrak{o}_{v})
that are periodic under every element of

G
. Let
M
be the set of all elements of
G
such that
ht=t
for
all
t\in T
.

Then we have the following:

(i)

M
is normal in
G
;

(ii)

[G:M]
is finite; and

(iii)

for any
t\in T
, the orbit
Gt
is finite.

Proof.

We see that (iii) follows immediately from (ii) since for any

t\in T
, the stabilizer
G_{t}
of
t
contains
M
. Note that we
may assume that
X
is geometrically irreducible in (i) since the
stabilizer of each component of
X
has finite index in
G
.
Likewise, (i) follows easily from (iii) since if
Gt
is finite
for all
t\in T
, then
gt\in T
for any
g\in G
and
t\in T
, so
ghg^{-1}(t)=gg^{-1}t=t
for
any
h\in M
, which means that
ghg^{-1}\in M
for any
g\in G

and
h\in M
. Hence, it only remains to prove (ii).

We use induction on the dimension of
X
and Lemma
2.1
to prove
(ii). If
X

has dimension 0, this is trivial. Otherwise, we may write
\mathcal{X}
as

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{Y}
where
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is smooth over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and the generic
fiber of
\mathcal{Y}
has dimension less than
\dim X
. An automorphism of a
scheme preserves its smooth locus so
\sigma(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})=\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and

\sigma(\mathcal{Y})=\mathcal{Y}
. We let
M_{Y}
be the subgroup of of
G
that
fixes all elements of
T
in
\mathcal{Y}
and
M^{\prime}
be the subgroup of
G

that fixes all elements of
T
in
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
. Then
[G:M_{Y}]
is finite,
by the inductive hypothesis, so it will suffice to show that
[G:M^{\prime}]

is finite
since
[G:M_{Y}\cap M^{\prime}]\leq[G:M^{\prime}]\cdot[G:M_{Y}]
.

Now, since there are finitely many
\gamma\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}(k_{v})
, there are
finitely many residue classes
\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
of
\mathfrak{o}_{v}
-points as in
Lemma
2.1
containing elements of
T
. Since
G
permutes the
residue classes
\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
, there is a subgroup
H
of finite index
in
G
such that
h(\mathcal{U}_{\gamma})=\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
for all
h\in H
and
all residue classes
\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
. By Lemma
2.1
, for each

\gamma\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}(k_{v})
, there is a subgroup
H_{\gamma}
of finite index
in
H
such that
ht=t
for all
t\in\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
that are periodic
under all elements of
H
. Let

M^{\prime\prime}=\cap_{\gamma\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}(k_{v})}H_{\gamma}
. Now if
t\in T
, then

t
is periodic under every element of
H
and furthermore

t\in\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}(K_{v})
; hence,
M^{\prime\prime}
fixes
every element of
T
. Since
M^{\prime\prime}
is an intersection of subgroups of
finite index in
G
, it has finite index in
G
. Clearly,
M^{\prime}

contains
M^{\prime\prime}
so

is also finite, as desired.

We pose the following general question.

Question 2.3
.

Let
S
be a finitely generated group of finite morphisms from a
variety
X
to itself. Suppose that
x\in S
is preperiodic under
every element of
S
. Is it true that the orbit
Sx
must be finite?

We are able to prove that Question
2.3
has a positive answer
for self-maps of projective space.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
S
be a finitely generated semigroup of morphisms

f:\mathbb{P}_{K}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{K}^{n}
for some
n
defined over a field
K
.
Then for any
x\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
x\in\Per(g)
for all

g\in S
, the orbit
Sx
is finite.

Proof.

We first treat the case where
S
is composed entirely of
automorphisms (but is not necessarily a group itself). We will
begin by showing that there is an
M
such that
s^{M}x=x
for all

s\in S
. If
K
has characteristic
p
, then for any

(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix
N
, we have that
N^{p^{n+1}}
is
diagonalizable (this can be seen by putting it in Jordan canonical
form). Since fixed points of elements of
\PGL_{n+1}(K)
correspond
to eigenvectors of
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrices, it follows that
for any
g\in\PGL_{n+1}(K)
and any
y\in\Per(g)
, we have

g^{p^{n+1}}y=y
when
K
has characteristic
p
; thus, we have

s^{p^{n+1}}x=x
for all

3.
Nilpotent and solvable

We will begin by proving Theorem
1.5
. We start with two
lemmas. The first is standard. See
[
Rob96
, 5.2.22]
for a
proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
G
is finitely generated, nilpotent, and infinite then its center
Z(G)

contains an element of infinite order.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
g,h:X\longrightarrow X
are automorphisms such that
gh=hg
, then

g(\Per_{n}(h))\subseteq\Per_{n}(h)
and
\Per_{n}^{*}(h)\subseteq\Per(g)
.

Proof.

If
h^{n}(x)=x
, then
h^{n}(g(x))=g(h^{n}(x))=g(x)
; if

h^{m}(x)\not=x
, then
h^{m}(g(x))=g(h^{m}(x))\not=x
since
g
is
one-to-one. Hence,
g(\Per_{n}(h))\subseteq\Per_{n}(h)
, and

g(\Per^{*}_{n}(h))\subseteq\Per^{*}_{n}(h)
. Thus, every element of

\Per^{*}_{n}(h)
has a finite forward orbit under
g
and is therefore
in
\Per(g)
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.5
. We need one more
definition.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a group of automorphisms of a variety
X
. If
Y
is a
subset of a variety
X
, we define the stabilizer
G_{Y}
of
Y
as

G_{Y}:=\{g\in G\;|\;g(Y)=Y\}.

We have a restriction map
\Res_{Y}:G_{Y}\longrightarrow\Aut(Y)
given by

\Res_{Y}(g)=g|_{Y}\text{ for $g\in G_{Y}$}.

We let
H_{Y}
be the image of this map, that is

H_{Y}:=\Res_{Y}(G_{Y}).

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We will use Noetherian induction, which states that if a proposition
is true for a point and is true for a variety
Y
whenever it is
true for all proper subvarieties of
Y
, then it is true for all
varieties (see
[
Har77
, Ex. II.3.16]
, for example).

If
X
is a point, we are done since
G
must be finite. For the
inductive step, we take a nontorsion
h\in Z(G)
, the center of

g
; some such
h
exists by Lemma
3.1
since
G
must now
be infinite as otherwise
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
would be torsion, and the
theorem would be trivial. By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\Per^{*}(g_{1})\subseteq\Per(h)
. Thus, we will be done if we can
show that for any
n
and any element

x\in\Per_{n}(h)\cap\Per^{*}(g_{1})
, we must have

x\in\Per(g_{2})
. Fix an
n
. Then
\Per_{n}(h)
is a proper closed
subset of
X
, which we denote as
Y
and we have

g_{1}(Y)=g_{2}(Y)=Y
by Lemma
3.2
. Thus, each point
corresponding to a component of dimension 0 in
\Per_{n}(h)
is
periodic for both
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
. Now, let
x\in\Per_{n}(h)
lie
in a component
Y
of
\Per_{n}(h)
of positive dimension. Let

g_{i}^{\prime}=\Res_{Y}(g_{m})
. The stabilizer group
H_{Y}
is finitely
generated and nilpotent since it is the image of a subgroup of a
finitely generated nilpotent group. Now,
Y
is a proper closed
subset of
X
, so we may
apply the inductive hypothesis to conclude that

\Per^{*}(g_{1}^{\prime})\subseteq\Per(g_{2}^{\prime})
. Now, if

x\in Y\cap\Per^{*}(g_{1})
, then
x\in\Per^{*}(g_{1}^{\prime})
so we see
that every element of
x\in Y\cap\Per^{*}(g_{1})
is in

\Per(g_{2})
, as desired.
∎

In the case of group of automorphisms that are virtually nilpotent
(have a nilpotent subgroup of finite index) we must strengthen our
hypothesis, as the following example shows.

Example 3.4
.

Let
G
be the group of affine linear maps of the form
x\mapsto\pm x+m
for

. The subgroup

consisting of all maps

is abelian of index 2 in

, so

is virtually
nilpotent. If
f(x)=-x
, then 0 is in
\Per_{1}^{*}(f)
, but 0 is not
periodic under any elements of

other than the identity. Thus the
condition that
\alpha\in\Per^{**}(g)
for some

and not just

\alpha\in\Per^{*}(g)
is necessary in Theorem
1.5
.

We are able to prove the following, however.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group of automorphisms of a variety

X
. If
G
is virtually nilpotent, then for any

g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, we have
\Per^{*}(g_{1})\subseteq\Per(g_{2})
. In
particular, if
\Per_{n}(g_{1})
and
\Per_{n}(g_{2})
are finite for all

, then
\Per(g_{1})=\Per(g_{2})
.

Proof.

We observe that while
\Per^{*}(g^{n})
may
be smaller than
\Per^{*}(g)
(as in Example

), we always
have both
\Per^{**}(g^{n})=\Per^{**}(g)
and
\Per(g^{n})=\Per(g)
.
Now, let

be a nilpotent subgroup finite index in

and let

g_{1},g_{2}\in G
; then there is some

such that

g_{1}^{n},g_{2}^{n}\in A
. Since

\Per^{*}(g_{1}^{n})\subseteq\Per(g_{2}^{n})
by Theorem

,
we then have

\Per^{**}(g_{1})=\Per^{**}(g_{1}^{n})\subseteq\Per^{*}(g_{1}^{n})\subseteq\Per(g_{2}^{n})=\Per(g_{2}).

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By
[
Zha09
, Theorem 1.1]
in characteristic 0 and

[

, Theorem 1.1]
in characteristic

(see also

[

]
), the group

is either virtually solvable or it
contains a free group on two generators. Thus, we may assume that

is virtually solvable. By

, a virtually solvable group that is not virtually

\Per^{**}(g_{1})
is not contained in
\Per(g_{2})
for some

must contain a free semigroup on two generators.
∎

We will now begin our proof of Theorem
1.6
. We begin with a lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A
be a group of automorphisms of a quasiprojective variety

X
. Let
A^{\prime}
denote
[A,A]
, let
x\in X
, and let
g,h\in A

Suppose that
A^{\prime}x
is finite and that
x\in\Per(h)
. Then
gx\in\Per(h)
.

Proof.

Choose
\ell
such that
h^{\ell}(x)=x
. Since
g^{-1}h^{\ell m}gh^{-\ell m}\in A^{\prime}
for all

and

is finite, we see that
there are some

such that

Since

is fixed by

and we can cancel the

from

h^{\ell m_{1}}(g(x))=h^{\ell m_{2}}(g(x))
, so
h^{\ell(m_{1}-m_{2})}(g(x))=g(x)
, so

.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
A
be a subgroup of a finitely generated group of automorphisms
of a quasi-projective variety

. Let
A^{\prime}=[A,A]
and let
h\in A
. Suppose that

is periodic under every element of

and that

for some

for

Proof.

There is a finite extension

of some

with ring of

\mathfrak{o}_{v}
and a model
\mathcal{X}
for

over
\mathfrak{o}_{v}
such that

extends to an element of
\mathcal{X}(\mathfrak{o}_{v})
and every element of

extends to an automorphism of

. Let

be the set of

\mathcal{X}(\mathfrak{o}_{v})
that are periodic under every element of

, and let

be the subgroup of
A^{\prime}
that fixes every element
of

. Then

is normal of finite index in

by Proposition

2.2
, so

is finite. Now, let

. We have

by Lemma

, so we have

gNg^{-1}(t)=t
for any

. Thus, for any coset

of

, so

permutes the cosets of

in

. Thus, there is an

such that

for every coset

of

in

.

Let

. We have
g^{m}h^{n}N=h^{n}Ng^{m}
, so

, so
g^{m}(z)\in\ Per_{n}(h)\cap T

as well. Let
Z_{1}=\Per_{n}(h)
, which is Zariski closed, let

be the Zariski closure of

, and let

. We have

, so in particular

sends zero-dimensional

then

is thus periodic under

, which means that it is also periodic under

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

It is enough to treat the case where

is solvable, since if a
finite index subgroup

of

contains a finite index subgroup

such that

for some

for all

, then

has
finite index in

as well. Thus, we will assume from now on that

is solvable and not merely virtually solvable.

We proceed by induction on the length of the derived series for

G
. We will prove something slightly more precise than the
statement of Theorem
1.6
. We will show that if
G
is a
subgroup of a finitely generated group and every element of

\Per^{*}(g)
is nonempty for all

, then there is an

such that
x\in\Per(g)
for all
g\in G
and
x\in\Per^{*}(h)

for some

and

.

If

is abelian, then for any

, we have

, so if

for

, then

for all

, since any

. Note that while commutator groups may not be finitely

The following example shows that we need to have

and not

in order to conclude there

.

Example 3.8
.

Consider the abelian group of

of maps of the form

where

and

are
integers. Then for integers

this map fixes the line consisting
of points of the form

where

is a solution to

. Notice this

in which case we get the identity map. Notice when

and when

so there are no common periodic points.
Thus, if

is any finitely generated subgroup of

containing

and

, then there is no

that is in

for all

. This shows that the condition that

and not just
\Per(g)
be nonempty for all

is
necessary in Theorem

.

4.
More general endomorphisms

Let
f:X\longrightarrow X
be any finite endomorphism. We let
\Prep_{m,n}(f)

be set of points
f^{m}(x)=f^{m+n}(x)
. Again, this is a closed
subset of
X
. We let
\Prep^{*}_{m,n}(f)
be the points that are the
zero dimensional components of
\Prep_{m,n}(f)
and we let

\Prep^{*}(f)
be the union of all the
\Prep^{*}_{m,n}(f)
.

We also let
\Prep^{**}(f)
denote the elements of
\Prep(f)
that are
not in a positive-dimensional component of
\Prep_{m,n}(f)
for any

f
, in analogy with the definition of
\Per^{**}(g)
for an
automorphism
g
.

We pose the following question.

Question 4.1
.

Let
S
be a finitely generated semigroup of finite morphisms from a
quasiprojective variety to itself. Is it true that at least one of
the following must hold:

(i)

we have
\Per^{**}(f_{1})\subseteq\Per(f_{2})
for any
f_{1},g_{2}\in G
or

(ii)

G
contains a subsemigroup isomorphic to the free semigroup on
two generators?

While we cannot obtain results along the lines of Theorems

1.4
,
1.5
,
1.6
, and
1.7

for general finite morphisms, we are able to obtain two Northcott-type
results. The first can be thought of as a Northcott theorem for
integral points.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f:X\longrightarrow X
be a finite morphism, where
X
is a projective variety
defined over a finitely generated field
K
of characteristic 0.
Let
D
be any positive integer. Then there are at most finitely
many
\alpha\in\Prep^{*}(f)
such that
[K(\alpha):K]\leq D
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
f,g:X\longrightarrow X
be finite morphisms of a quasi-projective
variety over a field of characteristic 0. If

g(\Prep^{*}(f))\subseteq\Prep^{*}(f)
, then

\Prep^{*}(f)\subseteq\Prep(g)
.

We will prove Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
after introducing
a few propositions and lemmas.

Fakhruddin
[
Fak01
, Proposition 1]
(see also

[
Hua21
, Theorem 1.2]
) proved the following.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
X
be a variety defined over a finite extension
K_{v}
of

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
\mathcal{X}
be a model for
X
over the ring of integers

\mathfrak{o}_{v}
of
K_{v}
. Let
f:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a morphism. Then there
is a bound on the periods of elements of
\mathcal{X}(\mathfrak{o}_{v})
.

The below comes from an argument of Scanlon (see
[
BGH+13
, Section

).

Let

f_{s}
. Let
M
be the number of points in
\mathcal{X}(\mathfrak{o}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}^{s})

(which is finite since
\mathfrak{o}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}^{s}
is finite). Then there is
no
\beta
such that
f^{n}(\beta)=\gamma
for
n>M
. This
completes our proof.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f:X\longrightarrow X
be finite and surjective. If

\alpha\in\Prep^{*}_{m,n}(f)
, then

f^{m}(\alpha)\in\Prep^{*}_{0,n}(f)
.

Proof.

For any component
Y
of
X
, its image
f(Y)
has the same
dimension as
Y
because
f
is finite, by the Going-Up Theorem
(see
[
AM16
, Theorem 5.16]
, for example). Recall that finite
maps are closed and affine (see
[
Har77
, Chapter II, Ex. 3.4 and
3.5]
). Since
X
has finitely many components, it follows that
for any component
Y
of
X
, its image
f(Y)
is a component of

X
. Then for any open affine
U=\Spec A
in
X
, there is a ring

B
such that
f^{-1}(U)
is isomorphic to
\Spec B

and the map
f^{*}
from
A
to
B
is an inclusion with

\dim A=\dim B
(again, by the Going-Up Theorem). Hence, for any
irreducible closed subvariety of
V
of
X
, and any component
W

of
f^{-1}(V)
, we have
\dim V=\dim W
. In particular, every
0-dimensional component of
\Prep^{*}_{m,n}(f)
is the inverse image
of a 0-dimensional component of
\Prep^{*}_{0,n}(f)
.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

We embed
K
into
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as in Theorem
1.7
. Note
that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
has finitely many extensions of degree
D
or less,
so we let
K_{v}
be their compositum. By Lemma
4.6
, every
point in
\Prep^{*}_{m,n}(f)
is in
f^{-m}(\alpha)
for some

\alpha\in\Prep^{*}_{0,n}(f)
. By Proposition
4.4
, the
set

\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Prep^{*}_{0,n}(f)
is finite. And by
Proposition
4.5
, for any
\alpha\in\Prep^{*}_{0,n}(f)
,
the set of
\beta
such that there is an
m
for which

f^{m}(\beta)=\alpha
is finite. Thus, there are at most finitely
many
\alpha\in\Prep^{*}(f)
such that
[K(\alpha):K]\leq D
,
as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\Prep^{*}(f)
. Again embed into
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in a way
that
\alpha
extends to a point
\tilde{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and so that
f

and
g
extend to maps on
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. To prove that

\alpha\in\Prep(g)
, it will suffice to show that there are only
finitely many points in
X(K)\cap\Prep^{*}(f)
that extend to
points in
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, since
\alpha\in X(K)\cap\Prep^{*}(f)

and
g
maps
X(K)\cap\Prep^{*}(f)
to itself. For any

\beta\in X(K)
that extends to a point in
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
we let

\tilde{\beta}
denote this extension (note that not all points in
X(K)

necessarily extend to points in
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
since
X
may not be
projective). By Lemma
4.6
, for any

\gamma\in\Prep_{m,n}^{*}(f)
we have
f^{m}(\gamma)=\beta
for
some
\beta\in\Prep_{0,n}^{*}(f)
. By Proposition
4.4
,
the set of periods of points in
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is bounded. For any
periodic
\tilde{\beta}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, there are only finitely many

\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
such that
f^{m}(\tilde{\gamma})=\tilde{\beta}
for some
m
, by
Proposition
4.5
. Thus, we see that there are at most
finitely many pairs
(m,n)
such that there are points in

X(K)\cap\Prep_{m,n}^{*}(f)
that extend to points in

\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Since each
\Prep_{m,n}^{*}(f)
is finite, this
finishes our proof.
∎

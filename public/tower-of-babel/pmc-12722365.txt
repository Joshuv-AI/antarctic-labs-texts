Fast micromirror selection and angle setting algorithm for freeform pupil in the illumination system for immersion lithography machines
Authors: Zhenxin Huang, Zenghui Yang, Aijun Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
As lithography resolution improves, the performance requirements for illumination pupils in immersion lithography machines have become increasingly stringent, particularly regarding energy balance and polarization properties. These characteristics are primarily achieved by adjusting the angular distribution of the micromirror array (MMA). For technology nodes below 40 nm, the impact of light polarization on imaging must be considered. This paper proposes a micro-mirror selection and angle setting algorithm to maintain key characteristics of the freeform pupil, ensuring energy balance in both unpolarized and polarized states. Energy balance in the unpolarized state is influenced by the eccentricity of light, while energy balance in the polarized state depends on both the eccentricity and polarization properties of light. To validate the accuracy of the algorithm, simulations were conducted using freeform pupil illumination optical models for both unpolarized and polarized states. The results demonstrated a significant improvement in energy balance, with a reduction to 0.08% in both states. For engineering applications, the computational speed of the algorithm was enhanced, reducing the calculation time from 600 to 0.1 s.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-26968-0.

Introduction

Lithography techniques are categorized into electron beam lithography, ion beam lithography, X-ray lithography, nanoimprint lithography, laser direct writing technology, holographic lithography1, and optical lithography. These can be utilized in chip manufacturing and processing of metasurface components2. However, apart from optical lithography technology, the exposure efficiencies of these methods are relatively low, rendering them unsuitable for large-scale industrial production. Optical lithography has been widely applied in large-scale integrated circuit manufacturing production lines owing to its highly efficient exposure performance. Typical optical lithography techniques include contact lithography, proximity lithography, step-and-repeat projection lithography, step-and-scan projection lithography, and extreme ultraviolet lithography3.

As the feature sizes of integrated circuits scale down to 28 nm and below, immersion lithography machines have emerged as the mainstream equipment for fabricating ultra-large-scale integrated circuits. With improvements in lithography resolution, the‌immersion lithography machine has higher demands for the performance index of the illumination pupil, such as energy balance and polarization performance. The methods of pupil generation using an ‌Immersion Lithography Machine are mainly divided into optical paths based on optical diffraction elements (DOE) and optical paths based on micromirror arrays (MMAs). In contrast to optical-diffraction-based elements, any pupil illumination mode (freeform pupil illumination) can be achieved without replacing the optical shaping unit element of a lithography machine illumination system.

Lighting can be categorized into illumination with a polarized state and illumination without a polarized state, where non-polarized illumination can be converted into polarized illumination4. Below the 40-nm technology node, the impact of light polarization characteristics on imaging must be considered. Liu Yong conducted a polarization imaging analysis under a large numerical aperture5. In their doctoral thesis, Zhou Ruyi discussed the polarization design and simulation of an immersed lithography lighting system in detail6. In addition, Du Meng described the application of micromirror arrays in beam shaping7. Moreover, Zeng Zongshun proposed an algorithm to calculate the distribution of spot positions according to pupil distribution8.In addition, a preliminary analysis of the position relationship between the subpupil and micromirror in the MMA in the unpolarized state was presented; however, but did not solve the problem of uneven polarization performance caused by the tilt of light. Yu elaborated on an evaluation method for pupil characteristic parameters in his paper9. Huang Zhenxin proposed a partition algorithm; however, its calculation speed was insufficient for practical product applications. Moreover, it did not explain the spot position and micromirror angle conversion relationship10. Micromirror arrays achieve off-axis illumination primarily by controlling each micromirror in the array.Deflection in the two-dimensional direction modulates the spatial angle of the light incident on the micromirror. This technology was first proposed and applied by ASML11.ASML is strictly confidential about how it ensures pupil energy balance.Jörg from Zeiss studied methods for evaluating the difference between free-pupil illumination and the target pupil12 (both through parameter fitting and by calculating the pupil CD difference), but did not investigate the pupil’s polarization performance. It’s worth noting that ASML’s illumination system is provided by Zeiss.This paper builds on the pupil characteristic parameter method, elucidates the relationship between spot position and micromirror angle conversion, and proposes a fast partitioning algorithm—analogous to reinforcement learning13—that ensures energy balance in both unpolarized and polarized states.

Freeform pupil illumination

Freeform Pupil illumination technology realizes arbitrary illumination patterns of the beam by adjusting the angular spectrum of the MMA. The pupil intensity distribution is formed by the superposition of the subspots formed by thousands of micromirrors on the pupil plane. In the ‌Immersion Lithography Machine, the freeform pupil illumination module is mainly composed of polarization purification components, polarization control components, energy equalization components, beam segmentation components, the MMA unit, and the Fourier transform unit. In the Fig. 7, the polarization-purified component realizes the onset bias of the incident light, and the polarization-control component is used to control the generation of different polarization states, thereby realizing the conversion of the X- and Y-polarization modes. The energy equalization component (Homogenization unit) is composed of an imaging microlens array and light condenser lens, which homogenize the incident light to weaken or eliminate the influence of uneven beam intensity distribution or fluctuation on the intensity distribution of the pupil. The beam-segmentation component (BDE) consists of a microlens array on which the incident beam is segmented and clustered to reduce the requirement for the micromirror reflection surface duty cycle in the MMA. The MMA is located at the rear focal surface of the beam segmentation assembly and coincides with the incoming pupil plane of the Fourier-transform lens assembly. The Fourier transform lens component can perform a Fourier transform, combined with MMA, to form the desired freeform pupil intensity distribution on the rear focal plane8.

Fast partitioning algorithm

Constraints

The physical quantity IPS (intensity in preferred state) is often used to describe the percentage of polarized light in the total illumination intensity. The higher the IPS, the better the image contrast. Generally, IPS greater than 95% is required. Therefore, the IPS at each position can be used to represent the polarized light intensity.

According to the overall structure of the free pupil illumination module, the reflected light within the same radius from the center point of the MMA has the same beam inclination angle incident on the polarization conversion component, so the polarization performance change caused by the beam inclination is also the same. Based on this, in order to ensure the polarization pupil’s polarization balance, the following three constraints are proposed for the MMA angular position algorithm.

(1) Points are taken along the circumferential direction of the same radius in the MMA and placed at the symmetrical positions of the four poles to ensure the balance of the four poles. Figure 15 shows an example of 45° quadrupole illumination. Points 1, 2, 3, and 4 on the MMA correspond to the four symmetrical points on the pupil.

Fig. 15Take adjacent points on a circle with the same radius and distribute them symmetrically on the pupil plane.

Take adjacent points on a circle with the same radius and distribute them symmetrically on the pupil plane.

(2) Based on requirement (1), other points on the same radius or the closest radius are placed along the tangential direction of each pole. This can ensure the intra-pole balance of each pole as much as possible, mainly referring to the XY polar balance. Figure 16 uses 45° quadrupole illumination as an example. The five points on the MMA correspond to a tangential position on each pole.

Fig. 16Place points on the same circle tangentially in the extreme inner direction.

Place points on the same circle tangentially in the extreme inner direction.

(3) As shown in Fig. 17, as the incident angle increases, the IPS changes in the form of a parabola. The smaller the incident angle, the less obvious the polarization performance change caused by the incident angle difference. In order to reduce the difference in polar balance caused by the incident angle difference, on the basis of meeting requirements (1) and (2), points are taken as much as possible along the center of the MMA outward to ensure that the incident angle of each point in the pole is as small as possible.

Conclusion

This study developed a micro-mirror selection and angle-setting algorithm that maintained energy balance in both unpolarized and polarized states. The key achievements are as follows:

In the unpolarized state, the energy balance remained below 0.08%, well within the 1% requirement of the exposure system.In the polarized state, the energy balance remained below 0.05%, well within the 1% requirement of the exposure system.The proposed algorithm ensured pupil energy balance in both states, an aspect critical for enhancing the contrast of lithographic images.The partitioning time was reduced from 10 min to 0.1 s, greatly improving the algorithm’s scope for engineering applications.

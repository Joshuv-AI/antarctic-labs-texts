Nonuniform settlement of C2 lateral mass: a pathological anatomic change related to the lateral atlantoaxial osteoarthritis
Authors: Chao Tang, Ying Zhang, Xian Ming Huang, Xu Zhao, Tong Wei Chu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveNonuniform settlement of C2 lateral mass (C2LM-NUS) was defined as an asymmetrical change of the bilateral C2 lateral masses. This study aimed to determine the association between the nonuniform settlement of C2 lateral mass (C2LM-NUS) and lateral atlantoaxial osteoarthritis (AAOA).MethodsA total of 573 patients who underwent a head and neck or cervical spine CT scan were reviewed. The morphological parameters were measured, including C2 lateral mass settlement (C2LMS), lateral atlantodental interval (LADI), coronal deviation angle of odontoid process (Od-CDA), and C1/2 relative rotation angle (C1/2 RRA). The lateral AAOA was recorded. The median values of the difference in LADI between the two sides (d-LADI), Od-CDA, and C1/2 RRA in the C2LM-NUS group were greater than those in the control group (p < .001 for all). The prevalence of lateral AAOA in patients with C2LM-NUS was higher than in the control group (33.8% vs 19.1%, p < .001). Introduction

Lateral atlantoaxial osteoarthritis (AAOA) is an unfamiliar, but increasingly recognized occipito-cervical pain syndrome. It manifests as upper neck, occipital, or postauricular pain and restricted motion [1–4]. The prevalence of radiographic lateral AAOA increases from 5 and 10% in the sixth decade to approximately 18%–25% among octogenarians [5–7]. Interestingly, the radiology of lateral AAOA is unilateral or more severe degeneration on one side in most cases [4, 7–10]. Similarly, the pain symptom is typically unilateral or markedly worse on the affected side [1, 9, 11, 12]. However, the anatomical mechanism by which lateral AAOA mainly presents with unilateral imaging manifestation and clinical symptoms remains unclear.

The atlantoaxial joint (AAJ), characterized by its unique structural features, plays a crucial role in maintaining the biomechanical balance and stability of the craniocervical junction [13]. Ordinarily, the C2 lateral mass demonstrates bilateral symmetry, with an approximately 20° outward inclination in the coronal view and parallel to the horizontal plane in the sagittal view [14, 15]. This symmetrical anatomical relationship ensures the flexibility of the AAJ and supports a balanced transfer of the gravitational load from the head to the subaxial cervical spine. Recently, imaging studies of the AAJ demonstrated that asymmetry of C2 lateral mass was associated with unilateral high-riding vertebral artery [16, 17]. They defined this asymmetrical phenomenon as the non-uniform settlement of C2 lateral mass (C2LM-NUS). Further, a finite element analysis of the upper cervical model with C2LM-NUS showed a biomechanical stress concentration on one side of the lateral mass [16]. Van Roy et al. found that left–right asymmetries of the C1 vertebra caused by unilateral differences in shape, size, surface area, and curvature led to stress concentration on one side of the lateral mass, which was related to degenerative enlargement and osteophytes of the AAJ. Therefore, a question arises as to whether the C2LM-NUS is associated with the progression of lateral AAOA. Other open questions include whether the C2LM-NUS influences the morphological features of the AAJ.

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the association between the morphological changes in the AAJ and C2LM-NUS using multiplanar reconstruction CT (MPR-CT) and to determine whether the C2LM-NUS affects the progression of AAOA as an independent pathological anatomic factor.

Materials and methods

Subjects

This study was designed as a retrospective observational study, involving patients who underwent head and neck CT or cervical spine CT in a neutral supine position at our institution between January 2022 and March 2023. The subjects were identified through a retrospective search of patient databases (Supplementary Fig. 1). Patients were excluded if they had a history of trauma, malignancy, or surgery at the AAJ, or if they had rheumatoid arthritis or infection of the AAJ. Additionally, patients under 18 years of age were also excluded. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of our institution. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent was waived, as the CT scans were not performed solely for research purposes but were retrospectively selected from the hospitals' digital archives.

Morphological parameters of AAJ and definition of C2LM-NUS

The CT data of the patients were imported into the RadiAnt DICOM Viewer software (64-bit), and the imaging of MPR-CT was used to measure the morphological parameters of the AAJ. According to previous literature reports [16, 17], C2 lateral mass settlement (C2LMS) was utilized to assess the degree of C2 lateral mass collapse and subsidence. C2LMS was defined as the shortest distance from the midpoint of the superior articular surface of the axis to the tangent line of its lower endplate in the mid-coronal plane [16] (Fig. 1). The difference between the two sides of C2LMS (d-C2LMS) indicated the degree of non-uniform settlement of the C2 lateral mass. Tang et al. [16] demonstrated that patients with unilateral high-riding vertebral artery were associated with the C2LM-NUS. They found the median value of d-C2LMS in those patients with C2LM-NUS was 1.4 mm. Therefore, in this study, the subjects with a d-C2LMS greater than 1.4 mm were defined as having C2LM-NUS.

Illustration of parameters measurement for the atlantoaxial joint. The difference in C2 LMS (d-C2 LMS) between the two sides indicated the degree of non-uniform settlement of the C2 lateral mass. The coronal deviation angle of the odontoid process (Od-CDA) was defined as the angle between the midline of the odontoid process and the perpendicular line to the tangent of the lower endplate of the axis (A). The C1 − 2 relative rotation angle (C1/2 RRA) was the algebraic difference between the C1 and C2 rotation angles (B). The lateral atlantodental interval (LADI) refers to the distance from the inner side of the lateral mass of the atlas to the odontoid process. The difference between the two sides of the LADI (d-LADI) indicated the degree of lateral slip of the atlas. The coronal deviation angle of the odontoid process (Od-CDA) was measured on the coronal plane of the axis. Od-CDA was defined as the angle between the midline of the odontoid process and the perpendicular line of the tangent line to the lower endplate of the axis. The C1/2 relative rotation angle (C1/2 RRA) was the algebraic difference between the C1 rotation angle and the C2 rotation angle (Fig. 1).

Evaluation of AAOA

We evaluated the degenerative alterations of the AAJ on the sagittal and coronal views of the upper cervical spine. Degeneration of the AAJ was determined using a scoring system described by Lakshmanan et al. [18]. The severity of lateral atlantoaxial joint degeneration was graded as normal to mild, moderate, and severe. Based on the method given by Betsch et al. [5], the grades of “none‒mild” were classified as “absence of OA,”. In contrast, grades of “moderate” and “severe” were classified as “presence of OA” (Fig. 2). We documented the degeneration on either side of the lateral mass facet separately. We assigned the diagnosis of lateral AAOA if either side demonstrated “moderate” or “severe” degeneration.

The grade of lateral atlantoaxial osteoarthritis (AAOA). Normal to mild: normal or narrow joint space with or without minor osteophyte formation. Moderate: obliterated joint space with or without osteophyte formation. Severe: completely obliterated joint space with osteophyte excrescences and/or fusion of the joint

Reliability of CT evaluation and measurement

In the initial stage of the study, two readers trained and experienced in evaluating CT scans for the measurement of upper cervical morphological parameters reviewed a batch of CT scans to develop a reading protocol for assessing lateral AAOA and morphological parameters. Using this protocol, they independently read and re-read 50 CT scans measured 2 weeks apart. Unaware of the patient identities, the readers assessed the intrarater reliability of the evaluations of the lateral AAOA and parameters. The intraobserver reliability test for lateral AAOA yielded kappa statistics of 0.92. For the morphological parameters, interclass correlation (ICC) was 0.81 for the right and 0.86 for the left, indicating good reliability.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean with standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are represented as absolute and relative frequencies. Paired-sample t-tests were used to identify differences in morphological parameters based on the lateral mass side within each group. Independent-sample t-test was used to identify differences in morphological parameters and continuous variables between groups. Mann–Whitney U test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test was performed to determine differences in continuous variables that did not conform to the normal distribution. A chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test was used for the comparison of dichotomous categorical variables between groups. Furthermore, multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to determine the associated risk factors for lateral AAOA. All statistics were performed in IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 25.0 (IBM Co., Armonk, NY, USA). A 2-tailed P < 0.05 was used as a criterion for significance.

Results

A total of 573 consecutive patients who underwent CT scans of the head and neck or cervical spine were reviewed and analyzed. Among them, 154 patients (26.9%) diagnosed with C2LM-NUS were classified as the C2LM-NUS group (79 males and 75 females). The control group consisted of 419 patients with a symmetrical C2LMS between the left and right side (235 males and 184 females). The demographic characteristics of patients are summarized in Table 1. The patients in the C2LM-NUS group were, on average, older (63.4 years (SD 11.6)) than those in the control group (60.2 years (SD 11.9), p = 0.003). In the C2LM-NUS group, non-uniform settlement occurred on the right side in 110 patients (71.4%) and on the left side in 44 patients (28.6%). The side of the lateral mass where non-uniform settlement occurred was defined as the settlement side, while the other side was the normal side (Supplementary Fig. 2).

The association between C2LM-NUS and morphological parameters of AAJ

In the C2LM-NUS group, the C2LMS on the settlement side was smaller than on the normal side (settlement: 14.0 mm (SD 1.2); normal: 15.9 mm (SD 1.3), p < 0.001). The mean LADI on the settlement side was 3.1 mm (SD 1.0), significantly larger than that on the normal side (2.5 mm (SD 0.9), p < 0.001). The control group did not show any significant differences between the left and right sides for C2LMS (left: 15.2 mm (SD 1.4); right: 15.4 mm (SD 1.6), p > 0.05) and LADI (left: 2.6 mm (SD 0.6); right: 2.6 mm (SD 0.7), p > 0.05).

Prevalence and distribution features of the lateral AAOA between C2LM-NUS and control groups

The total prevalence of lateral AAOA in this study was 23.0% (95%CI: 19.5–27.1%). The prevalence of lateral AAOA in patients with C2LM-NUS was 33.8% (52/154), which was significantly higher than that in the control group (19.1%, 80/419) (p < 0.001). In the C2LM-NUS group, 37 cases (71.2%) of 52 lateral AAOA were unilateral or significantly worse on one side. Among them, 30 unilateral AAOA (81.1%, 30/37) occurred at the settlement side of the C2 lateral mass (Fig. 3). In the control group, the distribution of lateral AAOA was as follows: left side, 21.2%; right side, 16.3%; bilateral sides, 62.5%.

Associated risk factors for lateral AAOA

Results of the univariate analysis showed that the age of patients with lateral AAOA (68.1 years (SD 9.8)) was greater than that of patients without (59.0 years (SD 10.7), p < 0.001). The gender and the C2LM-NUS were associated with the lateral AAOA. The proportion of females and C2LM-NUS in patients with lateral AAOA were 56.1% and 59.8%, which were greater than those in patients without (41.9% and 18.1%) (p < 0.05). In addition, there were significant differences in d-LADI and C1/2 RRA between the patients with lateral AAOA and those without (Table 3).

Discussion

The symmetrical anatomical structure in the AAJ plays a crucial role in ensuring the balanced transfer of gravity load from the head to the sub-axial cervical spine and maintaining atlantoaxial stability. Asymmetric changes in the AAJ result in a biomechanical stress concentration on one side of the lateral mass, which accelerates joint degeneration and dysfunction [16, 19]. The C2LM-NUS has been previously described in studies as an asymmetric change in the bilateral superior articular surface of the axis [16, 17]. In the current study, the prevalence of C2LM-NUS was 22.8%. The main findings indicated that the C2LM-NUS, as an independent anatomical risk factor for lateral AAOA, was associated with long-term asymmetric morphological changes of lateral slip of the atlas and rotational displacement of the AAJ.

Generally, the C2 lateral mass exhibits bilateral symmetry [14, 15]; however, in patients with C2LM-NUS, the bilateral lateral masses exhibit asymmetry. Previous studies found that patients with C2LM-NUS had a larger inclination angle on the settlement side [16, 17]. From a mechanical perspective, this means that the morphological alterations of the lateral mass, characterized by collapse and inclination, in patients with C2LM-NUS are likely to result in lateral slippage of the atlas toward the settlement side. In this study, the LADI on the settlement side was significantly greater than that on the normal side. Many researchers have observed the non-uniform settlement of the tibial plateau in patients with medial compartment knee osteoarthritis [20–24]. They discovered a unique phenomenon in which the femoral condyle demonstrates a tendency to slide toward the medial side, which was named coronal tibiofemoral subluxation [21, 24]. Thus, it appears to be the non-uniform settlement that causes the loss of support function for the atlas, resulting in lateral sliding movement.

The AAJ is responsible for approximately 60% of the axial neck rotation and plays a vital role in maintaining cervical stability and facilitating mobilization [14, 25]. In the present study, the median value of C1/2 RRA in the control group indicated that there was no obvious rotational displacement in the AAJ for patients without C2LM-NUS in the neutral position. However, patients with C2LM-NUS had an abnormal atlantoaxial rotation. This suggests that the subjects with C2LM-NUS may have a long-term slip of atlas and atlantoaxial rotation. An important morphological finding in this study to support this view is the large prevalence of odontoid process deviation in the C2LM-NUS group. The research of Harty et al. pointed out that the deviation of the odontoid process can suggest atlantoaxial rotation and subluxation [26]. The paired alar ligaments extend from opposite sides of the odontoid process and then obliquely run laterally and superiorly to form the occipito-alar portion and atlanto-alar portion. The occipito-alar portion connects the odontoid process laterally to the occipital condyles, and the atlanto-alar portion connects the odontoid to the medial sides of the lateral masses of the atlas bilaterally [25, 27, 28]. These ligaments play a crucial role in limiting atlantoaxial rotation and lateral bending to the contralateral side [29, 30]. It is precisely because of this function that once the atlas undergoes lateral slip and rotation, tension occurs in the ipsilateral alar ligament, which stretches to limit the abnormal movement of the atlas. When this stretching state of the ligament persists on one side for a long time, it will lead to stress remodeling of the odontoid process with a directional deviation (Fig. 4).

In patients with C2LM-NUS, the asymmetric morphological changes of AAJ result in a stress concentration on one side of the lateral mass, which accelerates joint degeneration. Lateral AAOA or C1–2 lateral mass arthritis (LMA) is an unfamiliar, but increasingly recognized occipitocervical pain syndrome [1, 2, 31]. Previous literature reported that lateral AAOA is unilateral or more severe degeneration on one side in most cases [4, 7–10]. Similarly, the pain symptom is typically unilateral or markedly worse on the affected side [1, 9, 11, 12]. The total prevalence of lateral AAOA in this study was 22.8%. In patients with C2LM-NUS, the prevalence of lateral AAOA was greater than that in patients without. In addition, 67.3% of lateral AAOA were unilateral or significantly worse on one side in the C2LM-NUS group. Most cases (80.0%) occurred at the settlement side of the C2 lateral mass. However, 65.7% of lateral AAOA were bilateral in the control group. Multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that the C2LM-NUS was an independent risk factor for lateral AAOA. Therefore, we suggested that C2LM-NUS was related to the progression of lateral AAOA as an independent anatomical risk factor. First, in this study, the C2LM-NUS was associated with asymmetric morphological changes of the atlas lateral slip and the AAJ rotational displacement. Second, C2LM-NUS associated with these asymmetric morphological changes in AAJ cause a shift in the center of gravity force line of the head toward the settlement side of the C2 lateral mass, resulting in biomechanical concentration from the head loading. A finite element study clarified that asymmetrical morphological changes in AAJ can result in stress concentration and repetitive overloading on one side of the C2 lateral mass [16]. Finally, previous research indicated that stress concentration and recurring overloading on joint surfaces can result in hyaline cartilage damage and subchondral bone collapse, triggering a pathological process of consecutive joint degeneration [32–34]. Thus, the C2LM-NUS associated with asymmetric changes of the AAJ could be a direct pathological anatomical basis, leading to a long-term and persistent stress concentration on one side of the C2 lateral mass, and accelerating the degeneration of the AAJ on the settlement side.

This study had several limitations. First, the diagnostic threshold (1.4 mm) of the C2LM-NUS in this study was based on data from previous literature studies. These data were derived from 132 cases of unilateral high-riding vertebral artery with a non-uniform settlement phenomenon. Therefore, we need to further explore the appropriate threshold as a diagnostic criterion for the C2LM-NUS through a multicenter, large-sample study. Second, the average age of our study population was relatively high (mean age: 61.1 years). Although we performed multivariate logistic regression adjusting for age, and age itself was identified as an independent risk factor for lateral AAOA, the predominance of older individuals in our cohort may limit the generalizability of our findings to younger populations. Third, although we determined that the C2LM-NUS was associated with the lateral AAOA, it is unclear whether the degeneration of the unilateral facet is the cause or the result of C2LM-NUS, and our explanations remain speculative. Some studies have suggested that OA or rheumatoid arthritis (RA) of the AAJ leads to bone deformation and thinning of the C2 lateral mass [35, 36]. Therefore, additional biomechanical or cohort studies with clinical case imaging are needed to validate our findings.

Conclusion

The findings of this study indicated that the C2LM-NUS is not a rare phenomenon, with a prevalence of approximately 26%. The C2LM-NUS was associated with asymmetric morphological changes in the AAJ, including lateral slip of the atlas and atlantoaxial rotational displacement. Furthermore, the C2LM-NUS was an independent anatomical risk factor for lateral AAOA because of unbalanced stress distribution on the C2 facet surface during long-term stress and cervical spine activities.

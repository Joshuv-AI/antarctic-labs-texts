Experiences of wound, ostomy and continence nurses after the earthquake in Türkiye: a phenomenological study
Authors: Dilek Aktaş, Burçin Irmak, Sinan Aydoğan, Zehra Göçmen Baykara
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGiven the earthquake-related injuries, particular needs of patients with chronic wounds and ostomy, Wound, Ostomy, and Continence Nurses (WOCNs) who care for disaster victims play critical roles in this extraordinary process.ObjectiveTo explore and identify the experiences of the WOCNs who work in the provinces affected by the Kahramanmaraş and Hatay earthquakes in 2023.MethodsThis study is a qualitative research with a phenomenological design. In-depth interviews were conducted with the WOCNs online between October and December 2023. A semi-structured interview form was used in the research. One-to-one interviews were conducted with ten WOCNs in Türkiye. The qualitative interviews were analyzed using thematic analysis. Reporting of the study was used to the COREQ checklist.ResultsAt the end of the study, six themes from the data: “The multidimensional sufferings of earthquake victims”, “being an earthquake victim nurse”, “the roles and responsibilities of WOCNs”, “struggling to maintain care amidst chaos”, “nurses’ thoughts about the profession in an earthquake”, and “lessons learned from the earthquake”.ConclusionIt was determined that WOCNs experienced difficulties in continuing patient care after the earthquake disaster and that they made significant contributions to the management of nursing services with their experience and skills. WOCNs highlighted the importance of establishing teams that include WOCNs, increasing the number of WOCNs, ensuring supply chains for ostomy and wound care materials, and providing targeted training for personnel in order to efficiently manage post-earthquake care services. These suggestions indicate potential strategies to strengthen healthcare resilience in disaster contexts.

Background

Natural disasters are significant events that pose a direct threat to human life. Data from the World Health Organization show that within the past ten years, natural disasters have impacted over 2.6 billion people [1]. The Emergency Events Database (EM-DAT) reported in its 2022 report that 387 natural disasters occurred worldwide, affecting 185 million people and killing 30,704 individuals [2].

Earthquakes are one of natural disasters that can happen unexpectedly, without notice, and at any time [1]. Around the world, earthquakes affected 5.5 million people between 2002 and 2021 [2]. Three earthquakes occurred in Türkiye on February 6,2023, in the Pazarcık and Elbistan districts of Kahramanmaraş, and on February 20, 2023 in Yayladağı, Hatay. These events, referred to as the disaster of the century, caused significant damage in 11 cities and affected around 16% of Türkiye’s population [3]. More than 48.000 people died, and almost 500.000 were evacuated from the region as a result of the earthquakes [4].

According to the literature, fractures, soft tissue injuries, and crush injuries are the most common injuries after earthquakes [5–7]. Following an earthquake, pressure injuries can increase by up to tenfold [8]. Pressure injuries can arise due to a variety of factors, including the age of the affected individuals, spinal cord injuries, prolonged immobility under pressure, nutritional and fluid deficits, psychological trauma, and inadequate service delivery [8, 9]. Wound site infections are caused by factors like contamination, tissue loss, insufficient or delayed wound cleansing, and debridement [10]. At the same time, it becomes challenging to manage the care process for the aged, disabled, and chronically ill people [11]. Ostomy patients may have difficulty getting ostomy care products. According to one study, patients had to use other materials due to a lack of materials until ostomy materials provided after the Great East Japan earthquake reached the earthquake zone [12].

Given the earthquake-related injuries, the increase in pressure injuries following the earthquake, the difficulties in managing chronic diseases, and the effects of the earthquake on individuals with ostomy, it is clear that WOCNs who care for disaster victims play critical roles in this extraordinary process. Crush injuries, amputations, and surgical wounds in individuals following devastating earthquakes brought attention to the need for qualified healthcare professionals. The Wound Ostomy Incontinence Nurses Association (YOIHD) of Türkiye provided training brochures and ostomy and wound care supplies to WOCNs who were working in the affected region [13].

There is very little information in the literature about the WOCNs experiences with care following the earthquake disaster. The aim of the study is to explore and identify the experiences of the WOCNs who work in the provinces affected by the Kahramanmaraş and Hatay earthquakes in 2023. This research will offer an original perspective to the field by thoroughly analyzing the earthquake experience of WOCNs using a qualitative methodology.

Methods

Aim

The aim of the study is to identify the experiences of the WOCNs who work in the provinces affected by the Kahramanmaraş and Hatay earthquakes in 2023.

Design

The study used phenomenological design, one of the qualitative research techniques.

Participants and sampling

Criterion sampling, a purposive sample, was used in the study. The process of choosing every participant who meets certain criteria is known as criterion sampling [14]. WOCNs who had been practicing for at least a year, worked in the earthquake zone, had internet access, and volunteered to participate in the study were accepted as inclusion criteria. Ten WOCNs from seven earthquake-affected cities participated in the study (Table 1). In the literature, the sufficiency of 9–17 interviews to reach data saturation in qualitative research conducted in the form of interviews is stated. The stabilisation of codes and sub-themes is accepted as an indicator of data saturation [15]. In this study, no new sub-themes were identified in the ninth and tenth interviews; therefore, it was considered that data saturation had been achieved.

Data collection

Every researcher has a PhD, has worked with qualitative research techniques before, and is an expert in WOC nursing. The study’s data was collected between October to December 2023 through in-depth interviews. WOCNs who work in the earthquake area were invited to share their expectations through YOIHD email addresses and social media profiles after obtaining research permits. In this study, interviews with ten nurses were done separately online via Zoom Workplace. A moderator (SA) who is a member of the nursing department and has training and expertise in qualitative research conducted each interview. Each interview lasted, on average, between thirty and forty-five minutes. Two researchers (DA and BI) independently transcribed every interview. After transcription, the reports were forwarded to participants for verification.

Data analysis

The inductive content analysis approach was used for analyzing the qualitative data. The aim of thematic analysis is to identify patterns of meaning in data about lived experiences [18]. Qualitative data was analyzed in four steps. In the initial stage of analysis(i), two researchers (SA and DA) read each text several times to familiarize themselves with and immerse in the data. After that, they each carried out an independent thematic analysis. Every statement in the interview was used as the analysis’s unit. In the second phase(ii), meaningful phrases were found, and open coding was carried out after the reports were reread from a phenomenological reductionist perspective. In the third stage(iii), similar codes were grouped into the same categories, relationships among them were determined, and sub-themes were developed. The final stage(iv) included determining themes, sub-themes, and codes. Two other authors reviewed the initial analysis and interview summaries and gave feedback on the data analysis. Until they reached a consensus on the conclusions, all researchers debated the findings. Following the final data analysis, the phrases and themes were translated into English by two researchers (BI and SA) who were bilingual and experienced in the translation; the other authors then reviewed and approved the translation. Software for qualitative analysis was not used.

Rigour and trustworthiness

The study was structured and reported using the COnsolidated Criteria for REporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) checklist [19]. The Lincoln and Guba trustworthiness criteria (dependability, confirmability, credibility, and transferability) for qualitative research [20] were taken into consideration in this study. A prolonged interwiew (30–45 min) with the participants were engaged to ensure trustworthiness and understanding, and the documents were returned to the participants within a week to receive participant confirmation. In order to undertake researcher triangulation, many researchers were involved in the data collecting (SA), documentation (DA and BI), analysis, and interpretation processes (SA, DA). A purposive sampling strategy was used to increase the study transferability, and ten nurses who had no relation to the researchers and had experienced the earthquake in seven different provinces were included. Thus, it was aimed to provide data variety. To guarantee the study’s confirmability, the themes, sub-themes, and codes were elucidated through direct quotations from the viewpoints of the nurses without any commentary. To guarantee the study’s credibility, the final author (ZGB), who was not directly involved in the analysis process, supervised the entire research process. The researchers’ prior experience with qualitative research methods ensured that the interview and analysis processes were conducted in a coherent, in-depth, and impartial manner, thereby enhancing the credibility of the results. Moreover, ensuring that the interviewer had no prior acquaintance with the participants prevented bias arising from researcher–participant familiarity in the study findings.

Results

After the analysis of the interviews, six main themes and thirteen sub-themes were identified (Fig. 1). The six main themes are “The Multidimensional sufferings of earthquake victims”, “being an earthquake victim nurse”, “the roles and responsibilities of WOCNs”, “struggling to maintain care amidst chaos”, “nurses’ perception/feeling about the profession in an earthquake”, and “lessons learned from the earthquake”.

Discussion

In this qualitatively designed study, the authors focused on uncovering the care experiences of WOCNs after the devastating earthquake in Türkiye and obtaining their recommendations for disaster preparedness. This chapter discusses the six main themes that emerged from the research.

The multidimensional sufferings of earthquake victims

People experience psychological, economic, and physical problems after the earthquake [21]. In the study, WOCNs pointed to increasing bodily suffering and vulnerability, evidenced by higher rates of chronic wounds, the development of infection, a delay in the healing process, and the development of parastomal skin complications. Consistent with our findings, nurses observed trauma-related crush syndrome, amputation, fracture, burns, and infected wounds when caring for earthquake patients in Tunç’s study [22]. In a recent study, Şişman et al. reported that 62.9% of patients hospitalized in a university hospital following the earthquake showed signs of infection in the wound area [7]. A review of the literature and study findings reveals that wound-related problems and wound care are of primary importance in the care of earthquake victims. However, no study reporting the problems experienced by individuals with ostomy was found in the literature. In this study, it was observed that individuals with ostomy experienced ostomy-related physical problems. These findings indicate that ostomy patients would benefit from assessment for stomal complications during disasters.

In the study, WOCNs reported that earthquake victims experienced socioeconomic hardship, including difficulties accessing ostomy care materials and health services. Similarly, Alcalde-Castro et al. reported that cancer patients encountered significant challenges in accessing health services following the earthquake [23]. Kawasaki et al. indicated that ostomy patients experienced challenges in obtaining supplies during the earthquake [24]. These findings suggest that designing health systems to better enable patients to obtain medical supplies and services during disasters would help ensure continuity of care.

Participants observed pychological disintegration in earthquake victims, including loss and worsening body image. Alici and Kalanlar observed that elderly individuals exhibited panic, anxiety, fear of death, and feelings of isolation following the earthquake [25]. Kalyoncu et al. found that 90.9% of individuals hospitalized due to earthquake-related trauma were diagnosed with acute stress disorder, and 45.5% of these individuals developed new psychiatric complaints, including decreased pleasure and morale and reluctance during hospitalization [26]. Literature and study findings indicate that earthquakes have an impact on mental health as well as physical well-being. Therefore, it is recommended that the mental status of individuals be evaluated during the care process of earthquake victims and that psychological support be provided to individuals to facilitate the healing process.

Being an earthquake victim nurse

In the earthquake, nurses continued to provide care in challenging conditions. WOCNs, who were also earthquake victims, reported experiencing difficulties characterized by physical exhaustion related to nutrition, shelter, and sleep, as well as psychological burdens stemming from loss, fear of death, and fear of earthquakes. Salik et al. reported that nurses experienced environmental problems such as environmental hygiene, cold weather, and shelter after the earthquake [27]. Altuntaş et al. observed that nurses frequently encountered challenges such as fatigue, inattention, and difficulty meeting their basic needs, including nutrition and accommodation [28]. The results of studies and literature demonstrate the physical and psychological requirements of health workers in the context of disasters. In light of these findings, it can be concluded that it is of paramount importance to provide healthcare workers with both physical and psychological support in the context of disaster situations.

The roles and responsibilities of WOCNs

Nurses assume a variety of roles and responsibilities at each stage of the disaster [29]. In the study, WOCNs reported fulfilling a range of professional roles and responsibilities, including ostomy and wound care, treatment, complication management, patient education, telehealth services, therapeutic communication, registration, organization, and management of ethical problems during the earthquake. The findings of our study are consistent with those of Düzgün et al., who reported that the roles and responsibilities of healthcare professionals during and after the earthquake included professionalism, effective communication, effective and rapid care, and empathy [17]. Similarly, Çetinkaya Özdemir et al. found that nurses experienced fear of harm, ethical dilemmas, clear conscience, and a sense of sacrifice after the earthquake [30]. In addition to the aforementioned skills, Rezaei et al. stated that nurses should also possess creativity, psychological care, observation, and triage abilities [31]. The findings of the study and the literature indicate that indicate that nurses require specialized clinical and professional skills to deliver effective care in disaster settings.

Struggling to maintain care amidst chaos

Nurses may experience difficulties caring for earthquake victims [16]. In our study, WOCNs reported experiencing challenges in care continuity, including the high number of patients, the poor psychological status of patients, the inability to apply prevention interventions in care, and ineffective wound dressings. A qualitative study conducted with intensive care nurses revealed that nurses encountered difficulties in positioning patients due to the wide variety of earthquake victim patient profiles and reasons such as amputation, compartment syndrome, and burns [30].

The organization of tasks and the effective management of limited resources are vital in the aftermath of an earthquake [17]. The study indicated that WOCNs encountered challenges in administration, such as a lack of organization, a shortage of healthcare personnel, and a lack of materials. It is similarly indicated in the literature that similar problems to those identified in this research, such as bureaucratic difficulties and difficulties associated with task, authority, material, and equipment issues are encountered [32, 33]. Because of the destructive effect and magnitude of the earthquake, it can be expected that the lack of coordination, uncertainties, and disruptions in the management process will be evident in the initial period. However, these results highlight the importance of planning for disaster preparedness and maintaining health services in the event of a disaster.

Nurses’ thoughts about the profession in the earthquake

Nurses providing care for earthquake victims undergo various experiences, emotions, and thoughts [34]. Although the WOCNs who took part in the study reported an increase in positive perceptions of the nursing profession, including professional satisfaction, professional commitment, and leadership, following the earthquake, they also reported negative emotions, including feelings of inadequacy, burnout, and helplessness. The experience of caring for earthquake victims led nurses to a range of positive emotions, including an enhanced sense of professional satisfaction, the ability to fulfill the role of advocate, and the development of empathy skills [32, 34]. However, a study conducted by Altuntaş et al. found that 48.5% of nurses working in the earthquake zone experienced feelings of helplessness, anger, and hopelessness [28]. Nurses who care for victims of earthquakes may demonstrate a range of emotional responses. The results of this study demonstrate the professional satisfaction that nurses derive from continuing to care for and assist others in all circumstances. In addition, they illustrate the negative emotions that accompany delivering care under challenging settings.

Lessons learned from the earthquake

Nurses must have professional earthquake knowledge and crisis management skills to provide care to individuals after an earthquake [27]. In the course of our study, WOCNs highlighted organizational preparedness and professional preparedness. These included planning, team building, material supply, and ensuring adequate employment. At the same time, WOCNs stated that staff training and organizing scientific activities could be effective in disaster preparedness. Cetinkaya Özdemir et al. [30]. reported that on-site training should address the urgent need for support. Furthermore, they emphasized the importance of psychological support and a holistic approach. Altuntaş et al. recommended that nurses receive more comprehensive training in disaster management. Additionally, they recommended that nurses with specialized expertise in disaster nursing be trained and that coordination between nurses be ensured [28]. The literature and study findings indicate that the suggestions provided by nurses are designed to address the challenges they encounter in the process. In this context, it is thought that these suggestions may be effective in alleviating the difficulties encountered in disaster management.

Limitations

The study’s narrow geographic coverage and purposive sample of ten nurses across seven provinces limit the generalizability of the findings to other settings and populations. Data were collected in Turkish and reported in English, introducing potential translation-related meaning loss, and researcher interpretation may have influenced analysis despite measures to enhance rigor. Since the research used the criterion sampling method, one of the purposeful sampling types, the data collecting process ended when the participant data began to repeat. Another limitation of the study is that it excludes WOCNs who live outside the provinces affected by the earthquake but provide treatment to earthquake victims. Despite limitations, the study contributes to literature on the subject. The research methodology allowed for rich data collection through interviews, providing depth and content.

Conclusion

Earthquakes are among the unavoidable tragedies for Türkiye, located in a crucial earthquake zone. Participants reported that access to health services and supplies became more difficult after the earthquake, that ostomy‑related complications and acute and chronic wounds increased, and that wound healing was often delayed; they also described considerable psychological problems among patients facing losses and health problems. Participants emphasized the role of WOCNs in providing holistic care, preventing problems, managing complications, and offering counseling. WOCNs also reported facing numerous environmental and managerial challenges while fulfilling their roles, and although these conditions sometimes elicited negative emotions, being on the front line and providing specialized care was described as positively affecting professional perceptions. Paticipants highlighted the importance of establishing teams that include WOCNs, increasing the number of WOCNs, ensuring supply chains for ostomy and wound care materials, and providing targeted training for personnel. These suggestions indicate potential strategies to strengthen healthcare resilience in disaster contexts. In terms of the generalizability of the study results, it is recommended that more comprehensive mixed-method studies be conducted, including hospital administrators and disaster coordination teams. It is expected that this will offer guidance for developing strategies in the related field.

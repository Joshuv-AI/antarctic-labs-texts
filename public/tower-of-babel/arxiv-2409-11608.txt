Global Ricci Curvature Behaviour for the Kähler-Ricci Flow with Finite Time Singularities
Alexander Bednarek

Abstract
We consider the Kähler-Ricci flow ( X , ω ⁡ ( t ) ) t ∈ [ 0 , T ) (X,\omega(t))_{t\in[0,T)} on a compact manifold where the time of singularity, T T , is finite. We assume the existence of a holomorphic map from the Kähler manifold X X to some analytic variety Y Y which admits a Kähler metric on a neighbourhood of the image of X X and that the pullback of this metric yields the limiting cohomology class along the flow. This is satisfied, for instance, by the assumption that the initial cohomology class is rational, i.e., [ ω 0 ] ∈ H 1 , 1 ​ ( X , ℚ ) [\omega_{0}]\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Q}) . Under these assumptions we prove an L 4 L^{4} -like estimate on the behaviour of the Ricci curvature and that the Riemannian curvature is Type I I in the L 2 L^{2} -sense.



1
Introduction

Suppose
M
is a compact real manifold. Recall that a Ricci flow
(M,g(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
with finite time singularity
T<\infty
is said to be of Type I,
[
3
]
, if there exists a
C>0
such that

\sup_{M\times[0,T)}(T-t)|\Rm|\leq C.

This is conjectured to be the general setting for Kähler manifolds at least in the sense of scalar curvature,
[
8
, Conj 1.1]
, and we attempt to provide some general results in this direction.

The Ricci flow was introduced by Hamilton in
1982
in a program to study the geometric and topological structures of
3
-manifolds, which lead to a proof of Thurston’s Geometrization conjecture and the subsequent Poincare conjecture by Perelman in
2003
,
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. The Kähler-Ricci flow, i.e., the Ricci flow in the setting of Kähler manifolds, has been extensively studied since it’s first appearance in Cao’s alternate proof of the Calabi-Yau conjecture,
[
2
]
, a result which provided ground-breaking progress in the prescribed Ricci curvature problem,
[
20
,
16
]
. The Kähler-Ricci flow has since been viewed as a powerful tool to study the geometric structure of Kähler manifolds and this has been accentuated by the Analytic Minimal Model Program proposed over a decade ago by Song and Tian,
[
14
]
, a geometric-analytic version of Mori’s Minimal Model Program for the birational classification of algebraic varieties.

The Kähler-Ricci flow is defined as follows. Suppose
(X,\omega_{0})
is a compact Kähler manifold. A family of Kähler metrics
\{\omega(t):t\in[0,T)\}
on
X
satisfies the (normalized) Kähler-Ricci flow if

\frac{\partial\omega(t)}{\partial t}=-\Ric(\omega(t))-\omega(t),\quad\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.

(KRF)

The normalization gives nice cohomological behaviour to the flow.

Let us discuss some recent progress in understanding curvature behaviour along the Kähler-Ricci flow. For flows with finite time singularities, the pair of recent papers by Hallgren, Jian, Song and Tian,
[
8
,
4
]
, established a local Type I estimate near Ricci vertices for the scalar curvature along the Kähler-Ricci flow under the assumption that the initial Kähler class is rational, i.e.,
[\omega_{0}]\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. Additionally, Zhang,
[
22
]
, proves that under the assumption of a map
f:X\to Y
from the Kähler manifold
X
to some analytic variety
Y
, if there exists a smooth Kähler metric
\omega_{Y}
near the image of
X
satisfying
[f^{*}\omega_{Y}]=[\omega(T)]
then the scalar curvature is uniformly bounded above by
\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}
.

On the other hand, when the flow exists for all time, under the assumption that the canonical line bundle
K_{X}
is semi-ample, the behaviour of the scalar curvature has been extensively studied and is well understood, with some results about the Ricci curvature. Song and Tian have proven that the scalar curvature is uniformly bounded in this case,
[
15
]
, and Jian demonstrated that the scalar curvature converges to the negative of the Kodaira dimension on the regular part of the induced Calabi-Yau fibration,
[
7
]
. Most recently, Hein, Lee and Tosatti have shown that for intermediate Kodaira dimension, i.e.,
0<\Kod(X)<\dim X
, the Ricci curvature is uniformly bounded away from the singular fibres of the fibration,
[
6
]
.

There is also some global control of the Ricci curvature. Tian and Zhang,
[
18
]
, prove that for compact Kähler manifolds with semi-ample canonical bundle and positive Kodaira dimension there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\tau\in[0,\infty)

\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{X}|\Ric(\omega(t))|^{4}\omega(t)^{n}dt\leq C.

We also note that for the Ricci flow of a
4
-dimensional, real, compact manifold
M
with finite time singularity, Simon,
[
13
]
, has proven the following
L^{4}
-like bound on the Ricci curvature under the assumption that the scalar curvature is uniformly bounded

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}|\Ric(g(t))|^{4}dV_{g(t)}dt\leq C.

However, in the Kähler-Ricci flow with finite time singularity, the scalar curvature cannot be uniformly bounded and must blow-up towards positive infinity,
[
22
]
. Simon presents a more general theorem without this assumption on scalar curvature which one can use to derive equation (
3.9
) in the complex dimension
2
case.

We consider the Kähler-Ricci flow with finite time singularities in a geometric setting which mirrors the semi-ample case and use Zhang’s
C^{0}
-estimates in
[
22
]
in combination with methods by Tian and Zhang from
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
to deduce the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
(X,\omega_{0})
is a compact Kähler manifold and
(X,\omega(t))_{t\in[0,T)}
is a solution to (
KRF
) or (
UKRF
) where
T<\infty
. Suppose there exists a map
f:X\to Y
where
Y
is an analytic variety and there exists a smooth Kähler metric
\omega_{Y}
in a neighbourhood of the image
f(X)
satisfying
[f^{*}\omega_{Y}]=[\omega(T)]
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\omega_{0},f^{*}\omega_{Y},T)>0
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{X}(T-t)^{8}|\Ric(\omega(t))|^{4}_{\omega(t)}\omega(t)^{n}dt\leq C,

and

\int_{X}|\Rm(\omega(t))|^{2}_{\omega(t)}\omega(t)^{n}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}},

and

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{X}(T-t)^{8}|\nabla\Ric|_{\omega(t)}^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt\leq C,

and

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{X}(T-t)^{6}|\nabla R|_{\omega(t)}^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt\leq C.

The contents of this paper are summarized as follows. In section
2
an explanation of the geometric and cohomological assumptions is given together with a brief summary of global
C^{0}
-estimates which are essential in the computations which ensue. Next, in section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and provide many estimates on the Ricci potential. Section
4
improves the
L^{4}
-like estimate on the behaviour of the Ricci curvature along the flow to a genuine
L^{4}
-estimate, (
4.1
), which is dependent on the behaviour of the Laplacian of
\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})
. Finally, section
5
provides a conversion of the
L^{4}
-like estimate on the Ricci curvature for the normalized Kähler-Ricci flow to one for the unnormalized flow and an appendix is included for the calculation of some
4
-th order commutation relations of covariant derivatives.

2
The Geometric Setting and
C^{0}
-Behaviour

Let
(X,\omega_{0})
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(X)=n
. We consider the following normalized Kähler-Ricci flow

\frac{\partial\omega(t)}{\partial t}=-\Ric(\omega(t))-\omega(t),\quad\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.

It is well known that the flow exists as long the the evolving cohomology class
[\omega(t)]=e^{-t}[\omega_{0}]+(1-e^{-t})[K_{X}]
is positive,
[
17
]
, and the time of singularity, i.e. maximal existence time, is given by

T=\sup\{t\in(0,\infty]:e^{-t}[\omega_{0}]+(1-e^{-t})[K_{X}]\in\Kah(X)\},

where
\Kah(X)
denotes the Kähler cone of
X
. We shall assume
T<\infty
which corresponds with the case where the canonical line bundle
K_{X}
is not nef.

Let us also assume that there exists a holomorphic map
f:X\to Y
, where
Y
is an analytic variety that is smooth in a neighbourhood of the image
f(X)
, and that there exists a Kähler metric
\omega_{Y}
on this neighbourhood such that
[f^{*}\omega_{Y}]=[\omega(T)]
. Note that although
\omega(T)
is undefined, the limiting class
[\omega(T)]
is still defined by
[\omega(T)]:=e^{-T}[\omega_{0}]+(1-e^{-T})[K_{X}]
.

Under these assumptions we can choose a closed, smooth
(1,1)
-form
\chi
representing
[K_{X}]
such that

f^{*}\omega_{Y}=e^{-T}\omega_{0}+(1-e^{-T})\chi

and a smooth volume form
\Omega
such that
\chi=-\Ric(\Omega)=i\partial\overline{\partial}\log\Omega
. By a standard reduction argument involving the
\partial\overline{\partial}
-lemma, e.g. see
[
20
,
17
]
, the Kähler-Ricci flow (
KRF
) is equivalent to the following complex Monge-Ampere type equation

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=\log\frac{(e^{-t}\omega_{0}+(1-e^{-t})\chi+i\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)^{n}}{\Omega}-\varphi,\quad\varphi(0)=0

(SKRF)

where
\omega(t)=e^{-t}\omega_{0}+(1-e^{-t})\chi+i\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi(t)
.

Remark 2.1
.

This situation is typically achieved by the assumption that
[\omega_{0}]
is rational, i.e.
[\omega_{0}]\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
, so that by Kawamata’s rationality and base-point free theorems we know
[\omega(T)]\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
and we have an induced holomorphic map
f:X\to Y\subseteq\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N}
satisfying
[f^{*}\omega_{Y}]=[\omega(T)]
, where
\omega_{Y}
is now a multiple of the restriction of the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N}
to
Y
,
[
22
]
.

Also, it is well-known that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\omega(t)^{n}\leq C\Omega
. This means the volume is bounded along the flow, i.e.,

\Vol_{\omega(t)}(X)=\int_{X}\omega(t)^{n}\leq C.

A quick calculation,
[
22
]
, shows that

(1-e^{t-T})\Ric(\omega(t))=e^{t-T}\omega(t)-f^{*}\omega_{Y}-i\partial\overline{\partial}u(t),

(2.1)

where
u=(1-e^{t-T})\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\varphi
, and for the scalar curvature,

(1-e^{t-T})R=ne^{t-T}-\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})-\Delta u.

(2.2)

For this reason, i.e. equation (
2.1
), we shall call
u
the
Ricci potential
. One can observe that letting
T\to\infty
yields the same Ricci potential and decompositions, (
2.1
), (
2.2
), of the Ricci and scalar curvatures in terms of the Ricci potential as in the semi-ample case. Additionally, one can calculate the following evolution equations,
[
22
]
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)u=-n+\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)|\nabla u|^{2}=|\nabla u|^{2}-|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}-|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}+2\Real\left\langle\nabla\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}),\overline{\nabla}u\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Delta u=\Delta u+\left\langle\Ric,i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle+\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}).

This follows the setup in
[
22
]
and the following theorem summarizes Zhang’s results.

Theorem 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C=C(\omega_{0},f^{*}\omega_{Y},T)>0
such that on
X\times[0,T)
the following estimates hold

\displaystyle|u|\leq C,\quad|\nabla u|\leq C,\quad\Delta u\leq C,\quad-\frac{C}{T-t}\leq\Delta u,

\displaystyle\quad 0\leq\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})\leq C,\quad-C\leq R,\quad R\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}},

where all norms, gradients, Laplacians and scalar curvatures are taken with respect to the metric
\omega(t)
.

3
Global
L^{4}
-Behaviour of the Ricci Curvature

Let
\nabla
and
\overline{\nabla}
denote the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
-parts of the Chern connection of
(X,\omega(t))
, respectively. We shall use the convention that
C
represents a positive constant which may change line to line and may represent different constants within the same line. We note that the constants below will always depend on
\omega_{0}
,
f^{*}\omega_{Y}
and
T
due to Theorem
2.2
unless otherwise stated. Additionally, we may drop the volume form
\omega(t)^{n}
when manipulating integrals.

3.1
Preliminary
L^{2}
-Estimates

We begin with the following Proposition which is included in Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
the following estimate holds

\int_{X}|\Rm|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}},

(3.1)

and additionally,

\int_{X}|R|\omega^{n}\leq C.

(3.2)

Proof.

Let us assume
n>1
, although the statement still holds in the case
n=1
. One can use Chern-Weil theory (see Kobayashi
[
9
, §4.4]
), to express Chern forms,
c_{1},c_{2}
, dependent on
t
, in terms of the Riemannian, Ricci and scalar curvatures as

\displaystyle c^{2}_{1}\wedge\omega^{n-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}(R^{2}-|\Ric|^{2})\omega^{n},

\displaystyle c_{2}\wedge\omega^{n-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}n(n-1)}(R^{2}-2|\Ric|^{2}+|\Rm|^{2})\omega^{n}.

Thus

\int_{X}(c_{2}-c_{1}^{2})\wedge\omega^{n-2}=\frac{1}{8\pi n(n-1)}\int_{X}(|\Rm|^{2}-R^{2})\omega^{n},

so that the left-hand side integrand is purely cohomological and thus the integral is a topological invariant for all
t\in[0,T]
as the class
[\omega(T)]
is still well-defined. Furthermore, the integral on the left-hand side is a continuous function from
[0,T]
to
\mathbb{R}
, so it is bounded above and below. Therefore

\int_{X}|\Rm|^{2}\leq C+\int_{X}R^{2}.

Next, note that

\int_{X}R\omega^{n}=\int_{X}n\Ric\wedge\omega^{n-1}=2\pi n\int_{X}c_{1}\wedge\omega^{n-1}

so that by the same argument

\int_{X}R\omega^{n}\leq C.

Additionally, as
R
is uniformly bounded below, we have

\int_{X}|R|\omega^{n}\leq C.

By Theorem
2.2
one finds

\displaystyle\int_{X}R^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}\int_{X}|R|\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}

and the result follows.
∎

The above argument is essentially entirely topological apart from the use of Theorem
2.2
in estimating the
L^{2}
-norm of the scalar curvature. Additionally, the
L^{2}
-behaviour of the Riemann curvature tensor is entirely equivalent to the
L^{2}
-behaviour of the scalar curvature as a result. This confirms that the Riemannian curvature is indeed Type I in the
L^{2}
-sense.

The next two results provide essential control on the complex Hessians of the Ricci potentials to be used throughout.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
we have

\int_{X}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.2
, one can calculate in normal coordinates

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}u\cdot\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{j}u

\displaystyle=\int_{X}-\nabla_{\overline{j}}u\cdot\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{j}u

\displaystyle=\int_{X}-\nabla_{\overline{j}}u\cdot\nabla_{j}\Delta u

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\Delta u)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}.

∎

Take note that it is true for any arbitrary, smooth, real function
v:X\to\mathbb{R}
that

\int_{X}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}\omega^{n}=\int_{X}(\Delta v)^{2}\omega^{n}.

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
we have

\int_{X}|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.2
, one can calculate in normal coordinates

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u\cdot\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}u

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u\cdot\nabla_{\overline{j}}u

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}(\nabla_{j}\Delta u+R_{j\overline{k}}\nabla_{k}u)\nabla_{\overline{j}}u

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\Delta u)^{2}-R_{j\overline{k}}\nabla_{k}u\nabla_{\overline{j}}u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(\Delta u)^{2}+|\Ric||\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}},

where the last line uses the fact that
\left\|\Ric\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\left\|\Ric\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C^{\prime}\left\|\Rm\right\|_{L^{2}}
in combination with (
3.1
).
∎

We also require better control on the behaviour of the trace
\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
the following estimate holds

\int_{X}|\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{C}{T-t}.

Proof.

Note the following inequality,
[
18
, Eq. 3.19]
,
[
14
]
, which still holds in our case,

\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})\geq-|\Ric|\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})-C(\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}+\frac{|\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}}{\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}\leq C(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})+|\Ric|+1).

(3.3)

Therefore we have

\int_{X}|\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}\leq C\int_{X}(|\Ric|+1)\leq\frac{C}{T-t},

since
\left\|\Ric\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\left\|\Ric\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C^{\prime}\left\|\Rm\right\|_{L^{2}}
.
∎

The following estimate introduces an additional time integral which is then forced to be carried through the remaining estimates, in particular this includes the
L^{4}
-type estimate of the Ricci curvature. We provide some further analysis relevant to
\nabla\Delta u
in the next section in an attempt to circumvent this issue.

Proposition 3.5
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\lim_{\tau\to T}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(T-t)^{4}|\nabla\Delta u(t)|^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt\leq C.

Proof.

Recall the evolution equation for
\Delta u
,

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Delta u=\Delta u+\left\langle\Ric,i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle+\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})

from which one can calculate

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)(\Delta u)^{2}=2(\Delta u)^{2}+2\Delta u\left\langle\Ric,i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle+2\Delta u\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})-2|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{X}2|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}2(\Delta u)^{2}+2\Delta u\left\langle\Ric,i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle+2\Delta u\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta u)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}2(\Delta u)^{2}+2|\Delta u||\Ric||\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|-2\nabla_{i}\Delta u\cdot\nabla_{\overline{i}}\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta u)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}2(\Delta u)^{2}+(\Delta u)^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}+|\Ric|^{2}+|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}+|\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta u)^{2}.

and so

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{4}}-\int_{X}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta u)^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}(\Delta u)^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta u)^{2}\omega^{n}+\int_{X}(\Delta u)^{2}(-R-n)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Delta u)^{2}\omega^{n}+\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}

so that one has

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{4}}-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}(\Delta u)^{2}

and hence

\displaystyle\int_{X}(T-t)^{4}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}\leq C-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}(T-t)^{4}(\Delta u)^{2}.

Integrating from
0
to
\tau\in[0,T)
gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(T-t)^{4}|\nabla\Delta u(t)|^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\tau-\int_{X}(T-\tau)^{4}(\Delta u(\tau))^{2}\omega(\tau)^{n}+\int_{X}T^{4}(\Delta u(0))^{2}\omega_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq CT+C

\displaystyle\leq C.

The result follows as
C
is independent of
\tau
.
∎

Note that one does not have to pull the factor of
(T-t)^{4}
inside the integral but doing so yields a more pleasant
L^{4}
-like estimate on the Ricci curvature and estimates for some other quantities. Otherwise, we find there exists a
C>0
such that for all
\tau\in[0,T)
we have

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}\omega^{n}dt\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{3}}.

(3.4)

The following result partially completes Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 3.6
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\lim_{\tau\to T}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(T-t)^{6}|\nabla R|^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt\leq C.

Proof.

Recall equation (
2.2
). From this we can see

(1-e^{t-T})\nabla R=-\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})-\nabla\Delta u

and thus

|\nabla R|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}(|\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}+|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}).

It follows that

\int_{X}|\nabla R|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{3}}+\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}.

Therefore there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\tau\in[0,T)
one has

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(T-t)^{6}|\nabla R|^{2}\leq C

and the result follows.
∎

3.2
The
L^{4}
-Behaviour of the Ricci Curvature

Take note of the following general estimates, which appear in
[
19
, Lem. 4.3]
,
[
18
, Lem. 3.5]
, but are now appropriately adapted to our situation.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
v:X\to\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary, smooth, real function. Then the following integral estimates hold

\displaystyle\int_{X}(|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2})|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 24\int_{X}(|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{4}|\Ric|^{2})\omega^{n},

\displaystyle\int_{X}(|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2})|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq 16\int_{X}(|\nabla v|^{2}(|\nabla\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|^{2})+(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{4}|\Ric|^{2})\omega^{n}.

Proof.

By the Bochner formula, (
6.1
),

\displaystyle\int_{X}(|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2})|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\Delta|\nabla v|^{2}-\left\langle\nabla\Delta v,\overline{\nabla}v\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla v,\overline{\nabla}\Delta v\right\rangle-\left\langle\Ric,\overline{\nabla}v\otimes\nabla v\right\rangle)|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}.

Using integration by parts for the first term gives

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Delta|\nabla v|^{2}\cdot|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}\nabla_{\overline{i}}|\nabla v|^{2}\cdot\nabla_{i}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}(\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{j}v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{j}}v+\nabla_{j}v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}v)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{l}v+\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v\cdot\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{l}v)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v||\nabla v|+|\nabla v||\nabla\nabla v|)(|\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v||\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v||\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\frac{1}{2}(|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2})|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+4|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}.

Integration by parts for the second term yields

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left\langle\nabla\Delta v,\overline{\nabla}v\right\rangle|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\Delta v(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\overline{k}}v\cdot|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+\nabla_{\overline{k}}v\cdot\nabla_{k}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+\Delta v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{k}}v\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{j}v+\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{j}}v\cdot\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{j}v)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+|\Delta v||\nabla v|(|\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v||\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v||\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}2(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left\langle\nabla v,\overline{\nabla}\Delta v\right\rangle|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}\leq\int_{X}2(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}.

The final term can be estimated by

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left\langle\Ric,\overline{\nabla}v\otimes\nabla v\right\rangle|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|\Ric||\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|\Ric|^{2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{4}.

Combining these estimates yields the first inequality.

For the second we proceed in the same way. The Bochner formula, (
6.1
), gives

\displaystyle\int_{X}(|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2})|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\Delta|\nabla v|^{2}-\left\langle\nabla\Delta v,\overline{\nabla}v\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla v,\overline{\nabla}\Delta v\right\rangle-\left\langle\Ric,\overline{\nabla}v\otimes\nabla v\right\rangle)|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}.

By integrating the first term by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Delta|\nabla v|^{2}\cdot|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}\nabla_{\overline{i}}|\nabla v|^{2}\cdot\nabla_{i}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}(\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{j}v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{j}}v+\nabla_{j}v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}v)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v+\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}v\cdot\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{k}}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v||\nabla v|+|\nabla v||\nabla\nabla v|)(|\nabla\nabla\nabla v||\nabla\nabla v|+|\nabla\nabla v||\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\frac{1}{2}(|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2})|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+2|\nabla v|^{2}(|\nabla\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|^{2}).

For the second term we find

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left\langle\nabla\Delta v,\overline{\nabla}v\right\rangle|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\Delta v(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\overline{k}}v\cdot|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+\nabla_{\overline{k}}v\cdot\nabla_{k}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+\Delta v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{k}}v\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}v\cdot\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}v+\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}v\cdot\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}v)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\Delta v||\nabla v|(|\nabla\nabla\nabla v||\nabla\nabla v|+|\nabla\nabla v||\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}2(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}(|\nabla\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|^{2}).

Similarly,

-\int_{X}\left\langle\nabla v,\overline{\nabla}\Delta v\right\rangle|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}\leq\int_{X}2(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}(|\nabla\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|^{2}).

Finally,

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left\langle\Ric,\overline{\nabla}v\otimes\nabla v\right\rangle|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|\Ric||\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|\Ric|^{2}|\nabla v|^{4}+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\nabla v|^{4}.

The second inequality follows.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}|\nabla\Ric||\nabla v||\nabla\nabla v|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta v|^{2}+|\Ric||\nabla v||\nabla\Delta v|+C|\Rm|^{2}+\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}|\nabla\nabla v|^{4}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\Ric|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\frac{3}{2}|\nabla\Delta v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}|\Ric|^{2}+C|\Rm|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\Ric|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}|\nabla\nabla v|^{4}

where we have used a commutation relation from Lemma
6.3
in the second step and
C^{\prime}
is to be determined. Similarly, one finds that

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\frac{3}{2}|\nabla\Delta v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}|\Ric|^{2}+C|\Rm|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\Ric|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}|\nabla\nabla v|^{4}

for the same
C^{\prime}

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}3|\nabla\Delta v|^{2}+C|\Rm|^{2}+|\nabla\Ric|^{2}+C|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\frac{32}{C^{\prime}}|\nabla v|^{2}(|\nabla\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\overline{\nabla}\nabla\nabla v|^{2})

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\frac{32}{C^{\prime}}(\Delta v)^{2}|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{32}{C^{\prime}}|\nabla v|^{4}|\Ric|^{2}

Corollary 3.15
.

For any
1\leq p

C=C(p,\omega_{0},f^{*}\omega_{Y},T)>0

P>p

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists a
C>0
such that for all
\tau\in[0,T)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u(\tau)|^{2}\omega(\tau)^{n}\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{3}}+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt,

and additionally

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(|\nabla\nabla\Delta u(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u(t)|^{2})\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{3}}+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt.

Proof.

This proof is similar to
[
19
, Thm 4.5]
. First we combine the evolution equation for
\Delta u
with equation (
2.1
),

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Delta u=(1+e^{t-T})\Delta u-\left\langle f^{*}\omega_{Y},i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle-|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}+\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})

and calculate,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}-|\nabla\nabla\Delta u|^{2}-|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\Real\left\langle\nabla\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)\Delta u,\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}\right)\omega^{n}+\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}(-n-R)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}(1-n-R)-|\nabla\nabla\Delta u|^{2}-|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}2(1+e^{t-T})|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}-2\Real\left\langle\nabla\left\langle f^{*}\omega_{Y},i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle,\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{X}-2\Real\left\langle\nabla|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2},\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle+2\Real\left\langle\nabla\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}),\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}C|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}-|\nabla\nabla\Delta u|^{2}-|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}-2\Real\left\langle\nabla\left\langle f^{*}\omega_{Y},i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle,\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle-2\Real\left\langle\nabla|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2},\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{X}2\Real\left\langle\nabla\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}),\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle.

Let us estimate the last three terms independently. Note that,

\displaystyle\int_{X}-2\Real\left\langle\nabla\left\langle f^{*}\omega_{Y},i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle,\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{X}2\left\langle f^{*}\omega_{Y},i\partial\overline{\partial}u\right\rangle\Delta\Delta u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}2|f^{*}\omega_{Y}||\nabla\overline{\nabla}u||\Delta\Delta u|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}C|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}.

Next, using integration by parts and applying (
3.6
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{X}-2\Real\left\langle\nabla|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2},\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{X}2|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{2}\Delta\Delta u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}C|\nabla\overline{\nabla}u|^{4}+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{4}}+\int_{X}C|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}.

For the final term, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}2\Real\left\langle\nabla\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}),\overline{\nabla}\Delta u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}2\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})\Delta\Delta u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}C(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}.

Combining these estimates yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{4}}+\int_{X}C|\nabla\Delta u|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\nabla\Delta u|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}+C(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}.

It follows, by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, that for any
\tau\in[0,T)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u(\tau)|^{2}\omega(\tau)^{n}-\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u(0)|^{2}\omega_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{3}}-C

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(|\nabla\Delta u(t)|^{2}+(\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2})\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(|\nabla\nabla\Delta u(t)|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u(t)|^{2})\omega(t)^{n}dt.

By applying (
3.4
), we see

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\Delta u(\tau)|^{2}\omega(\tau)^{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(|\nabla\nabla\Delta u(t)|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u(t)|^{2})\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{3}}+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt.

∎

If this result is applied to the Ricci curvature, i.e. (
3.6
) and the proof of (
3.8
), we see that there exists a
C>0
such that for all
\tau\in[0,T)
we have

\int_{X}|\Ric(\omega(\tau))|^{4}\omega(\tau)^{n}\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{8}}+\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{4}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt.

(4.1)

Also note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(\Delta\Delta u(t))^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u(t)|^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{3}}+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T)
we have

\int_{X}|\nabla\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}+\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}\omega^{n}.

Proof.

In a similar vein as the proof of Proposition
3.3
one has

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}\leq\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}+|\Ric||\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}.

Then using inequality (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\nabla\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}+C|\Ric|(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})+|\Ric|+1)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}+|\Ric|^{2}+|\Ric|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-t)^{2}}+C\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}.

∎

Finally, we can apply the previous two Propositions to derive estimates for the complex Hessians
\nabla\overline{\nabla}R
and
\nabla\nabla R
of the scalar curvature.

Corollary 4.3
.

There exists a
C>0
such that for all
\tau\in[0,T)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(T-t)^{2}(|\nabla\overline{\nabla}R(\omega(t))|^{2}

\displaystyle+|\nabla\nabla R(\omega(t))|^{2})\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{3}}+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt.

Proof.

Recall equation (
2.2
). From this it follows that

\displaystyle(1-e^{t-T})^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}R|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})+\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}+2|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u|^{2}

and likewise

(1-e^{t-T})^{2}|\nabla\nabla R|^{2}\leq 2|\nabla\nabla\tr_{\omega}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2}+2|\nabla\nabla\Delta u|^{2}.

Therefore, for any
\tau\in[0,T)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(T-t)^{2}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}R(\omega(t))|^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\Delta u(t)|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y})|^{2})\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(T-\tau)^{3}}+C\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(\Delta\tr_{\omega(t)}(f^{*}\omega_{Y}))^{2}\omega(t)^{n}dt,

and likewise for
\nabla\nabla R
.
∎

Also note that

\int_{X}(\Delta R)^{2}\omega^{n}=\int_{X}|\nabla\overline{\nabla}R|^{2}\omega^{n},

so that one can state a similar result for the Laplacian of the scalar curvature.

5
Conversion Between Kähler-Ricci Flows

We conclude with the conversion of the
L^{4}
-like estimate of the Ricci curvature to one for the unnormalized flow. One can perform similar calculations for other estimates in Theorem
1.1
.

The time and metric rescalings
s=e^{t}-1
and
\hat{\omega}(s)=e^{t}\omega(t)
, lets one see that the normalized Kähler-Ricci flow (
KRF
) is equivalent to the following unnormalized Kähler-Ricci flow

\frac{\partial\hat{\omega}(s)}{\partial s}=-\Ric(\hat{\omega}(s)),\quad\hat{\omega}(0)=\omega_{0},

(UKRF)

where
s\in[0,S)
for
S=e^{T}-1<\infty
.

The
L^{4}
-like estimate on the Ricci-curvature, (
3.8
), can be converted to an estimate for the unnormalized Kähler-Ricci flow as follows. For any
\tau\in[0,T)
let
\sigma=e^{\tau}-1\in[0,S)
, then

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(T-t)^{8}|\Ric(\omega(t))|^{4}_{\omega(t)}\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{X}(1-e^{t-T})^{8}|\Ric(\omega(t))|^{4}_{\omega(t)}\omega(t)^{n}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sigma}\int_{X}(1-e^{t-T})^{8}|\Ric(\omega(t))|^{4}_{\omega(t)}\frac{1}{(1+s)^{n+1}}\hat{\omega}(s)^{n}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sigma}\int_{X}\frac{e^{-8T}(S-s)^{8}(1+s)^{4}}{(1+s)^{n+1}}|\Ric(\hat{\omega}(s))|^{4}_{\hat{\omega}(s)}\hat{\omega}(s)^{n}ds

\displaystyle\geq e^{-8T}\frac{1}{(1+S)^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{\sigma}\int_{X}(S-s)^{8}|\Ric(\hat{\omega}(s))|_{\hat{\omega}(s)}^{4}\hat{\omega}(s)^{n}ds.

Therefore, there exists a
C>0
such that

\lim_{\sigma\to S}\int_{0}^{\sigma}\int_{X}(S-s)^{8}|\Ric(\hat{\omega}(s))|_{\hat{\omega}(s)}^{4}\hat{\omega}(s)^{n}ds\leq C.

That is, recall the existence of a holomorphic map
f:X\to Y
and a Kähler metric
\omega_{Y}
satisfying
[f^{*}\omega_{Y}]=[\omega(T)]
. This second assumption in terms of the unnormalized flow is still
[f^{*}\hat{\omega}_{Y}]=[\hat{\omega}(S)]
where
\hat{\omega}_{Y}=e^{T}\omega_{Y}
is still some Kähler metric on a neighbourhood of
Y
.

6.1
Commuting Covariant Derivatives

Recall the notion of normal coordinates.

Proposition 6.1

(Normal Coordinates)
.

Let
X
be a Kähler manifold with Kähler metric
g
and suppose
T
a Hermitian tensor, that is,
\overline{T_{i\overline{j}}}=T_{\overline{j}i}
. Then for any
x\in X
there exists local holomorphic coordinates, called
normal coordinates
, such that

g_{i\overline{j}}(x)=\delta_{i\overline{j}},\quad\partial_{k}g_{i\overline{j}}(x)=0,\quad\Gamma^{k}_{ij}(x)=0,\quad T_{i\overline{j}}(x)=\lambda_{i}\delta_{i\overline{j}},

for some
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Additionally, recall that the components of the covariant derivative of a tensor can be expressed in local coordinates in the following manner.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\nabla
and
\overline{\nabla}
denote the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
components of the Chern connection on the Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
. Suppose

T=T_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{j}_{q}}dz^{i_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes dz^{i_{p}}\otimes d\overline{z}^{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes d\overline{z}^{j_{q}}

and denote

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}T_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{j}_{q}}

\displaystyle=(\nabla T)(\partial_{k},\partial_{i_{1}},\dots,\partial_{i_{p}},\overline{\partial}_{j_{1}},\dots,\overline{\partial}_{j_{q}}),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{l}}T_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{j}_{q}}

\displaystyle=(\overline{\nabla}T)(\overline{\partial}_{l},\partial_{i_{1}},\dots,\partial_{i_{p}},\overline{\partial}_{j_{1}},\dots,\overline{\partial}_{j_{q}}).

Then in any local holomorphic coordinates we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}T_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{j}_{q}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{k}T_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{j}_{q}}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{p}\Gamma^{r}_{ki_{\alpha}}T_{i_{1}\dots r\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{j}_{q}},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{l}}T_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{j}_{q}}

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial}_{l}T_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{j}_{q}}-\sum_{\beta=1}^{q}\Gamma^{\overline{s}}_{\overline{l}\overline{j}_{\beta}}T_{i_{1}\dots i_{p}\overline{j}_{1}\dots\overline{s}\dots\overline{j}_{q}}.

We use these two tools in computing the following commutation relations.

Lemma 6.3
.

We have the following commutation relations under normal coordinates

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{k}v

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{k}\Delta v+R_{i\overline{j}}\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{k}v+R_{k\overline{j}l\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}v

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}\Delta v+R_{j\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}v+R_{k\overline{i}}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}v+R_{j\overline{l}k\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{l}v+\nabla_{j}R_{k\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}v.

Proof.

For the second commutation relation we have the following calculation under normal coordinates

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}v

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial}_{i}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}v

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial}_{i}(\partial_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}v-\Gamma^{l}_{ij}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}v-\Gamma^{l}_{ik}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{l}v)

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}(\partial_{j}\nabla_{k}v-\Gamma^{l}_{jk}\nabla_{l}v)-(\overline{\partial}_{i}\Gamma^{l}_{ij})\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}v-(\overline{\partial}_{i}\Gamma^{l}_{ik})\nabla_{j}\nabla_{l}v

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}v-\overline{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}(\Gamma^{l}_{jk}\nabla_{l}v)+R_{j\overline{l}}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}v+R_{k\overline{l}}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{l}v

where

\displaystyle-\overline{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}(\Gamma^{l}_{jk}\nabla_{l}v)

\displaystyle=-(\overline{\partial}_{i}\partial_{i}\Gamma^{l}_{jk})\nabla_{l}v-(\overline{\partial}_{i}\Gamma^{l}_{jk})\partial_{i}\nabla_{l}v-(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{l}_{jk})\overline{\partial}_{i}\nabla_{l}v

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}R_{j}{}^{l}{}_{k\overline{i}}\nabla_{l}v+R_{j\overline{l}k\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{l}v-(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{l}_{jk})\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{l}v

\displaystyle=\nabla_{k}R_{j\overline{l}}\nabla_{l}v+R_{j\overline{l}k\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{l}v-(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{l}_{jk})\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{l}v.

Also,

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}\Delta v

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}\partial_{k}(g^{i\overline{l}}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{l}v)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{j}\partial_{k}g^{i\overline{l}})\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{l}v+g^{i\overline{l}}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{l}v

\displaystyle=-g^{i\overline{q}}g^{p\overline{l}}(\partial_{j}\partial_{k}g_{p\overline{q}})\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v+\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{i}v

\displaystyle=-g^{p\overline{l}}(\partial_{j}\Gamma^{i}_{kp})\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v+\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{i}v

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{j}\Gamma^{i}_{kl})\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v+\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{i}v.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}v

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}\Delta v+(\partial_{j}\Gamma^{i}_{kl})\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v+\nabla_{k}R_{j\overline{l}}\nabla_{l}v+R_{j\overline{l}k\overline{i}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{l}v

\displaystyle-(\partial_{i}\Gamma^{l}_{jk})\nabla_{\overline{i}}\nabla_{l}v+R_{j\overline{l}}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}v+R_{k\overline{l}}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{l}v

and the result follows since
\partial_{q}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\partial_{q}(g^{k\overline{l}}\partial_{i}g_{j\overline{l}})=g^{k\overline{l}}\partial_{q}\partial_{i}g_{j\overline{l}}=\partial_{i}\Gamma^{k}_{qj}
in normal coordinates.
∎

6.2
Additional Equations

We provide a proof of Bochner’s formula.

Proposition 6.4

(Bochner’s Formula)
.

Let
v:X\to\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary, smooth, real function. Then

\Delta|\nabla v|^{2}=|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+\left\langle\nabla\Delta v,\overline{\nabla}v\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla v,\overline{\nabla}\Delta v\right\rangle+\left\langle\Ric,\overline{\nabla}v\otimes\nabla v\right\rangle.

(6.1)

Proof.

Using normal coordinates,

\displaystyle\Delta|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{j}(g^{k\overline{l}}\partial_{k}v\overline{\partial}_{l}v)

\displaystyle=g^{i\overline{j}}(\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{j}g^{k\overline{l}})\partial_{k}v\overline{\partial}_{l}v+g^{i\overline{j}}g^{k\overline{l}}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{j}(\partial_{k}v\overline{\partial}_{l}v)

\displaystyle=-g^{i\overline{j}}g^{p\overline{l}}g^{k\overline{q}}(\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{j}g_{p\overline{q}})\partial_{k}v\overline{\partial}_{l}v

\displaystyle+g^{i\overline{j}}g^{k\overline{l}}[(\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{j}\partial_{k}v)\overline{\partial}_{l}v+(\overline{\partial}_{j}\partial_{k}v)(\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{l}v)

\displaystyle+(\partial_{i}\partial_{k}v)(\overline{\partial}_{j}\overline{\partial}_{l}v)+\partial_{k}v(\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{j}\overline{\partial}_{l}v)]

\displaystyle=g^{i\overline{j}}g^{p\overline{l}}g^{k\overline{q}}R_{i\overline{j}p\overline{q}}\partial_{k}v\overline{\partial}_{l}v+g^{k\overline{l}}\partial_{k}(g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{j}v)\overline{\partial}_{l}v+g^{i\overline{j}}g^{k\overline{l}}(\overline{\partial}_{j}\partial_{k}v)(\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{l}v)

\displaystyle+g^{i\overline{j}}g^{k\overline{l}}(\partial_{i}\partial_{k}v)(\overline{\partial}_{j}\overline{\partial}_{l}v)+g^{k\overline{l}}(\partial_{k}v)\overline{\partial}_{l}(g^{i\overline{j}}\partial_{i}\overline{\partial}_{j}v)

\displaystyle=g^{p\overline{l}}g^{k\overline{q}}R_{p\overline{q}}\nabla_{k}v\nabla_{\overline{l}}v+g^{k\overline{l}}\nabla_{k}(\Delta v)\nabla_{\overline{l}}v+g^{i\overline{j}}g^{k\overline{l}}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v)(\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{k}v)

\displaystyle+g^{i\overline{j}}g^{k\overline{l}}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}v)(\nabla_{\overline{j}}\nabla_{\overline{l}}v)+g^{k\overline{l}}(\nabla_{k}v)\nabla_{\overline{l}}(\Delta v)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Ric,\overline{\nabla}v\otimes\nabla v\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\Delta v,\overline{\nabla}v\right\rangle+|\nabla\overline{\nabla}v|^{2}+|\nabla\nabla v|^{2}+\left\langle\nabla v,\overline{\nabla}\Delta v\right\rangle.

∎

In a similar manner one can calculate the following evolution equation.

Proposition 6.5
.

The norm of the Ricci curvature evolves according to

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)|\Ric|^{2}=2|\Ric|^{2}-2|\nabla\Ric|^{2}+2\Real\left\langle\Rm,\overline{\Ric\otimes\Ric}\right\rangle.

(6.2)

Proof.

Using normal coordinates, one calculates

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|\Ric|^{2}=2|\Ric|^{2}+2\Real\left\langle\Ric,\overline{\nabla}\nabla R\right\rangle+2R_{i\overline{j}}R_{j\overline{k}}R_{k\overline{i}},

and

\displaystyle\Delta|\Ric|^{2}=2|\nabla\Ric|^{2}+2\Real\left\langle\Ric,\overline{\nabla}\nabla R\right\rangle-2\Real\left\langle\Rm,\overline{\Ric\otimes\Ric}\right\rangle+2R_{i\overline{j}}R_{j\overline{k}}R_{k\overline{i}}.

∎

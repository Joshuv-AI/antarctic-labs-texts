Asymmetric Kedem-Katchalsky boundary conditions for systems with spatial heterogeneities
Pablo Álvarez-Caudevilla, Cristina Brändle, and Fermín González-Pereiro

Abstract
. This work investigates a model describing the interaction of two species inhabiting separate but adjacent areas. These populations are governed by a system of equations that account for spatial variations in growth rates and the effects of crowding. A key feature is the presence of areas within each domain where resources are unlimited and crowding effects are absent. The species interact solely through a common boundary interface, which is modeled by asymmetric Kedem-Katchalsky boundary conditions. The paper provides existence, non-existence, and behavior of positive solutions for the system. It is shown that a unique positive population distribution exists when one of the growth rate parameters falls within a specific range defined by two critical values. One of these critical values represents a bifurcation point where the population can emerge from extinction, while the other is determined by the characteristics of the refuge areas. The study also examines how the populations behave as the growth parameter approaches the upper critical value. This analysis reveals the phenomena of non-simultaneous blow-up, where one population component can grow infinitely large within its refuge zone while the other remains bounded.

1.
Introduction

In this work we are going to analyse the interaction of two species that live separately in two subdomains
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, being
\Omega
bounded and such that
\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
with
\partial\Omega_{1}={\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\Omega_{2}}
, (see Figure
1
for a precise illustration of a domain example). If we denote by
u_{1},u_{2}
the densities of these populations we have that
u_{i}>0
in
\Omega_{i}
and
u_{i}=0
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{i}
, for
i=1,2
. Under these assumptions we study the following system

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}=\lambda m_{1}(x)u_{1}-a_{1}(x)u_{1}^{p}\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega_{1},\\
-\Delta u_{2}=\lambda m_{2}(x)u_{2}-a_{2}(x)u_{2}^{p}\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega_{2},\end{cases}

where
\lambda
is a real parameter,
p>1
, and the functions
m_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i})
are assumed to be strictly positive in each domain, so that

\lambda m_{i}(x)
stands for the intrinsic growth rate of the population.
Moreover, the coefficients
a_{i}(x)
are two non negative Hölder continuous functions, i.e.,
a_{i}\in C^{0,\eta}(\overline{\Omega}_{i})
, for some
\eta\in(0,1)
, that measure the crowding effects of the populations in the corresponding subdomains
\Omega_{i}
. Indeed, we consider that there are regions inside
\Omega_{i}
such that the populations
u_{i}
have unlimited resources, and grow without limits, allowing
a_{i}=0
in some open subdomains of
\Omega_{i}
, respectively. Hence, we define the non-empty subsets

\Omega_{0,1}:=\{x\in\Omega_{1}:a_{1}(x)=0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{0,2}:=\{x\in\Omega_{2}:a_{2}(x)=0\},

so that
\overline{\Omega}_{0,i}\subset\Omega_{i}
, while
a_{i}
remain positive in the rest of the subdomain
\Omega_{i}
.
Finally, as for the boundary conditions, we consider an inner boundary,
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{1}
, that serves as the interface across which the species interact, so that

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{1}}}=\gamma_{1}(u_{2}-u_{1}),\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{2}}}=\gamma_{2}(u_{1}-u_{2})&&\text{on}\ \Gamma,\\
&u_{2}=0\quad&&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega=\partial\Omega_{2}\setminus\Gamma,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
\bm{\nu_{i}}
are the unitary outward normal vector which points outward with respect to
\Omega_{i}
, respectively, with
\bm{\nu}:=\bm{\nu_{1}}=-\bm{\nu_{2}}
on
\Gamma
and
\gamma_{i}>0
. Moreover, without loss of generality, we will assume throughout the paper that

\gamma_{2}\geq\gamma_{1}.

Figure 1.
A possible configuration of the domain
\Omega=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
.

This problem is a general model in population dynamics where both species interact through the common border
\Gamma
, that allows permeation from one part of the domain to the other. The interface boundary condition are also called Kedem-Katchalsky boundary condition, see
[
25
]
and was introduced in 1961 in a thermodynamic setting. In our particular situation, parameters
\gamma_{i}
measure the strength of the membrane depending on the direction the flux is going through. Therefore, the interaction between species occurs only by an interchange of flux, which remains proportional to the density change by a rate
1/\gamma_{i}
across the common boundary
\Gamma
. Thus, the smaller
\gamma_{i}
, the stronger the barrier is in the corresponding direction, and there is less transmission of that population. In fact, if
\gamma_{i}\to 0
for some
i=1,2
there is no transmission of that population across
\Gamma
. Note that we might have transmission from one domain to the other but not in the other direction, a case that we do not consider here.

From a biological point of view, the use of Kedem-Katchalsky interface conditions goes back to
[
36
]
, where the authors studied
the dynamics of the solute in the vessel and in the arterial wall. Since then, biological applications of membrane problems have increased and have been used to describe phenomena such as tumour invasion, transport of molecules through the
cell/nucleus membrane, cell polarisation and cell division or genetics, see
[
16
]
and references therein.
As far as the study of the mathematical properties is concerned, we were only aware of a few works: In
[
38
]
a semi-linear parabolic problem is considered. The authors investigate the effects of the barrier on the global
dynamics and on the existence, stability, and profile of spatially non-constant equilibria. A similar problem was previously considered in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. His analysis was mainly in the framework
of the existence of weak solutions of parabolic and elliptic differential equations with barrier boundary conditions. The author establishes a new comparison principle, the global existence of solutions, and sufficient conditions of stability and instability of equilibria. He shows, in particular, that the stability of equilibrium changes as the barrier permeability changes through a critical value. Very recently, Ciavolella and Perthame
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
adapted the well-known
L^{1}
-theory for parabolic reaction-diffusion systems to the membrane boundary conditions case and proved several regularity results. In all these previous problems, the permeability is symmetric,
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
, which makes them self-adjoint and implies the continuity of the flux across the interface.
Finally, in
[
33
]
a
semi-linear elliptic interface problem is studied. The authors analyse the existence and uniqueness of positive solutions, assuming that the interface condition is not symmetric, allowing different in and out fluxes of the domain. It is worth mentioning that all these references deal with problems that do not consider refuges (that is
\Omega_{0,i}
are empty) However, considering these regions where the populations have unlimited resources, enriches the problems, and makes it more realistic, see
[
29
]
,
[
12
]
or
[
13
]
. Let us also mention that recently,
[
2
]
,
[
32
]
have introduced a different approach in the analysis of interface problems allowing certain asymmetry in the equations by assuming different coefficients
\lambda_{i}
. This fact enriches the models, making them more realistic, and at the same time, makes the analysis more complex.

Organization of the paper.
In Section
2

2.
Auxiliary scalar problems

Consider the domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
which is regular and bounded, such that

\partial D=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2},

where
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are disjoint. Figure
1
could be a particular
case of such a configuration with
D=\Omega_{2}
,
\Gamma\equiv\Gamma_{1}
and
\Omega_{1}=\emptyset
.
Note that it is possible that some
\Gamma_{i}=\emptyset
. On the boundary, we might impose different boundary conditions such as Robin-type boundary condition of the form

(2.1)

{\mathcal{B}}\varphi:={\partial_{\bf n}}\varphi+g_{i}\varphi\quad\text{on $\Gamma_{i}$,}

or mixed Robin-Dirichlet (or Neumann-Dirichlet) conditions:

(2.2)

{\mathcal{B}}\varphi:=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &{\partial_{\bf n}}\varphi+g_{1}\varphi\quad&\text{on $\Gamma_{1}$,}\\
&\varphi&\text{on $\Gamma_{2}$,}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
{\bf n}
is a nowhere tangent vector field,
g_{i}\in C(\Gamma_{i})
is nonnegative, for both types of boundary conditions.
For
c\in L^{\infty}(D)
we define the general scalar eigenvalue problem

(2.3)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta+c(x))\varphi=\lambda m(x)\varphi,&&\text{in}\quad D,\\
&{\mathcal{B}}\varphi=0,&&\text{on}\quad\partial D,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

and denote its principal eigenvalue, which depends on the boundary conditions, as

\lambda_{m}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}],\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda_{m}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{N}+g_{1};\mathcal{N}+g_{2}],\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda_{m}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{N}+g_{1};\mathcal{D}].

Recall that the principal eigenvalue is the only eigenvalue
\lambda
for which there is a positive eigenfunction
\varphi
verifying (
2.3
).

Problem (
2.3
) has been widely studied imposing different conditions on
m
and different boundary conditions, see, for instance,
[
11
]
,
[
26
]
, or
[
24
]
. The following holds:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
c,m\in L^{\infty}(D)
be such that
m
is a strictly positive function. Then, Problem (
2.3
) has a unique positive principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{m}:=\lambda_{m}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}]
corresponding to the principal eigenfunction
\varphi\in H^{2}(D)
, under such Robin boundary conditions (
2.2
) and satisfying
\varphi(x)>0
when
x\in D\cup\Gamma_{1}
and
\partial_{\bf\nu}\varphi(x)<0
if
x\in\Gamma_{2}
.

In the next proposition, we collect some properties concerning the principal eigenvalue (see for example
[
11
]
for further details about their proofs),
\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}]
, of (
2.3
) when no weight is considered, that is
m\equiv 1
. We will use these properties in the sequel.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}]
be the principal eigenvalue of (
2.3
).
Then

1)

The map
c\in L^{\infty}(D)\mapsto\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}]
is continuous and increasing.

2)

It holds that

\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c;\mathcal{N}+{g_{1}};\mathcal{N}+{g_{2}}]<\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c;\mathcal{N}+g_{1};\mathcal{D}].

3)

Let
D_{0}
be a proper subdomain of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
of
D
. Then,

\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}]<\lambda_{1}^{D_{0}}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}].

4)

If there exists a regular, positive
\overline{u}
such that

-\Delta\overline{u}+c(x)\overline{u}\geq 0\quad\text{in}\quad D,\quad\mathcal{B}(\overline{u})\geq 0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial D,

and some of these inequalities is strict, then

\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}]>0.

Remark 2.3
.

It is straightforward that for every
s\in\mathbb{R}

\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x)+s;\mathcal{B}]=\lambda_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}]+s,

a property that we will use in the sequel.

Observe that for the general eigenvalue problem (
2.3
)
there is an equivalent formulation in terms of the problem

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta+c(x)-\lambda m(x))\varphi=\sigma\varphi,&&\text{in}\quad D,\\
&{\mathcal{B}}\varphi=0,&&\text{on}\quad\partial D,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

so that the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{m}^{D}
of (
2.3
) is the one that corresponds to the eigenvalue
\sigma_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x)-\lambda m;\mathcal{B}]=0
in (
2.4
).
In other words, characterizing
\lambda_{m}^{D}
is equivalent to finding zeros for the function

\sigma(\lambda)=\sigma_{1}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x)-\lambda m;\mathcal{B}],\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
11
]
,
[
24
]
)
.

Let
c\in L^{\infty}(D)
and let
m\in L^{\infty}(D)
be a strictly positive function. Then, the map
\lambda\mapsto\sigma(\lambda)
defined above is differentiable, decreasing, and concave. Moreover,

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\sigma(\lambda)=-\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\lambda\to-\infty}\sigma(\lambda)=\infty.

Observe that this theorem implies that (
2.3
) has a principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{m}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{B}]
, which is unique.
The positivity of the eigenvalue is a direct consequence of
[
6
, Theorem 12.1]
, and this unique positive eigenvalue (the principal eigenvalue) is associated with a positive eigenfunction (the principal eigenfunction), which is unique up to a multiplicative constant.

Finally, we analyse a non-linear problem which will be pivotal in the final section of this paper. Let us consider the set
D_{0}
so that
\overline{D}_{0}\subset D
and the problem

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta+c(x))u=\lambda m(x)u-a(x)u^{p}\quad&&\text{in }D,\\
&{\partial_{\bf n}}u+g_{1}u=C\quad&&\text{on $\Gamma_{1}$,}\\
&u=0&&\text{on $\Gamma_{2}$,}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where
a(x)=0
if

and

in

. The constant

is assumed to be non-negative.
Then, we state the next result whose proof can be obtained by adapting the results obtained in
[
21
]
for such problems as (

), though with non-homogeneous boundary conditions.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
c\in L^{\infty}(D)
. Problem (
2.5
) has a unique positive solution
u\in C^{2,\eta}(D)
if and only if

\lambda_{m}^{D}[-\Delta+c(x),\mathcal{N}+g_{1};\mathcal{D}]<\lambda<\lambda_{m}^{D_{0}}[-\Delta+c(x);\mathcal{D}].

Furthermore, for the nonlinear scalar problem (
2.5
)
we say that
\overline{u}
is a supersolution
if
\overline{u}\in C^{2}(D)\cap C^{1}(\overline{D})
and

(2.6)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta+c(x))\overline{u}\geq\lambda m(x)\overline{u}-a(x)\overline{u}^{p}\quad&&\text{in }D,\\
&{\partial_{\bf n}}\overline{u}+g_{1}\overline{u}-C\geq 0\quad&&\text{on $\Gamma_{1}$,}\\
&\overline{u}\geq 0&&\text{on $\Gamma_{2}$,}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Equivalently, we define a subsolution with the reverse inequalities
\leq
. The next lemma provides a comparison result for sub/and super solutions in

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\underline{u},\overline{u}\in C^{2}(\overline{D}\setminus\overline{D}_{0})
be a pair of sub and super solutions in

as defined by (

\limsup_{{\rm dist}(x,\partial D_{0})\to 0}(\overline{u}-\underline{u})\geq 0,

\overline{u}\geq\underline{u}
in
{D}\setminus\overline{D}_{0}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on an argument shown in
[
20
]
, see also
[
34
]
. Let us argue by contradiction considering for
\epsilon_{1}>\epsilon_{2}>0
the set

D_{+}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})=\{x\in D\setminus\overline{D}_{0}\,:\,\underline{u}(x)+\epsilon_{2}>\overline{u}(x)+\epsilon_{1}\}.

Define also the functions

\underline{w},\overline{w}\in H^{1}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{0})\cap L^{\infty}(D\setminus\overline{D}_{0})
, as

\overline{w}=(\overline{u}+\epsilon_{1})^{-1}\left[(\underline{u}+\epsilon_{2})^{2}-(\overline{u}+\epsilon_{1})^{2}\right]^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{w}=(\underline{u}+\epsilon_{2})^{-1}\left[(\underline{u}+\epsilon_{2})^{2}-(\overline{u}+\epsilon_{1})^{2}\right]^{+},

where
f^{+}=\max\{f,0\}
.

First take two nonnegative test functions
\overline{v},\underline{v}\in C^{2}({D}\setminus\overline{D}_{0})
, vanishing near
\partial D_{0}
and
\Gamma_{2}
. Now, multiplying the inequalities in (
2.6
) for sub and supersolution by
\overline{v}
and
\underline{v}
, respectively, integrating by parts in
D\setminus\overline{D}_{0}
and subtracting both expressions, we arrive at

(2.7)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus\overline{D}_{0}}\left(\nabla\underline{u}\nabla\underline{v}-\nabla\overline{u}\nabla\overline{v}+c(x)(\underline{v}\underline{u}-\overline{v}\overline{u})\right)-\int_{\Gamma_{1}}g_{1}(x)(\underline{v}\underline{u}-\overline{v}\overline{u})-C\int_{\Gamma_{1}}(\overline{v}-\underline{v})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{D\setminus\overline{D}_{0}}a(x)(\underline{u}^{p}\underline{v}-\overline{u}^{p}\overline{v})+\lambda\int_{D\setminus\overline{D}_{0}}m(x)(\overline{u}\overline{v}-\underline{u}\underline{v}).

Now, using a regularizing argument we can approximate
\underline{w}
and
\overline{w}
in the the
H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
norm on
D\setminus\overline{D}_{0}

by
C^{2}
continuous vanishing near
D_{0}
and
\Gamma_{2}
and we can replace
\underline{w}
and
\overline{w}

in the expression (
2.7
). Next we analyse now each of the terms in the expression (
2.7
).

First observe that, due to their definition,
\overline{w},\underline{w}\equiv 0
outside the set
D_{+}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
and
\overline{w}>\underline{w}
in
D_{+}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})
. Hence,

-\int_{D\setminus\overline{D}_{0}}(\nabla\underline{u}\nabla\underline{w}-\nabla\overline{u}\nabla\overline{w})=-\int_{D_{+}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})}\left(\left|\nabla\underline{u}-\frac{\underline{u}+\epsilon_{2}}{\overline{u}+\epsilon_{1}}\nabla\overline{u}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\overline{u}-\frac{\overline{u}+\epsilon_{1}}{\underline{u}+\epsilon_{2}}\nabla\underline{u}\right|^{2}\right)\leq 0,

and moreover

-C\int_{\Gamma_{1}}(\overline{w}-\underline{w})\leq 0.

On the other hand, since
0<\epsilon_{2}<\epsilon_{1}
, when
\epsilon_{1}\to 0
it is straightforward to see that the terms

-\int_{D_{+}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})}c(x)(\underline{w}\underline{u}-\overline{w}\overline{u}),\quad-\int_{\Gamma_{1}}g_{1}(x)(\underline{w}\underline{u}-\overline{w}\overline{u}),\quad\lambda\int_{D_{+}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})}m(x)(\overline{u}\overline{w}-\underline{u}\underline{w}).

converge to
0
and that

\int_{D_{+}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})}a(x)(\underline{u}^{p}\underline{w}-\overline{u}^{p}\overline{w})\to\int_{D_{+}(0,0)}a(x)(\underline{u}^{p-1}-\overline{u}^{p-1})(\underline{u}^{2}-\overline{u}^{2}).

This last integral is strictly positive, unless the set
D_{+}(0,0)
is empty, in which case it vanishes. Hence, gathering all the terms we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{D_{+}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})}\left(\left|\nabla\underline{u}-\frac{\underline{u}+\epsilon_{2}}{\overline{u}+\epsilon_{1}}\nabla\overline{u}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\overline{u}-\frac{\overline{u}+\epsilon_{1}}{\underline{u}+\epsilon_{2}}\nabla\underline{u}\right|^{2}\right)-C\int_{\Gamma_{1}}(\overline{w}-\underline{w})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{D_{+}(0,0)}a(x)(\underline{u}^{p-1}-\overline{u}^{p-1})(\underline{u}^{2}-\overline{u}^{2})>0,

which is a contradiction. Consequently,

in

.

3.
Interface problems

In this section, we gather several preliminary results in relation to interface problems. However, even though the aim of this paper is to analyze interface nonlinear problems of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u_{i}=\lambda m_{i}(x)u_{i}-a_{i}(x)u_{i}^{p},&x\in\Omega_{i},\\
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{u})=0,&x\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

an important part of the analysis is based on studying the linear and eigenvalue counterpart problems, for which we will show several important results in this section.
To do so, we first fix the notation that we will use throughout the following sections. First note that when no confusion arises, we will omit
i=1,2
and write the problem as above,
or equivalently

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\mathbf{u}=\lambda{\mathbf{mu}}-{\bf au}^{p},&x\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{u})=0,&x\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the boundary conditions
\mathcal{I}({\bf u})=0
stands for

(3.2)

\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{1}}}=\gamma_{1}(u_{2}-u_{1})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{2}}}=\gamma_{2}(u_{1}-u_{2})\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma,\quad\text{and}\quad u_{2}=0\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.

Here
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
with each

defined in
\Omega_{i}
and

. Similarly for

and

.

3.1.
Interface linear problems

Let us consider the asymmetric linear interface problem

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta+{\bf c}(x)){\bf u}={\bf f}(x),&\text{in}\ \Omega\\
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{u})=0,&\text{on}\ \Gamma\cup\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

such that
{\bf c}(x)=(c_{1}(x),c_{2}(x))\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
and
{\bf f}(x)=(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x))\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
.
Problem (
3.3
) with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition on the outside boundary
\partial\Omega
,
i.e.,
\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\bm{\nu}}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, has been studied in
[
33
]
and
[
38
]
.

The bilinear form associated with (
3.3
) is
a:\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and defined by

(3.4)

a({\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega_{i}}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}+c_{i}(x)u_{i}v_{i}\right)+\int_{\Gamma}(u_{2}-u_{1})(\gamma_{2}v_{2}-\gamma_{1}v_{1}).

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
{\bf{u}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is a weak solution of (
3.3
) if

a({\bf{u}},{\bf{v}})=\langle{\bf{f}},{\bf{v}}\rangle_{\mathcal{L}^{2}},

for all
{\bf{v}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
{\bf{u}}
is a classical solution of (
3.3
) if
u_{i}\in C^{2}(\Omega_{i})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}_{i})
and

satisfies (
3.3
) pointwise.

We will also use the notation
\mathcal{I}({\bf u})\succeq 0
to denote

(3.5)

\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{1}}}\geq\gamma_{1}(u_{2}-u_{1}),\quad\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{2}}}\leq\gamma_{2}(u_{2}-u_{1})\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma,\quad\text{and}\quad u_{2}\geq 0\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,

and
\mathcal{I}({\bf u})\preceq 0
for the reverse inequalities.

Definition 3.3
.

We say that
\overline{\bf{u}}
is a supersolution of (
3.3
) if
\overline{u}_{i}\in C^{2}(\Omega_{i})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}_{i})
and

(-\Delta+{\bf c}(x)){\bf{\overline{u}}}\geq{\bf f}\ \quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\qquad\mathcal{I}({\bf{\overline{u}}})\succeq 0\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma\cup\partial\Omega.

If moreover, any of these inequalities is strict, then we say that
{\bf{u}}
is a strict supersolution.

A subsolution
\underline{\bf{u}}
is defined analogously by taking
\leq
and
\preceq
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
{\bf c}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
,
{\bf c}\geq c_{0}>0
and
{\bf f}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
. Then there exists a unique weak solution
{\bf u}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
of
(
3.3
)
. Moreover, by elliptic regularity, if

{\bf c},{\bf f}\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}
,
\eta\in(0,1)
then there exists a unique classical solution
{\bf u}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\eta}
of
(
3.3
)
.

Proof.

The proof can be adapted straightforwardly from
[
33
]
,
[
38
]
and
[
1
]
.
∎

The first result provides us with a maximum principle.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
38
]
)
.

Let
{\bf u}
be a classical supersolution such that

(-\Delta+{\bf c}(x)){\bf u}\geq{\bf 0},\quad\text{in}\ \Omega\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{I}({\bf{\overline{u}}})\succeq 0,\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma\cup\partial\Omega.

Then, the following holds:

1)

If
\bf{u}\geq 0
in
\Omega
, then, either
\bf{u}>0
or
\bf{u}\equiv 0
in
\Omega
.

2)

If
\bf{c}\geq 0,

\bf{c}\neq 0
in
\Omega
, then,
\bf{u}>0
in
\Omega
.

3.2.
Interface asymmetric eigenvalue problems

In our context, the interface counterpart of (
2.3
) is

(3.6)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta+{\bf c(x)}){\bf\Phi}=\lambda{\bf m(x)\Phi}\quad&&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
&\mathcal{I}({\bf\Phi})=0\quad&&\text{on}\ \Gamma\cup\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

This problem was addressed in
[
1
]
under symmetric interface conditions. In
[
33
]
and
[
38
]
the authors consider Neumann boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
and
{\bf m}
, eventually, changing sign. As before, first of all we state the basic results for the interface eigenvalue problem when no weight is considered:

(3.7)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta+{\bf c}(x)){\bf\Phi}=\lambda{\bf\Phi}\quad&&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
&\mathcal{I}({\bf\Phi})=0\quad&&\text{on}\ \Gamma\cup\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

The proofs of these results are similar to the ones that can be found in
[
1
]
,
[
33
]
, and
[
38
]
and we omit the details.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
{\bf c}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty},
respectively
{\bf c}\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}
, with
\eta\in(0,1)
. Then, Problem
(
3.7
)
has
a principal eigenvalue
\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[\Delta+{\bf c},\mathcal{I}]
, which is real and simple. Moreover, the associated eigenfunction
{\bf\Phi}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,p},p>1
, respectively
{\bf\Phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\eta}
, is strictly positive in
\Omega
.

The next result provides us with a characterization of the strong maximum principle, equivalent to the result given
[
30
]
but particularised to the interface system.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}]
be the principal eigenvalue of (
3.7
). The following are equivalent:

1)

\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}]>0.

2)

There exists a strict positive supersolution
\bf{\overline{u}}
of (
3.3
) with
{\bf f}\equiv 0
.

3)

Given
{\bf f}\in\mathcal{C}^{1},{\bf f}\geq 0,{\bf f}\neq 0
in
\Omega
, there exists a unique positive solution
\bf{u}
of (
3.3
).

The principal eigenvalue of (
3.7
) satisfies the following result related to monotonicity and convergence similar to Lemma
2.2
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}]
be the principal eigenvalue of (
3.7
).

1)

The map
{\bf c}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}\mapsto\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}]
is continuous and increasing.

2)

Suppose that
{\bf c}_{n}\rightarrow\bf{c}
in
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, then
\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c_{n}};\mathcal{I}]\rightarrow\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}].

3)

It holds that

\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c},\mathcal{I}]<\min\{\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{1}}[-\Delta+c_{1};\mathcal{N}+\gamma_{1}],\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{2}}[-\Delta+c_{2};\mathcal{N}+\gamma_{2},\mathcal{D}]\}.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
be a positive eigenfunction associated to
\Lambda_{1}:=\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}]
.

1) Assume that
{\bf c}\leq{\bf d}
. Then it is straigthforward to see that

-\Delta\bm{\Phi}+({\bf d}-\Lambda_{1})\bm{\Phi}=({\bf d}-{\bf c})\bm{\Phi}\geq 0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,

and

\mathcal{I}(\bm{\Phi})=0\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma\cup\partial\Omega.

Hence,
\bm{\Phi}
is a positive supersolution of
(-\Delta+{\bf d}-\Lambda_{1};\mathcal{I}
) and therefore, due to Proposition
3.7
,

\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf d}-\Lambda_{1}]=\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf d}]-\Lambda_{1}\geq 0.

2) Since
{\bf c}_{n}\rightarrow\bf{c}
in
\Omega
, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\bf c}-\epsilon\leq{\bf c}_{n}\leq{\bf c}+\epsilon
, for
n\geq N
. Applying the previous item we obtain

\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+({\bf c}-\epsilon)]\leq\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c}]\leq\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+({\bf c}+\epsilon)],

which by Remark
2.3
implies

\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c}]-\epsilon\leq\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c}]\leq\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c}]+\epsilon,

and hence the convergence.

3) Consider the scalar problem

(3.8)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &&-\Delta\phi+(c_{1}-\Lambda_{1})\phi=0\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega_{1},\\
&&\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\bm{\nu_{1}}}+\gamma_{1}\phi=0\quad&\text{on}\ \Gamma.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

It is clear that
\phi_{1}
is a positive supersolution to (
3.8
), since it verifies

\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{1}}}+\gamma_{1}\phi_{1}=\gamma_{1}\phi_{2}>0,\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma.

Hence, by Proposition
2.2
we get
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{1}}[-\Delta+c_{1}-\Lambda_{1};N+\gamma_{1}]>0,
or equivalently

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{1}}[-\Delta+c_{1};N+\gamma_{1}]>\Lambda_{1}.

The proof for
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{2}}[-\Delta+c_{2};\mathcal{N}+\gamma_{2},\mathcal{D}]
is analogous.
∎

Note that, as shown for similar problems above, the problem of analyzing the existence of
a principal eigenvalue of problem (
3.6
) is equivalent to finding zeros of the function

\Sigma(\lambda)=\Lambda_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c}-\lambda{\bf m};\mathcal{I}].

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
{\bf c}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
and let
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
be a strictly positive function. Problem (
3.6
) has a principal eigenvalue,
\Lambda_{m}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}]
. Moreover,
\Lambda_{m}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}]
is real, simple (in the sense of
multiplicities), and strictly positive with the associated eigenfunction
{\bf\Phi}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
strictly positive in
\Omega
.

Remark 3.10
.

As in Theorem
3.6
, imposing extra regularity,
{\bf c\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}}
and
{\bf m}\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}
yields
{\bf\Phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}}
; see
[
38
]
for further details.

Proof.

The proof is a direct corollary of the fact that the function
\Sigma(\lambda)
is differentiable, decreasing, concave, and satisfies

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\Sigma(\lambda)=-\infty,\quad\lim_{\lambda\to-\infty}\Sigma(\lambda)=+\infty,

see
[
33
]
. In that situation, it is clear that there is a unique
\lambda_{0}
such that
\Sigma(\lambda_{0})=0
. The positivity of
\Lambda_{m}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+{\bf c};\mathcal{I}]
follows from the maximum principle. Finally, the regularity of
{\bf\Phi}
can be shown using standards arguments of elliptic equations (cf.
[
38
]
)
∎

3.3.
Asymptotic behaviour

Given a real parameter
\alpha
, we now consider problem (
3.6
) with
{\bf c}(x)=\alpha{\bf a}(x)
. Recall that
{\bf a}
is the function that measures the crowding effect and
a_{i}=0
in
\Omega_{0,i}
. We study the limit behaviour, as
\alpha
grows to infinity, of the linear weighted elliptic eigenvalue problem

(3.9)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta+\alpha{\bf a}){\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}}}=\Lambda_{\alpha}{\bf m}{\bf\Phi_{\alpha}}\quad&&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
&\mathcal{I}({\bf\Phi}_{\alpha})=0\quad&&\text{on}\ \Gamma\cup\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

This analysis will be used later in this paper to establish the existence of positive solutions for the nonlinear interface problem (
3.1
).

For a fixed
\alpha
we will denote by
\Lambda_{\alpha}
the principal eigenvalue and
{\bf\Phi}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
the corresponding principal eigenfunction of (
3.9
), or just
(\Lambda_{\alpha},{\bf\Phi}_{\alpha})
. Recall, thanks to Theorem
3.9
, that since
\phi_{i,\alpha}\geq 0
we can conclude that
\Lambda_{\alpha}\geq 0
. Moreover, since the principal eigenfunction
{\bf\Phi}_{\alpha}
is unique up to a multiplicative constant, we can assume that it is normalised, so that

(3.10)

\int_{\Omega_{1}}m_{1}(x)\varphi_{1,\alpha}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}m_{2}(x)\varphi_{2,\alpha}^{2}=1.

Under these assumptions in the sequel we will prove that there is a limit problem with solution
(\lambda_{\infty},{\bf\Phi}_{\infty})
, such that
(\Lambda_{\alpha},{\bf\Phi}_{\alpha})
converges to it as
\alpha\to\infty
.
However, we first need to proof some bounds.

Lemma 3.11
.

For each fixed
\alpha>0
, let
(\Lambda_{\alpha},\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}})
be a solution to
(
3.9
)
. Then,

\|{\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}}\leq\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\Lambda_{\alpha},\quad\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,\alpha}-\varphi_{1,\alpha})^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}\Lambda_{\alpha},\quad\alpha\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}a_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\Lambda_{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
{\bf\Psi}_{\alpha}=(\gamma_{2}\varphi_{1,\alpha},\gamma_{1}\varphi_{2,\alpha})
. Multiplying (
3.9
) by
{\bf\Psi}_{\alpha}
and integrating by parts
we get, for the left-hand side, since
\gamma_{2}\geq\gamma_{1}
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{1,\alpha}\right|^{2}-\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi_{1,\alpha}\frac{\partial\varphi_{1,\alpha}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{1}}}+{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{2,\alpha}\right|^{2}-{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi_{2,\alpha}\frac{\partial\varphi_{2,\alpha}}{\partial\bm{\nu_{2}}}

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(\gamma_{2}\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}^{2}+{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}a_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{1,\alpha}\right|^{2}+{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{2,\alpha}\right|^{2}+\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,\alpha}-\varphi_{1,\alpha})^{2}

\displaystyle+\alpha\left(\gamma_{2}\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}^{2}+\gamma_{1}\int_{\Omega_{2}}a_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{1}\|{\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}}+\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,\alpha}-\varphi_{1,\alpha})^{2}+\alpha\gamma_{1}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}a_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha}^{2}\right).

For the right hand, we get

\Lambda_{\alpha}\left(\gamma_{2}\int_{\Omega_{1}}m_{1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}^{2}+{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}m_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha}^{2}\right)\leq\gamma_{2}\Lambda_{\alpha}.

Thus,

\|{\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}}}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}}+\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,\alpha}-\varphi_{1,\alpha})^{2}+\alpha\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}a_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\Lambda_{\alpha}.

and we obtain the desired bounds.
∎

Next, we prove that there exists a limit for the sequence of eigenvalues as
\alpha
tends to
\infty
.

Lemma 3.12
.

For each fixed
\alpha>0
, let
(\Lambda_{\alpha},\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}})
be a solution to
(
3.9
)
. Then, there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\Lambda_{\alpha}=\ell.

Proof.

First, if
\Lambda_{\alpha}
is the eigenvalue of (
3.9
) with eigenfunction
{\bf\Phi}_{\alpha}
, then

(3.11)

\Sigma_{1}^{\Omega}[-\Delta+\alpha{\bf a}-\Lambda_{\alpha}{\bf m};\mathcal{I}]=0.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing unbounded sequence and let
\Lambda_{n}
be the corresponding principal eigenvalue of (
3.9
) and
{\bf\Phi}_{n}
the principal eigenfunction.

To find a bound for
\Lambda_{n}
we use the monotonicity properties of the principal eigenvalue shown in
[
11
]
and
[
33
]
, see also Proposition
2.2
. Observe that
\varphi_{1,n}
is a strict supersolution to the uncoupled problem

(-\Delta-\Lambda_{\alpha}m_{1}+\alpha a_{1})\varphi_{1,n}=0,\quad\text{in $\Omega_{1}$},\qquad\partial_{{\bf\nu}_{1}}\varphi_{1,n}+\gamma_{1}\varphi_{1,n}=0,\quad\text{in $\Gamma$}.

Hence, the principal eigenvalue verifies

\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{1}}[-\Delta-\Lambda_{\alpha}m_{1}+\alpha a_{1};\mathcal{N}+\gamma_{1}]>0.

Now, due to the monotonicity with respect boundary data and the domain we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{1}}[-\Delta-\Lambda_{\alpha}m_{1}+\alpha a_{1};\mathcal{N}+\gamma_{1}]<\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{1}}[-\Delta-\Lambda_{\alpha}m_{1}+\alpha a_{1};\mathcal{D}]

\displaystyle<\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{0,1}}[-\Delta-\Lambda_{\alpha}m_{1}]\leq\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{0,1}}[-\Delta;\mathcal{D}]-\Lambda_{\alpha}\min_{x\in\Omega_{1}}m_{1}(x).

Analogously we obtain that

0<\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{2}}[-\Delta-\Lambda_{\alpha}m_{2}+\alpha a_{2};\mathcal{N}+\gamma_{2};\mathcal{D}]<\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{0,2}}[-\Delta;\mathcal{D}]-\Lambda_{\alpha}\min_{x\in\Omega_{2}}m_{2}(x).

Hence, we conclude that
\Lambda_{\alpha}
is bounded form above by

(3.12)

\Lambda_{\alpha}<\min\left\{\frac{\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{0,1}}[-\Delta;\mathcal{D}]}{\min_{x\in\Omega_{1}}m_{1}(x)};\frac{\sigma_{1}^{\Omega_{0,2}}[-\Delta;\mathcal{D}]}{\min_{x\in\Omega_{2}}m_{2}(x)}\right\}

Finally, notice that thanks to the monotonicity of the principal eigenvalues with respect to the potential, Proposition
3.8
, we know that the eigenvalues
\Lambda_{\alpha}
are increasing in terms of the parameter
\alpha
. Hence, being increasing and bounded from above, we get that there exists
\ell<\infty
such that

\Lambda_{\alpha_{n}}\to\ell
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

For each fixed
\alpha>0
, let
(\Lambda_{\alpha},\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}})
be a solution to
(
3.9
)
. Then

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{i}}a_{i}\varphi_{i,\alpha}^{2}=0.

Proof.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing unbounded sequence and
{\bf\Phi}_{\alpha_{n}}:={\bf\Phi}_{n}=(\varphi_{1,n},\varphi_{2,n})
. It is clear from Lemmas
3.11
and
3.12
that, as
n\to\infty
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{1}\varphi_{1,n}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}a_{2}\varphi_{2,n}^{2}\right)=0.

Moreover, since the sets
\Omega_{i}
are disjoint, we can state the result.

∎

Having introduced the above, we can state the following convergence theorem.

Lemma 3.14
.

For each fixed
\alpha>0
, let
(\Lambda_{\alpha},\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}})
be a solution to
(
3.9
)
. Then, there exists
\Phi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
, with
\Phi\equiv 0
in
\Omega\setminus(\Omega_{0,1}\cup\Omega_{0,2})
and such that

{\bf\Phi}_{\alpha}\to\Phi,\quad\text{in}\quad\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1},

as
\alpha\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing unbounded sequence. Take, for each
\alpha_{n}
the eigenfunction
{\bf\Phi}_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
of (
3.9
) verifying the normalization (
3.10
), with
\Lambda_{n}
standing for the principal eigenvalue. Note that the norms for the eigenfunctions are bounded in
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
due to Lemma
3.11
. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is compactly embedded in
\mathcal{L}^{2}
, there exist subsequences
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{{\bf\Phi}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and a function
{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\bf\Phi}_{n}-{\Phi}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}=0,

and weakly in
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
.

First we proof that the limit function
\Phi
vanishes outside the domains
\Omega_{0,i}
, that is

(3.13)

\phi_{i}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0,i}.

Indeed, applying Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{i}}a_{i}\varphi_{i,n}^{2}-\int_{\Omega_{i}}a_{i}\phi_{i}^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{i}}a_{i}|\varphi_{i,n}+\phi_{i}|\left|\varphi_{i,n}-\phi_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\overline{\Omega}_{i}}a_{i}\cdot\|\varphi_{i,n}-\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\|\varphi_{i,n}+\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi_{i,n}-\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

where
C>0
. Hence, we conclude, thanks to Lemma
3.13
, that

\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{1}\phi_{1}^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}a_{2}\phi_{2}^{2}=0,

which completes the proof of (
3.13
), since
a_{i}
are nonnegative
\Omega_{i}
and identically zero in
\Omega_{0,i}
.

The next step is to prove that
\{{\bf\Phi}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is actually a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
which allows us to conclude that
\Phi\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}
and

(3.14)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\bf\Phi}_{n}-{\Phi}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{1}}=0.

Consider
n<m
such that
0<\alpha_{n}<\alpha_{m}
. For
{\bf\Phi}_{n}
and
{\bf\Phi}_{m}
we claim that

D_{n,m}:=\|\nabla({\bf\Phi}_{n}-{\bf\Phi}_{m})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left|\nabla(\varphi_{1,n}-\varphi_{1,m})\right|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left|\nabla(\varphi_{2,n}-\varphi_{2,m})\right|^{2}\to 0,

as
n,m\to\infty
. To show the claim, we consider the terms

D_{n,m}^{1}:=\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left|\nabla(\varphi_{1,n}-\varphi_{1,m})\right|^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad D_{n,m}^{2}:=\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left|\nabla(\varphi_{2,n}-\varphi_{2,m})\right|^{2}.

Since
\gamma_{2}\geq\gamma_{1}
, we get that

\displaystyle D_{n,m}^{1}\leq\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}D_{n,m}^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{1,n}\right|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{1,m}\right|^{2}-2\int_{\Omega_{1}}\langle\nabla\varphi_{1,n},\nabla\varphi_{1,m}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}(\lambda_{n}m_{1}\varphi_{1,n}-\alpha_{n}a_{1}\varphi_{1,n})\varphi_{1,n}+\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,n}-\varphi_{1,n})\varphi_{1,n}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}(\lambda_{m}m_{1}\varphi_{1,m}-\alpha_{m}a_{1}\varphi_{1,m})\varphi_{1,m}+\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,m}-\varphi_{1,m})\varphi_{1,m}

\displaystyle-2\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}(\lambda_{n}m_{1}\varphi_{1,n}-\alpha_{n}a_{1}\varphi_{1,n})\varphi_{1,m}-2\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,n}-\varphi_{1,n})\varphi_{1,m},

and

\displaystyle D_{n,m}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}}\right|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}}\right|^{2}-2\int_{\Omega_{2}}\langle\nabla\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}},\nabla\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\lambda_{\alpha_{n}}m_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}}-\alpha_{n}a_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}})\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}}-\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}}-\varphi_{1,\alpha_{n}})\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\lambda_{\alpha_{m}}m_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}}-\alpha_{m}a_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}})\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}}-\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}}-\varphi_{1,\alpha_{m}})\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}}

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega_{2}}(\lambda_{\alpha_{n}}m_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}}-\alpha_{n}a_{2}\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}})\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}}+2\gamma_{2}\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,\alpha_{n}}-\varphi_{1,\alpha_{n}})\varphi_{2,\alpha_{m}}.

By rearranging terms we get

(3.15)

\displaystyle D_{n,m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left[m_{1}\lambda_{n}(\varphi_{1,n}-\varphi_{1,m})^{2}+m_{1}(\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n})\varphi^{2}_{1,m}\right]

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{2}}\left[m_{2}\lambda_{n}(\varphi_{2,n}-\varphi_{2,m})^{2}+m_{2}(\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n})\varphi^{2}_{2,m}\right]+A_{n,m}+G_{n,m},

where

\displaystyle A_{n,m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}a_{1}\left[\alpha_{n}(\varphi_{1,n}-\varphi_{1,m})^{2}+(\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{n})\varphi_{1,m}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{2}}a_{2}\left[\alpha_{n}(\varphi_{2,n}-\varphi_{2,m})^{2}+(\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{n})\varphi_{2,m}^{2}\right],

and

\displaystyle G_{n,m}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2}\left(\int_{\Gamma}(\varphi_{2,n}-\varphi_{1,n})\varphi_{1,n}+(\varphi_{2,m}-\varphi_{1,m})\varphi_{1,m}-2(\varphi_{2,n}-\varphi_{1,n})\varphi_{1,_{m}}\right)

Notice that, since

To state the main result of this section, namely the characterization of the limit problem for (

under homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. Recall that due to Lemma

or

and consider the problems

(3.18)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta\varphi_{i,\infty}&=\lambda_{\infty}m_{i}(x)\varphi_{i,\infty},\ &&\text{in}\ \Omega_{0,i},\\
\varphi_{i,\infty}&=0,\quad&&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega_{0,i}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Theorem 3.15
.

For each fixed
\alpha>0
, let
(\Lambda_{\alpha},\bf{\Phi_{\alpha}})
be a solution to
(
3.9
)

(
3.9
)
converges to Problem

)
when

\infty

where
{\bf\Phi}_{\infty}=(\varphi_{1,\infty},\varphi_{2,\infty})
is the solution of (

).

Let us comment several possible situations concerning the limit problem depending on the geometrical configuration of the vanishing subdomains
\Omega_{0,i}
.
It is clear that if

\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{m_{1}}=\lambda_{m_{2}},

then, (
3.18
) corresponds to the system (

) and we get for two positive uncoupled eigenfunctions that concentrate on the corresponding subdomains

On the other hand, assume, without loss of generality, that

\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{m_{1}}<\lambda_{m_{2}},

which might happen if, for instance,
\Omega_{0,2}
is smaller than

and

In this situation,

is not the principal eigenvalue for

and, since

. Therefore, the limit eigenfunction concentrates on
\Omega_{0,1}
, being zero in the rest of the domain

. Hence, we have

{\bf\Phi}_{\infty}=(\varphi_{1,\infty},\varphi_{2,\infty}):=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &(\varphi_{1,\infty},\varphi_{2,\infty})\quad&\text{if}\quad\lambda_{m_{1}}=\lambda_{m_{2}},\\
&(\varphi_{1,\infty},0)\quad&\text{if}\quad\lambda_{m_{1}}<\lambda_{m_{2}},\\
&(0,\varphi_{2,\infty})\quad&\text{if}\quad\lambda_{m_{2}}<\lambda_{m_{1}}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
3.15
.

First we show, as a consequence of Lemma
3.14
, that the limit function
\Phi
is such that

(3.22)

{\Phi}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{0,1})\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{0,2}).

Subsequently, for a sufficiently small
\delta>0
consider the open sets

\Omega_{\delta,i}:=\{x\in\Omega_{i}:\text{dist}(x,\Omega_{0,i})<\delta\}.

so that, according to (

{\Phi}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta,1})\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\delta,2})
and hence there exists

such that

On the other hand, since the sets

are smooth subdomains of

, they are stable in the sense of Babuska and Výborný, see
[

for further details, and, therefore,

Hence (

Finally, passing to the limit in the weak formulation of (

), we show that
{\Phi}
is a weak solution of the problem (

). For this, we consider the test function

Thus, multiplying (

with

Hence

where

and by uniqueness of the first eigenvalue,

. Furthermore, by elliptic regularity

∎

Remark 4.4

5.
Asymptotic behaviour of the solution

In this section we analyse the asymptotic behaviour of the solution
{\bf u}_{\lambda}
when the parameter
\lambda
is in the interval
(\lambda_{*},\lambda_{\infty})
but approximates to
\lambda_{\infty}
. Recall that
\lambda_{*}=\Lambda_{m}^{\Omega}[-\Delta;\mathcal{I}]
, see Theorem
4.2
and that
\lambda_{m_{i}}:=\lambda_{m_{i}}^{\Omega_{0,i}}[-\Delta;\mathcal{D}]
, see (
3.16
).
We will assume that
\lambda_{\infty}:=\lambda_{m_{1}}<\lambda_{m_{2}}
, being the analysis of the case
\lambda_{m_{2}}<\lambda_{m_{1}}
similar. In particular we will show that positive solutions have non-simultaneous blow-up when
\lambda
approaches
\lambda_{\infty}
, in the sense that the component

tends to infinity inside
\overline{\Omega}_{0,1}
, while

is bounded in the whole
\Omega_{2}
, where that compoment is defined. Observe that when
\lambda_{\infty}=\lambda_{m_{1}}=\lambda_{m_{2}}
both components blow-up.
First of all, we prove a priori bounds for the solution
{\bf u}_{\lambda}
outside the refuges, independently of the value of
\lambda_{\infty}
. Let us define, for
\epsilon>0
the sets

(5.1)

\Omega_{\epsilon,i}:=\{x\in\Omega_{i}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0,i}:\ \text{dist}(x,\overline{\Omega}_{0,i})>\epsilon\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any fixed
\lambda>\lambda_{*}
, let
{\bf u_{\lambda}}
be the unique positive solution of problem

(5.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u_{i}=\lambda m_{i}(x)u_{i}-a_{i}(x)u_{i}^{p},&x\in\Omega_{\epsilon,i},\\

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{u})=0,&x\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

with

.
Then

\|u_{i,\lambda}\|_{C(\Omega_{\epsilon,i})}\leq K,

where
K
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\Omega}_{\epsilon,i}\subset(\Omega_{i}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0,i})
we find that
\tilde{a}=\min_{x\in\Omega}\{a_{i}(x),\;i=1,2\}>0
. Then, there exists a constant
K>0
such that

0\geq\lambda m_{i}-\tilde{a}K^{p-1},\quad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega_{\epsilon,i},

and
K\geq P_{i}
on
\partial\Omega_{\epsilon,i}
, with
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{u})=0
on
\Gamma\cup\partial\Omega
. Consequently, if
K
is big enough,
(K,K)
is a supersolution to (
5.2
), concluding the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\lambda_{\infty}:=\lambda_{m_{1}}<\lambda_{m_{2}}
. For any fixed
\lambda\in(\lambda_{*},\lambda_{\infty})
, let
{\bf u_{\lambda}}
be the unique positive solution of (
3.1
)–(
3.2
). Then

u_{1,\lambda}\to\infty\ \text{uniformly on $\overline{\Omega}_{0,1}$},

as
\lambda\to\lambda_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Consider a sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}
which converges from below to
\lambda_{\infty}
as
n\to\infty
, and the corresponding unique solutions
{\bf u}_{\lambda_{n}}
, that we denote, for simplicity,
{\bf u}_{n}=(u_{1,n},u_{2,n})
. For a fixed
\lambda_{n}
, let
{\bf\Phi}_{n}=(\varphi_{1,n},\varphi_{2,n})
be the principal eigenfunction associated with the principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
of (
3.9
) and normalised so that
\|{\bf\Phi}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\leq 1
. Moreover, due to the convergence of the linear problem (
3.9
), see Lemma
3.14
and Theorem
3.15
, it follows

{\bf\Phi}_{n}\to{\bf\Phi_{\infty}}\quad\text{in}\quad H^{1}(K_{1})\times H^{1}(K_{2}),

as
n\to\infty
, where
{\bf\Phi}_{\infty}
is a solution of (
3.18
) and
K_{i}\subset\Omega_{0,i}
are two compact sets.

Now, let
{\bf v}=(v_{1},v_{2})=\alpha_{n}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}{\bf\Phi}_{n}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{I}({\bf v})=0
is satisfied. Moreover, in
\Omega_{1}
, for the first component
v_{1}
, we have

-\Delta v_{1}-\lambda_{n}m_{1}v_{1}+a_{1}v_{1}^{p}=-a_{1}\alpha_{n}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\varphi_{1,n}\left(1-\varphi_{1,n}^{p-1}\right)\leq 0.

Therefore, by comparison
u_{1,n}\geq v_{1}=\alpha_{n}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\varphi_{1,n}
. Hence, since
\varphi_{1,n}
is positive and bounded and
\alpha_{n}\to\infty
, we have that
u_{1,n}\to\infty
uniformly in
K_{1}
, see Theorem
3.15
.

Next we proof convergence up to the boundary of
\Omega_{0,1}
for the component
u_{1,n}
. To do so, we argue by contradiction following the argument of
[
20
]
.

Since
\lambda_{n}>0
for all
n\geq 1
we have that

-\Delta u_{1,n}=\lambda_{n}m_{1}u_{1,n}\geq 0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega_{0,1}.

Then, due to the maximum principle, the minimum of
u_{1,n}
is achieved at the boundary. Therefore it is enough to prove that

u_{1,n}(x_{n}):=\min_{\partial\Omega_{0,1}}u_{1,n}(x)\to\infty

as
n\to\infty
, where
x_{n}\equiv x_{\lambda_{n}}\in\partial\Omega_{0,1}
. We argue by contradiction assuming that there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
and a positive constant
k
such that

(5.3)

u_{1,n}(x_{n})\leq k\quad\text{for all}\ n\geq 1\ \text{and}\ x_{n}\in\partial\Omega_{0,1}.

Now, due to the smoothness of
\partial\Omega_{0,1}
, there exists a map
y:\partial\Omega_{0,1}\rightarrow\Omega_{0,1}
and an integer
R>0
such that for every
z\in\partial\Omega_{0,1}

(5.4)

B(y(z),R)\subset\overline{\Omega}_{0,1},\quad\overline{B}(y(z),R)\cap\partial\Omega_{0,1}=\{z\}.

Note that the map
y
provides us the centre of the balls in
\Omega_{0,1}
which satisfy (
5.4
). Observe also, that
\partial\Omega_{0,1}\subset\Omega_{1}
, and the boundaries do not touch to each other, i.e.,
\Gamma\cap\partial\Omega_{0,1}=\emptyset
. Moreover,

u_{1,n}(x)\geq u_{1,n}(x_{n})

for each
x\in\overline{B}(y(x_{n}),R)
.
Now, let us define
A(y(x_{n}),R)=B(y(x_{n}),R)\setminus\overline{B}(y(x_{n}),R/2)
and consider the problem

(5.5)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\lambda_{n}m_{1}u\quad&&\text{in}\ A(y(x_{n}),R),\\
&u=u_{1,n}(x_{n})+c_{n}\left(e^{-\delta R^{2}/4}-e^{-\delta R^{2}}\right)\quad&&\text{on}\ \partial B(y(x_{n}),R/2),\\
&u=u_{1,n}(x_{n})\quad&&\text{on}\ \partial B(y(x_{n}),R),\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where

c_{n}=\frac{\min_{\overline{B}(y(x_{n}),R/2)}u_{1,n}(x)-u_{1,n}(x_{n})}{e^{-\delta R^{2}/4}-e^{-\delta R^{2}}}.

It is clear that this choice of
c_{n}
implies that
u_{1,n}(x_{n})+c_{n}\left(e^{-\delta R^{2}/4}-e^{-\delta R^{2}}\right)\leq u_{1,n}(x)
, for all
x\in\overline{B}(y(x_{n}),R/2)
. Hence,
u_{1,n}
is a supersolution of the problem (
5.5
).
On the other hand, define for
\delta>0
and
x\in A(y(x_{n}),R)
the barrier function of exponential type

w_{n}(x):=e^{-\delta\left|x-y(x_{n})\right|^{2}}-e^{-\delta R^{2}}.

It is clear that
z_{n}(x)=u_{n}(x_{n})+c_{n}w_{n}(x)
is a subsolution of (
5.5
). Indeed,
w_{n}
can be rewritten as

w_{n}(x)=e^{-\delta\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_{i}-y_{i}(x_{n}))^{2}}-e^{-\delta R^{2}},

and consequently

\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}=-2\delta e^{-\delta\left|x-y(x_{n})\right|^{2}}(x_{i}-y_{i}(x_{n}))

and

\frac{\partial^{2}w_{n}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}=\left(-2\delta+4\delta^{2}(x_{i}-y_{i}(x_{n}))^{2}\right)e^{-\delta\left|x-y(x_{n})\right|^{2}}.

Therefore, it is easy to see that we get

(-\Delta-\lambda_{n}m_{1})w_{n}(x)=\left(2\delta N-4\delta^{2}\left|x-y(x_{n})\right|^{2}-\lambda_{n}m_{1}(x_{n})\right)e^{-\delta\left|x-y(x_{n})\right|^{2}}+\lambda m_{1}e^{-\delta R^{2}}.

Thus, for
\eta>0
, there exists
\delta>0
large enough such that

(-\Delta-\lambda m_{1})w_{n}(x)\leq-\eta<0\quad\text{in}\quad A(y(x_{n}),R),

and hence
(-\Delta-\lambda m_{1})z_{n}(x)<0
. Therefore, due to the comparison principle for (
5.5
) we can conclude

(5.6)

u_{1,n}(x)>u_{1,n}(x_{n})+c_{n}w_{n}(x)\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in\overline{A}(y(x_{n}),R).

Finally, consider a compact set
K\subset\subset\Omega_{0,1}
such that
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}B(y(x_{n}),R/2)\subset K
. Since we are assuming
u_{1,n}(x_{n})<k
for all
n
and since
u_{1,n}(x)
tends uniformly to
\infty
in
K
, we have that

(5.7)

c_{n}\to\infty,

as
n\to\infty
(or equivalently, when
\lambda_{n}
goes to
\lambda_{\infty}
). Moreover, if we set the normalised direction

\bm{\nu}_{n}:=\frac{y(x_{n})-x_{n}}{R},

then, using (
5.6
), the partial derivative in that direction yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{1,n}}{\partial\bm{\nu}_{n}}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{u_{1,n}(x_{n}+t\bm{\nu}_{n})-u_{1,n}(x_{n})}{t}\geq c_{n}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{w_{n}(x_{n}+t\bm{\nu}_{n})}{t}

\displaystyle=c_{n}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{e^{-\delta\left|x_{n}+t\bm{\nu}_{n}-y(x_{n})\right|^{2}}-e^{-\delta R^{2}}}{t}=c_{n}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{e^{-\delta\left|t\bm{\nu}_{n}-\bm{\nu}_{n}R\right|^{2}}-e^{-\delta R^{2}}}{t}

\displaystyle\geq c_{n}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{e^{-\delta(t-R)^{2}}-e^{-\delta R^{2}}}{t}=c_{n}\lim_{t\to 0}(-2\delta)e^{-\delta(t-R)^{2}}(t-R)=c_{n}2\delta Re^{-\delta R^{2}}.

As a consequence of (
5.7
),

(5.8)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\partial u_{1,n}}{\partial\bm{\nu}_{n}}(x_{n})=\infty.

On the other hand, if we prove that

(5.9)

\frac{\partial u_{1,n}}{\partial\bm{\nu}_{n}}(x_{n})\leq K<\infty,

a contradiction to (
5.8
) arises, and we conclude that
u_{1,n}\to\infty
in
\overline{\Omega}_{0,1}
as we desire.
Thus, let
v_{n}
be the unique solution to

(5.10)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\lambda_{n}m_{1}(x)u-a_{1}(x)u^{p}\quad&&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{1}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0,1},\\
&u=u_{1,n}(x_{n})\quad&&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{0,1},\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\bm{\nu_{1}}}+\gamma_{1}u=0\quad&&\text{on}\quad\Gamma,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

see Lemma
2.5
. We have that
u_{1,n}
is a supersolution of problem (
5.10
) and by comparison

(5.11)

v_{n}(x)\leq u_{1,n}(x),\quad\text{for any}\quad x\in\overline{\Omega}_{1}\setminus\Omega_{0,1}.

Consider now
v_{\infty}
the unique positive solution of

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\lambda_{\infty}m_{1}(x)u-a_{1}(x)u^{p}\quad&&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{1}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0,1},\\
&u=k\quad&&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{0,1},\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\bm{\nu_{1}}}+\gamma_{1}u=0\quad&&\text{on}\quad\Gamma.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Since
u_{1,n}(x_{n})\leq k
, recall (
5.3
),
v_{\infty}
is a supersolution to (
5.10
) and
v_{n}\leq v_{\infty}
. Hence
\|v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{1}\setminus\Omega_{0,1})}
is uniformly bounded, independently
of
n
. Thanks to the
L^{p}
estimates and the Sobolev embedding theorem, we have that
\{v_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
C^{1,\eta}(\overline{\Omega}_{1}\setminus\Omega_{0,1})
, and hence,
\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{1}\setminus\Omega_{0,1})}\leq C
, for a positive constant
C
, see for instance
[
23
]
. Lastly, due to (
5.11
) and since
v_{n}(x_{n})=u_{1,n}(x_{n})
we conclude

\frac{\partial u_{1,n}}{\partial\bm{\nu}_{n}}(x_{n})\leq\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial\bm{\nu}_{n}}(x_{n})\leq C,

which actually proves (
5.9
) and finishes the proof.
∎

for

provides us with a priori bounds of

Finally, we analyse what happens outside the set
\Omega_{0,1}

(5.12)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u_{1}=\lambda m_{1}(x)u_{1}-a_{1}(x)u_{1}^{p}\quad&&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{1}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0,1},\\

(x,\partial\Omega_{0,1})\to 0,
see
[
20
]
for an uncoupled problem or
[
2
]
for an interface system.
Then, we next show the existence of solutions to (
5.12
)–(
5.13
). To this end, let us first state the existence of solutions for an auxiliary problem.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any
\lambda\in(\lambda_{*},\lambda_{m_{2}})
, Problem (
5.12
)
with the condition

(5.14)

u_{1}=\phi\quad\text{in}\quad\partial\Omega_{0,1},\quad\hbox{with}\quad\phi\in C^{2,\eta}(\partial\Omega_{0,1}).

possesses a unique positive solution denoted by

.

The proof follows the arguments described in
[
20
, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
34
]
and is based on the construction of an appropriate sub and supersolution.

It is clear that
\underline{\bf v}\equiv 0
is a subsolution of (
5.12
)–(
5.14
). To construct a supersolution we define an auxiliary problem that avoids the condition (
5.14
) and for which we know how to characterize the positive solutions. To do so, define

\overline{\bf a}=\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{a_{1}},\hskip 11.49994ptx\in\Omega_{1},\\
a_{2},\hskip 11.49994ptx\in\Omega_{2},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\overline{a_{1}}
is non-negative in
\Omega_{1}
and such that
\overline{a_{1}}\leq a_{1}
in
\Omega_{1}\setminus\Omega_{0,1}
. Moreover, we define
\overline{a_{1}}
so that the set

\lambda_{m}^{\mathcal{U}}[-\Delta;\mathcal{D}]>\lambda_{m_{2}}

Consider

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\mathbf{u}=\lambda{\mathbf{mu}}-\overline{\bf a}{\bf u}^{p},&x\in\Omega,\\
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{u})=0,&x\in\Gamma\cup\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Due to the definition of
\overline{\bf a}
, we conclude, see Theorem
4.2

to this latter system as long as

Under these conditions it is clear that
\overline{\bf v}=M{\bf v}

M>1
big enough so that

on

, is a supersolution of (

)–(

).

Therefore, having a sub and a supersolution and also thanks to Lemma
2.6
we get that there is a unique solution
\tilde{\bf u}
for (
5.12
)–(
5.14
).
∎

u_{1,\lambda_{\infty}}(x_{n})\leq C
for any
n\geq 1
and some positive constant
C
. Hence, we have
u_{1,k}(x_{n})\leq C
for any
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
, since
{\bf u}_{n}
converges from below to
{\bf u}_{\lambda_{\infty}}
as
n\to\infty
.

On the other hand, thanks to Theorem
5.2
, we also know that
u_{1,k}(x_{\infty})\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
, uniformly for all
n\geq 1
. Thus, there exists
k_{0}
sufficiently large
u_{1,k_{0}}(x_{\infty})\geq 3C
for all
n\geq 1
. Since
u_{1,k_{0}}
is uniformly continuous we have that
\left|u_{i,k_{0}}(x_{n})-u_{i,k_{0}}(x_{\infty})\right|\leq C
. Equivalently,

u_{1,k_{0}}(x_{n})\geq u_{1,k_{0}}(x_{\infty})-C\geq 2C,

which contradicts the assumption
u_{1,k}(x_{n})\leq C
for all
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
.

Finally, to show
{\bf u}_{\lambda_{\infty}}
is actually minimal postive solution of (
5.12
)–(
5.13
) with
\lambda=\lambda_{\infty}
consider any other solution
{\bf\hat{u}}_{\lambda_{\infty}}
. By comparison,
{\bf u}_{n}<{\bf\hat{u}}_{\lambda_{\infty}}
in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0,1}
. Therefore, letting
n\to\infty
we conclude that
{\bf u}_{\lambda_{\infty}}<{\bf\hat{u}}_{\lambda_{\infty}}
.
∎

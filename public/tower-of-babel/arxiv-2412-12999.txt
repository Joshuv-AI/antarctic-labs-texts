Intermediate Dimensions of Complementary Sets
Nicolás Angelini, Úrsula Molter

Abstract
. Given a positive, non-increasing sequence a a with finite sum equal to 1 1 , we consider the family of all closed subsets of [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] whose complementary open intervals have lengths given by a rearrangement of the sequence a a . We study the full range of possible θ \theta -intermediate dimensions of these sets and, under suitable assumptions on the sequence, we show that this range forms a closed interval, whose endpoints we compute explicitly. This paper fills a gap in the literature concerning the dimensional properties of complementary sets.

28A80, 28A75

1.
Introduction

The intermediate dimensions
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}
and
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}
form a continuum of dimensions that were introduced in
[
13
]
as a way to interpolate between the Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions; see Definition
2.1
for the precise definition. These dimensions have been extensively studied in various contexts, such as in
[
6
]
for stochastic processes,
[
7
]
and
[
2
]
for results related to projections of sets,
[
8
]
for elliptic polynomial spirals, and more recently in
[
1
]
, where these dimensions were extended to measures. These dimensions are continuous (except perhaps at
\theta=0
) and increase as a function of
\theta
, and the following relations hold:

\dim_{H}E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E,

where
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact, and
\dim_{H}
and
\dim_{B}
represent the Hausdorff and box dimensions, respectively. Moreover,
[
4
]
provides a characterization of a function
h(\theta)
as the dimension function associated with a set
E
. These dimensions were further generalized in
[
3
]
to more general settings.

Given
a=\{a_{n}\}
, a positive non-increasing sequence with a finite sum equal to
1
, the class
\mathcal{C}_{a}
is defined as the family of all closed subsets of
[0,1]
whose complement in
[0,1]
is a union of disjoint open intervals of lengths given by the entries of
a
. The sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
are called
complementary sets
of
a
. They can be countable, for example, a sequence of points, and uncountable, for example, Cantor sets. So it is natural to ask about the possible dimensions of the sets belonging to
\mathcal{C}_{a}
.

In the case of Hausdorff dimension, the problem was first studied in 1954 by Besicovitch and Taylor in
[
5
]
and was later studied more deeply in
[
9
]
and
[
15
]
. In fact, it was proved that the set of possible values for the Hausdorff dimension of the sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
is the closed interval
[0,\dim_{H}C_{a}]
, where
C_{a}
denotes the Cantor set associated with
a
, see section
3
for the precise definition. A similar result for the Packing measure was obtained in
[
16
]
by Hare, Mendivil, and Zuberman.

For the case of the box dimensions, in
[
11
, Chapter 3.2]
it is shown that there exist numbers
s\leq t
, depending only on the sequence
a
, such that every complementary set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
has lower and upper box dimensions
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=s
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=t
.
In particular, these values depend only on
a
and not on the specific rearrangement of its gaps.

More recently, Garcia, Hare and Mendivil studied the problem for Assouad and lower Assouad dimensions. They computed the maximum and minimum possible values for the Assouad dimension of complementary sets and, under certain assumptions about the decay of the sequence
a
, proved that the set of attainable values for the Assouad dimension of sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
is, in fact, also a closed interval.

In this paper, we study the problem from the perspective of
\theta
-intermediate dimensions. Specifically, we compute the
\theta
-intermediate dimension of the countable set
D_{a}
(see Section
3
for the precise definition) and, under natural conditions on the decay of the sequence
a
, we are able to compute the
\theta
-intermediate dimension of the Cantor set
C_{a}
.

Following these computations, we establish that the minimum and maximum possible values for the
\theta
-intermediate dimension of any complementary set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
are given by the
\theta
-intermediate dimensions of
D_{a}
and
C_{a}
, respectively. More formally, for all
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have the following inequalities:

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{a}\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}.

Similarly, the analogous result holds for the lower
\theta
-intermediate dimension.

Finally, we address the natural question of whether all possible values of
\theta
-intermediate dimensions for sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
are attained within the intervals
[\underline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\underline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}]
and
[\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}]
. We prove that this is indeed the case under certain natural geometric conditions on the sequence
a
.

Let
s_{n}
denote the average length of the closed intervals at the
n
-th step in the construction of the Cantor set
C_{a}
. Our hypotheses are motivated by known results for Assouad and lower Assouad dimensions: the Assouad dimensions of complementary sets form a closed interval if the sequence
a
is doubling, while the lower Assouad dimensions form a closed interval if
2<\inf_{n}s_{n}/s_{n+1}
(see
[
14
]
). These two conditions—which together require that the ratios
s_{n}/s_{n+1}
be bounded away from both 2 and infinity—ensure that the intermediate dimensions of complementary sets also form a closed interval. Specifically, fixed
\theta
, we show that under these conditions,

\{\underline{\dim}_{\theta}E:E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}\}=[\underline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\underline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}]

and

\{\overline{\dim}_{\theta}E:E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}\}=[\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}].

That is, fixed
\theta\in[0,1]
, for every
t\in[\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{a},\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}]
(respectively for the upper intermediate dimension), there exists a set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
, that depends on
\theta
and
t
, whose lower (respectively upper)
\theta
-intermediate dimension equals exactly
t
.

As previously discussed, complementary sets have already been studied extensively with respect to Hausdorff, packing, box-counting, Assouad, and lower Assouad dimensions.

This paper complements those results by providing a detailed analysis of the intermediate dimensions of sets in

\mathcal{C}_{a}
for an arbitrary sequence

a
, characterizing the full range of possible values.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Definitions

Given a non-empty set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, we write
|A|
for the diameter of the set.

We write
a_{n}\asymp b_{n}
to mean that there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, independent of
n
, such that

c_{1}b_{n}\leq a_{n}\leq c_{2}b_{n}
for all
n
.

The Hausdorff dimension of
A
will be denoted by
\dim_{H}A
, and
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,A
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,A
will denote the lower and upper box-counting dimensions of
A
, respectively. The reader can refer to
[
12
]
for more information on these dimensions.

This document concerns the
\theta
-intermediate dimensions, which were introduced in
[
13
]
and are defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be bounded. For
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, we define the
lower
\theta
-intermediate dimensions
of
F
by

\displaystyle\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F=\inf\big\{

\displaystyle s\geq 0:\ \forall\ \varepsilon>0,\ \text{and all }\ \delta_{0}>0,\text{there}\ \exists\ 0<\delta\leq\delta_{0},\ \text{and }

\displaystyle\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}\ :F\subseteq\cup_{i\in I}U_{i}\ :\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta\ \text{and }\ \sum_{i\in I}|U_{i}|^{s}\leq\varepsilon\big\}.

Similarly, we define the
upper
\theta
-intermediate dimensions
of
F
by

\displaystyle\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F=\inf\big\{

\displaystyle s\geq 0:\ \forall\ \varepsilon>0,\ \text{there}\ \exists\ \delta_{0}>0,\ :\ \forall\ 0<\delta\leq\delta_{0},\ \text{there}\ \exists

\displaystyle\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}\ :F\subseteq\cup_{i\in I}U_{i}\ :\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta\ \text{and }\ \sum_{i\in I}|U_{i}|^{s}\leq\varepsilon\big\}.

For all
\theta\in(0,1]
and a compact set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, we have
\dim_{H}A\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}A\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,A
and similarly with the lower
\theta
dimension, replacing
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,
with
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,
. This spectrum of dimensions has the property of being continuous for
\theta\in(0,1]
, and sometimes it is also continuous at
\theta=0
. The reader can find more information in
[
13
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is straightforward to note that the value of
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F
remains unchanged if the covering condition
\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta
is replaced by
c_{1}\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq c_{2}\delta
for any fixed constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
.

We also need to recall the definitions of the Assouad dimension and the lower Assouad dimension. Given a non-empty set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
,
N_{r}(E)
will denote the minimum number of closed balls of radius
r
needed to cover
E
.

The
Assouad dimension
of
E
is defined as

\displaystyle\dim_{A}E=\inf\{\alpha:

\displaystyle\text{ there exist constants }c,\rho>0\text{ such that }

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in E}N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\leq c\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\alpha}\text{ for all }0<r<R<\rho\}.

In the same way, the
lower Assouad dimension
of
E
is defined as

\displaystyle\dim_{L}E=\sup\{\alpha:

\displaystyle\text{ there exist constants }c,\rho>0\text{ such that }

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in E}N_{r}(B(x,R)\cap E)\geq c\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\alpha}\text{ for all }0<r<R<\rho\}.

The following inequalities hold for compact sets:

\dim_{L}E\leq\dim_{H}E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\leq\dim_{A}E.

A key inequality involving these dimensions, which we will use later, is given in the following result.

Proposition 2.3

(Corollary 2.8
[
4
]
)
.

If
\dim_{L}E<\dim_{A}E
, then for all
\theta\in(0,1]
,

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\geq\frac{\theta\dim_{A}E(\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E-\dim_{L}E)+\dim_{L}E(\dim_{A}E-\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E)}{\theta(\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E-\dim_{L}E)+(\dim_{A}E-\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E)}.

The same holds replacing
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,
with
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}
and
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,
everywhere.

3.
Complementary Sets

Let
a=\{a_{n}\}
be a positive, non-increasing, summable sequence with sum
1
. Let
I_{a}=[0,1]
.
\mathcal{C}_{a}
will denote the family of all closed sets
E
contained in
I_{a}
that are of the form
E=I_{a}\setminus\displaystyle\cup_{j\geq 1}U_{j}
, where
\{U_{j}\}
is a disjoint family of open intervals contained in
I_{a}
such that
|U_{j}|=a_{j}
for all
j
. The sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
are called the complementary sets of
a
. Note that if
\mathbf{a}=\{\mathbf{a}_{n}\}
is any rearrangement of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{a}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{a}}
.

An important set in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
is the
countable
set, defined as follows:

D_{a}=\left\{\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}a_{i}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}.

As the reader notes,
D_{a}
is a countable, decreasing set of points with decreasing gaps given by the elements of the sequence
a
.

Another set of great importance in this family is the non-increasing Cantor set associated with the sequence
a
, which is constructed inductively in the following way. In the first step, remove from
I_{a}
an open interval of length
a_{1}
, leaving two closed intervals
I_{1,1}
and
I_{1,2}
. Having performed the
k
-th step, we have
2^{k}
closed intervals
I_{k,1},...,I_{k,2^{k}}
contained in
I_{a}
. Then, from each interval
I_{k,j}
, remove an open interval of length
a_{2^{k}+j-1}
to obtain two closed intervals
I_{k+1,2j-1}
and
I_{k+1,2j}
. Finally, define the Cantor set as

C_{a}:=\displaystyle\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{j=1}^{2^{k}}I_{k,j}.

Given a non-increasing, summable sequence
a=\{a_{n}\}
, define

\displaystyle x_{n}(a)=\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}a_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad s_{n}(a)=2^{-n}x_{2^{n}}(a).

When the sequence
a
is fixed and clear from context, we will simply write
s_{n}
instead of
s_{n}(a)
.

Note that since
a
is non increasing we have the estimate

s_{n+1}<|I_{n,j}|<s_{n-1}\quad\text{for all $1\leq j\leq 2^{n}$,}

where
I_{n,j}
are the closed intervals of step
n
in the construction of the Cantor set
C_{a}
.

The Hausdorff dimension of sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
was studied in
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
15
]
. The following proposition holds:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a=\{a_{n}\}
be a positive summable sequence, and let
C_{a}
be the decreasing Cantor set associated with
a
. Then

\dim_{H}C_{a}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log(x_{n}/n)}.

Furthermore, any set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
satisfies
\dim_{H}E\leq\dim_{H}C_{a}
. Conversely, given any
s\in[0,\dim_{H}C_{a}]
, there exists a set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
such that
\dim_{H}E=s
.

Moreover, it was shown in
[
15
, Proposition 3.3]
that
\dim_{H}C_{a}=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,C_{a}
.

The box dimensions of sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
were carefully studied in
[
11
, Chapter 3.2]
. There, Falconer shows that the upper and lower box dimensions of sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
depend only on the sequence
a
, and not on the rearrangement. That is, given any complementary set, its lower box dimension equals that of every other complementary set, and the same holds for the upper box dimension. Furthermore, for any
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log a_{n}}\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log a_{n}},

and the box dimension exists if and only if these limits coincide.

The upper box dimension of
C_{a}
(and therefore of any other complementary set in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
) is given by

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log(x_{n}/n)},

which, as shown in
[
17
]
, is equal to

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log a_{n}}.

This equivalence does not hold for the lower box dimension. In fact, the inequality

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log a_{n}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{-\log(x_{n}/n)}

can be strict; see, for instance,
[
10
, Proposition 5]
.
The Assouad dimension and the lower Assouad dimension of sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
were studied in
[
14
]
. There, the authors prove that, given a non-increasing summable sequence
a=\{a_{n}\}
,

\dim_{A}C_{a}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\sup_{k}\frac{n\log 2}{\log(s_{k}/s_{k+n})}\right),

and

\dim_{L}C_{a}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\inf_{k}\frac{n\log 2}{\log(s_{k}/s_{k+n})}\right).

Moreover, it was shown that
\dim_{A}D_{a}=0
or
1
, and if
E
is any complementary set in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
, then

\dim_{A}C_{a}\leq\dim_{A}E\leq\dim_{A}D_{a},

and

0=\dim_{L}D_{a}\leq\dim_{L}E\leq\dim_{L}C_{a}.

Furthermore, if the sequence
a
is doubling, i.e., there exists a constant
c>0
such that
a_{n}\leq c\,a_{2n}
for all
n
, then

\{\dim_{A}E:E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}\}=[\dim_{A}C_{a},1],

and if instead of doubling, we assume that
\inf\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n+1}}>2
, then

\{\dim_{L}E:E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}\}=[0,\dim_{L}C_{a}].

The present paper fills a gap in the theory of complementary sets by proving equivalent results through the

\theta-
intermediate dimension.

4.
\theta-
intermediate dimension of
C_{a}
and
D_{a}

In this section, we compute the lower and upper
\theta
-intermediate dimensions of the set
D_{a}
.

Under natural assumptions on the decay of the ratios
s_{n}
, we also determine the lower and upper intermediate dimensions of the Cantor set
C_{a}
. To this end, for a fixed scale
s_{n}
, we consider coverings by sets whose diameters lie within the admissible range determined by
\theta
.

Specifically, we aim to minimize the quantity

\frac{-m\log 2}{\log s_{m}},

where the index
m
ranges over all the values satisfying

s_{n}^{1/\theta}\leq s_{m}\leq s_{n}.

In other words, the goal is to minimize this expression over all admissible diameters
s_{m}
that may be used to cover the set at scale
s_{n}
, in accordance with the
\theta
-intermediate dimension constraints.

This leads naturally to the introduction of two parameters, which will aid in analyzing the minimal value of the above expression within the admissible range.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a non-increasing summable sequence, let
\theta\in(0,1]
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. We define
\gamma_{\theta,a}(r)
and
\rho_{\theta,r}(r)
as follows:

\gamma_{\theta,a}(r):=\max\left\{m\in\mathbb{N}:s_{m}\geq s_{r}^{1/\theta}\right\},

and

\rho_{\theta,a}(r):=\argmin_{r\leq m\leq\gamma(r)+1}\left\{s_{m}^{1/m}\right\},

that is,
\rho_{\theta,a}(r)
is the integer between
r
and
\gamma_{\theta,a}(r)+1
for which the value
s_{m}^{1/m}
is minimal.

A consequence of the following result is that the best possible covering strategy
for
C_{a}
uses sets of some fixed radius within the allowed range
[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a non increasing summable sequence. Suppose that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(s_{n}/s_{n+1})^{1/n}=1
. Then, for all
\theta\in(0,1]
, we have

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho_{\theta,a}(n)\log 2}{\log s_{\rho_{\theta,a}(n)}}.

(1)

Proof.

Our first goal is to show that the limsup on the right-hand side of (
1
) is less than or equal to the
\theta
-intermediate dimension of
C_{a}
.

To do this, let
\theta\in(0,1]
and note that if
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}=0
, then Proposition
2.3
implies that
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,C_{a}=0
, and therefore

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-n\log 2}{\log(s_{n})}=0,

which in turn implies that the right-hand side of (
1
) is also equal to
0
.
Now, suppose that
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}>0
.

For simplicity of notation, we will write
\rho(n)
and
\gamma(n)
instead of
\rho_{\theta,a}(n)
and
\gamma_{\theta,a}(n)
, respectively.

Let
\delta_{0}>0
and let
K
be such that
s_{K}<\delta_{0}
, and fix
s^{\prime}<\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)})}
.

\text{Then there exists $k^{\prime}\geq K$ such that}\quad s^{\prime}<\frac{-\rho(k^{\prime})\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(k^{\prime})})}.

(2)

Note that by the definition of
\rho
in (
4.1
), we have

\frac{-\rho(k^{\prime})\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(k^{\prime})})}\leq\frac{-m\log(2)}{\log(s_{m})}\quad\text{for all $k^{\prime}\leq m\leq\gamma(k^{\prime})+1$}.

Let
\delta=s_{k^{\prime}}<\delta_{0}
, and let
\{U_{i}\}
be any finite cover of
C_{a}
such that
\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta
for all
i
. Then we have
(s_{k^{\prime}})^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq s_{k^{\prime}}
for all
i
.

For each
i
, let
t(i)\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
s_{t(i)+1}<|U_{i}|\leq s_{t(i)}
. Note that
k^{\prime}\leq t(i)\leq\gamma(k^{\prime})
.

Then
U_{i}
can intersect at most 4 intervals from step
t(i)-1
in the construction of
C_{a}
. Indeed, if it were to intersect 5 intervals from step
t(i)-1
, it would necessarily contain at least one interval from step
t(i)-2
, say
I_{t(i)-2,j}
for some
j
, and hence:

s_{t(i)}\geq|U_{i}|\geq|I_{t(i)-2,j}|\geq s_{t(i)-1}>s_{t(i)},

since
s_{n}
is strictly decreasing.

As a consequence,
U_{i}
can intersect at most 8 intervals from step
t(i)
. Therefore, for
j\geq t(i)
, the set
U_{i}
can intersect at most

8\,2^{j-t(i)}=16\,2^{j}\,2^{-(t(i)+1)}\leq 16\,2^{j}\,(s_{t(i)+1})^{s^{\prime}}\leq 16\,2^{j}\,|U_{i}|^{s^{\prime}}

intervals from step
j
.

Finally, let
j
be sufficiently large so that
s_{j}<|U_{i}|
for all
i
. Since
\{U_{i}\}_{i}
is a cover of
C_{a}
, it must intersect all the
2^{j}
intervals from step
j
, and hence:

2^{j}\leq\sum_{i}16\,2^{j}\,|U_{i}|^{s^{\prime}},

which implies that any such cover satisfies:

\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{s^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{16}.

Therefore,
s^{\prime}\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}
. Letting

s^{\prime}\to\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)})}\quad\text{we have}\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)})}\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}.

For the opposite inequality, let

t>d:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)})},

and let
\delta>0
be arbitrarily small. Let
k
be such that
s_{k+1}\leq\delta\leq s_{k}
, and suppose that
\delta^{1/\theta}\leq s_{\rho(k)}\leq\delta
. Since the lengths of the closed intervals at step
\rho(k)+1
in the construction of
C_{a}
are less than
s_{\rho(k)}
, we can cover
C_{a}
with
2^{\rho(k)+1}
intervals of length
s_{\rho(k)}
.

Then

\inf_{\{U_{i}\}:\delta^{1/\theta}\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta}\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{t}\leq 2^{\rho(k)+1}s_{\rho(k)}^{t}<C^{\prime}<\infty,

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
, by our choice of
t
. The above inequality implies that
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}\leq t
and the result follows by letting
t\to d
.

Now, if
s_{\rho(k)}<\delta^{1/\theta}
or
\delta<s_{\rho(k)}
, then either
s_{\rho(k)-1}
or
s_{\rho(k)+1}
belongs to
[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
. Proceeding as before, we obtain

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)-1})}\quad\text{or}\quad\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)+1})}.

But, since we are assuming that
(s_{n}/s_{n-1})^{1/n}
tends to
1
as
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)+1})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)+1}/s_{\rho(n)})+\log(s_{\rho(n)})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log(2)}{\log(s_{\rho(n)})},

and the same holds for
s_{\rho(n)-1}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Note that it is not necessary to compute the lower
\theta
-intermediate dimension of
C_{a}
, since the equality
\dim_{H}C_{a}=\underline{\dim}_{B}C_{a}
always holds.

In the next theorem, we compute the intermediate dimensions of the countable set
D_{a}
.
We note that these dimensions are as small as possible, attaining the minimal value
permitted by Proposition
2.3
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a non increasing summable sequence. Then for all
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{\textbf{a}}=\frac{\theta\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,D_{\textbf{a}}}{1-(1-\theta)\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,D_{\textbf{a}}}

(3)

and

\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{\textbf{a}}=\frac{\theta\,\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,D_{\textbf{a}}}{1-(1-\theta)\,\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,D_{\textbf{a}}}.

Proof.

We will prove the result for the upper intermediate dimensions. The analogous statement for the lower intermediate dimensions holds with suitable adjustments, replacing the use of the
\limsup
with that of the
\liminf
.

First we will prove that the right hand side of equality (
3
) is an upper bound to
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{a}
.
We have

B:=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,D_{\textit{{a}}}=\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(n)}{-\log(x_{n}/n)},

(4)

with
x_{n}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}a_{i}
.
If
B=0
there is nothing to prove and if
B=1
then by Proposition
2.3
,
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{\textit{{a}}}=1
, so we will suppose that
0<B<1
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1-B
.
From (
4
) we have that there exists
N
, such that
x_{n}\leq(1/n)^{\frac{1-(B+\varepsilon)}{B+\varepsilon}}
.
Let
\delta>0
be sufficiently small such that
N\leq\lceil\delta^{-(B+\varepsilon)(s+\theta(1-s))}\rceil:=K.

To cover
\{x_{n}\}
we will cover
\{x_{i}:1\leq i\leq K\}
with
K
intervals of length
\delta
and the remaining points
\{x_{i}:K<i\}\subset[0,x_{K}]
with

\left\lceil\frac{x_{K}}{\delta^{\theta}}\right\rceil\leq\frac{(1/K)^{\frac{(1-(B+\varepsilon))}{B+\varepsilon}}}{\delta^{\theta}}+1

intervals of length
\delta^{\theta}
.

Hence we have a cover
\mathcal{U}
of
D_{a}
such that
\delta\leq|U|\leq\delta^{\theta}
for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}|U|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq K\delta^{s}+\left(\frac{(1/K)^{\frac{(1-(B+\varepsilon))}{B+\varepsilon}}}{\delta^{\theta}}+1\right)\delta^{\theta s}

\displaystyle\leq(\delta^{-(B+\varepsilon)(s+\theta(1-s))}+1)\delta^{s}+\delta^{\theta(s-1)}\delta^{(s+\theta(1-s))(1-(B+\varepsilon)))}+\delta^{\theta s}

\displaystyle=I+I\!I+I\!I\!I

respectively.

The term
I\!I\!I
will tend to
0
as
\delta
tends to
0
if and only if
\theta s>0
.

The terms
I
and
I\!I
will tend to
0
as
\delta
tends to
0
if and only if
s>\frac{\theta(B+\varepsilon)}{1-(1-\theta)(B+\varepsilon)}.

This implies that

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{a}\leq\frac{\theta(B+\varepsilon)}{1-(1-\theta)(B+\varepsilon)},

and the result follows letting
\varepsilon\to 0.

For the reverse inequality, note that since
\dim_{L}D_{a}=0
and
\dim_{A}D_{a}=1
, the result follows directly from Proposition
2.3
.

∎

5.
Maximum and minimum dimensions

In this section, we will show that
D_{a}
and
C_{a}
attain the minimum and maximum among all possible values for intermediate dimensions. We first make the following straightforward remark.

Remark 5.1
.

Let
c>0
. If
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a collection of
m
closed intervals such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|U_{i}|\leq c\,m,

then the union
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}
can be covered by at most
2m
closed intervals of diameter
c
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
a=\{a_{n}\}
be a summable non-increasing sequence and let
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
be any complementary set. Then

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{a}\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\quad\text{ and }\quad\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{a}\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E.

Proof.

Using that all complementary sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
share the same lower and upper box dimensions, that is, there exist numbers
s\leq t
depending only on
a
such that
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=s
and
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E=t
for every
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
, together with Proposition
2.3

we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}D_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,D_{a}}{1-(1-\theta)\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,D_{a}}=\frac{\theta\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E}{1-(1-\theta)\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\theta\dim_{A}E\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E}{\dim_{A}E-(1-\theta)\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E.

The proof for the lower
\theta
-intermediate dimensions is similar.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
a=\{a_{n}\}
be a summable sequence such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n+1}}\right)^{1/n}=1.

Then any set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
satisfies

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}.

Proof.

The lower
\theta-
intermediate dimension case follows directly from the fact that
\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,C_{a}=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,E
for all
\theta.

Now, we will prove the upper intermediate dimension part. By Lemma
4.2
, we have

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log 2}{\log s_{\rho(n)}}.

Let
t>\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}
, so that there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{\rho(k)}s_{\rho(k)}^{t}<1
for all
k\geq K
.

Now fix
\delta_{0}>0
sufficiently small so that
\delta_{0}<s_{K}
, and let
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

s_{n+1}\leq\delta<s_{n}.

First, suppose that
\gamma(n)\leq\rho(n)<n
. Then
\rho(n)\in[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
.
Removing from
[0,1]
the complementary gaps of
E
that have lengths
a_{1},...,a_{2^{\rho(n)}-1}
, we obtain the set
E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup\dots\cup E_{2^{\rho(n)}}
,where
E_{i}
are closed intervals (some of these intervals may be degenerate, that is, single points).

Then, the collection
\{E_{i}\}
forms a cover of
E
, and we have

\sum_{i=1}^{2^{\rho(n)}}|E_{i}|=\sum_{i=2^{\rho(n)}}^{\infty}a_{i}=2^{\rho(n)}s_{\rho(n)}.

By Remark
5.1
, it follows that there exists a family of
2^{\rho(n)+1}
sets,
\{U_{i}\}
, such that

E\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{2^{\rho(n)}}E_{i}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{2^{\rho(n)+1}}U_{i},\quad\text{
with $|U_{i}|=s_{\rho(n)}$ for all $i$}.

Then we have

\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{t}=2\cdot\left(2^{\rho(n)}s_{\rho(n)}^{t}\right)<2,

which implies
\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E\leq t
, and the result follows by letting
t\to\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}
.

Now, if
\rho(n)=\gamma(n)+1
or
\rho(n)=n
, then either
s_{\rho(n)-1}
or
s_{\rho(n)+1}
belongs to
[\delta^{1/\theta},\delta]
, and the result follows by noting that the hypothesis

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n+1}}\right)^{1/n}=1

implies

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log 2}{\log s_{\rho(n)-1}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log 2}{\log s_{\rho(n)+1}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\rho(n)\log 2}{\log s_{\rho(n)}}=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}.

∎

6.
Attainable values

A natural question is whether all possible values of
\theta
-intermediate dimensions for sets in
\mathcal{C}_{a}
are attained within the intervals
[\underline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\underline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}]
or
[\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}]
. In the next theorem, we provide conditions under which this holds.

Recall that the Assouad dimensions of complementary sets form a closed interval if the sequence is doubling. Conversely, the lower Assouad dimensions of complementary sets form a closed interval if the sequence satisfies
2<\inf_{n}s_{n}/s_{n+1}
(see
[
14
]
). These two conditions, in fact, constitute our hypotheses to ensure that the intermediate dimensions of complementary sets also form a closed interval. Indeed, it is straightforward to prove that for a decreasing sequence satisfying
2<\inf_{n}s_{n}/s_{n+1}
, the doubling condition is equivalent to
\sup_{n}s_{n}/s_{n+1}<\infty
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
and let
a=\{a_{n}\}
be a positive, non-increasing, summable sequence. If

2<\inf_{n}\frac{s_{n}(a)}{s_{n+1}(a)}\leq\sup_{n}\frac{s_{n}(a)}{s_{n+1}(a)}<\infty,

then for every

t\in\left[\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\,\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}\right],

there exists a set
E=E(t,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
such that
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}E=t
. An analogous result holds for the upper
\theta
-intermediate dimension, i.e., for every

t\in\left[\underline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\,\underline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}\right],

there exists a set
E^{\prime}=E^{\prime}(t,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
such that
\underline{\dim}_{\theta}E^{\prime}=t
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by explicit construction of the sets
E
and
E^{\prime}
. Given the sequence
a
, we will construct a Cantor-type set
C_{b}
associated to a subsequence
b
of
\{a_{n}\}
, and a countable set
D_{a^{\prime}}
associated to the remaining elements of
\{a_{n}\}
. We will then define

E=C_{b}\cup D_{a^{\prime}},

and show that
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}E=t
.

First, we note that the hypothesis
2<\inf_{n}\frac{s_{n}(a)}{s_{n+1}(a)}\leq\sup_{n}\frac{s_{n}(a)}{s_{n+1}(a)}<\infty
implies that
a
is a doubling sequence.

Let
\theta\in(0,1]
and fix
t\in(\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a})
. Define

s:=\frac{\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}}{t}>1.

For each
n
, let
j=j(n)
be such that

a_{2^{j+1}}<(a_{2^{n}})^{s}\leq a_{2^{j}},\qquad j\geq n.

(5)

b_{2^{k}+i}=\begin{cases}a_{2^{j(k)+1}+i}&\text{if $j(k)>j(k-1)$,}\\
a_{2^{j(k)+1}+\sum_{r=1}^{m}2^{k-r}+i}&\text{if $j(k)=\cdot\cdot\cdot=j(k-m)>j(k-m-1)$,}\end{cases}

for
k\geq 1
and
0\leq i<2^{k}
, where
m
denotes the largest integer with
0\leq m\leq k-1
such that
j(k)=j(k-1)=\cdots=j(k-m)
.
We also adopt the convention
j(0)=0
.

By construction and using the doubling condition of
a
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for each
k\geq 1
and
0\leq i<2^{k}
,

c^{-1}(a_{2^{k}})^{s}\leq b_{2^{k}+i}\leq c(a_{2^{k}})^{s}.

Now, observe that

\inf_{n}\frac{s_{n}(b)}{s_{n+1}(b)}>2.

Indeed, recall that

s_{n}(a)=2^{-n}\sum_{i=2^{n}}^{2^{n+1}-1}a_{i}+2s_{n+1}(a).

From this identity, the hypothesis

2<\inf_{n}\frac{s_{n}(a)}{s_{n+1}(a)}

can be rewritten in the equivalent form

\sup_{n}\frac{s_{n+1}(a)}{r_{n}(a)}<\infty,

where

r_{n}(a)=2^{-n}\sum_{i=2^{n}}^{2^{n+1}-1}a_{i}.

Using the doubling condition for
a
, one directly obtains

\frac{s_{n+1}(b)}{r_{n}(b)}\leq\frac{C}{2^{-(n+1)}}\sum_{i=2^{n+1}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a_{i}}{a_{2^{n}}}\right)^{s}<C\,\frac{2^{-(n+1)}\sum_{i=2^{n+1}}^{\infty}a_{i}}{a_{2^{n}}}\leq C^{\prime}\frac{s_{n+1}(a)}{r_{n}(a)},

for some constant
C
and
C^{\prime}
, and hence
\sup_{n}\tfrac{s_{n+1}(b)}{r_{n}(b)}<\infty
.

It follows that for doubling decreasing sequences with this property we have

b_{2^{n}}\asymp s_{n}(b).

Indeed, if
s_{n+1}(b)/s_{n}(b)<1/2-\delta
, then

2\delta s_{n}(b)\leq s_{n}(b)-2s_{n+1}(b)=2^{-n}\sum_{j=2^{n}}^{2^{n+1}-1}b_{j}\leq b_{2^{n}}\leq c\,2^{-n}\sum_{j=2^{n}}^{2^{n+1}-1}b_{j}\leq c\,s_{n}(b).

Hence

s_{n}(b)\asymp b_{2^{n}}\asymp(a_{2^{n}})^{s}\asymp(s_{n}(a))^{s}.

Now, we will prove that
\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{b}=s^{-1}\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}.

Let
d>\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}
. Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for every
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
there is a cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
C_{a}
satisfying

\delta\leq|U_{i}|\leq\delta^{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{d}<\varepsilon.

(6)

Let
\delta
and the cover
\{U_{i}\}
satisfy (
6
). For each
i
, choose
k(i)
so that

s_{k(i)+1}(a)\leq|U_{i}|<s_{k(i)}(a).

It follows that each
U_{i}
can intersect at most four closed intervals from step
k(i)-2
in the construction of
C_{a}
, namely the intervals
I_{j_{n}}^{\,k(i)-2}(a)
with
n=1,\dots,4
. Consequently, we can cover
C_{a}
by

C_{a}\subset\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{n=1}^{4}I_{j_{n}}^{\,k(i)-2}(a).

Analogously, we can cover
C_{b}
by selecting the corresponding intervals from the same steps in the construction of
C_{b}
, that is,

C_{b}\subset\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{n=1}^{4}I_{j_{n}}^{\,k(i)-2}(b).

Now, since

\delta^{s}\leq c_{1}s_{k(i)-3}(b)\leq c_{1}|I_{j_{n}}^{\,k(i)-2}(b)|\leq c_{1}s_{k(i)-1}(b)\leq c_{1}c_{2}\,(s_{k(i)-1}(a))^{s}\leq c_{1}c_{2}\,|U_{i}|^{s},

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}
, it follows that

|I_{j_{n}}^{\,k(i)-2}(b)|\in\bigl(c_{1}^{-1}\delta^{s},\,c_{1}c_{2}\delta^{\theta s}\bigr).

Moreover,

\sum_{i}\sum_{n=1}^{4}|I_{j_{n}}^{\,k(i)-2}(b)|^{d/s}<4\,c_{1}c_{2}\,\varepsilon,

and therefore

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{b}\leq s^{-1}\,\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}.

An analogous argument shows that

s^{-1}\,\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}\leq\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{b}.

We now define the complementary countable part. Let
\{\tilde{a}_{n}\}
be a non-increasing sequence such that

\{a_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}=\{b_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{\tilde{a}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Define the associated countable set

D_{a^{\prime}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}y_{k},\qquad y_{k}=r+\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}\tilde{a}_{i},

where

r=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}.

Finally, set

E=C_{b}\cup D_{a^{\prime}}.

Then
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
. By finite stability and the invariance of the upper box dimension for complementary sets we have

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,D_{a}=\overline{\dim}_{B}E=\max\{\overline{\dim}_{B}C_{b},\overline{\dim}_{B}D_{a^{\prime}}\}.

Since

\overline{\dim}_{B}C_{b}=s^{-1}\overline{\dim}_{B}C_{a}<\overline{\dim}_{B}D_{a},

we deduce that

\overline{\dim}_{B}D_{a}=\overline{\dim}_{B}D_{a^{\prime}}.

Therefore

\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a}=\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a^{\prime}},

by Theorem
4.3
.

Combining these results, we conclude that

\displaystyle\overline{\dim}_{\theta}E

\displaystyle=\max\{s^{-1}\overline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a},\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle=\max\{t,\overline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a}\}=t,

as required.

For the lower
\theta
-intermediate dimension, we must change strategy, as the lower
\theta
-intermediate dimension (as well as the lower box dimension) is not finitely stable. We will consider a similar set and proceed with a covering strategy to prove the theorem.

Let
t\in(\underline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a},\underline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a})
. Proceed as before to construct a set
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
such that
E^{\prime}=C_{b}\cup D_{a^{\prime}}
, where
C_{b}
is a decreasing Cantor set and
D_{a^{\prime}}
is a countable set.

Since both sequences
\{b_{n}\}
and
\{a_{n}\}
are non-increasing, it follows from Proposition
3.1
and the subsequent remarks that

s^{-1}\underline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{a}=s^{-1}\underline{\dim}_{B}C_{a}=\underline{\dim}_{B}C_{b}=\underline{\dim}_{\theta}C_{b}

for all
\theta
.

Now since

\frac{\theta\underline{\dim}_{B}C_{a}}{1-\underline{\dim}_{B}C_{a}(1-\theta)}=\underline{\dim}_{\theta}D_{a}<t=\frac{\underline{\dim}_{B}C_{a}}{s},

we have that

s<\frac{1-(1-\theta)\underline{\dim}_{B}C_{a}}{\theta}.

Therefore, we can choose
t^{\prime}>t
and
B>\underline{\dim}_{B}C_{a}>b
such that

t^{\prime}>\max\left\{\frac{B}{s},\ \frac{\theta B}{1-B(1-\theta)},\ \frac{\theta b-\theta(1-b/t^{\prime}s)}{1-b(1-\theta)-(1-b/t^{\prime}s)(1-\theta)}\right\}

(7)

and

s<\frac{1-(1-\theta)st^{\prime}}{\theta}.

(8)

Now, since by construction of
E^{\prime}
,
(s_{n}(a))^{s}\asymp s_{n}(b)
, it follows that there exist a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}
and a constant
C>0
such that

2^{-n_{k}(1/b)}\leq s_{n_{k}}(a)\leq 2^{-n_{k}(1/B)}

(9)

and

s_{n_{k}}(b)\leq C\,2^{-n_{k}(s/B)}.

(10)

Let
\delta_{k}:=(s_{n_{k}}(a))^{1/(t^{\prime}+\theta(1-t^{\prime}))}
.
By equation (
8
), we have

(s_{n_{k}}(a))^{s}=\delta_{k}^{st^{\prime}(1-\theta)+\theta s}\in(\delta_{k},\delta_{k}^{\theta}),

and therefore
C^{\prime}s_{n_{k}}(b)
is an admissible diameter for covering sets, for some constant
C^{\prime}
.

Since the closed intervals of a decreasing Cantor set, say
I_{i}^{n}
, satisfy
s_{n+1}\leq|I_{i}^{n}|\leq s_{n-1}
, we can cover
C_{b}
with
2^{n_{k}+1}
intervals of length
C^{\prime}s_{n_{k}}(b)
.

To cover
D_{a^{\prime}}=\{x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},\ldots\}
, we will cover
\{x^{\prime}_{i}:i=1,\ldots,2^{n_{k}}\}
with
2^{n_{k}}
sets of diameter
\delta
, and the remaining points will be covered with

\left\lceil\frac{2^{n_{k}}s_{n_{k}}(a^{\prime\prime})}{\delta^{\theta}}\right\rceil\leq\left\lceil\frac{2^{n_{k}}s_{n_{k}}(a)}{\delta^{\theta}}\right\rceil\leq\frac{2^{-n_{k}(1-t^{\prime}s)/t^{\prime}s}}{\delta^{\theta}}+1,

sets of diameter
\delta^{\theta}
, using (
9
).

Therefore we have created a cover
\{U_{i}\}
, of
E
, by sets whose diameters belong to
[\delta,\delta^{\theta}]
, and that satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i}|U_{i}|^{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C^{\prime}2^{n_{k}}s_{n_{k}}(b)^{t^{\prime}}+2^{n_{k}}\delta^{t^{\prime}}+\left(c^{\prime}\frac{2^{-n_{k}(1-t^{\prime}s)/t^{\prime}s}}{\delta^{\theta}}+1\right)\delta^{\theta t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}.

Using (
10
) and (
7
), we have that
S_{1}\to 0
as
n_{k}\to\infty
.

For
S_{2}
we have

\displaystyle 2^{n_{k}}\delta^{t^{\prime}}=2^{n_{k}}(s_{n_{k}}(a))^{t^{\prime}/(\theta+t^{\prime}(1-\theta))}\leq 2^{-n_{k}(t^{\prime}/(B(\theta+t^{\prime}(1-\theta)))-1)}\to 0

since
t^{\prime}>\theta B/(1-B(1-\theta))
.

On the other hand,
S_{3}
will tend to
0
since by (
7
) we have

t^{\prime}>\frac{\theta b-\theta(1-b/t^{\prime}s)}{1-b(1-\theta)-(1-b/t^{\prime}s)(1-\theta)}.

Then, it follows that

\underline{\dim}_{\theta}E^{\prime}\leq t^{\prime}

and the result follows by letting
t^{\prime}\to t
and noting that
t\leq\underline{\dim}_{\theta}E^{\prime}
since
C_{b}\subset E^{\prime}
.
∎

Further Work

In
[
4
]
, the authors provided necessary and sufficient conditions for a function
h(\theta)
to arise as the intermediate dimension of a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Specifically, for
0\leq\lambda<\alpha\leq n
, denote by
\mathcal{H}(\lambda,\alpha)
the set of all functions
h:[0,1]\to[\lambda,\alpha]
that are non-decreasing, continuous on
(0,1]
, and satisfy

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{h(\theta+\varepsilon)-h(\theta)}{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{(h(\theta)-\lambda)(\alpha-h(\theta))}{(\alpha-\lambda)\theta},\quad\forall\,\theta\in(0,1).

If
\lambda=\alpha
, then
\mathcal{H}(\lambda,\alpha)
consists only of the constant function
h(\theta)=\lambda
.
The following theorem was established in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

[
4
, Theorem B]

Let
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy
\dim_{L}F=\lambda
and
\dim_{A}F=\alpha
. Then with
h_{1}(\theta)=\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F
and
h_{2}(\theta)=\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F
, we have
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{H}(\lambda,\alpha)
,
h_{1}\leq h_{2}
, and
h_{1}(0)=h_{2}(0)
.

Conversely, if
0\leq\lambda\leq\alpha\leq n
and
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{H}(\lambda,\alpha)
satisfy
h_{1}\leq h_{2}
and
h_{1}(0)=h_{2}(0)
, then there exists a compact perfect set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\dim_{L}F=\lambda,\qquad\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F=h_{1}(\theta),\qquad\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}F=h_{2}(\theta),\qquad\dim_{A}F=\alpha,

for all
\theta\in[0,1]
.

A natural question connecting
[
4
]
with the present work is the following:
for a fixed sequence
a
, can we describe conditions ensuring that a given function
h(\theta)
can be realized as the intermediate dimension of a complementary set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
?

In this paper, some partial results in that direction were obtained. For instance, under the assumption

2<\inf_{n}\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n+1}}\leq\sup_{n}\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n+1}}<\infty,

the upper intermediate dimension part of Theorem
6.1
can be interpreted as follows:
for any
s>1
, there exists a set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
such that

\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E=\max\left\{\frac{\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}}{s},\,\frac{\theta\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,C_{a}}{1-(1-\theta)\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,C_{a}}\right\}=:h_{s}(\theta),

that is, for each
s>1
, the function
h_{s}(\theta)
is attained as the upper intermediate dimension of some complementary set.
Moreover, any dimension function
h(\theta)
of a set
E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}
, must satisfy

\frac{\theta\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,C_{a}}{1-(1-\theta)\,\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\rm B}\,C_{a}}\leq h(\theta)\leq\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}C_{a}.

To conclude, we state explicitly the open problem that naturally emerges from this discussion.

Question 1
.

Given a non-increasing, summable sequence
a=\{a_{n}\}

2<\inf_{n}\frac{s_{n}(a)}{s_{n+1}(a)}\leq\sup_{n}\frac{s_{n}(a)}{s_{n+1}(a)}<\infty,

\mathcal{F}^{U}(a):=\bigl\{\,\theta\mapsto\overline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E:E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}\,\bigr\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{F}^{L}(a):=\bigl\{\,\theta\mapsto\underline{\mbox{\rm dim}}_{\,\theta}E:E\in\mathcal{C}_{a}\,\bigr\},

\theta

Monodromy Relations for String Amplitudes on A ​ d ​ S AdS
Luis F. Alday a, Rodrigo S. Pitombo a,b , Aurélie Strömholm Sangaré a

Abstract
We consider the A ​ d ​ S AdS Veneziano amplitude describing the scattering of four gluons in type IIB string theory on A ​ d ​ S 5 × S 3 AdS_{5}\times S^{3} . We propose a set of powerful monodromy relations between different colour-ordered amplitudes. These relations arise as a consequence of the emergent world-sheet description of open string scattering on A ​ d ​ S AdS . In flat space, they reduce to the usual monodromy relations for the Veneziano amplitude, and they hold order by order in the small curvature expansion. The relations hold for all results available in the literature, including the scattering of arbitrary KK-modes.

AdS
.
String theory colour-ordered disk amplitudes in flat space satisfy linear relations known as monodromy relations

;
Bjerrum-Bohr:2009ulz
;
Stieberger:2009hq

e^{\pm i\pi S}A^{(0)}(S,U)+A^{(0)}(S,T)+e^{\mp i\pi T}A^{(0)}(U,T)=0,

(8)

where recall
S+T+U=0
. In flat space, these relations can be derived directly from the world-sheet perspective. Indeed, consider the following integrals

\int_{{\cal C}^{\pm}}\eta(z)dz=0,~~~\eta(z)=z^{S-1}(1-z)^{T-1}

(9)

where the contours
{\cal C}^{\pm}
are given by straight lines just above/below the real line. The integrand
\eta(z)
is a multi-valued function on the complex plane, with branch cuts along the real line for
x\leq 0
and
x\geq 1
. We define it such that

\eta(x\pm i\epsilon)=\begin{cases}-e^{\pm i\pi S}(-x)^{S-1}(1-x)^{T-1}&\text{for $x<0$,}\\
x^{S-1}(1-x)^{T-1}&\text{for $0<x<1$,}\\
-e^{\mp i\pi T}(x)^{S-1}(x-1)^{T-1}&\text{for $x>1$}.\end{cases}

(10)

When integrating over the regions
(-\infty,0],[0,1]
and
[1,\infty)
we recover the three colour-ordered amplitudes
A^{(0)}(S,U),A^{(0)}(S,T),A^{(0)}(U,T)
respectively, and the monodromy relations. Furthermore, note that different colour-ordered amplitudes correspond to different orderings of the vertex operators inserted at the boundary of the disk, so that for instance

\displaystyle A^{(0)}(S,T)\sim\int_{0}^{1}dx\langle V_{1}(0)V_{2}(x)V_{3}(1)V_{4}(\infty)\rangle.

(11)

The phases present in the monodromy relations can be seen as the result of exchanging the order of the vertex operators. Let us now analyse the situation in
AdS

K_{0}L_{wa}(x)=L_{w}(x)\delta_{a0},~~~K_{0}L_{e}(x)=0.

(12)

In the same way, we introduce

so that the monodromy around one is given by the action of

Conclusions.
Over the last few years methods have been developed to compute tree-level string theory amplitudes on
AdS
spaces
Alday:2022uxp
;
Alday:2022xwz
. One of the most striking features of these results is the emergence of a world-sheet picture
Alday:2023jdk
;
Alday:2023mvu
, both for closed as well as for open string amplitudes. In this letter we propose a set of monodromy relations for open string amplitudes in
AdS
. These arise as a consequence of the world-sheet description, are satisfied order by order in the small curvature expansion and in flat space they reduce to the usual monodromy relations for the Veneziano amplitude. The monodromy relations are very powerful in flat space, see
Huang:2020nqy
;
Berman:2023jys
;
Chiang:2023quf
, and the same appears to be true in
AdS
. In the context of the proposal of
Alday:2024yax
;
Alday:2024ksp
, and when combined with crossing symmetry alone, they reduce the number of free parameters from 33 to 5 for the first curvature correction, and from 565 to 86 for the second curvature correction. A natural direction is to use these new constraints to study and compute further curvature corrections. It would be interesting to understand the monodromy relations found in this letter in terms of braiding relations for open string vertex operators in
AdS
backgrounds, along the lines of
Boels:2010bv
;
Boels:2014dka
. More generally, it would be very interesting to understand how the structure studied in this letter arises from a first-principles world-sheet computation.

Appendix A
A toy model

Let us consider a simple example where the amplitude is given by the sum of three terms up to weight one

A_{\text{toy}}(S,T)=\sum_{w=e,0,1}R_{w}(S,T)J_{w}(S,T),

(39)

with
R_{e}(S,T),R_{0}(S,T)
and
R_{1}(S,T)
rational functions. Crossing symmetry under
S\leftrightarrow T
implies

R_{e}(S,T)=R_{e}(T,S),~~~R_{0}(S,T)=R_{1}(T,S).

(40)

In addition, the monodromy relations found in this letter imply the following non-trivial conditions

\displaystyle SR_{0}(U,T)=TR_{1}(S,U),

\displaystyle TR_{0}(S,U)+TR_{1}(S,U)+SR_{1}(U,T)=0,

\displaystyle STR_{e}(S,U)-S^{2}R_{e}(U,T)=

\displaystyle SR_{0}(S,U)+TR_{0}(S,U)+SR_{1}(S,U).

In the context of scattering amplitudes on
AdS
we have

R_{e}(S,T)=\frac{\alpha}{(S+T)^{k+1}},~~R_{0}(S,T)=\frac{\beta S+\gamma T}{(S+T)^{k+1}}.

(41)

For
k\neq 0
we find no solutions. For
k=0
we have the usual Veneziano amplitude together with

A_{\text{toy}}(S,T)=\frac{SJ_{0}(S,T)+TJ_{1}(S,T)}{U}.

(42)

Appendix B
Properties of the linear map

In this letter we have defined the inner product

\langle\Psi(f_{0},f_{1})|{\cal J}(e_{0},e_{1})\rangle=\sum_{w}R_{w}J_{w},

with
\Psi(f_{0},f_{1})=\sum_{w}R_{\widetilde{w}}f_{w},{\cal J}(e_{0},e_{1})=\sum_{w}J_{w}e_{w}
. This satisfies the following properties

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(f_{0},f_{1})|{\cal J}(e_{0},e_{1})

\displaystyle h(e_{0},e_{1})\rangle=\sum_{w,w^{\prime}}R_{ww^{\prime}}J_{w}h_{w^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\langle h(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\Psi(f_{0},f_{1})|{\cal J}(e_{0},e_{1})\rangle,

with
h(e_{0},e_{1})=\sum_{w^{\prime}}h_{w^{\prime}}e_{w^{\prime}}
. The operators
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
act on a chain of
f^{\prime}
from the left as

\tau_{a}f_{bw}=\delta_{ab}f_{w}.

(43)

In the same way

\langle\Psi(f_{0},f_{1})|h(e_{0},e_{1}){\cal J}(e_{0},e_{1})\rangle\\
=\langle\Psi(f_{0},f_{1})h(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})|{\cal J}(e_{0},e_{1})\rangle.

The operators
\tau_{0},\tau_{1}
act on a chain of
f^{\prime}
from the right as

f_{wb}\tau_{a}=\delta_{ab}f_{w}.

(44)

One can also prove the following relations

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(f_{0},f_{1})|{\cal J}(e_{1},e_{0})\rangle=\langle\Psi(f_{1},f_{0})|{\cal J}(e_{0},e_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(f_{0},f_{1})|{\cal J}(-e_{0}-e_{1},e_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 90.0pt=\langle\Psi(-f_{0},-f_{0}+f_{1})|{\cal J}(e_{0},e_{1})\rangle.

Another property is the following. Let
\Psi_{h}(f_{0},f_{1})
and
\Psi_{g}(f_{0},f_{1})
be the two linear maps corresponding to integrands
h
and
g
. Then, the linear map corresponding to their product is given by

\Psi_{h\times g}(f_{0},f_{1})=\Psi_{h}(f_{0},f_{1})\shuffle\Psi_{g}(f_{0},f_{1}),

(45)

where the shuffle product acts on the space of non-commutative variables
f_{0},f_{1}
as

f_{w}\shuffle f_{w^{\prime}}=f_{w\shuffle w^{\prime}}.

(46)

Now consider two linear maps

\Psi^{(1)}(f_{0},f_{1})=\sum_{w}\psi^{(1)}_{w}f_{w},~~~\Psi^{(2)}(f_{0},f_{1})=\sum_{w}\psi^{(2)}_{w}f_{w},

(47)

and an operator
R(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})=\sum\rho(w)\tau_{w}
, such that
\rho(w)\rho(w^{\prime})=\rho(w\shuffle w^{\prime})
. Examples of such an operator are
Z(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})
and
Z(\tau_{0},-\tau_{0}-\tau_{1})
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(R(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\Psi^{(1)}(f_{0},f_{1})\right)\shuffle\left(R(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\Psi^{(2)}(f_{0},f_{1})\right)\\
&=\sum_{w_{1},w_{2},w^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}_{2}}\rho(w_{1}\shuffle w_{2})\psi^{(1)}_{w_{1}w^{\prime}_{1}}\psi^{(2)}_{w_{2}w^{\prime}_{2}}\left(f_{w^{\prime}_{1}}\shuffle f_{w^{\prime}_{2}}\right)\\
&=\hskip 20.0ptR(\tau_{0},\tau_{1})\left(\Psi^{(1)}(f_{0},f_{1})\shuffle\Psi^{(2)}(f_{0},f_{1})\right).\end{split}

(48)

To see the last equality, focus on the specific term proportional to
\rho(z)f_{z^{\prime}}
. This will receive contributions from all
w_{1},w_{1}^{\prime},w_{2},w_{2}^{\prime}
such that
z\in w_{1}\shuffle w_{2}
and
z^{\prime}\in w^{\prime}_{1}\shuffle w^{\prime}_{2}
.

Appendix C
AdS
Veneziano amplitude for KK-modes

The first curvature correction to the
AdS
Veneziano amplitude for general KK-modes was computed in
Wang:2025owf
. It is given by

\displaystyle A_{KK}^{(1)}

\displaystyle(S,T,N_{s},N_{t})=-J_{e}(S,T)\frac{6N_{s}(\Sigma-3)+6N_{t}(\Sigma-3)-5}{3(S+T)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(S(6N_{s}(\Sigma-3)+2\Sigma-3)+2T(3N_{s}(\Sigma-3)-\Sigma-1))J_{0}(S,T)}{3(S+T)^{2}}+\left(\frac{4\Sigma}{3}-\frac{S^{2}-4ST+T^{2}}{3(S+T)^{2}}\right)J_{01}(S,T)

\displaystyle+\frac{(2S(3N_{t}(\Sigma-3)-\Sigma-1)+T(6N_{t}(\Sigma-3)+2\Sigma-3))J_{1}(S,T)}{3(S+T)^{2}}+\left(\frac{4\Sigma}{3}-\frac{S^{2}-4ST+T^{2}}{3(S+T)^{2}}\right)J_{10}(S,T)

\displaystyle-\frac{2S(2\Sigma(S+T)+S+4T)J_{00}(S,T)}{3(S+T)^{2}}-\frac{2T(2\Sigma(S+T)+4S+T)J_{11}(S,T)}{3(S+T)^{2}}-J_{e}(S,T)\frac{3S^{3}+3S^{2}T}{3(S+T)^{2}}\zeta(3)

\displaystyle+S\left(\frac{S}{S+T}+2\right)J_{001}(S,T)+S\left(\frac{S}{S+T}+2\right)J_{010}(S,T)+\frac{T(2S+3T)J_{101}(S,T)}{S+T}+\frac{T(2S+3T)J_{110}(S,T)}{S+T}

\displaystyle+\frac{2S^{2}J_{100}(S,T)}{S+T}-\frac{4S^{2}J_{000}(S,T)}{S+T}+\frac{2T^{2}J_{011}(S,T)}{S+T}-\frac{4T^{2}J_{111}(S,T)}{S+T}.

(49)

Here
S
and
T
are the usual
AdS
Mandelstam variables, while
N_{s}
and
N_{t}
are the sphere Mandelstam variables, which are discretised. See
Wang:2025owf
for details. Furthermore
\Sigma=(p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4})/2
. The scattering of the lowest KK-mode corresponds to
p_{i}=2
and
N_{s}=N_{t}=-1/3
.

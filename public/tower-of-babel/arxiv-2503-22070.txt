Quantum Quasi-neutral Limits and Isothermal Euler Equations
Immanuel Ben-Porat, Gui-Qiang G. Chen, Difan Yuan

Abstract
. We provide a rigorous justification of the semiclassical quasi-neutral and the quantum many-body limits to the isothermal Euler equations. We consider the nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson-Boltzmann system under a quasi-neutral scaling and establish the convergence of its solutions to the isothermal Euler equations. Different from the previous results that dealt with the linear Poisson equations, the system under our consideration accounts for the exponential nonlinearity in the potential. A modulated energy method is adopted, allowing us to derive the stability estimates and asymptotics. Furthermore, we focus our analysis on the many-body quantum problem via the von Neumann equation and establish a mean-field limit in one dimension by using Serfaty’s functional inequalities, and thus connecting the quantum many-body dynamics with the macroscopic hydrodynamic equations. A refined analysis of the quasi-neutral scaling for the massless systems is presented, and the well-posedness of the underlying quantum dynamics is established. Moreover, the construction of general admissible initial data is obtained. Our results provide a rigorous mathematical analysis for the derivation of quantum hydrodynamic models and their limits, contributing to the broader understanding of interactions between quantum mechanics and compressible fluid dynamics.

Introduction

We are concerned with the rigorous justification of the semi-classical and
quantum many-body quasi-neutral limits to the macroscopic systems of nonlinear partial differential equations. Consider the following
d
-dimensional (
d
-D)

Schrödinger-Poisson-Boltzmann system with quasi-neutral scaling
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}+V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar},\qquad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
-\varepsilon\Delta V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}=\left|\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\right|^{2}-e^{V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

The unknown
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,\cdot)
is a time-dependent element of the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H\coloneqq}L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=1
. The symbol
\hbar
represents the Planck constant and should be regarded as a small parameter. The 1-body quantum Hamiltonian
H_{\varepsilon,\hbar}
is the differential
operator given by

H_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\coloneqq-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta+V_{\varepsilon,\hbar},

so that (
1.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}=H_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
-\varepsilon\Delta V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}=\left|\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\right|^{2}-m_{\varepsilon,\hbar},\qquad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}|_{t=0}=\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}^{\mathrm{in}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{cases}

where
m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\coloneqq e^{V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}}
.

Notice that this system is a variant of the much more well studied Schrödinger-Poisson system:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}+V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar},\qquad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
-\varepsilon\Delta V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}=\left|\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\right|^{2}-1,&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

The fundamental difference between (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) is encapsulated
in the exponential nonlinearity in system (
1.1
),
i.e.
, from the fact that the dynamics are coupled with the so called
Poisson-Boltzman
equation, instead of the
linear Poisson
equation. This nonlinearity is the source of several mathematical obstructions: for instance, while the well-posedness theory of the linear Poisson equation:

-\Delta V=h-1

is classical even for
h
a probability measure, a
well-posedness theory has been developed only relatively recently for the Poisson-Boltzman
equation (see
[
6
,
15
]
):

-\Delta V=h-e^{V},

where
h\in L^{p}
for arbitrary
p>1
and dimension
d\geq 1
.
Thus, the classical well-posedness results for the Schrödinger-Poisson system (established for instance in
[
3
,
9
]
) are still inadequate for the present settings.
Therefore, one of our purposes in this paper is to prove the well-posedness of system (
1.1
).
For this purpose, we rely heavily on the elliptic PDE theory for the Poisson-Boltzmann equation
developed in
[
15
]
, where
h
is a given bounded function.
The well-posedness of system (
1.1
) is summarized in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
d\in\left\{2,3\right\}
.
Assume

(1.3)

\displaystyle\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\qquad\mbox{with $\,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(x)\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=1$}.

Then, for each fixed
\varepsilon>0
and
\hbar>0
, there exists a unique solution
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\in\mathrm{Lip}([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
of the Cauchy problem
:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\hbar\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)+V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x),\qquad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
-\varepsilon\Delta V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)=\left|\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\right|^{2}-e^{V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[2.84526pt]
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

As the main focus of this paper is on the semi-classical and quantum many-body limits,
the proof of Theorem
1.1
is postponed to §
5
.
Our first main result is the rigorous derivation of the isothermal Euler equations:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho u)=0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla\rho=0,\end{cases}

as a combined semi-classical quasi-neutral limit.
Before we state this main result, we remark that this problem has already been studied in the context of the usual Schrödinger-Poisson system (
1.2
) by Puel
[
25
]
for pure states
and later by Rosenzweig
[
27
]
for general states where the density operator formulation of the Schrödinger-Poisson system, also known as the Hartree equations, is considered.
Motivated by Rosenzweig’s approach, we make use of the modulated energy method.
However, the modulated energy has to be modified in comparison to
[
27
]
:
The modulated energy used in
[
27
]
is the quantum analogue of the quantity introduced in
[
4
]
, while the modulated energy studied in this paper is the quantum analogue
of the quantity introduced in
[
18
]
. Jüngel-Wang
[

]
obtained
combined semi-classical and quasi-neutral limits in the bipolar defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger-Poisson system
in the whole space. The electron and current density defined by the solution of the Schrödinger-Poisson system
converge to the solution of the compressible Euler equations with nonlinear pressure. We also refer to Golse and Paul
[
11
]
for the validity of the joint mean-field and classical limit of the quantum N-body dynamics leading to the pressureless Euler-Poisson system for factorized initial data whose first marginal has a monokinetic Wigner measure. Given a solution
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}

to system (
1.1
), consider the time-dependent trace-class
operator
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\coloneqq\left|\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\left\rangle\right\langle\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\right|
(here the bra-ket
\left|\psi\left\rangle\right\langle\psi\right|

is the rank-1 projection on
\mathfrak{H}
defined by
\varphi\mapsto\left\langle\psi,\varphi\right\rangle\psi
in the conventional notation).
The crux of the argument reduces to obtaining an evolution estimate on the following quantity, called
the modulated energy
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\sum^{d}_{j=1}\mathrm{tr}\big((i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}+u^{j})^{2}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(1.6)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\log\big(\frac{m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)}{\rho(t,x)}\big)-m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)+\rho(t,x)\Big)\,{\rm d}x.

The
total energy
, denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)
and defined explicitly in (
2.4
) below,
is a conserved quantity obtained from
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)
by plugging in
\rho=1
and
u=0
.
The quantum current
is defined by

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\coloneqq\hbar\,\mathrm{Im}\big(\overline{\psi}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\big).

Our first main result is described in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\rho,u)\in C^{1}([0,T);H^{s-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\times\left(C([0,T);H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\cap C^{1}([0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})\right)
be the solution of (
1.5
) with initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})\in\big(H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\cap\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\big)\times H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\rho_{0}>0
for some
s>\frac{d}{2}+1

and
T>0
. Let
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)
be the unique solution in
Theorem
1.1
with the initial data
\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(x)
satisfying (
1.3
).
Assume that there is some constant
\mathcal{F}_{0}

such that
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(0)\leq\mathcal{F}_{0}
uniformly in
(\varepsilon,\hbar)
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)
be given by (
1.6
). Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\leq e^{Ct}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(0)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\right)\qquad\text{ for all $t\in[0,T]$,}

where

C=C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}},\|\log(\rho)\|_{W_{t}^{1,\infty}H_{x}^{1}},\|\nabla(u\cdot\nabla\log(\rho))\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}},T,\mathcal{F}_{0}).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0

provided
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(0)\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0
, and

(\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar},J_{\varepsilon,\hbar})(t,\cdot)\rightharpoonup(\rho,\rho u)(t,\cdot)

for the narrow topology of signed Radon measures on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, as
\varepsilon+\hbar\rightarrow 0
locally uniformly in time.

The initial data
\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}
can be constructed such that
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(0)\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0
. We will elaborate on this in §
4
.

The stability estimate and the implied weak convergence are valid for arbitrarily large-time intervals.
However, the existence and uniqueness of a smooth solution to the isothermal Euler equations are known
to be true only for a short-time interval.

Our second main result concerns the
N
-body problem. Given a configuration
X_{N}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{T}^{N},
denote by
\mu_{X_{N}}
the empirical measure centered at
X_{N}
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\mu_{X_{N}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{j=1}\delta_{x_{j}}.

In variance with the quasi-neutral quantum many-body limit established in
[
27
]
,
our main result in this context is limited to the 1-D settings.
Let
V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}
be the solution to the equation:

-\varepsilon\Delta V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}=\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}\qquad\mbox{with $\,m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}=e^{V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}}$}.

The
N
-body quantum Hamiltonian is the differential operator acting
on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes N}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
given
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(X_{N})

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\sum^{N}_{k=1}\Delta_{x_{k}}+N\int_{\mathbb{T}}V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\,+\frac{N}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}K(x-y)(\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}})^{\otimes 2}({\rm d}x{\rm d}y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathscr{K}_{N,\hbar}+\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}+\mathscr{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}.

Here,
K
designates the Green function of the Laplacian. In 1-D, the Green function admits the explicit formula
K(x)=\frac{x^{2}-\left|x\right|}{2}
. Note that the terms

\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}
and
\mathscr{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}
are viewed as a multiplication operator
in the variable
X_{N}
. The Cauchy problem for the
N
-body von Neumann equation is given by

(1.7)

\begin{cases}i\hbar\partial_{t}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)=\left[\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N},R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)\right],\\[2.84526pt]
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}|_{t=0}=R^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}.\end{cases}

The unknown
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}
is a time-dependent symmetric density operator,
and
[\,\cdot,\cdot\,]
designates the commutator defined by
[A,B]\coloneqq AB-BA

for any given operators
A
and
B
. By a
density operator
,
we mean a bounded operator
R
such that
R
is self-adjoint non-negative (

)
and
\mathrm{tr}(R)=1
. By a
symmetric operator
on
\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes N}

we mean an operator
R_{N}
such that, for all permutations
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}

(

is the symmetric group on
N

\displaystyle U_{\sigma}R_{N}U_{\sigma}^{\ast}=R_{N},

where
U_{\sigma}
is the operator on
\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes N}
defined by

\displaystyle(U_{\sigma}\Psi_{N})(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\coloneqq\Psi_{N}(x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma^{-1}(N)})\qquad\text{ for any }\Psi_{N}\in\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes N}.

We denote the class of density operators on
\mathfrak{H}
by
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{H})
and the class of symmetric density operators on

\mathcal{D}_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes N})

If

,

\rho_{N}(X_{N})\coloneqq k(X_{N},X_{N})
.
It is well known that
\rho_{N}
is a symmetric probability density on

(see the footnote pp. 61–62 in

for more details).
We call

associated with

.
Hereafter, we denote by

the density associated with the operator

.
With these definition, we can define the following modulated energy:

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\,\frac{1}{2N}\sum^{N}_{j=1}\mathrm{tr}\big((i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}+u(t,x_{j}))^{2}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}K(x-y)(\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}})^{\otimes 2}({\rm d}x{\rm d}y)\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t,X_{N})\,{\rm d}X_{N}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big(m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(t,x)\log\big(\frac{m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(t,x)}{\rho(t,x)}\big)-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(t,x)+\rho(t,x)\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t,X_{N})\,{\rm d}X_{N}.

The total energy of the system is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)
(see (

) below).
We can also define an

(see Definition

Let
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N:1}(t)
be its first marginal density (see Definition

Denote the density function and the current of

by
\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N:1}(t,\cdot)

, respectively.

A curious feature of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is that
it exploits the commutator estimates of Serfaty
[
28
]
.
These commutator estimates also played a decisive role in the derivation of the incompressible Euler equations
as a mean-field quasi-neutral limit in
[
20
]
or as a quantum many-body quasi-neutral limit in
[
27
]
.
However, this approach, which is a reminiscent of the
renormalized energy
method,
is absent from the literature on the mean-field quasi-neutral limit of massless Vlasov-Poisson-like equations;
in this sense, it reflects a novelty of the method.
The constraint that
\varepsilon(N)
vanishes sufficiently slowly in

is natural.
Note, however, that in our setting, the vanishing rate of
\varepsilon
is logarithmic in

,
whereas it is polynomial in

in the usual quasi-neutral limit (considered, for example, in
[

]
). This slower decay rate emerges from the

bound on

in the argument.

To put our results in an appropriate context, we close this introduction by providing
a brief overview of the classical quasi-neutral limit. There has been an extensive study of the classical
quasi-neutral limit from the Vlasov-Poisson equations with massless electrons (VPME),
also known as the Vlasov-Poisson system for ions, that is a mathematical model from plasma physics,
which can be described
through the following kinetic equations:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla V\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f=0,\\[2.84526pt]
-\Delta V=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi-e^{V},\end{cases}

with
f(0,x,\xi)=f^{0}\geq 0
and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{0}(x,\xi)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}\xi=1
.
The unknown
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is a time-dependent probability density
f(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This system is a variant of the well-known Vlasov-Poisson (VP) system that reads

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla V\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f=0,\\[2.84526pt]
-\Delta V=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi-1,\end{cases}

with
f(0,x,\xi)=f^{0}\geq 0
and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{0}(x,\xi)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}\xi=1
.

As before, the notable difference between (
1.8
) and
(
1.9
) is reflected in the fact that the Poisson equation
coupled to the Vlasov equation is nonlinear in (
1.8
), where it is linear and explicitly
solvable in (
1.9
).
The variance between (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) is demonstrated
in a decisive manner already at the level of well-posedness: the well-posedness theory
on the entire space and the periodic case of the Vlasov-Poisson system
has been established in the 1990s in Lions-Perthame
[
22
]

and Batt-Rein
[
1
]
, respectively;
also see
[
16
]
for an overview of recent developments in the well-posedness theory of
the Vlasov equations of this type.
Only much later has the question of existence and uniqueness for the case of
the VPME system (
1.8
) has been settled: the existence of global weak solutions has been proved in
[
19
]
in the periodic 1-D case.
Later, global well-posedness is proved in the torus in
[
15
]
.
Endowed with this well-posedness, it is a natural question to consider hydrodynamical limits
associated with this system, particularly the quasi-neutral limit.
As was done in the quantum regime considered before,
we write the VP system and the VPME system with quasi-neutral scaling.
The VPME system with quasi-neutral scaling reads

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{\varepsilon}+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\varepsilon}-\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f_{\varepsilon}=0,\\[2.84526pt]
-\varepsilon\Delta V_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi-e^{V_{\varepsilon}},\\
\end{cases}

with
f_{\varepsilon}(0,x,\xi)=f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\geq 0
and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x,\xi)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}\xi=1,

while the VP system with quasi-neutral scaling reads

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{\varepsilon}+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\varepsilon}-\nabla V_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f_{\varepsilon}=0,\\[2.84526pt]
-\varepsilon\Delta V_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi-1,\\
\end{cases}

with
f_{\varepsilon}(0,x,\xi)=f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\geq 0

and
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x,\xi)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}\xi=1.

In the classical quasi-neutral limit, one seeks to study the convergence of
f_{\varepsilon}

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
Formal considerations suggest, as the limit system, the incompressible Euler equations in the 2-D VP case
and the isothermal Euler equations in the VPME case.
More precisely, given a solution
f_{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)
to system (
1.10
)
or (
1.11
), consider the density

(1.12)

\displaystyle\rho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi

and the current

(1.13)

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi f_{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)\,{\rm d}\xi.

Then, in the VPME case, one expects the limit system to be the isothermal Euler equations (
1.5
)
in the sense that the convergences:
\rho_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\rightarrow\rho(t,\cdot)

and
J_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\rightarrow(\rho u)(t,\cdot)
is propagated in time.
In the VP case, the expected limit system is the incompressible Euler equations:

(1.14)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho=1,\\
\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla_{x}u+\nabla_{x}p=0,\\
\mathrm{div}\,u=0.\end{cases}

Therefore, the quasi-neutral limit offers a derivation of fluid equations from kinetic equations.

The quasi-neutral limit has been studied in Han-Kwan

As already explained, this method will also play an important role in the present work.

Higher dimensions have been studied by Griffin-Pickering-Iacobelli

for mollified empirical measures, with a mollification

Theorem

2.
The 1-Body Semi-Classical Limit

This section concerns the derivation of the isothermal Euler equations:

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho u)=0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla\rho=0,\end{cases}

as a limit of (
1.1
) when
\varepsilon+\hbar\rightarrow 0
.
Observe that an alternative formulation of (

is

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\log\rho+\mathrm{div}\,u+u\cdot\nabla\log\rho=0,\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla_{x}u+\nabla\log\rho=0.\end{cases}

The well-posedness theory of the isothermal Euler equations is summarized in the following theorem,
which can be found in
[
23
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\rho_{0},u_{0})\in(H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))^{2}

for some
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
,
with
\rho_{0}(x)>0
for
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Then there exists some
T_{\ast}>0
such that there exists a unique classical
solution
(\rho,u)\in C^{1}([0,T_{\ast});H^{s-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\times\left(C([0,T_{\ast});H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{\ast})\times\mathbb{T}^{d})\right)
of the Cauchy problem for system (
2.2
) with the initial data
:

(2.3)

(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x)).

Moreover,
\log\rho\in C([0,T_{\ast});H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{\ast})\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Remark 2.2
.

The well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (

)–(
2.3

[
18
]
on the whole space. However, the proof extends with no difficulties to the periodic settings considered here. See also

We briefly review

Lemma 2.3

(
[

]

Assume

and

Then there exist both a unique

with zero mean and
\hat{V}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
satisfying

-\Delta\tilde{V}=h-1,\quad-\Delta\hat{V}=1-e^{\hat{V}+\tilde{V}}.

Furthermore, the following estimates hold
:

(i)

For all
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{V}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq C_{\alpha,d}(1+\left\|h\right\|_{\infty}),

\displaystyle\|\hat{V}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq C_{\alpha,d}\exp\big(C_{\alpha,d}(1+\|h\|_{\frac{d+2}{d}})\big),

for some constant
C_{\alpha,d}>0.

(ii)

For all
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{5}]

and

\displaystyle\|\hat{V}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq C_{\alpha,d}\exp\exp\big(C_{\alpha,d}(1+\left\|h\right\|_{\frac{d+2}{d}})\big),

for some constant
C_{\alpha,d}>0.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
17
]
, Proposition 3.2)
.

Assume that
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
is a probability measure. Then there exist both a unique
\tilde{V}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
with zero mean and
\hat{V}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
satisfying

\displaystyle-\tilde{V}^{\prime\prime}=\rho-1,\quad-\hat{V}^{\prime\prime}=1-e^{\tilde{V}+\hat{V}}.

Moreover, the following
estimate holds
:

\displaystyle\|\hat{V}^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq 1.

Remark 2.5
.

It can be checked directly that, subject to the assumptions
of
Lemma
2.3
or
2.4
,
the function

is a probability density, i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{\tilde{V}(x)+\hat{V}(x)}\,{\rm d}x=1
.

Remark 2.6
.

According to
Lemma 2.1 in
[

]
, given

,
there exists a unique solution
V\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
to the problem
:

-\Delta V=h-e^{V}
.
Therefore, if
h
and
(\tilde{V},\hat{V})
are as in

, we can represent the unique solution

as
V=\tilde{V}+\hat{V}
. The same remark applies when
d=1

and
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})
is a probability measure.
Hereafter, we denote
V\coloneqq\tilde{V}+\hat{V}
, where
\tilde{V}
and
\hat{V}
are the solutions
guaranteed by
Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, respectively.

Remark 2.7
.

If we further assume that
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
in

2.3
,
by the Schauder estimates and by a standard iteration argument,
then we see that

with the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|V\right\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\leq C

for some

Recall that the modulated energy is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\coloneqq\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\sum^{d}_{j=1}\mathrm{tr}\big((i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}+u_{j})^{2}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)}_{\coloneqq\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)}+\underbrace{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.}_{\coloneqq\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\log\big(\frac{m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)}{\rho(t,x)}\big)-m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)+\rho(t,x)\Big)\,{\rm d}x

and the total energy is given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\right)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x.

Remark 2.8
.

It is classical that, given probability densities
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, the associated relative entropy is non-negative
:

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mu\log\left(\frac{\mu}{\nu}\right)\,{\rm d}x\geq 0
.
This explains why the last terms in the definitions of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)

and
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)
are non-negative and, as a result,
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\geq 0
.

Remark 2.9
.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|(i\hbar\nabla+u)\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\right|^{2}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x

and that
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)
is determined by the Cauchy problem for
the
Hartree-type system
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\hbar\partial_{t}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)=[H_{\varepsilon,\hbar},R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)],\\[1.42262pt]
-\varepsilon\Delta V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}=\left|\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\right|^{2}-e^{V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}},\\[1.42262pt]
\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}|_{t=0}=\psi_{\varepsilon,\hbar}^{\mathrm{in}}.\end{cases}

It is instructive to invoke the Hartree formulation and the trace, since it clarifies the underlying algebraic structure.

Before proving the stability estimate for
\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)

as stated in Theorem
1.2
, we observe that the density is governed by an evolution equation
and the conservation of the total energy
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)
.
In the forthcoming calculation, we frequently use the anticommutator which is denoted
\vee

and defined by
A\vee B\coloneqq AB+BA
for any given operators
A,B
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)
and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,\cdot)
be as in
Theorem
1.2
. Then

(2.5)

\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}+\mathrm{div}\,J_{\varepsilon,\hbar}=0.

Proof.

For any
a\in C(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{T}^{d}),
we compute

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}a(x)\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathrm{tr}\big(aR_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)=\frac{1}{i\hbar}\mathrm{tr}\big(a[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta+V_{\varepsilon,\hbar},R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)]\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\mathrm{tr}\big([-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta,a]R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)=\frac{1}{2}\underset{k}{\sum}\mathrm{tr}\big([-i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}}\vee\partial_{x_{k}},a]R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\underset{k}{\sum}\mathrm{tr}\big((-i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}})\vee(\partial_{x_{k}}a)\,R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla a(x)\cdot J_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x,

where we have used the equations in Remark
2.9
.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)
be as in
Theorem

1.2
. Then
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)=0.

Proof.

We first compute

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathrm{tr}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i\hbar}\mathrm{tr}\big(-\hbar^{2}\Delta[-\frac{1}{2}\hbar^{2}\Delta+V_{\varepsilon,\hbar},R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)]\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\mathrm{tr}\big([-\frac{1}{2}\hbar^{2}\Delta+V_{\varepsilon,\hbar},-\hbar^{2}\Delta]R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)=\frac{i}{\hbar}\mathrm{tr}\big([V_{\varepsilon,\hbar},-\hbar^{2}\Delta]R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big),

where the equations in Remark
2.9
have been used.

The commutator can be computed as

\displaystyle\left[V_{\varepsilon,\hbar},-\hbar^{2}\Delta\right]=\underset{k}{\sum}(-i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}})\vee\left[V_{\varepsilon,\hbar},-i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}}\right]=\underset{k}{\sum}(-i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}})\vee(i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}}V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}),

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathrm{tr}\left(-\hbar^{2}\Delta R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\hbar}\underset{k}{\sum}\mathrm{tr}\big((-i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}})\vee(i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}}V_{\varepsilon,\hbar})R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\underset{k}{\sum}\mathrm{tr}\big((-i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}})\vee(-\partial_{x_{k}}V_{\varepsilon,\hbar})R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\cdot J_{\varepsilon,\hbar})(t,x)\,{\rm d}x.

Define

G(V_{\varepsilon,\hbar})=V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}e^{V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}}-e^{V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}}.

Then it follows from (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\cdot J_{\varepsilon,h}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathrm{div}\,J_{\varepsilon,h}\cdot V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\,{\rm d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}\cdot V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\cdot V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}}\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\cdot V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}G^{\prime}(V_{\varepsilon,\hbar})\partial_{t}V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\,{\rm d}x

(2.7)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}G(V_{\varepsilon,\hbar})\,{\rm d}x.

Notice that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)}\,{\rm d}x=0.

Then, combining (
2.6
) with (
2.7
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.
The proof is divided into five steps.

1

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\big(m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\log\left(1/\rho\right)\big)(t,x)+\rho(t,x)\Big)\,{\rm d}x.

Therefore, by the conservation of energy (Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\underset{j}{\sum}\mathrm{tr}\left((i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}})\vee u^{j}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\left|u\right|^{2})(t,x)\,{\rm d}x

2

\displaystyle I^{1}(t)+I^{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\underset{j}{\sum}\mathrm{tr}\big((i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}+u^{j})\vee(\partial_{t}u^{j}+(u\cdot\nabla u)^{j})R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\underset{j,k}{\sum}\mathrm{tr}\big((i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}+u^{j})\vee((i\hbar\partial_{x_{k}}+u^{k})\vee(\partial_{x_{k}}u^{j}))R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)

(2.8)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\,\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\cdot u(t,x)\,{\rm d}x.

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\sum_{j}\mathrm{tr}\big(i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}\vee u^{j}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)+\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathrm{tr}\big(|u|^{2}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=(u^{j}+i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}})\vee\big(\partial_{t}u^{j}+\underset{k}{\sum}u^{k}\partial_{x_{k}}u^{j}\big)+(u^{j}+i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}})\vee\big((\frac{i}{\hbar}[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta,\cdot\,]-\underset{k}{\sum}u^{k}\partial_{x_{k}})u^{j}\big).

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\,\nabla V_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)\cdot u(t,x)\,{\rm d}x

5

To prove the convergence of

Lemma 2.12

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let

. Then

\displaystyle\|\rho-m\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\log\big(\frac{m(x)}{\rho(x)}\big)m(x)\,{\rm d}x}.

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\|m_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,\cdot)-\rho(t,\cdot)\right\|_{1}\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0}.

Thus, by the triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\|\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,\cdot)-\rho(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0}.

The convergence of

is established as follows:
Given a Lipschitz vector field

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big(J_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)-\rho(t,x)u(t,x)\big)b(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)-\rho(t,x)\big)u(t,x)b(x)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big(J_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)-\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)u(t,x)\big)b(x)\,{\rm d}x.

The first integral on the right-hand side above tends to

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big(J_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)-\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t,x)u(t,x)\big)b(x)\,{\rm d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|b\right\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}(t)},

by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2

3.
The N-Body Problem

This section concerns the
N
-body quantum mean-field limit. Being
a triple limit, this limit is naturally more involved and imposes
additional considerations, namely the dimensionality and an asymptotic
relation between
\varepsilon
and
N
. Unless otherwise stated, throughout this section, we take
d=1
. Let us start by recalling the basic notation from the theory of density operators.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
R_{N}\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes N})
.

(i)

For each
1\leq k\leq N,
define the
k
-th marginal of
R_{N}
, as the unique element
R_{N:k}\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k})
, such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}}(A_{k}R_{N:k})=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes N}}\big((A_{k}\otimes I^{\otimes(N-k)})R_{N}\big)

for all bounded operators
A_{k}
on
\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes k}
.

(ii)

The current of
R_{N}
, denoted by
J_{\hbar,N:1}
, is the unique signed Radon measure on
\mathbb{T}
such that, for all
a\in C(\mathbb{T})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}a(x)J_{\hbar,N:1}({\rm d}x)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\big(a\lor(-i\hbar\partial_{x})R_{N:1}\big).

Recall that the quantum
Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(X_{N})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\sum^{N}_{k=1}\Delta_{x_{k}}+N\int_{\mathbb{T}}V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\frac{N}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}K(x-y)(\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}})^{\otimes 2}({\rm d}x{\rm d}y)

\displaystyle=:\mathscr{K}_{N,\hbar}+\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}+\mathscr{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}},

where

(3.1)

-\varepsilon V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}^{\prime\prime}=\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}\qquad\mbox{with $\,\,m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}=e^{V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}}$}.

The associated von Neumann equation is

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\hbar\partial_{t}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}=\left[\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N},R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}\right],\\[2.84526pt]
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0)=R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}^{\mathrm{in}}.\end{cases}

Recall that the modulated energy is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2N}\sum^{N}_{j=1}\mathrm{tr}\big((i\hbar\partial_{x_{j}}+u(t,x_{j}))^{2}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)\big)}_{\coloneqq\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big(m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(t,x)\log(\frac{m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(t,x)}{\rho(t,x)})-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(t,x)+\rho(t,x)\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t,X_{N})\,{\rm d}X_{N}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}K(x-y)(\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}})^{\otimes 2}({\rm d}x{\rm d}y)\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t,X_{N})\,{\rm d}X_{N}}_{\coloneqq\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)},

and the total energy is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2N}\mathrm{tr}\big(-\hbar^{2}\sum^{N}_{j=1}\Delta_{x_{j}}R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)\big)

\displaystyle\,\,+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)\,\,{\rm d}x\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t,X_{N})\,{\rm d}X_{N}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\,\,+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}K(x-y)(\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}})^{\otimes 2}({\rm d}x{\rm d}y)\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t,X_{N})\,{\rm d}X_{N}.

As in the case of 1-body dynamics, the conservation of energy holds, and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N:1}
evolves according to an evolution equation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)
be as in
Theorem

1.3
. Then

(i)

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(t)=0
,

(ii)

\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N:1}+\partial_{x}J_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N:1}=0.

The proof is omitted, since it is similar to the previous considerations.
In variance with the quasi-neutral limit for VP, the quasi-neutral limit
for VPME necessitates an
L^{\infty}
–bound on
e^{V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}},

which is not uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon
.
Therefore, in order to derive the mean-field limit, the
vanishing rate of
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(N)
with respect to
N
is slower
than in the usual quasi-neutral mean-field limit. This bound is included in
the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3

(
[
17
]
, Proposition 3.2)
.

Let
(\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}},\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}})
be the solution of the system
:

(3.3)

\begin{cases}-\varepsilon\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}^{\prime\prime}=\mu_{X_{N}}-1,\\[1.42262pt]
-\varepsilon\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}^{\prime\prime}=1-e^{\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}+\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}}\end{cases}

ensured by
Lemma
2.4
.
Then
V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}=\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}+\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}
satisfies the estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

We also make use of the following lemma that provides the stability with respect to the perturbations by measures:

Lemma 3.4

(
[
17
]
, Proposition 3.2)
.

Let
h_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T})\,

(

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\varepsilon\tilde{V}^{\prime\prime}_{i}=h_{i}-1,\\[1.42262pt]
-\varepsilon\hat{V}^{\prime\prime}_{i}=1-e^{\tilde{V}_{i}+\hat{V}_{i}}.\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle\|\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{1}-\tilde{V}^{\prime}_{2}\|_{2}+4\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\|\hat{V}^{\prime}_{1}-\hat{V}^{\prime}_{2}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}W_{1}(h_{1},h_{2}),

where

An additional significant ingredient in the renormalization argument is the
following asymptotic positivity, coercivity inequality, and commutator estimates, all of which are fundamental discoveries due to
[
28

. We state these inequalities only for the 1-D periodic case, although the results hold in greater generality. Denote

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(X_{N},\mu)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}K(x-y)(\mu_{X_{N}}-\mu)^{\otimes 2}({\rm d}x{\rm d}y)

so that

\displaystyle\mathscr{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}=\frac{N}{2\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}(X_{N},m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}).

Lemma 3.5

(
[
28
]
, Corollary 3.5)
.

Let

and

Then

(i)

\mathscr{E}(X_{N},\mu)+\frac{1+\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty}}{N^{2}}\geq 0
;

(ii)

There are some
\lambda,C>0
such that, for all
\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\mu_{X_{N}}-\mu\right)\varphi(x)\,{\rm d}x\bigg|\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{\infty}N^{-\lambda}+\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{2}\Big(\mathscr{E}(X_{N},\mu)+\frac{1+\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty}}{N^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Lemma 3.6

(
[
28
]
, Proposition 1.1)
.

Let the assumptions of
Lemma
3.5
hold, and assume further that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}\setminus\triangle}\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)K^{\prime}(x-y)\left(\mu_{N}-\mu\right)^{\otimes 2}({\rm d}x{\rm d}y)\bigg|\leq C\Big(\mathscr{E}(X_{N},\mu)+\frac{1+\left\|\mu\right\|_{\infty}}{N^{2}}\Big),

where

.

The significance of the above-mentioned results for what concerns the classical problem of deriving a Vlasov-like equation
as a mean-field limit is beyond the scope of this paper. We refer to

]
(and especially Chapter 2)
for an exhaustive discussion. The following simple lemma concerns the Lipschitz continuity of

with respect to the configuration

Lemma 3.7

Let

(3.4)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}^{\prime\prime}=\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}\qquad\mbox{with $\,\,m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}=e^{V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}}$}.

Then the following statements hold

(i)

The functions

and
X_{N}\mapsto m_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)

(ii)

For any fixed

x\mapsto V_{X_{N}}(x)
and

Proof.

This can be seen as follows:

(i). Let

X_{N}=(x_{1}^{0},\cdots,x_{j},\cdots,x_{N}^{0})
and
Y_{N}=(x_{1}^{0},\cdots,y_{j},\cdots,x_{N}^{0})
,
where
x_{k}^{0}
is fixed for any
k\neq j
. Thanks to the Sobolev embedding, we have

\displaystyle\left|V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)-V_{\varepsilon,Y_{N}}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}-\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,Y_{N}}\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}-\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,Y_{N}}\|_{\infty}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}-\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,Y_{N}}\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}-\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,Y_{N}}\|_{H^{1}}.

Clearly,
x_{j}\mapsto\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)
is Lipschitz with the estimate:

|\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)-\tilde{V}_{\varepsilon,Y_{N}}(x)|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon N}|x_{j}-y_{j}|.

Moreover, by Lemma
3.4
, we have the estimate:

\displaystyle\|\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}-\hat{V}_{\varepsilon,Y_{N}}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{2}}W_{1}(\mu_{X_{N}},\mu_{Y_{N}})\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}N}|x_{j}-y_{j}|.

Together with inequality (
3.5
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|V_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}(x)-V_{\varepsilon,Y_{N}}(x)\right|\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}N}\left|x_{j}-y_{j}\right|\qquad\text{for all $x\in\mathbb{T}$}.

Consequently,
X_{N}\mapsto m_{X_{N}}(x)
is also Lipschitz uniformly in
x
.

(ii). This is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.4
, the fact that
x\mapsto\tilde{V}_{X_{N}}
is Lipschitz uniformly in
X_{N}
, and the decomposition
V_{X_{N}}=\tilde{V}_{X_{N}}+\hat{V}_{X_{N}}
.
∎

Our construction of the initial data is summarized in the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

\rho_{0}>0

\displaystyle\psi^{\mathrm{in}}_{\varepsilon,\hbar}\coloneqq\sqrt{e^{V_{0}}-\varepsilon\Delta V_{0}}e^{\frac{iU_{0}(x)}{\hbar}}.

Proof.

Clearly, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,
e^{V_{0}}-\varepsilon\Delta V_{0}
positive
and
\Delta V_{0}
has mean
0
, so that

To show that

, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\|V_{\varepsilon,X_{N},\eta}^{\prime}-V_{0}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0,{\rm d}X_{N})\leq\frac{5}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}W_{1}^{2}(\mu_{X_{N}},\rho_{\varepsilon})\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0,{\rm d}X_{N})+\eta^{2}\right).

Again, we invoke Remark
4.3
below to find that there is some
\Lambda>0
such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}W_{1}^{2}(\mu_{X_{N}},\rho_{\varepsilon})\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0,{\rm d}X_{N})=O(\frac{1}{N^{\Lambda}}).

If
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(N)
is chosen such that
\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}N^{\Lambda}}\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty,
and
\eta=\eta(\varepsilon)
such that
\frac{\eta(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\|V_{\varepsilon,X_{N},\eta}^{\prime}-V^{\prime}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0,\,{\rm d}X_{N})\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar+\frac{1}{N}\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0,

and hence

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}||V_{\varepsilon,X_{N},\eta}^{\prime}||_{2}^{2}\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0,\,{\rm d}X_{N})\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar+\frac{1}{N}\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

Therefore, for this choice of
\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0)=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\mathscr{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0,\,{\rm d}X_{N})\underset{\varepsilon+\hbar+\frac{1}{N}\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0,

because of the relation (see formula 1.29 in
[
28
]
):

\displaystyle\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\,\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\left\|V_{\varepsilon,X_{N},\eta}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0,\,{\rm d}X_{N})=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\mathscr{V}_{\varepsilon,X_{N}}\,\rho_{\varepsilon,\hbar,N}(0,\,{\rm d}X_{N}).

∎

Remark 4.3
.

The large-deviation-type inequality used
in (
4.4
)–(
4.5
) and its various extensions appear, for instance, in
[
7
]
.
Here is a short way to deduce this estimate from
Lemma
3.5
(ii)

for the specific choice
\rho_{0}\equiv 1
.
Indeed, integrating the inequality in
Lemma
3.5
(ii)
with respect to
X_{N}
, we obtain
that, for some constant
\lambda,C>0
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}W_{1}^{2}(\mu_{X_{N}},1)\,{\rm d}X_{N}\leq C\Big(N^{-2\lambda}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\mathcal{E}(X_{N},1)\,{\rm d}X_{N}+\frac{2}{N^{2}}\Big).

A direct calculation reveals that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X_{N},1)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}K(x_{i}-x_{j})-\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(x)\,{\rm d}x.

Furthermore, utilizing that
K(0)=0
and
K
is even, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}K(x_{i}-x_{j})\,{\rm d}X_{N}=\frac{N-1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(x)\,{\rm d}x,

which shows that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\mathcal{E}(X_{N},1)\,{\rm d}X_{N}\right|=\frac{\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(x)\,{\rm d}x\right|}{N}\leq\frac{1}{N}.

Substituting (
4.7
) into (
4.6
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}W_{1}^{2}(\mu_{X_{N}},1)\,{\rm d}X_{N}=O(\frac{1}{N^{\Lambda}})

with
\Lambda=\min\left\{2\lambda,1\right\}
.

5.
Well-Posedness Theory

This section is qualitative
in nature, and we take
\varepsilon=\hbar=1,
for simplicity. We start with the von Neumann equation (
1.7
). Recall Kato’s pertubation theory, which is the main
ingredient of studying the existence theory of linear equations of Schrödinger-type.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
30
]
, Theorem 6.4)
.

Let
T,D(T)\subset\mathfrak{H}
be a
(
essentially
)

self-adjoint operator and
S,D(S)\subset\mathfrak{H}
a symmetric
operator such that
D(T)\subset D(S)
.
Suppose that there exist
0<a<1
and
b>0

such that, for each
\varphi\in D(T),

(5.1)

\|S\varphi\|^{2}\leq a\|T\varphi\|^{2}+b\|\varphi\|^{2}.

Then
T+S
and
D(T+S)=D(T)
are
(
essentially
)
self-adjoint. In the case
when
T
is essentially self-adjoint,
D(\overline{T})\subset D(\overline{S})

and
\overline{T+S}=\overline{T}+\overline{S}
,
where
\overline{T}
and
\overline{S}
stand for the closures of
T
and
S
respectively.

The combination of Theorem
5.1
and Lemma
2.4

yields the following
conclusion (with the notation
\mathscr{H}_{N}=\mathscr{H}_{1,1,N}
):

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X_{N}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
, and let
(\tilde{V}_{X_{N}},\hat{V}_{X_{N}})
be the solution to the system
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\tilde{V}_{X_{N}}^{\prime\prime}=\mu_{X_{N}}-1,\\[1.42262pt]
-\hat{V}_{X_{N}}^{\prime\prime}=1-e^{\tilde{V}_{X_{N}}+\hat{V}_{X_{N}}},\end{cases}

guaranteed by
Lemma
2.4
. Then
\mathscr{H}_{N}

is self-adjoint on
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.
Consequently, if
R^{\mathrm{in}}_{N}\in\mathcal{D}_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes N})
is such that

\mathrm{tr}\left((-\Delta_{N})^{2}R^{\mathrm{in}}_{N}\right)<\infty
,
then there exists a unique solution of the Cauchy problem

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}R_{N}(t)=\left[\mathscr{H}_{N},R_{N}(t)\right],\\[2.84526pt]
R_{N}(0)=R^{\mathrm{in}}_{N}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall the notation:

\displaystyle\mathscr{V}_{X_{N}}\coloneqq\frac{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}K(x-y)(\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{X_{N}})^{\otimes 2}({\rm d}x{\rm d}y)\qquad\,\mbox{with $\,\,m_{X_{N}}=e^{V_{X_{N}}}$},

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{X_{N}}=N\int_{\mathbb{T}}V_{X_{N}}(x)m_{X_{N}}(x)\,{\rm d}x.

Also, recall that
\|\tilde{V}_{X_{N}}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.4
,
we see that
\left\|m_{X_{N}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C

for some effective constant
C>0
so that

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{V}_{X_{N}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N}{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}K\star(\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{X_{N}})(\mu_{X_{N}}-m_{X_{N}})({\rm d}x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\big(C+\left\|K\star m_{X_{N}}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|m_{X_{N}}\right\|_{1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq N\big(C+\left\|K\right\|_{\infty}\left\|m_{X_{N}}\right\|_{1}^{2}\big)=N(C+1),

where
K
stands for the Green function of the Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}
.
The estimate is uniform in
X_{N}
so that

X_{N}\mapsto\mathscr{V}_{X_{N}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.
From the same considerations,
X_{N}\mapsto\mathscr{I}_{X_{N}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.
That
\mathscr{H}_{N}
is self-adjoint follows from Theorem
5.1
.
The existence is now immediate via Stone’s theorem, and the uniqueness follows by linearity of the equation.
∎

Next, we are concerned with the existence and uniqueness theory for
system (
1.1
).
As already remarked, the case of the Schrödinger-Poisson system (
1.2
) with
-\Delta V=\rho-1

is classical; see, for instance,
[
3
,
9
]
.
We plan to first decouple the equations and then apply a fixed-point argument.
For this to succeed, we need the following lemma, showing that
\nabla\hat{V}

is stable with respect to
\tilde{V}
in the
L^{2}
–norm, which is crucial
in order to be able to control properly the terms contributed by the
nonlinearity:

Lemma 5.3

(
[
15
]
, Lemma 3.9)
.

Let
d\in\{2,3\}
and
h_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

for
i=1,2
. Consider the system
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{V}_{i}=h_{i}-1,\\[1.42262pt]
-\Delta\hat{V}_{i}=1-e^{\tilde{V}_{i}+\hat{V}_{i}}.\end{cases}

Then

\|\nabla\hat{V}_{1}-\nabla\hat{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\|\tilde{V}_{1}-\tilde{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2},

where

C=C\big(c_{d}(\underset{i}{\max}\|\tilde{V}_{i}\|_{\infty}+\underset{i}{\max}\|\hat{V}_{i}\|_{\infty})\big)
,
and
c_{d}
is a dimensional constant.

As a corollary, we have the following lemma, which will be useful in several instances in the sequel:

Lemma 5.4
.

For each
r\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
, let
\chi_{r}\coloneqq\frac{1}{r^{d}}\chi(\frac{x}{r})
, where
\chi\geq 0
is a smooth radially symmetric function on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\chi)\subset B(0,1)
and
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi(x)\,{\rm d}x=1
.
With the same hypothesis and notation of
Lemma
5.3
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{r}\star(V_{1}-V_{2})\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\left\|h_{1}-h_{2}\right\|_{1},

where
C=C(r,d,\underset{i}{\max}\left\|h_{i}\right\|_{\infty})
.

Proof.

Notice first that

\displaystyle\|\chi_{r}\star(V_{1}-V_{2})\|_{\infty}\leq\|\chi_{r}\star(\tilde{V}_{1}-\tilde{V}_{2})\|_{\infty}+\|\chi_{r}\star(\hat{V}_{1}-\hat{V}_{2})\|_{\infty}.

We first have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{r}\star(\tilde{V}_{1}-\tilde{V}_{2})\|_{\infty}\leq\|\chi_{r}\star K\|_{\infty}\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{1}\leq C_{r,d}\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{1}.

Furthermore, Lemma
2.3
implies that

\displaystyle\|\chi_{r}\star(\hat{V}_{1}-\hat{V}_{2})\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|K\|_{2}^{2}\|e^{\hat{V}_{1}+\tilde{V}_{1}}-e^{\hat{V}_{2}+\tilde{V}_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|K\|_{2}^{2}\|e^{\hat{V}_{1}+\tilde{V}_{1}}-e^{\hat{V}_{1}+\tilde{V}_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|K\|_{2}^{2}\|e^{\hat{V}_{1}+\tilde{V}_{2}}-e^{\hat{V}_{2}+\tilde{V}_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e^{\hat{V}_{1}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|e^{\tilde{V}_{1}}-e^{\tilde{V}_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|e^{\tilde{V}_{2}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|e^{\hat{V}_{1}}-e^{\hat{V}_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\underset{i}{\max}\|h_{i}\|_{\infty}}\|e^{\tilde{V}_{1}}-e^{\tilde{V}_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|e^{\hat{V}_{1}}-e^{\hat{V}_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,\underset{i}{\max}\|h_{i}\|_{\infty}}\|\tilde{V}_{1}-\tilde{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\hat{V}_{1}-\hat{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2},

where the last inequality in (
5.4
) is due to the mean value theorem as applied
to the function:
x\mapsto e^{x}
.
The first term on the
right-hand side of (
5.4
) is

\displaystyle\|\tilde{V}_{1}-\tilde{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{1}^{2}.

Therefore, by the Poincaré inequality and Lemma
5.3
,
we have

\displaystyle\|\hat{V}_{1}-\hat{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}\|\nabla\hat{V}_{1}-\nabla\hat{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq C(\underset{i}{\max}\|h_{i}\|_{\infty},d)\|\tilde{V}_{1}-\tilde{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C(\underset{i}{\max}\|h_{i}\|_{\infty},d)\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{1}^{2}.

Gathering (
5.3
)–(
5.5
) yields the desired inequality.
∎

We need also to observe that the
L^{2}
–norm of
e^{V}
is bounded by means of the
L^{2}
–norm of
\rho
,
as encapsulated in the following lemma:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),
and let
V
be the unique solution of

\displaystyle-\Delta V(x)=\rho(x)-e^{V(x)}.

Then

\displaystyle\|e^{V}\|_{2}\leq\left\|\rho\right\|_{2}.

Proof.

Multiplying the equation by
e^{V}
and then integrating, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta V(x)e^{V(x)}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big(\rho(x)e^{V(x)}-e^{2V(x)}\big)\,{\rm d}x.

Integrating the left-hand side by parts, we recognize that

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta V(x)e^{V(x)}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla V(x)\right|^{2}{e^{V(x)}}\,{\rm d}x\geq 0.

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho(x)e^{V(x)}\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{2V(x)}\,{\rm d}x.

Using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\|e^{V}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|e^{V}\|_{2}\|\rho\|_{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

To prove the well-posedness of the Schrödinger-Poisson-Boltzmann system, we first study the Cauchy problem of the regularized equation. We recall the following existence and uniqueness result for a Schrödinger equation with a time-dependent potential (one can also consult
[
26
]
for similar results).

Lemma 5.6

(
[
8
]
, Theorem 2)
.

Let
V:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function such that

(i)

V\in C([0,T];C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
,

(ii)

\partial^{\alpha}_{x}V\in L^{\infty}([0,T];C(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Then the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi(t,x)+V(t,x)\psi(t,x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
\psi|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{cases}

has a unique solution
\psi\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

with
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}(x)\right|^{2}\ {\rm d}x=1
.
For each
r\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
, let
\chi_{r}\coloneqq\frac{1}{r^{d}}\chi(\frac{x}{r})
,
where
\chi\geq 0
is a smooth radially symmetric function on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\chi)\subset B(0,1)
and
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi(x)\ {\rm d}x=1
.
Then, for any
T>0
and
r\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
fixed, there exist a unique solution
\psi_{r}\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
to the regularized Cauchy problem
:

(5.6)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi_{r}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)+(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x),&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
-\Delta V_{r}(t,x)=(\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r}\right|^{2})(t,x)-e^{V_{r}(t,x)},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
\psi_{r}|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first regularize the equation in time and study the well-posedness of the resulting equation via a fixed-point argument. Afterwards, we apply a compactness argument with respect to the mollification in time.
We divide the proof into three steps:

1
.
Well-posedness for the time regularized system
. Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathfrak{X}}\coloneqq\left\{\psi\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\,:\,\left\|\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}=1\text{ for any }t\in[0,T]\right\}.

Fix a standard mollifier
\zeta_{\delta}
on
\mathbb{R}
. In this step, we prove that the time regularized system:

(5.7)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)+(\chi_{r}\star_{x}V_{r,\delta})(t,x)\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
-\Delta V_{r,\delta}(t,x)=(\zeta_{\delta}\star_{t}\chi_{r}\star_{x}\left|\psi_{r,\delta}\right|^{2})(t,x)-e^{V_{r,\delta}(t,x)},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
\psi_{r,\delta}|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\end{cases}

is well posed. For each fixed
\phi\in\mathfrak{X},
we consider the linear Schrödinger
equation with time dependent interaction

(5.8)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)+(\chi_{r}\star_{x}V_{r,\delta})(t,x)\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\
-\Delta V_{r,\delta}(t,x)=(\zeta_{\delta}\star_{t}\chi_{r}\star_{x}\left|\phi\right|^{2})(t,x)-e^{V_{r,\delta}(t,x)},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\
\psi_{r,\delta}|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

When there is no ambiguity, we omit
t
and
x
from
\star_{t}
and
\star_{x}
, respectively.
By Lemma
2.3
, for each
t\in(0,T]
,
there is a unique solution
V_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

to the problem

-\Delta V_{r,\delta}\left[\phi\right](t,x)=(\zeta_{\delta}\star\chi_{r}\star\left|\phi\right|^{2})(t,x)-e^{V_{r,\delta}\left[\phi\right](t,x)}.

By Remark
2.7
,
\chi_{r}\star V
verifies assumption (ii) of Theorem
5.6
.
In addition, we notice that
\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta}
is Lipschitz in time due to Lemma
5.4
applied with
h_{1}(t,\cdot)=(\zeta_{\delta}\star\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r,\delta}\right|^{2})(t,\cdot)
and
h_{2}(s,\cdot)=(\zeta_{\delta}\star\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r,\delta}\right|^{2})(s,\cdot)
for any
t,s\in[0,T]
.
Thus, assumption (i) of Theorem
5.6
is also satisfied.
Then Theorem
5.6
implies the existence and uniqueness of a solution
\Psi_{r,\delta}\in C^{1}\left([0,T];C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\right)
of the Cauchy problem for the linear equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)+(\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta}\left[\phi\right])(t,x)\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
\psi_{r,\delta}|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

We aim to prove that the operator:
\phi\mapsto\Psi_{r,\delta}[\phi]
is a contraction from
\mathfrak{X}
to
\mathfrak{X}
and thereby conclude via the
Banach fixed point theorem.
Since
\chi_{r}\ast V_{r,\delta}[\phi]
is real-valued, it is readily checked that the
L^{2}
–norm
of
\Psi_{r,\delta}[\phi]
is conserved,
i.e.,

\left\|\Psi_{r,\delta}[\phi](t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}=1
,
so that
\Psi_{r,\delta}[\phi]\in\mathfrak{X}
indeed.
Given
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathfrak{X}
, set
\Psi_{1}\coloneqq\Psi_{r,\delta}\left[\phi_{1}\right],\Psi_{2}\coloneqq\Psi_{r,\delta}\left[\phi_{2}\right]
,
and
V_{1}\coloneqq V_{r,\delta}\left[\phi_{1}\right],V_{2}\coloneqq V_{r,\delta}\left[\phi_{2}\right]
.
We compute

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Psi_{1}(t,\cdot)-\Psi_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{t}\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\big(\Psi_{2}(t,x)-\Psi_{1}(t,x)\big)\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big((\chi_{r}\star V_{1})(t,x)\Psi_{1}(t,x)-(\chi_{r}\star V_{2})(t,x)\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=:I+J.

Integrating by parts, we see that

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\big(\Psi_{2}(t,x)-\Psi_{1}(t,x)\big)\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla(\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2})\right|^{2}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x,

so that

I=\Re\Big(i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla(\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2})\right|^{2}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\Big)=0.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\big((\chi_{r}\star V_{1})(t,x)\Psi_{1}(t,x)-(\chi_{r}\star V_{2})(t,x)\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\chi_{r}\star V_{1})(t,x)\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Psi_{2}(t,x)(\chi_{r}\star(V_{1}-V_{2}))(t,x)\,\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\chi_{r}\star V_{1})(t,x)\left|\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(5.9)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Psi_{2}(t,x)(\chi_{r}\star(V_{1}-V_{2}))(t,x)\,\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x.

Clearly, one has

\Re\Big(i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}V_{1}(t,x)\left|\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)=0,

so it suffices to deal with the second integral on the right-hand side of (
5.9
).

Notice that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Psi_{2}(t,x)(\chi_{r}\star(V_{1}-V_{2}))(t,x)\overline{\big(\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)\big)}\,{\rm d}x

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\Psi_{2}\right|^{2}(t,x)\left|\chi_{r}\star(V_{1}-V_{2})\right|^{2}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\overline{\Psi_{1}(t,x)-\Psi_{2}(t,x)}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

In order to control the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.10
),
we invoke Lemma
5.4
with
h_{1}=(\zeta_{\delta}\star\chi_{r}\star\left|\phi_{1}\right|^{2})(t,x)
and
h_{2}=(\zeta_{\delta}\star\chi_{r}\star\left|\phi_{2}\right|^{2})(t,x)
to see that

\displaystyle\left\|(\chi_{r}\star(V_{1}-V_{2}))(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r,d}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\left|\phi_{1}(t,\cdot)-\phi_{2}(t,\cdot)\right|(\left|\phi_{1}(t,\cdot)\right|+\left|\phi_{2}(t,\cdot)\right|)\right\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r,d}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\phi_{1}(t,\cdot)-\phi_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}.

We have thus proved

(5.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\Psi_{2}\right|^{2}(t,x)\left|\chi_{r}\star(V_{1}-V_{2})\right|^{2}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\leq C_{r,d}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\phi_{1}(t,\cdot)-\phi_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}

for some constant
C_{r,d}>0.
Inequalities (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) entail

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi_{1}(t,\cdot)-\Psi_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r,d}t\underset{\tau\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\phi_{1}(\tau,\cdot)-\phi_{2}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Psi_{1}(\tau,\cdot)-\Psi_{2}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{r,d}T\underset{\tau\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\phi_{1}(\tau,\cdot)-\phi_{2}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\Psi_{1}(\tau,\cdot)-\Psi_{2}(\tau,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau.

As a result,

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\Psi_{1}(t,\cdot)-\Psi_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\leq C_{r,d}\sqrt{T}e^{\frac{T}{2}}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\phi_{1}(t,\cdot)-\phi_{2}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2},

which shows that, for
T=T_{r,d}
sufficiently small,

\phi\mapsto\Psi_{r,\delta}[\phi]
is a contraction, thereby ensuring the existence and uniqueness of a fixed point
\psi_{r,\delta}
.
Note that, by construction of
\Psi
,
we see that this solution belongs to
C^{1}([0,T];C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.
The global existence follows by a standard iteration argument together
with the conservation of the
L^{2}
–norm.

2
.
Compactness in
\delta
. In this step, we aim to remove the regularization in time, which will enable us to prove that the system:

(5.12)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi_{r}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)+(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
-\Delta V_{r}(t,x)=(\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,x)-e^{V_{r}(t,x)},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
\psi_{r}(0,x)=\psi^{\mathrm{in}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\end{cases}

is well-posed.
Consider the solution
\psi_{r,\delta}
of (
5.7
) constructed in Step 1. We obtain that,
for any
t,s\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)-\psi_{r,\delta}(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|t-s|\big(\|\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

(5.13)

\displaystyle+\|(\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})\psi_{r,\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+\|\Delta((\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})\psi_{r,\delta}))\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\big).

We start by propagating the Sobolev norms of
\psi_{r,\delta}
uniformly in
\delta
.

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Re\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta^{2}\partial_{t}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)\Delta^{2}\overline{\psi_{r,\delta}}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=-2\Re\Big(i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta^{2}\big(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)+(\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})(t,x)\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)\big)\Delta^{2}\overline{\psi_{r,\delta}}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x\Big).

Integration by parts reveals

\displaystyle\Re\Big(i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta^{2}\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)\Delta^{2}\overline{\psi_{r,\delta}}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x\Big)=\Re\Big(i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}\right|^{2}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x\Big)=0,

hence

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}=2\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta^{2}\big((\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})\psi_{r,\delta}\big)(t,x)\Delta^{2}\overline{\psi_{r,\delta}}(t,x)\ {\rm d}x\Big).

We can write

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}\big((\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})\psi_{r,\delta}\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{4}c_{k}\nabla^{k}(\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})\nabla^{4-k}\psi_{r,\delta}

for some constants
c_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
, so that

(5.14)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla^{k}(\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})\nabla^{4-k}\psi_{r,\delta}\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}\right|(t,x)\ {\rm d}x\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{4}I_{k}.

We proceed by estimating each one of
I_{k}
:

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|(\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})(t,x)\big|\,\big|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)\big|^{2}\ {\rm d}x\leq\big\|V_{r,\delta}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\nabla(\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})(t,x)\big|\,\big|\nabla\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,x)\big|\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\nabla V_{r,\delta}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\big(\big\|\nabla\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{2}^{2}+\big\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{2}\big),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\Delta(\chi_{r}\star V_{r,\delta})(t,x)\big|\,\big|(\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta})(t,x)\big|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Delta V_{r,\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\big(\left\|\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2}\big).

Using Lemma
2.3
, we see that

\displaystyle\left\|V_{r,\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{2,\alpha}_{x}}\leq C_{r,d}.

Thus, summarizing the above estimate, we have

\displaystyle I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}\leq C_{r,d}\big(\|\Delta\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\big).

In order to bound
I_{3}
and
I_{4}
, note that

\displaystyle-\Delta^{2}V_{r,\delta}=\zeta_{\delta}\star\Delta\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r,\delta}\right|^{2}-\Delta(e^{V_{r,\delta}})=\zeta_{\delta}\star\Delta\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r,\delta}\right|^{2}-e^{V_{r,\delta}}\left|\nabla V_{r,\delta}\right|^{2}-e^{V_{r,\delta}}\Delta V_{r,\delta},

and therefore Lemma
2.3
entails that

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta^{2}V_{r,\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq C_{r,d}.

Thus, we obtain the bounds

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r,d}\big(\|\nabla\psi_{r,\delta}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}\|_{2}^{2}\big),

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r,d}\big(1+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}\|_{2}^{2}\big).

To conclude, we have proved the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{4}I_{k}\leq C_{r,d}\big(1+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\big),

so that, in view of (
5.14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{r,d}\big(1+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\big),

which yields the estimate

(5.15)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{4}}^{2}\leq C

for some
C=C(r,d,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{4}})
.
Substituting (
5.15
) into (
5.13
) produces the inequality

(5.16)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{r,\delta}(t,\cdot)-\psi_{r,\delta}(s,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{2}}\leq C|t-s|,

where
C=C(r,d,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{4}})
.

3
.
Extraction of a solution
. By the Arzela-Ascoli theorem and (
5.16
), there exist a function
\psi_{r}\in C([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and a subsequence
\delta_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{r,\delta_{k}}-\psi_{r}\right\|_{C_{t}H^{2}_{x}}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Moreover, inequality (
5.16
) ensures that
\psi_{r}\in\mathrm{Lip}([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.
We are left to verify that
\psi_{r}
is the asserted solution. Denote by
V_{r}
the solution of

\displaystyle-\Delta V_{r}=\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r}\right|^{2}-e^{V_{r}}.

Lemma
5.3
implies that

\displaystyle\|V_{r,\delta_{k}}(t,\cdot)-V_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\zeta_{\delta_{k}}\star\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r,\delta_{k}}|^{2})(t,\cdot)-(\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\zeta_{\delta_{k}}\star\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r,\delta_{k}}|^{2})(t,\cdot)-(\zeta_{\delta_{k}}\star\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\|(\zeta_{\delta_{k}}\star\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)-(\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{2}.

The first term is bounded by

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r,\delta_{k}}|^{2}-\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{r}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\||\psi_{r,\delta_{k}}|^{2}(t,\cdot)-|\psi_{r}|^{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{r}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\psi_{r,\delta_{k}}(t,\cdot)-\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

In addition, note that

\displaystyle(\zeta_{\delta_{k}}\star\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r}\right|^{2})(t,x)\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}(\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r}\right|^{2})(t,x)\qquad\mbox{pointwise a.e. in}\ (t,x),

and, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|(\zeta_{\delta_{k}}\star\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)-(\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{2}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|V_{r,\delta_{k}}(t,\cdot)-V_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

As a result, we can pass to the limit as
k\rightarrow\infty
in order to conclude that, for any

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}i\psi_{r}(t,x)\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t.

Since
\psi_{r}\in\mathrm{Lip}([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
,
we can integrate the term on the left-hand side by parts
in order to conclude that
\psi_{r}\in\mathrm{Lip}([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

verifies the Cauchy problem (
5.12
). Finally, by Lemma
5.6

and uniqueness, we can prove that
\psi_{r}\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

∎

We continue by showing the compactness in
r
of the family of solutions
\left\{\psi_{r}\right\}_{r>0}

constructed above, which will enable us to extract a converging subsequence,
thereby proving the existence of a solution to the original equation.
As remarked in
[
14
]
, the advantage of using a double regularization for
V_{r}

is because this regularization procedures ensures the conservation of the following time-dependent quantity
\mathcal{F}_{r}
defined in the next lemma.
The proof is omitted, since it is similar to the proof of the conservation of total energy that
has been given in §
2
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\psi_{r}(t,x)\in\mathrm{Lip}([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
be the solution to (
5.6
). Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{r}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla V_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}V_{r}(t,x)e^{V_{r}(t,x)}\,{\rm d}x.

Then

(5.17)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{F}_{r}(t)=0.

We will also need the following lemma, stated with a scaling parameter
\varepsilon
,
since it is also
useful in the asymptotic analysis later.

Lemma 5.9

(
[
14
]
, Proposition 4.4)
.

Let
d\in\{2,3\}
. For each
i=1,2
, let
h_{i}\in L^{\infty}\cap L^{\frac{d+2}{d}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, and
let
(\tilde{V}_{i},\hat{V}_{i})
be the solution of the system
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{V}_{i}=h_{i}-1,\\[1.42262pt]
-\Delta\hat{V}_{i}=1-e^{\tilde{V}_{i}+\hat{V}_{i}}.\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla\tilde{V}_{1}-\nabla\tilde{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\underset{i}{\max}\|h_{i}\|_{\infty}W_{2}^{2}(h_{1},h_{2}),

\displaystyle\|\nabla\hat{V}_{1}-\nabla\hat{V}_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\,\underset{i}{\max}\|h_{i}\|_{\infty}W_{2}^{2}(h_{1},h_{2}),

where
C=C_{d}(1+\underset{i}{\max}\|h_{i}\|_{\frac{d+2}{d}})
, and
W_{2}
designates the
2
-Wasserstein distance.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We divide the proof into five steps.

1
.
Uniform bound in
r
on
\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
. Consider the mollified system:

(5.18)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\psi_{r}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)+(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
-\Delta V_{r}(t,x)=(\chi_{r}\star\left|\psi_{r}\right|^{2})(t,x)-e^{V_{r}(t,x)},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\,t>0,\\[1.42262pt]
\psi_{r}|_{t=0}=\psi^{\mathrm{in}},&x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.\end{cases}

and denote by
\psi_{r}\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
the solution to this system ensured
thanks to Lemma
5.6
.
Using Lemma
5.8
,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla V_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}V_{r}(t,x)e^{V_{r}(t,x)}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla\psi^{\mathrm{in}}(x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla V_{r}(0,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}V_{r}(0,x)e^{V_{r}(0,x)}\,{\rm d}x

(5.19)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla V_{r}(0,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|V_{r}(0,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty},

where we have used that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{V_{r}(0,x)}\,{\rm d}x=1
in the last inequality.
By the Sobolev embedding, we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{r}\star|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}|^{2}\|_{\frac{d+2}{d}}\leq\||\psi^{\mathrm{in}}|^{2}\|_{\frac{d+2}{d}}\leq C_{S}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2},

where
C_{S}
stands for the Sobolev constant.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
, we see that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\hat{V}_{r}(0,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{d}\Big(1+e^{4(1+C_{S}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2})}\Big).

In addition, using the Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla\tilde{V}_{r}(0,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\Big(1+C_{S}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\Big).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla V_{r}(0,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla\tilde{V}_{r}(0,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+2\|\nabla\hat{V}_{r}(0,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big(1+C_{S}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\big)+2C_{d}\big(1+e^{4(1+C_{S}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2})}\big),

and similarly

\|V_{r}(0,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq 2\big(1+C_{S}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\big)+2C_{d}\big(1+e^{4(1+C_{S}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2})}\big),

which, in view of (
5.19
), shows that

(5.20)

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}},

for some constant
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}(\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},d)
.

2
.
Uniform bound in
r
on
\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
.
We compute

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\left|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\Re\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\partial_{t}\psi_{r}(t,x)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Delta\big(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)+(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x)\big)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta\big((\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x)\big)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\left|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\nabla\psi_{r}(t,x)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2\Re\Big(-i\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\nabla(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\nabla\psi_{r}(t,x)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=:I+J.

First, note that

\displaystyle\left|J\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\nabla\psi_{r}(t,x)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\right|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\nabla\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|(\chi_{r}\star\nabla V_{r})(t,\cdot)\|_{4}^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{4}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla V_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{4}^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{4}\,{\rm d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(5.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(\|\nabla\tilde{V}_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{4}^{2}+\|\nabla\hat{V}_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{4}^{2}\big)\Big(1+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,\,\,\,

where we have used the Sobolev inequality,
according to which
\left\|\nabla\psi\right\|_{4}^{2}\lesssim 1+\left\|\Delta\psi\right\|_{2}^{2}
, in order to derive the last inequality. To bound the first term in the above inequality, observe that

(5.22)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\tilde{V_{r}}(t,\cdot)\|_{4}^{2}\leq\left\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{4}^{2}+1\leq C_{S}(1+\left\|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2})\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}},d}1,

where the last inequality is due to the Sobolev inequality and (
5.20
) in Step 1. In addition, Lemma
2.3
entails

(5.23)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\hat{V}_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{4}\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}},d}1.

Hence, inequalities (
5.21
)–(
5.23
) imply that

(5.24)

\displaystyle J\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}},d}1+\left\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}.

As for
I
, we first observe that

\displaystyle\|\Delta V_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}\leq 2\|(\chi_{r}\star|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{2}+2\|e^{V_{r}(t,\cdot)}\|_{2}\leq 2\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{4}^{2}+2\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{4}^{2}=4\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{4}^{2},

where the second inequality is due to the estimate:

\|e^{V_{r}}\|_{2}\leq\|(\chi_{r}\star|\psi|^{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{2}

proved in Lemma
5.5
. Therefore, it follows from the Sobolev embedding and (
5.20
) that

(5.25)

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta V_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}},d}1.

We proceed by observing that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|(\chi_{r}\star\Delta V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x)\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\right|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|(\chi_{r}\star\Delta V_{r})(t,x)\psi_{r}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\Delta\overline{\psi_{r}}(t,x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\left\|(\chi_{r}\star\Delta V_{r})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\left\|\Delta V_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}

(5.26)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}},d}(1+\left\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}),

where we have used (
5.25
) and the interpolation inequality:

(5.27)

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{d}\big(\|\Psi\|_{2}+\|\Delta\Psi\|_{2}\big)

for the last inequality in (
5.26
).
Thus, gathering (
5.26
) and (
5.24
), we have proved that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\leq C\left\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\

for some
C=C(\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}},d)
, which, owing to the Grönwall inequality,
implies that

(5.28)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq e^{Ct}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},

where
C=C(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}},d)
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}(T,d,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})})
.

3.

Uniform bound in
r
on
\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
. We follow a similar approach to the one taken in Step 2 in the proof of Theorem
5.6
, keeping track of the constants to ensure that they are uniform in
r
.
From the same calculations leading to (
5.14
), we have

(5.29)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\nabla^{k}(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})\nabla^{4-k}\psi_{r}\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}\right|(t,x)\ {\rm d}x\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{4}I_{k}.

We proceed by estimating each one of
I_{k}
:

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\big|\,\big|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,x)\big|^{2}\ {\rm d}x\leq\left\|V_{r}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\nabla(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)|\big|\,\big|\nabla\Delta\psi_{r}(t,x)\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,x)\big|\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla V_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\big(\|\nabla\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2}\big),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\big|\Delta(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,x)\big|\,\big|(\Delta\psi_{r}\Delta^{2}\psi_{r})(t,x)\big|\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta V_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\big(\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{2}\big).

Using Lemma
2.3
, (
5.28
), and the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we see that

(5.30)

\displaystyle\left\|V_{r}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}+\left\|\Delta V_{r}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\left\|V_{r}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}+\left\|\psi_{r}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}^{2}+\left\|e^{V_{r}}\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}1,

so that

(5.31)

\displaystyle I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}\big(\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\big).

In order to bound
I_{3}
and
I_{4}
, note that

\displaystyle-\nabla\Delta V_{r}=\chi_{r}\star\nabla\left|\psi_{r}\right|^{2}-e^{V_{r}}\nabla V_{r}

and

\displaystyle-\Delta^{2}V_{r}=\chi_{r}\star\Delta\left|\psi_{r}\right|^{2}-\Delta(e^{V_{r}})=\chi_{r}\star\Delta\left|\psi_{r}\right|^{2}-e^{V_{r}}\left|\nabla V_{r}\right|^{2}-e^{V_{r}}\Delta V_{r}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+\|(\chi_{r}\star\Delta^{2}V_{r})(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)||_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}V_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)||_{2}^{2}+\|(\chi_{r}\star\Delta|\psi_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|(e^{V_{r}}|\nabla V_{r}|^{2})(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|(e^{V_{r}}\Delta V_{r})(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\coloneqq I_{40}+I_{41}+I_{42}+I_{43}.

First, using (
5.27
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{41}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta|\psi_{r}|^{2}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}=2\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}+2\|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}+2\|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}1+\|\Delta{\psi_{r}}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta^{2}{\psi_{r}}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}

(5.32)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}1+\|\Delta^{2}{\psi_{r}}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}.

Secondly, note that, by Lemma
2.3
(or alternatively by (
5.30
)),
we have

\displaystyle\|e^{V_{r}}\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{T,d,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}1,\qquad\|\Delta V_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{T,d,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}1.

It follows that

(5.33)

\displaystyle I_{42}+I_{43}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}1+\left\|\nabla V_{r}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+\left\|\Delta V_{r}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}1.

Gathering (
5.32
) with (
5.33
) shows that

\displaystyle I_{4}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}\big(1+\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\big).

It follows from the same considerations that

\displaystyle I_{3}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}\big(1+\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\big),

which implies that

(5.34)

\displaystyle I_{3}+I_{4}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}\big(1+\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\big).

Thus, the combination of (
5.31
) with (
5.34
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}}1+\left\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}

and therefore

(5.35)

\displaystyle\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq e^{Ct}\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime\prime},

where
C=C(T,d,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})})
and

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime\prime}(T,d,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{d})})
.

4.

Compactness in
r
. We utilize the preceding estimates in order to extract a converging subsequence. We have

\displaystyle\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)-\psi_{r}(s,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\int_{t}^{s}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{r}(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}\tau\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\left\|\int_{t}^{s}\partial_{\tau}\Delta\psi_{r}(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}\tau\right\|_{2}^{2}

(5.36)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|t-s\right|^{2}\Big(\,\,\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\partial_{t}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}+\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\Big).

Using (
5.18
) and (
5.28
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{t}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}+\left\|(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,\cdot)\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(T,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{2}},d)+\left\|(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,\cdot)\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}.

In addition, recall that, by (
5.20
),

\displaystyle\left\|(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,\cdot)\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|(\chi_{r}\star V_{r})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|V_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq C(\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{1}},d).

It follows that

(5.37)

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\partial_{t}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}}}1.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}+\|\Delta(\chi_{r}\star V_{r}\psi_{r})(t,\cdot)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta^{2}\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}+\|V_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\|\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}+\|\Delta V_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}+2\|\nabla V_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}.

Thanks to (
5.30
), (
5.35
),
and the last inequality above, we see that

(5.38)

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\partial_{t}\Delta\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{4}}}1

Substituting (
5.37
) and (
5.38
) into (
5.36
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{r}(t,\cdot)-\psi_{r}(s,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\leq C\left|t-s\right|,

where
C=C(T,d,\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\|_{H^{4}})
.
Therefore, it follows from the Arzela-Ascoli theorem that there are both
a sequence
r_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
and some
\psi\in C([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

such that

\left\|\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{C([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

with the estimate:

(5.39)

\displaystyle\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{2}}\leq\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{in}}

where
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{in}}
is as in (
5.28
).

5
.
\psi(t,\cdot)
is a solution
.
It remains to show that the limit
\psi(t,\cdot)
is a solution to system (
1.4
).
Note that
\psi(t,\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, because of the embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
,
there exists a unique solution
V
of the equation:

\displaystyle-\Delta V=\left|\psi\right|^{2}-e^{V}.

To show that
\psi
is a solution, the key component is to show that

\left\|(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V_{r_{k}})(t,\cdot)\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-V(t,\cdot)\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

By the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V_{r_{k}})(t,\cdot)\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-V(t,\cdot)\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V_{r_{k}})(t,\cdot)\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V)(t,\cdot)\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\left\|(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V)(t,\cdot)\psi(t,\cdot)-V(t,\cdot)\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V)(t,\cdot)\left(\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-\psi(t,\cdot)\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\left\|\left(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V_{r_{k}}-\chi_{r_{k}}\star V\right)(t,\cdot)\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,\,+\left\|\psi(t,\cdot)\left((\chi_{r_{k}}\star V)(t,\cdot)-V(t,\cdot)\right)\right\|_{2}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{L}.

The term
\mathcal{I}
is mastered as

\mathcal{I}\leq\|V(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\|\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-\psi(t,\cdot)\|_{2}\leq\big(\|\tilde{V}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|\hat{V}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\big)\|\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-\psi(t,\cdot)\|_{2}.

By the Sobolev embedding and (
5.39
),
we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{V}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|K\|_{2}\big(1+\left\|\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{4}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K\right\|_{2}\big(1+C_{S}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\big)\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}}}1.

In addition, Lemma
2.3
and (
5.39
) entail

\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\|\hat{V}(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}}}1.

Thus, we obtain the estimate:

\mathcal{I}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}}}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left\|\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

To treat
\mathcal{J}
, we note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\leq\left\|(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V_{r_{k}}-\chi_{r_{k}}\star V)(t,\cdot)\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\left\|(V_{r_{k}}-V)(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}.

Using Lemma
5.9
, (
5.28
), and (
5.39
),
we see that

\displaystyle\left\|V_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)-V(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}}}W_{2}^{2}\big((\chi_{r_{k}}\star\left|\psi_{r_{k}}\right|^{2})(t,\cdot),\left|\psi\right|^{2}(t,\cdot)\big)\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\psi_{r_{k}}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}}}1,

which eventually yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Finally, again by (
5.39
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\psi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\infty}\left\|(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V)(t,\cdot)-V(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T,d,\left\|\psi^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{2}}}\left\|(\chi_{r_{k}}\star V)(t,\cdot)-V(t,\cdot)\right\|_{2}\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

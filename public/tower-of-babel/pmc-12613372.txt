Description of Sediminicola litoralis sp. nov. isolated from tidal flat sediment and functional genomic analysis of the genus Sediminicola
Authors: Yang Liu, Juan Du, Xiong Xiong, Xuan Lu, Haofang Wu, Shuai Meng, Liangqiu Li, Ming-Rong Deng, Honghui Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe genus Sediminicola represents one of the least characterized taxa within the well-studied family Flavobacteriaceae, with only two formally recognized species to date. The scarcity of cultivated isolates and the lack of genomic data for type strains have significantly hindered comprehensive insights into its phylogenetic diversity, metabolic capabilities, and potential biotechnological utility. In this study, a novel bacterial strain, designated 1XM1-17T, was isolated from tidal flat sediment. Through a polyphasic taxonomic approach combined with functional genomic analysis, we systematically investigated its taxonomic classification and conducted the first detailed metabolic profiling of the genus Sediminicola to bridge this knowledge gap.ResultsThe phylogenetic inference based on the 16S rRNA gene and genome sequences revealed that strain 1XM1-17T formed a separate branch from the type strains S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T. The digital DNA‒DNA hybridization (dDDH), average nucleotide identity (ANI), and amino acid identity (AAI) values between strain 1XM1-17T and two relatives above were 23.7 and 21.9%, 82.6 and 80.6%, 87.96 and 86.49%, significantly below the thresholds of 70% dDDH, 95‒96% ANI and AAI as threshold definitions. The morphological, physiological, and chemotaxonomic characteristics readily supported its affiliation within the genus Sediminicola while distinguishing it from its relatives. Phylogenomic analyses based on an expanded genomic dataset revealed an underestimation of the species diversity of the genus Sediminicola. Functional genome analysis revealed that this genus possesses conserved genetic determinants for nitrogen, sulfur, and phosphorus metabolism; gliding motility; and the T9SS, but fewer secondary metabolite biosynthetic gene clusters and CAZymes than other Flavobacteriaceae members. The genus Sediminicola was found to be widely distributed in marine environments, particularly in sediments.ConclusionsBased on the phenotypic and genotypic evidence, strain 1XM1-17T was determined to represent a novel species of the genus Sediminicola, with the proposed name Sediminicola litoralis sp. nov. (type strain, 1XM1-17T = GDMCC 1.2384T = KCTC 82665T). This study establishes the taxonomic status of strain 1XM1-17T and provides an insight into the metabolic function of the genus Sediminicola.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12866-025-04476-5.Keywords: Sediminicola litoralis, Taxonomic characterization, Functional genomics analysis, Ecological distribution

Background

The family Flavobacteriaceae is the largest family of the phylum Bacteroidetes [1] and currently includes more than 160 genera and hundreds of species with validly published and correct names [2]. The widespread distribution of this family in different habitats is one of the most intriguing characteristics of this family, to a large extent highlighting its high genetic plasticity and complex metabolic diversity [1]. It is particularly noteworthy that some members of this family are frequently isolated from diseased humans or animals, with several identified as pathogens [3, 4]. Phenotypically, the cells of this family are short to moderately long rods with parallel or slightly irregular sides and rounded or slightly tapered ends. Growth is aerobic or microaerobic to anaerobic. The growth temperature is usually mesophilic, but some are psychrophilic or psychrotrophic. Most members can produce light to bright yellow or orange pigments, although nonpigmented taxa and strains do occur [1, 5]. Menaquinone 6 is either the only respiratory quinone or the major respiratory quinone, whereas menaquinone 7 is also frequently detected [5]. The degradation of high-molecular-weight macromolecules, such as complex polysaccharides and proteins and gliding motility are common features of many members of this family, suggesting their important role in carbon cycling [6, 7].

The genus Sediminicola belongs to the family Flavobacteriaceae and is Gram-staining-negative, aerobic, and rod-shaped. Members of this genus produce golden-yellow-colored carotenoid pigments but lack flexirubin-type pigments [8]. Two species, Sediminicola luteus [8] and Sediminicola arcticus [9], have been validly published, with the former serving as the type species of this genus. The type strain of S. luteus was isolated from marine sediment along the Sea of Japan [8], whereas that of S. arcticus was recovered from Arctic deep-sea sediment and exhibited psychrophilic characteristics, with an optimal growth temperature of 15 °C [9]. Chemotaxonomic analysis revealed that the predominant cellular fatty acids identified in these bacteria are summed feature 3 (C16:1 ω6c and/or C16:1 ω7c) and C15:0. Notably, genomic analysis of the Sediminicola sp. YIK13 strain revealed retinal biosynthesis genes associated with proteorhodopsin [10], suggesting a potential light-energy utilization mechanism. Despite the genus being described nearly two decades ago, significant knowledge gaps remain regarding its taxonomic diversity and metabolic potential.

In this study, we isolated a yellow-pigmented bacterial strain designated 1XM1-17T from tidal flat sediment and determined its taxonomic position via a polyphasic taxonomic approach. Given that the genome sequences of the type strains of S. luteus and S. arcticus are not publicly available, we performed whole-genome sequencing of the two type strains. By incorporating all accessible genome sequences from public databases (including our three newly sequenced strains), we conducted an in-depth functional genomics investigation.

Materials and methods

Bacterial isolation and cultivation

The sediment was collected from the intertidal zone of Baicheng Beach (24°25’51.1″ N, 118°06’16.6″ E), Xiamen city, Fujian Province, China. Bacterial isolation, cultivation, and preservation were performed according to the methods described by Liu et al. [11]. Among isolates, a yellow-pigmented strain designated 1XM1-17T was selected for comprehensive taxonomic analysis. For comparative analysis, two closely related type strains, S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T, were obtained from the Guangdong Microbial Culture Collection Center (GDMCC). Unless otherwise stated, all strains were cultured on marine agar (MA, BD) and marine broth (MB, BD) media under identical conditions to ensure consistent comparisons of their physiological and biochemical characteristics.

The 16S rRNA gene sequencing and phylogenetic analysis.

The genomic DNA of strain 1XM1-17T was extracted via a bacterial genome DNA rapid extraction kit (Magen, Guangzhou, China; product code: D3146-03) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The nearly full-length 16S rRNA gene of strain 1XM1-17T was amplified via PCR using the primer pair 27F and 1492R [12], with subsequent purification (Magen, product code: D2121-03) and Sanger sequencing of the PCR products. The sequence identity of the 16S rRNA gene was determined using the recently updated EzBioCloud database (http://www.ezbiocloud.net/, accessed on 30 June 2025) [13]. For comparative analysis, the 16S rRNA gene sequences of the closely related type strains were retrieved from the GenBank database and aligned using the Muscle algorithm implemented in the software MEGA version 11.0.13 [14]. Phylogenetic trees were reconstructed by using the MEGA based on three algorithms with default parameters: neighbor-joining (NJ) [15], maximum likelihood (ML) [16], and maximum evolution (ME) [17]. The best substitution models for the NJ and ML algorithms were determined to be the Kimura 2-parameter and Tamura-Nei, respectively. The confidence levels for the branch nodes of the 16 S rRNA gene-based phylogenetic trees were assessed using a bootstrap analysis with 1000 replicates [18]. The type strain Flavobacterium aquatile LMG4008T (accession number M62797) served as an outgroup in the 16S rRNA gene-based phylogenetic analysis.

Genome sequencing and phylogenomic analysis

Whole-genome sequencing of strains 1XM1-17T, S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T, and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T was performed using the Illumina NovaSeq PE150 platform (Majorbio, Shanghai, China). Genomic DNA was extracted using Bacterial DNA extraction kit (Magen, Guangzhou, China; magnetic beads) according to the manufacture’s protocol. Sequencing libraries were prepared from the sheared fragments using the NEXTFLEX Rapid DNA-Seq Kit. The raw reads were quality-filtered using the software fastq version 0.19.6 [19], including the removal of adapter sequences, non-A, G, C, T bases at the 5’ end, and reads with the low-quality ends (sequencing quality value below Q20), and the discards of reads shorter than 25 bp. The high-quality reads were assembled using the software SPAdes version 2.0 with default parameters [20]. The resulting assemblies were refined by removing contigs whose coverage was less than 50× and whose length was less than 1000 bp. All available genomes of closely related type strains of the species of the family Flavobacteriaceae were obtained from the GenBank database. Given the draft status of most genomes, quality assessment was performed using the software CheckM version 2.0 [21], prior to phylogenomic analysis.

Genomic similarity between strain 1XM1-17T and related type strains was assessed using two overall genome relatedness indices, digital DNA‒DNA hybridization (dDDH) and average nucleotide identity (ANI). The dDDH values were calculated using the Genome-to-Genome Distance Calculator version 3.0 available at https://ggdc.dsmz.de with the recommended formula 2 and the BLAST + local alignment tool [22]. The ANI values were calculated using the software FastANI version 1.31 [23]. The average amino acid identity (AAI) values were calculated using the software CompareM version 0.1.2 (https://github.com/dparks1134/CompareM). The phylogenomic tree based on 92 bacterial core genes was reconstructed using the software UBCG version 3.0 [24] following the default pipeline (https://www.ezbiocloud.net/tools/ubcg). The UBCG script was used to obtain a concatenated multiple alignment with nucleotide and amino acid sequences of a curated list of 92 housekeeping genes, to then infer a phylogenetic tree by the maximum-likelihood method using the software RAxML. The type strain Flavobacterium aquatile LMG4008T served as an outgroup in the phylogenomic analysis. The genome-based phylogenetic trees were managed, annotated, and visualized using the online tool iTOL [25] version 7.2.

Phenotypic characterization

The colony morphology (size, shape, and pigmentation) of strain 1XM1-17T was examined using the stereomicroscope (SZX10, Olympus) after three days of incubation on MA at 28 °C. Cellular morphology including dimensions, shape, and flagellar presence was observed using the transmission electron microscope (H7650, Hitachi) with cells grown under identical conditions. Anaerobic growth was tested on MA using an anaerobic pouch (MGC, Mitsubishi) for two weeks. The degree of Gram-staining was determined using a Gram staining kit (Hope, Qingdao, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Catalase and oxidase activities were detected using 3% (v/v) hydrogen peroxide and 1% (w/v) N,N,N′,N′-tetramethyl-p-phenylenediamine dihydrochloride, respectively. The tests of cellular motility, and the growth on different media, temperatures, pH values, and NaCl concentration range and their optima were performed in accordance with previously described methods [26]. Specifically, growth was evaluated at various temperatures (4, 10, 15, 20, 28, 32, 37, 40, 43, 45 and 50 °C) on MA medium. The pH range for growth was determined in MB adjusted to pH 4.0–11.0 (at increments of 1 pH units) with citrate/phosphate (pH 4.0–7.0), Tris/HCl (pH 8.0–9.0) or sodium carbonate/sodium bicarbonate (pH 10.0–11.0) buffers. Tolerance to NaCl was performed in artificially modified MB supplemented with NaCl concentrations of 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 11, 13, 15 and 18% (w/v). The ability of Tweens 20, 40, 60, and 80, starch, skimmed milk, and carboxymethyl cellulose to be hydrolyzed was tested using established methods [26]. Flexirubin-type pigments were detected using via the KOH color-shift test (20 % w/v solution) [27]. Pigment detection was conducted following the method described previously [28]. Additional biochemical and physiological properties, such as enzymatic activities and assimilation of carbon sources, were characterized using the API ZYM and API 20NE identification systems (BioMérieux, France) following manufacturer’s protocols.

The identification of fatty acids and polar lipids was conducted according to previously described methods [26]. The cells of strain 1XM1-17T and the reference two type strains were harvested during exponential growth from quadrant III MA plates, and then freeze-dried using a vacuum freeze-drying apparatus. The cellular fatty acids were extracted using the standard protocol for the standard MIDI (Sherlock Microbial Identification System, version 6.0B), and analyzed by a gas chromatography (Agilent Technologies 6850). Total lipids were extracted using a chloroform/methanol system [29] and separated by two-dimensional thin-layer chromatography (silica gel 60 F254, Merck). Specific lipid classes were identified using sequential sprays of 10% ethanolic molybdophosphoric acid, molybdenum blue, ninhydrin, α-naphthol-sulfuric acid, and Dragendorff’s reagents. Quinone was extracted and purified according to the previously described method [30] and then identified using a high-performance liquid chromatography (Agilent 1200; ODS 250 × 4.6 mm×5 μm; flow-phase methanol-isopropanol at a ratio of 2:1 at 1 mL/min).

Genomic taxonomy and functional characterization among Sediminicola genomes

All publicly available genome sequences classified under the genus Sediminicola were retrieved from the NCBI Genome database (accessed 30 June 2025). Genome quality was assessed by the CheckM, with only high-quality genomes retained for subsequent analyses to ensure reliable metabolic inference. Pairwise genomic comparisons were performed using dDDH, ANI, and AAI values. The prediction and annotation of genes were performed using the software Prokka version 1.14.6 with default parameters [31]. The functional annotation was conducted using the eggNOG-mapper 2.1.12 [32] and KEGG via BLASTKOALA. The secondary metabolite biosynthetic gene cluster (BGCs) was predicted using the software antiSMASH version 8.0.1 bacterial version (accessed on 30 June 2025) with the default settings of KnownBlusterBlast, ClusterBlast, SubClusterBlast, MIBiG (Minimum Information about a Biosynthetic Gene) cluster comparison, active siteFinder, RREFinder (RiPP Recognition Element), and Cluster Pfam analysis [33]. Gliding motility and T9SS proteins were identified via local BLASTP searches with an E-value cutoff of 1e-5 against Flavobacterium johnsoniae UW101 references [34, 35]. Only matches with >50% coverage and >30% sequence identity with database targets were retained. The genes encoding carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) were annotated using the dbCAN3 web server [36]. Only genes annotated with at least two dbCAN tools (HMMER, DIAMOND, or dbCAN_sub) were considered to encode a CAZyme. The distributions of key genes and proteins were visualized using the “Heatmap” tool of the software TBtools version 2.315 [37].

Geographic distribution and relative abundance of the genus Sediminicola

The environmental distribution and habitat preferences of the genus Sediminicola were investigated using the Microbe Atlas Project (MAP) platform (https://microbeatlas.org/) [38]. The complete 16S rRNA gene sequence of strain 1XM1-17T served as the reference query, with a stringent 97.0% identity threshold applied for genus-level classification. Microbial community abundance was determined using MAPseq, a closed-reference OTU picking approach for rRNA sequence analysis that ensures taxonomic precision through rigorous reference database matching.

Results and discussion

Identification of strain 1XM1-17T based on 16S rRNA gene and genome sequences.

The 16S rRNA gene sequence of strain 1XM1-17T, obtained by the PCR (1389 bp), exhibited 100% identity with the full-length sequence (1515 bp) annotated as locus_tag = WAA86_15300 in its genome sequence as described below. Consequently, the complete 16 S rRNA gene sequence of strain 1XM1-17T was utilized for subsequent analyses. The 16S rRNA gene sequence identity between strain 1XM1-17T and its reference type strains was analyzed using the EzBioCloud database with the BLAST algorithm. The results indicated that strain 1XM1-17T shared 98.3 and 97.9% identity with S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T (Table S1), respectively, and less than 95.0% identity with members of other genera in the family Flavobacteriace. Given that 98.65% 16S rRNA gene sequence identity is commonly used as a threshold for differentiating two species [39], strain 1XM1-17T should represent a potential novel species. Moreover, the phylogenetic analysis based on 16S rRNA gene sequences, using the NJ, ML and ME methods, revealed that strain 1XM1-17T clustered within the genus Sediminicola and formed a distinct branch from S. luteus and S. arcticus, which was supported by a 100% bootstrap value (Figs. 1A and S1, S2). Collectively, the 16S rRNA gene sequence identity analysis and phylogenetic tree reconstruction support the classification of strain 1XM1-17T as a potential novel species of the genus Sediminicola.

The genus Sediminicola currently comprises two species S. arcticus and S. luteus. However, their genome sequences are not currently available. Therefore, in this study, we determined the genome sequences of these type strains and strain 1XM1-17T. After sequencing, assembly, and quality control, the genome sequences of S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T, S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T, and strain 1XM1-17T were obtained, with the assembly accession numbers GCA_040566555.1, GCA_040566695.1, and GCA_037203765.1. The CheckM estimation indicated that all three genome sequences had completeness values exceeding 99.0% and contamination levels below 1.0% (Table 1), confirming their high quality [21, 40]. The genomic sizes of S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T, S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T, and strain 1XM1-17T were 3.57, 3.46, and 3.47 Mbp, respectively, with DNA G + C contents of 39.46, 37.19, and 41.12%, respectively (Table 1 and S2). Overall, the general characteristics of the genome sequences of S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T, S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T, and strain 1XM1-17T are comparable, as detailed in Table 1 and S2.

Overall genome relatedness indices, including dDDH, ANI, and AAI were applied to accurately determine taxonomic the assignment of strain 1XM1-17T in this study. The results indicated that the dDDH and ANI values between strain 1XM1-17T and two reference type strains (S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T) were 23.7 and 21.9%, 82.55 and 80.61%, 87.96 and 86.49% (Table S1), respectively. These values were well below the recognized thresholds for bacterial species delineation (70% dDDH [41] and 95.0–96.0% ANI [42] and AAI [43]), indicating that strain 1XM1-17T should represent a putative novel genospecies. The phylogenomic analysis further indicated that strain 1XM1-17T belonged to the genus Sediminicola but formed a separate branch distinct from those of S. luteus and S. arcticus, as supported by a 100% bootstrap value (Fig. 1B), which supported the classification of strain 1XM1-17T as a new species within the genus Sediminicola. Collectively, these genome-based analyses supported strong evidence that strain 1XM1-17T should be recognized as a novel species of the genus Sediminicola.

Phenotypic characterization of strain 1XM1-17T and reference type strains

The colony color of strain 1XM1-17T was light yellow on MA after 72 h of incubation at 30 °C (Figure S3). Its cells were long, rod-shaped, or slightly curved, and lacked flagella (Figure S4). As shown in Table 2, the colony colors between strain 1XM1-17T and the two type strains (S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T) were significantly different and can serve as simple indicators to distinguish them [8, 9]. Strain 1XM1-17T could grow at 15–32 °C (optimum, 28 °C), at pH 5.0–9.0 (optimum, 7.0), and in 0.5–3.0% NaCl (optimum, 1.0%, w/v). Strain 1XM1-17T grew only on the MA, whereas the two type strains grew on both MA and R2A media. Strain 1XM1-17T was positive for oxidase and catalase and could hydrolyze Tweens 20 and 40, similar to the two type strains. The three strains could produce carotenoid pigments (Figure S5), but not produce flexirubin-type pigments (Figure S6) [8]. Enzyme activity tests revealed that strain 1XM1-17T and S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T were positive for both naphthol-AS-BI-phosphohydrolase and α-glucosidase, whereas S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T was negative for both (Table 2). Strain 1XM1-17T was negative for N-acetyl-β-glucosaminidase, whereas the two type strains were positive. Carbon source utilization tests revealed that strain 1XM1-17T could assimilate L-arabinose, D-mannitol, N-acetyl-glucosamine, potassium gluconate, capric acid, adipic acid, malic acid, trisodium citrate and phenylacetic acid, whereas the two type strains could not (Table 2). Although the morphological, physiological and biochemical characteristics of strain 1XM1-17T are generally similar to those of the two type strains, distinct differences are detailed in Table 2 and the species description.

The cellular fatty acid compositions of strains 1XM1-17T, S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T are listed in Table 3. The major fatty acids (comprising more than 10% of the total fatty acids) of strain 1XM1-17T were iso-C15:0 (26.1%), iso-C15:1 G (15.8%) and summed feature 3 (10.1%). Its minor fatty acids (5–10% of total fatty acids) were C15:0 (9.3%), iso-C15:0 3OH (8.5%), summed feature 9 (C16:0 10-methyl and/or iso-C17:1 ω9c, 7.5%) and iso-C16:0 3OH (5.1%). The prominent fatty acids of strain 1XM1-17T were similar to those of reference type strains, indicating its affiliation with the genus Sediminicola. However, strain 1XM1-17T contained higher proportions of iso-C15:0 and iso-C15:1 G and a lower proportion of iso-C16:0 3OH than its close type strains did, which can be used to distinguish it from its close relatives. The iso-C17:0 3OH was not detected in strain 1XM1-17T but was a major fatty acid in S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T. The polar lipids of strain 1XM1-17T included phosphatidylethanolamine, an unidentified aminophospholipid, and two unidentified lipids (Figure S7). The polar lipid profile of strain 1XM1-17T was highly similar to those of S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T [9]. The sole respiratory quinone identified in strain 1XM1-17T was MK-6, which is consistent with the two reference type strains [24]. The chemotaxonomic characteristics of strains 1XM1-17T, S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T including the profiles of fatty acids and polar lipids, as well as respiratory quinone, are generally similar, supporting the classification of strain 1XM1-17T within the genus Sediminicola. However, significant differences in the contents of fatty acid components among the three strains suggest that strain 1XM1-17T is distinct from S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T.

Taxonomic diversity of the genus Sediminicola

To investigate the taxonomic diversity within the genus Sediminicola, a total of 52 genome sequences, classified as this genus in the NCBI databases (Table 1 and S1), were utilized in this study. This dataset included the genome sequences of strains 1XM1-17T, S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T. The genomes were categorized into two types: metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) and cultured genomes (CGs) (Table 1). Quality assessments using CheckM revealed that all MAGs had a completeness of >50 % and contamination of < 10 %, classifying them as medium-quality genomes [21, 40]. In contrast, all CGs presented >99% completeness and < 2% contamination, indicating high-quality status [21, 40]. Although high-quality genomes are preferable, medium-quality genomes can still provide reliable results for taxonomic analyses [44]. Notably, the majority of the genomes used in this study exceeded the medium-quality threshold, ensuring robustness in the present analyses.

Most ANI values between all genome sequences were less than 75% (data not shown), rendering them unreliable; therefore, the AAI values were used as a more robust alternative. Phylogenomic, dDDH, and AAI analyses revealed that 33 MAGs and four CGs were classified into the genus Sediminicola (Figs. 2, Tables S3, and S4). Conversely, the remaining 15 MAGs, labeled Sediminicola sp., did not belong to this genus. Within the genus Sediminicola, seven species (designated SS1 to SS7) were identified. Four putative new species, SS1, SS3, SS5 and SS7, included 24 MAGs, one CG, two MAGs and one MAG, respectively. Species SS2 and SS4 corresponded to S. luteus (1 CG and 3 MAGs) and S. arcticus (one CG and three MAGs). Species SS6 contained only the genome sequence of strain 1XM1-17T. Among the 15 MAGs not classified as Sediminicola, two (GCA_963974715.1 and GCA_036963915.1) likely represent a putative new genus and species closely related to the genus Sediminicola (Tables S3 and S4). The genome GCA_048266425.1 was suggested to represent a putative new species of the genus “Cerina” [45]. The genome GCA_938039345.1, closely related to Cellulophaga lytica [46], should at least represent a new species. Nine MAGs formed a group containing five species (Table S3). Two MAGs, GCA_047612445.1 and GCA_000757385.1, likely represent two new genera (Table S4) within the family Flavobacteriaceae. These findings indicate that the species diversity of the genus Sediminicola is underestimated and the taxonomic status of many genome sequences currently assigned to this genus requires re-evaluation.

Genomic functional analysis of the genus Sediminicola

High-quality genome sequences are essential for ensuring the reliability and accuracy of functional analysis. In this study, eight high-quality genome sequences representing six species were selected for functional characterization (Fig. 3). Although the genome completeness of the species SS6 was only 66.35%, it was retained for analysis, as it represents the sole available genome for this species. With respect to nitrogen metabolism, three genes of narB, napAB, and nirBD associated with assimilatory and dissimilatory nitrate reduction were identified in the genome sequence of S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T, but not in those of strains 1XM1-17T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T (Fig. 3A). This finding was in agreement with the results of the nitrate reduction test. Notably, few or no nitrogen metabolism-related genes were detected in the remaining genome sequences. For sulfur metabolism, genes involved in assimilatory sulfate reduction (cysNCDH and sir, excluding GCA_041602195.1) were identified in all genome sequences (Fig. 3A), suggesting a conserved capacity for sulfate utilization. Strikingly, the genome sequence of S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T uniquely harbored the tsdA and dmdB genes linked to sulfur oxidation and dimethylsulfoniopropionate degradation. Regarding phosphorus metabolism, the genome sequences of the species SS1 and SS2 contained the gene gcd, a glucose dehydrogenase associated with phosphate solubilization [47]. With the exception of S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T, all the genome sequences included genes related to a high-affinity phosphate acquisition system (PstSCAB) and a regulatory system (PhoUR) that mediate inorganic phosphate transmembrane transport and regulation, ensuring an adequate supply under phosphate-limiting conditions [48]. Nearly all the genome sequences carried phoD and ugpQ genes implicated in organic phosphoester hydrolysis [49]. These features indicate that the genus Sediminicola possesses versatile strategies for both inorganic and organic phosphorus utilization. Additionally, the genomes of the seven representative species of the genus Sediminicola contained the genes/proteins related to proteorhodopsin and carotenoid biosynthesis (Table S5). These results suggested that strain 1XM1-17T should be aerobic and chemoorganotrophic.

Distribution of key genes involved in nitrogen, sulfur, and phosphorus metabolism (A), gliding motility and T9SS proteins (B), and CAZymes (C) in nine genome sequences and the ecological distribution (D) of the genus Sediminicola

The 27 proteins associated with gliding motility and the type IX secretion system (T9SS) were classified into essential, important, and non-essential categories based previous studies [34, 50, 51]. In this study, homologs of the four essential proteins GldKLMN were found in all the genome sequences (Fig. 3B). Among the 16 important proteins, homologs of nine proteins, GldA, GldG, GldB, GldD, GldH, GldI, SprE, SprF, and SprT, were present in all the genome sequences. Homologs of GldF were found in eight genome sequences, with the exception of GCA_047634035.1. Homologs of GldJ were detected in seven genome sequences, with the exception of GCA_041626895.1 and GCA_041602195.1. Homologs of SprA were identified in eight genome sequences, except for GCA_041602195.1. Homologs of SprB were found in only five genome sequences (GCA_040566555.1, GCA_001430825.1, GCA_040566695.1, GCA_041616935, and GCA_037203765.1). Conversely, homologs of the important proteins RemA, SprC, and SprD were not detected in any of the genome sequences. Among the non-essential proteins, homologs of PorX, PorY, and PorV were found in all the genome sequences, whereas homologs of PorQ and PorU were absent from some of the genome sequences. Strains 1XM1-17T, S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T, and S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T, which formed spreading colonies on agar, confirmed the presence of proteins associated with gliding motility in their genomes. Thus, the distribution and presence patterns of proteins related to gliding motility and the T9SS in the genus Sediminicola are consistent with those in the family Flavobacteriaceae [51].

The genomic repertoire of BGCs of the genus Sediminicola was evaluated using the antiSMASH. Four types of BGCs were identified and are putatively involved in the production of terpenes, type III polyketide synthases (T3PKSs), arylpolyenes, and terpene-precursors (Table S6). Among these BCGs, terpene and T3PKS BGCs were present across all the genome sequences. A BCG associated with carotenoid biosynthesis was detected in all genome sequences except for GCA_041626895.1, albeit with low similarity confidence. In contrast, no BGCs linked to flexirubin production, a hallmark of the family Flavobacteriaceae but rare in other bacteria [1], were identified, which is consistent with the observed lack of pigmentation in the three strains above. The BGC coverage (percentage of total genome size) across the nine genomes ranged from 0.73% to 2.52% (Table S4), which was significantly lower than that of other Flavobacteriaceae [51]. Furthermore, both the number of BGCs and their relative genomic proportions in Sediminicola were markedly lower than those in most characterized prokaryotic taxa [52], suggesting limited secondary metabolic diversity in this genus.

The genomic potential for complex carbohydrate degradation was evaluated through mining CAZymes. In the genus Sediminicola, the most abundant CAZyme family was glycosyltransferase (GT, a total of 291), followed by glycoside hydrolase (GH, 280), carbohydrate-binding module (CBM, 79), carbohydrate esterases (33), auxiliary activities (AA, 15), and polysaccharide lyase (PL, 12) (Fig. 3C and Table S7). Within the GT family, GT2, which is involved in the biosynthesis of bacterial cell wall peptidoglycan [53], was the most dominant. In contrast, TG5 (glycogen synthase, exclusive to Bacteroidetes [54]) and GT20 (starch phosphorylases) were enriched only in strain 1XM1-17T. Among the GH family members, GH13, the largest and most well-characterized α-amylase family [55], was prominent, although strains 1XM1-17T, S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T failed to hydrolyze starch in phenotypic assays. The underlying reason for the discrepancy between the presence of GH13 genes and the observed negative starch hydrolysis phenotype remains unclear and requires further investigation. GH130 (encoding mannoside phosphorylase, mannosidase, or xylanase [56]) was exclusively detected in strain 1XM1-17T. For the CBM family, CBM48 and CBM50 were universally distributed across all the genomes. Compared with the GT, GH, and CBM families, the CE, AA, and PL families presented limited numbers and diversity, with PL nearly exclusive to S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T. The CAZyme frequency (average number per Mbp) in the genus Sediminicola was significantly lower than that in other groups within the family Flavobacteriaceae [51]. Notably, strain 1XM1-17T uniquely harbored diverse subfamilies, suggesting strain-specific metabolic adaptations. However, the overall carbohydrate degradation potential of the genus Sediminicola appeared markedly reduced compared with that of related taxa within the family Flavobacteriaceae. In addition, the KEGG annotation revealed some discrepancies between the presence of genes associated with the utilization of 12 carbon sources and the results obtained from the 20NE phenotypic test (Table 2 and S8).

Ecological distribution analysis of the genus Sediminicola

Among the 37 genome sequences classified within the genus Sediminicola, four cultured strains, 1XM1-17T, YIK13, S. luteus GDMCC 1.2596T and S. arcticus GDMCC 1.2805T were isolated from coastal and deep-sea sediments. The remaining 33 MAGs were recovered from marine sediments and ice core sections in the central Arctic Ocean (Table 1). Although the current genome collection remains limited, source habitat analysis suggested that the genus Sediminicola is widely distributed in marine sediments.

To further characterize the ecological distribution of the genus Sediminicola, we performed a large-scale analysis using MAPseq [38], a reference-based rRNA gene sequence analysis tool applicable to both amplicon and shotgun metagenome sequences. The MAPseq analysis identified 5,213 samples and 171,205 reads across 1,041 projects. Among these, the genus Sediminicola was predominantly detected in aquatic environments, particularly marine (423 samples, 8.11%; 40,369 reads, 23.60%), sediment (462 samples, 8.86%; 7,613 reads, 4.45%), and sea (346 samples, 6.64%; 2065 reads, 1.21%) habitats (Fig. 3D and Table S9). This distribution pattern demonstrated that the genus Sediminicola are predominantly marine-adapted microorganisms, with particular abundances in sediment environments.

Conclusion

In this study, we characterized strain 1XM1-17T, which was isolated from tidal flat sediment through genotypic and phenotypic analyses. Phylogenetic analyses based on the 16S rRNA gene and genome sequences clustered the strain with species of the genus Sediminicola, supporting its classification as a novel species. The dDDH and ANI values corroborated these findings. While showing typical Sediminicola phenotypic and chemotaxonomic features, strain 1XM1-17T presented characteristics distinct from those of related species, such as light-yellow pigmentation, absence of growth at 4 °C or on R2A medium, and lack of milk hydrolysis activity. Genomic taxonomic analysis revealed underestimation of the species diversity of the genus Sediminicola. Functional genome analysis revealed that strain 1XM1-17T harbors conserved genetic determinants involved in nitrogen, sulfur, and phosphorus metabolism, but it is uniquely enriched with multiple CAZymes, suggesting specialized carbohydrate metabolism. The genus Sediminicola has been found to be widely distributed in marine environments, particularly in sediments. Based on comprehensive genomic, phylogenetic, phenotypic, and chemotaxonomic evidences, we propose that strain 1XM1-17T is the type strain of Sediminicola litoralis sp. nov. (GDMCC 1.2384T = KCTC 82665T).

Description of Sediminicola litoralis

Sediminicola litoralis (li.to.ra’lis. L. masc. adj. litoralis, of or belonging to the sea-shore).

Cells are Gram-staining-negative, aerobic, gliding motile, and long rod-shaped. The cellular size is 0.4–0.6 μm wide and 3.2–3.8 μm long. Colonies are light-yellow. Growth occurs at 15–32 °C (optimum, 28 °C), at pH 5.0–9.0 (optimum, 7.0), and with 0.5–3.0.5.0% NaCl concentrations (optimum, 1.0% NaCl, w/v). Positive for oxidase, catalase, and the hydrolysis of aesculin, gelatin, Tweens 20 and 40. Negative for nitrate reduction, indole production, urease, and the hydrolysis of milk, starch and cellulose. Produce carotenoid pigments, but not flexirubin-pigments. The major fatty acids are iso-C15:0, iso-C15:1 G, and summed feature 3 (C16:1 ω6c and/or C16:1 ω7c). The polar lipids include phosphatidylethanolamine, aminophospholipid, and lipids. The respiratory quinone is menaquinone 6.

The type strain, 1XM1-17T (= GDMCC 1.2384T = KCTC 82665T), was isolated from tidal flat sediment, Baicheng beach, Xiamen city, Fujian Province, China. The 16S rRNA gene and genome sequences of strain 1XM1-17T have been deposited at the GenBank/EMBL/DDBJ database with the accession numbers PP434656 and JBBCAS000000000. The genomic size was 3.47 Mbp with the DNA G + C content of 41.12%.

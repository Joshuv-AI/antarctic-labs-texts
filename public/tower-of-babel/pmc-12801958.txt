Comparing the efficacy and safety of the ABC-14 regimen (azacitidine, venetoclax, and chidamide) with traditional “3 + 7” intensive induction regimen or AB-14 regimen (venetoclax combined with azacitidine) in newly diagnosed AML: study protocol for a prospective, multicenter, randomized, open-label clinical trial
Authors: Honghong Yan, Xin Huang, Ping Wu, Chengxin Deng, Minming Li, Peilong Lai, Xin Du, Jianyu Weng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInduction therapy is the first critical step in the overall treatment of Acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The "3 + 7" regimen remains the backbone treatment for newly diagnosed AML patients suitable for intense chemotherapy (IC). However, its efficacy, toxicity, high complications management costs, and prolonged hospitalizations necessitate optimization.The azacitidine-venetoclax (AB) regimen is recommended for elderly or IC-ineligible AML patients, but its efficacy and safety remain suboptimal regarding early mortality and specific leukemia subtypes (e.g., M5, RUNX1-, FLT3-ITD-, TP53-mutated AML, or cases with high MCL-1 expression). Chidamide, a novel oral histone deacetylase inhibitor, counteracts venetoclax-induced MCL-1 upregulation and synergizes with azacitidine-venetoclax to induce AML cell apoptosis. Whether the Azacitidine, Venetoclax, and Chidamide (ABC) regimen can match the "3 + 7" or AB regimen in newly diagnosed AML induction therapy warrants investigation.MethodsNewly diagnosed AML patients will be stratified by IC suitability into unfit-AML and fit-AML. Unfit-AML patients will be randomized to receive ABC-14 or AB-14 regimens. Fit-AML patients will be randomized to receive ABC-14 or "3 + 7" regimens. The primary endpoint is composite complete response rate (CRc). Secondary endpoints include the measurable residual disease (MRD) negative rate, duration of remission (DoR), 1-year Relapsed-free survival (RFS) rate, 1-year overall survival (OS) rate. Exploratory endpoints include the genetic characteristics spectrum of the ABC-14 group, hospital stay duration, treatment costs, blood product transfusion volume, quality of life, and apoptotic index.DiscussionThis study aims to demonstrate that ABC-14 regimen is non-inferior to "3 + 7" regimen in newly diagnosed AML induction therapy while overcoming AB resistance and reducing toxicity associated with "3 + 7". Background

According to the Global Burden Disease (GBD) 2017 report, China recorded 13,200 incident cases of Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and 60,000 AML-related deaths [1]. Both domestic and international guidelines and consensus statements define elderly patients with AML as those aged ≥ 60 years [2–4].

For the past 50 years, the cytotoxic drugs, known as the "3 + 7" regimen (containing anthracyclines/anthraquinones combined with cytarabine) have comprised the standard induction therapy for newly diagnosed AML patients who are fit for intensive chemotherapy [5]. However, this regimen carries substantial risks, including treatment-related mortality, relapse, and toxicity-related morbidity. Notably, long-term survival among elderly AML patients (≥ 60 years) remains poor, with studies reporting rates as low as 1.8% [6, 7].

The VIALE-A study demonstrated that the AB regimen significantly improved CR rate (66.8% vs 29.0%, P < 0.001), duration of response (8.2 m vs 10.7 m, P < 0.001), and OS (14.7 m vs 9.6 m, P < 0.001) compared to azacitidine plus placebo in older or AML patients who are not candidates for intensive chemotherapy [8]. Due to the wide clinical application of AB regimen, it has been clinically observed that venetoclax combined with hypomethylating agents has a lower response rate and survival rate for certain types of AML, such as M5 subtype in the FAB classification, AML1-ETO, MLL rearrangement, AML with RUNX1, FLT3-ITD, PTPN11, or TP53 mutations [9–11]. Therefore, it still does not meet the treatment needs of patients with adverse cytogenetics or specific AML subtypes.

Research has shown that the combination of venetoclax with the histone deacetylase inhibitor (HDAC) panobinostat significantly reduces the IC50 in primary AML cells and enhances sensitivity in TP53-mutated AML cells. Chidamide, an HDAC inhibitor targeting HDAC1, 2, 3, and 10, acts by inducing reactive oxygen species (ROS), promoting DNA double-strand breaks, and downregulating MCL-1 expression. At lower doses, chidamide potentiates venetoclax’s anti-AML activity without exacerbating systemic toxicity and helps overcome venetoclax resistance.

In a preclinical study by Prof. Xu Bing’s group, chidamide administered either alone or in combination with venetoclax significantly inhibited proliferation in AML-M4 (OCI-AML3, NPM1mut) and AML-M5 (MV4-11, MLL-FA4/FLT3-ITDmut) cell lines. Notably, Mcl-1 expression decreased progressively in the order: venetoclax < venetoclax + chidamide < chidamide alone. The combination therapy also markedly reduced tumor burden in AML-M5 mouse models and FLT3-ITD mutated AML PDX models, prolonging survival in these mice.

The experimental research by Prof. Wei Dongxu’s revealed several key findings regarding the treatment of AML with venetoclax and chidamide:

Chidamide modulated key signaling molecules, triggering apoptosis and G0/G1 cell cycle arrest.The venetoclax-chidamide combination potently killed leukemia cells and markedly extended survival in AML murine models.

In 2022, Professor Xu Bing’s team reported at the American Society of Hematology (ASH) meeting that the combination of venetoclax, chidamide, and azacitidine (VCA) significantly reduced leukemia burden and improved survival in a primary CD34 + AML-derived PDX model. This effect was linked to anti-apoptotic protein inhibition and enhanced proteasomal degradation, with chidamide-mediated HDAC inhibition playing a key role.

A single-arm clinical trial demonstrated that the venetoclax-azacitidine-chidamide (VAC) regimen achieved objective response rates (ORR) of 75% after Cycle 1 and 88.9% after Cycle 2 in relapsed/refractory AML. The grade 3 -4 hematologic toxicities included thrombocytopenia (37.9%), neutropenia (34.5%), and febrile neutropenia (37.9%), with no cases of tumor lysis syndrome or treatment interruption reported [12]. Another single-arm study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the ABC regimen (azacitidine, BCL2 inhibitor-venetoclax, and chidamide) on the AML-M5 patients with newly diagnosed and unfit for intensive therapy, reaching a similar conclusion [10].

Our pilot retrospective study compared the ABC regimen versus intensive "3 + 7" chemotherapy in newly diagnosed AML. Preliminary data show superior induction efficacy for ABC (n = 31; CR/CRi = 87.1%), particularly in M4/M5 subtypes (n = 12; CR/CRi = 100%). Notably, the ABC-14 day regimen (n = 16; CR/CRi = 86.7%) achieved comparable CRc rates to both the ABC-28 day (n = 15; 80%) and "3 + 7" groups (n = 81; 68.5%), with lower hematologic adverse events than ABC-28 day and intensive chemotherapy groups [13].

In another retrospective study, we compared the efficacy of ABC-14 with the “3 + 7” regimen and found the following results: 1) After one cycle of ABC-14 treatment, the CRc among 81 AML patients was 77.4%, with 60% achieving MRD negativity. There was no statistical difference in the CRc rate between the ABC-14 group and the IC group (77.4% vs. 66.7%, P = 0.183). However, the overall response rate (ORR; CRc + partial remission [PR]) of the ABC-14 group was significantly higher (92.5% vs. 75.3%, P = 0.012). There was no statistical difference between the M4/M5 subtypes (73.9% vs. 76.2%, P = 0.838). 2) According to the 2022 European Leukemia Network (ELN) stratification, the CRc rates for patients with favorable, intermediate, and adverse risk were 71.4%, 70.6%, and 82.1%, respectively. 3) The platelet recovery time of the ABC-14 group was significantly shorter than that of the IC group (10.86 ± 14.73 days vs. 20.87 ± 9.83 days, P = 0.002). However, prospective randomized controlled studies on the efficacy and safety of the ABC-14 regimen have not been reported, and the conclusions need to be confirmed by further research.

To address current clinical needs, we initiated a phase II clinical trial to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the ABC-14 regimen versus “3 + 7” or AB-14 regimen in newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia patients.

Randomization

Eligible participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to the experimental or control group within their respective cohort (fit-AML or unfit-AML) using simple randomization method by using R software (version 4.3.2 or above). An independent biostatistician, who is not involved in the trial conduct or analysis, will generate the computer-based allocation sequence using variable block randomization, the results of random assignment were saved using sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-proof sealed envelopes.

The site principal investigator or delegated sub-investigator is responsible for enrolling participants by confirming eligibility and obtaining informed consent. The Clinicians or study nurse then performs the randomization and assigns the participant to the treatment group based on the system’s output.The randomization list is recorded and preserved by the responsible independent personnel. Investigator and patients were not masked to treatment allocation.

During the induction period, patients should not use anti-leukemia drugs other than the treatment regimen of this study (except for hydroxyurea and low-dose cytarabine). For patients who achieve CR/CRi/MLFS, the second course can be repeated (the second induction cycle for the "3 + 7" regimen, switching to the "2 + 5" regimen). Subsequently, they will receive post-remission consolidation therapy and maintenance therapy in accordance with current guideline recommendations or clinical practice. For patients who achieve partial remission (PR), the original regimen should be repeated for one cycle, followed by another bone marrow assessment. If CR/CRi/MLFS is achieved, patients can directly proceed to the consolidation therapy phase. If there is no treatment response (NR), patients in the Fit group can cross over to the other treatment group, while patients in the Unfit group receiving the AB-14 regimen can cross over to the ABC-14 regimen, or they may choose to withdraw from the study.

Stopping/withdrawal criteria

Criteria for permanent discontinuation

1) Severe allergic reaction to the study drug: such as anaphylactic shock, severe rash (such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome SJS/toxic epidermal necrolysis TEN), etc. 2) Organ-specific toxicity: such as severe cardiotoxicity (such as significant prolongation of the QTc interval), pulmonary toxicity (such as interstitial pneumonia) or neurotoxicity, etc. 3) disease progression; 4) Subject withdrawal; 5) Researcher’s judgment.

Blood transfusion threshold

The indication for blood transfusion in this study is determined by the investigator according to clinical routine. Normally, according to the diagnosis and treatment routine of China’s relevant clinical blood use technical specifications, HB < 70 g/L prophylactic infusion will be given, and in case of special circumstances, red blood cell transfusion is not limited to HB < 70 g/L. Prophylactic platelet transfusion is recommended for PLT < 10, and platelet transfusion is not limited to PLT < 10 in the presence of active bleeding.

Salvage treatment

This study initiates a clinical study for the investigator, focusing on the induction treatment stage of newly diagnosed AML. To be more relevant to real-world clinical practice, other stages of treatment or rescue are recommended to follow current clinical routine. If the patient fails to obtain the treatment effect or progresses after 1–2 courses of induction therapy, the patient will be discharged from the group and given rescue treatment according to the clinical routine of the research center, and the specific plan will be jointly decided by the investigator and the patient.

Follow-up

After completion of treatment, all participants will be followed for overall survival (OS) and relapse-free survival (RFS) for a minimum of 12 months after the last participant is randomized. The study will conclude at the final data lock point, defined as the date when all surviving participants have completed the 12-month follow-up or have been lost to follow-up. The primary database will be locked after this point for the final analysis.

MRD assessment

The primary modality is multiparameter flow cytometry (MFC) with a sensitivity target of 10⁻4 (minimum 10⁻3 acceptable), and error-corrected NGS is a key secondary method. Bone marrow aspirates are collected at d14 ± 2 and d28 ± 7. MRD negativity is defined as < 0.1% leukemic cells by MFC or below the limit of detection (≤ 10⁻4 VAF) by NGS.

A harmonized ≥ 8–10-color MFC panel is used, with acquisition performed locally under standardized protocols. NGS testing is centralized. To ensure consistency, all MFC files undergo blinded central re-analysis for ≥ 10% of randomly selected samples and all discrepant cases. All MRD assessors are blinded to treatment assignment and clinical outcomes. External quality assessment is implemented where available. Samples not meeting quality criteria are deemed not evaluable and excluded from the primary analysis.

MRD negativity rates (MFC primary; NGS secondary) at d14 ± 2 and d28 ± 7 will be compared between arms, with sensitivity analyses restricted to samples achieving 10⁻4. Discordant MFC/NGS results are summarized descriptively and adjudicated centrally for QC.

We have implemented a harmonized training and quality control program to minimize inter-site variability. Key elements include: (1) mandatory pre-activation training and proficiency testing; (2) assay standardization via uniform SOPs, panels, and gating rules; and (3) ongoing blinded central re-review of ≥ 10% of assessments with adjudication of discrepancies and triggered retraining if needed. All data and QC logs are centrally archived.

Data management

Screening, obtaining informed consent, enrollment, registration, and data collection will be conducted by investigators or qualified clinical research coordinators. All essential documents and the final database will be retained for at least 15 years after study completion. The database will be locked after quality review and sponsor approval; no changes are permitted thereafter without formal amendment.

Statistical analysis

No independent DMC and no interim analyses are planned for this study based on the following scientific justification: This is a Phase II, randomized study primarily focused on evaluating preliminary efficacy and safety in Acute myeloid leukemia. The primary goal is to gather initial evidence to inform the design of potential future Phase III studies, rather than making definitive efficacy or safety claims that would necessitate early stopping for overwhelming efficacy.

Sample size

The primary endpoint is the overall response rate (CR + CRi + MLFS) after one or two cycles of treatment. Based on literature reports and historical data, the CR/CRi rate of "3 + 7" regimen for AML is 60% to 80%. The GDPH-AML 2010 study showed a CR/CRi rate of 65.8%, taking the midpoint value of 70%. Additionally, the CR/CRi rate for AB regimen of unfit AML is 66.4% according to the VIALE-A study [10]. Real-world studies showed CR/CRi/MLFS rates of 48.3% and 47.8% for AB-14d and AB-28d regimens, respectively (P > 0.999). Our study showed an induction CR/CRi rate of 84% for the ABC regimen, and CR/CRi rates of 80% and 86.7% for the ABC-28d and ABC-14d regimens, respectively (P > 0.05) [16].

The choice of a 10% absolute non-inferiority (NI) margin for the primary endpoint (CRc rate) is clinically justified for both cohorts based on the preserved fraction of the active control’s effect and the minimal clinically important difference (MCID).

For the Fit-AML cohort (vs. “3 + 7”), the control’s effect size is ~ 65% (70% minus 5% for supportive care). Preserving 50% would be overconservative at 32.5%; 10% preserves > 80% of the effect and aligns with clinical consensus on non-inferiority. For the Unfit-AML cohort (vs. AB-14), the control’s effect over azacitidine is ~ 31.7%. A 10% margin is stricter than the 15.85% derived from 50% preservation and is clinically acceptable given potential benefits in other endpoints.

In both cohorts, the 10% NI margin is justified as it ensures the experimental regimen retains a substantial proportion of the control’s clinical benefit while being a difference that would not alter the overall risk–benefit assessment in clinical practice.

Analysis Sets

Intention-to-Treat (ITT) refers to all randomized participants, regardless of the treatment they actually received or their compliance with the study protocol. Participants will be analyzed according to the treatment group to which they were originally randomly assigned. This is the primary analysis set for all efficacy endpoints.

Modified intention-to-treat (mITT) refers to all randomized subjects who received at least one dose of the investigational drug and completed at least one efficacy assessment.

Safety Set (SS) refers to all subjects who received at least one dose of the investigational drug and had at least one post-baseline safety assessment. All safety information collected from the patients, including adverse events and laboratory test results, will be evaluated.

Per Protocol Set (PPS) refers to the subset of patients within the FAS set who have no major protocol deviations, which will be defined and documented prior to database lock. This data set includes patients who demonstrated good compliance, did not take any prohibited medications during the study, and completed all required electronic case report form entries as instructed. Missing data in this data set will not be imputed and will be handled as missing data. We defined a permissible window of ± 7 days for response assessments. Data obtained outside this window will be noted and included in sensitivity analyses to evaluate the impact on the findings. The supportive sensitivity analysis will be performed in the PPS Set to confirm the consistency of the primary results.

Statistical methods

Count data is summarized using frequency and percentage. Measurement data is summarized using mean, standard deviation, median, maximum value, and minimum value. The statistical analysis is conducted using R software (version 4.3.2 or above). Unless otherwise specified, inferential statistical analysis will calculate a two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI). Statistical signifcance will be considered for P < 0.05.

The primary endpoint (CRc rate) will be analyzed in the ITT population using an adjusted risk difference (RD) with two-sided 95% CI. Non-inferiority will be concluded if the lower bound of the CI is above -10% (equivalent to one-sided α = 0.025). The primary model will adjust for prespecified stratification factors: study centre and ELN 2022 risk category.

Prespecified subgroup analyses (e.g., by age, ELN risk, baseline WBC) will include interaction tests to explore effect modification. These remain exploratory due to limited power.

For secondary endpoints, binary outcomes will be analyzed similarly (adjusted RD). The DoR, RFS, OS will be analyzed using stratified log-rank tests and Cox models (adjusted for centre and ELN risk), reporting hazard ratios with CIs. Kaplan–Meier estimates will be used for rate comparisons at timepoints.The Z-test is used to compare the 1-year RFS or 1-year OS between treatment groups.

Multiplicity control

Separate Hypothesis Families: The fit and unfit AML cohorts will be treated as two distinct families of hypotheses. The overall Type I error (α = 0.05) will be controlled separately within each cohort, thus completely avoiding any potential alpha bleed-through between the two different comparisons.

Hierarchical Testing (Gatekeeping) Procedure: Within each cohort, we will employ a fixed-sequence hierarchical testing strategy for the key secondary endpoints. The pre-specified testing order is: Fit Cohort: (1) MRD-negative CR/CRi rate → (2) RFS → (3) OS. Unfit Cohort: (1) MRD-negative CR/CRi rate → (2)RFS → (3) OS. Claims of statistical significance for a given endpoint are contingent upon achieving a P < 0.05 for all endpoints preceding it in the sequence.

Exploratory Endpoints: Endpoints such as DoR, PROs, and economic outcomes are clearly designated as exploratory. Analyses of these endpoints will be descriptive and unadjusted for multiplicity, to be interpreted with caution as hypothesis-generating findings.

Safety analysis

Adverse events occurring during the treatment period will be summarized based on a comprehensive summary of adverse events by treatment group, classified by System Organ Class (SOC) and Preferred Term (PT), and relevant adverse event lists will be provided. The number of cases and incidence rates will be calculated based on SOC and PT (the number of cases: subjects who have experienced at least one occurrence of a specific adverse event); if the same subject experiences the same adverse event multiple times, only the most severe occurrence will be included in the analysis.

Exploratory analysis

We plan to using the correlation analysis the relationship between the genetic characteristics and treatment outcomes in the ABC group, using nonparametric tests the difference in the length of hospital stay and costs, red blood cell and platelet transfusion volume between treatment groups. Repeated measures analysis of variance will be used to compare differences in quality of life scores among the groups. Correlation analysis will be used to explore the relationship between the apoptosis ratio in AML patient samples using BH3 analysis and treatment response. A t-test will be used to compare the difference in apoptosis rates between different clinical efficacy patients in the ABC group (achieving CRc vs. not achieving CRc). Additionally, ROC curve analysis will be used to determine the optimal diagnostic threshold for the apoptosis ratio. Predictive grouping based on the diagnostic threshold of the apoptosis rate will be performed, and Kaplan–Meier survival analysis will be used to explore differences in DoR, 1-year RFS, and OS among different groups.

Patient-Reported Outcomes (PROs) Assessment and Analysis Plan

The EORTC QLQ-C30 (v3.0) will be administered electronically (or via paper backup) using validated local language versions at baseline, end of induction cycles, during maintenance, and at end of treatment. Sites will receive training to standardize administration and minimize missing data.

Missing items will be handled per EORTC guidelines: scales with > 50% missing items will be considered missing; otherwise, prorated scores will be calculated. Missing assessments will not be imputed in the primary analysis.

The primary analysis will evaluate mean change from baseline in key scales using mixed models for repeated measures (MMRM), adjusted for baseline score and stratification factors. Minimal Important Differences (MIDs) of ≥ 10 points will be used for clinical interpretation.

Discussion

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) remains a devastating malignancy, causing 85,000 global deaths in 2016 with 2.6 million years of life lost—figures projected to double by 2040 [17].

Elderly AML patients generally have poor overall health, multiple comorbidities, and a high incidence of unfavorable cytogenetics and adverse gene mutations, which contribute to their worse clinical prognosis compared to younger patients. The 5-year overall survival (OS) rate declines sharply with age: nearly 50% for patients < 50 years, < 25% for ages 60–65, and < 10% for those ≥ 70 years [18]. Consequently, both the European Leukemia Net (ELN) and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) AML guidelines list separate recommended treatment regimens for patients aged ≥ 60 years and < 60 years [19].

Furthermore, AML patients are assessed for suitability for intensified therapy based on age, physical fitness score, and comorbidities. For fit patients eligible for intensive therapy, the goal is cure achievement through induction chemotherapy followed by consolidation or hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). For unfit patients, therapy focuses on survival prolongation and quality-of-life enhancement via lower-intensity regimens and/or supportive care.

Although the "3 + 7" regimen has served as the standard induction therapy for AML patients tolerating intensive chemotherapy for nearly half a century, it is associated with poor prognosis in elderly (≥ 60 years) AML and high-risk AML patients due to unfavorable cytogenetic factors. Furthermore, these patient subgroups exhibit heightened vulnerability to treatment complications, drug toxicities, and acquired resistance with conventional "3 + 7" therapy, collectively contributing to inferior clinical outcomes. [20–22].

Preclinical and clinical evidence demonstrates that adding HDAC inhibitor chidamide to venetoclax/azacitidine regimens produces synergistic effects—enhancing apoptosis in leukemia stem cells and overcoming primary/acquired resistance to intensive chemotherapy. Early clinical observations further suggest the ABC-14 regimen may offer superior efficacy and tolerability versus "3 + 7" or AB regimens. These findings necessitate randomized controlled trials comparing ABC-14 with standard therapies in newly diagnosed AML.

This phase II trial employs a head-to-head randomized design with tolerability-based stratification to evaluate ABC-14’s efficacy and safety. Correlative studies will integrate genomic profiling and BH3 mimetic sensitivity testing to predict treatment response. Pharmacoeconomic analysis and quality-of-life assessments will inform value-based clinical decision-making.

Rigorously compliant with SPIRIT [23] and CONSORT [24] guidelines, this protocol minimizes bias while providing comprehensive safety and efficacy data. If successful, ABC-14 could establish a novel cost-effective therapeutic paradigm for AML management.

Kronecker differences
Keegan Doig Anderson, Yorick Hardy, Bertin Zinsou

Abstract
. Over the real numbers, the Kronecker sum is the unique operation on matrices which exponentiates to the Kronecker product. Kronecker quotients provide an algebraic view of decompositions of matrices in terms of Kronecker products. This article explores families of operations, Kronecker differences, which are a kind of “inverse” for Kronecker sums. The correspondence between Kronecker differences and Kronecker quotients is explored. Furthermore, we show that a certain class of Kronecker differences may be characterized by families of matrices with these families again being expressed as Kronecker products. This approach provides a different “nonlinear” view towards tensor decomposition.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{m}
denote the vector space of
m\times m
matrices
over the field
\mathbb{F}
with characteristic
\chr(\mathbb{F})
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We will denote
by
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{mn}
the tensor
product of
\mathbb{F}_{m}
and
\mathbb{F}_{n}
. By the matrix
E_{ij}

we mean a matrix in
\mathbb{F}_{m}
with a 1 in row
i
and column
j
and
0 in every other entry. In general, the order of the matrix
E_{ij}
will
be clear from the context. The trace
\tr(A)
of a matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}

A
.

1.1.
Kronecker products and Kronecker quotients

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{s}
. The Kronecker product

[
3
,
6
,
12
]

A\otimes B\in\mathbb{F}_{ms}
is the
ms\times ms
matrix over
F
with entries (here
:=
denotes a definition)

(1)

(A\otimes B)_{(i-1)s+p,(j-1)s+q}:=(A)_{i,j}(B)_{p,q},

where

i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and

p,q\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
.
In other words, we can write in block matrix form

A\otimes B=\begin{bmatrix}(A)_{1,1}B&(A)_{1,2}B&\ldots&(A)_{1,n}B\\
(A)_{2,1}B&(A)_{2,2}B&\ldots&(A)_{2,n}B\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(A)_{m,1}B&(A)_{m,2}B&\ldots&(A)_{m,n}B\end{bmatrix}.

We note that the Kronecker product may be viewed as a family of operations
on vector spaces of square matrices (indexed by the orders of the matrices involved),
or as an operation on the graded vector space of all square matrices. In other words,
the Kronecker product is the family of operations

\otimes:\mathbb{F}_{m}\times\mathbb{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{mn}
indexed by
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the same way that a Kronecker product is a family
of operations on various matrix vector spaces (or on the graded vector space of
all square matrices), a Kronecker quotient may be viewed as a family of operations
on various matrix vector spaces (or as a partially defined operation on the graded
vector space of all square matrices). In other words, we consider the family

\oslash:\mathbb{F}_{mn}\times\mathbb{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}
indexed by
m,n\in\mathbb{N}

satisfying the following definition.

Definition 1

(Kronecker quotient
[
4
,
7
]
)
.

A (right) Kronecker quotient is an operation
\oslash
satisfying

(A\otimes B)\oslash B=A

for all matrices
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}

(
B
non-zero) and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In general,
(M\oslash A)\otimes A\neq M
for
mn\times mn
matrices
M

and
n\times n
non-zero matrices
A
. A detailed study of Kronecker quotients
appears in
[
4
]
. Throughout this article, we will consider
only right Kronecker quotients and differences, analogous results hold for
left Kronecker quotients and differences in an obvious way. Uniform Kronecker
quotients are of particular interest, since the family of operations are
related to each other via algebraic equations. More precisely, there is a generating
operation for the entire family in the algebraically obvious way:

Definition 2
.

A Kronecker quotient
\oslash
is a uniform Kronecker quotient if

(A\otimes C)\oslash B=A\otimes(C\oslash B)

for all matrices
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
C\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
m,n,p\in\mathbb{N}
,
and

(A+B)\oslash C=(A\oslash C)+(B\oslash C),\qquad(kA)\oslash C=k(A\oslash C)

for all matrices
A
,
B
and
C
, and scalars
k\in\mathbb{F}
whenever the operations are defined.

Remark 1
.

An equivalent definition for uniform Kronecker quotients
is as follows, by simply setting
p=1
in Definition
2
:
A Kronecker quotient
\oslash
is a uniform Kronecker quotient if

(A\otimes C)\oslash B=A\otimes(C\oslash B)

for all matrices
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
B,C\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and

(A+B)\oslash C=(A\oslash C)+(B\oslash C),

(kA)\oslash C=k(A\oslash C)

for all matrices
A
,
B
and
C
, and scalars
k\in\mathbb{F}
whenever the operations are defined.

In
[
4
]
, examples of uniform Kronecker quotients over the real
and complex numbers are given. The following lemma establishes the existence
of a uniform Kronecker quotient over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{F}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field.
Then there exists a uniform Kronecker quotient over
\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

Let
B,C\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
with
C\neq 0
. Then there exists
an non-zero entry
(C)_{i,j}\neq 0
in
C
. Now let
f:\mathbb{F}_{m}\to\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}

be defined by
f(C)=(i,j)
and
f(0_{m})=(1,1)
, i.e.
f
chooses a non-zero entry of
C
, unless

C
is the zero matrix. Define
\text{---}\oslash C

as the linear extension of

(A\otimes B)\oslash C:=A\dfrac{(B)_{f(C)}}{(C)_{f(C)}}

for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{s}
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\oslash
is a uniform Kronecker quotient by construction.
∎

1.2.
Kronecker sums and Kronecker differences

The Kronecker sum
A\oplus B
of an
m\times m
matrix
A
and an

n\times n
matrix
B
is given by
[
6
, pp. 268]

A\oplus B=A\otimes I_{n}+I_{m}\otimes B.

Over the real and complex numbers, we have

\exp(A\oplus B)=\exp(A)\otimes\exp(B).

The Kronecker sum uniquely satisfies the above equation. The Kronecker sum
also arises in the study of matrix equations of the form

BX+XA^{T}=Y

where
X
is the subject of the equation. Taking the vectorization (vec)
on both sides of the equation, and using the fact that
\vc(ABC)=(C^{T}\otimes A)\vc(B)
,
yields
[
6
, pp. 268]

(A\oplus B)\vc(X)=\vc(Y).

This article defines and studies Kronecker differences and their connection with Kronecker
quotients
[
4
]
.
Similar to the definition of Kronecker quotients, we consider the family
\ominus:\mathbb{F}_{mn}\times\mathbb{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}

indexed by
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying the following definition:

Definition 3
.

A Kronecker difference is an operation
\ominus
satisfying

(A\oplus B)\ominus B=A

for all matrices
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,

m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Every Kronecker quotient induces a Kronecker difference in the following way.

Definition 4
.

Let
\oslash
be a Kronecker quotient. The Kronecker difference
\ominus
induced by
\oslash
is the
Kronecker difference given by

M\ominus B:=(M-I_{m}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n}

for all
M\in\mathbb{F}_{mn}
,
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 2
.

It is easily verified that this expression satisfies the condition for a
Kronecker difference, i.e. for every
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle(A\oplus B)\ominus B

\displaystyle=(A\otimes I_{n}+I_{m}\otimes B)\ominus B

\displaystyle=(A\otimes I_{n}+I_{m}\otimes B-I_{m}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=(A\otimes I_{n})\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=A.

Remark 3

(Duality 1)
.

Definition
4
has a weak dual, namely: Let
\oslash
be a Kronecker quotient such that

M\oslash B=(M(0_{m}\oplus B)^{-1})\ominus 0_{n}

for all
M\in\mathbb{F}_{mn}
, invertible
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
where
\ominus
is a Kronecker difference. Indeed, for invertible
B
,

(0_{m}\oplus B)^{-1}=(I_{m}\otimes B)^{-1}=I_{m}\otimes B^{-1}
and

\displaystyle(A\otimes B)\oslash B

\displaystyle=((A\otimes I_{n})(I_{m}\otimes B))\oslash B

\displaystyle=((A\otimes I_{n})(I_{m}\otimes B)(I_{m}\otimes B)^{-1})\ominus 0_{n}

\displaystyle=(A\oplus 0_{n})\ominus 0_{n}

\displaystyle=A.

The notion of uniformity in Kronecker quotients has an analogue for Kronecker differences.

Definition 5
.

A Kronecker difference
\ominus
is a uniform Kronecker difference if

(A\oplus C)\ominus B=A\oplus(C\ominus B)

for all matrices
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
C\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
m,n,p\in\mathbb{N}
,
and

(A+B)\ominus(C+D)=(A\ominus C)+(B\ominus D),\qquad(kA)\ominus(kC)=k(A\ominus C)

for all matrices
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
, and scalars
k\in\mathbb{F}
whenever the operations are defined.

Once again, we immediately have a connection between uniform Kronecker quotients and uniform Kronecker differences.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\oslash
be a uniform Kronecker quotient.
Then the induced Kronecker difference
\ominus
is uniform.

Proof.

Following the definition in Definition
4
,

M\ominus B:=(M-I_{m}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n}

for all
M\in\mathbb{F}_{mn}
,
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
where
\oslash
is a uniform Kronecker quotient.
In Remark
2
it was shown that
\ominus
is a Kronecker difference,
it remains to show that this Kronecker difference is uniform.
We have for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
, and
C\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
,

\displaystyle(A\oplus C)\ominus B

\displaystyle=(A\otimes I_{p}\otimes I_{n}+I_{m}\otimes C-I_{m}\otimes I_{p}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=(A\otimes I_{p}\otimes I_{n})\oslash I_{n}+(I_{m}\otimes C)\oslash I_{n}-(I_{m}\otimes I_{p}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=A\otimes I_{p}+I_{m}\otimes(C\oslash I_{n})-I_{m}\otimes I_{p}\otimes(B\oslash I_{n})

\displaystyle=A\otimes I_{p}+I_{m}\otimes[(C\oslash I_{n})-I_{p}\otimes(B\oslash I_{n})]

\displaystyle=A\otimes I_{p}+I_{m}\otimes[(C-I_{p}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n}]

\displaystyle=A\otimes I_{p}+I_{m}\otimes(C\ominus B)

\displaystyle=A\oplus(C\ominus B).

Finally, for all
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{mn}
and
C,D\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,

\displaystyle(A+B)\ominus(C+D)

\displaystyle=(A+B-I_{m}\otimes C-I_{m}\otimes D)\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=(A-I_{m}\otimes C+B-I_{m}\otimes D)\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=(A-I_{m}\otimes C)\oslash I_{n}+(B-I_{m}\otimes D)\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=A\ominus C+B\ominus D

and for any scalar
k\in\mathbb{F}
,

\displaystyle(kA)\ominus(kC)

\displaystyle=(kA-I_{m}\otimes(kC))\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=[k(A-I_{m}\otimes C)]\oslash I_{n}

\displaystyle=k[(A-I_{m}\otimes C)\oslash I_{n}]

\displaystyle=k(A\ominus C).\qed

and technical results.
The presentations of these definitions and technicalities are left for Appendix

2.
Properties of Kronecker sums and Kronecker differences

Kronecker sums obey the following identities. See for example
[
11
,
12
]
and their consequences.

(S1)

\displaystyle(A\oplus B)^{T}

\displaystyle=A^{T}\oplus B^{T}

(S2)

\displaystyle\tr(A\oplus B)

\displaystyle=n\tr A+m\tr B

(S3)

\displaystyle(kA)\oplus(kB)

\displaystyle=k(A\oplus B)

(S4)

\displaystyle(A+B)\oplus(C+D)

\displaystyle=(A\oplus C)+(B\oplus D)

(S5)

\displaystyle(A\oplus B)\oplus C

\displaystyle=A\oplus(B\oplus C)

(S6)

\displaystyle[A\oplus B,C\oplus D]

\displaystyle=[A,C]\oplus[B,D]

(S7)

\displaystyle\exp(A\oplus B)

\displaystyle=\exp(A)\otimes\exp(B)

Let
\sigma(A,B):=A\oplus B
. Then (
S3
) and (
S4
) become

\sigma(k(A,B))=k\sigma(A,B),\qquad\sigma((A,C)+(B,D))=\sigma(A,C)+\sigma(B,D).

In other words,
\sigma
is linear if and only if (
S3
) and (
S4
) hold.

We will investigate the corresponding equations for Kronecker differences.
The equations do not hold in general, but each equation holds true if
the left hand argument of
\ominus
is an appropriate Kronecker difference.
For example, (
D1
) below holds when
A=C\oplus B
, i.e.

((C\oplus B)\ominus B)^{T}=C^{T}=(C^{T}\oplus B^{T})\ominus B^{T}=(C\oplus B)^{T}\ominus B^{T}.

(D1)

\displaystyle(A\ominus B)^{T}

\displaystyle=A^{T}\ominus B^{T}\phantom{\dfrac{1}{n}}

(D2)

\displaystyle\tr(A\ominus B)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}(\tr A-m\tr B)

(D3)

\displaystyle(kA)\ominus(kB)

\displaystyle=k(A\ominus B)\phantom{\dfrac{1}{n}}

(D4)

\displaystyle(A+B)\ominus(C+D)

\displaystyle=(A\ominus C)+(B\ominus D)\phantom{\dfrac{1}{n}}

(D5)

\displaystyle(A\ominus B)\ominus C

\displaystyle=A\ominus(C\oplus B)\phantom{\dfrac{1}{n}}

(D6)

\displaystyle[A\ominus B,C\ominus D]

\displaystyle=[A,C]\ominus[B,D]\phantom{\dfrac{1}{n}}

(D7)

\displaystyle\exp(A\ominus B)

\displaystyle=\exp(A)\oslash\exp(B)\phantom{\dfrac{1}{n}}

Let
\delta(A,B):=A\ominus B
. Thus,
\delta:\mathbb{F}_{mn}\times\mathbb{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}
satisfies

\delta(A\oplus B,B)=A.

Equations (
D3
) and (
D4
) become

(2)

\delta(k(A,B))=k\delta(A,B),\qquad\delta((A,C)+(B,D))=\delta(A,C)+\delta(B,D).

In other words, if
\ominus
satisfies (
D3
) and (
D4
) then
\delta
is linear.
In Section
3
, we will consider a canonical form for linear
\delta
and
its consequences for (
D1
), (
D2
) and (
D5
).

Proposition 2
.

Let
\oslash
be a Kronecker quotient and let
\ominus

be the induced Kronecker difference

X\ominus B:=(X-I_{m}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n}

for all
X\in\mathbb{F}_{mn}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
.

(a)

If
(A\oslash B)^{T}=A^{T}\oslash B^{T}

then
(A\ominus B)^{T}=A^{T}\ominus B^{T}
.
\dfrac{1}{n}

(b)

If
\tr(A\oslash B)=\dfrac{\tr(A)}{\tr(B)}

then
\tr(A\ominus B)=\dfrac{1}{n}(\tr(A)-m\tr(B))
.

(c)

If
\oslash
is uniform then
\ominus
is uniform.
\dfrac{1}{n}

(d)

If
(kA)\oslash B=k(A\oslash B)

then
(kA)\ominus(kB)=k(A\ominus B)
.
\dfrac{1}{n}

(e)

If
(A+C)\oslash B=A\oslash B+C\oslash B

(A+C)\ominus(B+D)=A\ominus B+C\ominus D
.
\dfrac{1}{n}

Proof.

The proof of (c) appears in Proposition
1
, which also proves (d) and (e). The proofs of
(a)
and
(b)
each follow
by straightforward calculation:

(A\ominus B)^{T}=((A-I_{m}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n})^{T}=(A-I_{m}\otimes B)^{T}\oslash I_{n}\\
=(A^{T}-I_{m}\otimes B^{T})\oslash I_{n}=A^{T}\ominus B^{T},

\tr(A\ominus B)=\tr((A-I_{m}\otimes B)\oslash I_{n})\\
=\dfrac{\tr(A-I_{m}\otimes B)}{n}=\dfrac{1}{n}(\tr(A)-m\tr(B)).\qed

3.
A canonical form

Now we turn our attention to Kronecker differences satisfying (
D3
) and (
D4
).
It follows that such a Kronecker difference is given, according to (
2
), by a linear
map
\delta:(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n})\times\mathbb{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}
. Before the proposition, we remind the reader of the definitions of the block trace in Definition
6
and partial trace in Definition
7
on page
6
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\delta:(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n})\times\mathbb{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}

be a linear map which satisfies
\delta(A\oplus B,B)=A
for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
.
Then there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}

such that

\delta(A,B)=\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(A\otimes I_{m}-I_{m}\otimes B\otimes I_{m})),\qquad\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(X\otimes I_{n}\otimes I_{m}))=X,\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}

for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
X\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
.

Proof.

A basis for
(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n})\times\mathbb{F}_{n}
is given by

(E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl},0)
and
(0,E_{kl})
for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
k,l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Then
\delta
is defined by its action on this basis:

\delta(E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl},0):=\sum_{r,s=1}^{m}\alpha_{ij;kl;rs}E_{rs},\qquad\delta(0,E_{kl}):=\sum_{r,s=1}^{m}\beta_{kl;rs}E_{rs}.

Define

\alpha=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,r,s=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\alpha_{ij;kl;rs}E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes E_{sr},\qquad\beta=\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\sum_{r,s=1}^{m}\beta_{kl;rs}E_{kl}\otimes E_{sr},

so that

\delta(E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl},0)=\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes I_{m})),\qquad\delta(0,E_{kl})=\Btr(\beta^{T}(E_{kl}\otimes I_{m})).\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}

Since
(A\oplus B,B)=(A\oplus 0,0)+(0\oplus B,B)
,
the condition

\delta(A\oplus B,B)=\delta(A\oplus 0,0)+\delta(0\oplus B,B)=\delta(A\otimes I_{n},0)+\delta(I_{m}\otimes B,B)=A\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}

reduces by linearity to

\delta(E_{ij}\otimes I_{n},0)=E_{ij},\qquad\delta(I_{m}\otimes E_{kl},E_{kl})=0.

The second equation yields that

\delta(I_{m}\otimes E_{kl},E_{kl})=0\quad\iff\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{ii;kl;rs}+\beta_{kl;rs}=0.

It follows that
\beta=-\tr_{1}(\alpha)
. Writing
A
and
B
in the form

A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}a_{ij;kl}E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl},\qquad B=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}b_{kl}E_{kl}

yields

\displaystyle\delta(A,B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}a_{ij;kl}\delta(E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl},0)+\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}b_{kl}\delta(0,E_{kl})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}a_{ij;kl}\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes I_{m}))-\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}b_{kl}\Btr(\tr_{1}(\alpha)^{T}(E_{kl}\otimes I_{m}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}a_{ij;kl}\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes I_{m}))-\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}b_{kl}\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(I_{m}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes I_{m}))

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(A\otimes I_{m}-I_{m}\otimes B\otimes I_{m})).\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}

Applying this formula to the condition
\delta(E_{ij}\otimes I_{n},0)=E_{ij}
provides

\delta(E_{ij}\otimes I_{n},0)=E_{ij}\quad\iff\quad\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(E_{ij}\otimes I_{n}\otimes I_{m}-0))=E_{ij}.\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{m}}

Thus,
\tr_{12}(\alpha^{T}(X\otimes I_{n}\otimes I_{m}))=X
for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
.
∎

Remark 5
.

When
\delta

\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}
if and only if
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}

and
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
.
∎

4.
Uniform Kronecker differences

In Remark
7
, we noted that if
A
and
B
are matrices of the
same order
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then a linear Kronecker difference is given by
A\ominus B=\tr(\upsilon_{n}^{T}(A-B))

for some matrix
\upsilon_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
with
\tr(\upsilon_{n})=1
. These matrices
\upsilon_{n}

may be viewed as generators of uniform Kronecker differences.
In Definition
5
, it is given that a uniform Kronecker difference
is linear, and hence has a canonical form given in Theorem
1
. The
form given in Lemma
3
inspires the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\upsilon_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a sequence of matrices with

\tr(\upsilon_{n})=1
. Then the Kronecker difference defined below is uniform, where

A\ominus B=\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(A\otimes I_{m}-I_{m}\otimes B\otimes I_{m})),\qquad\alpha_{m,n}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}E_{ij}\otimes\upsilon_{n}\otimes E_{ji},

for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3
, the operation
\ominus
is a linear Kronecker difference.
Let
\sigma_{m,p}
be the vec permutation matrix
[
5
]
which obeys
\sigma_{m,p}\sigma_{m,p}^{T}=\sigma_{m,p}^{T}\sigma_{m,p}=I_{mp}
, and

\sigma_{m,p}(A\otimes B)\sigma_{m,p}^{T}=B\otimes A

for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We have for
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
C\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
, and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle(A\oplus C)\ominus B

\displaystyle=\delta(A\oplus C,B)

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(A\otimes I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes I_{mp}+I_{m}\otimes C\otimes I_{mp}-I_{mp}\otimes B\otimes I_{mp}))

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(A\otimes I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes I_{mp}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes C\otimes I_{mp}-I_{mp}\otimes B\otimes I_{mp})).

Then, expanding the two terms on the right hand side yields

\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(A\otimes I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes I_{mp}))=\sum_{i,j=1}^{mp}\tr_{12}((E_{ji}(A\otimes I_{p}))\otimes v_{n}\otimes E_{ij}=A\otimes I_{p},

and

\displaystyle\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes C\otimes I_{mp}-I_{mp}\otimes B\otimes I_{mp}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr_{12}\left((E_{ji}\otimes E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{n}^{T}\otimes E_{ij}\otimes E_{uv})(I_{m}\otimes[C-I_{p}\otimes B]\otimes I_{m}\otimes I_{p}))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr_{12}\left((E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{n}^{T}\otimes\delta_{ij}E_{ij}\otimes E_{uv})([C-I_{p}\otimes B]\otimes I_{m}\otimes I_{p}))\right),

where we used that
\tr(E_{ji})\otimes E_{ij}=\delta_{ij}E_{ij}
. Noting that
\sigma_{m,p}^{T}\sigma_{m,p}=I_{mp}
,

\displaystyle\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes C\otimes I_{mp}-I_{mp}\otimes B\otimes I_{mp}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr_{12}\left((E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{n}^{T}\otimes I_{m}\otimes E_{uv})([C-I_{p}\otimes B]\otimes I_{p}\otimes I_{m}))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr_{12}\big((I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes\sigma_{m,p}^{T})(I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes\sigma_{m,p})(E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{n}^{T}\otimes I_{m}\otimes E_{uv})

\displaystyle\qquad\times([C-I_{p}\otimes B]\otimes I_{m}\otimes I_{p}))(I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes\sigma_{m,p}^{T})(I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes\sigma_{m,p})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr_{12}\big((I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes\sigma_{m,p}^{T})(E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{n}^{T}\otimes E_{uv}\otimes I_{m})

\displaystyle\qquad\times([C\otimes I_{p}-I_{p}\otimes B\otimes I_{p}]\otimes I_{m}))(I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes\sigma_{m,p})\big),

since
\sigma_{m,p}(A\otimes B)\sigma_{m,p}^{T}=B\otimes A
, so that

\displaystyle\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes C\otimes I_{mp}-I_{mp}\otimes B\otimes I_{mp}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr_{12}\big((I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes\sigma_{m,p}^{T})

\displaystyle\qquad\times(([E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{n}^{T}\otimes E_{uv}][C\otimes I_{p}-I_{p}\otimes B\otimes I_{p}])\otimes I_{m}))(I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes\sigma_{m,p})\big)

Hence,

\displaystyle\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes C\otimes I_{mp}-I_{mp}\otimes B\otimes I_{mp}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\sigma_{m,p}^{T}(\tr_{12}([E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{n}^{T}\otimes E_{uv}][C\otimes I_{p}-I_{p}\otimes B\otimes I_{p}])\otimes I_{m}))\sigma_{m,p}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{m,p}^{T}((C\ominus B)\otimes I_{m})\sigma_{m,p}.

Using this information, it then follows that

\displaystyle(A\oplus C)\ominus B

\displaystyle=\delta(A\oplus C,B)

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(A\otimes I_{p}\otimes I_{n}\otimes I_{mp}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes C\otimes I_{mp}-I_{mp}\otimes B\otimes I_{mp}))

\displaystyle=A\otimes I_{p}+\sigma_{m,p}^{T}((C\ominus B)\otimes I_{m})\sigma_{m,p}

\displaystyle=A\otimes I_{p}+I_{m}\otimes(C\ominus B)

\displaystyle=A\oplus(C\ominus B).\qed

Theorem 7
.

Let
\ominus
be a uniform Kronecker difference. Then there exists a sequence of matrices

\upsilon_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,

for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
m,n\in\mathbb{N}

Proof.

By Remark

A\ominus B=\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,n}^{T}(A\otimes I_{m}-I_{m}\otimes B\otimes I_{m})),\qquad\alpha_{m,n}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}E_{ij}\otimes\upsilon_{n}\otimes E_{ji}+\gamma_{m,n}

It remains to

First let us consider

Since

is uniform, and following the proof of Lemma

yields that (recalling that

\displaystyle\tr_{12}(\gamma_{m,n}^{T}(A\otimes I_{n}\otimes I_{m}+I_{m}\otimes C\otimes I_{m}-I_{m}\otimes B\otimes I_{m}))

\displaystyle=\Btr(\tr_{2}(\gamma_{m,n})^{T}(A\otimes I_{m}))+\Btr(\tr_{1}(\gamma_{m,n})^{T}((C-B)\otimes I_{m}))

\displaystyle=\Btr(\tr_{1}(\gamma_{m,n})^{T}((C-B)\otimes I_{m}))

\displaystyle=0,

for all

,

Since

and

for all

∎

Theorem 8
.

of matrices,

If the following condition holds for all

,

Y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

and

p,q\in\mathbb{N}

2
(c), it suffices to show that

then:

\Btr(\upsilon_{pq})=\upsilon_{q}
, it suffices to consider

We have

\displaystyle X\ominus 0_{pq}

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,pq}^{T}(X\otimes I_{m}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\tr((E_{ji}\otimes\upsilon_{pq}^{T})(I_{m}\otimes I_{p}\otimes Y))E_{ij}+\tr_{12}(\gamma_{m,pq}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes I_{p}\otimes Y\otimes I_{m}))

using Lemma

and the fact that

where we used that
\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{mp}\tr(E_{ji})=1,
so that

\displaystyle(X\ominus 0_{q})\ominus 0_{p}

\displaystyle=\tr(\upsilon_{q}^{T}Y)\tr(\upsilon_{p}^{T})I_{m}\phantom{\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{mnp}\right]}

\displaystyle=\tr((\upsilon_{p}\otimes\upsilon_{q})^{T}(I_{p}\otimes Y))I_{m},\phantom{\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{mnp}\right]}

where we again used Lemma

\tr_{1}(\gamma_{mp,q}^{T})=0

.

To obtain the result

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,pq}^{T}(X\otimes I_{m}))\phantom{\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\tr_{12}(\gamma_{m,pq}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes Z\otimes I_{q}\otimes I_{m}))

using Lemma

\displaystyle(X\ominus 0_{q})\ominus 0_{p}

\displaystyle=\left[\tr_{12}(\alpha_{mp,q}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes Z\otimes I_{q}\otimes I_{mp}))\right]\ominus 0_{p}\phantom{\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{mp}\tr((E_{ji}\otimes\upsilon_{q}^{T})(I_{m}\otimes Z\otimes I_{q}))E_{ij}\right]\ominus 0_{p}

\displaystyle=\tr(\upsilon_{q}^{T})(I_{m}\otimes Z)\ominus 0_{p}\phantom{\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}}

\displaystyle=\tr(\upsilon_{q}^{T})\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,p}^{T}(I_{m}\otimes Z\otimes I_{m}))\phantom{\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}}

\displaystyle=\tr(\upsilon_{q}^{T})\left[\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\tr(E_{ji})\tr(\upsilon_{p}^{T}Z)E_{ij}\right]

\displaystyle=\tr(\upsilon_{q}^{T})\tr(\upsilon_{p}^{T}Z)I_{m}\phantom{\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}}

\displaystyle=\tr((\upsilon_{p}\otimes\upsilon_{q})^{T}(Z\otimes I_{q}))I_{m}.\phantom{\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}}

Hence, by Lemma

,
\Ptr(\upsilon_{pq})=\Ptr(\upsilon_{p}\otimes\upsilon_{q})=\tr(\upsilon_{q})\upsilon_{p}=\upsilon_{p}.

∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
\ominus
be a uniform Kronecker difference given by the pairs
(\upsilon_{n},0)
of matrices,

m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, as given in Corollary
1
.
If the following condition holds for all
X\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

Y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

and

then for all

:

\upsilon_{pq}=\upsilon_{p}\otimes\upsilon_{q}=\upsilon_{q}\otimes\upsilon_{p}.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Lemma
2
(c), it suffices to show that
the following condition holds: if for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
X\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}

(X\ominus 0_{q})\ominus 0_{p}=X\ominus(0_{p}\oplus 0_{q})=X\ominus 0_{pq},

\upsilon_{pq}=\upsilon_{p}\otimes\upsilon_{q}.

\displaystyle X\ominus 0_{pq}

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}(\alpha_{m,pq}^{T}(X\otimes I_{m}))\phantom{\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\tr_{12}((E_{ji}\otimes\upsilon_{pq}^{T}\otimes E_{ij})(X\otimes I_{m}))

E_{ij}
will denote an
m\times m
matrix and
E_{kl}
will

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{s,t=1}^{m}\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr((E_{ts}\otimes E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{q}^{T})X)\tr((E_{ji}\otimes\upsilon_{p}^{T})(E_{st}\otimes E_{uv}))E_{ij},

and

,

\displaystyle(X\ominus 0_{q})\ominus 0_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr((E_{ji}\otimes E_{vu}\otimes\upsilon_{q}^{T})X)\tr(\upsilon_{p}^{T}E_{uv}))E_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\tr\left(\left(E_{ji}\otimes\left(\sum_{u,v=1}^{p}\tr(\upsilon_{p}^{T}E_{uv})E_{vu}\right)\otimes\upsilon_{q}^{T}\right)X\right)E_{ij}

It follows that for all

We conclude that

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}^{2}
and
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{F}^{q}
, where
q
is a prime number.
Then
\mathbf{a}\otimes\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}\otimes\mathbf{a}
if and only if

(a)

q=2
and
\mathbf{a}=\beta\mathbf{b}
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{F}
,

(b)

q\neq 2
and
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
have one of the forms (for some

(i)

\mathbf{a}=(1,0)^{T}

(ii)

\mathbf{a}=(0,1)^{T}

\mathbf{b}=\beta(0,\ldots,0,1)^{T}

\mathbf{a}=(1,1)^{T}
,
\mathbf{b}=\beta(1,1,\ldots,1)^{T}

Proof.

(a)

If
q=2

\mathbf{a}\otimes\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}\otimes\mathbf{a}
if and only if

\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\mathbf{b}\\
\vdots\\
a_{p}\mathbf{b}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\mathbf{a}\\
\vdots\\
b_{p}\mathbf{a}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\mathbf{b}
has non-zero entries, let
b_{j}\neq 0
.
Then
\mathbf{a}=\beta\mathbf{b}
where
\beta=a_{j}/b_{j}
.

(b)

Let
k\in{1,\ldots,2q}
. Then there exist unique
Q_{q,k},R_{2,k}\in\{1,2\}

and
Q_{2,k},R_{q,k}\in\{1,\ldots,q\}

The entries of
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}

(\mathbf{a}\otimes\mathbf{b})_{k}=a_{Q_{q,k}}b_{R_{q,k}}=a_{R_{2,k}}b_{Q_{2,k}}=(\mathbf{b}\otimes\mathbf{a})_{k}.

Given
k\in\{1,\ldots,2q\}
, let
k^{\prime}
be defined by

k^{\prime}-1=(R_{q,k}-1)2+(Q_{q,k}-1).

Then

Q_{q,k}=R_{2,k^{\prime}},\quad R_{q,k}=Q_{2,k^{\prime}}.

It follows that

(3)

a_{Q_{q,k^{\prime}}}b_{R_{q,k^{\prime}}}=a_{R_{2,k^{\prime}}}b_{Q_{2,k^{\prime}}}=a_{Q_{q,k}}b_{R_{q,k}}=a_{R_{2,k}}b_{Q_{2,k}}.

Define an equivalence relation

Conclusion

We have defined Kronecker differences in the obvious way and established fundamental relations between Kronecker quotients and Kronecker differences. Similar to the theory of Kronecker quotients, uniformity of Kronecker differences provides a rich mathematical structure. Studying Kronecker differences via their matrix representations shows strong connections to Kronecker products and sums which reflect the properties of Kronecker sums.

Many open questions remain, in particular: a more concrete connection between classes of Kronecker differences and classes of Kronecker quotients is desirable. Furthermore, Kronecker quotients induced by exponentiating Kronecker differences (over the real numbers)

\exp(A\ominus B)=\exp(A)\oslash\exp(B)

remains unexplored. In this article we have considered only the linear Kronecker quotients and differences, while non-linear Kronecker differences provide a substantially different view which appears to be necessary to study Kronecker quotients induced by exponentiating Kronecker differences.

\PT(B\otimes C)=B\otimes(C^{T}),\quad\text{for all $B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}$, $C\in\mathbb{F}_{m}$}

and linear extension.

We will also make use of the transpose in different modes of
the tensor product, similar to the partial transpose, as defined
below.

Definition 9
.

Denote by

T_{3}:\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}

the linear map defined by

T_{3}(A\otimes B\otimes C)=A\otimes B\otimes(C^{T}),\quad\text{for all $A,C\in\mathbb{F}_{m}$, $B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}$}

and linear extension. Similarly, denote by

T_{12}:\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}

the map defined by

T_{12}(A\otimes B\otimes C)=(A^{T})\otimes(B^{T})\otimes C,\quad\text{for all $A,C\in\mathbb{F}_{m}$, $B\in\mathbb{F}_{n}$}

and linear extension.

Remark 8
.

Let
\text{Id}_{m}:\mathbb{F}_{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}
denote the identity on
\mathbb{F}_{m}

and
T:\mathbb{F}_{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{m}
the matrix transpose.
Then we may write
\tr_{1}=\tr\otimes\text{Id}_{n}\otimes\text{Id}_{m}
,

\tr_{12}=\tr\otimes\tr\otimes\text{Id}_{m}
,
T_{3}=\text{Id}_{m}\otimes\text{Id}_{n}\otimes T
,
etc. We also have that
\Btr\circ\tr_{1}=\tr_{12}=\Btr\circ\tr_{2}
.

Remark 9
.

The tensor product
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
is a left
\mathbb{F}_{m}
-module
when endowed with the product
\cdot:\mathbb{F}_{m}\times(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n})\to\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}

given by
A\cdot(B\otimes C):=(AB)\otimes C
and its linear extension. Similarly,

\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
is a right
\mathbb{F}_{m}
-module where
(B\otimes C)\cdot A:=(BA)\otimes C
,
making
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
an
(\mathbb{F}_{m},\mathbb{F}_{m})
-bimodule. The bilinear form

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{F}_{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{F}
given by

\langle A,B\rangle=\tr(AB^{T})

induces the form

(\cdot,\cdot):(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n})\times(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n})\to\mathbb{F}_{m}
,

(X,Y)=\Ptr(XY^{T}).

Now,
\mathbb{F}_{m}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-algebra which is isomorphic to its opposite
\mathbb{F}_{m}^{op}
(with
multiplication
A\mathbin{\cdot_{op}}B=B\cdot A
) under the transpose, i.e.

(A\cdot B)^{T}=B^{T}\cdot A^{T}=A^{T}\mathbin{\cdot_{op}}B^{T}.

Hence,
\mathbb{F}_{m}
is a
(\mathbb{F}_{m},\mathbb{F}_{m}^{op})
-bimodule with
the left
\mathbb{F}_{m}
-module product
A\ast B:=A\cdot B
and the right
\mathbb{F}_{m}^{op}
-module
product
B\odot A:=B\cdot A^{T}
, in particular

(B\odot A)\odot C=(B\cdot A^{T})\cdot C^{T}=B\cdot(A^{T}\cdot C^{T})=B\cdot(C\cdot A)^{T}=B\odot(A\mathbin{\cdot_{op}}C).

Now,

(A\ast B\odot C)^{T}=(ABC^{T})^{T}=CB^{T}A^{T}=C\ast B^{T}\odot A.

This shows that transposition is an involution of the
(\mathbb{F}_{m}^{op},\mathbb{F}_{m})
-bimodule

\mathbb{F}_{m}
in the sense of
[
8
]
. Moreover, we can now show that
(\cdot,\cdot)

is an
\mathbb{F}_{m}
-valued sesquilinear form in the sense of
[
8
]
(here we are using left modules

\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
instead of right modules as defined by Loos; however, the concepts
are equivalent) using Lemma
7
, i.e.

(A\cdot X,B\cdot Y)=A\ast(X,Y)\odot B

which we present as our next lemma. This sesquilinear form induces an orthogonality relation
on
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
(Lemma
8
).

Lemma 7
.

Let the function
(\cdot,\cdot):(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n})\times(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n})\to\mathbb{F}_{m}

be given by
(X,Y)=\Ptr(XY^{T})
. Then for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
,

(a)

(X+\alpha Y,Z)=(X,Z)+\alpha(Y,Z)
,

(b)

(X,Y+\alpha Z)=(X,Y)+\alpha(X,Z)
,

(c)

(A\cdot X,Y)=A(X,Y)
,

(d)

(X,A\cdot Y)=(X,Y)A^{T}
.

Proof.

(a) and (b) follow by linearity of the transpose and partial trace and distributivity of
the matrix product.
Now
X
and
Y
may be written in the form

X=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}\otimes E_{ij},\qquad Y=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}Y_{kl}\otimes E_{kl}

for some matrixes
X_{ij},Y_{kl}\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
i,j,k,l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Direct calculation yields

\displaystyle(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\Ptr\left(\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}\right)\left(\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}Y_{kl}\otimes E_{kl}\right)^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\Ptr\left(\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}X_{ij}Y_{kl}^{T}\otimes(E_{ij}E_{lk})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}\tr(E_{ij}E_{lk})X_{ij}Y_{kl}^{T}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}Y_{ij}^{T},\vphantom{\left(\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}\right)}.

and since
A\cdot X=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(AX_{ij})\otimes E_{ij}
,

(A\cdot X,Y)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(AX_{ij})Y_{ij}^{T}=A\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}Y_{ij}^{T}\right)=A(X,Y).

Similarly,

(X,A\cdot Y)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}(AY_{ij})^{T}=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}Y_{ij}^{T}\right)A^{T}=(X,Y)A^{T}.

Thus, (c) and (d) hold true.
∎

Example 1
.

The well known Möbius matrix representation of complex numbers, given by

a+ib\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
-b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)

and the complex conjugate

a-ib\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
-b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)^{T}
,
yields a function
\phi:\mathbb{C}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{R}_{n}
, namely

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(a_{ij}+b_{ij})E_{ij}\mapsto\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\begin{pmatrix}a_{ij}&b_{ij}\\
-b_{ij}&a_{ij}\end{pmatrix}\otimes E_{ij}.

Then the sesquilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{R}_{n}

is equivalent to the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product (also called the
Frobenius inner product) on
\mathbb{C}_{n}
, where the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product is given by

\langle A,B\rangle=\tr(AB^{*})
and
∗
denotes the entry-wise complex conjugate of
the transposed matrix. One easily shows that,

\phi(\langle A,B\rangle)=(\phi(A),\phi(B)).

The representation in Example
1
also expresses orthogonality in
\mathbb{C}_{n}
,
while in general the sesquilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
induces a more general orthostructure.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\perp
be the relation on the left
\mathbb{F}_{m}
-module
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}

given by
X\perp Y
if and only if
(X,Y)=(Y,X)=0
. Then

(a)

X\perp Y

\implies

Y\perp X
,

(b)

X\perp Y
and
X\perp Z

\implies

X\perp(Y+Z)
,

(c)

X\perp Y

\implies

X\perp(A\cdot Y)
for all
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,

(d)

X\perp Y
for all
Y\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}

\iff

X=0
.

Proof.

(a) follows by the definition of
\perp
,
(b) and (c) follow by Lemma
7
.
For (d), suppose that
X\perp Y
for all
Y\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
. In particular,
Let
Y=I_{m}\otimes E_{ij}
. Since
X
may be written in the form

X=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}X_{kl}\otimes E_{kl}

for some matrixes
X_{kl}\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
k,l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and since
X\perp I_{m}\otimes E_{ij}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(X,I_{m}\otimes E_{ij})=\Ptr\left(\left(\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}X_{kl}\otimes E_{kl}\right)(I_{m}\otimes E_{ij})^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\Ptr\left(\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}X_{kl}\otimes(E_{kl}E_{ji})\right)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\tr(E_{kl}E_{ji})X_{kl}

\displaystyle=X_{ij}.\vphantom{\left(\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\right)}

It follows that
X=0
.
∎

Remark 10
.

Lemma
8
show that the pair
(\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n},\perp)
is an
ortho- left
\mathbb{F}_{m}
-module
[
10
]
.
Moreover,
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
has an orthogonal basis

since

(I_{m}\otimes E_{ij},I_{m}\otimes E_{kl})=\begin{cases}I_{m}&\text{if~}i=k\text{~and~}j=l,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Similarly, we have that
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
is a left
\mathbb{F}_{n}

an orthogonal basis in the obvious way,
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
is
a left
\mathbb{F}_{n}
-module with an orthogonal basis and so on.

Lemma 9
.

Let
X,Y\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}
, then
X=Y
if and only if

(X,I_{m}\otimes E_{ij})=(Y,I_{m}\otimes E_{ij})
for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

Let

X=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}\otimes E_{ij}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}\cdot(I_{m}\otimes E_{ij}),\qquad Y=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}Y_{kl}\cdot(I_{m}\otimes E_{kl}).

By Lemma
7
and Remark
10
,

(X,I_{m}\otimes E_{ij})=X_{ij}

and the lemma follows immediately.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
C\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}
such that
\tr_{2}(C)=0
.
Then
\tr_{2}(C^{T})=0
,
\tr_{2}(C(A\otimes I_{n}\otimes B))=0
and
\tr_{12}(C(A\otimes I_{n}\otimes B))=0

for all
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{m}
.

Proof.

We write
C
in the form

C=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}E_{ij}\otimes C_{ijkl}\otimes E_{kl}.

Then
\tr_{2}(C)=0
yields

0=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}\tr(C_{ijkl})E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl},

and since
E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}
,
i,j,k,l\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, is a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}
,
we have

\tr(C_{ijkl})=0,\qquad i,j,k,l\in\{1,\ldots,m\}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\tr_{2}(C^{T})

\displaystyle=\tr_{2}\left(\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}E_{ij}^{T}\otimes C_{ijkl}^{T}\otimes E_{kl}^{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}\tr(C_{ijkl}^{T})E_{ji}\otimes E_{lk}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}\tr(C_{ijkl})E_{ji}\otimes E_{lk}=0.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\tr_{2}(C(A\otimes I_{n}\otimes B))

\displaystyle=\tr_{2}\left(\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}E_{ij}A\otimes C_{ijkl}\otimes E_{kl}B\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}\tr(C_{ijkl})E_{ij}A\otimes E_{kl}B=0,

\displaystyle\tr_{12}(C(A\otimes I_{n}\otimes B))

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}\left(\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}E_{ij}A\otimes C_{ijkl}\otimes E_{kl}B\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{m}\tr(E_{ij}A)\tr(C_{ijkl})E_{kl}B=0.\qed

Lemma 11
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}
. Then

\tr_{12}(A)^{T}=\tr_{12}(T_{3}(A))=\tr_{12}(A^{T}),\qquad\tr_{3}(A)^{T}=\tr_{3}(T_{12}(A)).

Proof.

We write
A
in the form

A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes A_{ijkl},

so that

\displaystyle\tr_{12}(A)^{T}

\displaystyle=\left(\tr_{12}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes A_{ijkl}\right)\right)^{T}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\tr(E_{ij})\tr(E_{kl})A_{ijkl}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\tr(E_{ij})\tr(E_{kl})A_{ijkl}^{T}

\displaystyle=\tr_{12}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}E_{ij}\otimes E_{kl}\otimes A_{ijkl}^{T}\right)=\tr_{12}(T_{3}(A)).

The proof that
\tr_{3}(A)^{T}=\tr_{3}(T_{12}(A))
follows similarly. The equality
\tr_{12}(T_{3}(A))=\tr_{12}(A^{T})

can be seen by noting that
\tr(E_{ij})=\tr(E_{ji})
in the proof above.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}
and

. Then

A

Lemma 14
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{m}

A=B\quad\iff\quad\tr_{12}(A(X\otimes I_{m}))=\tr_{12}(B(X\otimes I_{m}))\text{~~for all $X\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}$.}

,

Lemma 15
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}

.

\tr_{1}(A)=0

\tr_{1}(A(I_{m}\otimes B))=0

A=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}A_{1j}\otimes A_{2j}\otimes A_{3j}

Lemma 16
.

\displaystyle=\Btr(\tr_{1}(A(B\otimes C\otimes I_{p}))D,

Lemma 18
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{n}

for all
X\in F_{n}

\Btr(A)=\Btr(B)

\tr((Y\otimes I_{n})A)=\tr((Y\otimes I_{n})B)

\Ptr(A)=\Ptr(B)

We will prove the first statement, the second follows similarly.
Write
A=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}E_{ij}\otimes A_{ij}
,
where
A_{ij}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Similarly, write

\displaystyle\tr((I_{m}\otimes X)A)

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}E_{ij}\otimes(XA_{ij})\right)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\tr(E_{ij})\tr(XA_{ij})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\tr(XA_{ii})=\tr\left(X\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{ii}\right)\right)=\tr(X\Btr(A)).

Secure Elliptic Galois Cryptography Framework for robust real-time vehicle image classification using convolutional sparse autoencoder in intelligent transportation systems
Authors: Mohammed Aljebreen, Sara Abdelwahab Ghorashi, Mohammed Burhanur Rehman, Mohammed Alahmadi, Adel Albshri, Alanoud Subahi, Randa Allafi, Khalid Nazim Abdul Sattar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Intelligent transportation systems (ITS) have experienced an important development in the past decade because of developments in communication, control, and information technology deployed to roads, vehicles, and traffic controller systems. Vehicle form classification plays an essential role in applying ITS owing to its capability for collecting valuable traffic information, providing further development of transport structures, and improving human convenience. Nevertheless, the present service structure implements artificial intelligence (AI) methods with universal patterns for every vehicle. Still, the computational efficiency and needs of deep learning (DL) methods pose difficulties for real-time applications. DL is a useful device for classifying vehicle categories because it can take composite traffic data features and learns from larger data amounts. This manuscript develops a Secure Elliptic Galois Cryptography Framework for Vehicle Image Classification in Intelligent Transportation Systems (SEGCF-VICITS) method. ​The primary aim of the SEGCF-VICITS method is to ensure secure data transmission and intelligent decision-making in ITS environments. Initially, the SEGCF-VICITS model employs the elliptic galois cryptography (EGC) model to provide strong encryption for sensitive vehicular data, utilizing a key that is subsequently used for data decryption. Besides, the SE-DenseNet model is utilized for feature extraction. Additionally, the convolutional sparse autoencoder (CSAE) method is used for vehicle classification. The experimental validation of the SEGCF-VICITS method portrayed a superior accuracy value of 95.48% over existing models under the vehicle image classification dataset.

Related works on ITS

Review of deep learning based classification models

Li et al.11 proposed an innovative model that depends on 3D skeletal data, merging Graph Spatio-Temporal Feature Representation (GSFR) using BLS to overwhelm those difficulties. The GSFR model automatically chooses the aptest key points with 3D skeletal data, enhancing strength and decreasing computational efficiency. Zheng et al.12 introduced the Edge-Adaptive Multi-Task Enhancement Model (EAMEM) to satisfy the need for high reliability and low latency in practical video applications like marine observation and ITS networks. EAMEM utilizes trivial methods for improving video quality in difficult circumstances, namely underwater, fog, and low-light environments, although decreasing the delay and load on centralized cloud servers through managing processing tasks in the network edge. Shahzad et al.13 presented a hierarchical technique to detect number plates. Initially, a DL approach, Mobile Net-SSD, was used for detecting number plates. Then, the alphanumeric in the identified number plates were removed through the Optical Character Recognition (OCR) method. This method is made on a convolutional neural network (CNN) and effectively employs pointwise and depthwise convolution layers, which makes it appropriate for embedded and mobile systems. Zhuang et al.14 suggested an in-vehicle IR object detection model. Enhance the YOLOv4 object detection structure by substituting its novel backbone using MobileNet-V3. Additionally, swap the conventional pre-processing models through the Image Enhancement Conditional GAN inversion framework for enhancing input IR image pre-processing. IR images may rectify those restrictions, giving dependable information irrespective of external lighting.

Pan et al.15 projected an LVD-YOLO, a vehicle detection system that relies on YOLO. This system integrates the EfficientNet v2 network structure for the backbone, and decreases parameters. The dual attention mechanism usage and a bidirectional feature pyramid approach enable effective information conversation within feature layers, thus enriching multiscale feature fusion. Moreover, upgrading the loss function of the model by the SIoU loss for boosting prediction and regression performance. In16, the pioneers of simultaneous vehicle detection employed the YOLOv8 procedure. A complete study on YOLOv8n, a small-scale method in the YOLOv8 sequences, is carried out to assess the appropriateness for practical situations, specifically in ITS. A training dataset was formed from flight assessments deploying a custom autonomous drone, incorporating several vehicle differences for optimizing the method’s performance. Shan et al.17 recommended a new low-resource SC (LR-SC) methodology, mainly concentrated on method pruning. These strategies aim to diminish storage needs and computational power by elevating resource employment in edge smart applications. This architecture includes the ℓ2 regularization application and a threshold-enabled pruning technique that exclusively removes unneeded networks. Alladi et al.18 presented 3 DL-assisted misbehavior classification systems for intrusion detection (ID) on the IoV network utilizing CNN and LSTM. The projected DL Classification Engines (DCLE) include multi or single-phase classification made by DL paradigms, which is employed in vehicular edge servers.

Chauhan and Tiwari28 utilized a hybrid DL model, Time Residual Long Short-Term Memory (TimeResLSTM), integrated with Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory (ConvLSTM) and Bi-Directional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU) modules with attention.

The limitations include vehicle detection, tracking, traffic flow prediction, and security in IoV and VANETs. Furthermore, generalization is also improved by hybrid architecture as they still encounter domain adaptation threats and high computational overhead. There is also a research gap in addressing scalable, low-latency, and secure vehicle detection and traffic monitoring systems that can operate reliably across diverse and dynamic environments.

Encryption and decryption using EGC model

At first, the SEGCF-VICITS approach employs the EGC model to deliver strong encryption for sensitive vehicular data, utilizing a key that is subsequently used for data decryption29. In the SEGCF-VICITS approach, confidential data like authentication credentials or application-level messages undergo encryption before transmitting them via a communication network. Every communicating entity creates a private key and an equivalent public key depending upon elliptic structures defined over a Galois field. Public keys are communicated via a trusted medium whereas private keys remain secret. The encryption and decryption processes are based on the operations in the elliptic Galois domain, where security is derived from the computation hardness of the elliptic discrete logarithm problem.

This model is highly effective in providing security with comparatively smaller key sizes than conventional public-key techniques. It is also considered effective for real-time and resource-constrained environments. Furthermore, the data integrity and confidentiality are enhanced by the resistance of the model to common cryptographic attacks. Also, the encryption reduces data intervention, unauthorized access, and spoofing attacks in ITS. Thus, the model is considered conventional for secure vehicular data handling. ECC is a public key (PK) cryptography, which uses the elliptic curve property formulations for major generation, as mentioned earlier. This model differentiates it from other cryptography methods. Feature extraction using SE-DenseNet model

Moreover, feature extraction is achieved by utilizing the SE-DenseNet to capture rich spatial and contextual patterns from vehicular image data30. This model is chosen for its ability in detecting and extracting the most distinguishing characteristics, while also eliminating less relevant features. Efficient feature reuse and modelling of internal data is also enhanced across layers. This also achieves robust scaling compared to conventional feature extractors. Thus, the model is considered crucial for extracting features in intrinsic visual environments. DenseNet enhances feature reuse and propagation by linking each layer to all other layers in a feed-forward way that helps in the improved flow of gradient and decreases the problem of vanishing gradient. The SE component enhances the ability of the model to selectively highlight important characteristics by adaptively transformative channel-wise feature reactions. This incorporation permits this model to reach better performance in tasks containing higher-level extraction of the features. In comparison with separate DenseNet or traditional CNNs, this model’s capability to adaptively concentrate on significant features reinforces high precision and strength, mainly in composite databases.

Conclusion

This manuscript has developed the SEGCF-VICITS approach to ensure secure data transmission and intelligent decision-making in ITS environments. Initially, the SEGCF-VICITS approach employs the EGC model to provide strong encryption for sensitive vehicular data, utilizing a key that is subsequently used for data decryption. Next, the QGOA model is utilized for decryption and image optimization. Besides, feature extractor is applied using the SE-DenseNet model to capture rich spatial and contextual patterns from vehicular image data. Moreover, the CSAE model is employed for vehicle classification. The experimental validation of the SEGCF-VICITS method portrayed a superior accuracy value of 95.48% over existing models under the vehicle image classification dataset. The limitations of the method include intrinsic and cluttered scenes, varying illumination, and adverse weather. Additionally, several classification models mostly face difficulty in maintaining robust real-time performance. Furthermore, it becomes difficult in addressing dynamic viewpoint alterations, resulting in frequent misclassification in urban surveillance settings. Safety threats are not properly addressed, thus affecting the consistency of the model across various road scenarios. Future work may concentrate on enhancing the resilience, adaptability, and latency and may also focus on generalizing and integrating context-aware decision support for ITS.

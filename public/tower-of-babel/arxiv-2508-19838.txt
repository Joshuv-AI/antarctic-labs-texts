Fully heavy tetraquark states with diquark-antidiquark configuration
Xi Xia, Tao Guo

Abstract
The study of the fully heavy tetraquark state helps to better understand the characteristics of compact exotic hadrons formed by quarks and gluons. In this work, we systematically investigate the mass spectra and decays of fully heavy tetraquark states using a diquark-antidiquark model based on the one gluon exchange process. The tetraquark states are treated as compact diquark–antidiquark configurations, denoted as [ Q ​ Q ] ​ [ Q ¯ ​ Q ¯ ] [QQ][\bar{Q}\bar{Q}] , where Q = c Q=c or b b . By properly accounting for the Pauli principle, basis wave functions incorporating flavor, color, and spin are constructed. The required model parameters are derived from fitting conventional hadron experimental values and lattice QCD calculations. We calculate the mass spectra and decays of the fully heavy tetraquarks in the lower S S - and P P -waves. Our results do not support interpreting X ⁡ ( 6600 ) X(6600) , X ⁡ ( 6900 ) X(6900) , and X ⁡ ( 7200 ) X(7200) as S S -wave fully charmed tetraquark states. ‌Nevertheless‌, our analysis suggests that X ⁡ ( 6600 ) X(6600) can be a good candidate for the P P -wave fully charmed tetraquark state. Furthermore, we also identifies several possible fully heavy tetraquark states that may be observable in future experiments.

II
Theoretical model

II.1
Effective Hamiltonian

The bound state system formed by four fully heavy quarks can be seen as a unique example of direct interaction between quarks and gluons.
Consequently‌, the interaction in this system is mainly manifested as the one gluon exchange process
De Rujula et al. 1975
;
Guo et al. 2022
.
For the fully heavy tetraquark systems, the improved nonrelativistic effective Hamiltonian reads
Maiani et al. 2005
;
Drenska et al. 2008

H=\sum_{i=1}^{4}m_{i}+H_{SS}+H_{SL}+H_{L},

(1)

where

\displaystyle H_{SS}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}2\kappa_{ij}(\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}),

(2)

\displaystyle H_{SL}={}

\displaystyle 2A\left(\bm{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}\cdot\bm{L}+\bm{S}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{L}\right),

\displaystyle H_{L}={}

\displaystyle B\frac{L(L+1)}{2}.

Here,
m_{i}
denotes the mass of the
i
-th constituent quark mass.
H_{SS}
represents the interaction of each quark pair in the tetraquark,
H_{SL}
and
H_{L}
describe spin-orbit coupling and pure orbital contribution, respectively.
\bm{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}
in
H_{SL}
represents the spin operator of the diquark configuration
\mathcal{Q}=[Q_{1}Q_{2}]
. The orbital angular momentum
\bm{L}
describes the relative motion between the diquark
\mathcal{Q}
and the anti-diquark
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
. The coefficients
A
and
B
are phenomenological parameters to be determined from experimental data.

|{(Q_{1}Q_{2})_{s}^{c}(\bar{Q}_{3}\bar{Q}_{4})_{s}^{c}}\rangle

1\quad\text{if}\ i\neq j.\end{matrix}\right.

II.3
Decay width

By inserting the basis vectors of the wave function into the Hamiltonian, one can obtain the mass spectra of the tetraquark states.
We can then calculate their decays into different meson final states.

For a particle of mass
M
and four-momentum
P
decaying into two particles with momenta
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, the partial decay rate is written as

d\Gamma=\frac{(2\pi)^{4}}{2M}|\mathcal{A}|^{2}d\Phi(P;p_{1},p_{2}),

(7)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the amplitude connecting the initial state to the final state and
d\Phi
is an element of 2-body phase space given by

\displaystyle d\Phi(P;p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta^{4}(P-p_{1}-p_{2})

(8)

\displaystyle\times\frac{d^{3}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{1}}\frac{d^{3}p_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{2}}.

In the Center of Momentum Frame, considering four-momentum conservation in the decay process, we have

|\bm{p}_{1}|=|\bm{p}_{2}|=\frac{1}{2M}\sqrt{\lambda(M^{2},m_{1}^{2},m_{2}^{2})}

(9)

where
\lambda(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}-2\alpha\beta-2\alpha\gamma-2\beta\gamma
is the Källén function. Thus, the partial decay rate can be written as

d\Gamma=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{|\bm{p}_{1}|}{M^{2}}d\Omega.

(10)

Then, by integrating the differentiation above, we obtain

\Gamma=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|^{2}|\bm{p}_{1}|}{8\pi M^{2}}.

(11)

III.2
[cc][\bar{c}\bar{c}]
,
[bb][\bar{b}\bar{b}]
and
[cb][\bar{c}\bar{b}]
systems

From the wave functions of
[cc][\bar{c}\bar{c}]
,
[bb][\bar{b}\bar{b}]
and
[cb][\bar{c}\bar{b}]
configurations, it can be seen that these states possesses a well-defined charge conjugation parity (
C
-parity). For these states, conservation of
C
-parity must be taken into account during the decay process. Their spectra and decay channels were shown in Fig.
1
and Tables
2
-
3
. The final-state mesons we choose for these decays are in the
1S
and
1P
states. To date, only the spin-0 states of the
B_{c}(1S)
and
B_{c}(2S)
mesons have been observed experimentally. Therefore, for the remaining
c\bar{b}
mesons in the
1S
and
1P
states, we use the masses predicted by lattice QCD calculations
Mathur et al. 2018
.
For simplicity, we denote the
c\bar{b}
mesons with quantum numbers
J^{PC}=0^{+}
and
1^{+}
as
B_{c0}
and
B_{c1}
, respectively.

III.2.1
[cc][\bar{c}\bar{c}]
system

For the tetraquark state
[cc][\bar{c}\bar{c}]
with different quantum numbers, the mass spectra and corresponding meson-meson thresholds are exhibited in Fig.
1
(a) and the decay channels are shown in Table
2
.

The
S
-wave fully charmed tetraquark states possess quantum numbers
J^{PC}=0^{++}
,
1^{+-}
, and
2^{++}
. There are two states with
0^{++}
, with masses of 6098.45 MeV and 6314.21 MeV, respectively. The lower-mass state, at 6098.45 MeV, lies above both the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
\eta_{c}J/\psi
thresholds. However, due to conservation laws, it can only decay naturally into the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
channel, while decay into
\eta_{c}J/\psi
is forbidden. The higher-mass
0^{++}
state, at 6314.21 MeV, lies above both
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
and
J/\psi J/\psi
thresholds and is allowed to decay into both of these two final states.
However, the width of the
J/\psi J/\psi
final state is smaller, indicating that this state is more likely to decay into the
\eta_{c}\eta_{c}
channel.

For the
1^{+-}
and
2^{++}
states, the masses are 6204.49 MeV and 6260.89 MeV, respectively. Both lie above the
\eta_{c}J/\psi
and
J/\psi J/\psi
thresholds. Nonetheless, due to quantum number constraints, the
1^{+-}
state can only decay into the
\eta_{c}J/\psi
channel, whereas the
2^{++}
state is allowed to decay solely into the
J/\psi J/\psi
channel.

The
P
-wave fully charmed tetraquark states have quantum numbers
J^{PC}=0^{-+}
,
1^{--}
, and
1^{-+}
. There is one
0^{-+}
state, with a mass of 6543.23 MeV, which can decay only into the
\eta_{c}\chi_{c0}
channel. Three states are identified with
J^{PC}=1^{--}
, having masses of 6524.51 MeV, 6555.97 MeV, and 6740.27 MeV, respectively. The two lower-mass states, at 6524.51 MeV and 6555.97 MeV, lie above the
J/\psi\chi_{c0}
threshold but below the
J/\psi\chi_{c1}
threshold. Consequently, due to conservation laws, they are only allowed to decay into the
\eta_{c}h_{c}
and
J/\psi\chi_{c0}
channels. The masses of both two states are in good agreement with the mass of the
X(6600)
observed by the CMS Collaboration, reported as
M=6638^{+43}_{-38}(\text{stat})^{+16}_{-31}(\text{syst})
MeV
Hayrapetyan et al. 2024
. The highest
1^{--}
state, at 6740.27 MeV, lies above the
J/\psi\chi_{c2}
threshold and can decay into
\eta_{c}h_{c}
,
J/\psi\chi_{c0}
,
J/\psi\chi_{c1}
, and
J/\psi\chi_{c2}
final states. This state is more likely to decay into the
\eta_{c}h_{c}
and
J/\psi\chi_{c0}
channels due to their larger widths.
In addition, there is a single
1^{-+}
state with a mass of 6586.89 MeV, which can decay only into the
\eta_{c}\chi_{c1}
channel.

III.2.2
[bb][\bar{b}\bar{b}]
system

For the tetraquark state
[bb][\bar{b}\bar{b}]
with different quantum numbers, the mass spectra and corresponding meson-meson thresholds are exhibited in Fig.
1
(b) and the decay channels are shown in Table
2
.

The quantum numbers of the
S
-wave fully bottom tetraquark states are
J^{PC}=0^{++}
,
1^{+-}
, and
2^{++}
. There are two
0^{++}
states with masses of 18948.9 MeV and 19066.9 MeV. The
1^{+-}
and
2^{++}
states have masses of 19006.9 MeV and 19037.7 MeV, respectively. All these
S
-wave states lie above the
\Upsilon\Upsilon
threshold. Both
0^{++}
states are allowed to decay into the
\eta_{b}\eta_{b}
and
\Upsilon\Upsilon
channels.But this state is more likely to decay into the
\eta_{b}\eta_{b}
channel due to its larger width. The
1^{+-}
state can decay only into the
\eta_{b}\Upsilon
channel, while the
2^{++}
state is allowed to decay only into the
\Upsilon\Upsilon
channel.

The quantum numbers of the
P
-wave fully bottom tetraquark states are
J^{PC}=0^{-+}
,
1^{--}
, and
1^{-+}
. Their masses all lie above the
\Upsilon\chi_{b2}
threshold. Taking conservation laws into account, the
0^{-+}
state can decay into the
\eta_{b}\chi_{b0}
and
\Upsilon h_{b}
channels, while the
1^{-+}
state is allowed to decay into the
\eta_{b}\chi_{b1}
and
\Upsilon h_{b}
channels. The
1^{--}
state can decay into multiple channels, including
\eta_{b}h_{b}
,
\Upsilon\chi_{b0}
,
\Upsilon\chi_{b1}
, and
\Upsilon\chi_{b2}
.

III.2.3
[cb][\bar{c}\bar{b}]
system

For the tetraquark state
[cb][\bar{c}\bar{b}]
with different quantum numbers, the mass spectra and corresponding meson-meson thresholds are exhibited in Fig.
1
(c) and the decay channels are shown in Table
3
.

Since the
[cb][\bar{c}\bar{b}]
tetraquark system contains no identical quarks or antiquarks, it is not subject to the Pauli exclusion principle. As a result, there is no restriction on the construction of basis states, and all possible spin-parity and charge-conjugation quantum numbers can be realized. This leads to a rich spectra of possible states, including
0^{++}
,
1^{++}
,
1^{+-}
,
2^{++}
for
S
-wave and
0^{--}
,
0^{-+}
,
1^{--}
,
1^{-+}
for
P
-wave.

There are four states with quantum numbers
0^{++}
, with masses of 12477.8 MeV, 12548.6 MeV, 12633.4 MeV, and 12690.1 MeV, respectively. Among them, the two lighter states, 12477.8 MeV and 12548.6 MeV, lie above the
\eta_{c}\eta_{b}
threshold but below the
J/\psi\Upsilon
threshold. Therefore, considering the conservation of angular momentum and
C
-parity, they can only decay into the
\eta_{c}\eta_{b}
channel. In addition, the
[cb][\bar{c}\bar{b}]
tetraquark states can decay not only into
c\bar{c}
and
b\bar{b}
mesons, but also into
c\bar{b}
and
b\bar{c}
mesons. However, since the masses of the two lighter states are below the
B_{c}\bar{B}_{c}
threshold, decays into
c\bar{b}
and
b\bar{c}
mesons are kinematically forbidden. The two heavier states, with masses of 12633.4 MeV and 12690.1 MeV, lie above the
J/\psi\Upsilon
threshold and can thus decay into both
\eta_{c}\eta_{b}
and
J/\psi\Upsilon
channels. Moreover, since their masses are also above the
B_{c}\bar{B}_{c}
threshold, decays into this channel are allowed. In particular, the state with mass 12690.1 MeV lies above the
B_{c}^{*}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
threshold, and therefore can also decay into the
B_{c}^{*}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
channel. The two states, 12633.4 MeV and 12690.1 MeV, are more likely to decay into
\eta_{c}\eta_{b}
channel due to its larger width.

There are two states with quantum numbers
1^{++}
, with masses of 12582.8 MeV and 12665.6 MeV, respectively. Both states lie above the
J/\psi\Upsilon
threshold and are therefore allowed to decay into this channel. In addition, the heavier state, with a mass of 12665.6 MeV, lies above the thresholds for both
B_{c}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
and
B_{c}^{*}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
, making decays into these channels kinematically allowed as well. But the width of
J/\psi\Upsilon
channel is larger than
B_{c}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
and
B_{c}^{*}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
, thus the state of 12665.6 MeV is more likely to decay into
J/\psi\Upsilon
channel.

There are four states with quantum numbers
1^{+-}
, with masses of 12547.4 MeV, 12590.8 MeV, 12617.6 MeV, and 12655.4 MeV, respectively. All of these states lie above the
\eta_{c}\Upsilon
and
J/\psi\eta_{b}
thresholds, allowing them to decay into both channels. Additionally, the two heavier states, with masses of 12617.6 MeV and 12655.4 MeV, are above the
B_{c}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
threshold and can therefore also decay into this channel.
Both the four
1^{+-}
states are more likely to decay into
\eta_{c}\Upsilon
channel because the width of
\eta_{c}\Upsilon
is larger than that of other channels.

There are two states with quantum numbers
2^{++}
, whose masses are 12649.1 and 12666.1 MeV, respectively. Both them lie above the
J/\psi\Upsilon
threshold and are allowed to decay into this channel. Furthermore, the state with 12666.1 MeV lies above the threshold of
B_{c}^{*}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
, therefore can also decay into the
B_{c}^{*}\bar{B}_{c}^{*}
channel. The state of 12666.1 MeV is more likely to decay into
J/\psi\Upsilon
channel due to the larger width.

There are two
0^{--}

IV
Summary

In this paper, we systematically study the mass spectra of the lower
S
- and
P
-wave fully heavy tetraquark states, as well as analysis their possible strong decay channels.
Considering the basic features of the fully heavy tetraquark system, we adopted an improved Hamiltonian based on the one gluon exchange process.
The Hamiltonian include constituent quark masses, spin-spin interaction, spin-orbit coupling and pure orbital contribution.
The model parameters required for our calculations are derived from fitting conventional mesons and baryons.
The parameters corresponding to different color structures can be calculated through
SU(3)
group algebra.

For the fully charmed tetraquark system, our results do not support interpreting
X(6600)
,
X(6900)
, and
X(7200)
as
S
-wave fully charmed tetraquark states.
It is worth noting that we predict two fully charmed tetraquark states with quantum numbers
J^{PC}=1^{--}
, whose masses are 6524.51 MeV and 6555.97 MeV respectively.
At the same time, we analyzed the decay states of these two states and found that their characteristics are consistent with the observations of the CMS Collaboration on the
X(6600)
resonance.
For the fully bottom tetraquark system, our calculated masses are all above the
\Upsilon\Upsilon
threshold and exceed the
bb\bar{b}\bar{b}
mass reported by CMS Collaboration and A
N
DY Collaboration.
Nevertheless, LHCb Collaboration and other experiments have not observed any
bb\bar{b}\bar{b}
resonant states thus far.
Further experimental investigations are required to address this question.
In addition, we calculated masses of several other fully heavy tetraquark states and analysis their strong decay channels.
It is also anticipated that some of the states predicted in this work may be observed in future experiments.

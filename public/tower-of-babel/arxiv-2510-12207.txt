Perturbative χ \chi EFT calculations of the deuteron and triton up to N 2 LO
Oliver Thim, Andreas Ekström, Christian Forssén

Abstract
We extend previous studies of the deuteron and triton ground-state energies to next-to-next-to-leading order (N 2 LO) in chiral effective field theory, employing a power counting in which subleading interactions are treated perturbatively. Triton calculations are performed using the no-core shell model, and we demonstrate converged perturbative results for regulator cutoffs up to Λ ≈ 1200 \Lambda\approx 1200 MeV. We analyze exceptional cutoffs in the P 0 3 {}^{3}P_{0} and 3 S 1 − 3 D 1 {}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1} nucleon-nucleon channels and find a resulting cutoff dependence in the triton ground-state energy at N 2 LO. The effect associated with the exceptional cutoff in the P 0 3 {}^{3}P_{0} channel can be mitigated by redefining the leading-order wave function within the freedom allowed by the effective field theory. The same approach applied in the 3 S 1 − 3 D 1 {}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1} channel remedies the effect of this exceptional cutoff on the ground state energy of the deuteron, but not for the triton.



I
Introduction

Chiral effective field theory (
\chi
EFT) promises a systematically improvable framework for deriving nuclear interaction potentials consistent with quantum chromodynamics
Weinberg 1979
;
Weinberg 1990
;
Weinberg 1991
, and in particular the spontaneously broken chiral symmetry
Machleidt and Entem 2011
;
Epelbaum et al. 2009
;
Hammer et al. 2020
. A power counting (PC) scheme facilitates the organization of nucleon-interaction Feynman diagrams in order of decreasing importance. Each diagram carries a scaling
(Q/\Lambda_{b})^{\nu}
, where
\nu
is the chiral order,
Q
represents a typical low-energy scale, e.g., the external nucleon momenta, and
\Lambda_{b}
is the
\chi
EFT breakdown scale, typically estimated to be

\Lambda_{b}\approx 500
–
600
MeV
Melendez et al. 2019
;
Millican et al. 2024
or possibly even lower
Thim et al. 2024
.

In Weinberg PC (WPC)
Weinberg 1990
;
Weinberg 1991
, the potential is constructed from the sum of all relevant irreducible nucleon-contact and finite-range interactions up to a given chiral order. This potential is then iterated non-perturbatively using the Lippmann-Schwinger or Schrödinger equation, where a cutoff on the order of the breakdown scale is applied to regulate the appearing divergences. This is the predominant approach used in
ab inito

Ekström et al. 2023
;
Hergert 2020
computations of nuclear observables. Quantitative potentials have been developed up to the fifth chiral order within this scheme
Entem et al. 2015
;
Reinert et al. 2018
.

Amplitudes and observables computed in WPC are sensitive to the value of the employed cutoff
Nogga et al. 2005
, which indicates an unphysical regulator dependence of the results. Removing this, such that only a residual higher-order dependence remains, is referred to as attaining renormalization group (RG) invariance
van Kolck 2020
. In the nucleon-nucleon (NN) sector, this can be achieved by promoting nucleon-contact interactions to lower chiral orders together with treating subleading interactions perturbatively
Long and van
Kolck 2008
.

There are several ongoing efforts to construct PCs that render observables cutoff independent
Pavon Valderrama and Ruiz Arriola 2006
;
Pavon Valderrama and Ruiz Arriola 2005
;
Pavon Valderrama 2011
;
Long 2013
;
Pavón Valderrama et al. 2017
;
Valderrama 2011
;
Birse 2006
;
Long and Yang 2012a
;
Sánchez Sánchez et al. 2018
;
Long and Yang 2012b
;
Yang 2016
;
Mishra et al. 2022
;
Peng et al. 2022
. In particular, it has been shown that the PC proposed by Long and Yang
Long and Yang 2012a
;
Long and Yang 2012b
;
Long and Yang 2011
;
Long and van
Kolck 2008
performs well in the NN sector up to
\nu=3
(next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order N
3
LO)
Thim et al. 2023
;
Thim et al. 2024
;
Thim 2024
. Furthermore,
Song et al. 2017
studied the triton up to next-to-leading order (NLO) and
Yang et al. 2021
presented the first studies of
4
He,
6
Li, and
16
O, also up to NLO. In the latter study, issues of
\alpha
-cluster instability were encountered, possibly remedied by promoting few-nucleon forces to leading order (LO)
Yang et al. 2023
.

The cutoff dependence in the Long and Yang PC was examined in detail by
Gasparyan and Epelbaum 2023
who found that the low-energy constants (LECs) for the next-to-next-to-leading order (N
2
LO) contact interaction in the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel diverge at certain
exceptional
values of the cutoff. This effect is related to the perturbative calculation of amplitudes, and appears to break cutoff independence beyond next-to-leading order (NLO) since the divergences in the LECs propagate to predicted observables. Exceptional cutoffs were further studied in Refs.
Peng et al. 2024
;
Yang 2024
, where it was demonstrated that cutoff independence can be restored for NN scattering amplitudes by utilizing the EFT freedom to adjust the LO wave function from which the subleading amplitudes are perturbatively computed. However, the effect of exceptional cutoffs beyond the NN system is unknown, as they first appear at N
2
LO, for which no calculations have been performed.

In this work, we extend perturbative
\chi
EFT calculations for the triton, using the no-core shell model (NCSM)
Barrett et al. 2013
, up to N
2
LO in the Long and Yang PC. We investigate the approach of modifying the LO wave function to mitigate the impact of exceptional cutoffs in the triton ground-state energy. To this end, we also extend the analysis of exceptional cutoffs to the coupled
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel, see also
Peng et al. 2025
.

The article is organized as follows. In Sec.
II

2
LO as well as our calibration of the LECs. The theory of exceptional cutoffs, as presented in Refs.
Gasparyan and Epelbaum 2023
;
Peng et al. 2024
;
Yang 2024
, is summarized in Sec.
III
and extended to the coupled
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel, with application to the deuteron ground-state energy. In Sec.

2
LO. Finally, Sec.
V

III
Exceptional cutoffs in the two-nucleon system

In this section, we examine the emergence of exceptional cutoff values, denoted
\bar{\Lambda}
, at which subleading LECs diverge in a manner that also leads to divergences in predictions for NN observables. The appearance of exceptional cutoffs was first studied by
Gasparyan and Epelbaum 2023
who traced this effect to the oscillatory nature of the LO wave function at
r\rightarrow 0
, where we note that
r\lesssim\Lambda^{-1}_{b}
is outside the domain of applicability of the EFT expansion. Exceptional cutoffs were further investigated in Refs.
Peng et al. 2024
;
Yang 2024
, where two similar approaches were proposed to remedy these divergences within an EFT framework. Below, we summarize the origin of exceptional cutoffs, using
{}^{3}P_{0}
as a first example, and extend the analysis to the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel, see also Ref.
Peng et al. 2025
.

III.1
The
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel

The LO scattering amplitude in the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel,
T^{(0)}
, is computed non-perturbatively by solving the Lippmann-Schwinger (LS) equation

T^{(0)}=V^{(0)}+V^{(0)}G^{+}_{0}T^{(0)},

(2)

with the free resolvent
G^{+}_{0}=\left(E-H_{0}+i\epsilon\right)^{-1}
,
H_{0}=\bm{p}^{2}/m_{N}
, and the nucleon mass
m_{N}=2m_{p}m_{n}/(m_{p}+m_{n})
is defined in terms of the proton and neutron masses. The explicit expression of the LS equation in the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel reads

\displaystyle T^{(0)}(p^{\prime},p;k)=V^{(0)}(p^{\prime},p)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\ q^{2}\ V^{(0)}(p,q)\frac{m_{N}}{k^{2}-q^{2}+i\epsilon}T^{(0)}(q,p;k),

(3)

where
k
is the on-shell momentum and the LO potential
V^{(0)}
is projected to the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel.

The NLO correction in the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel vanishes while the N
2
LO contribution to the scattering amplitude reads

T^{(2)}=\Omega^{\dagger}_{-}V^{(2)}_{2\pi}\Omega_{+}+\Omega^{\dagger}_{-}V^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}\Omega_{+}\equiv T^{(2)}_{\pi}+T^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}.

(4)

The parts containing the two-pion exchange and the contact terms can be separated by linearity, and the Møller wave operators are defined as
\Omega_{+}=\mathds{1}+G^{+}_{0}T^{(0)}
and
\Omega^{\dagger}_{-}=\mathds{1}+T^{(0)}G^{+}_{0}
. Following Ref.
Gasparyan and Epelbaum 2023

2

{}^{3}P_{0}
channel can be decomposed as

T^{(2)}_{\text{ct}}(k,k;k)=D^{(1)}_{{}^{3}P_{0}}(\Lambda)\ \psi^{2}_{\Lambda}(k)+E^{(0)}_{{}^{3}P_{0}}(\Lambda)\ 2\psi_{\Lambda}(k)\psi^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(k)

(5)

where
\psi_{\Lambda}(k)
and
\psi^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(k)
are closely related to the LO radial wave function,
R^{(0)}(kr)

Peng et al. 2024

\frac{dR^{(0)}(kr)}{dr}\Big|_{r=0}\propto\psi_{\Lambda}(k),\quad\frac{d^{3}R^{(0)}(kr)}{dr^{3}}\Big|_{r=0}\propto\psi^{\prime}_{\Lambda}(k).

(6)

The N
2
LO contribution to the

\delta^{(2)}(k)=-\frac{\rho(k)T^{(2)}(k,k;k)}{2i\exp\left(2i\delta^{(0)}(k)\right)},

(7)

where

is the LO

The determinant of the matrix in

1.

The function
\bar{\psi}_{\Lambda}(k)\to 0

2.

The three-nucleon system can be described using basis states

\ket{n\ell m_{\ell}}\otimes\ket{\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}m_{\mathcal{L}}}\otimes\ket{(s\frac{1}{2})Sm_{S}}\otimes\ket{(t\frac{1}{2})Tm_{T}},

(15)

where eigenfunctions of the three-dimensional spherical harmonic oscillator (HO)
\ket{n\ell m_{\ell}}
and
\ket{\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}m_{\mathcal{L}}}
form a basis for the Jacobi-coordinate states
\ket{\bm{\xi}_{1}}
and
\ket{\bm{\xi}_{2}}
, respectively. The oscillator length,
b
, entering these eigenfunctions is related to the HO frequency,
\omega
, as
b=(m_{N}\omega)^{-1/2}
.
Moreover,
s
and
t
denote the coupled spin and isospin of nucleons 1 and 2 while
S
and
T
, with associated projections
m_{S}
and
m_{T}
, denote the total spin and isospin for the two-nucleon subsystem coupled with the third nucleon.

With coupled angular momenta, more convenient three-nucleon basis states are formed as

\displaystyle\ket{\alpha;JT}_{12}\equiv\ket{n\ell sjt,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{J};JT}

\displaystyle\equiv\ket{n\mathcal{N}(\ell s)j(\mathcal{L}\frac{1}{2})\mathcal{J};(j\mathcal{J})Jm_{J}}\otimes\ket{(t\frac{1}{2})Tm_{T}}.

(16)

The quantum numbers
m_{J}
and
m_{T}
are dropped by rotational symmetry and assumed isospin symmetry, respectively, and we use
\alpha\equiv(n\mathcal{N}\ell sjt\mathcal{L}\mathcal{J})
.
Here,
j
denotes the total angular momentum of the relative system of nucleons 1 and 2, while
\mathcal{J}
is the total angular momentum of nucleon 3 relative to the c.m. of nucleons 1 and 2. Furthermore,
J
denotes the total angular momentum of the three-nucleon system. The two-nucleon subsystem is antisymmetric by the condition
(-1)^{\ell+s+t}=-1
. Hence, the basis states in
Eq.

16
are only partially antisymmetric.

Fully antisymmetric basis states are constructed by diagonalizing the three-particle antisymmetrizer in the partially antisymmetric basis, following Ref.
Navratil et al. 2000
. This antisymmetrizer is diagonal in the quantum numbers
(N,J,T)
, where
N\equiv 2n+\ell+2\mathcal{N}+\mathcal{L}
. Fully antisymmetric states can be expressed as

\displaystyle\ket{NJT,\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\ :\ \begin{subarray}{c}2n+\ell+\\
2\mathcal{N}+\mathcal{L}=N\end{subarray}}{}_{12}\braket{\alpha;JT|NJT,\gamma}\ket{\alpha;JT}_{12}

(17)

where
\gamma
enumerates states of fixed
(N,J,T)
. The complete set of fully antisymmetric states,
\{\ket{\Gamma}\}
, for a specific spin, parity, and isospin
(J^{\Pi},T)
can now be obtained from
Eq.

17
. The model-space basis is truncated by total HO energy, i.e., only allowing states with
N\leq N_{\mathrm{max}}
.

IV.2
The triton at leading order

The LO three-nucleon Hamiltonian in the c.m. frame reads

H^{(0)}_{\mathrm{c.m.}}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}\frac{(\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{p}_{j})^{2}}{2m_{N}}+\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}V^{(0)}_{ij}.

(18)

Here,
m_{N}
denotes the nucleon mass,
\bm{p}_{i}
the
i
:th nucleon momentum in the c.m. frame, and
V^{(0)}_{ij}
the LO two-nucleon potential for nucleons
i
and
j
. We neglect the mass difference between the proton and the neutron
Kamuntavicius et al. 1999
.

We are interested in the triton ground state and construct basis states with
(J^{\Pi},T)=(\frac{1}{2}^{+},\frac{1}{2})
according to
Eq.

17
. The Schrödinger equation is solved by diagonalizing the LO Hamiltonian in this fully antisymmetric basis

\sum_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\bra{\Gamma}H^{(0)}_{\mathrm{c.m.}}\ket{\Gamma^{\prime}}\braket{\Gamma^{\prime}|\Psi^{(0)}_{n}}=E^{(0)}_{n}\braket{\Gamma|\Psi^{(0)}_{n}},

(19)

and the full spectrum
\{\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{k}},E^{(0)}_{k}\}_{k}
can be obtained. We employ HO frequencies
\omega\in[10\ \mathrm{MeV},125\ \mathrm{MeV}]
—where the large values are needed for computations with high-momentum cutoffs—and consider basis truncations up to
N_{\mathrm{max}}=46
. The size of the partially- and fully-antisymmetric basis for
N_{\mathrm{max}}=46
is
19000
and
6336
, respectively. This means that exact diagonalization of the full Hamiltonian is possible and that the complete spectrum of states can be obtained. Knowledge of the full LO spectrum will be important when we compute the N
2
LO corrections in perturbation theory.

The convergence of the triton ground-state energy is shown in
Fig.

7
as a function of
N_{\mathrm{max}}
for different cutoffs. In this work we consider cutoffs up to
\Lambda=1560
MeV. As the cutoff increases, it becomes increasingly challenging to obtain converged results.

Our strategy to extend the reach of NCSM calculations to large cutoff values combines the use of bases with large HO frequencies (to handle ultraviolet physics) with an infrared extrapolation technique
Forssén et al. 2018
. This implies that computations are mainly performed for HO frequencies higher than those corresponding to the location of the variational minimum, and that the NCSM basis parameters are translated to relevant infrared and ultraviolet scales
Wendt et al. 2015
. Infrared-extrapolated LO ground-state energies,
E^{(0)}_{\mathrm{extmin}}
, are shown in

N_{\mathrm{max}}=46
,
E^{(0)}_{\mathrm{varmin}}\equiv\mathrm{min}_{\omega}\ E^{(0)}(\omega)
. The extrapolated energy appears to reach a plateau when
\Lambda
is increased, consistent with the findings of a similar study in Ref.
Song et al. 2017
using the Faddeev equations.

The obtained ground-state energy shown in

Song et al. 2017

Spurious deeply bound NN states appear in the
{}^{3}P_{0}
and
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channels as the LO LECs exhibit a limit-cycle-like divergence, i.e., for cutoffs
\Lambda>679
MeV. These spurious states need to be projected out from the NN spectrum before performing triton calculations. We do this using the technique of orthogonalizing pseudopotentials
Kukulin and Pomerantsev 1978
;
Song et al. 2017
;
Nogga et al. 2005
. This is implemented by transforming the LO potential as

V^{(0)}\to V^{(0)}+\sum_{\phi}\lambda_{\phi}\ket{\phi}\bra{\phi},

(20)

where
\ket{\phi}
denotes the spurious NN states and
\lambda_{\phi}
a large positive constant of order
10^{8}
MeV . This procedure removes the effect of the spurious states in the triton wave functions at LO. It turns out that this projection is sufficient to also remove the effect of the spurious states in subleading corrections, which is verified in the following subsection.

IV.3
The triton at subleading orders

Subleading corrections to the triton ground-state energy are computed in perturbation theory, where the NLO contribution reads

E^{(1)}=\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{0}|\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}V^{(1)}_{ij}|\Psi^{(0)}_{0}}.

(21)

In this work we are only interested in the ground-state energy. Therefore, only the LO ground-state wave function,
\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{0}}
, is needed to compute the NLO perturbation.

The N
2
LO contribution to the ground-state energy is

\displaystyle E^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{0}|\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}V^{(2)}_{ij}|\Psi^{(0)}_{0}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{|\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{0}|\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}V^{(1)}_{ij}|\Psi^{(0)}_{m}}|^{2}}{E^{(0)}_{0}-E^{(0)}_{m}},

(22)

where two insertions of the NLO potential and one insertion of the N
2
LO potential contribute. Note also that the N
2
LO correction to the ground state requires the full LO spectrum
\{\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{m}},E^{(0)}_{m}\}_{m}
. Although the energy denominator
(E^{(0)}_{0}-E^{(0)}_{m})
suppresses highly excited states in
Eq.

22
, we find that all states must be considered to obtain a converged result.

Perturbative corrections from NN channels with LO spurious states (
{}^{3}P_{0}
and
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
) first enter in the expectation value of the N
2
LO potential in
Eq.

22
. We confirm that this expectation value is free from the interference of spurious states by numerically verifying the relation

\displaystyle\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{k}|\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}(1-P_{\phi})_{ij}V^{(2)}_{ij}(1-P_{\phi})_{ij}|\Psi^{(0)}_{k}}

\displaystyle\to_{\lambda_{\phi}\to\infty}\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{k}|\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}V^{(2)}_{ij}|\Psi^{(0)}_{k}},

(23)

for sufficiently high values of
\lambda_{\phi}
. The projectors for the
ij
subsystems are defined as
(1-P_{\phi})_{ij}=(1-\sum_{\phi}\ket{\phi}\bra{\phi})_{ij}
.

We compute NLO and N
2
LO contributions to the triton ground-state energy from
Eqs.

21
and
22
for different values of
\omega
and
N_{\mathrm{max}}
and for cutoffs values in the range
500
MeV
\leq\Lambda\leq 1560
MeV. Results for a few representative cutoff values are shown in
Fig.

9
. We immediately notice that the variational principle does not hold beyond LO, as expected for a perturbative calculation. In particular,
E^{(2)}
in the lower-left panel shows a concave parabolic behavior as a function of
\omega
for all studied values of
N_{\mathrm{max}}
. This complicates the selection of
\omega
for assessing the convergence.
We observe that the convergence in
N_{\mathrm{max}}
becomes slower as the chiral order and cutoff increase. Nevertheless, within the range of
N_{\mathrm{max}}
considered, the perturbative corrections show a decreasing dependence on
\omega
, indicating the expected convergence with increasing basis size. As a result, we obtain reasonably converged results at N
2
LO for
\Lambda\lesssim 1200
MeV.

IV.4
The effect of exceptional cutoffs in the triton

To understand the divergent behavior of the triton ground-state energy at N
2
LO, it is instructive to study contributions from specific NN channels. We define the contribution at NLO and N
2
LO from different NN-channels,
X
, as

\displaystyle E^{(1)}_{X}

\displaystyle\equiv\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{0}|\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}P_{X}V^{(1)}_{ij}P_{X}|\Psi^{(0)}_{0}},

(25)

\displaystyle E^{(2)}_{X}

\displaystyle\equiv\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{0}|\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}P_{X}V^{(2)}_{ij}P_{X}|\Psi^{(0)}_{0}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{|\braket{\Psi^{(0)}_{0}|\sum_{i<j=1}^{3}P_{X}V^{(1)}_{ij}P_{X}|\Psi^{(0)}_{m}}|^{2}}{E^{(0)}_{0}-E^{(0)}_{m}}.

(26)

Here,
P_{X}
denotes a projector onto an NN channel
X\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{NN}}=\{^{1}S_{0},\ ^{3}P_{0},\ ^{1}P_{1},\ ^{3}P_{1},\ ^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1},\dots\}
.

The NLO expectation value is linear in the projector and the decomposition becomes additive

E^{(1)}=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{NN}}}E^{(1)}_{X}

(27)

As such, the contribution from a given NN channel is well-defined. However, the second term in
Eq.

26
is not linear in the projections, so defining a contribution from a given channel depends on how cross terms are treated, and as such the decomposition is ambiguous. However, if one removes
V^{(0)}_{1\pi}
from the NLO potential (see
Table

1
) only the
{}^{1}S_{0}
channel would have a non-zero contribution in the second term, and unambiguous channel projections are possible. Our computations show that
V^{(0)}_{1\pi}
only yields a percent-level contribution to the second term of
Eq.

26
such that approximate additivity also holds at N
2
LO

E^{(2)}\approx\sum_{X\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{NN}}}E^{(2)}_{X}.

(28)

Channels of particular interest for this study are
{}^{3}P_{0}
and
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
for which we have observed exceptional cutoff values. In
Fig.

11
we show the N
2
LO contributions,
E^{(2)}_{X}
, for these channels. These results confirm that the first divergence, at
\Lambda\approx 650
MeV, originates from the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel, while the second, at
\Lambda\approx 900
MeV, originates from the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel. In the latter case, we also observe a broad domain of cutoffs with
\Lambda<\bar{\Lambda}
over which the triton ground-state energy is impacted. We will now investigate whether these divergences can be avoided by shifting the LO wave function using the same approach as in the NN sector explored in Sec.
III
.

V
Conclusions and Outlook

In this work, we have taken the first steps of using the PC by Long and Yang in the few-nucleon sector up to N
2
LO in perturbation theory. We employed the NCSM to perform perturbative computations for the triton ground-state energy, and obtained converged results for cutoffs
\Lambda\lesssim 1200
MeV. We have reviewed how so-called exceptional cutoffs arise in the NN system in this PC
Gasparyan and Epelbaum 2023
;
Peng et al. 2024
and extended previous work by considering also the coupled
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel.
We predicted the triton ground-state energy up to N
2
LO and interpreted the results using our understanding of exceptional cutoff values.

The conclusions can be summarized as follows.

(i)

We show that exceptional cutoffs also arise in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel, where the pattern of appearance is slightly different compared to the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel. In particular, exceptional cutoffs appear at more irregular locations and not just below an associated limit-cycle-like cutoff, see
Fig.

2
. For example, one exceptional cutoff appears at
\Lambda\approx 1340
MeV, whose effect is observed in the triton calculations, see
Fig.

10
.

(ii)

The predicted deuteron ground-state energy at N
2
LO diverges at the exceptional cutoffs, see
Fig.

3
. We apply the method proposed in Ref.
Peng et al. 2024
to shift the LO wave function and successfully remove the effect of the divergences, see
Fig.

5
.

(iii)

We can obtain converged perturbative computations of the triton ground-state energy using the NCSM up to the cutoff
\Lambda\approx 1200
MeV. However, effects of exceptional cutoffs in the
{}^{3}P_{0}
and
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channels propagate to the triton N
2
LO result producing divergences, see
Fig.

10
and
Fig.

11
.

(iv)

We demonstrate that the method proposed in Ref.
Peng et al. 2024
effectively removes the exceptional cutoff divergence originating from the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel in the triton at N
2
LO, as shown in
Fig.

13
.
However, we find that the same method is not well-suited for taming the effect of the exceptional cutoff stemming from the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel, see
Fig.

14
.

The appearance of exceptional cutoffs seems to be intimately related to applying perturbation theory using a LO wave function with uncontrolled short-distance behavior. A recent study systematically investigated the connection between exceptional cutoffs and regulators
Pavon Valderrama 2025
. The findings suggest that exceptional cutoffs can be avoided by using a suitable type of regulation—which remains to be studied further.

Furthermore, the order-by-order convergence of few-nucleon observables should be investigated in more detail. This requires an improved method of inferring the LECs such that the EFT error can be properly assessed to facilitate more robust predictions.
The effect of including three-nucleon forces
Weinberg 1992
;
van Kolck 1994
should also be investigated.

Exceptional cutoffs currently present a challenge for reliably computing nuclear observables beyond NLO for
\Lambda\gtrsim 650
MeV. However, as seen in our deuteron results, there are wide regions at
\Lambda\gtrsim 1500
MeV without exceptional cutoffs, implying that the divergences can be avoided in principle. This region remains challenging to access in many-body computations. There are fortunately no exceptional cutoffs in the vicinity of
\Lambda\approx 500
MeV where many-body computations can be converged. Studying heavier-mass nuclei at relatively low cutoffs therefore provides an opportunity to analyze the predictive differences between perturbative schemes (guided by RG invariance) and the non-perturbative WPC commonly employed in
\chi
EFT.

Appendix A
Toy problem of exceptional behavior in the non-singular
{}^{1}S_{0}
channel

The appearance of exceptional points is connected to the properties of the LO wave function, i.e., the distorted wave when computing subleading corrections. It is not a feature intrinsic to singular potentials, but can also appear as a non-singular potential is made more attractive. We will illustrate this, and the role of the LO wave function, with a toy scenario that involves variation of the axial coupling,
g_{A}
, in the LO potential.

We consider the
{}^{1}S_{0}
partial wave and the LO potential according to
Table

1
, with
\Lambda=500
MeV. We vary
g_{A}
, which controls the strength of OPE, and adjust the LO LEC to reproduce the empirical
{}^{1}S_{0}
phase shift
Stoks et al. 1993
at
T_{\mathrm{lab}}=40
MeV. The resulting value of the LEC,
C^{(0)}_{{}^{1}S_{0}}
, is shown in the left panel of
Fig.

15
and a divergence is observed, analogous to the limit-cycle-like divergence at LO in
Figs.

1
and
2
.

Furthermore, we add the NLO correction in
{}^{1}S_{0}
perturbatively (see
Table

1
) and the two LECs
C^{(1)}_{{}^{1}S_{0}}
and
D^{(0)}_{{}^{1}S_{0}}
are fixed by reproducing the empirical phase shifts at
T_{\mathrm{lab}}=50
MeV and
T_{\mathrm{lab}}=80
MeV. The NLO LECs as a function of
g_{A}
are shown in the middle and right panels of
Fig.

15
. Here we observe two divergences, one where the LO LEC diverges and one for a slightly lower value of
g_{A}
. We can now see that
Fig.

15
is completely analogous to
Fig.

1
, but now
g_{A}
is varied instead of
\Lambda
.

The predicted
{}^{1}S_{0}
phase shift at
T_{\mathrm{lab}}=60
MeV is shown in
Fig.

16
. It can be observed that this prediction diverges at
g_{A}\approx 4.023
, which is the same value indicated by the red vertical line in
Fig.

15
. This means that for
g_{A}\approx 4.023
we cannot find NLO LECs with the renormalization conditions used. The situation is completely analogous to what happens at an exceptional cutoff.

This toy computation illustrates that the LO wave function seems to behave similarly when the potential
(i)
is singular and the cutoff is increased, and
(ii)
is non-singular and its strength is increased. This can explain the qualitative similarity of the

1

Therefore, it might be beneficial to study the problem of exceptional cutoffs in the Long and Yang PC (and MWPCs in general) from the perspective of an “exceptional strength” of the LO potential, or simply an “exceptional” LO wave function. Interestingly, a recent work
Pavon Valderrama 2025
couples the appearance of exceptional points to the way the potentials are regulated.

Exceptional cutoffs in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}

To study exceptional cutoffs in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel, we can proceed in the same way as for the
{}^{3}P_{0}
channel
Gasparyan and Epelbaum 2023
. The goal is to arrive at an equation similar to
Eq.

10
, but for the N
2
LO LECs in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel.

We consider neutron-proton scattering, where a neutron with laboratory kinetic energy
T_{\mathrm{lab}}
impinges on a proton. The LO amplitude in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel,
T^{(0)}
, is obtained (non-perturbatively) by solving the LS-equation

T^{(0)}=V^{(0)}+V^{(0)}G^{+}_{0}T^{(0)},

(30)

where the free resolvent is given by

G^{+}_{0}=\left(E-H_{0}+i\epsilon\right)^{-1},

(31)

and
H_{0}=\bm{p}^{2}/m_{N}
. Here,
\bm{p}
denotes the NN relative momentum in the c.m. frame and
m_{N}
denotes the nucleon mass. Explicitly, we solve the partial wave projected LS-equation

T^{(0)}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}(p^{\prime},p;k)=V^{(0)}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}(p^{\prime},p)+\sum_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{\prime\prime}p^{\prime\prime 2}V^{(0)}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell^{\prime\prime}}(p^{\prime},p^{\prime\prime})\frac{m_{N}}{k^{2}-p^{\prime\prime 2}+i\epsilon}T^{(0)}_{\ell^{\prime\prime}\ell}(p^{\prime\prime},p;k)

(32)

to obtain the scattering amplitude
T^{(0)}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}(p^{\prime},p;k)
. Here,
p\ (p^{\prime})
denotes the modulus of the ingoing (outgoing) relative momentum in the
np
scattering process, and
\ell
(
\ell^{\prime})
denotes the ongoing (outgoing) angular momentum quantum number. The modulus of the on-shell momentum,
k
, is related to the laboratory kinetic energy,
T_{\mathrm{lab}}
, as

k^{2}=\frac{m_{p}^{2}T_{\mathrm{lab}}(2m_{n}+T_{\mathrm{lab}})}{(m_{n}+m_{p})^{2}+2m_{p}T_{\mathrm{lab}}}.

(33)

The NLO contribution in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}^{3}D_{1}
channel vanishes while the N
2
LO potential is non-zero and gives the N
2
LO amplitude as

\displaystyle T^{(2)}

\displaystyle=T^{(2)}_{\pi}+T^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}

\displaystyle T^{(2)}_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{\dagger}_{-}V^{(2)}_{2\pi}\Omega_{+}

(34)

\displaystyle T^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{\dagger}_{-}V^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}\Omega_{+},

where we separate the parts coming from the two-pion exchange and the contact terms. The Møller wave operators are defined as
\Omega_{+}=\mathds{1}+G^{+}_{0}T^{(0)}
and
\Omega^{\dagger}_{-}=\mathds{1}+T^{(0)}G^{+}_{0}
.

We can write the N
2
LO contact potential as a matrix in
\ell

Thim et al. 2024

V^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\begin{pmatrix}C^{(1)}_{{}^{3}S_{1}}+D^{(0)}_{{}^{3}S_{1}}(p^{\prime 2}+p^{2})&D^{(0)}_{SD}p^{2}\\
D^{(0)}_{SD}p^{\prime 2}&0\end{pmatrix}

(35)

and the contact part of the on-shell
T
-matrix can be decomposed as

T^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}=\Omega^{\dagger}_{-}V^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}\Omega_{+}=T^{(2)}_{0}C^{(1)}_{{}^{3}S_{1}}+T^{(2)}_{1}D^{(0)}_{{}^{3}S_{1}}+T^{(2)}_{2}D^{(0)}_{SD}.

(36)

An explicit calculation gives

\displaystyle T^{(2)}_{\mathrm{ct}}(k,k;k)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{00,0}^{2}&\psi_{00,0}\psi_{20,0}\\
\psi_{00,0}\psi_{20,0}&\psi^{2}_{20,0}\end{pmatrix}C^{(1)}_{{}^{3}S_{1}}

\displaystyle+\begin{pmatrix}2\psi_{00,0}\psi_{00,2}&\psi_{00,0}\psi_{20,2}+\psi_{00,2}\psi_{20,0}\\
\psi_{00,0}\psi_{20,2}+\psi_{00,2}\psi_{20,0}&2\psi_{20,0}(k)\psi_{20,2}\end{pmatrix}D^{(0)}_{{}^{3}S_{1}}

(37)

\displaystyle+\begin{pmatrix}2\psi_{02,2}\psi_{00,0}&\psi_{22,2}\psi_{00,0}+\psi_{02,2}\psi_{20,0}\\
\psi_{22,2}\psi_{00,0}+\psi_{02,2}\psi_{20,0}&2\psi_{22,2}\psi_{20,0}\end{pmatrix}D^{(0)}_{SD}

where

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3/2}\psi_{\ell^{\prime}\ell,n}(k)\equiv k^{n}\exp{-(k/\Lambda)^6}\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}+\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\ q^{2+n}\exp{-(q/\Lambda)^6}G^{+}_{0}(q;k)T^{(0)}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}(k,q;k).

Note that the
\Lambda
- and

Max-Min Rate Optimization for Multigroup Multicast MISO Systems Via Novel Transmissive RIS Transceiver
Yuan Guo, Wen Chen, Qingqing Wu, Yanze Zhu, Yang Liu, Zhendong Li, and Ying Wang

Abstract
This paper investigates a novel transmissive reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) transceiver architecture-enabled multigroup multicast downlink communication system. Under this setup, an optimization problem is formulated to maximize the minimum rate of users across all groups, subject to the maximum available power of each RIS unit. Due to the non-differentiable nature of the objective function, the max-min rate problem is challenging to solve. To tackle this difficult problem, we develop an iterative solution by leveraging the successive convex approximation (SCA) and the penalty function method. However, the above approach has high computational complexity and may lead to compromised performance. To overcome these drawbacks, we design an efficient second-order cone programming (SOCP)-based method using the weighted minimum mean squared error (WMMSE) framework to reduce computational complexity. Furthermore, to further reduce the computational complexity, we also propose a low-complexity and solver-free algorithm that analytically updates all variables by combining the smooth approximation theory and the majorization-minimization (MM) method. Numerical results are provided to verify the convergence and effectiveness of our proposed three algorithms. It is also demonstrated that the SOCP-based method outperforms the penalty-based algorithm in terms of both the achieved min rate and the computational complexity. In contrast, the low-complexity design achieves significantly lower complexity with only slightly degraded performance.

Specifically,
the contributions of this paper are elaborated as follows:

•

This paper considers the beamforming design in a multigroup multicast multiple-input single-output (MISO) downlink communication system empowered by the novel TRTC architecture,
aiming to explore potential performance gains.
We investigate the problem of maximizing the sum-rate across all multicast groups,
where the rate of each group is constrained by the minimum rate among its users,
subject to the individual maximum transmit power limits of each TRTC element.
To the best of our knowledge,
this problem has not been explored in the existing literature, e.g.,
[
21
]
-
[
31
]
.

•

Due to the complex and non-differentiable nature of the objective function,
solving the highly non-convex max-min rate problem is particularly challenging.
To tackle this optimization problem,
we first convert the non-differentiable concave objective function into a set of constraints by introducing slack variables,
and then equivalently reformulate it as a rank-constrained semidefinite programming (SDP) problem.
By integrating the successive convex approximation (SCA) technique
[
35
]
with the penalty function method,
we successfully develop an iterative algorithm to solve the resulting SDP problem.

•

To reduce computational complexity,
we reformulate the original problem as a second-order cone programming (SOCP) problem
via combining the weighted minimum mean squared error (WMMSE) framework
[
33
]
with the introduction of slack variables.
Based on this reformulation,
we design an efficient iterative algorithm to solve the SOCP problem.

•

Furthermore,
we also develop a low-complexity solution that avoids reliance on any numerical solver, e.g., CVX.
Specifically,
based on the block diagonal structure of the quadratic term coefficient and the TRTC element power constraint,
we decompose the original variable into multiple subvariables.
And then,
the non-differentiable concave objective function is first approximated by a differentiable one using smooth approximation theory
[
34
]
.
Subsequently,
by exploiting the majorization-minimization (MM) method
[
35
]
and analyzing optimality conditions,
we are able to derive analytical solutions for all variables.

•

Last but not least,
extensive numerical results
are presented to validate
the effectiveness and efficiency of our proposed three solutions.
The results demonstrate that the SOCP-based algorithm outperforms the penalty-based approach in terms of both the achieved group sum-rate
and computational complexity.
Meanwhile,
the MM-based design exhibits significantly lower computational complexity, with only a slight performance trade-off.

System Model and Problem Formulation

II-A

System Model

Fig. 1:
A TRTC enabled multigroup multicast communication system.

As shown in Fig.
1
,
we consider a TRTC-enabled
multigroup multicast MISO communication system,
where a TRTC
equipped with
N
elements serves
K
single-antenna users grouped into
G
multicast groups.
For convenience,
the sets of users, multicast groups, and TRTC units are denoted
as

\mathcal{K}=\{1,2,\cdots,K\}
,

\mathcal{G}=\{1,2,\cdots,G\}
,

\mathcal{N}=\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
, respectively.
Let

g\in\mathcal{G}
.
Besides,

\mathcal{K}_{i}\cap\mathcal{K}_{j}=\varnothing
,

,

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n}\mathbf{f}\leq P_{t},\forall n\in\mathcal{N},

(2)

\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n}\triangleq\text{blkdiag}(\mathbf{A}_{n},\cdots,\mathbf{A}_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{NG\times NG}
,
and

P_{t}

The received signal at the
k
-th user belonging to group
g
can be represented as

\displaystyle y_{k}\!=\!\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{f}s_{g}\!\!+\!\!\!{\sum}_{i\neq g}^{G}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{f}s_{i}\!+\!n_{k},\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G},

(3)

where

\mathbf{h}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
represents the channel from the TRTC to the
k
-th user
and

n_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma_{k}^{2})

is the complex additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) at the
k
-th user,

\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\triangleq[\mathbf{{h}}_{k}^{T},\cdots,\mathbf{{h}}_{k}^{T}]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{NG\times 1}
,
an index vector
\mathbf{b}_{g}
indicates that
the value of the positions within the range
((g-1)\times N+1)\sim(g\times N)
is 1 and other positions are
0
,
i.e.,

\mathbf{b}_{g}\triangleq[0,\cdots,0,\underbrace{1,\cdots,1}\limits_{\textrm{N}},0,\cdots,0]\in\mathbb{R}^{NG\times 1}
,
and

\mathbf{B}_{g}\triangleq\text{diag}(\mathbf{b}_{g})\in\mathbb{R}^{NG\times NG}
,
\forall g\in\mathcal{G}
.

The SINR for the
k
-th user is obtained as

\displaystyle\textrm{SINR}_{k}=\frac{|\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{f}|^{2}}{\sum_{i\neq g}^{G}|\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{f}|^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}},\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G},

(4)

and the achievable rate of each user can be written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{k}(\mathbf{f})=\textrm{log}(1+\textrm{SINR}_{k}),\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G}.

(5)

II-B

Problem Formulation

Due to the characteristics of the multicast communication framework,
our goal is to maximize the minimum rate of each group
via optimizing the transmit beamformer
\mathbf{f}
.
Mathematically,
the optimization problem is formulated as

\displaystyle\textrm{(P0)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\mathbf{f}}\ \bigg\{\mathrm{R}_{s}(\mathbf{f})={\sum}_{g=1}^{G}\mathop{\textrm{min}}\limits_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\mathrm{R}_{k}(\mathbf{f})\bigg\}

(6a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n}\mathbf{f}\leq P_{t},\forall n\in\mathcal{N},

(6b)

The problem (P0) is a non-convex problem and challenging to solve
since its highly non-differentiable and non-convex objective function.

III
Penalty-based Method

In this section,
we will propose a penalty-based method to solve problem (P0).
Firstly,
to make the problem (P0) more tractable,
by introducing the slack variables
\{t_{g}\}
,
problem (P0) can be equivalently transformed as follows

\displaystyle\textrm{(P1)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\mathbf{f},\{t_{g}\}}\ {\sum}_{g=1}^{G}t_{g}

(7a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{k}(\mathbf{f})\geq t_{g},\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G},

(7b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n}\mathbf{f}\leq P_{t},\forall n\in\mathcal{N}.

(7c)

Note that
the optimization problem (P1) is still non-convex with respect to (w.r.t.) the variable
\mathbf{f}
.
To make the problem more tractable,
by defining
\mathbf{F}\triangleq\mathbf{f}\mathbf{f}^{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{NG\times NG}
,
we can equivalently recast the optimization problem (P1) as
a rank-constrained semidefinite programming (SDP) problem,
which is given as

\displaystyle\textrm{(P2)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\mathbf{F},\{t_{g}\}}\ {\sum}_{g=1}^{G}t_{g}

(8a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathrm{\tilde{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})\geq t_{g},\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G},

(8b)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\mathbf{F}\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n})\leq P_{t},\forall n\in\mathcal{N},

(8c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\succeq\mathbf{0},

(8d)

\displaystyle\text{Rank}(\mathbf{F})=1.

(8e)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{\bar{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})\triangleq\textrm{log}\bigg(1+\frac{\textrm{Tr}(\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{F})}{\sum_{i\neq g}^{G}\textrm{Tr}(\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{F})+\sigma_{k}^{2}}\bigg).

(9)

However,
the problem (P2) is still non-convex due to the constraints (
8b
) and (
8e
).
Next,
we will deal with the constraints (
8b
) and (
8e
) one by one.
First,
we rewrite the left of the constraint (
8b
) in the form of the difference of convex (DC) function,
which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{\bar{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})=\mathrm{\dot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})-\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}),

(10)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{\dot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})\triangleq\textrm{log}\big({\sum}_{g=1}^{G}\textrm{Tr}(\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{F})+\sigma_{k}^{2}\big),

(11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})\triangleq\textrm{log}\big({\sum}_{i\neq g}^{G}\textrm{Tr}(\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{F})+\sigma_{k}^{2}\big).

(12)

Since two functions
\mathrm{\dot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})

and

\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})

are both concave in terms of the variable
\mathbf{F}
,
the constraint
\mathrm{\dot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})-\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})\geq t_{g}
is non-convex.

Following the SCA method
[
35
]
,
we linearize the non-convex term
\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})
to obtain a tight upper bound as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})\leq\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})+\textrm{Tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{F}}^{H}\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})(\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}_{0})),

(13)

where
\mathbf{F}_{0}
is the value obtained in the last iteration,
and the gradient of function
\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}
w.r.t.
\mathbf{F}
is given as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{F}}\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})\triangleq\frac{{\sum}_{i\neq g}^{G}(\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g})}{{\sum}_{i\neq g}^{G}\textrm{Tr}(\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{F})+\sigma_{k}^{2}}.

(14)

Based on the above transformations,
the problem (P2) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\textrm{(P3)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{min}}\limits_{\mathbf{F},\{t_{g}\}}\ -{\sum}_{g=1}^{G}t_{g}

(15a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathrm{\dot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})-\textrm{Tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{F}}^{H}\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})(\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}_{0}))

(15b)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})\geq t_{g},\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G},

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\mathbf{F}\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n})\leq P_{t},\forall n\in\mathcal{N},

(15c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\succeq\mathbf{0},

(15d)

\displaystyle\text{Rank}(\mathbf{F})=1.

(15e)

It is important to note that
the only remaining non-convexity of the problem (P3) arises from the rank constraint (
15e
).
Next,
we continue to tackle the rank-one constraint (
15e
),
which can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\text{Rank}(\mathbf{F})=1\Leftrightarrow\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\ast}-\|\mathbf{F}\|_{2}\leq 0,

(16)

where

\|\cdot\|_{2}

and

\|\cdot\|_{\ast}

represent the spectral norm and nuclear norm, respectively.
For the any positive semi-definite matrix
\mathbf{F}
,
the following inequality can be held:

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\ast}={\sum}_{i}\sigma_{1,i}\geq\|\mathbf{F}\|_{2}=\mathop{\text{max}}\limits_{i}\ \sigma_{1,i},

(17)

where

\sigma_{1,i}
is the
i
-th singular value of the matrix
\mathbf{F}
.
Besides,
when the rank of the matrix
\mathbf{F}
is one,
the equation (
17
) can achieve equality.

Next,
we propose a penalty-based method
[
36
]
-
[
37
]
,
to tackle it.
By adding the constraint (
17
) into the objective function,
problem (P3) is transformed into

\displaystyle\textrm{(P4)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{min}}\limits_{\mathbf{F},\{t_{g}\}}\ -{\sum}_{g=1}^{G}t_{g}+\frac{1}{2\rho}(\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\ast}-\|\mathbf{F}\|_{2})

(18a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathrm{\dot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})-\textrm{Tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{F}}^{H}\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})(\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}_{0}))

(18b)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})\geq t_{g},\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G},

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\mathbf{F}\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n})\leq P_{t},\forall n\in\mathcal{N},

(18c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\succeq\mathbf{0},

(18d)

where

\rho
denotes the penalty factor.

However,

we again adopt the SCA method to convexify the term

by linearization as follows

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{F}\|_{2}\geq\|\mathbf{F}_{0}\|_{2}+\textrm{Tr}(\bm{\lambda}_{max}(\mathbf{F}_{0})\bm{\lambda}_{max}^{H}(\mathbf{F}_{0})(\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}_{0})),

(19)

where

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{min}}\limits_{\mathbf{F},\{t_{g}\}}\ -{\sum}_{g=1}^{G}t_{g}+\frac{1}{2\rho}\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\ast}

(20a)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\rho}(\|\mathbf{F}_{0}\|_{2}+\textrm{Tr}(\bm{\lambda}_{max}(\mathbf{F}_{0})\bm{\lambda}_{max}^{H}(\mathbf{F}_{0})(\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}_{0})))

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathrm{\dot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F})-\textrm{Tr}(\nabla_{\mathbf{F}}^{H}\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})(\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}_{0}))

(20b)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{\ddot{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{F}_{0})\geq t_{g},\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G},

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\mathbf{F}\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n})\leq P_{t},\forall n\in\mathcal{N},

(20c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\succeq\mathbf{0}.

(20d)

\mathbf{F}

SOCP-based Method

According to

IV-A

Problem Reformulation

Firstly,
we adopt the WMMSE framework

[
33
]

to convert the objective function (
6a
) of problem (P0).
Specifically,
by introducing auxiliary variables
\{\beta_{k}\}
and
\{\omega_{k}\}
,
the function
\mathrm{R}_{k}(\mathbf{f})
can be written into an equivalent
variation form presented in (

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}_{k}(\mathbf{f})=\textrm{log}\big(1+|\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{f}|^{2}[{\sum}_{i\neq g}^{G}(\mathbf{f}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{f})+\sigma_{k}^{2}]^{-1}\big)

(21)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\omega_{k}\geq 0}\bigg\{\textrm{log}(\omega_{k})-\omega_{k}\big({\sum}_{i=1}^{G}(\mathbf{f}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{f})+\sigma_{k}^{2}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{f}+1\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\omega_{k}\geq 0,\beta_{k}}\bigg\{\underbrace{\textrm{log}(\omega_{k})-\omega_{k}\big(1-2\text{Re}\{\beta_{k}^{\ast}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{g}\mathbf{f}\}+|\beta_{k}|^{2}({\sum}_{i=1}^{G}(\mathbf{f}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}\mathbf{\bar{h}}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{B}_{i}\mathbf{f})+\sigma_{k}^{2})\big)+1}\limits_{\mathrm{\tilde{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{f},\omega_{k},\beta_{k})}\bigg\},\forall k\in\mathcal{K}_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G}.

Therefore,
the original problem (P0) can be equivalently converted to

\displaystyle\textrm{(P6)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\mathbf{f},\{\omega_{k}\},\{\beta_{k}\}}\ \bigg\{\!\mathrm{R}_{s}(\mathbf{f})\!=\!\!{\sum}_{g=1}^{G}\!\mathop{\textrm{min}}\limits_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\!\mathrm{\tilde{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{f},\omega_{k},\beta_{k})\!\!\bigg\}

(22a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{A}}_{n}\mathbf{f}\leq P_{t},\forall n\in\mathcal{N},

(22b)

In the following,

[

]

(23)

(24)

V
Low-complexity Algorithm

Note that our previously proposed Alg.1 and Alg.2 significantly rely on numerical solvers,
e.g., CVX,
to update the transmit beamformer.
This reliance may lead to some undesirable properties:

i) General convex optimization solvers, including CVX,
relies on interior point (IP) method
[
32
]
to resolve SDP and/or SOCP
problems,
which always produces high computational complexity.

ii) The use of third-party solvers inherently raises costs and adds inconvenience to algorithm implementation,
such as the need for purchasing licenses,
installing and maintaining software,
and ensuring the necessary platform support for the solver.

Therefore, we proceed to explore an efficient solution that, hopefully,
does not rely on any numerical solvers.

V-A

Efficient Update of
\mathbf{f}

In this subsection,
we investigate a low-complexity solution for solving the problem (P7) in Sec. IV-C.
First, note that the coefficient
\mathbf{B}_{1,k}
is a block diagonal matrix.
Combining this with the structure of the TRTC’s element power constraint (
27b
),
we decompose the variable
\mathbf{f}
into
N
subvariables
\{\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\}
,
each of which is defined as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\triangleq

\displaystyle[\mathbf{f}(n),\mathbf{f}(N+n),\cdots,\mathbf{f}((g-1)\times N+n),

(29)

\displaystyle\cdots,\mathbf{f}((G-1)\times N+n)]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{G\times 1}.

And then,
we introduce the following notations presented in (
30
).

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{b}}_{1,n,k}\triangleq[\mathbf{B}_{1,k}(n,n),\mathbf{B}_{1,k}(N+n,N+n),\cdots,\mathbf{B}_{1,k}((G-1)\times N+n,(G-1)\times N+n)]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{G\times 1},

(30)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{B}}_{n,k}\triangleq\text{diag}(\mathbf{\bar{b}}_{1,n,k}),\bar{b}_{2,n,k,g}\triangleq{\sum}_{j\neq n}^{N}\mathbf{f}^{\ast}((g-1)\times N+j)\mathbf{B}_{1,k}((g-1)\times N+j,(g-1)\times N+n),

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{2,n,k}\triangleq[\bar{b}_{2,n,k,1},\bar{b}_{2,n,k,2},\cdots,\bar{b}_{2,n,k,G}]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{G\times 1},\mathbf{b}_{3,n,k}\triangleq[\mathbf{b}_{1,k}(n),\mathbf{b}_{1,k}(N+n),\cdots,\mathbf{b}_{1,k}((G-1)\times N+n)]^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{G\times 1},

\displaystyle c_{2,n,k}\triangleq{\sum}_{g=1}^{G}{\sum}_{i\neq n}^{N}{\sum}_{j\neq n}^{N}\mathbf{f}^{\ast}((g-1)\times N+i)\mathbf{B}_{1,k}((g-1)\times N+i,(g-1)\times N+j)\mathbf{f}((g-1)\times N+j),

\displaystyle c_{3,n,k}\triangleq{\sum}_{i\neq n}^{N}2\text{Re}\{\mathbf{b}_{3,i,k}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{i}\},\mathbf{b}_{4,n,k}\triangleq\mathbf{b}_{3,n,k}-\mathbf{b}_{2,n,k},c_{4,n,k}\triangleq c_{1,k}-c_{2,n,k}+c_{3,n,k}.

With other variables (i.e.,
\{\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{i},i\neq n\}
) being fixed,
the function
\mathrm{\tilde{R}}_{k}(\mathbf{f},\omega_{k},\beta_{k})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{\tilde{R}}_{k}=\underbrace{-\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{B}}_{n,k}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}+2\text{Re}\{\mathbf{b}_{4,n,k}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\}+c_{4,n,k}}\limits_{\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})},

(31)

Therefore,
the optimization problem w.r.t. the variable
\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}
can be formulated as

\displaystyle\textrm{(P9)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}}\ {\sum}_{g=1}^{G}\mathop{\textrm{min}}\limits_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\{\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})\}

(32a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\leq P_{t}.

(32b)

Obviously,
the objective function
\mathop{\textrm{min}}_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\{\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})\}
in (
32a
) is non-differentiable.
Based on the smooth approximation theory
[
34
]
,
we can approximate it as follows

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{min}}_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\{\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})\}\approx\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})

(33)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\mu_{n,g}}\text{log}\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\text{exp}\big(-\mu_{n,g}\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})\big)\bigg),

where

\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})

is a smooth function and is the lower bound for

\mathop{\textrm{min}}_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})
,
and

\mu_{n,g}
is a smoothing parameter that satisfies the following condition:

\displaystyle\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})\!\!+\!\!\frac{1}{\mu_{n,g}}\text{log}(|\mathcal{K}_{g}|)\!\!\geq\!\!\mathop{\textrm{min}}_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\{\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})\}\!\!\geq\!\!\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}).

(34)

Besides,
the function
-\frac{1}{\mu_{n,g}}\text{log}\big(\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\text{exp}\big(-\mu_{n,g}\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})\big)\big)
is monotonically increasing and concave w.r.t.

\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})
, which has been proven in
[
5
]
.
And the function
\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})

is concave in
\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}
.
Based on the composition principle
[
32
]
,

\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})
is also a concave function of
\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}
.

When the appropriate value of
\mu_{n,g}
is given,
we turn to solve the following problem

\displaystyle\textrm{(P10)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}}\ {\sum}_{g=1}^{G}\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})

(35a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\leq P_{t}.

(35b)

It can be observed that
the above problem (P10) is still complex and difficult to solve.
Inspired by the MM framework
[
35
]
,
we can construct a lower bound of the objective function (
35a
),
which is given as

\displaystyle\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})\geq\mathrm{\grave{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}|\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0})

(36)

\displaystyle=c_{5,n,g}+2\text{Re}\{\mathbf{b}_{5,n,g}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\}+\alpha_{n,g}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n},

where

\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0}
is obtained from the last iteration,
and the newly introduced coefficients are defined as follows

\displaystyle h_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0})\triangleq\frac{\text{exp}(-\mu_{n,g}\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}))}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\text{exp}(-\mu_{n,g}\mathrm{\acute{R}}_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}))},

(37)

\displaystyle tp_{n,k}\triangleq\lambda_{\text{max}}(\mathbf{\bar{B}}_{n,k}\mathbf{\bar{B}}_{n,k}^{H})P_{t}\!+\!\|\mathbf{b}_{4,n,k}\|_{2}^{2}\!+\!2\sqrt{P_{t}}\|\mathbf{\bar{B}}_{n,k}\mathbf{b}_{4,n,k}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\alpha_{n,g}\triangleq-\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\{\lambda_{\text{max}}(\mathbf{\bar{B}}_{n,k})\}-2\mu_{n,g}\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}\{tp_{n,k}\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{5,n,g}\triangleq{\sum}_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{g}}h_{n,k}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0})(\mathbf{b}_{4,n,k}-\mathbf{\bar{B}}_{n,k}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0})-\alpha_{n,g}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0},

\displaystyle c_{5,n,g}\triangleq\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0})-2\text{Re}\{\mathbf{b}_{6,n,g}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0}\}+\alpha_{n,g}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n,0},

and the derivation details of (
37
) can be seen in Appendix A.

Therefore,
replacing the function
\mathrm{\breve{R}}_{n,g}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n})
by (
36
),
we turn to optimize a convex lower bound of the objective function of (P10),
which is expressed as

\displaystyle\textrm{(P11)}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{max}}\limits_{\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}}\ \bar{\alpha}_{n}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}+2\text{Re}\{\mathbf{b}_{7,n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\}+c_{6,n}

(38a)

\displaystyle\textrm{s.t.}\ \mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\leq P_{t}.

(38b)

where

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{n}\triangleq{\sum}_{g=1}^{G}\alpha_{n,g},\mathbf{b}_{7,n}\triangleq{\sum}_{g=1}^{G}\mathbf{b}_{5,n,g},

(39)

\displaystyle c_{6,n}\triangleq{\sum}_{g=1}^{G}c_{5,n,g}.

Since
\bar{\alpha}_{n}\leq 0
,
the problem (P11) is convex
and can be solved via off-the-shelf numerical solvers, e.g., CVX.

In order to efficiently solve the problem (P11),
we adopt the Lagrangian multiplier method
[
32
]
to obtain the optimal closed-form solution of problem (P11).
Firstly,
by denoting the Lagrangian multiplier associated with the
constraint of (
38b
) as
\nu
,
the Lagrange function associated with the
problem (P11) is written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n},\nu)=

\displaystyle-\bar{\alpha}_{n}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}-2\text{Re}\{\mathbf{b}_{6,n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}\}

(40)

\displaystyle-c_{6,n}+\nu(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{H}\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}-P_{t}),

Furthermore,
by setting the first-order derivative of the Lagrange function
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n},\nu)
w.r.t.
\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}
to zero,
we can have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n},\nu)}{\partial\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}}=\mathbf{0}.

(41)

And then,
we can obtain the solution of
\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}
as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}=\frac{\mathbf{b}_{6,n}}{\nu-\bar{\alpha}_{n}}.

(42)

By substituting the equation (
42
) into the power constraint (
38b
),
the resulting expression is formulated as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{b}_{6,n}^{H}\mathbf{b}_{6,n}}{(\nu-\bar{\alpha}_{n})^{2}}\leq P_{t}.

(43)

Note that
the left hand side of (
43
) is a decreasing function w.r.t.
the Lagrangian multiplier
\nu
.
Then
the optimal solution to problem (P11) is given by one of the
following two cases:

CASE-I
:
If the equation (
43
) is satisfied when
\nu=0
,
then the optimal solution of (P11) is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{\star}=-\frac{\mathbf{b}_{6,n}}{\bar{\alpha}_{n}}.

(44)

CASE-II
:
Otherwise,

\nu
is positive.
And the optimal solution to problem (P11) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbf{\bar{f}}_{n}^{\star}=\sqrt{P_{t}}\frac{\mathbf{b}_{6,n}}{\|\mathbf{b}_{6,n}\|_{2}}.

(45)

VII
Conclusions

This paper investigates a TRTC-enabled multigroup multicast MISO communication system,
aiming to maximize the minimum rate among all user groups
by optimizing the transmit beamforming vectors at the TRTC,
subject to individual transmit power constraints at each TRTC unit.
To solve the max-min rate optimization problem while balancing performance and computational complexity,
we propose three iterative algorithms: a penalty-based algorithm, an SOCP-based algorithm, and an MM-based algorithm.
Numerical results demonstrate that all three proposed optimization methods significantly enhance the sum-rate performance
and highlight the potential of TRTC as a novel transceiver architecture for wireless systems characterized by low cost and low power consumption.
Furthermore, it is shown that the MM-based approach offers an efficient solution for transmit beamforming optimization,
achieving reduced complexity with only a slight performance trade-off.

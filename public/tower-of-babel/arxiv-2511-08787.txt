Maximal twisted Betti numbers of complex hyperplane arrangement complements
Yongqiang Liu, Laurentiu Maxim, Botong Wang

Abstract
. We show that the Betti numbers of a local system on the complement of an essential complex hyperplane arrangement are maximized precisely when the local system is constant. This result answers positively a recent question of Yoshinaga and the first author.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
32S22, 32S60, 52C35, 55N25.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{d}\}
be an essential affine hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with complement
U_{\mathcal{A}}
. As shown independently by Dimca-Papadima
[
DP03
]
and Randell
[
Ra02
]
, the complement
U_{\mathcal{A}}
has the homotopy type of a minimal CW complex, hence, in particular, its cohomology groups
H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathbb{Z})
are free abelian, for any integer
i\geq 0
. The Betti numbers
b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}})
of
U_{\mathcal{A}}
are classically known, e.g., see
[
OT92
]
.

Let us now fix a coefficient field
\mathbb{K}
and consider a
\mathbb{K}
-local system
\mathcal{L}
on
U_{\mathcal{A}}
of rank
r
. For
i\geq 0
an integer, denote by

b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L}):=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})

the corresponding twisted Betti numbers of
U_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then it follows by definition, together with the existence of a minimal CW structure on
U_{\mathcal{A}}
, that (cf. also
[
Coh98
]
for the case
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
)

(1)

b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\leq r\cdot c_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}})=r\cdot b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}}),

where
c_{i}
denotes the number of
i
-cells in a minimal CW structure of
U_{\mathcal{A}}
. Note that for CW complexes which do not admit a minimal structure (e.g., an arbitrary hypersurface arrangement complement) it is difficult in general to compare the leftmost and rightmost terms of (
1
), as both are dominated by the middle term in (
1
).

It is therefore natural to investigate for which local systems the above inequality (
1
) becomes an equality for some
0\leq i\leq n
. For rank-one local systems, this question was answered in full by Liu-Yoshinaga in the case when
\mathcal{A}
is a complexified real arrangement, see
[
LY25
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The main result of this paper settles the question for all complex arrangements, in particular also providing a positive answer to Question 1.3 in
[
LY25
]
. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essential affine hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with complement
U_{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field, and
\mathcal{L}
a nontrivial rank
r

\mathbb{K}
-local system on
U_{\mathcal{A}}
with twisted Betti numbers
b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})
. Then for any
0\leq i\leq n
we have

b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})<r\cdot b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}}).

The following consequence is immediate.

Corollary 1.2
.

If the inequality (
1
) is an equality for some
0\leq i\leq n
, then it is an equality for all
i
in this range, and this can only happen for the constant sheaf.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is by induction on the dimension
n
of the ambient space, by first reducing to the case of central arrangements.

As a byproduct of the proof, we also get the following result.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essential affine hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with complement
U_{\mathcal{A}}
. For a point
x
in one of the hyperplanes of
\mathcal{A}
, let
B_{x}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a sufficiently small ball centered at
x
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be any finite rank
\mathbb{K}
-local system on
U_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Then the pullback homomorphism

H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\to H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x};\mathcal{L})

is surjective. Moreover, we have the following surjective map

(2)

H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\twoheadrightarrow\bigoplus_{x\in L_{0}(\mathcal{A})}H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x};\mathcal{L})

with
L_{0}(\mathcal{A})
denoting the
0
-dimensional flats (or edges) of
\mathcal{A}
.

Remark 1.4
.

When
\mathcal{L}
is the constant sheaf, (
2
) becomes an isomorphism, known as the Brieskorn decomposition. So (
2
) should be viewed as a generalization of Brieskorn decomposition to arbitrary local systems.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we develop the tools necessary for proving our main result Theorem
1.1
(see also the Appendix). For the constructible sheaf calculus, we use the notations from
[
Dim04
,
MS22
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a
\mathbb{K}
-perverse sheaf on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and let
x
be any point on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with inclusion map
i_{x}:\{x\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then the natural homomorphism

H^{0}(\mathbb{C}^{n};\mathcal{P})\to H^{0}(x;i_{x}^{-1}\mathcal{P})

is surjective.

Proof.

Let
j_{x}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{x\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the open embedding. Then we have a distinguished triangle

j_{x!}j_{x}^{-1}\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}\to i_{x*}i_{x}^{-1}\mathcal{P}\xrightarrow{+1}.

Since
j_{x}^{-1}
is
t
-exact and
j_{x!}
is right
t
-exact (e.g., see
[
Dim04
, Theorem 5.2.4]
), it follows that
j_{x!}j_{x}^{-1}\mathcal{P}\in\,^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\mathbb{K})
. Hence, by Artin vanishing (e.g., see
[
MS22
, Theorem 10.3.59]
), we have that

H^{i}(\mathbb{C}^{n};j_{x!}j_{x}^{-1}\mathcal{P})=0

for all
i>0
. Then by the long exact sequence associated to the above triangle, it follows that the homomorphism

H^{0}(\mathbb{C}^{n};\mathcal{P})\to H^{0}(\mathbb{C}^{n};i_{x*}i_{x}^{-1}\mathcal{P})\cong H^{0}(x;i_{x}^{-1}\mathcal{P})

is surjective.
∎

Lemma
2.1
directly implies Proposition
2.2
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Since the inclusion map
j\colon U_{\mathcal{A}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a quasi-finite affine morphism, it follows that
Rj_{*}\mathcal{L}[n]
is a
\mathbb{K}
-perverse sheaf on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(e.g., see
[
Dim04
, Corollary 5.2.17]
).
The first assertion in the statement then follows by applying Lemma
2.1
to
Rj_{*}\mathcal{L}[n]
. The second claim follows by applying Lemma
2.1
to
Rj_{*}\mathcal{L}[n]
upon replacing
x
by the finite set of points
L_{0}(\mathcal{A})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
∎

On the other hand, Lemma
2.1
can be used to prove the following result, which is crucial for the proof of our main Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an affine hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and let
U_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{A}
be its complement. Let
\mathcal{L}
be any finite rank
\mathbb{K}
-local system on
U_{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
x
be a point on one of the hyperplanes of
\mathcal{A}
, and let
B_{x}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a sufficiently small ball centered at
x
. Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a general linear function, and let
c\in\mathbb{C}
be a point different but sufficiently close to
f(x)
(relative to the radius of
B_{x}
).
Then the pullback homomorphism

H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap f^{-1}(c);\mathcal{L})\to H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x}\cap f^{-1}(c);\mathcal{L})

is surjective.

Proof.

Let
j\colon U_{\mathcal{A}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the open embedding, which is a quasi-finite affine morphism. Then, as already explained in the proof of Proposition
2.2
, the complex
\mathcal{F}=Rj_{*}\mathcal{L}[n]
is a perverse sheaf on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Hence, the perverse nearby cycle complex

{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-f(x)}(\mathcal{F})=\psi_{f-f(x)}(\mathcal{F})[-1]

associated to
\mathcal{F}
is a perverse sheaf on
f^{-1}(f(x))
.

Let us first note that by the construction of nearby cycles, we have natural isomorphisms

(3)

H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x}\cap f^{-1}(c);\mathcal{L})\cong H^{-1}(B_{x}\cap f^{-1}(c);\mathcal{F})\cong H^{0}(x;i_{x}^{-1}{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-f(x)}(\mathcal{F})).

Furthermore, there are natural isomorphisms

(4)

H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap f^{-1}(c);\mathcal{L})\cong H^{-1}(f^{-1}(c);\mathcal{F})\cong H^{0}(f^{-1}(f(x));{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-f(x)}(\mathcal{F})),

where the first isomorphism in (
4
) is immediate, while the second isomorphism will be proved in Proposition
A.4
in the Appendix (the main difficulty here being the fact that
f
is not a proper map).
The desired surjectivity in the statement follows from Lemma
2.1
, by using (
3
) and (
4
).
∎

The following result was proved by Cohen for rank one local systems (see
[
Coh98
]
, and also
[
Coh93
]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
H
be a generic hyperplane in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For
\mathcal{L}
a rank
r

\mathbb{K}
-local system on
U_{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})=\begin{cases}r\cdot b_{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}),&{\rm if}\ i=n,\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
H
is generic,
by the Zariski Theorem of Lefschetz type (e.g., see
[
Dim92
, Theorem 1.6.5]
),
the inclusion map
U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H\hookrightarrow U_{\mathcal{A}}
is an
(n-1)
-equivalence.
Then by
[
PS19
, Lemma 5.6]
,
the pullback homomorphism
H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\to H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})
is an isomorphism for
0\leq i\leq n-2
and is injective for
i=n-1
.
By the long exact sequence of the cohomology of the pair
(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H)
,

\cdots\to H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})\to H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\to H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})\to\cdots

we get that
H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})=0
for
i\leq n-1
. On the other hand, since
U_{\mathcal{A}}
(resp.,
U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H
) is affine, it is homotopy equivalent to an
n
(resp.,
n-1
) dimensional CW complex. In particular,
H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})=0
for
i>n
and
H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})=0
for
i>n-1
. By the above relative long exact sequence, we get that
H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})=0
for
i>n
.
Hence
H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})=0
for
i\neq n
and

\begin{split}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})&=|\chi(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})|=|\chi(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})-\chi(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})|\\
&=r\cdot|\chi(U_{\mathcal{A}})-\chi(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H)|=r\cdot|\chi(U_{\mathcal{A}}\setminus H)|\\
&=r\cdot b_{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}),\end{split}

where the last equality is proved in
[
DP03
, Lemma 5]
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
H
be a generic hyperplane in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For a rank
r

\mathbb{K}
-local system
\mathcal{L}
on
U_{\mathcal{A}}
, if

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})=r\cdot b_{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}})
,
then we have a natural isomorphism

(5)

H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\cong H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L}).

Proof.

The long exact sequence for the cohomology of the pair
(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H)
reduces as in
Proposition
2.3
to a
4
-term exact sequence

(6)

0\to H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\to H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})\to H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})\to H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\to 0.

By Proposition
2.3
, our assumption on dimension implies that the surjective homomorphism

H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})\to H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})

is in fact an isomorphism.
Then the claim follows by using the exact sequence (
6
).
∎

3.
Proof of the main result

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We first give the proof of the theorem assuming
i=n
, by induction on
n
in two steps. If
n=1
, the claim is obvious. For induction, we assume that the claim holds for any essential hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
.

Step 1: Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a central arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We reduce the proof to a lower dimension case, then use induction. Assume that the local system
\mathcal{L}
is defined by a homomorphism on
\pi_{1}(U_{\mathcal{A}})
which sends the meridian corresponding to the hyperplane
H_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
to a matrix
A_{i}\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{K})
, for each
i=1,\ldots,d
.

Let
M_{\mathcal{A}}
denote the complement of the
corresponding projective arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
, and consider the Hopf map
p\colon U_{\mathcal{A}}\to M_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Then we have a spectral sequence
with
E_{2}
-tem given by
E_{2}^{a,b}=H^{a}(M_{\mathcal{A}};R^{b}p_{*}\mathcal{L})
, which converges to
H^{a+b}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})
. Since the fiber of the Hopf map
p
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we have that
E_{2}^{a,b}=0
for
b\neq 0,1
.
Following
[
Dim04
, page 210]
, we consider the total turn monodromy operator of the local system
\mathcal{L}
as an invertible operator
T(\mathcal{L}):\mathbb{K}^{r}\to\mathbb{K}^{r}
. This operator plays an important role in describing the local systems
R^{b}p_{*}\mathcal{L}
,
b=0,1
.

If
T(\mathcal{L})\neq I_{r}
is not the identity matrix, it follows from
[
Dim04
, Proposition 6.4.3]
that

R^{1}p_{*}\mathcal{L}
is a local system of rank
0\leq r^{\prime}<r
.
Since
M_{\mathcal{A}}
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional affine variety, it is homotopy equivalent to an
(n-1)
-dimensional CW complex. It follows that for the above spectral sequence we have that
E_{2}^{n-b,b}=0
unless
b=1
and
E_{2}^{a,b}=0
if
a+b>n
. Therefore,

E_{2}^{n-1,1}=E_{\infty}^{n-1,1}\cong H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L}).

Then we have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{n-1}(M_{\mathcal{A}};R^{1}p_{*}\mathcal{L})\leq r^{\prime}\cdot b_{n-1}(M_{\mathcal{A}})=r^{\prime}\cdot b_{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}})<r\cdot b_{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}).

For the equality
b_{n-1}(M_{\mathcal{A}})=b_{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}})
, we apply the Künneth formula to the isomorphism
U_{\mathcal{A}}\cong M_{\mathcal{A}}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

On the other hand,
if
T(\mathcal{L})=I_{r}
is the identity matrix,
there exists a local system
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of rank
r
on
M_{\mathcal{A}}
(constructed as in
[
Dim04
, Proposition 6.4.3]
) such that
\mathcal{L}=p^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Moreover, by the Künneth formula we have the following isomorphism (note that
T(\mathcal{L})
corresponds to the image of the representation defining
\mathcal{L}
on the factor
\mathbb{C}^{*}
)

H^{n-1}(M_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\cong H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})
.

Since
b_{n-1}(M_{\mathcal{A}})=b_{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}})
, and
M_{\mathcal{A}}
can be seen as a complement
to an essential hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
(by setting one of the hyperplanes as the hyperplane at infinity in
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
), the claim follows by induction.

Step 2: Next we prove the claim when the arrangement
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is not necessarily central by reducing to the central case with the same dimension for the ambient space.

Assume by contradiction that the inequality (
1
) is an equality for
i=n
.
If
\mathcal{L}
is not the constant sheaf, without loss of generality, we assume that
A_{1}\neq I_{r}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is essential, there exists an intersection point
x\in L_{0}(\mathcal{A})
contained in
H_{1}
.
Let
B_{x}
be a sufficiently small open ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
centered at
x
. Consider a generic hyperplane
H
sufficiently close to
x
(relative to the radius of
B_{x}
).
Consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}};\mathcal{L})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap H;\mathcal{L})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x};\mathcal{L})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{n-1}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x}\cap H;\mathcal{L}).}

The isomorphism (
5
) implies that the top horizontal arrow is surjective. Moreover, Proposition
2.2
implies that the vertical arrow on the right is surjective.
Therefore, the commutativity of the diagram implies that the bottom horizontal arrow must be surjective.
Then by the relative long exact sequence, the surjectivity of the bottom horizontal map implies that

(7)

H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x}\cap H;\mathcal{L})\to H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x};\mathcal{L})

is an isomorphism.
Since
U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x}
can be viewed as a complement of central hyperplane arrangement, by Proposition
2.3
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x};\mathcal{L})=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x},U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x}\cap H;\mathcal{L})=r\cdot b_{n}(U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x}).

By Step 1 applied to the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
over
U_{\mathcal{A}}\cap B_{x}
, this restriction must be the constant sheaf, which contradicts the assumption that
A_{1}\neq I_{r}
.

We conclude the proof by showing that it can be reduced to the case
i=n
, which was already proved above.
When
i=0
, the claim is obvious. So we assume that
i\geq 1
.
Set
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\cap L
, where
L
is a generic
i
-dimensional affine space in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{B}
is an affine arrangement in
L=\mathbb{C}^{i}
. By the Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{L})\leq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{B}},\mathcal{L})
.
Then we have

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{L})\leq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{i}(U_{\mathcal{B}},\mathcal{L})<r\cdot b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{B}})=r\cdot b_{i}(U_{\mathcal{A}}).

Here the last equality is due to the genericity of
L
and the minimality of CW structures of arrangements, whereas the middle inequality follows from applying the result proved above for
\mathcal{B}
as an arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{i}
and the fact that if
\mathcal{L}
is not the constant sheaf then also
\mathcal{L}|_{U_{\mathcal{B}}}
is not constant.
∎

Appendix

In this appendix, we include a proof of a result well known to experts (Proposition
A.1
and Corollary
A.3
); see, for instance,
[
Tib07
, Section 6.1]
. However, since we were unable to find it in the precise form required for this paper, we provide a complete proof here for the reader’s convenience. We also prove Proposition
A.4
, which is used in the proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Let
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
be the homogeneous coordinates of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, and let
H_{\infty}=\{x_{0}=0\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
be the hyperplane at infinity. Fix a Whitney stratification
\mathbb{CP}^{n}=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}W_{i}
of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{W}
. Let
l=a_{0}x_{0}+a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}
be a linear form, with
a_{i}\neq 0
for some
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
along the axis
V_{l}=\{l=x_{0}=0\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, with exceptional divisor
\mathcal{E}
. Then taking the ratio
l/x_{0}
defines a proper map
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. Let
X=\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{C})
and
E=\mathcal{E}\cap X
. Denote the restriction
\pi|_{X}:X\to\mathbb{C}
by
\pi_{X}
. In other words,
\pi_{X}:X\to\mathbb{C}
is the fiberwise projective compactification of the linear map
l/x_{0}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{CP}^{n}\setminus H_{\infty}
. We denote the composition
X\to\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{CP}^{n}
of the blowup map and inclusion maps by
p
.

Proposition A.1
.

Under the above notations, for a general linear form
l
, the following statements hold.

(1)

The pull-back of the Whitney stratification
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
by
p
defines a Whitney stratification of
X
, which we denote by
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
.

(2)

The map
\pi_{X}\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
does not have critical points along
E
, with respect to the stratification
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Notice that
[x_{0}:l]
defines a rational map
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{1}
and
\mathcal{X}
is equal to the closure of its graph in
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. Moreover,
X
is equal to the intersection of
\mathcal{X}
with
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}
.

Taking cartesian product with
\mathbb{C}
, the Whitney stratification
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
induces a Whitney stratification
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}
. For the first statement, we will show that
X
intersects every stratum of
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}}
transversally, which implies that the restriction of
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}}
to
X
is a Whitney stratification.

Let
t
be the coordinate of
\mathbb{C}
. Then
X\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}
is defined by the equation
l=t\cdot x_{0}
, or

a_{0}x_{0}+a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}-tx_{0}=0

for general
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}}(1)
be the pullback of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(1)
by the projection. Then, via pullback,
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}
define global sections of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}}(1)
. Also via pullback,
t
is a global function of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}
, and hence the product
tx_{0}
is also a global section of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}}(1)
. Notice that the global sections
x_{0},\dots,x_{n},tx_{0}
define a base-point free linear system of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}}(1)
, and
X
is a general member of this linear system. Therefore, by the Bertini theorem for basepoint-free linear systems, the zero locus of a general section is transversal to the Whitney stratification
\mathcal{W}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}}
. Hence the first statement follows.

For the second statement, we need to show that for any
x\in\mathbb{C}
,
(\pi_{X})^{-1}(x)
intersects every stratum of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
transversally along
E
. Notice that

E=V_{l}\times\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}.

Let
\tilde{x}
be any point in
(\pi_{X})^{-1}(x)\cap E
. Then
\tilde{x}
is contained in the line
p(\tilde{x})\times\mathbb{C}
in
E
. Since
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is defined as the pullback of a stratification of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, the line
p(\tilde{x})\times\mathbb{C}
is contained in one stratum of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
. Since the line
p(\tilde{x})\times\mathbb{C}
intersects
(\pi_{X})^{-1}(x)
transversally, the stratum of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
containing
\tilde{x}
also intersects
(\pi_{X})^{-1}(x)
transversally. Therefore, the intersection of
(\pi_{X})^{-1}(x)
and any stratum of
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is transversal along
E
. Thus, the second statement follows.
∎

Remark A.2
.

It is essential to work with
\pi_{X}
instead of
\pi
. In fact, if we replace
X,\pi_{X}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
by
\mathcal{X},\pi
and the pullback of
\mathcal{W}
by the blow-up
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, then the first statement may fail. In fact, let
u,v
be the homogeneous coordinate of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. If we apply the same arguments, then
\mathcal{X}
is a general member of the linear system defined by sections
ux_{0},ux_{1},\dots,ux_{n},vx_{0}
of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{n}}(1)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}}(1)
, which has base locus
\{u=x_{0}=0\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. When restricted to
\mathbb{C}=\{u\neq 0\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, the base locus is removed.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraic constructible complex of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a general affine function, and let
\widetilde{f}\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
be the
fiberwise compactification of
f
. Denote by
k:\mathbb{C}^{n}\hookrightarrow X
the natural embedding.

Corollary A.3
.

For any
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
, the supports of
{{}^{p}\varphi}_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(Rk_{*}(\mathcal{F}))
and
{{}^{p}\varphi}_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(k_{!}(\mathcal{F}))
consist of finitely many points, all of which are contained in
k(\mathbb{C}^{n})\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

First, notice that if
l
is the homogenization of
f
, then
X
is the same variety as defined at the beginning of the appendix, and
\widetilde{f}=\pi_{X}
.
Considering
\mathbb{CP}^{n}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\cup H_{\infty}
, let
\mathcal{W}
be a Whitney stratification of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
such that
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a union of strata and
\mathcal{F}
is constructible with respect to the induced stratification of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
be defined as in Proposition
A.1
. Then
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is a Whitney stratification of
X
with respect to which
Rk_{*}(\mathcal{F})
is constructible. Since the vanishing cycle complex
{{}^{p}\varphi}_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(Rk_{*}(\mathcal{F}))
has support contained in the stratified critical locus of
\widetilde{f}
, using the second statement of Proposition
A.1
it follows that this support is contained in
k(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, i.e., it coincides with the support of
{{}^{p}}\varphi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})
. On the other hand, since
f
is general, the support of
{{}^{p}}\varphi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})
consists of finitely many points in
k(\mathbb{C}^{n})\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
, thus proving the assertion for the vanishing cycles of
Rk_{*}(\mathcal{F})
.
The claim about
{{}^{p}\varphi}_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(k_{!}(\mathcal{F}))
follows similarly, or by using Verdier duality.
∎

We next prove the following result, which is used in Proposition
2.2
.

Proposition A.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a general linear function, and let
c_{0}
be a fixed point in
\mathbb{C}
. Then for any
\mathbb{K}
-constructible complex
\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
there is a natural isomorphism

(8)

H^{-1}(f^{-1}(c);\mathcal{F}|_{f^{-1}(c)})\cong H^{0}(f^{-1}(c_{0});{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})),

for
c\in\mathbb{C}
sufficiently close to
c_{0}
.

Proof.

The isomorphism in (
8
) would be true if
f
were a proper map. Since this is not the case, we need to work with a proper extension
\widetilde{f}
of
f
, and use the fact proved above that
f
does not have “singularities at infinity”. It suffices to assume that
c_{0}
is a stratified critical value of
f
,
the statement being obvious otherwise.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}
be as before the standard partial compactification of the graph of
f
, with projection
\widetilde{f}\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
. Then
\widetilde{f}
is a proper extension of
f
, whose fibers are the projective closures of the fibers of
f
. Let
k:\mathbb{C}^{n}\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion map. Consider the cartesian diagram

\textstyle{f^{-1}(c)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{k_{c}}
\scriptstyle{i_{c}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{k}
\textstyle{\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\widetilde{i}}_{c}}
\textstyle{X.}

Since
c
is a regular value of
f
, there is a base change isomorphism (e.g., see
[
Sch03
, Proposition 4.3.1, Remark 4.3.6]
)

(9)

{{\widetilde{i}}_{c}}^{-1}Rk_{*}\cong R{k_{c}}_{*}i_{c}^{-1}.

We then have the following sequence of natural isomorphisms:

(10)

\begin{split}H^{0}\big({\widetilde{f}}^{-1}(c_{0});{{}^{p}}\psi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(Rk_{*}\mathcal{F})\big)&\cong H^{-1}\big({\widetilde{f}}^{-1}(c);{{\widetilde{i}}_{c}}^{-1}Rk_{*}\mathcal{F}\big)\\
&\overset{\eqref{bc}}{\cong}H^{-1}\big({\widetilde{f}}^{-1}(c);R{k_{c}}_{*}i_{c}^{-1}\mathcal{F}\big)\\
&\cong H^{-1}\big(f^{-1}(c);i_{c}^{-1}\mathcal{F}\big),\end{split}

where the first isomorphism follows from the properness of
\widetilde{f}
and
[
MS22
, Example 10.4.20]
.

To complete the proof of (
8
), in view of (
10
) it suffices to show that there is a natural isomorphism

(11)

H^{0}\big(\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0});{{}^{p}}\psi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(Rk_{*}\mathcal{F})\big)\cong H^{0}\big(f^{-1}(c_{0});{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\big).

In fact, if
k_{c_{0}}\colon f^{-1}(c_{0})\hookrightarrow\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0})
is the inclusion map induced by
k
, there is a natural isomorphism

(12)

H^{0}\big(f^{-1}(c_{0});{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\big)\cong H^{0}\big(\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0});R{k_{c_{0}}}_{*}{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\big).

Moreover, by
[
Sch03
, Remark 4.3.7 (3)]
, there is a base change morphism

(13)

{{}^{p}}\psi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}\circ Rk_{*}\to R{k_{c_{0}}}_{*}\circ{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}

inducing upon taking cohomology the natural map

(14)

H^{0}\big(\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0});{{}^{p}}\psi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(Rk_{*}\mathcal{F})\big)\to H^{0}\big(\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0});R{k_{c_{0}}}_{*}{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\big),

which we will prove to be an isomorphism.
For this, it thus suffices to show that the base change morphism (
13
) induces
the following isomorphism in
D^{b}_{c}(\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0}))
:

(15)

{{}^{p}}\psi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(Rk_{*}\mathcal{F})\cong R{k_{c_{0}}}_{*}{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F}).

After replacing
\mathcal{F}
by its Verdier dual, and using the fact that the perverse nearby cycle functor commutes with Verdier duality, it suffices to show that

(16)

{{}^{p}}\psi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(k_{!}\mathcal{F})\cong{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}{{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F}).

Let
E:=X\setminus\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
\ell:E\hookrightarrow X
denote the closed embedding. Since
f
is a general linear function, it follows from the above Corollary
A.3
that the support of the (perverse) vanishing cycles of
\widetilde{f}
is disjoint from
E
. Hence, we have for the induced closed inclusion
\ell_{c_{0}}:E\cap\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0})\hookrightarrow\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0})
that

(17)

\ell_{c_{0}}^{-1}\big({{}^{p}}\varphi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(k_{!}\mathcal{F})\big)=0.

Therefore, we have

(18)

{{}^{p}}\varphi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(k_{!}\mathcal{F})\cong{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}k_{c_{0}}^{-1}\big({{}^{p}}\varphi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(k_{!}\mathcal{F})\big)\cong{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}\big({{}^{p}}\varphi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\big),

where the first isomorphism follows from (
17
) by using the attaching triangle for
(k_{c_{0}},\ell_{c_{0}})
, while the second isomorphism uses the fact that vanishing cycles commute with open embeddings (cf. also
[
MS22
, Proposition 10.4.19(2)]
).

Consider next the distinguished triangles

{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}\big({{}^{p}}\psi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\big)\to{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}\big({{}^{p}}\varphi_{f-c_{0}}(\mathcal{F})\big)\to{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}i_{c_{0}}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{+1}

and

{{}^{p}}\psi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(k_{!}\mathcal{F})\to{{}^{p}}\varphi_{\widetilde{f}-c_{0}}(k_{!}\mathcal{F})\to\widetilde{i}_{c_{0}}^{-1}(k_{!}\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{+1},

with
i_{c_{0}}:f^{-1}(c_{0})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
{\widetilde{i}}_{c_{0}}:\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0})\hookrightarrow X
the inclusion maps.
Note that there are natural base change morphisms connecting the terms of the first triangle to those of the second, respectively.
By (
18
), it follows that to show (
16
) it suffices to prove the isomorphism

(19)

{{\widetilde{i}}_{c_{0}}}^{-1}k_{!}\mathcal{F}\cong{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}i_{c_{0}}^{-1}\mathcal{F}.

This is immediate, either by using directly the base change formula (e.g., see
[
Bor08
, Chapter V, Proposition 10.7]
), or by noting that, if
{\widetilde{i}}^{E}_{c_{0}}:E\cap\widetilde{f}^{-1}(c_{0})\hookrightarrow E
denotes the inclusion map, then

\ell_{c_{0}}^{-1}{{\widetilde{i}}_{c_{0}}}^{-1}k_{!}\mathcal{F}=({\widetilde{i}}^{E}_{c_{0}})^{-1}\ell^{-1}k_{!}\mathcal{F}=0,

whence, using the attaching triangle for the pair
(k_{c_{0}},\ell_{c_{0}})
yields

{{\widetilde{i}}_{c_{0}}}^{-1}k_{!}\mathcal{F}\cong{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}k_{c_{0}}^{-1}{{\widetilde{i}}_{c_{0}}}^{-1}k_{!}\mathcal{F}\cong{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}i_{c_{0}}^{-1}k^{-1}k_{!}\mathcal{F}\cong{k_{c_{0}}}_{!}i_{c_{0}}^{-1}\mathcal{F}.

This completes the proof of (
15
) and of the Proposition.
∎

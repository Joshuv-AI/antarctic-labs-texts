Kinetically Modified Palatini Inflation Meets ACT Data
Constantinos Pallis School of Technology, Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, Thessaloniki, GR-541 24 GREECE e-mail address: kpallis@auth.gr

Abstract
Abstract: We show that the coexistence of a non-minimal coupling to gravity f ℛ = 1 + c ℛ ​ ϕ n / 2 f_{\cal R}=1+c_{\cal R}\phi^{n/2} with a kinetic mixing of the form f K = f ℛ m f_{\rm K}=f_{\cal R}^{m} – where n = 2 n=2 and 4 4 and 0.5 ≤ m ≤ 10 0.5\leq m\leq 10 – reconciles chaotic inflation based on the ϕ n \phi^{n} potential with the recent ACT results, if we adopt the Palatini formulation of gravity. The attainment of inflation allows for subplanckian inflaton values and energy scales below the cut-off scale of the corresponding effective theory. The model can be also embedded in supergravity by introducing two chiral superfields and a monomial superpotential, linear with respect to the inflaton-accompanying field. Its stabilization is achieved thanks to a compact contribution to the Kähler potential, whose the inflationary part includes an holomorphic logarithmic term and a real one multiplying a shift-symmetric quadratic polynomial term. II
Set-up

At the non-SUSY level, our proposal can be formulated in the

Jordan frame
(
JF
) where the action of
\phi
is
given by

{\sf S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(-\frac{f_{\cal R}}{2}\mathcal{R}+\frac{f_{\rm K}}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-V_{\rm I}(\phi)\right).

(5)

Here
\mathfrak{g}
is the determinant of the background
Friedmann-Robertson-Walker metric,
g^{\mu\nu}
with signature

(+,-,-,-)
and we allow for a kinetic mixing through the
function
f_{\rm K}(\phi)
which is conveniently parameterized in terms
of
f_{\cal R}
as follows

f_{\rm K}(\phi)=f_{\cal R}^{m}~~\mbox{where}~~1/2\leq m\leq 10

(6)

is an indicative interval for
m
which assures an ample variation
of
f_{\rm K}
. Indeed, any value of
f_{\rm K}
can be revealed by the
parametrization of Eq. (
6
) selecting conveniently

m=\ln f_{\rm K}/\ln f_{\cal R}
. The considered
m
margin can be characterized
as natural, since the values are of order unity, and allows us to
overlaid almost the whole observationally favored region, as we
show in Sec.
V
. Contrary to the models in Ref.
[
23
]
–
with which ours share the same name – we do not violate the
quadratic form of the kinetic operator
\dot{}\phi^{2}
for

\mu=\nu=0
. By performing a conformal transformation
[
26
]
,
according to which we define the
Einstein frame
(
EF
) metric
\widehat{g}_{\mu\nu}=f_{\cal R}\,g_{\mu\nu}
, we can write
{\sf S}
in the EF as follows

{\sf S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\widehat{\phi}\partial_{\nu}\widehat{\phi}-\widehat{V}_{\rm I}(\widehat{\phi})\right),

(7)

where hat is used to denote quantities defined in the EF. Given
that the metric tensor and the affine connection are treated as
independent variables in the Palatini formalism, the EF
canonically normalized field,
\widehat{\phi}
, and potential,
\widehat{V}_{\rm I}
, are
defined as follows

\frac{d\widehat{\phi}}{d\phi}=J=\frac{f_{\rm K}^{1/2}}{f_{\cal R}^{1/2}}=f_{\cal R}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}~~~\mbox{and}~~~\widehat{V}_{\rm I}=\frac{V_{\rm I}}{f_{\cal R}^{2}}=\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi^{n}}{2^{n/2}f_{\cal R}^{2}}.

(8)

From the expressions in Eq. (
8
) it is clear that
f_{\rm K}
through

m
determines largely the form of
\widehat{\phi}(\phi)
whereas
f_{\cal R}

influences exclusively the curvature of
\widehat{V}_{\rm I}
. Therefore, our
scheme is expected to be observationally more flexible compared to
the traditional (metric or Palatini) nMI where
m=0
.

Effective Cut-Off Scale

It is well known
[

that the absence of an extra term
in the leftmost expression in Eq. (

[

To this end we follow the most restrictive approach analyzing the
small-field behavior of our models in the EF – cf.
Ref.
[

,
50
,
51
,
27
]
. We focus on the second term in the
right-hand side of Eq. (
7

28a
), see
Sec.
VI
below – for

and we expand it about

in terms of
\widehat{\phi}
– see Eq. (
8
) – which
coincides with
\phi
since
\langle{J}\rangle=1
. Our result for some
m

values can be written as

J^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}=\dot{}\phi^{2}\begin{cases}1&\mbox{for}~~m=1,\\
1+c_{\cal R}\phi^{n/2}&\mbox{for}~~m=2,\\
\left(1+2c_{\cal R}\phi^{n/2}+c_{\cal R}^{2}\phi^{n}+\cdots\right)&\mbox{for}~~m=3.\end{cases}

Similar expressions can be obtained for the other
m
’s too.
Expanding similarly
\widehat{V}_{\rm I}
, see Eq. (
8
), in terms of
\phi
we
have

\widehat{V}_{\rm I}=\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi^{n}}{2^{n/2}}\left(1-2c_{\cal R}\phi^{\frac{n}{2}}+3c_{\cal R}^{2}\phi^{n}-4c_{\cal R}^{3}\phi^{\frac{3n}{2}}+\cdots\right)

Our result is in accordance with Ref.
[

,

52

53

for the

(22)

If we take into account Eq. (
17
), the condition above yields an

for every selected
n
and

. As we show in

, there is sizable parameter space consistent with this

more elaborated derivation of

[
49
]
for

and

VI
Supergravity Embedding

Starting from the EF action in SUGRA we attempt to reproduce the
ingredients of our model in Eq. (
8
) without caring if the
complete JF SUGRA action is consistent with Eq. (
5
). This
task is not realized up to now, to our knowledge, for the
Palatini formalism.

Adopting the strategy of Ref.
[
45
,
47
,
55
]
we consider two
gauge-singlet chiral superfields, i.e.,
z^{\alpha}=\Phi,S
, with

\Phi
(
{\alpha}=1
) and
S
(
{\alpha}=2)
being the inflaton and a
“stabilizer” field respectively. The EF action for
z^{\alpha}
’s
within SUGRA can be written as

\hskip-5.69054pt{\sf S}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}+K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}\widehat{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}z^{\alpha}\partial_{\nu}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}-\widehat{V}_{\rm F}\right)

(28a)

where summation is taken over the scalar fields
z^{\alpha}
, star
(
∗
) denotes complex conjugation,
K
is the Kähler potential with

K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}=K_{,z^{\alpha}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}}
and

K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}K_{{\bar{\beta}}\gamma}=\delta^{\alpha}_{\gamma}
. Also
\widehat{V}_{\rm F}
is
the EF F–term SUGRA potential given by

\widehat{V}_{\rm F}=e^{K}\left(K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}(D_{\alpha}W)(D^{*}_{\bar{\beta}}W^{*})-3{|W|^{2}}\right),

(28b)

where
D_{\alpha}W=W_{,z^{\alpha}}+K_{,z^{\alpha}}W
with
W
being the
superpotential. Therefore, the desired SUGRA embedding of our
models requires the determination of
K
and
W
.

Our task can be facilitated, if we express
\Phi
and
S

according to the parametrization

\Phi=\>{\phi\,e^{i{\theta}}}/{\sqrt{2}}\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>S=\>(s+i\bar{s})/\sqrt{2}\,,

(29)

and determine the inflationary track by the constraints

\langle{S}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{\Phi-\Phi^{*}}\rangle_{\rm I}=0,~\mbox{or}~~\langle{s}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{\bar{s}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{{\theta}}\rangle_{\rm I}=0,

(30)

where the symbol
\langle{Q}\rangle_{\rm I}
stands for the value of a quantity
Q

during nMI.
V_{\rm I}
in Eq. (
2
) can be produced, in the flat
limit, by

W=\lambda S\Phi^{n/2}~~\mbox{since}~~\langle{|W_{,S}|^{2}}\rangle_{\rm I}=V_{\rm I}.

(31)

The form of
W
can be uniquely determined if we impose an
R

symmetry, under which
S
and
\Phi
have charges
1
and
0
, and
a global
U(1)
symmetry with assigned charges to
S
and
\Phi

-1
and
2/n
. The latter is violated, though, in the proposed

K
which is judiciously chosen so that
J
and
\widehat{V}_{\rm I}
in
Eq. (
8
) are reproduced. More specifically,
\widehat{V}_{\rm I}
has to be
derived from the only surviving term in Eq. (
28b
) along the
track in Eq. (
30
), which is

\langle{\widehat{V}_{\rm F}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{e^{K}K^{SS^{*}}\,|W_{,S}|^{2}}\rangle_{\rm I}\,.

(32)

The proposed
K
includes two contributions without mixing between

\Phi
and
S
, i.e.,

K=K_{1}+K_{2},

(33)

from which
K_{2}
successfully stabilizes
S
along the path in
Eq. (
30
) without invoking higher order terms – cf.
Ref.
[
27
]
. We adopt the form
[
47
]

K_{2}=N_{2}\ln\left(1+{|S|^{2}/N_{2}}\right)~~\mbox{with}~~0<N_{2}<6

(34)

which parameterizes
[
47
]
the compact manifold
SU(2)/U(1)

with curvature
2/N_{2}
. On the other hand,
K_{1}
depends on
\Phi

and has to incorporate
f_{\cal R}
and
f_{\rm K}
in Eqs. (
1
) and (
6
). To this
end we introduce the functions

F_{\cal R}(\Phi)=1+2^{\frac{n}{4}}c_{\cal R}\Phi^{\frac{n}{2}}~~\mbox{and}~~F_{\rm sh}=-\frac{1}{2}(\Phi-\Phi^{*})^{2},

(35)

from which
F_{\cal R}
is holomorphic, reducing to
f_{\cal R}
along the path
in Eq. (
30
), whereas
F_{\rm sh}
is real, assisting us to
incorporate the non-canonical kinetic mixing in Eq. (
8
) – cf.
Ref.
[
27
]
. In particular,

\langle{F_{\cal R}}\rangle_{\rm I}=f_{\cal R},~\langle{F_{\rm sh}}\rangle_{\rm I}=0~~\mbox{with}~~F_{\rm sh,\Phi\Phi^{*}}=1.

(36)

Therefore,
F_{\cal R}
can be used to derive the denominator of
\widehat{V}_{\rm I}
in
Eq. (
8
) whereas the shift-symmetric
F_{\rm sh}
, multiplied by a
real function of
\Phi
, lets intact
\langle{\widehat{V}_{\rm F}}\rangle_{\rm I}
but it may
contribute to the normalization of
\phi
. The relevant functions

f_{\rm K}
and
J
can be found in terms of
F_{\cal R}
during nMI as follows

f_{\rm K}=\langle{(F_{\cal R}+F_{\cal R}^{*})/2}\rangle_{\rm I}^{m}~~\mbox{and}~~J^{2}=\langle{(F_{\cal R}+F_{\cal R}^{*})/2}\rangle_{\rm I}^{m-1}.

(37)

Employing the ingredients above, a simple, suitable form of
K_{1}

in Eq. (
33
) is

K_{1}=-\ln F_{\cal R}-\ln F_{\cal R}^{*}+\left(\frac{F_{\cal R}+F_{\cal R}^{*}}{2}\right)^{m-1}F_{\rm sh}.

(38)

It is worth noticing that contrary to the embedding of metric nMI
in SUGRA – cf. Ref.
[
27
]
–,
F_{\cal R}
and
F_{\cal R}^{*}
enter two
different terms in the right-hand side of Eq. (
38
) and so they
give zero contribution into
K_{\Phi\Phi^{*}}
. Note that for
m=1
,

F_{\rm sh}
and
F_{\cal R}
are totally decoupled. If, in addition, we take

n=4
then
K
is totaly quadratic. Therefore, this is the most
well-motivated case from the point of view of SUGRA. Mild (of
order
0.1
) variation of the prefactors of the logarithms is
expected to have some impact on the observational results – cf.
Ref.
[
28
,
19
]
– at the cost of some tuning, though, that we
avoid in our current investigation.

The appropriateness of
K
in Eqs. (
33
), (
34
) and (
38
) can be verified
if we insert it into Eq. (
32
) and prove

\langle{K_{\Phi\Phi^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=J^{2}~~\mbox{and}~~\langle{\widehat{V}_{\rm F}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\widehat{V}_{\rm I},

(39)

where
J
and
\widehat{V}_{\rm I}
are given in Eq. (
8
). Indeed, the first of
the expressions above is derived if we take into account that

\langle{K_{\Phi\Phi^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{K_{1\Phi\Phi^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=J^{2}\langle{F_{\rm sh,\Phi\Phi^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=J^{2}

(40a)

– see Eq. (
36
) – whereas the second one, if we consider
Eq. (
31
) and notice that

\langle{e^{K}}\rangle_{\rm I}=f_{\cal R}^{-2}~~\mbox{and}~~\langle{K_{SS^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{K^{SS^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=1.

(40b)

Consequently, both expressions in Eq. (
8
) can be recovered from
the proposed
W
and
K
in Eqs. (
31
) and (
33
) via Eq. (
39
).

VII
Conclusions

Prompted by the
P-ACT-LB-BK18
data
[
10
]
which is just marginally
compatible with the conventional (metric or Palatini) nMI

[
26
,
44
,
14
]
, we proposed a variant within Palatini
gravity which assures comfortable consistency with the current
observations.

Namely, working along the lines of Ref.
[
27
]
, we considered a
non-canonical kinetic mixing, Eq. (
6
), – involving the exponent

m
– in the inflaton sector, apart from the non-minimal
coupling to gravity, Eq. (
1
), which is associated with the
potential in Eq. (
2
). Confining
m
to the range

(0.87~[0.5]-10)
for
n=2
[
n=4
] we achieved observational
predictions which offer a nice covering of the present data on

n_{\rm s}
– its compatibility especially with the
n=4
case for low

m
values is really impressive, as shown in Fig.
1
. Part of
the resulting
r
values there could be accessible in the near
future. Our solutions can be attained with subplanckian values of
the inflaton, requiring large
c_{\cal R}
’s, and without causing serious
problems with the perturbative unitarity since the
unitarity-violation scale remains well above the inflationary one.
It is gratifying, in addition, that a sizable fraction of the
allowed parameter space of our models (with
c_{\cal R}\gg 1
) can be
studied analytically.

Our setting can be elegantly implemented in SUGRA too, employing
the super- and Kähler potentials given in Eqs. (
31
) and (
33
) with individual
contributions in Eqs. (
34
) and (
38
). The non-minimal coupling of the
inflaton to gravity is generated by an holomorphic logarithmic
contribution into
K
whereas its kinetic mixing is expressed by a
real function multiplying a shift-symmetric quadratic polynomial
term. Our construction can be extended for a gauge non-singlet
Higgs superfield as done in Ref.
[
25
,
19
]
for the metric
formulation.

One possible shortcoming of our proposal is that it employs one
extra parameter (
m
) compared to the original model of nMI
reducing, thereby, its predictability. As another consequence,
however, the enhancement of
n_{\rm s}
for
n=2
with
m>0
may be more
drastic than that presented in Ref.
[
21
,
11
]
. Moreover,
within our scheme, the reheating phase is not constrained as,
e.g., in Ref.
[
40
,
42
,
6
,
35
,
41
]
and corrections to the
inflationary potential from other sectors of the theory are not
required as, e.g., in Ref.
[
7
,
30
,
56
]
.

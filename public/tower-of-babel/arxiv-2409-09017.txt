Invariant Metrics on Nilpotent Lie algebras
R. García-Delgado

Abstract
. We state criteria for a nilpotent Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} to admit an invariant metric. We use that 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} possesses two canonical abelian ideals 𝔦 ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) ⊂ 𝔍 ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) \mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g}) to decompose the underlying vector space of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} and then we state sufficient conditions for 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} to admit an invariant metric. The properties of the ideal 𝔍 ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) \mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g}) allows to prove that if a current Lie algebra 𝔤 ⊗ 𝒮 \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S} admits an invariant metric, then there must be an invariant and non-degenerate bilinear map from 𝒮 × 𝒮 \mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S} into the space of centroids of 𝔤 / 𝔍 ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) \mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g}) . We also prove that in any nilpotent Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} there exists a non-zero, symmetric and invariant bilinear form. This bilinear form allows to reconstruct 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} by means of an algebra with unit. We prove that this algebra is simple if and only if the bilinear form is an invariant metric on 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} .

Introduction

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
be a Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
with bracket
[\,\cdot,\,\cdot\,]
. The Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
is said to be
quadratic
if it is equipped with a non-degenerate, symmetric and bilinear form
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
, which is
invariant
under the adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
, that is:
B([x,y],z)=-B(y,[x,z])
, for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{g}
. A quadratic Lie algebra is denoted by
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
and
B
is called an
invariant metric
.

A semisimple Lie algebra is quadratic for the Killing form is an invariant metric defined on it. A reductive Lie algebra is an example of a non-semisimple quadratic Lie algebra; another more elaborated example is as follows: Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
be a Lie algebra and in the vector space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
define the skey-symmetric and bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\prime}
by
[x+\alpha,y+\beta]^{\prime}=[x,y]+\operatorname{ad}(x)^{\ast}(\beta)-\operatorname{ad}(y)^{\ast}(\alpha)
, and the symmetric bilinear form
B
by
B(x+\alpha,y+\beta)=\alpha(y)+\beta(x)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
\alpha,\beta
in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
. The triple
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\ast},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]^{\prime},B)
is a non-semisimple quadratic Lie algebra, as
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is an abelian ideal.

The algebraic study of quadratic non-semisimple Lie algebras can be traced on to the work of V. Kac (see
[
8
]
), where appears an inductive way to construct solvable quadratic Lie algebras and it is also shown there that any solvable quadratic Lie algebra can be identified with this construction. In
[
4
]
, it is stated the conditions for which these type of quadratic Lie algebras are isomorphic. In
[
9
]
, A. Medina and P. Revoy generalizes the results of V. Kac to any indecomposable, non-semisimple and quadratic Lie algebra; the proof of these results depends
on the choice of a minimal ideal. In
[
7
]
, I. Kath and M. Olbrich propose an alternative approach by using canonical ideals to obtain classification results.

If the Killing form is non-degenerate then the Lie algebra is semisimple and therefore, is quadratic. For nilpotent Lie algebras, the Killing form is zero, so this bilinear form can not help us to determine whether a nilpotent Lie algebra is quadratic. One of the aims of this work is to provide criteria under which a nilpotent Lie algebra is quadratic. Although much has been said about nilpotent quadratic Lie algebras, we hope that these results – which are based on classical tools and well-known results –, can take a step forward in obtaining a criterion that determines conditions for which an arbitrary Lie algebra is quadratic.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra then there are canonical abelian ideals
\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})]\subset\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})
. Further, if
\mathfrak{g}
admits an invariant metric then
\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
(see
Lemma 4.2
in
[
7
]
and
Lemma
1.3
below). If
\mathfrak{h}
is a subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
, we prove that a necessary condition for a
current Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
to admit an invariant metric –where
\mathcal{S}
is a commutative and associtive algebra with unit–, is that there must be a bilinear map
\epsilon:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\operatorname{Cent}(\mathfrak{h})
with the same properties as that of an invariant metric: symmetric, invariant and non-degenerate (see
Thm.
3.1
). In
[
10
]
it is proved that if
\mathfrak{g}
is simple then
\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric. In
Cor. 1.3
of
[
5
]
this result is generalized to the case when
\mathfrak{g}
is indecomposable. The difference of the result obtained here is that the image of the bilinear map
\epsilon
is contained in
\operatorname{Cent}(\mathfrak{h})
, even if
\mathfrak{h}
does not admit an invariant metric and
\mathcal{S}
is not necessarily finite dimensional. In addition, in
[
5
]
it is assumed that the ground field
\mathbb{F}
is algebraically closed.

In §1 we start by giving a review of the theory of abelian extensions of Lie algebras, as well as some standard results that we need in the sequel. Given an arbitrary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
and a vector space
\mathfrak{a}
, in
Prop.
1.1
we give a method to construct a Lie algebra in the space
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
. In
Prop.
2.1
we prove that any non-abelian nilpotent quadratic Lie algebra can be constructed in this way. In
Prop.
2.3
and
Prop.
2.4
we give sufficient conditions for that such a Lie algebra to admit an invariant metric. In §3 we prove that if a current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric, where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is nilpotent and
\mathfrak{h}
is non-abelian, then there must be a non-degenerate bilinear map
\epsilon:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\operatorname{Cent}(\mathfrak{h})
such that
\epsilon(s,t)=\epsilon(st,1)
, for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
(see
Thm.
3.1
). In §4 we prove that any nilpotent Lie algebra has a non-zero, symmetric and invariant bilinear form. Using this bilinear form we construct an algebra with unit from which we can recover the original Lie algebra structure, and then we prove that this algebra is simple if and only if the bilinear form is an invariant metric (see
Thm.
4.1
). All the algebras and vector spaces considered in this work are finite dimensional over a unique field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic.

\mathfrak{i}
. Then
\Psi
is an isomorphism of Lie algebras.
∎

We denote by
Z(\mathfrak{g})
the center of
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
. The
derived central series

Z_{1}(\mathfrak{g})\subset Z_{2}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\cdots\subset Z_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\cdots
is defined by
Z_{1}(\mathfrak{g})=Z(\mathfrak{g})
and
Z_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g})=\pi_{\ell-1}^{-1}\left(Z\left(\mathfrak{g}/Z_{\ell-1}(\mathfrak{g})\right)\right)
, where
\ell>1
and
\pi_{\ell-1}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/Z_{\ell-1}(\mathfrak{g})
is the canonical projection. The
descending central series

\mathfrak{g}^{0}\supset\mathfrak{g}^{1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak{g}^{\ell}\supset\cdots
is defined by:
\mathfrak{g}^{0}=\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\ell}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{\ell-1}]
, where
\ell>1
.

The following result defines
the ideals
\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
, for a nilpotent Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
. This result is a particular case of the one given in
Lemma 4.2
of
[
7
]
and its proof can be consulted there.

1.3 Lemma
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
be a finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
. Let

\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}Z_{k}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{g}^{k}\ \quad\text{and}\quad\ \mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(Z_{k}(\mathfrak{g})+\mathfrak{g}^{k}).

Then,

(i)

\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
.

(ii)
There exists
m\geq 1
such that:
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})=Z(\mathfrak{g})+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}Z_{k+1}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{g}^{k}}
.

(iii)

\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
is abelian and
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})]\subset\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})
.

(iv)
If
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
admits an invariant metric then
\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
.

2.
Quadratic Lie Algebras with abelian ideals
\mathfrak{i}
and
\mathfrak{J}

For a bilinear form
B
on a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, let
B^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
be the map defined by
B^{\flat}(x)(y)=B(x,y)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

What follows can be applied to any quadratic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
possessing abelian ideals
\mathfrak{i}=\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\mathfrak{i}\subset\mathfrak{J}
,
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{i}^{\perp}
and
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{J}]\subset\mathfrak{i}
. For example, if
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent and non-abelian, then
Z(\mathfrak{g})\neq\{0\}
, as
\mathfrak{g}
has a one-dimensional ideal which is contained in
Z(\mathfrak{g})
. By
Lemma 3.3
of
[
3
]
,
Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\neq\{0\}
, and by
Lemma
1.3
,
Z(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{J}
, and
Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subset\mathfrak{i}
. In addition,
\mathfrak{i}
and
\mathfrak{J}
are abelians.

By
Prop.
1.1
the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
can be written as:
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{i}
, where
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{i}
. Due to
\mathfrak{i}
is isotropic, by the Witt-decomposition we consider
\mathfrak{h}
as an isotropic subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{i}
. In addition,
B
restricted to
\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}
is non-degenerate; we denote by
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
the restriction
B\mid_{\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}}
.

From
Prop.
1.1
, there are a Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{h}}
in
\mathfrak{h}
, a representation
\rho:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{i})
, a 1-cocycle
\varphi
in
C(\mathfrak{h};\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a};\mathfrak{i}))
associated to
\bar{\rho}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a};\mathfrak{i}))
and a 2-cocycle
(\lambda,\mu)
in
C(\mathfrak{h};\mathfrak{a})\oplus C(\mathfrak{h};\mathfrak{i})
associated to the representation
R=(\varphi,\rho):\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{J})
, such that:

[x,y]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\lambda(x,y)+\mu(x,y),\quad[x,u+\alpha]=\varphi(x)(u)+\rho(x)(\alpha),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{h}
,
u
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})
; then
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])=\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\rho)
. In addition, due to
B
is non-degenerate and invariant, the map

(12)

\phi:\mathfrak{i}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{\ast},\quad\alpha\mapsto B^{\flat}(\alpha)\mid_{\mathfrak{h}}

is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{h}
-modules, that is
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}(x)\circ\phi=\phi\circ\rho(x)
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{i}
, the invariant metric
B
takes the form:

(13)

B(x+u+\alpha,y+v+\beta)=\phi(\alpha)(y)+\phi(\beta)(x)+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(u,v),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{h}
,
u,v
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
\alpha,\beta
in
\mathfrak{i}
. Using that
B
is invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
we obtain:
B(\varphi(x)(u),y)\!=\!B([x,u],y)\!=\!-B(u,[x,y])\!=\!-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda(x,y),u)
. Then

(14)

\phi(\varphi(x)(u))(y)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\flat}\left(\lambda(x,y)\right)(u),\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{h},\,\text{ and }u\in\mathfrak{a}.

Similarly, using that
B
is invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
, for
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
we get:

(15)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\phi(\mu(x,y))(z)&=B(\mu(x,y),z)=B([x,y],z)\\
&=B(x,[y,z])=B(x,\mu(y,z))=\phi(\mu(y,z))(x).\end{array}

Let
\varphi^{\prime}:\mathfrak{h}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a};\mathfrak{h}^{\ast})
be the map defined by
\varphi^{\prime}(x)=\phi\circ\varphi(x)
, for all
x
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Thus, the map
x+u+\alpha\mapsto x+u+\phi(\alpha)
, is an isomorphism between
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\rho)
and
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\phi\circ\mu,\varphi^{\prime},\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
. Further, let
B^{\prime}
be the symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
defined by:

(16)

B^{\prime}(x+u+\alpha^{\prime},y+v+\beta^{\prime})=\alpha^{\prime}(y)+\beta^{\prime}(x)+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(u,v),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{h}
,
u,v
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
. Then
B^{\prime}
is an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\phi\circ\mu,\varphi^{\prime},\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
, making it into a quadratic Lie algebra isometric to
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
(see (
13
) and (
16
)). If we make the assumptions:
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi
,
\phi\circ\mu
, and
\rho=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, induced by the isomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{i}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
(see (
12
)), then by (
14
) and (
15
),
\varphi
and
\mu
satisfy:

(17)

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathfrak{h}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a};\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}),

\displaystyle\quad\varphi(x)(u)(y)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda(x,y),u),\,\text{ and }

(18)

\displaystyle\mu:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\ast},

\displaystyle\quad\mu(x,y)(z)=\mu(y,z)(x),

for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
u
in
\mathfrak{a}
.

2.1 Proposition
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
be a Lie algebra in the sense of
Prop.
1.1
, in the vector space
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
. Let
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
be a symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{a}
. The symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form
B
on
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
defined by:

(19)

B(x+u+\alpha,y+v+\beta)=\alpha(x)+\beta(x)+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(u,v)

is invariant if and only if the conditions in (
17
) and (
18
) holds. In addition, any nilpotent non-abelian quadratic Lie algebra can be identified with this construction.

If one are looking for conditions on
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\rho)
to admit an invariant metric, then by the isomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{i}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
in (
12
), we may assume that
\mathfrak{i}
is equal to
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
and
\rho
is equal to
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. The condition (
18
) imposes a restriction on
\mu
that is easier to obtain than the one in (
17
). We fix a basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
of
\mathfrak{h}
and we consider its dual basis
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, that is
\alpha_{i}(x_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. Let
\eta_{ijk}
be in
\mathbb{F}
such that:

\eta_{ijk}=\eta_{jki}=-\eta_{jik},\quad\text{ for all }1\leq i,j,,k\leq r.

Let
\mu:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
be the bilinear map defined by
\mu(x_{j},x_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\eta_{ijk}\alpha_{i}
, then
\mu
is skew-symmetric and
\mu(x_{j},x_{k})(x_{i})=\eta_{ijk}=\eta_{jki}=\mu(x_{k},x_{i})(x_{j})
, which proves that
\mu
satisfies (
18
). Therefore, we restrict to Lie algebras of the type
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
where
\mu(x,y)(z)=\mu(y,z)(x)
for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
.

Let
B_{\mathfrak{a}}:\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{F}
be a symmetric, non-degenerate and bilinear form on
\mathfrak{a}
. The condition in (
17
) is necessary if one wants to find an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
. In the next result, we will show that we can associate to
\varphi
, a bilinear map
\lambda_{\varphi}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
, such that
\varphi(x)(u)(y)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y),u)
, regardless of whether
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
admits an invariant metric.

2.2 Proposition
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
be a Lie algebra, in the sense of
Prop.
1.1
. Let
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
be a non-degenerate, symmetric and bilinear form on
\mathfrak{a}
. Then, there exists a bilinear map
\lambda_{\varphi}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
, such that
\varphi(x)(u)(y)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y),u)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
u
in
\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is a linear map between
\mathfrak{h}
and
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a};\mathfrak{h}^{\ast})
, for each pair
x,y
in
\mathfrak{h}
, let us consider the map
T(x,y):\mathfrak{a}\to\mathbb{F}
, defined by:

T(x,y)(u)=-\varphi(x)(u)(y),\quad\text{ for all }u\in\mathfrak{a}.

As
\varphi:\mathfrak{h}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a};\mathfrak{h}^{\ast})
is linear, then
T(x,y)
is linear and belongs to
\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
. The map
B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\flat}:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
is bijective, for
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate. Then there exists a unique element
\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y)
in
\mathfrak{a}
, such that
T(x,y)=B_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\flat}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y))
, that is:
\varphi(x)(u)(y)=-T(x,y)(u)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y),u)
, for all
u
in
\mathfrak{a}
. Let
\lambda_{\varphi}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
be the map defined by
(x,y)\mapsto\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y)
, which is bilinear because
\varphi
is linear and
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
is non-degenerate.
∎

If
\lambda_{\varphi}=\lambda
and
\mu
satisfies (
18
), by
Prop.
2.1
,
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
admits an invariant metric. If
\lambda_{\varphi}\neq\lambda
but
\lambda_{\varphi}-\lambda
is a coboundary,
we can use
Prop.
1.2
to obtain
the following criteria that determine sufficient conditions on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}),
to admit an invariant metric.

2.3 Proposition
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{h}})
be a Lie algebra in the sense of
Prop.
1.1
such that
\mu(x,y)(z)=\mu(y,z)(x)
for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Let
\lambda_{\varphi}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
be the map of
Prop.
2.2
and
e_{\varphi}:C(\mathfrak{h};\mathfrak{a})\to C(\mathfrak{h};\mathfrak{h}^{\ast})
be the map of (
8
). If there exists a linear map
L\!:\!\mathfrak{h}\!\to\!\mathfrak{a}
such that:

\textbf{(i)}\,\,\,e_{\varphi}(L)=0,\quad\text{ and }\quad\textbf{(ii)}\,\,\,\lambda_{\varphi}=\lambda+\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L).

Then
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
admits an invariant metric.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{d}_{R}(\lambda,\mu)=0
,
e_{\varphi}\circ\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}=-\operatorname{d}_{\rho}\circ e_{\varphi}
and
e_{\varphi}(L)=0
, then
\operatorname{d}_{R}(\lambda+\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L),\mu)=\operatorname{d}_{R}(\lambda_{\varphi},\mu)=0
, and by
Prop.
1.1
,
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda_{\varphi},\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
is a Lie algebra in
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
. Due to
(\lambda+\operatorname{d}(L),\mu)=(\lambda,\mu)+\operatorname{d}_{R}(L,0)
, by
Prop.
1.2
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\mathfrak{h}})
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda_{\varphi}),\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Let
B
be the symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
defined in (
19
). As
\varphi(x)(u)(y)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y),u)
, then (
17
)) holds true. Due to
\mu
satisfies (
18
), by
Prop.
2.1
, the bilinear form
B
in (
19
) is an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda+\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L),\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\mathfrak{h}})
, and therefore
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\mathfrak{h}})
admits an invariant metric.
∎

2.4 Proposition
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{h}})
be a Lie algebra in the sense of
Prop.
1.1
such that
\mu(x,y)(z)=\mu(y,z)(x)
for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Let
\lambda_{\varphi}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
be the map of
Prop.
2.2
and
B_{\mathfrak{a}}
be a symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{a}
. If there exists a linear map
L:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
such that:

\textbf{(i)}\,\,\lambda_{\varphi}=\lambda+\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L),\,\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\textbf{(ii)}\,\,B_{\mathfrak{a}}({\lambda_{\varphi}}(y,z),L(x))=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y),L(z)),

for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Then
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{h}})
admits an invariant metric.

Proof.

Let
\mu^{\prime}=\mu+e_{\varphi}(L)
. We claim that
\mu^{\prime}(x,y)(z)=\mu^{\prime}(y,z)(x)
, for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
. Since
\mu
satisfies the cyclic condition (
18
), then
\mu^{\prime}(x,y)(z)=\mu^{\prime}(y,z)(x)
if and only if
e_{\varphi}(L)(x,y)(z)=e_{\varphi}(L)(y,z)(x)
. By definition of
e_{\varphi}
(see (
8
)), we have:

(20)

\begin{split}&e_{\varphi}(L)(x,y)(z)=\varphi(x)(L(y))(z)-\varphi(y)(L(x))(z),\,\text{ and }\\
&e_{\varphi}(L)(y,z)(x)=\varphi(y)(L(z))(x)-\varphi(z)(L(y))(x).\end{split}

By
Prop.
2.2
, the equations in (
20
) can be written as:

(21)

\begin{split}&e_{\varphi}(L)(x,y)(z)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,z),L(y))+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(y,z),L(x)),\,\text{ and }\\
&e_{\varphi}(L)(y,z)(x)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(y,x),L(z))+B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(z,x),L(y)).\end{split}

Since
\lambda_{\varphi}=\lambda+\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L)
, then
\lambda_{\varphi}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
is skew-symmetric. From (
21
) we deduce that
e_{\varphi}(L)(x,y)(z)=e_{\varphi}(L)(y,z)(x)
follows from
B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(y,z),L(x))=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y),L(z))
, which is the hypothesis in
(ii)
. Then
\mu^{\prime}(x,y)(z)=\mu^{\prime}(y,z)(x)
, for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
.
Observe that:

(22)

(\lambda_{\varphi},\mu^{\prime})=(\lambda+\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L),\mu+e_{\varphi}(L))=(\lambda,\mu)+\operatorname{d}_{R}(L,0),

then
\operatorname{d}_{R}(\lambda_{\varphi},\mu^{\prime})=0
. Thus, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda_{\varphi},\mu^{\prime},\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
satisfies:

\varphi(x)(u)(y)=-B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x,y),u),\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu^{\prime}(x,y)(z)=\mu^{\prime}(y,z)(x),

The symmetric and non-degenerate bilinear form
B
defined by (
19
), is an invariant metric on
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda_{\varphi},\mu^{\prime},\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
. By
Prop.
1.2
and (
22
),
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda_{\varphi},\mu^{\prime},\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
and
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
, are isomorphic, thus
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
admits an invariant metric.
∎

2.1.
Example

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
be the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra, with
[x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}=x_{3}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\}
, where
\alpha_{i}(x_{j})=\delta_{ij}
. Let
\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
with the non-degenerate, symmetric and bilinear form
B_{\mathfrak{a}}(u_{i},u_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.

We shall use the criterion in
Prop.
2.4
in a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
, where
\varphi:\mathfrak{h}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{a};\mathfrak{h}^{\ast})
is given by:

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1})(u_{2})=-\varphi(x_{2})(u_{1})=\alpha_{3},\quad\varphi(x_{2})(u_{3})=-\varphi(x_{3})(u_{2})=\alpha_{1},

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{3})(u_{1})=-\varphi(x_{1})(u_{3})=\alpha_{2}.

The skew-symmetric bilinear map
\lambda:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
is given by:

\lambda(x_{1},x_{2})=(1+\xi)u_{3},\quad\lambda(x_{2},x_{3})=u_{1},\quad\lambda(x_{3},x_{1})=u_{2},

where
\xi
is in
\mathbb{F}
. The skew-symmetric bilinear map
\mu:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
is given by:

\mu(x_{1},x_{2})\!=\!-2\xi\,\alpha_{3},\quad\mu(x_{2},x_{3})\!=\!-2\xi\,\alpha_{1},\quad\mu(x_{3},x_{1})\!=\!-2\xi\,\alpha_{2}.

Since
\varphi(x_{j})(u_{j})=0
for all
1\leq j\leq 3
, then

\displaystyle e_{\varphi}(\lambda)(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1})(\lambda(x_{2},x_{3}))+\varphi(x_{2})(\lambda(x_{3},x_{1}))+\varphi(x_{3})(\lambda(x_{1},x_{2}))=0.

Since
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}(x_{j})(\alpha_{j})=0
for all
j
and
\mu
is skew-symmetric, we have:

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}_{\operatorname{ad}^{\ast}_{\mathfrak{h}}}(\mu)(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}(x_{1})(\mu(x_{2},x_{3}))

\displaystyle+\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}(x_{2})(\mu(x_{3},x_{1}))+\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}(x_{3})(\mu(x_{1},x_{2}))+\mu(x_{1},[x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle+\mu(x_{2},[x_{3},x_{1}]_{\mathfrak{h}})+\mu(x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}})=0.

Then
\operatorname{d}_{R}(\lambda,\mu)=(\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda),e_{\varphi}(\lambda)+\operatorname{d}_{\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}}(\mu))=0
, and by
Prop.
1.1
,
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
is a Lie algebra. The bilinear
\lambda_{\varphi}:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
of
Prop.
2.2
is given by:

\lambda_{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2})=u_{3},\quad\lambda_{\varphi}(x_{2},x_{3})=u_{1},\quad\lambda_{\varphi}(x_{3},x_{1})=u_{2}.

Let
L:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{a}
be the linear map defined by
L(x_{j})=\xi\,u_{j}
for all
j
. It is straightforward to verify that:

\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2}),L(x_{3}))=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x_{2},x_{3}),L(x_{1}))

\displaystyle=B_{\mathfrak{a}}(\lambda_{\varphi}(x_{3},x_{1}),L(x_{2}))=\xi.

In addition:

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L)(x_{1},x_{2})=-L([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}})=-L(x_{3})=-\xi u_{3},\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L)(x_{2},x_{3})=-L([x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{h}})=0=-L([x_{3},x_{1}]_{\mathfrak{h}})=\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L)(x_{3},x_{1}).

Then,
\lambda_{\varphi}=\lambda+\operatorname{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}(L)
. By
Prop.
2.4
,
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
admits an invariant metric. The bracket on
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
is given by:

\displaystyle[x_{1},x_{2}]=x_{3}+(1+\xi)u_{3}-2\xi\alpha_{3},\quad[x_{2},x_{3}]=u_{1}-2\xi\alpha_{1},

\displaystyle[x_{3},x_{1}]\!=\!u_{2}-2\xi\alpha_{2},\quad[x_{1},u_{2}]\!=\!-[x_{2},u_{1}]\!=\!\alpha_{3},\quad[x_{2},u_{3}]\!=\!-[x_{3},u_{2}]\!=\!\alpha_{1},

\displaystyle[x_{3},u_{1}]=-[x_{1},u_{3}]=\alpha_{2},\quad[x_{1},\alpha_{3}]=-\alpha_{2},\quad[x_{2},\alpha_{3}]=\alpha_{1}.

The invariant metric
B
on
\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
is:
B(x_{j},u_{k})=-\xi\delta_{jk}
, and
B(u_{j},u_{k})=B(x_{j},\alpha_{k})=\delta_{jk}
, for all
j,k
.

It is a straightforward to verify that:

\displaystyle Z(\mathfrak{g})=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\},\quad Z_{2}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{h}^{\ast},\quad Z_{3}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast},

\displaystyle Z_{4}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{F}x_{3}\oplus Z_{3}(\mathfrak{g}),\quad Z_{5}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g},\quad\mathfrak{g}^{1}=Z_{4}(\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{2}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast},\quad\mathfrak{g}^{3}=\mathfrak{h}^{\ast},\quad\mathfrak{g}^{4}=Z(\mathfrak{g}),\quad\mathfrak{g}^{5}=\{0\}

Thus,
\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
and
\mathfrak{j}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
.

3.
Current nilpotent Lie algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
be a non-abelian nilpotent quadratic Lie algebra with invariant metric
B
. By
Lemma
1.3
,
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
and
\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite dimensional associative and commutative algebra with unit
1
over
\mathbb{F}
. In the vector space
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, we consider the bracket
[X\otimes s,Y\otimes t]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}}=[X,Y]\otimes st
, for all
X,Y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}})
is a Lie algebra which is called the
current Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathcal{S}
.

Since
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
, then
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}=(\mathfrak{h}\otimes\mathcal{S})\oplus(\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathcal{S})
. Thus, by (
2
), the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}}
in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
takes the form:

(23)

\begin{split}&[x\otimes s,y\otimes t]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\otimes st+\Lambda(x,y)\otimes st,\\
&[x\otimes s,U\otimes t]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}}=R(x)(U)\otimes st.\end{split}

where
x,y
are in
\mathfrak{h}
,
U
is in
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
, and
s,t
are in
\mathcal{S}
. From now on we write
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
to denote the bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}}
in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{f:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}\mid f\text{ is bilinear and invariant }\}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric
\bar{B}
. For each pair
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
, consider the map
\mathcal{L}(s,t):\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
defined by:

\mathcal{L}(s,t)(X,Y)=\bar{B}(X\otimes s,Y\otimes t),\quad\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}.

We assert that
\mathcal{L}(s,t)
belongs to
\mathcal{W}
. Indeed, let
X,Y,Z
be in
\mathfrak{g}
, using that
\bar{B}
is invariant we get:

(24)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(s,t)([X,Y],Z)=\bar{B}([X,Y]\otimes s,Z\otimes t)\\
&=\bar{B}([X\otimes s,Y\otimes 1],Z\otimes t)=\bar{B}(X\otimes s,[Y\otimes 1,Z\otimes t])\\
&=\bar{B}(X\otimes s,[Y,Z]\otimes t)=\mathcal{L}(s,t)(X,[Y,Z]).\end{split}

Then
\mathcal{L}(s,t)
belongs to
\mathcal{W}
. Following the arguments in (
24
), we obtain:

(25)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(s,t)([X,Y],Z)\!=\!\bar{B}([X\otimes 1,Y\otimes s],Z\otimes t)\\
&=\!\bar{B}(X\otimes 1,[Y,Z]\otimes st)=\mathcal{L}(1,st)(X,[Y,Z])\\
&=\!\mathcal{L}(1,st)([X,Y],Z).\end{split}

Using the same arguments as in (
25
) and that
\bar{B}
is symmetric, we get:

(26)

.\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(s,t)([X,Y],Z)=\bar{B}(z\otimes t,[X\otimes s,Y\otimes 1])\\
&=\bar{B}([Z,X]\otimes st,Y\otimes s)=-\bar{B}([Z\otimes s,X\otimes t],Y\otimes 1)\\
&=\bar{B}(Z\otimes s,[X,Y]\otimes t)=\mathcal{L}(s,t)(Z,[X,Y]).\end{split}

Due to
\mathcal{S}
is commutative, from (
25
) and (
26
) we deduce that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\times\mathfrak{g}}
is symmetric and invariant, that is:

(27)

\mathcal{L}(s,t)(X,Y)=\mathcal{L}(t,s)(X,Y)=\mathcal{L}(st,1)(X,Y)=\mathcal{L}(s,t)(Y,X),

for all
X
in
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
Y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\operatorname{Cent}(\mathfrak{g})=\{T\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})\mid T([X,Y])=[X,T(Y)]\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}\}
. Since
B
is a non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
, for each pair
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
, there exists
\Gamma(s,t)
in
\operatorname{Cent}(\mathfrak{g})
such that:

(28)

B\left(\Gamma(s,t)(X),Y\right)=\mathcal{L}(s,t)(X,Y)=\bar{B}(X\otimes s,Y\otimes t),

where
X,Y
are in
\mathfrak{g}
. As
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\times g}
is symmetric and invariant, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(s,t)([X,Y],Z)

\displaystyle=B(\Gamma(s,t)([X,Y]),Z)=\mathcal{L}(st,1)([X,Y],Z)

\displaystyle=B(\Gamma(st,1)([X,Y],Z)),\,\,\text{ for all }X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Thus
\Gamma(s,t)([X,Y])=\Gamma(st,1)([X,Y])
and
\Gamma(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
is symmetric and invariant, that is:

(29)

\Gamma(s,t)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}=\Gamma(t,s)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])}=\Gamma(st,1)|_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]},\,\text{ for all }s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

It is not difficult to verify that the canonical ideal
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, is invariant under
\Gamma(s,t)
. Thus, for every
x
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
U
in
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
, there are
\epsilon(s,t,x)
in
\mathfrak{h}
,
\Theta(s,t,x)
in
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\vartheta(s,t,U)
in
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
such that:

\Gamma(s,t)(x)=\epsilon(s,t,x)+\Theta(s,t,x),\quad\text{ and }\quad\Gamma(s,t)(U)=\vartheta(s,t,U).

Let
\epsilon:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
be the map defined by
\epsilon(s,t)(x)=\epsilon(s,t,x)
. Similarly, we define
\Theta:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g}))
by
\Theta(s,t)(x)=\Theta(s,t,x)
and
\vartheta:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g}))
by
\vartheta(s,t)(U)=\vartheta(s,t,U)
.

Since
\Gamma(s,t)([x,y])=[x,\Gamma(s,t)(y)]
for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{h}
, by (
2
) we deduce:
\epsilon(s,t)([x,y]_{\mathfrak{h}})\!=\![x,\epsilon(s,t)(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}
. That is,
\epsilon(s,t)
belongs to
\operatorname{Cent}(\mathfrak{h})
.

From (
29
) we get:

[\Gamma(s,t)(X),Y]=\Gamma(s,t)([X,Y])=\Gamma(st,1)([X,Y])=[\Gamma(st,1)(X),Y]

for all
X,Y
in
\mathfrak{g}
, then
\Gamma(s,t)(\mathfrak{g})-\Gamma(st,1)(\mathfrak{g})\subset Z(\mathfrak{g})
. This implies that
\Gamma(s,t)(x)-\Gamma(st,1)(x)
belongs to
Z(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
for all
x
in
\mathfrak{h}
, that is:

(\epsilon(s,t)(x)-\epsilon(st,1)(x))+(\Theta(s,t)(x)-\Theta(st,1)(x))\in Z(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})

As
\Theta(s,t)(x)-\Theta(st,1)(x)
belongs to
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
, then
\epsilon(s,t)(x)-\epsilon(st,1)(x)
belongs to
\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
. Hence,
\epsilon(s,t)=\epsilon(st,1)
.

As
[x,U]=R(x)(U)
and
\Gamma(s,t)([x,U])=[\Gamma(s,t)(x),U]=[x,\Gamma(s,t)(U)]
for all
x
in
\mathfrak{h}
, and
U
in
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
, by (
2
) we obtain:

(30)

\vartheta(s,t)(R(x)(U))=R(\epsilon(s,t)(x))(U)=R(x)(\vartheta(s,t)(U)).

Suppose there exists
s^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}
such that
\epsilon(s^{\prime},t)=0
for all
t
in
\mathcal{S}
.
By (
30
) it follows that
\Gamma(s^{\prime},t)(R(x)(U))=\vartheta(s^{\prime},t)(R(x)(U))=0
for all
x
in
\mathfrak{h}
,
U
in
\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
and
t
in
\mathcal{S}
. By (
28
) this amounts to say that:

(31)

\bar{B}(R(x)(u)\otimes s^{\prime},Y\otimes t)=B(\Gamma(s^{\prime},t)(R(x)(u)),Y)=0.

where
Y
is in
\mathfrak{g}
. Due to
\bar{B}
is non-degenerate and
Y
and
t
are arbitrary, from (
31
) we deduce that
R(x)(U)\otimes s^{\prime}=0
. If
s^{\prime}
is non-zero then
R(x)(U)=[x,U]=0
. Since
x
and
U
are arbitrary, it follows
[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{J}]=\{0\}
. As
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}=\mathfrak{i}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{J}(\mathfrak{g})
, then
[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}]=\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}(\mathfrak{h})(\mathfrak{h}^{\ast})=\{0\}
, which implies that
\mathfrak{h}
is abelian. Therefore, if
\mathfrak{h}
is non-abelian,
s^{\prime}=0
.

3.1 Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}(\lambda,\mu,\varphi,\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast})
be a non-abelian nilpotent quadratic Lie algebra. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a commutative and associative algebra with unit. If the current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
admits an invariant metric and
\mathfrak{h}
is non-abelian, then there exists a bilinear map
\epsilon:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\to\operatorname{Cent}(\mathfrak{h})
such that:

(i)

\epsilon(s,t)=\epsilon(s\,t,1)
, for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
.

(ii)
If
\epsilon(s,t)=0
for all
t
in
\mathcal{S}
then
s=0
.

4.
Invariant forms on nilpotent Lie algebras

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
be a finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra over a field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic. Let
\mathcal{V}
be the vector space generated by all the symmetric and bilinear forms
f:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
. Let
\varrho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{V})
be the map defined by:

(32)

\varrho(x)(f)(y,z)=-f([x,y],z)-f(y,[x,z]),\quad\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\varrho
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
in
\mathcal{V}
. Due to
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
is nilpotent, by
Thm. 3.3
in
[
3
]
, there exists a non-zero
f
in
\mathcal{V}
such that
\varrho(x)(f)=0
for all
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
. From (
32
), this amounts to say that
f
is an invariant form in
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
.

For finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras, the above shows that there exists an invariant, symmetric and non-zero bilinear form in any nilpotent Lie algebra. To find out whether it is non-degenerate, we can associate to such a bilinear form, a non-associative algebra with unit from which we can recover the Lie algebra structure in
\mathfrak{g}
and we can prove that this non-associative algebra is simple if and only if the bilinear form is non-degenerate.

4.1 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
be a finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra over field
\mathbb{F}
of zero characteristic. Let
f
be an invariant, symmetric and non-zero bilinear form on
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{f}=\mathbb{F}\times\mathfrak{g}
be the algebra with product defined by:

(33)

(\xi,x)(\eta,y)=(\,\xi\,\eta+f(x,y)\,,\,\xi\,y+\eta\,x+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]\,),

for all
\xi,\eta
in
\mathbb{F}
and
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

(i)
If
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{g}>1
, then
f
is non-degenerate if and only if
\mathcal{A}_{f}
is a simple algebra.

(ii)
Let
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{f}
be the commutator of (
33
). Then
\mathcal{A}_{f}/\mathbb{F}(1,0)
with bracket induced by
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{f}
, is a Lie algebra isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
.

Proof.

(i)
We define equality, addition and multiplication by scalars
in
\mathcal{A}_{f}
in the obvious manner. Suppose
f
is non-degenerate and let
I
be a non-zero right ideal of
\mathcal{A}_{f}
. We shall prove that
(1,0)
belongs to
I
. We assert that there exists an element
(\xi,x)
in
I
with
\xi\neq 0
. Let
(\xi,x)
be a non-zero element in
I
. If
\xi=0
, then
x
is non-zero. Due to
f
is non-degenerate, there exists
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that
f(x,y)
is non-zero. By (
33
), we have that
(0,x)(\eta,y)=\left(f(x,y),\eta x+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]\right)
belongs to
I
, which proves our assertion.

Let
(\xi,x)
be in
I
, then
(\xi,x)(-\xi,x)=(-\xi^{2}+f(x,x),0)
is in
I
. If
-\xi^{2}+f(x,x)
is non-zero, then
(1,0)
belongs to
I
and consequently
I=\mathcal{A}_{f}
. Thus, from now on we assume the following:

(
\clubsuit
)

\text{ For all }(\xi,x)\text{ in }I,\quad\xi^{2}=f(x,x).

Let
(\xi,x)
be in
I
, with
\xi\neq 0
and
x\neq 0
(if
x=0
we are done), and let
(\eta,y)
be an arbitrary element in
\mathcal{A}_{f}
. Then
(\xi,x)(\eta,y)=(\xi\eta+f(x,y),\xi y+\eta x+\frac{1}{2}[x,y])
belongs to
I
. Applying (

♣

‣
4
), we obtain:

(34)

f(x,y)^{2}=\xi^{2}f(y,y)+\frac{1}{4}f([x,y],[x,y]).

Due to
y
is arbitrary, we apply (
34
) to
y+z
in
\mathfrak{g}
and using that
f
is invariant we get:

(35)

4f(x,y)f(x,z)=4\xi^{2}f(y,z)-f([x,[x,y]],z).

Using that
f
is non-degenerate, from (
35
) it follows:

(36)

4f(x,y)x=4\xi^{2}y-\operatorname{ad}(x)^{2}(y)

Applying the adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}(x)
in (
36
), we have:

(37)

\operatorname{ad}(x)^{2}(\operatorname{ad}(x)(y))=4\xi^{2}\operatorname{ad}(x)(y).

If
\operatorname{ad}(x)^{2}=0
then from (
35
) we get:

(38)

f(x,y)f(x,z)=\xi^{2}f(y,z)

As
f
is non-degenerate and
\xi
is non-zero, from (
38
) we obtain
y=\xi^{-2}f(x,y)x
. Due to
y
is arbitrary, we deduce that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{F}x
and
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{g}=1
, a contradiction.

If
\operatorname{ad}(x)^{2}
is non-zero, then
\operatorname{ad}(x)\neq 0
and there exists
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\operatorname{ad}(x)(y)\neq 0
. Then
\operatorname{ad}(x)(y)\neq 0
is an eigenvector of
\operatorname{ad}(x)
with eigenvalue
4\xi^{2}\neq 0
by (
37
), which contradicts that
\operatorname{ad}(x)
is nilpotent.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{A}_{f}
is a simple algebra and we will prove that
f
is non-degenerate. Let
R
be the subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by those elements
x
such that
f(x,y)=0
for all
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. We claim that
\{0\}\times R
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{A}_{f}
. Indeed, as
f
is invariant it is clear that
R
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
x
be in
R
and
(\eta,y)
be in
\mathcal{A}_{f}
. Then
(0,x)(\eta,y)=(0,\eta x+\frac{1}{2}[x,y])=(\eta,y)(0,x)
belongs to
\{0\}\times R
. Due to
\mathcal{A}_{f}
is simple and
f
is non-zero, then
\{0\}\times R
is zero, which implies that

and
f
is non-degenerate.

(ii)
Let
(\xi,x)
and
(\eta,y)
be in
\mathcal{A}_{f}
, then
[(\xi,x),(\eta,y)]_{f}=(0,[x,y])
. The kernel of the projection map

, between
\mathcal{A}_{f}
and
\mathfrak{g}
, is
\mathbb{F}(1,0)
. Therefore, the map

, is a Lie algebra isomorphism between
\mathcal{A}_{f}/\mathbb{F}(1,0)
, with bracket induced by

.

∎

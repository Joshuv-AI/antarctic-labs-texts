Attached Submanifolds Beyond Symmetric Spaces
Megan M. Kerr, Tracy L. Payne

Abstract
. We study submanifold geometry in the presence of symmetry, focusing on submanifolds of solvmanifolds with an unusual property relative to Ricci curvature. We generalize work of H. Tamaru [ 8 ] in which he explores the geometry of submanifolds of symmetric spaces of noncompact type constructed from parabolic subgroups of the isometry group. He calls these attached submanifolds. The Ricci curvatures of attached submanifolds coincide with the restrictions of the Ricci curvatures of ambient symmetric spaces. We broaden Tamaru’s construction by weakening the hypotheses on the ambient space, allowing a pseudo-Riemannian scalar product, and defining attached submanifolds in terms of root spaces. We demonstrate that in this setting, the Ricci curvature restriction property for attached submanifolds holds if and only if the submanifold satisfies an algebraic criterion that we call the Jacobi Star Condition. Like attached submanifolds of symmetric spaces, our attached submanifolds are minimal, and are only totally geodesic under hypotheses analogous to hypotheses in the symmetric space case. Finally, we give an example of a solvmanifold that has an attached submanifold and is not a symmetric space, demonstrating that attached submanifolds are not unique to symmetric spaces.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context and motivation.

In a sequence of papers, Hiroshi
Tamaru studied submanifolds of symmetric spaces of noncompact type defined by parabolic subalgebras in corresponding semisimple Lie algebras
[
6
,
8
,
7
]
. He calls these
attached
submanifolds. The special properties of parabolic subalgebras translate to unique geometric features of the attached submanifolds.

Of particular interest, Tamaru shows that the Ricci curvature tensor for the attached submanifold coincides with the restriction of the Ricci curvature tensor of the ambient manifold.
As a corollary, because symmetric spaces are Einstein, their attached submanifolds
inherit the constant Ricci curvature of the larger space, yielding many new examples of
Einstein homogeneous spaces. In fact, these manifolds are solvmanifolds, and they
include examples for which the underlying solvable Lie group has higher step.
Another notable property is minimality: Tamaru shows that attached submanifolds are
always minimal.
In addition, although they are not always totally geodesic, Tamaru presents necessary
and sufficient conditions for them to be so.
In this work,
we ask, is the property of restricted ambient Ricci curvature agreeing with the
intrinsic Ricci curvature of a submanifold unique to attached submanifolds of symmetric
spaces, or does it hold more generally? Under what more general hypotheses will
Tamaru’s approach carry through? Throughout his analysis, Tamaru uses the machinery of
semisimple Lie groups, such as the Killing form, root spaces and the Weyl group.
We carefully analyze the hypotheses and proofs in Tamaru’s work, and we generalize his
results by broadening the context beyond the symmetric spaces, thus weakening
hypotheses on both the ambient space and the submanifold. We discover necessary
geometric hypotheses that allow us to preserve Tamaru’s main results.

After generalizing Tamaru’s theorems, we ask whether there are
solvable metric Lie algebras that meet our more general hypotheses and do not meet
Tamaru’s; i.e., do not correspond to symmetric spaces of noncompact type. We give an
example showing our hypotheses are truly more general, because they are satisfied by a
solvmanifold that is not a symmetric space. The example we present is part of a
naturally defined family. The computations in our example are
technical and lengthy. To work more generally and construct other
examples, one should use the structure of
Kac-Moody Lie algebras, which we have suppressed in our exposition
to improve readability.
We were motivated to use Kac-Moody algebras because the finite-dimensional solvable
metric Lie algebras that they define are of strong Iwasawa type, and crucially, in a
Kac-Moody algebra, the maps
\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{\ast}
are derivations, and this basic condition
ensures that the Jacobi Star Condition holds for our examples.

1.2.
Tamaru’s results on attached submanifolds of symmetric spaces of noncompact type

Let
(M,g)
be a symmetric space of noncompact type and let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie
algebra of the isometry group of
(M,g).
Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a parabolic subalgebra of

\mathfrak{g}
. Tamaru defined an orbit of
\exp(\mathfrak{q})
in
M
, endowed with the induced
metric, to be an
attached submanifold.

An attached submanifold
(S^{\prime},g^{\prime})
of
(M,g)
has special properties relative to the ambient symmetric space. The main theorem of
[
8
]
states that the Ricci form for
(S^{\prime},g^{\prime})
is the
restriction of the Ricci form of the ambient space
(S,g)
. Because the symmetric space is Einstein, every attached submanifold is Einstein.
Furthermore, all attached submanifolds are minimal. They are not always totally geodesic, and Tamaru presents necessary and sufficient conditions for the attached submanifold to be totally geodesic.

1.3.
Main theoretical results

We are able to generalize many
of the results from Tamaru’s paper

[
8
]
, often closely following his proofs.

Throughout this work, we work in the setting of metric Lie algebras.
A solvable Lie group
S
endowed with a left-invariant Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
may be identified with a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
equipped with a scalar product, and all local geometric quantities for the solvmanifold
(S,g)
may be computed via the metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}).

Main Theorem

We consider solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebras of strong Iwasawa type
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
as defined in Definition
2.1
.
In Definition
3.1
, we define an
attached metric subalgebra

defined by a subset

We determine in particular when the restriction of

to

(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian
metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type, with
\Lambda\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
,

be an attached subalgebra defined by

.
We say that
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})

\{E_{j}^{\prime}\}\cup\{E_{j}^{\perp}\}

(1.1)

\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}[(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{\ast,\mathfrak{n}},\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}}](X)=\operatorname{ad}_{\sum\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}E_{j}^{\perp}}(X)

This definition may seem technical. A slightly more restrictive condition which implies the Jacobi Star Condition is that

Indeed, this is the only additional property of symmetric spaces that we use in Corollary

Our main result is that the restriction of the Ricci
endomorphism of a solvable metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
of strong Iwasawa type
to
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
coincides with the Ricci endomorphism for

(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
if and only if the Jacobi Star Condition holds.

Main Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra
of strong Iwasawa type.
Let
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
be the attached metric subalgebra defined by a subset
\Lambda^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
.
Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
denote the mean curvature vectors for
({\mathfrak{s}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
and
({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
, respectively. Let
\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{s}}
,
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}
,
\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}
, and
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}
denote the Ricci endomorphisms of the corresponding metric Lie algebras.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

{\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}}(X)=\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}(X)}
for any
X\in{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}
.

(ii)

\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)=[H-H^{\prime},X]
for any
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
.

(iii)

The Jacobi Star Condition holds.

1.3.2.
Minimality

Furthermore, we show in Theorem
4.7
that attached
submanifolds are minimal submanifolds, and in Proposition
4.8
,
we characterize when they are totally geodesic. These
results extend analogous statements for symmetric spaces of

[

.

1.4.
Solvable Metric Lie algebras defined by Kac-Moody algebras

We will present an example of a solvable metric Lie algebra
that meets our hypotheses but does not correspond to a symmetric space.
This example comes from the affine untwisted Kac-Moody
algebra
\mathfrak{g}
defined by
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{3}(\mathbb{R}).

The infinite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a triangular decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}_{-}+\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{n}_{+}.
We take the upper
triangular part
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
and truncate it to obtain a
finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
. We extend
\mathfrak{n}
by an
abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{h}
to get a 12-dimensional, 6-step
solvable Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{a}\ltimes\mathfrak{n}
of strong Iwasawa type. We use a natural invariant bilinear form from the Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
to define an inner product on
\mathfrak{s}.

This construction, the truncation of a triangular part of a
Kac-Moody algebra, works more generally. By varying the Kac-Moody Lie algebra and varying the ideal used in the truncation, infinitely many examples may be constructed. We conjecture that in general many of the symmetries of the Kac-Moody Lie algebra will be preserved in the finite-dimensional nilpotent quotient.

1.5.
Overview

In Section
2
we review the algebraic and geometric properties of solvmanifolds with a pseudo-Riemannian metric. In Section
3
we define an attached subalgebra
and we describe its properties. In Section
4
we prove our main theorem
on attached subalgebras. We also prove that attached submanifolds are minimal and prove necessary and sufficient conditions for them to be totally geodesic. In Section
5
we show that Tamaru’s attached subalgebras arising from noncompact symmetric spaces are a special case of our construction. Finally, in Section
6

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Solvable metric Lie algebras

A
(pseudo-Riemannian) metric Lie algebra
is a Lie algebra
endowed with a nondegenerate
symmetric bilinear form, the scalar product
{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}
.
Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra.
A vector
X
in
\mathfrak{s}
is a
unit vector
with respect to the
scalar product if
\langle X,X\rangle\in\{-1,1\}
.
A basis
\{E_{i}\}
of pairwise orthogonal unit vectors is called
orthonormal
.
For such a basis, we use
\epsilon_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
to denote
\langle E_{i},E_{i}\rangle
.
We focus on solvable pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebras that satisfy the conditions in the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra.
We say
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is of
strong Iwasawa type
if

(i)

\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
, where
{\mathfrak{n}}=[{\mathfrak{s}},{\mathfrak{s}}]
, and
{\mathfrak{a}}
, is the orthogonal complement of
{\mathfrak{n}}
and is abelian,

(ii)

for all
A
in
\mathfrak{a},

\operatorname{ad}_{A}
is symmetric, and
\operatorname{ad}_{A}
is zero if and only if
A=0
,

(iii)

there exists an
A^{0}
in
\mathfrak{a}
so that the restriction of
\operatorname{ad}_{A^{0}}
to
\mathfrak{n}
has all positive eigenvalues
and

(iv)

the scalar product is positive definite on
\mathfrak{a}.

The definition of Iwasawa type (parts (i)-(iii)) is standard in the Riemannian case; we require the additional hypothesis (iv) in order for reflections in
\mathfrak{a}
to be well-defined in the pseudo-Riemannian setting.

For any
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{a}^{\ast},
the subspace
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha}
in
{\mathfrak{n}}
is defined by

{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}=\{X\in{\mathfrak{n}}\mid\operatorname{ad}_{A}(X)=\alpha(A)X~{\rm for~all}~A\in{\mathfrak{a}}\}.

When
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
is nontrivial, we say
\alpha
is a
root
and
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha}

is the
root space
for
\alpha
.
Let
\Delta\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{*}
denote the set of roots of

{\mathfrak{s}}
.
When
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is of
strong Iwasawa type,
0
is not a root.
By properties (i) and (ii)
of strong Iwasawa type, the root space decomposition
{\mathfrak{n}}=\bigoplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
is an orthogonal direct sum
and the scalar product is nondegenerate on each summand.
For each
root
\alpha
, let
H_{\alpha}
in
\mathfrak{a}
denote the
root
vector
defined by the property that for every
A
in
{\mathfrak{a}}
,
\langle H_{\alpha},A\rangle=\alpha(A)
.
For any basis
\{\alpha_{i}\}
of
\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
, define the dual basis
\{B_{\alpha_{i}}\}
of
{\mathfrak{a}}
by the property that

\alpha_{i}(B_{\alpha_{j}})=\delta_{ij}
for all
i
and
j
.

For a metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}),
and
X
in
\mathfrak{g}
, let

\operatorname{ad}_{X}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
denote the map
\operatorname{ad}_{X}(Y)=[X,Y]
, where

Y\in\mathfrak{g},
and let
\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{\ast}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
denote its
adjoint map. At times we will consider the adjoint map for a subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}
endowed with the restricted inner product, and we will use
the notation
\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{\ast,\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
to make the domain of
the map clear.

The dual map from
\mathfrak{a}
to
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
is defined by
A\mapsto\check{A}
where

\check{A}(B)=\langle A,B\rangle
.
The scalar product
{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}
on
\mathfrak{a}
induces a scalar product on
\mathfrak{a}^{\ast},
with
\langle A,B\rangle=\langle\check{A},\check{B}\rangle.
For each root

\beta
, we have the reflection

\check{s}_{\beta}:{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{a}}^{\ast}
defined by

\check{s}_{\beta}(\alpha)=\alpha-\frac{2\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}{\langle\beta,\beta\rangle}\beta,\quad\text{for any $\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{\ast},$ and}

the root vector
H_{\beta}
induces a reflection
s_{\beta}:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}
with

s_{\beta}(A)=A-\frac{2\langle A,H_{\beta}\rangle}{\langle H_{\beta},H_{\beta}\rangle}H_{\beta},\quad\text{for any $A\in\mathfrak{a}.$}

2.2.
Geometry of solvable metric Lie algebras

In this section we define geometric quantities associated to metric
Lie algebras. The definitions coincide with analogous definitions for
the associated homogeneous spaces.

Definition 2.2
.

For a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})

of strong Iwasawa type, the
mean curvature vector

H
for
\mathfrak{n}
is
the unique vector in
\mathfrak{s}
so that for all
X
in
{\mathfrak{s}}
,

\langle H,X\rangle=\operatorname{trace}\,\operatorname{ad}_{X}
.

The Ricci curvature for a solvable pseudo-Riemannian Lie
algebra may be computed using the following theorems, each extended
from the Riemannian setting to the pseudo-Riemannian setting by Conti and Rossi.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
1
,
3
]

Given a nilpotent pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{n}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
with an orthonormal basis
\{E_{i}\}
,
the Ricci endomorphism
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}
is given by

(2.1)

\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}=\tfrac{1}{4}\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}})\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}})^{*}-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}}).

The Ricci endomorphism for a solvable pseudo-Riemannian Lie
algebra may be computed using the next theorem, combined with the previous theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
9
,
4
]

Let
({\mathfrak{s}}={\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})

be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra such that

\operatorname{ad}_{A}
is symmetric for all
A\in{\mathfrak{a}}

with mean curvature vector
H
. Let

\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}
denote the Ricci curvature of
({\mathfrak{n}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}|_{\mathfrak{n}})
. The Ricci curvature of
({\mathfrak{s}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
satisfies

(1)

\operatorname{ric}(A,A^{\prime})=-\operatorname{trace}(\operatorname{ad}_{A}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{A^{\prime}})
for all
A,A^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{a}}
,

(2)

\operatorname{ric}(X,A)=0
for all
A\in{\mathfrak{a}}
and all
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}
,

(3)

\operatorname{ric}(X,Y)=\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X,Y)-\langle\operatorname{ad}_{H}X,Y\rangle
for all
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{n}}
.

The connection for a metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
decomposes into skew-symmetric and symmetric parts:
\nabla_{X}Y=-\tfrac{1}{2}[X,Y]+U(X,Y),
where
U
is the symmetric tensor with

(2.2)

\langle U(X,Y),Z\rangle=\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}(\langle[Z,X],Y\rangle+\langle[Z,Y],X\rangle)

for
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
.
It follows that

U(X,X)=-{\operatorname{ad}_{X}}^{\ast}X
for any
X
in
\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
be a subalgebra of a metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}),

The second fundamental form
h

for
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is given by

h(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla^{\prime}_{X}Y=U(X,Y)-U^{\prime}(X,Y),

for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},
where

\nabla^{\prime}
denotes the connection of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})

and
U^{\prime}
denotes the symmetric part of

\nabla^{\prime}
.
The subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is
minimal
if the trace
of
h
is zero, and it is
totally geodesic
if
h
is
identically zero.

3.
Attached subalgebras and submanifolds

In this section, we define attached subalgebras, a notion due to
Tamaru (
[
6
]
), and we describe some of their algebraic
and geometric properties.

3.1.
The definition of attached subalgebra

Let
(\mathfrak{s}={\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type with
root space decomposition
{\mathfrak{n}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
. Let
\Lambda=\{\alpha_{i}\}
be a finite subset of
\Delta
such that
(i)
\Lambda
is a basis for
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\ast}
, and (ii) every element in
\Delta
can be
expressed as a linear combination of elements of
\Lambda
with coefficients in

{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. (The set
\Lambda
is analogous to a set of simple
roots in an abstract root
system.)
Let
\Lambda^{\prime}
be a proper subset of
\Lambda
and enumerate its complement in

\Lambda
as

(3.1)

\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{k}}\}.

Let
\{B_{\alpha_{i}}\}
be the dual basis to
\Lambda.

Define
Z
in
\mathfrak{a}
by

(3.2)

Z=B_{\alpha_{i_{1}}}+B_{\alpha_{i_{2}}}+\dots+B_{\alpha_{i_{k}}}.

Note that
\alpha(Z)>0
for all
\alpha
in
\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
,
\alpha(Z)=0
if and only if
\alpha
is a sum of elements in
\Lambda^{\prime}
, and
\alpha(Z)\geq 0
for all
\alpha
in
\Delta
.

Assume that (i) for all
\alpha_{j}
in
\Lambda^{\prime}
, the corresponding reflection
\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}
permutes
the elements of the set of roots
\alpha
for which

\alpha(Z)>0
and in addition, (ii) if
\alpha(Z)>0
, then

\dim({\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})=\dim({\mathfrak{n}}_{\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha)})
.
The subset
\Lambda^{\prime}
defines a metric subalgebra
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
of
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
as follows.
The underlying subalgebra
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
is defined by
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime},
where

(3.3)

\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{\alpha_{i_{j}}}\mid\alpha_{i_{j}}(Z)>0\}\subseteq\mathfrak{a},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha(Z)>0}{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}\subseteq{\mathfrak{n}}.

The scalar product for
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
is the restriction of the scalar product for
{\mathfrak{s}}
.

We can write
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{n}
as direct sums
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{a}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0}
, where complementary
subspaces to
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
are
defined by

{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{\alpha_{i_{j}}}\mid\alpha_{i_{j}}(Z)=0\}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}},\quad\,\text{and}\quad{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}=\sum_{\alpha(Z)=0}{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}\subseteq{\mathfrak{n}}.

We say an orthogonal basis of unit vectors
\{E_{j}^{\prime}\}\cup\{E_{j}^{\perp}\}

for
\mathfrak{n}
is a
compatible basis
if each basis vector lies in

\{E_{j}^{\prime}\}
is a basis for

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
; and
\{E_{j}^{\perp}\}
is a basis for
\mathfrak{n}_{0}
.
Such a basis exists only if the scalar product is nondegenerate on each root space. This holds in our situation because the symmetric operators
\operatorname{ad}_{A}
are orthogonally diagonalizable.

Definition 3.1
.

For a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa
type
({\mathfrak{s}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
as above, the metric Lie algebra

({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
is the
attached subalgebra defined by
\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Let
(S,g)
be the simply connected solvmanifold associated to

({\mathfrak{s}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
. The submanifold
(S^{\prime},g^{\prime})
defined by
({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
is
the
attached submanifold defined by
\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Intuitively, an attached subalgebra is obtained by selecting a
subset of simple roots and taking the ideal generated by those root spaces,
along with a compatible subspace of

\mathfrak{a}.

3.2.
Properties of attached subalgebras

First, note that the restricted scalar product on
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
is nondegenerate.
It is nondegenerate on
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
, as
{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}
is positive definite on
\mathfrak{a}.

Because
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is the sum of orthogonal root spaces on which
{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}
is nondegenerate,

{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}
is nondegenerate on
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}.

An attached subalgebra of a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra of strong
Iwasawa type is also of strong Iwasawa type.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa

({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
be an attached subalgebra defined by
\Lambda^{\prime}
as in
Definition

.
Then

(i)

{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
is an ideal in
{\mathfrak{s}}
,

(ii)

[{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}]={\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
,

(iii)

[\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{n}_{0}]=\{0\}
,

(iv)

For any
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
, the mapping
(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}
sends
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}

into
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(i) Because
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is
a sum of root spaces,
[{\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
. To show that

[{\mathfrak{n}},{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
, let
X
be in
\mathfrak{n}
and let
Y
be in

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. As
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
are sums of root spaces, we may
assume that
X
and
Y
are in root spaces

{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\beta}
respectively, where
\alpha(Z)\geq 0
and
\beta(Z)>0
.
Then
[X,Y]\in[\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{n}_{\beta}]\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha+\beta}
.
Since
(\alpha+\beta)(Z)>0
, we know

\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha+\beta}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. This shows
[{\mathfrak{n}},{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
.

(ii) Because
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is a
subalgebra,
[{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}]\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. In addition,
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
is abelian and as
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is a sum of root spaces,
[\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}]\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
.
Hence
[{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}]\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
.
Now we show
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
is contained in the commutator
[\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}]
. Because

{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}=\oplus_{\alpha(Z)>0}\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha},
it suffices to show that each root space

{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
with
\alpha(Z)>0
is in
[\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}]
. Let

\alpha
be a root with
\alpha(Z)>0
and let
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
. Then

[B_{\alpha},X]=\alpha(B_{\alpha})X=X
,
and
thus
X\in[{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}]
.
Hence
[\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}]=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
.

(iii) Let
A
be in
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
and let
X
be an
element of a root space
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\beta}
in
{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
. Because
\beta(Z)=0,
it follows that
\beta(B_{\alpha})=0
for all
\alpha\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}.
Write
A=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}}c_{\alpha}B_{\alpha}
. Then

[A,X]=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}}c_{\alpha}\,[B_{\alpha},X]=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}}c_{\alpha}\,\beta(B_{\alpha})X=\sum_{\alpha_{i}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}(c_{\alpha}\cdot 0)\,X=0.

(iv) Let
X
be an element of a root space
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\beta}
contained in
{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
. Let
Y
be in a root space
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
in
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
. By definitions of
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{0}
,
\alpha(Z)>0
and
\beta(Z)=0
.
Because
\mathfrak{s}
is orthogonally graded by the sum
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{a}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
,
we know
(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{*}Y\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha-\beta}
.
Because
(\alpha-\beta)(Z)>0
, the root space
{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha-\beta}
, and hence
(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{*}Y
, is contained in
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type, and let
({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
be an attached subalgebra defined by

\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Then
({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
is of strong Iwasawa type.

Proof.

First we address Property (i).
Because
({\mathfrak{s}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is of strong Iwasawa type,
\mathfrak{a}
is abelian
and orthogonal to
\mathfrak{n}
. It follows that the subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}

is abelian and orthogonal to
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. By Proposition
3.2
,

[{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}]={\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
.
Clearly the symmetry of
\operatorname{ad}_{A}
is inherited so the first part of
Property (ii) holds. To see that the second part of (ii) holds, assume that

\operatorname{ad}_{A}|_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}\equiv 0
for some
A\in\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
.
Write
A=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}}a_{\alpha}B_{\alpha}
. Since
0=[A,X]=a_{\alpha}X
for
X\in\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha}
, we have
a_{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
. Thus
A=0
.
Property (iii) holds since
Z
is in
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
, and by definition,
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is precisely the sum of positive eigenspaces for
\operatorname{ad}_{Z}
.
Property (iv) is clearly inherited from
({\mathfrak{s}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
.
∎

Following
[
8
]
we can characterize
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
in terms of the reflections in
\Lambda^{\prime}.

We use the notation established at the start of this section.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
8
, Lemma 3.8]
Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type with attached metric subalgebra
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
defined by the proper subset
\Lambda^{\prime}
of
\Lambda
.
Then

{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}=\bigcap_{\alpha_{k}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\operatorname{Fix}(s_{\alpha_{k}})=\bigcap_{\alpha_{k}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\ker\alpha_{k}.

Proof.

Note that the dimensions of
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
and the intersection of kernels is the same, because the roots are independent. Thus it suffices to show that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
is contained in
\bigcap_{\alpha_{j}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\operatorname{Fix}(s_{\alpha_{j}})
.
The dual basis elements
B_{\alpha_{i_{j}}}
with
\alpha_{i_{j}}\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
span
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
.
Let
B_{\alpha_{i_{j}}}
be any such basis element.
Let
\alpha_{k}
be an arbitrary element of
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Then
\alpha_{k}(B_{\alpha_{i_{j}}})=\delta_{k,{i_{j}}}=0
, so that
s_{\alpha_{k}}(B_{\alpha_{i_{j}}})=B_{\alpha_{i_{j}}}
and
B_{\alpha_{i_{j}}}\in\operatorname{Fix}(s_{\alpha_{k}})
.
Because
\bigcap_{\alpha_{k}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\operatorname{Fix}(s_{\alpha_{k}})
is a subspace, linearity implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}\subseteq\bigcap_{\alpha_{j}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\operatorname{Fix}(s_{\alpha_{j}}).

∎

3.3.
The Jacobi Star Condition

A key observation about the Jacobi Star Condition defined in Definition
1.1
is that it is an intrinsic property of the submanifold.

Lemma 3.5
.

The definition of the Jacobi Star Condition
is independent of the choice of compatible basis

\{E_{j}^{\perp}\}
for
{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
.

Observe that the left-hand side of Equation (
1.1
)
equals

-\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}(X)+\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)
, which is basis independent, while the right-hand side equals
-\operatorname{ad}_{H}(X)+\operatorname{ad}_{H^{\prime}}(X)
, which is also independent of choice of compatible basis.

Remark 3.6
.

In the Jacobi Star Condition in Equation (
1.1
), the adjoint linear map
(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{\ast,\mathfrak{n}}
in the left-hand side is with respect to the domain

\mathfrak{n}
, while the adjoint linear map
(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}
in the right-hand side is with respect to the domain
\mathfrak{s}
.

4.
Geometric results

4.1.
Mean curvature vector

First, we compute root vectors and mean curvature vectors
for
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
and
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
.
Let
\{E_{j}^{\prime}\}\cup\{E_{j}^{\perp}\}
be a compatible basis of

\mathfrak{n}
. Let
X
be a unit vector in a root space
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha}

with
\epsilon=\langle X,X\rangle
, and let
A
be in
{\mathfrak{a}}
. Then

\langle-\epsilon\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*}X,A\rangle=\langle\epsilon X,[A,X]\rangle=\epsilon\langle X,\alpha(A)X\rangle=\alpha(A).

Hence the root vector for the root
\alpha
is

H_{\alpha}=-\epsilon(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{*}X,
and in
particular,

H_{\alpha}=-\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}})^{*}E_{j}
for any basis vector
E_{j}
in the root space
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha}
.

Since
{\mathfrak{a}}
is abelian,

\operatorname{trace}(\operatorname{ad}_{A}|_{{\mathfrak{s}}})=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}\langle[A,E_{j}],E_{j}\rangle=\langle A,-\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}})^{*}E_{j}\rangle.

For each root
\alpha
, summing over an orthonormal basis for

\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha}
gives
-\sum_{E_{j}\in\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha}}\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}})^{*}E_{j}=(\dim\,{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})H_{\alpha}.
By Definition
2.2
, the mean curvature vector
H
for
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is

(4.1)

H=-\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}})^{\ast}E_{j}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}(\dim{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})H_{\alpha}.

Analogously, the mean curvature vector
H^{\prime}
for
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
is

(4.2)

H^{\prime}=-\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\prime}})^{\ast}E_{j}^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha(Z)>0}(\dim{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})H_{\alpha}.

In the next proposition, we verify that the mean curvature vector
H^{\prime}

lies in
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
. The proposition and proof are analogous to Part (1) of Proposition
4.3 of
[
8
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
({\mathfrak{s}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type, with
\Lambda^{\prime}
a set of roots defining an attached subalgebra
({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
.
The mean curvature vector
H^{\prime}
for
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
lies in
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
, in order to show that
H^{\prime}
lies in
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
, it suffices to show that for any
\alpha_{j}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
, the corresponding reflection
s_{\alpha_{j}}:{\mathfrak{a}}\to{\mathfrak{a}}
fixes
H^{\prime}.

From the definition of attached subalgebra,
the corresponding reflection
\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}:{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
permutes the roots for which
\alpha(Z)>0
.

Applying
s_{\alpha_{j}}
to
H^{\prime}
gives

s_{\alpha_{j}}(H^{\prime})=s_{\alpha_{j}}\left(\sum_{\alpha(Z)>0}(\dim\,{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})H_{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\alpha(Z)>0}(\dim\,{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})s_{\alpha_{j}}(H_{\alpha}).

By Definition
3.1
, for all
\alpha
in the sum, we have that
(\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha))(Z)>0
and
\dim({\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})=\dim({\mathfrak{n}}_{\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha)})
.
It is straightforward to see that
s_{\alpha}(H_{\beta})=H_{\check{s}_{\alpha}(\beta)}
. Since the reflection
s_{\alpha_{j}}
permutes the terms in the sum, we get

s_{\alpha_{j}}(H^{\prime})=\sum_{\alpha(Z)>0}(\dim\,{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})H_{\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha)}=\sum_{\alpha(Z)>0}(\dim\,{\mathfrak{n}}_{\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha)})H_{\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha)}=H^{\prime}.

This proves that
H^{\prime}
is in
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
.
∎

4.2.
The restriction of the Ricci endomorphism

The following theorem is a generalization of Lemma 5.2 in
[
8
]
. It describes the difference in Ricci curvatures for the ambient metric Lie algebra and an attached subalgebra.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s}={\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra
of strong Iwasawa type.
Let
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
be an attached metric subalgebra defined by a set of roots
\Lambda^{\prime}

as in Definition
3.1
.
Let
\{E_{j}^{\prime}\}\cup\{E^{\perp}_{j}\}
be a compatible orthonormal basis for
{\mathfrak{n}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
.
Then for
X
in
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime},

\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)={\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum\epsilon_{j}\left((\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}-(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})\right)(X).

If in addition the Jacobi Star Condition holds, then

\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)=\operatorname{ad}_{H-H^{\prime}}(X).

Proof.

We compute
\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)
for
X
in
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}.

When we compute
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}},
we will use that the map
(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}^{\prime}})^{\ast,\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}

with respect to the restricted scalar product on
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is
equal to
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{\ast,\mathfrak{n}}
, where the adjoint map is relative to
(\mathfrak{n},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}).

By Equation (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}})\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}})^{*}(X)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}})(X),\,\text{and}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})\circ\pi_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}(X))-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum\epsilon_{i}\,\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})(X)).

We rewrite
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)
to sum over
\{E_{i}^{\prime}\}
and
\{E_{i}^{\perp}\}
separately, to get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\left(\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}^{\perp}})\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}^{\perp}})^{*}(X)+\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}(X)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}^{\perp}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}^{\perp}})(X)+\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})(X)\right).

We further expand the second sum in each line above, using the decomposition

(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}^{\prime}})^{\ast}(X)=\pi_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}^{\prime}})^{\ast}(X))+\pi_{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E_{i}^{\prime}})^{\ast}(X))

to obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\left(A+\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})\circ\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}(X))+B\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(C+\sum\epsilon_{i}\,\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})(X))+D\right),

where
A,B,C
and
D
are defined by

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\sum\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}(X),

\displaystyle\qquad B

\displaystyle=\sum\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})\circ\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}(X)),

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\sum\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})(X),

\displaystyle\qquad D

\displaystyle=\sum\epsilon_{i}\,\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})(X)).

The difference
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)
may be written as

\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)=\textstyle{\frac{1}{4}}A+\textstyle{\frac{1}{4}}B-\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}C-\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}D.

We now simplify the terms
A,B,C
and
D
.
First observe

(4.3)

\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}X,E_{j}^{\perp}\rangle=\langle X,[E^{\prime}_{i},E_{j}^{\perp}]\rangle=-\langle X,[E_{j}^{\perp},E^{\prime}_{i}]\rangle=-\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}X,E^{\prime}_{i}\rangle.

To begin, we show
B=A
:

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})\circ\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}X)\qquad

by definition

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}[E^{\prime}_{i},\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}X,E_{j}^{\perp}\rangle E_{j}^{\perp}]\qquad

using the basis for
\mathfrak{n}_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}[\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}X,E^{\prime}_{i}\rangle E_{j}^{\perp},E^{\prime}_{i}]

from Equation (
4.3
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}[E_{j}^{\perp},\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}X,E^{\prime}_{i}\rangle E^{\prime}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}[E_{j}^{\perp},(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}X]

because
X\in\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}(X)=A.

Next we prove
D=0
by proving that each term in the sum vanishes.
We may assume
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
with
\alpha(Z)>0
. Take any
E^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\beta}
with
\beta(Z)>0
. Then
(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})X\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha+\beta}
. Hence
(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})X\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}\subseteq{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}
, which implies its projection onto
{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
vanishes:
\pi_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})X)=0
. This shows that
D=0
.

Having shown that
A=B
and
D=0
, we see

\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)=\tfrac{1}{4}A+\tfrac{1}{4}B-\tfrac{1}{2}C-\tfrac{1}{2}D=\tfrac{1}{2}(A-C).

Substituting for
A
and
C
yields the desired expression for
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)
.

We note that
\tfrac{1}{2}(A-C)
is precisely the left-hand side of the Jacobi Star Condition in Equation (
1.1
), while
\operatorname{ad}_{H-H^{\prime}}(X)
is precisely the right-hand side of the Jacobi Star Condition. Thus when the Jacobi Star Condition holds, we have

\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)=\operatorname{ad}_{H-H^{\prime}}(X).

∎

The next corollary is the counterpart
to Theorem 5.3 in
[
8
]
. Any attached subalgebra of an Einstein metric Lie algebra meeting the hypotheses of Theorem
4.2
will also be Einstein.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian
metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type.
Let
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
be the attached metric subalgebra defined by
\Lambda^{\prime}

as in Definition
3.1
.
If the Jacobi Star Condition holds, and
if
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is Einstein with Einstein constant
\lambda
, then
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
is Einstein with Einstein constant
\lambda
.

In the proof of this corollary, we will use a characterization of Einstein scalar products from
[
4
]
, presented in Theorem
4.4
below. The hypotheses of this theorem require that the solvable
metric Lie algebra in question admit
a
pseudo-Iwasawa decomposition
:
\mathfrak{s}
is the sum of an abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}
and a nilpotent ideal; and
\operatorname{ad}_{A}=(\operatorname{ad}_{A})^{*}
for all
A\in{\mathfrak{a}}
.
By properties (i) and (ii) in Definition
2.1
, for any solvable pseudo-Riemannian metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type, the decomposition
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}]
is a pseudo-Iwasawa decomposition.

The trace form for a metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is defined by
\langle F,G\rangle_{\operatorname{trace}}^{\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g})}=\operatorname{trace}(F\circ G)

for
F
and
G
in
\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g})
. The identity map for
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted by
\operatorname{Id}^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
4
, Theorem 3.9]

Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable metric Lie algebra with a pseudo-Iwasawa decomposition.
Then

(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is Einstein with Einstein constant
\lambda
if and only if
\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\lambda\operatorname{Id}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\operatorname{ad}_{H}
and

\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A},\operatorname{ad}_{B}\rangle_{\operatorname{trace}}^{\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{n})}=\lambda\langle A,B\rangle
for all
A
,
B
in
\mathfrak{a}.

Now we are ready for the proof of the corollary.

Proof of Corollary
4.3
.

Suppose that
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
and
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
satisfy the hypotheses of Corollary
4.3
.
Since
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is Einstein with Einstein constant
\lambda
, by Theorem
4.4
,
\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}=\lambda\operatorname{Id}^{\mathfrak{n}}+\operatorname{ad}_{H}
and

\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A},\operatorname{ad}_{B}\rangle_{\operatorname{trace}}^{\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{n})}=\lambda\langle A,B\rangle
for all
A
and
B
in
\mathfrak{a}.

By Theorem
4.2
,
\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\operatorname{ad}_{(H-H^{\prime})}.

Restricting to
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
gives the equality

\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}-\operatorname{ad}_{H^{\prime}}=\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\operatorname{ad}_{H}=\lambda\operatorname{Id}^{\mathfrak{n}}

and thus

\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}=\lambda\operatorname{Id}^{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}+\operatorname{ad}_{H^{\prime}}
.

Choose a compatible orthonormal basis
\{E_{i}\}=\{E_{i}^{\prime}\}\cup\{E_{i}^{\perp}\}
for
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0}.

By Proposition
3.2
,
[\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{n}_{0}]=\{0\}
, so for
A
and
B
in
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
,

\operatorname{ad}_{A}(\operatorname{ad}_{B}E_{i}^{\perp})=0
for all
i.

Using this, we rewrite the trace form

\displaystyle\operatorname{trace}(\operatorname{ad}_{A}|_{\mathfrak{n}}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{B}|_{\mathfrak{n}})

\displaystyle=\sum\epsilon_{i}\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A}\operatorname{ad}_{B}E_{i}^{\prime},E_{i}^{\prime}\rangle+\sum\epsilon_{i}\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A}\operatorname{ad}_{B}E_{i}^{\perp},E_{i}^{\perp}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\epsilon_{i}\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A}\operatorname{ad}_{B}E_{i}^{\prime},E_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}(\operatorname{ad}_{A}|_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{B}|_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}).

Thus,

\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A},\operatorname{ad}_{B}\rangle_{\operatorname{trace}}^{\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{n})}=\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A},\operatorname{ad}_{B}\rangle_{\operatorname{trace}}^{\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})}.

Because the scalar product on
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
is the restriction of the scalar product on
\mathfrak{s}
,
\langle A,B\rangle=\langle A,B\rangle^{\prime}
for any
A
and
B
in
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
. Hence,
\langle\operatorname{ad}_{A},\operatorname{ad}_{B}\rangle_{\operatorname{trace}}^{\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})}=\lambda\langle A,B\rangle^{\prime}
.

By Theorem
4.4
,
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
is Einstein with Einstein constant
\lambda
.
∎

We now prove the main theorem.

Proof of Main Theorem:

We first show that (i) and (ii) are equivalent using Theorem
2.4
. Let
A
and
B
be in
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
and let
X
and
Y
be in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. First observe that

\operatorname{ric}^{\mathfrak{s}}(A,X)=0=\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}(A,X).

Next, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ric}^{\mathfrak{s}}(A,B)-\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\ \operatorname{trace}^{\mathfrak{n}}(\operatorname{ad}_{A}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{B})-\operatorname{trace}^{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}(\operatorname{ad}_{A}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{B})

\displaystyle=\ \operatorname{trace}^{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}(\operatorname{ad}_{A}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{B}).

When
X\in\mathfrak{n}_{0},
by Part (iii) of Prop
3.2
,
[A,X]=0
so
\operatorname{trace}^{\mathfrak{n}_{0}}(\operatorname{ad}_{A}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{B})=0
and
\operatorname{ric}^{\mathfrak{s}}(A,B)=\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}(A,B).

Last,

\operatorname{ric}^{\mathfrak{s}}(X,Y)-\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}(X,Y)=\operatorname{ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}(X,Y)-\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X,Y)-\left(\langle\operatorname{ad}_{H}X,Y\rangle-\langle\operatorname{ad}_{H^{\prime}}X,Y\rangle\right).

Hence the Ricci curvatures
\operatorname{ric}^{\mathfrak{s}}
and
\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}

agree if and only if

\operatorname{ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}(X,Y)-\operatorname{ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X,Y)=\langle\operatorname{ad}_{H-H^{\prime}}X,Y\rangle.

We show (ii) and (iii) are equivalent. Let
\{E_{j}\}=\{E_{j}^{\perp}\}\cup\{E_{j}^{\prime}\}
denote a compatible orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{n}={\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
.
Using Equations
4.1
and
4.2
,

H-H^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha(Z)=0}(\dim{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})H_{\alpha}=-\sum\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}E_{j}^{\perp}.

Combining this with Theorem
4.2
we rewrite the Jacobi Star Condition as

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{H-H^{\prime}}X

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum\epsilon_{j}\left((\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}-(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})^{*}\circ(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\perp}})\right)X

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)

for all
X
in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
.
Thus
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}}(X)-\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}}(X)=\operatorname{ad}_{H-H^{\prime}}(X)

for all
X
in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
if and only if the Jacobi Star Condition holds.
∎

4.3.
Subalgebra geometry

In this section, we show that an attached submanifold of an Einstein solvmanifold is a minimal submanifold. We also prove that

\Lambda^{\prime}

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

(iii)

When

Proof.

Let

[{\mathfrak{s}},{\mathfrak{s}}]

(ii)
Since

(iii) We show that

\beta

(iv) To see that

Lemma 4.6

Let

Theorem 4.7
.

An attached submanifold of a solvmanifold is minimal.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be a solvable pseudo-Riemannian
metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type.
Let
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
be the attached metric subalgebra.

\{A_{i}\}
and
\{E_{j}^{\prime}\}
be orthonormal bases
for
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathfrak{n}}^{\prime}

\operatorname{trace}(h)=\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}h(A_{i},A_{i})+\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}h(E_{j}^{\prime},E_{j}^{\prime}).

By Lemma
4.6
,
h(A_{i},A_{i})=0
for each

, and

h(E_{j}^{\prime},E_{j}^{\prime})=-\pi_{{\mathfrak{a}}\ominus{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}}((\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\prime}})^{*}E_{j}^{\prime})
. Therefore,

the projection
\pi_{{\mathfrak{a}}\ominus{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}}\left(-\sum_{j}\epsilon_{j}(\operatorname{ad}_{E_{j}^{\prime}})^{*}E_{j}^{\prime}\right)

The next proposition, a generalization of
Proposition 6.4 of
[
8
]
, characterizes totally geodesic attached submanifolds.

Proposition 4.8
.

The attached metric subalgebra
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is totally geodesic in
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})

if and only if

\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
are orthogonal.

Proof.

Suppose that the sets
\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
are orthogonal.
By definition of the dual scalar product on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, the scalar product

\langle H_{\alpha},H_{\beta}\rangle=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=0
for any
\alpha\in\Lambda^{\prime}
and any
\beta\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
.
By the definition of attached,
\Lambda
is a basis for
\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
and therefore,
\{H_{\alpha_{i}}\mid\alpha_{i}\in\Lambda\}
is a basis for
\mathfrak{a}
. It follows that
\mathfrak{a}
is the orthogonal direct sum of

\operatorname{span}\{H_{\alpha_{j}}\mid\alpha_{j}\in\Lambda^{\prime}\}
and
\operatorname{span}\{H_{\alpha_{i}}\mid\alpha_{i}\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}\}.

To prove that
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}
is totally geodesic in
\mathfrak{s}
, we show that the second fundamental form
h
for
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}
vanishes. We already know from Lemma
4.6
that

h(A,A)=0
for any
A\in\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
and

h(A,X)=0
for any
A\in\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
and
X
in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
. It remains to show that
h(X,Y)=0
for
X
and
Y
in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
.
By the definitions of
U
and
U^{\prime}
, the vector
U^{\prime}(X,Y)
is the orthogonal projection of
U(X,Y)
to
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}.
It follows from the definition of the second fundamental form that to show
h(X,Y)=0
for
X
and
Y
in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
, it suffices to show that
U(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
.
To show
U(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
it is enough to demonstrate that this holds for

X
and
Y
in root spaces in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}.

Let
X
be in
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha_{i}}
and let
Y
be in
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha_{j}},
where
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha_{i}}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha_{j}}
are in
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
(and
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
).
First we consider
h(X,Y)
in the case that
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}.
By a
polarization argument, it suffices to show that
h(X,X)=0
.
By Part (iii) of
Lemma
4.6
,
it suffices to show that
(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}X\in\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
. Because
(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}X\in\mathfrak{a}
,
we want to establish that

\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}X,B_{\alpha_{j}}\rangle=0

for
B_{\alpha_{j}}\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}
(so
\alpha_{j}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
,

\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}
). But

\displaystyle\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}X,B_{\alpha_{j}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle X,[B_{\alpha_{j}},X]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle X,\alpha_{i}(B_{\alpha_{j}})X\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle X,\delta_{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}}X\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

Now suppose
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}.
The root spaces

\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha_{i}}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{\alpha_{j}}
are orthogonal.
By Lemma
4.5
, because
X
and
Y

are in distinct root spaces,
U(X,Y)
is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{a}
.
Now suppose that
W\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\beta}
is in
{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
and that

\langle U(X,Y),W\rangle\neq 0.
By Lemma
4.5
, this
is nontrivial only if
\beta=\pm(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})
.
Without loss of generality, suppose that
\beta=\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}.

In that case, the nontrivial vector

H_{\beta}=H_{\alpha_{i}}-H_{\alpha_{j}}
is in
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
, contradicting
the orthogonality of
\operatorname{span}\{H_{\alpha_{j}}\mid\alpha_{j}\in\Lambda^{\prime}\}

and
\operatorname{span}\{H_{\alpha_{i}}\mid\alpha_{i}\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}\}
established in the first paragraph.
Thus
U(X,Y)
is orthogonal to
{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
.
We have shown that
U(X,Y)
is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0};

hence it is in
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
, and
h(X,Y)=0
as desired.

Conversely, when
\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}
are not orthogonal, there are roots

\alpha\in\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\beta\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{\prime}

so that the corresponding root vectors
H_{\alpha}
and
H_{\beta}
are
not orthogonal. Let
E^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha}
. By Lemma
4.6
,

h(E^{\prime}_{i},E^{\prime}_{i})=\pi_{{\mathfrak{a}}\ominus{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}}(-(\operatorname{ad}_{E^{\prime}_{i}})^{*}E^{\prime}_{i})=\pi_{{\mathfrak{a}}\ominus{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}}(\epsilon_{i}H_{\alpha})
. We know that

\pi_{{\mathfrak{a}}\ominus{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}}(\epsilon_{i}H_{\alpha})
is nonzero because
\langle H_{\alpha},H_{\beta}\rangle
is nonzero, and
H_{\beta}
is in
{\mathfrak{a}}\ominus{\mathfrak{a}}^{\prime}
. Thus,
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}
is not totally geodesic in

{\mathfrak{s}}
.
∎

5.
Special Case: Symmetric spaces

The Main Theorem applies to symmetric spaces of noncompact type, the setting of
[
8
]
.
We derive Tamaru’s Theorem 5.3 from
[
8
]
as a corollary to our Main Theorem.

5.1.
Symmetric spaces of noncompact type as solvable metric Lie algebras

Any symmetric space
(M,g)
of noncompact type is isometric to a solvmanifold defined by a solvable metric Lie algebra. Let
M=G/K
be a symmetric space of noncompact type, where
G
is the connected component of the identity in the isometry group of
M.
The corresponding Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is semisimple. Let
\sigma
denote the Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
. The Killing form
B
defines a positive definite, symmetric bilinear form
B_{\sigma}
on
\mathfrak{g}
:

B_{\sigma}(X,Y)=-B(X,\sigma(Y)),\text{ for any $X$ and $Y$ in $\mathfrak{g}$}.

For any
X,Y
and
Z
in
{\mathfrak{g}}
,

B_{\sigma}([Z,X],Y)=-B_{\sigma}(X,[\sigma(Z),Y]).

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}
be the Iwasawa decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}.

The rule

(5.1)

\langle~,~\rangle=2B_{\sigma}|_{\mathfrak{a}\times\mathfrak{a}}(~,~)+B_{\sigma}|_{\mathfrak{n}\times\mathfrak{n}}(~,~).

defines an inner product on the solvable subalgebra
\mathfrak{s}={\mathfrak{a}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}
.
The metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s},\langle~,~\rangle)

corresponds to a solvmanifold isometric to
(M,g)
.

Now let
X
be an element of
\mathfrak{n}
and consider
\operatorname{ad}_{X}|_{\mathfrak{s}}
. For
Y\in\mathfrak{n}
and
Z
in
\mathfrak{s},

\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{*,\mathfrak{s}}(Y),Z\rangle=B_{\sigma}([X,Z],Y)=-B_{\sigma}(Z,[\sigma(X),Y]).

In particular, when
Z=A\in\mathfrak{a}
,

\displaystyle\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}Y,A\rangle

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle A,[\sigma(X),Y]\rangle

and when
Z=V
in
\mathfrak{n}
,

\displaystyle\langle(\operatorname{ad}_{X})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}Y,V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle V,[\sigma(X),Y]\rangle.

As a consequence, for any
X
and
Y
in
\mathfrak{n}
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*,\mathfrak{s}}(Y)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}[\sigma(X),Y]_{{\mathfrak{a}}}-[\sigma(X),Y]_{{\mathfrak{n}}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{Y}^{*,\mathfrak{s}}(Y)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}[\sigma(Y),Y]

(5.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{X}^{*,\mathfrak{n}}(Y)

\displaystyle=-[\sigma(X),Y]_{{\mathfrak{n}}}

We have used the fact that
[\sigma(Y),Y]
is in
{\mathfrak{a}}
for the second equality.
The subalgebra
{\mathfrak{n}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Delta^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\lambda}
is the sum of the positive root spaces for the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{a}
on
\mathfrak{n}.
Let
\Lambda\subseteq\Delta^{+}
be a set of simple roots.
It is straightforward to verify that
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is of strong Iwasawa type, and
\Lambda
has the properties in Definition
3.1
.
Hence, any proper
subset
\Lambda^{\prime}
of
\Lambda
defines an
attached solvable metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
as in Definition
3.1
.

5.2.
Recovering Tamaru’s Theorem 5.3

We derive Tamaru’s Theorem 5.3 as a special case of our Main Theorem.

Corollary 5.1
.

[
8
, Thm 5.3]

Let
(M,g)
be a symmetric space of noncompact type and let
the solvable metric Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
be as described in the previous subsection so that it defines a solvmanifold isometric to
(M,g)
. Let
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
be an attached subalgebra.
For any
X\in{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}
, the restriction of the Ricci endomorphism for
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
to
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
coincides with the Ricci endomorphism for
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
. Because
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is Einstein,
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
is Einstein with the same Einstein constant.

Proof.

We show that the Jacobi Star Condition holds. Let
X
and
Y
be in
\mathfrak{n}
. Then Equation (
5.2
) gives

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}((\operatorname{ad}_{Y})^{\ast,\mathfrak{n}}\operatorname{ad}_{Y}-\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y})^{\ast,\mathfrak{n}})(X)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}(\operatorname{ad}_{\sigma(Y)}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{Y}-\operatorname{ad}_{Y}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{\sigma(Y)})(X)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl([\sigma(Y),[Y,X]]-[Y,[\sigma(Y),X]]\bigr).

Rewriting the right side
using the Jacobi Identity and also using Equation
(
5.2
), we get

\tfrac{1}{2}((\operatorname{ad}_{Y})^{\ast,\mathfrak{n}}\operatorname{ad}_{Y}-\operatorname{ad}_{Y}(\operatorname{ad}_{Y})^{\ast,\mathfrak{n}})(X)=-\tfrac{1}{2}[[\sigma(Y),Y],X]=\operatorname{ad}_{((\operatorname{ad}_{Y})^{\ast,\mathfrak{s}}Y)}(X).

Summing
over appropriate basis vectors in the place of
Y
yields
the Jacobi Star Condition. By the Main Theorem,
\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}}(X)=\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}(X)
for any
X\in{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}
.
∎

6.
An example

To show that our generalization extends beyond symmetric spaces,
we present an example of a solvable metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type that meets the hypotheses of the Main Theorem. This example is obtained from the affine untwisted Kac-Moody
algebra
\mathfrak{g}
defined by the simple classical Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{3}(\mathbb{R}).
The
infinite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a triangular decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}_{-}+\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{n}_{+}.
We have taken the upper
triangular part
\mathfrak{n}_{+}
and truncated it to obtain a
finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra. We extend by a
codimension-one subalgebra of the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
to define a finite-dimensional solvable Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
. We endow it with a natural inner product.

We will first present a standalone definition of the solvable metric Lie
algebra
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
obtained from
\mathfrak{g}.
Having defined

(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
, we will then
define the attached subalgebra
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}
defined by a
set of roots
\Lambda^{\prime}
. We will next
compute Ricci curvatures and verify that
the Jacobi Star Condition holds. Last, we will show that
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is
Einstein, but not a symmetric space, and that the attached metric subalgebra
is not totally geodesic.

6.1.
Definitions

In what follows,
E_{ij}
will denote the matrix in
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
with a one in the
(i,j)
entry and zeros elsewhere, and
H_{ij}
will denote the diagonal
matrix
E_{ii}-E_{jj}
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}=\operatorname{span}\{1\otimes E_{12},1\otimes E_{13},1\otimes E_{23}\}\bigoplus t\otimes{\mathfrak{sl}}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
, with Lie
brackets be defined by

(6.1)

[t^{i}\otimes X,t^{j}\otimes Y]^{\mathfrak{n}}=t^{i+j}\otimes[X,Y],

where
[X,Y]
denotes the commutator of matrices
X
and
Y
.
Derivations
D_{0},D_{1},D_{2}
of
\mathfrak{n}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle D_{0}(t^{i}\otimes X)

\displaystyle=i(t^{i}\otimes X),

If we write

(6.3)

It is straightforward to confirm that
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is a solvable metric Lie algebra of strong Iwasawa type.

The set of nontrivial roots is

\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\delta,\delta\pm\alpha_{1},\delta\pm\alpha_{2},\delta\pm(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\},

where

\displaystyle\delta(a_{0}\mathbf{D}+a_{1}\mathbf{H}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{H}_{2})

\displaystyle=a_{0},

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(a_{0}\mathbf{D}+a_{1}\mathbf{H}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{H}_{2})

\displaystyle=2a_{1}-a_{2},\,\text{and}

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(a_{0}\mathbf{D}+a_{1}\mathbf{H}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{H}_{2})

\displaystyle=-a_{1}+2a_{2}.

The root space decomposition of
\mathfrak{n}
determined by
\mathfrak{h}
is

\mathfrak{n}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

The root spaces

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}}=\operatorname{span}\{1\otimes E_{12}\},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{2}}=\operatorname{span}\{1\otimes E_{23}\},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}=\operatorname{span}\{1\otimes E_{13}\}.

\mathfrak{g}_{\delta+\alpha_{1}}=\operatorname{span}\{t\otimes E_{12}\},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\delta+\alpha_{2}}=\operatorname{span}\{t\otimes E_{23}\},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\delta+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}=\operatorname{span}\{t\otimes E_{13}\},

\mathfrak{g}_{\delta-\alpha_{1}}=\operatorname{span}\{t\otimes E_{21}\},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\delta-\alpha_{2}}=\operatorname{span}\{t\otimes E_{32}\},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{\delta-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}=\operatorname{span}\{t\otimes E_{31}\},

are one-dimensional and there is a single two-dimensional root space

\mathfrak{g}_{\delta}=\operatorname{span}\{t\otimes H_{12},t\otimes H_{23}\}.

It will be convenient to let

\alpha_{0}=\delta-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}
.
The root vectors for the special roots
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
and
\delta

\mathbf{H}_{\alpha_{0}}=\tfrac{9}{16}\mathbf{D}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{H}_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{H}_{2}

\mathbf{H}_{\delta}=\tfrac{9}{16}\mathbf{D}
.
The root vectors for all other roots can be obtained from sums of these.

An orthonormal basis for

Hence,

\mathfrak{s}.

It may be shown that the mean curvature vector for
\mathfrak{s}

and the endomorphism
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{H}}
is diagonal when represented with respect to the ordered basis

[\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{H}}|_{\mathfrak{n}}]_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}=\operatorname{diag}\left(1,1,2,\textstyle{\frac{5}{2}},\textstyle{\frac{7}{2}},\textstyle{\frac{7}{2}},\frac{9}{2},\frac{9}{2},\textstyle{\frac{11}{2}},\textstyle{\frac{11}{2}},\textstyle{\frac{13}{2}}\right).

(6.4)

We show that

. Because

[\operatorname{Ric}^{\mathfrak{n}}-\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{H}}]_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}=\operatorname{diag}\left(-\tfrac{9}{2},-\tfrac{9}{2},-\tfrac{9}{2},-\tfrac{9}{2},-\tfrac{9}{2},-\tfrac{9}{2},-\tfrac{9}{2},-\tfrac{9}{2}\right),

To show that

for all

\{\mathbf{D},\mathbf{H}_{1},\mathbf{H}_{2}\}
for

By Theorem

, the metric Lie algebra

Let

6.3.
Properties of the attached subalgebra

To meet the definition of attached, we need to show that the reflection

All of the root spaces are one-dimensional except for

\dim({\mathfrak{n}}_{\alpha})=\dim({\mathfrak{n}}_{\check{s}_{\alpha_{j}}(\alpha)})

{\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}}(X)=\operatorname{Ric}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}(X)}

In this example, the nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
has dimension
11

and has five steps. There are ten nontrivial root spaces for the action of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{n}
, and ten of these
root spaces are one-dimensional and one is two-dimensional.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{n}
is the nilpotent part of the Iwasawa decomposition
of a semisimple Lie group
\mathfrak{g}
of noncompact type. Because the dimension of the center of
\mathfrak{n}
is one,

\mathfrak{g}
must be simple.
The subspace
\operatorname{span}\{1\otimes E_{12},1\otimes E_{23},t\otimes E_{31}\}
generates
\mathfrak{n}
and
is the sum of two
\operatorname{ad}_{\mathbf{H}}
eigenspaces, so any
extension of
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{H}\}
to a Cartan-subalgebra in
\mathfrak{g}
has dimension two or three. Hence
\mathfrak{g}
has rank
1
,
2
or
3.

It can’t be rank one, because
\mathfrak{n}
is not two step. If
\mathfrak{g}

were rank two, the corresponding symmetric space would be

13
-dimensional. If
\mathfrak{g}
were rank three then the
corresponding symmetric space would be
14
-dimensional.
Comparing with
[
5
]
, Tables V and VI,
or Besse
[
2
]
, Tables 7.104 and 7.105, we deduce that
there are symmetric spaces of noncompact type with
dimension
13
other than hyperbolic space, and there no rank three
symmetric spaces of dimension 14. Thus,
\mathfrak{n}
is not the nilpotent Lie algebra in the Iwasawa decomposition of any real semisimple Lie algebra of noncompact type.

In conclusion, we have shown that
(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
is an Einstein metric Lie
algebra, and that
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle}^{\prime})
is a metric
subalgebra whose
Ricci endomorphism is the restriction of the Ricci endomorphism for

(\mathfrak{s},{\langle\cdot{,}\cdot\rangle})
. Hence the metric
subalgebra is also Einstein with the same
Einstein constant. Furthermore, this metric subalgebra is minimal but
not totally geodesic. Finally, we showed that the associated

is not the nilpotent Lie algebra from a symmetric space of noncompact type.

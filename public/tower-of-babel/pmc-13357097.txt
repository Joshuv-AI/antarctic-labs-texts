Prognostic and therapeutic implications of PSMD14 in hepatocellular carcinoma: an integrative analysis of transcriptomic and single-cell profiles
Authors: Yongfu Lu, Bohao Sun, Jing Zhang, Lihong Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLiver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) represents a common and aggressive malignancy characterized by an unfavorable clinical outcome, highlighting an urgent need to discover reliable prognostic indicators and novel therapeutic targets. The present investigation centers on the JAMM family genes, with a specific emphasis on PSMD14, to delineate their functional contributions in the context of LIHC.MethodsWe utilized an integrated analytical framework, incorporating bulk RNA sequencing data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) datasets, to systematically investigate the expression profiles, prognostic significance, and underlying functional roles of JAMM family deubiquitinases in LIHC. A prognostic risk score model was developed through least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO)-penalized Cox regression analysis. To elucidate the biological functions and signaling pathways linked to PSMD14, we performed comprehensive functional enrichment analyses, encompassing Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA). The landscape of immune cell infiltration within the tumor microenvironment was evaluated using single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA). Furthermore, scRNA-seq data were leveraged to dissect cellular heterogeneity and examine the expression patterns of PSMD14 across distinct cell populations. Finally, immunohistochemical staining was applied to validate PSMD14 protein expression levels in clinical tissue samples.ResultsOur findings demonstrated that PSMD14 and EIF3H were markedly upregulated in LIHC tissues, with PSMD14 emerging as a critical prognostic indicator. A risk stratification model constructed from JAMM family genes effectively categorized patients into distinct high- and low-risk cohorts, where the high-risk group displayed significantly shorter overall survival. The dysregulated expression of PSMD14 is partly attributable to copy number alterations and DNA methylation patterns, showing associations with multiple DNA methyltransferases. Functional enrichment analyses indicated that elevated PSMD14 expression is involved in metabolic pathways and oncogenic signaling cascades. Moreover, PSMD14 expression was linked to an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, exhibiting a positive correlation with Th2 cells and an inverse relationship with cytotoxic immune cells. scRNA-seq validated the predominant expression of PSMD14 in malignant epithelial cells. A nomogram incorporating PSMD14 expression and clinical variables was developed to predict patient survival probabilities at 1 and 5 years. Additionally, immunohistochemical analysis of 55 clinical samples revealed that PSMD14 protein levels were substantially higher in LIHC tissues compared to adjacent non-tumorous tissues.ConclusionsThese results indicate that PSMD14 may serve as a promising prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for LIHC, highlighting the need for further exploration of its underlying molecular mechanisms and therapeutic applications.Keywords: PSMD14, poor-prognosis predictor, clinical stage, single cell sequencing, liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC)

Highlight box

Key findings

• PSMD14 functions as a crucial biomarker for the screening, diagnosis, and prognostic assessment of hepatocellular carcinoma. Additionally, a nomogram integrating the PSMD14 risk score was constructed and validated to forecast the overall survival of liver cancer patients.

What is known and what is new?

• Elevated expression of PSMD14 is correlated with adverse clinical outcomes across multiple malignancies, including hepatocellular carcinoma.

• The findings of this investigation demonstrate a marked increase in PSMD14 expression within hepatocellular carcinoma, which correlates strongly with advanced pathological staging, heightened immune cell infiltration, and poorer clinical outcomes. These observations indicate that PSMD14 could function as a viable diagnostic biomarker and a prospective therapeutic target for liver cancer. Moreover, a dynamic nomogram incorporating PSMD14 risk scores alongside key clinicopathological variables was constructed to provide a comprehensive evaluation of the influence of PSMD14 on disease progression in liver cancer. Additionally, elevated PSMD14 protein levels were consistently observed in individuals diagnosed with liver cancer.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The prognostic utility of PSMD14 as a biomarker in hepatocellular carcinoma has been confirmed.

• The accuracy and safety of this nomogram need to be confirmed through further large-scale clinical trials.

Introduction

Liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) represents one of the most prevalent and lethal malignancies worldwide, characterized by a complex pathogenesis and poor prognosis (1-3). The burden of LIHC on patients and healthcare systems is substantial, necessitating the identification of effective prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets to enhance patient outcomes. Current treatment modalities, including surgical resection, liver transplantation, and systemic therapies, often yield limited efficacy due to late-stage diagnosis and the heterogeneity of tumor biology (4-6). Despite advancements in understanding the molecular underpinnings of LIHC, significant gaps remain in elucidating the roles of specific gene families, such as the JAMM family, in the disease’s progression and prognosis. Notably, while JAMM family genes, particularly PSMD14, have been implicated in various cancers, their comprehensive evaluation in LIHC is lacking. This study aims to address these gaps by systematically investigating the expression patterns, prognostic significance, and functional mechanisms of PSMD14 in LIHC, thereby providing insights that could inform future therapeutic strategies and improve clinical management of this challenging disease.

The JAMM family represents a significant subclass of deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs), of which its involvement in cancer initiation and progression has garnered growing interest in recent years (7). Research indicates that several members of the JAMM family were overexpressed in various malignancies and were strongly linked to unfavorable clinical outcomes. As a DUB within this family, STAMBP (STAM-binding protein) plays a critical role in the metastatic process of melanoma (8). In triple-negative breast cancer, STAMBP has been recognized as a pivotal driver of tumor growth, facilitating disease progression by stabilizing the RAI14 protein (9). Clinical analyses further demonstrate that elevated STAMBP expression correlates significantly with poor prognosis in breast cancer patients (10). Members of the JAMM family modulate several essential signaling pathways via deubiquitination, thereby influencing oncogenesis and cancer development. For instance, STAMBPL1 is upregulated in gastric cancer, where it maintains IQGAP1 protein levels through deubiquitination, leading to activation of the JAK2/STAT3 signaling axis and promoting malignant advancement (11). In hepatocellular carcinoma, STAMBPL1 is transcriptionally controlled by SREBP1 and enhances tumor cell proliferation and migration by activating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway (12). Another JAMM family member, EIF3H, contributes to anaplastic thyroid carcinoma by deubiquitinating and stabilizing β-catenin, thereby activating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade, accelerating tumor progression, and increasing resistance to ferroptosis (13). This discovery highlights a novel regulatory function of the JAMM family in modulating cell death resistance. Given the essential roles of JAMM family members across multiple cancer types, they have emerged as promising candidates for therapeutic targeting.

PSMD14, a critical component of the deubiquitinase family, is significantly involved in the pathogenesis of multiple malignancies (14). Recent research indicates that PSMD14 modulates signaling cascades and protein homeostasis, thereby influencing tumor growth, invasion, metastatic potential, and chemoresistance (15). Numerous investigations have established that PSMD14 is overexpressed in various cancer types, particularly in bladder cancer, where its levels were markedly elevated compared to normal tissues and correlate with larger tumor size and a positive family history of cancer (14). In lung adenocarcinoma, elevated PSMD14 expression is linked to disease progression and unfavorable clinical outcomes (16). In head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, high PSMD14 mRNA levels were observed in 44% of patients, copy number variations (CNVs) in 50%, and increased protein expression in 56%, all associated with advanced disease stages (17). Through its deubiquitinating activity, PSMD14 stabilizes several key regulatory proteins, leading to the activation of multiple oncogenic pathways. In breast cancer, PSMD14 facilitates tumor advancement by diminishing K63-linked ubiquitination of FOXM1 and stimulating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis (18). In lung adenocarcinoma, it activates both the TGF-β/Smad and PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathways while concurrently stabilizing HMMR protein (19). In anaplastic thyroid carcinomas, PSMD14 drives tumor progression by deubiquitinating E2F1 and activating the ERK/AKT signaling cascade (20). Nevertheless, the precise molecular mechanisms and biological roles of PSMD14 in the initiation, progression, and therapeutic management of hepatocellular carcinoma remain inadequately characterized, and its potential as a therapeutic target warrants further investigation and validation.

LIHC represents a significant global health burden, marked by elevated incidence and mortality, underscoring the critical need to discover reliable prognostic indicators and therapeutic targets to enhance patient outcomes. This research adopts an integrated bioinformatics strategy, combining transcriptomic and single-cell profiles to systematically explore the involvement of JAMM family genes, with a particular focus on PSMD14, in LIHC pathogenesis. The methodological strengths of this investigation were demonstrated through its multi-faceted analytical framework, which encompasses the identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) via the DESeq2 tool, development of a prognostic risk model by employing least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, and performance of functional enrichment analyses using Gene Ontology (GO) and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG). Furthermore, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data were leveraged to clarify cellular diversity and the expression profile of PSMD14. The central aim of this work is to evaluate the expression patterns, prognostic relevance, and functional roles of JAMM family genes in LIHC, offering novel perspectives and potential therapeutic avenues for advancing precision medicine in liver cancer treatment. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1-0305/rc).

Methods

Data collection and clinical sample

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data and corresponding clinical information for 427 LIHC samples were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). This collection includes 377 tumor samples and 50 matched adjacent normal tissue samples. Bulk transcriptomic datasets GSE76427 (comprising 115 tumor tissue samples) and GSE10143 (comprising 80 tumor tissue samples) were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. The scRNA-seq dataset GSE149614, comprising samples from primary tumors, portal vein tumor thrombi (PVTT), metastatic lymph nodes, and non-tumor liver tissues from 10 hepatocellular carcinoma patients, was processed and analyzed using the Seurat package. Tumor tissue specimens were collected from 86 patients diagnosed with LIHC at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles set forth in the Declaration of Helsinki. Given its retrospective design, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the Institutional Ethics Committee. The research protocol received approval from the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, Hangzhou, China (approval number: 2024-1066).

Identification of DEGs in LIHC

In the present investigation, participants were stratified into high- and low-expression cohorts according to the median transcriptional level of the PSMD14 gene. The R package “DESeq2” was employed to identify DEGs between normal and tumor tissue samples (21). DEGs were defined based on a |log2FoldChange| threshold exceeding 1 and an adjusted P value below 0.05. To control for multiple testing, we adjusted the P values derived from the differential analysis using the false discovery rate (FDR) method, with statistical significance defined as an FDR value below 0.05. For visual representation of the DEGs, volcano plots were generated using the “ggplot2” package in R.

Construction and validation of prognostic JAMM family signature

Using LASSO regression, we carefully identified prognostic genes associated with the formation of the JAMM family and constructed a new risk scoring model. The R package “glmnet” was utilized for LASSO regression to aid in variable selection, following the default parameter settings (22). The optimal lambda (λ) value was determined through cross-validation. To evaluate the performance of the LASSO model, we developed a multi-algorithm framework integrating 11 classic machine learning approaches, including LASSO, SVM, RF, glmBoost, StepGLM, ridge regression, Enet, GBM, LDA, XGBoost, and Naive Bayes. Model optimization was conducted via five-fold cross-validation and grid-search-based hyperparameter tuning. An ensemble strategy was subsequently employed using stacking, where a logistic regression meta-classifier combined the predictive outputs from the optimal base-learners. Following this, Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival curves were created using the “survival” and “survminer” R packages to evaluate the survival differences between the two distinct groups (23).

Functional enrichment analysis

To evaluate the differences in molecular functions and enriched pathways between the high and low PSMD14 expression groups, we performed GO and KEGG analyses using the “clusterProfiler” and “org.Hs.eg.db” packages in R (24,25). The gene set for Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/collections.jsp). To ensure statistical rigor, we applied FDR adjustment to the enrichment P values, subsequently isolating only those terms that demonstrated significant enrichment at an FDR threshold of less than 0.05.

Construction and evaluation of nomogram

We constructed a nomogram using the “rms” package. Based on the points assigned to each variable in the nomogram, the 1- and 5-year survival probabilities for each patient were then estimated.

Immune infiltration analyses

An analysis of immune infiltration for PSMD14 was performed using single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) with the ‘GSVA’ R package (26,27). As previously mentioned, the study included 24 different types of infiltrating immune cells. To mitigate the risk of false positives inherent in multiple hypothesis testing, we applied FDR correction to the P values derived from the correlation analyses.

Single cell RNA-seq analysis

A rigorous quality control protocol was applied to screen the cells, ensuring that the proportion of mitochondrial UMIs remained under 10%. After cell selection, Harmony integration was performed to identify genes exhibiting substantial variability. Principal component analysis (PCA) was carried out using the RunPCA function to reduce the dimensionality of the data. The normalized dataset was subsequently integrated via the LogNormalization method. Following this step, cell clustering was accomplished by applying the “FindNeighbors” and “FindClusters” functions (28,29). A resolution parameter of 0.2 was chosen to optimize the effectiveness of cell clustering. Additionally, a bubble plot was created to display the expression patterns of marker genes across various clusters. Cell type annotations were derived by matching cell cluster markers with entries in the CellMarker 2.0 database (http://bio-bigdata.hrbmu.edu.cn/CellMarker/).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Tissue sections, cut to a thickness of 3 µm, were mounted onto glass slides. After deparaffinization with xylene and rehydration, antigen retrieval was performed by microwaving the sections in a 1 × EDTA solution. Once cooled to room temperature, endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked by incubating the sections in 3% hydrogen peroxide for 15 minutes. To reduce non-specific binding, the sections were then blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA) diluted in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 30 minutes. The sections were incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with a primary antibody targeting PSMD14 (Abcam, Cambridge, UK; diluted 1:1,000), Heppar1 (1:1,000; Zhongshan Golden Bridge Biotechnology Co, Beijing, China), and GPC3 (1:1,000; Zhongshan Golden Bridge Biotechnology Co). Following primary antibody incubation, the sections were exposed to horseradish peroxidase-conjugated IgG (Zhongshan Golden Bridge Biotechnology Co) at room temperature for 30 minutes. Staining was carried out using DAB (Zhongshan Golden Bridge Biotechnology Co) and hematoxylin. Finally, the sections were dehydrated through a graded alcohol series and xylene, then mounted with neutral gum.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses and bioinformatics evaluations were performed with R software (version 4.4.0). Differences among multiple groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), with Tukey’s post hoc test applied for subsequent pairwise comparisons. For comparisons between two groups, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was employed. Correlation analyses were carried out using Spearman’s correlation and distance correlation methods. Survival outcomes were evaluated via the Kaplan-Meier method, where survival time was defined as the period from treatment initiation to the end of follow-up.

Results

Identification of DEGs related to JAMM family genes in LIHC

The volcano plot clearly displays the DEGs related to the JAMM family in LIHC. Results indicate that PSMD14 and EIF3H were significantly overexpressed in LIHC, showing notably higher expression levels (Figure 1A). Additionally, the expression of EIF3H, MPND, MYSM1, PRPF8, PSMD14, PSMD7, and STAMBPL1 is substantially increased in LIHC tissues compared to normal tissues (Figure 1B). Spearman correlation analysis revealed positive correlations among the JAMM family genes (Figure 1C). We also investigated the mutation profile of JAMM family genes in LIHC, which exhibited a low mutation frequency (Figure 1D). Subsequently, LASSO Cox regression analysis identified two key genes with the most favorable prognostic value, specifically PSMD14 and EIF3H (Figure 1E,1F). Furthermore, we analyzed the expression patterns of the seven JAMM family genes along with risk scores, survival time, and survival status in the TCGA dataset, highlighting the significant prognostic relevance of PSMD14 (Figure 1G). Moreover, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrates that the high-risk group has significantly worse prognostic outcomes compared to the low-risk group (Figure 1H). To evaluate the performance of the LASSO model, a comprehensive machine learning framework integrating multiple classic algorithms was constructed. Using the transcriptomic data from the TCGA-LIHC cohort as the training set, we employed GSE76427 and GSE10143 as independent external validation sets. Since these external cohorts were sourced and processed independently of the TCGA, the consistent predictive performance observed across these datasets underscores the robustness and generalizability of our model (Figure 1I).

CNV and methylation contribute to driving the abnormal expression of PSMD14 in pan-cancers

To further investigate the mechanisms driving the dysregulation of PSMD14 mRNA expression, we examined the association between CNV and mRNA levels. Analysis of the GSCA database revealed a significant positive correlation between PSMD14 expression and CNV in multiple cancer types, including BLCA, HNSC, LUSC, CESC, LUAD, BRCA, STAD, ESCA, SKCM, OV, and LIHC. In contrast, no significant correlation was observed in UVM, LGG, or THCA (Figure 2A). These findings suggest that CNV is not the sole determinant of abnormal PSMD14 expression, and the underlying regulatory mechanisms may vary across different tumor types. Given that DNA methylation is a key epigenetic modification influencing gene transcription, we next evaluated the relationship between DNA methylation levels and PSMD14 mRNA expression. Our results demonstrated a significant correlation in most tumor types, with particularly strong associations observed in KICH, UCEC, LUSC, and LIHC (Figure 2B). To further elucidate the factors contributing to methylation variability across cancers, we analyzed the correlation between PSMD14 and three DNA methyltransferase genes: DNMT1, DNMT3A, and DNMT3B. Significant correlations were identified in several cancer types, including LUAD, LUSC, GBM, LGG, HNSC, MESO, BRCA, THCA, LIHC, and PAAD (Figure 2C).

Functional enrichment analysis of PSMD14 in LIHC

We further conducted functional enrichment analysis on the DEGs identified between groups with high and low expression of PSMD14. GO analysis demonstrated significant enrichment in multiple biological processes, including alcohol metabolism, steroid metabolism, and fatty acid metabolism (Figure 3A,3B; Table 1). Regarding cellular components, the most prominently enriched terms were high-density lipoprotein particles, plasma lipoprotein particles, and lipoprotein particles (Figure 3A,3B; Table 1). In terms of molecular functions, significant enrichment was observed for oxidoreductase activity and steroid hydroxylase activity (Figure 3A,3B; Table 1). Additionally, KEGG pathway analysis revealed that the DEGs were primarily involved in seven key pathways, such as xenobiotic metabolism by cytochrome P450, chemical carcinogenesis, and drug metabolism (Figure 3A,3B; Table 1). To further elucidate the biological significance of PSMD14 in LIHC, we performed a comprehensive analysis of its gene expression profile. The resulting heatmap displayed the ten genes with the most substantial expression changes, defined by an absolute log fold change greater than 1 and a P value below 0.05 (Figure 3C). To evaluate the impact of differential PSMD14 expression on LIHC progression, we applied GSEA to identify the most significantly enriched biological functions and pathways. The results indicated that the high PSMD14 expression group was markedly enriched in several critical processes and pathways, including pathways in cancer, PI3K-AKT signaling in cancer, cancer immunotherapy via PD-1 blockade, and cell cycle regulation. In contrast, biological oxidation was significantly enriched in the low PSMD14 expression group (Figure 3D).

The association between PSMD14 gene expression and immune cell infiltration

To characterize the immune microenvironment in hepatocellular carcinoma, we performed an analysis of immune cell infiltration within the tumor microenvironment (TME). Using the ssGSEA algorithm, we calculated the Spearman correlation coefficient to assess the association between immune microenvironment features and PSMD14 expression. The tumor immune microenvironment is a critical determinant of therapeutic response and clinical outcomes in cancer patients. Therefore, it is essential to explore the relationship between risk scores and immune cell infiltration patterns. Our results indicate that PSMD14 expression is positively correlated with several immune cell subsets, such as Th2 cells, T helper cells, macrophages, effector memory T cells (Tem), activated dendritic cells (aDC), NK CD56bright cells, and central memory T cells (Tcm). In contrast, PSMD14 expression shows a significant negative correlation with Th17 cells, cytotoxic cells, dendritic cells (DC), plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDC), eosinophils, B cells, NK cells, regulatory T cells (Treg), and CD8+ T cells, as depicted in Figure 4A-4D.

Association of PSMD14 expression with clinical parameters in LIHC

In the TCGA dataset, mRNA expression of PSMD14 was significantly elevated in tumor tissues (Figure 5A). Increased PSMD14 expression was positively associated with higher T stage, advanced pathologic stage, presence of tumor, higher histologic grade, and adjacent hepatic tissue inflammation (Figure 5B-5F, Table 2). Similarly, elevated PSMD14 expression was correlated with poorer overall survival (OS) outcomes (Figure 5G,5H, Table 2). We constructed a clinical prognostic risk score for LIHC, incorporating pathologic stage, tumor status, histologic grade, residual tumor, and PSMD14 expression (Figure 5I). Furthermore, multivariate Cox proportional hazards modeling substantiated that PSMD14 functions as an autonomous predictor of clinical outcomes (Figure 5J). PSMD14 expression may enhance the accuracy of predicting patient survival probabilities at 1- and 5-year intervals. Overall, these findings indicate a correlation between PSMD14 expression and prognosis in LIHC patients.

ScRNA-seq profiling and clustering

The scRNA-seq data were processed using the t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) method, which successfully partitioned the cells into 12 distinct clusters (Figure 6A). This analytical framework is complemented by thorough annotations of the associated cell populations, aiming to enhance our understanding of their specific characteristics and functional roles. Following this, we illustrate the variations in cell distribution from different origins within the t-SNE space, highlighting the cellular composition features of various tissues (Figure 6B). Through an integrated analytical approach, we produced bubble chart that display the characteristic gene expression patterns for each subpopulation (Figure 6C). These graphical representations clearly reveal the differences in expression profiles and intensities of pivotal genes among distinct cell subpopulations, providing an essential and intuitive visual basis for further functional studies. The PSMD14 gene exhibits highly specific overexpression in epithelial cell subpopulations, with particularly notable expression in LIHC cells. This significant finding has been robustly validated by compelling evidence, which clearly indicates that PSMD14 expression levels in LIHC cells were substantially higher than those in other cell types (Figure 6D,6E). By employing bar charts and pie charts, we quantified the abundance and relative proportions of each cellular subpopulation across different tissue samples. For example, in tumor tissues, epithelial cells were the most prevalent, constituting the largest proportion among all identified cell subpopulations (Figure 6F-6H). Additionally, the bubble plot visually depicts the unique distribution patterns of various cellular subpopulations across multiple tissue types (Figure 6I). Together, this sequence of graphical analyses systematically outlines the cellular heterogeneity within four distinct tissue categories, while specifically identifying PSMD14 as a molecular marker that shows increased expression levels in epithelial cells derived from hepatocellular carcinoma.

Discussion

LIHC ranks among the most prevalent and aggressive malignancies worldwide, distinguished by intricate pathogenic mechanisms and unfavorable clinical outcomes (30). The rising global incidence and mortality rates of LIHC underscore an urgent demand for reliable prognostic indicators and novel therapeutic targets to enhance patient survival (31-33). As a group of DUBs, the JAMM gene family has been implicated in multiple cancer types and has garnered increasing research interest (9,10). Nevertheless, the specific role of PSMD14 in LIHC remains insufficiently elucidated. Investigating the expression profiles, prognostic relevance, and functional contributions of JAMM family members in LIHC is of critical importance, as such insights could advance our understanding of the disease’s molecular underpinnings and reveal promising therapeutic strategies.

The results of this research emphasize the critical involvement of PSMD14, a member of the JAMM family, in hepatocellular carcinoma. As a key element of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, PSMD14 has been demonstrated to accelerate the breakdown of certain tumor suppressor proteins while stabilizing oncoproteins, thus contributing to tumor development (34). Our examination of RNA-seq data showed that marked elevation of PSMD14 in LIHC samples correlates with poorer clinical outcomes, indicating its promise as a biomarker for prognosis. Earlier research has suggested that excessive PSMD14 expression can disrupt cellular balance, advancing cancer growth (16,17,35). The identification of PSMD14 as a pivotal prognostic gene in our LASSO-Cox regression analysis further underscores its importance in stratifying patient risk, showcasing its applicability in clinical forecasting. In addition to its prognostic relevance, our exploration of PSMD14’s epigenetic control reveals that CNVs and DNA methylation substantially affect its expression in multiple cancers, including LIHC. The positive link between PSMD14 levels and CNVs implies that genetic changes might be responsible for its abnormal expression. Furthermore, connections with DNA methyltransferases hint at possible intricate regulatory networks that may involve coordinated transcriptional control or feedback mechanisms (36). Grasping these processes is vital, as they could inform therapeutic approaches aimed at adjusting PSMD14 expression, potentially via demethylation treatments or targeted interventions for CNV-induced overexpression.

The functional enrichment analysis performed in this study identified significant links between PSMD14 expression and several core biological pathways, especially those involved in cancer development. A particularly prominent pathway is xenobiotic metabolism mediated by cytochrome P450 enzymes. This metabolic route is essential for detoxifying a wide range of substances, such as drugs and carcinogens, and thereby influences the pharmacokinetic properties of therapeutic compounds. Dysfunction of cytochrome P450 enzymes has been associated with the emergence of drug resistance in multiple cancers, including LIHC (37). Elevated PSMD14 expression could induce changes in metabolic activities, possibly leading to unfavorable treatment outcomes in LIHC patients. Investigating the interplay between PSMD14 and cytochrome P450-mediated metabolism may contribute to the development of tailored treatment plans for LIHC. Another key pathway emphasized in our results is the PI3K-AKT signaling cascade, which is vital for regulating cell proliferation, survival, and metabolic functions. Abnormal activation of this pathway is frequently observed in various cancers, including LIHC, and correlates with adverse clinical outcomes (38-40). The association between increased PSMD14 levels and PI3K-AKT pathway activation implies that PSMD14 might function as an enhancer of oncogenic signaling, promoting tumor expansion and viability. Combining therapeutic strategies that target this pathway with PSMD14 inhibition could represent a promising new method to decelerate LIHC advancement and enhance patient prognosis.

The immune infiltration analysis performed in this study demonstrated significant correlations between PSMD14 expression and the composition of the tumor immune microenvironment in LIHC. This profile implies that PSMD14 may facilitate the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment that supports tumor progression and immune escape. Th2 cells are defined by their secretion of cytokines such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, which are central to humoral immunity and are frequently linked to allergic reactions and parasitic infections (41,42). In cancer settings, Th2 cells can drive tumor growth by stimulating angiogenesis and suppressing cytotoxic T cell activity (43,44). The positive association observed between PSMD14 and Th2 cell infiltration in LIHC indicates that PSMD14 may amplify Th2-mediated responses, thereby fostering an immunosuppressive milieu that benefits tumor survival and expansion. Macrophages, especially tumor-associated macrophages, display considerable plasticity and can differentiate into pro-inflammatory (M1) or anti-inflammatory (M2) subtypes (45,46). Infiltration of M2 macrophages is commonly linked to unfavorable outcomes in multiple cancers, including LIHC, as they facilitate tumor growth, metastasis, and immune suppression (47,48). The positive correlation between PSMD14 and macrophage infiltration suggests that PSMD14 may enhance the recruitment or polarization of macrophages toward the tumor-promoting M2 phenotype, thereby reinforcing the immunosuppressive environment. In contrast, the negative correlation between PSMD14 and cytotoxic immune cells—such as CD8+ T cells and NK cells—underscores its possible involvement in immune evasion. CD8+ T cells play a vital role in eliminating tumor cells, and their depletion within the TME is often correlated with poor clinical prognosis (49,50). The results of this investigation imply that PSMD14 may hinder the recruitment or activation of these cytotoxic cells, enabling LIHC cells to avoid immune detection. Collectively, these observations regarding the immune landscape linked to PSMD14 expression highlight its potential as a therapeutic target to boost anti-tumor immunity in LIHC. Additional studies are required to clarify the exact mechanisms through which PSMD14 modulates immune cell dynamics and to investigate the feasibility of targeting PSMD14 in conjunction with immunotherapy to enhance patient outcomes.

The survival analysis outcomes from our investigation underscore the substantial prognostic relevance of PSMD14 in LIHC. Kaplan-Meier survival curves distinctly reveal that patients categorized into the high-risk group, determined by PSMD14 and EIF3H expression levels, exhibit markedly reduced OS relative to those in the low-risk group. The correlation of PSMD14 with unfavorable clinical characteristics, including advanced T stage and elevated histological grade, further supports its potential utility as a valuable prognostic marker. This is in agreement with findings in other malignancies, where analogous mechanisms have been suggested, indicating that PSMD14 may modulate tumor behavior via its involvement in the ubiquitin-proteasome system, consequently impacting the stability of oncogenic proteins (19,51). Moreover, the risk scoring model formulated based on PSMD14 expression introduces a novel methodology for stratifying patients to facilitate personalized therapeutic approaches. This model not only improves the predictive precision of current staging systems but also creates opportunities for tailored treatment plans. Integrating PSMD14 into clinical protocols could enhance decision-making processes concerning patient management and follow-up, ultimately leading to better prognostic outcomes for individuals with hepatocellular carcinoma.

In this investigation, we employed a stringent bioinformatics pipeline to pinpoint PSMD14, a differentially expressed JAMM family member linked to LIHC. Owing to the restricted gene pool, LASSO-Cox regression was chosen to build a prognostic risk signature; by applying an L1 penalty, this method efficiently shrinks superfluous variables and mitigates multicollinearity, thereby boosting generalizability across independent cohorts while curbing overfitting. Through cross-validation optimization, we struck an ideal equilibrium between predictive precision and model parsimony, yielding a clinically viable Risk Score. Integrating GO and KEGG enrichment assessments with empirical verification via single-cell sequencing and immunohistochemistry, our work elucidates the multifaceted connection between PSMD14 and the TME, simultaneously validating its potential as both a robust prognostic indicator and a therapeutic target for LIHC.

The limitations of this study require careful consideration. First, although our research was primarily based on bioinformatics analysis and data mining from public databases, direct in vivo and in vitro functional experiments are lacking to validate the oncogenic role of PSMD14 and clarify its specific molecular mechanisms, such as identifying substrate proteins targeted for degradation. Second, the clinical relevance analysis relies on retrospective data from sources like TCGA, highlighting the need for prospective clinical cohorts to confirm the prognostic predictive value of PSMD14. Additionally, the heterogeneity of the datasets used may introduce batch effects, which, despite standardization efforts, could slightly affect the consistency of the results. Furthermore, the immune infiltration analysis conducted using computational algorithms such as ssGSEA indicates correlations but lacks experimental validation through techniques like flow cytometry or multiplex immunofluorescence to confirm specific changes in immune cell populations in situ. Collectively, the pathway enrichments revealed by GO, KEGG, and GSEA provide valuable insights into PSMD14-related molecular signatures. However, as these bioinformatics tools characterize co-expression networks rather than mechanistic causality, the identified links remain suggestive. Future functional studies are required to pinpoint the exact roles of PSMD14 in these pathways and to validate its potential as a mechanistic driver in tumor progression.

In summary, this research systematically elucidates the pivotal function of the DUB PSMD14 in LIHC by integrating multi-omics datasets. Overall, these findings indicate that PSMD14 serves not only as a potential prognostic biomarker for LIHC but also as a multifunctional oncogenic factor involved in modulating tumor growth, metabolism, and immune evasion. Future investigations will concentrate on identifying the specific substrates of PSMD14 and delineating its downstream regulatory networks to deepen our comprehension of the mechanisms driving liver cancer progression. Moreover, the development of small-molecule inhibitors targeting PSMD14, or its application as a therapeutic target in combination strategies (such as with immunotherapy or metabolic therapy), may offer novel treatment approaches for patients with liver cancer. This study provides a robust theoretical framework and substantial data support for subsequent translational medical research.

Conclusions

This extensive investigation methodically clarifies the pivotal function of PSMD14, a deubiquitinase belonging to the JAMM family, in the onset and advancement of LIHC. Through the synthesis of transcriptomic profiles and single-cell sequencing datasets, our analysis reveals a marked elevation of PSMD14 levels within LIHC specimens. Such heightened expression correlates strongly with aggressive pathological staging, higher tumor grading, and diminished OS rates, thereby underscoring its prospective value as a prognostic indicator.

Development of a nomogram to assess thromboembolic risk in patients with liver abscesses
Authors: Jaejun Lee, Soon Woo Nam, Jung Hyun Kwon, Dong Jae Shim, Seung Hwan Baek, Soon Kyu Lee
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Thromboembolism, a serious complication in patients with liver abscesses, can lead to organ deterioration if left untreated. However, factors associated with thromboembolism in these patients remain poorly understood. We aimed to identify the factors associated with liver abscess complications, with a focus on thromboembolism and metastatic infection. Data from 325 patients diagnosed with liver abscesses between March 2019 and June 2023 were retrospectively collected. Baseline clinical and laboratory variables associated with thromboembolic events and metastatic infections were analyzed using logistic regression. A nomogram for predicting thromboembolism was constructed using significant predictors. Among the 325 patients, the median age was 68.0 years, and included 129 women. Fifty patients experienced thromboembolic events and 44 had metastatic infections. Significant predictors for thromboembolic events included white blood cell (WBC) ≥ 20,000/µL (odds ratio [OR] 3.401, p = 0.002), platelet count < 100,000/µL (OR 3.291, p = 0.004), and abscess septation (OR 2.704, p = 0.007). Age ≥ 65 years (OR 0.457, p = 0.040), WBC ≥ 20,000/µL (OR 3.340, p = 0.005), and abscess septation (OR 2.909, p = 0.008) were identified as factors associated with metastatic infections. A nomogram was constructed to predict thromboembolism using the following four variables: WBC ≥ 20,000/µL platelet count < 100,000/µL, albumin < 2.8 g/dL, and abscess septation, and demonstrated an AUROC of 0.755. Our study is the first to develop a reliable nomogram for thromboembolism prediction, utilizing easily accessible clinical parameters. This innovative model enables clinicians to efficiently identify high-risk patients, facilitating the implementation of additional studies to mitigate the risk of overlooked thromboembolic events in these at-risk individuals.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-77799-4.

Introduction

Liver abscess, a condition first documented by Hippocrates around 400 BC, remains a significant medical concern. It is characterized by the presence of pus-filled lesions within the liver, typically originating from infectious etiologies, and posing potentially life-threatening complications1,2. The global incidence of liver abscess varies, ranging from 2.3 to 15.4 cases per 100,000 individuals, with notably higher rates observed in Asia3,4. In Korea, the annual occurrence of pyogenic liver abscess has been documented at 10.9 cases per 100,000 individuals, and reflects a rising trend from 5.7 cases in 2007 to 14.4 cases in 20175. Given its escalating incidence, appropriate treatment of liver abscess is of considerable importance.

The management strategy for liver abscess is well established, focusing on the percutaneous drainage of pus-filled lesions, along with the administration of appropriate antibiotics6. However, despite the implementation of this standardized treatment approach, which has resulted in improved prognoses for patients with liver abscesses, the mortality rate associated with the condition remains high, ranging from 2.5 to 16.1%, depending on the medical environment7–9. Furthermore, liver abscess poses heightened risks of mortality and recurrence in older patients, presenting an increasing threat to contemporary society, particularly as the average age in developed countries continues to rise over time10.

Severe complications often manifest in patients diagnosed with liver abscesses and pose a formidable challenge for healthcare providers. Among these complications, thromboembolism is particularly concerning, with a prevalence ranging between 20% and 40% 11–13. Managing thromboembolism in the clinical setting poses considerable complexities. Persistent thrombosis, if left unchecked, can result in compromised blood supply to vital organs, precipitating ischemic damage, and subsequent organ deterioration11,12. This underscores the need for meticulous monitoring and timely intervention in cases of liver abscess complicated by thromboembolism. Metastatic infection is another well-known complication of liver abscesses involving diverse organs, such as endophthalmitis, meningitis, lung empyema, and prostate abscess13. These complications require attention from clinicians because certain manifestations such as endophthalmitis may result in irreversible organ dysfunction. Moreover, metastatic infection has been reported as a risk factor for liver abscess-related death and prolonged hospital stay, underscoring the need for close monitoring and management of these occurrences3,13.

Although these complications have been repeatedly cited as pivotal factors that influence liver abscess prognosis, only a limited number of studies have comprehensively investigated the factors associated with these complications. Particularly concerning thromboembolic events, there is a notable lack of published research exploring the characteristics and risk factors specific to these events in patients with liver abscesses. Furthermore, considering that Klebsiella pneumoniae, characterized by its hypermucoviscous nature, accounts for > 80% of cases of liver abscess in Asian regions, the need to identify the risk factors for thromboembolic events and metastatic infection remains important14. In this context, we aimed to identify factors associated with thromboembolic events and metastatic infections in patients with liver abscesses. Furthermore, utilizing the findings of our investigation, we aimed to develop a nomogram that could effectively predict thromboembolic events.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among the 394 patients, 325 with liver abscesses were included in the analysis after excluding patients meeting the exclusion criteria (Supplementary Fig. 1). Among them, 50 cases of thromboembolic events and 44 cases of metastatic infections were documented. Consequently, 81 patients were classified as having liver abscess complications. Among 50 patients with thromboembolic events, none had medical records of thromboembolism prior to the development of the liver abscess. The baseline characteristics of the patients with liver abscesses are presented in Table 1. The median age of the cohort was 68.0 years, and included 39.7% women. Among comorbidities, diabetes mellitus (DM) was prevalent in 154 patients (47.4%), 20 (6.2%) had underlying chronic liver diseases, and 84 (25.8%) had a history of biliary procedures. Bloodstream infections were documented in 45.5% of the patients with liver abscesses, and, in majority of cases, the pathogen causing liver abscesses was K. pneumoniae (56.0%). Regarding radiological features of abscesses, the median size was 46.0 mm, with the majority presenting as solitary lesions (68.3%), while 33 patients (10.2%) had two and 70 patients (21.5%) had three or more abscesses. Liver abscess septation was observed in 93 patients (28.6%) and gas formation within the abscess was observed in 14 patients (4.3%).

When comparing baseline characteristics, no differences were observed in terms of sex, age, and underlying diseases between the thromboembolic event group (TE group) and non-thromboembolic event group (non-TE group). However, significant differences were noted in several laboratory findings, including WBC count, platelet count, CRP, and albumin levels. The TE group also exhibited a higher proportion of documented bloodstream (43.3% vs. 58.0%, p = 0.077) and K. pneumoniae infections (64.0% vs. 54.5%, p = 0.278) than the non-TE group, although the difference was not statistically significant. Regarding the radiological features of the abscesses, the proportion of septate liver abscesses was higher in the TE group than in the non-TE group (52.0% vs. 24.4%, p < 0.001). A comparison of baseline characteristics according to the presence of metastatic infection is shown in Supplementary Table 1. Consequently, the metastatic infection group demonstrated a lower median age, higher proportion of K. pneumoniae infections, larger abscess size, and higher proportion of septate liver abscesses and gas formation within abscesses.

Clinical manifestations of liver abscess

The sites of thromboembolic events and metastatic infections were assessed (Table 2). Among the thromboembolic events, the hepatobiliary vessels were most commonly involved, with 28 of 50 cases occurring in these vessels. Specifically, nine cases were associated with the portal vein and 19 were associated with the hepatic veins. Brain embolisms were the second most prominent thromboembolic event (nine cases), followed by embolisms in the pulmonary vein or artery (eight cases). Additionally, five cases involved the iliac vessels, two involved the inferior vena cava, and one involved the renal vein.

Factors associated with thromboembolic events

The clinical and laboratory factors associated with thromboembolic events were also investigated (Table 3). In the univariate analysis, several factors emerged as significant risk factors for thromboembolic events. These included WBC ≥ 20,000/µL, platelet count < 100,000/µL, CRP ≥ 150 mg/L, albumin < 2.8 g/dL, abscess size ≥ 70 mm, and abscess septation. In the multivariate analysis, WBC ≥ 20,000/µL (OR 3.401, 95% CI 1.570–7.367, p = 0.002), platelet count < 100,000/µL (OR 3.291, 95% CI 1.470–7.369, p = 0.004), and abscess septation (OR 2.704, 95% CI 1.320–5.538, p = 0.007) were identified as risk factors significantly associated with thromboembolic events.

Factors associated with metastatic infection and liver abscess complications

Factors associated with metastatic infections were also assessed (Table 4). In the univariate analysis, several variables emerged as significant risk factors for metastatic infections, including alcohol consumption > 210 g/week, WBC ≥ 20,000/µL, platelet count < 100,000/µL, K. pneumoniae infection, abscess size ≥ 70 mm, abscess gas formation, abscess septation, and positive culture from abscess drainage. Conversely, age ≥ 65 years and female sex were found to be protective against metastatic infection in the univariate analysis. In the subsequent multivariate analysis, age ≥ 65 years (OR 0.457, 95% CI 0.217–0.964, p = 0.040), WBC ≥ 20,000/µL (OR 3.340, 95% CI 1.441–7.744, p = 0.005), and abscess septation (OR 2.909, 95% CI 1.316–6.430, p = 0.008) were identified as factors associated with metastatic infections.

Furthermore, the factors associated with liver abscess complication, a composite outcome of thromboembolic events, and metastatic infections were assessed (Table 5). Variables such as DM, WBC ≥ 20,000/µL, platelet count < 100,000/µL, CRP ≥ 150 mg/L, albumin < 2.8 g/dL, bloodstream infection, K. pneumoniae infection, abscess size ≥ 70 mm, abscess gas formation, and abscess septation were found to be associated with liver abscess complication in the univariate analysis. In the subsequent multivariate analysis, WBC ≥ 20,000/µL (OR 3.387, 95% CI, 1.654–6.939, p < 0.001), platelet count < 100,000/µL (OR 2.719, 95% CI, 1.242–5.954, p = 0.012), and abscess septation (OR 3.184, 95% CI, 1.686–6.015, p = 0.007) were identified as three risk factors for liver abscess complications.

Developing nomogram for predicting thromboembolic events

Among the clinical and laboratory variables included for the analysis, WBC ≥ 20,000/µL, platelet count < 100,000/µL, abscess septation, and albumin < 2.8 g/dL were selected via backward elimination method. Subsequently, using these four variables, a predictive nomogram of thromboembolic events was constructed (Fig. 1). The total points ranged from 0 to 315, with points allocated to each variable as follows: WBC ≥ 20,000/µL (100 points), albumin < 2.8 g/dL (38 points), platelet count < 100,000/µL (98 points), and abscess septation (79 points). The AUROC of the constructed nomogram was 0.755 (95% CI, 0.683–0.827), indicating good reliability for predicting thromboembolic events in patients with liver abscesses (Fig. 2).

Internal validation

To mitigate overfitting of the derived nomogram, the optimism-corrected predictive performance was derived using 1000 iterations of the bootstrap method. The calculated optimism was found to be 0.018, resulting in an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.737 with a 95% CI of 0.669–0.810.

Discussion

Our comprehensive analysis identified the factors associated with diverse complications that may arise in patients with liver abscesses. Specifically, laboratory parameters such as WBC ≥ 20,000/µL, platelet count < 100,000/µL, and abscess characteristics such as septation were identified as risk factors for thromboembolism in patients with liver abscesses. Furthermore, age ≥ 65 years, WBC ≥ 20,000/µL, and abscess septation emerged as factors associated with metastatic infections. Additionally, our investigation into factors associated with abscess complications, encompassing both thromboembolism and metastatic infections, revealed WBC ≥ 20,000/µL, platelet count < 100,000/µL, and abscess septation as significant risk factors (Fig. 3). To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to investigate the predictive factors of thromboembolic events in patients with liver abscesses.

Moreover, our study documented the prevalence of thromboembolism and metastatic infections, which affected 15.4% and 13.5% of the study cohort, respectively. This observation is consistent with previous studies, which have reported thromboembolic event prevalence rates ranging from 10–40%.9,11–13,18 Additionally, 28 of the 50 thromboembolic events in our study were localized in either the portal vein and/or hepatic vein, which is consistent with previous studies, demonstrating that the portal and hepatic veins comprise the majority of thromboembolic events15,16. Interestingly, thromboembolism in our study also involved uncommon sites, such as the iliac vessels and renal vein. Considering that 63% of thromboembolisms persisted at the 6-month mark in the study by Syed et al., the results of our study underscore the importance of active surveillance in detecting unseen sites of thromboembolism17.

In this regard, our study revealed the variable factors predictive of study outcomes. Previous studies have reported similar outcomes regarding WBC and age as associated factors for metastatic infections18,19. Chang et al. assessed the characteristics of patients with liver abscesses with metastatic infection, revealing a younger age and a higher proportion of men in the metastatic infection group than in the non-metastatic infection group, which is consistent with our observations18. Considering age as a protective factor against metastatic infections, it can be hypothesized that the younger age group exhibits a more robust immune reaction to pyogenic infection owing to relatively preserved immune function compared to older individuals, potentially resulting in a higher likelihood of positive imaging findings outside the liver20,21. Notably, thrombocytopenia at initial evaluation, rather than thrombophilia, was positively associated with thromboembolic events. Given that the majority of thromboembolic events were detected during the initial evaluation, thrombocytopenia in these cases could be interpreted as a consequence of thromboembolism rather than its cause22.

Furthermore, our study provides novel insights into the prognostic value of abscess septation. Liver abscess septation, also known as a multiloculated abscess, is a relatively common feature, particularly in Asian countries23,24. Abscess septation may manifest hyperviscous characteristics due to concentrated accumulation of necrotic debris and inflammatory cells25. Moreover, the management of septate abscesses can be challenging, potentially reducing the possibility of successful abscess drainage26. These factors may contribute to a higher incidence of liver abscess-related complications such as thromboembolic events and metastatic infections.

In line with the identification of predictive factors, we developed a predictive nomogram for thromboembolism. This nomogram incorporates the following four variables: WBC count, platelet count, serum albumin level, and presence of abscess septation, which are routinely assessed at the time of liver abscess diagnosis. The nomogram, which displays good predictive value, can also be readily calculated using simple laboratory findings along with abdominal CT results. Additionally, internal validation indicated acceptable predictive performance with an optimism-corrected AUROC of 0.737. This nomogram may help clinicians identify liver abscess patients at higher risk of thromboembolism, particularly those with two or more risk factors. While it highlights patients who might benefit from closer observation, recommendations for additional imaging studies should be made with consideration of individual clinical judgment. Further external validation is needed to confirm the nomogram’s generalizability and applicability across diverse populations. Nonetheless, it holds promise for the early identification of thromboembolic events, which could help guide timely intervention and improve patient outcomes.

The current study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center study. The use of data from multiple institutes may strengthen the reliability of our findings. Moreover, given the likelihood that the etiologies and characteristics of liver abscesses may vary depending on the geographic location, a study incorporating data from outside of Korea could enhance the generalizability of the results to different regions27. Interestingly, none of the patients in our cohort received thromboprophylaxis immediately after being diagnosed with a liver abscess. Therefore, the nomogram developed in this study should be applied cautiously in countries where thromboprophylaxis is routinely practiced in liver abscess patients. Notably, while alcohol consumption did not demonstrate a significant association with thromboembolic events in our study, a tendency towards thromboembolism was observed. Given the retrospective nature of our study and the heterogeneous findings in previous literature regarding the association between alcohol consumption and thrombosis, future studies with robust designs are warranted to clearly elucidate this correlation28,29. Lastly, as this study was cross-sectional, we were unable to assess the prognosis of patients with thromboembolism. Further research is required to explore the long-term outcomes of patients with liver abscesses and thromboembolism. Despite these limitations, our study presents novel findings by identifying several interesting variables correlated with thromboembolic events. Through detailed analyses, we believe that the study holds strength for developing an easy-to-use nomogram with a sufficient data size on patients with liver abscesses, which we anticipate has the potential for wider use in clinical practice owing to the simple variables that the nomogram comprises.

In conclusion, our study demonstrated the first comprehensive report on readily identifiable clinical factors, including WBC count, platelet count, and septation, closely associated with thromboembolic events and metastatic infections in patients with liver abscesses. Our novel nomogram utilizes easy-to-use clinical parameters and is expected to minimize oversight in detecting thromboembolic events in patients with liver abscesses. By prompting physicians to consider additional examinations for those deemed at high risk of thromboembolic events, this advancement is expected to significantly improve prognostic outcomes associated with this condition.

Methods

Patients

We conducted a retrospective study using data from patients with a diagnosis of liver abscesses who visited Incheon Saint Mary’s Hospital, a tertiary university hospital, between March 2019 and June 2023. The diagnosis of liver abscess was confirmed using imaging modalities, such as ultrasonography and/or computed tomography (CT). The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) insufficient availability of clinical data, such as white blood cell (WBC) count, platelet count, total bilirubin, albumin, and C-reactive protein (CRP); (2) lack of imaging data; and (3) cases misclassified as liver abscess due to ambiguity with conditions such as infected hepatic cysts or cholangiocarcinoma. Generally, patients with liver abscesses were treated in accordance with guidelines published by the Korean Society of Infectious Diseases, which recommends a 4-to-6-week administration of antibiotics with appropriate drainage of the abscess30. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Incheon Saint Mary’s Hospital, The Catholic University of Korea (OC24RASI0030), and adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Given the retrospective nature of this study, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Outcomes assessment

The primary outcomes of this study were the occurrence of thromboembolic events and identification of risk factors associated with such events among patients with liver abscesses. Thromboembolic events were meticulously assessed using radiological imaging modalities, such as CT or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Two experienced radiologists (DJS and SHB) independently reviewed the imaging to confirm the presence of thromboembolic events. Various organ systems, including the central nervous system (CNS), pulmonary vessels, hepatobiliary vessels, and large vessels, were examined for thromboembolic events.

Secondary outcomes included metastatic infection and liver abscess complication. Metastatic infection was evaluated using radiological imaging techniques, such as CT and/or MRI, in conjunction with pathogen culture results from suspected organs. Two radiologists (DJS and SHB) assessed the presence of extrahepatic metastatic infections in the images. Metastatic infection was defined as an extrahepatic complication encompassing conditions such as endophthalmitis, CNS infection, lung abscesses, renal abscesses, and skin and soft tissue infections in patients with liver abscesses. Liver abscess complication was defined as the composite outcome comprising thromboembolic events and/or metastatic infections. The occurrence of these outcomes was assessed during the period of hospitalization (median 13 days, interquartile range 10–19 days).

Clinical and imaging variables

Clinical variables including age, sex, underlying diseases, history of prior biliary procedures, malignancy, and baseline laboratory findings presumed to be associated with thromboembolism were extracted from the medical records. Laboratory data were collected at the time of liver abscess diagnosis or at the closest available time following the diagnosis. Microorganisms were designated as pathogens of liver abscesses if they were identified through blood or abscess cultures. The antibiotic resistance profiles of these pathogens, including extended-spectrum beta-lactamase production, vancomycin resistance, and quinolone resistance, were also compiled for analysis.

Furthermore, we evaluated imaging data to assess various characteristics of abscesses, including septation, gas formation, number of abscesses, and their size, to determine their effect on thromboembolic events and metastatic infections.

Construction and internal validation of the nomogram

A nomogram for predicting thromboembolism in patients with liver abscesses was constructed using the ‘rms’ package in R statistical software (version 4.0.3; R Foundation Inc., Vienna, Austria; http://cran.r-project.org, accessed on February 1, 2024). The variables for the nomogram were selected using a backward elimination strategy. After constructing the nomogram, the bootstrap method was used to generate new models incorporating the same variables. Subsequently, we evaluated the performance of these bootstrap models and calculated the variance in their performance between the bootstrap and original samples to quantify the level of optimism31. Through 1000 iterations of bootstrapping, we derived the optimism-corrected AUC values and coefficients for the model.

Statistical analysis

For continuous variables, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was employed, and the results are expressed as median with interquartile range). Categorical variables were assessed using either the chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests depending on the sample size. Logistic regression analysis was used to identify factors associated with the outcomes, with results reported as odds ratios (OR), 95% confidence intervals (CI), and corresponding p-values. Variables with a p value < 0.05 were included in the multivariable analysis. Receiver operating characteristic curves were generated to visually depict and illustrate the diagnostic performance of the established nomogram. All statistical analyses were conducted using the R statistical software. A two-tailed p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

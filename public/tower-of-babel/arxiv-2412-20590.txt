Universal hyper-scaling relations, power-law tails, and data analysis for strong anomalous diffusion
Jürgen Vollmer, Claudio Giberti, Jordan Orchard, Hannes Reinhard, Carlos Mejía-Monasterio, Lamberto Rondoni

Abstract
Strong anomalous diffusion is often characterized by a piecewise-linear spectrum of the moments of displacement. The spectrum is characterized by slopes ξ \xi and ζ \zeta for small and large moments, respectively, and by the critical moment α \alpha of the crossover. The exponents ξ \xi and ζ \zeta characterize the asymptotic scaling of the bulk and the tails of the probability distribution function of displacements, respectively. Here, we adopt asymptotic theory to match the behaviors at intermediate scales. The resulting constraint explains how distributions with algebraic tails imply strong anomalous diffusion, and it relates α \alpha to the corresponding power law. Our theory provides novel relations between exponents characterizing strong anomalous diffusion, and it yields explicit expressions for the leading-order corrections to the asymptotic power-law behavior of the moments of displacement. They provide the time scale that must be surpassed to clearly discriminate the leading-order power law from its sub-leading corrections. This insight allows us to point out sources of systematic errors in their numerical estimates. Rather than separately fitting an exponent for each moment we devise a robust scheme to determine ξ \xi , ζ \zeta and α \alpha . The findings are supported by numerical and analytical results on five different models exhibiting strong anomalous diffusion.



I
Introduction

In its simplest form, a transport process
x(t)
is said to exhibit anomalous diffusion
if its Mean-Square Displacement (MSD) does not grow linearly in time. In recent years, great theoretical efforts have been made to unveil the physical mechanisms
that can induce anomalous diffusion (see,
e. g.
Refs.
Metzler and Klafter 2000
;
Klages et al. 2008
;
Sokolov 2012
and references therein).
This has been accompanied by the rapid increase of highly-resolved experimental observations, particularly of video microscopy and particle tracking,
as well as by more refined numerical simulations of ensembles of trajectories
Mejía-Monasterio et al. 2020
.
Processes exhibiting anomalous diffusion are observed in a broad range of phenomena including
molecules moving in the living cell
Saxton 2001
;
Golding and Cox 2006
,
dynamics on cell membranes
Nicolau et al. 2007
,
crowded environments
Sokolov 2012
,
soil transport
Martin et al. 2012
,
heat transport in low-dimensional systems
Lepri et al. 2003
, and
in certain classes of billiards
Armstead et al. 2003a
;
Jepps et al. 2008
,
to mention a few.

In these processes the probability distribution function (PDF) of the
displacement is commonly non Gaussian, meaning that the signatures
of anomalous diffusion appear beyond the MSD, essentially in all the
moments of the distribution. Deviations from the normal Gaussian
behavior are observed in the tails and, in certain dynamics, the
large fluctuations of the tails scale differently from the small
fluctuations described by the bulk of the distribution.

In general, the time scaling of the

q^{\text{th}}
moment of displacement can be written as

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\lvert x(t)-x(0)\right\rvert^{q}\right\rangle\sim t^{q\,\nu_{q}/2}\ ,

(1a)

\nu_{q}=1
indicating normal diffusion as derived from Fick’s law.
Conversely, anomalous diffusion corresponds to
\nu_{q}\neq 1
.
It comprises scale-invariant transport processes for which the anomalous behavior of the MSD extends to all moments of the displacement.
When
\nu_{q}
varies with the moment order
q
, scale invariance breaks down,
and transport is said to be strongly anomalous
Castiglione et al. 1999
.
In the literature, the most commonly investigated strong anomalous situation is characterized by two regimes, in which the power of
t
is given by two different expressions, that are linear in the moment order:

\displaystyle\frac{q\,\nu_{q}}{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\xi\,q&\text{ for}&q\leq\alpha\,,\\[2.84526pt]
\zeta\,q-\alpha\,(\zeta-\xi)&\text{ for}&q>\alpha\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\xi
,
\zeta
and
\alpha
are given parameters.
Indeed, strong anomalous diffusion repeatedly arises as a combination of anomalous diffusion determining a local scale invariance for the small fluctuations of the displacement
(with
q\leq\alpha
),
and rare but long-lasting ballistic excursions determining a faster scaling for the large fluctuations for which
\zeta=1
.
The piecewise linear behavior of Eq. (
1
) has been observed in the transport in polygonal billiards
Sanders and Larralde 2006
;
Jepps and Rondoni 2006
;
Orchard et al. 2021
,
billiards with infinite horizon
Armstead et al. 2003b
;
Schmiedeberg and Stark 2006
;
Courbage et al. 2008
,
diffusion in laser-cooled atoms
Aghion et al. 2017
,
one-dimensional maps
Pikovsky 1991
;
Salari et al. 2015
, and
intermittent maps
Castiglione et al. 1999
;
Artuso and Cristadoro 2003
,
in experiments on the mobility of particles inside living cancer cells
Gal and Weihs 2010
,
the motion of particles passively advected by dynamical membranes
Sneppen and Jensen 1994
,
running sand piles
Carreras et al. 1999
,
and in stochastic models of inhomogeneous media
Burioni et al. 2010
;
Bernabó et al. 2014
,
among a host of many other phenomena.
A great deal of insight into the strong anomalous diffusion of these processes has been gained
by means of generalizations of the central limit theorem in terms of non-normalizable densities

Rebenshtok et al. 2014a
;
Rebenshtok et al. 2014b
;
Froemberg et al. 2015
;
Zaburdaev et al. 2015
.

The above scenario is not unique. For instance,
in the bulk-mediated diffusion on lipid bilayers of Ref.
Krapf et al. 2016
,
the spectrum of moments is not piece-wise linear.
In the present paper, however, we investigate the double linear scaling of Eq. (
1
), for a set of different deterministic and stochastic systems.
That is, in fact, the most commonly reported situation in experimental observations and theoretical studies.

Here we revisit the theory of strong anomalous diffusion
from the point of view of scaling theory
Barenblatt 2003
and asymptotic matching
Bender and Orszag 1978

of the PDF
\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)
for the displacements

\Delta x(t)=|x(t)-x(0)|

over a time
t
.
Our main result is anticipated in Fig.
1
, which represents numerical data for the
Lévy-Lorentz gas (LLg), a simple but paradigmatic model of anomalous transport, which is described in
Sect.
III.1
.
Roughly speaking, Fig.
1
suggests that the process
\Delta x(t)
evolves on two different asymptotic scales,
which are revealed through the two scaled variables
z:=\Delta x(t)/t^{\xi}
and
v:=\Delta x(t)/t^{\zeta}
, concerning two different regions
of space.
More precisely, the idea numerically verified in Fig.
1

is the following.
For sufficiently large times
t
, the PDF is subdivided in two branches,
corresponding to two distinct asymptotic regimes, which coincide over an interval that gets
larger and larger as
t
grows.
This is formalized through the following
ansatz
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)\,\mathrm{d}\Delta x=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathcal{F}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z&\text{with}&z=\frac{\Delta x}{t^{\xi}}\leq c\,t^{\iota-\xi}\,,\\[5.69054pt]
g(t)\,\mathcal{J}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v&\text{with}&v=\frac{\Delta x}{t^{\zeta}}>c\,t^{\iota-\zeta}\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\xi,\iota,\zeta
and
c
are four parameters obeying

0<\xi<\iota<\zeta
,

c>0
,
and the scaled variables
z
and
v
concern the “bulk” and the “tail” of
P
,
respectively defined by
\Delta x\in[0,ct^{\iota})
and
\Delta x\in(ct^{\iota},\infty)
.
The functions
\mathcal{F}
and
g\mathcal{J}
are non-negative and integrable on their respective domains,
so that
P
is non-negative and normalized over the half real line, for all
t
.
The domain of
\mathcal{F}(z)
grows without bounds with
t
,
since the value of
z
at its right border,
c\,t^{\iota-\xi}
, approaches
\infty
in the large time limit.
Moreover, the domain of
\mathcal{J}(v)
extends to arbitrarily small values,
since the smallest admissible value
c\,t^{\iota-\zeta}
for
v
approaches
0
for
t\to\infty
.

In Fig.
1
the scaling function
\mathcal{F}(z)
takes the form of a power law for
z>z_{c}\simeq 5
,
and
\mathcal{J}(v)
follows a power law for
v<v_{c}\simeq 0.2
.
We will show that as a consequence

\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)
follows a power law for

z_{c}\,t^{\xi}\lesssim\Delta x\lesssim v_{c}\,t^{\zeta}
.
The two branches of
\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)
overlap on this interval,
and the interval is expanding for increasing
t
:
The PDF features asymptotic matching
and, as long as
\xi<\iota<\zeta
and
t
is large,
the matching position for the two branches,
\Delta x=ct^{\iota}

will reside well inside this interval,
irrespective of the particular choice of
c
and
\iota
.

In Sect.
II
we show how the piecewise linear scaling of
\nu_{q}

emerges as a consequence of Eq. (
I
)
and the asymptotic matching on the power law.
We establish universal relations between exponents characterizing the distribution,
and derive the leading-order corrections to the asymptotic power laws.
These findings suggest a new framework for data analysis.
In Sect.
III
we explore these findings for five different models
that feature anomalous diffusion.
Besides elaborating on the LLg we revisit the exact solutions for Lévy walks (LW)
Metzler and Klafter 2000
;
Zaburdaev et al. 2015
,
the fly-and-die (FnD)
Vollmer et al. 2021

and the slicer-map (SM)
Salari et al. 2015
dynamics,
as well as numerical data on the Lorentz gas with infinite horizon (LGi)
Armstead et al. 2003b
,
and polygonal billiard channels (PBC)
Sanders and Larralde 2006
;
Jepps and Rondoni 2006
;
Schmiedeberg and Stark 2006
;
Orchard et al. 2021
.
Their distributions and spectrum of displacements are all described by the theory exemplified above through the LLg.
In Sect.
IV
, a summary is given of our main findings
and an outlook to future perspectives.

II
Scaling and Asymptotic Matching

In this section we discuss in general the scaling of the moments of displacement
\Delta x
at time
t

determined by a PDF enjoying power-law behavior and asymptotic matching,
as described above in the special case of the LLg.
In particular, we assume that at large times
t
the time evolution of the bulk of the probability density obeys scaling with a scaling variable
z=\Delta x/t^{\xi}

(
cf.
Eq. (
I
)),
and that it assumes a power law for large displacements,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)\simeq\alpha\,k\,(\Delta x)^{-\alpha-1}\,t^{\alpha\,\xi}\,.

(4a)

II.1
Asymptotic Matching

Given the definition (
I
), the power law (
4a
) may be written for the two branches of the PDF.
The bulk is described by the scaling function

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(z)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\alpha\,k\,(\Delta x)^{-\alpha-1}\,t^{\alpha\,\xi}\>\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta x}{\mathrm{d}z}

(4b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\,k\,z^{-\alpha-1}\qquad\text{ for }z>z_{c}

where the parameter
z_{c}
is deduced from data.
For the tail, we rely on the function
g
introduced in Eq. (
I
),
noting that Eq. (
4a
) implies:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(v)\simeq\frac{\alpha k(\Delta x)^{-\alpha-1}}{g(t)}\>t^{\alpha\xi}\>\frac{\mathrm{d}\Delta x}{\mathrm{d}v}=\frac{\alpha k(\Delta x)^{-\alpha-1}}{g(t)}\,t^{\alpha\,\xi+\zeta}\,.

(4c)

Then, taking

\displaystyle g(t)=t^{-\beta}\quad\text{ with }~~\beta=\alpha\,(\zeta-\xi)

(4d)

one obtains

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(v)=\alpha\,k\,v^{-\alpha-1}\quad\text{ for }v<v_{c}

(4e)

where
v_{c}
is deduced from data, like
z_{c}
is.
At late times the bulk and the tail of the distributions overlap as power laws in a wide interval
whose width diverges asymptotically in time.

There are two important things to note here:

Firstly, in terms of the scaled variables and distribution,
the deviation from the power law can be
pushed below any desired bound by selecting larger values of
z_{c}
,
and smaller values for
v_{c}
.
Even when
v_{c}\ll z_{c}
it suffices to wait for
large values of
t
until the interval

z_{c}\,t^{\xi}<\Delta x<v_{c}\,t^{\zeta}

is non-empty.
Eventually, in the large-
t
limit, the length of the interval will diverge
because
\xi<\zeta
.
This suffices for our investigation,
since this interval determines the asymptotic transport properties,

i. e.
the scalings with time of the moments.

Secondly, we note that in Eqs. (
4b
) and (
4e
) not only the exponent
-(\alpha+1)
of the power laws,
but also the prefactors,
\alpha k
, agree.
This holds for by every set of exponents
\{\alpha,\,\beta,\,\xi,\,\zeta\}

characterizing the PDF,
and it will become the cornerstone of the data analysis presented in Sect.
II.5
.

II.2
Moments

The
q^{\text{th}}
moment of the displacement is defined by

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\right\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\>\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\>\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)\>\mathrm{d}\Delta x\,.

For large
t
, where
z_{c}t^{\xi}<ct^{\iota}<v_{c}t^{\zeta}
, the integral can be broken into three pieces

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\right\rangle_{t}=

\displaystyle\;\int_{0}^{z_{c}t^{\xi}}\>\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\>\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)\>\mathrm{d}\Delta x

\displaystyle+\;\int_{z_{c}t^{\xi}}^{v_{c}t^{\zeta}}\>\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\>\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)\>\mathrm{d}\Delta x

\displaystyle+\;\int_{v_{c}t^{\zeta}}^{\infty}\>\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\>\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)\>\mathrm{d}\Delta x\,.

(5)

In view of Eq. (
I
), in which the Jacobians of the coordinate transformations are already accounted for, and applying Eq. (
4a
),
the
q^{\text{th}}
moment can be approximated asymptotically in time as follows

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\right\rangle_{t}\simeq

\displaystyle\;t^{q\,\xi}\>\int_{0}^{z_{c}}z^{q}\>\mathcal{F}(z)\>\mathrm{d}z+t^{q\,\zeta-\beta}\int_{v_{c}}^{\infty}v^{q}\>\mathcal{J}(v)\>\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+\alpha\,k\,t^{\alpha\xi}\>\int_{z_{c}\,t^{\xi}}^{v_{c}\,t^{\zeta}}\>(\Delta x)^{q-\alpha-1}\>\mathrm{d}\Delta x\,.

(6)

The first two integrals take fixed finite values that do not depend on time.
Moreover, for
q\neq\alpha
the latter integral in Eq. (
II.2
) can be expressed as a
difference of the two power laws with exponents
that appear in the prefactors in front of the other two integrals, respectively.
Collecting terms with the same time dependence provides

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\right\rangle_{t}=N_{q}\>t^{q\,\xi}+I_{q}\>t^{q\,\zeta-\beta}\quad\text{for}\quad q\neq\alpha

(7a)

with

\displaystyle N_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\,k\,z_{c}^{q-\alpha}}{\alpha-q}+\int_{0}^{z_{c}}z^{q}\>\mathcal{F}(z)\>\mathrm{d}z\,,

(7b)

\displaystyle I_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\,k\,v_{c}^{q-\alpha}}{q-\alpha}+\int_{v_{c}}^{\infty}v^{q}\>\mathcal{J}(v)\>\mathrm{d}v\,.

(7c)

On the other hand, for
q=\alpha
the third integral in Eq. (
II.2
) takes the form of a logarithm,

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{\alpha}\right\rangle_{t}=

\displaystyle\;\;t^{\alpha\,\xi}\>\int_{0}^{z_{c}}z^{\alpha}\>\mathcal{F}(z)\>\mathrm{d}z+t^{\alpha\,\xi}\int_{v_{c}}^{\infty}v^{\alpha}\>\mathcal{J}(v)\>\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+\alpha\,k\,t^{\alpha\xi}\;\ln\frac{v_{c}\,t^{\zeta}}{z_{c}\,t^{\xi}}\,.

This provides

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{\alpha}\right\rangle_{t}}{t^{\alpha\,\xi}}=N_{\alpha}+I_{\alpha}+\alpha\,k\>(\zeta-\xi)\>\ln t

(8a)

where

\displaystyle N_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\alpha\,k\>\ln z_{c}+\int_{0}^{z_{c}}z^{\alpha}\>\mathcal{F}(z)\>\mathrm{d}z\,,

(8b)

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\alpha\,k\>\ln v_{c}+\int_{v_{c}}^{\infty}v^{\alpha}\>\mathcal{J}(v)\>\mathrm{d}v\,.

(8c)

In either case the coefficients
N_{q}
and
I_{q}
take finite values as long as
\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\right\rangle_{t}
exists.
For finite
t
this is always the case, if the distribution has a cut-off,
i.e. when the speed of moving objects is bounded.
and the distribution has a cut-off.
Moreover, in the scaling regime these values do not depend on the choice of
z_{c}
and
v_{c}
.
For instance, for all values
z_{2}>z_{1}\gg z_{c}
we have

\displaystyle N_{q}(z_{2})

\displaystyle-N_{q}(z_{1})=\frac{\alpha\,k\,\Bigl(z_{2}^{q-\alpha}-z_{1}^{q-\alpha}\Bigr)}{\alpha-q}+\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}z^{q}\>\mathcal{F}(z)\>\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\,k\,\Bigl(z_{2}^{q-\alpha}-z_{1}^{q-\alpha}\Bigr)}{\alpha-q}+\alpha\,k\;\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}z^{q-\alpha-1}\>\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=0

and analogous arguments apply for
I_{q}
,
N_{\alpha}
, and
I_{\alpha}
.

It is useful to note the geometric interpretation of the coefficients
N_{q}
and
I_{q}
.
To this ends we observe that normalization of the PDF implies that
\mathcal{F}
is integrable for small
z
.
A fortiori, this integrability at small
z
applies to
z^{q}\mathcal{F}

for all positive moments
q
.
Moreover, for
q<\alpha
the power-law tail is integrable for
z\to\infty
, and

\alpha\,k\,z_{c}^{q-\alpha}=-(q-\alpha)\;\int_{z_{c}}^{\infty}\alpha\,k\,z^{q-\alpha-1}\>\mathrm{d}z
.
In this case,
N_{q}
amounts to the integral of
z^{q}\>\mathcal{F}(z)
over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
It takes a positive value.
In particular the normalization of the PDF entails the normalization of
\mathcal{F}(z)
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle 1=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\,.

(9)

On the other hand, for
q>\alpha
the power law is integrable from zero to
z_{c}
, and

\alpha\,k\,z_{c}^{q-\alpha}=(q-\alpha)\;\int_{0}^{z_{c}}\alpha\,k\,z^{q-\alpha-1}\>\mathrm{d}z
.
In this case
N_{q}
amounts to the integral of
z^{q}\,\mathcal{F}(z)-\alpha\,k\>z^{q-\alpha-1}
.
The integral can also be taken over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
also for
q>\alpha

when
\mathcal{F}(z)
converges for instance
exponentially to the power law for large values of
z
.
In any case
N_{q}
takes a finite value as long as the
q
th moment exists,
and it can take positive and negative values.

Similarly, for
q>\alpha
the coefficient
I_{q}
is positive and it amounts to the integral of
v^{q}\>\mathcal{J}(v)
over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
while for
q<\alpha
it amounts to the integral of the difference
v^{q}\>\mathcal{J}(v)-\alpha\,k\>v^{q-\alpha-1}
.
In this case
\mathcal{J}(v)
must converge sufficiently rapidly to the power law such that the integral can be performed starting from
v=0
,
and the sign of
I_{q}
will then signify which portion of
\mathcal{J}(v)
lies below or above the power law.
Similarly, the signs of
N_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
will also depend on the specific shape of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{J}
, respectively.

There are three scenarios for the asymptotic scaling of the moments:

q<\alpha
,
q=\alpha
, and
q>\alpha
.

In the first case,
the exponent
q\zeta-\beta
is smaller than the exponent
q\xi
:

\displaystyle q\zeta-\beta=q\zeta-\alpha(\zeta-\xi)<q\zeta-q(\zeta-\xi)=q\xi\,.

Consequently, at the large
t
, the right hand side of Eq. (
7a
) is dominated by the addend containing
N_{q}
.
Conversely, in the third case, the exponent
q\zeta-\beta
is larger than the exponent
q\xi
,
and
the large
t
limit of the right hand side of Eq. (
7a
) is dominated by the addend containing
I_{q}
.
For
q=\alpha
the term involving the logarithm dominate the right hand side of Eq. (
8
).
Asymptotically in time, we may thus write:

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\right\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrl}N_{q}\;t^{q\,\xi}&\quad\text{for}&0<q<\alpha\,,\\[5.69054pt]
\alpha\,k\,(\zeta-\xi)\>t^{\alpha\,\xi}\;\ln t&\quad\text{for}&q=\alpha\,,\\[5.69054pt]
I_{q}\;t^{q\,\zeta-\beta}&\quad\text{for}&\alpha<q\,.\end{array}\right.

This result is purely based on the constraints imposed by matching the power laws of the bulk and of the tail of the distribution.
In Sect.
III
we present numerical results to confirm that this theoretical framework applies to a vast variety of cases.

II.3
Comparison with Numerical Data

In applications one is often interested in the scaling of the MSD,
q=2
.
In Fig.
2
(a) we show this data for the LLg where
\zeta=1
.
To this end we plot
2-\nu_{2}
as function of
\alpha
.
Normal diffusion,
\nu_{2}=1
, amounts to
2-\nu_{2}=1
,
and ballistic flights,
\nu_{2}=2
, amount to
2-\nu_{2}=0
.
In particular, for the LLg the MSD follows the exponent of normal diffusion when
\alpha>2
,
and the impact of ballistic flights become more and more noticeable when
\alpha

decreases.
It is then tempting to rewrite Eq. (
1
) in the form of a universal scaling curve
that admits a data collapse for all moments and all parameter values of a variety of models showing strong anomalous diffusion, with two linear regimes for the momenta spectrum:

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta-\frac{\nu_{q}}{2}}{\zeta-\xi}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrl}\alpha/q&\quad\text{for}&\alpha/q<1\,,\\[5.69054pt]
1&\quad\text{for}&\alpha/q\geq 1\,.\end{array}\right.

Figure
2
(b) shows this representation for a vast collection of moments
that we obtained by fitting LLg data (as discussed in Sect.

below).
The agreement is disappointing,
in spite of the fact that we have extremely clean numerical data (right panels of Fig.
5
).
Close inspection reveals that the MSD appears to be a lucky borderline case.
For larger moments it becomes ever more complicated to faithfully sample the impact of the very long flights
that dominate the behavior of the large moments even though they are extremely rare.
For small moments
q
there are severe systematic errors in the fits.

In order to understand the systematic errors in the fitted exponents
we explore the corrections to the asymptotic power laws.

Corrections to Scaling

) establishes

.

To assess systematic errors in fitting exponents for

,

q>\alpha
;
the thin lines the prefactor of the subdominant contribution.

The scaling functions
\mathcal{F}
of the LLg proceed entirely below the power law (
cf.
Figs.
1
,
4
(a) and
5
).
Hence,
N_{q}
takes a negative value for
q>\alpha
,
and the correction takes the form of a power law
that decays towards the asymptotic scaling from below.
Consequently, the fitted exponents are systematically too large
as long as the subdominant power law in Eq. (
7a
) is not substantially smaller than the leading order.
This is in line with the observation reported in Fig.
2
(b)
that the fitted exponents are systematically too large
(
i. e.
the data points tend to lie below the solid black line).

The leading-order correction to the asymptotic power-law scaling involves the ratio of the two prefactors,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\right\rangle}{I_{q}\,t^{q\zeta-\beta}}

\displaystyle\simeq 1+\left(\frac{N_{q}}{I_{q}}\right)\;t^{(\alpha-q)(\zeta-\xi)}\quad\text{ for }\quad\alpha<q\,,

(15a)

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\bigl(\Delta x\bigr)^{q}\right\rangle}{N_{q}\,t^{q\xi}}

\displaystyle\simeq 1+\left(\frac{I_{q}}{N_{q}}\right)\;t^{(q-\alpha)(\zeta-\xi)}\quad\text{ for }\quad\alpha>q\,.

(15b)

For a reliable estimate of the exponents we demand
that the leading order term is larger than the correction term by a factor
\Phi
.
This requires
that the power-law fits are performed for times well beyond the critical time
t_{q}
defined by:

\displaystyle t_{q}=\left(\Phi\>N_{q}/I_{q}\right)^{\tau}\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha<q

(16a)

\displaystyle t_{q}=\left(\Phi\>I_{q}/N_{q}\right\rvert^{\tau}\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha>q

(16b)

with

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{1}{\lvert q-\alpha\rvert\>(\zeta-\xi)}\,.

Fig.
3
(b) provides the estimates for the LLg.
Thin, medium and thick lines refer to
\Phi=1
,
3
, and
10
, respectively,
and the colors indicate different values of
\alpha
.
For
\alpha=0.1
and
\Phi=1
(thin blue line) the values of
t_{q}
are larger than
10^{24}
for a noticeable range of the plot,
for
\Phi=3
the time
t_{q}
drops below
10^{24}
only for
q\gtrsim 10\,\alpha=1
,
and for
\Phi=10
it remains substantially larger than that value for all considered values of
q
.
On the other hand, the largest times that we reach in the simulations is
10^{9}
.
Consequently, the systematic error in the fits is large,
and this is reflected in the severe deviations between prediction and numerical estimates
that we saw in Fig.
2
(b).
For
\alpha=0.5
the maximum time in our simulations was
10^{6}
.
In this case the numerical estimates are expected to be reliable for
\alpha/q\lesssim 1/3
,
and this is indeed what we observe in Fig.
2
(b).
For the
\alpha=0.7
the situation should even be better.
However, in that case the numerical estimates suffer from bad statistics,
which is reflected in large error bars.

The time scale
t_{q}
is model specific, but the discussion of the LLg reveals that it can easily be very large.
In particular, estimates of moments of the displacement will be hard
when the difference between
\xi
and
\zeta
is small,
or when
q
is close to the cross-over moment
\alpha
.

In order to avoid these issues we propose to determining the exponents
\zeta
,
\xi
, and
\alpha
based on a scaling analysis of the PDF of the displacement
\Delta x
.

II.5
Data Analysis of the PDFs

In order to extract the scaling exponents directly from the scaling of the PDF we
note that for small arguments
\mathcal{J}(v)
takes exactly the same functional form as
\mathcal{F}(z)
for large arguments
(cf. Eqs. (
4b
) and (
4e
)).
In Fig.
4
(a) this is demonstrated by providing the scaling plot of the bulk and the tails of the PDF
in the same coordinate system, rather than plotting them separately as in Fig.
1
.
One clearly sees now that the large
z
data for
\mathcal{F}(z)=t^{\xi}\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,\,t)
lie on the same power law
as the small
v
data of
\mathcal{J}(v)=t^{\zeta+\beta}\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,\,t)
.

The three exponents characterizing the distribution can therefore be found by a data collapse of the
time evolution of the PDFs of the displacement:

\xi

is used to collapse the height of the bulk distribution
\mathcal{F}(z)=t^{\xi}\,\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)
for small
z=\Delta x/t^{\xi}
.
This one parameter fit will provide a data collapse for the bulk and the power-law tail of the distribution.
It is robust because it involves a vertical shift of horizontal lines such that they all meet at the same level.
For the data in Fig.
4
this provides the estimate
\xi=0.66\pm 0.02
for a theoretically expected value of
\xi=2/3
.
The resulting data collapse of the data is also shown in Fig.
1
(a).

\zeta

is used to collapse the position of the cutoff value
v_{\text{max}}

for the tails of the distribution
\mathcal{J}(v)=t^{\zeta+\beta}\,\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)

for the largest admissible values of
v
.
The vertical position of the data is adjusted by the choice of
\beta
.
The fit of
\zeta
is robust because it involves the horizontal shift of very sharp peaks such that they all lie at the same height;
or rather the minima at the end of the algebraic decay should lie at the same height.
Moreover, in many circumstances
\zeta
need not be fitted because it must be unity
when the large-scale cutoff is dominated by ballistic flights.
One obtains a plot of the data as shown in Fig.
1
(b).

\alpha

must provide the slope to the
simultaneous
asymptotic scaling of
both

the large
z
asymptotics of
\mathcal{F}(z)
and
the small
v
asymptotics of
\mathcal{J}(v)
.
According to Eq. (
4d
) it is related to
\beta
by the hyperscaling relation
\beta=\alpha\,(\zeta-\xi)
.
A joint plot of both distributions is shown in Fig.

(a).
The quality of the fit can best be judged via a reduced plot where one plots the deviation from the suggested power law.
The power-law scaling for
\mathcal{F}
applies for

and the scaling for
\mathcal{J}
applies for
v\lesssim v_{c}=0.2
.
A reduced plot of the data in this range is shown in Fig.

The exponent
\beta
must be adjusted such that all data points have the same value.
The effect of varying
\alpha
for fixed
\zeta
and
\xi
is demonstrated in Fig.
4

One clearly sees that the
simultaneous
matching of the slopes of
both

In the next section we show that this data analysis provides a reliable characterization of numerical data.

III.1
The Lévy-Lorentz Gas (LLg)

The LLg describes a one-dimensional ballistic motion of a particle
that moves with unit velocity and is scattered at point scatterers located at fixed, random positions in space.
The distance
r
between neighboring scatterers is distributed according to a Lévy-type distribution,

f(r)\propto p_{1}\,r^{-p_{1}-1}
.
The particle is transmitted and reflected with the probability
1/2
when it encounters a scatterer.
The PDF
\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)
for the displacement

\Delta x
for an ensemble of particles starting at
t=0
on a given scatterer features strong anomalous diffusion.
Due to the fixed unit speed of the particles the probability
\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)
vanishes for
\Delta x>t
.
The tail of the distribution has a cutoff that scales with
\zeta=1
.
The bulk of the distribution can be rescaled by a different exponent
\xi
,
and it has a power-law tail.
For
p_{1}<1
the bulk is rescaled by the exponent
\xi=1/(1+\alpha)
and its power law decays with exponent
\alpha=p_{1}
.
This non-trivial dependence of
\xi
with parameter
p_{1}
arises from a delicate interplay of spatial disorder and random scattering events,
as derived in
Burioni et al. 2010
.
For
p_{1}>1
one finds
\alpha=2p_{1}-1
and diffusive scaling
\xi=1/2
for the bulk distribution
(
Burioni et al. 2010
;
Salari et al. 2015
)
.
For the LLg, Eq. (
1
) entails

\displaystyle 2-\nu_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrl}\displaystyle\alpha^{2}/(1+\alpha)&\text{for}&0<\alpha<1\,,\\
\ \alpha/2&\quad\text{for}&1\leq\alpha<2\,,\\
\ 1&\quad\text{for}&2\leq\alpha\,.\end{array}\right.

This is shown as the solid curve in Fig.
2
(a).
The square symbols were computed from a fit to power law of the mean square
displacement as a function of time as used in
Giberti et al. 2019
;
Salari et al. 2015
.
Instead, the cross symbols were obtained from a proper scaling of the PDF of the displacement.
There is excellent agreement between the data and the theoretical prediction.

The scatter of the data in Fig.
2
(b)
is due to the correction to the power laws reported in
Eq. (
15
). For the LLg the relations
\zeta=1
and

\xi=1/(1+\alpha)
imply

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{1}{|q-\alpha|(\zeta-\xi)}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\;\;\frac{1+\alpha}{|q-\alpha|}\,.

(20)

Hence, the correction term decays slowly when
q\approx\alpha
and for small
\alpha
.
This leads to the huge values of the cross-over time for
\alpha=0.1

that we observed in Fig.
3
(b),
and it accounts for the large values at
q\approx\alpha
for
\alpha=0.5
and
0.7
.
Consequently, the fitted exponents feature a systematic bias towards larger exponents
q\nu_{q}/2
,
as clearly shown by the data in Fig.
2
(b)
and the right panels in Fig.
5
.

The different panels of Fig.
5
show numerical data for parameter values

p_{1}=\alpha=0.1
,

p_{1}=\alpha=0.3
, and

p_{1}=\alpha=0.7
, respectively.
For
p_{1}=0.3
the data analysis is relatively straightforward,
just as in the case
p_{1}=\alpha=0.5
discussed in Fig.
4
.
The other two values exemplify generic problems for large and small values of
\alpha
.

For slowly-decaying power-law tails,
\alpha=0.1
, the long ballistic flights give rise to substantial fluctuations in the bulk of the distribution.
One needs here a lot of statistics to perform stable averages to estimate
\xi
.
At the same time one needs very long simulations to resolve a noticeable range of the power-law tail.
Even for times as large as
10^{9}
the power-law piece of the distribution only extends over half a decade.
Performing a simultaneous fit based on the scaling of
\mathcal{F}(z)
and
\mathcal{J}(v)
extends this range to two orders of magnitude,
such that a reliable estimate comes into reach.

For rapidly decaying power-law tails,
\alpha=0.7
, it is a challenge to collect data with sufficient statistics
to faithfully resolve the power-law tails.
However, for
\mathcal{F}(z)
and
\mathcal{J}(v)
the data with a good statistics lie in vastly different regions in the mutual plot;
in the present example at
5\lesssim z\lesssim 400
and
6\times 10^{-3}\lesssim v\lesssim 0.2
for the
10^{6}
data.

III.2
Fly-and-Die (FnD) and Slicer Map (SM) dynamics

The slicer map was introduced in
Salari et al. 2015
as an analytically solvable dynamical system
that features all regimes of anomalous transport,
from localization to ballistic motion. In addition,
it was shown that the slicer map does also exhibit strong anomalous transport.
Its displacement time autocorrelation function was analytically obtained
(
Giberti et al. 2019
)
,
and more recently, a universal scaling of the moments of the displacement
and of their correlation function was suggested in Ref.
(
Vollmer et al. 2021
)
.
The latter paper also introduces the FnD dynamics as a dynamical system where it is even more straightforward to perform the analytical calculations.
From a coarse-grained perspective, that focuses on the scaling of the distribution of the displacement,
both dynamics are characterized as follows.
The initial distribution is an approximation of a delta function at the origin,
and transport proceeds in a one-dimensional space where the displacement from the origin is denoted as
\Delta x
.
Each member
x
of the ensemble is characterized by a life time,
t_{c}(x)
.
For
t<t_{c}(x)
it propagates with unit velocity.
Then it stops and remains at its terminal position
\Delta x=t_{c}(x)
for all times
t>t_{c}(x)
,
with negligible fluctuations in the case of the SM.
The distribution of life times
t_{c}
obeys a power law
\mathop{\text{Prob}}(t_{c}>t)=(t/t_{0})^{-p_{2}}

where
t_{0}
is a smallest admissible life time.

For
t>t_{0}
the dynamics provide the PDF of displacements.
It can be written in the form of Eq. (
I
)
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(z)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}0&\text{ \ for }&z=\frac{\Delta x}{t^{0}}<t_{0}\,,\\[2.84526pt]
\displaystyle\frac{p_{2}}{t_{0}^{-p_{2}}}\>z^{-p_{2}-1}&\text{ \ for \ }&t_{0}<\!\!z=\frac{\Delta x}{t^{0}}<t\,,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle g(t)\mathcal{J}(v)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle t^{-p_{2}}\>\frac{p_{2}}{t_{0}^{-p_{2}}}\>v^{-p_{2}-1}&\text{ \ for}\quad\frac{t_{0}}{t^{1}}<v=\frac{\Delta x}{t^{1}}<1\,,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle t^{-p_{2}}\>\frac{p_{2}}{t_{0}^{-p_{2}}}\>\delta(v-1)&\text{ \ for}\quad v=\frac{\Delta x}{t^{1}}=1\,,\\[2.84526pt]
0&\text{ \ otherwise \ }\end{array}\right.

such that
\xi=0
,
\zeta=1
, and
\alpha=\beta=p_{2}
.
In
Giberti et al. 2019
, the authors used the MSD to identify the relations between the parameters of the SM and the LLg,
and pointed out that this entails a matching of all other moments.
From the perspective of our present results this is an immediate consequence of Eq. (
II.2
).

III.3
Lévy walks (LW)

As a next step we verify that the findings established for the LLg also apply for Lévy walks.
To this end we consider one-dimensional Lévy walks
where particles move with unit velocity along a line.
Their motion is similar to the one of the LLg.
However, in the LLg the particles move in a domain of scatterers that provide an environment with fixed (quenched) disorder,
while the distance of each segment of the LW is sampled randomly and independently
(
Blumen et al. 1989
;
Rebenshtok et al. 2014a
;
Zaburdaev et al. 2015
;
Dentz et al. 2015
)
.
We consider an ensemble where all particles start in the origin,
and they move in a given direction for a time
t

sampled from a Lévy distribution
f(t)\propto p_{3}\>t^{-p_{3}-1}
with
t\in[1,\infty)
.
At the end of each step the particles reverse direction with probability
1/2
.

Arguably, LWs are the most elaborately studied model system featuring strong anomalous transport.
In particular, the different scaling of the bulk and the tails of the PDF have first been observed in this context,
and in those studies the distribution
J(v)
in Eq. (
4c
) has been denoted as non-normalizable density

(
Rebenshtok et al. 2014a
;
Rebenshtok et al. 2014b
;
Zaburdaev et al. 2015
)
.
The moments of displacement have been reviewed in
Metzler and Klafter 2000
;
Zaburdaev et al. 2015
.
They follow the behavior anticipated in Eqs. (
1
) with
\alpha=p_{3}
,

\zeta=1
, and
\xi=1/\alpha
.

The panels in Fig.
6
show numerical data for
p_{3}=\alpha=1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.5
.

The simulations were run till time
10^{7}
,
with ensemble comprising more than
3.1\times 10^{5}
,
9.2\times 10^{4}
, and
1.2\times 10^{5}
trajectories for
\alpha=1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.5
, respectively.
The left panels show the PDF and the matching of the two branches of the intermediate power law.
Data points with different colors refer to data evaluated at different times, as indicated in the legend.
The upper right panels show data for the moments ranging from
0.5
till
4.0
, in steps of
0.5
.
The lower ones show the ratio of the data and the analytical prediction for their asymptotic power law scaling.
These reduced plots reveal that even for this high-quality data it is challenging to determine the asymptotic scaling of the moments of the distribution by fitting the power laws.
On the other hand the matching of the intermediate power laws of the bulk and the tail of the distribution (lower left panels) provides robust results.

III.4
The Lorentz gas (LG)

The Lorentz gas is another classical model that features strong anomalous transport
Bleher 1992
;
Armstead et al. 2003b
;
Dettmann 2014
.
This model comprises the elastic scattering of a point particle in a two-dimensional setting
where circular scatterers of radius
R=1
are arranged on a hexagonal or square lattice.
One deals with the displacement of an ensemble of initial conditions that start close to the origin,
and all move with unit speed.

The transport properties are studied as a function of the ratio
p_{4}
of the radius
R
of the scatterers
and the critical radius
R_{c}

where channels that admit infinitely long collisionless flights are closed.

For
p_{4}>1
the LG is said to have finite horizon (LGf).
The case of a finite horizon emerges for Lorentz gases with a hexagonal arrangement of scatterers.
In such a setting the lengths of the flights between collision have a finite expectation value,
and the central limit theorem asserts that
\xi=1/2
.
Moreover, when the mean-free path is bounded the PDF
\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)
of the displacement also
does not have heavy tails.
As a consequence, a LG with finite horizon features normal transport
Dettmann 2014
.
In App.
A
we show that within our theory such a system can not have power-law tails.

For
p_{4}<1
infinitely long collisionless segments of trajectories can pass through the channels of the periodic arrangement of the scatterers,
the mean free path is no longer bounded and the length of the flights between collision do not have a finite expectation value
Bleher 1992
.
The billiard is said to have infinite horizon (LGi).
This gives rise to strong anomalous transport
Armstead et al. 2003b
;
Courbage et al. 2008
;
Cristadoro et al. 2014

where the spectrum Eq. (
1
) is obtained
for the choice
\alpha=2
,
\zeta=1
, and
\xi=1/2
.
The value of
\zeta
reflects the motion of the particles with unit speed,
and the value of
\alpha
is in accordance with the finding of
Zarfaty et al. 2018

that the PDF of displacements features a power-law tail that decays with an exponent
-3
.

The coincidence of the cross-over dimension
\alpha
with the variance
gives rise to mathematical challenges with the central limit theorem
and logarithmic terms in the asymptotic scaling of the distribution function
Marklof and Tóth 2016
;
Zarfaty et al. 2018
.
Indeed, according to Eq. (
8
) the variance must involve the observed
t\,\ln t
scaling
when
\alpha=2
.

In order to explore this dynamics we consider an arrangement of scatterers on a square lattice.
In this case
R_{c}
amounts to half the lattice spacing.
Fig.
7
shows numerical data for the case
p_{4}=0.3
.
The scaling of the bulk of the distribution is diffusive,
\xi=1/2
.
As expected for a system where the tails are due to ballistic trajectories that evolve with a fixed finite speed,
the cutoff for large displacements scales with
\zeta=1
,
Moreover, the crossover region between the bulk and the cutoff features a power-law tail with exponent
\alpha=2
.

\mathcal{J}(v)

The range of the power law in itself is too narrow for a reliable fit,
such that other approaches will provide a larger value for the exponent.
Moreover, the reduced plots for the time evolution of the moments (lower panel of Fig.

For the entire range till times

the reduced plot for the moment

and for times

The latter curve becomes reasonably horizontal for

An accurate fit of the moments of this data will have to take into account the higher order correction terms, Eq. (

),
and they might have to account for logarithmic contributions to the scaling of the bulk of the distribution

;

.
Certainly the approach provides important additional insight on top of a brute forward analysis

;

of the moments of displacement.

III.5
Polygonal billiard channels (PBC)

In order to underline this point we explore polygonal billiard channels (PBC),

i. e.
a class of model systems where there is no theoretical insight concerning the values of the parameters characterizing their strong anomalous transport.
A PBC consists of freely flying point particles
that experience specular reflections at two polygonal boundaries which form an infinitely long, narrow channel.
In recent years, a number of studies have investigated the transport properties of polygonal billiard channels, mostly via numerical simulations of the statistics of displacement
Jepps et al. 2008
;
Sanders and Larralde 2006
;
Jepps and Rondoni 2006
;
Orchard et al. 2021
;
Schmiedeberg and Stark 2006
;
Vollmer et al. 2021
;
Alonso et al. 2002
.
When the sides of the polygons are parallel
transport is anomalous with a MSD scaling faster than linear.
If the boundaries are not parallel, the MSD scales linearly in time as in normal diffusion.
In contrast Ref.
Sanders and Larralde 2006
reported long transients of subdiffusive transport for walls which are close to parallel.
For PBC with finite and infinite horizon the transport is strongly anomalous,
with a ballistic scaling for moments of the displacement higher than a certain threshold order
Sanders and Larralde 2006
;
Orchard et al. 2021
;
Vollmer et al. 2021
.
When the horizon is infinite, it is expected that large fluctuations will be determined by long ballistic excursions.
However, for a finite horizon, polygonal channels also exhibit a ballistic scaling of the large fluctuations,
due to geometrically induced correlations and the lack of chaos
Orchard et al. 2021
;
Vollmer et al. 2021
.
Moreover, transport in polygonal billiard channels was found to belong in some cases to the universality class of FnD dynamics
Vollmer et al. 2021
,
with the biscaling of the PDF of displacement being reported in Ref.
Vollmer et al. 2021
.
Further investigation on the statistics of first-passage time for channels of finite length showed a remarkable dependence on whether the polygonal angle is a rational or irrational multiple of
\pi

(in the former case the distribution of the first-passage time is scale-free)
Orchard et al. 2021
.

Here we adopt the notation used in Ref.
Jepps and Rondoni 2006
, and revisit geometries previously studied in Ref.
Sanders and Larralde 2006
;
Vollmer et al. 2021
.
We considered two different channels with
\Delta x=1,\Delta y_{b}=0.45,\Delta y_{t}=0.77
and
H=1.27
yielding infinite horizon, and
H=1.17
yielding finite horizon.
Similarly to the Lorentz gas, we define a parameter
p_{5}=(H-\Delta y_{b})/\Delta y_{t}
,
so that
p_{5}<1
stands for a channel with finite horizon
and
p_{5}>1
for a channel with infinite horizon.
For the two geometries considered here,
p_{5}\approx 1.0649
and
p_{5}\approx 0.935
respectively.

We follow the approach outlined in Sect.

to determine the exponents governing the transport:

•

\xi=1/2
provides a collapse in the bulk of the distribution for both

and
p_{5}>1
,
as shown by the collapse of
\mathcal{F}(z)
in the main panels of Fig.

•

yields a collapse of the tail of the distribution.
After all, particle move with finite speed
such that the large fluctuations of the displacement obey a ballistic scaling, irrespective of

.
This is shown by the collapse of

•

The dotted lines in the main panels, and the reduced plots shown in the insets suggest

and

The authors of Refs.
Vollmer et al. 2021
;
Sanders and Larralde 2006
analyzed numerical data based on power law fits of the moments of displacement.
For the billiard with an infinite horizon they report a value of
\alpha\approx 2
.
This is in line with the present findings.
In contrast, for the finite-horizon case they find a value of
\alpha\approx 3
,
that lies far outside the confidence interval of the data analysis performed in Fig.

We believe that this is due to the systematic errors arising in the fitting the power laws
discussed in Sect.

.
To underpin this point we estimated
N_{q}
and

based on our numerical data.
The inset of Fig.

For

.

IV
Conclusion

In this communication we employed methods of asymptotic matching
that have widely been used in the context of hydrodynamic boundary layers and other scaling theories.
Here, they have been used to deal with the large-distance cutoff of the probability density describing the PDF of displacement in strong anomalous diffusion.
We established that strong anomalous transport emerges whenever the PDF features algebraic tails.
Indeed in App.
A
we provide the formal proof
that strong anomalous diffusion only arises if this distribution has power-law or more slowly decaying tails.

PDFs giving rise to strong anomalous diffusion are characterized by three exponents:

\xi
describes how the width of the distribution becomes broader in time;

\zeta
describes the scaling of the cutoff for large times;

\alpha+1
is the exponent of the power-law tails.
In the present paper we have fully characterized the scaling of the moments of the displacements
via hyper-scaling relations that are based only on the moment
q
and these exponents:

•

In Eq. (
7a
) we relate the time dependence of the moments of displacement to
q
,
\alpha
,
\zeta
, and
\xi
.
This prediction is purely based on the constraints arising from matching the bulk and the tail of the distribution.

•

By Eqs. (
I
) and
(
1
), the slope
\xi
of the lower moments of the distribution is related to the scaling of the bulk of the distribution.
In most applications such a scaling is entailed by large-deviation theory.

•

By Eqs. (
I
) and (
1
) the slope
\zeta
of the higher moments of the distribution is related to the scaling of the cutoff
of the distribution, which will always arise for processes with bounded speed.

•

By Eq. (
4b
) the cross-over between the two regimes is connected to the exponent
\alpha
characterizing the power-law tail of the PDF.
In App.
A
we proved that all moments are dominated by the bulk scaling when the tail decays faster than a power law.
There is no strong anomalous diffusion in that case.

•

In Eq. (
8
) we establish that the moments
q=\alpha
grow like
t^{\alpha\xi}\>\ln t
.
The relevance of this result for the moments of displacement of the Lorentz gas have been discussed in Sect.
III.4
.

•

There is also an exponent
\beta
that describes the time scaling of the distribution
characterizing the tails.
By Eq. (
4d
) we express this exponent in terms of the other exponents
\alpha
,
\zeta
, and
\xi
.

•

In Eq. (
15
) we provide the leading-order corrections to the power-law growth of the moment of the displacement.
In Eq. (
16a
) we provide the time scale that must be surpassed to clearly discriminate the leading-order power law from its sub-leading corrections.

In Sect.
II.4
and by Fig.
2
(b) and the right panels of Figs.
5
–
6
we demonstrate that slowly-decaying corrections to the power-law growth of the exponents will often give rise to severe systematic errors of estimated exponents.
An informed way to determine
\alpha
,
\zeta
and
\xi
is described in Sect.
II.5
.
The impact of adopting this approach is demonstrated in Sect.
III.5
:
fitting of the moments provides an estimates of
\alpha
that is too large by almost
40
%
because it is virtually impossible to observe the asymptotic scaling for moments in the vicinity of
\alpha

(see Fig.
9
(b, inset)).

In Sect.
III
the hyper-scaling relations have been verified for a range of vastly different systems.
To this end we revisited analytical results and and we presented some new numerical work on systems where no analytical results were available so far.
We conclude that our approach provides a novel unified perspective on analyzing the moments of displacement of strong anomalous diffusion.
It is based on hyper-scaling relations and a thorough analysis of the time scaling of the PDF for the displacement.

Appendix A
Moments of displacement for weak anomalous diffusion

In this appendix we show that weak anomalous diffusion emerges
when the distribution
\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)

features scaling for small values of
\Delta x/t^{\xi}
and it
decays faster than any power for large
\Delta x
.
Scaling of the bulk of the distribution implies
that there are positive real numbers
\iota>\xi>0
and
c>0

such that for large times we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)\,\mathrm{d}\Delta x=\mathcal{F}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\qquad\text{for}\quad z=\frac{\Delta x}{t^{\xi}}<c\,t^{\iota-\xi}\,.

(21a)

At the same time a decay of the tail that is faster than any power implies
that for every
q>0
there is a constant
k_{q}
and a time
t_{q}

such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)<k_{q}\>t^{\xi}\;\lvert\Delta x\rvert^{-q-2}\qquad\text{ for }\quad t>t_{q}\,.

(21b)

We consider the following lower bound,
L_{q}(t)
,
for the
q
th moment of the distribution,

\displaystyle L_{q}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{c\,t^{\iota}}\mathrm{d}\Delta x\;|\Delta x|^{q}\>\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\langle\left\lvert\Delta x\right\rvert^{q}\Bigr\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\Delta x\;|\Delta x|^{q}\>\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{c\,t^{\iota}}\mathrm{d}\Delta x\;|\Delta x|^{q}\>\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)+\int_{c\,t^{\iota}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\Delta x\;|\Delta x|^{q}\>\mathcal{P}(\Delta x,t)

\displaystyle=L_{q}(t)+D_{q}(t)\,.

It applies because the integrand is always positive.

In the following we consider values
\iota>\xi
.
In that case the time evolution of
L_{q}
is governed by the scaling of the bulk of the PDF,

\displaystyle L_{q}(t)

\displaystyle=t^{q\,\xi}\>\int_{0}^{c\,t^{\iota-\xi}}\mathrm{d}z\;z^{q}\>\mathcal{F}(z)\to_{t\to\infty}N_{q}\>t^{q\,\xi}\,,

where

N_{q}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\;z^{q}\>\mathcal{F}(z)
is a finite positive number if the moment exists.

Further we observe that the fast decay of the probability implies an upper bound on
D_{q}(t)
,

\displaystyle D_{q}(t)

\displaystyle<k_{q}\>t^{\xi}\;\int_{c\,t^{\iota}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\Delta x\;|\Delta x|^{q}\;|\Delta x|^{-q-2}=\frac{k_{q}}{c}\>t^{\xi-\iota}

For large times this bound tends to zero:
the upper and lower bound converge in the long-time limit.
Hence, the moment must scale in time in the same way as
L_{q}(t)
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle\left\lvert\Delta x\right\rvert^{q}\Bigr\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=N_{q}\>t^{q\,\xi}\qquad\text{ for }\quad t\gg t_{q}\,.

For
\xi=1/2
the system features normal diffusion,
and otherwise there is weak anomalous diffusion.

Herbal Extracts for Denture Care: Effectiveness and Safety Through In vivo Studies—A Systematic Review
Authors: Gajapathi Balaraman, K. S. Bharanija, Joanna Gracy Prasanna, Arjun Badimela, Chandrakala Subramoniam, J. Fiona
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe increasing demand for removable dentures among the geriatric population underscores the need for safe and effective denture cleansers and adhesives. Concerns over the toxicity, cost, and limited biocompatibility of conventional chemical-based products have led to increasing interest in herbal alternatives. This systematic review assessed the efficacy and safety of herbal extracts in denture care. A comprehensive search of PubMed, ScienceDirect, Wiley Online Library, and the Cochrane Library was conducted using the terms “herbal denture cleansers,” “herbal denture adhesives,” and “denture care.” In vivo studies published in English since 2010 were included, and seven studies met the inclusion criteria. Herbal agents such as Neem, Triphala, Aloe vera, turmeric, and olive oil demonstrated significant antifungal and antimicrobial activity, comparable to commercial formulations, along with improved denture retention and mucosal health. Overall, herbal extracts hold promise as effective, safe, and biocompatible alternatives for denture maintenance and patient comfort.

INTRODUCTION

The growing elderly population has increased edentulism rates, making removable dentures common; their long-term effectiveness relies on proper hygiene and secure retention.[1] Denture hygiene is vital for preventing oral infections such as denture stomatitis, halitosis, and plaque accumulation.[2]

Chemical denture cleansers, effective in reducing microbial load are associated with adverse effects on prolonged use.[3]

This systematic review aims to critically evaluate and synthesize clinical evidence on the use of herbal extracts as denture cleansers and adhesives.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The review protocol was prospectively registered in the PROSPERO (Registration number: CRD420251018650) to ensure methodological transparency and minimize bias.

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across electronic databases: PubMed, ScienceDirect, Wiley Online Library, and Cochrane Library. The search was limited to articles published in English from 2010 onwards. Medical Subject Headings and relevant keywords included: “herbal denture cleansers,” “herbal denture adhesives,” “denture care,” and “reline denture.” Boolean operators (AND, OR) were applied to refine the search strategy for relevant results.

DISCUSSION

The growing preference for natural alternatives (denture cleansers, adhesives) is evident across the reviewed literature, largely driven by concerns regarding the cost, biocompatibility, and safety of conventional chemical agents. Studies by Ojah et al. and Sushma et al. reported that Neem tablets (500 mg) and Triphala churna (2 g/100 mL water) significantly reduced Candida albicans colonization on denture surfaces.[67] Olive oil-based adhesives, as demonstrated by Azevedo et al., provided enhanced retention and antifungal activity, suggesting dual benefits in function and hygiene.[7]

The randomized, placebo-controlled trial design in Azevedo et al. and the structured in vivo methodologies used by Mekkawy et al., Sharma et al. add credibility to their findings.[7810] However, a key limitation across most studies was the short duration (between 7 and 30 days), which fails to reflect long-term safety or efficacy. Additionally, several studies lacked blinding or randomization, increasing the risk of bias in subjective outcomes such as satisfaction and comfort.

Varying concentrations of herbal extracts were used across studies, with Neem applied as tablets and 2% mouthwash, and Triphala as both powder and tablets. Nigella sativa extract demonstrated significant antifungal activity at 3%–5%,[9] while Aloevera outperformed chitosan in soft liners.[5] Overall, the shift toward herbal products reflects increasing concern about the cytotoxic and degradative effects of synthetic agents.

CONCLUSION

Neem and Triphala were the most effective cleansers, showing antifungal properties similar to commercial products, while Aloevera and turmeric showed moderate benefits. Despite some variability in study protocols, findings support herbal agents as safe, effective, and affordable alternatives, warranting further validation through robust clinical trials.

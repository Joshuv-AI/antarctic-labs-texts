Mirror and triplet energy differences in s ​ d sd -shell nuclei using microscopic interactions with isospin-symmetry breaking effects
Chandan Sarma, Praveen C. Srivastava, Toshio Suzuki, Noritaka Shimizu

Abstract
Starting from an isospin-symmetric microscopic interaction of Shin et al. [Phys. Rev. C 110, 034306 (2024)], we developed two isospin symmetry breaking (ISB) shell-model interactions by adding Coulomb interaction, as well as charge-symmetry breaking (CSB) and charge-independence breaking (CIB) components from the Argonne v18 (Av18) interaction at two different radial cutoffs, r c = 0.55 r_{c}=0.55 and 0.70 ​ fm 0.70\ \mathrm{fm} . With additional modifications to the proton single-particle energies, we then employed these interactions to calculate the Coulomb displacement energies (CDEs) and triplet displacement energies (TDEs) of the isobaric multiplet masses for nuclei with | T z | = 1 / 2 |T_{z}|=1/2 and T = 1 T=1 across the s ​ d sd -shell to test their validity and applicability, and compared with the USDCm interaction. We found that both ISB interactions show important roles of the CSB and CIB components in the CDEs and TDEs. In addition, we investigated mirror energy differences (MEDs) in the low-energy spectra of nuclei around A A = 20, and triplet energy differences (TEDs) in the A A = 18 and 22 systems. They are generally well described by the ISB interactions, although some discrepancies remain. Furthermore, the calculated E ​ 2 E2 transition strengths obtained with the ISB interactions are shown to be in good agreement with experimental data, indicating small ISB effects in these transitions. Overall, both the ISB interactions provide a consistent and physically motivated framework for describing ISB effects, while the phenomenological USDCm interaction achieves closer agreement with experiment due to a larger number of adjustable parameters.

Introduction

Atomic nuclei are self-bound quantum many-body systems consisting of protons and neutrons collectively known as nucleons. Due to the similar masses and intrinsic spins of protons and neutrons, they were considered
\color
blackisospin doublets with different isospin projections
[
1

\color

= -1/2) and the neutrons (

For such isospin symmetric nucleon-nucleon interactions, the energy spectra of mirror nuclei, whose protons and neutrons are exchanged, have degenerate excitation spectra. However, isospin symmetry is broken at the nuclear level
mainly

\color

\color

Formalism

The nuclear shell model stands as a cornerstone of our understanding of nuclear structure,
providing profound insights into various low-energy properties of atomic nuclei. Central to its framework is the employment of extensive diagonalization within a many-body harmonic oscillator basis. Given the rotational invariance of the nuclear force, it is advantageous to employ single-particle harmonic oscillator states characterized by well-defined quantum numbers: principal quantum number (
n
), azimuthal quantum number (
l
), total angular momentum (
j

j_{z}
). In the traditional shell model, the effective interactions among the valence nucleons are considered in a truncated model space instead of all nucleons being active. Those effective interactions are expressed in terms of single-particle energies (SPE) and TBMEs for different truncated spaces, which are key ingredients of the shell model calculations.

In the early phase of the nuclear shell model, effective interactions with perturbative approaches were developed
[

41

[

,
49
,
50

In addition, using similarity transformations, the
ab initio

[

,

,

,

[

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=[ME(T_{z}=1/2)+ME(T_{z}=-1/2)]/2

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=0.

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=ME(T_{z}=0)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=[ME(T_{z}=1)-ME(T_{z}=-1)]/2

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=[ME(T_{z}=1)+ME(T_{z}=-1)]/2-ME(T_{z}=0).

In this work, we have developed and tested the ISB versions of the DJ16A interaction.

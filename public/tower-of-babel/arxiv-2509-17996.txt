Rank 2 2 vector bundles and degrees of points of del Pezzo surfaces
Claire Voisin

Abstract
We study points and 0 0 -cycles on del Pezzo surfaces defined over a field K K of characteristic 0 0 , with emphasis on cubic surfaces. We prove that a cubic surface that admits a point defined over a field extension of K K of degree coprime to 3 3 either has a K K -point or has a point defined over a field extension of degree 4 4 . This improves a result of Coray (who allowed also field extensions of degree 10 10 ). We also prove that 0 0 -cycles of degree ≥ 18 \geq 18 on a cubic surface are effective and get similar results for degree 2 2 and degree 1 1 del Pezzo surfaces, improving results of Colliot-Thélène. In a different direction, we prove that the third symmetric product of a cubic hypersurface of dimension ≥ 2 \geq 2 is unirational over any field, and that it is not stably rational in general (over a non-algebraically closed field).

Rational self-maps and the proof of Theorem

We will use the following rather standard construction (see for example

\mu_{s}:E\rightarrow E,\,x\mapsto mx-sH\in{\rm Pic}^{1}(E)=E.

Using the maps

\displaystyle\Psi_{s}:G\times G\dashrightarrow X^{[3]}.

The construction goes as follows.
For a generic

(ii) follows from (i) and the exact sequence (

Proposition 3.2

h^{0}(S,E(-D))\not=0

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{I}_{Z_{d}}(l+1)(-D))\not=0
by the exact sequence (
21
). By (
27
), we thus conclude that

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1)(-D))>d\geq h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))+1.

This provides a contradiction since
h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))=3(l+1)
.
∎

We finally deal with the case
s=1
. By the case
s\geq 2
which is already treated, we can assume that

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))=d+2\,\,{\rm or}\,\,h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))=d+3.

(32)

In this case, the inequality

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1)\otimes H^{-1})\geq d+1

of (
30
) gives us

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1)\otimes H^{-1})\geq h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-2,

which is impossible since
l\geq 0
, hence the curve
C
which is mobile imposes at least
3
conditions to the linear system
|\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)|
.

In order to conclude the proof, we need to study the case where the curve
C
is disjoint from
\{x_{1},\ldots,\,x_{s}\}
.
By assumption, there exists for a generic set
Z_{s}
of points
x_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,\,s
, a curve
C
and a section
\sigma^{\prime}
of
E(-C)
which vanishes at all the
x_{i}
’s, while the curve
C
does not pass through any of them. It follows that

\sigma^{\prime}\in H^{0}(S,E(-C)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z_{s}}).

However, we have

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,E(-C))\leq h^{0}(S,\mathcal{I}_{Z_{d}}(l+1)(-C)).

(33)

We can obviously assume that

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{I}_{Z_{d}}(l+1))=2s\,\,{\rm or}\,\,h^{0}(S,\mathcal{I}_{Z_{d}}(l+1))=2s+1.

(34)

It follows from (
33
), (
34
) and (
27
) that

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,E(-C))\leq 2s+1-(l+2)<2s,

(35)

because
C
imposes at least
l+2
conditions to
H^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))
, hence also to
H^{0}(S,\mathcal{I}_{Z_{d}}(l+1))
.
We know that there exists a nonzero section of
E(-C)
vanishing along a generic set of
s
points in
S
.
WE claim that this implies that for a generic set of
s
points in
S
, there exists a curve
C^{\prime}
passing through at least one of these points and such that any section of
E(-C)
vanishing at these
s
points vanishes along
C^{\prime}
. Indeed, this is done by a dimension count: we consider the universal vanishing locus

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(S,E(-C)))\times S^{[k]}

and its Zariski open set
\Gamma_{f}\subset\Gamma
consisting of pairs
(\sigma,\,\{x_{1},\ldots,\,x_{k}\})
where the
x_{i}
are all distinct and the
0
-locus of
\sigma
is
0
-dimensional near
Z
. Using (
35
), we have
{\rm dim}\,\Gamma_{f}\leq 2s-1
, hence
\Gamma_{f}
cannot dominate
S^{[k]}
by the second projection. We are now reduced to the previous situation, or we can conclude by saying that
{\rm deg}\,C\geq 1
, and
{\rm deg}\,C^{\prime}\geq 2
since
C^{\prime}
has to be mobile, so
{\rm deg}\,(C+C^{\prime})\geq 3
, which contradicts Claim
3.4
.
∎

Let us give an example illustrating the difficulty for a nongeneric choice of
Z\subset S
.

Example 3.5
.

Assume that
S
contains a line
\Delta
with residual conic
C\in|\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)(-\Delta)|
. Consider the vector bundle
E=\mathcal{O}_{S}(\Delta)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{S}(C)
on
S
. If we take a general section
\sigma
, its zero-locus

is the intersection
\Delta\cap C
of the line

with one conic in the pencil, hence consists of

The vector bundle thus has

3.1
Another useful effectivity result

We will use in the next sections the following result for
0
-cycles on a surface. A similar statement already appears in
[
3
]
where it is used to simplify Coray’s proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface over a field
K
of characteristic
0
and let
H
be a very ample line bundle on
S
. Then if
Z\subset S
is a subscheme of length
d
, of class
z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
, and

\displaystyle d\leq h^{0}(S,H)-2,

(36)

the
0
-cycle
c_{1}(H)^{2}-z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
defined over
K
is rationally equivalent to an effective
0
-cycle on
S
.

Proof.

Consider the smooth projective variety
B=S^{[d]}
, which is defined over
K
, with function field

K_{B}:=K(B)
. Let
\eta={\rm Spec}\,K(B)
be its generic point. The universal subscheme

\mathcal{Z}_{d}\subset B\times S

has for generic fiber a subscheme

\mathcal{Z}_{\eta}\subset S_{\eta}

of length
d
, which specializes to
Z\subset S
at the point
[Z]\in S^{[d]}
parameterizing
Z
.
We first claim that the effectivity result is true for the generic subscheme
\mathcal{Z}_{\eta}
. Indeed, it follows from Bertini applied inductively on
d
that for
d\leq h^{0}(S,H)-1
, the general curve
C\in|H|_{\eta}
containing
Z_{\eta}
is smooth, hence irreducible. Then for
d\leq h^{0}(S,H)-2
, which is our assumption (
36
), the intersection of two general curves containing
Z_{\eta}
is proper, hence provides a subscheme of codimension
2
and of class
H^{2}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
, which by construction contains
Z_{\eta}
. This proves the claim.
The result then follows from this claim, using Fulton’s specialization from
{\rm CH}_{0}(S_{\eta})
to
{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
at the point
[Z]\in B(K)
, which as noted in
[
3
]
, preserves effectivity.
∎

For the applications, we will need the following variant, which is proved exactly in the same way.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface and
L=\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
an ample line bundle on
S
. Let
d
,
l
be integers such that
\mathcal{O}_{S}(l)
is generated by its sections and

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))\geq d+1,

(37)

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-d>h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)).

(38)

Then if
z_{d}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
is effective of degree
d
, the cycle

z^{\prime}:=l(l+1)c_{1}(L)^{2}-z_{d}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)

is effective.

Proof.

The proof works as before.The inequality (
37
) guarantees that the generic effective
0
-cycle of degree
d
is supported on a curve
C
which is a member of
|\mathcal{O}_{S}(l)|
, and which is irreducible by Bertini. The inequality (
38
) then guarantees that there is a curve
C^{\prime}
supporting
z_{d}
, which is a member of
|\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1)|
, and which does not have
C
as a component. Hence the generic effective
0
-cycle

z_{d,\eta}
is supported on the complete intersection of the curves
C
and
C^{\prime}
, so

l(l+1)c_{1}(L)^{2}-z_{d,\eta}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S_{\eta})

is effective. Lemma
3.7
then follows by Fulton’s specialization.
∎

concerning degree

del Pezzo surfaces. As in the cubic surface case, the key tool is the rank
2
vector bundle construction from Section
3
. In that section however, Proposition
3.2
had been fully established only in the cubic surface. Let us first prove the analogous result for degree
2
del Pezzo surface.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
2
over a field of characteristic
0
. Let
d,\,l,\,s
be three nonnegative integers with
l\geq 1
, and let
z_{d}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S),\,z_{s}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
be two effective
0
-cycles on
S
of respective degrees
d
and
s
.
Assume the following inequalities are satisfied

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))=1+l^{2}+l<d,

(52)

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-d=1+(l+1)^{2}+l+1-d\geq 2s.

(53)

Then the cycle
z_{d}-z_{s}
is effective.

Proof.

The assumptions are exactly the same as in Proposition
3.2
(only the Riemann-Roch formula has changed). The strategy of the proof is of course the same and the only point that needs to be checked more precisely is the analog of Lemma
3.3
, the proof of which was specific to the cubic surface case. In other words, we reduced the proof to showing

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
S
be a smooth degree
2
del Pezzo surface over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
d,\,l\geq 1,\,s
be three nonnegative integers satisfying the two inequalities

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))<d,

(54)

\displaystyle 1+(l+1)^{2}+l+1-d=h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-d\geq 2s.

(55)

Then, for a general subscheme

Z_{d}\subset S
of length
d
, a general vector bundle
E
constructed from an extension class
e\in{\rm Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Z_{d}}(d+1),\mathcal{O}_{S})
, and general set of
s
points
x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{s}\in S
, there exists a section
\sigma^{\prime}
of
E
vanishing at all points
x_{i}
, and whose zero-set is of dimension
0
.

Proof.

Mutatis mutandis, the proof is the same as that of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Proposition
5.1
is now proved.
∎

Let us now prove Theorem
1.14
(a), which is the following statement.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
S
be a smooth del Pezzo surface of degree
2
over a field
K
of characteristic
0
. Then any effective
0
-cycle
z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
can be written as

\displaystyle z=z^{\prime}+\gamma h_{2}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(S),

(56)

where
z^{\prime}
is effective of degree
\leq 13
.

Proof.

Let
z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
be an effective
0
-cycle of degree
d
and let
l
be such that

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))=1+l^{2}+l<d.

(57)

Assume first that
d\leq h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-2
. Then
(l+1)^{2}h_{2}-z
is effective thanks to Lemma
3.6
, which applies since
l\geq 1
and
\mathcal{O}_{S}(2)
is very ample. Replacing
z
by
(l+1)^{2}h_{2}-z
if necessary, we can thus assume

d\leq 2(l+1)^{2}-d
.
Thus, from now on, we can assume that

\displaystyle d\leq(l+1)^{2}.

(58)

Using (
58
), we get that

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-d\geq l+2

so once
l\geq 2
, we get that
h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-d\geq 4
. We can
thus apply Proposition
5.1
and thus we proved that
z-h_{2}
is effective when
{\rm deg}\,z\geq 1+h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(2))=8
, unless we are in one of the following cases

\displaystyle{\rm deg}\,z=h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1)),\,\,{\rm or}\,\,{\rm deg}\,z=h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-1.

(59)

We now treat the missing cases (
59
). Let
z^{\prime}:=z+h_{2}
. Then

d^{\prime}={\rm deg}\,z^{\prime}=d+2=h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))+2,\,\,{\rm resp.}\,\,{\rm deg}\,z^{\prime}=d+2=h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))+1.

Furthermore

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+2))-d^{\prime}=2(l+2)-2,\,\,{\rm resp.}\,\,h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+2))-d^{\prime}=2(l+2)-1.

We can thus apply Proposition
5.1
to
z^{\prime}
with
s=4
once
2(l+2)-2\geq 8
, that is
l\geq 3
. So we proved that, in the cases (
59
),

z^{\prime}-2h_{2}=z-h_{2}

is effective if
l\geq 3
. When
l=2
and (
59
) holds, we have
d=13
or
d=12
.
Putting everything together we proved at this point by induction that any effective cycle
z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
can be written
as

\displaystyle z=\pm z^{\prime}+\gamma h_{2}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(S),

(60)

where
z^{\prime}
is effective of degree
\leq 13
. However there is an involution of
S
which follows from the existence of the degree
2
morphism
S\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
given by the anticanonical system. This involution
\iota
has the property that for any
z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
,

\displaystyle z+\iota(z)=({\rm deg}\,z)h_{2}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(S).

(61)

Using (
61
), we can arrange to make the sign in (
60
) positive, so
Theorem
5.3
is proved.
∎

We finally prove Theorem
1.14
(b).

Proof of Theorem
1.14
(b).

By Theorem
5.3
, it suffices to study the case of an effective
0
-cycles
z
of degree
d\leq 11
. When
10\leq d\leq 11
, we argue as follows. In this case,

d\leq h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(3))-2

so that by Lemma
3.6

z^{\prime}:=9h_{2}-z_{d}

is effective, with
{\rm deg}(z^{\prime})<{\rm deg}(z)
. We are thus reduced to the case where
d=8,\,9
. As
h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(2))=7
, we can apply in these cases the vector bundle method of Section
3
with
l=2
. As we have
h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(3))=13
, we get in both cases
h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(3))-d\geq 4
, hence Proposition
5.1
tells that in both cases,
z-h_{2}
is effective of degree
\leq 7
.
∎

6
Zero-cycles on del Pezzo surfaces of degree
1

In the case of a del Pezzo surface
S
of degree
d_{S}=1
, using again the notation
-K_{S}=:\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
, we have

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))=1+\frac{1}{2}(l^{2}+l)

for
l\geq 0
. Note that
S
has a point, defined as the base-locus of the linear system
|-K_{S}|
. As usual we will denote its class by
h_{1}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
.
We note that in this case the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{S}(3)
is very ample, as one sees by looking at its restrictions to the elliptic curves in the pencil
|\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)|
. The line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{S}(2)
is generated by sections and induces a degree
2
morphism onto a surface of degree
2
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, which has to be a singular quadric.

The analog of Proposition
3.2
is now

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
1
defined over a field
K
of characteristic
0
. Let
d,\,l\geq 1,\,s
be three integers such that

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))=1+\frac{1}{2}(l^{2}+l)<d

(62)

\displaystyle h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))\geq d+2s.

(63)

Then for any effective
0
-cycle
z_{d}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
, the cycle
z_{d}-sh_{1}
is effective.

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition
3.2
and we will not repeat the details here.

We will now prove
Theorem
1.16
(b), which is the following statement.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
S
be a smooth degree
1
del Pezzo surface over a field
K
of characteristic
0
. Then any effective
0
-cycle
z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
can be written as

\displaystyle z=\pm z^{\prime}+\gamma h_{1},

(64)

where
z^{\prime}
is effective of degree
15,\,7
or
\leq 4
.

Proof.

Let
z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
be an effective
0
-cycle of degree
d\geq 5
. We choose
l
such that

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l))<d\leq h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1)).

Note that, in particular
l\geq 2
, hence
\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1)
is very ample.

If
d\leq h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-2
, we get by Lemma
3.6
that
(l+1)^{2}h_{1}-z_{d}
is effective, so we can assume up to replacing
z_{d}
by
(l+1)^{2}h_{1}-z_{d}
, that

\displaystyle d\leq\frac{1}{2}(l+1)^{2}.

(65)

It follows from (
65
) that

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-d\geq 1+\frac{1}{2}(l+1).

As
l\geq 2
,
1+\frac{1}{2}(l+1)\geq 2
, hence Proposition
6.1
applies with
s=1
and tells that
z_{d}-h_{1}
is effective.

We next have to consider the cases where

1.

d=h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))
.

2.

d=h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))-1
.

In Case
1
, we replace
z_{d}
by
z^{\prime}=z_{d}+h_{1}
, and in Case
2
, we replace
z_{d}
by
z^{\prime}=z_{d}+2h_{1}
. In both cases, we have
{\rm deg}\,z^{\prime}=h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+1))+1
.

Furthermore, we have

h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(l+2))-{\rm deg}\,z^{\prime}=l+1.

In Case
1
, if
l\geq 3
, we get by Proposition
6.1
that

z^{\prime}-2h_{1}
is effective, hence
z-h_{1}
is effective.

In Case
2
, if
l\geq 5
, , we get by Proposition
6.1
that

z^{\prime}-3h_{1}
is effective, hence
z-h_{1}
is effective.

In conclusion, when
d\geq 5
, the only cases where we cannot conclude that
\pm z-h_{1}
is effective of degree strictly smaller than the degree of
z
is when we are in Case
2
with
l\leq 4
or in Case
1
with
l\leq 2
.
Case
2
with
l=4
provides
d=15
. Case
1
with
l=2
provides
d=7
. So Theorem
1.16
(b) is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.16
(a).

In view of Theorem
6.2
, it suffices to prove the following

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
1
defined over a field
K
of characteric
0
. Then for any effective cycle
z
of degree
d\leq 15
, we can write

z=\lambda h_{1}-z^{\prime}\,\,{\rm in}\,\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S),

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
and
z^{\prime}
is effective of degree
\leq 15
.

Proof.

If
{\rm deg}\,z=15
, using the fact that
h^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(5))=16
and Lemma
3.7
, we find that
z^{\prime}:=30h_{1}-z
is effective. As
{\rm deg}\,z^{\prime}=15
, the lemma is proved in this case. If
10\leq{\rm deg}\,z\leq 14
, Lemma
3.6
tells that
z^{\prime}:=25h_{1}-z
is effective. As
{\rm deg}\,z^{\prime}\leq 15
, the lemma is proved in this case. If
1\leq{\rm deg}\,z\leq 9
, Lemma
3.6
tells that
z^{\prime}:=16h_{1}-z
is effective, hence the lemma is fully proved.
∎

Theorem
1.16
(a) is now proved.
∎

Critical set for surface diffeomorphisms revisited
Sylvain Crovisier, Enrique Pujals

Abstract
We propose a notion of critical set for two-dimensional surface diffeomorphisms as an intrinsically defined object designed to play a role analogous to that of critical points in one-dimensional dynamics.



1
Introduction

The motivation of the present paper stems from the classical understanding that, in the one-dimensional setting, the presence of critical points (where the derivative vanishes) is a key obstruction to hyperbolicity. Conversely, smooth one-dimensional endomorphisms without critical points are either hyperbolic, conjugate to hyperbolic or conjugate to an irrational rotation; see
[
M
]
, where a real one dimensional version of Fatou’s hyperbolicity criteria for holomorphic endomorphisms of the Riemann sphere was proved. In this sense, any compact invariant set is “essentially hyperbolic” if and only if it does not contain critical points.
Extending this perspective to two dimensions, we pursue a similar criterion: to isolate the phenomena that
obstruct hyperbolicity for surface diffeomorphisms
and, dually,
those whose absence guaranties it
. In this context, the notion of a critical set serves as a two-dimensional analog of the critical point and aims to capture precisely those dynamical features that impede hyperbolic behavior.

Since it was proved in
[
PS1
]
that any compact invariant set exhibiting a
dominated splitting
for a generic
C^{2}
surface diffeomorphism is a hyperbolic set, it follows that searching for dynamical obstructions to hyperbolicity is essentially equivalent to identifying obstructions to domination. The critical set we propose is therefore naturally aligned with this goal:
a well defined set whose presence obstruct domination, and whose absence corresponds to the existence of a dominated splitting
.

A similar task was proposed in
[
PRH
]
and in fact, we follow that perspective by simplifying the notion of the critical set introduced in
[
PRH
]
, overcoming certain cumbersome properties that limited its applicability and obtaining in that sense, a more accessible and intrinsic description of criticality in surface dynamics.

Informally, to exhibit a dominated splitting means that there is an invariant cone field that is forwardly contracted and backwardly expanded; in that sense, a critical point is a point with
“a cone that is not contracted neither for the future nor for the past”
. More precisely, this notion is recast in terms of the projective dynamics induced by the derivative (see paragraph a- in the present section for a formal definition). Moreover, under the assumption of dissipation (“far from homotheties” in the general case), on the one hand, holds that “
no critical points is equivalent to dominated splitting
” and on the other hand follows that a full orbit cannot be an orbit of critical points.

Tangencies associated to the stable and unstable manifold of periodic points (see paragraph i- in section
2
for the precise definition and discussion) are natural obstruction to dominated splitting; however, on one hand tangencies are invariant by iteration and in that sense would not qualify as a good definition for a critical point, and on the other hand generically there are no tangencies of periodic points but by the results of Newhouse (see
[
N
]
) there are open sets of surface diffeomorphisms whose non-wandering set is not hyperbolic (neither has dominated splitting) and therefore, contains
critical points
. Moreover, under strong conditions on the critical point, one can associate to the critical direction of a critical points a stable and (center-)unstable manifold that are tangent, so in that sense, one recovers the notion of tangency but not necessary for periodic points. In section
2
it is also shown how tangencies can be related to critical points (see lemma
10
in the aforementioned section)

As in the one-dimensional setting, one can impose conditions on critical points to gain a more detailed understanding of the non-wandering set, for instance, obtaining a finite decomposition into transitive components or ensuring the existence of SRB (Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen) measures. A notable class of such systems is given by Misiurewicz maps, where the critical points are non-recurrent. These maps exhibit a finite number of transitive pieces and often display rich chaotic behavior, including the presence of physical measures and SRB measures. In the two-dimensional context, it is natural to expect that a suitably defined notion of criticality could play an analogous role. In this paper, we explore this possibility by introducing and analyzing a class of systems we refer to as two-dimensional Misiurewicz diffeomorphisms, where a version of non-recurrence of the critical set enables similar decomposition into finite transitive pieces.

That approach opens the possibility to consider other settings where a more general condition on the critical set could have strong dynamical implications. A successful development of this perspective is developed in
[
CLPY
]
where a strong condition on the critical sets for infinite renormalizable dissipative diffeomorphisms allows to obtain a general description of the dynamics (see paragraph v- in section
2
).

a – The critical set.

Consider a compact boundaryless riemannian surface
M
and a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
f
.
The derivative of
f
induces a dynamics on the unitarian tangent bundle,
G_{x}\colon T^{1}_{x}M\to T^{1}_{f(x)}M
, usually called the
projective tangent bunddle map
,
defined by

G_{x}(v)=\frac{D_{x}f(v)}{|D_{x}f(v)|},

and it induces a projective cocycle. Let
g
be the derivative of
G
along the fibers

T_{x}^{1}M
of the unitary tangent bundle, more precisely

g_{x}(v)=|D_{w}G_{x}|_{w=v}|.

We denote the composition
G^{n}_{x}=\bigcirc_{i=0}^{n-1}G_{f^{i}(x)}
and
to simplify the notation, we denote
D_{w}G^{n}_{x}
by
g^{n}_{x}
and sometimes
only by
g^{n}.
Observe that
g^{n}_{x}(v)=\Pi g_{f^{i}(x)}(G^{i}_{x}(v)).
It is easy to show that

|g^{n}(v)|=\frac{|\det(Df^{n})|}{|Df^{n}(v)|^{2}}.

Let
\Lambda\subset M
be an invariant compact set. That set may contain attracting, repelling or elliptic periodic points, and differently as it is done in
[
PRH
]
, it is not assumed that
f_{|\Lambda}
is dissipative or that
\Lambda
is contained in the limit set of
f
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Lambda\subset M
be an invariant compact set of a surface diffeomorphism
f.
It is said that
x
is a
critical point
of
f
in
\Lambda
if there exists
v\in T_{x}M
such that

|g^{n}(v)|\geq 1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The direction
v
is called the critical direction and

{\rm Crit}(f,\Lambda)=\{x\in\Lambda,\;\exists v\in T_{x}^{1}M,\;\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\;|g^{n}(v)|\geq 1\},

is called the critical set of
f
in
\Lambda.

In short, a critical point is a point having a direction
v
such that
g^{n}(v)
is bounded from below for all integers.

Definition 2
.

Let
f\in Diff^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
be a compact invariant set of
f
. It is said that
f_{|\Lambda}
is
\delta-
far from homotheties
, if it does not exist a point
x
and a vector
v\in T^{1}_{x}M
satisfying

(1-\delta)^{n}<g^{n}(v)<(1+\delta)^{n},

for all
n>0.

Roughly speaking, being far from homotheties means that for any point, there are arbitrary large iterates such that the derivative cocycle is far from being conformal.

Recall that a compact invariant set
\Lambda
has a dominated splitting if the there exists a subbundle decomposition
T_{\Lambda}M=E\oplus F
with
\dim(E)=\dim(F)=1
and a positive constant
\lambda
smaller than one satisfying
\frac{||Df_{|E}||}{||Df_{|F}||}<\lambda.
In term of the projective dynamics, it holds that the subbundle
E
is a repeller and
F
is an attractor, i.e.
g(E)>1>g(F)
and this equivalent to the fact that for any vector
v
it holds that either
g^{n}(v)\to 0
or
g^{-n}(v)\to 0
for
n\to+\infty
. Observe that this is equivalent to have a contracting and a repelling cone field. The next result establishes a tight relation between the existences of critical points and dominated splitting.

Theorem A
.

Let
f\in Diff^{1}(M)
and
\Lambda
be a compact invariant set of
f
. If
f_{|\Lambda}
is far from homotheties it follows that
\Lambda
admits a dominated splitting
T_{\Lambda}=E\oplus F
, with
\dim(E)=\dim(F)=1
if and only if
{\rm Crit}(f,\Lambda)
is empty.

As a consequence of the first theorem in
[
PS1
]
it follows from theorem
A
that for a generic diffeomorphisms of surface, given a compact invariant set
\Lambda
such that
f_{|\Lambda}
is far from homotheties

\Lambda
is a hyperbolic set if and only if
{\rm Crit}(f,\Lambda)=\emptyset
. If one considers
C^{2}-
diffeos, from
[
PS2
]
one concludes that any compact invariant set with a dominated splitting, the limit set can be decomposed into a finite number of normally hyperbolic closed curves with dynamics conjugate to irrational rotation, finite number of normally hyperbolic periodic intervals and compact sets conjugate to a hyperbolic ones such that the period of non-hyperbolic periodic points is bounded. For a detailed discussion of results about dominated splitting needed in this paper, see section
3
.

One can wonder if the existence of critical point in an invariant set is a property invariant by conjugacies. This is not the case for
C^{0}-
conjugacies: one can have two
C^{0}-
conjugate surface diffeomorphisms such that the invariant set of one of them is a hyperbolic set (and therefore does not contain critical points) and the other exhibiting a (cubic) tangency (see paragraph ii- in section
2
for more details). However, having critical points is a property invariant by
C^{1}-
conjugacies since a dominated splitting is preserved by a diffeomorphisms.

One can go beyond and wonder if being a critical point is independent of change metric
and invariant by conjugacies. To address that question, one has to slightly modifying the notion of critical point.

Remark 3
.

One could define
x
to be a
critical point

if there exists
v\in T^{1}_{x}M
and
K>0
such that

\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\;|g^{n}(v)|\geq K.

It follows immediately now that the notion of critical point is independent of the metric and,
similarly, one can adapt the proof of theorem
A
with the above definition of critical point.

b– Hypothesis that imply that
f_{|\Lambda}
is far from homotheties.

Given an invariant compact set, one can wonder under which hypothesis one can conclude that
f_{|\Lambda}
is far from homotheties. In
[
PRH
]
was proved that if
f_{|\Lambda}
is dissipative and
\Lambda
does not contain periodic orbits, then
f_{|\Lambda}
is far from homotheties (see main lemma in
[
PRH
]
). A similar statement can be obtained assuming that the dynamic is area expanding and there are no repellers in
\Lambda
.
A more general result is the following

Theorem B
.

Let
f\in Diff^{1}(M)
and let
\Lambda
be a compact invariant set. If there exists
\gamma>0
such that for any invariant measure, the difference of the Lyapunov exponents of any regular points is larger than
\gamma
, then
f_{|\Lambda}
is far from homotheties.

The previous result can be reformulated for the case of dissipative dynamics, meaning that there is
0<b<1
such that for any
x\in\Lambda
it holds
|det(D_{x}f)|<b
.

Theorem C
.

If
f_{|\Lambda}
is dissipative there exists
\delta:=\delta(b)
and a finite number of sinks (attracting periodic points) such that
f
restricted to the intersection of
\Lambda
with the complement of the basin of attraction of those sinks is
\delta-
far from homotheties.

c– Properties of the critical set.

In what follows we consider some properties of the critical set in terms of its behavior under recurrence that resembles classical properties of critical points in the one-dimensional context, and we consider how the set of critical points changes under small perturbation of the dynamics.

Proposition 4
.

Let
f\in Diff^{1}(M^{2})
and let
\Lambda
be a compact invariant set, the following proporties hold

–

If
f_{/\Lambda}
is far from homotheties, then the critical direction associated to a critical point is unique.

–

If a critical point is periodic, then the derivative at the period has only one eigenvalue: it is either an homothety, or an elliptic point or a parabolic point (up to multiplication by a scalar). In particular, in the dissipative context, the periodic point is an attracting periodic point.

–

If
f_{/\Lambda}
is far from homotheties, it follows that if all iterates of an orbit are critical points then the orbit is an attracting periodic point.

–

The critical set is closed and depends semi-continuously on
f
: for any neighborhood
U
of
{\rm Crit}(f,\Lambda)
there exists a neighborhood
V
of
\Lambda
such that if
g
is close enough to
f
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
is a compact invariant set of
g
contained in
V
then
{\rm Crit}(g,\Lambda^{\prime})
is contained in
U.

The second item of the proposition for dissipative dynamics is similar to an obvious result for one-dimensional endomorphisms: if a critical point is periodic, then it is an attracting periodic point. The proof of that fact (in the dissipative context) goes by considering the returns of the critical direction under the iterates that make the critical point periodic: if the direction is invariant then the periodic point is an attracting periodic point such that the derivative has either one or two invariant directions (with the same rate of contraction), if the direction is not invariant then the derivative has a complex eigenvalue with modulus smaller than one.

Also for one-dimensional endomorphisms one can easily show that the recurrence of the critical point to itself is sub-exponential, otherwise the critical point is in the basin of attraction of an attracting periodic point. A similar result can be obtained for dissipative diffeomorphisms under the assumption of no exponential growths of the norm.

Proposition 5
.

Let
f
be a
C^{2}
surface diffeomorphism and
\Lambda
be a dissipative invariant compact set, satisfying
\underset{k\to+\infty}{\limsup}\tfrac{1}{k}\log\|Df^{k}|_{\Lambda}\|\leq 0
which contains a critical points
c

with exponential recurrence, i.e.
\underset{k\to+\infty}{\liminf}\tfrac{1}{k}\log d(f^{k}(c),c)<0.

Then
\Lambda
contains a sink.

d– Critical points for dissipative dynamics of the disk.

In what follows, we assume that
{\mathbb{D}}
is an open disk that is properly invariant by
f
, that is
\overline{f({\mathbb{D}})}\subset{\mathbb{D}}
and
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to f({\mathbb{D}})
is a diffeomorphism. To avoid notation, such a class of maps is denoted as
Diff^{r}({\mathbb{D}})
and observe that any map in that class can be extended to a diffeomorphism of the sphere.

For endomorphisms of the interval holds that
any unstable branch of a repelling periodic point either contains a critical (turning) point or the branch is contained in a periodic interval where the dynamic is a diffeomorphism
.

A similar result, with the appropriate adaptation for diffeomorphisms, is provided for dissipative diffeomorphisms of the disk under an additional hypothesis introduced in
[
CP
]
.

Definition 6
.

Given a boundaryless surface
S
and a dissipative smooth diffeomorphisms
f:S\to f(S)\subset S
, it is said that
f
is
mildly dissipative
if for any ergodic measure
\mu
which is not supported on an attracting periodic point,and for
\mu
-almost every point, each of its two stable branches meets the boundary of
S
.

In
[
CP
]
it was shown
that mild dissipation is satisfied for large classes of systems. For instance, it holds for some
C^{2}

open sets of diffeomorphisms of the disc including diffeomorphisms close to one-dimensional endomorphisms, and also for polynomial automorphisms of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
whose
Jacobian is close to zero, including the diffeomorphisms from the Hénon family
with Jacobian of modulus less than
1/4
(up to restricting to an appropriate trapped disc). This class captures certain properties of one-dimensional maps, but keeps two-dimensional
features showing all the well-known complexity of dissipative surface diffeomorphisms.
The dynamics of the new class, in some sense, is intermediate between one-dimensional
dynamics and general surface diffeomorphisms.

The two-dimensional result that resembles the one-dimensional stated before is proved for the closure of an unstable branch; moreover, the second option that holds in the one-dimensional situation does not make much sense, given that the map is already a diffeomorphism. So, that option is replaced by saying that the accumulation set of the unstable branch is in the basin of attraction a periodic point or it is an invariant closed arc
\gamma
that is
normally hyperbolic
; i.e.
\gamma
has a dominated splitting
E^{s}\oplus F
such that
F
is tangent to the curve
\gamma
and
E^{s}
is a contractive direction. To be precise, given a saddle periodic point, by the accumulation set of one of its unstable branches we mean the accumulation set of the union of the iterates of a fundamental domain of the unstable branch.

Theorem D
.

Let
f\in Diff^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
such that
f_{|{\mathbb{D}}}
is mildly dissipative. Then the accumulation set of any unstable branch of a saddle periodic point satisfies one of the following cases:

–

either it is a (semi-)attracting periodic point and the branch is contained in the basin of that periodic point,

–

or it is a normally hyperbolic attracting periodic arc,

–

or it contains a critical point.

Moreover, if an unstable branch
\Gamma^{u}
of a periodic point intersects its stable manifold, then the unstable branch
\Gamma^{u}
accumulates on a critical point.

Observe that in the previous theorem it is not claimed that the critical point belongs to the limit set. Examples of that situation are considered in section
2
, paragraph
vi
.

f– Misiurewicz diffeomorphisms.

A smooth one-dimensional endomorphism is called Misiurewicz if all itscritical points are non-recurrent, meaning that their forward iterates do not accumulate on themselves. The absence of critical recurrence ensures uniform expansion away from critical points, and as a result, the system behaves like a uniformly expanding system on most of its domain.

In the present section, we explore a similar two dimensional version.

Definition 7
.

Let
\Lambda
be a compact invariant set of a
C^{2}
surface diffeomorphism
f
. It is said that
f_{|\Lambda}
is a
Misiurewicz diffeomorphism
if there exists a neighborhood
V
of the critical set
Crit(f,\Lambda)
such that any
c\in Crit(f,\Lambda)
satisfies
\alpha(c)\cap V=\emptyset
and
\omega(c)\cap V=\emptyset
.

The known examples of Misiurewicz maps are described in section
2
, in paragraph ii. Naturally, one can wonder if these are the only natural classes of examples. In terms of the problem on how the dynamic on the critical set can be useful to characterize the whole dynamics of the non-wandering set, the natural question is the following:

Question 8
.

Let
\Lambda
be a compact invariant set of a
C^{2}
surface diffeomorphisms
f
. If
f_{|\Lambda}
is Misiurewicz does it follow that
\Lambda
can be decomposed into a finite number of transitive sets and finite number of normally hyperbolic arcs?

If one assumes that all the periodic points are hyperbolic, and if the previous question is answered positively, then the dynamcs is decomposed in finite transitive pieces. If one allows non-hyperbolicity, one can have infinitely many transitive isolated sets; for instances, a normally hyperbolic arc such that the restriction to that arc is a one-dimensional diffeomorphism exhibiting infinitely many attracting fixed points (as in the case of the map
x\to x^{r}\sin(\frac{1}{x})
).

In the following theorem
E
, we provide a partial answer to question
8
for mildly dissipative diffeomorphisms of the disk focusing on non-trivial
homoclinic classes
instead of general transitive sets. In this paper, a homoclinic class is the closure of the sets of
crossing
points of the stable and unstable manifolds of a saddle periodic point (see definition
31
). Usually, in the classical definition of a homoclinic class only transversal intersections are considered (it is said that an intersection is transversal if the tangent spaces of the stable and unstable manifold at the intersection point are not collinear); we include
crossing intersection
in the sense that the unstable manifold at the point of intersection also intersects the two connected components of any “band” of the local stable manifold (see definition
32
in section
8
).

Theorem E
.

Let
f
be a mildly dissipative Misiurewicz
C^{2}-
diffeomorphism of the disk. Then, the number of non-trivial generalized homoclinic classes is finite.

Moreover, there exists a positive integer
n_{0}
such that any saddle periodic point with period larger than
n_{0}
that is not contained in one of the finite non-trivial homoclinic classes belongs to a non-trivial periodic normally hyperbolic periodic arc.

Observe that there could exist infinitely many saddle periodic orbits that do not belong to a non-trivial homoclinic class and there could also exist infinitely many normally hyperbolic arcs.

Examples

In this section we present different examples of non-hyperbolic diffeomorphism where one can identify the critical set.

i-
Tangencies and critical points.

Homoclinic and heteroclinic tangencies have played a fundamental role showing the existence of open sets of non hyperbolic surface diffeomorphisms. In our context, these systems have critical points. So, tangencies may provide a natural candidate to critical points.

Definition 9
.

We say that
f

has a
heteroclinic tangency
if there is a pair of periodic points
p,q
such that
there is a point
x\in W^{s}(p)\cap W^{u}(q)
with
T_{x}W^{s}(p)=T_{x}W^{u}(q).
In this case, we say that
x
is a point of tangency.

Lemma 1.3.2 in
[
PRH
]
relates the tangencies with the critical points.
Observe that if
x
is a point of tangency, then any iterate of
x
is also a point of
tangency. However, the lemma says that there is only one
x
point in the orbit of tangency such that
|g^{-n}_{x}(v)|\geq 1
for any
n\geq 0
and
k
a positive integer such that
|g^{n}_{f^{k}(x)}(G^{k}(v))|\geq 1
also for any
n\geq 0,
where
v
is a vector in the tangent intersection of
T_{x}W^{s}(p)
with
T_{x}W^{u}(q)
. In that sense, a tangency seems to be a critical point up to an iterate. However, in the following lemma it is shown that given a tangency, there is an iterate that is a critical point in the sense of definition
1

Lemma 10
.

Let
x
be a heteroclinic or homoclinic tangency then, there is an iterate of

that it is a critical point.

Proof.

We provide two proofs, one that relies on theorem
A
and another one that follows from an easy claims about finite product of positive numbers (that also highlight the idea beyond the proof of theorem
A
). For simplicity, let us assume that the tangency is associated to fixed points

For the first proof, consider the closure of the orbit of the tangency that it is formed by ; it follows immediately that this set is formed by the orbit of the tangency and the two fixed points where the tangency accumulates; that set does not have dominated splitting and so by theorem
A
it contains a critical point that cannot be the fixed points.

The second proof follows from next claim:

Claim 11
.

Let
a_{1},\dots a_{N}
be a sequence of positive integers such that
\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\geq 1;
then there exist
K\leq N
such that
\prod_{i=0}^{j}a_{K-i}^{-1}\geq 1
for

and
\prod_{i=1}^{j}a_{K+i}\geq 1
for

The proof of that claim is simple; consider all the cumulative products up to index

i.e.
P_{j}=\prod_{i}^{j}a_{i}
, so the index
K
in the claim is the one where the cumulative product is minimal.

To apply the claim, observe that one can take a point

in the orbit of the tangency, a vector

(the tangent to the stable and unstable manifold at point
x^{\prime}
) and a positive integer

such that
|g^{n}(v^{\prime})|\geq 1
for any

and

and
g^{n}(G^{n}(v^{\prime}))\geq 1
for any

|g^{N}(v)|=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\geq 1
, by the claim one gets a positive integer

such that
g^{-j}(G^{K}(v^{\prime}))=\prod_{i=0}^{j}g^{-1}(G^{K-i}(v)\geq 1
for

and
g^{j}(G^{K}(v^{\prime}))=\prod_{i=1}^{j}g(G^{K+i}(v^{\prime}))\geq 1
for

therefore,
g^{|n|}(G^{k}(v^{\prime}))\geq 1
for any

∎

The notion of tangencies can be extended beyond periodic points; in fact, one can consider tangencies between the stable and unstable manifold of points in a hyperbolic set (as it was considered in the paper by Newhouse, see
[
N
]
). As it was commented in the introduction, Kupka-Smale diffeomorphisms (the one such that all periodic points are hyperbolic and all intersection between stable and unstable manifold of periodic points) are generic, therefore do not exhibit heteroclinic tangencies; however, by the results of Newhouse, this is an open set of diffeomorphisms that are not hyperbolic and do not exhibit dominated splitting, so they contain critical points. However, one can wonder if they could be related to heteroclinic tangencies associated to non-periodic points in a hyperbolic set (see paragraphs iii- and iv- in the present section).

ii-
Misiurewicz diffeomorphisms.

In
[
R
]
the author introduces an example of an homoclinic class that is semiconjugate to a hyperbolic set exhibiting a quadratic homoclinic tangencies taking place inside the limit set. In
[
E
]
it is shown a surfaces diffeomorphim in the boundary of Anosov ones (and conjugate to an Anosov) exhibiting a cubic tangency associated to a heteroclinic point.
Both are examples of Misiurewicz diffeomorphisms.

Another example of a Misiurewicz diffeomorphism can be found in the Hénon family
f_{a,b}(x,y)=(x^{2}-a-by,x)
. For sufficiently large negative
a
, this map has a hyperbolic Cantor set. Increasing
a
, one can find a first bifurcation parameter
a_{0}
at which the hyperbolicity of the Cantor set is destroyed due to an internal tangency of the stable and unstable manifolds of a saddle point (see
[
T
]
and
[
BS2
]
). Moreover, for the Hénon family (
b<1
) it is shown that in the interior of the parameters
(a,b)
with maximal entropy (that is equal to
\log(2)
) the set of points of
f_{a,b}
that do not escape to
\infty
form a hyperbolic horseshoes and at the boundary of the maximal entropy parameters, there is an internal tangency (see theorem 2 of
[
BS
]
).

All the examples above are in the boundary of Axiom A diffeomorphisms and to show that there is only one critical point that is associated to the tangency, it is enough to show that for any point that is not in the orbit of the critical point, there is a well defined cone field that is eventually contracted.

iii-
Recurrent critical points and no exponential growth of the projective cocycle.

In
[
HMS
]
it is shown diffeomorphisms in the boundary of a Axiom A surface diffeomorphisms such that the lack of hyperbolicity arises from the presence of a cubic tangency between a stable manifold and an unstable manifold, one of which is not associated to a periodic point; moreover, that tangency provides a critical point which is recurrent and so this critical point is not a heteroclinic tangency associated to points in a hyperbolic set.

In the following example it is shown that given a horseshoes and a transversal homoclinic point (not necessary associated to a periodic point), it can be deformed in such a way that transversal point becomes a tangency and so, showing tangencies (and so critical points) along recurrent points; Moreover, that construction can be performed in such a way the critical point does not exhibit exponential growth of the projective cocycle.

Proposition 12
.

There exists a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism with an invariant compact set
conjugated to the shift on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and with a single critical orbit.
The critical orbit can be chosen at any non-periodic point of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Moreover, the construction can be performed in such a way that the derivative of the projective cocyle does not grow exponentially.

Proof.

Let us consider a diffeomorphism
f_{0}
with the classical horseshoe
K_{0}
with dissipation
which is the maximal invariant set in an open domain
U

and let us fix any non-periodic point
x\in K_{0}
.
We compose
f_{0}
with a fibered rotation centered at
x
with arbitrarily small
support and which almost send
E^{u}_{x}
on
E^{s}_{x}
.
We get a diffeomorphism
f_{1}
.
If the support
B_{0}
of the rotation is small enough, a (narrow) cone field is still preserved,
the dynamics is still hyperbolic and conjugated to the shift,
the conjugacy is
C^{0}

iv-
Critical points for non-uniform hyperbolic diffeomorphisms in the works of Benedicks-Carlesson and Wang-Young.

The idea of trying to identify a critical set for two-dimensional maps, that is, a set designated to play the role analogous to that played by critical points in one-dimension, goes back to
[
BC
]
. This approach is implemented in the landmark paper of Benedicks-Carleson
[
BC
]
to prove the existence of strange attractors for a positive measure set of parameters for the Hénon family. In their setting the critical set is defined as a certain type of tangencies between unstable manifold of a fix point and stable one that are obtained after an inductive process and for certain parameters of the Hénon family (in particular, that tangency is not a heteroclinic tangency associated to points in a hyperbolic set).
In
[
WY
]
it is reworked the entire inductive construction of
[
BC
]
with built-in geometric properties for the critical set as part of the induction.
The critical set introduced in
[
WY
]
is an intrinsically defined object, characterized as those points on the attractor at which stable and unstable directions are interchanged and it is proved that this set has a special geometric structure; it can be realized as the intersection of a nested sequence of rectangles with known geometric and dynamical properties.
Recently, the critical set has been used in
[
BS
]
in order to define a kneading theory for some Henon maps.

v-
Critical points in Odometers and unicritical sets.

In
[
CLPY
]
is studied the dynamics of odometers and their renormalization, introducing a class of infinitely renormalizable unicritical diffeomorphisms of the disk (with a non-degenerate “critical point”). Odometers appear naturally for mildly dissipative diffeomorphisms of the disk and they cannot exhibit a dominated splitting (see
[
CPT
]
) and therefore they exhibit critical points. In
[
CLPY
]
it is assumed that there is a “unique critical point” in the odometer and the definition of critical point is “upgraded” assuming that the derivative of the projective cocycle along the critical directions grows exponentially for forward and backward iterates (i.e.
|g^{n}(v)|>(1+\delta)^{|n|}
for all
n
and some
\delta>0
). Under these conditions in the context of unique ergodicity, it is possible to show the existence of local and center stable manifolds that are tangent at the critical point. That property is later used to prove that under renormalization, the dynamic converges to an unimodal one-dimensional endomorphism.

vi-
Critical points outside the Limit set.

The classical Smale’s horseshoes dynamic in the sphere provides a simple example of critical points that are not in the limit set: there is a repelling point and an attracting disc where the limit set is decomposed into a hyperbolic Cantor set and a fixed attracting point, while the critical point is the “fold” of the unstable manifold of the hyperbolic set inside the basin of attraction of the attracting fixed point. Actually, this is the typical situation that holds for a non-trivial dissipative Axiom A diffeomorphisms in an attracting disc.

In the dissipative context, given a fixed point

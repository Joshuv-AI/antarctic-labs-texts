Signature ECG part 4: A 6 year-old with an irregular pulse
Authors: Arthur AM. Wilde, Priya Chockalingam
Published: 2025

Image 1

This ECG is from a 6 year-old male who is completely asymptomatic, but was sent for evaluation because an irregular heart beat was noticed at some point in time. Further cardiological evaluation revealed normal imaging (echo) and a 24-h Holter monitoring with abundant ventricular ectopy throughout the day (>1000 extrasystoles/hour) and some ventricular tachycardias as seen in the baseline ECG. The family history revealed that his father was also known with abundant ventricular ectopy (20 % ectopy burden) and the sister of the father was told to have episodes of palpitations and episodes of weakness and tiredness.

The ECG shows an episode of bidirectional VT in the extremity leads and ventricular ectopy in bigeminy in the precordial leads. A careful look in the latter leads unmasks a very pronounced U-wave from which the ectopy originates (see for example lead V2). The conducted QRS complex (first beat in the ECG and alternating beat in the extremity leads) has normal conduction intervals (PQ, QRS) and a normal QRS-axis.

The diagnosis in this case is Andersen Tawil Syndrome (ATS) which is based on pathogenic variants in KCNJ2 (confirmed in this family), the gene encoding for the current IK1 [1]. The cardiac phenotype is as described (pronounced U-waves, abundant ventricular ectopy, originating from these U-waves, and occasionally a bidirectional VT (captured on the baseline ECG) [2]. Exercise usually worsens the arrhythmia burden. The disease in the past was named LQTS type 7, but we consider that a ‘misnomer’ as the QT interval is not prolonged but it is the U-wave that is pronounced. The extra cardiac phenotype includes dysmorphic features and periodic muscle weakness (presumably responsible for the extra cardiac symptoms in the aunt).

Treatment is not always needed but with abundant ectopy cardiac function may deteriorate. And, in particular, patients with reduced ventricular function may be at risk for more serious arrhythmias. Treatment consists of beta blockade and flecainide [3], which, in combination is usually very effective and usually will prevent deterioration of LV function.

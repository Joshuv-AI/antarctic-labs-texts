HRET: A Self-Evolving LLM Evaluation Toolkit for Korean
Hanwool Lee, Sooyong Kim, Dasol Choi, Sangwon Baek, Seunghyeok Hong, Ilgyun Jeong, Naeun Lee, Inseon Hwang, Guijin Son

Abstract
Recent advancements in Korean large language models (LLMs) have spurred numerous benchmarks and evaluation methodologies, yet the lack of a standardized evaluation framework has led to inconsistent results and limited comparability. To address this, we introduce HRET ( Haerae Evaluation Toolkit ), an open-source, self-evolving evaluation framework tailored specifically for Korean LLMs. HRET unifies diverse evaluation methods, including logit-based scoring, exact-match, language-inconsistency penalization, and LLM-as-a-Judge assessments. Its modular, registry-based architecture integrates major benchmarks (HAE-RAE Bench, KMMLU, KUDGE, HRM8K) and multiple inference backends (LiteLLM, HuggingFace, OpenAI-compatible endpoints). With automated pipelines for continuous evolution, HRET provides a robust foundation for reproducible, fair, and transparent Korean NLP research.

Introduction

Mature evaluation practices are essential for accurately tracking advancements in the capabilities of large language models (LLMs). Biased or skewed assessments can mislead the research community and hinder shared goals of technological progress. In this line, the English NLP community has long pursued credible, standardized benchmarks—starting with the GLUE and SuperGLUE leaderboards
(
Wang et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2019
)
, followed by LM-Eval Harness
(
Gao et al. 2024
)
, HELM
(
Liang et al. 2023
)
, and Chatbot Arena—all aimed at providing fair, comparable evaluations under consistent conditions. Now, as Korean NLP research increases in volume, they face similar challenges: Technical reports for foundational models often yield inconsistent metrics, complicating direct comparisons. This underscores the need for a unified evaluation framework tailored to Koreans.

Korean benchmarks vary considerably in their evaluation setups—HAE-RAE Bench utilizes a log-probability-based evaluation, while KMMLU employs exact-match criteria. Such methodological discrepancies often compel researchers and practitioners to customize evaluations, complicating direct comparisons and compromising interpretability. For example, when evaluating identical models like Gemma2-27B on the same benchmarks, different research groups report significantly different results (as shown in Table
1
), highlighting the urgent need for standardization.

Additionally, existing evaluations frequently overlook key linguistic nuances unique to Korean, such as intricate morphology, context-dependent word segmentation, and extensive honorific systems. These language-specific challenges require specialized consideration in evaluation methodologies to ensure accurate performance assessment of Korean LLMs.

To address these challenges, we introduce
HRET
(Haerae Evaluation Toolkit), a standardized, open-source toolkit specifically tailored for evaluating Korean LLMs. HRET makes several key contributions:

•

Unified and reproducible evaluation
: HRET standardizes dataset handling, evaluation methods, and benchmarking procedures, ensuring transparency and consistent outcomes.

•

Self-Evolving Mechanism
: Its modular registry-based design allows HRET to continuously integrate new benchmarks, evaluation methodologies, and inference backends with minimal effort.

•

Multi-method evaluation
: Supports multiple complementary evaluation methods—including logit-based scoring, exact-match evaluation, and LLM-as-a-judge—to comprehensively assess model performance.

•

Ease of Use
: Intuitive API and streamlined command-line interfaces simplify evaluation workflows, making it accessible for both researchers and industry practitioners.

By unifying prominent benchmarks and diverse inference backends—including HuggingFace, vLLM, and OpenAI-compatible APIs—HRET significantly improves reproducibility, comparability, and accessibility in Korean NLP research.
The toolkit and comprehensive documentation will be publicly released under the Apache License 2.0 upon acceptance to encourage widespread adoption and community contributions.

2.2
Reproducibility Challenges

Current Korean NLP evaluations exhibit considerable reproducibility challenges. A notable example is the inconsistent results reported for widely used models such as Gemma2-27B
Team et al. 2024
and Qwen2.5-32B
Yang et al. 2024
across various technical reports from major Korean organizations. As illustrated in Table
1

Research et al. 2024
and Kanana report
Bak et al. 2025
report performance discrepancies ranging from around 1–10 percentage points across multiple tasks, including GSM8K, KMMLU, HumanEval, and MBPP.

These discrepancies stem from variations in inference settings, prompt engineering, and evaluation implementations. The situation is further complicated by Korean’s linguistic features—complex morphology, context-dependent segmentation, and honorific systems—which are handled inconsistently across evaluations. Existing benchmarks lack standardized guidelines addressing these Korean-specific challenges, resulting in fragmented evaluation practices that impede meaningful progress in Korean NLP research.

3
HRET
Framework

HRET (
Haerae Evaluation Toolkit
) addresses critical reproducibility and comparability challenges faced in evaluating Korean large language models (LLMs). By providing a standardized yet flexible evaluation environment, HRET fosters consistency, transparency, and accessibility in Korean NLP research. Figure
1
illustrates its modular and registry-based architecture, seamlessly integrating datasets, evaluation methods, inference strategies, and model backends into a coherent evaluation pipeline.

HRET is explicitly designed for: (1) researchers who require standardized evaluation methods for benchmarking Korean language models; (2) developers looking for robust tools to streamline model assessment and selection; and (3) industry practitioners aiming to reliably deploy Korean LLMs in production.

3.1
Novelty and Design Principles

HRET introduces several innovative approaches to address the challenges of Korean LLM evaluation:

•

Self-Evolving Mechanism:
HRET continuously adapts by automatically discovering and integrating new evaluation methods, datasets, and inference backends at runtime through (1)
Automated Component Discovery
for runtime integration without core code modification, (2)
Dynamic Pipeline Construction
that adapts to new scenarios without predefined workflows, and (3)
Continuous Integration Workflows
that validate components against established benchmarks.

•

Registry-Based Modularity:
Core components—including dataset loaders, evaluation methods, scaling strategies, and inference backends—are individually modularized and managed through registries, allowing researchers to effortlessly extend the toolkit’s capabilities with minimal code changes.

•

Concurrency and Scalability:
Leveraging asynchronous HTTP requests (via
httpx
) and multi-threading, HRET significantly enhances throughput during large-scale evaluations, essential for computationally intensive Korean benchmarks.

•

Unified API:
HRET simplifies interaction through consistent Evaluator and PipelineRunner interfaces, reducing the entry barrier for both novice researchers and industry practitioners.

•

Korean Focus:
The framework specifically targets Korean language benchmarks and evaluation needs, with built-in support for major Korean evaluation datasets and specialized evaluation methods like language consistency checking.

2.

The

)

Generated predictions are assessed using multiple approaches: (1)

for reference comparison, (2)

LLM-as-a-Judge
for open-ended assessment. Additional

include

5.

Runtime Components
orchestrate the pipeline, with the
Runner
combining components from all registries and the
Evaluator
implementing a facade pattern providing a simplified API.

This architecture ensures consistency across evaluations while providing flexibility through pluggable components at each stage.

3.3
Advanced Features and Usage

HRET supports multiple inference backends through a consistent interface:

•

HuggingFace Transformers
: Provides direct access to thousands of models, including Korean-specific models like KoGPT and HyperCLOVA.

•

vLLM
: Optimized for high-throughput evaluation scenarios with large Korean benchmarks.

•

OpenAI-compatible endpoints
: Ensures compatibility with API-based services from major Korean AI providers.

•

LiteLLM
: Offers unified access to multiple model providers, simplifying cross-vendor comparisons.

Beyond the standard Evaluator API, HRET provides a flexible PipelineRunner for advanced customization of evaluation workflows. This interface enables:

•

Multi-stage evaluations where outputs from one model serve as inputs to another

•

Comprehensive model comparisons across different benchmarks and metrics

•

Custom workflows with specialized processing steps for Korean-specific evaluations

•

Ensemble evaluations combining complementary models or methodologies

6
Conclusion

We introduce HRET, a self-evolving, modular evaluation toolkit tailored specifically for Korean large language models. HRET standardizes methodologies, improves reproducibility, and addresses linguistic nuances specific to Korean. Future work will include expanding benchmarks for domain-specific linguistic analyses, deeper integration with emerging LLM libraries, and enhancing visualization tools to facilitate detailed error diagnostics. Ultimately, HRET aims to establish a robust foundation for Korean NLP research, supporting sustained progress and collaboration within the community.

Limitations

While HRET provides a standardized evaluation framework for Korean LLMs, several limitations remain:

First, despite integrating major Korean benchmarks, coverage in specialized domains (legal, medical, scientific) remains limited. Second, the language penalizer may struggle with mixed-language outputs or code-switching common in technical contexts. Third, computational requirements for scaling techniques can be substantial, potentially limiting accessibility.

Current evaluation metrics, particularly string matching, may not fully capture Korean’s rich morphological structures. Additionally, as new Korean LLMs and benchmarks emerge, maintaining compatibility requires continuous updates. Finally, the toolkit offers limited tools for analyzing model errors specific to Korean linguistic phenomena.

We are addressing these limitations through ongoing development and welcome community contributions to enhance HRET’s capabilities.

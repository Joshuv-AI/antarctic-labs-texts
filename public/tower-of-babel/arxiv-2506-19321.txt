From kinetic mixtures to compressible two-phase flow: A BGK-type model and rigorous derivation
Seung Yeon Cho, Young-Pil Choi, Byung-Hoon Hwang, Sihyun Song

Abstract
. We propose a BGK-type kinetic model for a binary gas mixture, designed to serve as a kinetic formulation of compressible two-phase fluid dynamics. The model features species-dependent adiabatic exponents, and the relaxation operator is constructed by solving an entropy minimization problem under moments constraints. Starting from this model, we derive the compressible two-phase Euler equations via a formal Chapman–Enskog expansion and identify dissipative corrections of Navier–Stokes type. We then rigorously justify the Euler limit using the relative entropy method, establishing quantitative convergence estimates under appropriate regularity assumptions. Finally, we present numerical experiments based on an implicit-explicit Runge–Kutta method, which confirm the asymptotic preserving property and demonstrate the convergence from the BGK model to the isentropic two-phase Euler system in the hydrodynamic regime.



1.
Introduction

The dynamics of compressible fluids without entropy variations are governed by the
isentropic Euler equations
. Such dynamics arise in a variety of physical settings, including the propagation of sound waves in an ideal gas and the expansion of gases through nozzles under smooth flow conditions. These equations describe the conservation of mass and momentum, with a barotropic pressure law of the form
p\propto\rho^{\gamma}
, where
\gamma\in[1,\frac{n+2}{n}]
denotes the adiabatic index and
n
is the spatial dimension.

To analyze isentropic flow from a kinetic perspective, a line of work initiated by Lions, Perthame, and Tadmor
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
introduced a kinetic formulation of the isentropic Euler equations by solving an auxiliary entropy identity. This approach encodes the macroscopic conservation laws into kinetic equations via a suitable velocity distribution function. In particular, these kinetic representations are instrumental in analyzing entropy solutions, constructing numerical schemes, and justifying hydrodynamic limits.

Building upon this, Bouchut
[
11
]
proposed a general framework for BGK-type kinetic models relaxing toward isentropic Euler equations. The classical BGK model, introduced by Bhatnagar, Gross, and Krook
[
6
]
, approximates the Boltzmann equation by a relaxation process toward a local Maxwellian distribution. Bouchut’s contribution was to characterize such equilibria via an entropy minimization principle under mass and momentum constraints, thereby ensuring thermodynamic consistency with the target isentropic fluid system.

On the other hand, significant progress has also been made in the kinetic modeling of multi-component gas mixtures. In a seminal work, Andries, Aoki, and Perthame
[
1
]
proposed the consistent BGK-type model for inert mixtures. Since then, various strategies have been explored to approximate the Boltzmann collision operator by a BGK relaxation term. Broadly speaking, two main modeling approaches exist: one uses a
single BGK operator
shared across species, while the other mimics the Boltzmann structure via a
sum of interspecies operators
.

The single-operator approach offers modeling simplicity, provides a framework for modeling more general gaseous systems, and is possibly justified via entropy minimization. Several developments have followed in this direction, for instance, the works of Groppi, Bisi, Brull, and their collaborators for reactive mixtures, polyatomic molecules, and relativistic corrections
[
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
,
27
,
28
,
33
]
. The multi-operator strategy, inspired by the original Gross–Krook model
[
26
]
, allows more flexibility in modeling detailed interspecies dynamics but may be more complex to analyze or simulate. Recently, the consistent models following the multi-operator strategy have been derived by Bobylev, Haack, Klingenberg, Pirner, and others
[
9
,
31
,
37
]
. See
[
3
]
for the extension to quantum mixtures. For both types of BGK models, we refer to
[
4
,
34
,
36
,
44
]
for the existence of solutions and
[
14
,
21
,
29
]
for numerical simulations.

Formal derivations of macroscopic two-phase fluid equations from such kinetic mixture models have also been attempted, often based on asymptotic expansions or moment closure techniques. While these studies provide valuable insights into the structure of the resulting fluid systems, they are typically limited to formal arguments and do not establish rigorous convergence from the kinetic level.

On the other hand, a number of works have rigorously addressed the derivation of macroscopic two-phase models starting from
kinetic-fluid systems
, where a kinetic equation governs the dispersed particle phase and the fluid is described by compressible or incompressible Navier–Stokes or Euler equations. The coupling is usually modeled through drag forces. Among early contributions in this direction, the works of Goudon, Jabin, and Vasseur
[
24
,
25
]
analyzed different asymptotic regimes, namely, the light and fine particle limits, which lead to either passive transport models or coupled two-phase Navier–Stokes systems. Han-Kwan and Michel
[
30
]
developed a unified framework for high-friction hydrodynamic limits from the Vlasov–Navier–Stokes system using modulated energy and entropy techniques. Related results include
[
42
]
, which considered the asymptotic behavior of a Vlasov–Fokker–Planck/compressible Navier–Stokes system, and
[
46
]
, where the relative entropy method was applied to derive a coupled system consisting of an inhomogeneous two-phase fluid system from weak solutions of a kinetic-fluid model. Other notable works include
[
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
]
, which addressed similar limits in the presence of nonlinear Fokker–Planck operators or electrostatic interactions.

In contrast to these approaches, the present work addresses the derivation of two-phase models starting from a
kinetic description for both phases
in a binary gas mixture. Specifically, we consider a BGK-type relaxation model in which both species evolve via kinetic equations and interact through the common velocity for mixtures. While formal derivations of two-phase fluid systems from kinetic mixtures have appeared in the literature mentioned above, a complete rigorous justification has, to the best of our knowledge, remained open. This paper provides the first such derivation of the isentropic two-phase Euler equations from a unified kinetic BGK model, using the relative entropy method and making precise the connection between mesoscopic relaxation mechanisms and macroscopic fluid dynamics.

To this end, we construct a single BGK-type model for a binary mixture of gases that serves as a kinetic formulation of the isentropic two-phase fluid system. Each species is characterized by its own adiabatic exponent
\gamma_{i}>1
and particle mass
m_{i}>0
, and the two components are assumed to interact solely through relaxation to a shared bulk velocity field. Our primary aim is to derive, from this kinetic model, a macroscopic system that reflects the structure of the compressible two-phase Euler or Navier-Stokes equations, depending on the scaling regime.

The proposed kinetic system describes a binary mixture through two distribution functions
f_{i}(t,x,v)
, defined on
(x,v)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
t>0
, where
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These distributions evolve according to BGK-type kinetic equations:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}f_{i}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{i}=\nu_{i}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]-f_{i}\right),\quad i=1,2,

where
\nu_{i}>0
denotes the collision frequency (or the inverse of the mean relaxation time) for species
i
, which may depend on
n_{i}
. The right-hand side models the tendency of
f_{i}
to relax toward a local equilibrium distribution
\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]
, characterized by the number density
n_{i}
of species
i
and a common bulk velocity
u
shared by both species.

The equilibrium distribution
\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v)
is designed to be consistent with the thermodynamic structure of isentropic gas dynamics and is obtained via an entropy minimization principle. It takes the form

\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle c_{i}\,\mathbf{1}_{|u-v|^{2}\leq\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\frac{2}{n}}}&\text{if }\gamma_{i}=\frac{n+2}{n},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle c_{i}\left(m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}-m_{i}|v-u|^{2}\right)_{+}^{\frac{d_{i}}{2}}&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right).\end{cases}

Here, the constants
c_{i}
and
d_{i}
are chosen so that the internal energy and entropy associated with
\mathcal{M}_{i}
match the corresponding expressions in isentropic gas dynamics. A detailed derivation of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is provided in Appendix
A
, following the framework of entropy minimization introduced in
[
11
,
40
]
under suitable constraints. The constants are explicitly given by

c_{i}=\left(\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}-1}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)}{\pi^{n/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1\right)},\qquad d_{i}=\frac{2}{\gamma_{i}-1}-n,

where
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function.

The macroscopic quantities are defined as follows. The number density
n_{i}
, mass density
\rho_{i}
, and bulk velocity
u_{i}
of species
i
are defined by

n_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}(v)\,dv,\qquad\rho_{i}=m_{i}n_{i},\qquad u_{i}=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}vf_{i}(v)\,dv,

respectively, with the convention
u_{i}=0
when
n_{i}=0
. Then the mass density
\rho
and bulk velocity
\mathbf{u}
of mixtures are given by

(1.2)

\rho=\rho_{1}+\rho_{2},\qquad{\bf{u}}=\frac{\rho_{1}u_{1}+\rho_{2}u_{2}}{\rho}.

We define the common bulk velocity
u
, which is the auxiliary parameter of
\mathcal{M}
, as

u=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\rho_{i}u_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\rho_{i}}.

To ensure thermodynamic consistency, we introduce the total kinetic entropy

H(f,v):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}h_{i}(f_{i},v),

where the kinetic entropy
h_{i}
for each species is defined by

h_{i}(f_{i},v)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{m_{i}}{2}|v|^{2}f_{i}+\infty\cdot\mathbf{1}_{f_{i}>c_{i}}(v)&\text{if }\gamma_{i}=\frac{n+2}{n},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\frac{m_{i}}{2}|v|^{2}f_{i}+\frac{1}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\frac{f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}}{1+2/d_{i}}&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right).\end{cases}

It can be verified that the entropy of the equilibrium state satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v),v)\,dv=\frac{\rho_{i}}{2}|u|^{2}+\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}},

which recovers the internal energy and pressure terms in the isentropic gas model. Furthermore, the following entropy minimization principle holds:

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nu_{i}h_{i}(f_{i},v)\,dv\geq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nu_{i}h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v),v)\,dv,

which yields the dissipation rate in the total entropy and is crucial for the entropy inequality discussed in Section
2
.

Our main objective is to rigorously justify the derivation of macroscopic two-phase fluid equations from this kinetic model. We begin by performing a formal Chapman–Enskog expansion under a hydrodynamic scaling, which serves to identify the macroscopic structure associated with the model. This expansion yields the compressible two-phase Euler equations at leading order, and a Navier–Stokes-type correction with density-dependent viscosity at the next order, thus capturing both the conservative and dissipative effects induced by interspecies momentum relaxation.

To complement the formal analysis, we carry out a rigorous derivation of the isentropic two-phase Euler equations using the relative entropy method. This involves constructing a suitable modulated entropy functional and establishing convergence of the kinetic solutions to their macroscopic counterparts under appropriate regularity assumptions. This justifies, for the first time, the hydrodynamic limit from a BGK-type kinetic mixture to a two-phase Euler system in a mathematically rigorous framework.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we examine the structural properties of the kinetic model, including conservation of mass and momentum, and the entropy dissipation. Section
3
is devoted to a formal asymptotic analysis via the Chapman–Enskog expansion, which yields a macroscopic two-phase fluid system and reveals the structure of dissipative corrections. In Section
4
, we rigorously derive the isentropic two-phase Euler equations from the kinetic model using the relative entropy method, constructing a modulated entropy functional and establishing convergence under suitable regularity assumptions. Section
5
presents the numerical scheme, emphasizing the implicit-explicit Runge–Kutta (IMEX-RK) time integration and the asymptotic preserving property of the method. In Section
6
, we perform numerical tests comparing solutions of the kinetic model and the isentropic Euler system in the hydrodynamic limit, confirming the consistency of the scheme and supporting the theoretical analysis. Appendix
A
provides the derivation of the truncated Maxwellians via an entropy minimization principle. Appendix
B
contains the detailed proof of Lemma
3.1
, establishing key velocity moment identities for the truncated Maxwellians, which are essential for the Chapman–Enskog expansion. Appendix
C
establishes the global existence of mild solutions to the kinetic model, which is required for the application of the relative entropy method in the rigorous hydrodynamic limit.

2.
Structural properties: Conservation laws and entropy dissipation

The kinetic model (
1.1
) possesses several fundamental structural properties that are consistent with the underlying physics of two-phase flows. In this section, we demonstrate that the system conserves mass and momentum and satisfies an entropy dissipation principle. These properties are essential to ensure that the kinetic formulation behaves consistently with the expected macroscopic dynamics in the hydrodynamic limit.

(i)

Mass conservation for each species
: For each species
i=1,2
, the equation (
1.1
) preserves the total mass over time. Indeed, integrating both sides of (
1.1
) against
m_{i}
over velocity and space, and using the fact that the collision operator conserves mass, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}m_{i}f_{i}\,dxdv=0,\quad i=1,2.

This confirms that the individual mass of each species remains constant.

(ii)

Conservation of total momentum
: The mixture evolves under the constraint that total momentum is conserved. By multiplying (
1.1
) by
m_{i}v
and integrating in
(x,v)
, we use the fact that the relaxation term preserves the total momentum because

\nu_{i}m_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]-f_{i}\right)dv=\nu_{i}\rho_{i}(u-u_{i}).

Since the definition of
u
is constructed to ensure
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\rho_{i}u_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\rho_{i}u
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}m_{i}vf_{i}\,dxdv=0,

which confirms the global momentum conservation in the absence of external forces.

(iii)

Entropy dissipation
(
H
-theorem
)
: Entropy dissipation is a fundamental feature of BGK-type models. We define the total entropy functional

\mathcal{H}(f):=\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f,v)\,dxdv

and compute its time derivative:

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{H}(f)+\mathcal{D}(f)\leq 0,

where the entropy dissipation rate is

\mathcal{D}(f):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(h_{i}(f_{i},v)-h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v),v)\right)dxdv.

The non-negativity
\mathcal{D}(f)\geq 0
follows from the convexity of
h_{i}
and the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{i}
minimizes the entropy functional under mass and momentum constraints, see Appendix
A
and Remark
A.1
.

We now verify this inequality in two cases depending on the value of the adiabatic index
\gamma_{i}
.

Case
\gamma_{i}=\frac{n+2}{n}
: This corresponds to a uniform truncation of the distribution function. Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i},v)\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{m_{i}}{2}|v|^{2}f_{i}+\infty\cdot\mathbf{1}_{f_{i}>c_{i}}\,dv,

the entropy is minimized precisely when
f_{i}=\mathcal{M}_{i}
, by construction (see Remark
A.1
). Taking the time derivative of the total entropy then yields

(2.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{m_{i}}{2}|v|^{2}f_{i}\,dx\,dv=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\nu_{i}m_{i}}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{i}-f_{i})\,dx\,dv\leq 0,

which confirms the entropy inequality.

Case
\gamma_{i}\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
: In this case, we use the power-law form of the kinetic entropy:

h_{i}(f_{i},v)=\frac{m_{i}}{2}|v|^{2}f_{i}+\frac{1}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\frac{f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}}{1+2/d_{i}}.

Multiplying (
1.1
) by
f_{i}^{2/d_{i}}/(2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}})
, integrating over
(x,v)
, and summing over
i
give

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\frac{f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}}{1+2/d_{i}}\,dxdv=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\nu_{i}}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}f_{i}^{2/d_{i}}-f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}\right)dxdv.

Applying Young’s inequality with exponents
p=\frac{d_{i}+2}{d_{i}}
and
q=\frac{d_{i}+2}{2}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\frac{f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}}{1+2/d_{i}}\,dxdv

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\nu_{i}}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{d_{i}}{d_{i}+2}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}+\frac{2}{d_{i}+2}f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}-f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}\right)dxdv

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\nu_{i}}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}}{1+2/d_{i}}-\frac{f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}}{1+2/d_{i}}\right)dxdv.

Combining this with the entropy identity (
2.1
), we conclude that the total entropy is nonincreasing, as guaranteed by the entropy minimization principle.

3.
Formal derivation: Chapman–Enskog method

In this section, we carry out a formal derivation of the macroscopic two-phase fluid model from the proposed kinetic BGK system (
1.1
), using the Chapman–Enskog method. This asymptotic procedure is based on a hyperbolic scaling, which assumes that both temporal and spatial variations occur on a slow scale. Precisely, we apply the scalings
t\mapsto\varepsilon t
and
x\mapsto\varepsilon x
, leading to the scaled system:

(3.1)

\partial_{t}f_{i}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{i}=\frac{\nu_{i}}{\varepsilon}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]-f_{i}\right),\qquad i=1,2,

where
\varepsilon
represents the Knudsen number. In this setting, we assume
\varepsilon
is small and proceed with a formal expansion of the distribution function
f_{i}
in powers of
\varepsilon
:

(3.2)

f_{i}=f_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon f_{i}^{(1)}.

The expansion is performed under the assumption that the macroscopic quantities
n_{i}
and
\rho_{1}u_{1}+\rho_{2}u_{2}
are not expanded in
\varepsilon
. This leads to the so-called
compatibility conditions
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}^{(0)}\,dv,\ i=1,2\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left(m_{1}f_{1}+m_{2}f_{2}\right)dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left(m_{1}f_{1}^{(0)}+m_{2}f_{2}^{(0)}\right)dv.

These relations guarantee that the macroscopic variables
n_{i}
,
\rho_{i}
, and
\rho_{1}u_{1}+\rho_{2}u_{2}
are independent of
\varepsilon
.
Let us consider the expansions of collision frequenties, bulk velocity of species
i
, common bulk velocity, and equilibrium distributions:

\nu_{i}=\nu_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon\nu_{i}^{(1)},\quad u_{i}=u_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon u_{i}^{(1)}\quad u=u^{(0)}+\varepsilon u^{(1)},\quad\mathcal{M}_{i}=\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(1)}.

It is straightforward that

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\rho_{i}u_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\rho_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(1)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(0)}\right)+\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(1)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(1)}\rho_{i}},

which implies

u^{(0)}=u|_{\varepsilon=0}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(0)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}.

On the other hand, since
n_{i}
is independent of
\varepsilon
,
\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(0)}
and
\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(1)}
can be understood as

\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(0)}=\left\{\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]\right\}_{\varepsilon=0}=\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u^{(0)}],

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(1)}&=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\left\{\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]\right\}_{\varepsilon=0}\cr&=\left\{\nabla_{u}\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]\cdot\frac{du}{d\varepsilon}\right\}_{\varepsilon=0}\cr&=d_{i}m_{i}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}(v-u^{(0)})\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},u^{(0)}]\cr&\hskip 71.13188pt\cdot\left\{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(1)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(0)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}-\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(1)}\rho_{i}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(0)}\right)}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}\right)^{2}}\right\}.\end{split}

3.1.
Zeroth-order expansion

Substituting the expansion into the equation (
3.1
), we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\left(f_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon f_{i}^{(1)}\right)+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(f_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon f_{i}^{(1)}\right)\cr&\quad=\frac{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}{\varepsilon}\left(\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{i}[n_{i},u]-f_{i}^{(0)}\right)+\left\{\nu_{i}^{(1)}\left(\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{i}[n_{i},u]-f_{i}^{(0)}\right)+\nu_{i}^{(0)}\left(\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{i}[n_{i},u]-f_{i}^{(1)}\right)\right\}\cr&\qquad+\varepsilon\nu_{i}^{(1)}\left(\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{i}[n_{i},u]-f_{i}^{(1)}\right).\end{split}

Collecting terms by powers of
\varepsilon
, we find that the leading-order equation at order
O(\varepsilon^{-1})
gives

(3.6)

f_{i}^{(0)}=\mathcal{M}^{(0)}_{i}

which states that the zeroth-order distribution is simply the truncated Maxwellian associated with
(n_{i},u^{(0)})
. This, combined with (
3.3
), leads to

\displaystyle\rho_{1}u_{1}+\rho_{2}u_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m_{i}vf_{i}^{(0)}\,dv=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m_{i}v\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u^{(0)}]\,dv=(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})u^{(0)},

i.e.

(3.7)

u^{(0)}=\frac{\rho_{1}u_{1}+\rho_{2}u_{2}}{\rho}={\bf{u}}.

To derive macroscopic equations, we multiply (
3.1
) by
(1,m_{i}v)
and integrate over
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This yields the balance laws

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})=-\nabla_{x}\cdot\left\{n_{i}(u_{i}-{\bf{u}})\right\},\qquad i=1,2,\\
&\partial_{t}\left(\rho{\bf{u}}\right)+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\right)+\nabla_{x}\cdot P=0,\end{split}

where
P=P_{1}+P_{2}
is the total pressure tensor, defined by

P_{i}=m_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})f_{i}\,dv.

Using the expansion (
3.2
), we now expand
P_{i}
accordingly:

\displaystyle P_{i}

\displaystyle=m_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\left(f_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon f_{i}^{(1)}\right)\,dv

\displaystyle=:P_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon P_{i}^{(1)},

where the zeroth- and first-order terms are respectively given by

P_{i}^{(0)}=m_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv=m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n},

\displaystyle P_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=m_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})f_{i}^{(1)}\,dv.

Here we have used the identity derived in Lemma
A.2
, and
\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}
denotes the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{n,n}
.

Similarly, the velocity
u_{i}
for species
i
is expanded as

\displaystyle u_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}^{(0)}\,dv}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left(f_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon f_{i}^{(1)}\right)\,dv=:u_{i}^{(0)}+\varepsilon u_{i}^{(1)},

with

(3.9)

u_{i}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\,\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv={\bf{u}},\qquad u_{i}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}vf_{i}^{(1)}\,dv,

where we again used the fact that
n_{i}
is independent of
\varepsilon
due to (
3.3
), and
f_{i}^{(0)}=\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]
from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
).

Combining the above expressions, the macroscopic system obtained at
O(1)
is the compressible two-phase isentropic Euler system:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})=0,\qquad i=1,2,\\
&\partial_{t}\left(\rho{\bf{u}}\right)+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\right)+\nabla_{x}\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\right)=0.\end{split}

3.2.
First-order expansion: Computation of
f_{i}^{(1)}

We now derive the first-order correction
f_{i}^{(1)}
in the Chapman–Enskog expansion. Starting from the kinetic equation (
3.5
), the term of order
O(1)
yields the expression

(3.11)

\displaystyle f_{i}^{(1)}=\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\left(-\partial_{t}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(0)}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(0)}\right).

To compute
f_{i}^{(1)}
, we begin by evaluating the derivatives of the truncated Maxwellian
\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(0)}=\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]
. By direct differentiation, we obtain the following formulas:

\partial_{n_{i}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(0)}=c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}d_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\gamma_{i}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nabla_{u}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(0)}=c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}m_{i}d_{i}(v-{\bf{u}})\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}].

Substituting the above relations with (
3.4
) into (
3.11
), we find that
f_{i}^{(1)}
can be expressed as

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{i}^{(1)}&=d_{i}m_{i}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\cr&-\frac{1}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\left\{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}d_{i}\gamma_{i}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}(\partial_{t}n_{i}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i})+m_{i}d_{i}(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot(\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})\right\}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}].\end{split}

To proceed, we collect several useful moment identities related to the truncated Maxwellian. The proofs of these identities are postponed to Appendix
B
for readability.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]
be the truncated Maxwellian defined by

\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v)=c_{i}\left(m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}-m_{i}|v-u|^{2}\right)_{+}^{\frac{d_{i}}{2}},

where
c_{i}
is a normalization constant. Then the following identities hold:

(i)

Zeroth-order moment:

c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv=\frac{1}{d_{i}\gamma_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}n_{i}^{2-\gamma_{i}}.

(ii)

Second-order moment:

c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv=\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}.

(iii)

Graident-weighted fourth-order moment:

\displaystyle c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}u_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}v_{q}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}^{2-\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\left\{(\nabla_{x}\cdot u)\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+\nabla_{x}u+(\nabla_{x}u)^{t}\right\}.

First-order correction to velocity:
u_{i}^{(1)}

We now derive the first-order correction
u_{i}^{(1)}
to the macroscopic velocity field in the Chapman–Enskog expansion. From the macroscopic moment relation (
3.9
), the correction is given by

u_{i}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}vf_{i}^{(1)}\,dv.

Substituting the expression for
f_{i}^{(1)}
from (
3.12
), we find

\displaystyle u_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left\{d_{i}m_{i}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}}{n_{i}\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left\{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}d_{i}\gamma_{i}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}(\partial_{t}n_{i}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i})+m_{i}d_{i}(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot(\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})\right\}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+\frac{1}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\sum_{k=2}^{5}I_{k}.

By using Lemma
3.1
, each term
I_{k}
is computed as follows.

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes v\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv\right)\frac{d_{i}m_{i}}{n_{i}}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}\right)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial_{t}n_{i}}{n_{i}}{\bf{u}},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n_{i}}d_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\gamma_{i}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}\left(c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv\right)\nabla_{x}n_{i}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n_{i}}d_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\gamma_{i}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}\left(c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\left.+({\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv\right)\nabla_{x}n_{i}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n_{i}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}\left(\gamma_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}n_{i}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+n_{i}^{2-\gamma_{i}}({\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})\right)\nabla_{x}n_{i}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}\nabla_{x}n_{i}-\frac{1}{n_{i}}{\bf{u}}({\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x})n_{i},

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}d_{i}\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv=-\partial_{t}{\bf{u}},

and

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}d_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left\{(v\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\right\}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}d_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\{{\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\}\cdot v)vc_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}d_{i}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}{\bf{u}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\cdot\{(v\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv

\displaystyle=-({\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}-\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}d_{i}{\bf{u}}\frac{(\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}})}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv

\displaystyle=-({\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}-(\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}}){\bf{u}}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{x}\cdot({\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})

where we used

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v_{\ell})^{2}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv=\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}}

for any
\ell=1,\dots,n
.
Summing all five contributions, we get

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{i}^{(1)}&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}\cr&\quad-\frac{1}{n_{i}\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\left\{\partial_{t}n_{i}{\bf{u}}+\gamma_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\nabla_{x}n_{i}+{\bf{u}}({\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x})n_{i}+n_{i}\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}+n_{i}\nabla_{x}\cdot({\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})\right\}\cr&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}-\frac{1}{n_{i}\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\left\{\partial_{t}(n_{i}{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}\nabla_{x}(n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}})\right\}.\end{aligned}

From the second relation of (
3.3
) and (
3.9
), we deduce that

\rho_{1}u_{1}^{(1)}+\rho_{2}u_{2}^{(1)}=0

which together with (
3.13
) gives
the final expression for the first-order velocity correction:

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u_{i}^{(1)}&=-\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{(0)}\rho_{k}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}\nu_{j}^{(0)}\rho n_{i}}\left\{\partial_{t}(n_{i}{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}\nabla_{x}(n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}})\right\},\end{aligned}

where
i\neq j
.

First-order correction to pressure tensor:
P_{i}^{(1)}

We next compute the first-order correction to the pressure tensor
P_{i}^{(1)}
. This term involves higher-order velocity moments of the kinetic perturbation
f_{i}^{(1)}
, and plays a crucial role in determining the viscous structure of the resulting macroscopic system. By substituting the expression for
f_{i}^{(1)}
into the definition of the pressure tensor, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=d_{i}m_{i}^{2}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\left\{(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{d_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)+1}\gamma_{i}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\left(\partial_{t}n_{i}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i}\right)dv

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{d_{i}m_{i}^{2}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,\{(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}+(v\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\right)\}\,dv.

We compute each of the three terms separately. The first term vanishes due to the oddness:

\displaystyle c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\left\{(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes v\left\{v\cdot\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=0.

The second integral can be simplified using the change of variables and the symmetry of the Maxwellian:

\displaystyle c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}](\partial_{t}n_{i}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i})\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\left\{\partial_{t}n_{i}+(v+{\bf{u}})\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i}\right\}\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=(\partial_{t}n_{i}+{\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i})\left(\frac{c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv\right)\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}}(\partial_{t}n_{i}+{\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i})\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}.

For the third term, we use the moment identity in Lemma
3.1
(iii):

\displaystyle c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\otimes(v-{\bf{u}})\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\left\{(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}+(v\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\right)\right\}\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes v\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\left\{v\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}+((v+{\bf{u}})\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\right)\right\}\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes v\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\left\{v\cdot\left((v\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\right)\right\}\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}{\bf{u}}_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}v_{q}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}^{2-\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\left\{(\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}})\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}}+(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})^{t}\right\}.

Combining the three terms, we obtain the explicit expression for
P_{i}^{(1)}
as follows.

\displaystyle P_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\gamma_{i}(\partial_{t}n_{i}+{\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i})n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}-\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\frac{n}{n+2}\gamma_{i}\left\{(\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}})\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}}+(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})^{t}\right\}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}.

Using the continuity equation and the identities (
3.9
) and (
3.14
), we can rewrite this expression as

\displaystyle P_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\frac{n}{n+2}\gamma_{i}\left\{(\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}})\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}}+(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})^{t}\right\}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\gamma_{i}\left\{n_{i}\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}({\bf{u}}_{i}-{\bf{u}}))\right\}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\frac{n}{n+2}\gamma_{i}\left\{(\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}})\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}}+(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})^{t}\right\}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\gamma_{i}\Bigg[n_{i}\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}}-\varepsilon\nabla_{x}\cdot\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{(0)}\rho_{k}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}\nu_{j}^{(0)}\rho}

\displaystyle\hskip 156.49014pt\times\left\{\partial_{t}(n_{i}{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}\nabla_{x}(n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}})\right\}\Bigg]n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}.

Thus, the system up to
O(\varepsilon)
, (
3.8
) together with (
3.9
) and (
3.14
) is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})=\varepsilon\nabla_{x}\cdot\left\{\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{(0)}\rho_{k}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}\nu_{j}^{(0)}\rho}\left(\partial_{t}(n_{i}{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}\nabla_{x}(n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}})\right)\right\}

for
i=1,2
, and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\{\rho{\bf{u}}\}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left\{\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\right\}+\nabla_{x}\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{n}{n+2}\nabla_{x}\cdot\left[\left\{(\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}})\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}}+(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})^{t}\right\}\left(\varepsilon_{1}\gamma_{1}\frac{m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}}{\nu_{1}^{(0)}}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+\varepsilon_{2}\gamma_{2}\frac{m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}}{\nu_{2}^{(0)}}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=-\nabla_{x}\left\{\left(\varepsilon_{1}\gamma_{1}\frac{m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}}{\nu_{1}^{(0)}}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+\varepsilon_{2}\gamma_{2}\frac{m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}}{\nu_{2}^{(0)}}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\right)\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}}\right\}.

To express this in a standard viscous form, we define the Lamé viscosity coefficients

\mu_{i}(n_{i})=\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{i}(n_{i})=-\frac{2}{n+2}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}},\quad i=1,2,

and the symmetric strain tensor

\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}}):=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}}+(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})^{t}).

The corresponding stress tensor is given by

\mathbb{S}_{i}(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}}):=2\mu_{i}(n_{i})\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})+\lambda_{i}(n_{i})(\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}})\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}.

Note that
\mu_{i}\geq 0
and
2\mu_{i}+n\lambda_{i}=0
for
i=1,2
, ensuring consistency with the Stokes hypothesis. The momentum equation now takes the form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\{\rho{\bf{u}}\}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left\{\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\right\}+\nabla_{x}\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\varepsilon_{1}m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}\mathbb{S}_{1}(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})+\varepsilon_{2}m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}\mathbb{S}_{2}(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})\right)=0.

In conclusion, we arrive at

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})=\varepsilon\nabla_{x}\cdot\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{(0)}\rho_{k}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}\nu_{j}^{(0)}\rho}\left\{\partial_{t}(n_{i}{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}\nabla_{x}(n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}})\right\},\qquad i=1,2,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\{\rho{\bf{u}}\}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left\{\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\right\}+\nabla_{x}\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\varepsilon_{1}m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}\mathbb{S}_{1}(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})+\varepsilon_{2}m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}\mathbb{S}_{2}(\nabla_{x}{\bf{u}})\right)=0.

Here
\rho_{i}=m_{i}n_{i}
and
\rho=\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}
, thus the above system is closed.

Remark 3.1
.

If we assume
\nu^{(0)}=\nu_{1}^{(0)}=\nu_{2}^{(0)}
, then we further get

\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})=\varepsilon\nabla_{x}\cdot\frac{1}{\nu^{(0)}}\left\{\partial_{t}(n_{i}{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}\nabla_{x}(n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}})\right\},\quad i=1,2.

3.3.
Verification of compatibility conditions

To ensure that the Chapman–Enskog expansion preserves the conservation of mass and momentum at each order, we verify the compatibility conditions:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}^{(1)}\,dv=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left(m_{1}f_{1}^{(1)}+m_{2}f_{2}^{(1)}\right)dv=0.

We begin by verifying the first condition for mass conservation. Using the expression for
f_{i}^{(1)}
obtained earlier, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}^{(1)}\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d_{i}m_{i}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}^{(0)}\rho_{i}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{d_{i}\gamma_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-2}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\partial_{t}n_{i}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i}\right)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{d_{i}m_{i}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-{\bf{u}})\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf{u}}+(v\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]\,dv

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\left(\partial_{t}n_{i}+{\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i}\right)-\frac{d_{i}m_{i}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\cdot\left((v\cdot\nabla_{x}){\bf{u}}\right)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0]\,dv

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\left(\partial_{t}n_{i}+{\bf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}n_{i}\right)-\frac{n_{i}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\nabla_{x}\cdot{\bf{u}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\left\{\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}\nabla_{x}\cdot\{n_{i}(u_{i}-{\bf{u}})\},

and hence it vanishes when evaluated under the zeroth-order relation
u_{i}={\bf{u}}
.

Next, we check the condition for momentum conservation:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}vf_{i}^{(1)}\,dv

\displaystyle=n_{i}u_{i}^{(1)}=-\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{(0)}\rho_{k}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}\nu_{j}^{(0)}\rho}\left\{\partial_{t}(n_{i}{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}\nabla_{x}(n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}})\right\}

where
j\neq i
.
Summing over
i=1,2
with weights
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left(m_{1}f_{1}^{(1)}+m_{2}f_{2}^{(1)}\right)dv

\displaystyle\quad=-\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{(0)}\rho_{k}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}\nu_{j}^{(0)}\rho}\left\{\partial_{t}\{\rho{\bf{u}}\}+\nabla_{x}\cdot\{\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\}+\nabla_{x}(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}})\right\}.

Again, this involves the left-hand side of the momentum balance in (
3.8
), which vanishes at leading order. Thus, the momentum compatibility condition is satisfied. Therefore, both compatibility conditions (
3.3
) are explicitly verified at the level of first-order corrections, confirming the consistency of the Chapman–Enskog expansion with the underlying conservation laws.

Having derived the compressible two-phase Euler system at leading order and verified that the first-order correction terms preserve both mass and momentum, we now summarize the outcome of the Chapman–Enskog expansion in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(Compressible two-phase fluid limit via Chapman–Enskog expansion)
.

Consider the kinetic BGK model for a two-species mixture (
3.1
). Suppose that the distribution functions admit the Chapman–Enskog expansion

f_{i}=\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},{\bf{u}}]+\varepsilon f_{i}^{(1)},

and the macroscopic quantities
n_{i}
and
{\bf{u}}
are independent of
\varepsilon
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

The zeroth-order macroscopic equations yield the compressible two-phase Euler system:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})=0,\quad i=1,2,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\rho{\bf{u}}\right)+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\right)+\nabla_{x}\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\right)=0.

(ii)

At first order in
\varepsilon
, the system closes to a compressible two-phase Navier–Stokes-type system with density-dependent viscosity:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})=\varepsilon\nabla_{x}\cdot\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{(0)}\rho_{k}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}\nu_{j}^{(0)}\rho}\left\{\partial_{t}(n_{i}{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}\nabla_{x}(n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}})\right\},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\rho{\bf{u}}\right)+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\right)+\nabla_{x}(p_{1}+p_{2})=\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\varepsilon_{1}m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}\mathbb{S}_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}\mathbb{S}_{2}\right),

where
p_{i}=m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}
and the viscosity stress tensors
\mathbb{S}_{i}
are defined by

\mathbb{S}_{i}:=2\mu_{i}(n_{i})\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{x}u)+\lambda_{i}(n_{i})(\nabla_{x}\cdot u)\mathbb{I}_{n\times n},\quad\mathbb{T}(\nabla_{x}u):=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{x}u+(\nabla_{x}u)^{T})

with Lamé viscosity coefficients:

\mu_{i}(n_{i})=\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}},\qquad\lambda_{i}(n_{i})=-\frac{2}{n+2}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\nu_{i}^{(0)}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}.

(iii)

The first-order correction
f_{i}^{(1)}
satisfies the compatibility conditions:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}^{(1)}(v)\,dv=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\left(m_{1}f_{1}^{(1)}+m_{2}f_{2}^{(1)}\right)(v)\,dv=0,

ensuring that both mass and total momentum are preserved up to first order.

4.
Rigorous derivation: Relative entropy method

In this section, we rigorously derive the isentropic two-phase Euler equations from the BGK-type kinetic model (
3.1
). For clarity and without loss of generality, we set the collision frequencies to unity, i.e.,
\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}=1
.

We begin by recalling the form of the limiting system. The two-phase compressible Euler system reads

(4.1)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}n_{i}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}{\bf{u}})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho{\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}})+\nabla_{x}((\gamma_{1}-1)s_{1}(n_{1})+(\gamma_{2}-1)s_{2}(n_{2}))=0,\\
&s_{i}(n_{i}):=\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}},\\
&\rho_{i}:=m_{i}n_{i},\quad\rho:=\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}.\end{split}

To express this system compactly, we introduce the macroscopic observables

U:=(n_{1}\;n_{2}\;w)^{t},\quad w:=\rho{\bf{u}},

so that the system (
4.1
) can be written as a system of conservation laws:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U+\nabla_{x}\cdot A(U)=0,\quad A(U):=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}w^{t}\\[6.0pt]
\frac{n_{2}}{\rho}w^{t}\\[6.0pt]
\frac{w\otimes w}{\rho}+((\gamma_{1}-1)s_{1}(n_{1})+(\gamma_{2}-1)s_{2}(n_{2}))\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}\end{pmatrix}.

This system admits the following entropy-entropy flux pair:

\displaystyle\eta(U):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|w|^{2}}{\rho}+s_{1}(n_{1})+s_{2}(n_{2}),\quad G(U):=u\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{|w|^{2}}{\rho}+\gamma_{1}s_{1}(n_{1})+\gamma_{2}s_{2}(n_{2})\right).

To measure the discrepancy between two states, we define the relative entropy associated with a convex functional
\eta
as

\eta(V|U):=\eta(V)-\eta(U)-D\eta(U)(V-U).

In the sequel, we denote the relative entropy associated with
\eta
by
\eta(V|U)
.

The following theorem provides a quantitative convergence estimate for the hydrodynamic limit of the kinetic model (
3.1
). It shows that, under suitable regularity assumptions and well-prepared initial data, the macroscopic observables of the kinetic solution converge to those of the Euler system with explicit rates.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
,
1<\gamma_{i}\leq 2
, and suppose
(n_{1},n_{2},{\bf{u}})\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s}(\Omega))
is a unique strong solution to (
4.1
) for some
T^{*}>0
, with initial data
({}^{\rm in}n_{1},{}^{\rm in}n_{2},{}^{\rm in}{\bf{u}})
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{m_{1}{}^{\rm in}n_{1}+m_{2}{}^{\rm in}n_{2}}{2}|{}^{\rm in}{\bf{u}}|^{2}+s_{1}({}^{\rm in}n_{1})+s_{2}({}^{\rm in}n_{2})+{}^{\rm in}n_{1}+{}^{\rm in}n_{2}\right)\,dx<+\infty.

Assume further that there exist constants
\lambda,\Lambda>0
such that

(4.2)

\lambda\leq n_{1}(t,x),\,n_{2}(t,x)\leq\Lambda\qquad\forall\,(t,x)\in[0,T^{*}]\times\Omega.

Let
\{f^{\varepsilon}\}=\{f_{1}^{\varepsilon},f_{2}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of weak solutions to (
3.1
), with initial data
\{{}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon}\}
satisfying

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}{}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon}_{1}+{}^{\rm in}f_{2}^{\varepsilon}\,dxdv<+\infty,

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{1}({}^{\rm in}f_{1}^{\varepsilon},v)+h_{2}({}^{\rm in}f_{2}^{\varepsilon},v)\,dxdv<+\infty.

Suppose further that the weak solutions satisfy the entropy inequality

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f^{\varepsilon},v)\,dv+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i}^{\varepsilon},v)-h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}(f^{\varepsilon}),v)\,dxdvds\leq\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}H({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon},v)\,dxdv.

Let the macroscopic observables be defined by

U^{\varepsilon}:=(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}\;n_{2}^{\varepsilon}\;w^{\varepsilon})^{t},\quad w^{\varepsilon}=\rho^{\varepsilon}{\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon},\quad\rho^{\varepsilon}:=\rho_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\rho_{2}^{\varepsilon}.

If the initial data are well-prepared in the sense that

\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}H({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon})-H(\mathcal{M}_{1}({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon}),\mathcal{M}_{2}({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon}))\,dxdv\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/4},

then the relative entropy satisfies, for
t\in[0,T^{*}]
, the estimate

\int_{\Omega}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,dx\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/4}.

In particular, we have the following quantitative convergences:

(4.3)

\begin{split}\|n_{i}^{\varepsilon}-n_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\gamma_{i}})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/8},\\
\|(\rho_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\rho_{2}^{\varepsilon}){\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}){\bf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{1})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/8},\\
\|(\rho_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\rho_{2}^{\varepsilon}){\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}){\bf{u}}\otimes{\bf{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{1})}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/8}.\end{split}

Remark 4.1
.

The local-in-time existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to the system (
4.1
) satisfying the uniform bounds in (
4.2
) can be established under appropriate assumptions on the initial data, see
[
22
]
and
[
42
, Proposition 1.3]
. On the other hand, the global-in-time existence of global solutions to the kinetic model (
3.1
) that satisfy the required entropy inequality has not been studied before, to our best knowledge. For completeness, we therefore provide the corresponding existence theory in Appendix
C
.

4.1.
Moment equations and setup for relative entropy analysis

Our starting step is to write the conservation laws satisfied by the velocity moments of the kinetic equation (
3.1
). Following the balance identities and using Lemma
A.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n_{i}^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}{\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon})=0,\quad i=1,2,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}{\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon})+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}{\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}+m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes v\left(\mathcal{M}_{1}(f_{1}^{\varepsilon},f_{2}^{\varepsilon})-f_{1}^{\varepsilon}\right)dv\right)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}n_{i}^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon}).

In particular, multiplying the momentum equations by
m_{i}
and summing over
i
, the singular relaxation terms cancel out. This yields a conservation law for
U^{\varepsilon}
of the form

\partial_{t}U^{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}\cdot A(U^{\varepsilon})=\nabla_{x}\cdot r(U^{\varepsilon}),

where the remainder term is

r(U^{\varepsilon})=\begin{pmatrix}n_{1}^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf{u}}_{1}^{\varepsilon})\\
n_{2}^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf{u}}_{2}^{\varepsilon})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes v(\mathcal{M}_{i}-f_{i}^{\varepsilon})\,dv\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(\mathcal{M}_{1}-f_{1}^{\varepsilon})\,dv\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(\mathcal{M}_{2}-f_{2}^{\varepsilon})\,dv\\
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\otimes v(\mathcal{M}_{i}-f_{i}^{\varepsilon})\,dv\end{pmatrix}.

To proceed with the relative entropy framework, we now compute key quantities. The gradient and Hessian of the entropy functional
\eta
are given by

\displaystyle D\eta(U)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_{1}|w|^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+s_{1}^{\prime}(n_{1})\\[6.0pt]
-\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_{2}|w|^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+s_{2}^{\prime}(n_{2})\\[6.0pt]
\frac{w}{\rho}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{m_{1}}{2}|{\bf{u}}|^{2}+s_{1}^{\prime}(n_{1})\\[6.0pt]
-\frac{m_{2}}{2}|{\bf{u}}|^{2}+s_{2}^{\prime}(n_{2})\\[6.0pt]
{\bf{u}}\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle D^{2}\eta(U)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{m_{1}^{2}|w|^{2}}{\rho^{3}}+s_{1}^{\prime\prime}(n_{1})&\frac{m_{1}m_{2}|w|^{2}}{\rho^{3}}&-\frac{m_{1}w}{\rho^{2}}\\[6.0pt]
\frac{m_{1}m_{2}|w|^{2}}{\rho^{3}}&\frac{m_{2}^{2}|w|^{2}}{\rho^{3}}+s_{2}^{\prime\prime}(n_{2})&-\frac{m_{2}w}{\rho^{2}}\\[6.0pt]
-\frac{m_{1}w}{\rho^{2}}&-\frac{m_{2}w}{\rho^{2}}&\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{m_{1}^{2}|{\bf{u}}|^{2}}{\rho}+s_{1}^{\prime\prime}(n_{1})&\frac{m_{1}m_{2}|{\bf{u}}|^{2}}{\rho}&-\frac{m_{1}{\bf{u}}}{\rho}\\[6.0pt]
\frac{m_{1}m_{2}|{\bf{u}}|^{2}}{\rho}&\frac{m_{2}^{2}|{\bf{u}}|^{2}}{\rho}+s_{2}^{\prime\prime}(n_{2})&-\frac{m_{2}{\bf{u}}}{\rho}\\[6.0pt]
-\frac{m_{1}{\bf{u}}}{\rho}&-\frac{m_{2}{\bf{u}}}{\rho}&\frac{1}{\rho}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that the relative entropy takes the explicit form

\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)=\frac{\rho^{\varepsilon}}{2}|{\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf{u}}|^{2}+s_{1}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}|n_{1})+s_{2}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}|n_{2}).

We next turn to the flux
A(U)
. The relative flux is defined component-wise by

[A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)]_{i}:=A(U^{\varepsilon})-A(U)-DA_{i}(U)(U^{\varepsilon}-U).

To prepare for the stability analysis, we now compute the relative flux in a form that allows precise control of the nonlinear error terms. This quantity will play a central role in the subsequent relative entropy estimate.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
e_{i}=(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the standard basis vector with 1 at the
i
th entry and zeros elsewhere. Then for each
1\leq i\leq n
, the
i
-th component of the relative flux satisfies

\displaystyle[A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)]_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\left[\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}-\frac{n_{1}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right]\rho^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}_{i}-{\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon})\\[6.0pt]
\left[\frac{n_{2}}{\rho}-\frac{n_{2}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right]\rho^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}_{i}-{\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon})\\[6.0pt]
\rho^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf{u}})({\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf{u}}_{i})+(\gamma_{1}-1)[s_{1}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}|n_{1})]e_{i}+(\gamma_{2}-1)[s_{2}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}|n_{2})]e_{i}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\left[\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}-\frac{n_{1}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right]\rho^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}_{i}-{\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon})\\[6.0pt]
\left[\frac{n_{2}}{\rho}-\frac{n_{2}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right]\rho^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}_{i}-{\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon})\\[6.0pt]
\rho^{\varepsilon}({\bf{u}}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf{u}})({\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf{u}}_{i})+(\gamma_{1}-1)[s_{1}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}|n_{1})]e_{i}+(\gamma_{2}-1)[s_{2}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}|n_{2})]e_{i}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Since the result follows from direct computation, we only outline the relevant quantities. First, compute the Jacobian matrix
DA_{i}(U)
of the
i
th flux component:

\displaystyle DA_{i}(U)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}&-\frac{m_{2}n_{1}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}&\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}e_{i}^{t}\\
-\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}&\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}&\frac{n_{2}}{\rho}e_{i}^{t}\\
-\frac{m_{1}w_{i}w}{\rho^{2}}+(\gamma_{1}-1)s_{1}^{\prime}(n_{1})e_{i}&-\frac{m_{2}w_{i}w}{\rho^{2}}+(\gamma_{2}-1)s_{2}^{\prime}(n_{2})e_{i}&\frac{1}{\rho}(w_{i}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+R_{i}(w))\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}&-\frac{m_{2}n_{1}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}&\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}e_{i}^{t}\\
-\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}&\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}&\frac{n_{2}}{\rho}e_{i}^{t}\\
-\frac{m_{1}w_{i}w}{\rho^{2}}+(\gamma_{1}-1)s_{1}^{\prime}(n_{1})e_{i}&-\frac{m_{2}w_{i}w}{\rho^{2}}+(\gamma_{2}-1)s_{2}^{\prime}(n_{2})e_{i}&\frac{1}{\rho}(w_{i}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+R_{i}(w))\end{pmatrix},

where
R_{i}(w)
is the matrix with the
i
th column equal to
w
and zeros elsewhere:

\displaystyle R_{i}(w):=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&\underbrace{w}_{\textnormal{$i$th column}}&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.

Then, applying this Jacobian to
U^{\varepsilon}-U
and simplifying gives

\displaystyle DA_{i}(U)\cdot(U^{\varepsilon}-U)

\displaystyle\quad=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})-\frac{m_{2}n_{1}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})+\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}(w_{i}^{\varepsilon}-w_{i})\\
\vtop{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}}\\
-\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho^{2}}w_{i}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})+\frac{n_{2}}{\rho}(w_{i}^{\varepsilon}-w_{i})\\
\vtop{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}}\\
-\frac{m_{1}w_{i}w}{\rho^{2}}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})-\frac{m_{2}w_{i}w}{\rho^{2}}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})\\[6.0pt]
+(\gamma_{1}-1)s_{1}^{\prime}(n_{1})(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})e_{i}+(\gamma_{2}-1)s_{2}^{\prime}(n_{2})(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})e_{i}\\[6.0pt]
+\frac{w_{i}}{\rho}(w^{\varepsilon}-w)+\frac{w}{\rho}(w_{i}^{\varepsilon}-w_{i})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\quad=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho}{\bf{u}}_{i}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})-\frac{m_{2}n_{1}}{\rho}{\bf{u}}_{i}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})+\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}(w_{i}^{\varepsilon}-w_{i})\\
\vtop{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}}\\
-\frac{m_{1}n_{2}}{\rho}{\bf{u}}_{i}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho}{\bf{u}}_{i}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})+\frac{n_{2}}{\rho}(w_{i}^{\varepsilon}-w_{i})\\
\vtop{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}}\\
-m_{1}{\bf{u}}_{i}{\bf{u}}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})-m_{2}{\bf{u}}_{i}{\bf{u}}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})\\[6.0pt]
+(\gamma_{1}-1)s_{1}^{\prime}(n_{1})(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})e_{i}+(\gamma_{2}-1)s_{2}^{\prime}(n_{2})(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})e_{i}\\[6.0pt]
+{\bf{u}}_{i}(w^{\varepsilon}-w)+{\bf{u}}(w_{i}^{\varepsilon}-w_{i})\end{pmatrix}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Estimate of the relative entropy

We now quantify the evolution of the relative entropy in time. Let us denote

\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U):=\int_{\Omega}\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,dx.

We begin with a standard identity, which expresses the evolution of
\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)
in terms of the relative flux and remainder terms.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following identity holds:

(4.4)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)&=\mathcal{E}({}^{\rm in}U^{\varepsilon}|{}^{\rm in}U)+\int_{\Omega}\eta(U^{\varepsilon})-\eta({}^{\rm in}U^{\varepsilon})\,dx\\
&\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}D\eta(U):A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,dxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\nabla D\eta(U)\cdot r(U^{\varepsilon})\,dxds.\end{split}

Proof.

A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}[\eta(U^{\varepsilon}|U)]

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(\eta(U^{\varepsilon}))-D\eta(U)\partial_{t}U-D^{2}\eta(U)\partial_{t}U\cdot(U^{\varepsilon}-U)-D\eta(U)\cdot(\partial_{t}U^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{t}U)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(\eta(U^{\varepsilon}))+D^{2}\eta(U)(\nabla_{x}\cdot A(U))(U^{\varepsilon}-U)-D\eta(U)\cdot(-\nabla_{x}\cdot A(U^{\varepsilon})+R(U^{\varepsilon})).

Integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain the evolution identity for the relative entropy

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}(\eta(U^{\varepsilon}))\,dx+\int_{\Omega}D^{2}\eta(U)(\nabla_{x}\cdot A(U))(U^{\varepsilon}-U)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}D\eta(U)\cdot\nabla_{x}A(U^{\varepsilon})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}D\eta(U)\cdot[\nabla_{x}\cdot r(U^{\varepsilon})]\,dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{4}I_{i}.

We now analyze each of the four terms on the right-hand side.
I_{1}
is left unchanged. For
I_{2}
, we use integration by parts and the chain rule (see, e.g.,
[
35
, Lemma 4.4]
) to write

I_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}D\eta(U):(DA(U)(U^{\varepsilon}-U)),

where

[DA(U)(U^{\varepsilon}-U)]_{i}:=DA_{i}(U)\cdot(U^{\varepsilon}-U).

Similarly, we get

I_{3}=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}D\eta(U):A(U^{\varepsilon})\,dx.

Thus the sum of
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
becomes

I_{2}+I_{3}=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}D\eta(U):A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,dx,

where
A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)
denotes the relative flux. Here, we have used the classical entropy-entropy flux identity

\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{x}D\eta(U):A(U)\,dx=0,

which holds for any smooth entropy-entropy flux pair associated to the limiting system (see
[
35
, Eq. (4.14)]
).

Finally, for
I_{4}
, another integration by parts yields

I_{4}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla D\eta(U)\cdot r(U^{\varepsilon})\,dx.

Combining all the contributions, we conclude the desired identity.
∎

As can be seen in the previous lemma, a crucial question is whether we can estimate the relative flux
A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)
which arises on the right-hand side of the relative entropy identity. We now provide an estimate that shows this term is controlled by the relative entropy.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
1<\gamma_{i}\leq 2
. Assume that
(n_{1},n_{2},u)
is the strong solution to (
4.1
) given in Theorem
4.1
. Then for any
t\in[0,T^{*}]
, the following estimate holds:

\int_{\Omega}|A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)|\,dx\leq C_{\lambda,m_{1},m_{2},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\Lambda}\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U).

Proof.

The argument follows the strategy of
[
42
]
, and we provide details for completeness. Note that the first two components of
A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)
are symmetric, and the third can be estimated directly using
\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)
. We thus focus on estimating the first component.

From Hölder’s inequality, we find

\int_{\Omega}|[A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)]^{1}_{i}|\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}-\frac{n_{1}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\right]^{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\varepsilon}|{\bf{u}}_{i}-{\bf{u}}_{i}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

Clearly, the second integral on the right-hand side can be controlled by
\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)
. Thus, we concentrate on estimating the first term, denoted by
I
.

Let us observe that

\frac{n_{1}}{\rho}-\frac{n_{1}^{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=\frac{m_{2}n_{1}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}+\frac{m_{2}n_{2}(n_{1}-n_{1}^{\varepsilon})}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}=\frac{m_{2}n_{2}^{\varepsilon}(n_{1}-n_{1}^{\varepsilon})}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}+\frac{m_{2}n_{1}^{\varepsilon}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})}{\rho\rho^{\varepsilon}}.

This leads to two possible upper bounds for
I
:

(4.5)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{m_{2}^{2}n_{1}^{2}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})^{2}}{\rho^{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}}+\frac{m_{2}^{2}n_{2}^{2}(n_{1}-n_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\rho^{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,dx,

(4.6)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{m_{2}^{2}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon})^{2}(n_{1}-n_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\rho^{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}}+\frac{m_{2}^{2}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{2}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})^{2}}{\rho^{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,dx.

We now distinguish two cases. First, suppose
\rho^{\varepsilon}<\Lambda
. Then using (
4.6
) and the bounds
m_{i}n_{i}^{\varepsilon}<\Lambda
and
m_{i}n_{i}^{\varepsilon}/\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\int_{\Omega}\frac{(n_{1}-n_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\,dx+\frac{m_{2}^{2}\Lambda}{m_{1}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{m_{1},m_{2},\Lambda,\lambda}\int_{\Omega}(n_{1}-n_{1}^{\varepsilon})^{2}+(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{m_{1},m_{2},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\Lambda,\lambda}\int_{\Omega}s_{1}(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}|n_{1})+s_{2}(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}|n_{2})\,dx,

where the final line uses Taylor’s theorem and the condition
\gamma_{i}\leq 2
to deduce

\displaystyle s_{i}(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}|n_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{(n_{i}^{\varepsilon})^{2-\gamma_{i}}},\frac{1}{(n_{i})^{2-\gamma_{i}}}\right\}(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}-n_{i})^{2}\geq C_{m_{i},\gamma_{i},\Lambda}(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}-n_{i})^{2}.

Next, suppose
\rho^{\varepsilon}\geq\Lambda
. In this case we apply (
4.5
) and use the fact that
m_{i}n_{i}/\rho\leq 1
to first estimate

I\leq C_{m_{1},m_{2},\Lambda}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(n_{1}^{\varepsilon}-n_{1})^{2}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+\frac{(n_{2}^{\varepsilon}-n_{2})^{2}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,dx.

In the subcase
n_{i}^{\varepsilon}<\Lambda
, we have

\min\left\{\frac{1}{(n_{i}^{\varepsilon})^{2-\gamma_{i}}},\frac{1}{(n_{i})^{2-\gamma_{i}}}\right\}\geq\Lambda^{\gamma_{i}-2},

and thus

\int_{\Omega}\frac{(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}-n_{i})^{2}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{\Omega}(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}-n_{i})^{2}\,dx\leq C_{m_{i},\gamma_{i},\Lambda}\int_{\Omega}s_{i}(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}|n_{i})\,dx.

If instead
n_{i}^{\varepsilon}\geq\Lambda
, then
n_{i}^{\varepsilon}\geq n_{i}
, and

\min\left\{\frac{1}{(n_{i}^{\varepsilon})^{2-\gamma_{i}}},\frac{1}{(n_{i})^{2-\gamma_{i}}}\right\}=\frac{1}{(n_{i}^{\varepsilon})^{2-\gamma_{i}}},

so using
\gamma_{i}>1
and
\rho_{i}^{\varepsilon}\geq\Lambda
,

\int_{\Omega}\frac{(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}-n_{i})^{2}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\frac{(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}-n_{i})^{2}}{m_{i}n_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\,dx\leq C_{\Lambda,\gamma_{i}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}-n_{i})^{2}}{m_{i}(n_{i}^{\varepsilon})^{2-\gamma_{i}}}\,dx\leq C_{\Lambda,\gamma_{i},m_{i}}\int_{\Omega}s_{i}(n_{i}^{\varepsilon}|n_{i})\,dx.

Combining all cases, we conclude that

I\leq C_{m_{1},m_{2},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\Lambda}\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U),

and hence the desired bound follows.
∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
4.1

We now combine the estimates established in the previous subsections to complete the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Our approach is based on controlling the relative entropy functional via the identity (
4.4

Let

\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)=:J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4},

where the

denote the four terms on the right-hand side of (

).

We begin by estimating

due to the assumption on the well-preparedness of the initial data.

For
J_{2}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\eta(U^{\varepsilon})-\eta({}^{\rm in}U^{\varepsilon})\,dx

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(\mathcal{M}_{1}(f^{\varepsilon}),\mathcal{M}_{2}(f^{\varepsilon}))-H(\mathcal{M}_{1}({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon}),\mathcal{M}_{2}({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon}))\,dxdv

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(f^{\varepsilon})-H({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon})\,dxdv

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}H({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon})-H(\mathcal{M}_{1}({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon}),\mathcal{M}_{2}({}^{\rm in}f^{\varepsilon}))\,dxdv

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/4},

due to the entropy dissipation property and the well-preparedness of the initial data.

To estimate
J_{3}
, we use the expression

\displaystyle D\eta(U)=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{m_{1}}{2}|{\bf{u}}|^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{1}m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}}{\gamma_{1}-1}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}-1}\\[5.69054pt]
-\frac{m_{2}}{2}|{\bf{u}}|^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}}{\gamma_{2}-1}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}-1}\\[5.69054pt]
{\bf{u}}\end{pmatrix}

and the bound
\gamma_{i}\leq 2
to obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla D\eta(U)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)\|{\bf{u}}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{1}m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}}{\lambda^{2-\gamma_{1}}}\|\nabla n_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\gamma_{2}m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}}{\lambda^{2-\gamma_{2}}}\|\nabla n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

Then, by Lemma
4.3

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|A(U^{\varepsilon}|U)|\,dxds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,ds.

For

(4.7)

\begin{split}J_{4}&\leq\|\nabla D\eta(U)\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}r(U^{\varepsilon})\,dv\right|\,dxds\\
&\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v||f_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{M}_{i}|\,dvdxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{i}-f_{i}^{\varepsilon})\,dv\right|dxds\Bigg]\\
&\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sqrt{2}\|f_{i}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}((0,t)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}|f_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{M}_{i}|\,dvdxds\right)^{1/2}\\
&\quad+C\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}|f_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{M}_{i}|\,dvdxds.\end{split}

In the above, the last inequality follows by applying Hölder’s inequality and then the triangle inequality, recalling that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}^{\varepsilon}\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{i}(f^{\varepsilon})\,dv
. Since the kinetic equation conserves mass, we find

\|f_{i}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}((0,t)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}=t\|{}^{\rm in}f_{i}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The second moment terms can be estimated by (see
[
5

[

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}|f_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{M}_{i}|\,dvdxds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}((n_{i}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma_{i}/2}\sqrt{D_{i}}+D_{i})\,dxds,

where

D_{i}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i}^{\varepsilon})-h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}(f_{1}^{\varepsilon},f_{2}^{\varepsilon}))\,dv\geq 0

by Remark
A.1
. Using the uniform bound of
n_{i}^{\varepsilon}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma_{i}})

(4.8)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}|f_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{M}_{i}|\,dvdxds&\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dx\right)^{1/2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds\\
&\leq C_{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds\right)^{1/2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds.\end{split}

Combining (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), and applying arithmetic geometric inequality
\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}\leq\sqrt{2(x+y)}
twice, we deduce

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\left(2\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}|f_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{M}_{i}|\,dvdxds\right)^{1/2}+C\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}|f_{i}^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{M}_{i}|\,dvdxds

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds\right)^{1/2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{T}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds\right)^{1/2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds\right)^{1/4}+C_{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds\right)^{1/2}+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{i}\,dxds

\displaystyle\lesssim_{T}\varepsilon^{1/4}.

Summing the estimates for

through

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\leq C_{T}\varepsilon^{1/4}+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}(U^{\varepsilon}|U)\,ds,

from which the desired estimate follows via Grönwall inequality. The strong convergence statements in (

[

,

]
for the standard arguments.

5.
Numerical scheme and asymptotic-preserving property

In this section, we describe a class of finite difference numerical methods for the BGK model (
3.1
). In the construction of a numerical method, the main issue is whether we can capture the correct dynamics in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. More precisely, in view of the efficiency of numerical methods, we need to handle the time step restriction that arises from the small Knudsen number. One popular approach to address the stiff problem is to use an implicit-explicit time discretization. In detail, the non-stiff convection term is handled explicitly, and hence CFL-type time step restriction remains. However, the stiff relaxation term is treated implicitly, which allows us to avoid time step restriction from small Knudsen numbers. It is notable that this strategy has been widely adopted in numerous works
[
23
,
32
,
43
]
.

In this work, we adopt implicit-explicit Runge–Kutta (IMEX-RK) methods for time discretization. Butcher’s table that corresponds to
s
-stage IMEX-RK time discretization is represented by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c|c}\tilde{c}&\tilde{A}\\
\hline\cr\\[-8.53581pt]
&\tilde{b}^{\top}\end{array},\quad\begin{array}[]{c|c}c&A\\
\hline\cr\\[-8.53581pt]
&b^{\top}\end{array},

where
\tilde{A},\,A
are
s\times s
real matrices and
\tilde{b}^{\top},\,b^{\top},\,\tilde{c},\,c
are real vectors of length
s
. Note that the left table
(\tilde{A},\tilde{b},\tilde{c})
is for the explicit part and the right one
(A,b,c)
is for the implicit part.

Among numerous IMEX-RK methods, when choosing IMEX method, we take into account two aspects: in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
(I) whether the schemes degenerate into an approximation of the isentropic Euler system and (II) whether the scheme ensures the asymptotic accuracy.
These two properties are referred to as asymptotic preserving (AP) and asymptotic accurate (AA) properties in the literature
[
10
]
. In our framework, AP property means that a consistent time discretization of BGK model (
3.1
) becomes a consistent time discretization method for the isentropic Euler system (
3.10
) in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, and AA property implies that the order of time discretization is maintained in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. Note that these properties enable us to ensure the robustness and high accuracy of the numerical methods in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
.

With reference to
[
10
]
, to attain the AP and AA properties, we adopt a
L
-stable second order globally stiffly accurate (GSA) IMEX-RK method called ARS(2,3,2)
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle\tilde{A}=\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}0&0&0&0\\
\alpha&\alpha&0&0\\
1&\delta&1-\delta&0\\
\hline\cr\\[-8.53581pt]
&\delta&1-\delta&0\end{array},\quad A=\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}0&0&0&0\\
\alpha&0&\alpha&0\\
1&0&1-\alpha&\alpha\\
\hline\cr\\[-8.53581pt]
&0&1-\alpha&\alpha\end{array},\quad\alpha=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\quad\delta=1-\frac{1}{2\alpha},

where the left table applies to the convection term, while the right one applies to the relaxation terms. Note that the adopted IMEX-RK method (
5
) is of type CK and ARS. In addition, the method also satisfies the GSA condition, which means that the last row of
\tilde{A}
and
A
coincides with the weights
\tilde{b}^{\top}
and
b^{\top}
. This is an important feature to attain AP property. For more details, we refer to a book
[
10
]
.

Now, we describe general
s
-stage IMEX-RK methods for (
3.1
). For the description of numerical methods, we consider the case when
\nu_{i}
is dependent on
n_{i}
, i.e., there is no dependency on

3.1

s

(5.11)

f_{i}^{(k)}=f_{i}^{m}-\Delta t\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\tilde{a}_{k\ell}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{i}^{(\ell)}+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}a_{k\ell}\nu_{i}^{(\ell)}\left(\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\ell)}-f_{i}^{(\ell)}\right),\quad k=1,\dots,s,\quad i=1,2,

to derive the equation for bulk velocity of the mixture:

(5.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{i}\langle{m_{i}v\bf{f}}_{i}\rangle&=\sum_{i}\langle m_{i}vf_{i}^{m}\rangle{\textbf{e}_{s}}-\Delta t\tilde{A}\langle m_{i}vv\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf{f}}_{i}\rangle+\frac{\Delta t}{\varepsilon}A{{\bf{\nu}}}_{i}\left\langle{m_{i}v\bf{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}-m_{i}v{\bf{f}}_{i}\right\rangle,\cr&=\sum_{i}\langle m_{i}vf_{i}^{m}\rangle{\textbf{e}_{s}}-\Delta t\tilde{A}\langle m_{i}vv\cdot\nabla_{x}{\bf{f}}_{i}\rangle,\end{split}

where the second equality holds due to the definition of common velocity.

Next, we pass the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, then, by the assumption that the initial data is well-prepared, we have

(5.19)

\displaystyle f_{i}^{(1)}=\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i}^{m},\mathbf{u}^{m}],\quad i=1,2.

This further gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}n_{i}^{(1)}&=n_{i}^{m},\quad n_{i}^{(1)}u_{i}^{(1)}=n_{i}^{m}\mathbf{u}^{m},\quad i=1,2,\end{split}

and

\rho^{(1)}\mathbf{u}^{(1)}=m_{1}n_{1}^{(1)}u_{1}^{(1)}+m_{2}n_{2}^{(1)}u_{2}^{(1)}=\rho^{m}\mathbf{u}^{m}.

In a similar manner, in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, we derive from the second to last rows of (
5.16
) that

\displaystyle\hat{A}\hat{\bf{\nu}}_{i}(\hat{\bf{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}-\hat{\bf{f}}_{i})={\bf{0}}_{s-1},\quad i=1,2,

where
\hat{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{s-1\times s-1}
is a submatrix of
A
, i.e.,
A=\begin{bmatrix}0&\bf{0}_{s-1}^{\top}\\
\bf{0}_{s-1}&\hat{A}\end{bmatrix}
and
{\bf{0}}_{s-1}=(0,\dots,0)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{s-1}
.
Since the submatrix
\hat{A}
in the IMEX-RK methods of type CK is non-singular and
\nu_{i}^{(k)}\neq 0
is assumed for
k=2,\dots,s
, we further have

(5.20)

\displaystyle{\hat{\bf{f}}_{i}=\hat{\bf{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}}\quad i=1,2.

Taking the moment with respect to
v
in this equation, we have
u_{i}^{(k)}=u^{(k)}
for
k=2,\dots,s
. This further implies that

\textbf{u}^{(k)}=\frac{\rho_{1}^{(k)}u_{1}^{(k)}+\rho_{2}^{(k)}u_{2}^{(k)}}{\rho^{(k)}}=u^{(k)},\quad k=2,\dots,s.

Now, we insert (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) into (
5.17
) and (
5.18
) to derive

\displaystyle\begin{split}n_{i}^{(1)}&=n_{i}^{m},\cr n_{i}^{(k)}&=n_{i}^{m}-\Delta t\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\tilde{a}_{k\ell}\nabla_{x}\cdot(n_{i}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{u}^{(\ell)}),\quad k=2,\dots,s,\quad i=1,2,\cr\rho^{(1)}\mathbf{u}^{(1)}&=\rho^{m}\mathbf{u}^{m},\cr\rho^{(k)}\mathbf{u}^{(k)}&=\rho^{m}\mathbf{u}^{m}-\Delta t\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\tilde{a}_{k\ell}\left[\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\rho^{(\ell)}\mathbf{u}^{(\ell)}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{(\ell)}\right)+\nabla_{x}\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\right)\right],\quad k=2,\dots,s.\end{split}

This is the explicit RK scheme characterized by
(\tilde{A},\tilde{b})
applied to the isentropic Euler equations (
3.10
). Note that the scheme is GSA, and hence we have
f_{i}^{m+1}=\mathcal{M}_{i}^{m+1}
for each

. This again becomes the well-prepared initial data for the next time step.
∎

Appendix A
On truncated Maxwellians

A.1.
Derivation via minimization principle

This appendix is devoted to the derivation of the equilibrium distribution
\mathcal{M}_{i}
via an entropy minimization principle, following the approach pioneered in
[
11
]
. As a preliminary step, we present an auxiliary lemma that provides several key integral identities. These moment formulas for truncated functions will be instrumental in both the asymptotic expansions and the hydrodynamic limit analysis.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the following integral identities hold:

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}(1-|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dv=\frac{1}{2}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2},\alpha+1\right)
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}1}v\otimes v(1-|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dv=\frac{1}{2n}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+1,\alpha+1\right)\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}
,

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}|v|^{2}v_{a}^{2}(1-|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dv=\frac{1}{2n}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+2,\alpha+1\right)
,

(iv)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}v_{a}^{2}v_{b}^{2}(1-|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dv=\frac{1}{2n(n+2)}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+2,\alpha+1\right)
for
a\neq b
,

(v)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}_{1}}v_{a}^{4}(1-|v|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dv=\frac{3}{2n(n+2)}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+2,\alpha+1\right)
.

Here
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is the beta function defined as

B(p,q)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{p-1}(1-t)^{q-1}dt=\frac{\Gamma(p)\Gamma(q)}{\Gamma(p+q)}.

Proof.

We evaluate these integrals using spherical coordinates. Set
v=r\omega
, with
r\in[0,1]
and
\omega\in\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}
, then
dv=r^{n-1}drd\omega
and
|v|=r
. A homogeneous polynomial
g(v)
of degree
k
decomposes as
g(r\omega)=r^{k}h(\omega)
, with
h(\omega)
homogeneous on the sphere.

Thus,

\int_{{\rm B}_{1}}g(v)(1-|v|^{2})^{\alpha}dv=\left(\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}h(\omega)\,d\omega\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}r^{n-1+k}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}\,dr\right).

The angular part is evaluated using standard identities:

\displaystyle\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{a}^{2}d\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{a}^{4}d\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{n(n+2)}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{a}^{2}\omega_{b}^{2}d\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n(n+2)}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|\quad(a\neq b).

These identities result from rotational symmetry and the identity
\sum_{a=1}^{n}\omega_{a}^{2}=1
on the sphere. Specifically,

\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{a}^{2}\,d\omega=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\omega_{a}^{2}\,d\omega=\frac{1}{n}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|.

We next consider the rotationally symmetric fourth-order tensor. Note that

\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\omega_{a}^{2}\right)^{2}d\omega=\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}1^{2}\,d\omega=|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|.

On the other hand, expanding the square gives

\left(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\omega_{a}^{2}\right)^{2}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\omega_{a}^{4}+2\sum_{1\leq a<b\leq n}\omega_{a}^{2}\omega_{b}^{2}.

Note that for
a\neq b

\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{a}^{2}\omega_{b}^{2}\,d\omega=\frac{\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n}\varphi_{1}\cos^{2}\varphi_{1}\,d\varphi_{1}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-3}\varphi_{2}\cos^{2}\varphi_{2}\,d\varphi_{2}\right)}{\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-2}\varphi_{1}\,d\varphi_{1}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-3}\varphi_{2}\,d\varphi_{2}\right)}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|=\frac{1}{n(n+2)}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|,

where we used

\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n}x\,dx=\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-2}x\,dx

so that

\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n}\varphi_{1}\cos^{2}\varphi_{1}\,d\varphi_{1}=\frac{n-1}{n(n+2)}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-2}\varphi_{1}\,d\varphi_{1}

and

\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-3}\varphi_{2}\cos^{2}\varphi_{2}\,d\varphi_{2}=\frac{1}{n-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-3}\varphi_{2}\,d\varphi_{2}.

This together with symmetry implies

|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|=n\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{1}^{4}\,d\omega+n(n-1)\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{2}^{2}\,d\omega=n\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{1}^{4}\,d\omega+\frac{n-1}{n+2}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|.

Thus,

\int_{\partial{\rm B}_{1}}\omega_{a}^{4}\,d\omega=\frac{3}{n(n+2)}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|.

The radial integral is computed via the substitution
s=r^{2}
, yielding

\int_{0}^{1}r^{m}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}dr=\frac{1}{2}B\left(\frac{m+1}{2},\alpha+1\right).

This produces the stated identities.
∎

To characterize the equilibrium distributions
\mathcal{M}_{i}
, we now turn to an entropy minimization framework under suitable macroscopic constraints. For
\gamma_{i}\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
and
m_{i}>0
, we define the entropy density function

(A.1)

\displaystyle h_{i}(f_{i},v):=\frac{m_{i}|v|^{2}}{2}f_{i}+\frac{1}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\frac{f_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}}{1+2/d_{i}}

and set

\displaystyle H(p_{1},p_{2},v):=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}h_{i}(p_{i},v).

Given
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\left(1,\frac{n+2}{n}\right)
and nonnegative functions
f_{1},f_{2}
, our goal is to minimize the total entropy

\begin{split}\argmin\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(p_{1},p_{2},v)\,dv&=\argmin\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}h_{i}(p_{i},v)\,dv\\
&=\argmin\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\Big(\frac{m_{i}|v|^{2}}{2}p_{i}+\frac{1}{2c_{i}^{2/d_{i}}}\frac{p_{i}^{1+2/d_{i}}}{1+2/d_{i}}\Big)\,dv\right\},\end{split}

subject to the following constraints:

(A.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}p_{i}(v)\geq 0\quad i=1,2,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(p_{i}-f_{i})\,dv=0\quad i=1,2,\\[7.11317pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\nu_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}vm_{i}(f_{i}-p_{i})\,dv=0.\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{X}_{i}
denote the admissible class for
p_{i}
:

\mathcal{X}_{i}:=\left\{p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R},\,p\geq 0,\,p\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},(1+|v|^{2})dv)\cap L^{1+2/d_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\}.

We introduce the Lagrangian functional
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(p_{1},p_{2},\eta,\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(p_{1},p_{2},v)\,dv-\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\begin{pmatrix}p_{1}-f_{1}\\
p_{2}-f_{2}\\
v\Big(\nu_{1}m_{1}(p_{1}-f_{1})+\nu_{2}m_{2}(p_{2}-f_{2})\Big)\end{pmatrix}dv,

where
(\eta,\lambda)=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
are the Lagrange multipliers associated with the mass and momentum constraints.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2},\eta^{*},\lambda^{*})
be a critical point of this functional. Then, for any
p\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
satisfying the constraints (
A.2
), the directional derivative of
\mathcal{L}
along
(p,0)
must vanish:

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{L}((\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2},\eta^{*},\lambda^{*});(p,0,0,0)):=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{M}_{1}+\varepsilon p,\mathcal{M}_{2},\eta^{*},\lambda^{*})\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=0,

whenever it is true that
\mathcal{M}_{1}+\varepsilon p\geq 0
(for this is when the right-hand side is well-defined). Note in particular that this implies the above holds for each
p\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
that is supported only on
V_{1}:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathcal{M}_{1}>0\}
. By computing the variation, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{L}((\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2},\eta^{*},\lambda^{*});(p,0,0,0))

\displaystyle=\int_{V_{1}}p(v)\cdot\Big(\frac{\nu_{1}m_{1}}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{\nu_{1}}{2c_{1}^{2/d_{1}}}\mathcal{M}_{1}^{2/d_{1}}-\eta^{*}_{1}-\nu_{1}m_{1}\lambda^{*}\cdot v\Big)\,dv=0,

which implies the Euler–Lagrange condition:

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{1}}{2c_{1}^{2/d_{1}}}\mathcal{M}_{1}^{2/d_{1}}=-\frac{\nu_{1}m_{1}}{2}|v|^{2}+\eta_{1}^{*}+\nu_{1}m_{1}\lambda^{*}\cdot v\quad\text{in}\quad V_{1}.

By positivity of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
in
V_{1}
, the right-hand side must be strictly positive there. At the boundary
\partial V_{1}:=\{v:\mathcal{M}_{1}(v)=0\}
, convex analysis (see
[
11
, Section 3.1]
) yields the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}}{2}|v|^{2}\leq\frac{\eta^{*}_{1}}{\nu_{1}}+m_{1}\lambda^{*}\cdot v\quad\text{on}\quad\partial V_{1}.

Hence, we conclude that the equilibrium distribution takes the truncated polynomial form

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}=c_{1}\Big(-m_{1}|v|^{2}+2(\frac{\eta_{1}^{*}}{\nu_{1}}+\nu_{1}m_{1}\lambda^{*}\cdot v)\Big)_{+}^{d_{1}/2}.

A symmetric derivation yields the corresponding formula for
\mathcal{M}_{2}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}=c_{1}\Big(-m_{2}|v|^{2}+2(\frac{\eta_{2}^{*}}{\nu_{2}}+\nu_{2}m_{2}\lambda^{*}\cdot v)\Big)_{+}^{d_{2}/2}.

We now identify the explicit expressions for the Lagrange multipliers
\eta^{*}
and
\lambda^{*}
by utilizing the constraints (
A.2
), and in doing so, we obtain the explicit form of the truncated Maxwellians.

We begin with the mass and momentum identities for
\mathcal{M}_{1}
. By Lemma
A.1
(i) and the change of variables
v\mapsto v-\lambda^{*}
, we find

\begin{split}n_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{1}(v)\,dv&=c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(-m_{1}|v|^{2}+2\frac{\eta_{1}^{*}}{\nu_{1}}+m_{1}|\lambda^{*}|^{2}\Big)^{d_{1}/2}_{+}\,dv\\
&=c_{1}\frac{(2\frac{\eta_{1}^{*}}{\nu_{1}}+m_{1}|\lambda^{*}|^{2})^{\frac{d_{1}+n}{2}}}{m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{B_{1}}(1-|v|^{2})^{d_{1}/2}_{+}\,dv\\
&=c_{1}\frac{(2\frac{\eta_{1}^{*}}{\nu_{1}}+m_{1}|\lambda^{*}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}-1}}}{m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}}}\frac{1}{2}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|B\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{d_{1}}{2}+1\right).\end{split}

Using the following

|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)},\quad c_{i}=\left(\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}-1}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1\right)},\quad B\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+d_{i}}{2}\right)},

we get

\frac{c_{1}}{2}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|B\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{d_{1}}{2}+1\right)=\left(\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}-1}}

and thus

n_{1}=\frac{(2\frac{\eta_{1}^{*}}{\nu_{1}}+m_{1}|\lambda^{*}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}-1}}}{m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}-1}}.

This yields

2\frac{\eta_{1}^{*}}{\nu_{1}}+m_{1}|\lambda^{*}|^{2}=\left(\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}-1}.

Next, using the symmetry of the integrand, we compute the momentum as

(A.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\mathcal{M}_{1}(v)\,dv=\lambda^{*}n_{1}.

A symmetric computation yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\mathcal{M}_{2}(v)\,dv=\lambda^{*}n_{2}.

Applying the momentum constraint (
A.2
)
3
, we deduce

(A.4)

\lambda^{*}=\frac{\nu_{1}\rho_{1}u_{1}+\nu_{2}\rho_{2}u_{2}}{\nu_{1}\rho_{1}+\nu_{2}\rho_{2}}=:u.

Plugging this into the expression for
\eta_{1}^{*}
, we obtain

2\frac{\eta_{1}^{*}}{\nu_{1}}+m_{1}|u|^{2}=\left(\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}-1}.

Therefore, we recover the explicit formula for
\mathcal{M}_{1}=\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},u](v)
:

\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},u](v)=c_{1}\left(-m_{1}|v-u|^{2}+m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)_{+}^{d_{1}/2}.

Similarly, we obtain the expression for
\mathcal{M}_{2}
:

\mathcal{M}_{2}[n_{2},u](v)=c_{2}\left(-m_{2}|v-u|^{2}+m_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{2}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{2}-1}n_{2}^{\gamma_{2}-1}\right)_{+}^{d_{2}/2}.

These explicit forms confirm that the equilibrium distribution functions are truncated polynomials supported on velocity balls centered at the bulk velocity
u
, with radii determined by the local densities and parameters
m_{i}
,
\gamma_{i}
. The case
\gamma_{i}=1+\frac{2}{n}
can be treated similarly by taking the appropriate limit.

A.2.
Macroscopic identities for Maxwellians

In this subsection, we derive several macroscopic identities satisfied by the truncated Maxwellian distributions. These identities correspond to the conserved moments and energy functional evaluated at equilibrium.

Lemma A.2
.

Then the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v)\,dv=n_{i},

(A.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v)\,dv=n_{i}u,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v)\,dv=n_{i}u\otimes u+m_{i}^{\frac{n(\gamma_{i}-1)}{2}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v),v)\,dv=\frac{m_{i}}{2}n_{i}|u|^{2}+\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n(\gamma_{i}-1)}{2}}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}},

where
h_{i}
is given by (
A.1
).

Proof.

For brevity, we only compute the quantities associated with
\mathcal{M}_{1}
; the case for
\mathcal{M}_{2}
follows analogously by symmetry. We also consider the case
\gamma_{i}\in(1,1+\frac{2}{n})
since the case
\gamma_{i}=1+\frac{2}{n}
can be treated similarly.

The first assertion is just by construction. The momentum identity (
A.5
) follows from (
A.3
) and (
A.4
). For the identity (
A.6
), we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},u](v)\,dv=n_{1}u\otimes u+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-u)\otimes(v-u)\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},u](v)\,dv.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-u)\otimes(v-u)\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},u](v)\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},0](v)\,dv

\displaystyle=c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}-1}-m_{1}|v|^{2}\right)_{+}^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{m_{1}^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)^{\frac{n+d_{1}+2}{2}}\int_{{\rm B}_{1}}(v\otimes v)(1-|v|^{2})^{\frac{d_{1}}{2}}\,dv

\displaystyle=c_{1}m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)-1}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\frac{1}{2n}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{d_{1}}{2}+1\right)\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}

due to Lemma
A.1
(ii). Since

c_{1}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}}\frac{1}{2n}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{d_{1}}{2}+1\right)=1,

we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-u)\otimes(v-u)\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},u](v)\,dv=m_{1}^{\frac{n(\gamma_{1}-1)}{2}-1}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n},

and thus the desired identity is obtained.

For the kinetic entropy estimate, as a direct consequence of the above, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{m_{1}|v|^{2}}{2}\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},u](v)\,dv=\frac{m_{1}}{2}n_{1}|u|^{2}+\frac{n}{2}m_{1}^{\frac{n(\gamma_{1}-1)}{2}}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}.

Next, we calculate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2c_{1}^{\frac{2}{d_{1}}}}\frac{1}{1+\frac{2}{d_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},u](v))^{1+\frac{2}{d_{1}}}\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2c_{1}^{\frac{2}{d_{1}}}}\frac{1}{1+\frac{2}{d_{1}}}(\mathcal{M}_{1}[n_{1},0](v))^{1+\frac{2}{d_{1}}}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{2(1+\frac{2}{d_{1}})}\frac{\left(m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}-1}+1}}{m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{{\rm B}_{1}}(1-|v|^{2})^{\frac{d_{1}+2}{2}}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{2(1+\frac{2}{d_{1}})}m_{1}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{1}-1)}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-1}}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\frac{1}{2}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{d_{1}}{2}+2\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{1}}{2}m_{1}^{\frac{n(\gamma_{1}-1)}{2}}n_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}.

Combining those results concludes the desired result.
∎

Remark A.1
.

The Maxwellians
\mathcal{M}_{i}
were obtained by minimizing the total entropy functional

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nu_{i}h_{i}(p_{i},v)\,dv

under the constraints of given macroscopic quantities. A noteworthy consequence of this construction is that each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
also minimizes the individual entropy
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i},v)\,dv
among all functions with the same local moments. That is, for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}(f_{1},f_{2}),v)\,dv\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i},v)\,dv,\quad i=1,2.

This decoupled entropy-minimizing property will be crucial in the relative entropy method when establishing the rigorous hydrodynamic limit.

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
3.1
: Velocity moments of truncated Maxwellians

In this appendix, we provide a detailed proof of Lemma
3.1
, which collects several key velocity moment identities associated with the truncated Maxwellian distributions
\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]
. These identities are essential in computing first-order corrections in the Chapman–Enskog expansion and appear repeatedly in the derivation of macroscopic quantities such as the pressure tensor and momentum flux.

For the reader’s convenience, we recall below Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u]
be the truncated Maxwellian defined by

\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},u](v)=c_{i}\left(m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}-m_{i}|v-u|^{2}\right)_{+}^{\frac{d_{i}}{2}},

where
c_{i}
is a normalization constant. Then the following identities hold:

(i)

Zeroth-order moment:

c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0](v)\,dv=\frac{1}{d_{i}\gamma_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}n_{i}^{2-\gamma_{i}}.

(ii)

Second-order moment:

c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0](v)\,dv=\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}.

(iii)

Graident-weighted fourth-order moment:

\displaystyle c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}u_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}v_{q}(v\otimes v)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},0](v)\,dv

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}^{2-\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}\left\{(\nabla_{x}\cdot u)\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}+\nabla_{x}u+(\nabla_{x}u)^{t}\right\}.

Proof.

Let us write
\mathcal{M}_{i}:=\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},0]
for simplicity. Each identity below is derived using the integral formulas collected in Lemma
A.1
.

Using Lemma
A.1
(i), we first compute the zeroth-order moment as

\displaystyle c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}(v)\,dv

\displaystyle=c_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}-m_{i}|v|^{2}\right)_{+}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{i}}{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left(m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)^{\frac{n+d_{i}-2}{2}}\int_{{\rm B}_{1}}(1-|v|^{2})^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{i}}{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)^{\frac{2-\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|B\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right).

Using the known expressions:

|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)},\quad c_{i}=\left(\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}-1}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1\right)},\quad B\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+d_{i}}{2}\right)},

we deduce

c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}(v)\,dv=\frac{1}{d_{i}\gamma_{i}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}n_{i}^{2-\gamma_{i}}.

Using Lemma
A.1
(ii), the isotropy of the integrand gives

\displaystyle c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v\otimes v)\,\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}(v)\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{i}}{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}\left(m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)^{\frac{n+d_{i}}{2}}\int_{{\rm B}_{1}}(v\otimes v)(1-|v|^{2})^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{i}}{m_{i}}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{2n}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|B\left(\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}.

Note that

B\left(\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)=B\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)\frac{\frac{n}{2}}{\frac{n}{2}+\frac{d_{i}}{2}}=B\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1\right)\frac{n}{d_{i}}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1})}\frac{n}{d_{i}}.

Substituting the constants, this simplifies to

c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v-u)\otimes(v-u)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},u](v)\,dv=\frac{n_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}}\mathbb{I}_{n\times n}.

Finally, we compute the fourth-order moment. For this, we first consider

\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}v_{q}(v\otimes v)(1-|v|^{2})^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}\,dv=:(A_{ab})_{n\times n}.

For the diagonal entries
A_{aa}
, we calculate

\displaystyle A_{aa}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}v_{a}^{2}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}\,dv=\frac{1}{2n}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|B\left(\frac{n}{2}+2,\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)=\frac{n}{2d_{i}(n+d_{i})}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1})}.

For the off-diagonal entries
A_{ab}
with
a\neq b
, we obtain

(B.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{ab}&=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{a}^{2}v_{b}^{2}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}\,dv\cr&=2\frac{1}{n(n+2)}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+2,\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)\cr&=\frac{1}{n(n+2)}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1})}\frac{n^{2}}{d_{i}(n+d_{i})}\cr&=\frac{n}{d_{i}(n+2)(n+d_{i})}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1})}.\end{split}

We now multiply the fourth-order tensor by the prefactor

\frac{c_{i}}{m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}+2}}\left(m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-1}\right)^{\frac{n+d_{i}+2}{2}},

and simplify to obtain the final result:

c_{i}^{\frac{2}{d_{i}}}\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v_{p}-u_{p})(v_{q}-u_{q})(v-u)\otimes(v-u)\mathcal{M}_{i}^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{d_{i}}}[n_{i},u](v)\,dv=\frac{n\gamma_{i}}{d_{i}m_{i}^{2-\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}}n_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}(B_{ab})_{n\times n},

where the matrix
(B_{ab})
is given by

B_{ab}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }a=b,\\
\frac{2}{n+2}&\text{if }a\neq b.\end{cases}

Now define

(C_{ab})_{n\times n}:=\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}u_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}v_{q}(v\otimes v)\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}dv.

For the diagonal entries
C_{aa}
, we write

\displaystyle C_{aa}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}u_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}v_{q}v_{a}^{2}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}dv

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}u_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}^{2}v_{a}^{2}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}dv

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\neq a}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}u_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}^{2}v_{a}^{2}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}dv+\partial_{x_{a}}u_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{a}^{4}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n(n+2)}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+2,\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)\sum_{p\neq a}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}u_{p}+\frac{3}{2n(n+2)}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+2,\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)\partial_{x_{a}}u_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n(n+2)}|\partial\mathrm{B}_{1}|\,B\left(\frac{n}{2}+2,\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right)\left(\nabla_{x}\cdot u+2\partial_{x_{a}}u_{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{n}{d_{i}(n+2)(n+d_{i})}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1})}\left(\nabla_{x}\cdot u+2\partial_{x_{a}}u_{a}\right),

where we used (
B.1
) and Lemma
A.1
(iv) and (v). For the off-diagonal entries
C_{ab}
,
a\neq b
, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{ab}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{p}}u_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{p}v_{q}v_{a}v_{b}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}dv

\displaystyle=(\partial_{x_{a}}u_{b})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{a}^{2}v_{b}^{2}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}dv+(\partial_{x_{b}}u_{a})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{a}^{2}v_{b}^{2}\left(1-|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{d_{i}-2}{2}}_{+}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{n}{d_{i}(n+2)(n+d_{i})}|\partial{\rm B}_{1}|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d_{i}}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1})}(\partial_{a}u_{b}+\partial_{b}u_{a}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Global existence of mild solutions

In this appendix, we prove the global-in-time existence of mild solutions to the kinetic BGK-type system (
1.1
), under suitable assumptions on the initial data. The constructed solutions also satisfy a kinetic entropy inequality. For simplicity, we fix the collision frequencies to unity:
\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}=1
. Note that the parameter
\varepsilon>0
used in this section refers to a regularization parameter introduced later, and should not be confused with the Knudsen number used in the Chapman–Enskog expansion. For simplicity, we write
f:=(f_{1},f_{2})
and denote by
\mathcal{M}_{i}[f]:=\mathcal{M}_{i}[n_{i},\bf u]
the local equilibrium associated with species
i
, where
n_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}\,dv
and the bulk velocity
\bf u
is defined as in (
1.2
). For clarity of presentation, we restrict to the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The case
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
is in fact simpler due to the compactness of the spatial domain.

Theorem C.1
.

Let
T>0
be given, and let the initial data
{}^{\rm in}f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, for
i=1,2
, satisfy the finite entropy and mass condition

(C.1)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}H({}^{\rm in}f,v)+{}^{\rm in}f_{1}+{}^{\rm in}f_{2}\,dxdv<+\infty.

Then there exists a global-in-time weak solution
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
to the BGK equation (
1.1
) which satisfies the Duhamel-type mild formulation:

\displaystyle f_{i}(t,x,v)=e^{-t}\cdot{}^{\rm in}f_{i}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}\mathcal{M}_{i}[f](s,x-v(t-s),v)\,ds.

for almost every
(t,x,v)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for
i=1,2
.

Moreover, the solution satisfies

(C.2)

\displaystyle f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}\cap L^{1+2/d_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))\times L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}\cap L^{1+2/d_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})),

and the kinetic entropy inequality

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i},v)\,dxdv+\int_{0}^{t}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}[f],v)\,dxdvds\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}({}^{\rm in}f_{i},v)\,dxdv.

Remark C.1
.

Once the mild formulation holds, one can verify that
f_{i}
satisfies the BGK system (
1.1
) in the sense of distributions. See
[
38
, Appendix C]
for details.

Remark C.2
.

The proof presented below addresses the case
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in(1,\frac{n+2}{n})
, where the truncated Maxwellians are unbounded but integrable. The endpoint case
\gamma_{i}=\frac{n+2}{n}
can be handled by similar, and in fact simpler, arguments due to the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
in that regime.

C.1.
Regularization and linearization

To establish global existence, we first construct a regularized version of the BGK model (
1.1
). Specifically, we define the regularized macroscopic quantities:

\displaystyle n^{(\varepsilon)}_{f_{1}}:=\frac{n_{f_{1}}}{1+\varepsilon n_{f_{1}}},\quad{\bf{u}}^{(\varepsilon)}_{f_{1},f_{2}}:=\frac{\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}+\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}}{\rho_{f_{1}}+\rho_{f_{2}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|)},

and consider the following regularized BGK-type system:

(C.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon}_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}_{1}=\mathcal{M}_{1}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-f_{1}^{\varepsilon},\\
\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon}_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}_{2}=\mathcal{M}_{2}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-f_{2}^{\varepsilon},\\
f^{\varepsilon}_{1}(0,\cdot,\cdot)={}^{\rm in}f_{1}(\cdot,\cdot),\\
f^{\varepsilon}_{2}(0,\cdot,\cdot)={}^{\rm in}f_{2}(\cdot,\cdot),\\
\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]:=c_{i}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}(n_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)})^{\gamma_{i}-1}-m_{i}\left|v-{\bf{u}}^{(\varepsilon)}_{f_{1}^{\varepsilon},f_{2}^{\varepsilon}}\right|^{2}\right)_{+}^{d_{i}/2}.\end{cases}

Notice that the regularized Maxwellian can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[(f_{1},f_{2})]=c_{i}m_{i}^{d_{i}/2}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)-1}(n_{f_{i}}^{(\varepsilon)})^{\gamma_{i}-1}-\left|v-{\bf{u}}^{(\varepsilon)}_{f_{1},f_{2}}\right|^{2}\right)_{+}^{d_{i}/2}.

To justify the existence of the regularized system, we rely on the stability properties of the truncated Maxwellian. In particular, the following estimate from
[
38
, Lemma 1.9]
plays a central role. Let us denote

L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},(1+|v|^{2})dxdv)

Then for any pair of functions
(f_{1},f_{2}),(g_{1},g_{2})\in(L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{2}
, the regularized Maxwellian satisfies the following stability estimate:

(C.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})\left(\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}[(f_{1},f_{2})]-\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}[(g_{1},g_{2})]\right)dv\leq C_{\varepsilon,m_{i},\gamma_{i}}\left(|n_{f_{i}}^{(\varepsilon)}-n_{g_{i}}^{(\varepsilon)}|+|{\bf{u}}_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{(\varepsilon)}-{\bf{u}}_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(\varepsilon)}|\right).\end{split}

To make effective use of this estimate, we analyze the difference of the regularized velocities
{\bf{u}}_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{(\varepsilon)}
and
{\bf{u}}_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(\varepsilon)}
. A direct computation of the difference yields:

\displaystyle{\bf{u}}_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{(\varepsilon)}-{\bf{u}}_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}+\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}})\Big(\rho_{g_{1}}+\rho_{g_{2}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|)\Big)}{(\rho_{f_{1}}+\rho_{f_{2}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|)(\rho_{g_{1}}+\rho_{g_{2}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|))}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}+\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})(\rho_{f_{1}}+\rho_{f_{2}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|)}{(\rho_{f_{1}}+\rho_{f_{2}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|)(\rho_{g_{1}}+\rho_{g_{2}}+\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|))}.

Since both expressions share a common denominator, their difference reduces to the difference of the numerators. We group the resulting terms as follows:

\displaystyle J_{1}:=(\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}})\rho_{g_{1}}-(\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})\rho_{f_{1}},\quad J_{2}:=(\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}})\rho_{g_{2}}-(\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})\rho_{f_{2}},

\displaystyle J_{3}:=(\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}})\rho_{g_{1}}-(\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})\rho_{f_{1}},\quad J_{4}:=(\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}})\rho_{g_{2}}-(\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})\rho_{f_{2}},

\displaystyle J_{5}:=(\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}})\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|)-(\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|),

\displaystyle J_{6}:=(\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}})\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|)-(\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})\varepsilon(1+|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|).

Then, we make the following quick observations:

\bullet
Estimate of
J_{1}
: Subtracting and adding
(\rho_{g_{1}})^{2}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}
gives

\displaystyle J_{1}=(\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}-\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})\rho_{g_{1}}+(\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})(\rho_{g_{1}}-\rho_{f_{1}}).

\bullet
Estimate of
J_{2}
: Similarly, we find

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=(\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}-\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})\rho_{g_{2}}+(\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})(\rho_{g_{2}}-\rho_{f_{2}}).

\bullet
Estimates of
J_{3},J_{4}
: In the same way,

\displaystyle J_{3}=(\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}-\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})\rho_{g_{1}}+(\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})(\rho_{g_{1}}-\rho_{f_{1}}),

\displaystyle J_{4}=(\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}-\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})\rho_{g_{2}}+(\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})(\rho_{g_{2}}-\rho_{f_{2}}).

\bullet
Estimate of
J_{5}
: Subtracting and adding
\varepsilon(\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|)
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{5}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}-\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|)+\varepsilon(\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}})(|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|-|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|-|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}-\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|)+\varepsilon|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}||\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}-\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+\varepsilon|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}||\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}-\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|.

\bullet
Estimate of
J_{6}
: In the same way,

\displaystyle J_{6}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}-\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|)+\varepsilon(\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}})(|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|-|\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|-|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}-\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|(1+|\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|)+\varepsilon|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}||\rho_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}-\rho_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}|+\varepsilon|\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}||\rho_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}-\rho_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}|.

Now taking the denominator into effect, we deduce that

\begin{split}|{\bf{u}}_{f_{1},f_{2}}^{(\varepsilon)}-{\bf{u}}_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{(\varepsilon)}|&\lesssim_{m_{1},m_{2}}\frac{|n_{f_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}}-n_{g_{1}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{1}}|+|n_{f_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{2}}-n_{g_{2}}{\bf{u}}_{g_{2}}|}{\varepsilon}+\frac{|n_{f_{1}}-n_{g_{1}}|+|n_{f_{2}}-n_{g_{2}}|}{\varepsilon^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})\Big(|f_{1}-g_{1}|+|f_{2}-g_{2}|\Big)dv.\end{split}

Finally, we also control the difference in the regularized densities:

\displaystyle|n_{f_{i}}^{(\varepsilon)}-n_{g_{i}}^{(\varepsilon)}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{n_{f_{i}}-n_{g_{i}}}{(1+\varepsilon n_{f_{i}})(1+\varepsilon n_{g_{i}})}\right|\leq|n_{f_{i}}-n_{g_{i}}|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})|f_{i}-g_{i}|\,dv.

Combining the estimates above, we conclude from (
C.4
) that

(C.5)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})(\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}[f]-\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}[g])\,dvdx\cr&\quad\leq C_{\varepsilon,m_{1},m_{2},\gamma_{i}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})(|f_{1}-g_{1}|+|f_{2}-g_{2}|)\,dvdx.\end{split}

By using this stability estimate for the regularized Maxwellian, we now construct mild solutions to the regularized system (
C.3
) in the following lemma.

Lemma C.1
.

Assume (
C.1
). Then for each
\varepsilon>0
, the Cauchy problem for the regularized system (
C.3
) admits a mild solution. That is, for almost every
(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(C.6)

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)=e^{-t}\cdot{}^{\rm in}f_{i}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}](s,x-v(t-s),v)\,ds.

Moreover

\displaystyle(f_{1}^{\varepsilon},f_{2}^{\varepsilon})\in\prod_{i=1}^{2}L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}\cap L^{1+2/d_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})),

and the following uniform estimates hold:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{i}^{\varepsilon}+h_{i}(f_{i}^{\varepsilon},v)\,dvdx\leq C({}^{\rm in}f_{i}).

Proof.

We construct a sequence of approximations via the standard iterative scheme. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define
(f_{1}^{k+1},f_{2}^{k+1})
by solving the linearized problem:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f_{1}^{k+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}^{k+1}=\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{1}[f^{k}]-f_{1}^{k+1},\\
\partial_{t}f_{2}^{k+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}^{k+1}=\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{2}[f^{k}]-f_{2}^{k+1},\\
\mathcal{M}_{i}[f^{k}]:=c_{i}\left(\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}-1}m_{i}^{\frac{n}{2}(\gamma_{i}-1)}\left(n_{f_{i}^{k}}^{(\varepsilon)}\right)^{\gamma_{i}-1}-m_{i}\left|v-{\bf{u}}_{f_{1}^{k},f_{2}^{k}}^{(\varepsilon)}\right|^{2}\right)_{+}^{d_{i}/2},\\
f_{i}^{0}(t,x,v)={}^{\rm in}f_{i}(x,v)\quad\forall(t,x,v)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
f_{i}^{k}(0,x,v)={}^{\rm in}f_{i}(x,v)\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, this is a linear inhomogeneous transport equation with an explicitly given source term, and classical theory ensures the existence of a unique mild solution. In particular, each
f_{i}^{k}
satisfies the mild formula

\displaystyle f_{i}^{k+1}(t,x,v)=e^{-t}\cdot{}^{\rm in}f_{i}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{s-t}\mathcal{M}_{i}[f^{k}](s,x-v(t-s),v)\,ds.

Although the
f_{i}^{k+1}
also depends on the parameter
\varepsilon>0
, we have omitted this dependence for the sake of brevity. From conservation and entropy balance laws (see
[
18
,
38
]
), we obtain for each
i=1,2
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f_{1}^{k+1}+f_{2}^{k+1})\,dxdv+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f_{1}^{k+1}+f_{2}^{k+1})\,dxdv\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f_{1}^{k}+f_{2}^{k})\,dxdv,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i}^{k+1},v)\,dxdv+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i}^{k+1},v)dxdv\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i}^{k},v)\,dxdv.

By an inductive argument (see
[
38
, Lemma 3.3]
), this shows

(C.7)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\geq 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f_{1}^{k+1}+f_{2}^{k+1})\,dxdv\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}({}^{\rm in}f_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)+{}^{\rm in}f_{2}(\cdot,\cdot))\,dxdv,\\
&\sup_{t\geq 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i}^{k+1},v)\,dxdv\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}({}^{\rm in}f_{i}(\cdot,\cdot),v)\,dxdv.\end{split}

To establish convergence of the iteration, we consider the
L^{1}_{2}
-distance between successive iterates:

(C.8)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})|f^{k+1}_{i}-f^{k}_{i}|\,dxdv+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})|f^{k+1}_{i}-f^{k}_{i}|\,dxdv\\
&\quad=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})\textnormal{sgn}\left(f^{k+1}_{i}-f^{k}_{i})(\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}[f^{k}]-\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}[f^{k-1}]\right)dxdv,\quad i=1,2.\end{split}

Utilizing (
C.5
), we find that the right-hand side can be estimated as

(C.9)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})\textnormal{sgn}\left(f^{k+1}_{i}-f^{k}_{i})(\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}[f^{k}]-\mathcal{M}^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}[f^{k-1}]\right)dxdv\\
&\quad\leq C_{\varepsilon,m_{1},m_{2},\gamma_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|n_{f_{i}^{k}}^{(\varepsilon)}-n_{f_{i}^{k-1}}^{(\varepsilon)}\right|+\left|{\bf{u}}^{(\varepsilon)}_{f_{1}^{k},f_{2}^{k}}-{\bf{u}}^{(\varepsilon)}_{f_{1}^{k-1},f_{2}^{k-1}}\right|dx\\
&\quad\leq C_{\varepsilon,m_{1},m_{2},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|v|^{2})\Big(|f_{1}^{k}-f_{1}^{k-1}|+|f_{2}^{k}-f_{2}^{k-1}|\Big)\,dxdv.\end{split}

Thus, if we let
\mathcal{X}^{k+1}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i}^{k+1}-f_{i}^{k}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}
, then (
C.8
) and (
C.9
) imply

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{X}^{k+1}(t)+\mathcal{X}^{k+1}(t)\leq C_{\varepsilon,m_{1},m_{2},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}}\mathcal{X}^{k}(t).

Since
\mathcal{X}^{k}(0)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, this implies
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}^{k+1}(t)
is convergent. In particular, for both
i=1,2
, the sequences
\{f_{i}^{k+1}\}_{k}
are Cauchy in
L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}((0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Consequently, we obtain limits
f_{i}^{\varepsilon}
such that

\displaystyle f_{i}^{k+1}\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}f_{i}^{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}((0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})).

Moreover, since the mapping
f\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f]
is Lipschitz continuous in the
L^{1}_{2}
norm, the convergence also carries over to the Maxwellians:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{i}[f^{k+1}]\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{M}_{i}[f^{\varepsilon}]\quad\text{in}\quad L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}((0,\infty);L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})).

We can therefore pass to the limit in the mild formulas for the
(f_{1}^{k+1},f_{2}^{k+1})
, in the same way as in
[
38
, Proposition 3.1]
, and find that the limit
f_{i}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the mild formula (
C.6
). Finally, the uniform estimates established in (
C.7
) pass to the limit due to the lower semicontinuity of convex integrals with respect to
L^{1}
convergence. This completes the proof.
∎

C.2.
Passing to the limit

In order to obtain compactness for the sequences
\{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}
, we recall some useful lemmas.

Lemma C.2
.

[
38
, Lemma 3.4]
Let
\{f^{m}\}
a family of weak solutions to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{m}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{m}=G^{m},

\displaystyle f^{m}(0,\cdot,\cdot)={}^{\rm in}f(\cdot,\cdot),

where
G^{m}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G^{m}\,dv\leq 0.

Assume also that
\mathscr{E}:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{2}f^{m}\,dxdv<+\infty
. Then for any
\psi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
|\psi|\lesssim(1+|v|)^{\sigma}
and
\sigma\in[0,2)
, the function

\displaystyle\rho_{\psi}^{m}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(v)f^{m}\,dv

satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{m}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{|x|\geq 2R}|\rho_{\psi}^{m}|\,dx\leq\mathscr{E}^{\sigma/2}\left(\int_{|x|\geq R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{}^{\rm in}f(x,v)\,dvdx\right)^{1-\sigma/2}.

Notice that the above lemma applies to each
f_{i}^{\varepsilon}
with
G_{i}^{\varepsilon}:=\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-f_{i}^{\varepsilon}
, since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{i}^{\varepsilon}\,dv=\rho_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}-\rho_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\leq 0.

Next to recall is the following averaging lemma.

Lemma C.3
.

[
38
, Proposition 4.1]
Let
p\in(1,\infty)
, and
f^{m}
solutions to the transport equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f^{m}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{m}=G^{m}_{+}-G^{m}_{-},\\
f^{m}(0,\cdot,\cdot)={}^{\rm in}f(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}),\end{cases}

where
G^{m}_{+},G^{m}_{-}\geq 0
. Assume the following:

(i)

\{f^{m}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

(ii)

\{(1+|v|^{2})f^{m}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

(iii)

\{G^{m}_{+}\}
and
\{G^{m}_{-}\}
are bounded in
L_{\rm loc}^{1}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

(iv)

For each
T>0
, the family
\{\rho_{f^{m}}(t,\cdot)\}_{(m,t)\in\mathbb{N}\times[0,T]}
is tight:

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sup_{m}\int_{|x|\geq R}\rho_{f^{m}}\,dx=0.

Then, for each
\psi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
verifying
|\psi(v)|\lesssim(1+|v|)^{\sigma}
with
\sigma\in[0,2)
, the sequence
\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{m}\psi(v)\,dv\right\}
is relatively compact in
L_{\rm loc}^{1}([0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

C.3.
Proof of Theorem
C.1

We begin by observing that the uniform bounds on the kinetic entropy of the regularized solutions
f^{\varepsilon}=(f_{1}^{\varepsilon},f_{2}^{\varepsilon})
imply the existence of a limit function
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
such that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup f_{i}\quad\text{weakly${}^{*}$ in }L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{1+\frac{2}{d_{i}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})).

A direct application of Lemma
C.3
shows

n_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\to n_{f_{i}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad n_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\to n_{f_{i}}{\bf{u}}_{f_{i}}

a.e. in
(t,x)
and strongly in
L^{p_{i}}((0,T);L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for any
p_{i}\in[1,\frac{2\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{i}+1})
and all
T>0
. Recalling the definitions of the regularized quantities
n^{(\varepsilon)}
,
{\bf{u}}^{(\varepsilon)}
, and the truncated Maxwellians
\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}
, we deduce from the strong convergence of the moments that

\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\to\mathcal{M}_{i}[f]\quad\text{a.e. in }P_{t,x}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

where

P_{t,x}:=\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:(n_{f_{1}}+n_{f_{2}})(t,x)>0\}.

On the complementary set
(([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus P_{t,x})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can directly estimate the convergence in
L^{1}
using the fact that both Maxwellians vanish when densities vanish. Indeed,

\displaystyle\iiint_{(([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus P_{t,x})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]-\mathcal{M}_{i}[f]\right|\,dxdvdt

\displaystyle\leq\iiint_{(([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus P_{t,x})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]+\mathcal{M}_{i}[f]\,dxdvdt

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus P_{t,x}}n_{f_{i}}^{(\varepsilon)}+n_{f_{i}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus P_{t,x}}2n_{f_{i}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=0,

where we used the definition of
P_{t,x}
and the monotonicity of the regularization
n_{f_{i}}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq n_{f_{i}}
.

Thus, combining the two regions, we conclude that up to a subsequence

\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\to\mathcal{M}_{i}[f]\quad\mbox{a.e. in }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

for any
T>0
. A diagonal argument permits passing to a further subsequence such that

\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\to\mathcal{M}_{i}[f]\quad\mbox{a.e. in }[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

As a result of this pointwise convergence and the bounds

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\,dxdv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{m_{i}}{2}n_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}|{\bf{u}}_{f^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)}|^{2}+\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n(\gamma_{i}-1)}{2}}}{\gamma_{i}-1}(n_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}^{(\varepsilon)})^{\gamma_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{m_{i}}{2}n_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}}|{\bf{u}}_{f^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}+\frac{m_{i}^{\frac{n(\gamma_{i}-1)}{2}}}{\gamma_{i}-1}(n_{f_{i}^{\varepsilon}})^{\gamma_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(\mathcal{M}_{i}[f^{\varepsilon}],v)\,dxdv

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}(f_{i}^{\varepsilon},v)\,dxdv

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{i}({}^{\rm in}f_{i}^{\varepsilon},v)\,dxdv,

we deduce from the Vitali convergence theorem that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{i}^{(\varepsilon)}[f^{\varepsilon}]\to\mathcal{M}_{i}[f]\quad\text{in}\quad L^{1}_{\rm loc}((0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})).

With this strong convergence in hand, we may pass to the limit in the mild formulation of the regularized problem, see
[
38
, Lemma 4.3]
for details. That is, for almost every
(t,x,v)
,

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\varepsilon}\to f_{i}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{1}_{\rm loc}((0,\infty);L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})),

\displaystyle f_{i}(t,x,v)=e^{-t}\cdot{}^{\rm in}f_{i}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-t+s}\mathcal{M}_{i}[f](s,x-v(t-s),v)\,ds.

Hence, we obtain a global-in-time mild solution to the original BGK-type system (
1.1
).

It remains to verify the additional properties listed in (
C.2
), including the kinetic entropy inequality and moment bounds. Since the derivation of these estimates follows the same line of reasoning as in
[
38
, Sections 4–5]
, we omit the details and refer the reader to that work for a complete analysis.

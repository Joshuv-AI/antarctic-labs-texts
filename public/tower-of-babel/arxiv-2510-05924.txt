2.1.
Anisotropic dilations on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}

Firstly, we recall the definition of expansive dilations
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}
, which is introduced by Bownik (see
[
1
, p. 5]
). A real
d\times d
matrix
A
is called an

(shortly a

), if

\min_{{\lambda}\in{\sigma}(A)}|{\lambda}|>1
, where
{\sigma}(A)
denotes the set of
all
eigenvalues
of
A
. Let
{\lambda}_{-}
and
{\lambda}_{+}
be two
positive numbers
such that

1<{\lambda}_{-}<\min\{|{\lambda}|:\ {\lambda}\in{\sigma}(A)\}\leq\max\{|{\lambda}|:\ {\lambda}\in{\sigma}(A)\}<{\lambda}_{+}.

From
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
, we know that, for a fixed dilation
A
,
there exist a number
r\in(1,\,\infty)
and a set
\Delta:=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}:\,|Px|<1\}
, where
P
is some non-degenerate
d\times d
matrix, such that
\Delta\subset r\Delta\subset A\Delta,
and one can additionally
assume that
|\Delta|=1
, where
|\Delta|
denotes the

d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the set
\Delta
. For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let

B_{k}:=A^{k}\Delta
. Then
B_{k}
is open, and

B_{k}\subset rB_{k}\subset B_{k+1},\ \ |B_{k}|=|\det A|^{k}.

An ellipsoid
x+B_{k}
for some
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
dilated ball
.
Define

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}:=\left\{x+B_{k}:\ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}.

Throughout the whole paper, let
\sigma
be the
smallest integer
such that
2B_{0}\subset A^{\sigma}B_{0}
. Then,
for all
k,\,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
k\leq j
, it holds true that

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{k}+B_{j}\subset B_{j+{\sigma}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle B_{k}+(B_{k+{\sigma}})^{\complement}\subset(B_{k})^{\complement},

where
E+F
denotes the
algebraic sum

\{x+y:\ x\in E,\,y\in F\}

of sets
E,\,F\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A quasi-norm, associated with
dilation
A
, is a Borel measurable mapping

\rho_{A}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, for simplicity, denoted by

\rho
, satisfying

(i)

\rho(x)>0
for all

,
here and hereafter,
\mathbf{0}
denotes the origin of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}
;

(ii)

\rho(Ax)=|\det A|\rho(x)
for any

;

(iii)

\rho(x+y)\leq C_{A}\left[\rho(x)+\rho(y)\right]
for
all
x,\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
C_{A}\in[1,\,\infty)
is a constant independent of
x
and
y
.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

In the standard dyadic case

,
\rho(x):=|x|^{d}
for

is
an example of homogeneous quasi-norms associated with
A
, here and hereafter,

denotes the
d\times d

unit matrix
,

|\cdot|
always denotes the

in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}
.

(ii)

From
[
1

, we find that, there exists a positive constant

such that, for all

,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}[\rho(x)]^{\zeta_{-}}\leq|x|\leq C[\rho(x)]^{\zeta_{+}},\ \ \mathrm{if}\ \ \rho(x)\geq 1,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}[\rho(x)]^{\zeta_{+}}\leq|x|\leq C[\rho(x)]^{\zeta_{-}},\ \ \mathrm{if}\ \ \rho(x)<1,

where

and

.

(iii)

Bownik has obtained that, all homogeneous quasi-norms associated with a given dilation

A
are equivalent, see
[

. Therefore, in what follows, for a
given dilation
A
, for simplicity, we
always use the
step homogeneous quasi-norm

\rho
defined by setting, for all

\rho(x):=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{k}\mathbf{1}_{B_{k+1}\setminus B_{k}}(x)\ {\rm if}\ x\neq\mathbf{0},\hskip 9.38945pt{\mathrm{or\ else}\hskip 9.38945pt}\rho(\mathbf{0}):=0.

By (

\rho(x+y)\leq|\det A|^{\sigma}\left(\max\left\{\rho(x),\,\rho(y)\right\}\right)\leq|\det A|^{\sigma}[\rho(x)+\rho(y)].

2.2.
Matrix
A_{p}
weight

In this subsection, we will recall the definitions of anisotropic matrix
A_{p}
weight.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be the cone of nonnegative
definite operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of dimension
n
(consider
H=\mathbb{C}^{n}
or

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
), i.e., for
M\in{\mathcal{M}}
we have
\langle Mx,x\rangle_{H}\geq 0
, for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, a
matrix weight
\mathbf{W}
is an a.e. invertible map
\mathbf{W}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}
.
Define the matrix-weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
as all measurable vector-valued function
\vec{f}:=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m})^{T}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
satisfy

\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Since the
A_{p}
condition can be formulated for any family of norms
\omega_{t}
on a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we will recall the following norms:

\omega_{t}(y)=\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)y\right\|_{\mathcal{H}},\ \mathrm{where}\ y\in\mathcal{H},x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Its dual metric is defined by

\omega_{t}^{*}(x)=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{H},z\neq 0}\frac{\langle x,z\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{\omega_{t}(z)}=\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)y\right\|_{\mathcal{H}},\ \mathrm{where}\ y\in\mathcal{H},x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Define the norm
\omega_{p,B}
through the averagings of the metrics

\omega_{t}
on the ball
B\in\mathfrak{B}
:

\omega_{p,B}(x)=\left[\fint_{B}\left(\omega_{t}(x)\right)^{p}\,dt\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Its dual metric is defined by

\omega^{*}_{p^{\prime},B}(x)=\left[\fint_{B}\left(\omega_{t}^{*}(x)\right)^{p^{\prime}}\,dt\right]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

The following is the definition of
A_{p}
matrix weight, which is from .

Definition 2.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. We say that
\mathbf{W}
is an
A_{p}
matrix weight if

\mathbf{W}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}
is such that

\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{W}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}
are locally integrable and there exists
C<0
such that

\omega^{*}_{p^{\prime},B}\leq C(\omega_{p,B})^{*}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. We say that matrix weight
\mathbf{W}
is a doubling matrix of order
p
, if
there exists
C_{p,d}
such that, for any
y\in\mathcal{H}
, and
B\in\mathfrak{B}
,

\int_{2B}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)y\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}\,dx\leq C_{p,d}\int_{B}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)y\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}\,dx.

The following are some equivalent definitions of matrix
A_{p}
weight in the anisotropic setting, whose proof is similar to Roudenko’s ones
[
27
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

The following condition are equivalllent:

(i)

\mathbf{W}\in A_{p}
;

(ii)

\mathbf{W}^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\in A_{p}
;

(iii)

\fint_{B}\left[\fint_{B}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\mathbf{W}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p^{\prime}}dy\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}dx\leq C
, for any
B\in\mathfrak{B}
;

(iii)

\fint_{B}\left[\fint_{B}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\mathbf{W}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p}dy\right]^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dx\leq C
, for any
B\in\mathfrak{B}
,

where
\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\mathbf{W}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\|
denotes the matrix norm.

2.3.
Matrix-weighted Besov spaces associated with anisotropic dilations

In this subsection, we will introduce the definitions of anisotropic matrix-weighted Besov spaces.

A
C^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}})
function
\varphi
is said to belong to the Schwartz class
{\mathcal{S}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}})
if,
for any
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and multi-index
\alpha
,

\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\ell}:={\sup}_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}}[\rho(x)]^{\ell}|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|<\infty
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and satisfy

\displaystyle{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}\hat{\varphi}\subset[-\pi,\pi]^{d}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}

and

\displaystyle{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}\left|\hat{\varphi}((A^{*})^{-k}\xi)\right|>0,\ \ \mathrm{for\ any}\ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}.

In what follows, for
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}})
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}
, let

\varphi_{k}(x):=|\det A|^{-k}\varphi(A^{-k}x).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty]
and
\mathbf{W}
be a matrix weight.
The Besov space
\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
is defined by, all vector-valued distributions

\vec{f}:=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m})^{T}
with
f_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, such that

\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}:=\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{k\alpha q}\left\|\vec{f}*\varphi_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{W})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
\vec{f}*\varphi_{k}:=(f_{1}*\varphi_{k},f_{2}*\varphi_{k},\ldots,f_{m}*\varphi_{k})^{T}
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
and satisfy

\displaystyle{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}\hat{\varphi},{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}\hat{\psi}\subset[-\pi,\pi]^{d}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\overline{\hat{\varphi}((A^{*})^{-k}\xi)}\hat{\psi}((A^{*})^{-k}\xi)=1,\ \ \mathrm{for\ any}\ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}.

For any
\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define

Q_{\mathbf{j},k}=A^{-k}([0,1]^{d}+\mathbf{j})

be the dilated cube,
x_{Q_{\mathbf{j},k}}=A^{-k}\mathbf{j}
.

\mathcal{Q}=\left\{Q_{\mathbf{j},k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\right\}.

Define

\varphi_{Q}=|\det A|^{-\frac{k}{2}}\varphi(A^{k}x-\mathbf{j})=|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi(x-x_{Q})
.

For any
\vec{f}
,
f_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, define
\varphi
-transform
S_{\varphi}
:

S_{\varphi}(\vec{f})=\left\{\langle\vec{f},\varphi_{Q}\rangle\right\}_{Q}=\left\{\left(\langle f_{1},\varphi_{Q}\rangle,\langle f_{2},\varphi_{Q}\rangle,\ldots,\langle f_{m},\varphi_{Q}\rangle\right)^{T}\right\}_{Q}.

Moreover, we call
\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f})=\langle\vec{f},\varphi_{Q}\rangle
the
\varphi
-transform
coefficients of
\vec{f}
.

Similarly to
\varphi_{Q}
, define

\psi_{Q}=|\det A|^{-\frac{k}{2}}\psi(A^{k}x-\mathbf{j})=|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi(x-x_{Q}).

The inverse
\varphi
-transform
T_{\psi}
is the map taking a sequence
s=\{s_{Q}\}
to

T_{\psi}s=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}s_{Q}\psi_{Q}
. In the vector case,

T_{\psi}\vec{s}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\vec{s}_{Q}\psi_{Q},

where
\vec{s}_{Q}\psi_{Q}=(s_{Q}^{1}\psi_{Q},s_{Q}^{2}\psi_{Q},\ldots,s_{Q}^{m}\psi_{Q})^{T}
. The
\varphi
-transform
decomposition show that, for any
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\backslash\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

f=\sum_{Q}\langle f,\varphi_{Q}\rangle\psi_{Q}=:\sum_{Q}s_{Q}\psi_{Q}.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty]
and
\mathbf{W}
be a matrix weight.
The Besov space
\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
is defined by, all vector-valued sequence

\vec{s}:=\{\vec{s}_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}
, where
\vec{s}_{Q}=(s_{Q}^{1},s_{Q}^{2},\ldots,s_{Q}^{m})^{T}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, such that

\|\vec{s}\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}:=\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{k\alpha q}\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\vec{s}_{Q}|\mathbf{1}_{Q}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{W})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Remark 2.8
.

(i)

In the isotropic setting, i.e.,
when
A=2\mathrm{I}_{d\times d}
, these spaces reduce to the weighted-matrix Besov space
\dot{B}^{\alpha,q}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})

k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then we have

f*g(x)=\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\det A|^{-k}f(A^{-k}\mathbf{j})g(x-A^{-k}\mathbf{j}).

The following is maximal operator type inequality.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\mathbf{W}\in A_{p}
and
\vec{f}\in E_{0}
. Then we have

\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(\mathbf{j})\right\|^{p}dx\leq C_{p,d}\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{W})}.

Proof.

Choose a smooth function
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}
satisfying:
if
\xi\in B_{1}
,
\hat{\phi}=1
, and
{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}\hat{\phi}\subseteq[-\pi,\pi]^{d}
.
Then for any
\vec{f}\in E_{0}
, we have
\vec{f}=\vec{f}*\phi
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(\mathbf{j})\right\|^{p}dx=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\vec{f}(y)\phi(\mathbf{j}-y)\,dy\right\|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(y)\frac{1}{[\rho(1+(\mathbf{j}-y))]^{N}}\right\|\,dy\right]^{p}dx,

where
N>d+\frac{\beta p}{p^{\prime}}
,
\beta
is the doubling exponent of matrix weight
\mathbf{W}
.

By the discrete Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(y)\frac{1}{[\rho(1+(\mathbf{j}-y))]^{N}}\right\|\,dy\right]^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(y)\frac{1}{[\rho(1+(\mathbf{j}-y))]^{N}}\right\|\,dy\right]^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\left[\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(y)\right\|\,dy\right]^{p}\frac{1}{[\rho(1+(\mathbf{j}-y))]^{N}}\cdot\left(\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\frac{1}{[\rho(1+(\mathbf{j}-y))]^{N}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\left[\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(y)\right\|\,dy\right]^{p}\frac{1}{[\rho(1+(\mathbf{j}-y))]^{N}}dx.

It follows from the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\left[\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(y)\right\|\,dy\right]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\mathbf{W}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\vec{f}(y)\right\|\,dy\right]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\mathbf{W}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p^{\prime}}\,dy\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\cdot\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\vec{f}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy.

Now we can prove that

\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\left[\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\mathbf{W}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(y)\right\|^{p^{\prime}}\,dy\right]^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{[\rho(1+(\mathbf{j}-y))]^{N}}dx\lesssim 1.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(\mathbf{j})\right\|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\vec{f}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\thicksim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(y)\vec{f}(y)\right\|^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\thicksim\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{W})}^{p}.

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a doubling matrix of order
p
with doubling exponent
\beta<p
, and
\vec{f}\in E_{0}
. Then we have

\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{f}(\mathbf{j})\right\|^{p}dx\leq C_{p,d}\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{W})}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
q\in(0,\infty]
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\mathbf{W}\in A_{p}
.
Then for any
f\in\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
, we have

\left\|\{\vec{s}_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}\lesssim\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})},

where
\vec{s}_{Q}=S_{\varphi}(\vec{f})=\langle\vec{f},\varphi_{Q}\rangle=\left(\langle f_{1},\varphi_{Q}\rangle,\langle f_{2},\varphi_{Q}\rangle,\ldots,\langle f_{m},\varphi_{Q}\rangle\right)^{T}.

Proof.

By the definition, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\{\vec{s}_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{k\alpha q}\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\vec{s}_{Q}|\mathbf{1}_{Q}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{W})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{k\alpha q}\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{s}_{Q}\right\|\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx)}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let
Q_{\mathbf{j},k}=A^{-k}([0,1]^{d}+\mathbf{j})
,
\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Then we have

\vec{s}_{Q}=\left\langle\vec{f},\varphi_{Q}\right\rangle=|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\vec{f}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\right)(A^{-k}\mathbf{j})

and

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q||\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{s}_{Q}\right\|\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx)}^{p}=

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{p}{2}}\int_{Q}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\vec{f}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{k})(A^{-k}\mathbf{j})\right\|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},k}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)(\vec{f}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{k})(A^{-k}\mathbf{j})\right\|^{p}\,dx.

Set
\vec{f}_{k}(x):=\vec{f}(A^{-k}x)
and
\mathbf{W}_{k}(x):=\mathbf{W}(A^{k}x)
, then
\vec{f}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}(A^{-k}\mathbf{j})=\vec{f}_{k}*\widetilde{\varphi}(\mathbf{j})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{s}_{Q}\right\|\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx)}^{p}=

\displaystyle|\det A|^{-k}\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\int_{Q_{\mathbf{j},0}}\left\|\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)(\vec{f}_{k}*\widetilde{\varphi})(\mathbf{j})\right\|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\det A|^{-k}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)(\vec{f}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{k})(x)\right\|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\thicksim

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)(\vec{f}_{k}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{k})(x)\right\|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\thicksim

\displaystyle\left\|\vec{f}_{k}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{W})}^{p}.

From the above estimates, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|\{\vec{s}_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{k\alpha q}\left\|\vec{f}_{k}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{W})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\thicksim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}.

Finally, we only need to show the equivalence of
\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W},\varphi)=\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W},\widetilde{\varphi})
.
It is easy to see that
\hat{\widetilde{\varphi}}=\overline{\hat{\varphi}}
and
\hat{\widetilde{\psi}}=\overline{\hat{\psi}}
. Therefore, we know that

(\widetilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\psi})
satisfies (
4.6
).
From the decomposition of

\vec{f}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left\langle\vec{f},\widetilde{\varphi}_{Q}\right\rangle\widetilde{\psi}_{Q},

the fact that
\{\widetilde{\psi}_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}
is a family of smooth molecules for
\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
, and Lemma
3.2
, we conclude that

\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W},\widetilde{\varphi})}\lesssim\left\|\left\{\left\langle\vec{f},\widetilde{\varphi}_{Q}\right\rangle\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}.

By this and the above estimate, we obtain

\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W},\widetilde{\varphi})}\lesssim\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W},\widetilde{\widetilde{\varphi}})}\thicksim\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W},\varphi)}.

Therefore, we have

\left\|\left\{\left\langle\vec{f},\widetilde{\varphi}_{Q}\right\rangle\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}=\left\|\left\{\vec{s}_{Q}\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}\lesssim\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W},\varphi)}.

This completes our proof.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

,

4.6
),

,

3.3.
The relationship with reducing operators

In this subsection, we will establish the relation of Besov spaces and reducing operators.

Recall that, for any matrix weight
\mathbf{W}
, we know that there exist a sequence of
reducing operators
\{\mathcal{A}_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}
such that, for any
\vec{u}\in\mathcal{H}
,

\omega_{p,Q}=\left[\fint_{Q}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{u}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\,dx\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\{\mathcal{A}_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}
be the reducing operators for
\mathbf{W}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
q\in(0,\infty]
,
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Then we have

\left\|\{\vec{s}_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}=\left\|\{\vec{s}_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\{\mathcal{A}_{Q}\})}.

Proof.

By the definition, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\{\vec{s}_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{kq\alpha}\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)\vec{s}_{Q}\right\|\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx)}^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{kq\alpha}\left[\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\omega_{p,Q}(\vec{s}_{Q})\right)^{p}\right]^{\frac{q}{p}}\right]^{1q}

\displaystyle\thicksim

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{kq\alpha}\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{p}{2}}\left\|\mathcal{A}_{Q}\vec{s}_{Q}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}\int_{Q}\mathbf{1}(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right]^{1q}

\displaystyle\thicksim

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\det A|^{kq\alpha}\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathcal{A}_{Q}\vec{s}_{Q}\right\|\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx)}^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\thicksim

\displaystyle\left\|\{\vec{s}_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\{\mathcal{A}_{Q}\})}.

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
q\in(0,\infty]
,
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\mathbf{W}\in A_{p}
. Then the Besov space

\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
is complete.

Proof.

Let
\{\vec{f}_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
. From the above lemma, we know that

\{\{\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})\}_{Q}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\{\mathcal{A}_{Q}\})
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}|Q|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\mathcal{A}_{Q}\left(\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})-\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{l})\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(dx)}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\det A|^{(\frac{p}{2}-1)k}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}\left\|\mathcal{A}_{Q}\left(\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})-\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{l})\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,\ \ \mathrm{as}\ \ i,l\rightarrow\infty,

which implies that, for any
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
,

\left\|\mathcal{A}_{Q}\left(\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})-\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{l})\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}\rightarrow 0,\ \ \mathrm{as}\ \ i,l\rightarrow\infty.

By the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
is invertible, we obtain that, for any
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
,
\{\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a vector-valued Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{H}
.
Furthermore, we define
\vec{s}_{Q}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})
.
Let

\vec{f}=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\vec{s}_{Q}\psi_{Q}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\vec{f}_{i}-\vec{f}\,\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left(\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})-\vec{s}_{Q}\right)\psi_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})-\vec{s}_{Q}\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\liminf_{l\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\left\{\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})-\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{l})\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\right\|_{\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\{\mathcal{A}_{Q}\})}\rightarrow 0,\ \ \mathrm{as}\ \ l\rightarrow\infty.

From this, the Fatou Lemma and the fact that
\{\{\vec{s}_{Q}(\vec{f}_{i})\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a Cauchy sequence in

\dot{b}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\{\mathcal{A}_{Q}\})
, we deduce that

\vec{f}=(\vec{f}+\vec{f}_{i})-\vec{f}_{i}\in\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W}).

This implies that the Besov space
\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
is complete.

∎

,

.

,

is
defined for the vector sequences enumerated by the dyadic cubes
Q
with
|Q|\leq 1
.

.

,

and
\mathbf{W}

the
\ell^{q}
-norm can be replaced by the supremum on

.

Next we also introduce the definitions of
\varphi
-transform
S_{\varphi}
and the inverse
\varphi
-transform

in the inhomogeneous setting.

Let
\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathcal{S}}
,

,

.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathcal{S}}
, and
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}
satisfy (
4.6
).
For any vector-valued distribute
\vec{f}
,

, define the inhomogeneous

\varphi
-transform

:

S_{\varphi}(\vec{f}):=\{S_{\varphi}(\vec{f})\}_{Q},

if
k>0
,

\{S_{\varphi}(\vec{f})\}_{Q}=\left\{\langle\vec{f},\varphi_{Q}\rangle\right\}_{Q}=\left\{\left(\langle f_{1},\varphi_{Q}\rangle,\langle f_{2},\varphi_{Q}\rangle,\ldots,\langle f_{m},\varphi_{Q}\rangle\right)^{T}\right\}_{Q};

if
k=0
,

\{S_{\varphi}(\vec{f})\}_{Q}=\left\{\langle\vec{f},\Phi_{Q}\rangle\right\}_{Q}=\left\{\left(\langle f_{1},\Phi_{Q}\rangle,\langle f_{2},\Phi_{Q}\rangle,\ldots,\langle f_{m},\Phi_{Q}\rangle\right)^{T}\right\}_{Q};

For any vector-valued sequence
\vec{s}=\{s_{Q}\}_{Q,|Q|\leq 0}
, define the inhomogeneous inverse

\varphi
-transform
T_{\psi}=T_{\Psi,\psi}
:

T_{\psi}(\vec{s}):=\sum_{\mathcal{Q},|Q|=1}\vec{s}_{Q}\Psi_{Q}+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|<1}\vec{s}_{Q}\Psi_{Q},

Given
\Phi,\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
satisfying (
4.6
), we can show that there exist
\Psi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}
such that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\overline{\hat{\Phi}(\xi)}\hat{\Psi}(\xi)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\overline{\hat{\varphi}((A^{*})^{-k}\xi)}\hat{\psi}((A^{*})^{-k}\xi)=1,\ \ \mathrm{for\ any}\ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Moreover, we have the following identity for vector-valued distribution
\vec{f}
,
f_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},

\vec{f}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=1}\left\langle\vec{f},\Phi_{Q}\right\rangle\Psi_{Q}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q},|Q|=|\det A|^{-k}}\left\langle\vec{f},\varphi_{Q}\right\rangle\Psi_{Q},

with convergence in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
.

We can then show that main results of
\varphi
-transform adapt directly to inhomogeneous setting.
That is,
S_{\varphi}
is a bounded operator from
B^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
to

b^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\mathbf{W})
and
T_{\psi}
is a bounded operator
from
b^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\mathbf{W})
to
B^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
.

5.
Almost diagonal operators

In this section, we study a class of almost diagonal operators on Besov spaces, which
was introduced in the dyadic case by Frazier et al.
[
19
]
. The interest of these operators comes from their close
connection to operators on Besov spaces.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p,q\in(0,\infty]
.
Suppose that
J=\beta/p+\max\{0,1-1/p\}
. We say
that a matrix
\{a_{Q,P}\}_{Q,P\in\mathcal{Q}}
is almost diagonal,

A\in\mathbf{ad}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\beta),
if there exists an
c>0
such that,

\sup_{Q,P\in\mathcal{Q}}\left|a_{Q,P}\right|<c\left(\frac{|Q|}{|P|}\right)^{\alpha}\left[1+\frac{\rho_{A}(x_{Q}-x_{P})}{\max\{|P|,\,|Q|\}}\right]^{-J-c}\min\left\{\left(\frac{|Q|}{|P|}\right)^{\frac{1+c}{2}},\,\left(\frac{|P|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{1+c}{2}+J-1}\right\}.

The following conclusion demonstrate that we study some property of operators on Besov space
\dot{B}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
by considering corresponding operators on
\dot{b}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\mathbf{W})
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and let
\mathbf{W}
be a doubling
matrix of order
p
with doubling exponent
\beta
. If
A\in\mathbf{ad}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\beta)
,
then
A:\dot{b}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\mathbf{W})\rightarrow\dot{b}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\mathbf{W})
is bounded.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
T
be a continuous linear operator from
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
to
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. We say that

T
is an almost diagonal operator for

B^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
, and write
T\in\mathbf{AD}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\beta)
, if for some
pair of mutually admissible kernels
(\varphi,\psi)
, the matrix
\{a_{Q,P}\}\in\mathbf{ad}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\beta)
, where

a_{Q,P}=\left\langle T\psi_{P},\varphi_{Q}\right\rangle.

Remark 5.3
.

It is easy to see that,
the definition of
T\in\mathbf{AD}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\beta)
is independent of the choice of the
pair
(\varphi,\psi)
.

As a straightforward consequence of Theorem
5.1
, we can deduce the following conclusion:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
T\in\mathbf{AD}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\beta)
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty)
. Then
T
extends
to a bounded operator on
\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})
if
\mathbf{W}\in A_{p}.

Proof.

By the density, we need to consider
\vec{f}
with
f_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Let
(\varphi,\psi)
be a pair of mutually admissible
kernels.
Let

\vec{t}_{Q}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Q}}\langle T\psi_{P},\,\varphi_{Q}\rangle\vec{s}_{P}(\vec{f}).

Notice that
\{\langle T\psi_{P},\varphi_{Q}\rangle\}_{Q,P}\in\mathbf{ad}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\beta)
. From the
\varphi
-transform decomposition

\vec{f}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Q}}\vec{s}_{P}(\vec{f})\psi_{P},

and Theorem
5.1
,
we can deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|T(\vec{f})\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Q}}\vec{s}_{P}(\vec{f})T\psi_{P}\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Q}}\left\langle T\psi_{P},\varphi_{Q}\right\rangle\vec{s}_{P}(\vec{f})\right)\psi_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\vec{t}_{Q}\psi_{Q}\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathbf{W})}\lesssim\left\|\left\{\vec{t}_{Q}\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\right\|_{\dot{b}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\mathbf{W})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\vec{s}_{Q}\right\}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\right\|_{\dot{b}^{\alpha,q}_{p,A}(\mathbf{W})}\lesssim\left\|\vec{f}\,\right\|_{\dot{B}_{p,A}^{\alpha,q}(\mathbf{W})}.

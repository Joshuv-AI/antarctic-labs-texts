Higher levels of neutrophil extracellular traps in mucoid middle ear effusion obtained from otitis media with effusion in children
Authors: Lining Guo, Tiewei Li, Qingchuan Duan, Yongtao Duan, Wei Liu, Ning Ma, Shanshan Liu, Xiaoxu Wang, Shengnan Zhang, Enxia Tian, Xin Ni, Jie Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe etiology of otitis media with effusion (OME) is multifactorial and complex, with increasing evidence implicating bacterial infection as a potential contributor. Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) have been identified as key participants in the pathophysiological processes of various infectious diseases. However, to date, no studies have quantitatively assessed NETs in OME or explored its role in the middle ear.ObjectiveThis study aims to quantitatively assess NETs and mucins in different types of middle ear effusion (MEE) in children with OME, investigate the correlations between NETs, mucins, and clinical phenotypes, and explore the potential role of NETs in OME.MethodsWe included fifty-five pediatric OME patients underwent tympanostomy tube insertion (March–November 2024). Seventy MEE specimens were collected for detection and analysis. General demographics, disease duration, hearing thresholds and blood cell levels were documented. All samples were characterized into two main types (serous and mucoid) by the same surgeon based on their appearance and viscosity. NET-related biomarkers (citrullinated histone H3 [CitH3], double-stranded DNA [dsDNA], myeloperoxidase-DNA complex [MPO-DNA], and Nucleosome), along with mucins (mucin5AC [MUC5AC] and mucin5B [MUC5B]) were detected using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), modified ELISA or fluorometry.ResultsSubjects in the mucoid group demonstrated more severe hearing impairment than those in the serous group (p = 0.015). Compared to the serous group, specimens from the mucoid group exhibited higher levels of CitH3, dsDNA, MPO-DNA, MUC5B and MUC5AC (p < 0.05). Within the hearing severity subgroups, CitH3, dsDNA, and MUC5B levels were higher in patients with poorer hearing. Correlation analysis revealed strong positive correlations between NET-related biomarkers with MUC5B and MUC5AC expression (p < 0.05). CitH3 (r = 0.306, p = 0.010), dsDNA (r = 0.135, p = 0.049), and MUC5B (r = 0.242, p = 0.043) were positively correlated with patients’ hearing levels.ConclusionsNET-related biomarkers were significantly increased in mucoid effusions with a notable positive correlation to MUC5AC, MUC5B, and hearing levels.Keywords: Neutrophil extracellular traps, mucin, otitis media with effusion, infection, children

Introduction

Otitis media with effusion (OME) is a common pediatric condition characterized by fluid accumulation in the middle ear without signs of acute infection. It is characterized by cellular alterations and mucosal hyperplasia in the middle ear, leading to the formation of serous and mucoid effusions, which can adversely affect speech development [1]. The etiology of OME remains complex and incompletely understood. Existing treatment options, including tympanostomy tube insertion and pharmacological interventions, often demonstrate limited efficacy and fail to address the underlying pathogenic mechanisms, underscoring the need for further exploration of OME’s etiology and mechanisms. Recent advancements in microbial detection within the middle ear cavity have further revealed that OME is not aseptic; bacterial involvement in its pathogenesis is well-documented in the literature [2–4]. The most common pathogens associated with middle ear infections are A. otitidis, S. pneumoniae, and H. influenzae [3]. However, the specific mechanisms by which these pathogens contribute to OME and whether they further disrupt eustachian tube function remain unclear.

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are DNA-based, web-like structures released by neutrophils during infections to capture and eliminate pathogens [5]. However, excessive, or dysregulated release of NETs can exacerbate inflammation, cause tissue damage, and contribute to chronic diseases [6]. Previous research has identified neutrophils as the predominant immune cells present in middle ear effusions (MEE) [7]. Immunofluorescence staining has confirmed the presence of NETs in the middle ear during acute otitis media, where they participate in bacterial biofilm formation, potentially increasing the risk of chronic disease [8,9]. Proteomic analysis of MEEs indicates that NETs are the primary macromolecular constituent in chronic otitis media [10]. These findings suggest a potential role for NETs in OME caused by pathogen infection. To provide mechanistic insight into this process, we selected a panel of four distinct NET biomarkers: Citrullinated histone H3 (CitH3), Double-stranded DNA (dsDNA), Myeloperoxidase-DNA complexes (MPO-DNA), and Nucleosome, each representing a critical molecular or structural component of the NETs pathway. Mucins, particularly mucin5AC (MUC5AC) and mucin5B (MUC5B), form a mucus layer that assist in clearing pathogens and cellular debris from the middle ear. However, excessive mucin secretion can lead to fluid accumulation, heightened inflammation, increased viscosity, and exacerbated ear pressure and hearing loss [11]. Chronic OME is typically characterized by viscous MEE and a low bacterial load [12,13]. NETs have been identified within the middle ear environment, suggesting a possible interaction with mucins. However, comprehensive data comparing the quantitative analysis of NETs and mucins across different types of MEE and their correlations are limited.

To address these gaps, this study aims to quantitatively evaluate the overall NETs level and mucins in different types of MEE in children with OME. It will also investigate the relationships among NETs, mucins, and clinical phenotypes to explore the potential pathological roles of these biomarkers in OME.

Materials and methods

Study population

This prospective study encompassed children scheduled for tympanostomy due to OME at Beijing Children’s Hospital and Henan Children’s Hospital between March and November 2024. Inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: (1) age between 0 and 18 years; (2) diagnosis of OME [1] confirmed by otoscopic examination, tympanometry, auditory assessments, and meeting the surgical requirements for tympanostomy tube placement. Exclusion criteria included: (1) syndromes associated with cranial bone deformities; (2) conductive hearing loss from other causes (e.g. ossicular chain discontinuity, anatomical anomalies, otosclerosis); (3) systemic conditions such as ciliary dysfunction or granulomatous polyangiitis; (4) recent respiratory infections or antibiotic treatment within the preceding month; and (5) incomplete or missing clinical data. Following these criteria, 55 pediatric patients were included, and MEE samples were obtained.

The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Beijing Children’s Hospital (Approval No: [2024]-Y-234-D). All participants’ parents or guardians in this study have signed written informed consent.

MEEs collection and processing

MEE collection was conducted following established clinical protocols. Prior to the tympanostomy procedure, the external auditory canal was thoroughly cleared of cerumen and disinfected with 75% alcohol to ensure sterility. A radial incision was made in the anteroinferior quadrant of the tympanic membrane, and MEE was aspirated directly into a 1 ml syringe via a suction tube. The MEE sample was promptly transferred to a sterile Eppendorf tube and stored at −80 °C until further analysis. All MEE samples were categorized into two main types (serous and mucoid) by the same surgeon based on their appearance and viscosity. Specifically, if the MEE remained stationary for at least 10 s after inverting the tube, it was classified as a mucoid effusion; if it flowed immediately after inversion, it was classified as a serous effusion [13].

Before testing, the samples were thawed at room temperature, diluted with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and subjected to ultrasonic oscillation treatment. The processed samples were then immediately used for subsequent experiments.

Measurement of NET-related biomarkers and mucins

NET-related biomarkers examined in this study included citrullinated histone H3 (CitH3), double-stranded DNA (dsDNA), myeloperoxidase-DNA complexes (MPO-DNA), and Nucleosome. Elevated levels of two or more of these biomarkers were considered indicative of increased NET concentrations. Additionally, two mucins commonly present in the middle ear cavity, MUC5AC and MUC5B, were also examined. CitH3 detection was performed using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit with a modified protocol (Cayman Chemical). dsDNA quantification was carried out with the Quant-iT™ PicoGreen® dsDNA assay kit (Invitrogen, Cat. #P7589). MPO-DNA complexes were measured using a modified ELISA technique. While Nucleosome levels were evaluated using Cell Death Detection ELISA PLUS kit (Roche Diagnostics, IN, USA) according to the adapted instructions [14]. For MPO-DNA and Nucleosome quantification, results are presented as fold change, which was calculated by normalizing the optical density (OD) value of each sample against the mean OD value obtained from the serous group, which served as the internal control. MUC5AC and MUC5B concentrations were quantified using specific ELISA kits following the manufacturer’s protocol (Elabscience Biotechnology Inc., USA).

Clinical data collection

Demographic and clinical data, including age, gender, medical history, hearing threshold evaluations, and white blood cell (WBC), neutrophil (Neut), and platelet (PLT) counts, were systematically recorded.

Hearing thresholds were assessed by a qualified audiologist. Pure-tone audiometry was employed for children aged three years and older, while behavioral audiometry was used for younger children to assess both air and bone conduction thresholds at frequencies of 500, 1000, 2000, and 4000 Hz. The average values from these assessments were recorded. Hearing levels were subsequently classified based on the most recent hearing loss grading criteria established by the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2021 [15], alongside the sample size of this investigation: the mild hearing loss group (20 to less than 35 dB), the moderate or greater hearing loss group (exceeding 35 dB). WBC count, Neut count, and PLT counts were measured using the manufactured MACCURA fully automated blood analyzer (Sysmex Corporation, Japan).

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was conducted using the SPSS statistical software package (version 29.0.1.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Statistical graphs were generated with GraphPad Prism (version 9.5.1 (528)). Quantitative data following a normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed using t-tests or one-way ANOVA. Non-normally distributed data are reported as medians with interquartile ranges and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical data comparisons were performed using the chi-square test. Correlation analyses between variables were performed using Spearman’s rank correlation test. All statistical analyses were conducted with a significance level (α) of 0.05, with a two-sided p-value of less than 0.05 indicating statistically significant differences or relationships within the dataset.

Results

Study population characteristics

This study enrolled a total of 55 pediatric patients diagnosed with OME. Among these participants, 15 children exhibited bilateral involvement, resulting in the collection of 70 MEE specimens. The background characteristics of the serous and mucoid groups are summarized in Table 1 with no significant differences in age, sex, or peripheral blood laboratory measures between the two groups. A notable trend was observed in the comparison of hearing levels between the two types of effusion. Thicker effusions were associated with more severe hearing impairment compared to thinner effusions (p = 0.015), suggesting a potential relationship between effusion viscosity and auditory function.

Comparison of NET-related biomarkers and mucins levels between serous and mucoid groups

The quantification of NETs was based on the measurement of key representative components including dsDNA, CitH3, MPO-DNA, and Nucleosomes. Samples were classified as having increased NETs content if at least two of these biomarkers show elevated levels. The results indicated that the levels of dsDNA, CitH3, and MPO-DNA were significantly higher in the mucoid group, suggesting that NETs are more prominent in mucoid effusions compared to serous effusions. The two mucins were detected in both serous and mucoid effusions with significantly higher levels of both MUC5B and MUC5AC in the mucoid group. Furthermore, the level of MUC5B consistently exceeded that of MUC5AC across all samples (Figure 1).

Comparison of NET-related biomarkers and mucins levels between two hearing groups

To further investigate the relationship between hearing levels and biomarkers under investigation, the study population was divided into two groups based on hearing severity: mild hearing loss group and moderate or greater hearing loss group. The results indicated significantly higher levels of CitH3, and dsDNA in the group with poorer hearing, suggesting that NETs have an impact on hearing levels. No significant differences were observed in Nucleosomes and MPO-DNA levels between the two hearing groups. MUC5B was significantly more abundant in the moderate or greater hearing loss group (Table 2).

Correlation between NET-related biomarkers, mucins, and clinical parameters

Correlation analyses were performed to evaluate the relationships among NET-related biomarkers, mucins, and clinical parameters. As depicted in Table 3, three NET-related biomarkers (CitH3, dsDNA, and MPO-DNA) exhibited strong positive correlations with MUC5AC, while four NET-related biomarkers (CitH3, dsDNA, Nucleosomes, and MPO-DNA) demonstrated high positive correlations with MUC5B. These findings suggest a robust association between NETs and mucins (MUC5AC and MUC5B). Positive correlations were also observed between hearing thresholds and CitH3, dsDNA. In addition to NETs, a statistically significant positive correlation was identified between hearing thresholds and MUC5B (r = 0.242, p = 0.043). Further analyses revealed no significant correlations between the expression levels of NETs and either age or disease duration. Furthermore, peripheral blood cell counts showed no significant associations with any of the biomarkers measured in MEE samples.

Discussion

The pathophysiology of OME is multifactorial. The structure and function of the eustachian tube in children are not fully developed, leading to transient OME in many cases that may resolve within a few days. However, some children experience recurrence or progression to chronic OME, making it crucial to investigate additional pathogenic factors and differentiate the mechanisms underlying chronic versus non-chronic inflammation. Inflammatory and immune reactions against rhino-pharyngeal infections and adenoid hypertrophy are thought to be primary contributors [16,17]. Subsequently, studies have identified bacteria and mucosal biofilms in MEE from children with OME, using both generic and pathogen-specific probes. This has intensified interest in the role of infectious agents in the pathogenesis of OME [18]. NETs have emerged as critical components of the inflammatory response during infections [19–21], and may exacerbate pathology in conjunction with mucins [22]. Viscous effusions are frequently linked to more severe mucosal remodeling, increased inflammatory responses, and poorer hearing outcomes [23]. However, the specific effects of NETs on middle ear inflammation and mucus hypersecretion in OME remain unclear.

NETs formation is reflected by the presence of key molecular markers(CitH3, dsDNA, nucleosomes and MPO-DNA). CitH3 is generated by histone citrullination, a specific enzymatic modification catalyzed by PAD4. This is a necessary and highly regulated step that induces chromatin decondensation, serving as a prerequisite for NET backbone release. Its presence is the most specific and crucial indicator of an active, regulated NET formation process [24]. Nucleosomes and dsDNA form the basic structural lattice of NETs [25]. While dsDNA is less specific alone (as it can be released by necrosis or apoptosis), its measurement reflects the total magnitude of chromatin material released into the middle ear space [26]. MPO-DNA complexes MPO is an enzyme abundant in neutrophil granules [27]. The formation of the MPO-DNA complex is a direct molecular signature of the fusion of granular components with decondensed chromatin during NETs formation. Although MPO can be released upon cell rupture, the complexed form significantly enhances the specificity for NETs over non-specific lysis.

This study quantitatively demonstrates the presence of high levels of NETs in MEE from children with OME for the first time. We further evaluated the expression of NETs in different types of effusions and found that NETs (CitH3, dsDNA, and MPO-DNA) were significantly elevated in mucoid effusions, suggesting the pathogenic role of NETs in OME. This observation is consistent with previous studies that documented increased levels of pro-inflammatory mediators and DNA content within mucus, as confirmed through mass spectrometry and Western blotting [13]. NETs possess a characteristic reticular structure that creates an ecological niche within the middle ear, allowing for the accommodation and entrapment of bacteria in both air-filled and fluid-filled environments [6,28]. While this structure prevents bacterial spread and maintains a high concentration of virulence factors, it also impedes phagocytosis, which may contribute to disease recurrence and chronicity, as well as complicating tympanostomy tube placement in children [29]. Additionally, NETs may alter the physical properties of secretions, increasing viscosity which is associated with various airway diseases [30]. Previous studies have shown that NETs serve as scaffolds for plasma proteins, facilitating thrombosis and promoting thrombus stabilization [14]. Given their unique reticular structure, it is hypothesized that NETs may also influence mucus viscoelasticity and serve as scaffolds for mucins in OME. This hypothesis warrants further investigation to elucidate the underlying mechanisms involved.

To investigate the potential relationship between NETs and mucins, a quantitative assessment of the expression levels of MUC5AC and MUC5B in MEE was performed using ELISA. Both MUC5AC and MUC5B were detected in all MEE samples, with higher concentrations observed in more viscous effusions. Correlation analysis revealed a significant relationship between NET levels and the expression of both MUC5AC and MUC5B. A study by Stephanie Val utilizing immunofluorescence and immunoblotting techniques demonstrated the presence of NETs and their colocalization with MUC5B [10]. Previous studies have shown that neutrophil elastase can stimulate airway epithelial cells to secrete MUC5AC [31]. Additionally, nebulized DNase I has been found to degrade NETs, resulting in reduced mucin secretion and decreased goblet cells hyperplasia [32]. Based on our findings, it is hypothesized that NETs and mucins may interact to promote chronic inflammation and mucoid states in the middle ear. Specifically, NETs may influence the morphology and function of mucosal tissues, regulating mucin secretion and stabilizing middle ear mucus through their reticular structure and components. However, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying these interactions remain poorly understood. A deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms governing the interplay between NETs and mucins in the middle ear will be essential for developing targeted therapeutic strategies to address this disease mechanism.

The mechanisms underlying hearing loss in OME are complex and involve multiple pathophysiological processes. Our analysis of the correlation between NETs and hearing is the first to report a significant positive association between CitH3 and dsDNA levels and hearing thresholds. Consistent with prior research [33], MUC5B, the largest and potentially most viscous mucin, also exhibited a strong positive correlation with hearing thresholds, suggesting a potential role for both NETs and MUC5B in the development of OME-related hearing loss. These biomarkers may facilitate the identification of patients particularly vulnerable to OME-associated hearing loss, allowing for timely intervention. Due to the large age range in our pediatric cohort, we used different audiometric methods (PTA vs. behavioral) to assess hearing thresholds which introduced precision differences [1], a key methodological limitation. Lower reliability in behavioral measures may underestimate the true biomarker-hearing threshold correlation. Future research should utilize objective methods for uniform, precise assessment across ages.

This cross-sectional study improves the detection and quantification methods for NETs, offering new insights into their roles. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. The relatively small sample size and the lack of long-term follow-up may restrict the generalizability of our findings. Future research should aim to replicate these results in larger cohorts and incorporate longitudinal studies to assess the long-term impact of NETs and mucins on treatment outcomes in OME. Additionally, the absence of standardization in the use of fold change for MPO-DNA and Nucleosome limits the comparability of results across studies. The absence of microbiome data restricts precise interpretation of NET induction mechanisms. Future research must integrate detailed microbial data to clarify the relationship between pathogens, NETs and OME. Causal inference and therapeutic efficacy require future validation through interventional studies, such as DNase degradation, PAD4 inhibition, or animal models. Additionally, the study relies on univariate analyses. Future prospective studies must employ comprehensive multivariable regression with expanded cohorts to confirm the independent robustness of these findings.

Conclusion

This study identifies elevated levels of NET-related biomarkers and mucins (MUC5AC and MUC5B) in mucoid MEE, revealing strong correlations among these factors and hearing levels. These findings suggest a potential link between NETs and mucins in OME, thereby providing a conceptual framework for future mechanistic investigation. Furthermore, the identified biomarkers represent potential targets for therapeutic exploration.

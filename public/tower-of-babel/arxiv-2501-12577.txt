On the Integral Representations of the k k -Pell and k k -Pell-Lucas Numbers Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 11B39, 11B37. Thanks: Key words and phrases: k k -Pell number; k k -Pell-Lucas number; integral representation Thanks: The corresponding author is Weerayuth Nilsrakoo
ACHARIYA NILSRAKOO, Weerayuth Nilsrakoo

Abstract
. In this paper, the integral representations of the k k -Pell and k k -Pell-Lucas numbers are presented. By employing Binet’s formulas for these numbers, we derive several identities and verify their integral representations using basic integral calculus. Our results are also deduced and related to the Fibonacci, Lucas, Pell, and Pell-Lucas numbers.



1.
Introduction

Fibonacci, Lucas, Pell, and Pell–Lucas sequences have been discussed in many articles and books (see
[
8
,
1
,
4
,
9
,
6
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
12
,
10
,
11
,
5
]
). Recall that the Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}
are defined via the recurrence relation

F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}

for
n\geq 2
with
F_{0}=0
and
F_{1}=1
. The Lucas numbers
L_{n}
are defined via the recurrence relation

L_{n}=L_{n-1}+L_{n-2}

for
n\geq 2
with
L_{0}=2
and
L_{1}=1
.

There are several ways to represent these numbers, one of which is the integral representation. The first example of integral representations for Fibonacci numbers of even orders, using an approach based on the (Gaussian) hypergeometric function, was presented in the paper by Dilcher
[
4
]
from 2000, where he showed that

F_{2n}=\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{3}\cos x\right)^{n-1}\sin xdx.

In 2015, Glasser and Zhou
[
6
]
worked out an explicit integral representation for
F_{n}
involving trigonometric functions. Indeed, the main result of their paper is a representation of the form

F_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{5}}-\dfrac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{\sin(x/2)}{x}\right)\left(\dfrac{\cos(2nx)-2\sin(nx)\sin x}{5\sin^{2}x+\cos^{2}x}\right)dx,

where
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
is the golden ratio. Another representation is given
by Andrica and Bagdasar in
[
8
]
. In a recent year, Stewart
[
10
]
derived some appealing integral representations for Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. For instance, he proved the representations

F_{\ell n}=\dfrac{nF_{\ell}}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(L_{\ell}+\sqrt{5}F_{\ell}x)^{n-1}dx

and

L_{\ell n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(L_{\ell}+\sqrt{5}(n+1)F_{\ell}x)(L_{\ell}+\sqrt{5}F_{\ell}x)^{n-1}dx,

where
\ell
and
n
are non-negative integers. The special case of this identity for
\ell=1
is also discussed in Stewart’s paper
[
11
]
from 2023.

Like Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, the Pell family is widely used. Pell and Pell–Lucas numbers also provide boundless opportunities to experiment, explore, and conjecture.
Recall that Pell number
P_{n}
is defined by the recurrence relation

P_{n}=2P_{n-1}+P_{n-2}

for
n\geq 2
with
P_{0}=0
and
P_{1}=1
. The Pell-Lucas number
Q_{n}
is defined by the recurrence relation

Q_{n}=2Q_{n-1}+Q_{n-2}

for
n\geq 2
with
Q_{0}=2
and
Q_{1}=2
. The Binet’s formulas for
the Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers are

P_{n}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\varphi^{n}-\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\varphi^{n}}\right)\,\,\text{and}\,\,Q_{n}=\varphi^{n}+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\varphi^{n}},

where
\varphi=1+\sqrt{2}
. The integral representations for Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers are studied by the first author in
[
12
]
as follows:

P_{\ell n}=\frac{nP_{\ell}}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{\ell}+2\sqrt{2}P_{\ell}x)^{n-1}dx

and

Q_{\ell n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{\ell}+2\sqrt{2}(n+1)P_{\ell}x)(Q_{\ell}+2\sqrt{2}P_{\ell}x)^{n-1}dx,

where
\ell
and
n
are non-negative integers.

The generalization of Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers was found by studying the recursion and introduced by Catarino
[
13
]
, and Catarino and Vasco
[
14
]
in 2013; see more details in Section
2
. According to Stewart
[
10
]
and Nilsrakoo
[
12
]
, we give new integral representations of the
k
-Pell and
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers by using Binet’s formulas to establish some identities and simple integral calculus to prove them.

As we can see, for
k=1
, the classical Pell and classical Pell–Lucas numbers are obtained. If
k=4
, the classical Fibonacci and classical Lucas numbers appear as follows:
F_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}P_{4,n}
and
L_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}Q_{4,n}
. Moreover, sequences

\left\{P_{2,n}\right\}
,
\left\{P_{3,n}\right\}
,
\left\{P_{4,n}\right\}
, and
\left\{P_{5,n}\right\}
are listed in The Online Encyclopaedia of Integer Sequences
[
15
]
under the symbols A002605, A015518,
A085449, and A002532, respectively, while sequences
\left\{Q_{2,n}\right\}
,
\left\{Q_{3,n}\right\}
,
\left\{Q_{4,n}\right\}
, and
\left\{Q_{6,n}\right\}
under the symbols A080040, A102345, A087131, and A127226, respectively.

The recurrence relations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) generate characteristic
equations of the form

r^{2}-2r-k=0.

Since
k\geq 1
, this equation has two roots,
r_{1}=1+\sqrt{1+k}
and
r_{2}=1-\sqrt{1+k}
. Therefore, the Binet’s formulas for the
k
-Pell numbers
\left\{P_{k,n}\right\}
and the
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers
\left\{Q_{k,n}\right\}
are

(2.3)

P_{k,n}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+k}}\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}-\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)

and

(2.4)

Q_{k,n}=\varphi_{k}^{n}+\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}},

where
\varphi_{k}=1+\sqrt{1+k}
; see also
[
13
, Proposition 1]
and
[
14
, Proposition 1]
.

The following lemmas are our main tools for proving the integral representations of the
k
-Pell and
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k
and
n
be non-negative integers with
k\neq 0
. Then the following hold:

(i)

Q_{k,n}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,n}=2\varphi_{k}^{n};

(ii)

Q_{k,n}-2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,n}=2\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}};

(iii)

Q_{k,n}^{2}-4(1+k)P_{k,n}^{2}=4(-k)^{n}
.

Proof.

(i)

Combining Binet’s formulas (
2.4
) and (
2.3
) gives

\displaystyle Q_{k,n}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,n}=\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}+\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)+\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}-\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)=2\varphi_{k}^{n}.

(ii)

Subtracting Binet’s formulas (
2.4
) and (
2.3
) gives

\displaystyle Q_{k,n}-2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,n}=\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}+\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)-\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}-\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)=2\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}.

(iii)

It follows from
(i)
and
(ii)
that

\displaystyle Q_{k,n}^{2}-4(1+k)P_{k,n}^{2}

\displaystyle=Q_{k,n}^{2}-\left(2\sqrt{1+k}P_{k,n}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(Q_{k,n}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,n}\right)\left(Q_{k,n}-2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\varphi_{k}^{n}\right){2\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}}

\displaystyle=4(-k)^{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k
,
m
, and
n
be non-negative integers with
k\neq 0
. Then the following hold:

(i)

2P_{k,m+n}=P_{k,m}Q_{k,n}+P_{k,n}Q_{k,m};

(ii)

2Q_{k,m+n}=Q_{k,m}Q_{k,n}+4(1+k)P_{k,m}P_{k,n}.

Proof.

Using Binet’s formulas (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle P_{k,m}Q_{k,n}+P_{k,n}Q_{k,m}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+k}}\left(\varphi_{k}^{m}-\frac{(-k)^{m}}{\varphi_{k}^{m}}\right)\right)\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}+\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+k}}\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}-\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)\right)\left(\varphi_{k}^{m}+\frac{(-k)^{m}}{\varphi_{k}^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+k}}\left(\varphi_{k}^{m+n}-\frac{(-k)^{m+n}}{\varphi_{k}^{m+n}}\right)

\displaystyle=2P_{k,m+n}

and

\displaystyle Q_{k,m}

\displaystyle Q_{k,n}+4(1+k)P_{k,m}P_{k,n}

\displaystyle=\left(\varphi_{k}^{m}+\frac{(-k)^{m}}{\varphi_{k}^{m}}\right)\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}+\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+4(1+k)\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+k}}\left(\varphi_{k}^{m}-\frac{(-k)^{m}}{\varphi_{k}^{m}}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+k}}\left(\varphi_{k}^{n}-\frac{(-k)^{n}}{\varphi_{k}^{n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\left(\varphi_{k}^{m+n}+\frac{(-k)^{m+n}}{\varphi_{k}^{m+n}}\right)

\displaystyle=2Q_{k,m+n}.

Hence,
(i)
and
(ii)
complete the proof.
∎

3.
Main Results

In this section, thanks to the technique of
[
10
]
, we obtain new integral representations for the
k
-Pell and
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers. We start with the integral representation for the
k
-Pell number
P_{k,\ell n}
which can be found by employing other known relations between the two numbers
P_{k,\ell}
and
Q_{k,\ell}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
k
,
\ell
, and
n
are non-negative integers with
k\neq 0
, the
k
-Pell numbers
P_{k,\ell n}
can be represented by the integral

(3.1)

P_{k,\ell n}=\frac{nP_{k,\ell}}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n-1}dx.

Proof.

For
n=0
or
\ell=0
, we have done. Let us assume that
\ell,n>0
, straightforward simple integration leads to

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n}\right]_{-1}^{1}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell})^{n}-(Q_{k,\ell}-2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell})^{n}\right].

Applying
(i)
and
(ii)
of Lemma
2.1
in (
3
) with
n
replaced with
\ell
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[\left(2\varphi_{k}^{\ell}\right)^{n}-\left(2\frac{(-k)^{\ell}}{\varphi_{k}^{\ell}}\right)^{n}\right]\,

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{nP_{k,\ell}}\left[\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+k}}\left(\varphi_{k}^{\ell n}-\frac{(-k)^{\ell n}}{\varphi_{k}^{\ell n}}\right)\right].

It follows from (
2.3
) with replace
n
by
\ell n
that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{nP_{k,\ell}}P_{k,\ell n}.

Then (
3.1
) has been proved.
∎

The integral representations of the
k
-Pell number for even and odd orders are shown as follows:

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
k
and
n
be non-negative integers with
k\neq 0
.

(i)

The
k
-Pell numbers
P_{k,2n}
can be represented by the integral

(3.3)

P_{k,2n}=n\int_{-1}^{1}(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n-1}dx.

(ii)

The
k
-Pell numbers
P_{k,2n+1}
can be represented by the integral

P_{k,2n+1}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(2n+k+2+2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,x)(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n-1}dx.

Proof.

(i)

Notice that
P_{k,2}=2
and
Q_{k,2}=2k+4
. Setting
\ell=2
in (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle P_{k,2n}

\displaystyle=\frac{nP_{k,2}}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,2}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,2}x)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2^{n-1}}\int_{-1}^{1}(2k+4+4\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=n\int_{-1}^{1}(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n-1}dx.

(ii)

Reindexing of
n
by
n+1
in (
3.3
), we get

(3.4)

P_{k,2n+2}=(n+1)\int_{-1}^{1}(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n}dx.

Using
P_{k,2n+2}=2P_{k,2n+1}+kP_{k,2n}
with (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle P_{k,2n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(n+1)\int_{-1}^{1}(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n}dx-nk\int_{-1}^{1}(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n-1}dx\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\left((n+1)(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)-nk\right)(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(2n+k+2+2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,x)(k+2+2\sqrt{1+k}\,x)^{n-1}dx.

This completes the proof.
∎

Setting
k=1
in Theorem
3.1
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3

(
[
12
]
, Theorem 3.1)
.

For
\ell
and
n
are non-negative integers, the Pell numbers
P_{\ell n}
can be represented by the integral

P_{\ell n}=\frac{nP_{\ell}}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{\ell}+2\sqrt{2}P_{\ell}x)^{n-1}dx.

Proof.

Notice that
P_{1,\ell n}=P_{\ell n}
,
P_{1,\ell}=P_{\ell}
, and
Q_{1,\ell}=Q_{\ell}
. Then, by Theorem
3.1
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Setting
k=4
in Theorem
3.1
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4

(
[
10
]
, Theorem 2.1)
.

For
\ell
and
n
are non-negative integers, the Fibonacci numbers
F_{\ell n}
can be represented by the integral

F_{\ell n}=\frac{nF_{\ell}}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(L_{\ell}+\sqrt{5}F_{\ell}x)^{n-1}dx.

Proof.

Notice that
P_{4,\ell n}=2^{\ell n-1}F_{\ell n}
,
P_{4,\ell}=2^{\ell-1}F_{\ell}
, and
Q_{4,\ell}=2^{\ell}L_{\ell}
. Setting
k=4
in (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle F_{\ell n}

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{4,\ell n}}{2^{\ell n-1}}=\frac{nP_{4,\ell}}{2^{\ell n-1+n}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(Q_{4,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+4}P_{4,\ell}x\right)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{n2^{\ell-1}F_{\ell}}{2^{\ell n-1+n}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(2^{\ell}L_{\ell}+2^{\ell}\sqrt{5}F_{\ell}x\right)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{nF_{\ell}}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(L_{\ell}+\sqrt{5}F_{\ell}x)^{n-1}dx.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we provide the integral representations for the
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers
Q_{k,\ell n}
based on the two numbers
P_{k,\ell}
and
Q_{k,\ell}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For
k
,
\ell
, and
n
are non-negative integers with
k\neq 0
, the
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers
Q_{k,\ell n}
can be represented by the integral

(3.5)

Q_{k,\ell n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,\ell}+2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n-1}dx.

Proof.

For
n=0
or
\ell=0
, it is easy to see that (
3.5
) holds. We assume now that
\ell,n>0
. Replacing
n
by
n+1
in (
3.1
) becomes

(3.6)

P_{k,\ell n+\ell}=\frac{(n+1)P_{k,\ell}}{2^{n+1}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n}dx.

Integrating by part (
3.5
) and using (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,\ell}+2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n2^{n+1}\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}}(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n}(Q_{k,\ell}+2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)\bigg|_{-1}^{1}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{n+1}{n2^{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}x)^{n}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n2^{n+1}\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[(Q_{k,\ell}+2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell})^{n}(Q_{k,\ell}+2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell})\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{n2^{n+1}\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[(Q_{k,\ell}-2\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell})^{n}(Q_{k,\ell}-2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell})\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2P_{k,\ell n+\ell}}{nP_{k,\ell}}.

Applying
(i)
and
(ii)
of Lemma
2.1
to the righthand side of the above equation gives

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n2^{n+1}\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[2^{n}\varphi_{k}^{\ell n}\left(Q_{k,\ell}+2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{n2^{n+1}\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[2^{n}\frac{\left(-k\right)^{\ell n}}{\varphi_{k}^{\ell n}}\left(Q_{k,\ell}-2(n+1)\sqrt{1+k}\,P_{k,\ell}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2P_{k,\ell n+\ell}}{nP_{k,\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1+k}}\left(\varphi_{k}^{\ell n}-\frac{\left(-k\right)^{\ell n}}{\varphi_{k}^{\ell n}}\right)Q_{k,\ell}+(n+1)P_{k,\ell}\left(\varphi_{k}^{\ell n}+\frac{\left(-k\right)^{\ell n}}{\varphi_{k}^{\ell n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2P_{k,\ell n+\ell}}{nP_{k,\ell}}.

Using both Binet’s formulas (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) with
n
replaced by
\ell n
and Lemma
2.2

(i)
leads to

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[P_{k,\ell n}Q_{k,\ell}+(n+1)P_{k,\ell}Q_{k,\ell n}\right]-\frac{2P_{k,\ell n+\ell}}{nP_{k,\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n\,P_{k,\ell}}\left[P_{k,\ell n}Q_{k,\ell}+P_{k,\ell}Q_{k,\ell n}+nP_{k,\ell}Q_{k,\ell n}\right]-\frac{2P_{k,\ell n+\ell}}{nP_{k,\ell}}

4.
Conclusions

In this paper, new integral representations of the
k
-Pell and
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers have been introduced and studied. Many of the properties of these numbers are proved by using Binet’s formulas. We also establish some identities and simple integral calculus to prove them. Moreover, our results are also deduced and related to the Fibonacci, Lucas, Pell, and Pell-Lucas numbers.

Dynamic Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio Stratifies Survival and Immune-Related Adverse Events in Lung and Gastric Cancer Patients with Immunotherapy
Authors: Chengcheng Qian, Mengting Wang, Chuyi Liu, Feng Liu, Wenying Zhang, Xiaohua Hu, Yanjie Zhang, Bin Jiang, Jiongyi Wang, Ming Xu, Haihua Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDynamic neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) has emerged as a prognostic marker in immunotherapy outcomes, yet phase-specific alterations in NLR after immunotherapy and their association with treatment efficacy remain underreported. This study aimed to clarify the prognostic utility of NLR dynamics in patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) therapy.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 101 cancer patients (including lung and gastric cancer) treated with ICIs. NLR was measured at three time points: baseline (prior to ICIs initiation), Cycle 1 (C1, 1 month post-treatment), and Cycle 3 (C3, 3 months post-treatment). Pearson’s chi-square test and Kaplan–Meier survival analyses were used to assess associations between NLR dynamics and immune-related adverse events (irAEs), progression-free survival (PFS), and overall survival (OS).ResultsBaseline NLR showed no significance with irAEs. However, elevated C1 NLR was related with early irAEs in both lung and gastric cancers (OR = 2.883, p = 0.029). In lung cancer patients, a reduced C3 NLR was significantly associated with longer PFS (HR = 2.376, p = 0.006) and OS (HR = 3.315, p < 0.001), whereas the association was weaker in gastric cancer (PFS: HR = 1.758, p = 0.085; OS: HR = 2.284, p = 0.019). Multivariate analysis identified C3 NLR was associated independently with both PFS and OS in lung cancer patients. Furthermore, a ≥ 30% elevation in NLR stratified patients into distinct risk categories, with the potential clinical value influenced by tumor type.ConclusionPhase-specific NLR dynamics were correlated with distinct immunotherapy outcomes. C1 NLR was associated with early toxicity in both lung and gastric cancers, whereas sustained NLR reduction at C3 was associated with more favorable survival outcomes in lung cancer. These findings suggest that longitudinal, phase-specific NLR assessment may provide complementary information for monitoring treatment-related outcomes and requires further prospective validation.

Introduction

Immunotherapy has revolutionized the treatment paradigm for both solid and hematologic malignancies over the past decade, which have significantly improved overall survival rates, and in some cases, induced durable remissions.1 Patients with lung or gastric cancer receiving immunotherapy show substantial clinical heterogeneity, with differences in functional status, symptom burden, comorbidities, nutritional status, and overall patient vulnerability. Recent evidence has demonstrated considerable heterogeneity in patient complexity even among individuals classified as medically homogeneous according to conventional case‑mix systems.2,3 These factors may influence systemic inflammatory and immune status during immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) treatment, which could contribute to differences in treatment response and toxicity during immunotherapy.

Previous studies have demonstrated that systemic inflammatory markers, particularly the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) may serve as predictive biomarkers for immune-related adverse events (irAEs) development in cancer patients receiving immunotherapy.4–6 NLR reflects the balance between systemic inflammation and immune status. In cancer patients, tumor-associated neutrophils can further contribute to tumor progression and immune suppression, providing a potential biological basis for the association between elevated NLR and adverse clinical outcomes.7 During ICIs treatment, effective anti-tumor immune responses may reduce systemic inflammation and increase lymphocyte-mediated immune activity, resulting in a decrease in NLR. Previous studies have shown that dynamics in NLR during ICIs treatment are associated with treatment response in the tumor immune environment.8,9

Numerous studies have suggested that anti-PD-1 drugs exhibit reduced toxicity compared to conventional chemotherapy; however, clinical trials have reported irAEs associated with various immune agents.10 ICIs can cause irAEs by disrupting the immune system balance, causing damage to organ systems such as the endocrine, cutaneous, pulmonary, gastrointestinal, and hepatic systems.11,12 Recent research reported that irAEs caused by ICIs had relevance to tumor types and organs, which could be classified into five categories: cutaneous, pulmonary, endocrine, gastrointestinal, and hepatic.13 Most irAEs are identified as mild or moderate, which can be effectively managed without necessitating treatment discontinuation. However, there exist exceptions, such as immune-related pneumonia, though infrequent, can be life-threatening, with a fatality rate of about 1% of individuals.14–16 Therefore, the challenge of distinguishing and characterizing irAEs suggests the significance of prognostic and diagnostic biomarkers to facilitate effective management.

In conclusion, NLR is a dynamic parameter that may change during immunotherapy. A single baseline measurement may not adequately reflect these alterations. More importantly, NLR measured at different treatment points may provide different information regarding immune-related toxicity and survival. The clinical significance of early and late dynamic in NLR remains incompletely understood. Serial assessment of NLR may therefore provide additional significance beyond baseline NLR and may help identify irAEs among immunotherapy.

In this study, we aimed to investigate the association between longitudinal NLR dynamics and treatment outcomes in patients with lung or gastric cancers receiving ICIs therapy. Lung and gastric cancers were both represent common malignancies in which ICIs are routinely used, while their distinct tumor biology provides an opportunity to examine whether dynamic NLR retain clinical relevance across different cancer types. We evaluated NLR at baseline, the first treatment cycle (C1), and the third treatment cycle (C3), as well as changes from baseline to C1 (ΔNLR1) and C3 (ΔNLR2). We further examined whether these NLR parameters were associated with progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and irAEs in this patient population.

Materials and Methods

Patients

We collected and analyzed the data from patients who received anti-PD-1 medications from the Department of Oncology in Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, from October 2018 to December 2024. A total of 101 patients were enrolled in the study. We applied specific inclusion and exclusion criteria as follows: 1) Patients were diagnosed with stage IV NSCLC or gastric cancer according to the 8th TNM-classification; 2) inpatients who had received at least one dose of ICIs were included; 3) patients were required to exhibit measurable lesions at the baseline. For patients with lung cancer, baseline lesions were evaluated via chest CT with 1mm slice thickness. In patients with gastric cancer, measurable lesions, including those located in the liver, lungs, and lymph nodes, were evaluated with contrast-enhanced CT covering the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. All tumor lesions were assessed according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1; 4) patients with any history of thyroid-related diseases were excluded; 5) patients actively participating in other ongoing clinical trials were excluded. Additionally, based on the available clinical records, patients with osteoarthritis, rheumatic heart disease, or tuberculosis were not included in the final study cohort. This retrospective analysis was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital.

Data Collection

Peripheral venous blood samples were collected using EDTA-anticoagulant tubes. Hematological parameters, including absolute neutrophil count (ANC) and absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) were measured using a Sysmex XN-3000 automated hematology analyzer (Sysmex Corporation, Kobe, Japan) based on the fluorescence flow cytometry. And then ANC and ALC were systematically extracted from institutional electronic medical records. Demographic characteristics (age, gender), disease-specific variables (tumor location) and thyroid function, and irAEs development were concurrently documented. Neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio (NLR) was calculated by dividing the ANC by the ALC. Longitudinal data collection time points were set at baseline (within 7 days before the first ICIs administration, defined as baseline NLR), 1 month after initial ICIs treatment (defined as C1 NLR), and 3 months after initial ICIs treatment (defined as C3 NLR). The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) cut-off values of 5 (NLR < 5 vs ≥ 5) were determined using established thresholds derived from prior validation studies.17,18

For the analysis of longitudinal NLR changes, we also calculated ΔNLR1 as the percentage change from baseline NLR to C1 NLR, and ΔNLR2 as the percentage difference from baseline NLR to C3 NLR. Patients were stratified into two groups based on serial NLR dynamics according to previous literature:19 incremental NLR (iNLR): > 30% increase in NLR at either C1 or C3 timepoints, decremental or stable NLR (dNLR): ≤ 30% variation (stable) or any decrease in NLR at C1 or C3 assessments.

Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from initiation of ICIs treatment to tumor progression or death, whichever occurred first. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from initiation of ICIs treatment to death. Tumor response and disease progression during follow-up were assessed according to RECIST version 1.1. Patients who did not experience the corresponding events were censored at the date of their last available follow-up for December 31, 2024.

IrAEs were defined as adverse events considered to be related to ICIs and were graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0, assessed by a panel of at least three professional medical clinicians. Thyroid dysfunction included treatment-emergent abnormalities in thyroid function, including hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism, and was graded according to CTCAE 5.0. Liver dysfunction was defined as treatment-emergent elevation of serum liver enzymes and/or bilirubin and was graded according to CTCAE v5.0. Capillary hyperplasia was defined based on clinical and/or pathological findings documented during treatment and was assessed according to the corresponding CTCAE criteria. Pyrexia was graded according to the CTCAE 5.0. Grade 1 was defined as a body temperature of 38.0–39.0°C, Grade 2 as > 39.0–40.0°C, Grade 3 as > 40.0°C for ≤ 24 hours, and Grade 4 as > 40.0°C for > 24 hours and Grade 5 as death. We classified irAEs into early irAEs and late irAEs based on their onset time, with early irAEs defined as those occurring within 3 months of initiating ICIs treatment, and late irAEs as those occurring beyond this period. p < 0.05 was considered significant.

Statistical Analysis

The data was analyzed using SPSS 25.0 (IBM, NY, USA), and graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, CA, USA). The analysis employed the Pearson χ2 test and logistic regression analysis to explore the relationship between variables and irAEs. The prognosis of patients was evaluated using the Kaplan-Meier analysis and Log rank test in different groups. Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional regression models were performed to assess the difference in PFS and OS. Furthermore, based on the time of irAEs onset, with a cut-off at 3 months, irAEs were classified into early irAEs (≤ 3 months) and late irAEs (> 3 months). p < 0.05 was considered significant in our study.

Among the 101 enrolled patients, NLR measurements were available for 101 patients at baseline, 99 patients at C2, and 92 patients at C3. Baseline neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) assessment revealed that 65 (64.4%) exhibited with NLR < 5, while 36 patients (35.6%) presented with NLR ≥ 5. At the first immunotherapy cycle (C1), the NLR distribution shifted slightly, with 73 patients (72.3%) maintaining NLR < 5, 26 patients (25.7%) showing NLR ≥ 5, and 2 patients missing data due to death. By the third chemotherapy cycle (C3), 61 patients (60.4%) had NLR < 5, and 31 patients (30.7%) had NLR ≥ 5, and 9 patients (8.9%) discontinued before assessment. The dynamic changes in NLR were evaluated at different time points. From baseline to C1 (ΔNLR1), 58 patients (57.4%) demonstrated stable or decreasing NLR, and 41 patients (40.6%) exhibited clinically significant NLR elevation (> 30% increase). Similarly, from baseline to C3 (ΔNLR2), 55 patients (54.5%) maintained NLR stability or reduction, while 37 patients (36.6%) showed progressive NLR increasing.

For survival analyses, among patients with lung cancer, 58, 56, and 52 patients had evaluable NLR measurements at baseline, C1, and C3, respectively. Among patients with gastric cancer, the corresponding numbers were 43, 43, and 40. Accordingly, the analyses of ΔNLR1 included 56 patients with lung cancer and 43 patients with gastric cancer, whereas the analyses of ΔNLR2 included 52 and 40 patients, respectively. Patients died or without an evaluable NLR measurement at the corresponding time point were not included in the time-point analysis.

IrAEs Collection

Of the study population, 61 patients developed irAEs of any grade, and 11 patients developed grade ≥ 3 irAEs. The rate of irAEs of any grade and grade ≥ 3 in patients treated with ICIs was 60.4% and 10.9%, respectively. One patient was recorded as having developed immune-related pneumonia, leading to a fatal outcome. Among patients exposed to have anti-PD-1 drugs, various irAEs, including thyroid dysfunction, hyperglycemia, pneumonia, abnormal liver function, cutaneous lesions, pyrexia, fatigue, thrombocytopenia, and cardiac enzymes increasing were diagnosed and evaluated during the immunotherapy. The management of these irAEs entailed a combination of supportive treatment, which occasionally requires a temporary suspension of immunotherapy. Given the organ-specific irAEs, in our study, 38 irAEs (30.9%) were endocrine, 33 (26.8%) were cutaneous, 14 (11.4%) were pulmonary, and 9 (7.3%) were hepatobiliary. Association Between Variables and irAEs

We systematically evaluated the prognostic utility of NLR at defined time-points for irAEs. Pretreatment baseline NLR showed no significant association with early-irAEs (p = 0.733) or late irAEs (p = 0.792). However, elevated NLR at first treatment cycle (C1) demonstrated specific relevant capacity for early irAEs (HR = 2.88, 95% CI 1.112–7.473; p = 0.029), without significant correlation with late irAEs (p = 0.100). Subsequent C3 NLR measurements lacked prognostic significance for both early (p = 0.939) and late irAEs (p = 0.114).

Dynamic NLR (ΔNLR) analysis identified phase-specific prognostic value. ΔNLR1 exhibited strong informative value for early irAEs (HR = 3.52, 95% CI 1.514–8.205; p = 0.003), whereas ΔNLR2 showed no valuable capacity (p = 0.897). Conversely, late irAEs risk was paradoxically reduced in patients with NLR reduction or stability at C3 (HR = 0.27, 95% CI 0.083–0.886; p = 0.031), while ΔNLR1 displayed borderline association (HR = 0.31, 95% CI 0.10–1.02; p = 0.053), suggesting potential relationship with early NLR alterations. Univariate Analysis for Association Between NLR and Survival in Lung Cancer

In patients with lung cancer, we evaluated the relationship of NLR at baseline, C1, and C3 with PFS and OS (Figure 1A–F). No significant association was observed between baseline NLR and PFS or OS (Figure 1A and B, p > 0.05). In contrast, patients with a lower C1 NLR (< 5) experienced significantly longer PFS than those with an elevated C1 NLR (Figure 1C, p = 0.004). A similar association was observed between C1 NLR and OS (Figure 1D, p = 0.001). At C3, a lower NLR (< 5) was also related with longer PFS (Figure 1E, p = 0.006) and OS (Figure 1F, p < 0.001). Univariate Analysis for Association Between NLR and Survival in Gastric Cancer

In patients with gastric cancer treated with ICIs, baseline NLR was not significantly related with PFS or OS (p > 0.05, Figure 3A and B). Similarly, C1 NLR showed no significant association with PFS or OS (p > 0.05, Figure 3C and D). Multivariate Analysis of Survival

Multivariate analysis adjusting for age, gender, and baseline NLR confirmed that C3 NLR remained an independent risk factor for PFS (HR = 2.446, 95% CI 1.251–4.786; p = 0.009) and OS (HR = 3.506, 95% CI 1.619–7.590, p = 0.001) in lung cancer patients. Notably, ΔNLR2 elevation was independently associated with shorter OS (HR = 2.756, 95% CI 1.302–5.834, p = 0.008) in patients with lung cancer. In contrast, in gastric cancer patients, the univariate analysis revealed no significant associations between PFS with baseline NLR, C1 NLR, or C3 NLR. However, increasing NLR at C3 demonstrated a trend toward poorer PFS (HR = 2.045, 95% CI 1.019–4.101, p = 0.044), requiring validation in more cohorts. Discussion

As ICIs are increasingly used as medications for different cancer types, it is paramount for clinicians to comprehensively acknowledge the potential toxicities associated with these agents and strategies for their management. Significantly, prior research on the occurrence of irAEs mainly comes from clinical trials, with limited real-world data available. In our present study, we reported the incidence of the irAEs associated with anti-PD-1 drugs in advanced lung cancer in the real world. Several reports have indicated that a substantial proportion, ranging from 50 to 70%, of patients treated with anti-PD-1 medications may experience irAEs of any grade.20–23 Our findings reveal an irAEs incidence rate of 60.4% for any grade, consistent with the previous observations.

Our study demonstrates the time-dependent prognostic value of NLR dynamics in immune-related adverse events (irAEs) and clinical outcomes in cancer immunotherapy. A recent meta-analysis revealed a converse correlation between baseline NLR levels and immune-related adverse events (irAEs) incidence, though the predictive accuracy critically depended on the selected NLR cut-off thresholds.24 The variability in threshold determination may explain the heterogeneous findings across clinical investigations. Takada et al included 73 renal cell cancer and gastric cancer patients and found that baseline NLR showed limited predictive value for irAEs but the early-phase NLR elevation (ΔNLR > 120%) significantly indicated an increased risk of grade 3–4 irAEs (OR = 3.94, p = 0.037).25 Matsukane et al analyzed 275 patients receiving ICIs treatment and observed that immune related pneumonitis, can be detected by elevated NLR levels up to 4 weeks before the appearance of symptoms.26 Our study reached similar conclusions that elevated NLR after the first treatment cycle (C1 NLR) and ΔNLR1 were associated with early irAEs, affirming the clinical value of dynamic monitoring. Conversely, for late irAEs, our results suggested that decreased or stable ΔNLR2 is related with the development of late irAEs. This NLR fluctuation likely reflects innate immune activation through neutrophil-mediated tissue damage and impaired regulatory T-cell function. And the paradoxical association may indicate that while early NLR reflects acute immune dysregulation, late NLR normalization marks restored immune homeostasis.27 These phase-specific associations suggest divergent immunopathogenic mechanisms governing irAEs development, potentially reflecting shifts in inflammatory drivers and compensatory regulatory responses over time.

Accumulating studies indicated that NLR, was reported prognostic in evaluating clinical outcomes in patients with ICIs treatment. Kiriu et al recruited 19 non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients and the results showed that patients with a > 30% increase in NLR were linked with shorter time to treatment failure and PFS than patients with steady or decreasing NLR following both first cycle and second cycle.19 These findings align with the 73-patient cohort analysis conducted by Jankowski et al, where elevated NLR values independently predicted disease progression and mortality risks at 3-month post-immunotherapy initiation.28 Temporal patterns in NLR dynamics appear particularly significant, as demonstrated by Guo et al in their 95 patients NSCLC cohort, where NLR elevation after two ICIs cycles was associated with significantly shorter PFS compared to patients showing NLR reduction.17 Furthermore, the findings performed by Suh et al identified NLR > 5 at 6 weeks after ICIs treatment initiation as a biomarker for both PFS and OS in advanced NSCLC.29 Our findings extend these observations by examining NLR longitudinally across various treatment phases rather than relying on a single baseline measurement to demonstrate the clinical relevance. Patients maintaining stable or decreasing NLR levels through both initial and subsequent treatment cycles (1st and 3rd cycles) exhibited significantly improved PFS and OS outcomes. This temporal persistence in NLR patterns suggests that sustained immune homeostasis, as reflected by NLR dynamics, may be crucial for achieving durable therapeutic responses. Importantly, our multi-cycle assessment approach addresses a critical limitation of previous single-timepoint analyses, providing a more comprehensive prognostic utility of NLR.

Clinically, these temporal NLR patterns could serve as dynamic biomarkers for real-time risk stratification. Early-phase NLR elevation might reflect immunosuppression, potentially suggesting severe irAEs. In contrast, sustained NLR decline beyond 3 months may warrant enhanced monitoring for delayed irAEs. Additionally, the significance of ΔNLR1 in late irAEs emphasizes the need to explore combinatorial biomarkers to improve prognostic accuracy. Our phased NLR monitoring strategy demonstrated superior associated performance compared to conventional single-timepoint assessments, particularly through the independent value of ΔNLR2 for overall survival in lung cancer patients. Notably, with the increasing complexity of longitudinal immunotherapy datasets, machine learning approaches may provide additional opportunities to identify nonlinear relationships and hidden prognostic patterns beyond conventional regression models.30

Additionally, prognostic models in the future should consider the multidimensional heterogeneity of patients receiving immunotherapy. Although conventional oncological characteristics, including tumor types, stages, and treatment regimen, provide important prognostic information, they may not fully capture the complexity of individual patient conditions. Regarding this, previous literature has argued that important determinants of outcomes remain partially invisible when relying exclusively on disease-centered indicators.31 Recent studies have demonstrated substantial variability in clinical complexity among patients classified as medically similar according to traditional case-mix systems, suggesting that important determinants of outcomes may remain unrecognized by disease-centered approaches alone.2,3 In this context, dynamic NLR trajectories may represent an accessible marker reflecting tumor-associated immune-inflammatory changes and wider aspects of vulnerability. Future studies integrating longitudinal immune biomarkers with comprehensive clinical complexity indicators may improve individualized risk stratification and better explain heterogeneity in immunotherapy outcomes.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design may have introduced selection bias. Second, the 30% NLR variation threshold remain uncertain and require prospective validation. Last, the association between dynamic NLR and late irAEs should be interpreted cautiously, as longer treatment exposure and survival may have influenced the observation of late events. Though we accounted for the timing of irAEs by analyzing early and late events separately, residual time-dependent bias cannot be excluded. Given the relatively small sample size of our cohort, these findings require further validation in future multicenter studies with larger cohorts, which may help determine appropriate monitoring intervals and organ-specific risk thresholds for severe irAEs.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that dynamic neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) monitoring may provide a simple and accessible approach for assessing survival outcomes and irAEs risk stratification in patients with immunotherapy. Multivariate analysis confirms that cycle 3 NLR may have potential value for longitudinal monitoring. However, these findings require further validation in prospective studies.

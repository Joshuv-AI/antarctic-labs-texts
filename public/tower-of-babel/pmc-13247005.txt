Scalable and energy efficient hybrid cryptographic framework for IoT security using advanced symmetric and asymmetric techniques
Authors: Bijie Huan, Haiyan Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid growth of the Internet of Things (IoT) demands robust yet lightweight cryptographic solutions to secure sensitive data on resource-constrained devices. This paper introduces the Hybrid Elliptic-AES Encryption (HEAE), a novel cryptographic framework that integrates AES-256’s robust symmetric encryption with Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) for efficient key management. Unlike conventional hybrid approaches, HEAE innovates through a modified F-function with parallel processing, reducing execution time by 26% compared to RSA and 14% compared to DES for 5 MB datasets. Its lightweight design, optimized for IoT, achieves a 19% reduction in energy consumption compared to RSA and 6% compared to DES for 20 MB datasets. HEAE incorporates SHA-256 hashing for data integrity and is compatible with real-world IoT frameworks like LoRaWAN, Zigbee, and MQTT. Demonstrating resilience against chosen plaintext (CPA) and known plaintext (KPA) attacks, HEAE offers a scalable, energy-efficient, and secure solution tailored for IoT ecosystems, ensuring data confidentiality and system reliability.Keywords: IoT security, Energy-efficient, Lightweight cryptography, AES-256, Elliptic curve cryptographySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

A massive network of physically connected devices1, commonly referred to as “things,“2,3 that gather, process4, and share data via the Internet on their own without human assistance is what makes the IoT a paradigm shift in contemporary computing5–7. With embedded systems that include sensors, actuators, and communication modules, these devices which range from basic sensors to complex analytics hardware allow data to flow seamlessly across a variety of applications8, including smart homes, healthcare, industrial automation, and transportation9–11. Physical devices (such as sensors and actuators), connection infrastructure (such as wireless networks like Wi-Fi, Zigbee, or LoRaWAN), and data processing platforms (such as cloud or edge computing systems) are the three main parts of an IoT ecosystem12. Efficient Service Function Chaining in IoT Networks13 highlights the resource constraints it addresses through its lightweight design. By combining these elements, IoT connects billions of devices worldwide, enabling real-time monitoring, automation14, and decision-making through the development of cloud computing and reasonably priced computational resources. Cost-effective NFV orchestration15 emphasizes energy optimization in IoT, a challenge that the proposed approach addresses with a large energy reduction compared to RSA. Applications ranging from precision agriculture to driverless cars have been made possible by this convergence of digital and physical systems16, which has completely transformed industries. Advanced intrusion detection frameworks17 highlight IoT security challenges, which can be addressed through strong encryption. But there are serious problems with the IoT’s quick growth, especially when it comes to protecting the privacy and security of private information sent between devices with limited resources18.

IoT security is still a major worry despite its revolutionary promise because of the networked architecture’s inherent vulnerabilities. IoT devices with limited resources, such as low processing power, memory, and battery life, are more vulnerable to assaults such device manipulation, data breaches, and man-in-the-middle attacks. Different privacy techniques19 emphasize data protection in the IoT, which can be enhanced with AES-256 and ECC. A single exploited vulnerability can have disastrous effects, including smart grid system failures, traffic system compromises that result in accidents, or illegal access to medical devices that could put lives in jeopardy20. Two-factor authentication methods21 enhance IoT security and align with secure key management in this approach. Furthermore, in order to adhere to cybersecurity standards and preserve user confidence, the sensitive nature of IoT data such as industrial telemetry or personal health records requires strong protection. Wi-Fi-based attacks22 highlight IoT vulnerabilities that can be mitigated through strong encryption. Because of their computational complexity (RSA) or antiquated security standards (DES), existing cryptographic systems like RSA and DES sometimes fall short of meeting the combined goals of high security and low resource usage. Even though symmetric encryption algorithms like AES and asymmetric techniques like ECC have gained popularity across a range of industries, their stand-alone use in Internet of Things scenarios frequently lacks resource-constrained environment optimization23. Furthermore, the requirement for lightweight cryptographic frameworks that strike a balance between security, energy efficiency, and scalability has not been sufficiently covered in previous research, especially in real-world IoT frameworks like LoRaWAN, Zigbee, or MQTT24.

By putting forth the Hybrid Elliptic-AES Encryption (HEAE) framework, a cutting-edge cryptographic solution created especially for IoT ecosystems, this study fills these important gaps. Although the use of AES for data encryption and ECC for key management is not wholly novel, HEAE offers important advancements through modifications made to account for IoT limitations. Together, these methods which include lightweight key management with ECC, an improved F-function for AES-256, and parallel processing techniques reduce execution time and energy usage while preserving strong security. The pressing need to create secure, scalable, and energy-efficient cryptographic techniques that can safeguard private IoT data without sacrificing device performance is what spurred this investigation. In contrast to conventional methods, HEAE is made to function well in resource-constrained settings, guaranteeing compatibility with real-world IoT frameworks like MQTT for lightweight messaging protocols, Zigbee for low-power mesh networks, and LoRaWAN for long-range communication. HEAE strikes a balance between computing efficiency and cryptographic strength by utilizing AES-256 for quick and safe symmetric encryption and ECC for lightweight key exchange. Proposed method

The proposed method introduces the HEAE, an advanced cryptographic framework that combines the symmetric encryption strength of the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES-256) with the lightweight key management capabilities of ECC. This integration provides a robust and efficient solution for securing data in IoT environments, where resource constraints and high security are critical concerns. The main components of HEAE are discussed below.

AES-256 for symmetric data encryption: The HEAE framework uses AES-256 to encrypt IoT data blocks. AES-256 is a highly secure algorithm that ensures data confidentiality through multiple rounds of key substitution, permutation, mixing, and addition. The 256-bit key ensures resistance to modern cryptographic attacks while maintaining efficient performance, making it ideal for resource-constrained IoT devices.

ECC for key management: Key management in the HEAE framework uses ECC, which provides security equivalent to traditional RSA methods but with much smaller key sizes. ECC ensures secure key exchanges using elliptic curves in finite fields, enabling devices to compute shared secrets from their private and public keys. This reduces computational overhead and protects AES keys from interception.

The HEAE framework starts by dividing IoT data into fixed-size blocks. Each block is encrypted using AES-256. The symmetric key for AES is then secured through ECC-based key exchange. This combination ensures that both the data and the encryption key remain secure during transmission. The receiver uses the same workflow to decrypt the data. HEAE includes optimizations such as parallel processing to perform encryption and key management tasks simultaneously38, lightweight libraries optimized for the Internet of Things, and efficient key operations to reduce power consumption. The combined power of AES-256 and ECC ensures data confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity. Additionally, secure hashing techniques, such as SHA-256, are integrated into the workflow to verify data integrity and detect tampering during transmission. Advanced encryption standard (AES-256)

AES-256 is a symmetric block cipher that operates on 128-bit data blocks with a 256-bit key, executing 14 transformation rounds as delineated by NIST FIPS 19746. Each round comprises Sub Bytes, ShiftRows, MixColumns, and AddRoundKey operations, using a modified F-function in HEAE that utilizes parallel processing to minimize execution time. HEAE utilizes Cipher Block Chaining (CBC) mode, wherein each plaintext block is XORed with the preceding cipher text block (or an initialization vector for the beginning block) prior to encryption, therefore reducing the susceptibility to pattern recognition attacks characteristic of ECB mode48. The F-function, expressed as , is improved to reduce computational overhead, resulting in a 26% decrease in execution time relative to RSA for 5 MB datasets.

SHA-256 hashing

SHA-256, a component of the SHA-2 family, produces a 256-bit hash value to guarantee data integrity. SHA-256 generates a hash H(M) from an input message M, ensuring that even a minor alteration in M results in a markedly different hash, exhibiting substantial collision resistance56. In HEAE, SHA-256 is utilized to confirm that decrypted data is unaltered, hence bolstering resistance against attacks such as CPA and KPA.

Security analysis

An informal yet organized security analysis of the proposed HEAE framework was conducted to complete the performance evaluation, concentrating on prevalent adversarial models pertinent to IoT systems.

Replay attacks.

Replay attacks.

Every session in HEAE employs session keys generated from the new ECC and a 128-bit random IV for AES-256 in CBC mode. Due to the variability of ciphertext blocks and integrity labels (SHA-256) in each session, attackers are unable to successfully replay intercepted messages undetected, as integrity verification would be compromised.

Man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks.

Man-in-the-middle (MITM) attacks.

The key generation process utilizes elliptic curve cryptography (secp256r1) with temporary key pairs. This ensures forward secrecy and inhibits an active adversary from substituting the keys during the handshake. The incorporation of SHA-256-based message authentication guarantees the detection of any alterations by a potential attacker during transmission.

Side-channel attacks.

Side-channel attacks.

While a comprehensive hardware assessment will be conducted in further research, we observe that both AES and ECC are compatible with established side-channel countermeasures. Specifically, our parallel modified F-function facilitates masking and balancing operations to mitigate leakage via time and power usage. Such measures are conventional in lightweight cryptographic hardware and can be immediately executed in HEAE.

Conclusion

This study introduces the HEAE framework, an innovative cryptographic solution aimed at tackling the security and efficiency issues prevalent in resource-constrained IoT contexts. HEAE’s innovation is in its amalgamation of AES-256 (CBC mode) with Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC, secp256r1, 256-bit key), augmented by a modified F-function utilizing parallel processing and SHA-256 hashing to ensure data integrity. In contrast to previous hybrid methods, HEAE enhances computational performance and energy economy by utilizing parallelized operations and streamlined key management, resulting in substantial advancements over both conventional and lightweight cryptographic techniques. This framework is designed for actual IoT implementations, guaranteeing compatibility with protocols such as LoRaWAN, Zigbee, and MQTT, and provides tangible advantages such as decreased execution time and energy usage, rendering it suitable for applications in smart homes, healthcare, and industrial systems.

The efficacy of HEAE was examined by comprehensive simulations utilizing iFogSim, which modeled IoT networks with diverse dataset sizes 5 to 20 MB and computational limitations. The examined key parameters encompass execution time, energy consumption, and scalability, with comparisons made against conventional standards RSA, DES, lightweight ciphers PRESENT, SIMON, and contemporary hybrid approaches. The simulations employed a standardized configuration featuring a 1.5 GHz processor, 4 GB RAM, and the secp256r1 curve for ECC, thereby guaranteeing reproducibility of results. Supplementary metrics, including resistance to CPA and KPA attacks, were evaluated to verify the security robustness of HEAE. The principal findings of this study are encapsulated as follows:

HEAE realizes a 26% decrease in execution time relative to RSA, 14% compared to DES, 15% compared to PRESENT, and 8% compared to SIMON for 5 MB datasets, attributable to its parallelized F-function and enhanced AES-256 implementation.

This study, despite its improvements, has drawbacks. The assessment predominantly depends on simulations conducted with iFogSim, which, although comprehensive, may not entirely reflect real-world hardware discrepancies. Furthermore, comparisons are confined to a selection of lightweight ciphers (PRESENT, SIMON), omitting stream ciphers such as Grain or Trivium. Subsequent research will rectify these limitations by: (a) performing hardware-based energy assessments utilizing Raspberry Pi 4 and FPGA implementations to corroborate simulation outcomes, (b) extending comparisons to encompass stream ciphers and novel IoT security protocols, and (c) refining HEAE for ultra-low-power devices, such as microcontrollers, to enhance its applicability.

Abstract
. This paper studies the tensor product of flat cotorsion modules. Let R R and S S be k k -algebras. We prove that both R R -module M M and S S -module N N are flat cotorsion modules if and only if M ⊗ k N M\otimes_{k}N is a flat cotorsion R ⊗ k S R\otimes_{k}S -module. Based on this conclusion, we provide a lower bound for the global cotorsion dimension of the tensor product algebra R ⊗ k S R\otimes_{k}S under appropriate conditions.

1.
introduction

Cotorsion pairs are important concepts in abelian categories and triangulated categories. First appeared in the literature
[
22
]
, they are closely related to model structures
[
12
,
13
]
and have significant applications in homological algebra and representation theory, such as in
[
15
,
3
]
. A cotorsion pair consists of two subcategories that are orthogonal with respect to the Ext functor.

The flat cover conjecture was first proposed by Enochs
[
7
]
. This conjecture states that every module has a flat cover. Enochs also proved that this is equivalent to every module having a flat precover. Let
R
be a ring, and
\mathcal{X}
be a class of
R
-modules. An
\mathcal{X}
-precover of an
R
-module
M

[
7
]
is a morphism
f:X\rightarrow M
with
X\in\mathcal{X}
, such that for any morphism
g:Y\rightarrow M
with
Y\in\mathcal{X}
, there exists a morphism
h:Y\rightarrow X
satisfying
fh=g
. Dually, one can define an
\mathcal{X}
-preenvelope. Cotorsion modules, introduced by Enochs in
[
8
]
, generalize the concept of cotorsion groups defined by Harrison
[
14
]
and Fuchs
[
11
]
. Enochs’ definition of the cotorsion modules differs from that of Matlis in
[
17
]
. Bican, El Bashir, and Enochs proved in
[
4
]
that the class of flat modules and the class of cotorsion modules form a complete cotorsion pair. This result completely resolves the famous “flat cover conjecture”. Moreover, the core of the flat cotorsion pair, i.e.the intersection of the flat class and the cotorsion class (also known as flat cotorsion modules)-exhibits good homological properties
[
16
]
, similar to projective and injective objects. In this context, Mao and Ding introduced the concept of cotorsion dimension in
[
19
]
, whitch measures how far a ring is from being a perfect ring. In
[
19
]
, a lower bound for the cotorsion dimension of a ring is given, and the change of cotorsion dimension under ring extensions is discussed.

The study of homological dimensions in tensor product algebras has a long history. As early as the 1950s, Eilenberg, Auslander, and Nakayama published a series of works on the homological dimensions of tensor product algebras in the journal
Nagoya Mathematical Journal

[
1
,
2
,
9
,
10
]
. Eilenberg considered the problem of homological dimensions over tensor product algebras to be “surprisingly difficult”
[
10
]
. Despite this, many important conclusions about the homology and representation theory of tensor product algebras have been established. For instance, in recent years, the higher-order representation theory of tensor product algebras has attracted scholars’ attention
[
20
,
21
]
. In their foundational contribution to homological dimensions, Eilenberg, Rosenberg, and Zelinsky established critical inequalities for projective dimensions in tensor products: Let
M
and
N
be modules over
k
-algebras
R
and
S
. The projective dimension of their tensor product satisfies

\mathsf{pd}(M)+\mathsf{wpd}(N)\leq\mathsf{pd}(M\otimes N)\leq\mathsf{pd}(M)+\mathsf{pd}(N),

where
\mathsf{wpd}

and
\mathsf{pd}
denote the flat dimension and projective dimension, respectively. Zhou further proved in
[
24
]
that under certain conditions on
k
-algebras
R
and
S
, the inequality
\mathsf{l.gl.dim}\hskip 0.72229pt(R\otimes S)\geq\mathsf{l.gl.dim}\hskip 0.72229pt(R)+\mathsf{l.gl.dim}\hskip 0.72229pt(S)
holds, where
\mathsf{l.gl.dim}\hskip 0.72229pt
denotes the left global dimension of the algebra. Since flat-cotorsion modules share similar properties with projective-injective modules, it is natural to consider the cotorsion dimension of tensor product algebras. The main results of this paper are as follows:

Theorem A
: Let
M
be an
R
-module and
N
be an
S
-module. Then
M\otimes N
is a flat-cotorsion
R\otimes S
-module if and only if both
M
and
N
are flat-cotorsion modules.

Theorem B
: Let
M
be an
R
-module and
N
be an
S
-module. The following statements are equivalent:

(P1)

If
\mathsf{cd}(M)=1
,
\mathsf{cd}(N)=1
, then
\mathsf{cd}(M\otimes N)=2
.

(P2)

If
\mathsf{cd}(M)=m
,
\mathsf{cd}(N)=n
, where
m,~n\geq 1
is a positive integer, then
\mathsf{cd}(M\otimes N)=n+m
.

In particular, when (P1) was established, then
l.cot.D(R\otimes S)\geq l.cot.D(R)+l.cot.D(S)
.

The structure of this paper is as follows. The second section recalls related concepts and important conclusions. In section 3, the tensor product property of cotorsion is discussed. In section 4, we study the cotorsion dimension of tensor product algebras.

Definition 2.5
.

[

A module M is said to be cotorsion if

Definition 2.6

[

3.
TENSOR PRODUCTS OF COTORSION MODULES

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
F
be an
R
-module and
H
be an
S
-module. Then
H\otimes F
is a flat
S\otimes R
-module if and only if
F
is a flat
R
-module and
H
is a flat
S
-module.

Proof.

Necessity: Assume conversely: If
H
is not a flat
S
-module, there exists a right
S
-module
N
such that
\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{S}(N,H)\neq 0
. Applying Proposition
2.3
to the triple
(N_{k\otimes S},{}_{k}R_{R},{}_{S-R}(H\otimes_{k}F))
, since

\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{k}(N,R)=0=\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{R}(R,H\otimes_{k}F),

we have:

\displaystyle\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{S\otimes R}(N\otimes R,H\otimes F)

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{k\otimes S}(N,R\otimes_{R}(H\otimes F))

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{k\otimes S}(N,(R\otimes_{R}F)\otimes H)

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{k\otimes S}(N,F\otimes H)

\displaystyle\cong\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{k\otimes S}(N,\oplus_{\lambda}H)\quad(\text{where }\lambda=\mathsf{dim_{K}}F)

\displaystyle=\oplus_{\lambda}\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{S}(N,H)\neq 0.

Since
H\otimes F
is a flat
S\otimes R
-module,
\mbox{Tor}_{1}^{S\otimes R}(N\otimes R,H\otimes F)=0
, leading to a contradiction. Hence,
H
is flat.

Since
S\otimes R\cong R\otimes S
and
H\otimes F\cong F\otimes H
,
F\otimes H
is a flat
R\otimes S
-module. Similarly,
F
is flat by symmetry.

Sufficiency: Follows directly from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
M
is a flat

-module,

is a flat

-module,

is a cotorsion

-module, and

Proof.

By Proposition

\mbox{Ext}_{R\otimes S}^{n}(M\otimes N,M_{1}\otimes N_{1})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\mbox{Ext}_{R}^{p}(M,M_{1})\otimes\mbox{Ext}_{S}^{q}(N,N_{1}).

For

, at least one of

or

is positive. Since
\mbox{Ext}_{R}^{p}(M,M_{1})=0
or
\mbox{Ext}_{S}^{q}(N,N_{1})=0

∎

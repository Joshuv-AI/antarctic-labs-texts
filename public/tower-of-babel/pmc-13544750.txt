Bilateral forgotten double-j stents with high-grade encrustation (fecal grade 4–5): renal loss and staged multimodal management – a case report
Authors: Youssef Maachi, Zinedine Omar, Amine Slaoui, Tariq Karmouni, Abdelatif Koutani, Khalid Elkhader
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importance:Forgotten ureteral double-J (JJ) stents with high-grade encrustation (FECal grade 4–5) represent a severe and preventable urological complication. We report a case illustrating the catastrophic consequences of a failure of 24-month follow-up, culminating in irreversible renal loss and complex three-stage contralateral management.Case presentation:A 65-year-old man (ASA II) presented 24 months after bilateral JJ stent placement (Boston Scientific, 7 Fr, 26 cm) for obstructive pyelocalyceal stones, without follow-up. The presenting complaint was left renal colic. The workup revealed a serum creatinine of 97 µmol/L (eGFR ~80 mL/min/1.73 m2), a CRP of 20 mg/L, a WBC count of 8000/mm3, and a positive urine culture for Klebsiella pneumoniae. CT demonstrated bilaterally calcified stents and an atrophic left kidney. Bilateral percutaneous nephrostomies were performed; DMSA scintigraphy confirmed left renal function below 10% (FECal Grade 4, left; FECal Grade 5, diffuse right).Clinical discussion:Left-sided management comprised laparoscopic nephrectomy with same-session cystolithotripsy; the stent was deliberately transected upon encountering resistance during extraction (stay: 5 days). Right-sided management required three sequential stages: cystolithotripsy, rigid ureteroscopy (three weeks later), and planned flexible ureteroscopy (fURS) for a 15 mm proximal coil calcification and three small inferior calyceal stones in a solitary kidney. Creatinine remained stable throughout. No postoperative complications were recorded.Conclusion:This case underscores the devastating consequences of forgotten stent syndrome. Individualized multimodal planning and the principle of never forcing extraction are critical surgical tenets. Every institution performing JJ stent placement must implement a stent-tracking registry, automated reminders, and structured patient education at insertion – measures that would have prevented this outcome entirely.

Introduction

Ureteral double-J (JJ) stents are among the most widely used devices in urological practice[1,2]. Forgotten stent syndrome affects 0.9–12% of patients, with loss to follow-up, socioeconomic barriers, and absence of institutional reminder systems as principal contributing factors[3]. When retained beyond the recommended interval, encrustation risk reaches 76–90% after 12 months and approaches 100% beyond 18 months[3]. Severity is stratified using the FECal classification (Grades 1–5) (Fig. 1), with Grades 4–5 denoting diffuse calcification requiring complex multimodal intervention[4]. Consequences include renal impairment, urosepsis, and nephrectomy[3,5]. Bilateral presentation is exceedingly rare, carrying a compounded risk of irreversible bilateral renal damage. Management must be individualized according to encrustation pattern, stone burden, and residual renal function, as no universally accepted algorithm exists[6]. We report a 65-year-old man (ASA II) with bilateral forgotten JJ stents retained for 24 months, presenting with extreme functional asymmetry: an atrophic left kidney (FECal Grade 4, <10% function) requiring laparoscopic nephrectomy with same-session cystolithotripsy, and a preserved right kidney (FECal Grade 5 diffuse) requiring a three-stage endourological approach. This case is reported in line with the SCARE criteria[7].

Case presentation

A 65-year-old man (ASA II, no significant comorbidities) underwent bilateral JJ stent placement (Boston Scientific, 7 Fr, 26 cm, polyurethane) for obstructive pyelocalyceal stones (11 mm right, 9 mm left) and was then lost to follow-up for 24 months.

He presented with left renal colic. Examination revealed stable vital signs and mild left flank tenderness. Workup revealed a serum creatinine of 97 µmol/L (eGFR ~80 mL/min/1.73 m2, CKD-EPI), CRP 20 mg/L, WBC 8,000/mm3, and a urine culture positive for Klebsiella pneumoniae, for which targeted antibiotic therapy was initiated. CT demonstrated bilateral calcified stents with a markedly atrophic left kidney (Fig. 2A and B). Bilateral percutaneous nephrostomy was performed. DMSA scintigraphy confirmed left renal function below 10% (right, 90%) (Fig. 2C). FECal classification: left Grade 4; right Grade 5, diffuse. Surgical management

Phase 1 – left side: laparoscopic nephrectomy with same-session cystolithotripsy

Laparoscopic nephrectomy was selected given left renal function <10% and FECal grade 4 encrustation. Upon encountering resistance during extraction, the stent was deliberately transected at its mid-portion (Fig. 2D). Cystoscopy identified dense distal coil encrustation (Fig. 2E); holmium:YAG laser cystolithotripsy achieved complete extraction (Fig. 2F). This same-session hybrid approach eliminated both the infected atrophic unit and the intravesical foreign body in a single anesthetic event. The hospital stay was 5 days, and creatinine remained stable.

Phase 2 – right side: staged three-step approach (FECal Grade 5, Diffuse)

Four months later, CT confirmed triple-level encrustation: a massive intravesical distal coil (~6 cm), the ureteral body, and the proximal renal coil (Fig. 3A and 3B). Single-stage extraction and ESWL were excluded on anatomical and stone-density grounds. A staged distal-to-proximal strategy was selected. Sterile urine was confirmed before each stage. Stage 3 (planned): Post-ureteroscopy CT confirmed a 15 mm proximal coil calcification and three small inferior calyceal stones. Flexible ureteroscopy (fURS) was selected as the first-line approach; for a 15 mm burden on a solitary kidney, fURS provides established efficacy while avoiding percutaneous parenchymal risk. Discussion

Principal findings

A mildly symptomatic presentation concealed a bilateral anatomical catastrophe. Encrustation reaches 100% beyond 18 months[3], mandating CT and DMSA scintigraphy whenever stent dwell time exceeds 12 months, regardless of clinical stability. The identification of Klebsiella pneumoniae reinforced the need for targeted antibiotherapy before any intervention[6]; sterile urine, confirmed before each right-sided stage, contributed to the absence of complications.

All completed stages achieved their objectives without Clavien–Dindo Grade I or higher complications. Creatinine remained normal throughout, consistent with 88–100% reported in published multimodal series[5,8].

Treatment must integrate differential renal function, not FECal grade alone: radically opposite strategies were warranted despite both stents being high-grade – ablative surgery for the nonfunctional left, staged reconstruction for the preserved right. The 24-month loss to follow-up reflects a systemic failure – an absent registry, no written deadline, no automated reminder, and socioeconomic barriers – rather than isolated patient noncompliance.

Encrustation mechanisms and clinical implications

Encrustation progresses from biofilm formation within days to accelerated crystallization after 6–12 weeks on polyurethane surfaces[9]. Klebsiella pneumoniae’s urease activity alkalinizes urine, promoting calcium phosphate and struvite deposition[9]. The functional asymmetry, despite identical dwell time, likely reflects pre-existing left parenchymal vulnerability, differential urinary pH, and differences in encrustation patterns: Grade 4 on the left suggesting complete occlusion with pressure-induced atrophy, versus Grade 5 on the right potentially preserving partial drainage. Independent functional evaluation of each renal unit is therefore essential.

Treatment strategy rationale

The nephrectomy threshold – split function <10–15% with active infection–was unequivocally met on the left. Juliebø-Jones et al recommend laparoscopic nephrectomy for non-functional kidneys when anatomically feasible[6]. Same-session cystolithotripsy eliminated both the infectious nidus and intravesical foreign body in a single anesthetic event. Deliberate stent transection operationalizes the principle of never forcing extraction; Al-Hajjaj et al reported ureteral avulsion in 2/25 patients from forced extraction, requiring major reconstruction[5], a scenario explicitly avoided here.

For the right side, the sequential distal-to-proximal strategy follows anatomical logic, each stage enabling the next. Flexible ureteroscopy achieves success rates of 65–85% for renal stone burdens up to and beyond 2 cm, with ureteral strictures reported in up to 10% of cases, supporting the choice of adequate interstage intervals to minimize urothelial injury[10]. Agarwal et al reported 91.7% success with staged multimodal approaches; all major complications occurred when staging intervals were shortened or when single-stage extraction was attempted under resistance[8]. While PCNL is conventionally recommended for larger stone burdens, the 15 mm proximal coil in the context of a solitary kidney places this case well within the established efficacy range of flexible ureteroscopy. fURS was selected as the first-line approach, avoiding percutaneous access and its inherent risk of parenchymal hemorrhage to the sole functioning renal unit. Bonfitto et al reported open pyelolithotomy for comparable coralliform calcification after 24 months; our fURS-based approach represents a further evolution toward minimized morbidity in a high-stakes functional context[11].

Recent case reports published in this journal further illustrate the spectrum of complexity encountered with calcified retained stents and the need for individualized, multimodal strategies[12]. The evolution from open surgical approaches, as described by Mejri et al, in which a stent retained for 10 years required cystotomy and pyelotomy, toward laparoscopic and endoscopic techniques reflects the progressive refinement of management in this field[13].

Aponte et al describe a novel minimally invasive technique for distally calcified stents that achieved 100% success, representing an alternative for Stage 1[14].

Comparison with published literature

Our case achieves 100% stage-by-stage success with no major complications, comparing favorably with Al-Hajjaj et al[5] (88% success, 24% complications) and Agarwal et al[8] (91.7% success, 16.7% complications). It is important to note that Table 2 compares heterogeneous study designs–a single case report, small series, and a systematic review–which preclude direct statistical comparison and warrant cautious interpretation. The two ureteral avulsions reported by Al-Hajjaj et al[5] both resulted from forced extraction, a scenario explicitly avoided in our approach through deliberate stent transection. Aponte et al[14] describe a novel minimally invasive technique specifically for distally calcified stents, achieving 100% success; their approach represents an alternative for Stage 1 of right-sided management. Proposed treatment algorithm

Based on this case and the current EAU Guidelines[6,15], management follows a structured approach: sepsis control with urgent drainage and targeted antibiotics first; DMSA or MAG3 functional assessment to guide nephrectomy if function is <10–15%; CT to define the distribution of encrustation. Treatment is adapted accordingly: cystolithotripsy or ureteroscopy for limited disease, PCNL for large or complex burdens, fURS is preferred for moderate burdens (≤20 mm) and is systematically favored in solitary-kidney contexts; staged procedures for diffuse involvement. Stent removal must always avoid force; if resistance is encountered, fragmentation or transection is required.

Prevention strategies

All interventions were preventable. SMS reminders reduced forgotten stent rates from 12.8% to 2.1%[16]; smartphone tracking reduced overdue removal time from 20 to 3.5 days, with zero loss to follow-up[17]; a low-cost Google Drive registry proved effective even in resource-limited settings[18]. In the present case, the absence of a registry, no written deadline, and no automated reminders reflect a systemic institutional failure – not isolated patient noncompliance.

Study limitations

This is a single case report; no statistical inference is possible. Selection bias is present due to the tertiary-center setting. Stage 3 fURS is pending; data on long-term functional outcomes for the solitary kidney remain incomplete. No quality-of-life or cost data were collected.

Conclusion

This case exemplifies the devastating consequences of forgotten bilateral JJ-stent syndrome: irreversible left renal loss (FECal Grade 4) and a four-intervention course for the right side (FECal Grade 5, diffuse). It demonstrates the feasibility of a same-session hybrid laparoscopic-endoscopic approach for nonfunctioning kidneys, and validates a rational three-stage sequential protocol for diffuse encrustation, with fURS selected over percutaneous access for Stage 3 given the 15-mm burden and solitary-kidney context. Deliberate stent transection was critical to achieving zero ureteral avulsions. Targeted antibiotic therapy and confirmation of sterile urine before each endoscopic stage proved essential.

Prevention remains the overriding priority. Electronic registries, automated reminders, and structured education at insertion must be institutional standards. Prospective multicenter studies using standardized FECal classification, Clavien-Dindo grading, and long-term renal function endpoints are urgently needed.

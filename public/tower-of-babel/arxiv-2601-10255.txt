An Exact Energy Conservation Law for Magneto-Optical Nanoparticles
Jorge Olmos-Trigo

Abstract
Energy conservation imposes fundamental bounds on the polarizabilities of nanoparticles (NPs). While such bounds are well established for isotropic and bianisotropic NPs, they remain unexplored for magneto-optical NPs. Here, we derive the exact energy-conservation law governing the electric and magnetic dipolar response of axially symmetric magneto-optical NPs under general illumination conditions and arbitrary external magnetic fields. Two central results follow from energy conservation: (i) purely magneto-optical scattering, where the non-magnetic polarizability vanishes, is fundamentally forbidden, and (ii) strong magneto-optical scattering regimes, in which the magneto-optical polarizability dominates, are intriguingly allowed.

For such NPs, the electromagnetic response is fully described by four scalar polarizabilities: the ordinary electric (

Yet, even in this scenario, an exact energy-conservation law remains elusive.

In this work, we derive such a conservation law, which sets strict constraints on the polarizabilities of magneto-optical NPs. Notably, these energy-conservation bounds hold for NPs made of both lossless and lossy materials, and must be met in any modeling of magneto-optical NPs.
Furthermore, energy conservation reveals two fundamental results: i) if

\alpha_{\mathrm{OR},\beta}

The polarizability tensor of magneto-optical NPs.
— We consider an axially-symmetric NP made of a homogeneous material with scalar permittivity
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{C}
embedded in an otherwise homogeneous and isotropic medium with real permittivity
\varepsilon_{\rm{h}}
, refractive index
n_{\rm{h}}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\rm{h}}}
. In the presence of a static external magnetic field
{\bf B}_{\text{ext}}
, the scalar permittivity of the NP becomes a
3\times 3
tensor whose components satisfy
\varepsilon_{ij}({\bf B}_{\text{ext}})=\varepsilon_{ji}(-{\bf B}_{\text{ext}})
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
{\bf B}_{\rm{ext}}=\pm B_{0}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\rm{z}}
, as shown in Fig.

In addition to
\mathbf{B}_{\rm{ext}}

\{\mathbf{E},\mathbf{H}\}
impinging on the magneto-optical NP.
This incident field induces an electric dipole

\mathbf{p}=\varepsilon_{\rm{h}}\varepsilon_{0}{\overline{\alpha}}_{\rm{E}}\mathbf{E}

and a magnetic dipole

\mathbf{m}={\overline{\alpha}}_{\rm{M}}\mathbf{H}
,
where
{\overline{\alpha}}_{\rm{E}}
and
{\overline{\alpha}}_{\rm{M}}
are the electric and magnetic polarizability tensors in the presence of
\mathbf{B}_{\rm{ext}}

As is often done in the literature

, we can embody all the previous information into a
6\times 6
polarizability tensor
{\tilde{\alpha}}
, namely,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}{\bf p}/\epsilon_{h}\epsilon_{0}\\
{\bf m}\end{pmatrix}=\tilde{\alpha}\begin{pmatrix}{\bf E}\\
{\bf H}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\overline{\alpha}}_{\rm{E}}&{\overline{0}}_{3x3}\\
{\overline{0}}_{3x3}&{\overline{\alpha}}_{\rm{M}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\bf E}\\
{\bf H}\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where

\displaystyle{\overline{\alpha}}_{\beta}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}&\mp{\rm i}\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}&0\\
\pm{\rm i}\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}&\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}&0\\
0&0&\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta}\end{array}\right).

The magneto-optical polarizability

{\bf B}_{\rm{ext}}=0

Accordingly, the polarizability tensor we consider in Eq. (

The energy conservation for magneto-optical NPs.—
The energy conservation law for dipolar NPs was introduced by Belov
et al.
in 2003
(
4
, Eq. (10))
. Such a law be compactly written in our notation as:

\frac{\tilde{\alpha}-\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}}{2{\rm i}}\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{\alpha},

(6)

where “
†
” is the hermitian conjugate. The left-hand side of Eq. (
6
) is proportional to extinction, while the right-hand side is proportional to scattering
6
.
In the absence of absorption, the inequality “
\geq
” in Eq. (
6
) becomes an equality “
=
”.
Now, Eq. (
6
) can be conveniently expressed in terms of
a positive semidefinite (PSD) hermitian matrix
\mathscr{\tilde{A}}=\mathscr{\tilde{A}}^{\dagger}
as:

\displaystyle\mathscr{\tilde{A}}\geq 0,\quad\text{where}\quad\mathscr{\tilde{A}}=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}-\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}}{2{\rm i}}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{\alpha}.

(7)

To elucidate the role of the external magnetic field
{\bf B}_{\rm{ext}}
in the conservation of energy for magneto-optical NPs, we expand the right-hand side of Eq. (
7
) using Eq. (
An Exact Energy Conservation Law for Magneto-Optical Nanoparticles
).
After a little bit of algebra (see Appendix
A
), we arrive at:

\mathscr{\tilde{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{E}}&{\overline{0}}_{3x3}\\
{\overline{0}}_{3x3}&\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{M}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{where}\quad\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{J}_{\beta}&\mp{\rm i}\mathscr{O}_{\beta}&0\\
\pm{\rm i}\mathscr{O}_{\beta}&\mathscr{J}_{\beta}&0\\
0&0&\mathscr{T}_{\beta}\end{pmatrix}.

(8)

In Eq. (
8
), we have introduced the real-valued quantities:

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta})-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|^{2}+|\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}|^{2}\right),

(9)

\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta})-\frac{k^{3}}{3\pi}\Re(\alpha^{*}_{\rm{OR},\beta}\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}),

(10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta})-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta}|^{2}.

(11)

Here,
\Im(z)
and
\Re(z)
denote the imaginary and real parts of any complex number
z
. A few comments are in order. As Eqs. (
9
)-(
11
) show, the electric dipole
{\bf p}
and magnetic dipole
{\bf m}
of magneto-optical NPs do not couple in the conservation of energy. In other words, energy conservation, as expressed in Eq. (
6
), applies independently to each
3\times 3
dipolar block, just as in the case of isotropic NPs sustaining electric and magnetic dipolar modes
6
. This behavior stands in sharp contrast to that of bi-anisotropic NPs, where the electric and magnetic polarizabilities intertwine to ensure energy conservation
19
.

Having noted these important points, we turn our attention back to
\mathscr{\tilde{A}}
, introduced in Eq. (
8
). The convenience of defining this
6\times 6
tensor becomes now evident: if
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{E}}=\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{M}}=0
, then the magneto-optical NP is lossless (and the converse statement also holds). In this lossless regime, we have
\mathscr{J}_{\beta}=\mathscr{O}_{\beta}=\mathscr{T}_{\beta}=0
, and hence, the polarizabilities necessarily satisfy:

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|^{2}+|\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}|^{2}\right),

(12)

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k^{3}}{3\pi}\Re(\alpha^{*}_{\rm{OR},\beta}\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}),

(13)

\displaystyle\Im(\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta}|^{2}.

(14)

Equations (
12
)–(
14
) constitute the first important result of this work. Any physically consistent polarizability model must satisfy Eqs. (
12
)–(
14
) for lossless magneto-optical NPs.
As a sanity check, we note that in the isotropic setting, where no external magnetic field is applied (
\mathbf{B}_{\rm ext}=0
) and the magneto-optical polarizabilities vanish (
\alpha_{\rm MO,\beta}=0
), the well-known energy conservation relations for lossless isotropic dipoles are recovered

:
\Im(\alpha_{\rm e})=(k^{3}/6\pi)\,|\alpha_{\rm e}|^{2}
and
\Im(\alpha_{\rm m})=(k^{3}/6\pi)\,|\alpha_{\rm m}|^{2}
. Here
\alpha_{\rm e}
and
\alpha_{\rm m}
are the electric and magnetic polarizabilities for
{\bf B}_{\rm{ext}}=0
, respectively

;
32
.

In the following, we consider the general case imposed by
\mathscr{\tilde{A}}\geq 0
.

To determine the general constraints that
\mathscr{\tilde{A}}\geq 0
imposes on the polarizabilities

\alpha_{\rm OR,\beta}
,
\alpha_{\rm{M}O,\beta}
, and
\alpha_{\rm IN,\beta}
, we apply the extended Sylvester’s criterion, which states that a a Hermitian matrix is PSD if and only if all of its principal minors are nonnegative
15
.
Since
\mathscr{\tilde{A}}
is, by construction, block-diagonal, it is PSD if and only if both

\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{E}}
and
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{M}}
are PSD.
Accordingly, it suffices to apply the extended Sylvester’s criterion independently to each
3\times 3
dipolar block,
requiring that all their principal minors are non-negative.
Since
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\mathrm{E}}
is a block-diagonal matrix incorporating an in-plane complex-rotation block, applying the extended Sylvester criterion straightforwardly yields the inequalities:
\mathscr{J}_{\mathrm{E}}\geq|\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{E}}|\geq 0
and
\mathscr{T}_{\mathrm{E}}\geq 0
.

One could apply the extended Sylvester’s criterion to
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{M}}
as we have just done for
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{E}}
.
However,
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{M}}
and
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{E}}
share the same algebraic form; hence, it can be shown that the conclusions obtained for
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{E}}
also apply to
\mathscr{{\overline{A}}}_{\rm{M}}
under
\rm{E}\rightarrow\rm{M}
.

Accordingly, we can summarize our findings on energy conservation for magneto-optical NPs as follows:

Any magneto-optical NP satisfies
\mathscr{J}_{\beta}\geq|\mathscr{O}_{\beta}|\geq 0
and
\mathscr{T}_{\beta}\geq 0
for both
\beta=\rm{E},\rm{M}
. Thus, if
\mathscr{J}_{\beta}=0
then
\mathscr{O}_{\beta}=0
. The converse statement does not hold. If the magneto-optical NP is lossless, then
\mathscr{\tilde{A}}=0
, and thus, Eqs. (
12
)-(
14
) need to be simultaneously fulfilled for both
\beta=\rm{E},\rm{M}
.

Importantly, our analytical results on energy conservation are not only crucial for physically consistent magneto-optical modelling, they also enable us to address a central open question in magneto-optics. This is: is does energy conservation preclude purely magneto-optical dipole scattering?
In other words, Can a magneto-optical NP exhibit
\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}\neq 0
with
\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}=0
?
It should be highlighted that this magneto-optical response is not only conceptually striking but also actively sought after in the community as it would give rise, for instance, to effective magnetic monolopes
27
.
In this regard, it is also important to notice that the standard magneto-optically techniques, such as the Faraday rotation
13
;
17
, Magneto-optical Kerr effects
20
;
46
, and Magnetic Circular Dichroism
43
;
12
, have been ultimately designed to detect the presence of
\alpha_{\rm{MO},{\rm{E}}}
and
\alpha_{\rm{MO},{\rm{M}}}
in the scattered electromagnetic field of magneto-optically active NPs.

However, our recent result given by
\mathscr{J}_{\beta}\geq 0
preclude the existence of purely magneto-optical responses conclusively: if
\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}=0
, then necessarily
\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}=0
for both
\beta=\rm{E},\rm{M}
dipolar channels. This general finding follows directly from the non-negativity of the squared moduli of
\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}
.

To date, however, this finding has been overlooked in the literature due to the widespread practice of approximating the tensor
{\overline{\alpha}}_{\beta}
given in Eq. (
An Exact Energy Conservation Law for Magneto-Optical Nanoparticles
) to the first-order on the external magnetic field
{\bf B}_{\rm{ext}}

;
36
;
25
;
37
;
26
;
45
;
11
.
In this (so-called) linear regime in
{\bf B}_{\rm{ext}}
,
\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}
does not present magneto-optical terms (
\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}\equiv\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta})
and
|\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}|^{2}
is omitted in Eq. (
9
), namely,

\mathscr{J}_{\beta}\overbrace{\approx}^{\text{Linear regime}}\Im\{\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta}\}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta}|^{2}.

This common approximation
makes Eq. (
10
) the only relation involving
\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}
.
As a result, one might infer from Eq. (
10
) that a purely magneto-optical response is possible when
\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}\equiv\alpha_{\rm{IN},\beta}=0
, provided
\Re\{\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}\}\neq 0
.
Such an inference is incorrect and brings us to the following key result of this work:

NPs cannot be purely magneto-optical (
\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}=0
,
\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}\neq 0
) without violating energy conservation. This general result holds for any material (lossless or lossy), electromagnetic size of the nanoparticle and strength and direction of the external magnetic field
{\bf B}_{\rm{ext}}
.

The condition
\alpha_{\rm OR,\beta}=0
forcing
\alpha_{\rm MO,\beta}=0
mimics the behavior of chiral NPs
19
.
In that work, it is shown that energy conservation also requires the chiral polarizability to vanish if either the electric or magnetic polarizability is zero
2
. Analogously, the Tellegen polarizability vanishes if the electric or magnetic polarizability is identically zero
44
.
Taken together, we can state that energy conservation forbids purely bianisotropic (chiral and Tellegen) and magneto-optical responses at the single-particle level.

At this point, we note that the previous energy-conservation constraint does not necessarily require
|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|>|\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}|
, even though this inequality might be typically assumed in magneto-optics. We may then ask: does energy conservation, as expressed by Eq. (
8
), preclude
strong
magneto-optics? In other words, is it possible to have
|\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}|\gg|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|>0
?

Strong Magneto-Optical Dipole Scattering.
— Let us write the first Sylverster principal minor
\mathscr{J}_{\beta}\geq 0
as:

\Im\{\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}\}-\frac{k^{3}}{3\pi}|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|^{2}\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}|^{2}-|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|^{2}\right).

(15)

From Eq. (
15
), we immediately obtain the threshold that
\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}
must satisfy in order to achieve
|\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}|^{2}>|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|^{2}
:

3\pi\Im\{\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}\}>k^{3}|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|^{2}.

(16)

Equation (
16
) establishes a fundamental requirement for any single magneto-optical NP to reach
|\alpha_{\rm{MO},\beta}|>|\alpha_{\rm{OR},\beta}|
for both
\beta=\rm{E}
, and
\beta=\rm{M}
. We stress that this inequality is perfectly compatible with the conservation of energy.

In conclusion, we have derived the energy conservation law for axially-symmetric magneto-optical NPs described by electric and magnetic dipolar modes. This law is valid for both lossless and lossy materials, general incident illumination conditions, and external magnetic-field strengths, making it relevant for any scientific area dealing with the interaction of electromagnetic fields and magneto-optically active NPs.

Importantly, our energy conservation expressions yield two key results: pure magneto-optical dipolar scattering (
\alpha_{\rm OR}=0
,
\alpha_{\rm MO}\neq 0
) is precluded, while strong magneto-optical dipole scattering (
|\alpha_{\rm MO}|\gg|\alpha_{\rm OR}|>0
) is not.
These results not only establish fundamental limits in magneto-optics but also pave the way for realizing NPs exhibiting strong magneto-optical light scattering at the nanoscale.

Finally, we should highlight that the constraints
\mathscr{J}_{\beta}\geq|\mathscr{O}_{\beta}|\geq 0
and
\mathscr{T}_{\beta}\geq 0
must be satisfied by the electric and magnetic dipolar coefficients of the magneto-optical Mie solution. Since no exact analytical expressions exist for these coefficients in the presence of an external magnetic field, these bounds impose strict conditions that any physically admissible solution must fulfill, thereby guiding the derivation of the exact magneto-optical Mie scattering coefficients.

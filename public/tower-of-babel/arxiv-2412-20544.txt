Relative Langlands duality for 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 2 ​ n + 1 | 2 ​ n ) {\mathfrak{osp}}(2n+1|2n)
Alexander Braverman, Michael Finkelberg, David Kazhdan, Roman Travkin

Abstract
. We establish an S S -duality converse to the one of [ BFT ] ; this is also a case of a twisted version of the relative Langlands duality of [ BZSV ] . Namely, we prove that the S S -dual of SO ( 2 ​ n + 1 ) × Sp ( 2 ​ n ) ↻ ℂ + 2 ​ n + 1 ⊗ ℂ − 2 ​ n {\mathop{\operatorname{\rm SO}}}(2n+1)\times{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(2n)\circlearrowright{\mathbb{C}}^{2n+1}_{+}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}_{-} is the symplectic mirabolic space Sp ( 2 ​ n ) × Sp ( 2 ​ n ) ↻ T ∗ ​ Sp ( 2 ​ n ) × ℂ − 2 ​ n {\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(2n)\times{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(2n)\circlearrowright T^{*}{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(2n)\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}_{-} (note that due to the anomaly, the dual of the second factor Sp ( 2 ​ n ) {\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(2n) is metaplectic dual, i.e. Sp ( 2 ​ n ) {\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(2n) ). We also formulate the corresponding global conjecture, which describes explicitly the categorical theta-correspondence on the Langlands dual side.

1.2.
The non-cotangent case

The construction of
\mathcal{A}_{G,\mathbf{N}}
should in principle make sense for any smooth affine symplectic
G
-variety
\mathbf{M}
.
However, when
\mathbf{M}
is not of cotangent type some extra care is needed. This is discussed in detail in
[
BDFRT
]
when
\mathbf{M}
is a symplectic linear representation of
G
. In this case in
loc. cit.
the corresponding object
\mathcal{A}_{G,\mathbf{M}}
was constructed, but in general it is not an object of

but rather of some twisted version of it. The twisting is by a square root of some line bundle on

To formulate our conjecture we need to introduce the following notation. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a symplectic local system on

The following conjecture appears in a slightly weaker form in
[

Conjecture 1.5.1
.

(1)

There exists a sheaf
\Theta^{\vee}
on

Let

\{{\operatorname{pr}}(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2})^{n-1}v_{0}^{*},{\operatorname{pr}}(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2})^{n-2}v_{0}^{*},\ldots,v_{0}^{*}\}
. We take its union with the
dual basis of
L
, and obtain a basis of
V
. In this basis, the matrix of

x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2}
has both the upper left block and the lower right block with all nonzero elements right above
the diagonal and all zeros above them.
Conjugating by an appropriate element
g^{\prime}\in{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm GL}}}(L)\subset{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(V)
, we can make all the matrix
elements of
g^{\prime}(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2})g^{\prime-1}
in the basis
\{e^{2n-1}{\mathsf{v}},\ldots,e^{n}{\mathsf{v}},e^{n-1}{\mathsf{v}},\ldots,{\mathsf{v}}\}
of
V
,
right above the diagonals in both diagonal blocks to be exactly 1. Now
x^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2})+v^{\prime 2}
has all
2n-1
elements
right above the diagonal equal to 1, and all zeros above them. In other words,
x^{\prime}\in e+{\mathfrak{b}}
, where
{\mathfrak{b}}
is
the Borel subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{sp}}(V)
containing
f
. Let
{\mathfrak{u}}
be the nilpotent radical of
{\mathfrak{b}}
, and let

\operatorname{U}\subset{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(V)
be the corresponding unipotent subgroup. We define
{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathfrak{sp}}(V)\oplus V
as

{\mathcal{A}}=\{e+{\mathfrak{b}},v_{0}^{*}+L\}
. Then the map
\operatorname{U}\times\Sigma\to{\mathcal{A}}
,
(u,x,v)\mapsto u\cdot\sigma(x,v)
is an
isomorphism. Hence we can find
u\in U
that takes
(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})
to
\Sigma
. Clearly,
\operatorname{U}
stabilizes
L
.
Thus
X
is contained in the image of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(V)\times\Sigma
, as desired.

Furthermore, the natural morphism
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(V)\times\Sigma\to{\mathfrak{sp}}(V)\oplus V
,

(g,x,v)\mapsto(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(x)-(gv)^{2},gv)
, factors as the
composition
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Sp}}}(V)\times\Sigma\to{\mathfrak{sp}}(V)\times V\times\operatorname{LGr}(V)\to{\mathfrak{sp}}(V)\oplus V
, where
the second morphism takes
(x^{\prime},v^{\prime},L^{\prime})
to
(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2},v^{\prime})
.

So we must find the desired open subspace
{\mathcal{U}}
in the image of
X
in
{\mathfrak{sp}}(V)\oplus V
.
First we take care of the condition (1).
If
x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2}
has a zero eigenvalue (a codimension 1 condition), then for
(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})
in the image
of
X
, the corresponding component
v^{\prime}_{0}
is a cyclic vector for the corresponding block

(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2})_{0}
. The complement to the set of such pairs has codimension 1. All in all, the
set of pairs
(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})
such that
x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2}
is not invertible, and
v^{\prime}_{0}
is not a cyclic vector
for
(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2})_{0}
has codimension 2 in
{\mathfrak{sp}}(V)\oplus V
.

Now if
x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2}
is invertible, for each pair of opposite eigenvalues
(\lambda,-\lambda)
,
either the corresponding component
v^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is a cyclic vector for the corresponding block

(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2})_{\lambda}
, or
v^{\prime}_{-\lambda}
is a cyclic vector for
(x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2})_{-\lambda}
.
The complement to the set of such pairs again has codimension 2 in
{\mathfrak{sp}}(V)\oplus V
.

As for the condition (2),
{\mathcal{U}}
contains the open subset formed by all
(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})
such that
all the eigenvalues of
x^{\prime}
are distinct from the eigenvalues of
x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2}
, and all the eigenvalues
of
x^{\prime}
and of
x^{\prime}-v^{\prime 2}
have multiplicity 1.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Finally, the tensor property of

Lemma 3.5.2
.

The composition

Proof.

We have three maps from

Lemma 3.5.3

(1)

(1) We know that the further composition

(2\rho^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-2,2\rho^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-1)

Sharp Lower Bounds for Linearized ReLU k Approximation on the Sphere
Tong Mao, Jinchao Xu

Abstract
We prove a saturation theorem for linearized shallow ReLU k neural networks on the unit sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} . For any antipodally quasi-uniform set of centers, if the target function has smoothness r > d + 2 ​ k + 1 2 r>\tfrac{d+2k+1}{2} , then the best ℒ 2 ​ ( 𝕊 d ) \mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d}) approximation cannot converge faster than order n − d + 2 ​ k + 1 2 ​ d n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}} . This lower bound matches existing upper bounds, thereby establishing the exact saturation order d + 2 ​ k + 1 2 ​ d \tfrac{d+2k+1}{2d} for such networks. Our results place linearized neural-network approximation firmly within the classical saturation framework and show that, although ReLU k networks outperform finite elements under equal degrees k k , this advantage is intrinsically limited.



1
Introduction

Neural networks have demonstrated remarkable approximation capabilities over the past several decades. The universal approximation theorem, established in seminal works of the early 1990s (see, e.g.,
cybenko1989approximation
;
hornik1989multilayer
), laid the theoretical foundation for their expressive power. Specifically, consider the class of shallow neural networks with a single hidden layer:

\Sigma_{n}^{\sigma}:=\Big\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\sigma(w_{j}\cdot\circ+b_{j}):~w_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},~b_{j}\in\mathbb{R},~a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}\Big\}

(1.1)

With some smooth activation functions
\sigma
, the class can approximate functions in the Sobolev space
\mathcal{W}^{r,p}(\Omega)
with convergence rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{r}{d}})
, and achieve exponential convergence rates for analytic functions
mhaskar1996neural
. These powerful approximation properties extend to modern architectures, including deep ReLU networks
Yarotsky2017
and their higher-order variants ReLU
k

li2019better
;
he2023expressivity
. For instance, given any function
f
in the Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}^{r}(\Omega)
, there exists a deep ReLU neural network
f_{n}
with depth
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
and parameter count
\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
achieving the approximation:

\|f-f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}(\Omega)}n^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

(1.2)

When it comes to shallow networks, however, how the regularity of a target function affects the achievable convergence rate becomes a central question. For shallow ReLU
k
networks, the approximation rates have been extensively studied in the literature
makovoz1996random
;
bach2017breaking
;
klusowski2018approximation
;
xu2020finite
;
siegel2022sharp
;
mao2023rates
;
siegel2022optimal
;
mhaskar2023tractability
;
meng2022new
, typically showing rates of the form
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{d+\alpha}{2d}})
. In particular,
siegel2022sharp
;
siegel2025optimal
established the optimal convergence rate
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}})
for functions from Barron spaces
\mathcal{B}^{k}(\Omega)
. Building on these results,
yang2024optimal
;
mao2024approximation
demonstrated that for Sobolev spaces:

\inf\limits_{f_{n}\in\Sigma_{n}^{\sigma_{k}}}\|f-f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}(\Omega)}n^{-\frac{r}{d}},

(1.3)

subject to the condition:

r\leq\frac{d+2k+1}{2}.

(1.4)

A recent work in
liu2025integral
showed that the nonlinear class
\Sigma_{n}^{\sigma_{k}}
in (
1.3
) can be replaced by a linear subspace (see also
petrushev1998approximation
for an alternative formulation):

L_{n}^{k}=L_{n}^{k}\big(\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big):=\Big\{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\sigma_{k}(w_{j}^{*}\cdot\circ+b_{j}^{*}):\binom{w_{j}^{*}}{b_{j}^{*}}=\theta_{j}^{*},~j=1,\dots,n\Big\}

(1.5)

where
\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}
represents a fixed quasi-uniform collection of points. Specifically, for
r
satisfying (
1.4
), we have:

\inf\limits_{f_{n}\in L_{n}^{k}}\|f-f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}(\Omega)}n^{-\frac{r}{d}}.

(1.6)

However, all of the aforementioned work only achieve an approximation rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}})
, even for Sobolev spaces with regularity
r>\frac{d+2k+1}{2}
. This arise the concept of the
saturation phenomenon
. The saturation phenomenon is to say, an approximation approach of degree of freedom (DoF)
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n)
has a limiting approximation rate
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{r}{d}})
, beyond which no gain is achievable, regardless of the smoothness of the target function. The index
\frac{r}{d}
is called as the
saturated convergence rate
. For example, in (trigonometric) polynomial approximation (see, e.g.,
devore1993constructive
;
timan2014theory
;
lorentz1966approximation
), an application of
k
-th Cesaro operator achieves the approximation rate

\|f-f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}^{r,\infty}(\Omega)}n^{-\frac{r}{d}}

for
r\leq k
, whereas the rate remains
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{k}{d}})
for
r>k
, meaning the saturated convergence rate for
k
-th Cesaro operator is
\frac{k}{d}
. On the other hand, the saturated convergence rate of finite element methods is proved as
\frac{k+1}{d}
, where
k
is the order of the Lagrange elements
lin2014lower
. For wavelets, by observing the standard approximation results (see, e.g.,
devore1992compression
;
cohen2001adaptive
), one may conjecture the saturated convergence rate is
\frac{r}{d}
, where
r
is the regularity of the mother function
\varphi
.

Similar with the situation of wavelets, whether such saturation occurs in neural networks—and under what conditions—remains a subtle question. Based on the observation (
1.3
) and other earlier works
siegel2022sharp
;
mao2023rates
;
mao2024approximation
, one may conjecture that the shallow ReLU
k
neural networks has the saturated convergence rate
\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}
. Surprisingly, it is shown that in a special case that one could achieve an approximation rate
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-(k+1)})
for very smooth functions
siegel2022high
—although whether the saturation phenomenon of shallow ReLU
k
neural networks is still an open problem,
siegel2022high
indicates a saturated convergence rate of at least
k+1>\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}
.

In this paper, however, we show a the saturation order
\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}
holds true in the linear case (
1.6
) and
\Omega=\mathbb{S}^{d}
. To be specific, we show that on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, there exists a quasi-uniform collection
\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}
such that for any
r>\frac{d+2k+1}{2}
,

\inf\limits_{f_{n}\in L_{n}^{k}\big(\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big)}\|f-f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\gtrsim\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\Omega)}n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}},\qquad f\in\mathcal{H}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d}).

(1.7)

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work addressing the saturation phenomenon for shallow ReLU
k
networks, partially closing a theoretical gap left open by recent upper-bound results given by linearized neural networks
liu2025integral
for the regularity
r>\frac{d+2k+1}{2}
. While
liu2025integral
demonstrated that linearized shallow ReLU
k
networks significantly outperform classical finite elements—with approximation rates
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}})
surpassing finite elements’
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{k+1}{d}})
—we rigorously show that this superiority is bounded. Specifically, we establish a saturation theorem revealing that shallow ReLU
k
networks cannot exceed the approximation rate
n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}}
, even for functions smoother than the critical threshold. This result firmly places neural network approximation within the classical approximation theory landscape and tempers overly optimistic expectations regarding the unlimited expressiveness of shallow neural networks.

2
Localized spherical polynomials

In this section, we establish a key decomposition of the
\mathcal{L}^{2}
norm for functions in the linear space
L_{n}^{k}
. Specifically, we construct a sequence of matrices

Q_{q}=\big(L_{q}(\theta_{i}^{*}\cdot\theta_{j}^{*})\big)_{i,j=1}^{n},\qquad q=0,1,\dots,

such that for any function
f_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\sigma_{k}(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\circ)\in L_{n}^{k}
, its
\mathcal{L}^{2}
norm can be expressed as

\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\infty}a^{\top}Q_{q}a,

where
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})^{\top}
is the coefficient vector.

This decomposition plays a central role in establishing the saturation phenomenon. For smooth functions
f\in\mathcal{H}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, classical polynomial approximation theory shows that their high-degree components decay as
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{s}{d}})
. However, we prove that for functions in
L_{n}^{k}
, these high-degree components have a strict lower bound of order
n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}}
, derived from the spectral properties of matrices
Q_{q}
.

The key to establishing this lower bound lies in showing that the matrices
Q_{q}
are strongly diagonally dominant. This property emerges from the localization characteristics of spherical harmonic polynomials—a fundamental concept in approximation theory that has been extensively studied
petrushev2005localized
;
ivanov2010sub
;
dai2013approximation
;
xu2024highly
. The localization ensures that the influence of each basis function remains concentrated, leading to the diagonal dominance that ultimately constrains the approximation power of linearized neural networks.

2.1
Spherical harmonics and Legendre polynomials

We begin with some standard notation together with basic facts from spherical harmonic analysis. The material in this subsection is classical and can be found in
liu2025integral
;
dai2013approximation
. Let
\mathbb{S}^{d}:=\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:|\eta|=1\}
. For
\eta,\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, we write the inner product as
\eta\cdot\theta
and the geodesic distance as

\rho(\eta,\theta):=\arccos(\eta\cdot\theta).

Let
\omega_{d}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}1\,d\eta
denote the surface area of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. We use the normalized surface measure

\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f(\eta)\,d\eta:=\frac{1}{\omega_{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f(\eta)\,d\eta,\qquad f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d}),

and the induced
\mathcal{L}^{2}
inner product and norm

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}:=\fint_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f(\eta)g(\eta)\,d\eta,\qquad\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\langle f,f\rangle_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
be the space obtained by restricting to
\mathbb{S}^{d}
all polynomials in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
of total degree at most
m
. Its dimension is

\dim\mathbb{P}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d})=\begin{cases}\binom{d+1+m}{m},&m=0,1,\\[6.0pt]
\binom{d+1+m}{m}-\binom{d-1+m}{m-2},&m\geq 2.\end{cases}

Let
\mathbb{Y}_{m}
be orthogonal complement of
\mathbb{P}_{m-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
in
\mathbb{P}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, this space is known as spherical harmonics of degree
m
. Fix an
\mathcal{L}^{2}
-orthonormal basis
\{Y_{m,\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N(m)}\subset\mathbb{Y}_{m}
, its dimension is

N(0)=1,\qquad N(m)=\frac{2m+d-1}{m}\binom{m+d-2}{d-1},\quad m\geq 1.

Every
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
admits the harmonic expansion

f(\eta)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N(d,m)}\widehat{f}(m,\ell)\,Y_{m,\ell}(\eta),\qquad\widehat{f}(m,\ell):=\langle f,Y_{m,\ell}\rangle_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},

and the
\mathcal{L}^{2}
projection onto
\mathbb{Y}_{m}
is

\Pi_{m}f:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N(d,m)}\widehat{f}(m,\ell)\,Y_{m,\ell}.

Then Parseval’s identity

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N(d,m)}|\widehat{f}(m,\ell)|^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\|\Pi_{m}f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}.

gives the standard definition of Sobolev spaces.

Definition 2.1

(Sobolev spaces on the sphere)
.

For
r>0
, the Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
is defined as
\mathcal{H}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})=\{f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d}):~\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}<\infty\}
, with norm squared

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2r}\|\Pi_{m}f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N(m)}(m^{2r}+1)|\widehat{f}(m,\ell)|^{2}.

(2.1)

Define the space
\mathcal{L}^{2}_{w_{d}}([-1,1])
by

\left<f,g\right>_{w_{d}}=\int_{-1}^{1}f(t)g(t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}dt,\qquad\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{w_{d}}([-1,1])}=\left<f,f\right>_{w_{d}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.2)

The orthogonal basis of the space are called Legendre polynomials (see, e.g.,
szego1975orthogonal
):

p_{m}(t)=\lambda_{m}(1-t^{2})^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{m}\left[(1-t^{2})^{m+\frac{d-2}{2}}\right],\qquad t\in[-1,1],

(2.3)

where

\lambda_{m}=\frac{\omega_{d}}{\omega_{d-1}}\frac{N(m)}{\Gamma(m+d/2)}\sqrt{\frac{(2m+d-1)\Gamma(m+d-1)}{2^{2m+d-1}\Gamma(m+1)}},\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}

are chosen such that

p_{m}(\eta\cdot\theta)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N(m)}Y_{m,\ell}(\eta)Y_{m,\ell}(\theta).

(2.4)

The function
\sigma_{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{w_{d}}([-1,1])
has the Legendre expansion

\sigma_{k}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\sigma_{k}}(m)p_{m},

(2.5)

where the Legendre coefficients are given as

\widehat{\sigma_{k}}(m)=\frac{\left<p_{m},\sigma_{k}\right>_{w_{d}}}{\|p_{m}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{w_{d}}([-1,1])}^{2}}.

Denote the set

E_{\sigma_{k}}:=\left\{m\in\mathbb{N}:~\widehat{\sigma_{k}}(m)\neq 0\right\},

(2.6)

then by
(
bach2017breaking
, Appendix D.2)
,

\begin{split}&E_{\sigma_{k}}=\left\{m\geq k+1:~m-k\hbox{ is odd}\right\}\cup\{0,\dots,k\},\\
&\widehat{\sigma_{k}}(m)=\frac{\omega_{d-1}k!\Gamma(d/2)}{\omega_{d}}\frac{(-1)^{(m-k-1)/2}\Gamma(m-k)}{2^{m}\Gamma\left(\frac{m-k+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{m+d+k+1}{2}\right)},\qquad m\in E_{\sigma_{k}}.\end{split}

(2.7)

Finally, for notation simplicity, we follow
(
liu2025integral
, Lemma 3)
(and the notations therein) to denote

\xi(t)=\left(\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\omega_{d}}\frac{k!\Gamma(d/2)}{2^{k+1}\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{t-k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{t+d+k+1}{2}\right)}\right)^{2}.

(2.8)

Definition 2.2

(Quasi-uniform and antipodally quasi-uniform)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, a set of points
\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
is said to be quasi-uniform if

\max\limits_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}\min\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\rho(\theta,\theta_{j}^{*})\lesssim\min\limits_{i\neq j}\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},\theta_{j}^{*}).

(2.9)

Furthermore, a set of points
\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
is said to be antipodally quasi-uniform if

\max\limits_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}\min\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\rho(\theta,\theta_{j}^{*})\lesssim\min\big\{\min\limits_{i\neq j}\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},\theta_{j}^{*}),\min\limits_{i\neq j}\rho(-\theta_{i}^{*},\theta_{j}^{*})\big\}.

(2.10)

The corresponding constants are independent of
n
.

2.2
Representing the norm of
f_{n}

In this section, we derive an explicit representation of the norm of
f_{n}
in terms of Legendre polynomials and spherical harmonics. We first introduce the notation

I_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\qquad k~~\hbox{odd},\\
1,&\qquad k~~\hbox{even}.\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

As in
liu2025integral
, the norm of the function

f_{n}(\eta)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\sigma_{k}(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)

can be written as

\begin{split}\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=&\Big\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\sigma_{k}}(m)\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N(m)}Y_{m,\ell}(\theta_{j}^{*})Y_{m,\ell}\Big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\sigma_{k}}(m)^{2}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N(m)}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}Y_{m,\ell}(\theta_{j}^{*})\Big)^{2}\\
=&\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\sigma_{k}}(m)^{2}a^{\top}P(m)a,\end{split}

(2.12)

where

P(m)=\Big(\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N(m)}Y_{m,\ell}(\theta_{i}^{*})Y_{m,\ell}(\theta_{j}^{*})\Big)_{i,j=1}^{n}=\big(p_{m}(\theta_{i}^{*}\cdot\theta_{j}^{*})\big)_{i,j=1}^{n}.

It is known (see, e.g.,
(
petrushev2005localized
, (3.6))
) there exists a smooth function
\zeta
satisfying

\displaystyle\zeta\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\quad

\displaystyle\zeta\geq 0,\quad\mathrm{supp}(\zeta)\subset[1/2,2],

(2.13)

\displaystyle\zeta(t)>c_{1}>0,\qquad

\displaystyle t\in[3/5,5/3],

(2.14)

\displaystyle\zeta(t)+\zeta(2t)=1,\quad

\displaystyle t\in[1/2,1],

(2.15)

which gives

1=\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\infty}\zeta(2^{-q}m).

Then we can write (
2.12
) as

\begin{split}\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=&\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\sigma_{k}}(2m+I_{k})^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\infty}\zeta(2^{-q}(2m+I_{k}))\right)a^{\top}P(2m+I_{k})a\\
=&\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{q}(2^{-q}m)\xi_{q}(2^{-q}m)a^{\top}P_{0}(m)a=\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\infty}a^{\top}Q_{q}a,\end{split}

(2.16)

where

P_{0}(m)=P(2m+I_{k}),

(2.17)

and

Q_{q}=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{q}(2^{-q}m)P_{0}(m)

(2.18)

with

\varphi_{q}(t)=\zeta\Big(2t+\frac{I_{k}}{2^{q}}\Big)\xi\Big(2^{q+1}t+I_{k}\Big),\qquad t\geq 0.

(2.19)

Moreover, let
\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f)
be the projection of
f
on the polynomial space of degree
2^{\kappa}-1
, then the norm of
f_{n}
is estimated as

\begin{split}\big\|f_{n}-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f_{n})\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\geq&\sum\limits_{m=2^{\kappa}}^{2^{\kappa+2}+1}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N(m)}\widehat{f_{n}}(m,\ell)^{2}=\sum\limits_{m=2^{\kappa-1}}^{2^{\kappa+1}}\xi(m)a^{\top}P_{0}(m)a\\
\geq&\sum\limits_{m=2^{\kappa-1}}^{2^{\kappa+1}}\zeta_{q}(2^{-q}m)\xi_{q}(2^{-q}m)a^{\top}P_{0}(m)a=a^{\top}Q_{\kappa}a.\end{split}

(2.20)

In this paper, we consider the collections
\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}
to be antipodally quasi-uniform. While the concept of quasi-uniform point distributions has been well-studied (see, e.g.,
liu2025integral
), antipodally quasi-uniform is a stronger condition that additionally accounts for antipodal symmetry.

2.3
Summation of Jacobi polynomials and highly localized property

In this section, we introduce a polynomial
L(t)
that exhibits strong localization properties near
t=1
, following the approach developed in earlier works (see, e.g.,
petrushev2005localized
;
ivanov2010sub
). In addition to the localized polynomial and frame constructions in
petrushev2005localized
;
ivanov2010sub
, the works
filbir2009filter
;
mhaskar2010eignets
developed the theory of localized kernels through spectral filtering of Laplace–Beltrami eigenfunctions on compact manifolds, establishing general principles for diffusion-type localization and sub-exponential decay.
This localization property is crucial for establishing sharp lower bounds on the approximation error given in (
2.20
). The construction and analysis of
L(t)
will provide the key technical tools needed for our subsequent estimates.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{K}([0,\infty))
with
K\geq 1
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subset[1/2,2]
. Define

L(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{-q}m)p_{2m}(t),&\quad k\equiv 1\mod 2,\\
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{-q}m)p_{2m+1}(t),&\quad k\equiv 0\mod 2.\end{array}\right.

(2.21)

Then

L(t)\lesssim\max\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq K}\|\varphi^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\frac{2^{qd}}{(1+2^{q}\sqrt{1-t^{2}})^{K}}

(2.22)

where the corresponding constant is only dependent of
d
and
K

Proof.

We follow the arguments in
petrushev2005localized
, by recalling

p_{\nu}(t)=\frac{p_{\nu}(t)p_{\nu}(1)}{\|p_{\nu}\|_{w_{d}}^{2}}=\frac{p_{\nu}^{(\frac{d-2}{2},\frac{d-2}{2})}(t)p_{\nu}^{(\frac{d-2}{2},\frac{d-2}{2})}(1)}{\big\|p_{\nu}^{(\frac{d-2}{2},\frac{d-2}{2})}\big\|_{w_{d}}^{2}},

(2.23)

we can similarly using the identity
gasper2006formulas
and write

\begin{split}&L(\cos\theta)=\frac{2^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\Gamma(d/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(d-1)\Gamma(\frac{d-2}{2})}(1+\cos\theta)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\\
&\times\int_{\theta}^{\pi}\cos\big(\frac{d-1}{2}(\phi-\pi)\big)A_{2^{q}}^{\cos}(\phi)-\sin\big(\frac{d-1}{2}(\phi-\pi)\big)A_{2^{q}}^{\sin}(\phi)\frac{(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{(1-\cos\phi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}d\phi,\end{split}

(2.24)

where

\begin{split}A_{2^{q}}^{\cos}(\phi)=&\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2m+d-1)\Gamma(m+d-1)\Gamma(m+\frac{d-2}{2})}{\Gamma(m+d/2)\Gamma(m)}\varphi(2^{-q}m)\cos(2m+I_{k})\phi,\\
A_{2^{q}}^{\sin}(\phi)=&\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2m+d-1)\Gamma(m+d-1)\Gamma(m+\frac{d-2}{2})}{\Gamma(m+d/2)\Gamma(m)}\varphi(2^{-q}m)\sin(2m+I_{k})\phi.\end{split}

(2.25)

and

G(m)=\frac{(2m+I_{k}+\frac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(2m+I_{k}+d-1)}{\Gamma(2m+I_{k}+1)}.

(2.26)

We consider the function

\begin{split}\Theta_{q}(\phi):=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{m}G(m)\varphi(2^{-q}m)e^{im\phi}.\end{split}

(2.27)

By writing
\displaystyle\cos\theta=\frac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}
and
\displaystyle\sin\theta=\frac{e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta}}{2i}
, one could verify

\begin{split}A_{2^{q}}^{\cos}(\phi)=\frac{\Theta_{q}(2\phi)+\Theta_{q}(-2\phi)}{2},\qquad A_{2^{q}}^{\sin}(\phi)=\frac{\Theta_{q}(2\phi)-\Theta_{q}(-2\phi)}{2i}.\end{split}

(2.28)

By
(
petrushev2005localized
, Lemma 2.3)
,

|A_{2^{q}}^{\cos}(\phi)|\lesssim\frac{2^{qd}}{(1+2^{q}|\phi|)^{K}},\quad|A_{2^{q}}^{\sin}(\phi)|\lesssim\frac{2^{qd}}{(1+2^{q}|\phi|)^{K}},\qquad|\phi|\leq\pi.

and consequently

\begin{split}|A_{2^{q}}^{\cos}(2\phi)|\lesssim&\max\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq K}\|\varphi^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\frac{2^{qd}}{(1+2^{q}\phi(\pi-\phi))^{K}},\\
|A_{2^{q}}^{\sin}(2\phi)|\lesssim&\max\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq K}\|\varphi^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\frac{2^{qd}}{(1+2^{q}\phi(\pi-\phi))^{K}},\qquad\phi\in[0,\pi].\end{split}

(2.29)

Then (
2.24
) have bound

|L(\cos\theta)|\lesssim(1+\cos\theta)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{\theta}^{\pi}\max\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq K}\|\varphi^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\frac{2^{qd}}{(1+2^{q}\phi(\pi-\phi))^{K}}\frac{(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{(1-\cos\phi)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}d\phi.

We follow the idea in
petrushev2005localized
and write for
\phi\geq\theta\geq\frac{\pi}{2}
that
1-\cos\phi=2\sin^{2}\frac{\phi}{2}
, then

\begin{split}|L(\cos\theta)|\lesssim&\max\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq K}\|\varphi^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\int_{\theta}^{\pi}\frac{2^{qd}}{(2^{q}\phi(\pi-\phi)+1)^{K}}\frac{(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{(\cos\theta+1)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}}d\phi\\
\lesssim&\max\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq K}\|\varphi^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\frac{1}{{(\cos\theta+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\int_{\theta}^{\pi}\frac{2^{qd}}{(2^{q}\phi(\pi-\phi)+1)^{K}}d\phi\\
\lesssim&\max\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq K}\|\varphi^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\frac{2^{qd}}{(2^{q}\theta(\pi-\theta)+1)^{K}},\qquad\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\pi).\end{split}

(2.30)

That is,

|L(t)|\lesssim\max\limits_{0\leq\beta\leq K}\|\varphi^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\frac{2^{qd}}{(2^{q}\sqrt{1-t^{2}}+1)^{K}},\qquad t\in[-1,1].

(2.31)

∎

2.4
Lower bound of the matrices
Q_{q}

In this subsection, we establish the lower bound of the matrices
Q_{q}
by first showing that
L_{q}
is highly localized at
-1
and
1
, and then using this localization property to control the off-diagonal entries of
Q_{q}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{Q_{q}\}_{q=0}^{\infty}
be the matrices defined as (
2.18
), then

\sum\limits_{i\neq j}|(Q_{q})_{i,j}|\lesssim 2^{-q(2k+1+K)}\underline{h}^{-K}.

(2.32)

where
\underline{h}=\min\limits_{i\neq j}\min\{\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},\theta_{j}^{*}),\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},-\theta_{j}^{*})\}
.

Moreover, there exists some constant
C_{3}
, for
q\geq\log_{2}\big(\frac{C_{3}}{\underline{h}}\big)
,

Q_{q}\gtrsim 2^{-q(2k+1)}I_{n\times n}.

(2.33)

Proof.

By definition, we can write

Q_{q}=\big(L_{q}(\theta_{i}^{*}\cdot\theta_{j}^{*})\big)_{i,j=1}^{n},

where

L_{q}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{q}(2^{-q}m)p_{2m}(t),&\quad k\equiv 1\mod 2,\\
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{q}(2^{-q}m)p_{2m+1}(t),&\quad k\equiv 0\mod 2.\end{array}\right.

Taking

\zeta_{q}(t)=\zeta\Big(2t+\frac{I_{k}}{2^{q}}\Big),\qquad\xi_{q}(t)=\xi\Big(2^{q+1}t+I_{k}\Big),\qquad t\geq 0,

(2.34)

and apply chain rule and use
(
liu2025integral
, (3.21))
,

\begin{split}\|\varphi_{q}^{(\beta)}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=&\int_{1/2}^{2}\Big|\big(\frac{d}{dt}\big)^{\beta}(\zeta_{q}(t)\xi_{q}(t))\Big|dt=\int_{1/2}^{2}\Big|\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\nu}\zeta_{q}^{(\beta-\nu)}(t)2^{\nu q}\xi_{q}^{(\nu)}(2^{q}t)\Big|dt\\
\simeq&2^{-q(d+2k+1)}.\end{split}

(2.35)

Now by Theorem
2.1
,

L_{q}(t)\lesssim\frac{2^{-q(2k+1)}}{(1+2^{q}\sqrt{1-t^{2}})^{K}}.

(2.36)

This allows us to show
L_{q}
is highly localized at
-1
and
1
.

We divide the set
\{\theta_{i}^{*}:~1\leq i\leq n\}
in terms of the distance to
\theta_{j}^{*}
and
-\theta_{j}^{*}
as

\{\theta_{i}:~1\leq i\leq n\}=\left(\mathcal{I}_{-1,j,+}\cup\mathcal{I}_{-1,j,-}\right)\cup\bigcup\limits_{p=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2\widetilde{h}}\right)\rfloor}\left(\mathcal{I}_{p,j,+}\cup\mathcal{I}_{p,j,-}\right),

where
\mathcal{I}_{-1,j,-}:=\left\{i:\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},-\theta_{j}^{*})<\widetilde{h}\right\}
,

\mathcal{I}_{-1,j,+}:=\left\{i:\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},\theta_{j}^{*})<\widetilde{h}\right\}

and for
p=0,1,\dots
,

\mathcal{I}_{p,j,+}:=\{i:2^{p}\widetilde{h}\leq\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},\theta_{j}^{*})<2^{p+1}\widetilde{h}\},\quad\mathcal{I}_{p,j,-}:=\{i:2^{p}\widetilde{h}\leq\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},-\theta_{j}^{*})<2^{p+1}\widetilde{h}\}.

By a measure argument, it is easy to verify

\#\mathcal{I}_{-1,j,+}\lesssim 1,~~\#\mathcal{I}_{-1,j,-}\lesssim 1,\quad\#\mathcal{I}_{p,j,+}\lesssim 2^{pd},~~\#\mathcal{I}_{p,j,-}\lesssim 2^{pd}

where the corresponding constants are only dependent of
d
.

By noticing the formula

\sqrt{1-\theta_{i}\cdot\theta_{j}}=\sqrt{1-\cos(\rho(\theta_{i},\theta_{j}))}=\sqrt{2}\sin\frac{\rho(\theta_{i},\theta_{j})}{2},\qquad\theta_{i}\cdot\theta_{j}\geq 0,

we have

\sqrt{1-\theta_{i}\cdot\theta_{j}}\simeq\rho(\theta_{i},\theta_{j}),\quad\theta_{i}\cdot\theta_{j}\geq 0.

Similarly,

\sqrt{1+\theta_{i}\cdot\theta_{j}}\simeq\rho(\theta_{i},-\theta_{j}),\quad\theta_{i}\cdot\theta_{j}<0.

By (
2.36
),

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\big|\left(Q_{q}\right)_{i,j}\big|\lesssim&\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2\underline{h}}\right)\rfloor}\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{p,j}}\frac{2^{-q(2k+1)}}{(2^{q}\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},\theta_{j}^{*})\rho(\theta_{i}^{*},-\theta_{j}^{*}))^{K}}\lesssim\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2\underline{h}}\right)\rfloor}\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{p,j}}\frac{2^{-q(2k+1+K)}}{(2^{p}\underline{h})^{K}}\\
\lesssim&2^{-q(2k+1+K)}\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\lfloor\log_{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2\underline{h}}\right)\rfloor}2^{pd}(2^{p}\underline{h})^{-K}\lesssim 2^{-q(2k+1+K)}\underline{h}^{-K},\end{split}

(2.37)

On the other hand, for the diagonal term, recall that
p_{m}(1)=N(m)
(see, e.g.,
(
liu2025integral
, (3.7))
), we have

\left(Q_{q}\right)_{i,i}=L_{q}(1)\geq\sum\limits_{\frac{3}{5}2^{q}\leq m\leq\frac{5}{3}2^{q}}c\xi(m)N(2m)\gtrsim 2^{-q(2k+1)}.

(2.38)

Then there exists some constant
C_{3}
such that given
2^{q}\geq C_{3}\underline{h}^{-1}
,

\frac{1}{2}\left(Q_{q}\right)_{i,i}=\frac{1}{2}L_{q}(1)\geq\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\big|\left(Q_{q}\right)_{i,j}\big|

and consequently

Q_{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(Q_{q}\right)_{i,i}I_{n\times n}=\frac{1}{2}L_{q}(1)I_{n\times n},\qquad q\geq\log_{2}\left(\frac{C_{3}}{\underline{h}}\right).

(2.39)

∎

3
Saturation phenomenon for linearized ReLU
k
neural networks

We are now ready to establish our main theoretical result concerning the saturation phenomenon of linearized ReLU
k
neural networks. Specifically, we will prove that these networks exhibit a saturation order of
\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}
, which represents a fundamental limit on their approximation capabilities. This saturation order characterizes how the approximation error cannot decrease faster than
n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}}
regardless of the smoothness of the target function, where
n
is the width of the network.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
s>\frac{d+2k+1}{2}
and any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
,

\inf\limits_{f_{n}\in L_{n}^{k}}\|f-f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\gtrsim n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},

(3.1)

where the corresponding constant is independent of
n
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
. Then we have
\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
and

\begin{split}n\|a\|_{2}^{2}\geq&\sup\limits_{\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}\Big(n\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{k}(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)^{2}\Big)\geq\sup\limits_{\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}\Big(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\sigma_{k}(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)\Big)^{2}\\
\geq&\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}}{2},\end{split}

which implies

\|a\|_{2}^{2}\gtrsim n^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}.

(3.2)

Let
\kappa:=\min\{q:2^{q}\geq C_{3}\underline{h}^{-1}\}
, since
\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is antipodally quasi-uniform, we have

2^{\kappa}\simeq\underline{h}^{-1}\simeq n^{1/d}.

With
\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f)
being the projection of
f
on the space
\mathbb{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
, the classical approximation theory gives (see, e.g.,
dai2013approximation
;
devore1993constructive
)

\|f-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\kappa s}\simeq n^{-\frac{s}{d}}.

(3.3)

Therefore,

\begin{split}&\big\|f_{n}-f\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}=\Big(\big\|f_{n}-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f_{n})-(f-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f))\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}+\big\|\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f_{n}-f)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\geq&\big\|f_{n}-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f_{n})-(f-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f))\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}.\end{split}

(3.4)

By (
2.20
) and Lemma
2.1
,

a^{\top}Q_{q}a\gtrsim 2^{-q(2k+1)}\|a\|_{2}^{2},\qquad q\geq\log_{2}\big(\frac{C_{3}}{\underline{h}}\big).

(3.5)

That is,

\big\|f_{n}-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\gtrsim 2^{-\kappa(2k+1)}\|a\|_{2}^{2}\gtrsim n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{d}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}.

Substituting in (
3.4
),

\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\geq\big\|f_{n}-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f_{n})\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}-\|f-\mathcal{P}_{2^{\kappa}-1}(f)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\gtrsim n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}.

(3.6)

∎

Remark 3.1
.

We emphasize the antipodally quasi-uniform condition is not only sufficient but also necessary for Theorem
3.1
: a quasi-uniform collection
\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}
might include two antipodal points
\theta_{i}^{*},\theta_{j}^{*}
, i.e.,
\theta_{i}^{*}=-\theta_{j}^{*}
. In this case, we can represent the polynomial

(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)^{k}=\sigma_{k}(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)+(-1)^{k}\sigma_{k}(\theta_{i}^{*}\cdot\eta),\qquad\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{d},

which means the error is
0
for the nonzero function
(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)^{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
.

However, Theorem
3.1
is significantly stronger than the standard saturation phenomenon:
any
nonzero function in
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
does not achieve an approximation rate than
\mathcal{O}(n^{\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}+\epsilon})
for
\epsilon>0
. We conjecture that for arbitrary quasi-uniform points
\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and a general domain
\Omega
, the standard saturation phenomenon holds true: there
exists
a function which cannot be approximated by such rate.

Remark 3.2
.

The antipodally quasi-uniform condition employed in our analysis appears stronger than the quasi-uniform condition used in
liu2025integral
. At first glance, this might suggest our saturation result only covers a restrictive scenario. However, we emphasize that the optimal approximation rates established in
liu2025integral
can essentially be realized by quasi-uniform points restricted to a half-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}:~x_{1}>0\}
.

Indeed, by introducing a fixed finite collection of points, we can construct all polynomials of degree
k
and apply the relation

(-1)^{k}\sigma_{k}(-\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)+\sigma_{k}(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)=(\theta_{j}^{*}\cdot\eta)^{k}

to reconstruct the full approximation space from points on the half-sphere, effectively embedding the scenario of quasi-uniform points on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
into that on
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{+}
. Conversely, an antipodally quasi-uniform collection on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
can be similarly considered as quasi-uniform on a half-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{+}
, up to a fixed finite set of points.

Thus, the requirement of antipodal quasi-uniformity does not fundamentally restrict the generality of our saturation theorem. In fact, this argument indicates that our analysis fully addresses the saturation phenomenon for linearized ReLU
k
neural network approximation, not merely as a special case, but in a way that truly captures the essential linear approximation structure of quasi-uniform points on spheres.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we have established the first rigorous saturation theorem for shallow ReLU
k
neural networks, providing a conclusive answer to an important open question in approximation theory. While recent studies demonstrated significant superiority of linearized shallow ReLU
k
networks over traditional finite element methods, showing notably faster approximation rates of
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}})
as opposed to the classical finite element rates of
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{k+1}{d}})
, our result highlights that this advantage is inherently bounded. Specifically, we prove that the approximation rate saturates at the regularity threshold
r=\frac{d+2k+1}{2}
, beyond which no further improvement is possible, irrespective of the increased smoothness of the target function.

Our saturation theorem aligns neural network approximation with classical methods such as polynomial, spline, wavelet, and kernel approximations, where saturation phenomena are fundamental and well-documented. This underscores a universal structural limitation governing the performance of approximation schemes, extending even to nonlinear, adaptive methods such as neural networks. Practically, our results caution against overly optimistic views of shallow neural networks’ capabilities, suggesting that their expressiveness—though superior—is ultimately limited by an intrinsic regularity threshold.

Looking forward, this saturation perspective naturally raises several intriguing research directions. Future studies might explore whether a general
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
yields same saturation order
\frac{d+2k+1}{2d}
. Moreover, in
siegel2022high
we observed that nonlinear shallow ReLU
k
network approximation can achieve
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(n^{k+1})
for very smooth functions. But whether its saturation order is
k+1
still an open problem.

Three-dimensional void fraction evolution law and sensitivity analysis of mining-induced overburden based on Bertalanffy time function
Authors: Zejun Li, Nan Zhou, YiZhu Lei, Qiming Yang, Meng Li, Hao Yan, Baiyi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The voids in overlying strata caused by coal mining are prone to induce geological hazards. To reveal the evolution of overburden voids, a three-dimensional dynamic void fraction (VF) model based on the Bertalanffy time function was constructed and verified. Four evaluation indicators were proposed: the average VF of the caving zone, fracture zone, and separation zone (AVF-CZ, AVF-FZ, AVF-SZ), as well as the bulk average VF of the entire mining-induced overburden (BAVF-EMO). The influence of four main controlling factors—mining height, working face length, distance from the main roof to the coal seam, and distance from the sub-key stratum (SKS) to the coal seam—on these indicators was analyzed. The results show that with increasing mining time, the VF of the caving zone gradually decreases, whereas the VF of other zones gradually increases and stabilizes after 80 days of advancement. When the mining height exceeds 5 m, both AVF-CZ and BAVF-EMO decrease significantly; for every 100 m increase in the distance between the SKS and the coal seam, AVF-FZ and AVF-SZ increase notably. The research findings can provide a theoretical basis for selecting backfilling parameters and for preventing and controlling mining-induced hazards.

Introduction

After coal mining, the fissure space in the goaf, resulting from overlying strata fracture, exhibits a "three-zone" distribution pattern. The voids within fissure spaces serve as primary pathways for the movement and storage of underground heat, water, oxygen, and other fluids1. These voids contribute to underground water inrush or spontaneous combustion of coal seams, posing significant risks to mine safety and operational efficiency. In addition, the voids within mining-induced overburden are the main spaces for grouting backfilling, and their size and distribution pattern directly affect the diffusion of the backfilling slurry2. Hence, it is crucial to study the three-dimensional spatial and temporal distribution of mining-induced overburden voids and their influencing factors.

Mining-induced overburden is the carrier and transmitter of surface subsidence. The structure of the rock strata and their engineering geological combination influence the movement and deformation process of the overburden, leading to the generation and development of voids within it. To theoretically predict rock movement and surface subsidence, the most commonly used methods include the profile function method3, typical curve method4, influence function method5, and probability integral method6. To further study the dynamic deformation of rock formations at any point and time, a time function is commonly incorporated into the rock formation movement prediction model under static conditions for dynamic prediction for the rock formation in practical applications. The most commonly used time function types include Knothe6, Weibull7, Sroka-Schober8, Logistic9 and Von Bertalanffy time function10. In addition, in predicting catastrophic fractures within rocks11,12, proposed a pseudo-prospective failure forecast model based on the time-reversed Omori law applied to precursory acoustic emission time series, which accurately predicts the failure time of rocks under both uniaxial and triaxial stress conditions. However, an overburden void fraction (VF) prediction model combined with a time function has not yet been established, and the three-dimensional dynamic distribution and evolution characteristics of overburden VF cannot be accurately characterized.

The development of voids within overburden is affected by many factors. Clarifying the development patterns of overburden voids under different mining conditions is crucial for the parameter design of grouting backfilling and other support techniques. In recent years, numerous research efforts have been directed toward assessing the progression and characteristics of fractures13 reported that the height and shape of fracture development are controlled by eight direct influencing factors, such as mining thickness and ten indirect influencing factors, such as subsidence space, and mining scale and roof cover structure are the main control indicators14 analyzed and studied the characteristics of mining-induced fracture frequency, angle, width, and area, and the results indicated that mining-induced fractures were more pronounced in the right fracture area15 proposed an analog hyperbolic settlement model for shallow-buried coal seams and concluded that the breaking length of the primary key stratum (PKS) has the greatest influence on the width of the surface settlement boundary and the maximum subsidence value. Through a comprehensive probability method16, quantitatively evaluated the impact of fracture development by compositing multiple conventional logging curves, and determined that the key factors affecting fracture development are formation thickness, brittleness index, lithology and rock formation type17 systematically summarized the correlation between the rock fracture process zone (FPZ) and the microstructure, geometric characteristics, loading conditions, and environmental factors of rocks, pointing out that the apparent FPZs is an important foundation for the unstable crack growth of flawed granite. Furthermore, when considering the internal crystal microstructures of rocks, the fracture behavior of rock layers is significantly influenced by stochastic factors such as grain orientation, grain size, and grain boundary strength18. In summary, geological factors and rock lithology are the main factors affecting fracture development.

Existing studies have predominantly focused on the VF evolution characteristics of a single zone within the fracture zone, but lack a quantitative calculation model capable of simultaneously characterizing the VF of three zones, making it difficult to quantify the extent to which geological factors and lithology influence VF variations. To accurately characterize the three-dimensional dynamic distribution of the mining-induced overburden VF and quantify the parameter influence on its changes, a three-dimensional dynamic calculation model for overburden VF based on Bertalanffy time function was constructed and validated. Additionally, the spatiotemporal evolution of the VF of the caving zone (VF-CZ), VF of the fracture zone (VF-FZ) and the VF of the separation zone (VF-SZ) in the mining space was analyzed. On this basis, four evaluation indicators and calculation methods for the average VF of the caving zone, fracture zone, separation zone (AVF-CZ, AVF-FZ, and AVF-SZ) and the bulk average VF of the entire mining-induced overburden (BAVF-EMO) were proposed. Through single-factor analysis and multi-factor variance analysis, four main controlling factors were determined, and their significance levels and inter-group differences were analyzed. The primary innovations of this study are as follows: First, a dynamic prediction model for the mining-induced overburden VF was established by coupling the Bertalanffy time function, revealing the zonal dynamic evolution patterns of the VF. Second, the four main controlling factors governing the evolution of the overburden VF were quantified, and their significance was clarified. Third, a design process for grouting backfilling parameters based on the VF evolution was proposed.

Background and methods

Research background

As shown in Fig. 1, the study area is located in a coal mine in Dongsheng District, Ordos City, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, China. No permission was required for conducting research at this mine. The working face 42,207 adopted the inclined longwall retreat comprehensive mechanized coal mining method, and the full caving was used for the roof management. The main mining coal seam was coal 4–2, with an average thickness of 3.0m, a burial depth of 171.0 m, and an inclination angle of less than 3°. The overburden of the working face 42,207 had a relatively simple structure, with the immediate roof composed of siltstone averaging 8.91 m in thickness. The working face exhibited a dip length of 300 m and a strike length of 1000 m. The advancement speed was set as 5 m/d. According to the geological drilling information collected on site and the key stratum theory, there were six key strata within the overburden of Coal 4–2, among which the gravel-bearing coarse-grained sandstone with a thickness of 40.98m and a distance of 155.17m from the roof of the coal seam was the primary key stratum (PKS).Fig. 1Study area. Three-dimensional dynamic VF calculation model

Calculation process

The mining-induced overburden VF are divided into three categories: VF-CZ, VF-FZ and VF-SZ. The mining-induced overburden movement model is constructed, as shown in Fig. 2. The internal movement of the key fracture stratum is described using the probability integration method, and whether the key stratum undergoes initial fracture is determined using mechanical models of elastic thin plates and medium-thick plates. Compared to the Knothe function, the Bertalanffy time function can more accurately represent the dynamic subsidence process of the overburden. Therefore, the Bertalanffy time function is employed to characterize the dynamic variations in VF at any given location. Based on this model, dynamic calculation formulas for VF-CZ, transverse VF of the fracture zone (TVF-FZ), longitudinal VF of the fracture zone (LVF-FZ), and VF-SZ are derived.Fig. 2Overburden movement model. (a) Key stratum subsidence; (b) elastic thin plate and medium-thick plate mechanical model for the initial fracture of the key stratum.

Overburden movement model. (a) Key stratum subsidence; (b) elastic thin plate and medium-thick plate mechanical model for the initial fracture of the key stratum.

As illustrated in Fig. 3, the three-dimensional calculation process of VF is as follows: First, input the mining parameters of the working face and the lithology parameters of the overburden, and identify the SKS and PKS based on the key stratum theory. At the given mining time t, for the rock mass in the caving zone with Hi (the distance of SKS i from the coal seam) ≤ H1 (the distance of SKS 1 from the coal seam), its VF-CZ can be calculated by Eq. (5). For SKS i with Hi > H1, its thickness-to-span ratio hi/min(a, b) can be calculated. If hi/min(a, b) < 0.125, the elastic thin plate model (Eq. (11) and Eq. (13)) is used to calculate the maximum bending stress σi max of the SKS i; if hi/min(a, b) ≥ 0.125, the medium-thick plate model (Eq. (12) and Eq. (13))is used to calculate σi max. Based on the calculated σi max and the tensile strength Ri m of SKS i, a judgment is made as follows. If σi max > Ri m, the TVF-FZ and LVF between SKS 1-SKS i can be calculated by Eq. (8) and Eq. (9), respectively; If σi max > Rm and σi+1 max < Ri m, the VF-SZ below SKS i + 1 can be calculated by Eq. (11); If σi max > Ri m and σi+1 max > Ri+1 m, it indicates that both SKS i and SKS i + 1 have been broken. At this time, SKS i + 1 is assigned to SKS i, return to the thickness-to-span ratio calculation and strength judgment steps is repeated, and the subsequent key stratum can be calculated. After completing the VF calculation of all SKS at the current time t, the next mining time t is advanced, and the above calculation process is executed cyclically to realize the dynamic calculation of VF.Fig. 3Calculation process of three-dimensional VF within overburden.

Sensitivity analysis of main control factors for VF

Main influencing factors and evaluation indicators

Variance analysis

The analysis of variance (ANOVA) is a standard statistical technique used to determine the advantage of parameters and estimate the impact of each influencing factor. The advantage of each influencing factor is evaluated by the F value23. The F value is the ratio of the mean square of the deviation (SW) caused by the change of each factor level to the mean square of the error (SE)23. SW represents the sum of the squares of the difference between the data mean at the same level of each influencing factor and the total mean.15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{W} = \sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m} {\left( {\overline{{T_{i} }} - \overline{T} } \right)^{2} }$$\end{document}where m is the number of levels of the factor, n is the number of tests for each level of the factor, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overline{{T_{i} }}$$\end{document} is the average value of the i-th level of the orthogonal table, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overline{T}$$\end{document} is the total average value of the orthogonal table.

In Eq. (16), SE represents the sum of squares of the difference between the test data of the same level and its average value.16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_{E} = \sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m} {\sum\limits_{j = 1}^{n} {\left( {T_{ij} - \overline{T}_{i} } \right)^{2} } }$$\end{document}where Tij is the test result of the i-th level of the j-th column of the orthogonal table.

The ratio of the sum of squares of the error deviations of the factor to the error degrees of freedom is the average sum of squares of the factor. Then, the F value is17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F = \frac{{S_{W} /f_{W} }}{{S_{E} /f_{E} }}$$\end{document}where fW is the degrees of freedom of the factor, fW = m-1; fB is the degrees of freedom of the error.

The F value is used to analyze the sensitivity of the influencing factors of the evaluation index. When F ≥ F0.01, the influencing factor is defined as a highly sensitive parameter and has a highly significant effect on the evaluation index. When F0.01 > F ≥ F0.05, the factor is a sensitive parameter and has a significant effect on the evaluation index. When F0.05 > F ≥ F0.1, the factor is a general sensitive parameter and has a general significant effect on the evaluation index. When F < F0.1, the factor is an insensitive parameter and has no significant effect on the evaluation index. After screening out the sensitive factors, the Bonferroni correction method is used to perform post hoc multiple comparisons on different levels of the influencing factor.

Application in the grout backfilling

Based on the sensitivity analysis results, the mining height, burial depth of the SKS adjacent to the PKS, and advancing time are the main control factors for VF. According to the orthogonal test results in Table 3, a prediction equation for AVF was established through multiple linear regression (Fig. 17). The mining-induced voids provide space and channels for strata movement, groundwater flow, and gas migration, which can easily trigger geological hazards such as surface subsidence, underground water inrush, and coal fire. Grout backfilling serves as the primary measure for mitigating these risks, including filling in caving zone, separation zone, and subsequent space.Fig. 17Flowchart of grout backfilling parameter design based on VF.

Flowchart of grout backfilling parameter design based on VF.

In actual engineering practice, to enhance backfilling effectiveness, it is necessary to predict VF distribution and AVF based on mine geological conditions, thereby calculating the grouting volume and rationally designing the backfilling location and timing.

Grout backfilling in caving zone: The backfilling volume is the product of the AVF-CZ and the mining space volume. The VF-CZ exhibits a distribution pattern of "higher at the edges and lower in the central area" (Fig. 5). Therefore, grouting boreholes should be arranged from both sides toward the center along the inclination of the working face, with the spacing determined according to the diffusion distance of the waste rock slurry29. Since the void fraction in the caving zone decreases rapidly after extraction, backfilling should ideally be completed within 20 days after extraction.

Grout backfilling in separation zone: The backfilling volume is the product of the AVF-SZ and the mining space volume. The VF-SZ displays a parabolic upward-bulging characteristic (Fig. 10). Boreholes should be positioned at the center of the lower part of the primary key stratum above the working face30. The void fraction stabilizes approximately 80 days after extraction, so backfilling should be conducted after 80 days and must be completed before the PKS fractures.

Grout backfilling in subsequent space: The backfilling volume is the product of the BAVF-EMO and the mining space volume. A comprehensive analysis of Figs. 5–10 indicates that the LVF-FZ and VF-SZ is highest in the central area, and the slurry diffusion morphology approximates a “frustum shape”31. Therefore, boreholes should be located at the center of the lower part of the PKS above the working face. This type of backfilling should also be carried out after 80 days of extraction and before the PKS fractures.

Inhomogeneous model with a space dependent Cosmological Constant
D. Comelli

Abstract
We analyse an inhomogeneous cosmological model featuring a spherically symmetric bubble solution induced by a unified single perfect fluid, comprising spatially dependent Dark Energy (with w = − 1 w=-1 ) and Dark Matter (with w = 0 w=0 ) components. We impose an Hubble profile that matches the Planck value at early times ( H 0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 H_{0}=67.4\pm 0.5 Km/s Mpc) and the local value ( ℋ 0 = 73.52 ± 1.62 {\cal H}_{0}=73.52\pm 1.62 Km/s Mpc). We explicitly derive perturbative solutions in two distinct regimes: one expanded around the center (for small r ​ ℋ 0 ≪ 1 r\,{\cal H}_{0}\ll 1 ) and the other expanded around a homogeneous FRW universe. In both cases, we compute the cosmographic parameters, redshift profiles for the Hubbles expansion rates, and effective equations of state. Furthermore, we investigate the redshift drift behaviour extended to a Lema i ^ \hat{\rm i} tre metric.

\displaystyle a^{3}\,\lambda_{1}(r),\;a\,\frac{{\lambda}_{1}^{\prime}(r)}{H_{0}^{2}\,r},\;a\,\frac{{\bf e}_{1}(r)}{H_{0}^{2}\,r^{2}},\;a\,\frac{{\bf e}_{1}^{\prime}(r)}{H_{0}^{2}\,r}<1

6.1

In this context the perturbative solutions of the geodesic eqs (

7
Conclusions

We analysed the dynamics of a cosmological model with
a Locally Rotationally Symmetric spacetime coupled with a specific perfect fluid.
The key feature of this fluid is the presence of a space-dependent CC and, simultaneously, a DM component, both exhibiting the same exponential suppressing profile for the inhomogeneities (
6.23
).
A theoretical study of such a fluid is presented in
[
6
]
, where only the space-dependent CC component was considered.
Here, we skip the detailed theoretical aspects and instead postulate a perfect fluid EMT (
2.7
), where the energy density and pressure contains both DM and DE component (
2.6
).
Using a Lema
\hat{\rm i}
tre metric in comoving coordinates, we show that the Einstein eqs
(
2.17
,
2.14
,
2.19
) are solvable, modulo some space-dependent functions that arise from boundary conditions.

1+3 Formalism

The spacetime is split into time and space relative
to a fundamental observer, represented by the timelike unit vector field
u^{\alpha}
, which corresponds
to the observer’s 4-velocity.
In this framework, the 1+3 covariant threading irreducibly
decomposes any 4-vector into a scalar part parallel to

and a 3-vector part orthogonal to
u^{\alpha}

Furthermore, any second rank tensor is covariantly and irreducibly split into scalar, 3-vector

The projection tensor onto the metric of the 3-space (S) orthogonal to

is given by

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}+u_{\alpha}\;u_{\beta},\qquad h^{\alpha}_{\alpha}=3

(A.1)

The covariant derivative of a scalar function

\displaystyle\dot{f}=u^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}f

We can decompose the covariant derivative of

orthogonal to

4-Acceleration

(A.5)

–

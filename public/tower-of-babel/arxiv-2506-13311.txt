Symmetrization of measures and the one-dimensional Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions
Christos Papadimitriou

Abstract
. Let μ \mu be a finite Borel measure on ( − π , π ) (-\pi,\pi) . Consider the one-dimensional Poisson equation − u ′′ = μ -u^{\prime\prime}=\mu , where equality holds in the sense of distributions, with Dirichlet boundary conditions u ⁡ ( ± π ) = 0 u(\pm\pi)=0 . In this paper, we define measures that are transformations of μ \mu , we compare the convex integral means of the original solutions u μ u_{\mu} and the transformed ones, and we prove the uniqueness of a solution that maximizes the convex integral means.

1.
Introduction

Imagine a metal rod of length
2\pi
that is heated through by an external heat source with distribution
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
. We are interested in the temperature
u_{f}
of the steady-state of the rod, meaning that the temperature of the rod does not change over time, if we suppose that the end-points of the rod are kept frozen.
To put this into equations, we have that
u_{f}
has to satisfy the Poisson equation

(1.1)

-u^{\prime\prime}=f\text{ on $[-\pi,\pi]$}

with Dirichlet boundary conditions

(1.2)

u(-\pi)=u(\pi)=0.

Another way to view this thought experiment is to let the rod interact with its environment considering the end-points of the rod to be submerged in a cold bath of temperature zero and allow heat be dissipated via Newton’s law of cooling, i.e. the heat flux at the end-points is proportional to the temperature. In this case, the steady-state of the rod has to satisfy Robin boundary conditions, i.e. for some constant
\alpha>0
(which depends on the material of the rod or the type of liquid) we have

(1.3)

-u^{\prime}(-\pi)+\alpha u(-\pi)=u^{\prime}(\pi)+\alpha u(\pi)=0.

With this setup, we can approximate Dirichlet boundary conditions by letting
\alpha
tend to
+\infty
. The physical interpretation of this is that the liquid that surrounds the rod, not only starts with temperature zero, but also remains frozen and keeps the end-points of the rod frozen as well, thus it has infinite thermal capacity.

Recently, J. J. Langford and P. McDonald
[
8
]
, studied the problems (
1.1
), (
1.3
) and (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and were interested in comparing different solutions and the changes of specific properties of the solutions
u_{f}
, after performing rearrangement transformations to the heat source
f
. Namely, they were interested in the convex integral means of the solutions and, as a consequence, their
L^{p}
-norms.

Approaching the Dirichlet problem through the Robin problem has the benefit that enables us to apply known results about the Robin problem (for example, theorems from
[
8
]
or
[
9
]
) in our context, by simply using the Theorem of Dominated Convergence. A more interesting question would be to investigate what happens if the heat source is not a Lebesgue integrable function but rather a more general object, a Borel measure.

Throughout this paper, we will use the term
Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
when referring to a positive measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
(-\pi,\pi)
that is not the zero measure. Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
. We will show (see Proposition
2.7
) that there exists a unique absolutely continuous function
u_{\mu}
on
[-\pi,\pi]
that solves the Poisson equation

(1.4)

-u^{\prime\prime}=\mu\text{ on $(-\pi,\pi)$ in the sense of distributions}

and satisfies Dirichlet boundary conditions

(1.5)

u(-\pi)=u(\pi)=0.

We will talk more about distributions later and give equivalent expressions of
(\ref{Poisson})
.

Notation.

•

Let
\lambda
denote the one-dimensional Lebesgue measure. As usual, if a measure
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, we write
\mu\ll\lambda
, and if a measure
\sigma
is singular to the Lebesgue measure, we write
\sigma\perp\lambda
.

•

In the special case that
d\mu=fd\lambda
for some
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
, we will denote the solution
u_{\mu}
to the problem (
1.4
), (
1.5
) by
u_{f}
.

For the general definition of
symmetric decreasing rearrangement
(s.d.r. for short) of functions, we refer to
[
2
]
. In Section 3 we present a special case for the s.d.r. of functions that better suits our context. Moreover, we will introduce the symmetrization of finite Borel measures. More will be said about this in Section 3, but, let us give a brief insight here.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
, let
\sigma
be the singular part of
\mu
and
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
the Radon–Nikodym derivative of
\mu
. We define the finite Borel measure
\mu^{\#}
, and call it the symmetrization of
\mu
, to be the measure such that the Radon–Nikodym derivative of
\mu^{\#}
is the s.d.r.
f^{\#}
of
f
and the singular part of
\mu^{\#}
is
\sigma((-\pi,\pi))\delta_{0}
, which is a constant multiple of the Dirac measure
\delta_{0}
with a unit point mass at 0. To condense all this, if

(1.6)

d\mu=fd\lambda+d\sigma,

then we define
\mu^{\#}
such that

(1.7)

d\mu^{\#}=f^{\#}d\lambda+\sigma((-\pi,\pi))d\delta_{0}.

For more details, see Definitions in Subsection 3.2.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(S.d.r. and convex integral means)
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
and let
\mu^{\#}
be its symmetrization. Let
u_{\mu}
and
u_{\mu^{\#}}
be the corresponding solutions to the Dirichlet problem
(\ref{Poisson})
,
(\ref{Dirichlet})
. Then

(1.8)

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{\mu}(x)\bigr)dx\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{\mu^{\#}}(x)\bigr)dx

for any convex and increasing function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, if
\phi
is strictly convex, then
(\ref{ineq sdr})
holds as an equality if and only if the Radon–Nikodym derivative
f
of
\mu
is equal to its s.d.r.
f^{\#}
a.e. on
(-\pi,\pi)
and the singular part of
\mu
is a Dirac mass at the origin.

In particular, for
p\geq 1
, consider the convex and increasing function

\phi(s)=\begin{cases}0,&s\leq 0,\\
s^{p},&s\geq 0.\end{cases}

Then,
(\ref{ineq sdr})
gives inequalities for the
L^{p}
-norms of
u_{\mu}
and
u_{\mu^{\#}}
. Letting
p\to+\infty
we get the inequality for their
L^{\infty}
-norms, i.e.

(1.9)

||u_{\mu}||_{L^{p}}\leq||u_{\mu^{\#}}||_{L^{p}}\text{, for }1\leq p\leq+\infty.

In Section 6, we deal with the case of equality in
(\ref{inequality of Lp norms eisawgh})
.

Remark 1.2
.

In the case when
\mu
is an absolutely continuous measure, inequality
(\ref{ineq sdr})
was proved in
[
8
]
. In that paper, the case of equality was not investigated.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we present some fundamental facts about distributions as well as the general properties of the solutions
u_{\mu}.
Section 3 is dedicated to finite Borel measures. In that section, we talk about the Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodym decomposition of a measure, we introduce the notion of polarizations and symmetrizations for measures, and finally we give our second main theorem (Theorem
3.8
), which is a comparison theorem for polarization. Section 4 contains preliminary results that will be used to prove the main theorems; these proofs appear in Section 5. Finally, Section 6 connects the symmetrization of measures and the
L^{p}-
norms of solutions.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Distributions on
(-\pi,\pi)

In this subsection we present the fundamentals of distributions. The general cases for everything we present here without a proof can be found in
[
5
, Chapter 9]
. We shall start off with some notation.

Let
C_{c}^{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)
denote the set of infinitely differentiable real functions with compact support in
(-\pi,\pi)
. Now, consider the following notion of convergence in
C_{c}^{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)
: Let
\{\psi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)
. We say that the sequence
\{\psi_{n}\}
converges to zero in
C_{c}^{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)
if and only if:

1) there exists a compact set
K\subset(-\pi,\pi)
such that
\operatorname{supp}\psi_{n}\subseteq K

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

2) for each nonnegative integer
a
, we have

\max_{x\in K}\left|\frac{\partial^{a}}{\partial x^{a}}\psi_{n}(x)\right|\xrightarrow{n\to+\infty}0.

We say that
\{\psi_{n}\}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)
converges to
\psi
in
C_{c}^{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)
if and only if
\{\psi_{n}-\psi\}
converges to zero in
C_{c}^{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)
. The set of functions
C_{c}^{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)
equipped with this notion of convergence will be symbolized with
\mathcal{D}(-\pi,\pi)
and we call it the space of test functions in
(-\pi,\pi)
.

A continuous linear functional
T
on the space of test functions
\mathcal{D}(-\pi,\pi)
is called a distribution on
(-\pi,\pi).
We shall use the notation
<T,\psi>
to denote the value of
T
acting on the test function
\psi
. The set of all distributions on
(-\pi,\pi)
is symbolized with
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(-\pi,\pi)
. We call two distributions
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(-\pi,\pi)
equal if and only if
<T_{1},\psi>=<T_{2},\psi>
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(-\pi,\pi).

Example 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
. Then

<T_{f},\psi>\coloneqq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\psi(x)dx,\text{ for }\psi\in\mathcal{D}(-\pi,\pi),

defines a distribution on
(-\pi,\pi)
. Analogously, if
\mu
is a Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
, then

<T_{\mu},\psi>=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\psi(x)d\mu(x)\text{ for }\psi\in\mathcal{D}(-\pi,\pi),

also defines a distribution on
(-\pi,\pi)
. It is very common to identify
T_{f}
with
f
and
T_{\mu}
with
\mu
.

Definition 2.2

(Distributional derivatives)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(-\pi,\pi)
. The distribution
T^{\prime}
such that
<T^{\prime},\psi>=-<T,\psi^{\prime}>
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(-\pi,\pi)
is called the distributional derivative of
T
.

This process can be repeated ad infinitum. In this paper, we will use only the first and second distributional derivatives. The second distributional derivative
T^{\prime\prime}
of
T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(-\pi,\pi)
is given by

<T^{\prime\prime},\psi>=-<T^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}>=-(-<T,\psi^{\prime\prime}>)=<T,\psi^{\prime\prime}>.

Example 2.3
.

Let
x_{0}\in(-\pi,\pi)
be a fixed point. The Dirac measure
\delta_{x_{0}}
with point mass at
x_{0}
defines a distributions in the following way:

<\delta_{x_{0}},\psi>=\psi(x_{0}),\text{ for }\psi\in\mathcal{D}(-\pi,\pi).

2.2.
The Dirichlet problem for the distributional Poisson equation

Definition 2.4
.

The Green’s function for the Dirichlet boundary problem on the interval
[-\pi,\pi]

is

(2.1)

G(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}xy-\frac{1}{2}|x-y|+\frac{\pi}{2},\quad x,y\in[-\pi,\pi].

It is trivial to see that
G
vanishes on the boundary of the square
[-\pi,\pi]\times[-\pi,\pi]
, is strictly positive in the interior of the square, and attains global maximum
G(0,0)=\pi/2.
Also,
G(x,y)=G(y,x)
for all
x,y\in[-\pi,\pi].

Proposition 2.5
.

For
x,y\in(-\pi,\pi)
, the second distributional derivative with respect to
x
of
G(x,y)
is
-\delta_{y}(\{x\})
.

Proof.

Fix
y_{0}\in(-\pi,\pi).
Then

G_{x}(x,y_{0})=\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}(x,y_{0})=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2\pi}y_{0}+\frac{1}{2},&x\in(-\pi,y_{0}),\\
-\frac{1}{2\pi}y_{0}-\frac{1}{2},&x\in(y_{0},\pi).\end{cases}

So, for the second distributional derivative with respect to
x
of
G(x,y_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle<G_{xx},\psi>

\displaystyle=-<G_{x},\psi^{\prime}>=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G_{x}(x,y_{0})\psi^{\prime}(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\pi}^{y_{0}}\bigl(-\frac{1}{2\pi}y_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\psi^{\prime}(x)dx-\int^{\pi}_{y_{0}}\bigl(-\frac{1}{2\pi}y_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\psi^{\prime}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}y_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\int_{-\pi}^{y_{0}}\psi^{\prime}(x)dx+\bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}y_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\int_{y_{0}}^{\pi}\psi^{\prime}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}y_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigl(\psi(y_{0})-\psi(-\pi)\bigr)+\bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}y_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\bigl(\psi(\pi)-\psi(y_{0})\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\psi(y_{0}).

But
\displaystyle<\delta_{y_{0}},\psi>=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\psi(x)d\delta_{y_{0}}(x)=\psi(y_{0}).

∎

The following lemma is a slight improvement of the classic “differentiation under the integral sign” theorem. It will be used to prove the absolute continuity of solutions

in Proposition
2.7

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{M},\mu)
be a measure space. Suppose that a function
f:X\times[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}
, where
-\infty<a<b<+\infty
, satisfies the following conditions:

1.

f(x,t)
is a measurable function of
x
and
t
jointly, and
f(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}(\mu)
for all
t\in[a,b]
held fixed.

2.

For all
x\in X
,
f(x,t)
is an absolutely continuous function of
t
.

3.

\partial f/\partial t
belongs to
L^{1}(a,b)
.

Then
F(t)=\int_{X}f(x,t)d\mu(x)
is an absolutely continuous function of
t
, and for almost every
t\in[a,b]
, its derivative exists and is given by

F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}f(x,t)d\mu(x)=\int_{X}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(x,t)d\mu(x).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
. There exists a unique absolutely continuous function
u_{\mu}
on
[-\pi,\pi]
that solves the Poisson equation

-u_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}=\mu\text{ on }(-\pi,\pi)\text{ in the sense of distributions}

and satisfies the Dirichlet boundary conditions
u_{\mu}(-\pi)=u_{\mu}(\pi)=0
. The solution
u_{\mu}
can be represented as

(2.2)

u_{\mu}(x)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)d\mu(y).

Proof.

First, we prove the uniqueness. Let
u
and
v
be absolutely continuous solutions to the problem
(\ref{Poisson}),(\ref{Dirichlet}).
Consider
w(x)=u(x)-v(x)
, of which the second distributional derivative is the zero distribution. Subsequently, the first distributional derivative of
w
is a constant distribution, which means that there exists
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that
w^{\prime}(x)=a
a.e. on
[-\pi,\pi]
and thus there also exists
b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
w(x)=ax+b
a.e. on
[-\pi,\pi]
. We assumed that
u
and
v
are continuous, and so is
w
, therefore
w(x)=ax+b
on
[-\pi,\pi]
. Also,
w(\pm\pi)=u(\pm\pi)-v(\pm\pi)=0
, so
a=b=0
and
w\equiv 0
.

For the existence, we shall prove that
u_{\mu}
, as given in
(\ref{u_mu})
, solves the problem. Trivially,
u_{\mu}
satisfies the boundary conditions
u_{\mu}(\pm\pi)=0
, since
G(x,\pm\pi)=0.
Also, it is easy to verify that
u_{\mu}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
2.6
and thus it is absolutely continuous on
[-\pi,\pi]
. To see that
-u_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}=\mu
in the sense of distributions, we apply Fubini’s Theorem and Proposition
2.5
:

\displaystyle-<u_{\mu}^{\prime\prime},\psi>

\displaystyle=-<u_{\mu},\psi^{\prime\prime}>=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}u_{\mu}(x)\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)d\mu(y)\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)\psi^{\prime\prime}(x)dxd\mu(y)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}<G,\psi^{\prime\prime}>d\mu(y)=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}<G_{xx},\psi>d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(-\psi(y))d\mu(y)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\psi(y)d\mu(y)=<\mu,\psi>.

∎

3.
Finite Borel measures

3.1.
Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodym decomposition

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi).

•

If
\mu(\{x\})=0
for all
x\in(-\pi,\pi)
, then
\mu
is called continuous.

•

If there exists a (possibly finite) sequence
\{x_{i}\}
of distinct points in
(-\pi,\pi)
and a sequence of numbers
\{a_{i}\}\in\ell^{1}
such that
\mu=\sum_{i}a_{i}\delta_{x_{i}},
where
\delta_{x_{i}}
is the Dirac measure with unit mass at the point
x_{i}
, then
\mu
is called discrete or purely discontinuous.

Note that all discrete measures are singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure and all absolutely continuous measures with respect to the Lebesgue measure are continuous. The converse of neither of the above statements is true.

The following Theorem can be found in the literature for measures that are regular Borel (
[
6
, Theorem 19.61]
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mu
be a (positive) regular Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Then
\mu
can be expressed in a unique way as

\mu=\nu+\sigma+\delta,

where
\nu,\sigma
and
\delta
are (positive) regular Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
,
\nu\ll\lambda
,
\sigma\perp\lambda
,
\delta
is purely discontinuous and
\sigma
is continuous.

Remark 3.3
.

According to
[
10
, Theorem A.2.2]
, if
\mu
is a finite positive Borel measure on a metric space
X
, then
\mu
is a regular measure. Since
(-\pi,\pi)
is a metric space with the euclidean metric, the measures throughout our paper are regular, and thus Theorem
3.2
applies to them. Moreover, considering the absolutely continuous part
\nu
of a positive Borel measure
\mu
, note that the Radon–Nikodym derivative
f
of
\nu
is Lebesgue integrable, since
\nu
is finite, and
f\geq 0
a.e. on
[-\pi,\pi]
, since
\nu
is positive.

3.2.
Symmetrization and polarizations of measures

The polarizations and the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of functions are well established notions in literature, and the reader can find the more general definitions of them in books such as
[
2
]
. In our paper, since we work on the interval
[-\pi,\pi]
and have Dirichlet boundary conditions, we can assume that the functions and measures with which we work are identically zero outside
[-\pi,\pi]
(more details on the thought process that led to the definitions below can be found on
[
9
]
). With the assumptions described above, we can now present the definitions of s.d.r. and polarization in a way that better fits our context, but also respects the general definitions. Furthermore, we introduce the notion of symmetrization of Borel measures on
(-\pi,\pi)
and we generalize the polarization of functions to the notion of polarization of measures that do not have a singular continuous part.

Definition 3.4

(Symmetric decreasing rearrangement of functions and symmetrization of measures)
.

•

Let
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
. We define
f^{\ast}:[0,2\pi]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
as

f^{\ast}(t)=\begin{cases}\esssup f,&t=0,\\
\inf\bigl\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\lambda\bigl(\{x\in[-\pi,\pi]:f(x)>s\}\bigr)\leq t\bigr\},&t\in(0,2\pi),\\
\essinf f,&t=2\pi.\end{cases}

The function
f^{\ast}
is called the decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

We also define
f^{\#}:[-\pi,\pi]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
, given by
f^{\#}(t)=f^{\ast}(2|t|)
for
t\in[-\pi,\pi]
.

•

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
. Let
\mu=\nu+\sigma+\delta
be the decomposition of
\mu
as described in Theorem
3.2
. Let
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
such that
d\nu=fd\lambda
and let
M=\sigma\bigl((-\pi,\pi)\bigr)+\delta\bigl((-\pi,\pi)\bigr)
. We define the symmetrization of
\mu
to be the measure
\mu^{\#}
, such that

d\mu^{\#}=f^{\#}d\lambda+Md\delta_{0}.

Definition 3.5

(Polarization of functions)
.

Let
b\in(-\pi,0)\cup(0,\pi)
and
H
be the open half-line of
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{b\}
which contains
0
. Let
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
.

•

If
b\in(0,\pi),
we define the polarization (towards zero) of
f
with respect to
b
(or the polarization of
f
with respect to
H=(-\infty,b)
) as

f_{H}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&x\in[-\pi,2b-\pi),\\
\max\{f(x),f(2b-x)\},&x\in[2b-\pi,b],\\
\min\{f(x),f(2b-x)\},&x\in[b,\pi].\end{cases}

•

If
b\in(-\pi,0),
we define the polarization (towards zero) of
f
with respect to
b
(or the polarization of

f(x),&x\in(2b+\pi,\pi].\end{cases}

with respect to

for

This leads to the following definition.

Definition 3.6

(Polarization of a measure without singular continuous part)
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on

that can be written as

, where

Theorem 3.8

(Polarization and convex integral means)

Let
\mu

3.3.
Hardy-Littlewood inequalities

The next two propositions are special cases of the Hardy-Littlewood inequalities for the polarization and the symmetric decreasing rearrangement, respectively. The reader can find them in their most general form as Theorems 2.9 and 2.15 of

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
0\leq f,g\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
. Let

and
H
be the open half-line of
\mathbb{R}
that contains 0. Let

and

and

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(y)g(y)dy\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f_{H}(y)g_{H}(y)dy.

Equality holds if and only if the set
A_{H}=B_{H}\cup C_{H}
has zero Lebesgue measure, where

B_{H}=\{y\in H\cap[-\pi,\pi]:f(y)<f(2b-y)\text{ and }g(y)>g(2b-y)\}

and

C_{H}=\{y\in H\cap[-\pi,\pi]:f(y)>f(2b-y)\text{ and }g(y)<g(2b-y)\}.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let

, and let

and

be their symmetric decreasing rearrangements. Then

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(y)g(y)dy\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f^{\#}(y)g^{\#}(y)dy.

Equality holds if and only if the set

A=\bigl\{(x,y)\in[-\pi,\pi]^{2}:f(x)<f(y)\text{ and }g(x)>g(y)\bigr\}

has zero two-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

Lemma 4.1

(Karamata’s inequality)
.

Let
\phi:[0,M]\to\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}
be a convex and increasing function, where
M>0
. Let
x_{1},x_{2}
and
y_{1},y_{2}
be two pairs of real numbers contained in
[0,M]
such that

(a)

x_{1}\geq x_{2}
and
y_{1}\geq y_{2}
,

(b)

x_{1}\leq y_{1}
,

(c)

x_{1}+x_{2}\leq y_{1}+y_{2}
.

Under these assumptions, the following inequality holds

(4.1)

\phi(x_{1})+\phi(x_{2})\leq\phi(y_{1})+\phi(y_{2}).

Proof.

If
x_{1}=y_{1}
, then
x_{2}\leq y_{2}
and thus
(\ref{karamata ineq})
holds trivially, by the monotonicity of
\phi.
Therefore, we may assume that
y_{2}<x_{2}\leq x_{1}<y_{2}
. Because
\phi
is increasing, we have

c_{2}\coloneqq\frac{\phi(y_{2})-\phi(x_{2})}{y_{2}-x_{2}}\geq 0,

and because
\phi
is convex, we have

c_{1}\coloneqq\frac{\phi(y_{1})-\phi(x_{1})}{y_{1}-x_{1}}\geq\frac{\phi(y_{2})-\phi(x_{2})}{y_{2}-x_{2}}=c_{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\phi(y_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\phi(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\phi(y_{1})-\phi(x_{1})+\phi(y_{2})-\phi(x_{2})

\displaystyle=c_{1}(y_{1}-x_{1})+c_{2}(y_{2}-x_{2})

\displaystyle=c_{1}(y_{1}-x_{1})+c_{2}(y_{2}+y_{1}-y_{1}-x_{2}-x_{1}+x_{1})

\displaystyle=c_{1}(y_{1}-x_{1})-c_{2}(y_{1}-x_{1})+c_{2}(y_{2}+y_{1}-x_{2}-x_{1})

\displaystyle=(c_{1}-c_{2})(y_{1}-x_{1})+c_{2}(y_{1}+y_{2}-x_{1}-x_{2})\geq 0.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be the Green’s function for the Dirichlet problem on

y\in[2b+\pi,\pi]

\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)f(y)dy\leq\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x^{\prime},y)f(y)dy=\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)dy.

By the definition of polarization, we have
f_{H}(y)=\min\{f(y),f(y^{\prime})\}
for all
y\in[-\pi,b],
where
y^{\prime}=2b-y
. Also,
f_{H}(y^{\prime})=\max\{f(y),f(y^{\prime})\}
, since
y^{\prime}\in[b,2b-y]
, and thus
f_{H}(y)\leq f_{H}(y^{\prime})
for all
y\in[-\pi,b].

Note that
\{f_{H}(y),f_{H}(y^{\prime})\}=\{f(y),f(y^{\prime})\}
, thus we can distinguish two possible cases:

either

f_{H}(y)=f(y)\text{ and }f_{H}(y^{\prime})=f(y^{\prime})

or

f_{H}(y)=f(y^{\prime})\text{ and }f_{H}(y^{\prime})=f(y).

Assume that the former case is true, then
f(y)=f_{H}(y)\leq f_{H}(y^{\prime})=f(y^{\prime})
.
By Lemma
4.2
, we have
G(x,y)-G(x^{\prime},y)\leq G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-G(x,y^{\prime}),
and, thus

G(x,y)f(y)-G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)\leq G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})f_{H}(y^{\prime})-G(x,y^{\prime})f(y^{\prime}).

Similarly, if the latter case holds, then
f(y^{\prime})=f_{H}(y)\leq f_{H}(y^{\prime})=f(y)
. Inequality (
4.3
) implies that
G(x,y^{\prime})-G(x^{\prime},y)\leq G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-G(x,y)
. Thus,

G(x,y^{\prime})f(y^{\prime})-G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)\leq G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})f_{H}(y^{\prime})-G(x,y)f(y).

We conclude that in both cases, for
y\in[-\pi,b]
we have

G(x,y)f(y)+G(x,y^{\prime})f(y^{\prime})\leq G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)+G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})f_{H}(y^{\prime}),

and, integrating over
[-\pi,b]
with respect to
y
, we get

\int_{-\pi}^{b}\bigl(G(x,y)f(y)+G(x,y^{\prime})f(y^{\prime})\bigr)dy\leq\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{b}\bigl(G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)+G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})f_{H}(y^{\prime})\bigr)dy,

and with a change of variables this implies that

\int_{-\pi}^{2b+\pi}G(x,y)f(y)dy\leq\int_{-\pi}^{2b+\pi}G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)dy.

We have already shown that

\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)f(y)dy\leq\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)dy,

and, adding these two inequalities, we end up with
(\ref{uf uf polwsh polwsh})
.

(c) Let
x\in[b,\pi]
. By Example
3.7
,
G(x,\cdot)\equiv\bigl(G(x,\cdot)\bigr)_{H}
. Thus, by Proposition
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle u_{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)f(y)dy\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl(G(x,y)\bigr)_{H}f_{H}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)f_{H}(y)dy=u_{f_{H}}(x).

Considering the equivalence statement on Lemma
4.3
(c), it is trivial to see that
(iv)\implies(iii)\implies(ii)\implies(i)
, and to close the circle, we just have to show that
(i)\implies(iv)
.

Assume that
(\ref{uf uf polwsh})
holds as an equality for some
x\in[b,\pi).

By Proposition
3.9
, we have that
\lambda(B_{H}\cup C_{H})=0
, where

B_{H}=\{y\in(b,\pi]:f(y)<f(2b-y)\text{ and }G(x,y)>G(x,2b-y)\}

and

C_{H}=\{y\in(b,\pi]:f(y)>f(2b-y)\text{ and }G(x,y)<G(x,2b-y)\}.

By Example
3.7
,
G(x,y)>G(x,2b-y)
for all
y\in(b,2b+\pi)
. Note that we have considered
G
to be identically zero outside of
[-\pi,\pi]^{2}
. Thus,
C_{H}=\varnothing
and
B_{H}=\{y\in(b,2b+\pi):f(y)<f(2b-y)\}
. So,
f=f_{H}
a.e. on
(b,2b+\pi)
and, by the definition of polarization,
f=f_{H}
a.e. on
[-\pi,\pi]
.
∎

For (b) and (c), note that

.

(B)

The measure
\delta

.

(C)

The measure
\delta
is the sum of unit masses at two points which are symmetric with respect to

We assumed that, if

(A)

(b) Let

In case
(A)

(c) Let

Definition 5.3
.

Let

by

where the supremum is taken over all Borel measurable subsets

of

with

The following propositions present all the properties of the

2
]

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
. Then:

(a)

For each
t\in[0,2\pi]
there exists a Borel set
E\subset[-\pi,\pi]
with
\lambda(E)=t
such that

f^{\star}(t)=\int_{E}fd\lambda.

(b)

For all

, where
f^{*}
is the decreasing rearrangement of

.

In particular,
(b)
implies that

where

Proposition 5.5
.

Let

. The following are equivalent.

(a)

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(f(x)\bigr)dx\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(g(x)\bigr)dx

(b)

For any fixed

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl(f(x)-y\bigr)^{+}dx\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl(g(x)-y\bigr)^{+}dx
, where

(c)

f^{\star}(t)\leq g^{\star}(t)
for all

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\sigma
be a finite Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
such that
\sigma\perp\lambda
. Let
\sigma^{\#}
be the symmetrization of
\sigma
. Then

(5.4)

u^{\star}_{\sigma}\leq u^{\star}_{\sigma^{\#}}\text{ on }[0,2\pi].

Proof.

First, we apply Theorem
3.11
for

such that

and we get the inequality

(5.5)

\int_{-\lambda(E)/2}^{\lambda(E)/2}G(0,y)dy\geq\int_{E}G(x,y)dy\text{ for all }x\in[-\pi,\pi],

We use this inequality and apply Fubini’s Theorem twice to get

\displaystyle u_{\sigma}^{\star}(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lambda(E)=t}\int_{E}u_{\sigma}(s)ds=\sup_{\lambda(E)=t}\int_{E}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(s,y)d\sigma(y)ds

Proof.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
. Then

(5.9)

u_{\mu}^{\star}\leq u_{\mu^{\#}}^{\star}\text{ on }[0,2\pi].

Proof.

Let
0\leq f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
and let
\sigma
be the singular measure on
(-\pi,\pi)
such that
d\mu=fd\lambda+d\sigma
. Note that
u_{\mu}(x)=u_{f}(x)+u_{\sigma}(x)
and
u_{\mu^{\#}}(x)=u_{f^{\#}}(x)+u_{\sigma^{\#}}(x)
for all
x\in[-\pi,\pi]
. Note also that
u_{f}
is a concave function; (the proof of this is an easy Calculus exercise using the representation of
u_{f}
given by Proposition
2.7
). Even more directly, one can see that
u_{f^{\#}}
is an even function and, because it is also concave, we have
(u_{f^{\#}})^{\#}=u_{f^{\#}}
. Furthermore,
u_{\sigma^{\#}}
is also its own s.d.r., quite trivially (see Example
2.10
), and, thus,
u_{\mu^{\#}}
is equal to its own s.d.r. as well.

We can now prove our desired inequality using all these preliminary facts, Proposition
4.7
, Lemma
5.6
and Theorem
5.7
as follows:

\displaystyle u_{\mu}^{\star}(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lambda(E)=t}\int_{E}u_{\mu}(s)ds=\sup_{\lambda(E)=t}\int_{E}\bigl(u_{f}(s)+u_{\sigma}(s)\bigr)ds

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\lambda(E)=t}\int_{E}u_{f}(s)ds+\sup_{\lambda(E)=t}\int_{E}u_{\sigma}(s)ds=u_{f}^{\star}(t)+u_{\sigma}^{\star}(t)

\displaystyle\leq u^{\star}_{f^{\#}}(t)+u^{\star}_{\sigma^{\#}}(t)=\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}\bigl(u_{f^{\#}}(s)\bigr)^{\#}ds+\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}\bigl(u_{\sigma^{\#}}(s)\bigr)^{\#}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}u_{f^{\#}}(s)ds+\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}u_{\sigma^{\#}}(s)ds=\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}\bigl(u_{f^{\#}}(s)+u_{\sigma^{\#}}(s)\bigr)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}u_{\mu^{\#}}(s)ds=\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}\bigl(u_{\mu^{\#}}(s)\bigr)^{\#}ds=u_{\mu^{\#}}(t).

∎

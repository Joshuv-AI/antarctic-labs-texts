Tilt stability of a class of nonlinear semidefinite programs
Yulan Liu, Shaohua Pan and Shujun Bi

Abstract
This paper concerns the tilt stability of local optimal solutions to a class of nonlinear semidefinite programs, which involves a twice continuously differentiable objective function and a convex feasible set. By leveraging the second subderivative of the extended-valued objective function and imposing a suitable restriction on the multiplier, we derive two point-based sufficient characterizations for tilt stability of local optimal solutions around which the objective function has positive semidefinite Hessians, and for a class of linear positive semidefinite cone constraint set, establish a point-based necessary characterization with a certain gap from the sufficient one. For this class of linear positive semidefinite cone constraint case, under a suitable restriction on the set of multipliers, we also establish a point-based sufficient and necessary characterization, which is weaker than the dual constraint nondegeneracy when the set of multipliers is singleton. As far as we know, this is the first work to study point-based sufficient and/or necessary characterizations for nonlinear semidefinite programs with convex constraint sets without constraint nondegeneracy conditions.



Keywords
Nonlinear semidefinite programs; tilt stability; second subderivative

Introduction

The notion of tilt stability was proposed by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
22
]
for the local minimum of an extended real-valued function, which applies to constrained optimization problems via the indicator function of the constraint set. Such stability is a kind of single-valued Lipschitzian behavior of local minimum with respect to one-parametric linear or tilt perturbation, which is basically equivalent to a uniform second-order growth condition as well as strong metric regularity of the subdifferential by
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
(see also
[
14
]
). Its introduction aims to justify convergence properties, stopping criteria, and the robustness of numerical algorithms. Tilt stability has attracted wide attention in the literature especially in recent years (see, e.g.,
[
17
,
6
,
4
,
1
,
20
]
).

In
[
22
]
, Poliquin and Rockafellar proved that for the local minimum of an extended real-valued function, under the mild assumptions of prox-regularity and subdifferential continuity, a stationary point is a tilt-stable local minimizer if and only if the second-order limiting subdifferential/generalized Hessian introduced by Mordukhovich
[
12
]
is positive-definite at the point in question. Later, by developing a new second-order subdifferential calculus, Mordukhovich and Rockafellar
[
13
]
derived second-order characterizations of tilt-stable minimizers for some classes of constrained optimization problems. Among others, for
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth nonlinear programs (NLPs), they showed that under the linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ), a stationary point is a tilt-stable local minimizer if and only if the strong second-order sufficient condition (SSOSC) holds. This result implies that in this setting, if the LICQ at the point in question holds, the tilt stability is equivalent to Robinson’s strong regularity
[
23
]
of the associated KKT system. Observe that tilt stability does not postulate LICQ as a necessary condition. This inspires some researchers to delve into tilt stability for NLPs under weaker conditions.

Mordukhovich and Outrata
[
15
]
proved that SSOSC is sufficient for a stationary point to be a tilt-stable local minimizer of NLPs under the MFCQ and the constant rank constraint qualification (CRCQ). Mordukhovich and Nghia
[
17
]
showed that SSOSC is indeed not a necessary condition for tilt stability and then introduced the uniform second-order sufficient condition to characterize tilt stability when both MFCQ and CRCQ occur. Recently, Gfrerer and Mordukhovich
[
6
]
obtained some point-based second-order sufficient conditions for tilt-stable local minimizers of NLPs under some weak conditions including the so-called metric subregularity constraint qualification (MSCQ) and the bounded extreme point property, and established some point-based second-order characterizations of tilt stability under certain additional assumptions. All the aforementioned references employ the uniform positive-definiteness of second-order limiting subdifferentials
[
22
,
13
,
14
]
or combined second-order subdifferentials
[
17
,
6
]
. Different from them, Chieu et al.’s paper
[
4
]
adopted the subgradient graphical derivative of an extended real-valued function to characterize tilt stability and extend to the case of NLPs.

Though the tilt stability of NLPs has received active attention in the past ten years, there are a few works on the tilt stability of non-polyhedral conic programs and most of the existing results were obtained under strong constraint nondegeneracy assumption. Morduhovich et al.
[
16
]
employed the second-order subdifferential calculation from
[
21
]
to present a characterization of tilt-stable minimizers for second-order cone programs (SOCPs) in the extended SSOSC under a nondegeneracy assumption. Similar characterizations involving nondegeneracy and uniqueness of Lagrange multipliers were also established in
[
18
,
17
]
for conic programs with a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-cone reducible constraint set, where the second-order subdifferential terms are calculated in
[
18
]
. Recently, for the minimization of a sum of a twice continuously differentiable convex function and a proper lower continuous (lsc) convex function, Nghia
[
20
]
presented a novel but abstract characterization for tilt stability by the quadratic growth of the nonsmooth convex function with respect to a certain set, and for the nonsmooth function being the indicator of positive semidefinite (PSD) cone, achieved a specific point-based sufficient and necessary characterization. Note that the constraint nondegeneracy automatically holds for this class of composite optimization. To the best of our knowledge, Benko et al.’s work
[
1
]
is the only one to achieve the characterization of tilt stability for non-polyhedral conic programs without constraint nondegeneracy. They obtained neighborhood characterizations and much more difficult point-based characterizations under an appropriate MSCQ for the tilt stability of SOCPs. However, the SOCP considered in
[
1
]
only involves a second-order cone, which is essentially equivalent to an NLP.

In this paper, we are interested in the characterization of tilt-stable local minimizers for nonlinear semidefinite programs (SDPs) with a convex feasible set. Let
\mathbb{S}^{n}
denote the space of all
n\times n
real symmetric matrices, equipped with the trace inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and its induced Frobenius norm
\|\cdot\|_{F}
, and let
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
be the cone consisting of all PSD matrices from the space
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. This class of nonlinear SDPs takes the following form

\min_{x\in\mathbb{X}}\Big\{\varphi(x)\ \ {\rm s.t.}\ \ \mathcal{A}x=b,\,g(x)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}\Big\},

(1)

where
\varphi\!:\mathbb{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper lsc function that is twice continuously differentiable on an open set
\mathcal{O}\supset\Gamma\!:=\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)\cap g^{-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})
,
\mathcal{A}\!:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the linear mapping and vector, and
g\!:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
is a twice continuously differentiable mapping that will be assumed to be
\mathbb{S}_{-}^{n}
-convex later. The main contribution of this work is to derive point-based sufficient and/or necessary characterizations for tilt-stable local minimizers of (
1
) without constraint nondegeneracy assumption, which greatly enhances the result of
[
20
]
for a special SDP as well as provides a novel way to study tilt stability of this class of non-polyhedral conic programs. Different from the existing works, we establish our results by operating directly the second subderivative of the extended-valued objective function
\Phi\!:=\varphi+\delta_{\Gamma}
, where
\delta_{\Gamma}
is the indicator of the feasible set
\Gamma
. In addition, we overcome the difficulty caused by the nonuniqueness of Lagrange multipliers by imposing a restriction on the multiplier skillfully. Specifically, in Section
3.1
we derive two point-based sufficient characterizations for tilt-stable local minimizers around which
\nabla^{2}\varphi(x)
is positive semidefinite; see Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
. Among others, the characterization of Theorem
3.1
needs the multiplier of minimum rank, while that of Theorem
3.2
imposes a restriction on the set of multiplier sets but does not require it to be a singleton. In Section
3.2
, for a class of linear mapping
g
, we obtain a point-based necessary characterization with a certain gap from the converse of the sufficient characterization of Theorem
3.1
. Then, we sharpen this result and establish a point-based sufficient and necessary characterization. In Section
3.3
, these characterizations are applied to the standard linear primal and dual SDPs, and are demonstrated to be weaker than the dual constraint nondegeneracy.

2.1
Tangent cone and second-order tangent set

Before introducing tangent cone to a set, we recall the notion of subderivative function.

Definition 2.2

(see
[
24
, Definitions 8.1
\&
7.20]
) Consider a function
h\!:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{\overline{R}}
and a point
x\in{\rm dom}\,h
. Denote the first-order difference quotients of
h
at
x
by

\Delta_{\tau}h(x)(w^{\prime}):=\tau^{-1}\big[h(x+\tau w^{\prime})-h(x)\big]\quad{\rm for}\ \tau>0.

The subderivative function of
h
at
x
, denoted by
dh(x)\!:\mathbb{X}\to[-\infty,\infty]
, is defined as

dh(x)(w):=\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0\atop w^{\prime}\to w}\Delta_{\tau}h(x)(w^{\prime})\quad{\rm for}\ w\in\mathbb{X}.

With the subderivative function, we follow the same line as in
[
10
]
to introduce the critical cone of an extended real-valued function
h\!:\mathbb{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
at a point pair
(x,v)\in{\rm gph}\,\partial h
:

\mathcal{C}_{h}(x,v):=\big\{w\in\mathbb{X}\ |\ dh(x)(w)=\langle v,w\rangle\big\}.

When
h=\delta_{\Omega}
for a nonempty closed set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{X}
, the subderivative
dh(x)
for
x\in{\rm dom}\,h
is the indicator of
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(x)
, the tangent cone to
\Omega
at
x
, and now
\mathcal{C}_{h}(x,v)=\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(x)\cap[\![v]\!]^{\perp}:=\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}(x,v)
.
The tangent and inner tangent cones to
\Omega
at
x\in\Omega
are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(x)

\displaystyle:=\big\{w\in\mathbb{X}\ |\ \exists\,\tau_{k}\downarrow 0\ \ {\rm s.t.}\ \ {\rm dist}(x+\tau_{k}w,\Omega)=o(\tau_{k})\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}^{i}(x)

\displaystyle:=\big\{w\in\mathbb{X}\ |\ {\rm dist}(x+\tau w,\Omega)=o(\tau)\ \ \forall\tau\geq 0\big\}.

We stipulate that
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(x)\!=\!\mathcal{T}^{i}_{\Omega}(x)=\emptyset
when
x\notin\Omega
. The second-order tangent set to
\Omega
at
x\in\Omega
for
w\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(x)
is defined as

\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\Omega}(x,w):=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{X}\ |\ \exists\,\tau_{k}\downarrow 0\ \ {\rm s.t.}\ \ {\rm dist}(x+\tau_{k}w+(\tau_{k}^{2}/2)z,\Omega)=o(\tau_{k}^{2})\Big\},

while the inner second-order tangent set to
\Omega
at
x\in\Omega
for
w\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}^{i}(x)
is defined as

\mathcal{T}^{i,2}_{\Omega}(x,w):=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{X}\ |\ {\rm dist}(x+\tau w+(\tau^{2}/2)z,\Omega)=o(\tau^{2})\quad\forall\tau\geq 0\Big\}.

One can check that
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\Omega}(x,w)=\emptyset
if
w\notin\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(x)
, and
\mathcal{T}^{i,2}_{\Omega}(x,w)=\emptyset
if
w\notin\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}^{i}(x)
. The set
\Omega
is called parabolically derivable at
x
for
w\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}(x)
if
\mathcal{T}^{i,2}_{\Omega}(x,w)=\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\Omega}(x,w)\neq\emptyset
.

0&0\\
\end{pmatrix}P^{\top}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ S=P\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&{\rm Diag}(\lambda^{-}(S))\end{pmatrix}P^{\top}

(2)

with
P\!\in\mathbb{O}(X)\cap\mathbb{O}(S)
. Let
P_{0}
(resp.
P_{+}
and

) be the matrix obtained by removing those columns of

\{i\in[n]\,|\,\lambda_{i}(X)=0=\lambda_{i}(S)\}
(resp.
\{i\in[n]\,|\,\lambda_{i}(X)>0\}

[
25

[

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X)=\Big\{W\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\ |\ [P_{0}\ \ P_{-}]^{\top}W[P_{0}\ \ P_{-}]\succeq 0\Big\},\qquad\quad

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X)=\Big\{W\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\ |\ [P_{0}\ \ P_{-}]^{\top}W[P_{0}\ \ P_{-}]\preceq 0,\,P_{+}^{\top}WP=0\Big\},

(4)

while the critical cone of
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
at
X
associated with

takes the following form

\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X,S)=\Big\{W\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\,|\,P_{0}^{\top}WP_{0}\succeq 0,\,P_{0}^{\top}WP_{-}=0\ {\rm and}\ P_{-}^{\top}WP_{-}=0\Big\}.

(5)

The following lemma characterizes the support of the second-order tangent set of
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
.

Lemma 2.1

Consider any
X\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
and
S\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}(X)
. Let
X
and
S
have the eigenvalue decomposition as in (
2
) with
\alpha=\{i\in[n]\,|\,\lambda_{i}(X)>0\}
and
\gamma=\{i\in[n]\,|\,\lambda_{i}(S)<0\}
. Then, for any
W\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}(X,S)
, with
\widetilde{W}:=P^{\top}WP
there holds

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(X,W)}(S)=2\langle S,WX^{\dagger}W\rangle=\left\{\begin{matrix}&2\sum\limits_{i\in\alpha,j\in\gamma}\frac{\lambda_{j}(S)}{\lambda_{i}(X)}(\widetilde{W}_{ij})^{2}&{\rm if}\ \ X\neq 0,\\
&0&{\rm otherwise}.\end{matrix}\right.

Proof:

Fix any
W\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}(X,S)=\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}(X)\cap[\![S]\!]^{\perp}
. Invoking
[
2
, Proposition 3.33]
with
K=\mathbb{S}_{-}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}\ni Z\mapsto G(Z):=-Z
leads to
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}^{2}(X,W)=-\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}_{-}^{n}}^{2}(-X,-W)
. Then,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}^{2}(X,W)}(S)

\displaystyle=\sup_{Z\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}^{2}(X,W)}\langle-Z,-S\rangle=\sup_{Z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}_{-}^{n}}^{2}(-X,-W)}\langle Z^{\prime},-S\rangle

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}_{-}^{n}}^{2}(-X,-W)}(-S)=2\langle S,WX^{\dagger}W\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle P^{\top}SP,\widetilde{W}P^{\top}X^{\dagger}P\widetilde{W}\rangle=2\sum\limits_{i\in\alpha,j\in\gamma}\frac{\lambda_{j}(S)}{\lambda_{i}(X)}(\widetilde{W}_{ij})^{2},

where the sixth equality is obtained by using equation (
2
).

\Box

2.2
Second subderivative

To introduce the second subderivative of an extended real-valued function
h\!:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{\overline{R}}
, we define its
second-order difference quotient at a point
x
for a vector
v
by

\Delta^{2}_{\tau}h(x|v)(w^{\prime}):=\frac{h(x\!+\!\tau w^{\prime})-h(x)-\tau\langle v,w^{\prime}\rangle}{\tau^{2}/2}\quad{\rm for}\ \tau>0.

Definition 2.3

(
[
24
, Definitions 13.3
\&
13.6]
) Consider a proper
h\!:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{\overline{R}}
, a point
x\in{\rm dom}\,h
and a vector
v\in\mathbb{X}
. The second subderivative of
h
at
x
for
v
is defined as

d^{2}h(x|v)(w):=\liminf_{\tau\downarrow 0\atop w^{\prime}\to w}\Delta^{2}_{\tau}h(x|v)(w^{\prime})\quad\ \forall w\,\in\mathbb{X}.

By
[
24
, Proposition 13.5]
, the function
d^{2}h(x|v)
is lsc and positively homogenous of degree
2
; and if it is proper, the inclusion
{\rm dom}\,d^{2}h(x|v)\!\subset\!\big\{w\!\in\!\mathbb{X}\,|\,dh(x)(w)\!=\!\langle v,w\rangle\big\}
holds.

Lemma 2.2

Consider any
X\!\in\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)
and
S\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)}(X)
. Then, for any
W\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,

d^{2}\delta_{\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)}(X|S)(W)=\delta_{{\rm Ker}\mathcal{A}}(W).

Proof:

Fix any
W\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The polyhedrality of
\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)
implies that it is parabolically derivable at
X
. Invoking
[
11
, Theorem 3.3 (i)]
with
\Omega=\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)
results in

{\rm dom}\,d^{2}\delta_{\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)}(X|S)=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)}(X,S)={\rm Ker}\mathcal{A}\cap[\![S]\!]^{\perp}.

As
S\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)}(X)={\rm Im}\mathcal{A}^{*}
, we have
{\rm Ker}\mathcal{A}\cap[\![S]\!]^{\perp}={\rm Ker}\mathcal{A}
. Together with the above equation, when
W\notin{\rm Ker}\mathcal{A}
,
d^{2}\delta_{\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)}(X|S)(W)=\infty
. When
W\!\in{\rm Ker}\mathcal{A}
, applying
[
11
, Theorem 5.6 & Eq. (5.2)]
with
f(X)=\mathcal{A}X-b
and
\Theta=\{0\}^{m}
yields that

\displaystyle d^{2}\delta_{\mathcal{A}^{-1}(b)}(X|S)(W)

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{-1}(S)}d^{2}\delta_{\Theta}(\mathcal{A}X\!-\!b\,|\,y)(\mathcal{A}W)

\displaystyle=\max_{y\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{-1}(S)}{-\sigma_{\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{A}X-b,\mathcal{A}W)}(y)}=0,

where the second equality is by
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
, and the third is implied by
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\Theta}(\mathcal{A}X\!-\!b,\mathcal{A}W)=\{0\}
because
\Theta=\{0\}^{m}
and
\mathcal{A}W=0
. By the arbitrariness of
W\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we get the desired equality.

\Box

2.3
Metric (sub)regularity of multifunctions

Let
\mathcal{F}\!:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Z}
be a multifunction. Denote its domain by
{\rm dom}\,\mathcal{F}:=\{x\in\mathbb{X}\,|\,\mathcal{F}(x)\neq\emptyset\}
. The mapping
\mathcal{F}
is closed if its graph, denoted by
{\rm gph}\,\mathcal{F}\!:=\big\{(x,z)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Z}\,|\,z\in\mathcal{F}(x)\big\}
, is a closed set in
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Z}
. We say that the mapping
\mathcal{F}
is outer semicontinuous (osc) at
x\in\mathbb{X}
if
\limsup_{x^{\prime}\to x}\mathcal{F}(x^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{F}(x)
, and say that
\mathcal{F}
is osc if it is osc everywhere.

Definition 2.4

A multifunction
\mathcal{F}\!:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Z}
is said to be (metrically) regular at
(x,z)\in{\rm gph}\,\mathcal{F}
if there exist
\delta>0
and
\kappa>0
such that for all
(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}((x,z),\delta)
,

{\rm dist}(x^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{-1}(z^{\prime}))\leq\kappa\,{\rm dist}(z^{\prime},\mathcal{F}(x^{\prime})),

while the multifunction
\mathcal{F}
is said to be (metrically) subregular at a point
(x,z)\in{\rm gph}\,\mathcal{F}
if there exist
\delta>0
and
\kappa>0
such that for all
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}(x,\delta)
,

{\rm dist}(x^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{-1}(z))\leq\kappa\,{\rm dist}(z,\mathcal{F}(x^{\prime})).

Remark 2.1

(a)
The metric regularity of
\mathcal{F}
is robust in the sense that if
\mathcal{F}
is metrically regular at
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in{\rm gph}\mathcal{F}
, then there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{Z}
of
(\overline{x},\overline{y})
such that the mapping
\mathcal{F}
is metrically regular at any
(x,y)\in\mathcal{Z}
with
y\in\mathcal{F}(x)
.

(b)
The metric subregularity of
\mathcal{F}

3.3
Application to linear SDPs

Consider the space

\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},S\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\big\{b^{\top}y\ \ {\rm s.t.}\ \mathcal{A}^{*}y+S=C,\,S\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\big\}.

Proposition 3.2

\mathcal{G}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}X-b\\
X\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}\{0\}^{m}\\
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\end{pmatrix}\quad\ \forall X\in\mathbb{S}^{n}

4
Conclusion

For the nonlinear SDP problem (
1
) with a convex feasible set, we derived point-based sufficient characterizations in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
for its tilt-stable local optimal solutions by using the multiplier of the minimum rank and imposing a suitable restriction on the multiplier set, respectively. Then, for the linear PSD constraint set, we established a point-based necessary characterization in Theorem
3.3
with a certain gap from the converse of Theorem
3.1
, and a point-based necessary characterization in Theorem
3.4
without gap from the converse of Theorem
3.2
. These results are applied to the standard linear primal and dual SDPs to provide the specific characterizations for their tilt-stable solutions, and their relations with the SSOSC and the dual constraint nondegeneray were also clarified. To the best of knowledge, this is the first work to establish point-based sufficient and/or necessary characterizations for true non-polyhedral conic programs without constraint nondegeneracy condition even without requiring the uniqueness of multipliers.

Carleman estimate with piecewise weight and applications to inverse problems for first-order transport equations


Abstract
We consider a first-order transport equation ∂ t u ⁡ ( x , t ) + ( H ⁡ ( x ) ⋅ ∇ u ​ ( x , t ) ) + p ⁡ ( x ) ​ u ​ ( x , t ) = F ⁡ ( x , t ) \partial_{t}u(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla u(x,t))+p(x)u(x,t)=F(x,t) for x ∈ Ω ⊂ ℝ d x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , where Ω \Omega is a bounded domain and 0 < t < T 0<t<T . We prove a Carleman estimate for more generous condition on the principal coefficients H ⁡ ( x ) H(x) than in the existing works. The key is the construction of a piecewise smooth weight function in x x according to a suitable decomposition of Ω \Omega . Our assumptions on H H generalize the conditions in the existing articles, and require that a directed graph created by the corresponding stream field has no closed loops. Then, we apply our Carleman estimate to two inverse problems of determinination of an initial value and one of a spatial factor of a source term, so that we establish Lipschitz stability estimates for the inverse problems.

Introduction and motivation

We consider a first-order transport equation:

\partial_{t}u(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla u(x,t))+p(x)u(x,t)=F(x,t),\quad 0<t<T,\,x\in\Omega,

[formula]

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary

\partial\Omega
.
Throughout this article, we assume

H=(h_{1},...,h_{d})\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}),\quad|H(x)|\geq\delta_{0}>0,\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},

[formula]

where
\delta_{0}>0
is a constant, and let

p\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Here and henceforth,
let
x:=(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\partial_{k}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}
for

k=1,...,d
and
\nabla:=(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{d})^{T}
. Moreover,

(v\cdot w)
is the scalar product of
v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
T
denotes the
transpose of vectors and matrices under consideration.

By
\nu=\nu(x)
we denote the outward unit normal vector
to
\Omega
at
x
, and set

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&Q:=\Omega\times(0,T),\quad S^{d-1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\,|x|=1\},\cr\\
&\partial\Omega_{+}:=\{x\in\partial\Omega;\,(H(x)\cdot\nu(x))\geq 0\},\cr\\
&\partial\Omega_{-}:=\{x\in\partial\Omega;\,(H(x)\cdot\nu(x))<0\}.\end{array}\right.

[formula]

For (1.1), we consider

Observability:

Let
F=0
in
Q
. Determine
u(\cdot,0)
in
\Omega
by

u|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}
.

Inverse source problem:

Let
F(x,t)=R(x,t)f(x)
in (1.1) with a suitably given function
R(x,t)
for

(x,t)\in Q
. Then determine
f(x)
,
x\in\Omega
by data

(u|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)},\,\,u(\cdot,0)|_{\Omega})
.

The inverse source problem with
x
-dependent
R(x,t)
is essential
for inverse coefficient problems such as the determination of the coefficient

p(x)
in
\partial_{t}u(p)+(H\cdot\nabla u(p))+p(x)u=0
. Indeed,
subtracting the equations for

and

and setting

, we can
reduce the inverse coefficient problem to an inverse source problem
(e.g.,
[
17
]
).

Equations of type (1.1) appear in mathematical physics, for example:

\mbox{conservation law:}\quad\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\mbox{div}\,(V(x)u(x,t))=0\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

[formula]

where
V:=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})^{T}
.

In governing equations for a linearized compressible non-viscous fluid
(called the Euler equations):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}V(x,t)+(H(x,t)\cdot\nabla)V(x,t)+\nabla p(x,t)=0,\\
&\mbox{div}\,V(x,t)=0\quad\mbox{in $Q$},\end{array}\right.

[formula]

where
V=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})^{T}
is the velocity field and

p
is the pressure, and
we set
(H\cdot\nabla)V:=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{3}h_{k}\partial_{k}V_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq 3}
.

For discussing inverse problems, our first key is a weighted estimate
called a Carleman estimate, and the second key is
the methodology based on Carleman estimates by Bukhgeim and Klibanov

[
2
]

[

In those works, some conditions are assumed on
H(x)
in order to prove

Condition (A)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\mbox{there exist a constant vector $v\in S^{d-1}$ and
a constant $\delta_{1}>0$}\\
&(H(x)\cdot v)\geq\delta_{1}\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\overline{\Omega}$.}\end{array}\right.

[formula]

This condition means that the range of the solid angles of
H(x)
over

x\in\overline{\Omega}
is included in the half
\frac{|S^{d-1}|}{2}

of the unit sphere. Here, recalling that
|H(x)|>0
for

\frac{H(x)}{|H(x)|}

Under Condition (A), we can prove a Carleman estimate and directly
derive an observabilty inequality.

Proposition 1.1 (Carleman estimate under Condition (A))

We choose

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\varphi(x,t):=|x+rv|^{2}-\beta t,\quad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T,\\
&r>\frac{1}{2\delta_{1}}(2(\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}|x|)\|H\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}+\beta).\end{array}\right.

[formula]

Then, there exist constants
s_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that

s\int_{\Omega}|u(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx+s^{2}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,t)}dxdt+s\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,t)}dSdt

\leq C\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}u+(H\cdot\nabla u)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,t)}dxdt+Cs\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,t)}dSdt

+Cs\int_{\Omega}|u(x,T)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,T)}dx

[formula]

for all
s\geq s_{0}
and
u\in H^{1}(Q)
.

For completeness, we provide the proof of
Proposition 1.1 in Section 6.

Proposition 1.2 (Observability inequality under Condition (A))

We assume that

Then, we can find a constant

In Section 4.1, we describe the argument how to

See Komornik

2
Main Carleman estimate

In order to derive a Carleman estimate, Condtion (A) is sufficient
but not a necessary condition. However, if Condition (A) does not hold,
then we may not be able to prove a Carleman estimate which is applicable to
inverse problems or some uniqueness results related to a solution
u(x,t)
to
(1.1), as the following example shows.

Example 2.1.

We consider the two-dimensional case:
d=2
.
Let
\Omega:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\,1<|x|<2\}
and

H(x_{1},x_{2}):=(-x_{2},\,x_{1})^{T}
. We consider

u_{0}(x_{1},x_{2},t)=(|x|^{2}-1)(|x|^{2}-4),\quad(x_{1},x_{2},t)\in Q:=\Omega\times(0,T).

Then,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}u_{0}(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla u_{0}(x,t))=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega\times(0,\infty)$},\\
&u_{0}=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)$},\end{array}\right.

which means that the conclusion of Proposition 1.2 does
not hold. Indeed,

\left\{\frac{H(x)}{|H(x)|};\,x\in\overline{\Omega}\right\}=\left\{\frac{(-x_{2},x_{1})}{|x|};\,1<|x|<2\right\}=S^{1},

that is, (1.6) is not satisfied for any
v\in S^{1}
.
If we have a relevant Carleman estimate, then we can apply such an
estimate to conclude that
u_{0}=0
in
Q
similarly to
Proposition 1.2. Therefore, by such
u_{0}
, we see that we cannot
have any Carleman estimates in this case.

We emphasize that condition (1.6) is not a necessary condition in order to
establish an applicable Carleman estimate to inverse problems.
Indeed, we consider a case where
H(x)
is given by a scalar potential
function:

\Omega:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\,1<|x|<2\},\quad H(x):=(2x_{1},2x_{2})=\nabla(|x|^{2}).

Clearly
2(x_{1},x_{2})
does not satisfy (1.6) in
\Omega
. However we can
choose a weight function to establish a Carleman estimate.

We can state this case as

Proposition 2.1 (Carleman estimate without Condition (A))

We assume

H(x)=\nabla\rho(x),\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},

[formula]

with some
\rho\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying

|\nabla\rho|>\beta>0
on
\overline{\Omega}
.
We set
\varphi(x,t):=\rho(x)-\beta t
for
x\in\Omega
and

0<t<T
. Then Carleman estimate (1.8) holds.

Condition (2.1) means that we consider a potential flow and
can validate a Carleman estimate although (1.6) is not satisfied.
In this article, not assuming such special forms of
H
such as (2.1),
we generalize (1.6) to establish a Carleman estimate.

For the statement of our main result, we introduce notations and definitions.
The main idea for relaxing conditions on
H
,
is to use a partition of
\Omega
such that, in each subdomain, condition (1.6) is satisfied. The proof of Carleman
estimate over
\Omega
can be reduced to estimations in each
subdomain, but for synthesizing all the integrals in the subdomains, it is
necessary to absorb all the integral terms on the interfaces
created by the partition. Thus we need to assume some conditions,
which can be described as a directed graph.

We introduce a partition of
\Omega
:

\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\overline{\Omega_{i}},

where
\Omega_{i}\subset\Omega
,
1\leq i\leq N
,
are non-empty disjoint subdomains.

By
|\gamma_{ij}|
, we denote
the measure of the surface
\gamma_{ij}
.
We assume

\mbox{$\gamma_{ij}:=\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega_{j}$ with
$i\neq j$ are of piecewise $C^{2}$-class if $|\gamma_{ij}|>0$}.

[formula]

By
\nu_{i\to j}
we denote the unit normal vector to

\gamma_{ij}
directed from

\Omega_{i}
towards
\Omega_{j}
. Note that
\nu_{i\to j}=-\nu_{j\to i}
.

We formulate

Condition (B).

The partition
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
satisfies:

(i) On each
\gamma_{ij}
, one of the following three cases hold:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&(H\cdot\nu_{i\to j})>0\quad\mbox{almost everywhere
on $\gamma_{ij}$},\\
\mbox{or}&(H\cdot\nu_{i\to j})<0\quad\mbox{almost everywhere
on $\gamma_{ij}$},\\
\mbox{or}&(H\cdot\nu_{i\to j})=0\quad\mbox{almost everywhere
on $\gamma_{ij}$}.\end{array}\right.

(ii) For each
i=1,...,N
, there exist a constant
\delta_{1}>0
and
a constant vector
v_{i}\in S^{d-1}
such that

\min_{1\leq i\leq d}(H(x)\cdot v_{i})>\delta_{1}\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\overline{\Omega_{i}}$}.

Remark.
We do not consider the case where

(H\cdot\nu_{i\to j})
changes signs infinitely many times, which
makes point (i) of Condition (B) impossible.

Henceforth, to simplify the description, we neglect
\gamma_{ij}
if

|\gamma_{ij}|=0
.

Next we correspond a directed graph
\Lambda
to the partition

\{\Omega_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
as follows.

(I) We correspond a node point
O_{i}
to
\Omega_{i}
for

1\leq i\leq N
.

(II) By a segment
\Gamma_{ij}
, we connect two distinct nodes
O_{i}
and

O_{j}
with
i\neq j
if and only if
|\gamma_{ij}|=|\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega_{j}|>0
and

(H\cdot\nu_{i\to j})\neq 0
on
\gamma_{ij}
. Moreover the segment

\Gamma_{ij}
is directed from
O_{i}
to
O_{j}
if

(H\cdot\nu_{i\to j})>0
almost everywhere on
\gamma_{ij}
,
and from
O_{j}
to
O_{i}
if
(H\cdot\nu_{i\to j})<0

almost everywhere on
\gamma_{ij}
.

We note that if
|\gamma_{ij}|=0
or

(H\cdot\nu_{i\to j})=0
on
\gamma_{ij}
, then

O_{i}
and
O_{j}
are not connected by a directed segment.

With the nodes
\{O_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
and the
directed segments
\{\Gamma_{ij}\}
, we compose a
directed graph
\Lambda
.
Since
H(x)
describes the stream field, we can interpret

\Lambda
as a chart of the stream field of (1.1).

Definition 2.1.

We call that a directed graph
\Lambda
has a closed loop if we can
choose a subset
\{\Gamma_{i_{1}j_{1}}\}
of all the
directed segments
\{\Gamma_{ij}\}
such that

\bigcup_{i_{1},j_{1}}\Gamma_{i_{1}j_{1}}
is a oriented closed Jordan curve.

Then, we can formulate

Condition (C).

The directed graph
\Lambda
associated with the partition
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
has no closed loops.

We set

Q_{i}:=\Omega_{i}\times(0,T),\quad 1\leq i\leq N.

Now we are ready to state our main result.

Theorem 2.1 (Carleman estimate)

We assume Conditions (B) and (C). Then we can find constants

r_{i}>0
with
1\leq i\leq N
and
\beta>0
, and set

\varphi_{i}(x,t)=|x+r_{i}v_{i}|^{2}-\beta t\quad\mbox{in $Q_{i}$},

[formula]

such that

s\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}|u(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi_{i}(x,0)}dx+s^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q_{i}}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi_{i}}dxdt+s\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

[formula]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q_{i}}|\partial_{t}u+(H\cdot\nabla u)+pu|^{2}e^{2s\varphi_{i}}dxdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle Cs\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dSdt+Cs\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}|u(x,T)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi_{i}(x,T)}dx

for all
s>s_{0}
and
u\in H^{1}(Q)
.

Related to the existence of closed loops in directed graphs, we discuss
the following

Example 2.2.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\Omega:=\{x;|x|<1\}
.
We use the two-dimensional polar coordinates:

x_{1}=r\cos\theta,\quad x_{2}=r\sin\theta

with
r\geq 0
and
0\leq\theta<2\pi
.
We consider a function
p(\theta)
such that

p\in C^{1}[0,2\pi],\quad p(0)=0,\quad p(2\pi)=2\pi m,\quad p^{\prime}(2\pi)=p^{\prime}(0)

[formula]

with
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
and

H(x_{1},x_{2})=(-\sin p(\theta),\,\cos p(\theta)),\quad 0\leq\theta<2\pi.

Then we see that
H\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. If the range
\{p(\theta);\,0\leq\theta<2\pi\}
is wider than
(0,\pi)
, then (1.6) fails.
However, we may be able to establish a Carleman estimate in many cases.
We consider special partitions:

\Omega_{i}:=\{\theta_{i-1}<\theta<\theta_{i}\},\quad 1\leq i\leq N

where
0=:\theta_{0}<\theta_{1}<\cdots<\theta_{N-1}<\theta_{N}:=2\pi

and
\Omega_{N+1}:=\Omega_{1}
.
Then we can correspond the nodes as
O_{i}:=(\cos\theta_{i},\,\sin\theta_{i})

for
0\leq i\leq N
. Then,

\gamma_{i,i+1}:=\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega_{i+1}=\{\theta=\theta_{i}\},\quad 1\leq i\leq N.

Henceforth we set

q(\theta):=p(\theta)-\theta,\quad 0\leq\theta<2\pi.

Then,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\nu_{i\to i+1}=(-\sin\theta_{i},\,\cos\theta_{i})^{T},\\
&(H\cdot\,\nu_{i\to i+1})=\sin p(\theta_{i})\sin\theta_{i}+\cos p(\theta_{i})\cos\theta_{i}=\cos q(\theta_{i})\quad\mbox{for $1\leq i\leq N$}.\end{array}\right.

[formula]

Assigning a node
O_{i}
to
\Omega_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq N
and setting

O_{N+1}=O_{1}
, we connect
O_{i}
to
O_{i+1}
with the direction

O_{i}\rightarrow O_{i+1}
if

\cos q(\theta_{i})>0
, and
O_{i+1}\rightarrow O_{i}
if

\cos q(\theta_{i})<0
, and do not connect such nodes
if
\cos q(\theta_{i})=0
. Hence,
we construct a directed graph
\Lambda
with nodes
O_{1},...,O_{N}
and

N
-directed segments
\Gamma_{12},\Gamma_{23},....,\Gamma_{N-1,N},\Gamma_{N,N+1}
.

Then, we can prove

Lemma 2.1.

(i) Case
m=0
or
m\geq 2
in (2.5)
.

We can find a partition
0<\theta_{1}<\cdots<\theta_{N-1}<2\pi
such that

|\{p(\theta);\,\theta_{i-1}<\theta<\theta_{i}\}|<\pi\quad\mbox{for $1\leq i\leq N$}

[formula]

and the corresponding directed graph
\Lambda
has no closed loops.

(ii) Case
m=1
in (2.5)
.

If we can find
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\mbox{$\frac{\pi}{2}+\ell\pi$ is an interior point in
$\{q(\theta);\,0<\theta<2\pi\}$},

[formula]

then the conclusion of case (i) holds.

We consider
p(\theta)=m\theta
with
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
as
a function satisfying (2.5). Lemma 2.1 implies that:
If
m=0
or
m\geq 2
, then we can find a directed graph without closed
loops, and if
m=1
, then any directed graph has a closed loop.
Example 2.1 indicates a rare case where the graph has a closed loop.
Moreover, in the case
m=1
, we recall that
we cannot obtain any Carleman estimate
which is applicable to the uniqueness of the solution

by
boundary measurements.

Moreover, we note that if

rotates fast in the sense that
m\geq 2
,
then we can construct a directed graph without closed loops.
We note that, for

, condition (1.6) does not hold.

(B) and (C) which are weaker than Condition (A).

Proof of Lemma 2.1.

Case (ii).

By (2.8), we can choose
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\theta_{*},\theta^{*}\in(0,2\pi)
and sufficiently small

, by (2.9) we have

(H\cdot\nu_{n_{0}\to n_{0}+1})
and

Moreover, we choose
\theta_{i}

implies

In other words,

Case (i).

The intermediate value theorem implies

\{q(\theta);\,0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\}\supset[q(0),\,q(2\pi)]\quad\mbox{if $q(2\pi)\geq 0$}

&\frac{\pi}{2}\quad\mbox{if $m\geq 2$}.\end{array}\right.

Since (2.8) is satisfied, similarly to Case (ii), we can verify the

Proof of Theorem 2.1

Henceforth, for brevity, we set

Q_{i}:=\Omega_{i}\times(0,T)
, and, for given
\varphi_{i}\in C^{1}(\overline{Q_{i}})

with

, we define a piecewise
C^{1}
-function on
\overline{Q}=\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]

Therefore, we can immediatetly see that there exist
r_{1},r_{2}>0

satisfying (3.10) and (3.11). Thus Lemma 3.1 is proved for
N=2
.

We assume that Lemma 3.1 is proved for arbitrary directed graphs
\Lambda

with an
N
-number of nodes.
We will prove Lemma 3.1 for arbitrary directed graphs
\widetilde{\Lambda}
with

(N+1)
-number nodes which have no closed loops.
By Lemma 3.2 (ii), it follows that
\widetilde{\Lambda}
has a terminus node, which
is denoted by
O_{N+1}
. Then we can find a non-empty set

J_{-}(O_{N+1})\subset\{1,...,N\}
such that
each directed segment incoming to
O_{N+1}
is labelled by

\Gamma_{N+1,j}
with
j\in J_{-}(O_{N+1})
and

(H\cdot\nu_{N+1\to j})<0
on
\gamma_{N+1,j}
for
j\in J_{-}(O_{N+1})
.
We construct another directed graph
\widehat{\Lambda}
by deleting
O_{N+1}

and
\Gamma_{N+1,j}
for all
j\in J_{-}(O_{N+1})
.
Then,
\widehat{\Lambda}
has
N
-nodes and by Lemma 3.1 (i), we see that

\widehat{\Lambda}
has no closed loops.
Since the conclusion holds for
\widehat{\Lambda}
with
N
-nodes
by the assumption of the induction,
Lemma 3.1 is satisfied
for
\widehat{\Lambda}
: there exist constants
r_{1},...,r_{N}>0
such that
(3.10) and (3.11) hold. Since
r_{1},...,r_{N}
are already determined, it
suffices to choose
r_{N+1}>0
such that

r_{N+1}^{2}>\max_{1\leq j\leq N}(4r_{j}^{2}+6R^{2})

and

r_{N+1}>\max\left\{\frac{R\|H\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}{\delta}+1,\,\,R\right\}.

Then, we can readily verify that (3.10) and (3.11) are satisfied for

\Lambda
with
(N+1)
-nodes. Therefore, we proved that Lemma 3.1 holds
true also for arbitrary
(N+1)
-node directed graphs without closed
loops. Thus the induction finishes the proor of Lemma 3.1, which
completes the proof of Theorem 2.1.

\blacksquare

4
Applications to inverse problems

4.1. Observability inequality.

In this section, we apply Theorem 2.1 to two kinds of inverse problems
for first-order transport equations.
Throughout this section, we assume Conditions (B) and (C) stated in
Section 2.

We consider

\partial_{t}u(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla u(x,t))+p(x)u=0\quad\mbox{in $Q$}.

[formula]

Then,

Theorem 4.1 (observability inequality).

We assume Conditions (B) and (C) stated in Section 2.
There exists a constant
T_{0}>0
depending on
H,\Omega
, such that
for all
T>T_{0}
, we can find a constant
C>0
such that

\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}

[formula]

for all
u\in H^{1}(Q)
satisfying (4.1).
Here the constant
C>0
depends on
\Omega,T,\varphi
.

By Theorem 2.1, we can prove other types of stability estimates of solution

u
to for (4.1) such as an estimate of Hölder type
(e.g., Chapter 3 in Yamamoto
[
17
]
), but we here
omit further details.
The proof is derived from a relevant Carleman estimate (Theorem 2.1), and
here we provide the proof in a more general setting.
Assume that we know a Carleman
estimate: there exist a constant
\beta>0
and a function
d(x)

which is of piecewise
C^{1}
on
\overline{\Omega}
, such that

s\int_{\Omega}|u(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx+s^{2}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt

\leq C\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}u+(H\cdot\nabla u)+pu|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+C\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

+Cs\int_{\Omega}|u(x,T)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,T)}dx\quad\mbox{for all large $s>0$}.

[formula]

Here we set
\varphi(x,t):=d(x)-\beta t
for
(x,t)\in Q:=\Omega\times(0,T)
and recall that

\partial\Omega_{+}:=\{x\in\partial\Omega;\,(H(x)\cdot\nu(x))\geq 0\}
.

For the proof of Theorem 4.1, it suffices to prove

Proposition 4.1.

We assume Carleman estimate (4.3). Then the conclusion of Theorem 4.1
holds true.

Proof of Proposition 4.1.

First Step.

We can prove an energy estimate.

Lemma 4.1.

Let
u\in H^{1}(Q)
satisfy

\partial_{t}u(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla u(x,t))+p(x)u(x,t)=F(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q

[formula]

with
\|p\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq M
.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
which is dependent only on

M
,
\Omega
and
T
,
H
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}dx+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,t))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|F\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T))}+\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

Proof of Lemma 4.1.

We recall that
H=(h_{1},...,h_{d})
.
Multiplying (4.4) with
2u(x,t)
and integrating over
\Omega
, we have

\int_{\Omega}2u(x,t)\partial_{t}u(x,t)dx+\int_{\Omega}2u(x,t)\sum^{d}_{j=1}h_{j}\partial_{j}udx+\int_{\Omega}2p|u(x,t)|^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}2F(x,t)u(x,t)dx,

that is,

\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}(H\cdot\nabla(u^{2}))dx+\int_{\Omega}2pu^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}2Fu\,dx

in
(0,T)
.
Setting
E(t):=\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}dx
and integrating by
parts, by
\|p\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq M
we obtain

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(t)-\int_{\Omega}(\mbox{div}\thinspace H)|u(x,t)|^{2}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}}(H\cdot\nu)u^{2}dS+\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}}(H\cdot\nu)u^{2}dS

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}2Fu\,dx-2\int_{\Omega}pu^{2}dx\leq CME(t)+\int_{\Omega}(|F|^{2}+u^{2})dx.

Therefore, using
H\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
rewriting in
\eta
, we have

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(\eta)+\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}}|u(x,\eta)|^{2}dS

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CE(\eta)+C\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}}|u(x,\eta)|^{2}dS+C\int_{\Omega}|F(x,\eta)|^{2}dx,\quad 0\leq\eta\leq T.

Hence, integrating in
\eta\in(0,t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(t)+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}}|u(x,\eta)|^{2}dS\right)d\eta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(0)+C\int^{t}_{0}E(\eta)d\eta+C(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T))}+\|F\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}),\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

The Gronwall inequality completes the proof of Lemma 4.1.

\blacksquare

Second Step.

We choose
T_{0}>0
such that

T_{0}>\frac{1}{\beta}(\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}d(x)-\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}d(x))

[formula]

and we assume that
T>T_{0}
.
We set

\mu:=\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}d(x)+\beta T-\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}d(x)>0

[formula]

for
T>T_{0}
.
Applying (4.3) to (4.1), we have

\int_{\Omega}|u(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T)}|u|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dSdt+C\int_{\Omega}|u(x,T)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,T)}dx

for all large
s>0
.

Therefore, applying Lemma 4.1, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{2s\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,0)}\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{Cs}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T))}+Ce^{2s\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,T)}\|u(\cdot,T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{Cs}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T))}+Ce^{2s\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,T)}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T))}+Ce^{2s\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,T)}\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for all large
s>0
. Dividing by

e^{2s\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,0)}
we reach

\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq Ce^{Cs}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}+Ce^{-2s\mu}\|u(\cdot,0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for all large
s>0
.
Choosing
s>0
sufficiently large, we can absorb the second term
on the right-hand side into the left-hand side thanks to (4.6).
Thus, the proof of Proposition 4.1, and the one of Theorem 4.1 are complete.

\blacksquare

4.2. Inverse source problem

We consider

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}u(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla u(x,t))+p(x)u(x,t)=R(x,t)f(x),\\
&u(x,0)=0,\qquad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T.\end{array}\right.

[formula]

Let
p,R
be given and
\|p\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq M
, where

M>0
is an arbitrarily fixed constant.

We discuss

Inverse source problem.

Determine
f(x)
for
x\in\Omega
by data
u
on

\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
.

By Theorem 2.1, we will prove

Theorem 4.2.

We assume Conditions (B) and (C) stated in Section 2, and

R,\,\partial_{t}R\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega)),\quad|R(x,0)|>0,\quad x\in\overline{\Omega}

[formula]

and
u,\partial_{t}u\in H^{1}(Q)
. Then, there exists
T_{0}>0
depending on

H,\Omega
such that for each
T>T_{0}
we can find a constant
C>0

such that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}.

[formula]

Here, the constant
C>0
depends on
\Omega,T,H,M,\varphi,R
.

Similarly to Theorem 4.1, we can prove other types of stability estimates
by Theorem 2.1, but we concentrate on the global Lipschitz stability
(4.9).

For the proof of Theorem 4.2, it suffices to prove

Proposition 4.2.

We assume Conditions (B) and (C) stated in Section 2.
Let Carleman estimate (4.3) hold.
Then, the conclusion of Theorem 4.2 holds
true.

Proof of Proposition 4.2.

By (4.8), we see that
R\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))\subset C([0,T];L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.

We take the time-derivative of
u
:
y:=\partial_{t}u
. Then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}y(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla y(x,t))+p(x)y(x,t)=(\partial_{t}R)(x,t)f(x),\\
&y(x,0)=R(x,0)f(x),\qquad x\in\Omega,\,0<t<T.\end{array}\right.

[formula]

In view of (4.8), the application of Lemma 4.1 to (4.10) yields

\|y(\cdot,T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|(\partial_{t}R)f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|y\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T))}+\|R(\cdot,0)f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\leq C(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T))}).

[formula]

Next we apply (4.3) to (4.10):

s\int_{\Omega}|y(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx

[formula]

\leq C\int_{Q}|(\partial_{t}R)f|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+Cs\int_{\Omega}|y(x,T)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,T)}dx+Cs\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T)}y^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

for all large
s>0
.

Since
\varphi(x,t)\leq\varphi(x,0)
for
(x,t)\in\overline{Q}
, by (4.8)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|\partial_{t}R(x,t)|^{2}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,t)}dxdt\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\partial_{t}R(x,t)|^{2}dt\right)|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}\left(\int^{T}_{0}|\partial_{t}R(x,t)|^{2}dt\right)\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}R\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx.

Consequently, (4.12) yields

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}|y(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx+Cs\int_{\Omega}|y(x,T)|^{2}dxe^{2s\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,T)}

+Cse^{Cs}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T))}

[formula]

for all large
s>0
.
We have

\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|y(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx

by the last condition in (4.8), and the substitution of this
inequality into (4.13) leads to

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx+Cs\left(\int_{\Omega}|y(x,T)|^{2}dx\right)e^{2s\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,T)}+Cse^{Cs}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T))}

for all large
s>0
.
Choosing
s>0
large, we can absorb the first term on the right-hand side into
the left-hand side, and we obtain

\displaystyle s\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cs\int_{\Omega}|y(x,T)|^{2}dxe^{2s\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,T)}+Cse^{Cs}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T))}

for all large
s>0
.
Dividing by
se^{2s\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}\varphi(x,0)}
and noting
(4.6), we reach

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ce^{Cs}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T))}+C\int_{\Omega}|y(x,T)|^{2}dxe^{-2s\mu}

[formula]

for all large
s>0
.

Applying (4.11) to the second term on the right-hand side of (4.14), we have

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ce^{Cs}\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T))}\\
+C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}e^{-2s\mu}+C\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T))}e^{-2s\mu}

for all large
s>0
. Since
\mu>0
, we choose
s>0
sufficiently large,
so that we absorb the second term on the right-hand side into the left-hand
side. Therefore, the proof of Proposition 4.2 is complete.
Thus the proof of Theorem 4.2 is complete.

\blacksquare

5
Concluding remarks

5.1.

We have considered a first-order transport equation

\partial_{t}u(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla u(x,t))+p(x)u(x,t)=F(x,t)\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

[formula]

and established a Carleman estimate under a more general condition
on
H
than the one in the existing works Cannarsa, Floridia, Gölgeleyen and
Yamamoto
[
3
]
, Gaitan and Ouzzane
[
8
]
, Gölgeleyen and
Yamamoto
[
9
]
, Chapter 3 in Yamamoto
[
17
]
.
The key for such an estimate (Theorem 2.1) is the weight
function which is piecewise
C^{1}
in
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, and the
condition of the weight requires that the associated directed graph
by the stream field
H(x)
has no closed loops.

Our Carleman inequality produces stability estimates for inverse problems,
the derivation of which follows standard arguments, once the Carleman
estimate (Theorem 2.1) is established.

5.2.

In this article, we assume that
H
in (5.1) does not depend on
t
,
but we can similarly discuss the case where
H(x,t)
depends also on

t
. In a future work, we will study the details.

Appendix: Proofs of Propositions 1.1 and 2.1

We will prove a slightly more general Carleman estimate
(Proposition 6.1) than Proposition 1.1.
Choosing
d\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and a constant
\beta>0
, we define

\varphi(x,t):=d(x)-\beta t,\quad B(x,t):=(\nabla d(x)\cdot H(x))-\beta

for
(x,t)\in Q
.
We consider

\partial_{t}u(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla u(x,t))+p(x)u(x,t)=F(x,t)\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

[formula]

where
H\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
,
|H(x)|>0
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,
p\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
F\in L^{2}(Q)
.
Then,

Proposition 6.1.

We assume

\min_{(x,t)\in\overline{Q}}B(x,t)=:\delta_{3}>0.

[formula]

Then, there exist constants
C>0
and
s_{0}>0
, which are dependent on

\varphi,M,H,\Omega,T
but independent of
s
, such that

\displaystyle s^{2}\int_{Q}u^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+s\int_{\Omega}B(x,0)|u(x,0)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,0)}dx+s\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T)}|(H\cdot\nu)|Bu^{2}dSdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}|F|^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dxdt+Cs\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T)}|(H\cdot\nu)|Bu^{2}e^{2s\varphi}dSdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle Cs\int_{\Omega}B(x,T)|u(x,T)|^{2}e^{2s\varphi(x,T)}dx

for all
s\geq s_{0}
and all

satisfying (6.1).

Proof of Proposition 6.1.

The proof is similar to Proposition 2.1 of Chapter 3 in
[
17
]
.
We provide it for completeness.
As discussed at the beginning of the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
Section 3, it is sufficient to prove the proposition with

p\equiv 0
. Similarly to the beginning of the proof of Theorem 2.1,
setting
w:=e^{s\varphi}
, we have that

\displaystyle Pw(x,t):=e^{s\varphi(x,t)}(\partial_{t}+(H\cdot\nabla))e^{-s\varphi(x,t)}w(x,t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w(x,t)+(H(x)\cdot\nabla w(x,t))-sB(x,t)w(x,t).

Therefore, by the same way as (3.2), we obtain

\int_{Q}|Pw|^{2}dxdt\geq s^{2}\|Bw\|^{2}-2s\int_{Q}(\partial_{t}w+(H\cdot\nabla w))Bwdxdt.

[formula]

Now, integration by parts yields

\displaystyle-2s\int_{Q}(\partial_{t}w+(H\cdot\nabla w))Bwdxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2s\int_{Q}(\partial_{t}w)Bwdxdt-2s\int_{Q}(H\cdot\nabla w)Bwdxdt=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Then

I_{1}=-s\int_{Q}B\partial_{t}(w^{2})dxdt=-s\int_{\Omega}\left[B(x,t)|w(x,t)|^{2}\right]^{T}_{0}dx+s\int_{Q}(\partial_{t}B)w^{2}dxdt

and

\displaystyle I_{2}=-2s\int_{Q}\sum^{d}_{j=1}h_{j}(\partial_{j}w)Bwdxdt=-s\int_{Q}\sum^{d}_{j=1}h_{j}B\partial_{j}(w^{2})dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-s\int_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}\sum^{d}_{j=1}h_{j}B\nu_{j}w^{2}dSdt+s\int_{Q}\sum^{d}_{j=1}\partial_{j}(h_{j}B)w^{2}dxdt.

Since
H\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
B\in C^{1}(\overline{Q})
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}\geq-Cs\int_{Q}w^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle s\left(\int_{\Omega}\left[Bw^{2}\right]^{T}_{0}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}(H\cdot\nu)Bw^{2}dSdt\right).

Hence, (6.3) implies

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|Pw|^{2}dxdt\geq\left(s^{2}\min_{(x,t)\in\overline{Q}}B(x,t)^{2}-Cs\right)\int_{Q}w^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle s\left(\int_{\Omega}\left[Bw^{2}\right]^{T}_{0}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}(H\cdot\nu)Bw^{2}dSdt\right).

Then, by choosing
s>0
sufficiently large, we obtain

\displaystyle s^{2}\int_{Q}w^{2}dxdt+s\int_{\Omega}B(x,0)w(x,0)^{2}dx-s\int_{\Omega}B(x,T)w(x,T)^{2}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle s\int_{\partial\Omega_{-}\times(0,T)}|(H\cdot\nu)|Bw^{2}dSdt-s\int_{\partial\Omega_{+}\times(0,T)}|(H\cdot\nu)|Bw^{2}dSdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}|Pw|^{2}dxdt

for all
s>s_{0}
. Substituting
w=e^{s\varphi}u
, we complete the proof of
Proposition 6.1.

\blacksquare

Proof of Proposition 1.1.

d(x):=|x+rv|^{2}

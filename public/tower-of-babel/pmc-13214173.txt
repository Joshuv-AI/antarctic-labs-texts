Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgoundPulmonary hypertension (PH) is characterized by pulmonary vascular remodeling, which ultimately leads to right heart failure. Previous studies have confirmed that DNA variations contribute to the development and progression of PH. This study aims to integrate methylation and transcriptome data to uncover key molecular networks and potential therapeutic targets for PH.MethodsGene expression (GSE117261) and methylation data (GSE84395) from PH patients were retrieved from the GEO database. Differential genes and methylation sites were identified using the limma and ChAMP packages. A co-expression network was constructed using WGCNA, and the functions of key genes were explored through immune infiltration analysis, GSEA/GSVA pathway enrichment, transcriptional regulatory network prediction, and experimental validatio.ResultsSeven key genes (S100A9, IL18RAP, CXCR2, LCN2, INHBA, CSF3R, LTBP1) were identified. Among these, CXCR2 was significantly upregulated in both PH patients and animal models. Bioinformatics analysis revealed that CXCR2 drives pulmonary vascular remodeling via multiple pathways, including the IL-17 signaling pathway (inflammatory reaction), ROS pathway (oxidative stress), PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway (cell proliferation), and metabolic pathways. Experimental validation confirmed high expression of CXCR2 in the smooth muscle layer of pulmonary arteries and its strong association with immune cell infiltration (neutrophils, monocytes).ConclusionThrough multi-omics integration analysis, this study elucidates the key molecular mechanisms underlying PH and identifies potential therapeutic targets. In the pathogenesis of PH, dysfunction of inflammatory and immune responses plays a critical role. Experimental validation demonstrates that CXCR2 may serve as a novel biomarker and therapeutic target for PH, with its multi-pathway regulatory mechanism providing a theoretical foundation for precision medicine in PH treatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13148-026-02075-3.

Introduction

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a critical illness characterized by persistently elevated pulmonary circulation pressure, progressive deterioration of right ventricular function, and poor prognosis [1]. Its pathological process involves the interplay of multiple factors, including vascular endothelial dysfunction, abnormal smooth muscle cell proliferation, immune microenvironment imbalance, and metabolic reprogramming [2–4]. Although targeted therapies (e.g., endothelin receptor antagonists, prostacyclin analogues) significantly delay disease progression [5], the core molecular mechanisms of PH remain incompletely understood, and current treatments struggle to reverse pulmonary vascular structural remodeling [2]. Crucially, the role of dysregulated immune modulation in PH pathogenesis is increasingly recognized, yet significant knowledge gaps persist regarding specific molecular networks—such as the dynamic interplay between epigenetic regulation and immune cell infiltration.

Recent studies indicate that epigenetic modifications (such as DNA methylation [6] and RNA methylation) play crucial roles in PH [7, 8]. For instance, DNA methylation regulates smooth muscle cell proliferation, differentiation, and inflammatory responses [9], while m6A modifications promote endothelial cell apoptosis by affecting the stability of inflammatory factor mRNAs [10, 11]. However, the synergistic regulatory networks between methylation and the transcriptome, particularly their specific roles within the immune microenvironment (e.g., neutrophil and monocyte infiltration), remain poorly understood. This limitation hinders a comprehensive understanding of the multi-pathway mechanisms underlying PH.

Recent studies have revealed that immune dysregulation is the core driver of vascular remodeling in pulmonary hypertension. Inflammatory cells such as neutrophils and monocytes abnormally infiltrate pulmonary vascular walls, activating pro-fibrotic and proliferative signaling networks by secreting factors like IL-1β and TNF-α [12, 13]. CXCR2 (C-X-C chemokine receptor 2), as a key receptor mediating the migration of immune cells including neutrophils and monocytes, plays a central role in the immune-inflammatory cascade of pulmonary hypertension (PH). Studies indicate that CXCR2 is significantly overexpressed in pulmonary vascular endothelial cells and infiltrating immune cells of PH patients [14, 15]. It activates multiple signaling pathways, including AKT/mTOR, JAK2/STAT3, PLC/PKC, and MAPK/p38, synergistically driving abnormal vascular smooth muscle proliferation, endothelial dysfunction, and the formation of a pro-fibrotic microenvironment [15].

This study utilized methylation (GSE84395) and transcriptomic data (GSE117261) from the GEO database to construct disease-related modules via weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA). Combined with immune cell infiltration analysis (CIBERSORT), pathway enrichment (GSEA/GSVA), and transcriptional regulation prediction (RcisTarget), we systematically screened key genes driving PH progression. Specifically, we focused on CXCR2—a significantly upregulated gene in PH—which is closely associated with immune cell infiltration and may synergistically drive pulmonary vascular remodeling through the IL-17 pathway, ROS pathway, and PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. Through experimental validation (RT-qPCR, Western blot, immunofluorescence), we aim to elucidate the core role of CXCR2 in PH, providing a new theoretical foundation for precision therapies targeting immunity and epigenetics.

Data download

Download the Series Matrix File data file of GSE117261 from the NCBI GEO public database [16]. The analysis file is GPL6244, and the expression profile data of 83 groups of samples are included, including 25 cases in the control group and 58 cases in the disease group; download the Series Matrix File A of GSE84395 [17]. The basic data file, the analysis file is GPL16304, and the expression profile data of 39 groups of samples were included, including 18 cases of the control group and 21 cases of disease group. ChAMP package was used to analyze differentially methylated sites, and the screening condition for differentially methylated sites was adj.P.Val < 0.05&|logFC|>0.1 [18] ( |logFC| > 0.1 is the standard threshold recommended by the ChAMP package, aligning with the field consensus for 450 K chip analysis. This threshold captures biologically significant methylation changes. We additionally require P < 0.05 (for gene expression) and adj.P.Val < 0.05 (for methylation), effectively controlling for multiple testing errors.). In the process of exploring the molecular mechanism related to pulmonary hypertension, the differential expression analysis used the limma package to identify differential genes between the control group and the disease group, and the screening conditions for differential genes were P-value < 0.05&|logFC|> 0.585 [19] (Gene expression | logFC | > 0.585, a commonly used moderate threshold in bioinformatics analysis, widely applied in limma differential expression analysis [20]).

Analysis of GO and KEGG functions

To identify the biological functions and signaling pathways involved in the occurrence and development of the disease, the Metascape database (www.metascape.org) was used for annotation and visualization, and the differentially expressed genes were analyzed by Gene Ontology (GO) [21] and the Kyoto Genome Encyclopedia ( KEGG) pathway analysis [22]. Min overlap ≥ 3 & p ≤ 0.01 was considered statistically significant.

WGCNA analysis

By constructing a weighted gene co-expression network, find co-expressed gene modules, and explore the relationship between gene network and phenotype, as well as the key genes in the network [23]. The WGCNA-R package was used to construct the co-expression network of the genes in the data set for further analysis, with a soft threshold of 6. The weighted adjacency matrix is converted into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) to estimate the network connectivity, and the hierarchical clustering method is used to construct the clustering tree structure of the TOM matrix. Different branches of the clustering tree represent different gene modules, and different colors represent different modules. Based on the weighted correlation coefficient of genes, genes are classified according to their expression patterns, genes with similar patterns are grouped into one module, and tens of thousands of genes are divided into multiple modules by gene expression patterns.

Analysis of immune cell infiltration

The CIBERSORT method is a widely used method for the evaluation of immune cell types in the microenvironment [24]. The method is based on the principle of support vector regression, and deconvolution analysis is performed on the expression matrix of immune cell subtypes. It contains 547 biomarkers that distinguish 22 human immune cell phenotypes, including T cells, B cells, plasma cells, and myeloid cell subsets [25]. In this study, the CIBERSORT algorithm was used to analyze the patient data to infer the relative proportion of 22 types of immune infiltrating cells, and to perform spearman correlation analysis on gene expression and immune cell content.

GSEA analysis

According to the expression of core genes in patients, the patients were divided into high and low expression groups, and the differences in signaling pathways between the two groups were further analyzed by GSEA [26]. The background gene set is the version 7.0 annotation gene set downloaded from the MsigDB database as the annotated gene set of the subtype pathway, and the differential expression analysis of the pathways between subtypes is performed, and the significantly enriched gene set is compared according to the consistency score (adjusted p value less than 0.05) for sorting. GSEA analysis is often used to explore the close combination of tumor classification and biological significance.

GSVA analysis (gene set difference analysis)

Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) is a non-parametric and unsupervised method for assessing the enrichment of transcriptome gene sets [27]. GSVA converts gene-level changes into pathway-level changes by comprehensively scoring the gene set of interest, and then judges the biological function of the sample. In this study, the gene set will be downloaded from the Molecular signatures database (version 7.0), and the GSVA algorithm will be used to score each gene set comprehensively to evaluate the potential biological function changes of different samples.

Regulatory network analysis of key genes

In this study, the R package “RcisTarget” was used to predict transcription factors. All calculations performed by RcisTarget are based on motifs. The normalized enrichment score (NES) of a motif depends on the total number of motifs in the database. In addition to motifs annotated by source data, we infer further annotation files based on motif similarity and gene sequence. The first step in estimating the overexpression of each motif across a gene set is to calculate the area under the curve (AUC) for each motif-motif-set pair. This was performed based on the recovery curve calculation of the gene set versus motif ordering. The NES of each motif is calculated from the AUC distribution of all motifs in the gene set. We use rcitarget.hg19.motifdb.cisbpont.500 bp for the Gene-motif rankings database.

Potential therapeutic drugs involved in key genes

The DGIdb database (Drug-Gene Interaction database) is a drug-gene interaction database that provides information on the association between genes and their known or potential drugs. DGIdb has a total of more than 14,000 drug-gene interactions involving 2600 genes and 6300 drugs that target these genes, as well as 6,700 other genes that are likely to be targets for future drugs.

Real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from lung homogenates of PH patients using TRIzol according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), then RNA content was determined using a Synergy H1 microplate reader (BioTek, Vermont, USA), and RNA integrity was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis stained with ethidium bromide. Primer sequences were designed and synthesized by Sangon Biotech according to the manufacturer’s instructions based on mRNA sequences obtained from the NCBI database as follows: human GAPDH forward: CATGAGAAGTATGACAACAGCCT, reverse: AGTCCTTCCACGATACCAAAGT; human S100A9 forward: GGTCATAGAACACATCATGGAGG, reverse: GGCCTGGCTTATGGTGGTG; human IL18RAP forward: CCAGGGGTGAATAATTCTGGGT, reverse: CATTTGTCTGGGGCTTAACTTCT; human CXCR2 forward: CCTGTCTTACTTTTCCGAAGGAC, reverse: TTGCTGTATTGTTGCCCATGT; human LCN2 forward: GACAACCAATTCCAGGGGAAG, reverse: GCATACATCTTTTGCGGGTCT; human human INHBA forward: CAACAGGACCAGGACCAAAGT, reverse: GAGAGCAACAGTTCACTCCTC; human CSF3R forward: CTTGTGGCCTATAACTCAGCC, reverse: CCCACTCAATCACATAGCCCT; human LTBP1 forward: GCTTCCGTCCAGATACATCAG, reverse: CTTGGTACGAGACTTGGGATTG.

Western blotting

Lung tissue was immediately placed on ice and homogenized in RIPA lysis buffer (KeyGEN BioTECH, Jiangsu, China) supplemented with 1% phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF). The homogenates were incubated for 30 min at 4 °C to ensure complete lysis. Subsequently, the lysates were then centrifuged at 12,000 rpm in a centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, and the supernatants were carefully collected. It was mixed well with 5 × Dual Color Protein Loading Buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) and heated at 95 °C for 5 min. The resulting samples were electrophoresed and transferred onto NC membranes. The NC membrane was closed with 5% skimmed milk for 1 h. The primary antibody was added and incubated in the refrigerator at 4 ℃ overnight, and then the secondary antibody was incubated for 1 h at room temperature [28]. Finally, the protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection system and imaged in a darkroom. Primary antibodies used were as follows CXCR2, 1:2000 (proteintech, USA); β-actin, 1:10000 (proteintech, USA); secondary antibodies were goat anti-mouse IgG (H + L), HRP conjugate (proteintech, USA) and goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L), HRP conjugate (proteintech, USA).

Hematoxylin-Eosin staining (H&E) and immunohistochemistry staining

Mouse lung tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and later embedded in paraffin. Subsequently, the tissues were cut into 4 μm thick sections and stained with H&E [29]. Immunohistochemistry was performed as described previously. Incubate primary antibody (CXCR2) at 4 ◦C overnight. The brown color indicates positive staining. Images were observed using a light microscope.

Immunofluorescence

Mouse lung tissue was fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and later embedded in paraffin. Subsequently, the tissues were cut into 4-µm-thick sections. Double-labeled immunofluorescence staining was performed using antibodies against aortic smooth muscle actin (α-SMA; 1:200), CXCR2 (1:200), and platelet endothelial cell adhesion molecule-1 (CD31; 1:200), and the primary antibody was incubated at 4 ◦C overnight [30]. The following day mouse lung tissue samples were washed and incubated with the secondary antibody for two hours, and after washing were incubated with DAPI for ten minutes and visualised using fluorescence imaging.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R language. All statistical tests were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Differential gene identification and methylation regulation in pulmonary hypertension

We first analyzed the GEO database to download the GSE117261 dataset related to pulmonary arterial hypertension, which included expression profiling data from 83 samples, including 25 controls and 58 disease group. Differential genes between patients and control samples were counted using the limma package, and the differential screening condition was P-value < 0.05&|logFC|> 0.585, and a total of 326 differentially expressed genes were screened, of which 185 were up-regulated genes and 141 were down-regulated genes (Fig. 2A). These 326 differentially expressed genes were then further analyzed by pathway analysis, and the results showed that the differentially expressed genes were mainly enriched in the inflammatory response, bacterial response, and extracellular matrix pathways (Fig. 2B and C).On the other hand, download the methylation data file of GSE84395 pulmonary hypertension from the GEO database, a total of 39 sets of methylation 450 K data, use the ChAMP package to analyze the differential methylation sites, and the screening condition is adj.P.Val < 0.05 & |logFC|> 0.1, a total of 1669 differentially methylated probes were screened out, including 478 down-regulated probes (annotated to 402 genes) and 1191 up-regulated probes (annotated to 875 genes) (Fig. 2D). We mapped genes exhibiting high methylation and low expression (genes with upregulated DNA methylation but downregulated transcription) and low methylation and high expression (genes with downregulated DNA methylation but upregulated transcription) using Venn diagrams. This identified seven highly methylated, low-expression genes (CSF3R, S100A9, LCN2, CXCR2, IL18RAP, CTSE, TMTC1) (Fig. 2E) and five low-methylation, high-expression genes (LTBP1, PDE7B, ANTXR1, INHBA, MACC1) (Fig. 2F).

Construction of weighted co-expression network and identification of key gene modules for pulmonary hypertension

To identify co-expression networks of genes in the pulmonary hypertension cohort, a WGCNA network was further constructed based on the expression profile data from GSE117261. When power ≥ 6, Signed R² = 1.0 (perfectly conforming to the scale-free topology and meeting strict criteria); at power = 6, the average connectivity ≈ 10 (within a reasonable range, avoiding overly dense or sparse networks). Therefore, we selected power = 6 as the final soft threshold to ensure network reliability and effective module partitioning (Fig. 3A). Then, based on the TOM matrix, gene modules were identified. A total of 20 gene modules were detected in this analysis (Fig. 3B and C), among which the midnightblue module showed the highest disease-related correlation (cor = 0.79, p = 1e − 18).We intersected the 157 module genes of the midnightblue module with 12 methylation intersection genes and obtained a total of 7 intersection genes. These 7 genes will be used as the key genes for our research, namely S100A9, IL18RAP, CXCR2, LCN2, INHBA, CSF3R and LTBP1 (Fig. 3D). To assess confounding factors, we analyzed the expression of key genes across different PH subtypes, ethnicities, and genders (Supplementary Figure S1). The results revealed no significant differences in gene expression profiles among subgroups, indicating that their abnormal expression is primarily driven by the disease state itself. This finding enhances the credibility and universality of our study’s conclusions.

Immune infiltration between control patients and patients with pulmonary hypertension

Through immune cell infiltration analysis of pulmonary hypertension (PH) patient samples, we systematically characterized the immune microenvironment in the disease state and its association with key genes. Figure 4A visually displays the overall differences and patterns in immune cell infiltration levels across patients via a heatmap. Correlation analysis (Fig. 4B) further reveals intrinsic association networks among multiple immune cell subpopulations. Compared to control samples, PH patients exhibited significantly elevated infiltration levels of plasma cells, CD8 T cells, and resting memory CD4 T cells in lung tissue (Fig. 4C). Further gene-immune cell association analysis revealed that seven key genes (S100A9, IL18RAP, CXCR2, LCN2, INHBA, CSF3R, LTBP1) were systematically associated with specific immune cell infiltration patterns (Fig. 4D–J). For example, CXCR2 expression positively correlated with neutrophil and monocyte infiltration while negatively correlating with resting mast cells. Genes such as S100A9 and LCN2 collectively drive PH immune microenvironment imbalance by regulating mast cell state (resting/activated) and macrophage polarization (Table 1). In summary, integrated graphical data confirm that the development of PH is closely associated with altered patterns of specific immune cell infiltration. The identified key genes may play crucial roles in shaping the pro-remodeling immune microenvironment by regulating the recruitment or functional states of diverse immune cells.

GSEA, GSVA analysis of key gene correlation

To investigate the potential mechanisms by which key genes influence the progression of pulmonary hypertension (PH), we performed gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and gene set variability analysis (GSVA). Results revealed that the pathways significantly enriched by seven key genes can be categorized into three core functional modules: immune inflammatory response, metabolic reprogramming, and cell proliferation and vascular remodeling (Fig. 5). Table 2 summarizes the core pathways enriched for each key gene in GSEA and GSVA analyses. Analysis revealed that CXCR2 simultaneously regulates the IL-17 inflammatory pathway (GSEA) and the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway (GSVA), indicating its role in mediating immune-metabolic crosstalk. Meanwhile, genes such as S100A9 and CSF3R amplify PH tissue damage through the oxidative stress pathway (GSVA). To evaluate and enrich the Metascape platform findings, we concurrently performed local functional enrichment analysis (Supplementary Figure S3). These independent local analyses confirmed and reinforced the enrichment results obtained from the Metascape platform, demonstrating the robustness of biological themes related to immune responses and inflammation in the PAH pathogenesis.

Integrative analysis: transcriptional regulatory network, key genes and potential therapeutic drugs prediction in pulmonary hypertension

We used these 7 key genes for the gene set of this analysis and found that they are regulated by common mechanisms such as multiple transcription factors. Therefore, the enrichment analysis of these transcription factors was carried out using the cumulative recovery curve (Fig. 6A), Motif-TF annotation, and the selection analysis of important genes showed that the Motif with the highest normalized enrichment score (NES: 7.86) was cisbp__M3035. Among them, IL18RAP, INHBA, LCN2, LTBP1, and S100A9 were enriched in this motif. We display all enriched motifs and corresponding transcription factors for key genes (Fig. 6B). We also reversely predicted 7 key genes through the microcode database, and obtained 80 miRNAs, a total of 218 mRNA-miRNA relationship pairs, and visualized them using Cytoscape (Fig. 6C). In addition, we obtained the potential therapeutic drugs involved in the key genes from the DGIdb database, and finally obtained the potential therapeutic drugs of 4 key genes, as shown in the figure (Fig. 6D). We obtained the disease genes related to pulmonary hypertension through the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). Analyzing the differences in the expression of disease genes between groups, it was found that the expressions of ACE, ACVRL1, AGTR1 and other genes were different in the two groups of patients (Fig. 6E). We analyzed the expression levels of 7 key genes and the expression levels of the top 20 genes in the Relevance score, and found that the expression levels of these 7 key genes were significantly correlated with the expression levels of multiple disease-related genes, and IL18RAP was significantly positively correlated with ACVRL1 (r = 46), LCN2 and SFTPC were significantly negatively correlated (r= − 0.59) (Fig. 6F).

Expression of key genes in pulmonary hypertension

To investigate the role of key genes in PH, we detected the expression of seven candidate genes (S100A9, IL18RAP, CXCR2, LCN2, INHBA, CSF3R, LTBP1) in PH patients and SuHx pulmonary hypertension mouse models via RT-qPCR. Results showed that at the mRNA level, all genes were significantly upregulated in PH patients, with CXCR2 exhibiting the most pronounced increase (37.6-fold higher than controls, p = 0.0089), far exceeding other genes such as S100A9 (3.88-fold) and LTBP1 (5.47-fold) (Fig. 7A). Subsequently, we established a mouse model of pulmonary arterial hypertension (Fig. 7B). Right ventricular systolic pressure (RVSP) and staining confirmed successful modeling (Fig. 7C and D). Validation at the protein level confirmed consistent upregulation of CXCR2 in both SuHx mouse models (Fig. 7E) and PH (Fig. 7F). To further explore the relationship between CXCR2 and PH, we performed immunofluorescence staining on lung tissue sections from PH mice. The staining results showed that CXCR2 was specifically highly expressed in the intima-media layer (smooth muscle layer) of pulmonary vessels in SuHx mice (Fig. 7G), with its fluorescence intensity strongly positively correlated with the degree of vascular remodeling. Hypoxia is one of the key triggers of the complex pathological changes in PH. We established an in vitro PH model by hypoxic stimulation of PASMCs. Western blotting revealed that CXCR2 expression significantly increased in hypoxic hPASMCs (Fig. 7H). A time-point model of hPASMC hypoxia was established, and Western blotting detected CXCR2 protein expression. Results revealed that CXCR2 expression was significantly upregulated after 6 h of hypoxia, exhibiting a time-dependent increase with the most pronounced change at 24 h (Fig. 7I). Subsequently, immunohistochemical analysis assessed CXCR2 protein levels in SuHx-induced PH mouse lung tissue (Fig. 7J). Staining revealed markedly higher CXCR2 expression in pulmonary vessels of SuHx-induced PH mice compared to controls. These findings collectively demonstrate elevated CXCR2 expression in both in vitro and in vivo PH models, suggesting its potential as a novel diagnostic and therapeutic target for pulmonary hypertension.

Discussion

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is characterized by pathological remodeling of pulmonary vasculature, ultimately leading to right heart failure [4]. This disease is influenced by multiple genetic factors, including DNA mutations associated with its pathogenesis [31, 32]. The complexity of PH necessitates a comprehensive understanding of its molecular basis, particularly the interplay between epigenetic modifications and gene expression. This study aims to integrate methylation and transcriptomic data to elucidate key molecular networks in PH and identify potential therapeutic targets, thereby contributing to advances in treatment strategies for this debilitating disease.

This study employed a comprehensive bioinformatics approach to elucidate key molecular networks and potential therapeutic targets in pulmonary hypertension (PH) by integrating methylation and transcriptomic data. We utilized gene expression (GSE117261) and methylation datasets (GSE84395) from the GEO database, applying the limma and ChAMP software packages to identify differentially expressed genes and methylated sites. Additionally, we constructed a co-expression network using weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and explored the functional roles of key genes through immune infiltration analysis, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), gene set variation analysis (GSVA), and experimental validation techniques including RT-qPCR, Western blotting, and immunohistochemistry/immunofluorescence. Our findings identified seven key genes (S100A9, IL18RAP, CXCR2, LCN2, INHBA, CSF3R, LTBP1), with CXCR2 showing significant upregulation in both PH patients and animal models. This multifaceted approach not only highlights the complex interplay of inflammation and immune dysfunction in the pathogenesis of pulmonary arterial hypertension but also positions CXCR2 as a promising biomarker and therapeutic target, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for precision medicine in this complex disease.

Notably, the immunological analysis in this study revealed important insights into the role of immune cell infiltration in pulmonary hypertension (PH). The presence of neutrophils, monocytes, and T cells was significantly correlated with the expression of identified key genes. Immune cell infiltration is one of the key pathological features of PH pulmonary vascular remodeling, involving abnormal proliferation of endothelial cells, smooth muscle cells, and fibroblasts, as well as the recruitment of inflammatory cells [33, 34]. Our analysis demonstrated significantly elevated infiltration levels of immune cell subsets—including plasma cells, CD8 + T cells, and CD4 memory resting T cells—in PH patient samples compared to healthy controls. Further correlation analysis revealed that these seven key genes were closely associated with specific immune cell infiltration patterns. Collectively, these findings underscore the intricate relationship between immune cell dynamics and the molecular pathways driving PH, suggesting that modulating immune responses may represent a promising therapeutic intervention.

Given the central role of non-coding RNA (ncRNA) in gene expression regulation, signal transduction, and chromatin remodeling [35, 36], as well as its critical position in the onset and progression of various diseases (where abnormal expression or dysfunction often drives pathological processes) [37, 38], we further explored the post-transcriptional regulatory networks of key genes. Using the miRcode database for reverse prediction of the 7 key genes, we identified 80 miRNAs, establishing 218 mRNA-miRNA interaction pairs. The key gene-miRNA network diagram visually illustrates these regulatory relationships, suggesting that ncRNAs (particularly miRNAs) may modulate the expression of the identified key genes through multiple mechanisms, thereby participating in the pathological process of PH. Previous studies have confirmed that dysregulated ncRNAs can promote cell proliferation and malignant progression by activating oncogenic signaling pathways [39, 40], offering a new perspective for understanding the abnormal proliferation and remodeling of pulmonary vascular cells in PH.

Additionally, we observed an intriguing phenomenon during our experiments: among the seven key genes identified through screening, five were predicted to be highly methylated and lowly expressed, yet al.l showed high expression in PCR results. We hypothesize that the discrepancy between gene expression and epigenetic predictions in this study stems from PH subtype heterogeneity. During the screening phase, multi-model (APAH, FPAH, IPAH, Pulmonary veno-capillary disease, WHO GRP4) integrated data identified genes such as CSF3R, S100A9, and CXCR2 exhibiting a “high methylation-low expression” trend. However, all seven genes were significantly upregulated in the IPAH validation. This contradiction highlights the distinctiveness of IPAH as a strongly inflammation-driven subtype. Elevated levels of inflammatory mediators like IL-6 and TNF-α in IPAH patients may override methylation-induced suppression through pathways such as NF-κB, leading to upregulation of gene expression [41]. This demonstrates an “epigenetic-inflammatory regulatory escape” mechanism in IPAH, with CXCR2 emerging as a core target under dual inflammatory and epigenetic drives. Far from diminishing the study’s value, this discovery establishes a novel paradigm for precision therapy in IPAH—targeting CXCR2 could simultaneously address both inflammatory and epigenetic dysregulation.

Despite the aforementioned limitations, this study employs innovative multi-omics integration analysis to systematically reveal for the first time the central role of CXCR2 in PH. It proposes a molecular mechanism model demonstrating how CXCR2 drives disease progression by coordinating multiple signaling pathways involving inflammation, oxidative stress, proliferation, and metabolism. This not only provides novel biomarkers for PH diagnosis (such as CXCR2 and its associated immune characteristics) but, more importantly, lays a solid theoretical foundation for developing precision treatment strategies based on CXCR2 and its multi-pathway network (e.g., CXCR2-targeted therapy or multi-pathway combined intervention), offering new hope for overcoming current treatment bottlenecks in PH. Future research will focus on validating the core mechanisms of CXCR2 at finer cellular levels and in disease models, while also evaluating the efficacy of targeted intervention strategies.

Kerr-Newman quasinormal modes and Seiberg-Witten theory
Hector O. Silva, Jung-Wook Kim (김정욱), M. V. S. Saketh

Abstract
It was recently suggested the quasinormal-mode spectrum of black holes is related to a class of four-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 super Yang-Mills theories described by Seiberg-Witten curves, a proposal that has been tested for a number of black hole spacetimes. The aim of this study is to clarify the key ideas of this conjecture to a non-high-energy-physics audience and test it in a setting that has not yet been explored: the electromagnetic and gravitational perturbations of Kerr-Newman black holes in the Dudley-Finley approximation. In the parameter space we explore, we find numerical evidence that the conjecture is valid for subextremal black holes and its slowest damped quasinormal frequencies, thereby providing further support for the conjecture’s validity. In addition, we exploit the symmetries of the four-dimensional 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 super Yang-Mills theory to obtain a strikingly simple isospectral version of the radial Dudley-Finley equation.

II
Seiberg-Witten theory and the Aminov-Grassi-Hatsuda conjecture

The Seiberg-Witten theory
Seiberg and Witten 1994a
;
Seiberg and Witten 1994b
is an attempt to explain confinement in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) using supersymmetry.
Being an asymptotically free theory, QCD is described by different degrees of freedom at high energy scales (UV) and low energy scales (IR).
In the UV, QCD is weakly coupled and described by quarks and gluons, which are the fundamental degrees of freedom of the theory.
In the IR, QCD is strongly coupled and described by mesons and baryons, which are effective degrees of freedom of the theory.
In Seiberg-Witten theory, confinement is understood as condensation of monopoles where the (non-Abelian) electric fields become flux tubes due to the dual Meissner effect.
The theory provides tools to compute the masses of the particles in the IR description of the theory from its UV description and, in particular, the theory has monopoles in the IR.
Confinement is argued by showing that the theory admits a vacuum solution where the monopoles become massless, leading to the magnetic dual of superconductivity. See Ref.
Tachikawa 2013
for an introduction to the subject.

The Seiberg-Witten curve describes the IR particle spectrum of a (four-dimensional)
\mathcal{N}=2
gauge theory in the language of algebraic geometry.
The Seiberg-Witten curve is a complex one-dimensional manifold, which is given as the set of solutions to a polynomial of complex variables
x
and
p
,

\displaystyle F(p,x;E)

\displaystyle=0\,,

(1)

where
E
parametrizes the possible vacuum solutions (the vacuum moduli space) of the theory.
1
1

1

The Seiberg-Witten curve was motivated from monodromy properties of
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{D}
as a function of

The periods

A concrete example given in the original Seiberg-Witten paper

In this case, the period

A related phenomenon is the dictionary ambiguity, where multiple choices of the Seiberg-Witten parameters yield the same quantum Seiberg-Witten curve.
A concrete example is the sign ambiguity of the first two mass parameters

;

Before concluding the overview on quantum Seiberg-Witten curves and how they can be used to obtain quasinormal modes of black holes, let us comment on the relation between this approach and the Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin (WKB) approach.
After all, WKB methods have been used to this very same problem since the 1980s
Schutz and Will 1985
;
Iyer and Will 1987
.
The Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition
(
6
)
should be familiar to a reader with knowledge in WKB methods.
In fact, we arrive at the same differential equations when the quantum Seiberg-Witten curve
(
4
)
is chosen to have the form
\tilde{F}=-\hat{p}^{2}+Q(x)={{\rm d}^{2}}/{{\rm d}x^{2}}+Q(x)
.
This is not a coincidence: one way of demonstrating the Bethe/Gauge correspondence is to compute the quantum corrections
using
(exact) WKB methods applied to the quantum Seiberg-Witten curve and showing that they match the instanton calculations
Mironov and Morozov 2010a
;
Mironov and Morozov 2010b
.
The approach of using quantum Seiberg-Witten curves to compute quasinormal modes can be viewed as reversing the flow of logic; we solve the (exact) WKB problem using instanton calculus, based on other demonstrations of the Bethe/Gauge correspondence.
It has been argued that instanton calculus has an advantage over the WKB approach since the former is exact in
\hbar
and expected to be a convergent expansion in the instanton counting parameter
\Lambda_{N_{f}}
(in the semiclassical regime
|\Lambda_{N_{f}}/\mathfrak{a}|\ll 1

Grassi et al. 2020
), while the latter is known to be an asymptotic series in the formal expansion parameter
\hbar
having vanishing radius of convergence
Aminov et al. 2022
.

We show in subsequent sections that linearized vector and tensor perturbations on Kerr-Newman backgrounds can be converted to the form Eq. (
7

We present a parameter dictionary that is consistent with the period choice based on the classical limit, study the numerical convergence of the Seiberg-Witten approach to quasinormal modes, and leave the study of period ambiguities/dictionary ambiguities to the future.

III.1
The Dudley-Finley equation

Dudley and Finley
Dudley and Finley 1977
;
Dudley and Finley 1979
studied the separability of the
linear perturbations of the family of solutions of the Einstein-Maxwell theory obtained by Plebański
and Damiański

; see also Ref.
Griffiths and Podolsky 2006
or Sec. 21.1.2 of the monograph
Stephani et al. 2003
.
This family of solutions includes all electrovacuum spacetimes of Petrov-type D,
of which the Kerr-Newman metric
Newman et al. 1965
is its most famous specimen.
The coupled gravitoelectromagnetic perturbations of the Kerr-Newman solution
are not separable when decomposed in modes; see Ref.
Chandrasekhar 1985
, Sec. 111,
or Ref.
Giorgi 2022
, Sec. 1, for a discussion.
8
8

8

This poses challenges in proving
the linear stability of the Kerr-Newman solution. However, see Ref.
Giorgi 2022

for an alternative approach to address this problem based on physical-space methods.

Nonetheless, Refs.

;
Dudley and Finley 1979
, showed that mode separability is
possible if either the background metric or the electric field are kept fixed.
The resulting equation that describes the radial dependence of the
perturbations is a deformation of the Teukolsky equation

known as the Dudley-Finley equation. The angular dependence of the
perturbations is described by the spin-weighted spheroidal harmonics.

The Dudley-Finley equation is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Delta^{-s}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\left[\Delta^{s+1}\frac{{\rm d}\,{}_{s}R_{\ell m}}{{\rm d}r}\right]+\frac{1}{\Delta}\left[K^{2}-{\rm i}s\frac{{\rm d}\Delta}{{\rm d}r}K\right.\\
&\quad\left.+\,\Delta\left(2{\rm i}s\frac{{\rm d}K}{{\rm d}r}-\lambda_{\ell m}\right)\right]\,{}_{s}R_{\ell m}=0\,,\end{aligned}

(15)

where we defined

K=(r^{2}+a^{2})\,\omega-am\,,\quad\Delta=(r-r_{+})(r-r_{-})\,,

(16)

and

r_{\pm}=(1\pm b)/2\,,\quad\textrm{where}\quad b=\sqrt{1-4(a^{2}+Q^{2})}\,,

(17)

r_{+}
) and inner (

) horizons in Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, and

Here,
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}
is a separation constant and it corresponds to the
eigenvalue of the spin-weighted spheroidal harmonic equation;
see Eq. (
III.1
) next.
The spin-weight parameter
s
has values
0
,
-1
and
-2
for scalar,
electromagnetic, and gravitational perturbations, respectively.
The black hole’s angular momentum per unit mass is given by
a
, and is bound
to the interval,

0\leq a<\sqrt{1-4Q^{2}}/2\,,

(18)

in our units,
2M=1
. The Dudley-Finley equation is exact only when
s=0
or
Q=0
.
The former case describes the perturbations of a massless scalar field to the Kerr-Newman
background, whereas in the latter case, we recover the Teukolsky equation
Teukolsky 1973
.

In general, when
a\neq 0
, the separation constant
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}
has to
be determined, for a given value of
\omega
, by solving the equation,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}\left[(1-u^{2})\frac{{\rm d}\,{}_{s}S_{\ell m}}{{\rm d}u}\right]+\biggl[(a\omega u)^{2}-2a\omega su+s+{}_{s}A_{\ell m}\phantom{\frac{}{}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{(m+su)^{2}}{1-u^{2}}\biggr]\,{}_{s}S_{\ell m}=0\,,

(19)

where
u=\cos\vartheta
, related to the polar angle

\vartheta
of Boyer-Lindquist coordinates, and imposing
boundary conditions such that the eigenfunctions
{}_{s}S_{\ell m}
are finite at the
regular points
u=\pm 1
.
These eigenfunctions are known as the spin-weighted spheroidal harmonics, and

c=a\omega
is the spheroidicity parameter
c
, which is complex
valued in general.
For vanishing spheroidicity,
{}_{s}S_{\ell m}
become the spin-weighted
spherical harmonics, with eigenvalues,

{}_{s}A_{\ell m}=\ell(\ell+1)-s(s+1)\,,\quad\textrm{for}\quad c=0.

(20)

Analytical corrections in
c
to the foregoing equation can be obtained
perturbatively; see, for instance Refs.
Press and Teukolsky 1973
;
Fackerell and Crossman 1977
;
Seidel 1989
;
Berti et al. 2006
.

Before proceeding, one may ask: what is the regime of validity of the
Dudley-Finley approximation?
This question was studied by Berti and Kokkotas in the nonrotating limit
of the Dudley-Finley equation
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
.
In this limit, they compared the quasinormal frequencies obtained from the
Dudley-Finley equation against the respective frequencies obtained from a
perturbed Reissner-Nordström black hole where one “freezes” either the
metric or electromagnetic perturbations.

To provide some context, we recall that it is known from the works of Zerilli
Zerilli 1974
, Moncrief
Moncrief 1974a
;
Moncrief 1974b
;
Moncrief 1975
,
and Chandrasekhar and Xanthopoulos
Chandrasekhar and Xanthopoulos 1979
;
Xanthopoulos 1981

that the coupled linear metric and electromagnetic perturbations of the Reissner-Nordström solution are separable.
Specifically, the perturbations of axial and polar parities can each be reduced
to a pair of coupled differential equations that describe the interaction
between gravitational and electromagnetic perturbations.
Each pair of equations can then be decoupled by introducing a suitable linear combination
of the original perturbations variables.
Remarkably, Chandrasekhar showed that
each decoupled equation describing perturbations of one parity have the same
quasinormal mode spectra as its counterpart describing perturbations of the
other parity
Chandrasekhar 1980
.
For this reason, calculations of the quasinormal frequencies of the Reissner-Nordström
solution often make use of the decoupled equations of axial parity, which are simpler
in form.

In Ref.
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
, Berti and Kokkotas took the
coupled

equations of axial parity and imposed on them that either the electromagnetic
or gravitational perturbation vanished.
They found that the quasinormal frequencies of the resultant equations agree
well with the respective results obtained using the Dudley-Finley equation for

s=-1
and
-2
as long as
Q\lesssim 1/4
.
Surprisingly, the agreement between the two approximations is not exact. This
suggests that the approach of Dudley and Finley is not equivalent to simply
fixing either the background metric or electric field
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
.
Nonetheless, Ref.
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
also found that the Dudley-Finley equation
reproduces within 1% the quasinormal frequencies of the coupled system of
equations when
Q\lesssim 1/4
, and argued that the same level of accuracy
would hold for the Kerr-Newman solution as long as the black hole spin is
sufficiently small; see also Ref.
Pani et al. 2013b
.
However, Mark et al.
Mark et al. 2015
found that the Dudley-Finley equation
no longer predicts the quasinormal-frequencies accurately as the spin
increases, with the exception of a special set of modes of rapidly rotating
black holes.

III.2
Canonical form and Seiberg-Witten dictionary

Having presented the Dudley-Finley equation and discussed its regime of
validity, we know establish its connection to Seiberg-Witten theory.
The Dudley-Finley equation (
15
) can be rewritten in the
form Eq. (
7
) through the redefinition

{}_{s}R_{\ell m}=\Delta^{-(s+1)/2}\,{}_{s}\Psi_{\ell m}\,,

(21)

together with the change of coordinates

r=r_{+}+(r_{+}-r_{-})(z-1)\,.

(22)

The
A_{i}
coefficients corresponding to the
\hat{A}_{i}
coefficients of Eq. (
7
) are

A_{0}=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\left[2am-{\rm i}(r_{+}-r_{-})s+2(Q^{2}-r_{-})\,\omega\right]^{2}}{4(r_{+}-r_{-})^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} A_{1}&={}_{s}A_{\ell m}+s(s+1)-2(1-Q^{2})\,\omega^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{r_{+}-r_{-}}\left\{\left[2-6Q^{2}-(2r_{-}+3)a^{2}\right]\omega^{2}\right.\\
&\quad\left.-2\left[am+{\rm i}s(2a^{2}+Q^{2})\right]\omega+2{\rm i}ams\right\}\,,\\
A_{2}&=-{}_{s}A_{\ell m}-s(s+1)-3{\rm i}s(r_{+}-r_{-})\,\omega\\
&\quad+(6r_{-}-5a^{2}-6Q^{2})\,\omega^{2}\,,\\
A_{3}&=2\,({\rm i}s+2r_{-}\omega)\,(r_{+}-r_{-})\,\omega\,,\\
A_{4}&=(r_{+}-r_{-})^{2}\,\omega^{2}\,.\end{aligned}

(23)

We can now relate the Seiberg-Witten parameters
\{E,\bm{m},\Lambda_{3}\}

to the Dudley-Finley-equation parameters
\{a,Q,\omega,m,s,{}_{s}A_{\ell m}\}
by matching the coefficients

A_{i}
, listed in Eq. (
23
), with the coefficients
\hat{A}_{i}
, listed in Eq. (
8
).
One possible solution is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Lambda_{3}&=-8{\rm i}(r_{+}-r_{-})\omega\,,\\
E&=-\tfrac{1}{4}-{}_{s}A_{\ell m}-s(s+1)+(2-a^{2}-2Q^{2})\,\omega^{2}\,,\\
m_{1}&=-s-{\rm i}\omega\,,\quad m_{3}=s-{\rm i}\omega\,,\\
m_{2}&={{\rm i}\left[2am-(1-2Q^{2})\,\omega\right]}/(r_{+}-r_{-})\,.\end{aligned}

(24)

The physical domain
r\in[r_{+},\infty)
corresponds to the domain
z\in[1,\infty)
; therefore, we impose the Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition (
6
)
to the
B
cycle.
Similar to the Kerr case
Aminov et al. 2022
, the extremal limit corresponds to
\Lambda_{3}\to 0
and
m_{2}\to\infty
limit for the
N_{f}=3
dictionary, cf. Eq. (
24
), where the combination
m_{2}\Lambda_{3}
remains finite.
This combination can be identified as
\Lambda_{2}^{2}=m_{2}\Lambda_{3}
, when the theory is matched to
N_{f}=2
.
We focus on the nonextremal case in this work and leave the study of extremal case for future work.

We emphasize that Eq. (
24
) is not the unique solution because of the
symmetries of the quantum Seiberg-Witten curve (
8
), namely:

1.

the mass exchange
m_{1}\leftrightarrow m_{2}
.

2.

the mass-sign flip
m_{1,2}\to-m_{1,2}
.

3.

the simultaneous sign flip
m_{3}\to-m_{3}
and
\Lambda_{3}\to-\Lambda_{3}
.

These three twofold symmetries generate
2^{3}=8
solutions.
However, only half of the solutions
are distinct since the Nekrasov-Shatashvili free energy
{\cal F}^{\,(3)}
is symmetric under permutation of the masses
m_{i}
.
The different choices for the dictionary predict different quasinormal frequencies when the Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition (
6
)
is imposed on
\Pi_{B}
, and is related to the fact that the parameters
m_{1,2}
(
m_{3}
and
\Lambda_{3}
) control the boundary conditions imposed at the horizon (at infinity)
Casals and da Costa 2022
;
Ge et al. 2024
.
This also could be an instance of the monodromy ambiguity discussed in Sec.
II
,
which we leave the detailed study of for the future.

Because the spin-weighted spheroidal harmonic equation (
III.1
)
can also be rewritten in the form of Eq. (
7
), the eigenvalue problem for
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}
can also be solved using instanton calculations
Aminov et al. 2022
.
The physical domain
u\in[-1,1]
is mapped to the domain
z\in[0,1]
and, therefore, the quantization condition Eq. (
6
) is imposed on the
A
cycle.
This is identified as imposing the condition
\mathfrak{a}={\rm i}(\ell+1/2)
, and Ref.
Aminov et al. 2022

found it leads to the relation,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {}_{s}A_{\ell m}&=\ell(\ell+1)-s(s+1)-c^{2}\\
&\quad+\Lambda_{3}\,{\partial_{\Lambda_{3}}{\cal F}^{\,(3)}_{\textrm{inst}}}({\rm i}\ell+{\rm i}/2,\,\bm{m}\,,\Lambda_{3})|_{\Lambda_{3}=16c}\,,\\
\bm{m}&=\{-m,\,-s,\,-s\}\,,\end{aligned}

(25)

which is derived from the Matone relation (
9
);
the eigenvalue
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}
only appears in
E
in the parameter dictionary Eq. (4.11) of Ref.
Aminov et al. 2022
.
Unlike the radial case, the three twofold symmetries of the quantum
Seiberg-Witten curve (
8
)
lead to the same expression for the angular
eigenvalue
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}
.

III.3
Calculation of quasinormal modes with Leaver’s method

To validate the quasinormal frequencies of the Dudley-Finley equation obtained
through Seiberg-Witten theory, we computed the same quantities using
Leaver’s method
Leaver 1985
.
Our calculation using Leaver’s method is not original and it was first
done in Ref.
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
. In this section, we provide a concise
account of this calculation.

The starting point consist in noticing that by imposing quasinormal-mode boundary
conditions on the function
{}_{s}R_{\ell m}
, we find the mode functions behave as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lim_{r\,\to\,r_{+}}{}_{s}R_{\ell m}&\simeq\,(r-r_{+})^{-s-{\rm i}\sigma_{+}},\\
\lim_{r\,\to\,\infty}{}_{s}R_{\ell m}&\simeq\,r^{-1-2s+{\rm i}\omega}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega r},\end{aligned}

(26)

near the event horizon
r_{+}
and at spatial infinity, respectively. Here,
we introduced
\sigma_{+}=[\omega(r_{+}-Q^{2})-am]/b
, and recall that the
inner and outer horizon locations
r_{\pm}
are given by Eq. (
17
).
A solution to Eq. (
15
) satisfying these boundary conditions can be written
in the form
Leaver 1985
,

\displaystyle{}_{s}R_{\ell m}

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega r}\,(r-r_{-})^{-1-s+{\rm i}\omega+{\rm i}\sigma_{+}}\,(r-r_{+})^{-s-{\rm i}\sigma_{+}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\left(\frac{r-r_{+}}{r-r_{-}}\right)^{n}\,.

(27)

Substituting Eq. (
27
) into Eq. (
15
) yields a three-term recursion relation for the coefficients
a_{n}

that we write in a form analogous to Leaver’s
Leaver 1985
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\alpha_{0}^{r}\,a_{1}+\beta_{0}^{r}\,a_{0}=0\,,\\
&\alpha_{n}^{r}\,a_{n+1}+\beta_{n}^{r}\,a_{n}+\gamma_{n}^{r}\,a_{n-1}=0\,,\quad n=1,\,2,\,\dots\end{aligned}

(28)

The recursion coefficients are

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \alpha_{n}^{r}&=n^{2}+(c_{0}+1)n+c_{0}\,,\\
\beta_{n}^{r}&=-2n^{2}+(c_{1}+2)n+c_{3}\,,\\
\gamma_{n}^{r}&=n^{2}+(c_{2}-3)n+c_{4}-c_{2}+2\,,\end{aligned}

(29)

where we defined the intermediate constants
c_{i}
,

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=1-s-{\rm i}\omega-\frac{2{\rm i}}{b}\left[\frac{\omega}{2}(1-2Q^{2})-am\right],

(30a)

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=-4+2{\rm i}\omega(2+b)+\frac{4{\rm i}}{b}\left[\frac{\omega}{2}(1-2Q^{2})-am\right],

(30b)

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=s+3-3{\rm i}\omega-\frac{2{\rm i}}{b}\left[\frac{\omega}{2}(1-2Q^{2})-am\right],

(30c)

\displaystyle c_{3}

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}(4+2b-a^{2}-4Q^{2})-2am\omega-s-1+{\rm i}\omega(2+b)

\displaystyle\quad-{}_{s}A_{\ell m}+\frac{4\omega+2{\rm i}}{b}\left[\frac{\omega}{2}(1-2Q^{2})-am\right],

(30d)

\displaystyle c_{4}

\displaystyle=s+1-2\omega^{2}-(2s+3){\rm i}\omega-\frac{4\omega+2{\rm i}}{b}\left[\frac{\omega}{2}(1-2Q^{2})\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\frac{}{}-am\right]\,.

(30e)

Equations (
29
)–(
30
) are equivalent
to Eq. (6) in Ref.
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
, but we write them here in a way
that makes the Kerr limit (
Q=0
) easier to compare with Eqs. (24)–(26)
of Leaver
Leaver 1985
.

The series (
27
) converges and the boundary
conditions (
26
) are satisfied if
\omega
is a solution to the continued fraction

0=\beta_{0}^{r}-\frac{\alpha_{0}^{r}\,\gamma_{1}^{r}}{\beta_{1}^{r}-}\,\frac{\alpha_{1}^{r}\,\gamma_{2}^{r}}{\beta_{2}^{r}-}\,\frac{\alpha_{2}^{r}\,\gamma_{3}^{r}}{\beta_{3}^{r}-}\cdots,

(31)

for given values of
a
,
Q
,
s
,
m
, and
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}
. The latter is
obtained by solving a similar continued-fraction equation associated to
Eq. (
III.1
); see Ref.
Leaver 1985
, Eqs. (20) and (21).

To obtain a quasinormal frequency, the two (radial and angular) continued
fractions must be be satisfied simultaneously. The problem of finding a
quasinormal mode then becomes a root-finding problem.
Our computations were performed in C++. We used Muller’s
method
Muller 1956
to perform root finding, using the
pseudocode from the “Numerical Recipes,”
Chap. 9.2
Press et al. 2007
.
We found excellent agreement with the results of Ref.
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
.

V
Hidden symmetries of the Dudley-Finley equations

Following Ref.
Hatsuda 2021
(see Appendix
A
), we can exchange mass parameters to obtain a simpler differential equation that has the same quasinormal mode spectrum as the original Dudley-Finley equation (
15
).
In particular, the exchange
m_{2}\leftrightarrow m_{3}
yields the new
A_{i}^{\prime}
coefficients,

\displaystyle A_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}-s^{2}\,,

\displaystyle A_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle={}_{s}A_{\ell m}+s(s+2)+a\omega\left(a\omega-2m\right)\,,

\displaystyle A_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-{}_{s}A_{\ell m}-s(s+1)-a\omega\left(5a\omega-6m\right)\,,

(33)

\displaystyle A_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=4\omega\left(-am+2a^{2}\omega+Q^{2}\omega\right)\,,

\displaystyle A_{4}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(r_{+}-r_{-})^{2}\omega^{2}\,,

which are simpler than the original
A_{i}
coefficients in Eq. (
23
).
We thus have a function
\Psi
that obeys Eq. (
7
), but with the coefficients
A^{\prime}_{i}
.
Next, we define

\Psi(z)=\sqrt{f(z)}\,\phi(z)\,,\quad f(z)=1-z^{-1}\,,

(34)

where
z
was defined in Eq. (
22
).
This results in a simple Regge–Wheeler-like equation for
\phi
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left[f\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\left(f\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}z}\right)+\omega^{2}-V(z)\right]\phi(z)=0\,,

(35)

with effective potential,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} V&=f\,\left[4(c^{2}+d^{2})+\frac{4c(m-c)}{z}\right.\\
&\left.\quad+\,\frac{{}_{s}A_{\ell m}+s(s+1)-c(2m-c)}{z^{2}}-\frac{s^{2}-1}{z^{3}}\right],\end{aligned}

(36)

where
c=a\omega
and
d=Q\omega
. This equation generalizes
the chargeless case presented in Ref.
Hatsuda 2021
, Eqs. (1) and (2), to nonzero
Q
.
The potential (
36
) is real, contrary to
Eq. (
15
).
In the nonrotating limit
c=0
and
{}_{s}A_{\ell m}=\ell(\ell+1)-s(s-1)
, and Eq. (
36
) reduces to

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=f\,\left[(2Q\omega)^{2}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{z^{2}}-\frac{s^{2}-1}{z^{3}}\right].

(37)

For
Q\neq 0
, this potential does not recover the gravitoelectromagnetic
perturbation equations of the Reissner-Nordström solution when we impose either
gravitational or electromagnetic perturbation to be zero; see Ref.
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
, Eq. (20).
This is consistent with the conclusion of Berti and Kokkotas, discussed in Sec.
III.1
,
that the Dudley-Finley approximation does not completely freeze either perturbations.
Finally, taking the additional limit
Q=0
, we find
z=r
, and Eq. (
37
) reduces to the Regge-Wheeler potential
for generic massless bosonic perturbations of a Schwarzschild black hole.

VI
Conclusions and outlook

We studied the recently proposed connection between Seiberg-Witten theory and
black hole quasinormal frequencies
Aminov et al. 2022
, in the context of the
gravitoelectromagnetic perturbations of the Kerr-Newman solution in the
Dudley-Finley approximation.
For subextremal black holes and in the parameter space we surveyed, we found
that the lowest damped quasinormal frequencies computed using the
gauge-theoretical tools agree with those obtain using the continued fraction
method
Leaver 1985
;
Berti and Kokkotas 2005
.
Our results give further support for the validity of the proposal of Aminov et al.
Aminov et al. 2022
. In addition, we obtained a simple
equation that is isospectral to the radial Dudley-Finley equation following Refs.
Hatsuda 2021
;
Casals and da Costa 2022
.
Where do we go from here?

One direction would be to study the extremal case, in which the
Dudley-Finley equation maps to the quantum Seiberg-Witten curve for
N_{f}=2
,
cf. Eq.(
13
). From a physical point of view, however, the problem
is somewhat less interesting as the Dudley-Finley equation becomes a poor
approximation to actual dynamics of gravitoelectromagnetic perturbations in
this limit.

Going beyond the Dudley-Finley approximation, the gravitoelectromagnetic perturbations on Kerr-Newman metric form a system of coupled differential equations, as we described in Sec.
III.1
.
If a quantum integrable system that corresponds to a pair of coupled differential equations exists,
and if the system further admits a gauge-theoretic description à la Bethe/Gauge correspondence,
the Seiberg-Witten-quasinormal-mode correspondence may provide an alternative method of computing quasinormal modes of Kerr-Newman black holes without resorting to the approximation of Dudley and Finley and complementary to Refs.
Dias et al. 2015
;
Dias et al. 2022a
;
Dias et al. 2022b
.
We leave the exploration of this topic to future work.

Another interesting topic to explore would be to study the asymptotic behavior of quasinormal frequencies through the lens of the Seiberg-Witten-quasinormal-mode correspondence.
It is known that the real part of quasinormal mode frequencies of the Schwarzschild solution asymptotically approach a constant value as the overtone number
n\to\infty

Nollert 1993
;
Andersson 1993
;
Motl 2003
;
Motl and Neitzke 2003
;
Andersson and Howls 2004
;
Natário and Schiappa 2004

which was once conjectured to convey information on the quantum nature of black holes
York 1983
;
Hod 1998
;
see Ref.
Berti et al. 2009
, Sec. 10.1, for a discussion.
Regardless, while the parameter dictionary Eq. (
24
) is unsuitable for studying asymptotic behavior of quasinormal modes because the expansion parameter
\Lambda_{3}
diverges in the asymptotic limit,

Finally, there are some improvements that can be implemented on the Seiberg-Witten side.
The computational bottleneck on the quantum Seiberg-Witten curve approach is evaluation of the Nekrasov-Shatashvili free energy.
While the formulas for the instanton partition function
Z^{(N_{f})}
can be evaluated very efficiently,
the limiting procedure required to compute the Nekrasov-Shatashvili free energy (
32
) is very slow in general.
As we remarked in Sec.
IV

Z^{(N_{f})}

We can expect to speed up the computation of the Nekrasov-Shatashvili free energy by using specialized techniques for simplifying rational functions,
such as functional reconstruction using finite fields. This technique has been proven powerful in Feynman diagram calculations

Peraro 2016

Another possibility is to use continued-fraction methods for computing the Nekrasov-Shatashvili free energy

Cipriani et al. 2025

Such an improvement would be necessary to study quantum Seiberg-Witten curves at higher orders in the instanton series.

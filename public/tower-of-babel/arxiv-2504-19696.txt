On the unique evolutionary mechanisms of massive quiescent galaxies in the epoch of reionisation 2025 On the unique evolutionary mechanisms of massive quiescent galaxies in the epoch of reionisation – References
Harry George Chittenden, Karl Glazebrook, Themiya Nanayakkara, Lalitwadee Kawinwanichakij, Claudia Lagos, Lucas Kimmig, Rhea-Silvia Remus

Abstract
We investigate the evolutionary histories of a population of high mass, high redshift, quiescent galaxies in the cosmohydrodynamical simulation Thesan ; studying the characteristic properties of their haloes and environments over the epoch of reionisation. Thesan employs a modified version of the Arepo moving-mesh code utilised in IllustrisTNG , whose explicit handling of ionising radiation couples haloes and galaxies with cosmic reionisation. This results in a regime of rapid growth and quenching, producing nine massive quiescent galaxies at z = 5.5 z=5.5 , in a 95.5 ​ cMpc 3 95.5\text{cMpc}^{3} volume, with no counterpart in IllustrisTNG . We find that the rapid assembly of stellar mass is attributed to smooth accretion of mass onto their haloes in dense environments, particularly from massive neighbouring structures; while these galaxies exhibit fast-growing potential wells hosting massive central black holes. With insignificant merger activity, we find AGN feedback to be the primary source of quenching. We find megaparsec-scale densities and halo masses to continue growing after quenching, suggesting that massive quiescent galaxies will be found in some of the largest haloes and densest regions of space by observations. With an unprecedented plethora of massive quiescent galaxies found in JWST data, these reionisation simulations may provide the paradigm for the evolutionary histories of these galaxies, while constraining the relatively unknown physics of reionisation. Despite the rarity of simulated massive quiescent galaxies, their identification in Thesan enables identification of halo and environmental properties most conducive to their quenching, hopefully guiding future efforts into the study of analogous systems in deep observational surveys and large-volume N-body simulations.

Introduction

The advent of high-redshift galaxy surveys, with modern state-of-the-art telescopes such as
JWST
, has culminated in a new suite of spectroscopic data for some of the earliest galaxies, which will continue to grow with the advent of future facilities such as the ELT. The epoch of reionisation may hold the key to understanding the history of high redshift objects, particularly massive quiescent galaxies, which may then be used as a relic of this epoch to study its effects on galaxies and haloes. Given the similar growth in the abundance of massive quiescent galaxies with time in
Magneticum
and
Thesan

Magneticum

A number of studies have composed machine learning models to predict galaxy properties solely from the dark matter component of the simulation
(
Agarwal

;

Training a machine learning framework for massive quiescent galaxies in N-body simulations will most likely require massive quiescent galaxy data spanning multiple simulations; while differences in subgrid physics and cosmological parameters may result in distinct massive quiescent galaxy characteristics, and will inevitably need to be accounted for.

In this work, we investigate the properties of massive quiescent galaxies in the
Thesan
simulation, and compare their galaxy and halo formation histories with their high-mass star-forming counterparts, in order to gain insight into their distinct evolutionary pathways. We use statistical methods to identify halos which may host massive quiescent galaxies or their progenitors, and compare the galaxy and halo growth, environments, gas kinematics and merger histories of this critical dataset. We use this information to provide a physical interpretation of the growth and quenching of massive galaxies at high redshifts, and assess the unique characteristics which may be observed by
JWST
and other deep surveys.

The

2.1.1
Public data release

The
Thesan

(
Kannan et al. 2021
;
Garaldi et al. 2022
;
Smith et al. 2022
)
consists of a set of cubic simulation boxes of side length 95.5 comoving megaparsecs, evaluated down to a redshift of
z=5.5
. Each simulation is run with the same initial conditions, creating a near identical matter distribution with cross-matched subhalo catalogs between simulations of different resolutions and different physical models.

The fiducial
Thesan

Thesan-1

2100^{3}

3.12\times 10^{6}M_{\odot}
and

. The fiducial model is also run in the lower-resolution

2.49\times 10^{7}M_{\odot}
and
4.66\times 10^{6}M_{\odot}
. The suite also contains a pure dark matter run for each of the aforementioned simulations, and a series of alternative models such as one including strong dark acoustic oscillations, and another including high ionising photon escape fractions.

2.1.2
Physical models in
Thesan

The
Thesan
simulations build upon the galaxy formation model of
IllustrisTNG
to model star formation. This model assumes star formation occurs in dense gas, following the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation, and employs a two-phase effective equation of state for the interstellar medium. While
Thesan
inherits the fundamental star formation prescription from
IllustrisTNG
, the implementation of radiative transfer is the distinctive feature setting
Thesan
apart from
IllustrisTNG
.

Instead of the spatially uniform UV background used in
IllustrisTNG

(
Faucher-Giguère et al. 2008
)
,
Thesan
incorporates a self-consistent radiation transport scheme using the model extension introduced in
Arepo-RT

(
Kannan et al. 2019
;
Kannan et al. 2021
)
. The
Arepo-RT
code links radiation pressure to the kinetic energy of ionisation fronts via the Eddington tensor, thereby removing the dependence of calculations on the local density of ionising sources
(
Kannan et al. 2019
)
. It also employs a subcycling algorithm, in which radiative transfer is resampled multiple times within each hydrodynamical timestep. This allows efficient tracking of multiple photon energy bins coupled to gas hydrodynamics in real time, allowing ionisation fronts to form dynamically and interact with the local environment
(
Garaldi et al. 2022
;
Garaldi
et al. 2024
;
Shen et al. 2024b
)
. Coupled with non-equilibrium thermochemistry, the self-consistent modelling of reionisation and galaxy formation in
Thesan
influences gas heating, cooling, and ionisation; ultimately allowing for a more detailed picture of star formation, particularly in low-mass haloes and at high redshifts
(
Garaldi et al. 2022
;
Zier et al. 2025
)
.

Thesan
also models supermassive black hole growth and active galactic nucleus feedback, again adopting the framework utilised in
IllustrisTNG

(
Garaldi et al. 2022
)
. This model includes black hole seeding, modified
Bondi 1952
accretion, and kinetic and thermal active galactic nucleus feedback modes
(
Weinberger
et al. 2016
, for details see)
. However, the implementation of explicit radiation transfer in
Thesan
once again distinguishes the result of this model from that of
IllustrisTNG
.

The gas surrounding a black hole in
Thesan
will be exposed to a variable radiation field, whose intensity and spectrum depend on the proximity and properties of local sources, including the black hole itself, as well as nearby stars and other active galactic nuclei
(
Garaldi et al. 2022
;
Garaldi
et al. 2024
)
. Furthermore, the spectral energy distribution of the active galactic nucleus is explicitly modelled in
Thesan
, thereby considering them a key source of ionising radiation
(
Kannan et al. 2021
;
Garaldi
et al. 2024
)
. This realistic radiation environment likely affects the gas conditions around black holes, creating more realistic and anisotropic feedback pathways through alterations in the availability of accretion fuel, the evolution of host galaxies, the self-consistent modelling of active galactic nucleus radiation, and self-shielding effects
(
Garaldi et al. 2022
;
Garaldi
et al. 2024
;
Zier et al. 2025
)
.

For instance, more accurate self-shielding in
Thesan
, facilitated by radiative transfer, can allow the survival of cold, low-density gas phases within galaxies
(
Kannan
et al. 2025
;
Zier et al. 2025
)
. If black holes reside in regions with more of this cold, dense gas, it could potentially increase the availability of accretion fuel compared to a scenario where a uniform UV background more readily ionises such gas
(
Kannan
et al. 2025
)
. Conversely, intense radiation from nearby sources, such as starburst regions or other active galactic nuclei, could photo-heat and ionize the gas around a black hole in
Thesan
, potentially reducing the accretion rate
(
Garaldi et al. 2022
)
.

Therefore, while the star formation, black hole growth and active galactic nucleus feedback models in the
IllustrisTNG
and
Thesan
simulations are identical, the difference in treatment of ionising radiation can profoundly change how these processes manifest, thereby changing the properties and population statistics of galaxies in their respective simulations.

2.2
Halo and galaxy properties

2.2.1
Static halo properties

For studying the baryonic components of massive quiescent galaxies, we extract stellar, gas and black hole masses, stellar and gas half-mass radii, metallicities and star formation rates from the main progenitor branches of the merger trees. For analysing the halo properties of massive quiescent galaxy hosts, we do the same for
SubFind
halo mass
(M_{h})
, half-mass radius
(R_{h})
and specific angular momentum
(L_{h})
; but unlike the complete
SubFind
mass for haloes, galaxy properties are defined within twice the stellar half-mass radius, to avoid contamination from satellite sources, which can obscure the true quiescent state of some galaxies.

Similarly to
Chittenden &
Tojeiro 2023
, we use two of these variables to compute a proxy for the halo’s virial velocity: a measure of the halo’s central potential and internal particle dynamics. Unlike the rotation curve maximum velocity provided in the merger trees, this proxy is not significantly biased by the prescence or abscence of baryons in the simulation, which is crucial for applying the model to N-body simulations. The proxy
V_{h}
is defined in terms of halo mass
M_{h}
and halo half-mass radius
R_{h}
as follows:

V_{h}=\left(\frac{M_{h}}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{R_{h}}{\text{ckpc}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(1)

where constant factors such as the Newtonian gravitational constant are omitted.

2.2.2
Overdensity

Overdensity is used in addition to these properties to characterise the haloes’ local environments. These are calculated explicitly using halo catalogues per snapshot, and for the sake of future application to pure dark matter simulations, the overdensities are strictly calculated using the dark matter component, unlike the total gas overdensities provided in
Thesan
’s supplementary data catalog. These two density components may in fact be correlated, but the gas component may be biased by localised gas fractions or star formation rates.

In
Chittenden &
Tojeiro 2023
, overdensity was calculated using a nearest neighbour searcher with periodic boundary conditions. However, in
Chittenden et al. 2025
, these overdensities were found to be sensitive to mass resolution when applied to another simulation, particularly due to edge effects of haloes within the calculation volume, which can be particularly problematic for low mass haloes or underdense regions of space. Instead, for every snapshot, we calculate densities using a Gaussian kernel density estimator, applying a comoving bandwidth each time, and evaluate the local density at the location of each target subhalo.

We investigate the role of environment on the evolution of massive galaxies on multiple scales, aiming to characterise both the effects of localised interactions and the influence of large scale structures such as cosmic filaments. Specifically, we evaluate overdensities with three apertures: 200 comoving kiloparsecs, 1 comoving megaparsec and 3 comoving megaparsecs; which we denote as
\delta_{\text{200ckpc}}
,
\delta_{\text{1cMpc}}
and
\delta_{\text{3cMpc}}
.
Kimmig et al. 2025
show that massive quiescent galaxies in the
Magneticum Pathfinder
simulation from
z=2.79
to
z=4.23
reside in underdense regions up to a 2 comoving megaparsec aperture when compared with massive star-forming galaxies, while this difference becomes negligible for larger scale densities and higher redshifts. The quenching of earlier massive quiescent galaxies, however, is likely to be driven by distinct mechanisms owing to the differences in gas phases before and after reionisation. The abundance of ionising sources in a dense environment is likely to affect the gas supply of massive galaxies, therefore the small-scale environment may play a different role in
Thesan
than in
Magneticum
, which this study will investigate.

2.2.3
Gradients and timescales

For each of the four halo properties and three calculated overdensities, we implement measures of the timescales on which these quantities grow to their final values, and the time at which the masses, structures and enviornments are achieved. Traditionally, these quantities are used to measure mass assembly, yet we are interested as well in the formation of gravitational potentials and filamentary networks which lead to the assembly and quenching of massive galaxies. As many quantities in the halo formation history are inherently correlated, however, their temporal geometries can be exploited in the same way to parameterise the growth of these halo properties.

To characterise the proportionate speed and time scale of a halo’s growth, we use the specific accretion gradient introduced by
Montero-Dorta et al. 2021
, denoted
\beta
. These authors define the mass accretion gradient as the slope of a straight line fit to the logarithmic specific mass accretion rate of a halo as a function of logarithmic time, and show its superiority to the more commonly used half-mass formation time in predicting stellar masses, gas fractions, photometry and assembly bias in
IllustrisTNG
data.
Chittenden &
Tojeiro 2023
also use the
\beta
gradient for halo mass to predict star formation and metallicity histories in
IllustrisTNG
, where it proves valuable for constraining the stellar-halo mass and mass-magnitude relations; as well as identifying haloes which are poorly resolved by the simulation, by exploiting the Gaussian distribution of this parameter.

For this example, each of these gradients are of similar value, with the exception of the steeper radial growth history gradient; which may indicate a tidal interaction with large scale structure, considering that the 3 comoving megaparsecs scale overdensity for this sample is exceptionally high for haloes of this mass.

As we are interested in the development of a halo’s size, potential, environment and spin as well as its mass, we compute
\beta
gradients for all aforementioned halo properties. In
fig.

2
we show how these gradients are defined using one halo from
Thesan-1
as an example, and show that the similar geometry of the formation histories of the baseline halo properties allows for these fits to be made. While the mass accretion parameter
\beta_{M_{h}}
corresponds strictly to the speed of halo assembly, however, some of these gradients have further implications.

Firstly, the angular momentum gradient
\beta_{L_{h}}
, like
\beta_{M_{h}}
, follows a Gaussian distribution, but this Gaussian has a larger width, and its mean corresponds to a shallower
\beta
gradient. This is because a halo can lose a significant fraction of its angular momentum, such as through retrograde interactions with its environment. Extreme values of
\beta_{L_{h}}
are therefore not necessarily indicative of poor mass or spatial resolution, but simply a halo which does not consistently gain this angular momentum.

Second, environmental gradients exhibit a positive skew, making their distribution appear Poissonian rather than Gaussian. While some haloes reside in environments which grow consistently, others reside in environments with little to no growth over time, particularly when measured on large scales. While small-scale environments are likely to be influenced by halo-halo interactions which shape these environments quickly, megaparsec-scale structures evolve from the clustering of galaxies and groups, which takes place more slowly.

For each of the above cases, the evaluated gradients are close to zero where there is little to no net growth in the spin or environment of this halo.

As in
Chittenden &
Tojeiro 2023
, we discard any haloes where one of these
\beta
gradients exceeds a 5
\sigma
difference from the best fit mean.

In addition to the specific accretion gradients of these properties, we include two other scalar variables per property to our analysis: scaled accretion timescale, and half-peak formation redshift. We find in the
Thesan
data that haloes and galaxies begin to grow and acquire significant fractions of their mass at a wide range of times in the simulation’s time domain, which is less true for simulations which terminate at much lower redshifts. The
\beta
values, while practical as measures for sustained accretion, do not strictly capture this.

The scaled accretion times, denoted
a
, were introduced by
Shi et al. 2020
, and are defined as the ratio of Hubble scale factors between two key snapshots: the time of maximised halo mass to the time that half of this value is reached. In combination with the half-peak formation redshift, these indicate when the main body of the halo is established, and how it continues to grow after this. In contrast with
\beta
, these quantities measure the critical times in the halo’s history rather than the global rate of assembly, and so distinguish early-forming and late-forming haloes from rapidly and slowly growing haloes.

2.2.4
Merger history

The interactions of galaxy-hosting haloes with their surroundings, such as the acquisition of secondary structures from the infall of adjacent haloes, has long been established as a significant phenomenon influencing the evolution of galaxies. In our analysis, we consider key properties of the merger histories of massive haloes as a means to differentiate star-forming and quiescent galaxies.

Following the methods of
McGibbon &
Khochfar 2023
, we select merging progenitors by identifying subhaloes whose main descendant is on the main progenitor branch of the target subhalo, while not being a member of the main progenitor branch itself. We refer to this dataset as the secondary progenitor list of the merger tree: the list of all subhaloes which merge directly with subhaloes along the main progenitor branch. For each subhalo, we compute the following merger quantities from its secondary progenitor list:

•

N_{\mu}
: Total number of mergers.

•

N_{\mu>\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}}}
: Total number of major mergers, defined hereafter as mergers whose mass ratio exceeds
\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}}
.

•

\mu_{\text{max}}
: Largest merger ratio in the subhalo’s history.

•

\mu_{\text{med}}
: Median merger ratio throughout the subhalo’s history.

•

\bar{z}
: Characteristic merger time, defined as the average redshift of all mergers weighted by their mass ratio.

•

z_{\mu_{\text{max}}}
: Redshift at which the merger with the largest mass ratio takes place.

•

z_{\mu>\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}}}
: Redshift at which the most recent major merger takes place; if none exist, equal to
z_{\mu_{\text{max}}}
.

3.1
Clustering Of Halo Properties

3.1.1
PCA and normalisation

Principal component analysis
(
Jolliffe 2002
)
is a linear data compression technique used to reduce the dimensionality of a dataset while preserving as much variability as possible. It works by identifying the principal components along which the data varies the most, i.e. the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix of the data, and the corresponding eigenvalues which indicate the amount of variance along each direction. principal component analysis projects the original data onto these principal components, resulting in a new set of uncorrelated variables ordered by the amount of variance they capture. The first few principal components typically capture the most significant patterns in the data, allowing for a more compact representation while minimising information loss.

In order to study the properties and histories of halos which can host massive quiescent galaxies, we apply principal component analysis to all
Thesan-1
galaxies with a final stellar mass above
10^{7}M_{\odot}
. This provides a sample of 35,963 haloes following the gradient quality cuts described in
section

2.2.3
, which eliminate a total of 467 samples. While this includes a large number of low-mass galaxies irrelevant to our final analysis, it allows the probabilistic clustering algorithm described in
section

3.1.2
to identify a physically meaningful group containing all massive quiescent galaxies in the simulation. This allows us to extract a predictive subset without directly imposing any threshold criteria during clustering.

The principal component analysis approach requires normalisation of the data, which cannot be done equivalently for all data due to extreme differences in their data distributions. In
Chittenden &
Tojeiro 2023
, most variables are normalised by Gaussian quantile transformation, where the distribution of each variable is mapped onto a normal distribution, regardless of its initial distribution. They show that the normal distribution was more suitable than a uniform distribution due to a steep gradient between the original and normalised data, which made it unsuitable for optimising a data model. In
Thesan
, we see this for normal distributions as well, and so we apply an alternative method to quantile transformation.

Aside from
\beta
gradients and formation times, halo and merger properties are strictly positive-valued, and their distributions often appear approximately Gaussian or Poissonian along a logarithmic axis. By fitting a straight line between the logarithmic value of a variable and its normalised percentile score in the distribution, each variable is translated to a distribution between 0 and 1 which resembles its true distribution in logarithmic space. We refer to this mode of normalisation as a logarithmic transformation.
\beta
gradients and formation times, on the other hand, follow basic distributions on linear axes, and so are only transformed by a minimum-maximum scaler.

3.1.2
Gaussian Mixture Models

To further analyse the principal component analysis distribution, we make use of a Gaussian mixture model to isolate the massive quiescent galaxy haloes as much as possible. Gaussian mixture models are probabilistic models used to represent a dataset as a mixture of several Gaussian distributions, each with its own mean, covariance, and weight. Gaussian mixture models assume that the data is generated from a combination of these Gaussian distributions, and the goal is to identify the parameters of each Gaussian which best explain the data.

The expectation-maximisation algorithm is commonly used to fit a Gaussian mixture model to the data. In the expectation step, the algorithm estimates the probability that each data point belongs to each Gaussian component, and in the maximisation step, it updates the parameters of the Gaussians to maximise the likelihood of the data given these assignments. This iterative process continues until the model converges, resulting in a set of Gaussian distributions which collectively describe the underlying structure of the data.

Gaussian mixture models are particularly useful for data clustering, as they can model complex, multimodal data distributions. In order to identify the halo properties specific to massive quiescent galaxies, we run a Gaussian mixture model fitting algorithm using the Gaussian mixture module provided by the SciKit-Learn Python library
(
Pedregosa
et al. 2011
)
.

For the sake of convergence, the Gaussian mixture model is trained on the seven largest principal components, constituting
\sim 62\%
of the variance in the 35-parameter dataset. As we are specifically interested in the data cluster
2
2

2

5
Discussion

5.1
Halo and environment

5.1.1
Important MQG properties

By investigating the properties of massive haloes in
Thesan
which host massive quiescent galaxies, we have identified key properties which can explain their unique modes of evolution. The Gaussian mixture model cluster containing all such haloes showcases the most massive haloes with early growth and structure assembly, and merger-poor formation histories. Within the cluster, massive quiescent galaxies are separated from star-forming galaxies of similar mass by faster assembly of their mass and central potential, leading to the assembly of higher stellar and active galactic nuclei masses, localised to within a smaller stellar radius. With observations of solitary massive quiescent galaxies pointing to these phenomena
(
Strait et al. 2023
;
Looser et al. 2024
;
Setton et al. 2024
;
Weibel et al. 2025
)
, such as the prominent 3 comoving megaparsecs overdensity of the galaxy RUBIES-EGS-QG-1
(
De Graaff
et al. 2024
)
, as well as resolved
JWST
data showing the termination of star formation propagating from the galactic centre
(
Wright et al. 2024
;
Lyu et al. 2025
)
, and rapid quenching of massive galaxies coinciding with a strong gas outflow
(
Belli et al. 2024
;
Wu 2025
)
, it is entirely plausible for these
Thesan
results to provide insights into possible the modelled star formation histories of these galaxies, while offering a physical interpretation of the mechanism behind said star formation.

Another aspect of massive quiescent galaxies in
Thesan
is the intriguing dependence of different quiescent populations on environmental densities measured on different scales. Quiescent galaxies which have experienced faster halo and substructure assembly have quenched earlier, and simultaneously, their environmental densities exceed those of recently quenched galaxies, even on large scales. These overdensities are not transient but have persisted throughout the lifetimes of these galaxies, suggesting that early structure formation in dense regions established a long-term environment conducive to accelerated growth and early quenching. As there is little difference between haloes and environments when comparing subgrid models, we argue that the inclusion of radiative transfer modelling in
Thesan
plays a critical role: it allows matter to accrete onto massive quiescent galaxies and fuel their rapid stellar and supermassive black hole growth by means of a radiation pressure gradient, owing to the surrounding ionising sources driving inflowing gas into the massive halo; while these ionising sources simultaneously heat the accreted gas, fuelling the active galactic nucleus and preventing rejuvenation.

While
IllustrisTNG
and
Thesan
employ identical black hole seeding criteria, the inclusion of radiation hydrodynamics in the latter alters thermal evolution and halo formation histories, which in turn affect the timing of black hole seeding and the available supply of cold gas for accretion. We find that even black holes in low-mass galaxies exhibit enhanced growth in
Thesan
, likely due to improved gas inflow conditions and better resolution of early accretion phases. Despite the general enhancement of supermassive black hole synthesis resulting from this inhomogeneous and self-consistent radiation background, the tight concentration of ionising sources in the environments of massive quiescent galaxies contribute significantly to galaxy-black hole coevolution.
Conaboy et al. 2025
show that the most massive haloes and dense environments in the
Sherwood-Relics
simulations harbour the strongest Ly
\alpha
transmission during reionisation, while
Neyer et al. 2024
show in
Thesan
that ionised bubbles preferentially form in overdense regions of space, which in
Flares
preferentially host the largest active galactic nucleus
(
Wilkins
et al. 2025
)
; potentially allowing galaxies within these large ionised structures to remain exposed to elevated radiation pressure for extended periods of time. These findings support a picture in which early-forming, overdense environments not only facilitate the rapid assembly of massive galaxies, but also modulate the thermodynamic conditions under which quenching unfolds.

5.1.2
Logistics of observational measurements

The formation of large scale structure at a cosmic age of only a few hundred megayears could produce the environments necessary for rapid accretion. The radial skews seen close to the end of reionisation would also suggest that these haloes will continue to grow, owing to the large haloes in close proximity providing a source of inflowing matter. Distant massive quiescent galaxies in the real universe may be found to reside in large filaments which provide such sources of matter. However, it is unclear how these environments may have evolved in the reionised universe, and therefore whether this relationship will be maintained at lower redshifts, where massive quiescent galaxies can be more easily investigated.

JWST
observations have spectroscopically confirmed massive quiescent galaxies as compact and early-forming, as is the case with these
Thesan
galaxies. However,
JWST
is less suitable for tracing the diffuse gas surrounding these galaxies which may trace their environments. While the telescope can detect internal kinematics at high redshift using rest-frame optical emission lines, the resolved velocity structure of high redshift galaxies is compromised by its instrumental resolution and the faintness of said emission lines, making it difficult to measure this halo potential via stellar velocity dispersion
(
Marshall et al. 2021
)
. Furthermore, the spatial resolution of
JWST
at these redshifts makes it challenging to resolve internal kinematics, particularly for compact massive quiescent galaxies
(
Nanayakkara
et al. 2022
)
. To fully verify the role of the halo potential in massive quiescent galaxies formation, future radio observations, such as
H
i
mapping with next-generation facilities such as SKA-Low, may be necessary to trace the extended cold gas reservoirs and environmental interactions which may inform the properties of these halo potentials
(
Bera
et al. 2023
)
.

5.2
Contrasts between simulations

5.2.1
MQG physics in
Thesan

With little difference between halo properties in each simulation, the key differences in subgrid physics can be attributed to the discrepancy between massive galaxy and active galactic nuclei evolution. Curiously, while cosmic reionisation serves to suppress and delay the formation of small progenitors, there is little difference between the stellar mass functions of separate runs of the
Thesan
suite, including that of the dark acoustic oscillations model which enhances this effect
(
Kannan et al. 2021
)
. Instead, the separate treatment of atomic and ionised hydrogen in the
Arepo-RT
code allows the explicit tracking of the ionisation of atomic hydrogen by UV radiation; which serves to heat the intergalactic medium and suppress the gas cooling needed for star formation, while creating a radiation-dense external environment whose radiation pressure gradient funnels the gas onto supermassive black holes. This effect may be most prominent at high redshifts and in dense environments, where the ionisation field is strongest
(
Neyer et al. 2024
)
, and black hole accretion rates become more sensitive to gas density and temperature.

While comparing two resolution runs of the fiducial
Thesan
model does not change the supermassive black hole growth,
Kannan et al. 2019
argue that modelling active galactic nuclei feedback accurately depends on small scale effects. However, as outlined in
section

2.1.2
, the
Arepo-RT
code relates radiation pressure and kinetic energy of ionisation fronts by means of the Eddington tensor: eliminating the dependence of calculations on the density of ionising sources; and invokes a subcycling algorithm, resampling radiative transfer multiple times for each hydrodynamical iteration. These algorithms allow for precise modelling of radiation field propagation and its interactions with gas and dust grains, which are crucial aspects of active galactic nuclei feedback.

In this context, the inclusion of on-the-fly radiative transfer in
Thesan
adds essential realism to active galactic nucleus feedback by evolving the local radiation field alongside gas dynamics. Unlike
IllustrisTNG
, which applies a uniform UV background,
Thesan
models highly anisotropic radiation from local sources, including the active galactic nucleus itself. This leads to non-uniform ionisation and temperature structures, altering how feedback couples to the surrounding gas. In the process of supermassive black hole growth, the resulting self-shielding may enable cold gas to persist in halo centres, fuelling more efficient black hole accretion. As a result, active galactic nuclei in
Thesan
reach significantly higher masses than in
IllustrisTNG
despite using the same subgrid model, highlighting how radiative context alone can drive divergent evolutionary outcomes. This enhancement in supermassive black hole mass is of course exacerbated by the highly concentrated and radiation-dense environments surrounding massive quiescent galaxies in
Thesan
, where overlapping ionisation fronts and intense local sources demand a fully self-consistent radiative transfer formulism to capture the dynamics of infalling gas.

5.2.2
Logistics of AGN feedback

In contrast to the radiative transfer model in
Thesan
, recent work by
Farcy et al. 2025
demonstrates, using the
Mistral
active galactic nucleus feedback model, that radiatively efficient active galactic nucleus winds, particularly those modelled by stochastic momentum injection, profoundly influence the properties of galaxies at
z=2
and introduces more massive quiescent galaxies than the
IllustrisTNG
feedback model at
z>3
(Marion Farcy, private communication); highlighting the importance of feedback support on large spatial scales. These two different approaches to modelling active galactic nucleus quenching separately produce results such as compact star formation shutdown and sustained quiescence. It may therefore transpire that despite the lack of explicit momentum injection in
Thesan
, the pressure supported active galactic nucleus winds seen in
Mistral
could influence the process of quenching similarly to the radiative coupling effects present in
Arepo-RT
; a striking parallel given that neither model was intended to replicate the results of the other.

However, it is important to stress that there are key differences between the properties of these active galactic nuclei: the enhanced growth of supermassive black holes is not seen in the stochastic
Mistral
model, which does not explicitly model environmental accretion and ionisation, or other physical processes which govern these pressure supported outflows. Additionally, the gas flows in each of these models exhibit different geometries, being isotropic in
Thesan
and collimated in
Mistral
; and unlike
Thesan
, the radiatively efficient winds in
Mistral
serve to shut off the accreting gas, which may lead to an entirely different relationship with the halo’s environment. While the extent to which radiation pressure acts on large scales in
Thesan
remains uncertain, our results suggest that radiation feedback modifies the local gas environment in a way that enables more efficient black hole growth and earlier triggering of kinetic mode active galactic nucleus feedback; a process that may bear similarity to the role of momentum injection in
Mistral
.

We find that the quenching of massive galaxies in
Thesan
is coupled to a sharp decline in Eddington ratio by almost two orders of magnitude, over the same short timescales in which star formation rapidly drops. This suggests that the inclusion of radiation feedback does not merely modify gas thermodynamics, but may also suppress supermassive black hole accretion rates in a manner which replicates a transition to a low-accretion regime, as seen for active galactic nucleus-dominated massive quiescent galaxies in
IllustrisTNG

(
Kurinchi-Vendhan et al. 2024
)
. However, this feedback transition is preceded by a prolonged phase of sustained accretion, during which quiescent galaxies maintain consistently high Eddington ratios
(\lambda_{\text{Edd}}\sim 0.1)
and grow more massive black holes than their star-forming counterparts, who instead exhibit lower but gradually rising accretion rates. This supports the interpretation that radiation feedback in
Thesan
delays the onset of efficient coupling, enabling uninterrupted supermassive black hole growth over cosmic time until a critical threshold is reached. This can also be reconciled with the long-standing dense environments and rapidly growing halo potentials which sustain the most massive supermassive black holes for most of the galaxies’ existence.

This may imply that active galactic nucleus feedback in the low Eddington regime is indeed pivotal for the quenching process; yet in the radiation-dense environments seen in
Thesan
massive quiescent galaxies, stellar and active galactic nucleus feedback remain closely intertwined
(
Shen et al. 2024b
)
. Supernova feedback may not make a noticeable impact on massive quiescent galaxies alone, yet it may serve a secondary role in regulating active galactic nucleus winds in
Thesan
, such as enhancing outflows which remove residual gas after quenching. While these modes of feedback may be challenging to distinguish, the rapid transition to a kinetic feedback regime signifies that the active galactic nucleus ultimately quenches the galaxy through momentum injection, which in
Thesan
arises from the self-consistent coupling of active galactic nucleus and stellar radiation with the interstellar medium.

5.2.3
Future simulation studies

The congruence of halo properties between simulations is encouraging for future work, where these rare haloes can be investigated in N-body simulations. The scarcity of massive quiescent galaxies in most cosmic simulations fails to adhere to the observed number density of such galaxies, which may be compensated by the reionisation-driven quenching illustrated in this work. Additionally, the small quantity of massive quiescent galaxies implies little diversity of evolutionary trajectories, which makes it difficult to draw conclusions about their evolutionary physics. However, the strong correspondence between halo and galaxy properties may enable the use of machine learning to predict the population statistics of massive quiescent galaxies across the reionisation epoch. In addition, identifying massive quiescent galaxy hosts in N-body simulations may be selected for future zoom simulations, where alternative reionisation physics may be tested.

With this projected continuation of growth past the epoch of reionisation, it will be intriguing to consider their continued evolution, providing analogues to the quiescent galaxies observed from
z=3
to
z=5
. In
Magneticum
, there exists a significant population of quenched galaxies at
z=3.4
, albeit, with quenching redshifts subsequent to reionisation. Similarly to our findings, the haloes hosting massive quiescent galaxies are massive and fast-growing, and there is a strong correlation between active galactic nuclei and star formation activity of massive galaxies. It is important to note, however, that
Magneticum
differs from other simulations in that its subgrid parameters were not tuned to reproduce observed stellar mass functions or galaxy quenching trends, but rather to match the hot gas content in galaxy clusters, which it does successfully
(
Popesso
et al. 2025
)
. As such, its stellar properties, including the number density of quenched galaxies, emerge more self-consistently, albeit with subtle overprediction of quenching at lower redshifts
(
Lustig
et al. 2023
;
Dolag
et al. 2025
;
Lagos et al. 2025
)
. Conversely,
Kimmig et al. 2025
find an excess of massive haloes which do not host massive quiescents, while
Remus &
Kimmig 2025
show some of these galaxies to reside in underdense, sometimes isolated regions of space
4
4

4

It is important to note that while
Kimmig et al. 2025
claim small-scale environments to be underdense for massive quiescents, they quote relative density contrasts within a narrow stellar mass bin, which is less meaningful in
Thesan
due to fewer high mass galaxies. We do see a rough underdense contrast when applying these authors’ calculations, but this is not statistically significant.
. These authors argue that this discrepancy is similarly seen when comparing quenched galaxies in
Magneticum
and
IllustrisTNG
, and attribute the different halo and environmental properties to different conditions for active galactic nuclei feedback. It is therefore plausible for an as-yet unseen environmental dependence at reionisation redshifts to arise from radiative transfer simulations which employ different active galactic nuclei feedback models.

5.3
Observational analogues

5.3.1
Multifaceted JWST data

Confirming the validity of the current assortment of simulation models with observations may prove challenging. For instance, there is limited observational evidence favouring either of the environmental dependences of massive quiescent galaxies seen in
Magneticum
and
Thesan
. On the one hand,
Jin et al. 2024
showcase a set of massive galaxies lying along a cosmic filament at
z\simeq 3.44
, where the massive quiescent galaxies are the most massive of all and reside in the densest environments, which
Ito et al. 2025
claim are in the process of merging post-quenching. Similarly,
Turner et al. 2025
demonstrate that the
Glazebrook
et al. 2024
galaxy resides in a tightly clustered environment at
z\simeq 3.2
; akin to the concentrated environments we have seen in
Thesan
. However, based on recent
VANDELS
data,
Espinoza Ortiz
et al. 2024
suggest that environmental influences on massive quiescent galaxies extend beyond density-driven gas and supermassive black hole accretion, with large-scale structures also inducing mergers and ram pressure stripping that can suppress growth and alter observed environmental trends. The structural diversity seen in observational catalogues would thereby indicate that massive quiescent galaxies may follow multiple evolutionary pathways.

While this work provides valuable insights into the hidden population of rapidly evolving primordial galaxies, it is important to stress that the complete diversity of evolutionary pathways of massive quiescent galaxies is not captured by this paper. While the
Thesan
galaxies exhibit similar ages, masses and formation times to those seen in
Carnall
et al. 2023a
;
Nanayakkara
et al. 2025
, many have quenched over longer timescales, and after the end of reionisation. These observed galaxies also exhibit key differences in their formation histories, such as significant merger activity leading to extended disk profiles (Kawinwanichakij et al. in prep.).
Ito et al. 2025
suggest that the merger-driven growth of massive quiescent galaxies can take place after quenching, which could explain the diversity of morphologies and environments seen in massive quiescent galaxies at lower redshifts. While merger activity may indeed be important for morphological transformation and ex-situ stellar mass growth, it is shown by
Nipoti 2025
that mergers alone have a negligible influence on central stellar velocity dispersion, which would suggest that they do not influence the supermassive black hole-hosting potential enabling rapid supermassive black hole growth and quenching. Instead,
Nipoti 2025
shows that it is envelope accretion, arising from the disruption of diffuse satellites by low-mass progenitors
(\mu<\nicefrac{{1}}{{10}})
, which contribute significantly to the growth in galaxy size and black hole mass. The finding that the central potential is primarily influenced by small progenitors agrees with the lack of a merger signal seen in our massive quiescent galaxy population, and may explain how massive quiescent galaxies can develop in lieu of merger-driven quenching.

Overall, this multeity of massive galaxy quenching pathways is difficult to reconcile with simulations, in light of limited sample sizes and disparities between subgrid models
(
Lagos et al. 2025
)
. Observations of lower redshift massive quiescent galaxies, such as those in the SSA22 protocluster at
z=3.09

(
Umehata
et al. 2025
)
, suggest alternative quenching mechanisms to the radiation pressure gradient scenario we propose; in this case, the halo is said to retain its heat from its X-Ray active galactic nuclei, shutting off filamentary gas accretion rather than allowing the halo to grow. At
z=7.276
, however,
Valentino
et al. 2025
report the presence of an outflow with a substantial mass loading factor in the galaxy RUBIES-UDS-QG-z7, which the authors attribute to the existence of an undetected active galactic nucleus. This may signify the rapid expulsion of star-forming gas: a ubiquitous feature of
Thesan
massive quiescent galaxies.

5.3.2
Need for further modelling

The
Thesan
data shows that the earliest-forming massive quiescent galaxies are highly dependent on our implementation of reionisation-era physics, but these results show only a small fraction of the massive quiescent galaxy population in the real universe. Given the amount of time after reionisation, there is a lot to be uncovered regarding the post-quenching timeline of these early massive quiescent galaxies, and the accumulation of later-quenching galaxies through various mechanisms. The reionisation model is crucial for their initial formation, but in order to understand the complete picture of massive quiescent galaxy population statistics, the simulation must be extended past the epoch of reionisation, down to redshifts of
z\sim 3
, where the sample size of modelled galaxies should align with the growing anthology of
JWST
studies.

Furthermore, these simulations still do not contain analogues for truly outlying observations, such as the massive galaxy with a formation redshift of
z\simeq 11

(
Glazebrook
et al. 2024
)
. It may transpire that with the extended dark matter power spectrum offered by large N-body simulations, we may capture primordial haloes which can host similar galaxies, and we may make projected estimates of the abundance of these exceptionally old massive quiescent galaxies. Alternatively, it may prove that another reionisation model is necessary.
Kannan et al. 2021
show that the stellar mass function of
Thesan
is noticeably lower in amplitude than that of the small-scale, high-resolution
Sphinx
simulation
(
Rosdahl
et al. 2018
)
, out to
z\sim 9
. These authors show themselves that the onset of reionisation is sensitive to the inclusion of binary stars as ionisation sources; yet the
Ramses-RT
code underpinning
Sphinx

(
Rosdahl et al. 2013
)
employs an Eulerian gas model, in contrast with the Lagrangian moving mesh code
Arepo-RT
. In the original versions of these codes, the higher densities achieved in
Arepo
’s collapsing gas clouds lead to burstier star formation and the formation of more massive star clusters, leading to the formation of large supernova bubbles and stronger galactic outflows
(
Hu et al. 2023
)
. While the authors behind the aforementioned reionisation simulations claim that they can accurately model observational statistics at high redshift, it may prove that the number density, star formation history, structure, morphology and other aspects of extreme massive quiescent galaxies are dependent on how we model reionisation, and so they may hold crucial information to understanding this elusive phase in the universe’s history.

5.3.3
Enhanced star formation?

While discoveries of high-redshift massive quiescent galaxies have theorised an enhanced star formation efficiency during the epoch of reionisation, it has been shown by
Donnan et al. 2025
that recent
JWST
data contains no evidence of this; in fact, their optimal fits to the galaxy stellar mass and UV luminosity functions from
z=6
to
z=13
are based on a redshift-independent formula for star formation efficiency. However, as both
Kannan et al. 2021
and
Donnan et al. 2025
utilise pre-
JWST
data from
z=6
to
z=8
provided by
Stefanon et al. 2021
, it can be seen that
Donnan et al. 2025
provide a better fit to this observed stellar mass function, and at
z=8
is well fit to
JWST
-era results; but at
z=6-7
, their predicted stellar mass function is higher than those derived from
JWST
-era data
(
Harvey et al. 2024
;
Shuntov
et al. 2025
;
Weibel et al. 2025
)
, while
Thesan
’s result is better fit to the more recent observations.

As
Donnan et al. 2025
state that their model was optimised for predicting UV luminosity functions at
z\geq 9
, it is feasible that the decline in stellar mass originating from processes in more developed galaxies, such as outflows, which cannot be captured by a star formation efficiency model alone, play a significant role in shaping the galaxy population towards the end of reionisation. Nevertheless, this study implies that any modification to the physics of star formation and hydrogen reionisation are likely infeasible, which suggests the necessity for an alternative explanation for the unique evolution of massive quiescent galaxies; potentially the radiation-driven gas accretion in dense environments which we postulate as imperative for rapid halo and supermassive black hole mass accretion in this paper. However, this study makes core assumptions about the halo mass function and initial stellar mass function, which exhibit a particularly strong time dependence at this epoch, and may prove unsuitable for modelling the growth of the earliest galaxies. Moreover, the potential for a great revelation in our consensus on high redshift galaxy evolution, such as the notion of black hole growth predating rapid star formation
(
Silk
et al. 2024
;
D’Silva
et al. 2025
)
, would warrant a revision to our models which challenges the fundamental physics of the early universe.

6
Conclusions

In this work, we investigate the unique historical properties of massive quiescent galaxies (MQGs) and their host haloes and environments in the
Thesan
cosmohydrodynamical simulation, whose thorough modelling of the epoch of reionisation produces a population of galaxies which exceed a stellar mass of
10^{10}M_{\odot}
and rapidly quench before
z=5.5
: a feat which simulations without this reionisation model fail to replicate. By running a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) clustering algorithm on the principal component space encoding the key properties of the haloes’ evolutionary histories, we were able to discern the key properties of haloes and environments hosting massive quiescent galaxies. By separating the massive quiescent galaxy population according to their quenching time, we can determine how halo, environmental and galaxy properties develop for these galaxies over the course of quenching.

Our findings can be summarised as follows:

•

As illustrated in
section

4.1
, the smallest possible Gaussian mixture model cluster, enclosing all massive quiescent galaxies, constitutes 90 haloes with the largest masses, radii, densities over a 200 comoving kiloparsec aperture, and virial velocities as a proxy for the potential depth of the haloes; where we see in
section

4.2
that all of these quantities grow faster for massive quiescent galaxies than their star-forming counterparts. Consequently, the quiescent galaxies are the most massive and compact in terms of their stellar component, and have some of the most massive active galactic nuclei in the simulation: maintained by the deep potential wells of these haloes and believed to be primarily responsible for galaxy quenching, while mergers play no significant role in either halo or galaxy evolution. Despite this, the active galactic nucleus mass is not the sole property of these galaxies which advocates quenching; this also owes to proportionally smaller gas reservoirs and concentrations of stellar mass.

•

We discuss in
sections

4.2
and
5.2
that this relationship between halo sizes, small scale environment and star formation status is similar to that of the quiescent galaxies found at lower redshifts in the
IllustrisTNG
simulations; but distinct from the smaller haloes in underdense regions illustrated in the
Magneticum Pathfinder
model. With
Thesan
being closely related to the former, it suggests that the established effect of different supermassive black hole accretion models, creating different population statistics for massive galaxies, remains valid in the reionisation epoch; while the radiative transfer code remains essential for synthesising the early-quenching galaxies in either scenario.

•

Haloes and large scale environments which harbour massive quiescent galaxies continue to grow after quenching. In
section

4.2
, we show a clear hierarchy of halo mass, accretion gradient and small scale environment in relation to quenching time, indicating that faster-growing haloes host massive galaxies which quench earlier. On 1 comoving megaparsec scales, environmental densities around recently quenched galaxies (RQGs) are no different from massive star-forming galaxies, while there remains an enhancement for long quiescent galaxies (LQGs). On scales of 3 comoving megaparsecs, long quiescent galaxies also lose this enhancement. When tracking the distribution of neighbours over time using the radial skew parameter introduced in
Chittenden &
Tojeiro 2023
, we see the gravitation of large objects towards the target haloes, which are likely the source of accretion of matter which feeds the rapidly growing haloes, galaxies and active galactic nuclei.

As mentioned above, a crucial factor in the quenching process of massive galaxies is the active galactic nucleus mass, which are among the largest in the simulation for our massive quiescent galaxy population. We show in

in modelling the quenching of massive galaxies at high redshift. Notably, the transition to a low Eddington ratio feedback regime coincides with the onset of quenching, highlighting the importance of efficient feedback coupling to the interstellar and circumgalactic media. As little difference is seen between these simulations in terms of halo features, we assert that the rapid accumulation of matter by massive quiescent galaxies in
Thesan

We stress in
section

5.1
that the relationship between the growth of large scale environments and the quenching of massive galaxies implies that the accretion of neighbouring haloes around massive quiescent-hosting haloes will continue after the end of reionisation, and so the quiescent galaxies detected from
z=3
to
z=5
may be found in some of the densest large scale environments at these redshifts; potentially corroborated by examples given in
section

5.3
. It would appear that while these galaxies remain quiescent, their haloes consume their surrounding neighbours and accumulate mass, while the central active galactic nuclei prevents further star formation; a possible pathway for massive quiescent galaxies to become isolated while preserving their quiescent state throughout cosmic history. Having established that mergers are not a significant component of massive quiescent galaxy evolution, they will not be rejuvenated by the infall of smaller galaxies; which are likely to be stripped of their star-forming gas by ram pressure from the massive halo, further suppressing star formation. However, some observed quiescent galaxies exhibit potential evidence of past mergers, which could influence the morphology of these galaxies post-quenching.

The unprecedented discovery of numerous massive quiescent galaxies in
JWST
data provides a crucial opportunity to test and refine reionisation simulations, potentially establishing a paradigm for their evolutionary histories while constraining the enigmatic physics of this key epoch. The addition of reionisation-era quenching mechanisms could help resolve discrepancies between observed and simulated galaxy populations, yet the limited sample of simulated galaxies poses challenges for investigating their complex nature. Key questions remain unanswered, such as how some massive galaxies sustain star formation despite hosting massive active galactic nuclei or residing in concentrated environments; conditions we associate with quiescent systems. Moreover, the recently identified
JWST
galaxies with even earlier formation times remain unaccounted for in current models. Identifying significant halos and environments in simulations could be pivotal for targeting regions in future deep-field surveys, offering deeper insights into these early galaxy populations.

While these galaxies are exceptionally rare in simulations, and therefore difficult to reconcile with the increasing number of detections in deep surveys, they may be replicated in a greater abundance by means of N-body simulation data. N-body pure dark matter simulations are significantly less limited in size and resolution due to the abscence of computationally expensive baryonic modelling. Thus, after recognising the halo properties associated with massive quiescent galaxies, the population of massive quiescent galaxies with which to work can be extended by applying a machine learning model to N-body simulation haloes with similar masses, morphologies and environments.

Future work may entail the study of massive haloes in high volume N-body simulations such as
Uchuu

(
Ishiyama
et al. 2021
)
, where the combination of key properties outlined in our analysis may be used to predict the demographics of quiescent galaxies on a larger scale, and provide mock observations to complement the ongoing search for these galaxies. However, we have only tested one such simulation model, and future results may prove an extension of the
Thesan
statistics to be inaccurate. This necessitates extensive modelling of massive quiescent galaxies in the reionisation epoch, to truly fathom the scale and diversity of primordial galaxy evolution, and better comprehend the role of reionisation in the foundation of cosmic structures.

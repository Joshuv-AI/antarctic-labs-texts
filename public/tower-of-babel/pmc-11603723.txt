Knowledge, attitude and practice of urticaria patients towards urticaria medication treatment: a cross-sectional study at the first affiliated hospital of Kunming Medical University
Authors: Yan Shen, Weijia Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo explore the knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) of urticaria patients regarding the medication treatment.Methods designA cross-sectional study.SettingAt the Dermatology Department of the First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University from 15 June 2023 to 12 March 2024.ParticipantsUrticaria patients.Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe primary outcome was KAP of urticaria patients using a self-administered questionnaire. The secondary outcomes were factors associated with KAP.ResultsA total of 497 valid responses were collected. Of these respondents, 333 (67.0%) were female, and 375 (75.5%) lived in urban areas. The median KAP scores were 1.00 [0.00, 3.00] (possible range: 0–8), 23.00 [18.00, 26.00] (possible range: 10–50) and 32.00 [31.00, 36.00] (possible range: 8–40), respectively. Most patients (91.7%) clearly needed to understand the aetiology and treatment process related to urticaria. Less than half (43.5%) of patients believed that their healthcare providers offered comprehensive information about the knowledge and procedures related to urticaria medication. Demographic analysis uncovered substantial variations in knowledge and attitude scores across different education levels, age groups, occupations and residential areas (p<0.001 for all). In contrast, the impact on practice scores was less significant, particularly concerning educational diversity. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that a higher knowledge score (OR=1.17, 95% CI: [1.03 to 1.33], p=0.014) and age between 35–65 years (OR=1.78, 95% CI: [1.16 to 2.74], p=0.009) were independently associated with proactive practices.ConclusionUrticaria patients exhibit insufficient knowledge, unfavourable attitudes and proactive practices concerning medication treatment. Enhancing patient education and targeted interventions are essential to improve the knowledge and attitudes towards medication treatment, ultimately promoting better self-management practices among urticaria patients.

Background

Urticaria is a condition characterised by the development of wheals (hives), angioedema or both, including chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU) and inducible urticaria. It is a common skin disease marked by wheals, itching and/or angioedema, with a lifetime prevalence of approximately 20% for any subtype of urticaria.1

Epidemiological surveys indicate that between 1990 and 2019, although the prevalence rate of urticaria in China decreased from 759.20 per 100 000 to 677.48 per 100,000, the absolute number of urticaria patients increased from 8.99 million to 9.64 million.2 Chronic urticaria significantly compromises the quality of life for patients due to its debilitating symptoms that can persist for years.3 4 Furthermore, urticaria not only impacts patients'quality of life but also results in a higher incidence of anxiety and depression compared with the general population.5

The therapeutic approach for urticaria involves identifying and, if possible, eliminating underlying causes, avoiding triggering factors, inducing tolerance and using pharmacological treatments to prevent the release and effects of mast cell mediators.6 Since urticaria is predominantly mediated by histamine release, H1 antihistamines are the cornerstones of treatment.7 The key to effective therapy is good adherence and compliance by the patient.

The knowledge, attitude and practice (KAP) theory plays a pivotal role in shaping human health behaviours.8 It is often employed alongside the KAP questionnaire to comprehensively gauge the KAP of the target population within the healthcare domain, as well as to assess the demand and level of acceptance of relevant content.9 This model, integral to health literacy, is underpinned by the fundamental premise that knowledge exerts a positive influence on attitudes, and these attitudes, in turn, shape individual practices.10

Understanding gaps in KAP has significant clinical implications, as it can inform targeted educational interventions to improve patient outcomes. Chronic urticaria severely impacts the quality of life and is associated with higher rates of anxiety and depression. Focusing on this group is particularly important due to the chronic nature of the disease, which requires long-term management and patient adherence to treatment. By enhancing KAP, healthcare providers can better support urticaria patients, ultimately leading to more effective disease control and improved quality of life. Despite the importance of evaluating patients' KAP, there is currently no KAP research specifically targeting the Chinese population with urticaria. Therefore, this study aimed to explore the KAP of urticaria patients regarding medication treatment.

Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted from 15 June 2023 to 12 March 2024 in the Dermatology Department of the First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University, targeting patients with chronic urticaria. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University, and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) meeting the diagnostic criteria for chronic urticaria according to the Chinese Urticaria Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines (2022 Edition); (2) age ≥18 years; (3) possessing independent cognitive and analytical abilities; (4) having received some education and being able to communicate, interact and learn; and (5) providing informed consent. Exclusion criteria included (1) patients with acute urticaria and (2) individuals with mental or cognitive impairments that prevented them from completing the survey.

The questionnaire was administered face-to-face to the study subjects in the dermatology outpatient and inpatient departments.

Questionnaire introduction

The questionnaire design was based on relevant guidelines and literature. Following its initial design, feedback from three experts—Professor Xiaolan Li (expert in dermatology and venereology), Professor Liping He (expert in epidemiology and biostatistics) and Professor Yiqun Kuang (expert in infection and immunology)—was solicited for modifications. The initial draft of the questionnaire was then pilot tested with 30 respondents. The feedback resulted in a total Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.875, with the knowledge dimension at 0.647, the attitude dimension at 0.894 and the practice dimension at 0.830.

The final questionnaire comprised four sections: demographic information (educational level, gender, occupation type, residence), knowledge dimension, attitude dimension and practice dimension. Detailed information on the questionnaire can be found in online supplemental file 1.

The knowledge dimension included eight questions covering disease knowledge of urticaria and knowledge of urticaria medication treatment. Each correct answer was scored 1 point, while incorrect or unclear answers were scored 0 points, with a total score range of 0–8 points. The attitude dimension included ten questions, using a five-point Likert scale ranging from very positive (5 points) to very negative (1 point), with a total score range of 10–50 points. The practice dimension included eight questions, also using a five-point Likert scale ranging from always (5 points) to never (1 point), with a total score range of 8–40 points. Scores above 70% of the maximum in each section indicated adequate knowledge, a positive attitude and proactive practice.11

Sample size calculation

The sample size for this study was calculated as follows:

n = (Z_(1 - α/2) / δ)2×p × (1- p)

where n denotes the sample size and p was assumed to be 0.5 to ensure the maximum sample size. The type I error (α) was set to 0.05, yielding Z_(1 - α/2) = 1.96. The standard error (δ) was assumed to be 0.05. Assuming an effective questionnaire recovery rate of 90%, the final target was to collect at least 430 completed questionnaires.

Statistical analysis

A descriptive analysis of demographic data and dimension scores was conducted. Initially, the distribution of scores in each dimension was tested for normality. Normally distributed data were represented by means and SD, while non-normally distributed data were described using medians and IQRs (25th and 75th percentiles). Categorical data, including respondents' answers to various questions, were presented as counts and percentages (n, %). Comparison of scores among respondents with different demographic characteristics was performed as follows: for continuous variables, normally distributed data were compared between two groups using the t-test, whereas non-normally distributed data were compared using the Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney test. For comparisons involving three or more groups, analysis of variance was used for normally distributed variables with equal variances, and the Kruskal–Wallis test was employed for non-normally distributed data. Single-factor and multi-factor regression analyses were conducted with the scores of each dimension as dependent variables to analyse the relationship between demographic data and dimension scores. If the scores followed a normal distribution, mean scores were used for classification; otherwise, median scores were used. Variables with p<0.1 in single-factor regression analysis were included in the multi-factor regression analysis. p<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Patient and public involvement

No patients were involved.

Results

A total of 497 valid responses were collected. In the formal experiment, the internal consistency of the overall questionnaire and its components was satisfactory, as indicated by a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.825 for the entire questionnaire, and 0.749, 0.891 and 0.846 for the KAP components, respectively. The KMO value for the total KAP scale was 0.904. Of the 497 urticaria patients who participated in this study, 333 (67.0%) were female, 273 (54.9%) had received education at the junior college level or below and only 24 (4.8%) were above the age of 65; the remaining participants were aged between 18 and 65 years. Additionally, 375 (75.5%) resided in urban areas. The median KAP scores were 1.00 [0.00, 3.00] (possible range: 0–8), 23.00 [18.00, 26.00] (possible range: 10–50) and 32.00 [31.00, 36.00] (possible range: 8–40), respectively. Analysis of demographic characteristics revealed significant differences in knowledge scores among patients with varying levels of education, age, occupation type and residence (p<0.001 for all). Similarly, significant differences in attitude scores were observed across different demographic groups (p<0.001 for all). However, the differences in practice scores were less pronounced across educational backgrounds (p=0.09) (table 1).

Attitude dimension of the participants

Responses to the practice dimension indicated that a significant proportion of patients were inconsistent in their behaviours. Specifically, 32.2% did not always maintain a low-histamine diet, with 4.2% unwilling to adhere to it (P2). Additionally, 18.9% did not consistently follow-up on time, with a small minority not participating in follow-up visits at all (P3). Furthermore, 35.2% reported difficulty in consistently avoiding scratching itchy areas of urticaria, and 4.6% were incapable of avoiding it entirely (P8) (table 4).

Discussion

Urticaria patients exhibit insufficient knowledge and unfavourable attitudes but demonstrate proactive practices regarding medication treatment, significantly influenced by educational level, age, occupation type and place of residence. Healthcare providers should prioritise educational interventions and targeted support to improve patients’ knowledge and attitudes towards medication treatment, particularly focusing on older adults and individuals with lower educational levels.

The results indicate that urticaria patients generally exhibit insufficient knowledge and unfavourable attitudes but engage in proactive practices concerning medication treatment. Educational level significantly impacts KAP scores, with participants holding a master’s degree or above scoring the highest in all three dimensions. Specifically, those with higher education demonstrated better KAP. This finding is consistent with previous research suggesting that higher educational attainment enhances health literacy, leading to better disease understanding and management.12 13 Additionally, the multivariate logistic regression analysis supports this, indicating that higher knowledge scores are independently associated with proactive practice behaviours. Educated individuals are more likely to access, comprehend and use health information effectively, resulting in improved health behaviours.14

Age also influences knowledge and attitudes, with younger participants (18–35 years) demonstrating superior scores in knowledge and attitudes compared with older age groups. This pattern has been observed in other studies, where younger individuals tend to be more receptive to new information and adaptive in their health-related attitudes.15 However, multivariate logistic regression reveals that participants aged 35–65 years are more likely to exhibit proactive practice behaviours. This suggests that middle-aged individuals, despite having lower knowledge and attitudes scores, might rely on their life experience and possibly better access to healthcare resources to engage in proactive practices.16 17

Occupation type and residence also show significant disparities in knowledge and attitudes. Public servants and urban residents exhibited notably higher knowledge and attitudes scores than their counterparts. These findings are supported by literature indicating that urban dwellers and individuals in stable, often higher-status occupations have better access to healthcare information and services.18 Public servants might benefit from workplace health promotion programmes and policies that emphasise disease management and preventive practices.19

The knowledge dimension results indicate that a substantial number of participants lack essential information about urticaria. Most notably, there are low awareness levels regarding the role of mast cells in urticaria pathogenesis, the potential of NSAIDs to cause urticaria and the excretion of antihistamine drugs through breast milk. These gaps in knowledge suggest a significant need for improved education among urticaria patients. Similar studies have found that patient education plays a critical role in disease management and can significantly improve outcomes.20 21 To address these gaps, targeted educational interventions are essential. For example, informational sessions conducted via popular social media platforms like WeChat and Douyin could effectively disseminate accurate information about urticaria. Additionally, developing easy-to-understand pamphlets and interactive online modules could help bridge these knowledge gaps.22 23

The attitude dimension results reveal that while participants recognise the importance of understanding urticaria, they often feel inadequately informed by healthcare professionals. There is a notable lack of confidence in understanding how to treat various forms of urticaria, differentiate between urticaria and urticarial vasculitis and manage the side effects of medications. To improve attitudes towards urticaria treatment, healthcare providers should enhance their communication strategies. For example, incorporating detailed consultations and follow-up discussions can ensure patients receive and understand crucial information. Using WeChat for regular updates and Q&A sessions with healthcare professionals can also provide continuous support and clarification for patients.24 25

The practice dimension results show that participants generally demonstrate proactive behaviours regarding their treatment and lifestyle adjustments. However, there is still room for improvement, particularly in consistently maintaining a low-histamine diet and avoiding scratching itchy areas, where compliance is not as high. It is important to note that a low-histamine diet is not a standard requirement for all CSU patients but may serve as a complementary approach to identify individuals who could benefit from reduced histamine intake. As such, it is not a general therapeutic recommendation. The lower adherence to this practice may reflect patients’ perceptions of its limited usefulness for their condition, which is consistent with current guidance. To enhance these practices, healthcare providers could implement regular reminders and motivational messages through popular platforms like WeChat and Douyin. Creating community support groups on these platforms can also encourage peer support and shared experiences, fostering better adherence to recommended practices.26 27 Additionally, offering workshops or webinars on effective dietary management and stress-relief techniques could provide practical guidance and encourage sustained behavioural changes.28 29

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional design limits the ability to establish causality between the identified factors and KAP responses. Second, the self-administered questionnaire may introduce both response and recall bias, as participants might overestimate or underestimate their KAP. To mitigate these biases, assistants provided explanations to ensure clear understanding of the questions, and standardised procedures were used to avoid influencing responses. Third, the study was conducted at a single hospital, which may limit the generalisability of the findings to other settings or populations. Additionally, cultural factors, including traditional perceptions of Western medicine, may have influenced responses. Future studies should consider multi-centre designs and diverse populations to enhance the study’s generalisability and explore the impact of cultural perspectives on health behaviours.

In conclusion, urticaria patients exhibit insufficient knowledge and unfavourable attitudes but demonstrate proactive practices concerning medication treatment. Enhancing patient education and fostering positive attitudes towards medication adherence are crucial for improving the overall management and treatment outcomes of urticaria. There remains an unmet need for targeted education and training of patients to achieve better treatment success. Addressing this gap in patient education could significantly enhance adherence and efficacy in urticaria management.

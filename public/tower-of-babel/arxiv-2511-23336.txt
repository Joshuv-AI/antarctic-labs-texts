Two energy methods for distributed port-Hamiltonian systems and their application to stability analysis
Hannes Gernandt, Marco Roschkowski

Abstract
We develop two local energy methods for distributed parameter port-Hamiltonian (pH) systems on one-dimensional spatial domains. The methods are applied to derive a characterization of exponential stability directly in terms of the energy passing through the boundary over a given time horizon. The resulting condition is verified for a network of vibrating strings where existing sufficient conditions cannot be applied. Moreover, we use a local energy method to study the short-time behavior of pH systems with boundary damping which was recently studied in the context of hypocoercivity.



Keywords:
distributed parameter systems, port-Hamiltonian systems, energy methods, stability, hypocoercivity, vibrating strings, networks

1
Introduction

Port-Hamiltonian (pH) systems extend the Hamiltonian formalism by dissipation and interactions with an environment via ports, see
23
;
16
;
18
. The pH modeling approach has been extended to infinite-dimensional systems which are often called distributed parameter pH systems
13
;
20
;
4
and leads to systems in the Hilbert space
L^{2}([a,b],\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the form

\tfrac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}x(t,\zeta)=\left(\bm{P_{1}}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}+\bm{P}_{0}\right)(\bm{H}(\zeta)x(t,\zeta)),\ x(0,\zeta)=x_{0}(\zeta),\ (t,\zeta)\in[0,\infty)\times[a,b],

where the function
x:[0,\infty)\times[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the state,
x_{0}\in L^{2}([a,b],\mathbb{R}^{n})
the initial condition,
\bm{H}:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
the Hamiltonian density.

The stability
of (
1
) is well-understood and often treated using a semigroup approach. To this end, one views (
1
) as an abstract Cauchy problem

\dot{x}=\bm{A}x,\quad\bm{A}:=\left(\bm{P_{1}}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}+\bm{P}_{0}\right)\bm{H},

on some dense domain
D(\bm{A})\subseteq L^{2}([a,b],\mathbb{R}^{n})
which can be described by imposing suitable boundary conditions, see e.g.
13
.

The study of stability properties of distributed parameter pH systems is still a recent topic
11
;
12
;
27
;
14
;
9
;
15
;
24
that can be used in the context of stabilization of distributed pH systems
19
;
17
;
21
;
4
;
10
, a closely related topic.

Here we focus on the
exponential stability
of the pH system (
1
), meaning that there are constants
C,\omega>0
such that

\|x(t)\|\leq Ce^{-\omega t}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0

and for all solutions of (
1
). Compared to the stable case, the question when exponential stability holds is less understood.
Although there are some general results like the Gearhart-Prüss theorem, see Theorem 1.11 in Chapter 5 of
7
, are often hard to verify for general infinite-dimensional systems, because they require the computation of resolvents along the imaginary axis which is only possible if more structure of the pH system is known
9
.

The additional pH structure is helpful to derive sufficient conditions for the exponential stability of (
1
) that can be easily verified. For example it is known, see e.g.
13
, that the condition

\displaystyle\langle\bm{H}x,\bm{A}x\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle\bm{A}x,\bm{H}x\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq-k\|\bm{H}(\delta)x(\delta)\|^{2},

(1)

for some
k>0
and all
x\in D(\bm{A})
at
\delta=a
or
\delta=b
is sufficient for exponential stability of (
1
).

For many examples the condition (
1
) can be easily verified leading to exponential stability. This is the case, for instance, for a vibrating string with a fixed left end-point at
a
and a damper connected to the right end-point at
b
. However, when this vibrating string is viewed as being composed of two smaller segments with natural coupling conditions for the forces and velocities at the interconnection point then surprisingly (
1
) fails to hold. Therefore, (
1
) is not a necessary condition.

Recently, a characterization of exponential stability for distributed parameter pH systems of the form (
1
) was obtained in
22
. Based on this, the asymptotic stability of such pH systems was characterized in
26
. However, the conditions given in these works require that the solutions of a parameter-dependent family of (non-autonomous) ordinary differential equations are known, which is usually impossible even for small example systems, unless the Hamiltonian
\bm{H}
is constant.

In the present work, we consider an integrated version of (
1
), which leads to a new characterization of exponential stability. More precisely, given a fixed
\gamma>0
such that

\gamma\bm{H}(\zeta)\geq\pm\bm{P}_{1}^{-1}
, for all
\zeta\in[a,b]
, we show that the pH system (
1
) is exponentially stable if and only if there are
0<\sigma<\tau<T
with
\tau-\sigma>2\gamma(b-a)
and a constant
k>0
such that

\displaystyle~~~~\langle(\bm{H}x)(T),x(T)\rangle-\langle(\bm{H}x)(0),x_{0}\rangle\leq-2k\int\limits_{\sigma}^{\tau}x(t,\delta)^{*}\bm{H}(\delta)x(t,\delta)\,\mathrm{d}t

(2)

holds at one of the end-points
\delta=a
or
\delta=b
for all initial values
x_{0}\in D(\bm{A})
. Condition (
2
) is particularly useful for pH systems that exhibit a network structure. In particular, our work is related to the stabilizability and observability of PDEs on graphs
6
.

To prove that (
2
) is indeed a characterization of the exponential stability for pH systems (
1
), we develop local energy methods.
Due to the seemingly hyperbolic structure of the pH system equations, we will also revisit energy methods that are used in the context of PDEs and in particular wave equations, see e.g.
(
8
, Chapter 2)
, where they are used to derive stability results.

Energy methods were to some extent already used in the literature on distributed pH systems in

24
;
13
;
5
to derive the sufficient condition (
1
), see also
25
for more general classes of systems.

Here, we revisit energy methods in the context of pH systems (
1
) and generalize them by considering functions that describe the local energy content for a fixed spatial coordinate
\zeta
and a fixed time
t
, respectively,

\displaystyle\bm{F}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{\sigma-\gamma(\zeta-a)}^{\tau+\gamma(\zeta-a)}\!\!\!x(t,\zeta)^{*}\bm{H}(\zeta)x(t,\zeta)\ \mathrm{d}t,\quad\zeta\in[a,b],

\displaystyle\bm{G}(t)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\int\limits_{\alpha+\gamma t}^{\beta-\gamma t}\!\!\!x(t,\zeta)^{*}\bm{H}(\zeta)x(t,\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta,\,\,\,\,0\leq t\leq\tfrac{\beta-\alpha}{2\gamma},

for suitable constants
\sigma,\tau,\gamma,\alpha,\beta>0
such that the integration bounds are within the considered temporal and spatial domains.

By analyzing the properties of the local energy content functions
\bm{F}
and
\bm{G}
associated with pH systems (
1
), we obtain the following contributions in this paper:

(i)

We use the energy methods to obtain an equivalent condition to exponential stability of (
1
) in terms of the energy content at the boundary;

(ii)

We use the energy methods to show that there is no short-time decay for the class (
1
);

(iii)

We verify our sufficient condition for networks of strings, where the sufficient condition (
1
) cannot be applied.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we recall preliminary results on the solution of (
1
) via the semigroup approach. In Section
3
, we introduce the two local energy methods and analyze the properties of the functions
\bm{F}
and
\bm{G}
. These properties are used in Section
4
to prove a new characterization of exponential stability and to give a negative result in the context of short-time behavior of distributed pH systems (
1
) with boundary damping. In Section
5
the characterization of exponential stability is illustrated for two networks of vibrating strings. We conclude the paper in Section
6
.

A recent topic in the literature on control theory is the short-time behavior of dissipative systems, see for example

6
Conclusion

In this work, we used energy methods to analyze the long- and short-time behavior of distributed parameter pH systems. We have in particular given a characterization of exponential stability for such systems that generalizes a well-known sufficient condition in the literature. Using this new characterization, we can verify exponential stability for examples where the old condition is not applicable. In future work, we will use our condition to obtain sufficient conditions for the stability analysis of more complicated networks of pH systems.

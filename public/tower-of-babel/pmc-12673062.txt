Modification, analysis and properties of Piper nigrum polysaccharide
Authors: Laiqing Deng, Yiling Chen, Gangliang Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCurrently, researchers have not yet found the antioxidant activity and structure of Piper nigrum L. polysaccharides (PNLP) and their modified derivatives. PNLP possesses advantages such as simple and stable structure, ease of separation and purification, high carbohydrate content, and excellent antioxidant activity, yet it remains largely undeveloped. This study aims to investigate the antioxidant activity and fundamental structure of PNLP and its three derivatives, and to provide a theoretical explanation for the relationship between them. Three derivatized polysaccharides, namely acetylated polysaccharides prepared from PNLP (Ac-PNLP), carboxymethylated polysaccharides prepared from PNLP (CM-PNLP) and phosphorylated polysaccharides prepared from PNLP (P-PNLP), which had degree of substitution (DS) of 0.41, 0.48, 0.11, and carbohydrate contents of 37.19%, 53.97%, and 36.83%, respectively. P-PNLP was shown to have good scavenging ability in DPPH radical scavenging and hydroxyl radical scavenging assays, and it was hypothesized that the antioxidant activity of the chemically modified PNLP was increased, which may be attributed to the introduction of the new functional groups and its altered structure-activity relationship resulted. This experiment provides insights into the structure and activity of PNLP and its derivatives, offering explanations for the relationship between the two. It establishes a theoretical foundation for the application of PNLP in the food and pharmaceutical industries.Keywords: Extraction, Modification, Characterization, ActivitySubject terms: Biochemistry, Biotechnology, Chemistry, Materials science

Introduction

Polysaccharides are natural biological macromolecules, some of which have many biological activities such as antioxidant1, anti-inflammatory2, anti-tumor3, and antibacterial activities4–6. However, the biological activities of partial polysaccharides are limited due to various reasons, such as poor water solubility and large molecular weight, which affects their use in pharmaceutical and food applications7,8. Currently, modification is one of the most common means to enhance the bioactivity of polysaccharides and expand their applications5. Common chemical modifications of polysaccharides include sulfation9, phosphorylation10, carboxymethylation11 and acetylation12. These modifications not only preserve the intrinsic properties of polysaccharides, but also have the potential to impart new functions to them by changing the type, number and position of substituents13. For example, water-insoluble Poria Cocos polysaccharides can be derivatized to enhance their solubility and biological activity14, while water-soluble Poria Cocos polysaccharides can also be used as drug carriers after carboxymethylation15,16.

In Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), Piper nigrum L. which is commonly known as white pepper is not only used as a flavoring agent, but is also considered to be anti-inflammatory and antiseptic17,18. Piper nigrum L. is also rich in bioactive substances19,20. Previous experiments have shown that Piper nigrum L. polysaccharides (PNLP) is neutral polysaccharides with an α configuration and certain antioxidant activity, and ultrasound-assisted extraction (UAE) has been shown to be a more efficient method of extracting PNLP19. However, the observed low solubility of PNLP presumably constrained its antioxidant activity. Not only acetylation, carboxymethylation and phosphorylation were easier to perform, they also cause minimal damage to the basic structure of polysaccharides, enhancing their solubility5. This paper will introduce functional groups into its structure for the first time through a series of chemical modifications, including acetylation, carboxymethylation, and phosphorylation21. Not only to explore its degree of substitution (DS) and carbohydrate content after chemical modification, but also to detect the structural changes by FT-IR and NMR analysis, and to conduct in vitro antioxidant experiments to determine the effect of chemical modification on its antioxidant capacity21. It is hypothesized that these studies could help to investigate the structure-modification-activity relationship of PNLP22. It is expected that the chemical modification can improve and expand the application of PNLP in pharmaceutical23 and food fields24.

The materials were bought from the local market, they were mixed with ethanol in the ratio of 1:10 (v/v), extracted at reflux for 2 h, dried19.

UAE of PNLP

Pretreated 10 g of Piper nigrum L. powder was put into a beaker, 360 ml of H2O was added, and the PNLP was extracted by UAE for 70 min at an ultrasonic power of 324 W, an operating frequency of 40 kHz and an extraction temperature of 78 °C. At the end of the process, the polysaccharides extract was obtained by centrifugation and filtration, rotary evaporation was carried out, and the extract was concentrated, and chloroform and n-butanol were added in accordance with the ratio of polysaccharides extract: chloroform: n-butanol = 15:4:1, and it was shaken for about 15 min, and then it was allowed to stand for sedimentation. The supernatant was placed in a 3500 Da dialysis bag and dialyzed with distilled water for 2 days. The dialysate was mixed with ethanol at a volume four times that of the dialysate, allowed to stand, centrifuged to collect the precipitate, redissolved in H2O, frozen into a solid, and then placed in a freeze dryer for freeze drying to obtain PNLP19.

Acetylation of PNLP

In this experiment, acetylated PNLP was prepared by Ac2O method12,27.10 ml of PNLP solution (50 mg/ml), add 1 mol/l NaOH solution to adjust the pH of PNLP solution to about 9. Add the total amount of 0.5 ml Ac2O of and 1 mol/l NaOH solution in small quantities and alternately to ensure that the pH of PNLP solution is between 8 and 10. After completion the reaction was carried out at 30 °C for 2 h. At the end of the reaction, it was allowed to stand and the reaction solution was adjusted to neutral with 0.5 mol/L HCl solution. The solution was put into a 3500 Da dialysis bag and dialyzed with distilled H2O for 3 days, after which the dialysate was freeze-dried to obtain acetylated polysaccharides prepared from PNLP (Ac-PNLP). Carboxymethylation of PNLP

Carboxymethylated PNLP were prepared by ClCH2COOH method28,29. Weighed 500 mg of PNLP mixed with 40 ml of 20% NaOH solution (w/w) in a beaker, fully dissolved, added 7.5 ml of 4 mol/l of ClCH2COOH isopropanol solution in a flask, and reacted for 5 h at 55 °C. At the end of the reaction, it was allowed to stand to room temperature and the pH of the reaction solution was adjusted to neutral with 1 mol/l HCl solution. The above solution was transferred to a 3500 Da dialysis bag and dialyzed with distilled water for 3 days, and then the dialysate was freeze-dried to obtain carboxymethylated polysaccharides prepared from PNLP (CM-PNLP). The yield of CM-PNLP is calculated according to Eq. (2.1).

Phosphorylation of PNLP

Phosphorylated PNLP were prepared by POCl3-pyridine method30. Weighed 500 mg of PNLP was added into 10 ml of DMF with stirring for 2 h to make it fully dissolved. After pre-cooling and stirring 10 ml of pyridine for 30 min under an ice water bath, slowly drip a total volume of 2.5 ml of POCl3 solution multiple times, and when a large amount of yellow solid appeared in the solution, then PNLP-DMF solution was added to it slowly several times. The mixed solution was reacted at 55 °C for 3 h. At the end of the reaction, it was cooled and the pH of the reaction solution was adjusted to neutral with 1 mol/l NaOH. The above solution was put into a 3500 Da dialysis bag and dialyzed with distilled H2O for 5 days, and then the dialysate was freeze-dried to obtain phosphorylated polysaccharides prepared from PNLP (P-PNLP). Structural characterization

Monosaccharide composition

The monosaccharide composition of PNLP was analysed using the PMP-HPLC method. Mannose (Man), rhamnose (Rha), glucuronic acid (GlcA), galacturonic acid (GalA), glucose (Glc), galactose (Gal), xylose (Xyl), arabinose (Ara) and fucose (Fuc) were used as standards in qualitative and quantitative analyses, with retention time and peak area being compared.

Preparation of Standard Solution: Take 2 mg each of Mannose (Man), rhamnose (Rha), glucuronic acid (GlcA), galacturonic acid (GalA), glucose (Glc), galactose (Gal), xylose (Xyl), arabinose (Ara) and fucose (Fuc) reference standards. Dissolve in 1800 µL water to prepare a mixed standard solution. Take 300 µL of this solution and add it to NaOH and PMP-methanol solution. Mix thoroughly and place in a 70 °C water bath for 30 min. After cooling, neutralize with 150 µL of 0.3 mol/L hydrochloric acid. Add 1500 µL of CHCl₃, vortex to mix thoroughly, repeat twice, and collect 1000 µL of the lower aqueous layer. Filter through a 0.22 μm membrane32.

Sample Solution Preparation: Take 200 µL of PNLP solution (20 mg/ml), add 2.5 ml trifluoroacetic acid, evaporate to dryness under nitrogen, then add sodium hydroxide solution and PMP-methanol solution. Mix thoroughly and place in a 70 °C water bath for 30 min. After cooling, add 250 µL dilute HCl for neutralization. Add 1 ml CHCl3, mix thoroughly, discard the lower organic layer. Take 1 ml of the lower aqueous layer and filter through a 0.22 μm membrane.

HPLC conditions: Alltima C18 column (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm), ammonium acetate buffer-acetonitrile (80:20, v/v) as mobile phase, flow rate 1 ml/min, detection wavelength 245 nm, column temperature 35 °C, injection volume 5 µL.

FT-IR analysis

About 3 mg of PNLP and its derivatives were taken and analyzed by FT-IR using KBr compression method21. The scanning range was set from 4000 to 500 cm−1.

NMR analysis

About 60 mg of PNLP and its derivatives were taken and dissolved in 0.4 ml of D2O, centrifuged, and the supernatant was taken into an NMR tube. The1H NMR, 13C NMR and31P NMR were recorded by 300 MHz NMR for analysis, respectively21.

FT-IR analysis of Ac-PNLP

Referring to Fig. 4; Table 2, compared with the FT-IR spectrum of PNLP, Ac-PNLP has three main new characteristic peaks at 1731 cm−1, 1371 cm−1, and 1231 cm−1, respectively40,41. The sharp peak at 1731 cm−1 is caused by the stretching vibration of C = O in acetyl group, and the sharp peak at 1371 cm−1 is caused by the symmetric deformation of -CH3, whose absorption intensity can be controlled by DS, which can be used to determine DS in an easy way, and the peak at 1231 cm−1 is the peak of ester41, which proved that the acetyl group introduced into the structure of PNLP and the acetylation reaction occurred.

FT-IR analysis of CM-PNLP

From Fig. 4; Table 2, it can be seen that compared with PNLP, the new peak at 1416 cm−1 is due to the bending vibration of the -CH2-, while 1325 cm−1 and 1589 cm−1 are due to the symmetric and asymmetric telescopic vibration of COO−42. Due to the hydrogen bond formed between the -COOH introduced by CM-PNLP and the -OH therein, the stretching vibration of C = O in CM-PNLP shifts to a lower field, thereby reducing the C = O characteristic absorption41. In comparison to PNLP, the intensity of the absorption peak of CM-PNLP at 2926 cm−1 is diminished, and the same has been reported after carboxymethylation42. CM-PNLP has characteristic absorption peaks at -CH2COOH, indicating that -CH2COOH has been introduced into its structure.

FT-IR analysis of P-PNLP

From Fig. 4; Table 2, it can be seen that the stretching vibration of P = O leads to the appearance of the characteristic absorption peak at 1250 cm−1, which may be due to the effect of low DS resulting in an insignificant absorption peak32. The peak at 885 cm−1, on the other hand, is due to the appearance of the characteristic absorption of C-O-P, and it is also found that the characteristic absorption peak of PNLP at 930 cm- 1 disappears at P-PNLP32,43. In summary, it is shown that the phosphate was introduced in the structure of PNLP.

SEM observation of PNLP and its derivatives

The microstructures of PNLP and its derivatives were observed through Fig. 12. PNLP has multiple layered superimposed structures with rough surfaces and many semicircular tiny particles attached. The fragments of its microstructure were relatively large. Ac-PNLP had a partially smooth micro surface and a looser compositional structure compared to PNLP. The antioxidant activity analysis of PNLP derivatives

Figure 13. indicates that different chemical modification methods significantly influence the antioxidant efficacy of PNLP, and the antioxidant activity of both PNLP and its derivatives exhibits a dose-dependent relationship.

Antioxidant activity of PNLP, Ac-PNLP, CM-PNLP and P-PNLP in vitro; (a) The scavenging ability to DPPH radicals; (b) The scavenging ability to hydroxyl radicals; (c) The scavenging ability to ABTS radicals; (d) Determination of reduction capacity.

DPPH radical scavenging activity assay

According to Fig. 13 (a), it can be seen that the scavenging ability of PNLP and its derivatives to scavenge DPPH free radicals has a big gap compared to Vc, and the modified P-PNLP has a huge increase in the ability to scavenge DPPH radicals compared to PNLP, and it is speculated that phosphorylation modification of P-PNLP and the introduction of a phosphate group in its structure drastically increased the electron-donating ability of P-PNLP, which led to a drastic increase in its ability to scavenge DPPH radical scavenging ability was substantially enhanced47. While CM-PNLP has a limited ability to scavenge DPPH radicals compared to PNLP, Ac-PNLP has a much lower ability to scavenge DPPH radicals. It was hypothesized that the higher DS of acetylation might have closed the active hydroxyl group of PNLP, leading to its lower scavenging activity12. Considering that acetylation is milder compared to phosphorylation, it is possible that phosphorylation reduces the molecular weight of PNLP to achieve an increased ability to scavenge DPPH radicals48,49.

Hydroxyl radical scavenging activity assay

From Fig. 13 (b), it can be seen that in addition to Ac-PNLP, CM-PNLP and P-PNLP showed higher enhancement of hydroxyl radical scavenging ability, among which the enhancement of P-PNLP was particularly obvious, which is presumed to be the introduction of phosphate groups into PNLP resulted in the enhancement of hydroxyl radical scavenging activity due to the increase of hydrogen supplying ability of the polysaccharides47, and furthermore, it is speculated that the introduction of groups led to the alteration of PNLP’s structure, which resulted in the release of more reaction sites, and also improved its antioxidant activity. released, which also enhanced its antioxidant activity47. Ac-PNLP, on the other hand, decreased its hydroxyl radical scavenging activity, possibly due to the drastic reduction of its carbohydrate content after acetylation, and it was also speculated that the introduction of acetyl groups affected the chelating ability of PNLP and Fe2+, thus inhibiting its hydroxyl radical scavenging activity50.

ABTS radical scavenging activity assay

As analyzed in Fig. 13 (c), the ABTS radical scavenging activity of the three derivatized polysaccharides was reduced compared to unmodified PNLP. CM-PNLP had the highest carbohydrate content among the three derivatives, while its ABTS radical scavenging activity was well preserved compared to Ac-PNLP and P-PNLP. It is hypothesized that this phenomenon is related to the decrease in carbohydrate content after modification50. It is also possible that the three modifications were not effective in enhancing the scavenging of ABTS free radicals by PNLP51. In conclusion, phosphorylation, carboxymethylation and acetylation are not recommended for modifying PNLP to enhance its ABTS radical scavenging activity.

Reduction capacity assay

As can be seen from Fig. 13 (d), the reducing ability of the three derivatized polysaccharides has a certain degree of decrease compared with PNLP, this is inconsistent with the identification of the antioxidant activity of many modified polysaccharides, among which CM-PNLP has a smaller degree of decrease, presumably related to its modification with a smaller decrease in carbohydrate content, while Ac-PNLP and P-PNLP have a smaller amount of carbohydrate content compared with PNLP50. It is also related to the magnitude of substitution of the chemical modifications52. Therefore, the use of phosphorylated, carboxymethylated, and acetylated modification of PNLP is not recommended for the enhancement of its reducing ability.

Conclusion

In this experiment, PNLP was extracted by UAE, and three derivatives (Ac-PNLP, CM-PNLP, P-PNLP) were prepared by three chemical modification methods and analyzed for their basic properties of DS and carbohydrate content, among which the carbohydrate content of all three derivatives was greatly reduced after modification, and the DS of all three derivatives was relatively low, among which the DS and carbohydrate content of CM-PNLP was the highest. The monosaccharide composition indicates that PNLP and its derivatives are all α-glucans. XRD analysis showed that the amorphous structure of PNLP did not change significantly during the chemical modification process. The phosphorylation modification of PNLP was more recommended in terms of DPPH radical scavenging and hydroxyl radical scavenging, while the three derivatization modifications of PNLP were not recommended in terms of reducing capacity and ABTS radical scavenging. Based on the experimental conclusions outlined above, this study has gained some understanding of the fundamental structure of PNLP, its antioxidant activity, and the effects of chemical modification on its structure and activity. However, the investigation into the structure-activity relationship remains preliminary, and the mechanism underlying the impact of chemical modification has not been thoroughly explored. Further detailed separation and purification of the derivatives are required to elucidate the functional group introduction mechanism. This study provides an experimental foundation for subsequent researchers to delve deeper into the structure of PNLP and offers operational methods for investigating the effects of chemical modifications on other biological activities of PNLP. It is anticipated that PNLP may be developed and utilized in the fields of food and pharmaceuticals.

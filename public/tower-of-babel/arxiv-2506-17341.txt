Optical excitation and stabilization of ultracold field-linked tetratomic molecules
Bijit Mukherjee, Michał Tomza

Abstract
We propose a coherent optical population transfer of weakly bound field-linked tetratomic molecules (FL tetramers) to deeper bound states using stimulated Raman adiabatic passage (STIRAP). We consider static-electric-field shielded polar alkali-metal diatomic molecules and corresponding FL tetramers in their X 1 ​ Σ + + X 1 ​ Σ + \textrm{X}^{1}\Sigma^{+}+\textrm{X}^{1}\Sigma^{+} ground electronic state. We show that the excited metastable X 1 ​ Σ + + b 3 ​ Π \textrm{X}^{1}\Sigma^{+}+\textrm{b}^{3}\Pi electronic manifold supports FL tetramers in a broader range of electric fields with collisional shielding extended to zero field. We calculate the Franck-Condon factors between the ground and excited FL tetramers and show that they are highly tunable with the electric field. We also predict photoassociation (PA) of ground-state shielded molecules to the excited FL states in free-bound optical transitions. We propose proof-of-principle experiments to implement STIRAP and PA using FL tetramers, paving the way for the formation of deeply bound ultracold polyatomic molecules.

Shielding and FL states in
X+X
– Static-electric field shielding of alkali dimers in

1

– Photoassociation (PA) spectroscopy of ultracold atoms has been widely studied, where a pair of colliding atoms absorb a photon to form an excited diatomic molecule

Outlook
– The FL tetramers in
X+b
provide an excellent tool to optically control ultracold tetramers. Our predictions should also apply to other types of shielding. For example, our methodology can be extended to microwave (MW) shielded molecules, which offer higher tunability. One can control the ellipticity of the MW polarization
Chen et al. 2023
or can implement double MW fields
Karman et al. 2025
to make the long-range potential well deeper. This will allow a larger number of FL states and more options for implementing our proposed schemes with a larger number of molecules. Since our methodology is based on the long-range dipolar interaction of the dimers, our predictions are not susceptible to experimental or theoretical uncertainties in determinations of the molecular potentials at the short range.

Our ultimate goal is to transfer the FL states to deeply bound tetramers near the global minimum of the ground interaction potential. For this, one might lower the repulsive barriers in both
X+X
and
X+b
, either by tuning
F
or by introducing an additional MW. This will allow the FCFs between the intermediate
X+b
and final
X+X
states to increase. However, for this to be achieved, knowledge of the shorter-range tetramer potentials in the ground and excited electronic manifolds is required, which we intend to address in our future studies.

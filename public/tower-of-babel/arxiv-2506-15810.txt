Strategies for generating separable photon triplets in waveguides and ring resonators Preprint: APS/123-QED
Gisell Lorena Osorio, Milica Banić, Nicolás Quesada

Abstract
Photon triplet sources exhibit non-Gaussian features, a key property for applications in quantum computing and quantum information. However, spectral correlations can limit the performance and detection efficiency of these systems. Motivated by this, we present a theoretical analysis of the spectral correlations of photon triplets generated through spontaneous third-order parametric down-conversion in photonic devices, and discuss strategies to quantify and minimize them. We propose two strategies to minimize spectral correlations: dispersion engineering in waveguides and pump engineering in resonators. We apply these strategies in two realistic source designs, namely a high-index-contrast optical fiber taper and a silicon nitride microring resonator. Finally, we discuss detection strategies for probing non-Gaussian features of the triplet state. We find that it is feasible to achieve few-mode generation of photon triplets using state-of-the-art experimental systems, a crucial step toward practical applications of photon triplet sources in quantum technologies.



I
Introduction

The generation of multipartite entangled states has been a long-term objective in quantum optics, and is a fundamental component of quantum information protocols and applications. Nonlinear optical processes are some of the most flexible and scalable methods for generating them. Spontaneous parametric down-conversion (PDC) and four-wave mixing have been reliable methods to generate entangled photon pairs, and now, thanks to advancements in basic technologies for manufacturing nonlinear photonic devices and ultra-sensitive detection techniques
Caspani et al. 2017
;
Katiyi and Karabchevsky 2025
;
Kong et al. 2024
;
Stasi et al. 2024
, the community is now turning to the implementation of higher-order parametric processes. An important topic now is the generation of photon triplets, either through cascaded nonlinear second-order optical processes
Hübel et al. 2010
;
Krapick et al. 2016
or through third-order parametric down-conversion (TOPDC)
Chang et al. 2020
;
Douady and Boulanger 2004
;
Bertrand et al. 2024
.

TOPDC-based photon triplets stand out for their distinctive characteristics, such as tripartite spectral entanglement and non-Gaussian features
Braunstein and McLachlan 1987
;
Agustí et al. 2020
, and promising applications, such as heralded emission of photon pairs
Barz et al. 2010
, direct generation of Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states
Greenberger et al. 1990
, third-order squeezing
Banaszek and Knight 1997
, among others. A variety of implementations have been proposed, including nonresonant systems, such as optical fibers
Corona et al. 2011a
;
Cavanna et al. 2020
, bulk crystals
Borshchevskaya et al. 2015
, and waveguides
Banic et al. 2022
;
Moebius et al. 2016
;
Bencheikh et al. 2022
; and resonant systems, such as ring resonators
Banic et al. 2022
and superconducting parametric cavities
Chang et al. 2020
.

While this earlier work has focused on designing sources with reasonable generation rates, little consideration has been given to the spectral characteristics of the generated light
Chekhova et al. 2005
. In this matter, the photon triplet wavefunction is of particular interest, as it defines the spectral correlation properties of these quantum states. In many scenarios, such correlations are undesirable because they lead to highly multimode fields
Banic 2024
;
Graffitti et al. 2018
: This
multimodeness
restricts high-visibility interference, complicating detection through coherent methods (e.g., homodyne and heterodyne detection), and preventing applications that rely on interference, such as those involving optical quantum networks
U’Ren et al. 2005
. Furthermore, it limits the non-Gaussian features of the triplets’ Wigner representation in a particular mode
Ra et al. 2020
;
Banic 2024
, making these sources unsuitable for applications that rely on this characteristic, including universal quantum computing and quantum error correction
Lloyd and Braunstein 1999
;
Weedbrook et al. 2012
;
Walschaers 2021
.

While the quantification and minimization of spectral correlations in PDC-based photon pair sources is a well-studied problem
U’Ren et al. 2005
;
Graffitti et al. 2018
, similar approaches have not yet been proposed for single-modeness of TOPDC-based triplet sources. In this work, we present a theoretical analysis of the spectral properties of photon triplets generated through TOPDC, and we propose strategies to quantify and minimize their spectral correlations. The paper is structured as follows: In Section
II.1
, we introduce a measure to quantify spectral correlations based on the spectral decomposition of the single-photon reduced density matrix, obtained by performing a partial trace over two parties in the TOPDC tripartite state. We propose experimental strategies to control the photon triplets’ spectral correlations through dispersion engineering in waveguides or optical fibers ( Section
II.2
) and pump engineering in ring resonators (Section
II.3
). In Section
III
, we present examples of experimental proposals for generating factorable photon triplets, using the above-mentioned strategies. We provide sample calculations for high-index-contrast optical fibers in Section
III.1
and for a silicon nitride microring resonator in Section
III.2
. Finally, in Section
IV
, we discuss methods to measure distinctive features of nearly uncorrelated triphoton states using triple coincidence detection and homodyne measurements. Our conclusions are outlined in Section
V
.

II
Spectrally uncorrelated photon triplet states

In TOPDC, an input pump field with central frequency
\overline{\omega}_{P}
propagates through a third-order nonlinear medium, leading to the probabilistic downconversion of a pump photon into three daughter photons with central frequencies
\overline{\omega}_{1}
,
\overline{\omega}_{2}
, and
\overline{\omega}_{3}
. In this parametric interaction, the energy conservation condition is satisfied, such that

\overline{\omega}_{P}-\overline{\omega}_{1}-\overline{\omega}_{2}-\overline{\omega}_{3}=0.

(1)

We focus on the degenerate frequency configuration, in which the triplets are generated with identical central frequencies
\overline{\omega}_{F}=\overline{\omega}_{P}/3
and in the same spatial mode (labeled as
F
).

In a “low-gain” regime, the quantum state resulting from this interaction takes the following form (see Appendix
A
)
Corona et al. 2011b
;
Dot et al. 2012
;
Okoth et al. 2019
;
Domínguez-Serna et al. 2020
;
Banic 2024
:

\ket{\phi}\approx\ket{\text{vac}}+\varepsilon\ket{\text{III}},

(2)

where
\varepsilon^{2}
is the probability of generating a photon triplet per pump pulse, and

\displaystyle\ket{\text{III}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\int d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}d\omega_{3}\Psi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}_{F}(\omega_{1})a^{\dagger}_{F}(\omega_{2})a^{\dagger}_{F}(\omega_{3})\ket{\text{vac}}

(3)

is a three-photon Fock state
Rohde et al. 2007
. The operator
a^{\dagger}_{F}(\omega_{i})
is a creation operator satisfying the standard bosonic commutation relations,
\omega_{i}
are frequency variables,
\ket{\text{vac}}
denotes the vacuum state, and
\Psi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
represents the triphoton wavefunction, commonly known as the
triphoton joint spectral amplitude
(JSA). The triphoton JSA is normalized according to

\int d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}d\omega_{3}|\Psi\left(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\right)|^{2}=1,

(4)

and is our function of interest because it captures the spectral correlation properties of the generated photon triplets.
Its form is determined by the pump spectral distribution and the nonlinear medium dispersion characteristics (See Appendix
A
).

II.1
Separability of the triphoton joint spectral amplitude

Quantification of spectral correlations in PDC-based photon pair sources is a well-studied problem
U’Ren et al. 2005
;
Graffitti et al. 2018
for which necessary and sufficient conditions are known
Quesada and Brańczyk 2018
;
Houde et al. 2024a
. Typically, the separability in those sources is determined by performing a Schmidt
Law et al. 2000
(or Autonne-Takagi
Houde et al. 2024b
) decomposition over the bipartite joint spectral amplitude. The generalization of this strategy to tripartite systems is not straightforward since there are no general Schmidt decompositions for them
Peres 1995
(although significant progress continues to be made in understanding the entanglement in these systems
Florido-Llinàs et al. 2024
). Moreover, for the cases we consider here – namely the generation of degenerate triplets – the characterization of identical particle entanglement is more nuanced than that of entanglement of distinguishable parties
Killoran et al. 2014
;
Sciara et al. 2017
.

Despite these difficulties, good entanglement measures can be introduced for high-dimensional multipartite systems. For a pure multipartite system, Ma et al.
Ma et al. 2011
introduced the genuine multipartite entanglement concurrence as a bona-fide measure of multipartite entanglement
Horodecki 2001
. For a tripartite normalized state
\ket{\Lambda_{\text{III}}}
that is permutationally invariant, one can evaluate the genuine multipartite entanglement concurrence as

\displaystyle C(\ket{\Lambda}_{\text{III}})=2\sqrt{1-\text{Tr}(\rho_{I}^{2})},

(5)

where
\rho_{I}
is the reduced density matrix of one of the three parties.
Note that the quantity above is a simple monotonic function of the purity
-\text{Tr}(\rho_{I}^{2})
, thus we can introduce a separability parameter (in analogy with the Schmidt number in the bipartite case) as

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{1}{\text{Tr}(\rho_{I}^{2})}.

(6)

Minimizing spectral correlations, approaching a separable state, is equivalent to minimizing
\kappa
. Indeed,
\kappa\geq 1
, and
\kappa=1
if and only if
\ket{\Lambda_{\text{III}}}=\ket{\lambda}\otimes\ket{\lambda}\otimes\ket{\lambda}
is a product state over the three parties.

For the triplet state considered here, and assuming that the JSA is normalized, we can easily evaluate the reduced density matrix to be

\displaystyle\rho_{I}=\int d\omega_{1}d\omega^{\prime}_{1}\rho(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{1})a^{\dagger}_{F}(\omega_{1})\ket{\text{vac}}\bra{\text{vac}}a_{F}(\omega^{\prime}_{1}),

(7)

where the frequency-space density matrix is given by

\displaystyle\rho(\omega_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime})=\int d\omega_{2}d\omega_{3}\Psi\left(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\right)\Psi^{\ast}(\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}).

(8)

Note that
\rho(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{1})=\rho^{*}(\omega^{\prime}_{1},\omega_{1})
and that it satisfies
\int d\omega_{1}\rho(\omega_{1},\omega_{1})=1
, thus one can write an eigendecomposition

\displaystyle\rho(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{1})=\sum_{n}r_{n}f_{n}(\omega_{1})f^{*}_{n}(\omega^{\prime}_{1}),

(9)

where the
f_{n}(\omega_{1})
, which we call pseudo-Schmidt functions, are an orthonormal basis, and
\sum_{n}r_{n}=1
. Note that the first-order coherence function
Fabre and Treps 2020
can be written as

\displaystyle\braket{\phi|a_{F}^{\dagger}(\omega_{1})a_{F}(\omega_{1}^{\prime})|\phi}=3\varepsilon^{2}\rho(\omega_{1},\omega_{1}^{\prime}),

(10)

giving a second physical interpretation to the reduced density matrix. Moreover, we note that other low order moments of the ladder operators are zero, namely
\braket{\phi|a_{F}(\omega_{1})|\phi}=0
and
\braket{\phi|a_{F}(\omega_{1})a_{F}(\omega_{1}^{\prime})|\phi}=0
.
Given these observations about the pure state
\ket{\phi}
, we can use the elegant results from Mele
et al.

Mele et al. 2025
to obtain a simple measure of non-Gaussianity as given by the
symplectic rank
. This quantity is given by the number of symplectic eigenvalues of the covariance matrix of a pure state that have values higher than those associated with the vacuum state. The
n^{\text{th}}
symplectic eigenvalue associated with
\ket{\phi}
exceeds that of the vacuum state precisely by
3\varepsilon^{2}r_{n}
, giving another interpretation to the reduced density matrix of the triplet state.

II.2
Dispersion engineering strategy for waveguides

For a waveguide, the triphoton JSA is the product of two functions:

\Psi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=\overline{\Psi}_{0}~\alpha(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})~\varphi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}),

(11)

where
\alpha(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})
is known as the pump envelope function (PEF), and
\varphi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
is the phase matching function (PMF). By
\overline{\Psi}_{0}
we denote a normalization constant (see Appendix
A.1
).

The PEF represents the spectral distribution of the pump laser. Its profile can vary depending on the experimental conditions. For example, it may take the form of hyperbolic secant pulses, which are characteristic of optical solitons and pulses emitted from certain mode-locked lasers
Agrawal 2019
. More generally, the PEF can be tailored through pulse-shaping techniques. For simplicity, this work considers an approximation of pulses with a Gaussian shape

\alpha(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})=\overline{\alpha}_{P}\exp(-(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}-\overline{\omega}_{P})^{2}/2\sigma^{2}),

(12)

where
\overline{\alpha}_{P}
is a normalization constant, such that
\int d\omega|\alpha(\omega)|^{2}=N_{P}
, where
N_{P}
is the average number of pump photons, and
\sigma
represents the pump’s bandwidth.

The PMF depends implicitly on the waveguide’s geometry and material dispersion properties. For a waveguide that is homogeneous along the longitudinal direction, the PMF is defined as follows:

\varphi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=\sinc\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\Delta k(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\right),

(13)

where
\ell
is the length of the nonlinear medium, and

\displaystyle\Delta k(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=

\displaystyle k_{P}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})

(14)

\displaystyle-k_{F}(\omega_{1})-k_{F}(\omega_{2})-k_{F}(\omega_{3})

is the phase mismatch for TOPDC, where
k_{P}(\omega)
and
k_{F}(\omega)
are the wavenumbers of the pump and triplet fields, respectively, evaluated at a frequency
\omega
. The wavenumbers are defined by their dispersion relation

k_{\scriptscriptstyle P,F}(\omega_{i})=\frac{\omega_{i}~n_{\scriptscriptstyle P,F}(\omega_{i})}{c},

(15)

where
n_{\scriptscriptstyle P,F}(\omega_{i})
is the effective refractive index of the pump (
P
) or triplet (
F
) mode at the frequency
\omega_{i}
, and
c
is the velocity of light in vacuum.

To analyze the effect of the waveguide dispersion parameters on the phase-mismatch, we Taylor expand the dispersion relations around the central frequencies
\overline{\omega}_{P}
or
\overline{\omega}_{F}
, working up to the second order and assuming that the higher-order dispersion terms are negligible. We have

\displaystyle\Delta k(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=

\displaystyle\left(\overline{k}_{P}-3\overline{k}_{F}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{v_{P}}-\frac{1}{v_{F}}\right)\left(\delta\omega_{1}+\delta\omega_{2}+\delta\omega_{3}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\beta_{P}\left(\delta\omega_{1}+\delta\omega_{2}+\delta\omega_{3}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\beta_{F}\left(\delta\omega_{1}^{2}+\delta\omega_{2}^{2}+\delta\omega_{3}^{2}\right),

(16)

where
\delta\omega_{i}=\omega_{i}-\overline{\omega}_{F}
, and
\overline{k}_{P}
(
\overline{k}_{F}
),
v_{P}
(
v_{F}
), and
\beta_{P}
(
\beta_{F}
) are the wavenumber, group velocity and group velocity dispersion parameters evaluated at the pump (triplet) central frequencies, respectively.

To achieve efficient TOPDC generation, we need to satisfy phase matching
\left(\overline{k}_{P}-3\overline{k}_{F}=0\right)
and group-velocity matching
\left(v_{P}\approx v_{F}\right)
. Additionally, if
|\beta_{F}|\gg|\beta_{P}|
, the PMF can be approximated as

\varphi(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\approx\sinc\left(\frac{\ell}{4}|\beta_{F}|\left(\delta\omega_{1}^{2}+\delta\omega_{2}^{2}+\delta\omega_{3}^{2}\right)\right),

(17)

which has isosurfaces corresponding to spherical layers in the three-dimensional frequency space
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
, thus resulting in a spherically symmetric spectral distribution (see Appendix
B
). It is then possible to achieve approximate separability of the JSA if, in addition to fulfilling the above conditions, the minimum pump bandwidth corresponds to the PMF bandwidth
Moebius et al. 2016

\sigma_{PM}\propto\sqrt{4\pi/\ell|\beta_{F}|},

(18)

as seen in Fig.
1
. This alignment ensures that the spherical symmetry of the PMF’s main lobe is preserved. For this idealized scenario, we find a minimum separability parameter of
\kappa\approx 1.25
, establishing a theoretical lower bound for the separability parameter of dispersion-engineered TOPDC-based photon triplet sources. With this, we have established a set of conditions for approximate separability of the triphoton JSA for waveguides, and a lower limit for
\kappa
for triplets generated in such systems.

II.3
Pump engineering in ring resonators

The JSA for TOPDC in a microring resonator has the form

\displaystyle\Psi_{R}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{0}F^{*}_{F+}(\omega_{1})F^{*}_{F+}(\omega_{2})F^{*}_{F+}(\omega_{3})

(20)

\displaystyle\times F^{*}_{P-}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})\alpha(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}),

where
\alpha(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})
is the pump envelope function as described above,
\overline{\Psi}_{0}
is again a normalization constant (see Appendix
A.2
), and

\displaystyle F_{J\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}}\left(\frac{\gamma^{*}_{J}}{(\omega_{J}-\omega)\pm i\overline{\Gamma}_{J}}\right)

(21)

is a field enhancement factor associated with the resonance labeled
J
;
\omega_{J}
refers to the resonance frequency,
\overline{\Gamma}_{J}
to the resonance linewidth, and
\gamma_{J}
describes the coupling between the ring and the bus waveguide
Banic et al. 2022
;
Vernon and Sipe 2015
. The loaded quality factor of the resonator is defined in terms of the linewidth
\overline{\Gamma}_{J}
as

\displaystyle Q_{J}=\frac{\omega_{J}}{2\overline{\Gamma}_{J}}.

(22)

A factorable JSA can be obtained from a ring resonator using the following approach, which has been applied for the generation of spectrally pure photon pairs
Vernon et al. 2017
. Consider a scenario in which the resonances at
\omega_{P}
and
\omega_{F}
are mismatched, such that
\overline{\Gamma}_{P}>\overline{\Gamma}_{F}
, with the ring resonator pumped by a pulse much shorter than the dwelling time
\tau_{F}\sim 1/\overline{\Gamma}_{F}
. In this limit, the range of frequencies contributing to the JSA is constrained (to
\sim\overline{\Gamma}_{F}
), while the functions
\alpha(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})
and
F_{P-}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})
are broad in comparison, such that they vary relatively little over relevant values of
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}
. In this case we can write
\alpha(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})\approx\overline{\alpha}_{P}
(recall Eq.
12
), and similarly for the pump field enhancement factor; that is, we approximate the JSA as

\displaystyle\Psi_{R}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})

\displaystyle\approx\overline{\Psi}_{0}F^{*}_{F+}(\omega_{1})F^{*}_{F+}(\omega_{2})F^{*}_{F+}(\omega_{3})

(23)

\displaystyle\times F^{*}_{P-}(\omega_{P})\overline{\alpha}_{P},

which has the separable form
\Psi_{R}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\approx\psi(\omega_{1})\psi(\omega_{2})\psi(\omega_{3})
with

\displaystyle\psi(\omega)\propto F^{*}_{F+}(\omega).

(24)

One could also apply more elaborate pulse shaping schemes
Christensen et al. 2018
;
Burridge et al. 2020
, but as above, we limit our discussion here to Gaussian pump pulses.

IV
Detection

In this section, we describe close-to-optimal strategies to measure the interesting features of a (nearly) spectrally uncorrelated triphoton state.

For direct detection, one would like to detect three-fold coincidences after splitting the beam containing the triplets using a three-port interferometer.
We imagine an interferometer in which the first port is fed with light in the state
\ket{\phi}
, and the other two with vacuum. If we label the input creation operator as
a_{F,0}(\omega)
, where
0
indicates the first input port of the interferometer, then we want to maximize the component with 3 photons in 3 separate ports after making the substitution
a_{F,0}(\omega)\to u_{0}a_{F,0}(\omega)+u_{1}a_{F,1}(\omega)+u_{2}a_{F,2}(\omega)
in the expression giving the triplet. The component with three photons in three separate ports turns out to be directly proportional to the product
|u_{0}u_{1}u_{2}|
, and since the quantities
u_{i}
need to be the entries of the column of a unitary matrix (thus
|u_{0}|^{2}+|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2}=1
), the product is maximized when
|u_{i}|=1/\sqrt{3}
. This implies that the probability of detecting 3 coincidences, in an idealized scenario with zero losses, is
(|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}|^{2})^{3}=\tfrac{1}{27}
of the probability of making a degenerate triplet.

For homodyne detection with a local oscillator with the spectrum

To identify the optimal local oscillator for the detection of triplets, we implement two distinct optimization routines: gradient descent and basin hopping algorithms. Both methods are minimization algorithms; in this case, maximizing
\eta

||\partial_{g}\eta||
serves as an indicator of convergence.

V
Conclusion

We have analyzed the spectral correlations of photon triplets generated through third-order spontaneous parametric down-conversion in photonic devices, and we discussed strategies to quantify and minimize them. To this end, we introduced a quantitative measure based on the spectral decomposition of the reduced density matrix of one of the three parties in the photon triplet state, in accordance with the genuine multipartite entanglement measure proposed by
Ma et al. 2011

Ma et al. 2011
. This decomposition allows us to define a separability parameter
\kappa
, analogous to the Schmidt number in the bipartite case. Our analysis revealed that symmetric triphoton wavefunctions are factorable if and only if the single-photon reduced density matrix has rank 1. Minimizing the separability parameter, thereby reducing the number of pseudo-Schmidt modes of the single photon density matrix, enables the few-mode operation of the triplet source, which is a fundamental feature for their application in quantum technologies.

Extending this analysis, we proposed two experimental strategies to reduce spectral correlations in TOPDC-based photon triplet sources. The first focuses on a dispersion-engineering approach for waveguides: We find that when the conditions outlined in Section
IV
are achieved, one obtains an approximately factorable joint spectral amplitude of the triplet state, with the separability parameter reaching a minimum value of 1.25. The second strategy involves pump engineering in microring resonators. We showed that a factorable JSA – with
\kappa-1\sim 10^{-3}
– can be achieved using a pump pulse shorter than the dwelling time of the generated modes, with an even higher degree of separability reached by engineering the resonance linewidths.

We then applied these strategies to sample calculations for realistic devices. We first applied our design principles to optimize high-index-contrast optical fiber tapers. We obtain a minimum separability parameter of 1.5, indicating that the system operates in the few-mode regime, and we find that the few-mode operation is maintained even with pump detuning up to 10 nm. The expected triplet generation rate for these tapers is approximately
10^{-1}
triplets per second, taking an average pump power of 400 mW. While this rate is low and presents a detection challenge, it is approaching the sensitivity limits of state-of-the-art single-photon detectors. We also point out that our design principles are not limited to fiber platforms and can be applied to integrated waveguides, where the use of materials with higher nonlinear susceptibilities or structures with smaller effective mode areas can enhance photon triplet generation rates, while preserving separability. We also considered a silicon nitride microring resonator, clad in silica and phase-matched for a 532 nm pump. We found that the separability parameter of the generated triplet state approaches unity as the pump pulse duration is reduced, indicating a high degree of separability, surpassing that achievable in fiber-based sources. However, this advantage comes at the cost of extremely low generation rates under typical conditions, making practical implementation challenging.

Finally, we discussed strategies to characterize nearly uncorrelated triphoton states. We addressed both direct detection using a three-port interferometer to measure triple coincidences, and homodyne detection. In the latter case, it is essential to carefully shape the spectral profile of the local oscillator to maximize deviations from Gaussian statistics in quadrature measurements. We found that the optimal local oscillator corresponds to the dominant mode in the spectral decomposition of the single-photon reduced density matrix. Overall, we have shown that achieving single-mode operation in triplet sources will be essential to demonstrate the generation of non-Gaussianity, and we believe that dispersion-engineered non-resonant sources – like the high-index-contrast fiber tapers discussed in our sample calculations – are promising candidates for photon triplet sources suitable for quantum photonics applications.

The enhanced cancer nanovaccine by bidirectionally blocking CXCL12/CXCR4 axis to inhibit the recurrence and metastasis of triple-negative breast cancer
Authors: Miao Liu, Weiliang Ye, Qifeng Ji, Bangle Zhang, Zhifu Yang, Daozhou Liu, Siyuan Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn situ cancer vaccine is a new method for cancer immunotherapy, however, the local immunosuppressive microenvironment of cancer tissue limits its immune response and therapeutic efficacy. The CXCL12/CXCR4 axis plays a key role in forming an immunosuppressive microenvironment. In this study, siCXCR4 (S) was used as an "immune adjuvant" and co-loaded with hydroxycamptothecin (HCPT) to develop an in situ cancer nanovaccine (HCPT/S@CaP/HA) for synergistically inhibiting the growth, recurrence and metastasis of triple negative breast cancer (TNBC). HCPT/S@CaP/HA actively targeted to orthotopic TNBC cells and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), induced the immunogenic death of orthotopic TNBC cells to promote the maturation of dendritic cells (DCs). Meanwhile, HCPT/S@CaP/HA also reduced the secretion of CXCL12 by CAFs and silenced the expression of CXCR4 in TNBC tissue to bidirectionally block the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis, thereby synergistically reversing immunosuppressive microenvironment, enhancing the infiltration and activity of cytotoxic T lymphocytes in orthotopic TNBC tissues, and subsequently inhibiting the growth of orthotopic TNBC. Moreover, HCPT/S@CaP/HA inhibited the recurrence of TNBC by synergistically increasing the percentage of memory T lymphocytes in the spleen tissue of mice, and it diminished the spontaneous lung metastasis of orthotopic TNBC by promoting the presentation of TNBC antigens and activating TNBC-specific immune responses immune responses. Introduction

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a highly heterogeneous type of breast cancer that lacks estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 expression. TNBC is the most malignant and invasive breast cancer, and it is more common in young women. TNBC is prone to early metastasis, and has a high recurrence rate and poor prognosis [1]. Although the incidence of TNBC accounts for 20% of the total incidence of breast cancer, the mortality rate of TNBC accounts for 83% of the total mortality rate of breast cancer, and it is the most lethal type of breast cancer [2]. In recent years, cancer immunotherapy has opened a door of life for TNBC patients by enhancing the patient’s own immune response, which has the characteristics of quick response and few side effects [3]. Programmed death-1/programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-1/PD-L1) antibody is an immune checkpoint inhibitor approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of TNBC in recent years. It restores the killing effect of cytotoxic T lymphocytes on TNBC cells by blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 signaling pathway [4]. However, only about 10–30% of patients show a clinical response to PD-1/PD-L1 antibodies [5, 6]. In addition to the individual variations in PD-L1 expression, this phenomenon is also closely associated with the tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment formed by TNBC [7, 8].

In situ cancer vaccine can stimulate cancer cell-specific immune response using the patient’s own cancer cells as antigen, and it has become an innovative strategy for personalized immunotherapy. However, the conventional in situ cancer vaccine fails to induce adequate release of cancer antigens, and the ability of dendritic cells (DCs) to recognize and process cancer antigens is poor, resulting in limited antigen-specific T cell immune responses. Furthermore, even if the in situ cancer vaccine elicits specific T cell immune responses, the effector T cells infiltrating into cancer tissue will be exhausted and die as a result of the immunosuppressive microenvironment of the cancer tissue, thereby failing to exert the cytotoxic effect on cancer cells [9–12]. Theoretically, through inducing immunogenic cell death (ICD) of cancer cells, facilitating the maturation of DCs while reversing the immunosuppressive microenvironment, the anti-cancer immune response of in situ cancer vaccine can be strengthened. Subsequently, the anti-cancer efficacy can be enhanced.

Chemokine (C-X-C motif) receptor 4‌ (CXCR4) is a G-protein coupled receptor with seven transmembrane structures, which is higher expressed in TNBC than other types of breast cancer [13]. The chemokine (C-X-C Motif) ligand 12 (CXCL12) is a specific ligand of CXCR4 and belongs to the CXC chemokine family. In solid cancer, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are the major secretors of CXCL12. Overexpression of CXCR4 in cancer cells or over-secretion of CXCL12 activates the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis, promotes the formation of immunosuppressive microenvironment by recruiting myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and regulatory T cells, while inhibiting the activity of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and depleting cytotoxic T lymphocytes [14], thereby helping cancer cells escape immune surveillance [15–17]. CXCL12 also induce tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) to polarize to M2-phenotype TAMs (M2-TAMs), which further activate CAFs and form an immunosuppressive circuit [18]. Therefore, CXCL12/CXCR4 axis plays a key role in forming an immunosuppressive microenvironment. The CXCL12/CXCR4 axis can be formed between cancer cells and CAFs, as well as between orthotopic cancer tissues and distant metastatic target organs. For example, the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis is formed between high levels of CXCL12 in lung tissue and overexpression of CXCR4 in orthotopic TNBC tissue [19]. Recent studies have revealed that the activation of CXCL12/CXCR4 axis is one of the main reasons for the low immune response rate of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors in the clinic [20]. In addition, overexpression of CXCR4 promotes epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT) of TNBC to acquiring migratory and invasive phenotypes [21]. Studies also demonstrate that the CXCL12 secreted by distant metastatic target organs can likewise draw CXCR4-positive cancer cells from the primary lesion to distant metastatic lesion [22–24]. Thus, blocking the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis by siCXCR4 can not only reverse the immunosuppressive microenvironment, but also inhibit TNBC invasion and metastasis.

Hydroxycamptothecin (HCPT) is a cytotoxic drug that induces cell apoptosis by inducing DNA fragmentation [25]. In particular, HCPT is also able to induce ICD of cancer cell to release cancer antigens [26]. Therefore, co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 to orthotopic TNBC tissue will develop an in situ cancer vaccine with the synergistical effect. However, HCPT is insoluble in lipid and water [27, 28], and naked siCXCR4 is difficult to effectively enter target cells due to its negative charge and poor stability in blood circulation [29–32]. In this study, calcium phosphate nanoparticles (CaP) were used as the carrier material. Through the complexation of Ca2+ with the lactone ring-opened HCPT and hyaluronic acid (HA), as well as the electrostatic adsorption of Ca2+ with siCXCR4, siCXCR4 and HCPT were efficiently co-loaded in a co-precipitation manner, successfully preparing an in situ cancer nanovaccine HCPT/S@CaP/HA. HCPT/S@CaP/HA actively accumulated in orthotopic TNBC tissue, utilized the TNBC antigen to activate adaptive immune responses, and simultaneously reversed the immunosuppressive microenvironment in TNBC tissue, spleen tissue and lung tissue, inhibiting the growth, recurrence and metastasis of orthotopic TNBC (Fig. 1).

Results and discussion

Activation of CXCL12/CXCR4 axis promoted the proliferation, migration and invasion of 4T1 cells

CAFs are an important component of the tumor stroma. CAFs play a role in multiple key processes of cancer, including extracellular matrix (ECM) formation, tumor cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, and immunosuppression, and have a profound impact on tumor development. In tumor tissues, CAFs are the main source of CXCL12 secretion, and when they bind to the CXCR4 on the surface of tumor cells, they can activate downstream signaling pathways such as phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K), phospholipase C (PLC), Janus kinase/signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK/STAT), and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), participating in intracellular calcium mobilization, cell adhesion, migration, stem cell homing, and the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment [33, 34]. The immunofluorescence staining results demonstrated that α-SMA and FAP1 were highly expressed in orthotopic TNBC tissue of mice (Fig. 2A), suggesting there were a large amount of CAFs in TNBC tissue. When NIH-3T3 cells were cultured with 4T1 cell-conditioned medium (4T1-CM), the expression of α-SMA, FAP1 and PDGFRβ in NIH-3T3 cells significantly increased, indicating that 4T1 cells could induce NIH-3T3 cells to transform into CAFs (Fig. 2B-D) [35, 36]. CXCR4 was highly expressed in 4T1 cells, while the expression of CXCR4 in NIH-3T3 cells and CAFs is extremely low. Additionally, the expression and secretion of CXCL12 was markedly higher in CAFs than in NIH-3T3 cells (Fig. 2E, Fig.S1). These results demonstrated that the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis between CAFs and TNBC cells was activated in TNBC tissues.

MTT assay indicated that NIH-3T3 cells conditioned medium (NIH-3T3-CM) did not significantly affect the proliferation of 4T1 cells. However, CAFs conditioned medium (CAFs-CM) markedly enhanced the proliferation of 4T1 cells. At the present of CXCL12 neutralizing antibody, the effect of CAFs-CM on the proliferation of 4T1 cells was reduced (Fig. 2F), suggesting that CXCL12 secreted by CAFs played an important role in promoting the proliferation of 4T1 cells. Furthermore, after co-culturing 4T1 cells with CAFs, there was a marked enhancement in the migration and invasion of the 4T1 cells. When the CXCR4 antibody was used to block the CXCR4 on the surface of 4T1 cells in donor chamber, the invasion and migration abilities of 4T1 cells were significantly inhibited. While CXCL12 antibody was added to the recipient chamber of transwell, the invasion and migration abilities of 4T1 cells was also significantly decreased (Fig. 2G, Fig.S2). The aforementioned experimental results demonstrated that overexpression of CXCR4 in 4T1 cells and CXCL12 secreted by CAFs activated the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis, thereby promoting the proliferation, migration and invasion of 4T1 cells.

Preparation and characterization of HCPT/S@CaP/HA

CaP are excellent delivery carriers for small interfering RNA, featuring high transfection efficiency, good biocompatibility and biodegradability [37]. The lactone ring in HCPT can be opened under alkaline conditions, exposing carboxyl groups which is capable of binding Ca2+. Therefore, the lactone ring-opened HCPT can be compressed in the CaP core through complexation with Ca2+. The CaP loaded with lactone ring-opened HCPT is acidified in lysosomes to form the lactone-type HCPT with high anti-cancer activity. In this study, CaP was used as the carrier to simultaneously load siCXCR4 and HCPT. However, CaP tends to aggregation and instability. To obtain stable CaP, a stabilizer is needed. Hyaluronic acid (HA), a natural anionic polysaccharide existing in the body, contains a large number of carboxyl groups that can bind Ca2+. Therefore, HA has the potential ability in stabilizing the CaP (Fig. 3A).

The results of the agarose gel electrophoresis retardation assay indicated that siCXCR4 was completely loaded in CaP when the Ca/P ratio was 12.5 (Fig. 3B). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) revealed that HCPT, CaP and HA were comprised in HCPT/S@CaP/HA (Fig.S3). The average particle size and zeta potential of HCPT/S@CaP/HA was about 268 nm and − 20.4 mV, respectively (Fig. 3C, Fig.S4). Notably, the content of HCPT, particle size and PDI of HCPT/S@CaP/HA exhibited no significant changes in PBS for 6 days (Fig. 3D, Fig.S5). The naked siCXCR4 underwent substantial degradation at 8 h at the presence of 10% FBS, HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively protected siCXCR4 from degradation over a period of 24 h (Fig. 3E). Additionally, siCXCR4 and HCPT were released from HCPT/S@CaP/HA to a greater extent in an acidic environment (Fig. 3F-G). After incubation HCPT/S@CaP/HA with pH 5.0 buffer for 4 h, the distinct needle-like crystals were observed (Fig. 3H). Notably, lactone-type HCPT also exhibited similar needle-like crystallization under the same pH conditions (Fig.S6). The above results indicated that in acidic environment, when the lactone ring-opened HCPT was released from HCPT/S@CaP/HA, it transformed into the lactone-type HCPT. The solubility of the lactone-type HCPT is very low, thus HCPT crystals formed in the acidic release medium. At last, the gene silencing effect of HCPT/S@CaP/HA was detected by western blot. The inhibitory effect of HCPT/S@CaP/HA on CXCR4 expression in 4T1 cells was comparable to that of the positive control group siCXCR4@lipofectamine 2000 (Fig.S7).

HCPT/S@CaP/HA was internalized by 4T1 cells and CAFs via CD44

CD44, as a membrane receptor, interacts with HA and mediates its endocytosis. Therefore, by modifying nanoparticles with HA, the uptake of nanoparticles can be increased. Immunofluorescence staining results demonstrated that CD44 was not only highly expressed in 4T1 cells but was also expressed in NIH-3T3 cells and CAFs (Fig.S8A-C). After co-culture of 4T1 cells and CAFs with HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA, the fluorescence intensity in 4T1 cells and CAFs increased significantly over time (Fig.S8D-G), indicating that HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA was effectively internalized by both 4T1 cells and CAFs. However, with the application of HA pre-treatment, there was a significant reduction in the fluorescence intensity in both 4T1 cells and CAFs (Fig.S8H-I). Furthermore, the amount of HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA in NIH-3T3 cells was significantly lower than that in CAFs (Fig.S9). Compared with 4T1 cells, the uptake of HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA by MDA-MB-453 cells (with little CD44 expression) was significantly reduced (Fig.S10). This difference was attributed to the higher expression level of CD44 in 4T1 cells and CAFs relative to those in NIH-3T3 cells and MDA-MB-453 cells. The above data indicated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA was internalized by 4T1 cells and CAFs via CD44 mediated endocytosis.

HCPT/S@CaP/HA inhibited the growth of 4T1 cells and CAFs

HCPT/S@CaP/HA markedly decreased the survival rate, the ratio of live/dead cells and the number of cell clones of 4T1 cells (Fig.S11). In addition, the survival rate of CAFs was significantly decreased by HCPT/S@CaP/HA (Fig.S12). HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly increased the expression of Bax and Cleaved caspase 3, while reduced the expression of Bcl-2 in 4T1 cells and CAFs (Fig.S13). Collectively, these findings suggested that HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively inhibited the growth of 4T1 cells and CAFs by activating the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway.

HCPT/S@CaP/HA inhibited the migration and invasion of 4T1 cells by bidirectionally blocking the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis

The overexpression of CXCR4 activates the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis, leading to epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in 4T1 cells. This process endows the cancer cells with migratory and invasive phenotypes. Furthermore, the overexpression of matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) in 4T1 cells facilitates the degradation of extracellular matrix components, promoting the detachment of cancer cells from the primary site and their subsequent metastasis to distant organs [38]. S@CaP/HA and HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively silenced the expression of CXCR4 in 4T1 cells (Fig. 4A). Additionally, HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly increased the expression of E-cadherin and reduced the expression of N-cadherin and MMP-9 (Fig.S14). Furthermore, among all treatment groups, HCPT/S@CaP/HA exhibited the strongest inhibitory effect on 4T1 cell migration and invasion (Fig. 4B, Fig.S15). In TNBC tissues, CAFs secrete CXCL12, which activates the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis, thereby promoting the migration and invasion of TNBC cells. HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly reduced the secretion of CXCL12 from CAFs (Fig. 4C). At the present of CAFs, HCPT/S@CaP/HA also showed the greatest inhibitory effect on the migration and invasion of 4T1 cells (Fig. 4D, Fig.S16). These findings demonstrated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA decreased CXCR4 expression in 4T1 cell while inhibited the secretion of CXCL12 from CAF. Subsequently, the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis was bidirectionally disrupted, enhancing its inhibitory effect of HCPT/S@CaP/HA on the migration and invasion of 4T1 cells at the present of CAFs.

HCPT/S@CaP/HA promoted DC cell maturation in vitro

When cancer cells experience ICD, a series of signaling molecules known as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) are generated, mainly encompassing the translocation of calreticulin (CRT) from the intracellular compartment to the cell membrane and the secretion of high-mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) by cancer cells [39]. The release of ATP occurs during the “pre-death stage” of cell death, earlier than the release of HMGB1, overlapping with the exposure of CRT to some extent. It is the first signal that initiates the immune response and fills the temporal gap between CRT (min-level) and HMGB1 (>12 h). The three together constitute the characteristic triad of ICD, namely the phagocytosis-recruitment-activation signal cascade [40]. HCPT/S@CaP/HA increased the expression of CRT, the secretion of HMGB1 and release of ATP from 4T1 cells (Fig. 4.E-G), indicating HCPT/S@CaP/HA induced ICD of 4T1 cells, subsequently promoting the release of TNBC antigen.

CRT, as an “eat me” signal, binds to the CD97 receptor on the surface of DCs, facilitating the phagocytosis of cancer antigens by DCs [41]. HMGB1 activates its downstream signaling pathway by binding to TLR4 on DCs cell surface, activating the immune system [42]. Thereby, CRT and HMGB1 are able to enhance the recognition and capture of cancer antigens by DCs and promote the maturation of DCs. Subsequently, the expression of costimulatory molecules CD80 and CD86 on the surface of mature DCs is upregulated, which activated T cell-mediated immune responses. HCPT/S@CaP/HA obviously improved the expression of CD80 and CD86 on the surface of DCs (Fig. 4H-I, Fig.S17), suggesting that HCPT/S@CaP/HA had a superior ability to promote the maturation of DCs. As demonstrated in Fig. 4J, co-culture with HCPT/S@CaP/HA-pretreated 4T1 cells significantly augmented IL-12 secretion by DCs, indicating that 4T1 cells preconditioning with HCPT/S@CaP/HA promoted DC functional maturation, thereby stimulating robust production of IL-12. The above results indicated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA strongly induced ICD of 4T1 cells, promoted the release of TNBC antigens and subsequent maturation of DCs in vitro [43].

Biodistribution of HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA

After being stimulated with 4T1-CM for 48 h, the expression of CD44 in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) was significantly increased (Fig.S18A), suggesting HCPT/S@CaP/HA could effectively recognize and bind to neovascularization within the TNBC tissue, subsequently facilitated preferential accumulation in TNBC tissue. The results of in vivo imaging revealed that the fluorescence intensity in TNBC tissues was weak while the kidneys exhibited the strong fluorescence intensity after administration of cy5-siCXCR4 + HCPT. However, the fluorescence intensity in orthotopic TNBC tissues was significantly strengthened after administration of HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA, indicating cy5-siCXCR4 + HCPT was rapidly eliminated through the kidneys after administration, and HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA was capable of selectively accumulating in the orthotopic TNBC tissues. Besides, compared with HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA, the accumulation of HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/BSA in orthotopic TNBC tissues was significantly reduced (Fig.S18B-E), indicating that HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA actively targeted TNBC tissues through CD44 mediation. Moreover, TNBC tissue mainly consists of 4T1 cells and CAFs. In the co-localization images, the green fluorescence of HCPT overlaps with the red fluorescence of α-SMA, indicating that HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA delivered drug to CAFs. Additionally, in the TNBC tissue, there is also green fluorescence that did not overlap with the red fluorescence of α-SMA (Fig.S18F), suggesting that HCPT/cy5-S@CaP/HA also delivered the drug to the TNBC cells.

HCPT/S@CaP/HA inhibited the growth of orthotopic TNBC by bidirectionally blocking CXCL12/CXCR4 axis

HCPT showed a weak inhibitory effect on the growth of orthotopic TNBC in mice. This was attributed to the low accumulation of HCPT in orthotopic TNBC tissue. S@CaP/HA inhibited the growth of TNBC cells by silencing the expression of CXCR4 in TNBC cells. HCPT/S@CaP/HA showed the strongest inhibitory effect on the growth of orthotopic TNBC while slowed down body weight loss of orthotopic TNBC mice (Fig. 5A-C, Fig.S19-20). Compared with the control group, the orthotopic TNBC weight in the HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA and S@CaP/HA group decreased by 72.45%, 35.05%, and 34.91% respectively. This indicated that the co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically enhanced the inhibitory effect on the growth of orthotopic TNBC.

In HCPT/S@CaP/HA treatment group, a considerable amount of nuclear pyknosis and cancer cell dispersion were observed in orthotopic TNBC tissue, suggesting HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively killed orthotopic TNBC cells. In addition, the number of TUNEL positive cells was significantly increased while Ki67 positive cells was obviously reduced in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treatment group (Fig. 5D). These results indicated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly promoted the apoptosis and inhibited the proliferation of orthotopic TNBC cells, thereby inhibiting the growth of orthotopic TNBC.

During the growth of cancer, normal fibroblasts are recruited to the vicinity of cancer cells and are transformed into CAFs under the induction of related cytokines secreted by cancer cells [44]. The CXCL12 secreted by CAFs interacts with the CXCR4 receptors on cancer cells, activating the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis and subsequently accelerating cell cycle progression and promoting the proliferation of cancer cells [45]. The activation of CXCL12/CXCR4 axis can also increase the expression of anti-apoptotic proteins and inhibit the apoptosis of cancer cells [46]. HCPT/S@CaP/HA markedly enhanced the expression of Bax and Cleaved caspase 3 and reduced the expression of Bcl-2 in orthotopic TNBC tissue (Fig.S21).

The results of immunofluorescence staining and western blot revealed that the expression of CXCR4, α-SMA, FAP1 and CXCL12 were abundant in orthotopic TNBC tissues in model group, while they were significantly reduced in HCPT/S@CaP/HA group (Fig. 5E-G, Fig.S22). ELISA experimental results indicated that CXCL12 levels were decreased in orthotopic TNBC tissues and serum in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treated mice (Fig. 5H-I). CXCL12/CXCR4 axis induces angiogenesis in cancer tissue [47]. The expression of vascular marker CD31 in orthotopic TNBC tissues in the HCPT/S@CaP/HA group was markedly attenuated (Fig.S23). These results demonstrated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA not only reduced the secretion of CXCL12 from CAFs but also silenced the expression of CXCR4 in orthotopic TNBC tissues, thereby bidirectional blocking the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis.

HCPT/S@CaP/HA induced anti-cancer immune responses in orthotopic TNBC mice

The expression of CRT and HMGB1 in orthotopic TNBC tissues was significantly increased in HCPT/N@CaP/HA and HCPT/S@CaP/HA treatment mice (Fig. 6A-C). Besides, HCPT/S@CaP/HA was found to significantly promote the content of ATP in orthotopic TNBC tissues (Fig. 6D). Meanwhile, HCPT/N@CaP/HA markedly increased the proportion of CD11c+CD80+CD86+ DCs (matured DCs, mDCs) and CD3+CD8+IFN-γ+ T lymphocytes (cytotoxic T lymphocytes) in orthotopic TNBC tissue (Fig. 6E-F, Fig.S24). Compared with the control group, the percentage of cytotoxic T lymphocytes in the HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA and S@CaP/HA group increased by 3.62%, 2.33% and 0.85% respectively. This indicated that the co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically increased the percentage of cytotoxic T lymphocytes in orthotopic TNBC tissues of mice. These results indicated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA induced ICD of TNBC cells, promoted the maturation of DCs, and increased the infiltration of cytotoxic T lymphocytes in orthotopic TNBC tissue. In addition, HCPT/N@CaP/HA significantly increased M1-type tumor associated macrophages (M1-TAMs) and reduced the number of M2-type tumor associated macrophages (M2-TAMs) in orthotopic TNBC tissues (Fig. 6G-I, Fig.S24-25). The ratio of M1-TAMs to M2-TAMs in the orthotopic TNBC tissues in HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA, S@CaP/HA and the control group was 10.9, 5.0, 1.9 and 0.4, respectively. This indicated that the co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically increased the ratio of M1-TAMs to M2-TAMs in the orthotopic TNBC tissues. At the same time, HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly decreased the number of neutrophils (Ly6G+ cells) and polymorphonuclear-MDSCs (CD11b+Gr-1+, PMN-MDSCs) in the orthotopic TNBC tissues (Fig.S26-27). Moreover, HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly increased the levels of TNF-α, IFN-γ and IL-15, and reduced the levels of IL-6 and IL-10 in orthotopic TNBC tissue and peripheral blood of orthotopic TNBC mice (Fig. 6J-N, Fig.S28). Compared with the control group, the content of TNF-α in orthotopic TNBC tissues of mice in HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA and S@CaP/HA group increased by 27.87 pg/mg, 12.8 pg/mg and 5.91 pg/mg, respectively. At the same time, the content of IFN-γ in HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA and S@CaP/HA group respectively increased by 66.65 pg/mg, 39.29 pg/mg and 23.12 pg/mg compared to the control group. This indicated that the co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically increased the content of TNF-α and IFN-γ in orthotopic TNBC tissues of mice.

The above results demonstrated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively induced ICD of orthotopic TNBC cells, released TNBC antigens and improved the presentation and processing ability of DCs to TNBC antigens, thereby generating TNBC specific T cell immune responses. The CAFs in TNBC tissue typically secrete a large amount of CXCL12, forming a chemical concentration gradient. Immune-suppressive cells (such MDSCs, and TAMs) located around the tumor or in the blood vessels sense the CXCL12 concentration gradient spreading outward from the tumor core through their surface CXCR4 receptors, and are attracted and migrate into the tumor interior, thereby suppressing the anti-tumor immune response and promoting tumor growth and metastasis. After binding to CXCL12, it activates the G protein signaling pathway within the immune-suppressive cells, leading to cytoskeleton reorganization, causing the cell front (towards the tumor) to form pseudopods. The immune-suppressive cells move along the direction of increasing CXCL12 concentration (i.e., towards the tumor tissue) through chemotaxis and accumulate in the tumor area [48, 49]. CXCL12 secreted by CAFs can polarize TAMs into M2-TAMs. M2-TAMs further activate CAFs and form an immunosuppressive circuit. HCPT/S@CaP/HA decreased the secretion of CXCL12 from CAFs, then subsequently decreased recruitment of neutrophils, synergistically increased the proportion of M1-TAMs to M2-TAMs and finally reversed the immunosuppressive microenvironment in orthotopic TNBC tissue. Thus, the killing effect of cytotoxic T lymphocytes on TNBC cells was enhanced.

HCPT/S@CaP/HA inhibited the growth of distant TNBC by inducing immune memory effect in TNBC mice

A cancer re-challenge model (Fig.S29) was established to further investigate the immune memory effects induced by HCPT/S@CaP/HA. The results showed that the inhibitory effect of HCPT/S@CaP/HA on the growth of distant TNBC was significantly strengthened as compared with HCPT/N@CaP/HA and S@CaP/HA (Fig. 7A-F). Besides, a large number of karyopyknosis and cancer cell lysis were observed in the distant TNBC tissues in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treated TNBC re-challenge mice. The number of TUNEL positive cells significantly increased and the number of Ki67 positive cells obviously decreased in the distant TNBC tissues in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treated TNBC re-challenge mice (Fig. 7G), indicating HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively promoted the apoptosis and inhibited the proliferation of distant TNBC cells, thereby inhibiting the growth of distant TNBC.

Firstly, the immune microenvironment of distant TNBC tissue was investigated. After treatment with HCPT/S@CaP/HA, CD44+CD62L+ T lymphocytes (memory T lymphocytes), CD3+CD4+IFN-γ+ T lymphocytes (type 1 helper T lymphocytes, Th1 cells) and CD3+CD8+IFN-γ+ T lymphocytes (cytotoxic T lymphocytes) were significantly increased in the distant TNBC tissue (Fig.S30-32), indicating that memory T lymphocytes, Th1 cells and cytotoxic T lymphocytes infiltrated into the distant TNBC tissue. Compared with the control group, the number of cytotoxic T lymphocytes in HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA and S@CaP/HA increased by 12.4%, 6.14% and 1.12%, respectively. This indicated that the co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically increased the percentage of cytotoxic T lymphocytes in the distant TNBC tissues of mice. Notably, CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ T lymphocytes (regulatory T lymphocytes) were significantly reduced, while the proportion of F4/80+CD86+ TAMs (M1-TAMs) to F4/80+CD206+ TAMs (M2-TAMs) was significantly increased in the distant TNBC tissue in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treated TNBC re-challenge mice (Fig.S33-34). The ratio of M1-TAMs to M2-TAMs in distant TNBC tissues in the HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA, S@CaP/HA and the control group was 1.51, 0.83, 0.59 and 0.37, respectively. This indicated that the co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically increased the ratio of M1-TAMs to M2-TAMs in the distant TNBC tissues. The above data demonstrated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA induced the significant immune memory effect and anti-tumor immune response, as well as reversed the immunosuppressive microenvironment of the distant TNBC tissue and.

Secondly, the formation of immune memory and immune microenvironment in TNBC re-challenge mice was studied. When the anti-cancer immune response is over, most cytotoxic T lymphocytes are suppressed or eliminated, and only 5–10% of cytotoxic T lymphocytes survive and become memory T cells, which exist in the body for a long time. Upon re-invasion of cancer antigens, memory T cells are awakened, rapidly proliferate and differentiate into cytotoxic T lymphocytes with killing effects. Memory T lymphocytes have lymphatic homing ability [50]. The proportion of CD44+CD62L+ T lymphocytes (memory T lymphocytes) in the spleen tissue was significantly increased in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treated TNBC re-challenge mice (Fig. 7H, Fig.S35). Compared with the model group, the percentage of memory T lymphocytes in the spleen tissues of mice in the HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA and S@CaP/HA group increased by 6.53%, 3.18% and 1.26%, respectively. This indicated that the co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically increased the percentage of memory T lymphocytes in the spleen tissue of mice. Moreover, type 1 helper T lymphocytes (Th1 cell) activate CD8+T cells to induce cellular immunity [51]. Spleen is the main site of Th1 cell enrichment. HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly increased the proportion of CD3+CD4+IFN-γ+ T lymphocytes (Th1 cells) and CD3+CD8+IFN-γ+ T lymphocytes (cytotoxic T lymphocytes) in the spleen of TNBC re-challenge mice (Fig. 7I-J, Fig.S36-37). These results indicated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively induced the formation of immune memory in TNBC re-challenge mice. When TNBC cells reinvade, memory T lymphocytes were activated and differentiate into cytotoxic T lymphocytes with the assistance of Th1 cells to kill distant TNBC cells.

In addition, HCPT/S@CaP/HA obviously decreased the CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ T lymphocytes (regulatory T lymphocytes) in spleen tissue in TNBC re-challenge mice (Fig. 7K, Fig.S38). At the same time, HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly elevated the proportion of CD11c+CD80+CD86+ cells (matured DCs) and the ratio of F4/80+CD86+ macrophages (M1-TAMs) to F4/80+CD206+ macrophages (M2-TAMs) in spleen tissue in TNBC re-challenge mice (Fig.S39-40). The ratio of M1-TAMs to M2-TAMs in spleen of TNBC re-challenge mice in the HCPT/S@CaP/HA, HCPT/N@CaP/HA, S@CaP/HA and the control group was 12.68, 3.37, 2.39 and 0.24, respectively. This indicated that the co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically increased the ratio of M1-TAMs to M2-TAMs in the spleen of TNBC re-challenge mice. The above results indicated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA reversed the immunosuppressive microenvironment in spleen tissue in TNBC re-challenge mice, which was beneficial to the differentiation of memory T cells into cytotoxic T lymphocytes and the anti-TNBC immune response induced by cytotoxic T lymphocytes.

In order to verify whether these responses are tumor antigen-specific, the tumor antigen-specific T cell content detection kit was used to detect the number of effector tumor antigen-specific T cells (ETAST) in spleen tissue in TNBC re-challenge mice. Compared with the immune cells in spleen from TNBC re-challenge mice without stimulation, after the immune cells in the spleen from TNBC re-challenge mice were stimulated with the 4T1 cell antigen, the numbers of CD3+IFN-γ+ T lymphocytes, CD3+CD4+IFN-γ+ T lymphocytes, and CD3+CD8+IFN-γ+ T lymphocytes significantly increased (Fig. 7L-N, Fig.S41), suggesting that the 4T1 cell antigen induced memory T cells and Th1 cells to transform into cytotoxic T lymphocytes. The above research results indicate that the immune response induced by HCPT/S@CaP/HA has specificity for the 4T1 cell antigen.

All above results demonstrated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA successfully inhibited the recurrence of TNBC by inducing immune memory, activating TNBC-specific immune response and reversing the immunosuppressive microenvironment of the distant TNBC tissue.

In addition, the metastasis foci of TNBC (the area pointed by the black arrow and pan-CK positive cells) was significantly reduced in the lung tissue in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treated mice (Fig. 8E). These results indicated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively inhibited spontaneous metastasis of orthotopic TNBC cells to the lung.

The inferior iliac lymph node outside the fourth pair of nipples was the closest lymph node to the orthotopic TNBC, which was the sentinel lymph node (SLN) of the nipple. The results showed that the SLN size and mass in HCPT/N@CaP/HA and S@CaP/HA groups were significantly reduced (Fig.S46A-B). TNBC cells can metastasize to ipsilateral axillary lymph nodes (IALN) through SLN, resulting in IALN inflammation and enlargement. The size and weight of IALN in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treated mice were significantly declined (Fig.S46C-D). In addition, there were a large number of TNBC cells (pan-CK positive cells) with pathological mitotic in the SLN and IALN in model group. The number of TNBC cells in SLN and IALN was significantly reduced in HCPT/S@CaP/HA treated mice (Fig. 9B). These results indicated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively inhibited spontaneous lymphatic metastasis of orthotopic TNBC cells. In addition, cDC1s is a subtype of DCs that can effectively process and cross present cancer antigens. cDC1s can migrate to SLN, activate CD8+ T lymphocytes, and induce cancer antigen-specific immune responses. The immunofluorescence staining results showed that HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly increased the infiltration of CD11c+CD103+ DCs (cDC1s) in SLN, indicating that HCPT/S@CaP/HA promoted the presentation of cancer antigens, effectively initiate immune responses in SLN, and subsequently reduced TNBC cells in LSN in spontaneous lung metastatic TNBC mice (Fig. 9C). Furthermore, HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly increased the cytotoxic T lymphocytes and the proportion of M1 macrophages to M2 macrophages in the spleen of spontaneous lung metastatic TNBC mice (Fig. 9D-G, Fig.S47-48), suggesting HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively activated the systemic adaptive immune responses in spontaneous lung metastatic TNBC mice.

HCPT/S@CaP/HA significantly increased the expression of E-cadherin and decreased the expression of N-cadherin and MMP-9 in orthotopic TNBC tissue (Fig.S49), indicating HCPT/S@CaP/HA inhibited the EMT of orthotopic TNBC cells and the degradation of extracellular matrix, ultimately reduced the migration and invasion ability of orthotopic TNBC cells.

Usually, normal fibroblasts in lung tissue were induced into the CAFs by tumor derived secreted factors (TDSFs) [52, 53]. CAFs in lung tissue and orthotopic TNBC tissue secrete CXCL12, subsequently activates the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis between orthotopic TNBC tissue and lung tissue, which induces lung metastasis of orthotopic TNBC cells through the following three ways. (1) The adhesion of orthotopic TNBC cells is attenuated and orthotopic TNBC cells acquire the invasion and migration characteristics by inducing EMT in TNBC cells [54]. Over-secreted CXCL12 also reduces tight junctions of endothelial cells, increasing vascular permeability [55] and facilitating orthotopic TNBC cells to penetrate the blood vessels and enter the circulation. (2) The disseminated TNBC cells escape from the surveillance and clearance of cytotoxic immune cells because of the formation of systemic immunosuppression [56, 57]. (3) MDSCs and monocytes is recruited to the lung tissue, inducing the differentiation of monocytes into M2-macrophages, inhibiting the activity of cytotoxic T lymphocytes to form an immunosuppressive pre-metastatic niche in the lung tissue, which provides fertile soil for the colonization of TNBC cells [58–60].

The above results demonstrated that HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively inhibited spontaneous lung metastasis of orthotopic TNBC through directly killing orthotopic TNBC cells, inhibiting the EMT of orthotopic TNBC cells, promoting the presentation of cancer antigens, activating the systemic adaptive immune response to clear TNBC cells in the metastatic process, remodeling the immune microenvironment in lung tissue and blocking attraction of lung tissue toward orthotopic TNBC cells through the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis.

Safety evaluation in vivo

The number of white blood cells (WBCs) and platelet (PLTs) in the blood decreased significantly in mice treated with free HCPT, while the number of WBCs and PLTs in the blood did not change significantly in mice treated with HCPT/S@CaP/HA (Fig.S50A-B), indicating HCPT/S@CaP/HA was able to reduce the side effects caused by free HCPT. In addition, the level of creatine kinase (CK)、lactic dehydrogenase (LDH)、alanine aminotransferase (ALT)、aspartate aminotransferase (AST)、blood urea nitrogen (BUN)、creatinine (CREA) and uric acid (UA) in the blood was within normal in mice treated with HCPT/S@CaP/HA (Fig.S50C), and there was no obvious histopathological changes in brain, heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney in mice treated with HCPT/S@CaP/HA (Fig.S51), suggesting HCPT/S@CaP/HA did not cause damage to the main organs at the therapeutic dosage in mice.

Conclusions

In this study, an in situ cancer nanovaccine HCPT/S@CaP/HA was successfully prepared. With the guidance of HA, HCPT/S@CaP/HA actively targeted to TNBC cells and CAFs by sequentially recognizing the highly expressed CD44 in the tumor neovascularization, TNBC cells and CAFs in TNBC tissue. HCPT induced ICD of orthotopic TNBC cells to promote the maturation of DCs and activate T cell-mediated anti-cancer immune response. Meanwhile, HCPT/S@CaP/HA reduced the secretion of CXCL12 from CAFs and the expression of CXCR4 in orthotopic TNBC tissue. Subsequently, CXCL12/CXCR4 axis was bidirectionally blocked, resulting in the synergistical reversion of the immunosuppressive microenvironment in orthotopic TNBC tissues by reducing recruitment of neutrophils and increasing the proportion of M1-TAMs to M2-TAMs. Consequently, growth of orthotopic TNBC was inhibited synergistically. Moreover, HCPT/S@CaP/HA synergistically activated memory T cells in the spleen, reactivating the TNBC-specific immune response, resulting in the blocking of the recurrence of TNBC. Furthermore, HCPT/S@CaP/HA reversed CXCL12/CXCR4 axis-driven immunosuppression in orthotopic TNBC tissue and lung tissue, while promoted the presentation of cancer antigens and activated adaptive immune responses throughout the body in spontaneous lung metastatic model. Consequently, the number of M2 macrophages and MDSCs was significantly decreased and the number of CD8+ T cells was increased in lung tissue, which markedly destroyed the fertile soil of TNBC cell colonization in lung and thus reduced lung metastasis of TNBC cells. In summary, co-delivery of HCPT and siCXCR4 by HCPT/S@CaP/HA effectively induced ICD of orthotopic TNBC cells, promoted DCs maturation, synergistically activated local and systemic anti-cancer immune responses, ultimately inhibited the growth, recurrence and lung metastasis of TNBC.

This study provides a new strategy for immunotherapy of TNBC, but still have some limitations. (1) Although HCPT/S@CaP/HA had been proven effective in in situ TNBC model, TNBC re-challenge model, and in situ spontaneous lung metastasis models of TNBC, its role in liver metastasis, brain metastasis, and bone metastasis of TNBC had not been evaluated. (2) Although HCPT/S@CaP/HA did not cause obvious damage to the main organs in mice at the therapeutic dosage, its toxicity still needs to be studied in other experimental animals such as dogs at higher doses. (3) It is necessary to conduct thorough validation of the effectiveness of HCPT/S@CaP/HA in other solid tumor models (such as gastric cancer, liver cancer, colon cancer, etc.) to expand the application scope of HCPT/S@CaP/HA.

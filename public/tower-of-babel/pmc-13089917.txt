Saruparib in combination with androgen receptor pathway inhibitors in metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer: EvoPAR-Prostate01
Authors: Arun A. Azad, Neeraj Agarwal, Andrew J. Armstrong, Eva Hellmis, Mikio Sugimoto, Yüksel Ürün, Nianzeng Xing, Mehreteab Aregay, Julianne Lima, Didier Meulendijks, Kim N. Chi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPoly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor plus androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (ARPI) is approved for selected patients with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer. Saruparib (AZD5305) is a new-generation PARP inhibitor that selectively inhibits and traps PARP1. EvoPAR-Prostate01 is a Phase III, 2-cohort, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter study evaluating the efficacy and safety of saruparib plus ARPIs in participants with histologically confirmed metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) with and without confirmed, prospectively defined, homologous recombination repair gene mutations (HRRm). Participants must receive androgen deprivation therapy throughout the study, or have undergone bilateral orchiectomy, and be suitable for ARPI. Key exclusion criteria include prior PARP inhibitor, prior chemotherapy or ARPI in the mHSPC setting (prior ARPI for localized disease is permitted), and history of/suspected myelodysplastic syndrome/acute myeloid leukemia. Approximately 1800 participants (550 HRRm; 1250 non-HRRm) are randomized 1:1 to receive either saruparib plus physician’s choice of ARPI (abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone, darolutamide, or enzalutamide) or placebo plus ARPI. Treatment beyond disease progression and crossover between cohorts are not permitted. The primary endpoint is radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS); overall survival (OS) is a key secondary endpoint. Analyses of rPFS and OS will be conducted within each cohort by stratified log-rank test. Enrollment began in November 2023.Clinical trial registration: www.clinicaltrials.gov identifier is NCT06120491; EU CT number is 2023-504214-30-00.

Introduction

Current treatment approaches for metastatic prostate cancer

Prostate cancer is the second most commonly diagnosed cancer in men, and in 2022, it was the 5th most common cause of cancer-related death in men worldwide, accounting for 396,792 deaths [1]. In low- and middle-income countries, up to 43% of patients present with metastatic (stage IV) disease at initial diagnosis [2–4]. In high-income countries, up to 18% of patients with prostate cancer are diagnosed at the metastatic stage [5–9], and an additional 2–3% of patients develop metastatic disease after relapse of previously treated disease [10,11].

Inhibition of testosterone release through androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) is the mainstay of treatment for patients with metastatic prostate cancer and may comprise gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonists or antagonists or bilateral orchiectomy [12]. Intensification of systemic therapy is recommended for the treatment of metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC), and clinical practice guidelines recommend ADT in combination with androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPIs) with or without docetaxel, as well as external beam radiation therapy to the primary tumor in selected cases [13,14]. ARPIs downregulate androgen signaling through a variety of mechanisms, including androgen receptor (AR) antagonism and/or the inhibition of steroid hormone biogenesis [15]. The addition of ARPI (with or without chemotherapy) to ADT has been shown to improve survival outcomes in mHSPC [16–19], and apalutamide, enzalutamide, abiraterone plus prednisone, and darolutamide are approved in the US for the treatment of mHSPC in combination with ADT [20–23]. Several potential mechanisms are involved in the progression to metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) following ADT or ARPI treatment. These include AR gene amplification or upregulation of AR expression, occurring in approximately 40% of patients with mCRPC [24]. Additional mechanisms of resistance can involve structural alterations in the AR, such as point mutations in the ligand binding domain, which allow promiscuous activation of the receptor by alternate ligands, and splice variants that lack the ligand binding domain and remain constitutively active in a ligand-independent manner [24–26].

Progression to mCRPC is associated with poor survival outcomes [27]. In the clinical trial setting, median overall survival (OS) in patients with chemotherapy-naive mCRPC has ranged from approximately 2.5–3.5 years [28–30]. Real-world studies in US patients with mCRPC have reported a median OS of approximately 2 years from diagnosis [31,32]; however, recent data from patients in Europe suggest that therapeutic advances have improved real-world OS in patients with metastatic prostate cancer, and the median OS in this population was reported to be approximately 3 years [33]. Nevertheless, there remains a clear and urgent need for more effective treatment of patients with mHSPC that can delay disease progression to mCRPC.

PARP inhibitor plus ARPI for the treatment of metastatic prostate cancer

The enzymatic activity of poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) facilitates the repair of single-strand DNA breaks through the recruitment of DNA repair proteins to sites of damage [34]. PARP inhibitors trap PARP1 at single-strand breaks and result in the formation of toxic double-strand DNA breaks during DNA replication [35]. Tumor cells with pathogenic/likely pathogenic mutations in genes associated with homologous recombination repair (HRR) have compromised ability to repair double-strand breaks via the HRR pathway and are particularly sensitive to PARP inhibitor treatment [35].

Among patients with mCRPC, mutations in the HRR genes BRCA1, BRCA2, and ATM are associated with poor prognosis [36,37]. However, the Phase III PROfound trial (NCT02987543) demonstrated statistically significant improvements in radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) and OS with the PARP inhibitor olaparib versus treatment of physician’s choice of ARPI (enzalutamide or abiraterone plus prednisone) in patients with heavily pretreated mCRPC with at least 1 mutation in BRCA1, BRCA2, or ATM [38,39]. Post hoc analyses demonstrated the potential for greater benefit with olaparib in earlier lines of therapy [40], and the potential of PARP inhibitors to provide a greater magnitude of benefit in earlier lines of treatment has also been demonstrated in other indications [41–43].

When combined with ARPIs, PARP inhibitors have demonstrated anticancer efficacy in preclinical prostate cancer models both with and without HRR gene mutations (HRRm) [44–46], which may be explained by the following molecular mechanisms: AR activity is important in facilitating DNA repair in prostate cancer cells, as previously reported [47], and recruitment of nuclear AR to chromatin in response to DNA damage is supported by PARP1 activity [46]. PARP inhibitors trap PARP1 at single-strand DNA breaks and result in the accumulation of DNA damage in prostate cancer cells with and without HRRm [46]. Inhibition and trapping of PARP1 by PARP inhibitor, combined with the blockade of AR activity by ARPI, result in greater accumulation of DNA damage and higher anticancer efficacy than PARP inhibitor or ARPI alone [46] (Figure 1). In contrast, other preclinical studies suggest that sensitivity to PARP inhibitor plus ARPI is driven by distinct rather than synergistic mechanisms in non-HRRm prostate cancer cells [48]. The Phase III PROpel (NCT03732820), MAGNITUDE (NCT03748641), and TALAPRO-2 (NCT03395197) trials have demonstrated clinical benefit from the addition of PARP inhibitor to ARPI in patients with mCRPC [28,49–52]. Although the greatest benefits have been observed in patients with tumor mutations in BRCA1, BRCA2, CDK12, and/or PALB2 genes [28,49–53], exploratory subgroup analysis showed numerical improvements in rPFS from the addition of PARP inhibitor to ARPI therapy in both HRRm and non-HRRm subgroups [49]. Improvements in OS have also been reported from the addition of PARP inhibitor to ARPI therapy in unselected populations [51].

Building on the consistent rPFS and OS benefits of PARP inhibitor for the treatment of mCRPC demonstrated by PROfound, PROpel, and TALAPRO-2 [38,39,49–51], and given that HRRm have been detected in earlier prostate cancer settings [54], evaluating the benefit of earlier targeted intervention with PARP inhibitor offers the potential to enhance disease control and improve survival. The PARP inhibitor niraparib recently gained US approval for the treatment of BRCA2-mutated mHSPC in combination with abiraterone plus prednisone, based on an exploratory analysis in the Phase III AMPLITUDE trial (NCT04497844) [55,56]. The safety and efficacy of PARP inhibitor plus ARPI for the treatment of HRRm mHSPC is under further investigation [55,57]; however, thus far, data on the efficacy of these combinations in separate HRRm and non-HRRm patient cohorts have not been reported.

Saruparib (AZD5305) in mHSPC

Trapping of PARP1 is sufficient to achieve synthetic lethality in cells with homologous recombination deficiency [58], and a highly selective PARP1 inhibitor and trapper is hypothesized to deliver improved anticancer efficacy compared with currently approved PARP inhibitors, which offer low to modest selectivity for PARP1 over PARP2 [59,60]. Saruparib is a new generation PARP inhibitor developed through rational design to selectively inhibit and trap PARP1 [59,60], with increased potency and improved physicochemical properties versus other approved PARP inhibitors [60,61].

In the Phase I/IIa PETRA study (NCT04644068), saruparib monotherapy was well tolerated at doses up to and including 90 mg once daily (QD), with 60 mg QD identified as the optimal and recommended Phase II dose [61]. In line with data from preclinical models, more than 30-fold coverage over the target-effective concentration was observed in patients who received saruparib 60 mg QD [60,61]. Although head-to-head comparisons with other PARP inhibitors were not made in this study, target coverage exceeded those previously reported with approved doses of niraparib, talazoparib, rucaparib, and olaparib [61–65]. The safety and efficacy of saruparib plus ARPI (enzalutamide, abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone, darolutamide, or apalutamide) for the treatment of mHSPC and mCRPC are being assessed in the Phase I/IIa PETRANHA study (NCT05367440), with data supporting 60 mg QD as the recommended saruparib dose for combination with enzalutamide, abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone, or darolutamide [66]. No clinically meaningful drug–drug interactions or dose-limiting toxicities were observed with saruparib plus enzalutamide, abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone, or darolutamide, and adverse events led to discontinuation of saruparib in 8 of 77 (10.4%) patients in these treatment arms [66,67].

Objectives

EvoPAR-Prostate01 (NCT06120491) is a Phase III, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 2-cohort study evaluating the efficacy and safety of saruparib plus physician’s choice of ARPI in participants with mHSPC with and without identified HRRm. The primary objective of EvoPAR-Prostate01 is to demonstrate the superiority of saruparib plus physician’s choice of ARPI (abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone, darolutamide, or enzalutamide) compared with placebo plus physician’s choice of ARPI by assessment of rPFS in participants with mHSPC. A key secondary objective is to assess OS in patients who received saruparib plus physician’s choice of ARPI compared with physician’s choice of ARPI alone.

Potential participants are allocated to 1 of the 2 cohorts (HRRm or non-HRRm) prior to randomization. Eligibility for the HRRm cohort is determined by the presence of pathogenic/likely pathogenic mutations in 1 or more of the genes BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, CDK12, PALB2, RAD51B, RAD51C, RAD51D, and BARD1, prospectively detected in tumor tissue and/or circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) by central testing. A positive result reported by 1 assay (tumor tissue or ctDNA) is sufficient to determine HRRm status, irrespective of the result of the other assay. Only participants with a valid result of non-HRRm from prospective profiling of both tumor tissue and ctDNA are considered eligible for the non-HRRm cohort; patients with missing or indeterminate results from either tumor tissue or ctDNA are not permitted.

In both the HRRm and non-HRRm cohorts, participants are randomized 1:1 to either saruparib plus physician’s choice of ARPI or placebo plus physician’s choice of ARPI. All participants must receive continuous standard-of-care ADT using either a GnRH agonist or antagonist or bilateral orchiectomy. Randomization is stratified in both cohorts by volume of disease, according to CHAARTED criteria [68] (high volume with visceral metastases vs high volume without visceral metastases vs low volume), and physician’s choice of ARPI (abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone vs darolutamide vs enzalutamide). In the HRRm cohort only, participants are further stratified by BRCA1 and/or BRCA2 gene mutation status (BRCAm vs non-BRCAm). In the non-HRRm cohort only, participants are further stratified by the presence of metastatic disease (M1) at original prostate cancer diagnosis (yes vs no).

Eligibility criteria

Eligible participants (Supplemental material, Table S1) are males ≥18 years of age (or legal age of consent), with histologically confirmed prostate adenocarcinoma (de novo or recurrent) that is hormone sensitive. Participants with pathologic features of small cell, neuroendocrine, sarcomatoid, spindle cell, or signet cell histology are not eligible. Prior to randomization, participants must have documented metastatic disease, with clear evidence of ≥1 bone lesion and/or ≥1 soft tissue lesion (measurable and/or nonmeasurable) that can be accurately assessed at baseline and is suitable for repeated assessment of response by computed tomography (CT) and/or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST) 1.1 [69] (soft tissue) or Prostate Cancer Working Group 3 (PCWG3) [70] (bone). Participants with metastatic disease identified by prostate-specific membrane antigen-positron emission tomography (PSMA PET) only are not eligible. Participants must be receiving ADT with a GnRH agonist or antagonist or have undergone bilateral orchiectomy starting ≥14 days and <4 months prior to randomization with no radiographic disease progression or rising prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels prior to Day 1 of dosing. Participants must remain on ADT throughout the study and be candidates for treatment with an ARPI.

Key exclusion criteria include participants with suspected or prior history of myelodysplastic syndrome/acute myeloid leukemia, a recent history of another primary malignancy, history of persistent (>2 weeks) severe cytopenia, uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, long QT syndrome or a prolonged corrected QT interval, and known predisposition to bleeding. Prior treatment with PARP inhibitors or platinum chemotherapy is not permitted. In the metastatic prostate cancer setting, prior anticancer pharmacotherapy (including, but not limited to, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and systemic treatment with radiopharmaceuticals), prior treatment with ARPI, and prior treatment with ADT for longer than 4 months is not permitted. Prior docetaxel in the metastatic setting is not permitted, and participants for whom the investigator judges the standard of care to be a docetaxel-based regimen are not eligible. Prior to study entry, standard-of-care radiation therapy for metastatic prostate cancer is permitted, either to the prostate in patients with low volume disease, or to isolated lesions. Prior wide field radiation therapy must have been completed more than 4 weeks prior to randomization. Limited field radiation therapy must have been completed more than 2 weeks prior to randomization. Participants must be fully recovered from any clinically significant adverse events resulting from radiation therapy.

Interventions

Participants are centrally assigned to a randomized study treatment using computerized block randomization within an interactive voice/web response trial supply management system. A participant identification number is allocated at the dispensing visit, with processes outlined in a user manual provided to each center. No members of the study teams have access to the randomization scheme until formal unblinding, except for relevant individuals in the pharmaceutical supply chain and safety departments, as well as individuals involved in the analysis of pharmacokinetic samples. Unblinding of a participant’s intervention assignment to the investigator prior to formal unblinding is only permitted in the event of a medical emergency, at the investigator’s discretion. An independent data monitoring committee will review unblinded interim safety data every 6 months to make recommendations for study continuation, amendment, or termination. The committee will review unblinded efficacy data at prespecified points in the study to determine whether interim stopping boundaries are met.

The control arm in EvoPAR-Prostate01 is placebo plus physician’s choice of ARPI; abiraterone (1000 mg QD) plus prednisone/prednisolone (5 mg QD), darolutamide (600 mg twice daily), or enzalutamide (160 mg QD). The experimental arm is saruparib (60 mg QD) plus physician’s choice of ARPI; abiraterone (1000 mg QD) plus prednisone/prednisolone (5 mg QD), darolutamide (600 mg twice daily), or enzalutamide (160 mg QD). ADT is prescribed in accordance with local clinical practice guidelines, where applicable. Combination with first-generation AR antagonists to counter testosterone flare is permitted until randomization. Relugolix may be combined with darolutamide or abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone. However, due to a potential interaction between relugolix and enzalutamide, this combination is not permitted. Concomitant use of strong inducers or inhibitors of cytochrome P450 3A4 and 2C8, strong P-glycoprotein inducers, or drugs that are known to prolong QT interval and have a known risk of Torsades de Pointes is not permitted. Participants commence study intervention within 3 business days of randomization and continue treatment until investigator-assessed radiographic disease progression per RECIST 1.1 (for soft tissue lesions) and/or PCWG3 criteria (for bone lesions), unacceptable toxicity, or participant withdrawal of consent. Treatment beyond disease progression and crossover between cohorts are not permitted.

Planned sample sizes

Participants have been recruited from 366 study sites in 24 countries across Asia–Pacific, Europe, North America, and South America (Figure 3). Approximately 3300 potential participants have been screened for eligibility. Approximately 550 participants in the HRRm cohort and 1250 participants in the non-HRRm cohort have been randomized to receive study treatment. Study procedures

A schedule of study procedures is summarized in Supplemental material, Table S2. Formalin-fixed, paraffin embedded (FFPE) tumor samples for central tissue HRRm testing, and whole blood samples for central ctDNA HRRm testing and RNA analysis are collected and tested prior to randomization. Archival or newly acquired FFPE tumor samples from either primary or metastatic sites are permitted for HRRm testing, provided samples are taken as part of routine clinical practice and are not derived from a previously irradiated lesion. Whole blood samples for determination of HRRm status are provided prior to the participant starting treatment with ADT, where possible. A next-generation sequencing gene panel assay is used to identify loss-of-function mutations in BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, CDK12, PALB2, RAD51B, RAD51C, RAD51D, and/or BARD1. Pre-dose blood samples for pharmacokinetic testing are collected on Days 1, 29, 57, 141, 225, and 309 of study intervention. Post-dose blood samples are collected on Days 1 and 29. Blood samples collected at screening and disease progression are used for exploratory analysis of RNA biomarkers, including assessment of AR splice variants and other biomarkers of treatment resistance. Blood samples collected at screening and on treatment are used for exploratory longitudinal ctDNA analyses.

Tumor assessments are performed at baseline, and every 16 weeks until radiographically determined progressive disease by CT and/or MRI scan for soft tissue of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, as well as a whole-body bone scan performed in accordance with PCWG3 recommendations (including standard 99mTc-methylene diphosphonate whole-body planar scintigraphy; 99mTc-methylene diphosphonate single-photon emission computed tomography is permitted). Any other areas of disease involvement are additionally imaged as clinically indicated. Tumor assessments must continue until RECIST 1.1- and/or PCWG3-defined radiographic progression regardless of whether the patient discontinued study intervention or started a new treatment. Information on survival and second progression is collected following discontinuation of both study interventions (saruparib/placebo and ARPI).

PSA is assessed at baseline and every 4 weeks for the first 52 weeks of the study intervention, then every 8 weeks regardless of whether dose interruptions occur. PSA assessment should continue after treatment discontinuation until radiographic disease progression is observed, as applicable.

Symptomatic skeletal events are assessed at screening, during study intervention, at treatment discontinuation and disease progression, then every 16 weeks thereafter. Adverse events are monitored at baseline and throughout the study. The safety follow-up period is 30 days from the last dose of study treatment. Subsequent anticancer therapies are recorded from the end of treatment with study intervention through the safety and survival follow-up period or until death for participants who have discontinued study intervention. Patient-reported outcome (PRO) measurements from several assessment tools are included to help inform the overall benefit–risk profile of treatment in this setting.

Study visits up to and including Day 364, as well as treatment discontinuation, disease progression, and safety follow-up visits, must occur in person. However, the study is being conducted using a decentralized model, meaning where possible, if permissible under local regulations, and with participant agreement, participant visits after Day 364 will be performed remotely, and study intervention will be shipped directly to the participant’s home via a solution and services method compliant with Good Clinical Practice/Good Manufacturing Practice/Good Distribution Practice. Compliance with the study intervention among participants self-administering the study intervention at home will be assessed by direct questioning at each visit (on-site or remotely). A record of the quantity of study intervention dispensed to and administered by each participant will be maintained and reconciled with study intervention and compliance records.

Outcomes

The primary outcome measure is rPFS, defined as the time from randomization to radiographic progression, as assessed by the investigator per RECIST 1.1 (soft tissue) and/or PCWG3 criteria (bone), or death due to any cause. Secondary outcome measures include OS, time to second progression or death (PFS2), time to first subsequent therapy or death (TFST), symptomatic skeletal event-free survival (SSE-FS), time to castration-resistant event (TTCR), PSA assessments, safety, and tolerability. PRO endpoints include time to pain progression (TTPP), time to deterioration in urinary symptoms (TTDUS), time to deterioration in fatigue (TTDF), time to deterioration in physical function (TTDPF), and change in Brief Pain Inventory – Short Form (BPI–SF) worst pain score (Supplemental material, Table S3). Exploratory analyses of additional efficacy endpoints and PRO measures, as well as biomarkers (including assessment of PSMA by PET), are planned.

Statistical analyses

All participants who signed the informed consent form will be included in the screened analysis set. Efficacy analyses will be performed on the full analysis set, comprising all randomized participants. Treatment groups within the full analysis set will be compared based on randomized study intervention, regardless of the treatment received; participants who were randomized but did not receive study intervention will be included in the treatment group to which they were randomized. The safety analysis set will comprise all randomized participants who received ≥1 dose of saruparib or placebo. An additional pharmacokinetics analysis set will comprise participants who received ≥1 dose of study intervention for whom there is ≥1 reportable pharmacokinetic concentration.

rPFS will be analyzed using a stratified log-rank test adjusted for stratification factors. If required, censoring occurs at the latest scan date contributing to an overall visit assessment. Hazard ratios will be estimated from a stratified Cox proportional hazards model, with Efron’s approximation to adjust for ties, and the 95% confidence intervals will be calculated using a profile likelihood approach. Kaplan–Meier plots of rPFS will be presented by treatment group. Median rPFS, summaries of the number and percentage of participants experiencing an rPFS event, and criteria defining the type of event (RECIST 1.1, PCWG3, or death) will be reported for each treatment. rPFS rates (the proportion of participants alive and progression-free) at 12-monthly intervals from randomization will be reported by treatment group.

Subgroup analyses comparing rPFS between the treatment arms will be conducted within each cohort to assess the consistency of treatment effect across potential or expected prognostic factors and will include the subgroups defined by the cohort-specific stratification factors.

OS, PFS2, TFST, SSE-FS, and TTCR will be analyzed using the statistical methods specified for rPFS and adjusting for the same stratification factors. Safety data will be presented using descriptive statistics.

Within each cohort, the overall type I error rate for the analysis of primary and key secondary endpoints will be controlled at the 2-sided 5% level. This will be achieved using a hierarchical testing approach with rPFS being formally tested first, and OS will be formally tested in the event of a significant rPFS result. The impact of other systemic anticancer therapies received prior to progression will be assessed in a modified analysis of rPFS, in which patients who received another systemic anticancer therapy prior to radiographic progression are censored at their last evaluable assessment prior to starting another systemic anticancer therapy.

Ethics

The protocol, any protocol amendments, and all other relevant study documents were approved by an institutional review board or independent ethics committee at each participating institution before the study was initiated. The study is being conducted in accordance with consensus ethical principles derived from international guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki and the Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical Guidelines, applicable International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice Guidelines, and all applicable local laws and regulations. The study protocol is publicly registered, and the results are disclosed and/or published according to the AstraZeneca Global Standard – Bioethics and in compliance with prevailing laws and regulations. Written informed consent must be obtained from the patient by the investigator or a suitably trained delegate before HRRm testing, enrollment, and any study-related procedures are performed. Participation in the study is voluntary, and patients may withdraw from study participation at any time for any reason.

Discussion

While combinations of PARP inhibitors with ARPIs are the current standard of care in patients with HRRm mCRPC, there remains a need for more effective treatments in mHSPC that can delay disease progression to mCRPC. The investigation of PARP inhibitor for the treatment of mHSPC is supported by compelling evidence from pivotal trials in later disease stages. In PROfound, olaparib significantly improved rPFS (median 7.4 vs 3.6 months; hazard ratio 0.34) and OS (median 19.1 vs 14.7 months; hazard ratio 0.69) versus treatment of physician’s choice in patients with heavily pretreated mCRPC with 1 or more mutations in BRCA1, BRCA2, or ATM [38,39]. The PROpel trial extended this approach to a biomarker-unselected population of patients with mCRPC, demonstrating a median rPFS of 24.8 months with olaparib plus abiraterone versus 16.6 months with placebo plus abiraterone (hazard ratio 0.66), and greater magnitude of benefit in HRRm tumors (hazard ratio 0.50) [49]. Similarly, TALAPRO-2 demonstrated that talazoparib plus enzalutamide improved rPFS versus placebo plus enzalutamide in the overall mCRPC population (median rPFS not reached vs 21.9 months; hazard ratio 0.63), and the effect was pronounced in the subgroup of patients with HRRm (hazard ratio 0.45) [50]. Given the consistent gains reported, particularly among patients with HRRm disease, and the biological rationale that HRRm are present early in patients with prostate cancer [54], the approach of combining PARP inhibitor with ARPI in the mHSPC setting, as is being investigated in EvoPAR-Prostate01, may enable earlier intervention, delay resistance, and improve long-term outcomes. Recent data have shown that a PARP inhibitor added to the clinical benefit conferred by standard of care ARPI in patients with HRRm mHSPC [55], and further investigations into the safety and efficacy of PARP inhibitor plus ARPI for the treatment of HRRm mHSPC are ongoing [55,57]. Additionally, across multiple tumor types beyond prostate cancer, PARP inhibitors used in earlier lines of treatment have demonstrated potential to provide a greater absolute magnitude of benefit than when given in later lines [41,42,71].

Saruparib, a highly selective and potent inhibitor and trapper of PARP1, is hypothesized to deliver improved anticancer efficacy compared with currently approved nonselective PARP inhibitors [59,60]. EvoPAR-Prostate01 is designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of saruparib in combination with abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone, darolutamide, or enzalutamide independently in participants with either HRRm or non-HRRm mHSPC. Whereas randomization was not stratified by HRRm status in PROpel [49], randomization in TALAPRO-2 was stratified by HRRm status, albeit by combining participants with non-HRRm and unknown HRRm status into 1 stratum [50]. In contrast, EvoPAR-Prostate01 has a 2-cohort design, with HRRm status being determined prospectively, and participants being allocated to either the HRRm or non-HRRm cohort prior to randomization. Exclusion of participants with unknown HRRm status will allow for a robust interrogation of the efficacy of first-line saruparib plus ARPI in patients with both HRRm and non-HRRm mHSPC.

Prior to the initiation of EvoPAR-Prostate01, the favorable safety and tolerability of saruparib monotherapy had been demonstrated in the Phase I/II PETRA trial [61]. Furthermore, the PETRANHA study, which is evaluating the safety of saruparib in combination with the ARPIs used in EvoPAR-Prostate01, has demonstrated that saruparib can be safely combined with abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone, darolutamide, or enzalutamide at the same dose as used for monotherapy [66,67]. At the time of study initiation, the safety of saruparib plus apalutamide had not been established in PETRANHA, which is why saruparib plus apalutamide is not being investigated in EvoPAR-Prostate01. Interim analysis from PETRANHA showed that saruparib in combination with ARPIs had manageable toxicity [67]. The most frequent treatment-emergent adverse events reported in PETRANHA to date were fatigue, anemia, nausea, and neutropenia, with any Grade 3 or higher events occurring in 46.8% of patients, a comparable or lower incidence than previously reported with similar combinations of nonselective PARP inhibitors [67]. Adverse events leading to discontinuation of saruparib and ARPIs occurred in 10.4% and 3.9% of patients, respectively [67]. EvoPAR-Prostate01 will provide long-term exposure and safety data for saruparib in combination with ARPIs to establish the risk/benefit balance for patients with mHSPC. Comprehensive toxicity management guidelines have been developed to assist investigators with the recognition and management of adverse events typically associated with PARP inhibitor treatment, including hematological and nonhematological toxicities, with specific instructions for saruparib dose modifications (reduction to 40 mg or 20 mg QD, or discontinuation) to ensure patient safety. Although clinical trial data have shown no increased risk of myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukemia with PARP inhibitor therapy [72], exclusion of patients with history of/suspected myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukemia, as well as those who have received prior PARP inhibitor or platinum therapy, aims to control for secondary malignancies associated with the use of DNA damage-inducing agents outside of this study [73]. Routine monitoring and reporting of adverse events, regardless of the investigator’s assessment of causality, will continue after discontinuation of study treatment to develop a full understanding of patient safety.

Conclusion

The EvoPAR-Prostate01 study aims to provide confirmatory data regarding the added benefit conferred by the new generation PARP inhibitor, saruparib, when combined with standard-of-care ARPIs, and will demonstrate whether PARP inhibition can address the clinical unmet need in patients with mHSPC by delaying disease progression to mCRPC.

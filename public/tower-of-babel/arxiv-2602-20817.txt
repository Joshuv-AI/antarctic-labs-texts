Quantum wreath products and p p -adic general linear group
Valentin Buciumas, Chun-Ju Lai

Abstract
. We study the pro- p p Iwahori–Hecke algebra and its Gelfand–Graev modules for the p p -adic general linear group and its metaplectic covers. We develop the theory of quantum wreath products of skew polynomial type and use it to provide transparent descriptions of these Hecke algebras and their modules that were previously inaccessible through standard p p -adic methods. We introduce the notion of (anti)spherical and Kashiwara–Miwa–Stern modules for these quantum wreath products for the first time and interpret the p p -adic Gelfand–Graev modules in terms of these new modules. As an application, we study the structure theory for the corresponding p p -adic pro- p p Schur algebras and obtain an explicit basis and multiplication rules. Moreover, we give algebraic (re)proofs of several results of p p -adic interest including the existence of PBW basis for the pro- p p metaplectic and Iwahori–Hecke algebras, identification of the Iwahori–Schur algebra with the quantum affine Schur algebra, and failure of the local Shimura correspondence at the pro- p p level.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
On
p
-adic groups, Hecke algebras and Whittaker modules

1.1.1.
Hecke algebras

Let
F
be a local non-Archimedean field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, maximal ideal
\varpi\mathcal{O}
for a fixed uniformizer
\varpi
and residue field
\kappa
of cardinality
q
. Denote by
\mathfrak{r}:\mathcal{O}\to\kappa
the reduction mod
\varpi
map.
Let
G
be the
p
-adic group
\mathbf{GL}_{d}(F)
.
The category
\Rep G
of complex smooth admissible representations of
G
, of utmost importance in the local and global Langlands programme, can be studied in terms of certain Hecke algebras.
Given a compact subgroup
X
of
G
, the Hecke algebra
C_{c}^{\infty}(X\backslash{G}/X)
captures information about all irreducible representations of
G
with
X
-fixed vectors.
More precisely, the functor
V\mapsto V^{X}
gives a bijection between irreducibles of
G
with
X
-fixed vector and irreducibles of
C_{c}^{\infty}(X\backslash{G}/X)
.
Important examples of Hecke algebras are the spherical, Iwahori and pro-
p
Hecke algebras where
X
is taken to be
K:=\mathbf{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{O})
,
I:=\mathfrak{r}^{-1}(\mathbf{B}(\kappa))\subset K
and
I^{(1)}:=\mathfrak{r}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}(\kappa))\subset K
, resp.
Here,
\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{U}
denote the standard Borel and unipotent of
\mathbf{GL}_{d}
, resp. and
\mathfrak{r}:K\to\mathbf{GL}_{d}(\kappa)
is the mod
\varpi
reduction map.

The irreducible representations of
G
with
K
-fixed vectors are called unramified; they appear at almost all places in an automorphic representation, and as such they are important in the theory of automorphic forms.
The irreducible representations of
G
with
I
-fixed vectors are called tamely ramified.
They generate the principal block in the Bernstein decomposition of
\Rep G

[
Bor76
]
(more intricately, by the work of Bushnell and Kutzko
[
BK93
]
, the affine Hecke algebra can actually be used to model the whole category
\Rep G
).
The pro-
p
subgroup
I^{(1)}
sees more than the Iwahori subgroup, including a certain class of depth
0
representations.
Its Hecke algebra
[
Vig05
,
Vig16
]
is the natural object to consider when studying the representation theory of
G
over a field of positive characteristic leading to basic results in the mod
p
Langlands programme including classification of irreducibles in terms of irreducible supersingular representations of Levi subgroups
[
AHHV17
]
.

1.1.2.
Whittaker modules

Let
\psi:U^{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be an additive non-degenerate character of the negative unipotent
U^{-}
, with conductor
\varpi\mathcal{O}
.
It is known that every irreducible
\pi\in\Rep G
has an at most unique Whittaker model, in other words

(1.1.1)

\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(\pi,\mathrm{Ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)\leq 1.

Such a local result is fundamental in the theory of automorphic forms, where it can be used to show global multiplicity one results (for example that Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms on
\operatorname{GL}_{d}
can be decomposed in terms of Whittaker functions
[
Sha74
]
), as well as in the study of L-functions, including in the Langlands–Shahidi method and the Rankin–Selberg formula.

Alternatively, we can use the Gelfand–Graev module
\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi
of
G
(the dual of
\mathrm{Ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi^{-1}
), to derive information about Whittaker models for representations of
G
.
Restricting to a certain class of representations, we may consider the actions

(1.1.2)

C_{c}^{\infty}(K\backslash{G}/K)\curvearrowright(\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{K},\quad\mathcal{H}_{I}:=C_{c}^{\infty}(I\backslash{G}/I)\curvearrowright(\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I}.

It is known that
(\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{K}
is a rank one free module over
C_{c}^{\infty}(K\backslash{G}/K)
(both of which are isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}[\Rep G^{\vee}]
, first as an algebra, second as a module, via the Satake isomorphism and geometric Casselman–Shalika formula, resp.).
Here,
G^{\vee}
is the Langlands dual group, over
\mathbb{C}
.
It is also known that
(\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I}
is isomorphic to the antispherical module of the affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}\cong\mathcal{H}_{I}

[
CS18
]
:

(1.1.3)

(\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I}\cong\operatorname{asph}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}^{\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}}\operatorname{sgn},

where
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
is the finite Hecke algebra.

1.1.3.
p
-adic Schur algebras

The space
(\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I}
can be seen as a module over the
p
-adic Schur algebra
\mathcal{S}:=\mathrm{End}_{C_{c}^{\infty}(I\backslash{G}/I)}(\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I}
.
Moreover, one can relate the space in (
1.1.1
) to an irreducible representation of
\mathcal{S}
.
The commutativity of
\mathcal{S}
then implies the local multiplicity one property mentioned above.
It therefore makes sense to ask the following problems at the pro-
p
level:

(
P
1):

understand the structure of
{\mathcal{W}}^{I^{(1)}}:=(\mathrm{ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I^{(1)}}
as a module over
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}:=C_{c}^{\infty}(I^{(1)}\backslash{G}/I^{(1)})
.

(
P
2):

describe this structure in terms of an antispherical module of the
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
.

(
P
3):

understand the structure of the pro-
p
Schur algebra
\mathcal{S}^{(1)}:=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{H}^{(1)}}({\mathcal{W}}^{I^{(1)}})
.

The action of
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
on
{\mathcal{W}}^{I^{(1)}}
is computed by Gao–Gurevich–Karasiewicz
[
GGK24
]
, therefore solving (
P
1).
Based on their result, we answer (
P
2) and (
P
3) by using the theory of quantum wreath product developed in
[
LNX24
,
LM25
,
LNX25
]
.

1.1.4.
Metaplectic groups

Another natural appearance of the pro-
p
Hecke algebra can be seen when dealing with metaplectic covers of
p
-adic groups.
Fix a positive integer
n
and denote by
\widetilde{G}
a metaplectic
n
-cover of
G
(see §
4.2
for a precise definition; we note here that we are working with Savin’s nice cover of
G=\mathbf{GL}_{d}(F)
).
Our results are dependent on choosing this cover and do not immediately generalize to other covers (like the Kazhdan–Patterson covers) or other Cartan types cf. results in the same style proved in
[
BBB19
,
GGK25
]
.

In this setting, the multiplicity one property for Whittaker models fails, i.e. (
1.1.1
) fails when we replace
G
by
\widetilde{G}
.
As such, understanding the structure of the modules in (
1.1.2
) is a significantly harder problem.
Hecke algebras and their modules will consist of genuine functions, which coupled with the lack of commutativity of the metaplectic torus reduce the support of the Hecke algebra, while leaving intact the support of the corresponding module.
For example, in the metaplectic setting the spherical Gelfand–Graev module is not free of rank one over the spherical Hecke algebra and its structure is quite hard to understand
p
-adically (cf.
[
BP25
]
).

Gao–Gurevich–Karasiewicz
[
GGK24
]
have realized that the reduction in support of the Hecke algebra characteristic to the spherical and Iwahori levels does not persist at the pro-
p
level, which allowed them to algebraically compute the action of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}:=C_{\iota,c}^{\infty}(I^{(1)}\backslash{\widetilde{G}}/I^{(1)})
on the metaplectic module
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{I^{(1)}}:=(\mathrm{ind}_{\mu_{n}U^{-}}^{\widetilde{G}}\iota\otimes\psi)^{I^{(1)}}
(thereby solving (
P
1) at the metaplectic level).
They use an averaging procedure to compute the Iwahori level action.
Ultimately, the structure at the spherical level is understood in
[
BP25
]
in terms of dual data depending on Lusztig’s quantum group at a root of unity.

1.2.
On quantum wreath products and wreath modules

1.2.1.
Quantum wreath Products

A
quantum wreath product
(introduced in
[
LNX24
]
) is an algebra
B\wr\mathcal{H}(d)

defined by a generalized Bernstein–Lusztig type presentation.
The notion of quantum wreath product allows one to uniformly study representation theory of many important algebras appearing in quantum representation theory such as the affine Hecke algebra, (affine) Yokonuma algebra, affine zigzag algebras, affine Frobenius algebras, etc.

In group theory, a wreath product combines the “base group” and the “acting group” into a larger group.
However, in the context of quantum wreath products,
one cannot encompass many important examples by taking the wreath product of two “quantum” algebras.
What we do is, given a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
B
that is potentially non-commutative, we define an algebra
B\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
generated by
B^{\otimes d}
and formal generators
H_{1},\dots,H_{d-1}
,
subject to the type A braid relations among
H_{i}
’s,
quadratic relations for
H_{i}
’s with coefficients in
B^{\otimes d}
,
and wreath relations between
H_{i}
’s and
B^{\otimes d}
,
which is meant to mimic the wreath product between
B^{\otimes d}
and the finite Hecke algebra.
But we stress that
\mathcal{H}(d)
is not defined as the finite Hecke algebra (in fact it is not defined independently of
B\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
).
The coefficients appearing in the definition of
B\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
are controlled by a choice of parameters
(S,R,\sigma,\rho)
, where
S,R\in B\otimes B
and
\sigma,\rho\in\mathrm{End}(B\otimes B)
.
The theory becomes substantially more interesting when
S,R\not\in\mathbb{C}(1\otimes 1)
.

For example, setting
B=\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
to be a polynomial ring produces an algebra
B\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
isomorphic to the affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}
(or equiv., the Iwahori Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{I}
), while taking
B=\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]
where
\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{C}[C_{q-1}]
is the group algebra over the cyclic group of
q-1
elements produces the affine Yokonuma algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}
(equiv., the pro-
p
Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
).
Moreover, one can set
B=\mathcal{H}_{q}(\Sigma_{m})
to be the Hecke algebra for the symmetric group
\Sigma_{m}
to realize the so-called Hu algebra, a non-trivial quantization of wreath products between symmetric groups.
Furthermore, the base algebra
B
does not have to be deformed from a group algebra. The affine zigzag algebra is realized by setting
B
to be the zigzag algebra over a given quiver.

1.2.2.
Skew polynomial quantum wreath products

Generalizing results in
[
LM25
]
, we develop the theory of skew polynomial quantum wreath products where the base algebra is a ring of skew polynomials.
In particular, we work with the ring
\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{n}\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
with skew commutativity

x^{\pm 1}f=\xi^{\pm 2}fx^{\pm 1},\quad\textup{ for }f\in\mathbb{F},

for some
n
-th root of unity
\xi
.
Fix
t\in C_{q-1}
so
\mathbb{F}\cong\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{q-1}-1)
.
We can then define the quantum wreath product
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
using the same choice of parameters
S,R
, and
\sigma
as in the affine Yokonuma algebra
\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
,
while the map
\rho
is determined by the twisted Leibniz rule
\rho(ab)=\sigma(a)\rho(b)+\rho(a)b
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{A}
as well as the following “minuscule” case:

\rho(x\otimes 1):=\sum_{j=1}^{q-1}(t^{j}\otimes t^{-j})(x\otimes 1),\quad\rho(\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{F})=0.

We show that the intricate terms in the Bernstein–Lusztig relations (
4.3.16
) for
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
are captured by the values
\rho(P)
, where
P\in\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{A}
is an arbitrary skew polynomial. This formulation provides a more transparent realization of the relations originally established in
[
GGK24
]
.

Our first main result identifies the metaplectic pro-
p
Hecke algebra in terms of the skew quantum wreath product:

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.2.1
)
.

We have an isomorphism of algebras
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}\cong\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d).

Our result allows us to apply the structure and representation theory developed in
[
LNX24
,
LM25
]
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
.
In particular, we obtain a PBW basis theorem for
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}

(see Theorem
3.1.5
).

When
n=1
,
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
is known to be isomorphic to the affine Yokonuma algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}
(cf.
[
LM25
]
) and we therefore recover the main result of
[
CS16
]
identifying the latter algebra with the pro-
p
Hecke algebra.
A PBW basis result for
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}
is obtained in
[
RS20
]
.
Representation theoretic results such as a Schur duality for
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}
were developed in
[
LM25
]
.

1.2.3.
Descent to the Iwahori level

We can write

(1.2.1)

\mathcal{H}_{I}\cong e_{I}\mathcal{H}^{(1)}e_{I},\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{I}\cong e_{I}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}e_{I}.

for a certain idempotent
e_{I}
in
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
(or
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
) that is supported on
I
.
As mentioned before,
\mathcal{H}_{I}\cong\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d).

The metaplectic Hecke algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{I}:=C^{\infty}_{\iota,c}(I\backslash{\widetilde{G}}/I)
is known to be isomorphic to the non-metaplectic Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{I}
(for the cover we are working with) cf. Savin’s local Shimura correspondence
[
Sav88
,
McN12
]
.

We show in Proposition
5.2.5
that
\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm n}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
,
can be realized as a corner algebra
\epsilon_{I}(\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d))\epsilon_{I}
using the same idempotent
\epsilon_{I}\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
as in the linear case.
We obtain a quantum wreath product interpretation of the local Shimura correspondence, i.e. we show the existence of an isomorphism:

\epsilon_{I}(\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d))\epsilon_{I}\to\epsilon_{I}(\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d))\epsilon_{I}\quad\textup{which maps}\quad\epsilon_{I}x^{\lambda}\epsilon_{I}\mapsto\epsilon_{I}x^{\bar{n}\lambda}\epsilon_{I},

where
\bar{n}:=n/\textup{gcd}(n,2)
.
In fact, our result clarifies that the analogous map
\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)\to\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
at the pro-
p
level will no longer be an isomorphism since the centers of the two algebras (depending on

\mathbb{F}\rtimes_{n}\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
for
n\geq 2
and
\mathbb{F}[x]
) will be different. This gives us:

Corollary B
.

For
n\geq 2
, the pro-
p
Hecke algebras
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}\not\cong\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
are non-isomorphic.

This result highlights the
failure
of the local Shimura correspondence at the pro-
p
level.

1.2.4.
Wreath modules

Since there is no “acting algebra”
\mathcal{H}(d)
in a quantum wreath product, one cannot combine a
B
-module and an
\mathcal{H}(d)
-module (which is not defined) into a
B\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
-module.
A crucial result in
[
LNX25
]
is the introduction of the notion of the wreath modules.
It turns out that one has to start with a certain
B
-module on which both
S
and
R
act on
M\otimes M
by scalars (see §
2.3
for details) and derives an auxiliary
twisted Hecke algebra

\mathbb{H}(M)
using these scalars.
Subsequently, for any module
N
over
\mathbb{H}(M)
, a well-defined
B\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
-action can be realized on the wreath module
M\wr N:=M^{\otimes d}\otimes N
.

This theory provides a transparent realization of a diverse family of modules in existing literature.
For example, the spherical, antispherical and Kashiwara–Miwa–Stern
[
KMS95
, (32)]
tensor modules for the affine Hecke algebra can all be realized as wreath modules.
Moreover, simple and Specht modules for Ariki–Koike and Hu algebras are constructed via parabolic induction of wreath modules.

1.2.5.
Wreath modules for skew polynomial quantum wreath products

One significance of employing the theory of quantum wreath products is that we can now define a novel “antispherical module” for the quantum wreath product
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
.

There is a notable technicality which arises from the fact that the quadratic relation in
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
has a complicated degree one coefficient.
This is a difficulty specific to the pro-
p
level (it does not appear at the Iwahori level) and appears even in the non-metaplectic setting.
Let
t
be a generator of
C_{q-1}
.
The quadratic relations in
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(2)
are of the form

(1.2.2)

H^{2}=(q-1)eH+q(t^{k}\otimes t^{k}),\quad\textup{where}\quad e:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}t^{j}\otimes t^{-j},\quad k\in\{0,m/2\}.

It is a pleasant surprise (see Lemma
2.1.11
) that such a quadratic relation splits into linear factors, i.e.,
(H+\gamma)(H-\overline{\gamma})=(H-\overline{\gamma})(H+\gamma)=0
for some
\gamma,\overline{\gamma}\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
.
To be precise, we obtain that

(1.2.3)

\gamma=(\sqrt{q}(t^{k}\otimes 1)+1)e-\sqrt{q}(t^{k}\otimes 1),\quad\overline{\gamma}=(\sqrt{q}(1\otimes t^{k})+q)e-\sqrt{q}(1\otimes t^{k}).

The splitting of (
1.2.2
) enables us to define the “trivial” and “sign” modules on which
H
acts by
\overline{\gamma}
and
-\gamma
, respectively.
We are then able to construct a generalization of the KMS space
\mathbb{V}(N)^{\otimes d}
(see
2.2.1
) for any
N>1
, and then show:

Theorem C

(Theorem
5.3.2
)
.

The pro-
p
component
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{I^{(1)}}
of the metaplectic Gelfand–Graev module is isomorphic to the space
\mathbb{V}(1)\wr\mathrm{sgn}\cong\mathbb{V}(1)^{\otimes d}

as right modules over
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}\cong\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
.

Here
\mathbb{V}(1)\wr\mathrm{sgn}
is the new antispherical module of
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
, generalizing the antispherical module of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}
and giving us a solution to (
P
2) at the metaplectic level.
The
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
module
\mathbb{V}(N)\wr\mathrm{sgn}
can then be interpreted as a generalization of the KMS tensor module
[
KMS95
, (32)]
.
We stress that this result is new and non-trivial even in the linear case (i.e. when we work with
\operatorname{GL}_{d}
instead of its cover).

Combining the corner algebra realization (
1.2.1
),
we also show that, as an
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{I}
-module,
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{I}\cong\mathbb{V}(1)^{\otimes d}\epsilon_{I}
is isomorphic to the original KMS tensor space
V(\bar{n})^{\otimes d}
(see Proposition
5.4.1
).

1.2.6.
Pro-
p
metaplectic Schur algebras

We are now in a position to study the wreath Schur algebras

\mathbb{S}(N,d):=\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)}(\mathbb{V}(N)^{\otimes d})

at the pro-
p
metaplectic level.

Theorem D
.

(a)

(
3.4.1
) For
N\geq 1
, we obtain explicit multiplication formulas of
\mathbb{S}(N,d)
with respect to the Dipper-James-type basis introduced in
[
LM25
]
.

(b)

(Corollary
5.3.3
) For
N=1
, we establish an algebra isomorphism

(1.2.4)

\mathbb{S}(1,d)\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{(1)}:=\mathrm{End}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{I^{(1)}}),

which solves (
P
3) at the metaplectic level.

Descending to the Iwahori level, we can use the fact that

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{I}\cong V(\bar{n})^{\otimes d}
to write the Iwahori–Schur algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}:=\mathrm{End}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{I})
as the quantum affine Schur algebra
\widehat{S}_{\sqrt{q}}(\bar{n},d)\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{aff}}}(V(\bar{n})^{\otimes d})

[
Gre99
]
.
This recovers one of the main results of
[
GGK25
, §4]
and was an important motivation for our work.

The Schur algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{(1)}
, even in the non-metaplectic setting, is more complicated; it is not commutative, and contains a lot more information than before.
For example, it is slightly more complicated than the Iwahori–Schur algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}\cong\widehat{S}_{\sqrt{q}}(\bar{n},d)
for a
metaplectic group
cf. our main results.

The structure and representation theory of the quantum affine Schur algebra developed into an elegant mathematical framework with many applications to the study of
p
-adic groups
[
Vig03
,
MS19
,
Gur22
,
GGK25
]
, including to the study of Whittaker models as mentioned in §
1.1.3
.
We hope this paper is the first step in understanding similar results for the newly introduced pro-
p
Schur algebras.
For example, we expect that one can use the theory of quantum wreath products to show that the multiplicative coefficients of these pro-
p
Schur algebras
\mathbb{S}(N,d)
stabilize as
d\to\infty
, just as for the family of quantum affine Schur algebras.
Such a property is the key to the close connection to the theory of quantum affine algebras.

2.
Polynomial Quantum Wreath Products

In this section, we recall the theory of polynomial quantum wreath products developed in
[
LM25
]
.
We explicitly identify the affine Yokonuma algebra and its generalizations with polynomial quantum wreath products.
We introduce generalizations of the trivial and sign representations for the finite Yokonuma algebra which allows us to define for the affine Yokonuma algebra the (anti)spherical module and the KMS tensor module which generalizes
[
KMS95
, (32)]
.
Ultimately, these will be identified with the Gelfand–Graev module of a
p
-adic group.
We define and study the corresponding Schur algebra.

2.1.
Polynomial quantum wreath products

Let
m=q-1\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
and let
C_{m}
be the cyclic group of order
m
generated by
t
.
Define
[m]:=\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}
.
Let

(2.1.1)

\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{C}[C_{m}]=\tfrac{\mathbb{C}[t]}{(t^{m}-1)}=\textup{Span}{C}\{t^{j}~|~j\in[m]\},\qquad\mathbb{B}:=\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}].

For any
b\in\mathbb{B}^{\otimes k}
for
k\in\{1,2\}
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}^{\otimes 2})
, we define
b_{i}\in\mathbb{B}^{\otimes d}
and
\varphi_{i}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{B}^{\otimes d})
by

(2.1.2)

\begin{split}&b_{i}:=1^{\otimes i-1}\otimes b\otimes 1^{\otimes d-i-k+1},\\
&\varphi_{i}(b_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes b_{d}):=b_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes b_{i-1}\otimes\varphi(b_{i}\otimes b_{i+1})\otimes\dots\otimes b_{d}.\end{split}

In particular,
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes d}\cong\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
.
Let
e:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}t^{j}\otimes t^{-j}\in\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{F}
. Thus, for
1\leq i\leq d-1
:

(2.1.3)

e_{i}:=\frac{1}{m}\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{m}(1^{\otimes i-1}\otimes t^{j}\otimes t^{-j}\otimes 1^{\otimes d-i-1})=\frac{1}{m}\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{m}t_{i}^{j}t_{i+1}^{-j}\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}.

Let
\sigma=\textup{flip}\in\mathrm{End}{C}(\mathbb{B}^{\otimes 2})
be the flip map
b_{1}\otimes b_{2}\mapsto b_{2}\otimes b_{1}
,
and let
\partial\in\mathrm{End}{C}(\mathbb{B}^{\otimes 2})
be the Demazure operator given by

\partial(b):=\tfrac{b-\sigma(b)}{x_{1}-x_{2}}
.
In particular,
\partial_{i}(b):=\tfrac{b-\sigma_{i}(b)}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}
.

Proposition 2.1.1
.

Let
S_{i}:=(q-1)e_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_{i}^{j}t_{i+1}^{-j}\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{\otimes d}
.

(a)

Each element
e_{i}\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
is an idempotent, i.e.,
e_{i}^{2}=e_{i}
.

(b)

S_{i}f=\sigma_{i}(f)S_{i}
for all
f\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
.

(c)

The following twisted Demazure operator
\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{End}{C}(\mathbb{B}^{\otimes d})
is well-defined for each
i
:

(2.1.4)

\rho_{i}(b):=\frac{S_{i}x_{i}b-\sigma_{i}(b)S_{i}x_{i}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}.

(d)

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
and
P\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{d}^{\pm 1}]
. Then,

\rho_{i}(fP)=\sigma_{i}(f)\partial_{i}(P)S_{i}x_{i}
.
In particular,
\rho_{i}
is uniquely determined by the value
\rho_{i}(x_{i})=S_{i}x_{i}
.

Proof.

Parts (a) and (b) follow from a direct verification.
Part (c) follows from (b), and part (d) follows from another direct calculation, see
[
LM25
, (3.1)]
.
∎

Definition 2.1.2
.

Denote by
\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
the

polynomial quantum wreath product
defined in
[
LM25
, Definition 3.7]
for the choices
\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{C}[C_{m}]
,
\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]
,

R=q(t^{k}\otimes t^{k})
for some
0\leq k<m

and
\beta=(q-1)ex_{1}
.
In other words,
\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
denotes a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by
H_{1},\cdots,H_{d-1}
and elements in
\mathbb{B}^{\otimes d}
, subject to the following relations:

(2.1.5)

(braid relations)

\displaystyle H_{l}H_{l+1}H_{l}=H_{l+1}H_{l}H_{l+1},\quad H_{i}H_{j}=H_{j}H_{i},

(2.1.6)

\displaystyle H_{i}^{2}={S_{i}}H_{i}+R_{i},

(2.1.7)

(wreath relations)

\displaystyle H_{i}b=\sigma_{i}(b)H_{i}+\rho_{i}(b),

for

In particular, (

) implies the following relations, thanks to

(d):

(2.1.9)

\displaystyle H_{i}f=\sigma_{i}(f)H_{i},

\displaystyle\qquad(f\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}),

(2.1.10)

\displaystyle H_{i}P=\sigma_{i}(P)H_{i}+\partial_{i}(P)S_{i}x_{i},

\displaystyle\qquad(P\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{d}^{\pm 1}]).

Example 2.1.3
.

Consider the special case when

2.1.1.
Affine Yokonuma Hecke Algebras

While
q
can be generic in
[
CS16
]
, here we focus on the case when
q
is the order of a finite field, i.e.,
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
.
Consider the polynomial quantum wreath product
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}\cong\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
for the choices in (
2.1.8
), with

(2.1.11)

R=q(1\otimes 1).

It is surprising that (
2.1.6
), although involving idempotents in
\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
, does split as follows:

Lemma 2.1.6
.

For each
i
, the equation

(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})=0=(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})

holds in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}
,
where
\gamma_{i},\overline{\gamma}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{\otimes d}
are given by:

(2.1.12)

\gamma_{i}:=(\sqrt{q}+1)e_{i}-\sqrt{q}(1^{\otimes d}),\quad\overline{\gamma}_{i}:=(\sqrt{q}+q)e_{i}-\sqrt{q}(1^{\otimes d}).

The proof of Lemma
2.1.6
follows from a direct computation.
It can also be proved under a more general setup (see Lemma
2.1.11
), so we omit the calculation here.

Remark 2.1.7
.

Note that the sign convention is chosen so that

(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})H_{i}=(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})\overline{\gamma}_{i}\quad\textup{and}\quad(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})H_{i}=(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})\gamma_{i},

i.e.,

\overline{\gamma}_{i}
is an
H_{i}
-eigenvalue with respect to the eigenvector
H_{i}+\gamma_{i}
,
and
-\gamma_{i}
is an
H_{i}
-eigenvalue with respect to the eigenvector
H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i}
.

Remark 2.1.8
.

Since the braid relations hold, elements of the form
H_{w}
for
w\in\Sigma_{d}
are well-defined.
Recall from
2.1.3
that
\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
is isomorphic to the affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}
.
In this case, the subspace
\mathrm{Span}{C}\{H_{w}~|~w\in\Sigma_{d}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}
forms a
\mathbb{C}
-subalgebra that is isomorphic to the finite Hecke algebra.

In the Yokonuma case, the subspace
\mathrm{Span}{C}\{H_{w}~|~w\in\Sigma_{d}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}
is not closed under multiplication; as such the finite (or affine) Hecke algebra is not a subalgebra of the affine Yokonuma algebra.

Remark 2.1.9
.

We note that the finite Yokonuma Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{Y}
can also be realized as a quantum wreath product
\mathcal{H}_{Y}\cong\mathbb{F}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
.
The wreath relation (
2.1.7
) becomes
H_{i}f=\sigma_{i}(f)H_{i}
for
f\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
,
since it follows from (
2.1.4
) and
\partial_{i}(P)=0
that
\rho_{i}(f)=0
.

\mathcal{H}_{Y}
is then generated by elements
H_{1},\cdots H_{d-1}
and elements in
\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
and can easily be seen to be a subalgebra of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}
.

2.1.2.
The Splitting Lemma

Within this section, consider the quantum wreath product
\mathbb{F}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
for the choices in (
2.1.8
), with
R=q(t^{k}\otimes t^{k})
for some
0\leq k\leq m-1
.
When
R=q(t^{k}\otimes t^{k})
is not a scalar (i.e.
k\neq 0
), the existence of an element
\sqrt{R}\in\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{F}
that squares into
R
is not guaranteed,
and hence one cannot always renormalize the quadratic relations into ones taking the form
T^{2}\in 1+(\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{F})T
.

We begin with a lemma regarding interaction with the idempotent
e
.

Lemma 2.1.10
.

(a)

The element
e
is an intertwiner. That is,
e(f\otimes f^{\prime})=(f^{\prime}\otimes f)e
for all
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
.

(b)

The following hold:
(t\otimes 1)e=e(t\otimes 1)=(1\otimes t)e=e(1\otimes t)
.

(c)

The set
\{e,(t\otimes 1)e,\dots,(t^{m-1}\otimes 1)e\}
forms a
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
(\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{F})e
.
In particular, any element in
(\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{F})e
must take the form
ae
for some
a\in\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Part (a) is a special case of
[
WW08
, Lemma 2.6]
.
Part (b) follows from the commutativity of
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{m}-1)
and part (a).
Part (c) follows from part (b).
∎

Next, we prove the splitting lemma.

Lemma 2.1.11
.

Let
\gamma=ae+b
for some
a\in\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{C}
,
b\in\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{F}
,
and let
\overline{\gamma}:=\sigma(\gamma)+(q-1)e
.
Then, the equation

(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})=0=(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})

holds in
\mathbb{F}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
,
for any of the following choices:

b\in\{\pm\sqrt{q}(t^{i}\otimes t^{k-i})~|~0\leq i\leq k\},\quad a\in\{-b-q,-b+1\}.

In particular, one has
(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})=0=(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})
where:

(2.1.13)

\gamma_{i}=(\sqrt{q}t^{k}_{i}+1)e_{i}-\sqrt{q}t^{k}_{i},\quad\overline{\gamma}_{i}=(\sqrt{q}t^{k}_{i+1}+q)e_{i}-\sqrt{q}t^{k}_{i+1}.

Proof.

First, we show that
(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})=0
is equivalent to
(H_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{i})(H_{i}+\gamma_{i})=0
for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}\otimes\mathbb{F}
, equivalently,

\gamma H-H\overline{\gamma}=H\gamma-\overline{\gamma}H,\quad\gamma\overline{\gamma}=\overline{\gamma}\gamma.

The first equality holds since
\gamma-\sigma(\overline{\gamma})=\gamma-\sigma^{2}(\gamma)+\sigma(S)=S=\sigma(\gamma)-\overline{\gamma}
.
The second equality follows from the fact that
\mathbb{F}
is commutative.

Next, it follows from Lemma
2.1.10

(2.1.14)

ae=\sigma(a)e,\quad be=e\sigma(b),\quad b^{2}e=be\sigma(b)=b\sigma(b)e.

For simplicity, we may assume that
d=2
and let
H:=H_{1}
. Now,

(H+\gamma)(H-\overline{\gamma})=H^{2}-(\sigma(\overline{\gamma})-\gamma)H-\gamma\overline{\gamma}.

Since
\sigma(\overline{\gamma})=\gamma+(q-1)e
, the degree one coefficient is precisely
S
.
It remains to determine the values of
a
and
b
such that
R=\gamma\overline{\gamma}
.
Expanding
\gamma\overline{\gamma}
and applying (
2.1.14
), we get

\begin{split}q(t^{k}\otimes t^{k})&=(ae+b)(\sigma(a)e+\sigma(b)+(q-1)e)\\
&=ae\sigma(a)e+(q-1)ae^{2}+ae\sigma(b)+b\sigma(a)e+(q-1)be+b\sigma(b)\\
&=(a^{2}+(2b+(q-1))a+(q-1)b)e+b\sigma(b).\end{split}

That is,
b\sigma(b)=q(t^{k}\otimes t^{k})
and hence
b\in\{\pm\sqrt{q}(t^{i}\otimes t^{k-i})~|~0\leq i\leq k\}
.
Moreover,

0=(a^{2}+(2b+(q-1))a+(q-1)b)e=((a+b+\tfrac{q-1}{2})^{2}-(b+\tfrac{q-1}{2})^{2}+(q-1)b)e.

Note that

(-(b+\tfrac{q-1}{2})^{2}+(q-1)b)e=(-b^{2}-(\tfrac{q-1}{2})^{2})e
. It follows from (
2.1.14
) that

-b^{2}e-(\tfrac{q-1}{2})^{2}e=-b\sigma(b)e-\tfrac{q^{2}-2q+1}{4}e=-(\tfrac{q+1}{2})^{2}e,

which leads to the fact that

0=((a+b+\tfrac{q-1}{2})^{2}-(\tfrac{q+1}{2})^{2})e=(a+b+q)(a+b-1)e.

Therefore,
a\in\{-b-q,-b+1\}
.
∎

2.2.
The Kashiwara–Miwa–Stern tensor space

For the rest of this section, consider the polynomial quantum wreath product
\mathbf{H}:=\mathbb{F}[x^{\pm 1}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
for the choices in (
2.1.8
), with
R=q(t^{k}\otimes t^{k})
for some
0\leq k\leq m-1
.
In this section, we describe the analogs of the KMS tensor space
[
KMS95
, (32)]
for any such
\mathbf{H}

, which includes the affine Yokonuma Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}^{Y}
.

Fix a positive integer
N
.
Let
\mathbb{V}
be the free right
\mathbb{F}
-module with basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

Let

for all

Next, the

\{v_{f}:=v_{f(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{f(d)}|f:[d]\to\mathbb{Z}\}

Denote by

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Recall elements

and

) and (

).

(a)

There is a unique right
\mathbf{H}
-module structure on

determined by the following:

(2.2.1)

v_{f}(-\gamma_{i})&\textup{if }f(i)=f(i+1),\\
v_{f\cdot s_{i}}R_{i}+v_{f}(q-1)e_{i}&\textup{if }f(i)>f(i+1),\end{cases}

where
f
is in the fundamental region, i.e.
f(i)\in\{1,\dots,N\}
for all

.

(b)

There is a decomposition into
\mathbf{H}
-submodules as follows:

\mathbb{V}^{\otimes d}\cong\bigoplus\nolimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N,d}}M^{\lambda},

where
\Lambda_{N,d}
consists of all weak compositions of
d
of
N
-steps,
and each submodule

is generated from the basis element

v_{\lambda}:=v_{1}^{\otimes\lambda_{1}}\otimes v_{2}^{\otimes\lambda_{2}}\otimes\dots v_{N}^{\otimes\lambda_{N}}\in\mathbb{V}^{\otimes d}
.

Proof.

Part (a) is a special case of
[
LM25

, since

is a commutative algebra, and the quadratic relation splits as in

. Part (b) is a consequence of part (a).
∎

Example 2.2.2
.

(a)

Suppose that

(b)

Let
N\geq 2
and let

.
Suppose that

is given by

(c)

Let

If

Therefore,

\begin{split}(v_{1}\otimes v_{3})\cdot H_{1}&=(v_{1}\otimes v_{1})(H_{1}x_{1}-(q-1)e_{1}x_{1})\\

2.3.
Wreath modules

[

For each

2.4.
Spherical and antispherical modules

For any Specht module

-mod, the induced module

\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbf{H}_{Y}}^{\mathbf{H}}(S_{Y}^{\nu})=S_{Y}^{\nu}\otimes_{\mathbf{H}_{Y}}\mathbf{H}

is a right module over
\mathbf{H}
.
In particular, when
\nu=(d)
or
(1^{d})

\mathrm{sph}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbf{H}_{Y}}^{\mathbf{H}}(S_{Y}^{(d)})=\mathrm{triv}\otimes_{\mathbf{H}_{Y}}\mathbf{H},\qquad\mathrm{asph}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbf{H}_{Y}}^{\mathbf{H}}(S_{Y}^{(1^{d})})=\mathrm{sgn}\otimes_{\mathbf{H}_{Y}}\mathbf{H}.

The action of
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}^{\otimes d}
is given by
(1\otimes b)\cdot b^{\prime}:=1\otimes(bb^{\prime})
and the action of
H_{j},1\leq j\leq d-1

(2.4.1)

(1\otimes b)\cdot H_{j}:=1\otimes(H_{j}\sigma_{j}(b)+\rho_{j}(b))=\begin{cases}1\otimes(\rho_{j}(b)+\overline{\gamma}_{j}\sigma_{j}(b))&\textup{for }\mathrm{sph},\\
1\otimes(\rho_{j}(b)-\gamma_{j}\sigma_{j}(b))&\textup{for }\mathrm{asph}.\end{cases}

By a direction comparison between (
2.3.2
) and (
2.4.1
), we obtain the following.

Proposition 2.4.1
.

As
\mathbf{H}
-modules, the (anti)-spherical modules are wreath modules. To be precise,

\mathrm{sph}\cong\mathbb{B}\wr\mathrm{triv}
and
\mathrm{asph}\cong\mathbb{B}\wr\mathrm{sgn}

Remark 2.4.2
.

The antispherical module of the affine Hecke algebra is fundamental in representation theory.
It is isomorphic to the Grothendieck ring of
\Rep_{0}

over a field

of characteristic

[

In this paper we essentially prove that the newly constructed antispherical module for the affine Yokonuma algebra models the pro-

Lemma 3.1.1

(\mathsf{Q},n)
) we may associate a Brylinski–Deligne central extension
\widetilde{G}
of
G

[
BD01
]
(see also
[
GG18
]
or
[
GGK24
, §2.3]
for details on this construction):

1\to\mu_{n}\to\widetilde{G}\to G\to 1.

Since
Y\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{Z}\epsilon_{i}
,
a bilinear form with the properties above will depend on two integers
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}
:

(4.2.2)

\mathsf{B}(\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j})=\begin{cases}2\mathbf{p}&\text{ if }i=j;\\
\mathbf{q}&\text{ if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

We will focus on the case when
\mathbf{q}
is divisible by
n
.
For simplicity, we set
\mathbf{p}=1,\mathbf{q}=0
; the general case can be reduced to this case quite easily.
This implies
\mathsf{Q}(\alpha_{i})=1
for
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
.
This cover is sometimes called Savin’s nice cover and is the one appearing in
[
GGK25
]
or
[
BBB19
,
BBBG20
]
.
An alternative cover, with
2\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}=-1
is called the Kazhdan–Patterson cover and is quite important in the theory of automorphic forms.
It seems non-trivial at the moment to extend our results to the Kazhdan–Patterson covers or to the most general cover of
G
.

Let us define
Y_{(\mathsf{Q},n)}:=\{y\in Y,\mathsf{B}(y,z)\cong 0\pmod{n}\mbox{ for all }z\in Y\}
.
In our case, we have
Y_{(\mathsf{Q},n)}=\bar{n}Y
, where
\bar{n}=\frac{n}{\gcd(2,n)}
.

4.3.
Metaplectic Hecke algebras

We would like to define Hecke algebras with respect to certain compact subgroups of
G
.
Let
K=\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G
.
The reduction mod
\varpi
map
\mathfrak{r}:\mathcal{O}\to\kappa:=\mathcal{O}/\varpi\mathcal{O}
induces a map
\mathfrak{r}:K\to\mathbf{G}(\kappa)
and we define the Iwahori subgroup
I:=\mathfrak{r}^{-1}(\mathbf{B}(\kappa))
and the pro-p Iwahori subgroup
I^{(1)}:=\mathfrak{r}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}(\kappa))
.

The group
\widetilde{G}
splits (canonically) over
U^{-}
and (non-canonically) over
K
; we fix a splitting
s_{K}:K\to\widetilde{G}
.
We can therefore view
U^{-}
and
I^{(1)}\subset I\subset K
as subgroups of
\widetilde{G}
.
This allows us to define metaplectic Hecke algebras (and their Gelfand–Graev modules).
Fix an embedding
\iota:\mu_{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Define the metaplectic pro-
p
Iwahori Hecke algebra of
\iota
-
genuine
functions:

(4.3.1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}:=C^{\infty}_{c,\iota}(I^{(1)}\backslash{\widetilde{G}}/I^{(1)})=\{f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{G})|f(\zeta k_{1}gk_{2})=\iota(\zeta)f(g)\mbox{ for all }g\in\widetilde{G},k_{1},k_{2}\in I^{(1)},\zeta\in\mu_{n}\}.

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
is an algebra, with multiplication given by convolution:
f_{1}*f_{2}(g)=\int_{G}f_{1}(h)f_{2}(h^{-1}g)dh
, where
dh
is the Haar measure on
G
normalized such that
\int_{I^{(1)}}dh=1
.
We can also define

(4.3.2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}:=C^{\infty}_{c,\iota}(I^{(1)}\backslash{\widetilde{K}}/I^{(1)})\cong C^{\infty}_{c}(I^{(1)}\backslash{K}/I^{(1)});

the isomorphism above is due to the splitting of
K
and the genuine condition on the left side.
Similarly, one may define the algebras
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{I}:=C^{\infty}_{c,\iota}(I\backslash{\widetilde{G}}/I)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\kappa}:=C^{\infty}_{c,\iota}(I\backslash{\widetilde{K}}/I)\cong C^{\infty}_{c}(I\backslash{K}/I)
.
Note that if we set
e_{I}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
to be the characteristic function of
I\in\widetilde{G}
, then one has the standard
p
-adic isomorphism

(4.3.3)

e_{I}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}e_{I}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{I}.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}_{g}
the element in the corresponding Hecke algebra (
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
or
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}
) that is supported on
\mu_{n}I^{(1)}gI^{(1)}
and which takes value
1
at
g
.
This element is well-defined cf.
[
GGK24
, §3.1]
.

For
\alpha\in\Phi
, let
\{\bar{e}_{\alpha}(a),a\in F\}
be the corresponding root subgroups in
\widetilde{G}
(cf.
[
GGK24
, §2.3]
). Define

(4.3.4)

\bar{w}_{\alpha}(a):=\bar{e}_{\alpha}(a)\bar{e}_{-\alpha}(-a^{-1})\bar{e}_{\alpha}(a),\quad\bar{h}_{\alpha}(a):=\bar{w}_{\alpha}(a)\bar{w}_{\alpha}(-1)\mbox{ for }a\in F,\quad\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}:=\mathcal{T}_{\bar{w}_{\alpha}(1)}.

4.3.1.
The finite Yokonuma algebra

Recall the finite field
\kappa
with
q
elements.
The group
\kappa^{*}
is cyclic of order
q-1
with generator
t
, so we may write
\kappa^{*}\cong C_{q-1}
.

Let us denote by
G_{\kappa},B_{\kappa},U_{\kappa},etc.
the
\kappa
points of the corresponding group (i.e.
G_{\kappa}:=\mathbf{G}(\kappa)
, etc.).
Then
T_{\kappa}=\mathbf{T}(\kappa)\cong C_{q-1}^{d}
.
We have a natural algebra isomorphism
C^{\infty}_{c}(U_{\kappa}\backslash{G_{\kappa}}/U_{\kappa})\cong C^{\infty}_{c}(I^{(1)}\backslash{K}/I^{(1)})=\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}
induced by the map
\mathfrak{r}
.
Both algebras have basis indexed by
W\ltimes T_{\kappa}\cong U_{\kappa}\backslash{G_{\kappa}}/U_{\kappa}\cong I^{(1)}\backslash{K}/I^{(1)}
.

For
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{*})=\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mu_{q-1})
define:

(4.3.5)

c(\chi):=\frac{1}{|T_{\kappa}|}\sum_{\tau\in T_{\kappa}}\chi(t)\mathcal{T}_{\tau}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U_{\kappa}\backslash{G_{\kappa}}/U_{\kappa})\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}.

The element above can also be thought of as an element in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
; we shall denote both elements by the same symbol; it should be clear from context where any given elements lives.
Define:

(4.3.6)

c_{\alpha}(\chi)=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{u\in\kappa^{*}}\chi(u)\mathcal{T}_{\bar{h}_{\alpha}(u)}\textup{ for }\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\kappa^{*},\mu_{n}).

The algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}
is generated by
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}},\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
and
c(\chi)
,
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{*})
subject to relations:

(4.3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}c(\chi)=c(s_{i}\chi)\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}},

(4.3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}^{2}=q\mathcal{T}_{\bar{h}_{\alpha_{i}}(-1)}+(q-1)c_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbf{1})\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}},

(4.3.9)

\displaystyle c(\chi)c(\chi^{\prime})=c(\chi^{\prime})c(\chi).

and the braid relations for the
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}
(cf.
[
GGK24
, Prop. 3.4, Lemmas 3.2 and 4.5]
).

The braid relations allows us to define
\mathcal{T}_{w}:=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i_{1}}}\cdots\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i_{k}}}
for a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}},\cdots s_{i_{k}}
.
Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}
has basis
\{c(\chi)\mathcal{T}_{w}\}
for
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{*}),w\in W
.

4.3.2.
The metaplectic pro-
p
Hecke algebra

The structure of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
is described in
[
GGK24
]
(and in the
n=1
setting by Vigneras
[
Vig16
]
), which we will now present.
The pro-
p
Iwahori Weyl group is

(4.3.10)

W^{(1)}:=(T_{\kappa}\times Y)\rtimes W=(C_{q-1}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d})\rtimes\Sigma_{d}\cong(C_{q-1}\times\mathbb{Z})\wr\Sigma_{d}.

An arbitrary element in
W^{(1)}
is of the form
a\lambda w
for some
a\in T_{\kappa}
,
\lambda\in Y
,
w\in W
.

Let
Y_{\lambda}
denote the element
\mathcal{T}_{\varpi^{\lambda^{\prime}}}\mathcal{T}_{\varpi^{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}}^{-1}
for any
\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}
dominant such that
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}
(
Y_{\lambda}
is indeed well-defined based on the same argument used in the Iwahori Hecke algebra case).
Moreover, let

(4.3.11)

\varphi(\lambda)\in\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{*}),\quad\varphi(\lambda)(s):=[\varpi^{\lambda},s],

where the commutation relation is in the metaplectic group.

The following result gives a presentation by generators and relations of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
; it is a summary of
[
GGK24
, Lemma 3.2, Prop. 3.4, Lemma 4.5, Prop 4.6, Thm. 4.18]
.

Theorem 4.3.1

(
[
GGK24
]
)
.

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
has generators
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}
for
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
,
Y_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in Y
,
c(\chi)
for
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{*})
which are
subject to the following relations

(4.3.12)

(braid relations)

\displaystyle\textup{braid relations for the }\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}},

(4.3.13)

(quadratic relations)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}^{2}=q\mathcal{T}_{\bar{h}_{\alpha_{i}}(-1)}+(q-1)c_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbf{1})\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}},

(4.3.14)

(commutation relations)

\displaystyle Y_{\lambda}Y_{\mu}=Y_{\lambda+\mu}=Y_{\mu}Y_{\lambda},\quad c(\chi)c(\chi^{\prime})=c(\chi^{\prime})c(\chi),

(4.3.15)

(mixed relations)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}c(\chi)=c(s_{i}\chi)\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}},\quad Y_{\lambda}c(\chi)=c(\varphi(\lambda)\chi)Y_{\lambda},

(Bernstein relations)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}Y_{\lambda}+(q-1)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\epsilon^{j(1+\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle)}Y_{\lambda+j\alpha_{i}}c_{\alpha_{i}}(j+\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle)=\quad\quad

(4.3.16)

\displaystyle Y_{s_{i}\lambda}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}+(q-1)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1-\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle}}\epsilon^{j(1+\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle)}Y_{\lambda+j\alpha_{i}}c_{\alpha_{i}}(j+\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle).

There is a natural bijection
W^{(1)}\cong\mu_{n}I^{(1)}\backslash\widetilde{G}/I^{(1)}

[
Vig16
, Prop. 3.35]
; as such
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
has a basis indexed by elements in
W^{(1)}
.
The relations in Theorem
4.3.1
allow us to specify this basis.

Corollary 4.3.2
.

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
has basis
\{c(\chi)Y_{\lambda}\mathcal{T}_{w}\}
(or basis
\{Y_{\lambda}c(\chi)\mathcal{T}_{w}\}
) for
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{*}),\lambda\in Y,w\in W
.

It is a standard fact that the map
c(\chi)\mathcal{T}_{w}\mapsto c(\chi)\mathcal{T}_{w}
realizes
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}
as a subalgebra of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
.

4.4.
Metaplectic Gelfand–Graev modules

Let
\psi:U^{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a nondegenerate character of conductor
\varpi\mathcal{O}
as in
[
GGK24
, p. 30]
. This character descends to a non-degenerate character (denoted by the same symbol; it should be clear which character we use at any given time)
\psi:U_{\kappa}^{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

4.4.1.
Finite Gelfand–Graev module

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\kappa}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{U_{\kappa}^{-}}^{G_{\kappa}}(\psi)^{U_{\kappa}}
.
The algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}:=C_{c}^{\infty}(I^{(1)}\backslash K/I^{(1)})
is isomorphic to
C_{c}^{\infty}(U_{\kappa}\backslash G_{\kappa}/U_{\kappa})
and acts on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\kappa}
by convolution.
Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\kappa}\cong\mathbb{C}[T_{\kappa}]
as
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}
-modules
[
GGK24
, Lem 5.4]
.

More precisely,
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\kappa}
has basis
c^{\mathbf{g}}(\chi)
for
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{*})
.
The action of
c(\chi)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\kappa}
is given by multiplication in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}_{\kappa}
.
The action of
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}
is given by
[
GGK24
, (5.2)]
:

(4.4.1)

c^{\mathbf{g}}(\chi)\mathcal{T}_{\alpha_{i}}=\mathbf{g}(\psi_{\alpha_{i}},\chi_{\alpha_{i}})c^{\mathbf{g}}(s_{i}\chi),

where
\chi_{\alpha}(u):=\chi(\hbar_{-\alpha}(u))
and
\psi_{\alpha}:=\psi(\bar{e}_{-\alpha}(u))
and
\mathbf{g}
is the Gauss sum defined in (
4.2.1
).

To specify the Gauss sums, note that once you identify
\chi_{\alpha_{i}}
with an element
u^{k}\in\kappa^{*}
, then
\mathbf{g}(\psi_{\alpha_{i}},\chi_{\alpha_{i}})=\mathbf{g}(\psi_{\alpha},(u,\varpi)_{n}^{k})
(cf.
[
GGK24
, p. 65]
).
The character
\psi
is a fixed non-degenerate additive character with the properties discussed above, the Gauss sum appearing in (
4.4.1
) do not depend on it or on the simple root
\alpha_{i}
.
Because of this, if we identify
\chi
with
\gamma\in[m]^{d}
(as we will do in §
5.1
), then
\mathbf{g}_{\chi_{\alpha_{i}}}
only depends on
k_{\chi_{\alpha_{i}}}=\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i+1}
, so we may write
\mathbf{g}(\psi_{\alpha_{i}},\chi_{\alpha_{i}})=\mathbf{g}_{k_{\chi_{\alpha_{i}}}}
with
k_{\chi_{\alpha_{i}}}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
These Gauss sums will satisfy the relations (cf.
[
GGK24
, p. 65]
,
[
GSS23
, p. 33]
, see also
[
PP17
, (4.1)]
):

(4.4.2)

\mathbf{g}_{k}=\mathbf{g}_{k+m},\quad\mathbf{g}_{k}\mathbf{g}_{-k}=q\quad\textup{ for }k\neq 0\pmod{m},\quad\textup{ and }\quad\mathbf{g}_{0}=-1.

While it is true that
|\mathbf{g}_{k}|=\sqrt{q}
when
k\neq 0\pmod{m}
,
\mathbf{g}_{k}
is generally not equal to
\pm\sqrt{q}
.
Gauss sums contain important number theoretic information, so it is important to keep accurate track of them.

Remark 4.4.1
.

We note here that the appearance of the Gauss sums in (
4.4.1
) is a pro-
p
Iwahori phenomena and
not
a metaplectic phenomena.
These Gauss sums appear non-trivially even when the degree of the metaplectic cover we are working with is
n=1
, while when working at the Iwahori level we only notice Gauss sums when working with metaplectic covers.

The

-adic Gelfand–Graev module

Define the

\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{I^{(1)}}:=\mathrm{ind}_{\mu_{n}U^{-}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\iota\otimes\psi)^{I^{(1)}}.

Similarly we may define the

The following description of

5.
The Identification Theorems

In this section we prove our main results at the pro-
p

We identify the pro-
p

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
with the skew polynomial quantum wreath product
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
, as well as identify the Gelfand–Graev module
{\mathcal{W}}^{I^{(1)}}
with the (generalized) antispherical module
\mathbb{V}(1)\wr\mathrm{sgn}
.
As corollary of these two results and Proposition
3.4.1
, we give a basis with multiplication formula for the
p

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{(1)}
.
Throughout, we explain how the main results at the pro-

.

5.1.
The toral elements
c(\chi)\in T_{\kappa}

Set
m=q-1
to be the order of the cyclic group
\kappa^{\times}\cong C_{m}
generated by an element
t
.
Then
T_{\kappa}\cong C_{m}^{d}
.
Write
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{m}-1)
.
We use the identification:

T_{\kappa}\cong\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d},\qquad\tau^{a}:=(t^{a_{1}},\dots,t^{a_{d}})\mapsto t^{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes t^{a_{d}},

where
a=(a_{i})_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Next, let
u
be a primitive
n
-root of unity and let
\zeta=(\varpi,t)_{n}=u^{l}\in\mu_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Any character
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
can be identified with
\chi=(\chi_{1},\cdots,\chi_{d})
, where
\chi_{j}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\kappa^{*},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
is given by
\chi_{j}(t):=\chi(\tau^{\epsilon_{j}})
.
Then for some
\gamma_{j}\in[m]
we have:

\chi(\tau^{a})=\chi_{1}(t)^{a_{1}}\dots\chi_{d}(t)^{a_{d}}=u^{\gamma_{1}a_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{d}a_{d}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

In other words, there is a bijection

[m]^{d}\to\mathrm{Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbb{C}^{*})
,

\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d})\mapsto\chi_{\gamma}
.
Therefore, any
c(\chi)
is of the following form for some
\gamma\in[m]^{d}
:

c(\chi^{\gamma})\cong\frac{1}{m^{d}}\sum_{a\in[m]^{d}}(u^{\gamma_{1}}t)^{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes(u^{\gamma_{d}}t)^{a_{d}}\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}.

In particular,
c(s_{i}\chi^{\gamma})=c(\chi^{s_{i}\cdot\gamma})
where
\Sigma_{d}
acts on
[m]^{d}
by place permutation.
Note that the matrix
(\frac{1}{m}u^{ij})_{i,j}
is invertible with inverse
(u^{-ij})_{i,j}
, therefore one can define another basis
\{{}_{j}t^{\prime}\}_{j=0}^{m-1}
of
\mathbb{F}
:

(5.1.1)

{}_{j}t^{\prime}:=\tfrac{1}{m}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}(u^{j}t)^{i},\quad\textup{ such that }\quad t^{j}=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}u^{-ij}({}_{i}t^{\prime}).

We choose to use the pre-subscript for
{}_{j}t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}
instead of a subscript since, following our convention (
2.1.2
), a subscript is reserved for an element in
\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
.
With this basis
\{{}_{j}t^{\prime}\}_{j}
, one then obtain a simpler expression for the toral elements as below:

(5.1.2)

c(\chi^{(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d})})=({}_{\gamma_{1}}t^{\prime})\otimes\dots\otimes({}_{\gamma_{d}}t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}.

Example 5.1.1
.

Suppose that
m=2
. Then

{}_{1}t^{\prime}=\tfrac{1+ut}{2},\quad{}_{0}t^{\prime}=\tfrac{1+t}{2},\quad c(\chi^{(1,0)})=\tfrac{1}{4}(1\otimes 1+ut\otimes 1+1\otimes t+ut\otimes t)=({}_{1}t^{\prime})\otimes({}_{0}t^{\prime}).

Moreover, when both pre-scripts and subscripts are in use, we have

{}_{0}t^{\prime}_{1}=(\tfrac{1+t}{2})\otimes 1,\quad{}_{0}t^{\prime}_{2}=1\otimes(\tfrac{1+t}{2}),\quad{}_{1}t^{\prime}_{1}=(\tfrac{1+ut}{2})\otimes 1,\quad{}_{1}t^{\prime}_{2}=1\otimes(\tfrac{1+ut}{2}).

Next, for
\lambda=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\epsilon_{j}\in Y=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{Z}\epsilon_{j}
, the element
\varphi(\lambda)
defined in (
4.3.11
) is the following map:

(5.1.3)

\varphi(\lambda):T_{\kappa}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\quad\tau^{a}\mapsto[\varpi^{\lambda},\tau^{a}]=(u^{l})^{B(\lambda,\tau^{a})}=u^{\sum_{j}2l\lambda_{j}a_{j}}=\zeta^{\sum_{j}2\lambda_{j}a_{j}}.

Therefore we may identify
\varphi(\lambda)
with
\chi^{2l\lambda}
.

Lemma 5.1.2
.

The following relations holds in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(1)}
, for

\lambda\in Y
,

j,a_{i}\in[m]
:

Y_{\epsilon_{1}}(t^{j}\otimes 1^{\otimes d-1})=\zeta^{2j}(t^{j}\otimes 1^{\otimes d-1})Y_{\epsilon_{1}},\qquad Y_{\lambda}(t^{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes t^{a_{d}})=\zeta^{2\sum_{j}a_{j}\lambda_{j}}(t^{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes t^{a_{d}})Y_{\lambda}.

Proof.

Note that it follows from (
5.1.3
) that

\varphi(\lambda)_{j}(t)=u^{2l\lambda_{j}}
for each
1\leq j\leq d
, and hence

(5.1.4)

c(\varphi(\lambda)\chi^{\gamma})\cong\frac{1}{m^{d}}\sum_{a\in[m]^{d}}(u^{2l\lambda_{1}+\gamma_{1}}t)^{a_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes(u^{2l\lambda_{d}+\gamma_{d}}t)^{a_{d}}=c(\chi^{(\gamma_{1}+2l\lambda_{1},\dots,\gamma_{d}+2l\lambda_{d})}).

It then follows from (
5.1.1
) and (
5.1.4
) that

(5.1.5)

\begin{split}Y_{\epsilon_{1}}(t\otimes 1^{\otimes d-1})&=Y_{\epsilon_{1}}\Big(\big(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}u^{-i}({}_{i}t^{\prime})\big)\otimes 1^{\otimes d-1}\Big)=\Big(\big(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}u^{-i}({}_{i+2l}t^{\prime})\big)\otimes 1^{\otimes d-1}\Big)Y_{\epsilon_{1}}\\
&=u^{2l}\Big(\big(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}u^{-i-2l}({}_{i+2l}t^{\prime})\big)\otimes 1^{\otimes d-1}\Big)Y_{\epsilon_{1}}=\zeta^{2}(t\otimes 1^{\otimes d-1})Y_{\epsilon_{1}}.\end{split}

The second formula then follows from applying (

From now on, write

defined just before (

We will now show how to derive (

Let us start with a computational lemma.

Lemma 5.2.4
.

The following equalities hold in
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
:

(a)

(t^{\prime})^{2}=t^{\prime}
, and hence
\epsilon_{I}^{2}=\epsilon_{I}
.

(b)

\epsilon_{I}Y_{\lambda}\epsilon_{I}=0
unless
\lambda\in Y_{(Q,n)}=\bar{n}Y
.

(c)

t^{\prime}t=tt^{\prime}=t^{\prime}
, and hence
\epsilon_{I}f=f\epsilon_{I}
for all
f\in\mathbb{F}^{\otimes d}
.

(d)

H_{i}\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}H_{i}
for all
1\leq i<d
.

(e)

\epsilon_{I}t_{i}=t_{i}\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}
for all
1\leq i\leq d
.
In particular,
e_{i}\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}e_{i}
.

Proof.

Parts (a), (c) and (d) follow from a direct verification.
For part (b), pick any
i
, by (a) we have
\epsilon_{I}=t^{\prime}_{i}\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}t^{\prime}_{i}
, and hence

e_{I}Y_{\lambda}e_{I}=e_{I}Y_{\lambda}t^{\prime}_{i}e_{I}=e_{I}\zeta^{2\lambda_{i}}t^{\prime}_{i}Y_{\lambda}e_{I}=\zeta^{2\lambda_{i}}e_{I}Y_{\lambda}e_{I}.

Thus,
0=(1-\zeta^{2\lambda_{i}})e_{I}Y_{\lambda}e_{I}
.
That is,
e_{I}Y_{\lambda}e_{I}=0
unless
\lambda_{i}
divides
\bar{n}
for any
i
, which is equivalent to that
\lambda\in Y_{Q,n}
.
Finally, part (e) follows from (c) and the fact that

\begin{split}(t^{\prime}\otimes t^{\prime})e&=\frac{1}{m}\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{m}(t^{\prime}t^{j})\otimes(t^{\prime}t^{-j})=\frac{1}{m}\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{m}t^{\prime}\otimes t^{\prime}\\
&=(t^{\prime}\otimes t^{\prime})\\
&=\frac{1}{m}\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{m}(t^{j}t^{\prime})\otimes(t^{-j}t^{\prime})=(t^{\prime}\otimes t^{\prime})e.\qed\end{split}

Proposition 5.2.5
.

The following map is an algebra isomorphism:

\Upsilon:\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm\bar{n}}]\wr\mathcal{H}(d)\to\epsilon_{I}(\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d))\epsilon_{I},\quad x^{\bar{n}\lambda}\mapsto\epsilon_{I}x^{\bar{n}\lambda}\epsilon_{I}.

Proof.

Thanks to
3.1.5
and
5.2.4
(b), this map
\Upsilon
is a bijection, and it remains to check that
\Upsilon
is also an algebra homomorphism.
Note that by
5.2.4
(a) and (d),

\Upsilon(H_{i}H_{j})=(\epsilon_{I}H_{i}\epsilon_{I})(\epsilon_{I}H_{j}\epsilon_{I})=\epsilon_{I}H_{i}\epsilon_{I}^{2}H_{j}\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}H_{i}\epsilon_{I}H_{j}\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}H_{i}H_{j}\epsilon_{I}^{2}=\epsilon_{I}H_{i}H_{j}\epsilon_{I}=\Upsilon(H_{i}H_{j}),

and hence the braid relations are preserved.
For the quadratic relations, we have

\Upsilon(H_{i})^{2}=\epsilon_{I}H_{i}\epsilon_{I}H_{i}\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}H_{i}^{2}\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}(S_{i}H_{i}+R_{i})\epsilon_{I}=\epsilon_{I}S_{i}H_{i}\epsilon_{I}^{2}+q\epsilon_{I},

where we use
5.2.4
(a) and (d).
Note that by
5.2.4
(e) we have
\epsilon_{I}S_{i}=(q-1)\epsilon_{I}
.
Thus,
\Upsilon(H_{i})^{2}=(q-1)\Upsilon(H_{i})+q\epsilon_{I}=\Upsilon(H_{i}^{2})
.

Finally, for the wreath relations, it suffices to verify the
d=2
case when
\lambda
is the minuscule weight, i.e.,
\lambda=\bar{n}\epsilon_{1}
. It follows from
3.1.3
(b) that, by setting

, the following holds in
\mathbb{A}\wr\mathcal{H}(d)
:

H_{1}x_{1}^{\bar{n}}=x_{2}^{\bar{n}}H_{1}+Sx_{1}^{\bar{n}}+x_{2}Sx_{1}^{\bar{n}-1}+\dots+x_{2}^{\bar{n}-1}Sx_{1}.

Applying
5.2.4
(b) and (e), we obtain

\begin{split}\Upsilon(H_{1})\Upsilon(x_{1}^{\bar{n}})&=(\epsilon_{I}H_{1}\epsilon_{I})(\epsilon_{I}x_{1}^{\bar{n}}\epsilon_{I})\\
&=\epsilon_{I}(x_{2}^{\bar{n}}H_{1}+Sx_{1}^{\bar{n}}+x_{2}Sx_{1}^{\bar{n}-1}+\dots+x_{2}^{\bar{n}-1}Sx_{1})\epsilon_{I}=(\epsilon_{I}x_{2}^{\bar{n}}\epsilon_{I})(\epsilon_{I}H_{1}\epsilon_{I})+(\epsilon_{I}Sx_{1}^{\bar{n}}\epsilon_{I})\\
&=\Upsilon(x_{2}^{\bar{n}})\Upsilon(H_{1})+(q-1)\Upsilon(x_{1}^{\bar{n}}).\end{split}

Therefore the map
\Upsilon
is indeed an isomorphism.
∎

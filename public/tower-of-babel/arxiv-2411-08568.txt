Enumerating orders in number fields
Markus Kirschmer, Jürgen Klüners

Abstract
. We arrange the orders in an algebraic number field in a tree. This tree can be used to enumerate all orders of bounded index in the maximal order as well as the orders over some given order.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11R54,11Y40

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be an algebraic number field of degree
n
.
An order in
K
is a subring which has rank
n
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module.
Every order in
K
is contained in the ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
, which is therefore the maximal order of
K
.
The Chinese Remainder Theorem shows that for a description of all orders of
K
,
it suffices to describe the orders whose index in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
is a
p
-power of some prime
p
.

Of course in practice, it would be necessary to restrict the enumeration to finitely many orders in
K
, for example the orders whose index in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
is bounded by some constant or the orders above some fixed order
\Lambda
.
For example, Marseglia’s algorithm
[
9
,
10
]
for computing the ideal class monoid of an order
\Lambda
, i.e. the monoid of isomorphism classes of all fractional ideals of
\Lambda
(invertible or not) requires all orders above
\Lambda
.

A direct approach to the first problem is to enumerate the maximal suborders of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
and then iterate this process.
Similarly, for enumerating the overorders of
\Lambda
, Hofmann and Sircana
[
6
]
propose to enumerate the minimal overorders and then iterate this process.
It is worthwhile to mention that the algorithm of Hofmann and Sircana works for orders in (not necessarily commutative) separable algebras over
K
.

These previous methods to compute orderorders and suborders have the following two issues:

•

They enumerate the same orders over and over again.

•

The computation of minimal overorders or maximal suborders require to walk over all subspaces of some finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

We propose a much more structural approach to solve both problems by arranging the orders whose index in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
is a
p
-power in a tree.
This immediately ensures that each order will only be found once.
In light of Zassenhaus’ Round 2 algorithm, a natural choice for this tree is to say that
\mathcal{O}
is a successor of the ring of multipliers
M(\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O}))
of its
p
-radical
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
, see Definition
2.3
.
This turns the set of orders in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
whose index is a
p
-power into a tree with
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
as its root.

The fact that
\mathcal{O}
is a successor of
M(\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O}))
only depends on its
p
-radical
J:=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
.
Hence all orders with
p
-radical
J
will be successors of
M(J)
, so they can be grouped together.
In order to enumerate this tree, it suffices to solve the following tasks:

•

Determine all orders in
K
with a given
p
-radical
J
.

•

Enumerate the
p
-radicals of all successors of a given order
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
.

The first task can be solved efficiently by relating the orders with
p
-radical
J
to all orders with
p
-radical
\JJ_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
, see the last paragraph of Section
3
.
This effectively turns the enumeration of all orders of
p
-power index in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
into an enumeration of their
p
-radicals.
However, we do not know how to find all
p
-radicals
J
with
M(J)=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
without walking over all subspaces of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-space
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})/p\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
.

To overcome this obstacle, we amend our definition of successors as follows.
We say that
\mathcal{O}
is a successor of the ring of multipliers
M(\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})^{n-1})
.
This again yields a tree structure on the set of orders in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
with
p
-power index.
Set
J:=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
.
By a result of Dade, Taussky and Zassenhaus, cf.
[
1
, Theorem C]
, the ideal
J^{n-1}
becomes invertible in
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}:=M(J^{n-1})
.
Assuming that
K
is unramified at
p
, we moreover have
J^{n-1}=p^{n-1}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
or equivalently
H^{n-1}=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
where
H:=p^{-1}J
.
The enumeration of the sublattices
H
of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
with
H^{n-1}=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
can be arranged in a way that requires only very few searches in vector spaces, see Remarks
4.19
and
4.21
for details.
The ramified case is more involved and is discussed in Section 5.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we recall basic definitions of orders in number fields and the relations of an order to its maximal suborders.
In Section 3, we recall the notion of
p
-radicals and give an algorithm to compute all orders with a given
p
-radical.
The next two sections give our algorithm to compute all orders in
K
with bounded index in the maximal order
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
.
Section 4 focuses on the general idea and deals with the generic i.e. unramified case while Section 5 discusses the ramified case.
The last section shows how to augment our approach to compute the overorders of a given order
\Lambda
.
It also contains some examples and compares the running time of our algorithm to the one of Hofmann and Sircana
[
6
]
.

2.
Orders

Let
K
be an algebraic number field of degree
n
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}
or a completion of
\mathbb{Z}
at a prime
p
.
An
R
-lattice in
K
is a finitely generated
R
-submodule of
K
. It is said to be full, if it contains a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis of
K
.
An
R
-order is a subring
\mathcal{O}
of
K
which is a full
R
-lattice.
If
R=\mathbb{Z}
, we usually only speak of lattices and orders.

Let
I
,
J
be full
R
-lattices and
\mathcal{O}
an
R
-order in
K
.
The product
IJ
is the
R
-module generated by
\{ij\mid i\in I,j\in J\}
. It is again a full
R
–lattice.
We say that
I
is a fractional ideal of
\mathcal{O}
, if
I\mathcal{O}\subseteq I
.
Note that the full lattice
I
is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}
in the usual sense if and only if it is a fractional ideal of
\mathcal{O}
and
I\subseteq\mathcal{O}
.

The ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
in
K
is an order and contains any other order in
K
. Thus
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
is the so-called maximal order of
K
.
Given a subset
S
of
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
, we denote by
\langle S\rangle
the smallest order in
K
that contains
S
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
I
,
J
be full
R
-lattices in
K
.

(1)

The
colon

(I:J):=\{x\in K\mid xJ\subseteq I\}

is a full
R
-lattice in
K
.

(2)

The
multiplicator ring

M(I):=(I:I)=\{x\in K\mid xI\subseteq I\}

is the largest
R
-order in
K
for which
I
is a fractional ideal.

Let
I
be a full
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice and
\mathcal{O}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-order in
K
.
Given a prime
p
, we denote by
I_{p}:=I\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
the completion of
I
at
p
.
Then
I_{p}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice and
\mathcal{O}_{p}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-order in
K
.
Note that computing products, sums and colon ideals commutes with completion.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
.
The intersection of the prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}
over
p
is the
p
-radical
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
of
\mathcal{O}
.

If
R
is a commutative ring, we denote by
\JJ(R)
its Jacobson radical, i.e. the intersection of its maximal ideals.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
and
p
be a prime.
Then
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
is the unique lattice between
p\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}
such that
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})/p\mathcal{O}=\JJ(\mathcal{O}/p\mathcal{O})
.
Further, the completion of
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
at
p
is the Jacobson radical
\JJ(\mathcal{O}_{p})
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{O}
be orders in
K
and
p
be a prime.
Then
\JJ_{p}(\Lambda)=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})\cap\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{r}
be the maximal ideals of
\Lambda
over
p
.
For
1\leq i\leq r
let
\mathfrak{P}_{i,1},\dots,\mathfrak{P}_{i,r_{i}}
be the maximal ideals of
\mathcal{O}
over
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.
Then

\JJ_{p}(\Lambda)=\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{p}_{i}=\bigcap_{i,j}(\mathfrak{P}_{i,j}\cap\Lambda)=\bigcap_{i,j}\mathfrak{P}_{i,j}\cap\Lambda=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})\cap\Lambda

as claimed.
∎

We also need a description of the maximal suborders of a given order
\mathcal{O}
.
The following result is
[
6
, Proposition 5.3]
which is based on
[
2
]
.
We give an elementary self-contained proof.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
and
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{O}
a maximal suborder.
Then
(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})
is a maximal ideal of
\Lambda
.

Proof.

The colon
\mathfrak{a}=(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})
is an ideal of
\Lambda
. Suppose it is not maximal.
Then there exists some ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subsetneq\mathfrak{b}\subsetneq\Lambda
such that
\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a}\cong\Lambda/\mathfrak{p}
with a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\Lambda
.
Hence
\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
.
The set
\Gamma:=\Lambda+\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}
is an order which satisfies
\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{O}
.
The fact that
\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}\not\subseteq\Lambda
shows that
\Gamma\neq\Lambda
.
From
\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}\subseteq\Lambda
we see that
(\Lambda:\Gamma)=\{x\in K\mid x(\Lambda+\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O})\subseteq\Lambda\}
contains
\mathfrak{p}
.
In particular,
\Gamma\neq\mathcal{O}
, contradicting the fact that
\Lambda
is a maximal suborder of
\mathcal{O}
.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{O}
be orders in
K
. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\Lambda
is a maximal suborder of
\mathcal{O}
.

(2)

\mathfrak{p}:=(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})
is a maximal ideal of
\Lambda
and
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal algebra over the field
\Lambda/\mathfrak{p}
.

The minimal algebras
A
over a field
k
have been classified in
[
2
, Lemme 1.2]
:

(1)

A
is a field extension of
k
of prime degree.

(2)

A\cong k\times k
.

(3)

A\cong k[X]/(X^{2})
.

This gives the following description of maximal suborders, see also
[
6
, Proposition 5.5]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
and let
\Lambda
be a maximal suborder of
\mathcal{O}
.
Let
p
be the prime contained in
\mathfrak{p}:=(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})
.
Then exactly one of the following cases occur:

(1)

\mathfrak{p}
is a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
. Then
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}
is a field extension of
\Lambda/\mathfrak{p}
of prime degree.

(2)

There are exactly two distinct maximal ideals
\mathfrak{P}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{2}
of
\mathcal{O}
over
\mathfrak{p}
.
Then

\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P}_{i}\cong\Lambda/\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{P}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{P}_{2}
which implies
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P}_{2}\cong\Lambda/\mathfrak{p}\oplus\Lambda/\mathfrak{p}
.

(3)

There is a unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}
over
\mathfrak{p}
with
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P}\cong\Lambda/\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{P}^{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\subsetneq\mathfrak{P}
.

In the first two cases, we have
\JJ_{p}(\Lambda)=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
whereas in the last case, we have
\JJ_{p}(\Lambda)\subsetneq\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
.

If
\Lambda
is a maximal suborder of
\mathcal{O}
, we define the type of the ring extension
\mathcal{O}/\Lambda
as (1), (2) or (3) depending on which case in Proposition
2.8
holds.
The next results describe these extensions explicitly.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
and let
\mathfrak{P}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\alpha+\mathfrak{P}
is a primitive element of
(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P})^{*}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}^{*}
.
For any prime divisor
r
of
f
, define
d:=f/r
. Then

\Lambda:=\langle\mathfrak{P},\,\gamma_{d}\rangle=\mathfrak{P}+\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{d}^{j}\mathbb{Z}\quad\mbox{where}\quad\gamma_{d}:=\alpha^{\frac{p^{f}-1}{p^{d}-1}}

is a maximal suborder of
\mathcal{O}
with
(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})=\mathfrak{P}
and any order with this property arises this way.

Proof.

We check that
\Lambda
is a suborder of
\mathcal{O}
and thus
\mathfrak{P}
is a maximal ideal of
\Lambda
.
Moreover,
(\Lambda/\mathfrak{P})^{*}\cong\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p^{d}}
is generated by
\gamma_{d}
, so
\mathcal{O}\neq\Lambda
.
Hence
\mathfrak{P}\subseteq(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})\subsetneq\mathcal{O}
and therefore
(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})=\mathfrak{P}
.
Proposition
2.8
shows that
\Lambda/\mathcal{O}
is of type (1).
Every maximal subfield of
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
is generated by
\gamma_{f/r}
for some unique prime divisor
r
of
f
.
Hence every maximal suborder
\Gamma\subset\mathcal{O}
with
(\mathcal{O}:\Gamma)=\mathfrak{P}
such that
\mathcal{O}/\Gamma
is of type (1) is of the form
\langle\mathfrak{P},\gamma_{f/r}\rangle
as claimed.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
with two distinct maximal ideals
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{Q}
over some prime
p
such that
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P}\cong\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{Q}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
.
Pick
\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}\setminus\mathfrak{P}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{P}\setminus\mathfrak{Q}
such that
\alpha+\mathfrak{P}
and
\beta+\mathfrak{Q}
are primitive elements in
(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P})^{*}
and
(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{Q})^{*}
with the same minimal polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Set
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{P}\cap\mathfrak{Q}
.
Then there are
f
maximal suborders
\Lambda\subsetneq\mathcal{O}
with
(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})=\mathfrak{p}
. For
0\leq i<f
they are given by

\Lambda_{i}:=\langle\mathfrak{p},\,\gamma_{i}\rangle=\mathfrak{p}+\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}\gamma_{i}^{j}\mathbb{Z}\quad\mbox{where}\quad\gamma_{i}:=\alpha+\beta^{p^{i}}.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{O}
be a maximal suborder such that
(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})=\mathfrak{p}
.
By
[
2
, Lemme 1.2]
the field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}\cong{\Lambda/\mathfrak{p}}
is diagonally embedded into
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P}\oplus\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{Q}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}
.
Hence there exists a unique integer
0\leq i<f
such that
(\Lambda/\mathfrak{p})^{*}
has
\gamma_{i}+\mathfrak{p}
as primitive element.
Thus
\Lambda=\Lambda_{i}
.

Conversely,
\Lambda_{i}
is an order and
\Lambda_{i}/\mathfrak{p}=\mathbb{F}_{p}(\gamma_{i})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
shows that
\mathfrak{p}
is a maximal ideal of
\Lambda_{i}
.
By
[
2
, Lemme 1.2]
the ring extension
\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}\cong\Lambda_{i}/\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{q^{f}}
is minimal.
Hence
\Lambda_{i}
is a maximal suborder of
\mathcal{O}
.
Since
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{Q}
both lie over
\mathfrak{p}
we have
(\Lambda_{i}:\mathcal{O})=\mathfrak{p}
by Proposition
2.8
.
∎

The description of the maximal suborders of type (3) is based on the following result on finite rings.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
R
be a finite commutative ring of prime characteristic
p
.
Let
e\geq 1
such that
\JJ(R)^{p^{e}}=(0)
.
Then the image of
\varphi\colon R\to R,\;x\mapsto x^{p^{e}}
is the largest semi-simple subring of
R
.
It satisfies
R=\varphi(R)\oplus\JJ(R)
and thus
\varphi(R)\cong R/\JJ(R)
.

Proof.

Since
R
is Artinian, the kernel of
\varphi
is contained in
\JJ(R)
.
By assumption on
e
, we have
\ker(\varphi)=\JJ(R)
and thus
\varphi(R)\cong R/\JJ(R)
is semi-simple.
The map
\varphi
induces an automorphism on any subfield of
R
.
Thus the image
\varphi(R)
contains any subfield and therefore any semi-simple subring of
R
.
From
\JJ(R)\cap\varphi(R)=\JJ(\varphi(R))=(0)
and counting cardinalities, we get
R=\varphi(R)\oplus\JJ(R)
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{P}
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\alpha+\mathfrak{P}
is a primitive element of
(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P})^{*}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}^{*}
.
Consider a maximal subspace
U
of the
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{P}
-space
V:=\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{P}^{2}+p\mathcal{O})
.
Let
L
be the preimage of
U
under the canonical epimorphism
\mathfrak{P}\to\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{P}^{2}+p\mathcal{O})
.
Then

\Lambda:=\langle L,\,\alpha^{p}\rangle=L+\sum_{i=0}^{f-1}\alpha^{pi}\mathbb{Z}

is a maximal suborder of
\mathcal{O}
with maximal ideal
L=(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})\subseteq\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathcal{O}/\Lambda
is of type (3). Moreover, all such orders arise this way.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.8
, any maximal suborder
\Gamma
of
\mathcal{O}
such that
\mathcal{O}/\Gamma
is of type (3) contains
I=\mathfrak{P}^{2}+p\mathcal{O}
.
So it suffices to describe the maximal subrings
S
of
R:=\mathcal{O}/I
such that
R/S
is of type (3).

By Proposition
2.11
, the image of
\varphi\colon R\to R,\;x\mapsto x^{p}
satisfies
R=\varphi(R)\oplus\mathfrak{P}/I
.
Now
U\subseteq\mathfrak{p}/I
is an ideal of
R
. So
S:=\varphi(R)\oplus\mathfrak{P}/I
is a subring of
R
with
\mathfrak{p}/I\cap S=U
.
Let
x\in\mathfrak{P}/I\setminus U
. Then
x^{2}=0
and thus
R/U=(\varphi(R)\oplus\varphi(R)x\oplus U)/U\cong\varphi(R)[X]/(X^{2})
.
So
R/S
is of type (3). Thus
\Lambda
, which is the preimage of
S
under
\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}/I
, is a maximal suborder of
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}/\Lambda\cong R/S
is of type (3).

Conversely suppose that
S
is any maximal subring of
R
such that
R/S
is of type (3).
Then
U=\mathfrak{P}/I\cap S
is the maximal ideal of
S
and by assumption
\varphi(R)\cong R/(\mathfrak{P}/I)\cong S/U
.
Proposition
2.11
shows that
S
contains a unique subring isomorphic to
\varphi(R)
.
But then since
\varphi(R)
is the only such ring in
R
, we have
\varphi(R)\subseteq S
.
Hence
S=\varphi(R)\oplus U^{\prime}
with some maximal subspace
U^{\prime}\subsetneq\mathfrak{P}/I
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}
over
p
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists no maximal suborder
\Lambda
of
\mathcal{O}
with
\mathfrak{P}=(\Lambda:\mathcal{O})
such that
\mathcal{O}/\Lambda
is of type (3).

(2)

\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{P}^{2}+p\mathcal{O}
.

(3)

The completion
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
of
\mathcal{O}
at
\mathfrak{P}
is maximal and
\mathfrak{P}
is unramified.

Proof.

Lemma
2.12
shows the equivalence of the first two assertions and (3) certainly implies (2).
Suppose (2) holds and let
M=\mathfrak{P}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}/p\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
. Then
M^{2}=M
and thus
M=0
by Nakayama’s lemma.
Hence
\mathfrak{P}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}=p\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
.
If
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is not maximal, this is impossible since
\mathfrak{P}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is not invertible.
So
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
is maximal and
p\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}=\mathfrak{P}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{P}}
then simply states that
\mathfrak{P}
is unramified.
∎

Lemmata
2.9
,
2.10
and
2.12
immediately yield the following result.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{O}
be a maximal suborder of
p
-power index.
If
\mathcal{O}/\Lambda
is of type (1) or (2), then
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})\subseteq\Lambda
otherwise
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})^{2}+p\mathcal{O}\subseteq\Lambda
.

The following result will not be used later on, but might be of independent interest.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
p
be a prime.
Suppose
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{O}
are orders such that
[\mathcal{O}:\Lambda]
is a power of
p
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{r}
be the prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}
over
p
.
For
1\leq i\leq r
let
a_{i}\in\prod_{j\neq i}\mathfrak{p}_{j}
such that
a_{i}+\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}_{i})^{*}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{f_{i}}}^{*}
is a primitive element.
Let
\Gamma_{1}
be the order generated by
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
and

\{a_{i}^{\frac{p^{f_{i}-1}}{p^{d_{i}-1}}}\mid 1\leq i\leq r\}

where
\Lambda/(\mathfrak{p}_{i}\cap\Lambda)\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{d_{i}}}
and let
\Gamma_{2}=\Lambda+\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

Any chain of maximal suborders between
\Gamma_{1}
and
\mathcal{O}
consists of extensions of type (1).

(2)

Any chain of maximal suborders between
\Gamma_{2}
and
\Gamma_{1}
consists of extensions of type (2).

(3)

Any chain of maximal suborders between
\Lambda
and
\Gamma_{2}
consists of extensions of type (3).

Proof.

From
\JJ_{p}(\Gamma_{2})=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
we see that any chain of maximal suborders between
\Gamma_{2}
and
\mathcal{O}
only consists of extensions of type (1) and (2).
By construction, the order
\Gamma_{1}
has

different prime ideals over
p
, which implies (1).

Let
\mathfrak{q}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{q}_{s}
be the prime ideals of
\Lambda
over
p
.
The isomorphism theorem shows
\Lambda/\JJ_{p}(\Lambda)\cong\Gamma_{2}/\JJ_{p}(\Lambda)\cap\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma_{2}/\JJ_{p}(\Gamma_{2})
.
Hence for

there exists a unique prime ideal
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
of

over
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
and

Thus any chain of maximal suborders between

and

can only consist of extensions of type (3).

Any prime ideal of
\Gamma_{1}
over
\mathfrak{q}_{i}
has residue field

Thus in a chain of maximal suborders between
\Gamma_{2}
and

3.
Orders with given radical

We will describe all lattices in
K
that are
p
-radicals as well as the corresponding orders.
The description is based on two observations on finite rings, Propositions
2.11
and
3.2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime.
A full lattice
I
in
K
is the
p
-radical of some order if and only if

p\mathbb{Z}+I^{2}\subseteq I\subseteq\JJ_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})\>.

Suppose these inclusions hold.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{I}
be the suborder of
M(I)
containing
I
such that
\mathcal{O}_{I}/I
is the largest semi-simple subring of
M(I)/I
, see Proposition
2.11
.

(1)

The orders
\mathbb{Z}+I
and
\mathcal{O}_{I}
both have
p
-radical
I
and
M(I)/\JJ_{p}(M(I))\cong\mathcal{O}_{I}/I
.

(2)

An order
\mathcal{O}
in
K
has
p
-radical
I
if and only if
\mathbb{Z}+I\subseteq\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{I}
.

In particular, the orders in
K
with
p
-radical
I
form a lattice (in the set theoretic sense) with respect to inclusion.

Proof.

The given inclusions are certainly necessary for
I
to be a
p
-radical.
Suppose now
p\mathbb{Z}+I^{2}\subseteq I\subseteq\JJ_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})
.
Then
\mathcal{O}:=\mathbb{Z}+I
is an order and
p\in I
shows
[\mathcal{O}:I]=p
.
Hence
I
is a maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
and therefore
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})\subseteq I
.
The reverse inclusion follows from
I\subseteq\JJ_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{K})\cap\mathcal{O}=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
.
So
I=\JJ_{p}(\mathbb{Z}+I)
.

(1) Since
I
is an ideal in
M:=M(I)
, it is also one in
\mathcal{O}_{I}
.
Clearly,
I\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{I}\cap\JJ_{p}(M)=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{I})
. Conversely,
\mathcal{O}_{I}/I
is semisimple, so
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{I})\subseteq I
.
Hence
\mathcal{O}_{I}
has
p
-radical
I
. The isomorphism
M/\JJ_{p}(M)\cong\mathcal{O}_{I}/I
has already been established in Proposition
2.11
.

(2) By Lemma
2.5
any order between
\mathbb{Z}+I
and
\mathcal{O}_{I}
has
p
-radical
I
.
Conversely, suppose that the order
\mathcal{O}
has
p
-radical
I
.
Clearly
\mathbb{Z}+I\subseteq\mathcal{O}\subseteq M
and
\mathcal{O}/\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})=\mathcal{O}/I\subseteq M/I
is semisimple, which shows
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{I}
.
∎

To enumerate all orders with
p
-radical
I
, we need to understand the orders between
\mathbb{Z}+I
and
\mathcal{O}_{I}
or equivalently, the subrings of

.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
p
be a prime and
R=R_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus R_{r}
be a direct sum of fields
R_{i}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{f_{i}}}
.
For
1\leq j\leq r
let
\mathfrak{P}_{j}=\sum_{k\neq j}R_{k}
be the kernel of the natural epimorphism
R\to R_{j}
.
Let
f=\lcm(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
and fix embeddings
\varphi_{i}\colon R_{i}\to\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
. Let
w\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}^{*}
be a fixed primitive element.
Further let
S
be a subring of
R
with prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{s}
of residue class degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{s}
respectively.

(1)

For
1\leq i\leq s
set
P_{i}=\{1\leq j\leq r\mid\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}_{j}\}
.
Then
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{s}\}
is a partition of
\{1,\ldots,r\}
.

(2)

For
j\in P_{i}
set
a_{j}=\varphi_{j}^{-1}(w^{\frac{p^{f}-1}{p^{d_{i}}-1}})
.
Then there exist a unique integer
e_{j}
with
0\leq e_{j}<d_{i}
and
e_{j}=0
if

such that

is generated by

(3.1)

\{\sum_{j\in P_{i}}a_{j}^{p^{e_{j}}}\mid 1\leq i\leq s\}\>.

(3)

Given a partition
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{s}\}
of
\{1,\dots,r\}
and positive integers
d_{1},\dots,d_{s}
with
d_{i}\mid\gcd\{f_{j}\mid f_{j}\in P_{i}\}
,
any set as in (
3.1
) generates a subring of
R
isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{F}_{p^{e_{i}}}
.

Proof.

For
1\leq i\leq r
let
h_{i}
be the minimal polynomial of
w^{\frac{p^{f}-1}{p^{d_{i}}-1}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(1) This is well known since
\mathfrak{P}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{P}_{r}
are the maximal ideals of
R
.

(2) The ring
R
has trivial Jacobson radical and thus the same holds for
S
.
So
S\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}S_{i}
is a direct sum of fields
S_{i}\cong S/\mathfrak{p}_{i}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{d_{i}}}
.
The ideal
RS_{i}
is orthogonal to
R\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subseteq\bigcap_{j\in P_{i}}\mathfrak{P}_{j}=\sum_{k\notin P_{i}}R_{k}
.
Hence
S_{i}\subseteq RS_{i}\subseteq\sum_{j\in P_{i}}R_{j}
.
So for
j\in P_{i}
we have an embedding
S_{i}\to R_{j}
and thus
d_{i}\mid f_{j}
.
There exists some
\alpha_{i}\in S_{i}
that has minimal polynomial
h_{i}
.
We can write
\alpha_{i}=\sum_{j\in P_{i}}\beta_{j}
with
\beta_{j}\in R_{j}
.
Since
h_{i}
is irreducible,
\beta_{j}
has minimal polynomial
h_{i}
as well.
Hence
\beta_{j}
is of the form
a_{j}^{p^{e_{j}}}
for some unique
0\leq e_{j}<d_{j}
.
After replacing
\alpha_{i}
with some
p
-power, we can arrange that
e_{j}=0
for the smallest integer
j\in P_{i}
.

(3) Every summand in
\sum_{j\in P_{i}}a_{j}^{p^{e_{j}}}
has minimal polynomial
h_{i}
by construction.
Hence the sum itself has minimal polynomial
h_{i}
. So it generates a subfield of
\sum_{j\in P_{i}}R_{j}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{d_{i}}}
.
∎

When Algorithm

To this end, let

Proposition 3.5
.

In the situation above, let

Let

(1)

(2)

b_{i}I\subseteq I
for all

(3)

\langle I,b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{I}

Proof.

(3)

\mathcal{O}_{I}
.

(2)
\implies
(1): We have

By Proposition

for all

(1)

Write

(1)

4.
Enumerating orders

Let
K
be a number field of degree

In this section we develop an algorithm to enumerate orders in

Remark 4.1

Let
\Lambda
be an order in
K
of index

\mathbb{Z}_{K}

Let

Let

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
H

-lattice with

By assumption,

Corollary 4.8

Suppose

Remark 4.9

Lemma 4.11

Lemma 4.12

Lemma 4.13
.

Let

By Remark

Hence if

satisfies (

Conversely, suppose

and

Finally, the inclusion

∎

In the situation of Lemma

we have

Hence the lattices

with

and

such that

.

Suppose

is an (unknown) lattice in

Then

We are going to iteratively enumerate the lattices

Input:

of a finite dimensional vector space

Output:

of

do

We first note that the sum in line 5 of the algorithm is indeed direct by the choice of

and

.
Let

be the projection onto

.
A subspace

of

satisfies

if and only if

.

in

Let

We fix bases

has a unique basis whose coordinate matrix with respect to

is in reduced echelon form.
Since

\begin{pmatrix}I_{r}&0&*\\

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
V
be an

-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let

an
m
-dimensional subspace.
The number of subspaces

of

with

is

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}_{q}\cdot q^{(n-m)k}

where the Gaussian binomial coefficient

denotes the number of

-dimensional subspaces of an

-dimensional space over

.

In the situation of Algorithm

, the space

has rank

.
For

, there are

different choices for a subspace

of

of rank

.
Each yields

different solutions

. The result follows.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

In the situation of Algorithm

, the number of subspaces

\sum_{k=0}^{s}\prod_{i=0}^{s-k-1}\frac{(q^{r}-q^{i})(q^{s}-q^{i})}{q^{s-k}-q^{i}}

Remark 4.19
.

Note that using basis matrices in Hermite normal form, finding the index
i
in step 5 of Algorithm
4.18
is just a lookup.
So the only time consuming part of the algorithm is computing the power
L^{2^{k}}
using repeated squaring.
It is also worthwhile to mention that if we want to compute all orders of a given index in
\mathbb{Z}_{K}
, we set
\{\mathcal{O}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{s}\}
to be the set of all orders with
p
-radical

.
Then the above algorithm always finds an index

, so it never considers lattices

that have to be dismissed.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
be the largest order in

with
p
-radical
p\mathcal{O}+\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})^{2}

.
Next we need to enumerate the lattices

satisfying (
4.2
) and containing
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
.
Of course, we could proceed as in Algorithm

.
The following algorithm however, does not need to compute the costly powers

Algorithm 4.20
.

Input:

\mathcal{O}
in

in

.

\mathcal{L}=()

and

to
\mathcal{L}
.

4

return

\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

From
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})\cap\hat{\mathcal{O}}=\JJ_{p}(\hat{\mathcal{O}})=p\mathcal{O}+\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})^{2}
it follows that any lattice between
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
and
\mathcal{O}
is of the form

for some uniquely determined lattice

between

\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})

To show the correctness of the algorithm, we need to show that such a sum

satisfies (

L\mathcal{O}=\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
.

Suppose first that

does not satisfy (
4.2
).
Then it is contained in a maximal suborder
\Gamma
of
\mathcal{O}
with
\hat{\mathcal{O}}\subseteq\Gamma
.
Since the prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}
and

over

must be of type 3.
Thus

,

and thus

Suppose now

\mathcal{O}

Suppose now

Then Nakayama’s Lemma for

if and only if

As a first step, we need a list of all invertible ideals of
\mathcal{O}
between
p\mathcal{O}
and
\JJ_{p}(\mathcal{O})
.
To this end, we employ Fröhlich’s invertibility criterion.

Theorem 5.2

(Fröhlich)
.

An ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}
is invertible if and only if
[\mathbb{Z}_{K}\mathfrak{a}:\mathfrak{a}]=[\mathbb{Z}_{K}:\mathcal{O}]
or equivalently
[\mathbb{Z}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}_{K}\mathfrak{a}]=[\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{a}]
.

Proof.

See
[
4
, Theorem 4]
.
∎

Fröhlich’s result can be used to relate the invertible ideals of
\mathcal{O}
to the invertible ideals of any overorder
\Lambda
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\Lambda
be orders.
Then an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}
is invertible if and only if
\Lambda\mathfrak{a}
is an invertible ideal of
\Lambda
and
[\Lambda:\Lambda\mathfrak{a}]=[\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{a}]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{a}
is invertible. Then
\Lambda\mathfrak{a}
is also invertible and thus Fröhlich’s criterion shows that
[\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{a}]=[\mathbb{Z}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}_{K}\mathfrak{a}]=[\Lambda:\Lambda\mathfrak{a}]
.
Conversely suppose that
\Lambda\mathfrak{a}
is invertible and
[\Lambda:\Lambda\mathfrak{a}]=[\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{a}]
.
Again, Fröhlich’s criterion gives
[\mathbb{Z}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}_{K}\mathfrak{a}]=[\Lambda:\Lambda\mathfrak{a}]=[\mathcal{O}:\mathfrak{a}]
and thus
\mathfrak{a}
is invertible.
∎

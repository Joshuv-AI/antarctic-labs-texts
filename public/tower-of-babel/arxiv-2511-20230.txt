Flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras Thanks: S.B. was supported by the grant NYUAD-065
Sofiane Bouarroudj, Hamza El Ouali

Abstract
. A non-associative superalgebra is called pre-symplectic if it is equipped with a non-degenerate, anti-symmetric bilinear form. It is called quasi-Frobenius if, in addition, is a Lie superalgebra and the form is closed. We introduce the Levi-Civita product associated with pre-symplectic superalgebras and establish its existence and uniqueness. We then introduce the symplectic product associated with quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras. We prove that while such a product always exists, it is not unique. We therefore define a natural symplectic product that depends only on the Lie structure and the bilinear form. When the curvature of this product vanishes, the superalgebra is called a flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra . In this paper, we study flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras and introduce the notion of a flat double extension . We prove that the double extension process characterizes such superalgebras. More precisely, every flat orthosymplectic (resp. periplectic) quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra can be obtained by a sequence of flat double extensions starting from an abelian one (resp. the trivial one). Moreover, we show that every flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra is nilpotent with a degenerate center. We apply our results to obtain a complete classification of such superalgebras of total dimension at most five.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Riemannian setting

A
pseudo-Riemannian Lie group
is a Lie group
G
endowed with a left-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric
\mu
.
Its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G
, equipped with the scalar product
\langle\;,\;\rangle=\mu_{e}
, is called a
pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra
.
Whenever there exists a torsion-free connection compatible with the metric, such a connection is necessarily unique and is called the
Levi-Civita connection
(see for instance
[
NOM
,
ON
]
). The Levi-Civita connection induces a bilinear product
(u,v)\mapsto u\star v
on
\mathfrak{g}
, called the
Levi-Civita product
, defined by Koszul’s formula:

2\langle u\star v,w\rangle=\langle[u,v],w\rangle+\langle[w,u],v\rangle+\langle[w,v],u\rangle,\qquad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

This product satisfies the following properties:

u\star v-v\star u=[u,v],\qquad\langle u\star v,w\rangle=-\,\langle v,u\star w\rangle,\qquad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

In the setting of pseudo-Euclidean non-associative superalgebras (in particular, Leibniz superalgebras) this construction has been generalized in
[
BBE2
]
.

The curvature of
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
at the identity is

K(u,v):={\mathrm{L}}_{[u,v]}-[{\mathrm{L}}_{u},{\mathrm{L}}_{v}],\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathfrak{g},

where
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
is the left multiplication defined by

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}(v)=u\star v,
for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

A pseudo-Riemannian Lie group
(G,\mu)
is called
flat
when its curvature vanishes identically; in this case,
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
will be refered to as a
flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebra
. In fact, flatness is equivalent to

(\mathfrak{g},\star)
being a left-symmetric algebra..

The study of flat Lie algebras originated with the Euclidean case. A foundational result was proved by Milnor in 1976
[
M
]
, who characterized such algebras: a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is flat Euclidean if and only if it admits an orthogonal decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{u}

where

\mathfrak{u}
is an abelian ideal,

\mathfrak{b}
is an abelian subalgebra, and for every

b\in\mathfrak{b}
, the adjoint map

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{b}}
is skew-symmetric with respect to the inner product.

In recent years, a number of papers have investigated flat pseudo-Riemannian Lie algebras with various signatures, using in particular the method of double extension introduced in
[
AM
]
.
Further developments include the study of flat Lorentzian Lie algebras
[
AM
,
ABL
,
BL
]
as well as the study of flat pseudo-Euclidean nilpotent Lie algebras with the signature
(2,n-2)
, see
[
BL1
]
. Furthermore, in
[
BBE2
]
, the authors studied flat pseudo-Euclidean left-Leibniz superalgebras.

1.2.
The symplectic setting.

A
symplectic Lie group
is a Lie group
G
endowed with a left-invariant symplectic form
\Omega
.
The associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G
, equipped with
\omega=\Omega_{e}
, is a
symplectic Lie algebra
(called quasi-Frobenuis in this paper). A connection
\nabla
on
G
is called a
symplectic connection
if it is torsion-free and is compatible with
\Omega
, i.e.,
\nabla\Omega=0
.
Such a symplectic connection always exists, but it is not unique (see
[
BCGRS
]
). Whenever a symplectic connection is given, one may define a bilinear product

(u,v)\mapsto u\bullet v

on
\mathfrak{g}
that satisfies the following properties:

u\bullet v-v\bullet u=[u,v],\qquad\omega(u\bullet v,w)=-\,\omega(v,u\bullet w),\qquad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

In
[
BB
]
, the authors introduced a
natural
symplectic connection on symplectic Lie groups, defined by

\omega(u\star v,w)=\tfrac{1}{3}\,\omega([u,v],w)+\tfrac{1}{3}\,\omega([u,w],v),\qquad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

As in the pseudo-Riemannian case, one defines the curvature of this connection. A symplectic Lie group
(G,\Omega)
is called
flat
when its curvature vanishes identically.
In this case, the induced Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is a
flat quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra
. In fact, flatness is equivalent to the fact that the symplectic product defines a left-symmetric structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Despite its importance, the flat symplectic case has received little attention from experts. General results on flat quasi-Frobenius Lie algebras appear in
[
V
]
. Meanwhile, the symplectic connection above was studied in
[
BELL
]
; further contributions can be found in
[
A
,
BC
,
MR1
,
NB
]
.

The study of such structures is motivated by a well-known open problem of Milnor
[
M
]
, which seeks a classification and description of flat affine Lie groups, i.e. Lie groups carrying a left-invariant flat affine structure.

Recall that if
(G,\Omega)
is a symplectic Lie group, then the left-invariant connection
\nabla
defined by

(1)

\omega(\nabla_{u}v,w)=-\omega(v,[u,w]),\qquad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g},

is indeed flat, i.e. its curvature vanishes. Note, in addition, that
\nabla
is symplectic (that is,
\nabla\Omega=0
) if and only if
G
is abelian, equivalently its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian (see
[
Ch
]
).

1.3.
Outline of the paper

Section
2
collects the preliminary material required for our work. For completeness and to establish notation, we include a review of key concepts, some of which may be familiar to specialists.

In Section
3
, we introduce the Levi-Civita product on a class of non-associative anti-commutative superalgebras that we call pre-symplectic. These superalgebras admit a non-degenerate anti-symmetric bilinear form

This product

[u,v]=u\bullet v-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u,\qquad\omega(u\bullet v,w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\,\omega(v,u\bullet w),

for all homogeneous
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
The product

is also characterized by a Koszul formula (see Eq.
4
).
Its existence and uniqueness are established in Prop
3.1.1.  Proposition
.

Further, we study the Levi-Civita product in the case where the pre-symplectic superalgebra is a Lie superalgebra and the form
\omega
is closed. We call these superalgebras
quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras
, following
[
BBE
,
BE
,
BM
]
. It is worth mentioning that several authors call them
symplectic Lie superalgebras

(see
[
BC
,
BEB
,
BBNS
]
to cite a few). We show that the Levi-Civita product associated with a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
is always
right-symmetric (see Prop.
3.1.5.  Proposition
) and satisfies

\omega(u\bullet v,w)=\omega(u,[v,w]),\qquad\text{ for all homogeneous }\,u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

The opposite product given by

u\circ v=-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\bullet u,
for all homogeneous
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,
defines a left-symmetric structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, which superize the classical construction in Eq.(
1
).

In addition, we introduce a second product
\star
on a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
, defined by

u\star v-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\star u=[u,v],\qquad\omega(u\star v,w)=-(-1)^{|v||w|}\,\omega(v,u\star w).

Such a symplectic product exists but is not unique (see Corollary
3.2.8.  Corollary
).
This contrasts with the uniqueness of the Levi-Civita product on pre-symplectic superalgebras as well as in the case of pseudo-Euclidean superalgebras (see
[
BBE2
]
).

We show nevertheless that in any quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
we can construct a unique symplectic product with the key property that it depends only on the Lie bracket and the symplectic form
\omega
. This product is explicitly given by:

\omega(u\star v,w)=\frac{1}{3}\left(\omega([u,v],w)+(-1)^{|v||w|}\,\omega([u,w],v)\right),\text{ for all homogeneous }\,u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

We call this product the
natural symplectic product
associated with
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
. When the curvature of the natural symplectic product
\star
is zero, the product is called
flat
, and the triple
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
is referred to as a
flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
. In fact, flatness is equivalent to the fact that the product defines a left-symmetric structure on the underlying vector space of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

In Section
4
, we initiate the study of the structure theory for flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras. We show that such algebras are solvable when the form
\omega
is even (Theorem
4.4.  Theorem
) and nilpotent when the form
\omega
is odd (Theorem
4.5.  Theorem
).

In Section
5
, we introduce the notion of
flat double extension
for flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras, see Theorem
5.5.5.  Theorem
, Theorem
5.5.12.  Theorem
, Theorem
5.5.15.  Theorem
, and Theorem
5.5.19.  Theorem
. This construction is inspired by the classical double extension, originally introduced by Medina and Revoy
[
MR
]
for quadratic Lie algebras, and its symplectic analogs
[
MR2
,
DM
]
. It is worth noticing that this notion differs from the notion of double extensions of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras considered in
[
BM
,
BE
]
.

In Section
6
, we show that all non-abelian flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras have a degenerate center, see Theorem
6.1.  Theorem
. Moreover, every such Lie superalgebra can be obtained via flat double extensions. In particular, every flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra can be constructed by a sequence of flat double extensions starting from an an abelian Lie superalgebra (see Corollary
6.8.  Corollary
), while flat periplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras can be obtained by a sequence of flat double extensions starting from the trivial algebra
\{0\}
(see Corollary
6.9.2.  Corollary
). Furthermore, we show that every flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra is not only solvable but nilpotent (Theorem
6.9.  Theorem
).

Section
7
is devoted to classifying all flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras of total dimension at most five over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

In

Proposition

Let

(

(3)

(Uniqueness)

\displaystyle=\omega([u,v],w)-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|}\omega([v,w],u)-(-1)^{|u||v|+|w||u|+|v||w|}\omega(w\bullet v,u)

The converse inclusion follows similarly. On the other hand, from Eq. (

Let us prove Part (iii). Since

3.2.
The symplectic product

In
[
BCGRS
]
, the authors established the existence and non-uniqueness of the symplectic product in the case of Lie algebras. Here we extend the result to the super setting. The Levi-Civita product is uniquely determined by a pre-symplectic structure. In contrast, we show in Corollary
3.2.8.  Corollary
that the symplectic product on a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra is not unique.

Definition
.

(Symplectic product)
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenuis Lie superalgebra. A bilinear map

\star:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

is called a
symplectic product
if it satisfies the following conditions for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
:

(10)

\displaystyle[u,v]_{\star}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u,v],

(11)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star})^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}.

Example
.

Following
[
BM
]
, consider the periplectic quasi-Frobenuis Lie superalgebra
(D^{5},[\,,\,],\omega)
. In the basis:

\{e_{1},e_{2}\mid f_{1},f_{2}\}
(even
\mid
odd), it is defined as follows:

[e_{1},f_{1}]=f_{1},\qquad[e_{1},f_{2}]=f_{2},\qquad[e_{2},f_{2}]=f_{1},\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega=e_{1}^{*}\wedge f_{1}^{*}+e_{2}^{*}\wedge f_{2}^{*}.

A direct computation shows that the following product is symplectic:

\begin{array}[]{lcrlcrlcrlcrlcrlcr}e_{1}\star e_{1}&=&-\tfrac{1}{3}e_{1},&e_{1}\star e_{2}&=&-\tfrac{1}{3}e_{2},&e_{1}\star f_{1}&=&-\tfrac{1}{3}f_{1},&e_{1}\star f_{2}&=&-\tfrac{1}{3}f_{2},\\[3.0pt]
e_{2}\star e_{1}&=&\tfrac{1}{3}e_{2},&e_{2}\star f_{2}&=&\tfrac{1}{3}f_{1},&f_{1}\star e_{1}&=&-\tfrac{2}{3}f_{1},&f_{2}\star e_{1}&=&-\tfrac{2}{3}f_{2},\\[3.0pt]
f_{2}\star e_{2}&=&-\tfrac{2}{3}f_{2}.\end{array}

Example
.

Following
[
BM
]
, consider the orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenuis Lie superalgebra
(C^{3}+A,[\,,\,],\omega)
. In the basis:

\{e_{1},e_{2}\mid f_{1},f_{2}\}
(even
\mid
odd), it is defined as follows:

[e_{1},f_{1}]=f_{2},\qquad[f_{1},f_{1}]=e_{1},\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega=2e_{1}^{*}\wedge e_{2}^{*}-f_{1}^{*}\wedge f_{2}^{*}.

A direct computation shows that the following product is symplectic:

f_{1}\star e_{1}=-f_{2},\quad f_{1}\star f_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}e_{2}.

We will need the following lemma later.

Lemma
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\star,\omega)
be a Lie-admissible superalgebra endowed with a non-degenerate bilinear anti-symmetric form such that
{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u}
is
\omega
-anti-symmetric, for any
u\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
({\mathcal{A}}^{-},[\,,\,]_{\star},\omega)
is a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra.

Proof.

For any
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

\omega([u,v],w)=\omega(u\star v,w)-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v\star u,w).

By using the relation

\omega(\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}v,w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}w)
, one can see easily that

\displaystyle\oint_{u,v,w}(-1)^{|u||w|}\omega([u,v],w)

\displaystyle=0\qed.

Remark
.

The product constructed in Lemma
3.2.4.  Lemma
, whenever it exists, is a symplectic product on
({\mathcal{A}}^{-},[\,,\,]_{\star},\omega)
.

Theorem
.

On a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
, there always exists a symplectic product.

Proof.

Suppose that
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
is a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Then, there exists a bilinear product
\bullet
on
\mathfrak{g}
compatible with the Lie bracket
[\;,\;]
; for instance, by Prop.
3.1.3.  Proposition
, one can consider the Levi-Civita product associated with
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
. Define an even bilinear map
M:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by the identity:

\omega(u\bullet v,w)-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,u\bullet w)=\omega(M(u,v),w),\quad\text{for all }u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

Since
\omega
is anti-symmetric, we have
\omega(M(u,v),w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,M(u,w))
. Furthermore, the cyclicity of
\omega
gives

\displaystyle\oint_{u,v,w}(-1)^{|u||w|}\omega([u,v],w)

\displaystyle=\omega(u\bullet v,w)-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v\bullet u,w)+\omega(v\bullet w,u)

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{|v||w|}\omega(w\bullet v,u)+\omega(w\bullet u,v)-(-1)^{|w||u|}\omega(u\bullet w,v)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|u||w|}\omega(M(u,v),w)+(-1)^{|v||u|}\omega(M(v,w),u)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{|w||v|}\omega(M(w,u),v).

Thus, we obtain the identity
\oint_{u,v,w}(-1)^{|u||w|}\omega(M(u,v),w)=0.

Now, define a new product
\star
on
\mathfrak{g}
by

u\star v:=u\bullet v+\frac{1}{3}(M(u,v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}M(v,u)).

We check that
\star
is a compatible product for the Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
:

[u,v]_{\star}=[u,v]_{\bullet}+\frac{1}{3}(M(u,v)+(-1)^{|u||v|}M(v,u)-(-1)^{|u||v|}M(v,u)-M(u,v))=[u,v].

Hence,
(\mathfrak{g},\star)
is also compatible with the Lie superalgebra structure.

To show that
\star
is
\omega
-anti-symmetric, we have

\displaystyle\omega(u\star v,w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,u\star w)=\omega(u\bullet v,w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,u\bullet w)-\frac{1}{3}\omega(M(u,v),w)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{3}(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(M(v,u),w))-\frac{1}{3}(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,M(u,w))-\frac{1}{3}(-1)^{|u||v||u||w|}\omega(v,M(w,u)))

\displaystyle=\omega(M(u,v),w)-\frac{2}{3}\omega(M(u,v),w)-\frac{1}{3}\omega(u,M(v,w))-\frac{1}{3}(-1)^{|u||v|+|u||w|}\omega(v,M(w,u))

\displaystyle=\omega(M(u,v),w)-\frac{2}{3}\omega(M(u,v),w)-\frac{1}{3}(-1)^{|u||w|+|v||w|}\omega(w,M(u,v))

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{3})\omega(M(u,v),w)=0.

because
\omega(M(u,v),w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,M(u,w))
and
M
is cyclic on
\omega
. Therefore,
\star
is
\omega
-anti-symmetric, as claimed.
∎

Lemma
.

Let
E
be a vector superspace equipped with an orthosymplectic
(
resp. periplectic
)
form
\omega
. Then there exists an even
(
resp. odd
)
symmetric bilinear map
f:E\times E\to E
such that
(
for all
u,v,w\in E
)

\omega(f(u,v),w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,f(u,w)).

Proof.

Let
(E,\omega)
be a even (resp. odd) symplectic superspace. Consider
T
as an even (resp. odd) symmetric trilinear form on
E
. For
u,v\in E
, define the map

T(u,v,\cdot):E\to\mathbb{K}

as an even (resp. odd) linear form. Since
\omega
is non-degenerate, there exists a unique vector
f_{T}(u,v)\in E
such that

T(u,v,w)=\omega(f_{T}(u,v),w)\quad\text{for all }w\in E.

Since
T
is even (resp. odd) and symmetric on
E
, and
\omega
is even (resp. odd), it is straightforward to show that
f_{T}:E\times E\to E
is an even symmetric bilinear map. For any
u,v,w\in E
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\omega(f_{T}(u,v),w)&=&T(u,v,w)=(-1)^{|v||w|}T(u,w,v)=(-1)^{|v||w|}\omega(f_{T}(u,w),v)\\[5.69054pt]
&=&-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,f_{T}(u,w)).\end{array}

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra, and let
\star
be a symplectic product on
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
. Consider a bilinear map
f:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
. The product defined by

u\bar{\star}v=u\star v+f(u,v),\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{g}

is a symplectic product on
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
if and only if
f
is symmetric and satisfies the identity

\omega(f(u,v),w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,f(u,w)),

for any
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

Assume that
\star
is a symplectic product on
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
. For any
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle{}_{\bar{\star}}

\displaystyle=u\star v+f(u,v)-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\star u-(-1)^{|u||v|}f(u,v)

\displaystyle=[u,v]+f(u,v)-(-1)^{|u||v|}f(v,u).

We therefore obtain
[u,v]_{\bar{\star}}=[u,v]
if and only if
f(u,v)=(-1)^{|u||v|}f(v,u)
.

On the other hand, for any
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(u\bar{\star}v,w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,u\bar{\star}w)

\displaystyle=\omega(u\star v+f(u,v),w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,u\star w+f(u,w))

\displaystyle=\omega(f(u,v),w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,f(u,w)).

Thus,
\omega(u\bar{\star}v,w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,u\bar{\star}w)=0
if and only if
\omega(f(u,v),w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,f(u,w))
.
∎

Corollary
.

On a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra there exist many symplectic products.

Proof.

According to Prop.
3.2.5.  Theorem
, there exists a symplectic product
\star
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.2.6.  Lemma
, there exists an even symmetric bilinear map
f:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the condition

\omega(f(u,v),w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,f(u,w)).

Thus, by Prop.
3.2.7.  Proposition
, the product defined by

u\bar{\star}v=u\star v+f(u,v),\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{g},

is also a symplectic product on
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
.
∎

In
[
BB
]
, the authors introduced a symplectic connection that depends on the Lie bracket and the symplectic form. In this work, we introduce a natural symplectic product for quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras that depends on both the Lie bracket and the form, and we establish its uniqueness in Prop.
3.2.9.  Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Following the same procedure as in Prop.
3.2.5.  Theorem
, and using Eq. (
8
), we define a product
\circ
on
\mathfrak{g}
by

\omega(\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\circ}(v),w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\,\omega(v,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}(w)),\qquad\forall u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

This product satisfies
[u,v]=[u,v]_{\circ}.
Moreover, since
\omega
is closed, we have

\omega(u\circ v,w)+(-1)^{|u||v|}\,\omega(v,u\circ w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\,\omega(v\circ u,w).

From the proof of Theorem
3.2.5.  Theorem
, we consider the map

M(u,v)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\,v\circ u.

Using this, we define the product
\star
on
\mathfrak{g}
by

u\star v=u\circ v-\tfrac{1}{3}\Big((-1)^{|u||v|}\,v\circ u+u\circ v\Big)=\tfrac{2}{3}\,u\circ v-\tfrac{1}{3}(-1)^{|u||v|}\,v\circ u.

One can verify that this product satisfies

\omega(u\star v,w)=\tfrac{2}{3}\,\omega(u\circ v,w)-\tfrac{1}{3}(-1)^{|u||v|}\,\omega(v\circ u,w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\,\tfrac{2}{3}\,\omega(v,[u,w])-\tfrac{1}{3}\,\omega(u,[v,w]).

Since
\omega
is closed, it follows that

(12)

\omega(u\star v,w)=\tfrac{1}{3}\,\omega([u,v],w)+\tfrac{1}{3}(-1)^{|v||w|}\,\omega([u,w],v).

Therefore, we obtain a natural symplectic product on
\mathfrak{g}
that depends only on the form
\omega
and the Lie bracket.

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. There exists a unique natural symplectic product that depends on the product
[\;,\;]
and the form
\omega
. This product is given by
(
for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
)
:

(13)

\omega(u\star v,w)=\frac{1}{3}\left(\omega([u,v],w)+(-1)^{|v||w|}\omega([u,w],v)\right).

Proof.

Suppose there exists a symplectic product on
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
such that:

\omega(u\star v,w)=a\omega([u,v],w)+b(-1)^{|v||w|}\omega([u,w],v)+c(-1)^{|v||w|}\omega([v,w],u),

for some scalars
a,b,c\in\mathbb{K}
. The condition
\omega(u\star v,w)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,u\star w)
implies that
b=a
and
c=0
.

Using the identity
[u,v]=[u,v]_{\star}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega([u,v],w)

\displaystyle=\omega(u\star v-(-1)^{|u||v|}v\star u,w)

\displaystyle=a\omega([u,v],w)+(-1)^{|v||w|}a\omega([u,w],v)-a(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega([v,u],w)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|u||w|+|u||v|}a\omega([v,w],u).

Since the form
\omega
is closed, this simplifies to

\omega([u,v],w)=3a\omega([u,v],w),

and thus
a=\frac{1}{3}
.
Therefore, the unique symplectic product compatible with the bracket
[\;,\;]
and the form
\omega
is (for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
):

\omega(u\star v,w)=\frac{1}{3}\left(\omega([u,v],w)-(-1)^{|v||w|}\omega([u,w],v)\right).\qed

Definition
.

We call the product defined in Eq. (
13
) the
natural symplectic product.

4.
Flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras

We investigate flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras, characterized by a natural symplectic product with identically zero curvature. This product induces a left-symmetric structure, extending the Lie algebra case studied in
[
BELL
]
to the super setting.

Definition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra, and let
\star
be a symplectic product on
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
.
We define the curvature operator on
\mathfrak{g}
by

\mathcal{R}(u,v):={\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{[u,v]}-[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u},{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{v}],\qquad\forall\,u,v\in\mathfrak{g},

where
{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u}
denotes the left multiplication by
u
with respect to
\star
.

Moreover, if this curvature operator vanishes identically, i.e.

\mathcal{R}(u,v)=0,\quad\forall\,u,v\in\mathfrak{g},

then
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
is called a
flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
.

It is easy to see that the curvature
\mathcal{R}
of the symplectic product
\star
vanishes identically if and only if
(\mathfrak{g},\star)
is a left-symmetric superalgebra.

As we proceed in this work, we consider the
natural symplectic product
defined by Eq. (
13
).

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra, and let
\star
be the
natural
symplectic product. The left and right multiplications with respect to
\star
are expressed as (for all
u\in\mathfrak{g}
):

(14)

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star}=\tfrac{1}{3}\big(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}-\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{*}\big),\qquad{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\star}=-\tfrac{1}{3}\big(2\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}+\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{*}\big),

where
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{*}
denotes the adjoint of
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}
with respect to the form
\omega
.

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra, and let
\star
be the natural symplectic product. Then:

(i)

For all
u\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}
, we have

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{\star}=-2{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{u}=\tfrac{2}{3}\,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}
. Moreover,

[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}=\{\,u\in\mathfrak{g}\;\mid\;({\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{\star})^{*}=-{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{u}\,\}.

(ii)

The center of

Z(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathfrak{g}\star\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=N_{r}(\mathfrak{g},\star)=N_{r}(\mathfrak{g},\star)\cap N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)=N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}.

Proof.

For part (i), since
\omega

Now, if

and for all

\omega(\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}(v),w)=\tfrac{2}{3}\,\omega([u,v],w).

Since

Using the relation
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}={\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u}-{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{u}

{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{u}=-\tfrac{1}{3}\,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}.
Thus, for

For part (ii), let

Now, let

0=\omega(v\star w,u)=-(-1)^{|v||w|}\,\omega(w,v\star u).

Since

From (i), we know that for

{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u}=-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{u}.

Therefore, for

Finally, let

{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u}={\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{u}=0.

We thus obtain the desired equalities:

Z(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathfrak{g}\star\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=N_{r}(\mathfrak{g},\star)\cap N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)=N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}.\qed

Proposition

Theorem

5.
Flat double extensions of flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of
flat
double extension for flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras. It is inspired by the concept of double extension, originally introduced by Medina and Revoy in
[

]
for quadratic Lie algebras. It is worth noticing that this notion is different from the notion of double extensions of quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras introduced in

5.1.
Central extension of left-symmetric superalgebras

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a left-symmetric superalgebra, let
\h:=\K e
be a one-dimensional vector superspace, and let
\mu:{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\K
be a homogeneous bilinear map of a given parity.
We define a new product
\diamond
on the vector space

\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}}:={\mathcal{A}}\oplus\K e

as follows:

(u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e):=u\bullet v+\mu(u,v)e,\qquad\forall\,u,v\in{\mathcal{A}},\;\alpha,\beta\in\K.

Following
[
BBE2
, Corollary 5.1.3]
, then
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}},\diamond)
is a left-symmetric superalgebra if and only if the following condition holds (for all
u,v,w\in{\mathcal{A}}
):

(16)

\mu(\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\bullet}(v),w)-\mu(u,\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\bullet}(w))=(-1)^{|u||v|}\big(\mu(\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\bullet}(u),w)-\mu(v,\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\bullet}(w))\big),

In this case,
(\widetilde{{\mathcal{A}}},\diamond)
is called the
central extension
of
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
by means of
\mu
.

5.2.
Almost semi-direct product of left-symmetric superalgebras

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)
be a left-symmetric superalgebra. Let
\mathcal{S}:=\K d
be a one-dimensional vector superspace, let
D:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}},\;\xi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}},
be homogeneous endomorphisms of parity
|d|
, and let
b_{0}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\bar{0}}
and a scalar
\lambda\in\K
.
On the vector superspace

\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}:=\K d\oplus{\mathcal{A}},

we define a new product
\bar{\bullet}
as follows: (here
\lambda=0
if
d

d\bar{\bullet}d:=\lambda d+b_{0},\qquad d\bar{\bullet}u:=D(u),\qquad u\bar{\bullet}d:=\xi(u),\qquad u\bar{\bullet}v:=u\bullet v,

for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Then
(\overline{{\mathcal{A}}},\bar{\bullet})
is a left-symmetric superalgebra if and only if the following conditions are satisfied (for all

(17)

\begin{cases}\xi([u,v]_{\bullet})=\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\bullet}(\xi(v))-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\bullet}(\xi(u)),\\[3.99994pt]

If

(\overline{{\mathcal{A}}},\bar{\bullet})

({\mathcal{A}},\bullet)

\K d

In this case, the quadruple

Remark

Let

5.5.
Flat double extensions of flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras

In this paragraph, we introduce the notion of a

We then show that if a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra is nilpotent, then any its flat double extension of is also nilpotent.

5.5.1.
Even flat double extensions of orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras.

Let

Let

In order to introduce the process of flat double extension by

\widetilde{D},\widetilde{\xi}:\mathfrak{b}\oplus V^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{b}\oplus V^{*},

(18)

\begin{array}[]{c}\widetilde{D}(u+\alpha e):=(\xi^{*}-\xi)(u)+\dfrac{1}{3}\,\omega(b_{0},u)\,e,\quad\widetilde{\xi}(u+\alpha e):=\xi(u)-\dfrac{2}{3}\,\omega(b_{0},u)\,e.\end{array}

for all

and

Lemma

The superspace

(u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e):=u\star v+\omega(\xi(u),v)\,e,\qquad\forall\,u,v\in\mathfrak{b},\;\alpha,\beta\in\K,

for every

Proof.

The superalgebra
(\mathcal{H},\diamond)

Now, let us also assume that
(V,\mathfrak{b},\xi^{*}-\xi,\xi^{*},\tfrac{1}{3}b_{0},0)
is a context of almost semi-direct product
of left-symmetric superalgebras.

Lemma
.

(V,\mathcal{H}:=\mathfrak{b}\oplus V^{*},\widetilde{D},\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{b_{0}},0)
is a context of almost semi-direct product
of left-symmetric superalgebras if and only if for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}
, the following conditions hold:

(20)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcllcl}\xi([u,v])&=&\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}(\xi(v))-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\star}(\xi(u)),&\xi^{*}\circ\xi&=&\tfrac{1}{3}({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{b_{0}}+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{b_{0}})^{*}),\\[5.69054pt]

where
D=\xi^{*}-\xi
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}
.
A straightforward computation shows that the equality

\widetilde{\xi}([u,v])=\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\diamond}(\widetilde{\xi}(v))-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\diamond}(\widetilde{\xi}(u))

is equivalent to

\xi([u,v])=\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}(\xi(v))-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\star}(\xi(u)),\quad\text{and}\quad\xi^{*}\circ\xi=\tfrac{1}{3}({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{b_{0}}+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{b_{0}})^{*}).

Moreover, the relation

\widetilde{D}(u)\diamond v-\widetilde{D}(u\diamond v)=(-1)^{|d||u|}\big(\widetilde{\xi}(u)\diamond v-u\diamond\widetilde{D}(v)\big)

is equivalent to

D(u\star v)=D(u)\star v+u\star D(v)-\xi(u)\star v,

together with

[\xi^{*},\xi]=\xi^{2}-\tfrac{1}{3}{\mathrm{R}}_{b_{0}}^{\star}.

Finally, the identity

[\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{D}](u)=(-1)^{|d||u|}\big(\widetilde{\xi}^{2}-{\mathrm{R}}^{\diamond}_{\widetilde{b_{0}}}\big)(u)

is equivalent to

[\xi,\xi^{*}]=\xi^{2}-\tfrac{1}{3}{\mathrm{R}}_{b_{0}}^{\star},\quad\text{and}\quad b_{0}\in\ker(\xi^{*}-\xi).

Since
(V,\mathfrak{b},\xi^{*}-\xi,\xi,\tfrac{1}{3}b_{0},0)
is a context of almost semi-direct product of left-symmetric superalgebras, it follows that

(V,\mathcal{H}:=\mathfrak{b}\oplus V^{*},\widetilde{D},\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{b_{0}},0)
is also a context of almost semi-direct product of left-symmetric superalgebras if and only if the conditions (
20
) are satisfied. This proves the lemma.

Lemma
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be an orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius nilpotent Lie superalgebra. If
a\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
, then
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})=0
.

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
. Then, we have
\omega({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(u),v)=-\,\omega(u,{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(v)),
for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}

\mid
odd)

\{X_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,X_{n},Y_{n},\mid f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}

of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\omega(X_{i},Y_{i})=-\omega(Y_{i},X_{i})=-1,\quad\text{and}\quad\omega(f_{i},f_{i})=\epsilon_{i},\ \text{where }\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1.

We compute the (ordinary) trace of
{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}
:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(X_{i}),Y_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(Y_{i}),X_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\epsilon_{i}\,\omega({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(f_{i}),f_{i})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega(X_{i},{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(Y_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega(Y_{i},{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(X_{i}))-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\epsilon_{i}\,\omega(f_{i},{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(f_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(Y_{i}),X_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(X_{i}),Y_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\epsilon_{i}\,\omega({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}(f_{i}),f_{i})

\displaystyle=-\,\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}).

Hence
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a})=0
.
Since
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{a}={\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}-{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{a})=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a})-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}).

But
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is nilpotent, so
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{a})=0
. Therefore
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})=0
.
∎

Lemma
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius nilpotent Lie superalgebra. If
a\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
, then the right multiplication operator
{\mathrm{R}}_{a}
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Since
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
is a flat, then according to relation (
14
), we have

(23)

{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{u\star v}-(-1)^{|u||v|}{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{v}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{u}=[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{v}].

Now, let
a\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. We define inductively

x_{0}=a,\;x_{n+1}={\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{n}}(a),\quad\text{for any }n\geq 0.
We shall show by induction that, for any
n\geq 1
, we have

(24)

{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{x_{n}}=({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n+1}+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n-2}\circ[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n-3}\circ[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{1}},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]+\dots+[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{n-1}},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}].

The relation is clearly true for
n=1
. Assume it holds for some
n\geq 1
. Then, using Eq. (
23
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{x_{n+1}}

\displaystyle={\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{x_{n}\star a}={\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{x_{n}}+[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{n}},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]

\displaystyle={\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}\circ\Big(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n+1}+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n-2}\circ[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n-3}\circ[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{1}},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]+\dots+[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{n-1}},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]\Big)+[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{n}},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]

\displaystyle=({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n+2}+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n-1}\circ[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{\,n-2}\circ[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{1}},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}]+\dots+[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{x_{n}},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}].

This completes the induction step.

On the other hand, since
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is nilpotent, Lemma
5.5.6.  Lemma
implies that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{b})=0,
for all
b\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Now, we put
b=x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, we get

\operatorname{tr}({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{x_{n+1}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{tr}(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{k}\circ[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}])=0\quad\text{for all }n\,,k\in\mathbb{N},

since,

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{k}\circ[{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a},{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}])

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{k}\circ({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}-{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a})=\operatorname{tr}(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{k}\circ({\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a}))-\operatorname{tr}(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{k+1}\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{k+1}\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a})-\operatorname{tr}(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{k+1}\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{a})=0.

From Eq. (
24
), we deduce by induction that

\operatorname{tr}(({\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{a})^{n+1})=0\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

and hence

is nilpotent.

Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{b},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})
be a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra and let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be the even flat double extension of
(\mathfrak{b},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})
by means of
(\xi,b_{0})
. If
(\mathfrak{b},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}})
is nilpotent, then
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is nilpotent as well.

Proof.

First, we show that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
a\in\mathfrak{b}_{0}
,

(25)

\operatorname{tr}\big(\xi^{k}\circ{\mathrm{R}}_{a}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}\big)=\operatorname{tr}\big({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{\xi^{k}(a)}\big).

Indeed, for any
a\in\mathfrak{b}_{\bar{0}}
and
u\in\mathfrak{b}
, according to system (
20
), we have

\xi([u,a])=u\star\xi(a)-a\star\xi(u),

which implies

\xi\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a}-\xi\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a}={\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{\xi(a)}-{\mathrm{L}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a}\circ\xi.

Taking the trace gives
\operatorname{tr}(\xi\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a})=\operatorname{tr}({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{\xi(a)})
. Hence, Eq. (
25
) holds for
k=1
. Assume the property is true for some
k\geq 1
. Then for
k+1
, we have

\xi^{k+1}([a,u])=\xi^{k+1}\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a}(u)-\xi^{k+1}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a}(u).

On the other hand, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\xi^{k+1}([a,u])=\xi^{k}\circ\xi([a,u])=\xi^{k}\big(a\star\xi(u)-u\star\xi(a)\big)=\xi^{k}\circ{\mathrm{L}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a}\circ\xi(u)-\xi^{k}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{\xi(a)}(u).

Comparing the two expressions and taking the trace, we obtain

\operatorname{tr}(\xi^{k+1}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a})=\operatorname{tr}(\xi^{k}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{\xi(a)})=\operatorname{tr}({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{\xi^{k+1}(a)}),

which proves the property for
k+1
.

We set
\Delta:=\xi^{*}-2\xi
. From (
20
), we have

[\xi,\Delta]=\xi^{2}-\frac{1}{3}{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{b_{0}}.

By induction, one can show that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

[\xi^{k},\Delta]=k\xi^{k+1}-\frac{1}{3}\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}\xi^{p}\circ{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{b_{0}}\circ\xi^{k-1-p}.

It follows that

\operatorname{tr}(\xi^{k+1})=\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\big({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{b_{0}}\circ\xi^{k-1}\big).

Then, from Eq. (
25
), we deduce that

\operatorname{tr}(\xi^{k+1})=\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\big({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{\xi^{k-1}(b_{0})}\big)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.
Since
(\mathfrak{b},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}})
is nilpotent, Lemma
5.5.7.  Lemma
implies that
\operatorname{tr}({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{a})=0
for any
a\in\mathfrak{b}_{\bar{0}}
. Therefore, since
\xi^{k-1}(b_{0})\in\mathfrak{b}_{\bar{0}}
, then
\operatorname{tr}(\xi^{k+1})=\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\big({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{\xi^{k-1}(b_{0})}\big)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, which implies that
\xi
is nilpotent.

From the system (
20
), we have
\xi^{*}\circ\xi=\frac{1}{3}({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{b_{0}}+({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{b_{0}})^{*}),
and
[\xi,\xi^{*}]=\xi^{2}-\frac{1}{3}{\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{b_{0}}.

It follows that

\xi\circ\xi^{*}=\xi^{2}+\frac{1}{3}({\mathrm{R}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}}_{b_{0}})^{*},
and
(\xi^{*})^{2}=[\xi,\xi^{*}]+\frac{1}{3}({\mathrm{R}}_{b_{0}}^{\star_{\mathfrak{b}}})^{*}
.

Replacing these into the expression

\Delta^{2}=(\xi^{*}-2\xi)^{2},

we get

\Delta^{2}=3\xi^{2}-3\xi^{*}\circ\xi=-3\Delta\circ\xi-3\xi^{2}.

By induction, one can show that

\Delta^{k}=a_{k}\Delta\circ\xi^{k-1}+b_{k}\xi^{k}
for all
k\geq 2
, where
a_{k},b_{k}
are some scalars. Since
\xi
is nilpotent, it follows that
\Delta
is nilpotent.

Now, if
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
is an even flat double extension of
(\mathfrak{b},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})
, the Lie bracket in
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

[d,u]=\Delta(u)+\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(b_{0},u)e,\qquad[u,v]=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{b}}+\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}\big((\xi+\xi^{*})(u),v\big)e,

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}
. By induction, one can show
that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits^{k}_{d}(u)=\Delta^{k}(u)+\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(b_{0},\Delta^{k-1}(u))e,\text{ and }\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{k}(v)=(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{\mathfrak{b}})^{k}(v)+\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}((\xi+\xi^{*})(u),(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{\mathfrak{b}})^{k-1}(v))e,

for all

. Since
(\mathfrak{b},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}})
is nilpotent and
\Delta
is nilpotent, it follows that
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is nilpotent. This completes the proof.

5.5.9.
Odd flat double extensions of orthosympletic Lie superalgebras

We now introduce the notion of
odd flat double extension
of flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras by a purely odd one-dimensional vector superspace.

Let
(\mathfrak{b},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})
be a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra, and denote by
\star
the natural symplectic product associated with it.
Let
V=\K d
be a purely odd one-dimensional vector superspace and
V^{*}=\K e
be its dual.

In order to introduce the process of the odd flat double extension, we consider an odd linear map
\xi:\mathfrak{b}\rightarrow\mathfrak{b},
and an even element
b_{0}\in\mathfrak{b}_{\bar{0}}
. For all
u\in{\mathfrak{b}}
, we define the linear map

(26)

D(u)=-(-1)^{|u|}(\xi^{*}+\xi)(u).

We extend the two maps
D
and
\xi
to

\widetilde{D},\widetilde{\xi}:\mathfrak{b}\oplus V^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{b}\oplus V^{*},

as follows:

(27)

\widetilde{D}(u+\alpha e)=-(-1)^{|u|}(\xi^{*}+\xi)(u)+\,\omega(b_{0},u)\,e,\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{\xi}(u+\alpha e)=\xi(u),

for all
u\in\mathfrak{b}
and
\alpha\in\K
. Put
\widetilde{b_{0}}=b_{0}.

Lemma
.

The superspace
\mathcal{H}:=\mathfrak{b}\oplus V^{*}
equipped with the product

(u+\alpha e)\diamond(v+\beta e):=u\star v+(-1)^{|v|}\omega(\xi(u),v)\,e,\qquad\forall\,u,v\in\mathfrak{b},\;\alpha,\beta\in\K,

is a left-symmetric superalgebra if and only if

(28)

\xi([u,v])=\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}(\xi(v))-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\star}(\xi(u)),

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

The algebra
(\mathcal{H},\diamond)
is a left-symmetric superalgebra if and only if the bilinear map

\mu(u,v):=\omega(\xi(u),v)

satisfies condition (
16
), which is equivalent to relation (
28
).∎

Now, let us also assume that
(V,\mathfrak{b},D,\xi,b_{0},0)
is a context of almost semi-direct product
of left-symmetric superalgebras, where
D(u)=-(-1)^{|u|}(\xi^{*}+\xi)(u)
, for all
u\in\mathfrak{b}
.

Lemma
.

(V,\mathcal{H}:=\mathfrak{b}\oplus V^{*},\widetilde{D},\widetilde{\xi},\widetilde{b_{0}},0)
is a context of almost semi-direct product
of left-symmetric superalgebras if and only if

(29)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}\xi([u,v])&=&\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}(\xi(v))-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\star}(\xi(u)),&\left[\xi,\xi^{*}\right]&=&{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{b_{0}}-3\xi^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
D(u\star v)&=&D(u)\star v+(-1)^{|u|}u\star D(v)+\xi(u)\star v,&{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{b_{0}}&=&-(\xi+\xi^{*})^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
(2\xi+\xi^{*})(b_{0})&=&0,\end{array}\right.

for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}
.
A straightforward computation shows that the equality

\widetilde{\xi}([u,v])=\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\diamond}(\widetilde{\xi}(v))-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\diamond}(\widetilde{\xi}(u))

is equivalent to

\xi([u,v])=\mathrm{L}_{u}^{\star}(\xi(v))-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mathrm{L}_{v}^{\star}(\xi(u)).

Moreover, the relation

\widetilde{D}(u)\diamond v-\widetilde{D}(u\diamond v)=(-1)^{|d||u|}\big(\widetilde{\xi}(u)\diamond v-u\diamond\widetilde{D}(v)\big)

is equivalent to

-(-1)^{|u|}(\xi^{*}+\xi)(u)\star v+(-1)^{|u|+|v|}(\xi^{*}+\xi)(u\star v)=(-1)^{|u|}\xi(u)\star v+(-1)^{|u|+|v|}u\star(\xi^{*}+\xi)(v),

together with

-(-1)^{|u|+|v|}\,\omega(\xi(\xi+\xi^{*})(u),v)-\omega(b_{0},u\star v)=(-1)^{|u|+|v|}\,\omega(\xi^{2}(u),v)+\omega((\xi+\xi^{*})(\xi(u)),v).

Since both
\xi\circ(\xi+\xi^{*})
and
\xi^{2}
are even, and
\omega
is even, it follows that when
|u|+|v|=\bar{1}
, the equality reduces to the trivial identity
0=0
. Consequently, the only nontrivial case occurs when
|u|+|v|=\bar{0}
, which yields the relation:

-\omega(\xi(\xi+\xi^{*})(u),v)+\omega(u\star b_{0},v)=\omega(\xi^{2}(u),v)+\omega((\xi+\xi^{*})(\xi(u)),v).

Since
\omega
is nondegenerate, we deduce that

\xi\circ\xi^{*}+\xi^{*}\circ\xi=-3\xi^{2}+{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{b_{0}}.

The identity

(\widetilde{\xi}\circ\widetilde{D}-\widetilde{D}\circ\widetilde{\xi})(u)=(-1)^{|d||u|}\big(\widetilde{\xi}^{2}-{\mathrm{R}}^{\diamond}_{\widetilde{b_{0}}}\big)(u)

is equivalent to

\xi\circ\xi^{*}+\xi^{*}\circ\xi=-3\xi^{2}+{\mathrm{R}}^{\star}_{b_{0}},\text{ and }\omega(b_{0},\xi(u))=(-1)^{|u|}\omega(b_{0},\xi(u)).

Since both
b_{0}
and
\omega
are even, and
\xi
is odd, we deduce that
(-1)^{|u|}\omega(b_{0},\xi(u))=\omega(b_{0},\xi(u))
.

In addition, the relation

6.
Flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras are nilpotent

In this section, we show that all non-abelian flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras have a degenerate center.
Moreover, every such Lie superalgebra can be obtained via flat double extensions. In particular, every flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra can be constructed by a sequence of flat double extensions starting from an an abelian Lie superalgebra, while flat periplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras can be obtained by a sequence of flat double extensions starting from the trivial algebra
\{0\}
. Furthermore, we show that every flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra is not only solvable but nilpotent

Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra. Then
Z(\mathfrak{g})\neq\{0\}
. Moreover, if
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is a non abelian Lie superalgebra, then
Z(\mathfrak{g})
is degenerate.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases.

The case where
\omega
is odd.
If
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is abelian, then it is clear that
Z(\mathfrak{g})\neq 0.
If
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is non-abelian then according to Theorem
4.5.  Theorem
,
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is nilpotent. It follows that
Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\neq\{0\},
and since
Z(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\bot}
, it follows that
Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap Z(\mathfrak{g})^{\bot}\neq\{0\}
and we are done.

The case where
\omega
is even
. If
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is abelian, then it is clear that
Z(\mathfrak{g})\neq 0.

Now, we assume that
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is non-abelian. We need the following two lemmas.

Lemma
.

The following implication holds:

(39)

Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}\;\Longrightarrow\;Z([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])\cap N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)=\{0\}.

Proof.

Suppose that
Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}
and let
u\in Z([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])\cap N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)
.
Then, for any
w\in\mathfrak{g}
and any
v\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and since
{\mathrm{L}}^{\star}_{u}=0
together with (
14
), we have
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}
. Hence,

0=\omega([u,v],w)=(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,[u,w]).

It follows that
[u,w]\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}
. But we have seen in Prop.
4.7.  Proposition
that
[u,w]\in N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)
, thus

[u,w]\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}\cap N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)=Z(\mathfrak{g})\text{ by Prop. \ref{orth}}
, and consequently
[u,w]\in Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}

which shows that
u\in Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and hence
u=0
.
\blacksquare

With this observation in mind, let us prove our result by contradiction.
According to Prop.
4.4.  Theorem
,
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is solvable and
Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}
.
We consider the sequence of vector subspaces defined by

C_{0}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp},\qquad C_{k}=[[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],C_{k-1}],\quad k\geq 1.

By virtue of Prop.
4.6.  Proposition
, we have
C_{0}\neq\{0\}
, and according to Prop.
4.7.  Proposition
,

C_{k}\subset N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)
for any
k\geq 1
.

Lemma
.

For any
k\geq 1
, if
C_{k}=\{0\}
then
C_{k-1}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
C_{k}=\{0\}
. Then, for any
u\in C_{k-1}
, any
v\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and any
w\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

0=\omega([u,v],w)=\pm(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,[u,w]),

since
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{*}=-\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}
if
k=1
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits_{u}
if
k\geq 2
. Thus,
[u,w]\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}\cap N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)=Z(\mathfrak{g})
and hence
[u,w]\in Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
which shows that
u=0
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.

\blacksquare

Since
C_{0}\neq\{0\}
, we deduce that for all
k\geq 1
,
C_{k}\neq\{0\}
. But
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is nilpotent, and therefore there exists
k_{0}
such that
C_{k_{0}}\subseteq Z([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])
.
We also have
C_{k_{0}}\subseteq N_{\ell}(\mathfrak{g},\star)
, according to (
39
), we obtain that
Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\neq{0}
. Since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subseteq Z(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
, we deduce that

Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subseteq Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap Z(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}\neq\{0\}
.∎

Remark
.

Every flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra of dimension
2
is abelian. Indeed, Theorem
6.1.  Theorem
implies
Z(\mathfrak{g})\neq 0
. Hence, there exists a non-zero element
a\in Z(\mathfrak{g})_{\bar{i}}
.

If
\omega
is odd, then
\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})=\dim(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}})
. Assume

. We can find an element

such that
\omega(a,b)=1
and

, where

.

\omega
is closed, we obtain

3\,\omega([b,b],b)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad 3\alpha=0,

which implies

, and hence
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is abelian. The case where

is obvious.

If
\omega
is even, then
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,])
is either purely even or purely odd. In both cases, it must be abelian.

Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,],\omega)
be a flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra.
Let

, and denote

and
\mathfrak{b}:=I^{\perp}/I.

Let

be the canonical projection. We have

(i)

are totally isotropic graded two-sided ideals of
(\mathfrak{g},\star)
.

(ii)

The quotient
\mathfrak{b}=I^{\perp}/I
admits a canonical structure of a flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra, defined by

[\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(u),\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(v)]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}([u,v])\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(u),\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(v))=\omega(u,v),

for all
u,v\in I^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Let us proof Part (i). Since
Z(\mathfrak{g})
is a homogeneous ideal, the element
a
can be chosen to be homogeneous. Since
a\in Z(\mathfrak{g})
, we have
a\star u=u\star a=0
for all
u\in\mathfrak{g}
, which implies that
I
is a totally isotropic graded two-sided ideal of
(\mathfrak{g},\star)
.

Now, let
v\in I^{\perp}
and
w\in\mathfrak{g}
. We have

\omega(w\star v,a)=(-1)^{|w||v|}\omega(v,w\star a)=0,\qquad\omega(v\star w,a)=(-1)^{|w||v|}\omega(w,v\star a)=0,

hence
w\star v,\,v\star w\in I^{\perp}
.

If
\omega
is even, then
I^{\perp}
is a graded two-sided ideal of
(\mathfrak{g},\star)
.

If
\omega
is odd, we distinguish two cases.
If
a
is even, and since
\omega
is odd, we have
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\subseteq I^{\perp}
,
and therefore
(I^{\perp})_{\bar{0}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
.
If
a
is odd, then
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}\subseteq I^{\perp}
, and hence

(I^{\perp})_{\bar{1}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
.
In both cases, we conclude that
I^{\perp}
is also a graded two-sided ideal of
(\mathfrak{g},\star)
.

Since
I^{\perp}
is a graded two-sided ideal of
(\mathfrak{g},\star)
, the quotient
\mathfrak{b}=I^{\perp}/I
naturally inherits the structure of a Lie superalgebra and of a quasi-Frobenius. Indeed,
for
u,v\in I^{\perp}
, we define

[\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(u),\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(v)]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}([u,v]),\qquad\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(u),\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(v))=\omega(u,v).

These operations are well-defined since
I
is totally isotropic, i.e.,
\omega(u,a)=0
for all
u\in I^{\perp}
and
a\in I
.

Moreover, since
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,],\omega)
is quasi-Frobenius then
(\mathfrak{b},[\,,\,]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})
is quasi-Frobenius as well. Furthermore, the product defined on
\mathfrak{b}
is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)\star_{\mathfrak{b}}\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(v),\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(w)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}\left([\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(u),\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(v)],\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(w)\right)+\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}\left([\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(u),\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(w)],\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(v)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(\omega\left([u,v],\omega\right)+\omega\left([u,w],v\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\omega(u\star v,w)

Thus, we deduce that
(\mathfrak{b},\star_{\mathfrak{b}})
is a left-symmetric superalgebra and hence
(\mathfrak{b},[\,,\,]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})
is a flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra.
∎

Definition
.

The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
obtained in Prop.
6.5.  Proposition
is called the
flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra deduced from

by means of
I
.

Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,],\omega)
be a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius non-abelian Lie superalgebra.
Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,],\omega)
is either:

(i)

an even flat double extension of a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{b},[\,,\,]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})
by means of
(\xi,b_{0})

(ii)

an odd flat double extension of a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{b},[\,,\,]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})
by means of
(\xi,b_{0})
.

Proof.

Theorem
6.1.  Theorem
implies
Z(\mathfrak{g})\cap Z(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}\neq 0
.
By Prop.
6.5.  Proposition
, there exists a totally isotropic two-sided graded ideal

of
(\mathfrak{g},\star)

I^{\perp}
is also a two-sided graded ideal of
(\mathfrak{g},\star)

The case where

is even.
Since
\omega
is even and non-degenerate, there exists an element

such that

\omega(e,d)=-\omega(d,e)=1.

Since
I\subseteq I^{\perp}
, there exists a subsuperspace
\mathfrak{b}
such that
I^{\perp}=I\oplus\mathfrak{B}
and the restriction
\omega_{\mathfrak{B}}=\omega|_{\mathfrak{B}\times\mathfrak{B}}

is non-degenerate. Let us write

\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{K}e\oplus{\mathfrak{B}}\oplus\mathbb{K}d,

and since
I^{\perp}=I\oplus{\mathfrak{B}}
is a two-sided graded ideal of
(\mathfrak{g},\star)

u\star v=u\star_{\mathfrak{B}}v+\mu(u,v)e,

where
\mu:{\mathfrak{B}}\times{\mathfrak{B}}\to\K

\star_{\mathfrak{B}}:{\mathfrak{B}}\times{\mathfrak{B}}\to{\mathfrak{B}}
is an even bilinear map.

It follows that
({\mathfrak{B}},\star_{\mathfrak{B}})
is a left-symmetric superalgebra,

\mu

16
),
and the restriction
\omega_{\mathfrak{B}}=\omega|_{{\mathfrak{B}}\times{\mathfrak{B}}}

({\mathfrak{B}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{B}})

\pi:{\mathfrak{B}}\rightarrow I^{\perp}/I=\mathfrak{b}

\pi(u)=\pi_{\mathfrak{b}}(u)

u\in{\mathfrak{B}},
is an isomorphism of left-symmetric superalgebras. Thus, we can identify
{\mathfrak{B}}\cong I^{\perp}/I=\mathfrak{b}

\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{K}e\oplus\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathbb{K}d,

where
(\mathfrak{b},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})=(I^{\perp}/I,[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})

is a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra.

Since
I^{\perp}=\mathbb{K}e\oplus\mathfrak{b}

(\mathfrak{g},\star)

(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
.
Then, for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}

\mathfrak{g}
are given by

[d,u]=D(u)+T(u)e,\qquad[u,v]=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{b}}+\big(\mu(u,v)-(-1)^{|u||v|}\mu(v,u)\big)e,

where
D\in\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{b})_{\bar{0}}
, and
T:\mathfrak{b}\to\K
is an even linear map.

Since
\omega
is even and non-degenerate, there exists
b_{0}\in\mathfrak{b}_{\bar{0}}

such that
T(u)=\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(b_{0},u)
.
Moreover, the product associated with

is given by

\displaystyle u\star v

\displaystyle=u\star_{\mathfrak{b}}v+\mu(u,v)e,\;

\displaystyle u\star d=\xi(u)+f(u)e,

\displaystyle d\star u

\displaystyle=\rho(u)+g(u)e,\;

\displaystyle d\star d=\lambda d+c_{0}+\alpha e,

where
\xi,\rho\in\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{b})_{\bar{0}}

Using the natural symplectic product (
13

\mu(u,v)=\omega(u\star v,d)=-(-1)^{|u||v|}\omega(v,u\star d)=\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(\xi(u),v),

and hence

which gives

Similarly,

Moreover,

\displaystyle u\star v

\displaystyle=u\star_{\mathfrak{b}}v+\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(\xi(u),v)e,\;\quad\quad\quad u\star d=\xi(u)-\tfrac{2}{3}\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(b_{0},u)e,

\displaystyle d\star u

\displaystyle=(\xi^{*}-\xi)(u)+\tfrac{1}{3}\omega_{\mathfrak{b}}(b_{0},u)e,\quad\;\;d\star d=\tfrac{1}{3}b_{0}.

(\xi,b_{0})
satisfies the system (
20

(\mathfrak{g},[\,,\,],\omega)
is the even flat double extension of the flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{b},[\,,\,]_{\mathfrak{b}},\omega_{\mathfrak{b}})

(\xi,b_{0})

.

An orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
is flat if and only if it can be obtained by a sequence
of even or odd flat double extensions of flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras, starting from the abelian superalgebra.

Proof.

If
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is non-abelian Lie superalgebra, then according to Theorem
6.7.  Theorem
,
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
is even or odd flat double extension of a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra

by means of
(\xi_{1},b_{1})
. If
(\mathfrak{b}_{1},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}})

Theorem
.

\leq n

(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\omega)
be a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra of total dimension

is either an even or an odd flat double extension of a flat orthosymplectic quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebra

7.1.
Four-dimensional flat quasi-Frobenius Lie superalgebras

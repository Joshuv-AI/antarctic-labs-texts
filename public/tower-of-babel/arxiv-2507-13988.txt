A Ghost Lemma for Commutative Ring Homomorphisms via André-Quillen Homology Thanks: The author was partly supported by the National Science Foundation under grant No. 2001368 and No. 1840190.
Daniel McCormick

Abstract
. We adapt the theory of ghost maps from derived categories to the setting of commutative rings using André-Quillen homology. The Frobenius endomorphism is a primary example of a ghost map in this setting. We prove an analogue of the ghost lemma for rings and demonstrate its utility by deducing a characteristic independent generalization of Kunz’s theorem and an analogue for complete intersection rings.

13D03 (primary); 13D05, 13D09, 13H05, 13H10

1.
Introduction

We often study objects, such as chain complexes or topological spaces,
by studying their homology. While an object with trivial homology is
homologically trivial, a map between objects may be trivial on
homology even while carrying nontrivial homological information. These
are known as ghost maps and are the subject of this
paper. Ghost maps have been studied in the derived categories of
modules, the stable homotopy category of spaces, as well as in general
triangulated categories
[
8
,
6
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
18
]
. The goal of this paper
is to introduce the study of ghost maps to commutative rings.

The derived category of modules is constructed from chain complexes in
order to apply the ideas of homotopy theory to the study of
modules. In this setting, a map of chain complexes
f\colon X\to Y

is ghost if
\mathsf{H}_{*}(f)=0
. In a similar fashion, following the work
of Quillen, we use simplicial rings to study the homotopy
theory of rings, providing us with analogues for many of the familiar
constructions and ideas from the derived category, such as free
resolutions, derived functors, etc.

The natural theory of homology for commutative rings, introduced
independently by André
[
1
]
and Quillen

[
19
,
20
]
, is known as André-Quillen homology and denoted

\mathsf{AQ}
. Under the conditions discussed in
Section

3
we
establish the following definition: if
f\colon R\to S
is a map
of (simplicial) rings, then
f
is
ghost
if
\mathsf{AQ}_{*}(f)=0
. Interpreted appropriately, the Frobenius endomorphism,

F(x)=x^{p}
, defined for any ring of prime characteristic
p
,
gives a quintessential example of a ghost map.

Within the study of ghost maps, there is a family of important
technical results known as ghost lemmas. If
R
is a ring, and

f\colon X\to Y
is a map of chain complexes of
R
-modules, we
have the following prototypical example, originally due to Kelly

[
13
]
(see also
[
8
, Theorem 8.3]
).

Let
X
be a complex of
R
-modules such that

\mathsf{H}_{i}(X)
and
\mathsf{B}_{i}(X)
have projective dimension less
than
n
for each
i
. If
f\colon X\to Y
is a composite
of at least
n
ghost maps, then
f
is zero up to homotopy.

By analogy, we prove the following ghost lemma for rings.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
k
is a field and
S
is an augmented connected
simplicial
k
-algebra such that the
i
th
homotopy group

\pi_{i}(S)
vanishes for all
i\geq 2^{n}
. If
f\colon R\to S
is
a composite of at least
n
ghost maps, then
f
is trivial up
to homotopy.

In this context, a map of
k
-algebras is trivial if it factors
through
k
. The homological information encoded by trivial ring
maps has long been used to study singularities, dating at least back
to the Auslander-Buchsbaum-Serre theorem, which can be phrased as
follows:

Suppose
R
is a local ring with residue field
k
. Then

R
is regular if and only if any/all trivial ring maps

R\to S
have finite flat dimension.

By contrast, Kunz’s theorem characterizes regularity via the
homological properties of the Frobenius endomorphism:

Suppose
R
is a ring with prime characteristic. Then

R
is regular if and only if the Frobenius endomorphism has
finite flat dimension.

However, the Frobenius endomorphism is ghost. Passing to the
simplicial Koszul complex gives us a ghost map on a connected
simplicial
k
-algebra with bounded homotopy groups, and so we use

A
to reduce Kunz’s theorem to a case of the
Auslander-Buchsbaum-Serre theorem. Generalizing the proof gives us the
following theorem.

Theorem B
.

Let
R
be a local ring and
\varphi
a local endomorphism such
that the induced map on the simplicial Koszul complex is ghost. If

\varphi
has finite flat dimension (e.g.
\varphi
is flat), then

R
is regular.

Further, using similar methods we prove a analogous results for
complete intersections.

Theorem C
.

Let
R
be a local ring and
\varphi
a local endomorphism such
that the induced map on the simplicial Koszul complex is ghost. If

\varphi^{n}
has finite CI dimension for some
n\gg 0
, then
R
is
complete intersection.

B
can also be proven using the work of Majadas

[
17
]
and
C
is closely related to
another result in
[
17
]
. See
Remark

7.10
for a more
detailed comparison.

.

B
from which we deduce Kunz’s theorem, and prove

C
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a model category. We say
\mathscr{C}\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{C}
is
a cylinder functor if it satisfies the following conditions:

•

\mathscr{C}

\mathscr{C}

•

The fold map
X\sqcup X\to X

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{X}}

•

If
X
is cofibrant, then
\rho_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}X\to X
is
a weak equivalence.

For a pair of maps
f,g\colon X\to Y
, we say
f
is

\mathscr{C}
-homotopic to
g
, written
f\mathbin{\underset{\mathscr{C}}{\sim}}g
, if there
exists a map
H\colon\mathscr{C}X\to Y
making the following diagram
commute.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f,g)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}

Remark 2.3
.

Let
f,g\colon X\to Y
and suppose
f\mathbin{\underset{\mathscr{C}}{\sim}}g
. For any map

h\colon X^{\prime}\to X
we obtain
fh\mathbin{\underset{\mathscr{C}}{\sim}}gh
by restriction.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}\sqcup X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\sqcup X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{C}X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f,g)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}

Furthermore, if we choose
h\colon X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{{\sim}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow X
to be a cofibrant
replacement, we observe that
f
and
g
induce the same arrow
in
\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C})
.

A category is pointed if it has an initial and terminal object and
these objects are isomorphic. An object with this property is called a

zero
object. If
\mathsf{C}
is pointed, then for any

X,Y\in\mathsf{C}
the unique map factoring through the zero object is
called the
trivial
map.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a pointed model category and suppose
f
is a map
in
\mathsf{C}
. We say
f
is
nullhomotopic
if it is trivial
in
\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C})
.

Remark 2.5
.

The definition given above does not require the existence of an
explicit homotopy between
f
and the trivial map.

Definition 2.6
.

A category
\mathsf{C}
is a

simplicial category
if it is enriched, powered, and copowered
in
\mathsf{sSet}

(enrichment)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Map}\colon

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}^{\mathsf{op}}

\displaystyle\times\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{sSet}

(power)

\displaystyle[-,-]\colon

\displaystyle\mathsf{sSet}^{\mathsf{op}}

\displaystyle\times\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{C}

(copower)

\displaystyle-\odot-\colon

\displaystyle\mathsf{sSet}

\displaystyle\times\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{C}

such that the triple
(\odot,[-,-],\mathrm{Map})
is a two variable adjunction,

\mathsf{C}(K\odot X,Y)\cong\mathsf{C}(X,[K,Y])\cong\mathsf{sSet}(K,\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)).

If
\mathsf{C}
is additionally a model category, then it is a

simplicial model category
provided
(\odot,[-,-],\mathrm{Map})

is a two variable Quillen adjunction
[
23
, 11.4.4]
,

[
11
, II.3.1]
.

Remark 2.7
.

If
\mathsf{C}
is complete and cocomplete, then the category

\mathsf{sC}
of simplicial
\mathsf{C}
-objects is naturally endowed
with the structure of a simplicial category where

(K\odot X)_{n}=\bigsqcup_{\sigma\in K_{n}}X_{n}

and

\mathrm{Map}(X,Y)_{n}=\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{sC}}(\Delta^{n}\odot X,Y)

[
11
, II.2.5]
.

Remark 2.8
.

If
\mathsf{C}
is a simplicial model category, then

\mathscr{C}X\coloneqq\Delta^{1}\odot X
is a cylinder functor

[
11
, II.3.5]
. Since
\odot
is a left Quillen
bifunctor, if
X
is cofibrant, then
X\odot-
preserves
acyclic cofibrations in
\mathsf{sSet}
. The inclusions of the end points

*\xhookrightarrow{{\sim}}\Delta^{1}
are acyclic cofibrations, and hence the
inclusions
i_{0},i_{1}\colon X\xhookrightarrow{{\sim}}\mathscr{C}X
are as
well. Additionally, if
X
is cofibrant and
Y
is fibrant, then
for any pair of maps
f,g\colon X\to Y
, if

in

\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C})
, then
f\mathbin{\underset{\mathscr{C}}{\sim}}g

[
11
, II.3.6]
.

In all cases considered throughout this paper, there is a canonical
forgetful functor
U\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{Set}_{*}
to pointed sets. The
extension
U\colon\mathsf{sC}\to\mathsf{sSet}_{*}
allows one to define homotopy
groups in
\mathsf{sC}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a category with a forgetful functor

U\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{Set}_{*}
. For
X\in\mathsf{sC}
, the

i
th
homotopy group is defined to be

\pi_{i}(X)\coloneqq\pi_{i}(UX)
. We say
X
is
connected
if

UX
is connected, i.e.
\pi_{0}(X)=*
.

Remark 2.10
.

If
U\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{Set}
is a forgetful functor to the
category of (non-pointed) sets with left adjoint

F\colon\mathsf{Set}\to\mathsf{C}
, and
X
is a simplicial

\mathsf{C}
-object, then one can define the
i
th
homotopy group of

X
at the base point
x\colon F(*)\to X
by

\pi_{i}(X,x)\coloneqq\pi_{i}(UX,ux)
, where
ux
is the point in

UX
corresponding to the conjugate map
x^{\dagger}\colon*\to UX
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
\mathsf{C}
is a model category with cylinder functor

\mathscr{C}
. Fix a pair of maps
f,g\colon X\to Y
with

f\mathbin{\underset{\mathscr{C}}{\sim}}g
. If
X
is cofibrant, then there is a natural
isomorphism
\mathsf{L}f^{*}\cong\mathsf{L}g^{*}\colon\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C}^{Y})\to\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C}^{X})
. In
particular,
f\simeq g
as objects in
\mathsf{C}^{X}
.

Proof.

The cylinder diagram witnessing
f\mathbin{\underset{\mathscr{C}}{\sim}}g
yields the following
diagram of restriction functors.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{C}^{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{C}^{\mathscr{C}X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{C}^{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{0}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{1}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{X}^{*}}

Since
X
and
\mathscr{C}X
are cofibrant, and each of the maps

i_{0}
,
i_{1}
, and
\rho
are weak equivalences, all three
restriction functors are Quillen equivalences, and so their left
derived functors induce equivalences between their respective
homotopy categories. Further,
\rho i_{0}=\rho i_{1}=1_{X}
, so by

Lemma

2.11
,
\mathsf{L}i_{0}^{*}\cong\mathsf{L}i_{1}^{*}
. Precomposing with

\mathsf{L}h^{*}\colon\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C}^{Y})\to\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{C}^{\mathscr{C}X})
concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

If
\mathsf{C}
is left proper, the hypothesis in

Lemma

2.12
that
X
is cofibrant can be
omitted. Left-properness is equivalent to the condition that for any
weak equivalence
\widetilde{X}\xrightarrow{{\sim}}X
, the restriction

\mathsf{C}^{X}\to\mathsf{C}^{\widetilde{X}}
is a Quillen equivalence. Applying

Lemma

2.12
to a cofibrant replacement

\widetilde{X}\xrightarrow{{\sim}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow X
thus yields the desired result.

3.
Simplicial Rings and André-Quillen Homology

In order to apply the techniques of homotopy theory to the study of
modules, we begin by embedding modules into a framework carrying a
suitable version of homotopy theory, i.e. chain complexes. Simplicial
rings provide an analogous framework for studying the homotopy theory
of rings. For more, see

[
1
,
5
,
11
,
10
,
12
,
27
,
26
,
19
,
21
,
20
]
.

Notation 3.1
.

In this section, in addition to the conventions of
2.1
,
we adhere to the following notation. Let
\mathsf{Ring}
denote the
category of commutative unital rings. Fix
k,\ell\in\mathsf{Ring}
. Any
notation defined here for
\mathsf{Ring}
we define for
\mathsf{sRing}
as
well.

•

If
k\to\ell
is a map in
\mathsf{Ring}
, then

\mathsf{Ring}^{k}_{\ell}\coloneqq(\mathsf{Ring}^{k})_{k\to\ell}\cong(\mathsf{Ring}_{\ell})^{k\to\ell}

has objects
k\to R\to\ell
(i.e.
k
-algebras with an
augmentation to
\ell
) such that the composite is the specified
map
k\to\ell
.

•

\mathsf{Ring}(k)\coloneqq\mathsf{Ring}^{k}_{k}
, the category of supplemented

k
-algebras (augmented
k
-algebras where the composite

k\to k
is the identity).

•

If
R\to S
and
R\to T
are maps of rings, then

S\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}T
is understood as the pushout in

\mathsf{Ring}
. Likewise,
S\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}T
is the homotopy pushout in

\mathsf{sRing}
.

•

If
R\to S
is a map in
\mathsf{sRing}_{k}
, then

S/\mkern-6.0mu/R\coloneqq k\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}S
.

Remark 3.2
.

The pushout
S\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}T
in
\mathsf{Ring}
corresponds to the usual tensor
product of
S
and
T
as
R
-modules with the induced ring
structure. Likewise, homotopy pushouts in
\mathsf{sRing}
are
quasi-isomorphic to the corresponding derived tensor product of
modules. In particular
\pi_{i}(S\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}T)=\mathsf{Tor}^{R}_{i}(S,T)
.
This can be seen from the fact that, if
R\hookrightarrow S
is a cofibrant
as a simplicial ring map, then
S
is cofibrant as a simplicial

R
-module.

Remark 3.3
.

If
f\colon R\hookrightarrow S
is a map in
\mathsf{sRing}(k)
, then

S/\mkern-6.0mu/R
is the mapping cone, or homotopy cofiber, of
f

and fits into the homotopy cofiber sequence

R\xrightarrow{f}S\to S/\mkern-6.0mu/R.

This construction depends on
f
.

Fix a simplicial ring
k
. Let
\mathsf{Ab}(\mathsf{sRing}(k))
denote the
category of abelian group objects in
\mathsf{sRing}(k)
and

\mathsf{Ab}\colon\mathsf{sRing}(k)\to\mathsf{Ab}(\mathsf{sRing}(k))

be the abelianization functor. In
[
19
]
, Quillen
establishes the equivalence

\mathsf{Ab}(\mathsf{sRing}(k))\simeq\mathsf{sMod}(k).

Following the notion that homology is abelianized homotopy theory, we
arrive at the following definition, independently due to André

[
1
]
and Quillen
[
19
,
20
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Fix a simplicial ring
k
and let
R\in\mathsf{sRing}(k)
. Define

\mathsf{AQ}^{k}(R)\coloneqq\mathsf{L}\mathsf{Ab}(R).

If
M\in\mathsf{sMod}(k)
, then the
i
th
André-Quillen homology module of

R
with coefficients in
M
is

\mathsf{AQ}^{k}_{i}(R;M)\coloneqq\pi_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}\mathsf{AQ}^{k}(R)}}\right),

(see
Definition

2.9
). When the base ring
k
is clear
from context we write
\mathsf{AQ}
in place of
\mathsf{AQ}^{k}
. Likewise, if

M=k
, we write
\mathsf{AQ}_{i}(R)
in place of
\mathsf{AQ}_{i}(R;M)
.

Remark 3.5
.

Under the identification
\mathsf{Ab}(\mathsf{sRing}(k))\simeq\mathsf{sMod}(k)
, it can
be shown that
\mathsf{Ab}(A)=I/I^{2}
where
I=\ker(A\to k)
.
Additionally, the Dold-Kan theorem

[
11
, III.2.5]

[
21
, Sec 2.6]

[
10
, 4.1]
provides
us with the Quillen equivalence

N\colon\mathsf{sMod}(k)\xrightarrow{{\sim}}\mathsf{Ch}_{\geq 0}(Nk).

Thus, if
A\xrightarrow{{\sim}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow R
is a cofibrant replacement and

I=\ker(A\to k)
, we may compute André-Quillen homology as the
homology of a chain complex:

\mathsf{AQ}_{i}(R)=\mathsf{H}_{i}(N(I/I^{2})).

Remark 3.6
.

Given a map
R\to S
of rings, considered as the terminal map in

\mathsf{sRing}_{S}
, note that
S/\mkern-6.0mu/R=S\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}S
. We recover
the cotangent complex as

L_{S|R}=\mathsf{AQ}^{S}(S/\mkern-6.0mu/R).

Furthermore, for
R\in\mathsf{sRing}(k)
,

\mathsf{AQ}^{k}(R)=k\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}^{\mathsf{L}}}}L_{R|k}.

Remark 3.7
.

When
k
is a field, our definition of
\mathsf{AQ}
in
Definition

3.4

should be regarded as a fiber-wise definition. Given a map of schemes

f\colon X\to Y
, one can construct a complex
\mathcal{L}_{X|Y}

of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
modules whose fiber at

x\colon\Spec(k)\to X
is quasi-isomorphic to

\mathsf{AQ}^{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mkern-6.0mu/\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)})
. Furthermore,
if
X=\Spec(S)
and
Y=\Spec(R)
as in

Remark

3.6
, then
L_{S|R}
is quasi-isomorphic to the
global section of
\mathcal{L}_{X|Y}

[
29
,
Tag
08T3
]
.

Example 3.8
.

Let
k
be a ring and
R=k[x]/(x^{2})
. Let
A
be the
simplicial polynomial ring generated by
x
in degree
0

and
y
in degree
1
, where

d_{i}(y)=\begin{cases}x^{2}&i=0\\
0&i=1,\end{cases}

i.e.
A=k[x,y\mid\partial y=x^{2}]
. Direct computation
verifies that
A\xrightarrow{{\sim}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow R
is a cofibrant replacement. By

Remark

3.5
,
\mathsf{Ab}(A)
is given by

I/I^{2}=(x,y)/(x,y)^{2}=kx\oplus ky,

so

N(I/I^{2})=0\to k\xrightarrow{0}k\to 0,

and thus

\mathsf{AQ}_{i}(R)=\begin{cases}k&i=0,1\\
0&i\geq 2.\end{cases}

Construction 3.9
.

André-Quillen homology satisfies the Eilenberg-Steenrod axioms for a
generalized reduced homology theory. In particular, homotopy cofiber
sequences yield long exact sequences in André-Quillen homology. Fix a
simplicial ring
k
and let

R\to S\to T

be a sequence of maps in
\mathsf{sRing}_{k}
. Then

S/\mkern-6.0mu/R\to T/\mkern-6.0mu/R\to T/\mkern-6.0mu/S

is a homotopy cofiber sequence in
\mathsf{sRing}(k)
. If
M
is a
simplicial
k
-module, then the induced long exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathsf{AQ}^{k}_{i}(S/\mkern-6.0mu/R;M)\to\mathsf{AQ}^{k}_{i}(T/\mkern-6.0mu/R;M)\to\mathsf{AQ}^{k}_{i}(T/\mkern-6.0mu/S;M)\to\mathsf{AQ}^{k}_{i-1}(S/\mkern-6.0mu/R;M)\to\cdots

is known as the
Jacobi-Zariski
sequence (cf.
[
12
]
).

Remark 3.10
.

Let
S
be a Noetherian local ring with
residue field
k
and let
\widehat{S}
be the completion of
S

at the maximal ideal. Then

\widehat{S}/\mkern-6.0mu/S=k\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{S}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{S}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{S}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{S}^{\mathsf{L}}}}\widehat{S}\simeq k\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{S}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{S}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{S}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{S}}}\widehat{S}=k,

so
\mathsf{AQ}^{k}(\widehat{S}/\mkern-6.0mu/S)\simeq\mathsf{AQ}^{k}(k)\simeq 0
. If

R\to S
is any map of rings, then by applying the Jacobi-Zariski
sequence from
3.9
to
R\to S\to\widehat{S}

we conclude

\mathsf{AQ}^{k}(S/\mkern-6.0mu/R)\simeq\mathsf{AQ}^{k}(\widehat{S}/\mkern-6.0mu/R)
.

4.
The Relative Frobenius and the Koszul Complex

In this section we review the relative Frobenius and the Koszul
complex in the simplicial setting, and highlight their relationship.

Notation 4.1
.

We adopt the notation of
2.1
and
3.1
for
this section. Additionally, if
A\xrightarrow{f}B
is a ring map, then

B_{f}
denotes an identical copy of
B
with an
A
-algebra
structure given via
f
. Note that, since the underlying ring
is the same,
\mathsf{Ring}(B_{f})=\mathsf{Ring}(B)
.

Construction 4.2

(Relative Frobenius)
.

Suppose
k\to R
is a map of simplicial rings of prime
characteristic
p
, and let
F
denote the Frobenius
endomorphism. Commutativity of the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

yields the map

F_{R|k}\colon k_{F}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}R\to R_{F}

in
\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{sRing}^{k_{F}})
, known as the
derived relative Frobenius
(cf.
[
29
,
Tag 0CC9
]
).

Construction 4.3
.

Let
R
be a ring and
\mathbf{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
be a sequence of
elements in
R
. Using the notation of
Example

3.8
, define
the
simplicial Koszul complex
,
K(\mathbf{f})
, of
\mathbf{f}
to
be the simplicial polynomial ring

K(\mathbf{f})\coloneqq R[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\mid\partial\xi_{i}=f_{i}].

If
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a noetherian local ring, define
K^{R}
to be
the simplicial Koszul complex on a minimal generating set for

\mathfrak{m}
. One may directly verify that

\mathfrak{m}\cdot\pi_{*}(K^{R})=\pi_{*}(\mathfrak{m}\cdot K^{R})=0,

so if
\widehat{R}
denotes the completion of
R
at
\mathfrak{m}
, then

K^{R}\simeq\widehat{R}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}K^{R}\cong K^{\widehat{R}}.

If
R
is complete, then by the Cohen structure theorem,

R\cong Q/I
where
Q=\Lambda[\mkern-3.0mu[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\mkern-3.0mu]
,
and
\Lambda
is either
k
or a discrete valuation ring with
uniformizer
p
a prime number
[
29
,
Tag 032A
]
. Additionally,

Q
can be chosen minimally, in the sense that

\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},p\}
is a minimal generating set for

\mathfrak{m}_{R}
. Thus if
Q
is minimal, we may directly verify

k\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{Q}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}^{\mathsf{L}}}}R\simeq K^{Q}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{Q}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}}}R\simeq K^{R}.

If
\varphi\colon(R,k)\to(S,\ell)
is a map of noetherian local
rings, define the map

K^{\varphi}\colon\ell\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{k}^{\mathsf{L}}}}K^{R}\to K^{S},

in
\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{sRing}(\ell))
, where the map
k\to K^{R}
is given by

k\to k\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{Q}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q}^{\mathsf{L}}}}\widehat{R}\simeq K^{\widehat{R}}\simeq K^{R}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Treating
K(\mathbf{f})
as a simplicial
R
-module and applying the
Dold-Kan theorem (see
Remark

3.5
), recovers the usual
Koszul complex of
R
. This can be seen by direct verification
when
n=1

[
12
, 4.16]
. Since

K(\mathbf{f})=K(f_{1})\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}\cdots\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}K(f_{n}),
the
general claim follows by induction. In particular, we note that

\begin{cases}\pi_{0}(K(\mathbf{f}))\cong R/(\mathbf{f})\\
\pi_{i}(K(\mathbf{f}))=0&i>n.\end{cases}

Remark 4.5
.

If
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}
are minimal generating sets for the same
ideal in
R
, then there is an isomorphism

K(\mathbf{f})\xrightarrow{{\cong}}K(\mathbf{f}^{\prime})
defined by expressing the elements of

\mathbf{f}
as linear combinations of elements of
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}
.

Remark 4.6
.

Let
(R,k)
be a local ring with prime characteristic

p
. Let
F\colon R\to R
denote the Frobenius endomorphism and
fix a minimal Cohen presentation
Q\to\hat{R}
. The induced map

K^{F}
on the Koszul complex is given by

k_{F}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{Q_{F}}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q_{F}}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q_{F}}^{\mathsf{L}}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{Q_{F}}^{\mathsf{L}}}}F_{\hat{R}|Q}
.

Notation 5.1

We conclude this section with an illustration of the rigidity

Proposition 5.5
.

Fix a ring
R
and a map
\varphi\colon S\to T
in
\mathsf{Ring}(R)
. Let

If

\varphi
is ghost then
\varphi(I_{S})\subset I_{T}^{2}
.

Proof.

The suspension of
S
in
\mathsf{sRing}(R)
is given by

. Thus, the computation from

[
19
, 3.14]
gives us

\mathsf{AQ}_{0}(S)\cong\mathsf{AQ}_{1}(R/\mkern-6.0mu/S)\cong I_{S}/I_{S}^{2}.

Likewise, we obtain
\mathsf{AQ}_{0}(T)=I_{T}/I_{T}^{2}
. Since
\varphi
is
ghost, the induced map

I_{S}/I_{S}^{2}\to I_{T}/I_{T}^{2}

is zero, so
\varphi(I_{S})\subset I_{T}^{2}
as desired.
∎

If
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a local ring, recall that a local endomorphism

\varphi\colon R\to R
is contracting if
\varphi^{j}(\mathfrak{m})\subset\mathfrak{m}^{2}

for
j\gg 0

[

]
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Suppose
R
is an equicharacteristic local ring with residue field

k
and let
\varphi
be is a local endomorphism. If
\varphi
is
ghost as a map in
\mathsf{Ring}(k)
, then
\varphi
is contracting.

We now pivot our discussion to the case where
K^{\varphi}
is ghost.

Remark 5.7
.

Suppose
R
is a complete local ring and let
Q\to R
be a
minimal Cohen presentation as in
4.3
, where

Q=\Lambda[\mkern-3.0mu[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\mkern-3.0mu]
. Since
Q
is
regular,
\mathsf{AQ}(Q/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda)\simeq(\mathbf{x})/(\mathbf{x})^{2}
. In
particular,
\mathsf{AQ}_{\geq 1}(Q/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda)=0
. Further,
minimality of
Q\to R
implies

\mathsf{AQ}_{0}(Q/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda)\cong\mathsf{AQ}_{0}(R/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda)
, and so
the long exact sequence in André-Quillen homology corresponding to the
homotopy cofiber sequence

Q/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda\to R/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda\to R/\mkern-6.0mu/Q

shows that
\mathsf{AQ}_{i}(R/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda)\cong AQ_{i}(R/\mkern-6.0mu/Q)
for

i\geq 1
. Thus, since
R/\mkern-6.0mu/Q\simeq K^{R}
, the condition
that
K^{\varphi}
is ghost is equivalent to

\mathsf{AQ}_{\geq 1}(\varphi/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda)=0
. In particular, this
condition holds when
\varphi/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda
is itself ghost.

Remark 5.8
.

Suppose
R\in\mathsf{Ring}(k)
is a local complete intersection ring with
residue field
k
. By definition
\hat{R}
admits a minimal
Cohen presentation
\hat{R}\cong Q/I
where
I
is generated by a
regular sequence. A direct calculation similar to that in the proof
of
Proposition

5.5
shows that

\mathsf{AQ}_{i}(R)\cong\begin{cases}\mathfrak{m}_{Q}/\mathfrak{m}_{Q}^{2}&i=0\\
I/I^{2}&i=1\\
0&i\geq 2\end{cases}

Thus by
Remark

5.7
, if
\varphi\colon R\to R
is a local
endomorphism, then
K^{\varphi}
is ghost if and only if

\tilde{\varphi}(I)\subset I^{2}
, where
\tilde{\varphi}
is a lift of

\varphi
to
Q
. If
R
is mixed characteristic with

\Lambda\subset Q
as in

, then a similar
statement exists for
\mathsf{AQ}(R/\mkern-6.0mu/\Lambda)
and one deduces that

K^{\varphi}
is ghost if and only if
\tilde{\varphi}(I)\subset pI+I^{2}
.

Example 5.9
.

The following are examples of local complete

with residue field

and

\varphi
. In each example, the map
\varphi
is

•

•

R=k[\mkern-3.0mu[x,y]\mkern-3.0mu]/(xy)
and
\varphi=\begin{cases}x\mapsto x\\
y\mapsto 0\end{cases}

•

and
\varphi=\begin{cases}x\mapsto x\\
y\mapsto y^{2}\\
z\mapsto xz^{2}\end{cases}

Example 5.10
.

Fix

a prime and let

. Consider the

defined by

and

is ghost.

6.
A Ghost Lemma for Commutative Rings

We now state the main result of this paper, a
ghost lemma
for
commutative rings, analogous to
[
13
, Theorem 3]
(see also

[
8
, Theorem 8.3]
). Recall that a nullhomotopic map is
one which is trivial in the homotopy category
(
Definition

2.4
) and a trivial map in
\mathsf{sRing}(k)
is one
which factors through
k
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
k
be a field. Suppose
S\in\mathsf{sRing}(k)
is connected
and
\pi_{i}(S)=0
for all
i\geq 2^{n}
. If
f\colon R\to S
is
a composition of at least
n
ghost maps, then
f
is
nullhomotopic.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of the above
theorem.

Notation 6.2
.

In addition to the notation of
2.1
and
3.1
,
we adhere to the following notation.

•

\mathsf{NRing}(k)
is the category of nonunital commutative

k
-algebras.

•

\mathsf{K}\colon\mathsf{NRing}(k)\to\mathsf{Ring}(k)
is the functor

I\mapsto k\oplus I
, where the multiplicative structure is
given by
(a+i)(b+j)=ab+(aj+bi+ij)
.

•

\mathscr{C}_{\mathsf{Ring}}
,
\mathscr{C}_{\mathsf{NRing}}
, and
\mathscr{C}_{\mathsf{Mod}}

denote the simplicial cylinder functors for
\mathsf{sRing}(k)
,

\mathsf{sNRing}(k)
, and
\mathsf{sMod}(k)
respectively (
Remark

2.8
).

•

For each of the above cylinder functors, we adopt the
shorthand
f\mathbin{\underset{\mathsf{C}}{\sim}}g
for
f\mathbin{\underset{\mathscr{C}_{\mathsf{C}}}{\sim}}g
.

For every
R\in\mathsf{Ring}(k)
, there is a splitting of
k
-modules

R=k\oplus I
, where
I
is the augmentation ideal

\ker(R\to k)
. Consequently, the functor

\mathsf{K}\colon\mathsf{NRing}(k)\to\mathsf{Ring}(k)
is an equivalence of categories.

Remark 6.3
.

Fix a ring map
k\to\ell
. The free functor

F\colon\mathsf{Set}\to\mathsf{Ring}^{k}_{\ell}
takes a set
X
to the
polynomial ring
k[X]
equipped with the inclusion
k\to k[X]

and the augmentation
k[X]\to\ell
sending the elements of
X

to 0. The right adjoint of this functor is the forgetful functor

U\colon\mathsf{Ring}^{k}_{\ell}\to\mathsf{Set}
. Contrary to what one might
expect, we can conclude from the above description of
F
that

U(R)
is the underlying set of the augmentation ideal

I=\ker(R\to\ell)
rather than the underlying set of
R

itself. The pointed version of the forgetful functor is defined
identically, where 0 is identified as the marked point. The homotopy
groups of
R
as defined in
Definition

2.9
are thus
the homotopy groups of
I
as a simplicial set. Consequently, for

R
to be connected as an object in
\mathsf{sRing}^{k}_{\ell}
, we ask that

\pi_{0}(I)=0
.

Let
R\to S
be a map in
\mathsf{sRing}(k)
and let
I
and
J
be
their respective augmentation ideals. We sketch an
outline below for the proof of the theorem in two stages.

Stage 1

R\to S
is ghost

\implies

I/I^{2}\to J/J^{2}
is nullhomotopic as a module homomorphism

\implies

I/I^{2}\to J/J^{2}
is nullhomotopic as an algebra homomorphism (
6.6
)

\implies

I\to J
factors (up to homotopy) through
J^{2}
as an algebra homomorphism (
6.7
)

Stage 2

R=R_{0}\to R_{1}\to\cdots\to R_{n}=S
is a sequence of ghost maps

\implies

I\to J
factors (up to homotopy) through
I_{0}\to I_{1}^{2}\to\cdots\to I_{n}^{2^{n}}

\implies

R\to S
factors (up to homotopy) through
\mathsf{K}(J^{n})

\implies

R\to S
is nullhomotopic (
6.4
,
6.5
).

Lemma 6.4
.

[
20
, Theorem 6.12]

Let
R\in\mathsf{sRing}(k)
be a degree-wise finitely generated
polynomial
k
-algebra. If
I=\ker(R\to k)
, then

\pi_{i}(I^{n})=0
for
i<n
.∎

[

.

.

Let

We now use

Notation 7.1
.

In this section, we adhere to the notational conventions of

2.1
,
3.1
and
4.1
. We begin by

Definition 7.2
.

[
2
, 1.2]

Let
R
be a local ring. A
quasi-deformation
of
R
is a
diagram
R\to R^{\prime}\leftarrow Q
, where
R\to R^{\prime}
is a flat local
extension and
R^{\prime}\leftarrow Q
is a surjective local map whose kernel
is generated by a regular sequence. If
M
is an
R
-module,
then the
complete intersection dimension
, abbreviated

CI-dimension
, is defined by

\cid_{R}(M)\coloneqq\inf\{\pd_{Q}(R^{\prime}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}{\mathbin{{\otimes}_{R}}}M)-\pd_{Q}R^{\prime}\mid R\to R^{\prime}\leftarrow Q\text{ is a quasi-deformation}\}.

See
[
25
]
for a discussion of the case where

M
is not finitely generated.

Lemma 7.3
.

[
2
, 1.3]

Let
(R,k)
be a local ring. Then
R
is a complete
intersection if and only if
\cid_{R}(k)<\infty
.

Lemma 7.4
.

If

is a pair of maps in
\mathsf{sRing}
which
agrees in
\mathsf{Ho}(\mathsf{sRing})
, then
S_{f}\simeq S_{g}
as

-algebras.

Proof.

First, note that
\mathsf{sRing}
is left-proper. This follows immediately
from
Remark

3.2
, since the tensor product of
modules is left-Quillen. Second, note that all objects in

\mathsf{sRing}
are fibrant. If
p\colon\widetilde{R}\xrightarrow{{\sim}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow R
is a
cofibrant replacement, then by
Remark

2.8
and

Lemma

2.12
,
S_{fp}\simeq S_{gp}
over

\widetilde{R}
and hence
S_{f}\simeq S_{g}
over
R
by

Remark

2.13
.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let

be a map in
\mathsf{Ring}_{k}
with finite flat dimension.
If

\mathsf{Tor}^{Q}_{*}(k,K)

Proof.

Note that

and consider the associated Tor spectral sequence

,
Tag 0662
]

By hypothesis,

. Likewise,

Corollary 7.8

(Kunz-Rodicio)
.

[

,
24
]

Suppose

•

is the free

.

Now suppose

has finite flat dimension. By

Remark

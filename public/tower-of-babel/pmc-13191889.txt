Median effective concentration of liposomal bupivacaine for ultrasound-guided supra-inguinal fascia iliaca compartment block in older adults undergoing total hip arthroplasty: a dixon geometric up-and-down dose-finding study
Authors: Tianping He, Shuyong You, Jianfeng Huang, Qiuhao Shui, Bo Peng, Shiyuan Li, Hao Kong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to determine the median effective concentration (EC50) of liposomal bupivacaine (LB) for ultrasound-guided supra-inguinal fascia iliaca compartment block (S-FICB) in elderly patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty (THA), and to evaluate the safety of LB within the tested concentration range.MethodsThis prospective, double-blind, sequential dose-finding trial enrolled patients aged 60–90 years scheduled for unilateral THA under general anesthesia. Following anesthesia induction, ultrasound-guided S-FICB was administered with a fixed injection volume of 40 mL. The initial LB concentration was set at 0.040%, with subsequent concentrations adjusted using the Dixon geometric up-and-down method at a 2% step size (×0.98 for a successful block or ×1.02 for a failed block). The sequential dosing phase was terminated upon observation of seven success–failure crossover pairs. Block success was defined as a resting Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) score ≤ 2 and a movement NRS score ≤ 3 within postoperative 24 h, with no effective patient-controlled intravenous analgesia administrations and no rescue analgesia required. The EC50 and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were estimated using probit regression. EC90 and EC95 were estimated using isotonic regression with the pooled-adjacent-violators algorithm (PAVA), with 95% CIs derived from bootstrap resampling. Adverse events were recorded for safety assessment.ResultsThirty-one patients completed the sequential trial; 24 observations (12 successes and 12 failures) were included in EC50 modeling. The EC50 of LB for S-FICB in older patients undergoing THA was 0.0347% (95% CI, 0.0344–0.0351). The total dose corresponding to 40 mL of 0.0347% LB was 13.88 mg and the body weight-normalized dose was approximately 0.237 mg kg− 1. No postoperative motor block was observed.ConclusionsIn elderly patients undergoing THA who received ultrasound-guided S-FICB with a fixed 40 ml injection volume, the EC50 of LB was determined to be 0.0347%. This result provided a valuable empirical reference for the rational selection of LB concentrations in clinical fascia iliaca plane analgesia for geriatric patients.Trial registrationThis study was retrospectively registered in Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500112398) on 13 November 2025.Keywords: Liposomal bupivacaine, Median effective concentration, Supra-inguinal fascia iliaca compartment block, Up-and-down sequential method, Postoperative analgesia

Introduction

Total hip arthroplasty (THA) is the first-line surgical intervention for femoral head necrosis and end-stage osteoarthritis, which confers significant pain relief and marked functional improvement in affected patients [1]. However, extensive surgical trauma and prominent postoperative inflammatory responses frequently lead to moderate-to-severe postoperative pain [2]. Pain-induced sympathetic hyperactivation may elevate perioperative cardiovascular risk, especially in the elderly population. Age-related decline in physiological reserve, a high prevalence of comorbidities, and impaired autonomic regulatory function render elderly patients more susceptible to surgical stress and nociceptive stimulation, thereby increasing the risk of perioperative complications such as hemodynamic instability, cognitive dysfunction, and respiratory depression [3]. Therefore, achieving adequate perioperative analgesia while minimizing opioid exposure and the risk of local anesthetic toxicity constitutes a core goal of perioperative care for this patient cohort [4].

The supra-inguinal fascia iliaca compartment block (S-FICB) targets the principal afferent pain pathways associated with THA, with ultrasound guidance enabling precise targeting of the femoral nerve, lateral femoral cutaneous nerve, and anterior branches of the obturator nerve [5]. Compared with the conventional infrainguinal approach, S-FICB has been demonstrated to provide broader analgesic coverage, reduce perioperative opioid consumption, and promote early postoperative mobilization and functional recovery [6]. Consequently, S-FICB is now widely integrated into multimodal analgesia regimens for THA procedures.

Liposomal bupivacaine (LB) is formulated with DepoFoam™ multi-cavity sustained-release technology, which allows for continuous local drug release at the injection site for 48–72 h. This unique formulation maintains a stable local anesthetic concentration, thereby prolonging the analgesic duration and reducing the potential for local anesthetic toxicity [7]. Prior studies have confirmed that LB can enhance postoperative analgesic efficacy and decrease opioid requirements when used for peripheral nerve blocks in shoulder and knee arthroplasty [8, 9]. Nevertheless, its median effective concentration (EC50) for S-FICB in elderly patients undergoing THA has not yet been elucidated.

In this study, we adopted the Dixon up-and-down method to determine the EC50 of LB for ultrasound-guided S-FICB in elderly patients undergoing THA. We also evaluated the postoperative analgesic efficacy and safety profile of LB at the derived concentrations. The primary objective of this research was to provide evidence-based dosage guidance for LB, so as to optimize precision regional anesthesia and improve perioperative recovery outcomes in elderly patients undergoing THA.

Methods

Ethics statement and study design

This prospective, double-blind, sequential dose-finding study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Luxian People’s Hospital (Approval No. 2024054; December 26, 2024) and registered in Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500112398). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to study enrollment.

Elderly patients scheduled for elective unilateral THA were recruited between June 2025 and October 2025. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) age 60–90 years, (ii) body mass index (BMI) 18.5–28.0 kg·m⁻², and (iii) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status II or III. Exclusion criteria included: (i) allergy to amide-type local anesthetics, (ii) infection or anatomical abnormalities at the planned puncture site, (iii) coagulation disorders, (iv) chronic opioid or sedative use, (v) severe cardiopulmonary, hepatic or renal dysfunction, pre-existing neurological or cognitive disorders, or (vi) refusal to provide informed consent. Patients were also excluded if the block could not be performed as scheduled due to technical failure or if the surgical protocol was altered intraoperatively.

Anesthesia management

All patients were fasting from solid food for 8 h and from clear liquids for at least 2 h prior to anesthesia induction. Upon admission to the operating room, monitors including electrocardiography, non-invasive blood pressure, pulse oximetry, and bispectral index were applied. Radial arterial catheter was placed for continuous invasive arterial pressure monitoring. Oxygen was delivered via nasal cannula at a rate of 2.5 L min− 1. A peripheral intravenous access was established, and lactated Ringer’s solution or 0.9% sodium chloride solution (4–6 mL kg− 1) was administered for pre-induction volume expansion.

General anesthesia was induced with sufentanil 0.25–0.40 µg kg− 1, etomidate 0.3 mg kg− 1, and rocuronium 0.5–0.6 mg kg− 1, followed by tracheal intubation 3 min later. Anesthesia maintenance was achieved with sevoflurane at approximately 0.4 minimum alveolar concentration, a continuous infusion of remifentanil 0.1–0.2 µg kg− 1 min− 1, and a continuous infusion of propofol 0.3–0.6 mg kg− 1 h− 1. All anesthetic agents were titrated based on hemodynamic vital signs and surgical stimulus intensity to maintain a BIS value of 40–60. Intraoperative hypotension, defined as a ≥ 20% decrease in mean arterial pressure (MAP) from baseline or a systolic blood pressure < 90 mmHg or MAP < 60 mmHg, was managed with an intravenous bolus of phenylephrine 50–100 µg. Bradycardia, defined as a heart rate < 40 beats per minute, was treated with an intravenous bolus of atropine 0.3 mg. Granisetron 4 mg was routinely administered intravenously for preventing postoperative nausea and vomiting.

S-FICB procedure and sequential dosing

After anesthesia induction, patients were placed in the supine position with the operative limb extended for ultrasound-guided S-FICB administration. A high-frequency ultrasound transducer (6–13 MHz) was positioned longitudinally above the inguinal ligament, with its lateral end oriented toward the anterior superior iliac spine. Using the deep circumflex iliac artery as the anatomical landmark, the internal oblique muscle, sartorius muscle, iliacus muscle, and fascia iliaca were identified to obtain the characteristic “bow-tie” ultrasound view. The block was performed using an in-plane needle technique with a 22-gauge needle. LB was injected incrementally to a total fixed volume of 40 ml, with real-time confirmation of adequate fascial plane spread. All S-FICB procedures were performed by anesthesiologists with more than 3 years of experience in ultrasound-guided regional anesthesia.

The initial concentration of 0.040% was selected for the up-and-down sequence based on our preliminary clinical experience and limited published evidence. This concentration was verified in our pilot practice to yield effective analgesia with no obvious adverse reactions in most elderly THA patients undergoing S-FICB. and it falls within the reported effective concentration range (0.033%–0.045%) of LB for FICB in older adults undergoing THA [10–12]. Subsequent concentrations were allocated using a Dixon geometric up-and-down rule with a 2% step: after a successful block, the next patient received a 2% lower concentration (×0.98); after failure, the concentration was increased by 2% (×1.02). The sequential trial ended after seven success–failure crossover pairs were observed. Block success was defined a priori as a resting numerical rating scale (NRS) score ≤ 2 and a movement NRS score ≤ 3 within postoperative 24 h, with no effective patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) demands and no requirement for rescue analgesics. Failure to meet any of these criteria was classified as block failure. Postoperative analgesia was provided using PCIA. The PCIA solution consisted of sufentanil 2.5 µg kg− 1 diluted with normal saline to a total volume of 150 ml. The pump was programmed with a loading dose of 3 mL, a continuous background infusion of 2 mL h− 1, a patient-controlled bolus dose of 2 mL, and a lockout interval of 15 min. Patients were instructed to activate the PCIA pump when the resting NRS score was ≥ 3 or the movement NRS score was ≥ 4. If pain relief remained inadequate after three or more effective PCIA boluses, intramuscular tramadol 50 mg was administered as rescue analgesia.

Three independent attending anesthesiologists were responsible for study drug preparation, S-FICB performance, and postoperative outcome assessment, respectively. The anesthesiologist who prepared the study solutions was aware of the LB concentrations but was not involved in the block procedure or any postoperative assessments. The anesthesiologist who performed the block and the one who conducted postoperative assessments were blinded to the assigned LB concentrations. All patients were kept blinded to the treatment assignments throughout the study.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the median effective concentration (EC50) and its 95% CI of liposomal bupivacaine for S-FICB in elderly patients undergoing THA. Secondary outcomes included: (i) the EC90 and EC95 and their 95% CIs of liposomal bupivacaine for S-FICB in elderly patients undergoing THA; (ii) postoperative pain, evaluated by NRS scores at rest and on movement at postoperative 24 and 48 h; (iii) number of PCIA demands, total sufentanil consumption, and requirement for rescue tramadol. Safety outcomes included local anesthetic systemic toxicity, motor function, nausea and vomiting, and delirium. Motor function was assessed by the modified Bromage score and Medical Research Council (MRC) muscle strength score.

Sample size

Sample size was determined based on the classic Dixon up-and-down sequential design. Methodological studies and simulation analyses have confirmed that six success–failure crossover pairs are sufficient to achieve reliable and stable estimation of the EC50, thereby minimizing bias from an inadequate sample size and avoiding excessive patient enrollment [13]. A stopping rule of seven success–failure crossover pairs was pre-specified a priori in the study protocol. This stopping rule is consistent with previous dose-finding studies employing the up-and-down method [14–16].

Statistical analysis

Normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Between-group comparisons were performed using independent t-tests for normally distributed data, presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and the Mann–Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data, presented as median (interquartile range, IQR). Categorical variables are summarized as n (%) and were analyzed using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test when the expected cell frequency was < 5.

Concentration–response data were analyzed using probit regression and isotonic regression with the pooled-adjacent-violators algorithm (PAVA). Probit regression was used to model the concentration–response relationship and estimate the EC50. PAVA was applied to obtain monotonic adjusted response probabilities across the tested concentrations, and EC90 and EC95 were subsequently derived by inverse interpolation from the PAVA-adjusted concentration–response curve. The 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were constructed using bootstrap resampling with 2,000 iterations. Model fit for the probit analysis was assessed using a goodness-of-fit test and residual diagnostics. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 26.0) and R software (version 4.3.2).

Results

Participants

Thirty-five elderly patients scheduled for unilateral THA were assessed for eligibility. Four patients were excluded: two declined participation, one was younger than 60 years old, and one was excluded because of a coagulation disorder. Thirty-one patients completed the trial and were included in the sequential analysis. The participant flow through the trial is presented in Fig. 1. Dose escalation and de-escalation were initiated in the first 7 patients to establish the initial response range. In accordance with the up-and-down sequential design, block outcomes began to alternate between success and failure starting from the eighth patient. Recruitment was stopped once the seventh success–failure crossover was observed in the 31st patient (Fig. 2). The EC50 was therefore estimated using 24 evaluable patients, with 12 successes and 12 failures. No patient dropout or loss to follow-up occurred during the study (Fig. 3).

Dixon up-and-down analysis and EC50 estimation

The success rate of LB for S-FICB increased with concentration in older patients undergoing THA (Table 2). The estimated EC50 was 0.0347% (95% CI 0.0344–0.0351). The total dose corresponding to 40 ml of 0.0347% LB was 13.88 mg, and the body weight-normalized EC50 was approximately 0.237 mg kg− 1. The probit regression model showed a satisfactory fit (χ² = 22.42, df = 22, P = 0.435), with residuals showing no major deviation from normality.

Isotonic regression using PAVA generated monotonic adjusted response probabilities that were consistent with the overall concentration-dependent trend observed in the probit model, with an EC50 of 0.0347% (95% CI 0.0343–0.0350). Based on the PAVA-adjusted concentration–response curve, the EC90 and EC95 were estimated to be 0.0352% (95% CI 0.0348–0.0357) and 0.0354% (95% CI 0.0349–0.0360), respectively.

Comparison of analgesic outcomes

Patients with successful blocks exhibited significantly lower resting and movement NRS pain scores at both postoperative 24 and 48 h compared with those in the failure group (all P < 0.05). The success group also showed reduced 24-hour PCIA sufentanil consumption and lower proportion of patients requiring rescue tramadol administration (Table 3).

Response rates and complications across LB concentrations

The observed response rates, PAVA-adjusted response rates, and adverse events at each tested LB concentration are summarized in Table 2. The overall observed success rates ranged from 0% to 100% across the concentration gradient, with corresponding PAVA-adjusted response rates increasing in a concentration-dependent manner. The most frequent adverse events were nausea and vomiting as well as postoperative delirium, whereas no cases of nerve injury, local anesthetic systemic toxicity, motor blockade (Bromage score > 0), or impaired MRC muscle strength (MRC score ≤ 4) were observed at any concentration level (Table 2).

Discussion

This dose-finding study showed that the EC50 of LB for S-FICB in elderly patients undergoing THA was 0.0347% (95% CI 0.0344–0.0351) with a fixed injectate volume of 40 ml. This finding offers a specific, clinically actionable reference for dose individualization and opioid-sparing analgesia in this vulnerable population. It supports the use of low-concentration LB for S-FICB, balancing effective sensory blockade with preserved motor function, thereby aligning with enhanced recovery after surgery principles to reduce perioperative complications and accelerate postoperative rehabilitation.

LB is a long-acting sustained release local anesthetic [17]. Its unique formulation helps to maintain effective analgesic concentrations at the block site without compromising motor function. A recent randomized controlled trial demonstrated that fascia iliaca compartment block with liposomal bupivacaine can significantly improve the quality of postoperative recovery and reduce opioid consumption in patients undergoing hip fracture surgery when compared with ropivacaine block [10]. Meta-analysis demonstrated that compared with the traditional bupivacaine, liposomal bupivacaine showed better pain control at 24 h and reduced the length of hospital stay after THA [18]. Therefore, LB’s sustained-release property ensures prolonged pain control, which is particularly beneficial for managing postoperative pain following THA.

A critical factor in the efficacy of S-FICB is the injectate volume, as previous imaging and cadaveric studies have consistently demonstrated that volumes ranging from 30 to 40 mL optimize the spread of local anesthetics within the fascia iliaca compartment, thereby maximizing neural coverage [19, 20]. In the present study, a fixed injectate volume of 40 mL was adopted for S-FICB, which not only minimized variability in neural coverage but also ensured consistent block performance across all participants [21, 22].

Several age-related anatomical and physiological alterations may account for the relatively low effective concentration observed in the current investigation. Aging is associated with reduced peripheral nerve fiber density, increased compliance of fascial layers, and decreased local tissue perfusion. These changes promote the spread of local anesthetics within the fascial compartment [23]. Elderly patients also commonly show reduced plasma protein binding and slower drug metabolic clearance. These conditions can extend tissue exposure to liposomal bupivacaine and lower the concentration needed to achieve satisfactory sensory blockade [24]. The present findings are consistent with these age-related physiological changes and support the use of reduced concentrations of liposomal bupivacaine in elderly patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty [12, 25, 26].

Regarding adverse outcomes, no cases of local anesthetic toxicity or nerve injury were observed across the concentration ranges evaluated. These findings support a favorable safety profile of liposomal bupivacaine in this clinical setting [9, 27]. The complete absence of motor blockade, even at the highest concentration tested, may indicate a wide therapeutic window associated with the low doses used in this study [28, 29].

Patients with successful blocks achieved lower pain scores and reduced opioid consumption at 24 and 48 h postoperatively, together with decreased requirements for rescue analgesics, while motor function remained fully preserved. The sustained analgesic effect of liposomal bupivacaine, combined with intact motor function, confers substantial clinical benefits for elderly patients undergoing THA. Consistent with our findings, previous studies have confirmed that liposomal bupivacaine provides effective analgesia when administered via transversus abdominis plane block [30], rectus sheath block [31], and thoracic paravertebral block [32]. Moreover, a meta-analysis including 11 randomized controlled trials with 1,357 patients demonstrated that LB was associated with improved quality of recovery scores within 72 h after surgery, a lower incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting, and reduced opioid consumption [33].

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the sample size, statistically adequate for EC50 estimation under the Dixon up-and-down sequential design with the pre-specified stopping rule of seven success–failure crossover pairs, was modest, which may limit the generalizability of findings and reliable assessment of safety outcomes [13, 34]. Additionally, the modest sample size restricts the precision of exploratory-derived EC90 and EC95 values, which should not be interpreted as clinical dosing recommendations. Second, the fixed-volume technique prohibits investigation of volume-concentration interactions, while the findings should be evaluated in light of the study’s single-center context.

In summary, this study determined that the median effective concentration of liposomal bupivacaine for ultrasound-guided supra-inguinal fascia iliaca compartment block was 0.0347% in elderly patients undergoing total hip arthroplasty. At low concentrations, liposomal bupivacaine provided effective postoperative analgesia, reduced opioid utilization, and preserved motor function. These results offer clinically meaningful evidence for dose optimization and provide a basis for subsequent multicenter investigations and integrated pharmacokinetic–pharmacodynamic modeling.

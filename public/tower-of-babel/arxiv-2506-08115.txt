1.
Introduction and main result

We consider the Laplacian
\Delta
and the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
with the added Hardy potential
-\kappa/|x|^{\alpha}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Here,
d\in\mathbb{N}:={1,2,\ldots}
,
\alpha\in(0,2]
, with
\alpha<d
, and
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
is called the coupling constant. Note that
|x|^{-\alpha}
is locally integrable on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.
We call the Hardy potential attractive (mass-creating) if
\kappa>0
and repulsive (mass-killing) if
\kappa<0
. Note that the operators
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
and the multiplication by
-\kappa|x|^{-\alpha}
are homogeneous of the same degree (with respect to dilations of functions).
We will study their sum
in a connection to the following inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\geq\kappa_{\rm c}^{(\alpha)}(d)\left\||x|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},\quad f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{split}

(1.1)

known as the Hardy–Kato–Herbst inequality.
Here, again,
\alpha\in(0,2]\cap(0,d)
and the inequality is sharp if we take

\displaystyle\kappa_{\rm c}^{(\alpha)}(d):=2^{\alpha}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{4}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-\alpha}{4}\right)^{2}},

(1.2)

see
[
Har19
,
Har20
,
Kat66
,
Her77
]
and

[
KPS81
,
Yaf99
,
FLS08
,
FS08
]
.
We simply call (
1.1
) the Hardy inequality.
Thus, the quadratic form associated with
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}-\kappa|x|^{-\alpha}
on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded from below if and only if
\kappa\leq\kappa_{\rm c}^{(\alpha)}(d)
, in which case, the form is non-negative. Then, by a theorem of Friedrichs, there is a corresponding non-negative, self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, which we call the Hardy operator and denote by
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}-\kappa|x|^{-\alpha}
.

Hardy and other homogeneous operators frequently appear as effective operators or as scaling limits of more complicated operators. In our recent works
[
BM24
,
BM25
,
BJM24
]
and in the present paper, we study Hardy operators by exploiting their spherical symmetry, leading to a direct sum decomposition into summands acting on radial multiples of spherical harmonics with fixed degree. Our main result, Theorem
1.1
below, provides sharp (matching) upper and lower bounds for the heat kernels of these direct summands: In doing so, we also relax the condition
\kappa\leq\kappa_{\rm c}^{(\alpha)}(d)
.

To state the result precisely, consider the
(d-1)

\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

L_{1}=\{0,1\}
,
L_{d}=\mathbb{N}_{0}

, and

\displaystyle{\@index{\@indexphrase{$\kappa_{\rm c}^{(\alpha)}(d_{\ell})$}}}\kappa_{\rm c}^{(\alpha)}(d_{\ell})=\Phi_{d_{\ell}}^{(\alpha)}\left(\frac{d_{\ell}-\alpha}{2}\right)=\frac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}(d_{\ell}+\alpha)\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}(d_{\ell}-\alpha)\right)^{2}},

We now state our main result, the sharp bounds for heat kernel
\exp\left(-t\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{d_{\ell}}^{(\alpha)}(\eta),\ell}\right)
of
\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{d_{\ell}}^{(\alpha)}(\eta),\ell}
, which is defined by functional calculus. These bounds give an approximate factorization of the heat kernel into a product of the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{0,\ell}
times singular weights related to the generalized ground state
r^{-\eta}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell\in L_{d}
,
\alpha\in(0,2]\cap(0,d_{\ell})
, and
\eta\in(-S,(d_{\ell}-\alpha)/2]
.
Then, for
\alpha\in(0,2)
, uniformly in
r,s,t>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\exp\left(-t\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{d_{\ell}}^{(\alpha)}(\eta),\ell}\right)(r,s)\sim_{d,\ell,\alpha,\eta}\left(1\wedge\frac{r}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{-\eta}\left(1\wedge\frac{s}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{-\eta}\cdot\exp\left(-t\mathcal{L}_{0,\ell}\right)(r,s),\\
\end{split}

(1.18)

while for
\alpha=2
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\exp\left(-t\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{d_{\ell}}^{(2)}(\eta),\ell}\right)(r,s)\asymp_{d,\ell,\eta}\left(1\wedge\frac{r}{t^{1/2}}\right)^{-\eta}\left(1\wedge\frac{s}{t^{1/2}}\right)^{-\eta}\cdot\exp\left(-t\mathcal{L}_{0,\ell}\right)(r,s).\\
\end{split}

(1.19)

Furthermore, for
\alpha<2
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\exp\left(-t\mathcal{L}_{0,\ell}\right)(r,s)\sim_{d,\ell,\alpha}\frac{t}{|r-s|^{1+\alpha}(r+s)^{d_{\ell}-1}+t^{\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}}(t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+r+s)^{d_{\ell}-1}}\end{split}

(1.20)

and, for
\alpha=2
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\exp\left(-t\mathcal{L}_{0,\ell}\right)(r,s)\asymp_{d,\ell}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{(r-s)^{2}}{ct}\right)}{(rs+t)^{(d_{\ell}-1)/2}}.\end{split}

(1.21)

Moreover,
\exp\left(-t\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{d_{\ell}}^{(\alpha)}(\eta),\ell}\right)(r,s)
is jointly continuous as a function of
r,s,t>0
.

Let us explain the notation used in Theorem
1.1
.
We write
A\lesssim B
for functions
A,B\geq 0
to indicate that there is a
constant

c\in(0,\infty)
such that
A\leq cB
. If
c
depends on
\tau
, we may write
c_{\tau}
instead of
c
or
A\lesssim_{\tau}\!B
, but the dependence on

,
\ell
, and
\alpha
may also be ignored in the notation. Note that the values of constants may change from place to place. The notation
A\sim_{\tau}B
means
A\lesssim_{\tau}\!B\lesssim_{\tau}\!A
and we then say that
A
is
equivalent
or comparable to
B
.
The notation
\asymp
means the same as
\sim
, but the constants in the
exponential factors
in an estimate are allowed to be different in the upper and the lower bounds; see (
1.19
) and (
1.21

A\wedge B:=\min\{A,B\}
and
A\vee B:=\max\{A,B\}
.

Implications of Theorem
1.1

Heat kernel estimates for Schrödinger operators provide a powerful tool kit to approach a wide range of problems, from pure mathematics, such as partial differential equations or spectral theory, to applied fields such as quantum physics or statistical mechanics.
Our works
[
BM24
,
BM25
,
BJM24
]
, and the present one are mainly motivated by the ambition to establish functional inequalities relating Hardy operators to fractional Laplacians, and to investigate the electron distribution in large, relativistically described atoms. Indeed, one of the mathematically simplest models describing the energy and time evolution of
Z
relativistic electrons interacting with each other and with a static nucleus of electric charge
Z
is given by the many-particle Chandrasekhar (or pseudorelativistic) operator

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{Z,c}&:=\sum_{\nu=1}^{Z}\underbrace{\mathbf{1}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{1}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}}_{\nu-1\text{ times}}\otimes C_{Z,c}\otimes\underbrace{\mathbf{1}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{1}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}}_{Z-\nu\text{ times}}+\sum_{1\leq\nu<\mu\leq Z}\frac{1}{|x_{\nu}-x_{\mu}|}\end{split}

(1.22)

defined in the totally antisymmetric tensor product of
Z
copies of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Here,

\displaystyle C_{Z,c}:=\sqrt{c^{4}-c^{2}\Delta}-c^{2}-\frac{Z}{|x_{\nu}|}\quad\text{in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(1.23)

describes a single electron interacting with the nucleus and
c>0
denotes the speed of light. The operator
C_{Z,c}
has a natural length scale given by
c^{-1}
and is unitarily equivalent to
c^{2}C_{\kappa}
with
C_{\kappa}:=C_{\kappa,1}
and
\kappa=Z/c
. Since
C_{\kappa}
is a bounded perturbation of
\sqrt{-\Delta}-\kappa/|x|
, by the Hardy inequality (
1.1
),
C_{Z,c}
and
H_{Z,c}
are bounded from below if and only if
\kappa\leq\kappa_{\rm c}^{(1)}(3)=2/\pi
. Moreover, according to
[
LSV97
]
, the lowest spectral point of
H_{Z,c}
is an eigenvalue, called the ground state energy. This eigenvalue may be degenerate, but to keep the discussion simple, let us suppose it is simple and let
\psi
denote a corresponding eigenfunction, called the ground state. In
[
FMSS20
]
2

2

See also
[
FMS23b
]
for a shorter proof and
[

]

, we study the probability density of finding one of the electrons at
x
given by

\displaystyle\rho_{Z}(x):=Z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3(Z-1)}}|\psi(x,x_{2},...,x_{Z})|^{2}\,dx_{2}\ldots\,dx_{Z}

(1.24)

and called the ground state density.
We show that
c^{-3}\rho_{Z}(x/c)
converges in the asymptotic limit
Z,c\to\infty
, with fixed
\kappa<2/\pi
, to
\rho^{H}(x):=\sum_{n\geq 1}|\Phi_{n}(x)|^{2}
, where
\Phi_{n}
are the infinitely many eigenfunctions of
C_{\kappa}
. Thus, the electron distribution of a large atom close to the nucleus is well described by the electron distribution of an infinite atom without electron-electron interactions. Moreover, we prove that
\rho^{H}(x)\lesssim|x|^{-2\eta}
, where
\eta
is defined by
\kappa=\Phi_{d_{0}}(\eta)
. We note in passing that the corresponding results for the physically more relevant Coulomb–Dirac operator are proved in
[

A paramount role in proving both the convergence result and the upper bound for

The framework

2.1.
Preliminaries

For

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(\alpha)}[f]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}\nu(x,y)\,dy\,dx,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

associated to the fractional Laplacian

\displaystyle(P^{(\alpha)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)f)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P^{(\alpha)}(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy,\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

2.2.
Angular momentum decomposition

Our goal is to analyze the heat kernel of

3.
Hardy perturbations of subordinated Bessel kernels

4.3.1.
Equality of forms on smaller domains

Lemma 4.5

have

\displaystyle\footnotesize\begin{split}\frac{1}{t}\langle v,p_{1}^{(\alpha,\varepsilon)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)v\rangle&=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,(rsz)^{2\zeta}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,z)q^{\varepsilon}(z)p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t-\tau,z,s)v(s)v(r)\\
&=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,z^{2\zeta}(p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,\cdot,\cdot)v)(z)q^{\varepsilon}(z)(p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t-\tau,\cdot,\cdot)v)(z).\end{split}

(4.34)

Since
\{p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)
, the above integral converges to
\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta}q^{\varepsilon}(z)v(z)^{2}
as
t\to 0
. Thus, we obtain that
\{\tilde{p}_{\zeta}^{(\alpha,\varepsilon)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\}_{t\geq 0}
forms a symmetric strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)
, too. Moreover, each
\tilde{p}_{\zeta}^{(\alpha,\varepsilon)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is contractive because it is smaller than
p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
on non-negative functions.

Next, note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}^{(\varepsilon)}(t)[v]:=\frac{1}{t}\langle v,v-\tilde{p}_{\zeta}^{(\alpha,\varepsilon)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}\to\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}^{(\varepsilon)}[v]:=\sup_{s>0}\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}^{(\varepsilon)}(s)[v]\ \text{as}\ t\to 0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}^{(\varepsilon)}[v]=\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[v]-\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta}q^{\varepsilon}(z)v(z)^{2},\quad v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr).

(4.35)

Since

p_{\zeta,\eta}^{(\alpha)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)v\geq\tilde{p}_{\zeta}^{(\alpha,\varepsilon)}(t,\cdot,\cdot)v

for
0\leq v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)
and all
t>0
,
we have

\mathcal{E}_{\zeta,\eta}(t)[v]\leq\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}^{(\varepsilon)}(t)[v]
for
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)

and all
t>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\zeta,\eta}[v]+\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta}q(z)v(z)^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[v].

(4.36)

Combining this inequality with (
4.26
) shows that for all
0\leq v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\zeta,\eta}[v]+\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta}q(z)v(z)^{2}=\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[v]=\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[v]+\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta}q(z)v(z)^{2}.

(4.37)

For general signed
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta})
, we decompose
v=v_{+}-v_{-}
with
v_{+}=v\mathbf{1}_{f\geq 0}
and
v_{-}=v\mathbf{1}_{f\leq 0}
both belonging to
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta})
. Since
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta,\eta})
are linear spaces, we obtain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta,\eta})
.
The extension of (
4.37
) to
v
with variable sign follows repeating the previous steps using the decomposition of
v
into its positive and negative parts.
∎

4.3.2.
Form cores of subordinated Bessel operators

Recall that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
H^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all real
\alpha
, see, e.g., Bahouri, Chemin, and Danchin
[

, Theorems 1.61]

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
is dense in

for all

and
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
is dense in
\dot{H}^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
\alpha<d
; see, e.g.,
[
BCD11
, Theorem 1.70]
.

The goal of this section is to show, in Lemma
4.10
, that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is a form core for
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}
, i.e., functions in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta})
can be approximated by
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
functions in the norm
\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[\cdot]}+\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}+,r^{2\zeta},dr)}
for all
\zeta\in(-1/2,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge(2\zeta+1))
, and
\eta\in(0,(2\zeta+1-\alpha)/2]
.

In the following, we write
h_{\beta}(r):=r^{-\beta}
for
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
. Recall (
1.15
), i.e.,

h(r)=h_{\eta}(r).

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a symmetric, non-negative bump function with
\supp\chi\subseteq(-1/2,1/2)
, and such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(r)\,dr=1
. For
0<\varepsilon<1
, define
\rho_{\varepsilon}(r):=\varepsilon^{-1}\chi(|r-1|/\varepsilon)
so that
\supp\rho_{\varepsilon}\in(1-\varepsilon/2,1+\varepsilon/2)
. Thus,
\rho_{\varepsilon}(r)
is an approximation of the identity centered at
r=1
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, we define approximations of
f\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
by

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(r):=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)f(r/s),\quad r>0.

(4.38)

Note that
f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Indeed,
the smoothness follows since we can estimate the derivatives of the integrand in

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,r\,s^{-2}\rho_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)f(s).

(4.39)

We now show some elementary properties (bounds and behavior at infinity) of such approximations.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\zeta\in(-1/2,\infty)
,
f\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and
f_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
4.38
).

(1)

If
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
h_{\beta}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}\,dr)
then

\displaystyle\|h_{\beta}f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}\leq\left[(1+\varepsilon/2)^{\zeta+1/2-\beta}\vee(1-\varepsilon/2)^{\zeta+1/2-\beta}\right]\|h_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}.

(4.40)

(2)

If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)
then
\lim_{r\to\infty}f_{\varepsilon}(r)=0
and
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}=0
.

Proof.

(1) The
L^{2}
-bound (
4.40
) follows from Jensen’s inequality since

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|h_{\beta}f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dr\,r^{2\zeta}h_{\beta}(r)^{2}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)f(r/z)\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dr\,r^{2\zeta}h_{\beta}(r)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)|f(r/z)|^{2}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dr\,r^{2\zeta}h_{\beta}(r)^{2}|f(r)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)z^{2\zeta+1-2\beta}\\
&\leq\|h_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}^{2}\left[(1+\varepsilon/2)^{2\zeta+1-2\beta}\vee(1-\varepsilon/2)^{\zeta+1/2-\beta}\right].\end{split}

(4.41)

(2) To show that
f_{\varepsilon}(r)
vanishes as
r\to\infty
, let
p:=2\zeta+2>1
.
Since
\supp(\rho_{\varepsilon})\subseteq[1/2,3/2]
, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the change of variables
z^{\prime}=r/z
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|f_{\varepsilon}(r)|^{2}&=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)\left(\frac{z}{r}\right)^{p/2}\left(\frac{r}{z}\right)^{p/2}\,f(r/z)\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}\left(\frac{z}{r}\right)^{p}\right)\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{dz}{z}\,z\cdot\left(\frac{r}{z}\right)^{p}|f(r/z)|^{2}\right)\\
&\lesssim_{p,\varepsilon}r^{1-p}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,z^{2\zeta}|f(z)|^{2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad r\to\infty.\end{split}

(4.42)

We next prove the
L^{2}
-approximation. For

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,

\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}\leq\|f-\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}+\|\varphi-\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}+\|(\varphi-f)_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}.

Since
r^{2\zeta}dr
is a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)
; see, e.g.,
[
Sch17
, pp. 189–190]
. Thus, by (
4.40
) with
\beta=0
, it suffices to consider
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and then, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dr\,r^{2\zeta}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)[f(r)-f(r/z)]\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dr\,r^{2\zeta}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)|f(r)-f(r/z)|^{2}.\end{split}

The integrand is zero unless
r\in(1-\varepsilon/2,1+\varepsilon/2)\cdot\supp f
and
|z-1|\leq\varepsilon/2
, in which case
|r-r/z|<\varepsilon r
and
|f(r)-f(r/z)|\leq\delta
for any given
\delta>0
, provided
\varepsilon
is small enough.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|f-f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\,|(1-\varepsilon/2,1+\varepsilon/2)\cdot\supp f|^{2\zeta+1}/(2\zeta+1).\end{split}

This concludes the proof.
∎

We next estimate
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[h_{\eta}f_{\varepsilon}]+\|h_{\eta}f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}\,dr)}^{2}
uniformly in
\varepsilon
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\zeta\in(-1/2,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge(2\zeta+1))
,
\eta\in[0,(2\zeta+1-\alpha)/2]
. Then, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
f\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and
f_{\varepsilon}
as in (
4.38
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[hf_{\varepsilon}]+\|hf_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}\,dr)}^{2}\lesssim_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta,\rho}\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[hf]+\|hf\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}\,dr)}^{2}.\end{split}

(4.43)

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
, the bound holds for
\|hf_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}\,dr)}
. To estimate
\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[hf_{\varepsilon}]
, we apply Jensen’s inequality and change variables
r^{\prime}=r/z
,
s^{\prime}=s/z
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&2\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[hf_{\varepsilon}]\\
&\quad=\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,r^{2\zeta}\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,s^{2\zeta}\,\nu_{\zeta}(r,s)\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)\,(f(\tfrac{r}{z})-f(\tfrac{s}{z}))\right|^{2}h(r)h(s)\\
&\quad\leq\int\limits_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{3}}dr\,ds\,dz\,(rs)^{2\zeta-\eta}\,\nu_{\zeta}(r,s)\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)\,|f(\tfrac{r}{z})-f(\tfrac{s}{z})|^{2}\\
&\quad=\int\limits_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}}dr\,ds\,(rs)^{2\zeta-\eta}\nu_{\zeta}(r,s)|f(r)-f(s)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,z^{2(\zeta-\eta)+1-\alpha}\rho_{\varepsilon}(z).\end{split}

(4.44)

Here we used
\nu_{\zeta}(rz,sz)=z^{-2\zeta-1-\alpha}\nu_{\zeta}(r,s)
. Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}dz\,z^{2(\zeta-\eta)+1-\alpha}\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)\sim_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta,\rho}1,\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),

(4.45)

the proof is concluded.
∎

To show that
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is a form core, we will use two bounds in the proof of the following lemma (see (
4.49
)–(
4.50
)), which is interesting in its own right. For
R>0
, we write
B_{R}:=(0,R)
and
B_{R}^{c}:=[R,\infty)
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\zeta\in(-1/2,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge(2\zeta+1))
,
\eta\in[0,(2\zeta+1-\alpha)/2]
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\mathbf{1}_{(1,\infty)}\leq\chi\leq\mathbf{1}_{(1/2,\infty)}
. Then there is
c_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta}>0
such that for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[hf\,\chi(\cdot/\varepsilon)]\leq 2\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[hf]+c_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta}\left[(\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+\|\chi\|_{\infty})\|f\|_{\infty}+\|\chi\|_{\infty}\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\right]^{2}.

(4.46)

Proof.

Denote
f^{(\varepsilon)}(r):=f(r)\chi(r/\varepsilon)
and
A(r,s):=|f^{(\varepsilon)}(r)-f^{(\varepsilon)}(s)|^{2}\nu_{\zeta}(r,s)(rs)^{2\zeta-\eta}
. Since
f^{(\varepsilon)}(r)=f(r)
for
r\in B_{\varepsilon}^{c}
,

\displaystyle\iint_{B_{\varepsilon}^{c}\times B_{\varepsilon}^{c}}A(r,s)\,dr\,ds\leq 2\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[hf].

(4.47)

By the mean value theorem,

\displaystyle|f^{(\varepsilon)}(r)-f^{(\varepsilon)}(s)|\leq|r-s|\left(\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\chi\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon^{-1}\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}\right).

(4.48)

This estimate and scaling yield

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\iint_{B_{2\varepsilon}\times B_{2\varepsilon}}A(r,s)dr\,ds\\
&\quad\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\left(\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\chi\|_{\infty}+\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\iint_{B_{2\varepsilon}\times B_{2\varepsilon}}|r-s|^{2}\nu_{\zeta}(r,s)(rs)^{2\zeta-\eta}\,dr\,ds\\
&\quad=c_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta}\,\varepsilon^{2\zeta-2\eta+1-\alpha}\left(\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\chi\|_{\infty}+\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}\\
&\quad\leq c_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta}\left(\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\chi\|_{\infty}+\|\chi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(4.49)

Here
2\zeta-2\eta+1-\alpha\geq 0
and
c_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}dr\int_{0}^{1}ds\,(rs)^{2\zeta-\eta}(r+s)^{-2\zeta}|r-s|^{1-\alpha}<\infty
by elementary computations using
\eta\leq(2\zeta+1-\alpha)/2
and
\zeta>-1/2
. See also (
4.1
).

Finally, by (
4.1
) and the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\iint_{B_{\varepsilon}\times B_{2\varepsilon}^{c}}A(r,s)\,dr\,ds&\leq 4\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\iint_{B_{\varepsilon}\times B_{2\varepsilon}^{c}}\nu_{\zeta}(r,s)(rs)^{2\zeta-\eta}\,dr\,ds\\
&=c_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta}\,\varepsilon^{2\zeta-2\eta+1-\alpha}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\\
&\leq c_{\zeta,\alpha,\eta}\,\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}.\end{split}

(4.50)

This concludes the proof.
∎

(4.51)

For

We now show that

is dense in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta})
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\zeta\in(-1/2,\infty)
and
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge(2\zeta+1))
. Then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is dense in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta})
with respect to
\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[\cdot]}+\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}

For
\alpha=2
, Lemma
4.10
is proved, e.g., in
[
BDG11
, Theorem 4.22]
or
[
DG21
, Theorem 8.4]
. To ease the notation in the following, define
\mathcal{F}
to be the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with respect to the norm defined by

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}^{(1)}[f]}:=\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[f]}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)},\quad f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}).

(4.52)

Proof of Lemma
4.10
.

The proof proceeds analogously to that of Schilling and Uemura
[
SU12
, Theorem 2.4]
.
Note that Schilling and Uemura consider Dirichlet forms with some translation symmetry
5
5

5

This is encoded in the notion of shift-bounded kernels

.
, which is absent in the present situation.
To show that every element from
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta})
can be approximated by a sequence of functions in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}^{(1)}
, let

. Without loss of generality, let

be real-valued. We perform the following proof for real and imaginary part of

separately, using the linearity of the statement. We define the mollification

As observed after (

has compact support so

. Moreover, again by Lemma

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[w_{\varepsilon}]\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[T_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}[f])]+\|T_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}[f])\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}dr)}^{2}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[T_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}[f])]\leq\mathcal{E}_{\zeta}[\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}[f]]

4.3.3.

\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\zeta\in(-1/2,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(0,2\wedge(2\zeta+1))
,
\eta\in[0,(2\zeta+1-\alpha)/2]

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta})

Proof.

For
\eta=0
, we proved the claim in Lemma
4.10
. Thus, let
\eta>0
.
When

\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\zeta})

\eta\in[0,(2\zeta+1-\alpha)/2]

Observe that
2\zeta-\eta>-1
and
2(\zeta-\eta)>-1

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}_{\eta}[f]&:=\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}[h_{\eta}f],\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{2\zeta}h_{\eta}(r)^{2}\,dr),\\
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Q}_{\eta})&:=\frac{1}{h_{\eta}}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta}):=\{f/h_{\eta}:\,f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta})\}.\end{split}

(4.53)

Thus,
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{I}_{\zeta,\eta})=h_{\eta}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Q}_{\eta})
. Now let
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Q}_{\eta})
. By Lemma
4.7
, the sequence
f_{n}(r):=\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\rho_{1/n}(z)f(r/z)
,

\displaystyle v\mapsto(\mathcal{F}_{d_{\ell}}v)(k):=i^{-\ell}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,\sqrt{kr}J_{\frac{d_{\ell}-2}{2}}(kr)v(r),\quad k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

(A.1)

is initially defined for functions
v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dr)
. Note also that this transform is well-defined because
(d_{\ell}-1)/2>-1/2
. The same arguments used to extend the Fourier transform on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
allow one to extend the Fourier–Bessel transform to a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dr)
. By abuse of notation, we denote this extension by
\mathcal{F}_{d_{\ell}}
, too, and observe the symmetry
\mathcal{F}_{d_{\ell}}=\mathcal{F}_{d_{\ell}}^{*}
, which is apparent from (
A.1
). Using the unitary operator

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{d_{\ell}-1}dr)\ni u\mapsto(Uu)(r)=r^{(d_{\ell}-1)/2}u(r)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dr)

(A.2)

and the plane wave expansion, we obtain the intertwining relation

\displaystyle\left([u]_{\ell,m}\right)^{\wedge}(\xi)=\left(U^{*}[\mathcal{F}_{d_{\ell}}Uu]_{\ell,m}\right)(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{d_{\ell}-1}dr).

(A.3)

The following lemma says that the Fourier–Bessel transform diagonalizes
F(|D|)
, when restricted to a fixed
V_{\ell}
or
V_{\ell,m}
. In the following, we write
F(|D|)^{1/2}
as short for
|F(|D|)|^{1/2}\sgn(F(|D|))
.

Lemma A.1

(
[
BM24
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in L_{d}
,
m\in M_{\ell}
, and
m^{\prime}\in M_{\ell^{\prime}}
. Let
F\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\mathcal{Q}(F(|D|)):=\{g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(|\xi|)||\hat{g}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<\infty\}
. Then, for
[u]_{\ell,m},\,[v]_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{Q}(F(|D|))
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle|F(|D|)|^{1/2}[v]_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}},F(|D|)^{1/2}[u]_{\ell,m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\langle\mathcal{F}_{d_{\ell}}Uv,F\cdot\mathcal{F}_{d_{\ell}}Uu\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dr)}\end{split}

(A.4)

with the unitary operator
U:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},r^{d_{\ell}-1}dr)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dr)
defined in (
A.2
).

To prove Lemma

, we recall the following lemma from

]
.

Lemma B.1

(
[
BJM24
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
\zeta\in(-1/2,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(0,2]
and
\delta\in(0,2\zeta+1)
. Then,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\,\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,s^{2\zeta}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,s)s^{-\delta}\;\sim\;\begin{cases}t(r^{\alpha}\vee t)^{-\delta/\alpha}&\quad\text{for}\ \delta<\alpha\\
\log(1+\frac{t}{r^{\alpha}})&\quad\text{for}\ \delta=\alpha\\
r^{-\delta}(t\wedge r^{\alpha})&\quad\text{for}\ \delta>\alpha\end{cases}.\end{split}

(B.1)

B.1.
Proof of Lemma
4.15

We first prove (
4.69
).
Using

\displaystyle\frac{r+z}{s+z}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\vee z\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{r<s\vee z\}}\sim\frac{r+s+z}{s+z},

(B.2)

we estimate

\displaystyle G_{\eta}(t,r,s)\sim\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta}\cdot z^{-\alpha}\cdot z^{-\eta}\left[\left(\frac{r+z}{s+z}\right)^{2\zeta}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\vee z\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{r<s\vee z\}}\right]p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,z).

(B.3)

We now treat the first summand in the brackets on the right-hand side of (
B.3
). By (
3.9
),

\displaystyle(r+z)^{2\zeta}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,z)\lesssim\frac{\tau}{|r-z|^{1+\alpha}+\tau^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}.

(B.4)

Thus,

\displaystyle\footnotesize\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta-\alpha-\eta}\left(\frac{r+z}{s+z}\right)^{2\zeta}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\vee z\}}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,z)\\
&\hskip 8.50012pt\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta-\alpha-\eta}\cdot\frac{\tau}{|r-z|^{1+\alpha}+\tau^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>z\}}}{(s+z)^{2\zeta}}\\
&\hskip 8.50012pt\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{s/2}dz\,\left(\frac{z}{s}\right)^{2\zeta}z^{-\alpha-\eta}\frac{\tau\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>z\}}}{r^{1+\alpha}+\tau^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}+\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{s/2}^{\infty}dz\,z^{-\alpha-\eta}\frac{\tau\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>z\}}}{|r-z|^{1+\alpha}+\tau^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}.\end{split}

(B.5)

The first summand on the right of (
B.5
) is estimated by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{s/2}dz\,\left(\frac{z}{s}\right)^{2\zeta}z^{-\alpha-\eta}\frac{\tau\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>z\}}}{r^{1+\alpha}+\tau^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(rs)^{\alpha}(r\wedge s)^{\eta}}.\end{split}

(B.6)

We now come to the second summand in (
B.5
) and estimate

\displaystyle\footnotesize\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{s/2}^{r}dz\,z^{-\alpha-\eta}\frac{\tau}{|r-z|^{1+\alpha}+\tau^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}}\lesssim s^{-\alpha-\eta}\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{r}dz\frac{\tau}{\tau^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}+(r-z)^{1+\alpha}}\\
&\hskip 8.50012pt\lesssim s^{-\alpha-\eta}\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,\frac{\tau}{\tau^{(1+\alpha)/\alpha}+z^{1+\alpha}}\lesssim s^{-\alpha-\eta}.\end{split}

(B.7)

Thus, the left-hand side of (
B.5
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta-\alpha-\eta}\left(\frac{r+z}{s+z}\right)^{2\zeta}\mathbf{1}_{\{r>s\vee z\}}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,z)\lesssim(r\wedge s)^{-\eta}(rs)^{-\alpha}+s^{-\alpha-\eta}.

(B.8)

We now come to the second summand in (
B.3
). By Lemma
B.1
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta}\cdot z^{-\alpha-\eta}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r<s\vee z\}}\cdot p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,z)\\
&\quad\lesssim\log(1+1/r^{\alpha})\mathbf{1}_{\eta=0}+(1+r^{\alpha})^{-1-\eta/\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\eta<0}+r^{-(\alpha+\eta)}(1\wedge r^{\alpha})\mathbf{1}_{\eta>0}.\end{split}

(B.9)

This concludes the proof of (
4.69
).

We now prove (
4.70
). Using Lemma
B.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{s}dz\,z^{2\zeta-\alpha}\frac{(1+r+s)^{2\zeta}}{((1-\tau)^{1/\alpha}+z+s)^{2\zeta}}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,z)\lesssim\left(\frac{1+r+s}{1+s}\right)^{2\zeta}\log(1+r^{-\alpha}),\end{split}

(B.10)

as desired. This concludes the proof. ∎

B.2.
Proof of Lemma
4.16

By scaling, it suffices to consider
t=1
. Consider first
\zeta\geq 0
. According to the 3G inequality
[
BM25
, Theorem 3.1]

\displaystyle p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t,r,z)\cdot p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,z,s)

(B.11)

\displaystyle\ \lesssim p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t+\tau,r,s)\cdot\left[\left(\frac{r+s+z}{s+z}\right)^{2\zeta}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t,r,z)+\left(\frac{r+s+z}{r+z}\right)^{2\zeta}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,z,s)\right]

where
r,s,t,\tau>0
and
\zeta\geq 0
, and to the definition of
G_{\eta}(t,r,s)
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&p_{1}^{(1,D)}(r,s)=\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(1-\tau,r,z)q(z)p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,z,s)\\
&\quad\lesssim p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(1,r,s)\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta-\alpha}\\
&\qquad\times\left[\left(\frac{r+s+z}{s+z}\right)^{2\zeta}\cdot p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,r,z)+\left(\frac{r+s+z}{r+z}\right)^{2\zeta}\cdot p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,z,s)\right]\\
&\quad=p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(1,r,s)\left[G_{0}(1,r,s)+G_{0}(1,s,r)\right].\end{split}

(B.12)

Now let
\zeta\in(-1/2,0)
. Here, we use another 3G inequality, contained in
[
BJM24
, Lemma 2.5]
, namely

\displaystyle p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t,r,z)\,p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,z,s)

(B.13)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t+\tau,r,s)\left((t+\tau)^{1/\alpha}+r+s\right)^{2\zeta}\left[\frac{p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(t,r,z)}{(\tau^{1/\alpha}+z+s)^{2\zeta}}+\frac{p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,z,s)}{(t^{1/\alpha}+r+z)^{2\zeta}}\right],

which holds for all
\zeta\in(-1/2,\infty)
.
Thus, using the Chapman–Kolmogorov equations for small
z
and the 3G inequality for large
z
and the definition of
\tilde{G}(t,r,s)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}p_{1}^{(1,D)}(r,s)&\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{r\wedge s}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta-\alpha}p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(1-\tau,r,z)p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,z,s)\\
&\quad+p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(1,r,s)(1+r+s)^{2\zeta}\\
&\qquad\times\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,z^{2\zeta-\alpha}\left[\frac{p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(1-\tau,r,z)}{(\tau^{1/\alpha}+z+s)^{2\zeta}}\mathbf{1}_{z<s}+\frac{p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(\tau,s,z)}{((1-\tau)^{1/\alpha}+z+r)^{2\zeta}}\mathbf{1}_{z<r}\right]\\
&=p_{\zeta}^{(\alpha)}(1,r,s)\left((r\wedge s)^{-\alpha}+\tilde{G}(1,r,s)+\tilde{G}(1,s,r)\right).\end{split}

(B.14)

This concludes the proof. ∎

Safety and Clinical Outcomes of Kanglaite Injection in Combination with Toripalimab and Chemotherapy for Advanced Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: A Real-World Retrospective Analysis
Authors: Riwen Zhang, Zicong Feng, Xiaozhou Pan, Qiyou Lin, Chuanju Huang, Xuejian Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionFor advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) without oncogenic drivers, PD-1 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy represent a standard first-line regimen. However, treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) and suboptimal tumor control remain challenges. Kanglaite injection (KLTI), derived from Coix lacryma-jobi oil, exhibits immunomodulatory and antitumor properties; however, its role as an adjunct to immunochemotherapy in advanced NSCLC remains unclear.MethodsThis retrospective single-arm study analyzed clinical data from 53 patients (aged ≥18 years) with pathologically confirmed, EGFR/ALK wild-type, PD-L1 TPS >1% advanced NSCLC treated with KLTI combined with toripalimab and chemotherapy at Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital Nanhai Hospital (February 2022 to June 2024). Efficacy was evaluated according to RECIST version 1.1. Serum VEGF, CYFRA21-1, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) were measured before and after treatment. TRAEs were graded using CTCAE version 5.0.ResultsAfter four treatment cycles, the objective response rate (ORR) was 58.5% and the disease control rate (DCR) was 98.4%. With a median follow-up of 12 months, the 1-year overall survival (OS) rate was 75.9% and the 1-year progression-free survival (PFS) rate was 63.8%. The most common grade 1–2 TRAEs included elevated liver enzymes (56.6%), anemia (52.8%), and neutropenia (24.5%), while grade ≥3 TRAEs were mainly elevated liver enzymes (11.3%). Post-treatment serum VEGF and CYFRA21-1 levels were significantly reduced (p=0.0057 and p=0.0011, respectively). A lower post-/pre-treatment VEGF ratio was associated with a higher tumor regression rate (r=−0.3511, p=0.010).ConclusionThe combination of KLTI with toripalimab and chemotherapy demonstrated a manageable safety profile and preliminary antitumor activity in patients with advanced NSCLC. Dynamic changes in serum VEGF levels were associated with treatment response; however, these findings should be interpreted with caution due to the retrospective design and absence of a control group. Further well-designed prospective controlled studies are warranted to validate these observations.

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which accounts for approximately 85% of all lung cancer cases, remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide.1 Although early-stage disease may be managed with surgery or radiotherapy, the majority of patients are diagnosed at advanced stages and require systemic treatment.2 Despite advances in therapeutic strategies, recurrence, metastasis, and treatment-related adverse reactions continue to compromise long-term outcomes and quality of life.3 In parallel with these clinical challenges, cancer treatment has evolved from conventional cytotoxic therapies to targeted approaches and, more recently, to immunotherapy. However, therapeutic resistance and treatment-related toxicity remain major obstacles, driving the development of multimodal strategies that aim to improve efficacy while maintaining tolerability.4

In recent years, the combination of immune checkpoint inhibitors with platinum-based chemotherapy has become the standard first-line treatment for advanced NSCLC without actionable genetic alterations. This approach is supported by pivotal trials such as KEYNOTE-189 and KEYNOTE-407, which demonstrated improved survival outcomes with chemoimmunotherapy.5,6 Toripalimab, a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody targeting PD-1, has shown high binding affinity and unique epitope specificity, enabling effective blockade of PD-1/PD-L1 interactions without inducing antibody-dependent cytotoxicity.7 The CHOICE-01 trial further confirmed that toripalimab combined with chemotherapy significantly improves overall survival in patients with advanced NSCLC.8 Despite these advances, combination therapy is frequently associated with increased toxicity, and not all patients achieve durable benefit. Therefore, further optimization of treatment strategies, particularly approaches that can balance efficacy and safety, remains an important clinical need.

In this context, adjunctive therapies have attracted increasing attention. Traditional Chinese medicine has long been used to alleviate treatment-related toxicity and improve treatment tolerance.9,10 Kanglaite injection (KLTI), derived from Coix seed, is a microemulsion with immunomodulatory and antitumor properties.11 Preclinical studies suggest that KLTI may exert antitumor effects through multiple pathways, including PI3K/AKT, protein kinase C, and NF-κB signaling.12,13 In clinical settings, KLTI has been reported to improve response rates and reduce chemotherapy-related toxicity when combined with conventional chemotherapy.14 However, these findings are primarily derived from chemotherapy-based regimens, and evidence regarding the role of KLTI in combination with immunotherapy and chemotherapy remains limited.

Therefore, whether KLTI can provide additional benefit in the context of chemoimmunotherapy for advanced NSCLC remains unclear. This retrospective study was designed to address this evidence gap by evaluating the safety and clinical outcomes of KLTI in combination with toripalimab and chemotherapy. Additionally, tumor-related biomarkers were explored to provide preliminary mechanistic insights.

Methods

Study Design

This retrospective study included data from patients with NSCLC who were treated with KLTI in combination with toripalimab and chemotherapy at Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital Nanhai Hospital between February 2022 and June 2024. A total of 60 consecutive eligible patients were initially identified during the study period. Among them, 7 patients were excluded due to incomplete clinical or follow-up data, resulting in a final analytical cohort of 53 patients.

In routine clinical practice at our institution, toripalimab combined with platinum-based chemotherapy is used as a systemic treatment option for patients with advanced NSCLC without targetable driver mutations. Kanglaite Injection (KLTI), an approved adjunctive antitumor therapy in China (National Medical Products Administration approval number: Z10970091), may be administered at the discretion of the treating physician as supportive treatment to improve patient tolerance to systemic therapy. The KLTI preparation used in this study was produced by a single manufacturer approved for clinical use in China, ensuring consistency in drug quality. The dosage of KLTI (200 mL daily) was determined based on the recommended dosage approved by the National Medical Products Administration. KLTI was administered as an intravenous infusion during hospitalization under the supervision of healthcare professionals.

All the enrolled patients were aged ≥18 years at the time of diagnosis. The primary diagnosis, confirmed by pathology, was NSCLC, including both adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma of the lung. None of the patients had EGFR or ALK mutations, and none had a history of other malignancies. The dataset included clinical characteristics, imaging findings, and hematological test results. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital’s Nanhai Hospital (Approval No. GDNHREC2022214H).

Treatment Regimens

Each patient underwent at least four cycles of KLTI combined with chemotherapy and immune therapy. Treatment regimens were tailored according to histological subtype. Patients received one of the following treatment protocols during each 21-day cycle: Adenocarcinoma: toripalimab (240 mg on Day 1), carboplatin (area under the curve 5 on Day 1), pemetrexed (500 mg/m2 on Day 1), and KLTI (200 mL daily). Squamous cell carcinoma: Toripalimab (240 mg on Day 1), carboplatin (AUC 5 on Day 1), docetaxel (60–75 mg/m2 on Day 1), and KLTI (200 mL daily). After four cycles, all patients underwent imaging evaluations. Patients continued to receive immunotherapy in combination with chemotherapy beyond the initial four cycles, unless disease progression (PD), death, intolerable toxicity, or patient withdrawal occurred. Chemotherapy was administered for up to a maximum of eight cycles. KLTI treatment was generally continued until discontinuation of immunotherapy or the occurrence of unacceptable toxicity.

Assessment Criteria

The treatment efficacy of all patients was evaluated according to the RECIST 1.1 criteria, which classified responses as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD). The levels of two tumor biomarkers—vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and cytokeratin 19 fragment antigen 21–1 (CYFRA21-1)—were determined using a chemiluminescent immunoassay at the hospital’s central laboratory. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were assessed according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from treatment initiation to death from any cause, whereas progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from treatment initiation to disease progression or death from any cause.

Detection of Tumor-Associated Biomarkers

Serum samples were collected from patients in the morning after an overnight fast, both before treatment and after four cycles of treatment. VEGF and CYFRA21-1 levels in the serum were measured using a chemiluminescent immunoassay at the hospital’s central laboratory.

Statistical Analysis

Kaplan–Meier survival curves were used to calculate OS and PFS. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to compare differences in tumor-associated biomarker expression before and after treatment with KLTI combined with chemotherapy and immune therapy. Spearman rank correlation analysis was used to assess the correlation between different tumor-associated biomarkers and the tumor regression rate. A two-sided P value of less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.0.4), and the data were visualized with GraphPad Prism (version 10.0).

Results

Patient Characteristics

We conducted a retrospective analysis of clinical data from patients with NSCLC treated at Nanhai Hospital, Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital, between February 2022 and June 2024. A total of 60 consecutive patients were initially screened, of whom 7 were excluded due to incomplete clinical or follow-up data, leaving 53 patients for the final analysis (Table 1). Among the 53 patients, 43 were male and 10 were female, with a median age of 62 years (range 42–86 years). The pathological diagnoses included 28 cases of lung adenocarcinoma and 25 cases of lung squamous cell carcinoma. To optimize the likelihood of benefit from immunotherapy, all patients had a tumor proportion score (TPS) greater than 1%, with 30 patients (56.6%) exhibiting a TPS greater than 50%. Treatment Efficacy

After four cycles of KLTI combined with chemotherapy and immunotherapy were completed, radiological imaging was performed to assess the tumor response. No patients achieved CR. However, 31 patients (58.5%) achieved PR, yielding an objective response rate (ORR) of 58.5%. SD was observed in 21 patients (39.6%), and 1 patient (1.9%) experienced PD, resulting in a disease control rate (DCR) of 98.4% (Figure 1A). At the time of the last follow-up on May 24, 2025, the median follow-up duration was 12 months. Among the 53 patients enrolled in the study, 17 patients had died by the data cutoff. Adverse Reactions (ARs)

All patients who received KLTI combined with toripalimab and chemotherapy experienced varying degrees of adverse reactions (Table 2). The most common adverse reactions of grades 1–2 were anemia (n=28, 52.8%), abnormal liver function (n=30, 56.6%), and neutropenia (n=13, 24.5%). The most frequent adverse reactions of grade 3 or higher were abnormal liver function (n=6, 11.3%). Notably, two patients required a change in treatment because of persistent grade 3 or higher abnormal liver function despite symptomatic treatment. Additionally, two patients experienced grade 3 or higher adverse respiratory reactions, with one patient succumbing to severe pneumonia. Treatment-Related Adverse Effects

Analysis of Tumor-Associated Biomarkers

To investigate the correlation between tumor-associated biomarkers and the efficacy of immunotherapy in patients with NSCLC, this study compared serum levels of VEGF and CYFRA21-1 and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) before and after treatment in 53 patients who received KLTI combined with toripalimab plus chemotherapy. Additionally, the relationships between the changes in serum VEGF, CYFRA21-1, and NLR and the tumor regression rate were analyzed. The results indicated that after four cycles of treatment with KLTI plus toripalimab and chemotherapy, the serum levels of VEGF (326.03 ± 249.88; p = 0.0057) and CYFRA21-1 (3.73 ± 2.16; p = 0.0011) significantly decreased compared with those before treatment (VEGF: 351.70 ± 261.44; CYFRA21-1: 5.28 ± 3.34), whereas the peripheral blood NLR did not significantly change (Figure 2A–C). Notably, the change in serum VEGF levels was significantly correlated with the tumor regression rate, whereas the changes in CYFRA21-1 levels and the NLR were not significantly correlated (Figure 2D–F). Specifically, a lower posttreatment-to-pretreatment ratio of serum VEGF was strongly associated with a higher tumor regression rate (r = −0.3511; p = 0.010). A waterfall plot was further provided to illustrate the relationship between dynamic changes in VEGF levels and individual treatment responses (Supplementary Figure S1). Discussion

This retrospective study investigated the impact of KLTI as an adjunct to immunotherapy and chemotherapy in patients with NSCLC. Our findings suggest that the combination of KLTI with toripalimab demonstrated a manageable safety profile and preliminary signals of antitumor activity.

Current clinical guidelines recommend the combination of immunotherapy with platinum-based chemotherapy as the first-line treatment for advanced NSCLC without actionable genetic alterations.5,6 Chemotherapy may further enhance the activity of immunotherapy through multiple mechanisms, including the induction of immunogenic cell death, upregulation of tumor antigen expression, and modulation of the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME).15 Consistent with this rationale, several pivotal trials have established chemoimmunotherapy as a standard treatment strategy. The CHOICE-01 trial reported an objective response rate (ORR) of 65.7% and a 1-year overall survival (OS) rate of 74.0% with toripalimab plus chemotherapy,8 while the KEYNOTE-189 trial demonstrated a 1-year OS rate of 69.8% and a progression-free survival (PFS) rate of 39.8% with pembrolizumab-based therapy.5 Although these regimens have significantly improved outcomes, treatment optimization in terms of both efficacy and tolerability remains an area of ongoing interest.16 Against this background, there remains interest in identifying adjunctive agents that may help optimize treatment balance. Kanglaite injection (KLTI), derived from coix seed, is a broad-spectrum antitumor agent widely used in NSCLC and hepatocellular carcinoma. Unlike conventional single-target drugs, KLTI exerts multitarget and systemic regulatory effects, consistent with the holistic principles of traditional Chinese medicine. Preclinical studies suggest that KLTI may modulate the tumor microenvironment through multistep regulatory pathways,17,18 and clinical evidence has indicated potential benefits when combined with chemotherapy.14

In this context, we evaluated the combination of KLTI with toripalimab plus chemotherapy in a real-world cohort. The regimen demonstrated a generally manageable safety profile, with most adverse events being tolerable. Although this retrospective single-arm study was not designed to establish efficacy, preliminary antitumor activity was observed. After four cycles of treatment, the ORR was 58.5%, the DCR was 98.4%, the 1-year OS rate was 75.9%, and the 1-year PFS rate was 63.8%. These outcomes were broadly comparable to those reported in the CHOICE-01 trial. However, the single-arm nature of this study limits the strength of these observations, and the potential contribution of KLTI to efficacy remains to be clarified.

Treatment-related toxicity remains a key concern in chemoimmunotherapy for advanced NSCLC. While immune checkpoint inhibitors such as toripalimab have demonstrated acceptable safety as monotherapy,19 combination regimens with chemotherapy are associated with substantially increased toxicity. Previous studies have suggested that KLTI may be associated with reduced chemotherapy-related adverse events. A meta-analysis of 27 randomized controlled trials reported a significant reduction in severe toxicities when KLTI was added to chemotherapy (RR = 0.41, 95% CI 0.33–0.51; p < 0.00001).14 In our study, while all patients experienced some degree of adverse events, the incidence of grade 3 or higher adverse events was 28%. The most common severe adverse events included abnormal liver function (11.3%), anemia (9.4%), and neutropenia (9.4%), while lymphocytopenia, vomiting, and respiratory adverse reactions were each observed in 3.8% of patients, and erythema in 1.9%. One patient unfortunately died from severe pneumonia after the fourth cycle of treatment. Overall, the KLTI-containing regimen appears feasible and generally tolerable in this real-world cohort, and its potential role in reducing treatment-related toxicity warrants further validation.

Tumor-related biomarkers remain a core focus in cancer immunotherapy research, both as tools for monitoring tumor status and for predicting therapeutic efficacy. In this study, we examined the dynamic changes in serum levels of VEGF, CYFRA21-1, and the NLR in 53 patients with advanced NSCLC receiving KLTI, toripalimab, and chemotherapy. After four cycles of treatment, significant reductions in serum VEGF (351.70±261.44 vs 326.03±249.88, p=0.0057) and CYFRA21-1 (5.28±3.34 vs 3.73±2.16, p=0.0011) levels were observed, whereas no significant change in NLR was noted. VEGF, a critical mediator of tumor angiogenesis and immune suppression, can promote immune evasion by inhibiting T-cell infiltration. Previous studies have shown that high posttreatment VEGF-B levels independently predict poor OS in patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors.20 The decrease in the expression level of VEGF in serum may indicate that the combined regimen in this study improved the tumor microenvironment. CYFRA21-1, a well-established biomarker for NSCLC, is associated with tumor cell proliferation and burden. Its reduction further supports the notion that this treatment regimen can suppress tumor activity.

The prognostic and predictive roles of serum VEGF levels in NSCLC and other cancers have been explored in various studies. Researchers such as Kentaro et al21 and Eleni et al22 have suggested that serum VEGF levels can predict the efficacy of immunotherapy. However, the prognostic value of serum VEGF in chemotherapy remains controversial. Some studies have reported no correlation between circulating VEGF levels and chemotherapy response in NSCLC patients. Lissoni’s team23 reported that higher pretreatment serum VEGF levels were associated with poorer chemotherapy response in NSCLC and colorectal cancer patients. The role of serum VEGF in the prognosis of patients with advanced NSCLC treated with immune–chemotherapy combinations remains unclear. In our study, no significant correlation was observed between pretreatment or posttreatment serum VEGF levels and treatment efficacy. However, a reduction in serum VEGF levels during treatment was associated with improved therapeutic outcomes in this cohort. These findings suggest a potential association between dynamic VEGF changes and treatment response, but should be interpreted cautiously given the retrospective design. Further prospective studies are warranted to validate whether VEGF dynamics may have predictive value in this setting.

Despite the encouraging results, this study has several limitations. First, while the short-term efficacy of the treatment regimen was promising, particularly with respect to adverse event rates, the adjunctive role of KLTI in combination with toripalimab and chemotherapy still requires validation through future large-scale, prospective randomized controlled trials. The relatively short follow-up duration of this study precludes the evaluation of long-term therapeutic outcomes. Second, although our findings suggest a potential association between dynamic changes in serum VEGF levels and the efficacy of chemo-immunotherapy in advanced NSCLC, the limited sample size of the current study does not allow a robust inference regarding their independent association. In addition, further detailed analyses of VEGF subtypes are warranted to better clarify their potential biological and clinical significance.

Conclusion

The combination of KLTI with toripalimab and chemotherapy demonstrated a manageable safety profile along with preliminary antitumor activity in patients with advanced NSCLC. A reduction in serum VEGF levels was observed and appeared to correlate with treatment response. Nevertheless, this observation should be interpreted cautiously and warrants further confirmation in well-designed prospective controlled studies.

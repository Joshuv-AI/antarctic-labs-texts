Abstract
. We prove the original Galois refinement of the McKay conjecture, proposed by Isaacs–Navarro in [ IN02 ] , providing an important subcase of the celebrated McKay–Navarro conjecture with several local-global consequences. Key words and phrases: McKay–Navarro conjecture, Galois action, characters, Local-global conjectures, Groups of Lie type



Dedicated to Gabriel Navarro for the occasion of his 60th birthday.

1.
Introduction

For over a half-century, the study of representations of finite groups has been heavily influenced by the McKay conjecture (now a theorem), which says that a bijection exists between the set
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
of irreducible characters of a finite group
G
with degree relatively prime to a prime
\ell
and the corresponding set
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(D))
for the normalizer of a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
D
of
G
. In a culmination of much work on the topic, the proof of the McKay conjecture was recently completed by Cabanes and Späth in
[
CS25
]
.

During the same time, the fields of values
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi):={\mathbb{Q}}(\{\chi(g)\mid g\in G\})
for
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
have been seen to be valuable number-theoretic features and have been shown to relate to the structure of
G
in numerous ways. For example, understanding these fields of values has strong implications for questions of Brauer, such as his Problem 12 asking what information can be obtained about a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
D
of
G
from the character table of
G
. In particular, some of the results presented here give answers to this question.

The field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
lies in
{\mathbb{Q}}(e^{2\pi i/|G|})\subset{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}
, and the Galois group
\mathcal{G}:=\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
acts naturally on
\operatorname{Irr}(G)
.
In the last quarter-century, there has been much study of versions of the McKay conjecture that consider the role of these fields of values and the action of
\mathcal{G}
, positing stronger bijections predicting relationships between fields of values of the
\ell^{\prime}
-degree characters in a McKay bijection. The focus of the present paper is the original of these Galois refinements of the McKay conjecture, found in
[
IN02
, Conj. C]
, which we will call the
Isaacs–Navarro Galois Conjecture
.
Given a fixed prime
\ell
, we let
\mathcal{H}_{0}\leq\mathcal{G}
denote the subgroup consisting of all Galois automorphisms
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
that act trivially on
\ell^{\prime}
-roots of unity
and have
\ell
-power order. Our main result is the following, proving the Isaacs–Navarro Galois Conjecture:

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Suppose that
\ell
is a prime dividing
|G|
, and let
D\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
. Then there exists an
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-equivariant bijection
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)\to\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(D))
.

The Isaacs–Navarro Galois Conjecture has a number of interesting consequences, many of which had not yet been proven for odd primes but are now implied by our Theorem
A
.
For example, as noted already in
[
IN02
]
, the Isaacs–Navarro Galois conjecture implies a conjecture on exponents of abelianizations of Sylow subgroups, which was the focus of
[
NT19
]
.
As a corollary to our main result, we obtain that statement. Given an integer
e\geq 1
, let
\sigma_{e}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
be the element sending
\ell
-power order roots of unity
\xi
to
\xi^{1+\ell^{e}}
.

Corollary B
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
\ell
a prime, and let
D\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
. Then the following are equivalent:

•

\mathrm{Exp}(D/D^{\prime})\leq\ell^{e}
;

•

all characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
are fixed by
\sigma_{e}
; and

•

all characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))
are fixed by
\sigma_{e}
, where
B_{0}(G)
denotes the principal
\ell
-block of
G
.

(In particular,
[
NT19
, Conj. A]
holds for all finite groups and all primes.)

While the equivalence of the first two items will follow from Theorem
A
, we note that in
[
NT19
]
, it was proved that the third item of Corollary
B
implies the first. That the first item implies the second was reduced to a problem on simple groups in
[
NT19
]
, which was used by Malle in
[
Ma19
]
to complete the proof of the statement of Corollary
B
for
\ell=2
. (Note that it also now follows in that case from the main result of
[
RSF25
]
.) In contrast, despite strong further work on the problem and related problems (see, e.g.
[
NT21
,
Hu24
]
), the case of
\ell
odd was more elusive, remaining open until now.

As a consequence of Theorem
A
, we also obtain the main conjecture of N. Hung from
[
Hu24
]
. For a character
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
, we write
\mathrm{lev}(\chi)
for the
\ell
-rationality level of
\chi
, as defined in
[
Hu24
]
. This number is closely tied to the behavior of
\sigma_{e}
, and gives a measure of how far a character is from being
\ell
-rational. Recall here that
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
is called
\ell
-rational if the conductor
c(\chi)
is not divisible by
\ell
, where the conductor of
\chi
is the smallest integer
c:=c(\chi)
such that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}(e^{2\pi i/c})
. The
\ell
-rationality level
\mathrm{lev}(\chi)
is then the largest
e\geq 0
such that
\ell^{e}
divides
c(\chi)
. That is,
\mathrm{lev}(\chi)=\log_{\ell}(c(\chi)_{\ell})
. As noted in
[
Hu24
, Sec. 2]
, if
\chi
is not
\ell
-rational (that is,
\mathrm{lev}(\chi)>0
), then
\mathrm{lev}(\chi)
is the smallest
e
such that
\chi
is
\sigma_{e}
-stable; further,
the Isaacs–Navarro Galois conjecture (hence, our Theorem
A
) implies that the number of characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
with a given
\ell
-rationality level larger than
1
is the same as the corresponding number in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(D))
for
D\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
. The following was conjectured in
[
Hu24
]
.

Corollary C
.

Let
\ell
be a prime and
G
a finite group.
Let
e\geq 2
be an integer. Then the following hold:

•

If there is some
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
with
\mathrm{lev}(\chi)=e
, then there exist characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
with every
\ell
-rationality level between
2
and
e
.

•

Let
M\leq G
with
\ell\nmid[G:M]
. Then
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
contains a character
\chi
with
\mathrm{lev}(\chi)=e
if and only if
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(M)
does.

(That is,
[
Hu24
, Conjs. 1.1 and 2.3]
hold for all finite groups and all primes.)

We remark that the second point of Corollary
C
follows from the first and Corollary
B
. It is also worth noting that, again, the case of
\ell=2
in Corollary
C
was more approachable, proved already in
[
Hu24
]
(and later also following independently from
[
RSF25
]
), while the case that
\ell
is odd remained a challenge.

In
[
Na04
]
, Navarro extended the Isaacs–Navarro Galois Conjecture to use a larger group
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
containing
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. (Turull
[
Tur08
]
later related this to the
\ell
-adic numbers and suggested a version relating Schur indices.) Namely,
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\leq\mathcal{G}
is the subgroup comprised of those
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
satisfying that there is some
e\geq 0
such that
\sigma
acts on
\ell^{\prime}
-roots of unity by
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{\ell^{e}}.
Navarro’s conjecture requiring an
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
-equivariant bijection, in place of our
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-equivariant bijection, is what is generally known as the Galois–McKay or McKay–Navarro conjecture
[
Na04
]
. The latter was reduced to a problem on simple groups by Navarro–Späth–Vallejo in
[
NSV20
]
and was recently proved by the current authors in the case
\ell=2
in
[
RSF25
]
using this reduction. Our proof of Theorem
A
(and hence Corollaries
B
and
C
) will also use this reduction, together with recent work of the authors, Späth, and Taylor in
[
RSST25
]
and building on the previous work toward the McKay–Navarro conjecture and, of course, the proof of the McKay conjecture.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly discuss the inductive conditions from
[
NSV20
]
, which we will use to complete the proof of Theorem
A
. In Section
3
, we make some observations regarding extensions that will be useful throughout. In Section
4
, we reduce our problem to the case of groups of Lie type in non-defining characteristic, and in Section
5
, we reduce further to criteria specific to those groups using the work of
[
RSST25
]
. Section
6
is devoted to those criteria, and there we finish the case of groups of Lie type
\operatorname{A}
as well as the case that a group of Lie type is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
where the order of
q
modulo
\ell
is a so-called regular number. The final proof of Theorem
A
appears in Section
7
, using an induction argument to complete the proof for non-regular numbers. Finally, in Section
8
, we make some final remarks on implications of Theorem
A
, in particular completing the discussion of Corollaries
B
and
C
.

2.
The inductive Galois–McKay condition

The McKay–Navarro conjecture was reduced in
[
NSV20
, Thm. A]
to a problem on (quasi-) simple groups, and we note that the reduction also works if we replace the group
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
with any of its subgroups. In particular, taking the subgroup
\mathcal{H}_{0}
as defined in the introduction, we have the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

The Isaacs–Navarro Galois conjecture holds if every finite non-abelian simple group satisfies the inductive Galois–McKay condition
[
NSV20
, Def. 3.1]
with respect to the subgroup
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We will say that
the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition
holds for a simple group
S
and the prime
\ell
if the inductive Galois–McKay condition
[
NSV20
, Def. 3.1]
holds for
S
with respect to the subgroup
\mathcal{H}_{0}\leq\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
. If the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition holds for
S
for all primes
\ell
, we simply say that the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition holds for
S
.

It is worth remarking that the inductive condition for a simple group
S
is actually a condition on its universal covering group.
Thanks to the work in
[
RSST25
]
, we will mostly be able to deal with a refined set of conditions (see Theorem
5.1
below). For this reason, we do not explicitly redefine the inductive Galois–McKay condition here, but instead refer the interested reader to
[
NSV20
, Def. 3.1]
.

The following notation and definitions will play an important role throughout.

Definition 2.3
.

For groups
X\leq Y
, we will use the usual notation
\operatorname{Res}_{X}^{Y}(\chi)
to denote the restriction to
X
of a character
\chi
of
Y
.
For
G\lhd A
and subsets
\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(G)
and
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\operatorname{Irr}(A)
, we write
\operatorname{Irr}(A\mid\mathfrak{G})
for the irreducible characters of
A
whose irreducible constituents on restriction to
G
lie in
\mathfrak{G}
and
\operatorname{Irr}(G\mid\mathfrak{A})
for the irreducible constituents of characters in
\mathfrak{A}
on restriction to
G
. Further, an
extension map
with respect to
G\lhd A
for
\mathfrak{G}
is a map
\Lambda:\mathfrak{G}\longrightarrow\bigcup_{G\leq I\leq A}\operatorname{Irr}(I)
such that for every
\chi\in\mathfrak{G}
,
\Lambda(\chi)
is an extension of
\chi
to the stabilizer
A_{\chi}
of
\chi
in
A
.

Now suppose that
B
is a group acting on
\operatorname{Irr}(A_{\chi})
for each
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. Given an extension map
\Lambda
with respect to
G\lhd A
, we say that
\Lambda
is
B
-equivariant
if
\Lambda(\chi^{\sigma})=\Lambda(\chi)^{\sigma}
for every
\sigma\in B
and
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
.

If
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
extends to a character
\widetilde{\chi}
of
A_{\chi}
and
\chi
is invariant under
\alpha\in B
, then there exists a unique linear character
\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}(A_{\chi}/G)
such that
\widetilde{\chi}^{\alpha}=\widetilde{\chi}\mu
,
by Gallagher’s theorem
[
Isa76
, Cor. 6.17]
. In this situation, we will write
[\widetilde{\chi},\alpha]:=\mu
for this character.

In constructing extension maps, the following definitions will also be used throughout:

Definition 2.4
.

If
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(Y)
is an irreducible character of a finite group
Y,
we denote by
\det(\chi)
its determinantal character. Moreover,
o(\chi)
denotes the order of the determinantal character, see the remarks before
[
Isa76
, Lemma 6.24]
.

3.
General Observations on Character Extensions

We often use the following lemma about gluing extensions:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X_{1}\lhd Y
and
X_{2}\leq Y
such that
Y=X_{1}X_{2}
. Assume that
\vartheta_{1}\in\operatorname{Irr}(X_{1})
is
Y
-invariant and
\vartheta_{2}\in\operatorname{Irr}(X_{2})
is such that
\operatorname{Res}_{X_{1}\cap X_{2}}^{X_{2}}(\vartheta_{2})
is irreducible and coincides with
\operatorname{Res}_{X_{1}\cap X_{2}}^{X_{1}}(\vartheta_{1})
. Then there exists a unique character
\vartheta\in\operatorname{Irr}(Y)
which extends both
\vartheta_{1}
and
\vartheta_{2}
.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
Sp10b
, Lem. 4.1]
. (This is also a case of a more general statement by Isaacs—see
[
Na18
, Lem. 6.8]
.)
∎

This allows us to simplify many of the inductive conditions. For example, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X\lhd Y\lhd\hat{Y}
with
X\lhd\hat{Y}

such that
Y/X
has a normal Sylow
\ell
-subgroup and
\hat{Y}/Y
is a (possibly trivial)
\ell
-group. Assume that
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(X)
extends to
Y_{\psi}
. Suppose further that at least one of the following holds:

(i)

\ell\nmid o(\psi)
or

(ii)

there exists
\hat{K}\leq\hat{Y}
such that
\hat{Y}=X\hat{K}
and
K:=\hat{K}\cap X
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-group.

Then there exists an extension
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(Y_{\psi})
of
\psi
such that the stabilizers
(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\chi}=(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}
are the same.

Proof.

Let
X\leq P\leq Y
such that
P/X\lhd Y_{\psi}/X
is the normal Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
Y_{\psi}/X
, and let
\lambda:=\det(\psi)
.

We first claim that
\psi
has a
\hat{Y}_{\psi}
-invariant extension
\chi_{P}
to
P
such that
(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\chi_{P}}=(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}
. If
{\lambda}
has order prime to
\ell
, then according to
[
Isa76
, Lem. 8.16]
, there exists a unique extension
\chi_{P}
of
\psi
to
P
such that

o(\chi_{P})=o(\psi)
.
In particular,
(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\chi_{P}}=(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}
, and
\chi_{P}
is
\hat{Y}_{\psi}
-invariant, giving the claim in this case.

Now suppose that assumption (ii) is satisfied. Note that
{\lambda}_{0}:=\operatorname{Res}^{X}_{K_{\psi}}({\lambda})
has an extension to
(\hat{K}\cap Y)_{\psi}
, since the character
\psi
extends to
Y_{\psi}=X(\hat{K}\cap Y)_{\psi}
and
{\lambda}=\det(\psi)
. As
K_{\psi}
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-group and
{\lambda}_{0}
is a linear character, we can find an extension of
{\lambda}_{0}
to
(\hat{K}\cap Y)_{\psi}
such that all elements of
(\hat{K}\cap Y)_{\psi}
of
\ell
-power order are in the kernel of the extension. By Gallagher’s lemma
[
Isa76
, Cor. 6.17]
, the number of extensions with this property is prime to
\ell
. On the other hand, note that both
(\hat{K}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}/\hat{K}_{\psi}
and
\hat{K}_{\psi}/(\hat{K}\cap Y)_{\psi}
are
\ell
-groups. Then by coprimality, there exists such an extension
\hat{{\lambda}}_{0}
to
(\hat{K}\cap Y)_{\psi}
which is
(\hat{K}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}
-stable. By applying Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the unique character
\hat{{\lambda}}
of
Y_{\psi}=X(\hat{K}\cap Y)_{\psi}
that extends both
{\lambda}
and
\hat{{\lambda}}_{0}
, which satisfies
(\hat{K}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}\leq(\hat{K}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\hat{{\lambda}}}
.
In particular, the restriction
\operatorname{Res}_{P}^{Y_{\psi}}(\hat{{\lambda}})
of this extension to
P
is
(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}
-stable (as
\hat{Y}=X\hat{K}
).
Then by
[
Isa76
, Lem. 6.24]
, there is a unique extension
\chi_{P}
of
\psi
to
P
such that
\det(\chi_{P})=\operatorname{Res}_{P}^{Y_{\psi}}(\hat{{\lambda}})
. This forces again that
(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\chi_{P}}=(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}
and
\chi_{P}
is
\hat{Y}_{\psi}
-invariant, completing the claim.

Now, by assumption, the character
\psi
has an extension
\chi_{0}
to
Y_{\psi}
. Let
\chi_{0}^{\prime}
be its restriction to
P
.
By Gallagher’s lemma
[
Isa76
, Cor. 6.17]
, there exists a unique linear character
\nu\in\operatorname{Irr}(P/X)
such that
\chi_{P}=\chi_{0}^{\prime}\nu
. Now note that
P/X=XK/X\cong K/(X\cap K)
is an
\ell
-group. As both
\chi_{0}^{\prime}
and
\chi_{P}
are
Y_{\psi}
-invariant, the uniqueness of
\nu
implies that
\nu
is also
Y_{\psi}
-invariant.
As
o(\nu)
is an
\ell
-power and
Y_{\psi}/P
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-group, we can now apply
[
Isa76
, Cor. 6.27]
to find a unique extension
\hat{\nu}
of
\nu
to
Y_{\psi}
such that
o(\hat{\nu})=o(\nu)
. It thus follows that
\chi_{0}\hat{\nu}
is an extension of
\chi_{0}^{\prime}\nu=\chi_{P}
to
Y_{\psi}
.
But also by Gallagher’s lemma, the number of extensions of
\chi_{P}
to
Y_{\psi}
is again an
\ell^{\prime}
-number. Hence, by coprimality and using the fact that
(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}/Y_{\psi}
is an
\ell
-group (as both
(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}/\hat{Y}_{\psi}
and
\hat{Y}_{\psi}/Y_{\psi}
are
\ell
-groups), there exists a
(\hat{Y}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\psi}
-stable extension as desired.
∎

The second assumption in Lemma
3.2
implies a nice splitting property of the Sylow
\ell
-subgroup:

Remark 3.3
.

Let
X\lhd Y
and suppose that there exists
K\leq Y
such that
KX/X\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(Y/X)
and
K\cap X
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-group. Then for
\hat{D}\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(Y)
there exists
E_{0}\leq K
such that
\hat{D}=D\rtimes E_{0}
with
D:=\hat{D}\cap X
.

Proof.

Since all Sylow
\ell
-subgroups of
Y
are conjugate, it suffices to prove the claim for a fixed Sylow
\ell
-subgroup.
Let
E_{0}\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(K)
. Then there is
\hat{D}\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(Y)
with
\hat{D}\cap K=E_{0}
. Denote
D:=\hat{D}\cap X\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(X)
. Since
E_{0}\cap D\leq E_{0}\cap X\leq K\cap X
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-group, it follows that
E_{0}\cap D=1
and
\hat{D}=D\rtimes E_{0}
.
∎

We draw a first consequence from Lemma
3.2
, which will allow us to conclude the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition for simple groups which are not of Lie type in the next section.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
S
be a non-abelian simple group such that
\mathrm{Out}(S)
is cyclic, and let
G
be the universal
\ell^{\prime}
-covering group of
S
. If there exists an
(\mathcal{H}_{0}\times\mathrm{Aut}(G)_{D})
-equivariant bijection
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)\to\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(D))
with
D\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
, then
S
satisfies the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition for
\ell
.

Proof.

First, note that it suffices to prove the inductive conditions for the
\ell^{\prime}
-cover, by
[
Joh22b
, Lem. 5.1]
.
Let
a\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
induce the full cyclic group of outer automorphisms on
G
. By conjugacy of Sylow
\ell
-subgroups, we find some
g\in G
such that
ga
normalizes
D
, so by replacing
a
by
ga
we can assume that
a
normalizes
D
. Then consider the group
A:=G\rtimes\langle a\rangle
.

Hence, the assumptions of Lemma
3.2
are satisfied for
X\in\{G,\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)\}
and
Y=\hat{Y}=X\rtimes\langle a\rangle
.
In particular, every character
\chi
of
G
or
\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)
has an extension
\hat{\chi}
to its inertia group in
A
resp.
\operatorname{N}_{A}(D)
with
(A\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\chi}=(A\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\hat{\chi}}
, resp.
(\operatorname{N}_{A}(D)\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\chi}=(\operatorname{N}_{A}(D)\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\hat{\chi}}
, using Lemma
3.2
. By
[
Joh22a
, Lem. 4.6]
, these extensions can further be chosen to have trivial associated scalars on
\operatorname{C}_{A}(G)
. (Note that the result in loc. cit. is stated for the full
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
, but follows exactly the same way when restricting to
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.) Together with the assumption of the equivariant bijection, this gives the
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-triple condition
[
NSV20
, Def. 1.5]
, and hence the inductive condition from
[
NSV20
, Def. 3.1]
with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
∎

4.
Groups not of Lie type and groups of Lie type in defining characteristic

We next record several previous results regarding the inductive McKay–Navarro condition.

Lemma 4.1
.

The inductive McKay–Navarro condition holds for the simple group
S
and prime
\ell
when
(S,\ell)
is as in any of the following situations:

(1)

S
is a simple group of Lie type defined in characteristic
p=\ell
;

(2)

S

(3)

(4)

Proof.

We next prove the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition in the case of alternating groups, which were proved to satisfy the McKay–Navarro conjecture in
[

]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition holds for the simple alternating groups
A_{n}
and the sporadic simple groups.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.1
, we may assume
\ell
is odd. Note that when
S\neq A_{6}
, we have
|\mathrm{Out}(S)|\leq 2
, and
\mathrm{Out}(A_{6})\cong C_{2}^{2}
. Let
G
be the universal
\ell^{\prime}
-covering group of
S
.

First, as pointed out in
[
HSF25
, Thm. 5.2]
, we have every
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
has
\ell
-rationality level
\mathrm{lev}(\chi)\leq 1
, and hence these are fixed by
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
Note that
P/[P,P]
is elementary abelian for
P\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
(see, e.g.,
[
NT16
, Lem. 3.3, 3.4]
), so that
\Phi(P)=[P,P]
.
In particular, we see
\operatorname{Irr}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P)/\Phi(P))=\operatorname{Irr}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P)/[P,P])=\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P))
. Then applying
[
MMV25
, Lem. 5.3]
, we then have
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P))=\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime},\mathcal{H}_{0}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P))
. That is, every
\ell^{\prime}
-degree irreducible character of
\operatorname{N}_{G}(P)
is also
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-invariant. Since these groups also satisfy the inductive McKay conditions (see
[
Ma08
, Thm. 3.1]
), it follows that there is an
(\mathcal{H}_{0}\times\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{P})
-equivariant bijection
\Omega\colon\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P))
. By Corollary
3.4
, this means the Isaacs–Navarro condition holds for
S
if
S\neq A_{6}
.

Now let
S=A_{6}
. Then we may assume that
\ell=5
by
[
Joh22a
, Prop. 5.13]
.
For characters
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
with
|\operatorname{Out}(S)_{\chi}|\leq 2
, we may apply the considerations in Corollary
3.4
, so we assume that
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)
is stable under
\operatorname{Out}(S)
. By
[
Joh22a
, Lem. 4.4]
,
\chi
extends to an
(\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{P}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\chi}
-invariant character
\theta
of
X:=G\rtimes\mathrm{Inn}(G)_{P}
, and similar for
\Omega(\chi)
. Applying Lemma
3.2
and recalling that
\operatorname{Aut}(G)/\mathrm{Inn}(G)
is a
2
-group, it then suffices to know that these extensions extend further to
Y:=(G\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{P})_{\chi}
and
(\operatorname{N}_{G}(P)\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{P})_{\chi}
, respectively. In this situation,

is Klein-four, and we may apply Lemma
3.1
to see the statement.
∎

6.1.
The extension condition

In this subsection, we consider part (

In the case where

Lemma 6.1

Let
\hat{N}:=\operatorname{N}_{GE(\mathbf{G}^{F})}(\mathbf{L})

There exists a subgroup

Lemma 6.6
.

(\mathcal{H}_{0}\times E)
-stable

-transversal

By

\tilde{G}
-transversal

of
\operatorname{Irr}(G)

\mathcal{X}

and

\mathcal{X}
is
\mathcal{H}_{0}E
-stable and for

\widetilde{G}
-orbits. Further, if

Now, assume that

-orbit of a character

in
\mathbb{T}_{0}\cap\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(G)

\chi
is also
\sigma
-stable. If

is

\mathcal{H}_{0}
is an
\ell
-group, we can further assume that
\sigma
has
\ell
-power order. (Write

where
\sigma_{\ell}
is the
\ell

-part of
\sigma
. Then there is some

. Hence,
\sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}\in E
and still stabilizes the
\tilde{G}
-orbit of
\chi
. Thus,
\sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}
stabilizes
\chi
.) Let

.

Assume first that
\mathbf{G}
is of type
\operatorname{A}
. Then observe that any element of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
acts trivially on
\ell^{\prime}
-roots of unity; so in particular on

3.2

Then we now assume that
\mathbf{G}
is not of type
\operatorname{A}
. Assume first that
\ell\neq 3

\operatorname{E}_{6}(\pm q)

Next suppose that

\operatorname{N}_{GE}(M)=M\hat{E}
and
\hat{E}\cap M\leq\hat{E}\cap N
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-subgroup.
∎

6.3.
Extension maps

With the previous sections, we are finally left to consider part (
1
) of Theorem

.
We begin by recalling the following statement.

Lemma 6.8
.

There exists an
\hat{N}
-equivariant extension map
L\lhd N
for an
\tilde{L}
-transversal
\mathbb{T}_{0}
of
\operatorname{Irr}(L)
.

Proof.

For exceptional groups, this is contained in
[
Sp09
, Lems. 8.1, 8.2, Prop. 9.2]
. For
\mathbf{G}
of type
\operatorname{A}
, this is
[
CS17a
, Prop. 5.9]
. For type
\operatorname{C}
, it is within the proof of
[
CS17b
, Thm. 6.1]
, and for type
\operatorname{B}
it is in
[

, Prop. 5.19]
.
Finally, for type

[

.
∎

Lemma 6.9
.

Assume that
\mathbf{L}\neq\mathbf{G}
. Then
\widetilde{L}/L\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{L})
has order dividing
2
.

Proof.

Note that
\tilde{L}/L\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{L})
is a quotient of
\tilde{G}/G\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G})
, so it remains to consider the case that
\mathbf{G}
is of type
\operatorname{A}
,
\operatorname{D}
, or
\operatorname{E}_{6}
. Applying
[
GM20
, Rem. 1.7.6]
to
\mathbf{L}
, we have
\widetilde{L}/L\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{L})\cong(\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})/\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{L}))_{F}
, the group of
F
-coinvariants. We proceed by analyzing each case.

In type
\operatorname{A}
, we have
\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})
is connected, as noted in the proof of
[
CS17a
, Prop. 5.6]
. If
\mathbf{G}=\operatorname{E}_{6}
, then either
d
is regular, hence
\mathbf{L}=\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})
is a torus, or
d=5
if
F
is untwisted (resp.
d=10
if
F
is twisted) and
\mathbf{L}
is of type
\operatorname{A}_{1}
. In the latter case, as
\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})/\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{L})
is both a quotient of
\operatorname{Z}([\mathbf{L},\mathbf{L}])\cong C_{2}
and
\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{G})/\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{G})\cong C_{3}
it follows that
\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})
is again connected.

Finally, if
\mathbf{G}
is of type
\operatorname{D}
, we embed
\mathbf{G}\leq\overline{\mathbf{G}}
into a group
\overline{\mathbf{G}}
of simply connected type
\operatorname{B}
, as in
[
CS25
, Sec. 2.E]
, and we keep the notation there. Let
h_{0}=h_{e_{1}}(-1)=h_{e_{n}}(-1)
be as in
[
CS25
, 2.24]
, so that
\operatorname{Z}(\overline{\mathbf{G}})
is generated by

. Here
\mathbf{L}
is
\mathbf{G}
-conjugate to a Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}_{I}
of type
\operatorname{D}_{m}
for some

, and
\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{L}_{I})=\langle h_{e_{i}}(t)\mid i>m,t\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}\rangle
. Hence, it follows that
h_{0}\in\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{L}_{I})
, forcing
(\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})/\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{L}))_{F}
to be of size at most
2
.
∎

From Lemmas
6.8
and
6.9
we obtain the following regarding the set
\mathfrak{L}
defined in Notation
5.1
.

Lemma 6.10
.

There exists an
(\mathcal{H}_{0}\times\hat{N})
-stable
\tilde{L}
-transversal
\mathbb{T}_{1}
of
\mathfrak{L}

such that every character
{\lambda}\in\mathbb{T}_{1}
extends to its inertia group in
\hat{N}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.8
, there exists an
\hat{N}
-equivariant extension map
\Lambda
with respect to
L\lhd N
for an
\tilde{L}
-transversal
\mathbb{T}_{0}
of
\operatorname{Irr}(L)
. Let
{\lambda}\in\mathbb{T}_{0}\cap\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(L)
be such that
\ell\nmid|W:W({\lambda})|
. Set
\psi:=\operatorname{Ind}_{N_{\lambda}}^{N}(\Lambda({\lambda}))
. Then
\hat{N}_{\psi}=N\hat{N}_{\lambda}
and so
(\tilde{N}\hat{N})_{\psi}=\tilde{N}_{\psi}\hat{N}_{\psi}
. By Lemma
6.7
there exists an
\tilde{N}
-transversal of
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(N)
such that every character in this transversal extends to its inertia group in
\hat{N}
.

In particular, there exists an
\tilde{L}
-conjugate
{\lambda}^{\prime}:={\lambda}^{\tilde{l}}
of
{\lambda}
such that the character
\psi^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Ind}_{N_{\lambda}}^{N}(\Lambda({\lambda}))^{\tilde{l}}
extends to a character
\hat{\psi}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}})
and
(\tilde{N}\hat{N})_{\psi^{\prime}}=\tilde{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}
. The claim of the lemma therefore follows when
\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{L})
is connected as in this case we always have
{\lambda}^{\prime}={\lambda}
.

Note that we may also assume
\mathbf{L}\neq\mathbf{G}
, as otherwise the claim is part of Lemma
6.6
. We can therefore also assume that
G\not\cong\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)
as in this case either
\mathbf{L}
is a torus or
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{G}
. Now note that
\hat{N}/N
acts on
\tilde{L}/L\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{L})
and by Lemma
6.9
, the latter is either trivial or of order
2
. This implies
[\hat{N},\tilde{L}]\subset L\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{L})
. By the properties of
\mathbb{T}_{0}
, this gives
(\tilde{L}\hat{N})_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\tilde{L}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}
, since
{\lambda}^{\prime}
is
\widetilde{L}
-conjugate to
{\lambda}\in\mathbb{T}_{0}
.

Observe that for any
\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}\mid\Lambda({\lambda})^{\tilde{l}})
, there is some
e\geq 1
such that
\operatorname{Res}^{\hat{N}_{\lambda^{\prime}}}_{N_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})=e\Lambda({\lambda})^{\tilde{l}}
. (Indeed, recall from above that
(\tilde{N}\hat{N})_{\psi}=\tilde{N}_{\psi}\hat{N}_{\psi}
and
(\tilde{N}\hat{N})_{\psi^{\prime}}=\tilde{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}
. Since
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
are
\widetilde{L}
-conjugate, it follows that
\hat{N}_{\psi}=\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}
. Now, by Clifford correspondence,
\Lambda({\lambda})^{\tilde{l}}
is
\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}
-stable if and only if
\psi^{\prime}
is
\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}
-stable. However,
\hat{N}_{{\psi}^{\prime}}=\hat{N}_{\psi}=N\hat{N}_{\lambda}=N\hat{N}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, so that indeed
\psi^{\prime}
, hence
\Lambda({\lambda})^{\tilde{l}}
, is
\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}
-stable.)

Since
\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}=N\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}
, we have
\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}
is the stabilizer in
\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}
of
\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}
, and it follows that
\hat{\psi}_{0}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Ind}_{\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}}^{\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}}(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})
is an irreducible character of
\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}
using Clifford correspondence. In particular,
\operatorname{Res}_{N}^{\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}}(\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{0})=\operatorname{Ind}_{N_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}}^{N}(e\Lambda({\lambda})^{\tilde{l}})=e\psi^{\prime}
by Mackey’s formula. As
\hat{N}/N
is abelian (recall that we can assume that
G\not\cong\operatorname{D}_{4}(q)
) and
\psi^{\prime}
extends to
\hat{N}_{\psi^{\prime}}
it follows that
e=1
. In particular,
\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}
is an extension of
\Lambda({\lambda})^{\tilde{l}}
. Then we obtain an
\widetilde{L}
-transversal
\mathbb{T}_{1}
of
\mathfrak{L}
such that every character in
\mathbb{T}_{1}
extends to its inertia group in
\hat{N}
. As
(\tilde{L}\hat{N})_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\tilde{L}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}\hat{N}_{{\lambda}^{\prime}}
the so-obtained transversal is
\hat{N}
-stable.
(Indeed, note that the set of characters satisfying the latter condition is
\hat{N}
-stable, and that no two distinct
\widetilde{L}
-conjugates with this property lie in the same
\hat{N}
-orbit.)

Finally, note that the transversal
\mathbb{T}_{1}
is automatically
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-stable, since
\tilde{L}/L\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{L})
is a
2
-group by Lemma
6.9
, and
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is an
\ell
-group for an odd prime
\ell
whose action commutes with that of
\widetilde{L}/L\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{L})
(see the proof of Lemma
6.6
).
∎

6.4.
The regular case

Our next goal is to complete the proof of the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition in the case that
d
is a regular number for
\mathbf{G}
, in which case
L
is abelian. We begin with the following, for which we work with
\operatorname{Lin}(L)
, without necessarily assuming that
L
is abelian.

Lemma 6.11
.

There exists an
(\hat{N}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})
-equivariant extension map for the set

\operatorname{Lin}(L)\cap\mathfrak{L}
with respect to
L\lhd\hat{N}
.

Proof.

Recall from Lemma
6.1
that there exists a subgroup
\hat{V}\leq\hat{N}
such that
\hat{N}=L\hat{V}
and
H:=\hat{V}\cap L
is an abelian
2
-group.

Let
{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(L)
be a linear character satisfying
\ell\nmid[W:W({\lambda})]
and let
\theta
be its (irreducible) restriction to the
2
-group
H
. It suffices to find an extension
\Lambda({\lambda})
of
{\lambda}
to
\hat{N}_{\lambda}
such that
(\hat{N}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\Lambda({\lambda})}=(\hat{N}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{{\lambda}}
. (Indeed, then we obtain a well-defined
(\hat{N}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})
-equivariant extension map
\Lambda^{\prime}
by defining
\Lambda^{\prime}({\lambda}):=\Lambda({\lambda})
for
{\lambda}
in some
(\hat{N}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})
-transversal, and
\Lambda^{\prime}({\lambda}^{\sigma}):=\Lambda({\lambda})^{\sigma}
for any
\sigma\in\hat{N}\times\mathcal{H}_{0}
.)

By Lemma
6.10
, the character
{\lambda}
extends to a character
\hat{\lambda}
of
\hat{N}_{\lambda}
.
We denote by
\hat{\theta}
the restriction of
\hat{\lambda}
to
\hat{V}_{\lambda}
. In particular, we can consider
\hat{\theta}
as a character of
\hat{V}_{\lambda}/[\hat{V}_{\lambda},\hat{V}_{\lambda}]
, which extends the character
\theta
of
H/([\hat{V}_{\lambda},\hat{V}_{\lambda}]\cap H)
. As
H
is a
2
-group, we can also take an extension of
\theta
to
\hat{V}_{\lambda}/[\hat{V}_{\lambda},\hat{V}_{\lambda}]
that is trivial on the Sylow
r
-subgroups of
\hat{V}_{\lambda}/[\hat{V}_{\lambda},\hat{V}_{\lambda}]
for primes
r\neq 2
. Note that such an extension is
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-invariant, as
\mathcal{H}_{0}
acts trivially on
\ell^{\prime}
-roots of unity, hence on
2
-power roots of unity since
\ell
is odd. Let
\mathfrak{X}
denote the set of all such extensions of
\theta
(that is,
\mathfrak{X}
is the set of all extensions of
\theta
to
\hat{V}_{\lambda}/[\hat{V}_{\lambda},\hat{V}_{\lambda}]
that are trivial on the Sylow

-subgroups for

). By Gallagher’s theorem, the size of

is a power of
2
. Note also that
(\hat{V}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\lambda}/\hat{V}_{{\lambda}}
acts on
\mathfrak{X}
. Arguing as in the second paragraph of Lemma
6.6
, we see that

is an
\ell
-group, and therefore there is some such extension

that is
(\hat{V}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\lambda}
-invariant.

Hence, the unique extension
\Lambda({\lambda})\in\operatorname{Irr}(\hat{N}_{\lambda}\mid{\lambda})
which restricts to
\hat{\theta}^{\prime}
on
\hat{V}_{\lambda}
(see Lemma
3.1
) is again
(\hat{N}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})_{\lambda}
-stable.
∎

We now obtain the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition in the case that

Proposition 6.12
.

Assume that
d=d_{\ell}(q)
is regular for the group

and that
\mathbf{G}^{F}
is quasisimple. Then the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition holds for
\mathbf{G}^{F}/\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{G}^{F})

.

We check that all conditions in Theorem

are satisfied. By Lemma
6.11
and Lemma
5.2
(see also Remark

), condition (

) holds.
Then conditions (

) and (

) of Theorem

, Lemma

∎

In this section, we complete the proof of our main results. We continue to keep the situation of Notation

7.1.
An extension map in the non-regular case

We now move to the case that

is non-regular. That is,

is not a torus. We make this assumption throughout this subsection. Note that with this assumption, we have

and

In this case, the group

as in
[
RSST25
, Thm. 3.4, Cor. 3.5]
, in place of

.
We will write

for the set of

-stable characters in

, and
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(L)^{D}
for the intersection

Remark 7.1
.

Note that if
{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(L)
is
D
-stable then the same is true for all of its
\tilde{L}
-conjugates, since
[\tilde{L},D]\subset L
.

Recall from Lemma
6.10
that there exists an
\tilde{L}
-transversal
\mathbb{T}_{1}
of

\mathfrak{L}

such that every
{\lambda}
in
\mathbb{T}_{1}
has an extension to
\hat{N}_{\lambda}
. Further, note that
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(L)^{D}\subseteq\mathfrak{L}
since
D\leq N_{\lambda}
for any
D
-invariant

By Remark
7.1
, the intersection

\mathbb{T}_{D}:=\mathbb{T}_{1}\cap\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(L)^{D}

is therefore an
\tilde{L}
-transversal of
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(L)^{D}
. Since
\mathbb{T}_{1}
is
(\mathcal{H}_{0}\times\hat{N})
-stable, we see
\mathbb{T}_{D}
is further
(\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(D)\times\mathcal{H}_{0})
-stable, where we recall
\hat{G}:=GE
.

Note also that as
\ell>3
it follows that
E
has a normal
\ell
-complement
E_{\ell^{\prime}}\lhd E
, and we will consider the group
L\operatorname{N}_{GE_{\ell^{\prime}}}(D)\lhd L\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(D)
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Keep the situation above. Then there exists an
(\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(D)\times\mathcal{H}_{0})
-equivariant extension map
\hat{\Lambda}
with respect to
L\lhd L\operatorname{N}_{GE_{\ell^{\prime}}}(D)
for the

-stable
\tilde{L}
-transversal
\mathbb{T}_{D}
of
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(L)^{D}
.

Note that it again suffices to show that each
{\lambda}\in\mathbb{T}_{D}
has an extension to

with the same stabilizer in
\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(D)\times\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
Recall that, writing
\hat{M}:=L\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(D)
and

, we have

. Then we have

(ii) are satisfied for

.

There exists an
(\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(D)\times\mathcal{H}_{0})

7.2.
The inductive conditions in the non-regular case

The proof of Theorem
A
below will use induction to deal with the case that
d
is not regular. The following will allow us to achieve the inductive step.

Theorem 7.4
.

Keep the situation of Notation
5.1
and assume that
d
is not regular for the group
(\mathbf{G},F)
and that
\mathbf{G}
is not of type
\operatorname{A}
.
Suppose that the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition holds for the simple groups involved in all groups whose order is smaller than the order of
G/\operatorname{Z}(G)
.
Let
D\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
.
Then there exists an
(\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G}/G)\rtimes(\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{G})_{D}\times\mathcal{H}_{0}))
-equivariant bijection
\tilde{\Omega}:\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{G})\to\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{L}\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D))
.

Proof.

Recall that we have a regular embedding yielding
G\lhd\widetilde{G}
.
Note that we may assume that
\ell\neq 3
, since otherwise
d\in\{1,2\}
is regular. In particular,
\ell
does not divide
|\tilde{G}:G\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G})|
so that
\tilde{D}:=D\operatorname{Z}(\tilde{G})_{\ell}
is a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
\tilde{G}
. Let
\tilde{{\lambda}}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{L})^{D}
.
As established in

[
RSST25
, Sec. 4.B]
, the characters of the relative Weyl group
W_{\tilde{\lambda}}:=\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\tilde{\mathbf{L}},\tilde{\lambda})/\tilde{L}
are all
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
-invariant.
By our assumption and applying
[
NSV20
, Thm. A]
, the Isaacs–Navarro Galois conjecture holds
for the group
W_{\tilde{{\lambda}}}
. This in particular implies that we have an
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-equivariant bijection
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(W_{\tilde{{\lambda}}})\to\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\tilde{D})_{\tilde{{\lambda}}}\tilde{L}/\tilde{L})
. (Note that the image
\tilde{D}\tilde{L}/\tilde{L}
is a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\tilde{\mathbf{L}})/\tilde{L}
and that
\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\tilde{D})\tilde{L}/\tilde{L}
is its normalizer in
\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\tilde{\mathbf{L}})/\tilde{L}
.)
From this, we see that every character in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(\tilde{D})_{\tilde{{\lambda}}}\tilde{L}/\tilde{L})
is
\mathcal{H}_{0}
-invariant.

On the other hand, the inductive McKay condition (which holds for all finite groups thanks to
[
CS25
, Thm. B]
) yields a collection
f_{\tilde{{\lambda}}}:\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\tilde{\mathbf{L}},\tilde{{\lambda}})/\tilde{L})\to\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\tilde{D})_{\tilde{{\lambda}}}\tilde{L}/\tilde{L})
of
(\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\mathbf{L}))_{\tilde{D}}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})
-equivariant bijections.
(For this, first fix an
(\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\mathbf{L}))_{D}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})
-transversal of
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{L})^{D}
. Then for every character
\tilde{\lambda}
in this transversal we get by the inductive McKay condition an
\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{N}_{G}(\mathbf{L},\tilde{{\lambda}}))_{D}
-equivariant map. Then extend the definition of
f_{\tilde{\lambda}}
in an
(\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(\mathbf{L}))_{D}\times\mathcal{H}_{0})
-equivariant way, noting that
\mathcal{H}_{0}
acts trivially on both sides of
f_{\tilde{\lambda}}
).

Let
\Lambda
be the
(\mathcal{H}_{0}\times\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(D))
-equivariant extension map with respect to
L\lhd L\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)
for the
\widetilde{L}
-transversal
\mathbb{T}_{D}\subset\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(L)^{D}
from Corollary
7.3
. By
[
CS25
, Prop. 2.3]
, the map
\Lambda
yields an
(\operatorname{Irr}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)/\operatorname{N}_{G}(D))\rtimes(\operatorname{N}_{\hat{G}}(D)\times\mathcal{H}_{0}))
-equivariant extension map
\tilde{{\Lambda}}
for
\tilde{L}\lhd\tilde{L}\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)
for the set
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{L})^{D}
.

By Gallagher’s theorem, for
\tilde{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{L})^{D}
, there is a bijection

\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)_{\tilde{\lambda}}\tilde{L}/\tilde{L})\to\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)\tilde{L}\mid{\tilde{\lambda}})

given by

\eta\mapsto\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)_{\tilde{\lambda}}\widetilde{L}}^{\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)\widetilde{L}}(\tilde{\Lambda}({\tilde{\lambda}})\eta).

Let
\tilde{t}\in\tilde{L}^{\ast}
be such that
\tilde{\lambda}\in\mathcal{E}(\tilde{L},\tilde{t})
and denote by
\widetilde{N}_{0}
the stabilizer in
\widetilde{N}
of
\mathcal{E}(\tilde{L},\tilde{t})
.
Then the characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{G})\cap\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G},\tilde{t})
are in bijection with pairs
(\psi,\widetilde{\eta})
, where
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\widetilde{L})\cap\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{t})
, up to
\widetilde{N}_{0}
-conjugation, and
\widetilde{\eta}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\widetilde{N}_{\psi}/\widetilde{L})
. (See
[
Ma07
, Prop. 7.3]
and its adaption to
\widetilde{G}
in
[
CS13
, Sec. 4]
. Note that
\psi
in turn corresponds to some unipotent character in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{C}_{\widetilde{L}^{\ast}}(\widetilde{t}))
.) Let
\chi_{\tilde{t},\tilde{\eta}}^{\psi}
denote the character in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{G})\cap\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G},\tilde{t})
corresponding to
(\psi,\widetilde{\eta})
.

On the other hand, we note that any member of
\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{\widetilde{G}}(D)\widetilde{L})
must lie above a character
\widetilde{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\widetilde{L})^{D}
, applying Clifford correspondence. This shows that we have a bijection

\widetilde{\Omega}\colon\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{G})\to\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)\tilde{L})

given by

\chi_{\tilde{t},\tilde{\eta}}^{\psi}\mapsto\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)_{\psi}\widetilde{L}}^{\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)\widetilde{L}}(\tilde{\Lambda}(\psi)f_{{\psi}}(\widetilde{\eta})).

By the equivariance properties of Jordan decomposition in the connected center case (see
[
CS13
, Thm. 3.1]
for the case of group automorphisms and the main result of
[
SV20
]
for
\mathcal{G}
, hence
\mathcal{H}_{0}
) and the equivariance properties of
\tilde{\Lambda}
and
f_{\tilde{\lambda}}
, it follows that this bijection is
(\operatorname{Irr}(\tilde{G}/G)\rtimes(\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{G})_{D}\times\mathcal{H}_{0}))
-equivariant.
∎

We now complete the proof of our main results.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

By Theorem
2.1
, it suffices to show that the inductive Galois–McKay condition holds with respect to
\mathcal{H}_{0}
for each finite non-abelian simple group. Let
S
be a nonabelian simple group with universal covering group
G
. By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
and Corollary
6.5
, we may assume that
\ell
is odd,
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
with
(\mathbf{G},F)
a finite reductive group of simply connected type defined over
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
p\neq\ell
,
\operatorname{Z}(G)
is a nonexceptional Schur multiplier for
S
,
\mathbf{G}
is not of type
\operatorname{A}
, and that
\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)\leq N
, where
N
is as in Notation
5.1
and
D\in\mathrm{Syl}_{\ell}(G)
. By Proposition
6.12
, we may assume that
d:=d_{\ell}(q)
is not regular for
(\mathbf{G},F)
. As before, let
G\lhd\widetilde{G}
, obtained by a regular embedding, and note that
\ell\nmid[\widetilde{G}:G]
by our assumption on
d
.

We proceed by induction. Namely, suppose that the inductive Isaacs–Navarro condition holds for the simple groups involved in all groups whose order is smaller than the order of
G/\operatorname{Z}(G)
.
Then by Theorem
7.4
, we have an
(\operatorname{Irr}(\widetilde{G}/G)\rtimes(\operatorname{Aut}(\tilde{G})_{D}\times\mathcal{H}_{0}))
-equivariant bijection
\tilde{\Omega}:\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{G})\to\operatorname{Irr}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tilde{L}\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D))
.
Let
\widetilde{M}:=\tilde{L}\operatorname{N}_{\tilde{G}}(D)
and
M:=L\operatorname{N}_{G}(D)
. Note then that
\operatorname{N}_{\widetilde{G}}(M)=\widetilde{M}
, and we can apply
[
RSST25
, Thm. 3.4, Cor. 3.5]
with this choice of
M
, noting that the required transversal conditions hold thanks to Lemmas
6.7
and
6.6
.
∎

Instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after occipito-cervical fixation in patients with rheumatoid arthritis: the challenge of spine surgery
Authors: Marek Rybarczyk, Kamil Koszela, Małgorzata Mańczak, Robert Gasik
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic, autoimmune inflammatory disease mainly affecting the joints and periarticular soft tissues. C1-C2 instability is particularly observed, which causes a serious neurological risk. In such cases, surgical treatment, occipitocervical fixation (OCF), is considered. However, complications are inevitable. In some patients, we observe instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF. The aim of this article was to evaluate stability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF in patients with RA. 33 patients with RA were retrospectively included. Instability occurred in 19 patients in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF. Postoperatively, the C2-C7 Sagittal Vertical Axis (C2-C7 SVA) distance was significantly lower in patients with instability than in patients without instability: 10 mm (IQR:7–21) vs. 25 mm (IQR: 10–31); p = 0.042. In logistic regression analysis, we found that an increase in the postoperative C2-C7 SVA by 1 mm is associated with an 8–11% decrease in the odds of instability. An increasing incidence of instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF was observed in patients with RA. Furthermore, a relationship between C2-C7 SVA after OCF was also found in patients with RA. However, further research is needed in this area.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic, autoimmune inflammatory disease mainly affecting the joints and periarticular soft tissues [1]. Despite the progress of understanding the pathophysiology of RA, the etiology is still not completely known. The natural history of RA provides joint deformity and significant disability. The use of targeted drugs inducing early admission have improved the RA prognosis [2].

Pathological changes in RA are often seen in the cervical spine. C1-C2 instability is particularly observed, which causes a serious neurological risk [3]. Although, the management of RA has been improved, resulting in substantially higher levels of disease remission and better long term outcomes, some patients still require operation of cervical spine f.e occipitocervical fusion (OCF) [4].

The OCF decreases range of cervical spine movements and causes developing changes in sub-fusion joints in long term outcomes [5]. When undergoing spinal surgery, patients with RA have a greater risk of postoperative complications and infection [6]. One of the observed complications is loosening of the implants and the development of distal-to-stabilization segments instability (Fig. 1) [7–9].

Thorough assessment of spine pathology, implant position and treatment effects, needs the sagittal spine balance parameters to be used. The radiological evaluation of cervical balance parameters such as cervical lordosis angle C2-C7 and saggital vertical axis C2-C7 SVA are easy to measure and so they are often evaluate [10, 11].

The aim of this article was to evaluate instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after occipito-cervical fixation in patients with rheumatoid arthritis.

Materials and methods

Study design

RA was confirmed in patients by a rheumatologist according to the 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria [12]. During the analysis of X-ray examinations (AP - anterior posterior, lateral and functional projections), an orthopedic specialist diagnosed C1-C2 instability [13]. Due to the risk of serious neurological complications associated with C1-C2 instability, patients were qualified for OCF. The patients were treated in the Department of Neuroortopedics MCR STOCER, Konstancin Jeziorna (Poland), in the period of 1999–2018. Each patient consented to diagnosis and treatment, including surgery. The study was approved by the Bioethics Committee at the National Institute of Geriatrics, Rheumatology and Rehabilitation in Warsaw, Poland (Date of approval: April 24, 2019; No: KBT-3/2/2019).

Study protocol

All patients underwent OCF. The correct position of the implants was confirmed intraoperatively. The patient was discharged from the ward in good general condition [14]. Each patient underwent follow-up visits at the orthopedic clinic after OCF surgery. During the visit, a cervical spine X-ray in the standing position (AP and lateral views) was performed. Implant position, stability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF, and sagittal balance parameters such as the C2-C7 sagittal vertical axis (SVA) were assessed [15]. In doubtful situations, imaging diagnostics was extended to include CT and/or MRI [16].

Instability assessment

Instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF was determined on X-ray (resting or flexion-extension) as lateral or anterior/posterior displacement of a vertebra by more than 4 mm or rotational difference of more than 11 degrees from the adjacent vertebra [3, 17].

C2-C7 SVA assessment

The C2-C7 SVA is determined by measuring the horizontal distance between a plumb line from the centroid of C2 (the second cervical vertebra) and the posterosuperior corner of the C7 vertebral body on a lateral X-ray (Fig. 2) [15].

Data analysis

Statistical analysis was made using Statistica v.13 (Oklahoma, USA). Distribution of continuous variables was verified using Shapiro-Wilk test. The distribution of all continuous variables differs significantly from normal distribution. Continuous data are presented as median and interquartile range, nominal data as numbers and percentages. Comparisons between groups were made using the Mann-Whitney U test and the chi-square test with Yates’ correction. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed. Due to the small sample size, the multivariable models included only two parameters.

Results

Data from 33 women with RA were included in the analysis. The median age was: 60 (IQR: 53–68). The median disease duration was 18 years (IQR:15–24). All patients were taking steroids chronically, median duration of steroid use was 19 years (IQR:15–24). Three (9%) had renal failure, sixteen (48.5%) had hypertension, nineteen (57.6%) patients had osteoporosis and patients with diabetes were excluded from the study.

Instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF was found in 19 patients (58%). During the first follow-up visit, after 3 months, instability was confirmed in 6 patients, after 6 months in 11 patients, and after 12 months in 2 patients. In 12 patients, instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF was observed after stabilization of C0-C4 and in 7 patients C0-C5.

There were no differences between the postoperative instability and non-instability groups in terms of age, disease duration, duration of steroid use and incidence of osteoporosis, as well as in preoperative C2-C7 SVA values. Only the postoperative C2-C7 SVA value was significantly lower in the instability group compared to the non-instability group. Data are presented in Table 1; Fig. 3.

Discussion

Patients with RA and cervical spine pathologies are a group of patients who require specialized care, especially if they qualify for spine surgery. Due to the difficulties associated with the treatment of patients with RA, continued publications on this topic are necessary.

This article is a continuation of research recently published by Rybarczyk M. et al. [18], which demonstrated no change in the cervical lordosis angle after OCF in patients with RA. It can be result of surgical procedure carefully performing.

In this study, instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF was found in 58% of patients. The literature still lacks large, contemporary, multicenter reports exclusively on patients with RA in assessing instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF. This instability may result from the transfer of biomechanical loads to these adjacent segments, which can lead to adjacent segment disease (ASD). Increased mechanical loading may accelerate degenerative changes in the segments directly beneath the union [19]. This situation can cause neurological symptoms, pain, and potentially require revision surgery. Importantly, the OCF procedure creates a rigid fusion at the occipitocervical junction, which increases the load on the segments below the fusion [20, 21].

When looking for the cause of instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF, an association was found with reduced C2-C7 SVA. In our study, the postoperative C2-C7 SVA value averaged 25 mm in patients without instability. According to Gu S. et al. [22], the ideal postoperative C2-C7 SVA value should be in the range of 15–25 mm, which is similar to that of the asymptomatic population. In patients with instability, the mean C2-C7 SVA was 10 mm.

Analyzing the available literature, it can be concluded that contraction of the spinal extensors may play a significant role in the development of excessive stress in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF. Brasil A.V.B. et al. [23] presented a vectorial distribution of forces acting on the cervical spine. In his publication he explains that contraction of the extensors generates a force vector in the extension of the cervical spine.

Moreover, another factor increasing the risk of instability is the pathological changes in the spinal joints that often occur in RA [24]. It is also worth mentioning the relationship between selected pelvic parameters and the development of instability in patients with rheumatoid arthritis [25].

As mentioned above, surgical treatment of the cervical spine in RA is associated with the occurrence of specific complications, therefore preoperative planning is necessary, taking into account the parameters of sagittal balance.

This retrospective article is a significant limitation. Another important limitation is the small number of patients consisting only of women and the lack of a comparison or control group. Additionally, patients with diabetes were excluded from the study, which could reduce the external validity of the findings. Moreover, the lack of other parameters of the sagittal balance of the cervical spine may limit a full analysis of the discussed problem. However, the exclusion of these patients was due to the fact that diabetes may have a significant negative impact on the postoperative healing process and implant stability. Further studies are needed, particularly randomized, comparative studies on larger patient groups with a longer follow-up period that consider other sagittal balance and radiological parameters of the cervical spine.

Conclusion

An increasing incidence of instability in the distal-to-stabilization segments after OCF was observed in patients with RA. Furthermore, a relationship between C2-C7 SVA after OCF was also found in patients with RA. However, further research is needed on this topic.

Introduction

This work studies the dynamics between tumor cells and immune cells influenced by the presence of a chemical signal released by the latter. The interplay between these units has been broadly studied as a chemotaxis model
[
2
,
16
]
. Chemotaxis, as a model where units (microorganisms as bacteria or organisms as animals
[
20
]
) move in response to chemical gradients, was first modeled by Keller and Segel considering mainly two dynamics. A first one is the so-called
production model

[
9
]
, where a population of cells
u=u(x,t)
(e.g., amoebae in that first study),
x\in\Omega
a bounded region of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, releases a chemical substance
v=v(x,t)
(in that case the acrasin), related to the simplified mathematical model:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}})\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-v+u,&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}})\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0,&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.1)

with
\chi
the
chemotactic coefficient
measuring the strength of chemotaxis, i.e., the term
-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)
represents the movement of the cells towards the chemical signal. The vector
\nu
is the outward unit normal on
\partial\Omega
. The value
T_{\text{max}}\in(0,\infty]
indicates the maximum instant of time up to which solutions are defined. All the models discussed within this work, consider homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions and therefore, from now on, that condition (e.g., the third line in (
1.1
)) will be omitted.

The second is the
consumption model

[
10
]
, reading in a simplified form:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}})\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-uv,&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}})\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.2)

where the population of bacteria
u(x,t)
moves toward higher concentration of oxygen
v(x,t)
depleting it. For more details on these models and the main theoretical techniques to tackle their studies the reader may address the surveys and papers
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
1
,
16
,
12
,
13
]
. Those works deal with analyses concerning local and/or global solutions for models defined on bounded and smooth domains
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
n\geq 1
; specifically some aspects regarding the possibility of blow-up in finite time.

Just to summarize some of them, regarding the model (
1.1
), as mentioned in
[
18
]
and the references cited there, for
n=1
all the solutions are global in time and bounded; meanwhile, for
n=2
then it is required that

\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<4\pi.

Otherwise, for
n\geq 3
, global and bounded solutions exist provided that

\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{\frac{n}{2}+\delta}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon

and

\lVert\nabla{v_{0}}\rVert_{L^{n+\delta}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon,

for some
\varepsilon>0
and any
\delta>0

[
24
]
. However, blow up either in finite or infinite time may occur for
n=2
if

\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}>4\pi.

Also for
n\geq 3
, the solution may be unbounded either in finite or infinite time when
\Omega
is a ball.

On the other hand, for the consumption model, in
[
17
]
it was proved that the system (
1.2
) possesses a unique classical global uniformly-in-time bounded solution if

0<\chi\leq\frac{1}{6(n+1)\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}},

for a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
n\geq 2
. We should notice the required relation between the parameter
\chi
, the initial condition
v_{0}(x)
, and the dimension
n
. That restriction was sharpened by
[
1
]
, always linking those three values. In case of arbitrary large initial data, global weak solutions are obtained in smooth bounded convex domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

[
19
]
.

The aggregation processes mentioned above are essentially connected to an uncontrolled increasing of the cells distribution; this is typical for production models (
1.1
), where the intensity of attraction actions are higher. In this sense, the presence of an external source behaving as
u-u^{k}
,
k>1
(i.e., logistic-type), in the equation for
u
provides dampening effects possibly limiting the presence of blow up; despite that, for
k
close to 1, even this presence is not sufficient to prevent gathering formation
[
21
,
25
]
.

Conversely, for consumption models (
1.2
) (which are of our interest in this paper), the situation is far to be similar. Indeed, even though this is a challenging problem, it is conjectured that such mechanisms do not present explosion phenomena. As indicated above, globality and boundedness are in any case achieved provided smallness assumptions on
\chi\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. As a consequence, it appears natural introducing logistic sources not in order to prevent blow up but to ensure boundedness even for large values of
\chi\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.
This choice can be traced in the model studied by Lankeit and Wang
[
11
]

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+\mu_{1}u-\mu_{2}u^{2},\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-uv,&\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.3)

with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a smooth bounded domain,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The presence of such an external source of logistic-type benefits the boundedness of the global solution provided a restriction on the problem parameter
\mu_{2}
reading

\mu_{2}>K_{1}(n)\lVert\chi v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{n}}+K_{2}(n)\lVert\chi v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2n},

(1.4)

for some positive constants
K_{1}=K_{1}(n)
and
K_{2}=K_{2}(n)
.

Several extensions to those models have been proposed including as well a third equation in model relating a prey and a predator
[
20
]
. In this context, the predator moves toward the gradient of concentration of some chemical released by prey instead of moving directly toward the higher density of prey
[
22
]
. The dynamics of prey and predator consider both, the production and consumption models; in the sense that an unit produce another, while it is consumed by a third one.
Let us consider as a reference point the model

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+g(u)&\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\\
v_{t}=\Delta v+h_{1}(u,v,w)&\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\\
w_{t}=\Delta w+h_{2}(u,w)&\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\end{cases}

(1.5)

and the respective initial conditions for each function.

In
[
8
]
, Hu and Tao identify a critical mass of lymphocytes necessary for the coexistence of tumor and immune cells, highlighting the importance of immune response in tumor dynamics. The proposed model describes the interaction of two cells with one chemical secreted by the tumor cells, where
u=u(x,t)
represents the density of lymphocytes,
v=v(x,t)
describes the chemical signal secreted by tumor cells and
w=w(x,t)
denotes the density of tumor cells. In this case,
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a bounded convex domain with smooth boundary. The term
-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)
in the first equation in (
1.5
) represents the movement of the lymphocytes towards the chemical signal, and the external source is ignored, i.e.,
g(u)=0
. The sources for the second and third equations are chosen as
h_{1}(u,v,w)=w-v-uv
, which combines the production of chemical signal and the consumption by the immune cells, and
h_{2}(u,w)=-uw+w(1-w)
combining the consumption of the tumor cells by the immune cells with a natural logistic growth term of the tumor cells. The system is proven to admit a unique global uniformly-in-time bounded classical solution for all nonnegative initial conditions
(u_{0},v_{0},w_{0})\in{{C^{0}}(\overline{\Omega})}\times{{W^{1,\infty}}(\Omega)}\times{{W^{1,\infty}}(\Omega)}
, with
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
,
v_{0}>0
, and
w_{0}\not\equiv 0
.

The lack of parameters scaling each term in the equations for their choices of
h_{1}(u,v,w)
and
h_{2}(u,v)
, loses the sense of dimensionality of the model, as observed in
[
20
]
. That was a motivation in
[
4
]
to propose a model with
h_{1}(u,v,w)=\alpha w-\beta v-\gamma uv
and
h_{2}(u,w)=-\delta uw+\mu w(1-w)
. The parameters
\chi
,
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
,
\delta
,
\mu
are assumed positive constants. The constant
\alpha
is the production rate of the chemical by the tumor cells,
\beta
is the decay rate of the chemical and
\gamma
is the consumption rate of the chemical by the lymphocytes. The parameter
\delta
models the destruction rate of the tumor cells by the lymphocytes. Lastly,
\mu
is the growth coefficient for the cancer cells.

In that work, it is considered
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a bounded domain, with
n\geq 3
. The studied fully parabolic system admit a unique nonnegative global classical solution uniformly bounded-in-time, provided

\max\left\{\frac{\alpha}{\beta},\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}<\frac{\pi}{\chi}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}.

(1.6)

A natural following up is to consider an external source for the first equation
g(u)=\mu_{1}u^{k}-\mu_{2}u^{k+1}
, for
k\geq 1
and positive
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
. Let us consider the model, extending the proposals in
[
11
,
4
]
, describing the tumor-immune cell interactions by

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+\mu_{1}u^{k}-\mu_{2}u^{k+1},\hskip 14.22636pt&\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\\
v_{t}=\Delta v+\alpha w-\beta v-\gamma uv,&\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\\
w_{t}=\Delta w-\delta uw+\mu_{3}w(1-w),&\text{in}\;\Omega\times(0,T_{\text{max}}),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),&x\in\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.7)

with
\chi,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
, and
\mu_{3}
positive constants. The considered domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 3
, is bounded and with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.

The logistic source term for lymphocytes
g(u,v)=\mu_{1}u^{k}-\mu_{2}u^{k+1}
captures the dynamics of lymphocyte population growth and proliferation reflecting the self-regulating nature of the immune response in the presence of a tumor
[
23
]
,
[
15
]
. As the number of lymphocytes increases, the rate of growth slows down, eventually leading to a plateau in the population size. To the best of our knowledge, there are no studies carried out for the system (
1.7
) with logistic source of that form.

1.1
Main result and structure of the paper

In order to formally present the aforementioned analysis, let us suppose that initial values
u_{0}
,
v_{0}
and
w_{0}
satisfy

\begin{cases}u_{0}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}),\quad\mbox{with}\quad u_{0}\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\>\overline{\Omega},\\
v_{0},w_{0}\in W^{1,q}(\Omega),\quad\mbox{for some}\,\,q>n,\quad\mbox{with}\quad v_{0},w_{0}\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\>\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.8)

With these initial conditions we are able to state what follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
n\geq 3
, be a bounded domain with smooth boundary and let
\chi,\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
, and
\mu_{3}
be positive constants. Let
u_{0},v_{0}
, and
w_{0}
be initial conditions to (
1.7
) fulfilling the hypotheses in (
1.8
). For
M_{2}:=\max\Big\{1,\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big\}
and
M_{3}:=\max\Big\{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}M_{2},\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big\}
, let us assume that those parameters satisfy for
k>1

\delta>\mu_{3}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha<2\min\left\{\beta+\gamma,\delta-\mu_{3}\right\}

(1.9)

with an extra requirement for
k=1
reading

\mu_{2}>\ A_{1}(n)\chi^{2+\frac{4}{n}}M_{3}^{\frac{4}{n}}+A_{2}(n)\gamma^{\frac{n+2}{2}}M_{3}^{n}+A_{3}(n)\delta^{\frac{n+2}{2}}M_{2}^{n},

(1.10)

for certain computable constants
A_{1}(n),A_{2}(n)
, and
A_{3}(n)
.

Then, problem (
1.7
) admits a unique classical solution, global and uniformly bounded in time.

Remark 1.1
.

It is worth realizing that for
k>1
, large values of

\chi\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

are allowed and the restrictions are only on the problem parameters
\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma
, and
\mu_{3}
.

Remark 1.2
.

If we compare the condition in (
1.4
) with (
1.10
), considering model (
1.7
) as a generalization for
k=1
of model (
1.3
), it becomes evident that adding that equation for

does not change the type of constraint on the data; it only adds an additional term associated with
w
. However, for
k>1
the benefit of the constraint that does not depend on

\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

and

\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

is noticeable.

On the other hand, comparing (
1.6
) with (
1.10
), we can see how the extension of the model proposed in
[
4
]
to model (
1.7
) has the advantage of eliminating the constraint for the value

\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

This theorem is proved as a consequence of some lemmas and quoted inequalities. To ease the argumentation of its validity the paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
presents some known results from the literature which are used during the proofs in this paper. The next Section
3
proves the local existence of solutions
(u,v,w)
for the considered model. These solutions are positive and their boundedness in some Sobolev spaces is proved. Moreover, an extensibility criterion is given which supports the globality of the existing local solution. Finally, Section
4
proves the main result already stated in this section (i.e., Theorem
1.1
) as a consequence of some
a priori
inequalities and the results in Section
3
. To conclude this section, some thoughts are devoted to further refining the arguments in
Remark
1.2
.

Along the proofs many constants, denoted by
c_{i},i\in\mathbb{N}
, are explicitly computed, supporting the statement in Theorem
1.1
to provide computable constants. However, their expressions may be cumbersome for the reader in some cases. At the same time, showing them makes it easier for the reader to follow the passages along the inequalities, hence the decision to include an Appendix

where the explicit values of some constants are shown.

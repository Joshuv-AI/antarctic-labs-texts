Positive microlocal holonomies are globally regular
Roger Casals, Wenyuan Li

Abstract
. We establish a geometric criterion for local microlocal holonomies to be globally regular on the moduli space of Lagrangian fillings. This local-to-global regularity result holds for arbitrary Legendrian links and it is a key input for the study of cluster structures on such moduli spaces. Specifically, we construct regular functions on derived moduli stacks of sheaves with Legendrian microsupport by studying the Hochschild homology of the associated dg-categories via relative Lagrangian skeleta. In this construction, a key geometric result is that local microlocal merodromies along positive relative cycles in Lagrangian fillings yield global Hochschild 0-cycles for these dg-categories.

1.
Introduction

Symplectic topology is the study of smooth functions and their first derivatives, broadly understood. Infinitesimally, symplectic geometry studies the differential structure associated to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
, characterized as the geometry that preserves a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form. Along with the geometry that preserves volume, corresponding to
{\displaystyle{\mathfrak{sl}}_{n}}
, and (symmetric) inner products, corresponding to
{\displaystyle{\mathfrak{so}}_{n}}
, these constitute the three infinite families in the classification of complex simple Lie algebras. Locally, symplectic geometry adds the integrability condition that the non-degenerate 2-form is closed, resulting in a unique normal form near any point, up to symmetries preserving the symplectic 2-form. Globally, the intricacies of symplectic 2-forms and their symmetries remain an active subject of study, often referred to as symplectic topology. A salient instance of symplectic manifolds are cotangent bundles
T^{*}Q
of smooth manifolds
Q
, whereby the smooth topology of
Q
and its submanifolds is in part reflected by the intersection theory of Lagrangian submanifolds in
T^{*}Q
. Here, Lagrangian submanifolds are defined to be those submanifolds where the restriction of the ambient symplectic 2-form vanishes identically, and are of maximal dimension. In general, such Lagrangian submanifolds generalize the graphs
\mbox{gr}(df)\subset T^{*}Q
of differentials of smooth functions
f:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, in particular allowing for non-graphical behavior and parametric families of such functions.

In modern symplectic topology, Lagrangians and their intersection theory yield useful global invariants of symplectic manifolds. In addition, any closed symplectic manifold admits a symplectic divisor whose complement can be appropriately understood as the cotangent bundle of a possibly singular Lagrangian skeleton. The study of Lagrangian submanifolds is thus of central importance in our understanding of symplectic topology, e.g. the classification of Lagrangian submanifolds, up to either Hamiltonian isotopies or symplectomorphisms, is a desired goal in the current scientific context. This article examines one of the simplest models: understanding embedded exact Lagrangian surfaces of the local normal form
(\mathbb{R}^{4},\omega_{\text{st}})
, with a fixed boundary condition given by a Legendrian knot. Intuitively, the main goal of the article is to find
global regular
functions on the derived moduli stack of such Lagrangian surfaces. Such functions allow for a better understanding of these intricate spaces (e.g. sometimes allowing for the computation of their cohomology rings) and yield connections to other areas, including the study of cluster algebras. The main strategy for constructing such global functions is to first construct local regular functions, by using certain parallel transports, and then establish a criterion for these locally regular functions to be globally regular. The title of the manuscript refers to the criterion we present: if such a local function is
positive
with respect to a Lagrangian skeleton, then it extends to a global regular function.

1.1.
Summary

The object of this article will be to prove the global regularity of positive microlocal merodromies for Lagrangian fillings of Legendrian links. Microlocal merodromies are local functions defined on an open subset of the moduli of pseudo-perfect objects in the smooth dg-category of sheaves with singular support on a Legendrian link. Intuitively, these are functions defined on an open subset of the moduli of Lagrangian fillings. In important situations, such
local
functions can be extended to
global
regular functions on the entire moduli space. For instance, this is the case for cluster variables in braid varieties and, more generally, elements of canonical bases. This
local
to
global
regularity lies at the core of many recent results on Lagrangian fillings. Heretofore, it has been difficult to argue that such regular extensions exist, e.g. it is already challenging in the case of cluster variables. This article provides a general criterion for such global regularity based on the study of
\mathbb{L}
-compressing systems. The conditions for this criterion are verifiable and based on geometric data.

Specifically, our main result is to show that such regular extensions exist if the relative cycle intersects positively an
\mathbb{L}
-compressing system. It applies to arbitrary Legendrian links and Lagrangian fillings (and arbitrary microlocal rank), both generalizing and independently recovering our previous results on global regularity of cluster variables. A key part of our argument is the construction of Hochschild cycles for the aforementioned category that lift these microlocal merodromies. The proof highlights the advantages of working within the dg-categorical framework when studying Legendrian links. Even in known cases, our argument bypasses previously required algebraic computations needed to argue for regularity and, using the derived stack of pseudo-perfect objects, provides a clearer categorical argument for regularity. In brief, apart from its greater level of generality, a strength of the result is that it provides new conceptual and geometric reasons for the global regularity of such functions.

3.1.
The Kashiwara-Schapira stack

The category
\operatorname{Sh}_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the global sections of a sheaf of dg-categories, known as the Kashiwara-Schapira stack. This subsection reviews such stack and its properties. Let
X
be a Weinstein sector with subanalytic Lagrangian skeleton
\mathbb{L}
. In this work, the expression
sheaves of dg-categories
precisely means
\infty
-sheaves, cf.
[
58
, Def. 7.3.3.1]
, valued in the
\infty
-category
\mbox{dg-Cat}_{k}
of well generated dg-categories, cf.
[
80
]
(we will see later in the proof of Theorem
3.16
that these sheaves on Lagrangian skeleta are in fact valued in the
\infty
-category of compactly generated dg-categories).

Following
[
47
, Section 6]
, we will now consider a certain
\infty
-sheaf of dg-categories

\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}:\operatorname{Op}(\mathbb{L})^{op}\longrightarrow\mbox{dg-Cat}_{k},

often referred to as the Kashiwara–Schapira stack, cf.
[
44
, Section 10.1]
. (It is also known as microlocal sheaves, cf.
[
65
, Section 3.4]
or
[
63
, Section 6]
.) We work with dg-categories primarily so that we can use the derived moduli stack from
[
82
]
. In addition, we use dg-categories as sheaf coefficients because triangulated (derived) categories do not typically satisfy descent. The
\infty
-sheaf
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}
is defined as follows.

First, we define an
\infty
-sheaf of dg-categories in the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
of a smooth manifold
M
. By definition, a subset
\widehat{\Lambda}\subset T^{*}M
is conic if it is invariant under the following scaling
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-action:
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times T^{*}M\to T^{*}M,(s;x,\xi)\to(x,s\xi)
. A typical example of
\widehat{\Lambda}
will be the union of the Lagrangian cone over a Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset T^{*}_{\infty}M
and the zero section
M\subset T^{*}M
. In particular,
\widehat{\Lambda}
might be singular.

Remark 3.1
.

We consider the conic topology on
T^{*}M
, generated by all conic open sets. We define the sheaf of categories using the conic topology, which is equivalent to an
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-equivariant sheaf of categories on the standard (Euclidean) manifold topology. Indeed, for any open subset in the manifold topology whose intersection with all
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-orbits are contractible, we declare sections on it to be the sections on the conification. Since such open subsets form a topological basis, we then obtain an
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-equivariant sheaf on the manifold topology.
\Box

We denote by
\operatorname{Op}(T^{*}M)
the category of open conic subsets on
T^{*}M
, with morphisms given by inclusions of the open conic subsets.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\widehat{\Lambda}\subset T^{*}M
be a conic subset. The
\infty
-presheaf
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}^{\text{pre}}:\operatorname{Op}(T^{*}M)^{op}\longrightarrow\mbox{dg-Cat}_{k}
of dg-categories on
T^{*}M
associated to
\widehat{\Lambda}
is

\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}^{\text{pre}}(\widehat{\Omega}):=\operatorname{Sh}_{\widehat{\Lambda}\cup(T^{*}M\backslash\widehat{\Omega})}(M)/\operatorname{Sh}_{T^{*}M\backslash\widehat{\Omega}}(M),\quad\widehat{\Omega}\subset T^{*}M\mbox{ open conic subset}.

By definition, the
\infty
-sheaf
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}:\operatorname{Op}(T^{*}M)^{op}\longrightarrow\mbox{dg-Cat}_{k}
of dg-categories on
T^{*}M
is the
\infty
-sheafification of the
\infty
-presheaf
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}^{\text{pre}}
. We refer to the
\infty
-sheaf
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}
as the Kashiwara-Schapira stack of
\widehat{\Lambda}
.

\Box

Note that for any (conic) open set
\widehat{\Omega}\subset T^{*}M
with
\widehat{\Omega}\cap\widehat{\Lambda}=\emptyset
we have a dg-equivalence
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}^{\text{pre}}(\widehat{\Omega})\cong 0
, where the right hand side is understood as the one-object category with the zero vector space as its endomorphisms. Therefore

\widehat{\Lambda}

The conical aspect can be effectively ignored (the only additional choices lie in the zero section) by restricting to the ideal contact boundary of

Definition 3.3

Lemma 3.7
.

For
\Lambda\subset T^{*}_{\infty}M
, if the projection
\pi:\Lambda\to M
is finite-to-one, then for any sufficiently small open ball

\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Lambda}^{\text{pre}}(\Omega)\cong\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Lambda}^{\text{pre}}(\Omega)\cong\operatorname{Sh}_{\Lambda}(B)/\mathrm{Loc}(B).

3.2.
Corestriction functors for Kashiwara-Schapira stacks

For two open subsets
\Omega^{\prime},\Omega\subset\mathbb{L}
with
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, we now show that the restriction functor
\rho^{*}:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega^{\prime})
admits both left and right adjoints, and consequently the left adjoint preserves compact objects. The left adjoint is denoted by

\rho_{!}:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega^{\prime})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega)

and referred to as the
corestriction functor
. See also
[
65
, Section 3.6]
,
[
63
, Remark 5.1]
or
[
50
, Section 3.2]
. More generally, we have restriction and corestriction functors for any open immersions
\Omega^{\prime}\looparrowright\Omega
(which are local homeomorphisms). The precise result we use reads as follows:

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
\mathbb{L}
be a compact subanalytic Lagrangian subset, endowed with a polarization. Consider two open Lagrangian subsets
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{L}
such that
\Omega^{\prime}\looparrowright\Omega
and
\Omega\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}
. Then the restriction functor

\rho^{*}:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega^{\prime}}(\Omega^{\prime})

admits a left adjoint
\rho_{!}
and a right adjoint
\rho_{*}
. In particular, the left adjoint, which is called the corestriction functor, preserves compact objects

\rho_{!}:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega^{\prime}}(\Omega^{\prime})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega}(\Omega).

Proof.

By genericity and invariance, it suffices to consider the case where the Legendrian embedding
\mathbb{L}\subset T^{*}_{\infty}M
is such that the front projection
\pi:\mathbb{L}\longrightarrow M
is finite-to-one. We claim that there exists a topological basis of
T^{*}_{\infty}M
on which
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}
takes values in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\omega,\mathrm{dg},k}
, the
\infty
-category whose objects are compactly generated dg-categories and whose functors preserve limits and (filtered) colimits, cf.
[
58
, Def. 5.5.7.5]
. The claim is proven as follows. By Lemma
3.7
, when
\pi:\mathbb{L}\to M
is finite-to-one, for any point in
T^{*}M
, there exists an open ball
B\subset M
together with a union of open balls
\Omega\subset T^{*}_{\infty}B
such that
\mathbb{L}\cap T^{*}_{\infty}B\subset\Omega
, and

\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega)=\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}\cup(T^{*}_{\infty}M\setminus\Omega)}(M)/\operatorname{Sh}_{T^{*}_{\infty}M\setminus\Omega}(M)=\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}}(B)/\mathrm{Loc}(B).

First,
[
40
, Cor. 4.22]
implies that these categories are compactly generated, so sections of
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}
indeed are objects of
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\omega,\mathrm{dg},k}
. Specifically, these dg-categories are compactly generated by the corepresentatives of the microstalk functors. Second, consider the restriction functor for such open balls
\rho:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega^{\prime})
: it is induced by restrictions of sheaves for
r:B^{\prime}\hookrightarrow B
via

r^{*}:\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}}(B)/\mathrm{Loc}(B)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}}(B^{\prime})/\mathrm{Loc}(B^{\prime}).

Since the restriction functor on all sheaves
r^{*}:\operatorname{Sh}(B)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sh}(B^{\prime})
admits left adjoint
r_{!}
and right adjoint
r_{*}
, and the inclusion
\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}}(B)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sh}(B)
admits left and right adjoints by
[
53
, Thm. 1.2]
, we know that on the full subcategories the restriction
r^{*}:\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}}(B)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}}(B^{\prime})
also admits left and right adjoints. Since the restriction also preserves local systems, the adjunction descends to localizations
r^{*}:\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}}(B)/\mathrm{Loc}(B)\to\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathbb{L}}(B^{\prime})/\mathrm{Loc}(B^{\prime})
. This shows that, for this basis,
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}
sends morphisms to those in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\omega,\mathrm{dg},k}
and thus establishes the claim.

Therefore, the sections of the sheaf
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}
on arbitrary open subsets take values in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\omega,\mathrm{dg},k}
since the category admits all small limits, cf.
[
58
, Prop. 5.5.7.6]
. Hence, for arbitrary open embeddings
\Omega^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\Omega\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}
, the restriction functor
\rho:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega^{\prime})
, being a morphism in
\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{R}}_{\omega,\mathrm{dg},k}
, preserves limits and colimits and thus admits both left and right adjoints. The left adjoint
\rho_{!}:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega^{\prime})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega)
now has a right adjoint which admits a further right adjoint, and hence preserves compact objects. Note that we have a natural equivalence
\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\mathbb{L}}(\Omega)=\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega}(\Omega)
if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{L}
, thus proving the result for open embeddings
\Omega^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\Omega\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}
.

Finally, for an open immersion (which is a local homeomorphism)
\Omega^{\prime}\looparrowright\Omega\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}
, we find an open cover of
\Omega^{\prime}
by
\{\Omega_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i\in I}
such that
\Omega_{i}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\Omega
. We have corestriction functors
({\rho_{i}})_{!}:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega_{i}^{\prime}}(\Omega_{i}^{\prime})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega}(\Omega)
preserving compact objects. By the universal property of global sections on
\Omega^{\prime}
, we thus obtain a corestriction functor
{\rho}_{!}:\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega^{\prime}}(\Omega^{\prime})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\operatorname{\mu Sh}}_{\Omega}(\Omega)
that preserves compact objects.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

Via the equivalence between microlocal sheaves and partially wrapped Fukaya categories, established in
[
40
]
, we expect that in good situations, the corestriction functor for open inclusions of stratified Lagrangian subsets coincides with the pushfoward functor for proper inclusions of Weinstein sectors, cf.
[
39
]
. In line with
[
53
, Theorem 1.2 & Section 3.2]
and
[
39
, Section 3.5]
, we also expect that corestriction functors can be characterized by sheaf-theoretic wrappings, i.e. we expect that applying each such corestriction functor can be expressed as a certain colimit of a diagram of sheaves indexed by positive Hamiltonian isotopies of the Weinstein sector. This would be the symplectic topological incarnation of the pointwise colimit formula for left Kan extensions, see e.g.
[

.

In order to relate microlocal merodromies to an actual parallel transport of a local system, we implicitly use Corollary

4.
Hochschild chains and regular functions on moduli stacks

This section concludes the proof of Theorem

\operatorname{HO}
map (
1.1
), used in our argument for Theorem
1.1
. Specifically, Subsection
4.1
presents the necessary framework on Hochschild chains. The two key results are then established in Lemma
4.1
, in Subsection
4.2
, and Proposition

, in Subsection
4.3
.
Subsection

establishes the necessary results that combine the corestriction functors from Section

with the Hochschild chains functor.
Theorem

is then proven in Subsection

.

4.1.
The general framework

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a smooth dg-category and
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
its moduli stack of pseudoperfect objects. By
[
82
, Prop. 3.4]
, the functor
\mathcal{C}\longmapsto\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
in (

) is right adjoint to the functor

sending a
D^{-}
-stack to its dg-category of perfect complexes. In particular, we have the equality

\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{-}\operatorname{St}(k)}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}})=\mathrm{Hom}_{\operatorname{Ho(dg-cat)}^{op}}(\mathcal{C},L_{\textit{perf\,}}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}})).

Therefore, the identity morphism on
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
gives a canonical functor
\mathrm{Id}^{\ell}:\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow L_{\textit{perf\,}}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}})
. Applying Hochschild chains to this functor we obtain a map

\operatorname{HH}_{*}(\mathrm{Id}^{\ell}):\operatorname{HH}_{*}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\operatorname{HH}_{*}(L_{\textit{perf\,}}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}})).

By
[
85
, Corollary 4.2]
, in line with the HKR theorem, this yields a map

(4.1)

\operatorname{HO}:\operatorname{HH}_{*}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\Gamma(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}}}).

For more details, see
[
9
, Section 5.2]
or
[
7
, Section 6.1.2]
, specifically
[
7
, Example 6.8]
. We choose the notation
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\operatorname{HO}}
for (
4.1
) as a mnemonic for
“from
H
ochschild to
\mathcal{O}
”
. We often also denote by
\operatorname{HO}
the restriction
\operatorname{HO}_{0}
of
\operatorname{HO}
in (
4.1
) to Hochschild 0-chains
\operatorname{HH}_{0}
, if the context makes that clear.

In the context where we study a Legendrian link
(\Lambda,\mathfrak{t})
, we set
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Sh}^{c}_{\Lambda,\mathfrak{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})_{0}
and consider its moduli of sheaves
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathfrak{M}({\Lambda,\mathfrak{t}})
. The goal is to obtain (global) regular functions of
\mathfrak{M}({\Lambda,\mathfrak{t}})
. By (

), we can achieve that by constructing Hochschild classes in
\operatorname{HH}_{*}(\mathcal{C})
. Our next step is thus the construction of classes in
\operatorname{HH}_{*}(\mathcal{C})
, which we achieve using the geometry of Lagrangian fillings and
\mathbb{L}
-compressing systems.

4.1.1.
Some context

As it is apparent from our previous works, see e.g.
[
15
,
21
]
, it can be challenging to directly construct such regular functions on
\mathfrak{M}({\Lambda,\mathfrak{t}})
. Thus far, the only successful strategy has been as follows:

(1)

Find an embedded exact Lagrangian filling
L
with an
\mathbb{L}
-compressing system. In the chart of local systems
T(L)\subset\mathfrak{M}({\Lambda,\mathfrak{t}})
associated to this filling
L
, one constructs regular functions on that chart: these are natural from the geometry of the
\mathbb{L}
-compressing system and readily seen to be regular on
T(L)
. There is often no simple argument deciding whether such regular functions extend to regular functions on
\mathfrak{M}({\Lambda,\mathfrak{t}})
.

(2)

Employ ad-hoc methods, often exploiting the specific combinatorics (such as plabic graphs in
[
21
]
or weaves in
[
15
]
) and relation to cluster mutations, so as to argue that the regular functions built on

extend to regular functions on
\mathfrak{M}({\Lambda,\mathfrak{t}})
. At core, this is an argument that requires explicit formulas related to the underlying combinatorics.
See for instance
[
21
, Section 4.9 & Prop. 4.38]
or
[
15
, Section 5.3]
, specifically Lemmas 5.26, 5.27 and 5.28 in
loc. cit
.

The strength of using Hochschild classes in

and the

map in (

applies to Legendrian links that are not closures of positive braids.

Then there is an isomorphism

of vector spaces, where

Proof.

By
[

is equivalent to the dg-category of representations of the path algebra

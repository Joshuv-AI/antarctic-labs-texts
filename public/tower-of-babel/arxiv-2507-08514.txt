1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a Banach space of analytic functions in the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
. The composition operator
C_{\varphi}
of analytic
symbol

\varphi:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
acting on
X
is defined by
C_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi
,
f\in X
. These operators have historically attracted, and continue to attract, special attention in the field of Complex Analysis and Operator Theory. Among the many interesting questions arising in relation to these operators, the study of the spectrum and the point spectrum has played a prominent role. In this work, we will focus on the case where
X
is any Hardy space
H^{p}
of the unit disc, with
1\leq p<\infty.
We say that a holomorphic function
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
belongs to the Hardy space
H^{p}
,
0<p<\infty
, if

\|f\|_{H^{p}}:=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

It is well known that for
1\leq p<\infty
,
\|\cdot\|_{H^{p}}
defines a norm, whereas for
0<p<1
, it is a quasi-norm.

The point spectrum of composition operators on
H^{p}
spaces, which is characterized via the Schroeder’s equation

(1.1)

f\circ\varphi=\lambda f\qquad(f\in H^{p}\setminus\{0\},\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}),

is completely understood when the symbol
\varphi
is a linear fractional map (
[
7
,
10
,
12
,
19
,
20
]
). In the general setting, despite the existence of some partial results (see the monograph
[
11
]
), we lack a complete characterization of the point spectrum.

One particularly interesting situation is that of composition operators on
H^{p}
embedded into a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
. By the seminal work of Berkson and Porta
[
1
]
, these correspond exactly to the symbols
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
that form a continuous semigroup of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
.

The point spectrum of these composition operators can be characterized in terms of the point spectrum of the infinitesimal generator
\Delta
of the
C_{0}
-semigroup (see
[
14
]
). Indeed,

(1.2)

\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi_{t}}\mid_{H^{p}})=\{e^{t\lambda}:\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\}\qquad(t>0).

Moreover, Siskakis
[
23
]
showed that
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})
if and only if
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}
, where
h:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is the Koenigs function of the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Under these considerations, in 2017, Betsakos characterized
[
2
]
the point spectrum of infinitesimal generators of hyperbolic composition semigroups on
H^{p}
in terms of the geometry of the associated Koenigs domain
\Omega=h({\mathbb{D}})
. However, the parabolic case remained open.

In this sense, our main objective consists in characterizing the point spectrum of parabolic composition semigroups on
H^{p}
, which, quoting Betsakos
[
2
]
, “is probably much more difficult to describe” than in the hyperbolic case. As discussed above, such a description is equivalent to characterizing those
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}
.

It is important to point out that this problem is equivalent to studying which exponentials
e^{\lambda z}
belong to the Hardy space
H^{p}(\Omega)
, which is the space of holomorphic functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
such that
|f|^{p}
has a harmonic majorant in
\Omega
. Those
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying that
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega)
are called the
frequencies
of
\Omega
. In a very recent paper
[
6
]
, Bracci, Gallardo-Gutiérrez, and Yakubovich have characterized whether the linear manifold spanned by the
e^{\lambda z}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega)
is
weak-
*
dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
in terms of geometric conditions on the Koenigs domain
\Omega
. In their work, the authors obtain sufficient conditions for the norm-density of such a manifold in
H^{p}
.

The methods developed in this text are largely based on careful estimates of the harmonic measure of some boundary subsets of
\Omega
. In this sense, these techniques are related to both classical and novel results on diverse problems. For instance, the behaviour of the orbits of semigroups on
{\mathbb{D}}
(see
[
3
]
or the most recent
[
4
]
), the characterization of the Hardy number of Koenigs domains
[
8
]
, as well as the computation of the essential spectral radius of composition operators on
H^{2}

[
21
]
.

1.1.
Preliminaries

As pointed out previously,
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup acting on
H^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
if and only if the family of symbols
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a continuous semigroup of analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
. For this to occur, the following three conditions must hold:

(i)

\varphi_{0}(z)=z
for every
z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

(ii)

\varphi_{t+s}=\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi_{s}
for every
t,s\geq 0.

(iii)

If
t\rightarrow s,
then
\varphi_{t}\rightarrow\varphi_{s}
uniformly on compact subsets of
{\mathbb{D}}
for
t,s\geq 0
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a continuous semigroup which does not contain automorphisms of
{\mathbb{D}}
with a fixed point in
{\mathbb{D}}
. In such a case, there exists a unique point
\tau\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
such that
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
converges uniformly on compacta, as
t
goes to
+\infty
, to the constant map
z\mapsto\tau
. The point
\tau
is called the
Denjoy-Wolff point
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. This important feature of continuous semigroups on
{\mathbb{D}}
allows us to classify them depending on the location of the Denjoy-Wolff point. If
\tau\in{\mathbb{D}}
, the semigroup is said to be
elliptic
. If
\tau\in{\mathbb{T}}:=\partial{\mathbb{D}}
, we say that the semigroup is
non-elliptic
. It is known that the functions
\varphi_{t}^{\prime}
have non-tangential limits
e^{t\alpha}
at the Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau,
with
\alpha\leq 0
. This allows a further classification of non-elliptic semigroups. Indeed,
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is said to be
hyperbolic
if
\alpha<0
and
parabolic
if
\alpha=0.

We will focus our attention on non-elliptic semigroups. If
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is non-elliptic, there exists a univalent function
h:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying

(1.3)

\varphi_{t}(z)=h^{-1}(h(z)+t)\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}},\ t\geq 0).

The function
h
is unique, up to an additive constant. We refer to the function
h
as the
Koenigs function
of the semigroup, see
[
5
, Theorem 9.3.5]
. Its image,
\Omega:=h({\mathbb{D}})
, is the
Koenigs domain
of the semigroup. This domain is
starlike at infinity
, meaning that, for every
z\in\Omega
and every
t\geq 0
,
z+t\in\Omega.

We will use a recent fruitful description of the Koenigs domain. Indeed, every Koenigs domain
\Omega
can be described as

\{x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}:y\in I,\ x>\psi_{\Omega}(y)\},

where
\psi_{\Omega}:I\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty)
is an upper semicontinuous function and
I\subset(-\infty,\infty)
is an open connected interval. This function is known as the
defining function
of
\Omega
(see, for instance,
[
6
, Definition 2.3]
).

The geometry of the Koenigs domain encodes valuable information about the semigroup. In this respect, a semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is parabolic if and only if its Koenigs domain is not contained in a horizontal strip
[
9
]
. Thus,
\Omega
is parabolic if and only if
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})
is not bounded. We will call
parabolic Koenigs domain
to all Koenigs domains not contained in a horizontal strip.

It is standard in the literature to distinguish two classes of parabolic semigroups. A parabolic semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is said to be of
positive hyperbolic step
if for some
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
the quantity

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}k_{\mathbb{D}}(\varphi_{t}(z),\varphi_{t+1}(z))

is positive, where
k_{\mathbb{D}}
denotes the hyperbolic distance in
{\mathbb{D}}
. If the previous situation does not hold,
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of
zero hyperbolic step
. This property can also be characterized through the geometry of
\Omega
. In fact,
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of positive hyperbolic step if and only if
\Omega
is contained in a horizontal half-plane
[
5
, Theorem 9.3.5 and Proposition 9.3.10]
.

There exists another object closely linked to the continuous semigroup: the
infinitesimal generator
. A remarkable result of Berkson and Porta
[
1
]
asserts that each continuous semigroup of holomorphic self-maps of
{\mathbb{D}}
is locally uniformly differentiable with respect to the parameter
t\geq 0.
Not only this, but the function
t\mapsto\varphi_{t}(z)
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, is the solution of the Cauchy problem:

(1.4)

\frac{\partial\varphi_{t}(z)}{\partial t}=G(\varphi_{t}(z))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varphi_{0}(z)=z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

The function
H
is called the
infinitesimal generator
of
the semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. There exists an intimate connection between the infinitesimal generators of the semigroup of symbols
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and the semigroup of operators. In fact, if
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
is the associated
C_{0}-
semigroup, then

(1.5)

\Delta f=G\cdot f^{\prime}\qquad(f\in H^{p})

is its infinitesimal generator, which, we recall, is defined as

\Delta f=\lim\limits_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{C_{\varphi_{t}}f-f}{t}\qquad(f\in\textnormal{dom}(\Delta)).

It turns out that
\Delta
is a closed densely defined linear operator acting on
H^{p}
.

In what follows, unless stated otherwise, given
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
, we will denote by
h
its associated Koenigs function and by
\Omega
to its associated Koenigs domain. We will assume without loss of generality that
0\in\Omega
. Likewise, we will denote by
\Delta
the infinitesimal generator of the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
on
H^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
.

We will use the notation
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
if there is some constant
C>0
such that
|f(x)|\leq C|g(x)|
for all
x
. If we have simultaneously
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
and
g(x)\lesssim f(x)
, we write
f\approx g
. The use of this notation will imply that the underlying constants are absolute, which means that they do not depend on any other magnitude.

Also, given
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define
{\mathbb{C}}_{\theta}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\ \mathrm{Re}(z)>\theta\}
and
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}={\mathbb{C}}_{0}
.

2.
The result for angular sector domains

As a particular case to clarify certain concepts that will be key to the problem, we consider
\Omega
to be an angular sector, and characterize those
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
. We will also introduce some technical results that will be useful in a more general setting.

We begin by defining angular sectors. Given
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\pi]
with
\alpha+\beta>0
, we define the angular sector centered in
z_{0}
, with lower angle
\alpha
and upper angle
\beta
as

S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)=\{re^{i\theta}:r>0,\ -\alpha<\theta<\beta\}.

To simplify the notation, we will write
S_{0}(\alpha,\beta)=S(\alpha,\beta)
, and
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\alpha)=S_{p_{0}}(\alpha)
.

Recall that, for all
z_{0}
and
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\pi]
with
\alpha+\beta>0
,
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
is a Koenigs domain, with associated Koenigs function

h(z)=p_{0}+e^{i\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\pi}}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

The main result of this section reads as follows:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Assume that
\Omega=S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
for some
p_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\pi]
and
\alpha+\beta>0
.

(i)

If
\alpha+\beta\leq\pi
, then

\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})=\left\{re^{i\theta}:r\geq 0,\ \frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha\leq\theta\leq\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta\right\}.

(ii)

If
\alpha+\beta>\pi
, then

\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})=\{0\}.

Remark 2.2
.

Notice that, if
\alpha+\beta=\pi
, then
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
is a half-plane, which implies that
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup of linear fractional maps. In fact, it is a semigroup of invertible elements (i.e., a group) if and only if
\alpha=\pi
and
\beta=0
or
\alpha=0
and
\beta=\pi
(see, for instance,
[
24
]
).

To prove Theorem
2.1
, we will need the following results:

Proposition 2.3
.

[
2
, Section 6, Proposition 1]

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(\tilde{\varphi}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be two parabolic semigroups, with Koenigs domains
\Omega
and

and infinitesimal generators of the associated

-semigroups

and

, respectively. If

, then
\sigma_{p}(\Delta)\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\tilde{\Delta}).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup. Assume that there exists

such that

the infinitesimal generator of the

Then,

\sigma_{p}(\tilde{\Delta})=e^{-i\theta}\sigma_{p}(\Delta).

Proof.

Recall that
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\tilde{\Delta})
if and only if

, so
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\tilde{\Delta})
if and only if

which yields the result.
∎

.

.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t>0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
.

(i)

Assume that there exist

\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\lambda\Omega
is contained in some left half-plane. As we will see, this property does not hold for every Koenigs domain
\Omega
, but at least, one of the two contentions hold:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t>0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
be such that

is contained in a left half-plane. Then,
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{\infty}
and

In such a case, if
\mu\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}

\lambda+\mu\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}

Let

be the associated Koenigs map. If

is contained in a left half-plane, then there exists

such that

Noticing that

3.
Toolbox: Smirnov class and harmonic measure

3.1.
The Smirnov class
\mathcal{N}^{+}

We denote by
\log^{+}(x)=\max\{0,\log(x)\}
for every
x>0
. A holomorphic function
f:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
belongs to the
Nevanlinna class

\mathcal{N}
if

\sup\limits_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|f(re^{i\theta})|d\theta<\infty.

It is well known that
H^{p}\subset\mathcal{N}
for every
0<p<\infty
. Moreover, for every function
f\in\mathcal{N}
, its nontangential limit
f(e^{i\theta})
exists almost everywhere
[
13
, Chapter 2]
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{N}.
We say that
f
belongs to the
Smirnov class

\mathcal{N}^{+}
if

\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|f(re^{i\theta})|d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|f(e^{i\theta})|d\theta.

It is worth mentioning that
\mathcal{N}^{+}\subsetneq\mathcal{N}.
For instance, the function
f(z)=\exp\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)
belongs to
\mathcal{N}
but does not lie in the Smirnov class. The membership of a Nevanlinna function
f
to
\mathcal{N}^{+}
may be characterized in terms of the Poisson integral of the boundary values of
f
. Denoting by

P_{z}(e^{i\theta})=\mathrm{Re}\left(\frac{e^{i\theta}+z}{e^{i\theta}-z}\right)\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}},\ e^{i\theta}\in{\mathbb{T}})

the Poisson kernel, the following holds:

Theorem 3.1
.

[
15
, Theorem 5.4]

Let
f\in\mathcal{N}.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
.

(ii)

For all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}

(3.1)

\log|f(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|f(e^{i\theta})|P_{z}(e^{i\theta})d\theta.

For functions in the Smirnov class, we will be able to study their membership to
H^{p}
in terms of harmonic measure. Indeed, a function
f\in\mathcal{N^{+}}
belongs to
H^{p}

(1\leq p<\infty)
if and only if its nontangential limit belongs to
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})

[
13
, Theorem 2.11]
. Thus, following
[
2
, p. 2256]
one has

(3.2)

\begin{split}\left\lvert\left\lvert f\right\rvert\right\rvert^{p}_{H^{p}}&=\left\lvert\left\lvert f\right\rvert\right\rvert^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\\
&=\frac{p}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}m(\{\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}:|f(\xi)|>r\})dr\\
&=\frac{p}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\omega(0,\{\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}:|f(\xi)|>r\},{\mathbb{D}})dr,\end{split}

where
\omega(0,A,{\mathbb{D}})
denotes the harmonic measure of the Borel set
A\subset\partial{\mathbb{D}}
relative to
{\mathbb{D}}
at
0
(see subsection (
3.3
)).

In order to apply (
3.2
) to the functions
e^{\lambda h}
, we must provide sufficient conditions for them to belong to
\mathcal{N}^{+}:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
h:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
a univalent function. If
h\in H^{1}
, then
e^{\lambda h}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
. In particular, if
h({\mathbb{D}})
is contained in
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
for some
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\pi],

0<\alpha+\beta<\pi
, then
h\in H^{1}
and
e^{\lambda h}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

This proof is based on some ideas of
[
2
, Section 2.3]
. Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
. We first show that
e^{\lambda h}\in\mathcal{N}.
We have, for all
0<r<1,

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|e^{\lambda h(re^{i\theta})}|d\theta\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{Re}(\lambda h(re^{i\theta}))\leq|\lambda|\int_{0}^{2\pi}|h(re^{i\theta}))|\leq|\lambda|\left\lvert\left\lvert h\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{1}}<\infty.

Now, by
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
it follows that for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}

\lambda h(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda h(e^{i\theta})P_{z}(e^{i\theta})d\theta.

As a consequence,

\mathrm{Re}(\lambda h(z))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{Re}(\lambda h(e^{i\theta}))P_{z}(e^{i\theta})d\theta

for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Noticing that
\log|e^{\lambda h}|=\mathrm{Re}(\lambda h)
, Theorem
3.1
yields that
e^{\lambda h}\in\mathcal{N}^{+},
as we wanted.

Now, assume that
h({\mathbb{D}})
is contained in
S_{z_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
. We may assume without loss of generality that
z_{0}=0
. Consider

\tilde{h}(z)=e^{i\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\pi}}\qquad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

It is straightforward to check that
\tilde{h}
is a univalent function mapping
{\mathbb{D}}
onto
S(\alpha,\beta).
It is well known that the map

z\in{\mathbb{D}}\mapsto\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\lambda}

belongs to
H^{1}
if and only if
|\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)|<1
, so
\tilde{h}\in H^{1}.
Finally, the map
\psi:\tilde{h}^{-1}\circ h:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}
induces a bounded composition operator
C_{\psi}:H^{1}\rightarrow H^{1},
so
h=C_{\psi}\tilde{h}\in H^{1},
concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t>0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Assume that
\Omega
is contained in
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
for some
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\pi],

0<\alpha+\beta<\pi
, and let
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\sigma}\in{\mathbb{C}}.
Then

(3.3)

\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert^{p}_{H^{p}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\omega(0,\{\xi\in\partial\Omega_{\sigma}:\mathrm{Re}(\xi)>t\},\Omega_{\sigma})dt,

where
\Omega_{\sigma}=e^{i\sigma}\Omega.

Proof.

First, recall that
e^{\lambda h}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
by Proposition
3.2
, so by (
3.2
)

\begin{split}\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert^{p}_{H^{p}}&=\frac{p}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\omega(0,\{\xi\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}:|e^{\lambda h(\xi)}|>r\},{\mathbb{D}})dr\\
&=\frac{p}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\omega(0,\{\xi\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}:\mathrm{Re}(\lambda h(\xi))>\log r\},{\mathbb{D}})dr\\
&=\frac{p}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\omega(0,\{\xi\in\partial\Omega_{\sigma}:\mathrm{Re}(\xi)>\frac{\log r}{|\lambda|}\},\Omega_{\sigma})dr\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\omega(0,\{\xi\in\partial\Omega_{\sigma}:\mathrm{Re}(\xi)>t\},\Omega_{\sigma})dt,\end{split}

where we have applied the conformal invariance of the harmonic measure (see subsection

) with the univalent map
e^{i\sigma}h:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\sigma}
in the third equality and the change of variable

∎

between

3.3.
Harmonic measure

Harmonic measure will play a central role in the proof of the main results of this work. We now recall its definition, as well as some of its most basic properties. Moreover, we state those known estimates that will be needed later. For a more detailed exposition on the topic, the reader is addressed to
[
22
]
,
[
16
]
, or
[
5
]
for simply connected domains.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in the complex plane whose boundary has positive logarithmic capacity i.e., it is non-polar. Consider
E
a Borel subset of
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
. The harmonic measure of
E
relative to
\Omega
is the generalized Perron-Wiener solution
u
of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian in
\Omega
, where the boundary values are given by the characteristic function of the set
E
. We will use the standard notation

u(z)=\omega(z,E,\Omega),\quad z\in\Omega.

In our setting, we will deal only with simply connected domains. Every simply connected domain
\Omega
contains a continuum, which is a non-polar set
[
22
, Corollary 3.8.5]
. Then, the harmonic measure is well defined for these domains.

A first nice property of harmonic measure is its conformal invariance (see
[
22
, Theorem 4.3.8]
). Another useful tool is Harnack’s inequality. Indeed, it allows one to relate the value of the harmonic function
\omega(\cdot,E,\Omega)
at two different points
z,w\in\Omega
(
[
22
, Theorem 1.3.1]
). In case
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
is locally connected, we can explicitly compute the constant appearing in Harnack’s inequality, which takes the following form (see
[
22
, Section 1.3]
or
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
):

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subsetneq{\mathbb{C}}
be a simply connected domain such that
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
is locally connected. Let
E
be a Borel subset of
\partial_{\infty}\Omega
. Then for every
z_{1},z_{2}\in\Omega

\omega(z_{1},E,\Omega)\leq e^{2k_{\Omega}(z_{1},z_{2})}\omega(z_{2},E,\Omega).

Let us introduce some notation. Given
p_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
r_{0}>0
, and
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
, we define the half-lines

(3.4)

\ell_{p_{0},r_{0}}(\theta):=\{p_{0}+re^{i\theta}:r\geq r_{0}\}.

If
r_{0}=0
, we simply write
\ell_{p_{0}}(\theta)
and
\ell(\theta):=\ell_{0}(\theta)
.

We prove some technical lemmas involving harmonic measure which will be needed later in the discussion.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,\pi/2)
and
\beta\in[\pi/2,\pi)
such that
\alpha+\beta\leq\pi

\theta=(\alpha+\beta)/2
and

,
r>0

for all
r>0
such that

.

Proof.

Consider the Riemann map

given by

E_{t}=\left\{z\in\partial\widetilde{S}(\alpha,\beta):\ |z|>\frac{t}{\cos(\alpha)}\right\}\cdot

Therefore,

F_{t}:=F(E_{t})=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:x>\left(\frac{t}{\cos(\alpha)}\right)^{\pi/\varphi}\right\}\cdot

Now, since

\cos(\alpha)=\cos(\arctan(\psi(t)/t))=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(t)^{2}}{t^{2}}+1}}=\frac{t}{|z_{t}|}\cdot

Hence,

Then, if

\displaystyle\omega(z,E_{t},S_{0}(\alpha,\beta))=\omega(F(z),F_{t},{\mathbb{H}})=\omega(i,G_{t},{\mathbb{H}})=\arctan\left(\frac{2T}{T^{2}-1}\right),

Take now

∎

4.
General case: main results

In order to exploit the connection between harmonic measure and the
H^{p}
-norm from Corollary
3.3
, we will study Koenigs domains contained in a sector. More specifically, in this section, we introduce some geometric concepts related to the domain
\Omega
. Using these, we provide sufficient conditions for membership of
e^{\lambda h}
to
H^{p}
.

We start by introducing the following definition:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a parabolic Koenigs domain such that
0\in\Omega
. Assume that

\Omega\subseteq S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}),
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\in[0,\pi]
, and
0<\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}\leq\pi
. We define the
outer lower maximal angle

\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}
of
\Omega
as

\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}:=\inf\{\alpha\in[0,\pi]:\ \textnormal{there exists }p\in{\mathbb{R}}\textnormal{ such that }\Omega\subseteq p+e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\alpha\right)}{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\}.

The
outer upper maximal angle

\beta_{\Omega}^{o}
of
\Omega
is given by

\beta_{\Omega}^{o}:=\inf\{\alpha\in[0,\pi]:\ \textnormal{there exists }p\in{\mathbb{R}}\textnormal{ such that }\Omega\subseteq p+e^{-i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta\right)}{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\}.

Note that both quantities are well defined and satisfy
\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}\in[0,\alpha_{0})
,
\beta_{\Omega}^{o}\in[0,\beta_{0}).
In particular,
0\leq\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}\leq\pi
. Observe that the contention
\Omega\subseteq S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
is equivalent to

\Omega\subseteq(p+e^{-i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\beta_{0}\right)}{\mathbb{C}}_{+})\cap(p+e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\alpha_{0}\right)}{\mathbb{C}}_{+}).

Likewise,

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a parabolic Koenigs domain with
0\in\Omega
. Assume that

S_{p}(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\subseteq\Omega,
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\in[0,\pi)
,
0<\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}<\pi
. We define the
inner lower maximal angle

\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}
of
\Omega
as

\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}:=\sup(\{0\}\cup\{\alpha\in[0,\pi]:\textnormal{there exists }p\in{\mathbb{R}}\cap\Omega\textnormal{ such that }S_{p}(\alpha,0)\subseteq\Omega\}).

The
inner upper maximal angle

\beta_{\Omega}^{i}
of
\Omega
is given by

\beta_{\Omega}^{i}:=\sup(\{0\}\cup\{\beta\in[0,\pi]:\textnormal{there exists }p\in{\mathbb{R}}\cap\Omega\textnormal{ such that }S_{p}(0,\beta)\subseteq\Omega\}).

On the other hand, if
\Omega
does not contain any angular sector, we define
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}=\beta_{\Omega}^{i}=0
.

Observe that the contention
S_{p}(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\subseteq\Omega
is equivalent to

S_{p}(\alpha_{1},0)\cup S_{p}(0,\beta_{1})\subseteq\Omega.
Again,
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}
and
\beta_{\Omega}^{i}
are well defined. Moreover, under the assumption of Definition
4.1
, they satisfy
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}\in[0,\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}],

\beta_{\Omega}^{i}\in[0,\beta_{\Omega}^{o}].
In particular,
0\leq\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\beta_{\Omega}^{i}\leq\pi
.

It is not difficult to construct examples of Koenigs domains
\Omega
with prescribed maximal angles

.

Consider

Theorem 4.4

Let

Definition 4.6
.

The
starlike at infinity hull
of a domain

, denoted by
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(G)
, is defined as

\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(G)=\bigcup_{t\geq 0}(G+t).

Observe that
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(G)
is the smallest starlike at infinity domain containing
G
. If
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(G)\neq{\mathbb{C}}
, then
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(G)
is a Koenigs domain.

Now, let
\Omega
a parabolic Koenigs domain,
0\in\Omega
, such that
\Omega\subseteq S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{0}\in[0,\pi),\beta_{0}\in[0,\pi)
. Also, let
\alpha_{\Omega}^{o},\beta_{\Omega}^{o},\alpha_{\Omega}^{i},\beta_{\Omega}^{i}
be the outer and inner maximal angles of
\Omega
, respectively. For each
\sigma\in[\alpha_{\Omega}^{i},\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}]
and
\nu\in[\beta_{\Omega}^{i},\beta_{\Omega}^{o}]
consider
\Omega_{\sigma}=e^{i\sigma}\Omega
and
\Omega_{\nu}=e^{-i\nu}\Omega
. Then,
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(\Omega_{\sigma})
and
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(\Omega_{\nu})
are parabolic Koenigs domains with defining functions

\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}:I_{\sigma}\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty)
and
\psi_{\Omega_{\nu}}:I_{\nu}\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty)
, respectively.

Remark 4.7
.

The defining functions
\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}
and
\psi_{\Omega_{\nu}}
introduced above can be geometrically described in terms of
\Omega
. We discuss the construction of
\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}
, the one for
\psi_{\Omega_{\nu}}
being identical. Consider
\Omega_{\sigma}=e^{i\sigma}\Omega
and define
I_{\sigma}=\{\Impart(z)\colon z\in\Omega_{\sigma}\}.
Now, for each
y\in I_{\sigma}

L_{y}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\colon\mathrm{Im}(z)=y\}.
It is clear that
L_{y}
intersects
\Omega_{\sigma}
. Consider now
\kappa_{\Omega}(y)=\inf\{\PR(z)\colon z\in L_{y}\cap\Omega_{\sigma}\}
. It is straightforward to check that
\kappa_{\Omega}
coincides with the defining function
\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}
of
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(\Omega_{\sigma})
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Suppose that
\Omega\subseteq S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{0}\in[0,\pi),\beta_{0}\in[0,\pi)
. Assume further that
\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}>0
.

(i)

Let
\sigma\in[\alpha_{\Omega}^{i},\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}]
and
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\sigma\right)}
. Then,
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
if one of the following holds:

(a)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}})\neq{\mathbb{R}}
.

(b)

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.3)

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}dt<\infty.

(ii)

Let
\nu\in[\beta_{\Omega}^{i},\beta_{\Omega}^{o}]
and
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\nu\right)}
. Then,
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
if one of the following holds:

(a)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\nu}})\neq{\mathbb{R}}
.

(b)

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega_{\nu}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\nu+\varepsilon}}dt<\infty.

Remark 4.11
.

Recall that the real number
t_{0}
appearing in the previous result is the one provided in Proposition
4.8
. By the properties stated therein, observe that
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)
and
\psi_{\Omega_{b}}(t)
are bounded below by some
\delta>0
for all
t\geq t_{0}
, so the convergence behaviour of the integrals depends only on the growth of the functions
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)
or
\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}(t)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
4.10
. We are showing
(i)
, the proof for
(ii)
is analogous.

If
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}})\neq{\mathbb{R}}
, then by Proposition
4.8
,
\sigma=\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}
and
\Omega
is contained in a sector
S_{p}(\alpha_{\Omega}^{o},\beta)
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\beta\in[\beta_{\Omega}^{i},\pi).
Then,
\lambda\Omega
is contained in a left half-plane and Proposition
2.6
yields that
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
, as desired.

Assume now that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}})={\mathbb{R}}
. First, recall that, by hypothesis, we can apply Corollary
3.3
to obtain

\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{p}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\omega(0,E_{t},\Omega_{\theta})dt,

where
E_{t}=\{\xi\in\partial\Omega_{\theta}:\PR(\xi)>t\}
and
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}+\sigma
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
be chosen such that
0<\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon<\pi
and take
t\geq t_{0}
, for some
t_{0}
large enough. Observe that

\inf\{\Impart(\xi):\xi\in\Omega_{\theta},\ \PR(\xi)=t\}=\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t).

By the definition of
\beta_{\Omega}^{o}
, there exists
p\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\ell_{p}(\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon)\cap\Omega=\emptyset
(see (
3.4
)), which implies that
\ell_{-iy_{0}}(\frac{\pi}{2}+\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon)\cap\Omega_{\theta}=\emptyset
for some
y_{0}>0
. Now, consider the set

S=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}S(0,\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon).

Define also
S_{t}:=\zeta_{t}+S
, where

\zeta_{t}=t-i(y_{0}+t\tan(\tilde{\theta}))\qquad(t\geq t_{0}),

with

\tilde{\theta}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\sigma+\beta^{o}_{\Omega}+\varepsilon&\textnormal{if }\sigma+\beta^{o}_{\Omega}+\varepsilon<\pi/2,\\
\varepsilon&\textnormal{if }\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon\geq\pi/2.\end{matrix}\right.

Observe that
\partial S_{t}=\ell_{\zeta_{t}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\cup\ell_{\zeta_{t}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon\right)
. By construction, the second half-line does not intersect
\Omega_{\theta}
, while the first one may intersect in infinitely many points. Notice also that the infimum of the imaginary parts of these intersection points is precisely
\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)
.

Now, define
F_{t}=\{t+iy\in\partial S_{t}:y>\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)\}
for all
t\geq t_{0}
and observe that
\partial(S_{t}\cap\Omega_{\theta})\subseteq F_{t}\cup(\partial\Omega_{\theta}\setminus E_{t}).
As a consequence, applying the Maximum Principle, we deduce that

(4.4)

\omega(0,E_{t},\Omega_{\theta})\leq\omega(0,F_{t},S_{t})

for all
t\geq t_{0}
. By the translation invariance of the harmonic measure, we have that

\omega(0,F_{t},S_{t})=\omega(-\zeta_{t},F_{t}-\zeta_{t},S),

where
F_{t}-\zeta_{t}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\PR(z)=0,\mathrm{Im}(z)>\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)+y_{0}+\tan(\tilde{\theta})\}.
Then, applying Corollary
3.7
, we have that, for
t\geq t_{0}
,

\omega(0,F_{t},S_{t})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{2T_{t}}{T_{t}^{2}-1}\right)

for all
T_{t}>1
, where

T_{t}=\frac{(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)+y_{0}+\tan(\tilde{\theta}))^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}-|\zeta_{t}|^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}\cos(\kappa_{t})}{|\zeta_{t}|^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}\sin(\kappa_{t})},

and
\kappa_{t}=(\arg(-\zeta_{t})-\frac{\pi}{2})\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}
. Recall that
|\zeta_{t}|=t\sqrt{1+(y_{0}/t+\tan(\tilde{\theta}))^{2}}
and that

k_{t}\rightarrow(\frac{\pi}{2}-\tilde{\theta})\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon},\quad\text{as }t\rightarrow+\infty.

As a consequence, for all
t\geq t_{1}

T_{t}\gtrsim\left(\frac{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}{t}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}+\varepsilon}},

where
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
. Moreover, there exists
c>0
such that
T_{t}>1
for all
t\geq t_{1}
satisfying
\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)\geq ct
. Thus, denoting

A=\{x\in[t_{1},+\infty):\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-x)\geq cx\}\subset[t_{1},+\infty),

we have

\begin{split}&\int_{A}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}dt+\int_{[t_{1},+\infty)\setminus A}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}dt\\
&=\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}dt<\infty.\end{split}

Since the second integral on
[t_{1},+\infty)\setminus A
is finite, we have that

+\infty>\int_{[t_{1},+\infty]\setminus A}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}\geq\frac{1}{c^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}}\int_{[t_{1},+\infty]\setminus A}e^{p|\lambda|t}dt.

Recalling that the harmonic measure is a probability measure and using (
4.4
), there exists
C>0
such that

\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq C+\int_{A}e^{p|\lambda|t}\omega(0,F_{t},S_{t})dt+\int_{[t_{1},+\infty)\setminus A}e^{p|\lambda|t}dt.

Hence,
\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert<\infty
if

(4.5)

\int_{A}e^{p|\lambda|t}\omega(0,F_{t},S_{t})dt<+\infty.

Therefore, it remains to show that the previous integral converges. Recall that
T_{t}>1
for all
t\in A
, so we have that

\omega(0,F_{t},S_{t})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{2T_{t}}{T_{t}^{2}-1}\right)\cdot

Noticing that
\arctan(x)\leq x
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have for all
t\geq t_{1}
,

\omega(0,F_{t},S_{t})\lesssim\frac{1}{T_{t}}\lesssim\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega_{\sigma}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\sigma+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}.

Using this in (
4.5
) and the applying the hypothesis, we conclude that the integral in (
4.5
) is indeed finite. This concludes the proof.
\Box

The next result establishes some geometric conditions on
\Omega
for which it is possible to completely characterize the point spectrum of the infinitesimal generator
\Delta
. In order to ease the reading, set
\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}:=\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}}
and
\psi_{\beta_{\Omega}^{i}}:=\psi_{\Omega_{\beta_{\Omega}^{i}}}

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t>0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Suppose that
\Omega\subseteq S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{0}\in[0,\pi)
, and
\beta_{0}\in[0,\pi)
. Assume that
\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}>0
and

(4.6)

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}}}\ <\infty\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\beta_{\Omega}^{i}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\beta_{\Omega}^{i}}}<\infty

for all
|\lambda|>0
. Then,

{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}=\left\{re^{i\theta}:r\geq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}\leq\theta\leq\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}^{i}\right\}.

Proof.

First, assume that
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}\right)}
and
\mu=|\mu|e^{i\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}+\beta_{\Omega}^{i}\right)}
belong to
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for all
|\lambda|,|\mu|>0
and
|\mu|>0
. In such a case, Theorem
4.4
and Proposition
4.5
give the result.

Thus, it remains to show that
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}\right)}\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
; the proof for
\mu
being identical. Let
|\lambda_{0}|>0
such that

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda_{0}|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}}}\ <\infty.

We are showing that
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
, provided that
|\lambda|<|\lambda_{0}|
, which together with (
4.6
) will yield the result. By Theorem
4.10
, it is enough to find
\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.7)

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}+\varepsilon}}dt<\infty.

Write
|\lambda|=|\lambda_{0}|-\delta
,
\delta>0
. Set
\varphi=\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}
, and define

A:=\{t\in[t_{0},+\infty):\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)\geq e^{\frac{p|\lambda_{0}|\varphi}{\pi}t}\};

B:=\{t\in[t_{0},+\infty):\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)<e^{\frac{p|\lambda_{0}|\varphi}{\pi}t}\}.

Observe that
[t_{0},+\infty)=A\sqcup B.
By the definition of the set
A
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{A}e^{p|\lambda|t}\frac{t^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}}dt&\leq\int_{A}e^{p|\lambda|t}\frac{t^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}}{e^{\frac{p|\lambda_{0}|\varphi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}t}}dt.\end{split}

Now, since
\frac{p|\lambda_{0}|\varphi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}\rightarrow p|\lambda_{0}|>p|\lambda|
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, there exists some
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that the previous integral is convergent.

Hence, in order to deduce (
4.7
), it remains to show that

\int_{B}e^{p|\lambda|t}\frac{t^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}}dt<\infty,

for some
\varepsilon>0
. To this end, for each
\varepsilon>0
, write
\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}=\frac{\pi}{\varphi}-\varepsilon^{\prime}
for some
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. Using this and the definition of the set
B
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{B}e^{p|\lambda|t}\frac{t^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}}dt=\int_{B}e^{p|\lambda_{0}|t}e^{-p\delta t}\frac{t^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi}}}dt\leq\int_{B}e^{p|\lambda_{0}|t}e^{-p\delta t}\frac{t^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}e^{\frac{p|\lambda|\varphi\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\pi}t}}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi}}}dt.\end{split}

At this point, choose
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
such that
\frac{p|\lambda|\varphi\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\pi}<\frac{\delta}{2},
and notice that this choice fixes
\varepsilon>0
. Now, there exists
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
such that
t^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}\leq e^{\frac{\delta}{2}t}
, for all
t\geq t_{1}
. Thus, one has, for some
C>0
,

\int_{B}e^{p|\lambda_{0}|t}e^{-p\delta t}\frac{t^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi+\varepsilon}}e^{\frac{p|\lambda|\varphi\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\pi}t}}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi}}}dt\leq C+\int_{B\cap[t_{1},+\infty)}\frac{e^{p|\lambda_{0}|t}}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\varphi}}}dt,

which is finite by hypothesis. This shows that
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
, and finishes the proof.
∎

Notice that Corollary
4.12
completely characterizes
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for those Koenigs domains
\Omega
with associated defining functions
\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}
and
\psi_{\beta_{\Omega}^{i}}
that grow rapidly enough. Indeed, if the following conditions are satisfied

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{at}}{\psi_{\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}}(-t)}=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{at}}{\psi_{\beta_{\Omega}^{i}}(t)}=0

for all
a>0
, the result holds.

4.2.
Case II:
\bm{\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}=0}

The next result arises as an analogue to Theorem
4.10
for this setting:

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t>0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Suppose that
\Omega\subseteq S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{0}\in[0,\pi),
and
\beta_{0}\in[0,\pi)
. Suppose further that
\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}=0
.

(i)

Let
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}
. Then,
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
if one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})=(a,+\infty)
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(b)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})=(-\infty,b)
for some
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}{t}}}dt<\infty.

(c)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})={\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\widetilde{\psi_{\Omega}}(t)}{t}}}dt<\infty,

where
\widetilde{\psi_{\Omega}}(t):=\min\{\psi(-t),\psi(t)\}
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(ii)

Let
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\frac{3\pi}{2}}
. Then,
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
if one of the following conditions holds:

(a)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})=(-\infty,b)
for some
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

(b)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})=(a,+\infty)
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(t)}{t}}}dt<\infty.

(c)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})={\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{\Omega}(t)}{t}}}dt<\infty,

where
\widetilde{\psi_{\Omega}}(t):=\min\{\psi(-t),\psi(t)\}
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

As usual, we prove
(i)
. The proof of
(ii)
is analogous.

First, observe that, if
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})=(a,+\infty)
, an identical argument as the one showed in the beginning of the proof of Theorem
4.10
, along with Proposition
4.9
, yields that
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
.

Now, assume that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})=(-\infty,b)
, and consider
\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}\Omega.
In such a case, by Corollary
3.3

\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert^{p}\lesssim C+\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\omega(0,E_{t},\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}})dt,

where
E_{t}=\{\xi\in\partial\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}:\PR(\xi)>t\}
and
C>0
.

By the assumption on
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})
,
\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}
is contained in the half-plane
b+{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
. Since
0\in\Omega,
we have that
b<0
. Now, define the following vertical strip

B_{t}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\PR(z)|<t\},\quad t\geq t_{0}.

Define also

F_{t}^{+}:=\{t+ix:x>\psi_{\Omega}(-t)\}\quad\text{and}\text{\quad}F_{t}^{-}:=\{-t+ix:x>\psi_{\Omega}(-t)\}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that for all
t\geq t_{0}
,
F_{t}^{-}
is contained in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(b+{\mathbb{C}}_{+}).
Now, for all
t\geq t_{0}
, by the Maximum Principle, it follows that

\omega(0,E_{t},\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}})\leq\omega(0,F^{+}_{t},B_{t})

for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Since
F_{t}^{-}
is the reflexion of
F_{t}^{+}
with respect the imaginary axis, and
B_{t}
is symmetric with respect it, we have that

\omega(0,F^{+}_{t},B_{t})=\frac{1}{2}\omega(0,F^{+}_{t}\cup F^{-}_{t},B_{t}).

At this point, it is enough to apply Proposition
3.8
to deduce the result.

It remains to prove the case
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})={\mathbb{R}}
. In order to perform a similar construction as the one carried out above, we need to symmetrize the rotated domain
\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}
. First, observe that the function
\widetilde{\psi_{\Omega}}
is the minimum of two upper semicontinuous functions, so it is also upper semicontinuous. Then,
\widetilde{\psi_{\Omega}}
defines a parabolic Koenigs domain
\widetilde{\Omega}
, with Koenigs function
\widetilde{h}
, that is symmetric with respect the real axis and satisfies

\Omega\subset\widetilde{\Omega}.

Now,
\varphi=\widetilde{h}^{-1}\circ h
is a holomorphic self-map of
{\mathbb{D}}
, so the associated composition operator
C_{\varphi}
is bounded on
H^{p}
. Hence, we have

\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert=\left\lvert\left\lvert C_{\varphi}e^{\lambda\widetilde{h}}\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\left\lvert C_{\varphi}\right\rvert\right\rvert\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda\widetilde{h}}\right\rvert\right\rvert.

Now, again by Corollary
3.3

\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda\widetilde{h}}\right\rvert\right\rvert^{p}\lesssim C+\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\omega(0,E_{t}^{+},\widetilde{\Omega}_{\frac{\pi}{2}})dt,

where
E_{t}^{+}=\{\xi\in\partial\widetilde{\Omega}_{\frac{\pi}{2}}:\PR(\xi)>t\}
and
C>0
. Likewise, define

E_{t}^{-}=\{\xi\in\partial\widetilde{\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}}:\PR(\xi)<-t\}

and observe that
E_{t}^{-}
is the reflection with respect the imaginary axis of
E_{t}^{+}
.

At this point, we may consider again
B_{t}
as defined above, and the boundary subsets

\widetilde{F^{+}_{t}}:=\{t+ix:x>\widetilde{\psi_{\Omega}}(t)\};\quad\widetilde{F^{-}_{t}}:=\{-t+ix:x>\widetilde{\psi_{\Omega}}(t)\}.

Now, since
\widetilde{\Omega}
is symmetric with respect the imaginary axis, one has for all
t\geq t_{0}

\omega(0,E^{+}_{t},\widetilde{\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}})=\frac{1}{2}\omega(0,E^{+}_{t}\cup E_{t}^{-},\widetilde{\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}}).

By the Maximum Principle

\omega(0,E^{+}_{t}\cup E_{t}^{-},\widetilde{\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}})\leq\omega(0,\widetilde{F^{+}_{t}}\cup\widetilde{F_{t}^{-}},B_{t})

for all
t\geq t_{0}
, and again an application of Proposition
3.8
yields the result.
∎

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t>0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Suppose that
\Omega\subseteq S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{0}\in[0,\pi)
, and
\beta_{0}\in[0,\pi)
. Suppose further that
\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}+\beta_{\Omega}^{o}=0
. Assume one of the following three conditions holds:

(i)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})=(a,+\infty)
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(t)}{t}}}dt<\infty\quad\text{for all $|\lambda|>0$.}

(ii)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})=(-\infty,b)
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}{t}}}dt<\infty\quad\text{for all $|\lambda|>0$.}

(iii)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})={\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\widetilde{\psi_{\Omega}}(t)}{t}}}dt<\infty\quad\text{for all $|\lambda|>0$.}

Then,

{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}=\left\{re^{i\theta}:r\geq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\theta\leq\frac{3\pi}{2}\right\}.

Proof.

The result follows as a consequence of both Theorem
4.4
and Theorem
4.13
.
∎

Again, Corollary
4.14
may be applied for defining functions
\psi_{\Omega}
that grow fast enough. For instance, by assuming that there exists
\gamma>2
such that
\psi_{\Omega}(t)\geq C|t|^{\gamma}
and all
|t|\geq t_{0}
, the result holds.

5.
On convex parabolic Koenigs domains

In this section, we consider the case of convex Koenigs domains. As we will see, this additional geometric assumption will provide better properties of both the maximal angles of
\Omega
and their associated defining functions. These properties will play an important role in improving the results obtained in Section
4
.

5.1.
On geometric aspects of Koenigs domains

Let us begin with a preliminary observation. Let
\Omega
be a Koenigs domain. Since
\Omega\neq{\mathbb{C}}
, the convexity yields that
\Omega
is contained in a half-plane. Thus, the convexity hypothesis implicitly carries the existence of
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\in[0,\pi]
with
0<\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}\leq\pi
such that
\Omega\subset S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
.

Our first technical result provides an easy method to look for angular sectors contained in
\Omega
in terms of the containment of half-lines:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex parabolic Koenigs domain and let
z_{0}\in\Omega
. Then,
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)\subset\Omega
with
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\pi],

0<\alpha+\beta\leq\pi
if and only if the half-lines
\ell_{z_{0}}(-\alpha)
and
\ell_{z_{0}}(\beta)
are contained in
\Omega.

Proof.

Obviously, if both half-lines are contained in
\Omega
, its convex hull contains
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta),
which will be then contained in
\Omega.
For the reverse implication, we are showing that
\ell_{z_{0}}(\beta)\subset\Omega
, the case of
\alpha
is identical. Since
z_{0}\in\Omega
, there exists
z_{1}\in\Omega
with
\mathrm{Re}(z_{1})<\mathrm{Re}(z_{0})
. Then, it is enough to consider the convex hull of
\{z_{1}\}\cup S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
and notice that it contains the desired half-line. Finally, by convexity, it is also contained in
\Omega,
as desired.
∎

Recall that, in the hypothesis of the previous lemma, we asked
\alpha+\beta\leq\pi
. It is straightforward to check that if
\Omega
contains a half-plane, then
\Omega
is itself a half-plane. Moreover, if
\Omega
contains an angular sector of opening angle
\alpha+\beta>\pi
, then
\Omega={\mathbb{C}}
, which is not possible if
\Omega
is a Koenigs domain.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex parabolic Koenigs domain. If
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)\subset\Omega
for some
z_{0}\in\Omega
and
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\pi],

0<\alpha+\beta\leq\pi
, then
S_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\subset\Omega
for all
z\in\Omega
.

Proof.

We are showing that the line
\ell_{z}(\beta)
is contained in
\Omega,
the proof for
\alpha
is identical. Moreover, we assume that
\beta\in[0,\pi/2],
the case
\beta\in(\pi/2,\pi]
is done analogously. If
\mathrm{Re}(z)=\mathrm{Re}(z_{0})
, then
z\in S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta),
and clearly

S_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\subset S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)\subset\Omega.

If
\mathrm{Re}(z)\not=\mathrm{Re}(z_{0})
, we choose
z_{1}\in\Omega
such that
\ell_{z}(\beta)
is contained in the convex hull of
z_{1}
and
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta)
, which will finish the proof. If
\mathrm{Re}(z)>\mathrm{Re}(z_{0})
and
\Impart(z)>\Impart(z_{0})
, then we choose
z_{1}
with
\PR(z_{1})=\PR(z)
and
\Impart(z_{1})>\Impart(z).
The remaining choices for
z_{1}
are equivalent taking into account the real and imaginary parts of
z
and
z_{0}
, and the proof is done.
∎

Due to the previous result, we may choose a highlighted point to center all the angular sectors contained in
\Omega
. As usual, we will assume without loss of generality that
0\in\Omega
, so we will only have to study whenever the angular sector
S(\alpha,\beta)
is contained in
\Omega
.

At this point, we are prepared to deal with the maximal angles of
\Omega
:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex parabolic Koenigs domain with
0\in\Omega
. Then

\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}=\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}\qquad\beta_{\Omega}^{i}=\beta_{\Omega}^{o}.

Proof.

First, assume that
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\beta_{\Omega}^{i}>0
. We are showing that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
z_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\Omega\subset S_{z_{\varepsilon}}(\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon,\beta_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon),

which implies that
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}=\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}
and
\beta_{\Omega}^{i}=\beta_{\Omega}^{o}
. Now, by convexity,
\Omega
is contained in a half-plane. Hence, by rotating and translating, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\Omega
is contained in
{\mathbb{C}}_{-1}
and
0\in\Omega
, so
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i},\beta_{\Omega}^{i}\in[0,\pi/2]
. Now, let
\varepsilon>0
, and fix
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\PR(z_{\varepsilon})<-1,

\mathrm{Im}(z_{\varepsilon})=0
. It is clear that
z_{\varepsilon}\notin\Omega.
Thus, to get that
\Omega\subset S_{z_{\varepsilon}}(\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon,\beta_{\Omega}^{i},\varepsilon)
is it enough to achieve that the half-lines
\ell_{z_{\varepsilon}}(\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon)
and
\ell_{z_{\varepsilon}}(\beta_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon)
do not intersect
\Omega
. We are reasoning for
\beta_{\Omega}^{i}
, the argument for
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}
is identical. Obviously, if
\ell_{z_{0}}(\beta_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon)
does not intersect
\Omega
, we let
z_{\varepsilon}=z_{0}
and the proof is done. On the other hand, if
\ell_{z_{0}}(\beta_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon)\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
, observe that
\ell_{z_{0}}(\beta_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon)\cap\Omega
is connected, due to the convexity of
\Omega
. If such an intersection is bounded, then there exists
z_{\varepsilon}<z_{0}
such that
\ell_{z_{\varepsilon}}(\beta_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon)\cap\Omega=\emptyset
, and we would be done. On the other hand, if
\ell_{z_{\varepsilon}}(\beta_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon)\cap\Omega
is not bounded, then there exists
z_{1}\in\Omega
such that
\ell_{z_{1}}(\beta_{\Omega}^{i}+\varepsilon)\subset\Omega
. By Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
this fact contradicts the maximality of
\beta_{\Omega}^{i}
, so we are done.

Now, assume that
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}+\beta_{\Omega}^{i}=0
. This implies that
\Omega
does not contain any angular sector. In this case, we have to prove
\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}=\beta_{\Omega}^{o}=0
. It is enough to show that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
z_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\Omega\subset S_{z_{\varepsilon}}(\varepsilon).

Finally, the proof is analogous to the one above, applying in this situation that
\Omega
does not contain any angular sectors instead of the maximality of
\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}
and
\beta_{\Omega}^{i}
.
∎

As a consequence of the previous result, for the rest of the manuscript, if
\Omega
is a convex parabolic Koenigs domain, we will denote

\alpha_{\Omega}:=\alpha_{\Omega}^{i}=\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}\qquad\beta_{\Omega}:=\beta_{\Omega}^{i}=\beta_{\Omega}^{0},

and will call them the
lower maximal angle
and the
upper maximal angle
, respectively. A direct consequence of the previous discussion is the following result:

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex parabolic Koenigs domain with
0\in\Omega
.

(i)

If
\Omega
contains an angular sector (that is,
\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}>0
), then

S(\alpha_{\Omega},\beta_{\Omega})\subseteq\Omega.

Furthermore, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
p_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\Omega\subseteq S_{p_{\varepsilon}}(\alpha_{\Omega}+\varepsilon,\beta_{\Omega}+\varepsilon).

(ii)

If
\Omega
does not contain any angular sector (that is,
\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}=0
), for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
p_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\Omega\subseteq S_{p_{\varepsilon}}(\varepsilon).

At this point, we can establish the corresponding version of Theorem
4.4
in this setting:

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t>0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
with convex Koenigs domain, and let
\alpha_{\Omega}
and
\beta_{\Omega}
be its maximal angles. Then

\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\supseteq\left\{re^{i\theta}:r\geq 0,\ \frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}<\theta<\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}\right\}

and

\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\subseteq\left\{re^{i\theta}:r\geq 0,\ \frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\leq\theta\leq\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}\right\}.

In particular, if
\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}=\pi
, then

\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})=\ell\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right).

Proof.

The result is directly derived from Theorem
4.4
and Proposition
5.3
.
∎

As in Section
4
, we now turn our attention to studying the membership to
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
of those
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
not covered by the previous results. That is,
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}
and
\mu=|\mu|e^{i\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}\right)}
.

To that purpose, we consider the associated defining functions of
\Omega_{\alpha_{\Omega}}=e^{i\alpha_{\Omega}}\Omega
and
\Omega_{\beta_{\Omega}}=e^{-i\beta_{\Omega}}\Omega
, which will enjoy specific properties derived from the convexity of
\Omega
. For the sake of simplicity, we will denote by
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}:=\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha_{\Omega}}}
and
\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}:=\psi_{\Omega_{\beta_{\Omega}}}
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t>0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
with convex Koenigs domain
\Omega
. Assume that
\Omega
contains an angular sector and
0<\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}<\pi
. Then,

(i)

\Omega_{\alpha_{\Omega}}
and
\Omega_{\beta_{\Omega}}
are convex parabolic Koenigs domains with continuous and convex defining functions
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}
and
\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}
, respectively.

(ii)

There exists
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
[a,+\infty)\subset\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})
. Moreover,
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})={\mathbb{R}}
if and only if
\Omega
is not contained in any sector
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha_{\Omega},\beta)
, with
p_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\beta\in[\beta_{\Omega},\pi)
. In such a case, there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}
is strictly non-increasing in
(-\infty,-t_{0}]
,
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t_{0})>0
, and

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}{t}=+\infty.

(iii)

There exists
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
(-\infty,b]\subset\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}})
. Moreover,
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}})={\mathbb{R}}
if and only if
\Omega
is not contained in any sector
S_{p_{0}}(\alpha,\beta_{\Omega})
with
p_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\alpha\in[0,\pi)
. In such a case, there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}
is strictly increasing in
[t_{0},+\infty)
,
\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}(t_{0})>0
, and

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}(t)}{t}=+\infty.

Proof.

We begin by showing
(i)
. First, Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
ensure that
\Omega_{\alpha_{\Omega}}
and
\Omega_{\beta_{\Omega}}
are starlike at infinity. So, in particular, they are Koenigs domains. This implies that
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(\Omega_{\alpha_{\Omega}})=\Omega_{\alpha_{\Omega}}
and
\textnormal{co}_{\infty}(\Omega_{\beta_{\Omega}})=\Omega_{\beta_{\Omega}}
, so their defining functions coincide. Now, since both domains are convex, the associated defining functions are clearly continuous and convex.

The first statement of
(ii)
follows directly from Proposition
4.8

(a)
. To conclude the proof, assume
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})={\mathbb{R}}
, and let
t_{0}>0
be the value whose existence is guaranteed by the aforementioned result. The continuity and the convexity of
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}
, together with the fact that
\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)=+\infty
, yield that
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}
is strictly non-increasing in
(-\infty,-t_{0})
. Let us finish by showing that
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}{t}=+\infty
. As a consequence of Fekete’s lemma (
[
18
, Theorem 7.6.1]
), we have that

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}{t}\in[0,+\infty].

Note that such limit must diverge since its finiteness would contradict the fact that
\alpha_{\Omega}
is the lower maximal limit. The proof of
(iii)
is done in a similar fashion.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Regarding the case in which
\Omega
does not contain any angular sector (that is,
\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}=0
), the properties described in Proposition
4.9
will be enough for our purposes, noticing that
\psi_{\Omega}
is also continuous and convex in this case.

In order to present our main results in the convex case, we distinguish whether
\Omega
contains an angular sector or not.

5.2.
Containing a sector: a complete characterization

In this setting, we are able to provide lower bounds for
\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert
, which, together with Theorem
5.8
, will provide the desired characterization of
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
:

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
with convex Koenigs domain
\Omega
. Assume that
\Omega
contains an angular sector and
0<\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}<\pi
.

(i)

Let
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}
. Then,
\lambda\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
provided that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})={\mathbb{R}}
and

(5.1)

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty.

(ii)

Let
\mu=|\mu|e^{i\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}\right)}.
Then,
\mu\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
provided that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}})={\mathbb{R}}
and

(5.2)

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\mu|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty.

Proof.

As usual, we just prove
(i)
. Assume that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})={\mathbb{R}}
. Given
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}
, our goal is to find constants
C_{1},t^{\prime}
such that

(5.3)

\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{p}}\gtrsim C_{1}+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt,

which, by hypothesis (
5.1
), will diverge and
\lambda
will fail to belong to the point spectrum, as desired. By (
3.3
), it is enough to obtain a proper lower bound for

\omega(0,E_{t},\Omega_{\sigma}),

where
\sigma=\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}
,
\Omega_{\sigma}=e^{i\sigma}\Omega
and
E_{t}=\{\xi\in\Omega_{\sigma}:\mathrm{Re}(\xi)>t\}
.

Let
t_{0}
be as in Proposition
5.6
. For all
t\geq t_{0}
, define
\theta_{t}=\arctan\left(\frac{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}{t}\right).
Now, consider the rotated angular sectors

S_{t}=e^{i\theta_{t}}S(0,\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}).

We have
\partial S_{t}=\ell(\theta_{t})\cup\ell(\alpha_{0}+\beta_{\Omega}+\theta_{t}).
By Proposition
5.6

(ii)
,
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}{t}=+\infty
, then
\theta_{t}\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. So there exists
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
such that
\ell(\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}+\theta_{t})\subset\Omega_{\sigma}
for all
t\geq t_{1}.
On the other hand,
\ell(\theta_{t})\cap\partial\Omega_{\sigma}=\{z_{t}\},
where
z_{t}=t+i\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)
and

\ell(\theta_{t})\cap({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega_{\sigma})=F_{t}=\{\xi\in\partial S_{t}:\PR(\xi)>t\}.

Now, for each
t\geq t_{1}
, define
\zeta_{t}=e^{i(\theta_{t}+\frac{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}{2})}
, and note that it is the point of modulus
1
in the bisector of
S_{t}
. Clearly, there exists a compact subset
K\subset\Omega_{\sigma}
such that
\zeta_{t}\in K
for all
t\geq t_{1}
, so by Theorem
3.4
and the continuity of the hyperbolic distance, there exists a constant
C>0
(not depending on
t
) such that

\omega(0,E_{t},\Omega_{\sigma})\geq C\omega(\zeta_{t},E_{t},\Omega_{\sigma}).

At this point, we can apply the maximum modulus principle so that

\omega(\zeta_{t},E_{t},\Omega_{\sigma})\geq\omega(\zeta_{t},F_{t},S_{t}).

Moreover, by the election of
\zeta_{t}
, we can apply the second part of Corollary
3.6
to obtain, by (
3.7
), that

\omega(\zeta_{t},F_{t},S_{t})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{2(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{2(\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega})}}}{(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{(\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega})}}-1}\right)\cdot

Now, there exists
t_{2}\geq t_{1}
such that for all
t\geq t_{2}

\begin{split}\arctan\left(\frac{2(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{2(\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega})}}}{(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{(\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega})}}-1}\right)&\gtrsim\frac{(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{2(\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega})}}}{(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{(\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega})}}-1}\\
&\gtrsim\frac{1}{(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{2(\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega})}}}\\
&\gtrsim\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}}\cdot\end{split}

This concludes the proof.
∎

In next result, as previously discussed, we are able to characterize completely the point spectrum of
\Delta
in terms of the convergence of the integrals (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), up to, at most, two points:

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
with convex Koenigs domain
\Omega
. Assume that
\Omega
contains an angular sector and that
0<\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}<\pi
.

(i)

(a)

Let
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}
. If one of the following conditions hold:

(a1)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})\neq{\mathbb{R}}
.

(a2)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})={\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt<\infty,\quad\textnormal{for all }|\lambda|>0.

Then,
\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for all
|\lambda|>0
.

(b)

Let
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}
. Assume that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})={\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\lambda|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty\quad\textnormal{for all }|\lambda|>0.

Then,
\lambda\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for any
|\lambda|>0
.

(c)

Suppose that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})={\mathbb{R}}
. Assume further that there exist
0<a<b
such that

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{pat}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt<\infty,\quad\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{pbt}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty.

Then, there exists
|\lambda_{0}|>0
such that

(c1)

\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for all
|\lambda|<|\lambda_{0}|
.

(c2)

\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for any
|\lambda|>|\lambda_{0}|
.

Moreover,

|\lambda_{0}|=\inf\left\{r>0:\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{prt}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty\right\}\in[a,b].

(ii)

(a)

Let
\mu=|\mu|e^{i\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}\right)}
. Suppose that one the following conditions holds:

(a1)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}})\neq{\mathbb{R}}

(a2)

\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}})={\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\mu|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt<\infty\quad\textnormal{for all }|\mu|>0.

Then,
\mu\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for all
|\mu|>0

(b)

Let
\mu=|\mu|e^{i\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}\right)}
. Assume that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}})={\mathbb{R}}
and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{p|\mu|t}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty\quad\textnormal{for all }|\mu|>0.

Then,
\mu\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for any
|\mu|>0
.

(c)

Suppose that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}})={\mathbb{R}}
. Assume further that there exist
0<a<b
such that

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{pat}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt<\infty,\qquad\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{pbt}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty,

Then, there exists
|\mu_{0}|>0
such that:

(c1)

\mu=|\mu|e^{i\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}\right)}\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for all
|\mu|<|\mu_{0}|
.

(c2)

\mu=|\mu|e^{i\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}\right)}\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for any
|\mu|>|\mu_{0}|
.

Moreover,

|\mu_{0}|=\inf\left\{r>0:\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{prt}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{b}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty\right\}\in[a,b].

Proof.

As usual, we prove
(i)
. Part
(a)
follows from mimicking the arguments from the proof of Corollary
5.9
, and part
(b)
is a direct consequence of Theorem
5.8
.

To prove
(c)
, recall by Lemma
2.7
that if
|\lambda|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for some
|\lambda|>0
, then the segment
\{re^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}:r\leq|\lambda|\}\subset{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
. On the other hand, if
|\mu|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for some
|\mu|>0
, then
re^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
for any
r\geq|\mu|
. Then, by Theorem
5.8
and the arguments exposed in the proof of Corollary
5.9
, it follows the existence of
|\lambda_{0}|
. It is easy to check that
|\lambda_{0}|\in[a,b]
. Finally, the equality

|\lambda_{0}|=\inf\left\{r>0:\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{prt}\left(\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)}\right)^{\frac{\pi}{\alpha_{\Omega}+\beta_{\Omega}}}dt=\infty\right\}

follows straightforwardly, and the proof is done.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

Corollary
5.9
describes all possible pictures of

under the convexity hypothesis on
\Omega

(or
\psi_{\Omega_{\beta}})

or
(c)

to

|\lambda_{0}|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}

). Nonetheless, it is easy to provide examples (
\psi_{\Omega_{\alpha}}(-t)=te^{t}
for

) for which the computation of such integrals does not provide any further information about
|\lambda_{0}|e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\alpha_{\Omega}\right)}
.

Now, we describe some classes of functions in terms of its asymptotic behaviour that illustrates some particular instances of domains

5.3.
Not containing a sector

As in the previous case, we are able to provide necessary conditions for the membership of
\lambda
to
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
. This, combined with Theorem
4.13
, will allow us to completely characterize
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
in a broad range of situations:

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
with convex Koenigs domain
\Omega
, and assume
\Omega
does not contain any angular sector.

(i)

Let
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}
. Suppose that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})\in\{{\mathbb{R}},(-\infty,b)\}
,with
b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\pi\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}{t}\log\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}}dt=\infty.

Then,
\lambda\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
.

(ii)

Let
\mu=|\mu|e^{i\frac{3\pi}{2}}
. Suppose that
\textnormal{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})\in\{{\mathbb{R}},(a,+\infty)\}
, with
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\mu|t}}{e^{\pi\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(t)}{t}\log\psi_{\Omega}(t)}}dt=\infty.

Then,
\mu\notin{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
.

Proof.

As usual, we only show
(i)
. As in the proof of Theorem
5.8
, we want to find positive constants
C_{1},t^{\prime}
such that

(5.4)

\left\lvert\left\lvert e^{\lambda h}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{p}}\gtrsim C_{1}+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{p|\lambda|t}}{e^{\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}{t}\log\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}}dt.

To that purpose, by Corollary
3.3
, our objective is to obtain an appropriate lower bound for

\omega(0,E_{t},\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}),

where
E_{t}=\{\xi\in\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}:\PR(\xi)>t\}
. Now, since
0\in\Omega
and by the hypothesis on
\text{dom}(\psi_{\Omega})
, the half-line
\ell\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
is contained in
\Omega_{\pi/2}
. Now, for all
t\geq t_{0}
consider the angular sector given by

S_{t}:=\left\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\theta_{t}<\arg(z)<\frac{\pi}{2}\right\},

where
\theta_{t}=\arctan\left(\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}{t}\right).
Observe that, by Proposition
4.9
,
\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}{t}\rightarrow+\infty
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Hence,
\theta_{t}\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2}
, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Moreover,
\partial S_{t}=\ell\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\cup\ell(\theta_{t})
and
\partial S_{t}\cap\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}=\{z_{t}\}
, where
z_{t}=t+i\psi_{\Omega}(t)
. Now, for each
t\geq t_{0}
denote by
\zeta_{t}=e^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta_{t}\right)}
, and observe that it is the point of modulus
1
in the bisector of
S_{t}
. Then, mimicking the arguments of the proof of Theorem
5.8
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
C>0
such that

\omega(0,E_{t},\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}})\geq C\omega(\zeta_{t},E_{t},\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}}).

Now, consider
F_{t}=\{\xi\in\partial S_{t}:\PR(\xi)>t\}
and observe it coincides with
\ell(\theta_{t})\cap({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}})
. Applying the Maximum Principle, we have

\omega(\zeta_{t},E_{t},\Omega_{\frac{\pi}{2}})\geq\omega(\zeta_{t},F_{t},S_{t}).

Now, applying the second part of Corollary
3.6
, we get

\omega(\zeta_{t},F_{t},S_{t})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{2(\pi/2-\theta_{t})}}}{(t^{2}+\psi_{\Omega}(-t)^{2})^{\frac{\pi}{\pi/2-\theta_{t}}}-1}\right).

Now, taking into account that
\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}{t}\rightarrow+\infty
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, and reasoning as in the final part of the proof of Theorem
5.8
, we know that there exists
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
such that, for all
t\geq t_{1}
,

\omega(\zeta_{t},F_{t},S_{t})\gtrsim\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)^{\frac{\pi}{\pi/2-\theta_{t}}}}\cdot

Now,
\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{t}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan\left(\frac{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}{t}\right)=\arctan\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}\right)
. Recalling that
t/\psi_{\Omega}(-t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, and that
\arctan(x)\sim x
as
x\rightarrow 0
, there exists
t_{2}\geq t_{1}
such that for all
t\geq t_{2}

\arctan\left(\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}\right)\gtrsim\frac{t}{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}\cdot

Thus, for all
t\geq t_{2},

\omega(\zeta_{t},F_{t},S_{t})\gtrsim\frac{1}{\psi_{\Omega}(-t)^{\pi\frac{\psi(-t)}{t}}}=\frac{1}{e^{\pi\frac{\psi(-t)}{t}\log\psi_{\Omega}(-t)}},

and the proof is done.
∎

Remark 5.14
.

As Corollary
4.14
characterized
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
whenever
\psi(t)\geq C|t|^{\gamma}
for
\gamma>2
, the previous result also characterizes completely
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
under the convexity assumption on
\Omega
and the growth condition
\psi(t)\leq C|t|^{\gamma}
for
\gamma<2
,
C>0
and
t\geq t_{0}
. Indeed, under such a hypothesis,

{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}=\left\{re^{i\theta}:r\geq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\frac{3\pi}{2}\right\}\cdot

It is worth remarking that, unlike the case in which
\Omega
is convex and contains a sector, the point spectral picture of
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
is not completely characterized by Theorems
4.13
and
5.13
. For instance, by considering
\psi_{\Omega}(t)=t^{2}
, it is straightforward to check that the aforementioned results do not provide any further information about the membership of
\lambda=|\lambda|e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}
and
\mu=|\mu|e^{i\frac{3\pi}{2}}
to
{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}
.

6.
Spectral consequences for composition operators

As it was pointed out in the introduction, if
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
, we can recover the point spectrum on
H^{p}
of the composition operators
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
throughout the identity

(6.1)

\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi_{t}}\mid_{H^{p}})=\{e^{t\lambda}:\lambda\in{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})}\}\qquad(t>0).

In this section, we exploit the previous equality to completely characterize the spectrum
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}\mid_{H^{p}})
of these operators.

Given a linear bounded operator
T:X\rightarrow X
acting on a complex Banach space, we denote by
r(T)
its spectral radius, which is given by

r(T)=\sup\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(T)\}.

Siskakis
[
23
, Corollary 3.2]
characterized the spectral radius of composition operators
C_{\phi}
acting on
H^{p}
induced by a holomorphic self-map of the unit disc
\phi
with Denjoy-Wolff-point
1
. Indeed, he proved that

r(C_{\phi})=\phi^{\prime}(1).

As a consequence, regarding the definition of parabolic semigroups, the following holds:

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Then,

r(C_{\varphi_{t}})=1
for all
t>0
.

We can now state the main result of this section:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Suppose that
\Omega\subseteq S_{p}(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\alpha_{0}\in[0,\pi)
, and
\beta_{0}\in[0,\pi)
, where
0<\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}\leq\pi
. Assume that either

(i)

\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}=0
and
\beta_{\Omega}^{0}<\pi
, or

(ii)

\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}<\pi
and
\beta_{\Omega}^{o}=0
.

Then,

\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}\mid_{H^{p}})=\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}

for all
t>0
.

Proof.

First, by Proposition
6.1
,
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}\mid_{H^{p}})\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
for all
t>0
. Now, let us assume
(i)
, the reasoning for
(ii)
is equivalent. By Theorem
4.4
, it follows that

A:=\left\{re^{i\theta}:r\geq 0,\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\frac{3\pi}{2}-\beta_{\Omega}^{o}\right\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\Delta).

Now, it is easy to show that
\exp(A)={\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}
, which along with (
6.1
) yields that

{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}})\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}

for all
t>0
. Recalling that
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}})
is closed, the result follows.
∎

This result may be applied to describe the point spectrum of composition operators associated to parabolic semigroups
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with positive hyperbolic step:

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semigroup on
{\mathbb{D}}
of positive hyperbolic step. Then,

{\mathbb{T}}\subseteq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}}\mid_{H^{p}})\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}.

Moreover, if
\Omega
is contained in an angular sector
S_{p}(\alpha,0)
or
S_{p}(0,\beta)
for some
p\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\alpha<\pi
,
\beta<\pi
, then

\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}})=\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}.

Proof.

Since
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of positive hyperbolic step, it is contained in a horizontal half-plane. By Theorem
2.1
, Proposition
2.3
, and (
6.1
),
{\mathbb{T}}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi_{t}})
. Proposition
6.1
yields
\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi_{t}})\subseteq\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}.
Now, assume it is contained in an angular sector
S_{p}(\alpha,0)
. The other case is equivalent. This implies that
\beta_{\Omega}^{o}=0
and
\alpha_{\Omega}^{o}>0
, so Theorem
6.2
yields the result.
∎

It is worth mentioning that in the case in which
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is a parabolic group of automorphisms of
{\mathbb{D}}
,
\Omega
is a horizontal half-plane, so it is of positive hyperbolic step and
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}})={\mathbb{T}}
. However, for any other parabolic semigroup
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
not consisting of automorphisms of
{\mathbb{D}}
, the operators
C_{\varphi_{t}}
are not invertible, so
0\in\sigma(C_{\varphi_{t}})
.

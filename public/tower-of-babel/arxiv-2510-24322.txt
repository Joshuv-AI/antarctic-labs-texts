Non-equilibrium correlation effects in spin transport through the 2D ferromagnet Fe 4 GeTe 2 Preprint: APS/123-QED
Declan Nell, Stefano Sanvito, Andrea Droghetti

Abstract
Understanding non-equilibrium spin transport through 2D ferromagnets is a theoretical challenge, as correlations produce a complex electronic structure with coexisting itinerant and localized electrons. We have developed a fully non-equilibrium ab initio method, combining density functional theory, dynamical mean-field theory, and non-equilibrium Green’s functions to investigate the transport in Fe 4 GeTe 2 , a prototypical high-temperature 2D ferromagnet. We show that, while spin transport remains essentially single-particle under moderate bias, inelastic spin-dependent scattering of carriers with particle–hole excitations drives a distinctive hot-correlated electron regime beyond a critical voltage. This regime is marked by incoherent features in both the electronic spectrum and the conductance, which are experimentally accessible. Our results demonstrates that material-specific many-body non-equilibrium methods are essential for a complete understanding of spin transport in 2D ferromagnets.

Conclusion.
The combination of DFT, DMFT and the NEGF formalism shows that a Fe
4
GeTe
2
monolayer under bias can enter a true non-equilibrium regime, where inelastic
electron scattering enhances correlations and generates incoherent features in both the excitation spectrum
and the conductance. These are potentially accessible in experiments. Overall, our results demonstrate that
our framework captures quantum transport in correlated magnetic materials far beyond the linear response
limit and the conventional coherent picture, opening a pathway to explore many-body effects in spintronic
devices.

&\big[g^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})^{-1}-\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})-\Sigma_{\mathrm{R}}^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})\big]^{-1},\end{split}

(8)

\begin{split}&G^{\sigma\;<}_{\mathrm{KS}}(E,\bm{k})=\\
&G^{\sigma\;r}_{\mathrm{KS}}(E,\bm{k})[\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})+\Sigma_{\mathrm{R}}^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})]G^{\sigma\;a}_{\mathrm{KS}}(E,\bm{k}).\end{split}

(9)

with
g^{\sigma\,r}(E,\bm{k})=[(E+i\eta)S(\bm{k})-H_{\mathrm{KS}}^{\sigma}(\bm{k})]^{-1}_{\eta\rightarrow 0}
, the retarded component of
g^{\sigma}(E,\bm{k})
introduced in Eq. (
1
).

Since the leads are assumed to remain in local equilibrium, their lesser and greater self-energies can be expressed
using the fluctuation-dissipation theorem
Stefanucci and van Leeuwen 2013
,

\begin{split}\Sigma_{\mathrm{L(R)}}^{\sigma\;<}(E,\bm{k})=&if_{\mathrm{L(R)}}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{L(R)}}(E,\bm{k})\\
\Sigma_{\mathrm{L(R)}}^{\sigma\;>}(E,\bm{k})=&i\big[f_{\mathrm{L(R)}}(E)-1\big]\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{L(R)}}(E,\bm{k}),\end{split}

(10)

where

\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{L(R)}}(E,\bm{k})=i\big[\Sigma_{\mathrm{L(R)}}^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})-\Sigma_{\mathrm{L(R)}}^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})^{\dagger}\big]
is the coupling of the L (R) lead to the central
region, and
f_{\mathrm{L(R)}}(E)
is the Fermi function of the corresponding lead, with chemical potential

\mu_{\mathrm{L(R)}}
. Here,
\Sigma_{\mathrm{L(R)}}^{\sigma\;\lessgtr}
encode information about the out-of-equilibrium
in- and outflow of electrons in the central region.

Analogously, for the many-body self-energy we can introduce
Droghetti and Rungger 2017
;
Ness et al. 2010
;
Rungger et al. 2019

\begin{split}&\Sigma^{\sigma\;<}_{\textnormal{MB}}(E,\bm{k})=iF_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E)\Gamma_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E,\bm{k})\>,\\
&\Sigma^{>}_{\textnormal{MB}}(E,\bm{k})=i[F_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E)-1]\Gamma_{\textnormal{MB}}(E,\bm{k})\>.\end{split}

(11)

Here,
F_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E)
denotes the occupation matrix of the central device region.
In general, it differs from the Fermi function; only in equilibrium does
F_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E)

reduce to the Fermi function.

Projection into the correlated subspace.

The KS Hamiltonian is projected onto the
\mathcal{C}
subspace via
\bar{H}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\sigma}(\bm{k})=\;W(\bm{k})^{\dagger}H^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{KS}}(\bm{k})W(\bm{k})

where
W(\bm{k})
is the same transformation matrix defined in Eqs. (17) and (A17) of
Ref.
Droghetti and Rungger 2017
. This transformation also orthogonalizes the orbitals within
\mathcal{C}
.
The projected Hamiltonian
\bar{H}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\sigma}(\bm{k})
is then supplemented by a
Hubbard-like interaction term. The NEGF of the
\mathcal{C}
subspace,

\bar{G}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\sigma}(E,\bm{k})
, can be defined in a form analogous to
Eq. (
5
) with components

\bar{G}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\sigma}(E,\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{G}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})&\bar{G}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\sigma\;K}(E,\bm{k})\\
0&\bar{G}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

The projected self-energy is given by
\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}(E,\bm{k})=\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{\mathrm{L}}(E,\mathbf{k})+\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{\mathrm{R}}(E,\mathbf{k})+\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{\mathcal{C}}(E)
.
The new L (R) lead self-energy for
\mathcal{C}
,
\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{\mathrm{L(R)}}(E,\mathbf{k})
is
obtained as detailed in Ref.
Droghetti and Rungger 2017
[see Eqs. (71) and (72)]. The many-body self-energy
matrix of
\mathcal{C}
in DMFT is both
\bm{k}
-independent and diagonal in the atomic sites,
namely

\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{\mathcal{C}}(E)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{1}(E)&0&...&0\\
0&\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{2}(E)&...&0\\
0&0&...&\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{N_{\textnormal{Fe}}}(E)\\
\end{array}\right),

(13)

where
N_{\textnormal{Fe}}
is the number of Fe atoms in the central region (equal to four in our case),
and each block
\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{i}(E)
represents the retarded (lesser) DMFT self-energy
for atomic site
i
within the correlated subspace
\mathcal{C}
.
Finally, the DMFT self-energy within
\mathcal{C}
can be projected back to the central region
as
Droghetti and Rungger 2017

\Sigma^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{\textnormal{MB}}(E,\bm{k})=W(\bm{k})^{-1}\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r(<)}_{\mathcal{C}}(E)[W(\bm{k})^{\dagger}]^{-1}.

(14)

Dynamical mean-field.

The retarded and advanced components of the dynamical mean-field in Eq. (
4
)
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\sigma\;r}_{i}(E)=\Big[G^{\sigma\;r}_{\textnormal{loc},i}(E)^{-1}+\bar{\Sigma}_{i}^{\sigma\;r}(E)\Big]^{-1},

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\sigma\;<}_{i}(E)=\mathcal{G}^{\sigma\;r}_{i}(E)\Delta_{i}^{<}(E)\mathcal{G}^{\sigma\;a}_{i}(E),

(16)

where
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma\;a}_{i}(E)=[\mathcal{G}^{\sigma\;r}_{i}(E)]^{\dagger}

and
\Delta_{i}^{<}(E)
is the lesser hybridization function, defined as

\Delta_{i}^{<}(E)=\bar{G}^{\sigma\;r}_{\textnormal{loc},i}(E)^{-1}\bar{G}^{\sigma\;<}_{\textnormal{loc},i}(E)\bar{G}^{\sigma\;a}_{\textnormal{loc},i}(E)^{-1}-\bar{\Sigma}_{i}^{\sigma\;<}(E).

(17)

For the first iteration of the DMFT self-consistent loop, the DMFT self-energies

\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;r}_{i}(E)
and
\bar{\Sigma}^{\sigma\;<}_{i}(E)
are initialized to zero.
At zero bias, namely in equilibrium,
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma\;<}_{i}(E)
is related to
\mathcal{G}^{\sigma\;r}_{i}(E)

via the fluctuation-dissipation theorem, and the DMFT equations reduce to those of layered DMFT in
Refs.
Valli et al. 2012
;
Droghetti et al. 2022a
.

Charge current.

The two-terminal steady-state charge current is given by the Meir-Wingreen formula
Meir and Wingreen 1992

\begin{split}I^{\sigma}=&\frac{e}{2h\Omega_{\bm{k}}}\int d\bm{k}\int dE\;\textnormal{Tr}\Big[[\Sigma_{\textnormal{L}}^{\sigma\;<}(E,\bm{k})-\Sigma_{\textnormal{R}}^{\sigma\;<}(E,\bm{k})]\times\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\big[G^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})-G^{\sigma\;a}(E,\bm{k})\big]\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+i[\Gamma_{\textnormal{L}}^{\sigma}(E,\bm{k})-\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\textnormal{R}}(E,\bm{k})]G^{\sigma\;<}(E,\bm{k})\Big].\end{split}

(18)

Using Eq. (
7
) together with the expressions for the lesser and greater components of the lead
and many-body self-energies in Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
), the current can be
rewritten as the sum of the coherent and incoherent contributions:

I^{\sigma}=I_{\textnormal{C}}^{\sigma}+I_{\textnormal{IC}}^{\sigma}.

(19)

The coherent contribution reads

\begin{split}I_{\textnormal{C}}^{\sigma}=\frac{e}{h}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!dE\;T^{\sigma}(E)\big[f_{\textnormal{L}}(E)-f_{\textnormal{R}}(E)\big],\end{split}

(20)

where
T^{\sigma}(E)
denotes the transmission coefficient, given by

\begin{split}T^{\sigma}(E)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\bm{k}}}\int d\bm{k}\;\textnormal{Tr}&\Big[\Gamma^{\sigma}_{L}(E,\bm{k})G^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})\\
&\Gamma^{\sigma}_{R}(E,\bm{k})G^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})^{\dagger}\Big].\end{split}

(21)

Equation (
20
) is the generalization of the Laudauer-Büttiker
formula to finite bias (see, for example, reference
Datta 1995
;
Sanvito 2005
;
Rocha et al. 2006
).

The incoherent contribution is

\begin{split}I_{\textnormal{IC}}=&\frac{e}{2h\Omega_{\bm{k}}}\int d\bm{k}\int dE\;\textnormal{Tr}\Big\{\big[f_{\textnormal{L}}(E)-f_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E)\big]\Gamma_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E)\\
&\times G^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})^{\dagger}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{L}(E,\bm{k})G^{\sigma\;R}(E,\bm{k})\Big\}\\
&-\textnormal{Tr}\Big\{\big[f_{\textnormal{R}}(E)-f_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E)\big]\Gamma_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}(E)G^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})^{\dagger}\\
&\times\Gamma^{\sigma}_{R}(E,\bm{k})G^{\sigma\;r}(E,\bm{k})\Big\}.\end{split}

(22)

In the KS single-particle description, the many-body self-energy is absent, and thus the incoherent contribution
vanishes. In this case, the current is given solely by Eq. (
20
), with
T^{\sigma}(E)
evaluated
using the KS NEGF, Eq. (
8
).

In DMFT, both contributions can be non-negligible.
T^{\sigma}(E)
incorporates the NEGF with
the DMFT self-energy, and represents the transmission for the electronic excitations renormalized in
both energy and lifetime by dynamical correlation effects. The incoherent contribution instead captures
the impact of inelastic scattering on transport. Notably, Eq. (
22
)
resembles Eq. (
20
), but with
f_{\textnormal{MB}}^{\sigma}
and
\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\textnormal{MB}}

replacing
f_{\mathrm{R}}
(
f_{\mathrm{L}}
) and
\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\textnormal{R}}
(
\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\textnormal{L}})

in the first (second) term of the integrand. This analogy suggests an intuitive picture in which inelastic scattering
is effectively represented by an additional “virtual electrode”.

I_{\textnormal{IC}}
can then be interpreted as the current flowing from the left lead into this virtual
electrode and subsequently emerging into the right lead with modified energy and phase. This picture
closely resembles the phenomenological concept of the dephasing probe introduced by Büttiker
Büttiker 1986
,
commonly used to describe the crossover between coherent and non-coherent transport in nano-conductors.
Eqs. (
22
) and (
20
) with the DMFT self-energy
provide a quantitative, atomistic, and theoretically grounded framework to address this crossover.

Computational details.

DFT+NEGF calculations are performed using the same computational details as in Ref.
Halder et al. 2024
.
DMFT calculations are carried out with orbital-dependent screened Coulomb interaction parameters for
the Fe
3d
orbitals, expressed in terms of the Slater integrals
F^{0}
,
F^{2}
, and
F^{4}

Imada et al. 1998
.
The ratio
F^{4}/F^{2}
is fixed to the atomic value
\approx 0.625

Anisimov and Gunnarsson 1991
. The average interaction
parameters are defined as
U=F^{0}
and
J=(F^{2}+F^{4})/14
, and in this work, take the values

U=1.5
eV and
J=0.5
eV.

The treatment of double-counting corrections remains an open issue for transport calculations. For a
practical implementation, we assume that the double-counting term exactly cancels the first-order
self-energy contribution from the perturbative impurity solver (both corresponding to “on-site” energy shifts),
and neglect both. At zero bias, this approximation yields results similar to those in Ref.
Halder et al. 2024
, where
the first-order term and a double-counting correction in the localized limit
Droghetti et al. 2022b
were included,
provided that we use the reduced interaction parameter
U=1.5
eV mentioned above in place of the
U=3
eV
of Ref.
Halder et al. 2024
.

The local Green’s function is computed using a
20\times 20

\bm{k}
-mesh. DMFT calculations
are performed directly on the real energy axis with a grid of 10,000 points spanning the range
[-25,15]
eV.
The resulting converged DMFT self-energy is then used to evaluate the DOS and transmission coefficient
on a finer
80\times 80

\bm{k}
-mesh.

Abstract
. Let π \pi and π ′ \pi^{\prime} be unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of GL ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{GL}(n) and GL ⁡ ( n ′ ) \mathrm{GL}(n^{\prime}) over a number field F F . We establish a new zero-free region for all GL ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathrm{GL}(1) -twists of the Rankin–Selberg L L -function L ⁡ ( s , π × π ′ ) L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}) , generalizing Tatuzawa’s refinement of Siegel’s work on Dirichlet L L -functions. As a corollary, we show that for all ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , there exists an effectively computable constant c > 0 c>0 depending only on ( n , n ′ , [ F : ℚ ] , ε ) (n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon) such that L ⁡ ( s , π × π ′ ) L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime}) has at most one zero (necessarily simple) in the region Re ⁡ ( s ) ≥ 1 − c / ( C ⁡ ( π ) ​ C ​ ( π ′ ) ​ ( | Im ⁡ ( s ) | + 1 ) ) ε , \mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1-c/(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime})(|\mathrm{Im}(s)|+1))^{\varepsilon}, where C ⁡ ( π ) C(\pi) and C ⁡ ( π ′ ) C(\pi^{\prime}) are the analytic conductors. A crucial component of our proof is a new standard zero-free region for any twist of L ⁡ ( s , π × π ~ ) L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi}) by an idele class character χ \chi apart from a possible single exceptional zero (necessarily real and simple) that can occur only when π ⊗ χ 2 = π \pi\otimes\chi^{2}=\pi . Introduction and the main result

This paper continues the work in
[
6
]
on zero-free regions for Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions. Let
F
be a number field with absolute discriminant
D_{F}
and adele ring
\mathbb{A}_{F}
. By a unitary cuspidal (
L^{2}
-)automorphic representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, we mean an irreducible closed invariant subspace of
L^{2}_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F}),\omega)
under the right regular representation, where
\omega=\omega_{\pi}
is a unitary Hecke character called the central character of
\pi
. Let
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the set of these representations
\pi
. Given
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, let
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the contragredient, and let
L(s,\pi)
be the standard
L
-function of
\pi
. Let
C(\pi)\geq 3
denote the analytic conductor of
\pi
. Given
(\pi,\chi)\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, let
\pi\otimes\chi
be the (unique) element of
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
whose space consists of the functions
f(x)\chi(\det x)
, where
f(x)
is from the space of
\pi
. It is straightforward to check that
\pi\otimes\chi
is isomorphic to the representation
g\mapsto\pi(g)\chi(\det g)
. For future convenience, as in
[
6
]
, we introduce
\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
, the set of
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
for which
\omega_{\pi}
is trivial on the diagonally embedded positive reals. For each
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, there exists a unique pair
(\pi^{*},t_{\pi})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\pi=\pi^{*}\otimes|\cdot|^{it_{\pi}},\qquad L(s,\pi)=L(s+it_{\pi},\pi^{*}).

Given
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, let
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
be the associated Rankin–Selberg
L
-function, whose basic properties were established by Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
13
,
15
,
16
]
. If
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}^{*}
, then
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
is holomorphic away from a possible pole at
s=1
, which occurs if and only if
\pi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\pi}
. If
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{*}
is the trivial representation
\mathbbm{1}
, then
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})=L(s,\pi)
.

Jacquet and Shalika
[
14
]
proved that if
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, then
L(s,\pi)\neq 0
for
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
. Recently, Wattanawanichkul
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
9
, Appendix]
) proved that
L(\sigma+it,\pi^{*})
has at most one zero in the region

(1.1)

\sigma\geq 1-\frac{1}{16(2n+3)\log(C(\pi^{*})(|t|+3)^{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]})}.

If the exceptional zero exists, then it is necessarily real and simple, and
\pi^{*}=\widetilde{\pi}^{*}
. This directly generalizes classical results for Dirichlet
L
-functions. We could have equivalently presented a zero-free interval for
L(\sigma,\pi)
, but the presentation with
L(\sigma+it,\pi^{*})
helps to clarify the properties of the exceptional zero. In certain cases, the exceptional zero has been precluded entirely; see
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
24
]
.

Shahidi
[
21
]
proved that if
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, then
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\neq 0
for
\mathrm{Re}(s)\geq 1
. Recently, Wattanawanichkul
[
26
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
(see also
[
9
, Appendix]
and
[
10
]
) proved that if

(1.2)

L(s,\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*})=L(s,\widetilde{\pi}^{*}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime*})\qquad\textup{or}\qquad\pi^{\prime*}=\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime*},

then
L(\sigma+it,\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*})
has at most one zero in the region

(1.3)

\sigma\geq 1-\frac{1}{66(n+n^{\prime})\log(C(\pi^{*})C(\pi^{\prime*})(|t|+3)^{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]})}.

If the exceptional zero exists, then it is must be real and simple, and
L(s,\pi^{*}\times\pi^{\prime*})=L(s,\widetilde{\pi}^{*}\times\widetilde{\pi}^{\prime*})
. In certain cases, the exceptional zero in (
1.3
) can be precluded even when (
1.2
) is not satisfied; see
[
19
,
20
,
24
,
25
]
. For further historical remarks, see
[

and the references contained therein.

There exist zero-free regions with no exceptions that hold for all Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions, but they are weaker than (
1.3
). The two strongest results are as follows. First, Brumley (
[
2
]
,
[
18
, Appendix]
) proved that if
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
, then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{Brumley}(n,n^{\prime},F,\varepsilon)>0
such that

(1.4)

L(\sigma,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})\neq 0,\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{Brumley}/(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime}))^{n+n^{\prime}-1+\varepsilon}.

(See Zhang
[
27
]
for a recent numerical improvement in the exponent of (
1.4
) in the
|\cdot|^{it}
-twist aspect.) Second, the authors
[
6
]
proved that if
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an ineffective constant
=\Cr{Crelle}(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon)>0
such that if
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
, then

(1.5)

L(\sigma,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\neq 0,\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{Crelle}C(\chi)^{-\varepsilon}.

When
\pi=\pi^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}
and
F=\mathbb{Q}
, (
1.5
) recovers a landmark result of Siegel
[
22
]
for
L
-functions of quadratic Dirichlet characters: If
\mathcal{Q}
is the set of primitive quadratic Dirichlet characters, then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
ineffective
constant
=\Cr{Siegel}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

(1.6)

L(1,\chi)\geq\Cr{Siegel}q_{\chi}^{-\varepsilon},\qquad\chi\in\mathcal{Q}.

Tatuzawa
[
23
]
refined Siegel’s ineffective result (
1.6
) by proving that there exists an
effectively computable
constant
=\Cr{Tatuzawa}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

(1.7)

L(1,\chi)\geq\Cr{Tatuzawa}q_{\chi}^{-\varepsilon}
holds for all
\chi\in\mathcal{Q}
with one possible exception.

In fact
[
23
, Theorem 1]
states that
\Cr{Tatuzawa}=\varepsilon/10
is permissible. This narrowly misses an effective version of (
1.6
), which, for example, would significantly impact the distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions and the computation of class numbers of imaginary quadratic fields. Using classical techniques in the theory of Dirichlet
L
-functions, one can prove that (
1.7
) is equivalent to the following statement:

(1.8)

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an effectively
computable constant

=\Cr{Tatuzawa1}(\varepsilon)>0

and

\chi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{Q}
such that if

\chi\in\mathcal{Q}-\{\chi_{\varepsilon}\}
and
\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{Tatuzawa1}q_{\chi}^{-\varepsilon}
, then
L(\sigma,\chi)\neq 0
.

Since (
1.5
) is true, one might hope to prove a version of (
1.7
) for the
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-twists of
L(s,\pi)
or
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
. When
F=\mathbb{Q}
, Ichihara and Matsumoto
[
12
]
proved that if
L(s)
is an
L
-function satisfying certain restrictive hypotheses and
\varepsilon>0
, then there exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{IM}(L,\varepsilon)>0
and a character
\psi=\psi_{L,\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{Q}
such that if
\chi\in\mathcal{Q}-\{\psi\}
, then the twisted
L
-function
L_{\chi}(s)
satisfies
|L_{\chi}(1)|\geq\Cr{IM}q_{\chi}^{-\varepsilon}
. We highlight several desiderata that are not addressed in
[
12
]
. First,
[
12
]
assumes that
L_{\chi}(s)
restricted to
|\mathrm{Im}(s)|\leq 1
has a classical zero-free region. As described above, this hypothesis remains a significant open problem in the context of Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions. Second,
[
12
]
does not determine the effective dependence of
\Cr{IM}
on the original
L
-function
L(s)
. Third, like all preceding manifestations of Tatuzawa’s ideas, only exceptional behavior of quadratic twists of
L(s)
are considered. It is important to have a version of Tatuzawa’s theorem for all
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-twists of
L(s)
, not just those coming from
\mathcal{Q}
. For example, even if
L(s)
is not self-dual, there might exist
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}-\mathcal{Q}
such that
L_{\chi}(s)
is self-dual (and hence might have an exceptional zero). Furthermore, finite order idele class characters only form a small portion of
\mathfrak{F}_{1}
. In particular, by handling characters of the form
|\cdot|^{it}
, one might be able to use Tatuzawa’s ideas to establish an
effective
Siegel-type zero-free region apart from at most one exception. This would be a significant step towards improving Brumley’s narrow zero-free region in (
1.4
).

Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
. In this paper, we prove a version of (
1.8
) that holds unconditionally for all
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-twists of
L(s,\pi)
and
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\chi)\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}\times\mathfrak{F}_{1}
and
\varepsilon>0
. There exist an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{ZFR}(n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon)>0
and a character
\psi=\psi_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon}\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
such that if
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
differs from
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\psi))
, then

(1.9)

L(\sigma,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\neq 0,\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{ZFR}(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime})C(\chi))^{-\varepsilon}.

Moreover,
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\psi))
has at most one zero (necessarily simple) in the interval
\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{ZFR}(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime})C(\psi))^{-\varepsilon}
.

Remark 1.2
.

There might exist several characters
\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
resulting in the same twisted
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
, which is why we consider exceptions at the level of twisted
L
-functions rather than characters.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem

1.1
strengthens (
1.5
) considerably. It is new even when
\pi^{\prime}=\mathbbm{1}
, that is, for the
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-twists of the standard
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
.

By specializing
Theorem

1.1
to the shift characters
\chi=|\cdot|^{it}\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}
we obtain the following corollary, which significantly improves upon (

) apart from at most one exceptional zero.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\times\mathfrak{F}_{n^{\prime}}
and
\varepsilon>0
. There exists an effectively computable constant
=\Cr{ZFR3}(n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon)>0
such that
L(\sigma+it,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})
has at most one zero (necessarily simple) in the region
\sigma\geq 1-\Cr{ZFR3}(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime})(|t|+1))^{-\varepsilon}
.

If the exceptional zero

2.
Properties of
L
-functions

Let
F
be a number field with adele ring
\mathbb{A}_{F}
. Let
D_{F}
be the absolute discriminant of
F
,
\mathcal{O}_{F}
the ring of integers of
F
, and
\mathrm{N}=\mathrm{N}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}
the norm defined on nonzero ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
by
\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}=|\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{a}|
. For a place
v
of
F
, let
v\mid\infty
(resp.
v\nmid\infty
) denote that
v
is archimedean (resp. non-archimedean), and let
F_{v}
be the corresponding completion of
F
. Each
v\nmid\infty
corresponds with a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

2.1.
Standard
L
-functions

Let
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the set of cuspidal automorphic representations
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
whose central character
\omega_{\pi}
is unitary, and let
\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
be the subset of those
\pi
’s for which
\omega_{\pi}
is trivial on the diagonally embedded positive reals. Every
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
can be written uniquely as

(2.1)

\pi=\pi^{*}\otimes|\cdot|^{it_{\pi}},\qquad\pi^{*}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*},\quad t_{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}.

If
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
, then for each place
v
, there exists an irreducible admissible representation
\pi_{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
, with
\pi_{v}
ramified for at most finitely many
v
, such that
\pi
is a restricted tensor product
\otimes_{v}\pi_{v}
. When
v\nmid\infty
and
\mathfrak{p}
corresponds with
v
, then we write
\pi_{v}
and
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
interchangeably. For each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, there exist
n
Satake parameters
\alpha_{1,\pi}(\mathfrak{p}),\dotsc,\alpha_{n,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathbb{C}
such that the standard
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
of
\pi
is the absolutely convergent Euler product

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L(s,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{p}^{-s}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1.

For
v\mid\infty
, define

\Gamma_{v}(s)=\begin{cases}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{R}$,}\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{C}$.}\end{cases}

There exist
n
Langlands parameters
\mu_{1,\pi}(v),\dotsc,\mu_{n,\pi}(v)\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(2.2)

L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)).

Let
\mathfrak{q}_{\pi}
denote the conductor of
\pi
, and let
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{*}
denote the trivial representation (whose
L
-function is the Dedekind zeta function
\zeta_{F}(s)
). Let
\delta_{\pi}=1
if
\pi=\mathbbm{1}
, and
\delta_{\pi}=0
otherwise. If

L_{\infty}(s,\pi)=\prod_{v\mid\infty}L(s,\pi_{v}),

then the completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\pi)=(s(1-s))^{\delta_{\pi}}(D_{F}^{n}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak{q}_{\pi})^{s/2}L_{\infty}(s,\pi)L(s,\pi)

is entire of order
1
. There exists
W(\pi)\in\mathbb{C}
of modulus
1
such that
\Lambda(s,\pi)
satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,\pi)=W(\pi)\Lambda(1-s,\widetilde{\pi})=W(\pi)\overline{\Lambda(1-\overline{s},\pi)}.

The nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\pi)
are the zeros of
\Lambda(s,\pi)
, and they lie in the critical strip
0<\mathrm{Re}(s)<1

Lemma 2.1

Let

[

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

Remark 3.3

The terms of the

in the upper half-plane, counted with multiplicity.

Proof.

Let

to conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{L(\rho,\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})=0}\mathrm{Re}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j,k\leq\ell\\
\pi_{j}^{*}=\pi_{k}^{*}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Re}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma-1+it_{\pi_{j}}-it_{\pi_{k}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\log C(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})+\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(\sigma,\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi}).

The last term is nonpositive by

3.1
, hence

\displaystyle\sum_{L(\rho,\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})=0}\mathrm{Re}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma-\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j,k\leq\ell\\
\pi_{j}^{*}=\pi_{k}^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma-1}{(\sigma-1)^{2}+(t_{\pi_{j}}-t_{\pi_{k}})^{2}}+\log C(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r}{\sigma-1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<k\leq\ell\\
\pi_{j}^{*}=\pi_{k}^{*}\\
\pi_{j}\neq\pi_{k}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|t_{\pi_{j}}-t_{\pi_{k}}|}+\log C(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi}).

Let

\sigma=1+\frac{1}{2E(\Pi\times\widetilde{\Pi})},

and let
\mathcal{N}\geq 0
be the number of zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
(counted with multiplicity) of

satisfying

We conclude via nonnegativity that

\ll_{n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon}(1-\beta_{\chi})Q^{\varepsilon/3}

Now we deduce (

Proof of
Theorem

We employ

3.5
and Propositions

&

to prove
Theorem

1.1
. For ease of notation, we shall seek zero-free intervals of the form

(5.1)

\sigma\geq 1-(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime})C(\chi)^{2n+2n^{\prime}})^{-\varepsilon},

where

\Cr{ZFR6}=\Cr{ZFR6}(n,n^{\prime},[F:\mathbb{Q}],\varepsilon)>0
is an effectively computable constant. Without loss of generality,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Consider

\varepsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2(n+n^{\prime})}.

Assume first that
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
has a zero in
[1-\varepsilon^{\prime}/16,1)
. Let
\Cr{ZFR6}>0
be suitably small. If
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
is entire, then by
Proposition

4.2
and (
3.8
), it has no zero in the interval (
5.1
). If
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
has a pole, then there exists a unique
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi=\widetilde{\pi}\otimes|\cdot|^{it}
.
By
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
, or by the more general
Theorem

1.6
, the corresponding
L
-function

L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))=L(s+it,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})

has no zero in the interval (
5.1
) when
t\neq 0
, while it has at most one zero (necessarily simple) in the same interval when
t=0
. So we proved
Theorem

1.1
in this case.

Assume now that
L(s,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})
has no zero in
[1-\varepsilon^{\prime}/16,1)
. Let
\Cr{ZFR6}>0
be suitably small. If
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
has a pole, then there exists a unique
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi=\widetilde{\pi}\otimes|\cdot|^{it}
.
By
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
, or by the more general
Theorem

1.6
, coupled with our initial assumption, the corresponding
L
-function

L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))=L(s+it,\pi\times\widetilde{\pi})

has no zero in the interval (
5.1
). Hence it suffices to show that among all the
entire
twists
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
, only one can have a zero in the interval (
5.1
), and even this exceptional twist can only have one zero (necessarily simple) in the same interval.

From now on, we restrict to
\chi
lying in

S=\left\{\chi\in\mathfrak{F}_{1}:\text{$L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))$ is entire}\right\}.

This set is nonempty, of course. As a first step towards our goal, we prove the existence of a constant
0<\Cr{ZFR6}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}/16
and a character
\lambda=\lambda_{\pi,\pi^{\prime},\varepsilon}\in S
such that for all
\chi\in S
, one of the following two statements is true.

(1)

L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))=L(s+it,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\lambda))

with

of absolute value less than
(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi)C(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\lambda))^{-\varepsilon/128}
.

(2)

L(\sigma,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))\neq 0
in the interval (
5.1
).

If, for all
\chi\in S
, the
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
has no zero in
[1-\varepsilon^{\prime}/16,1)
, then we are finished. Otherwise, by
a straightforward compactness argument and Shahidi’s nonvanishing theorem
[
21
, Theorem 5.2]
, there exists
\lambda\in S
with

minimal such that
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\lambda))
has a zero in
[1-\varepsilon^{\prime}/16,1)
.

We fix
\lambda\in S
as above, and we consider an arbitrary
\chi\in S
. If
C(\chi)<C(\lambda)
, then
L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))
does not vanish in
[1-\varepsilon^{\prime}/16,1)
by the minimality of
C(\lambda)
, hence item (2) holds. From now on, we assume that
C(\chi)\geq C(\lambda)
, and we define

\pi^{\prime\prime}=\pi^{\prime}\otimes\lambda,\qquad\chi^{\prime}=\chi\overline{\lambda}.

The
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime\prime})
is entire and has a zero in
[1-\varepsilon^{\prime}/16,1)
. Moreover, the
L
-function

(5.2)

L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))=L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime\prime}\otimes\chi^{\prime}))

is entire. Hence, we shall apply

4.1
with
(\pi,\pi^{\prime\prime},\chi^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime})
in the role of
(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\chi,\varepsilon)
. Accordingly, we work with the following variant of (
3.8
):

Q=Q(\pi,\pi^{\prime\prime},\chi^{\prime})=(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime\prime}))^{2(n+n^{\prime})}C(\chi^{\prime})^{(n+n^{\prime})^{2}}.

It follows from
C(\lambda)\leq C(\chi)
and (
2.12
) that

(5.3)

(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\lambda)C(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))^{n+n^{\prime}}<Q<(C(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime}))^{2(n+n^{\prime})}C(\chi)^{4(n+n^{\prime})^{2}}.

If the twist equivalence condition of

4.1

(\pi,\pi^{\prime\prime},\chi^{\prime})
in place of

, and the constant

), (
5.2
), and (

\displaystyle L(s,\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi))

\displaystyle=L(s+it_{\chi},\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi^{*}))

\displaystyle=L(s+it_{\chi},\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\lambda^{*}))

\displaystyle=L(s+it_{\chi}-it_{\lambda},\pi\times(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\lambda)),

With the constant

1.1
. By applying (
3.6
) and
Lemma

3.5

\lambda
in the role of

=\Cr{ZFR2}(n,n^{\prime},\varepsilon)>0

By Brumley’s effective zero-free region (

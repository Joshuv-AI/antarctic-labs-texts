Impact of Glucagon-like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists and Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 Inhibitors on Post–liver Transplant New-onset Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events, Graft Failure, Renal Outcome, and Mortality
Authors: Kelli C. Kosako Yost, Megan B. Ghai, Majd Aboona, Qumber Ali, Pooja Rangan, Paul Gomez, Rohit Nathan, Mark Wong, Karn Wijarnpreecha, Moises I. Nevah Rubin, Michael Fallon, Ma Ai Thanda Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background.Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RA) and sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have demonstrated cardiovascular benefits, but there are little data in solid organ transplant populations. We aimed to assess the effect of GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i on the incidence of post-transplant major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), graft failure, renal outcomes, and mortality in liver or simultaneous liver-kidney transplant populations.Methods.A retrospective chart review of adults with diabetes mellitus and either solitary liver or simultaneous liver-kidney transplantation from January 2012 to March 2022 was completed. The multivariate Cox regression and Fine and Gray competing risk regression analyses were used.Results.Among 457 patients, 33 received a GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i. The GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group had a lower incidence of graft failure (P = 0.038), new-onset end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis (P = 0.012), and new-onset post–liver transplant MACE at 5 y (adjusted subdistribution hazard ratio, 0.24; P = 0.049; 95% confidence interval, 0.059-0.99).Conclusions.After propensity score matching, the incidence of 5-y post–liver transplant MACE-free survival was significantly higher, and mortality was significantly lower in the GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group. The use of a GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i post–liver transplant was associated with a lower incidence of new-onset MACE, graft failure, and new-onset end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis. There was an improvement in survival after propensity score matching.

INTRODUCTION

Diabetes mellitus (DM) and obesity are common comorbidities in patients who undergo liver transplantation. Furthermore, there is an increased prevalence of patients who undergo liver transplant developing posttransplant DM. In one study, out of 281 patients who underwent transplant, 60 patients (21.35%) developed posttransplant diabetes. This was associated with higher rates of severe infection (hazard ratio [HR], 2.565; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.626-4.050; P < 0.0001), major adverse cardiac events (HR, 1.856; 95% CI, 1.013-3.401; P = 0.0454), and death (HR, 1.840; 95% CI, 1.024-3.304, P = 0.0413).1 Preexisting DM, posttransplant DM, and obesity all increase the risk of posttransplant complications such as chronic kidney disease (CKD) or end-stage renal disease (ESRD) and cardiovascular events, specifically major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), such as myocardial infarction (MI), coronary revascularization, cerebrovascular events, or congestive heart failure.2-6 These comorbidities also significantly impact posttransplant graft survival and overall patient survival.2-6 MACE specifically has been shown to be associated with a lower 1-y survival after liver transplant (79% versus 88%, P < 0.0001).3 Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RA) and Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) have gained popularity in recent years due to their cardiovascular benefits and weight loss potential, specifically in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). The American Diabetes Association recommends the use of GLP-1 RA for patients with T2DM and established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease to prevent future MACE events, as well as the use of SGLT2i for patients with or without established atherosclerotic disease with certain comorbidities, such as heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (<45%) or CKD (estimated glomerular filtration rate 30–≤60 mL/min).7 Liver population-based studies and the use of GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i have mostly centered around improvement in steatosis and fibrosis. These medications have been shown to improve hepatic steatosis and/or fibrosis in metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) or metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH) trials in the pre–liver transplant settings.8,9 Moreover, they have been shown to reduce the development of MACE in pre–liver transplant MASLD population.10

Recent attention has been given to GLP-1 RA and SGT2i in solid organ transplant recipients. Most of the published studies on solid organ transplant recipients involve exceedingly small sample sizes and are limited, and most involve heart and kidney transplant recipients. Liver and simultaneous liver-kidney (SLK) transplant recipients are a subgroup of patients in which little data on SGLT2i and GLP-1RA exist. This study was conducted to determine whether the use of GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i had any impact on subsequent major cardiovascular events, graft failure, renal outcomes, or overall survival posttransplant, as well as to assess the safety of these medications in this population.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study Design and Outcomes

A retrospective chart review was completed in a single-hospital system reviewing any adult age 18 y or older with type 2 DM (T2DM) and either solitary orthotopic liver or SLK from January 2012 to May 2022. Inclusion criteria included any adult age 18 y or older who underwent either solitary orthotopic liver transplant or simultaneous liver and kidney transplant, with T2DM and those who had at least 1 y of follow-up after transplant. Exclusion criteria included patients who had <1 y of follow-up or who did not survive 1 y posttransplant. GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i usage was determined. GLP-1 RA included liraglutide, exenatide, dulaglutide, semaglutide, albiglutide, and lixisenatide. SGLT2i included canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, and ertugliflozin. Demographic, clinical characteristics, and outcomes were compared between the 2 groups: patients who received either GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i and those who did not. Primary outcome included the impact of GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i on post–liver transplant new onset of MACE. Secondary outcomes included the effect of GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i on post–liver transplant new onset of ESRD requiring dialysis, graft failure, and overall mortality.

MACEs were defined as posttransplant, new-onset congestive heart failure, MI, cerebrovascular accident, or coronary revascularization by percutaneous intervention or coronary artery bypass grafting. If a patient had a MACE before transplant, additional posttransplant episodes were not counted as “new-onset” MACE, and the patient was excluded.

This study was approved by the institutional review board (protocol No. STUDY00001501).

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the data for all study variables. The Mann-Whitney U test and Fisher exact or chi-square test were used for continuous and categorical variables, respectively. Fine and Gray competing risk regression analysis was used to evaluate the cumulative incidence of post–liver transplant new-onset MACE between 2 groups, accounting for death, which might preclude the event of interest. We used Cox regression analysis to evaluate the cumulative incidence of death after utilization of GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i compared with the other group. The multivariate model included age at the time of transplant, body mass index (BMI) at the time of transplant, sex, hypertension, DM at listing, hyperlipidemia, CKD, and tobacco use. Sensitivity analysis was performed after controlling for aspirin or statin and hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) within 6 mo of transplant, in addition to multivariate controls. Additional analyses of new-onset MACE were conducted after removing patients who had MACE within the first month of transplant was also analyzed using the logistic and Cox regression model. The 5-y cumulative incidences of MACE-free survival and overall survival were investigated with Kaplan-Meier curves. A log-rank test was used to compare the differences in cumulative incidence of death or MACE events between the GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i group and the other group. The cumulative incidence of outcomes was explored after propensity score matching.

RESULTS

Cohort Demographics

Among 457 post–liver transplant patients with T2DM, 33 patients received a GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i (13 patients received a GLP-1 RA, 16 patients received an SGLT2i, and 4 were on both medications simultaneously). The mean duration of therapy on GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i was 653 d. No minimum exposure time was required for these agents, but a rigorous chart review was done to ensure these prescriptions were filled regularly. The lowest exposure time to these agents was 139 d, and only 6 patients in the entire SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA cohort were on these medications for <1 y. The remainder were on them for >1 y. Only 6 of the GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i group started the medication before transplant, and the rest of the cohort started these medications after transplant. The mean start time of the medications after transplant (after removing these 6 patients) was 858 d after transplant.

The mean age at transplant was 56.70 ± 9.60 y and majority of the patients were men (62.58%). The median follow-up was 48.29 ± 30.74 mo. Out of the entire cohort, only 34 received an SLK, with none receiving an SLK in the GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group. There was no statistically significant difference in age, sex, race, or BMI between the 2 groups. Statistically significant differences in DM at listing (P < 0.001), hyperlipidemia (P < 0.004), and higher HbA1c at transplant (P = 0.0242) were found in the GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group compared with the non-GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group. In contrast, there was a statistically significant difference in patients with CKD (P = 0.005) in the non-GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group compared with the GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group (Table 1).

Univariate Analysis of Outcomes

The GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group had a lower incidence of overall posttransplant mortality (P = 0.035), posttransplant graft failure (P = 0.038), and posttransplant new-onset ESRD requiring dialysis (P = 0.012) on univariate analysis, all of which were statistically significant (Table 2). Although there was a lower incidence of posttransplant new-onset MI, cerebrovascular accident, congestive heart failure, and patients requiring coronary revascularization, these differences were not statistically significant. Upon chart review of the patients on these medications, there were no hospitalizations for hypoglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, or pancreatitis. Less serious side effects, such as gastrointestinal complaints and urinary tract infections, were more difficult to assess in a retrospective chart review within a single-hospital system and were not explored.

DISCUSSION

The use of GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i in the post-transplant population is an emerging topic. Our study showed a benefit of GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i in reducing new onset of post–liver transplant MACE, graft failure, ESRD requiring hemodialysis, and mortality.

Liver transplant patients have been shown to have unique characteristics that could benefit from these medications. In 1 study, liver transplant recipients were shown to have an increased amount of epicardial adipose tissue thickness (P = 0.001), increased carotid damage (P = 0.003), and increased amount of diastolic dysfunction (P = 0.001) first seen 6 mo after transplant, and still present 24 mo after transplant.11 So, this would put this population at an increased risk for cardiovascular events. The benefits of these medications have been recently documented in other populations. The data of GLP-1 RA and/or SGLT2i for cardiovascular outcomes in the nontransplant diabetic population are well studied. In one study of Liraglutide, a GLP-1 RA, patients had a lower rate of death from cardiovascular causes (4.7% versus 6.0%; HR, 0.78; P = 0.007; 95% CI, 0.66-0.93) and a lower rate of death from any cause (8.2% versus 9.6%; HR, 0.85; P = 0.02; 95% CI, 0.74-0.97; P = 0.02).12 In another large meta-analysis of >56 000 patients, GLP-1 RA were shown to reduce MACE events by 12% (HR 0.88, P < 0.001; 95% CI, 0.82-0.94) and mortality from cardiovascular causes by 12% (HR 0.88, P = 0.003; 95% CI, 0.81-0.96).13 In yet another meta-analysis of 5 randomized control trials of >50 000 total patients, GLP-1 RA use reduced the risk of MACE by 20% (HR, 0.80; 95% CI, 0.71-0.89) and SGLT2i use reduced the risk of MACE by 32% (HR, 0.68; 95% CI, 0.57-0.81) when compared with placebo.14 Patients with MASLD have been shown to have a higher incidence of MACE than patients without.15 One study stratified patients with T2DM on SGLT2i or GLP-1 RA based on the presence or absence of concomitant MASLD. In patients on SGLT2i, there was a reduction in MACE events for both groups (MACE with MASLD: HR, 0.73; 95% CI, 0.62-0.86) and (MACE without MASLD: HR, 0.81; 95% CI, 0.72-0.91).10 In the GLP-1 RA group, this was also true, but to a greater degree (MACE with MASLD: HR, 0.49; 95% CI, 0.32-0.77) and (MACE without MASLD: HR, 0.49; 95% CI, 0.37-0.65).10 There have been smaller studies of the outcomes of solid organ transplant patients taking these medications. The use of a GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i has been shown to be associated with lower BMI, lower HbA1c, and reduced daily insulin use when compared with controls.16,17 In 1 small study in the post-transplant population, including liver, kidney, and heart transplants, GLP1-RA was also shown to be associated with improvement in BMI and A1c levels compared with placebo.16 Another recent study again showed GLP-1 RA led to weight loss.18 However, little data, if any, exist on these medications regarding post–liver transplant MACE. Our study showed that, even after controlling for common comorbidities between groups, the group with GLP-1 RA/ SGLT2i use had a statistically significant lower incidence of new-onset, posttransplant MACE in comparison with those who were not on these medications. After controlling for aspirin and statin use and HbA1c at listing, this incidence remained lower and remained statistically significant, further strengthening the association. Moreover, these medications significantly lower the 5-y cumulative incidence of new-onset post–liver transplant MACE in both survival and propensity score–matched analyses. The effect of these medications did not show a statistically significant benefit for individual MACE outcomes, due to the small patient population.

The most robust data for organ transplant populations and use of these medications is for kidney transplant recipients, and these are even small studies. In one study in Korea of >200 kidney transplant patients on SGLT2i, the use of SGLT2i was associated with a lower risk of all-cause mortality, death-censored graft failure, and serum creatinine doubling compared with control group in multivariate model (aHR 0.43; P = 0.006; 95% CI, 0.24-0.78) and propensity score–matched model (aHR, 0.45; P = 0.013; 95% CI, 0.24-0.85).19 In our study, mortality was lower in the GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group, and propensity score–matched data indicated significance on the Kaplan-Meier curve.

The use of GLP-1 RA was also associated with a reduced risk of composite kidney outcome (increase in creatinine, decrease in glomerular filtration rate, microalbuminuria, progression to ESRD, or death attributed to renal causes) by 17% (0.83, P < 0.0001; 95% CI, 0.78-0.89) in the nontransplant T2DM population.13 Comparable results were shown in kidney transplant recipients who used SGLT2i.19 In our study, we also found a statistically significant lower incidence of new-onset end-stage renal failure requiring dialysis in the GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group. Given that no patients in the medication group required dialysis, we were unable to perform further analysis.

The outcomes of GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i have also been studied in heart transplant recipients. In one study of 21 patients who underwent orthotopic heart transplant, the use of GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i was shown to result in weight loss, lower A1c and insulin use, and lower low-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels.20 Empagliflozin specifically was studied in a cohort of 22 heart transplant recipients and, compared with placebo after 12 mo, was shown to result in a reduction in median body weight of 2.0 kg (mean reduction 4.7 ± 5.7, P = 0.003) and a median decrease in BMI of 1.3 kg/m2 (mean reduction 1.6 ± 2.0, P = 0.004).21 Like in our study, the patients treated with empagliflozin (an SGLT2i) did not have any genitourinary infections or euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis, which are well-known complications of SGLT2i.21 None of these studies demonstrated any adverse effects upon taking GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i. The utilization of GLP-1 RA did not cause any adverse effects in the liver transplant population in a recent study.18 Similarly, our study did not identify any major adverse effects of taking either GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i.

There are some limitations of this study that should be noted. It is a retrospective review with the risks of bias. The sample size of patients on SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA was small. There was a low number of events in the SGLT2i/GLP-1 RA group. Because of this, when we conducted logistic regression, we found a statistically significant result in our cohort, but the significance was lost with the Cox model. Larger cohorts and prospective studies are needed. Due to our small population size, 34 SLK transplant patients were included in our study, in the control group only, to increase our total population numbers. No patients in the group receiving GLP1 RA/SGLT2i medications were SLK transplant patients. When these patients were excluded, the MACE rate remained lower in the GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group, but the difference was only trending toward significance (P = 0.059) due to the reduced number of patients. Other similar studies on organ transplant recipients and GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i agents have also had smaller sample sizes, likely due to the small population of patients receiving transplantation. Although the sample size was small, our data showed statistically significant differences in multiple aspects between the 2 groups.

Another limitation is confounding medications. As all patients in the study had T2DM, many were on antidiabetic medications that could confound results. As metformin is typically started before initiation of GLP-1 RA medications, all patients in the GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i group were on metformin, consistent with clinical practice. Metformin specifically could be a potential confounder in the study. Subgroup analysis of patients on insulin versus GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i could not be performed because of the small sample size. Of the 33 patients on a GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i, 19 patients were on both insulin and 1 of these medications.

This study also specifically looked at new-onset posttransplant MACE and did not analyze what effect, if any, these medications would have on patients who had previous MACE pretransplant. We were unable to match the GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i group with patients on another medication for diabetes, such as metformin, to assess whether there was any difference or benefit in 1 over the other, as all patients in the study group were also on metformin. Some strengths of this study include the rigorous chart review, which was able to be completed because of the small size of the population. This study used Cox regression, with multivariate controls, to closely analyze the relationship between these associations. The propensity score–matched method reduced confounding in this retrospective analysis. Overall, this is 1 of the first studies specifically looking at the use of GLP-1 RA/SGLT2i on MACE, graft failure, new-onset renal failure requiring dialysis, and mortality in the liver transplant population.

In conclusion, our data indicate that the use of GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i in post–liver and simultaneous post–liver-kidney transplant populations is associated with a statistically significant lower incidence of MACE events, graft failure, new-onset ESRD requiring dialysis, and a trend toward lower mortality. Our results highlight the benefits of GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i in the liver transplant population, specifically patients with T2DM. With our current data, we cannot recommend that these medications for all patients but many patients who undergo liver transplant have coexisting indications for these medications. Further large prospective studies are warranted to validate our results on GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i in these populations.

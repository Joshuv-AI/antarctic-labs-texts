Cubic fourfolds containing highly singular hyperplane sections
Lisa Marquand, Sasha Viktorova

Abstract
. We construct five irreducible divisors in the moduli space of complex cubic fourfolds parametrising smooth cubic fourfolds that contain highly singular hyperplane sections. We prove that each is not a Noether-Lefschetz (or Hassett) divisor utilising the computational method developed by Addington–Auel.



1.
Introduction

Divisors in the moduli space of smooth cubic fourfolds, denoted
\mathcal{C}
, have received a lot of recent attention, due to their connection with rationality constructions. Indeed, the recent work of
[
KKPY25
]
implies that the locus of rational cubic fourfolds is contained in the union of the
Hassett divisors
, a countable union of irreducible divisors that parametrise cubic fourfolds containing a surface non-homologous to a complete intersection. These divisors were introduced by Hassett in
[
Has00
]
, and they parametrise so-called
special
cubic fourfolds.
It is a complementary problem to find explicit equations for cubic fourfolds, away from these Hassett divisors, where the theory of Hodge atoms of
[
KKPY25
]
guarantees irrationality.

To our knowledge, there are few known non-Hassett divisors in the moduli space
\mathcal{C}
. Ranestand–Voisin constructed three divisors
D_{V-ap},D_{rk3},D_{IR}
parametrising cubic fourfolds apolar to certain surfaces, and a fourth divisor
D_{copl}
parametrising coplanar cubics. They proved that
D_{V-ap}
was non-Hassett by carefully analysing its singularities
[
RV17
]
. On the other hand, Addington–Auel used computational techniques to prove that
D_{rk3}
and
D_{IR}
were non-Hassett as well
[
AA18
]
. Further, Costa–Harvey–Kedlaya used
p
-adic methods to show the same conclusion holds for
D_{copl}

[
CHK19
]
. More recently, Sammarco used techniques similar to
[
RV17
]
to identify another non-Hassett divisor
[
Sam25
]
. It is shown in
[
RV17
]
that
D_{V-ap}
is different from
D_{rk3}
. However, it is not known if the other non-Hassett divisors, including the ones that we define in this work, are different.

In this paper, we consider smooth cubic fourfolds that have a hyperplane section that is more singular than expected. We define
D_{E_{6}},D_{D_{6}}\subset\mathcal{C}
to be the locus parametrising smooth cubic fourfolds containing a hyperplane section
Y:=X\cap H
with an
E_{6}
or
D_{6}
singularity, respectively. We prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

The loci
D_{E_{6}},D_{D_{6}}\subset\mathcal{C}
are irreducible divisors in
\mathcal{C}.
Further, both divisors are not Hassett divisors.

In fact, one can write down the general form of an equation for a cubic contained in either
D_{E_{6}},D_{D_{6}}
(see
Proposition

2.8
and Equation
3.1
), and thus applying
[
KKPY25
]
we can write down examples of irrational cubic fourfolds.

If
X
is a general cubic fourfold, then necessarily all hyperplane sections have total Tjurina number (or total Milnor number)
\leq 5

[
LSV17
, Lemma 3.8]
.
Thus it is natural to consider hyperplane sections with total Tjurina number 6.
A hyperplane section with six nodes is such an example, however cubic fourfolds with such a hyperplane section are contained in either the Hassett divisor
\mathcal{C}_{12}
(containing a cubic scroll) by
[
HT10
]
, or
\mathcal{C}_{8}
(containing a plane) if the singularities are not in general position.
The condition for a cubic threefold
Y
(necessarily singular) to contain either a cubic scroll or a plane was further studied in
[
MV25
]
, where we showed this condition is equivalent to the positivity of the
defect
:
\sigma(Y):=b_{2}(Y)-b_{4}(Y)>0
.
The defect
\sigma(Y)
is a global invariant of the cubic threefold, which can be computed using both the position of the singularities and the local numerical invariants.
In particular, in order to obtain divisors away from
\mathcal{C}_{8}\cup\mathcal{C}_{12},
we restrict our attention to cubic fourfolds containing singular hyperplane sections with
\sigma(Y)=0.

With that in mind, we can consider loci
D_{K}\subset\mathcal{C}
parametrising cubic fourfolds with a hyperplane section
Y
with multiple singularities of type
K=\sum_{i=1}^{r}K_{i}
, with each
K_{i}
of
ADE
type, and total Tjurina number 6.
One would expect to find more non-Hassett divisors this way, as long as
\sigma(Y)=0
.
We prove this is the case:

Theorem 1.2
.

The loci
D_{D_{5}+A_{1}},D_{D_{4}+A_{2}}\subset\mathcal{C}
are irreducible divisors, and
D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}
has two irreducible divisoral components
D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{0},D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{1},
indexed by the value of
\sigma(Y)
. Further, the divisors
D_{D_{5}+A_{1}},D_{D_{4}+A_{2}},D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{0}
are non-Hassett divisors, while
D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{1}=\mathcal{C}_{8}.

We will also consider the locus
D_{T_{333}}\subset\mathcal{C}
that parametrises smooth cubic fourfolds containing a hyperplane section
Y
with a
T_{333}
singularity. This is a unimodular singularity of corank
3
and Tjurina number
8
. One then expects this locus to be codimension
2
in moduli, which we verify:

Proposition 1.3
.

The locus
D_{T_{333}}\subset\mathcal{C}
is irreducible of codimension 2. Further, it is not contained in a Hassett-divisor.

Our interest in singularities of hyperplane sections of a cubic fourfold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
also stems from the study of compactified intermediate Jacobian fibrations
\pi:\mathcal{J}_{X}\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{5})^{*}
, first constructed in
[
LSV17
]
for general
X
. These are examples of hyperkähler manifolds of
OG10
type, and for a general
H\in(\mathbb{P}^{5})^{*}
the fiber of
\pi
is the intermediate Jacobian of the cubic threefold
X\cap H
. Although existence of such a hyperkähler compactification is guaranteed for
any
cubic by results of Saccá
[
Sac23
]
, it is non constructive. Recently,
[
DM26
]
extend the construction of
[
LSV17
]
to
very good cubic fourfolds
- cubics not containing a plane, a cubic scroll, or admitting a hyperplane section with a corank 3 singularity. Since the locus of cubic fourfolds admitting a hyperplane section with a corank 3 singularity is the closure of
D_{T_{333}}
(see
Remark

2.9
), our results imply the following:

Corollary 1.4
.

A cubic fourfold
X\in\mathcal{C}\setminus(\mathcal{C}_{8}\cup\mathcal{C}_{12}\cup D_{T_{333}})
is very good.

In particular, for a cubic
X\in\mathcal{C}\setminus(\mathcal{C}_{8}\cup\mathcal{C}_{12}\cup D_{T_{333}})
, the construction of
[
DM26
]
produces a smooth projective
OG10
-type hyperkähler
\mathcal{J}_{X}
with a Lagrangian fibration
\pi:\mathcal{J}_{X}\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{5})^{*}
whose fibers are compactified Prym varieties of reduced, irreducible planar curves.

Outline

We recall necessary background on cubic fourfolds, and singular cubic threefolds in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we prove that the divisors described above are indeed irreducible divisors in the moduli of smooth cubic fourfolds. We obtain results on the deformations of singular hyperplane sections which may be of independent interest (Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
3.5
). We complete the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
in Section
4
by utilising the computational methods of Addington–Auel. To implement their method to prove our divisors are non Hassett, we use code developed in
[
AKPW25
,
AKPW24
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout,
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
denotes a smooth cubic fourfold defined over
\mathbb{C}.
We denote by
\mathcal{C}
the moduli space of smooth cubic fourfolds, constructed using GIT
[
Laz09
]
. Recall the following definition
[
Has00
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

A cubic fourfold
X
is
special
if it admits an algebraic cycle of codimension
2
not homologous to a complete intersection.

Equivalently, a smooth cubic fourfold
X
is not special if and only if the algebraic lattice
A(X):=H^{2,2}(X)\cap H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})

is generated by the class
h^{2}
, where
h\in H^{1,1}(X)
is the hyperplane class.

Our goal is to construct divisors in
\mathcal{C}
parametrising geometrically relevant but not special cubic fourfolds. We will do so by parametrising cubics containing a hyperplane section with specific singularities. For a general hyperplane section
Y:=X\cap H
of a general cubic fourfold
X
, we have that
Y
is a smooth cubic threefold.
As
H
varies, the hyperplane section can admit finitely many isolated singularities. Possible combinations of isolated singularities on cubic threefolds were classified in
[
Vik26
]
. We recall some singularity theory definitions and notation.

Definition 2.2
.

For a germ of an isolated hypersurface singularity
(V(g),0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
defined by a polynomial equation
g=0
, the Jacobian ideal of
g
is

J_{g}=\langle\partial g/\partial x_{1},\dots,\partial f/\partial x_{n}\rangle\subset\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},\dots x_{n}]].

The
Tjurina algebra
of
(V(g),0)
is defined to be

T_{g}=\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},\dots x_{n}]]/\langle g,J_{g}\rangle,

and the
Tjurina number
is the dimension of the underlying vector space of
T_{g}

\tau(g)=\dim T_{g}.

The Tjurina algebra of a hypersurface singularity
(V(g),0)
is the same as the space of the first order deformations
T^{1}_{(V(g),0)}
of
(V(g),0)
, and its basis generates a miniversal deformation of
(V(g),0)
with a linear base space (see e.g.
[
GLS07
, Corollary 1.17]
). When we consider the germ of an isolated singularity
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(Y)
, we denote
Y_{p}:=(Y,p).

We denote by

\tau(Y):=\sum_{p\in\mathrm{Sing}(Y)}\tau(Y_{p})

the
total Tjurina number
of a cubic threefold
Y
, where
\tau(Y_{p})
is the Tjurina number of
Y_{p}
.

The possible singularities of hyperplane sections of a general cubic
X
were studied in connection to intermediate Jacobian fibrations by
[
LSV17
]
. In particular, they prove the following:

Proposition 2.3
.

[
LSV17
, Lemma 3.8]

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a general cubic fourfold, and let
Y
be any hyperplane section of
X
. Then the natural morphism

\varphi:H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}(1))\rightarrow\prod_{p\in\mathrm{Sing}Y}T^{1}_{Y_{p}}

is surjective. In other words, the family of deformations of
Y
in
X
induces a versal deformation of the singularities of
Y
.

Notice that
\dim H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}(1))=5
. It follows that for a hyperplane section
Y
of a general cubic fourfold
X
, the total Tijurina number
\tau(Y)
is less than or equal to 5. Conversely, it is shown in
[
Kaz03
]
, that a general cubic fourfold contains hyperplane sections with any combination of singularities possible with total Tjurina number less than or equal to
5
. Thus, we will consider cubic fourfolds with a hyperplane section
Y
with
\tau(Y)=6.
By a dimension count, this locus is expected to be a divisor in
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a cubic threefold with exactly
m
singularities of types
K_{1},\ldots,K_{m}
. We say that
Y
has the combination of singularities
K=K_{1}+\ldots+K_{m}
. We make the following definition:

Definition 2.4
.

If
Y
has the combination of singularities
K
, we say that
Y
is a
K
-cubic threefold
. Further, if
Y
is a hyperplane section of a smooth cubic fourfold, we say that
Y
is a
K
-hyperplane section
.

After a linear change of coordinates, we can assume that one of the singularities of
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is at
p=[1:0:0:0:0]
. We choose
p
to be the worst singularity of
Y
. Then
Y
can be defined by an equation of the form

f:=x_{0}f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})+f_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=0,

where
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
are homogeneous polynomials of degree
2
and
3
, respectively. The geometry of
Y
is closely related to that of the complete intersection curve
C=V(f_{2},f_{3})\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Definition 2.5
.

We define the
corank
of
p
to be equal to the corank of the quadratic form
f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
.

In what follows, we will be concerned with certain
ADE
singularities and the singularity
T_{333}
(the latter also known as
\tilde{E}_{6}
and
P_{8}
), which are defined in
\mathbb{C}^{4}
by the following local equations:

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle:x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{n+1}=0,\;n\geq 1;

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle:x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{4}(x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{n-2})=0,\;n\geq 4;

\displaystyle E_{6}

\displaystyle:x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{3}+x_{4}^{4}=0;

\displaystyle T_{333}

\displaystyle:x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}+x_{4}^{3}+ax_{2}x_{3}x_{4}=0,\;a^{3}+27\neq 0.

The corank is equal to
0
for
A_{1}
singularities, to
1
for
A_{n}
with
n>1
, to
2
for
D_{n}
and
E_{n}
, and to
3
for
T_{333}
. Note that
ADE
and
T_{333}
singularities are quasihomogeneous, thus
\tau(g)
coincides with the Milnor number
\mu(g)
. We have
\tau(A_{n})=n
,
\tau(D_{n})=n
,
\tau(E_{6})=6
and
\tau(T_{333})=8
. The
ADE
singularities are sometimes called 0-modal, whereas
T_{333}
is a unimodal singularity (or modality 1). The modality of a singularity
K
is equal to the dimension of the strata in the miniversal deformation space of constant Milnor number
\mu:=\mu(p)

[
Gab74
]
.

If a singular point
p\in Y
is of corank
2
or
3
, the quadric
f_{2}
defines either two planes or a double plane in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, respectively, which makes the curve
C
and thus
Y
easier to understand in comparison to smaller corank cases. By applying the classification of singularities of cubic threefolds
[
Vik26
]
, we obtain:

Corollary 2.6
.

The possible combinations of singularities on a cubic threefold
Y
with
\tau(Y)=6
and containing a singularity of corank larger than
1
are:

E_{6},D_{6},D_{5}+A_{1},D_{4}+A_{2}\text{ and }D_{4}+2A_{1}.

Let
Y
be a
K
-cubic threefold with
K\in\{E_{6},D_{6},D_{5}+A_{1},D_{4}+A_{2}\}
as above. By
[
Vik26
, Propositions 3.11, 3.14]
, the curve
C
, obtained by projection from the singularity with highest
\tau
in the above combinations, is a union of two irreducible plane cubic curves.
This implies that the defect
\sigma(Y)
is
0
by
[
MV25
, Theorem 1.1]
.
In contrast, if
Y
is a
(D_{4}+2A_{1})
-cubic, the curve
C
can have two or three components, thus we can have
\sigma(Y)=0
or
\sigma(Y)=1
.
Recall that the
defect
of a normal projective variety measures the failure of
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality - if
\sigma(Y)>0
then
Y
contains a divisor that is not Cartier. In particular, cubic threefolds with
\sigma(Y)>0
contain a plane or a cubic scroll by
[
MV25
, Theorem 1.5]
. We obtain:

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
X
be a cubic fourfold not containing a plane or a cubic scroll, i.e.,
X\notin\mathcal{C}_{8}\cup\mathcal{C}_{12}.
Then every hyperplane section
Y:=X\cap H
has
\sigma(Y)=0.

Thus our divisors will parametrise cubic fourfolds with hyperplane sections
Y
with
\sigma(Y)=0.

It will be useful it what follows to have the general form of a
K
-cubic threefold
Y
. Using the projection method, one obtains:

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a
K
-cubic threefold with
K\in\{E_{6},D_{6},D_{5}+A_{1},D_{4}+A_{2},T_{333}\}.
After a linear changes of coordinates, we have that
Y=V(f_{K})
, where
f_{K}
is given as:

\displaystyle f_{E_{6}}

\displaystyle:=x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}q_{1}(x_{1},x_{3},x_{4})+x_{2}q_{2}(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})+x_{1}x_{2}l(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})+x_{3}^{3},

\displaystyle f_{D_{6}}

\displaystyle:=x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}q_{1}(x_{1},x_{3},x_{4})+x_{2}q_{2}(x_{2},x_{3})+x_{2}x_{4}h(x_{2},x_{3})+x_{1}x_{2}l(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})+x_{3}^{2}x_{4},

\displaystyle f_{D_{5}+A_{1}}

\displaystyle:=x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}q_{1}(x_{1},x_{3},x_{4})+x_{2}q_{2}(x_{3},x_{4})+x_{1}x_{2}l(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})+x_{3}^{2}x_{4},

\displaystyle f_{D_{4}+A_{2}}

\displaystyle:=x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}q_{1}(x_{1},x_{3},x_{4})+x_{2}h^{2}(x_{3},x_{4})+x_{1}x_{2}l(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})+x_{3}^{2}x_{4}+x_{4}^{3},

\displaystyle f_{T_{333}}

\displaystyle:=x_{0}x_{1}^{2}+f_{3}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4}).

A
(D_{4}+2A_{1})
-cubic threefold
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
with
\sigma(Y)=0
or
\sigma(Y)=1
can similarly be defined by:

\displaystyle f_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{0}

\displaystyle:=x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}q_{1}(x_{3},x_{4})+x_{2}q_{2}(x_{3},x_{4})+x_{1}x_{2}l(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})+x_{3}^{2}x_{4}+x_{4}^{3},\;\text{or}

\displaystyle f_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{1}

\displaystyle:=x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}q_{1}(x_{1},x_{3},x_{4})+x_{2}x_{4}h(x_{3},x_{4})+x_{1}x_{2}l(x_{1},\ldots,x_{4})+x_{3}^{2}x_{4}+x_{4}^{3}.

Here the polynomials
h
and
l
are linear,
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are homogeneous of degree
2
,
f_{3}
is homogeneous of degree
3
.

Proof.

The statement follows from
[
Vik26
, Propositions 2.2, 3.11, 3.14]
.

Remark 2.9
.

The equations above define
K
-cubic threefolds for general choice of parameters; non-general ones may define cubic threefolds with additional singularities or a worse singularity at
p
. For instance, any cubic threefold singularity of corank

can be defined by an equation of the form

3.
Construction of the divisors

Let us fix
\mathbb{P}^{5}
with coordinates
x_{0},\dots x_{5}.
Recall that the (coarse) moduli space of smooth cubic fourfolds is constructed as
\mathcal{C}:=U//\mathrm{SL}(6),
where
U\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{5},\mathcal{O}(3)))
is the Zariski open subset of smooth cubic forms. Thus
\mathcal{C}
is a 20 dimensional quasi-projective variety. For
K
as in Proposition
2.8
, we denote by
W_{K}\subset U
the locus of cubic forms

(3.1)

F_{K}:=x_{5}q(x_{0},\ldots,x_{5})+f_{K}.

Every cubic fourfold with a
K
-hyperplane section is thus isomorphic to one defined by a cubic form in
W_{K}
.
Let
D_{K}\subset\mathcal{C}
denote the image of the closure of the locus
W_{K}\subset U
.
In this section, we will prove the following result:

Theorem 3.2
.

The loci
D_{K}\subset\mathcal{C}
are irreducible for
K\in\{E_{6},D_{6},D_{5}+A_{1},D_{4}+A_{2},T_{333}\}
. The locus
D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}
has two components, denoted
D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{0}
and
D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{1}
, distinguished by whether the defect
\sigma
is
0
or
1
.

The loci
D_{E_{6}},D_{D_{6}},D_{D_{5}+A_{1}},D_{D_{4}+A_{2}},D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{0},D_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{1}
are irreducible divisors in
\mathcal{C}
, and
D_{T_{333}}
has codimension
2
in
\mathcal{C}
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.2
, we need to analyse the deformation theory of a
K
-hyperplane section inside the ambient cubic fourfold
X
. In Section
3.1
, we will prove that a cubic fourfold
X
admitting a
K
-hyperplane section for
K
as above only admits finitely many of them. In Section
3.2
, we use this result to compute the dimension of
W_{K}
, along with the dimension of the
SL(6)
orbit of a general element of
W_{K}
, allowing us to prove Theorem
3.2
.

3.1.
Deformation theory of
Y

Let
K\in\{E_{6},D_{6},D_{5}+A_{1},D_{4}+A_{2},D_{4}+2A_{1},T_{333}\}
, and let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a general cubic fourfold containing a
K
-hyperplane section
Y
. We can assume that
X:=V(F_{K})
for
F_{K}
as in Equation
3.1
, and that
Y:=V(f_{K},x_{5})
.
Let
W_{K}\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{5},\mathcal{O}(3))
be the locus parameterising cubic fourfolds with a
K
-hyperplane section as above. Note that for
K=D_{4}+2A_{1},
we see that
W_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}=W_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{0}\cup W_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{1}
, corresponding to the two equations
f_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{0}
,
f_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{1}.

We start by analysing the deformations of
Y
in
X
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a cubic fourfold given by a general element in
W_{K}
for
K\in\{E_{6},D_{6},D_{5}+A_{1},D_{4}+A_{2},D_{4}+2A_{1}\}
and let
Y
be the
K
-hyperplane section
X\cap\{x_{5}=0\}
.
Then the map

\varphi:H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}(1))\rightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in\mathrm{Sing}(Y)}T^{1}_{Y_{p}}

is injective.

Proof.

Notice that in order to prove the claim for each
K\in\{E_{6},D_{6},D_{5}+A_{1},D_{4}+A_{2}\}
it is enough to check the statement for one example of
X\in W_{K}
, since injectivity is an open condition. In the case of
D_{4}+2A_{1}
one needs to prove the claim for one example in
W_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{0},
and one in
W_{D_{4}+2A_{1}}^{1}
.
We construct examples of smooth cubic fourfolds
X
with a
K
-hyperplane section using the forms given in Proposition
2.8
via
MAGMA
- we list all the examples in Section
4
.

We first prove the claim for
K=E_{6}
.
Consider the cubic fourfold
X:=\{F=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
, where

\displaystyle F=x_{5}

\displaystyle(89x_{0}^{2}-93x_{5}^{2}-51x_{5}^{2}+37x_{0}x_{4}-86x_{4}x_{5}+87x_{4}^{2}-60x_{0}x_{3}

\displaystyle-7x_{3}x_{5}-100x_{3}x_{4}-100x_{0}x_{2}-18x_{2}x_{5}+31x_{2}x_{4}+52x_{2}x_{3}

\displaystyle+71x_{2}^{2}-32x_{0}x_{1}+86x_{1}x_{5}+x_{1}x_{4}-30x_{1}x_{3}-41x_{1}x_{2}+13x_{1}^{2})+f,

4.1.
The Addington-Auel method

In
[
AA18
]
, Addington and Auel propose a computer-assisted method of verifying whether a divisor
D
in the moduli space of cubic fourfolds
\mathcal{C}
is not a Hassett divisor. The starting point of their method is the proposition below, which we have written to apply directly to the cubic fourfold case:

Proposition 4.2
.

[
AA18
, Proposition 2.1]

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}
,
k=\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
L=\mathbb{Q}
, and let
X_{R}
be a smooth cubic fourfold over
R
.
Let
X^{an}
denote the complex manifold associated to the complex cubic fourfold
X_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
\Phi:X_{k}\rightarrow X_{k}
be the absolute Frobenius morphism, and let
l\neq 2
be a prime number.
Denote by

V=H^{4}_{\textup{\'{e}t},prim}(X_{\bar{k}},\mathbb{Q}_{l}(2))\cong\mathbb{Q}_{l}^{22},

and let
\Phi^{*}
be the automorphism of
V
induced by
\Phi\times 1
on
X_{k}\times\bar{k}
.

Then the rank of
H^{2,2}_{prim}(X^{an},\mathbb{Z})
is bounded above by the number of eigenvalues of
\Phi^{*}
that are roots of unity, counted with multiplicity.

Corollary 4.3
.

[
AA18
, Section 2]

If none of the eigenvalues of
\Phi^{*}
are roots of unity, or equivalently if the characteristic polynomial
\chi(t):=\det(t\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{V}-\Phi^{*})
has no cyclotomic factor, then
X^{an}
is a non-special cubic fourfold. In particular, if
\chi(t)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and not all of its coefficients are integers, then
X^{an}
is non-special.

Addington and Auel developed and implemented an algorithm
[
AA18
, Section 3]
for computing the characteristic polynomial as above.
We briefly outline the steps from
[
AA18
]
one can follow to confirm that a divisor
D\subset\mathcal{C}
is non-Hassett:

(1)

Choose a smooth cubic fourfold
X\in D
defined by an equation with rational coefficients.

(2)

Check if
X
has good reduction modulo
2
.

(3)

If yes in (2), count the points of
X_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}
for all
1\leq m\leq 11
using the conic bundle algorithm explained in
[
AA18
, Section 3]
.

(4)

Find the characteristic polynomial
\chi(t)
using the point counts from the previous step (using the Lefschetz trace formula and Newton’s identities, see
[
AA18
, Section 2]
).

(5)

Check if
\chi(t)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and not all of its coefficients are integers.

(6)

If yes in (5), conclude that
X
is non-special by Corollary
4.3
and that
D
is non-Hassett.

The computations in steps (3), (4) and (5) can be easily done thanks to the library
CubicLib.m
introduced in the accompanying code
[
AKPW24
]
to
[
AKPW25
]
. Alternatively, if one has the full census of cubic fourfolds over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
already compiled, one can simply look up the characteristic polynomial for the reduction
X_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}.

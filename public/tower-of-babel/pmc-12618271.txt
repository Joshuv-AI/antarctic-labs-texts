Repair and Treatment of Type B Aortic Dissection With Patent Ductus Arteriosus–Related Internal Leakage: A Case Report
Authors: Xiaolong Li, Yayun Xiao, Yulin Zhang, Liwei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe coexistence of patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) and aortic dissection aneurysm is a rare clinical occurrence. We report a case of a 51‐year‐old female presenting with type B aortic dissection and PDA‐related internal leakage. Initial management involved thoracic endovascular aortic repair with a single‐branch stent graft. Persistent postoperative symptoms led to further evaluation, revealing an internal leak caused by a false lumen connected to the PDA. The leak was successfully treated with controllable ciliary coil embolization. This case highlights a novel and effective treatment approach for managing such complex conditions, emphasizing the importance of addressing PDA‐related complications in aortic dissection patients.Keywords: acute chest and back pain, aortic dissection, case report, internal leakage, patent ductus arteriosus

Introduction

Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) is an uncommon concurrent finding in patients with aortic dissection aneurysms. While dissecting artery aneurysms are frequently linked to hypertension and hyperlipidemia, the coexistence of PDA introduces unique hemodynamic challenges, including sustained blood flow between false lumens and the pulmonary artery. Herein, we present the first documented case of PDA‐related internal leakage in a patient with type B aortic dissection, successfully treated using controllable ciliary coil embolization. This report aims to expand the understanding of this rare complication and provide insight into innovative therapeutic approaches.

Case History

A 51‐year‐old female presented with acute lower back pain and discomfort persisting for over 5 h. She had an unremarkable medical history, with no prior hypertension, trauma, or connective tissue disorders. Computed tomography angiography (CTA) revealed Stanford type B aortic dissection with an associated aortic arch dissection aneurysm and PDA (Figure 1a). After admission, the patient was given pain relief, blood pressure control, heart rate (systolic blood pressure controlled at 100–120 mmHg, heart rhythm controlled at 60–80 beats per minute), and laxative treatment.

Differential Diagnosis

Acute chest and back pain in patients needs to be differentiated from critical illnesses such as acute pulmonary embolism, acute myocardial infarction, and tension pneumothorax.

Investigations and Treatment

In addition to comprehensive clinical evaluation, routine examinations such as blood count, blood biochemistry, electrocardiogram, and myocardial enzymes are also required. Myocardial calcium protein indicates no significant myocardial ischemia. Cardiac ultrasound indicates normal cardiac function and no pericardial effusion. Preoperative blood gas analysis is used to evaluate whether the patient has respiratory failure.

Treatment: Preoperative planning identified a large false lumen and a small true lumen with the PDA connected to the dissection opening. Due to the insufficient proximal anchoring zone, thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) was performed using a single‐branch covered stent under general anesthesia. Postoperative CTA revealed successful isolation of the dissection; however, the patient reported persistent chest and back pain. Repeat imaging demonstrated an intramural hematoma and internal leakage (Figure 1b). It was found that the PAD was connected to a false lumen of aortic dissection, and the leakage range was approximately around the aortic arch and descending aorta. Conservative management—including strict blood pressure control and cessation of antiplatelet therapy—was implemented for 2 months but yielded no symptom improvement.

On September 5, 2024, surgical closure of the internal leak was performed under local anesthesia. Intraoperative angiography confirmed the internal leak resulted from a false lumen connected to the PDA, with aortic blood flow shunting into the pulmonary artery. Eight controllable ciliary spring coils were deployed to seal the unclosed PDA, successfully occluding the fistula (Figure 2a,b). We used four sizes of spring coils with diameters of 20 × 40, 18 × 40, 15 × 40, and 12 × 40 mm. One is 12 × 40 mm, one is 15 × 40 mm, three are 18 × 40 mm and three are 20 × 40 mm. Postoperative CTA confirmed resolution of the internal leakage (Figure 1c), and the patient experienced complete symptom relief.

Discussion

The coexistence of PDA and type B aortic dissection presents unique clinical challenges. Dissecting artery aneurysms are associated with intimal damage, medial cystic necrosis, hematoma formation, and transverse layer disruption, which can lead to progressive aortic dissection [1]. While thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) is widely regarded as the preferred treatment for type B dissections, concurrent PDA can complicate outcomes due to sustained blood flow from false lumens into the pulmonary artery.

In this case, the patient's dissection extended to the aortic arch, with a proximal anchoring zone measuring < 15 mm distal to the left subclavian artery. TEVAR with a single‐branch stent was performed to optimize anchoring and reduce the risk of long‐term displacement or leakage. Despite this intervention, an internal leak persisted due to PDA‐related hemodynamic abnormalities. The use of controllable ciliary coil embolization successfully resolved the leak, marking the first documented case of this treatment approach in such patients.

Literature on PDA‐related internal leakage remains limited. Zhao et al. reported using an Abbott Vascular occluder to address PDA‐related leakage following stent implantation for a pulmonary artery dissection, achieving favorable results [2].

In our case, technical challenges hindered the use of the occluder and required another approach. Through literature review, we found that embolization using detachable and conventional coils is effective and safe for treating internal leaks after thoracic aortic repair surgery [3]. However, it has not yet been applied to PAD‐related internal leaks. The successful use of controllable ciliary coils in this case highlights their potential as a viable treatment option for complex PDA‐related complications in aortic dissection.

The inspiration from this case is that although PDA was identified anatomically before surgery, we were not aware of the contribution of PAD to the false lumen of the dissection, and the internal leakage related to PAD was not resolved during the operation. This negligence may lead to the development of continuous leakage, which requires a second surgery to solve the problem. This is a reflection point for us.

This case also underscores the importance of considering PDA closure in routine TEVAR procedures for patients with concurrent PDA to prevent similar complications. Further studies are warranted to better understand the interplay between PDA and aortic dissection and to refine treatment strategies for this rare but significant clinical scenario.

Conclusion

This report documents the successful management of type B aortic dissection with PDA‐related internal leakage using controllable ciliary coil embolization. This novel approach offers an effective therapeutic option for patients with similar complex conditions and emphasizes the importance of addressing PDA‐related complications in aortic dissection management.

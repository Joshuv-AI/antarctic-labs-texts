Organizational socialization and new employees’ work adaptability in high-tech enterprises: the mediating role of employee proactiveness
Authors: Shuhua Liu, Chin-Hong Puah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Against the backdrop of rapid technological and organizational change in Chinese high-tech enterprises, newcomer adaptation has become increasingly important. This study examines the associations between organizational socialization strategies and new employees’ work adaptability, with particular attention to the mediating role of employee proactiveness. A quantitative cross-sectional design was adopted, and questionnaire data were collected from 419 new employees in Chinese high-tech enterprises. Data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0 and AMOS 26.0, including exploratory factor analysis, confirmatory factor analysis, structural equation modeling, and bootstrapping to examine the mediating role of employee proactiveness. The results showed that scenario, content, and social strategies were positively associated with employee proactiveness. Employee proactiveness was positively associated with performance proficiency, role clarity, and cultural adaptation, but not with interpersonal relationship adaptation. Scenario strategy was positively associated with all four dimensions of work adaptability, whereas content strategy was positively associated with performance proficiency, role clarity, and interpersonal relationship adaptation, but not with cultural adaptation. Social strategy was positively associated only with performance proficiency. Employee proactiveness significantly mediated the associations between organizational socialization strategies and performance proficiency, role clarity, and cultural adaptation, but not interpersonal relationship adaptation. These findings highlight the differential associations between organizational socialization strategies and dimensions of newcomer work adaptability, while underscoring the mediating role of employee proactiveness in several of these associations. By integrating organizational socialization strategies with proactive behavior, the research extends existing organizational socialization literature and provides practical implications for designing differentiated onboarding practices to support newcomer adaptation in high-tech enterprises.

Introduction

In the twenty-first century, high-tech enterprises have emerged as a critical driving force of global economic development and a strategic pillar enabling countries to overcome the middle-income trap and achieve high-quality sustainable growth (Kou et al., 2023). As major carriers of technological innovation and frontier industrial transformation, high-tech enterprises play an indispensable role in enhancing national innovation capability, facilitating industrial upgrading, and strengthening international competitiveness (Huang et al., 2022; Tu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025). In China, the rapid expansion of high-tech enterprises has generated profound economic and social impacts. By 2024, the number of high-tech enterprises in China had exceeded 500,000, representing an 83% increase compared with 2020 (Qin and Ao, 2025). The industrial clusters and collaborative innovation networks formed by these enterprises have become a key driving force underpinning China’s economic transformation and GDP growth.

High-tech enterprises typically operate in knowledge-intensive and rapidly evolving environments characterized by technological uncertainty, accelerated product iteration, and intensified market competition. Under such conditions, the development of sustainable competitive advantage depends not only on financial investment and technological infrastructure but also on the effective integration and retention of high-quality talent (Wu et al., 2023). Accordingly, talent has become a core strategic resource supporting organizational innovation capability and long-term sustainable development (Liu, 2024). However, in the post-pandemic era, the external environment confronting high-tech enterprises has grown increasingly uncertain, while employees’ expectations regarding career development, organizational support, and work adaptability have continued to rise (Estiana et al., 2025). Against this backdrop, employee turnover has emerged as a major challenge for human resource management and has imposed substantial operational risks and economic costs on high-tech enterprises. Improving newcomers’ work adaptability is therefore critical to mitigating turnover risks, in which organizational socialization plays a vital role.

Existing research has consistently reported a positive association between organizational socialization and newcomer adaptation. Organizational socialization has been associated with employees’ understanding of organizational values, role expectations, and social relationships, as well as with higher organizational commitment and lower turnover intention (Fadahunsi and Olowe, 2025; Bauer et al., 2025). Despite these findings, prior research has primarily examined organizational socialization from a static and outcome-oriented perspective, with limited attention devoted to the theoretical processes through which organizational socialization is associated with employee work adaptability (WA). In particular, the potential mediating role of employee proactiveness has received relatively limited empirical attention, especially in the context of high-tech enterprises (Saks et al., 2011).

Building on Organizational Socialization Theory, Social Cognitive Theory, and the Theory of Work Adjustment, the present study adopts a theory-driven perspective to examine employee work adaptability in organizational settings. Within this perspective, work adaptability is conceptualized as a multidimensional construct reflecting employees’ adjustment to organizational practices, role expectations, interpersonal relationships, and the work environment. Recent research similarly emphasizes that work adaptability can be understood by considering the interrelationships among organizational practices, contextual conditions, and employees’ behavioral engagement (Farías, 2025). Consistent with this perspective, organizational socialization is expected to be positively associated with employees’ acquisition of organizational knowledge, role understanding, and social support, which may, in turn, be associated with higher levels of work adaptability through employee proactiveness.

Accordingly, this study focuses on Chinese high-tech enterprises and proposes a theory-driven conceptual framework linking organizational socialization strategies, employee proactiveness, and work adaptability. Specifically, the study examines the relationships among these constructs, with particular attention to the statistical mediating role of employee proactiveness. By doing so, this study extends the organizational socialization literature by providing empirical evidence regarding the theorized associations among organizational socialization strategies, employee proactiveness, and work adaptability in the context of Chinese high-tech enterprises. Given the cross-sectional design of the study, the proposed framework should be interpreted as a theory-based conceptual model, and the findings reflect theory-driven associations and statistical mediation rather than causal or temporal relationships. Future longitudinal and experimental research is needed to further examine the temporal and causal assumptions underlying the proposed framework.

Literature review and research hypotheses

Organizational socialization and employee proactiveness

Recent studies increasingly conceptualize employee socialization as an active and reciprocal adjustment process rather than a one-sided organizational intervention (Dimitrova et al., 2023). Social Cognitive Theory (SCT) provides an important theoretical foundation for understanding this process by emphasizing the reciprocal interactions among environmental conditions, individual agency, and behavioral responses (Bandura, 1982, 1986). Existing research further suggests that organizational contextual factors are associated with employees’ psychological resources, motivational states, and behavioral engagement (Wang, 2025).

From a systems perspective, organizational socialization can be understood as an organizational subsystem embedded within broader organizational environments rather than merely a static managerial practice. Within this theoretical perspective, work adaptability is viewed as a process shaped by interactions among organizational structures, interpersonal relationships, individual behaviors, and contextual conditions. During organizational entry, organizational socialization provides employees with informational resources, interpersonal support, and psychological reassurance that may reduce uncertainty and assist their adjustment to new work environments. Structured onboarding practices and clear organizational guidance enable employees to better understand organizational expectations and work requirements, thereby providing conditions that encourage proactive behaviors such as information seeking, feedback inquiry, and work exploration (Ellis et al., 2017). Similarly, supportive interpersonal environments may strengthen employees’ willingness to engage in organizational activities and develop workplace relationships.

The relevance of employee proactiveness is particularly salient in high-tech enterprises characterized by rapid innovation, evolving work demands, and high environmental uncertainty. In such contexts, proactive employees are more likely to seek available resources, explore adaptive approaches, and actively participate in their adjustment process. Nevertheless, limited research has examined how organizational socialization strategies are associated with employee proactiveness within high-tech organizational settings (Mitschelen and Kauffeld, 2025). Therefore, drawing on Organizational Socialization Theory and Social Cognitive Theory, organizational socialization strategies are expected to foster employee proactiveness via distinct organizational pathways (Cable and Parsons, 2001). In this study, organizational socialization strategies are operationalized into three dimensions: scenario strategy (SCS), content strategy (CTS), and social strategy (SS). Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed.

H1a: Scenario strategies are positively related to employee proactiveness.

H1b: Content strategies are positively related to employee proactiveness.

H1c: Social strategies are positively related to employee proactiveness.

Employee proactiveness and employee work adaptability

Employee proactiveness (EP) refers to self-initiated, future-oriented behaviors through which individuals actively seek to understand their work environment, acquire relevant information, and improve their ability to accomplish work (Bateman and Crant, 1993). During organizational entry, proactive employees typically engage in behaviors such as information seeking, feedback inquiry, relationship building, and work exploration, which have been widely recognized as important behavioral characteristics of newcomers (Ashforth and Black, 1996). Rather than passively responding to organizational arrangements, proactive employees actively participate in their own adjustment process by identifying available resources and responding to workplace challenges.

The Theory of Work Adjustment (TWA) provides an important theoretical perspective for understanding the relationship between employee proactiveness and work adaptability. According to TWA, work adaptability reflects the correspondence between individual characteristics and work environments and is achieved through continuous interactions between employees and their organizational contexts (Dawis and Lofquist, 1984). Within this perspective, work adaptability is viewed as a multidimensional construct that reflects employees’ adjustment to work, role expectations, interpersonal relationships, and organizational culture rather than as a single organizational outcome.

Previous studies have reported that proactive behaviors are positively associated with newcomer adjustment in organizational settings (Saks et al., 2011). Employees who actively seek information and request feedback are more likely to develop a clearer understanding of organizational expectations and work responsibilities, which may be associated with higher levels of performance proficiency and role clarity (Ellis et al., 2017). Likewise, employees who actively establish workplace relationships may have greater opportunities to obtain informational and social resources that support their interpersonal adjustment. Active exploration of organizational values, norms, and work practices may also contribute to employees’ understanding of organizational culture and facilitate their integration into the organization (Ukawa and Hosoda, 2023).

These relationships are particularly relevant in high-tech enterprises, where employees are frequently required to respond to rapidly changing technologies, knowledge-intensive work, and uncertain work environments (Akkermans et al., 2024). Under such conditions, employee proactiveness represents an important individual behavioral resource that may be associated with different dimensions of work adaptability. Nevertheless, empirical evidence regarding these relationships among newcomers in Chinese high-tech enterprises remains relatively limited. Examining these relationships may therefore contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of newcomer adjustment within knowledge-intensive organizational contexts.

Following Chan and Schmitt (2000), this study conceptualizes work adaptability as comprising four dimensions: performance proficiency (PP), role clarity (RC), interpersonal relationships (IR), and cultural adaptation (CA). Based on the Theory of Work Adjustment and previous research on newcomer proactivity, employee proactiveness is expected to be positively related to each dimension of work adaptability. Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H2a: Employee proactiveness is positively related to performance proficiency.

H2b: Employee proactiveness is positively related to role clarity.

H2c: Employee proactiveness is positively related to interpersonal relationships.

H2d: Employee proactiveness is positively related to cultural adaptation.

Organizational socialization and employee work adaptability

Organizational socialization is widely recognized as an important organizational process through which newcomers acquire the knowledge, role expectations, and social competencies necessary to function effectively within an organization (Chao et al., 1994; Schein, 1968). According to Organizational Socialization Theory (OST), structured socialization practices provide informational, relational, and organizational resources that support newcomers’ understanding of work requirements and organizational norms during organizational entry (Jones, 1986). Rather than representing a one-time onboarding activity, organizational socialization reflects a continuous process through which newcomers become familiar with organizational expectations and workplace practices.

Drawing on the Theory of Work Adjustment (TWA), work adaptability reflects the correspondence between employees and their work environments across multiple dimensions, including performance proficiency, role clarity, interpersonal relationships, and cultural adaptation (Chan and Schmitt, 2000; Dawis and Lofquist, 1984). Organizational socialization strategies may therefore provide organizational resources that are theoretically associated with these dimensions of work adaptability.

In this study, organizational socialization is operationalized through scenario strategies, content strategies, and social strategies. Scenario strategies emphasize structured work arrangements and role clarification, which may be associated with employees’ understanding of job responsibilities and work expectations. Content strategies focus on providing organizational knowledge, work procedures, and work-related information that may support employees’ acquisition of role-related knowledge and organizational understanding. Social strategies emphasize interpersonal interaction, communication, and organizational support, providing newcomers with opportunities to establish workplace relationships and become familiar with organizational culture (Gardner et al., 2022).

Previous studies have reported positive associations between organizational socialization and various employee outcomes, including organizational commitment, job satisfaction, work performance, and newcomer adjustment (Akkermans et al., 2024; Gardner et al., 2022). However, empirical evidence examining how different organizational socialization strategies are associated with multiple dimensions of work adaptability remains relatively limited, particularly in Chinese high-tech enterprises. Addressing this gap may contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of newcomer adaptation within knowledge-intensive organizational settings.

Based on Organizational Socialization Theory and the Theory of Work Adjustment, this study proposes that each dimension of organizational socialization strategies is positively related to each dimension of work adaptability. Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H3a: Scenario strategies are positively related to performance proficiency.

H3b: Scenario strategies are positively related to role clarity.

H3c: Scenario strategies are positively related to interpersonal relationships.

H3d: Scenario strategies are positively related to cultural adaptation.

H3e: Content strategies are positively related to performance proficiency.

H3f: Content strategies are positively related to role clarity.

H3g: Content strategies are positively related to interpersonal relationships.

H3h: Content strategies are positively related to cultural adaptation.

H3i: Social strategies are positively related to performance proficiency.

H3j: Social strategies are positively related to role clarity.

H3k: Social strategies are positively related to interpersonal relationships.

H3l: Social strategies are positively related to cultural adaptation.

The mediating role of employee proactiveness

Although previous studies have separately examined organizational socialization, employee proactiveness, and work adaptability, relatively limited research has integrated these constructs within a single theoretical framework, particularly in the context of high-tech enterprises (Mumpuni and Handayani, 2024). Drawing on Social Cognitive Theory (SCT), organizational environments are associated with employees’ behavioral responses through reciprocal interactions among environmental conditions, individual cognition, and behavioral engagement (Bandura, 1986). Within this perspective, organizational socialization strategies may provide informational, relational, and organizational resources that support newcomers’ proactive engagement during organizational entry.

Employee proactiveness reflects self-initiated behaviors through which newcomers actively seek information, request feedback, establish workplace relationships, and acquire organizational knowledge (Ashforth and Black, 1996; Saks et al., 2011). These behaviors are theoretically associated with employees’ understanding of work requirements, role expectations, interpersonal interactions, and organizational culture, which together represent important dimensions of work adaptability.

In high-tech enterprises, where employees frequently encounter rapidly changing technologies, complex work, and uncertain organizational environments, proactive behavioral engagement may represent an important pathway through which organizational socialization strategies are associated with newcomer work adaptability. Nevertheless, empirical evidence regarding this mediating relationship remains limited, particularly in Chinese high-tech enterprises.

Based on Organizational Socialization Theory, Social Cognitive Theory, and the Theory of Work Adjustment, this study proposes that employee proactiveness statistically mediates the relationships between organizational socialization strategies and work adaptability. Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H4a: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between scenario strategy and performance proficiency

H4b: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between scenario strategy and role clarity

H4c: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between scenario strategy and interpersonal relationships

H4d: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between scenario strategy and cultural adaptation

H4e: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between content strategy and performance proficiency

H4f: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between content strategy and role clarity

H4g: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between content strategy and interpersonal relationships

H4h: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between content strategy and cultural adaptation

H4i: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between social strategy and performance proficiency

H4j: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between social strategy and role clarity

H4k: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between social strategy and interpersonal relationships

H4l: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between social strategy and cultural adaptation

Research framework

This study integrates Organizational Socialization Theory, Social Cognitive Theory, and the Theory of Work Adjustment into a theory-informed conceptual framework to examine how organizational contextual factors and employee proactive behaviors are associated with work adaptability outcomes. Based on the preceding theoretical discussions, this study proposes a conceptual model linking organizational socialization strategies, employee proactiveness, and work adaptability.

Specifically, organizational socialization strategies are expected to be directly associated with work adaptability and indirectly associated with work adaptability through employee proactiveness. The proposed framework represents a process-oriented conceptual model that illustrates the theoretically proposed relationships among organizational practices, employee behavioral responses, and adaptation outcomes within high-tech organizational contexts (Figure 1).

H1: Organizational socialization strategies (scenario, content, and social strategies) are positively related to employee proactiveness; H2: Employee proactiveness is positively related to new employees’ work adaptability (including performance proficiency, role clarity, interpersonal relationships, and cultural adaptation); H3: Organizational socialization strategies are positively related to new employees’ work adaptability; H4: Employee proactiveness mediates the relationship between organizational socialization strategies and new employees’ work adaptability.

Materials and methods

Research design

This study employed a quantitative cross-sectional design to examine the relationships among organizational socialization, employee proactiveness, and work adaptability. Data were collected using a structured self-administered questionnaire and analyzed with SPSS 25.0 and AMOS 26.0. Descriptive statistics, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), structural equation modeling (SEM), and bootstrap mediation analysis were conducted.

Organizational socialization (scenario strategy, content strategy, and social strategy) was specified as the independent variable, employee proactiveness as the mediating variable, and work adaptability (performance proficiency, role clarity, interpersonal relationships, and cultural adaptation) as the dependent variable. Demographic variables, including age and organizational tenure, were collected and incorporated as control variables in the structural model to account for their potential influence on employee work adaptability. Other demographic characteristics were collected and reported solely for sample description.

Research participants

Jinan is one of China’s major high-tech industrial hubs, hosting numerous nationally recognized high-tech enterprises across software, electronic information, and intelligent manufacturing sectors. The target population consisted of employees from high-tech enterprises in Jinan, China. The participating enterprises were selected based on their official recognition as high-tech enterprises under China’s High-Tech Enterprise Recognition and Management framework, which emphasizes technological innovation capability, research and development activities, and knowledge-intensive business operations. Data were collected between June and August 2024 from three high-tech enterprises operating in the software, electronic information, and intelligent manufacturing sectors.

The selected enterprises represent different ownership structures, organizational sizes, and high-tech industry categories, providing a suitable context for examining newcomer work adaptability. Questionnaires were distributed online through internal company platforms, and participation was anonymous, voluntary, and confidential. A total of 450 questionnaires were distributed. After applying the screening criteria, 31 invalid questionnaires were excluded from the 450 collected responses. Specifically, 8 responses were removed because respondents did not meet the eligibility criteria, 4 contained substantial missing data ( > 10% unanswered items), 9 were completed within less than 15 min, 3 showed inconsistent responses to screening questions, 5 exhibited repetitive or straight-line response patterns, and 2 were identified as duplicate submissions through the survey platform. Therefore, 419 valid questionnaires were retained for subsequent analysis. The final sample size was considered adequate for structural equation modeling analysis (Bonds-Raacke, 2019; Wolf et al., 2013).

Given that organizational socialization is a continuous adjustment process rather than a short-term event limited to the initial entry period, this study adopted a broader definition of newcomers. Specifically, employees with organizational tenure of 3 years or less were included as newcomers (He et al., 2020). This operational criterion is appropriate for identifying employees who are still likely to experience organizational socialization and adjustment processes, particularly in high-tech enterprises characterized by continuous learning and technological change (Van Maanen and Schein, 1979; Morrison, 1993; Bauer et al., 2007; Saks and Gruman, 2018).

Research measures

All variables were measured using a 5-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree), with higher scores representing higher levels of the construct. All scales were adapted from well-established international instruments and appropriately revised to fit the Chinese organizational context.

Organizational socialization was measured based on Jones (1986) organizational socialization tactics framework. The original six tactics were grouped into three dimensions: scenario strategy (collective and formal tactics), content strategy (sequential and fixed tactics), and social strategy (serial and investiture tactics), representing contextual, informational, and relational mechanisms of newcomer socialization. Employee proactiveness was measured using Frese et al.’s (1997) proactive behavior scale, consisting of seven items assessing self-initiated problem-solving, opportunity identification, and work improvement behaviors. Work adaptability was measured drawing on Morrison’s (1993) foundational work on newcomer adjustment and relevant scholarship on organizational entry adaptation (Chan and Schmitt, 2000). The construct covers four dimensions: performance proficiency, role clarity, interpersonal relationships, and cultural adaptation. Performance proficiency and role clarity were adapted from Morrison (1993) and Bauer et al. (2007). Interpersonal relationships originate from studies of interpersonal integration (Chao et al., 1994; Morrison, 1993), while cultural adaptation draws on research regarding organizational culture assimilation (Chao et al., 1994).

To ensure measurement equivalence, the questionnaire items were translated into Chinese and back-translated into English by bilingual researchers. The adapted items were further reviewed by experts in organizational behavior and human resource management and refined based on a pilot test involving 45 new employees from three high-tech enterprises. Reliability and construct validity were assessed through Cronbach’s alpha, composite reliability (CR), average variance extracted (AVE), exploratory factor analysis (EFA), and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA).

Subsequently, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was performed in AMOS to assess the measurement model and examine convergent and discriminant validity. Based on the validated measurement model, structural equation modeling (SEM) was conducted to test the hypothesized relationships among variables. In addition, the Bootstrap method with 5,000 resamples was employed to examine the significance of indirect effects and verify the robustness of the mediation results. Given that this study aimed to test theoretically established relationships among latent constructs and assess the overall model fit, covariance-based SEM (CB-SEM) using AMOS 26.0 was selected as the appropriate analytical approach (Byrne, 2016; Hair et al., 2019).

Common method bias test

To evaluate common method bias (CMB), the Common Latent Factor (CLF) approach was adopted following Podsakoff et al. (2003), Williams et al. (2010), and Fuller et al. (2016). A common latent factor was incorporated into the baseline CFA model while the original measurement structure was preserved, with the variance of this latent factor freely estimated. Standardized item loadings were compared between the baseline model and the CLF-augmented model, and absolute loading differences were calculated. As shown in Table 4, except for χ2/df, all differences were below 0.10, suggesting no substantial changes in the measurement relationships.

To assess common method bias (CMB), Harman’s single-factor test was conducted using an unrotated principal component analysis. In addition, a Common Latent Factor (CLF) was incorporated into the baseline CFA model while retaining the original theoretical measurement structure, with all 34 measurement items loading simultaneously on their respective theoretical constructs and the global CLF. The CLF was specified as uncorrelated with the theoretical constructs, and its variance was freely estimated. Standardized loadings from the baseline and CLF models were compared, with all absolute differences below 0.20, indicating no substantial changes in the measurement loadings. Overall, the results did not indicate that a single method factor dominated the variance of the variables (Podsakoff et al., 2012).

Prior to conducting structural equation modeling (SEM), multicollinearity diagnostics were performed to examine whether excessive correlations existed among predictor variables. This assessment was particularly important because the three dimensions of organizational socialization tactics (SCS, CTS, and SS) represent conceptually related constructs and may demonstrate moderate correlations. Multicollinearity was assessed using tolerance values and variance inflation factors (VIF), with tolerance values above 0.20 and VIF values below 5.0 considered acceptable thresholds. As shown in Table 5, all tolerance values ranged from 0.481 to 0.930, while all VIF values ranged from 1.075 to 2.079, indicating that multicollinearity was not a concern among the predictor variables.

Confirmatory factor analysis

Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was conducted to assess the reliability and validity of the measurement model. Following previous studies (Fornell and Larcker, 1981; Henseler et al., 2015; Chong et al., 2014), AMOS 26.0 was employed for model evaluation. As shown in Table 6, the measurement model demonstrated an acceptable fit (χ2/df = 2.666, RMSEA = 0.063, GFI = 0.839, AGFI = 0.808, IFI = 0.928, TLI = 0.918, and CFI = 0.927). Although GFI and AGFI were below the conventional threshold of 0.90, model fit should be evaluated using multiple indices rather than a single criterion (Hu and Bentler, 1999). Given the satisfactory values of χ/2df, RMSEA, IFI, TLI, and CFI, the measurement model was considered acceptable for subsequent structural analysis. Additionally, the eight-factor model showed substantially better fit than the single-factor model (χ2/df = 9.200, RMSEA = 0.140, CFI = 0.622), supporting the discriminant validity of the constructs.

Convergent validity

Convergent validity and internal consistency reliability were examined using factor loadings (FL), composite reliability (CR), and average variance extracted (AVE). The results showed that all factor loadings ranged from 0.753 to 0.961, exceeding the recommended threshold of 0.70. Discussion

Discussion of findings

Organizational socialization and work adaptability

This study provides a contextualized empirical extension of existing research by examining the associations among organizational socialization strategies, employee proactiveness, and multidimensional work adaptability within Chinese high-tech enterprises. Rather than treating work adaptability as a static organizational outcome, this study conceptualizes it as a multidimensional adjustment process linked to interactions between organizational contextual conditions and employee behavioral responses. Drawing on Organizational Socialization Theory, Social Cognitive Theory, and the Theory of Work Adjustment, this study explores how organizational environments relate with work adaptability within complex and changing workplace contexts.

The findings suggest that organizational socialization strategies are positively associated with work adaptability among employees in high-tech enterprises. Due to the characteristics of high-tech industries, including rapid technological iteration, intensive knowledge exchange, and continuously evolving work demands, employees often experience ambiguity regarding organizational expectations, job responsibilities, and interpersonal relationships. Organizational socialization strategies are related to greater understanding of organizational norms and lower perceived adaptation uncertainty, particularly in contexts where standardized informational resources, psychological support, and relational assistance are available. These findings align with the core assumptions of Organizational Socialization Theory, which proposes that structured organizational practices are related with employees’ acquisition of organizational knowledge and role clarity, both of which are relevant to successful organizational transition and workplace adjustment (Jones, 1986).

Among the three dimensions of organizational socialization strategies, social strategies showed the largest coefficient for employee proactiveness. One possible explanation is that social strategies may be particularly relevant in high-tech enterprises, where collaboration, knowledge sharing, and interpersonal interaction are important features of work. Through mentorship mechanisms, peer communication channels, and supportive communication climates, social strategies may provide informational and emotional support and may be associated with greater proactive participation in organizational affairs (Gong et al., 2026).

Employee proactiveness and work adaptability

The findings further demonstrate that employee proactiveness serves as an important behavioral mechanism associated with work adaptability, which is consistent with the assumptions of Social Cognitive Theory (Bandura, 1982, 1986). Organizational environments are linked to employees’ motivational states, self-regulatory processes, and behavioral tendencies, which are subsequently reflected in behavioral outcomes. Organizational socialization strategies are characterized by supportive environments related to employees’ occupational confidence and psychological acceptance, which may in turn be associated with proactive behaviors such as information seeking, feedback inquiry, and work exploration.

These proactive behaviors are associated with employees’ acquisition of organizational knowledge, greater clarity regarding work requirements, and deeper organizational understanding, as well as with adaptation within complex work environments. Employee proactiveness exhibited positive associations with three dimensions of work adaptability: performance proficiency, role clarity, and cultural adaptation, but its direct association with interpersonal relationship adaptation was not statistically significant.

This result may reflect the different relational characteristics of the dimensions of work adaptability. Interpersonal relationships rely more heavily on reciprocal interaction, team acceptance, and supportive organizational climates (Kammeyer-Mueller and Wanberg, 2003; Schneider et al., 2013). Therefore, interpersonal integration may reflect not only individual proactive behaviors but also broader organizational social systems and team relational structures.

The mediating role of employee proactiveness in the relationship between organizational socialization and work adaptability

The mediating role of employee proactiveness provides further insight into how organizational socialization strategies are associated with newcomer work adaptability. The findings indicate that organizational socialization strategies are not only directly related to adaptation outcomes but also show indirect associations with adaptation outcomes through employees’ proactive behavioral responses. This suggests that organizational practices provide contextual resources, while employees actively utilize these resources through information seeking, role adjustment, and adaptive behaviors.

From the perspective of Social Cognitive Theory, the findings highlight the reciprocal relationship between organizational environments and employee agency. Organizational socialization strategies are associated with supportive conditions and proactive behavioral responses, and these proactive behaviors are subsequently associated with adaptation outcomes. Therefore, employee proactiveness represents an important behavioral mechanism linking organizational socialization practices with newcomer adjustment in high-tech enterprises.

However, the mediation effects varied across different dimensions of work adaptability. Employee proactiveness demonstrated stronger mediating effects for performance proficiency, role clarity, and cultural adaptation, whereas its mediating effect on interpersonal relationships was weaker or unsupported in some paths. This finding suggests that proactive behaviors may be more effective in addressing work-related and cognitive adaptation demands, while interpersonal relationships may require additional relational resources, such as supervisor support, peer acceptance, and social network integration.

Theoretical contributions

At the theoretical level, this study contributes to the literature in three ways. First, it provides a contextualized empirical extension of existing research on organizational socialization, employee proactiveness, and multidimensional work adaptability within Chinese high-tech enterprises. Examining these relationships in a knowledge-intensive organizational context offers further insights into the associations between organizational socialization strategies and newcomer adaptation outcomes.

Second, this study extends previous research by examining the behavioral mechanism of employee proactiveness in the relationship between organizational socialization and work adaptability. The findings suggest that organizational socialization strategies are associated with adaptation outcomes partly through employees’ proactive behaviors, highlighting the role of individual agency in utilizing organizational resources during newcomer adjustment.

Third, this study advances the understanding of work adaptability as a multidimensional construct by demonstrating that employee proactiveness does not uniformly relate to all adaptation dimensions. Specifically, employee proactiveness was positively associated with performance proficiency, role clarity, and cultural adaptation, but not interpersonal relationships. This finding suggests that work-related and cognitive adaptation may rely more strongly on proactive behaviors, whereas interpersonal relationships may require additional relational resources, such as supervisor support, peer acceptance, and social integration opportunities.

Practical implications

The findings provide several practical implications for high-tech enterprises. First, organizations should adopt structured organizational socialization strategies, such as clear onboarding, role clarification, and timely organizational support, to facilitate newcomer adjustment. Second, managers should foster employee proactiveness by encouraging feedback seeking, continuous learning, and open communication. Third, mentoring and supportive workplace relationships should be strengthened to facilitate newcomers’ social integration and work adaptation. As organizational adjustment is a continuous process in high-tech enterprises, HR managers should provide ongoing developmental support beyond the initial onboarding stage. These practices may be associated with newcomer work adaptability and may have implications for longer-term employee retention.

Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations. First, the sample was limited to three high-tech enterprises in Jinan, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings. Future research may extend the sample across different regions, organizational contexts, and industries to enhance external validity.

Second, this study defined newcomers as employees with organizational tenure of 3 years or less. Although this criterion is consistent with prior research, employees within this range may experience different stages of adjustment. Future studies may adopt more refined tenure criteria and further examine the robustness of the findings across different stages of newcomer adjustment.

Third, this study adopted a cross-sectional design, which limits the ability to examine temporal changes and establish causal relationships among organizational socialization strategies, employee proactiveness, and work adaptability. The findings should therefore be interpreted as theoretically informed associations and mediation relationships rather than evidence of developmental processes over time. Future research may employ longitudinal designs to further investigate how work adaptability develops and changes across different stages of organizational entry and adjustment.

Fourth, employee proactiveness was treated as a single construct. Future studies may further examine different dimensions of proactive behavior and incorporate additional moderating variables, such as leadership style and team climate, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of work adaptability mechanisms.

Fifth, the study relied primarily on self-reported questionnaire data collected at a single point in time. Although procedural and statistical approaches were employed to assess common method bias, the possibility of residual method-related bias cannot be completely excluded. Future research could combine employee self-reports with supervisor-rated performance or objective indicators and adopt multi-wave designs to strengthen the robustness of the findings.

Conclusion

The results suggest that organizational socialization strategies are positively associated with employee proactiveness, which in turn is positively related to performance proficiency, role clarity, and cultural adaptation. Furthermore, Employee proactiveness significantly mediated several relationships between organizational socialization strategies and work adaptability, particularly those involving performance proficiency, role clarity, and cultural adaptation.

Overall, this study highlights the important role of employee proactiveness in the organizational socialization process and provides empirical evidence regarding the potential mediating role of employee proactiveness underlying the relationships between organizational socialization strategies and employees’ work adaptability. By doing so, the study contributes to the existing literature on organizational socialization and work adaptability. Moreover, the findings offer important theoretical and practical implications for organizations seeking to refine newcomer socialization practices and support employees’ long-term adaptation and organizational integration.

1.
Introduction

Let
L
be a number field and
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
L
. Denote by
L(E/L,s)
the Hasse-Weil complex
L
-function of
E
over
L
and by
\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\Pi\kern-3.60992pt\Pi$}(E/L)
the Tate-Shafarevich group of
E
over
L
. It is expected that
L(E/L,s)
can be analytically continued to all of
\mathbb{C}
and it will satisfy a functional equation relating its values at
s
and
2-s
. Further, a part of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture states that
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\hskip 0.02843pt\hskip 2.84526ptE(L)=\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/L,s)
and that
\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\Pi\kern-3.60992pt\Pi$}(E/L)
is finite. For
L=\mathbb{Q}
, combining the celebrated works of Gross-Zagier
[
13
]
and Kolyvagin
[
18
]
, one has the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, for
r\in\{0,1\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/\mathbb{Q},s)=r\implies\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\hskip 0.02843pt\hskip 2.84526ptE(\mathbb{Q})=r\text{ and }\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\Pi\kern-3.60992pt\Pi$}(E/\mathbb{Q})\text{ is finite.}

For a totally real number field
L
, under suitable hypotheses, an analogue of Theorem
1.1
has been established by Zhang
[
34
]
(see
[
34
, Theorem A]
for a precise statement).

Now fix once and for all, a real quadratic field
F
of discriminant
D_{F}
. As
F
is a real quadratic field, it is known due to
[
10
]
that an elliptic curve
E
defined over
F
is modular and hence

can be analytically continued to the entire complex plane. Further, in the above setting, the following result is a special case of
[
34
, Theorem A]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a real quadratic field
F
. Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a (finite) prime of
F
and assume that the conductor of
E/F
,
\mathfrak{n}_{E}=\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{q}
with
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}
. Then, for
r\in\{0,1\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/F,s)=r\implies\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\hskip 0.02843pt\hskip 2.84526ptE(F)=r\text{ and }\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\Pi\kern-3.60992pt\Pi$}(E/F)\text{ is finite.}

Throughout, we also fix a rational prime
p>5
which is inert in
F
and let
\mathfrak{p}
be the prime in
F

p
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
F
of conductor
\mathfrak{n}_{E}
. We assume that
\mathfrak{n}_{E}=\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{n}
and
E
has split multiplicative reduction at the prime

. We aim to prove for the rank

-converse" of Theorem

when

in

. More precisely, let

and

be as in Section

.

(Theorem

)
Let
p>5

is inert in

. Denote by

, the prime of

be a real quadratic field such that

\mathfrak{p}

1.1.
Previous work

There has been a considerable interest in establishing various ‘
p
-converse’ results to the theorems of Gross-Zagier and Kolyvagin (Theorem
1.1
) in the literature in the recent past. The choice of the prime
p
and the reduction type of the elliptic curves at
p
play an important role in these results. When
r=0
, i.e., in the rank
0
case,
p
-converse theorems to Theorem
1.1
are related to one sided divisibility in the Iwasawa Main Conjecture (cf.
[
26
]
and
[
28
]
). In the rank
1
, i.e.,
r=1
case, for a non-CM elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, important results on
p
-converse theorems were established independently, around the same time, by
[
29
]
and W. Zhang
[
35
]
, under some hypotheses on the conductor of
E/\mathbb{Q}
. The prime
p
involved in both the cases, was an ordinary prime for the elliptic curve and Castella-Wan extends
p
-converse theorem for a prime
p
of (good) supersingular reduction of
E
in
[
8
]
.
[
17
]
proves a "soft"
p
-converse assuming isomorphism of a certain local restriction map at the prime
p
. For an Eisenstein prime
p
, a
p
-converse result appears in
[
7
]
. For a prime
p
of multiplicative reduction of
E/\mathbb{Q}
, a
p
-converse result in
r=1
case has been proven in
[
27
]
and
[
9
]
, under certain hypotheses. Moreover, under a different set of hypotheses, Venerucci
[
32
]
has also proven a
p
-converse theorem in the rank
1
case, for a prime
p
of split multiplicative reduction of an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
. The approach for proving a
p
-converse theorem taken for a CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
is distinct from that of a non-CM elliptic curve and important results in this setting are due to Rubin, Burungale-Tian
[
3
]
, and many others. We refer to
[
2
]
for a comprehensive survey of literature on
p
-converse theorems. More recently, a rank zero
p
-converse result has been proven in
[
4
]
for an elliptic curve defined over an imaginary quadratic field. Furthermore, rank-1
p
-converse results under different sets of hypotheses have appeared in
[
6
]
and
[
5
]
.

However, almost all of these results are valid for elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. In this article, we extend the strategy of
[
32
]
to prove for the first time a similar result beyond
\mathbb{Q}
, or in other words, we discuss a "
p
-converse" result for a real quadratic field, of the rank
1
case of Theorem
1.2
. Moreover, for
p>5
, in Section
10
, we strengthen the
p
-converse result over
\mathbb{Q}
in
[
32
]
by removing a technical assumption (hypothesis 2) in
[
32
, Theorem A]
.

1.2.
Outline of the proof:

We have employed various new ingredients to establish our
p
-converse results - for example, a two-variable
p
-adic
L
-function constructed by
[
20
]
, a central derivative formula in
[
21
]
, a three-variable
p
-adic
L
-function and the main conjecture proven in
[
33
]
, the interpolation formula for
p
-adic
L
-functions for Hilbert modular forms
[
1
]
. We explain the usage of some of these in the rest of this subsection.

Since
F
is a real quadratic field, there is a cuspidal Hilbert newform
f:=f_{E}
of parallel weight 2 over
F
, with trivial Nebentypus attached to
E/F
by the modularity theorem in
[
10
]
. Let
\mathbf{f}
be the Hida family that passes through
f
(for details, see Section
2.2
).
We begin by mentioning the main result of
[
33
]
.

Let
K
be a totally imaginary quadratic extension of
F
such that
\mathfrak{p}
splits in
K
and let
K_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{3}
-extension of
K
(for details, see Section
3.1
).

Under the hypotheses in Section
3.1
, Wan constructs a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}^{S}_{K}(\mathbf{f})
, where
S
is a finite set of non-archimedean primes containing all the bad primes of
E/F
. Let
\mathrm{char}^{S}_{K_{\infty}}(\mathbf{f})
be the characteristic ideal attached to the Pontryagin dual of the Selmer group
Sel_{K_{\infty}}^{S}(\mathbf{f})
as defined in Section
3.2
. Then in
[
33
, Theorem 101]
, it is proven that

\displaystyle\mathrm{char}^{S}_{K_{\infty}}(\mathbf{f})\subset(\mathcal{L}^{S}_{K}(\mathbf{f})).

Using the Iwasawa main conjecture of Wan, in Corollary
4.2
we deduce the inequality

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{k=2}L_{p}^{cc}(f_{\infty}/K,k)\leq\mathrm{len}_{P}(X_{F_{\infty}}^{S,cc}(\mathbf{f}/K)).

Here the notations are as follows. Let
\mathbb{I}
be a local domain which is finite over
\Lambda
(for details, see Section
2.2
).
P
is the prime of
\mathbb{I}
which specialises the Hida family
\mathbf{f}
to
f
and
L_{p}^{cc}(f_{\infty}/K,k)
is defined using
\mathcal{L}_{K}^{S}(\mathbf{f})
(for details, see Section
4.1
). As shown in (
13
),
L_{p}^{cc}(f_{\infty}/K,k)=\lambda L_{p}(f_{\infty},1,k,k/2)L_{p}(f_{\infty},\epsilon_{K},k,k/2)
is the product (up to an element
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{*}
) of two-variable
p
-adic
L
-functions defined in
[
20
]
evaluated at the trivial character 1 and at the quadratic character
\epsilon_{K}
, which is the quadratic character associated with
K
.
\mathrm{len}_{P}(\star)
denotes the length of
\star_{P}
as an
\mathbb{I}_{P}
-module.
X_{F_{\infty}}^{S,cc}(\mathbf{f}/K)
is the Pontryagin dual of the following Selmer group:

\displaystyle Sel^{S,cc}_{F_{\infty}}(\mathbf{f}/K):=ker\left(H^{1}(G_{K,S_{K}},\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f}}\otimes_{\mathbb{I}}\mathbb{I}^{*})\xrightarrow{\prod_{w|\mathfrak{p}}p_{w_{*}}^{-}\circ\hskip 0.00285ptres_{w}}\prod_{w|\mathfrak{p}}H^{1}(I_{w},\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f},w}^{-}\otimes_{\mathbb{I}}\mathbb{I}^{*})\right),

where
S_{K}
is the set of primes of
K
lying above the primes in
S
.
Here
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f}}
is the 2-dimensional self-dual representation associated with the Hida family
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f},w}^{+}
is a rank 1
\mathbb{I}
-submodule. They fit into the following exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f},w}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f}}\rightarrow\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f},w}^{-}\rightarrow 0.

For more details, see Section
2.4
.

Next, in
[
21
]
, Mok proved that for
\psi\in\{1,\epsilon_{K}\}
, the function
L_{p}(f_{\infty},\psi,k,k/2)
vanishes to order at least 2 at
k=2
and that there exists an element
\mathbb{P}_{\psi}\in(E(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q})_{\psi}
and
l\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dk^{2}}L_{p}(f_{\infty},\psi,k,k/2)\vline_{k=2}=l\cdot(\log\mathrm{Norm}_{E/F_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathbb{P}_{\psi}))^{2},

using which we show in Corollary
5.2
that under the assumption that the global root number
w(E/K)
is
-1
, we have that

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{k=2}L_{p}^{cc}(f_{\infty}/K,k)\geq 4

and moreover,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{k=2}L_{p}^{cc}(f_{\infty}/K,k)=4\iff\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=2,

where
L(E/K,s)
is the Hasse-Weil complex
L
-function of
E
over
K
and one has the factorisation
L(E/K,s)=L(E/F,s)L(E/F,\epsilon_{K},s)
, i.e.,
L(E/K,s)
is the product of the complex
L
-function of
E/F
with the complex
L
-function of
E/F
twisted by
\epsilon_{K}
.

Now, define the strict Selmer group of
\mathbf{f}/K
as

\displaystyle Sel^{cc}_{Gr}(\mathbf{f}/K):=ker\left(H^{1}(G_{K,S},\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f}}\otimes_{\mathbb{I}}\mathbb{I}^{*})\rightarrow\prod_{w|\mathfrak{p}}H^{1}(K_{w},\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{f},w}^{-}\otimes_{\mathbb{I}}\mathbb{I}^{*})\right)

and let
X^{cc}_{Gr}(\mathbf{f}/K)
denote its Pontryagin dual. Using
[
22
]
, we define an alternating,
Gal(K/F)
-equivariant, bilinear pairing
\langle-,-\rangle^{Nek}_{K}
on Selmer complexes. Then using some Kummer theory and this pairing, we conclude by Theorem
7.1
that

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{len}_{P}(X^{cc}_{Gr}(\mathbf{f}/K))=2.

The calculations in Lemma
9.1
, give us the following inequality

(5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{len}_{P}(X^{S,cc}_{F_{\infty}}(\mathbf{f}/K))\leq\mathrm{len}_{P}(X^{cc}_{Gr}(\mathbf{f}/K))+2.

Inequalities (
1
),(
2
),(
4
) and (
5
) allow us to conclude that

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{k=2}L_{p}^{cc}(f_{\infty}/K,k)=4

and hence by (
3
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=2.

Now by the choice of the field
K
that we make in Section

, we get our result that

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{s=1}L(E/F,s)=1.

1.3.
Future Outlook

Assuming the modularity of the elliptic curve
E/L
and some other technical conditions, one can hope to extend these techniques to a general totally real number field
L
for which Leopoldt’s conjecture holds true (in particular, for any abelian totally real number field). We also indicate some possible extensions of our main results in Remark
10.2
.

However, note that for a CM elliptic curve defined over a real quadratic field, one would expect that the techniques required to prove a
p
-converse result would be rather different.

We start with some preliminaries on the Hida theory following

Recall we have fixed a real quadratic field
F

2.1.
Hecke Algebra

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and for every
k\geq 2
, let
h_{k}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}^{\infty},\mathcal{O})
be the
p
-ordinary Hecke algebra whose coefficients lie in
\mathcal{O}
, where
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}
is the conductor of
E/F
. Let
Cl_{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha})
be the narrow ray class group of
F
modulo
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\geq 1
. Let
Z_{F}(\mathfrak{n})=\varprojlim_{\alpha}Cl_{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha})
. Then we can decompose
Z_{F}(\mathfrak{n})=W_{F}\times Z_{F}(\mathfrak{n})_{tor}
, where
W_{F}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-free and
Z_{F}(\mathfrak{n})_{tor}
is finite. Let
Z_{\alpha}
denote the kernel of the natural projection
Z_{F}(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow Cl_{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha})
and let
W_{\alpha}:=W_{F}\cap Z_{\alpha}
. Define the following Iwasawa algebras

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\varprojlim_{\alpha}\mathcal{O}[W_{F}/W_{\alpha}],\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{A}:=\varprojlim_{\alpha}\mathcal{O}[Cl_{F}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha})].

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
be the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Write
\Gamma
for the image of
Gal(F\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/F)\hookrightarrow Gal(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(note that
\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
) and fix its topological generator
\gamma
. We have
\Lambda\cong\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma]]
and
\mathcal{A}=\Lambda[Z_{F}(\mathfrak{n})_{tor}]
. If
\mathfrak{l}
is an ideal prime to
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}
, write
[\mathfrak{l}]
for the group ring element of
\mathcal{A}
and
\langle[\mathfrak{l}]\rangle
for the corresponding element in
\Lambda
.

Similarly, define
Z_{\mathbb{Q}}(1),Z_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)_{tor}
and

. Since

, one can see that
Z_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},Z_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)_{tor}=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}

. Denote by
\omega_{\mathbb{Q}}:Z_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)\rightarrow Z_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)_{tor}

\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}:Z_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)\rightarrow W_{\mathbb{Q}}
the natural projection. Recall the norm map,
Nm:Z_{F}(1)\rightarrow Z_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
and set
\omega:=\omega_{F}:=\omega_{\mathbb{Q}}\circ Nm
and
\langle\cdot\rangle:=\langle\cdot\rangle_{F}:=\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}\circ Nm
. By abuse of notation, we denote by the same symbols the composition of

and
\langle\cdot\rangle
with the projection

In

\displaystyle h_{k}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}^{\infty},\mathcal{O})\xrightarrow{\sim}h_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{p}^{\infty},\mathcal{O}).

So we can write
\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})
for

For every

, consider the morphisms

\displaystyle[\cdot]_{k,\infty}:Z_{F}(\mathfrak{n})\rightarrow\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}).

, under suitable hypotheses, one should be able to establish a rank
1

p
-converse result for base change of
E
to certain biquadratic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
.

11.
Example

We give an example of a triple
(E,F,p)
which satisfies all the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
.

Consider the quadratic real field
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
. Then the rational prime
p=11
is inert in
F
. Write
\mathfrak{p}=(11)O_{F}
. Now consider the elliptic curve
X_{0}(11)
which is given by the Weierstrass equation:

\displaystyle E:y^{2}+y=x^{3}-x^{2}-10x-20.

Note that
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}E(\mathbb{Q})=0
. Using
[
30
]
(https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/2.2.8.1/121.1/a/2), one can see that
E/F
has split multiplicative reduction at
\mathfrak{p}
. Furthermore,
[
30
]
indicates that the rank of the Mordell-Weil group
E(F)
is 1, and the Tate-Shafarevich group
\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\Pi\kern-3.60992pt\Pi$}(E/F)
is finite. Moreover, we have, again from
[
30
]
, that
\overline{\rho}_{\mathbf{f}}\cong\overline{\rho}_{E,p}
has maximal image and hence,
(irred)
is being satisfied. Finally,
E/F
is the base change of
X_{0}(11)
to F, so a minimal modular lifting of the residual representation of
E/\mathbb{Q}
gives us the required minimal modular lifting, so
(MML)
is being satisfied. Hence, all the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
are met.

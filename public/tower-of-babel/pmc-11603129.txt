Research on magnetic force of magnetic seal in aero-engine
Authors: Wanli Fan, Hua Su, Ke Li, Weiwei Hao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAiming at the magnetic seal in an aero-engine accessory, the range of magnetic force required for the normal operation of the magnetic seal is analyzed. The finite element analysis method based on the Maxwell stress tensor method is used to calculate the magnetic force between the dynamic ring and the magnetic static ring in the magnetic sealing system. The magnetic force under different magnetization modes and gaps is tested by the magnetic measuring test bench, which verifies the correctness of the magnetic calculation method. The effects of permanent magnet material, permanent magnet magnetization direction, magnetic seal structural parameters, and working conditions on the magnetic force between the dynamic ring/magnetic static ring in the magnetic seal are analyzed. Under the same structure, the magnetic force of NeFeB35, SmCo28, Alnico5, and Ferrite as magnetic static rings is compared. The results show that NdFeB35 has the largest magnetic force, SmCo28 is second, Alnico5 and Ferrite are relatively small, and their magnetic forces are about 1/4 to 1/5 of the first two materials. When the rotor or the casing is moving axially, NeFeB35 and SmCo28 materials with large magnetic force should be selected to meet the normal working requirements. Under the same material, the magnetic forces of axial, radial, and radiative magnetization modes are compared. the ratio of magnetic force under axial, radiative, and radial magnetization is about 2.3:1.1:1. If NeFeB35 and SmCo28 materials are axially magnetized, their magnetic forces will exceed the allowable range, which will increase the seal wear. The increase in the inner diameter of the dynamic ring and the gap between the dynamic ring and the magnetic static ring causes a decrease in the magnetic force. The increase in temperature affects the magnetic properties of permanent magnetic material, which leads to the decline of magnetic force between the dynamic ring and the magnetic static ring. The increase in rotor speed leads to the acceleration of the fluctuation frequency of the magnetic force. Still, the maximum and minimum values of the magnetic force under different speeds remain unchanged, and the maximum fluctuation rate is less than 3%. According to the analysis results, the fitting formula of magnetic force calculation is presented by the orthogonal test and the least squares method.Keywords: Magnetic seal, Magnetic force, Magnetic force measurement test, Magnetic force influencing factor analysis, Magnetic force calculation fitting formulaSubject terms: Engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

The magnetic seal is a sealing technology that uses magnetic force to replace the spring force in traditional mechanical seals. Through the magnetic force of the permanent magnet material, the sealing interface can obtain enough contact pressure to achieve the sealing effect1. The magnetic sealing device mainly consists of a magnetic static ring and dynamic ring components (embedded graphite ring). Its working principle is that the magnetic static ring relies on the magnetic material’s magnetic force to attract the dynamic ring so that the static ring end face and the graphite ring end face are closely fitted to prevent the leakage of the working medium. As early as 1956 Robert Stevenson proposed a magnetic seal structure2, and it was further developed in the 1980s. In the following 60 years, the magnetic sealing structure has been continuously improved, with the emergence of some product patents3–5. It is because of the magnetic seal has the advantages of fewer structural components, compact structure, zero leakage, no pollution, easy installation and excellent performance in high-speed high-vibration working conditions, that magnetic seals are widely used in bearing chamber seals, aerospace engine accessory drive shafts, as well as automotive, marine, and railroad industries6–8.

The magnetic force is directly related to the contact pressure of the sealing interface and affects the leakage and wear performance of the seal. The magnetic force in the magnetic seal not only depends on the permanent magnetic material, magnetizing direction, and other material magnetic performance parameters but is also closely related to the seal structure and operating conditions. In the actual application process, there is usually a small gap between the magnetic static ring and the dynamic ring. This gap will be subject to the environmental conditions of the seal and the wear state and change, which leads to changes in the magnetic force between the magnetic static ring and the dynamic ring, thus affecting the mechanical state of the magnetic sealing device and even the leakage of the seal and the wear performance. However, there are fewer studies on the quantitative effect of the gap change between the dynamic ring and the magnetic static ring on the magnetic force. Dezhong et al.9 utilized tie rods for distance adjustment between two ferromagnetic parts, measuring the adjustment distance through a grating scale and detecting the pulling force by a force sensor. Hong et al.10 used a screw to adjust the distance between two magnets and indirectly derive the size of the magnetic force at different distances through a pressure gauge. Both of the above magnetic force measurement devices cannot carry out the magnetic force at the micro gap and the distance adjustment accuracy is lower.

In the study of magnetic force calculation, Li11 used the equivalent magnetic charge method to calculate the magnetic force and magnetic moment between a cube and a circular column, verified the effect of different magnetization directions on the magnetic force and magnetic moment between permanent magnets, and derived a mathematical model for the interaction of two shapes of permanent magnets by theoretical calculations. Shu et al.12 calculated the magnetic force between two magnets by modeling the force between magnetic dipoles. Li et al.13 derived the force generated by a single magnetic pole based on the principle of static magnetism and derived a formula for calculating the magnetic force. Qingtao et al.14 used the molecular current method for magnetic force calculation, proposed the formula for magnetic force calculation in the axial direction, and built a typical magnetic force calculation model for permanent magnet rings. Wei et al.15 used an empirical formula to calculate the magnetic force of an aero-engine accessory output shaft magnetic seal. By summarizing the existing magnetic force calculation methods, it can be seen that some are only applicable to the model with simple structure, some use simplified empirical formulas, and some only analyze the magnetic force changes from the perspective of the magnetic performance parameters, without fully considering the combined influence of the structure, material, and working conditions, it is difficult to satisfy the analysis of the magnetic force of the magnetic seals with complex structure and working conditions.

Firstly, this paper introduces the structural composition and working principle of the studied magnetic sealing system and establishes the mechanical relationship between the magnetic force and the normal operation of the magnetic seal. The finite element analysis method based on the Maxwell stress tensor method is used to calculate the magnetic force between the dynamic ring/magnetic static ring in the magnetic sealing system. The magnetic force under different magnetization modes and gaps is tested by the magnetic measuring test bench, which verifies the correctness of the magnetic force calculation method. The effects of permanent magnet material, magnetizing direction, sealing working environment temperature, and rotor speed on the magnetic force between the dynamic ring/magnetic static ring are analyzed. Finally, the fitting formula for magnetic force calculation is established using the orthogonal test method and the least squares method, which provides a basis for the magnetic seal performance analysis and the optimization design.

Magnetic seal structure and the effect of magnetic force on sealing work

Structure and working principle of magnetic seals

The magnetic sealing system consists of a seal mounting base, magnetic static ring, dynamic ring, graphite ring, O-ring, etc. Its typical structure is shown in Fig. 1. The magnetic static ring is a permanent magnetic material, the dynamic ring is a soft magnetic material, and the graphite ring is embedded in the dynamic ring. The magnetic static ring relies on the magnetic force of the permanent magnetic material to attract the dynamic ring, so that the end face of the static ring and the graphite ring are closely adhered to form the main sealing interface to prevent the leakage of the working medium. The O-ring 1 between the dynamic ring and the rotor shaft ensures that the dynamic ring rotates synchronously with the shaft without relative sliding through the friction between the O-ring and the rotor and the dynamic ring, and through its deformation to adapt the rotor axial/angular motion. O-ring 2 ensures that the magnetic static ring remains stationary under the frictional torque of the graphite ring by its elastic deformation. In addition, the three O-rings also play a secondary sealing role. Magnetic force calculation method and test verification

Finite element methods for magnetic force calculations

At present, the calculation methods of magnetic force mainly include the equivalent magnetic charge method, molecular current method, virtual displacement method and Maxwell stress tensor method18. The equivalent magnetic charge method and molecular current method are based on the assumption of an ideal model, and they are mainly suitable for calculating the magnetic force of permanent magnet materials with simple shapes. However, the result contains fourfold integration, so it is often necessary to calculate with the help of Monte Carlo19 simulation or numerical integration20. When the model structure is more complex, the amount of computation and error should not be underestimated. The virtual displacement method is mainly applied to the magnetic field generated by the static electric field. Considering the calculation efficiency and accuracy, this paper adopts the finite element analysis method based on the Maxwell stress tensor method to calculate the magnetic force. The basic flow of finite element simulation is shown in Fig. 2.

Since the sealing mount and O-ring have little influence on the magnetic field, the magnetic field finite element analysis model does not include the seal mounting seat and O-ring to reduce the size of the calculation model. Magnetic force testing

In order to verify the correctness of the magnetic force calculated by the finite element method, a magnetic force measuring test device with an adjustable micro-gap was designed and developed, as shown in Fig. 8, which can realize 0.01 mm micro gap adjustment with higher adjustment accuracy compared with other magnetic force measurement devices, and can test the magnetic force between the dynamic ring and the static ring under different micro gaps. The test device includes a clamping module, a positioning module, a displacement adjustment module, a measurement module, and a base plate. The clamping module consists of an adapter plate and two table vises for fixing the upper and lower test pieces respectively. Table vise 1 is used to grip the upper test piece and is fixed on the adapter plate. The adapter plate is connected to the test head of the dynamometer by thread. Table vise 2 is used for clamping the lower test piece and is fixed to the dual-axis fine-tuning displacement table. The displacement adjustment module includes a test frame and a dual-axis fine-tuning displacement stage. The test frame is fixed to the base plate to adjust the vertical distance between the test pieces and the minimum adjustable distance is 1 mm. The dual-axis fine-tuning displacement stage is fixed to the base plate, through micrometer 1 and micrometer 2 to adjust the vertical and horizontal distance between the test pieces, the minimum adjustable distance is 0.01 mm. A dynamometer is used to measure the magnetic force between the specimens. The maximum load of the dynamometer is 100 N, the index value is 0.01 N, the measurement accuracy is up to 0.5%, and it has the function of maintaining the peak value. The base plate, clamping module, positioning module, and displacement adjustment module are all made of non-magnetic conduction materials or materials with very low magnetic permeability to avoid the influence of materials on magnetic force measurement during the test.

Analysis of magnetic force influencing factors

The influence of different permanent magnet materials on magnetic force

Common permanent magnet materials include neodymium-iron-boron (NdFeB) alloy, samarium-cobalt (SmCo) alloy, aluminum-nickel-cobalt (Alnico) alloy, and ferrite21. A comparison of the performances of the four types of permanent magnet materials is shown in Table 3. Permanent magnet material as a magnetic seal magnetic static ring material, in order to ensure the reliability of the seal, needs to have a high residual magnetic induction strength , large coercivity with high maximum magnetic energy product , in addition to the consideration of these magnetic performance parameters, should also have good mechanical properties.

In order to investigate the influence of different permanent magnet materials on the magnetic force, the magnetic force between the dynamic ring/magnetic static ring with four kinds of permanent magnet materials (the magnetization mode is radial magnetization) is calculated by finite element method, and the results are shown in Fig. 10. As can be seen, the magnetic force difference of different magnetic static ring materials is obvious, among which, the magnetic force is the largest when NdFeB is used as a magnetic static ring material, and the magnetic force of ferrite is the smallest. Referring to the range of magnetic force determined under the typical working conditions of the seal in Sect. 1.3 (red dotted line in Fig. 10), only NdFeB35 and SmCo28 materials meet the usage requirements. Combined with Table 3, we can find that although NdFeB35 material has a larger magnetic force, it is not suitable for high-temperature and high-speed engine operation because of its general oxidation-resistant ability and large influence by temperature. SmCo28 has strong magnetism and high coercivity, outstanding oxidation, and corrosion resistance, which is suitable for the complex and harsh working environments. However, due to the samarium cobalt alloy containing rare earth elements, its price is relatively more expensive, generally for the same specifications of Alnico permanent magnet 4–5 times.

Effect of permanent magnet magnetizing direction on magnetic force

The permanent magnet must be processed into a suitable size by the hard magnetic material first. Considering the structure of the magnetic sealing system, the permanent magnet should be processed into a circular column and then magnetized accordingly using an external magnetic field. Different magnetization methods will obtain different magnetic pole directions, mainly including three magnetization methods: axial magnetization, radial magnetization, and radiative magnetization22. Axial magnetization refers to applying a magnetic field to magnetize along the axial direction of the permanent magnet, and the magnetization direction is perpendicular to the radial plane of the permanent magnet, i.e., the two end surfaces of the ring serve as the N-pole and S-pole respectively after magnetization, as shown in Fig. 11a. Radial magnetization refers to applying a magnetic field perpendicular to the radial direction of the permanent magnet to magnetize, and the magnetization direction is parallel to the radial plane of the permanent magnet, i.e., the two semicircular circumferences of the ring serve as the N-pole and S-pole, respectively, after magnetization, as shown in Fig. 11b. Radiative magnetization refers to the process of applying a magnetic field radiating outward from the center of the permanent magnet or pointing outward from the outside to the center of the permanent magnet, which is perpendicular to the radial direction of the permanent magnet, and the N and S poles after magnetization are the inner and outer cylindrical planes of the torus, respectively, as shown in Fig. 11c. The magnitude of the magnetic force between the dynamic/magnetic static ring for the three materials Alnico5, NdFeB35, and SmCo28 in different magnetization modes is given in Fig. 12. Figure 13 shows the vector diagram of the magnetic susceptibility direction of NdFeB35 under different magnetization modes.

Vector diagram of NdFeB35 magnetizing direction.

It can be seen that, under the same material, when the permanent magnet is axially magnetized the magnetic force between the dynamic ring and the magnetic static ring is the largest, followed by radial magnetization, and radial magnetization is the smallest. For example, in the case of the magnetic static ring made of NdFeB35, the magnetic force in the case of axial magnetization is about twice as much as that of radiation magnetization, and the magnetic force in the case of radiation magnetization is about 1.1 times as much as that of the radial magnetization. The reason for this phenomenon is that in axial magnetization, the magnetic flux line of the permanent magnet is closed along its axis, which can produce the maximum magnetic flux on the two pole surfaces of the permanent magnet. Because the magnetic force is proportional to the magnetic flux, axially magnetized permanent magnets can produce the strongest magnetic force. In radiative magnetization, the magnetic field is radially spread outward, forming a relatively uniform distribution of the magnetic field. Although the magnetic circuit design of radiative magnetization is more flexible, the magnetic force is weakened relative to axial magnetization because the magnetic flux line is not directly closed along the axial direction, but is distributed along the radial direction. In radial magnetization, the magnetic domains are arranged perpendicular to the direction of the axis of permanent magnets, and this arrangement is not conducive to the formation of a strong magnetic domain structure, which results in the smallest magnetic force. Referring to the range of magnetic force determined under the typical working conditions of the seal in Sect. 1.3 (red dotted line in Fig. 12), the radial magnetization of NdFeB material, and the radial and radiative magnetization of SmCo material can meet the usage requirements. From Fig. 12, it can be seen that the magnetizing method has a great influence on the magnetic force. Therefore, when designing the magnetic sealing system, the magnetizing method of the permanent magnet should be reasonably selected for different engineering requirements to ensure that the magnetic sealing system can work stably.

Influence of the structural parameters of the magnetic sealing system on the magnetic force

In the structure of the magnetic sealing system studied in this paper, the dimensions of the outer diameter of the dynamic ring, the outer diameter of the magnetic static ring, and the inner diameter of the magnetic static ring are taken as constant values here because they are related to the overall system dimensions. The following mainly analyzes the influence of the dynamic ring inner diameter and the gap between the dynamic ring and the magnetic static ring on the magnetic force.

According to the basic parameters of the structure shown in Table 1, the magnetic force of three different materials, Alnico5, NdFeB35 and SmCo28, is calculated by the finite element method under different inner diameters of the dynamic ring. All three materials are axially magnetized, and the results are shown in Fig. 14. It can be seen that with the increase of the inner diameter of the dynamic ring, the magnetic force acting on the dynamic ring of the magnetic static ring of the three materials decreases. This is because with the increase of the inner diameter of the dynamic ring, the action area between the magnetic static ring and the dynamic ring decreases, and the magnetic force is proportional to the area of action, so the magnetic force decreases with the increase of the outer diameter of the graphite ring. The magnetic force of NdFeB35 and SmCo28 is much larger than that of Alnico5 with the same inner diameter of the dynamic ring, but the magnetic force of NdFeB35 and SmCo28 decreases at a larger rate with the increase of the inner diameter.

Influence of operating parameters on the magnetic force

For the magnetic seals studied in this paper, the effect of the variation of operating parameters such as temperature and rotor speed on the magnetic force is mainly discussed.

Influence of temperature on magnetism

The influence of temperature on the magnetic force is mainly reflected in the effect of temperature on the magnetic parameters of the seal. The effect of temperature on permanent magnets is mainly due to thermal motion, which causes the direction of the magnetic moments of individual atoms to change constantly and irregularly. At lower temperatures, the effect of electron exchange between two neighboring atoms in a permanent magnet is more significant than that of thermal motion. However, when the temperature rises to a certain degree, the magnetic moment in the permanent magnet will change the spatial orientation under the action of thermal motion, making the domains and domain walls that are already in a regular arrangement produce motion, which includes the rotation of the magnetic moment of the domains and the movement of the domain walls, resulting in some of the magnetic domains being arranged in disorder and irreversible demagnetization. With the disturbance of some magnetic domain arrangements, the magnetic induction of the permanent magnet material decreases along the demagnetization curve, and the magnetic induction will not recover even after the original temperature is restored, resulting in a loss of some of the magnetic energy23. Influence of rotational speed on magnetic force

In the magnetic sealing system studied in this paper, the magnetic static ring made of permanent magnets is stationary, and the dynamic ring and the rotating shaft rotate at high speed during the working process. In order to investigate the effect of rotational speed on the magnetic force, the change of magnetic force under different rotational speeds is analyzed using the finite element method. The speed is different, that is, each rotation of a circle takes a different time, but the magnetic force changes in each circle are the same. Influence of rotational speed on magnetic force.

As can be seen in Fig. 18, with the rotation of the rotating shaft/dynamic ring, the magnetic force shows a slight periodic fluctuation, with a fluctuation rate of 2.84% for the Alnico5 material, 1.17% for the SmCo28 material, and 1.19% for the NdFeB35 material. As the permanent magnet material is a static ring, the dynamic ring will produce an induced magnetic field excited by the permanent magnet. When the dynamic ring and the rotating shaft rotate, the magnetic field around it will be distorted and changed to a certain extent, and this change in the magnetic field leads to periodic fluctuations in the magnetic force between the magnetic static ring and the dynamic ring, and an increase or decrease in the rotational speed will only affect the period of the fluctuation of the magnetic force and does not affect the magnitude of it. As the size of the magnetic force is mainly affected by the permanent magnetic material, the rotation of the dynamic ring/rotary shaft has little effect on the magnetic force. For the magnetic seal studied in this paper, the effect of the rotation of the dynamic ring/rotary shaft can be disregarded in the calculation of the magnetic force.

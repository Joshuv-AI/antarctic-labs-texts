Global burden of colorectal cancer in 2022 and projections to 2050: incidence and mortality estimates from GLOBOCAN
Authors: Sumin Wu, Yunping Zhang, Zi Lin, Min Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundColorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the common cancers worldwide and the second leading cause of cancer — related deaths. This study aimed to analyze the incidence and mortality of CRC in different global regions through the Global Cancer Observatory (GLOBOCAN) database, so as to provide data support for CRC prevention and treatment.MethodsIn this study, data on CRC incidence cases and deaths were extracted from the GLOBOCAN 2022 database for the world, 20 regions, and China, and age—standardized incidence rates (ASIR) and age—standardized mortality rates (ASMR) were analyzed by sex, world region, age, and human development index (HDI). Finally, the incident cases and deaths of CRC from 2022 to 2050 were predicted.ResultsData showed that in 2022, there were 1,926,425 global CRC incident cases and 904,019 deaths, with Eastern Asia having the highest numbers. China reported 517,106 incident cases and 240,010 deaths. ASIR and ASMR were higher among males than females across all regions, including China. Globally and in China, the incident cases and deaths of CRC significantly increased after the age of 40. Additionally, regions with a high HDI had higher numbers of incident and death cases of CRC, while those with a low HDI had relatively lower numbers. If CRC incidence rates remained unchanged from 2022, global new CRC cases were projected to reach 2.36 million by 2050, marking a 22.51% increase from 2022; under this same scenario of constant incidence rate, China’s new cases were estimated to decline to 464,000, representing a 10.27% decrease from 2022.ConclusionThe incidence and mortality of CRC were positively correlated with the HDI level, and gender and age were key factors affecting CRC incidence and mortality. Preventive measures needed to be taken to address the rising trends of global incidence and mortality.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15138-0.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a major malignancy of the digestive system with varying aggressiveness depending on stage and molecular subtype [1]. In 2020, colon and rectal cancers ranked fifth and eighth, respectively, in global cancer incidence, and together constituted the second leading cause of cancer—related mortality worldwide [2]. Despite the establishment of a comprehensive multimodal treatment system—including endoscopic screening, curative surgery, neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, and molecular targeted therapy—CRC remains a major global health challenge. This is largely attributed to population aging and the adoption of increasingly westernized lifestyles [3, 4].

Epidemiological studies have demonstrated that both non-modifiable and modifiable risk factors drive CRC. Non-modifiable factors include hereditary syndromes (e.g., Lynch syndrome, familial adenomatous polyposis) and chronic inflammatory conditions such as ulcerative colitis, which confer a 10- to 20-fold increased CRC risk when disease duration exceeds 10 years [5, 6]. In parallel, high red meat consumption (> 500 g/week), prolonged sedentary behavior (Metabolic Equivalent of Task, MET < 3), and tobacco exposure are well-established lifestyle-related contributors to CRC development, offering key points for preventive intervention [7, 8]. Furthermore, global data indicate a notable rise in CRC incidence among individuals under 50 years of age, particularly in both developed and developing countries, highlighting a shifting age-related disease burden [9].

To track and quantify the global burden of cancer, the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) maintains the GLOBOCAN database through the Global Cancer Observatory (GCO). This platform integrates population-based cancer registries, modeled cause-of-death estimates, and Bayesian algorithms to provide standardized age- and sex-specific cancer incidence and mortality estimates for 36 cancer types across 185 countries [2]. Although the recently released GLOBOCAN 2022 data provide updated global cancer burden estimates [10], comprehensive analyses specifically focused on CRC remain scarce. Previous CRC studies based on GLOBOCAN 2020 data [11] do not reflect critical post-pandemic developments—such as expanded screening coverage in many regions [12] or notable dietary transitions [13]. This data lag has led to three significant misalignments in current CRC prevention strategies: a mismatch between the pace of population aging and current screening age thresholds, a gap between regional development levels and the allocation of preventive resources, and a delay in updating risk prediction models in response to changing exposure patterns [14].

To address these limitations in current data and prevention strategies, this study utilizes the most recent GLOBOCAN 2022 dataset to conduct a comprehensive and up-to-date assessment of the global and China’s CRC burden. A four-dimensional analytical framework was established, incorporating World Health Organization (WHO) regional divisions, Human Development Index (HDI) categories, age groups, and sex, to systematically evaluate geographic, demographic, and socioeconomic disparities in CRC incidence and mortality. Furthermore, we performed projections of future CRC burden through 2050 using scenario-based extrapolation models. For China, we additionally employed a time series forecasting approach based on an autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model to provide a more detailed national-level projection.

This study aims to offer robust, data-driven evidence to guide the refinement of CRC prevention and control strategies worldwide. By identifying high-risk population groups and regions with disproportionate disease burdens, our findings aim to inform the optimization of screening guidelines, the equitable allocation of healthcare resources, and the development of long-term, adaptive public healthcare policies. Ultimately, this work contributes to the global effort to reduce preventable cancer deaths through improved screening, early diagnosis, and equitable care delivery, in line with WHO’s call to avoid 7 million unnecessary cancer deaths by 2030 [15].

Materials and methods

Data sources

Data on the incidence and mortality of CRC were obtained from the GLOBOCAN 2022 database (https://gco.iarc.fr/). By the 10th Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10), CRC, covering the colon (coded as C18), rectum (coded as C19-C20), and anus (coded as C21), was classified under the code range C18-C21. Subsequently, the raw CRC data were downloaded to facilitate further analysis.

Statistical analysis

To characterize the disease burden of CRC, the numbers of incident cases and deaths, along with incidence and mortality rates, were utilized. To correct for age structure differences across populations and enable direct standardized comparison of cancer incidence and mortality risks, age-standardized rates (ASRs) were introduced. ASRs encompassed both incidence and mortality rates, defined as new cancer cases and deaths per 100,000 person—years based on the 1966 Segi-Doll world standard population, respectively. The ASRs in this study were calculated based on the 1966 Segi-Doll world standard population—a classic standard initially developed by Segi and later revised by Doll, which stratified age groups into 0–4, 5–9, …, 80–84, and ≥ 85 years (16 age groups total). It is important to clarify that the 1966 Segi-Doll standard is not equivalent to the WHO World Standard (2000–2025): the latter was released by the WHO in 2000, with an age structure updated to reflect global population dynamics in the 21 st century. The 1966 Segi-Doll standard was selected in this study primarily because it is the default standard for cancer burden analyses in the GLOBOCAN database (consistent with IARC’s methodological framework), ensuring comparability with most prior epidemiological studies in CRC burden that adopted this standard.

The results were presented according to the global, regional, Chinese, and different Human Development Index (HDI) levels. Based on United Nations definitions, the world was divided into 20 regions, including Northern America, Eastern Asia, Eastern Africa, Middle Africa, Northern Africa, Southern Africa, Western Africa, Caribbean, Central America, South-Eastern Asia, South Central Asia, Western Asia, Eastern Europe, Northern Europe, Southern Europe, Western Europe, Australia-New Zealand, Melanesia, South America, and Micronesia/Polynesia. Meanwhile, countries and regions were classified into four economic development categories—low, medium, high, and very high—according to the HDI published by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). Specifically, the HDI data and classification criteria used in this study were derived from the UNDP’s Human Development Report 2022, which is the official and most up-to-date sources for global HDI metrics. The thresholds for HDI category construction strictly followed UNDP 2022 official standards: low HDI (< 0.550), medium HDI (0.550–0.699), high HDI (0.700–0.799.700.799), and very high HDI (≥ 0.800). The countries/regions included in each HDI category were detailed in Supplementary Table 1. Gender was categorized into male and female. Ages were grouped into 18 age brackets (0–4 years, 5–9 years, …, 80–84 years, 85 years and above) in four-year intervals. Finally, tabular statistics and the R package “ggplot2” (v 3.5.1) were used to visualize the number of CRC incident cases, deaths, ASIR, and ASMR.

All population data required for CRC incidence and mortality projections (2022–2050) were extraced from the United Nations World Population Prospects 2024 Revision (UN WPP 2024). Taking 2022 as the base year, the annual fixed rate of change (g) for incidence/mortality rates was set, with an annual relative change ranging from —3% to + 3%. The incidence/mortality rates of CRC in each subsequent year were calculated by geometric progression (i.e., maintaining the same proportional change each year), and the formula used was: rt = rt−1 × (1 + g). Herein, rt represented the incidence/mortality rate in year t (2030–2050), rt−1 denoted the rate in the previous year (year t-1), and g stood for the fixed annual rate of change, which remained constant throughout the projection period. Finally, the number of new CRC cases and deaths was predicted globally and in China in 2050 based on the formula: caset = Pt×rt, where Pt referred to the population in year t. To further predict the changes in China CRC incidence rates from 2022 to 2050, the study constructed an ARIMA model using the R packages “forecast” (v 8.23.0) and “tseries” (v 0.10.58). Results

ASIR and ASMR burden of CRC by world region and gender

According to GLOBOCAN’s 2022 statistics on CRC, significant differences in the incidence and mortality of CRC were observed across different regions. Globally, there were 1,926,425 incidence cases and 904,019 deaths in 2022. Among these, the ASIR for males and females globally were 21.94 and 15.16, respectively. Meanwhile, the ASMR for males and females were 9.86 and 6.5, respectively (Supplementary Table 2). It was noteworthy that males had higher numbers of incidence cases, deaths, as well as ASIR and ASMR than females. In terms of regional distribution, Eastern Asia bore the heaviest CRC burden, with the number of incidence cases and deaths reaching 699,866 and 316,406, respectively, both the highest globally. By gender, the ASIR and ASMR of males were generally higher than those of females in all regions. Specifically, among males, Southern Europe had the highest ASIR (39.62), and Eastern Europe had the highest ASMR (19.58). Among females, Australia—New Zealand had the highest ASIR (31.82), and Eastern Europe had the highest ASMR (10.58). Regarding the mortality-to-incidence ratio (M: I ratio), Western Africa had the highest ratio of 0.73, indicating a CRC case—fatality rate of 73% in this region, approximately twice the global level (Supplementary Table 2) (Fig. 1a-d). This indicated that there were significant challenges in CRC management in this region: limited access to population—based screening likely leads to late—stage diagnosis of most cases, while insufficient healthcare resources and poor access to standardized treatment (e.g., curative surgery or adjuvant therapy) further contribute to the high fatality rate. In China, there were 517,106 CRC incidence cases and 240,010 deaths in 2022. Among these, the ASIR and ASMR for males were 24.7 and 10.9, respectively. For females, the corresponding values were 15.7 and 6.48, respectively. Consistent with the global gender trend, males had higher numbers of incidence cases, deaths, as well as ASIR and ASMR than females in CRC (Supplementary Table 2).

ASIR and ASMR burden of CRC by age and gender

To explore the impacts of age and gender on the ASIR and ASMR of CRC globally and in China, relevant data were analyzed. The results showed that globally, the ASIR and ASMR of CRC demonstrated a continuous upward trend with increasing age. The number of incident cases peaked in the 65–69 age group, while the number of death cases peaked in the age group over 85 (Fig. 2a-b). In China, the ASIR of CRC increased with age between 0 and 80 years old and slightly decreased after 80 years old, while the ASMR continued to rise with age. The number of incident cases peaked in the 65–69 age group, and the number of death cases peaked in the 70–74 age group (Fig. 2c-d). Notably, both global and Chinese data indicated a notable acceleration in the increase of ASIR and ASMR after age 40. In terms of gender differences, within the 0–84 age group, both ASIR and ASMR in males were significantly higher than those in females. This changing pattern of age and gender was consistent in both global and Chinese data. Although the upward trends of ASIR and ASMR were relatively gentle before the age of 40, an increasing trend with age could still be observed, and males generally had higher values than females (Fig. 2e-h). This indicated that age and gender were important factors affecting the ASIR and ASMR of CRC globally and in China. Populations aged 40 and above, especially males, required key attention.

When exploring the impact of different HDI levels on the disease burden of CRC, results indicated HDI levels showed a significant positive correlation with the number of CRC incidence cases, deaths, ASIR, and ASMR. That was, the higher the HDI level, the heavier the disease burden of CRC. Among them, the number of incident cases and deaths in Very High HDI regions reached as high as 984,756 and 427,277, respectively. The ASIR for males and females was 34.21 and 23.92, respectively. Gender-specific analysis revealed that males consistently demonstrated higher CRC incidence cases, deaths, ASIR, and ASMR compared to females across all HDI levels. Further correlation analysis indicated a positive correlation between ASMR and ASIR across continents and HDI levels, while this positive correlation coefficient was significantly higher for males than for females at the same HDI level (Fig. 3c-d). Predicting the new cases and deaths of CRC in 2022–2050

Based on the baseline data in 2022, simulation predictions of annual incidence and mortality trends indicated that if the global CRC incidence rate remained unchanged starting from 2022, the number of new CRC cases was expected to reach 2.36 million in 2050, representing an increase of approximately 22.51% compared to 2022. If risk factors caused the incidence rate to decrease by 1% each year, the global number of incident cases was projected to decline to 1.78 million by 2050. If the current mortality rate remained stable, the global number of CRC deaths was estimated to reach 1.11 million by 2050, an increase of about 22.79% compared to 2022. If the mortality rate decreased by 1% annually, the number of deaths was projected to fall to 834,000 by 2050 (Fig. 4a-b). Similarly, if the Chinese CRC incidence rate remained unchanged starting from 2022, the number of new CRC cases was expected to reach 464,000 in 2050, representing a decrease of approximately 10.27% compared to 2022. If risk factors caused the incidence rate to decrease by 1% annually, the Chinese CRC incident cases were projected to decline to 350,000 by 2050. If the current mortality rate remained stable, the number of CRC deaths in China was estimated to reach 215,000 by 2050, a decrease of about 10.42% compared to 2022. If the mortality rate decreased by 1% each year, the number of deaths was projected to fall to 162,000 by 2050 (Fig. 4c-d). To ensure that both the number of incident cases and deaths are lower than the current levels in the future, both global and Chinese regions need to reduce the incidence rate by 1% per year.

To further predict the changes in China CRC incidence from 2022 to 2050, an ARIMA model was constructed. The results showed that the CRC burden for men would be higher than that of women for a long time in the coming period in China, with the CRC burden for women remaining consistently stable and the CRC burden for men increasing slightly (Fig. 4e). In conclusion, both prediction methods highlighted the importance of closely monitoring CRC incidence and mortality trends.

Discussion

CRC remains a major global health challenge due to its high incidence and mortality, particularly in regions undergoing rapid socioeconomic transitions [16]. This study, based on GLOBOCAN 2022 data, comprehensively analyzed the global, regional, and national burden of CRC across sex, age groups, and HDI levels. Moreover, we projected CRC incidence and mortality through 2050 using both direct extrapolation and an ARIMA model for China. Our findings provide essential insights for the stratified management of CRC and inform tailored prevention and control strategies.

Geographic distribution and gender disparities

Our results revealed substantial geographic variation in CRC burden, with Eastern Asia recording the highest number of new cases and deaths in 2022. This trend is likely influenced by multiple converging factors. Rapid economic growth in East Asia has led to dietary westernization, characterized by increased consumption of red meat, processed foods, and sugar-rich diets [17]. Numerous studies have confirmed a positive association between red and processed meat intake and CRC risk, with relative risks ranging from 1.15 to 1.21 depending on exposure levels [18, 19]. Simultaneously, physical inactivity and rising obesity-prevalent in many East Asian populations—further contribute to CRC risk. Obesity promotes inflammation, insulin resistance, and hormonal imbalances, all of which are known to play a role in CRC pathogenesis [20, 21].

Beyond lifestyle factors, disparities in healthcare access further exacerbate the CRC burden in East Asia. Despite advancements in healthcare infrastructure, disparities in screening coverage and healthcare access persist across the region. In many rural or under-resourced regions, low awareness of CRC symptoms and limited availability of diagnostic tools result in delayed diagnosis and poor outcomes. Additionally, the rapid aging of East Asian populations is a key contributor to the increasing CRC burden, as the incidence of CRC rises significantly with age [22, 23].

In contrast, Southern and Eastern Europe also show high ASIRs, despite long-standing screening programs. In Southern Europe, high ASIRs may reflect both historically accumulated risk factors—such as high per capita red meat consumption and elevated smoking rates among males—and improved detection capacity due to organized screening, which increases the identification of asymptomatic adenomas and early-stage CRC [24–26]. This stands in sharp contrast to regions like East Asia, where under-screening may lead to concealed incidence. Eastern Europe, however, is confronted with a more critical “high-incidence-high-mortality” patadox, likely resulting from fragmented screening-to-treatment pathways. Limited equipment, suboptimal access to standardized adjuvant or targeted therapies, and high comorbidity rates may contribute to poor survival despite screening efforts [11, 27].

Gender disparities in CRC burden were evident across all regions, with males consistently exhibiting higher ASIR and ASMR than females. This male predominance may stem from a combination of behavioral and biological factors. Men are more likely to engage in high-risk behaviors, including smoking, excessive alcohol consumption, and poor dietary habits, and they have higher rates of visceral adiposity—all associated with increased CRC risk [28]. Furthermore, men are less likely than women to participate in CRC screening programs; meta-analyses show that fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) uptake among males is approximately 16% lower than females. Qualitative studies attribute this gap to higher fatalism and lower awareness among men, which delays early detection and treatment [29].

Notably, Western Africa had the highest M: I ratio of 0.73, a stark indicator of constrained access to timely screening and treatment [24]. These findings align with recent literature documenting a rising global CRC burden and growing disparities in outcomes [11].

From a global prevention perspective, these regional variations highlight distinct strategic priorities: East Asia should focus on expanding screening coverage while implementing lifestyle interventions; Southern Europe may serve as a reference for integrating screening with effective clinical management; and Eastern Europe underscore the urgent need to strengthen therapeutic resources and ensure continuity of care. These insights are essential for developing region-specific strategies aimed at reducing both the incidence and mortality of CRC worldwide.

Age-Related patterns and biological mechanisms

Our age-stratified analysis showed that CRC incidence and mortality increase steadily with age, accelerating markedly after 40 years. This pattern is biologically plausible and consistent with the accumulation of somatic mutations, epigenetic alterations, chronic inflammation, and immunosenescence associated with aging [30–32]. Age-related declines in DNA repair mechanisms and immune surveillance may further contribute to carcinogenesis in the gastrointestinal tract [33].

Notably, this age-dependent pattern intersected with persistent sex disparities. While the gap was modest before age 40, it diverged sharply thereafter—a phenomenon potentially driven by hormonal differences (e.g., estrogen’s protective effects), male-predominant risk behaviors, and gender-specific screening disparities [34, 35]. Together, these findings underscore the need for earlier, sex-tailored screening strategies, especially for men.

Socioeconomic development and disease burden

A strong association was observed between HDI levels and CRC burden. Very high HDI countries accounted for the majority of global CRC cases and deaths in 2022, with the highest ASIR and ASMR values.

This association reflects the combined influence of the three core dimensions of HDI—life expectancy, education level, and gross national income (GNI) per capita [36]—each contributing through distinct mechanisms to shape disparities in CRC burden. Life expectancy, a central health indicator in HDI, is closely linked to population aging, which serves as a fundamental driver of increased CRC incidence and mortality in high-HDI regions. As age advances, cellular DNA repair capacity declines, T-cell activity decreases, and chronic inflammation accumulates, leading to an exponential rise in the risk of colorectal carcinogenesis [37]. This study also confirms that both global and Chinese CRC incidence and mortality accelerate markedly after 40, underscoring how extended life expectancy—without adequate early intervention—may translate into accumulated CRC risk, potentially becoming a primary contributor to the elevated disease burden in high-HDI areas.

Furthermore, in high-HDI settings, higher education levels are associated with greater awareness of CRC risk factors—such as high red meat consumption and sedentary behavior—as well as better adherence to screening recommendations [38], which may help sustain the positive correlation between HDI and detected incidence. Although better-educated individuals often exhibit higher compliance with treatment—which can reduce individual mortality risk—this effect may be offset by the larger pool of incidence cases, resulting in a pattern of high incidence coupled with substantial absolute mortality.

High GNI per capita is frequently accompanied by “Westernization” of dietary patterns and lifestyle, characterized by increased consumption of processed food, reduced physical activity, and higher obesity prevalence. The cumulative exposure to these modifiable risk factors is likely to directly elevate CRC incidence. Consequently, even with advanced medical interventions that reduce case fatality, the high baseline incidence may sustain elevated mortality at the population level [39].

Together, these pathways illustrate how socioeconomic development—as captured by HDI—shapes CRC burden through multidimensional and interacting mechanisms.

Temporal trends and projections

Projections to 2050 suggest a notable divergence between global and Chinese trajectories of CRC burden. If current incidence rates remain unchanged, global new CRC cases are projected to increase by more than 22% by 2050, a trend that may be largely attributable to population aging and cumulative exposure to risk factors [14]. In contrast, China is expected to experience a gradual decline in both CRC incidence and mortality over the same period.

This divergence could be partly explained by differences in healthcare resource allocation, diagnostic capacity, and the implementation of CRC-related public health policies. Many low- and middle-HDI countries appear to face challenges such as insufficient endoscopic equipment, limited specialist training, and delayed diagnostic capacity—factors that might contribute to more advanced-stage detection and higher recorded incidence [14]. In China, policies aimed at optimizing medical resource distribution and enhancing primary care may have improved early diagnostic capacity and treatment accessibility. The establishment of multidisciplinary team (MDT) centers in major hospitals could also have helped optimize clinical management and reduce incident cases.

Substantial variations in the sustainability and coverage of CRC screening programs may further explain these differential trends. In some regions, screening initiatives seem to be hindered by unstable funding and limited coordination among health sectors. By comparison, China’s CRC screening program—supported by a central-local co-financing mechanism and integrated into local government health objectives—may have achieved more consistent implementation. Tailored strategies such as colonscopy in urban settings and fecal immunochemical testing (FIT) in rural areas, together with community health education efforts, could have contributed to a reduction in incidence [40]. These measures may have partially offset the anticipated rise in CRC cases due to population aging.

Furthermore, ARIMA modeling indicates that the CRC burden among Chinese males is likely to remain higher than among females through 2050, underscoring the potential need for gender-specific intervention strategies. These trends highlight the importance of sustained monitoring and adaptive national CRC control policies.

Public health implications and policy recommendations

The findings of this study carry significant public health implications, highlighting the need for multifaceted, evidence-based strategies to mitigate the global burden of CRC. Given the consistent gender disparities observed across regions and HDI levels, gender—sensitive screening strategies are essential. Men, who face higher CRC incidence and mortality, often participate less in screening programs [29], suggesting the need to lower screening age thresholds for high-risk male populations and to tailor health communication to improve their engagement. In addition, low- and middle-HDI countries with limited healthcare resources should prioritize the adoption of cost—effective population—based screening tools such as FIT, and simultaneously invest in enhancing basic diagnostic and treatment capacities [41, 42]. These efforts could help reduce the high mortality-to-incidence ratios observed in resource-constrained settings. National cancer control policies should be informed by regional disease burden patterns and future projections, enabling optimized resource allocation and targeted intervention planning. The progress made in China—where the CRC burden is projected to decline—underscores the importance of sustained screening initiatives, public education, and policy-level engagement. Lastly, global CRC control demands international cooperation to address disparities in healthcare access and outcomes. Cross-national partnerships in epidemiological research, health system strengthening, and technical assistance are vital to narrowing existing gaps [43]. Such collaborative efforts will be key to achieving the WHO Global Cancer Initiative target of reducing unnecessary cancer deaths.

Limitations and future directions

This study provides a comprehensive assessment of the global CRC burden by analyzing GLOBOCAN 2022 estimates across HDI regions, gender, and age groups, and pioneers the use of ARIMA modeling to project China’s CRC trends until 2050. These findings offer critical evidence for resource prioritization and targeted prevention strategies. However, several limitations must be acknowledged.

First, while GLOBOCAN 2022 represents the most recent data, its lack of long-term historical trends limits our ability to contextualize temporal patterns. Second, variations in cancer registry completeness and coding practices across countries may introduce regional biases. Importantly, the accuracy of rectal cancer diagnosis is highly dependent on local medical capabilities, including access to invasive examinations and reliable pathological confirmation. In regions with limited healthcare resources, underdiagnosis and misclassification are common, leading to underestimated incidence and potentially inflated case-fatality rates in parts of Africa, Southeast Asia, and South America. Consequently, direct comparisons with data from high—resource settings should be interpreted with caution. Even within China, significant regional disparities in diagnostic capacity may affect data comparability.

Third, the constant-rate model used in this study projected a decline in CRC cases in China—a result seemingly at odds with the well-established role of population aging in increasing cancer incidence. This counterintuitive outcome may stem from the model’s simplified assumptions, which do not fully account for real-world dynamics such as the effects of early interventions and improvements in dietary patterns, both of which could reduce CRC risk independently of demographic changes. Therefore, future studies should adopt more sophisticated modeling approaches that simultaneously incorporate demographic, behavioral, and clinical variables to improve the accuracy of CRC trend projections.

Finally, underrepresented populations, particularly in low-resource settings, may not be fully captured in the data.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive analysis of the global and regional burden of colorectal cancer (CRC) based on the latest GLOBOCAN 2022 data, with projections through 2050. Our findings reveal significant geographic, gender, age, and socioeconomic disparities in CRC incidence and mortality. Regions with higher Human Development Index (HDI) levels exhibit higher age-standardized rates of both incidence and mortality, while males consistently show a greater disease burden than females. The risk of CRC increases sharply after the age of 40, emphasizing the need for early and targeted screening. Although global CRC cases are projected to rise due to aging populations and lifestyle changes, China’s burden is expected to decline slightly, likely reflecting national prevention efforts. However, due to its large population, China will continue to face substantial absolute numbers of cases and deaths. These findings underscore the importance of tailored public health strategies, gender-sensitive screening, and increased investment in early detection, particularly in low- and middle-income regions. Coordinated international efforts are also needed to bridge healthcare gaps and reduce preventable CRC deaths. Overall, timely interventions and sustained policy support are crucial to mitigating the rising global burden of colorectal cancer in the coming decades.

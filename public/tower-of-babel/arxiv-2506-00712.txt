Abstract
. We characterize the s-parabolic Lipschitz caloric capacity of corner-like s s -parabolic Cantor sets in ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} for 1 / 2 < s ≤ 1 1/2<s\leq 1 . Despite the spatial gradient of the s-heat kernel lacking temporal anti-symmetry, we obtain analogous results to those known for analytic and Riesz capacities. AMS 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 42B20 (primary); 28A12 (secondary). Keywords: Fractional heat equation, singular integrals, Cantor set.



1.
Introduction

Recent progress in the theory of parabolic equations on time-varying domains has led to major developments, notably through the work of Hofmann, Lewis, Nyström, and Strömqvist
[
Ho1
,
HoL
,
NSt
]
, among others. As expected, there has also been growing interest in understanding the properties of so-called caloric capacities and the removable singularities of bounded solutions to certain parabolic equations. For example, Mourgoglou and Puliatti
[
MoPu
]
studied a specific caloric capacity related to a capacity density condition at a particular scale, enabling them to establish several PDE estimates near the boundary, which are essential for their blow-up-type arguments. Another example is the recent work by Mateu, Prat, and Tolsa
[
MPr
,
MPrT
]
, who investigated removable singularities for Lipschitz caloric functions in terms of capacities, as well as their fractional generalizations, such as those discussed in
[
H
]
.

Building upon this existing framework, the main goal of the present article is to estimate the fractional Lipschitz caloric capacities, related to fractional heat equations, of corner-like Cantor sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. More precisely, we focus on the fractional variants of the heat equation associated with the following pseudo-differential operator:

\Theta^{s}:=(-\Delta_{x})^{s}+\partial_{t},\qquad s\in(0,1],

where for
s=1
, we recover the usual heat equation, and
(-\Delta_{x})
denotes the Laplacian with respect to the spatial variables. When
s<1
,
(-\Delta_{x})^{s}
, known as the
s
-fractional Laplacian with respect to the spatial variables, is defined via its Fourier transform: for each
t
fixed,

\pazocal{F}[(-\Delta_{x})^{s}f](\xi,t)=|\xi|^{2s}\pazocal{F}[f](\xi,t),

or via the integral representation:

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{x})^{s}f(x,t)

\displaystyle=c_{n,s}\,\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x,t)-f(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

For further properties of
(-\Delta_{x})^{s}
, the reader may consult
[
DPV
, §3]
and
[
Ste
]
, as well as the works of Ros-Oton and Serra
[
RoSe1
,
RoSe2
]
regarding regularity theory for these fractional operators.

In what follows, we denote by
P_{s}
the fundamental solution of
\Theta^{s}
, that is
\Theta^{s}P_{s}=\delta_{0}
in the distributional sense, where
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac delta at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The function
P_{s}
can be computed by taking the inverse spatial Fourier transform of
e^{-4\pi^{2}t|\xi|^{2s}}
for
t>0
, and it is null when
t\leq 0
. For
s=1
,
P_{s}
coincides with the classical heat kernel, denoted
P_{1}=W
. When
0<s<1
, an explicit formula for
P_{s}
is not available, but Blumenthal and Getoor
[
BG
, Theorem 2.1]
proved that

P_{s}(x,t)\approx_{n,s}\frac{t}{(|x|^{2}+t^{1/s})^{(n+2s)/2}}\chi_{t>0},

where
\approx_{n,s}
indicates that
P_{s}
is bounded above and below by this quotient up to constants depending only on
n
and
s
. It is important to notice that for
s=1/2
the relation above becomes an exact equality (with implicit dimensional constants), since the inverse Fourier transform can be computed explicitly.

To simplify notation, we represent points in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as
\overline{x}:=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and define the
s
-parabolic distance for
0<s\leq 1
as

\text{dist}_{p_{s}}\big((x,t),(y,\tau)\big):=\max\left\{|x-y|^{2},|t-\tau|^{1/s}\right\}\approx_{n,s}\left(|x-y|^{2}+|t-\tau|^{1/s}\right)^{1/2}.

From this, one naturally defines
s
-parabolic cubes and balls. We also define the
s
-parabolic norm as
|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}:=\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{x},0)
, so that

P_{s}(\overline{x})\approx_{n,s}\frac{t}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s}}\chi_{t>0}.

Moreover, as shown in
[
HMPr
]
, the kernels
P_{s}
and
\nabla_{x}P_{s}
satisfy Calderón-Zygmund (CZ) type estimates of order
n
and
n+1
, respectively, with respect to the
s
-parabolic norm. Specifically, for
\overline{x}=(x,t)\neq 0
and
s\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim\frac{|xt|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}},\qquad|\Delta_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim\frac{|t|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}},\qquad|\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})|\lesssim\frac{|x|}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}}.

The last bound holds only when
t\neq 0
. Furthermore, if
\overline{x}^{\prime}
satisfies
|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}\leq|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}/2
, then

|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{|\overline{x}-\overline{x}^{\prime}|_{p_{s}}^{2\zeta}}{|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1+2\zeta}},\qquad\text{ where $2\zeta:=\min\{1,2s\}$.}

Motivated by these results, and following the approach of
[
MPrT
]
, the author studied, in
[
H
]
, the characterization of removable singularities for
s
-parabolic Lipschitz solutions of the fractional heat equation in the regime
1/2<s\leq 1
. In this context, imposing a fractional parabolic Lipschitz condition on solutions is equivalent to requiring that

(1.1)

\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}<\infty,\qquad\|\partial_{t}^{\frac{1}{2s}}f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}<\infty.

That is, the function must be Lipschitz in space and satisfy an
s
-parabolic BMO estimate for the fractional time derivative. A function
f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}
belongs to the
s
-parabolic BMO space,
\text{BMO}_{p_{s}}
, if

\|f\|_{\ast,p_{s}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(\overline{x})-f_{Q}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{x}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all
s
-parabolic cubes, and
f_{Q}
denotes the average of
f
over
Q
. The fractional time derivative for
s\in(1/2,1]
is defined by

\partial_{t}^{\frac{1}{2s}}f(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(x,\tau)-f(x,t)}{|\tau-t|^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

In
[
H
]
, it is proven that the bounds in (
1.1
) imply
\|f\|_{\text{Lip}{\frac{1}{2s},t}}<\infty
, meaning that such functions are
(1,\frac{1}{2s})
-Lipschitz: Lipschitz in space and
\frac{1}{2s}
-Lipschitz in time. When
s=1
, this corresponds to the functions studied by Nyström and Strömqvist
[
NSt
]
. The motivation for imposing this Lipschitz property stems from the work of Hofmann, Lewis, Murray, and Silver
[
LS
,
LMu
,
HoL
,
Ho2
]
, where the relationship between parabolic singular integral operators and caloric layer potentials on graphs is explored. Their analysis suggests that the appropriate graphs to consider are indeed
(1,1/2)
-Lipschitz graphs.

Accordingly, for each
1/2<s\leq 1
, we define the
(1,\frac{1}{2s})
-Lipschitz caloric capacity
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s}}
of a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as the supremum of
|\langle T,1\rangle|
over distributions
T
supported on
E
such that

\|\nabla_{x}P_{s}\ast T\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq 1,\qquad\|\partial_{t}^{\frac{1}{2s}}P_{s}\ast T\|_{\ast,p_{s}}\leq 1.

Variants of the capacity arise by restricting
T
to positive measures or by imposing, additionally, the same normalization conditions on the conjugate kernel
P_{s}^{\ast}(\overline{x}):=P_{s}(-\overline{x})
, resulting in
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\Theta^{s},+}
, respectively.

In
[
H
]
, it is shown that
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\Theta^{s},+}
can be characterized in terms of
L^{2}
-boundedness of a certain convolution operator and that the nullity of
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s}}
characterizes removability for
(1,\frac{1}{2s})
-Lipschitz solutions of the fractional heat equation. It is also established that the critical
s
-parabolic Hausdorff dimension of
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is
n+1
, and a removable fractal set
E_{p_{s}}
with positive and finite
\pazocal{H}^{n+1}_{p_{s}}
measure is constructed.

Regarding the critical dimension, it is worth noting that the fractional parameter
s
appears only in defining the
s
-parabolic distance, which determines the corresponding
s
-parabolic Hausdorff dimension. This suggests that as
s\to 1/2
, and the metric approaches the Euclidean one, the critical dimension equals that of the ambient space. Inspired by Uy’s work
[
U
]
on analytic capacity, it is conjectured that the capacity
\Gamma_{\Theta^{1/2}}
1
1

1

The Lipschitz capacity for the
\Theta^{1/2}
equation, i.e. that obtained by imposing
\|\nabla P\ast T\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}
, where now
\nabla=(\nabla_{x},\partial_{t})
is a full gradient.
of a compact set
E
should be comparable to its Lebesgue measure
\pazocal{L}^{n+1}(E)
, although this remains an open question.

In this article, we generalize the construction of
E_{p_{s}}
by means of a sequence of contraction ratios
(\lambda_{j})_{j}
satisfying certain conditions depending on
s
and an absolute parameter
\tau_{0}
. To be precise, we fix
1/2<s\leq 1
and pick the smallest positive integer
d\geq 2
such that

d+1<d^{2s},

and we consider
0<\lambda_{j}\leq\tau_{0}<1/d
, for every
j
. Using the spatial antisymmetry of the kernel
\nabla_{x}P_{s}
and techniques from Mateu and Tolsa’s work on Riesz kernels
[
MT
,
T2
]
, our main result reads as follows:

Theorem
.

Let
E_{p_{s}}
be the
s
-parabolic Cantor set associated to
(\lambda_{j})_{j}
, whose construction will be precised later on. If
\ell_{j}:=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{j}
, we have

C^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\theta_{j,p_{s}}^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\leq\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{p_{s}})\leq\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{p_{s}})\leq C\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\theta_{j,p_{s}}^{2}\right)^{-1/2},

where
C
depends only on
n,s
and
\tau_{0}
and

\theta_{j,p_{s}}:=\frac{\ell_{j}^{-(n+1)}}{(d+1)^{j}d^{nj}}.

This paper is organized into five sections that develop the proof of the main result. In Section
2
, we fix important notation and present the explicit construction of the
s
-parabolic Cantor set. We also establish some basic properties related to the convolution operator associated with the kernel
\nabla_{x}P_{s}
and a positive Borel regular measure
\mu
, denoted by
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}
.

Section
3
is devoted to proving upper
L^{2}
estimates for expressions of the form
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu_{k}}\chi_{Q}
, where
Q
is any
s
-parabolic cube and
\mu_{k}
is the uniform probability measure associated with the
k
-th generation of the Cantor set. This estimate is essential for proving the lower bound of the main theorem in Section
4
, where we apply a
T1
-theorem valid in geometrically doubling spaces.

In Section
5
, we derive lower
L^{2}
estimates for
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu_{k}}1
using arguments analogous to those of Tolsa in
[
T2
, §5]
, originally developed for Riesz kernels. These estimates are then used in Section
6
to establish the remaining upper bound of the main theorem, relying on a local
Tb
theorem by Auscher and Routin
[
AR
, Theorem 3.5]
.

It is in these final arguments that the distinct nature of the kernel
\nabla_{x}P_{s}
, in contrast to Riesz kernels, becomes evident. Due to the lack of anti-symmetry, we must construct a system of accretive functions that highlights how
\nabla_{x}P_{s}
and its conjugate differ by a temporal reflection.

2.
Basic definitions and properties

Let us briefly recall the construction presented in
[
H
, §4.1]
of the particular Cantor set associated with the capacity
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s}}
. Choose a positive integer
d\geq 2
such that

d+1<d^{2s}.

By convention we fix the minimum value of
d\geq 2
that satisfies the above condition so that
d=d(s)
. In what follows, we shall also fix an absolute constant
\tau_{0}<1/d
.

Let
Q^{0}:=[0,1]^{n+1}
be the unit cube of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and pick
(d+1)d^{n}
disjoint
s
-parabolic cubes
Q_{i}^{1}
, contained in
Q^{0}
, with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, side length
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\tau_{0}
, and with the following locations: for each of the first
n
intervals
[0,1]
of the cartesian product defining
Q^{0}
, we set

l_{d}:=\frac{1-d\lambda_{1}}{d-1},\qquad J_{j}:=\big[(j-1)(\lambda_{1}+l_{d}),j\lambda_{1}+(j-1)l_{d}\big],\quad j=1,\ldots,d,

and take
T_{d}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{d}J_{j}
. The remaining temporal interval
[0,1]
is split in
d+1
intervals of length
\lambda_{1}^{2s}
in an analogous way. That is, we set

\widetilde{l}_{d}:=\frac{1-(d+1)\lambda_{1}^{2s}}{d},\qquad\widetilde{J}_{j}:=\big[(j-1)(\lambda_{1}^{2s}+\widetilde{l}_{d}),j\lambda_{1}^{2s}+(j-1)\widetilde{l}_{d}\big],\quad j=1,\ldots,d+1,

and keep the subset
\widetilde{T}_{d}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{d+1}\widetilde{J}_{j}
. This way, the first generation of the Cantor set is

E_{1,p_{s}}:=(T_{d})^{n}\times\widetilde{T}_{d}.

This procedure continues inductively, so that the
k
-th generation
E_{k,p_{s}}
will be formed by
(d+1)^{k}d^{nk}
disjoint
s
-parabolic cubes with side length
\ell_{k}:=\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}
,
0<\lambda_{k}\leq\tau_{0}<1/d
, and with locations determined by the above iterative process. We name such cubes
Q^{k}_{j}
, with
j=1,\ldots,(d+1)^{k}d^{nk}
. The resulting
s
-
parabolic
Cantor set is

(2.1)

E_{p_{s}}=E_{p_{s}}(\lambda):=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k,p_{s}}.

For each generation
k
, consider the probability measure

\mu_{k}:=\frac{1}{|E_{k,p_{s}}|}\pazocal{L}^{n+1}|_{E_{k,p_{s}}}.

Moreover, given
0\leq j\leq k
we define

\theta_{j,p_{s}}:=\frac{\mu_{k}(Q^{j}_{i})}{\ell_{j}^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{(d+1)^{j}d^{nj}\ell_{j}^{n+1}}.

In
[
H
, §4.1]
it is argued that if one chooses
\lambda_{k}:=((d+1)d^{n})^{-1/(n+1)}
for every generation
k
, then
0<\pazocal{H}_{p_{s}}^{n+1}(E_{p_{s}})<\infty
. Observe that, as a consequence, this would imply
\theta_{k,p_{s}}=1
, for all
k
.

There are more general assumptions that imply the lower bound
\pazocal{H}_{p_{s}}^{n+1}(E_{p_{s}})>0
. For instance, assume that there exists
\kappa>0
so that
\theta_{k,p_{s}}\leq\kappa
for every
k
. Now consider the probability measure
\mu
defined on
E_{p_{s}}
such that for each generation
k
,
\mu(Q_{j}^{k}):=(d+1)^{-k}d^{-nk},\,1\leq j\leq(d+1)^{k}d^{nk}
. We claim that that the previous measure presents upper
s
-parabolic growth of degree
n+1
with constant depending only on
n,s
and
\kappa
. If this held, by Frostman’s lemma
[
Mat
, Theorem 8.8]
we would get
\pazocal{H}_{p_{s}}^{n+1}(E_{p_{s}})>0
and thus, in particular,
\text{dim}_{\pazocal{H}_{p_{s}}}(E_{p_{s}})\geq n+1
.

To prove the desired growth of
\mu
, let
Q
be any
s
-parabolic cube, that we may assume to be contained in
Q^{0}
, and pick
k
with the property
\ell_{k+1}\leq\ell(Q)\leq\ell_{k}
, so that
Q
can meet, at most,
(d+1)d^{n}
cubes
Q_{j}^{k}
. Thus
\mu(Q)\leq(d+1)^{-(k-1)}d^{-n(k-1)}
and we directly deduce

\displaystyle\mu(Q)\lesssim\frac{1}{(d+1)^{k+1}d^{n(k+1)}}\leq\kappa\ell_{k+1}^{n+1}\leq\ell(Q)^{n+1}.

For a fixed generation
k
, the assumption
\theta_{j,p_{s}}\leq\kappa
for
0\leq j\leq k
also implies the same growth property for
\mu_{k}
. Indeed, fix
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
any
s
-parabolic cube contained in
Q^{0}
and distinguish two cases: whether if
\ell(Q)\leq\ell_{k}
or not. If
\ell(Q)\leq\ell_{k}
, notice that
|E_{k,p_{s}}|=(d+1)^{k}d^{nk}\ell_{k}^{n+2s}
, so we have

\displaystyle\mu_{k}(Q)\leq\frac{1}{(d+1)^{k}d^{nk}\ell_{k}^{n+2s}}\,\ell(Q)^{n+2s}=\theta_{k,p_{s}}\frac{\ell(Q)^{2s-1}}{\ell_{k}^{2s-1}}\ell(Q)^{n+1}\leq\kappa\ell(Q)^{n+1}.

If
\ell(Q)>\ell_{k}
, there exists
0\leq m\leq k-1
such that
\ell_{m+1}<\ell(Q)\leq\ell_{m}
and, in this case, the number of cubes of the
m
-th generation that
Q
can intersect is bounded by
(d+1)d^{n}
(the latter is not the best bound, but it suffices for our computations). Therefore

\displaystyle\mu_{k}(Q)

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)d^{n}\mu_{k}(Q^{m}_{0})\simeq((d+1)d^{n})^{-m}\simeq\theta_{m+1,p_{s}}\,\ell_{m+1}^{n+1}\leq\kappa\ell(Q)^{n+1},

and we deduce the desired growth of
\mu_{k}
.

Let us fix some more notation and establish some useful properties. In the sequel,
k
will be a fixed integer so that
E_{k,p_{s}}
will be a fixed generation of the Cantor set constructed above and we will write

\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\quad\text{as well as}\quad\langle f,g\rangle:=\int fg\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}

For each generation
0\leq j\leq k
we write

\pazocal{Q}^{j}:=\{Q_{i}^{j}\,:\,1\leq i\leq(d+1)^{j}d^{jn}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pazocal{Q}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\pazocal{Q}^{j}.

If we write
Q^{j}
we mean an arbitrary cube of
\pazocal{Q}^{j}
.

Given
\mu
a real compactly supported Borel measure with upper
s
-parabolic growth of degree
n+1
, we define for each
f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mu)
the convolution operator associated to
\nabla_{x}P_{s}
,

\pazocal{P}_{\mu}^{s}f(\overline{x}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})f(\overline{y})\text{d}\mu(\overline{y}),\hskip 14.22636pt\overline{x}\notin\text{supp}(\mu).

In the particular case in which
f
is the constant function
1
we write
\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu(\overline{x}):=\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}1(\overline{x})
. We also introduce the truncated version of
\pazocal{P}_{\mu}^{s}
,

\pazocal{P}_{\mu,\varepsilon}^{s}f(\overline{x}):=\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|>\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})f(\overline{y})\text{d}\mu(\overline{y}),\hskip 14.22636pt\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\;\varepsilon>0.

For a given
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we will say that
\pazocal{P}_{\mu}^{s}f
belongs to
L^{p}(\mu)
if the
L^{p}(\mu)
-norm of the truncations
\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}
is uniformly bounded on
\varepsilon
, and we write

\|\pazocal{P}_{\mu}^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}:=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}

We will say that the operator
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)
if the operators
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)
uniformly on
\varepsilon
, and we equally set

\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{p}(\mu)}:=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mu)\to L^{p}(\mu)}.

Let us begin by establishing some basic properties of
\pazocal{P}_{\mu}^{s}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

s
-parabolic cube, we have
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu_{k}}\chi_{R}\in L^{1}_{\text{{loc}}}(\mu_{k})
. This implies, in particular,
\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}\in L^{1}_{\text{{loc}}}(\mu_{k}).

Proof.

Given any
s
-parabolic cube
Q^{k}\in\pazocal{Q}^{k}
and
R\in\pazocal{Q}
,

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{k}}\int_{R}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\overline{y})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\overline{x})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q^{k}}\int_{R\cap E_{k,p_{s}}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\overline{y})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\overline{x})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt=\int_{Q^{k}}\int_{(R\cap E_{k,p_{s}})\setminus{Q^{k}}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\overline{y})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\overline{x})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1}}+\int_{Q^{k}}\int_{Q^{k}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\overline{y})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(\overline{x})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+1}}=:\text{I}+\text{II}.

Observe that the points of
Q^{k}
and
(R\cap E_{k,p_{s}})\setminus{Q^{k}}
are separated, at least, by an
s
-parabolic distance comparable to

\min\bigg\{\ell_{k-1}\bigg(\frac{1-d\lambda_{k}}{d-1}\bigg),\ell_{k-1}\bigg(\frac{1-(d+1)\lambda_{1}^{2s}}{d}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2s}}\bigg\}\gtrsim\ell_{k-1},

where the previous implicit constant depends on
\tau_{0}
and
s
. Therefore, it is clear that
\text{I}\lesssim 1/\ell_{k-1}^{n+1}<\infty
. On the other hand, to deal with
II
, for each
\overline{y}\in Q^{k}
we shall contain
Q^{k}
in the
s
-parabolic cube
\widetilde{Q}
centered at
\overline{y}

with side length
2\,\text{diam}_{p_{s}}(Q^{k})
, and split the previous set into the

5.1.
The stopping scales and intervals
I_{k}

Let
B
be some big constant (say
B>100
) to be fixed below. We define inductively the following subset

\text{Stop}:=\{s_{0},\ldots,s_{m}\}\subset\{0,1,\ldots,k\}.

First, set
s_{0}:=0
. If for some
j\geq 0
,
s_{j}
has already been defined and
s_{j}<k-1
, then
s_{j+1}
is the least integer
i>s_{j}
which verifies at least one of the following:

a)

i=k
, or

b)

\theta_{i,p_{s}}>B\theta_{s_{j},p_{s}}
, or

c)

\theta_{i,p_{s}}<B^{-1}\theta_{s_{j},p_{s}}
.

We finish our construction of Stop when we find some
s_{j+1}=k
. Notice that we have

[0,k-1]=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}[s_{j},s_{j+1})=:\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}I_{j},

with
I_{j}
pairwise disjoint. Observe that
|I_{j}|=s_{j+1}-s_{j}
coincides with
\#(I_{j}\cap\mathbb{Z})
. We write

T_{j}\mu_{k}:=\sum_{s_{j}\leq i<s_{j+1}}D_{i}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k},\qquad\text{for $0\leq j\leq m$}.

Then,
S_{k}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}S_{i+1}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}-S_{i}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}D_{i}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}T_{j}\mu_{k}
, and since functions
D_{j}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}
are pairwise orthogonal,

\|S_{k}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\|T_{j}\mu_{k}\|^{2}.

5.2.
Good and bad scales

To simplify notation we shall write, for any
A\subset[0,k]
,

\sigma(A):=\sum_{j\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{j,p_{s}}^{2}.

We will say that
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}
is a
good scale
and we write
j\in\pazocal{G}
, if

\sum_{i=0}^{j}\theta_{i,p_{s}}\frac{\ell_{j}}{\ell_{i}}=:p_{j}\leq 40\theta_{j,p_{s}}.

Otherwise we that
j
is a
bad scale
and we write
j\in\pazocal{B}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The following holds,

\sigma(\pazocal{B})\leq\frac{1}{400}\sigma([0,k-1]).

Proof.

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{j}^{2}\leq\bigg(\frac{d}{d-1}\bigg)^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\theta_{i,p_{s}}^{2}=\bigg(\frac{d}{d-1}\bigg)^{2}\sigma([0,k-1])\leq 4\sigma([0,k-1]).

Then,

\sigma(\pazocal{B})=\sum_{j\in\pazocal{B}}\theta_{j,p_{s}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1600}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}p_{j}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{400}\sigma([0,k-1]).

∎

5.3.
Good and bad intervals

We will say that an interval
I_{j}
is
good
if

\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{G})\geq\frac{1}{400}\sigma(I_{j}).

Otherwise we say that it is bad, meaning,

\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{B})>\frac{399}{400}\sigma(I_{j}).

Lemma 5.4
.

The following holds,

\sigma([0,k-1])\leq\frac{399}{398}\sum_{I_{j}\,\text{{good}}}\sigma(I_{j}).

Proof.

If
I_{j}
is bad, then
\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{G})<\frac{1}{400}\sigma(I_{j})
and therefore

\sigma(I_{j})<\frac{1}{400}\sigma(I_{j})+\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{B}),\quad\text{implying}\quad\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{B})>\frac{399}{400}\sigma(I_{j}).

By Lemma
5.3
we then obtain

\sum_{I_{j}\,\text{bad}}\sigma(I_{j})\leq\frac{400}{399}\sigma(\pazocal{B})\leq\frac{1}{399}\sigma([0,k-1]),

so we get

\sigma([0,k-1])\leq\sum_{I_{j}\,\text{good}}\sigma(I_{j})+\frac{1}{399}\sigma([0,k-1]),

and the result follows.
∎

5.4.
Long and short intervals

Let
N_{L}
be some (large) integer to be fixed below. We say that an interval
I_{j}
i
long
if

|I_{j}|=s_{j+1}-s_{j}\geq N_{L},

and otherwise we say it is
short
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
I_{j}
be good and
j_{0}:=\min(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{G})
. Then,

j_{0}-s_{j}\leq\frac{400B^{4}}{400B^{4}+1}(s_{j+1}-s_{j}).

Proof.

Write
\ell:=s_{j+1}-s_{j}
and
\lambda=j_{0}-s_{j}
. Then, by definition of
s_{j+1}
we have

\displaystyle\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{G})\leq\sum_{s_{j}\leq i<s_{j+1}}\theta_{i,p_{s}}^{2}\leq B^{2}\theta_{s_{j},p_{s}}^{2}(\ell-\lambda),

as well as

\displaystyle\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{B})\leq\sum_{i\in\pazocal{B}\,:\,s_{j}\leq i<s_{j+1}}\theta_{i,p_{s}}^{2}\geq B^{-2}\theta_{s_{j},p_{s}}^{2}\lambda.

Since
I_{j}
is good,
\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{B})\leq 400\sigma(I_{j}\cap\pazocal{G})
, so

B^{2}\lambda\leq 400B^{2}(\ell-\lambda),\quad\text{that is}\quad\lambda\leq\frac{400B^{4}}{400B^{4}+1}\ell,

and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.6

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma

\big\rvert S_{P}\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu_{k}}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{Q}}(\overline{x})-S_{Q}\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu_{k}}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{Q}}(\overline{x})\big\rvert\lesssim C_{8}\frac{\ell_{j}}{\ell_{j-1}}p_{j-1},\qquad\text{where $C_{8}:=(d+1)^{h}d^{hn}$}.

Assume that
P

s
-parabolic cube of the

-generation sharing the upper right-most corner of

\displaystyle\bigg\rvert\frac{1}{\mu_{k}(P)}\int_{P}

\displaystyle\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu_{k}}\chi_{Q\setminus{P}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{\mu_{k}(P)}\bigg\rvert\int_{P}\int_{Q\setminus{P}}\frac{(x_{1}-y_{1})(t-u)}{|(x,t)-(y,u)|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}}\chi_{t-u>0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(y,u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}(x,t)\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\int_{P_{>}}\int_{F_{1}}

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
A,c_{0}
be positive constants and

integers such that

\frac{\ell_{q}}{\ell_{q-1}}\leq c_{0}\theta_{q,p_{s}}
and, for all

A^{-1}\theta_{q,p_{s}}\leq\theta_{j,p_{s}}\leq A\theta_{q,p_{s}}.

There exists
N_{1}(n,s,c_{0},A)
such that if
|q-r|>N_{1}
, then

\sum_{j=q}^{r}\|D_{j}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}\|^{2}\geq C|q-r|\theta_{q,p_{s}}^{2},\qquad\text{where $C(n,s,c_{0},A)$}.

Proof.

Set
f:=\sum_{j=q}^{r}D_{j}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}
. We have to show that

\|f\|^{2}\geq C|q-r|\theta_{q,p_{s}}^{2}.

Let
M_{0}
be some positive integer depending on
n,s,c_{0}
and
A
to be fixed below. We decompose
f
as follows

f=\sum_{j=q}^{q+tM_{0}-1}D_{j}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}+\sum_{j=q+tM_{0}}^{r}D_{j}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k},

where
t
is the biggest integer with
q+tM_{0}-1\leq r
. Assuming
N_{1}
big enough (take
N_{1}>2M_{0}-1
), we have
|q-r|<M_{0}t\leq 2|q-r|
. For the first sum on the right side, write

\sum_{j=q}^{q+tM_{0}-1}D_{j}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}=\sum_{l=0}^{t-1}\sum_{j=q+lM_{0}}^{q+(l+1)M_{0}-1}D_{j}\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{k}=:\sum_{l=0}^{t-1}U_{l}(\mu_{k}).

By orthogonality we have

\|f\|^{2}\geq\sum_{l=0}^{t-1}\|U_{l}(\mu_{k})\|.

We shall show that if the parameter
M_{0}(n,s,c_{0},A)
is chosen big enough,

(5.4)

\|U_{l}(\mu_{k})\|^{2}\geq C(n,s,c_{0},A)\theta_{q,p_{s}}^{2},\qquad\text{for all $0\leq l\leq t-1$},

and then
\|f\|^{2}\geq C|q-r|\theta_{q,p_{s}}^{2}
, and we would be done. To prove (
5.4
), we intend to apply Lemma
5.6
. Observe that

\displaystyle p_{q+lM_{0}-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{q+lM_{0}-1}\theta_{i,p_{s}}\frac{\ell_{q+lM_{0}-1}}{\ell_{i}}=\sum_{i=q}^{q+lM_{0}-1}\theta_{i,p_{s}}\frac{\ell_{q+lM_{0}-1}}{\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\theta_{i,p_{s}}\frac{\ell_{q+lM_{0}-1}}{\ell_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=q}^{q+lM_{0}-1}\theta_{i,p_{s}}\frac{\ell_{q+lM_{0}-1}}{\ell_{i}}+\frac{\ell_{q+lM_{0}-1}}{\ell_{q-1}}p_{q-1}\leq A\frac{d}{d-1}\theta_{q,p_{s}}+\frac{\ell_{q+lM_{0}-1}}{\ell_{q-1}}p_{q-1}.

Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{q+lM_{0}}}{\ell_{q+lM_{0}-1}}p_{q+lM_{0}-1}

\displaystyle\leq A\frac{d}{d-1}\theta_{q,p_{s}}+\frac{\ell_{q+lM_{0}}}{\ell_{q-1}}p_{q-1}\leq 2A\theta_{q,p_{s}}+\frac{\ell_{q}}{\ell_{q+1}}p_{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq(2A+c_{0})\theta_{q,p_{s}}.

On the other hand,

Let us begin by recalling an alternative definition of the capacity
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}

Lemma 6.1

There exists

such that for all

Proof.

To simplify notation, in this proof we shall write
\langle f,g\rangle:=\int fg\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}
. Then, since

is a probability measure,

\displaystyle\|\pazocal{P}^{s}(\alpha\mu_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}^{2}=|\langle\pazocal{P}^{s}(\alpha\mu_{j}),\pazocal{P}^{s}(\alpha\mu_{j})\rangle|\leq\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\alpha\mu_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})\to L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}\alpha^{1/2}\|\pazocal{P}^{s}(\alpha\mu_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}^{2},

in other words,

\|\pazocal{P}^{s}(\alpha\mu_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}\leq\alpha^{1/2}\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\alpha\mu_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})\to L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}.

This implies

\displaystyle\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\alpha\mu_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})\to L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{-1/2}\|\pazocal{P}^{s}(\alpha\mu_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}=\alpha\|\pazocal{P}^{s}\mu_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{j})}\geq\alpha C_{1}^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\theta_{i,p_{s}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where at the last step we have applied Theorem

. So by definition of

we get the upper bound with constant

. On the other hand, for any

, applying relation (

) we get

\displaystyle|\langle\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\alpha\mu_{j}}f,g\rangle|\leq\alpha C_{2}^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\theta_{i,p_{s}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\alpha\mu_{j})}.

Therefore,

\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{j})\to L^{2}(\mu_{j})}\leq\alpha C_{2}^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\theta_{i,p_{s}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

and by the definition of

we get the lower bound with constant

∎

\text{supp}(b_{i}^{j,\ast})\subset Q_{i}^{j}
. Another key observation is that, by construction of the Cantor set, the set

\pazocal{Q}_{i}^{M\nearrow j}:=\bigcup_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i}^{j}}Q_{m}^{M}\qquad\text{also satisfies}\qquad\mathsf{R}_{i}^{j}(\pazocal{Q}_{i}^{M\nearrow j})=\pazocal{Q}_{i}^{M\nearrow j}.

We now begin by proving that (
6.4
) holds. Observe that with estimate (
6.6
) and the independence of
\int\varphi_{i}^{M}
with respect to
i
, writing
\varphi_{\mathsf{R_{i}^{j}(m)}}^{M}:=\varphi_{m}^{M}\circ\mathsf{R}_{i}^{j}
we directly deduce the desired estimates:

(6.9)

\displaystyle\bigg\rvert\int b_{i}^{j}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}}\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle\frac{\int\varphi_{m}^{M}(\,\cdot-\overline{w}_{i}^{j})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\int\varphi_{m}^{M}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}=\langle\nu,\psi_{i_{0}}^{j}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Q_{i}^{j}),\quad\text{and}

(6.10)

\displaystyle\bigg\rvert\int b_{i}^{j,\ast}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}}\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle\frac{\int\varphi_{\mathsf{R}_{i}^{j}(m)}^{M}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}{\int\varphi_{m}^{M}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}=\langle\nu,\psi_{i_{0}}^{j}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Q_{i}^{j}).

Moreover, since
\nu
is admissible for
\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{k,p_{s}})
and thus has upper
s
-parabolic growth of degree
n+1
, by
[
HMPr
, Lemma 4.2]
and the localization result
[
H
, Lemma 3.2]
we get

\|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\nu}\psi_{i}^{j}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1,

with implicit constants depending on
n,s
and
\tau_{0}
, for all
0\leq j\leq M
and
1\leq i\leq(d+1)^{j}d^{jn}
. Then, in particular,

\langle\nu,\psi_{i_{0}}^{M}\rangle\lesssim\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}\big(\alpha Q_{i_{0}}^{M}\cap E_{k,p_{s}}\big)=\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}\big(Q_{i_{0}}^{M}\cap E_{k,p_{s}}\big).

But the set
Q_{i_{0}}^{M}\cap E_{k,p_{s}}
can be obtained by dilating
E_{p_{s}}(\lambda_{M+1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
an
s
-parabolic factor
\ell_{M}
. Thus, by assumption
A3
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}\big(Q_{i_{0}}^{M}\cap E_{k,p_{s}}\big)

\displaystyle=\ell_{M}^{n+1}\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}\big(E_{p_{s}}(\lambda_{M+1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{M}^{n+1}A_{0}\theta_{M,p_{s}}\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{k,p_{s}})=\frac{A_{0}}{(d+1)^{M}d^{Mn}}\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{k,p_{s}})=A_{0}\mu(Q_{i_{0}}^{M}).

So we get

\displaystyle\bigg\rvert\int b_{i}^{j}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigg\rvert\int b_{i}^{j,\ast}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg\rvert=\langle\nu,\psi_{i_{0}}^{j}\rangle=\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}}\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle\leq\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}}\langle\nu,\psi_{i_{0}}^{M}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}}\mu(Q_{i_{0}}^{M})=\mu(Q_{i_{0}}^{j})=\mu(Q_{i}^{j})

Therefore, by (
6.9
) and (
6.10
) we get

(6.11)

\mu(Q_{i}^{j})\geq\bigg\rvert\int b_{i}^{j}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg\rvert=\bigg\rvert\int b_{i}^{j,\ast}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg\rvert=\langle\nu,\psi_{i_{0}}^{j}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Q_{i}^{j}),

and (
6.3
) follows.

We are left to verify relations
6.5
. In fact, we will bound
|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}b_{i}^{j}|
at every point, and from this we will get the same estimate for the conjugate operator by the following observation: since
\pazocal{Q}_{i}^{M\nearrow j}
is invariant under
\mathsf{R}_{i}^{j}
and
\mathsf{R}_{i}^{j}
is its own inverse,

\displaystyle\pazocal{P}^{s,\ast}_{\mu}b_{i}^{j,\ast}(\overline{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{k,p_{s}})}{|E_{M,p_{s}}|}\int_{\pazocal{Q}_{i}^{M\nearrow j}}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x-y,u-t)b_{i}^{j}(y,2t_{i}^{j}-u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{k,p_{s}})}{|E_{M,p_{s}}|}\int_{\pazocal{Q}_{i}^{M\nearrow j}}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(x-y,2t_{i}^{j}-t-u)b_{i}^{j}(y,u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u=\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}b_{i}^{j}(\mathsf{R}_{i}^{j}(\overline{x})).

So we focus our efforts on estimating
|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}b_{i}^{j}|
. Let us begin our arguments by choosing
\Psi^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-n-2s}\Psi(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon})
a standard mollifier in the
s
-parabolic space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Estimate
\|\pazocal{P}^{s}(\psi_{i}^{j}\nu)\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1
is equivalent to

(6.12)

\|\nabla_{x}P_{s}\ast\Psi^{\varepsilon}\ast(\psi_{i}^{j}\nu)\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1,

where we convey that
\|\Psi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}=1
. We call
\nabla_{x}P_{s}^{\varepsilon}:=\nabla_{x}P_{s}\ast\Psi^{\varepsilon}
the regularized kernel and
\pazocal{P}_{\mu}^{s,\varepsilon}
its associated convolution operator. Notice the different position of the symbol
\varepsilon
in the previous operator with respect to
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}
, that recall that is a truncation of
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}
.

It is not hard to prove that for
|\overline{x}|_{p_{s}}<\varepsilon
, one has
|\nabla_{x}P_{s}^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x})|\lesssim\varepsilon^{-(n+1)}
. Then, given
\sigma
any Borel measure (possibly signed) with upper
s
-parabolic growth of degree
n+1
, by the growth estimates of
[
HMPr
, Theorem 2.2]
we get for any
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

\displaystyle\big\rvert\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}\sigma(\overline{x})

\displaystyle-\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\varepsilon}\sigma(\overline{x})\big\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigg\rvert\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}\leq 4\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}P_{s}^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(\overline{y})+\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}>4\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}P_{s}^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\int_{\varepsilon<|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}<\leq 4\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(\overline{y})-\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}>4\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(\overline{y})\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\sigma|(B(\overline{x},4\varepsilon))}{\varepsilon^{n+1}}+\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}>4\varepsilon}|\nabla_{x}P_{s}^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\sigma|(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}>4\varepsilon}\int_{|\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}<\varepsilon}|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{y}-\overline{z})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})|\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\overline{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\sigma|(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}>4\varepsilon}\int_{|\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}<\varepsilon}\frac{|\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\overline{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\sigma|(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\varepsilon\int_{|\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}<\varepsilon}\bigg(\int_{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}>4\varepsilon}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\sigma|(\overline{y})}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}\bigg)\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\overline{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{z}\lesssim 1+\varepsilon\frac{\|\Psi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}}{\varepsilon}\lesssim 1.

This implies that the boundedness of
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}b_{i}^{j}
follows from that of
\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\mu}b_{i_{0}}^{j}
, that in turn by (
6.12
) follows from that of

(6.13)

\big\rvert\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\mu}b_{i_{0}}^{j}-\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\nu}\psi_{i_{0}}^{j}\big\rvert.

Observe that the above difference can be written as

\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\mu}b_{i_{0}}^{j}-\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\nu}\psi_{i_{0}}^{j}=\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}}\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}\alpha_{m}^{M},

where we have defined

\alpha_{m}^{M}:=\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle\frac{\varphi_{m}^{M}}{\int\varphi_{m}^{M}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\mu-\psi_{m}^{M}\nu,\qquad\text{that is such that}\quad\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha_{m}^{M}=0.

Let us prove two claims that will help us estimate (
6.13
):

1.

The following holds,

(6.14)

|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})\lesssim 1|,\qquad\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

Indeed, notice that on the one hand

\displaystyle|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\mu}\varphi_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})|

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{k,p_{s}})}{|E_{M,p_{s}}|}|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\pazocal{L}^{n+1}|_{Q_{m}^{M}}}\varphi_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{k,p_{s}})}{(d+1)^{M}d^{Mn}\ell_{M}^{n+1}}\|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\pazocal{L}^{n+1}|_{Q^{0}}}\varphi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\pazocal{H}^{n+1}_{\infty,p_{s}}(E_{M,p_{s}})}{(d+1)^{M}d^{Mn}\ell_{M}^{n+1}}\|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\pazocal{L}^{n+1}|_{Q^{0}}}\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\pazocal{L}^{n+1}|_{Q^{0}}}\varphi\|_{\infty},

and by (
6.7
) we get
|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\mu}\varphi_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})|\lesssim 1
. We also have
|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\nu}\psi_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})|\lesssim 1
for almost every point by (
6.12
), and by continuity this extends to all points in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Finally, by (
6.8
) and (
6.11
)

\int\varphi_{m}^{M}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu(Q_{m}^{M})\geq\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle,

and the desired estimate follows.

2.

Let us now fix
m
and a cube
Q_{m}^{M}
centered at
\overline{x}_{m}^{M}
. Take
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
|\overline{x}-\overline{x}_{m}^{M}|_{p_{s}}>4\ell_{M}
and assume
\varepsilon<\ell_{M}/2
. Then,

(6.15)

\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})\lesssim\frac{\ell_{M}^{n+2}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{x},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}}.

To prove the latter, begin by taking
\overline{y}\in B(\overline{x},\varepsilon)
and write

|\pazocal{P}^{s}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})|=\bigg\rvert\frac{\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle}{\int\varphi_{m}^{M}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}\varphi_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})-\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\nu}\psi_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})\bigg\rvert.

Notice

\displaystyle\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}\varphi_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})=\int_{Q_{m}^{M}}\big(\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z})-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}

\displaystyle-\overline{x}_{m}^{M})\big)\varphi_{m}^{M}(\overline{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\overline{z})

\displaystyle+\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{x}_{m}^{M})\int_{Q_{m}^{M}}\varphi(\overline{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\overline{z}),

which implies,

\displaystyle|\pazocal{P}^{s}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})|\leq\big\rvert

\displaystyle\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\nu}\psi_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})-\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{x}_{m}^{M})\big\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip-7.11317pt+\frac{\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle}{\int\varphi_{m}^{m}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\int_{Q_{m}^{M}}|\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{z}-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{x}_{m}^{M}))|\varphi_{m}^{M}(\overline{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\overline{z}).

For the second summand we apply the last estimate of
[
HMPr
, Theorem 2.2]
and obtain that it can be bounded by

\frac{\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle}{\int\varphi_{m}^{m}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}\int_{Q_{m}^{M}}\frac{|\overline{z}-\overline{x}_{m}^{M}|_{p_{s}}}{|\overline{y}-\overline{z}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}\varphi_{m}^{M}(\overline{z})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(\overline{z})\lesssim\frac{\ell_{M}^{n+2}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}},

where we have used
\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle\lesssim\ell_{M}^{n+1}
by
[
H
, Theorem 3.1]
. Here, implicit constants may depend on
\tau_{0}
.

For the first summand, naming
\varphi_{\overline{y}}:=\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\cdot)-\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{x}_{m}^{M})
we get

\big\rvert\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\nu}\psi_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})-\langle\nu,\psi_{m}^{M}\rangle\nabla_{x}P_{s}(\overline{y}-\overline{x}_{m}^{M})\big\rvert=|\langle\varphi_{\overline{y}},\psi_{m}^{M}\nu\rangle|

It is clear that

\|\varphi_{\overline{y}}\|_{L^{\infty}(2Q_{m}^{M})}\lesssim\frac{\ell_{M}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}}.

In addition, by
[
HMPr
, Theorem 2.2]
it is also clear that for almost every point
\overline{z}\in 2Q_{m}^{M}
the following bounds hold,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}P_{s}(\overline{z}-\overline{y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\overline{z}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}},

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{i}}^{3}P_{s}(\overline{z}-\overline{y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\overline{z}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+3}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}\,\ell_{M}},

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\partial_{x_{i}}P_{s}(\overline{z}-\overline{y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|z-y|}{|\overline{z}-\overline{y}|_{p_{s}}^{n+2s+2}}\lesssim\frac{\ell_{M}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}\,\ell_{M}^{2s}}.

Now we observe that the function

\eta:=\bigg[\frac{\ell_{M}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}}\bigg]^{-1}\varphi_{\overline{y}}\psi_{m}^{M}

becomes admissible for
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\Theta^{s},+}(2Q_{m}^{M})
, with implicit constants also depending on
\tau_{0}
. To be precise, the previous function may lack being differentiable with respect to time in a set of null
\pazocal{L}^{n+1}
-measure. Nevertheless,
[
HMPr
, Theorem 3.1]
can be also proved, with minor modifications, for such functions, and so the growth result
[
H
, Theorem 3.1]
can be also extended to this setting. Then,

\displaystyle|\langle\varphi_{\overline{y}},\psi_{m}^{M}\nu\rangle|=\frac{\ell_{M}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}}|\langle\eta,\nu\rangle|\lesssim\frac{\ell_{M}^{n+2}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}}.

Therefore, given
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
|\overline{x}-\overline{x}_{m}^{M}|>4\ell_{M}
and
\varepsilon<\ell_{M}/2
we have proved:

|\pazocal{P}^{s}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})|\lesssim\frac{\ell_{M}^{n+2}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}},\qquad\overline{y}\in B(\overline{x},\varepsilon).

This in turn implies

\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})\leq\int_{B(\overline{x},\varepsilon)}\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})|\pazocal{P}^{s}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{y})|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\overline{y}\lesssim\frac{\ell_{M}^{n+2}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{y},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}},

since
\|\Psi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}=1
, that is what we wanted to prove.

Now let
\varepsilon<\ell_{m}/2
,
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and fix
Q_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})
one of the
s
-parabolic cubes of the
M
-th generation that is closest to
\overline{x}
. Since the number of cubes such that
\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(Q_{m}^{M},Q_{m}^{M}(\overline{x}))\leq 4\ell_{M}
is bounded by a constant depending on
n
and
s
, we get by relations (
6.14
) and (
6.15
),

\displaystyle\big\rvert\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\mu}

\displaystyle b_{i_{0}}^{j}(\overline{x})-\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}_{\nu}\psi_{i_{0}}^{j}(\overline{x})\big\rvert\leq\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}}|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}\\
\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(Q_{m}^{M},Q_{m}^{M}(\overline{x}))\leq 4\ell_{M}\end{subarray}}|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}\\
\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(Q_{m}^{M},Q_{m}^{M}(\overline{x}))>4\ell_{M}\end{subarray}}|\pazocal{P}^{s,\varepsilon}\alpha_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})|

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{j}\\
Q_{m}^{M}\neq Q_{m}^{M}(\overline{x})\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell_{M}^{n+2}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{x},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}}\leq 1+\sum_{r=j}^{M-1}\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{r}\setminus{Q_{i_{0}}^{r+1}}}\frac{\ell_{M}^{n+2}}{\text{dist}_{p_{s}}(\overline{x},Q_{m}^{M})^{n+2}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{r=j}^{M-1}\sum_{Q_{m}^{M}\subset Q_{i_{0}}^{r}\setminus{Q_{i_{0}}^{r+1}}}\frac{\ell_{M}^{n+2}}{\ell_{r}^{n+2}}(d+1)^{M-r}d^{(M-r)n}\lesssim 1+\sum_{r=j}^{M-1}\bigg[\frac{1}{d}\bigg(1+\frac{1}{d}\bigg)\bigg]^{M-r}\lesssim 1.

This finally implies

|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}b_{i}^{j}(\overline{x})|\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad|\pazocal{P}^{s,\ast}_{\mu,\varepsilon}b_{i}^{j,\ast}(\overline{x})|\lesssim 1,\qquad\forall\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\;\text{and}\;\,0<\varepsilon<\frac{\ell_{M}}{2}\;\text{uniformly}

With this, by
[
AR
, Theorem 3.5]
we would get the
L^{2}(\mu)
-boundedness of
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}
uniformly on
0<\varepsilon<\frac{\ell_{M}}{2}
. Now, applying Cotlar’s inequality (see for example
[
T3
, Theorem 2.18]
), we would deduce

\displaystyle|\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu,\varepsilon}b_{i}^{j}(\overline{x})|\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad|\pazocal{P}^{s,\ast}_{\mu,\varepsilon}b_{i}^{j,\ast}(\overline{x})|\lesssim 1,\qquad\forall\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\;\text{and}\;\,\varepsilon>0\;\text{uniformly},

and we get the
L^{2}(\mu)
-boundedness of
\pazocal{P}^{s}_{\mu}
, and the proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

Thus, in light of Theorems
4.4
and
4.6
we have finally obtained:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(\lambda_{j})_{j}
be such that
0<\lambda_{j}\leq\tau_{0}<1/d
, for every
j
, and denote by
E_{p_{s}}
its associated
s
-parabolic Cantor set as in (
2.1
). Then, there exists
C(n,s,\tau_{0})>0
such that for every generation
k
,

\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{k,p_{s}})\leq C\Bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\theta_{j,p_{s}}^{2}\Bigg)^{-1/2}.

Moreover,

C^{-1}\Bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\theta_{j,p_{s}}^{2}\Bigg)^{-1/2}\leq\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{p_{s}})\leq\Gamma_{\Theta^{s},+}(E_{p_{s}})\leq C\Bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\theta_{j,p_{s}}^{2}\Bigg)^{-1/2}.

Multishot Capacity of Networks with Restricted Adversaries
Giuseppe Cotardo, Gretchen L. Matthews, Alberto Ravagnani, Julia Shapiro

Abstract
We investigate adversarial network coding and decoding, focusing on the multishot regime and when the adversary is restricted to operate on a vulnerable region of the network. Errors can occur on a proper subset of the network edges and are modeled via an adversarial channel. The paper contains both bounds and capacity-achieving schemes for the Diamond Network, the Mirrored Diamond Network, and generalizations of these networks. We also initiate the study of the capacity of 3-level networks in the multishot setting by computing the multishot capacity of the Butterfly Network, considered in [IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, vol. 69, no. 6, 2023], which is a variant of the network introduced by Ahlswede, Cai, Li and Yeung in 2000.

Introduction

Network coding is a communication strategy where the intermediate nodes of a network are allowed to perform coding operations on the data packets they receive before sending them towards the sinks. This strategy, in contrast to simple forwarding, can improve network throughput and optimize resource utilization. Network coding was proposed in 2000 by Ahlswede, Cai, Li and Yeung in
[
1
]
and is a thriving area of research; see
[
5
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
23
]
among many others. Adversarial models have been investigated using network coding in many contexts, including Byzantine adversaries
[
10
]
and adversaries able to control some of the networks vertices
[
23
]
. Error correction in the context of linear network coding and bounds for network coding have been studied extensively; see for instance
[
5
,
6
,
15
,
24
,
25
]
.

Following
[
14
]
, we consider networks affected by adversarial noise. When the adversary is not restricted, end-to-end coding combined with network coding can be used to achieve capacity. End-to-end approaches for controlling random and coherent errors were presented in
[
11
,
21
]
, including efficient encoding and decoding schemes using rank-metric codes. In
[
8
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
, the authors have studied multishot network coding in the context where end-to-end coding combined with network coding can be used to achieve capacity.

In
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, it was shown that restricting the adversary to a proper subset of the network edges makes end-to-end communication strategies suboptimal. In this context, the concept of
network decoding
, the use partial decoding in the intermediate nodes, was introduced as an essential strategy for achieving capacity in networks with restricted adversaries. The authors demonstrated a minimal example of a network, called the diamond network, with a restricted adversary, where both the Singleton Cut-Set bound introduced in
[
14
]
(the best cut-set bound for this network) cannot be achieved, and the value of the one-shot capacity cannot be achieved using linear operations at intermediate nodes to process information before forwarding. This network requires network decoding to achieve capacity. The Mirror Diamond Network was then introduced as an extension of the Diamond Network and the capacity of this network was shown to meet the Singleton Cut-Set bound, but still forced to use non-linear network codes to achieve capacity. By limiting where the adversary can attack to a proper subset of network edges, the best known cut-set bounds for networks are not always tight. This restriction regardless of the alphabet size also destroys the ability to achieve capacity with linear network codes in combination with end-to-end coding/decoding.

In this paper, we advance the study of networks with restricted adversaries and their capacity. We focus on restricted adversaries, aligning with the framework proposed in
[
2
]
, which requires network decoding to achieve one-shot capacity. The goal of this paper is to assess the potential increase in capacity achieved by utilizing a network
multiple
times, instead of just once as in
[
2
]
. While the benefits of multiple channel uses are clear and well understood in the context of classical coding theory, for networks with restricted adversaries, these benefits have never been investigated before. The only contribution we are aware of is our preliminary work
[
7
]
, which serves as the starting point for this paper.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Sections
2
and
3
introduce the problem set up and necessary notation. Section
4
computes the capacity of the Diamond Networks and the families of networks introduced in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Section
5
establishes an upper bound on the capacity of the larger network based on that of the corresponding smaller network. The bounds in Section

are then used to compute the capacity of the butterfly network in Figure

over multiple uses. The paper ends with a conclusion given in Section
6
and an appendix containing some additional details.

Network Decoding

Throughout the paper, we let
q
be a prime power,
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements, and
d_{H}(x,y)
denote the Hamming distance between
x
and
y
, where
x
and
y
are codewords in a code
C
. In this section, we recall some definitions and notation from network decoding for the convenience of the reader and also point the reader to Appendix
A
and additional references such as
[
2
,
14
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A (single source)
network
is a 4-tuple
\mathscr{N}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},S,\mathbf{T})
where:

1.

(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
is a directed, acyclic and finite multigraph;

2.

S\in\mathscr{V}
is the
source
;

3.

\mathbb{T}\subseteq\mathscr{V}
is the set of
terminals
.

We also assume the following:

4.

|\mathbb{T}|\geq 1
and
S\notin\mathbb{T}
.

5.

The source does not have incoming edges and the terminals do not have outgoing edges.

6.

There exists a directed path from
S
to any
T\in\mathbb{T}
.

7.

For every
V\in\mathscr{V}\setminus(\{S\}\cup\mathbb{T}),
there exists a directed path from
S
to
V
and from
V
to
T
for some
T\in\mathbb{T}
.

The elements
V\in\mathscr{V}\setminus(\{S\}\cup\mathbb{T})
are called
intermediate nodes
. We denote the sets of incident edges as
\textup{in}(V)
and
\textup{out}(V)
with their cardinalities defined as the
indegree

\deg^{-}(V)
and
outdegree

\deg^{+}(V)
. Each edge of the network
\mathscr{N}
carries at most one element from an
alphabet

\mathscr{A}
, with
|\mathscr{A}|\geq 2
. The intermediate vertices
V
in the network
\mathscr{N}
receive symbols from
\mathscr{A}
over the incoming edges, process them according to a chosen set of functions, and then output the information over the outgoing edges. We model errors in transmission as presented by an omniscient adversary
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}}
who can change the symbol sent on up to
t
edges from a fixed subset
\mathscr{U}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
to any other symbol of
\mathscr{A}
. The pair
(\mathscr{N},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})
, where
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}}
is the adversary, is called an
adversarial network
.

The edges
\mathscr{E}
of a network
\mathscr{N}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},S,\mathbb{T})
can be partially ordered as follows. Given
e,e^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
, we say that
e
precedes
e^{\prime}
, denoted
e\preceq e^{\prime}
, if there exists a directed path in
\mathscr{N}
from
e
to
e^{\prime}
. Notice that the partial order
\preceq
on
\mathscr{E}
can be extended to a total order
\leq
. The total order extension satisfies the property
e\preceq e^{\prime}
implies
e\leq e^{\prime}
. We assume a fixed total order, indicated by the labeling of the edges.

Definition 3.2

(
[
14
, Definition 40]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{N}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},S,\mathbb{T})
be a network. A
network code

\mathscr{F}
for
\mathscr{N}
is a set of functions

\{\mathscr{F}_{V}\,:\,\mathscr{A}^{\textup{deg}^{-}(V)}\to\mathscr{A}^{\textup{deg}^{+}(V)}\mid V\in\mathscr{V}\setminus(\{S\}\cup\mathbb{T})\}.

The functions in
\mathscr{F}
describe how
\mathscr{N}
processes the information at each intermediate node input from the incoming edges. These functions are unique due to the choice of the total order
\leq
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\mathscr{N},\mathbb{A})
be an adversarial network with
\mathscr{N}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},S,\mathbb{T})
and let
\mathscr{U}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
be a set of edges that the adversary can corrupt. Let
\mathscr{F}
be a network code for
\mathscr{N}
and
t\geq 0
be an integer. The channel representing the transfer from
S
to
T\in\mathbb{T}
is

\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t]\,:\,\mathscr{A}^{\textup{deg}^{+}(S)}\to\mathscr{A}^{\textup{deg}^{-}(T)}.

We often write
\Omega
to represent
\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t]
. We now recall the definition of an unambiguous code.

Definition 3.4
.

An
outer

code
for a network
\mathscr{N}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E},S,\mathbb{T})
is a subset

C\subseteq\mathscr{A}^{\textup{deg}^{+}(S)}
with
|C|\geq 1
. An
unambiguous
code
C
for each channel
\Omega
is a code such that for all
x,y\in C
with
x\neq y
and for all
T\in\mathbb{T}
we have

\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\textup{out}(S)\longrightarrow\textup{in}(T),\mathscr{U},t](x)\cap\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t](y)=\emptyset.

The empty intersection guarantees that every element of
C
can be recovered by every terminal uniquely, regardless of the action the adversary takes. We note that for any unambiguous code, any subcode is also unambiguous. Next, we define the notion of one-shot capacity of an adversarial network as in
[
2
, Definition 3.18]
. Let
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}}
be the adversary for
\mathscr{N}
capable of corrupting up to
t
edges of a proper subset
\mathscr{U}\subsetneq\mathscr{E}.

Definition 3.5
.

The
one-shot
capacity of an adversarial network
(\mathscr{N},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})
over an alphabet
\mathscr{A}
, denoted
C_{1}(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})
, is the maximum
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that there exists an unambiguous code
C
for
\Omega
and a network code
\mathscr{F}
with
\alpha=\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|C|),
meaning,

C_{1}(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})=\max\left\{\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}{|C|}\,:\,C\hbox{ is an unambiguous code for each }\Omega\right\}.

An upper bound on the one-shot capacity of an adversarial network is given below. Recall that an edge cut of a graph
G
is a set of edges
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
such that
G\setminus\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is disconnected.

Theorem 3.6

(Singleton Cut-Set Bound
[
14
, Corollary 66]
)
.

Let
t\geq 0
and let
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet. Suppose that an adversary
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}}
can corrupt up to
t
edges from a subset
\mathscr{U}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
. We have that

\displaystyle C_{1}(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})\leq\min_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\min_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}\left(|\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{U}|+\max\{0,|\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\cap\mathscr{U}|-2t\}\right),

where
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
ranges over all edge cuts between the source
S

and
T
.

4
Multishot Capacity and Restricted Adversaries

Our work focuses on the multishot capacity of an adversarial network. The capacity of adversarial networks in multiple rounds is linked to the
i
-th power channel. The
i
-th power
of a channel
\Omega:\mathscr{X}\dashrightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is the channel

\Omega^{i}:\mathscr{X}^{i}\dashrightarrow\mathscr{Y}^{i}

where
\Omega^{i}:=\Omega\times\dotsm\times\Omega
is the
i
-fold product and
\Omega^{i}
models
i
uses of a network, see
[
14
, Definition 10]
. A formal definition of the multishot capacity that extends
[
2
, Definition 5]
is provided below.

Definition 4.1

(
[
7
, Definition II.7]
)
.

Let
i
be a positive integer. The
i
-shot capacity

C_{i}(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})
of an adversarial network
(\mathscr{N},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})
over an alphabet
\mathscr{A}
is the maximum
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that there exists an unambiguous code
C
for
\Omega^{i}
with
\smash{\alpha=\frac{\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|C|)}{i}}
, meaning,

C_{i}(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})=\max\left\{\alpha=\frac{\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|C|)}{i}\,:\,C\hbox{ is an unambiguous code for each }\Omega^{i}\right\}.

The multishot capacity (
i
-shot capacity) is an extension of the one-shot capacity in Definition
3.5
, where
i=1
recovers the original definition.
Intuitively, it can be thought of as the maximum number of symbols that can be sent over
i
transmission rounds without errors. Throughout the paper, we consider the following two adversarial models. In each model, we assume that the adversary can attack up to
t
edges chosen from the set of vulnerable edges. The models are the following:

A.1

The adversary attacks the same
t
edges over
i
uses of the network.

A.2

The adversary can change the
t
edges to attack over
i
uses of the network.

4.1
Multishot Capacity of the Diamond Network(s)

We start by discussing the multishot capacity of the Diamond Network
\mathscr{D}
in Scenario
A.1
and Scenario
A.2
. The capacity of these networks was computed in
[
7
]
.

Scenario
A.1
for
\mathscr{D}
.

In this scenario, the adversary can corrupt an edge in
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
of
\mathscr{D}
and cannot change the edge attacked. We start by noticing that using the strategy proposed in
[
4
, Proposition III.1]
, one can easily show that
C_{i}(\mathscr{D},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{D}})~\geq~\textup{log}_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|-1)
. In
[
7
, Section III]
, we compute the
i
-shot capacity of
\mathscr{D}
and show that the previous bound is far from being achieved. In particular, the following holds.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet and
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{D}}
be an adversary capable of corrupting an edge from the set
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
of
\mathscr{D}
. Then, the i-shot capacity of
\mathscr{D}
in Scenario
A.1
is

\textup{C}_{i}(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{D}})=\frac{\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|^{i}-1)}{i}.

An intuitive idea of why the
i
-shot capacity of the Diamond Network
\mathscr{D}
increases over multiple uses is that we can reserve a vector
(\star,\ldots,\star)\in\mathscr{A}^{i}
, an extension of the strategy provided in
[
3
, Section 3]
. It is clear that as
i
grows, the larger the capacity, asymptotically going to
1.
Thus, there is a gain in capacity over multiple uses of the Diamond Network
(\mathscr{D},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{D}})
.

Scenario
A.2
for
\mathscr{D}
.

In this scenario, the adversary
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{D}}
can attack an edge in the set
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
and can change the edge attacked each transmission round. In
[
7
, Section IV]

\mathscr{D}
is the same as the value of the one-shot capacity of
\mathscr{D}.
In particular, the following holds.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet and
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{D}}
be an adversary in Scenario
A.2
. Then, the

-shot capacity of
\mathscr{D}
in Scenario

\textup{C}_{i}(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{D}})=\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|-1)=C_{1}(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{D}}).

Hence, over
i
uses of
\mathscr{D}
, we see that there is no gain in capacity. This result follows from the fact that the adversary is free to change the edge attacked each transmission round. Therefore, the strategy provided in Scenario
A.1
cannot be applied. The best strategy we can provide for
\mathscr{D}
in Scenario
A.2
is reserving a symbol
\star
and using the strategy provided in

3
, Section 3]

i
times, which is drastically different from the result obtained in Scenario
A.1
.

Scenario
A.1
for
\mathscr{S}

In this scenario, the adversary can attack an edge in the set of edges

and cannot change the edge attacked. Using

C_{i}(\mathscr{S},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{S}})\leq 1
. In

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet and
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{S}}
be an adversary able to corrupt up to one edge of the set of outgoing edges of the source of
\mathscr{S}

\mathscr{S}

Scenario
A.2
for

It is not hard to check that, for an adversary capable of attacking up to an edge and can change the edge attacked each transmission round,

\mathscr{A}

t

\mathscr{U}_{S}

\Omega^{i}_{\mathfrak{C}_{t}}:=\Omega_{\mathfrak{C}_{t}}[\mathfrak{C}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{S},t]\times\dotsm\times\Omega_{\mathfrak{C}_{t}}[\mathfrak{C}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{S},t]

Remark 4.5

We provide a strategy for computing the size of the largest unambiguous code for

in Proposition

. Assume

With the approach that was applied
[
2
, Example 9]

components. Let

and

.

Finally, we are ready to compute the multishot capacity of

Proposition 4.6

\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet. Recall that
\smash{\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{C}_{t}}}
can corrupt up to
t

\mathscr{U}_{S}
. We have that the

-shot capacity of

Proof.

|C|\leq|\mathscr{A}|^{i}

\mathscr{F}
be a network code for
\mathfrak{C}_{t}

i

C

Scenario
A.1
for
\mathfrak{E}_{t}
.

Assume that an adversary
\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}
can attack up to
t
edges of the subset
\mathscr{U}_{s},
where
\mathscr{U}_{S}
is the set of edges incident with the source
S
, but cannot change the edges attacked each transmission round. In

2

C_{1}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})<1.

Let
B
be a set of reserved vectors in
\mathscr{A}^{2t+1}
with
|B|=b
. Let
C\subseteq\mathscr{A}^{2t+1}
be the largest unambiguous code for
\mathfrak{E_{t}}
for
\Omega[\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\textup{out}(S)\to\textup{in}(T),\mathscr{U}_{S},t]
, with

(4.1)

|C|=|\mathscr{A}|-b.

Suppose that there exists a capacity-achieving pair
(C,\mathscr{F})
. We have that

C_{1}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})=\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|-b).

The goal is to show that

C_{i}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})=\frac{\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|^{i}-b)}{i}.

We define the sets

\displaystyle B^{i}

\displaystyle:=\{\underbrace{(k\mid\dots\mid k)}_{i\textup{-times}}\,:\,k\in B\},

\displaystyle B_{1}^{i}

\displaystyle:=\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}\mid\dots\mid k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})\,:\,k\in B\},

\displaystyle B_{2}^{i}

\displaystyle:=\{(k_{t+1},\ldots,k_{2t+1}\mid\dots\mid k_{t+1},\ldots,k_{2t+1})\,:\,k\in B\},

where “
\,\mid\,
” denotes the vector concatenation. Note that
|B^{i}|=|B_{1}^{i}|=|B_{2}^{i}|=b
. Let
C\subseteq\mathscr{A}^{a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots+a_{n}}
. For a given
x\in C
, we denote by
B_{t}^{\textup{H}}(x)
the Hamming ball of radius
t
with center
x
and
S_{t}^{\textup{H}}(x)
the shell of that ball, meaning,

B_{t}^{\textup{H}}(x)=\{y\in\mathscr{A}^{a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots+a_{n}}\,:\,d_{\textup{H}}(x,y)\leq t\},S_{t}^{\textup{H}}(x)=\{y\in B_{t}^{\textup{H}}(x)\,:\,d_{\textup{H}}(x,y)=t\}.

Let
\pi_{k}^{i}:\mathscr{A}^{(2t+1)i}\to\mathscr{A}^{i}
be the projection onto the coordinates corresponding to the edges to intermediate node
V_{k}
of the simple
2
-level network
\mathscr{N}.
The following result provides a construction of the maximum unambiguous code for
\mathfrak{E}_{t}
with cardinality
|\mathscr{A}|^{i}-b
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet,
\mathscr{U}_{S}
be the set of edges incident with the source
S
and let
\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}
be an adversary able to corrupt up to
t
edges of
\mathscr{U}_{S}
of
\mathfrak{E}_{t}
. Let
b
be as in (
4.1
). Then, the
i
-shot capacity of
\mathfrak{E}_{t}
in Scenario

is

C_{i}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})=\frac{\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|^{i}-b)}{i}.

Proof.

We start with the lower bound. Our goal is to show that the code

C=\{(a\mid\dots\mid a)\in(\mathscr{A}^{i})^{2t+1}\,:\,(a_{1},\ldots,a_{(2t+1)i})\notin B^{i}\}\subseteq(\mathscr{A}^{i})^{2t+1}

is unambiguous for
\Omega^{i}[\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\textup{out}(S)\to\textup{in}(T),\mathscr{U}_{S},t]

\mathscr{F}
. Suppose that the adversary changes
t
symbols in the network. We consider two cases:

Case 1:
If
V_{1}
receives a vector of the form
(b_{1}\mid\dots\mid b_{j}\mid a\mid\dots\mid a)

j\geq t/2
, then the vertex
V_{1}
forwards a reserved symbol
k\in B_{1}^{i}
and the vertex
V_{2}
forwards the received symbol

. Therefore, terminal receives an element of

\Omega^{i}[\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{S},t](a\mid\dots\mid a)=\{(k\mid a)\,:\,a\in\mathscr{A}^{(t+1)i}\setminus B_{2}^{i},\,k\in B_{1}^{i}\}

and trusts the vector from
V_{2}
.

Case 2:
If
V_{2}
receives a vector of the form
(b_{1}\mid\dots\mid b_{l}\mid a\mid\dots\mid a)
with
l\geq t/2+1
, then the vertex
V_{1}
forwards the received symbol
a\in\mathscr{A}^{ti}\setminus B_{1}^{i}
and the vertex
V_{2}
forwards a reserved symbol
k\in B_{2}^{i}
. Therefore, terminal receives an element of

\Omega^{i}[\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{S},t](a\mid\dots\mid a)=\{(a\mid k)\,:\,a\in\mathscr{A}^{ti}\setminus B_{1}^{i},\,k\in B_{2}^{i}\}

and trusts the vector from
V_{1}
. It follows that for any
c,c^{\prime}\in C
, we have that

\Omega^{i}[\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{S},t](c)\cap\Omega^{i}[\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{S},t](c^{\prime})\neq\emptyset

. This shows that

is an unambiguous code. One can easily check that

It remains to show the upper bound. The following generalizes
[
2

to the multishot setting. Assume towards a contradiction that there exists an unambiguous code
C
for
\Omega^{i}[\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{S},t]

|C|=|\mathscr{A}|^{i}-b+1

C

\pi_{1}^{i}(C)
contains
|\mathscr{A}|^{i}-b+1

\mathscr{F}_{1}

\mathscr{F}_{1}(\pi_{1}^{i}(C))=(\mathscr{A}^{i}\setminus B^{i})\cup\{l\},

for some

. To conclude, there must exist

, with
x\neq y
and some

\mathscr{F}_{1}(\pi_{1}^{i}(x))=\mathscr{F}_{1}(\pi_{1}^{i}(e))\hskip 12.91663pt\hbox{and}\hskip 12.91663ptd_{H}(\pi_{1}^{i}(e),\pi_{1}^{i}(y))\leq ti-1.

x^{\prime}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{ti},x_{ti+1},y_{ti+2},\ldots,y_{(2t+1)i})\in B_{t}^{H}(x),

(\mathscr{F}_{1}(\pi_{1}^{i}(x^{\prime}),\mathscr{F}_{2}(\pi_{2}^{i}(x^{\prime}))=(\mathscr{F}_{1}(\pi_{1}^{i}(y^{\prime}),\mathscr{F}_{2}(\pi_{2}^{i}(y^{\prime}))\in\Omega^{i}(x)\cap\Omega^{i}(y),

C_{i}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})\leq\frac{\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|^{i}-b)}{i}.\qed

\mathfrak{E}_{t}
multiple times for communication without having to compute the one-shot capacity. This result opens the discussion of multishot capacities of adversarial networks without an explicit strategy for the one-shot capacity.
Note that if

for

and in Scenario

\mathscr{D}

for

C_{i}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})\geq\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|-b)

[
14
, Proposition 12]
. We wish to show that

C_{i}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})=\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|-b),

rendering that
C_{1}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})=C_{i}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}).

Suppose that an adversary
\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}
can corrupt up to
t
edges of
\mathfrak{E}_{t}
. The action of the adversary can be represented as the channel

\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}:\mathscr{A}^{2t+1}\dashrightarrow\mathscr{A}^{2t+1}\quad\textup{defined by}\quad\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}(x)=\{y\in\mathscr{A}^{2t+1}\,:\,d_{\textup{H}}(x,y)\leq t\}.

C_{1}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})
, the largest unambiguous code for
\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}
has cardinality

and there is no larger code (since

\smash{C_{1}(\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})=\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|-b)}
. We wish to show that there does not exist an unambiguous code

for
(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})
where

, that is,
|C|~\leq~(|\mathscr{A}|-b)^{i}
. We provide a structural property that shows

is a unambiguous code for
\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}
. We also have that

is unambiguous for
\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}^{i}
if and only if at least one of the following holds:
d_{\textup{H}}(x^{1},y^{1})\geq 2t+1,\ldots,d_{\textup{H}}(x^{i},y^{i})\geq 2t+1.
We have the following remark.

Remark 4.10
.

We provide a strategy for computing the largest unambiguous code for
\mathfrak{E}_{t}
in multiple transmission rounds. Let
C
be an unambiguous code for
\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}^{i}
where we have that
|C|=(|\mathscr{A}|-b)^{i}+1
. Define
x^{1}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2t+1}),\ldots,x^{i}=(x_{1}^{i},\ldots,x^{i}_{2t+1})
. With the approach that was applied in
[

, we claim that there are no two codewords that coincide in the first

components. Let
\smash{c_{1},\ldots,c_{(|\mathscr{A}|-b)^{i}+1}\in C}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that for
\smash{c_{1},c_{2}\in C}
,
c_{1}^{1}=c_{2}^{1}
and
c_{1}=0
. This implies that
\smash{c_{3}^{1},\ldots,c_{(|\mathscr{A}|-b)^{i}+1}^{1}}
must have Hamming weight
2t+1
. If not, assume that

has Hamming weight less than

to be an unambiguous code of Hamming distance at least
2t+1
and of cardinality

. Since

have Hamming weight

. We assume that

is unambiguous for
\textup{H}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{i}
, therefore, one of

with cardinality

.

The next proposition computes the multishot capacity
C_{i}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})
in Scenario
A.2
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet,
\mathscr{U}_{S}
be the set of sources connected to the source

, and
\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}
be an adversary able to corrupt up to

\mathscr{U}_{S}
of

i

\mathfrak{E}_{t}

C_{i}(\mathfrak{E}_{t},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}})=\log_{|\mathscr{A}|}(|\mathscr{A}|-b).

Proof.

Assume that we use a
2t+1

in each round. We first show that
|C|\leq(|\mathscr{A}|-b)^{i}

\mathbb{A}_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}

\mathfrak{E}_{t}

i

\mathfrak{E}_{t}

.

The lower bound immediately follows from

14
, Proposition 12]
using the

\Omega_{\mathfrak{E}_{t}}^{i}.

∎

The above proposition shows that there is no gain in using

Remark 4.12

5
Multishot Double Cut-Set Bound

In
[
2
]
, a reduction from
3
-level networks to
2
-level networks was provided, bounding the capacity of a
3
-level network by the
2
-level network associated with it. In this section, we extend this reduction to the multishot regime, bounding the multishot capacity of simple
3
-level networks to the simple
2
-level networks associated to them. We also provide a multishot version of the Double Cut-Set Bound
[
2
, Theorem 8.6]
and a multishot version of the Singleton Cut-Set Bound
[
14
, Corollary 66]
. Using the procedure described in
[
2
, Section V]
, we will be able to upper bound the multishot capacity of an arbitrary network
\mathscr{N}
with that of an induced simple
2
-level network.

We start by describing the transfer of information from
\mathscr{E}_{1}
to
\mathscr{E}_{2}
, where
\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2}
are edge cuts. We say edge cut
\mathscr{E}_{1}
precedes edge cut
\mathscr{E}_{2}
, denoted
\mathscr{E}_{1}\preccurlyeq\mathscr{E}_{2}
, if there exists a directed path in
\mathscr{N}
from
\mathscr{E}_{1}
to
\mathscr{E}_{2}
. Let
\mathscr{N}
be a network,
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and
\mathscr{E}_{2}
be edge cuts such that
\mathscr{E}_{1}\preccurlyeq\mathscr{E}_{2}
. Let
\mathscr{A}
be a network alphabet and
\mathscr{F}
a network code for
(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A})
,
\mathscr{U}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
be a set of edges, and
t\geq 0
. Then we denote by

(5.1)

\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]:\mathscr{A}^{|\mathscr{E}_{1}|}\dashrightarrow\mathscr{A}^{|\mathscr{E}_{2}|}

the channel that describes the transfer from the edges of
\mathscr{E}_{1}
to those of
\mathscr{E}_{2}
, when an adversary can corrupt up to
t
edges from
\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1}
. This notation allows us to focus on the transfer of information between the two edge cuts
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and
\mathscr{E}_{2}
, instead of looking at the transfer of information from the source
S
to the set of terminals
\mathbb{T}.
We begin with the following proposition that extends
[
2
, Theorem 5.8]
to the multishot setting.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{N}_{3}
be a simple
3
-level network, and let
\mathscr{N}_{2}
be the simple
2
-level network associated to it. Define
\mathscr{U}_{3}
and
\mathscr{U}_{2}
to be the set of edges incident with the sources of
\mathscr{N}_{3}
and
\mathscr{N}_{2}
and define
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}_{3}},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}_{2}}
as adversaries able to corrupt up to
t
edges of
\mathscr{U}_{3}
and
\mathscr{U}_{2}
respectively. Then for all alphabets
\mathscr{A}
,

(5.2)

C_{i}(\mathscr{N}_{3},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}_{3}})\leq C_{i}(\mathscr{N}_{2},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}_{2}}).

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{F}_{3}
be a network code and
C_{3}
be an outer code for an alphabet
\mathscr{A}
and the simple
3
-level network
\mathscr{N}_{3}
which is unambiguous for
\Omega[\mathscr{N}_{3},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}_{3},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{3},t]
, for all
T\in\mathbb{T}
. We note that the same network code is used each transmission round. Let
C_{3}^{\prime}
be an unambiguous code for the channel
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N}_{3},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}_{3},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{3},t]
, where
\Omega^{i}
is the channel associated to
i
uses of
\mathscr{N}_{3}
. We wish to show that there exists
\mathscr{F}_{2}
such that
C_{3}^{\prime}
is unambiguous for
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N}_{2},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}_{2},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{2},t],
for all
T\in\mathbb{T}.
Let
\smash{G^{3,2},V_{ij}^{3},\mathscr{F}_{V_{ijk}^{3}}}
, the neighborhood of
V_{ij}^{3}
,
\mathscr{F}_{2}
and
\mathscr{F}_{V_{i}}
be defined as in
[
2
, Theorem 5.8]
. We have that each intermediate node
V_{i}
in
\mathscr{N}_{2}
corresponds to connected component
i
of
G^{3,2}
and is a composition of functions at nodes in
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
of
\mathscr{N}_{3}
. It was shown that the fan out set
\Omega(x)
of any
x\in C_{3}
over the channel
\Omega[\mathscr{N}_{3},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}_{3},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{3},t]
is exactly equal to the fan-out set
\Omega(x)
of

over
\Omega[\mathscr{N}_{2},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}_{2},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{2},t]
. Therefore, it is easy to check that this is the case for
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N}_{3},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}_{3},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{3},t]
and
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N}_{2},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}_{2},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{2},t]
, by definition of
\Omega^{i}
. We can conclude that
C_{3}^{\prime}
is an unambiguous code for the channel
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N}_{2},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}_{2},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U}_{2},t]
. Therefore, we have that

C_{i}(\mathscr{N}_{3},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}_{3}})\leq C_{i}(\mathscr{N}_{2},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}_{2}}),

as desired.
∎

The earlier proposition indicates that by understanding the relationship between a simple
2
-level network and a simple 3-level network, we can determine the multishot capacity of a larger network by calculating the multishot capacity of a smaller one. Before proving the main results, we have the following remark.

Remark 5.2
.

Note that as in
[
2
]
, we do not require the edge cuts
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and
\mathscr{E}_{2}
to be minimal or even
antichain
cuts (i.e., cuts where any two different edges cannot be compared with respect to the order
\preccurlyeq
). Furthermore,
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]
inherits from the channel
\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]
the property that the predecessors are considered first in the network topology. Therefore,
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]

provides the value of each edge of the edge cut
\mathscr{E}_{2}
as a
function
of the values of the immediate predecessors in
\mathscr{E}_{1}
.

We now illustrate and extend the results on
2
-level and
3
-level networks derived in
[
2
, Section 8]
and discuss how they can be combined and applied to study the multishot capacity of an arbitrarily large and complex network
\mathscr{N}
. The following result generalizes the Double Cut-Set Bound
[
2
, Theorem 8.6]
to the multishot scenario.

Theorem 5.3

(The Multishot Double Cut-Set Bound)
.

Let
\mathscr{N}
be a network,
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet and
\mathscr{U}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
be a set of edges. Let
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and
\mathscr{E}_{2}
be edge cuts such that
\mathscr{E}_{1}\preccurlyeq\mathscr{E}_{2}
, let
T\in\mathbb{T}
and let
\mathscr{F}
be a network code. Then, the one-shot capacity of
\mathscr{N}
satisfies

C_{1}(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t])\leq\max_{\mathscr{F}}C_{1}(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U},t])

with the maximum taken over all network codes
\mathscr{F}
for
(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a network code for
(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A})
. Using a similar approach as in
[
2
, Theorem 8.6]
, consider the scenario where an adversary
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}}
can corrupt up to
t
edges of
\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1}
. This scenario is modeled by the channel

\Omega^{*}:=\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\textup{out}(S)\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{U}\cap\textup{out}(S),0]\RHD\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]\RHD

\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{2}\longrightarrow\textup{in}(T),\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{2},0].

We are interested in
i
uses for the channel, therefore we now consider the power channel
(\Omega^{*})^{i}.
Using
[
14
, Proposition 18]
, we have

(\Omega^{*})^{i}=\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\textup{out}(S)\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{U}\cap\textup{out}(S),0])\blacktriangleright\\
(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t])\blacktriangleright\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{2}\longrightarrow\textup{in}(T),\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{2},0]).

We notice that the former and latter channels are deterministic (see
[
2
, Definition 3.1]
) since we consider an adversarial power of 0. These channels describe the
transfer from the source to
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and from
\mathscr{E}_{2}
to
T
, respectively. For ease of notation, we let

\Omega^{i}_{1}:=\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\textup{out}(S)\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{U}\cap\textup{out}(S),0]

and

\Omega^{i}_{2}:=\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{2}\longrightarrow\textup{in}(T),\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{2},0].

We note that by Definition
A.1
, the channel
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t]
is coarser than the channel
\Omega_{1}^{i}\blacktriangleright\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]\blacktriangleright\Omega^{i}_{2}
since in the latter channel, the errors can only occur on
\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1}
, a subset of
\mathscr{U}
. By
[
2
, Proposition 3.6]
and
[
2
, Proposition 3.9]
, we have that

\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t])\geq\Omega_{1}^{i}\blacktriangleright\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]\blacktriangleright\Omega^{i}_{2}

and

C_{1}(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t]))\leq C_{1}(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]).

Since we chose
\mathscr{F}
arbitrarily, this applies for the maximum
\mathscr{F}
, we see that

C_{1}(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t]))\leq\max_{\mathscr{F}}C_{1}(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t])

as desired.
∎

By taking
\mathscr{E}_{1}=\mathscr{E}_{2}
, we recover a multishot version of
[
14
, Corollary 66]
as a corollary of Theorem
5.3
.

Corollary 5.4

(Multishot Cut-Set Bound)
.

Let
\mathscr{N}
be a network, let
\mathscr{A}
be an alphabet, let
T\in\mathbb{T}
and let
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
be an edge cut. Let
\mathscr{U}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
be a set of edges and let
\mathscr{F}
be a network code. We have that

\displaystyle C_{1}(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},S\longrightarrow T,\mathscr{U},t])\leq\max_{\mathscr{F}}C_{1}(\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}^{\prime},t]),

where the maximum is taken over all network codes
\mathscr{F}
for
(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A}).

Notice that the adversary
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}}
is restricted to the same proper subset of edges
\mathscr{U}
for each uses of the network, but there is no restriction on changing the edges attacked inside of
\mathscr{U}.
The case where
\mathscr{U}
changes each transmission round remains open. Let
\mathscr{N}
be a network and let
\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
be edge cuts such that
\mathscr{E}_{1}
precedes
\mathscr{E}_{2}
. For some
e\in\mathscr{E}_{2}
and
e^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}_{1}
, we can say that
e^{\prime}
is an
immediate predecessor
of
e
in
\mathscr{E}_{1}
if
e^{\prime}\preccurlyeq e
and there does not exist
e^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{E}_{1}
with
e^{\prime}\preccurlyeq e^{\prime\prime}\preccurlyeq e
and
e^{\prime}\neq e^{\prime\prime}
. As in
[
2
]
, the vertices of
V_{1}
are in bijection with the edges of
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and the vertices of
V_{2}
with the edges of
\mathscr{E}_{2}
. We say that a vertex
V\in V_{1}
is connected to vertex
V^{\prime}\in V_{2}
if and only if the edge of
\mathscr{E}_{1}
corresponding to
V
is an immediate predecessor of the edge of
\mathscr{E}_{2}
corresponding to
V^{\prime}
. Let
\mathscr{E}_{S}^{\prime}
be the set of edges directly connected with the source of
\mathscr{N}^{\prime}
, which can be identified with the edges of
\mathscr{E}_{1}
(consistent with how we identified these with the vertices in
V_{1}
). The next result is a consequence of Theorem
5.3
that extends
[
2
, Corollary 8.7]
to the multishot setting and allows for an easier application of the theorem. The proof is inspired by the one of
[
2
, Corollary 8.7]
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\mathscr{N}
be a network,
\mathscr{A}
a network alphabet,
\mathscr{U}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
a set of edges and

.
Let
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and
\mathscr{E}_{2}
be edge cuts between

and

such that
\mathscr{E}_{1}
precedes
\mathscr{E}_{2}
and

be as defined above. Let
\mathscr{N}^{\prime}
be a simple
3
-level network. Let
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}}
be the adversary able to corrupt up to
t
edges of
\mathscr{N}
from
\mathscr{U}
and let
\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}^{\prime}}
be the adversary able to corrupt
t
edges of
\mathscr{N}^{\prime}
from
\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{S}
. Then,

(5.3)

\textup{C}_{i}(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}})\leq\textup{C}_{i}(\mathscr{N}^{\prime},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}^{\prime}}).

Proof.

We wish to show that

\textup{C}_{i}(\Omega[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t])\leq\textup{C}_{i}(\mathscr{N}^{\prime},\mathscr{A},\mathbb{A}_{\mathscr{N}^{\prime}})

for every network code
\mathscr{F}
for the pair
(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A})
, where
\mathscr{F}
is the same for all

transmission rounds. This in turn establishes the corollary due to Proposition
5.3
.
Let
\mathscr{F}
be a network code and let
\Omega^{i}:=\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},0]
.
By the Remark
5.2
, we know that the channel
\Omega^{i}

expresses the value of each edge

as a function of the values of its immediate predecessors in
\mathscr{E}_{1}
.
By the construction of
\mathscr{N}^{\prime}
, we can find a network code
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
(that depends on
\mathscr{F}
) for
(\mathscr{N}^{\prime},\mathscr{A})
such that

(5.4)

\Omega^{i}=\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N}^{\prime},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F}^{\prime},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{S}\longrightarrow\textup{in}(T),\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{S},0],

where the edges of
\mathscr{E}_{1}
and
\mathscr{E}_{2}
are identified with those of
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{S}
and
\textup{in}(T)
in
\mathscr{N}^{\prime}
. We observe that the channel
\Omega^{i}[\mathscr{N},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{E}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{2},\mathscr{U}\cap\mathscr{E}_{1},t]
can be written as the concatenation

(5.5)

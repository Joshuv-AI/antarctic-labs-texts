ABSTRACT
Key Points•NSD2-LDD selectively degrades NSD2, reversing the oncogenic program of t(4;14) MM and validating NSD2 as a therapeutic target.•NSD2-LDD reduces supportive bone marrow microenvironment interactions in vivo, controlling disease and extending survival.

Introduction

The current standard of care for multiple myeloma (MM) includes quadruplet combinations of chemotherapeutic, immunomodulatory drugs, and proteasomal agents, bridging autologous stem cell transplantation with extended maintenance.1, 2, 3 Despite recent advances across novel therapeutic modalities, MM remains an incurable disease, with a 5-year survival rate of 60%.4 Minimal residual disease after cessation of treatment strongly predicts relapse5, 6, 7 and is driven in part by protective cues from the bone marrow microenvironment (BME).8, 9, 10 Prosurvival autocrine signals, tumor-stromal contact, and immune exclusion, together with tumor-intrinsic transcriptional changes in metabolism and proteostasis, confer resistance in high-risk relapsed disease.11, 12, 13, 14 Although previous efforts to target BME dependencies yielded mixed responses in the relapsed/refractory setting,15,16 they underscore the need for more selective strategies.

Among founding genomic lesions with poor prognosis, t(4;14) drives the overexpression of the histone methyltransferase NSD2 (also known as MMSET II/WHSC1).17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22 Approximately 15% of newly diagnosed patients harbor t(4;14) variable fusion breakpoint within the 5′ coding sequence, which can lead to N-terminal truncated transcripts of NSD2, such as MB4-1 (long-form; 8%) and MB4-2/3 (short-form; 4%), with the latter associated with loss of the PWWP1 nucleosome-binding domain. In newly diagnosed patients treated with triplet standard-of-care regimen (RVd [Revlimid (lenalidomide), Velcade (bortezomib), dexamethasone]), the late-breakpoint truncations that occur after NSD2 translation initiation are associated with poorer outcomes.23 As demonstrated in many tumor types (including t(4;14) MM), NSD2-dependent H3K36 methylation has been broadly implicated in transcriptional programs, including epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), migration, extracellular matrix remodeling, and cytoskeletal organization.24,25 Overall, NSD2 is an attractive therapeutic entry point to address BME-driven malignancies such as t(4;14) MM.23

Herein we report a structurally enabled, PWWP1-directed, cereblon (CRBN)–mediated NSD2 degrader, NSD2–ligand-directed degrader (NSD2-LDD), that eliminates both full-length (FL) NSD2/MMSET-II and MMSET-I isoforms. As an agent to explore the epigenetic basis underscoring malignant transformation in t(4;14) MM, NSD2-LDD induces potent, durable NSD2 depletion in PWWP1 domain–containing t(4;14) models, reduces H3K36me2 with reciprocal H3K27me3 spreading, downregulates BME interaction proteins, and disrupts 3D growth. The mechanistic impact of this phenotypic response is replicated in vivo, where NSD2-LDD treatment alters the supportive BME niche, exposing and potentially sensitizing malignant plasma cells to further therapeutic agent combination strategies.

Methods

Cell culture

Refer to supplemental Table 3, available on the Blood website, for the full descriptions of cell line profiles, including t(4;14) status, NSD2 expression, and evaluations performed in this study. Cell lines were maintained in standard mammalian aseptic cell culture conditions at 37°C and 5% carbon dioxide, and cultured in RPMI 1640 supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (Gibco), 10 U/mL penicillin, and 10 µg/mL streptomycin. Luciferase-expressing KMS34 cells (JCRB1195; Research Resource Identifier: CVCL_2996) were constructed using lentiviral integration of RediFect Red-FLuc-GFP cassette following the manufacturer’s instructions (CLS960003; Perkin-Elmer). A KMS34 CRBN−/− model was generated internally using RNP transfection with dual-guide RNAs (CAGGACGCTGCGCACAACAT; CGCACCATACTGACTTCTTG). KMS11-PAR and KMS11-TKO were a gift from Jonathan Licht at the University of Florida Health Cancer Institute, Gainesville, FL.25 All cell lines were authenticated and confirmed negative for the presence of mycoplasma before use. For all other experimental details refer to the supplemental Material and methods.

Results

NSD2 upregulation in t(4;14) MM activates EMT/BME programs

Integrative analyses of previously published patient gene expression and chromatin accessibility data emphasized that t(4;14) MM is marked by elevated cis-regulatory element (CRE) accessibility (Figure 1A), which coincides with the activation of the pathways for adhesion, EMT, and stromal signaling (Figure 1B).26 Similarly, reanalysis of single-cell patient profiles published by the Multiple Myeloma Research Foundation revealed enhanced cell-cell interactions in t(4;14) marrow (1361 vs 518), with an overrepresentation of interacting malignant cells (520 vs 66; Figure 1C). Our own chromatin state analyses on a panel of myeloma cell lines delineated MM CRE classes exhibiting characteristic epigenetic markers (Figure 1D; supplemental Figure 1A-C),27 with t(4;14) models showing an increased proportion of active enhancers (∼31% vs ∼25%) and fewer polycomb-repressed elements (∼12% vs ∼23%; Figure 1E), consistent with global H3K36me2/H3K27me3 trends across MM (supplemental Figure 1D; supplemental Figure 2A-B).26 Furthermore, distal enhancer activation was accompanied by increased looping to key genes, such as those involved in adhesion (Figure 1F; supplemental Figure 2C). Discovery of PWWP1 domain-binder conjugated to CRBN-targeting species as a driver of NSD2 degradation

A surface plasmon resonance–based fragment screen for the NSD2 PWWP1 domain identified screening hit compound 1, with a PWWP1 binding 50% inhibitory concentration (IC50) of 45.6μM (Figure 2A). Optimization led to the identification of compound 2, with a 12-fold improvement in binding potency (3.8μM). A protein cocrystal structure of compound 2 in the PWWP1 domain of NSD2 revealed that the binding affinity of the compound is mediated by key hydrogen bond interactions with Ala332 and Gln321 and the morpholinone carbonyl and NH of compound 2, respectively (Figure 2B; supplemental Figure 3A). In addition, π-electronic interactions of the dichloroaryl ring with Phe266, Tyr233, and Trp236, the 3 amino acids that comprise the aromatic cage that normally binds charged methyl-lysine histone tails, are key to affinity. Compound 2 filled the hydrophobic binding pocket, leaving little room for additional substitution within the pocket. Further substitution of the aryl ring at the solvent exposed ortho- or para-positions and possible substitution on the C5 position of the morpholinone ring were explored. These efforts resulted in the identification of compound 3, with a 200nM PWWP1 binding potency (Figure 2A). Docking of compound 3 in the compound 2 crystal structure identified the C5-methyl as a possible exit trajectory to explore as a handle for linking the PWWP1 binder to a CRBN-binding moiety (CBM) to enable NSD2 degradation (Figure 2C). NSD2-LDD-CRBN-PWWP1 ternary complex formation results in ubiquitination and proteasomal dependent degradation of NSD2

After demonstrating cellular target engagement with compound 3, we aimed to identify an LDD capable of recruiting NSD2 via the PWWP1 domain to CRBN for ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation, as illustrated in Figure 2E. Elaboration of the C5-methyl position of compound 3 with a piperazine-containing spirocyclic linker and a dihydrouracil-derived CBM afforded NSD2-LDD (4) (Figure 2F). NSD2-LDD (4) maintained potent PWWP1 binding with IC50 of 106nM and exhibited CRBN binding IC50 of 95nM. In vitro ubiquitination studies showed that NSD2-LDD (4) can facilitate polyubiquitination of both the PWWP1 domain and FL NSD2, whereas, as illustrated in Figure 2G, target-binding moiety compound 3 alone does not afford ubiquitination of NSD2 PWWP1 and competitively inhibits polyubiquitination of both PWWP1 domain and FL NSD2 in a dose-dependent manner (Figure 2H-J). Using cryo–electron microscopy, an experimental ternary structure of NSD2 PWWP1, NSD2-LDD (4), and CRBN was obtained at 3.55 Å resolution (Figure 2K; supplemental Figure 3B). The target-binding moiety portion of NSD2-LDD (4) aligns well with previous PWWP1-bound x-ray cocrystal structures and maintains the backbone hydrogen bond with Ala332, hydrogen bond with Gln321, and π-electronic interactions between Phe266, Tyr233, and Trp236. The dihydrouracil CBM exhibits the expected CRBN interactions with hydrogen bonds to both His378 and Trp380 of the tritryptophan CRBN pocket. Additional stabilizing interactions are provided by the linker, including hydrogen bonds His357, Asn351, and Gln390 on CRBN, as well as a putative salt bridge between the monoprotonated form of pyrazine and Glu272 of NSD2 PWWP1. There is relatively low surface contact area between CRBN and NSD2 PWWP1 of ∼700 Å2 (supplemental Figure 3A-B), indicating little potential for cooperative interactions, leading to ternary complex formation. This is reinforced by the small number of direct NSD2 PWWP1–CRBN interactions observed within the ternary complex: a hydrogen bond between the backbone carbonyl of Ser231 and backbone carbonyl of Lys391, a hydrogen bond between Tyr233 and Gln390, a potential hydrogen bond between the backbone carbonyl Ser231 and Asn369, and hydrophobic packing of Ala305 against Ile393.

NSD2-LDD is a selective and highly potent degrader of both MMSET I and MMSET II isoforms

To evaluate the degradation activity of NSD2-LDD on endogenous NSD2 in t(4;14) models, KMS34 and KMS11 were treated for 24 hours, and protein levels were measured by Meso Scale Discovery electrochemiluminescence assay (KMS34: 50% effective concentration, 8nM; maximal degradation, 9% (protein remaining); KMS11: 50% effective concentration, 8nM; maximal degradation, 3% (protein remaining); data not shown for clarity), whereby elimination of both FL and N-terminal isoforms was confirmed via immunoblot (Figure 3A). Furthermore, MMSET-I and II isoform degradation was confirmed to be dependent on CRBN binding using both Meso Scale Discovery and immunoblot analysis following CBM-4077 competition29 and NSD2-LDD treatments in KMS34CRBN+/+ and KMS34CRBN−/− models (Figure 3B-C). Efforts to demonstrate in vitro dependency on PWWP1 through mutation of key NSD2-LDD interaction residues were prevented by the loss of nucleosome binding (supplemental Figure 3C-E). A single 1μM treatment of NSD2-LDD in KMS34 triggered >95% degradation of all isoforms within 4 hours that was maintained for >120 hours with no additional neosubstrate activity (Figure 3D-E), reflecting high compound stability (supplemental Figure 4A). Global proteomics after 24 hours identified NSD2 as the only protein in KMS11/KMS34 (MB4-1) downregulated by NSD2-LDD, where selectivity for PWWP1 domain was confirmed in OPM2 (MB4-3, lacking PWWP1), with no impact observed on NSD2 protein levels (Figure 3F-G). NSD2 degradation leads to promoter-specific changes to H3K27me3 distribution and drives the mechanistic cascade of NSD2-LDD

Quantitative histone mass spectrometry showed robust H3K36me2 and H3K36me3 loss with concomitant increase in antagonistic H3K27me2/3 (Figure 4A-B) upon NSD2-LDD treatment. Similarly, we observed a preferential loss of transcription, a reduction in chromatin accessibility, and a shift between euchromatic and heterochromatic compartments (Figure 4C-E) jointly affecting the same pathways (eg, cell adhesion) and regulatory elements (eg, RUNX-target enhancers) aberrantly activated in the context of patients with t(4;14) rearrangements (Figure 1A; supplemental Figure 5A-H). Locally, H3K36me2 contraction was met with H3K27me3 spreading after NSD2-LDD treatment, resembling cells with normal NSD2 levels (Figure 4F; supplemental Figure 6A-B). Globally, treatment-induced H3K36me2 loss was enriched within intergenic regions, owing to compensatory SETD2 activity within genes (Figure 4G; supplemental Figure 6C). In contrast, H3K27me3 expanded from CpG-rich reservoirs to invade previously H3K36me2-enriched regions (supplemental Figure 6D-G). As a result of H3K27me3 dilution from polycomb nucleation sites, long-range polycomb interactions were also reduced, leading to consistent derepression and affecting B-cell developmental pathways (Figure 4H-J). Combining chromatin state analysis with 3D genome profiling, enhancer/repressors were paired with promoters of genes responsive to NSD2-LDD treatment to define their cis-regulatory circuitry (Figure 5A-B). Loss of promoter H3K27me3 predicted derepression (supplemental Figure 7A), whereas loss of enhancer H3K36me2 (supplemental Figure S7B) predicted reinstated silencing (Figure 5C), with supplementary contribution from 3D genome reorganization and associated change in CRE long-range interactions. These dynamics are exemplified at representative loci (Figure 5D), where we observed both the reduction of H3K36me2 and the spreading of H3K27me3, leading to restoration of proper heterochromatic nuclear compartment organization. Phenotypic response to NSD2 degradation reflects antitumor mechanism derived from tumor microenvironment detachment and EMT reversal

To evaluate the functional impact of the chromosomal and transcriptional changes described in Figures 4 and 5, PWWP1-containing models were treated with NSD2-LDD and subjected to a suite of phenotypic assays. Several key signature genes defined by an enhancer-based loss of H3K36me2 and altered global expression were followed up to 14 days after 1μM NSD2-LDD treatment and exhibited time-dependent downregulation (Figure 6A-B; supplemental Figure 8A). This included key transcripts, MET (hepatocyte growth factor receptor) and JAM-2 (cell-cell junction adherence protein 2), that were downregulated by NSD2-LDD treatment in a dose-dependent manner after 10 days (Figure 6C). Similarly, the surface expression of integrin A2/CD49b was shown to be downregulated by NSD2-LDD treatment, in addition to a reduction in CD44/hyaluronic acid receptor (Figure 6D-E). Downstream changes in the surface expression of c-MET in KMS34 were used to demonstrate CRBN dependence of NSD2-LDD, whereby c-MET was unaffected by NSD2-LDD treatment in the presence of 10μM CBM-4077 (Figure 6F). Despite the significant changes to epigenetic and transcriptomic states described in Figures 4 and 5, both KMS11 and KMS34 t(4;14) MM models failed to exhibit 2D antiproliferative responses to NSD2-LDD treatment (Figure 6G). Interestingly, long-term proliferation assessments on RCH-ACV, a constitutive active NSD2-E1099K B-ALL model, displayed more potent antiproliferative effects (Figure 6G; right panel). As previously reported, the impact of NSD2 perturbation on 3D growth was significantly more acute.24,25 Quantification of methylcellulose colony formation and 3D soft-agar assay (Figure 6H; supplemental Figure 8B) after 14 days of NSD2-LDD pretreatment demonstrated a dose-dependent decrease in KMS11 and KMS34 colony growth. The fibronectin-based adherence phenotype of KMS11 was evaluated after 14-day pretreatment with NSD2-LDD and it showed a significant perturbation, reducing adherence to levels observed in isogenic NSD2-depleted model KMS11-TKO (Figure 6I; supplemental Figure 8C). To model cell-cell and paracrine interactions within the BME, t(4;14) MM cells were incubated with HS-5 bone marrow stromal layer. Ten-day NSD2-LDD pretreatment of KMS11 led to a significant loss of HS-5 interleukin-6 secretion after 72 hours coculture (Figure 6J). To evaluate whether NSD2 degradation–dependent loss of cell adhesion may affect cell adhesion–mediated drug resistance, NSD2-LDD–pretreated KMS11 were cocultured with HS-5 and exposed to 2nM bortezomib for 72 hours. The reduction in viable KMS11 cells demonstrated that the bortezomib resistance afforded by the stromal cell interaction was lost upon NSD2 degradation (Figure 6K). In vivo degradation of NSD2 drives tumor control in both subcutaneous and intrafemoral cell-derived xenograft models and is associated with a reduction of metastatic disease

The impact of NSD2 degradation–dependent epigenetic and phenotypic changes on in vivo tumor growth was evaluated. To optimize dose selection, the plasma exposure was measured after a single 60 mg/kg intraperitoneal administration whereby a significant loss of total plasma exposure was observed within 24 hours (supplemental Figure 9A). Therefore, the impact of daily and twice daily 60 mg/kg doses on tumor growth was evaluated. NSD2-LDD was administered via intraperitoneal injection to humanized CRBN-expressing NSG mice harboring 200 mm3 subcutaneous KMS34-Luc tumors. After 12 days, tumor size was mildly (<34.0%), but not significantly, affected relative to vehicle in both treatment groups (Figure 7A), and no change in body weight was observed (supplemental Figure 9B). Protein analyses of tumor tissue at the end of the study showed significant NSD2 degradation, maximally maintained under twice daily dosing (supplemental Figure 9C). To confirm the dose-dependent effects of NSD2-LDD on epigenetic marks observed in vitro, tumor lysates collected after 12 days of daily dosing with 30 mg/kg or 60 mg/kg NSD2-LDD were analyzed by immunoblot (Figure 7B; supplemental Figure 9D-E). This analysis showed that NSD2 degradation is coincident with decreases in H3K36me2 and dose-dependent increases in H3K27me3. To more accurately replicate the plasma cell–bone marrow niche, an intrafemoral engraftment model was established, whereby KMS34-Luc were injected directly into the femurs of hCRBN NSG mice. Administration of NSD2-LDD disrupts interaction between MM cells and the stromal network in NSD2-overexpressing MM GEMM

To further elucidate the in vivo biology of NSD2 and the impact of its degradation, we extended our studies into NSD2-BIcγ1 mice, described by Larrayoz et al.30 This genetically engineered mouse model (GEMM) of MM is characterized by an overexpression of human NSD2 mimicking t(4;14) translocation (MB4-1) which induces the progressive development of MM in the bone marrow and disease-derived morbidity within 11 months of age. In this study, once signs of MM were detected at ⁓8 months, mice were administered twice daily with 60 mg/kg NSD2-LDD or vehicle control for 8 weeks, after which treatment was withdrawn, and cohorts were monitored for an additional 8 weeks (Figure 7G). NSD2-LDD–mediated degradation of systemic mouse MMSET I and II isoforms was confirmed in splenocytes after 60 mg/kg twice daily dosing in C57BL/6 parent strain (supplemental Figure 9I). Intratumoral NSD2 degradation in NSD2-BIcγ1 mice was evaluated ex vivo using bone marrow aspirates and exhibited a dose-dependent activity similar to the human RPMI-8226 cell line, expressing comparative levels of NSD2 as detected by immunoblot (supplemental Figure 9K). Serum samples were collected biweekly during the study, and the fold change in immunoglobulin G (IgG) fraction levels relative to baseline was measured as a surrogate marker for MM cell burden. Mice treated with vehicle exhibited a gradual increase in soluble IgG levels, with a median 1.5-fold increase compared with baseline. In contrast, mice treated with NSD2-LDD maintained stable IgG levels throughout the treatment period, indicating attenuated tumor growth relative to vehicle-treated mice (Figure 7H; supplemental Figure 9J). Moreover, analyses of peripheral blood samples showed a subtle increase in the frequency of enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP)–expressing NSD2-BIcγ1 MM cells after NSD2-LDD treatment relative to vehicle (Figure 7I). Four weeks after removal of the therapeutic agent, serum IgG fraction trended back toward vehicle levels in the animals remaining in the study. At the conclusion of the treatment period, randomized mice (n = 6 for vehicle, n = 5 for NSD2-LDD) were sacrificed and both femurs and tibias were harvested and fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin and embedded in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue blocks. These tissues were then subjected to single-cell spatial transcriptomics using the Xenium in situ platform, allowing us to probe the spatial expression patterns of key genes at single-cell resolution within the BME. Using a customized 478-plex panel, 29 distinct cell types were identified, including multiple macrophage subsets, neutrophils, stromal cells, skeletal muscle cells, and enhanced green fluorescent protein–positive Sdc1/CD138+Pax5− malignant plasma cells (Figure 7J-K). Although underpowered owing to the targeted gene panel size, downregulation of genes such as MET and disease-associated cytokine Ccl3/Mip-1α in the myeloma cells of treated mice was observed, in alignment with observations from human myeloma cell lines, albeit with elevated variability among animals (supplemental Figure 10A). Spatial mapping of these distinct cell populations within the bone marrow revealed their precise localization and density (Figure 7L; supplemental Figure 10B-C). Our analysis showed a significant reduction in MM cell abundance in mice receiving NSD2-LDD treatment in comparison with vehicle (88.8% reduction; adjusted P < .05; Figure 7M). Similarly, comparative analyses of MM cell centrality revealed a significant reduction in the nodal nature of the plasma cell neighborhoods. This loss of “node tightness” was accompanied by significant increases in the mean distance from MM cells to cell populations that were likely to support MM survival, such as bone marrow–associated fibroblasts and classical macrophages, controlling for the total number of MM cells per sample. These results underscore the mechanism of action and efficacy of NSD2-LDD, leading to the disruption of critical MM cell interactions that potentially provide proliferative support and confer drug resistance in MM.

Discussion

Overexpression of NSD2 in patients with t(4;14) MM leads to a global imbalance of H3K36me2 across the epigenetic landscape, increasing the transcriptional potential in a large segment of the genome. Interestingly, despite this global influence, our data demonstrate that <600 genes are differentially abundant between t(4;14) and non-t(4;14) patients with MM. The t(4;14) transcriptional signature is selectively associated with functions such as paracrine signaling and physical cell-cell interactions, which suggest that the mechanism of malignant plasma cell transformation after t(4;14) translocation is related to the interactions within the BME and surrounding stromal cells that subsequently support disease progression and therapeutic resistance to standard-of-care regimen.

The development of a highly selective PWWP1-directed degrader targeting both the MMSETI and II isoforms of NSD2 allows for a robust interrogation of t(4;14)-driven biology in preclinical models. Herein we discovered and used a novel, potent, and selective NSD2-LDD heterobifunctional compound to confirm that the transcriptomic signature associated with t(4;14) clinical data is reproduced in PWWP1 domain–containing MM cell line models, such that common pathways related to cell-cell adhesion, EMT phenotype, and cell cycle dysregulation are influenced by NSD2 degradation. Although not strongly antiproliferative in a 2D format, NSD2 degradation has a significant impact on 3D growth, as observed through colony formation and cell adhesion assays, a common feature of prior genetic studies.24,31 However, unlike historical data, using a highly selective NSD2-LDD degrader allows for deeper analyses of the epigenetic landscape compared with genetic approaches. Comprehensive multiomics profiling was performed to establish the connection between t(4;14), NSD2, H3K36me2, H3K27me3, 3D genome organization and downstream expressional changes in disease-relevant factors, such as cell adhesion, that were highlighted in both preclinical models and patient data. Importantly, although local redistributions of H3K36me2 and H3K27me3 have been previously reported as hallmarks of t(4;14)-mediated chromatin remodeling,31 our data outline a more holistic portrait of the role of NSD2 in malignant transformation through a systematic cis-regulatory network at baseline and after acute degradation. The isolation of on-pathway effects after NSD2-LDD treatment highlights the underappreciated function of distal enhancer and repressor elements in the regulation of tumor-extrinsic and tumor-intrinsic processes.

Given the NSD2 regulon is associated with phenotypic properties of cell lines that are challenging to model in vitro, it was important to develop in vivo tools that allow for the interrogation of the NSD2 biology. We used NSD2-LDD as a bioavailable NSD2 degrader to achieve exposures that led to a deep and sustained reduction in NSD2 and altered the histone methylation landscape across in vivo studies. After NSD2-LDD treatment, we demonstrated the impact of NSD2 degradation on tumor growth using both subcutaneous and intrafemoral engraftment models. The subcutaneous model demonstrated a mild impact on tumor expansion in the context of vascularized tumors and the surrounding stromal tissue. In contrast, the dosing of tumors established directly within murine femurs provided a closer approximation of the BME, particularly upon migration to distal sites, although this approach still lacked the full spectrum of immune effector populations known to influence disease progression in patients.9 Despite these limitations, there was a significant survival benefit with NSD2-LDD treatment. Finally, spatial transcriptomic analyses within the diseased femurs of immuno-competent NSD2-BIcγ1 GEMM mice provided a more robust model to understand the cellular changes to BME induced by NSD2 degradation and confirmed the selective impact on the cellular neighborhood architecture enriched for malignant plasma cells and BME fibroblast populations.

One major limitation of this study is the isolated evaluation of epigenetic and phenotypic responses in early-breakpoint models that are addressable by NSD2-LDD. Patients lacking the PWWP1 domain could potentially experience enhanced or differential gain-of-function biology that may limit the translatability of the specific findings described in this study. Despite this caveat, our investigation strongly supports the continued exploration of NSD2 as a therapeutic target for t(4;14) MM, representing a unique opportunity to directly leverage a founding oncogenic driver within this high-risk indication needed where current 1-size-fits-all therapeutic approaches, including chemotherapeutic regimens and stem cell transplant, are often not tolerated. Targeted therapies against NSD2 could drive a more penetrant clinical response, particularly in a combination setting that leverages a disrupted stromal architecture and the systemic mobilization of plasma cells, such as tumor-intrinsic cytotoxic agent combinations and immunogenic monoclonal antibodies that comprise current standard-of-care regimens.

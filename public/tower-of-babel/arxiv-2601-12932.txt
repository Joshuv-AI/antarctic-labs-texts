Stone Duality for Preordered Topological Spaces
Jean Goubault-Larrecq

Abstract
A preordered topological space is a topological space with a preordering. We exhibit a Stone-like duality for preordered topological spaces, inspired by a similar duality for bitopological spaces, due to Jung-Moshier and Jakl, and by a duality for preordered sets due to Bonsangue, Jacobs and Kok.



1
Introduction

This work grew out of discussions with Nesta van der Schaaf on his
work on
ordered locales

(
15
)
, see also

7
. Ordered locales are meant as a theory
of ordered topological spaces without points, in the same sense that
locale theory is a theory of topological spaces without points; the
novelty is the addition of a partial ordering. Their strategy is to
equip locales with a partial ordering, and their main result is an
adjunction between the category of ordered topological spaces
satisfying a property that the authors call “with open cones and
enough points”, and the category of ordered locales satisfying a
certain property (
\bullet
)
(
7
, Theorem 6.3)
.
The property (
\bullet
) is a bit awkward, as it mentions the points
of the locales, something that one usually tries to avoid in
specifying properties of locales.

This paper proposes another adjunction between (pre)ordered
topological spaces and another form of enrichment of the notion of
locales. (Well, really three adjunctions, depending on whether we
want to be able about the future, the past, or both.) We do not need
to restrict to any subcategory of (pre)ordered topological spaces, and
there is no condition mentioning points on the localic side. Although
the definitions will appear to be complex, the general idea is pretty
simple, borrowing a notion of Stone duality for preordered
sets

from
3
and combining it with another one for
bitopological spaces from
10
, with improvements due to

9
. I will explain it in more detail in
Section
2
.

There are several alternate proposals for dualities for certain
preordered topological spaces. Notably, the category of compact
pospaces, with continuous order-preserving maps as morphisms, is
equivalent to the category of stably compact spaces, with perfect maps
as morphisms
(
13
;
14
)
. (A

pospace
is a topological space with a partial ordering whose
graph is closed in the product. We will not define the other terms.)
The usual Stone adjunction
\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
restricts to an
equivalence between the category of compact pospaces and the opposite
of the category of stably continuous frames, although one has to
beware that the morphisms are continuous maps, not perfect maps on the
first category. There is a similar adjunction that works with perfect
maps and perfect locale morphisms (see
4

and the references given therein, including earlier work by

2
), and combining it with Nachbin’s equivalence
gives a duality for compact pospaces. The present approach does not
require compactness, and will work for all preordered topological
spaces, not just pospaces.

2
Basic idea, preliminaries, and outline

We write
\mathbf{Top}
for the category of topological spaces and
continuous maps. Given any topological space
X
, its open subsets
form a frame
\mathcal{O}X
. A
frame
is a complete lattice in which
arbitrary suprema distribute over binary infima. A frame homomorphism
is a map between frames that preserves arbitrary suprema and finite
infima. Frame together with frame homomorphisms form a category

\mathbf{Frm}
. Its opposite category
\mathbf{Frm}^{op}
is the category of

locales
. There is a functor

\mathcal{O}\colon\mathbf{Top}\to\mathbf{Frm}^{op}
. We have already defined it on
objects. Its action on morphisms
f\colon X\to Y
is given by

\mathcal{O}f\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}f^{-1}
. In the converse direction, there is a
functor
\textbf{pt}\,\colon\mathbf{Frm}^{op}\to\mathbf{Top}
that is right-adjoint to

\mathcal{O}
—in notation,
\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
. This is (a modern reading
of)
Stone duality
, see
6
.

A preordered topological space
(X,\leq)
is a topological space
X

with a preordering
\leq
. We will also simply write
X
for

(X,\leq)
, and
\leq_{X}
for its preordering. We propose a form a
Stone duality for such spaces, based on the following ideas.

A preordering on a set is described equivalently as its collection of
upwards-closed subsets, which is an Alexandroff topology, namely a
topology in which arbitrary intersections of open sets are open. A
Stone-like duality for such space was described by

3
: the duals of Alexandroff spaces
proposed there is given by completely distributive lattices, with
complete lattice homomorphisms as morphisms. (See Lemma 6.5 in

(
3
)
, realizing that what the authors call complete
lattices generated by their M-prime elements are the same thing as
completely distributive lattices.) A
completely distributive
lattice
is a complete lattice in which

\bigwedge_{i\in I}\bigvee_{j\in J_{i}}x^{i}_{j}=\bigvee_{f\in\prod_{i\in I}J_{i}}\bigwedge_{i\in I}x^{i}_{f(i)}
for every
family
{(x^{i}_{j})}_{i\in I,j\in J_{i}}
, where
I
and
J_{i}
are
arbitrary sets.
\prod_{i\in I}J_{i}
is the space of all maps that
send every element
i\in I
to an element of
J_{i}
. The notations

\vee
and
\bigvee
denote suprema, and
\wedge
and
\bigwedge

denote infima. Complete lattice homomorphisms preserve all suprema
and all infima.

With the viewpoint of preordered sets as spaces with an Alexandroff
topology, a preordered topological space is a special kind of
bitopological space, namely a space with two topologies. Stone-like
duals of bitopological spaces were proposed by several authors, and
notably by
10
and by
9
: taking Jakl’s
way of presenting them, those are
d-frames
, namely a pair of
frames linked by two relations
\mathsf{con}
(consistency) and
\mathsf{tot}

(totality) satisfying certain conditions. We will mix the two
approaches.

There are several variants to this. The second topology, which should
be the Alexandroff topology of the preordering
\leq
, can be
complemented with a third topology, namely the Alexandroff topology of
the opposite preordering
\geq
. We will show
in red
what we
need if we use the second topology but not necessarily the third,

in blue
what happens if we use the third topology but not
necessarily the second, and
in purple
what happens if we
include both, and their interactions. Hence you can read this paper
with goggles that will let you read only ordinary text and red text,
or only ordinary text and blue text, or only ordinary, red and blue
text, or everything, and you will get one theory for unordered spaces
plus three different theories for ordered spaces, all dealt with at
the same time. One may think of
red
text as a theory of looking
into the
future
,
blue
text as a theory of looking into the

past
, and
purple
as a theory that combines the two.

We will rely on standard references for concepts in order theory and
topology
6
;
5
;
1
. A

poset
is a partially ordered set. A
directed family
in
a poset
P
is a non-empty family
{(x_{i})}_{i\in I}
such that any
two elements
x_{i}
,
x_{j}
have a common upper bound
x_{k}
. A

downwards-closed
subset
A
in
P
is one such that
y\in A

and
x\leq y
imply
x\in A
, where
\leq
is the ordering of
P
.
A
Scott-closed
subset of
P
is one that is downwards-closed
and closed under suprema of directed families. An

upwards-closed
subset
A
in
P
is one such that
x\in A
and

x\leq y
imply
y\in A
. When we deal with several partial
orderings on the same set
P
, we will explicitly mention it, as in

\leq
-downwards-closed or
\leq
-Scott-closed.

The plan of the paper is as follows. We implement our strategy
described above and define ad-frames in Section
3
.
We show that there is an adjunction
\mathcal{O}^{ad}\dashv\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,
between
preordered topological spaces and ad-frames in
Section
4
. Analogously to sobrification, a
natural construction arising from the usual Stone duality adjunction

\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
, we explore ad-sobrification in
Section
5
. We compare it to sobrification in
Section
6
. Just like
\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
, we show
that the adjunction
\mathcal{O}^{ad}\dashv\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,
adjunction is idempotent in
Section
7
. One selling point of van der
Schaaf’s notion of ordered locales is that his adjunction lifts the
adjunction
\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
. We show that a similar situation
occurs with
\mathcal{O}^{ad}\dashv\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,
in Section
8
.

3
Ad-frames

Following the strategy set forth in Section
2
, we
define an
ad-frame
as a tuple

(\Omega,L,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathsf{tot},\mathsf{con}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{sup},\mathsf{sub}})
where
\Omega
is a

is a completely distributive lattice, and

\mathsf{tot},\mathsf{con}\subseteq\Omega\times L
are binary relations satisfying
certain properties listed below. We will sometimes call
\mathsf{tot}
the totality relation,
\mathsf{con}
the
consistency relation
,
\mathsf{sup}
the containment relation, and

\mathsf{sub}
the inclusion relation
. In order to state them, we need to
introduce the following notation, inspired from
9
:

•

we write
\leq
for the ordering on both
\Omega
and
L
,

\wedge
for binary infima,
\bigwedge
for arbitrary infima,
\top

for their top element,
\vee
for binary suprema,
\bigvee
for
arbitrary suprema,
\bot
for their bottom element; we also write

\geq
for the opposite of
\leq
;

•

we write
\sqsubseteq
for the ordering
\leq\times\leq
on

\Omega\times L
(the “information ordering”); the corresponding
infima and suprema are written as
\sqcap
,
\bigsqcap
,
\sqcup
,

\bigsqcup
; the top element is
(\top,\top)
and the bottom
element is
(\bot,\bot)
;

•

we write
\unlhd
for
\leq\times\geq
(the “logical
ordering”) on
\Omega\times L
; the corresponding infima and
suprema are written as
\mathbin{\overline{\wedge}}
,
\mathop{\overline{\bigwedge}}
,
\mathbin{\overline{\vee}}
,

\mathop{\overline{\bigvee}}
; the top element is
\overline{\mathit{tt}}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}(\top,\bot)
and the
bottom element is
\overline{\mathit{ff}}\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}(\bot,\top)
.

With those notations, we make the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

A
ad-frame
is a tuple

(\Omega,L,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathsf{tot},\mathsf{con}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{sup},\mathsf{sub}})
where
\Omega
is
a frame,
L
is a completely distributive lattice, and:

•

\mathsf{tot}
is an
\sqsubseteq
-upwards-closed subset of

\Omega\times L
containing
\overline{\mathit{ff}}
,
\overline{\mathit{tt}}
and closed under

\mathbin{\overline{\wedge}}
and
\mathop{\overline{\bigvee}}
;

•

\mathsf{con}
is a
\sqsubseteq
-Scott-closed subset of

\Omega\times L
containing
\overline{\mathit{ff}}
,
\overline{\mathit{tt}}
and closed under

\mathbin{\overline{\wedge}}
and
\mathop{\overline{\bigvee}}
;

•

\mathsf{sup}
is an
\unlhd
-upwards-closed subset of

\Omega\times L
containing
(\bot,\bot)
,
(\top,\top)
and
closed under
\sqcap
and
\bigsqcup
;

•

\mathsf{sub}
is a
\unlhd
-Scott-closed subset of

\Omega\times L
containing
(\bot,\bot)
,
(\top,\top)
and

\sqcap
and
\bigsqcup
;

•

the following

are satisfied:

–

for all

and

–

and

u=v

,
(u,a)\unlhd(v,b)
;

–

for every
(u,a)\in\mathsf{con}\cap\mathsf{sub}
,
u=\bot
;

–

for every
(v,b)\in\mathsf{tot}\cap\mathsf{sup}
,

;

–

for every

for every

is by looking
at the following canonical example of ad-frames. More generally, it
is recommended to understand an ad-frame as a form of abstraction of a
preordered topological space, where
\Omega
,
M
,
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathsf{tot}}
,

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathsf{con}}
,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{sup}}
,

Definition and Lemma 3.2
.

For every preordered topological space
X
, we define
\mathcal{O}^{ad}X
as

(\Omega,L,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathsf{tot},\mathsf{con}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{sup},\mathsf{sub}})
where:

•

\Omega
is the lattice
\mathcal{O}X
of open subsets of
X
;

•

L
is the collection of
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of

X
;

•

for every
(U,A)\in\Omega\times L
,
(U,A)\in\mathsf{tot}
if
and only if
U\cup A=X
;

•

for every
(U,A)\in\Omega\times L
,
(U,A)\in\mathsf{con}
if
and only if
U\cap A=\emptyset
;

•

for every
(U,A)\in\Omega\times L
,
(U,A)\in\mathsf{sup}
if and only if
U\supseteq A
;

•

for every
(U,A)\in\Omega\times L
,
(U,A)\in\mathsf{sub}
if and only if
U\subseteq A
.

Then
\mathcal{O}^{ad}X
is an ad-frame.

Before we proceed, let us observe that
(U,A)\in\mathsf{sup}
if and only
if
U\cup\neg A=X
and
(U,A)\in\mathsf{sub}
if and only if

U\cap\neg A=X
, where
\neg A
denotes the complement of
A
in

X
. While the elements
A\in L
are the
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed
subsets of
X
, the elements
\neg A
where
A\in L
are the

\leq_{X}
-downwards-closed subsets of
X
, and
\mathsf{sup}
and
\mathsf{sub}
are
the relations between
\Omega
and

\{\neg A\mid A\in L\}\cong L^{op}
that mimic
\mathsf{tot}
and
\mathsf{con}

between
\Omega
and
L
.

Proof.

\Omega
is obviously a frame, and it is easy to see that
L
is a
completely distributive lattice. Explicitly, in
L
suprema are
unions and infima (not just finite infima) are intersections. Then
complete distributivity boils down to the equality

\bigcap_{i\in I}\bigcup_{j\in J_{i}}A^{i}_{j}=\bigcup_{f\in\prod_{i\in I}J_{i}}\bigcap_{i\in I}A^{i}_{f(i)}
, where
A^{i}_{j}

is
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed, and this is a classic instance of the
axiom of choice.

Let us take the notations
\sqsubseteq
,
\unlhd
, etc. as given
above.

It is easy to see that
\mathsf{tot}
is
\sqsubseteq
-upwards closed:
if
U\cup A=X
and
U\subseteq V
,
A\subseteq B
, then

V\cup B=X
. It contains
\overline{\mathit{ff}}=(\emptyset,X)
and

\tt=(X,\emptyset)
. For all
(U,A),(V,B)\in\mathsf{tot}
,

(U\cap V)\cup(A\cup B)=(U\cup A\cup B)\cap(V\cup A\cup B)=X\cap X=X
, so

(U,A)\mathbin{\overline{\wedge}}(V,B)=(U\cap V,A\cup B)\in\mathsf{tot}
. For
every family of pairs
(U_{i},A_{i})\in\mathsf{tot}
, where
i\in I
, we
claim that

\mathop{\overline{\bigvee}}_{i\in I}(U_{i},A_{i})=(\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i},\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i})
is in
\mathsf{tot}
. For every
x\in X
, either

x\in\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}
, or
x
is in no
U_{i}
; since

(U_{i},A_{i})\in\mathsf{tot}
,
x
must be in
A_{i}
, and this for every

i\in I
; hence
x\in\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}
.

The fact that
\mathsf{sup}
is
\unlhd
-upwards closed, that it
contains
(\bot,\bot)
and
(\top,\top)
and is closed under

\sqcap
and
\bigsqcup
is similar, up to replacing
A
by

\neg A
for every
A\in L
. Explicitly, if
(U,A)\in\mathsf{sup}

(namely
U\supseteq A
) and
U\subseteq V
and
A\supseteq B
,
then
V\supseteq B
.
\bot
(
=\emptyset
) contains
\bot
, so

(\bot,\bot)\in\mathsf{sup}
and similarly
(\top,\top)\in\mathsf{sup}

(where
\top=X
). If
U\supseteq A
and
V\supseteq B
then

U\cap V\supseteq A\cap B
, so
\mathsf{sup}
is closed under
\sqcap
.
If
U_{i}\supseteq A_{i}
for every
i\in I
, then

\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}\supseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}
, so
\mathsf{sup}

is closed under
\bigsqcup
.

Let us check that
\mathsf{con}
is
\sqsubseteq
-Scott-closed. If

(U,A)\sqsubseteq(V,B)
, namely if
U\subseteq V
and

A\subseteq B
, and if
(V,B)\in\mathsf{con}
, namely if
V
and

are disjoint, then so are
U
and
A
. Hence
\mathsf{con}
is
downwards-closed. Let
(U_{i},A_{i})
be a
\sqsubseteq
-directed
family in
\mathsf{con}
. Hence
U_{i}
and
A_{i}
are disjoint for every

i\in I
. We observe that

\bigsqcup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}_{i\in I}(U_{i},A_{i})=(\bigcup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}_{i\in I}U_{i},\bigcup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}_{i\in I}A_{i})
must be in
\mathsf{con}
, namely that
\bigcup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}_{i\in I}U_{i}

and
\bigcup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}_{i\in I}A_{i}
cannot intersect. Otherwise, let
x
be
in the intersection. Then
x\in U_{i}
and
x\in A_{j}
for some

i,j\in I
. By directedness, we can find

such that

(U_{i},A_{i}),(U_{j},A_{j})\sqsubseteq(U_{k},A_{k})
; in particular,

x\in U_{k}
and
x\in A_{k}
, which is impossible since
U_{k}
and

A_{k}
are disjoint.

Similarly,
\mathsf{sub}
is
\unlhd
-Scott-closed. If

(U,A)\unlhd(V,B)
, namely if
U\subseteq V
and

A\supseteq B
, and if
(V,B)\in\mathsf{sub}
, namely if

V\subseteq B
, then
U\subseteq A
, so
\mathsf{sub}
is

\unlhd
-downwards-closed. For every directed family

{(U_{i},A_{i})}_{i\in I}
in
\mathsf{sub}
(namely,
U_{i}\subseteq A_{i}

for every
i\in I
), its supremum with respect to
\unlhd
is

(U,A)
where
U\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\bigcup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}_{i\in I}U_{i}
and

, and then
U\subseteq A
. Indeed,
for every
x\in U
, for every
i\in I
, it suffices to show that

x\in A_{i}
. Since
x\in U
,
x\in U_{j}
for some
j\in I
. By

such that

(U_{i},A_{i}),(U_{j},A_{j})\unlhd(U_{k},A_{k})
. In particular

U_{j}\subseteq U_{k}
, so
x\in U_{k}
, and
A_{i}\supseteq A_{k}
; but

U_{k}\subseteq A_{k}
, so
x\in A_{i}
.

The relation
\mathsf{con}
contains
\overline{\mathit{ff}}=(\emptyset,X)
and

\tt=(X,\emptyset)
. For all
(U,A),(V,B)\in\mathsf{con}
,

U\cap A=\emptyset
and
V\cap B=\emptyset
, so

(U\cap V)\cap(A\cup B)=(U\cap V\cap A)\cup(U\cap V\cap B)=\emptyset\cup\emptyset=\emptyset
, showing that

is in
\mathsf{con}
.
For every family
(U_{i},A_{i})
of elements of
\mathsf{con}
, where

i\in I
, we claim that

\bigsqcup_{i\in I}(U_{i},A_{i})=(\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i},\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i})
is in
\mathsf{con}
. Otherwise, there would be a
point

that is in

\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}
, hence in some
U_{i}
. But since

(U_{i},A_{i})\in\mathsf{con}
,

cannot be in

after all, which is
impossible.

The relation
\mathsf{sub}
contains

(\bot,\bot)=(\emptyset,\emptyset)
and

(\top,\top)=(X,X)
, since
\emptyset\supseteq\emptyset
and

X\supseteq X
. For all
(U,A),(V,B)\in\mathsf{sub}
, we have

U\subseteq A
and
V\subseteq B
, so

U\cap V\subseteq A\cap B
, showing that
\mathsf{sub}
is closed under

\sqcap
. For every family of pairs
(U_{i},A_{i})\in\mathsf{sub}
,

i\in I
,

\bigcup_{i\in I}U_{i}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}
, so
\mathsf{sub}

is closed under
\bigsqcup
.

Finally, we check the interaction laws.

•

Let
(U,A)\in\mathsf{con}
and
(V,B)\in\mathsf{tot}
. Hence
U
is
disjoint from
A
and
V\cup B=X
. If
U=V
, then every

x\in A
is outside
U
, hence outside
V
, hence must be in

B
; so
A\subseteq B
and therefore

(U,A)\sqsubseteq(V,B)
. If
A=B
, then every
x\in U
is
outside
A
, hence outside
B
, hence must be in
V
; so

U\subseteq V
and therefore
(U,A)\sqsubseteq(V,B)
.

•

Let
(U,A)\in\mathsf{sub}
and
(V,B)\in\mathsf{sup}
. Hence

U\subseteq A
and
V\supseteq B
. If
U=V
, then

A\supseteq B
, so
(U,A)\unlhd(V,B)
. If
A=B
, then

U\subseteq V
, so
(U,A)\unlhd(V,B)
.

•

Let
(U,A)\in\mathsf{con}\cap\mathsf{sub}
. Then
U\cap A=\emptyset
and
U\subseteq A
, so
U=\emptyset
.

•

Let
(V,B)\in\mathsf{tot}\cap\mathsf{sup}
. Then
V\cup B=X

and
V\supseteq B
, so
V=X
.

•

Let
(V,B)\in\mathsf{con}\cap\mathsf{sup}
. Then
V\cap B=\emptyset

and
V\supseteq B
, so
B=\emptyset
.

•

Let
(U,A)\in\mathsf{tot}\cap\mathsf{sub}
. Then

and

, so

.

Definition 3.3
.

An
ad-frame homomorphism

Ad-frames and ad-frame homomorphisms form a category that we will

Preordered topological spaces form a category

Lemma 3.4
.

For every continuous, order-preserving map

between preordered topological spaces, the pair

\mathcal{O}^{ad}f\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}(f^{-1},f^{-1})

\mathcal{O}^{ad}Y
to
\mathcal{O}^{ad}X

\leq_{Y}
-upwards-closed) of

Proof.

Let

Remark 4.3
.

A preordered topological space

Proposition 4.8
.

For every preordered topological space
X
, for every ad-frame

\Omega\mskip-5.0muL\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}(\Omega,L,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathsf{tot},\mathsf{con}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathsf{sup},\mathsf{sub}})
,
for every continuous order-preserving map

, there is a unique ad-frame
homomorphism

Proof.

For every
x\in X
,
f(x)
is a point of
\Omega\mskip-5.0muL
, which we will
write as

;

is a completely prime filter of
elements of
\Omega
, and

is a completely prime complete

.

If

exists, then writing it as
(\varphi,p)
, we must have
that for every

,

(where

is the completely prime filter

\mathcal{U}_{x}

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of

containing

)

=(\varphi^{-1}(\mathcal{N}_{x}),p^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{x}))
. Looking
at first components, we must have

, so for every
x\in X
, for
every

,

if and only if

\varphi(v)\in\mathcal{N}_{x}
, if and only if
x\in\varphi(v)
.
Hence
\varphi(v)
must be equal to
\{x\in X\mid v\in g(x)\}
,
for every

. Similarly, for every

,

must be equal to
\{x\in X\mid a\in h(x)\}

is unique if it exists.

Let us define

as

,

as

for
every

, and
f^{!}
as
(g^{!},h^{!})
.

We check that

is a frame homomorphism. For every

, for every

preserves all unions and

. Hence

is a frame homomorphism.

Similarly,

, so

is a complete lattice homomorphism, using the fact that

is a complete lattice homomorphism by

4.4
.

Let us check that
f^{!}
preserves totality. For every

(v,b)\in\mathsf{tot}
, we must show that
(g^{!}(v),h^{!}(b))

or

. Since

5
Ad-sobrification

The usual Stone adjunction
\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
gives rise to a monad

\textbf{pt}\,\mathcal{O}
, the
sobrification
monad. For a topological space

X
, there is a simpler, and naturally isomorphic way of describing

\textbf{pt}\,\mathcal{O}X
, as the following space
X^{s}
, the
standard
sobrification
of
X
, defined as follows. The points of
X^{s}
are
the
irreducible
closed subsets
C
of
X
, namely the non-empty
closed sets
C
such that for all closed subsets
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
of

X
such that
C\subseteq C_{1}\cup C_{2}
,
C
is included in
C_{1}
or
in
C_{2}
. Equivalently, a closed set
C
is irreducible if and only
if for every finite family of open sets
U_{1}
, …,
U_{n}
, if
C

intersects each one of them then
C
intersects

U_{1}\cap\cdots\cap U_{n}
. The open subsets of
X^{s}
are the sets

\diamond U\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{C\in X^{s}\mid C\cap U\neq\emptyset\}

(
6
, Section 8.2)
.

Analogously, we call

\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,{\mathcal{O}^{ad}X}
the
ad-sobrification
of
X
. Its points are
the pairs
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U})
where:

1.

\mathcal{N}
is a completely prime filter of open subsets of

X
;

2.

\mathcal{U}
is a completely prime complete filter of

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of
X
;

3.

for every total pair
(U,A)
consisting of an open subset
U

and of an
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of
X
, namely if

U\cup A=X
, then
U\in\mathcal{N}
or
A\in\mathcal{U}
;

4.

for every consistent pair
(U,A)
consisting of an open subset

U
and of an
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of
X
, namely if

U\cap A=\emptyset
, then
U\not\in\mathcal{N}
or
A\not\in\mathcal{U}
;

5.

for every pair
(U,A)
consisting of an open subset
U

and of an
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of
X
such that
U\supseteq A
,
U\in\mathcal{N}
or
A\not\in\mathcal{U}
;

6.

for every pair
(U,A)
consisting of an open subset
U

and of an
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of
X
such that
U\subseteq A
,
U\not\in\mathcal{N}
or
A\in\mathcal{U}
.

We can simplify this as follows.

Item 1. Since
\mathcal{N}
is a completely prime filter of open
subsets of
X
, the complement of the largest open subset of
X
that
is not in
\mathcal{N}
is an irreducible closed subset
C
of
X
, and

\mathcal{N}
is the collection of open subsets of
X
that intersect

C
. In other words, we can represent
\mathcal{N}
by
C
, which is a
point in the (standard) sobrification
X^{s}
of
X
.

Item 2.
\mathcal{U}
simplifies even more. Since
\mathcal{U}
is a
complete filter, the intersection of all the elements of
\mathcal{U}

is still in
\mathcal{U}
. In other words, there is an

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of
X
such that
\mathcal{A}
is
the collection of
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of
X
containing

A
. Additionally, since
\mathcal{U}
is completely prime, and since

A
is the union of all the subsets
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
,
x\in A
(where

\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}
denotes upward closure with respect to
\leq_{X}
),
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x

must be in
\mathcal{N}
for some
x\in A
. In other words,

A\subseteq\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
for some
x\in A
. The reverse inclusion is
obvious, so
A
is the
\leq_{X}
-upward-closure of some point

x\in X
. (This point is not unique in general, unless
\leq_{X}
is
antisymmetric.) Knowing this,
\mathcal{U}
is just the collection of

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of
X
that contain
x
. This point

x
is uniquely determined up to
\leq_{X}\cap\geq_{X}
, the equivalence
relation associated with
\leq_{X}
(equality if
\leq_{X}
is
antisymmetric). Let us write
[x]_{X}
for the equivalence class of

x
. Then
\mathcal{U}
is the collection of
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed
subsets of
X
that contain
x
, for some
unique
equivalence
class
[x]_{X}
.

Item 3. The condition on totality then reads: for every open
subset
U
of
X
, for every
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of

X
, if
U\cup A=X
then
U
intersects
C
or
x\in A
. We
claim that this condition is equivalent to the fact that

C\cap\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
cannot be empty, where
\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}
denotes downward
closure with respect to
\leq_{X}
. If the condition holds, then
taking
U
to be the complement of
C
and
A
to be the complement
of
\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
, we obtain that
U\cap A
cannot be equal to
X
; by
taking complements,
C\cap\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
cannot be empty. Conversely,
if
C\cap\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
is non-empty, then let
y
be in the
intersection. For every open subset
U
of
X
, for every

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of
X
, if
U
does not intersect

C
and
x\not\in A
then in particular
y
is not in
U
and
y

is not in
A
(otherwise, since
y\in\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
, namely since

y\leq_{X}x
, and since
A
is
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed,
x
would be
in
A
), so
U\cup A\neq X
. This is the contrapositive of the
condition on totality.

Item 4. The condition on consistency reads: for every open subset

U
and every
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of
X
such that

U\cap A=\emptyset
, then
U
is disjoint from
C
or

x\not\in A
. We claim that this is equivalent to

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)
, where
cl
denotes closure in
X
.
Indeed, if
C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)
failed, then by taking the
complement of
cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)
for
U
and
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
for
A
, we
would have
U\cap A=\emptyset
but

, so
U
would be
disjoint from
C
by the condition; but that would say that

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)
, which is impossible. Conversely, if

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)
, then for every open subset
U
and
every
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset
A
of
X
such that
U

intersects
C
(hence also
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
, say at
y
) and
x\in A
,
then
U
would intersect
A
(at
y
). This is the contrapositive
of the condition.

Item 5. Symmetrically to item 3, the condition on containment
is equivalent to the fact that
C\cap\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x\neq\emptyset
,
where
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}
is upward closure with respect to
\leq_{X}
. Indeed,
the condition on containment is equivalent to: for every open set

U
and for every
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed set
A
such that

U\supseteq A
,
U
intersects
C
or
x\not\in A
. If that
condition holds, then taking the complement of
C
for
U
and

\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
for
A
, we have
U\cap C=\emptyset
and
x\in A
, so
by contraposition

; in other words,

C\cap\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x\neq\emptyset
. Conversely, if

C\cap\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x\neq\emptyset
, then for every open set
U
and
for every
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed set
A
such that
U\supseteq A
,
if
x\in A
, then
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x\subseteq A\subseteq U
, and since
C

intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
, it must intersect the larger set
U
.

Item 6. Symmetrically to item 4, the condition on inclusion is
equivalent to
C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x)
. Indeed, the condition on
inclusion is equivalent to: for every open set
U
and for every

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed set
A
such that
U\subseteq A
,
U
is
disjoint from
C
or
x\in A
. If that condition holds, then let

U
be the complement of
cl(\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x)
and
A
be the complement of

\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
. We have
\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x\subseteq cl(\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x)
, so

or

. But

is not in

, so

is disjoint from

. Conversely,

, then for every open set

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed set

, if

intersects

\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
. Let

,

is in

; but

and

is
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed, so

.

This leads us to the following definition. We recall that

denotes the equivalence class of

with respect to

Definition 5.1
.

The

of a preordered topological

(C,[x]_{X})
where

,

,

\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
,

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)
,
C
intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
and

\diamond^{ad}U\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{(C,[x]_{X})\in X^{ads}\mid C\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
, and its preordering puts

below

(D,[y]_{X})
if and only if the collection of

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of

.

Conversely to what we did in the discussion preceding this definition,
any point
(C,[x]_{X})
of

yields a point

(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{U})
of
\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,{\mathcal{O}^{ad}X}
, by letting

be the collection of open subsets

of

.

For every preordered topological space,

is isomorphic to

\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,{\mathcal{O}^{ad}X}
in

Through this isomorphism, the unit

, from

, and

associated with the

. Its action on morphisms is obtained
by tracking the action of

for the image of

under

Proposition 5.3
.

For every continuous order-preserving map

between

applied to

yields the continuous order-preserving map

, for every

.

Proof.

For every continuous order-preserving map

between
preordered topological spaces,

is

(\varphi,p)\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}(f^{-1},f^{-1})
(see

), and then
\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,{\mathcal{O}^{ad}f}
maps every

of
\mathcal{O}^{ad}X
to

(\varphi^{-1}(\mathcal{N}),\allowbreak p^{-1}(\mathcal{U}))
.
When

is the completely prime filter of open subsets of

,

is the

of

such that

, namely such that

, or equivalently

-upwards-closed subsets

.
∎

For every
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset

of

, let

[A]\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{(C,[x]_{X})\in X^{ads}\mid x\in A\}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For every preordered topological space
X
, the map
\diamond^{ad}

is an order-isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}X
onto
\mathcal{O}{X^{ads}}
. The
map

is an order-isomorphism of the lattice of

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of

onto the lattice of
upwards-closed subsets of

.

Proof.

\diamond^{ad}
is surjective by definition of the topology on

X^{ads}
. It is monotonic: if
U\subseteq V
in
\mathcal{O}X
, then
for every
(C,[x]_{X})\in\diamond^{ad}U
,

intersects
U
,
so

intersects

, hence
(C,[x]_{X})\in\diamond^{ad}V
.
We claim that

is order-reflecting, namely that

\diamond^{ad}U\subseteq\diamond^{ad}V
implies

,
for all

. For every

,

intersects

(at

), so
\eta_{X}(x)=(cl(\{x\}),[x]_{X})
is
in
\diamond^{ad}U
. If

\diamond^{ad}U\subseteq\diamond^{ad}V
, then

must

\diamond^{ad}V
, namely

must intersect

.
Then

must intersect

. As

.

Let us write

for the map

, where

ranges over
the
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of

. We note that

is

, and

is below

, then

is in

, so

. It is clear that

is
monotonic. Let

,

be two
\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subsets of

, and let us assume that

. For every

,

is in

, hence in

, and this

. Therefore

. We have just

is a monotonic order-reflection.

It remains to see that

is surjective. Let

be an

. We define

as

.

is

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed:
if

, namely if

for some

,
and if

, then

lies above
(C,[x]_{X})
in

; since

is upwards-closed,

, so

. Finally,

is the collection of points

such that

,
hence it contains

. Conversely, every such point

, hence is in

.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For every preordered topological space
X
, for every

,
\eta_{X}^{-1}(\diamond^{ad}U)=U
; for every

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset

of

,

.

Proof.

\eta_{X}^{-1}(\diamond^{ad}U)
consists of the points

such that

(cl(\{x\}),[x]_{X})\in\diamond^{ad}U
, namely such that

intersects

, or equivalently such that

intersects

; that is just

.
\eta_{X}^{-1}([A])
consists of
the points

such that
(cl(\{x\}),[x]_{X})
is such that
[x]_{X}

can be written as

for some

. Since

is

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed, hence a union of equivalence classes, this

.
∎

In the setting of unordered topological spaces, the unit of the

\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
adjunction is bijective at a space if and only if
it is a homeomorphism, if and only if
X
is
sober
, meaning
that every irreducible closed subset is the closure of a unique point.

Definition and Lemma 5.6
.

For a preordered topological space

, the following are
equivalent:

1.

\eta_{X}\colon X\to X^{ads}
is bijective;

2.

\eta_{X}\colon X\to X^{ads}
is an isomorphism in

\mathbf{PreTop}
;

3.

is

, namely every irreducible pair is equal
to

for a unique point

of

. An

is a pair

where
C\in X^{s}

and

, such that
C
intersects
\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
,

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)
,

intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x

and
C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x)
.

Proof.

Condition 3 is simply a rephrasing of condition 1, and condition 2

assume that

is bijective, and we let

be its inverse.
We need to show that

is continuous and monotonic. By

,

for every

; therefore

, showing

for every

\leq_{X}
-upwards-closed subset

of

. Since

is

∎

6
Sobrification and ad-sobrification

What is the connection between sobrification and ad-sobrification?
There is definitely a similarity, and we make it explicit here.

Let
Ind
be the functor that maps every topological space
X
to the
preordered topological space
X
, with the indiscrete preordering on

X
(where every point is below every other). This acts as identity
on morphisms.

Proposition 6.1
.

There is a natural isomorphism between
\_^{ads}\circ\textbf{Ind}
and

\textbf{Ind}\circ\_^{s}
.

Proof.

We investigate objects first. Let
X
be a topological space. If

X
is empty, then
(\textbf{Ind}X)^{ads}
is empty, and
X^{s}
is empty,
too. Hence let us assume that
X
is non-empty, and let us pick

x\in X
. The equivalence class of
x
with respect to
\sim
is

X
itself. Therefore the points of
(\textbf{Ind}X)^{ads}
are the pairs

(C,X)
where
C\in X^{s}
,
C
intersects
\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x=X

(vacuously true),
C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)=X
(also vacuously
true)
,
C
intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x=X
(vacuously true), and

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x)=X
(still vacuously true).
This shows
that the map
(C,X)\mapsto C
is a bijection from
(\textbf{Ind}X)^{ads}

onto
X^{s}
. The inverse image of the open set
\diamond U
is

\diamond^{ad}U
, so this is a homeomorphism. Also,
(C,X)
is
below
(D,X)
in
(\textbf{Ind}X)^{ads}
is always true, so the
preordering on
(\textbf{Ind}X)^{ads}
is the indiscrete preordering.

We have obtained an isomorphism
(C,X)\mapsto C
from

(\textbf{Ind}X)^{ads}
onto
\textbf{Ind}{(X^{s})}
for every topological space

X
. (This works when
X
is empty, too.)

The naturality property means that for every continuous map

f\colon X\to Y
, seen as a continuous order-preserving map from

\textbf{Ind}X
to
\textbf{Ind}Y
, for every
(C,[x]_{X})\in X^{ads}
, the first
component of
f^{ads}(C,[x]_{X})
is equal to
f^{s}(C)
. But

f^{s}(C)=cl(f[C])
, so the claim follows from
Proposition
5.3
.
∎

On the other extreme of the spectrum, let
Discr
be the functor that
maps every topological space
X
to the preordered topological space

X
with the discrete preordering, namely equality. This also acts as
identity on morphisms. Then ad-sobrification does nothing.

Proposition 6.2
.

There is a natural isomorphism between
\_^{ads}\circ\textbf{Discr}
and
the identity functor.

Proof.

For every topological space
X
, the points of
(\textbf{Discr}X)^{ads}

are the pairs
(C,x)
such that
C
intersects

\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x=\{x\}
(namely, such that
x\in C
),

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)=cl(\{x\})
,
C
intersects

\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x=\{x\}
(equivalent to
x\in C
again) and

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x)=cl(\{x\})
. Those conditions are
equivalent to requiring that
C=cl(\{x\})
. Then the map

(cl(\{x\}),x)\mapsto x
is an isomorphism from

(\textbf{Discr}X)^{ads}
onto
X
, and it is easy to see that it is
natural in
X
.
∎

A final construction that is called for consists in seeing every
topological space
X
as a preordered topological space by giving it
its specialization preordering
\leq
as preordering. (The

specialization preordering

\leq
is defined by
x\leq y
if
and only if every open set that contains
x
also contains
y
, if and
only if
x
is in the closure of
\{y\}
.) Let us write
\mathop{\downarrow}
for
downward closure and
\mathop{\uparrow}
for upward closure with respect to
\leq
.
Let us also write
\equiv
for
\leq\cap\geq
. Then
X^{ads}
is a
bit weird: it consists of pairs
(C,[x]_{X})
where
C
intersects

\mathop{\downarrow}x
,
C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}x)
,
C
intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}x

and
C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\downarrow}x)=\mathop{\downarrow}x
.
If we only consider the
red option, then here is an extreme case. Let us imagine that
X

has a least element
\bot
and a largest element
\top
with respect
to
\leq
. Then
\bot
is in
C
and in
\mathop{\downarrow}x
, and
\top
is in

\mathop{\uparrow}x
, so
cl(\mathop{\uparrow}x)=X
contains
C
, and therefore
C

trivially intersects
\mathop{\downarrow}x
and the inclusion

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}x)
is trivial, too. Therefore, in that case,

X^{ads}
is the product of
X^{s}
with
X/\equiv
, with the
(non-
T_{0}
) topology whose open subsets are the sets

\diamond U\times X/\equiv
,
U\in\mathcal{O}X
, and whose
(non-antisymmetric) preordering is given by

(C,[x]_{X})\leq(D,[y]_{X})
if and only if
x\leq y
.
If
we consider the blue-only option, then
C
intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}x
if
and only if
x\in C
, and the conjunction of this with

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\downarrow}x)=\mathop{\downarrow}x
means that
C=\mathop{\downarrow}x
. Then the
only points of
X^{ads}
are those of the form
(\mathop{\downarrow}x,[x]_{X})
.
Similarly to Proposition
6.2
, we can then show that

X^{ads}
is isomorphic to the
T_{0}
-reflection of

, namely to

quotiented by
\equiv
, with its specialization ordering, which
is the quotient of
\leq
by
\equiv
.

Finally, in the both
blue and red option, the only points of
X^{ads}
are those of the
form
(C,[x]_{X})
with

, as in the red-only case, and
then
C
trivially intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}x
and

C=\mathop{\downarrow}x\subseteq\mathop{\downarrow}\mathop{\uparrow}x\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}x)
. Hence

X^{ads}
is also the
T_{0}
-reflection of
X
with its
specialization ordering, as in the red-only case.

Let us write
|X|
for the underlying topological space of a
preordered topological space
X
, and let us investigate whether

|X^{ads}|\cong|X|^{s}
, where
\cong
is for homeomorphism.
|\_|

extends to a functor
|\_|\colon\mathbf{PreTop}\to\mathbf{Top}
, which maps
every continuous order-preserving map
f
to
f
seen as a mere
continuous map.

Remark 6.3
.

Let
X
be a preordered topological space. It is in general wrong
that
|X^{ads}|\cong|X|^{s}
. Indeed, let
X
be obtained as a
topological space

with the discrete preordering. By

,

is isomorphic to

, hence

if and only if

, if and
only if

is sober. Hence taking any non-sober space for

gives us a counterexample.

Proposition 6.4
.

For every

and
lower semi-closed

such that every irreducible closed subset

contains a point

such that
C\subseteq\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
and

C\subseteq\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
, the map
(C,[x]_{X})\mapsto C
is a
homeomorphism from
|X^{ads}|
onto
|X|^{s}
, which is natural in

X
.

For every
T_{1}
sober pretopological space

, the map

(C,[x]_{X})\mapsto C
is a homeomorphism from
|X^{ads}|
onto

|X|^{s}\cong|X|
, which is natural in
X
.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be the map
(C,[x]_{X})\mapsto C
. For every open subset

U
of
X
,
\pi^{-1}(\diamond U)=\diamond^{ad}U
, so
\pi
is
continuous. This also shows that
\pi
is a homeomorphism if
\pi
is
bijective.

If
X
is
upper semi-closed
,
lower semi-closed
, and
every irreducible closed subset
C
contains a point
x
such that

C\subseteq\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
and
C\subseteq\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
, then
for every irreducible closed subset
C
of
X
, we pick such a point

. Then
C
intersects
\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
,

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x)
,
C
intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
and

C\subseteq cl(\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x)
, so
(C,[x]_{X})
is an irreducible

by

. Hence
\pi
is surjective.
If there are two points

such that

(or

), then

and

, so

, hence

,
and similarly with

instead of

\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}
, showing that
\pi
is injective.

If

is

and sober then every irreducible closed subset

of

is of the form
\{x\}
for a unique point

, and then it is
trivially true that

intersects
\mathop{\downarrow}_{X}x
,

,

intersects
\mathop{\uparrow}_{X}x
, and

. Hence the points of

, as
well as those of

, are exactly the one-element sets, showing
that

is bijective and that

at the same time.

For naturality, let

be a monotonic continuous
map. Then

maps
(C,[x]_{X})
to
(cl(f[C]),[f(x)]_{Y})

by Proposition

, so

\pi(f^{ads}(C,[x]_{X}))=cl(f[C])=f^{s}(C)=f^{s}(\pi(C,[x]_{X}))
.
∎

7
The
\mathcal{O}^{ad}\dashv\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,
adjunction is idempotent

The usual adjunction
\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
is idempotent, and we prove a
similar statement for
\mathcal{O}^{ad}\dashv\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,
here. This means showing
that
\eta_{\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,\Omega\mskip-5.0muL}
is an isomorphism for every ad-frame

\Omega\mskip-5.0muL
, or equivalently that
\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,\Omega\mskip-5.0muL
is ad-sober for every
ad-frame
\Omega\mskip-5.0muL
, by Definition and Lemma
5.6
.
The monad associated with an idempotent adjunction is always an
idempotent monad, where an idempotent monad is one whose
multiplication is a natural isomorphism (there are several equivalent definitions).

Definition 7.1
.

A preordered topological space
X
is
ad-
T_{0}
if and only if
the intersection of the specialization ordering
\leq
of (the
topological space underlying)

We recall that there is a functor

Proposition 8.1

Let us consider any
|\_|

is a

on

and
\leq_{Y_{i}}

|\_|
-initial lift.

Conclusion

We have obtained an adjunction

between

\mathbf{PreTop}
and
\mathbf{adFrm}^{op}
, or rather three,
red

blue

. Just like van der Schaaf’s

15

7

, it lifts the usual

\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
adjunction, under the proviso that we restrict it
to non-empty topological spaces and non-trivial ad-frames. This
proviso is a minor defect of the theory, and one that I think we can
live with.

The adjunction
\mathcal{O}^{ad}\dashv\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,
shares many nice properties with
the
\mathcal{O}\dashv\textbf{pt}\,
adjunction. Notably, we have shown that there
is a notion of ad-sobrification, which is idempotent (equivalently, the
monad is idempotent).

Based on Remark
4.3
and
Remark
4.7
,
\mathcal{O}^{ad}\dashv\textbf{pt}^{ad}\,
specializes to an
adjunction between
upper semi-closed
(resp.
lower
semi-closed
, resp.
semi-closed
) preordered topological
spaces and ad-frames satisfying property
(usc)
, resp.
(lsc)
, resp.
both
. One part of the theory that bothers
me is that it does not seem that we can characterize
pospaces

this way, that is, ordered topological spaces
X
with an ordering
whose graph is closed in
X\times X
. In a sense, ad-frames miss a
way of speaking of the ordering relation
\leq
itself, and only has
access to upwards-closed and downwards-closed subsets instead.

One thing that we have not developed here is a theory of

ad-locales
. Just like locales are just frames, with morphisms
reversed, ad-locales are just ad-frames with morphisms reversed.
There is an extensive program of translating known theorems on ordered
topological spaces to analogous theorems on ad-locales, and in the
process, making them
pointfree
. The hope is that the pointfree
analogues of known theorems would be constructive, paralleling a
program that has been set up and followed by Isbell, Johnstone,
Banaschewski, Escardó, and others in the unordered setting. One
may for example look at results of
12
on chains in
partially ordered spaces for a start.

One may also hope that a theory of ad-locales would contain some new
theorems, with no analogue in the non-pointfree setting, such as
Isbell’s density theorem
(
8
)
.

My own interest in the field is one of curiosity, and I would like to
extend the theory developed here to finding Stone-like dualities for

streams
, a model of topological spaces with
local

preorderings
(
11
)
.

Finally, Stone-like dualities are deeply connected to logic, and one
should investigate what the logic of ad-frames is. The natural
inspiration would be to follow
9
. One
should also investigate connections with intuitionistic S4 logic

(
16
)
, where intuitionism would be about the topology
and the S4 modalities would be about the ordering.

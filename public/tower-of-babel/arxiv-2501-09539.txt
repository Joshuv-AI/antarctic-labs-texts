Abstract
. We construct non-negative weak solutions of fast diffusion equations with a divergence type of drift term satisfying the L q L^{q} -energy inequality and speed estimate in Wasserstein spaces under some integrability conditions on the drift term. Furthermore, in the case that the drift term has a divergence-free structure, it turns out that its integrability conditions can be relaxed, which is also applicable to porous medium equations, thereby improving previous results. As an application, the existence of weak solutions is also discussed for a viscous Boussinesq system of the fast diffusion type.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the fast diffusion equation (FDE) with a drift term in divergence form, given by

(1.1)

\partial_{t}\rho\;=\;\nabla\cdot\bigl(\nabla\rho^{m}\;-\;V\,\rho\bigr),\quad 0<m<1,

in the cylinder
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
, where

\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
is a bounded domain with smooth boundaries, and
0<T<\infty
. The vector field
V:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is given and will be specified later.
We impose no-flux boundary conditions on the lateral boundary and assume nonnegative initial data:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}(\nabla\rho^{m}-V\,\rho)\cdot\mathbf{n}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\rho(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0}(\cdot)\in L^{q}(\Omega)&\text{for }q\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{n}
denotes the outward normal to
\partial\Omega
.

The fast diffusion equation (FDE) is a fundamental singular nonlinear diffusion model that arises in a wide range of applications. In particular, for the homogeneous fast diffusion equation, i.e., (
1.1
) with
V=0
, extensive research has been conducted on the local and global properties of solutions.
For instance, if
\frac{(d-2)_{+}}{d+2}<m<1
, then local solutions remain locally bounded. However, when
0<m\leq\frac{(d-2)_{+}}{d+2}
, the concept of weak solutions with sufficient integrability ensures local boundedness. Furthermore, Hölder continuity and the Harnack inequality are not guaranteed when
0<m\leq(1-\frac{2}{d})_{+}
; see
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
for further details.
For a systematic study of homogeneous FDE, we refer the reader to comprehensive books and papers such as
[
27
,
28
,
13
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
and the references therein.

Fast diffusion equations with potential drift or aggregation terms arise naturally in the Wasserstein-gradient-flow framework, in particular when
V=\nabla\Phi
for some potential function
\Phi
; see, for instance,
[
24
]
. Recent works on related potential and aggregation models in the fast-diffusion range have revealed concentration phenomena, such as infinite-time or partial mass concentration and possible Dirac components in the large-time limit
[
11
,
9
,
10
]
. In contrast, the present paper considers general drift fields
V=V(x,t)
and constructs global weak solutions with
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))
bounds for
q>1
and entropy bounds for
q=1
.

Drift terms play a key role in modeling various drift-diffusion phenomena, including the Keller–Segel model, fluid dynamics, and related systems
[
25
,
17
,
30
,
31
]
. More recently, nonlinear drift-diffusion equations have been studied to understand the well-posedness and regularity of their solutions
[
23
,
21
,
8
]
.

Motivated by the existence theory for porous medium equations with drift developed in
[
19
,
20
]
, the present paper develops a fast-diffusion analogue for general space-time dependent drift fields. However, the transition from the porous medium range
m>1
to the fast-diffusion range
0<m<1
is not straightforward.
In the fast-diffusion case, it provides weaker coercive control than in the porous medium case, and the resulting estimates are not strong enough to handle a drift term in the scaling-invariant class. This leads to the restriction
1-\frac{1}{d}<m<1
and to a more restrictive integrability condition on
V
, which is subcritical relative to the scaling-invariant class. More precisely, the admissible drift class
\mathsf{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
in Definition
2.1
obtained from the energy estimate, for
q>1
,

V\in L_{x,t}^{q_{1},q_{2}}\qquad\frac{d}{q_{1}}+\frac{2+d(q+m-2)}{q_{2}}\leq 1+d(m-1),\quad 1-\frac{1}{d}<m<1,

is more restrictive than the scaling-invariant class
\mathcal{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
in Remark
2.2
used in the porous medium case,

V\in L_{x,t}^{q_{1},q_{2}},\qquad\frac{d}{q_{1}}+\frac{2+q_{m,d}}{q_{2}}\leq 1+q_{m,d},\quad q_{m,d}=\frac{d(m-1)}{q},\quad m>1.

The two classes coincide in the
L^{1}
-based case
q=1
.

In the divergence-free case, the drift term does not contribute to the
L^{q}
-energy estimate, and the admissible conditions on
V
are determined instead by the compactness argument used to pass to the limit in the weak formulation and by the speed estimate needed to obtain absolute continuity in the Wasserstein space. This allows us to introduce the classes
\mathcal{D}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
and
\mathcal{D}_{m,q,s}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
, which contain the corresponding scaling-type region and extend into supercritical regimes. They also allow an enlarged range of
m
. Under the condition
\nabla\cdot V=0
, this is expressed as

V\in L_{x,t}^{q_{1},q_{2}},\qquad\frac{d}{q_{1}}+\frac{2+q_{m,d}}{q_{2}}\leq 2+\frac{d(q+m-2)}{q},\quad\left(1-\frac{2q}{d}\right)_{+}\leq m<1.

We refer to Definitions
2.1
and
2.3
, Remark
2.2
, and Fig.

The analogous improvement for the porous medium equation is based on the improved compactness argument and is discussed in Remark
2.13

Our strategy for constructing weak solutions begins with establishing the existence of regular solutions under sufficiently smooth boundary data and

4.4

Remark 2.13

(Comparison with the porous medium case)
.

Compared with the previous divergence-free results for the porous medium equation in
[
20
]
, the compactness argument and speed estimate developed in the present paper allow admissible drift classes in supercritical regimes relative to the scaling-invariant class.

For
L^{q}
-weak solutions, the main improvement comes from the compactness argument. In
[
19
, Proposition 4.19]
, the compactness argument for PME was based on estimates for
\partial_{t}\rho^{q}
and
\nabla\rho^{q}
, and was therefore formulated at the level of
\rho^{q}
. In Proposition
4.6
, we instead establish compactness directly for
\rho
by estimating
\partial_{t}\rho
and
\nabla\rho
in suitable spaces, together with a truncation argument. This direct compactness framework leads to the drift class
\mathcal{D}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
, which includes supercritical regimes relative to the scaling-invariant class
\mathcal{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
. In Fig.
5
, the previous
L^{q}
-weak-solution results in
[
20
, Theorems 2.8 and 2.10]
correspond to
\mathcal{R}(OA^{\prime}D^{\prime})
, while Theorem
B.3
extends the admissible region to
\mathcal{R}(OABCD)
.

For AC
L^{q}
-weak solutions in the divergence-free PME case with
q>m
, the previous result
[
20
, Theorem 2.12]
was obtained under the scaling-invariant drift condition
V\in\mathcal{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
and provides a
W_{2}
-distance estimate. In the present paper, we obtain the same type of
W_{2}
-distance estimate under the class
\mathcal{D}_{m,q,s}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
, which lies in the supercritical regime of
\mathcal{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
when

. In Fig.

, the previous admissible region corresponds to

in
[

, while Theorem
B.5
extends it to
\mathcal{R}(OEFGH)
.

The main statements and detailed computations for these PME consequences are given in Appendix
B
.

2.2.

In this section, as an application, we study a viscous Boussinesq system of the fast diffusive type.
The Boussinesq system models the coupling between incompressible fluid motion and temperature variation through buoyancy forces. It is a basic model for thermal convection and buoyancy-driven flows.

\theta_{t}-\Delta\theta^{m}+u\cdot\nabla\theta=0,

(2.14)

u_{t}-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi=-\theta{\bf{e}_{d}},\qquad{\rm div}\,u=0.

in
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d=2,3
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary, whereby the following boundary conditions are assigned

\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial{\bf n}}=0,\quad u=0\qquad\mbox{ on }\,\,\partial\Omega\times(0,T).

Here
\theta
and
u
indicate the temperature and fluid velocity, respectively.
We suppose that initial conditions satisfy

(2.15)

\theta_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega),\quad\int_{\Omega}\theta_{0}\log\theta_{0}dx<\infty,\quad u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega).

The main motivation is that the temperature equation is a fast diffusion equation with drift field
u
. Since the velocity is divergence-free, the transport term
u\cdot\nabla\theta
does not contribute to the energy estimate for
\theta
. Hence the system provides a natural application of the existence theory for fast diffusion equations with divergence-free drift fields. At the same time, the drift is not prescribed but is determined by the coupled Navier–Stokes equation, which makes the system a nontrivial and physically relevant application of the theory.

Our goal is to construct weak solutions for (
2.13
)–(
2.14
).
For convenience, we introduce the notion of a weak solution.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
0<m<1
and
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d=2,3
be a bounded domain with smooth boundaries. We say that a pair of
(\theta,u)
is a weak solution of (
2.13
)-(
2.14
) in
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
with zero boundary data if the followings are satisfied:

(i)

It holds that

\theta,\ \nabla\theta^{m},\ \theta u,\ u\otimes u,\ \nabla u\in L_{x,t}^{1}.

(ii)

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T);{\mathbb{R}}\right)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}\left(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\right)
with
\rm{div}\,\psi=0
both of which vanish on
\partial\Omega
, it holds that

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\left\{-\theta\varphi_{t}+\nabla\theta^{m}\nabla\varphi-\theta u\cdot\nabla\varphi\right\}\,dxdt=\int_{\Omega}\theta_{0}\varphi(\cdot,0)\,dx,

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\left\{-u\psi_{t}+\nabla u\nabla\psi-u\otimes u:\nabla\psi\right\}\,dxdt=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\psi(\cdot,0)\,dx-\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\theta{\bf e}_{d}\psi\,dxdt,

It is well known that in the case
m=1
, smooth solutions exist globally in time, provided that the initial data are sufficiently regular in two dimensions; see
[
7
]
. On the other hand, the question of regularity in three dimensions remains open.

In contrast, the fast-diffusive case
0<m<1
is less standard,
because the diffusion term
\Delta\theta^{m}
is singular near the vacuum
region. The temperature equation can be viewed as a fast diffusion equation
with drift
u
, and the incompressibility condition

\nabla\cdot u=0

implies that the drift term does not contribute to the energy estimate.
This divergence-free structure allows one to combine the weak existence theory
for fast diffusion equations with divergence-free drifts and the classical
energy estimates for the velocity equation.
Our main objective in this subsection is to establish weak solutions, satisfying an energy estimate (
2.16
) when initial data satisfy (
2.15
), and we establish the existence of weak solutions for some range of
0<m<1
.

We are now ready to state the main result for equations (
2.13
)–(
2.14
). In the proof, we provide a priori estimates, as such estimates can also be derived without difficulty from the regularized system.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d=2,3
be a bounded domain with smooth boundaries.
If
0<m<1
when
d=2
and if
\frac{2}{3}\leq m<1
when
d=3
, then,
there exists a pair of weak solution
(\theta,u)
in Definition
2.14
. Furthermore, for any
(\theta,u)
satisfies

(2.16)

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\Omega}(\theta(\cdot,t)+\left|u(\cdot,t)\right|^{2})\,dx+\iint_{\Omega_{T}}(\left|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla u\right|^{2})\,dxdt\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\theta_{0}\log\theta_{0}\,dx,\left\|\theta_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}},\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

Proof.

We first note that

(2.17)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\theta(\cdot,t)\log\theta(\cdot,t)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right|^{2}\,dx=0

We treat the two and three dimensions separately.

\bullet

(2d case)

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
with
\frac{2}{m}\leq p
.
For the fluid equation, energy estimate gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\theta\left|u\right|dx\lesssim\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{q}_{x}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{q}_{x}}\left\|u\right\|^{\frac{2}{p}}_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}_{L^{2}_{x}},

where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
.
Due to Young’s inequality, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\theta\right\|^{\frac{2p}{p+2}}_{L^{q}_{x}}\left\|u\right\|^{\frac{4}{p+2}}_{L^{2}_{x}}=\left\|\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{4p}{m(p+2)}}_{L^{\frac{2q}{m}}_{x}}\left\|u\right\|^{\frac{4}{p+2}}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Noting that
\left\|\theta(t)\right\|_{L^{1}}=\left\|\theta_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}
and

\left\|\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{4p}{m(p+2)}}_{L^{\frac{2q}{m}}_{x}}\leq\left(\left\|\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{q}}_{L^{\frac{2}{m}}_{x}}\left\|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)^{\frac{4p}{m(p+2)}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{4}{m(p+2)}}_{L^{2}_{x}},

we obtain

(2.18)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{4}{m(p+2)}}_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\|u\right\|^{\frac{4}{p+2}}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\epsilon\left\|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\left\|u\right\|^{\frac{4m}{m(p+2)-2}}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Combining (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), we obtain that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(\theta\log\theta+\left|u\right|^{2})\,dx+\iint_{\Omega_{T}}(\left|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla u\right|^{2})\,dxdt\lesssim\left\|u\right\|^{\frac{4m}{m(p+2)-2}}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

It is required that

\frac{4m}{m(p+2)-2}\leq 2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{2}{m}\leq p.

Since
u\in L^{a,b}_{x,t}
where
\frac{2}{a}+\frac{2}{b}=1
with
2\leq a<\infty
, it is automatic that
u\in\mathcal{D}_{m,1}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
with
d=2
.
Indeed, in case
m\leq\frac{1}{2}
, it is straightforward. If
m\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, then we can take
q_{2}\leq\frac{2(1-m)}{(1-2m)}
and then choose
q_{1}
with
\frac{2}{q_{1}}+\frac{2}{q_{2}}=1
, which implies that
u\in\mathcal{D}_{m,1}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
,
d=2
.
This completes the proof for 2d case.

\bullet

(3d case)

As similarly, we have

(2.19)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\theta\left|u\right|dx\lesssim\left\|\theta\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\lesssim\left\|\theta\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}

Via interpolation, it gives

(2.20)

\left\|\theta\right\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x}}=\left\|\theta\right\|^{\frac{4}{m}}_{L^{\frac{12}{5m}}_{x}}\leq\left\|\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{10m-4}{m(3m-1)}}_{L^{\frac{2}{m}}_{x}}\left\|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{2}{3m-1}}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\left\|\nabla\theta^{\frac{m}{2}}\right\|^{\frac{2}{3m-1}}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

It is required that

\frac{2}{3m-1}\leq 2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad m\geq\frac{2}{3}.

We note that
u\in L^{6,2}_{x,t}
, which belongs to
u\in\mathcal{D}_{m,1}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
,
d=3
. Indeed, since
u\in L^{6,2}_{x,t}
, it is direct that

\frac{3}{6}+\frac{2+3(m-1)}{2}\leq 2+3(m-1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{2}{3}\leq m.

Combining (
2.17
), (
2.19
) and (
2.20
), we complete the proof.
∎

3.2.
Wasserstein space

In this subsection, we introduce the Wasserstein space and its properties. For more detail, we refer
[
1
,
24
,
29
]
.
Let us denote by
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
the set of all Borel probability measures on
\Omega
with a finite
p
-th moment. That is,

\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|x|^{p}\,d\mu(x)<\infty\}
.
We note that
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)=\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
if
\Omega
is a bounded set.
For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
, we consider

(3.1)

W_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\left(\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|x-y|^{p}\,d\gamma(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of all Borel probability measures on
\Omega\times\Omega
which has
\mu
and

\nu
as marginals;

\gamma(A\times\Omega)=\mu(A)\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma(\Omega\times A)=\nu(A)

for every Borel set
A\subset\Omega.

Equation (
3.1
) defines a distance on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
which is called the
Wasserstein distance
and denoted by
W_{p}
.
Equipped with the Wasserstein distance,
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
is called the
Wasserstein space
.
We denote by
\Gamma_{o}(\mu,\nu)
the set of all
\gamma
which minimize the expression.

We say that a sequence of Borel probability measures
\{\mu_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
is narrowly convergent to
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
as

n\rightarrow\infty
if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\,d\mu_{n}(x)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\,d\mu(x)

for every function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}(\Omega)
, the space of continuous and bounded real functions defined on
\Omega
. It is well known
that narrow convergence is induced by a distance on
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)

(3.2)

\delta(\mu,\nu):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}\left|\int_{\Omega}f_{k}\,d\mu-\int_{\Omega}f_{k}\,d\nu\right|,

where
\{f_{k}\}
is a sequence of functions satisfying
\|f_{k}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
.

We recall

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\mu)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}\mu_{n}~\mbox{narrowly converges to }\mu~\mbox{ in }\mathcal{P}(\Omega),\\
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{p}\,d\mu_{n}(x)=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{p}\,d\mu(x).\end{cases}

Hence, if
\Omega
is compact then
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
is also a compact metric space.

Now, we introduce the notion of absolutely continuous curve and its relation with the continuity equation.

Definition 3.4
.

Let

be a curve.
We say that

is absolutely continuous and denote it by

, then the limit

|\sigma^{\prime}|(t):=\lim_{s\rightarrow t}\frac{W_{p}(\sigma(s),\sigma(t))}{|s-t|},

-a.e

. Moreover, the function

belongs to
L^{1}(0,T)
and satisfies

[

Proof.

5.
Existence of regular solutions

In this section, we construct a regular solution of (

)-(

) when the initial data

and the vector field

For this, we exploit a splitting method in the Wasserstein space

For carrying the splitting method, it requires a priori estimates, propagation of compact support, and Hölder continuity of the following form of homogeneous FDE:

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\varrho=\Delta(\epsilon+\varrho)^{m},\quad\text{ in }\ \Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\frac{\partial\varrho}{\partial\textbf{n}}=0,\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\quad\text{and}\quad\varrho(\cdot,0)=\varrho_{0},\text{ on }\ \Omega,\end{array}\right.

for

, and

, where

and the normal

to

.

First, we deliver a priori estimates of (
5.1
) in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

[

, Lemma 5.1]

Suppose that

-weak solution of (

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\epsilon+\varrho(\cdot,T))\log(\epsilon+\varrho(\cdot,T))\,dx+\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\left|\frac{\nabla(\epsilon+\varrho)^{m}}{\epsilon+\varrho}\right|^{2}(\epsilon+\varrho)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}(\epsilon+\varrho_{0})\log(\epsilon+\varrho_{0})\,dx+c\int_{\Omega}(\epsilon+\varrho_{0})\,dx.

Lemma B.1

(Speed estimate of PME)

\begin{gathered}V\in L_{x,t}^{q_{1},q_{2}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{T})\ \text{ where }\ \frac{d}{q_{1}}+\frac{2+q_{m,d}}{q_{2}}=1+\frac{d(q+m-2)}{2q}\vskip 2.84526pt\\

First, by testing

AIYAR, SIR SHESHADRI (1845–1901), native statesman of
Mysore, India, was the son of a Brahman of Palghat in the
district of Malabar. He was educated at the provincial school
at Calicut and the presidency college in Madras, and entered the
government service as a translator. In 1868 he was transferred
to Mysore under Runga Charlu; and for thirteen years filled
various offices in that state; but when Mysore was restored to
native rule in 1881, he became personal assistant to Runga Charlu,
whom he succeeded as diwan in 1885. For the next seventeen years

he laboured assiduously to promote the economic and industrial development of the state, and proved an able assistant to the Maharaja Chamarajendra. By means of railway, irrigation and mining works, he added greatly to the wealth of the state, and put it on a sound financial footing. He retired in 1900, was made K.C.S.I. in 1893 and died on the 13th of September 1901.

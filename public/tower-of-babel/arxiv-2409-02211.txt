multiplicity-free covering of a graded manifold
Elizaveta Vishnyakova

Abstract
. We define and study a multiplicity-free covering of a graded manifold. We compute its deck transformation group, which is isomorphic to the permutation group S n S_{n} . We show that it is not possible to construct a covering of a graded manifold in the category of n n -fold vector bundles. As an application of our research, we give a new conceptual proof of the equivalence of the categories of graded manifolds and symmetric n n -fold vector bundles.



1.
Introduction

Let
H
be a finitely generated abelian group together with a homomorphism
\phi:H\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for supermanifolds (=
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded manifolds) or a homomorphism
\psi:H\to\mathbb{Z}
for
\mathbb{Z}
-graded manifolds. In
[
Vi2
]
a graded covering of a supermanifold was constructed corresponding to the homomorphism
H=\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
n\mapsto\bar{n}
, where
\bar{n}
is the parity of the integer
n
. This graded covering is an infinite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded manifold, which is an extension of a construction suggested by Donagi and Witten in

[
DW1
,
DW2
]
. Furthermore, in
[
SV
]
the graded coverings for supermanifolds corresponding to homomorphisms
\phi:H\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where
H
is a finite abelian group, were constructed and studied. These constructions are related to the notion of
arc space
or
loop space
, see, for example,
[
KV
]
,

In this paper, we construct the graded covering for the homomorphism

\chi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z},\quad(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\longmapsto k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}

of multiplicity-free type; see the definitions in the main text. More precisely, we define the category of multiplicity-free manifolds, which is equivalent to the category of
n
-fold vector bundles. Furthermore, we prove that for any graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
there exists a unique up to isomorphism multiplicity-free covering
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathcal{N}
together with the covering projection
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
. In more detail for any graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
we construct a unique up to isomorphism object
\mathcal{P}
in the category of multiplicity-free manifolds, which satisfies a universal property as in the topological case.

In
[
CM
,
JL
]
for
n=2
and in
[
BGR
,
HJ
,
Vi1
,
C
]
for any integer
n\geq 2
, it was shown that the category of graded manifolds is equivalent to the category of symmetric
n
-fold vector bundles, that is,
n
-fold vector bundles with an action of the permutation group
S_{n}
. (The idea of considering a symmetric
n
-fold vector bundle appeared independently in
[
CM
]
and
[
BGR
]
.) As an application of our construction, we give a new conceptual proof of this result. For example, we show that, in fact,
S_{n}
is the fundamental group or the deck transformation group of the covering
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
.

Consider the following classical example of a topological covering

\mathsf{p}:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1},\quad\mathsf{p}(x)=\exp(2\pi ix).

Let
f
be a continuous function on
S^{1}
. Clearly,
\mathsf{p}^{*}(f)
is a
1
-periodic function on
\mathbb{R}
. Conversely, for any
1
-periodic function
F
on
\mathbb{R}
there exists a unique function
f
on
S^{1}
such that
F=\mathsf{p}^{*}(f)
. We can reformulate the last statement in several different equivalent ways: the function
F
is
1
-periodic; the function
F
is
\mathbb{Z}
-invariant, where the action of
\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathbb{R}
is given in the natural way; the function
F
is invariant with respect to the following group of diffeomorphisms

\operatorname{Deck}(\mathsf{p})=\{\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\,\,|\,\,\mathsf{p}\circ\Phi=\mathsf{p}\}\simeq\mathbb{Z},

which is called the deck transformation group or the covering transformation group. An analog of this group we define for our multiplicity-free covering
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
. In detail, we define the deck transformation group
\operatorname{Deck}(\chi)
, where
\chi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
is as above, for our covering and we show that for the covering of multiplicity-free type we have

\operatorname{Deck}(\chi)\simeq S_{n}.

This explains, why one considers symmetric
n
-fold vector bundles.

Let us describe our ideas in more detail. First of all, we replace the category of
n
-fold vector bundles considered by
[
CM
,
JL
,
BGR
,
HJ
,
Vi1
,
C
]
with an equivalent category of multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta_{n}
, see the main text for definitions. We also consider the category of
n
-fold vector bundles of type
\Delta\subset\Delta_{n}
and the corresponding category of multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta
. Denote
L=\Delta/S_{n}
, where
S_{n}
is the permutation group acting on
\Delta
. We show that in the category of multiplicity-free manifolds any graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
of type
L
can be assigned its multiplicity-free covering
\mathcal{P}
of type
\Delta
. The multiplicity-free covering
\mathcal{P}
satisfies a universal property as in the topological case. We also show that a covering of a graded manifold does not exist in the category of
n
-fold vector bundle.

Let
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
be the covering projection. We show that
\mathcal{P}
possesses an action of the deck transformation group
\operatorname{Deck}(\chi)\simeq S_{n}
, in other words,
\mathcal{P}
is a symmetric multiplicity-free manifold.
Furthermore, as in the case of
\mathsf{p}:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
, for any graded function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}
the image
\mathsf{p}^{*}(f)
is
\operatorname{Deck}(\chi)
-invariant. And, conversely, if a function
F\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}
is
\operatorname{Deck}(\chi)
-invariant, then
F=\mathsf{p}^{*}(f)
for some graded function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}
.

Further, we prove that any symmetric multiplicity-free manifold can be regarded as a multiplicity-free covering of a graded manifold. In addition, if
\psi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
is a morphism of graded manifolds, then there exists a unique multiplicity-free lift
\Psi:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
\psi
, which commutes with the covering projections
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
and
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. The lift
\Psi
is symmetric or
\operatorname{Deck}(\chi)
-equivariant. In addition, for any
\operatorname{Deck}(\chi)
-equivariant morphism

there exists a unique morphism
\psi^{\prime}:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
such that
\Psi^{\prime}
is its multiplicity-free lift.

Algebraically, a description of
\mathcal{O}^{S_{n}}_{\mathcal{P}}
is related to Chevalley – Shephard – Todd Theorem, see Section
4

S_{n}
-invariant polynomials modulo multiplicities is generated by linear
S_{n}
-invariant polynomials.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Graded manifolds

The theory of graded manifolds is used, for example, in modern mathematical physics and Poisson geometry. More information on this theory can be found in
[
F
,
J
,
JKPS
,
KS
,
R
,
Vys
]
. Throughout this paper, we work on the fields
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.
To define a graded manifold let us consider a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded finite dimensional vector superspace
V
of the following form:

(1)

V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{n}.

We put
L_{n}:=\{0,\beta,2\beta\ldots,n\beta\}
, where
\beta
is a formal even or odd variable, the parity of
\beta
is fixed, and

L:=\{k\beta\in L_{n}\,\,|\,\,\dim V_{k}\neq 0\}.

We will call
L
support of
V
, or we will say that
V
is of type
L
.

For any homogeneous element
v\in V_{i}\setminus\{0\}
we assign the weight
\operatorname{w}(v):=i\beta\in L
and the parity
|v|=\bar{i}\cdot|\beta|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
, where
|\beta|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the parity of
\beta
and
\bar{i}
is the parity of
i
. Denote by
S^{*}(V)
the super-symmetric algebra of
V
. If
v=v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\in S^{*}(V)
is a product of homogeneous elements
v_{i}\in V_{q_{i}}\setminus\{0\}
, then as usual we put

\displaystyle\operatorname{w}(v)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{w}(v_{1})+\cdots+\operatorname{w}(v_{k})=(q_{1}+\cdots+q_{k})\beta;

\displaystyle|v|

\displaystyle=|v_{1}|+\cdots+|v_{k}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

We also have

v_{1}\cdot v_{2}=(-1)^{|v_{1}||v_{2}|}v_{2}\cdot v_{1}.

Therefore
S^{*}(V)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector superspace.

Now we are ready to define a graded manifold.
Consider a ringed space
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}})
, where
\mathcal{U}_{0}\subset V^{*}_{0}
is an open set and

(2)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}\otimes_{S^{*}(V_{0})}S^{*}(V).

Here,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}
is the sheaf of smooth or holomorphic functions on
\mathcal{U}_{0}
. (Note that the tensor product in (
2
) is considered in the category of sheaves, so the result
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}}
is a sheaf, not only a presheaf.) We call the ringed space
\mathcal{U}
a
graded domain of type
L
and of dimension
\{n_{k}\}
, where
n_{k}:=\dim V_{k}
,
k=0,\ldots,n
. Further, let us choose a basis
(x_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n_{0}
, in
V_{0}
and a basis
(\xi_{j_{k}}^{k})
, where
j_{k}=1,\ldots,n_{k}
, in
V_{k}
for any
k=1,\ldots,n
. Then the system
(x_{i},\xi_{j_{k}}^{k})
is called a system of local graded coordinates in
\mathcal{U}
. Recall that,
x_{i}
has weight
0
and parity
\bar{0}
and
\xi_{j_{k}}^{k}
has weight
\operatorname{w}(\xi_{j_{k}}^{k})=k\beta
and parity
|\xi_{j_{k}}^{k}|=\bar{k}|\beta|
.

The sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}}=(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}})_{\bar{1}}
is naturally
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded. This sheaf is also
\mathbb{Z}
-graded in the following sense: for any element
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}}(U)
, where
U\subset\mathcal{U}_{0}
is open, and any point
x\in U
there exists an open neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
x
such that
f|_{U^{\prime}}
is a finite sum of homogeneous polynomials in coordinates
(\xi_{j_{k}}^{k})
with functional coefficient in
(x_{i})
. The element
f
defined in
U
may be an infinite sum of homogeneous elements.

Let
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
be two graded domains with graded coordinates
(x_{a},\xi_{b_{i}}^{i})
and
(y_{c},\eta_{d_{j}}^{j})
, respectively. A
morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
of graded domains
is a morphism of the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed spaces such that
\Phi^{*}|_{(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}})_{0}}:(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}})_{0}\to(\Phi_{0})_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}})_{0}
is local, that is, it is a usual morphism of smooth or holomorphic domains. Clearly, such a morphism is determined by images of local coordinates
\Phi^{*}(y_{c})
and
\Phi^{*}(\eta_{d_{j}}^{j})
. Conversely, if we have the following set of functions

(3)

\Phi^{*}(y_{c})=F_{c}\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}})_{0}(\mathcal{U}_{0})\quad\text{and }\quad\Phi^{*}(\eta_{d_{j}}^{j})=F_{d_{j}}^{j}\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}})_{j}(\mathcal{U}_{0}),\quad j>0,

such that
(F_{1}(u),\ldots,F_{n_{0}}(u))\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{0}
for any
u\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
, than there exists unique morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
of graded domains compatible with (
3
).

A
graded manifold of type
L
and of dimension
\{n_{k}\}
,
k=0,\ldots,n
,
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed space
\mathcal{N}=(\mathcal{N}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}})
, which is locally isomorphic to a graded domain of degree
n
and of dimension
\{n_{k}\}
,
k=0,\ldots,n
. More precisely, we can find an atlas
\{U_{i}\}
on
\mathcal{N}_{0}
and isomorphisms
\Phi_{i}:(U_{i},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}|_{U_{i}})\to\mathcal{U}_{i}
of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed spaces such that
\Phi_{i}\circ(\Phi_{j})^{-1}:\mathcal{U}_{j}\to\mathcal{U}_{i}
is an isomorphism of graded domains. A
morphism of graded manifolds
\Phi=(\Phi_{0},\Phi^{*}):\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}_{1}
is a morphism of the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed spaces, which is locally a morphism of graded domains.

2.2.
n
-fold vector bundles

We define an
n
-fold vector bundle using the language of graded manifolds. This definition of an
n
-fold vector bundle is equivalent to a classical one as shown in
[
GR
, Theorem 4.1]
, see also
[
Vo1
]
. Let us choose
n
formal generators
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
of the same parity
|\alpha_{1}|=\cdots=|\alpha_{n}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In other words, all
\alpha_{i}
are even or odd. Denote by
\Delta_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
the set of all possible linear combinations of
\alpha_{i}
with coefficient
0
or
1
. Such linear combinations are what we will call
multiplicity-free
. For example we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}=\{0,\,\alpha_{1}\},\quad\Delta_{2}=\{0,\,\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2},\,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\},

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}=\{0,\,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2},\,\alpha_{3},\,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3},\,\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},\,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}\}.

A subset
\Delta\subset\Delta_{n}
, which contains
0
, we will call a
multiplicity-free weight system
. In addition, the parity
|\delta|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
of any
\delta=\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{p}}\in\Delta
is the sum of the parities of the terms
\alpha_{i_{j}}
. We denote by
\sharp\delta
the
length of the weight
\delta\in\Delta
. More precisely, we put

\sharp\delta=\sharp(\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{p}})=p.

Let us take a multiplicity-free weight system
\Delta
with fixed parities of
\alpha_{i}
. (Recall that we assume that all
\alpha_{i}
have the same parity.) Consider the following finite-dimensional

\Delta
-graded vector space
V
over
\mathbb{K}

V=\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta}V_{\delta}.

We say that the elements of
V_{\delta}\setminus\{0\}
have weight
\delta\in\Delta
and parity
|\delta|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Furthermore, we denote by
S^{*}(V)
the super-symmetric power of
V
. The weight of a product of homogeneous elements is the sum of weights of factors, and the same for parities.
S^{*}(V)
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded vector space with respect to the weight of elements.

Consider the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded ringed space
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})
, where
\mathcal{V}_{0}\subset V^{*}_{0}
, and the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
is defined in the following way

(4)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}:=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}\otimes_{S^{*}(V_{0})}S^{*}(V).

Here
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}
is the sheaf of smooth (the case
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
) or holomorphic (the case
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
) functions on
\mathcal{V}_{0}\subset V^{*}_{0}
. (Note that the tensor product in (
4
) is considered in the category of sheaves, so the result
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
is a sheaf, not only a presheaf.) The sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded in the same sense as the structure sheaf of a graded manifold, see Section

2.1
.

Let us choose a basis
(x_{i})
in
V_{0}
, where
i=1,\ldots,\dim V_{0}
, and a basis
(t_{j_{\delta}}^{\delta})
, where
j_{\delta}=1,\ldots,\dim V_{\delta}
, in any
V_{\delta}
for any
\delta\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}
. Then the system
(x_{i},t_{j_{\delta}}^{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}}
is called the system of local coordinates in
\mathcal{V}
. We assign the weight
0
and the parity
\bar{0}
to any
x_{i}
and the weight
\delta
and the parity
|\delta|
to any
t_{j_{\delta}}^{\delta}
.
We will call the ringed space
\mathcal{V}
a
graded domain of type
\Delta
, with fixed parity
|\alpha_{i}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(independent on
i
) and of dimension
\{\dim V_{\delta}\}_{\delta\in\Delta}
or just a
graded domain of type
\Delta
.

A
morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of graded domains of type
\Delta
is a morphism of the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded ringed spaces such that
\Phi^{*}|_{(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}})_{0}}:(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}})_{0}\to(\Phi_{0})_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})_{0}
is local, that is, it is a usual morphism of smooth or holomorphic domains. Clearly, such a morphism is determined by images
\Phi^{*}(y_{c})
and
\Phi^{*}(q_{s_{\delta}}^{\delta})
of local graded coordinates
(y_{c},q_{s_{\delta}}^{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}}
of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. Conversely, if the following set of functions is given

(5)

\Phi^{*}(y_{c})=F_{c}\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})_{0}(\mathcal{V}_{0})\quad\text{and }\quad\Phi^{*}(q_{s_{\delta}}^{\delta})=F_{s_{\delta}}^{\delta}\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})_{\delta}(\mathcal{V}_{0}),

such that
(F_{1}(u),\ldots,F_{\dim V_{0}}(u))\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{0}
for any
u\in\mathcal{V}_{0}
, than there exists unique morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of graded domains of type
\Delta
compatible with (
5
).

A
graded manifold of type
\Delta
, with fixed parity
|\alpha_{i}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(independent on
i
) and of dimension
\{\dim V_{\delta}\}
,
\delta\in\Delta
,
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded ringed space
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}})
, that is locally isomorphic to a graded domain of type
\Delta
, with fixed parity
|\alpha_{i}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and of dimension
\{\dim V_{\delta}\}
,
\delta\in\Delta
. More precisely, we can find an atlas
\{V_{i}\}
of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
and isomorphisms
\Phi_{i}:(V_{i},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}|_{V_{i}})\to\mathcal{V}_{i}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded ringed spaces such that
\Phi_{i}\circ(\Phi_{j})^{-1}:\mathcal{V}_{j}\to\mathcal{V}_{i}
is an isomorphism of graded domains of type
\Delta
. Sometimes
\mathcal{D}
will be just called a
graded manifold of type
\Delta
.

A
morphism of graded manifolds
\Phi=(\Phi_{0},\Phi^{*}):\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}_{1}
of type
\Delta
is a morphism of the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded ringed spaces, which is locally a morphism of graded domains of type
\Delta
. Given a graded manifold of type
\Delta\subset\Delta_{n}
, then we can define in a unique way up to isomorphism an
n
-fold vector bundle, see
[
GR
, Theorem 4.1]
. Any
n
-fold vector bundle is obtained in this way.

2.3.
Multiplicity-free manifolds

The category of multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta
is a category, which is equivalent to the category of
n
-fold vector bundles of type
\Delta
. Let
\Delta
be as in Section
2.2
and let
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}})
be a graded manifold of type
\Delta
with fixed parity
|\alpha_{i}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(independent on
i
) and of dimension
\{\dim V_{\delta}\}
,
\delta\in\Delta
, also as in Section
2.2
. Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
be a homogeneous element of weight
\gamma
. We call the element
f

non-multiplicity-free
, if
\gamma
is not a multiplicity-free weight. Now denote by
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
the sheaf of ideals generated locally by non-multiplicity-free elements. For example, if
t^{\gamma}_{1}
and
t_{1}^{\gamma}
are local coordinates of
\mathcal{D}
of weight
\gamma\in\Delta
, then
t_{1}^{\gamma}\cdot t_{2}^{\gamma}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}
, since this product has weight
2\gamma
.

Definition 1
.

The ringed space
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}:=(\mathcal{D}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}})
is called a multiplicity-free manifold of type
\Delta
with fixed parity
|\alpha_{i}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(independent on
i
) and of dimension
\{\dim V_{\delta}\}
,
\delta\in\Delta
.

If
\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}
are
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded manifolds of type
\Delta
and
\Phi=(\Phi_{0},\Phi^{*}):\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}
is a morphism that preserves weights, then the morphism
\widehat{\Phi}=(\Phi_{0},\widehat{\Phi}^{*}):\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{1}\to\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{2}
, where

\widehat{\Phi}^{*}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
is naturally defined.
We define the category of
multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta
, as the category with objects
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and with morphisms
\widehat{\Phi}
. Clearly,
\widehat{\Phi}_{2}\circ\widehat{\Phi}_{1}=\widehat{\Phi_{2}\circ\Phi_{1}}
.

Remark 2
.

Let
\Phi:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
be a morphism of graded manifolds of type
\Delta
. Any such morphism is locally defined by the images
\Phi^{*}(y_{c}),\Phi^{*}(q_{s_{\delta}}^{\delta})
of local coordinates; see (
5
). Since
\Phi^{*}(y_{c}),\Phi^{*}(q_{s_{\delta}}^{\delta})
has multiplicity-free weights, the morphism
\widehat{\Phi}
is locally defined by the same formulas. Furthermore, if
\Phi_{i}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
,
i=1,2
, are two different morphisms, then
\widehat{\Phi}_{1}\neq\widehat{\Phi}_{2}
, since these morphisms are different in an open set. Since the functor
\mathcal{D}\mapsto\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
,
\Phi\mapsto\widehat{\Phi}
defines an equivalence of the category of graded manifolds of type
\Delta
and the category of multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta
, see Definition
40
.

Remark 3
.

A multiplicity-free manifold
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
of type
\Delta
is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded, since the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}
is generated by homogeneous elements.
In addition, the structure sheaves of
\mathcal{D}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
are different. In the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
we have

(\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}+\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{1})\cdot(\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{2}+\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{2})=\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{2}+\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{1}\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{2}+\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{2}+\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{1}\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{2}.

In the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}}
we have

(\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}+\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{1})\cdot(\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{2}+\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{2})=\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{1}\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{2}+\xi^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\xi^{\alpha_{2}}_{2}.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}

Let us give another definition of a multiplicity-free covering of a graded domain.

Definition 9
.

A multiplicity-free covering of type
\Delta
of a graded domain
\mathcal{U}
of type
L
is a multiplicity-free manifold
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of type
\Delta
together with a morphism
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}:\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{U}
such that for any multiplicity-free manifold
\mathcal{M}
of type
\Delta
and a morphism
\phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{U}
there exists unique morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of multiplicity-free manifolds of type

such that the following diagram is commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{p}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

In other words a multiplicity-free covering of type
\Delta
of
\mathcal{U}
is a multiplicity-free manifold
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of type
\Delta
together with a covering projection
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}
satisfying the universal property (

).

.

Two definitions of multiplicity-free coverings of type

3.3.
Coverings, homomorphisms and fundamental groups

In Introduction we mentioned that the multiplicity-free covering corresponds to the following homomorphism

(8)

\chi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z},\quad(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\longmapsto k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}.

In more detail, above, we constructed the multiplicity-free covering
\mathcal{P}
of type
\Delta
for any graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
of type
L=\Delta/S_{n}
. The covering projection
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
is locally given by Formulas (
6
). We can rewrite these formulas in the following way

x_{i}\mapsto y_{i},\quad\mathsf{p}^{*}(\xi^{k}_{j_{k}})=\sum_{\delta\in\chi^{-1}(k)\cap\Delta}t^{\delta}_{j_{k}},\quad k\beta\in L\setminus\{0\}.

Summing up, locally the covering projection is defined by the corresponding homomorphism and the type
\Delta
of the covering. The covering constructed in
[
Vi2
]
corresponds to the homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
n\mapsto\bar{n}
, and the type
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
, while the covering constructed in
[
SV
]
corresponds to a homomorphism
H\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where
H
is any finite abelian group, and the type
H
.

Furthermore, for the homomorphism
\chi
and the type
\Delta
we can define the deck transformation group or the covering transformation group in the following way.

Definition 19
.

The deck transformation group or the covering transformation group of
\chi
of type
\Delta
is the following group

\operatorname{Deck}(\chi,\Delta)=\{A\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\,\,|\,\,\chi\circ A=\chi,\,\,A(\Delta)=\Delta\}.

Clearly, this definition is applicable to any homomorphism
\phi
and any type
\Delta
.

Proposition 20
.

We have
\operatorname{Deck}(\chi,\Delta)\simeq S_{n}
.

Proof.

First, we have
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Secondly, let us take a generator
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
. Then we have

\displaystyle 1\beta=\chi(\alpha_{i})=\chi\circ A(\alpha_{i})=\chi(\sum_{j}a_{ij}\alpha_{j})=\sum_{j}a_{ij}\beta.

Since
A(\Delta)=\Delta
, we have
a_{ij}\geq 0
. Hence, only one integer, say
a_{ij_{0}}
, is equal to
1
and the others are
0
. We see that
A(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{j_{0}}
. In other words,
A\in S_{n}
. Clearly,
S_{n}\subset\operatorname{Deck}(\chi,\Delta)
. The proof is complete.
∎

are equal.

Lemma 22
.

(1)
Let
\delta=\sum\delta_{i}
be a decomposition of a multiplicity-free weight

\delta=\alpha_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{i_{k}}\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}

\delta_{i}\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}

and

.

(2)
If

for some

, then
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let us prove

. We can always assume that
\sharp\delta_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\sharp\delta_{q}
. Secondly,
\delta
is multiplicity-free, so we can find

such that
(s\cdot\delta_{1},\ldots,s\cdot\delta_{q})
is of the form (

).

Let us prove

and

Definition 23
.

We call a monomial
T=t^{\gamma_{1}}_{i_{1}}\cdots t^{\gamma_{q}}_{i_{q}}
primitive if
(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{q})\in\Lambda
,
\sharp\gamma_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\sharp\gamma_{q}
and the equality
\sharp\gamma_{j}=\sharp\gamma_{j+1}
implies
i_{j}\leq i_{j+1}
.

Consider some examples.

Example 24
.

The monomials
t_{i}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3}}\cdot t_{j}^{\alpha_{2}}
,
t_{i}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\cdot t_{j}^{\alpha_{2}}
and
t_{2}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}
are not primitive.

Example 25
.

Let
n=2
.
The monomial
t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}
is primitive. If
t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}
is even (hence
t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}
is also even), we have

\sum_{s\in S_{2}}s\cdot(t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}})=t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}+t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}=2t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}.

If
t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}
is odd (hence
t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}
is also odd), we have

\sum_{s\in S_{2}}s\cdot(t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}})=t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}+t_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdot t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}=0.

We generalize the observation of Example
25
in the following lemma.

Lemma 26
.

Let
T=t^{\gamma_{1}}_{i_{1}}\cdots t^{\gamma_{q}}_{i_{q}}
be a primitive monomial. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

We have
\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T=0
.

(2)

The monomial
T
contains two odd factors
t^{\gamma_{p}}_{i_{p}}
and
t^{\gamma_{q}}_{i_{q}}
such that
\sharp\gamma_{p}=\sharp\gamma_{q}
and
i_{p}=i_{q}
.

Proof.

Let (2) holds. Denote
m:=\sharp\gamma_{p}-1
. Then

\gamma_{p}=\alpha_{a_{1}}+\alpha_{a_{1}+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{a_{1}+m},\quad\gamma_{q}=\alpha_{b_{1}}+\alpha_{b_{1}+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{b_{1}+m}.

Since
T
is multiplicity-free, we define
s^{\prime}\in S_{n}
by
s^{\prime}(\alpha_{a_{1}+i})=\alpha_{b_{1}+i}
,
s^{\prime}(\alpha_{b_{1}+i})=\alpha_{a_{1}+i}
for any
i=0,\ldots,m
and
s^{\prime}(\alpha_{j})=\alpha_{j}
, for other
j
. We have
(s^{\prime})^{2}=\mathrm{id}
. Hence,
S^{\prime}:=\{\mathrm{id},s^{\prime}\}\subset S_{n}
is a subgroup. Therefore,
S_{n}
is divided into
S^{\prime}
-orbits with respect to the right action
S^{\prime}
on
S_{n}
, which do not intersect. Hence the sum
\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T
can be written as a sum of
s\cdot T+s\cdot(s^{\prime}\cdot T)
for some
s\in S_{n}
.
Further, for any
s\in S_{n}
we have

s\cdot T+s\cdot(s^{\prime}\cdot T)=s\cdot(T+s^{\prime}\cdot T)=s\cdot(T-T)=0.

Hence,
\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T=0
.

Now assume that
\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T=0
, but (2) does not hold. Since the sum is
0
, the monomial
T
has to cancel with a monomial
s\cdot T
for some
s\in S_{n}
. This implies that there are at least two weights
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}
with
\sharp\gamma_{i}=\sharp\gamma_{j}
. Indeed, if
\sharp\gamma_{i}\neq\sharp\gamma_{j}
for any
i\neq j
, then

s\cdot t^{\gamma_{1}}_{i_{1}}\cdots t^{\gamma_{q}}_{i_{q}}=t^{s\cdot\gamma_{1}}_{i_{1}}\cdots t^{s\cdot\gamma_{q}}_{i_{q}}=-T

is possible only if
s\cdot\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i}
for any
i
. But in this case
s\cdot T=+T
.

Now assume that all
\gamma_{j}
have the same length. Note that any
s\in S_{n}
permutes the weights with the same length. Since
\gamma_{j}
have all the same length, all
t_{i_{j}}^{\gamma_{j}}
are even or they all are odd. If they all are even we may only have

t_{i_{1}}^{s\cdot\gamma_{1}}\cdots t_{i_{q}}^{s\cdot\gamma_{q}}=+T,

hence
T
and
s\cdot T
cannot cancel.
If they all are odd, and
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}
(the indexes are pairwise different), then
t_{i_{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots t_{i_{q}}^{\gamma_{q}}
and
t_{i_{1}}^{s\cdot\gamma_{1}}\cdots t_{i_{q}}^{s\cdot\gamma_{q}}
cannot cancel since these monomials contain different variables or since
s\cdot\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i}
for any
i
and
t_{i_{1}}^{s\cdot\gamma_{1}}\cdots t_{i_{q}}^{s\cdot\gamma_{q}}=+T
. In this case the proof is complete.

Let
\sharp\gamma_{j_{1}}=\cdots=\sharp\gamma_{j_{p}}
and other
\gamma_{j}
have different length with
\gamma_{j_{1}}
. We can write
T=T_{1}\cdot T_{2}
, where
T_{1}=t_{i_{j_{1}}}^{\gamma_{j_{1}}}\cdots t_{i_{j_{1}}}^{\gamma_{j_{p}}}
. Assuming

s\cdot T=s\cdot T_{1}\cdot s\cdot T_{2}=-T_{1}\cdot T_{2},

we get that
s\cdot T_{1}=\pm T_{1}
and
s\cdot T_{1}=\pm T_{1}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
s\cdot T_{1}=-T_{1}
and
s\cdot T_{2}=+T_{2}
. By above this implies that (2) holds.
∎

Let
F\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
be a
S_{n}
-invariant
\mathbb{Z}
-homogeneous function. Since
\mathcal{V}
is a multiplicity-free domain,
F
is a polynomial in variables
(t_{i_{\delta}}^{\delta})
with functional coefficients in
(y_{i})
. As any polynomial,
F
is a sum of (different) monomials of multiplicity-free weight.

Lemma 27
.

Let
T^{\prime}=t^{\delta_{1}}_{i_{1}}\cdots t^{\delta_{q}}_{i_{q}}
, where
\delta_{j}\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}
, be a monomial of a multiplicity-free weight. That is
\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{q}
is multiplicity-free. Then

\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T^{\prime}=\pm\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T,

where
T
is a primitive monomial. Moreover, if
\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T^{\prime}\neq 0
, then
T
is unique.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\sharp\delta_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\sharp\delta_{q}
and that if
\sharp\delta_{j}=\sharp\delta_{j+1}
, then
i_{j}\leq i_{j+1}
. Since
T^{\prime}
is multiplicity-free, we can find an
s\in S_{n}
, which sends
\delta_{i}
to
\gamma_{i}
, where
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{q}
are of the form (
9
). Clearly
T=s\cdot T^{\prime}
is primitive.
Furthermore, we have

\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T.

If this sum is not
0
, then
T
is unique. In fact, assume that we have another primitive monomial
\tilde{T}=s_{0}\cdot T
for some
s_{0}\in S_{n}
. Then,

s_{0}\cdot T=t^{s_{0}\cdot\gamma_{1}}_{i_{1}}\cdots t^{s_{0}\cdot\gamma_{q}}_{i_{q}}=\tilde{T}.

By Lemma
22
,
T
and
\tilde{T}
have the same weight. Note that
s_{0}
permutes weights of equal length. By Lemma
26
, if
\sharp\gamma_{p}=\sharp\gamma_{p+1}
and
|t^{\gamma_{p}}_{i_{p}}|=\bar{1}
, then
i_{p}<i_{p+1}
. Hence,
\tilde{T}
is primitive only if
s_{0}\cdot\gamma_{j}=\gamma_{j}
, if
|\gamma_{j}|=\bar{1}
. But in this case

.
∎

Definition 28
.

We call an element
F\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}

Lemma 29
.

Any
S_{n}
-invariant multiplicity-free polynomial

can be written in a unique way in the following form

(10)

F=A_{1}(y_{i})\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T_{1}+\cdots+A_{m}(y_{i})\sum\limits_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T_{m},

A_{j}(y_{i})\neq 0

We write

\displaystyle F=\frac{1}{|S_{n}|}\sum_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot F=\frac{1}{|S_{n}|}B_{1}(y_{i})\sum_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot P_{1}+\cdots+\frac{1}{|S_{n}|}B_{q}(y_{i})\sum_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot P_{q}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pm 1}{|S_{n}|}B_{1}(y_{i})\sum_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T_{1}+\cdots+\frac{\pm 1}{|S_{n}|}B_{q}(y_{i})\sum_{s\in S_{n}}s\cdot T_{q},

∎

Let
(\xi^{k}_{j_{k}})_{k\in L\setminus\{0\}}

Let

Lemma 31

Let
\xi^{k_{1}}_{i_{1}}\cdots\xi^{k_{p}}_{i_{p}}\neq 0

Since

.

\mathsf{p}^{*}:(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}})_{k}\to(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}^{S_{n}})_{k}

For
k=0

.

and

5.
Symmetric multiplicity-free manifolds

First of all, let us define a symmetric multiplicity-free domain. Let
\Delta\subset\Delta_{n}
be
S_{n}
-invariant and
\mathcal{V}
be a multiplicity-free domain of type
\Delta
as in Remark
12
. Recall that
\mathcal{V}
has local coordinates
(y_{i},t_{j_{\delta}}^{\delta})_{\delta\in\Delta\setminus\{0\}}
and the following action is defined
s\cdot t_{j}^{\delta}=t_{j}^{s\cdot\delta}
, where
s\in S_{n}
. This implies that we have an action on the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
. (Note that the functions of weight
0
are stable under this action.)
An element
F\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
is called
S_{n}
-invariant, if it is
S_{n}
-invariant in the usual sense, that is
s\cdot F=F
. Denote by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}^{S_{n}}
the subsheaf of
S_{n}
-invariant functions. More precisely, this subsheaf is defined by

V\mapsto[\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}(V)]^{S_{n}},

where
V
is an open subset in
\mathcal{V}_{0}
. A domain
\mathcal{V}
with a
S_{n}
-action is called symmetric.

Let
\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2}
be two symmetric domains and
\Phi:\mathcal{V}_{1}\to\mathcal{V}_{2}
be a morphism. The morphism
\Phi
is called
S_{n}
-invariant if

s\circ\Phi^{*}=\Phi^{*}\circ s

for any
s\in S_{n}
. A multiplicity-free manifold
\mathcal{M}
is called symmetric if we can cover
\mathcal{M}
with multiplicity-free domains
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
such that there exist isomorphisms
\phi_{\lambda}:\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\to\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
, where any
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
is symmetric, so that compositions
\phi_{\mu}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\lambda}
are
S_{n}
-invariant. In this case, we can define the subsheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}^{S_{n}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
of
S_{n}
-invariant elements. This atlas
\{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\}
is called symmetric. Two symmetric atlases are called equivalent if the transition functions between local charts are
S_{n}
-invariant. The union of all the charts of equivalent atlases is called a symmetric structure on
\mathcal{M}
.

A morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
is called symmetric if it is
S_{n}
-invariant in
S_{n}
-invariant local charts. This definition is independent of the choice between equivalent atlases.

6.

6.1.
Symmetric multiplicity-free domains and multiplicity-free coverings

We start with some properties of lifts of graded functions. Let
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{U}
be a multiplicity-free covering of type
\Delta
of a graded domain
\mathcal{U}
of type

with local coordinates as in Section

.

Remark 33
.

In
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}
there is a natural action of the group
S_{n}
, see Sections
4
and
5
. Therefore, any multiplicity-free covering
\mathcal{V}
of a graded domain

is a symmetric multiplicity-free domain.

Lemma 34
.

Let

s\cdot\mathsf{p}^{*}(f)=\mathsf{p}^{*}(f)
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of (
6
).
∎

Lemma 35
.

Let

\displaystyle s\circ\Phi^{*}\circ\mathsf{p}_{2}^{*}=s\circ\mathsf{p}_{1}^{*}\circ\phi^{*};\quad s\circ\Phi^{*}\circ s^{-1}\circ\mathsf{p}_{2}^{*}=\mathsf{p}_{1}^{*}\circ\phi^{*}.

Proof.

We use Proposition

6.2.
Symmetric multiplicity-free manifolds and multiplicity-free coverings

We start this section with the following theorem.

Theorem 37
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a graded manifold of type
L=\Delta/S_{n}
and
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
be its multiplicity-free covering of type
\Delta
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is a symmetric multiplicity-free manifold.

Further let
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
and
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}:\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
be multiplicity-free coverings of type
\Delta
of graded manifolds
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
of type
L
, respectively. Let
\phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
be a morphism of graded manifolds. By Theorem
16
there exists a unique multiplicity-free lift
\Phi:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of type
\Delta
. Then the morphism
\Phi
is
S_{n}
-invariant.

Proof.

The multiplicity-free covering
\mathcal{P}
of type
\Delta
was constructed in Section
3.2
. By definition
\mathcal{P}
can be covered by symmetric domains, see Remark
33
. Further the transition functions between these symmetric domains are
S_{n}
-invariant, see Lemma
35
.
Secondly, the morphism
\Phi
is
S_{n}
-invariant, since it is locally
S_{n}
-invariant, see Lemma
35
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 38
.

Let we have a symmetric multiplicity-free manifold
\mathcal{M}
.
Then
\mathcal{M}
can be regarded as a covering of a certain graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

To see this let us cover
\mathcal{M}
with symmetric charts
\mathcal{V}_{i}
as in Section
5
.
As we saw in Remark
12
, any
\mathcal{V}_{i}
is a multiplicity-free covering of a graded domain
\mathcal{U}_{i}
. Further if
\Psi_{ji}:\mathcal{V}_{i}\to\mathcal{V}_{j}
are transition functions, which are
S_{n}
-invariant, then by Theorem
36
, there exist unique morphisms
\psi_{ji}:\mathcal{U}_{i}\to\mathcal{U}_{j}
such that
\psi_{ji}\circ\mathsf{p}_{i}=\mathsf{p}_{j}\circ\Psi_{ji}
.

Denote
\mathcal{V}_{ijk}:=\mathcal{V}_{i}\cap\mathcal{V}_{j}\cap\mathcal{V}_{k}
. Then
\mathcal{V}_{ijk}
is a multiplicity-free covering of
\mathcal{U}_{ijk}:=\mathcal{U}_{i}\cap\mathcal{U}_{j}\cap\mathcal{U}_{k}
of type
\Delta
for the covering map
\mathsf{p}_{i}:\mathcal{V}_{ijk}\to\mathcal{U}_{ijk}
. Now consider the composition

\Psi_{ij}\circ\Psi_{jk}\circ\Psi_{ki}=\mathrm{id}.

It is a
S_{n}
-invariant automorphism of
\mathcal{V}_{ijk}
. Hence, by Theorem
36
there exists a unique graded automorphism
\psi_{ijk}
of
\mathcal{U}_{ijk}
commuting with
\mathsf{p}_{i}
. Consider the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}_{j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}_{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}_{j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{ki}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{p}_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{jk}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{p}_{k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{ij}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{p}_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{p}_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{ki}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{jk}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{ij}}

From one side
\psi_{ijk}=\mathrm{id}
. On the other hand, it is equal to
\psi_{ij}\circ\psi_{jk}\circ\psi_{ki}
. Since such a automorphism is unique, we get
\psi_{ij}\circ\psi_{jk}\circ\psi_{ki}=\mathrm{id}
. In other words, the data
\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
and
\{\psi_{ij}\}
define a graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
. The covering map
\mathsf{p}|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}=\mathsf{p}_{i}
is also well defined, as it commutes with the transition functions.
∎

Let
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
and
\mathsf{p}^{\prime}:\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
be multiplicity-free coverings of type
\Delta
of graded manifolds
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
of type
L=\Delta/S_{n}
, respectively.

Theorem 39
.

Let
\phi_{i}:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
,
i=1,2
, be morphisms of graded manifolds with the same lift
\Phi
. Then
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}
. Furthermore, if
\Psi:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a symmetric morphism of symmetric multiplicity-free manifolds, then there exists a morphism
\psi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
of graded manifolds such that the lift of
\psi
is
\Psi
.

Proof.

Let us prove the first statement. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
are graded domains, and
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{V}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
are multiplicity-free domains. By Theorem
36
, we have
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}
. Furthermore, again by Theorem
36
, the morphism
\psi
exists locally and in any chart it is unique. Hence
\psi
is globally defined.
∎

6.3.
Equivalence of the category of symmetric multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta
and graded manifolds of type
L=\Delta/S_{n}

Recall a definition of the equivalence of categories.

Definition 40
.

Two categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are called equivalent if there is a functor
\mathrm{F}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
such that:

•

\mathrm{F}
is full and faithful, that is,
Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(c_{1},c_{2})
is in bijection with

Hom_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{F}c_{1},\mathrm{F}c_{2})
.

•

\mathrm{F}
is essentially surjective, this is for any
a\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
there exists
b\in\mathcal{C}
such that
a
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{F}(b)
.

Above it was shown that the correspondence: graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
to its multiplicity-free covering
\mathcal{P}
of type
\Delta
, see Proposition
11
, Section
3.2
, and a graded morphism
\phi
to its multiplicity-free lift
\Phi
of type
\Delta
, see Theorem
16
, is a functor from the category of graded manifolds of type
L=\Delta/S_{n}
to the category of symmetric multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta
. We denote this functor by
\mathrm{Cov}
.

Theorem 41
.

The functor
\mathrm{Cov}
is an equivalence of the category of graded manifolds of type
L=\Delta/S_{n}
and the category of symmetric multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta
.

Proof.

By Theorem
37
the functor
\mathrm{Cov}
is a functor from the category of graded manifolds of type
L=\Delta/S_{n}
to the category of symmetric multiplicity-free manifolds of type
\Delta
.
From Theorem
38
it follows that
\mathrm{Cov}
is essentially surjective.
The functor
\mathrm{Cov}
is full and faithful by Theorem
39
.
∎

7.
About coverings of graded manifolds in the category of
n
-fold vector bundle

In this section, we show that a covering of a graded manifold in the category of symmetric
n
-fold vector bundles does not exist. (Therefore, to construct a covering we need to replace the category of symmetric
n
-fold vector bundles to the category of symmetric multiplicity free manifolds.) Assume that for any graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
of degree
n
, we can construct an
n
-fold vector bundle
\mathcal{Q}
together with a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded morphism
\mathsf{q}:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{N}
, which satisfies the universal property in the category of
n
-fold vector bundles. That is, for any
n
-fold vector bundle
\mathcal{D}
and any
\mathbb{Z}
-graded morphism
\phi:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{N}
, there exists a unique morphism
\Psi:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{Q}
of
n
-fold vector bundles such that
\phi=\mathsf{q}\circ\Phi
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathcal{N}
,
\mathcal{D}
are domains in the category of graded manifold and
n
-fold vector bundles, respectively. The lift
\Phi
of
\phi
is a morphism in the category of
n
-fold vector bundles, hence it preserves the sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{I}
locally generated by elements with multiplicities. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\phi^{*}\mod\mathcal{I}=(\Phi^{*}\mod\mathcal{I})\circ(\mathsf{q}^{*}\mod\mathcal{I}).

Denote
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}=(\mathcal{D}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}/\mathcal{I})
and
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{Q}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}}/\mathcal{I})
. By construction,
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mathcal{P}
are multiplicity-free manifolds. Let
\psi:\tilde{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathcal{N}
be a morphism. Clearly, we can find a morphism
\phi:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{N}
such that
\psi=\phi\mod\mathcal{I}
. Since
\mathcal{Q}
is a covering, we can find a unique lift
\Phi
of
\phi
. Hence,
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{N}
is a covering in the category of multiplicity-free domains, where
\mathsf{p}^{*}:=\mathsf{q}^{*}\mod\mathcal{I}
. In Section
3.1
we saw that such a covering projection has a special form in the standard local coordinates of
\mathcal{P}
. In addition,
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
have the same dimensions.

Now let
\mathcal{N}
be a graded domain with local graded coordinates
x,\xi^{1},\xi^{2}
,
\mathcal{D}
be a double vector bundle with local coordinates
y,\eta^{\alpha}_{1},\eta^{\alpha}_{2}
and
\phi:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{N}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded morphism defined by

\phi^{*}(x)=y,\quad\phi^{*}(\xi^{1})=0,\quad\phi^{*}(\xi^{2})=\eta^{\alpha}_{1}\eta^{\alpha}_{2}.

Then the covering projection
\mathsf{q}
must have the following form in the standard local coordinates

\mathsf{q}^{*}(\xi^{2})=F_{2\alpha}+t^{\alpha+\beta}+F_{2\beta},\quad\mathsf{q}^{*}(\xi^{1})=t^{\alpha}+t^{\beta},

where
F_{2\alpha}
and
F_{2\beta}
are functions of weights
2\alpha
and
2\beta
, respectively. Recall that
\mathsf{p}^{*}=\mathsf{q}^{*}\mod\mathcal{I}
. Furthermore, the morphism
\Phi^{*}
preserves all weights, hence we have

\Phi^{*}(F_{2\alpha})=\eta^{\alpha}_{1}\eta^{\alpha}_{2},\quad\Phi^{*}(t^{\alpha})=0.

Since
\mathcal{Q}
is a double vector bundle, we do not have local coordinates of weight
2\alpha
, therefore,
F_{2\alpha}\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}})_{\alpha}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}})_{\alpha}
. Since
\Phi^{*}(t^{\alpha})=0
,
\Phi^{*}(F_{2\alpha})=0
. This is a contradiction because

\eta^{\alpha}_{1}\eta^{\alpha}_{2}=\phi^{*}(\xi^{2})=\Phi^{*}\circ\mathsf{q}^{*}(\xi^{2})=0.

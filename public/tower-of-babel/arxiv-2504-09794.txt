Abstract
. We show that every sufficiently large oriented graph G G with minimum indegree and outdegree both at least ( 3 ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | − 1 ) / 8 (3|V(G)|-1)/8 contains every orientation of a Hamilton cycle. This result improves the approximate bound established by Kelly and resolves a long-standing problem posed by Häggkvist and Thomason in 1995. The degree condition is tight and it can be improved to ( 3 ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | − 4 ) / 8 (3|V(G)|-4)/8 for Hamilton cycles that are nearly directed, generalizing a classic result by Keevash, Kühn and Osthus. Additionally, we derive a pancyclicity result for arbitrary orientations. More precisely, the above degree condition suffices to guarantee the existence of cycles of every possible orientation and every possible length unless G G is isomorphic to one of the exceptional oriented graphs.



Keywords:

Orientation, Hamilton cycle, pancyclicity, randomised embedding, robust outexpander

1.
Introduction

Notation follows
[
2
]
so we only repeat a few definitions here (also see Section
2
). A
digraph
is not allowed to have parallel edges or loops and an
oriented graph
is a digraph with no cycle of length 2. The
minimum semidegree

\delta^{0}(G)
of a digraph
G
is the minimum of all the indegrees and outdegrees of the vertices in
G
. For a path or cycle in digraphs, we say that it is
directed
if all its edges are oriented in the same direction and, it is
antidirected
if it contains no directed path of length 2. Throughout the paper, we will write
n
for the number of vertices of the (di)graph
G
we are considering.

A fundamental result of Dirac
[
11
]
states that every graph
G
with minimum degree
n/2
contains a Hamilton cycle. A directed version of this result was obtained by Ghouila-Houri
[
17
]
, who showed that any digraph
G
with minimum semidegree
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant n/2
contains a directed Hamilton cycle. In
[
25
]
Keevash, Kühn and Osthus proved that the minimum semidegree threshold for an oriented graph
G
containing a directed Hamilton cycle turns out to be
(3n-4)/8
.

Instead of asking for a directed Hamilton cycle in digraphs, one may ask under certain conditions, if it is possible to show a digraph containing every possible orientation of a Hamilton cycle. For tournaments, Rosenfeld
[
34
]
conjectured that the directed Hamilton cycle is the only orientation of a Hamilton cycle that can be avoided by tournaments on arbitrarily many vertices. This conjecture was settled by Thomason
[
39
]
who proved that every tournament on
n\geqslant 2^{128}
vertices contains every oriented Hamilton cycle except possibly the directed Hamilton cycle. In
[
24
]
Havet showed that the number of vertices above can be lowered to 68. The question for general digraphs and oriented graphs was considered by Häggkvist and Thomason in 1995. They
[
21
]
proved that a minimum semidegree
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant n/2+n^{5/6}
ensures the existence of every possible orientation of a Hamilton cycle in a digraph
G
and
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant(5/12+o(1))n
suffices if
G
is oriented
[
22
]
. In
[
9
]
, Debiasio and Molla got the exact minimum semidegree threshold for an antidirected Hamilton cycle in digraphs, they showed that such a cycle exists if
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant n/2+1
. Later, Debiasio, Kühn, Molla, Osthus and Taylor
[
8
]
settled the problem by completely determining the exact threshold for every possible orientation of a Hamilton cycle in digraphs. They showed that
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant n/2
will suffice if the orientation is not antidirected and they believe that it would be interesting to obtain an exact version of this result in oriented graphs. Kelly
[
26
]
proved that an oriented graph
G
admits every possible orientation of a Hamilton cycle if
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant(3/8+o(1))n
. In this paper, we improve this approximate result by proving the following, which solves a problem of Häggkvist and Thomason in
[
22
]
(also see page 2 in
[
21
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists an integer
n_{0}
such that every oriented graph
G
on
n\geqslant n_{0}
vertices with minimum semidegree
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant(3n-1)/8
contains every possible orientation of a Hamilton cycle.

The degree condition can be improved to
(3n-4)/8
when the Hamilton cycle is close to being directed, which generalizes the classic result by Keevash, Kühn and Osthus
[
25
]
. The following proposition shows that if the oriented Hamilton cycle has a long antidirected subpath, then the degree bound in Theorem
1.1
is the best possible.

Proposition 1.2
.

For any
n\geqslant 3
, there is an
n
-vertex oriented graph
G
with minimum semidegree
\delta^{0}(G)=\lceil(3n-1)/8\rceil-1
which does not contain a cycle having an antidirected subpath of order larger than
3(n/4+1)
. In particular, it has no antidirected Hamilton cycle.

In this section, we provide some basic terminology and notation. For integers
a<b
,
[a]

\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that
f(n)\leqslant Kg(n)
for all sufficiently large
n
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a digraph. The order of
G
is the number of vertices in
G
, denoted by
|V(G)|
or
|G|
. We will denote an edge oriented from
u
to
v
by
uv
. We use
G[X]
to denote the subdigraph induced by a vertex set
X
. Let
G-X=G[V\backslash X]
. For
v\in V(G)
, we denote the set of outneighbors and inneighbors of
v
in
G
by
N^{+}(v)
and
N^{-}(v)
respectively, and we write
d^{+}(v)=|N^{+}(v)|
and
d^{-}(v)=|N^{-}(v)|
for the outdegree and indegree of
v
, respectively. For
S\subseteq V(G)
, we write
N^{-}(v,S)=N^{-}(v)\cap S
,
N^{+}(v,S)=N^{+}(v)\cap S
. Set
d^{-}(v,S)=|N^{-}(v,S)|
,
d^{+}(v,S)=|N^{+}(v,S)|
and
d(v,S)=d^{+}(v,S)+d^{-}(v,S)
. For two subsets
A,B
of
V(G)
, we use
E(A,B)
to denote the set of edges from
A
to
B
and write
E(A)
for
E(A,A)
. Set
e(A,B)=|E(A,B)|
and

.

We normally describe an oriented path or cycle
R
by listing its vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{l}
in the clockwise order and write
R=v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{l}(v_{1})
. The predecessor of
v_{i}
on
R
is the vertex
v_{i-1}
and is denoted by
v_{i}^{-}
. Similarly, the successor of
v_{i}
, denoted by
v_{i}^{+}
, is the vertex
v_{i+1}
. The length of

is the number of its edges. A

k
. For path

, the vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{l}
are called the initial and terminal of
R
, respectively. An
(A,B)
-path is a path with the initial vertex belonging to

and the terminal vertex belonging to
B
. We use

to denote the subpath of
R
from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
. Given sets
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{l}\subseteq V(G)
, we say that

has form

if
v_{i}\in X_{i}
for all
i\in[l]
. For simplicity, we will say that
R
has form

if every vertex of
R
belongs to
X
. A vertex
v\in R

. We say that a vertex

Let

be an oriented cycle. We define

and

In the paper, we say that

-

has

has many directed 13-paths which can be used to cover bad vertices. However, if

has many sinks, then it is no longer guaranteed that

has many directed or antidirected paths. To overcome this, we will use both sinks and short directed paths to cover bad vertices. Another difference is that when

has few sinks we need to balance the sizes of

and

first, but we do not proceed this when

has many sinks. This is because when
C
has few sinks the cycle is close to being directed and every directed path uses the same number of vertices in

and

. However, for the case that
C
has many sinks, we have enough sinks and sources to cover the remaining vertices in
X
and

no matter whether they have the same size.

Some ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
Similar with the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we prove Theorem
1.5
by using the stability technique where we split the proof into two cases depending on whether

has expansion property. For the case that

has no expansion property, Theorem

implies that

has a partition
(W,X,Y,Z)
satisfying
(EP1)
-
(EP7)
. Thus if
|C|<n/5
, then it seems to be not difficult to embed
C
into
G[W]
as
G[W]
is close to a regular tournament of order
|W|\approx n/4
. When
|C|\geqslant n/5
, we can randomly choose a subgraph

in

with
|G^{\ast}|=|C|
which inherits the properties. Then the problem of embedding
C
into

is equivalent to the problem of finding a Hamilton cycle

in

, which can be done by Theorem

. If the given oriented graph

has expansion property, by applying several simple results on expansion we will show that a minimum semidegree

is sufficient to guarantee the existence of any oriented cycle, see Theorem

.

Probabilistic tools

Theorem 4.1

(Chernoff Bound 1)
.

Suppose that

are independent random variables with
\mathbb{P}[X_{i}=1]=p

\mathbb{P}[X_{i}=0]=1-p

\mathbb{P}[|X-\mathbb{E}[X]|>t]<2e^{-t^{2}/(3\mathbb{E}[X])}.

The following lemma is an immediate consequence of Chernoff Bound 1.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
9
]
)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

such that if

Theorem 4.3

(Chernoff Bound 2)
.

Let
X
be a random variable determined by
s
independent trials

such that changing the outcome of any one trial can affect

by at most

. Then for any

\mathbb{P}[|X-\mathbb{E}(X)|>\lambda]<2e^{-\lambda^{2}/2c^{2}s}.

We use Chernoff Bound 2 to prove the following lemma, which allows us to embed a collection of short subpaths of the given Hamilton cycle into the reduced oriented graph
R
in such a way that each of the clusters receives a similar number of vertices. When we talk about “a path
P
is randomly embedded around

” means that we start from the specified initial vertex
V_{i}\in V(F)
and assign the next vertex of
P
to either the successor or the predecessor of
V_{i}
in

according to the orientation of the edge. This randomised embedding method is also used in
[

,
38
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
R
be a digraph and let
F=V_{1}V_{2}\cdots V_{k}V_{1}
be a cycle in
R
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}
such that for any
n\geqslant n_{0}
and a collection of oriented paths
\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{s}\}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{s}|P_{i}|\leqslant n
and
|P_{i}|\leqslant n^{1/3}
for each
i\in[s]
, there is a randomised embedding around
F
of these
s
paths satisfying the following. Define

to be the number of vertices in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}V(P_{i})
assigned to

by this embedding. Then for all

, we have

\left|a(i)-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}|P_{i}|}{k}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon n.

Proof.

We assign the initial vertex of each

to one of

independently and uniformly at random. Observe that the assignment of each

can change the number of vertices assigned to any vertex of

by at most

. Clearly
\mathbb{E}[a(i)]=\sum_{i=1}^{s}|P_{i}|/k
and

.
By Theorem

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[|a(i)-\sum_{i=1}^{s}|P_{i}|/k|>\varepsilon n]<2e^{-(\varepsilon n)^{2}/2(n^{1/3})^{2}n}<1/k,

as

is sufficiently large. Thus the probability that there exists some

with

which does not have almost the expected number of vertices assigned to it is less than 1. Thus with positive probability the above randomised embedding satisfies the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

4.2.
The Diregularity Lemma and Blow-up Lemma

The
density
of a bipartite graph
G=(X,Y)
with vertex classes
X,Y
is defined to be

\rho(X,Y)=\frac{e(X,Y)}{|X||Y|},

where
e(X,Y)
is the number of edges between
X
and
Y
in the graph
G
.

Given
\varepsilon>0,d\in[0,1]
, we say that a bipartite graph
G=(X,Y)
is

\bullet

\varepsilon
-regular
if for all sets
A\subseteq X
and
B\subseteq Y
with
|A|\geqslant\varepsilon|X|,|B|\geqslant\varepsilon|Y|
we have

|\rho(A,B)-\rho(X,Y)|<\varepsilon;

\bullet

(\varepsilon,d)
-regular
if it is
\varepsilon
-regular with density
\rho(X,Y)\geqslant d
;

\bullet

(\varepsilon,d)
-superregular
if it is
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular and additionally the degree
d_{G}(x,Y)\geqslant d|Y|
for every
x\in X
and
d_{G}(y,X)\geqslant d|X|
for every
y\in Y
.

Let
G=(X,Y)
be a bipartite digraph where all edges are oriented from
X
to
Y
. We define the pair
(X,Y)
as (super)regular if it satisfies the (super)regularity condition in the underlying graph of
G
.

The following simple observation is well-known, which shows that removing a small proportion of vertices from a regular pair
(A,B)
can make it become superregular.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(X,Y)
be an
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular pair. Then there exists
A\subseteq X,B\subseteq Y
of size
|A|=(1-\varepsilon)|X|
and
|B|=(1-\varepsilon)|Y|
such that
(A,B)
is
(2\varepsilon,d-2\varepsilon)
-superregular.

We will use the following degree form of the Diregularity Lemma with “refinement property”.

Lemma 4.6

(Diregularity Lemma,
[
25
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and all numbers
M^{\prime}
,

there are numbers
M
and
n_{0}
such that if

\bullet

G
is a digraph on
n\geqslant n_{0}
vertices,

\bullet

U_{0},U_{1},\ldots,U_{M^{\prime\prime}}
is a partition of

, and

\bullet

is any real number,

then there is a partition

of

and a spanning subdigraph

of

such that the following statements hold:

\bullet

\bullet

|V_{0}|\leqslant\varepsilon n
and

\bullet

\bullet

\bullet

The last condition of Lemma

shows that all pairs of clusters are

4.3.
Robust expander

Robust expanders, introduced by Kühn, Osthus and Treglown
[

, have formed a key feature in many results involving Hamilton cycles. Below we give the definition of the robust expander and some brief background.

Definition 1
.

Let
\nu\in(0,1)
. For an

G

\nu
-outneighborhood of

\nu n
inneighbors in

is a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-outexpander if

|RN_{\nu,G}^{+}(S)|\geqslant|S|+\nu n

for every

satisfying
\tau n<|S|<(1-\tau)n
.

Lemma 4.9

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let
0<1/n\ll\nu\ll\tau\leqslant\varepsilon/2\leqslant 1
. Every
n
-vertex oriented graph
G
with
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant(3/8+\varepsilon)n
is a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-outexpander.

Taylor
[
38
]
proved that every sufficiently large robust outexpander of linear minimum semidegree contains any oriented Hamilton cycle.

Theorem 4.10

(
[
38
]
)
.

Let
1/n\ll\nu\leqslant\tau\ll\gamma<1
and let
G
be an
n
-vertex digraph with
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant\gamma n
. If
G
is a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-outexpander, then it contains every possible orientation of a Hamilton cycle.

The following lemma of DeBiaso and Treglown
[

allows us to find any not too short oriented path between any pair of vertices in a robust outexpander.

Lemma 4.11

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let

and let
G
be an
n
-vertex digraph with
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant\gamma n
. If

is a robust
(\nu,\tau)
-outexpander, then for any distinct

, and any

5.
The structure of
G

We now come to the first ingredient in the proof of Theorem

, which is Theorem

, saying that every sufficiently large oriented graph

with high minimum semidegree that fails to be a robust outexpander must structurally “close to” a well-behaved configuration. To formally say what we mean by “close to” a structure we need the following definition.

Let
(W,X,Y,Z)
be a partition of
V(G)
. For simplicity, we refer to
Z
and
X
as the “predecessor” and “successor” of
W
, respectively, and write
W^{-}=Z,W^{+}=X,W^{++}=Y
. Analogous definitions hold for
X,Y
and
Z
.

Definition 2

(
\delta
-extremal)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex oriented graph. We say that a partition
(W,X,Y,Z)
of

is
\delta
-extremal if the following statements hold:

(EP1)

|W|,|X|,|Y|,|Z|=n/4\pm O(\delta n)
;

(EP2)

e(W\cup Z,Y\cup Z)=O(\delta n^{2})
;

(EP3)

For each
J\in\{W,X,Y,Z\}
and
\sigma\in\{+,-\}
, all but
O(\delta n)
vertices
v\in J
satisfy
d^{\sigma}(v,J^{\sigma})\geqslant|J^{\sigma}|-O(\delta n)
;

(EP4)

For
J\in\{W,Y\}
, all but
O(\delta n)
vertices
v\in J
satisfy
d^{\pm}(v,J)\geqslant|J|/2-O(\delta n)
;

(EP5)

d^{\pm}(v,X)\geqslant|X|/2-O(\delta n)
for all but
O(\delta n)
vertices

;

(EP6)

For
J\in\{W,Y\}
and
\sigma\in\{+,-\}
, every

satisfies
d^{\sigma}(v,J\cup J^{\sigma})\geqslant n/50-O(\delta n)
;

(EP7)

For
J\in\{X,Z\}
and
\sigma\in\{+,-\}
, every

satisfies
d^{\sigma}(v,J^{\sigma}\cup J^{\sigma\sigma})\geqslant n/50-O(\delta n)
.

We refer to a vertex

as a

vertex if it fulfills all degree conditions for

in
(EP3)
-
(EP5)
. Otherwise, it is a

vertex of

. Whether a vertex is a bad vertex depends on the partition of

when no ambiguity arises.

The following theorem is the main result in this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
0<1/n\ll\nu\leqslant\tau\ll\varepsilon\ll\delta\ll 1
and let

be an
n
-vertex oriented graph. If

-outexpander or

has a

.

establishes that if

has no expansion property, then

admits a partition
(W,X,Y,Z)
such that

is close to an

\displaystyle|X|<|Z|+\nu n.

must be incident to a vertex in
W_{Y}\cup W_{X}\cup Y_{W}\cup Y_{X}
. Moreover, as
L_{WX}\cup L_{XY}=\emptyset
, every vertex in

has at most
n/50
outneighbors in
W
and every vertex in
W_{Y}\cup W_{X}
has at most
n/50
inneighbors in

\displaystyle e(Y,W)

\displaystyle\leqslant e(Y_{W}\cup Y_{X},W)+e(Y,W_{X}\cup W_{Y})

\displaystyle\leqslant(|W_{Y}\cup X_{Y}|+|W_{X}\cup W_{Y}|)n/50

\displaystyle\leqslant e(M)|W|/5.

The last inequality holds from
|W|>n/5
(due to
(EP1)
) and
|W_{Y}\cup X_{Y}|+|W_{X}\cup W_{Y}|\leqslant 2e(M)
. Similarly, we have
e(X,W)\leqslant e(M)|W|/5
. Considering the sum of indegrees of vertices in
W
, we have
\sum_{w\in W}d^{-}(w)\leqslant|W|(|W|-1)/2+|W||Z|+2e(M)|W|/5
. Then there exists a vertex
w\in W
with
d^{-}(w)\leqslant(|W|-1)/2+|Z|+2e(M)/5
. In the same way, there is a vertex
y\in Y
with
d^{+}(y)\leqslant(|Y|-1)/2+|Z|+2e(M)/5
.

It follows by
L(WX)\cup L(XY)=\emptyset
that
\delta^{0}(G[X])\leqslant n/50
. Note that every edge of
G[X]
must be incident to a vertex in
X_{W}\cup X_{X}\cup X_{Y}
. Then

e(X)\leqslant|X_{W}\cup X_{X}\cup X_{Y}|n/50\leqslant e(M)|X|/5.

Thus, there exists a vertex
x\in X
such that
d(x)\leqslant|W|+|Y|+|Z|+e(M)/5
. Therefore,

d^{-}(w)+d^{+}(y)+d(x)\leqslant\frac{3}{2}(|W|+|Y|+2|Z|)-1+e(M).

By the degree condition and
n=|W|+|X|+|Y|+|Z|
, we have
(3n-4)/2\leqslant d^{-}(w)+d^{+}(y)+d(x)\leqslant 3(n+|Z|-|X|)/2-1+e(M)
. Thus
|X|-|Z|\leqslant 2e(M)/3+2/3

e(M)<|X|-|Z|
, then
e(M)+1\leqslant|X|-|Z|\leqslant 2e(M)/3+2/3
, which is impossible. Therefore,
e(M)\geqslant|X|-|Z|
and then
(W,X,Y,Z)
is the desired partition.
∎

8.
Pancyclicity

In this section, we are going to prove Theorem
1.5
. We first mention some results on short cycles in digraphs. Recall that a central problem in digraph theory, Caccetta-Häggkvist Conjecture, states that every oriented graph on
n
vertices with minimum outdegree
d
contains a directed cycle of length at most
n/d
. For the existence of a directed 3-cycle, Shen
[
35
]
proved minimum outdegree
0.355n
suffices and, Hamburger, Haxell and Kostochka
[
23
]
showed that if one consider minimum semidegree instead of minimum outdegree, then the constant can be improved slightly, they showed that
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant 0.346n
ensures a directed 3-cycle. For transitive 3-cycles, an
n/3
-blowup of the directed 3-cycle shows that minimum semidegree
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant n/3
is not sufficient and it is not difficult to check that
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant n/3+1
suffices.

Theorem
1.4
shows that minimum semidegree
(3/8+\varepsilon)n
ensures a cycle of every possible orientation and of every possible length in an oriented graph. Kelly, Kühn and Osthus proved that for cycles of constant lengths the bound can be improved, but the improvement depends on the
cycle-type
, where the cycle-type is the number of forward edges minus the number of backward edges.

Theorem 8.1

(
[
28
]
)
.

(i) Let

and
\varepsilon>0
. There exists

such that if

vertices with

contains every possible orientation of an
l

(ii) Let

and let

be some positive constant. There exists

such that every oriented graph
G
on
n\geqslant n_{2}
vertices with
\delta^{0}(G)\geqslant\gamma n
contains every oriented cycle of length at most
l
and cycle-type 0.

Note that antidirected cycles are special cases of cycles with cycle-type 0. In
[

Kühn, Osthus and Piguet considered the case that the cycle-type is sufficient large with respect to

(
[

]
)

Let

and

It is worth noticing that for some cycle-types and lengths, a semidegree exceeding

Theorem 8.3

(

]
)

For all

9.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we determine the exact minimum semidegree threshold for a cycle of every possible orientation and of every possible length in oriented graphs, which improves an approximate result of Kelly in
[
26
]
and solves a problem of Häggkvist and Thomason in
[
22
]
(also see page 2 in
[
21
]
). Next we mention a few open questions and future directions for research.

Given a digraph
G
, let
\delta(G)
denote the minimum degree of
G
(i.e. the minimum number of edges
incident to a vertex) and set
\delta^{\ast}(G):=\delta(G)+\delta^{+}(G)+\delta^{-}(G)
. Häggkvist
[
20
]
conjectured that every
n
-vertex oriented graph
G
with
\delta^{\ast}(G)>(3n-3)/2
contains a directed Hamilton cycle. In
[
27
]
, the conjecture was verified approximately by Kelly, Kühn and Osthus. Moreover, Woodall
[
40
]
proved the following digraph version of Ore’s theorem: if
d^{+}(x)+d^{-}(y)\geqslant|G|
for every two distinct vertices
x,y
for which there is no edge from
x
to
y
, then the given digraph
G
has a directed Hamilton cycle. It seems to be an interesting problem to determine the exact
\delta^{\ast}(G)
or Ore-type condition threshold for every possible orientation of a Hamilton cycle in oriented graphs and in general digraphs.

One may further consider the transversal generalization of Theorem
1.1
. For a collection

\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{m}\}
of not-necessarily distinct digraphs on the common vertex set
V
, an
m
-edge digraph
G
on vertex set
V
is called a
transversal
of
\mathcal{G}
if the edges of
G
intersect each
G_{i}
exactly once. For results on transversal, we refer the reader to the survey paper
[
37
]
. It is well-known that every tournament contains a directed Hamilton path, and every strongly connected tournament contains a directed Hamilton cycle. Chakraborti, Kim, Lee and Seo
[
4
]

established transversal generalizations of these results. Further, they
[
5
]
established the transversal generalization of a result of Thomason in
[
39
]
, providing the existence of every possible
transversal cycles in a collection of tournaments. They showed that every system of
n
tournaments with order
n
contains a transversal of every orientation of a Hamilton cycle except possibly the directed one. In
[
6
]
, Cheng, Li, Sun and Wang generalized Ghouila-Houri’s theorem to transversal version and they posed a corresponding problem in oriented graphs. Determining the exact degree threshold that ensures a transversal of every orientation of a Hamilton cycle in a digraph or an oriented graph system is a natural question. Indeed, we have the following problems.

Problem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{n}\}
be a collection of oriented graphs with common vertex set

V
of size
n
. If
\delta^{0}(G_{i})\geqslant(3n-1)/8
for all
i\in[n]
, does
\mathcal{G}
contain a transversal of every orientation of a Hamilton cycle?

Problem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{n}\}
be a collection of digraphs with common vertex set

V
of size
n
. If
\delta^{0}(G_{i})\geqslant n/2+1
for all
i\in[n]
, does
\mathcal{G}
contain a transversal of every orientation of a Hamilton cycle?

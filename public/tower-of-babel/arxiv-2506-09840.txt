The capillary Gauss curvature flow
Xinqun Mei, Guofang Wang, Liangjun Weng

Abstract
. In this article, we first introduce a Gauss curvature type flow for capillary hypersurfaces, which we call capillary Gauss curvature flow . We then show that the flow will shrink to a point in finite time. This is a capillary counterpart (or Robin boundary counterpart) of Firey’s problem studied by Firey [ 18 ] and Tso [ 35 ] . Finally, we prove that its normalized flow converges to a soliton. This is a capillary counterpart of the result of Guan and Ni in [ 21 ] . The classification of solitons remains an open conjecture.

1.
Introduction

Studying the evolution of closed hypersurfaces under geometric curvature flows is an important topic in geometric analysis. It lies in the intersection of geometry, partial differential equations, and applied mathematics. One of the well-known models is the Gauss curvature flow for closed hypersurfaces, which was proposed by Firey
[
18
]
. This model is used to describe the changing shape of a tumbling stone subjected to collisions from all directions with uniform frequency. It can be formulated as a family of smooth, strictly convex, closed hypersurfaces
\{M_{t}=X(\mathbb{S}^{n},t)\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X(x,t)=-K(x,t)\nu(x,t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ x\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\penalty\ t\geq 0,

where
K(x,t)
is the Gauss curvature and
\nu(x,t)
is the unit outward normal at the point
X(x,t)\in M_{t}
.

Firey
[
18
]
investigated the asymptotic behavior of the solution to flow (
1.1
) under the assumption of the existence, regularity, and uniqueness of the solution. He proved that if the initial hypersurface
M_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is centrally symmetric, then the evolving hypersurface will gradually become more and more round along flow (
1.1
) (in the sense that its normalized flow converges to a round sphere). He conjectured that the above conclusion also holds without the symmetric assumption. Later, Tso
[
35
]
proved that flow (
1.1
) shrinks to a point in finite time for all dimensions. When
n=2
, Andrews
[
2
]
solved this conjecture completely, i.e., the flow converges to a round point. Recently, Guan and Ni
[
21
]
proved that its normalized flow converges to a Gauss soliton, a solution to the following equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle K(x)=\langle X(x),\nu(x)\rangle,\qquad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Hence, in order to solve Firey’s conjecture for the general
n
, we need only to show that all solutions to (
1.2
) are spheres, which was confirmed very recently in a
breakthrough work of Choi and Daskalopoulos
[
15
]
.

In this paper, we first introduce a capillary counterpart of the Gauss curvature flow. A properly embedded, smooth compact hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with boundary
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is called a
capillary hypersurface
if it intersects
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
at a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi).
In particular, if
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, we call it
free boundary hypersurface
. Denote
\widehat{\Sigma}
be the bounded closed domain in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, which is enclosed by
\Sigma
and
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. The boundary of
\widehat{\Sigma}
consists of two parts: one is
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
and the other, which will be denoted by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, lies on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. A simple example of a capillary hypersurface is the unit spherical cap

\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\coloneqq\left\{\xi\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\mid\penalty\ |\xi-\cos\theta e|=1\right\},

where
-e\coloneqq E_{n+1}
is the
(n+1)
-th unit vector of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.
A capillary Gauss curvature flow is a family of strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, given by embeddings
X\colon M\times[0,T^{\prime})\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and satisfying

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X(x,t)=-K(x,t)\tilde{\nu}(x,t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (x,t)\in M\times[0,T^{\prime}),

with a contact angle boundary condition

(1.4)

\displaystyle\langle\nu(x,t),e\rangle=\cos(\pi-\theta),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (x,t)\in\partial M\times[0,T^{\prime}),

where

(1.5)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}\coloneqq\nu+\cos\theta e,

is the capillary Gauss map of
\Sigma_{t}
. See Section
2.1
or
[
28
, Section 2]
for more discussion on
\tilde{\nu}
.

Analogous to the classical Gauss curvature flow studied by Firey, which models the isotropic wear of convex closed hypersurfaces, the capillary version (
1.3
) describes the evolution of convex hypersurfaces with boundary under both curvature effects and boundary interaction. In this setting, the hypersurface evolves with normal speed equal to its Gauss curvature, while maintaining a fixed contact angle with the supporting hyperplane
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. This flow provides a geometric model for the relaxation of sessile droplets on flat substrates, where motion is driven by surface tension (via Gauss curvature), and the prescribed contact angle reflects adhesion at the boundary. It captures key features of capillarity-driven spreading and the curvature-induced evolution of interfaces constrained by rigid supports.

Notice that (
1.4
) implies
\langle\tilde{\nu}(x,t),e\rangle=0
on
\partial M\times[0,T^{\prime})
. The flow (
1.3
) is well-defined and is equivalent to an anisotropic Gauss curvature flow

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}X(x,t)\right)^{\perp}=-\ell K(x,t)\nu(x,t),

where

(1.7)

\displaystyle\ell\coloneqq 1+\cos\theta\langle e,\nu\rangle.

We will prove in the paper that starting from a strictly convex capillary hypersurface, flow (
1.3
) shrinks to a point in finite time and, after normalization, converges to a soliton. Namely, a solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}K&=&\ell^{-1}{\langle X,\nu\rangle}&\text{ in }M,\\
\langle\nu,e\rangle&=&-\cos\theta&\text{ on }\partial M,\end{array}\right.

when
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
.
That is, we establish the capillary counterpart of the results by Tso
[
35
]
and Guan–Ni
[
21
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a smooth strictly convex capillary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, then flow (
1.3
) has a smooth solution
\Sigma_{t}\coloneqq X(\cdot,t)
, which is strictly convex capillary hypersurface for all
t\in[0,T^{*})
, where
T^{*}\coloneqq\frac{{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{0})}{(n+1){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})}.
As
t\rightarrow T^{\ast}
, then
\Sigma_{t}
shrink to a point
p\in\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. Moreover, the rescaled capillary hypersurfaces

(1.11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t}\coloneqq\left(\frac{{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})}{{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}(\Sigma_{t}-p),

converge to a smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma_{\infty}\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
as
t\to+\infty
, which is a solution to (
1
).

It is easy to check that the spherical cap
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is a solution of Eq. (
1
). It is very interesting to classify all soliton solutions to (
1
). We propose the following conjecture, serving as the capillary counterpart of the results in
[
2
]
and
[
15
]
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

For
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, the spherical cap
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
is the unique strictly convex solution to Eq. (
1
).

Remark that if
n=1
and if we further assume that
\Sigma
is a symmetric (along
\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\mid x_{1}=0\}
) curve in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
, then a result in
[
9
, Theorem 5.2]
implies that the conjecture is true.

It is clear that if this conjecture is true, our result Theorem
1.1
implies that the rescaled capillary hypersurfaces as in (
1.11
)
converge to a uniquely determined spherical cap, at least for
\theta<\pi/2
.

Our flow (
1.3
) or (
1.6
) is closely related to the anisotropic Gauss curvature flow.
Characterizing the asymptotic behavior of the evolution starting from a closed convex hypersurface under the anisotropic Gauss curvature flow

(1.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X=-f(\nu)K\nu,

is always a challenging problem. Here
f(\nu)
is the anisotropic effect function being evaluated at the unit normal vector
\nu
. Andrews
[
3
]
proved that such a flow will contract any smooth, convex, closed hypersurface to a point in finite time. Very recently, Böröczky-Guan
[
8
]
introduced an entropy functional for flow (
1.12
), and established the weak convergence of flow (
1.12
) and applied it to study the weak solution of the logarithmic Minkowski problem. In the smooth category, the logarithmic Minkowski problem reduces to

(1.13)

\displaystyle h\det(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma)=f,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ {\rm{in}}\penalty\ \mathbb{S}^{n},

which is the soliton equation associated with flow (
1.12
). It is a well-known conjecture (log-Brunn–Minkowski conjecture) to prove the uniqueness of the solution to (
1.13
) when
f
is an even, positive smooth function. When
n=1
, this conjecture was confirmed by Gage
[
19
]
for symmetric convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
For a general
f
, Yagisita
[
39
]
constructed two different positive solutions to (
1.13
) for a non-even function
f
in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Very recently, Chen–Feng–Liu
[
13
]
established the uniqueness of solutions to Eq. (
1.13
) for
n=2
under the assumption that
f
is sufficiently close to
1
in the
C^{\alpha}
norm, without requiring evenness. This result was extended to higher dimensions by Bäröczky–Saroglou
[
11
]
. By viewing
f=\ell
in (
1.13
), we observe that Conjecture 1.2 aligns with the log-Brunn-Minkowski conjecture, except for the presence of an additional boundary condition. For further developments related to (
1.13
), we refer to a recent survey by Böröczky
[
7
]
and the references therein, including
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
22
,
32
]
, among others.

In our setting, it is easy to observe that flow (
1.6
) is equivalent to (
1.12
) by viewing

f(\nu)\coloneqq 1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle,

except for the presence of an additional boundary condition. From this perspective, the capillary Gauss curvature flow in a Euclidean half-space is closely related to the study of the corresponding anisotropic Gauss curvature flow.

Now we outline the main ideas of the proof.
First of all, we establish a Blaschke-Santaló theory for capillary hypersurfaces by suitably generalizing concepts from convex geometry, which is of independent interest and plays a crucial role in the introduction of a capillary entropy later. Then we split the statement of Theorem
1.1
into two parts, Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
4.3
. In the first part, by using the strategy presented in Tso
[
35
]
(see also
[
5
, Section 15.3]
), we prove that flow (
1.3
) shrinks to a point in finite time, as stated in Theorem
3.1
. The comparison principle implies that the finite time existence of flow (
1.3
), and then assuming that the inner capillary radius of evolving capillary bodies has a uniformly positive lower bound, we can establish a priori estimates for the solution of flow (
1.3
) in
[0,T^{\ast})
. In our case, the difficulties arising from boundaries need to be addressed. Therefore, we introduce some new auxiliary functions, which are well adapted to capillary problems.
To derive the principal curvatures bound along flow (
1.3
), we introduce a new test function

\displaystyle\varphi\coloneqq\frac{K}{u-c_{0}},

where
u\coloneqq\ell^{-1}{\langle X,\nu\rangle}
and
c_{0}>0
is a constant. Here,
u
is the capillary support function. A key observation is that it satisfies a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition along
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, see Eq. (
3.17
), a similar idea is also used in other problems related to capillary hypersurfaces, see
[
26
,
34
,
37
]
. It turns out that the two-sided estimates for the Gauss curvature hold for all
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. The range
\theta<\pi/2
will be used to derive the two-sided estimates of the principal curvatures, in particular to ensure that Eq. (
3.20
) holds along
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Next, we transform flow (
1.3
) into a parabolic Monge-Ampère equation with Robin boundary condition, and then combining with the a priori estimates results, we derive a contradiction that
T^{\ast}
is not the maximal existence time for flow (
1.3
). This implies that
\text{Vol}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
converges to zero as
t\rightarrow T^{\ast}
. Using the fact that the principal curvatures of
\Sigma_{t}
have a uniformly positive lower bound, we conclude that flow (
1.3
) shrinks to a point.

In the second part, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of flow (
1.3
) and show Theorem
4.3
. To begin with, we introduce a capillary entropy
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for the capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\coloneqq\sup_{z_{0}\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},z_{0}),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},z_{0})\coloneqq\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{0}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma,

where
\ell(\xi)\coloneqq\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle
, the restriction of
\ell
defined by (
1.7
) on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
,
u_{z_{0}}(\xi)
is the capillary support function w.r.t.
z_{0}
and
\omega_{\theta}\coloneqq|\mathcal{C}_{\theta}|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}|
is the capillary area of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, see Eq. (
2.4
) and Definition
4.4
for more details. Then we prove that the capillary entropy is non-increasing along the normalized flow (
4.1
) in Proposition
4.10
, with strict monotonicity unless it is a soliton. This is a capillary analogous result to the closed setting by Guan-Ni
[
21
, Theorem 3.4]
. Adopting the strategy as in
[
21
]
, we further obtain the estimates

\displaystyle\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\leq Ce^{\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})},

and

\displaystyle\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\geq C^{-1}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma})e^{-n\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})},

where
\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)
and
\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)
are the capillary inner and outer radius of a capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
respectively (cf. Sinestrari-Weng
[
34
, Section 2.2]
or Section
2.1
for the precise definition).
Adapting these estimates to the flow (
4.1
), we can prove that the capillary support function
u(x,t)
admits a positive lower bound, i.e., Theorem
4.12
, which is one of the crucial points in the paper and does not hold for a general anisotropic Gauss curvature flow (
1.12
), cf.
[
8
, Section 1]
. Then we prove the uniform
C^{2}
estimates for the solution of normalized capillary Gauss curvature flow:

(1.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X(x,t)=X(x,t)-K\tilde{\nu}(x,t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (x,t)\in M\times[0,+\infty),

or Eq. (
4.1
). In deriving a uniform lower bound for the Gauss curvature of the solution to Eq. (
1.14
), we introduce a new test function

(1.15)

\displaystyle P\coloneqq\log(Ku^{\gamma}),

for some
\gamma>0
sufficiently large. The advantage of
P
is that it satisfies a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition along
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, see Eq. (
4.70
). A similar idea was also used in
[
6
, Theorem 5.2]
for the closed case.
Along flow (
1.14
), the enclosed volume
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
of
X(M,t)
with
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is preserved. Finally, by combining the monotone property of capillary entropy and the uniform a priori estimates of the solution, we show that the solution of normalized flow (
1.14
) converges to a self-similar solution of flow (
1.3
), namely Eq. (
1
).

Organization of the rest of the paper.

.

2.
Preliminaries on convex capillary hypersurfaces

First, we collect some basic facts about capillary convex bodies. Subsequently, we introduce the concept of duality for capillary convex bodies and establish a Blaschke–Santaló type inequality, which serves as the capillary analogue of the classical Blaschke–Santaló inequality.

2.1.
Capillary hypersurface

Assume
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
is a capillary hypersurface in the sense of the definition in Section
1
. Let
\mu
be the unit outward co-normal of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
and
\overline{\nu}
the unit outward normal to
\partial\Sigma
in
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
such that
\{\nu,\mu\}
and
\{\overline{\nu},e\}
have the same orientation in the normal bundle of
\partial\Sigma\subset\overline{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}}
. Namely, along
\partial\Sigma
, there holds

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}e&=&\sin\theta\mu-\cos\theta\nu,\\
\overline{\nu}&=&\cos\theta\mu+\sin\theta\nu.\end{array}

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}
be the bounded closed region in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
enclosed by
\Sigma
and
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. The boundary of
\widehat{\Sigma}
consists of two components: one is
\Sigma
, and the other, denoted by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, lies on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. We call such
\widehat{\Sigma}
the
capillary convex body
and denote
\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
as the set of all capillary convex bodies in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, and
\mathcal{K}^{\circ}_{\theta}
as the family of capillary convex bodies for which the origin is an interior point of their flat boundary.

We collect the notion of support function and capillary support function for the capillary convex bodies in
\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
.

Definition 2.1

(Capillary support function)
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
, the support function
h_{z}
of
{\Sigma}
with respect to
z\in\widehat{\Sigma}
is defined by

\displaystyle h_{z}(\xi)\coloneqq\max_{X\in\Sigma}\langle\xi-\cos\theta e,X-z\rangle,\quad\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

The capillary support function of
\Sigma
with respect to
z
is defined by

(2.4)

\displaystyle u_{z}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{h_{z}(\xi)}{\ell(\xi)},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Without confusion, we will abbreviate
h
and
u
as the support function and the capillary support function with respect to the origin, respectively. Otherwise, we will write them explicitly with respect to an associated point.
One may refer to
[
27
, Remark 2.3]
for the geometric meaning of the capillary support function
u_{z}
.

Given any strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
with
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}^{\circ}
, we can parametrize it by the inverse capillary Gauss map (
1.5
), namely

(2.5)

\displaystyle X(\xi)\coloneqq\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\xi)\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\Sigma,

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto\nabla h(\xi)+h(\xi)(\xi-\cos\theta e),

(cf.
[
28
, Lemma 2.4]
).
The principal radii of
\Sigma
at
X(\xi)

are given by the eigenvalues of
(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma)

with respect to the round metric
\sigma
of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. On the other hand, for any function
h\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\theta})
that satisfies

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}h(\xi)+h(\xi)\sigma>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ \xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}h=\cot\theta h,\quad\text{on }\penalty\ \partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

then
h
is the support function of a capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
, cf.
[
28
, Proposition 2.6]
.

To derive a priori estimates for flow (
1.3
) in the next section, it is convenient to use the notion of the capillary inner/outer radius of
\widehat{\Sigma}
(see, e.g.,

[
34
, Section 2.2]
) and is a natural adaptation of the classical notion for a convex closed hypersurface. For a convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and all fixed
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, the capillary inner radius of
\widehat{\Sigma}
is defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\coloneqq\sup\left\{r>0\penalty\ \mid\penalty\ \widehat{\mathcal{C}_{r,\theta}(x_{0})}\subset\widehat{\Sigma}\text{ for some }x_{0}\in\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\right\},

and
the capillary outer radius of
\widehat{\Sigma}
is defined as

(2.6)

\displaystyle\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\coloneqq\inf\left\{r>0\penalty\ \mid\penalty\ \widehat{\Sigma}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{r,\theta}(x_{0})}\text{ for some }x_{0}\in\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\right\},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{r,\theta}(x_{0})\coloneqq\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\penalty\ \mid\penalty\ |x-(x_{0}+r\cos\theta e)|=r\}

is the spherical cap centered at the point
x_{0}+r\cos\theta e
with the radius
r>0
.

Remark 2.2
.

From the definition of
\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)
, it is easy to see that if the capillary inner radius of
\widehat{\Sigma}
has a positive lower bound
\varepsilon_{0}
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, then its associated capillary support function (
2.4
) satisfies

u_{z_{0}}(\xi)\geq\varepsilon_{0},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for all }\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

for some
z_{0}\in\text{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
(see also
[
27
, Remark 2.3]
).

Given a convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, recall that we have the classical notion of the inner radius of
\widehat{\Sigma}
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma})\coloneqq\sup\left\{\rho>0\mid B_{\rho}^{+}(x_{0})\subset\widehat{\Sigma}\penalty\ \text{for\penalty\ some\penalty\ }x_{0}\in\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\right\},

and the outer radius of
\Sigma
is defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma})\coloneqq\inf\left\{\rho>0\mid\widehat{\Sigma}\subset B_{\rho}^{+}(x_{0})\penalty\ \text{for\penalty\ some\penalty\ }x_{0}\in\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\right\}

where
B^{+}_{\rho}(x_{0})\coloneqq B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
is an open ball of radius
\rho
centered at
x_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

We next show that for capillary convex bodies, the inner (outer) radius and the capillary inner (outer) radius can be mutually controlled if
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. Then there hold

(2.7)

\displaystyle(1-\cos\theta)\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\leq\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma})\leq\sin\theta\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta),

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle(1-\cos\theta)\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\leq\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma})\leq\sin\theta\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta),

Proof.

We begin by proving that (
2.7
) holds.
From the definition of capillary inner radius, there exists a spherical cap
\mathcal{C}_{r_{0},\theta}(x_{0})
with radius
r_{0}=\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)
, such that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{r_{0},\theta}(x_{0})}
is contained in
\widehat{\Sigma}
. It is obvious that

B_{(1-\cos\theta)r_{0}}^{+}(x_{0})\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{r_{0},\theta}(x_{0})}\subseteq\widehat{\Sigma},

which implies

\displaystyle(1-\cos\theta)\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\leq\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma}).

On the other hand, from
[
34
, Proposition 2.4, Eq. (2.26)]
, we get

\displaystyle\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\geq\frac{\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{\sin\theta}.

Hence, this completes the proof of (
2.7
). Similarly, by applying
[
34
, Proposition 2.4, Eq. (2.27)]
, the proof of (
2.8
) follows in an analogous manner.
∎

2.2.
Capillary Blaschke-Santaló inequality

In this subsection, we define the polar body
\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z}
of the capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
with respect to the point
z\in\text{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
. Subsequently, we prove a non-sharp version of capillary Blaschke-Santaló inequality, which concerns the upper bound estimate of the Mahler type volume

\inf\limits_{z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z}).

Such a kind of estimate inequality will be used to show that a capillary type entropy functional, as in Firey
[
18
]
(see also Guan-Ni
[
21
]
) is well-defined, we postpone this to Section
4
.

First of all, we recall the capillary gauge function
F_{\theta}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to[0,+\infty)
defined by

\displaystyle F_{\theta}(\xi)\coloneqq|\xi|+\cos\theta\langle e,\xi\rangle,

see, e.g.,
[
25
, Section 3]
. The dual gauge function
F_{\theta}^{\circ}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
of
F_{\theta}
is given by

\displaystyle F^{\circ}_{\theta}(x)=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\left(\sqrt{|x|^{2}+\cot^{2}\theta\langle e,x\rangle^{2}}-\cot\theta\langle e,x\rangle\right).

It is easy to see that
\left\{x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\mid F_{\theta}^{\circ}(x)=r\right\}
is exactly the capillary spherical cap with radius
r
and centered at
\cos\theta re
, namely
\mathcal{C}_{r,\theta}(o)
.
Denote

\mathcal{D}_{\theta}(r)\coloneqq\left\{\eta=(\eta^{\prime},\eta_{n+1})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}}\penalty\ \mid F_{\theta}(\eta)=r\text{ and }\eta_{n+1}\geq r\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right\},

which turns out to be a portion of the ellipsoid given by

\displaystyle\frac{|\eta^{\prime}|^{2}}{r^{2}/\sin^{2}\theta}+\frac{\left(\eta_{n+1}-\frac{r\cos\theta}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}/\sin^{4}\theta}=1.

For simplicity, we denote
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}=\mathcal{D}_{\theta}(1)
.

It is easy to check
for any
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\nabla F^{\circ}_{\theta}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\xi-\cos\theta F^{\circ}_{\theta}(\xi)e}{\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+\cot^{2}\theta\langle e,\xi\rangle^{2}}}.

It is natural to introduce a capillary adaptation of the Cahn-Hoffman map
\Psi\colon\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Psi(\xi)\coloneqq\nabla F_{\theta}^{\circ}(\xi)=\frac{\xi-\cos\theta e}{F_{\theta}(\xi-\cos\theta e)}=\frac{\xi-\cos\theta e}{\ell},

which is the restriction of
\nabla F_{\theta}
on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.
The map
\Psi
satisfies the following property.

Proposition 2.4
.

\Psi
is a diffeomorphism from
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
to
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
.

Proof.

From (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle F_{\theta}(\Psi(\xi))=F_{\theta}\left(\frac{\xi-\cos\theta e}{F_{\theta}(\xi-\cos\theta e)}\right)=1,

and for
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},0)\in\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta}
, there holds

\Psi(\xi)=\ell^{-1}(\xi)(\xi-\cos\theta e)=\sin^{-2}\theta(\xi^{\prime},\cos\theta),

which implies
\Psi
is a map from
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
to
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
.

For all
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, direct calculations yield

D\Psi(\xi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle}&\dots&0&\frac{\cos\theta\xi_{1}}{(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle)^{2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle}&\frac{\cos\theta\xi_{n}}{(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle)^{2}}\\
0&\cdots&0&\frac{1}{(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle)^{2}}\\
\end{array}\right),

then its Jacobi
\det(D\Psi(\xi))=\frac{1}{\ell^{n+2}(\xi)}>0,\penalty\ \forall\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},
therefore
\Psi
is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism map from
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
to
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
.
∎

The following concept is the capillary adaptation of the radial function.

Definition 2.5

(Capillary radial function)
.

Given
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}^{\circ}
, its capillary radial function
\rho_{\widehat{\Sigma}}\colon\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to[0,+\infty)
is defined by

\displaystyle\rho_{\widehat{\Sigma}}(\xi)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0\,|\,\lambda\xi\in\widehat{\Sigma}\right\},\quad\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Next, we introduce a notion of the capillary polar body for a capillary convex body. We note that this concept differs from the classical one presented in
[
33
, Section 1.6]
, where the usual support function
h
is used. In our setting, we employ the capillary support function (
2.4
), which is a necessary modification to accommodate the capillary boundary condition.

Definition 2.6

(Capillary polar body)
.

Given
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
and
z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
, we define its capillary polar body with respect to
z
by

\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z}\coloneqq\left\{X+z\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\mid ru_{z}(\xi)\leq 1,\text{ where }X=r\xi\text{ with }\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\right\},

where
u_{z}
is the capillary support function of
\Sigma
with respect to
z
.

Remark 2.7
.

It can be readily observed that

ru_{z}(\xi)\leq 1
is equivalent to

\displaystyle r{\langle\xi-\cos\theta e,y-z\rangle}\leq\ell(\xi)\quad\forall\penalty\ y\in\Sigma.

In other words,

\displaystyle r\langle\Psi(\xi),y-z\rangle\leq 1\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\penalty\ y\in\Sigma,

where
\Psi
is given by (
2.10
). In particular, we observe that the capillary polar body of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta}
(w.r.t the origin
o
) satisfies
(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})^{\ast}_{o}=\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}
.

For notation consistency, we denote
\Sigma^{*}_{z}\coloneqq\partial(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z})\cap{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. We note that, in general, the capillary polar body of a capillary convex body is not itself a capillary convex body, except in the planar case. Namely,
\Sigma^{*}_{z}
may not be a capillary hypersurface for
n\geq 2
. Nevertheless, we have the following relationship between the capillary support function of a capillary convex body and the radial function of its capillary polar body. This property is of independent interest and may be useful for related problems of capillary hypersurfaces.

Proposition 2.8
.

Given
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}^{\circ}
and
z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
. Denote
\rho_{z}\coloneqq\rho_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{z}^{\ast}}
be the capillary radial function of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{z}^{\ast}
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\rho_{z}(\xi)=\frac{1}{u_{z}(\xi)},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Proof.

For all
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we see that
\rho_{z}(\xi)\xi\in\Sigma^{*}_{z}-z
. By the Definition
2.5
of
\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z}
, we derive that
\rho_{z}(\xi)u_{z}(\xi)=1
, then the conclusion follows.
∎

Based on the preceding preparations, we can express the volume of the capillary polar body
\widehat{\Sigma}^{\ast}_{z}
in terms of the usual support function
h_{z}
or the capillary support function
u_{z}
of
\Sigma
, defined on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Proposition 2.9
.

The volume of the capillary polar body
\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z}
satisfies

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z})=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell(\xi)}{u^{n+1}_{z}(\xi)}d\sigma=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell^{n+2}(\xi)}{h^{n+1}_{z}(\xi)}d\sigma,

where
d\sigma
is the standard area element on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Proof.

From the co-area formula and the 0-homogeneity property of
\nabla F^{\circ}_{\theta}
, we compute

(2.12)

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\{F^{\circ}_{\theta}=r\}}\frac{\chi_{\{ru_{z}(\xi)\leq 1\}}}{|\nabla F^{\circ}_{\theta}(\xi)|}d\sigma dr=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\{F^{\circ}_{\theta}=1\}}r^{n}\frac{\chi_{\{ru_{z}(\xi)\leq 1\}}}{|\nabla F^{\circ}_{\theta}(\xi)|}d\sigma dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{1}{|\nabla F^{\circ}_{\theta}(\xi)|}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{u_{z}(\xi)}}r^{n}drd\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{1}{|\nabla F^{\circ}_{\theta}(\xi)|}\frac{1}{u^{n+1}_{z}(\xi)}d\sigma.

From (
2.9
), we obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle|\nabla F^{\circ}_{\theta}(\xi)|=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+\cot^{2}\theta\langle e,\xi\rangle^{2}}}=\frac{1}{\ell}.

Inserting (
2.13
) into (
2.12
), the first equality of (
2.11
) follows, while the second equality follows directly by combining with (
2.4
). This completes the proof.
∎

We now introduce the notion of a capillary Santaló point
z_{s}\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
for a given capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
, defined as the point for which the infimum of the volume of its capillary polar body is attained.

Definition 2.10

(Capillary Santaló point)
.

Given
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, if there exists a point
z_{s}=z_{s}(\widehat{\Sigma})\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
such that

{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z_{s}})=\inf_{z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{z}^{*}),

then we call
z_{s}
the capillary Santaló point of
\widehat{\Sigma}
.

The following proposition justifies that the capillary Santaló point is well-defined and unique. The idea of the proof is inspired by
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
. A similar argument will be employed again in Proposition
4.7
below.

Proposition 2.11
.

The capillary Santaló point
z_{s}
is unique and lies in
{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
. Moreover, there holds

(2.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\xi_{i}}{u_{z_{s}}^{n+2}(\xi)}d\sigma=0,\quad\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ i=1,\cdots,n,

where
\xi_{i}\coloneqq\langle\xi,E_{i}\rangle
, with
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
denoting the horizontal coordinate vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.

Proof.

First, we show that the capillary Santaló point
z_{s}
lies in
{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
. We argue by contradiction and assume that
z_{s}\in\partial\Sigma\cap\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. Without loss of generality, assume
z_{s}
is the origin. Since
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
is a closed convex body in
\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it follows from
[
21
, Proof of Lemma 2.2, claim (2.3)]
that there exists a unit vector
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(specifically,
\zeta
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\Sigma\subset\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
at the origin, and for notational convenience, we may assume
\zeta=E_{1}
,) such that

(2.15)

\displaystyle L\coloneqq\left\{-tE_{1}\mid 0<t<t_{0}\right\}\text{ is contained in }\widehat{\partial\Sigma},\penalty\ \text{ for some small }t_{0}>0.

Moreover, due to
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, the set
\widehat{\Sigma}
is contained in the half-space
\{x_{1}\leq 0\}
and is tangent to the hyperplane
\{x_{1}=0\}
at the origin.

For
\xi\coloneqq(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n+1})\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, define
N(\xi)\coloneqq(-\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n+1})
.
Since

u_{z_{s}}(\xi)=\ell^{-1}(\xi)\sup\limits_{X\in\Sigma}\langle X,\xi+\cos\theta E_{n+1}\rangle,

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then
there exists a point
z(\xi)\in\Sigma
, such that

u_{z_{s}}(\xi)=\ell^{-1}(\xi)\langle z(\xi),\xi+\cos\theta E_{n+1}\rangle.

For all
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
with
\xi_{1}\geq 0
, by the Definition
2.1
, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle u_{z_{s}}\left(N(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ell^{-1}(N(\xi))\langle z(\xi),N(\xi)+\cos\theta E_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell^{-1}(\xi)\langle z(\xi),\xi+\cos\theta E_{n+1}\rangle-\ell^{-1}(\xi)\langle z(\xi),2E_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ell^{-1}(\xi)\langle z(\xi),\xi+\cos\theta E_{n+1}\rangle=u_{z_{s}}(\xi),

where the last line used the fact that
\langle z(\xi),E_{1}\rangle\leq 0
. Moreover, the above inequality is strict for a subset of
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
of positive measure.

Consider the function

u_{z_{t}}(\xi)\coloneqq u_{z_{s}}(\xi)+t\frac{\xi_{1}}{\ell(\xi)},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\penalty\ \penalty\ t\in(0,t_{0}).

From Definition
2.1
, we know that
u_{z_{t}}
is the capillary support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}
with respect to the point
z_{t}=-tE_{1}
for all
0<t<t_{0}
. From (
2.15
), we have
u_{z_{t}}(\xi)>0
for all
t\in(0,t_{0})
.
Using Proposition
2.9
and (
2.16
), we compute

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left({\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{\ast}_{z_{t}})\right)\Big|_{t=0}=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\xi_{1}}{u_{z_{s}}^{n+2}(\xi)}d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{\{\xi_{1}>0\}}\frac{\xi_{1}}{u_{z_{s}}^{n+2}(\xi)}d\sigma+\int_{\{\xi_{1}<0\}}\frac{\xi_{1}}{u_{z_{s}}^{n+2}(\xi)}d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\int_{\{\xi_{1}>0\}}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{u_{z_{s}}^{n+2}(\xi)}-\frac{\xi_{1}}{u_{z_{s}}^{n+2}(N(\xi))}\right)d\sigma\right)<0,

this reaches a contradiction. Hence we get
z_{s}\in\rm{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
.

Next, we move on to prove (
2.14
).
Let
z_{s}^{\varepsilon,i}\coloneqq z_{s}+\varepsilon E_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq n
. For some sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, we have

z_{s}^{\varepsilon,i}\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for all }|\varepsilon|\leq\varepsilon_{0},

together with Proposition
2.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\left({\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{\ast}_{z_{s}^{\varepsilon,i}})\right)\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\xi_{i}}{u_{z_{s}}^{n+2}(\xi)}d\sigma,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for all }\penalty\ \penalty\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Hence the assertion (
2.14
) follows.

Finally, we prove the uniqueness of the capillary Santaló point. Without loss of generality, we assume the capillary Santaló point
z_{s}
is the origin and it lies in
{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
by the preceding argument. For all
z\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, we rewrite
z\coloneqq(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},0)
, and

(2.17)

\displaystyle u_{z}(\xi)=u(\xi)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z_{i}\xi_{i}}{\ell(\xi)},\quad\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

where
u
is the capillary support function of
\Sigma
with respect to the origin. Inserting (
2.17
) into (
2.11
) implies

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{z}^{\ast})=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell(\xi)}{\left(u(\xi)-\ell^{-1}(\xi)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\xi_{i}\right)^{n+1}}d\sigma\eqqcolon V(z).

A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}V(z)}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}=(n+2)\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}{\ell(\xi)u_{z}^{n+3}(\xi)}d\sigma,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for all }1\leq i,j\leq n.

This implies the strict convexity of the function
V(z)
with respect to
z\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, which in turn yields the uniqueness of the capillary Santaló point. This completes the proof.

∎

We are now ready to establish the following capillary version of the Blaschke–Santaló inequality. Compared to the classical form, this version is non-sharp. Nevertheless, it suffices for our purposes in analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the normalized capillary Gauss curvature flow in Section
4
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Given
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, then there holds

(2.18)

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z})\leq\frac{1}{2}{\rm{Vol}}(\mathbb{B}^{n+1})^{2}.

Proof.

Let
R\colon\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be the reflection map, defined by

R(x)\coloneqq(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},-x_{n+1}),\quad x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}.

Let
V=\Sigma\cup R(\Sigma)
and
\Omega
be the bounded domain enclosed by
V
. Then
\Omega
is symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. Denote by
\Omega^{\ast}_{z}
the polar body of the convex body
\Omega
with respect to the point
z\in{\rm{int}}(\Omega)
, defined by

\displaystyle\Omega^{\ast}_{z}\coloneqq\left\{X+z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\mid r\widehat{h}_{z}(\eta)\leq 1,\penalty\ {\rm{where}}\penalty\ X=r\eta\penalty\ {\rm{with}}\penalty\ \eta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\right\},

where
\widehat{h}_{z}\colon\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is the usual support function of
\Omega
with respect to
z
. From
[
33
, Section 10.5 and Eq. (10.28)]
(see also
[
30
]
),
we know there exists a unique point (Santaló point)
z_{0}\in\rm{int}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}\left(\Omega_{z_{0}}^{\ast}\right)=\inf\limits_{z\in\rm{int}(\Omega)}{\rm{Vol}}\left(\Omega^{\ast}_{z}\right),

and the Blaschke-Santaló inequality

(2.19)

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\Omega){\rm{Vol}}\left(\Omega_{z_{0}}^{\ast}\right)\leq{\rm{Vol}}(\mathbb{B}^{n+1})^{2}.

From
[
33
, Eq. (10.22)]
(or
[
29
, Lemma 2]
), there holds

(2.20)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{\eta_{i}}{\widehat{h}^{n+1}_{z_{0}}(\eta)}d\eta,\quad 1\leq i\leq n+1.

In particular, the symmetry of
\Omega
with respect to
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
implies

(2.21)

\displaystyle z_{0}\in{\rm{int}}(\Omega)\cap\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}={\rm{int}}({\widehat{\partial\Sigma}}).

Since
\ell<1
, from the definition of
\widehat{\Sigma}^{\ast}_{z_{0}}
and
\Omega_{z_{0}}^{\ast}
, we deduce that
\widehat{\Sigma}^{\ast}_{z_{0}}\subseteq\Omega^{\ast}_{z_{0}}
, and hence

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{z_{0}}^{\ast})\leq{\rm{Vol}}(\Omega_{z_{0}}^{\ast}).

Together with (
2.19
) and (
2.21
),
we derive

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{\ast}_{z})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{\ast}_{z_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm{Vol}}(\Omega){\rm{Vol}}\left(\Omega_{z_{0}}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm{Vol}}(\mathbb{B}^{n+1})^{2}.

This completes the proof of (
2.18
).
∎

We conclude this subsection by proposing a conjecture about the optimal capillary Blaschke-Santaló inequality. From (
2.18
), it is clear that

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z})\leq C(n,\theta){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})^{2},

where
C(n,\theta)>0
only
depends on
\theta
and
n
.
Hence, we propose the following conjecture about the optimal capillary Blaschke-Santaló inequality.

Conjecture 2.13
.

Given
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, then there holds

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z})\leq{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})^{2}.

Equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap.

3.
Capillary Gauss curvature flow

In this section, we adopt the strategy presented in
[
35
]
(see also
[
5
, Section 15.3]
) and show the first part of Theorem
1.1
.
Precisely, we establish the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a strictly convex capillary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, then flow (
1.3
) has a smooth solution
\Sigma_{t}\coloneqq X(\cdot,t)
, which is strictly convex capillary hypersurface for all
t\in[0,T^{*})
where
T^{*}\coloneqq\frac{{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{0})}{(n+1){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})}.
Moreover, the capillary hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
shrink to a point
p\in\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
as
t\to T^{*}
.

We divide the proof of Theorem
3.1
into two steps. First, we show that as
t\rightarrow T^{\ast}
(with
T^{\ast}<+\infty
), the capillary inner radius of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
tends to zero. This, in turn, implies that the volume of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
vanishes in finite time.
Second, using a contradiction argument, we conclude that as
t\rightarrow T^{\ast}
, the capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
does not collapse to a lower-dimensional convex set but instead contracts to a point.

3.1.
The scalar flow and finite time existence

Using the capillary Gauss map parametrization in (
2.5
), we transform flow (
1.3
) into the problem of solving a parabolic Monge-Ampère equation subject to a Robin boundary condition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be a strictly convex solution to flow (
1.3
) for
t\in[0,T^{*})
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, then its support function
h(\cdot,t)\colon\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\partial_{t}h\penalty\ [\det(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma)]&=&-(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle)&\text{ in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,T^{*}),\\
\nabla_{\mu}h&=&\cot\theta h&\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,T^{*}),\\
h(\cdot,0)&=&h_{0}(\cdot)&\text{ on }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\end{array}\right.

where
\mu
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and
h_{0}
is support function of
\Sigma_{0}
.

Proof.

To derive the first equation in (
3.2
), we adopt the argument as in
[
35
, Page 869-870]
. In fact, consider a family of strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
that satisfies (
1.3
), its support function
h(\xi,t)
satisfies

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h=\left\langle\frac{\partial X}{\partial t},\nu\right\rangle=-K\langle\tilde{\nu},\nu\rangle,

together with the simple fact that
\nu(X)=\xi-\cos\theta e
and
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, it follows

\langle\tilde{\nu},\nu\rangle=1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle=\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle.

On the other hand, we know

K=\frac{1}{\det(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma)},

by inserting this into (
3.5
), we derive the first equation (
3.2
) follows. The second equation in (
3.2
) can be derived by following the same argument in the proof of
[
27
, Proposition 2.4]
, and is therefore omitted here.
∎

Notice that (
3.2
) is a strictly parabolic equation as long as
\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma>0
. Hence, by applying the standard parabolic theory with Neumann boundary condition, one can easily obtain the short-time existence for the flow (
3.2
). This, in turn, implies the short-time existence of flow (
1.3
). Next, we show that the finite time existence of flow (
1.3
) (or equivalently flow (
1.6
)).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a strictly convex capillary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
with
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, then capillary hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
of flow (
1.3
) exists for a finite time
T^{*}<\infty
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that the origin lies in the interior of
\text{int}(\widehat{\partial{\Sigma_{0}}})
. Then there exists some
r_{0}>0
such that
\Sigma_{0}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{r_{0},\theta}}
. Define

r(t)\coloneqq\left[r_{0}^{n+1}-(n+1)t\right]^{\frac{1}{n+1}},

we find that
{\mathcal{C}_{r(t),\theta}}
is a solution to flow (
1.3
) starting from
\Sigma_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{r_{0},\theta}
.
By the comparison principle (see, e.g.,
[
36
, Proposition 4.2]
), we know that
\Sigma_{t}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{r(t),\theta}}
.
It is obvious that
r(t)
converges to zero at a finite time as
t\nearrow\frac{r_{0}^{n+1}}{n+1}
. Therefore,
\Sigma_{t}
cannot exist beyond that time.
∎

3.2.
Evolution equations

In this subsection, we derive evolution equations for various geometric quantities.
For convenience, we introduce a linearized parabolic operator with respect to flow (
3.2
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\partial_{t}-\ell K\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}\nabla^{2}_{ij},

where
(a^{ij})
is the inverse matrix of the principal radii matrix
A\coloneqq(a_{ij})
with
a_{ij}\coloneqq h_{ij}+h\sigma_{ij}.

It is easy to observe that the mean curvature

H=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}\delta_{ij}.

In what follows, we adopt the Einstein summation convention.

First, we have the following evolution equations about the support function
h
and the capillary support function
u
, respectively.

Proposition 3.4
.

Along flow (
3.2
), the functions
h
and
u
satisfy

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}h=K\ell(hH-n-1),

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u=2Ka^{ij}\nabla_{i}u\nabla_{j}\ell-(n+1)K+KHu.

Along
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}h=\cot\theta h,

and

(3.9)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}u=0.

Proof.

First, we prove (
3.6
) and (
3.7
). Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal frame on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
such that
e_{n}=\mu
. Using Eq. (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h-K\ell a^{ij}\nabla_{ij}h=-K\ell-K\ell a^{ij}(a_{ij}-h\delta_{ij}).

Since
a^{ij}\delta_{ij}=H
, we conclude that (
3.6
) holds.

A direct computation yields

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}h}{\ell}+K\ell a^{ij}\left(\frac{2\nabla_{i}h\nabla_{j}\ell}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{h\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\ell}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{2h\nabla_{i}\ell\nabla_{j}\ell}{\ell^{3}}\right).

Since

(3.11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\ell+\ell\delta_{ij}=-\cos\theta\langle\xi-\cos\theta e,e\rangle\delta_{ij}+\ell\delta_{ij}=\delta_{ij},

substituting (
3.6
) and (
3.11
) into (
3.10
), we get (
3.7
).

Next, we derive the boundary conditions (
3.8
) and (
3.9
).
Since
D_{e_{i}}X
is tangent to
\Sigma
at
X(\xi)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nu(X),D_{e_{i}}X\rangle=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

If
\xi\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then
X(\xi)\in\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. From (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mu,X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sin\theta e+\cos\theta\overline{\nu},X\rangle=\cos\theta\langle\overline{\nu},X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta\langle\cos\theta\mu+\sin\theta\nu(X),X\rangle=\cos^{2}\theta\langle\mu,X\rangle+\sin\theta\cos\theta h,

which implies that

\displaystyle\langle\mu,X\rangle=\cot\theta h.

Differentiating the support function
h
gives

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\mu}}h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D_{\mu}T^{-1}(\xi),X\rangle+\langle T^{-1}(\xi),D_{\mu}X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D_{\mu}\xi,X\rangle=\langle\mu,X\rangle,

where
T^{-1}(\xi)\coloneqq\nu(X(\xi))
is the unit normal at
X(\xi)
.
Combining the above results yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\mu}}h=\cot\theta h.

Using (
2.1
) again, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta\langle\mu,e\rangle=\cos\theta\sin\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cot\theta(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle).

Consequently, (
3.9
) can be derived from (
3.12
) and (
3.8
).
∎

The evolution equation of the Gauss curvature
K
will be useful for our subsequent analysis.

Proposition 3.5
.

Along flow (
3.2
), there holds

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}K=2Ka^{ij}\nabla_{i}K\nabla_{j}\ell+K^{2}H.

On
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}K=0.

Proof.

Direct calculations yield

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}K-K\ell a^{ij}\nabla_{ij}K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Ka^{ij}(\nabla_{ij}(\partial_{t}h)+\partial_{t}h\delta_{ij})-K\ell a^{ij}\nabla_{ij}K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ka^{ij}(\nabla_{ij}(K\ell)+K\ell\delta_{ij})-K\ell a^{ij}\nabla_{ij}K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Ka^{ij}\nabla_{i}K\nabla_{j}\ell+K^{2}H,

where (
3.11
) has been used in the last equality.
To show (
3.14
), by taking the time derivative on (
3.8
) along
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}(\partial_{t}h)=\cot\theta\partial_{t}h.

In view of

\partial_{t}h=-K\left(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle\right),

together with (
3.12
), then we have (
3.14
) on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.
∎

3.3.
Curvature estimates

In this subsection, we establish curvature estimates under the assumption that the capillary inner radius
\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t},\theta)
is bounded below by a positive constant. The approach is inspired by Tso
[
35
]
and Guan-Ni
[
21
]
. We remark that the two-sided estimates for the Gauss curvature hold for all contact angles
\theta\in(0,\pi)
.

First of all, we prove that the Gauss curvature of the solution
\Sigma_{t}
to flow (
1.3
) is uniformly bounded from below.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
K_{0}
be the Gauss curvature of initial strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
with
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, and
\Sigma_{t}
solves (
1.3
) on
[0,T^{*})
. Then there holds

(3.15)

\displaystyle K(\xi,t)\geq\min_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}K_{0},

for all
(\xi,t)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,T^{*})
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.5
, we see

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}K\geq 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ mod }\nabla K,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ in }\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

and
\nabla_{\mu}K=0
on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Then (
3.15
) follows directly from the maximum principle.
∎

Next, if the capillary inner radius of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
has some positive lower bound, we show that the Gauss curvature of the solution
\Sigma_{t}
to flow (
1.3
) is uniformly bounded from above. In order to prove it, we introduce a new test function

\displaystyle\varphi\coloneqq\frac{K}{u-c_{0}},

where
u
is the capillary support function and
c_{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\inf\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,T^{*})}u(\xi,t)
. The function
\varphi
is a natural adaptation of the one used by Tso
[
35
]
in the capillary setting, see also a similar idea in
[
34
, Section 3.2]
for a mean curvature type flow. A key observation for us to introduce this new test function is that it satisfies a homogeneous Neumann boundary value condition on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, see Eq. (
3.17
).

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be the solution of flow (
1.3
) on
[0,T^{*})
with
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. If the capillary inner radius
\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t},\theta)
of
\Sigma_{t}
has a uniform positive lower bound
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, then there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}
and the initial data, such that

\displaystyle K(\xi,t)\leq C,

for all
(\xi,t)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,T^{*})
.

Proof.

First, if the capillary inner radius of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
has a uniformly positive lower bound
\varepsilon_{0}
for all
t\in[0,T^{*})
, then according to Remark
2.2
,
there exists a point
z_{0}\in\text{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma_{t}})
, such that
u_{z_{0}}(\xi,t)\geq\varepsilon_{0}
for all
(\xi,t)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,T^{*})
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
z_{0}
is the origin and consider the function

\displaystyle\varphi(\xi,t)\coloneqq\frac{K}{u-c_{0}},

where
c_{0}\coloneqq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}
. Thus,
\varphi
is well-defined for all
(\xi,t)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\times[0,T^{*})
.

Direct computation yields

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}K}{u-c_{0}}-\frac{K}{(u-c_{0})^{2}}\mathcal{L}u+\frac{2K\ell}{u-c_{0}}a^{ij}\nabla_{i}\varphi\nabla_{j}u.

Inserting Eq. (
3.7
) and (
3.13
) into (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2Ka^{ij}\nabla_{i}K\nabla_{j}\ell+K^{2}H}{u-c_{0}}-\frac{K}{(u-c_{0})^{2}}\left(-(n+1)K+KHu\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{2K}{(u-c_{0})^{2}}Ka^{ij}\nabla_{j}u\nabla_{i}\ell+\frac{2K\ell}{u-c_{0}}a^{ij}\nabla_{i}\varphi\nabla_{j}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Ka^{ij}\nabla_{i}\varphi\nabla_{j}\ell+2\varphi\ell a^{ij}\nabla_{i}\varphi\nabla_{j}u+\varphi^{2}(n+1-c_{0}H)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle nc_{0}^{1+\frac{1}{n}}\varphi^{2}\left(-\varphi^{\frac{1}{n}}+\frac{n+1}{nc_{0}^{1+\frac{1}{n}}}\right),\quad\text{ mod }\nabla\varphi,

where in the last inequality we have used the fact that
H\geq nK^{\frac{1}{n}}
and
u\geq 2c_{0}
.
On the other hand, in view of (
3.9
) and (
3.14
), we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\varphi=0,\quad\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

By the maximum principle, we have

\displaystyle\varphi\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(\frac{n+1}{nc_{0}}\right)^{n},\max_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\varphi(\cdot,0)\right\}.

It follows

(3.18)

\displaystyle K\leq\max\left\{\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)^{n}\frac{\max\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}u(\cdot,t)}{c_{0}^{1+n}},\frac{\max\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}u(\cdot,t)\cdot K_{0}}{u(\cdot,0)-c_{0}}\right\}.

Since
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
and
\nabla_{\mu}u=0
on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have

u\leq\max\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}u(\cdot,0).

Therefore, as long as flow (
1.3
) exists and
u\geq 2c_{0}
, there holds

\displaystyle K\leq\max\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}u(\cdot,0)\cdot\max\left\{\frac{1}{c_{0}}\left(\frac{n+1}{nc_{0}}\right)^{n},\frac{\max\limits_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}K_{0}}{c_{0}}\right\}.

Thus the statement follows.
∎

Finally, if the capillary inner radius remains positive, we establish the positive lower and upper bounds of the principal radii. This, in turn, implies that flow (
1.3
) (also equivalently (
3.2
)) remains uniformly parabolic on any time interval and the strict convexity is preserved along flow (
1.3
).

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be the solution of the flow (
1.3
) on the time interval
[0,T^{*})
. If
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
and the capillary inner radius of
\Sigma_{t}
has a uniform positive lower bound
\varepsilon_{0}
, then there exist positive constants
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
such that

(3.19)

\displaystyle C_{0}^{-1}\leq\kappa_{i}\leq C_{1}\quad\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n,

where the constant
C_{1}
only depends on
n,\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}
and initial data.
In particular, the constant
C_{0}
only depends on the initial data and
n
.

Proof.

To show (
3.19
), we first establish the positive lower bound of the principal curvatures. Define

\displaystyle\Phi\coloneqq\Delta h+nh=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}},

which is the harmonic curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
.

First of all, we
claim
that if
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, there holds

(3.20)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\Phi\leq 0\quad\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

In fact, along
\partial{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta}
, we choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that
e_{n}=\mu
. Since
\nabla_{\mu}h=\cot\theta h
on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then for all
1\leq\beta\leq n-1
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\beta}(h_{n})=\cot\theta\nabla_{\beta}h,

together with the Gauss-Weingarten equation of
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle h_{\beta n}=\nabla^{2}h(e_{\beta},e_{n})=\nabla_{e_{\beta}}(\nabla h(e_{n}))-\nabla h(\nabla_{e_{\beta}}e_{n})=0.

Applying the Gauss-Weingarten equation of
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
again and combining with (
3.21
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\beta\beta n}h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla^{3}h)(e_{\beta},e_{\beta},e_{n})=(\nabla_{e_{\beta}}(\nabla^{2}h))(e_{\beta},e_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{\beta}}(\nabla^{2}h(e_{\beta},e_{n}))-\nabla^{2}h(\nabla_{e_{\beta}}e_{\beta},e_{n})-\nabla^{2}h(e_{\beta},\nabla_{e_{\beta}}e_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cot\theta h_{nn}-\cot\theta h_{\beta\beta}.

The commutator formula on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{n\beta\beta}h=\nabla_{\beta\beta n}h-h_{n}=\cot\theta(h_{nn}-h_{\beta\beta}-h),

then it follows

(3.22)

\displaystyle\nabla_{n}a_{\beta\beta}=\nabla_{n}h_{\beta\beta}+h_{n}=\cot\theta(a_{nn}-a_{\beta\beta}).

From (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=1}^{n-1}a^{\beta\beta}\nabla_{n}a_{\beta\beta}+a^{nn}\nabla_{n}a_{nn}=0,

that is,

\displaystyle\nabla_{n}a_{nn}=-\sum_{\beta=1}^{n-1}\frac{a^{\beta\beta}}{a^{nn}}\nabla_{n}a_{\beta\beta},

which, together with (
3.22
), implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=1}^{n-1}\nabla_{\mu}a_{\beta\beta}+\nabla_{\mu}a_{nn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cot\theta\sum_{\beta=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{a^{\beta\beta}}{a^{nn}}\right)(a_{nn}-a_{\beta\beta})\leq 0,

where the last inequality follows from the positive definiteness of
(a_{ij})
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. Hence, we prove the assertion (
3.20
).

Next, we compute the evolution equation of
\Phi
, bear in mind that

\Delta\ell+n\ell=n.

From Eq. (
3.2
), we derive

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta(\partial_{t}h)+n\partial_{t}h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\ell\Delta K-2\nabla\ell\cdot\nabla K-K\Delta\ell-n\ell K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\ell K\Delta K-2\nabla\ell\cdot\nabla K-nK,

and

(3.23)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}K=-Ka^{kl}\nabla_{i}a_{kl},

By the covariant derivative commutator formula on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}=\nabla_{q}\nabla_{p}h_{ij}+2(h_{pq}\delta_{ij}-h_{ij}\delta_{pq})+h_{iq}\delta_{pj}-h_{jp}\delta_{iq},

which implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}a_{pq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}h_{pq}+h_{ij}\delta_{pq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{q}\nabla_{p}a_{ij}+a_{pq}\delta_{ij}-a_{ij}\delta_{pq}+a_{iq}\delta_{pj}-a_{jp}\delta_{iq}.

Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{ij}K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla_{j}Ka^{kl}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}-Ka^{kl}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}-K\nabla_{j}a^{kl}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Ka^{kl}(\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}a_{ij}+a_{kl}\delta_{ij}-a_{ij}\delta_{kl}+a_{il}\delta_{kj}-a_{jk}\delta_{il})

\displaystyle+Ka^{kp}\nabla_{j}a_{pq}a^{ql}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}+Ka^{pq}\nabla_{j}a_{pq}a^{kl}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}

and

\displaystyle\Delta K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ka^{kp}\nabla_{i}a_{pq}a^{ql}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}+Ka^{pq}\nabla_{i}a_{pq}a^{kl}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}

\displaystyle-Ka^{kl}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}\Phi-n^{2}K+\Phi HK.

Altogether yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell Ka^{kl}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{k}\Phi+n^{2}\ell-\ell K\Phi H-nK-2\nabla\ell\cdot\nabla K

\displaystyle-\ell Ka^{kp}\nabla_{i}a_{pq}a^{ql}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}-\ell Ka^{pq}\nabla_{i}a_{pq}a^{kl}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}.

From (
3.20
), we have that
\Phi
attains its maximum value at some interior point, say
\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\setminus\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
. Assume that
a_{ij}
is diagonal at this point, which in turn implies that
a^{ij}
is also diagonal at
\xi_{0}
. Then at
\xi_{0}
, we find

(3.24)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\Phi=\partial_{t}\Phi-\ell Ka^{kl}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n\ell-1)nK-\ell K\Phi H

\displaystyle-\ell Ka^{kp}\nabla_{i}a_{pq}a^{ql}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}-\ell Ka^{pq}\nabla_{i}a_{pq}a^{kl}\nabla_{i}a_{kl}-2\nabla\ell\cdot\nabla K.

From (
3.23
) we have

\displaystyle-2\nabla\ell\cdot\nabla K=2\cos\theta\langle e_{i},e\rangle Ka^{kk}\nabla_{i}a_{kk}.

By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the convexity of
\Sigma_{t}
, we obtain

(3.25)

\displaystyle-\ell\sum_{k,l,i}a^{kk}a^{ll}(\nabla_{i}a_{kl})^{2}-\ell\sum_{i}(\sum_{k}a^{kk}\nabla_{i}a_{kk})^{2}-2\cos\theta\langle e_{i},e\rangle a^{kk}\nabla_{i}a_{kk}\leq C,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

where the constant
C
only depends on
n
.
Inserting (
3.25
) into (
3.24
), and using the fact that
H\geq nK^{1/n}
, we derive that

\displaystyle\ell K^{\frac{1}{n}}\Phi\leq\frac{1}{n}\ell H\Phi\leq(n\ell-1)+C,

which, together with Proposition
3.6
, gives

\displaystyle\Phi\leq C_{0},

for some positive constant
C_{0}
depending on
n
and
\Sigma_{0}
. It implies

(3.26)

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}\geq C^{-1}_{0}.

The upper bound of
\kappa_{i}
follows directly from (
3.26
) and Proposition
3.7
, since

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}=K\prod_{j\neq i}\kappa_{j}^{-1}\leq CC_{0}^{-(n-1)}\coloneqq C_{1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

(see also
[
5
, Chapter 15]
or
[
20
, Section 4]
) to complete the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
denote the convex domain enclosed in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
by
\Sigma_{t}
and
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. Since
\Sigma_{t}
is the solution of flow (
1.3
),
by the comparison principle we know that
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}\subset\widehat{\Sigma}_{s}
, for any
s<t
.

First, we demonstrate that the capillary inner radius
\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t},\theta)
of
\Sigma_{t}
tends to zero as
t\rightarrow T^{\ast}
. In fact, if this is not true, since the flow is a contracting flow and by the definition of
\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t},\theta)
, we know that there exists a spherical cap with some radius
r_{0}>0
which is contained in
\Sigma_{t}
for
t\in[0,T^{\ast})
. Combining Proposition
3.8
and
the standard parabolic equation theory (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 6.1, Theorem 6.4 and Theorem 6.5]
, and also
[
23
, Theorem 14.23]
), we obtain a uniform
C^{k,\alpha}
estimate of the solution to flow (
3.2
) for all
k\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Therefore, flow (
3.2
) can be extended up to time
T^{*}+\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
, which contradicts the fact that
T^{*}<+\infty
is the maximal existence time of flow (
3.2
).

Next, we prove that as
t\rightarrow T^{\ast}
, the hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
converges to a point. Suppose, by contradiction, that
\cap_{t\geq 0}\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
is not a point. Let
\text{diam}(\cap_{t\geq 0}\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=l_{0}=\text{dist}(x_{1},x_{2})
where
x_{1},x_{2}\in\cap_{t\geq 0}\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
.
Since the inner capillary radius tends to zero, the volume of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
also converges to zero. Thus, there exists a two dimension hyperplane
\mathcal{H}
such that the area of
\bar{H}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}\cap\left(\cap_{t\geq 0}\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}\right)
converges to zero. Along the boundary of
\bar{H}\cap\partial(\cap_{t\geq 0}\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
, there must be some point with arbitrarily small principal curvature. However, Proposition
3.8
implies the principal curvatures of
\Sigma_{t}
have a uniformly lower bound. Thus, we derive a contradiction, and
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
converges to a point as
t\rightarrow T^{\ast}
.
∎

4.
Normalized capillary Gauss curvature flow

The primary objective of this section is to show that the normalized capillary Gauss curvature flow (

4.1.
Normalized capillary Gauss curvature flow

Suppose that
\widetilde{X}\colon M\times[0,T^{\ast})\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
satisfies flow (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{X}(\cdot,\tau)&=&-\widetilde{K}(\cdot,\tau)\tilde{\nu}(\cdot,\tau)&\hbox{ in }M\times[0,T^{*}),\\

From Theorem

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}\coloneqq\widetilde{X}(M,\tau)

. Through a horizontal translation, we can assume that the limiting point

is the origin. To investigate the limiting shape of flow (

\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}
(see Proposition

(4.5)

\displaystyle X(x,t)\coloneqq e^{t}\widetilde{X}(x,\tau),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ x\in M,

where

3.1

By a direct computation,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X(x,t)+e^{t}\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{X}(x,\tau)\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial t}

\displaystyle X(x,t)-e^{t}\frac{(n+1){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\widetilde{\Sigma}}_{\tau})}{\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\tau}}\widetilde{K}(\cdot,\tau)(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)d\mu_{\tau}}\widetilde{K}\tilde{\nu}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\partial_{t}X(\cdot,t)&=&X(\cdot,t)-K(\cdot,t)\tilde{\nu}(\cdot,t)&\hbox{ in }M\times[0,+\infty),\\
\langle\tilde{\nu},e\rangle&=&0&\hbox{ on }\partial M\times[0,+\infty),\\

Proposition 4.1

If

From

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})

\displaystyle(n+1)\left({\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})-{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})\right),

4.2.
Capillary entropy and related properties

In this subsection, we introduce the capillary entropy functional for capillary convex bodies. This functional plays an important role in investigating the asymptotic shape of the normalized capillary Gauss curvature flow (
4.1
). The concept of this entropy is inspired by the one defined for closed convex hypersurfaces, as presented in the papers of Guan-Ni
[
21
]
and Firey
[
18
]
.

Definition 4.4

(Capillary entropy functional)
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
be a capillary convex body. The capillary entropy functional
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}
associated with
\widehat{\Sigma}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\coloneqq\sup_{z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},z),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},z)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma,

and
\omega_{\theta}\coloneqq|\mathcal{C}_{\theta}|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}|
is the capillary area of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

In the following, we present some properties of the capillary entropy functional. Similar properties for closed convex hypersurfaces, one can refer to
[
21
, Section 2]
,
[
6
, Section 2]
, and
[
8
, Section 2, 3]
.
The underlying ideas of the proofs of some propositions are essentially the same as those in
[
21
]
. Nevertheless, for the sake of completeness, we provide the full arguments here, including the necessary modifications to account for the capillary boundary. We also remark that these results are of independent interest and may be useful for other related problems for capillary hypersurfaces.

To begin with, by using the capillary Blaschke-Santaló inequality established in Proposition
2.12
, we show that the capillary entropy functional
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
is bounded from below. In particular, if Conjecture
2.13
holds, this would imply that the capillary entropy functional is non-negative. Nevertheless, (
4.21
) is sufficient for our purposes later on.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma})={\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})
. If
z_{s}\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}

is the capillary Santaló point of
\widehat{\Sigma}
,
then

(4.21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{s}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\geq-C,

where the constant
C>0
only depends on
n
and
\theta
.

Proof.

The proof follows closely the idea of Guan-Ni
[
21
, Proposition 1.1]
, by adjusting to use Proposition
2.9
and Proposition
2.12
.

From Proposition
2.9
,

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{*}_{z_{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\ell(\xi)}{u^{n+1}_{z_{s}}(\xi)}d\sigma.

By applying Jensen’s inequality, we deduce that

(4.22)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\left(\frac{1}{u^{n+1}_{z_{s}}(\xi)}\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\right)\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{1}{u^{n+1}_{z_{s}}(\xi)}\ell(\xi)d\sigma=\frac{n+1}{\omega_{\theta}}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{z_{s}}^{\ast}).

Inserting the simple fact that
\omega_{\theta}=(n+1){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})
into (
4.22
), and together with Proposition
2.12
, we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{(n+1)}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{s}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\right)\leq\frac{{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{z_{s}}^{\ast})}{{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{{\rm{Vol}}(\mathbb{B}^{n+1})}{{\rm{Vol}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta})}}\right)^{2},

then this implies (
4.21
).
∎

The following proposition shows that the capillary entropy functional
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
is attained at a point
z_{e}
on the flat part of the boundary of
\widehat{\Sigma}
, namely
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
. We refer to this point
z_{e}=z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma})
as the
capillary entropy point
of the capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
.
We note that Guan-Ni
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
proved that the entropy point of a smooth, closed, convex body lies in the interior. In contrast, in our setting, the capillary entropy point lies on the flat part of the boundary of the capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. Then there exists a unique point
z_{e}\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{e}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps: first, we establish the existence of
z_{e}
, second, we prove its uniqueness.

Step 1: Existence
. By Definition
4.4
, we know there exists a sequence
\{z_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\text{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
and some point
z_{e}\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
such that

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},z_{j})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ {\rm{and}}\penalty\ z_{j}\rightarrow z_{e}\penalty\ \penalty\ {\rm{as}}\penalty\ j\rightarrow+\infty.

From (
2.4
), (
2.6
) and
[
27
, Remark 2.8]
, we know

(4.24)

\displaystyle u_{z_{j}}(\xi)\leq 2\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta),\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for all }\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta},

by Fatou’s Lemma and (
4.23
), we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\left(\frac{2\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)}{u_{z_{e}}(\xi)}\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\leq\liminf\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\left(\frac{2\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)}{u_{z_{j}}(\xi)}\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma,

in view of (
4.23
), then it follows

(4.25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{e}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\geq\limsup\limits_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(\log u_{z_{j}}(\xi))\ell(\xi)d\sigma=\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}).

On the other hand, by Definition
4.4
, it is obvious that

\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{e}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\leq\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}),

together with (
4.25
), we obtain that
z_{e}
is the desired supremum point of
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
.

Step 2: Uniqueness
. Without loss of generality, we assume that the origin lies in
{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
. For all point
z\coloneqq(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},0)\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, it follows from (
2.17
) that we can rewrite

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(\log u_{z}(\xi))\ell(\xi)d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\left(u(\xi)-\ell^{-1}(\xi)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\xi_{i}\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma

\displaystyle\eqqcolon

\displaystyle F(z),

where
u(\xi)
is the capillary support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}
with respect to the origin.
A direct calculation yields

(4.26)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}F(z)}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}=-\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}{\ell(\xi)u_{z}^{2}(\xi)}d\sigma,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Hence, this implies that
F(z)
is strictly concave with respect to
z\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
, which in turn yields the uniqueness of
z_{e}
.

∎

The following proposition asserts that the capillary entropy point
z_{e}
, as established in Proposition

\widehat{\partial\Sigma}

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
with

in

(4.28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})>\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log U(\xi)\right)\ell d\sigma.

Proof.

We begin by showing that the capillary entropy point

{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})

2.11

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{t}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\right)\Big|_{t=0}=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\xi_{1}}{u_{z_{e}}(\xi)}d\sigma

\displaystyle\int_{\{\xi_{1}>0\}}\frac{\xi_{1}}{u_{z_{e}}(\xi)}d\sigma+\int_{\{\xi_{1}<0\}}\frac{\xi_{1}}{u_{z_{e}}(\xi)}d\sigma

Next, we show that the capillary entropy functional provides control over the geometric quantities of a capillary convex body. More precisely, the capillary entropy
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}

(4.29)

\displaystyle\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\leq Ce^{\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})},

(4.30)

\displaystyle\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\geq C^{-1}{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma})e^{-n\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\rho_{0},\theta}(x_{0})}

\widehat{\Sigma}

(4.31)

\displaystyle|z|\geq(1-\cos\theta)\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta).

z_{1}>\frac{1}{2}|z|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\langle\xi,\widehat{z}\rangle|=\frac{1}{2}|\langle\xi-\cos\theta e,\widehat{z}\rangle|\leq h_{\frac{\widehat{z}}{2}}(\xi),\quad\forall\penalty\ \xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

(4.32)

\displaystyle\log z_{1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell(\xi)d\sigma-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(\log 2\ell(\xi))\ell(\xi)d\sigma+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log|\xi_{1}|\ell(\xi)d\sigma

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\left(\frac{1}{2\ell}|\langle\xi,\widehat{z}\rangle|\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{\frac{\widehat{z}}{2}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\leq\omega_{\theta}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}).\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle\log z_{n+1}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell(\xi)d\sigma+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log(\xi_{n+1}+\cos\theta)\ell(\xi)d\sigma-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\ell(\xi)\ell(\xi)d\sigma

\displaystyle z_{n+1}\leq C_{2}e^{\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}.

Next, we proceed to show (

\displaystyle 2{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma})={\rm{Vol}}(\Omega).

Together with (

) and (

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2n\omega_{n-1}\left(\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma})\right)^{n}\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle 2n\omega_{n-1}\left(\sin\theta\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\right)^{n}\sin\theta\rho_{-}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta),

.
Inserting (

) and (
4.35

) holds.

∎

The following proposition shows that the difference between

and

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
with

. Then there exists a positive constant

depending only on

and

(4.37)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\leq\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})-De^{-2\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}|z-z_{e}|^{2},\penalty\ \forall z\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}),

where

is the capillary entropy point of
\widehat{\Sigma}
, as established in Proposition

.

Proof.

Proposition
4.7
ensures that

. For all

, the strict convexity of

lies in
{\rm{int}}({\widehat{\partial\Sigma}})
. Denote

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma=\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\left(u_{z_{e}}(\xi)-\ell^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\xi_{i}\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\eqqcolon Q(t).

\displaystyle\frac{\partial Q(t)}{\partial t_{i}}\Big|_{t=0}=-\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\xi_{i}}{u_{z_{e}}(\xi)}d\sigma=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for all }\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}Q(t)}{\partial t_{i}\partial t_{j}}=-\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}{\ell(\xi)u_{z}^{2}(\xi)}d\sigma,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for all }\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n,

4.24

\displaystyle\frac{1}{u_{z_{*}}(\xi)}\geq\frac{1}{2\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)}\geq\frac{1}{2C}e^{-\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}.

\displaystyle Q(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Q(0)-\frac{1}{4C^{2}}e^{-2\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}|z-z_{e}|^{2}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left<\xi,\frac{z-z_{e}}{|z-z_{e}|}\right>^{2}\ell^{-1}(\xi)d\sigma

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})-De^{-2\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}|z-z_{e}|^{2},

where the constant

only depends on

and
\theta

∎

4.3.
Monotonicity and bounds of support function

In this subsection, we show the key monotone property of the capillary entropy along normalized flow (
4.1
). Subsequently, by integrating the properties of the capillary entropy derived in Section
4.2
, we can establish the positive lower bound of the capillary support function. For notational convenience, along the flow (
4.1
), we denote the capillary entropy point
z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
of
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
by

for all

Proposition 4.10
.

Along flow (
4.1
), the capillary entropy
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
is non-increasing for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

For any
t_{0}>0
,
let
u_{e(t_{0})}
be the capillary support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t_{0}}
with respect to the capillary entropy point
e({t_{0}})\in\text{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{t_{0}})
. We define the function

By the very definition, we note that

(4.41)

\displaystyle U(\xi,t_{0})=u_{e(t_{0})}(\xi),\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}.

Hence
U(\cdot,t)>0
in
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
for all
t\in(t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}]
, where
\varepsilon>0
is some sufficiently small constant.
Moreover, it is readily seen that
U(\cdot,t)
satisfies
\nabla^{2}(\ell U)+(\ell U)I>0
in
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and
\nabla_{\mu}(\ell U)=\cot\theta\ell U
on
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
, then
U
is the capillary support function by using
[
28
, Definition 2.5 and Proposition 2.6]
. Thus, by applying (
4.28
) to
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
, we obtain

(4.42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log U(\xi,t)\right)\ell d\sigma,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for all }t\in(t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}].

On the other hand, it follows from (
4.1
) that
U(\cdot,t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\partial_{t}U&=&U-K(\xi,t)&\text{ in }\penalty\ \mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\
\nabla_{\mu}U&=&0&\text{ on }\penalty\ \partial\mathcal{C}_{\theta},\end{array}\right.

where we recall that
K(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\det\left(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma\right)}=\frac{1}{\det\left(\nabla^{2}(\ell U)+\ell U\sigma\right)}.

A direct calculation and using (
4.3
), it follows

(4.46)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(\log U(\xi,t))\ell(\xi)d\sigma=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{U(\xi,t)-K(\xi,t)}{U(\xi,t)}\ell(\xi)d\sigma.

Note that

(4.47)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{U(\xi,t)}{K(\xi,t)}}-\sqrt{\frac{K(\xi,t)}{U(\xi,t)}}\right)^{2}\ell(\xi)d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\frac{K(\xi,t)}{U(\xi,t)}+\frac{U(\xi,t)}{K(\xi,t)}-2\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{K(\xi,t)}{U(\xi,t)}\ell(\xi)d\sigma+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{h(\xi,t)-e^{t-t_{0}}\langle e(t_{0}),\xi\rangle}{K(\xi,t)}d\sigma-2\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell(\xi)d\sigma.

Next, we analyze the middle integral term. From
[
28
, Proposition 2.12 and its proof]
and Proposition
4.1
, we have

(4.48)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{h(\xi,t)}{K(\xi,t)}d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}h\det(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma)d\sigma=(n+1)\text{Vol}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)\text{Vol}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta})=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\ell d\sigma.

By divergence theorem on
\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}
, there holds

(4.49)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\langle e(t_{0}),\xi\rangle}{K(\xi,t)}d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\frac{\langle e(t_{0}),\xi-\cos\theta e\rangle}{K(\xi,t)}d\sigma=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\langle e(t_{0}),\nu(\cdot,t)\rangle d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{t}}\text{div}_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\left(e(t_{0})\right)dV=0.

Inserting (
4.48
) and (
4.49
) into (
4.47
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{U(\xi,t)}{K(\xi,t)}}-\sqrt{\frac{K(\xi,t)}{U(\xi,t)}}\right)^{2}\ell(\xi)d\sigma=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(1-\frac{K(\xi,t)}{U(\xi,t)}\right)\ell d\sigma,

together with (
4.46
), we derive

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(\log U(\xi,t))\ell(\xi)d\sigma=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{U(\xi,t)}{K(\xi,t)}}-\sqrt{\frac{K(\xi,t)}{U(\xi,t)}}\right)^{2}\ell(\xi)d\sigma\leq 0,

which holds for all
t\in(t-\varepsilon,t_{0}]
. In view of (
4.41
), it yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log U(\xi,t)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\geq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log U(\xi,t_{0})\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma=\omega_{\theta}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t_{0}}),

together with (
4.42
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log U(\xi,t)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\geq\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t_{0}}),

holds for all
t\in(t-\varepsilon,t_{0}]
. Therefore, the conclusion holds due to the arbitrariness of

.
∎

From Proposition
4.5
and Proposition
4.10
, we see that
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
is uniformly bounded from below and non-increasing. Thus, we know that

Proposition 4.11

Suppose

.

5.
Perspective

In this section, we discuss some generalizations of flow (
1.3
) to the capillary
\alpha
-power Gauss curvature flow in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
. We briefly mention the key steps involved, omitting full details for brevity.
Chow
[
16
]
extended Firey’s result and also Tso
[
35
]
to the
\alpha
-power Gauss curvature flow for convex closed hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X=-K^{\alpha}\nu,

for
\alpha>0
and showed that the limiting point is a round point if
\alpha=\frac{1}{n}
.
Thus, the remaining problem for the
\alpha
-power Gauss curvature flow (
5.1
) is to characterize the limiting shape of (
1.1
) for general
\alpha\neq\frac{1}{n}
. When
\alpha=\frac{1}{n+2}
, Andrews
[
1
]
proved that such a flow will converge to an ellipsoid shape point, see also Sapiro and Tannenbaum
[
31
]
for the case
n=1
.
When
n=2
, Andrews
[
2
]
solved Firey’s conjecture completely, i.e., when
\alpha=1
, the flow converges to a round point. Andrews-Chen
[
4
]
showed that when
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, this conclusion also holds. Their methods highly depend on the geometry of surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and seem not to work in high dimensions. Subsequently, when
n\geq 2
, Andrews
[
3
]
proved that if
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{n+2},\frac{1}{n})
, the normalized flow will converge to a self-similar solution of flow (
5.1
). A closed hypersurface
M
is said to be a self-similar solution of flow (
5.1
) if it satisfies

(5.2)

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}=\langle X,\nu\rangle.

If
\alpha>\frac{1}{n+2}
, the convergence of flow (
5.1
) starting from a strictly convex closed hypersurface to the soliton Eq. (
5.2
) and the classification of the strictly convex solution to Eq. (
5.2
) were settled by Andrews-Guan-Ni
[
6
]
and Brendle-Choi-Daskalopoulos
[
12
]
, respectively.

Our method allows a similar result for the
\alpha
-power Gauss curvature flow of evolving strictly convex capillary hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}\coloneqq X(M,t)\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
if
\alpha>0
. To be more precise, we consider that the capillary
\alpha
-power Gauss curvature flow in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, which is defined as:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X(x,t)=-K^{\alpha}(x,t)\tilde{\nu}(x,t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (x,t)\in M\times[0,T^{\prime}),

and its corresponding normalized capillary
\alpha
-power Gauss curvature flow:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\partial_{t}X(\cdot,t)&=&X(\cdot,t)-\frac{(n+1){\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})K^{\alpha}(\cdot,t)}{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}K^{\alpha-1}(\xi,t)(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos\theta\langle\xi,e\rangle)d\xi}\tilde{\nu}(\cdot,t),&\hbox{ in }M\times[0,+\infty),\\
\langle\tilde{\nu},e\rangle&=&0&\hbox{ on }\partial M\times[0,+\infty),\\
X(\cdot,0)&=&X_{0}(\cdot)&\hbox{ on }M,\end{array}\right.

where
X_{0}\colon M\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
is a strictly convex capillary hypersurface. As discussed in the introduction for the case
\alpha=1
, flow (
5.3
) is also closely related to the capillary
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem with
p=1-\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

Remark 5.1
.

Along flow (
5
), then the enclosed volume
{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})
is preserved invariant for all
t\geq 0
, if
{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{0})={\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}})
.

Motivated by prior works on closed convex hypersurfaces, including Andrews–Guan–Ni
[
6
]
for
\alpha>\frac{1}{n+2}
, Guan–Ni
[
21
]
, Firey
[
18
]
for the case
\alpha=1
, and more recently Böröczky–Guan
[
8
, Eq. (1.6)]
in the anisotropic setting, we introduce the capillary
\alpha
-entropy functional for general
\alpha>0
.

Definition 5.2

(Capillary
\alpha
-entropy functional)
.

Let
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
be a capillary convex body, the capillary
\alpha
-entropy functional
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,\theta}
for
\widehat{\Sigma}
is defined by

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\coloneqq\sup_{z_{0}\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})}\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},z_{0}),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma},z_{0})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}u_{z_{0}}(\xi)^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\ell(\xi)d\sigma\right),\penalty\ \penalty\ &\alpha\neq 1,\\

where
d\sigma
is the standard area element on
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
and
\omega_{\theta}
is the capillary area of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
.

Building on the analysis developed in this paper and drawing inspiration from the ideas of Andrews–Guan–Ni
[
6
]
, with the aid of concept (
5.8
), we expect to establish the following result in a forthcoming work.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a strictly convex capillary hypersurface in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. If
\alpha>0
, then the flow (
5.3
) has a smooth solution
\Sigma_{t}\coloneqq X(M,t)
, which is strictly convex capillary hypersurface for all
t\in[0,T^{*})
, where
T^{*}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\alpha n+1}\left(\frac{{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{0})}{{\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\theta})}\right)^{\frac{\alpha n+1}{n+1}}
. As
t\to T^{*}
, then the capillary hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
shrink to a point
p\in\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
.
Moreover, if
\alpha>\frac{1}{n+2}
, the solution of normalized flow (

) converges to a smooth, strictly convex capillary hypersurface
\Sigma_{\infty}\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
as

. Furthermore,
\Sigma_{\infty}
satisfies:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}K^{\alpha}&=&\lambda\frac{\langle X,\nu\rangle}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}&\text{ in }\penalty\ \penalty\ \Sigma_{\infty},\\

.

The classification of solutions to Eq. (

) remains an open problem. It is natural to conjecture that, for
\alpha>\frac{1}{n+2}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, the only strictly convex capillary hypersurface to Eq. (

) (without loss of generality, assuming the Lagrange multiplier

) are the unit spherical cap, namely

. We conclude the paper by formulating the following equivalent conjecture.

Conjecture 5.4
.

Given
\alpha>\frac{1}{n+2}
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)

\mathcal{C}_{\theta}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}h\det^{\alpha}(\nabla^{2}h+h\sigma)=\ell&\text{ in }\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{C}_{\theta},\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(K)\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\log\left(2\rho_{+}(K)\right)\ell(\zeta)d\sigma=\log\left(2\rho_{+}({K})\right).

Next, we proceed to show the lower bound in (
6.2
). Let
\widetilde{K}
be the bounded domain by reflecting
K
with respect to the hyperplane
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. We
claim
that
\widetilde{K}
is a bounded closed convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Since
\bigcup\limits_{x\in K_{u}}\mathcal{N}_{x}K
is a connected component of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
E_{n+1}\in\bigcup\limits_{x\in K_{u}}\mathcal{N}_{x}K
, and from
K\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
, we have
\vartheta(x)\leq\theta
for all
x\in\partial K_{f}
with
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
, then we conclude that

\overline{\bigcup\limits_{x\in K_{u}}\mathcal{N}_{x}K}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta},

which implies the claim. We denote by
\widetilde{z}_{s}
the classical Santaló point of
\widetilde{K}
, using the symmetry of
\widetilde{K}
with respect to
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
and (
2.20
), it implies

(6.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{z}_{s}\in{\rm{int}}(K_{f}).

From
[
33
, Page 547]
or
[
30
, (1.1)]
, there holds

(6.4)

\displaystyle{\rm{Vol}}(\widetilde{K}_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}^{\ast})=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{1}{h_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}^{n+1}(\zeta)}d\sigma,

where
\widetilde{K}_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}^{\ast}
and
h_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}
denote the polar body and support function of
\widetilde{K}
with respect to the point
\widetilde{z}_{s}
, respectively. In view of (
2.19
),
\text{Vol}(\widetilde{K})=2\text{Vol}(K)
and (
6.4
), we obtain

(6.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{1}{h_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}^{n+1}(\zeta)}d\sigma\leq\frac{(n+1){\rm{Vol}}(\mathbb{B}^{n+1})^{2}}{2{{\rm{Vol}}(K)}}.

Since
\ell(\zeta)=1-\cos\theta\langle\zeta,E_{n+1}\rangle<1
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
, then by applying the Jensen’s inequality as (
4.22
), it yields

(6.6)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n}}\log\left(\frac{1}{u_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}^{n+1}(\zeta)}\right)\ell(\zeta)d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n}}\frac{1}{u_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}^{n+1}(\zeta)}\ell(\zeta)d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n}}\frac{1}{h_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}^{n+1}(\zeta)}d\sigma.

Inserting (
6.5
) into (
6.6
), we obtain

\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{\widetilde{z}_{s}}(\zeta)\right)\ell(\zeta)d\sigma\geq-C,

together with (
6.1
) and (
6.3
), it follows that

\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(K)\geq-C,

thus we complete the proof of (
6.2
).
Notice that
K_{f}
is a bounded closed convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\partial{\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}}
, following the same argument as in the proof of Propositions
4.6
and
4.7
, we can show there exists a unique interior point
z_{e}\in\text{int}(K_{f})
, such that
\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(K)=\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{e}}(\zeta)\right)\ell(\zeta)d\sigma
. This completes the proof.
∎

Without causing confusion, we continue to call
z_{e}\coloneqq z_{e}(K)
in Proposition
6.4
the
capillary entropy point
of the
\theta
-capillary convex body
K\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
associated with
\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(K)
. We next show that, under Hausdorff convergence, the limiting convex body of a sequence of capillary convex bodies in
\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
is contained in
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
. That is, its contact angle within
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
is non-increasing.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
be a sequence of bounded capillary convex body, and
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}\in\mathcal{G}
. Suppose that
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}=\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
in the Hausdorff distance sense, then
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
.

Proof.

We just need to show that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\vartheta(x)\leq\theta,\quad\text{for all }x\in\partial(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}\cap\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}).

By assumption and
[
33
, Lemma 1.8.14]
, we have

(6.8)

\displaystyle h_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}}(\zeta)\coloneqq\sup\limits_{y\in\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}}\langle y,\zeta\rangle\rightarrow h_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}}(\zeta),\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ \zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\text{ as }k\to+\infty,

where
h_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}}
and
h_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}}
are the classical support functions of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}
and

\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
with respect to the origin respectively.

Given
\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus{\rm{int}}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})
, then for each
\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
, there exists a point
x_{k}\in\partial\Sigma_{k}
, such that
h_{\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}}(\zeta)=\langle x_{k},\zeta\rangle
. Let
k\rightarrow+\infty
, we have
x_{k}\rightarrow x
(possibly up to a subsequence) for some
x\in\partial(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}\cap\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}})
. Together with(
6.8
) implies

\zeta\in\mathcal{N}_{x}\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus{\rm{int}}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta})\subset\bigcup_{x\in\partial(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}\cap\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}})}\mathcal{N}_{x}\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}.

By Definition
6.3
, we conclude that (
6.7
) holds.
∎

We conclude the appendix by showing that for all capillary convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}^{\circ}
, under natural constraints on
{\rm{Vol}(\widehat{\Sigma})}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
, the capillary entropy point
z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma})
of
\widehat{\Sigma}
will stay away from
\Sigma
. This result will play a pivotal role in deriving the positive lower bound of the solution to flow (
4.1
).
We adapt a compactness argument as in
[
21
, Lemma 4.2]
and
[
6
, Lemma 4.4]
for the general non-smooth setting. However, under Hausdorff convergence, the limiting convex body may not be a capillary convex body, i.e., the constant angle condition may not be preserved. To address this difficulty, we will leverage the concept of
\theta
-capillary convex body introduced in this section and its associated properties.

We first introduce a set of capillary convex bodies.
For a positive constant
A>0
, we denote

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A}\coloneqq\left\{\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{K}_{\theta}^{\circ}\mid\penalty\ {\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\Sigma})={\rm{Vol}}(\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}),\penalty\ \mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\leq A\right\}.

Now we are ready to state the result.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. We have

(1)

Suppose that
\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\Gamma_{A}
, and

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}=\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
in Hausdorff distance sense. Then
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
and

(6.9)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k})=\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}).

(2)

Then there exists a constant
\delta>0
, depending only on
n
and
A
, such that for all
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\Gamma_{A}
, the capillary entropy point
z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma})
of
\widehat{\Sigma}
satisfies

(6.10)

\displaystyle{\rm{dist}}\left(z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}),\Sigma\right)\geq\delta.

Proof.

(1)

Since the volume is preserved along the Hausdorff convergence, this implies that
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
cannot collapse into a lower-dimensional convex set, so we obtain
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{G}
. From Proposition
6.5
above, we have
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}\in\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
. Next, we proceed to prove (
6.9
).

From Proposition
4.8
and (
4.24
), given
\widehat{\Sigma}\in\Gamma_{A}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
, depending only on
n
and
A
, such that

(6.11)

\displaystyle u_{z}^{\Sigma}\leq 2\rho_{+}(\widehat{\Sigma},\theta)\leq 2Ce^{\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}\leq 2Ce^{A}\eqqcolon C_{0},

where
u_{z}^{\Sigma}
is the capillary support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}
with respect to the point
z\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
.

By
Proposition
6.4
, we derive that the capillary entropy point
z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})
of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
lies in
{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{\star})
with
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{\star}\coloneqq\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}\cap\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, therefore, for
k
sufficiently large, we have
z_{e}({\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}})\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{k})
. In view of (
6.11
) and the dominated convergence theorem, we derive

(6.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})}^{\Sigma_{\star}}(\zeta)\right)\ell(\zeta)d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})}^{\Sigma_{k}}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\leq\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

where
u_{z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})}^{\Sigma_{k}}
denote the capillary support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}
with respect to the point
z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})
and
u_{z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})}^{\Sigma_{\star}}=\frac{h^{\Sigma_{\star}}_{{z}_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})}}{\ell}
, where
h^{\Sigma_{\star}}_{{z}_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})}
is the classical support function of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
with respect to the point
{z}_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})
.
On the other hand, Proposition
4.5
implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\left(\frac{u_{z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k})}^{\Sigma_{k}}(\xi)}{C_{0}}\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma=\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k})-\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}(\log C_{0})\ell(\xi)d\sigma\geq-C,

for some positive constant
C
, depending only on
n
and
A
.
Together with (
6.11
) again, it implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left|\log\left(\frac{u^{\Sigma_{k}}_{z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k})}(\xi)}{C_{0}}\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma\right|d\sigma\leq C.

Let
z_{\star}\coloneqq\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}z_{e}({\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}})
. Since the capillary entropy point
z_{e}({\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}})\in{\rm{int}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{k})
and
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}=\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
, it follows that
z_{\star}\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{\star}
. By Fatou’s Lemma, we obtain

(6.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\log\left(\frac{u^{\Sigma_{\star}}_{z_{\star}}(\zeta)}{C_{0}}\right)\ell(\zeta)d\sigma\geq\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\log\left(\frac{u_{z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k})}^{\Sigma_{k}}(\xi)}{C_{0}}\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma,

which implies

(6.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\ast})\geq\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}).

In view of (
6.12
) and (
6.14
), we conclude that the assertion (
6.9
) holds.

(2)
Now we move on to prove (
6.10
) by arguing via contradiction. Suppose there exists a sequence
\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{l}\}_{l\geq 1}\subset\Gamma_{A}
such that

(6.15)

\displaystyle{\rm{dist}}\left(z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{l}),\Sigma_{l}\right)\rightarrow 0,\penalty\ {\rm{as}}\penalty\ l\rightarrow\infty.

By the Blaschke selection theorem, see, e.g.,
[
33
, Theorem 1.8.7]
,
there exists a subsequence of
\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
, still denoted by
\{\widehat{\Sigma}_{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
, such that
\widehat{\Sigma}_{l}
converges to
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
as
l\to+\infty
. Let
q_{0}\coloneqq\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{l})
. Since each
z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{l})\in\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{l}\subset\partial{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}
, together with (
6.15
), it yields that

(6.16)

\displaystyle q_{0}\in\partial\Sigma_{\star}.

Furthermore, from (
6.9
) and (
6.13
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{l})=\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}\left(\log u^{\Sigma_{l}}_{z_{e}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{l})}(\xi)\right)\ell(\xi)d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}\left(\log u_{q_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\star}}(\zeta)\right)\ell(\zeta)d\sigma\leq\mathcal{F}_{\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}),

which implies that
q_{0}
is the capillary entropy point of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\star}
. By applying Proposition
6.4
, we know that the point
q_{0}\in\text{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}_{\star})
. However, this contradicts (
6.16
). Hence, we complete the proof.
∎

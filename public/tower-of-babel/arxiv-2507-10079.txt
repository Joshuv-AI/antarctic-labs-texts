Eight-fold classification of superconducting orders
Alexander V. Balatsky, Saikat Banerjee

Abstract
We present a thorough symmetry-based classification of superconducting order parameters that is independent of their microscopic origins. Our approach involves classifying pairing states through the pairwise permutation of spatial, temporal, spin, and orbital indices, focusing on the parities of the relative coordinates and times of the paired fields. This framework leads to the emergence of new classes of superconducting states, which we visualize as a multidimensional cube representing the relative parities in space and time, referred to as the Berezinskii-Abrahams hypercube. While some predicted states, such as odd-frequency superconductors, have been previously discussed, our symmetry analysis also reveals states that have yet to be explored, including odd-frequency Fulde-Ferrell-Larkin-Ovchinnikov (FFLO) states. Furthermore, we draw parallels to the classification of particle-hole states, and reiterate the possibility of new states, such as odd-frequency density waves.



I
Introduction

Superconductivity is a remarkable example of a quantum state of matter that can occur on a truly macroscopic length scale. When a material becomes a superconductor, a significant fraction of the electrons near its Fermi surface gets condensed into a “superfluid” that extends over the entire system. This feature leads to macroscopic electronic flow/current with almost no resistance. Since its discovery more than a century ago
Onnes 1911
, the research in properties of superconductors has continued to be at the center of interest in the community. To date, a wide range of classes of superconductors are being discovered: – the conventional superconductors such as Al or Nb, which are well described by the seminal theory of Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS)
Bardeen et al. 1957
, high-
T_{c}
cuprate superconductors where an accurate microscopic description is still lacking
Varma 2020
, organic superconductors
Jérome and Schulz 1982
;
Ishiguro and Yamaji 1990
;
Saito and Yoshida 2011
, iron-based superconductors
Li et al. 2011
;
Chubukov et al. 2016
;
Kreisel et al. 2020
, etc. Recent interest in topology has led to a closer look at the topological aspects of superconductors based on various crystalline symmetries, primarily due to its potential for the realization of Majorana fermions
Qi and Zhang 2011

Superconductivity requires the development of long-range two-fermion correlation
\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t,t^{\prime})=\braket{\mathcal{T}_{t}c_{\alpha a}(\mathbf{r},t)c_{\beta b}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}

\{\alpha,\beta\}
are the spin degrees of freedom. The majority of the discussions on the nature of the superconducting state assume equal time pairing that can be perturbed away by allowing Eliashberg-like dependence of the superconducting order on time primarily through retardation effects.

Here, we consider another class of superconductors that have a pairing correlation function that exhibits non-trivial time dynamics – the so-called odd-frequency (odd-
\omega

We start by using the analysis proposed by Berezinskii

Here, we introduce the relevant parity operators for two-particle amplitude with respect to the relative spatial coordinates as

\mathcal{P}^{\ast}\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t,t^{\prime}){\mathcal{P}^{\ast}}^{-1}=\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}\,|\,t,t^{\prime}),

(1)

\mathcal{T}^{\ast}

\mathcal{T}^{\ast}\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t,t^{\prime}){\mathcal{T}^{\ast}}^{-1}=\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t^{\prime},t).

(2)

II
Symmetries of the superconducting pairing and Berezinskii-Abrahams hypercube

The time-ordered expectation value that defines the superconducting two-electron anomalous correlation function is given by

\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t,t^{\prime})=\braket{\mathcal{T}c_{\alpha a}(\mathbf{r},t)c_{\beta b}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})},

(6)

at spatial coordinate
\mathbf{r}
at time
t

\mathcal{T}
is the time-ordering operator defined earlier. It is natural to define a center-of-mass and relative coordinate for both the spatial and temporal components in the definition of the anomalous Green’s function
\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t,t^{\prime})
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}=\frac{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}{2},

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime},

(7a)

\displaystyle T=\frac{t+t^{\prime}}{2},

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=t-t^{\prime},

(7b)

\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t,t^{\prime})\rightarrow\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{R},\bm{\rho}\,|\,T,\tau).

(8)

In principle, the correlation/gap function defined in the mixed representation [see Eq. (
8

\mathbf{R}
, and

The origin of the BA-hypercube refers to conventional BCS
s
-wave superconductors, dictated by no explicit dependence of the correlation function on any of the spatial or temporal coordinates. The relative spatial coordinate
\bm{\rho}
-dependence signifies the various unconventional nodal superconductors such as with
p
-,
d
- or

-wave gap functions. On the other hand, relative temporal coordinate
\tau
-dependence corresponds to an even/odd-
\omega

Berezinskii 1974
;
Balatsky and Abrahams 1992
;
Linder and Balatsky 2019
. However, the mixed representation of the correlation function naturally leads to two more explicit parameter choices – center-of-mass spatial and temporal coordinates
\mathbf{R}
, and T. An explicit center-of-mass spatial coordinate
\mathbf{R}
-dependence leads to conventional Fulde-Ferell-Larkin-Ovchinnikov
Fulde and Ferrell 1964
;
Larkin and Ovchinnikov 1964

Agterberg et al. 2020

et al
.
Wilczek 2012
. An arbitrary point in our BA-hypercube with non-vanishing four coordinates would correspond to a hybrid state.

The idea of a BA hypercube is motivated by the cube of theories controlled by gravitational constant
G
, speed of light
c
and Planck’s constant
\hbar
, ascribed to Gamow, Okun, Beckestein and possibly others in the high-energy-gravity community. The cube allows us to represent interplay between different regimes, e.g. strong quantum vs. strong gravity vs. relativistic limits of theories. The BA hypercube places the emphasis on the importance of the order on relative coordinates and times vs their ”center of mass” in space (R) and time (T) orderings.

For conventional BCS or unconventional high-T

, and

(9)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{k};\omega)

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\beta\alpha,ba}(-\mathbf{k},-\omega).

A generic odd-

Extension to particle-hole channel: Dynamic density-waves

Based on the Cooper pair correlation function, the classification can be easily generalized to any density wave (correlation between particle- and hole degrees of freedom). Generalizing Eq. (
6

g_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t,t^{\prime})=\braket{\mathcal{T}c^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha a}(\mathbf{r},t)c_{\beta b}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}
where
c^{{\dagger}}_{\alpha a}(\mathbf{r},t)
annihilates an electron with spin
\alpha
and orbital
a
at spatial coordinate
\mathbf{r}
at time
t

g^{\rm R}_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},|,t,t^{\prime})=\mathcal{T}(t,t^{\prime})\braket{c^{\dagger}_{\alpha a}(\mathbf{r},t)c_{\beta b}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})},

(15)

where
\mathcal{T}(t,t^{\prime})
denotes the time-ordering operator, and the superscript
\rm R
indicates the retarded density correlator
Kedem and Balatsky 2015

7a

g^{\rm R}_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\,|\,t,t^{\prime})\rightarrow g^{\rm R}_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\mathbf{R},\bm{\rho}\,|\,T,\tau).

(16)

Here,
\mathbf{R}

g^{\rm R}_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\bm{\rho};\tau)=\pm\,g^{\rm R}_{\alpha\beta,ab}(\bm{\rho};-\tau),

where the explicit dependence on

and

\omega

IV
Future directions and summary

In this paper, we present the general classification of pairing orders. We find that in addition to the well-known axis of classification of pairing states along the pairing channels, spin singlet vs. triplet, and s,p,d orbital channels, the classification explicitly suggests the even- and odd-frequency states. We identify already discussed even- and odd-frequency channels, starting with simple even and odd channels of the time and coordinate swaps. We also find that this classification allows for other states like odd-frequency FFLO and pair-density states.

The odd-frequency states can be viewed as a sharp example of dynamic orders, which has been widely emphasized in the literature. The main discussion assumes equilibrium phases, which allows for a similar analysis of the swap parities in the Keldysh contour for non-equilibrium and driven states.

For simplicity, in our analysis we assumed that only real
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
representations are allowed for pairwise swaps. It is an interesting question about the nature of possible pairing states that can emerge,
e.g.
, in anyon matter in 2+1 dimensions where particle swap can generate fractional phase and even nonabelian braiding matrices. These questions remain to be addressed in future work. We also illustrate how the proposed classification can be extended to include the odd frequency density waves.

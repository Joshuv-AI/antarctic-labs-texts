A cubical fuzzy Dubois–Prade aggregation framework for renewable and sustainable green energy decision-making
Authors: K. Deva, S. Sangeetha, Fikadu Tesgera Tolasa, Jiregna Abdissa Olana, Segni Adugna Tamune
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rapid evolution of green energy management systems is transforming how organizations plan, monitor and optimize sustainable energy usage. However, selecting the most efficient strategies and technologies for renewable energy integration remains challenging due to uncertain operational data, fluctuating energy demands and incomplete expert evaluations. This study leverages the cubical fuzzy set (CFS) framework to capture the positive, neutral and negative degrees of decision attributes, providing a comprehensive representation of uncertainty in renewable energy decision-making. Two innovative aggregation operators (AOs) are proposed: the Cubical Fuzzy Interactive Dubois–Prade Weighted Average Aggregation Operator (CFIDPWAAO) and the Cubical Fuzzy Interactive Dubois–Prade Ordered Weighted Average Aggregation Operator (CFIDPOWAAO). A numerical example for selecting green energy technologies–including solar, wind and hybrid energy solutions–is provided using a cubical fuzzy decision matrix. Comparative and sensitivity analyses demonstrate that the proposed operators achieve more consistent rankings across varying parameter values, with stability rates exceeding 95% in sensitivity tests and improved agreement with expert evaluations compared to existing operators. These results quantitatively confirm the reliability, robustness and flexibility of the proposed framework for data-driven sustainable energy decision-making.

Introduction

In practical decision-making (DM) scenarios, uncertainty is an inherent challenge, often stemming from incomplete information, inconsistent data, system complexity, unpredictability and the cognitive limitations of human reasoning. Effectively modeling and managing this uncertainty has thus become a vital concern across diverse fields, including science, business and engineering. To address these challenges, Zadeh1 introduced fuzzy set (FS) theory in 1965, which quantifies uncertainty using a membership degree (MD) ranging from 0 to 1. Although FS provides a foundational framework for handling vagueness, it is limited by considering only the MD, without accounting for non-MD (NMD). To overcome this limitation, Atanassov2 developed the intuitionistic fuzzy (IF) set, incorporating both MD and NMD, constrained so that their sum does not exceed 1. Building on this advancement, Cuong3 proposed the cubic FS (CFS), which introduces a third component–neutrality–thereby extending the IF set. A CFS is represented by three interrelated functions: MD, neutral MD (NeMD), and NMD. The geometrical representation CFS are showed in Figure 1. This expanded framework allows for a more nuanced modeling of human judgment, particularly in situations where decision-makers may be hesitant, undecided, or adopt a neutral stance. For instance, in medical diagnosis, a physician evaluating a patient’s symptoms may feel confident that a particular condition is present (acceptance), suspect that it is absent (rejection), or remain uncertain due to ambiguous test results (neutrality). Unlike FS and IF sets, CFS can simultaneously capture all three aspects–acceptance, rejection and indifference–providing a richer representation of uncertainty. These properties make CFS a full and versatile tool for tackling complex DM problems in contemporary applications, such as green supplier selection4, artificial intelligence, environmental management and financial analysis, where multi-dimensional and nuanced evaluations are essential.Fig. 1Enhanced geometrical representation of Cubical, Spherical and Picture Fuzzy Sets, showing the expanded decision space under the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q=3$$\end{document} constraint.

Literature review

Multi-attribute DM (MADM) involves evaluating a set of alternatives against multiple attribute to identify the most suitable option. It aims to support rational and informed choices by considering both quantitative and qualitative attributes, which often interact in complex ways. Traditional MADM approaches assume precise information, but real-world decision problems are frequently characterized by uncertainty, imprecision and subjective judgments. The advent of FS theory and its extensions has significantly enhanced MADM by enabling the modeling of such uncertainty in a structured manner. More recently, the combination of aggregation operators (AO) with the CFS framework has gained attention, as it allows decision-makers to integrate multiple attribute more effectively while capturing nuanced degrees of acceptance, rejection and neutrality, thereby providing a more robust and realistic decision-support tool. Khan et al. (2022)5 introduced a novel approach to MADM by developing CF AOs. Building upon this foundation, Jan et al. (2023)6 explored the application of CF Hamacher AOs in MADM problems. These operators, based on the Hamacher t-norm (TN), allow for the aggregation of CF information by considering the interaction between different components of uncertainty. A central strategy for managing uncertainty in MADM involves the use of TN and triangular conorms (TCN), which form the mathematical backbone of FS theory and its extensions. Well-established examples include Frank, Archimedean, Einstein, Dombi, Algebraic, Aczél–Alsina, Schweizer–Sklar, Yager and Dubois–Prade (DP) operators, as comprehensively reviewed by Klir and Yuan7. These operators provide a systematic framework to aggregate information from diverse sources, thereby enhancing the robustness and reliability of DM processes. However, traditional AO often combine input information without explicitly capturing the interdependencies among the elements being aggregated.

To overcome this limitation, more advanced operators, such as Bonferroni mean operator (BMO)8, have been introduced. In recent years, various extensions of FS and IF theories have been introduced to address uncertainty and vagueness in DM problems. Khan et al.9 proposed novel construction methods for entropy measures of circular IF and demonstrated their applicability in engineering decision systems. Patel et al.10 developed a similarity measure for IF and illustrated its potential in pattern recognition and multimodal medical image fusion. Wu et al.11 introduced Jensen–Shannon divergence measures within the framework of IF, leading to a parametric IF TOPSIS approach. Similarly, Meher et al.12 explored Dombi weighted geometric AOs on trapezoidal-valued IF numbers for multi-attribute group DM. Extending this line of research, Khan et al.13 evaluated urban transport safety using IF rough power AOs. On the other hand, advanced fuzzy models have been incorporated into supply chain and network problems. Khan and Zaman14 proposed hesitant CF Dombi AO for the selection of green supplier chain management, while Broumi et al.15 examined shortest path problems through CF information. In the context of PF, Nwokoro and Ejegwa16 presented a comprehensive review outlining trends, gaps and future directions over a decade of picture fuzzy applications in MADM. Furthermore, Gul and Sarfraz17 enhanced artificial intelligence models by integrating interval-valued PF sets with Sugeno–Weber TN. Spherical and T-spherical fuzzy (T-SF) set theories have also received significant attention. Karamaz and Karaaslan18 proposed distance measures for r, s, t-spherical FS and applied them to MADM based on technique for order preference by sSimilarity to ideal solution (TOPSIS). Yang et al.19 introduced divergence measures for globular T-SF with applications in solar energy system selection. Similarly, Dong et al.20 applied spherical FS in the suitability assessment of hybrid renewable projects such as wind–photovoltaic–hydrogen–ammonia–alcohol integration. Collectively, these studies highlight the versatility of fuzzy, IF, PF and T-SF frameworks in addressing complex DM challenges across engineering, sustainability and energy management domains.

Owing to its ability to dynamically capture interdependencies among attributes, the AO has attracted considerable attention in recent research. For example, Yunzhi Peng21 proposed an interval-valued q-rung orthopair fuzzy (q-ROF) interactive DP operator, while Toqeer Jameela et al.22 developed a T-SF interactive DP aggregation framework to assess low-carbon technologies and environmental mitigation strategies. Despite these advancements, research on extending AO within the context of DPTN and DPTCN, particularly under the CF environment, remains limited. Motivated by this gap, the present study aims to develop novel AOs under the CF framework by combining the mutual reinforcement mechanism of AO with the flexibility of DPTN and DPTCN operational laws. The proposed approach is designed to improve the robustness of enhance DM outcomes. Figure 2 represents the schematic diagram of this proposed work. Recent research highlights the growing importance of advanced DM frameworks in various domains. For example, Mkhitaryan et al.23 demonstrated the integration of artificial intelligence and geospatial technologies for sustainable smart city development, showing how AI-driven models can support complex decision-making under uncertainty. Similarly, Şenol et al.24 applied GIS-based multi-attribute analysis for urban afforestation planning in semi-arid cities, illustrating the effectiveness of combining spatial data with multi-atribute approaches to achieve sustainable environmental outcomes. Beyond environmental applications, technological innovation in digital brand management also leverages AI and immersive experiences to support strategic decision-making25. These studies underscore the relevance of developing robust aggregation operators and decision-support frameworks capable of handling uncertain, conflicting and multidimensional attribute, as proposed in the current work. Recent advances in financial decision-making increasingly leverage artificial intelligence and fuzzy-based frameworks to handle uncertainty and complex interrelationships in economic and digital asset data26. MADM approaches, such as TOPSIS and fuzzy normalization techniques, have been successfully applied to assess economic performance and investment strategies27. These methods provide a systematic and data-driven basis for evaluating alternatives under uncertain and dynamic financial environments. Recent studies have highlighted the increasing role of advanced fuzzy and PF models in energy management and sustainable DM. Dhumras and Bajaj28 proposed PF hypersoft-based methodologies to enhance renewable energy source selection under complex uncertainty structures. Pal et al.29 extended this direction by utilizing T-SF soft Dombi AO for renewable energy evaluation, demonstrating improved handling of multi-attribute complexity. Furthermore, q-rung PF frameworks have been applied to develop strategic green supply chain models in the energy sector30, while modified TOPSIS models with R-norm q-rung PF information have been used effectively for green supplier selection31. Similar contributions also include PF R-norm TOPSIS-based decision models for environmentally responsible supplier evaluation32. Zhu et al.33 introduced multi-agent reinforcement learning-based joint precoding and phase shift optimization for RIS-aided cell-free massive MIMO systems. Cabrera et al.34 develop the electrocoagulation treatment of POME for COD removal through process variable fuzzy optimization. These studies collectively indicate a strong research interest in integrating richer fuzzy structures with MADM techniques. Practical interpretation of interactive Dubois–Prade operations in green energy decision-making Green energy DM has become an essential aspect of modern energy planning due to increasing concerns about environmental sustainability and climate change. The transition from conventional fossil fuels to renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, hydro, and biomass requires careful evaluation of multiple factors, including economic feasibility, environmental impact, technological efficiency, and resource availability. Decision-makers often face complex problems where several conflicting criteria must be considered simultaneously. Therefore, MADM techniques are widely applied to analyze and rank renewable energy alternatives in a systematic manner. In recent years, the integration of advanced computational tools, data analytics, and machine learning approaches has further improved the effectiveness of energy planning and optimization. Moreover, policy regulations, stakeholder preferences, and energy security considerations also influence the final decision-making process. Effective green energy decision-making not only supports sustainable development but also contributes to reducing greenhouse gas emissions and ensuring a reliable and resilient energy system for future generations. In practical green energy management, decision criteria such as Sustainability and Cost Effectiveness are inherently interdependent rather than independent. For instance, achieving higher sustainability often requires advanced renewable technologies, cleaner materials, or stricter environmental standards, which may increase initial investment costs and thus influence cost effectiveness. Conversely, prioritizing low-cost solutions may restrict long-term environmental performance.

The interactive Dubois–Prade operations are employed to capture this real-world dependence by regulating the degree of interaction among membership, non-membership, and neutral degrees during aggregation. The Dubois–Prade parameter reflects the strength of this interaction, allowing the aggregation process to account for trade-offs and synergies between criteria. Consequently, the proposed model produces decision outcomes that more accurately represent realistic green energy planning scenarios, where economic and environmental objectives jointly influence final decisions.

Although the mathematical derivations provide the structural form of the CFIDPWAAO and CFIDPOWAAO operators, deeper theoretical insights have now been incorporated to justify these formulations. Specifically, the use of DP operational laws is motivated by their unique ability to interpolate between independence and interdependence of attribute, offering a more flexible uncertainty modeling framework than Hamacher or Einstein counterparts. Each component of the operator is interpreted in terms of complement interaction, indeterminacy propagation and conflict sensitivity. The behavior of the proposed operator has been theoretically analyzed to demonstrate robustness, smooth information fusion and appropriate weighting response under conflicting input values. This added theoretical foundation clarifies not only how the operators are computed but also why they behave in a particular manner, thereby addressing the reviewer’s concern on symbolic manipulation.

Justification of the behavioral properties of CFIDPWAAO and CFIDPOWAAO

The behavioral characteristics of the proposed CFIDPWAAO and CFIDPOWAAO operators naturally arise from the intrinsic structure of the DP operational laws. These laws explicitly incorporate partial dependence between MD, NMD and NeMD components, allowing the operators to model real-world uncertainty more faithfully than purely independent (e.g., Einstein) or purely dependent (e.g., Hamacher) operators. Unlike classical CFS-based aggregation approaches, which often assume fixed interaction patterns, the DP formulation enables a flexible coupling mechanism that captures complement interaction and indeterminacy propagation. This provides a theoretical advantage by allowing the aggregated result to adjust dynamically according to the level of conflict among attribute. The internal structure of the operators, especially the weighted interaction terms, ensures smooth information fusion when the input evaluations are consistent, while maintaining strong sensitivity when contradictions or high indeterminacy are present. Consequently, the operators demonstrate improved robustness in MADM settings, particularly when dealing with heterogeneous or partially conflicting attribute. Moreover, the DP law is mathematically well-suited for striking a balance between dependence and independence because its generalized form interpolates between conjunctive and disjunctive behaviors without forcing strict correlation assumptions. This property makes it an appropriate operational foundation for capturing the nuanced relationships among attributes encountered in complex decision-making problems.

Problem statement

DM in green energy management involves evaluating multiple renewable energy alternatives under uncertainty, incomplete information and conflicting expert opinions. Traditional FS, IFS, CFS, PFS and T-SF models struggle to fully capture the combined effects of MD, NMD and NeMD when these components interact in complex or partially dependent ways. Existing aggregation operators often rely on fixed operational laws that cannot model such interactions, leading to limited flexibility and reduced accuracy in real-world applications. Therefore, there is a need for a more expressive framework that can represent uncertainty more comprehensively, incorporate interactive relationships among fuzzy components and provide stable and reliable rankings for sustainable energy DM. This motivates the development of CF interactive DP based AO to overcome these limitations and improve decision support in green energy selection problems.

Research gap

Despite the increasing adoption of CFS in MADM, several substantial gaps continue to hinder theoretical development and practical implementation. First, the operational mechanisms of CFS including aggregation, comparison and transformation operators remain limited, fragmented and insufficiently validated across diverse decision scenarios. Existing studies introduce isolated operators without systematically evaluating their performance, interpretability, or robustness under different uncertainty conditions. Second, although numerous AO have been proposed, there is a lack of comprehensive comparative analysis that examines their consistency, sensitivity and suitability for complex DM environments involving conflicting, imprecise, or partially dependent attribute. Third, the integration of CFS with modern intelligent systems such as machine learning models, hybrid MADM frameworks and optimization-based techniques remains underexplored. Addressing these gaps is crucial to advancing the reliability and practical usability of CFS in real-world uncertain and high-stakes environments.

Motivation

Modern DM problems across domains such as renewable energy planning, healthcare, finance, engineering design and environmental management are increasingly characterized by ambiguity, incomplete information and dynamic uncertainties. Traditional fuzzy models (e.g., standard FS,IFS, PFS) often fail to simultaneously represent point-valued membership and uncertainty intervals. CFS overcome this limitation by incorporating both precise MD and an interval-valued structure to better capture hesitation and vagueness. This capability makes CFS a powerful tool for modeling real-world problems. However, its full potential remains unrealized due to a lack of advanced operational laws and aggregation frameworks. This study is motivated by the need to strengthen CFS theory by introducing interactive and flexible aggregation operators that can capture the nuanced dependencies among attribute and enhance the overall reliability and meaningfulness of the DM outcomes.

Significance

This study significantly advances both the theoretical and practical dimensions of CFS by addressing key limitations in their operational and analytical structures. The proposed interactive AO enhance the ability of CFS to model complex dependencies among attribute, thereby producing more reliable and interpretable decision outcomes. The improved aggregation strategies enable practitioners to better manage uncertainty in high-dimensional and dynamic environments, particularly in domains such as renewable energy planning, environmental sustainability, risk assessment and engineering management. By extending the expressive power and computational robustness of CFS-based approaches, this work contributes to the evolution of fuzzy decision sciences and supports the development of more resilient and transparent MADM frameworks.

Green energy management and decision-making framework

Green energy management has emerged as a cornerstone of modern sustainability strategies, as nations and industries worldwide transition toward low-carbon and environmentally responsible operations. In today’s rapidly evolving energy landscape, rising demands, resource scarcity, and the pressing threats of climate change make the efficient utilization of renewable energy not just a choice, but a necessity. Renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, biomass, and hydropower present tremendous opportunities to reduce carbon footprints, enhance energy security, and drive economic growth. However, harnessing these resources effectively requires more than just investment in technology–it demands intelligent management, careful planning, and continuous optimization. Every decision, from selecting the right technology to integrating it into existing energy systems, carries implications for sustainability, operational efficiency, and long-term viability.

Managing green energy is inherently complex. Decision-makers face uncertainty due to fluctuating environmental conditions, variable energy outputs, and rapidly evolving technologies. Moreover, diverse stakeholder interests–ranging from policy regulations and community needs to economic and technical feasibility–further complicate the decision-making process. Traditional evaluation methods often fall short in addressing these uncertainties, which is why advanced frameworks and computational tools are essential. Recent developments in fuzzy set theory, MADM, and intelligent computational models have opened new pathways for managing this complexity. These approaches allow decision-makers to account for vagueness, ambiguity, and conflicting attribute, providing a more realistic and nuanced assessment of renewable energy projects. By incorporating such techniques, organizations can evaluate alternatives not only based on cost and efficiency but also on environmental impact, social acceptability, and long-term sustainability. Furthermore, the development of expressive AO and decision models has significantly enhanced our ability to synthesize multiple, often conflicting, evaluation attribute into actionable insights. These tools enable energy planners to make well-informed decisions, optimize resource allocation, and achieve a balanced approach that maximizes both environmental and economic benefits.

Green energy management is more than a technical challenge–it is a strategic imperative for a sustainable future. By combining innovative technologies, intelligent decision-support systems, and forward-thinking policies, we can accelerate the transition toward renewable energy, mitigate the adverse effects of climate change, and create resilient, low-carbon energy systems. The path to a sustainable energy future lies not only in adopting green technologies but in managing them wisely, intelligently, and responsibly.

Discussion on parameter sensitivity, weighting strategy, and computational complexity

The invariance of the ranking results with respect to different values of the parameter p in Table 3 is primarily a consequence of the specific data distribution and structural characteristics of the case study, rather than an indication that the parameter p is theoretically unnecessary. The parameter p in the proposed interactive DP based operator is designed to regulate the degree of interaction and compensation among attributes during aggregation. In decision problems where alternatives exhibit strong dominance patterns or relatively consistent performance across criteria, variations in p may not alter the relative ordering of alternatives, leading to stable rankings. This behavior reflects the robustness of the decision outcome, rather than insensitivity of the operator itself.

It is important to note that in datasets characterized by higher conflict, stronger trade-offs, or greater dispersion among attribute values, the parameter p plays a more influential role and can lead to noticeable changes in aggregation results and rankings. Therefore, the observed invariance in this study should be interpreted as a data-driven feature of the case study, not as a limitation of the proposed operator. Accordingly, the inclusion of the parameter p remains theoretically meaningful, as it ensures flexibility and adaptability of the operator across different decision-making scenarios, even though its impact is not explicitly manifested in the present application.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed a novel decision-making framework that integrates CFS with interactive Dubois–Prade aggregation operators (CFIDPWAAO and CFIDPOWAAO) to effectively address uncertainty, indeterminacy and incomplete information in complex green energy management problems. The proposed operators combine the strengths of DPTN and DPTCN with aggregation concepts, capturing interrelationships among atrtibute more effectively. Quantitative results from a comprehensive case study show that the operators produce stable rankings under varying parameter values, with sensitivity analysis indicating less than 5% variation in ranking outcomes. Comparative analysis confirms that the proposed operators outperform existing aggregation methods in terms of ranking consistency, alignment with expert judgments and robustness under uncertainty. These findings provide a flexible, data-driven framework for selecting sustainable energy technologies under real-world conditions.

Weighting strategy and its limitations

Entropy-based weighting is an objective and data-driven approach that does not explicitly incorporate subjective expert preferences. In the present study, objective weighting was intentionally adopted to address the challenges of incomplete, uncertain, and potentially biased expert evaluations that frequently arise in green energy decision-making contexts. By determining attribute importance based on data dispersion and inter-criteria contrast, the entropy method enhances transparency and improves the robustness of the evaluation process. Nevertheless, the absence of subjective judgments may limit the representation of expert knowledge in the decision framework. Therefore, the integration of subjective or hybrid weighting strategies that combine expert opinions with objective data-driven methods is identified as a promising direction for future research.

Computational complexity and scalability

The proposed interactive aggregation operators involve fractional computations and higher-order interaction terms, which may raise concerns regarding computational scalability. However, the computational complexity of the proposed operators increases polynomially with respect to the number of alternatives and attributes, making the approach suitable for medium- to large-scale DM problems when implemented using optimized numerical procedures. In practical applications such as real-time or large-scale energy management systems, computational efficiency can be further improved through parallel processing techniques and vectorized implementations. Although the proposed framework may require slightly higher computational effort compared with simpler aggregation operators, this trade-off is justified by its enhanced capability to capture inter-criteria interactions and provide more realistic and reliable decision outcomes.

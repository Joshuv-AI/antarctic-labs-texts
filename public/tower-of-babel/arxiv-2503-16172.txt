Conditions for sectoriality and compactness of the resolvent for a non-self-adjoint Sturm–Liouville operator with singular distributional potential
Sergey N. Tumanov

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to find necessary and sufficient conditions for sectoriality and compactness of the resolvent for Sturm–Liouville operators with complex-valued potentials of the class q ∈ W 2 , l ​ o ​ c − 1 ​ ( ℝ + ) q\in W_{2,loc}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}) in terms of its generalized antiderivatives s ∈ L 2 , l ​ o ​ c ​ ( ℝ + ) s\in L_{2,loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+}) .

Main results

In the classical theory of the Sturm–Liouville operator defined by the differential expression

l(y)=-y^{\prime\prime}+qy,

where
q\in L_{1,loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
general results that do not require additional smoothness of the potential
q
are often
formulated in terms of its antiderivative
s

q=s^{\prime}

[
1
]
and its generalizations

[
2

3

4

5

characterize the growth of the antiderivative on segments of constant length.

The nature of this phenomenon can be explained by the possibility of regularization of the differential equation
l(y)=f
for

:
there is an equivalent system of equations, the coefficients of which are functions of the antiderivative:

\begin{pmatrix}y(x)\\
y^{[1]}(x)\end{pmatrix}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}s(x)&1\\
-s^{2}(x)&-s(x)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}y(x)\\
y^{[1]}(x)\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}0\\
f(x)\end{pmatrix}.

The equivalent notation for the expression

is following:

l(y)=-(y^{[1]})^{\prime}-sy^{[1]}-s^{2}y,\ \mbox{where }y^{[1]}=y^{\prime}-sy.

(1)

It is expected that the general spectral properties of the regularized problem are expressed in terms of
s

In their pioneering work
[
6

Savchuk and Shkalikov showed that regularization is an effective way of
generalizing the Sturm–Liouville operator for potentials
q\in W_{2,loc}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Here
W_{2,loc}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

is the space of distributions with generalized antiderivatives

The history of the issue and references are presented in
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, on the regularization method and its development
for high-order operators we recommend
[
9
]
.

Here we consider an operator with a complex-valued potential, and our aim is to obtain generalizations of the known classical
criteria for the compactness of the resolvent in terms of the antiderivative
s
of the potential
q\in W_{2,loc}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Throughout what follows
q=s^{\prime}
, and the derivative is understood in the sense of distribution theory.

In a recent paper
[
5
]
we proposed a necessary condition for the compactness of resolvents for operators with arbitrary complex-valued potentials.

q\in L_{1,loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
:

\forall a>0\ \lim\limits_{x\to+\infty}\int\limits_{x}^{x+a}|q(\xi)|\,d\xi=+\infty.

Of course, it is not relevant for
q\not\in L_{1,loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and here we make up for this deficiency by proposing Theorem
1

— a stronger condition covering operators with arbitrary complex-valued
q\in W_{2,loc}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

In
[
5
]
we found the
operator
L
with essential spectrum, with potential
q\in L_{1,loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
\re q\geq 0
and
|q|\to+\infty
. The numerical range of
L
is contained in the
right half-plane, and the resolvent is bounded in the left half-plane.

It turned out that for a successful generalization of classical theorems of Molchanov type to the complex-valued case, one should require strict
sectoriality for the minimal operator
L_{0}
— when the central angle of the sector with the numerical range of
L_{0}
is strictly less than
\pi

For this reason, in the present paper we pay special attention to sectorial operators, for which we propose the corresponding theorems.

In the special case of a real-valued potential of the form:

q(x)=\sum_{x_{k}\in X}\alpha_{k}\delta(x-x_{k}),

(2)

and more general case, when the antiderivative
s
is a function of bounded
variation, the issues of semi-boundedness and compactness of the resolvent have been studied previously. For example, Brasche
[
10

, arguing

proposed a sufficient condition for semi-boundedness of the quadratic forms:

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}|y^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}|y(x)|^{2}\,ds(x),\quad y\in W_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L_{2}(\mathbb{R},s),

where
s
is (neither necessarily finite nor necessarily positive) a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}

\sup_{x>0}\bigl(s^{-}(x+1)-s^{-}(x)\bigr)\leq C,

(3)

where

is a decomposition into non-negative measures on
\mathbb{R}
.

A generalization of Molchanov’s criterion for potentials of the form (
2

[
11

Proposing a criterion for compactness of the resolvent for semi-bounded operators, the authors require the Brachet condition (

The classical result of Brink

suggests a possible weakening of this requirement to:

\sup_{x>0}\bigl(s(x+a)-s(x)\bigr)\geq-C\ \mbox{for all }0<a\leq 1

In fact, the component

In

Lemma 6
.

Let
s\in L_{2}[0,a]
, denote
y^{[1]}=y^{\prime}-sy
for arbitrary
y\in AC[0,a]

\mathscr{D}=\bigl\{y\in L_{2}[0,a]\,\bigl|\bigr.\,y,y^{[1]}\in AC[0,a],\ y(0)=y^{[1]}(0)=y(a)=y^{[1]}(a)=0\bigr\}

metric in the space

\overset{\circ}{W_{2}^{1}}[0,a]=\bigl\{y\in W_{2}^{1}[0,a]\,\bigl|\bigr.\,y(0)=y(a)=0\bigr\}.

Proof.
We perform an auxiliary construction: for arbitrary
\delta>0
and

we construct

\omega_{\delta}\in AC[0,a]
with the properties:
\|\omega_{\delta}\|_{2}<\delta
,

\omega_{\delta}(0)=A
,
\omega_{\delta}(a)=0

\omega_{\delta}\perp\psi
,
where

\psi(x)=\exp\int_{x}^{a}\overline{s}(t)\,dt

— the solution to the homogeneous equation
\psi^{\prime}+\overline{s}\psi=0
. The bar in
\overline{s}
corresponds to complex conjugation. By
\|\cdot\|_{2}
we denote the norm in
L_{2}[0,a]
.

If
A=0

\omega_{\delta}\equiv 0

Let
A\neq 0

\omega_{\delta}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}A+kx&\mbox{ for }x\in[0,\varepsilon/2]\\
(2A/\varepsilon+k)(\varepsilon-x)&\mbox{ for }x\in[\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon]\\
0&\mbox{ for }x\in[\varepsilon,a]\end{array}\right.,

selecting

\psi

kC\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\sim-\frac{3}{4}AC\varepsilon,\mbox{ or }k\sim\frac{3}{\varepsilon}A\ \mbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0+,

\displaystyle\|\omega_{\delta}\|_{2}^{2}=\int\limits_{0}^{\varepsilon/2}|A+kx|^{2}\,dx+|2A/\varepsilon+k|^{2}\int\limits_{\varepsilon/2}^{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-x)^{2}\,dx=

\displaystyle=|A|^{2}\varepsilon\int\limits_{0}^{1/2}(1+3x)^{2}\,dx\,(1+o(1))+|A|^{2}\,\frac{25}{24}\varepsilon\,(1+o(1))\sim|A|^{2}\frac{8}{3}\varepsilon.

y_{0}\in\overset{\circ}{W_{2}^{1}}[0,a]

to
\psi
in

\psi

\|f-f_{0}\|_{2}<\varepsilon

\|y-y_{0}\|_{2}<C_{1}\|f-f_{0}\|_{2}<C_{1}\varepsilon

\|y^{\prime}-y_{0}^{\prime}\|_{2}<\|f-f_{0}\|_{2}+\|s(y-y_{0})\|_{2}<\varepsilon+\|s\|_{2}C_{1}\varepsilon<C_{2}\varepsilon

Lemma 7
.

Given the sequence of real-valued non-decreasing functions
S_{k}
on the interval
I=[a,b]
and the quadratic forms for

y\in\overset{\circ}{W_{2}^{1}}(I)
in
L_{2}(I)
:

\mathfrak{l}_{k}[y]=\int\limits_{I}|y^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int\limits_{I}|y(x)|^{2}\,dS_{k}(x).

(24)

The condition

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\inf\limits_{\begin{smallmatrix}y\in\overset{\circ}{W_{2}^{1}}(I)\\
\|y\|_{I}=1\end{smallmatrix}}\bigl|\mathfrak{l}_{k}[y]\bigr|=+\infty.

(25)

is equivalent to the following: for any
0<d\leq b-a
the sequence
S_{k}(y)-S_{k}(x)\to+\infty
uniformly in
y-x=d
,
x,y\in I
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.
First we prove the direct statement. Assume the contrary: for some
d>0
and
C>0
there exist segments

I_{k}=[a_{k},b_{k}]\subset I
,
b_{k}-a_{k}=d
,
S_{k}(b_{k})-S_{k}(a_{k})<C
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
I_{k}\equiv I
. Otherwise, by shifting the variable
\xi=x-a_{k}
, we reduce the problem to the one on the segment
I=[0,d]
,
where (
25
) is also satisfied.

Consider
V_{k}(x)=S_{k}(x)-S_{k}(a)
, by Helly’s second theorem there is some subsequence
V_{k_{s}}(x)\to V(x)
at each point
x\in I
.
Then for every
y\in\overset{\circ}{W_{2}^{1}}(I)
,
\|y\|_{I}=1
by Helly’s first theorem:

\lim_{s\to\infty}\mathfrak{l}_{k_{s}}[y]=\int\limits_{I}|y^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int\limits_{I}|y(x)|^{2}\,dV(x)<+\infty,

which contradicts (
25
).

Now we prove the converse by contradiction. Let
S_{k}(y)-S_{k}(x)\to+\infty
uniformly in
y-x=d
,
x,y\in I
for any
0<d\leq b-a
.
Suppose,
there exist numbers
n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,
n_{k}\to\infty
,
the sequence

,

\|y_{n_{k}}\|_{I}=1
, such that
\mathfrak{l}_{n_{k}}[y_{n_{k}}]<C

n_{k}=k
.
Since each term in (
24

\|y_{k}^{\prime}\|_{I}<C
.

For any
\varepsilon>0

\varepsilon^{2}\mu_{S_{k}}(E_{k})=\varepsilon^{2}\int\limits_{E_{k}}dS_{k}(x)\leq\int\limits_{I}|y(x)|^{2}\,dS_{k}(x)<C,

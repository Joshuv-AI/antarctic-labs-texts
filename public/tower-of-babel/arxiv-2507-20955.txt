On the first Neumann eigenvalue for critical points of a weighted area functional with asymptotically flat ends
A.L. Martínez-Triviño

Abstract
In the following work, we obtain a lower bound for the first Neumann eingevalue of the drift Laplacian Δ φ \Delta^{\varphi} for a family of properly embedded [ φ , e → 3 ] [\varphi,\vec{e}_{3}] -minimal surfaces in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with concave function φ \varphi and asymptotically flat ends. As application, we obtain a control on the topology for that surfaces with finite total curvature. The strategy will consists in an integration of the Bouchner’s formula by the works of A. Lichnerowicz [ 15 ] and S. Brendle, R. Tsiamis [ 1 ] and relate said eigenvalue with the Poincare’s constant.

In 1983, H. Choi and A. Wang

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Delta)\geq k/2

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\Sigma

[

together with Reilly’s formula
[
32

. An analogous technique was used by S. Brendle and R. Tsiamis
[
1
]
to obtain a lower estimate in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}_{loc}(\Sigma)
for the Dirichlet energy of the properly embedded self-similar shrinkers
\displaystyle\Sigma
of the mean curvature flow, namely, hypersurfaces of the Euclidean space
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose mean curvature satisfies
\displaystyle H=\langle x,N\rangle/2
, where
\displaystyle x
is the identity vector field and
\displaystyle N
is the Gauss map of
\displaystyle\Sigma
. Here
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}_{loc}(\Sigma)
is the Hilbert space given by

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{loc}(\Sigma)=\bigg\{f\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\Sigma)|\int_{\Sigma}(f^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2})\,d\Sigma<+\infty\bigg\},

where
\displaystyle\nabla
is the gradient in
\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle d\Sigma
is the induced measure of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
whose scalar product is the induced scalar product of functions
\displaystyle L^{2}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
with metric
\displaystyle e^{-|x|^{2}/4}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the usual metric of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Physically, the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian can be interpreted as the squared frequency of the first nontrivial vibration of an elastic membrane. In fact, if the density is not uniform, then the weight function affects how the membrane vibrates. The estimate of the first eigenvalue indicates how ”easy” or ”difficult” it is for oscillations to occur in the given geometry and density. On the other hand, if we consider the weighted heat equation (i.e., a heat equation adapted to the weighted measurement), the first eigenvalue controls the exponential decay rate of the system’s energy with time. The larger the first eigenvalue, the faster the heat dissipates in the manifold. See, for instance
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
.

3
Minimum of a Dirichlet-Neumann problem.

In this section, we adapt the arguments of
[
1
]
so that the work is self-contained. Let
\displaystyle\Sigma
be a properly embedded
\displaystyle[\varphi,\vec{e}_{3}]
-minimal surface in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
without boundary. Let
\displaystyle\Omega,\widetilde{\Omega}
be the two connected components of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Sigma
. Define
\displaystyle\mathcal{DN}_{loc}^{1,\varphi}
the set of all non-constant functions
\displaystyle w\in\mathcal{H}_{loc}^{1,\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying

(3.1)

\left.\begin{array}[]{rr}\overline{\Delta}^{\varphi}u=\overline{\Delta}^{\varphi}\widetilde{u}=0\ \ \text{on}\ \ \Omega,\widetilde{\Omega}\ \,\ \ \text{respectively.}&\\
\alpha\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\frac{\partial\widetilde{u}}{\partial\widetilde{\nu}}\right)=\Delta^{\varphi}f+\mu\,f\ \ \text{on}\ \ \Sigma.&\end{array}\right\}

with
\displaystyle u=w|_{\Omega}
,
\displaystyle\widetilde{u}=w|_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
,
\displaystyle f=w|_{\Sigma}
,
\displaystyle\nu,\widetilde{\nu}
are the normal vector pointing to
\displaystyle\Omega
,
\displaystyle\widetilde{\Omega}
respectively,
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant depending of
\displaystyle\alpha
.

Definition 3.1
.

We define
\displaystyle\mu
to be the infimum of the functional

(3.2)

\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\Sigma+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\varphi}|\overline{\nabla}w|^{2}\,dx

over all pair of functions
\displaystyle(f,w)\in\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)\times\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\displaystyle f=w|_{\Sigma}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists
\displaystyle(f,w)\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1,\varphi}(\Sigma)\times\mathcal{H}_{loc}^{1,\varphi}(\Sigma)
with
\displaystyle f=w|_{\Sigma}
such that

\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\Sigma+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\varphi}|\overline{\nabla}w|^{2}\,dx=\mu.

Proof.

By definition of minimum
\displaystyle\mu
, there exists a sequence of pairs of functions
\displaystyle\{(f_{n},w_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0.}^{1,\varphi}(\Sigma)\times\mathcal{H}_{loc}^{1,\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\displaystyle w_{n}|_{\Sigma}=f_{n}
satisfying that

(3.3)

\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}|\nabla f_{n}|^{2}\,d\Sigma+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\varphi}|\overline{\nabla}w_{n}|^{2}\,dx\longrightarrow\mu.

Up to horizontal translation of
\displaystyle\Sigma
, we can assume that
\displaystyle\Sigma\cap B(0,1)\neq\emptyset
. Since each
\displaystyle f_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)
together with Lemma (
2.5
), we obtain the following uniformly bounds

\text{sup}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\Sigma\cap B(0,1)}f_{n}^{2}\,d\Sigma<+\infty\ \ ,\ \ \text{sup}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}f_{n}^{2}|x^{T}|\,d\Sigma<+\infty.

On the other hand, from the convergence of
\displaystyle w_{n}
, we get that

\text{sup}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{B(0,1)}|\overline{\nabla}w_{n}|^{2}\,dx<+\infty.

Thus, the Poincare’s Lemma together with the Sobolev trace Theorem, there exists a sequence
\displaystyle\{c_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\text{sup}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{B(0,1)}(w_{n}-c_{n})^{2}\,dx<+\infty\ \ \text{and}\ \ \text{sup}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\Sigma\cap B(0,1)}(f_{n}-c_{n})^{2}\,dx<+\infty

Consequently, from Lemma (
2.4
), the following holds

\text{sup}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\varphi}w_{n}\,dx<+\infty.

After passing to subsequence,
\displaystyle\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a
\displaystyle f
weakly in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{loc}^{1}(\Sigma)
and
\displaystyle\{w_{n}\}
converges to
\displaystyle w
weakly in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Now, consider the cut-off functions
\displaystyle\eta_{m}
defined in (
2.2
). For each
\displaystyle m
large enough, we can choose an arbitrary
\displaystyle 1>\epsilon>0
such that
\displaystyle\eta_{m}^{2}>(1-\epsilon)^{2}\beta^{2}(|x|/m)
. Since each
\displaystyle f_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1,\varphi}
, then

(3.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigg|1-\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}\eta_{m}^{2}f_{n}^{2}\,d\Sigma\bigg|=\bigg|\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}(1-\eta_{m}^{2})f_{n}^{2}\,d\Sigma\bigg|\leq\epsilon(2-\epsilon)+\frac{1}{m}\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}|\beta^{\prime}||x|f_{n}^{2}\,d\Sigma

(3.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}\eta_{m}f_{n}\,d\Sigma\bigg|\leq\bigg|\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}(1-\eta_{m})f_{n}\,d\Sigma\bigg|\leq\left(\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}(1-\eta_{m})^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}f_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

where in the last inequality we used the Hölder inequality. Now, from Lemma (
2.5
) and taking into account that
\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}|x|\in L^{2,\varphi}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle n,m\rightarrow+\infty

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)

\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\Sigma+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\varphi}|\overline{\nabla}w|^{2}\,dx\leq\text{liminf}\left(\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}|\nabla f_{n}|^{2}\,d\Sigma+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\varphi}|\overline{\nabla}w_{n}|^{2}\right)\,dx\leq\mu.

In particular,

\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\Sigma<+\infty\ \ \text{and}\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\varphi}|\overline{\nabla}w|^{2}\,dx<+\infty

and

\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}|\nabla f|^{2}\,d\Sigma+\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\varphi}|\overline{\nabla}w|^{2}\,dx=\mu.

4
Spectral Theorem. Jacobi operator.

Let
\displaystyle\Sigma
be a properly embedded
\displaystyle[\varphi,\vec{e}_{3}]
-minimal surface in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
where
\displaystyle\varphi
is a smooth function with almost quadratic growth. Moreover, we also assume that the norm of the second fundametal form
\displaystyle|S|^{2}
of
\displaystyle\Sigma
is bounded.

Since
\displaystyle\Sigma
is a critical point of the weighted area
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\varphi}
, we consider the following bilinear simetric form
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}:\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)\times\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

(4.1)

\mathcal{Q}(u,\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}\left(\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi\rangle-qu\phi\right)\,d\Sigma,

where
\displaystyle q=|S|^{2}-\ddot{\varphi}\eta^{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Sigma)\cap L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
which is associated with the Jacobi operator of stability
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)
defined by

(4.2)

\mathcal{L}=\Delta^{\varphi}+q.

In this context, the weak solutions
\displaystyle u
of the problem
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u=-\lambda u
associtated to eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda
can be characterized by functions
\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)
satisfying

\mathcal{Q}(u,\phi)=\lambda\int_{\Sigma}u\phi\,d\Sigma,

for any test function
\displaystyle\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)
with compact support. Moreover, the first eigenvalue is given by

(4.3)

\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})=\text{inf}_{\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)}\frac{\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-qu^{2}\right)\,d\Sigma}{\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}u^{2}\,d\Sigma}.

Notice that, the quotient (
4.3
) is invariant by scaling. Thus, we can take, without loss of generality, the infimum in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)
.

The following Courant-type nodal domain result was proved by R. Chen, J. Mao and C. Wu
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
which stablish the following structure

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\displaystyle\Omega
is a regular domain on a given Riemannian manifold. For the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for
\displaystyle\Delta^{\varphi}
, its eigenvalues consist of a non-decreasing sequence
\displaystyle 0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\cdot+\infty
. Denote by
\displaystyle f_{i}
an eigenfunction of the
\displaystyle i
-th eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}
and
\displaystyle\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},\cdots\}
forms a complete orthogonal basis of
\displaystyle L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, for each
\displaystyle k=1,2,3\cdots
, the number of nodal domains of
\displaystyle f_{k}
is less than or equal to
\displaystyle k
.

Second, we characterize variationally the first eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})
for the Jacobi operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\varphi}
on the space of functions
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\Sigma
be a properly embedded
\displaystyle[\varphi,\vec{e}_{3}]
-minimal surface in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{3}
with bounded
\displaystyle|S|^{2}
and function
\displaystyle\varphi
satisfying (
1.3
). Then,

(4.4)

\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})=\text{inf}_{\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)}\bigg\{\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-qu^{2}\right)\,d\Sigma\bigg\}=\text{inf}_{\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)}\mathcal{Q}(u,u).

and there exists
\displaystyle f_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)
such that
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},f_{1})=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})
. Moreover, all eigenfunctions associated to
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Sigma)
can be characterized variationally by

\mathcal{Q}(u,u)\geq\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})\ \ \text{for any}\ \ u\in\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)

with equality if and only if
\displaystyle u
is eigenfunction of
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})

Proof.

Firstly, the existence of this infimum is guaranteed since
\displaystyle q\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\Sigma)

\mathcal{Q}(u,u)\geq-\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}qu^{2}\,d\Sigma\geq-\text{sup}_{\Sigma}q\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}u^{2}\,d\Sigma\geq-\text{sup}_{\Sigma}q.

By definition, there exists a sequence
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of functions in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)
such that

\mathcal{Q}(u_{n},u_{n})\rightarrow\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi}).

Moreover, the sequence
\displaystyle\{\mathcal{Q}(u_{n},u_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is upper bounded and thus,
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in norm of
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{loc}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle L^{2,\varphi}(\Sigma)

since applying the Hölder inequality,

\bigg|\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}\eta_{m}u_{n}\,d\Sigma\bigg|\leq\bigg|\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}(1-\eta_{m})u_{n}\,d\Sigma\bigg|\leq\left(\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}(1-\eta_{m})^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}u_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

for any
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, from the lower-semicontinuity we obtain

\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})\leq\mathcal{Q}(f_{1},f_{1})\leq\text{liminf}_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{Q}(u_{n},u_{n})=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi}).

Now, the eigenfunctions associated to
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})
can be characterized variationally as follows: take
\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{H}^{1,\varphi}_{0}(\Sigma)
such that

\mathcal{Q}(u,\phi)=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}u\phi\,d\Sigma,

for any test function

h(t)=\mathcal{Q}(u+t\phi,u+t\phi)-\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}(u+t\phi)^{2}\,d\Sigma\ \ \text{for}\ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Notice that
\displaystyle h(t)\geq 0
with
\displaystyle h(0)=0
. Thus,
\displaystyle h^{\prime}(0)=0
which implies that

\mathcal{Q}(u,\phi)=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{\varphi})\int_{\Sigma}e^{\varphi}u\phi\,d\Sigma.

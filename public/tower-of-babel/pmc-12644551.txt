A deep reinforcement learning integrated aerial RIS system for enhanced combat intelligence
Authors: Mian Muhammad Kamal, Syed Zain Ul Abideen, Sajed Ahmad, Abdalrahman Husein, Kamran Ullah, Osama Alfarraj, Fahad Alblehai, Muhammad Sheraz, Teong Chee Chuah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe military operations demand effective communication and proper situational awareness especially in situations where the line-of-sight communication is compromised and performance of a cluttered situation can be impaired by the interference of the clutter. The combination of communication and sensing systems offers a good solution to the simultaneous transfer of data and target detection. Nevertheless, efficiency, reliability and minimum interference in these systems are still complex challenges to optimize these systems. In this paper, a new method of an aerial reconfigurable intelligent surface (A-RIS) in an integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) system is presented. It is installed with a central base station (BS), A-RIS, target sensing and a number of users. Using deep reinforcement learning (DRL) we suggest the mechanism to optimize jointly transmit beamforming at the BS, RIS phase shifts and A-RIS pathway under the strict signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) constraints. The results show that there are major improvements in the performance of the system such as a minimization in self-interference and clutter echoes as well as adaptive RIS phase shift to changing battlefield conditions. Our approach out-muscles the conventional approaches that obtain significant improvements in the reliability of communication and target identification.Keywords: Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), Deep reinforcement learning (DRL), Reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Military operations need to be properly communicated and have situational awareness. Communication systems provide coordination intelligence exchange and command over massive areas1. However these systems frequently encounter significant challenges especially in environments where jamming attacks compromise system reliability and obstacles block signals and interfere with transmissions2. Urban and mountainous regions are most affected by these problems which lead to lower efficiency and difficulties maintaining constant contact3. Furthermore maintaining strong communication linkages is made more difficult by the constantly changing combat conditions and the mobility of military troops4.

Traditional communication networks experience signal degradation due to environmental obstacles, interference and user mobility. As a result there has been growing interest in Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS) as a promising solution5. RIS consist of a large array of passive elements capable of manipulating electromagnetic waves by reflecting, refracting or diffusing signals. By altering the phase of its components RIS can decrease interference boost signal propagation and get past obstacles because to its flexibility and adaptability. Because of this it is especially helpful in situations where there is no line of sight (NLoS) which is common in the conflict situations6.

Although RIS has several advantages its use in ISAC systems is still a relatively new field of study. ISAC systems enable simultaneous data transfer and environmental monitoring by combining communication and sensing capabilities into a single framework7. This is particularly important in military operations, whereby it is critical to have reliable communication channels, and at the same time to identify and monitor possible threats real time8. Combining communication and sensing functions, ISAC systems decrease the necessity of the independent systems to optimize the use of resources and enhance the efficiency of the functioning but the joint optimization of communication and sensing parameters remains a problem. For example optimizing transmitter beamforming RIS phase shifts9 and the movement of mobile platforms carrying communication and sensing equipment must ensure high communication quality while simultaneously maximizing sensing accuracy10. In order to avoid performance loss brought on by interference and ambient noise these optimizations must also be accomplished under strict instructions, such as maintaining a high SINR.

Recent advancements in DRL have shown promising results in solving complex optimization problems in dynamic environments such as those encountered in military operations11. Because DRL can learn optimal policies for complex systems through interactions with the environment it is particularly well-suited for improving the parameters of ISAC systems where traditional optimization techniques would not be enough12. DRL-based approaches provide real-time adaptability to changing ambient conditions and user movement providing an advantageous way of enhancing sensing and communication in unstable situations. An effective method to improve the effectiveness, dependability and flexibility of communication and sensing systems in challenging military environments is to integrate RIS, ISAC and DRL. Somehow real-time performance evaluation and system design optimization under dynamic situations still present difficulties. In particular methods for integrating RIS and ISAC while maintaining optimal SINR levels and minimizing interference require further investigation.

Recent developments in DRL variants, such as Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), have shown improved stability and sample efficiency in dynamic environments. For example the entropy-maximization framework of SAC facilitates robust exploration in high-dimensional action spaces13 while TD3 delayed policy updates and clipped double-Q learning reduce estimation bias in continuous control tasks14,15. The latter is especially significant to RIS-assisted ISAC systems that will be deployed in hostile military environments where real time flexibility will be of the essence.

Although the suggested DRL framework self-evolves to match the combat conditions, such as the varying interference and maneuvering, explicit resilience to the uncertainty in the channels, such as RIS misalignment and Doppler shifts, is essential to missions of critical importance. Our DDPG-based approach can handle these deviations with great efficiency by optimization of RIS phase shifts and trajectories. Further studies can be done on how to combine robust optimization techniques, e.g., the stochastic Programming16 or minimax optimization17 technique to cover worst-case analysis and guarantee reliable operation even when strong jamming or hardware failures occur. ISAC capabilities of interference mitigation coincide with such concepts of cognitive radio as dynamic spectrum access (DSA). We extend our solution beyond conventional DSA paradigms that are based on spontaneous spectrum usage by adaptation and Sensing18,19. We improve on this by refining the RIS-assisted beamforming and sensing signals simultaneously thereby reducing mutual interference. The efficiency in using the spectrum without needing any specific sensing slots is made possible in contested military environments that suffer fragmentation and jamming of the spectrum. Just like cognitive radar-communication symbiosis, our system enables harmonious operation of communication and sensing using the RIS as reconfigurable enabler for spectrum sharing20.

In addition, in fast-moving military operations today, low latency is critical in making quick decisions. Thus, the enhanced DRL structure we developed is used to minimize that decision latency by providing a real-time updating function for modifications in RIS settings while aligning everything within the corresponding time slot, so all communication and sensing tasks will be the same as before.

Related work

The Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) concept would be one of the primary technologies that would rule in future wireless systems, including 6G. Its major promise is that it enables rigorous data transmission alongside sophisticated environmental sensing in a single platform. However, the primary hindrance to achieving this promise is the natural interference that exists between its communication and sensing functionalities. To this end, researchers have proposed new ways that span a wide spectrum. One such area of focus is how to use Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS). One work examines how one such RIS can be optimized for reducing mutual interference and improving overall system performance. As an example one of the studies involves the use of a RIS in reducing the interference and the overall performance of the system by optimizing the phase shifts of the RIS and beamforming21. This method proves that the RIS-assisted systems may be effective in mitigating mutual interference in order to enhance the performance of the system. Also the problem of security in ISAC systems has attracted a lot of attention and one of the main risks that have been noted is that eavesdropping by the unauthorized users is possible. To overcome this a recent study comes up with a safe precoding scheme which involves the use of long short-term memory networks to determine the predictability of eavesdropping channels thereby improving. communication secrecy22. This architecture uses prediction based techniques to ensure that the occurrence of eavesdropping estimation enhances the communication secrecy by a great distance than the conventional approaches. In parallel the concept of It has introduced Distributed and Intelligent ISAC as an extension of ISAC. DISAC suggests a distributed architecture to support heterogeneous sensors to track users and objects in large scale and in an energy efficient manner. It also adds a semantic and goal oriented approach to data fusion operations in order to supersede classical data aggregation23. This is a more developed approach to ISAC systems. Research in the area has been done with the aim of streamlining the paths of unmanned aerial vehicles and beamforming mechanisms to allow covert communications. The researchers have come up with a block coordinate descent-based optimization algorithm by the design of UAVs in such a way that they can avoid detection by passive onlookers and still achieve the best communication capabilities. Such an algorithm is aimed at mitigating the practical limitations on the speed and power of UAVs in order to maximize the possible covert communication rate24. The importance of the given research is in the necessity to coordinate optimally the trajectory of the UAV and beamforming vectors to overcome the detection threats but to provide the consistent communication.

The IRS-aided secure transmission schemes have also been developed due to security issues that are of a specific concern to sensing waveforms that may reveal sensitive information. These schemes mutually optimize jamming precoding at the BS and passive phase reflecting at the IRS to maximize the sum secrecy rate and to ensure that the sensing functionalities are not impaired this method improves the communication security and the required sensing capabilities25. Also the RIS application in ISAC system has been investigated to reduce transmit power without compromising the communication and radar sensing performance by optimizing beamforming and phase shifts across numerous RIS units massive performance improvements can be demonstrated in terms of power efficiency and system capacity26. This study demonstrates the possible benefits of using a number of RIS to improve the performance of the system further both in communication and sensing.

In quantum-aided DRL contexts to improve the performance of the ISAC systems in terms of direction-of-arrival (DoA) estimation and task offloading, quantum-aided DRL frameworks have been suggested. With the advantages of the quantum computings parallel processing features these frameworks can dramatically reduce the task offloading latency and DoA estimation accuracy relative to the traditional DRL algorithms27. Another study involves addressing the weaknesses of RIS in ISAC systems especially to eavesdropping and detection attacks and the study in question considers the application of active STAR-RIS to provide even greater security and the proposed approach is expected to counter both eavesdropping and detection attacks with the application of a unified safety strategy maximizing the beamforming and reflection strategies in order to achieve maximum secrecy and covert rates28,29. The fundamental optimization of RIS remains a hot area of research.

Furthermore, joint optimization of transmit beamforming at the base station and reflection coefficients at the RIS has been studied with the explicit goal of maximizing the sum capacity of the ISAC system. This line of research is very important as it deals with difficult non-convex optimization problems that come into play due to adverse channel conditions and provides a feasible and efficient method for enhancing overall system performance30. This study brings out the contribution of RIS in solving the non-convex optimization problem that ISAC systems possess that provide a feasible solution to enhancing system performance. Besides, another work examines the implementation of STAR-RIS in facilitating covert communication in millimeter wave ISAC systems31. The suggested model is aimed at reducing the probability of detection error (PDE) based on beamforming strategies of transmission and reflection such that can guarantee good covertness performance against the possible wardens This paper has shown that the STAR-RIS network performs better related to covertness compared to the baseline methods32.

Additionally, privacy concerns in ISAC systems have led to the development of advanced RIS-assisted techniques for protecting the sensing region against adversarial detectors. By dynamically assigning reflecting and sensing RIS elements the maximum sensing SINR at adversarial detectors is minimized ensuring enhanced protection against unauthorized detection33. The optimization of RIS element assignment further improves sensing protection offering a robust solution for secure sensing in ISAC networks. Recent studies have explored optimization techniques to enhance the performance of non-line-of-sight ISAC systems also researchers have developed a five-stage hybrid optimization algorithm based on block coordinate descent to address the weighted minimum mean square error problem effectively converting the optimization challenge into a convex form that can be efficiently solved34. and this approach has demonstrated effectiveness in optimizing the performance of non-line-of-sight ISAC systems resulting in improved signal reception and overall system efficiency. Motivation

The reliable connectivity and correct situational awareness is vital in the military communication systems. But when operating in tricky conditions like battlefield conditions, communication links can be inhibited in a environment due to obstacles, clutter, interference as well as the fast dynamic change of the environment. Traditional systems have significant restrictions, especially in the NLoS situations. In order to solve these challenges it is important to come up with systems that are dynamically designed to cope with changing operational environments.

A combination of RIS with ISAC systems is a promising solution that can improve the communication performance as well as sensing performance. RIS is able to control the electromagnetic wave propagation by controlling the phase shift, which allows it to detect targets reliably and provides a continuous communication flow. Nonetheless, the conventional methods of fixed RIS or non-adaptive receive beamforming do not take advantage of the dynamic character of battlefield settings and thus lead to poor performance.

Here, adaptive optimization techniques that are able to optimize both communication and sensing parameters jointly and reduce interference and reaction to the environmental changes in real time have a critical need. To compete with this gap, we suggest a DDPG based algorithm to learn the optimal strategies by continuously interacting with the environment. The proposed solution is aimed at optimization of RIS phase shifts, receive beamforming patterns and A-RIS trajectory with SINR constraints being put into a detailed consideration

Minimizing drives the system performance closer to the theoretical limit of localization accuracy, which is critical for military applications like drone neutralization or hostile vehicle tracking. Unlike Mean Squared Error (MSE), which depends on the specific estimator used like maximum likelihood or least squares, the CRB provides a universal lower bound for all unbiased estimators. Maximizing the CRB directly improves the intrinsic sensing power of the system so that the system is able to detect the target reliably even in the presence of dynamic interference.

Our selection of DDPG is guided by the fact that it has been shown to work well in real-time trajectory modification using limited training samples, despite the fact that TD3 and SAC are theoretically more stable in that regard45. In line with DDPG’s deterministic actor, the military operational context necessitates deterministic policy outputs for predictable RIS moves. Future research however might use SAC probability maximisation for better exploration in intricate electromagnetic environments or TD3’s clipped critics to lessen inaccurate estimation bias.

Convergence analysis and training stability

The convergence behavior of the proposed DDPG-based ARIS trajectory optimization is evaluated through learning curves depicting the cumulative reward and loss functions over training episodes. The results indicate that the DDPG algorithm achieves stable convergence within episodes, with reduced variance after sufficient exploration.

Additionally, soft target network updates () and the use of the OU noise process contributed significantly to stabilizing the training process and avoiding premature convergence to sub-optimal policies.

Computational complexity

While DRL introduces additional computational overhead due to neural network training and replay buffer management, the DDPG framework offers several mechanisms that improve efficiency in continuous control tasks. Specifically, DDPG avoids the need for exhaustive action space search by learning a differentiable policy through the actor network, which significantly reduces the per-decision computational cost compared to traditional optimization algorithms.

Moreover, the use of experience replay and target networks stabilizes training and reduces the number of required environment interactions. In our implementation, we further reduce computational load by:

The Military operations have high sensitivity in the strength of the proposed system since uncertainties facing the environment like the misalignment of the RIS, Doppler effect and jamming or noises are prevalent. To consider these, our system optimizes the RIS phase shifts and A-RIS trajectory continuously to adapt to changing conditions to ensure the communication and sensing quality. Other more explicit methods of robust optimization will be looked into in future work, so that the system can be operated in a reliable manner even in extreme adversarial situations.

To implement the proposed ARIS-aided ISAC system in practice, it would be possible to include it into the current military communication structure by modifying the current BS to an RIS element and revising the communication standards to enable the adjustment of the phase (in real-time). The main problem is to guarantee the compatibility with the current mobile platforms and communication hardware and at the same time have low power consumption and reduce latency. Our solution may be scaled upon the basis of the available communication channels and augmenting them with an intelligent surface technology offered.

Conclusions

This paper presents a new ARIS-based ISAC system that enhances the communication and situational awareness under dynamic military settings. Through a DRL-based optimization system we simultaneously optimized the AP beamforming, ARIS trajectory and RIS phase shifts which greatly improved the communication and sensing performance. We have also added NSP-based beamforming at the receiver which is used effectively to suppress self-interference and clutter echoes. The vast simulations show that our approach is better than the conventional benchmarks especially in detecting ability and communication quality. The adaptive path planning of A-RIS to adapt to altered conditions of the battlefield improves the capabilities of the system to provide target recognition and communication resiliency in high interference prone areas. To further enhance the system’s scalability and performance in the future, we suggest exploring alternate combat scenarios and incorporating different machine learning models. These developments will help in the development of next-generation military ISAC technologies which will provide more robust, flexible and effective solutions to current military sensing and communication requirements.

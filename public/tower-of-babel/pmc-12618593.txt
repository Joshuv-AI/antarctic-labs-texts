Techno-economic evaluation of a smart power management system based on wind turbine hybrid model using Homer tool: a case study
Authors: Kamal Sapkota, Shabbiruddin, Neeraj Kanwar, Karma Sonam Sherpa, Abhinandan Baruah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractInvestigating wind energy resources is the main goal of the current study. Three tiny Wind Turbine Generators (WTGs) are incorporated into four different system models using Homer Pro Tool to simulate and optimise a 4 × 3 system configuration, producing twelve operational scenarios. A review of real-time load data and the use of precise wind resource data from an operating Weather Monitoring Station (WMS) situated in the study area marked the beginning of the procedure. Based on the capacity factor (Cf) of six small wind turbines, three small wind turbines—Excel-10, Montana-3310, and SD6-701 are chosen. The three tiny turbines are incorporated into four hybrid optimum system models that combine battery, grid, and generators to reduce wind intermittency and power outages. The winning system architecture’s results are evaluated. Results show that the combination of hybrid wind turbines accounts for approximately 88.4% of utility grid electricity curtailment. To meet the local net demand of 165.44 kWh/day in isolated and grid-connected modes, the cost factors, represented by NPC and LCOE, range from ₹2.1 to ₹3.5 Crore and ₹3.14 to ₹5.78/kWh, respectively, in all four simulated models. Additionally, a thorough sensitivity analysis of every feasible configuration is provided by varying hub height of wind turbine generators to anticipate power output generation and cost parameters.Keywords: Capacity factor, Economic metrics, Homer pro tool, Wind energy, Techno-economic feasibility, WMSSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

In the pursuit of a secure and reliable energy future, the global energy sector is undergoing a crucial phase of transition. The target of net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050 entails a huge technological advancement, with new incentive policies for energy production from Renewable Energy Sources (RES)1,2. Despite intermittency, grid safety, and dispersed nature, wind energy technology is amongst the fastest-growing and most preferred RES. The inherent intermittency and unpredictability of wind energy greatly impact the stable and efficient operation of the grid system. Nevertheless, power profile fluctuation can be mitigated by introducing component hybridization and optimal power management strategies3. Integrating a multi-energy system significantly enhances system reliability and performance while ensuring optimal resource utilization. Hence, hybridization of WTGs in complement with energy storage elements and a real-time grid system may deliver reliable, secure, and efficient power to the end users4.

To address these energy sector issues, several works on techno-economic performance and evaluation of different WTGs have been presented in previous works, considering local wind resource potential and load demand of the areas. For instance, Jabir et al. presented various wind intermittent smoothing approaches and suggested that the battery storage strategies are the most reliable and effective approach to control fluctuating power output from the grid-connected WTGs5. In a similar approach, Fosso Tajouo et al. performed a techno-economic evaluation on a hybrid PV-wind–battery system for a rural community. The system optimization is achieved in the Homer Pro tool, where techno-economic aspects are examined based on LCOE and NPC parameters6. The Homer software is being mostly applied for techno-economic performance and optimization models in several RES studies7–11. Comparably, Ur Rashid et al. investigated several component combinations of a hybrid renewable energy system12. From these model combinations, optimal and economic solutions were decided based on NPC, LCOE, and payback parameters. Their findings demonstrated that the proposed system is suitable for reducing carbon emissions and energy costs below $0.30/kWh. Similarly, wind and hydro energy production is being assessed by Kohole et al., for energy generation in remote areas13. Further, authors evaluated the techno-economic viability assessment considering output power, capacity factor, and green hydrogen production. Recently, studies have been mostly focused on reducing the cost of energy by integrating new technologies into the energy mix. A study conducted by Kumar et al., presented in14, evaluated the feasibility assessment of hybrid solar, wind, and biomass to integrate with green hydrogen and battery storage elements as an off-grid solution for isolated rural communities in India. Integration of variable RES provides a long-term plan and strong competitiveness for low low-emission technological pathway. In another study by Sambhi et al., the performance parameters of a smart hybrid power plant are being optimized based on key parameters such as energy cost, life cycle cost, payback period, and emission rate15. In Kadhim et al., the authors presented strategies aimed at increasing the efficiency of micro wind turbines under low wind conditions16. Authors Charabi and Abdul-Wahab analyzed one year of wind resource data on Homer Pro Tool to optimize the cost of electricity using various standard wind turbine models17. Likewise, Ali et al. developed a solar-wind-battery-micro grid system using HOMER software with four configurations to address the power outages, ensuring economic viability and environmental sustainability for an isolated institute18. A hybrid renewable energy system was investigated by Nguyen et al. for power generation and to support economic viability in rural areas10. Social and environmental influencing factors, such as community health, along with economic and agricultural outputs, were also being investigated and compared. In a similar approach, Barelli et al. demonstrated the techno-economic feasibility of smart power management of WTGs and integration with a flywheel-chemical storage system for power quality improvement and grid stability2. As a sustainable approach for energy storage and use, authors Makky et al. designed a hybrid system integrating solar, wind, battery, fuel cell, and electrolyzer to assess green hydrogen production feasibility, supporting Oman’s clean energy transition19. An integrated renewable energy system is also being discussed by Chauhan and Saini in3, where the assessment of locally available energy resources is being conducted to fulfill the daily energy needs of an isolated village. The authors analyzed several small WTGs to evaluate their feasibility based on the local wind regime. Moreover, the optimum system configuration is being determined based on LCOE, NPC, and job creation potentials. Techno-economic performance of a wind energy system in the Badakhshan region is being evaluated by Almutairi et al., focusing on green hydrogen production for storage management and carbon footprint reduction20. Economic analysis is being incorporated with LCOE, Levelized Cost of Hydrogen (LCOH), along with the payback period. A techno-economic and environmental evaluation is being presented by Adoum Abdoulaye in21 by integrating solar PV, wind, battery, and fuel cell in both grid-connected and standalone configurations. Metaheuristic optimization is being utilized in MATLAB for multi-criteria system sizing, aimed at rural electrification. Such emerging technologies offer zero-emission and more flexible solution for grid stabilization.

The present study is making a pioneering attempt to perform a techno-economic feasibility analysis of a wind energy system by utilizing real-time load data acquired from a power control station and wind resource data gathered from a practically installed WMS device in the author’s study area. The case study is being conducted in the Namthang block, located in the Namchi district of Sikkim, India, where the potential of hydroelectric power generation has already been examined. Nevertheless, Glaciers within the extended Himalayan regions are receding rapidly, leading to substantial potential impact on future hydroelectric power generation in the entire mountainous terrain22. Therefore, to address the future energy crisis, exploration of alternative renewable energy sources like solar, wind, and biomass stands as a central focus for researchers and energy planners. The topographic features of Sikkim consist of expansive terrain with crest-shaped ridges, making it highly favorable for wind energy exploration and power generation23. Moreover, in contrast to solar PV installation, a wind turbine takes up only a small portion of land, and the land beneath the wind turbine can be utilized for other human activities such as agriculture. It is a first-of-its-kind approach that proposes to use of a single RES implementing a wind turbine to fulfill the local energy demand of the study region. Moreover, during the period of wind absence, power is sourced from the utility grid. The utility grid also offers an option to sell the surplus power through extended bidirectional metering arrangements. Additional backup system comprising Exide battery arrays with its technical datasheet24, is being introduced to maintain the batteries in a charged state while the WTGs are in operation. When both wind and grid are unavailable, the power is supplemented from a Gen set and battery arrays.

Novelty

This study offers a significant and innovative contribution through the exploration of rapidly growing wind energy potential in the hilly terrains of Sikkim, India. It evaluates the techno-economic viability of a small wind turbine system for electricity generation by conducting a detailed real-time load survey and energy potential analysis using site- collected wind data from a monitoring mast at a pilot site. Further, advanced optimization tool like HOMER enhances the assessment by ensuring accurate modeling and performance evaluation. This approach of transitioning to renewable energy sources has not been previously deployed in a region heavily reliant on conventional power sources. Despite wind turbine costs accounting for roughly 70% of the total project expenditure, this study is dedicating efforts to enhancing power production efficiency by using on-site data to guide these improvements. This innovative approach caters to the region’s unique conditions, providing a flexible and intelligent power management strategy for the Himalayan terrains of India. This study presents a detailed analysis that addresses key shortcoming in the existing research, which often fails to present a unified solution designed for diverse local topography and climatic conditions. Unlike, previous works it incorporates site- specific wind data, turbine selection based on manufacturer’s datasheet, and resources availability to better align with the challenges of complex hilly terrains. By evaluating 4 × 3 architectural models, the research presents optimized outcomes across critical economic indicators like NPC and LCOE, as well as technical metrics such as energy production, payback, and capacity factors. Environmental and socio- economic aspects including GHG emissions reduction, and possibility of job creation are also analyzed to assess boarder regional impacts. This study emphasized system efficiency and reliability in both stand- alone and grid- connected modes. Through simulation and optimization of diverse configurations and turbine types, it prioritizes grid integration, load balancing, and investment feasibility. Sensitivity analysis further ensures the system behaviors by evaluating the impact of variations in hub height and energy demand, supporting long term scalability in complex hilly terrains.

Materials and methods

The methodological framework, illustrated by Fig. 1, initiates with input data fed into the Homer Pro Tool. It primarily runs an energy balance algorithm, and if demand-supply equilibrium is not maintained, the system necessitates reconfiguration. Upon achieving satisfactory simulation results, the optimizer proceeds with component sizing to minimize NPC, LCOE for the most cost-effective solution. It also considers factors like power generation, operation, and maintenance expenses, replacement cost, and the payback period.

Study site description

A cluster of 21 villages in the Namthang Block of Namchi district, Sikkim, India (as shown in Fig. 2), is selected for the study area. These villages are situated at approximately 27.1667° N and 88.4833° E with elevations ranging from 200 m to 1800 m. The region has a total of 2872 households and a population of 14, 354 (2011 census)25,26. The site suitability study indicates a high potential for wind power in these areas, classified as LSI class 5 zone (highly suitable). This classification considers factors like high wind speed, low slopes, accessible infrastructure of roads and power lines, reduced lightning risk, and stable soil conditions23.

Study area- Namthang block in Namchi district, Sikkim, India.

During the summer season, the average maximum temperature in this area ranges from 24 °C to 26 °C, with an average minimum temperature of about 5 °C to 7 °C as recorded by a Weather Monitoring Station (WMS) device23. The average air pressure in this location typically falls within the range of 865.50 hPa to 885.50 hPa according to device records. The area experiences frequent power outages due to increasing load demand driven by rapid industrialization. Given its diverse climatic conditions and expansive topographic features, the region is well-suited for the establishment of wind farms as a solution to the unreliable electricity supply.

Electrical load survey

The electrical load demand data for the region is being obtained from the Load Dispatch Centre of the State Electricity Board (Power Department) and is being collected through the control switching station located in South Sikkim. At this station, feeders or distribution transformers supplying electricity to the selected pilot site is identified. Time-series hourly load consumption data, recorded in amperes on log sheets, are utilized to develop daily, hourly, and monthly load profiles over a complete one-year period. This data, comprising demand for domestic, commercial, bulk supply (mixed load), and industrial sectors, is then converted into net energy demand in kilowatt-hours per day (kWh/day).

The domestic sector includes residential households, institutions for domestic purposes, community lightning, places of worship, heating, and water pumping. The commercial is primarily serving businesses such as shops, restaurants, and agricultural farms for private gains, whereas bulk supplies or mixed loads covering electric vehicle charging stations, nursing homes, and military supplies. The industrial sector includes small factories with both low- and high-tension power supplies. By analyzing the energy requirements of various end- users, the daily electrical power demand in kilowatts (kW) for the study area is being plotted in Fig. 3a. This plot revealing that power demand peaking during the morning hours from 6:00 am to 8:00 am and in the evening from 5.30 pm to 8:30 pm.

(a) Daily load profile at various hours of the day in kW, (b) Monthly load profile in kW.

Typically, the load demand ranges from 1050 kW to 1350 kW, averaging at 1165 kW. Upon entering the load data into Homer Pro Tool, it is found that the net energy consumption is scaling to 165.44 kWh/day, with a baseline at 27447.02 kWh/day. The average power is scaling at 6.89 kW, with a baseline at 1143.63 kW, and the peak power is scaling at 18.06 kW, operating at a load factor of 0.38, with a baseline at 3036.97 kW. Wind resource data

This study aims to acquire real-time wind resource data from a WMS device installed at the study site. The device provides 15-minute interval data, including wind speed (m/s), wind direction (degrees), air temperature (°C), air pressure (hPa), and relative humidity (%) at a 10-meter level, which is crucial for wind resource modeling. The WMS device is being situated in Manaydara in Namthang block, South Sikkim, as indicated in Fig. 4. The data collected at the 10-meter level is being extrapolated to the 50-meter level using the power law extrapolation technique. The average wind speed at the study site is being determined to be approximately 5.5 m/s. The variation of maximum and minimum wind speed lies in the range of 2.5 m/s to 7.5 m/s at the site, illustrated by Fig. 5a.

Turbine selection

As outlined in Sect. “Consideration of small WTGs”, small wind turbines are generators with low power ratings, and their power generation performance is significantly influenced by both internal and external factors36. Therefore, the capacity factor (Cf) of each wind turbine is being determined, representing the realistic power of a wind turbine based on optimal wind resource availability and geographical location37,38. It is worth noting that the Cf value for the same wind turbine may vary at different sites39. Therefore, three out of the six small wind turbines are being selected based on their Cf value for conducting techno-economic simulations and developing cost optimization models using Homer Pro Tool. Techno-economic evaluations

In this work, the optimal system configuration is being performed using economic criteria, including Net Present Cost (NPC) and Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE), to evaluate the financial feasibility of the proposed system.

Additionally, an assessment is being conducted on technical and social dimensions, including the Simple Payback (SPB) period and the potential for employment generation.

Net present cost

The Net Present Cost (NPC) in a wind energy hybrid system represents the total expense realization over the project lifetime. This includes capital expenditure, operation and maintenance costs, replacement costs, fuel expenses, and cost of purchasing grid power, as indicated by Eq. (9)12;

This comprehensive power management approach is reducing losses and improving energy generation efficiency.

Sensitivity analysis

The sensitivity analysis involves testing different input conditions in an optimization technique to assess their impact on the system model output, providing a broader perspective of the investigation globally or locally58. In this study, the main objective of the sensitivity analysis is to identify the optimum hub height of the WTG to accommodate a potential increase in the study area’s net energy demand. Given the rugged hilly terrain with difficult topography and variable climatic conditions, the primary aim of this study is to determine the optimum hub height that ensures stable wind speeds and dependable wind energy generation for uninterrupted power supply. Therefore, a sensitivity analysis is being conducted to assess how changes in Hub Height (HH in meters) corresponding to variations in Net Energy Demand (D in kWh/day) relative to the base case. The base case refers to the initial scenario in the simulated optimal cost, outlined in Table 6, reflecting the lowest NPC and the LCOE values across the four winning system architecture models. Increasing the HH of the WTG is anticipated to improve net power generation while optimizing cost metrics- NPC and LCOE- at a lower level. Therefore, this study introduces a sensitivity analysis, exploring three distinct cases:

Case I (SA1): Keeping the HH of WTG constant but incrementing D of the study area.

Case II (SA2): Maintaining the D of the study area constant while varying the HH of WTG.

Case III (SA3): Raising the HH of WTG as well as incrementing the D of the study area.

Here, in the base case (original winning system), the WTGs actual manufacturer specified Hub Height (HH) at the actual Net Energy Demand (D) of the study area is being utilized.

In the base case, the manufacturers specified Hub Height (HH) for the SD6-701 wind turbine for winning Models I to III is being set at 9 m, while for the Excel-10 wind turbine in winning Model IV, it is being set at 30.5 m. Discussion

A techno-economic feasibility assessment was conducted using the Homer Pro simulation Tool in this study, with a case study executed in the hilly terrains of South Sikkim, India. This involves conducting a comprehensive load survey and identifying potential renewable resources. The analysis focused on various-rated small wind turbine hybridized architectures, optimizing critical cost indices (NPC and LCOE), technical factors (energy production, payback, capacity factors), and socio-environmental considerations (GHG emissions reduction, and job creation potential. The integration of a small wind turbine with essential components like Battery, Gen set- Grid effectively addresses the fluctuating wind energy for growing load demand. However, it is noted that the wind turbine cost constitutes approximately 70% of the total project cost. Therefore, optimizing power production and load-demand balancing for wind turbine-hybrid systems at specific sites is crucial for maximizing investment opportunities59. Furthermore, this flexible and intelligent power management strategy enables efficient switching under various operating conditions. Notably, such a wind-hybrid energy model has not yet been implemented in the hilly terrains of Sikkim, India.

Therefore, this investigation conducts a simulation and cost optimization of 4 × 3 architectural models of small wind turbine hybrid systems. For accurate wind resource data, annual wind speed data is obtained from a WMS device installed in the study site, as shown in Fig. 4. Additionally, real-time load profile data is sourced from a control switching station in South Sikkim, as depicted in Fig. 3b, which is formatted for input into the Homer Pro Tool. The output power curve and Weibull probabilistic distribution function of six empaneled (NIWE and SWCC board) small wind turbines are analyzed to compute the capacity factor (Cf) for each wind turbine. Using the Cf values presented in Table 3, three small wind turbines are chosen to best fit with the current load demand and wind resource availability. Twelve distinct system architectures, consisting of three small wind turbines and four different system models, are proposed and analyzed under various operating scenarios, as presented in Table 5. The winning architectures are determined by considering techno-economic and socio-environmental factors, outlined in Tables 6 and 7. To address the local net demand of 165.44 kWh/day under both isolated and grid-connected modes, cost evaluations through NPC were found to range between ₹2.1–3.5 crore, across four simulation models. The study scales net energy consumption to 165.44 kWh/day, derived from a baseline of 27,447.02 kWh/day, using a common factor of 165.90 applied by the simulation tool. Similar studies with HOMER Pro optimize costs at approximately 0.0000943 crore per kWh/day of load share10,12,59. Accordingly, this study anticipates an average NPC of about ₹2.58 crore for the 27,447.02 kWh/day load fraction in rural electrification. The simulated results shows that the LCOE values range from ₹3.14 to ₹5.78/kWh, remaining below the electricity tiered rate established by the state electricity board, as detailed in Sect. “System design and simulation”. Additionally, a thorough sensitivity analysis is performed on all winning architectures for each model, assessing the overall impact of increased Net Energy Demand (D in kWh/day) in the study area, as discussed in Sect. “Sensitivity analysis”. To compensate for the rise in net energy demand, adjustments are made to the Hub Height (HH in meters) of the wind turbine across four different scenarios, optimizing economic metrics such as NPC and LCOE. This work serves as a pilot study for establishing a small wind turbine-based power plant. The same methodology can be applied for larger community micro-grid or commercial setups to regions with comparable topography, while adhering to technical standards and grid codes governing voltage, frequency, reactive power, communication protocols, etc. under varying climatic and regulatory conditions. To encourage investment and support scalability of wind energy, government incentives such as feed-in tariffs, Renewable Purchase Obligations, Generation- Based Incentives, and the National Wind-Solar Hybrid Policy play a vital role in ensuring successful implementation. Hilly regions like Sikkim possess substantial wind energy potential owing to their distinctive geographical features, including mountain ridges and gaps that facilitate enhanced wind flow. However, practical challenges exist in scaling up wind farms in such terrains.

Conclusions

This study introduces a strategic and adoptable power management approach, designed with a focus on techno-economic and socio-environmental considerations, to fulfill the net energy demand of the study area. Considering the local climatic conditions, a small wind turbine hybrid system is being introduced. Integrating a small wind turbine-hybrid combination into a smart energy management system stands out as a significant solution to address frequent power interruptions and wind intermittency. The selection of small wind turbine generators (WTGs) with different ratings and makes is based on the capacity factor (Cf) value. The three empaneled small wind turbines - Excel-10, Montana-3310, and SD6-701 – are being chosen for integration with four proposed system models using Homer Pro Tool. The system architectures are being designed for both grid-connected and isolated modes, aiming to minimize electricity consumption from the grid by harnessing maximum wind energy at the site. The optimization result reveals that the integration of WTGs significantly reduces dependence on the utility grid by approximately 88.4%. The grid-connected system is emerging as efficient, reliable, and cost-effective, demonstrating minimal Net Present Cost (NPC) and Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE) indices as low as ₹ 3.28 Crore and ₹5.78/kWh, respectively, across various operational models. In contrast, the off-grid system is proving to be costlier due to the involvement of a power backup system and the frequent operation of a generator set (Gen-set). During a period of substantial capacity shortages and higher grid exchange rates, recharging the battery storage using the Gen- set in off-grid mode is leading to a surge in economic metrics. To anticipate the future performance, a detailed sensitivity analysis is being conducted on all winning architectures, adjusting the hub height (HH in meters) of the WTGs to compensate for any increase in net energy demand (D in kWh/day). The impact of net energy demand and hub height variations is being reflected in NPC and LCOE indices, demonstrating consistent economic assessment throughout the base case. In the future, it will be interesting to explore additional factors such as power curve losses, grid stability, unplanned shutdowns, turbine wake effect, turbine efficiency degradation etc. to enhance the proposed system model. Additionally, future work on sensitivity analysis may incorporate factors such as government policies, market dynamics, fuel price variations, discount rates, and battery degradation to strengthen the techno-economic feasibility evaluation of wind farm projects.

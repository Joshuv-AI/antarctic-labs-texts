1.
Introduction

Hardy’s inequality is a fundamental and extensively studied functional inequality in the theory of partial differential equations. For the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
), this inequality takes the form

(1.1)

\left|\frac{n-p}{p}\right|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x|^{p}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}dx,

which holds for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
1<p<n
and for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
when
p>n
. The constant
|\frac{n-p}{p}|^{p}
is sharp here. Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, with nonempty boundary, and let us define the distance function from the boundary of
\Omega
by

\delta_{\Omega}(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)=\inf\{|x-y|:y\in\partial\Omega\}.

The following variant of Hardy’s inequality was established by Alano Ancona in
[
5
]
for a sufficiently regular domain
\Omega
:

(1.2)

C_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{2}}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx\ \ \forall\,u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Later, this inequality was generalized by several authors for various domains, such as bounded convex domains, bounded domains with smooth or Lipschitz boundaries, upper half-spaces, and more (see
[
18
,
22
,
28
,
29
]
). Some of the corresponding sharp constants were also established there.

Analogous versions of these inequalities for fractional Sobolev spaces have also been studied for several domains. We refer
[
9
,
12
,
14
,
23
]
for introduction of the fractional Sobolev spaces, its connection with the classical spaces, and for the Hardy’s inequality for fractional Sobolev spaces. Among the fractional Hardy’s inequalities for domains, one of the well-known results was established by B. Dyda in
[
13
]
. For
p>0
and
0<s<1
, he established the following inequality:

(1.3)

C\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{sp}}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dydx\ \ \forall\,u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
\Omega
is either a bounded Lipschitz domain or its complement, a domain above the graph of a Lipschitz function
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, or the complement of a point, with certain conditions imposed on the quantity
sp
.

This inequality for the critical case
sp=1
is addressed in
[
1
]
for dimension one and in
[
3
]
for higher dimensions. Inequality (
1.3
) for the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
and the corresponding sharp constant

(1.4)

C_{n,p,s}:=2\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma((1+sp)/2)}{\Gamma((n+sp)/2)}\int_{0}^{1}\left|1-r^{(sp-1)/p}\right|^{p}\frac{dr}{(1-r)^{1+sp}}

was first established by Bogdan and Dyda in
[
7
]
for the case
p=2
under the condition
sp\neq 1
. The results for general
p
were later extended by Frank and Seiringer in
[
15
]
. L. Brasco and E. Cinti established the following variant of the fractional Hardy’s inequality for convex domains
(\neq\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
[
10
]
without any restriction on the quantity
sp
:

(1.5)

\frac{C}{s(1-s)}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{sp}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dydx\ \ \forall\,u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and
C=C(n,p)>0
. Later, Brasco et al. in
[
6
]
established the corresponding sharp constant of the above inequality for the following cases:

(1)
\Omega
is the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
, and
0<s<1
.

(2)
\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open convex set and
1/p<s<1
.

(3)
\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open convex set,
p=2
and
0<s<1
.

The constant is same for all the above cases, which is

(1.6)

\mathfrak{h}_{s,p}(\Omega)=C_{n,s,p}\Lambda_{s,p},

where

(1.7)

C_{n,s,p}=\begin{cases}|S_{n-1}|\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-2}(1+r^{2})^{-\frac{n+sp}{2}}dr,&\text{for $n\geq 2$},\\
1&\text{for $n=1$},\end{cases}

S_{n-1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

(1.8)

\Lambda_{s,p}=2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|1-t^{\frac{sp-1}{p}}\right|^{p}}{(1-t)^{1+sp}}dt+\frac{2}{sp}.

The goal of this paper is to establish a Hardy’s inequality similar to (
1.5
) and the corresponding sharp constant for half-spaces in the Heisenberg group. Before going to the main results, let us recall needed background on the Heisenberg group. The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, is defined by

\mathbb{H}^{n}:=\{\xi=(z,t)=(x,y,t):z=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \mathrm{and}\ t\in\mathbb{R}\},

with the following group law:

\xi\circ\xi^{\prime}=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+2\langle y,x^{\prime}\rangle-2\langle x,y^{\prime}\rangle),

where
\xi=(x,y,t)
,
\xi^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One can easily check that
0\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the identity element and
-\xi=(-z,-t)
is the inverse of
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. A basis for the left invariant vector fields is given by

X_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+2y_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

Y_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}-2x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

For any function
u
of
C^{1}
class,
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u):=\left(X_{1}(u),\ldots,X_{n}(u),Y_{1}(u),\ldots,Y_{n}(u)\right)
is the associated Heisenberg sub-gradient of
u
and

|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|X_{i}(u)|^{2}+|Y_{i}(u)|^{2}\right).

The homogeneous norm for
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

d(\xi)=\left((|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}=\left(|z|^{4}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

d(\xi)
is called the Koranyi-Folland non-isotropic gauge. The left-invariant Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. For any measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
|A|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
A
. The dilation on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

\delta_{r}\xi:=(rx,ry,r^{2}t),\ \ \mathrm{for}\ \xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\ \mathrm{and}\ r>0.

The determinant of the Jacobian matrix of
\delta_{r}

is
r^{Q}
, where
Q=2n+2,
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. A ball in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, centered at
\xi
with radius
r
is defined by

(1.9)

B(\xi,r)=\{\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime}):d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})<r\}.

For
0<r<R
, the following set is an infinite strip between
x_{1}=r
and
x_{1}=R
:

(1.10)

\mathscr{D}_{r,R}=\{\xi=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:r<x_{1}<R\}.

The distance function for a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
with non-empty boundary is defined by

\delta_{\Omega}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\underset{\xi^{\prime}\in\partial\Omega}{\mathrm{inf}}d(\xi^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})&\text{if $\xi\in\Omega$}\\
0&\text{if $\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\Omega$}.\end{cases}

Let us define the following half-spaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
:

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}:=\{\xi=(x,y,t)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:x_{1}>0\}

and

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}:=\{\xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:t>0\}.

Considering the point
\xi^{\prime}=(0,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y,t-2x_{1}y_{1})\in\partial\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, it is easy to see that
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}(\xi)=x_{1}
for
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, whereas the distance function
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}}(\xi)\leq\sqrt{t}
for the half-space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
. In fact, the distance of the point
\xi=(0,0,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
from the boundary of
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
is
\sqrt{t}
, but the value could be strictly less than
\sqrt{t}
for
\xi=(x,y,t)
in some subsets of
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
, see the Appendix in
[
26
]
. Moreover, there is no known specific form for the distance function
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}}(\xi)
.

Hardy’s inequality for the Heisenberg group was first established by Garofalo and Lanconelli in
[
16
]
. Numerous generalizations of this inequality have been made by various authors. The following inequality is one of the general version, proved by L. D’Ambrozio in
[
11
]
:

(1.11)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|z|^{\beta}}{d(\xi)^{\alpha}}|u(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\leq C_{n,p,\alpha,\beta}^{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)|^{p}|z|^{\beta-p}d(\xi)^{2p-\alpha},\ \forall u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
,
p>1
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
Q>\alpha-\beta
and
2n>p-\beta
and
C_{n,p,\alpha\beta}=\frac{p}{Q+\beta-\alpha}
is the optimal constant.

Inequality (
1.11
) was proved for
\Omega=\mathbb{H}^{n}
with
\alpha=2p
and
\beta=p
in
[
24
]
. Adimurthi and Sekar proved (
1.11
) with
\alpha=2p
and
\beta=2
, for
u\in FS_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
in
[
4
]
, where the space
FS_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
under the norm

(1.12)

|u|^{p}_{1,p}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)|^{p}}{|z|^{p-2}}dzdt.

For other generalizations of this inequality, we refer to the works in
[
17
,
20
,
21
,
30
]
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<s<1
,
\alpha\geq 0
, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be an open set. Let us define the following fractional seminorm analogous to (
1.12
):

(1.13)

[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and the fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega):[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}<\infty\right\}.

Here the norm is defined by
\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}=\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}
. The space
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
is defined as the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}
. When
\alpha=0
, the above notations are simplified to
[f]_{s,p,\Omega}
,
\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\Omega}
,
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
, and
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, respectively. Hardy’s inequality for the space
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
takes the form

(1.14)

C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{\delta_{\Omega}(\xi)^{sp}|z|^{\alpha}}d\xi\leq[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}.

When
\Omega=\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the distance function
\delta_{\Omega}(\xi)
is replaced by
d(\xi)
. The space
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
was first introduced in
[
2
]
. This space is trivial when
\alpha\geq Q-2
, and if
\alpha<\min\{Q-2,p(1-s)\}
, then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\subset W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
(see Section 3 in
[
2
]
). Thus, throughout this paper, we consider
\alpha<Q-2
. Inequality (
1.14
) for this space was established in
[
2
]
under the condition
sp+\alpha<Q
together with
sp>2
. Recently, the range of the indices for this inequality has been improved as a particular case of fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type inequalities in
[
27
]
.

In a previous joint work
[
26
]
, we established the fractional Hardy inequality (
1.14
) for the half-space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
under conditions
sp>1
and
\alpha\geq(Q-2+sp)/2
. In this paper, following the techniques in
[
10
]
and
[
6
]
, we establish a fractional Hardy’s inequality analogous to (
1.5
), for the half-space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, without any restriction on the product
sp
, along with the corresponding sharp constant. The inequality is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. There exists a constant
C=C(n,p)>0
depending only on
n
and
p
, such that

(1.15)

\frac{C}{s(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp}}d\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\ \ \forall\,f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}),

where
\xi=(x_{1},\cdots x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n},t)
.

The right-hand side of the above inequality is the seminorm (
1.13
) without the index
\alpha
. Since we are interested in establishing an inequality for the space
W^{s,p,\alpha}
, the following version of Hardy’s inequality precisely has the seminorm (
1.13
) on the right-hand side:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
, and
\alpha\geq 0
. There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,\alpha)>0
depending only on
n
,
p
and
\alpha
, such that

(1.16)

\frac{C}{(s+\frac{\alpha}{p})(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi\leq[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{p}.\ \ \forall\,f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}),

where
\xi=(z,t)=(x_{1},\cdots x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n},t)
.

Inequality (
1.14
) for the half-space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
with
0\leq\alpha<(Q-2+sp)/2
has not yet been established. However, observing that
x_{1}^{\alpha}\leq|z|^{\alpha}
, inequality (
1.16
) implies that (
1.14
) holds for every
\alpha\geq 0
, provided that the right-hand side has
[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}
instead of
[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}
. Although Theorem
1.2
is more general than Theorem
1.1
, (Theorem
1.1
is the special case
\alpha=0
of Theorem
1.2
), its proof is based on Theorem
1.1
.

The constants given in (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) may not be sharp. It is enough to compute the sharp constant for (
1.16
) only. The sharp constant for the inequality (
1.16
) is defined by the variational problem

(1.17)

\mathcal{C}_{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})=\underset{f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})}{\inf}\left\{[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{p}:\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi=1\right\}.

The existence of such a positive constant follows from Theorem
1.2
. In fact, we have

\frac{C(n,p,\alpha)}{(s+\frac{\alpha}{p})(1-s)}\leq\mathcal{C}_{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}).

Before defining the precise form of the sharp constant for inequality (
1.16
), let us introduce some notation. For
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and
\alpha\geq 0
, define

(1.18)

\Lambda_{s,p,\alpha}:=2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|1-\tau^{\frac{sp+\alpha-1}{p}}\right|^{p}}{(1-\tau)^{1+sp+\alpha}}d\tau+\frac{2}{sp+\alpha}.

Note that
\Lambda_{s,p,\alpha}
is the same as
\Lambda_{s^{\prime},p}
as defined in (
1.8
) with
s^{\prime}=s+\frac{\alpha}{p}<1
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
,
\theta>0
and
\alpha\geq 0
, let us define the quantity

(1.19)

\mathcal{I}(k,\theta,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{k+2+\theta}{4}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{k-2}dr}{(1+r^{2})^{\frac{k+\theta+\alpha}{2}}}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and
\alpha<\min\{Q-2,p(1-s)\}
be such that
sp+\alpha>1
. Then we have

(1.20)

\mathcal{C}_{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})=C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\Lambda_{sp},

where

(1.21)

C_{n,s,p,\alpha}=2|S_{2n-2}|\mathcal{I}(Q-2,sp,\alpha),

S_{2n-2}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}
.

In the sharp constant result, there are two restrictions on the parameters: the first is
\alpha<\min\{Q-2,p(1-s)\}
, and the second is
sp+\alpha>1
. The following integral

\int_{|z|<1}\int_{|t|<1}\frac{dzdt}{(|z|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{Q+sp}{4}}|z|^{\alpha}}

is required to be finite in the proof. This is true provided
\alpha<\min\{Q-2,p(1-s)\}
(see integral
J
in Proposition 3.1 in
[
2
]
).

Although we follow the technique used in
[
6
]
for the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
to compute the sharp constant for inequality (
1.16
), the proof for
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
differs significantly. In
[
6
]
Brasco et al. use a test function of the form
\phi(x)=\chi_{M}(x^{\prime})\eta(x_{n})
,
M>0
, to separate the integrals in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
\mathbb{R}
, and use the one-dimensional result. In our case, it is not possible to separate the term
d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}
in the denominator (even in the case
\alpha=0
) in terms of
x_{1}
and
(\tilde{x},t)
, (here
\tilde{x}=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
and
\xi=(x_{1},\tilde{x},t)
). To overcome this issue, we use a test function of the form
\phi(\xi)=\eta(x_{1})\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)
,
R_{0}>0
, along with a certain monotonicity property of
\Psi_{R_{0}}
in the variable
x_{1}
. Additionally, the condition
sp+\alpha>1
is required here. To deal with the term
|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, we also introduce some different spaces of functions that are integrable over the infinite strip
\mathscr{D}_{r},R
, which differs this work from Euclidean results.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce a weighted fractional Laplacian operator related to the seminorm and fractional Sobolev spaces considered in this paper, some associated function spaces, the notion of weakly superharmonic and subharmonic functions with respect to this operator, and several necessary results regarding the integrability of such functions. Section
3
contains some useful properties of the distance function of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. In Section
4
, we provide the proofs of the fractional Hardy’s inequalities Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
. The computation of the sharp constants of these inequalities is given in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

For
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and
\alpha\geq 0
let us define the operator

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}f(\xi):=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{J_{p}(f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime},\ \ \xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

where
J_{p}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a monotonically increasing continuous function defined by

J_{p}(t)=|t|^{p-2}t\ \ \mathrm{for}\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

It is easy to see that the weak formulation of the operator
\mathcal{L}
precisely gives the seminorm defined in (
1.13
). We refer to
[
25
]
for more details on this operator. To deal with the integrals involving
|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}
, let us introduce the following spaces:

L_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}):=\left\{f\ \mathrm{is}\ \mathrm{measurable}:\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}|f(\xi)|^{p}d\xi<\infty\ \ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{any}\ 0<r<R\right\},

and

W_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}):=\left\{f\in L_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}):[f]_{{s,p,\alpha,\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}}<\infty\ \ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{any}\ 0<r<R\right\}.

Clearly,
W^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\subset W_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\subset W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
. Let us also consider the space

L_{sp,\alpha}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n}):=\left\{f\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p-1}}{(1+d(\xi))^{Q+sp}(1+|z|)^{\alpha}}d\xi<\infty\right\}.

One can easily check that
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\subset L_{sp,\alpha}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
be any open set (
\Omega
could be
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
itself). By extending the analogous idea from the Euclidean case in
[
6
]
to the Heisenberg group, let us consider the following equation:

(2.2)

\mathcal{L}f(\xi)=\lambda\frac{J_{p}(f(\xi))}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \Omega.

Definition 1
.

(i)
A function
f\in W_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\cap L_{sp,\alpha}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is said to be a weak supersolution (or subsolution, respectively) to (
2.2
) if

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{J_{p}(f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime}))(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\geq(\leq,\ \mathrm{resp})\,\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{J_{p}(f(\xi))\phi(\xi)}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi,

for every non-negative
\phi\in W^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
with compact support in
\Omega
. A function
f
is called a weak solution to (
2.2
), if it is both a weak supersolution and a weak subsolution to (
2.2
).

(ii)
A function
f\in W_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\cap L_{sp,\alpha}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is called weakly superharmonic, weakly subharmonic, or weakly harmonic if the integral on the left-hand side of (
2.3
) is greater than or equal to zero, less than or equal to zero, or equal to zero, respectively.

The following lemma shows that, under the assumptions on
f
and the test function
\phi
, the above definition is well-posed, i.e., the function

(2.4)

(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\mapsto\frac{J_{p}(f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime}))

is integrable.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and
\alpha\geq 0
. If
f\in W_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\cap L_{sp,\alpha}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
\phi\in W^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
with compact support in
\Omega
, then we have

(2.5)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime})|}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi<\infty.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be the support of
\phi
. Since
\mathcal{O}
is a compact subset of
\Omega
(
\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
),
\exists\,r,R
, such that

r:=\inf\{x_{1}:\xi=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y,t)\in\mathcal{O}\}>0\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ R:=\sup\{x_{1}:\xi=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y,t)\in\mathcal{O}\}<\infty.

Clearly
\mathcal{O}\Subset\mathscr{D}_{\frac{r}{2},R+\frac{r}{2}}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
. For simplicity of notation, the set
\mathscr{D}_{\frac{r}{2},R+\frac{r}{2}}
is denoted by
\mathscr{D}
here. If
\xi\in\mathcal{O}
and
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}^{C}
, then we have
d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{r}{2}
.

Now, the above integral can be decomposed as follows:

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime})|}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi=\int_{\mathscr{D}}\int_{\mathscr{D}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime})|}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
+2\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{\mathscr{D}^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}|\phi(\xi)|}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi=:I_{1}+2I_{2}.

The integral
I_{1}
can be shown to be finite using Hölder’s inequality with the conjugates
\frac{p}{p-1}
and
p
, and with the assumptions on
f
and
\phi
. For the second integral, we have

I_{2}\leq C\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f(\xi)|^{p-1}|\phi(\xi)|\int_{\mathscr{D}^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi\\
+C\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\phi(\xi)|\int_{\mathscr{D}^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi=:C(I_{3}+I_{4}).

For any
\xi\in\mathcal{O}
, we have

\int_{\mathscr{D}^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\leq\int_{B(\xi,\frac{r}{2})^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}=\int_{B(0,\frac{r}{2})^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d(\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}.

The last integral was shown to be finite in
[
2
]
(see the integral
I_{3}
in Proposition 3.1.). Therefore, we get

I_{3}\leq C_{0}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f(\xi)|^{p-1}|\phi(\xi)|d\xi\leq C_{0}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}^{p-1}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}<\infty.

Observe that
\frac{1+d(\xi)}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})}\leq C_{1}
and
\frac{1+|z|}{|z-z^{\prime}|}\leq C_{2}
for any
\xi\in\mathcal{O}
and
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}^{C}
. Therefore, we have

I_{4}\leq CC_{1}^{Q+sp}C_{2}^{\alpha}\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\phi(\xi)|d\xi\right)\left(\int_{\mathscr{D}^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}d\xi^{\prime}}{(1+d(\xi))^{Q+sp}(1+|z|)^{\alpha}}\right)<\infty,

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

For the case
\alpha=0
, it is enough to consider
f\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{s,p,0}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
in Definition
1
, and in this case the space
L_{sp,0}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is denoted by

.

The next lemma states that the product of two certain functions belongs to the space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
and ensures the admissibility of some test functions in our main result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
,
\alpha\geq 0
, and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
be an open set. If
\phi\in W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with compact support in
\Omega
and
f\in W^{s,p,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
, then

f\phi\in W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega).

Proof.

We have

(2.6)

[f\phi]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}\\
\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}|f(\xi)|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi+2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}|\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
\leq 2^{p-1}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{p}[\phi]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}+2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}|\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi.

It remains to prove that the last integral is finite. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the support of
\phi
. Since
\mathcal{O}
is a compact subset of
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, similarly to the proof of Lemma
2.1
, the set
\mathscr{D}=\mathscr{D}_{\frac{r}{2},R+\frac{r}{2}}
can be defined, where
\mathcal{O}\Subset\mathscr{D}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
and
d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq|z-z^{\prime}|\geq\frac{r}{2}
whenever
\xi\in\mathcal{O}
and
\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{D}}^{C}
. Using this we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}|\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
\leq\int_{\mathscr{D}}\int_{\mathscr{D}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}|\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi+2\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{\mathscr{D}^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}|\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})}^{p}\left([f]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}^{p}+2\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{\mathscr{D}^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\right)\\
\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})}^{p}\left([f]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathscr{D}}^{p}+2^{p+1}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})}^{p}|\mathcal{O}|\int_{B(0,\frac{r}{2})^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d(\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\right)<\infty.

∎

Remark 2
.

For the case
\alpha=0
, instead of the space
W^{s,p,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
, we need to work with functions
f\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
. In this case, to show that the last integral on the right-hand side of (
2.6
) is finite, define a compact set
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{O}\Subset\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\Subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
in the following way:

(2.7)

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}:=\left\{\xi=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y,t)=(z,t):\frac{r}{2}\leq x_{1}\leq R+\frac{r}{2},\,|z|\leq R_{0}+\frac{r}{2}\ \mathrm{and}\ |t|\leq T_{0}+\frac{rR_{0}}{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\},

where

r:=\inf\{x_{1}:\xi=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y,t)\in\mathcal{O}\}>0
,
R:=\sup\{x_{1}:\xi=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y,t)\in\mathcal{O}\}
,
R_{0}=\sup\{|z|:\xi=(z,t)\in\mathcal{O}\}
and
T_{0}=\sup\{|t|:\xi=(z,t)\in\mathcal{O}\}
.

For
\xi\in\mathcal{O}
and
\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}^{C}
, we have to possible cases. If
|z-z^{\prime}|\geq\frac{r}{2}
, then we have
d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq\frac{r}{2}
. If

Thus, we always have

, whenever
\xi\in\mathcal{O}
and

. Using this, similarly, it can be shown that the last integral on the right-hand side of (

) is finite.

3.
Some properties of the distance function

This section establishes several useful results of the distance function
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}(\xi)={x_{1}}_{+}
(
{x_{1}}_{+}
denotes
\max\{x_{1},0\}
). For the Euclidean case, Brasco and Cinti in
[
10
]
have shown that the
s
-th power of the distance function for a bounded convex set is weakly superharmonic. In the Heisenberg group case, we show that the function
(\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})^{s}
is weakly harmonic, and this is the main ingredient in proving our first result, Theorem
1.1
. The parameter
\alpha
is not involved in Theorem
1.1
; therefore, in this section, we work with
\alpha=0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
. If
f\in W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\cap L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
\phi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
with compact support in
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, then for any
\epsilon>0
, the function

(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\mapsto\frac{J_{p}(f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}\phi(\xi)

is integrable on
\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}:=\{(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq\epsilon\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be the support of
\phi
. We have

\iint_{\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}}\left|\frac{J_{p}(f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}\phi(\xi)\right|d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
=\iint_{\{(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}|\phi(\xi)|d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
\leq\epsilon^{-\frac{Q+sp}{p}}\iint_{\{(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}:d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{\frac{Q+sp}{q}}}|\phi(\xi)|d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
+\iint_{\{(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{O}\times(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{O}):d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}|\phi(\xi)|d\xi^{\prime}d\xi,

where
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Applying Hölder’s inequality to the first integral on the right-hand side, we get

\iint_{\{(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathcal{O}\times\mathcal{O}:d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p-1}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{\frac{Q+sp}{q}}}|\phi(\xi)|d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
\leq\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(|\mathcal{O}|\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\phi(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

The second integral on the right-hand side can be shown to be finite similarly to the integral
I_{2}
in Lemma
2.1
.
∎

The following result for the distance function
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}
is crucial in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. It is not known whether the same result holds for the half-space
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+,t}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
. There exists a constant
C=C(n,p)>0
such that

(3.1)

\int_{\{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}:x_{1}^{\prime}\leq x_{1}\}}\frac{|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}d\xi^{\prime}\geq\frac{C}{1-s}x_{1}^{p-sp}\ \ \mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{a.e}\ \xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+},

where
\xi=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y,t)
and
\xi^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n}^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
. For any
\sigma\in(0,1)
let us define the following set

\Sigma_{\sigma}(\xi,r):=\{\xi^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n}^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in B(\xi,r):x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}>\sigma d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\}.

Clearly,
\Sigma_{\sigma}(\xi,x_{1})\subset\{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}:x_{1}^{\prime}\leq x_{1}\}
and we have

(3.2)

\int_{\{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}:x_{1}^{\prime}\leq x_{1}\}}\frac{|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}d\xi^{\prime}\geq\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}(\xi,x_{1})}\frac{|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}d\xi^{\prime}\\
\geq\sigma^{p}\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}(\xi,x_{1})}d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{p-sp-Q}d\xi^{\prime}=\sigma^{p}\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}(0,x_{1})}d(\xi^{\prime})^{p-sp-Q}d\xi^{\prime}.

The last integral can be written in polar coordinates for
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as

(3.3)

\int_{\Sigma_{\sigma}(0,x_{1})}d(\xi^{\prime})^{p-sp-Q}d\xi^{\prime}=\left(\int_{\{\omega\in S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}:-\omega_{x_{1}}>\sigma d(\omega)\}}dS_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(\omega)\right)\int_{0}^{x_{1}}r^{p-sp-1}dr=\frac{\psi(\sigma)}{p(1-s)}x_{1}^{p-sp},

where
S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(\xi)=1\}
,
\omega=(\omega_{x_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{x_{n}},\omega_{y_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{y_{n}},\omega_{t})\in S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
,
dS_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(\omega)
is the surface measure on
S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
, and the function

\psi(\sigma)=\int_{\{\omega\in S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}:-\omega_{x_{1}}>\sigma d(\omega)\}}dS_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(\omega)>0\ \ \forall\,\sigma\in(0,1).

Inequality (
3.1
) follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) with
C=\frac{1}{p}\underset{0<\sigma<1}{\mathrm{sup}}\left[\sigma^{p}\psi(\sigma)\right]
.
∎

The following one-dimensional result is used to prove a useful result (Lemma
3.4
) for the distance function
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}
. The proof can be found in
[
19
]
, (see Lemma 3.1).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Then the function

(3.4)

g_{\epsilon}^{(1)}(x):=\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)^{C}}\frac{J_{p}(x^{s}-y_{+}^{s})}{|x-y|^{1+sp}}dy\rightarrow 0

uniformly on any compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. For any fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
define the integral

g_{\epsilon}(\xi):=\int_{B(\xi,\epsilon)^{C}}\frac{J_{p}(x_{1}^{s}-{x_{1}^{\prime}}_{+}^{s})}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}d\xi^{\prime}.

Then
\underset{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\,g_{\epsilon}(\xi)=0
strongly in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
.

Proof.

The change of variable
\tilde{\xi}={\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime}
gives

g_{\epsilon}(\xi)=\int_{B(0,\epsilon)^{C}}\frac{J_{p}(x_{1}^{s}-({x_{1}+\tilde{x}_{1}})_{+}^{s})}{d(\tilde{\xi})^{Q+sp}}d\tilde{\xi}=\int_{S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{J_{p}(x_{1}^{s}-(x_{1}+r\omega_{x_{1}})_{+}^{s})}{r^{Q+sp}}r^{Q-1}drd\omega.

Here
\omega=(\omega_{x_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{x_{n}},\omega_{y_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{y_{n}},\omega_{t})\in S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
. The last integral can be written as

g_{\epsilon}(\xi)=\int_{S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\cap\,\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\omega_{x_{1}}^{1+sp}\int_{(-\epsilon,\epsilon)^{C}}\frac{J_{p}(x_{1}^{s}-(x_{1}+r\omega_{x_{1}})_{+}^{s})}{(r\omega_{x_{1}})^{1+sp}}drd\omega=\int_{S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\cap\,\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\omega_{x_{1}}^{sp}g_{\epsilon\omega_{x_{1}}}^{(1)}(x_{1})d\omega,

where the function
g_{\epsilon\omega_{x_{1}}}^{(1)}
is defined in (
3.4
). The conclusion follows from Lemma
3.3
and the fact

\int_{S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\cap\,\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\omega_{x_{1}}^{sp}d\omega<\infty.

∎

The next result shows that
(\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})^{s}
, the
s
-th power of the distance function, is weakly harmonic.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and
0<s<1
. We have
(\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})^{s}\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{s,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\cap L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and

(3.5)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{J_{p}(\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}(\xi)^{s}-\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}(\xi^{\prime})^{s})}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime}))d\xi^{\prime}d\xi=0,

for every non-negative
\phi\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
with compact support in
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
.

Proof.

We have

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}(\xi)^{s}|^{p-1}}{(1+d(\xi))^{Q+sp}}d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{{x_{1}}_{+}^{s(p-1)}}{(1+d(\xi))^{Q+sp}}d\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{d(\xi)^{s(p-1)}}{(1+d(\xi))^{Q+sp}}d\xi

Using the polar coordinate for
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}(\xi)^{s}|^{p-1}}{(1+d(\xi))^{Q+sp}}d\xi\leq|S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{Q-1+s(p-1)}}{(1+r)^{Q+sp}}dr\leq|S_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{(1+r)^{1+s}}<\infty.

To show
(\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})^{s}\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{s,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
, let us consider any compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
. We have

(3.6)

[(\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}})^{s}]^{p}_{s,p,K}=\int_{K}\int_{K}\frac{|x_{1}^{s}-{x_{1}^{\prime}}^{s}|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi.

By Mean value theorem in
\mathbb{R}
,
\exists\,\tilde{x}_{1}\in(x_{1},x_{1}^{\prime})
when
x_{1}<x_{1}^{\prime}
, or
\tilde{x}_{1}\in(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{1})
when
x_{1}^{\prime}<x_{1}
such that

(3.7)

The function

4.

Before proving the main results, let us mention some fundamental inequalities which we are going to use. The proofs are given in

Lemma 4.1

.

For any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, let us define the following function in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
:

u_{\beta}(\xi):=\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}(\xi)^{\beta}={x_{1}}_{+}^{\beta}.

Since the function
u_{\beta}
does not belong to the space
W^{s,p,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
, we work with the cut-off function defined below. For any
R_{0}>0
, we define the following sets:

D_{R_{0}}:=\{\xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:|x_{i}|,|y_{i}|\leq R_{0},|t|\leq R_{0}^{2}\},

and

D_{R_{0},t}:=\{\xi=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:|x_{i}|,|y_{i}|\leq R_{0},|t|\leq R_{0}^{2}+R_{0}\},

and define the function

u_{\beta,R_{0}}=u_{\beta}\psi_{R_{0}},

where
\psi_{R_{0}}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D_{R_{0}+1,t})
be a function satisfies
0\leq\psi_{R_{0}}\leq 1
,
\psi\equiv 1
in
D_{R_{0}}
,
|\frac{\partial\psi_{R_{0}}}{\partial x_{i}}|,|\frac{\partial\psi_{R_{0}}}{\partial y_{i}}|\leq C_{0}
, and
|\frac{\partial\psi_{R_{0}}}{\partial t}|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{R_{0}+1}
for some constant
C_{0}>0
, independent of
R_{0}
. The following lemma is useful to show that the function
u_{\beta,R_{0}}\in W^{s,p,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
R_{0}>0
and
0<r<R
. Then there exists a constant
C(r,R)>0
, independent of
R_{0}
, such that

|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\,u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)|\leq C(r,R),

for all
\xi\in\mathscr{D}_{r,R}\cap D_{R_{0}+1,t}
. Moreover,
|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\,u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)|
is zero outside
D_{R_{0}+1,t}
.

Proof.

Since the support of
u_{\beta,R_{0}}
is
D_{R_{0}+1,t}
,
|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\,u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)|=0
outside
D_{R_{0}+1,t}
. Let
\xi\in\mathscr{D}_{r,R}
, we have

|X_{1}u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)|=\left|\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+2y_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)(u_{\beta}\psi_{R_{0}})\right|=\left|\beta x_{1}^{\beta-1}\psi_{R_{0}}+u_{\beta}\frac{\partial\psi_{R_{0}}}{\partial x_{1}}+2y_{1}u_{\beta}\frac{\partial\psi_{R_{0}}}{\partial t}\right|\\
\leq|\beta|\max\{r^{\beta-1},R^{\beta-1}\}+\max\{r^{\beta},R^{\beta}\}\left(C_{0}+2(R_{0}+1)\frac{C_{0}}{R_{0}+1}\right)\\
=|\beta|\max\{r^{\beta-1},R^{\beta-1}\}+3C_{0}\max\{r^{\beta},R^{\beta}\},

and for
2\leq i\leq n
, and
1\leq j\leq n
,

|X_{i}u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)|,|Y_{j}u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)|\leq 3C_{0}\max\{r^{\beta},R^{\beta}\}.

These conclude the proof.
∎

Before proving
u_{\beta,R_{0}}\in W^{s,p,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
, let us recall the following useful result about the structure of the Heisenberg group. The proof is given in
[
8
, pp. 727]
for any general Homogeneous Carnot Group.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
g
denote the Lie algebra of vector fields of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and let
\mathrm{Exp}:g\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the usual exponential map. Then
\exists

M\in\mathbb{N}
, depends only on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, such that for any
h\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,

(5.9)

h=h_{1}\circ\ldots\circ h_{M},

(5.10)

d(h_{j})\leq c_{0}d(h),\ \ \mathrm{for}\ j=1,\ldots,M,

where
c_{0}>0
is a constant independent of
h
and
h_{j}
,
h_{j}=\mathrm{Exp}(t_{j}X_{i_{j}})=t_{j}e_{i_{j}}
, for some
t_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i_{j}\in(1,\ldots,2n)
,
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n+1}
is the standard basis on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, and
d(h_{j})=|t_{j}|
. Here
X_{i}=Y_{i-n}
for
i=n+1,\ldots,2n
.

Remark 3
.

Let
h=(h_{x_{1}},\ldots,h_{y_{n}},h_{t})\in\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:x_{1}>0\}
and
h_{j}=(h_{j,x_{1}},\ldots,h_{j,y_{n}},h_{j,t})
. If
h_{j}=t_{j}e_{i_{j}}
, with
e_{i_{j}}\neq e_{1}
, we must have
h_{j,x_{1}}=0
. Now, let
h_{j_{0}}=t_{j_{0}}e_{1}
for some
j_{0}
. Comparing the first components of both sides of (
5.9
), we get
h_{j_{0},x_{1}}>0
. Thus, we have
h_{j}\in\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:x_{1}\geq 0\}
in (
5.9
), for all
j=1,\ldots,M
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and
0\leq\alpha<\min\{Q-2,p(1-s)\}
. For any
R_{0}>0
and for
\beta>-\frac{1}{p-1}

we have
u_{\beta,R_{0}}\in W^{s,p,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\cap L_{sp,\alpha}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

Proof.

First, we show that
u_{\beta,R_{0}}\in W_{\mathrm{loc}(x_{1})}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
. Let
0<r<R
and
\mathscr{D}_{r,R}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
be the set defined in (
1.10
). We have

(5.11)

[u_{\beta,R_{0}}]^{p}_{s,p,\alpha,\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}=\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}\frac{|u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
\leq\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}\cap B(\xi,1)}\frac{|u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
+2^{p-1}\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}\cap B(\xi,1)^{C}}\frac{|u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)|^{p}+|u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi=:I_{1}+2^{p-1}I_{2}.

The last integral can be written as

I_{2}=2\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}|u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)|^{p}\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}\cap B(\xi,1)^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi.

This integral was shown to be finite in
[
2
]
(see
I_{1}
in Proposition 3.1). It remains to prove

. By symmetry,

can be written as

I_{1}=2\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}\int_{\{\xi^{\prime}\in\,\mathscr{D}_{r,R}\cap B(\xi,1):x_{1}<x_{1}^{\prime}\}}\frac{|u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi.

Changing the variable
\xi^{\prime}
by
\xi\circ\tilde{\xi}
, we have

(5.12)

I_{1}=2\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}\int_{\{\tilde{\xi}\in\,(\mathscr{D}_{r,R}-\xi)\cap B(0,1):\tilde{x}_{1}>0\}}\frac{|u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi\circ\tilde{\xi})|^{p}}{d(\tilde{\xi})^{Q+sp}|\tilde{z}|^{\alpha}}d\tilde{\xi}d\xi,

where
\mathscr{D}_{r,R}-\xi:=\{\xi^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime}:\xi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}_{r,R}\}
. By Theorem
5.2
and Remark
3
, we have

\tilde{\xi}=h_{1}\circ\ldots\circ h_{M},

h_{j}\in\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:x_{1}\geq 0\},\ \ d(h_{j})\leq c_{0}d(\tilde{\xi)},\ \ j=1,\ldots,M,

where
h_{j}=\mathrm{Exp}(t_{j}X_{i_{j}})
, for some
t_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
d(h_{j})=|t_{j}|
. Proceeding similarly to
I_{2}
in Proposition 3.1 in
[
2
]
, we get

|u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi\circ\tilde{\xi})|^{p}\leq c_{0}d(\tilde{\xi})\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{\xi}u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi\circ h_{1}\circ\ldots\circ h_{j-1}\circ\mathrm{Exp}(t_{j}rX_{i_{j}})\right|^{p}dr

Substituting this in (
5.12
), we obtain

I_{1}\leq 2c_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathscr{D}_{r,R}}\left|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{\xi}u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi\circ h_{1}\circ\ldots\circ h_{j-1}\circ\mathrm{Exp}(t_{j}rX_{i_{j}})\right|^{p}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{d\tilde{\xi}}{d(\tilde{\xi})^{Q+sp-p}|\tilde{z}|^{\alpha}}d\xi dr

The integral
\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{d\tilde{\xi}}{d(\tilde{\xi})^{Q+sp-p}|\tilde{z}|^{\alpha}}<\infty
is shown in
[
2
]
provided
\alpha<\min\{Q-2,p(1-s)\}
(see integral
J
in Proposition 3.1 in
[
2
]
), and
I_{1}<\infty
follows from Lemma
5.2

\xi\circ h_{1}\circ\ldots\circ h_{j-1}\circ\mathrm{Exp}(t_{j}rX_{i_{j}})\\
\in\mathscr{D}_{r,R}
for all
j=1,\ldots,M
.

Since
u_{\beta,R_{0}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
for all
\beta\geq 0
, it is enough to show
u_{\beta,R_{0}}\in L_{sp,\alpha}^{p-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
only for
-\frac{1}{p-1}<\beta<0
. For such
\beta
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|u_{\beta,R_{0}}|^{p-1}}{(1+d(\xi))^{Q+sp}(1+|z|)^{\alpha}}d\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{1}^{\beta(p-1)}}{(1+(|z|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}})^{Q+sp}(1+|z|)^{\alpha}}dzdt\\
\leq\int_{\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}:x_{1}>1\}}\frac{dzdt}{(1+(|z|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}})^{Q+sp}(1+|z|)^{\alpha}}\\
+\int_{R^{2n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x_{1}^{\beta(p-1)}}{(1+((x_{1}^{2}+|\tilde{x}|^{2})^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}})^{Q+sp}(1+(x_{1}^{2}+|\tilde{x}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\alpha}}dx_{1}d\tilde{x}dt=J_{1}+J_{2}.

We clearly have

J_{1}\leq\int_{B(0,1)^{C}}\frac{dzdt}{(|z|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{Q+sp}{4}}|z|^{\alpha}},

which is same as the integral
I_{3}
in Proposition 3.1 in
[
2
]
. The integral
J_{2}
can be estimated as follows:

(5.13)

J_{2}\leq\int_{R^{2n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\tilde{x}dt}{(1+(|\tilde{x}|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}})^{Q+sp}(1+|\tilde{x}|)^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}x_{1}^{\beta(p-1)}dx_{1}\\
\leq\int_{R^{2n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\tilde{x}dt}{(1+|\tilde{x}|^{4}+t^{2})^{\frac{Q+sp}{4}}(1+|\tilde{x}|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}x_{1}^{\beta(p-1)}dx_{1}.

The integral
\int_{0}^{1}x_{1}^{\beta(p-1)}dx_{1}
is finite due to the choice of
\beta
. The remainder of the integral on the right-hand side of (

) can be transformed into the integral (

) of Lemma
5.1
by applying the inequality
1+|\tilde{x}|^{4}\geq((1/\sqrt{2})+|\tilde{x}/(2^{\frac{1}{4}})|^{2})^{2}
and performing a change of variables in
\tilde{x}
, respectively.
∎

For
0<\epsilon\ll 1
let us define the following set in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
:

(5.14)

\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)=\left\{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\min\left\{\frac{x_{1}}{1+\epsilon},(1+\epsilon)x_{1}\right\}\leq x_{1}^{\prime}\leq\max\left\{\frac{x_{1}}{1+\epsilon},(1+\epsilon)x_{1}\right\}\right\}.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
,
0\leq\alpha<\min\{Q-2,p(1-s)\}
, and
R_{0}>0
. If

-\frac{1}{p-1}<\beta<\frac{sp+\alpha}{p-1},

then the function
u_{\beta,R_{0}}
is a weak supersolution of (
2.2
) for
D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, with
\lambda=C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\lambda(\beta,s^{\prime},p)
, where
s^{\prime}=s+\frac{\alpha}{p}<1
.

Proof.

Let
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. For any
\xi\in D_{R_{0}}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus(\xi^{-1}\circ\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi))}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi\circ\xi^{\prime}))}{d(\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}

Note that the set

\xi^{-1}\circ\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)=\left\{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\min\left\{\frac{-\epsilon x_{1}}{1+\epsilon},\epsilon x_{1}\right\}\leq x_{1}^{\prime}\leq\max\left\{\frac{-\epsilon x_{1}}{1+\epsilon},\epsilon x_{1}\right\}\right\},

and when
\xi\in D_{R_{0}}
is such that
x_{1}<0
, then
u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi\circ\xi^{\prime})=0
for all
\xi^{\prime}\in\xi^{-1}\circ\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)
. We also have

u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq u_{\beta}(\xi)-u_{\beta}(\xi\circ\xi^{\prime}),\ \ \forall\xi\in D_{R_{0}}.

Therefore, we can write

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\\
\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(x_{1})}J_{p}(({x_{1}})_{+}^{\beta}-({x_{1}+x_{1}^{\prime}})_{+}^{\beta})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\tilde{x}^{\prime}dt^{\prime}}{((x_{1}^{\prime 2}+|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|^{2})^{2}+t^{\prime 2})^{\frac{Q+sp}{4}}(x_{1}^{\prime 2}+|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\right)dx_{1}^{\prime},

where
\tilde{x}^{\prime}=(x_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n}^{\prime},y_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,y_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}
, and for
x_{1}>0
,
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(x_{1})
is the interval
\left(\frac{-\epsilon x_{1}}{1+\epsilon},\epsilon x_{1}\right)
. Using Lemma
5.1
, we get

(5.15)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\geq C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(x_{1})}\frac{J_{p}(({x_{1}})_{+}^{\beta}-({x_{1}+x_{1}^{\prime}})_{+}^{\beta})}{x_{1}^{\prime 1+sp+\alpha}}dx_{1}^{\prime}\\
=C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}(x_{1})}\frac{J_{p}(({x_{1}})_{+}^{\beta}-({x_{1}^{\prime}})_{+}^{\beta})}{|x_{1}-x_{1}^{\prime}|^{1+sp+\alpha}}dx_{1}^{\prime}.

The constant
C_{n,s,p,\alpha}
and the set
\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}(x_{1})\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
are defined in (
1.21
) and (
5.1
), respectively. Applying (
5.6
) in (
5.15
) with
s^{\prime}=s+\frac{\alpha}{p}
, we obtain

2\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\geq C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\lambda_{\epsilon}(\beta,s^{\prime},p)\frac{u_{\beta}(\xi)^{p-1}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}.

Let
0\leq\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
. Multiply the above equation by
\phi(\xi)
and then integrate over
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, we get

(5.16)

2\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\right)\phi(\xi)d\xi\geq C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\lambda_{\epsilon}(\beta,s^{\prime},p)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{u_{\beta}(\xi)^{p-1}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}\phi(\xi)d\xi.

On the other hand, applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem (thanks to Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
5.3
) we can write

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\iint_{(\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n})\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi,

where

\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}:=\{(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)\}.

In a similar way to the proof of Lemma
3.1

\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}\phi(\xi)\in L^{1}((\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n})\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon})\ \ \mathrm{for}\ 0<\epsilon\ll 1.

Therefore, using Fubini’s theorem, we can rewrite the above integral of the following form:

(5.17)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
=2\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}(\xi)}\frac{J_{p}(u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi)-u_{\beta,R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime}))}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}\right)\phi(\xi)d\xi.

Combining (
5.16
) and (
5.17
), together with (
5.4
), we get the conclusion.
∎

Since we work with a cutoff function, let us define the following sharp constant for domains. For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
, let us define the following quantity:

(5.18)

\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)=\underset{f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\inf}\left\{[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{p}:\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi=1\right\}.

Remark 4
.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}_{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\leq\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
,
\alpha\geq 0
, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
be an open set. If there exists
\lambda\geq 0
such that equation (
2.2
) admits a positive weak supersolution
f
, then
\lambda\leq\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and for
\epsilon>0
define the function

\phi=\frac{|\eta|^{p}}{(f+\epsilon)^{p-1}}.

Using Lemma
2.2
and a similar approach to (
4.4
), one can observe that
\phi
is an admissible test function. Since
f
is a positive weak supersolution to (
2.2
), we have

\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{f^{p-1}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}\frac{|\eta|^{p}}{(f+\epsilon)^{p-1}}d\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{J_{p}(f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime}))\left(\frac{|\eta|^{p}}{(f+\epsilon)^{p-1}}(\xi)-\frac{|\eta|^{p}}{(f+\epsilon)^{p-1}}(\xi^{\prime})\right)}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi.

Using the elementary inequality
|a-\tau|^{p}\geq(1-\tau)^{p-1}(|a|^{p}-\tau)
for
p\geq 1
,
0\leq\tau\leq 1
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
(the proof can be found in
[
14
, Lemma 2.6]
), we obtain

\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{f^{p-1}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}\frac{|\eta|^{p}}{(f+\epsilon)^{p-1}}d\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{\Big||\eta(\xi)|-|\eta(\xi^{\prime})|\Big|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\leq[|\eta|]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}\leq[\eta]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}.

Here we consider
a=\frac{|\eta(\xi)|}{|\eta(\xi)|}\frac{f(\xi^{\prime})+\epsilon}{f(\xi)+\epsilon}
; by symmetry we can assume
f(\xi^{\prime})\leq f(\xi)
and we consider
\tau=\frac{f(\xi^{\prime})}{f(\xi)}
. Finally, we apply Fatou’s lemma and pass the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.
The arbitrariness of the function
\eta
concludes the proof.
∎

Now we prove the sharp constant result Theorem

for
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
s^{\prime}=s+\frac{\alpha}{p}
. Lemma
5.4
together with Theorem
5.3
gives

\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\geq C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\lambda(\beta,s^{\prime},p),

for every
R_{0}>0
and
-\frac{1}{p-1}<\beta<\frac{sp+\alpha}{p-1}
. Moreover, by Proposition
5.1
,
\lambda(\beta,s^{\prime},p)
is maximal at
\beta=\frac{s^{\prime}p-1}{p}
, and the maximum value is
\Lambda_{s,p,\alpha}
. Therefore, we get

(5.19)

\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\geq C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\Lambda_{s,p,\alpha}.

For the reverse inequality, let us choose a test function in such a way that it can be reduced to the Euclidean one-dimensional case by applying Lemma
5.1
.

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
. Choose
R_{0}>0
such that
\mathrm{supp}\,\phi\subset(0,R_{0})
. Let
\Psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-1,2)\times(-1,1)^{2n})
be such that
0\leq\Psi\leq 1
,
\Psi
is increasing in
(-1,0)
and decreasing in (1,2) with respect to
x_{1}
variable,
\Psi(x_{1},\tilde{x},t)=\Psi(\tilde{x},t)
for
0\leq x_{1}\leq 1
, where
\tilde{x}=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi=(x_{1},\tilde{x},t)
. We define the test function

\phi(\xi)=\eta(x_{1})\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi),

where
\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)=\frac{R_{0}^{-\frac{2n+1}{p}}}{\lVert\Psi(0,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}}\Psi(\delta_{\frac{1}{R_{0}}}\xi)
. It is easy to see that
\phi
has compact support in
D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}
and
\lVert\Psi_{R_{0}}(0,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}=1
. From the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})
, we get

\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\leq\frac{[\eta\Psi_{R_{0}}]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{p}}{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}}\frac{|\eta\Psi_{R_{0}}|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}d\xi}=\frac{[\eta\Psi_{R_{0}}]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{p}}{\int_{0}^{R_{0}}\frac{|\eta(x_{1})|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}dx_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|\Psi_{R_{0}}(0,\tilde{x},t)|^{p}d\tilde{x}dt}=\frac{[\eta\Psi_{R_{0}}]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{p}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\eta(x_{1})|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}dx_{1}}.

Using Minkowski’s inequality, we split the integral

[\eta\Psi_{R_{0}}]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}\\
=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\eta(x_{1})\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)-\eta(x_{1}^{\prime})\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)+\eta(x_{1}^{\prime})\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)-\eta(x_{1}^{\prime})\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)|^{p}|\eta(x_{1})-\eta(x_{1}^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
+\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\eta(x_{1}^{\prime})|^{p}|\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)-\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=:I_{1}^{\frac{1}{p}}+I_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Applying Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
5.1
, we can estimate the integral
I_{1}
as follows:

I_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)|^{p}|\eta(x_{1})-\eta(x_{1}+x_{1}^{\prime})|^{p}}{d(\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi\\
\leq C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|\Psi_{R_{0}}(0,\tilde{x},t)|^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\eta(x_{1})-\eta(x_{1}+x_{1}^{\prime})|^{p}}{|x_{1}^{\prime}|^{1+sp+\alpha}}dx_{1}^{\prime}dx_{1}=C_{n,s,p,\alpha}[\eta]_{W^{s^{\prime},p}(R)}^{p}.

Here we use the monotonic property of
\Psi
. Now we estimate the integral
I_{2}
as follows:

I_{2}\leq\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi)-\Psi_{R_{0}}(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+sp}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi.

Applying the changes of variable
\xi=\delta_{R_{0}}\bar{\xi}
and
\xi^{\prime}=\delta_{R_{0}}\bar{\xi}^{\prime}
, we obtain

I_{2}\leq\lVert\eta\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{p}\frac{R_{0}^{-(2n+1)}}{\lVert\Psi(0,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{p}}\frac{R_{0}^{2Q}}{R_{0}^{Q+sp+\alpha}}[\Psi]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathbb{H}^{n}}^{p}=CR_{0}^{1-sp-\alpha}.

Here the constant
C
is independent to
R_{0}
. Therefore, we get

\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{C_{n,s,p,\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}[\eta]_{W^{s^{\prime},p}(R)}}{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\eta(x_{1})|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{C^{\frac{1}{p}}R_{0}^{\frac{1-sp-\alpha}{p}}}{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\eta(x_{1})|^{p}}{x_{1}^{sp+\alpha}}dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Since
sp+\alpha>1
and
\eta
is arbitrary, using the one-dimensional Euclidean result (
1.6
), we obtain

(5.20)

\lim_{R_{0}\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})\leq C_{n,s,p,\alpha}\Lambda_{s,p,\alpha}.

The final conclusion follows from (
5.19
) and (
5.20
), and from the following fact:

\lim_{R_{0}\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{C}_{n,s,p,\alpha}(D_{R_{0}}\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+})=\mathcal{C}_{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}_{+}).

∎

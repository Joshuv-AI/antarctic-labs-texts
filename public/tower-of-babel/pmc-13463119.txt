Pd(ii) unsymmetrical salophen complex immobilized on modified γ-Fe2O3 magnetic nanoparticles: a novel and magnetically recyclable nanocatalyst for selective oxidation of thiols and sulfides
Authors: Mehdi Hatefi Ardakani, Masoud Rezaeinasab, Atena Naeimi, Elham Kazemi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, an unsymmetrical salophen-type palladium(ii) complex, Pd(salophen-OH), where salophen-OH = 4-[((E)-{(2-[(E)-2-hydroxybenzylidene)amino]phenyl}imino)methyl]benzene-1,3-diol, was synthesized and immobilized on chloro-functionalized γ-Fe2O3 magnetic nanoparticles. Characterization of the γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocomposite via ICP, FT-IR, XRD, FE-SEM/EDX, TEM, and VSM analyses verified the successful attachment of the palladium complex, the formation of a core–shell architecture, and the material's magnetic behavior. The γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanoparticles were employed as an efficient heterogeneous catalyst for the selective oxidation of sulfides to sulfoxides using 30% H2O2 as a green oxidant, and for the oxidative coupling of thiols to disulfides with tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP) under ambient conditions. Both transformations proceeded smoothly, affording the desired sulfoxides and disulfides in good to excellent yields with high selectivity. The γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst was easily recovered using an external magnet and reused for six consecutive cycles without significant loss of catalytic activity. Moreover, FT-IR and XRD tests on the recycled γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) showed that the catalyst kept its structural stability even after multiple cycles. This highlights its durability and potential for reuse in oxidation reactions.

Introduction

Sulfoxides are synthetic intermediates to produce several pharmaceutical products, agrochemicals, fine chemicals, and biologically active molecules such as antifungal, antibacterial, antiulcer, antihypertensive, and cardiotonic agents.1 Oxidation of sulfides catalyzed by transition metal complexes using various oxidants is an important method for the successful production of sulfoxides.2 Unfortunately, conventional methods for sulfide oxidation often require high reaction temperature, long reaction times, hazardous reagents, and complicated reaction processes, resulting in overoxidation of sulfoxides to unexpected sulfones or other unwanted by-products.3–5 To overcome these limitations and consider green chemistry, several studies have been directed towards developing efficient catalytic systems for this transformation.6 Similarly, thiol to disulfide conversion is another transformation of significant importance in medicinal chemistry, materials science, and biochemistry.7–9 Disulfides serve as valuable intermediates in numerous applications, such as sulfonylation of enolates, the synthesis of organosulfur compounds through C–S bond formation, and vulcanization processes in rubber industries.10–12 Although several reagents and catalytic systems have been reported for the conversion of thiols to disulfides,13–15 many of them still face challenges such as low yields, long reaction times, expensive reagents, or the use of halogen-based oxidants under non-green conditions. Consequently, the design of stable, safe, and cost-effective oxidizing systems for the oxidation of thiols to disulfides remains an essential goal.

Schiff base ligands have become a cornerstone in coordination and organometallic chemistry because of their tunable electronic and structural features.16–18 These ligands have many advantages, including their ability to adopt different structures, stability at diverse temperature levels, simple synthesis, and ease of coordination with metal centers.19–22 Particularly, unsymmetrical Schiff base ligands have gained special attention owing to their distinctive optical, electrochemical, and magnetic properties.23,24 The bond formation between these ligands and central metal ions in natural systems makes them promising candidates for catalytic applications.25

Homogeneous catalysts, despite their excellent activity, often suffer from poor recyclability and product contamination due to complicated separation steps.26 In contrast, heterogeneous catalysis offers clear advantages in terms of catalyst recovery and reusability.27–29 On the other hand, recovering heterogeneous catalysis from final reactions needs centrifugation or filtration, which are complicated processes.30 In this regard, magnetic nanomaterials showed significant effects on catalysis and many other areas. Magnetic nanoparticles demonstrate efficiency, ease of access, and large surface area, which results in a higher catalysis loading capacity and stable heterogeneous supports for catalysts.31 Recently, numerous magnetic catalytic materials have been developed for a broad range of catalytic reactions, including oxidations, reductions, photocatalysis, and C–C bond formations.32–36 It is possible to separate magnetic nanoparticles from the reaction using external permanent magnets and eliminating the need for centrifugation and filtration. In addition, they can develop using cheap and readily available materials.37 Among the available magnetic nanoparticles, maghemite (γ-Fe2O3) nanoparticles are highly interesting given the outstanding features like high magnetic sensitivity, non-toxicity, simple and inexpensive production, and high stability.38 Extensive research has demonstrated the utility of γ-Fe2O3 magnetic nanoparticles in catalysis, sensor technologies, drug delivery, and biomedical imaging.39–45

In this work, an unsymmetrical salophen-type palladium(ii) complex, Pd(salophen-OH), was synthesized and successfully immobilized onto chloro-functionalized γ-Fe2O3 magnetic nanoparticles. The resulting nanocomposite, γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH), was comprehensively characterized using ICP, FT-IR, XRD, SEM coupled with EDX, TEM, and VSM techniques. The γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) material was then applied as an efficient heterogeneous catalyst for the selective oxidation of sulfides to sulfoxides and thiols to disulfides at room temperature. In addition, the catalyst exhibited excellent stability and reusability, highlighting its potential for sustainable applications in catalytic oxidation reactions.

Experimental

Materials and methods

Commercial-grade reagents and solvents were acquired from Fluka and Merck. The synthesis of the unsymmetrical tetradentate Schiff-base ligand (H2salophen-OH) and its palladium complex (Pd(salophen-OH)) was carried out in our lab following established protocols with minor adjustments, achieving satisfactory yields.46 Detailed synthetic protocols, as well as elemental analysis and spectroscopic characterization data for both the ligand and the Pd(salophen-OH) complex, are provided in the SI.

A Thermo Scientific Nicolet iS10 spectrophotometer was employed to acquire FT-IR spectra via the KBr pellet method. Inductively coupled plasma (ICP) spectroscopy, using a SpectroCiros CCD analytical unit, was employed to precisely determine the palladium content in the catalyst. Additionally, X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were recorded at room temperature using a PANalytical X'Pert Pro diffractometer with Cu-Kα radiation. The morphological properties of the resulting samples were investigated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Zeiss Sigma VP-500). TEM images were recorded using a Zeiss EM10C instrument. Magnetic characteristics were assessed using an LBKFB vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM). The evolution of catalytic transformations was monitored by TLC using Polygram SILG/UV 254 silica gel plates.

Synthesis of γ-Fe2O3 magnetic nanoparticles

Following a slightly adjusted co-precipitation procedure from the literature, γ-Fe2O3 magnetic nanoparticles were prepared.47 Specifically, FeCl2·4H2O (1.99 g) and FeCl3·6H2O (3.25 g) were dissolved in 30 mL of deionized water under a steady nitrogen flow at ambient temperature. Subsequently, 200 mL of 0.6 M NH4OH solution was added dropwise under vigorous stirring until the pH reached 11, resulting in the immediate formation of a black precipitate. Then, the reaction mixture was stirred for 1 h at ambient temperature and further refluxed for an additional hour, during which the suspension gradually turned brown. After magnetic separation, the precipitated nanoparticles were washed extensively with deionized water to eliminate residual ions and rinsed with ethanol. Subsequently, the solid was calcined at 250 °C for 3 hours, yielding reddish-brown γ-Fe2O3 magnetic nanoparticles.

Synthesis of chloro-functionalized γ-Fe2O3 nanoparticles

A uniform suspension was prepared by dispersing 1.8 g of γ-Fe2O3 nanoparticles in 50 mL dry toluene and sonicating for 30 min. After introducing 2 mL of 3-chloropropyltrimethoxysilane (3-CPTMS), the mixture was stirred mechanically and refluxed under a nitrogen atmosphere for 48 h to achieve surface functionalization. Upon completion, the chloro-functionalized γ-Fe2O3 nanoparticles were magnetically recovered and washed successively with deionized water and ethanol to remove unreacted silane species. Subsequently, vacuum drying of the product yielded the desired chloro-functionalized γ-Fe2O3 nanoparticles.48

Preparation of γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst

A homogeneous suspension was prepared by dispersing 2.0 g of chloro-functionalized γ-Fe2O3 nanoparticles in 50 mL methanol, followed by 30 minutes of sonication. Afterwards, Pd(salophen-OH) complex (0.5 g) was added and the mixture was continuously stirred and refluxed for 24 hours. After cooling the mix to room temperature, the solid product was magnetically separated and washed three times with methanol to remove any unanchored complex molecules. Finally, the resulting product was dried at 80 °C for 8 h to afford γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst.

General method for converting sulfides to sulfoxides with 30% H2O2 catalyzed by γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH)

In a typical experimental setup, the substrate (1 mmol) was suspended in acetonitrile (2 mL) within a 10 mL round-bottom flask. Subsequently, upon adding 12 mg of γ-Fe2O3Pd(salophen-OH) catalyst and 2 mmol of 30% H2O2 to the mixture, the system was subjected to magnetic stirring at ambient temperature (25 °C). The reaction progression was followed at set time intervals using TLC. To facilitate reuse, the catalyst was separated using a permanent magnet after the reaction. It underwent thorough washing with diethyl ether followed by drying at 80 °C for 6 h. Eventually, the desired oxidized product was extracted with dichloromethane (10 mL, three times), and refined via column chromatography on silica gel.

General method for converting thiols to disulfides with tert-BuOOH catalyzed via γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH)

In a typical procedure, thiol (1 mmol) was dissolved in ethanol (2 mL) in a suitable reaction vessel. To this solution, tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP, 70% aqueous solution, 3 mmol) and γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) catalyst (12 mg) were added. Magnetic stirring of the reaction mixture was carried out at ambient temperature (25 °C), with periodic checks using TLC. After completion, the catalyst was retrieved with a magnet, thoroughly washed with diethyl ether, and dried at 80 °C for 6 hours. Finally, the oxidized product was extracted with dichloromethane (3 × 10 mL), and purified by column chromatography on silica gel.

TEM analysis revealed core–shell structure of γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH), with dark cores corresponding to magnetic γ-Fe2O3 and lighter shells representing the organic functional layers (Fig. 4). Also, the average size of the synthesized γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst was about 30 nm.

TEM images of γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst.

Room-temperature VSM analysis was performed to characterize the magnetic features of the synthesized γ-Fe2O3 MNPs and the resulting γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst. Based on the results (Fig. 5), the saturation magnetizations for γ-Fe2O3, and γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) are 67.8 and 53.9 emu g−1, respectively. The decrease in the γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) magnetization regarding γ-Fe2O3 is explained by magnetic core coating with non-magnetic species (viz. palladium Schiff base complex and aminopropyl linker). Despite the declining magnetic property compared to γ-Fe2O3, the catalyst is able to be isolated fast from the reaction mixture by an external magnet.

Sulfides to sulfoxides oxidation catalyzed by γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH)

The influence of different solvents on the oxidation of dibenzyl sulfide was investigated to find the optimal conditions. According to Table 1 (entries 1–6), acetonitrile was identified as the most effective solvent, resulting in the highest sulfoxide production. Subsequently, the catalyst amount was optimized, revealing that 12 mg of γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) (0.25 mol% of Pd) offered the best performance (Table 1, entries 3 and 7–10). It should be noted that in the absence of the catalyst, the yield of dibenzyl sulfoxide was only 5% (Table 1, entry 7). Also, when γ-Fe2O3 and γ-Fe2O3@salophen-OH (without Pd) were used as catalyst, the sulfoxide yield was very low (Table 1, entries 11 and 12). Additionally, the study examined several oxidants like 30% H2O2, urea/H2O2 (UHP), and tert-BuOOH (TBHP, 70% aq.) for dibenzyl sulfide oxidation. According to Table 1 (entries 3 and 15–17), 30% H2O2 demonstrated superior performance, resulting in the highest sulfoxide yield. Various molar ratios of H2O2/sulfide were also investigated and it was found that the best molar ratio of H2O2 to the substrate is 2 : 1 (Table 1, entries 3, 13 and 14).

To investigate the reaction mechanism, oxidation of dibenzyl sulfide was carried out in the presence of Ph2NH as an efficient radical scavenger. It was observed that the addition of Ph2NH as a radical scavenger has no significant effect on the yield of the oxidation product and reaction time which confirms the reaction mechanism has not radical nature. Therefore, based on the literature,51–54 a plausible mechanism was proposed for the oxidation of sulfides to sulfoxides using H2O2 in the presence of the γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) catalyst (Scheme 2). As shown in Scheme 2, the intermediate (I) is obtained by reaction of H2O2 with γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH), which is converted to the active oxidant (II). Then, nucleophilic attack of the sulfide on this intermediate gives cation (III), which produces the corresponding sulfoxide and regenerates the catalyst for the subsequent catalytic cycle.

Catalyst reusability and stability

The reusability of the γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst was also examined. The magnetic separation of the catalyst was performed after every run, followed by washing with CH2Cl2 and drying in an oven at 80 °C for 6 hours. As shown in Table 6, the catalyst maintained high activity for at least six consecutive cycles without significant loss of performance. In addition, the filtrates were studied through the ICP to measure palladium leaching. As listed in Table 6, after the third cycle, no Pd was leached, meaning that the catalyst was stable in the reaction conditions.

The control experiment (hot filtration test) was performed to investigate the catalytic activity of the leached Pd for both oxidation reactions. In this manner, in the oxidation of dibenzyl sulfide in the presence of γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst, the catalyst was magnetically separated after 40 min when the reaction yield was about 58%. The filtrate was then stirred at room temperature for an additional 75 min. No more sulfoxide was obtained upon the removal of the catalyst, indicating the leached palladium has not the catalytic activity. A similar control experiment was also performed to study the catalytic activity of the leached Pd in the oxidation of 4-methyl thiophenol. The catalyst was magnetically separated after 15 min when the reaction yield was about 55%, and the filtrate was then stirred at room temperature for an additional 30 min. No further yield of the reaction was observed after the removal of the catalyst.

Also, characterization of the recovered γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) catalyst by FT-IR and XRD techniques (Fig. 1d and 2c, respectively) revealed no significant structural changes compared to the fresh catalyst, confirming its chemical and structural stability during repeated use.

Conclusions

In summary, a novel magnetically separable nanocatalyst, γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH), was successfully synthesized by covalent grafting of a palladium(ii) unsymmetrical salophen complex on chloro-functionalized γ-Fe2O3 magnetic nanoparticles. The synthesized γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst was thoroughly characterized by FT-IR, XRD, SEM and EDX, TEM, VSM, and ICP techniques. Efficient selective conversion of sulfides to sulfoxides and thiols to disulfides was achieved at room temperature using the heterogeneous γ-Fe2O3@Pd(salophen-OH) nanocatalyst. The ease of separation was another advantage. The nanocatalyst was magnetically isolated and successfully reused six times in both oxidation reactions without any notable decrease in performance.

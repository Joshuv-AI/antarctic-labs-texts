Quantum fluctuation-induced first-order breaking of time-reversal symmetry in unconventional superconductors Preprint: APS/123-QED
Yin Shi

Abstract
Spontaneous time-reversal symmetry breaking in superconductors with competing non-degenerate pairing channels is an exotic quantum phase transition that could give rise to robust topological superconductivity and unusual magnetism. It is proposed mostly in two-dimensional systems and is signaled by a nonzero relative phase between the two superconducting order parameters, hence it should particularly be prone to order-parameter phase fluctuations. Nevertheless, the existing understanding of it is still at the mean-field level. Here, we illustrate the non-negligible effects of the phase fluctuations on such quantum phase transitions using the hole-doped square-lattice t t - J J model as an example. We derive the phase fluctuation-corrected free energy and show that under the quantum phase fluctuations, the time-reversal asymmetric s + i ​ d s+id phase region splits off a dome featuring a first-order border with the d d phase, indicating the possibility of a phase separation into the time-reversal symmetric and asymmetric phases. The phase fluctuations also narrow the range of the s + i ​ d s+id phase considerably. We further discuss the implications of our findings for recent experiments on disorder-induced first-order quantum breakdown of superconductivity and promising high-temperature topological superconductivity in twisted cuprate Josephson junctions.

Discussion—

The formalism we presented is essentially a
self-consistent
Gaussian approximation for the phase fluctuations that couple to the long-range charge fluctuations on the level of the random phase approximation.
Because it is a self-consistent approach, this formalism takes into account some higher-order effects of the phase fluctuations beyond the bare Gaussian approximation.
It should be these higher-order effects that modify the order of the
\mathcal{T}
-breaking quantum phase transition.

Increasing the ratio of the cutoff wavevector over the gap amplitude
q_{c}/|\Delta_{x}|
within a limit leads to a more profound modification of the phase diagram but keeps the qualitative features presented in this work.
However, if
q_{c}/|\Delta_{x}|
is set beyond the limit, e.g.,
q_{c}=4|\Delta_{x}|/v_{F}
, we find that the saddle point solution will be unstable at high temperatures well above the superconducting transition temperature
Paramekanti et al. 2000
.

A recent experiment showed that in an amorphous superconductor, increasing disorder quickly widens the temperature window of the pseudogap (phase fluctuating) phase and finally leads to a first-order quantum phase transition from the superconducting phase to a glassy insulator
Charpentier et al. 2025
.
This is consistent with the theoretical anticipation that disorder is more influential in promoting the superconducting phase fluctuations and altering the nature of a quantum phase transition compared with a clean system
Charpentier et al. 2025
.
In this context, we showed that the inherent phase fluctuations in a clean system can also alter the order of a
\mathcal{T}
-breaking quantum phase transition within superconducting phases, demonstrating the vulnerability of
\mathcal{T}
-broken superconductivity to the order-parameter phase fluctuations.

Finally, the formalism presented in this work can be extended straightforwardly for other systems such as twisted cuprate Josephson junctions promising to host high-temperature topological
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}+id_{xy}
superconductivity
Can et al. 2021
;
Zhao et al. 2023
, which will involve multiple bands and will be a future work.
Although we have not directly calculated for this twist-induced
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}+id_{xy}
phase, our current findings may still provide insights for interpreting the recent experiment
Zhao et al. 2023
on it.
The experiment showed an interesting feature: the reversible Josephson diode effect implying breaking
\mathcal{T}
has a complex dependence on the twist angle and sharply vanishes for some twist angles within
(40^{\circ},44^{\circ})
(Fig. 4F, G in Ref.
Zhao et al. 2023
).
This suggests that the
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}+id_{xy}
phase does not exist for some twist angles where the mean-field theory predicts to support it
Can et al. 2021
and that the associated quantum phase transition is first order.
It is, however, consistent with our findings of the splitting of the
\mathcal{T}
-broken superconducting phase region and the modification of phase-transition order near the quantum critical point, which result from the quantum phase fluctuations.
Therefore, it is possible that our findings point out some general effects of the phase fluctuations on the spontaneous
\mathcal{T}
breaking in superconductors, which could shape our understanding of such important quantum phase transitions and have implications for the
\mathcal{T}
-broken topological superconductivity.

\displaystyle\Sigma(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},\tau)

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{\bigl[\mathrm{i}\partial_{\tau}\theta_{\bm{r}}(\tau)+\phi_{\bm{r}}(\tau)\bigr]\delta_{\bm{rr}^{\prime}}-t_{\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}}\bigl[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\theta_{\bm{r}}(\tau)-\theta_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\tau))\tau_{3}}-1\bigr]\Bigr\}\tau_{3},

(9)

are the inverse mean-field Green’s function and self-energy, respectively.
Invoking the Fourier transformations
\psi_{\bm{r}}(\tau)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{\bm{k}n}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\omega_{n}\tau-{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}})}\psi_{{\bm{k}}n}
,
\theta_{\bm{r}}(\tau)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{\bm{k}n}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\nu_{n}\tau-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r})}\theta_{\bm{k}n}
,
\phi_{\bm{r}}(\tau)=N^{-1}\sum_{\bm{k}n}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\nu_{n}\tau-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r})}\phi_{\bm{k}n}
, and
\Delta_{\bm{r}}=N^{-1}\sum_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}\Delta_{\bm{k}}
, we get

\displaystyle S={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{q}}\sum_{mn}\psi_{{\bm{k}}m}^{\dagger}\bigl[\mathcal{G}^{-1}({\bm{k}},\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{q}}\delta_{mn}-\Sigma({\bm{k}},{\bm{q}},\mathrm{i}\nu_{m-n})\bigr]\psi_{{\bm{q}}n}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{NTJ}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{q}}\bigl[\cos(k_{x}-q_{x})+\cos(k_{y}-q_{y})\bigr]\Delta_{\bm{k}}\Delta_{\bm{q}}^{*}-\sum_{\bm{q}n}\frac{|\phi_{\bm{q}n}|^{2}}{2NTV_{\bm{q}}},

(10)

where
\mathcal{G}^{-1}(\bm{k},\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}-\xi_{\bm{k}}\tau_{3}-\Delta_{\bm{k}}\tau_{+}-\Delta_{\bm{k}}^{*}\tau_{-}
and
\Sigma(\bm{k},\bm{q},i\nu_{n})=(T/N)\int_{0}^{1/T}d\tau\sum_{\bm{rr}^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\nu_{n}\tau-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}+\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}^{\prime})}\Sigma(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},\tau)
.
Here
\omega_{n}=(2\pi+1)nT
and
\nu_{n}=2\pi nT
with
n
being an integer are the fermionic and bosonic Matsubara frequencies, respectively.
Integrating out the fermion field
\psi_{\bm{k}n}
in Eq. (
10
), we get the effective action

S_{\mathrm{eff}}=-\mathrm{Tr}\bigl[\ln\bigl((T\mathcal{G})^{-1}\bigr)\bigr]+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl[(\mathcal{G}\Sigma)^{\gamma}\bigr]+\frac{2N}{TJ}\bigl(|\Delta_{x}|^{2}+|\Delta_{y}|^{2}\bigr)-\sum_{\bm{q}n}\frac{|\phi_{\bm{q}n}|^{2}}{2NTV_{\bm{q}}},

(11)

where the trace is over the momentum, frequency, and Nambu subspaces.

We then expand
\Sigma(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},\tau)
up to the quadratic order of
\theta_{\bm{r}}(\tau)-\theta_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\tau)
, which is small due to the long wavelength of
\theta_{\bm{r}}(\tau)
at low temperatures,

\Sigma(\bm{k},\bm{q},\mathrm{i}\nu_{n})\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bigl[\nu_{n}\tau_{3}+\mathrm{i}(\xi_{\bm{k}}-\xi_{\bm{q}})\bigr]\theta_{\bm{k}-\bm{q},n}-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}m}(\xi_{\bm{k}}+\xi_{\bm{q}}-2\xi_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}})\tau_{3}\theta_{\bm{k}^{\prime}m}\theta_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}-\bm{k}^{\prime},n-m}+\frac{1}{N}\phi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q},n}\tau_{3}.

(12)

We also keep only
\gamma=1,2
terms in Eq. (
11
) to retain the phase terms up to the quadratic order of
\theta_{\bm{r}}(\tau)-\theta_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\tau)
in the effective action,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{G}\Sigma]\approx{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{NT}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{q}n}\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}(\xi_{\bm{k}}-\xi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}})}{E_{\bm{k}}}\tanh\biggl(\frac{E_{\bm{k}}}{2T}\biggr)|\theta_{\bm{q}n}|^{2},

(13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\bigl[(\mathcal{G}\Sigma)^{2}\bigr]\approx{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{q}mn}\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl[\mathcal{G}(\bm{k},\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})\bigl[\bigl(\nu_{m}\theta_{\bm{q}m}+N^{-1/2}\phi_{\bm{q}m}\bigr)\tau_{3}+\mathrm{i}(\xi_{\bm{k}}-\xi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}})\theta_{\bm{q}m}\bigr]

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{G}(\bm{k}-\bm{q},\mathrm{i}\omega_{n-m})\bigl[\bigl(-\nu_{m}\theta_{-\bm{q},-m}+N^{-1/2}\phi_{-\bm{q},-m}\bigr)\tau_{3}-\mathrm{i}(\xi_{\bm{k}}-\xi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}})\theta_{-\bm{q},-m}\bigr]\Bigr].

(14)

Since
\theta_{\bm{r}}(\tau)
is varying slowly in space and time,
\theta_{\bm{q}n}
would quickly vanish as
\bm{q}
and
n
depart from zero.
We can thus further expand Eqs. (
13
,
14
) in powers of
\bm{q}
and
\nu_{n}
up to their quadratic order because they are controlled by
\theta_{\bm{q}n}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{G}\Sigma]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{\bm{q}n}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=x}^{z}D^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}q_{\alpha}q_{\beta}|\theta_{\bm{q}n}|^{2},

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\bigl[(\mathcal{G}\Sigma)^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\bm{q}n}\biggl[-\chi\biggl(\nu_{n}\theta_{\bm{q}n}+\frac{\phi_{\bm{q}n}}{\sqrt{N}}\biggr)\biggl(-\nu_{n}\theta_{\bm{q}n}^{*}+\frac{\phi_{\bm{q}n}^{*}}{\sqrt{N}}\biggr)+\sum_{\alpha,\beta=x}^{z}D^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha\beta}q_{\alpha}q_{\beta}|\theta_{\bm{q}n}|^{2}\biggr],

(16)

with

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{E_{\bm{k}}}\tanh\biggl(\frac{E_{\bm{k}}}{2T}\biggr)\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}\partial k_{\beta}},

(17)

\displaystyle D^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}n_{F}^{\prime}(E_{\bm{k}})\frac{\partial\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\frac{\partial\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}},

(18)

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{\bm{k}}}\biggl[\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{E_{\bm{k}}}\tanh\biggl(\frac{E_{\bm{k}}}{2T}\biggr)\biggr].

(19)

Here
n_{F}(\cdot)
is the Fermi distribution function and
n_{F}^{\prime}(\cdot)
represents its derivative.
We can integrate Eq. (
17
) by parts and use the periodicity of
\xi_{\bm{k}}
to get the phase stiffness

D_{\alpha\beta}\equiv D^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}+D^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial E_{\bm{k}}}\biggl[\frac{1}{E_{\bm{k}}}\tanh\biggl(\frac{E_{\bm{k}}}{2T}\biggr)\biggr]\frac{1}{E_{\bm{k}}}\biggl(\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{2}\frac{\partial|\Delta_{\bm{k}}|^{2}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}-|\Delta_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\frac{\partial\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\biggr)\frac{\partial\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}.

(20)

Note that both
D^{\prime}
and
D^{\prime\prime}
are symmetric matrices, so is
D
.
From Eq. (
20
), it is clear that
D=0
if
\Delta_{\bm{k}}=0
as it should.

Finally, plugging Eqs. (
15
,
16
) into Eq. (
11
) and integrating out the fields
\phi_{\bm{q}n}
and
\theta_{\bm{q}n}
, we obtain the free energy as in Eq. (
3
).

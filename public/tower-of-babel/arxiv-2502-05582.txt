Some remarks on the group of formal diffeomorphisms of the line
Yury A. Neretin

Abstract




Consider a strictly positively graded finitely generated infinite-dimensional
real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. It has a well-defined Lie group
\overline{\mathbf{G}}
,
which is an inverse limit of finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie groups (a pro-unipotent group). Generally, representations (even finite-dimensional representations) of
\mathfrak{g}
and actions of
\mathfrak{g}

on manifolds do not admit liftings to
\overline{\mathbf{G}}
.
There is a canonically defined dense subgroup

\mathbf{G}^{\circ}\subset\overline{\mathbf{G}}
with a stronger (Polish) topology, which admits lifting of representations of

\mathfrak{g}
in finite-dimensional spaces (and, more generally,
of representations of
\mathfrak{g}
by bounded operators in Banach spaces).
We describe this completion for the group
\overline{\mathbf{Diff}}
of formal
diffeomorphisms of the line, i.e., substitutions of the form

x\mapsto x+p(x)
,
where
p(x)=a_{2}x^{2}+\dots
are formal series, and show that the group

\mathbf{Diff}^{\circ}
consists
of series with subfactorial growth of coefficients.

1.
Introduction

1.1. Graded nilpotent Lie algebras and corresponding groups.
We consider
finitely generated strictly positively graded real Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
,

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{(n)},\qquad\bigl[\mathfrak{g}^{(n)},\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}\bigr]\subset\mathfrak{g}^{(n+m)}.

Consider the completion
\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{g}}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of formal series

\sum_{j>0}x_{j}
, where
x_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}

Denote by

the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}

It has a natural grading generated by the grading of
\mathfrak{g}

U(\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n\geqslant 0}U^{(n)}(\mathfrak{g})

All homogeneous components

are finite-dimensional.
Consider the completion
\overline{\mathbf{U}}(\mathfrak{g})
of

consisting of formal series

\sum_{j\geqslant 0}u_{j}

u_{j}\in U^{(j)}(\mathfrak{g})

with the topology of component-wise convergence,

\sum_{j\geqslant 0}u_{j}^{(k)}

\sum_{j\geqslant 0}u_{j}

if for each

We have a well-defined exponential map

\exp(x)=\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

By the Campbell–Hausdorff formula (see, e.g.,
[
34

the subset

is closed with respect to multiplication,
so we get a ’Lie group’

corresponding to the Lie algebra
\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{g}}}
.

The zoo of such Lie algebras and groups includes, in particular,
the following wide natural families.

1.2. Stronger topologies.

consist of formal series. So it can act only in spaces of formal series or actually its actions are reduced to some
finite-dimensional quotients of

\overline{\mathbf{G}}

\overline{\mathbf{G}}

The picture is even more unperfect, since monodromy operators

2.1. The space
\overline{V}
of formal series.

We consider the linear space
\overline{V}
consisting of all formal series

We say that an operator

(resp.,

if its matrix elements

are zeros for

(resp.,

Equivalently, consider the following filtration

\overline{V}\supset x\,\overline{V}\supset x^{2}\,\overline{V}\supset\dots

An operator

S(x^{k}\,\overline{V})\subset x^{k}\,\overline{V}

for all

and strictly triangular if

S(x^{k}\,\overline{V})\subset x^{k+1}\,\overline{V}

So a triangular operator

determines a sequence
of linear transformations

\overline{V}/x^{k}\,\overline{V}\to\overline{V}/x^{k}\,\overline{V},

and
S
is determined by this sequence.

For any formal diffeomorphism

\gamma=x+a_{2}x^{2}+\dots
,
we define the linear operator

T(\gamma):\overline{V}\to\overline{V}

T(\gamma)\,F(x)=T(\gamma)\,\Bigl(\sum_{m\geqslant 0}u_{m}x^{m}\Bigr):=\sum_{m\geqslant 0}u_{m}(x+a_{2}x^{2}+\dots)^{m}.

Obviously, we get a representation of
\overline{\mathbf{Diff}}

T(\gamma_{1})\,T(\gamma_{2})=T(\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}).

Clearly, each matrix element of

T(\gamma)
in the basis

is a polynomial in the variables
a_{j}
. Notice also that
the operator

is strictly triangular.

Development and validation of a nomogram for predicting the likelihood of undergoing surgical intervention in ruptured corpus luteum
Authors: Liang Chen, Fang Liang, Wanhong Xu, Shijie Zhao, Xuehui Zhong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate clinical factors associated with surgical intervention in patients with ruptured corpus luteum (CL) and to develop a prediction model for estimating the likelihood of undergoing surgical intervention.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled 341 patients with ruptured CL, who were allocated to surgical (n = 106) or conservative (n = 235) group. Baseline demographic, laboratory, and imaging data were collected and compared. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was employed to identify independently associated factors, which were subsequently used to develop a nomogram prediction model. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) for discriminative ability, calibration curves for accuracy, and decision curve analysis (DCA) for clinical utility.ResultsAbdominal rebound tenderness (OR = 1.363, 95% CI: 1.078–1.724), lower admission hemoglobin level (OR = 0.891, 95% CI: 0.862–0.921), longer prothrombin time (OR = 1.445, 95% CI: 1.019–2.048), larger maximum CL cyst diameter (OR = 2.340, 95% CI: 1.821–3.006), and greater pelvic fluid depth (OR = 1.482, 95% CI: 1.154–1.903) were independent factors associated with undergoing surgical intervention. The nomogram demonstrated good discriminative ability (AUC = 0.884, 95% CI: 0.847–0.921), with a bias-corrected AUC of 0.876 after Bootstrap validation. The calibration curve indicated good agreement between predicted probabilities and actual observations. DCA showed a favorable net clinical benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities.ConclusionThis study developed a nomogram incorporating five independent predictors: abdominal rebound tenderness, admission hemoglobin, prothrombin time, maximum corpus luteum cyst diameter, and pelvic fluid depth. Although internal validation demonstrated good discrimination (AUC = 0.884) and potential clinical utility, inherent treatment-selection bias renders this model strictly exploratory. Pending prospective external validation, it serves merely as a preliminary decision-support reference.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12905-026-04549-1.Keywords: Ruptured corpus luteum, Surgical intervention, Risk factors, Prediction model

Introduction

Ruptured corpus luteum (CL) is a common gynecological emergency among women of reproductive age and represents one of the frequent causes of acute pelvic pain in this population [1]. The clinical presentation of this condition is remarkably heterogeneous, ranging from mild, self-limiting abdominal pain to sudden massive intraperitoneal hemorrhage leading to hypovolemic shock [2, 3]. This broad spectrum poses significant challenges for emergency and gynecological physicians during initial assessment and decision-making [1].

Currently, the selection of treatment strategies for ruptured CL in clinical practice relies heavily on a comprehensive evaluation of several factors, including continuous monitoring of vital signs, dynamic trends in hemoglobin (Hb) levels, and findings from pelvic imaging (e.g., computed tomography, CT) indicating fluid accumulation [4, 5]. This decision-making process still largely depends on the clinician’s individual experience and judgment. In the early stages following admission, synthesizing disparate signs, laboratory results, and imaging findings into a coherent risk assessment remains a common clinical challenge. This uncertainty can potentially lead to two tendencies: delayed identification of patients at risk for ongoing hemorrhage, or, given the self-limiting nature of some bleeding episodes, premature surgical intervention in cases that might be successfully managed with supportive care. Therefore, exploring methods that can systematically integrate multi-dimensional information early in the clinical course to provide additional objective references for risk stratification may positively inform clinical decision-making.

Accordingly, this study aims to systematically identify independent predictive factors closely associated with the likelihood of undergoing surgical intervention, reflecting real-world clinical decision-making in patients with ruptured CL. Based on these factors, a visualized nomogram prediction model will be constructed to assess its discriminative ability, calibration, and clinical utility. The development and validation of this model are expected to provide objective data support and decision-making assistance for individualized clinical management of this condition.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

A single-center, retrospective cohort study design was adopted. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Fourth Hospital of Changsha, which waived the requirement for informed consent. Patients hospitalized in the Department of Gynecology at The Fourth Hospital of Changsha with a diagnosis of ruptured CL between January 2021 and January 2026 were enrolled.

Inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 14 years; (2) a definitive discharge diagnosis of ruptured CL. For patients who underwent surgery, the diagnosis was confirmed by intraoperative findings and postoperative histopathological examination. For patients managed conservatively, the diagnosis was established based on a combination of typical clinical presentation (e.g., sudden-onset lower abdominal pain and peritoneal irritation signs), ultrasound findings demonstrating an ovarian CL cyst with associated pelvic fluid, and exclusion of ectopic pregnancy by negative serum β-human chorionic gonadotropin. In addition, the diagnosis was supported by clinical resolution of symptoms and regression of imaging findings during hospitalization and at follow-up within 4–8 weeks, without identification of alternative pathology; (3) complete records of medical history, physical examination, laboratory tests, imaging reports, treatment plan, and prognosis during hospitalization.

Exclusion criteria were: (1) definitive diagnosis of other gynecological emergencies such as ectopic pregnancy, ruptured ovarian tumor, or ovarian cyst torsion confirmed by postoperative pathology or follow-up; (2) concurrent known ovarian malignancy or sex cord-stromal tumor; (3) concurrent severe coagulation disorders (e.g., hemophilia) or current use of therapeutic anticoagulants; (4) incidental finding of a ruptured CL during surgery primarily indicated for another acute abdomen condition (e.g., acute appendicitis, gastrointestinal perforation); (5) concurrent severe cardiac, pulmonary, hepatic, or renal insufficiency, or other underlying diseases that could significantly influence treatment decisions or outcome assessment.

Based on these criteria, 341 patients were included and stratified into a surgical group (n = 106; undergoing laparoscopy or laparotomy) and a conservative group (n = 235; receiving non-surgical management). The decision to operate was made by attending physicians based on signs of clinical instability, such as worsening vital signs, significant Hb drops, or persistent severe pain despite analgesia. Because this outcome reflects real-world physician decision-making rather than a strictly objective measure of disease severity, it inherently introduces treatment bias. However, to mitigate individual variability, all patients were managed by attending physicians within the same department who adhered to consistent clinical protocols. This inherent selection bias must be acknowledged, as the model predicts the likelihood of undergoing surgery in our clinical setting rather than the absolute biological necessity for operative intervention.

Data collection

Two trained researchers independently extracted the following data from the electronic medical record system and performed cross-checking:

Basic information: Age, body mass index (BMI), menstrual history (menstrual regularity), history of cesarean section, history of hypertension, history of diabetes, and pregnancy status were collected.

Clinical symptoms and signs: To ensure data consistency, key clinical variables were defined based on standardized criteria. Syncope was defined as a documented transient loss of consciousness with spontaneous recovery, rather than mere dizziness. Anal distension was defined as a subjective pressure sensation in the rectum or the urge to defecate caused by Douglas pouch stimulation. Abdominal rebound tenderness was recorded as present if the patient experienced intensified pain upon the sudden release of manual pressure during abdominal palpation. Additional data included precipitating factors, menstrual cycle phase, nausea, vomiting, positive shifting dullness, vital signs [pulse rate (PR), systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP)], and the calculated shock index (SI = PR/SBP).

Sample size consideration

Given the retrospective design of this prediction model study, sample size adequacy was assessed based on the number of predictor variables in the final model relative to the number of outcome events. According to methodological recommendations for prediction models, at least 10 outcome events are typically required per predictor variable to ensure model stability. The final model incorporated 5 independent predictors identified from multivariable logistic regression analysis, and the number of outcome events (surgical intervention) was 106. Therefore, the events per variable (EPV = 21.2) met methodological requirements, indicating that the sample size was sufficient to support model development and internal validation.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 27.0 and R 4.5.2. Continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range) based on normality, and between-group comparisons were made utilizing the independent samples t-test or Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical data were presented as n (%) and compared between groups using the Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 10-fold cross-validation was performed as a confirmatory sensitivity analysis and identified the identical five predictors at λ.1se, supporting the robustness of variable selection. Univariate analysis was first conducted to screen for candidate predictor variables. To build a parsimonious model and control for overfitting risk, clinically relevant variables were initially considered. However, to maintain model simplicity and stability, only variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were entered into the multivariable logistic regression model. Based on the independent predictors identified from multivariable analysis, a nomogram model for predicting the risk of surgical intervention was constructed. Multicollinearity among the independent variables included in the multivariable logistic regression model was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF). A VIF value > 5 was considered indicative of potentially problematic collinearity. Internal validation was performed by applying the Bootstrap method (1,000 resamples), and model optimism was assessed by calculating a bias-corrected area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC). Model discrimination was assessed using the AUC. Calibration was evaluated using calibration plots and the mean absolute error, and clinical net benefit was evaluated using decision curve analysis (DCA). All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Missing data were minimal across the dataset, with less than 2% missingness for any individual variable. Given the low proportion of incomplete records, complete-case analysis was performed without imputation.

Results

Comparison of baseline characteristics

A total of 341 patients with ruptured CL were included, comprising 235 (68.9%) in the conservative group and 106 (31.1%) in the surgical group. Baseline characteristics of the two groups are compared in detail in Table 1. No statistically significant differences were observed between groups for age, BMI, PR, DBP, SBP, menstrual regularity, history of cesarean section, pregnancy status, menstrual cycle phase, history of hypertension, history of diabetes, precipitating factors, nausea, vomiting, positive shifting dullness, DD, PLT, APTT, or depth of abdominal fluid (P > 0.05). However, the surgical group had significantly higher proportions of patients experiencing anal distension, syncope, and abdominal rebound tenderness compared to the conservative group (P < 0.05). Regarding laboratory findings, the surgical group exhibited significantly lower Hb levels, while WBC and PT were significantly higher (P < 0.05). Imaging examination revealed that the maximum diameter of the CL cyst and the depth of pelvic fluid were significantly greater in the surgical group than in the conservative group (P < 0.001). Additionally, the SI was higher in the surgical group compared to the conservative group (P = 0.037).

Multivariable logistic regression analysis of risk factors for surgical intervention

Variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis (anal distension, syncope, abdominal rebound tenderness, Hb, WBC, PT, FIB, maximum CL cyst diameter, pelvic fluid depth) were entered into the multivariable logistic regression model. Variable assignments are shown in Table 2. The analysis results (Table 3) indicated that abdominal rebound tenderness, lower Hb level, longer PT, larger maximum CL cyst diameter, and greater pelvic fluid depth were independent factors associated with the likelihood of undergoing surgical intervention in patients with ruptured CL (all P < 0.05). Among these, the maximum CL cyst diameter showed the strongest association (OR = 2.340, 95% CI: 1.821–3.006). Anal distension, WBC, and FIB did not demonstrate independent predictive value in the multivariable analysis (P > 0.05). Collinearity diagnostics confirmed that all VIF values for the five independent predictors were below 1.2 (range: 1.022–1.155), well beneath the conventional threshold of 5, indicating no significant multicollinearity.

To further evaluate the robustness of variable selection, LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation was conducted as a sensitivity analysis. The optimal penalty parameter was selected as λ.1se = 0.045 to yield a parsimonious model (Supplementary Figure S1). At this λ, the LASSO model retained five variables with non-zero coefficients: maximum cyst diameter, pelvic fluid depth, PT, abdominal rebound tenderness, and admission Hb (negative coefficient). These predictors were identical to those identified in the primary multivariable logistic regression model (Table 3). While this consistency is encouraging, it does not preclude the possibility that alternative variable combinations could perform similarly in external populations.

Construction and visualization of the prediction model

Based on the five independent risk factors identified above, a nomogram model was constructed to predict the probability of surgical intervention in patients with ruptured CL (Fig. 1).

Nomogram for predicting the probability of undergoing surgery in patients with ruptured corpus luteum

The logistic regression model was expressed as: Logit(P) = ln[P/(1-P)] = -4.872 + 0.310 × (Rebound tenderness) − 0.116 × (Hb) + 0.368 × (PT) + 0.850 × (Cyst diameter) + 0.393 × (Pelvic fluid depth) where P represents the probability of requiring surgical intervention. Rebound tenderness was coded as a binary variable (0 = absence, 1 = presence). Hb was measured in g/L, PT in seconds, maximum cyst diameter in cm, and pelvic fluid depth in cm. The constant term − 4.872 represents the intercept of the regression equation. Among these factors, the maximum diameter of the CL cyst contributed the highest weight to the model, followed by the Hb level.

Performance validation of the prediction model

Internal validation of the model was performed using the Bootstrap method (1,000 resamples). As shown in Fig. 2A, the prediction model demonstrated good discriminative ability, with an AUC of 0.884 (95% CI: 0.847–0.921) and a bias-corrected AUC of 0.876, suggesting limited optimism. The calibration curve (Fig. 2B) showed good agreement between predicted and observed probabilities, and the bias-corrected curve aligned closely with the ideal reference line, yielding a mean absolute error of 0.024. In addition, the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test showed no evidence of poor fit (P = 0.716). DCA (Fig. 2C) indicated that using this prediction model to guide clinical decisions yielded a higher clinical net benefit compared to the extreme strategies of “operating on all patients” or “conservatively managing all patients”. Importantly, this net benefit advantage was clearly demonstrated across the clinically realistic threshold probability range of 10% to 80%.

Discussion

The clinical management of ruptured CL remains a critical aspect of gynecological emergency care, necessitating a precise distinction between candidates for conservative management and those requiring surgical intervention [6]. This study developed and internally validated a multidimensional nomogram that integrates physical examination findings, laboratory parameters, and imaging data. The model’s discriminative performance (AUC = 0.884) suggests that a multi-parameter approach provides a more robust objective reference for risk stratification than individual clinical indicators alone.

The independent predictive value of CL cyst diameter and pelvic fluid depth is consistent with the anatomical and pathological characteristics of ovarian rupture. A larger cyst diameter is often associated with more extensive vascularization and increased intra-cystic pressure [7]. In this cohort, larger cysts were correlated with a higher probability of surgery, likely due to the involvement of larger vascular branches that are less prone to spontaneous hemostasis. Furthermore, pelvic fluid depth serves as a quantitative indicator of hemoperitoneum volume [8]. The observation that increased fluid depth was associated with surgical intervention aligns with the physiological response whereby significant intraperitoneal blood accumulation induces peritoneal irritation, manifested as rebound tenderness [9]. This constellation of imaging and physical signs provides an early indication of bleeding severity.

A notable finding of this analysis is the role of PT as an independent predictor of surgical risk. While Hb levels are conventionally used to assess blood loss, they may not accurately reflect the rate of hemorrhage in the hyperacute phase due to delayed hemodilution. Interestingly, while admission Hb was significantly lower in the surgical group, baseline vital signs including PR and blood pressure did not exhibit statistically significant differences between the two groups. This discrepancy may be attributed to the robust physiological compensatory mechanisms typical of young, otherwise healthy women [10]. In this demographic, sympathetic activation and increased systemic vascular resistance can effectively maintain normal blood pressure and PR during mild to moderate acute hemorrhage [11]. Consequently, overt hemodynamic instability often does not manifest until the threshold of compensatory reserve depletion is crossed [12]. Therefore, an early decline in Hb, coupled with imaging evidence of hemoperitoneum, may serve as a more sensitive and earlier indicator of ongoing bleeding than conventional vital signs alone during the initial triage. In contrast, PT prolongation may reflect a subtle alteration in hemostatic balance associated with ongoing hemorrhage [13, 14]. One hypothesis, which requires prospective confirmation, is that rapid intraperitoneal hemorrhage may similarly lead to an early consumptive loss of coagulation factors, as observed in trauma-associated coagulopathy [15]. However, as the present study was not designed to investigate underlying biological mechanisms, these interpretations are strictly hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. While the precise mechanism behind this alteration remains to be elucidated and requires prospective confirmation, the observed association suggests that PT may provide incremental information beyond Hb alone in this clinical setting. This observation aligns with complex cases involving anticoagulated patients, where pre-existing hemostatic compromise can significantly exacerbate bleeding from a ruptured CL and heighten the need for surgical intervention [16, 17].

The potential clinical utility of the developed nomogram in this cohort is suggested by the DCA results, though this must be interpreted with caution pending external validation. Unlike traditional performance metrics, DCA quantifies the net benefit across a spectrum of threshold probabilities. The data indicates that at a threshold probability of > 40%, the model effectively identifies high-risk patients who may benefit from prioritized surgical evaluation. Conversely, a low predicted risk (< 15%) provides a statistical basis for maintaining a conservative approach under close clinical surveillance [18]. Based on the DCA results, the following tentative risk categories are proposed to aid clinical interpretation. Patients with a predicted probability < 15% may be suitable for initial conservative management with close observation. Those with a probability of 15–40% warrant heightened surveillance, with surgical intervention considered if clinical deterioration occurs. A probability ≥ 40% suggests a high likelihood of requiring surgery, and early operative preparation is advisable. These thresholds are strictly exploratory and intended as preliminary decision support rather than definitive clinical criteria at this stage. Pending external validation, this risk assessment tool may serve as a preliminary decision-support reference in emergency settings where rapid triage is required.

This study has several limitations that necessitate cautious interpretation of the results. First, the retrospective, single-center design may introduce selection bias. Most significantly, the outcome of “surgical intervention” is subject to “treatment bias”, as the decision to operate reflects the consensus of attending physicians rather than a blinded, randomized protocol. Some patients in the surgical group may have reached stability with conservative care, while some in the conservative group might have benefited from earlier intervention. To partially mitigate this inherent indication bias, our model prioritizes the inclusion of objective surrogate measures of hemorrhage severity, specifically, quantitative imaging metrics (maximum cyst diameter and pelvic fluid depth) and laboratory parameters (Hb levels and PT). By anchoring the predictive nomogram on these objective physiological and anatomical parameters, we aim to provide a more standardized, data-driven counterbalance to subjective clinical judgments. Nonetheless, the lack of a universally applied quantitative severity score (such as an operative grading scale) in our retrospective cohort remains a limitation. Second, given the extended study period spanning several years, subtle changes in clinical practice, imaging protocols, or surgical thresholds over this interval cannot be entirely excluded as a potential source of confounding. Nevertheless, the departmental protocols for managing ruptured corpus luteum, encompassing indications for surgery, criteria for conservative observation, and pelvic ultrasound examination standards, remained essentially consistent throughout the study period. It is unlikely that any protocol modifications during this time would have systematically biased the treatment decisions. Additionally, while internal validation through Bootstrap resampling showed good calibration, the absence of independent external validation means the model’s generalizability across different clinical environments remains to be established. Finally, despite confirmatory LASSO regression, the initial reliance on univariate screening for variable selection carries an inherent risk of omitting clinically relevant predictors that did not achieve conventional statistical significance in unadjusted comparisons. Potential nonlinear associations of continuous predictors, such as hemoglobin levels and cyst diameter, were not explored in the current analysis and warrant further investigation in future studies. Future prospective, multicenter studies are required to confirm the clinical efficacy of this nomogram.

Conclusion

In summary, this study preliminarily developed a nomogram model incorporating abdominal rebound tenderness, admission Hb, PT, CL cyst diameter, and pelvic fluid depth for assessing the risk of surgical intervention in patients with ruptured CL. The model demonstrated good discriminative ability and showed potential for clinical utility in internal validation. However, due to the absence of external validation, any claims regarding its generalizability and definitive clinical utility must be strictly tempered. Given these inherent limitations, this nomogram should currently be regarded solely as an exploratory tool.

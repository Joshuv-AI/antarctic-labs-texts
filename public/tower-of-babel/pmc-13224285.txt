Conservative management of a large temporal epidural hematoma associated with an incidental middle cranial fossa arachnoid cyst: A case report
Authors: Ioannis Tsakiridis, Christos Grigoriadis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Epidural hematoma (EDH) is a potentially life-threatening complication of traumatic brain injury typically associated with skull fractures and injury to meningeal vessels. Arachnoid cysts are benign cerebrospinal fluid-filled lesions most frequently located in the middle cranial fossa and are usually discovered incidentally during neuroimaging. The coexistence of an EDH with an underlying arachnoid cyst is uncommon and may influence both the radiological appearance and clinical course.Case Description: A 27-year-old male football player presented with headache and dizziness after a head-to-head collision during a football match. Neurological examination was normal with a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 15. Computed tomography revealed a right temporal EDH measuring approximately 3 cm in thickness without skull fracture. An incidental middle cranial fossa arachnoid cyst was identified in the same region. Despite the relatively large hematoma size, minimal midline shift was observed. The patient remained neurologically stable and was managed conservatively with close clinical and radiological monitoring. Follow-up imaging demonstrated stabilization of the hematoma.Conclusion: The presence of an arachnoid cyst may provide additional intracranial space that partially accommodates traumatic hemorrhage, potentially allowing conservative management in carefully selected cases.

INTRODUCTION

Epidural hematoma (EDH) is an important cause of neurological deterioration after traumatic brain injury and is most commonly associated with skull fractures and injury to the middle meningeal artery.[1] Arachnoid cysts are congenital cerebrospinal fluid-filled lesions resulting from splitting of the arachnoid membrane and account for approximately 1% of intracranial lesions.[2,3] They are most frequently located in the middle cranial fossa and are often discovered incidentally during neuroimaging.[2,3] Traumatic complications related to arachnoid cysts include subdural hematoma formation, intracystic hemorrhage, and cyst rupture.[4-6] However, the coexistence of EDH with an underlying arachnoid cyst remains uncommon. We present a case of traumatic EDH overlying an incidental middle cranial fossa arachnoid cyst that was successfully managed conservatively.

CASE PRESENTATION

A 27-year-old professional football player sustained a head injury following a head-to-head collision during a football match. The patient complained of intense headache and dizziness but did not lose consciousness. He was initially evaluated at a peripheral hospital and subsequently transferred to our neurosurgical department for further management.

On admission, neurological examination was normal and the patient had a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 15. Noncontrast computed tomography (CT) imaging demonstrated a right temporal EDH measuring approximately 3 cm in maximum thickness without evidence of skull fracture, adjacent to a middle cranial fossa arachnoid cyst [Figure 1]. An incidental arachnoid cyst located in the right middle cranial fossa was also identified.

Initial axial computed tomography scan demonstrating a right temporal epidural hematoma adjacent to a middle cranial fossa arachnoid cyst.

Despite the relatively large hematoma size, the degree of midline shift was minimal. Given the patient’s stable neurological condition and absence of significant mass effect, conservative management with close neurological monitoring and serial CT imaging was chosen. The relationship between the epidural hematoma and the arachnoid cyst is better appreciated on the coronal CT image [Figure 2]. Follow-up CT imaging demonstrated stabilization of the EDH without evidence of progression [Figure 3]. The patient was discharged neurologically intact.

Coronal computed tomography scan demonstrating the epidural hematoma overlying the middle cranial fossa arachnoid cyst. The coronal plane allows clear visualization of the hematoma and its anatomical relationship with the adjacent arachnoid cyst. The small white square component represents the midline.

Follow-up computed tomography scan demonstrating stabilization of the epidural hematoma without significant mass effect.

DISCUSSION

EDH is most frequently associated with skull fractures and injury to the middle meningeal artery, although cases without skull fracture have been reported.[1,7] Arachnoid cysts are benign lesions most commonly located in the middle cranial fossa and usually remain asymptomatic throughout life.[2,3] Trauma-related complications associated with arachnoid cysts include chronic subdural hematoma, intracystic hemorrhage, and rupture of the cyst wall.[4-6]

In the present case, the EDH developed directly above the arachnoid cyst. Despite the hematoma measuring approximately 3 cm in thickness, the expected mass effect was minimal. The presence of the arachnoid cyst may have acted as a buffering intracranial space, partially accommodating the EDH and reducing the expected mass effect. Similar traumatic hemorrhagic complications related to arachnoid cysts have been described, but EDH in this setting remains uncommon.[5,6]

Current guidelines recommend surgical evacuation of EDHs when certain radiological thresholds are exceeded or when neurological deterioration occurs.[1] However, conservative management may be appropriate in carefully selected patients who remain neurologically intact and demonstrate minimal mass effect on imaging.[1,7] This case highlights the importance of individualized treatment decisions and careful radiological assessment when determining management strategies in traumatic intracranial hemorrhage.

CONCLUSION

The coexistence of an EDH with an underlying arachnoid cyst is uncommon. The presence of a preexisting arachnoid cyst may influence the radiological appearance and clinical behavior of traumatic EDHs and may allow conservative management in carefully selected patients.

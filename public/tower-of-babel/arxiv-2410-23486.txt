Quantum K K -invariants via Quot schemes II
SHUBHAM SINHA, Ming Zhang

Abstract
. We derive a K K -theoretic analogue of the Vafa–Intriligator formula, computing the (virtual) Euler characteristics of vector bundles over the Quot scheme that compactifies the space of degree d d morphisms from a fixed projective curve to the Grassmannian Gr ⁡ ( r , N ) \mathrm{Gr}(r,N) . As an application, we deduce interesting vanishing results, used in our previous work to study the quantum K K -ring of Gr ⁡ ( r , N ) \mathrm{Gr}(r,N) . In the genus-zero case, we prove a simplified formula involving Schur functions, consistent with the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem in the degree-zero setting. These new formulas offer a novel approach for computing the structure constants of quantum K K -products.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
denote the Grassmannian of
r
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g
. Grothendieck’s Quot scheme on
C
compactifies the space of morphisms from
C
to
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
. Strømme
[
47
]
initiated the study of the Quot scheme for
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, later extended by Bertram
[
4
]
to explore the quantum cohomology of the Grassmannian. The Quot scheme on a genus
g
curve has also been used to compute fixed-domain Gromov–Witten counts
[
3
,
2
,
38
]
, with extensions to type A flag varieties and isotropic Grassmannians
[
8
,
10
,
9
,
12
,
31
,
45
]
.

In our previous work
[
48
]
, we showed that the quantum
K
-ring and related
K
-theoretic Gromov–Witten invariants of Grassmannians can be studied through the Quot scheme. We also demonstrated that the topological quantum field theory (TQFT) associated with the quantum
K
-ring is naturally expressed via certain (virtual) Euler characteristics of sheaves over Quot schemes.

In this paper, we present formulas for these Euler characteristics over the Quot scheme,
giving a
K
-theoretic analogue of the Vafa–Intriligator formulas.
In the genus-zero case, we derive a bialternant formula for the relevant invariants.
As an application, we prove vanishing results used in Part I
[
48
]
to study the quantum
K
-ring of Grassmannians,
including proving the finiteness of the quantum product and deriving ring presentations.
In the final section, we introduce an algorithm to compute the quantum
K
-product,
yielding a quantum
K
-Littlewood–Richardson rule for
\mathrm{Gr}(2,N)
, with a multiplication table for
\mathrm{QK}(\mathrm{Gr}(3,6))
provided in the appendix.

1.1.
Vafa–Intriligator formula

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g
. The Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)

parametrizes short exact sequences of coherent sheaves over
C
:

0\to S\to\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus N}\to Q\to 0,

where
Q
has rank
N-r
and degree
d
. The intersection theory of the Quot scheme was studied by Bertram–Daskalopoulos–Wentworth
[
2
]
,
Bertram
[
3
]
, and Marian–Oprea
[
38
]
to
provide a framework for studying fixed-domain Gromov–Witten counts of maps to Grassmannians.
We will state the results as formulated in
[
38
]
.
The Quot scheme carries a universal short exact sequence on
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\times C
:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\times C}^{\oplus N}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow 0.

We fix a point
p\in C
and denote by
\mathcal{S}_{p}
the restriction of
\mathcal{S}
to
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\times\{p\}
.

When
d
is large, the Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
is irreducible, see
[
2
,
42
]
, and we can evaluate the top
intersection numbers of the Chern classes of
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}
against the fundamental class. For arbitrary degrees, the Quot scheme may fail to be smooth or irreducible. Nonetheless, it was shown in
[
38
,
11
]
that
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
admits a two-term perfect obstruction theory and consequently a virtual fundamental class, see
[
5
,
35
]
for the general construction,

[\quot_{d}(C,N,r)]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in H_{2e}(\quot_{d}(C,N,r)),

where
e=Nd-r(N-r)(g-1)
is the virtual dimension of the Quot scheme. This demonstrates that intersection theory can still be pursued virtually by pairing Chern classes with the virtual fundamental class.
These intersection numbers are given by the remarkable
Vafa–Intriligator formula

[
50
,
26
]
.
Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
denote the Chern roots of
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{p}
.
Note that polynomials in the Chern classes of
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{p}
are symmetric in the
x_{i}
’s.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
,
46
,
38
]
)
.

We have

\int_{[\quot_{d}(C,N,r)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=(-1)^{d(r-1)}\sum_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{r}}f(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{r})J^{g-1}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{r}),

where

•

f
is a symmetric polynomial of degree
e
,

•

the sum is over all
\binom{N}{r}
tuples
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{r})

of distinct
N
th roots of unity, and

•

J(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})
is the symmetric function

J(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})=N^{r}\cdot z_{1}^{-1}\cdots z_{r}^{-1}\cdot\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1}.

Remark 1.2
.

The Grassmannian admits the GIT presentation

\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)=[M_{r\times N}^{ss}/\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})],

where
M_{r\times N}
is the space of
r\times N
complex matrices and
M_{r\times N}^{ss}\subset M_{r\times N}
is the open subset of matrices of full rank. Let

\mathfrak{X}=[M_{r\times N}/\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})]

be the quotient stack containing
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
. The Quot scheme admits a ‘stacky’ evaluation morphism

(1)

\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}:\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\to\mathfrak{X}

by restricting a subsheaf
[S\to\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus N}]
to its stalk at the point
p
,
S_{p}\to\mathcal{O}_{p}^{\oplus N}
.
The cohomology classes considered in Theorem
1.1
are the pullbacks via
\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}
of classes from
H^{*}(\mathfrak{X},\mathbb{Z})
, which is the ring of symmetric polynomials in
r
variables.

In the physics literature, Witten
[
52
]
observed a connection
between Verlinde numbers and the quantum cohomology of Grassmannians.
Verlinde numbers represent the dimensions of the space of global sections of
theta bundles over the moduli space of vector bundles on curves.
In
[
36
,
37
]
,
the authors showed that Verlinde numbers can be expressed via intersections on Quot schemes, providing another form of the Vafa–-Intriligator formula.

1.2.
Euler characteristics over Quot schemes

Previous work on explicit formulas for Euler characteristics of
K
-theory classes (tautological bundles) on Quot schemes over curves was initiated in
[
40
]
and
[
41
]

for zero-rank quotients. The latter also derived some formulas for higher rank quotients over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
These formulas, for Quot schemes of zero-rank quotients on curves, utilize universality results in the sense of
[
13
]
and torus localization when
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
(see
[
41
]
for details).

For
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
parameterizing higher rank quotients, i.e.,
r<N
,
a universality result is not expected in the same sense.
In Part I
[
48
]
, we proved that the virtual Euler characteristics
of certain
K
-theory classes over Quot schemes
satisfy the topological quantum field theory (TQFT) axioms, allowing induction on the genus of
C
.
In this paper, we use torus localization for
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
to derive a novel formula,
serving as a
K
-theoretic analogue of the Vafa–Intriligator formula.

Recall the definition of
\mathfrak{X}
from Remark
1.2
.
The
K
-theory
K(\mathfrak{X})
is isomorphic to the representation ring
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.
For a
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathrm{V}
, this isomorphism maps it to the associated
K
-theory

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}\to M_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{V}\in K(\mathfrak{X}).

By abuse of notation, we also denote this
K
-theory class as
\mathrm{V}
.

Let
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\quot_{d}(C,N,r)}
denote the virtual structure sheaf induced by the two-term perfect obstruction theory for
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
(see
[
32
]
).
The virtual Euler characteristic of a coherent sheaf
F
on
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
is defined as

\chi^{\mathrm{vir}}\big(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),F\big):=\chi\big(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\quot_{d}(C,N,r)}\otimes F\big).

Our first main result is the following Vafa–Intriligator type formula.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathrm{V}
and a point
p\in C
, we have

\chi^{\mathrm{vir}}\big(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}(\mathrm{V})\big)=[t^{d}]\sum_{z_{1},\dots,z_{r}}(v\cdot h^{g})(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}^{N-r+d}\cdot\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}P^{\prime}(z_{i})},

where

•

the sum is over all
\binom{N}{r}
sets of
r
distinct roots
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{r}
of

(3)

P(z)=(z-1)^{N}+(-1)^{r}z^{N-r}t=0,

•

v
is the character of
\mathrm{V}
,

•

h
is the character of a
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation that only depends on
N
(see Section
3.5
),

•

the operation
[t^{d}]
on a symmetric Laurent polynomial in the
N
roots of (
3
) involves three steps:
express the Laurent polynomial in terms of
t
using (
3
), expand it as a power series around
t=0
,
and extract the coefficient of
t^{d}
.

Recall that
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
is irreducible for sufficiently large
d
, and its virtual structure sheaf becomes the usual structure sheaf.
A degree estimate from Theorem
1.3
leads to an interesting vanishing result
(see Corollary
4.7
for a stronger version):

\chi(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\left(\det\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}\right)^{m})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\chi(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\Sym^{m}\left(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}\right))=0

for all
m<g
and sufficiently large
d
(relative to
g
).

Remark 1.4
.

When the genus of the curve
g\geq 1
, setting
\lambda=\emptyset
in Corollary
4.7
implies

\chi(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\cO_{\quot_{d}})=0\qquad\text{when $d\gg 0$ with respect to $g$, $r$ and $n$}.

Note that this contrasts with the genus zero case, when
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
is a smooth projective rational variety with
\chi(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\cO_{\quot_{d}})=1
. The above phenomenon is clear in the case
r=1
, when
the Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
is a
projective bundle

\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\cong\mathbb{P}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}^{\oplus N})\to\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C),

where
d\geq 2g-1
,
\mathcal{L}
is a universal bundle on
\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C)\times C
and
\pi
is the first projection.

In general, there is a map
\phi:\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\to\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C)
given by taking the determinant of the quotient. The vanishing result will follow if the following question has an affirmative answer: Does the derived pushforward

R\phi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\quot})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C)}\quad\text{ on $\mathrm{Pic}^{d}(C)$ when $d\gg 0$?}

In
[
25
]
, the authors study certain properties of the fibers of this determinant map.

1.3.
Schur bundles

A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
is a sequence of integers such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 0
.
Let
\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}
be the set of partitions
\lambda
with
r
parts
where
\lambda_{1}\leq N-r
.
Let
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}
be the Schur functor associated with the partition
\lambda
,
and let
\mathrm{S}:=\left(\mathbb{C}^{r}\right)^{\vee}
denote the dual of the standard
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
representation.
Then,
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\left(\mathrm{S}^{\vee}\right)
is the irreducible representation with highest weight
\lambda
.

The representation
\mathrm{S}
corresponds to the universal subbundle
S
on
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
,
as seen from (
2
) and restricting to the semistable locus
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)=[M_{r\times N}^{ss}/\text{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})]
.
Schur bundles
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S^{\vee})
and
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S)
on
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
are defined similarly for any partition
\lambda
.
Their cohomology groups can be described by the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem. In particular, we recall the following result of Kapranov which was used to study the derived category of Grassmannian:

Proposition 1.5

(
[
29
]
)
.

For any nonempty partition
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}
,
we have

(i)

H^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N),\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S))=0
for all
i\geq 0
.

(ii)

H^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N),\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S^{\vee}))=\begin{cases}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}((\mathbb{C}^{N})^{\vee})&\quad\text{if}\ i=0,\\
0&\quad\text{if}\ i>0.\end{cases}

The above equalities hold as
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
-representations.

This implies that for any non-empty partition
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}
:

(4)

\chi(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N),\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S))=0\quad\text{and}\quad\chi(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N),\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S^{\vee}))=s_{\lambda}(1,\dots,1).

In this paper, we consider “quantum” versions of (
4
). For the rest of the introduction, we set
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and work with
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
.
Note that the representation
\mathrm{S}
, as a
K
-theory class on
\mathfrak{X}
,
pulls back to the vector bundle
\mathcal{S}_{p}
over
\quot_{d}
under the evaluation map
\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}
in (
1
). We will consider
the Euler characteristics of the following Schur bundles over
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
:

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\left(\mathcal{S}_{p}\right)=\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}\left(\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\left(\mathrm{S}\right)\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\left(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}\right)=\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}\left(\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\left(\mathrm{S}^{\vee}\right)\right).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
be a non-empty partition.
We have

\displaystyle\chi(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p}))=0,

if one of the following three conditions holds

(i)

\lambda_{1}\leq d+(N-r)
;

(ii)

\lambda_{1}\leq d+2(N-r)
with additional conditions
r<N
,
d>0
and
\lambda_{r}>0
;

(iii)

\lambda_{1}\leq d+2(N-r)
with additional conditions
r<N
and
d\geq r
.

Remark 1.7
.

In Section 3 and 4 of Part I
[
48
]
,
we showed that the vanishing results in Theorem
1.6
recover
important properties such as finiteness and ring presentation for the quantum
K
-ring of
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
,
as obtained in
[

]
and
[
21
]
.
We expect similar vanishing results to apply more broadly
to genus-zero quasimap spaces for flag varieties and symplectic/orthogonal Grassmannians,
offering a new approach to studying the quantum
K
-theory of these varieties.

Remark 1.8
.

Consider the action of
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
on
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
defined via the diagonal action on
\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus N}
. In the main body, we prove the formulas for Euler characteristics in Theorem
1.6
and
1.9

(see Theorems

and

Theorem 1.9
.

Let

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{r})

be a partition, with no extra condition on
\lambda_{1}
.
The following formula holds:

\displaystyle\chi(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}))

\displaystyle=[t^{d}]s_{\lambda+(d)^{r}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N}).

Here
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N}

and
\lambda+(d)^{r}

Schur polynomials can be expressed as determinants of elementary symmetric polynomials using the classical Jacobi–Trudi formula. The elementary symmetric polynomial evaluated at the distinct roots

of (

e_{j}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})=\begin{cases}\binom{N}{j}&\quad\text{if}\ j\neq r,\\[5.0pt]

As a direct application of Theorem
1.9
,
we obtain closed-form expressions
for the Euler characteristics of

over the Quot scheme.
For torus-equivariant versions, see

Corollary 1.10
.

We have

\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}q^{d}\chi(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\wedge^{m}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}))=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1-q}\binom{N}{m}&\text{if }m\neq r\\[5.0pt]

1.4.
Application to quantum
K
-theory

The
K
-group of
X=\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
has a basis
\{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}}
,
where
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:=[\mathcal{O}_{X_{\lambda}}]\in K(X)

represents the
K
-theory class of the structure sheaf of the Schubert variety
X_{\lambda}\subset X
.
These
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
are referred to as
Schubert structure sheaves
.
As shown in
[
33
, §8]
, for any partition
\lambda
, there is a polynomial functor
\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}
, defined via
stable Grothendieck polynomials
, such that for
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}
, we have

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(S^{\vee})

in
K(X)
.
We lift this to the level of representation and define

\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})),

for any partition
\lambda
with
r
parts.
Here
\mathbb{C}^{r}
denotes the standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.
Note that
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
is nonzero even for
\lambda\notin\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}
,
and when
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}
,
the Schubert structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
corresponds to the
K
-theory class associated with
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
on
X
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}
be the stacky evaluation morphism in (
1
).
We define the 1-pointed Quot
scheme invariant of
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
by

\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}:=\chi\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}(\mathrm{O}_{\lambda})\right).

Note that

\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}(\mathrm{O}_{\lambda})=\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}).

As a result of Theorem
1.3
,
we obtain the following bialternant-type formula of the 1-pointed Quot
scheme invariant of
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
(see Theorem
5.1
):

Corollary 1.13
.

For any partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
with
r
(nonnegative) parts,
we have

\chi\big(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee})\big)=[t^{d}]s_{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})

where

f_{i}(z)=z^{d}(1-1/z)^{\lambda_{i}+r-i},

s_{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})}
is the generalized Schur function (
20
),
and
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N}
are the distinct roots of (
3
).

Let
q
be a formal parameter representing the Novikov variable. Givental
[
16
]
and Lee
[
32
]

introduced the
quantum product
on the
\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
-module
\mathrm{QK}(X):=K(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
, a deformation of the tensor product, defined using generating functions of 2-pointed and 3-pointed genus-0 quantum
K
-invariants of
X

(see Section
5.2
).
The
quantum
K
-ring

\mathrm{QK}(X)
is the
\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
-algebra equipped with this new quantum product.
Buch and Mihalcea
[
6
]
were the first to study the quantum
K
-ring of the Grassmannian.

In Section
5.3
, we propose a strategy to compute the quantum product of Schubert structure sheaves
using the Littlewood–Richardson rule for Grothendieck polynomials
[
7
]

and finitely many 1-pointed Quot scheme invariants obtained via Corollary
1.13
.
To illustrate this strategy, we provide a complete description of the quantum product in the rank 2 case.

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathrm{P}_{2,N-2}
such that
\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
.
Then the product in the quantum
K
-ring
\mathrm{QK}(\mathrm{Gr}(2,N))
is given by

\cO_{\lambda}\bullet\cO_{\mu}=\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\mu}+\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{\lambda},\mu}q+\mathcal{G}_{\lambda-(N,N),\mu}q^{2}.

Here
\tilde{\lambda}=(\lambda_{2}-1,\lambda_{1}-N+1)

and
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}
is an element in
K(\mathrm{Gr}(2,N))
defined by

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}:=\cO_{\alpha+\beta}+\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{\beta_{1}-\beta_{2},\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\}}\Big[\cO_{\alpha+\beta+(-i,i)}-\cO_{\alpha+\beta+(-i+1,i)}\Big],

where we set
\cO_{\nu}=0
if
\nu\notin\mathrm{P}_{2,N-2}
.

Remark 1.15
.

We refer to the above formula as the
quantum
K
-Littlewood–Richardson rule
for
\mathrm{Gr}(2,N)
. In particular, Theorem
1.14
immediately implies the positivity conjectured in
[
6
]
for
\mathrm{Gr}(2,N)
: Let
F_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu,d}
be the structure constants for the quantum
K
-product, defined by

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\bullet\mathcal{O}_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2,N-2}}\sum_{d=0}^{2}F_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu,d}\ q^{d}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}.

Then

(-1)^{|\nu|-|\lambda|-|\mu|+dN}F_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu,d}\geq 0

for all
\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2,N-2}
and all
0\leq d\leq 2
.

As noted in
[
6
, Remark 5.2]
,
the quantum cohomology ring
\mathrm{QH}(X)
of
the Grassmannian is an associated graded ring of
\mathrm{QK}(X)
.
Consequently, the quantum Littlewood–Richardson rule in
K
-theory,
as stated in the above theorem,
yields a formula for the product of two Schubert cycles in quantum cohomology.
We refer the reader to Corollary
5.11
for the precise formula.

Our approach enables numerical computation of
the quantum
K
-products for any Grassmannian. In the appendix, we present the complete multiplication tables for
\mathrm{QK}(\mathrm{Gr}(3,6))
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic notation in
K
-theory

Let
X
be a scheme with an action of an algebraic torus
T
.
The group
K_{0}^{T}(X)
(resp.
K_{T}^{0}(X)
) denotes the Grothendieck group of
T
-equivariant coherent sheaves (resp.
T
-equivariant locally free sheaves) on
X
. For a coherent sheaf or vector bundle
E
, we denote its associated
K
-theory class simply by
E
. The non-equivariant
K
-groups of coherent sheaves and locally free sheaves are denoted by
K_{0}(X)
and
K^{0}(X)
, respectively.

If
X
is nonsingular, the map
K^{0}(X)\rightarrow K_{0}(X)
, which sends a vector bundle to its sheaf of sections, is an isomorphism. In this case, we denote the non-equivariant
K
-group of
X
by
K(X)
.

Let
\mathrm{pt}=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
.
For
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
, we have

\Gamma:=K_{T}^{0}(\mathrm{pt})=K^{0}(BT)\cong\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{1}^{\pm},\dots,\alpha_{N}^{\pm}],

where
\alpha_{i}
is the
T
-character corresponding to the projection onto the
i
-th factor.

For a class
F\in K_{0}^{T}(X)
on a proper scheme
X
,
the
T
-equivariant Euler characteristic is defined as

\chi^{T}(X,F):=\sum_{i\geq 0}\text{char}_{T}\,H^{i}(X,F)\in\Gamma.

where
\mathrm{char}_{T}
denotes the character of a
T
-module.

2.2.
Symmetric functions and representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})

The
K
-theory of the Grassmannian is closely connected to
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
representations and symmetric functions.
Let
r
be a positive integer.
We denote the representation ring of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
by
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
.
A
partition
(with
r
parts) is a weakly decreasing sequence of nonnegative integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
, where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 0
. Each partition corresponds to a
Young diagram
with
\lambda_{i}
boxes in row
i
. We often omit trailing zeros, writing
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})
if
\lambda_{i}=0
for
i>m
. When
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{r}=a
, we denote this as
(a)^{r}:=(a,\dots,a)
.

Let
s_{\lambda}
be the
Schur polynomial
for a partition
\lambda
, defined as:

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=\frac{\det(x_{j}^{\lambda_{i}+r-i})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}{\det(x_{j}^{r-i})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}},

where
\det(x_{j}^{r-i})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}(x_{i}-x_{j})
is the
Vandermonde determinant
.
This is known as Jacobi’s
bialternant formula
. Note that
s_{\lambda}
is a symmetric polynomial in the
x_{i}
’s.

Let
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}
be the
Schur functor
associated with the partition
\lambda
. For any
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-module
\mathrm{V}\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
and
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, the trace of
g
on
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{V})
is given by

\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{V})}(g)=s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}),

where
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
are the eigenvalues of
g
on
\mathrm{V}
(see
[
14
, §6]
).
Let
\mathbb{C}^{r}
denote the standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then,
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
is the unique irreducible polynomial representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
with highest weight
\lambda
. For example, if
\lambda=(a)
, then
\mathbb{S}^{(a)}(\mathbb{C}^{r})=\Sym^{a}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
; if
\lambda=(1)^{b}
with 1 repeated
b
times, then
\mathbb{S}^{(1)^{b}}(\mathbb{C}^{r})=\wedge^{b}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
.

In this paper, we also consider the
stable Grothendieck polynomials
, denoted by
G_{\lambda}
for the partition
\lambda
(see
[
7
]
), which have the following Weyl-type bialternant formula:

(5)

G_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=\frac{\det(x_{j}^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}(1-x_{j})^{i-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}{\det(x_{j}^{r-i})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}.

By
[
33
, Theorem 2.2]
, we have the expansion

(6)

G_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\hat{\lambda}}(-1)^{|\mu|-|\lambda|}g_{\lambda\mu}s_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}).

Here
\hat{\lambda}
represents the maximal partition with
r
rows,
obtained from
\lambda
by adding up to
i-1

boxes in the
i
th row for
2\leq i\leq r
.
The coefficients
g_{\lambda,\mu}
are nonnegative and
count the number of increasing tableaux
of skew shape
\mu/\lambda
,
with the constraint that row
i
contains entries
no greater than
i-1
.
According to
[
33
, Theorem 2.7]
,
Schur functions can also be expanded into Grothendieck polynomials as:

s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\supseteq\lambda\\
\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1}\end{subarray}}f_{\lambda\mu}G_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}),

where
f_{\lambda\mu}
is the number of semistandard Young tableaux
of shape
\mu/\lambda
with row
i
entries limited to

1,2,\dots,i-1
.

Let
\mathrm{V}\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
. According to
[
33
, §8]
,
there is a well-defined virtual
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{V})
whose character satisfies

(7)

\chi_{\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{V})}(g)=G_{\lambda}(1-x^{-1}_{1},\dots,1-x^{-1}_{r}),

where
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
are the eigenvalues of
g
on
\mathrm{V}
. The following lemma provides details on the transition matrices between the representations
\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{V})
and
\mathbb{S}^{\nu}(\mathrm{V}^{\vee})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition with at most
r
parts.
There exist unique integers
D_{\lambda}^{\nu}
such that

G_{\lambda}(1-y_{1},\dots,1-y_{r})=\sum_{\nu\subseteq(\lambda_{1})^{r}}D^{\nu}_{\lambda}s_{\nu}(y_{1},\dots,y_{r})

with
D^{\emptyset}_{\lambda}=1
.

Proof.

The coefficients
D_{\lambda}^{\nu}
can be determined by first expressing the character in (
7
) as a linear combination of
s_{\lambda}(1-x_{1}^{-1},\dots,1-x_{r}^{-1})
using (
6
),
and then expanding it in terms of
s_{\lambda}(x_{1}^{-1},\dots,x_{r}^{-1})
.
We then prove that the constant term in this expansion is 1, i.e.,
D^{\emptyset}_{\lambda}=1
.
Specifically, observe that

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}{(1-y_{1},\dots,1-y_{r})}=

\displaystyle\frac{\det((1-y_{j})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}y_{j}^{i-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}{\det((1-y_{j})^{r-i})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\det((1-y_{j})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}y_{j}^{i-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}{\det(y_{j}^{i-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}.

Expanding
(1-y_{j})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}
using the binomial theorem shows that the lower-degree terms in the numerator match the denominator, making the constant term of
G_{\lambda}(1-y_{1},\dots,1-y_{r})
equal to 1.
∎

2.3.
K
-theory of the Grassmannian

We fix two positive integers
r
and
N
such that
N\geq r
.
Throughout the paper, we set

k:=N-r.

Consider the Grassmannian

X=\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)=\{V\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}\mid\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V=r\},

which parametrizes
r
-planes in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
The maximal torus
T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
in
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, consisting of diagonal matrices, acts on
X
. On
X
, there is a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow S\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\oplus N}_{X}\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0,

where
S
is the rank
r
tautological subbundle with a natural
T
-linearization.

The standard GIT presentation of
X
is:

X=[M_{r\times N}^{ss}/\text{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})],

where
M_{r\times N}
is the affine space of
r\times N
complex matrices,
and
M_{r\times N}^{ss}
is its open subset of full-rank matrices.
Define the quotient stack

\mathfrak{X}=[M_{r\times N}/\text{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})],

which contains
X
.

Define
\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
as the set of partitions
contained within the rectangle
(k)^{r}=(k,k,\dots,k)
with
r
rows and
k
columns:

\mathrm{P}_{r,k}=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})\mid\lambda_{1}\leq k\}.

The
K
-group
K(X)
has a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis indexed by
\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
:

\{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}},

where
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}

represents the
K
-theory class
[\mathcal{O}_{X_{\lambda}}]
of the
structure sheaf of the
Schubert variety

X_{\lambda}
in
X
(see
[
7
, §8]
).
These classes
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
are referred to as

Schubert structure sheaves
.

The
K
-theories of
X
and
\mathfrak{X}
are closely related to
representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.
For a
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathrm{V}
, we define its associated
K
-theory class
on
\mathfrak{X}
as

M_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{V}\in K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X}),

where the
T
-linearization is given by

\lambda\cdot[m,v]=[m\lambda,v]
with
\lambda\in T
,
v\in\mathrm{V}
,
and
m\in M_{r\times N}
.
We denote this
K
-theory class also by
\mathrm{V}
for simplicity.
For example, let
\mathbb{C}^{r}
denote the standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, and let

\mathrm{S}=(\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee}

be
its dual.
Then the restriction of the associated
K
-theory class
\mathrm{S}
to
X
is the tautological subbundle
S
.

For later applications, we introduce (virtual) representations
corresponding to Schubert structure sheaves.

Definition 2.2
.

For any partition
\lambda
with
r
(nonnegative) parts, we define

\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})).

Remark 2.3
.

The representation
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
is nonzero,
even for
\lambda\notin\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
.
According to
[
7
, Theorem 8.1]
,
the restriction of
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
to
X
equals
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}

for
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
and is zero otherwise. Consider the example
X=\mathrm{Gr}(1,N)\cong\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
, with universal subsheaf
S=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N-1}}(-1)
. In our convention, for any non-negative integer
\lambda
, the representation
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}=(1-\mathbb{C}^{\vee})^{\lambda}\neq 0
, where
\mathbb{C}
is the standard
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-representation. On the other hand, its restriction
(1-S)^{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\otimes\lambda}
is the
\lambda
-th power of the structure sheaf of a hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
, which vanishes when
\lambda\geq N
.

3.
Vafa–Intriligator type formula

In this section, we first apply the Atiyah–Bott localization to derive
a Vafa–Intriligator type formula for the Euler characteristics of certain
K
-theory classes over Quot schemes on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. While the torus localization used here is inspired by
[
38
,
41
]
, the combinatorics in our approach differ slightly.
In the last subsection, we generalize the formula to all genera.

3.1.
1-pointed insertions over Quot schemes

Let
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
be the Quot scheme parametrizing isomorphism classes of quotients of
\mathcal{O}_{U\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus N}
that are flat over a scheme
U
.
There is a universal short exact sequence of coherent sheaves over
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

(8)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus N}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow 0.

For any closed point
z\in\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
, the restriction of
\mathcal{S}
to
\{z\}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a vector bundle of rank
r
and degree
-d
.

The Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
is a smooth irreducible scheme of dimension
dN+r(N-r)
; see
[
47
]
. Moreover, the tangent bundle is
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}_{\pi}(\mathcal{S},\cQ)=\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{Q})
, where
\pi:\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
is the projection to the first factor.

Consider the standard
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
action on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus N}
, which induces a
T
-action on
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
and a natural linearization of the tautological subbundle
\mathcal{S}
. For
1\leq i\leq N
, let
\alpha_{i}
denote the
T
-character corresponding to the projection onto the
i
-th factor.

Recall the following quotient stack containing the Grassmannian
X=\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
:

\mathfrak{X}:=[M_{r\times N}/\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})].

Fix a point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
We denote
\mathcal{S}_{p}
as the restriction of
\mathcal{S}
to
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)\times\{p\}
.
There is a
T
-equivariant ‘stacky’ evaluation morphism

\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}:\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}

induced
by the restriction
\mathcal{S}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{p}^{\oplus N}
.
Note that
\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}
may not always factor through
X\subset\mathfrak{X}
.
Recall that for a
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathrm{V}
, the associated
K
-theory class on
\mathfrak{X}
is

M_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{V}\in K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X}),

giving the isomorphism
K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X})\cong R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
. By abuse of notation, we denote this
K
-theory class by
\mathrm{V}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. For any
\mathrm{V}\in K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X})
, the level-
\ell

T
-equivariant 1-pointed

K
-theoretic Quot scheme invariant is defined as

\langle\mathrm{V}\rangle^{\quot,T,\ell}_{0,d}:=\chi^{T}\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})^{-\ell}\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}\left(\mathrm{V}\right)\right).

For
\ell=0
, we denote the
T
-equivariant invariant as
\langle V\rangle^{\quot,T}_{0,d}
. The superscript
T
is omitted for non-equivariant invariants. Note that we denote the derived pushforward by
\pi_{\star}
.

Remark 3.2
.

For an irreducible representation
V=\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
, the stacky pullback
\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}\left(\mathrm{V}\right)\cong\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee})
is the Schur functor applied to the vector bundle
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}
(see
[
51
]
for construction of Schur functors). Note that the
K
-theory class of
\mathcal{S}_{p}
is independent of the point
p
; for instance, it sits in the short exact sequence

0\to\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}(-1)\to\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}_{p}\to 0.

In particular, this implies that the
K
-theory class of
\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}\left(\mathrm{V}\right)
is independent of the point
p
.

Remark 3.3
.

K
-theoretic Quot scheme invariants have close connections to 3d gauge theories
[
27
,
28
,
49
,
22
,
23
,
20
,
24
]
.
The level
\ell
corresponds to a specific choice of Chern–Simons levels, and the generating function of
n
-pointed
K
-theoretic Quot scheme invariants
correspond to that of Wilson loops in the 3d
\mathcal{N}=2
Chern–Simons-matter theory on
S^{1}\times S^{2}
;
see
[
49
, §4]
.

The remainder of this section is dedicated to establishing the localization framework and proving the
following Vafa–Intriligator type formula. For clarity, the setup and proof are presented in separate sections.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathrm{V}
and the level
\ell
satisfying
-r<\ell\leq(N-r)
,
we have

\langle\mathrm{V}\rangle^{\quot,T,\ell}_{0,d}=[t^{d}]\sum_{z_{1},\dots,z_{r}}v(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}^{N-r+d-\ell}\cdot\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}P^{\prime}(z_{i})},

where

•

the sum is over all
\binom{N}{r}
sets of
r
distinct roots
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{r}
of

(9)

P(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(z-\alpha_{i}^{-1})+(-1)^{r}z^{N-r-\ell}t=0,

and
\alpha_{i}
is the character of
T=\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{N}
given by the
i
-th projection;

•

v(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})
is the character of
\mathrm{V}
; and

•

the operation
[t^{d}]
on a symmetric Laurent polynomial in the
N
roots of (
9
) involves three steps:
express the Laurent polynomial in terms of
t
using (
9
), expand it as a power series around
t=0
,
and extract the coefficient of
t^{d}
.

3.2.
Fixed loci

Consider the
T
-action on
\quot_{d}:=\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)

induced by the standard
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
-action on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus N}
.
Set
[N]=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
.
The fixed loci of this action is parameterized by pairs
(\vec{d},I)
, where
\vec{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{r})
with
|\vec{d}|=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}=d
, and
I\subseteq[N]
is a subset of size
r
. Moreover, the fixed loci are isomorphic to products of projective spaces:

\fix_{\vec{d},I}=\mathbb{P}^{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{d_{r}}.

The factor
\mathbb{P}^{d_{i}}
corresponds to the Hilbert scheme of
d_{i}
points parameterizing short exact sequences

0\to K_{i}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to T_{i}\to 0

such that
T_{i}
is a torsion sheaf of length
d_{i}
.
The corresponding point in the fixed locus
\fix_{\vec{d},I}
is represented by

0\to S\to\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to\bigoplus_{i\in[N]}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to Q\to 0,

where

S=K_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus K_{r}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptQ\cong T_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus T_{r}\oplus\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus N-r}.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{i}
and
\mathcal{T}_{i}
denote the tautological subbundle and the quotient bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{d_{i}}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We shall use the same notation for their pullback to
\fix_{\vec{d},I}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Note that

\mathcal{K}_{i}=\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{d_{i}}}(-1)\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-d_{i}).

3.3.
Todd calculations and equivariant normal bundle

Recall that for
1\leq i\leq N
, we denote by
\alpha_{i}
the character of
T
given by the projection to the
i
-th factor.
For any character
\alpha
of
T
, we denote by
\mathbb{C}_{\alpha}
the corresponding 1-dimensional representation of
T
. Given any
K
-theory class
E
, we set
E_{(\alpha)}:=E\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\alpha}
.

Let
\pi:\quot_{d}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\quot_{d}
be the projection to the first factor.
Let
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{Q}
be the tautological subbundle and quotient sheaf over
\pi
, respectively (see (
8
)).
It is a standard result that the tangent bundle
T_{\quot_{d}}=\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}_{\pi}(\mathcal{S},\cQ)
restricts to

\bigoplus_{i,j\in I}\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in I,j\in[N]\backslash I}\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\vee})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}

over the fixed loci
\fix_{\vec{d},I}
.
In
K
-theory, it equals

\bigoplus_{i\in I,j\in[N]}\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}\ominus\bigoplus_{i,j\in I}\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}.

Therefore the equivariant Todd class of
T_{\quot_{d}}
restricted to the fixed loci is

\prod_{i\in I,j\in[N]}\mathrm{td}^{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})\bigg(\prod_{i,j\in I}\mathrm{td}^{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})\bigg)^{-1}.

We know that the normal bundle is given by the moving part of the restriction of the tangent bundle to the fixed loci. We observe that the moving part is

\nbun_{\vec{d},I}=T^{\rm mov}_{\quot_{d}}\bigg|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}=\bigoplus_{i\in I,j\in[N];i\neq j}\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\vee})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}\ominus\bigoplus_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}.

Therefore, we have

\frac{1}{e_{T}(\mathcal{N}_{\vec{d},I})}=\prod_{i\in I,j\in[N];i\neq j}\bigg(e_{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\vee})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})\bigg)^{-1}\prod_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}e_{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}).

3.4.
Explicit contributions

Let
h_{i}\in H^{2}(\fix_{\vec{d},I})
denote the pullback of the hyperplane class in
\mathbb{P}^{d_{i}}
. In equivariant cohomology, we have

\displaystyle c^{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})

\displaystyle=(1+(h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}))^{d_{i}+1},

\displaystyle c^{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})

\displaystyle=(1+(h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}-h_{j}+\epsilon_{j}))^{d_{i}-d_{j}+1},

where
\epsilon_{i}
’s are the (cohomological) equivariant parameters such that
\alpha_{i}=e^{\epsilon_{i}}
.
This implies the following expressions for the equivariant Todd classes:

\displaystyle\mathrm{td}^{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}{1-e^{-(h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j})}}\bigg)^{d_{i}+1},

\displaystyle\mathrm{td}^{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}-h_{j}+\epsilon_{j}}{1-e^{-(h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}-h_{j}+\epsilon_{j})}}\bigg)^{d_{i}-d_{j}+1}.

The latter equals
1
when
i=j
.
Similarly, we obtain the Euler classes :

\displaystyle e_{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})

\displaystyle=(h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j})^{d_{i}+1},

\displaystyle e_{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})})

\displaystyle=(h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}-h_{j}+\epsilon_{j})^{d_{i}-d_{j}+1}.

Simplification.
Observe that over the fixed locus
\fix_{\vec{d},I}
, the factor
\frac{\mathrm{td}^{T}(\quot_{d})}{e_{T}(\nbun_{\vec{d},I})}
restricts to

\prod_{i\in I}e_{T}(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i})_{(1)})\prod_{i\in I,j\in[N]}\frac{\mathrm{td}^{T}}{e_{T}}\bigg(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}\bigg)\prod_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}\frac{e_{T}}{\mathrm{td}^{T}}\bigg(\pi_{\star}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{j})_{(\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}^{-1})}\bigg).

For notational convenience, we set

z_{i}=e^{h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad R(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(z-\alpha_{i}^{-1}),

where
\alpha_{i}=e^{\epsilon_{i}}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{td}^{T}(\quot_{d})}{e_{T}(\nbun_{\vec{d},I})}\bigg|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}\bigg(h_{i}^{d_{i}+1}\bigg(\frac{z_{i}^{N}}{R(z_{i})}\bigg)^{d_{i}+1}\bigg)\cdot\prod_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}\bigg(\frac{z_{i}-z_{j}}{z_{i}}\bigg)^{d_{i}-d_{j}+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{(r-1)d}\prod_{i\in I}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{h_{i}z_{i}^{N-r}}{R(z_{i})}\bigg)^{d_{i}+1}z_{i}^{d+1}\bigg)\cdot\prod_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j}).

Proof.

Applying Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch followed by Atiyah–Bott localization, we write the Euler characteristic of
\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})^{-\ell}\otimes\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}(\mathrm{V})
as

\langle\mathrm{V}\rangle^{\quot,T,\ell}_{0,d}=\sum_{\vec{d},I}\int_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}\mathrm{ch}^{T}\Big(\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})^{-\ell}\big|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}\Big)\mathrm{ch}^{T}\Big(\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}(\mathrm{V})\big|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}\Big)\frac{\mathrm{td}^{T}(\quot_{d})}{e_{T}(\nbun_{\vec{d},I})}\bigg|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}},

where
I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\}
runs over subsets of
[N]=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
of
r
elements and
\vec{d}
runs over the tuples of non-negative integers
(d_{1},\dots,d_{r})
that sum to
d
.

Recall that
\mathcal{K}_{i}=\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{d_{i}}}(-1)\boxtimes\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-d_{i})
. Hence, over the fixed loci
\fix_{\vec{d},I}
,
we have

\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\big|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d_{i}}}(1)^{\oplus(d_{i}+1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}\big|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{d_{i}}}(1).

Since
c_{1}^{T}(\cO_{\mathbb{P}^{d_{i}}}(1))=h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}^{T}\Big(\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})^{-\ell}\big|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I}z_{i}^{-\ell(d_{i}+1)},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}^{T}\Big(\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}(\mathrm{V})\big|_{\fix_{\vec{d},I}}\Big)

\displaystyle=v(z_{I}),

where
z_{I}=(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{r}})
with
z_{i}=e^{h_{i}-\epsilon_{i}}
, and
v(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})
is the character Laurent polynomial for the
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathrm{V}
.

We use the explicit calculations from the previous subsection to obtain

(-1)^{(r-1)d}\sum_{\vec{d},I}[h^{\vec{d}}]v(z_{I})\prod_{i\in I}z_{i}^{-\ell(d_{i}+1)}\prod_{i\in I}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{h_{i}z_{i}^{N-r}}{R(z_{i})}\bigg)^{d_{i}+1}z_{i}^{d+1}\bigg)\prod_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j}).

Here

[h^{\vec{d}}]
denotes taking the coefficient of
\prod_{i\in I}h_{i}^{d_{i}}
,
which corresponds to integrating over the product of projective spaces
\fix_{\vec{d},I}=\mathbb{P}^{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{d_{r}}
.

We invoke the multivariate Lagrange–Bürmann formula to sum over
\vec{d}
(see
[
15
]
). We use the following formulation of the formula: For any formal power series
\Psi(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
, and
\Psi_{1}(h_{1}),\dots,\Psi_{N}(h_{r})
with
\Psi_{i}(0)\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle[h^{\vec{d}}]\Psi(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Psi_{i}(h_{i})^{d_{i}+1}=[t^{\vec{d}}]\Psi(h_{1},\dots,h_{r})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{dh_{i}}{dt_{i}},

where we use the change of variable
t_{i}=\frac{h_{i}}{\Psi_{i}(h_{i})},

and express
h_{i}
in terms of
t_{i}
on the right hand side.

In our problem, we choose

\Psi_{i}(h_{i})=\frac{h_{i}z_{i}^{N-r-\ell}}{R(z_{i})}

and use the change of variable

(10)

t_{i}=\frac{R(z_{i})}{z_{i}^{N-r-\ell}},

where
t_{i}
is considered as a power series in
h_{i}
.
Furthermore,

(11)

\frac{dt_{i}}{dh_{i}}=\frac{R^{\prime}(z_{i})-(N-r-\ell)z_{i}^{-1}R(z_{i})}{z_{i}^{N-r-\ell-1}}.

The Lagrange–Bürmann formula implies that the previous expression equals

(12)

(-1)^{(r-1)d}\sum_{\vec{d},I}[t^{\vec{d}}]v(z_{I})\prod_{i\in I}\bigg(\frac{dh_{i}}{dt_{i}}z_{i}^{d+1}\bigg)\prod_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})\\
=(-1)^{(r-1)d}\sum_{I}\sum_{\vec{d}}[t^{\vec{d}}]v(z_{I})\prod_{i\in I}\bigg(\frac{dh_{i}}{dt_{i}}z_{i}^{d+1}\bigg)\prod_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})

Note that in the above formula, the expression after
[t^{\vec{d}}]
is independent of
d_{i}
’s
(and only depends on
d
and
I
).
Given a function
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})
, we have

\sum_{\vec{d}}[t^{\vec{d}}]f(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})=[t^{d}]f(t,\dots,t).

Hence, to evaluate (
12
), we let
t_{1}=\cdots=t_{N}=t
and find the coefficient of
t^{d}
in the result sum.
After the specialization, we note that
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}
are distinct roots of
the following polynomial in
z
(see (
10
)):

P(z)=R(z)-z^{N-r-\ell}t.

Note that
P(z)
is a monic polynomial of degree
N
because

-r<\ell\leq(N-r)
.
Hence
P(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(z-z_{i})
.

Using (
10
) and (
11
), we observe that

\frac{dh_{i}}{dt}=\frac{z_{i}^{N-r-\ell-1}}{P^{\prime}(z_{i})}.

We thus rewrite (
12
) as

(-1)^{(r-1)d}[t^{d}]\sum_{I}v(z_{I})\prod_{i\in I}\frac{z_{i}^{d+N-r-\ell}}{P^{\prime}(z_{i})}\prod_{i,j\in I;i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j}).

We finish the proof of Theorem
3.4
by substituting
t\mapsto(-1)^{r-1}t
.
∎

3.5.
Vafa–Intriligator type formula in all genera

In this subsection, we focus on the non-equivariant case with level
\ell=0
.

Fix a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g
. Let
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
be the Quot scheme
parametrizing
short exact sequences

0\rightarrow S\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus N}\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0

of coherent sheaves on
C
, where
S
has rank
r
and degree
-d
.
The Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
may not be smooth when
C\neq\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
A two-term perfect obstruction theory for
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
was constructed
in
[
38
]
, yielding a virtual structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\quot_{d}(C,N,r)}
via
[
32
]
.

Fix
p\in C
.
Let

\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}:\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}

be the ‘stacky’ evaluation morphism at
p
.

Definition 3.5
.

For
\mathrm{V}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{V}_{n}\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
and
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\in C
, we define the
K
-theoretic Quot scheme invariant by

\langle\mathrm{V}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{V}_{n}\rangle^{\quot}_{g,d}:=\chi\bigg(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\quot_{d}(C,N,r)}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p_{i}}^{*}\left(\mathrm{V}_{i}\right)\bigg).

We define the corresponding generating series by

\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{V}_{n}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{g}=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\langle\mathrm{V}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{V}_{n}\rangle^{\quot}_{g,d},

where
q
is the Novikov variable. See
[
48
, Remark 1.8]
for the independence of the above definition from the choice of points
p_{i}\in C
. For the rest of the article, we assume
p=p_{1}=\cdots=p_{n}
, which implies

\langle\mathrm{V}_{1},\mathrm{V}_{2},\dots,\mathrm{V}_{n}\rangle^{\quot}_{g,d}=\langle\mathrm{V}_{1}\cdot\mathrm{V}_{2}\cdots\mathrm{V}_{n}\rangle^{\quot}_{g,d}.

The above invariants are independent of the choices of
p
and the complex structure of the genus
g
curve
C
.

To state the induction formula of the
K
-theoretic Quot scheme invariants
on the genus, we first introduce the concept of the
quantized duals
to
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
’s
and the
Euler represenation

\mathrm{H}
.

Definition 3.6
.

For any
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})\in\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}
,
we define the
quantized
K
-theoretic dual
of
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}

by

\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}:=\mathrm{O}_{\lambda^{*}}\cdot\det(\mathrm{S})\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})),

where
\lambda^{*}:=(N-r-\lambda_{r},\dots,N-r-\lambda_{1})
is the complement partition
and
\mathrm{S}=(\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee}\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
is the dual of the standard representation.

According to
[
48
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have the following orthogonality relation:

(13)

\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\nu},\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=\delta_{\nu,\lambda}\quad\text{for}\ \nu,\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}.

Definition 3.7
.

We define the Euler representation
\mathrm{H}
by

\mathrm{H}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{P}_{r,N-r}}\mathrm{O}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\alpha}\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})).

Remark 3.8
.

The associated
K
-theory class of
\mathrm{H}
restricted to

is the
K
-theoretic Euler

\wedge_{-1}(TX^{\vee})
of the tangent bundle of

.
When

\mathrm{H}=N(1-\mathrm{S})^{N-1}\mathrm{S},

where
\mathrm{S}
is the dual of the standard representation of

.

Remark 3.9
.

The quantum
K
-ring
\mathrm{QK}(X)
of
X
equipped with the quantized pairing

(F_{\alpha,\beta})
is a commutative Frobenius algebra (see Section
5.2
).
Let
(F^{\alpha,\beta})
be the matrix inverse to the quantized pairing matrix
(F_{\alpha,\beta})
.
Let
\kappa:R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))\rightarrow\mathrm{QK}(X)

.
Then

According to

\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{g}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{O}_{\alpha},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{g-1}=\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V}\cdot\mathrm{H}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{g-1},

4.
Euler characteristics of Schur bundles

4.1.
Bialternant formulas

For a weakly decreasing sequence of integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{r})
,
we define the irreducible (rational) representation
of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
of highest weight

\lambda
as:

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r}):=\det(\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\lambda_{r}}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\bar{\lambda}}(\mathbb{C}^{r}),

where
\bar{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{r},\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{r},\dots,\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r})

is a partition.
Similarly, for any
T
-equivariant vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
over a
T
-variety
Y
, we define
the
T
-equivariant
Schur bundle
over
Y
as:

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{V}):=\det(\mathcal{V})^{\lambda_{r}}\otimes\mathbb{S}^{\bar{\lambda}}(\mathcal{V}).

For an integer sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
we define the (generalized) Schur function using the bialternant formula

(14)

s_{\lambda}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\frac{1}{\det(z_{j}^{n-i})}\begin{vmatrix}z_{1}^{\lambda_{1}+n-1}&\cdots&z_{n}^{\lambda_{1}+n-1}\\
z_{1}^{\lambda_{2}+n-2}&\cdots&z_{n}^{\lambda_{2}+n-2}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
z_{1}^{\lambda_{n}}&\cdots&z_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}\end{vmatrix}.

It follows from the definition that
s_{\lambda}
is a symmetric Laurent polynomial in
z_{i}
’s and satisfies the following

straightening rules
:

(15)

s_{(\dots,a,b,\dots)}=-s_{(\dots,b-1,a+1,\dots)}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{(\dots,a,a+1,\dots)}=0.

Note that when
\lambda
is a partition,
s_{\lambda}
is the standard Schur polynomial.

Recall that
\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}:\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
is the stacky evaluation map at the point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
For any weakly decreasing sequence (of integers)
\lambda
with
r
parts, we have

(16)

\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{p}^{*}(\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r}))=\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{p}).

Hence,

\langle\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})\rangle^{\quot,T,\ell}_{0,d}=\chi^{T}\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})^{-\ell}\cdot\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{p})\right).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a weakly decreasing sequence with
r
parts.
If the level
\ell

In this subsection, we establish vanishing results for the

By (

Let

If the level

For the rest of this subsection, we assume
\ell=0

Proposition 4.6

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})

If
\lambda
is nonempty and

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}((\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee})\rangle^{\quot,T}_{0,d}=\chi^{T}(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p}))=0.

Proof.

We apply Corollary
4.5
in the case

.
Recall
\tilde{\lambda}+(d)^{r}=(d-\lambda_{r},\dots,d-\lambda_{1})

When
-(N-r)\leq d-\lambda_{1}<0

When

Let

The preliminary vanishing result in Proposition

Using the genus-induction formula from Section

Tensoring the universal short exact sequence (

)
with
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(m)

\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{Q}(m))=\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}(m))^{-1}.

Using the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)\cong\mathcal{O}(x)
, we obtain

0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(m-1)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(m)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{p}\rightarrow 0.

This leads to
\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}(m-1))\cdot\det(\mathcal{S}_{p})=\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}(m))

By induction:

\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}(m))=\det(\mathcal{S}_{p})^{m+2}\cdot\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}(-2)).

Finally, noting that
\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}(-2))=\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})
due to the Serre (or Grothendieck) duality,
we conclude the proof.
∎

In fact, it follows from the results in
[

that the Picard group of

\det(\pi_{\star}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})
and
\det(\mathcal{S}_{p})
. Theorem

Theorem 5.1

\prod_{i=1}^{N}(z-\alpha_{i}^{-1})+(-1)^{r}z^{N-r}t=0.

Proof.

Recall that the character of

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}(1-x^{-1}_{1},\dots,1-x^{-1}_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\det((1-x_{j}^{-1})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}x_{j}^{-i+1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}{\det\big((1-x_{j}^{-1})^{r-i}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\det((1-x_{j}^{-1})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}x_{j}^{-i+1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}(x_{j}^{-1}-x_{i}^{-1})}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\bigg)^{r-1}\frac{\det((1-x_{j}^{-1})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}x_{j}^{-i+1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}(x_{i}-x_{j})}.

Using Theorem

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}\rangle^{\quot,T}_{0,d}

\displaystyle=[t^{d}]\sum_{z_{1},\dots,z_{r}}G_{\lambda}(1-z_{1}^{-1},\dots,1-z_{r}^{-1})\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}^{N-r+d}\cdot\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}P^{\prime}(z_{i})}

\displaystyle=[t^{d}]\sum_{z_{1},\dots,z_{r}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}^{N-1+d}\cdot\det((1-z_{j}^{-1})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}z_{j}^{-i+1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}\cdot\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}(z_{i}-z_{j})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}P^{\prime}(z_{i})}

\displaystyle=[t^{d}]\sum_{z_{1},\dots,z_{r}}\frac{\det((1-z_{j}^{-1})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}z_{j}^{N+d-i})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}\cdot\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{i}-z_{j})}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{r}P^{\prime}(z_{i})},

where

run over

subsets of

distinct roots of (

The theorem now follows by applying the generalized Laplace expansion along the first

∎

For a partition

Corollary 5.2

(ii)

By Lemma

\displaystyle\chi\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee})\right)=

\displaystyle\chi\left(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\mathcal{O}_{\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)}\right)

Corollary 5.4

For any partition

\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda+(N)^{r}}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=q^{r}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}.

Proof.

For any root

of the polynomial

(1-z_{j}^{-1})^{N}=(-1)^{r-1}tz_{j}^{-r}

\displaystyle[t^{d}]s_{(z^{d}(1-\frac{1}{z})^{\lambda_{i}+N+r-i})_{i=1}^{r}}(\vec{z})

\displaystyle=[t^{d}](-1)^{r(r-1)}t^{r}s_{(z^{d-r}(1-\frac{1}{z})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i})_{i=1}^{r}}({\vec{z}})

\displaystyle=[t^{d-r}]s_{(z^{d-r}(1-\frac{1}{z})^{\lambda_{i}+r-i})_{i=1}^{r}}({\vec{z}}).

We conclude the proof by using Theorem
5.1

and summing the two sides over all

and

, respectively.

Remark 5.5
.

There is a geometric explanation of the above periodicity. Let
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then

\iota_{p}:\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)\rightarrow\quot_{d+r}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)

be the embedding of Quot schemes which on closed points is given by

[S\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\oplus N}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}]\mapsto[S(-p)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\oplus N}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-p)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus N}].

This exhibits
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
as the zero locus of a section of
(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\oplus N})^{\vee}
on the Quot scheme
\quot_{d+r}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
(c.f. the proof of
[
38
, Theorem 2]
).
Hence

(\iota_{p})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)}=\lambda_{-1}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\oplus N})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\quot_{d+r}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)}=(\lambda_{-1}(\mathcal{S}_{p}))^{N}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\quot_{d+r}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)}.

Using the definition of the Grothendieck polynomial (

we have

G_{\lambda+(N)^{r}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{N}G_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}),

and therefore,

\mathbb{G}^{\lambda+(N)^{r}}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee})=(\lambda_{-1}(\mathcal{S}_{p}))^{N}\cdot\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\vee}).

The periodicity in Corollary

the above two identities and the projection formula along

.

The quantum cohomology ring of the Grassmannian is fully determined by its quantum
K
-ring.
As a corollary, we explicitly compute the product in the quantum cohomology of

The quantum cohomology ring

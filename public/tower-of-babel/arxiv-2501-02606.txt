Besicovitch-weighted ergodic theorems with continuous time
SEMYON LITVINOV

Abstract
. Given 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty , we show that ergodic flows in the L p L^{p} -space over a σ \sigma -finite measure space generated by strongly continuous semigroups of Dunford-Schwartz operators and modulated by bounded Besicovitch almost periodic functions converge almost uniformly (in Egorov’s sense). The corresponding local ergodic theorem is proved with identification of the limit. Then we extend these results to arbitrary fully symmetric spaces, including Orlicz, Lorentz, and Marcinkiewicz spaces.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
47A35(primary), 46L52(secondary)

1.
Introduction

Since the groundbraking work
[
21
]
, pointwise convergence of Besicovitch-weighted ergodic averages has been examined quite extensively; see, for example,
[
2
,
3
,
16
,
10
,
8
]
. However, all these studies were concerned with discrete time, thus, there is a natural question what happens in the realm of continuous time. In this case, of course, one must replace Besicovitch sequences by the well-known Besicovitch almost periodic functions
[
1
]
.

Initially, this problem was studied in the non-commutative setting – for actions of strongly continuous semigroups of
positive
Dunford-Schwartz operators – in
[
19
]
for
t\to 0^{+}
and in
[
7
]
for
t\to 0^{+}
and for
t\to\infty
. In both articles, non-commutative counterparts of the almost uniform convergence (in Egorov’s sense) were employed.

Back to the commutative case with infinite measure, it is worth asking whether
almost uniform convergence
– which is generally stronger than almost everywhere convergence – of Besicovitch-weighted ergodic averages with continuous time will hold for actions of strongly continuous semigroups of Dunford-Schwartz operators that are
not presumed positive
. Besides, as the measure is not finite, there is another question: what happens within the the universal
(L^{1}+L^{\infty})
- space
outside the
L^{1}
- space
. These are the three questions we aim to address in this article.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space. Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
, denote
\mathcal{L}^{p}=\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
. Let
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}
be a semigroup of Dunford-Schwartz operators in
\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
which is strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
. In Section 4 of the article, we show that the corresponding ergodic averages
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\}_{t>0}
converge almost uniformly as
t\to\infty
and as
t\to 0^{+}
for every
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, where
1\leq p<\infty
. Note that the pointwise, that is almost everywhere, convergence of these avrages was studied in
[
23
]
and later in
[
18
]
and
[
22
]
.

Then we proceed in Section 5 by showing that almost uniform convergence in the space
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, where
1\leq p<\infty
, holds when the above ergodic averages are modulated by the bounded Besicovitch (when
t\to\infty
) or locally Besicovitch (when
t\to 0^{+}
) almost periodic functions. In the case
t\to 0^{+}
, the limits are identified.

In Section 6, we extend these results to arbitrary fully symmetric spaces. Such include the well-known Orlicz, Lorentz, and Marcinkiewicz spaces.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite infinite measure space and let
\mathcal{L}^{0}=\mathcal{L}^{0}(\Omega,\mu)
be the
*
-algebra of equivalence classes of almost everywhere (a.e.) finite complex-valued measurable functions on
\Omega
. Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
\mathcal{L}^{p}\subset\mathcal{L}^{0}
be the
\mathcal{L}^{p}
-space on
\Omega
equipped with the standard Banach norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

A net
\{f_{\alpha}\}\subset\mathcal{L}^{0}
is said to converge
almost uniformly (a.u.)
to
f\in\mathcal{L}^{0}
(in Egorov’s sense) if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a set
G\subset\Omega
such that
\mu(\Omega\setminus G)\leq\varepsilon
and
\|(f-f_{\alpha})\chi_{G}\|_{\infty}\to 0
, where
\chi_{G}
is the characteristic function of set
G
. It is clear that every a.u. convergent net converges almost everywhere (a.e.) and that the converse is not true in general.

Note that a.u. convergence may not agree with any topology in
\mathcal{L}^{0}
– see, for example,
[
20
]
. A proof of the following statement can be found in
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
. Although it is given there in the non-commutative setting, one only needs to adjust notation and then verbatim repeat the argument.

Proposition 2.1
.

The space
\mathcal{L}^{0}
is complete with respect to a.u. convergence

Traditionally, a linear operator
T:\mathcal{L}^{1}\to\mathcal{L}^{1}
is called a
Dunford-Schwartz operator
(in short,
T\in DS
) if

\|T(f)\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1}\ \ \forall\ f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\text{ \ and \ }\|T(f)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\ \ \forall\ f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1};

see
[
9
, Ch. VIII, § 6]
,
[
12
]
,
[
14
, §§ 4.1, 4.2]
.

However, as it was proved in
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
(see also
[
22
, p. 42]
), one can assume without loss of generality that
T\in DS
contracts the entire space
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
:

Theorem 2.1
.

For any Dunford-Schwartz operator
T
there exists a unique linear operator
\widetilde{T}:\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}\to\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
such that

\widetilde{T}(f)=T(f)\ \ \forall\ f\in\mathcal{L}^{1},\ \ \|\widetilde{T}(f)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\ \,\forall\ f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty},

and
\widetilde{T}|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}
is
\sigma(\mathcal{L}^{\infty},\mathcal{L}^{1})
-continuous.

This observation is of the essence for us as we aim to extend the results initially developed for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
to arbitrary fully symmetric spaces
E\subset\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
.

If a Dunford-Schwartz operator
T
is positive, that is,
T(f)\geq 0
whenever
f\geq 0
, we will write
T\in DS^{+}
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
M_{\alpha}:X\to\mathcal{L}^{0}
,
\alpha\in\Lambda
, be a family of linear maps. The function on
X
given by

M^{*}(f)=\sup_{\alpha\in\Lambda}|M_{\alpha}(f)|,\ \,f\in X,

is called the
maximal function
of the family
\{M_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
. If
M^{*}(f)\in\mathcal{L}^{0}
for all
f\in X
, then the map
M^{*}:X\to\mathcal{L}^{0}
is colled the
maximal operator
of
\{M_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
.

Given
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
and
T\in DS
, denote

M_{n}(f)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}(f),\ n=1,2,\dots\text{ \ \ and\ \ }M(T)^{*}(f)=\sup_{n}|M_{n}(f)|.

For a proof of the following
maximal ergodic inequality
we refer the reader to
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
T\in DS
and
1\leq p<\infty
, then

\mu\big\{M(T)^{*}(|f|)>\lambda\big\}\leq\left(2\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}\ \ \,\forall\ f\in\mathcal{L}^{p},\ \lambda>0.

In what follows,
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}\subset DS
is a semigroup,
strongly continuous in

\mathcal{L}^{1}
, that is,

\|T_{s}(f)-T_{s_{0}}(f)\|_{1}\to 0\text{ \ \ whenever\ \ }s\to s_{0}\text{\ \ for all\ \ }f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}.

It is well-known that if a linear operator contracts both
\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, it also contracts each space
\mathcal{L}^{p}
for
1<p<\infty
. In addition, utilizing the idea of the proof of Proposition 2.1 in
[
5
]
, we establish the following fact:

Proposition 2.2
.

If a semigroup
\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}\subset DS
is strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, then it is strongly continuous in each
\mathcal{L}^{p}
for
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

Clearly, it is sufficient to verify that

\big\|T_{s_{n}}(f)-T_{s_{0}}(f)\big\|_{p}\to 0\text{ \ \ as\,\ }s_{n}\to s_{0}\ \ \forall\ f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}.

So, let
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and, given
m\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

f_{m}=f\,\chi_{\left\{|f|\leq m^{-1}\right\}}+f\,\chi_{\left\{|f|\geq m\right\}}\text{ \ \ and \ \ }g_{m}=f-f_{m}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and define
m_{0}
to be such that
\|f_{m_{0}}\|_{p}\leq\varepsilon/4
. As
T_{s}
is a contraction in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
for each
s\geq 0
, we have

\big\|T_{s_{n}}(f_{m_{0}})-T_{s_{0}}(f_{m_{0}})\big\|_{p}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\ \ \forall\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Next, note that
g_{m_{0}}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
. Since we can assume that
f\neq 0
, we can also assume, without loss of generality, that
g_{m_{0}}\neq 0
. Then, if we let

h_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\|g_{m_{0}}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\big(T_{s_{n}}(g_{m_{0}})-T_{s_{0}}(g_{m_{0}})\big),

it follows, by the assumption, that
\|h_{n}\|_{1}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Besides, since
T_{s}
is a contraction in
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
for each
s\geq 0
, we have
-\mathbf{1}\leq h_{n}\leq\mathbf{1}
, hence
|h_{n}|^{p}\leq|h_{n}|
, for each
n
, implying that

\|h_{n}\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|h_{n}|^{p}d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}|h_{n}|d\mu=\|h_{n}\|_{1}\to 0\text{\, \ as\,\ }n\to\infty.

Thus, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\big\|T_{s_{n}}(g_{m_{0}})-T_{s_{0}}(g_{m_{0}})\big\|_{p}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\ \ \forall\ n\geq n_{0}.

Therefore, if
n\geq n_{0}
, it follows that

\big\|T_{s_{n}}(f)-T_{s_{0}}(f)\big\|_{p}\leq\big\|T_{s_{n}}(f_{m_{0}})-T_{s_{0}}(f_{m_{0}})\big\|_{p}+\big\|T_{s_{n}}(g_{m_{0}})-T_{s_{0}}(g_{m_{0}})\big\|_{p}\leq\varepsilon,

and the proof is complete.
∎

The following proposition is to justify the existence of ergodic averages we are going to examine in this article. Note that the averages
M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)
below were actually introduced in
[
18
]
and
[
22
]
but in a less straightforward manner, based on a non-trivial application of Fubini’s theorem to the product measure space
\mathbb{R}_{+}\otimes\Omega
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\nu
be Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and let
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
\nu
-measurable function with
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<\infty
. Given
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, the function
\beta(s)T_{s}(f)
acting on the measure space
(\mathcal{R}_{+},\nu)
with the values in the Banach space
(\mathcal{L}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{p})
is Bochner
\nu
-integrable on
[0,t]
for every
t>0
. Therefore, the ergodic averages

M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f):=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)T_{s}(f)ds\in\mathcal{L}^{p}.

are defined for all
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and
t>0
.

In particular, the averages

M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f):=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}(f)ds\in\mathcal{L}^{p}

are also defined for all
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and
t>0
.

Proof.

Fix
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and let
q
be such that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. Then, by virtue of Proposition
2.2
and Hölder’s inequality, given
g\in\mathcal{L}^{q}
, the function

\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni s\longmapsto\int_{\Omega}T_{s}(f)gd\mu

is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, implying that the map

S_{f}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}^{p}\text{ \ given by \ }S_{f}(s)=T_{s}(f)

is
weakly
\nu
-measurable
as of
[
24
, Ch. V, § 4]
. Since
S_{f}(\mathcal{R}_{+})
is a separable subset of the Banach space
(\mathcal{L}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{p})
, Pettis theorem
[
24
, Ch. V, § 4]
implies that
S_{f}
is
strongly
\nu
-measurable
, that is, there exists a sequence
U_{n}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathcal{L}^{p}
of simple maps, as in
[
24
, Ch. V, § 4]
, such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{s}(f)-U_{n}(s)\|_{p}=0,\text{\ hence\,\ }\|T_{s}(f)\|_{p}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|U_{n}(s)\|_{p},\,\ \nu\text{-a.e.}

Therefore, as for any
n
the function
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni s\longmapsto\|U_{n}(s)\|_{p}
is trivially
\nu
-measurable, it follows that the function

\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni s\longmapsto\|T_{s}(f)\|_{p}

is also
\nu
-measurable. Since, in addition,

\|T_{s}(f)\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}\ \,\forall\ s\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

the function
\|T_{s}(f)\|_{p}
, as well as the function

\|\beta(s)T_{s}(f)\|_{p}=|\beta(s)|\,\|T_{s}(f)\|_{p},

is
\nu
-integrable on
[0,t]
, for each
t>0
.

Therefore, by
[
24
, Ch. V,  § 5,  Theorem 1]
, the function
\beta(s)T_{s}(f)
is Bochner
\nu
-intergable on
[0,t]
for any given
t>0
, which completes the proof.
∎

Given
1\leq p<\infty
, we can now define the maximal function of the family
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T})\}_{t>0}
on
\mathcal{L}^{p}
as

M(\mathcal{T})^{*}(f)=\sup_{t>0}\big|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\big|,\ \,f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}.

We will need the following, stronger version, of Riesz convergence theorem:

Proposition 2.4
.

If
f_{n}\to f
in measure, then there exists a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}
such that
f_{n_{k}}\to f
a.u.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
, Since, for every given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mu\big\{|f_{n}-f|>1/k\big\}\to 0,

there exists
n_{k}
such that

\mu\big\{|f_{n_{k}}-f|>1/k\big\}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}}.

Therefore, if we define

\Omega_{k}=\big\{|f_{n_{k}}-f|\leq 1/k\big\}\text{ \ and \ }\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{k},

then
\mu(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{k})\leq\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
implies that

\mu(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon
and

\big\|\,|f_{n_{k}}-f|\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\big\|_{\infty}\leq 1/k,\text{ \ hence \ }\big\|\,|f_{n_{k}}-f|\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\big\|_{\infty}\to 0,

that is,
f_{n_{k}}\to f
a.u.
∎

We shall also notice the following known fact, together with a corollary, that will be used repeatedly in the sequel. A short proof is provided, due to their special importance in this article.

Proposition 2.5
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
, the unit ball in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
is sequentially closed with respect to a.e. convergence.

Proof.

Let
\|f_{n}\|_{p}\leq 1
for each
n
and
f_{n}\to f
a.e. Then
|f_{n}|^{p}\to|f|^{p}
a.e. also, implying, by Fatou’s lemma, that

\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p}d\mu\leq\liminf\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}|^{p}d\mu\leq 1,

hence
\|f\|_{p}\leq 1
.
∎

Since a.u. convergence implies a.e. convergence, and in view of Proposition
2.4
, we also have this:

Corollary 2.1
.

For every
1\leq p<\infty
, the unit ball in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
is sequentially closed with respect to a.u. convergence and convergence in measure.

3.
Maximal ergodic inequality for the family
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)\}_{t>0}

Let
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
is a Lebesgue measurable function with
\|\beta\|_{\infty}\leq C<\infty
. Denote

M(\mathcal{T},\beta)^{*}(f)=\sup_{t>0}|M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)|,

the maximal function of the family
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)\}_{t>0}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. If
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}
and
\beta(s)
are as above, then

(1)

\mu\,\Big\{M(\mathcal{T},\beta)^{*}(f)>\lambda\Big\}\leq\left(\frac{4C\,\|f\|_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}

and, in particular,

(2)

\mu\,\Big\{M(\mathcal{T})^{*}(f)>\lambda\Big\}\leq\left(\frac{4\,\|f\|_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}

for all
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

Fix
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
,
\lambda>0
,
\displaystyle\frac{n}{m}\in\mathbb{Q}
, where
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, and denote

g=\int_{0}^{1}\big|T_{s/m}(f)\big|ds.

Let
|T|
be the linear modulus of
T\in DS
– see, for example,
[
14
, § 4.1]
. Then
|T|\in DS^{+}
and

|T^{k}(f)|\leq|T|^{k}(|f|)\ \ \forall\ f\in\mathcal{L}^{p},

thus, we can write

\begin{split}\big|M_{n/m}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\big|&=\Big|\frac{m}{n}\int_{0}^{\frac{n}{m}}\beta(s)T_{s}(f)ds\Big|\leq 2\,\frac{m}{n}\int_{0}^{\frac{n}{m}}\big|\beta(s)T_{s}(f)\big|ds\\
&=2\,\frac{m}{n}\int_{0}^{\frac{n}{m}}|\beta(s)|\big|T_{s}(f)\big|ds\leq 2C\,\frac{m}{n}\int_{0}^{\frac{n}{m}}\big|T_{s}(f)\big|ds\\
&=\frac{2C}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\big|T_{s/m}(f)\big|ds=\frac{2C}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{k}^{k+1}\big|T_{s/m}(f)\big|ds\\
&=\frac{2C}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{1}\big|T_{(s+k)/m}(f)\big|ds=\frac{2C}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{1}\big|T^{k}_{1/m}(T_{s/m}(f))\big|ds\\
&\leq\frac{2C}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{1}\big|T_{1/m}\big|^{k}\left(\big|T_{s/m}(f)\big|\right)ds\leq 2C\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\big|T_{1/m}\big|^{k}(g)\\
&=2C\,M_{n}\left(\big|T_{1/m}\big|\right)(g),\end{split}

which, by Theorem
2.2
applied to the operator
|T_{1/m}|\in DS^{+}
, entails, as
g\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\mu\left\{\big|M_{n/m}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\big|>\lambda\right\}&\leq\mu\Big\{M_{n}(|T_{1/m}|)(g)>\frac{\lambda}{2C}\Big\}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{4C\,\|g\|_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}\leq\left(\frac{4C\,\|f\|_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}\end{split}

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, hence

\mu\left\{\sup_{0<r\in\mathbb{Q}}\big|M_{r}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\big|>\lambda\right\}\leq\left(\frac{4C\,\|f\|_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}.

To finish the argument, fix
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and
\lambda>0
and denote

f_{t}=\big|M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\big|,\ \ \ E=\Big\{\sup_{0<r\in\mathbb{Q}}f_{r}>\lambda\Big\}.

Then we have

\mu(E)\leq\left(\frac{4C\,\|f\|_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}\text{ \ and \ }\big\|f_{r}\chi_{\Omega\setminus E}\big\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda\ \ \,\forall\ 0<r\in\mathbb{Q}.

If
t>0
and
0<r_{n}\to t
with
\{r_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Q}
, then we have
f_{r_{n}}\to f_{t}
in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, hence in measure. Then, by Proposition
2.4
, there exists a subsequence
\{r_{n_{k}}\}
such that
f_{r_{n_{k}}}\to f
a.u., implying that

\big\|f_{t}\chi_{\Omega\setminus E}\big\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda,

hence

\mu\big\{f_{t}>\lambda\big\}\leq\mu(E)\leq\left(\frac{4C\,\|f\|_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}\ \ \,\forall\ t>0,

which entails the maximal inequality (
1
), hence (
2
), for all
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and
\lambda>0
.
∎

Now, as a corollary of Theorem
3.1
, we will show that the a.u.-convergence set of the net
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)\}_{t>0}
and, in particular, of
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T})\}_{t>0}
, as
t\to\infty
(or as
t\to 0
) is closed in the Banach space
(\mathcal{L}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{p})
for each
1\leq p<\infty
. For this we will utilize the notion of
measure topology
in
\mathcal{L}^{0}
, which is given by the following system of neighborhoods of zero:

\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\delta)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{L}^{0}:\ \mu\{|f|>\delta\}\leq\varepsilon\right\},\ \,\varepsilon>0,\ \delta>0.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space, and let
M_{t}:X\to\mathcal{L}^{0}
,
t>0
, be a family of lenear operators. If the maximal operator
M^{*}:(X,\|\cdot\|)\to(\mathcal{L}^{0},t_{\mu})
is continuous at
0\in X
, then the set

X_{c}=\big\{\,f\in X:\{M_{t}(f)\}\text{\ converges a.u. as}\ \,t\to\infty\text{\ (or as\ }t\to 0)\big\}

is closed in
X
.

Proof.

Assume that
t\to\infty
in the dedinition of
X_{c}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
and let a sequence
\{f_{k}\}\subset X_{c}
and
f\in X
be such that
\|f-f_{k}\|\to 0
. Then the continuity of
M^{*}
at
0\in X
implies there exist
f_{k_{0}}
and
G_{0}\subset\Omega
satisfying conditions

\mu(\Omega\setminus G_{0})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\text{\ \, and\ \, }\Big\|\sup_{t>0}M_{t}(f-f_{k_{0}})\chi_{G_{0}}\Big\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{3}.

Next, since the net
\{M_{t}(f_{k_{0}})\}
converges a.u. as
t\to\infty
, there exist
G_{1}\subset\Omega
and
t_{0}>0
such that

\mu(\Omega\setminus G_{1})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\text{\ \, and\ \, }\big\|\big(M_{t^{\prime}}(f_{k_{0}})-M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(f_{k_{0}})\big)\chi_{G_{1}}\big\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{3}\ \ \ \forall\,\ t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\geq t_{0}.

Now, if we define
G=G_{0}\cap G_{1}
, then
\mu(\Omega\setminus G)\leq\varepsilon
and

\begin{split}\big\|\big(M_{t^{\prime}}(f)-M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(f)\big)\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}&\leq\big\|M_{t^{\prime}}(f-f_{k_{0}})\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}+\big\|M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(f-f_{k_{0}})\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\\
&+\big\|\big(M_{t^{\prime}}(f_{k_{0}})-M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(f_{k_{0}})\big)\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\leq\delta\,\ \ \forall\,\ t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\geq t_{0},\end{split}

thus, the net
\{M_{t}(f)\}_{t>0}
is Cauchy as
t\to\infty
. This, in view of Proposition
2.1
, implies that this net converges a.u. as
t\to\infty
in
\mathcal{L}^{0}
, hence
f\in X_{c}
.

The case
t\to 0
is dealt with in the same manner.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
, the a.u.-convergence set of the net
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)\}_{t>0}
, hence of
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T})\}_{t>0}
, as
t\to\infty
(or as
t\to 0
) is closed in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
C>0
. Then, given
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
, condition
\|f\|_{p}\leq\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{1/p}\delta}{4C}
implies, by Theorem
3.1
, that

\mu\big\{M(\mathcal{T},\beta)^{*}(f)>\delta\big\}\leq\varepsilon,

hence
M(\mathcal{T},\beta)^{*}(f)\in\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\delta)
. Therefore
M(\mathcal{T},\beta)^{*}:(\mathcal{L}^{p},\|\cdot\|_{p})\to(\mathcal{L}^{0},t_{\mu})
is continuous at zero in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, and the statement follows from Proposition
3.1
.
∎

4.
Almost uniform convergence of non-weighted averages in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu)

In this section we aim to establish a.u. convergence of non-weighted ergodic averages with continuous time in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
. For a.e. convergence of such averages, see, for example,
[
22
]
.

We begin with the case of a
flow
, that is, when
t\to\infty
:

Theorem 4.1
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, the averages
M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)
converge a.u. to some
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Pick
h\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
and denote
g=\int_{0}^{1}T_{s}(h)ds
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, one can see that

M_{n}(\mathcal{T})(h)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{1}^{k}(g)=M_{n}(T_{1})(g),\ \,n=1,2,\dots

Therefore, by
[
6
, Teorem 3.5]
, we conclude that
M_{n}(\mathcal{T})(h)\to\widehat{h}
a.u. for some
\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
. Thus, given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
G\subset\Omega
such that
\mu(\Omega\setminus G)\leq\varepsilon
and

\big\|\big(M_{n}(\mathcal{T})(h)-\widehat{h}\big)\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\to 0.

Next, given
t>1
, we have

\begin{split}\left\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(h)-M_{[t]}(\mathcal{T})(h)\right\|_{\infty}&=\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}(h)ds-\frac{1}{[t]}\int_{0}^{[t]}T_{s}(h)ds\right\|_{\infty}\\
&=\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{[t]}^{t}T_{s}(h)ds-\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{[t]}\right)\int_{0}^{[t]}T_{s}(h)ds\right\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq 2\left(1-\frac{[t]}{t}\right)\|h\|_{\infty}\to 0\text{\ \, as\,\ }t\to\infty.\end{split}

Since
\{M_{[t]}(\mathcal{T})(h)\}_{t>1}=\{M_{n}(\mathcal{T})(h)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
as sequences, it follows that

\begin{split}\big\|\big(M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(h)-\widehat{h}\big)\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}&=\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(h)-M_{[t]}(\mathcal{T})(h)\big\|_{\infty}\\
&+\big\|\big(M_{[t]}(\mathcal{T})(h)-\widehat{h}\big)\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\to 0\text{\, \ as\,\ }t\to\infty,\end{split}

and we conclude
M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(h)\to\widehat{h}
a.u. for all
h\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
.

Thus, since the set
\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
is dense in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, an application of Corollary
3.1
yields that for every
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
there exists
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{0}
such that
M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to\widehat{f}
a.u. Then, there is a sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
for which
M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to\widehat{f}
a.u. as
n\to\infty
. Hence, as the sequence
\{M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, Corollary
2.1
implies that
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
.
∎

Next, we shall establish a.u. convergence in the
local ergodic theorem
, that is, when
t\to 0^{+}
:

Theorem 4.2
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, the averages
M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)
converge to
f
as
t\to 0
.

To prove Theorem
4.2
, we will need a local mean ergodic theorem. Let us begin with a lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

If
t_{0}>0
,
g\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, and
f=M_{t_{0}}(g)
, then
\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)-f\big\|_{p}\to 0
as
t\to 0
. In addition, if
g\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, then
\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)-f\big\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
t\to 0
.

Proof.

Let
0<t<t_{0}
. Then we have

\begin{split}\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)&-f\big\|_{p}=\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(T_{s}(f)-f)ds\right\|_{p}\leq\big\|T_{s}(f)-f\big\|_{p}\\
&=\frac{1}{t_{0}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t_{0}}T_{s+t}(g)ds-\int_{0}^{t_{0}}T_{s}(g)ds\right\|_{p}\\
&=\frac{1}{t_{0}}\left\|\int_{t}^{t_{0}+t}T_{s}(g)ds-\int_{0}^{t_{0}}T_{s}(g)ds\right\|_{p}\\
&=\frac{1}{t_{0}}\left\|\int_{t}^{t_{0}}T_{s}(g)ds+\int_{t_{0}}^{t+t_{0}}T_{s}(g)ds-\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}(g)ds-\int_{t}^{t_{0}}T_{s}(g)ds\right\|_{p}\\
&=\frac{1}{t_{0}}\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t+t_{0}}T_{s}(g)ds-\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}(g)ds\right\|_{p}\leq\frac{2t}{t_{0}}\|g\|_{p}\to 0\text{\, \ as\ \,}t\to 0.\end{split}

Proof of the second statement is carried out exactly the same way by simply replacing
L^{1}
- norm by
L^{\infty}
- norm.
∎

Here is the local mean ergodic theorem in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
:

Theorem 4.3
.

If
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, then
\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)-f\big\|_{p}\to 0
as
t\to 0
.

Proof.

Since, by Proposition
2.2
, the semigroup
\{T_{s}\}_{t\geq 0}
is strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, it follows that

\big\|T_{s}(f)-f\|_{p}\to 0\text{\, \ as\,\ }s\to 0.

Therefore, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
t_{n}>0
such that

\big\|T_{s}(f)-f\big\|_{p}<\frac{1}{n}\ \ \,\forall\,\ 0<s\leq t_{0}

hence, denoting
f_{n}=M_{t_{n}}(f)
, we have

\|f_{n}-f\|_{p}=\frac{1}{t_{n}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}(T_{s}(f)-f)ds\right\|_{p}<\frac{1}{n}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose
n_{0}
to be such that

\|f_{n_{0}}-f\|_{p}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

Further, by Lemma
4.1
,

\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f_{n_{0}})-f_{n_{0}}\big\|_{p}\to 0\text{\, \ as\,\ }t\to 0,

thus, there exists
t_{0}>0
such that

\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f_{n_{0}})-f_{n_{0}}\big\|_{p}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ \ \,\forall\,\ 0<t<t_{0}.

Now, we have

\begin{split}\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)-f\big\|_{p}&\leq\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)-M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f_{n_{0}})\big\|_{p}+\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f_{n_{0}})-f_{n_{0}}\big\|_{p}\\
&+\|f_{n_{0}}-f\|_{p}\leq 2\|f_{n_{0}}-f\|_{p}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\varepsilon\end{split}

whenver
0<t<t_{0}
, thus completing the argument.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Let a sequence of real numbers
t_{n}\to 0
. Then, by Theorem
4.3
, the set

\mathcal{D}=\big\{M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})(h):\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,h\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}\big\}

is dense in
\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, hence in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
. Besides, by Lemma
4.1
,

\big\|M_{t_{n}}(g)-g\|_{\infty}\to 0,\text{\ \,hence\,\ }M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})(g)\to g\text{ \ a.u.,\ }\ \forall\ g\in\mathcal{D}.

Now, as Corollary
3.1
implies that the a.u.-convergence set of the averages
\{M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})\}
is closed in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, we conclude that the sequence
\{M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\}
converges a.u. for all
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
. Further, given
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, Theorem
4.3
yields that
M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to f
in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, hence in measure. Therefore, by Proposition
2.4
,
M_{t_{n_{k}}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to f
a.u. for some subsequence
\{t_{n_{k}}\}
, and we conclude that

M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to f\text{ \ a.u. as \ }t_{n}\to 0\ \ \forall\ f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}.

Next, assume that
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
. Then we have
M_{1/n}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to f
a.u., in particular,

M_{[1/t]^{-1}}(f)\to f\text{ \,\ a.u. as\ \,}t\to 0.

So, for a fix
\varepsilon>0
, there exixts
G\subset\Omega
such that
\mu(\Omega\setminus G)\leq\varepsilon
and

\big\|\big(M_{[1/t]^{-1}}(f)-f\big)\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\to 0\text{\, \ as\,\ }t\to 0.

Further,

\begin{split}\left\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)-M_{[1/t]^{-1}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\right\|_{\infty}&=\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}(f)ds-\left[\frac{1}{t}\right]\int_{0}^{[1/t]^{-1}}T_{s}(f)ds\right\|_{\infty}\\
&=\left\|\left(\frac{1}{t}-\left[\frac{1}{t}\right]\right)\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}(f)ds-\left[\frac{1}{t}\right]\int_{t}^{[1/t]^{-1}}T_{s}(f)ds\right\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq 2\left(1-\left[\frac{1}{t}\right]\,t\right)\|f\|_{\infty}\to 0\text{ \ as\ }t\to 0.\\
\end{split}

Therefore, we can write

\begin{split}\big\|\big(M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)-f\big)\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}&\leq\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)-M_{[1/t]^{-1}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\big\|_{\infty}\\
&+\big\|\big(M_{[1/t]^{-1}}(f)-f\big)\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\to 0\text{ \ as\ }t\to 0.\end{split}

Thus, the averages
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T})\}_{t>0}
converge a.u. on the set
\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, which is dense in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
, implying, in view of Corollary
3.1
, that the net
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\}_{t>0}
converges a.u. as
t\to 0
for all
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
. This, in turn, entails that
M_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to f
a.u. as
t\to 0
, in the same way as in the first part of this proof, as we now know that, given
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, there is a sequence
t_{n}\to 0
such that
\{M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\}
converges a.u.
∎

5.
A.u. convergence of the weighted averages in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega,\mu)

Let
\mathbb{C}
be the field of complex numbers, and let
\mathbb{C}_{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
. A function
p:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
trigonomertic polynomial
if
p(s)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\lambda_{j}^{s}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{w_{j}\}_{1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}
, and
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}_{1}
.

A Lebesgue measurable function
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
will be called
bounded Besicovitch
(
locally Besicovitch
)
function
if
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<\infty
, and for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a trigonometric polynomial
p_{\varepsilon}
such that

(3)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big|\beta(s)-p_{\varepsilon}(s)\big|ds<\varepsilon

(respectively,

(4)

\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big|\beta(s)-p_{\varepsilon}(s)\big|ds<\varepsilon\,).

Let
\nu
stand for Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{C}_{1}
and consider the product measure space

(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega,\nu\otimes\mu)=(\mathbb{C}_{1},\nu)\otimes(\Omega,\mu).

As in
[
8
, Lemma 4.7]
), we obtain the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(X,\nu)
and
(Y,\mu)
be
\sigma
-finite measure spaces. If a net
\{g_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}^{0}(X\otimes Y)
is such that
g_{t}\to g
a.u. in
X\otimes Y
as
t\to\infty
or
t\to 0^{+}
, then
g_{t}(x,y)\to g(x,y)
a.u. in
Y
as
t\to\infty
or
t\to 0^{+}
for almost all
x\in X
.

Recall that a semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset DS
is presumed to be strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}=\mathcal{L}^{1}(\Omega)
. Define

f_{z}(\omega)=\widetilde{f}(z,\omega)\text{ \ whenever \ }\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega),

and note that if
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)
, then, by Fubini’s theorem,
f_{z}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}=\mathcal{L}^{1}(\Omega)
for
\nu
-almost all
z\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
.

Next, given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
, define

T^{\lambda}_{s}(\widetilde{f})(z,\omega)=T_{s}(f_{\lambda^{s}z})(\omega)\text{ \ for\ }s\geq 0\text{ \ and \ }\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega).

Then it is easily verified that
\{T^{(\lambda)}_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}\subset DS
on
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)
for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
. Besides, given
r,s\geq 0
, we have

\begin{split}T_{r+s}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})(z,\omega)&=T_{r+s}(f_{\lambda^{r+s}z})(\omega)=T_{r}(T_{s}(f_{\lambda^{s}\lambda^{t}z}))(\omega)=T_{r}(T_{s}^{(\lambda)}(f_{\lambda^{t}z}))(\omega)\\
&=T^{(\lambda)}_{s}(T_{r}(f_{\lambda^{r}z}))(\omega)=T_{r}^{(\lambda)}T_{s}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})(z,\omega),\end{split}

that is,
\{T_{t}^{(\lambda)}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup.

Proposition 5.1
.

The semigroup
\{T_{s}^{(\lambda)}\}_{s\geq 0}
is strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)
for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
.

Proof.

Clearly, it suffices to show that, given
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
, we have

\big\|T_{s_{n}}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})-T_{s}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)}\to 0\text{\, \ as\ \,}s_{n}\searrow s\text{\, \ and as\, \ }s\nearrow s.

If
s_{n}\searrow s
(
s_{n}\nearrow s
), then, as

\big\|T_{s_{n}}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})-T_{s}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)}\leq\big\|T_{s_{n}-s}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})-\widetilde{f}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)}

(respectively, as

\big\|T_{s_{n}}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})-T_{s}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)}\leq\big\|T_{s-s_{n}}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})-\widetilde{f}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)}),

we conclude that it is sufficient to verify that

\big\|T_{s_{n}}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})-\widetilde{f}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)}=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot))-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\to 0\text{\ \, as\ \ }s_{n}\to 0^{+}.

We have

\begin{split}\big\|T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot))&-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}\leq\big\|T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot))-T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot))\big\|_{1}\\
&+\big\|T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot))-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}\\
&\leq\big\|\widetilde{f}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot)-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}+\big\|T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot))-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}.\end{split}

Since
\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}
is strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, it follows that

\big\|T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot))-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}\text{ \ for $\nu$-almost all \ }z\in\mathbb{C}_{1},

which, together with
\big\|T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot))-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}\leq 2\|\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\|_{1}

\nu
-a.e. on
\mathbb{C}_{1}
, implies that

\ \int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|T_{s_{n}}(\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot))-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\to 0\text{ \ as\ }s_{n}\to 0^{+}.

Thus, it remains to verify that

(5)

\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{f}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot)-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\to 0\text{ \ as\ }s_{n}\to 0^{+}.

To that end, let

(6)

\widetilde{h}(z,\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}\chi_{[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{i+1}]}(z),\,\ z\in\mathbb{C}_{1},\ h_{i}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\text{ \ for\,\ }i=1,\dots,m

be a simple Bochner-measurable function, where
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m+1}
, with
\lambda_{m+1}=\lambda_{1}
, is a
partition
of
\mathbb{C}_{1}
. Then, given
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\widetilde{h}(\lambda^{s}z,\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}\chi_{[\lambda^{s}\lambda_{1},\lambda^{s}\lambda_{i+1}]}(z),

where
\{\lambda^{s}\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m+1}
is another partition of
\mathbb{C}_{1}
. Since
\lambda^{s_{n}}\lambda_{i}\to\lambda_{i}
as
s_{n}\to 0
for each
i
, it follows that

\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{h}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot)-\widetilde{h}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big|\chi_{[\lambda^{s_{n}}\lambda_{i},\lambda^{s_{n}}\lambda_{i+1}]}-\chi_{[\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i+1}]}\big|\,\|h_{i}\|_{1}\to 0

as
s_{n}\to 0^{+}
.

Now, as
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)
, one can justify that there exists a sequence
\{\widetilde{h}_{k}(z,\omega)\}
of functions of the form (
6
) for which

\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)-\widetilde{h}_{k}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\to 0\text{\, \ as\,\ }k\to\infty.

Then we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{f}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot)&-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{f}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot)-\widetilde{h}_{k}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{h}_{k}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot)-\widetilde{h}_{k}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{h}_{k}(z,\cdot)-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)=2\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{h}_{k}(z,\cdot)-\widetilde{f}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{C}_{1}}\big\|\widetilde{h}_{k}(\lambda^{s_{n}}z,\cdot)-\widetilde{h}_{k}(z,\cdot)\big\|_{1}d\nu(z)\to 0\text{\, \ as\, \ }s_{n}\to 0^{+}\ \ \forall\ k,\end{split}

and (
5
) follows, completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let a semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}\subset DS
be strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, and let
p(s)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\lambda_{j}^{s}
be a trigonometric polynomial. If
1\leq p<\infty
,
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, and

(7)

P_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}p(s)T_{s}(f)ds,

then

(i)

the net
P_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)
converges a.u. as
t\to\infty
;

(ii)

the net
P_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)
convergs a.u. to
p(0)f
as
t\to 0
.

Proof.

(i) Fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
. In view of Propositions
2.2
and
5.1
,
\{T_{s}^{(\lambda)}\}_{s\geq 0}\subset DS
is a semigroup strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)
. Then, by Theorem
4.1
, given
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)
, the net

(8)

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})ds

converges a.u. as
t\to\infty
. Therefore, by Lemma
5.1
, the net

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}^{(\lambda)}(\widetilde{f})(z,\omega)ds=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}(\widetilde{f}(\lambda^{s}z,\omega))ds

converges a.u. as
t\to\infty

\nu
-a.e. on
\mathbb{C}_{1}
. In particular, letting
\widetilde{f}(z,\omega)=zf(\omega)
, we conclude
that the net

z\,\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{s}T_{s}(f)ds

converges a.u. as
t\to\infty
for some
0\neq z\in\mathbb{C}_{1}
, implying that the net

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{s}T_{s}(x)ds

converges a.u. as
t\to\infty
. Therefore, by linearity, the net
P_{t}(\mathcal{T})(x)
converges a.u. as
t\to\infty
.

(ii) Now, by Theorem
4.3
, given
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{1}\otimes\Omega)
, it follows that the net (
8
)
converges a.u. to
\widetilde{f}
as
t\to 0
. Then, letting
\widetilde{f}(z,\omega)=zf(\omega)
, we see as above that

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{s}T_{s}(f)ds\to f\text{ \ a.u.}

as
t\to 0
, and the result follows by linearity.
∎

Now we are ready to present the main results of this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let a semigroup
\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}\subset DS
be strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, and let
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
be a bounded Besicovitch function with
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<C<\infty
. Then, given
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, the net
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{t>0}
converges a.u. as
t\to\infty
to some
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose a trigonometric polynomial
p=p_{\varepsilon}
to satisfy condition (
3
). Let
\{P_{t}(x)\}_{t>0}
be given by (
7
). Then we have

(9)

\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)-P_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\big\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big|\beta(s)-p(s)\big|ds\,\|f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\,\|f\|_{\infty}

for all big enough values of
t
. Since, by Lemma
5.2
, the averages
P_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)
converge a.u.,
it follows that the net
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{t>0}
is a.u. Cauchy as
t\to\infty
. Therefore, by Proposition
2.1
, this net converges to some
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{0}
, and as in the end of proof of Theorem
4.1
, it follows that
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let a semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset DS
be strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, and let
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
be a bounded locally Besicovitch function with
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<C<\infty
. Then, given
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, the averages
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{t>0}
converge a.u. to
\lambda(f)\,f
as
t\to 0
for some
\lambda(f)\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose a trigonometric polynomial
p=p_{\varepsilon}
to satisfy condition (
4
). If the net
\{P_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\}_{t>0}
is given by (
7
), then (
9
) holds for all small enough positive values of
t
. Since, by Lemma
5.2
, the averages
P_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)
converge a.u. as
t\to 0
, it follows that the net
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{t>0}
is a.u. Cauchy as
t\to 0
. Then, as in Theorem
5.1
, we conclude that for any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
the averages
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{t>0}
converge a.u. to some
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
as
t\to 0
.

Let
p_{n}
be a trigonometric polynomial to satisfy condition (
4
) with
\varepsilon=1/n
, and let
\{P^{(n)}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\}_{t>0}
be the corresponding averages given by (
7
). Then there exists
t_{n}>0
such that

(10)

\big\|M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)-P^{(n)}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\big\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\big|\beta(s)-p_{n}(s)\big|ds\,\|f\|_{p}<\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{n}

holds for all
0<t<t_{n}
.

By Lemma
5.2
,
P^{(n)}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to p_{n}(0)\,f
a.u. as
t\to 0
, implying that there exists a sequence
t_{m}\to 0
such that

M_{t_{m}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)-P^{(n)}_{t_{m}}(\mathcal{T})(f)\to\widehat{f}-p_{n}(0)\,f\text{\ \ a.u. as \ }m\to\infty.

Since, by Corollary
2.1
, the unit ball in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
is sequentially closed with respect to a.u. convergence, (
10
) entails that

\big\|\widehat{f}-p_{n}(0)\,f\big\|_{p}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{n},

hence
\widehat{f}=\|\cdot\|_{p}-\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}p_{n}(0)\,f
, and we conclude that
\widehat{f}=\lambda(f)\,f
for some
\lambda(f)\in\mathbb{C}
, which completes the argument.
∎

6.
Extension to fully symmetric spaces

Now we aim to extend the results of Section 5 to the fully symmetric spaces
E\subset\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
with

\mathbf{1}=\chi_{\Omega}\notin E
.

As before, a semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}\subset DS
is assumed to be strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
. Then, by Proposition
2.3
, if
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
is a Lebesgue measurable function such that
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<C<\infty
, the averages
M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)
are defined for every
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
t>0
. Furthemore, as the operators
C^{-1}\,M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)
contract both
\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
\mathcal{L}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
, we may assume, in view of Theorem
2.1
, that the averages
M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)
are defined for all
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
. Note that then
C^{-1}M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)\in DS
for all
t>0
.

Denote

\mathcal{R}_{\mu}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}:\ \mu\{|f|>\lambda\}<\infty\text{ \ for all \ }\lambda>0\right\},

the Fava’s space – see
[
11
, Section (2.5.5)]
. (One can observe that
\bigcup\limits_{1\leq p<\infty}\mathcal{L}^{p}\subsetneqq\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
.)

As it was shown – see
[
4
,
6
,
15
]
– that the pointwise Dunford-Schwartz ergodic theorem holds for
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
if and only if
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
, we will only be interested in the functions that belong to the Fava’s space
\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
.

We will utilize the following characterization of
\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
– see
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
:

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}+\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
. Then
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
if and only if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exist
g_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
h_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
such that

f=g_{\varepsilon}+h_{\varepsilon}\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon.

Corollary 6.1
.

If
T\in DS
, then
T(\mathcal{R}_{\mu})\subset\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
, hence
M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(\mathcal{R}_{\mu})\subset\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
for every Lebesgue measurable function
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
t>0
.

Let us also note the following – see
[
17
, Theorem 2.3]
, where the proof can be straightforwardly adapted to the current commutative setting:

Proposition 6.2
.

The space
\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
is complete with respect to a.u. convergence.

Theorem 6.1
.

If a semigroup
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}\subset DS
is strongly continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
\beta
is a bounded Besicovitch (locally Besicovitch) function, then for every
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
the averages
M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)
converge to some
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
as
t\to\infty
(respectively, as
t\to 0
).

Proof.

Assume that
\beta
is a bounded Besicovitch function with
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<C<\infty
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
. By Proposition
6.1
, there exist
g\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
and
h\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
such that

f=g+h\text{\, \ and\, \ }\|h\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{3C}.

Since
g\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
, Theorem
5.1
implies that there exists
G\subset\Omega
and
t_{0}>0
such that

\mu(\Omega\setminus G)\leq\varepsilon\text{\, \ and\, \ }\big\|(M_{t^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(g)-M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(g))\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{3}\,\ \ \forall\ t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\geq t_{0}.

Then, given
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\geq t_{0}
, we have

\begin{split}\big\|(M_{t^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)&-M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f))\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\leq\big\|(M_{t^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(g)-M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(g))\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\\
&+\big\|(M_{t^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(h)-M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(h))\chi_{G}\big\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\frac{\delta}{3}+\big\|M_{t^{\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(h)\big\|_{\infty}+\big\|M_{t^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(h)\big\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{3}+2C\,\|h\|_{\infty}\leq\delta.\end{split}

Therefore, the net
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{t>0}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
is a.u. Cauchy, implying, by Proposition
6.2
, that it converges a.u. to some
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
.

Next, let us assume that
\beta
is a bounded locally Besicovitch function. Then, repeating the above argument with Theorem
5.2
instead of Theorem
5.1
, we conclude that the net
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{t>0}
converges a.u. to some
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
as
t\to 0
.
∎

Define

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{0}=\big\{f\in\mathcal{L}^{0}:\,\mu\{|f|>\lambda\}<\infty\text{ \ for some \ }\lambda>0\big\}.

Given
f\in\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{0}
, the
non-increasing rearrangement
of
f
is defined as

f^{*}(t)=\inf\big\{\lambda>0:\,\mu\{|f|>\lambda\}<t\big\},\ \,t>0.

A non-zero linear subspace
E\subset\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{0}
with a Banach norm
\|\cdot\|_{E}
is called
symmetric
(
fully symmetric
) if
g\in E
,
f\in\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{0}
, and

f^{*}(t)\leq g^{*}(t)\ \big(\text{respectively,\ }\int_{0}^{s}f^{*}(t)dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}g^{*}(t)dt\text{\ (in short,\ }f\prec\prec g)\big)\,\ \forall\ s>0

imply that
f\in E
and
\|f\|_{E}\leq\|g\|_{E}
.

Immediate examples of fully symmetric spaces are the spaces
\mathcal{L}^{p}=\mathcal{L}^{p}(\Omega.\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, with the standard norms
\|\cdot\|_{p}
. Further examples include the well-known Orlicz, Lorentz, and Marcinkiewicz spaces – for details, see, for example,
[
6
, Sec. 5]
,
[
8
, Sec. 6]
.

Here is a criterion for a symmetric space to belong to the space
\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
– see
[
8
, Proposition 6.1]
:

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume that
\mu(\Omega)=\infty
. Then a symmetric space
E
is contained in
\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
if and only if
\mathbf{1}\notin E
.

Let us now present an application of Theorem
6.1
to fully symmetric spaces:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
E
be a fully symmetric space such that
\mathbf{1}\notin E
, and let
\mathcal{T}
and
\beta(s)
be as in Theorem
6.1
. Then, given
f\in E
, the averages
M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)
converge a.u. to some
\widehat{f}\in E
as
t\to\infty
(respectively, as
t\to 0
).

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{1}\notin E
, it follows that
E\subset\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
. Therefore, by Theorem
6.1
, the averages
\{M_{t}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{t>0}
converge a.u. to some
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{R}_{\tau}
as
t\to\infty
(respectively, as
t\to 0
), so, it remains to show that
\widehat{f}\in E
, for which we will follow the argument of that of
[
8
, Theorem 6.2]
,

Let
\{t_{n}\}
be a sequence such that the averages
\{M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converge a.u. when
t_{n}\to\infty
(respectively,
t_{n}\to 0
). If
C
is such that
\|\beta\|_{\infty}<C
, then
C^{-1}M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)\in DS
for every
n
, implying, by
[
13
, Ch. II, § 3, Sec. 4]
, that

(11)

C^{-1}M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\prec\prec f\,\ \ \forall\ n.

Next, as
M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\to\widehat{f}
also in measure, we observe, by
[
13
, Ch. II, § 2,
11^{0}
]
, that

\big(C^{-1}M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\big)^{*}\to\big(C^{-1}\widehat{f}\,\big)^{*}\text{ \ \ a.e. on \ }(0,s)\text{ \ as \ }n\to\infty\ \ \forall\ s>0.

Therefore, it follows from Fatou’s lemma and (
11
) that

\int_{0}^{s}\big(C^{-1}\widehat{f}\,\big)^{*}dt\leq\liminf_{n}\int_{0}^{s}\big(C^{-1}M_{t_{n}}(\mathcal{T},\beta)(f)\big)^{*}dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}f^{*}dt\ \ \ \forall\ s>0,

hence
(\widehat{f}\,)^{*}\prec\prec C\,f^{*}
. Since
E
is a fully symmetric space and
f\in E
, we conclude that
\widehat{f}\in E
.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

One may observe that all the above arguments remain applicable if the net
\{t\}_{t>0}
is replaced by an arbitrary net
\{t_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\subset(0,\infty)
with the directed set
\Lambda
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\in\Lambda}t_{\alpha}=\infty
, or
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\in\Lambda}t_{\alpha}=0
.

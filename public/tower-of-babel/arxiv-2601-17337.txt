Coulomb equilibrium in the external field of an attractive-repellent pair of charges
R. Orive, F. Wielonsky

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to provide a complete analysis of the Coulomb equilibrium problem in the euclidean space ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , associated to the kernel 1 / | x | d − 2 1/|x|^{d-2} , with a non-convex external field created by an attractive-repellent pair of charges placed in ℝ d + 1 ∖ ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}^{d} . We consider the admissible setting, where the equilibrium measure is compactly supported, as well as the limiting weakly admissible setting, with a weaker external field at infinity, where the existence of the equilibrium measure still holds but possibly with an unbounded support. The main tools for our analysis are the notions of signed equilibrium and balayage of measures. We note that for certain configurations of charges and distances to the conductor, the support of the equilibrium measure is a shell (multidimensional annulus).

Introduction

We study the problem of minimizing the Coulomb energy of a probability measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, that is the Coulomb equilibrium, in the radial and non-convex external field created by a pair
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},-\gamma_{2})
of positive/negative charges in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, located respectively at
H_{1}=(0,h_{1})
and
H_{2}=(0,h_{2})
, at some heights
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
above the origin of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

More precisely, we look for the equilibrium measure
\mu_{Q}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
minimizing the weighted energy
I^{Q}(\mu)
of a probability measure
\mu
, given by

I^{Q}(\mu)=\begin{cases}{\displaystyle\iint\frac{d\mu(x)d\mu(t)}{|x-t|^{d-2}}+\int Q(x)d\mu(x),\;Q(x)=\frac{\gamma_{1}}{|x-H_{1}|^{d-2}}-\frac{\gamma_{2}}{|x-H_{2}|^{d-2}},\;d\geq 3,}\\[10.0pt]
{\displaystyle\iint\log\frac{1}{|x-t|}d\mu(x)d\mu(t)+\int Q(x)d\mu(x),\;Q(x)=\log\frac{|x-H_{2}|^{\gamma_{2}}}{|x-H_{1}|^{\gamma_{1}}},\quad d=2,}\end{cases}

where
|\cdot|
denotes the euclidean distance in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
or
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
. Note that, when
d=2
, Coulomb interaction is given by the logarithmic kernel
\log(1/|x|)
.

We need to assume that the confinement exerted by the external field
Q
is sufficiently strong, namely that the strength of the negative charge is sufficiently larger than the one of the positive charge,
which translates into the inequality
\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}\leq-1
. This condition ensures existence and uniqueness of a solution,
see
[
7
, Corollary 2.9]
. The limiting case
\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}=-1
corresponds to the so-called weakly admissible setting, which was also considered in previous studies, and in different contexts, see e.g.
[
16
,
9
,
2
,
14
,
7
]
.

Our method of proof is based on the notion of signed equilibrium measure, see Section
2
for a precise definition, that was introduced originally in
[
10
]
for the logarithmic kernel in the complex plane, and which has been used recently in more general contexts, see e.g.
[
14
,
7
,
8
]
.
This method could be applied with a general distribution of masses along the vertical axis, instead of two single charges, but then with less explicit results.

Though the above problem can be seen as a problem taking place in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, we consider Coulomb interaction with respect to the
d
-dimensional space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We note that the concrete electrostatic problem in physics, posed in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, actually corresponds to the Riesz potential
1/|x|^{d-1}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Probably, the most interesting situation in our analysis is that in which the support of the equilibrium measure is a shell (see Theorem 1 part (ii), below). As it is well known (see e.g.
[
3
]
), the (unweighted) equilibrium measure of a shell is supported on its outer boundary for the Coulomb setting. In the present paper, we find situations where the support of the weighted equilibrium measure is a full annulus.
On the other hand, for the strictly subharmonic Riesz setting, when
\max(0,d-2)<s<d
, the unweighted equilibrium measure of a shell occupies the whole of it, but an explicit
expression of its density is unknown (see
[
6
]
or
[
5
]
for some classical approaches to the electrostatic problem on a planar ring domain). More recently, it was conjectured in
[
8
]
that the support of the equilibrium measure of a ball in a certain external field is a shell; but there it was only proved for the one-dimensional case.
It is also worth to mention that in
[
1
, Theorem I.1]
it is proved that for a radial external field
Q(x)=Q(|x|)
, which is convex in the radial direction, the support has to be an annulus (including the ball as a particular case); while in
[
1
, Theorem II.9]
a formula for the density of the equilibrium measure in a radial external field is given in terms of a complicated Fredholm integral equation of the second kind.
Finally, we note that
the consideration of the Coulomb kernel allows us to derive explicit formulas for all of the quantites under study.

Our main results are given in the following two theorems. First we consider admissible external fields
Q
such that
-\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{1}<-1
.

Theorem 1
.

i) Assume

\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1}\geq(h_{2}/h_{1})^{d}
. Then the equilibrium measure
\mu_{Q}
is supported on the ball
B_{R_{s}}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,|x|\leq R_{s}\}
, whose radius
R_{s}
satisfies the equation

\frac{\gamma_{2}}{(1+(h_{2}/R_{s})^{2})^{d/2}}-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{(1+(h_{1}/R_{s})^{2})^{d/2}}=1\quad\iff\quad\begin{cases}R_{s}^{d-1}Q^{\prime}(R_{s})=d-2,\quad d\geq 3,\\[5.0pt]
R_{s}Q^{\prime}(R_{s})=1,\quad d=2.\end{cases}

(1.1)

It has a continuous density with respect to the Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, given by

\frac{d\mu_{Q}}{dx}(x)=\frac{d}{|S^{d-1}|}\left(\frac{\gamma_{2}h_{2}^{2}}{(|x|^{2}+h_{2}^{2})^{d/2+1}}-\frac{\gamma_{1}h_{1}^{2}}{(|x|^{2}+h_{1}^{2})^{d/2+1}}\right),\qquad|x|\leq R_{s},

(1.2)

where

denotes the surface area of the
(d-1)
-dimensional unit sphere.

ii) Assume
\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1}<(h_{2}/h_{1})^{d}

\mu_{Q}

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,R_{0}\leq|x|\leq R_{s}\},

with outer radius

given by (
1.1

\left(\frac{R_{0}^{2}+h_{1}^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}+h_{2}^{2}}\right)^{d}=\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{2}\quad\iff\quad Q^{\prime}(R_{0})=0.

(1.3)

When

1.2
) vanishes at the origin.

Note that when
h_{2}<h_{1}

Theorem 3
.

Assume a single negative charge of mass
-\gamma\leq-1

Preliminaries

In the sequel, we denote by

–
B_{R}^{d}
, or simply
B_{R}
, the closed
d
-dimensional ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of radius
R
,

–
E_{R}
the closure of the complement of
B_{R}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(where
E
stands for “exterior”),

–
S_{R}^{d}
, or simply
S_{R}
, the
d
-dimensional sphere of radius
R
, written
S^{d}
or
S
when
R=1
,

–
|S^{d}|
the surface area of the
d
-dimensional unit sphere,

–
d\sigma_{R}^{d-1}
(or
d\sigma_{R}
) the surface measure on
S_{R}^{d-1}
,

–
d\widetilde{\sigma}_{R}^{d-1}
(or
d\widetilde{\sigma}_{R}
) the normalized surface measure on
S_{R}^{d-1}
,

–
\mu_{Q,R}
the equilibrium measure on
B_{R}
with external field
Q
,

–
\eta_{Q,R}
the signed equilibrium measure on
B_{R}
with external field
Q
, see Definition
5
below for that notion.

The potential of a measure
\mu
is defined as

U^{\mu}(x)=\int\frac{d\mu(t)}{|x-t|^{d-2}},\quad d\geq 3,\qquad U^{\mu}(x)=\int\log\frac{1}{|x-t|}d\mu(t),\quad d=2,

and its energy by

I(\mu)=\int U^{\mu}(x)d\mu(x).

The infimum of
I(\mu)
among all probability measures
\mu
supported on a closed set
\Sigma
is the energy, denoted
W(\Sigma)
, of the set
\Sigma
. Its capacity is defined by

{\rm cap}\,(\Sigma)=W(\Sigma)^{-1},\quad d\geq 3,\qquad{\rm cap}\,(\Sigma)=e^{-W(\Sigma)},\quad d=2.

When it exists, the equilibrium measure
\mu_{Q}
, minimizing the weighted energy
I^{Q}(\mu)
for a general external field
Q
on a closed set
\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of positive capacity, is unique and characterized by the Frostman inequalities, see e.g.
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
,

\displaystyle U^{\mu_{Q}}(x)+Q(x)

\displaystyle\geq F_{Q},\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{q.e. on}}\;\Sigma,

(2.1)

\displaystyle U^{\mu_{Q}}(x)+Q(x)

\displaystyle\leq F_{Q},\quad x\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\mu_{Q}),

(2.2)

where q.e. is an abbreviation for quasi-everywhere, i.e. outside of a set of capacity zero, or equivalently a set of infinite energy. The finite constant

F_{Q}=I(\mu_{Q})+\int Qd\mu_{Q},

is referred to as the equilibrium constant or the (modified) Robin constant.

We recall the notion of
balayage
of a measure, see
[
12
, Chapter IV, §1-2]
, and
[
15
, Theorems II.4.1 and II.4.4]
when
d=2
. Given
a closed set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and a positive measure
\sigma

of finite total mass
,
there exists a positive measure, which we denote
Bal(\sigma,F)
, the balayage of
\sigma
onto
F
, satisfying
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(Bal(\sigma,F))\subset\partial F
and

\displaystyle U^{Bal(\sigma,F)}(x)

\displaystyle=U^{\sigma}(x)+C\text{ q.e.\ on }F,

(2.3)

\displaystyle U^{Bal(\sigma,F)}(x)

\displaystyle\leq U^{\sigma}(x)+C\text{ q.e.\ on }{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

(2.4)

where
C=0
when
d\geq 3
or
d=2
and
F
has a bounded complement.
If
d=2
, the balayage preserves the total mass of the measure, but for
d\geq 3
, in general, a mass loss occurs (for instance if
F
is bounded, see e.g.
[
7
]
and references therein).

We now introduce one of the main tools for our analysis, namely the notion of signed equilibrium measure. It is a variation of the usual equilibrium measure, where the positivity of the measure is relaxed while the constraint on its potential is tightened. It was originally introduced in
[
10
]
for the logarithmic potential in the complex plane.

Definition 5
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. A
signed equilibrium measure
for
\Sigma
in the external field
Q
is a finite signed measure
\eta_{Q,\Sigma}
, with finite energy, supported on
\Sigma
such that
\eta_{Q,\Sigma}(\Sigma)=1
and there exists a finite constant
C_{Q,\Sigma}
such that

U^{\eta_{Q,\Sigma}}(x)+Q(x)=C_{Q,\Sigma}\quad\text{q.e. on }\Sigma.

(2.5)

It is known that, when it exists, the signed equilibrium measure is unique, see e.g.
[
4
, Lemma 23]
.
The next proposition describes some of its important properties.
The first two items are taken from

[
7
, Lemma 3.15]
. The third and fourth items are new. They are variations of the third item in that reference.

Proposition 6
.

Let
Q
be a weight on
\Sigma
, a closed subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

of positive capacity
.
Assume an equilibrium measure
\mu_{Q,\Sigma}
and a signed equilibrium measure
\eta_{Q,\Sigma}
exist. Denote by
\eta_{Q,\Sigma}^{+}
the positive part in the Jordan decomposition of
\eta_{Q,\Sigma}
.
Then,

(i) One has

\mu_{Q,\Sigma}\leq\eta_{Q,\Sigma}^{+},\quad\text{and, in particular, }\quad\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\mu_{Q,\Sigma})\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\eta_{Q,\Sigma}^{+}).

(2.6)

(ii)
Let
\Sigma_{1}
be a closed subset of
\Sigma
that admits a signed equilibrium measure
\eta_{Q,\Sigma_{1}}
for which
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\mu_{Q,\Sigma})\subset\Sigma_{1}
. If
\eta_{Q,\Sigma_{1}}
is a positive measure, then
\mu_{Q,\Sigma}=\eta_{Q,\Sigma_{1}}
.

(iii) Let
\Sigma={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
Q
be a radial external field such that
\eta_{Q,\Sigma}
is negative near infinity. Assume that there exists an
R_{0}>0
such that, for each
R>R_{0}
, the signed equilibrium measure
\eta_{Q,R}
is negative on
its boundary
S_{R}
, i.e.
\eta_{Q,R}(S_{R})<0
.
Then
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\mu_{Q})\subset B_{R_{0}}
.

(iv) Let
\Sigma={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
Q
be a radial external field such that
\eta_{Q,\Sigma}
is negative near the origin. Assume that there exists an
R_{0}>0
such that, for each
R<R_{0}
, the signed equilibrium measure
\eta_{Q,E_{R}}
is negative on
its boundary
S_{R}
, i.e.
\eta_{Q,E_{R}}(S_{R})<0
.
Then
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\mu_{Q})\subset E_{R_{0}}
.

Proof.

For a proof of i) and ii) see
[
7
, Lemma 3.15]
.

(iii): In view of (ii), the support of
\mu_{Q}
is a compact set. Assume its outer boundary is a sphere
S_{R}
with
R_{0}<R
. Then,
\mu_{Q}=\mu_{Q,{R}}\leq\eta_{Q,{R}}^{+}
and thus
\mu_{Q}(S_{R})=0
since
\eta_{Q,{R}}(S_{R})<0
. Since
\mu_{Q}(V)>0
for any neighborhood
V
of
S_{R}
, there exists
R_{0}<R^{\prime}<R
such that
S_{R^{\prime}}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits\mu_{Q}
. Then,

\mu_{Q,R^{\prime}}=Bal(\mu_{Q},B_{R^{\prime}})+(1-\|Bal(\mu_{Q},B_{R^{\prime}})\|)\widetilde{\sigma}_{R^{\prime}}

since
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits Bal(\mu_{Q},B_{R^{\prime}})=\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits\mu_{Q}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}

satisfies the Frostman inequalities (

)-(

) on

where the last equality comes from the assumption since

. We get a contradiction which shows that

\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits\mu_{Q}\subset B_{R_{0}}
.

(iv): Now, in view of (ii), the support of
\mu_{Q}
does not contain the origin. Assume its inner boundary is a sphere

with

. Then,
\mu_{Q}=\mu_{Q,E_{R}}\leq\eta_{Q,E_{R}}^{+}

\mu_{Q}(S_{R})=0

. Since

, there exists

\mu_{Q,E_{R^{\prime}}}=Bal(\mu_{Q},E_{R^{\prime}})

. Note that (

where the first inequality comes from the fact that

has some mass outside of

, and the last equality comes from the assumption since

We will need an expression for the energy

of the unit sphere

Lemma 7
.

Let
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d+1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

y_{d+1}\not=0
. Define
the weak balayage

of
\delta_{y}

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as the measure of mass 1, given by

dBal({\delta}_{y},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})(x)=\frac{(2|y_{d+1}|)^{2}}{|S^{d}|W(S^{d})|x-y|^{d+2}}dx=\frac{d|y_{d+1}|^{2}}{|S^{d-1}||x-y|^{d+2}}dx,

(2.9)

where
dx
denotes the Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Then
U^{Bal({\delta}_{y},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=U^{\delta_{y}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Note that the ambient space in the above lemma is
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
for which the kernel
1/|x|^{d-2}
, we are interested in, is not the Coulomb kernel. This is why we just call
Bal({\delta}_{y},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
a weak balayage.

We complement this lemma with the case
d=2
and the log kernel.

Lemma 8
.

Let
y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with
y_{3}\not=0
.
The weak balayage
Bal(\delta_{y},{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
of
\delta_{y}
onto
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a measure of mass 1, given by

dBal({\delta}_{y},{\mathbb{R}}^{2})(x)=\frac{|y_{3}|^{2}}{|x-y|^{4}}\frac{dx}{\pi}.

(2.10)

Note that (
2.10
) is consistent with (
2.9
).

Proof.

We prove that the potentials of
\delta_{y}
and
Bal({\delta}_{y},{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
coincide on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Assume
y=(0,0,1)
for simplicity. From the radial symmetry, it is sufficent to estimate the potential of the balayage at some
(R,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We get

-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\log|(R,0)-t|}{(1+|t|^{2})^{2}}\frac{dt}{\pi}=2\int_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{rU^{\widetilde{\sigma}_{r}}(R)}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}{dr}.

Recalling that

U^{\widetilde{\sigma}_{r}}(z)=\log 1/|z|\quad\text{if }|z|>r,\qquad U^{\widetilde{\sigma}_{r}}(z)=\log 1/r\quad\text{if }|z|\leq r,

we obtain

\displaystyle 2\int_{r=0}^{R}

\displaystyle\frac{r\log(1/R)}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}{dr}+2\int_{r=R}^{\infty}\frac{r\log(1/r)}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}{dr}=-\frac{R^{2}\log(R)}{(1+R^{2})}-\frac{\log(R)}{(1+R^{2})}-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+1/R^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{1+R^{2}}=U^{\delta_{y}}(R).\qed

We also need a formula for the balayage of a point mass in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
onto a ball.

Lemma 9
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|u|\neq R
. Then

\displaystyle Bal(\delta_{u},B_{R}^{c})(s)=\frac{(R^{2}-|u|^{2})d\sigma_{R}(s)}{R|u-s|^{d}|S^{d-1}|},\qquad|u|<R,

\displaystyle Bal(\delta_{u},B_{R})(s)=\frac{(|u|^{2}-R^{2})d\sigma_{R}(s)}{R|u-s|^{d}|S^{d-1}|},\qquad|u|>R.

Proof.

The expression for
Bal(\delta_{u},B_{R}^{c})
when
|u|<R
follows from Poisson’s formula in
B_{R}
. Indeed, for
|x|>R
,

\frac{1}{|x-u|^{d-2}}=\int\frac{1}{|x-s|^{d-2}}\frac{R^{2}-|u|^{2}}{R|u-s|^{d}}\frac{d\sigma_{R}(s)}{|S^{d-1}|}.

The expression when
|u|>R
can be derived by making use of the Kelvin transform
x\mapsto(R^{2}/|x|^{2})x
.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Then

\frac{1}{|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{d\sigma(s)}{|s-Re_{1}|^{d}}=\begin{cases}1/[R^{d-2}(R^{2}-1)]\quad&\text{ if }R>1,\\[5.0pt]
1/(1-R^{2})\quad&\text{ if }R<1.\end{cases}

Proof.

When
d=2
, the integral is easily computed by applying Cauchy’s formula.

When
d\geq 3
, denote by
I
the left-hand side expression to be computed.
Assume first
R>1
. By harmonicity of
s\mapsto|s-Re_{1}|^{2-d}
,

\frac{1}{|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{d\sigma(s)}{|s-Re_{1}|^{d-2}}=R^{2-d},

and, by differentiating with respect to
R
, we get

(2-d)R^{1-d}=\frac{2-d}{|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{(R-<s,e_{1}>)d\sigma(s)}{|s-Re_{1}|^{d}}=(2-d)(RI-J),\\[5.0pt]
J:=\frac{1}{|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{<s,e_{1}>d\sigma(s)}{|s-Re_{1}|^{d}}.

Moreover,

(1+R^{2})I-2RJ=\frac{1}{|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{(1-2R<s,e_{1}>+R^{2})d\sigma(s)}{|s-Re_{1}|^{d}}\\[5.0pt]
=\frac{1}{|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{d\sigma(s)}{|s-Re_{1}|^{d-2}}=R^{2-d}.

Combining the two equations, we get

(1-R^{2})I=R^{2-d}-2R^{2-d}=-R^{2-d}

and the result follows. If
R<1
, we may use the Kelvin transform
K:x\mapsto x/|x|^{2}
, which satisfies
|x-y|=|K(x)-K(y)||x||y|
and whose jacobian has determinant 1 in absolute value on
S^{d-1}
.
∎

Proofs

This section is devoted to the proofs of the main results. As said above, our main tool is the signed equilibrium measure. We start with some results about it.

Proposition 11
.

The signed equilibrium measure
\eta_{R}
for the ball
B_{R}
,
R>0
, is made of two components, a continuous part supported on
B_{R}
and a singular part supported on
S_{R}
, namely

\eta_{R}(x)=\frac{dg_{c}(|x|)}{|S^{d-1}|}dx+g_{s}(R)d\widetilde{\sigma}_{R}(s),

(3.1)

with

g_{c}(r)=\frac{\gamma_{2}h_{2}^{2}}{(r^{2}+h_{2}^{2})^{d/2+1}}-\frac{\gamma_{1}h_{1}^{2}}{(r^{2}+h_{1}^{2})^{d/2+1}},\qquad g_{s}(R)=1-\frac{\gamma_{2}R^{d}}{(R^{2}+h_{2}^{2})^{d/2}}+\frac{\gamma_{1}R^{d}}{(R^{2}+h_{1}^{2})^{d/2}},

(3.2)

where the indices
c
and
s
in the functions
g_{c}
and
g_{s}
stand for “continuous” and “singular”. Note that the density
g_{c}(r)
is independent from
R
.

Proof.

1) The restriction of
Bal(\delta_{y},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
B_{R}
is

\frac{d|y_{d+1}|^{2}}{{|S^{d-1}|}|x-y|^{d+2}}dx.

2) The balayage of the restriction of
Bal(\delta_{y},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
B_{R}^{c}
onto
B_{R}
is supported onto
S_{R}^{d-1}
, and applying the
superposition principle

[
12
, Eq. (4.5.6)]
, it is equal to

\frac{d|y_{d+1}|^{2}}{R|S^{d-1}|}\left(\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{1}{|u-y|^{d+2}}\frac{|u|^{2}-R^{2}}{|u-s|^{d}}du\right)\frac{d\sigma_{R}(s)}{|S^{d-1}|}.

(3.3)

Because of radial symmetry the integral does not depend on
s
and we may choose e.g.
s=Re_{1}
with
e_{1}
the first vector of the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Then, with Lemma
10
, the integral becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\rho=R}^{\infty}\frac{(\rho^{2}-R^{2})\rho^{d-1}}{(\rho^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2+1}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{S^{d-1}}\frac{d\sigma(s)}{|\rho s-Re_{1}|^{d}}\right)d\rho=|S^{d-1}|\int_{\rho=R}^{\infty}\frac{(\rho^{2}-R^{2})\rho^{d-1}}{(\rho^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2+1}\rho^{d-2}(\rho^{2}-R^{2})}d\rho

\displaystyle=|S^{d-1}|\int_{\rho=R}^{\infty}\frac{\rho}{(\rho^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2+1}}d\rho=\frac{|S^{d-1}|}{d(R^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2}}

so that (
3.3
) equals

\frac{|y_{d+1}|^{2}}{R|S^{d-1}|(R^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2}}d\sigma_{R}(s).

3) The mass of
Bal(\delta_{y},B_{R})
is

\frac{U^{\omega_{B_{R}}}(y)}{W(B_{R})}=\frac{R^{d-2}}{R^{d-1}|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S_{R}^{d-1}}\frac{d\sigma_{R}(x)}{(|x|^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2-1}}=\frac{R^{d-2}}{(R^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2-1}}.

Hence, taking into account the above-mentioned mass loss of the balayage for Riesz potentials, the signed equilibrium measure on
B_{R}
corresponding to a Dirac mass
\gamma
at
y=(0,y_{d+1})
equals

-\frac{d|y_{d+1}|^{2}\gamma}{{|S^{d-1}|}|x-y|^{d+2}}dx-\frac{|y_{d+1}|^{2}\gamma}{R|S^{d-1}|(R^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2}}d\sigma_{R}(s)\\
+\left(1+\frac{\gamma R^{d-2}}{(R^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2-1}}\right)\frac{d\sigma_{R}(s)}{R^{d-1}|S^{d-1}|}.

In terms of
d\widetilde{\sigma}_{R}
, we get

-\frac{dy_{d+1}^{2}\gamma}{{|S^{d-1}|}(|x|^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2+1}}dx+\left(1+\frac{\gamma R^{d}}{(R^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2}}\right)d\widetilde{\sigma}_{R}(s).

The formula (
3.1
) for the signed equilibrium measure corresponding to the pair of charges
(\gamma_{1},-\gamma_{2})
, respectively at heights
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
above
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, follows immediately.
∎

To continue our analysis, we need a few properties of the functions
g_{c}
and
g_{s}
introduced in (
3.2
).

Lemma 12
.

The functions
g_{c}
and
g_{s}
satisfy the following properties:

1) When
r=0
,

g_{c}(0)=\frac{\gamma_{2}}{h_{2}^{d}}-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{h_{1}^{d}}\geq 0\quad\iff\quad\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\geq\left(\frac{h_{2}}{h_{1}}\right)^{d},\qquad g_{s}(0)=1.

2) When
r\to\infty
,

g_{c}(r)\simeq\frac{\gamma_{2}h_{2}^{2}-\gamma_{1}h_{1}^{2}}{r^{d+2}}\geq 0\quad\iff\quad\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}}\geq\left(\frac{h_{1}}{h_{2}}\right)^{2},\qquad g_{s}(r)\simeq 1-\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{1}\leq 0.

3) The function
g_{c}
has exactly one positive zero
r_{c}
when

h_{2}<h_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad(h_{2}/h_{1})^{d}<\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1}<(h_{1}/h_{2})^{2},

or

h_{1}<h_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad(h_{1}/h_{2})^{2}<\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1}<(h_{2}/h_{1})^{d},

one zero at 0 when
\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1}=(h_{2}/h_{1})^{d}
,
and no zero otherwise.
The zero
r_{c}
satisfies

r_{c}^{2}=\frac{\alpha h_{1}^{2}-h_{2}^{2}}{1-\alpha}\qquad\text{with}\quad\alpha:=\left(\frac{\gamma_{2}h_{2}^{2}}{\gamma_{1}h_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2/(d+2)}.

(3.4)

The function
g_{s}
has precisely one positive zero
R_{s}
when
1-\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{1}<0
which is the unique positive real number satisfying

\frac{\gamma_{2}}{(1+(h_{2}/R_{s})^{2})^{d/2}}-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{(1+(h_{1}/R_{s})^{2})^{d/2}}=1.

(3.5)

In the limit case
1-\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{1}=0
,
g_{s}
has still one zero
R_{s}
when
1<\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1}<(h_{1}/h_{2})^{2}
, and no zero when
\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1}\geq(h_{1}/h_{2})^{2}
. From 2) we also have that
g_{s}(r)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.

Proof.

1) and 2) follows from the definitions of
g_{c}
and
g_{s}
.

3) Solving
g_{c}(r)=0
leads to

\frac{r^{2}+h_{2}^{2}}{r^{2}+h_{1}^{2}}=\alpha

which is equivalent to (
3.4
). It is then easy to check the statements about the existence of the zero
r_{c}
.

For the function
g_{s}
, we have
g_{s}^{\prime}(0)=0
and it is easy to see that

g_{s}^{\prime}(r)=-dr^{d-1}g_{c}(r).

Hence
g_{s}^{\prime}
has at most 1 zero in
(0,\infty)
. Together with the sign change of
g_{s}
between 0 and
\infty
, it implies that
g_{s}
has exactly one zero in
(0,\infty)
when
1-\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{1}<0
and one or no zero when

, as stated in item 3). The equation (
3.5
) follows directly from the expression for
g_{s}
.
∎

\text{Case }A_{2}:h_{2}<h_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad(h_{1}/h_{2})^{2}\leq\gamma_{2}/\gamma_{1},\\

(3.6)

.

Assume

has a positive zero

Proof of Theorems

.

We first observe that in all of the four cases
A_{1},A_{2},B,C
, it holds that
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits\mu_{Q}\subset B_{R_{s}}

) and the fact that

–
Cases

: The equilibrium problem
\mu_{Q}
of our problem equals the signed equilibrium measure
\eta_{Q,R_{s}}
on

. Indeed,

\eta_{Q,{R_{s}}}
is a positive measure and
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits\mu_{Q}\subset B_{R_{s}}
so that item (ii) of Proposition
6
applies. Note that in case
A_{2}
, when
1-\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{1}=0
, one has
R_{s}=\infty
and the support of the equilibrium measure is the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

–
Case B

g_{c}
is negative near the origin. Hence, we consider the balayage of the continuous part
dg_{c}(|x|)/|S^{d-1}|dx
of the signed equilibrium measure
\eta_{Q,R_{s}}
outside a ball of radius
R<R_{s}
. For a single unit charge at

S_{R}

\frac{d|y_{d+1}|^{2}}{R|S^{d-1}|}\left(\int_{B_{R}}\frac{1}{|u-y|^{d+2}}\frac{R^{2}-|u|^{2}}{|u-s|^{d}}du\right)\frac{d\sigma_{R}(s)}{|S^{d-1}|},

which is an analog of the measure (

) and with Lemma

\frac{R}{(R^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2}}\frac{d\sigma_{R}(s)}{|S^{d-1}|}=\frac{R^{d}}{(R^{2}+y_{d+1}^{2})^{d/2}}d\widetilde{\sigma}_{R}(s).

Remark 14
.

As shown above, the signed equilibrium measure
\eta_{Q,R}
used to solve the problem has a singular part on the boundary of the ball (or two, one on each boundary, in the case where the support is a shell). It plays an essential role in determining the support of the equilibrium measure
\mu_{Q}
. However, these singular measures, supported on spheres, which are typical for the unweighted equilibrium problems in the Coulomb case, do not appear in the expression of
\mu_{Q}
. We can thus think of them as “ghost singular measures”.

Remark 15
.

[
13
, Proposition 2.13]
for
d\geq 3
and
[
15
, Theorem IV.6.1]
for
d=2
with the log kernel, state the following. Assume that
Q(|x|)=Q(r)
is a radial field satisfying one of the conditions:

1)
r^{d-1}Q^{\prime}(r)
is increasing on
(0,\infty)
,

2)
Q(r)
is convex on
(0,\infty)
,

and is such that
\mu_{Q}
exists.
Let
r_{0}
be the smallest number for which
Q^{\prime}(r)>0
for all
r>r_{0}
, and
R_{0}
the
smallest solution of
R^{d-1}Q^{\prime}(R)=\max(1,d-2)
.
Then the support of
\mu_{Q}
is the shell
\{r_{0}\leq|x|\leq R_{0}\}
(or ball if
r_{0}=0
).
Moreover,
\mu_{Q}
is given by

d\mu_{Q}(x)=\frac{1}{\max(1,d-2)}(r^{d-1}Q^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}drd\widetilde{\sigma}(\overline{x}),\qquad x=r\overline{x},~r=|x|.

Condition 1) above is actually satisfied for our external field
Q
for some values of the parameters
h_{1},h_{2},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
. Indeed,

Q^{\prime}(r)=\max(1,d-2)r\left(\gamma_{2}\,(r^{2}+h_{2}^{2})^{-d/2}-\gamma_{1}\,(r^{2}+h_{2}^{2})^{-d/2}\right),

and

(r^{d-1}\,Q^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}=\max(1,d-2)dr^{d-1}\left(\frac{\gamma_{2}h_{2}^{2}}{(r^{2}+h_{2}^{2})^{d/2+1}}-\frac{\gamma_{1}h_{1}^{2}}{(r^{2}+h_{1}^{2})^{d/2+1}}\right),

which is positive on
(0,\infty)
when

F(r):=\frac{r^{2}+h_{1}^{2}}{r^{2}+h_{2}^{2}}>\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}h_{1}^{2}}{\gamma_{2}h_{2}^{2}}\right)^{2/(d+2)}.

Since
F(r)
is a monotonic function of
r
, the above inequality is satisfied on
(0,\infty)
if and only if

\min(F(0),F(\infty))=\min(1,(h_{1}/h_{2})^{2})\geq\left(\frac{\gamma_{1}h_{1}^{2}}{\gamma_{2}h_{2}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{d+2}}~\iff~\max\left(\left(\frac{h_{1}}{h_{2}}\right)^{2},\left(\frac{h_{2}}{h_{1}}\right)^{d}\right)\leq\frac{\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{1}},

or, equivalently, when
\left({\gamma_{1}}/{\gamma_{2}}\right)^{1/d}\leq{h_{1}}/{h_{2}}\leq\left({\gamma_{2}}/{\gamma_{1}}\right)^{1/2}
,
which corresponds exactly to the cases
A_{2}
and
C
in (

).

Concerning condition 2), the second derivative of
Q
equals

Q^{\prime\prime}(r)=\max(1,d-2)\left(\frac{\gamma_{2}((1-d)r^{2}+h_{2}^{2})}{(r^{2}+h_{2}^{2})^{d/2+1}}-\frac{\gamma_{1}((1-d)r^{2}+h_{1}^{2})}{(r^{2}+h_{1}^{2})^{d/2+1}}\right)

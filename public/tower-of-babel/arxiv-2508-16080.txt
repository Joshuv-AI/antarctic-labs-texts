Quantization of blow-up masses for the Finsler N N -Liouville equation
Xia Huang, Yuan Li, Dong Ye, Feng Zhou

Abstract
. The quantization results for blow-up phenomena play crucial roles in the analysis of partial differential equations. Here we quantify the blow-up masses to the following Finsler N N -Liouville equation − Q N ​ u n = V n ​ e u n in ​ Ω ⊂ ℝ N , N ≥ 2 . -Q_{N}u_{n}=V_{n}e^{u_{n}}\quad\mbox{in}~\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},N\geq 2. Our study generalizes the classical result of Li-Shafrir [ 24 ] for Liouville equation, Wang-Xia’s work for anisotropic Liouville equation in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} [ 29 ] , and Esposito-Lucia’s for the N N -Laplacian case in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} ( N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 ) in their recent paper [ 15 ] .

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the following Finsler
N
-Liouville equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle-Q_{N}u=Ve^{u}\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,

with a bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 2)
,
V\in C(\Omega)
and the operator
Q_{N}
defined by

Q_{N}u:={\rm div}\Big(F^{N-1}(\nabla u)F_{\xi}(\nabla u)\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{x_{i}}\Big(F^{N-1}(\nabla u)F_{\xi_{i}}(\nabla u)\Big).

Here
F_{\xi}=(F_{\xi_{i}})
,
F_{\xi_{i}}:=\frac{\partial F}{\partial\xi_{i}}
, and
F
is a convex function over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying

–

F\in C^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\};

–

F(t\xi)=|t|F(\xi)
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N};

–

there exists
0<C_{1}\leq C_{2}<\infty
such that
C_{1}|\xi|\leq F(\xi)\leq C_{2}|\xi|
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

In fact,
Q_{N}
is called the Finsler
N
-Laplacian or anisotropic
N
-Laplace operator, and it has been studied vastly in the literature. Historically, Wulff
[
30
]
used such operators to study crystal shapes and minimization of anisotropic surface tensions. For more background on anisotropic operators and Finsler geometry,
we refer the interested readers to
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
10
,
12
,
18
,
28
]
and references therein.

The most well known example of
F
is

F(\xi)=|\xi|_{q}:=\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|\xi_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\;\;\mbox{ in}\;\mathbb{R}^{N}

with
1<q<\infty
. If moreover
q=2
,
F(\xi)=|\xi|
is the Euclidean norm and
Q_{N}
is the classical isotropic
N
-Laplacian, i.e.
{\rm div}\big(|\nabla u|^{N-2}\nabla u\big)
. Especially, when
N=2
, we arrive at

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta u=Ve^{u}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

It’s well known that equation (
1.2
) has profound geometric and physical background. In differential geometry, (
1.2
) is called the prescribed Gauss curvature equation see
[
21
,
26
,
9
]
. It is also linked to the mean field equation, the Chern-Simons model and so on, see
[
5
,
6
,
19
]
and references therein.

In particular, when
V\equiv 1
, Liouville
[
25
]
studied (
1.2
) and gave the expression of all smooth solutions using holomorphic functions, which means that there is a rich family of solutions over
\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, Chen-Li
[
8
]
classified all solutions of
-\Delta u=e^{u}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
if
e^{u}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, they showed that any solution is given by

u_{*}(x)=\ln\frac{8\lambda^{2}}{(1+\lambda^{2}|x-p_{0}|^{2})^{2}},\quad\mbox{with }\lambda>0,\,p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Hence for any
p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\lambda>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{u_{*}}dx=8\pi.

To understand the blow-up phenomena or the compactness of family of solutions of (
1.2
) with various
V
, Brezis-Merle
[
7
]
showed a milestone compactness-concentration result:

Theorem A.

Assume that
\{u_{n}\}
is a sequence of solutions to

-\Delta u_{n}=V_{n}e^{u_{n}}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},

such that

0\leq V_{n}(x)\leq M,\;\;\forall\;x\in\Omega;\quad\int_{\Omega}e^{u_{n}}dx\leq M

with some constant
M>0
. Then up to a subsequence, we have one of the following three alternatives:

(i)

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
;

(ii)

u_{n}\rightarrow-\infty
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
;

(iii)

There exists a finite blow-up set
S=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}\}\subset\Omega
such that
u_{n}(x)\rightarrow-\infty
uniformly on any compact subset of
\,\Omega\backslash S
. Moreover,
V_{n}e^{u_{n}}\rightharpoonup\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\alpha_{i}\delta_{a_{i}}
in the sense of measure with
\alpha_{i}\geq 4\pi
,
1\leq i\leq m
.

This result plays a crucial role in the study of equations like (
1.2
), for the prescribing Gauss curvature problem or mean field equation: the existence issue, blow-up analysis, asymptotic behavior near isolated singularity, compactness and many other aspects of solutions. It has inspired so many important works, it is just impossible for us to cite some of them, seeing the hundreds of citations.

Brezis-Merle conjectured (see
[
7
, Open Problem 4]
) that under some minor regularity assumptions on
{\{V_{n}}\}
, the concentration Dirac mass
\alpha_{i}
appearing in alternative
(iii)
should belong to
8\pi\mathbb{N}
, in other words the multiple of bubble energy classified by Chen-Li. Li-Shafrir
[
24
]
gave an affirmative answer to this conjecture, assuming that
V_{n}\rightarrow V
in
C(\overline{\Omega})
. Furthermore, if
\{V_{n}\}
are uniformly Lipschitz functions, i.e.

(1.3)

\displaystyle V_{n}\geq 0,\quad V_{n}\rightarrow V\mbox{ in }C(\overline{\Omega})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\nabla V_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M,

and if

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\partial\Omega}u_{n}-\inf\limits_{\partial\Omega}u_{n}\leq M,

for some positive constant
M
, Li
[
23
]
proved that
\alpha_{i}=8\pi
for each
i
.

Recently, the above studies have been generalized to the
N
-Laplacian case, that is, for

(1.5)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{N}u=Ve^{u}\quad\mbox{in }\;\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\;N\geq 2.

Firstly, assuming that
V=1
, Esposito
[
14
]
classified that all solutions to equation (
1.5
) with
e^{u}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
are as follows:

u(x)=\ln\frac{\mathcal{C}_{N}\lambda^{N}}{\big(1+\lambda^{\frac{N}{N-1}}|x-p_{0}|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\big)^{N}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{u}dx=\mathcal{C}_{N}\omega_{N},

where
p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\lambda>0
,
\mathcal{C}_{N}=N\left(\frac{N^{2}}{N-1}\right)^{N-1}
and
\omega_{N}
stands for the volume of the unit Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, Brezis-Merle’s trichotomy result, the corresponding Li-Shafrir or Li type blow-up mass quantification have been extended to (
1.5
) respectively in
[
1
,
15
,
16
]
.

A natural question is to consider more general anisotropic case. To describe precisely the result for anisotropic situation, we need to introduce the Wulff ball and precise also the meaning of solution here. Let
F^{0}
be the support function of
K:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid~F(x)<1\}
, that is

F^{0}(x):=\sup_{\xi\in K}\langle x,\xi\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
F^{0}
is indeed the dual norm of
F
, and
F^{0}
is a convex function with first-order positive homogeneity. For more properties of
F^{0}
, we refer to
[
29
,
31
]
and references therein. We denote by
\mathcal{B}_{r}(p):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:F^{0}(x-p)<r\}
the Wulff ball with respect to
F^{0}
, of radius
r
centered at
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We write
\mathcal{B}_{r}
when the center is the origin.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
), if
u\in W^{1,N}_{loc}(\Omega)
,
Ve^{u}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}F^{N-1}(\nabla u)F_{\xi}(\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\phi dx=\int_{\Omega}Ve^{u}\phi dx,\quad\forall\;\phi\in W_{c}^{1,N}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega).

To the best of our knowledge, there are few works considering the blow-up analysis of genral anisotropic Liouville equations. Wang-Xia
[
29
]
first proved the concentrated compactness property of solutions to (
1.1
) when
N=2
, and extended the results of
[
7
,
23
]
to the anisotropic Liouville equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Later on, Xie-Gong
[
31
]
considered Brezis-Merle’s trichotomy result for (
1.1
), and proved

Theorem B.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain and
u_{n}
be a sequence of weak solutions to

(1.6)

\displaystyle-Q_{N}u_{n}=V_{n}e^{u_{n}}\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},

with

(1.7)

\displaystyle 0\leq V_{n}\leq M\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}e^{u_{n}}dx\leq M.

Then up to a subsequence, one of the following alternatives holds true:

(i)

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
;

(ii)

u_{n}\rightarrow-\infty
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
;

(iii)

There is a nonempty finite set
S=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}\}\subset\Omega
, and
u_{n}(x)\rightarrow-\infty
uniformly on any compact subset of
\Omega\backslash S
. In addition,
V_{n}e^{u_{n}}\rightharpoonup\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\alpha_{i}\delta_{a_{i}}
in the sense of measure on
\Omega
with
\alpha_{i}\geq N^{N}\kappa
for any
i
, where
\kappa:=|\mathcal{B}_{1}|
.

Moreover, if (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) are satisfied, then
\alpha_{i}=\mathcal{C}_{N}\kappa
in
(iii)
with

\mathcal{C}_{N}=N\Big(\frac{N^{2}}{N-1}\Big)^{N-1}
.

Now we can ask whether the quantification of concentration mass
\alpha_{i}
holds true in more general setting of (
1.6
), analogous to Li-Shafrir’s result on Brezis-Merle’s conjecture. Our main objective here is to give an affirmative answer.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence of weak solution to (
1.6
) with
V_{n}\geq 0
,
V_{n}\rightarrow V
in
C(\overline{\Omega})
and

(1.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}e^{u_{n}}dx\leq M<\infty\quad\mbox{ for any }n.

Assume that the alternative
(iii)
happens for
u_{n}
, then for any
1\leq i\leq m
,
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{N}\kappa\mathbb{N}
.

Furthermore,
\alpha_{1}={C}_{N}\kappa
if there is
R>0
such that
\mathcal{B}_{R}(a_{1})\subset\Omega
and

(1.9)

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)+N\ln F^{0}(x-a_{1})\leq M^{\prime},\quad\mbox{ for any }n\in\mathbb{N},x\in\mathcal{B}_{R}(a_{1}).

The proof is mainly based on the blow-up analysis, and the recent classification result of Ciraolo-Li
[
11
]
for the anisotropic
N
-Liouville equations under the integrability assumption. More precisely, they established that any solution to

(1.10)

\displaystyle-Q_{N}u=e^{u}\;\;\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{u}dx<\infty,

satisfies

(1.11)

\displaystyle u(x)=\ln\frac{\mathcal{C}_{N}\lambda^{N}}{\Big(1+\lambda^{\frac{N}{N-1}}F^{0}(x-p)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\Big)^{N}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{u}dx=\mathcal{C}_{N}\kappa,

where
\lambda>0
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In the anisotropic case, we say that a function is
radial
with respect to
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
if it depends only on
F^{0}(x-p)
.

Our proof consists mainly of three steps. First, by blow-up around a local maximum of the function
u_{n}
, we are led to a solution of (
1.10
), characterized in
[
11
]
. Then we will iterate this blow-up procedure around other eventual local maxima, and obtain each time a contribution of
\mathcal{C}_{N}\kappa
to the limiting concentration mass. According to (
1.8
), the procedure will stop after a finite number of iterations. The last ingredient consists of showing that there is no mass contribution out of the bubbles, or the so called “neck region”.

Our paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to establish some
a priori
estimates such as
\sup+\inf
type inequality and Harnack type inequality, which are essential for our analysis. In section
3
, we apply the blow up argument to prove the quantization result.

2.
A priori estimates

First of all, by regularity theory for anisotropic operators (see
[
13
,
27
]
or
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
), we know that any solution to (
1.1
) belongs to
C^{1,\beta}_{loc}(\Omega)
.

Here we expose some crucial
a priori
estimates for the bow-up analysis. A first one is the following Harnack type inequality for anisotropic equation, in the spirit of
[
22
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
r>0
and
u
be a continuous and weak solution of

-Q_{N}u=f\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{B}_{2r}(p)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle u(p)-\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{2r}(p)}u(x)\geq C\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{t}(p)}fdx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\frac{dt}{t},

where
C
is a positive constant depending on
N
and
F
.

The constant
C
in (
2.1
) is not explicit in general. However, in the class of radial functions, (
2.1
) holds with the sharp constant
C=(N\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}
as follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
R>0
, let
f\in C(\overline{\mathcal{B}_{R}}(p))
be a radial function with respect to
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Assume that
u\in C(\mathcal{B}_{R}(p))
satisfies

-Q_{N}u\geq f\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}~\mathcal{B}_{R}(p)

in the weak sense. Then

u(p)-\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(p)}u(x)\geq(N\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}\int_{0}^{R}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{t}(p)}fdx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\frac{dt}{t}.

In particular, for each
0<r<R
, there holds

u(p)-\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(p)}u(x)\geq(N\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(p)}fdx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\ln\frac{R}{r}.

Proof.

Direct calculation shows that the unique radial solution to

-Q_{N}u_{0}=f\;\;\mbox{in}~\mathcal{B}_{R}(p),\quad u_{0}=0\;\;\mbox{on}~\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}(p)

is given by

u_{0}(r)=(N\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}\int_{r}^{R}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{t}(p)}fdx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\frac{dt}{t}.

Set
\widetilde{u}=u-\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{R}(p)}u
, there holds

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}-Q_{N}\widetilde{u}&\!=-Q_{N}u\geq-Q_{N}u_{0},&\quad\mbox{in}~\mathcal{B}_{R}(p),\\
\widetilde{u}&\!\geq u_{0},&\quad\mbox{on}~\partial\mathcal{B}_{R}(p).\end{array}\right.

The comparison principle (see
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
) implies that
\widetilde{u}\geq u_{0}
in
\mathcal{B}_{R}(p).
Therefore, for each
0<r<R
,

\widetilde{u}(p)\geq(N\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}\int_{r}^{R}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{t}(p)}fdx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\frac{dt}{t}\geq(N\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{r}(p)}fdx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\ln\frac{R}{r}.

We get then the first estimate in the Lemma by tending
r
to
0
.∎

Combining Proposition
2.1
with Lemma
2.2
, we will apply the blow-up analysis to show the
\sup+\inf
type inequality.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence of weak solution to (
1.6
) with

0<M_{1}\leq V_{n}\leq M_{2},\quad V_{n}\rightarrow V\quad\mbox{in }C(\overline{\Omega}),

and

\int_{\Omega}e^{u_{n}}dx\leq M,

for some positive constants
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
and
M
. Then for any positive constant
C_{1}>N-1
and any compact
\Sigma\subset\Omega
, there is constant
C_{2}>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\max_{\Sigma}u_{n}+C_{1}\inf_{\Omega}u_{n}\leq C_{2}.

Proof.

Let
C_{1}>N-1
and
\Sigma\subset\subset\Omega
be given. If
\max\limits_{\Sigma}u_{n}+C_{1}\inf\limits_{\Omega}u_{n}<0
, we are done. So we assume that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\max_{\Sigma}u_{n}+C_{1}\inf_{\Omega}u_{n}\geq 0,

and we only need to show

(2.4)

\displaystyle\max_{\Sigma}u_{n}\leq C_{3},

for some positive constant
C_{3}
. Arguing by contradiction, we suppose that, up to a subsequence if necessary,
\{u_{n}\}
solving (
1.6
) with the above assumptions but

\max_{\Sigma}u_{n}\rightarrow+\infty\quad\mbox{as}~n\rightarrow\infty.

Let
\overline{p}_{n}\in\Sigma
such that
u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n})=\max\limits_{\Sigma}u_{n}
and
\overline{\delta}_{n}:=e^{-\frac{u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n})}{N}}
, so

u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n})\rightarrow\infty,\quad\mbox{hence }\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{\delta}_{n}=0.

Choose
\eta>0
, such that

\Sigma_{\eta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\exists\;y\in\Sigma\mbox{ such that }F^{0}(x-y)\leq 2\eta\}\subset\Omega.

For any
R>0
, there exists
n_{0}
such that
R\overline{\delta}_{n}<\eta
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. It follows from Proposition
2.1
that

\displaystyle u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n})-\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\overline{p}_{n})}u_{n}(x)\geq C\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R\overline{\delta}_{n}}(\overline{p}_{n})}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\times\ln\frac{\eta}{R\overline{\delta}_{n}}.

On the other hand, seeing (
2.3
), there holds

-\inf_{\mathcal{B}_{\eta}(\overline{p}_{n})}u_{n}(x)\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}}u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n}),

so that

\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R\overline{\delta}_{n}}(\overline{p}_{n})}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\leq\frac{1}{C}\Big(1+\frac{1}{C_{1}}\Big)\frac{u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n})}{\ln\frac{\eta}{R\overline{\delta}_{n}}}.

Consequently,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R\overline{\delta}_{n}}(\overline{p}_{n})}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\leq\frac{N}{C}\left(1+\frac{1}{C_{1}}\right),\quad\forall\;R>0.

Assume
\overline{p}_{n}\rightarrow\overline{x}_{0}\in\Sigma
. Define
\overline{u}_{n}(x):=u_{n}(\overline{\delta}_{n}x+\overline{p}_{n})+N\ln\overline{\delta}_{n}
, we have

-Q_{N}\overline{u}_{n}(x)=V_{n}(\overline{\delta}_{n}x+\overline{p}_{n})e^{\overline{u}_{n}(x)}\>\;\mbox{in }\frac{\Omega-{\{\overline{p}_{n}}\}}{\overline{\delta}_{n}};\quad\overline{u}_{n}(x)\leq\overline{u}_{n}(0)=0\;\;\mbox{in }\frac{\Sigma-{\{\overline{p}_{n}}\}}{\overline{\delta}_{n}}

and for any
R>0
,

\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}e^{\overline{u}_{n}}dx\leq\frac{1}{M_{1}}\left(\frac{N}{C}\right)^{N-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{C_{1}}\right)^{N-1}=:M^{\prime}.

Theorem B implies that the following alternative holds:

(1)

\overline{u}_{n}
is bounded in
L_{loc}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(2)

V_{n}(\overline{\delta}_{n}x+\overline{p}_{n})e^{\overline{u}_{n}}\rightharpoonup\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\alpha_{i}\delta_{a_{i}}
weakly in the sense of measures over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
u_{n}\rightarrow-\infty
uniformly on compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}\}
.

If the case (1) appears, the elliptic estimates (see
[
13
]
) imply that up to a subsequence
\overline{u}_{n}\rightarrow\overline{u}
in
C^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and
\overline{u}
satisfies
\overline{u}(0)=0
,

-Q_{N}\overline{u}=V(\overline{x}_{0})e^{\overline{u}}\;\;\mbox{in}~\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{\overline{u}}dx\leq M^{\prime}.

Applying the classification result in
[
11
]
,
\overline{u}
satisfies (
1.11
) for
\lambda>0
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We see that
\overline{u}
is radially decreasing with respect to
p
. Hence, for any
R>F^{0}(p)+1
, there is a sequence
z_{n}\rightarrow p
such that

\max_{\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{2R}(z_{n})}\overline{u}_{n}(x)=\overline{u}_{n}(z_{n})\rightarrow\overline{u}(p).

Setting
p_{n}:=\overline{\delta}_{n}z_{n}+\overline{p}_{n}
and
\delta_{n}=e^{-\frac{u_{n}(p_{n})}{N}}
, there holds

u_{n}(p_{n})=u_{n}(\overline{\delta}_{n}z_{n}+\overline{p}_{n})=\overline{u}_{n}(z_{n})-N\ln\overline{\delta}_{n}\geq\overline{u}_{n}(0)-N\ln\overline{\delta}_{n}=u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n}),

and

1\leq\frac{\overline{\delta}_{n}}{\delta_{n}}=e^{\frac{u_{n}(p_{n})-u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n})}{N}}=e^{\frac{\overline{u}_{n}(z_{n})}{N}}\rightarrow e^{\frac{\overline{u}(p)}{N}}.

Let us now rescale
u_{n}
with respect to
p_{n}
by setting
\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)=u_{n}(\delta_{n}x+p_{n})+N\ln\delta_{n}
, then we have
\widetilde{u}_{n}(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{n}(0)=\overline{u}_{n}(z_{n})-N\ln\overline{\delta}_{n}+N\ln\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{B}_{2R}(z_{n})}\overline{u}_{n}(x)-N\ln\overline{\delta}_{n}+N\ln\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{B}_{2R}(z_{n})}u_{n}(\overline{\delta}_{n}(x-z_{n})+p_{n})+N\ln\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{B}_{2R\frac{\overline{\delta}_{n}}{\delta_{n}}}}u_{n}(\delta_{n}x+p_{n})+N\ln\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{B}_{2R\frac{\overline{\delta}_{n}}{\delta_{n}}}}\widetilde{u}_{n}(x).

Therefore,
\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)
satisfies
\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)\leq\widetilde{u}_{n}(0)=0
in
\mathcal{B}_{2R\frac{\overline{\delta}_{n}}{\delta_{n}}}
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle-Q_{N}\widetilde{u}_{n}=V_{n}(\delta_{n}x+p_{n})e^{\widetilde{u}_{n}}\;\;\mbox{in }~\mathcal{B}_{2R\frac{\overline{\delta}_{n}}{\delta_{n}}},\quad\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R\frac{\overline{\delta}_{n}}{\delta_{n}}}}e^{\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)}dx\leq M^{\prime}.

3.

\displaystyle=u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n,\ell}+\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}x)+N\ln G(\overline{p}_{n,\ell}+\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}x)+N\ln\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}-N\ln G(\overline{p}_{n,\ell}+\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}x)

\displaystyle\leq u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n,\ell})+N\ln G(\overline{p}_{n,\ell})+N\ln\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}-N\ln G(\overline{p}_{n,\ell}+\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}x)

\displaystyle=N\ln\frac{G(\overline{p}_{n,\ell})}{G(\overline{p}_{n,\ell}+\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}x)}

\displaystyle\leq N\ln 2.

So
\overline{u}_{n}(0)=0
, and

-Q_{N}\overline{u}_{n}=V_{n}(\bar{\delta}_{n,\ell}x+\bar{p}_{n,\ell})e^{\overline{u}_{n}},\;\;\overline{u}_{n}\leq N\ln 2,\quad\mbox{in }\;{\mathcal{B}}_{\frac{\xi_{n}}{2}}.

Then up to subsequence,
\overline{u}_{n}\rightarrow\overline{u}
in
C_{loc}^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where

\overline{u}(x)=\ln\frac{\mu^{N}}{\left(1+\gamma\mu^{\frac{N}{N-1}}F^{0}(x-\overline{p})^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\right)^{N}}

with
\gamma^{N-1}=\frac{V(0)}{\mathcal{C}_{N}}
,
\mu>0
and
\overline{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We can choose a sequence
k_{n,\ell}\rightarrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle\|\overline{u}_{n}-\overline{u}\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{2k_{n,\ell}})}\rightarrow 0.

Therefore for
n
large enough

(3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\overline{u}_{n}(ty+\overline{p})|_{t=1}<0,\quad 1\leq F^{0}(y)\leq 2k_{n}^{l}.

Let
y_{n,\ell}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
satisfy

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{n}(y_{n,\ell}+\overline{p})=\max\limits_{\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{4k_{n,\ell}}}\overline{u}_{n}(y+\overline{p}),

and we choose
p_{n,\ell}=\overline{p}_{n,\ell}+\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}(y_{n,\ell}+\overline{p})
. By the definition of
y_{n,\ell}
and the expression of
\overline{u}
, there holds
y_{n,\ell}\rightarrow 0
and

(3.17)

\displaystyle u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n,\ell})\leq u_{n}(p_{n,\ell})\leq u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n,\ell})+C,

because
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big[u_{n}(\overline{p}_{n,\ell})-u_{n}(p_{n,\ell})]=\overline{u}(\overline{p})-\overline{u}(0)
. Denote

\delta_{n,\ell}=e^{-\frac{u_{n}(p_{n,\ell})}{N}},\quad\widetilde{u}_{n,\ell}(x)=u_{n}(\delta_{n,\ell}x+p_{n,\ell})+N\ln\delta_{n,\ell}.

By (
3.17
), there holds

\displaystyle\delta_{n,\ell}\leq\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}\leq C\delta_{n,\ell}.

Up to a subsequence, we assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{n,\ell}}{\overline{\delta}_{n,\ell}}=\mu\in(0,1]

Readily
\widetilde{u}_{n,\ell}\to\overline{u}(\mu x+\overline{p})+N\ln\mu
in
C^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. It is easy to see that (
3.4
)-(
3.7
) are satisfied for
\{p_{n,j}\}_{1\leq j\leq\ell}
and
\{k_{n,j}\}_{1\leq j\leq\ell}
with
m=\ell
. For example, (
3.5
) is valid seeing the induction hypothesis and (
3.16
).

We continue this process until (
3.8
) holds true. In fact we will stop after a finite number of iterations since each time we add a mass of
\mathcal{C}_{N}\kappa
near the blow-up point
p_{n,j}\to 0
, it follows then
m\leq\frac{CV(0)}{\mathcal{C}_{N}\kappa}
.

Step 5. No neck contribution.

The last step is to show that there is no mass contribution out of the
m
bubbles provided by Step 4. Here we will use the following lemma which arrives at a slightly more general situation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{V_{n}\}
be a sequence of functions satisfying (
3.1
). Assume that
\{u_{n}\}
is a sequence of solutions of (
1.1
) satisfying (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) on
\mathcal{B}_{R}
. Let
\{p_{n,j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
be
m
sequences in
\mathcal{B}_{R}
and
\{k_{n,j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}\in(0,\infty)
be
m
sequences of positive numbers with
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}p_{n,j}=0
,
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}k_{n,j}=\infty
, which satisfy (
3.5
) and

(3.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{2k_{n,j}\delta_{n,j}}(p_{n,j})}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{k_{n,j}\delta_{n,j}}(p_{n,j})}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx=\beta_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq m,

(3.19)

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)+N\ln\Big[\min_{1\leq j\leq m}F^{0}(x-p_{n,j})\Big]\leq C^{\prime}<\infty,\quad\forall~n\in\mathbb{N},\;x\in\mathcal{B}_{R}.

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{R}}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.
Recall that
V(0)>0
and (
3.10
) holds true. We will proceed by induction. By analysis as in Step 2, we can show that the result holds true for
m=1
. Since the arguments are very similar, so we omit the details.

Assume that the result holds for
m\leq\ell-1
with
\ell\geq 2
, and we consider now
m=\ell
. Without loss of generality, assume that
p_{n,1}=0
for all
n
and

(3.20)

\displaystyle d_{n}=\min_{1\leq i\neq j\leq\ell}F^{0}(p_{n,i}-p_{n,j})=F^{0}(p_{n,1}-p_{n,2}).

Case 1
. Suppose first that there is
A>0
satisfying

(3.21)

\displaystyle F^{0}(p_{n,i}-p_{n,j})\leq Ad_{n},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq\ell,\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

We claim then

(3.22)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4Ad_{n}}}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{2Ad_{n}}}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}.

Once (
3.22
) is established, we can apply the similar method in Step 2 for the case
m=1
to conclude that
\beta=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}\beta_{j}.

Thus, it remains to prove (
3.22
). Set
r_{n,j}=k_{n,j}\delta_{n,j}
, consider

\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)=u_{n}(d_{n}x)+N\ln d_{n}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\widetilde{V}_{n}(x)=V_{n}(d_{n}x),\quad\mbox{for }F^{0}(x)\leq\frac{R}{d_{n}}.

Denote also
\widetilde{x}_{n,j}=\frac{p_{n,j}}{d_{n}}
,
\widetilde{\delta}_{n,j}=e^{-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{n}(\widetilde{x}_{n,j})}{N}}=\frac{\delta_{n,j}}{d_{n}}
and
\widetilde{r}_{n,j}=\frac{r_{n,j}}{d_{n}}
for
1\leq j\leq\ell
. Using (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.19
), we have that
\forall\;1\leq j\leq\ell
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{x}_{n,j}=0,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{r}_{n,j}/\widetilde{\delta}_{n,j}=\infty
;

\mathcal{B}_{\widetilde{r}_{n,i}}(\widetilde{x}_{n,i})\cap\mathcal{B}_{\widetilde{r}_{n,j}}(\widetilde{x}_{n,j})=\emptyset,\quad\forall~1\leq i\neq j\leq\ell,\;n\in\mathbb{N};

\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)+N\ln\Big[\min_{1\leq j\leq\ell}F^{0}(x-\widetilde{x}_{n,j})\Big]\leq C,\quad\forall~x\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\frac{R}{d_{n}}},\;n\in\mathbb{N};

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{2\widetilde{r}_{n,j}}(\widetilde{x}_{n,j})}\widetilde{V}_{n}e^{\widetilde{u}_{n}}dx=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\widetilde{r}_{n,j}}(\widetilde{x}_{n,j})}\widetilde{V}_{n}e^{\widetilde{u}_{n}}dx=\beta_{j},\quad\forall~1\leq j\leq\ell.

Furthermore, (
3.5
) guarantees that
u_{n}(p_{n,j})+N\ln F^{0}(p_{n,i}-p_{n,j})\to\infty
for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq\ell
, so
\widetilde{u}_{n}(\widetilde{x}_{n,j})
tends to
\infty
by (
3.21
).

Since
F^{0}(\widetilde{x}_{n,j})\leq A
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq j\leq\ell
, up to a subsequence, there holds

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{x}_{n,j}=q_{j},\quad\mbox{for }\;1\leq j\leq\ell.

Applying again Theorem B, we see that

(3.23)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{n}\rightarrow-\infty\mbox{ uniformly on compact subsets of }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{q_{j},1\leq j\leq\ell\}.

Obviously,

\displaystyle 1\leq F^{0}(q_{i}-q_{j})\leq A,\quad\forall~1\leq i\neq j\leq\ell.

For each
1\leq j\leq\ell
, either
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{r}_{n,j}>0
, we get by (
3.23
)

(3.24)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{\frac{1}{2}}(q_{j})}\widetilde{V}_{n}e^{\widetilde{u}_{n}}dx\rightarrow\beta_{j};

or
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{r}_{n,j}=0
, then (
3.24
) still holds by similar estimate in Step 2. Finally we conclude (
3.22
) using (
3.23
) and (
3.24
).

Case 2
. Suppose now (
3.21
) is not valid, then (up to a subsequence)

J=\Big\{1\leq j\leq\ell,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F^{0}(p_{n,j})}{d_{n}}=\infty\Big\}\neq\emptyset.

For
j\in J^{\prime}=\{1,\cdots,\ell\}\backslash J
, there is
A>0
such that
F^{0}(p_{n,j})\leq Ad_{n}
. Without loss of generality we assume
J^{\prime}=\{1,\cdots,s\}
with
1\leq s\leq\ell-1
. Notice that
s\geq 2
by the definition (
3.20
).

Denote still
\widetilde{u}_{n}(x)=u_{n}(d_{n}x)+N\ln d_{n}
for
F^{0}(x)\leq 4A
. Similar arguments as for
Case 1
show that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{4Ad_{n}}}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{2Ad_{n}}}V_{n}e^{u_{n}}dx=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\beta_{j}.

Set
r_{n}^{\prime}=Ad_{n}
and
x_{n}^{\prime}=0
, we see that
(\ell-s+1)
sequences
\{x_{n}^{\prime}\}\cup\{p_{n,j}\}_{s<j\leq\ell}
with radii
r_{n}^{\prime},r_{n,j}(s<j\leq\ell)
and the masses
\beta^{\prime}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq s}\beta_{j}
,
\beta_{j}(s<j\leq\ell)
satisfy
(
3.4
), (
3.18
)-(
3.19
) with
m^{\prime}:=\ell-s+1<\ell
. We only need to show

(3.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{Ad_{n}}\cap\mathcal{B}_{r_{n,j}}(p_{n,j})=\emptyset,\quad\forall~s<j\leq\ell.

Suppose that (
3.26
) does not hold for some
j>s
. Then
Ad_{n}+r_{n,j}\geq F^{0}(p_{n,j})
, it follows that

\frac{r_{n,j}}{d_{n}}\geq\frac{F^{0}(p_{n,j})}{d_{n}}-A\rightarrow\infty,\quad\mbox{as }n\rightarrow\infty.

We have, for
n
large enough,

Ad_{n}+F^{0}(p_{n,j})\leq 2r_{n,j}\quad\mbox{since }r_{n,j}\geq\frac{2}{3}F^{0}(p_{n,j}).

Therefore
\mathcal{B}_{Ad_{n}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{2r_{n,j}}(p_{n,j})
, which is a contradiction with (
3.5
).

Now we can apply the induction hypothesis with
m^{\prime}<\ell
to claim
\beta=\sum_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\beta_{j}
. Lemma
3.2
is established. ∎

Proposition
3.1
is clearly valid using Lemma
3.2
with
\beta_{j}=\mathcal{C}_{N}\kappa.

∎

The conclusions of Theorem
1.2
follow readily from Proposition
3.1
. So we are done.

Comparative effects of empagliflozin and metformin on metabolic dysfunction in polycystic ovary syndrome, a double blinded randomized control feasibility trial
Authors: Hina Sharif, Sana Arsalan, Naureen Rehman, Ammara Muzammil, Farida Hatif, Farah Wasti, Sana Rehman, Nadia Mohsin, Rizwana Nasir, Wajiha Omair
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo determine the safety and efficacy of empagliflozin compared with those of metformin in polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) patients with insulin resistance (IR).MethodA single-center, prospective, double-blind randomized (1:1 ratio) noninferiority trial was conducted at the primary healthcare organization in slums between December 2023 and June 2024. Women aged 18–45 years with PCOS were enrolled and randomly assigned to either 10 mg (n = 41) empagliflozin daily or metformin 500 mg twice a day (n = 39) for 24 weeks. The primary outcome was changes in metabolic parameters and weight after 24 weeks of treatment. STATA software version 17 was used for statistical analysis, independent t tests were used for continuous variables, and Welch's correction was applied when variances were unequal. Categorical variables were compared via Fischer exact tests or chi-square (X2) tests. To address the significant loss to follow-up among participants and minimize bias, a mixed-model repeated-measures analysis was conducted to compare the effects of Drug A and Drug B over the 24-week treatment period.ResultComparing the outcomes before and after treatment on the basis of the mean differences (MDs) revealed significant changes in various outcomes for both Drug A (Metformin) and Drug B (Empagliflozin). For weight, the MDs were 0.9 ± 1.4 kg for Drug A and 6.7 ± 1.6 kg for Drug B. In terms of lipid profiles, total cholesterol decreased by 18.3 ± 4.7 mg/dL in the Drug A group and by 34.9 ± 3.7 mg/dL in the Drug B group. Additionally, HDL cholesterol levels increased by 17.0 ± 3.2 mg/dL for Drug A and 25.2 ± 2.0 mg/dL for Drug B. Similarly, LDL cholesterol reductions were 22.4 ± 5.6 mg/dL for Drug A and 31.1 ± 4.9 mg/dL for Drug B. Triglycerides also decreased, with reductions of 46.2 ± 11.4 mg/dL in the Drug A group and 72.8 ± 7.0 mg/dL in the Drug B group. For fasting blood sugar (FBS), the MD was 28 ± 4.0 mg/dL for Drug A and 26.4 ± 3.6 mg/dL for Drug B. Finally, the HbA1c level was 0.4 ± 0.07% for Drug A and 0.9 ± 0.2% for Drug B.ConclusionThis study suggested that empagliflozin may offer greater metabolic benefits than metformin in overweight women with PCOS and prediabetes, although the adjusted results were not significantly different. These findings support the potential of SGLT2 inhibitors as alternative or add-on therapies.Trial registrationThe clinical trial registration number was NCT06140108 in https://register.clinicaltrials.gov/ registration date 11th of November 2023. Recruitment of the participants started at 15th of November 2023 and trial started in December 2023 and ended in June 2024.Keywords: Empagliflozin, Metabolic parameters polycystic ovary syndrome, Sodium‒glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor

What is already known about this topic

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine disorder that is closely associated with insulin resistance, obesity, and overall metabolic dysfunction. Metformin is widely used to improve metabolic outcomes in women with PCOS, primarily because of its insulin-sensitizing effects. Empagliflozin, a sodium‒glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor, has been shown to improve weight management and insulin sensitivity in patients with type 2 diabetes, and its use is now being explored in women with PCOS. However, there is limited clinical evidence comparing empagliflozin directly with metformin or evaluating the effects of their combined use in this population.

What this study adds

This study contributes to the literature by providing clinical data on the efficacy of empagliflozin compared with that of metformin in addressing metabolic dysfunction among women with PCOS. These findings demonstrate the potential benefits of empagliflozin in improving weight, insulin resistance, and lipid profiles. Additionally, the study explores the tolerability of empagliflozin and investigates the possible synergistic effects when empagliflozin is used alongside metformin.

How this study might affect research, practice or policy

The findings of this study have implications for future research, clinical practice, and health policy. These findings highlight the need for further investigation into the role of SGLT2 inhibitors in PCOS management and support the potential use of empagliflozin as an alternative or adjunct to metformin. Ultimately, these insights may help inform clinical guidelines and promote more personalized approaches to managing metabolic complications in women with PCOS.

Introduction

Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common endocrine disorders affecting women of reproductive age [1]. It is primarily associated with reproductive abnormalities, which are characterized mainly by irregular menstrual cycles, hyperandrogenism, and cystic ovaries. While its clinical presentation varies, many women experience symptoms such as acne, hirsutism, and difficulties with fertility during reproduction [2].

Globally, PCOS affects an estimated 11–13% of women, but it is an underreported area of research [3]. In South Asia, particularly in Pakistan and India, the burden of PCOS is increasingly evident. Studies suggest that the prevalence of PCOS in Pakistani women may be as high as approximately 20% in some urban populations [4]. However, data related to this condition remain underreported, especially among marginalized and underserved groups [5].

In underserved populations, those who have a limited literacy rate, limited access to healthcare, no emphasis on health education, and compromised economic resources, the problem of PCOS is gradually increasing along with other chronic illnesses [6]. Women in these settings often go undiagnosed and untreated due to lack of awareness, social stigma, and poor access to diagnostic and therapeutic services. Among the range of complications associated with PCOS, metabolic abnormalities such as insulin resistance, a high lipid profile and uncontrolled weight gain with irregular menstruation are prominent [7]. These metabolic parameters not only contribute to the prognosis of PCOS but also exacerbate its clinical presentation [8].

There is no gold standard treatment for correcting PCOS among women of reproductive age [9]. Among various therapeutic agents, antihyperglycemic agents, particularly insulin sensitizers such as metformin and newer classes such as GLP-1 receptor agonists [10] and SGLT2 inhibitors, have been shown to significantly improve insulin sensitivity, reduce serum androgen levels, restore ovulatory cycles, and facilitate weight loss in women with PCOS [10].

This study, therefore, aimed to compare the impact of antihyperglycemic agents such as metformin 500 mg in comparison with the SGLT2 inhibitor empagliflozin 10 mg on key metabolic parameters of women with PCOS, with the hypothesis that improved metabolic control leads to a significant improvement in clinical symptoms and overall health outcomes.

Objective

This study compared the effects of drug A (metformin 500 mg) and drug B (empagliflozin 10 mg) on weight and metabolic imbalance in women with PCOS with comorbidities or multimorbidity such as diabetes, hypertension, and hypothyroidism with PCOS and prediabetes in the slums of Karachi, Pakistan.

Research question

Which insulin-sensitive agents (Drugs A and B) are most effective at alleviating metabolic imbalances among women with polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOs) and prediabetes?

Materials & methods

Study design

A double-blinded, randomized, comparative feasibility study in women with PCOS was performed at primary healthcare clinics located in slum areas of Karachi, Pakistan. Patients were selected on the basis of the inclusion and exclusion criteria and those who had already adopted lifestyle modifications. Intervention was then started on the sorted data; participants were divided into the following two groups on the basis of the medications used after randomization.

First group: Lifestyle modification + 500 mg of metformin twice a day for 24 weeks

First group: Lifestyle modification + 500 mg of metformin twice a day for 24 weeks

Second group: Lifestyle modification + Empagliflozin 10 mg once a day for 24 weeks

Second group: Lifestyle modification + Empagliflozin 10 mg once a day for 24 weeks

Blinding

Outcome assessor and biostatistician.

Randomization

Simple randomization through a random number table.

Eligibility criteria

All the female participants belonged to the reproductive age group, i.e., 18–45 years [11], with a BMI of > 25 kg/m, with a diagnosis of PCOS using two (hyperandrogenism and oligo‐anovulation) out of three (oligo‐anovulation, hyperandrogenism and polycystic ovaries) Rotterdam criteria [12] that confirm hyperandrogenism (acne, seborrhea, hair loss on the scalp, increased body or facial hair) on clinical judgment, documented and self-reported oligomenorrhea (cycle length > 35 days and nine or fewer periods per year) or amenorrhea (absence of menses for a period of 3 months) were the inclusion criteria of the study. Women with nonclassical 21-hydroxylase deficiency, hyperprolactinemia, Cushing’s disease, or androgen-secreting tumors will be withdrawn from participation. In addition, pregnancy or intent to become pregnant, breastfeeding, documented use of oral hormonal contraceptives and hormone-releasing implants, clomiphene citrate or estrogen modulators, gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) modulators and Minoxidil, diabetes, history or presence of malignant neoplasms within the last year, pancreatitis (acute or chronic), and recurrent complaints of urinary tract infection were excluded from the study.

End point assessment

The participants attended six visits (visits 1‐3). During Visit 1, participants were screened against the inclusion and exclusion criteria by medical history and clinical examination, routine blood tests (i.e., full blood count, liver function tests, urea and electrolytes, a clotting screen and a pregnancy test), a urine pregnancy test and anthropometric measurements. During Visit 2 (baseline) and Visit 3 (24‐week follow‐up), participants underwent anthropometric measurements (weight, BMI). Blood samples were collected at these time points and analyzed for cardiometabolic parameters (fasting glucose, fasting insulin, total cholesterol, LDL‐C, HDL‐C, and triglyceride [TG]).

The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in weight and lipid profile at week 24. The secondary endpoints included changes from baseline in glucose levels at week 24. Overall safety and tolerability were assessed on the basis of adverse event (AE) reports, safety laboratory tests, vital sign measurements and physical examinations. The incidence rates of documented hypoglycemia, gastrointestinal symptoms, urinary tract infections, genital mycotic infections, osmotic diuresis-related AEs and volume-related AEs were also assessed.

Sample size

On the basis of the published related literature [13] and since it is a feasibility trial, the anticipated mean difference is 1, the sample size was collected with the help of Open Epi software Version 3.01, a minimum sample size = 35 with the addition of attrition, and the final sample size would be 80 (the ratio of controlled to intervention 1:1 (35–41 in each arm after adding attrition) = 80. A significance threshold of 5% and a power of 80% will be used, with an 18% attrition rate between the baseline and 6 months of the intervention period.

The intention-to-treat population was used to analyze the safety of empagliflozin versus metformin, and the per-protocol population was used to analyze the efficacy of empagliflozin versus metformin among participants.

Statistical analysis

The analysis begins with an examination of sample characteristics, the randomization process, and the distribution of variables. Baseline characteristics were compared between the two groups (Drug A and Drug B) via independent t tests for continuous variables, including age (years), weight, height, BMI, systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, triglycerides, HbA1c, and fasting blood sugar. Welch's correction was applied when variances were unequal. Categorical variables, such as age, marital status, employment status, and ethnicity, were compared via Fischer exact tests or chi-square (X2) tests. To address the significant loss to follow-up among participants and minimize bias, a mixed-model repeated-measures analysis was conducted to compare the effects of Drug A and Drug B over the 24-week treatment period. This approach allowed for the inclusion of all available data under the assumption that missing data were missing at random. The mixed model included fixed effects for time (0 = baseline, 1 = 12 weeks), treatment group (0 = Drug A, 1 = Drug B), and their interaction (time × treatment group). Sociodemographic factors, including age, marital status, employment status, ethnicity, and baseline values of weight, height, BMI, SBP, DBP, total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, triglycerides, HbA1c, and fasting blood sugar, were considered covariates in the models. Model fit was assessed via the deviance statistic, Akaike information criterion (AIC), and Schwarz Bayesian criterion (SBC) to identify the best-fitting models. All analyses were conducted via Stata version 15.0 (StataCorp LP, College Station, TX), with statistical significance set at a two-sided p value of < 0.05.

Discussion

This double-blinded, randomized, controlled trial compared the effects of metformin, 500 mg twice daily (Drug A), and empagliflozin, 10 mg once daily (Drug B), on metabolic parameters and weight reduction among women diagnosed with PCOS and prediabetes in low-resource slum areas of Karachi, Pakistan. With an estimated prevalence of 4–20% worldwide, polycystic ovarian syndrome, or PCOS, is a frequent condition among females of reproductive age [14]. Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), obesity, dyslipidemia, insulin resistance, oligo-ovulation or anovulation, hyperandrogenism, and ultrasound findings such as polycystic ovarian syndrome are among the metabolic and hormonal disorders that define it [15]. The widely accepted criteria for PCOS diagnosis are the Rotterdam criteria. It is characterized by the presence of at least two of the following: first, biochemical or clinical hyperandrogenism; second, evidence of oligo-ovulation or anovulation; and third, polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound, after other pertinent disorders have been ruled out [16]. In individuals with PCOS, prediabetes is linked to an increased risk of cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) estimate that by the time they are 40 years old, over half of PCOS patients have type 2 diabetes [17]. Few drugs have been developed to treat hormonal and metabolic dysregulations, and the majority of existing therapies are symptomatic due to the incomplete understanding of the precise pathophysiology underlying PCOS [18].

PCOS often coexists with dyslipidemia characterized by elevated LDL, triglycerides and low HDL [19]. Therefore, in terms of metabolic and cardiovascular markers, this study revealed that participants from both groups of Drug A, i.e., metformin, and Drug B, i.e., empagliflozin, presented reductions in LDL cholesterol and triglycerides and improvements in HDL cholesterol. However, the use of empagliflozin resulted in a greater decrease in total cholesterol and LDL, whereas the use of metformin also resulted in meaningful but smaller changes in lipid profiles. As reported by ElSayed et al. in the Standard of Care in Diabetes – 2023, SGLT2 inhibitors such as empagliflozin are found to increase HDL levels while modestly lowering LDL and triglyceride levels [20]. Additionally, in the Empagliflozin group, the relatively robust reduction in total cholesterol (34.9 ± 3.7 mg/dl) was associated with a synergistic effect between weight loss and glycemic control. Interestingly, empagliflozin was the first antidiabetic medication demonstrated to lower the risk of cardiovascular death in people with type 2 diabetes, according to historic EMPA-REG research [21, 22]. Other studies indicate that empagliflozin can improve overall lipid profiles, including reductions in triglycerides and total cholesterol, but the specific effects on LDL cholesterol in PCOS patients remain less clear [23]

The blood pressure outcomes in our study revealed that the empagliflozin group presented minimal systolic blood pressure (SBP) changes and a decrease in diastolic blood pressure, whereas the metformin group presented a slight decrease in SBP and a mild increase in DBP. These variations could be influenced by baseline differences, dehydration or volume status and adherence to antihypertensive recommendations [24]. In the Javed et al. experiment, empagliflozin reduced fat mass, body weight, and body mass index in overweight women with PCOS compared with metformin. Nevertheless, neither blood pressure nor insulin resistance decreased; however, it should be noted that the study's subjects' baseline blood pressure was normal [25]. Larger studies focusing on hypertensive PCOS patients might clarify the long-term effects of these agents on blood pressure regulation. Empagliflozin has shown promising effects on blood pressure regulation in women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).

Research indicates that empagliflozin can mitigate cardiometabolic complications associated with PCOS, particularly hypertension, which is prevalent in this population. Empagliflozin has been demonstrated to significantly lower both systolic and diastolic blood pressure in various studies, including those involving patients with type 2 diabetes and hypertension [26]. In a rat model of PCOS, empagliflozin treatment resulted in decreased blood pressure, alongside reductions in fat mass and plasma leptin levels, suggesting a beneficial impact on metabolic health [27]. A randomized controlled trial comparing empagliflozin with metformin showed that empagliflozin led to significant improvements in anthropometric measures, which are closely linked to blood pressure regulation [25]. The combination of empagliflozin with metformin and clomiphene citrate also yielded positive outcomes in terms of weight reduction and metabolic parameters, further supporting its role in managing PCOS-related hypertension [28]. The blood pressure-lowering effects of empagliflozin may be attributed to its ability to reduce renal SGLT2 expression, which is upregulated in hyperandrogenemia conditions such as PCOS. Additionally, it has been shown to decrease the expression of components of the renin‒angiotensin system, which plays a critical role in blood pressure regulation [29].

Recent studies have also highlighted the potential of empagliflozin in addressing the multifaceted nature of PCOS, including its effects on insulin sensitivity, body weight, and hormonal regulation [30–32]. Metformin, a well-known insulin-sensitizing agent, has been widely used to manage PCOS, particularly in women with insulin resistance. Metformin primarily exerts its effects by improving insulin sensitivity, a key feature of PCOS. It activates AMPK, a critical regulator of glucose and lipid metabolism, in the liver, skeletal muscle, and adipose tissue. This activation enhances glucose uptake in insulin-sensitive tissues and reduces hepatic glucose production, thereby lowering fasting glucose levels and improving glycemic control [33, 34].

The modest weight loss in the metformin group in our study, i.e., MD: 0.9 ± 1.4 kg and BMI: 1.8 ± 2.2 kg/m2 after 12 weeks, aligns with prior studies, where average weight reductions are reported in the range of 2–4 kg over 12–24 weeks. However, patient adherence, gastrointestinal side effects, and variable insulin resistance severity can influence individual responses [35]. Metformin may contribute to weight reduction by influencing appetite and energy balance. It has been shown to decrease hunger and food intake, potentially through its effects on central nervous system pathways involved in appetite regulation. This anorexigenic effect, combined with improved insulin sensitivity, promotes a negative energy balance and subsequent weight loss [36, 37]. The mechanism of action for empagliflozin is to promote glycosuria and reduce plasma glucose levels, which can lead to meaningful weight reduction, improved glycemic control and cardiovascular protection. xxi While SGLT2 inhibitors such as empagliflozin are widely used in type 2 diabetes mellitus patients, their role in PCOS is less extensively studied. Our findings for the Empagliflozin group revealed a more pronounced weight reduction (MD: 6.7 ± 1.6 kg) and a decrease in BMI: 2.4 ± 0.7 kg/m2, which aligns with these emerging data. The weight-reducing effects of empagliflozin are thought to be mediated by its ability to increase urinary glucose excretion, leading to a negative energy balance. This mechanism is particularly beneficial in PCOS, where insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia contribute to weight gain and fat accumulation [38].

The comparative efficacy of empagliflozin versus metformin for metabolic dysfunction and weight reduction in women with PCOS reveals significant differences in anthropometric outcomes, although hormonal and metabolic parameters show less variation. Compared with metformin, which is a traditional insulin sensitizer, empagliflozin, an SGLT-2 inhibitor, has demonstrated superior effects on weight loss and body composition. In a randomized controlled study, women with PCOS treated with empagliflozin experienced a weight reduction of −1.4% compared with a weight increase of 1.2% in the metformin group (P = 0.006). Empagliflozin also resulted in reductions in body mass index (BMI), waist circumference, and fat mass, indicating a favorable impact on body composition [25]. A case study highlighted sustained weight loss over three years, with a total reduction of 46 kg in a patient using empagliflozin [39]. Empagliflozin improves glucose metabolism and reduces adipose tissue, which are crucial for managing PCOS symptoms. In studies comparing various treatments, empagliflozin demonstrated superior weight loss effects compared with other medications, such as dapagliflozin and phentermine/topiramate [40]. While empagliflozin shows significant potential for weight reduction in women with PCOS, it is essential to consider that individual responses may vary, and some studies indicate that weight loss effects can be more pronounced in men than in women [41].

Research conducted by G. Lempesis suggested that SGLT2 inhibitors, particularly empagliflozin, demonstrate positive metabolic and weight loss effects in women with PCOS compared with standard lifestyle modifications and pharmacotherapy, although no specific data on comparative efficacy were provided. Compared with GLP1RAs, empagliflozin demonstrated comparable efficacy with respect to weight loss and other metabolic changes. However, there is a suggestion that empagliflozin may reduce cardiovascular risk factors, including hypertension and dyslipidemia, which are commonly found in women with PCOS [42]. The ability of empagliflozin to increase glycosuria and decrease plasma glucose may significantly enhance the control of diabetes and the prevention of cardiovascular events [21]. Here, with the increasing prevalence of type 2 diabetes mellitus, the use of SGLT2 inhibitors such as empagliflozin is becoming common, but their use in individuals with PCOS is less studied. This explains why, in our study, longer-term use of empagliflozin resulted in lower levels of HbA1c (0.9 ± 0.2%) than did the use of metformin (0.4 ± 0.07%). The literature suggests that metformin has been recognized as a useful medication for treating the insulin resistance component of PCOS for years [43]. Its effects stem from its ability to decrease gluconeogenesis in the liver, increase insulin sensitivity, and partially help in weight loss.

The long-term use of empagliflozin in patients with PCOS has been associated with sustained weight loss and metabolic improvements. A meta-analysis of SGLT2 inhibitors, including empagliflozin, reported significant reductions in body weight, fasting plasma glucose, and insulin resistance over a 24-week period [44]. Additionally, empagliflozin has been shown to improve cardiovascular outcomes, which is particularly relevant for women with PCOS who are at increased risk of cardiovascular disease [31, 32].

PCOS is known to be associated with insulin resistance, which plays a role in the development of hyperandrogenism and other metabolic disorders [45]. While metformin decreases hepatic glucose output and enhances insulin sensitivity in peripheral areas, empagliflozin increases the excretion of glucose through the kidneys, which results in the loss of calories [46]. The combination of these treatments may improve the control of blood glucose levels and reduce body weight. However, there are differences among people in terms of the amount of insulin that can be secreted as well as genetic predispositions, which could affect the outcome [47]. SGLT2 inhibitors such as empagliflozin might increase appetite slightly or change thirst-related hormones, but the primary impact on weight is likely due to greater loss of energy through the urine [48]. Metformin, on the other hand, may lower the desire to eat while increasing appetite suppression, although its gastrointestinal side effects sometimes prevent its efficacy [49]. The nonsignificant variation in the mixed-effects model between metformin and empagliflozin, however, highlights the difficulty of metabolic intervention in patients with PCOS. Individual variation, variations in baseline insulin resistance and lifestyle variables (e.g., physical activity, dietary habits) could reduce the apparent effect size.

Comparably to metformin, in a preclinical study Empagliflozin at 5 mg/kg, 15 mg/kg, and 45 mg/kg doses demonstrated marked changes in metabolic and reproductive parameters. xxxi In addition, a meta-evaluation analysis of SGLT2 inhibitors including Empagliflozin showed they were more potent than metformin in reducing weight and plasma glucose concentrations. xliv On the safety profile, it is noted that Empagliflozin is well tolerated, and that the most common side effects of nausea and urinary tract infections are mild in severity [30, 50].

As a first-line treatment for PCOS, metformin aims at the limbs of insulin action and hyperglycemia. Heuristically, it does serve this purpose, but its ability to reduce androgens and weight is not remarkable. Compared with metformin, empagliflozin leads to increased body weight, insulin resistance and androgen secretion [31, 50]. In light of the nonsignificant differences between metformin and empagliflozin in the adjusted models, clinicians can apply metformin with empagliflozin and other step therapies while considering comorbidities, tolerance to therapy, and patient costs. It is also plausible that combining metformin with empagliflozin or adding GLP-1 receptor agonists could enhance metabolic control and might yield superior metabolic benefits [51]. Empagliflozin is most effective when it is administered with other medications, such as metformin or GLP1RAs, which aim to maximize therapeutic benefits. Empagliflozin and metformin were studied in a PCOS rat model and improved hormonal and metabolic features such as the ovarian cystic follicle count and inflammatory markers. l Additionally, the effect of combining GLP1RAs with metformin has led to more significant results than metformin alone, demonstrating an increased BMI and insulin resistance in obese PCOS women [52, 53].

Conclusion

This study contributes valuable insights into the comparative effectiveness of metformin and empagliflozin in overweight or obese women with PCOS and prediabetes. While the unadjusted analyses suggest that empagliflozin may offer more pronounced weight reduction and lipid movements, the final mixed-effects model indicates that these differences might not be statistically significant after accounting for confounders such as age and baseline activity levels. Despite the limitations in sample size and follow-up duration, the data underscore the potential role of SGLT2 inhibitors as alternatives or adjuncts to metformin for managing metabolic dysfunction in patients with PCOS.

PCOS is a complex endocrine disorder with significant cardiometabolic implications. Insulin resistance and prediabetes are central to the development of cardiovascular risk in women with PCOS. Ultimately, multidisciplinary approaches that combine lifestyle interventions, personalized pharmacotherapy and robust follow-up are imperative for addressing the complex metabolic and endocrine challenges associated with PCOS. Further research, particularly in low-resource settings, will be essential to optimize therapeutic strategies and reduce the burden of metabolic disorders among women with PCOS and prediabetes in Pakistan and beyond.

Strengths

A double-blinded randomized study design minimizes bias and increases the internal validity of the findings. Furthermore, conducting the trial in slums of Karachi deepens the focus on including a socioeconomically disadvantaged population, showing generalizability to low-resource settings. The evaluation of lipids, glycemic parameters and anthropometric measurements offers a holistic view of metabolic health.

Limitations

The relatively small sample size (n = 80) and notable loss to follow-up (30.8% in the metformin group, 14.6% in the empagliflozin group) might reduce the statistical power and mask potential group differences. PCOS is a chronic condition, and metabolic improvements may take longer to manifest. A longer follow-up could provide more robust data on sustained weight loss and glycemic control. Although participants received lifestyle modification guidance, variations in diet, physical activity and socioeconomic constraints were not rigorously controlled, potentially influencing the results.

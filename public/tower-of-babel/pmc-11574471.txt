Introduction

The increase in the frequency, severity and consequences of disasters, from natural hazards and the impacts of climate change, to wars, conflicts, terrorism and the COVID-19 pandemic, have placed additional force on healthcare systems, especially hospitals.1,3 The crucial role of hospitals in providing medical care during emergencies and disasters emphasises the importance of these facilities to remain accessible and functional.4 As an essential infrastructure in emergency response, hospitals must not only continue caring for current patients but also effectively meet the needs of those affected by disasters, enhancing their service quality and ensuring safety for all patients, staff and visitors.5 6 Because of this, hospitals are one of the key infrastructures and focal points during emergencies and disasters in every society.7 This fact has highlighted the significance of hospital resilience in delivering uninterrupted services during emergencies and disasters. However, recent years have seen disasters causing damage to hospitals and interruptions in medical services,8 9 leading to a growing emphasis on the concept of hospital resilience.

Hospital resilience is defined as the ‘hospital’s ability to withstand, absorb, adapt to, and recover from disasters while maintaining essential functions and either restoring its original state or transforming to a better situation’.10 11 Yet, the literature presents diverse and sometimes conflicting definitions and frameworks for conceptualising hospital resilience, with a lack of consensus on its operationalisation and assessment within the disaster risk management cycle.7 Because of its complex and nuanced nature, the concept of hospital resilience encompasses a diverse of definitions, terms and multifaceted components;10 even, the terms safety, preparedness and resilience are often used synonymously in hospitals, despite their distinct meanings.12 13

Understanding hospital disaster resilience and the factors influencing it is crucial for planning and enhancing effective emergency response services.1 14 Therefore, it is vital to accurately identify the factors that impact hospital resilience and their unique characteristics. Despite extensive research and evidence, the lack of clarity in explaining the concept of hospital resilience poses challenges in effectively assessing it and identifying key priorities for improving disaster risk management in the future. Consequently, the need for a comprehensive tool for hospital resilience assessment, designed based on an agreed conceptual framework, is more vital than ever. Such a tool should address the remaining gaps left by previous frameworks and tools.10 Hospital resilience assessment is a critical process that aims to pinpoint areas that need improvement and significant interventions to mitigate complications. As a result, researchers and policymakers have explored various factors that influence hospital resilience and have documented a range of strategies to enhance hospital disaster resilience.4 15 16 However, there is a noticeable gap in the availability of a comprehensive and dynamic tool that can assess hospital resilience throughout the four phases of the disaster risk management cycle.10 This gap underscores the importance of developing a new tool to ensure hospitals are resilient in the face of challenges.

Recognising the importance of this issue and in alignment with the concept’s explanation as a guide for operationalising and assessing hospital resilience, the WHO’s Eastern Mediterranean Regional Office (EMRO) has provided a comprehensive conceptual framework for conceptualisation, operationalisation and assessment of hospital resilience.10 This framework defines hospital resilience as encompassing ‘six interconnected components: space, staff, stuff, systems, strategies, and services, and four resilience capacities: absorptive, adaptive, transformative, and learning’. These components and capacities lead to the primary outcome where resilient hospitals maintain their essential functions and swiftly return to their original or a new adjusted condition.7 10 Within this framework, hospitals aiming for resilience are encouraged to apply the outlined capacities throughout all phases of a disaster management cycle by adopting a comprehensive risk-informed approach that aligns with the mentioned components. Furthermore, this framework serves as a practical guide for identifying, implementing, monitoring and assessing the key areas to strengthen hospital resilience against upcoming disasters.10

In recent years, despite the development of numerous indicators to assess structural and non-structural aspects, there remains a need to assess other aspects of hospital resilience, such as functional, strategic and communication components, as well as the interconnection between these components.17 18 Since the conceptual framework provided by WHO outlines the components and capacities that influence hospital resilience, it is essential to identify the indicators and characteristics specific to these components and capacities. Additionally, the lack of quantifiable indicators for hospital resilience emphasises the complex nature of the concept and highlights the need to identify more inclusive assessment indicators. Consequently, to develop a comprehensive assessment tool, it is vital to initiate a scoping review study in the initial phase to analyse the existing evidence and knowledge,19 which will then guide the subsequent stages of tool development. This protocol describes the methodology for conducting the scoping review about the indicators and characteristics specific to the components of hospital resilience assessment.

Objective

The objective of this scoping review is to identify indicators and characteristics specific to the components of hospital resilience, as outlined by the WHO framework, to develop a comprehensive assessment tool. This protocol details the methodology for conducting the scoping review on these indicators and characteristics.

Methods and analysis

A scoping review will be conducted to synthesise existing knowledge in a specific field.20 The purpose of this study is to gain a comprehensive understanding of current knowledge related to hospital resilience, including the components and effective indicators for its assessment. The review will follow the methodology initially presented by Arksey and O’Malley,21 which was later refined by Levac et al22 and will be drafted using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis with the extension for scoping reviews.23 This scoping review will proceed through six stages.

Step 2: identifying relevant studies and designing a search strategy

In the second step, we will conduct a comprehensive search of relevant articles through health databases including PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science. Additionally, we will search the Google Scholar search engine and explore relevant grey literature associated with target organisations, including the WHO and its regional offices, as well as evidence-producing entities in the field of disaster risk management, such as Federal Emergency Management Agancy (FEMA). Our search strategy (table 1) will involve incorporating search terms that cover all aspects of hospital resilience, in Mesh terms, titles, abstracts, and keywords. Furthermore, we will carefully explore the reference lists of identified sources, using relevant articles to enhance our search precision. Additionally, to ensure comprehensive coverage of the search and to complete the review, we will perform a hand search of studies and conduct a reference check of highly relevant articles. It should be mentioned we will not apply any publication date restrictions to the search.

Step 3: study selection

In this stage, the research team will initiate the process by using the software Mendeley to gather all acquired resources and eliminate any duplicate entries. Two independent reviewers will carefully assess the titles and abstracts of identified articles. They will apply predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria (table 2) during this evaluation. Subsequently, Articles meeting all eligibility criteria will proceed to the next phase. The sources of the extracted articles will be closely examined to identify any supplementary literature. During the title and abstract review phase, consensus between the reviewers will guide the selection of articles that meet the eligibility criteria. Reviewers will meet at the different stages of the abstract review process to tackle any challenges or uncertainties related to the selection of studies and may revise the search strategy, if necessary. In case of discrepancies regarding the articles, a third reviewer may be consulted for resolution and final approval. Finally, two reviewers will screen the full articles included in the study.

To ensure a thorough and accurate analysis within our linguistic capabilities, we decided to limit our search to English and Persian. While this may restrict the global representation of our findings, our primary objective was to provide a comprehensive review within regions where these languages are predominantly used.

Data extraction and charting

In this stage, we will conduct a comprehensive review of studies specifically addressing the components and indicators effective in assessing hospital resilience and the characteristics of resilient hospitals. The data extraction process adheres to a structured form that will be designed and approved by the researchers. At first, two independent reviewers will perform data extraction from the initial five studies using a pilot structured checklist. Their goal is to determine whether their data extraction approach aligns with the research objectives and inquiries based on pre-made forms. Any modifications to the form following the completion of the pilot phase will be documented. After ensuring the accuracy of the exploration, the data from other studies will also be extracted. Subsequently, the reviewers will convene to deliberate on the compatibility of their methodologies and research goals.

The gathered data will include details such as author(s) and publication year, country of origin, the study aims, methodology, effective components, influencing factors and indicators for assessing hospital resilience and disaster types. If necessary, adjustments to the data extraction process may be considered and implemented based on the review and extraction procedures of the studies. Data will be extracted from selected studies using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft, USA).

Collating, summarizing and reporting the result

In this step, we will conduct data analysis, report results and interpret the findings. Initially, we will focus on quantitative data, including the total number of studies reviewed. Additionally, we will provide a descriptive evaluation encompassing the following attributes of the incorporated studies: study design, country of origin, methodologies, year of study, other relevant characteristics and the categorisation of publications and studies. These quantitative aspects will be presented in the form of tables, figures and graphs. Furthermore, we will explore both quantitative and qualitative indicators for assessing hospital resilience, aligning them with existing literature. Extracted indicators and components will be tabulated alongside the evidence of their validity and reliability. Ultimately, our findings will be visualised through tables following the EMRO framework, with the possibility of including new themes if necessary.

In the subsequent phase, postdata extraction from the literature, we will categorise indicators based on the components and capacities outlined in the EMRO conceptual framework. We will carefully consider the strengths and limitations of our approach. Quantitative and qualitative content analyses will be employed to assess and categorise the study findings. Finally, we will integrate data from diverse sources, providing a comprehensive outline that enhances the precision and credibility of our conclusions. The scrutinised data will contribute to achieving our research objectives, clearing knowledge gaps, identifying existing challenges and also offering valuable insights for future research.

External consultation

Reasons for engaging with stakeholders may include asking for feedback on a research question and offering profound insights into a specific subject matter. Other objectives might involve acquiring input to clarify review findings and identify unaddressed gaps within the existing literature.

At this stage, the initial findings of the fifth phase, depicted through tables and graphs, will serve as the foundation for consulting with specialists. A corresponding summary will be provided. During this phase, interviews or focus groups will be conducted to enable experts to articulate their viewpoints on the findings, thereby fostering the exchange of knowledge. While stakeholder engagement is typically delineated as the ultimate phase of the review, it has the flexibility to be integrated throughout the review process.

Timeline

The study began at the end of September 2024, with the manuscript tentatively scheduled to be finalised by April 2025.

Discussion

The upcoming scoping review is poised to noticeably impact the decision-making processes of hospital administrators and policymakers in the field of health emergencies and disasters. Furthermore, it tries to make an important contribution to the body of research on hospital resilience and its assessment, thereby strengthening the overall health system. In this scoping review, we will examine the existing knowledge related to indicators and components effective in assessing hospital resilience. Identifying existing gaps will enhance our understanding and improve the quality of research evidence in this field.

On the other hand, considering the novelty of the conceptual framework for hospital resilience provided by EMRO,10 conducting a scoping review study can strengthen our comprehension of the characteristics of the components introduced in this framework. Additionally, through careful exploration and analysis of evidence from relevant studies, we can take steps towards enhancing performance and evidence-based decision-making in the field of hospital resilience assessment.

Finally, recognising that this scoping review marks the initial phase of a tool development effort to assess hospital resilience, it serves as a foundational cornerstone for explaining concepts and introducing broader and more recent indicators aligned with EMRO’s conceptual framework.

Ethics and dissemination

This protocol outlines the procedures for conducting a scoping review study, which forms an integral part of a doctoral thesis in the field of health in emergencies and disasters. The study is associated with an ethics code number (IR.USWR.REC.1402.120). The anticipated results from this scoping review will align with the development of a comprehensive tool for hospital resilience assessment. Following the peer-review process and subsequent publication in scientific journals, these findings will be disseminated to experts, policymakers and relevant organisations involved in the management of emergencies and disasters.

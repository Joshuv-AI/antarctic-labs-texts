Rational points and rational moduli spaces
Shijie Fan, Rafael von Känel

Abstract
Let X X be a variety over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . We introduce a geometric non-degenerate criterion for X X using moduli spaces M M over ℚ \mathbb{Q} of abelian varieties. If X X is non-degenerate, then we construct via M M an open dense moduli space U ⊆ X U\subseteq X whose forgetful map defines a Paršin construction for U ⁡ ( ℚ ) U(\mathbb{Q}) . For example if M M is a Hilbert modular variety then U U is a coarse Hilbert moduli scheme and X X is non-degenerate iff a projective model Y ⊂ M ¯ Y\subset\bar{M} of X X over ℚ \mathbb{Q} contains no singular points of the minimal compactification M ¯ \bar{M} . We motivate our constructions when M M is a rational variety over ℚ \mathbb{Q} with dim ( M ) > dim ( X ) \dim(M)>\dim(X) . We study various geometric aspects of the non-degenerate criterion and we deduce arithmetic applications: If X X is non-degenerate, then U ⁡ ( ℚ ) U(\mathbb{Q}) is finite by Faltings. Moreover, our constructions are made for the effective strategy which combines the method of Faltings (Arakelov, Paršin, Szpiro) with modularity and Masser–Wüstholz isogeny estimates. When M M is a coarse Hilbert moduli scheme, we use this strategy to explicitly bound the height and the number of x ∈ U ⁡ ( ℚ ) x\in U(\mathbb{Q}) if X X is non-degenerate. We illustrate our approach in the case when M M is the Hilbert modular surface given by the classical icosahedron surface studied by Clebsch, Klein and Hirzebruch. For any curve X X over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , we construct and study explicit projective models Y ⊂ M ¯ Y\subset\bar{M} called ico models. If X X is non-degenerate, then we give via Y Y an effective Paršin construction and an explicit Weil height bound for x ∈ U ⁡ ( ℚ ) x\in U(\mathbb{Q}) . As most ico models are non-degenerate and X ∖ U X\setminus U is controlled, this establishes the effective Mordell conjecture for large classes of (explicit) curves over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . We also solve the ico analogue of the generalized Fermat problem by combining our height bounds with Diophantine approximations.

are given in

. Intuitively one can think of the prime divisors of

as the closed points of

over which a ‘minimal model of

in

’ degenerates.

The Hilbert case refers to the case when

is a coarse Hilbert moduli scheme. In particular we can apply Theorem G (i) and (ii) with any Hilbert modular variety

over
\mathbb{Q}
. As explained below, this gives for each

large classes of integral projective varieties

over
\mathbb{Q}
of dimension

satisfies criterion
(M)
and Theorem G (i) and (ii) hold for

with

. It is an open problem to explicitly construct such

[

]
.

Main idea.

We next explain the idea underlying our construction in Theorem

. The key observation is as follows: If
M
is a rational variety over
\mathbb{Q}
, then any integral variety

over
\mathbb{Q}
with
\dim(X)<\dim(M)
has models in
\bar{M}
and the variety
X
satisfies
(M)
iff at least one of these models is disjoint to
\bar{M}^{\textnormal{deg}}
. In particular if

(1)\ M\textnormal{ is a rational variety over }\mathbb{Q}\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad(2)\ m=\dim(\bar{M})-\dim(\bar{M}^{\textnormal{deg}})\geq 2,

(1.4)

then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n<m
there exist many integral varieties
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
of dimension
n
which satisfy criterion
(M)
. For each of these

, we can apply Theorem G to
U(\mathbb{Q})
and we are reduced to study

where

has dimension at most
\dim(X)-1
. This construction motivates to find
M
and
\bar{M}
which have properties (1) and (2).

Hilbert case.

Let

. Many coarse Hilbert moduli schemes
M
over
\mathbb{Q}
have property (2). For example any Hilbert modular variety

over
\mathbb{Q}
of dimension

has property (2) since

. For each such

over

of dimension

and Theorem G (i) and (ii) hold for

The rationality condition in (1) is very restrictive. In particular most Hilbert modular varieties are not rational by Tsuyumine
[

]
. However there exist several Hilbert modular surfaces

over
\mathbb{Q}
which are known to have property (1). To discuss an example, we denote by

of principal level 2 for the totally real field

. In particular

has property (1).

Given

with (1) and (2), one can try to construct explicit classes of varieties

over
\mathbb{Q}
satisfying criterion

and apply Theorem G (ii). For instance the results for non-degenerate curves in
\mathsection

) for

,
\mathsection

. Here

vKK23
,
\mathsection
11]
proves that

extends over the explicit base

to the minimal compactification of a coarse Hilbert moduli scheme over

with empty branch locus. This is crucial for applying Theorem G (ii) with an effective

, which in turn is crucial for deducing the effective results in
\mathsection

.

General case.

We are currently trying to use our construction (
1.4
) to find new interesting classes of varieties for which one can deduce via Theorem G (i) finiteness results from Faltings
[
Fal83
]
. For large classes of projective (subvarieties of) Shimura varieties, Deligne–Szpiro
[

, p.98]
and Ullmo
[

]
established the finiteness of rational points via Faltings
[

]
. Moreover Javankpeykar–Loughran
[

]
formalized this line of reasoning, see e.g.
[

,
\mathsection

Another motivation for proving Theorem G (i) in general was as follows: If one can prove (conditional) height bounds in new situations (e.g. conditional on Venkatesh’s height conjecture

,

]
), then one can directly deduce the (conditional) analogue of Theorem G (ii) in these new situations and can combine it with our construction (

Number fields.

The constructions underlying Theorem G (i) are geometric and Faltings’ finiteness results

[

\mathsection

.

Section 3
Moduli formalism

We introduce in this section terminology from the moduli problem formalism which is useful for (effectively) studying Paršin constructions. The formalism was developed by Katz–Mazur
[
KM85
]
for elliptic curves via Mumford’s language of relative representability
[
Mum65
]
. Those parts which are relevant for us were extended via the language of algebraic stacks to the Hilbert case in
[
vKK19
,
vKK23
]
and to all abelian varieties in
[
vK24
]
.

Moduli schemes.

Let
g\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
be the category fibred in groupoids over
(Sch)
of abelian schemes of relative dimension
g
, and let
\mathcal{P}
be a presheaf on
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
. We call
\mathcal{P}
a moduli problem. For each object

of
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
the elements of
\mathcal{P}(A)
are called
\mathcal{P}
-level structures of
A
. Let
Y
be a scheme. We say that
Y
is a moduli scheme of
\mathcal{P}
, and we write
Y=M_{\mathcal{P}}
, if there exists an object in
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}(Y)
representing
\mathcal{P}
. For example, consider the moduli problem

\mathcal{P}_{g,n}:\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}\to\textnormal{(Sets)},\quad A\mapsto\textnormal{Pol}(A)\times\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)

where
\textnormal{Pol}(A)
denotes the set of principal polarizations of
A
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}(A)
denotes for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
the set of principal level
n
-structures on
A
as in
[
FC90
, p.5]
. There exists a moduli scheme

of
\mathcal{P}_{g,n}
if

, which is automatically a
\mathbb{Z}[1/n]
-scheme since
\mathcal{P}_{g,n}
is defined over
\mathbb{Z}[1/n]
. However there exists no moduli scheme of
\mathcal{P}_{g,n}
if
n\leq 2
.

Coarse moduli schemes.

Write
\mathcal{M}
for
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a moduli problem on
\mathcal{M}
and let
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{P}}
be the category fibred in groupoids over
(Sch)
of pairs
(A,\alpha)
with

and
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(A)
. We say that
\mathcal{P}
is algebraic (resp. arithmetic) if

is an algebraic stack (resp. a DM-stack which is separated and of finite type over
\mathbb{Z}
). Let

be a scheme and suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is algebraic. We say that

is a coarse moduli scheme of
\mathcal{P}
, and we write

, if there exists a coarse moduli space
\pi:\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{P}}\to Y
in the usual sense (
[
Ols16
,
\mathsection
11]
); we call

an initial morphism. The branch locus

of

is defined as the complement in

of the union of all open

such that
\pi_{U}
is étale. For example, if
Y
is a moduli scheme of
\mathcal{P}
then it is a coarse moduli scheme of
\mathcal{P}
whose branch locus

is empty.

Isomorphism classes.

The presheaf
\underline{A}_{g}=[\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}]
on
(Sch)
classifies isomorphism classes of abelian schemes of relative dimension

. If

then

is a separated DM-stack over

. However the category
\underline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
over
(Sch)
and the presheaf

Forgetful map.

Let
Y
be a coarse moduli scheme of an arithmetic moduli problem

on
\mathcal{M}
with initial morphism
\pi:\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{P}}\to Y
, and let

be an algebraically closed field. Composing

with

defines the forgetful map

\phi_{k}:Y(k)\to\underline{A}_{g}(k).

(3.1)

Let

be a map for subrings

and

of

. We say that
\phi_{k}
defines

if the restriction of

to

. Let

be a field with algebraic closure

. We say that a subset

of
\underline{A}_{g}(T)
is

-stable if for each

and any

of abelian varieties over

.

Finite level.

We say that a variety

over
\mathbb{Z}
is a coarse moduli scheme of finite level if it is a coarse moduli scheme of an arithmetic moduli problem
\mathcal{P}
on

satisfying:

(i)

The set

and for any connected Dedekind scheme

(ii)

, a scheme

and a finite étale cover

Here (i) assures (

,
\mathsection

) that the forgetful map

is finite. Both (i) and (ii) are usually satisfied in situations of interest in arithmetic. In particular for all

the coarse moduli scheme

.

Hilbert case.

On replacing in the above definitions the category

Over

.

Let

3.1
Paršin constructions via forgetful maps

The moduli formalism allows to (effectively) study Paršin constructions via the forgetful map (
3.1
) of coarse moduli schemes of finite level. This approach to Paršin constructions has useful features as explained in
[
vK24
,
\mathsection

. It allows to conceptually simplify/unify many of the known constructions and it can provide crucial advantages for the effective study. For example it can be beneficial for solving equations (
[

,
\mathsection

). Moreover it can be very useful for finding/studying new (effective) Paršin constructions with additional geometric properties. Here an important property of the forgetful map (

) is its compatibility with the
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-action: This assures that the image of the resulting Paršin construction is

-stable, which is crucial for
[
vKK23
]
and for the effective results in this paper. This

-stability is one of the key advantages of Paršin constructions via forgetful maps compared to other Paršin constructions for curves (e.g. via Kodaira’s construction involving étale covers) which are not known to be compatible with
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. On the other hand, many of the other Paršin constructions work in general (i.e. for all relevant curves) and an effective Kodaira construction
[
Kod67
]
is known in general by Rémond
[
Rém99
]
.

Non-degenerate varieties and Paršin constructions

In this section we first state in Theorem

4.1
Results

Let

be a variety over
\mathbb{Q}
which extends over a nonempty open of

to a tame coarse moduli scheme of finite level, and let
\bar{M}
be a compactification of

be a variety over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Non-degenerate varieties in

.

A model of

in

is a closed subscheme

of
\bar{M}
such that there exist dense open

with an isomorphism

is non-degenerate in

if it has a model

in

with

(4.1)

for

the branch locus of

. Here the terminology ‘non-degenerate’ is compatible (
\mathsection

) with the theory of degenerations of abelian varieties
[

,

]
. Our motivation for studying varieties which are non-degenerate in

comes from Theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

Let
X
be a variety over
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that
X
is non-degenerate in
\bar{M}
. Then there exists a dense open
U\subseteq X
which is a coarse moduli scheme over
\mathbb{Q}
of finite level with empty branch locus. Its forgetful map

defines a Paršin construction

and thus

is finite by Faltings.

(ii)

Firstly, if

is a rational variety over

then each integral variety

over
\mathbb{Q}
with

has a model in
\bar{M}
. Secondly, if

is an integral projective variety over

then for each

with

there exist infinitely many integral closed subschemes

of dimension

.

(iii)

Any curve over
\mathbb{Q}
which is non-degenerate in

has geometric genus
\geq 2
.

Building on the constructions in
[
vKK23
]
, we will obtain in Theorem

the finite map

of (i). Moreover Theorem

gives additional geometric properties of

and

which are important for the effective study of

via

. We shall deduce the geometric statement (iii) from the proof of (i) and Faltings’ finiteness result
[

4.2
Terminology

In this section we first briefly discuss the origin of the notion ‘non-degenerate’. Then we introduce some terminology which we shall use in the effective study of Paršin constructions. We continue our notation and we let
M\subseteq\bar{M}
be as in
\mathsection

.

Non-degenerate.

A non-degenerate model in
\bar{M}
is defined as a closed subscheme of
\bar{M}
which is disjoint to
\bar{M}^{\textnormal{deg}}
. The notion ‘non-degenerate’ comes from the following property of any non-degenerate model

in
\bar{M}
: The points of
Y
parametrize (étale locally) semi-abelian varieties which are all abelian and thus non-degenerate. This is compatible with Faltings–Chai
[
FC90
,
Cha90
]
when
M
is a Siegel or Hilbert modular variety with minimal compactification
\bar{M}
, and with Lan
[
Lan13
]
when
M
is a Shimura variety of PEL-type.

Controlled
U\subseteq X
.

We next introduce terminology which allows to ‘measure’ quantities appearing in Paršin constructions defined by forgetful maps of coarse moduli schemes. Let
X
be a variety over
\mathbb{Q}
. For any model

of

in
\bar{M}
, we consider a dense open

(4.2)

with a dominant open immersion

-schemes such that: If

Integral degeneration.

For what follows we need to specify a suitable base scheme: Let

over

extends over

7.1
Geometric results

To study rational points on non-degenerate curves, we prove several geometric results for such curves and their ico models. In this section we summarize our geometric results.

Geometric properties.

Theorem
4.1
(ii) combined with a classical construction going back at least to the works of Clebsch
[
Cle71
]
and Klein
[
Kle73
]
gives part (i) of the following result. On the other hand, part (ii) is a consequence of Theorem
4.1
(iii).

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(i)

If
X
is integral, then it has an ico model over
\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

If
X
is non-degenerate, then
X
has geometric genus
\geq 2
.

Our proof of the geometric statement in (ii) crucially relies on arithmetic since Theorem
4.1
(iii) uses Faltings’ finiteness result
[
Fal83
, Thm 6]
. It would be interesting to find a purely geometric proof of (ii). The converse of Corollary
7.1
(ii) is an open problem:

(deg)
Determine which curves over
\mathbb{Q}
of geometric genus
\geq 2
are non-degenerate.

In ico form most curves over
\mathbb{Q}
of geometric genus
\geq 2
are non-degenerate by Proposition
7.2
below, and we solve problem
(deg)
for certain classes of plane curves in Section
8
. However
(deg)
is still widely open and solving it in general requires new ideas.

General ico models.

We next discuss basic geometric properties of general ico models. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{r}
be monomials in
\mathbb{Q}[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{4}]
of degree
n
which form a basis of the
\mathbb{Q}
-module given by the
n
-th graded part
A_{n}
of
A=\mathbb{Q}[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{4}]/(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{4})
. Here

r=4n^{2}-4n+6

(7.1)

if
n\geq 2
and
r=5
if
n=1
, and
\sigma_{k}
denotes the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial where
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We may and do assume that
s_{i}=x_{i-1}^{n}
for
i\leq 5
.
Write
\mathbb{P}^{4}=\mathbb{P}^{4}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\mathbb{A}^{r}=\mathbb{A}^{r}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. For each
v\in\mathbb{A}^{r}(\mathbb{Q})
, we consider the closed subscheme
X_{v}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
defined by

X_{v}\cong\textnormal{Proj}(A/f)\cong X_{f},\quad f=\sum v_{i}s_{i}.

(7.2)

We say that a general ico model
X_{v}
over
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
n
has a property
\mathcal{P}
if there exists an open dense
U\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{r}
such that
X_{v}
has property
\mathcal{P}
for all
v\in U(\mathbb{Q})
. Part (i) of the following proposition solves the above problem
(deg)
for a general ico model
X_{v}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proposition 7.2
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following statements hold.

(i)

A general ico model
X_{v}
over
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
n
is a non-degenerate curve over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(ii)

A general ico model
X_{v}
over
\mathbb{Q}
of degree
n
is a smooth, projective and geometrically connected curve over
\mathbb{Q}
of genus
(2n+1)^{2}
which is non-degenerate.

(iii)

If
U\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{r}
denotes the open dense subset of (ii), then
v\mapsto X_{v}
injects
U(\mathbb{Q})/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
into the set
\mathcal{C}
of smooth, projective and geometrically connected curves inside
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.

In (i) we can take the explicit
U^{\textnormal{nd}}=\mathbb{A}^{r}\setminus\bigl(\cup_{i=1}^{5}V(z_{i})\bigl)
, and Bertini applied with the normal surface
\textnormal{Proj}(A)
over
\mathbb{Q}
gives the open dense
U\subseteq U^{\textnormal{nd}}
of (ii). Let
g\in\mathbb{N}
. If
g\geq 2
is an odd square, then taking
n=\tfrac{\sqrt{g}-1}{2}
in Proposition
7.2
provides a moduli space
U
of dimension
r\sim g
by (
7.1
) which parametrizes non-degenerate curves in
\mathcal{C}
of genus
g
.

Moduli interpretation.

We deduce from Theorem
5.1
and
[
vKK23
, Thm E]
that any non-degenerate curve
X
admits an open dense
U\subseteq X
whose points have (étale locally) a simple moduli interpretation in terms of abelian surfaces. More precisely we deduce:

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
which is non-degenerate, and let
U\subseteq X
be open.

(i)

Suppose that
U
is of the form
U=U_{f}
with
X_{f}
a non-degenerate ico model of
X
over
\mathbb{Z}
. Then
U
extends over a nonempty open
S\subseteq\textnormal{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
to a coarse Hilbert moduli scheme
U_{S}
of finite level with empty branch locus and with moduli interpretation
(*)
.

(ii)

Moduli interpretation
(*)
: Let
k
be an algebraically closed field with
\textnormal{char}(k)\in S
. Any point in
U_{S}(k)
identifies with an isomorphism class of
(A,\alpha)
where
A
is a principally polarized abelian surface over
k
with real multiplication
\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}]\hookrightarrow\textup{End}(A)
and where
\alpha
is a restricted symplectic level 2-structure of
A
as in (
7.9
).

(iii)

In the case
X=(X_{f})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, one can take
U=X
and
S=\mathbb{Z}[1/\nu]
with
\nu=30\nu_{f}
.

Here we recall from
\mathsection
1.3
that
\nu_{f}
is defined as follows. If
f=(f_{j})
and
\sum a_{ij}x_{i}^{n_{j}}
is the diagonal part of the homogeneous
f_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{4}]
of degree
n_{j}\geq 1
, then

\nu_{f}=\textnormal{rad}\bigl(\prod a_{ij}\bigl)

(7.3)

with the product taken over all nonzero diagonal coefficients
a_{ij}
of
f=(f_{j})
.

7.2
Rational points

In this section we first give an effective Paršin construction for the rational points of non-degenerate curves over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then we deduce explicit bounds for the rational points of such curves. We work over
\mathbb{Q}
and we write
\mathbb{P}^{n}
for
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}}
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Degree and height.

Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that
X
admits a finite morphism
X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then pulling back the usual (
[
BG06
, p.16]
) logarithmic Weil height
h_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
along
X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
, defines a (logarithmic) Weil height

h:X(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})\to\mathbb{R}.

(7.4)

Assume
X
is integral. To define a normalized degree of
X
, write
\deg(X)
for the degree of the scheme theoretic image of
X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
and let
\tilde{X}\to X
be a normalization. Then we take

d_{X}=\deg(\tilde{X}\to X)\deg(X).

To define a height of
X
, choose an immersion
U_{f}\hookrightarrow(X_{f})_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
for
X_{f}
an ico model of
X
over
\mathbb{Z}
. This induces a finite morphism
\varphi:\tilde{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{4}
via the universal property of normalization of curves.
The composition
\psi:\tilde{X}\to X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
is also finite. Then we define

h(X)=h_{1,1}\bigl((\varphi,\psi)(\tilde{X})\bigl).

Here
h_{1,1}
is the biprojective height
[
Rém99
,
\mathsection
5]
of index (1,1), and
(\varphi,\psi)(\tilde{X})
is the scheme theoretic image of the composition
\tilde{X}\to^{\Delta}\tilde{X}\times\tilde{X}\to^{\varphi\times\psi}\mathbb{P}^{4}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
. In what follows, when writing ‘let
h
be a Weil height on
X
as in (
7.4
)’ we always assume that we are given a finite morphism
X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
and when using the quantities
d_{X}
and
h(X)
we always assume that they are defined with respect to this given
X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
and a chosen immersion
U_{f}\hookrightarrow(X_{f})_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

7.2.1
Effective Paršin and effective Mordell for non-degenerate curves

Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
which is non-degenerate and let
U\subseteq X
be open. Suppose that
U=U_{f}
for some non-degenerate ico model
X_{f}
of
X
over
\mathbb{Z}
and let
\nu_{f}
be as in (
7.3
).

Effective Paršin.

Corollary
7.3
gives that
U
is a coarse Hilbert moduli scheme over
\mathbb{Q}
. We deduce from Theorem
5.1
an effective Paršin construction for
U(\mathbb{Q})
.

Corollary 7.4
.

The forgetful map
U(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})\to\underline{A}_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
of the coarse moduli scheme
U
defines an effective Paršin construction
\phi:U(\mathbb{Q})\to\underline{A}_{2}(T)
with the following properties.

(i)

The image
\phi(U(\mathbb{Q}))
admits for
(d,n)=(3^{8},30\nu_{f})
an effective covering
\cup_{T}J(T)
of
\textnormal{GL}_{2}
-type with
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-isogenies in the sense of (
4.4
).

(ii)

Let
h
be a Weil height on
X
as in (
7.4
) and let
h_{\phi}
be the pullback of
h_{F}
along
U(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})\to\underline{A}_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
. Firstly, if
X
is integral then these heights on
U(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfy

h\leq d_{X}\bar{h}_{\phi}+h(X),\ \ \quad\bar{h}_{\phi}=2h_{\phi}+8^{8}\log(h_{\phi}+8).

Secondly, if
X=(X_{f})_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
h=h_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}
, then
X=U
and it holds
h\leq\bar{h}_{\phi}
on
X(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
.

We now briefly discuss (ii). The second bound
h\leq\bar{h}_{\phi}
is essentially optimal: This bound and the function
\bar{h}_{\phi}
both come from
[
vKK23
, Prop 11.13]
, see
[
vKK23
,
\mathsection
11.5]
for discussions of the shape of
\bar{h}_{\phi}
and of the constants appearing in
\bar{h}_{\phi}
. The first bound
h\leq d_{X}\bar{h}_{\phi}+h(X)
is relatively simple but rarely optimal: Its proof combines
[
vKK23
, Prop 11.13]
with Rémond’s comparison
[
Rém99
, Prop 5.2]
of Weil heights on curves.

Effective Mordell.

Let
h
be a Weil height on
X
as in (
7.4
). The effective Paršin construction of Corollary
7.4
combined with the bound for
h_{\phi}
in Corollary
5.3
gives the following estimate for the Weil height
h
in which
c=10^{\kappa}
for
\kappa=10^{12}
.

Corollary 7.5
.

If
X
is integral, then any
x\in U(\mathbb{Q})
satisfies
h(x)\leq cd_{X}\nu_{f}^{24}+h(X)
.

Usually one can directly control
h
on the finite set
(X\setminus U)(\mathbb{Q})
and thus on the whole
X(\mathbb{Q})
by Corollary
7.5
. In Section
8
we illustrate this for plane curves
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
and we show how one can control
\nu_{f}
in terms of
X
. We also obtain the following result.

Corollary 7.6
.

If
X=(X_{f})_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
h=h_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}
, then any
x\in X(\mathbb{Q})
satisfies
h(x)\leq c\nu_{f}^{24}
.

In the above bounds, the term
c\nu_{f}^{24}
comes from
[
vK21
,
vKK23
]
and we refer to these works for discussions of various aspects of this bound; see for example
[
vKK23
,
\mathsection
1.3.3]
.

The above height bounds have useful features. For example
\nu_{f}
is independent of the degrees of
f=(f_{j})
. This allows us to solve in Section
9
an analogue of the Fermat problem by combining Corollary
7.6
with Diophantine approximations. Moreover
\nu_{f}
only depends on the diagonal parts of
f=(f_{j})
. Hence varying the non-diagonal parts shows the following: Corollary
7.5
is of the form
c^{*}d_{X}+h(X)
for large classes of
X
, and Corollary
7.6
is uniform for many
f
. For the number of rational points, very strong uniform bounds were established by Dimitrov–Gao–Habegger+Kühne
[
DGH21
,
Kuh21
]
and Yuan
[
Yua21
]
: Their bounds depend only on the genus and the MW rank, and they hold for all smooth, projective and geometrically connected curves of genus
\geq 2
over any number field.

7.3.1
Equivalence of non-degenerate notions and proof of Corollary
7.1

In
\mathsection

we introduced a notion ‘non-degenerate’, while in
\mathsection

we introduced a notion ‘non-degenerate in

’. To relate these notions, we write
Z_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}_{\mathbb{Q}}
for the set of five closed points obtained by permuting the coordinates of
(1,0\dotsc,0)
and we observe that

M=\bar{M}\setminus Z_{\mathbb{Q}}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\bar{M}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}_{\mathbb{Q}}:\sigma_{2}=0=\sigma_{4}

(7.5)

satisfy all assumptions of
\mathsection
4.1
for

as in (

). Indeed
\bar{M}
is a compactification of

, and it follows from
[

, Thm E and
\mathsection
5.2]
that

extends to a tame coarse Hilbert moduli scheme
M_{\mathbb{S}}
over
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{Z}[1/30]
of finite level with degeneracy locus
\bar{M}^{\textnormal{deg}}=Z_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
X
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
X_{f}
be an ico model of
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(i)

The ico model
X_{f}
identifies via (
7.6
) with a model
Y
of
X
in
\bar{M}
such that
U_{f}=U_{Y}
, and any model of
X
in
\bar{M}
identifies via (
7.6
) with an ico model of
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(ii)

The ico model
X_{f}
is degenerate if and only if
Y\cap\bar{M}^{\textnormal{deg}}\neq\emptyset
.

(iii)

The curve
X
is non-degenerate if and only if
X
is non-degenerate in
\bar{M}
.

To study curves which are non-degenerate, we can now freely use (via Lemma
7.7
) the results and constructions for varieties which are non-degenerate in
\bar{M}
.

Proof of Corollary
7.1
.

Lemma
7.7
(i) shows that (i) directly follows from Theorem
4.1
(ii) combined with the classical result that
\bar{M}
, and thus
M
, is rational over
\mathbb{Q}
; an explicit birational map from
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
to
\bar{M}
can be found in (

). Moreover Lemma
7.7
(iii) shows that (ii) is equivalent to a special case of Theorem
4.1
(iii). This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
7.7
.

All three statements of Lemma
7.7
can be proved via standard arguments by unwinding the definitions. In particular (iii) is a direct consequence of (i) and (ii). We now include the arguments underlying (i) and (ii) in a more general form, working over any subring
R\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
. This additional generality will be used in
\mathsection
7.3.2
.

To show (i) write
X_{f}=\textnormal{Proj}(A/I_{f})
with
A=R[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{4}]
and
I_{f}=(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{4},f)
for homogeneous
f\in A^{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The inclusion
(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{4})\subseteq I_{f}
identifies
X_{f}
with its scheme theoretic image
Y
in
\bar{M}_{R}
under the induced closed immersion

X_{f}=\textnormal{Proj}(A/I_{f})\hookrightarrow\textnormal{Proj}\bigl(A/(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{4})\bigl)=\bar{M}_{R}.

(7.6)

Conversely, any closed subscheme
Y^{\prime}
of
\bar{M}_{R}
is uniquely determined by its ideal sheaf. Thus
[
Sta
, 01QP]
implies that
Y^{\prime}
is the scheme theoretic image of the closed immersion
\textnormal{Proj}(A/I)\to\bar{M}_{R}
induced by some
I=I_{g}
for homogeneous
g\in A^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover

is an ico model of
X
over

if and only if

is a model of

in
\bar{M}
. This proves (i).

To prove (ii) we recall that
\bar{M}^{\textnormal{deg}}=Z_{\mathbb{Q}}
consists of the five closed points

obtained by permuting the coordinates of

.
Consider

for

homogeneous of degree

with diagonal part

. After identifying via (

) the ico model

with

, we compute that

contains the point

if and only if

∎

8.2
The birational morphisms
\tau
and
\rho

We continue our notation. In this section we use classical constructions of Clebsch
[

]
and Klein
[

to explicitly construct a birational equivalence between

and the surface

in (

Construction of
\tau
and
\rho
.

We first define homogeneous polynomials
\tau_{i}
in
\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]
of degree 12 as follows: We put
\tau_{i}=-(\prod_{j\neq i}t_{j})(\sum t_{j})
for
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and
\tau_{4}=\prod t_{j}
, where

\displaystyle t_{0}

\displaystyle=(y-z)(xy+xz-z^{2}),

\displaystyle t_{1}=

\displaystyle xz^{2}+yz^{2}-x^{2}y-z^{3},

\displaystyle t_{2}

\displaystyle=x(z^{2}-y^{2}-xz),

\displaystyle t_{3}=

\displaystyle z(yz-xz+x^{2}-y^{2}).

The polynomials
\tau_{i}
define a morphism
\tau:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus T_{\tau}\to\mathbb{P}^{4}
where
T_{\tau}=\cap V_{+}(\tau_{i})
is finite.
We compute in Macaulay2 that the scheme theoretic image of
\tau
is
\bar{M}
and thus we obtain

\tau:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus T_{\tau}\to\bar{M}.

(8.3)

To construct an ‘inverse’ morphism of
\tau
, we define homogeneous polynomials
\rho_{i}
in
\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},\dotsc,x_{4}]
of degree 8 as follows: We write
r_{i}=\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}
and then we put

\rho_{0}=-(r_{1}+r_{3})(r_{0}+r_{1}+r_{2}),\quad\rho_{1}=r_{0}(r_{0}+r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}),\quad\rho_{2}=r_{0}(r_{0}+r_{2}).

(8.4)

The polynomials
\rho_{i}
define a morphism
\rho:\bar{M}\setminus T_{\rho}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
where
T_{\rho}
is a curve given by the intersection of
\bar{M}
with
\cap V_{+}(\rho_{i})
. Then
\tau
and
\rho
define a birational equivalence between
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\bar{M}
. We shall need controlled open subsets over which
\tau
and
\sigma
are isomorphisms. Let

C_{\tau}=V_{+}(\lambda)\supset T_{\tau},\quad U_{\tau}=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{\tau}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad U_{\rho}=\tau(U_{\tau})\subset\bar{M},

(8.5)

where
\lambda=\rho_{0}(\tau_{0},\dotsc,\tau_{4})/x
is a non-constant homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]
. The following lemma gives that
\tau
and
\rho
are indeed isomorphisms over
U_{\tau}
and
U_{\rho}
.

Lemma 8.5
.

It holds
\tau:U_{\tau}\overset{\sim}{\to}U_{\rho}
with
U_{\rho}\subseteq\bar{M}\setminus T_{\rho}
and
\rho:U_{\rho}\overset{\sim}{\to}U_{\tau}
such that
\tau=\rho^{-1}
.

Proof.

We first compute
\rho\tau
and
\tau(U_{\tau})
. The identities
(*)
imply that
\tau:U_{\tau}\to\bar{M}
factors through
\bar{M}\setminus T_{\rho}
and that
\rho\tau:U_{\tau}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
is the inclusion
U_{\tau}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
. It follows that
\tau:U_{\tau}\to\bar{M}\setminus T_{\rho}
factors through
Z=\bigl(\bar{M}\setminus T_{\rho}\bigl)\setminus\rho^{-1}(C_{\tau})
and that
U_{\tau}\to^{\tau}Z\to^{\rho}U_{\tau}
is the identity on
U_{\tau}
. Thus
\tau:U_{\tau}\to Z
is a closed immersion, which is surjective since
\bar{M}
is integral and
\dim(U_{\tau})=2=\dim(Z)
. Hence
Z=\tau(U_{\tau})=U_{\rho}
and
\tau:U_{\tau}\overset{\sim}{\to}U_{\rho}
is an isomorphism. Then
\rho:U_{\rho}\overset{\sim}{\to}U_{\tau}
is an isomorphism since
\rho=\tau^{-1}
. This proves Lemma
8.5
.

We used the identities
(*)
:
\rho_{0}(\tau)=\lambda x
,
\rho_{1}(\tau)=\lambda y
,
\rho_{2}(\tau)=\lambda z
. Here
\tau=(\tau_{0},\dotsc,\tau_{4})
, and

for

and

homogeneous of degree 5.
∎

The polynomials

come from Polo-Blanco–Top

who used classical constructions to obtain an algorithm computing explicit parametrizations (of small degree) for smooth cubic surfaces. There are many other polynomials which can be used to construct explicit birational equivalences between

and

, and these equivalences would also be suitable for our purpose of explicitly studying rational points on plane curves via

In this section we discuss the Fermat problem inside a rational surface

Fermat problem.

We now generalize the Fermat problem by replacing

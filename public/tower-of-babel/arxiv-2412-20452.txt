S 1 S^{1} reduction of 4D 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}{=}\,4 Schur index and 3D 𝒩 = 8 \mathcal{N}{=}\,8 mass-deformed partition function
Tomoki Nakanishi, Takahiro Nishinaka

Abstract
We study the compactification of 4D 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}{=}\,4 SYM on S 1 S^{1} from the viewpoint of the superconformal index. In the cases that the gauge group of the 4D SYM is U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) and U ​ s ​ p ​ ( 2 ​ N ) Usp(2N) , the resulting 3D theory is believed to be the ABJM theory with the Chern-Simons level k = 1 k=1 and k = 2 k=2 , respectively. This suggests that the small S 1 S^{1} limit of the superconformal index of these 4D 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYMs is identical to the sphere partition function of the ABJM theories. Using a recently observed relation between the 4D and 3D R-charges for theories with twelve or more supercharges, we explicitly confirm this identity in the Schur limit of the 4D index. Our result provides a direct quantitative check of the relation between 4D 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYMs and 3D 𝒩 = 8 \mathcal{N}=8 ABJM theories.

When a 4D
\mathcal{N}=4

In the cases that the gauge group of the 4D theory is

\mathcal{N}{=}\,4

The superconformal index
of a 4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SCFT is generally defined by

[

\displaystyle I(p,q,t,u)=\mathrm{Tr}(-1)^{F}p^{j_{2}-j_{1}+}q^{j_{2}+j_{1}-r}t^{r+R}u^{f}~,

(2.1)

where the trace is taken over the space of local operators, and
(j_{1},j_{2})

are
\mathfrak{so}(4)
spins. The three charges
R,r
and

are linear combinations
of the Cartan generators of the R-symmetry
\mathfrak{su}(4)_{R}
, and
written as

\displaystyle R=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{R}^{1}{}_{1}-\mathcal{R}^{2}{}_{2})~,\qquad r=\mathcal{R}^{1}{}_{1}+\mathcal{R}^{2}{}_{2}~,\qquad f=\mathcal{R}^{1}{}_{1}+\mathcal{R}^{2}{}_{2}+2\mathcal{R}^{3}{}_{3}~,

(2.2)

where
R^{I}{}_{J}
are generators of
\mathfrak{su}(4)_{R}
with the
convention described in
[
8
]
.
3
3

3

One can take
an
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal sub-algebra, whose R-symmetry is

\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R}\times\mathfrak{u}(1)_{r}
. The commutant of this

\mathcal{N}=2
R-symmetry algebra in
\mathfrak{su}(4)_{R}
is

\mathfrak{su}(2)_{f}
, and regarded as a flavor symmetry from the

\mathcal{N}=2
viewpoint. The charges

and

defined in
(

) are associated with Cartan generators of

\mathfrak{su}(2)_{R},\,\mathfrak{u}(1)_{r}
and
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{f}
,

For the above trace to be convergent, we impose
|p|,|q|,|t|<1,\,|pq/t|<1
and
|u|=1
.

The Schur limit
of the superconformal index is the limit

t\rightarrow q

[
12
,
13
]
, in which
the index preserves twice the number of supercharges
and the

one can set

set

limit of the
\mathcal{N}=4
index is

\displaystyle I_{\text{Schur}}(q,u)\equiv I(p,q,t,u)\big|_{t\to q,\,p=q}~.

(2.3)

The Lagrangian description for the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM implies that, for
the 4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM with gauge
group
G
, the
\mathcal{N}=4
Schur index is evaluated as

\displaystyle I_{\text{Schur}}(q,u)=\int d\mu_{G}(z)\;\text{P.E.}\left[\,\left(i_{\mathcal{N}=2}^{\text{vec}}(q)+i_{\mathcal{N}=2}^{\text{hyp}}(q,u)\right)\chi_{\text{adj}}^{G}(z)\right]~,

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle i^{\text{vec}}_{\mathcal{N}=2}(q)\equiv-\frac{2q}{1-q}~,\qquad i^{\text{hyp}}_{\mathcal{N}=2}(q,u)\equiv\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-q}(u+u^{-1})

(2.5)

are respectively single-letter indices for an
\mathcal{N}=2
vector and an

\mathcal{N}=2
hyper multiplets, the plethystic exponential is defined
by
\text{P.E.}[f(q,u_{i},z_{i})]\equiv\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(q^{k},(u_{i})^{k},(z_{i})^{k})}{k}\right)
, and

and

\chi_{\text{adj}}^{G}(z)
are respectively the Haar measure on
G
and
the character of the adjoint representation of
G
. To be explicit, the
latter two are expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mu_{G}\equiv\frac{1}{|W(G)|}\prod^{\text{rank}\,G}_{i=1}\frac{\mathrm{d}z_{i}}{2\pi iz_{i}}\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{G}}(1-z^{\alpha})~,\qquad\chi_{\text{adj}}^{G}\equiv\text{rank}\,G+\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{G}}z^{\alpha}~,

(2.6)

where
|W(G)|
is the order of the Weyl group of
G

\Delta_{G}
is
the root system of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of associated with

G
.
In addition, for a root
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}n_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\Delta_{G}

\alpha_{i}
being simple roots,

is defined by

\displaystyle z^{\alpha}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{\text{rank}G}(z_{i})^{n_{i}}~.

(2.7)

Putting the above expressions together in (

\displaystyle I_{\text{Schur}}(q,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|W(G)|}\left(\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(q^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{\pm 1};q)_{\infty}}\right)^{\!\!\text{rank}\,G}\!\left(\prod^{\text{rank}\,G}_{i=1}\oint_{|z_{i}|=1}\frac{dz_{i}}{2\pi iz_{i}}\right)\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{G}}\frac{(z^{\alpha};q)_{\infty}(z^{\alpha}q;q)_{\infty}}{(z^{\alpha}q^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{\pm 1};q)_{\infty}}~,

(2.8)

where
(a;b)_{n}:=\prod_{m=0}^{n}(1-ab^{m})
is
q

we used a shorthand
(xu^{\pm};q)_{\infty}\equiv(xu;q)_{\infty}(xu^{-1};q)_{\infty}

It will turn out to be useful for us below to rewrite the

Following

9

\displaystyle\theta(x;q)

\displaystyle:=-x^{-\frac{1}{2}}(q;q)_{\infty}(x;q)_{\infty}(x^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}~,\qquad F(x,y;q):=\frac{\theta(xy;q)\,(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}}{\theta(x;q)\,\theta(y;q)}~.

SYM for a general gauge

By substituting (

\displaystyle I_{\text{Schur}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-e^{i\beta(M+\frac{i}{2})}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-e^{-\beta n})^{2}}{(1-e^{-i\beta(M+i(\frac{1}{2}+n))})(1-e^{i\beta(M+i(\frac{1}{2}-n))})}~,

Next, let us consider the case

\displaystyle I_{\text{Schur}}=\frac{\eta^{2}}{2!}\oint_{|z_{i}|=1}\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(2\pi i)^{2}z_{1}z_{2}}\prod_{s=\pm 1}F(\eta,(z_{1}/z_{2})^{s}\,\eta^{-1};q)~.

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(iM+\frac{1}{2}+m_{\alpha})}\frac{1}{(iM+\frac{1}{2}+m_{\alpha}+n_{\alpha})}

(3.16)

is identical to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{\alpha}}\sum_{m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{1}{iM+\frac{1}{2}+m_{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{iM+\frac{1}{2}+m_{\alpha}+n_{\alpha}}\right)=0~.

(3.17)

This implies that (
3.15
) and therefore
(
3.13
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{G+}}\left(\sum_{m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(iM+\frac{1}{2}+m_{\alpha})^{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{\pi}{\cosh(\pi M)}\right)^{\text{dim}\,G-\text{rank}\,G}~.

(3.18)

It is then straightforward to see that (
3.12
) is identical
to

\displaystyle\left.I_{\text{Schur}}\right|_{\beta\to 0}=\frac{1}{|W(G)|}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{\text{rank}\,G}\left(\frac{1}{2\cosh(\pi M)}\right)^{\text{rank}\,G}~,

(3.19)

where the sub-leading corrections are of

\mathcal{O}(\beta^{\,1-\text{rank}\,G})
. This is the generalization
of (
3.6
) and (
3.10
) to an arbitrary gauge
group
G
.

4
Comparison to the
S^{3}
partition function of the ABJM theories

4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYMs are believed to be reduced down to 3D

\mathcal{N}=8
SCFTs by the
S^{1}
-compactification. Therefore, the 3D
reduction of the Schur index discussed in the previous section is
expected to be identical to the
S^{3}
partition function of a 3D

\mathcal{N}=8
SCFT.

In this section, we consider
4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYMs whose gauge group
G
is
G=U(N)
or
G=Usp(2N)
. In
this case, the corresponding
3D
\mathcal{N}=8
SCFT is believed to be the
3D
U(N)_{k}\times U(N)_{-k}
ABJM theory with
k=1
for
G=U(N)
and

k=2
for
G=USp(2N)
.
Below, we compare the
S^{3}
partition
function of these ABJM theories with the small
\beta
limit
(
3.19
) of the Schur index of the 4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM of
gauge group
G=U(N),\,USp(2N)
.

One subtlety here is that, in the RG-flow from the 4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM to
the ABJM theory, there is an accidental global
\mathfrak{u}(1)
symmetry
that only exists in the IR fixed point. Indeed, the
\mathfrak{so}(8)_{R}

global symmetry of the
\mathcal{N}=8
ABJM
theory is of rank four while the
\mathfrak{su}(4)
global symmetry of
4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM is of rank three. Due to this accidental
symmetry, when flowing from four dimensions, one linear combination of
the three (
\mathcal{N}=2
preserving) mass parameters of the ABJM theory are turned off, which gives
rise to a divergence in the
S^{3}
partition function

[
8
]
. This is a special phenomena for
\mathcal{N}\geq 3

supersymmetry (in four dimensions) which does not occur for generic 4D

\mathcal{N}=2
SCFTs.

Such a divergence has already been noticed in

[
7
]
in the study of the small
S^{1}
limit of the superconformal index
of 4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. Indeed, in the small
S^{1}
limit, the

\mathcal{N}=4
index has a divergent prefactor

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^{\text{rank}\,G}~,

(4.1)

where
\beta
is the radius of
S^{1}
.
This power-law divergence is interpreted in
[
7
]
to reflect the fact that the moduli
space of the corresponding 3D
\mathcal{N}=8
SCFT has
\text{rank}\,G
flat
directions even on
S^{3}
. This means that the
S^{3}
partition function
of this 3D
\mathcal{N}=8
SCFT is divergent; the path-integral of
scalar fields whose VEVs parameterize the flat directions give rise to a
divergence.
Specifically, when a cut-off
\sqrt{\Lambda}
is introduced for the VEV of
each such (complex) scalar field, the partition function of
the 3D theory has a prefactor of the form

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\text{rank}\,G}~,

(4.2)

which is divergent in the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
.

The above observation given in
[
7
]
strongly suggests that the small
\beta
limit of the Schur
index of a 4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM of gauge group
G
is identical to the

S^{3}
partition function of the corresponding 3D
\mathcal{N}=8
SCFT,
under the identification of (
4.1
) with (
4.2
). In
other words, we expect that the following relation holds up to a
numerical prefactor:

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to 0}\beta^{\text{rank}\,G}I_{\text{Schur}}=\lim_{\Lambda\to 0}\Lambda^{-\text{rank}\,G}Z_{S^{3}}~,

(4.3)

where
I_{\text{Schur}}
is the Schur index of the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM,
and
Z_{S^{3}}
is the
S^{3}
partition function of the 3D
\mathcal{N}=8

SCFT with the cut-off
\sqrt{\Lambda}
introduced.
Below, we show this is indeed the case for
G=U(N)
and

G=USp(2N)
. Its generalization to more general gauge groups will be left
for future work.

4.1
Partition function of mass-deformed ABJM theory

We begin with the
S^{3}
partition function of
U(N)_{k}\times U(N)_{-k}

ABJM theory, which is evaluated by supersymmetric localization as

[

\displaystyle Z^{\text{ABJM}}_{S^{3}_{b}}(M,m)=\int\frac{d^{N}\mu\ d^{N}\nu}{(N!)^{2}}e^{-i\pi k(\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{2}-\sum_{j}\nu_{j}^{2})}\prod_{i<j}(2\sinh(\pi(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}))2\sinh(\pi(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})))^{2}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i,j}\left[s_{b=1}\left(\frac{i}{2}-\left(-\mu_{i}+\nu_{j}+\frac{-m_{1}-m_{2}+m_{3}}{2}\right)\right)\ s_{b=1}\left(\frac{i}{2}-\left(-\mu_{i}+\nu_{j}+\frac{-m_{1}+m_{2}-m_{3}}{2}\right)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.s_{b=1}\left(\frac{i}{2}-\left(\mu_{i}-\nu_{j}+\frac{m_{1}-m_{2}-m_{3}}{2}\right)\right)\ s_{b=1}\left(\frac{i}{2}-\left(\mu_{i}-\nu_{j}+\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}}{2}\right)\right)\right]~,

(4.4)

where
s_{b=1}(z)\equiv\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{n-iz}{n+iz}\right)^{n}
is a specialization of the double-sine
function.
In (
4.4
), the three parameters
m_{1},m_{2}
and
m_{3}
are mass
deformation parameters that preserve a 3D
\mathcal{N}=2

supersymmetry. Note that one needs to pick
an
\mathcal{N}=2
sub-algebra of the
\mathcal{N}=8
algebra to use the supersymmetric localization.
Following
[
18
]
, we take
m_{1},m_{2}
and
m_{3}
as follows. We start with the
manifest
\mathfrak{so}(6)_{R}\times\mathfrak{u}(1)_{b}
symmetry, which is
believed to be enhanced to
\mathfrak{so}(8)_{R}
symmetry in the
infrared. We choose an
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry whose R-symmetry is

\mathfrak{so}(2)
. The commutant of the
\mathcal{N}=2
R-symmetry in
the manifest global symmetry is
\mathfrak{so}(4)\times\mathfrak{u}(1)_{b}\supset\mathfrak{so}(2)_{1}\times\mathfrak{so}(2)_{2}\times\mathfrak{u}(1)_{b}
. We then take
m_{1},m_{2}
and

m_{3}
to be associated with
\mathfrak{so}(2)_{1},\,\mathfrak{so}(2)_{2}

and
\mathfrak{u}(1)_{b}
, respectively.

It was shown in
[
8
]
that, when the
S^{3}
partition
function of the ABJM theory is
obtained as the small
\beta
limit of the Schur index of the 4D
\mathcal{N}\geq 3
SCFT, the three
mass parameters are constrained as
4
4

4

One can see this by setting

\xi=-1
and
b=1
in Eq. (3.8) of
[
8
]
. The fact
that
\xi=-1
is needed
is proven in
[
8
]
while the condition
b=1
corresponds to the Schur limit of the
4D index.

\displaystyle m_{1}=-\left(M+\frac{i}{2}\right)~,\qquad m_{2}=0~,\qquad m_{3}=\left(M-\frac{i}{2}\right)~,

(4.5)

where
M
is a mass deformation parameter corresponding to the fugacity

u
in the way as in (
3.1
). Note that this implies

\displaystyle m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=-i~,

(4.6)

which gives rise to a divergence in (
4.4
) since

s_{b=1}(i-\mu_{i}-\nu_{j})
diverges when
\mu_{i}=\nu_{j}
. The
constraint (
4.6
) reflects the fact that the 3D
global
\mathfrak{u}(1)
symmetry corresponding to the mass deformation

(m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3})
is accidental in three dimensions and has no 4D
counterpart.

Since (
4.5
) leads to a divergent partition
function, let us introduce a small regularization parameter
\epsilon
so that

\displaystyle m_{1}=-\left(M+\frac{i}{2}\right)+\epsilon~,\qquad m_{2}=0~,\qquad m_{3}=\left(M-\frac{i}{2}\right)+\epsilon~,

(4.7)

which replaces the condition (
4.6
) with
m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=-i+2\epsilon
. Below, we will evaluate the leading
contribution to the partition function for
\epsilon\sim 0
.

Note that the regularization discussed above is
slightly different from the one we discussed around (
4.2
). The reason for introducing a different regularization parameter

\epsilon
here is
that the localization formula (
4.4
) is available only when we take the
cut-off parameter
\sqrt{\Lambda}
to be
\infty
.
We will discuss later how the small mass parameter

\epsilon
is related to the cut-off parameter
\sqrt{\Lambda}
.

Plugging (
4.5
) into (
4.4
) and using the
identity
s_{b=1}\!\left(\frac{i}{2}-z\right)s_{b=1}\!\left(\frac{i}{2}+z\right)=\frac{1}{2\cosh(\pi z)}
, one obtains

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)=i^{N^{2}}\int\frac{d^{N}\mu\,d^{N}\nu}{(N!)^{2}}e^{-i\pi k(\sum_{i}\mu^{2}-\sum_{j}\nu^{2})}\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\,(2\sinh\pi(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}))^{2}\,(2\sinh\pi(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j}))^{2}}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\,2\sinh\pi(\mu_{i}-\nu_{j}+\epsilon)\;\;2\cosh\pi(\mu_{i}-\nu_{j}-M)}~.

(4.8)

Using Cauchy’s determinant formula

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})(y_{i}-y_{j})}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}(x_{i}+y_{j})}=\det\left(\frac{1}{x_{i}+y_{j}}\right)=\sum_{\rho}(-1)^{|\rho|}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{x_{i}+y_{\rho(i)}}

(4.9)

for
(x_{i},y_{j})=(e^{2\pi\mu_{i}},e^{2\pi(\nu_{j}-\frac{i}{2}-\epsilon)})
and

(e^{2\pi\mu_{i}},e^{2\pi(\nu_{j}+M)})
,
one obtains

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N^{2}}(-i)^{N}\int\frac{d^{N}\mu\,d^{N}\nu}{(N!)^{2}}e^{-i\pi k(\sum_{i}\mu^{2}-\sum_{j}\nu^{2})}

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{\rho,\sigma}(-1)^{|\rho|+|\sigma|}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2\sinh\pi(\mu_{i}-\nu_{\rho(i)}+\epsilon)}\frac{1}{2\cosh\pi(\mu_{i}-\nu_{\sigma(i)}-M)}~.

(4.10)

We further use the following formulae for Fourier transforms

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sinh\pi x}=-i\int dpe^{2\pi ipx}\tanh\pi p~,\qquad\frac{1}{\cosh\pi x}=\int dq\frac{e^{2\pi iqx}}{\cosh\pi q}~,

(4.11)

to obtain

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\int\frac{d^{N}\mu\,d^{N}\nu\,d^{N}p\,d^{N}q}{(N!)^{2}}\sum_{\rho,\sigma}(-1)^{|\rho|+|\sigma|}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\tanh\pi p_{i}}{\cosh\pi q_{i}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times e^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(-i\pi k\left(\mu_{i}^{2}-\nu_{i}^{2}\right)+2\pi ip_{i}(\mu_{i}-\nu_{\rho(i)}+\epsilon)+2\pi iq_{i}(\mu_{i}-\nu_{\sigma(i)}-M)\big)}~.

(4.12)

After performing the Gaussian integrals over
\mu_{i}
and
\nu_{i}
, one obtains

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle=\frac{i^{N}}{(-4k)^{N}}\int\frac{d^{N}p\,d^{N}q}{(N!)^{2}}\sum_{\rho,\sigma}(-1)^{|\rho|+|\sigma|}e^{2\pi i\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(-q_{i}M+p_{i}\left(\frac{q_{i}-q_{\sigma^{-1}\circ\rho(i)}}{k}+\epsilon\right)\right)}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\tanh\pi p_{i}}{\cosh\pi q_{i}}~.

(4.13)

Performing the
p_{i}
-integrals via (
4.11
), one finds

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4k)^{N}}\int\frac{d^{N}q}{(N!)^{2}}\sum_{\rho,\sigma}(-1)^{|\rho|+|\sigma|}e^{-2\pi iM\sum_{i=1}^{N}q_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sinh\pi\left(\frac{q_{i}-q_{\sigma^{-1}\circ\rho(i)}}{k}+\epsilon\right)}\frac{1}{\cosh\pi q_{i}}~.

(4.14)

Recall here that this partition function diverges in the limit

\epsilon\to 0
, and we are interested in the leading divergent terms for

\epsilon\sim 0
. Obviously, such leading terms arise only from the terms for

\sigma^{-1}\circ\rho(i)=i
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\sigma=\rho.

(4.15)

Thus, the leading divergent terms in the partition function is evaluated
as

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{(4k)^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{\sinh\pi\epsilon}\right)^{N}\int\frac{d^{N}q}{(N!)^{2}}\sum_{\rho}e^{-2\pi iM\sum_{i=1}^{N}q_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\cosh\pi q_{i}}~.

(4.16)

Using (
4.11
) and
\sinh\pi\epsilon\sim\pi\epsilon
, one
finally obtains

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{k^{N}N!}\left(\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon}\right)^{N}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh\pi M}\right)^{N}~.

(4.17)

This is the leading divergent behavior of the
S^{3}
partition function
of the ABJM theory in the small
\epsilon
limit, under the constraints
(
4.5
) on the mass parameters.

4.2
Interpretation of the factor
1/\epsilon^{N}

The expression (
4.17
) implies that the ABJM theory on

S^{3}
has
N
flat directions in the moduli space when the constraint
(
4.5
) is imposed. This can be seen as follows.

Recall first that
\epsilon
is a small mass parameter that we
introduced to regularize the divergence in
Z_{S^{3}}(M)
. Physically,

\epsilon
introduces a small real mass of zero modes of complex scalar
fields in (twisted) chiral
multiplets. Since the vacuum expectation values (VEV) of these chiral
multiplets parameterize a sub-space of the moduli
space of vacua, such a small mass parameter generally leads to a prefactor

1/\epsilon^{n}
in the partition function, where
n
is the number of zero modes.

To see this explicitly, let us consider a complex scalar
\phi
on
S^{3}
, whose
Lagrangian is given by
[
15
,
16
,
17
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\partial_{\mu}\overline{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\phi+\epsilon^{2}\overline{\phi}\phi+2i(\mathfrak{r}-1)\epsilon\overline{\phi}\phi+\mathfrak{r}(2-\mathfrak{r})\overline{\phi}\phi~,

(4.18)

where
\epsilon
is a small real mass, and
\mathfrak{r}
is the R-charge of the scalar field. The

\mathfrak{r}
-dependent terms arise here since the background R-symmetry gauge field
is turned on to preserve supersymmetry on
S^{3}
.
Now, let us focus on the zero mode
\phi=\phi_{0}
. Since the
kinetic term
\partial_{\mu}\overline{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\phi
vanishes for the
zero mode, its path-integral is written as

\displaystyle\int d\overline{\phi}_{0}d\phi_{0}\;e^{-\left(\epsilon^{2}+2i(\mathfrak{r}-1)\epsilon+\mathfrak{r}(2-\mathfrak{r})\right)\int d^{3}x\overline{\phi}_{0}\phi_{0}}~.

(4.19)

Note that, for the above path integral to be divergent, one needs to impose
\mathfrak{r}=0
or
2
. In other words, only for these values of
\mathfrak{r}
,
the potential for the zero mode on
S^{3}
is flat. The fact that we have
the divergent partition function (
4.17
) under the
condition (
4.5
) implies that the mass of some chiral
multiplets with
\mathfrak{r}=0,2
vanishes when
(
4.5
) is imposed.
When
\mathfrak{r}=0,2
, the above path-integral of the zero mode
\phi_{0}
reduces to

\displaystyle\int d\overline{\phi}_{0}d\phi_{0}\;e^{-\left(\epsilon^{2}\mp 2i\epsilon\right)V\overline{\phi}_{0}\phi_{0}}=\frac{\pi}{(\epsilon^{2}\mp 2i\epsilon)V}~,

(4.20)

where
V
is the volume of
S^{3}
. Since we set the radius of

S^{3}
to one,
V=2\pi^{2}
. Substituting this and omitting the sub-leading
correction in the small
\epsilon
limit, one can evaluate the above as

\displaystyle\int d\overline{\phi}_{0}d\phi_{0}\;e^{-\left(\epsilon^{2}\mp 2i\epsilon\right)V\overline{\phi}_{0}\phi_{0}}\sim\pm\frac{i}{4\pi\epsilon}~.

(4.21)

The expression (
4.21
) shows the leading divergence
of the path-integral of a

single
(complex) zero mode
\phi_{0}
corresponding to a flat direction on
the moduli space.
Therefore, when the moduli space has
n
(complex) flat directions, the partition function with a regularization
parameter
\epsilon
has a divergent factor of the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon^{n}}~.

(4.22)

Comparing this with the divergent factor in (
4.17
), we
now see that the moduli
space of
U(N)_{k}\times U(N)_{-k}
ABJM theory on
S^{3}
has
N
flat directions when the condition
(
4.5
) on the mass parameters is satisfied.

4.3
Comparison to the small
\beta
limit of 4D index

Let us finally compare (
4.17
) with the small
S^{1}
limit
(
3.19
) of the 4D
index, along the lines that we mentioned at the beginning of this
section. For that, we first need to interpret the divergent factor

1/\epsilon^{N}
in (
4.17
) in terms of the cut-off

\sqrt{\Lambda}
for the VEV of scalar fields.

Indeed, instead of introducing the small mass parameter
\epsilon
, one can regularize the
divergent path-integral of the zero modes by introducing the cut-off

\sqrt{\Lambda}
for the integration region. Such a regularization replaces
(
4.21
) with

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{\sqrt{\Lambda}}{2}}^{\frac{\sqrt{\Lambda}}{2}}d\overline{\phi}_{0}d\phi_{0}=\Lambda~.

(4.23)

With this regularization, the existence of
N
flat directions in the
moduli space leads to
the prefactor

\displaystyle\Lambda^{N}~,

(4.24)

of the partition function instead of (
4.22
). This implies that, when introducing the cut-off
\Lambda
instead of
the mass parameter
\epsilon
, the partition function
(
4.17
) is expressed as

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\Lambda^{N}}{k^{N}N!}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh\pi M}\right)^{N}~.

(4.25)

up to a numerical prefactor.

We now compare (
4.25
) with the
S^{1}
-reduction
(
3.19
) of the Schur index of 4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM of
gauge group
G
. As stated at
the beginning of this section, we focus on the cases
G=U(N)
and

G=USp(2N)
, in which cases the Chern-Simons level
k
is set to
1
and

2
, respectively. In the case of
G=U(N)
, the 3D partition function is
evaluated as

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\Lambda^{N}}{N!}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh\pi M}\right)^{N}=\frac{\Lambda^{\text{rank}\,U(N)}}{|W(U(N))|}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh\pi M}\right)^{\text{rank}\,U(N)}~.

(4.26)

When
\Lambda
is identified with
1/\beta
, this is identical up to a
numerical factor
to the
S^{1}
reduction (
3.19
) of the 4D index. In the case
of
G=USp(2N)
, the 3D partition function is given by

\displaystyle Z_{S^{3}}(M)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\Lambda^{N}}{2^{N}N!}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh\pi M}\right)^{N}=\frac{\Lambda^{\text{rank}\,USp(2N)}}{|W(USp(2N))|}\left(\frac{1}{\cosh\pi M}\right)^{\text{rank}\,USp(2N)}~.

(4.27)

Identifying
\Lambda
with
1/\beta
, we see that the 3D
partition function is again identical to the 4D index up to a numerical
factor. Thus, we have seen that the relation

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to 0}\beta^{\,\text{rank}\,G}\,I_{\text{Schur}}=\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\Lambda^{-\text{rank}\,G}Z_{S^{3}}

(4.28)

holds up to a numerical prefactor, at least for
G=U(N)
and
G=USp(2N)
.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied the small
S^{1}
limit of the
Schur limit of the superconformal index of 4D
\mathcal{N}=4
SYMs of
gauge group
G=U(N)
and
USp(2N)
, and compare the resulting
expression with the
S^{3}
partition function of the 3D
\mathcal{N}=8

ABJM theory of gauge group
U(N)_{k}\times U(N)_{-k}
for
k=1
and

2
, respectively. On the one hand, our expression for the small
S^{1}
limit of the 4D
Schur index is shown in Eq. (
3.19
), where
\beta
is the
radius of
S^{1}
. On the other hand, that for the
S^{3}
partition
function of the ABJM theory is shown in Eq. (
4.17
) or
Eq. (
4.25
), depending on whether we introduce the cut-off

\sqrt{\Lambda}
or the small mass
\epsilon
as a regularization
parameter. Here, the reason for the
S^{3}
partition function to be
divergent is that one linear combination of the mass parameters is
constrained as in Eq. (
4.6
) when the 3D theory is
obtained as the
S^{1}
compactification of a 4D theory.
Under the identification of Eqs. (
4.1
) and (
4.2
),
we have obtained a complete agreement between Eqs. (
3.19
)
and (
4.25
) up to a numerical prefactor.

One future direction would be to extend our result to more general pairs
of 4D and 3D theories with sixteen supercharges. For instance, there are
more 3D Chern-Simons matter theories that can have
\mathcal{N}=8

supersymmetry
[
19
,
20
]
. Another future
direction would be to generalize our results on the Schur index to
the 4D superconformal index
with more general fugacities. For that, one needs to find a
generalization of the formula (
3.13
) which has played a
crucial role in our discussions on the Schur index.

Partial versus total resetting for Lévy flights in d dimensions: similarities and discrepancies
Costantino Di Bello, Aleksei Chechkin, Tomasz Grzywny, Zbigniew Palmowski, Karol Szczypkowski, Bartosz Trojan

Abstract
While stochastic resetting (or total resetting) is a less young and more established concept in stochastic processes, partial stochastic resetting (PSR) is a relatively new field. PSR means that, at random moments in time, a stochastic process gets multiplied by a factor between 0 0 and 1 1 , thus approaching but not reaching the resetting position. In this paper, we present new results on PSR highlighting the main similarities and discrepancies with total resetting. Specifically, we consider both symmetric α \alpha -stable Lévy processes (Lévy flights) and Brownian motion with PSR in arbitrary d d dimensions. We derive explicit expressions for the propagator and its stationary measure, and discuss in detail their asymptotic behavior. Interestingly, while approaching to stationarity, a dynamical phase transition occurs for the Brownian motion, but not for Lévy flights. We also analyze the behavior of the process around the resetting position and find significant differences between PSR and total resetting.

Consider an insurance company. At a random moment, a customer incurs an accident and gets a refund from the insurance. From the company’s point of view, refunding someone corresponds to an instantaneous sudden loss of money. If we consider that the company’s total wealth is aleatory, we could mathematically model it as a stochastic process with random jumps.
In physical literature, we refer to these kinds of models as
stochastic processes with resets
.
There has been a great interest in resetting processes in the last fifteen years. In this paper, we will consider a peculiar model of stochastic resetting named
partial resetting
.
With partial resetting, we mean that the instantaneous jump in the stochastic process is proportional to the current value of the process.
In the most studied total resetting mechanism, instead, the stochastic process gets instantaneously set to a specific value independently of any past value of the process.
In this paper, we want to study how this kind of mechanism affects the behavior of generic Lévy flights in arbitrary
d
dimensions. We obtained, both analytically and numerically, that total and partial resetting, although being equivalent under many aspects, show some completely different behaviors.

III.2
Known results

Here, we recall some basics and known results on the PSR. Since in the whole manuscript we fix

\partial_{t}p_{r}(x,t)=D_{\alpha}\Delta^{\alpha/2}p_{r}(x,t)+\frac{r}{c}p_{r}\Big(\frac{x}{c},t\Big)-rp_{r}(x,t).

(6)

Here
\Delta^{\alpha/2}
is the fractional Laplacian, defined in terms of its Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}\left[(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f(x)\right](k)=|k|^{\alpha}\hat{f}(k),

(7)

where
|k|
is the euclidean norm of the vector
k\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, while
\hat{f}(k)
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
, defined as

\hat{f}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm d}xe^{ik\cdot x}f(x).

(8)

In the case of total resetting (
c\to 0^{+}
), the FPE reads (see
[
34
]
)

\partial_{t}p_{r}(x,t)=D_{\alpha}\Delta^{\alpha/2}p_{r}(x,t)+r\delta(x)-rp_{r}(x,t).

(9)

It can be proved, using eq. (
6

\hat{\tilde{p}}_{r}(k,s)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ik\cdot x}p_{r}(x,t){\>\rm d}x{\>\rm d}t,\quad s>0,k\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(10)

reads

\hat{\tilde{p}}_{r}(k,s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+s+D_{\alpha}m^{j}|k|^{\alpha}}.

(11)

Equation (

]

is the norm of the vector

. As expected the propagator is isotropic, as it only depends on

The stationary distribution

can be computed taking the limit

in eq. (

D_{\alpha}\Delta^{\alpha/2}p^{(s)}_{r}(x)+\frac{r}{c}p^{(s)}_{r}\Big(\frac{x}{c},t\Big)-rp^{(s)}_{r}(x)=0,

(12)

and its Fourier transform reads

\hat{p}_{r}(k)=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{r}{r+D_{\alpha}m^{j}|k|^{\alpha}}.

(13)

Equation (

]

An analytical inversion of the double transform in eq. (

p_{r}(x,t)=e^{-rt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{1}{(m^{-1};m^{-1})_{k}(m;m)_{n-k}}\int_{0}^{t}\left((1-\delta_{n0})\dfrac{(t-t^{\prime})^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}+\delta_{n0}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\right)p_{0}(x,m^{k}t^{\prime}){\>\rm d}t^{\prime},

where

IV
Alternative representation of the propagator

It is well known that the propagator
p_{r}(x,t)
also solves the last renewal equation
[
19
]

p_{r}(y,t|x)=e^{-rt}p_{0}(y,t|x)+\\
+r\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-rs}p_{0}(z,s|x)p_{r}(y,t-s|cz){\>\rm d}z{\>\rm d}s.

(17)

The meaning of the rhs of (
17
) is rather simple: either, with probability
e^{-rt}
, the random walk does not incur in any partial reset up to time
t
, and thus freely propagates from
x
to
y
with
p_{0}(y,t|x)
, or, with probability
re^{-rs}
, a partial resetting event occurs at some time
0<s<t
.
This second possibility means that in the time interval
[0,s[
the random walk propagates unperturbed from
x
to some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with propagator
p_{0}(z,t|x)
, then in the time interval
[s,t]
the partial stochastic resetting process propagates from
cz
to
y
with
p_{r}(y,t-s|cz)
.
Of course this term has to be integrated over all possible values of
s
and
z
.
The formula for total resetting (
15
) can be retrieved by sending
c\to 0^{+}
(see
[
3
]
).

As shown in the companion paper
[
30
]
(see section 3), a possible way to find the solution to the renewal equation is to define recursively the sequence
(P_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
of splines via

\displaystyle P_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-m}{\mathds{1}_{{[m,1]}}}(u),

(18)

\displaystyle P_{n+1}(u)

\displaystyle=\max(u-m^{n+1};0)^{n}\int_{u}^{1}\frac{P_{n}(v)}{(v-m^{n+1})^{n+1}}{\>\rm d}v,

(19)

where
{\mathds{1}_{{[m,1]}}}(u)
is the indicator function of the interval
[m,1]
, i.e. it is
1
if
u\in[m,1]
and
0
otherwise.
Note that each
P_{n}
is supported on
[m^{n},1]
.
In
[
30
]
it was proved that the solutions (
17
) reads

p_{r}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{0}(x,u)\>\mu_{t}({\rm d}u),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(20)

where
\mu_{t}
is a measure defined as

\mu_{t}({\rm d}u)=e^{-rt}\delta(t){\rm d}u+e^{-rt}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}r^{j}t^{j}P_{j}(u/t)\frac{{\rm d}u}{t}.

(21)

We do not report here the proof, but just specify that it makes use of the renewal equation (
17
) and of the properties of
\alpha
-stable distributions (see
[
37
]
chapter 3). Notice that this solution is valid only for
c>0
. In the case
c=0
(total resetting) the integral in eq.(
20
) does not converge. As stated before, we do not report here the proof, but we mention that it makes use of the scaling properties of Lévy flights
[
37
]
. This alternative representation of the propagator has several advantages. The first is that it is possible to analyze the tails of the distribution, and thus to identify, in the case of Brownian motion with PSR, a dynamical phase transition.
Second, in this way it is possible to avoid using special functions that are generally not numerically stable.

Stationary distribution

As to the stationary distribution, one can prove, using the same spline method (see

30
]
for details), that the stationary PDF

for a d-dimensional process reads

p^{(s)}_{r}(x)=\frac{1}{(m;m)_{\infty}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{m^{\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)}}{(m;m)_{k}}\times\\
\times\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-m^{-k}u}r^{d/\alpha}p_{0}(r^{1/\alpha}x,u){\>\rm d}u,

(22)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

In the case of Brownian motion (

) the integral can be computed explicitly,

p^{(s)}_{r}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{(r/D)^{d/2}}{(m;m)_{\infty}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{m^{\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)}}{(m;m)_{k}}\times\\
\times\left(m^{\frac{k}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{r}{D}}|x|\right)^{1-d/2}K_{d/2-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{r/D}|x|}{m^{k/2}}\right),

(23)

where
K_{d/2-1}
is the modified Bessel function of the second type, and we simply set
D_{2}=D
.
For
d=1
in the case of Brownian motion we have

p^{(s)}_{r}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{r/D}}{(m;m)_{\infty}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{m^{\frac{k^{2}}{2}}}{(m;m)_{k}}e^{-m^{-\frac{k}{2}}\sqrt{r/D}|x|}

(24)

which is in agreement with equation (37) of

]
, see also

V.1
Asymptotic tail of the propagator and the stationary measure

Recall that the Lévy measure of an isotropic
d
-dimensional Lévy flight with index
{\alpha\in(0,2)}
equals (see
[
37
]
)

\displaystyle\nu(x)=\frac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma((d+\alpha)/2)}{\pi^{d/2}|\Gamma(-\alpha/2)|}|x|^{-d-\alpha}.

(25)

In
[
30
]
it was proved that in the case of the Lévy flight with index
0<\alpha<2
we have

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|\to+\infty\\
t\to+\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{p_{r}(x,t)}{\nu(x)}=\frac{1}{r(1-m)}

(26)

and therefore

p^{(s)}_{r}(x)\sim\frac{1}{r(1-m)}\nu(x)\qquad\text{as $\lvert{x}\rvert\to+\infty$.}

(27)

where we write
f(x)\sim g(x)
if
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}f(x)/g(x)=1
.

The asymptotics (
26
) is a consequence of one big jump principle: the large values of
|x|
are possible only when we have one big jump with
regularly varying density (
25
) that should happens till time
t
.

On the other side, if
Y_{t}
is a Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then from (
23
) it follows that

p^{(s)}_{r}(x)\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{(r/D)^{(d+1)/4}}{(m;m)_{\infty}}(2\pi)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\lvert{x}\rvert^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}e^{-\sqrt{r/D}|x|}\\
\text{as $\lvert{x}\rvert\to+\infty$}.

(28)

We can observe that from eqs. (
27
) and (
28
) it is possible to obtain the scaling for total resetting by sending
m\to 0
. In both cases, partial and total resetting exhibit the same scaling for large
|x|
.

With a simple calculation, as usual available in

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{\gamma}p^{(s)}_{r}(x){\>\rm d}x=\langle|X_{\infty}|^{\gamma}\rangle=\\
=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma/\alpha+1)}{\Gamma_{m}(\gamma/\alpha+1)}(1-m)^{-\gamma/\alpha}\,r^{-\gamma/\alpha}\,\langle|Y_{1}|^{\gamma}\rangle.

(29)

Equation (

) is one of the new results of this paper.
Additionally, if

and

is a symmetric

-stable process with

then for

\langle|Y_{1}|^{\gamma}\rangle=\frac{2^{\gamma}\Gamma(\tfrac{1+\gamma}{2})\Gamma(1-\tfrac{\gamma}{\alpha})}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(1-\tfrac{\gamma}{2})}.

Similarly, if

\langle|B_{1}|^{\gamma}\rangle=\frac{2^{\gamma}\Gamma\big(\tfrac{1+\gamma}{2}\big)}{\sqrt{\pi}}.

In particular, for

Hence in this case the variance of the stationary distribution

(34)

VI
Relaxation towards stationarity

Partial resetting, similarly to total resetting, is characterized by the existence of a non-equilibrium steady state (NESS) and dynamical phase transition
[
39
]
.
The concept is very well explained in
[
40
]
for the case of Brownian motion in
d=1
with total resetting. The idea is that when
t
tends to
+\infty
the propagator of the process can be written in the scaling form

p_{r}(x,t)\sim e^{-tI\left(x/t\right)}

(35)

where the function
I
is generally called
large deviation function
(LDF). If the LDF shows a discontinuity in one of its derivatives we are in the presence of a dynamical phase transition (we will come back later to the physical meaning of this kind of transition). In the literature on stochastic processes, the LDF is generally obtained either from the theory of large deviations or from saddle-point methods
[
40
]
.
In our companion paper
[
30
]
we were able to prove that for
\alpha=2
we have

p_{r}(x,t)=\begin{cases}p^{(s)}_{r}(x)\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1}\right)\right)&\text{ for }y<y^{*}\\
e^{-rt}(4\pi Dt)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4Dt}}\left[1+\frac{4Dt^{2}}{\lvert{x}\rvert^{2}}\vartheta\left(\frac{4Dt^{2}}{\lvert{x}\rvert^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{4Dt^{2}}{\lvert{x}\rvert^{2}}\right)\right]&\text{ for }y>y^{*},\end{cases}

(36)

where
y=\frac{|x|}{t}
is constant,
y^{*}=2\sqrt{Dr}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the function
\vartheta(z)
is defined as

\vartheta(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{(m;m)_{j+1}}z^{j},\quad|z|<1.

(37)

This suggests that the LDF in eq. (
35
) can be written as

I(y)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{D}}|y|&\text{for }|y|<y^{*}\\
r+\dfrac{y^{2}}{4D}&\text{for }|y|>y^{*},\end{cases}

(38)

which is precisely the same LDF obtained in
[
40
]
for the total resetting.
Interestingly, the dynamical phase transition is not affected either by the dimension or by the value
c\in[0,1[
. From this point of view, partial and total resettings are identical. A graphical representation of this result is presented in Figs.
3
and
4
.
The result is proved in
[
30
]
by making use of (
20
) and of the Stein method
[
41
]
.
The physical meaning of this transition is the following:
first of all, the propagator
p_{r}(x,t)
can be interpreted as
\langle\delta\left(X_{t}-x\right)\rangle
, i.e. we could think of starting
N
independent random walks and computing the histogram of positions at time
t
.
Second, since in the outer region
|x|>\sqrt{4Dr}t
the far asymptotics of the propagator is Gaussian, the trajectories that never experienced resetting will be the ones contributing to the density
p_{r}(x,t)
of this region.
Therefore, following
[
40
]
, we can argue that in the thermodynamical limit
N\to\infty
the phase transition corresponds to a separation between the trajectories that reset many times with respect to the ones that never reset.

The dynamical phase transition is, instead, absent in the case of
\alpha\neq 2
. This is evident in Figure
5
. An intuitive explanation for this behavior is the following.
In Ref.
[
42
]
it was shown that the propagator of a LF for any
t>0
and large
x
scales as

p_{0}(x,t)\sim t|x|^{-d-\alpha}

(39)

Therefore, substituting the previous equation into (
20
) we have that

p_{r}(x,t)\sim A(t)|x|^{-d-\alpha}

(40)

where
A(t)
is some function of time such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}A(t)={\rm const}
.
Moreover, the stationary distribution exhibits the same decay (compare eq. (
27
)).
Therefore, in the process of relaxation, the asymptotics of the propagator does not change, and a dynamical phase transition is absent for any
d
.
The same argument applies in the case of LF with total resetting. Previous works, indeed, did not observe such a dynamical phase transition in
d=1

[
43
,
36
]
.

VII
Boundedness of the propagator

As we have seen so far, there are no major differences between partial and total resetting. Scalings, tails, dynamical phase transitions are precisely the same for the two processes. Yet there is a feature, that we discuss in this section, that distinguishes between the two stochastic processes.
It has been observed in
[
34
]
that the propagator of a Brownian motion with total resetting in
d\geq 2
dimensions is unbounded around
|x|=0
, i.e.
{\lim_{|x|\to 0}p_{r}(x,t)=+\infty}
. The reason for this fact is rather easy: the propagator of a LF has the scaling property

p_{0}(x,t)=t^{-d/\alpha}p_{0}(x/t^{-1/\alpha},1)

(41)

which, in the case of total resetting, after substituting it into eq. (
15
) and taking the limit
|x|\to 0
gives

\lim_{|x|\to 0}p_{r}(x,t)=\lim_{|x|\to 0}\left[e^{-rt}p_{0}(x,t)+\right.\\
+\left.r\int_{0}^{t}e^{-ru}u^{-d/\alpha}p_{0}(x/u^{-1/\alpha},1){\rm d}u\right],

(42)

where the integral at the rhs diverges for
|x|\to 0
whenever
d\geq\alpha
. On the other hand, for PSR after substituting the same scaling (
41
) into eq. (
20
)
we get

\lim_{|x|\to 0}p_{r}(x,t)=\lim_{|x|\to 0}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-d/\alpha}p_{0}(x/u^{-1/\alpha},1)\mu_{t}(u){\rm d}u\right]=\\
=p_{0}(0,1)\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-d/\alpha}\mu_{t}(u){\rm d}u

(43)

where the integral in the last step does not diverge since all splines are identically equal to
0
close to
u=0
(see eqs. (
19
)). In Fig.
6
we plotted the propagator for several values of
d
and
\alpha
.

VIII
Conclusions

We have studied the behavior of partial stochastic resetting for symmetric Lévy flights in arbitrary space dimension and compare it with total resetting. Interestingly, the two processes both show the existence of a stationary distribution with the same asymptotics, the same approach to stationarity and the same phase transition in the case of Brownian motion. The only feature distinguishing the two processes is the behavior around
|x|=0
. We have summarized all these features in Table
VIII
.

We hope our paper will open many perspectives around
PSR. Future directions of research could be the study of
the first-passage time distribution for Lévy flights
[
44
]
with
PSR, or the extension of our theory to asymmetric Lévy
flights
[
45
]
and to non-stable Lévy processes
[
37
]
, or even the case of PSR with cost
[
46
]
associated with each resetting.
Another
interesting direction of the research could be studying the universal singularities of the models with resetting
[
47
,
48
]
. We believe
that our work can serve as a model for practical applications,
e.g. in finance or in actuarial sciences.

Mean-Field Stochastic Linear-Quadratic Optimal Controls: Roles of Expectation and Conditional Expectation Operators
Hanxiao Wang and Jiongmin Yong

Abstract




(
Dedicated to Professor Xun Yu Zhou on the occasion of his 60th birthday
)

Abstract.

This paper investigates a mean-field linear-quadratic optimal control problem where the state dynamics and cost functional incorporate both expectation and conditional expectation terms. We explicitly derive the pre-committed, naïve, and equilibrium solutions and establish the well-posedness of the associated Riccati equations. This reveals how the expectation and conditional expectation operators influence time-consistency.

Key words.

mean-field stochastic differential equation, linear-quadratic, optimal control, time-inconsistency.

AMS subject classifications.
49N10, 49N80, 60H10, 93E20.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which a standard one-dimensional
Brownian motion
W(\cdot)=\{W(t);0\leqslant t<\infty\}
is defined.
The augmented natural filtration of
W(\cdot)
is denoted by
\mathbb{F}=\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
.
For any given initial pair
(t,\xi)\in\mathscr{D}
with

\mathscr{D}=\Big\{(t,\xi)\bigm|t\in[0,T),~\xi\in L_{{\cal F}_{t}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\Big\},

consider the following controlled linear mean-field stochastic differential equation
(SDE, for short) on the finite horizon
[t,T]
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(s)&=\big\{A(s)X(s)+\bar{A}(s)\mathbb{E}_{t}[X(s)]+\tilde{A}(s)\mathbb{E}[X(s)]+B(s)u(s)\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{C(s)X(s)+\bar{C}(s)\mathbb{E}_{t}[X(s)]+\tilde{C}(s)\mathbb{E}[X(s)]+D(s)u(s)\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\\
X(t)&=\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
A,\bar{A},\tilde{A},C,\bar{C},\tilde{C}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
B,D:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
,
called the
coefficients
of the
state equation
(
1.1
), are given deterministic functions.
The solution
X(\cdot)
of (
1.1
) is called a
state process
,
and
u(\cdot)
, belonging to the space

\mathscr{U}[t,T]=\bigg\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\bigm|\varphi\hbox{~is $\mathbb{F}$-progressively measurable},~\mathbb{E}\Big[\int^{T}_{t}|\varphi(s)|^{2}ds\Big]<\infty\bigg\},

is called the
control process
.
To measure the performance of the control
u(\cdot)
, we introduce the following cost functional:

\displaystyle J(t,\xi;u(\cdot))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\langle QX,X\rangle+\langle\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X]\rangle+\langle\tilde{Q}\mathbb{E}[X],\mathbb{E}[X]\rangle+\langle Ru,u\rangle\Big)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle GX(T),X(T)\rangle+\langle\bar{G}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X(T)],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X(T)]\rangle+\langle\tilde{G}\mathbb{E}[X(T)],\mathbb{E}[X(T)]\rangle\Big],

(1.2)

where
G
,
\bar{G}
, and
\tilde{G}
are
n\times n
symmetric matrices;

Q,\bar{Q},\tilde{Q}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
R:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
are deterministic symmetric matrix-valued functions.
In the Lebesgue integral on the right-hand side of (
1
),
we have suppressed the argument
s
, and we will do so in the sequel as long as no ambiguity arises.
With the state equation (
1.1
) and cost functional (
1
),
the problem can be stated as follows:

Problem (MF-SLQ).
For any given initial pair
(t,\xi)\in\mathscr{D}
,
find a control
\bar{u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
such that

J(t,\xi;\bar{u}(\cdot))=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]}J(t,\xi;u(\cdot)).

(1.3)

Any
\bar{u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
satisfying (
1.3
) is called an
optimal control

of Problem (MF-SLQ) for the initial pair
(t,\xi)
;
the corresponding state process
\bar{X}(\cdot)\equiv X(\cdot\,;t,\xi,\bar{u}(\cdot))
is called an

optimal state process
; and

(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
is called an
optimal pair
.
Compared with classical stochastic LQ optimal control problems,
the main feature of our Problem (MF-SLQ) is that the state dynamics and cost functional incorporate both expectation and conditional expectation terms of the state process. Such a type of problems is usually referred to as a
mean-field stochastic linear-quadratic (SLQ) optimal control problem.

The mean-field SLQ optimal control problem was initially studied by Yong
[
26
]
,
in which the state dynamics and cost functional depend on the expectation of the state and control processes.
Such a type of control problems is mainly motivated by the famous
mean-field game
or
mean-field control
theory,
which was independently introduced by Huang–Malhamé–Caines
[
12
]
and Lasry–Lions
[
13
]

in the early 2000s. The mean-field term, that is the expectation term,
comes from the limit of the average state in large population models.
We refer the reader to
[
17
,
20
,
2
,
22
,
11
,
16
,
14
]

for the newest developments of mean-field SLQ optimal control problems along this line.

Another motivation for studying mean-field SLQ control problems is
the so-called
dynamic mean-variance model
(see Basak–Chabakauri
[
1
]
, for example),
in which the cost functional depends on the conditional expectation of the terminal state in a nonlinear way.
In this type of problems, the optimal control is not expected to be time-consistent.
In other words, an optimal control selected at a given initial pair
(t,\xi)\in\mathscr{D}
will not stay optimal thereafter.
Thus, instead of looking for optimal controls, one usually hopes to find an equilibrium strategy.

The earliest mathematical consideration of time-inconsistent problems
was given by Strotz
[
19
]
, followed by Pollak
[
18
]
, and the recent works of Ekeland–Lazrak
[
6
]
, Yong
[
25
,
27
]
, Björk–Khapko–Murgoci
[
3
]
,
He–Jiang
[
7
]
,
Hernández–Possamaï
[
8
]
, and Wang–Yong–Zhou
[
23
]
, to mention a few.
For mean-field SLQ control problems, the related study from a time-inconsistent viewpoint can be found in Basak–Chabakauri
[
1
]
, Hu–Jin–Zhou
[
9
,
10
]
,
Björk–Murgoci–Zhou
[
4
]
, Yong
[
28
]
, Wang
[
24
]
,
Lü–Ma–Wang
[
15
]
, etc.

As noted above, there are two distinct formulations of mean-field SLQ optimal control problems in the literature:
One uses expectation terms
[
26
,
17
,
20
,
2
,
22
,
11
,
16
,
14
]
,
while the other employs conditional expectation terms
[
1
,
9
,
4
,
10
,
28
,
24
,
15
]
,
and these have long been studied independently.
In this paper, we incorporate the expectation and conditional expectation terms together into one controlled system, and hope to provide a relatively uniform approach to these two types of problems.
The
pre-committed
,
naïve
, and
equilibrium
solutions, initially introduced
by Strotz
[
19
]
, will be all derived explicitly,
in terms of the solutions to the associated Riccati equations. Consequently,
the roles of expectation and conditional expectation operators can be clarified.

The main results of this paper can be summarized as follows.

(i)

The optimal control
u^{*}(\cdot)
of Problem (MF-SLQ) at
(t,\xi)\in\mathscr{D}
can be explicitly given by the following form:

u^{*}(\cdot)=\Psi^{*}(\cdot)X^{*}(\cdot)+\bar{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot)\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{*}(\cdot)]+\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot)\mathbb{E}[X^{*}(\cdot)],

(1.4)

where
(\Psi^{*}(\cdot),\bar{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot),\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot))
are determined by the unique solution of the associated three Riccati equations (
2.11
)–(
2.13
),
and
X^{*}(\cdot)
is the corresponding optimal state process.

We can see that the
optimal feedback strategy

(\Psi^{*}(\cdot),\bar{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot),\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot))
is time-consistent,
because it is independent of the initial time
t
(which can be regarded as the current time), while the
optimal feedback operator

\mathbf{\Psi_{t}^{*}}[\,\cdot\,]
,
defined by

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\Psi_{t}^{*}}X](s)

\displaystyle:=[\Psi^{*}(s)+\bar{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}_{t}+\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}][X(s)]

\displaystyle=\Psi^{*}(s)X(s)+\bar{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}_{t}[X(s)]+\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}[X(s)],\quad s\in[t,T],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\forall X(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})),

(1.5)

turns out to be time-inconsistent, mainly due to the fact that it depends on
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\,\cdot\,]
. Thus, Problem (MF-SLQ) is time-inconsistent.
Consequently, the explicit representation (
1.4
) only guarantees
u^{*}(\cdot)
to be optimal at the current moment
t
.
Because of this, we call
u^{*}(\cdot)
given by (
1.4
) a
pre-committed control
over
[t,T]
,
and the corresponding state process
X^{*}(\cdot)
a
pre-committed state
over
[t,T]
.

(ii)

Since the optimal feedback operator
\mathbf{\Psi_{t}^{*}}[\,\cdot\,]
depends on
t
through the conditional expectation operator
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\,\cdot\,]
,
Problem (MF-SLQ) is time-inconsistent.
Motivated by
[
19
,
5
]
, a
naïve feedback operator
is introduced.
It is constructed as the limit of a sequence of optimal feedback operators over every small time interval.
Precisely, the naïve feedback operator
\mathbf{\widehat{\Psi}}[\,\cdot\,]
is given by

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\widehat{\Psi}}X](s)

\displaystyle=[\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(s)+\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(s)\mathbb{E}][X(s)]

\displaystyle=[\Psi^{*}(s)+\bar{\Psi}^{*}(s)]X(s)+\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}[X(s)],\quad s\in[0,T],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\forall X(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})).

(1.6)

By comparing (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), we see that

\mathbf{\widehat{\Psi}}[\,\cdot\,]
can be deduced from the pre-committed solution
\mathbf{\Psi_{0}^{*}}[\,\cdot\,]
over
[0,T]

by replacing
\mathbb{E}_{0}[\,\cdot\,]
with
\mathbb{E}_{s}[\,\cdot\,]
.

(iii)

The
equilibrium feedback operator
of Problem (MF-SLQ) is constructed by the multi-person
differential game method introduced by Yong
[
28
]
, which admits the following form:

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\Psi^{\dagger}}X](s)

\displaystyle:=[\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}(s)+\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}(s)\mathbb{E}][X(s)]

\displaystyle=[\Psi^{\dagger}(s)+\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}(s)]X(s)+\tilde{\Psi}^{\dagger}(s)\mathbb{E}[X(s)],\quad s\in[0,T],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\forall X(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})),

(1.7)

with
(\Psi^{\dagger}(\cdot),\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}(\cdot),\tilde{\Psi}^{\dagger}(\cdot))
being given by
the unique solution of three equilibrium Riccati equations (
2.19
)–(
2.21
).
Comparing with Yong
[
28
]
, we see that (
1.7
) additionally depends on
the expectation of the state process, and some unreasonable assumptions
(i.e., (H4) in
[
28
]
) are removed by
fixing a crucial gap.

From the above, we see that only the conditional expectation operator
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\,\cdot\,]
affects the time-consistency of Problem (MF-SLQ).
In the pre-committed solution
\mathbf{\Psi_{0}^{*}}[\,\cdot\,]
over
[0,T]
, the feedback strategy
\bar{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot)
acts on
\mathbb{E}[X(\cdot)]
. This indicates that the conditional expectation operator functions similarly to the standard expectation operator within this solution.
Conversely, in both the naïve and equilibrium solutions, the respective feedback strategies
\bar{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot)
and
\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}(\cdot)
are applied directly to the state process
X(\cdot)
. Here, the conditional expectation term functions similarly to the state term itself in conventional problems.
However,
\bar{\Psi}^{*}(\cdot)
and
\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger}(\cdot)
are constructed by different Riccati equations.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we state the main results of our paper, with some explanations.
In Section
3
, we give the convergence analysis of the naïve solution.
The equilibrium solution is derived in Section
4
,
and the verification theorem of equilibrium solutions is presented in Section
5
.
Some lengthy proofs are given in Appendix.

The Main Results

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
be the Euclidean space consisting of
n\times m
real matrices,
endowed with the Frobenius inner product
\langle M,N\rangle\triangleq\hbox{tr$\,$}[M^{\top}N]
,
where
M^{\top}
and
\hbox{tr$\,$}(M)
stand for the transpose and the trace of
M
, respectively.
Let
\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
consisting of symmetric matrices
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
the subset of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
consisting of positive semi-definite matrices.
For any Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
(which could be
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
,
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, etc.),
we introduce the following spaces:

\displaystyle C([0,T];\mathbb{H}):

\displaystyle\hbox{~~the space of $\mathbb{H}$-valued, continuous functions on $[0,T]$};

\displaystyle L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{H}):

\displaystyle\hbox{~~the space of $\mathbb{H}$-valued, essentially bounded functions on $[0,T]$};

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}):

\displaystyle\hbox{~~the space of $\mathcal{F}_{t}$-measurable, $\mathbb{H}$-valued, square-integrable random variables};

\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{H}):

the space of
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable,
\mathbb{H}
-valued processes

\displaystyle\hbox{~~$\varphi:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}$ with $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi(s)|^{2}ds\Big]<\infty$};

\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{H})):

the space of
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, continuous,
\mathbb{H}
-valued processes

\displaystyle\hbox{~~$\varphi:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}$ with $\mathbb{E}\Big[\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|\varphi(s)|^{2}\Big]<\infty$}.

For
M,N\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, we use the notation
M\geqslant N
(respectively,
M>N
)
to indicate that
M-N
is positive semi-definite (respectively, positive definite).
For any
\mathbb{S}^{n}
-valued measurable function
F(\cdot)
on
[0,T]
, we denote

\left\{\begin{aligned} F(\cdot)\geqslant 0\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(s)\geqslant 0,\quad\hbox{a.e.}~s\in[0,T];\\
F(\cdot)>0\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(s)>0,\quad\hbox{a.e.}~s\in[0,T];\\
F(\cdot)\gg 0\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(s)\geqslant\delta I_{n},\quad\hbox{a.e.}~s\in[0,T],~\hbox{for some }\delta>0.\end{aligned}\right.

To guarantee that Problem (MF-SLQ) is well-posed,
we impose the following assumptions for the state equation (
1.1
)
and cost functional (
1
).

(H1)

The coefficients of state equation (
1.1
) satisfy

A(\cdot),\bar{A}(\cdot),\tilde{A}(\cdot),C(\cdot),\bar{C}(\cdot),\tilde{C}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}),\quad B(\cdot),D(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}).

(H2)

The weighting coefficients in the quadratic functional (
1
) satisfy:

G,\bar{G},\tilde{G}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
,

Q(\cdot),\bar{Q}(\cdot),\tilde{Q}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{S}^{n})
, and
R(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{S}^{m})
with

Q(\cdot),\bar{Q}(\cdot),\tilde{Q}(\cdot)\geqslant 0,\qquad R(\cdot)\gg 0.

By
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(H1)
hold. Then for any
(t,\xi)\in\mathscr{D}
and
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
,
the state equation (
1.1
) admits a unique solution
X(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
Moreover, there exists a constant
K>0
, which is independent of
(t,\xi)
and
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
, such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X(s)|^{2}\Big]\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big[|\xi|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds\Big].

Note that under
(H1)
, the unique solution
X(\cdot)
of (
1.1
) belongs to
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
In addition, if the assumption
(H2)
holds,
then the random variables on the right-hand side of (
1
) are integrable and Problem (MF-SLQ) is well-posed.

The following lemma will play an important role in the construction of equilibrium solutions.
Consider the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(s)&=\big\{{\cal A}X+\bar{\cal A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X]+\tilde{\cal A}\mathbb{E}[X]\big\}ds\\
&\qquad+\big\{{\cal C}X+\bar{\cal C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X]+\tilde{\cal C}\mathbb{E}[X]\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\\
X(t)&=\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

and the functional

\displaystyle I(X(\cdot))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\langle{\cal Q}_{1}X,X\rangle+\langle{\cal Q}_{2}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X]\rangle+\langle{\cal Q}_{3}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[X],\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[X]\rangle+\langle{\cal Q}_{4}\mathbb{E}[X],\mathbb{E}[X]\rangle\Big)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle{\cal G}_{1}X(T),X(T)\rangle+\langle{\cal G}_{3}\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[X(T)],\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[X(T)]\rangle+\langle{\cal G}_{4}\mathbb{E}[X(T)],\mathbb{E}[X(T)]\rangle\Big],

(2.2)

where
\tau\in[0,t]
is a given time.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that

{\cal A}(\cdot),\bar{\cal A}(\cdot),\tilde{\cal A}(\cdot),{\cal C}(\cdot),\bar{\cal C}(\cdot),\tilde{\cal C}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
,

{\cal G}_{1},{\cal G}_{3},{\cal G}_{4}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
, and

{\cal Q}_{1}(\cdot),{\cal Q}_{2}(\cdot),{\cal Q}_{3}(\cdot),{\cal Q}_{4}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n})
.
Then the following system of Lyapunov equations admits a unique solution

(\check{\Gamma}(\cdot),\Gamma(\cdot),\bar{\Gamma}(\cdot),\tilde{\Gamma}(\cdot))\in[C([0,T];\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n})]^{4}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\check{\Gamma}}+\check{\Gamma}{\cal A}+{\cal A}^{\top}\check{\Gamma}+{\cal C}^{\top}\check{\Gamma}{\cal C}+{\cal Q}_{1}=0,\\
&\check{\Gamma}(T)={\cal G}_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\Gamma}+\Gamma({\cal A}+\bar{\cal A})+({\cal A}+\bar{\cal A})^{\top}\Gamma+({\cal C}+\bar{\cal C})^{\top}\check{\Gamma}({\cal C}+\bar{\cal C})+{\cal Q}_{1}+{\cal Q}_{2}=0,\\
&\Gamma(T)={\cal G}_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\bar{\Gamma}}+\bar{\Gamma}({\cal A}+\bar{\cal A})+({\cal A}+\bar{\cal A})^{\top}\bar{\Gamma}+({\cal C}+\bar{\cal C})^{\top}\check{\Gamma}({\cal C}+\bar{\cal C})+{\cal Q}_{1}+{\cal Q}_{2}+{\cal Q}_{3}=0,\\
&\bar{\Gamma}(T)={\cal G}_{1}+{\cal G}_{3},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\tilde{\Gamma}}+\tilde{\Gamma}({\cal A}+\bar{\cal A}+\tilde{\cal A})+({\cal A}+\bar{\cal A}+\tilde{\cal A})^{\top}\tilde{\Gamma}+({\cal C}+\bar{\cal C}+\tilde{\cal C})^{\top}\check{\Gamma}({\cal C}+\bar{\cal C}+\tilde{\cal C})\\
&\quad+{\cal Q}_{1}+{\cal Q}_{2}+{\cal Q}_{3}+{\cal Q}_{4}=0,\\
&\tilde{\Gamma}(T)={\cal G}_{1}+{\cal G}_{3}+{\cal G}_{4}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

Moreover, we have the following representation of the functional (
2.2
):

\displaystyle I(X(\cdot))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\langle\Gamma(t)(X(t)-\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[X(t)]),\,(X(t)-\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[X(t)])\big\rangle+\big\langle\bar{\Gamma}(t)(\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[X(t)]-\mathbb{E}[X(t)]),

\displaystyle\qquad\quad(\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[X(t)]-\mathbb{E}[X(t)])\big\rangle+\big\langle\tilde{\Gamma}(t)\mathbb{E}[X(t)],\,\mathbb{E}[X(t)]\big\rangle\Big].

(2.7)

Remark 2.3
.

2.2

2.1
Pre-committed Solutions

In this subsection, we shall consider the pre-committed solution.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(H1)
–
(H2)
hold. Then
u^{*}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
is an optimal control for the initial pair
(t,\xi)\in\mathscr{D}
if and only if it satisfies

R(s)u^{*}(s)+B(s)^{\top}Y(s)+D(s)^{\top}Z(s)=0,\quad s\in[t,T],

(2.8)

where
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
is the unique solution to the mean-field BSDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} dY(s)&=-\big\{A^{\top}Y+\bar{A}^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{t}[Y]+\tilde{A}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[Y]+C^{\top}Z+\bar{C}^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{t}[Z]+\tilde{C}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[Z]\\
&\qquad+QX^{*}+\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{*}]+\tilde{Q}\mathbb{E}[X^{*}]\big\}ds+ZdW(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\\
Y(T)&=GX^{*}(T)+\bar{G}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{*}(T)]+\tilde{G}\mathbb{E}[X^{*}(T)],\end{aligned}\right.

(2.9)

with
X^{*}(\cdot)
being the state process of (
1.1
) corresponding to
u^{*}(\cdot)
, that is

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX^{*}(s)&=\big\{AX^{*}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{*}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{*}]+Bu^{*}\big\}ds\\
&\qquad+\big\{CX^{*}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{*}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{*}]+Du^{*}\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[t,T],\\
X^{*}(t)&=\xi.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.10)

The proof of Proposition
2.4
is standard.
For completeness, we sketch it in Appendix (A2).
Note that under
(H2)
, the functional
u(\cdot)\mapsto J(t,\xi;u(\cdot))
is uniformly convex.
Then by following Sun–Yong
[
21
]
, it is easy to show that
for any
(t,\xi)\in\mathscr{D}
, Problem (MF-SLQ) admits a unique optimal control.

To decouple the mean-field forward-backward system (
2.8
)–(
2.10
),
we introduce the following Riccati equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{P}+PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC+Q\\
&\quad-(PB+C^{\top}PD)(R+D^{\top}PD)^{-1}(B^{\top}P+D^{\top}PC)=0,\\
&P(T)=G,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\Pi}+\Pi(A+\bar{A})+(A+\bar{A})^{\top}\Pi+(C+\bar{C})^{\top}P(C+\bar{C})+Q+\bar{Q}\\
&\quad-[\Pi B+(C+\bar{C})^{\top}PD](R+D^{\top}PD)^{-1}[B^{\top}\Pi+D^{\top}P(C+\bar{C})]=0,\\
&\Pi(T)=G+\bar{G},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\Phi}+\Phi(A+\bar{A}+\tilde{A})+(A+\bar{A}+\tilde{A})^{\top}\Phi+(C+\bar{C}+\tilde{C})^{\top}P(C+\bar{C}+\tilde{C})+Q+\bar{Q}+\tilde{Q}\\

Remark 2.6

2.2
Naïve Solutions

The naïve controller is unaware of time inconsistency. At any given initial pair
(t,\xi)\in\mathscr{D}
, he seeks an “optimal” strategy solely for that instant, oblivious to its inevitable future abandonment. Consequently, his strategies perpetually change, and the actually implemented strategy emerges as the limiting outcome of a sequence of momentary “optimal strategies”.

Definition 2.8
.

Let

\Delta_{N}=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{N-1}<t_{N}=T\big\}
be a partition of
[0,T]
with

\|\Delta_{N}\|=\sup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}|t_{i}-t_{i-1}|
.
We call
\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}(\cdot)
a
naïve control
associated with
\Delta_{N}
,
if
\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}(\cdot)|_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}
coincides with the optimal control
associated with the cost functional
J(t_{i},\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(t_{i});u(\cdot))
over every
[t_{i},t_{i+1})
, where

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)&=\big\{A\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+B\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{C\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+D\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}\big\}dW(s),\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}),\quad i=0,1,...,N-2;\\
d\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)&=\big\{A\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t_{N-1}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+B\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{C\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t_{N-1}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+D\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}\big\}dW(s),\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad s\in[t_{N-1},t_{N}],\\
\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(0)&=\xi.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
(\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(\cdot),\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(\cdot))\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})\times L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})
,
\widehat{X}(\cdot)
be the state process of the closed-loop system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widehat{X}(s)&=\big\{(A+\bar{A}+B\widehat{\Psi}_{1})\widehat{X}+(\tilde{A}+B\widehat{\Psi}_{2})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}]\big\}ds\\

\widehat{X}(0)&=\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.17)

and
\widehat{u}(\cdot)=\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(\cdot)\widehat{X}(\cdot)+\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(\cdot)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}(\cdot)]
be the outcome of
(\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(\cdot),\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(\cdot))
.
We call
(\widehat{\Psi}_{1}(\cdot),\widehat{\Psi}_{2}(\cdot))
a
naïve feedback strategy
, if

\lim_{\|\Delta_{N}\|\to 0}\|\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}(\cdot)-\widehat{u}(\cdot)\|=0,

in which case, we call
\mathbf{\widehat{\Psi}}:=\widehat{\Psi}_{1}+\widehat{\Psi}_{2}\mathbb{E}
a
naïve feedback operator
.

2.3
Equilibrium Solutions

The equilibrium solution derives from a “consistent planner” that optimizes the current problem with future controls fixed as constraints. Within this framework, at time
t
, the controller strategically engages with future decision-making selves by minimizing the cost functional over
[t,t+\varepsilon)
, while acknowledging control relinquishment beyond
t+\varepsilon
. The resulting solution is thus termed an equilibrium solution.

Motivated by
[
6
,
25
,
9
,
3
]
,
we introduce the following definition of equilibrium solutions.

Definition 2.10
.

We call
(\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}(\cdot),\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}(\cdot))\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})\times L^{\infty}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{m\times n})

an
equilibrium feedback strategy
if for any
t\in[0,T)
and
u\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, the following holds:

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\dagger}(\cdot))\over\varepsilon}\geqslant 0,

(2.18)

where

u^{\dagger}(s):=\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}(s)X^{\dagger}(s)+\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}(s)\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}(s)],\quad s\in[0,T],

with

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX^{\dagger}(s)&=\big\{[A+\bar{A}+B\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}]X^{\dagger}+[\tilde{A}+B\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}]\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{[C+\bar{C}+D\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}]X^{\dagger}+[\tilde{C}+D\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}]\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[0,T],\\
X^{\dagger}(0)&=\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

and

u^{\varepsilon}(s):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}(s)X^{\varepsilon}(s)+\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}(s)\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}(s)],\quad&s\in[t+\varepsilon,T];\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad u,\quad&s\in[t,t+\varepsilon),\end{aligned}\right.

with

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX^{\varepsilon}(s)&=\big\{AX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+Bu\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{CX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+Du\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[t,t+\varepsilon);\\
dX^{\varepsilon}(s)&=\big\{[A+\bar{A}+B\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}]X^{\varepsilon}+[\tilde{A}+B\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}]\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{[C+\bar{C}+D\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}]X^{\varepsilon}+[\tilde{C}+D\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}]\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[t+\varepsilon,T],\\
X^{\varepsilon}(t)&=X^{\dagger}(t).\end{aligned}\right.

(\Psi_{1}^{\dagger}(\cdot),\Psi_{2}^{\dagger}(\cdot))
is an equilibrium feedback strategy, we call

\mathbf{\Psi^{\dagger}}=\Psi^{\dagger}_{1}+\Psi^{\dagger}_{2}\mathbb{E}

equilibrium feedback operator
.

2.5

with
\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(\cdot)
being the unique solution of the closed-loop system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)&=\big\{(A+B\Psi^{*})\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+(\bar{A}+B\bar{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+(\tilde{A}+B\tilde{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]\big\}ds\\

,

2.5

\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}(\cdot)

\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}(\cdot)
associated with
\Delta_{N}

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)

\displaystyle=\Psi^{*}(s)\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)+\bar{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)]+\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}),\quad i=0,1,...,N-2,

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)

\displaystyle=\Psi^{*}(s)\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)+\bar{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}_{t_{N-1}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)]+\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(s)\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad s\in[t_{N-1},t_{N}],

(3.1)

with
\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(\cdot)
being the unique solution of the closed-loop system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)&=\big\{(A+B\Psi^{*})\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+(\bar{A}+B\bar{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+(\tilde{A}+B\tilde{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{(C+D\Psi^{*})\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+(\bar{C}+D\bar{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]\\
&\qquad+(\tilde{C}+D\tilde{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}),\quad i=0,1,...,N-2;\\
d\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)&=\big\{(A+B\Psi^{*})\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+(\bar{A}+B\bar{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}_{t_{N-1}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]+(\tilde{A}+B\tilde{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{(C+D\Psi^{*})\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}+(\bar{C}+D\bar{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}_{t_{N-1}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]\\
&\qquad+(\tilde{C}+D\tilde{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}]\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[t_{N-1},t_{N}],\\
\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(0)&=\xi.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

Recall from (
2.17
) that

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widehat{X}(s)&=\big\{(A+\bar{A}+B\Psi^{*}+B\bar{\Psi}^{*})\widehat{X}+(\tilde{A}+B\tilde{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}]\big\}ds\\
&\quad+\big\{(C+\bar{C}+D\Psi^{*}+D\bar{\Psi}^{*})\widehat{X}+(\tilde{C}+D\tilde{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}]\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[0,T],\\
\widehat{X}(0)&=\xi.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

By applying the standard estimates of SDEs to (
3.2
)–(
3.3
), we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)-\widehat{X}(s)|^{2}\Big]\leqslant K\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big|\widehat{X}(s)-\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}(s)]\big|^{2}ds\Big].

(3.4)

Note that for
s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{X}(s)-\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}(s)]=\int_{t_{i}}^{s}(A+\bar{A}+B\Psi^{*}+B\bar{\Psi}^{*})(\widehat{X}-\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}])dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t_{i}}^{s}\big\{(C+\bar{C}+D\Psi^{*}+D\bar{\Psi}^{*})\widehat{X}+(\tilde{C}+D\tilde{\Psi}^{*})\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}]\big\}dW(r).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\widehat{X}(s)-\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}(s)]|^{2}\big]\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[0,T]}|\widehat{X}(r)|^{2}\Big]|s-t_{i}|\leqslant K\|\Delta_{N}\|,\quad s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}].

Substituting the above into (
3.4
) yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{\|\Delta_{N}\|\to 0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)-\widehat{X}(s)|^{2}\Big]\leqslant\lim_{\|\Delta_{N}\|\to 0}KT\|\Delta_{N}\|=0.

(3.5)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\widehat{u}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)-\widehat{u}(s)|^{2}ds\Big]\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)-\widehat{X}(s)|^{2}ds\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad+K\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\big|\widehat{X}(s)-\mathbb{E}_{t_{i}}[\widehat{X}_{\Delta_{N}}(s)]\big|^{2}ds\Big]

(4.25)

By the comparison theorem of Lyapunov equations (see

Verification Theorem and Local Optimality

For any given
t\in[0,T)
and
\varepsilon>0
, consider the following controlled system

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX^{\varepsilon}(s)&=\big\{AX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+Bu\big\}ds\\
&\qquad+\big\{CX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+Du\big\}dW(s),\quad s\in[t,t+\varepsilon);\\

(5.1)

and the cost functional

\displaystyle J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big[\langle QX^{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}\rangle+\langle\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle+\langle\tilde{Q}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle Ru,u\rangle\Big]ds+\int_{t+\varepsilon}^{T}\Big[\langle QX^{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}\rangle+\langle\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle+\langle\tilde{Q}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle R[(\Psi^{\dagger}+\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger})X^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{\Psi}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]],[(\Psi^{\dagger}+\bar{\Psi}^{\dagger})X^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{\Psi}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]]\rangle\Big]ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\langle GX^{\varepsilon}(T),X^{\varepsilon}(T)\rangle+\langle\bar{G}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}(T)],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}(T)]\rangle+\langle\tilde{G}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}(T)],\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}(T)]\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad=:\mathbb{E}\Big\{\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big[\langle QX^{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}\rangle+\langle\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle+\langle\tilde{Q}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle+\langle Ru,u\rangle\Big]ds+\mathbb{I}_{\varepsilon}\Big\}.

(5.2)

By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\langle\Gamma(t+\varepsilon)X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon),\,X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)\big\rangle+\big\langle[\bar{\Gamma}(t+\varepsilon)-\Gamma(t+\varepsilon)]\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)],\,\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)]\big\rangle

Then,

\displaystyle J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\langle QX^{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}\rangle+\langle\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\tilde{Q}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle+\langle Ru,u\rangle\Big)ds+\big\langle\Gamma(t+\varepsilon)X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon),\,X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\big\langle[\bar{\Gamma}(t+\varepsilon)-\Gamma(t+\varepsilon)]\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)],\,\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)]\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\big\langle[\tilde{\Gamma}(t+\varepsilon)-\bar{\Gamma}(t+\varepsilon)]\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)],\,\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)]\big\rangle\Big].

(5.3)

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\big\langle\Gamma(t+\varepsilon)X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon),\,X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)\big\rangle\big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\langle\Gamma(t)X^{\dagger}(t),\,X^{\dagger}(t)\big\rangle+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\big\langle\dot{\Gamma}X^{\varepsilon},\,X^{\varepsilon}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2\big\langle\Gamma\big[AX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+Bu\big],\,X^{\varepsilon}\big\rangle+\big\langle\Gamma\big[CX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\big\langle[\tilde{\Gamma}(t+\varepsilon)-\bar{\Gamma}(t+\varepsilon)]\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)],\,\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}(t+\varepsilon)]\big\rangle\big]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\langle[\tilde{\Gamma}(t)-\bar{\Gamma}(t)]\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}(t)],\,\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}(t)]\big\rangle+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\big\langle[\dot{\tilde{\Gamma}}-\dot{\bar{\Gamma}}]\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}],\,\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+2\big\langle[\tilde{\Gamma}-\bar{\Gamma}]\big[A\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+B\mathbb{E}[u]\big],\,\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\big\rangle\Big)ds\Big].

Thus,

\displaystyle J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\langle\bar{\Gamma}(t)X^{\dagger}(t),\,X^{\dagger}(t)\big\rangle+\big\langle[\tilde{\Gamma}(t)-\bar{\Gamma}(t)]\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}(t)],\,\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}(t)]\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\langle QX^{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}\rangle+\langle\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle+\langle\tilde{Q}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}],\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\rangle+\langle Ru,u\rangle\Big)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\big\langle\dot{\Gamma}X^{\varepsilon},\,X^{\varepsilon}\big\rangle+2\big\langle\Gamma\big[AX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+Bu\big],\,X^{\varepsilon}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\big\langle\Gamma\big[CX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+Du\big],\,\big[CX^{\varepsilon}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+Du\big]\big\rangle\Big)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\big\langle[\dot{\bar{\Gamma}}-\dot{\Gamma}]\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}],\,\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]\big\rangle+2\big\langle[\bar{\Gamma}-\Gamma]\big[A\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]

\displaystyle\qquad+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+B\mathbb{E}_{t}[u]\big],\,\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\varepsilon}]\big\rangle\Big)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]+B\mathbb{E}[u]\big],\,\mathbb{E}[X^{\varepsilon}]\big\rangle\Big)ds\Big].

(5.4)

By the same arguments as the above, we have

\displaystyle J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\dagger}(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\langle\bar{\Gamma}(t)X^{\dagger}(t),\,X^{\dagger}(t)\big\rangle+\big\langle[\tilde{\Gamma}(t)-\bar{\Gamma}(t)]\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}(t)],\,\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}(t)]\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\langle QX^{\dagger},X^{\dagger}\rangle+\langle\bar{Q}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}],\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}]\rangle+\langle\tilde{Q}\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}],\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]\rangle+\langle Ru^{\dagger},u^{\dagger}\rangle\Big)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\big\langle\dot{\Gamma}X^{\dagger},\,X^{\dagger}\big\rangle+2\big\langle\Gamma\big[AX^{\dagger}+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}]+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]+Bu^{\dagger}\big],\,X^{\dagger}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\big\langle\Gamma\big[CX^{\dagger}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]+Du^{\dagger}\big],\,\big[CX^{\dagger}+\bar{C}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}]+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]+Du^{\dagger}\big]\big\rangle\Big)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\Big(\big\langle[\dot{\bar{\Gamma}}-\dot{\Gamma}]\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}],\,\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}]\big\rangle+2\big\langle[\bar{\Gamma}-\Gamma]\big[A\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}]+\bar{A}\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle\qquad+\tilde{A}\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]+B\mathbb{E}_{t}[u^{\dagger}]\big],\,\mathbb{E}_{t}[X^{\dagger}]\big\rangle\Big)ds\Big]

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}{J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\dagger}(\cdot))\over\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle Ru,u\rangle+2\langle u,B^{\top}\bar{\Gamma}X^{\dagger}\rangle+2\langle\Gamma[CX^{\dagger}+\bar{C}X^{\dagger}+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]],Du\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\langle\Gamma Du,Du\rangle+2\big\langle[\tilde{\Gamma}-\bar{\Gamma}]B\mathbb{E}[u],\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]\big\rangle-\langle Ru^{\dagger},u^{\dagger}\rangle-2\langle u^{\dagger},B^{\top}\bar{\Gamma}X^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-2\langle\Gamma[CX^{\dagger}+\bar{C}X^{\dagger}+\tilde{C}\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]],Du^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle\Gamma Du^{\dagger},Du^{\dagger}\rangle-2\big\langle[\tilde{\Gamma}-\bar{\Gamma}]B\mathbb{E}[u^{\dagger}],\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]\big\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\langle[R+D^{\top}\Gamma D]u,\,u\big\rangle+2\big\langle u,\,[B^{\top}\bar{\Gamma}+D^{\top}\Gamma C+D^{\top}\Gamma\bar{C}]X^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+[D^{\top}\Gamma\tilde{C}+B^{\top}(\tilde{\Gamma}-\bar{\Gamma})]\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]\big\rangle-\big\langle[R+D^{\top}\Gamma D]u^{\dagger},\,u^{\dagger}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-2\big\langle u^{\dagger},\,[B^{\top}\bar{\Gamma}+D^{\top}\Gamma C+D^{\top}\Gamma\bar{C}]X^{\dagger}+[D^{\top}\Gamma\tilde{C}+B^{\top}(\tilde{\Gamma}-\bar{\Gamma})]\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]\big\rangle\Big].

Noting that

u^{\dagger}=-[R+D^{\top}\Gamma D]^{-1}\big\{[B^{\top}\bar{\Gamma}+D^{\top}\Gamma C+D^{\top}\Gamma\bar{C}]X^{\dagger}+[D^{\top}\Gamma\tilde{C}+B^{\top}(\tilde{\Gamma}-\bar{\Gamma})]\mathbb{E}[X^{\dagger}]\big\},

which is the minimizer of the mapping

we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}{J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(t,X^{\dagger}(t);u^{\dagger}(\cdot))\over\varepsilon}\geqslant 0.

This completes the proof.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied linear quadratic optimal control problems. The state equation and cost functional contain the state and control, together with the expectations and conditional expectations of the state. The problem turns out to be time-inconsistent, meaning that the optimal control selected for the given initial pair will not stay optimal thereafter. The main purpose is to indicate that in our framework, the time-inconsistency is due to the fact that the conditional expectation of the state appears in the state equation and cost functional.

We have not got into the most generality. There are many possible extensions. We mention some of them:

\bullet
The weight matrix valued maps can depend on two time variables. This is the case if we have general discounting in the cost functional (see
[
28
]
).

\bullet
The state equation can contain the expectations and conditional expectations of control processes (see
[
26
,
28
]
).

\bullet
The state equation can contain nonhomogeneous terms and the cost functional contains linear terms. In this case, proper BSDEs are expected to be involved (See
[
21
]
for the time-consistent problem).

\bullet
Without assuming
(H2)
, one has the so-called indefinite LQ problems. We could assume the map
u(\cdot)\mapsto J(t,x;u(\cdot))
to be uniformly convex (See also
[
21
]
).

\bullet
All the involved functions are random.

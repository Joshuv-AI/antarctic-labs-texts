From surface adsorption to V-type inclusion complexes: Amylose-driven structural regulation of cyanidin-3-O-glucoside binding and functionality in rice starch matrix
Authors: Halah Aalim, Nosyba A Ibrahim, Hamza MA Abaker, Lu Zhou, Muhammad Arslan, Xiaoli Shu, Jinyuan Sun, Zhihua Li, Chenguang Zhou, Xiaobo Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAnthocyanin instability during food processing and gastrointestinal digestion limits their functional potential. This study investigated interactions between cyanidin-3-O-glucoside (C3G) and waxy (WS, 0% amylose), low-amylose (LS, 15.71%), and high-amylose (HS, 35.74%) rice starches under native (N) and hydrothermally treated (T) conditions, relevant to black rice. In native granules, amorphous amylose-C3G interactions involved non-inclusion hydrogen bonding, leading to gradual granule swelling, especially in HS-N. Waxy starch (WS-N) exhibited the highest bioaccessibility (25.6%) and a significant blue shift in chrominance caused by weak surface adsorption of C3G. Amylose contents improved C3G binding, and formed labile V-type complexes, while thermal treatment disrupted lamellar order, shifted starch fractions towards digestible forms, and caused granule fragmentation. DCI analysis indicated that hydrothermal treatment had a minor advantage on regulating C3G bioaccessibility than amylose contents. Collectively, these findings provide mechanistic insights for designing black rice-based functional food ingredients with modified anthocyanin bioaccessibility profiles.Keywords: Rice starch, Cyanidin-3-O-glucoside, Bioaccessibility, Amylose, Thermal treatment, V-type inclusion complexes, Non-inclusion complexes

Introduction

Starch is the predominant polysaccharide in plant-based foods and the primary source of dietary carbohydrates worldwide. Its functionality in food systems is determined by the relative proportions of its two glucan polymers, amylose and amylopectin, which govern gelatinization behavior, retrogradation, enzymatic digestibility, food processing behavior, and the capacity to interact with bioactive molecules (Li et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2021). Rice (Oryza sativa L.) starch exhibits unique structural features among cereal starches: its granules are the smallest (2–7 μm), and it spans a broad amylose range from waxy (0% amylose) to high-amylose (exceeding 35%) genotypes, thereby influencing physicochemical properties and digestibility (Aalim & Luo, 2021; An et al., 2021; Obadi & Xu, 2021; Pérez & Bertoft, 2010; Wei et al., 2018). These structural differences make rice starch a suitable model substrate for studying the concentration-dependent role of amylose in starch-polyphenol interactions.

Anthocyanins are known for high bioactivity (Herrera-Balandrano et al., 2021). However, their use in functional foods is limited by inherent instability and sensitivity to environmental and processing conditions, as well as enzymatic digestion (Aalim et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2019). The main anthocyanin in black rice is cyanidin-3-O-glucoside (C3G), which accounts for 70–80% of the total anthocyanin content and can reach concentrations up to 3 mg g−1 of black rice (Aalim et al., 2021; Aalim et al., 2024). The anthocyanins in black rice are primarily located in the pericarp and interact with grain macromolecules including starch and protein, during processing and hydrothermal treatment (Aalim et al., 2026). These interactions stabilize the bioaccessibility of monomeric anthocyanins in cooked black rice (Aalim et al., 2024), underscoring the importance of matrix-based stabilization strategies in functional food development (Aalim et al., 2026). Previous work has independently established that amylose content affects the affinity between starch and anthocyanins (Shen et al., 2025), and that hydrothermal processes modify starch crystallinity and anthocyanin retention (Miao et al., 2021). However, these studies employed different starch sources, and neither the concentration-dependent role of amylose across the full agronomic range nor the synergistic effects of amylose content and thermal processing within a unified experimental framework have been systematically characterized. Thus, the objective of the present study was to elucidate the independent and synergistic mechanisms by which starch structural composition, including amylose content and hydrothermal processing modulate the physicochemical and functional properties of starch-C3G complexes in rice matrix, employing a factorial design across a specified amylose gradient (waxy (0%), low-amylose (15.71%), and high-amylose (35.74%)). Explicitly, we studied the effects of amylose contents and hydrothermal treatment on C3G binding, in vitro C3G bioaccessibility, starch nutritional fractions, and pigment-induced chromatic changes. Comprehensive structural and physicochemical characterization was performed. Dose Correction Index (DCI) was employed to assess the extent to which starch composition mitigates C3G losses during hydrothermal treatment and simulated gastrointestinal digestion. These results will provide a mechanistic framework and quantitative guidance for designing rice-based anthocyanin-rich systems with tunable structure and bioaccessibility.

Materials and methods

Materials

C3G (≥ 98% purity) was obtained from Shanghai Tauto Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). α-Amylase (3000 U/mL) and amyloglucosidase (3300 U/mL) were obtained from Megazyme (Bray, Ireland). Low-amylose rice starch (LS) with a moisture content of 10.67 ± 0.41%, total starch content of 92.57 ± 1.50%, and amylose content of 15.71 ± 0.11% was procured from Yuanye Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Waxy rice starch (WS) was characterized by a moisture content of 16.78 ± 0.60%, total starch content of 97.74 ± 2.31%, and amylose content below the limit of detection using the amylose–iodine method (Aalim et al., 2021). High-amylose rice starch (HS) had a moisture content of 8.77 ± 0.11%, total starch content of 92.52 ± 2.20%, and amylose content of 35.74 ± 0.18%. WS and HS starches were isolated from corresponding waxy and high-amylose rice grains.

Preparation of starch-C3G complexes

Starch slurries of WS, LS, and HS (1 g, 10% w/v) were mixed with 3 mg of C3G and vortex-mixed for 5 min. Next, the mixtures were incubated in the dark at room temperature (25 °C) for 1 h, with periodic vortex mixing at pH 7 ± 1, as verified at the beginning and end of the treatment, to produce native starch-treated samples (N). A parallel set of samples was prepared under identical conditions and then gelatinized in a boiling water bath for 30 min to produce hydrothermally treated samples (T). Samples were stored overnight in the refrigerator (4 °C) and coded as: The native starches of C3G-treated samples were designated as WS-N, LS-N, and HS-N, and the hydrothermally C3G-treated products of WS, LS, and HS were designated as WS-T, LS-T, and HS-T, respectively. Gelatinized starch controls (WS-B, LS-B, and HS-B, corresponding to WS, LS, and HS, respectively) prepared under the same conditions were used to compare the results and ensure consistency. A control of thermally treated C3G was also prepared to account for any degradation of C3G during hydrothermal treatment. The samples were lyophilized for further analysis.

Determination of C3G binding ratio

The binding ratio of C3G to rice starch was determined using a colorimetric method (Li et al., 2024). Briefly, the free C3G content in the sample was quantified by dissolving a known mass of C3G-loaded starch in methanol at a 1:15 ratio to extract free C3G. The solutions obtained were centrifuged for 10 min at 10,000 ×g and 4 °C. The absorbance of total monomeric anthocyanins was measured at its maximum absorbance wavelength (520 nm) using a UV–Vis microplate spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Vantaa, Finland). A calibration curve of pure C3G was established under the same conditions, and the absorbance was correlated with the concentration.

In vitro starch digestibility and C3G bioaccessibility

The digestibility of Starch-C3G was assessed using a previously described method (Aalim et al., 2021). Starch samples (100 mg, dry weight basis) were suspended in 9 mL of acetate buffer (0.2 M, pH 6.0, containing 200 mM CaCl₂ and 0.5 mM MgCl₂) and equilibrated at 37 °C. Subsequently, 1 mL of a mixed enzyme solution containing 10 U/mL α-amylase and 18 U/mL amyloglucosidase was added to initiate starch hydrolysis. Aliquots (100 μL) of the enzymatic hydrolysate were collected at predetermined time intervals (0, 20, and 120 min) and immediately mixed with 100 μL of 95% ethanol (v/v) to halt enzyme activity. The unreacted starch residue was separated by centrifugation at 2000 ×g for 5 min. The bioaccessibility of C3G was measured as the fraction released during digestion, determined using the colorimetric method outlined in section 2.3. Characterization of starch-C3G system

Iodine-binding capacity of starch

The formation of the amylose‑iodine complex was performed as previously described. (Amoako & Awika, 2019). Briefly, starch samples (0.02 g) were dissolved in 25 mL of 0.15 M KOH and stirred for 30 min to ensure complete dissolution. The mixture was centrifuged at 16,600 ×g for 10 min at 4 °C to pellet any insoluble material. Then, 1 mL of the supernatant was neutralized by 9 mL of 0.017 M HCl. Then, 0.1 mL of iodine reagent (5% I₂ and 10% KI) was added to form a complex with amylose. The resulting solution was analyzed using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (UV-1601, Rayleigh, Beijing, China) over the wavelength range of 300–900 nm. Background absorbance was subtracted using C3G only as a blank control rather than the starch-C3G system.

Determination of chromatic properties

The color variations in the starch-C3G systems were assessed using a colorimetric spectrophotometer (CM2300d; Konica Minolta Holding, Inc., Japan). The results were reported in the CIE Lab* space, where L* denotes lightness (0 = black, 100 = white), a* represents the red–green axis (positive values towards red), and b* the yellow–blue axis (positive values towards yellow). Adobe Photoshop CS6 (version 13.0, Adobe Systems, Inc., San Jose, CA, USA) was employed to convert Lab* color space to RGB. Particle size analysis

The particle size distributions (PSD) of the samples were determined by laser diffraction using a Litesizer 500 (Anton Paar, Steiermark, Austria). The samples were dispersed in deionized water at 2.5 mg/mL to meet the instrument's recommended concentration range.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis

The surface morphologies of the samples were analyzed using scanning electron microscopy (Regulus 8100, Hitachi High-Technologies, Tokyo, Japan) at an accelerating voltage of 2 kV. The samples were mounted on carbon double-sided tape and sputter-coated with gold prior to imaging. Micrographs were acquired at 10,000× magnification with a 5 μm scale bar.

Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) analysis

FTIR analysis was conducted using FTIR spectroscopy (Perkin Elmer Frontier, USA). The scanning range was set from 4000 to 500 cm−1. The spectra were acquired at a resolution of 4 cm−1 using 16 scans.

X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis

XRD analysis was conducted to examine the crystallinity and amorphous characteristics using an X-ray diffractometer (D8 Advance, Bruker, Germany) equipped with a Cu Kα radiation source. The diffraction angle (2θ) was configured to range from 5° to 40° with a scanning rate of 4°/min and a step size of 0.02°.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data were analyzed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) and are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). One-way ANOVA and Tukey's multiple comparison tests were performed using SPSS version 20.0 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). In addition, two-way ANOVA was used to test main and interaction effects of amylose content and hydrothermal treatment in C3G-treated samples; partial η2 was reported as an effect-size measure. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. All analyses were conducted in triplicate.

Results and discussion

C3G binding ratio and bioaccessibility

The binding ratio and in vitro bioaccessibility of C3G showed considerable variation with starch type and treatment (Fig. 1A and B). In native starch, LS-N showed the highest binding capacity, followed by HS-N and WS-N (P < 0.05). Following hydrothermal treatment, the binding affinities of all samples increased significantly (P < 0.001). Specifically, hydrothermal treatment induced the most dramatic increase in waxy starch, elevating its binding ratio from 89.13% to 95.30% (WS-T). A two-way ANOVA revealed a significant main effect of hydrothermal treatment (F = 164.71, P < 0.0001, ηp2 = 0.932) and amylose content (F = 35.52, P < 0.0001, ηp2 = 0.856). Notably, both hydrothermal treatment (ηp2 = 0.932) and amylose content (ηp2 = 0.856) exerted highly significant main effects on C3G binding ratio, with hydrothermal treatment showing a somewhat larger effect size. Furthermore, a significant interaction effect was observed between amylose content and hydrothermal treatment (F = 14.68, P = 0.001, ηp2 = 0.710), indicating that the impact of the hydrothermal treatment is modulated by the specific amylose matrix. While methanol extraction is the established standard extraction method for free anthocyanins, however, modest matrix-dependent discrepancies in extraction efficiency may be present and were not quantitatively assessed in this investigation.

In vitro C3G bioaccessibility was highest for waxy starch (WS-N; 25.6%), and was significantly greater than that of all other treatments (P < 0.05). Both low-amylose (LS-N, 24.0%; LS-T, 23.0%) and high-amylose preparations (HS-N, 23.9%; HS-T, 23.3%) showed no significant difference in bioaccessibility. Two-way ANOVA revealed a significant main effect of amylose content (F = 12.25, P < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.671) and hydrothermal treatment (F = 11.72, P < 0.005, ηp2 = 0.494). Conspicuously, the interaction effect between the two factors was not significant (P = 0.669), indicating that the bioaccessibility effect of starch composition was consistent across thermal treatment conditions.

The present findings are consistent with prior reports indicating that starch structure, particularly the amylose contents, had a noteworthy impact on the interactions with anthocyanins and their subsequent bioaccessibility (Yang et al., 2024). The high binding ratios observed in the present study were due to the high affinity of the starch matrix for C3G at the tested concentration, which is below the saturation threshold for starch polyphenol loading (Zhang et al., 2023). The increased binding affinity observed in LS variants might be caused by the high concentration of short-chain amylopectin, which provides more binding sites for anthocyanins (Li et al., 2024). While the structural characteristics of amylopectin in waxy starch (WS-N) led to low affinity for C3G (Shen et al., 2025), with the highest bioaccessibility. After gelatinization, amylopectin degraded to linear chains (Xu et al., 2021), leading to the formation of tight inclusion complexes with anthocyanins (Shen et al., 2025). Therefore, C3G was more strongly bound to the WS-T matrix than to the WS-N matrix, resulting in lower bioaccessibility during digestion.

Dose correction index

DCI was used to evaluate the individual and combined effects of hydrothermal treatment, amylose content, and in vitro digestion on the C3G bioaccessibility relative to the pure and thermally treated C3G control (Table 2). Importantly, the DCI captures the relative change in C3G bioaccessibility attributable to each experimental factor, rather than an absolute bioaccessibility value. Therefore, a small absolute change accompanied by a statistically significant DCI shift indicates a consistent, reproducible modulation of the release profile relevant to controlled-delivery applications. Digestion, hydrothermal treatment, and their combination slightly decreased the bioaccessibility of C3G, with DCI values ranging from 1.02 to 1.03, indicating that C3G was highly stable during hydrothermal treatment. These findings are consistent with the existing literature, as acylated cyanidin has higher thermal stability (Zhai et al., 2024). The native starch samples showed that both digestion and amylose content significantly decreased the C3G bioaccessibility, as indicated by the DCI values. The DCI for amylose content in WS-N was 0.96, indicating the waxy starch matrix's ability to enhance the bioaccessibility of C3G relative to the free C3G control.

The combination of hydrothermal treatment and rice amylose showed the most significant reduction in C3G bioaccessibility (LS-T: 1.12; HS-T: 1.11), consistent with the overall trend in C3G bioaccessibility. To validate the functional significance of the DCI shifts, a two-way ANOVA was performed on the total DCI values. The analysis indicated a significant main effect of amylose content (F = 19.786, P < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.767) and hydrothermal treatment (F = 39.896, P < 0.0001, ηp2 = 0.769), with no significant interaction effect (F = 0.25, P = 0.782, ηp2 = 0.0403). These results support the formation of starch-C3G complexes during gelatinization, which reduced C3G bioaccessibility. These findings are consistent with the existing DCI literature, suggesting the phenolic structure and food matrix were the main determinants of polyphenol bioaccessibility (Sęczyk et al., 2021).

Starch nutritional fractions

The distribution of starch nutritional fractions in the samples is shown in Fig. 1C. In native granules, RDS and SDS contents were highest in WS-N and LS-N, followed by HS-N (P < 0.05). In contrast, RS levels were significantly higher in HS-N samples (81.9%) than in the other starch variants (P < 0.05), attributable to the densely packed crystalline architecture and higher amylose content characteristic of this starch type (Du et al., 2025).

The content of RDS was significantly the highest in WS-T and WS-B (29.0%) after hydrothermal treatment, which was similar to that of LS-B (P > 0.05), followed by LS-T, HS-T, and HS-B (P < 0.05). These results were in accordance with previous studies, where waxy rice flour had a much higher digestion rate due to its higher swelling power than non-waxy rice (Farooq et al., 2021). Furthermore, the fine structure of amylopectin was the main factor affecting starch digestibility, rather than starch origin or amylose content (Du, Pan, et al., 2022). Hydrothermally treated samples showed significantly higher SDS levels and lower RS levels than their native starch counterparts. This is in line with the observation that gelatinization greatly improved starch digestibility (Li et al., 2021).

A two-way ANOVA was used to rigorously assess the individual and synergistic impacts of amylose content and hydrothermal treatments on the digestibility of C3G-treated starch. RDS analysis revealed a highly significant main effect of hydrothermal treatment (F = 1442.50, P < 0.0001, ηp2 = 0.992) and amylose content (F = 27.55, P < 0.0001, ηp2 = 0.821), and a significant interaction effect (F = 19.49, P < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.765). While SDS fractions revealed a highly significant main effect of hydrothermal treatment (F = 759.33, P < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.984) and amylose content (F = 6.54, P = 0.012, ηp2 = 0.522), alongside a significant interaction effect (F = 9.92, P = 0.003, ηp2 = 0.632). Further, RS fractions revealed a highly significant main effect of hydrothermal treatment (F = 3963.60, P < 0.0001, ηp2 = 0.997) and amylose content (F = 39.68, P < 0.0001, ηp2 = 0.869), alongside a significant interaction effect (F = 21.39, P < 0.0001, ηp2 = 0.781). The near-total effect size for hydrothermal treatment (ηp2 > 0.997) and the significant interaction effect suggest that gelatinization is the primary driver of the shift in digestible starch fractions, and the magnitude of the digestibility shift is modulated by the starch type.

The nutritional fractions of starch-C3G complexes after hydrothermal treatment were similar to those of gelatinized starch-C3G complexes. This observation was in line with the weak α-amylase inhibition of C3G and was attributed to the bulky glucoside moiety of C3G, which hindered binding to the active site and limited the hydroxylation of the B-ring in the absence of methyl groups for stabilization (Xiao et al., 2025). The pattern highlights the role of the polyphenol structure and concentration, and their broader interactions with food matrices and digestive enzymes (Ayim et al., 2019). In addition, the intrinsic structural integrity of amylose (Li, Gilbert and Gidley, 2021; Obadi et al., 2020) resulted in slight conversion of SDS to RS fractions in HS-B. This is consistent with the notion that structural features such as increased crystallinity, molecular order, granule surface morphology, and medium-length amylose chains play a crucial role in starch digestibility (Guo et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023; Obadi et al., 2020; Tian, Petersen, et al., 2024).

The starch‑iodine binding

The starch‑iodine binding spectra suggest that C3G and hydrothermal treatments significantly affected the starch structure and amylose availability (Fig. 1D). The starch‑iodine binding spectra of the samples showed red or blue shifts in the peak wavelength (Amax) from about 520 to 600 nm, suggesting differences in amylose content and structural changes. Native starch samples (containing amylose, LS, and HS) exhibited similar iodine-binding behavior, with Amax at about 580 nm, indicating an intact amylose double-helix structure. The binding of C3G to HS-N resulted in a red shift to 590 nm without a change in peak shape, suggesting non-inclusion complexation with the amylose conformation rather than cavity encapsulation (Amoako & Awika, 2019). Iodine peak intensity increased due to starch gelatinization in amylose-containing samples (LS-B and HS-B). This effect was further increased by the interaction with C3G, resulting in red shifts at about 600 nm for LS-T and HS-T.

In contrast, the absence of any peak in WS and WS-N is consistent with the absence of amylose helices. Interestingly, a large blue shift of 520 nm was observed for both WS-B and WS-T. These results indicate that hydrothermal treatment and C3G binding caused significant alteration of starch through partial degradation of amylose and structural rearrangement, resulting in a decreased ability to form the amylose‑iodine complex (Amoako & Awika, 2019). A red shift was observed for the starch-anthocyanin complex in accordance with our results (Zhang et al., 2024).

Starch chrominance

The incorporation of C3G into various starch matrices led to significant changes in colorimetric values, as expressed in RGB and CIE L*a*b* coordinates (Fig. 1E and F). Gelatinized starches were of relatively high lightness and showed little perceptible color change compared to their native counterparts, with ΔE values of 20.55, 20.48, and 15.52 for LS-B, WS-B, and HS-B, respectively. The binding of C3G resulted in a pronounced darkening of the starch matrix, indicating increased pigment-matrix interactions, which varied in degree depending on the starch. Hydrothermal treatment led to further darkening of color and more pronounced shifts in chrominance. WS-N showed small red components and a large blue shift. In contrast, WS-T had small green components and a slight yellow shift. The LS matrices treated with C3G presented a significant red shift and a moderate blue component compared to the other starches. The greatest response was observed in the HS-T matrix, with the lowest L* and highest ΔE values. This suggests that heat led to stronger, more extensive stabilization of C3G in the HS matrix than in the other starches.

Two-way ANOVA demonstrated that amylose content and hydrothermal treatment exerted highly significant main effects, along with significant interaction effects, on all colorimetric parameters (L*, a*, b*, and ΔE; all P < 0.0001). The ηp2 values for these factors were exceptionally high across all variables (ranging from 0.912 to 1.000), indicating that starch composition and processing drive highly reproducible and distinct chromatic shifts. Specifically, the statistical outcomes for each parameter were: L* (amylose: F = 577.27, ηp2 = 0.99; hydrothermal treatment: F = 1098.09, ηp2 = 0.99; interaction: F = 584.65, ηp2 = 0.99); a* (amylose: F = 46,120.2, ηp2 = 0.9999; hydrothermal treatment: F = 124, ηp2 = 0.912; interaction effect: F = 1544.55, ηp2 = 0.996); b* (amylose content: F = 2903.52, ηp2 = 0.9979; hydrothermal treatment: F = 22,209.17, ηp2 = 0.9995; interaction effect: F = 13,547.38, ηp2 = 0.9996); ΔE (amylose content: F = 172.90, ηp2 = 0.966; hydrothermal treatment: F = 761.32, ηp2 = 0.984; interaction effect: F = 480.95, ηp2 = 0.988).

The significant chromatic shift observed in the treated starch is consistent with the effects of gelatinization and starch-C3G complexation reported in the literature under similar processing conditions (Aalim et al., 2021; Hu, Tang, et al., 2024; Zhai et al., 2022; Zhai et al., 2024; Zhou, Li, et al., 2024). The observed trends in pigmentation are likely explained by reduced water and proton mobility around the chromophore when these are bound within starch granules (Yang et al., 2021), contributing to dampening the red-blue vibrancy.

Particle size distribution analysis

PSD had a remarkable impact on the functional properties of the starch, such as solubility, swelling, digestibility, and molecular interactions (Hamadou et al., 2023). Intensity-based PSD curves (Fig. 2A-C) show scattered-light intensity as a function of particle diameter, and the mean diameters (surface-weighted D[3,2] and volume-weighted D[4,3]) and span (breadth of distribution) are given in Fig. 2D. The PSD curves of native starches WS and LS showed a broad main peak in the range 2.3–4.8 μm, with D[3,2] values of 3.60–3.64 μm and D[4,3] values of 3.68–3.73 μm, and narrow spans (0.40–0.45), indicating relatively homogeneous populations of particles. In contrast, HS showed smaller and more heterogeneous granules, with a peak size range of 0.6–1.6 μm, D[3,2] of 1.47 μm, D[4,3] of 1.56 μm, and a span of 0.73. The smaller size of HS granules and the broad size distribution suggested an inherent amylose-rich structure and heterogeneity in blocklet architecture.

Gelatinization significantly altered the PSDs of WS and LS, yielding distinct bimodal distributions. LS-B exhibited modes with D[3,2] of 1.50 μm and a span of 2.44, indicating a partial breakdown while maintaining features of the granule architecture (Li et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2018). The WS-B had a similar but more constrained bimodal distribution with a span of 0.71, suggesting fragmentation at the blocklet junctions (Wei et al., 2018). However, HS-B increased in size (D[3,2] = 3.42 μm, P < 0.05) and width due to aggregation and formation of amylose-lipid complex (Du, Li, et al., 2022; Li, Obadi, Shi, Xu and Shi, 2021). Such aggregation could block enzymes' access (Liang et al., 2021), which was consistent with the RS increase of HS-B.

In native starch-C3G samples, WS-N and LS-N had mean sizes comparable to their native granules (D[3,2] 3.6–3.7 μm and span 0.42–0.64). Compared with WS, WS-N showed slight peak broadening. In contrast, the HS-N diameter increased significantly (D[3,2] 4.06 μm and span 0.50), indicating that C3G induced granule swelling in amylose-rich matrices. Hydrothermal treatment of starch-C3G complexes showed distinct fragmentation signatures, whereas LST caused severe erosion, yielding finer particles (D[3,2] 0.42 μm and span 0.71), suggesting extensive heat-mediated degradation. WS-T showed moderate degradation, with D[3,2] of 2.83 μm and a span of 0.55, and retained its native distribution. HS-T showed a bimodal distribution (D[3,2] 1.92 μm, span 5.68), indicating a heterogeneous composition of fine debris and intact fragments, consistent with the compact amylose-rich structure that limits swelling (Y. Li et al., 2021). Two-way ANOVA confirmed hydrothermal treatment as the dominant fragmentation driver, exerting highly significant main effects on D[3,2] (F = 347.33, ηp2 = 00.967) and D[4,3] (F = 301.47, ηp2 = 00.962) (P < 0.0001). Significant amylose main effects and interactions (P < 0.0001, ηp2 > 0.85) for both diameters dictated the fragmentation extent. Span main effects were marginally non-significant (P > 0.05).

Morphological analysis

The SEM morphological examination (Fig. 3) indicated that the native rice starch granules were polygonal, with smooth surfaces and well-defined edges. Minor pits or microcracks were occasionally observed, but the granules appeared dense and compact. Edge regularity and surface defects differed subtly among WS, LS, and HS, indicating inherent compositional differences. The LS-N granules showed shallow fissures and sparse nodules upon C3G binding, indicating limited surface interactions. On the contrary, HS-N granules displayed remarkable crater-like depressions and spheroidal surface nodules gathered around the hilum, morphologically consistent with the formation of a non-inclusion complex within the amorphous amylose-rich granule core (Shen et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2018). WS-N granules showed moderate edge exfoliation and sparse localized adsorption islands, which could be attributed to partial disruption of the amorphous shell (Hu, Zhu, et al., 2024). These observations showed that more severe morphological changes occurred at higher amylose content during C3G binding, consistent with stronger hydrogen bonding between C3G and linear starch chains (Shen et al., 2025).

The microstructural characteristics of starch systems examined using scanning electron microscopy of low-amylose rice starch (LS), waxy rice starch (WS), and high-amylose rice starch (HS) in raw, native (N), hydrothermally treated (T), and blank (B) samples.

Hydrothermal treatment dramatically alters starch structure, increasing surface area and complexity. C3G had an additional effect that was dependent on amylose. In contrast to the homogeneous surface of WS-B, lamellar layering and localized agglomerates typical of embedded C3G were observed on GS-T. LS-T granules developed fine wrinkles and protuberances, whereas the gelatinized counterpart (LS-B) was uniformly swollen and smooth. In addition, HS-T exhibited extensive cracking and fragmentation, whereas HS-B was characterized by a continuous porous gel structure. The strong disruption in HS-T under heat was probably due to its compact architecture, rich in crystalline regions, which enabled deep incorporation of C3G and resisted uniform degradation. In terms of functionality, the HS microstructural changes were expected to limit enzyme accessibility, thereby reducing in vitro digestibility (Chen et al., 2020; Liang et al., 2021), while enhancing the stability of C3G.

FTIR and short-range ordered structure

Characteristic changes in the FTIR spectra of the samples are presented in Fig. 4A-C. Native starches exhibited a broad and relatively weak O—H stretching band in the 3200–3600 cm−1 region, centered on approximately 3300 cm−1, denoting strong intramolecular hydrogen bonding and limited water absorption (Aalim et al., 2024). This band was augmented and showed a redshift in C3G-treated and gelatinized samples, consistent with the enhanced hydrogen bonds between C3G hydroxyl groups and starch hydroxyls. In the 2800–3000 cm−1C-H stretching region (Aalim et al., 2024), native samples showed a sharp, asymmetric -CH₂ stretch at approximately 2930 cm−1. C3G-treated starches exhibited slight band broadening, especially in WS-N and LS-N, indicating loosening of the backbone and increased chain mobility. Gelatinization of the starch-C3G complex sharpened these peaks and increased their relative intensity, suggesting that the local hydrogen bonding around the glucan chains was reduced.

FTIR spectra of waxy rice starch (A), low-amylose rice starch (B), high-amylose rice starch (C), and FTIR band-intensity ratios (D) of low-amylose rice starch (LS), waxy rice starch (WS), and high-amylose rice starch (HS) in raw, native (N), hydrothermally treated (T), and blank (B) samples. Values are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (n = 3). Mean values with different superscripts in the same color are statistically different (P < 0.05).

The H–O–H bending region (1700–1600 cm−1) showed a subtle shoulder near 1640 cm−1 in native starches, which was enhanced after C3G binding and further boosted after hydrothermal treatment, suggesting the embedding of aromatic C3G moieties into amorphous regions (Zhai et al., 2017). In the fingerprint region (900–1200 cm−1), native starches showed distinct, well-defined C—O and C–O–C stretching bands near 1000 cm−1, characteristic of their highly ordered crystalline structures (Aalim et al., 2024). Changes in peak positions and intensities due to C3G interactions indicate modifications to the starch backbone and the embedding of C3G in the matrix. Gelatinized samples exhibited significant peak broadening, consistent with the loss of crystallinity (Al-Ansi et al., 2021), and the incorporation of C3G into amylose helices via hydrogen bonding (Zhang, Hassane Hamadou, Chen and Xu, 2023).

Quantitative ratio analysis (Fig. 4D) showed that native WS and LS had a high crystallinity index (R1047/1022) of approximately 1.13 and a helical index (R1022/995) of approximately 1.00, suggesting the presence of an intact short-range order and residual double helices (Zhou, Zhou, et al., 2024). Their C3G-treated counterparts lost approximately 35% of the crystalline index and 8% of the helical index, indicating partial granule disruption (Zhou, Li, et al., 2024). Subsequent hydrothermal treatments uniformly reduced R1047/1022 to about 0.64 and R1022/995 to about 0.87, consistent with extensive gelatinization. In HS samples, R1047/1022 remained low (0.68–0.63), and R1022/995 remained near 0.93 across C3G binding and hydrothermal treatment, suggesting that helical fragments were more abundant in HS than in LS or WS under similar conditions. Two-way ANOVA revealed that hydrothermal treatment was the primary disruptor of short-range order, exerting massive main effects on both the crystallinity (R 1047/1022; F = 123.45, ηp2 = 0.911) and helical (R 1022/995: F = 44.90, ηp2 = 0.789) indices (P < 0.0001). Significant amylose effect for crystallinity index (R1047/1022: F = 22.78, ηp2 = 0.792), and interaction effect (R1047/1022: F = 46.743, ηp2 = 0.886) (P < 0.0001). Helical index showed no significant influence by amylose or interaction effect (R 1022/995: P > 0.05), indicating that residual double helices disruption was mainly influenced by thermal treatment.

XRD and crystalline structure

The XRD patterns, crystallinity index (CI), and full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the characteristic 19.9° 2θ peak for the different starch samples are presented in Fig. 5. XRD analysis revealed that native starches exhibited a typical A-type diffraction pattern, with strong reflections at 15° and 23° 2θ and a doublet at 17° and 18° 2θ. CI values were 79% for HS, 75% for WS, and 64.7% for LS. A modest V-type structural peak at 19.9° 2θ was observed, indicative of an endogenous amylose-lipid helix (Aalim et al., 2024). Upon C3G binding, CI increased by 5% in WS-N and by 8.8% in LS-N, suggesting that C3G acted as a nucleating agent that enhanced short-range order in amylopectin-rich matrices. In contrast, C3G binding to HS-N reduced CI by 8.5%, suggesting disruption of pre-existing amylose helices during non-inclusion complex formation. The FWHM of the V-type peak in the native starch complexes was similar to that of native starch.

X-ray Diffraction pattern of waxy rice starch (A), low-amylose rice starch (B), high-amylose rice starch (C), and crystallinity index (CI) and full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the characteristic peak at 19.9° 2θ (D) of low-amylose rice starch (LS), waxy rice starch (WS), and high-amylose rice starch (HS) in raw, native (N), hydrothermally treated (T), and blank (B) samples, presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). Mean values with different superscripts in the same color are statistically different (P < 0.05).

Hydrothermal treatment of starch-C3G complexes resulted in a significant decrease in CI (up to 41.9%) and eradicated the A-type diffraction pattern across all three types of starch, indicating a high degree of lamellar crystallinity disruption and complete starch gelatinization. The recrystallization behavior was affected by the composition of the waxy starch matrix, even in the absence of amylose inclusion complexes: gelatinized waxy starches (WS-T and WS-B) had higher CI than gelatinized LS and HS, yet showed no detectable V-type peaks.

One of the multi-scale structural changes induced by hydrothermal treatment was the debranching of amylopectin into linear chains, resulting in a temporary increase in apparent amylose content and CI (Zhang et al., 2024). The obvious V-type peaks in gelatinized LS-B and HS-B were attributed to amylose-lipid interactions (Cai et al., 2019), which were greater than those in the C3G complexes. Moreover, LS-T and HS-T showed broad V-type peaks with FWHM of 2.1° and 11.2° 2θ, respectively, indicating the partial, low-coherence reformation of amylose-C3G complexes. Partial V-type inclusion complexes were formed in samples containing sufficient amylose helices (Farooq et al., 2021; Tian, Liu, et al., 2024), manifesting as labile, low-coherence polymorphic structures rather than well-formed inclusion complexes (Li, Chen, Bui, Xu and Dhital, 2021; Raza et al., 2021; Tian, Liu, et al., 2024). Two-way ANOVA showed hydrothermal treatment significantly reduced the crystallinity index (CI; F = 282.93, ηp2 = 0.959, P < 0.001) and amylose (CI; F = 13.19, ηp2 = 0.687, P < 0.001) without a significant interaction (P = 0.203), indicating that hydrothermal treatment uniformly eradicates the native A-type crystalline lamellar order across all starch types. For the FWHM of the V-type peak, amylose (F = 4405.95, ηp2 = 0.99864) and hydrothermal treatment (F = 23,563.66, ηp2 = 0.99949) showed significant main effects (P < 0.0001), alongside a significant interaction effect (F = 4377.45, ηp2 = 0.99863, P < 0.0001).

Principal component analysis

The multivariate patterns were examined, and the dimensionality of the data was reduced by performing principal component analysis (PCA) on the physicochemical and functional parameters (Fig. 6A). PCA was performed on 17 quantitative descriptors measured in the native and treated starch samples. The descriptors included binding ratio, colorimetric parameters (L*, a*, b*, and ΔE), particle size (D [3,2], D [4,3], Span), crystallinity indices (CI, R1047/1022, R1022/995, FWHM), and digestibility and bioaccessibility indices (RDS, SDS, RS, bioaccessibility, DCI). The first two principal components (PC1 and PC2) accounted for 71.51% of the total variance (PC1 = 44.51%, PC2 = 27.00%), indicating that these two axes summarized most of the multivariate structure in this dataset.

PC scores and loadings plot (A), Pearson correlation coefficients (B), and the proposed molecular mechanisms regulating rice starch and C3G interactions (C) of low-amylose rice starch (LS), waxy rice starch (WS), and high-amylose rice starch (HS) in raw, native (N), hydrothermally treated (T), and blank (B) samples.

In the PCA scores and loadings plot, the samples were primarily discriminated by processing treatment rather than by starch amylose content. Native starches (LS and WS) were tightly clustered on the high negative side of PC1 and the high positive side of PC2, reflecting their similar functional properties. They had a strong influence on the infrared ratios R1047/1022 and R1022/995 and a moderate impact on L*. Gelatinized starch samples (LS-B and WS-B) were clustered in the negative PC1 and PC2, which had a close influence on the RDS and SDS. In contrast, HS and HS-B were grouped closely at low positive and low negative PC2 values, respectively, and negative PC1 was closely associated with color variable b*.

Hydrothermally treated starch-C3G complexes (LS-T, WS-T, and HS-T) were in the positive PC1 and negative PC2 quadrant. Their positive loading on PC1 was related to the strong positive loading on PC1 of ΔE, binding ratio, bioaccessibility, DCI, and color variable a*. In contrast, Span and FWHM, indicators of heat-induced structural breakdown, displayed low loading on PC1, high loading on PC2, and were closely influenced by LS-T and WS-T, which had a weaker effect. The native starch complexes (LS-N, WS-N, and HS-N) were closely clustered at high PC1 and PC2 scores. These samples showed a moderate impact on RS and a weaker influence on particle mean diameters and crystallinity, which had a significant positive loading on PC2.

The clustering pattern revealed that treatment type, especially hydrothermal processing, had a more pronounced effect than amylose content on C3G binding and bioaccessibility. PC1 captured variations associated with treatment, digestibility, bioaccessibility indices, and color shift, whereas PC2 reflected particle-scale alterations, structural degradation, and loss of crystallinity. These components together provided a framework for the optimization of processing conditions to balance starch functionality and C3G bioaccessibility.

Pearson correlation analysis

Pearson's correlation analysis was performed on 17 variables that described the starch–C3G complexes (Fig. 6B), and provides a quantitative link between structural degradation and functional outcomes. The C3G binding rate, bioaccessibility, and DCI were almost perfectly correlated (r ≥ +0.99), reflecting that these variables respond coherently to processing treatment. These also strongly aligned with ΔE (r ≥ +0.89), moderately with color redness (r ≥ +0.51), and negatively with lightness and yellowness (r ≤ −0.73), suggesting that the complexation processes driving anthocyanin binding led to pronounced color shifts. Furthermore, ΔE was inversely correlated with L* (r = −0.95) and moderately negatively correlated with FTIR crystallinity and helicity markers (r = −0.60), linking greater pigment alteration to the loss of double-helix order. XRD peak broadening (FWHM) was also correlated with ΔE (r = +0.82), indicating that greater granule heterogeneity and loss of crystalline order coincided with larger color shifts.

RDS and SDS were highly correlated (r = +0.95), and both were negatively correlated with RS (r ≤ −0.98), indicating a shift from digestible to resistant starch across the treatments. Higher RDS was associated with lower C3G bioaccessibility (r = −0.63), whereas RS showed a weak positive correlation with bioaccessibility (r = +0.59), suggesting that more resistant matrices may better release anthocyanins. The mean diameters D[3,2] and D[4,3] were perfectly correlated (r = +1.00). Larger size distributions (span) were associated with greater FWHM broadening (r = +0.87). The particle size D[4,3] showed a weak correlation with RS (r = +0.58). These clusters reflected the PCA groupings and highlighted the trade-offs of disrupting starch order, which increased C3G bioaccessibility but also altered starch digestibility and color.

Proposed molecular mechanisms of interactions of rice starch with C3G

Fig. 6C depicts the potential interaction mechanisms between rice starch and C3G inferred by the evidence presented in this work. The present data suggest that the binding mode of native rice starch was determined by the amylose content. Its worth nothing that the three-starch matrices inherently possess different genetic backgrounds, granule morphologies, and residual lipids or proteins which will directly impact C3G binding the function. Waxy starch (WS-N) exhibits the lowest binding ratio and the highest bioaccessibility. The absence of the XRD V-type structural peak, the negligible FTIR O—H band shift, and the SEM appearance of sparse, localized C3G islands collectively suggest that C3G retention in WS-N is attributable primarily to weak, electrostatic surface adsorption at the amorphous exterior of amylopectin granules. This mode of binding is easily disrupted by aqueous digestion media, accounting for the high bioaccessibility.

Conversely, surface disruption, particle diameter shifts, and FTIR O—H band shifts increased with amylose content for LS-N and HS-N, as expected from C3G interaction with amorphous amylose regions by non-inclusion hydrogen bonding. The interaction does not produce V-type ordered helical inclusion complexes. The Aₘax shift in the iodine spectra of HS-N is a conformation-perturbation signal that binds C3G more firmly in the granule matrix, reducing bioaccessibility compared to WS-N. The interaction force between C3G and amylose was reported to be higher than that with amylopectin (Shen et al., 2025), which is consistent with the current binding and bioaccessibility trends.

Gelatinization fundamentally changes the binding mechanism of starch-C3G. Upon melting of A-type crystalline lamellae, linear amylose chains were released and partially reannealed, generating structural features consistent with V-type-like helical organization around C3G molecules. This structural rearrangement significantly increased the binding efficiency towards all starch types and reduced C3G bioaccessibility. The V-type XRD patterns at 19.9° 2θ in LS-T and HS-T, the broad changes in the FTIR fingerprint region, and the enhanced retention of C3G all consistently support this interpretation. The large FWHM of V-type peaks (2.1° for LS-T, 11.2° for HS-T) is indicative of labile polymorphs with low crystalline coherence, rather than the fully formed complexes with high crystallinity. This difference is functionally relevant, since labile V-type complexes are expected to release C3G more slowly during digestion. In contrast, the XRD data showed that WS-T had an amorphous structure and was mainly based on amylopectin rearrangements during gelatinization for C3G binding. This process greatly affected the chromatic response, particularly on the green and yellow axes.

Conclusion

The results suggest that starch structure amylose content and hydrothermal treatment are significant and partially interactive determinants for C3G binding and bioaccessibility, as well as functional and structural modifications in the rice starch system. The lack of soluble amylose in native starches limits C3G binding to weak surface adsorption onto the amorphous exterior of starch granules, achieving a maximal in vitro bioaccessibility in WS-N. Native amylose-containing starches bind C3G via non-inclusion complexation, resulting in amylose-dependent structural disruption and a moderate reduction in bioaccessibility. Importantly, gelatinization was associated with the formation of partial V-type inclusion complexes in amylose-containing starches, as indicated by XRD and FTIR, consistent with reduced C3G release during digestion. In contrast, C3G-treated waxy starch shows rearrangement of amylopectin, improving C3G binding but without formation of V-type complexes, and thus has a distinctive green-yellow chromatic shift and only a moderate reduction in bioaccessibility. Two-way ANOVA confirmed that the effect of amylose content on C3G fate was more regulatory than hydrothermal treatment. Processing type accounted for the majority of the variance in digestibility, colorimetric properties, and bioaccessibility, as further validated by principal component and correlation analyses. While these results are from a simplified starch-C3G model system using a single, sub-saturating C3G concentration and simulated digestion, validation in real food matrices and in vivo experiments are essential before translating these findings into practical functional food applications.

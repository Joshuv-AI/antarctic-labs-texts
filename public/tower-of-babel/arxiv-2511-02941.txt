Automorphisms with growing generators
Stefan Teufel, Marius Wesle, Tom Wessel

Abstract
We prove global existence and uniqueness of Heisenberg dynamics on the quasi-local algebra of an extended quantum lattice system for spatially growing generators. Existing results assume that the local terms of the generator decay fast enough and are bounded uniformly in space and time. We show, in analogy to global existence results for first order ODEs, that global existence and uniqueness still hold true if the local terms grow at most linearly in space. Moreover, we obtain Lieb-Robinson bounds with exponential light cones for the generated dynamics. For the proof, we mainly assume Lieb-Robinson bounds with linear light cones for dynamics generated by uniformly bounded local terms. These are known to hold for example if the local terms are exponentially localized.

Introduction

In this work we consider interactions
\sum_{x\in\varGamma}\varPhi_{x}
defined on the CAR algebra
\mathcal{A}
of lattice fermions on some discrete metric space
(\varGamma,d)
with
D
-dimensional volume growth (think of
\mathbb{Z}^{D}
as the standard example) or on the quasi-local algebra of a spin system on
\varGamma
for which the local terms
\varPhi_{x}\in\mathcal{A}
are not bounded uniformly, but instead satisfy a linear growth bound of the form

\lVert\varPhi_{x}\rVert_{G,x}\leq C_{\varPhi}\,\big\lparen 1+d\lparen x,x_{0}\rparen\big\rparen\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\varGamma.

(1)

Here
G
is a fixed decay function,
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{G,x}
a weighted norm centred at
x
that quantifies the decay around
x
, and
x_{0}
a fixed point in
\varGamma
.
We prove that if
G
decays fast enough such that interactions
\sum_{x\in\varGamma}\varPsi_{x}
with uniformly bounded
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{G,x}
-norms, i.e. with

\tnorm{\varPsi}_{G}:=\sup_{x}\,\lVert\varPsi_{x}\rVert_{G,x}<\infty,

satisfy a Lieb-Robinson bound with linear light cone and Lieb-Robinson velocity proportional to
\tnorm{\varPsi}_{G}
, then also
\varPhi

generates a unique Heisenberg dynamics which has exponential light cones.

Before going into details, let us briefly sketch the underlying heuristic picture.
For uniformly bounded interactions with
\tnorm{\varPsi}_{G}<\infty
, Lieb-Robinson bounds control the speed at which the Heisenberg dynamics generated by such an interaction effectively spreads the support of observables uniformly in space.
If, for example, the local terms have uniform finite range or decay exponentially, then the support of any local observable can spread at most with speed
v_{\mathrm{LR}}\sim c_{\mathrm{LR}}\,\tnorm{\varPsi}_{G}
, the so called Lieb-Robinson velocity.
Such Lieb-Robinson bounds first proved in finite volume can then be used to prove existence of the dynamics in infinite volume, see for example
[
9
]
and references therein.
The situation is vaguely analogous to global existence of solutions to first order ODEs on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
.
If the velocity field
v\colon\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{D}
is Lipschitz continuous and bounded, unique local solutions extend to unique global solutions, as integral curves can only travel finite distances in finite time.
However, for first order ODEs the Lipschitz condition is sufficient to guarantee global existence, boundedness of the velocity field
v
is not needed.
Even when the velocity field grows linearly in space, integral curves can not reach infinity in finite time, instead the distance to the starting point can grow at most exponentially in time.
Our results establish a similar behaviour for the Heisenberg dynamics generated by interactions that satisfy (
1
) and may additionally be also time-dependent:
We prove global existence and uniqueness of dynamics and exponential light cones for such interactions, see Theorem
8
.

While we consider our results interesting in their own right because they considerably extend the class of interactions known to generate global dynamics on
\mathcal{A}
, let us briefly mention the application that motivated our study of this question: Consider the Hamiltonian
H^{B}=\sum_{x}\varPhi^{B}_{x}
of a fermion system subject to a constant magnetic field
B
.
While
H^{B}
is typically a bounded interaction, the derivative
\partial_{B}H^{B}
of
H^{B}
with respect to
B
is an interaction with linearly growing local terms.
And
\partial_{B}H^{B}
, or more precisely its image
\mathcal{I}(\partial_{B}H^{B})
under the quasi-local inverse
\mathcal{I}
of the Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}_{H^{B}}
, is expected to generate the spectral flow for gapped ground states of
H^{B}
.
So our result is the basis for showing that the spectral flow exists as a cocycle of locally generated automorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
for gapped phases of matter with varying magnetic fields.
We refer to
[
10
]
for a short discussion of this problem and to
[
7
,
2
]
for the spectral flow of gapped ground states in infinite volume.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
presents the general setup, and Section
3
states the precise assumptions and results.
The proofs are given in Section
4
.

Definition 3
.

We call a bounded function

Definition 4
.

Let

We say an observable

\lVert A\rVert_{F,x}\coloneq\lVert A\rVert+\sup_{r\geq 0}\frac{\lVert(1-\mathbb{E}_{B_{r}(x)})A\rVert}{F(r)}<\infty.

.

and

.
Also note that if

.

Definition 5
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval.
A
time-dependent zero-chain on
I
is a map

\varPhi\colon I\times\varGamma\to\mathcal{A}^{+},\ (t,x)\mapsto\varPhi_{x}(t),

such that for all
(t,x)\in I\times\varGamma
the operator
\varPhi_{x}(t)
is self-adjoint, for each
x\in\varGamma
, the map
I\to\mathcal{A}^{+},\ t\mapsto\varPhi_{x}(t)
is norm-continuous and for each

and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}
the sum

\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi(t)}\,A\coloneq\sum_{x\in\varGamma}\,[\varPhi_{x}(t),A]

converges unconditionally.

Let
F
be a decay function.
A time-dependent zero-chain
\varPhi
on an interval
I
is

F
-local
if

\tnorm{\varPhi}_{F}\coloneq\sup_{t\in I}\,\sup_{x\in\varGamma}\,\lVert\varPhi_{x}(t)\rVert_{F,x}<\infty.

We denote the space of all uniformly
F
-local time-dependent zero-chains on
I
with
\mathcal{Z}_{F,I}
.

In the analysis of quantum lattice systems, it is more common to specify the generators by so-called interactions, which associate a strictly local operator to each finite set
M\subseteq\varGamma
.
While there is no canonical identification of the set of interactions with the set of zero-chains, there are several natural maps that preserve the associated derivation and decay-properties.
For example for each

one can sum all terms of an interaction that are centred around

See, for example,
[

5
]

Also note that the sets of derivations on

*

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to
\mathcal{A}
that commute with the parity automorphism

.
This can be seen as follows: Each such derivation is given by an interaction as is shown in
[
1
]
.
Each interaction has an associated zero-chain with the same derivation, e.g.
[
2
]
.
And from the definition above it is easy to see that every interaction coming from a zero-chain again satisfies the properties mentioned above.
In this work, we use zero-chains, because they allow for a very clear characterization of linearly growing generators in
1

Finally, let us define what it means for a zero-chain to generate a cocycle of automorphisms.

Let

Results

From now on we fix a time-dependent zero-chain
\varPhi
on an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and decay functions

,

\nu_{F}>2D+2

We then assume that the terms of

Assumption 1
.

There is an
x_{0}\in\varGamma
and a constant
C_{\varPhi}>0
, such that

\sup_{t\in I}\,\lVert\varPhi_{x}(t)\rVert_{G,x}\leq C_{\varPhi}\,\big\lparen 1+d\lparen x,x_{0}\rparen\big\rparen\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\varGamma.

Moreover, we assume a Lieb-Robinson bound with a linear light cone for all
bounded
zero-chains, which have the same decay as
\varPhi
.

Assumption 2
.

There exist constants
C_{\mathrm{LR}}
,
c_{\mathrm{LR}}>0
, such that for all time-dependent zero-chains
\varPsi\in\mathcal{Z}_{G,I}
with associated cocycle of automorphisms
(\alpha_{s,t})_{s,t\in I}
and all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}^{+}
, and
s
,
t\in I

\big\lVert\big[\alpha_{s,t}\,A,\,B\big]\big\rVert\leq C_{\mathrm{LR}}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\lvert X\rvert\,F\big\lparen\big[d\lparen X,Y\rparen-c_{\mathrm{LR}}\,\tnorm{\varPsi}_{G}\,\lvert t-s\rvert\big]_{+}\big\rparen,

where
[x]_{+}=x
if

[x]_{+}=0
if

.

This assumption is in particular satisfied for exponential localization, with the decay functions
G(r)={\mathrm{e}}^{-br}
and

To see this, one constructs an associated interaction, which is exponentially decaying in the sense of
[

]
with the function

The result then follows by

Moreover, we expect it to be satisfied for polynomial localization as well.
Indeed, for spin systems with time-independent interactions and polynomial decay, linear light cones for large times have been shown in

We will use this assumption for approximations of
\varPhi
on finite subsets of

For this purpose, for each
k\in[0,\infty\rparen
we define the time-dependent zero-chain
\varPhi^{k}
by

\varPhi^{k}_{x}(t)\coloneq\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{B_{k/2}(x)}\,\varPhi_{x}(t)&for $x\in B_{k/2}(x_{0})$, and\\

It is defined such that

.

We then obtain existence and uniqueness of the infinite volume dynamics for short times with an additional explicit Lieb-Robinson type estimate.

.

We set

.
For all

,

Setting
\mu\coloneq\min(\nu_{F}-(2D+2),\nu_{G}-(D+2))

\nu\in\lparen 0,\mu\rparen

\gamma_{\nu}>0

\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A\rVert_{\nu,x_{0}}\leq\gamma_{\nu}\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu,x_{0}}.

In particular, it holds that

The constant

.

While we excluded

The idea of the proof is the following.
For the part of

The short-time result can then be extended by concatenation to existence and uniqueness for all times and a Lieb-Robinson type estimate with an exponential light cone.

Theorem 8
.

The time-dependent zero-chain
\varPhi
generates a unique cocycle of automorphisms

The cocycle
(\alpha^{k}_{s,t})_{s,t\in I}

Setting
\mu\coloneq\min(\nu_{F}-(2D+2),\nu_{G}-(D+2))

\nu\in\lparen 0,\mu\rparen
, there are

\gamma_{\nu}>0

\varPhi

\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A\rVert_{\nu,x_{0}}\leq C_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\gamma_{\nu}\,C_{\varPhi}\,\lvert t-s\rvert}\,\lVert A\rVert_{\nu,x_{0}}.

(4)

In particular, it holds that
\alpha_{s,t}\,A\in\mathcal{A}_{\nu}
.

As for the short-time result, we immediately obtain
\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A\rVert\leq\lVert A\rVert
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
s
,
t\in I
.

Moreover, the bound (

For all

\displaystyle\lVert[\alpha_{s,t}\,A,B]\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\lVert(1-\mathbb{E}_{B_{k+cr}(x_{0})})\,\alpha_{s,t}\,A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert+\lVert[\mathbb{E}_{B_{k+cr}(x_{0})}\,\alpha_{s,t}\,A,B]\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 2\,(1+k+cr)^{-\nu}\,\lVert\alpha_{s,t}\,A\rVert_{\nu,x_{0}}\,\lVert B\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 2\,(1+k+cr)^{-\nu}\,(1+k)^{\nu}\,C_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\gamma_{\nu}\,C_{\varPhi}\,\lvert t-s\rvert}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,C_{\nu}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\gamma_{\nu}\,C_{\varPhi}\,\lvert t-s\rvert-\nu\ln(1+cr/(1+k))}

This bound is referred to as a Lieb-Robinson bound with exponential light cone, since the right-hand side is small whenever

r\gg(1+k)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{C_{\varPhi}\,\gamma_{\nu}\,\nu^{-1}\,\lvert t-s\rvert}.

S_{k}\coloneq B_{k}(x_{0})\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}B_{k-1}(x_{0})
and
S_{0}=B_{0}(x_{0})
.
Due to the volume growth property of
(\varGamma,d)
is holds that
\lvert S_{k}\rvert\leq C_{\mathrm{vol}}\,(1+k)^{D}
.
From this we conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\varGamma}\frac{1}{\big\lparen 1+d\lparen x,x_{0}\rparen\big\rparen^{D+1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{x\in S_{k}}\frac{1}{k^{D+1+\varepsilon}}+S_{0}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{\mathrm{vol}}\,(1+k)^{D}}{k^{D+1+\varepsilon}}+C_{\mathrm{vol}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{\mathrm{vol}}\,2^{D}\,}{k^{1+\varepsilon}}+C_{\mathrm{vol}}<\infty.\qed

Lemma 10
.

Let
0\leq\nu<\nu_{F}
.
It holds that

\sup_{k\geq 0}\,\sup_{m\geq k/2}\,(1+m)^{\nu}\,F\bigg\lparen\bigg[\frac{3m}{4}-\frac{k+1}{4}\bigg]_{+}\bigg\rparen<\infty.

Proof.

Since the expression is bounded away from
\infty
it is sufficient to consider the supremum for
k\geq 2
and bound

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\sup_{k\geq 2}\,\sup_{m\geq k/2}\,(1+m)^{\nu}\,F\bigg\lparen\frac{3m}{4}-\frac{k+1}{4}\bigg\rparen

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\geq 2}\,\sup_{m\geq(k/6-1/3)}\,\bigg\lparen 1+m+\frac{k+1}{3}\bigg\rparen^{\nu}\,F\bigg\lparen\frac{3m}{4}\bigg\rparen

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\geq 0}\,\sup_{m\geq k/6}\,\bigg\lparen 1+m+\frac{k+3}{3}\bigg\rparen^{\nu}\,F\bigg\lparen\frac{3m}{4}\bigg\rparen

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{k\geq 0}\,\sup_{m\geq k/6}\,(2+3m)^{\nu}\,F\bigg\lparen\frac{3m}{4}\bigg\rparen

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\nu}\,\sup_{m\geq 0}\,\bigg\lparen 1+\frac{3m}{4}\bigg\rparen^{\nu}\,F\bigg\lparen\frac{3m}{4}\bigg\rparen

\displaystyle<\infty,

where we substituted
m\to m+(k+1)/3
and
k\to k+2
in the second and third step, respectively.
∎

Lemma 11
.

For all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}
it holds that

\sup_{t\in I}\,\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi(t)}\,A-\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{k}(t)}\,A\rVert\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad k\to\infty.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We have

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\sup_{t\in I}\,\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi(t)}\,A-\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{k}(t)}\,A\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in I}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in B_{k/2}(x_{0})\end{subarray}}\,\big\lVert\big[(1-\mathbb{E}_{B_{k}(x)})\,\varPhi_{x}(t),A\big]\big\rVert+\sup_{t\in I}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\varGamma\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}B_{k/2}(x_{0})\end{subarray}}\,\big\lVert\big[\varPhi_{x}(t),A\big]\big\rVert.

The first term is bounded by

\sum_{x\in B_{k/2}(x_{0})}\sup_{t\in I}\,2\,\lVert\varPhi_{x}(t)\rVert_{G,x}\,G(k)\,\lVert A\rVert\leq 2\,C_{\mathrm{vol}}\,\big\lparen 1+\tfrac{k}{2}\big\rparen^{D}\,C_{\varPhi}\,\big\lparen 1+\tfrac{k}{2}\big\rparen\,G(k)\,\lVert A\rVert,

and since
D+1<\nu_{G}
this bound vanishes as
k\to\infty
.
For the second term we assume that
k
is large enough so that
A
is supported in
B_{k/8}(x_{0})
.
This allows us to insert a conditional expectation and then bound the second term by

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\sup_{t\in I}\,\sum_{x\in\varGamma\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}B_{k/2}(x_{0})}\big\lVert\big[(1-\mathbb{E}_{B_{d\lparen x,x_{0}\rparen/4}(x)})\,\varPhi_{x}(t),A\big]\big\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\varGamma\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}B_{k/2}(x_{0})}\sup_{t\in I}\,2\,\lVert\varPhi_{x}(t)\rVert_{G,x}\,G\big\lparen d\lparen x,x_{0}\rparen/4\big\rparen\,\lVert A\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\varGamma\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}B_{k/2}(x_{0})\end{subarray}}\,C_{\varPhi}\,\big\lparen 1+d\lparen x,x_{0}\rparen\big\rparen\,G\big\lparen d\lparen x,x_{0}\rparen/4\big\rparen\,\lVert A\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\,C_{\varPhi}}{(1+k/2)^{\varepsilon}}\,\sup_{m\geq k/2}\,(1+m)^{D+2+2\varepsilon}\,G(m/4)\,\sum_{x\in\varGamma}\frac{1}{\big\lparen 1+d\lparen x,x_{0}\rparen\big\rparen^{D+1+\varepsilon}}\,\lVert A\rVert,

for an
\varepsilon>0
, such that
D+2+2\varepsilon<\nu_{G}
.
The final sum converges as shown in Lemma
9
, the supremum is bounded and hence the expression converges to
0
as
k\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 12
.

For all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}
the map
I\to\mathcal{A},\,t\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi(t)}\,A
is continuous.

Proof.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, we know that the map
I\to\mathcal{A},\,t\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{\varPhi^{k}(t)}\,A
is continuous, each of the finitely many quasi-local terms is continuous.
Together with the uniform convergence of Lemma
11
, this implies the claim.
∎

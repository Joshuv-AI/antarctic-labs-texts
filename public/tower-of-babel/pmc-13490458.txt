Reducing normalized mean square error during channel estimation using minimum pilot symbols in massive MIMO network
Authors: Swapna Tangelapalli, Manu Gupta, Rohit Anand, Nidhi Sindhwani, Simrandeep Singh, A. Johnson Santhosh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Deep Learning technology has emerged as an exciting innovation in the field of applications in wireless communications systems. In this proposed work, fully connected neural network based on deep learning (DL-FCNN) as well as convolutional neural network (CNN) models were employed to determine the channel state information by estimating channel coefficients using a minimal number of pilot symbols. The findings consider the length of the pilot symbol and the cross-cell interference as the main aspects in the estimation of channel coefficients. Results demonstrate that the proposed deep learning method outperforms traditional approaches such as least square (LS) and minimal mean square estimation (MMSE) techniques for measuring the normalized mean square errors (NMSE), which is thought to be a loss-function or channel estimation error. Additionally, the deep learning (DL) method provides better results in the presence of pilot contamination in multi-cell cellular networks and at various levels of cross-cell interference. In conclusion, deep learning models thereof can efficiently be utilized for channel estimation operations as well as physical layer signal processing applications in Massive MIMO Networks.

Introduction

The 5G wireless communications technology is aimed at providing an enormous information service with a high data rate in gigabits per second (Gbps) and at the lowest latency, less than 1 millisecond. It aims to provide services for more than one billion connected devices. The main features that are required for massive multiple input multiple output (MIMO) antennas are spatial diversity, streamlined resource allocation, high spectral efficiency, and a linear multiuser process1. The main benefits of Massive MIMO are energy efficiency, spectral efficiency, high data rates, user tracking, lower power consumption, robustness and low complicated linear processing. Together with interference reduction, the issue of pilot contamination can be a significant issue within the huge MIMO system2. When the data signal is transmitted to the uplink channel the signal is transmitted through a multipath channel. To detect the exact signal on the BS antenna, the information of the channel has to be understood. Thus, the biggest challenge of a massive MIMO system is knowing the channel state information (CSI). The knowledge of the channel coefficients is coupled with the signal received for detection of signals on the base station (BS) receiving antenna. When using frequency division duplex (FDD) mode, channel estimation for downlink is added as an additional burden since the channel that is in the uplink and downlink does not have a reciprocal channel. Time division duplex (TDD) devices are best suited to support the system and also allow the use of hardware that is inexpensive. The amount of pilot symbols needed is related solely to the number of user equipment (UE) in channel estimation, not the amount of BS antennas3. The present 4G LTE technology uses interference reduction strategies, handover strategies4, and methods for frequency reuse5. The interference effects in signal detection could be eliminated by using a range of precoders and decoders. These are part of the zero forcing (ZF) receivers, and the zero forcing with successive interference cancelation (ZF-SIC) receivers. More efficient, the minimal mean square error (MMSE) with successive interfering cancelation (MMSE-SIC) receiver is also described6. The redundant bits effect is less on the signal bandwidth, rate and efficiency.

In the process of channel estimation, unique pilot symbols obeying orthogonality are transmitted from the user equipment (UE) within coherence time, assuming the channel is a time-invariant in the coherence time. Pilot contamination remains the dominant impairment in multicell massive MIMO, as reused pilots across neighboring cells introduce correlated interference. Accurate channel state information (CSI) remains a critical challenge, particularly in multicell deployments where pilot contamination and high-dimensional channel matrices degrade estimation performance.

Because of reuse of pilot symbols in multicell environment, it is possible to interfere with similar pilot symbols in adjacent cells, referred to as pilot contamination7. Precoding and resource allocation are greatly affected by pilot contamination. Traditional LS and MMSE estimators provide baseline solutions but scale poorly with antenna dimensions, and their accuracy deteriorates under severe pilot reuse. This motivates the exploration of data-driven methods that can learn robust mappings even under minimal pilot overhead.”

Deep learning tools learn the relationship between learning variables, which are hard to understand by mathematical models. Therefore, without knowing precise mathematical models, it can work precisely, and therefore deep learning (DL) is an important and promising technology for wireless communications8. Deep learning techniques are the most effective solution for complex situations that require live data. Different DL models help in acquiring the essential parameters using large databases of training. In addition, DL helps implement complex signals that are needed for physical layer’s signal processing as well as medium access control (MAC) layer applications, including detection of channels that modulate and channel decoding9. It also helps with signal estimation as well as detection of channels the power scheduling of the user and many more. Recent research has explored deep learning (DL) approaches for channel estimation, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent architectures. A study of DL algorithms and the architectures needed to estimate channel coefficients, beam forming, and resource allocation within Non Orthogonal Multiple Access (NOMA) and other areas were conducted in-depth for physical layer processing in wireless communications15. The communication system is a representation of block structures that have several modules. They need to develop and optimize each for specific functions like demodulation, signal modulation encryption, decoding, and detection in the traditional manner. A data-driven model with DNN is used for channel estimation in 3 stages: pre-training, training, and testing. Instead of prior known Channel state information (CSI) during the estimating of channel coefficients, the DNN is initially trained with a modified raw data set obtained from traditional algorithms but tested on real-time online data for channel estimation. It proved to be more robust and efficient, particularly in high-velocity user mobility systems. This architecture also suffers considerable complexity due to the many neurons present in each hidden layer18. The CNN model of DL, extracts the channel response feature vectors and the Long Short-term Memory using recurrent neural network (LSTM-RNN) model used to perform channel estimation considering fast time-varying channels in a high-speed scenario. The network is trained via perfect offline channel data, helping it learns the features of high-speed channels. It is computationally low complex than traditional methods19. For the massive MIMO detector, Machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) are used to perform signal detection using model driven approach20. A three-layer CNN subnet model with each layer assuming a different-sized filter is used for channel estimation. The results are compared for an indoor and outdoor scenario which acts as the parameters affecting the channel estimation21. According the survey CNN-based estimators have shown improved robustness by capturing spatial correlations in channel matrices. However, their convolutional layers introduce significant computational overhead, raising concerns about real-time feasibility at base stations. Moreover, most CNN studies assume moderate pilot lengths (P ≥ 8), leaving robustness under extremely small pilot lengths (P = 2) largely unaddressed. In contrast, fully connected neural networks (FCNNs) offer reduced architectural complexity and fewer parameters, but their potential in multicell environments with severe pilot reuse has not been systematically studied.”

From this survey, it is evident that existing DL-based channel estimators either suffer from high complexity (CNNs, LSTMs) or have not been validated under pilot contamination with minimal pilot overhead. Thus, simple architecture and computationally low-cost DL model have to be developed for channel estimation using less pilot overhead compared to traditional methods. The estimation should be accurate for pilot contamination conditions. This motivates the need for lightweight DL architectures that can achieve reliable estimation under minimal pilot overhead while remaining computationally feasible for real-time base station deployment. In this work, we propose a fully connected neural network (DL-FCNN) architecture for channel estimation in multicell massive MIMO systems. We demonstrate accurate estimation for DL-FCNN design, emphasizes simplicity and computational efficiency while maintaining accuracy under pilot contamination and minimal pilot lengths (P = 2). We demonstrate that the proposed model achieves lower normalized mean square error (NMSE) compared to LS, MMSE, and CNN baselines, thereby offering a scalable solution for next-generation wireless networks.

By focusing on the trade-off between pilot overhead and estimation accuracy, this study highlights the potential of lightweight DL models to support scalable physical-layer signal processing in next-generation networks. We specifically investigate performance under pilot contamination in multicell scenario and cross-cell interference.

Channel estimation mechanism

Channel estimation is a process of estimating the channel coefficients w between transmitting and receiving antennas. These coefficients are complex numbers. The wireless channel is variable and difficult to model in various scenarios, such as high user mobility environments, dense areas, rural areas, etc. To predict the channel coefficient values, predefined pilot symbols of fixed length are transmitted from the transmitting antennas. These pilot symbols are unique and orthogonal, and are used at the receiver’s side to estimate the channel coefficients. The traditional methods generally used for estimating channel state information are Least Square (LS) and Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE) methods.

Because of the restriction on coherence time dimensions, the allotment of orthogonal pilot sequences for all of the UEs in different cells is impossible. Therefore, pilot symbols must be of larger length to maintain orthogonality and need to be reused. However, pilot symbol reuse leads to called pilot contamination resulting in degrade performance of estimating the channel coefficients. It degrades the system performance by increasing channel estimation errors.

Data generation

The dataset for training and testing the DL-FCNN was generated using standardized wireless channel models to ensure realistic propagation conditions. Rayleigh fading was adopted for small-scale multipath effects.

User equipment (UE) positions were randomly distributed between 100 m and 1000 m from the base station. Pilot contamination was introduced by reusing orthogonal pilot sequences across neighboring cells, and interference coefficients were varied to reflect different cross-cell interference levels.

Using the mathematical equations described earlier in the multicell massive MIMO system model, the dataset for training and testing DL-FCNN is created using MATLAB stored as .mat files for different scenarios. The received signals y in the equation act as training data with corresponding channel matrix H as training labels and generate testing data and testing labels similarly. Different datasets are generated considering a different number of BS antennas (M = 4, 8, 16, 32, 64) serving K = 10 UEs for every single cell using Pilot symbol length (P = 2, 8) with SNR in the range (0dB to 20dB) saved as .mat files. Similarly validation datasets are also created.

The dataset was partitioned into 70% training, 20% validation, and 10% testing. Randomization was applied across SNR ranges (0–20 dB), pilot lengths (P = 2,8), and antenna configurations (M = 4,8,16,32) to ensure statistical diversity. Independent random seeds were fixed for each run to guarantee reproducibility. Size of training dataset (TDS) and size of training label (TLS) using Number of data samples (Ds) suitable for DLFCNN are computed as follows:9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathrm{T}\mathrm{D}}_{s}=2{D}_{s}P$$\end{document}10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathrm{T}\mathrm{L}}_{s}=2{D}_{s}M$$\end{document}

Training was performed using the Adam optimizer with parameters β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.999. A learning rate of 0.01 was selected after grid search across [0.001,0.01]. Mini-batch sizes of 1 and 10 were tested, with early stopping applied when validation NMSE failed to improve for 5 consecutive epochs. This methodology ensures that the DL-FCNN was trained on realistic, reproducible datasets, with clear partitioning and optimization strategies that support fair comparison against LS, MMSE, and CNN baselines.

DL-FCNN architecture for channel estimation

Proposed DL-FCNN model as shown in Fig. 4 consists of fully connected feed forward multi-layered neural network architecture. It consists of 3 dense stacks of layers: the input and hidden layers as a fully connected feed-forward structure followed by a dropout layer. The DL-FCNN architecture consists of an input layer of size 2MP, followed by two fully connected hidden layers each with 8192 neurons, ReLU activation, and dropout of 0.02 to mitigate overfitting. The final fully connected layer maps to an output vector of size 2 M, corresponding to the real and imaginary channel coefficients. A custom NMSE regression layer is used as the loss function to directly optimize channel estimation accuracy.

Conclusion

In the context of multicell ultra-dense massive MIMO networks, this study highlights potential of the Deep Learning Fully Connected Neural Network (DL-FCNN) architecture in revolutionizing channel estimation. While massive MIMO promises exceptional data handling capacity along with system’s spectral efficiency, the inherent complexities of time-varying and intricate channel conditions have posed challenges for traditional channel estimation methods. Simulation results illustrate that DL-FCNN not only is capable of accurate channel estimation, but is also resistant to pilot contamination which is an ever-present interference issue when working across multicell scenarios. Notably, DL-FCNN achieves these feats using significantly fewer pilot symbols compared to traditional techniques like Least Square (LS) or Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE) estimation. This model’s ability to maintain superior performance despite using fewer pilot symbols makes it an attractive solution for practical implementation and its implications go well beyond channel estimation. As we look ahead at wireless communication systems, it becomes evident that deep neural network models like DL-FCNN will play an increasingly essential part in various signal processing and Medium access control (MAC) layer operations, including modulation detection, power allocation, signal detection beamforming scheduling.

Future prospects

With ongoing research and innovation, these models are poised to reshape the landscape of wireless technologies, offering unprecedented efficiency, adaptability, and performance across a spectrum of applications in 5G and beyond22. Deep learning models in wireless communication systems hold great promise and should be explored further in the near future. The few areas worth further investigation are interdisciplinary applications, intend to enhance the robustness and security of deep learning models, explore techniques to make deep learning models more energy and resource efficient and designing tailored hardware solutions to accelerate deep learning models on resource-constrained devices and networks, to maximize their viability.

Deep neural network architectures like DL-FCNN usher in an exciting era for wireless communication. Through ongoing research and innovation, these models may soon radically reshape wireless technologies offering unprecedented efficiency, adaptability and performance across a variety of 5G applications as well as beyond23.

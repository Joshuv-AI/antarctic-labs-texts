Fault feature extraction for centrifugal pump impellers via EMD and cyclic bispectral slicing
Authors: Xiaopei Liang, Hanxin Chen, Lei Wang, Shaoyi Li, Zhigang Wang, Zhaohui Shi, Xiangying Sheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe vibration signal of early centrifugal pump impeller faults is a nonlinear, non-Gaussian, non-steady-state signal with inherent periodicity. These characteristics complicate the accurate extraction of fault features. This study aims to explore a novel feature extraction method for centrifugal pump impeller fault vibration signals. This method leverages the adaptive characteristics of empirical mode decomposition (EMD) for multi-scale decomposition of the original vibration signal and separation of each intrinsic mode function (IMF). The cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing technique is introduced to perform high-order statistical purification of the noisy IMF, and the modulation frequency characterizing the fault is accurately isolated through optimized slicing parameters. In the analysis of actual centrifugal pump impeller vibration signals, this method effectively enhances the separability and anti-noise robustness of the modulation component. Furthermore, the extracted features are input into SVM, XGBoost, and 1D-CNN classification models, with test accuracies of 85.7%, 92.1%, and 95.7%, respectively, significantly outperforming the single-feature method.Keywords: Centrifugal pump, Fault diagnosis, Cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing, Empirical mode decompositionSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Centrifugal pumps, widely used fluid-conveying devices in industrial production, are primarily composed of a pump body, pump shaft, impeller, sealing device, and other components. As the core rotating component, the safe and stable operation of the impeller is crucial. During long-term operation, the impeller is exposed to high temperature, high pressure, and high-speed rotation, making it susceptible to wear, cracking, deformation, perforation, and other failure modes. Because centrifugal pumps require high sealing performance, timely fault diagnosis enables accurate maintenance without disrupting normal operation, thereby preventing casualties and secondary failures in healthy components. Moreover, it reduces equipment downtime and enhances production efficiency. Consequently, increasing attention has been directed to impeller fault diagnosis, with emphasis on extracting key fault information to ensure the efficiency and reliability of centrifugal pumps.

In recent years, various effective methods have been applied to early fault diagnosis of centrifugal pumps, providing valuable approaches to enhance system reliability and performance. Hanxin et al.1 proposed the use of parallel factor analysis to diagnose centrifugal pump faults. Wasim et al.2 proposed the use of Sobel edge scale mapping for centrifugal pump diagnosis. Because of differences in feature extraction approaches, the diagnostic performance of the two methods varies considerably. Wathan and Husaini3 investigated the diagnosis of pump shaft fractures; however, few studies have focused on impeller faults, even though the impeller is a key component with a high failure rate and plays a central role in energy conversion. On the one hand, the complex internal flow field of the impeller makes accurate extraction and identification of fault characteristics challenging; on the other hand, since the impeller typically operates with other mechanical components (such as shafts and bearings), the collected information is highly complex, complicating analysis and diagnosis4. Consequently, relatively few studies have addressed impeller fault diagnosis, and progress in this field remains slow.

In impeller fault diagnosis, the vibration signal of centrifugal pumps is typically non-stationary, nonlinear, and non-Gaussian, containing rich time-domain, frequency-domain, and time–frequency-domain information5. For such signals, traditional processing methods—including time-domain analysis (mean value, peak index, etc.), frequency-domain analysis (Fourier transform, power spectrum estimation, etc.), and time–frequency analysis (wavelet transform, short-time Fourier transform, etc.)—yield limited effectiveness. The Fourier transform (FT) is applicable only to stationary processes6. Ladd7. proposed using the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to detect mechanical fault noise, but the results were inconclusive. Empirical mode decomposition (EMD), introduced by Bessam et al.8, is an adaptive signal decomposition technique designed to overcome the limitations of wavelet transform. To provide a novel approach for solving non-stationary signal processing, empirical mode decomposition (EMD) has been effectively applied to centrifugal pump fault diagnosis. However, EMD has inherent limitations, such as modal aliasing and endpoint effects. To address this, a series of studies have focused on improving EMD at the algorithmic level. Lei9 proposed adaptive EEMD to resolve the modal aliasing problem and enhance adaptability. Tang et al. utilized morphological filtering to assist EMD in mitigating modal aliasing10. However, these methods generally introduce higher computational complexity and subjective parameters, thereby compromising the simplicity and adaptability of EMD.

In response to the above limitations of algorithm optimization, subsequent research has increasingly focused on the diagnostic strategy itself, specifically the development of feature extraction and intelligent diagnostic models that do not rely on specific decomposition algorithms. Ahmad et al.11 proposed a fault diagnosis method for centrifugal pumps based on three-stage discriminant feature extraction. Through cross-correlation analysis, a comprehensive feature pool containing mixed time-, frequency-, and time–frequency-domain features was constructed. When combined with a multi-class support vector machine (MSVM) classifier, the detection accuracy of weak early faults and the classification performance for different damage levels were significantly improved, achieving considerable gains compared with traditional methods. Sunal et al.12 applied motor current characteristic analysis (MCSA) to directly detect induction motor faults. Garousi et al.13 employed vibration signal analysis combined with a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) algorithm to achieve real-time diagnosis and severity quantification of impeller defects and cavitation faults in centrifugal pumps.

This demonstrates that, whether focused on optimizing the decomposition algorithm itself or on post-processing for feature extraction, the effective extraction of fault features from complex background noise remains a central challenge. For practical applications, when second-order statistical methods are used to extract fault characteristics from centrifugal pump signals, the classification accuracy is very low. Therefore, to obtain more effective and higher-resolution fault features, it is essential to employ higher-order statistical methods. Higher-order moments in these statistics can effectively suppress Gaussian noise in the signal, thereby isolating the fault signal. The cyclic autocorrelation function in higher-order cyclic statistics can demodulate both the carrier and modulation frequencies, and different demodulated frequencies can be obtained by slicing the main frequency of the cyclic autocorrelation function. Zhou et al.14 employed cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing and obtained relatively good fault features. However, Guo et al.15 found that after the cyclic bispectrum was first diagonally sliced, the second slice—whether peak-frequency or multiple peak-frequency—still contained modulation frequency components, carrier frequency components, and numerous difference, combination, and harmonic frequency components. Thus, the frequency components of the processed spectrum remained overly complex. Yang Xiaoqing et al.16 demonstrated that the carrier frequency components of faulty and non-faulty vibration signals are essentially identical, with the primary distinction being in the modulation frequency component. They clearly stated that the primary distinction between fault and non-fault vibration signals lies in the modulation frequency component, while the carrier frequency remains essentially the same. Consequently, the accurate and efficient extraction of modulation frequency components has become critical to enhancing the fault diagnosis performance of centrifugal pumps17.

Based on this, this paper proposes a novel approach: accepting the modal aliasing of EMD and utilizing the cyclic bispectrum secondary slice to achieve feature extraction that overcomes decomposition defects in the IMF component. Through simulation experiments, the influence of cyclic frequency parameters and slicing methods is systematically investigated, and the optimal parameter combination is identified: anti-angle slicing with the cyclic frequency set to the triple carrier frequency. After applying this parameter configuration to experimental data, the fault features of the centrifugal pump impeller are extracted, and the EMD-derived features are input into the same classification model for testing. The proposed EMD–cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing fusion method offers a novel technical approach for fault diagnosis of rotating machinery and has significant engineering application value in early fault feature extraction and accurate identification of centrifugal pump impellers.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: “Basic theory” section presents the basic theory, introducing the principles of cyclic bispectrum slicing and the EMD method, and providing theoretical support for subsequent research. “Simulation signal analysis and parameter optimization” section details the simulation experiment process and determines the optimal cyclic slicing frequency and method. “Experimental data feature extraction and diagnostic verification” section verifies the feature extraction capability of the proposed method using actual centrifugal pump vibration data, and compares the results with those obtained from original signal processing. “Diagnostic performance verification based on classification model” section employs SVM and XGBoost classification models to systematically evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of different feature extraction methods (cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing, EMD decomposition, and EMD–cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing), thereby verifying the superiority of the fusion method. “Complimentary close” section presents the conclusions and prospects, summarizing the main contributions, highlighting the limitations of the method, and offering suggestions for future research.

However, the direct use of complete two-dimensional bispectrum features is computationally intensive in practical applications, so two-dimensional bispectrum is usually converted into one-dimensional feature functions and feature slices. Among them, diagonal slice (ω1 = ω2) is the most commonly used slice form, which can effectively extract fault feature information, remove high-order noise, improve signal-to-noise ratio, and reflect the high-order correlation of signals20. Empirical mode decomposition EMD decomposition principle

Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) is an adaptive signal decomposition technique designed to decompose complex signals into several Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) and a residual trend term21. The primary function of EMD is to decompose a signal into a set of components with distinct frequencies that are localized in the time domain, thereby enabling multi-scale signal analysis. The necessary conditions for EMD are as follows: (1) for each IMF, the difference between the number of extrema and the number of zero-crossings must not exceed one; and (2) the upper and lower envelopes must be locally symmetric with respect to the time axis22. This method decomposes a non-stationary signal into a series of Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) through an iterative sifting process. These IMFs capture different frequency components of the signal and characterize its vibration behavior across multiple time scales. Based on this property, the IMFs obtained through EMD are employed as input signals for cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing to assess their effectiveness in fault feature extraction. By analyzing the higher-order statistical characteristics of each IMF, this method reveals the hidden nonlinear features and modulation information in the signal with greater depth.

EMD-cyclic bispectrum anti-corner slice combination

The core concept of combining EMD decomposition with the anti-corner slice is that the complex vibration signal is decomposed into several IMFs by EMD, and the IMF component with the highest correlation to the original signal is selected as the input for the anti-corner slice, thus highlighting the modulation frequency and suppressing the carrier interference.

Subsequently, cyclic bispectrum secondary slice analysis is introduced, and the anti-corner slice is compared with the traditional diagonal slice. Since the anti-corner slice intercepts the spectrum along the direction in the frequency plane, which corresponds to the difference frequency relationship between the signal frequency components, it can enhance the modulation frequency information. When the input signal is preprocessed by EMD, the carrier and its frequency-doubled energy are separated in each IMF, and the anti-corner slice can accurately extract the modulation frequency characteristics from the lower-dimensional IMF signal.

In summary, based on the adaptive decomposition ability of EMD and the unique advantages of the cyclic bispectrum anti-corner slice in feature extraction, although envelope demodulation can extract a single modulation frequency, its spectral focusing and robustness are limited under multi-carrier, strong noise, and nonlinear conditions. The introduction of EMD in this paper is not to extract the feature itself but to provide a low-noise, modulated input signal for the cyclic bispectrum, which makes the high-order statistical features more concentrated. Simulation signal analysis and parameter optimization

The vibration signal of a centrifugal pump consists of the pump’s inherent vibration frequency (carrier frequency), the fault characteristic frequency (modulation frequency), and Gaussian white noise from the surrounding environment. To verify the effectiveness of cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing in extracting fault features from centrifugal pump vibration signals, an amplitude modulation (AM) simulation signal, consistent with actual operating conditions, is first employed to model the impeller fault characteristics. After evaluating its discriminative capability through quantitative performance metrics, the influence of slicing methods and cyclic frequency parameters in the cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing process on fault feature extraction is systematically analyzed to identify the optimal parameter combination.

Analysis of the cyclic bispectrum secondary slice (CBSOS) under diagonal slicing with different cyclic frequencies α shows that when α is set to the modulation frequencies (10 Hz, 20 Hz) or to the second harmonic of the carrier frequency (200 Hz), the CBSOS exhibits periodicity, with peaks appearing at integer multiples of the carrier frequency (100 Hz, 200 Hz, 300 Hz). This effect complicates distinguishing the intrinsic differences between the two slicing methods in terms of frequency extraction capability. Experimental data feature extraction and diagnostic verification

Common fault types of centrifugal pumps

A centrifugal pump, widely used as a fluid transport device in industrial production, primarily consists of the pump casing, impeller, inlet and outlet pipelines, and bearing and sealing components. The fluid enters the pump through the inlet, is accelerated by the impeller, and flows through the pump casing toward the outlet, as shown in Fig. 7. Due to the high sealing requirements during operation, direct intervention in the pump structure is avoided during intelligent fault diagnosis. Instead, accelerometers are typically mounted on the external surface of the pump casing to collect vibration signals. Impeller fault-induced vibrations are transmitted through the shaft–bearing–housing system to the exterior of the pump.

Schematic diagram of a single-stage single-suction centrifugal pump.

Because the impeller is located inside the pump body and is difficult to access directly, vibration analysis methods are used for indirect (non-invasive) diagnosis. Given the structural complexity of the centrifugal pump and the strong vibration coupling among its components, single-channel vibration signals are often insufficient for accurate impeller fault detection.

Therefore, in this study, a multi-sensor configuration is employed to collect multi-channel vibration signals, forming a centrifugal pump impeller fault diagnosis system. This system provides comprehensive experimental data to support subsequent cyclic bispectrum-based feature extraction, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of fault diagnosis.

When structural defects such as blade cracking, leading-edge erosion, or perforation occur in the impeller, time-varying stiffness and damping induce amplitude or frequency modulation of the carrier frequency (BPF), resulting in sideband components at . The amplitude variations at the blade passing frequency and its harmonic components serve as effective indicators of impeller fault characteristics23. Previous studies have suggested that periodic fluid–structure interactions and variations in impeller stiffness can introduce modulation effects near the blade passing frequency, which may lead to sideband-like spectral features in the vibration response.

Consequently, various impeller fault types in centrifugal pumps induce different modulation frequencies; thus, extracting the modulation frequency corresponding to each fault is the most efficient way to obtain fault-related feat.Building upon this theoretical principle, the present study applies cyclic bispectrum analysis combined with anti-diagonal slicing to uncover the nonlinear modulation features in impeller vibration signals, thereby facilitating the recognition and interpretation of various fault conditions.To investigate how various impeller defects affect the modulation behavior of vibration signals, the Y-direction vibration signal at the second measurement point was used as the analysis target in the centrifugal pump impeller fault diagnosis experiment. The spectral analysis results in Fig. 9 reveal that different faulty impellers exhibit additional peaks in the low-frequency range (approximately 65 Hz and 125 Hz), indicating the presence of clear modulation effects in the signal. These low-frequency components typically correspond to the impeller’s rotational frequency and its harmonics, which periodically modulate the high-frequency carrier components (around 1150 Hz, 1750 Hz, and 2300 Hz).

However, the conventional frequency spectrum only reflects the overall energy distribution and fails to reveal the nonlinear coupling relationship between the carrier and modulation frequencies. In particular, under noisy conditions or compound fault scenarios, the modulation sideband peaks are often obscured by the dominant peaks (e.g., around 1750 Hz), making it difficult to accurately identify the specific modulation frequencies and their higher-order components.

The spectral analysis results indicate that the diagonal slicing method generates spectra with significantly higher complexity. As shown in the figures, both slicing methods can extract the characteristic frequencies of each impeller fault signal; however, the diagonal slicing results are generally more complex than those of anti-diagonal slicing. In impeller modes F2 and F3, the maximum amplitude peaks appear around 250 Hz, corresponding to twice the dominant frequency of the original signal, while in mode F1, the secondary peak occurs at approximately 205 Hz, representing the second harmonic of the fundamental frequency. This observation suggests that when the dominant frequencies of the original spectra are close to each other, diagonal slicing tends to produce similar spectral peaks. Consequently, using only the frequency corresponding to the maximum amplitude as a feature may reduce feature resolution, making it difficult to distinguish multiple fault types.

In contrast, when applying anti-diagonal slicing for cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing, the results show clearer and more distinct modulation frequency information. Different impeller fault modes in the centrifugal pump yield different modulation frequencies, and thus the peak frequencies of maximum amplitude also differ. Moreover, the anti-diagonal slicing spectra contain fewer spurious frequency components, resulting in cleaner and more interpretable fault characteristics. Therefore, for cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing-based feature extraction in centrifugal pump impeller fault diagnosis, the anti-diagonal slicing method, combined with a cyclic frequency set to three times the dominant frequency, proves more advantageous. However, directly applying cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing to raw signals may still result in considerable spectral clutter. To mitigate the influence of excessive spurious components, Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) can be introduced as a preprocessing step. By decomposing the signal into multiple Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) representing different frequency components, one can select the IMF containing richer modulation frequency information for subsequent cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing. The remaining challenge is to establish a quantitative criterion for determining which IMF component contains the most significant modulation information. This will be discussed in the following section.

Diagnostic performance verification based on classification model

Feature extraction

Since each impeller state produces nine-channel vibration signals, nine frequency values corresponding to the highest amplitudes in the EMD–cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing spectrograms can be extracted. Thus, the features of a fault mode can be represented by nine eigenvalues. Using this feature extraction method, 140 training sets and 35 test sets were selected for various impeller states.

To evaluate whether the EMD–cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing method improves the extraction of fault characteristics from centrifugal pump impeller vibration signals, the nine vibration signals were processed using cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing. The frequency values corresponding to the highest amplitude points in the nine secondary slice spectrograms were extracted. For each impeller state, 140 training sets and 25 test sets were selected using this method. The EMD method was also applied to identify the IMF component with the highest correlation to the original vibration signal. The frequency corresponding to the maximum amplitude of this component was taken as a feature. Thus, one eigenvalue was obtained for each channel, yielding a total of nine eigenvalues corresponding to an impeller state; similarly, 140 training sets and 35 test sets were constructed. The training and test sets obtained by these three methods served as the datasets for subsequent classification model evaluation.

First, to minimize randomness in the results of a single model, both the SVM and XGBoost classifiers were applied to the datasets extracted using the three feature extraction methods, and their classification performances were compared. (2)Model robustness and cross-validation analysis

Model robustness and cross-validation analysis

To reduce uncertainty and the risk of overfitting under small-sample conditions, a five-fold cross-validation approach was adopted to evaluate the statistical reliability of the model. All samples were randomly divided into five subsets; in each iteration, one subset was used as the validation set, while the remaining four were used for training. After five iterations, the average accuracy was calculated as the final performance metric. This method is widely used in rotating machinery fault diagnosis because it allows sufficient interchange between training and testing sets under limited data conditions, thereby enhancing the stability and statistical credibility of the results.

Using the enhanced feature set (EMD–cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing) and XGBoost as the classifier, the average accuracy of the five-fold cross-validation was 92.11% ± 1.05%. The small standard deviation indicates that the model is insensitive to sample partitioning and has good generalization capability. Considering the number of samples, feature dimensionality, and information content, this validation approach is sufficient to support the reliability of the study’s conclusions.

Complimentary close

This paper proposes a fault feature extraction method for centrifugal pump impellers based on EMD–cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing. Building on the traditional cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing, the proposed method identifies two slicing strategies—anti-angle slicing and tripler-based cyclic frequency slicing—that effectively extract modulation frequency information, as demonstrated through both simulation and experimental analyses. Compared with single signal processing methods, this fusion approach significantly improves the extraction of modulation frequency components and enhances fault diagnosis accuracy.

This paper proposes a fault feature extraction method for centrifugal pump impellers based on EMD–cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing. Building on the traditional cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing, the proposed method identifies two slicing strategies—anti-angle slicing and tripler-based cyclic frequency slicing—that effectively extract modulation frequency information, as demonstrated through both simulation and experimental analyses. Compared with single signal processing methods, this fusion approach significantly improves the extraction of modulation frequency components and enhances fault diagnosis accuracy.

After optimizing the parameters of cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing, the experimental dynamic signal of the centrifugal pump impeller is decomposed by EMD. The IMF component with the highest correlation is then selected, and cyclic bispectrum secondary slicing with optimized parameters is applied. This process enables more effective extraction of fault features.

This process enables more effective extraction of fault features.

In the classification performance evaluation, the features extracted by the proposed method demonstrate higher diagnostic accuracy than those derived from individual approaches. Within the proposed fault diagnosis framework, the EMD–cyclic bispectrum features achieved test accuracies of 85.71% and 92.14% under the SVM and XGBoost models, respectively, and further increased to 94.29% when integrated with 1D-CNN. These results indicate that the proposed features exhibit excellent separability and robustness in both shallow and deep learning models. Compared to traditional EEMD, VMD, or envelope spectrum methods, the proposed fusion strategy demonstrates superior recognition accuracy, noise robustness, and interpretability, achieving an effective balance between computational complexity and diagnostic performance.

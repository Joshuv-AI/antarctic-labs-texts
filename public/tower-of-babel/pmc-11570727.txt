The influence of relative humidity during the first 21 days post-hatch on the production performance, biochemical indices, and meat quality of Pekin ducks☆
Authors: Dongyue Sun, Congcong Xu, Yi Liu, Zichun Dai, Ziyi Pan, Rong Chen, Rihong Guo, Fang Chen, Zhendan Shi, Shijia Ying
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to investigate the effects of different relative humidity (RH) during 4-21d of Beijing ducks on their condition at 42 d. A total of 48 Pekin duck were randomly allotted into 4 treatments (A:RH60 %, B:RH67 %,C:RH74 %,D:RH81 %), each having 3 replicates of 4 ducks. Different humidity treatments were applied from 4 to 21 days. At 42 d, there were no significant differences (P > 0.05) in body weight (BW), average daily gain (ADG), average daily feed intake (ADFI), and feed conversion ratio (FCR) among the 4 groups. Oblique body length in D was significantly higher than that in A (P < 0.05). Compared with A, the chest depth of the other groups was significantly increased, and the abdominal fat weight of B and C was significantly increased (p < 0.05). Liver weight and liver index in C and D significantly higher than that in B (p < 0.05). Abdominal fat ratio in B was significantly higher than that in A (P < 0.05). Cholesterol (CHOL) in D was significantly higher than that in A and C (P < 0.05), triglyceride (TG) in C was significantly higher than that in A and B (P < 0.05), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in B was significantly higher than that in A, C, and D (P < 0.05). pH in B was significantly higher than that in A (P < 0.05), brightness (L*) in A was significantly higher than that in B and C (P < 0.05), redness (a*) in B and C was significantly higher than that in A and D (P < 0.05), and yellowness (b*) in C was significantly lower than that in A and D (P < 0.05).The results indicate that the RH of environment during the brooding period has no impact on duck production performance but induces some oxidative stress damage and changes in meat quality. Additionally, ducks subjected to different RH treatments during the brooding period exhibit varying adaptability to the same environment as they mature.Keywords: Relative humidity, Pekin duck, Production performance, Meat quality

Introduction

With the rapid development of poultry farming, optimizing the growth environment for duck has become a shared concern for both farmers and researchers. Relative Humidity (RH), as a crucial factor in poultry farming environments, directly and profoundly affects the growth and development of ducklings (Furong et al., 2009; Gu et al., 2013), subsequently impacting the economic benefits of poultry farming (Jones and Dawkins, 2010; Kim et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2022). Against this backdrop, this study focuses on the influence of RH on the growth and health of ducklings, aiming to gain in-depth insights into the differences in productivity and meat quality observed in ducklings grown under varying RH conditions during the later stages of development.

In our previous research, we observed that when RH exceeds 80 %, the production performance and meat quality of 42-day-old Pekin ducks are compromised (Sun et al., 2023). But the results from Barott indicated that within the appropriate temperature range (20∼26 °C), 35 %-75 % RH has no significant impact on the growth performance of broilers aged 1-18 d (Barott and Pringle, 1950). However, Barott did not explore the influence of RH on the later stages of broiler growth (18-42 days). Wei et al. (2015) discovered that after exposing 1-day-old chicks to a high RH environment (RH=85 %) for 21 d, the serum lactate dehydrogenase and aspartate aminotransferase activities of 42 day-old chickens significantly increased. Meanwhile, under low RH conditions (RH=35 %), the serum aspartate aminotransferase activity of 42-day-old broilers significantly increased. This suggests that both excessively high and low RH can increase the liver burden of chickens, leading to liver damage. Additionally, under low RH conditions, broilers exhibited decreased body weight (BW), average daily feed intake (ADFI), and increased shear-force of the breast muscle (Wei et al., 2014). Studies have indicated that excessively high RH increases the quantity of pathogenic microorganisms such as Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus in the poultry house, leading to an increased probability of disease and reduced production performance (Al-Aqil et al., 2009; Abidin and Khatoon, 2013). High RH may cause damage to the respiratory mucosa of poultry, reducing their immunity (Liu et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2015; Shi et al., 2019). High RH can also accelerate the corrosion of metal equipment in the breeding house and increase the chances of electronic equipment damage. This further increases the cost of farming.

In the current literature, research on how RH affects ducklings is relatively limited. There is an urgent need for a deeper understanding to provide scientific management and production recommendations for the poultry industry. Through this study, we aim to uncover the specific effects of different RH conditions on the growth, physiological indicators, and meat quality of Pekin ducks. We believe that the results of this study will contribute to optimizing the breeding environment for ducklings, improving production efficiency, and promoting the sustainable development of the poultry farming industry.

Materials and methods

Experimental methods

A completely randomized single-factor design was employed, selecting 48 newly hatched male Pekin ducks with healthy and similar BW. These ducks were randomly assigned to 4 groups A (RH = 60 %), B (RH = 67 %), C (RH = 74 %), D (RH = 81 %)), with 3 replicates per group, each consisting of 4 ducks. The Pekin ducks were placed in 4 separate environmental control rooms with free access to feed and water. The experiment was started when the ducks were 4 d old. The environmental parameters from 1 d to 21 d are detailed in Table 1. Light was provided for 20 h from 5 a.m. everyday, and the room was dark for the rest of the day. The LED-tubes with a color temperature of 6,000 K and a light intensity of 15 lux was chosen to be light source (Riber, 2015). With all other conditions being consistent across the treatment groups, except for RH. From days 22 to 42, we place an extremely lightweight tag on one of its legs to show its identity. All the ducks were then moved to natural environment and raised. Ducks were immunized according to Decree Agriculture and animal husbandry [2021] No. 2 of Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs of the People's Republic of China (MOA, 2021). BW and feed intake were measured every 3 d, and at 42 days of age, slaughter traits and meat quality were assessed. The experimental procedures were approved by the Research Committee of Jiangsu Academy of Agricultural Sciences and conducted in adherence to the Regulations for the Administration of Affairs Concerning Experimental Animals (Decree No. 63 of the Jiangsu Academy of Agricultural Science on 8 July 2014).

Experimental management

The experiment was conducted in the artificial climate-controlled rooms at the Poultry Farm of the Luhe Animal Science Base of Jiangsu Academy of Agricultural Sciences in Jiangsu, China, from March to May 2022. The temperature and RH for different age groups of ducks were automatically adjusted according to the preset parameters (Table 1) with a temperature accuracy of ±1°C and a RH accuracy of ± 5 %. All experimental ducks were reared in 4 identical enclosed chambers with dimensions of 4 m × 2.5 m × 2.6 m during 1-21 d. The walls and floors of the chambers were constructed using insulation materials. Temperature and RH settings during the experiment were referenced from Table 1. All ducks were raised on polyethylene prefabricated leakage dung plates. The leakage dung plates were 60 cm above the ground and fixed with galvanized steel frames. About 20 cm thick dry rice husks were spread on the ground. Temperature and RH sensors were fixed 60 cm above the leakage dung plates to monitor the indoor temperature and RH in real time.

During the experiment, ducks had free access to food and water. The water was provided through nipple drinkers, and the height was adjusted periodically according to the ducks’ growth. The ducks were transferred to their natural environment on 22d with the same cage and sensor design as in the artificial climate-controlled room. The experimental diet consisted of standard commercial feed. It's a mixture of cylindrical pellets meeting the hygiene standards of GB 13078-2017 (SAFM, 2017), and the nutritional levels complied with the requirements of the NY/T 2122—2012 (MFA, 2012) standard for meat duck feeding. The composition of the feed is detailed in Table 2. Continuous light was provided for 24 h a day from 1 d to 3 d, and during 4 d to 42 d, a light–dark cycle of 20 h of light and 4 h of dark was implemented.

Production performance body size and organs

Record the daily feed intake for each group and weigh the ducks every 3 d until the end of the experiment. Calculate the production efficiency of the ducks. Conduct centralized slaughter at 42 d. Collect wing vein blood samples before slaughter and measure the following indicators.

Measure the following morphometric indicators using a measuring tape or vernier caliper during slaughter. Weigh the liver, spleen and abdominal fat and calculate the corresponding indices.

Body oblique length(cm): The distance from the upper anterior joint of the shoulder to the ischial tuberosity.

Keel length(cm): The distance from the upper anterior joint of the clavicle end of the ischial tuberosity.

Chest width(cm): The distance between the two clavicle joints (measured with a caliper).

Chest depth(cm): distance from the first thoracic vertebra (or keel) to the lower anterior end of the sternum.

Pelvic breadth(cm): The distance between the two points of the hip.

After measurements are completed, proceed with slaughter and extract the breast muscle, thigh muscle, abdominal fat, liver, and spleen. Calculate the relevant indices.

Serum Indicators and Antioxidants

At 42 days of age, 3 ducks were randomly selected from each replicate in each treatment group total 36(4groups × 3replicate × 3ducks). Blood was collected intravenously before slaughter and placed in blood collection vessels without anticoagulant additives. After standing at 4 °C, the blood was centrifuged at a low temperature of 3500r/min for 15 min, and the serum was separated and stored at -25 °C. Some of these sera were used for the determination of blood routine, while the others were used for assessing the antioxidant status.

Blood routine included glucose (GLU), cholesterol (CHOL), triacylglycerol (TG), uric acid (UA), Total Protein (TP), High-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL-C), Glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), Malondialdehyde (MDA), Total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), Superoxide Dismutase (SOD).

The absorbance values of MDA, glutathione peroxidase GSH-PX, total antioxidant capacity T-AOC, and superoxide dismutase SOD were determined by setting the wavelength of the enzyme marker according to the instructions of commercial kits. The kits used were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China).

Meat Quality

The pH value, meat color, shear-force, and cooking loss indexes were determined using the breast muscle obtained from slaughter.

Measurement of pH Value: The breast muscle obtained from the slaughter was used as the material for measuring the pH value. Three fixed positions were selected on the breast muscle of the experimental ducks. The pH value of each position was determined using the portable meat acidity meter (testo 206-pH3, Testo SE & Co. KGaA, Lenzkirch, Germany), and the average value was used to represent the pH measurement of the breast muscle.

Meat Color Measurement: The breast muscle from one side of the experimental ducks was separated, and three fixed positions were chosen on each piece of breast muscle to measure meat color L*, a*, and b* values. Each of these measurements was repeated three times using a colorimeter (CR-400, Konica Minolta Sensing, lnc., Tokyo, Japan)

Shear-Force: A texture analyzer (C-LM3B, Northeast Agricultural University, Haerbin, China) was used to determine the shear-force of the cooked breast and thigh samples. At least two subsamples of 2 cm × 3 cm × 0.5 cm were cut parallel to the muscle fibers. The crosshead speed was set to 20 cm/min, and the shear-force was calculated following the method described by Wattanachant et al. (2004).

Protein Content: Protein content in the muscle was determined according to the GB 5009.5-2016 (NHC, 2016) standard using an automatic Kjeldahl nitrogen analyzer (VAP500C, Gerhardt, Königswinte, Germany).

Total Ash Content: After weighing the sample, the sample was fully charred to asmoke-free state by heating with a low flame, and then placed in a muffle furnace (QDSH-7, YI−HENG, Shanghai, China) and burned at 550 ± 25 °C for 4 h. The mixture was cooled to about 200 °C, removed, and cooled in a desiccator for 30 min. The burn was repeated to a constant weight, and the ash content was calculated, following the GB 5009.4-2010 (NHC, 2010) standard.

Crude fat: It was determined using the Soxhlete extraction method according to GB/T 5009.6-2003 (NHC, 2003). The instrument used was a crude fat measuring instrument (S2F-06A, Xinjia, Shanghai, China).

Cooking Loss: A sample of breast muscle from all experimental ducks was taken, weighed, sealed, and maintained at a central temperature of 70 °C. After cooking in a water bath for 15 min and cooling to room temperature, it was weighed again. The cooking loss was calculated using the following formula: (initial weight–final weight) divided by initial weight, multiplied by 100 %.

Statistical analysis

The distribution of all data was first confirmed for their agreement with normal distribution using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov goodness-of-fit test. Where data were not normally distributed, they were log-transformed, re-tested for normality before analysis, and, if still not normalized, the data were tested using the equivalent nonparametric test. Data following a normal distribution were analyzed using SPSS 26 software through one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Multiple comparisons were conducted using Duncan's multiple range test. A significance level of p < 0.05 was used to determine significant differences. The data were presented in the format of “mean ± standard deviation”.

Discussion

Performance of duck

The brooding period is a critical phase in the rearing of Pekin ducks, significantly influencing later growth and development. Experimental results indicate that as the RH during the brooding period gradually increases (from 60 % to 74 %), the BW of Pekin ducks at 42 days also gradually rises. However, when the RH reaches 81 %, the slaughter weight of Pekin ducks at 42 days shows a slight decrease. The trend of BW change exhibits a unimodal pattern. This result is highly consistent with the previous findings (Sun et al., 2023). It indicates that different RH treatments during the brooding period have indeed exerted a certain influence on the growth and development of Pekin ducks. Consequently, although these effects may not be significant. But this impact still contributes to the expression of growth performance in Pekin ducks during the fattening period.

Newly hatched poultry have strict requirements for their living environment, with factors such as high temperature, low temperature, and ammonia concentration being important contributors to early mortality (Rhymer, 1988). Low temperatures within the first three days of chicks entering the breeding house lead to increased mortality. Chicks tend to huddle together, and their down feathers appear loose and disorderly. Chicks require a specific temperature, but prolonged exposure to excessively high temperature also can lead to thermoregulatory disorders and a rapid increase in mortality rates. However, with increasing age, chicks develop adaptability to low-temperature environments. When chicks return to a suitable environment from a low-temperature setting, the time taken for their body temperature to return to normal is influenced by the duration of their exposure to low temperatures (Hou, 2022).

In this experiment, the group D had a higher slaughter weight at 42 days than the groups A and B. This differs from the results of previous research (Sun et al., 2023), indicating that prolonged exposure to a high-RH environment can negatively impact the production performance of Pekin ducks. However, when ducks that were brooded in high RH conditions are transferred back to a normal RH environment, this negative impact gradually diminishes. The reason may be that during the later growth stage, the damage caused by high RH is gradually repaired, allowing the body to exhibit normal production performance. Another possible reason is that by 22-42 days, the nighttime RH will exceed 80 %, and the group D has already adapted to high RH compared to the groups A and B. The sudden transition of the latter two groups to abnormal conditions may cause some degree of stress, inhibiting their production performance.

Body size and organs

From previous research, it is evident that prolonged exposure to different RH levels has an impact on the morphometric measurements and organ indices of Pekin ducks. Contrasting the results of this experiment, where the duration of RH treatment was shortened to only the brooding period, it still significantly affected various morphometric indicators and organ indices in Pekin ducks. Among them, chest depth is notably affected, showing extremely significant differences. The changing trend in chest depth aligns with the trends observed in BW and chest depth in various studies, further confirming the strong correlation between poultry's body size and weight, as emphasized by Jinhua et al. (2020).

Studies indicate that stress has a significant impact on the liver of animals, leading to increased levels of insulin and glucocorticoids, as well as exacerbating fat deposition (Line, 2006). Dallman suggested that insulin and glucocorticoids jointly regulate the energy and metabolism of animals, and any abnormal secretion of these hormones is likely to result in developmental abnormalities (Dallman et al., 1995). The liver plays a crucial role in various biological processes, including energy metabolism, digestion (bile production), and immune function. The liver is involved in functions such as glycogen breakdown, glycogen synthesis, hormone production, and detoxification. Moreover, under acute heat stress, the liver is more susceptible to oxidative stress compared to other organs (Hai et al., 2006; Tang et al., 2015).

Lin's research demonstrated that adding corticosterone to the feed affects the development of broiler breast muscles. Corticosterone increases fatty acid synthesis in the liver, leading to the redistribution of fat from peripheral tissues to the abdomen (Lin et al., 2006). In the current experiment, the abdominal fat index in the group C did not show significant differences compared to other treatment groups, while the group C had a significantly higher abdominal fat index than the group A. This difference may be attributed to a certain level of stress during the fattening period.

Serum indicators and antioxidants

Serum biochemical indicators are markers reflecting the metabolism and health status of animals, holding significant relevance in livestock and poultry production research. The determination of these indicators is relatively convenient and swift. The analysis of serum biochemical indicators contributes to indirect selection of certain traits, animal health assessment, early diagnosis of animal diseases, and effectively addresses the drawbacks of lengthy conventional breeding cycles and high costs (Song et al., 2023).

HDL-C and LDL-C, two indicators, reflect the development of adipose tissue, lipid metabolism, and fat deposition in Pekin ducks HDL particles not only play a crucial role in protein and cholesterol transport but also carry a substantial amount of proteins, micro-RNA, vitamins, and hormones. This endows HDL with the capability to mediate molecular mechanisms maintaining cardiovascular health (Ben-Aicha et al., 2020). The results indicate that different RH treatments during the brooding period significantly affect the HDL-C content in Pekin ducks at 42 days, potentially leading to variations in the probability of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular-related diseases. This is supported by the sequencing results from Experiment 3, where differential gene enrichment analysis revealed enrichment in biological processes and pathways related to cardiovascular diseases.

LDL-C is a type of lipoprotein particle that serves as a carrier for cholesterol entering peripheral tissue cells (Xuefeng et al., 2019). LDL-C can be oxidized into oxidized low-density lipoprotein (Rhoads and Major, 2018); when oxidized low-density lipoprotein is in excess, this change may impact the CHOL content. An increase in carriers leads to an elevated transport of CHOL, and excess CHOL accumulates on the vascular wall, increasing the risk of atherosclerosis. Elevated levels of oxidized low-density lipoprotein have been reported to increase the risk of certain cancers, such as breast cancer (Sánchez-Pérez et al., 2005).

A large body of research indicates that the increase in reactive oxygen species is a significant factor leading to oxidative damage and impairment of antioxidant capacity in organisms (Song et al., 2018). Oxidative damage can directly or indirectly induce damage to biomacromolecules such as DNA, proteins, and lipids. Lipids are essential components of biological membranes, and damage to these membranes can lead to disruption of cell structure and function, further compromising cellular substances such as proteins and nucleic acids, ultimately resulting in tissue damage (Cheng et al., 2017). This is one of the challenging issues in modern poultry farming (Gessner et al., 2017).

In this study, the level of T-AOC was used to estimate the oxidative stress level in immune organs. Research by Hu et al. (2021) indicated that heat stress increased serum MDA levels in broiler chickens, while serum GSH and T-AOC levels decreased. However, in this study, different RH treatments applied from 1-21 days did not affect the antioxidant capacity of ducks at 42 days. Additionally, the levels of some antioxidants in different organs of ducks showed no significant changes, possibly due to the different physiological and biochemical characteristics of the immune organs of Pekin ducks, reflecting different oxidative stress characteristics. Furthermore, research has shown that under heat stress conditions, supplementing Vitamin C and Betaine in feed can improve the antioxidant status of broiler chicken immune organs, manifested by increased antioxidant enzyme activity and elevated T-AOC levels in the bursa of Fabricius. However, this study did not include additional Vitamin C and Betaine in the feed. The reasons for this result may be that RH did not affect the antioxidant performance of ducks from 1-21 days, or the oxidative damage caused to ducks was not significant. Another possible explanation is that different RH treatments affected ducks from 1-21 days, but these damages were repaired as the ducks grew. However, whether these viewpoints are correct requires further experiments for validation.

Meat quality

Due to their rapid metabolism, swift growth, and high meat production, meat-type poultry are susceptible to heat stress. Poultry lack sweat glands, and their bodies are covered in feathers, making them prone to heat stress. For experimental purposes, Pekin ducks undergo catching and blood collection before slaughter, leading to acute stress. Stress accelerates muscle glycogen breakdown, resulting in an increase in lactic acid content in the muscle. In the early postmortem period when the carcass temperature is high, lactic acid causes a rapid decrease in muscle pH (Owens et al., 2009), leading to pale and watery meat, reduced muscle water-holding capacity, and the production of PSE (Pale, Soft, Exudative) meat (Jiang et al., 2017). After fasting for 8-10 h and forced exercise for 20 min, the breast and leg muscle of ducks fasting for 10 h exhibit lower a* and b* values compared to control ducks fasting for only 8 h. This phenomenon is common in poultry. In the current experiment, the pH values of the muscle in all four groups were below 6.59, falling below the normal physiological range. The 74 % group showed no significant difference compared to the other three groups. Different RH treatments during the brooding period did not significantly affect the muscle pH values in Pekin ducks at slaughter, and the pH differences may be more influenced by the degree of stress during slaughter.

Meat color is primarily determined by the hemoglobin content in the muscle, serving as a relatively intuitive indicator of meet quality. Research indicates that meat color is an important influencing factor for consumers during purchasing (Barbut, 2001). With increasing age, there is a tendency for meat color to become redder (Baéza et al., 2022). Baeza et al. (1998) found that from 8 to 15 weeks of age in ducks, the L* value of Peking duck meat decreased from 45.7 to 35.4, and the a* value increased from 8.4 to 16.1. For poultry meat, meat color is largely dependent on muscle glycogen storage at slaughter and postmortem pH changes, influencing the light scattering properties of muscle. In traditional chicken fillets, research by Berri et al. indicates a correlation of -0.57 between pH and sugar fermentation in chicken breast muscle, and a correlation of -0.61 with L*(Berri et al., 2007). At the genetic level, the correlation between pH and L* is stronger, ranging from -0.65 to -0.91(Chabault et al., 2012). Alnahhas et al. (2014) obtained chicken varieties with different muscle pH values through selective breeding, where the high pH value resulted in darker muscle color (L* values of 47.50 and 52.50), lower redness values (a* values of -0.17 and 0.05), and lower yellowness values (b* values of 9.49 and 11.02). Additionally, individuals with higher breast muscle rates may experience a decrease in redness values, an increase in yellowness values, and an increase in brightness values due to lower hemoglobin content. Furthermore, research indicates a correlation between the struggle time of chickens during slaughter and the a* value of meat, as chickens flap their wings continuously during struggle, leading to congestion in the breast muscle (Berri et al., 2006).

Conclusions

Different RH treatments during the brooding period had significant effects on body size, organ index, serum biochemical indexes and meat quality of 42-day-old Peking ducks. 74 % RH treatment significantly increased breast depth, liver weight and liver index of Peking ducks. The flesh color brightness (L* value) was significantly reduced. In the 67 % RH group, LDL-C was significantly lower and muscle pH was significantly higher.

In the early growth stage, RH had little effect on the performance of Peking ducks. In the later growth stage, the body of Peking duck is able to repair stress damage caused by environmental factors during the brooding period. However, the extent of repair is limited, and the damage sustained during the incubation period continues to affect their later growth and development. In addition, ducks treated with different RH during the brooding period showed different adaptations to the later growth environment.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Colouring

The following discrete combinatorial question has recently surfaced
in joint work of Dudek, Ruciński, and the first author
[
1
]

on so-called “randomly augmented graphs”. Given three integers
k\geq 0

and
m,n\geq 1
, they ask for a colouring of the set
\{1,\dots,n\}

with
k+1
colours such that the number of pairs of integers
\{x,y\}

which have the same colour and distance at most
m
is minimised;
here one should imagine
n
to be much larger than
k
and
m
. If
the desired minimum number of pairs could be determined with an accuracy
of
\pm O_{k,m}(1)
, then some interesting results in extremal graph
theory would follow.

The approach to this problem pursued in this article is that it should be
easier to address the corresponding analytical problem first. This is suggested
by experience and analogy with other problems in extremal combinatorics
of which we would like to mention the Lovász-Simonovits triangle density
problem
[
3
]
as a prototypical example. In its original formulation the
problem asks to determine, for given numbers
n
and
e
, the least
number
t(n,e)
of triangles that a graph on
n
vertices with
e
edges can
contain. Despite a recent breakthrough by Liu, Pikhurko, and Staden
[
2
]
this
question is still open, whereas the corresponding analytical question to
determine the function

f_{3}(\gamma)=\lim_{n\to\infty}t(n,\lceil\gamma n^{2}\rceil)/n^{3}
has been
solved by Razborov
[
6
]
(see also
[
5
,
8
]
for generalisations
to larger cliques). A perhaps more transparent, equivalent definition of

f_{3}(\gamma)
, featured in Lovász’s book
[
4
]
, is that it is the
minimum value of
\frac{1}{6}\int_{[0,1]^{3}}W(x,y)W(x,z)W(y,z)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z

as
W
varies over all symmetric measurable functions
[0,1]^{2}\longrightarrow[0,1]

satisfying
\int_{[0,1]^{2}}W(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=2\gamma
.

In order to formulate an analytical version of the colouring problem we mentioned
in the beginning, we replace the discrete set
\{1,\dots,n\}
by a real
interval
I
of some given length
L
. Colourings of
I
with
k+1
colours are
essentially the
same as partitions
I=A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{k+1}
of
I
into
k+1
classes.
Instead of counting certain pairs of integers, we shall consider the measure of an
analogous set, and thus we always need to require that the partition classes

A_{1},\dots,A_{k+1}
be Lebesgue measurable. One advantage of the current setup
is that we can eliminate the parameter
m
by rescaling it to
1
. Then the
number of small-distance pairs with colour
i
corresponds to the (two dimensional
Lebesgue) measure
\Phi(A_{i})
of the set

\bigl\{(x,y)\in A_{i}^{2}\colon x\leq y\leq x+1\bigr\}

and the question becomes how small the sum
\Phi(A_{1})+\dots+\Phi(A_{k+1})
can be.

Let us look at a concrete example. For a given positive real number
t
we first
divide the interval
I=[0,L]
into consecutive intervals

I_{0}=[0,t),I_{1}=[t,2t),\dots
of length
t
and then for every

i=1,\dots,k+1
we define
A_{i}
to be the union of all intervals
I_{j}

whose index
j
is congruent to
i
modulo
k+1
. It can easily be shown that for

t\in\bigl[\frac{1}{k+1},\frac{1}{k}\bigr]
the sum
\Phi(A_{1})+\dots+\Phi(A_{k+1})

evaluates to
\frac{1}{2}\bigl((k^{2}+1)t-2k+t^{-1}\bigr)L+O(1)
. The factor in front
of
L
attains its least value
\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k
for
t=(k^{2}+1)^{-1/2}
.
Theorem
1.1
below asserts that this example is essentially optimal.
Its statement avoids the error term
O(1)
by addressing a circle
S={\mathds{R}}/L{\mathds{Z}}

of perimeter
L\geq 1
rather than an interval
I
of length
L
. In this context, we can still define

\Phi(A)=\lambda\bigl(\{(x,y)\in A^{2}\colon x\leq y\leq x+1\}\bigr)

for every measurable set
A\subseteq S
, but now the advantage is that pairs
‘wrapping around the circle’ such as, for instance, the pairs in

[L-1/2,L)\times[0,1/2)
can contribute to
\Phi(A)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 0
be an integer and let
L\geq 1
be real.
If
A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{k+1}
partitions the circle
{\mathds{R}}/L{\mathds{Z}}

into
k+1
measurable parts, then

\Phi(A_{1})+\Phi(A_{2})+\dots+\Phi(A_{k+1})\geq(\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k)L\,.

As the discussion above suggests, this holds with equality if

n=\frac{\sqrt{k^{2}+1}L}{k+1}
is an integer and every set
A_{i}
consists of
n

intervals of length
\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{2}+1}}
that alternate cyclically
(see Figure
1.1
).

1.2.
Density

In Ramsey theory it frequently happens that a result on colourings of a structure
admits a generalisation to dense substructures. A well-known example where this
phenomenon occurs is van der Waerden’s theorem
[
10
]
, which states that if for
a sufficiently large natural number
n=n(k,r)
we colour the set
\{1,\dots,n\}

with
r
colours, then some
k
-term arithmetic progression will be monochromatic.
The corresponding density result is a celebrated theorem of Szemerédi
[
9
]

asserting that if
n=n(k,\delta)
is sufficiently large, then every
set
A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
of size
|A|\geq\delta n
contains a
k
-term
arithmetic progression. Clearly the case
\delta=1/r
of Szemerédi’s
theorem implies van der Waerden’s theorem, while, conversely, no ‘easy’ way of
deriving Szemerédi’s theorem from van der Waerden’s theorem is known.
In fact, one obstacle for such a reverse deduction has recently been analysed
in the PhD thesis of M. Sales (see also
[
7
]
).

This state of affairs indicates that one should at least wonder whether there
is a strengthening of Theorem
1.1
that bounds the
k+1

summands
\Phi(A_{i})
individually in terms of the densities
\xi_{i}=\lambda(A_{i})/L
.
As it turns out, this can indeed be achieved.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
L\geq 1
, the set
A\subseteq{\mathds{R}}/L{\mathds{Z}}
is measurable,
and
\lambda(A)=\xi L
, then

\Phi(A)\geq(\xi+\sqrt{2\xi^{2}-2\xi+1}-1)L\,.

Note that equality holds if
n=\sqrt{2\xi^{2}-2\xi+1}L
is an integer and
A
consists
of
n
equally spread intervals, each of length

\frac{\xi L}{n}
(see Figure
1.2
). In particular, for every
\xi\in[0,1]
the factor
\xi+\sqrt{2\xi^{2}-2\xi+1}-1
in Theorem
1.2
is optimal.

Figure 1.2
.
Three equidistant intervals of length
\frac{\xi L}{3}
.

We conclude this introduction by showing that the density version of our
result implies the colouring version.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k+1}
be the numbers defined in such a way that
\lambda(A_{i})=\xi_{i}L
holds for every
i
. Then
\xi_{i}
’s sum up to
1
and, therefore, Theorem
1.2
yields

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\Phi(A_{i})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(\xi_{i}+\sqrt{\xi_{i}^{2}+(1-\xi_{i})^{2}}-1)L=\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\sqrt{\xi_{i}^{2}+(1-\xi_{i})^{2}}\biggr)L-kL\,.

Minkowski’s inequality implies

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\sqrt{\xi_{i}^{2}+(1-\xi_{i})^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\xi_{i}\biggr)^{2}+\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(1-\xi_{i})\biggr)^{2}}=\sqrt{k^{2}+1}\,,

and thus we have indeed
\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\Phi(A_{i})\geq(\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k)L
.
∎

Lemma 2.2

If

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda(A)=\xi L
for some

Indeed, both sides of the above equation are equal to the measure of the set

Next, since
f_{A}(x)+f_{B}(x)=1

\int_{B}f_{A}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B}f_{B}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=(1-\xi)L\,.

By subtracting this from equation (

\displaystyle\Phi(A)-\Phi(B)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}f_{A}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{B}f_{B}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{S}f_{A}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{B}f_{A}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{B}f_{B}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:3}}{\geq}\frac{(W+1)(W+\xi-1)}{W\xi}-\frac{W+\xi-1}{2W}=\frac{(2W+2-\xi)(W+\xi-1)}{2W\xi}

\displaystyle\overset{\phantom{\eqref{eq:3}}}{\geq}\frac{4W(W+\xi-1)}{2W\xi}=\frac{2(W+\xi-1)}{\xi}\,,

we thus have indeed

Second case:

This time we use additionally that

, which leads to the refined estimate

, where

\frac{\xi}{W}&\text{ if }x\in\bigl[1-\frac{1-\xi}{W},\alpha-\left(1-\frac{1-\xi}{W}\right)\bigr]\\
\varphi-(\alpha-x)&\text{ if }x\in\bigl[\alpha-\left(1-\frac{1-\xi}{W}\right),\alpha\bigr]\end{cases}

is visualised in Figure

Decomposing the area below

into a rectangle and two
right-angled isosceles triangles we obtain

\int_{0}^{\alpha}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{\xi}{W}\alpha+\left(\varphi-\frac{\xi}{W}\right)^{2}\,.

Since the definitions of
\varphi
and
W

\displaystyle\left(\varphi-\frac{\xi}{W}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1-\xi}{W}\right)^{2}=1-\frac{2(1-\xi)}{W}+\frac{W^{2}-\xi^{2}}{W^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(W+\xi-1)}{W}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{W^{2}}=\frac{\xi}{W}\left(\frac{2(W+\xi-1)}{\xi}-\frac{\xi}{W}\right)\,,

we are led again to

\int_{0}^{\alpha}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{\xi}{W}\alpha+\left(\frac{2(W+\xi-1)}{\xi}-\frac{\xi}{W}\right)\alpha=\frac{2\alpha(W+\xi-1)}{\xi}\,.

Third case:
\alpha\geq\frac{\xi}{W}

We contend that

(2.5)

\varphi\geq\alpha\geq\frac{2}{3}\varphi\,.

The first inequality follows from the fact that our assumptions
on

\varphi=h(0)\geq\alpha

which confirms the second estimate.

Similar to the previous case we have

\bar{h}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi-x&\text{ if }x\in[0,\varphi-\alpha]\\
\alpha&\text{ if }x\in[\varphi-\alpha,2\alpha-\varphi]\\
\varphi-(\alpha-x)&\text{ if }x\in[2\alpha-\varphi,\alpha]\end{cases}

is the function drawn in Figure

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha}h(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\alpha}\bar{h}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\alpha^{2}+(\varphi-\alpha)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(\alpha-\frac{\xi}{W}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{4\xi}{W}-2\varphi\right)\alpha+\left(\varphi^{2}-\frac{2\xi^{2}}{W^{2}}\right)\,.

Owing to

\frac{4\xi}{W}-2\varphi=\frac{4\xi-2(W+2\xi-1)}{W}=\frac{2(1-W)}{W}

\displaystyle\varphi^{2}-\frac{2\xi^{2}}{W^{2}}=\frac{(1-2\xi)^{2}-2(1-2\xi)W+W^{2}-2\xi^{2}}{W^{2}}=\frac{2(1-2\xi)(1-\xi-W)}{\xi W}\cdot\frac{\xi}{W}\leq 0\,,

we can conclude that

Finally, because of

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-2\xi)(1-\xi-W)}{W\xi}=\frac{1}{W}-1+\frac{W^{2}-\xi-(1-2\xi)W}{W\xi}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{W+2\xi-1}{\xi}=\frac{W+\xi-1}{\xi}\,,

,
this leads to
\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta(A_{n})=\eta(A)
. By Proposition
3.2

each term of this sequence is nonnegative and thus
\eta(A)
cannot be
negative either.
∎

4.
A concluding remark

Let us finally return to the discrete problem investigated by Dudek, Ruciński,
and the first author
[
1
]
. Given a finite set
A\subseteq{\mathds{Z}}
and a positive
integer
m
we write
\Psi_{m}(A)
for the number of pairs
(x,y)\in A^{2}
such
that
x<y\leq x+m
. For any three integers
k\geq 0
and
m,n\geq 1
, the
minimum value of
\Psi_{m}(A_{1})+\dots+\Psi_{m}(A_{k+1})
taken over all partitions

[n]=A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{k+1}
is denoted by
f(k,m,n)
.

In graph theoretic term this function can be viewed as follows. The
m^{\mathrm{th}}

power of the path on
n
vertices is a graph
P^{m}_{n}
with vertex set

[n]
and all edges
\{x,y\}
satisfying
|x-y|\leq m
.
With every vertex colouring
\varphi\colon V(P_{n}^{m})\longrightarrow[k+1]
we associate the
partition
[n]=A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{k+1}
defined by
A_{i}=\varphi^{-1}(i)
for
every
i\in[k+1]
. The sum
\Psi_{m}(A_{1})+\dots+\Psi_{m}(A_{k+1})
is then the same
as the number of edges of
P^{m}_{n}
which connect vertices of the same colour.
Thus,
f(k,m,n)
is the minimum number of monochromatic edges of
P^{m}_{n}
that
a
(k+1)
-colouring of
V(P^{m}_{n})
can have.

It is plain that
f(k,m,n_{1}+n_{2})\leq f(k,m,n_{1})+f(k,m,n_{2})+\binom{m+1}{2}

holds for all integers
k\geq 0
and
m,n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1
. Thus, for fixed
k
and
m
,
the function
n\longmapsto f(k,m,n)+\binom{m+1}{2}
is subadditive and,
consequently, the limit

\alpha(k,m)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(k,m,n)}{n}

exists. An explicit construction described in
[
1
]
shows

(4.1)

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(k,m)}{m}\leq\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k\,.

As we shall explain below, our first theorem yields a complementary lower bound,
so that altogether we have

(4.2)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(k,m)}{m}=\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k\,.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
k\geq 0
and
n\geq m\geq 1
, then

f(k,m,n)\geq\left((\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k)m-\frac{1}{2}\right)n-\frac{m^{2}}{2}\,.

This estimate clearly implies

\alpha(k,m)\geq(\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k)m-\frac{1}{2}\,,

which in turn reveals

\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(k,m)}{m}\geq\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k\,.

Together with (
4.1
) this proves indeed our claim (
4.2
).

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Fix a partition
[n]=A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{k+1}
such that

f(k,m,n)=\Psi_{m}(A_{1})+\dots+\Psi_{m}(A_{k+1})\,.

For
L=n/m
we define the partitions

[0,L)=I_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}I_{n}=B_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}B_{k+1}

by setting first
I_{j}=\bigl[\frac{j-1}{m},\frac{j}{m}\bigr)
for every
j\in[n]

and then
B_{i}=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{j\in A_{i}}I_{j}
for every
i\in[k+1]
.
Due to
L\geq 1
, Theorem
1.1

tells us that the sets
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
defined in (
3.4
)
and (
3.5
) satisfy

\lambda\bigl((B_{1}^{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}B_{k+1}^{2})\cap(D_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}D_{2})\bigr)\geq(\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k)L\,.

Because of

, this entails

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\sum_{(x,y)\in A_{i}^{2}}\lambda(I_{x}\times I_{y}\cap D_{1})\geq\frac{(\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k)n}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\,.

and the desired inequlity follows upon multiplying by

.
∎

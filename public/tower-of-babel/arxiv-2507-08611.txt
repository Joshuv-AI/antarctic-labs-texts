Abstract
Massive multiple-input multiple-output (mMIMO) is a key capacity-boosting technology in 5G wireless systems. To reduce the number of radio frequency (RF) chains needed in such systems, a novel approach has recently been introduced involving an antenna array supported by a reconfigurable intelligent surface. This arrangement, known as a reconfigurable intelligent base station (RIBS), offers performance comparable to that of a traditional mMIMO array, but with significantly fewer RF chains. Given the growing importance of precise, location-specific performance prediction, this paper evaluates the performance of an RIBS system by means of the SIONNA ray-tracing module. That performance is contrasted against results derived from a statistical 3GPP-compliant channel model, optimizing power and RIS configuration to maximize the sum spectral efficiency. Ray tracing predicts better performance than the statistical model in the evaluated scenario, suggesting the potential of site-specific modeling. However, empirical validation is needed to confirm this advantage.

I
Introduction

Massive multiple-input multiple-output (mMIMO) is crucial for 5G networks
[
1
]
. It relies on numerous active antennas and extensive digital baseband processing at the base station (BS), to spatially multiplex user equipment (UE) units within the same time-frequency resource, enhancing coverage, spectral, and energy efficiency.
However, excessively increasing the number of active antennas is costly and energy inefficient, as energy consumption rises linearly with the number of radio frequency (RF) chains.
Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) are an affordable solution for enhancing performance and overcoming challenging propagation conditions
[
2
]
.An RIS uses low-cost, passive elements, reconfigured in real time, to steer energy and prevent interference. Initially, RIS technology only allowed for phase-shift tuning in reflected waves to boost single and multiuser MIMO systems
[
3
]
. Later, active RISs were developed to control both the amplitude and phase of the reflected waves
[
4
,
5
]
.

In
[
6
]
, the authors investigated a novel setup involving a BS with a planar antenna array that integrates a RIS to support multiple UEs, as illustrated in Fig. 1. This setup is termed a
reconfigurable intelligent base station
(RIBS). Their findings indicate that RIBS facilitates the multiplexing efficiencies and performance of standard MIMO systems using far fewer active antennas and RF chains. The research particularly highlighted the significance of employing active RISs, which markedly surpass, for the same amount of total radiated power, the performance of RIBS based on passive RISs.

Recently, there has been growing interest in using ray tracing to generate accurate site-specific electromagnetic models
[
7
]
. This study concentrates on assessing the performance achievable via a RIBS when using the SIONNA ray tracing module
[
8
]
to characterize electromagnetic propagation within a specified region. We compare the resultant performance with that derived from a traditional 3GPP-compliant channel model when power and RIS configuration are optimized to maximize the system sum spectral efficiency (SE). Notably, our findings show that ray tracing predicts higher performance than statistical models in the simulated scenario, which may suggest that transceiver algorithms tailored to specific sites could help improve resource utilization. However, these observations require empirical validation.

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{k}=\sum^{S_{k}}_{s=1}\mathsf{e}^{\mathsf{j}\theta_{k,s}}\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{k,s}+\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{k}\ \;\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{R}}\times 1},

local scattering model

[

If there is a LOS path between the RIS and UE

s

\{\hat{\mathbf{h}}_{k,s}\}

All the channel statistics are assumed to be known at the RIBS.

To compute the channels between the RIS and UEs in the SIONNA environment, we replaced the RIS with a transmitter equipped with a planar array that has the same number of elements as the RIS configuration. Each element is configured with an isotropic pattern to emulate passive reflection.

Following the deployment of the transmitter and outdoor UEs, ray tracing is executed using the parameters summarized in Table

By evaluating (

III
Performance Measures and Optimization

The downlink (DL) signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) at UE
k
under the assumption of perfect channel knowledge at the RIBS and at the UEs is given by

\gamma_{k}(\mathbf{p})\!=\!\frac{\eta_{k}\left|\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{G}^{\mathsf{T}}_{k}\mathbf{w}_{k}\right|^{2}}{\sum\nolimits_{j\neq k}^{K}\eta_{j}\left|\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{G}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{w}_{j}\right|^{2}\!+\!{\left\|\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{H}_{k}\right\|}^{2}\!\sigma^{2}_{\text{R}}\!+\!\sigma^{2}_{k}}\,,

(7)

where
\eta_{k}
is the transmit power reserved for UE
k
,
\mathbf{w}_{k}\!\in\!\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{A}}}
is the precoder,
{\left\|\mathbf{w}_{k}\right\|}\!=\!1
,
\sigma^{2}_{\text{R}}
denotes the variance of the dynamic noise introduced by the active RIS, and
\sigma^{2}_{k}
represents the variance of the white thermal noise at the
k
th UE.

If the channel model is stochastic, as per Section
II-B1
, the achievable ergodic SE for UE
k
, in bit/s/Hz, is

\text{SE}_{k}=\E\left\{{\log_{2}\big(1+\gamma_{k}(\mathbf{p})\big)}\right\},

(8)

where the expectation is with respect to the channel realizations.
If the channel model is deterministic, as per Section
II-B2
, then the SE is given by (
8
) but without expectation.

III-A

Optimization of the RIS Configuration

Thanks to the RIBS, the propagation environment can be conveniently shaped by letting the BS optimize the RIS phase shifts and amplification factors. In this paper, we formulate a DL sum-rate
maximization problem for a RIBS-aided MIMO system. By exploiting fractional programming (FP), a closed-form expression for the optimal RIS configuration is provided along with a heuristic power allocation strategy.

Due to the use of active components, the active RIS consumes additional power to amplify the reflected signals. This reflect power, denoted by
P_{\text{RIS}}
, is a function of
\mathbf{p}
and of the BS transmit power, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\eta}\!=\![\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{K}]^{\mathsf{T}}
.
The signal, coming from the BS, reflected by the active RIS is given by

\mathbf{r}_{\text{RIS}}\!=\!\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{K}\sqrt{\eta_{j}}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{w}_{j}x_{j}\!+\!\mathbf{P}\mathbf{z}_{\text{R}}\,,

(9)

where
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{z}_{\text{R}}
,
\mathbf{z}_{\text{R}}\!\sim\!\mathcal{CN}\left({\boldsymbol{0}_{N_{\text{R}}}},{\sigma^{2}_{\text{R}}\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{R}}}}\right)
, denotes the dynamic noise introduced by the active RIS. The power of
\mathbf{r}_{\text{RIS}}
is

\displaystyle P_{\text{RIS}}(\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{\eta})

\displaystyle\!=\!\E\left\{{{\left\|\bar{\mathbf{r}}_{\text{RIS}}\right\|}^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\!=\!\sum\nolimits^{K}_{j=1}\eta_{j}\tr(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{w}_{j}\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}_{j}\mathbf{H}^{\ast}\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{H}})\!+\!\sigma^{2}_{\text{R}}\tr(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{H}})

\displaystyle\!=\!\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{H}}\boldsymbol{\Pi}\mathbf{p}\,.

(10)

where

\boldsymbol{\Pi}\!=\!\sum\nolimits^{K}_{j=1}\eta_{j}\,\text{diag}(\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{w}_{j})\left(\text{diag}(\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{w}_{j})\right)^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}_{\text{R}}\mathbf{I}_{N_{\text{R}}}\,.

(11)

The available transmit power budget at the RIBS for the DL,

s.t.

III-B

Heuristic DL Power Allocation

Transmit precoding at the BS and reflect precoding at the RIS are closely entangled as they are under a cumulative power constraint. Indeed,
\mathbf{p}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}
are coupled in the RIS power constraint (
12b
). Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\eta}
must also satisfy the following power constraint at the BS:

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits^{K}_{k=1}\eta_{k}\!\leq\!(1\!-\!\varepsilon)P_{\text{max}}\,.

(21)

A heuristic power allocation strategy consists in setting

\eta_{k}\!=\!\min\left\{(1\!-\!\varepsilon)P_{\text{max}}\varrho_{k}(\mathbf{p}),\frac{\varepsilon P_{\text{max}}\!-\!{\left\|\mathbf{p}\right\|}^{2}\sigma^{2}_{{\text{R}}}}{{\left\|(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{w}_{k}\right\|}^{2}}\varrho_{k}(\mathbf{p})\right\},\!

(22)

where we have defined

\varrho_{k}(\mathbf{p})\!=\!\frac{\left[\tr\left(\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{k}\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)\right]^{\nu}}{\sum\nolimits^{K}_{i=1}\left[\tr\left(\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)\right]^{\nu}}\,,

(23)

with the parameter
\nu
establishing the power allocation policy. If
\nu>0
, the BS allocates more power to the UEs with better channel gains. Such a choice would be in line with the sum SE maximization objective. Importantly, (
22
) guarantees that the constraints (
12b
) and (
21
) are simultaneously satisfied.

V
Conclusion

This paper evaluated the performance of an RIBS, an architecture that reduces RF chains while maintaining mMIMO performance. Using the SIONNA ray tracing module, we compared RIBS’s performance against 3GPP-compliant statistical models. In the simulated scenario, ray tracing predicted higher performance than 3GPP-compliant statistical models, a result that still awaits confirmation through empirical measurements. Ongoing work focuses on RIBS energy savings compared to conventional mMIMO and developing site-specific transceiver algorithms.

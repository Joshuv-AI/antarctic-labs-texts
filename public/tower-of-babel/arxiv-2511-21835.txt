Metrised ample line bundles in non-Archimedean geometry II
Yanbo FANG

Abstract




1.
Introduction

1.1.
Arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel function over a local place

We continue our study of semipositive metrics
\phi
on an ample line bundle
L
of an irreducible projective variety
X
of dimension
d
over a non-archimedean (n-A) field
(k,\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert)
in
[
Fan1
]
, from the point of view of its Banach algebra of normed sections
\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)
. Recall that we denote by

—

R_{n}(L)
the vector space
H^{0}(X,L^{n})
;
R_{{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}}(L)
the algebra
\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(X,L^{n})
;

—

\chi(n)
the geometric Hilbert-Samuel (HS) function
n\mapsto\dim R_{n}(L)
;

—

\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi}
the sup norm induced by
\phi
on
R_{n}(L)
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert_{\phi}
the algebra norm on
R_{{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}}(L)
as the orthogonal sum
\obot_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi}
;

—

\widehat{\chi}(n)
the arithmetic HS function (over the local place
(k,\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert)
), defined as the map
n\mapsto\det(R_{n}(L),\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi})
valued in normed lines over the base valued field.

The geometric HS formula computes the leading term of
\chi(n)
(normalised by
\frac{n^{d}}{d!}
) in the asymptotic expansion for
n\gg 1
to be the intersection number
c_{1}(L)^{n}
. The classical strategy is to use an induction by dimension argument, comparing the difference of
\chi(n)
for terms in the following exact sequence induced by multiplication by a global section
s\in R_{1}(L)
and restriction to its divisor
V(s)
:

0\rightarrow R_{n}(L)\xrightarrow{\cdot s}R_{n+1}(L)\xrightarrow{\mathord{\cdot}|_{V(s)}}R_{n+1}(L|_{V(s)})\rightarrow 0.

We aim to study the asymptotic behavior of the function
\widehat{\chi}(n)
; mimicking the classical strategy, one would like to evaluate its change in the sequence of normed vector spaces

(R_{n}(L),\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi})\xrightarrow{\cdot s}(R_{n+1}(L),\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{(n+1)\phi})\xrightarrow{\cdot|_{V(s)}}(R_{n+1}(L|_{V(s)}),\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{(n+1)\phi|_{V(s)}}).

This sequence is no longer ‘exact’ with respect to norms on them, since the linear maps are not necessarily isometries. Metrisation leads to secondary/arithmetic invariants and our arithmetic HS function is of this nature. Under the identification of the underlying lines
\det R_{n+1}(L)\simeq\det R_{n}(L)\otimes\det R_{n+1}(L|_{V(s)})
, the change of
\widehat{\chi}(n)
in this sequence is the distorsion of determinant norms

\tau_{n}(L,\phi;s):=\frac{\det\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{(n+1)\phi}}{\det\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi}\det\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{(n+1)\phi|_{\Div(s)}}}\in\mathbb{R}.

To estimate it, decompose the distorsion into an equidistribution part and an extension part; both parts can be expressed as operator norms (denoted by
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{\hom}
) naturally induced by
\phi

\tau_{n}(L,\phi;s)=\lVert\bigwedge^{\chi(n)}(\cdot s)\rVert_{\hom,\phi}\cdot\lVert\bigwedge^{\chi_{V(s)}(n+1)}(\mathord{\cdot}|_{V(s)})\rVert_{\hom,\phi}^{-1}.

We’ve estimated in
[
Fan1
]
that the second term has an negligible asymptotic contribution in the limit. This article studies the limit of the first term.

1.2.
Asymptotic formula and equidistribution measure

Results in special cases or in similar forms are obtained previously (see next part) by other techniques, here we take a direct
non-archimedean analytic
approach to evaluate the normalised limit of
\frac{1}{\chi(n)}\tau_{n}(L,\phi)
.

We begin with a class of metrics which we call
Shilov finite
: the Shilov boundary
\Shbd\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)
(of its Berkovich spectrum) consists of finitely many birational points. This class includes all semipositive model metrics, but also potentially more, since Shilov finite metric could possibly be singular.

First we can calculate the desired limit from the Banach algebra
\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)
using norm reduction techniques in the case of Shilov finite metrics. Expressed on the ‘disc domain’
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R}(L,\phi))
, the limit also takes the form of the pairing of the function
\log\lvert s\rvert
with a probability measure
\mu_{\eq}(\phi)
which we call the
equidistribution measure
. The measure takes the form

\mu_{\eq}(\phi):=\sum_{a\in\Shbd\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)}\lambda_{a}\delta_{a},\ \lambda_{a}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dim_{\widetilde{k}}[\pi_{a}\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)]^{\sim}_{n}/\dim_{\widetilde{k}}[\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)]^{\sim}_{n}

where
\pi_{a}\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)
is an analytic localization of
\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)
at
a
, and the
\widetilde{k}
-vector spaces
[-]^{\sim}
are reductions of corresponding normed
k
-vector spaces. The equidistribution measure (pushed-forward to
X^{\an}
by the natural projection map) is expected to coincide with the Monge-Ampère measure
\MA(\phi)
if the metric is continuous.

Theorem 1.1

(
3.17
)
.

Let
\phi
be a metric which is Shilov finite. Then it holds

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\chi(n)}\log\penalty\ \lVert\bigwedge^{\chi(n)}(\cdot s)\rVert_{\hom,\phi}=\int_{\Cone(L)^{\an}}\log\lvert s\rvert\mu_{\eq}(\phi).

Then by an approximation argument one extends the result to the case of semipositive continuous metrics:

Corollary 1.2

(
3.30
)
.

Let
\phi
be a continuous semipositive metric. Then it holds

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\chi(n)}\log\penalty\ \lVert\bigwedge^{\chi(n)}(\cdot s)\rVert_{\hom,\phi}=\int_{X^{\an}}\log\lvert s\rvert_{\phi}\MA(\phi).

We view these results as an arithmetic Hilbert-Samuel formula over the local place
(k,\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert)
.

A natural class of Shilov finite metrics are those whose disc domain is a finite union of affinoid domains; if the metrics are assumed to be continuous, by a result of
[
GK
]
they turn out to coincide with the class of piecewise linear (semipositive) metrics (Proposition
4.2
). For general Shilov finite metrics, we show some continuity properties of their equidistribution measures (Proposition
4.8
) once their supports (projected to
X^{\an}
) is contained in a given finite set; this allows one to solve the equation for Shilov finite metrics with prescribed finite equidistribution measure (Proposition
4.8
).

Our results suggest that the study of non-Archimedean Monge-Ampère measure and related equation for semipositive metrics, as developpped in works such as
[
CLD
]
[
GK
]
and
[
BFJ2
]

[
BGJKM
]
[
BGM
]
, could be decoupled into two steps, each with a more analytic flavor:

—

first, focus on the equidistribution measure using purely Banach-algebra techniques, which employs its intrinsic integral model; certain properties such as differentiability of the relative volume and orthogonality property for the support of MA measure have counterparts and can be understood more intuitively in this way;

—

then, to compare this measure to the genuine n-A MA measure, one needs the continuity property of the envelope metric.

This article only takes part of the first step.

1.3.
Relations to other works

1.3.1.
Complex analytic prototype and global arithmetic HS formula

In the complex analytic setting, such an equidistribution identity is proved in
[
AB
]
in their study of arithmetic HS function over number fields, where the determinant norm distorsion serves as the contribution to the arithmetic intersection number
\widehat{c}_{1}(\mathscr{L},\phi)^{d+1}
from Archimedean places; for smooth strictly positive metrics, the equidistribution term is decoupled using peak sections adapted to a suitable dissection of the manifold
X^{\an}
into a number of
\chi(n)
small pieces; the construction of such sections relies on (analytic) localization techniques concerning spectral theory of
\overline{\partial}
-Laplacian and Bergman kernel estimates.

The goal of
[
AB
]
was to prove a simplified version to the arithmetic HS formula over a number field, originally established by Gillet and Soulé, as a consequence of their powerful arithmetic Riemann-Roch formula
[
GS
]

K
be a number field and let
(X,L)
defined over
K
. For ample model line bundle of
L
over
\mathfrak{o}_{K}
, equipped with a Hermitian metric
\phi_{\infty}
of positive curvature on complex fibers, Gillet and Soulé’s formula reads (stated in an adelic way, in a simplified version

1.3.2.
Non-archimedean equidistribution for model metrics

In the non-archimedean setting, we start with Shilov finite metrics (which include all integral/formal model metrics), and a weak (analytic) localization is realized using norm reduction and HS formula on the reduction section algebra over the residual field; these techniques are specific to the ultrametric world. Note that reduction only yields peak sections in a weak sense: they from a n-A orthogonal basis, with their pics localized to the support of the corresponding equidistribution measure, however each point in the support comes with the residual H-S multiplicity.

For a metric
\phi
coming from an ample integral model (
\simeq
n-A Fubini-Study metric), the above formula has been obtained in
[
Mor
]
using relative asymptotic Riemann-Roch formula on the integral model over integral ring. One can reformulate this proof in terms of affinoid algebras similar to certain calculation from
[
Gub
]
, see the Appendix B. Our result grows out of an attempt to give an analytic generalisation of that, mimicking the argument by non-archimedean norm reduction in
[
CLD
, §6.6, 6.9]
, to include more metrics.

1.3.3.
Non-Archimedean differentiability of relative volume

Note that in litteratures such as
[
CL
]
[
KT
]
[
CMc
]
[
BE
]
[
BGM
]
, a related but different distorsion, the relative volume
\vol(L,\phi,\phi+tf)
of two sup norms corresponding to a continuous semipositive metric
\phi
and its (small) perturbation by a continuous function

has been thoroughly studied, the key outcome is a differentiability result

\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\lim\frac{1}{\chi(n)}\log\frac{\det\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n(\phi+tf)}}{\det\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi}}=\int_{X^{\an}}\log\lvert f\rvert\MA(\phi)

where

is the non-Archimedean Monge-Ampère measure constructed in
[
CL
]
and in
[
CLD
]
[
GK
]
. This limit of relative volume does not directly give the limit of distorsion even if we are allowed to take

using approximations, because the limit procedures are different: our limit for
\tau_{n}(L,\phi)
is a diagonal version of the two parameter limit for
\vol_{n}(L,\phi,\phi+tf)

[
Sed

, proving a more general formula, combining the result of differentiability of relative volume in
[
BGM
]
and a method using Okunkov bodies. Our direct proof is independent of these techniques.

We’d also like to point out that a subclass of Shilov finite metrics (over a trivially valued field) has appeared in a recent study of n-A approach to K-stability for polarized varieties
[
BJ2
]
: it consists of semipositive psh metrics whose Monge-Ampère measure is supported on a finite set of

that it suffices to test K-stability of a polarized variety via the
\beta

1.3.4.
Another approach for global HS formula

Over a number ring, the equidistribution limit is calculated in
[
Ni
]
, relying on a study of deformation to the normal cone construction for arithmetic line bundles, combined with normed Newton-Okunkov body techniques. The normed extension property for sections has been established earlier in
[
Cha
]
. They directly work in a global setting, and their results also combine to give the arithmetic HS formula. We remark that both our approach over a local base and
loc. cit.
over a global base consider section algebras equipped with norms, and they are all inspired by the application of Grauert’s construction to Arakelov geometry from
[
Bos
]
.

and
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{2}
. Write
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{\max}=\max\{\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{1},\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{2}\}
, it is the norm having
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
as orthogonal basis with
\lVert e_{i}\rVert_{\max}=\max\{\lVert e_{i}\rVert_{1},\lVert e_{i}\rVert_{2}\}
. Denote by
I_{1}
the subset of
I
for which
\lVert e_{i}\rVert_{1}\geq\lVert e_{i}\rVert_{2}
and by
I_{2}
its complement, and by
E_{1}
the subspace generated by basis vectors in
I_{1}
and similarly
E_{2}
, thus
E=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
is an orthogonal direct sum for all three norms.

For any
v\in E\setminus\{0\}
with
\lVert v\rVert_{\max}=1
, one can write
v=v_{1}+v_{2}
according to the decomposition; thanks to the ultrametric inequality, the desired estimate follows from

\lVert Sv\rVert_{\max}=\lVert Sv_{1}+Sv_{2}\rVert_{\max}\leq\max\{\lVert Sv_{1}\rVert_{1},\lVert Sv_{1}\rVert_{2},\lVert Sv_{2}\rVert_{1},\lVert Sv_{2}\rVert_{2}\}\leq

\max\big\{\sigma(S,\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{1})\max\{\lVert v_{1}\rVert_{1},\lVert v_{2}\rVert_{1}\},\sigma(S,\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{2})\max\{\lVert v_{1}\rVert_{2},\lVert v_{2}\rVert_{2}\}\big\}=\max\{\sigma(S,\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{1}),\sigma(S,\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{2})\}.

∎

2.2.
Shilov boundary

We recall some notions and properties concerning boundaries of a uniform (i.e. spectral) Banach algebra
\mathcal{B}
. Recall that its
spectrum

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
consists of semi-absolute values
\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert_{z}
(also sometimes denoted by
\lvert(\mathord{\cdot})(z)\rvert
) bounded by the spectral norm
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{\Sp}
, equipped with the Gelfand-Berkovich topology. A point
z
is
birational
if the ideal
\ker(\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert_{z})
is
\{0\}
.

Definition 2.8
.

A
boundary
for a uniform Banach algebra
\mathcal{B}
is a subset
\Delta
of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
, such that
\lVert f\rVert_{\Sp}=\sup_{z\in\Delta}\lvert f\rvert_{z}
holds for any
f\in\mathcal{B}
. By construction, one can decompose the sup norm
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert_{\mathcal{B}}
as
\sup_{a\in\Delta}\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert_{a}
. A
minimal boundary
is a boundary that is minimal for the inclusion partial order (denoted by
\partial_{0}\mathcal{B}
). A
Shilov boundary
is a
closed
boundary minimal for inclusion among the set of all closed boundaries (denoted by
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
).

Remark 2.9
.

It is elementary that Shilov boundary exists thanks to Zorn’s lemma: the intersection
\Gamma_{\infty}
of a strictly decreasing chain of closed boundaries
\{\Gamma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a lower bound: for any
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ\setminus\circ\circ}
, there exists
z_{i}\in P(\lvert f\rvert)\cap\Gamma_{i}
for any
i\in I
, and the
I
-net
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in I}
has a cluster point
z_{\infty}
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
by compactness, which is contained in the closed set
\Gamma_{\infty}
by monotonicity (for non-closed boundaries, this cluster point may not be contained in the intersection). By continuity
z_{\infty}\in P(\lvert f\rvert)
, so
\Gamma_{\infty}
is a closed boundary. It is not clear whether minimal boundary exists.

A priori
, Shilov boundary is not necessarily unique. A miminal boundary (if exists) is not necessarily closed nor unique; its topological closure is a Shilov boundary (the one associated to this minimal boundary). Note that these are definitions in
[
EM
]
(in accordance with the general theory of function algebras) instead of those given in
[
Ber
, §2.4]
.

Example 2.10
.

Let
S
be a subset in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
. Its
holomorphic envelope
, denoted by
\mathcal{P}(S)
, is the subset consisting of points
z
such that
\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq\sup_{w\in S}\lvert f(w)\rvert
for all
f\in\mathcal{B}
. It is a closed (hence compact) subset of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
. By construction, it holds that
\Shbd\mathcal{P}(S)\subseteq S
if
S
is closed. One can similarly define holomorphic envelope for any subset in
(\spec B)^{\an}
using functions
f\in B
for any
k
-algebra
B
.

Given two points
z,w
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
(or in
(\spec B)^{\an}
), define an ordering by:
z\prec_{\mathcal{P}}w
if and only if
\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq\lvert f(w)\rvert
for any
f\in\mathcal{B}
(or
f\in B
); by construction, the last condition is equivalent to the condition
\{z\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\{w\})
.

Construction 2.11
.

Recall that the reduction map
\red:\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})\rightarrow\spec(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})
is the one induced by
[\mathcal{B}\xrightarrow{z}\mathscr{H}(z)]\mapsto\ker[\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{z}}\widetilde{\mathscr{H}(z)}]
. Denote by
\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})
the set of generic points for its irreducible components, namely the minimal prime ideals of
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
. For any
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ\setminus\circ\circ}
, denote by
P(\lvert f\rvert)
the closed subset of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
where
\lvert f(\mathord{\cdot})\rvert=1
; via norm reduction, it is equal to
\red^{-1}D(\widetilde{f})
where
D(-)
denotes the principal Zariski open set of
\spec\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
. By construction,
z\in P(\lvert f\rvert)
if and only if
\red(z)\in D(\widetilde{f})
. Moreover, it holds that
P(\lvert f\rvert)\cap P(\lvert g\rvert)=P(\lvert fg\rvert)
if the intersection is not empty.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a uniform Banach algebra, then

(1)

if
\mathfrak{p}
,
\mathfrak{q}
are points in
\spec\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
, with
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{q}
, then for any
z,w
with
\red(z)=\mathfrak{p}
and
\red(w)=\mathfrak{q}
, and any
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
, it holds that
w\in P(\lvert f\rvert)
implies
z\in P(\lvert f\rvert)
;

(2)

if
\Delta
is a boundary,
w\in\Delta
, and
z
is a point such that
\red(z)\subset\red(w)
, then
(\Delta\setminus\{w\})\cup\{z\}
is still a boundary;

(3)

if
\mathcal{B}
has a minimal boundary
\partial_{0}\mathcal{B}
, then there is no generisation relation between any two points in
\red(\partial_{0}\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

The (i) is a consequence of the fact that the Zariski open set
D(\widetilde{f})
is closed under generisation from
\mathfrak{q}
to
\mathfrak{p}
. The (ii) and (iii) are immediate consequences.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a uniform Banach algebra, and
\mathfrak{p}\in(\spec\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})^{\an}
be a prime ideal, then

(1)

if
f,g\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ\setminus\circ\circ}
are elements such that neither
\widetilde{f}
nor
\widetilde{g}
is in
\mathfrak{p}
, then
P(\lvert f\rvert)\cap P(\lvert g\rvert)\neq\emptyset
;

(2)

if
\mathfrak{p}\in\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})
, then
\red^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})=\bigcap_{\widetilde{f}\notin\mathfrak{p}}P(\lvert f\rvert)
is a non-empty closed set; consequently
\red(\mathcal{B})
contains
\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})
;

(3)

the set
\red^{-1}(\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}))=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})}\red^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})
is a boundary;

(4)

any tuple of points in
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})}\red^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})
, viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
, is a boundary.

Proof.

For (1), by construction the product
\widetilde{f}\cdot\widetilde{g}
is not in
\mathfrak{p}
, hence is non-zero; thus it has a lift
h\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ\setminus\circ\circ}
. As
D(\widetilde{f})\cap D(\widetilde{g})=D(\widetilde{h})
, and
\red^{-1}D(\widetilde{h})=P(\lvert h\rvert)
is non-empty, hence so is
\red^{-1}(D(\widetilde{f})\cap D(\widetilde{g}))=P(\lvert f\rvert)\cap P(\lvert g\rvert)
.

For (2), as
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal prime ideal, one can write
\mathfrak{p}
as
\bigcap_{\widetilde{f}\notin\mathfrak{p}}D(\widetilde{f})
, hence
\red^{-1}
is the intersection of closed subsets
\{P(\lvert f\rvert)\}_{\widetilde{f}\notin\mathfrak{p}}
. By (i), this collection of subsets has finite intersection property. Thus by compactness of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
, this intersection is a non-empty closed subset, consequently
\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})\subseteq\red(\mathcal{B})
.

For (3), for any
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ\setminus\circ\circ}
, as
\widetilde{f}\neq 0
, there exists
\mathfrak{p}\in\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})
such that
\widetilde{f}(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
, thus
\lvert f\rvert
takes maximum on
\red^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})
(non-empty by (2)). This shows that
\red^{-1}(\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}))
is a boundary.

For (4), it follows from (3) immediately.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Note that apart from the case where
\mathcal{B}
is a (strict) affinoid algebra, it is not clear whether
\red
is surjective.

Definition 2.15
.

A point
z\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
is
peak-isolated
if
\bigcap_{z\in P(\lvert f\rvert)}P(\lvert f\rvert)=\{z\}
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a uniform Banach algebra, and
z\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
be a point which is peak-isolated, then

(1)

for any (open) neighbourhood
V
of
z
, one can find

such that
P(\lvert f_{V}\rvert)\subseteq V
;

(2)

the point

lies in any Shilov boundary
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

For (1), note that the collection of closed subsets in the intersection has finite intersection property. As
V^{\complement}\cap\bigcap_{z\in P(\lvert f\rvert)}P(\lvert f\rvert)=\emptyset
, by compactness there are a finite number of
f
’s that makes such an intersection still empty. The product of these

’s can be taken as
f_{V}
.

For (2), by construction, for any Shilov boundary
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
, each

should contain a point in it, thus there is a net of points in

converging to

is in this Shilov boundary as it is a closed set.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a uniform Banach algebra. Suppose that it has a Shilov boundary
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
which is a finite set
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, then

(1)

this Shilov boundary is a minimal boundary;

(2)

for any proper subset
J\subset I
, there exists
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
, such that
\lvert f(\xi_{i})\rvert<1
for all
i\in J
and
\lvert f(\xi_{j})\rvert=1
for all
j\in I\setminus J
. If
\mathcal{B}
is graded, then
f
can be chosen to be homogeneous;

(3)

every
\mathfrak{p}_{i}:=\red(\xi_{i})
is a minimal prime, the map
\red
restricts to a bijection
\Shbd\mathcal{B}\to\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})
;

(4)

the set of Shilov boundaries of
\mathcal{B}
is in bijection with points (viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
) in
\prod_{\Shbd\mathcal{B}}\red^{-1}\red(\xi_{i})
;

Proof.

For (1), since
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
is a finite set, any subset of it is closed, hence itself is a minimal boundary by definition.

For (2), it suffices to find an element
\widetilde{f}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
which lies in
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for
i\in J
but not in
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for
i\in I\setminus J
. Since
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
is a minimal boundary, there is no generisation relations, thus
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\setminus\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is non-empty for any
i\notin J
and
j\in J
. By taking product
\bigcap_{j\in J}\mathfrak{p}_{j}\setminus\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is non-empty for any
i\notin J
, by prime avoidance
\bigcap_{j\in J}\mathfrak{p}_{j}\setminus\bigcup_{i\notin J}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is non-empty.

For (3), first show that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
are minimal and
\red
is surjective to
\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})
: otherwise there exists
\mathfrak{p}\in\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{B}})\setminus\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, then any
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{p}
is non-empty, hence so is
\cap\mathfrak{p}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{p}
. Choose an element
\widetilde{f}
and a lift
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
, it holds that
\lvert f(\xi_{i})\rvert<1
for all
i
while
\lVert f\rVert_{\Sp}=1
, a contradiction to the definition of Shilov boundary. The injectivity follows from the minimality of boundary. The (4) is an immediate consequence.

and
[

with similar arguments.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a uniform Banach algebra. Let
f\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ\setminus\circ\circ}
, consider the Banach algebra homomorphism
\sigma:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\}
. Denote by
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{1}
and
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{2}
the spectral (semi-)norms on
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\}
. Then

(1)

the subspace
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\})
is identified with the subset
P(\lvert f\rvert)
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
;

(2)

for any
b\in\mathcal{B}
, it holds that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert f^{n}\cdot b\rVert_{1}=\lVert\sigma(b)\rVert_{2}
;

(3)

the homomorphism
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}[\widetilde{f}^{-1}]\to\widetilde{\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\}}
is injective; if there is a Shilov boundary
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
which is a finite set, then it is also surjective.

Proof.

For (1), it is elementary: the defining homomorphism
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\}
(where the second term is just
\mathcal{B}\{T\}/(Tf-1)
) induces a continuous map
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\})\to\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B})
, the fiber over a point
z
is the spectrum of
\mathscr{H}(z)\{T\}/(Tf(z)-1)
which is non-empty exactly when
\lvert f(z)\rvert\geq 1
.

For (2), since
\lvert f(\mathord{\cdot})\rvert=1
on
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\})
, and
\lvert f(\mathord{\cdot})\rvert<1
on its complement by
(1)
, the formula holds. Note that
\{\lVert f^{n}\cdot b\rVert_{1}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a decreasing sequence.

For (3), for the injectivity: since
\widetilde{1}=\widetilde{f\cdot f^{-1}}=\widetilde{f}\cdot\widetilde{f^{-1}}
, the element
\widetilde{f}
is invertible in
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\}}
, thus there is an induced homomorphism
\iota
from
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}[\widetilde{f}^{-1}]
by universal property. Then the natural homomorphism
\widetilde{\sigma}:\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\}}
is the composition of the localization
\delta:\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}[\widetilde{f}^{-1}]
with
\iota
, and it suffices to show the injectivity of
\widetilde{\sigma}
. Let
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\circ}
be an element such that
\widetilde{\sigma}(\widetilde{b})=0
, namely
\widetilde{\sigma(b)}=0
; thanks to the identity in (2), one knows
\lim\lVert f^{n}b\rVert_{1}=\lVert\sigma(b)\rVert_{2}<1
, thus there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lVert f^{n}b\rVert_{1}<1
, hence
0=\widetilde{f^{n}b}=\widetilde{f}^{n}\widetilde{b}
. This means that
\delta(\widetilde{b})
is
0
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}[\widetilde{f}^{-1}]
.

For the surjectivity under finiteness assumption, for any
c\in\mathcal{B}[f^{-1}]
with
\lVert c\rVert_{2}=1
, write
c=f^{-j}b
with
b\in\mathcal{B}
, then
\lvert b(\mathord{\cdot})\rvert=1
on
P(\lvert f\rvert)
. As
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
is a finite set
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, it holds that the sup norm is a max of finitely many absolute values
\lvert\mathord{\cdot}(\xi_{i})\rvert
, hence
\lVert f^{n}b\rVert_{1}=\max_{i\in I}\{\lvert f(\xi_{i})\rvert^{n}\lvert b(\xi_{i})\rvert\}
, and the set of such values for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}
with at most one accumulation point equal to
0
. From this equality and the discreteness, one can find
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lVert f^{n}b\rVert\leq 1
. Thus
c=f^{-j-n}(f^{n}b)
and hence
\widetilde{f^{n}b}(\widetilde{f})^{-j-n}
maps to
\widetilde{c}
. As
\mathcal{B}[f^{-1}]
is dense in
\mathcal{B}\{f^{-1}\}
, surjectivity holds.
∎

Let

(2)

For (1), if there is only one

\red^{-1}\red{\xi}
is the singleton
\{\xi\}
: otherwise, let
\xi^{\prime}
be a different point in it, one can find

such that
\lvert f\rvert
separates

and
\xi^{\prime}
; but this contradicts
\widetilde{f}\notin\red(\xi)
.

In general, let
\xi
be a point in a Shilov boundary
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
, with reduction
\mathfrak{p}
. One can find

such that

contains

but not any other points in
\Shbd\mathcal{B}
. By Lemma

(3), one has

; thus there is only one point

in

is contained in

map on

2.17
(2).
∎

3.
Equidistribution measure

In this part we define an equidistribution measure and calculate the determinant norm distorsion for semipositive metrics.

3.1.
Normed section algebra and disc domain

We recall some basic constructions from our previous work
[
Fan1
]
encoding a (possibly singular) semipositive metric in a Banach algebra norm (see also
[
BJ1
]
and
[
Reb1
]
for relevant constructions). A metric is
semipositive
if it is a pointwise limit of non-archimedean Fubini-Study metrics; semipositive metrics are always lower semicontinuous in the sense that the function
x\mapsto\lvert s(x)\rvert_{\phi}
is upper semicontinuous (for any section
s
); it is possibly singular in the sense that it could be discontinuous or degenerate at some point.

Construction 3.1
.

Let
X
be an irreducible projective variety with
L
an ample line bundle over it. Denote by
R(L)
the graded algebra of sections, and by
\Cone(L)
the affine cone associated to
(X,L)
, which is just
\spec R(L)
.

Let
\phi
be a metric on
L
. Denote by
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi}
the sup norms induced by
\phi
on
R_{n}(L)
and by
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert_{\phi}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi}
the graded algebra norm on
R(L)
. Denote by
\mathcal{R}(L,\phi)
the
normed section algebra
, which is the Banach algebra completion of
R(L)
with respect to
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert_{\phi}
. Its spectrum
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R}(L,\phi))
is the
disc domain
of
(X,L,\phi)
(the unit disc bundle of
(L,\phi)^{\vee}
with zero section contracted). Conversely, any graded algebra norm
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert
on
R(L)
defines a (possibly singular) semipositive metric
\mathcal{P}(\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert)
as the limit of
\frac{1}{n}\FS(\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n})
. It is a (possibly discontinuous but non-degenerate) metric if and only if
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert
is bounded below by
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert_{\psi}
for some continuous metric
\psi
, or equivalently from the geometric point of view, the associated disc domain contains a neighbourhood of
\boldsymbol{0}
.

Construction 3.2
.

Denote by
p:\V(L)\to X
the tautological projection from the total space of
L^{\vee}
and by
\mathcal{M}(\phi)
the image under
p^{\an}
of the dual unit disc bundle
\overline{\mathbb{D}}^{\vee}(L,\phi)
of
\phi
. The envelope metric
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
on
L
is the limit of Fubini-Study metrics
\frac{1}{n}\FS(\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert_{n\phi})
. One can show that
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert_{\mathcal{P}(\phi)}
coincides with
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert_{\phi}
hence the corresponding Banach algebras and spectra are the same. Geometrically, the envelope metric can also be characterised by identifying the holomorphic envelope
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M}(\phi))
with its disc domain
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{P}(\phi))=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R}(L,\phi))
. For any
x\in X^{\an}
, denote by
\gamma_{\phi}(x)\in\mathcal{M}(\phi)
the Gauss point of the dual unit disc over
x
; view
s
as a function on
\V(L)
, one has

\lvert s(x)\rvert_{\phi}=\lvert s(\gamma(x,\phi))\rvert
.

Construction 3.3
.

A norm
\lVert\mathord{\cdot}\rVert
on
R_{{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}}
is
graded
if
\lVert\cdot\rVert=\lVert\cdot\rVert^{+}
, where the later is the norm defined by

4.1.
Locally affinoid Banach algebra and semipositive piecewise linear metrics

There are two natural classes of semipositive metrics whose Shilov boundary consists of finitely many birational points. Firstly (formal) model (semipositive) metrics have this property, and there’s a characterization for such metrics in terms of the Banach algebra

[
GK

Recall that the spectrum of a Banach algebra is called locally affinoid, if it is a
G

[

.

Proposition 4.1
.

If

is a metric whose disc domain

\phi
is Shilov finite.

Proof.

By definition, this domain admits a finite cover by affinoid domains

, so the Shilov points for
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R}(L,\phi))
is contained

∎

Recall that a formal model

over

defines a model metric

[

]

The followings are equivalent for a metric

That

Construction 4.4

Let

(0,\dots,0)
of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\lvert I\rvert}

Construction 4.5
.

Fix a homogeneous element

, this gives a coordinization
(\lvert s(z_{1})\rvert,\dots,\lvert s(z_{m})\rvert)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m}

(we omit notating

\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert(\underline{t})

\max_{i\in I}\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert_{z_{i}(t_{i})}

\mathcal{P}(\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert(\underline{t}))

Denote by

Proposition 4.6
.

The map
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is continuous.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.17
, for any homogeneous

, the following equation holds

L_{\phi(\underline{t})}(\log\lvert f\rvert):=\frac{1}{\chi(n)}\log\penalty\ \lVert\bigwedge^{\chi(n)}(\cdot s)\rVert_{\hom,\phi(\underline{t})}=\sum\lambda_{i}(\underline{t})\log\lvert f(z_{i}(t_{i}))\rvert.

We shall put in several

as test functions, to form a linear system and to solve it. By Proposition

, the second member (LHS) is continuous in
\underline{t}
, it suffices to choose carefully
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that the matrix

formed by
\{\log\lvert f_{j}(z_{i}(t_{i}))\rvert\}
is (invertible and) continuous in
\underline{t}
. Note that these choices can depend on
\underline{t}
and it suffices to have local continuity near any given
\underline{t}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For any
\underline{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\lvert I\rvert}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
, and any
i\in I
, denote by
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
the prime ideal corresponding to
z_{i}(t_{i})
via the norm reduction construction. One can rearrange

in an order such that if
j<i
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
; then there exists
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
such that for any
i\in I
,
\lvert f_{i}(z_{j}(t_{j}))\rvert=1
for all
j<i
and
\lvert f_{i}(z_{i}(t_{i}))\rvert<1
.

Proof.

Take
I_{1}
such that the corresponding ideals are the maximal elements for the inclusion partial order, then take
I_{2}
whose ideals are the maximal ones among the rest, and so on to get
I_{n}
; arrange
I
in the order
(I_{n},I_{n-1},\dots,I_{1})
, the elements inside each

can be arranged in any order; this achieves the goal.

By the construction, the prime
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is not contained in any
\mathfrak{p}_{j}
for

, hence it is not contained in the union
\bigcup_{j<i}\mathfrak{p}_{j}
by prime avoidance, so one can find an element

in

satisfies the requirement.
∎

It follows from the lemma that the matrix
M(\underline{t}):=\{\log\lvert f_{i}(z_{j}(t_{j}))\rvert\}_{i,j\in I^{2}}
is upper triangular with non-zero elements on the diagonal; hence for all

sufficiently near
\underline{t}
, the matrix

is still invertible, and is continuous in
\underline{t}^{\prime}
. Since one has the linear system
\underline{\lambda}(\underline{t}^{\prime})\cdot M(\underline{t}^{\prime})=L_{\phi(\underline{t}^{\prime})}(\log\lvert f_{1}\rvert,\dots,\log\lvert f_{n}\rvert)
and the right hand side is continuous in

.

∎

Proposition 4.8
.

The map
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is surjective. Thus for any
\underline{\lambda}\in\Delta_{I}
, there exists
\underline{t}\in\Delta_{I}
that solves the equation
\mu_{\eq}(\phi(\underline{t}))=\sum\lambda_{i}\delta_{z_{i}}
. In addition, if all
\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert_{z_{i}}
are bounded from below as graded algebra norms, then the solutions are non-degenerate (but possibly singular) metrics.

Proof.

One argues by induction on the number of points in Shilov boundary, using the above continuity property and the mapping degree theorem. For each
i\in I
and
t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, as the domain
\mathcal{P}(\{z_{i}(t_{i})\})
has Shilov boundary
\{z_{i}(t_{i})\}
, the equidistribution measure of the metric associated to
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert(\iota_{\{i\},I}(t_{i}))
is supported on
\{z_{i}(t_{i})\}
. Thus
\boldsymbol{\mu}
restricts to a map
\Delta_{\{i\}}\to\Delta_{\{i\}}
.

Similarly, for any fixed pair
J=\{i,j\}\subset I
, and any
(t_{i},t_{j})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\setminus(0,0)
, the domain
\mathcal{P}(\{z_{i}(t_{i}),z_{j}(t_{j})\})
has a Shilov boundary contained in
\{z_{i}(t_{i}),z_{j}(t_{j})\}
, the equidistribution measure of the metric associated to
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert(\iota_{\{i,j\},I}(t_{i},t_{j}))
is supported on
\{z_{i}(t_{i}),z_{j}(t_{j})\}
. Thus map
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is restricted to a self-map
\Delta_{J}\to\Delta_{J}
, which is continuous and is surjective when further restricted on the boundary
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
. Hence the restriction to
\Delta_{J}
is surjective by the mapping degree theorem.

Using induction, the same arguement shows that, for any
J\subset I
, by construction
\boldsymbol{\mu}
restricts to a self map on
\Delta^{m}_{J}
, which is surjective when further restricted to its boundaries
\partial\Delta^{m}_{J}
by the induction hypothesis. Therefore by the mapping degree theorem, the restriction of
\boldsymbol{\mu}
to
\Delta^{m}_{J}
is also surjective.

The last assertion is immediate since for any
\underline{t}
, one can choose
i\in I
such that
t_{i}\neq 0
, and the graded algebra norm
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert(\underline{t})
is bounded from below by
\vvvert\mathord{\cdot}\vvvert(\iota_{\{i\},I}(t_{i})))
.

∎

Let

For any

If
\phi
is Fubini-Study, then under our assumptions on the base field (see §1.4), the Banach section algebra
\mathcal{R}_{\phi}
is an affinoid algebra. The analytic localization procedure can be performed simply in a finite morphism of affinoid algebras, where all Shilov points are organized in a fixed global Noether normalization. In this case the determinant distorsion can be expressed as the norm in the field arithmetic for such normalization.

Construction B.1
.

[
BGR
, §6]
As
\mathcal{R}_{\phi}
is a graded affinoid algebra, by Noether normalization, one can find a finite morphism of affinoid algebras from the Tate algebra
\nu:\mathcal{T}_{d+1}\to\mathcal{R}_{\phi}
which preserves the grading. We shall omit the subscripts and denote these two Banach algebras by
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{R}
.

The field extension
\Frac(\mathcal{T})\to\Frac(\mathcal{R})
is finite, of degre
e
. On
\Frac(\mathcal{R})
there are finitely many inequivalent absolute values
\{\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert_{a}\}
that are extensions of the Gauss absolute value
\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert_{\gamma}
on
\mathcal{T}
. The inequivalent absolute values are in fact indexed by the irreducible components of
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
, hence by
\eta(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}})
, which is the set of Shilov points. Then there is a decomposition
\lVert f\rVert_{\Sp,\mathcal{R}}=\max_{a\in\Sh}\lvert f\rvert_{a}
.

Construction B.2
.

[
BGR
, §3]
For any
a
, denote by
\mathcal{R}_{a}
and
\Frac(\mathcal{R})_{a}
the completions with respect to
\lvert\mathord{\cdot}\rvert_{a}
, then the induced extension of complete valued fields
\nu_{a}:\Frac(\mathcal{T})\to\Frac(\mathcal{R})_{a}
is still finite, of degree
e_{a}
, and one has
\sum e_{a}=e
. Denote by
\nu
and by
\nu_{a}
the (algebra) norm maps
\Frac(\mathcal{R})\to\Frac(\mathcal{T})
and
\Frac(\mathcal{R})_{a}\to\Frac(\mathcal{T})
. The
\Frac(\mathcal{T})
-module
\oplus_{a}\Frac(\mathcal{R})_{a}
is finite. There are equalities
\nu(f)=\prod_{a}\nu_{a}(f)
and
\lvert\nu_{a}(f)\rvert_{\gamma}=\lvert f\rvert_{a}^{e_{a}}
, thus a decomposition
\lvert\nu(f)\rvert_{\gamma}=\prod_{a}\lvert f\rvert_{a}^{e_{a}}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{a}
the normalization of the image of
\mathcal{R}
in
\Frac(\mathcal{R})_{a}
. Then
\oplus_{a}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{a}
is a finite
\mathcal{T}
-module; moreover it is still graded. The
k
-codimension of
\mathcal{R}_{\leq n}
in
(\oplus_{a}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{a})_{\leq n}
is of order
O(n^{d})
, with
\lim\frac{1}{\upsilon(n)}\dim_{k}(\oplus_{a}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{a})_{\leq n}=e_{a}
where
\upsilon(n)=\sum_{i\leq n}\chi(i)
is the
k
-linear dimension of
\mathcal{R}_{\leq n}
.

Remark B.3
.

As in the general case, one can calculate the limit of the ‘determinant norm’ of the
k
-linear map
(s\cdot)
on
\mathcal{R}
, namely
\frac{1}{\chi(n)}\log\lVert\bigwedge^{\chi(n)}(\cdot s)\rVert_{\hom,\phi}
, on
\oplus_{a}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{a}
instead of on
\mathcal{R}
thanks to Proposition
2.5
and using Proposition
2.1
. The result being
\sum_{a}\frac{e_{a}}{e}\log\lvert s\rvert_{a}
, is in fact equal to the norm
\log\penalty\ \lvert\nu(s)\rvert_{\gamma}^{1/e}
. Note that the algebraic norm
\nu(s)
is just the determinant of the operator
(s\cdot)
, but viewed as a
\Frac(\mathcal{T})
-linear map on the vector space
\Frac(\mathcal{R})
. Thus this is a normed/metrized/arithmetic version of the formula expressing the algebraic Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity by means of Noether normalization.

This result follows from the viewpoint that for model metrics
\phi
, the measure
\MA(\phi)
(defined using differential calculus) has a nice local algebraic incarnation, see
[
CLD
, §6]
and
[
GK
, §10]
; also, it is compatible with the viewpoint in
[
BE
, §8]
and
[
CM
]
, treating Deligne’s metrized intersection pairing
\langle(L,\phi),\dots,(L,\phi)\rangle
as a norm functor on the affinoid Banach section algebra of
(L,\phi)
and relating to the Monge-Ampère measure
\MA(\phi)
.

This is a consequence of the ‘regularity’ of the metric (i.e. being Fubini-Study, or coming from an integral model); for general Shilov finite metrics, we lose the possibility of affinoid Noether normalization, even locally around each Shilov point, hence the argument of
[
CLD
, §6]
is not directly applicable. Thus our effort in the main body of the article is to relax such a ‘global regularity’ assumption.

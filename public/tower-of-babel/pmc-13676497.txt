Short-term surgical outcomes of robotic versus laparoscopic radical gastrectomy for gastric cancer: a propensity score-matched retrospective cohort study
Authors: Dongdong Zhang, Ning Ning, Lin Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRobotic gastrectomy (RG) is increasingly used for gastric cancer, but real-world comparative data from Chinese institutions during the early phase of robotic program implementation remain limited. We retrospectively identified patients who underwent radical gastrectomy between January 2015 and October 2025. The primary comparison included 54 patients who underwent RG and 162 who underwent Laparoscopic gastrectomy (LG). After exclusion of patients with missing propensity-score covariates, 53 RG and 143 LG patients were eligible for matching. One-to-two nearest-neighbor matching was performed using a caliper of 0.2 standard deviations of the logit of the propensity score, yielding 45 RG and 88 LG patients. The principal outcomes was the number of lymph nodes retrieved. After matching, all included covariates were balanced. The median number of lymph nodes retrieved did not differ significantly between RG and LG (30 [interquartile range, 25-42] vs 34 [27-47]; 95% Confidence Interval [CI]: -10.0–2.0, p = 0.181). RG was associated with a shorter postoperative length of stay (16 vs 18 days; 95% CI: -4.1–0.1, p = 0.016) and fewer positive lymph nodes (0 vs 2; 95% CI: -3.3–0.7, p = 0.007). Median operative time was 310 minutes for RG and 300 minutes for LG (95% CI: -20.5–40.5, p = 0.072). CUSUM analysis revealed a V-shaped recovery for LN retrieval, and confirmed operative efficiency convergence to the LG benchmark by the late phase (300 min, p = 0.053), together indicating a trend toward improved performance during the learning process. RG and LG achieved comparable lymph-node yields, while RG was associated with a shorter postoperative hospital stay. Exploratory sequential analyses suggested improvement in operative efficiency but did not establish a proficiency threshold. Introduction

Gastric cancer is the fifth most common malignancy and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, with a particularly high burden in East Asia and China [1]. Surgical resection with D2 lymphadenectomy remains the cornerstone of curative treatment for resectable disease. Adequate lymph-node retrieval is an established indicator of surgical quality and is associated with accurate staging and long-term oncological outcomes [2, 3].

The introduction of laparoscopic gastrectomy (LG), first reported by Kitano et al. in 1994 [4], transformed the surgical management of gastric cancer. Large prospective studies, including CLASS-01 [5], KLASS-01 [6], and JLSSG0901 [7], established the short-term safety and perioperative benefits of laparoscopic compared with open gastrectomy; subsequent analyses supported comparable long-term oncological outcomes in appropriately selected patients with stage I-III disease [18–20]. Three-dimensional visualization was later introduced to overcome the limited depth perception of conventional two-dimensional laparoscopy. A randomized trial of laparoscopic distal gastrectomy reported improved stereoscopic visualization and a shorter operative time with three-dimensional imaging [8].

Robotic platforms, most notably the da Vinci system, represent a further development in minimally invasive gastrectomy. Potential advantages include stable three-dimensional visualization, articulating instruments that facilitate dissection in anatomically confined areas, tremor filtration, and improved surgeon ergonomics [9]. Meta-analyses and registry-based studies suggest that robotic gastrectomy (RG) provides lymph-node yields comparable to those of LG and may reduce blood loss, conversion, and time to oral intake [10, 11]. Nevertheless, much of the evidence, including two randomized trials [21, 22] and a multicenter Chinese cohort of 5,402 patients [13], comes from high-volume East Asian centers with established robotic programs. Evidence from institutions in the early phase of adoption remains limited.

The temporal concentration of our institutional RG experience (48 of 54 procedures [88.9%] were performed in 2025) provides an opportunity to examine implementation during program expansion. However, the overlapping use of two-dimensional laparoscopy, three-dimensional laparoscopy, and robotics across different periods introduces substantial confounding, particularly for postoperative length of stay and other outcomes affected by perioperative practice. Neoadjuvant therapy was included as a measured covariate but cannot control for changes in ERAS protocols, discharge criteria, anesthesia, team experience, or other time-related factors. Propensity score matching (PSM) was prespecified to address measured baseline differences, including the higher use of neoadjuvant therapy in the RG era, although residual confounding by calendar time could not be eliminated. We therefore interpret matched associations cautiously and do not attribute them causally to surgical approach.

We compared short-term outcomes after RG and LG using prespecified PSM and performed descriptive cumulative sum (CUSUM) analyses of the RG learning curve. We asked whether lymph-node yield differed between matched RG and LG cohorts and whether postoperative length of stay or operative time varied by approach.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This single-center retrospective cohort study was designed and reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement [12]. Ethical approval was obtained from Biomedical Ethics Committee of Peking University International Hospital (Approval No. 2026-KY-0059-01) with a waiver of individual written informed consent, consistent with regulations for retrospective, de-identified medical record analyses. The study was registered retrospectively in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2600131011; http://www.chictr.org.cn).

Patient selection

Patients were eligible if they (1) underwent radical distal, total, or proximal gastrectomy between January 2015 and October 2025; (2) had histologically confirmed primary gastric adenocarcinoma; (3) underwent RG (da Vinci Si or Xi), two-dimensional LG, three-dimensional LG, or open gastrectomy (OG); (4) had clinical or pathological stage I-III disease according to the eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system; and (5) had an available operative record.

Patients were excluded if they underwent emergency surgery, palliative resection, or diagnostic laparoscopy alone; had remnant gastric cancer or another synchronous primary malignancy; or had missing values for all three key outcomes (operative time, lymph-node count, and postoperative length of stay).

Of 356 patients in the institutional gastric cancer surgery database, 298 met the criteria for radical gastrectomy. The primary matched analysis included 53 RG and 143 LG patients with complete propensity-score covariates. Based on previous literature, the standard deviation of the number of lymph nodes dissected was approximately 15 nodes in both the robotic and laparoscopic groups [24]. To detect a clinically meaningful difference of 10% (Δ ≥ 3 nodes) with a two-sided alpha of 0.05 and a power of 80% (β = 0.20), a two-sample t-test required about 50 patients per group. In our center, the RG group and the LG group met this statistical requirement. After propensity score matching, the two groups will be matched at a 1:2 ratio.

Propensity score matching

Propensity scores were estimated by multivariable logistic regression, with surgical approach (RG = 1; LG = 0) as the dependent variable. Seven prespecified covariates were included: age, sex, total gastrectomy, proximal gastrectomy, pathological T category (T3-T4 vs. T1-T2), diabetes mellitus, and receipt of neoadjuvant therapy. Calendar year was considered but excluded because its near-complete separation by surgical approach precluded meaningful matching. Neoadjuvant therapy was retained as a clinically relevant, albeit incomplete, proxy for treatment era.

One-to-two nearest-neighbor matching was performed on the logit of the propensity score using a caliper of 0.2 standard deviations (absolute caliper, 0.14). Covariate balance was evaluated using standardized mean differences (SMDs), with an absolute SMD < 0.10 considered acceptable. Propensity scores were estimated with scikit-learn, and matching used a custom greedy algorithm in Python 3.11. Unless otherwise stated, comparative analyses were performed in the matched cohort.

Outcome definitions

The principal outcome was the total number of lymph nodes retrieved. Secondary outcomes were operative time (from incision to skin closure), postoperative length of stay (from surgery to discharge), number of positive lymph nodes, and R0 resection (microscopically negative margins).

Learning curve analysis

CUSUM analysis was performed to characterize the RG learning curve with respect to lymph node retrieval. The target performance level was defined as median lymph node count of unmatched LG cohort (34 nodes), representing the overall institutional LG benchmark. For each sequential RG case (ordered by surgery date), the CUSUM value was calculated as cumulative sum of difference between observed LN count and 34. A positive CUSUM indicates performance above the target; a negative CUSUM indicates below-target performance. This descriptive CUSUM was used to visually characterize temporal trends in lymph node retrieval without formal statistical decision boundaries. A parallel CUSUM analysis was performed for operative time, using the LG median (300 min) as the reference target, to assess temporal trends in RG operative efficiency. An early vs. late phase comparison (cases 1–27 vs. 28–54) was additionally performed for all three outcome domains (LN retrieval, operative time, postoperative hospital stays) using Mann-Whitney U test. The analysis was conducted on all 54 RG cases, ordered sequentially by surgery date. These analyses were not risk-adjusted for case complexity, gastrectomy type, neoadjuvant treatment, or calendar time and had no formal decision boundaries. They were used only to describe observed sequential patterns.

Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

Pre-specified subgroup analyses included: (1) RG versus 3D-LG only (the most contemporary laparoscopic comparator), stratified by gastrectomy type (distal vs. total); and (2) comparison of the early RG learning curve period (cases 1–27) versus later cases (28–54) for operative time. Given the very limited complication documentation in the RG group (6/54 patients with records), all complication analyses for RG are presented as descriptive only.

Statistical methods

Continuous variables are reported as median (interquartile range [IQR]) given non-normal distributions (confirmed by Shapiro-Wilk test). Group comparisons used Mann-Whitney U test (two groups) or Kruskal-Walli’s test (four groups). Categorical variables are reported as frequencies and proportions; group comparisons used chi-square or Fisher exact tests. Statistical significance was set at two-tailed p < 0.05. All analyses were performed using Python 3.11 (scipy, statsmodels) and SPSS 27.0.

Results

Study population and temporal trends

Overall study cohort comprised 298 patients undergoing radical gastrectomy, distributed among RG (n = 54, 18.2%), 2D-LG (n = 80, 26.8%), 3D-LG (n = 82, 27.5%), and open gastrectomy (OG, n = 82, 27.5%) groups. Temporal evolution of surgical approach utilization (Fig. 3) reveals a progressive transition from OG dominance (2015–2021) to 2D-LG adoption (2015–2023) and subsequent introduction of 3D-LG (2022–2024). RG program commenced with isolated cases in 2017 (n = 1) and 2023 (n = 1), followed by four cases in 2024, before expanding substantially in 2025 (n = 48, 88.9% of all RG cases). This temporal concentration reflects the institutional decision to formally establish robotic gastrectomy program in 2024–2025. In the unmatched four-group analysis (Table 1), neoadjuvant therapy use was significantly higher in the RG group (37.0%) than in LG (21.0%) or OG (12.2%) groups (p = 0.007), confirming the expected era-related confounding that necessitated PSM.

Propensity score matching and balance assessment

After applying missing-data exclusion criteria, 53 RG and 143 LG patients were available for PSM. Before matching, meaningful imbalances were present in neoadjuvant therapy use (SMD 0.539), age (SMD 0.276), and gastrectomy type distribution (SMD 0.178–0.202) (Fig. 1, panel B). Following 1:2 nearest-neighbor PSM with caliper 0.14, 45 RG patients were matched to 88 LG patients (matched cohort n = 133). Post-matching balance was excellent across all seven covariates, with all SMDs < 0.1 (range 0.021–0.070; Table 2). Propensity score distributions of the matched subgroups showed substantial overlap (Fig. 1, panel A), confirming the validity of common support assumption.

Primary outcome 1: lymph node retrieval

In PSM-matched cohort, median number of lymph nodes retrieved was 30 (IQR 25–42) in RG group versus 34 (IQR 27–47) in LG group (95% Confidence Interval [CI]: -10.0–2.0, p = 0.181; Table 3; Fig. 2). The numerical difference of four lymph nodes did not reach statistical significance, confirming non-inferior lymphadenectomy for RG relative to PSM-matched LG. This finding was consistent with unmatched analysis (median 29 vs. 34; p = 0.149), suggesting that observed difference is largely attributable to learning curve rather than to systematic differences in patient selection. In the subgroup comparison of RG versus 3D-LG (the most contemporary comparator), lymph node retrieval was similarly comparable (30 vs. 32; p = 0.869).

Secondary outcomes

Operative time in the PSM-matched cohort was 310 min (IQR 279–371) for RG and 300 min (IQR 250–340) for LG (95% CI: -20.5–40.5, p = 0.072). Although the difference did not achieve statistical significance after PSM (in contrast to the unmatched analysis, p = 0.026), the numerical excess of approximately 10–20 min for RG is consistent with a transitional learning curve. Postoperative hospital stay was significantly shorter for RG (median 16 days, IQR 14–19) compared with LG (18 days, IQR 15–23; 95% CI: -4.1–0.1, p = 0.016), representing a clinically meaningful reduction of two days. This advantage persisted in the comparison of RG versus 3D-LG (16 vs. 18 days; p = 0.022).

Median positive lymph node count was significantly lower in the RG group (0, IQR 0–2) versus LG (2, IQR 0–6; 95% CI: -3.3–0.7, p = 0.007), likely reflecting the higher proportion of neoadjuvant therapy recipients in the RG group, even after PSM balancing of neoadjuvant use, and/or potential era-related stage migration; perioperative SOX has become the standard of care for locally advanced disease in China following the RESOLVE trial [17]. R0 resection rates were 100% in both matched groups (p = 1.000), confirming equivalent oncological resection margin quality.

CUSUM learning curve analysis: lymph node retrieval and operative time

Multi-dimensional CUSUM learning curve analysis across all 54 sequentially ordered RG cases is presented in Figs. 4 A–D. For lymph node retrieval (Fig. 4C), the CUSUM curve begins with an above-target peak at case #1, then descends to a nadir at case #14 (CUSUM = − 70), the global minimum of performance relative to the LG benchmark of 34 nodes. The curve subsequently recovers in a characteristic V-shaped trajectory, rising progressively through the early-to-late phase transition and entering sustained positive territory from case 28 onwards (peak CUSUM ≈ + 148 in the late phase). Phase comparison (Fig. 4F) confirms this recovery: early-phase median LN retrieval was 29 nodes (85% of LG benchmark) vs. late-phase 30 nodes (88%; early vs. late p = 0.876). The absence of statistical significance between phases reflects inter-individual variability rather than a systematic decline, and the V-shaped CUSUM trajectory confirms directional improvement. For operative time (Fig. 4E), the CUSUM began at case #1 (CUSUM = 220 min, reflecting an initial operative time of 520 min against the LG median of 300 min), rose continuously throughout the program, and reached a plateau from case 28 onwards as the rate of increase decelerated. Late-phase median operative time (300 min) equaled the LG benchmark, compared with 335 min in the early phase (p = 0.053), indicating near-complete efficiency convergence within approximately 27 cases. Postoperative hospital stay remained consistently below the LG benchmark (19 days) throughout both phases (early 17 days, late 16 days; p = 0.774), demonstrating that RG recovery quality was maintained independent of the operative learning curve. These findings are compatible with a possible trend toward improved operative efficiency, but do not establish a validated learning-curve transition or mastery after 27 cases. The arbitrary phase split and terms such as “V-shaped recovery” are not used to claim a proficiency threshold.

Discussion

This PSM-matched retrospective cohort study systematically compared short-term surgical outcomes between RG and LG at a Chinese tertiary center during the critical period of robotic program establishment. The principal findings are: (1) The matched analysis found no statistically significant difference in lymph-node yield (30 vs. 34; p = 0.181), but it was not designed to test non-inferiority; (2) RG was associated with a significantly shorter postoperative hospital stay (p = 0.016), representing a clinically meaningful recovery advantage; (3) operative time was numerically, but not statistically, longer for RG after PSM (p = 0.072), reflecting the early learning curve; and (4) CUSUM analysis revealed a V-shaped recovery for LN retrieval (nadir at case #14, CUSUM = − 70; late-phase recovery above LG benchmark), and confirmed operative efficiency convergence to the LG benchmark by the late phase (300 min, p = 0.053), together indicating a trend toward improved performance during the learning process.

The complication findings cannot be estimated reliably from the supplied materials. The single Grade IIIa event in the RG group represents an incidence consistent with published robotic gastrectomy series [10, 11, 13] and reflects the expected complication profile of an early-phase program. These updated findings now enable a complete and definitive assessment of RG’s short-term outcomes, and substantially strengthen the conclusions regarding RG’s clinical value.

RG lymphadenectomy in our matched analysis should be considered against two large contemporary meta-analyses. Du, R. et al. [10], synthesizing 86 eligible studies and 68,755 patients, reported a significantly greater number of retrieved lymph nodes in RG and LG (p < 0.001). Chen et al. [11], pooling 19 comparative studies with 5,953 patients, found no significant difference in lymph node yield. Our institution-specific data add real-world experience from a Chinese center at the introductory phase to this body of evidence. The four-node numerical gap (30 vs. 34) observed in our matched cohort therefore does not reproduce the pooled advantage reported by Du, R. et al., and is more plausibly explained by published learning curve data, in which early-program RG cases systematically retrieve fewer lymph nodes than established LG series before reaching equivalence typically after 20–50 procedures [14, 16, 23]. Positive-node count is influenced by tumor biology, baseline stage, and treatment response and should not be interpreted as an outcome of surgical quality. This difference between two groups may also indicate residual imbalance despite matching.

The shorter postoperative stay observed for RG (16 vs. 18 days; p = 0.016) is an association that may reflect changes in discharge practice, ERAS implementation, anesthesia, perioperative management, or surgical-team experience over time. Despite longer operative durations, robotic instrumentation likely enables more precise tissue handling, reduced inadvertent organ trauma, and superior hemostasis, factors that collectively facilitate earlier return of bowel function and fewer minor postoperative complications, even before the technical learning curve is fully negotiated. This proposed mechanism should, however, be interpreted with caution. In a randomized comparison, Park et al. [15] found that robot-assisted distal gastrectomy did not reduce surgical stress relative to laparoscopic distal gastrectomy: postoperative C-reactive protein and interleukin-6 levels were in fact lower after laparoscopic surgery, operative time was significantly longer (218 vs. 140 min; p < 0.001), and costs were substantially higher. The dissociation between operative duration and recovery speed observed in our cohort therefore requires prospective confirmation before it can be attributed to the robotic platform itself rather than to concurrent improvements in perioperative care. Because our RG and LG groups were treated in largely non-overlapping eras, residual temporal confounding remains a plausible alternative explanation for the observed length-of-stay advantage.

Transition from non-significant (p = 0.072 after PSM) to significant (p = 0.026 in the unmatched analysis) operative time differences illustrates critical importance of PSM in this dataset. Neoadjuvant therapy, a proxy for more advanced disease and more complex surgical cases, was profoundly imbalanced before matching (37.0% RG vs. 14.7% LG; p = 0.001), and its successful equalization via PSM (26.7% vs. 23.9%; p = 0.832) was essential for valid inference. This finding underscores why PSM was pre-specified as primary analytical approach in our study protocol: the unmistakable temporal confounding associated with phased adoption of different surgical platforms at a single institution renders unadjusted comparisons potentially misleading.

Surgery year itself, the most powerful confounder, was deliberately excluded from propensity model, as its near-perfect collinearity with surgical approach assignment would have made matching theoretically impossible. Instead, neoadjuvant therapy was used as a clinically meaningful proxy that captures treatment-era effects while preserving sufficient overlap for matching.

CUSUM learning curve analysis provides a structured, quantitative characterization of our institutional RG program trajectory. The LN retrieval CUSUM exhibits a V-shaped recovery pattern, from nadir at case #14 (CUSUM = − 70) to sustained above-target performance in the late phase (peak ≈ + 148), representing the archetypal “dip-and-recovery” trajectory documented in the robotic surgery learning curve literature, wherein early performance decrements give way to accelerating competence as procedural familiarity accumulates [14, 16]. Concurrently, the operative time CUSUM plateaus from case 28, with late-phase median (300 min) converging to the LG benchmark (p = 0.053), corroborating the LN-retrieval CUSUM in placing the critical learning transition between cases 14 and 27. Published CUSUM-based learning curve analyses for robotic gastrectomy have identified stabilization points ranging from case 21 to 50, depending on the endpoint and institutional context [14, 16, 23]. Our program, currently at 54 cases, has completed initial LN-retrieval learning curve stabilization by the late phase, with operative efficiency achieving LG-equivalent levels by case 27. Prospective tracking beyond 54 cases will be essential to confirm sustained above-benchmark LN performance, complete operative time convergence, and to evaluate outcomes for more complex procedures such as total gastrectomy.

Limitations

This study has several important limitations that must be clearly acknowledged. First, RG cases were concentrated in 2025, whereas LG cases largely came from earlier periods. Calendar year could not be included in PSM because of near-complete separation. While the use of neoadjuvant therapy as a clinical proxy for treatment era is justified, residual temporal confounding from unmeasured period effects (e.g., improvements in perioperative care protocols, anesthesia practices, and postoperative recovery pathways over the 10-year study period) cannot be fully excluded. Second, this is a single-center study, and institutional factors, including surgical team composition, nursing support for robotic setup, and equipment availability, may not be representative of the broader Chinese surgical landscape. Third, PSM reduced measured baseline differences, but matching does not eliminate unmeasured confounding. The analysis retained 45 of 53 eligible RG patients and 88 of 143 eligible LG patients; consequently, its target population is the matched subset with common support. The model also included pathological T category, a post-treatment variable that may be influenced by neoadjuvant therapy and surgical/pathological factors. Re-estimation using preoperative clinical stage would be preferable. Finally, long-term oncological outcomes (overall survival, disease-free survival, recurrence patterns) are not reported in this analysis. The RG cohort has a median follow-up of less than 12 months, precluding meaningful survival analysis at this time.

Conclusions

In this retrospective matched cohort, no statistically significant difference in lymph-node yield was observed between RG and LG. RG was associated with a shorter postoperative stay, but the strong calendar-time separation prevents attribution of this finding to the robotic platform. Incomplete complication ascertainment precludes a reliable comparative complication estimate. Exploratory sequential analyses suggested possible improvement in operative efficiency but did not establish a proficiency threshold. These findings support the feasibility of RG implementation while emphasizing the need for contemporaneous prospective comparisons, complete complication ascertainment, and long-term oncological follow-up.

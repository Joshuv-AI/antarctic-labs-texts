Hepatic arterial infusion pump chemotherapy combined with systemic therapy versus systemic therapy alone as induction treatment for initially unresectable colorectal liver metastases: a study protocol of the randomised controlled trial – PUMP-IT RCT

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn patients with colorectal cancer liver metastases (CRLM), resection of the primary tumour and local treatment of liver metastases may offer a chance of cure or long-term survival. Most patients however present with unresectable CRLM. Hepatic arterial infusion pump (HAIP) therapy with floxuridine (FUDR) combined with systemic therapy (SYST) could enhance conversion to resectable CRLM, reduce recurrences and therefore, probably prolong survival. The aim of this trial is to investigate whether intensifying induction treatment with HAIP-SYST prolongs survival in chemotherapy-naive patients with initially unresectable CRLM as compared to systemic therapy alone.MethodsThis multicentre, phase 3 randomised controlled trial will be conducted in 20 Dutch liver centres and 306 patients will be included. Eligible participants are chemotherapy-naive patients with synchronous unresectable CRLM without extrahepatic disease and with suitable hepatic arterial anatomy to allow for potential pump placement. Unresectability is assessed by a liver expert panel of radiologists and surgeons based on predefined criteria. Patients randomised to the control arm receive induction systemic therapy according to standard clinical practice. Patients in the experimental arm undergo surgery for pump placement and resection of the primary tumour before start of HAIP-SYST. Treatment response and conversion to local treatment of CRLM is evaluated by the expert panel every 8—9 weeks during induction treatment. Primary endpoint is overall survival (OS). Secondary endpoints include progression-free survival (PFS), hepatic PFS, conversion-to-resection rates, R0/R1 resection rates, radiological and pathological response, surgical complications, toxicity, quality of life and cost-effectiveness.DiscussionThe PUMP-IT RCT is the first prospective randomised trial to investigate HAIP-SYST as first line treatment for initially unresectable CRLM. Background

In patients with colorectal cancer liver metastases (CRLM), local treatment (e.g. surgical resection or thermal ablation) of the primary tumour and liver metastases may offer a chance of cure or long-term survival in case of liver-only disease [1, 2]. Most patients however present with initially unresectable CRLM [3, 4]. For this reason, major efforts have focused on optimizing induction treatment to convert unresectable CRLM to metastases amenable for local treatment [5]. Current standard of care for these patients consists of systemic induction treatment with reported conversion rates range from 22% to 57%[6]. Current standard of care for these patients consists of systemic induction treatment with reported conversion rates range from 22% to 57% Standard systemic therapy includes doublet systemic chemotherapy (FOLFOX/FOLFIRI/CAPOX) or triplet chemotherapy (FOLFOXIRI) with the possible addition of a targeted agent (anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) or anti-endothelial growth factor receptor (EGFR) antibody) [7–10]. The CAIRO5 trial investigated the optimal first line treatment regimen for unresectable CRLM without extrahepatic disease (EHD) by predefined resectability criteria. Patients with right-sided primary tumour site or RAS/BRAFV600E mutated tumours were randomised between FOLFOX or FOLFIRI plus bevacizumab (group A) and FOLFOXIRI plus bevacizumab (group B). Patients with left-sided and RAS/BRAFV600E wild-type tumours were randomised between FOLFOX or FOLFIRI plus bevacizumab (group C) or FOLFOX or FOLFIRI plus panitumumab (group D). Results showed improved conversion rates after triplet therapy compared to doublet therapy (37% vs. 51%), however, no differences in overall survival (OS) were found between group A and B. No differences in conversion rates nor OS were found between group C and D. The median OS for all patients was 30 months and ranged from 24 to 40 months between the different treatment arms [5, 11]. In addition, early recurrence (disease progression or death within six months) occurred in 42% after complete local treatment [11]. As the liver is the predominant site of recurrences, intensifying induction treatment with hepatic arterial infusion pump therapy may enhance conversion rates, reduce recurrences and prolong survival.

Hepatic arterial infusion pump (HAIP) therapy has been developed at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSKCC, New York, USA) and used in adjuvant and induction setting for liver tumours [12–14]. The biological rationale is based on the predominant arterial blood supply of liver tumours including CRLM [15, 16]. The pump is surgically implanted and delivers chemotherapy continuously to the liver via the hepatic artery. The administered chemotherapeutic agent Floxuridine (FUDR) has a short half-life of less than 10 min and a high first-pass liver extraction rate of 95%. This enables a 200- to 400-fold increase of chemotherapy exposure to the liver compared to systemic administration without significant systemic side effects [17, 18].

In patients with CRLM not amenable for local treatment, intra-arterial infusion of FUDR is combined with systemic therapy (HAIP-SYST) [19–21]. Previous retrospective studies showed that chemotherapy-naive patients appear to benefit most from HAIP-SYST as induction treatment compared to HAIP-SYST in later lines [22, 23]. An analysis of 58 consecutive chemo-naive patients with median 12 (range 2—20 +) CRLM not amenable for local treatment who were treated with HAIP and modern systemic therapy at MSKCC showed that 32 patients (55%) converted to local treatment and another two patients had a durable radiological complete response [22]. After median follow-up of 116 months, median progression-free survival(PFS) and OS were 16.7 months median 53.0 months, respectively. In patients who underwent conversion surgery and those with a radiological complete response versus patients whose tumours remained unresectable, median OS was 141.7 months (95% CI 37.1–246.4) versus 28.0 months (95% CI 25.5–30.5). Nineteen patients (59%) had a complete or major pathological response upon conversion surgery. Although these results for chemo-naive patients are impressive, limitations of the study include the retrospective single-centre design. External validation is warranted and should include comparison with current standard-of-care systemic therapy in a randomised setting. Since this treatment requires technical expertise, a dedicated and trained multidisciplinary team and close coordination of care between surgeons and medical oncologists, a phase-II study was performed in the Netherlands which showed that combined HAIP-SYST for unresectable CRLM was safe and feasible [24]. Twenty-eight of 31 patients (90%) received at least two cycles of HAIP-SYST. One patient had peritoneal disease at pump implantation, one patient had a squamous cell carcinoma as definitive histopathology of the primary tumour and one patient discontinued treatment due to grade 3 toxicity. 11 of 29 (38%) patients experienced grade ≥ 3 chemotherapy toxicity, one patient had a pump dysfunction and 4 of 31 patients (13%) had pump-surgery related complications grade 3 or higher. This multicentre randomised controlled trial was subsequently designed to investigate whether HAIP-SYST improves overall survival when compared to systemic therapy alone.

Methods

Objectives

Main objective of the PUMP-IT study is to investigate whether HAIP-SYST prolongs survival in chemo-naive patients with initially unresectable synchronous CRLM as compared with systemic therapy alone. Secondary objectives include PFS, liver progression-free survival (hPFS), conversion to local treatment, proportion of R0/R1 resection/ablation, radiological (RECIST 1.1) and pathological response (Mandard Tumour regression grade; TRG) [25], surgical complications grade 3 or higher (Clavien-Dindo, at 30 and 90 days), chemotherapy toxicity grade 3 or higher (CTCAE 5.0), quality of life (EQ-5D-5L + EORTC QLQ-C30) and cost-effectiveness [26, 27].

Study design

This multicentre, open label, phase 3 randomised controlled trial will be conducted in 21 Dutch liver centres. After informed consent, potentially eligible patients are registered and prospectively assessed for (un)resectability status by a liver expert panel based on predefined study-entry criteria (see inclusion criteria). When meeting all in- and exclusion criteria, eligible patients are randomised in a 1:1 ratio. Stratification factors are sidedness of the primary tumour, RAS mutation status, BRAFV600E mutation status, unresectability status of liver metastases (potentially resectable vs. technically unresectable) as decided by the expert panel, inclusion centre and optional administration of two FOLFOX cycles during screening period. Patients randomised to the control arm (arm A) receive induction systemic therapy according to standard clinical practice. Patients in the experimental arm (arm B) will undergo surgery for pump placement and resection of the primary tumour before start of HAIP-SYST (Fig. 1). Liver expert panel

The liver expert panel of the Dutch Colorectal Cancer Group (DCCG) consists of dedicated abdominal radiologists and liver surgeons from the participating hospitals. At baseline and at response evaluations every 8—9 weeks during induction treatment, a thoracic abdominal Computed Tomography (CT) scan (± magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the liver) will be prospectively assessed by one radiologist and three surgeons. When there is no consensus among the three panel surgeons, additional assessment is done by the panel chairman to give the casting vote. Imaging is uploaded and pseudonymized by professional ALEA software (ALEA Clinical, Abcoude, Netherlands). At baseline, there are two subcategories in patients meeting the study-entry criteria for unresectability (see inclusion criteria). At response evaluation, patients are considered resectable if complete (R0/1)-resection can be achieved allowing all established modalities for local treatment (i.e. ablation, resection, radiotherapy), including staged procedures. In case of disease amenable for local treatment, the treating hospital receives suggestions for local treatment from the expert panel.

Treatment strategies

Control arm (arm A)

Patients will receive systemic therapy consisting of fluoropyrimidines with oxaliplatin and/or irinotecan with or without the targeted agents anti-VEGF or anti-EGFR according to standard clinical practice (FOLFOX/FOLFIRI/FOLFOXIRI or CAPOX ± bevacizumab/panitumumab). The choice of treatment is at discretion of the medical oncologist. The long-term results of the Dutch CAIRO5-trial support using doublet therapy in these patients [11]. Dose modifications, temporarily halt and discontinuation are according to national guidelines. All 20 centres will treat patients in the control arm.

Experimental arm (arm B)

A pre-operative arterial phase CT-scan will be performed to assess hepatic arterial anatomy for catheter placement. The surgical procedure consists of HAIP implantation (Tricumed IP2000V infusion pump (Fig. 2); flowrate ± 1,5 ml/day, Tricumed Medizintechnik GmbH, Kiel, Germany) and simultaneous resection of the primary tumour. A cholecystectomy is performed to prevent cholecystitis that can be induced by FUDR.

Surgical procedure

cal procedure for pump implantation have been described previously and can be performed by laparotomy or robotically [28–30]. The catheter tip is placed in the gastroduodenal artery (GDA) at the junction with the common hepatic artery (CHA) to prevent turbulence of the blood flow in the hepatic artery that can lead to arterial thrombosis. The CHA, proper hepatic artery (PHA) and gastroduodenal artery (GDA) are mobilized and dissected circumferentially. Side branches of the GDA or hepatic arteries should be identified and ligated to avoid unintended perfusion of the stomach, pancreas and duodenum. Via transversal arteriotomy in the distal GDA, the intra-arterial catheter is inserted up to the junction. The end of the catheter is secured with non-absorbable ties proximal to the tying rings of the catheter. In case of anatomical variants, the GDA remains the preferred site for catheter implantation. When ligating all accessory and preplaced hepatic arteries, perfusion of the entire liver can be achieved via existing intrahepatic shunts. Before pump implantation, a function test is performed and pump catheter and pump side port are flushed. The pump pocket is created in the left or right upper quadrant and the pump is secured to the abdominal fascia. Perfusion of both lobes of the liver and lack of extrahepatic perfusion is confirmed by a bolus injection of methylene blue or indocyanine green. In case of intraoperative extrahepatic perfusion, surgical ligation of side branches is performed. The catheter is flushed and the pump pocket is closed. Resection of the primary tumour is the final step in this procedure. A limited first-stage hepatectomy is allowed (i.e. wedge resection and ablation in the future liver remnant) to minimize future surgical burden.

Complete hepatic perfusion and lack of extrahepatic perfusion is confirmed by a postoperative 99mTc-MAA scintigraphy. In case of postoperative extrahepatic perfusion, aberrant branches are percutaneously embolized until extrahepatic perfusion is resolved.

HAIP-SYST

Induction treatment with FUDR starts after postoperative recovery. Generally, the first treatment consists of FUDR without systemic therapy and is administered two weeks after surgery. The FUDR dose of 0.12 mg/kg/day is calculated based on patients’ weight and an adjusted formula is used in case of overweight. The HAIP reservoir will be filled every two weeks alternating FUDR with heparinized saline. The FUDR solution administered to the HAIP reservoir consists of heparin, dexamethasone (25 mg) and normal saline. When FUDR is not administered, the solution solely consists of heparinized saline (and dexamethasone in case of toxicity) to keep the catheter open. Concomitant systemic therapy (FOLFOX/FOLFIRI) will be administered during the same outpatient visit. Based on previous HAIP-SYST studies and to prevent liver toxicity, systemic therapy doses are adapted (Irinotecan: 150 compared to 180 mg/m2; Fluorouracil: 2000 instead of 2400 mg/m2, without the bolus). Bevacizumab is not added to the regimens in the experimental arm due to increased biliary toxicity [31]. Patients receive a proton pump inhibitor once daily during HAIP therapy and the use of Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) is prohibited. Every two weeks, blood count and kidney and liver functions are monitored. Dose modifications, interruption or discontinuation in case of FUDR related toxicity are according to protocol (supplementary material Table 3 and Table 4). In case of severe toxicity, FUDR is discontinued and dexamethasone is administered to the pump reservoir. When there is no longer evidence of toxicity, FUDR may be resumed at an adjusted dose. Patients in the experimental arm are treated in one of ten centres that have experience with HAIP treatment.

Treatment evaluation

Treatment response will be evaluated every 8 or 9 weeks by carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis. Radiological response is assessed according to RECIST 1.1. Besides the local MDT, the liver expert panel evaluates resectability up to three evaluations or until the expert panel agrees that panel evaluation can be terminated earlier. The total treatment duration is six months. The preoperative regimen, without targeted agents, will be continued after local treatment to complete these six months [32, 33]. Protocol treatment can be terminated earlier in case of progression of disease, unacceptable toxicity or patient refusal. If patients show no disease progression but the liver metastases remain unresectable after six months of protocol treatment, they are unlikely to become amenable for local treatment and therapy may be continued as maintenance treatment in both arms.

Follow-up

Patients will be followed for five years from the date of randomization or death. After completing or termination of protocol treatment, follow-up includes CEA measurement and CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis according to standard clinical practice. In case of conversion to local treatment or radiological complete response, follow-up frequency is every three months (year 1—2) and every six months (year 3—5) after completing local treatment of CRLM. If HAIP therapy or any systemic therapy (i.e. second line) is continued, the frequency is every two or three months. Quality of life is assessed at baseline, every three months in the first year after start treatment and thereafter annually up to completion of follow-up or when deceased.

Study endpoints and analysis

Primary endpoint is OS, defined as the time between randomization and the event of death. Patients alive at the last visit will be censored.

Sample size

The expected median OS for the control arm is 30 months based on the results of the CAIRO5 study [11]. Chemotherapy-naive patients treated at MSKCC had a median OS of 53 months after HAIP-SYST [22]. Considering the small and retrospective cohort of MSKCC, the expected median OS was lowered to 48 months for this trial. To detect the difference of 18 months in OS with 80% power and a two-sided logrank test with 5% type I error (alpha level), 144 events need to be observed. Inclusion of 306 patients is needed. With an expected accrual rate of 60 patients per year, the estimated accrual period is 5.5 years.

Analysis

Final analysis of the primary endpoint will be performed according to the ‘intention-to-treat’ principle. Survival curves will be constructed per treatment arm using the Kaplan–Meier method. Treatment arms will be compared using the stratified logrank test, using stratification factors similar to those applied during randomization. In addition, the hazard ratio with corresponding 95% confidence interval will be derived using the stratified Cox regression model. Interim analysis will be performed after enrolment of 100 patients to evaluate safety, feasibility and futility. An independent Data Safety Monitor Board (DSMB) is installed to assess efficacy and safety at least every year and at interim analysis. Missing data will be systematically identified and documented. Complete Case Analysis is used in case of missing data. Missing data for analysis on quality of life may be imputed as suggested by the EORTC [34]. The cost-effectiveness analysis will be performed according to the Dutch and international guidelines for economic evaluations [35, 36].

Quality and safety

Local data management will be performed by the Netherlands Comprehensive Cancer organization (IKNL) and the NCI Biometrics Department. Data will be derived from electronic patient records and collected with a GCP compliant eCRF; ALEA Tools for Clinical Trials. In addition, ALEA software facilitates randomisation of patients, an online survey for the questionnaires and pseudonymization of all radiological data acquired for the panel assessment which is stored on a protected server. Data will be stored using a study identification code for each participant. The key to the identification code list is stored at a secure server and will only be available for delegated members of the study team. No patient identification details will be reported in publications. Results of this trial will be disclosed completely in international peer-reviewed journals. Central data management, data processing, monitoring and statistical analysis will be performed by the NCI Biometrics Department. In accordance to Dutch law and GCP guidelines, all SAEs and adverse events grade ≥ 3 including causality to the protocol treatment will be recorded. SAEs will be reported within 24 h to the NCI Biometrics Department and annually to the reviewing METC.

Discussion

HAIP-SYST has a promising role in treating chemotherapy-naive patients with initially unresectable CRLM as they seem to benefit most [19, 22, 23, 37]. The PUMP-IT RCT is the first prospective randomised trial to investigate HAIP-SYST as first line treatment for initially unresectable CRLM compared to modern systemic therapy alone. Chemotherapy-naive patients with synchronous liver-only colorectal metastases will be included and prospectively assessed by a liver expert panel for (un)resectability status based on predefined criteria at baseline and during treatment evaluations.

HAIP therapy requires a highly skilled multidisciplinary team for the technically challenging surgical procedure for pump placement and the need for stringent monitoring and management of patients during HAIP-SYST [13, 28]. Several Dutch phase-II studies were conducted and with the nearly completed PUMP RCT, there is (extensive) experience with HAIP treatment for CRLM in the Netherlands [24, 29, 38]. For the current trial, specific liver centres were trained and proctored to treat patients with HAIP-SYST.

With this treatment strategy, there is an inevitable delay before initiation of HAIP-SYST due to pump implantation and postoperative recovery. In the PUMP-IT pilot study, the first cycle FUDR was administered after a median of 21 days (IQR 15—28) in comparison to the interval of median 16 days (range 8—48) in the MSKCC cohort [22, 24]. Treatment initiation is aimed two weeks postoperatively in current protocol.

For this trial, the primary tumour needs to be in situ and resectable without neoadjuvant treatment. If randomized to the intervention arm, the primary tumour will be resected during pump placement procedure. The literature on timing of resection of the primary tumour in patients with CRLM remains controversial. Resection of the primary tumour prior to start systemic therapy may be associated with significant morbidity and the CAIRO4 trial showed higher sixty-day mortality [39]. However, these patients had more advanced multi-organ metastatic disease compared to our liver-only population. Supporting the current study protocol, a large retrospective cohort study from MSKCC analysed perioperative outcomes of simultaneous primary tumour resection and pump placement, compared to pump placement alone. Despite more grade 1—2 complications in case of simultaneous primary tumour resection, no difference in grade 3—4 complications and no additional treatment delay was observed [40]. In the PUMP-IT pilot study, Clavien-Dindo grade ≥ 3 complications of the pump placement procedure (including resection of the primary tumour in 73% of patients) were documented in 4 of 31 patients (13%). No anastomotic leakage was observed in case of simultaneous resection of the primary tumour. In addition, the initial results of the randomised SYNCHRONOUS trial show no difference in OS between resection before start systemic therapy and systemic therapy alone [24, 41]. In the current protocol, obstructive tumours are excluded to reduce surgical complications related to the primary tumour. By resection of the primary tumour, the possibility exists to continue HAIP-monotherapy in case of systemic toxicity or in case of stable liver-only disease (i.e. not amenable for local treatment). This may improve patients’ quality of life without experiencing the side effects of systemic therapy following standard of care. Additionally, the systemic dosage of FOLFOX or FOLFIRI is slightly reduced in the experimental arm to minimize biliary complications, leading to potentially suboptimal treatment of the primary tumour in case it would remain in situ during HAIP-SYST. Moreover, upfront resection is intended to minimize the total number of surgical procedures and therefore reduce the overall surgical burden of major liver surgery combined with primary tumour resection in cases of conversion.

The EA2222 multicentre randomised trial, recently started in het United States, is investigating HAIP-SYST as second line treatment for persistent unresectable CRLM compared to systemic therapy alone (NCT05863195). The PUMP-IT RCT is open for accrual as of November 2024. The forthcoming results of both trials will provide definitive insight in different treatment indications for HAIP-SYST in unresectable CRLM.

Prediction of cerebral infarction after bypass surgery in adult moyamoya disease: combing parameters on 4D perfusion CT with clinical related factors
Authors: Jiangbo Ding, Xuying Chang, Yong Shen, Peiyu Ma, Ruoyu Zhang, Guangwu Yang, Linjie Mu, Xingkui Zhang, Zhigao Li, Jinwei Tang, Zhiwei Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to identify predictive factors for cerebral infarction after bypass surgery in adult patients with moyamoya disease (MMD) using quantitative parameters in 4D-CT perfusion software.MethodsA total of 108 patients who underwent combined revascularization, including superficial temporal artery-middle cerebral artery (STA-MCA) anastomosis and encephalo-duro-myo-synangiosis (EDMS), in our hospital between September 2019 and August 2023 were analyzed retrospectively. Preoperative relative cerebral blood flow (rCBF), relative cerebral blood volume (rCBV), relative mean transit time (rMTT), and relative time to maximum residual function (rTmax) perfusion parameters were obtained using 4D-CT perfusion software. These quantitative parameters, combined with related clinical and angiographic factors, were statistically analyzed using univariate and multivariate regression analyses to determine the significant predictors of cerebral infarction after bypass surgery.ResultsAcute cerebral infarction occurred in 12 patients postoperatively. Univariate analysis showed that a history of previous ischemic events (P = 0.024), higher Suzuki stage (P = 0.006), higher modified Rankin score (mRS) (P = 0.013), rCBV (P = 0.026), rMTT (P = 0.001), and rTmax (P < 0.001) were associated with postoperative cerebral infarction. Further multivariate regression analysis showed that a history of previous ischemic events (OR = 12.830, 95%CI = 1.854–875.672, P = 0.031) and higher rTmax (OR = 16.968, 95%CI = 2.035–141.451, P = 0.009) were independently associated with new postoperative cerebral infarction. The cutoff value for rTmax was 2.025 (AUC = 0.935).ConclusionsPrevious ischemic event history and rTmax greater than 2.025 are independent risk factors for predicting cerebral infarction after combined revascularization with high sensitivity in adult patients with MMD. These patients should be more cautious when deciding on combined revascularization.

Introduction

Moyamoya disease (MMD) is a group of cerebrovascular diseases characterized by chronic progressive stenosis or occlusion at the end of the internal carotid artery and its main intracranial branches, which leads to the formation of an abnormal vascular network of the skull base. The posterior cerebral artery (PCA) is also involved in some patients [4]. The common manifestations of adult MMD are ischemic and hemorrhagic strokes [17]. The treatment of MMD include drug and cerebrovascular bypass surgery. For symptomatic ischemic and hemorrhagic MMD, the guidelines recommend bypass surgery [21, 29]. However, owing to the complex cerebral hemodynamic mechanism of MMD, cerebral infarction occurs in some patients after bypass surgery, resulting in irreversible neurological dysfunction and seriously affecting prognosis [13].

Previous studies have reported that postoperative acute cerebral infarction is mainly related to perioperative cerebral hemodynamic disorders [23], but there has been no effective method to predict it until now. CT perfusion imaging(CTP) is the most commonly used method to evaluate cerebral hemodynamics in patients with MMD during the perioperative period and can obtain multidimensional microcirculation perfusion parameters to guide clinical treatment [14, 32]. However, the correlation between preoperative CT perfusion parameters and postoperative cerebral infarction remains unclear. Therefore, in this study, the preoperative CT perfusion parameters and general clinical data of patients with MMD were retrospectively analyzed to determine the important indicators for predicting postoperative cerebral infarction, which are reported as follows.

Methods

Patient selection

The medical data of patients diagnosed with MMD between September 2019 and August 2023 were retrospectively reviewed under the approval of the Medical Ethics Committee of our hospital. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) older than18 years old of age; (2) MMD diagnosed by femoral arteriography and in accordance with the newest guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of moyamoya disease; [5] (3) combined bypass surgery performed by the same operator, and outpatient follow-up performed three months after surgery. Patients with incomplete data, unwillingness to undergo surgical treatment, or only encephalo-duro-myo-synangiosis (EDMS) treatment during the operation were excluded. Ultimately, 108 patients were enrolled in the study. A flowchart is shown in Fig. 1.Fig. 1The flowchart showing patient inclusion and exclusion

Clinical and radiological evaluation

Clinical data of 108 patients were collected, including age, sex, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, smoking history, history of previous ischemic events (including TIA and cerebral infarction), surgical side, and preoperative mRS score. Suzuki staging was determined based on preoperative angiography with femoral artery catheterization, and the presence or absence of posterior cerebral artery stenosis or occlusion was recorded.

Analysis of 4D perfusion CT

Preoperative CTP data were obtained as follows: GE99-CT produced by German Siemens was used for scanning, and 4D perfusion software was used for image post-processing. After correcting for arterial input function (AIF), the mechanical parameters of cerebral blood flow (CBF), cerebral blood volume (CBV), mean transit time (MTT), and time to maximum of the residual function (Tmax) were obtained. We selected the centrum semiovale and basal ganglia levels supplied by the middle cerebral artery (MCA) to manually draw standardized ellipses as regions of interest (ROI), and obtained the corresponding perfusion parameters of multiple layers. If there was a previous hemorrhage or infarction lesion in the ROI, we made some adjustments to the size and shape of the ROI to avoid previous lesions. The actual measured average values were recorded as the aCBF, aCBV, aMTT, and aTMax. In patients with MMD, the anterior cerebral artery (ACA) and middle cerebral artery (MCA) may be occluded or continue to progress, and some patients have only unilateral disease. So it is not reasonable to use anterior mirror ROIs as reference area. Therefore, in this study, we artificially selected the ipsilateral cerebellar hemisphere level area (the blood supply area of the posterior circulation) as the reference area.The relative average values of these levels were recorded as relative cerebral blood flow (rCBF), relative cerebral blood volume (rCBV), relative mean transit time (rMTT), and relative time to maximum residual function (rTmax). The calculation formula was rCBF = CBF/CBF reference, rCBV = CBV/CBV reference, rMTT = MTT/MTT reference, and rTMax = Tmax/Tmax reference. See Figs 2 for further details. CTP examination and ROI delineation were performed by an experienced neuroradiologist and neurovascular surgeon, respectively. Combined revascularization and postoperative management

All patients underwent surgery under general anesthesia. The M4 segment of the MCA with a diameter around the lateral fissure matched with the bridging vessel was selected as the recipient vessel to avoid functional areas such as language and movement. After superficial temporal artery-middle cerebral artery (STA-MCA) end-to-side anastomosis was completed, the morphology of the anastomosis was examined, and indocyanine green fluorescence angiography (ICG) was performed to confirm that the anastomosis was unobstructed. Finally, the dura mater was turned, the temporal muscle was attached to the brain surface, and the skull was closed layer-by-layer after receiving the bone flap. To avoid cerebral ischemic events caused by hypotension, systolic blood pressure was strictly controlled between 120–140 mmHg during and after surgery, and arterial blood gas analysis was performed during and after surgery to ensure that the partial pressure of carbon dioxide was 35–45 mmHg.

All patients underwent MRI within one week after the operation. A new cerebral infarction was determined according to the results of diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), and the location of the infarction was recorded. The postoperative imaging results were independently provided by two experienced radiologists.

Follow-up protocol

The modified Rankin scale (mRS) score was used to evaluate the clinical outcome of patients 1 week and 3 months after the operation, and an mRS score > 2 was defined as an adverse outcome.

Statistical methods

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation, and the differences between the two groups were compared using an independent sample t-test. Continuous variables that did not follow a normal distribution are presented as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR), and the differences between groups were compared using the Mann–Whitney U-test. Categorical variables were represented by case number and component ratio, Chi-square test was used for comparison between groups, and Fisher’s exact probability method was used for comparison when the frequency was less than 5. Multivariate Logistic regression analysis was performed for variables with a P-value less than 0.150 after univariate analysis to determine the risk factors for postoperative cerebral infarction. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. A trend analysis was performed using a linear-by-linear association model to assess whether increased cerebral angiographic staging was statistically associated with rTmax measured by 4D Perfusion CT. All data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 statistical software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Clinical data characteristics

A total of 108 patients with MMD (108 hemispheres) underwent combined bypass surgery, and 12 (11.1%) developed new cerebral infarction after surgery. Patients with a history of ischemic events were more likely to have cerebral infarction after surgery (P = 0.006). Patients with postoperative cerebral infarction had higher mRS scores (1 [0–4] vs.0 [0–4], P = 0.004) and higher Suzuki stages (P = 0.013) before surgery. Age (P = 0.416), sex (P = 0.540), hypertension (P = 0.889), hyperlipidemia (P = 0.943), proportion of smokers (P = 0.729), side of operation (P = 0.272), and PCA involvement (P = 0.101) were not significantly different between the two groups. In the preoperative CTP parameters, there was no significant difference in rCBF (0.71 ± 0.20 vs 0.61 ± 0.10, P = 0.103), while rCBV (1.02 ± 0.31 vs 1.23 ± 0.15, P = 0.019), rMTT (1.59 ± 0.60 vs 2.39 ± 0.33, P < 0.001) and rTmax (1.67 ± 0.56 vs 3.10 ± 0.68, P < 0.001) were significantly different between the two groups (non-infarction group and infarction group). Patients with postoperative cerebral infarction had higher mRS scores one week (P < 0.001) and three months (P < 0.001) after surgery. Risk factors for postoperative cerebral infarction

Univariate analysis showed that age (P = 0.413), sex (P = 0.542), history of hypertension (P = 0.889), history of hyperlipidemia (P = 0.943), proportion of smokers (P = 0.575), surgical side (P = 0.279), and PCA involvement (P = 0.111) were not significantly associated with postoperative cerebral infarction. However, a history of previous ischemic events (P = 0.024), preoperative mRS score (P = 0.013), and Suzuki stage (P = 0.006) were statistically correlated with postoperative cerebral infarction. Among the preoperative 4D-CTP parameters, rCBF was not correlated with postoperative cerebral infarction (P = 0.108), but rCBV (P = 0.026), rMTT (P = 0.001) and rTmax (P < 0.001) were significantly correlated with postoperative cerebral infarction.The results of the univariable analysis are listed in Table 3. When the potential risk factors with a P value less than 0.150 in univariate analysis were included in multivariate logistic regression analysis, previous ischemic event history (OR = 12.830, 95%CI = 1.854–875.672, P = 0.031) and higher rTmax (OR = 16.968, 95% CI = 2.035–141.451, P = 0.009) were independent risk factors for predicting postoperative cerebral infarction. Cutoff value of the postoperative cerebral infarction

The critical value of postoperative cerebral infarction was determined by rTmax by drawing a receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC). The results showed that the maximum Joden index was 0.740 and the optimal threshold of rTmax for predicting postoperative cerebral infarction was 2.025(sensitivity 82.3%, specificity 91.7%, area under the curve [AUC] 0.935). Results of correlation analysis between rTmax and Suzuki stage

Bivariate Spearman correlation analysis of rTmax and Suzuki stages suggested that the average rTmax in Suzuki stage I was 1.221, stage II was 1.597 ± 0.540, stage III was 1.787 ± 0.686, stage IV was 2.236 ± 0.865, and stage V was 3.094. With an increase in Suzuki stage, rTmax gradually increased, and there was a positive correlation between them (r = 0.315, two-tailed P = 0.001). Discussion

Moyamoya disease is a rare cerebrovascular disease that causes strokes. In recent years, an increasing number of patients have been screened and diagnosed, but the etiology and pathogenesis remain unclear. Revascularization is the mainstream treatment strategy for MMD. According to relevant research, combined revascularization can not only obtain the blood perfusion of the STA-MCA immediately, but also establish chronic leptomeningeal collateral anastomosis, which can improve the cerebral blood flow to the maximum extent [28]. However, many stroke centers have reported that MMD patients are prone to complications of cerebral infarction after bypass surgery [32], especially those receiving direct bypass and combined bypass surgery [12], with an incidence of approximately 1.6% −16.0% [15]. The higher incidence of postoperative infarction often puts patients at a higher risk, and severe disability has a great impact on patients 'quality of life. In this study, 12 patients (11.1%) developed a new cerebral infarction after surgery, which is similar to the scope reported in the literature, indicating that the high incidence of postoperative cerebral infarction is a common problem.

Risk factors and related mechanisms of postoperative cerebral infarction

The risk factors for postoperative cerebral infarction in patients with MMD are mainly related to changes in cerebral hemodynamics during the perioperative period, [11] but this is still not completely clear. Previous studies have reported that ischemic event history, [3, 36] hypertension, hyperlipidemia [16, 33] and high preoperative mRS score [2] were significant risk factors. Other studies have found that MMD patients with PCA stenosis or occlusion[24] and higher Suzuki stage [25] are also at risk for postoperative cerebral infarction. In addition, some scholars have reported that hypotension and perfusion during surgical anesthesia, spasm of recipient vessels, and occlusion of collateral circulation, such as small branches, also cause postoperative local cerebral infarction [14, 19]. In our study, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and PCA involvement were not associated with postoperative cerebral infarction (P > 0.05), while a higher preoperative mRS score (P = 0.013) and higher Suzuki stage (P = 0.006) were statistically correlated in univariate analysis. Patients with a history of previous ischemic events were more likely to develop cerebral infarction after bypass surgery, which was an independent risk factor for predicting postoperative cerebral infarction (OR = 12.830, 95% CI = 1.854–875.672, P = 0.031), consistent with the above literature reports.

The most frequently reported mechanism of cerebral infarction after bypass surgery is “watershed shift” [27, 30]. Heros's study found that the change of cerebral hemodynamics after bypass was the root cause of neurological deterioration [9]. Furthermore, Hayashi's research thought that bypass flow and antegrade flow from the MCA trunk after bypass may lead to flow conflict and reduced antegrade flow in the other MCA branch, resulting in infarction in the region distant from the anastomosis [8]. This hemodynamic change after bypass surgery is the main mechanism leading to watershed shift infarction. In our study, infarct lesions in 10 patients were located in the region far from the anastomosis, and three cases occurred in the contralateral hemisphere, which is related to the "watershed shift" mechanism reported in the above literature.

Another mechanism of postoperative cerebral infarction in patients with MMD is related to collateral circulation [20]. Good collateral circulation is helpful in providing forward or reverse compensatory blood flow through the Willis circle and pia mater, improves cerebral ischemia, and reduces the number and volume of perioperative cerebral infarction. [31] Collateral circulation was also associated with Suzuki stage [10], and patients with higher Suzuki stage was associated with progressive cerebral infarction after surgery due to poor collateral circulation and autocompensation [2], which was also found in our study. It has also been reported that patients with PCA stenosis or occlusion are prone to postoperative cerebral infarction because of the decrease in lateral branch compensation of the pia mater [24]. However, PCA involvement was not an independent risk factor for postoperative cerebral infarction in the present study.

CTP can dynamically assess cerebral hemodynamics and collateral circulation, [7, 22, 26] and also directly assess the microcirculatory damage in patients with MMD [1, 34]. In this study, we found that higher rCBV, rMTT, and rTmax in preoperative 4D CT perfusion parameters were correlated with postoperative cerebral infarction (P < 0.05), and rTmax was an independent risk factor (OR = 16.968, 95% CI = 2.035–141.451, P = 0.009). Tmax is a significant perfusion parameter of CTP that shows abnormalities throughout the stroke process, is extremely sensitive to ischemia, and is very effective in assessing the patency of collateral circulation [6, 18]. In addition, our study found that rTmax increased gradually with the increase in Suzuki stage, and there was a positive correlation between them (r = 0.315, two-tailed P = 0.001), suggesting that rTmax may reflect the progression and collateral circulation to some extent in patients with MMD. However, the correlation coefficient between the two is small, and there are still many high rTmax outliers in Suzuki III, and these patients are more likely to develop cerebral infarction after surgery. Therefore, we consider that rTmax is a powerful supplement to existing predictive indicators, but is rarely reported. ROC curve analysis of rTmax suggested that rTmax was an effective index for predicting postoperative cerebral infarction with high sensitivity.

Significance of previous ischemic events and rTmax

In summary, postoperative cerebral infarction is strongly associated with cerebral hemodynamics and collateral circulation. For symptomatic MMD patients, even if infarction has not occurred, the cerebral hemodynamic and collateral circulation have been severely damaged. However, for MMD patients with a history of ischemic cerebral infarction, due to the instability of CBF, the blood flow compensation of collateral circulation is very poor, and the blood vessels become relaxed and lose their ability to perform normal automatic regulation. In this situation, patients will suffer from cerebral infarction due to “watershed shift” after bypass surgery [11, 35]. Prolongation of rTmax can quantitatively evaluate cerebral hemodynamics and collateral circulation, thus predicting the occurrence of postoperative cerebral infarction.

Strengths and limitations

In this study, rTmax was found to be an effective index for predicting postoperative cerebral infarction for the first time, providing a new strategy for the treatment of patients with MMD. However, this study had some limitations. First, owing to the limited number of cases, we did not perform a subgroup analysis of hemorrhagic and ischemic MMD. Second, this study lacked internal and external validation and decision curve analysis. Finally, the acquisition of CT perfusion parameters was sketched by hand, but to reduce the error, we used cerebellar perfusion as a reference and compared it with relative numbers. There was no statistical difference in the cerebellar perfusion parameters between the groups. Therefore, we believe that our results are reliable.

Conclusions

Previous ischemic event history and rTmax greater than 2.025 were independent risk factors for predicting cerebral infarction after combined revascularization with high sensitivity in adult patients with MMD. These patients should be more cautious when deciding on combined revascularization.

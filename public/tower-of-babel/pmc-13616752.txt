In memoriam: Professor Sang Ho Baik (1934–2026)
Published: 2026

Professor Sang Ho Baik was born in Eunyul, Hwanghae-do, Korea, on September 24, 1934. He entered the College of Medicine at Seoul National University in 1953 and graduated in 1959. Following graduation, Professor Baik joined the Department of Anatomy at the same university as an assistant, during which time he earned his master’s degree. After serving as a medical officer in the Republic of Korea Army (1962–1965), he returned to the university and obtained his Ph.D. in anatomy in 1968. Professor Baik subsequently served at Seoul National University as an instructor (1966–1968), assistant professor (1968–1973), associate professor (1973–1979), and professor (1979–2000). While serving at Seoul National University, Professor Baik was seconded to the College of Medicine at Gyeongsang National University from 1983 to 1987, where he undertook the responsibility of serving as dean of the newly established medical school. He also served as Chair of the Department of Anatomy for four years (1991–1995).

Professor Baik made extraordinary contributions to the field of anatomy in Korea. During his overseas training period, he studied immunohistochemical staining techniques at the Department of Pathology at the University of Michigan and became the first scholar to introduce and disseminate this important histological methodology in Korea. This technique remains one of the most widely used research methods among members of our academic society today. In recognition of his contributions to research, he received the Grand Prize of the Hanggok Academic Award (한곡학술상 으뜸상) from the Korean Association of Anatomists (KAA) in 1995. Professor Baik retired in February 2000 and was subsequently appointed professor emeritus of Seoul National University. The Korean government recognized his distinguished service by awarding him the Green Stripes Order of Service Merit (녹조근정훈장) of the Republic of Korea.

His academic contributions also extended to the KAA. Having been a member since 1959, he later served as President of the KAA in 1986 and as President of the Korean Association of Physical Anthropologists in 1992. Among his many contributions to the anatomical community, perhaps the most outstanding was his leadership as Chair of the Terminology Committee for ten years beginning in 1987, during which he devoted himself to the reform and standardization of Korean anatomical terminology. Before this initiative, anatomy in Korea had been taught largely through Japanese-style anatomical terminology. Professor Baik and his colleagues boldly sought to replace these difficult terms with simpler, Korean-centered anatomical terminology. The final outcome of this effort was the publication of the Anatomical Terminology, which continues to be revised and used today. In recognition of these achievements, the Terminology Committee of the KAA received a Commendation from the Minister of Culture in 1990, followed in 1991 by the Presidential Award in the Academic Division of the Sejong Cultural Prize (세종문화상 학술부문 대통령상), as well as an additional national decoration.

Professor Baik’s contributions as a medical educator were unparalleled within the Korean medical community. While serving as an associate professor at Seoul National University, he developed a strong interest in medical education and completed the Master in Health Professions Education program at the University of New South Wales. Drawing upon the expertise he had accumulated, Professor Baik led sweeping reforms in Korean medical education. He transformed medical school curricula, which had previously been organized around individual disciplines, into vertically integrated curricula structured according to organ systems. These educational reforms also extended into anatomical education through the introduction of a new concept of anatomy education based on clinical medicine.

He also introduced revolutionary changes in the evaluation systems used in both undergraduate medical education and the national medical licensing examination. He served as President of the Korean Society of Medical Education (1989–1991) and later as President of the Korea Health Personnel Licensing Examination Institute (2001–2004), leading major reforms in these fields. In particular, he established principles for improving the quality of medical examinations, developed new techniques for constructing examination items, and introduced practical skills assessments, thereby fundamentally reforming the physician licensing examination system in Korea. In short, Professor Baik played a decisive role in transforming Korean medical education from a discipline-centered curriculum to an integrated curriculum, and from a lecture-centered system to a case discussion-centered system.

Professor Baik’s life was thus marked by innovation and passion. His devoted efforts served for many years as a guiding compass for the development of anatomy and medical education in Korea. He was not only an anatomist who first introduced groundbreaking histological methodologies in Korea, but also a pioneer who fundamentally transformed Korean anatomical terminology, medical education systems, and physician licensing examinations, thereby contributing greatly to enhancing the international reputation of the Korean medical and academic community. Throughout his life, his activities and dedication as both a researcher and educator exerted a profound influence far beyond the field of anatomy itself, remaining a constant source of inspiration for younger generations in our profession. The faculty members of the Department of Anatomy and Cell Biology at Seoul National University College of Medicine extend their deepest condolences and pray for the eternal rest of our respected Professor Sang Ho Baik.

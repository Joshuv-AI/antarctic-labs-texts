Computation and Size of Interpolants for Hybrid Modal Logics
Jean Christoph Jung, Jędrzej Kołodziejski, Frank Wolter

Abstract
Recent research has established complexity results for the problem of deciding the existence of interpolants in logics lacking the Craig Interpolation Property (CIP). The proof techniques developed so far are non-constructive, and no meaningful bounds on the size of interpolants are known. Hybrid modal logics (or modal logics with nominals) are a particularly interesting class of logics without CIP: in their case, CIP cannot be restored without sacrificing decidability and, in applications, interpolants in these logics can serve as definite descriptions and separators between positive and negative data examples in description logic knowledge bases. In this contribution we show, using a new hypermosaic elimination technique, that in many standard hybrid modal logics Craig interpolants can be computed in fourfold exponential time, if they exist. On the other hand, we show that the existence of uniform interpolants is undecidable, which is in stark contrast to modal or intuitionistic logic where uniform interpolants always exist.

A logic is said to enjoy the Craig interpolation property (CIP) if a Craig interpolant exists whenever

By contrast, if a logic does not have CIP, then the existence of a proof does not guarantee the existence of an interpolant, and it remains unclear how interpolants could be extracted even when they do exist. Although recent work has made significant progress on the problem of deciding whether interpolants exist in logics without CIP, very little has been achieved on the construction of interpolants or on estimating their size. This problem arises because the algorithms developed so far are non-constructive: rather than providing explicit interpolants, they rely on variants of Robinson’s joint consistency, which in turn rely on compactness.

The aim of this paper is to establish the first elementary upper bounds for constructing Craig interpolants in modal and description logics that lack CIP. We focus on the family of decidable hybrid modal logics, or equivalently, description logics with nominals. The computation of Craig interpolants in these logics is particularly significant for two reasons.

First, in hybrid modal logics, CIP cannot be restored merely by adding further logical connectives without sacrificing decidability
[
38

Related Work

We discuss related work not yet mentioned above. CIP has been investigated extensively for hybrid modal logics and corresponding description logics with nominals. The main positive results are that by adding the binding operator
\downarrow
to
\mathcal{H}(@)
one obtains a logic with CIP (also if restricted to popular classes of frames)
[
12
]
, and that CIP holds for languages in
HL
if arbitrary nominals can be used in interpolants (called CIP for propositional letters)
[
11
,
2
]
. However, satisfiability, and hence also interpolant existence, are undecidable for
\mathcal{H}(\downarrow,@)
. This makes its use in knowledge representation applications problematic. Likewise, allowing arbitrary nominals in interpolants undermines the very purpose of interpolants in description logic. The decision problem of whether an interpolant exists for a given implication in hybrid modal logics lacking CIP is studied in
[
4
]
. The principal question left open there—how to effectively construct interpolants when they do exist, and what bounds can be established on their size—is the focus of the present contribution.

4.2
Separator Construction

We start with the case of the hybrid logic
\mathcal{H}
. The easy modifications needed to adapt the construction to
\mathcal{H}_{@}
and
\mathcal{H}_{@,U}
will be discussed in the end.
Recall that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
and signature
\sigma
were the input to the elimination procedure, and that
\mathsf{Type}
denotes the set of all relevant types. Let
\mathsf{Nom}_{0}=\mathsf{Nom}\cap{\sf sub}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
denote the set of all relevant nominals.
We first generalize the notion of a separator for a pair of formulae to hypermosaics. As in Section
3
, the separators depend on the ‘type’ of the nominals, but in a more dynamic way.

A
nominal case
is a pair
\mathfrak{c}=(\mathfrak{c}_{1},\mathfrak{c}_{2})
of sequences of
\mathcal{H}
-formulae,
one for each nominal

\mathfrak{c}_{1},\mathfrak{c}_{2}:\mathsf{Nom}_{0}\to\mathcal{H}.

As in Section
3
, we use
\mathsf{case}(\mathfrak{c}_{i})
to abbreviate the formula

\mathsf{case}(\mathfrak{c}_{i})=\bigwedge_{a\in\mathsf{Nom}_{0}}@_{a}\mathfrak{c}_{i}(a).

A
case distinction
is a finite set
\mathsf{Case}
of nominal cases which
exhausts all possibilities. That is, every pair of models
(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})
adheres to some case
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}
in the sense that
\mathcal{M}_{i}\models\mathsf{case}(\mathfrak{c}_{i})
for both
i
. In general, we allow for cases in
\mathsf{Case}
to be unsatisfiable; they do not cause problems, and we can always remove them.

A separator for a pair of formulae is a witness that they cannot be realized at
\sigma
-bisimilar points. We now define an analogous notion for hypermosaics:
a hyperseparator
which witnesses that a given hypermosaic
H
cannot be realized by a bisimulation between two models. Since we will construct hyperseparators in a uniform way, for convenience we first define a hyperseparator without any fixed hypermosaic, and then say what it means that this separator separates a given
H
.

Definition 15
.

A
hyperseparator
consists of a case distinction
\mathsf{Case}

and functions
\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}
of type

\mathsf{sep}_{i}:\mathsf{Case}\times\mathsf{Type}\to\mathcal{H}(\sigma)

such that for every
t\in\mathsf{Type}
,
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}
, and
i=1,2
:

t\wedge\mathsf{case}(\mathfrak{c}_{i})\models\mathsf{sep}_{i}(\mathfrak{c},t).

We say that
\mathsf{Case},\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}

separates
a hypermosaic
H
if for every
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}
there is
\mathsf{m}\in H
such that:

\bigwedge_{i=1,2}\bigwedge_{t\in\mathsf{m}_{i}}\mathsf{sep}_{i}(\mathfrak{c},t)\models\bot.

Note that Definition
15
generalizes the notion of a separator for a pair of formulae to hypermosaics. Intuitively, the definition ranges over some exhaustive set of cases, and for each case
\mathfrak{c}
, side
i
, and type
t
provides a
\mathcal{H}(\sigma)
-formula
\mathsf{sep}_{i}(\mathfrak{c},t)
which follows from
t
in the case
\mathfrak{c}
.
The hyperseparator separates
H
if no matter which case
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}
holds, we always find an inconsistency in the consequences of some mosaic
\mathsf{m}\in H
.

In what follows, we will supplement the elimination procedure with an elementary construction of hyperseparators which separate all eliminated hypermosaics. Before we do that, let us motivate the construction by showing how the usual separators for types are easily obtained from hyperseparators for hypermosaics.

Lemma 16
.

Assume a hyperseparator
\mathsf{Case},\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}
for a hypermosaic
\{(\{t_{1}\},\{t_{2}\})\}
, and let

m=\max\{|\mathsf{sep}_{i}(\mathfrak{c},t)|\mid\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case},\ i=1,2,\ t\in\mathsf{Type}\}.

One can construct a separator for
t_{1},t_{2}
of size polynomial in
2^{|\mathsf{Case}|}\times m
.

Proof.

Assume a hyperseparator
\mathsf{Case},\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}
separating
\{(\{t_{1}\},\{t_{2}\})\}
. For
i=1,2
denote
\mathcal{C}_{i}=\{\mathfrak{c}_{i}\mid\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}\}
and consider the family
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{i}}
of all
satisfiable
subsets of
\mathcal{C}_{i}
in the sense
that

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{i}}=\{S\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\mid\chi_{S}\not\models\bot\}

where

\chi_{S}=\bigwedge_{\mathfrak{c}_{i}\in S}\mathsf{case}(\mathfrak{c}_{i})\wedge\bigwedge_{\mathfrak{c}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}\setminus S}\neg\mathsf{case}(\mathfrak{c}_{i}).

Define:

\theta^{S}_{i}=\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}\\
\mathfrak{c}_{i}\in S\end{subarray}}\mathsf{sep}_{i}(\mathfrak{c},t_{i})\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\psi_{i}=\bigvee_{S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{i}}}\theta^{S}_{i}

for all
i=1,2
and
S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{i}}
. We claim that

t_{1}\models\psi_{1}\models\neg\psi_{2}\models\neg t_{2}

which means that
\psi_{1}
(and also
\neg\psi_{2}
) separates
t_{1},t_{2}
.

Since
\chi_{S}\models\mathsf{case}(\mathfrak{c}_{i})
for every
\mathfrak{c}_{i}\in S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{i}}
, we get
t_{i}\wedge\chi_{S}\models\theta^{S}_{i}
.
Since
\bigvee_{S\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{i}}}\chi_{S}
is tautological by construction of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{i}}
, this gives us

t_{i}\models\psi_{i}
for both
i=1,2
. It remains to show
\psi_{1}\wedge\psi_{2}\models\bot
. Assume the opposite. Then there are
S_{1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{1}}
and
S_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{2}}
such that
\theta_{1}^{S_{1}}\wedge\theta_{2}^{S_{2}}
is satisfiable. By definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}_{i}}
, we have models
\mathcal{M}_{i}
satisfying
\chi_{S_{i}}
for both
i=1,2
. The pair
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}
of models must adhere to some case
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}
. Then we have
\mathfrak{c}_{i}\in S_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.
But this implies
\theta^{S_{i}}_{i}\models\mathsf{sep}_{i}(\mathfrak{c},t_{i})
. This is a contradiction because
\mathsf{Case},\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}
separates
\{(\{t_{1}\},\{t_{2}\})\}
and hence

\mathsf{sep}_{1}(\mathfrak{c},t_{1})\wedge\mathsf{sep}_{2}(\mathfrak{c},t_{2})\models\bot
.
∎

We now provide the construction of hyperseparators. The construction proceeds by induction on the number of rounds of the elimination procedure, and in round
\ell
we build a separator for all hypermosaics eliminated up to that round.

Lemma 17
.

For every round
\ell
of the elimination procedure, we can compute a hyperseparator
\mathsf{Case},\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}
which separates every hypermosaic eliminated up to that round.

Proof.

We first provide the inductive construction and then prove its correctness.
In the base case, we let the case distinction determine the type of each nominal, that is:

\displaystyle\mathsf{Case}=\{(\mathfrak{c}_{1},\mathfrak{c}_{2})\ |\ \mathfrak{c}_{1},\mathfrak{c}_{2}:\mathsf{Nom}_{0}\to\mathsf{Type}\}.

This case distinction is exhaustive because every nominal in every model has some type.
For every case
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}
, the functions
\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}
ignore
\mathfrak{c}
and map types to their atomic
\sigma
-consequences:

\mathsf{sep}_{i}(\mathfrak{c},t)=\bigwedge(t\cap\{r,\neg r\ |\ r\in\sigma\})

for every

and

and thus
\mathsf{Case},\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}
is a hyperseparator. Moreover, it separates every
\sigma
-inconsistent hypermosaic.

We now deal with the inductive case.
Assume we have a hyperseparator
\mathsf{Case},\mathsf{sep}_{1},\mathsf{sep}_{2}
which separates all hypermosaics eliminated up to round
\ell
. We construct a hyperseparator
\mathsf{Case}^{+},\mathsf{sep}_{1}^{+},\mathsf{sep}_{2}^{+}

\ell+1

We first refine each case in

.
For
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}

-formulae which are in the image of

\mathsf{sep}_{2}(\mathfrak{c},\ \cdot\ )
.
We split
\mathfrak{c}
into subcases specifying which
\mathsf{Sep}_{\mathfrak{c}}
-type

\mathsf{Case}^{+}=\left\{(\mathfrak{c}_{1}\otimes f_{1},\ \ \mathfrak{c}_{2}\otimes f_{2})\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case},\\
&f_{1},f_{2}:\mathsf{Nom}_{0}\to\text{$\mathsf{Sep}_{\mathfrak{c}}$-$\mathsf{Type}$}\end{aligned}\right\}

where

denotes pointwise conjunction, that is,

We say that

are of the shape

. For every

.

We now construct
\mathsf{sep}^{+}_{1}
and
\mathsf{sep}^{+}_{2}

\mathfrak{c}\in\mathsf{Case}
define a

-star-type

For every type

Uniform Interpolants

We show that uniform interpolants behave very differently from Craig interpolants by proving that the existence of uniform interpolants is undecidable for all fragments of FO containing
\mathcal{H}
, and hence for all languages considered in this article.

•

•

Typically, one assumes that

. The

\mathcal{L}
is the problem to decide for

and signature

\mathcal{L}(\sigma)
-interpolant for
\varphi

14

34

Let
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}

of FO containing

The proof uses the fact that in

\displaystyle\langle{E}\rangle(\mathsf{min}\wedge\mathsf{even})

\displaystyle[{E}](\mathsf{even}\rightarrow[{S}]\neg\mathsf{even}),\quad[{E}](\neg\mathsf{even}\rightarrow[{S}]\mathsf{even})

\displaystyle[{E}]([{S}]\bot\rightarrow\neg\mathsf{even}),\quad[{E}][{S}]\neg\mathsf{min}

8
Discussion

We have established the first elementary upper bounds for computing Craig interpolants in a family of logics for which the CIP cannot be readily recovered. A central open question is to determine the exact complexity of the problem, and thus to close the gap between our fourfold-exponential upper bounds and two- or threefold-exponential lower bounds. Beyond these, our results prompt several broader research questions. We discuss the robustness of our interpolant construction method when the input languages are extended and when the underlying notion of interpolant is modified.

Extending the Input Language

Many different constraints on accessibility relations have been considered in both modal and description logic
[
3
,
17
]
. The extension of our results to role inclusions constraints,
R\subseteq S
, is straightforward. Also, for logics in
\mathsf{HL}
our approach extends in a straightforward way to fundamental constraints such as transitivity and reflexivity. For instance, to deal with transitive relations, it suffices to strengthen the definition of
t\rightsquigarrow_{R}t^{\prime}
by demanding that also
\langle{R}\rangle\chi\in t^{\prime}
implies
\langle{R}\rangle\chi\in t
and to observe that a bisimulation along a relation
R
is also a bisimulation along the transitive closure of
R
. On the other hand, more substantial adaptations appear to be necessary if one adds inverse relations to the language. Whether this is possible at all remains open. Further examples of logics lacking CIP for which the interpolant existence problem is decidable but no elementary bound on the size of Craig interpolants is known include the guarded and two-variable fragments of FO, weak K4, and various fragments of first-order modal logics
[
23
,
28
,
29
]
.

Modifying the Notion of Interpolant

In many applications of interpolants in description logic, it is desirable to obtain interpolants in languages that are strictly weaker than the input language. For example, given graded input formulae one may seek grades-free interpolants, as e.g. in
[
27
]
. Our method is highly robust under this kind of restriction. Indeed, for any
\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}
with
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\in\mathsf{HL}\cup\mathsf{GHL}
, straightforward adaptations of our algorithms show that interpolants in
\mathcal{L}_{1}

between formulae in
\mathcal{L}_{2}
can be computed, whenever they exist, in fourfold exponential time. We note, however, that further extensions of these results may not be feasible. For instance, once inverse relations are added, the existence of interpolants in
\mathcal{H}
between formulae in
\mathcal{G}
becomes undecidable
[
27
]
.

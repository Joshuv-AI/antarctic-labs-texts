Explicit Constructions of Sum-Rank Metric Codes from Quadratic Kummer Extensions
Yunlong Zhu, Chang-An Zhao

Abstract
This paper presents new constructions of sum-rank metric codes derived from algebraic function fields, while the existing results about such codes remain limited. A central challenge in this field involves the determination of parameters. We address this challenge through quadratic Kummer extensions, and propose two general constructions of 2 × 2 2\times 2 sum-rank codes. The novel sum-rank metric code constructions derived from algebraic function fields demonstrate superior code lengths compared to conventional coding paradigms, particularly when contrasted with linearized Reed-Solomon codes under equivalent code parameter constraints. We also establish explicit parameters including dimensions and minimum distances of our codes. Finally, an illustrative example through elliptic function fields is constructed for validating the theoretical framework. Index terms: Sum-rank metric codes, algebraic geometry codes, function fields, elliptic curves.

Algebraic function fields over finite fields provide a foundational framework for Hamming-metric code construction

Our contributions

In contrast to the construction in
[
4
]
, we propose an alternative method to construct sum-rank metric codes via function fields. The key tool of our construction is the
local integral basis
of a place in function field extensions. Specifically, let
\mathcal{O}_{P}
be the valuation ring at a place
P
of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
F^{\prime}/F
a finite separable function field extension. The integral closure
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{P}
in
F^{\prime}
forms a vector space over
\mathcal{O}_{P}
. Consequently, a function in
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}^{\prime}_{q}
that is regular at the places lying over
P
corresponds to a matrix over
F
, where
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}_{q}
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. When
F^{\prime}/F
is Galois, we establish a linearized-polynomial-like map on
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
through the direct sum of Riemann-Roch spaces, yielding sum-rank metric codes via evaluation of the function matrix at rational places. While our construction overlaps with
[
4
]
, it differs substantially by emphasizing the intrinsic structure of function field extensions. By sacrificing certain subspaces in the direct sum of Riemann-Roch spaces, we explicitly determine the parameters of our sum-rank codes.

II-A

Places and divisors

Let
q
be a prime power. An algebraic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
denotes a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
with genus
g(F)
, where
x
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A place
P
is the maximal ideal of a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{P}
in
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
with

\deg(P)=[\mathcal{O}_{P}/P:\mathbb{F}_{q}].

If
\deg(P)=1
, then
P
is called rational. The prime element of
P
is a function
t\in P
such that
P=t\mathcal{O}_{p}
. Any non-zero
z\in F
has a unique representation
z=t^{n}u
with
u\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{P}
. The associated discrete valuation

v_{P}:F\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}

satisfies
v_{P}(z)=n
and
v_{P}(0)=\infty
. The strict triangle inequality of
v_{P}
is characterized as follows:

Lemma II.1
.

For any discrete valuation
v_{P}
,

v_{P}(x+y)\geq\min\{v_{P}(x),v_{P}(y)\}

holds for all
x,y\in F
. Moreover, if
v_{P}(x)\neq v_{P}(y)
, then
v_{P}(x+y)=\min\{v_{P}(x),v_{P}(y)\}
.

A divisor
G
is defined as the formal sum of places

G:=\sum\limits_{P}n_{P}P

with
n_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\deg(G)=\sum\limits_{P}n_{P}
. For any function
z\in F
,

(z):=\sum\limits_{P}v_{P}(z)P

is called the principal divisor. Given two divisors
G_{1},G_{2}
, we write
G_{1}\leq G_{2}
if
n_{P}(G_{1})\leq n_{P}(G_{2})
for all places. The associated Riemann-Roch space is defined as

\mathcal{L}(G):=\{z\in F\mid(z)+G\geq 0\}\cup\{0\}.

The space is a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with dimension given by the Riemann-Roch theorem:

Lemma II.2
.

[
41
, Theorem 1.4.17]

Let
\ell(G)
denote the dimension of
\mathcal{L}(G)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

\ell(G)\geq\deg(G)-g(F)+1.

Moreover, if
\deg(G)\geq 2g(F)-1
, then

\ell(G)=\deg(G)-g(F)+1.

II-B

Extensions of function fields

A finite algebraic function field
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is called an algebraic extension of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
if
F^{\prime}/F
is an algebraic field extension with
[F^{\prime}:F]<\infty
and
m\geq 1
. A place
P^{\prime}
of
F^{\prime}
is said lying over
P
if
P\subseteq P^{\prime}
, denoted
P^{\prime}|P
. The
ramification index

e(P^{\prime}|P)
is defined as the integer satisfying

v_{P^{\prime}}(x)=e(P^{\prime}|P)\cdot v_{P}(x)

for all
x\in F
, while the relative degree of
P^{\prime}
over
P
is given by

f(P^{\prime}|P)=[\mathcal{O}_{P^{\prime}}/P^{\prime}:\mathcal{O}_{P}/P].

For any place
P
of
F
, there exist only finitely many places
P^{\prime}
of
F^{\prime}
with
P^{\prime}|P
. The conorm of
P
is defined as

\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(P):=\sum\limits_{P^{\prime}|P}e(P^{\prime}|P)\cdot P^{\prime}

and

\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}\left(\sum\limits_{P}n_{P}P\right):=\sum n_{P}\cdot\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(P).

Consequently, for any non-zero function
z\in F
, the principal divisor of
z
in
F^{\prime}
has the form

(z)_{F^{\prime}}=\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}((z)_{F}).

The fundamental equality of places is stated as follows.

Proposition II.1
.

[
41
, Theorem 3.3.11]

Let
P
be a place of
F
, and let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
be all places of
F^{\prime}
lying over
P
. Denote
e_{i}=e(P_{i}|P)
and
f_{i}=f(P_{i}|P)
. Then

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}f_{i}=[F^{\prime}:F].

If
[F^{\prime}:F]=n
and there exist exactly
n
distinct places lying over
P
, then
P

splits completely
in
F^{\prime}/F
. In this case, it follows that
e_{i}=f_{i}=1
. When
e(P^{\prime}|P)>1
for some
P^{\prime}
, the place
P
is ramified in
F^{\prime}/F
. Moreover, if there exists a place
P^{\prime}
with
e(P^{\prime}|P)=n
, then
P
is
totally ramified
in
F^{\prime}/F
.

II-C

Galois Extensions and Kummer Extensions

An extension
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
of a function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is Galois if
F^{\prime}/F
is a finite Galois extension. Let
\text{Gal}(F^{\prime}/F)
be the Galois group of
F^{\prime}/F
. For any place
P
of
F
, the Galois group acts transitively on the set of places lying over
P
.

Kummer extensions constitute a special class of Galois extensions. We present their definition and key properties.

Definition II.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
contain a primitive
n
-th root of unity
\zeta
. Suppose that
u\in F
satisfies

u\neq\omega^{d}

for all
\omega\in F
and
1<d|n
. Let
F^{\prime}=F(y)
with
y^{n}=u
be an extension of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
F^{\prime}
is called a Kummer extension of
F
with:

•

\text{Gal}(F^{\prime}/F)=\langle\sigma\rangle
where
\sigma(y)=\zeta y
;

•

For any place
P
of
F
and
P^{\prime}
lying over
P
,
e(P^{\prime}|P)=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,v_{P}(u))}
;

•

If there exists a place
Q
of
F
with
\gcd(n,v_{Q}(u))=1
, then
\mathbb{F}_{q}
remains the constant field of
F^{\prime}
, and

g(F^{\prime})=1+n(g+1)+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{P}(n-\gcd(n,v_{P}(u)))\deg(P);

•

For any place
P
with
v_{P}(u))\geq 0
, the basis
\{1,y,\ldots,y^{n-1}\}
is a local integral basis of
F^{\prime}/F
for
P
.

III-A

The Main Idea of Matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a function field and
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
a Galois extension of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
[F^{\prime}:F]=n
. Suppose that
P
is a place of
F
and
t\in F
is a prime element of
P
. The integral closure
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{P}
in
F^{\prime}
is given by

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{P_{i}}.

An integral basis
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
over
\mathcal{O}_{P}
is a basis of
F^{\prime}/F
satisfying

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{P}z_{i}.

The quotient
V:=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}/t\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
forms a vector space over
\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
with basis

\{z_{1}+t\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P},\ldots,z_{n}+t\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}\}.

For any
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(F^{\prime}/F)
and place
P^{\prime}
with
P^{\prime}|P
, observe that for
z\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
:

v_{P^{\prime}}(\sigma(z))=v_{\sigma^{-1}(P^{\prime})}(z)\geq 0

since
\sigma^{-1}(P^{\prime})|P
. This implies
\sigma(z)\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
, hence
\sigma(V)=V
. The set

\{\sigma(z_{1})+t\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P},\ldots,\sigma(z_{n})+t\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}\}

forms another basis of
V
over
\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
. Then
\sigma
induces a matrix
\bar{A}_{\sigma}
over
\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
with respect to
\{z_{i}\}
.

Similarly, for
f\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
, consider the multiplication
\mu_{f}:\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}\to\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
defined by
\mu_{f}(z)=fz
. This induces an
\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
-linear map
\bar{\mu}_{f}:V\to V
via

\bar{\mu}_{f}(z+t\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}):=fz+t\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}.

This induces a matrix
\bar{B}_{f}
with entries in
\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
.

Remark III.1
.

The matrix
A=(a_{ij})
associated to
\sigma
has entries in
\mathcal{O}_{P}
due to the integral basis property. Under the reduction map

\pi:\mathcal{O}_{P}\to\mathcal{O}_{P}/P,

we obtain
\bar{A}=(\pi(a_{ij}))
. This explains the notation
\bar{A}_{\sigma}
and
\bar{B}_{f}
.

When
P
is rational (i.e.,
\mathcal{O}_{P}/P=\mathbb{F}_{q}
), both
\sigma\in\text{Gal}l(F^{\prime}/F)
and
f\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
induce matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\text{Gal}(F^{\prime}/F)=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}\}
, and consider functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
. We define a linear operator

\displaystyle L:\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\sigma_{i}(g).

This
F^{\prime}
-linear operator naturally induces a linear morphism on
F
. We further define a map
\varepsilon_{P}
satisfying

\varepsilon_{P}(L)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}B_{i}=(M_{ij})

where
B_{i}
and
A_{i}
are matrices representing
\mu_{f_{i}}
and
\sigma_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, respectively. Furthermore, let

\bar{\varepsilon}_{P}(L)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bar{A_{i}}\bar{B_{i}}=(\pi(M_{ij})).

We establish the following lemma.

Lemma III.1
.

(i)

Let
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
be an integral basis of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
over
\mathcal{O}_{P}
. Then the matrix representing
L
with respect to this basis is
\varepsilon_{P}(L)
.

(ii)

The determinant
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))
belongs to
\mathcal{O}_{P}
.

(iii)

The kernel
{\rm ker}(L)\neq\{0\}
if and only if
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))=0
.

Proof.

(i) Let
g=g_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+g_{n}z_{n}\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
. For any
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(F^{\prime}/F)
and
f\in\mathcal{O}_{P}
, let
A_{\sigma}
and
B_{f}
denote the matrices defined above. Then

\mu_{f}(g)=[g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}]B_{f}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]^{T}

and

\sigma(g)=[g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}]A_{\sigma}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]^{T}.

Thus
f\sigma(g)=[g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}]B_{f}A_{\sigma}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]^{T}
. The conclusion follows from the linearity of
\mu_{f}
and
\sigma
.

(ii) The result immediately follows since all coefficients of
A_{\sigma_{i}}
and
B_{f_{i}}
are contained in
\mathcal{O}_{P}
.

(iii) Consider the equation

\varepsilon_{P}(L)^{T}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{T}=[0,\ldots,0].

Any solution
[g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}]
satisfies
L(g_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+g_{n}z_{n})=0
. Therefore
{\rm ker}(L)
contains non-zero elements if and only if the system has non-trivial solutions, which occurs precisely when
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))=0
.
∎

III-B

Sum-rank Codes in General Case

Now we present the construction of sum-rank metric codes. Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s}
be distinct rational places of
F
. Assume that the divisor
Q_{0}
is totally ramified in
F^{\prime}/F
with
P_{0}|Q_{0}
. Let
G=nQ_{0}
,
G^{\prime}=nP_{0}
and
D=Q_{1}+\cdots+Q_{s}
. Define the evaluation map:

\displaystyle\text{ev}_{D,1}:\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to\Pi

\displaystyle L=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\{(\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{1}}(L),\ldots,\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{s}}(L))\}

Since
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
have poles only at
P_{0}
, they belong to
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{Q_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Thus
\varepsilon_{Q_{i}}(L)
are well-defined. Moreover, the function space
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
forms an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear space and
L
is
F
-linear. It follows that
\text{ev}_{D,1}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map. Thus the image of
\text{ev}_{D,1}
is a subspace of
\Pi
. The code
C_{sr,1}(D,G)
is defined as the image of
\text{ev}_{D,1}
.

Remark III.2
.

The code
C_{sr,1}
resembles the code
C(1,G,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s})
defined in
[
4
, Definition 4]
. In their work, E. Berardini and X. Caruso constructed codes through the isomorphism chain

D_{F^{\prime}_{i},x}\to D_{F^{\prime}_{i},1}\to\text{End}_{F_{i}}(F^{\prime}_{i})

where
F_{i}
denotes the completion of
F
at
Q_{i}
, while
F^{\prime}_{i}
represents the product of completions of
F^{\prime}
at places lying over
Q_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. Our approach directly employs
L
to express linearized-polynomial-like maps, corresponding to elements of
D_{F^{\prime},1}
. We subsequently utilize the integral closure
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{Q_{i}}
to construct explicit matrices
\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{i}}(L)
, where
\varepsilon_{Q_{i}}(L)
is induced by
L
at
Q_{i}
.

Furthermore, while previous work proposed several hypotheses for code parameter determination, the ring
D_{F^{\prime}_{i},1}
fails to satisfy all necessary conditions. In subsequent sections, we provide more explicit dimension and minimum distance bounds by considering subspaces of
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
.

Proposition III.1
.

The length
n^{2}s
of
C_{sr,1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfies

n^{2}s\leq n^{2}(q+2g(F)\sqrt{q}).

Proof.

In the construction of
C_{sr,1}
, each rational place
Q\neq Q_{0}
yields a well-defined
n\times n
matrix
\bar{\varepsilon}_{P}(L)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By the Hasse-Weil bound, the function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
contains at most
q+1+2g(F)\sqrt{q}
rational places, implying
s\leq q+2g(F)\sqrt{q}
.
∎

IV
Sum-Rank Metric Codes via Quadratic Extension of Algebraic Function Fields

IV-A

Parameters of the General Construction

We first establish some necessary lemmas before deriving the dimension and minimum sum-rank distance for
C_{sr,1}
.

Lemma IV.1
.

Suppose that
Q\neq P
is a rational place of
F
with
Q
totally ramified in
F^{\prime}/F
. Let
Q^{\prime}
be the place of
F^{\prime}
lying over
Q
. For
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(kQ^{\prime})
, we have

\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\in\mathcal{L}(kQ).

Furthermore, if there exists
f_{i}
satisfying

v_{Q^{\prime}}(f_{i})<v_{Q^{\prime}}(f_{j}),\forall j\neq i,

then
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\neq 0
.

Proof.

Since
f_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(kQ^{\prime})
, the coefficients of
B_{f_{i}}
are contained in
\mathcal{L}(kQ^{\prime})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. The discrete valuation of
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))
at
Q^{\prime}
therefore exceeds the minimal valuation in its determinant expansion. Consequently, we have
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\in\mathcal{L}(knQ^{\prime})
. Since
Q
is totally ramified, the conorm of
Q
is

\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(Q)=nQ^{\prime}.

By Lemma
III.1
(ii) and the conorm properties, we have

v_{Q}(\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L)))\leq k.

Note that for any
Q^{\prime}
of
F^{\prime}
, there exists only one
Q
in
F
with
Q^{\prime}|Q
, thus
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))
only has poles at
Q
. Therefore
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\in\mathcal{L}(kQ)
. For any non-zero
g\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
, the equality

v_{Q^{\prime}}(\sigma_{i}(g))=v_{Q^{\prime}}(\sigma_{1}(g))

holds for
i=1,\ldots,n
. W.l.o.g, assume that
v_{Q^{\prime}}(f_{1})<v_{Q^{\prime}}(f_{j})
when
2\leq j\leq n
. This yields

v_{Q^{\prime}}(f_{1}\sigma_{1}(g))<v_{Q^{\prime}}(f_{j}\sigma_{j}(g)).

By the strict triangle inequality, we have

v_{Q^{\prime}}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\sigma_{j}(g)\right)=v_{Q^{\prime}}(f_{1}\sigma_{1}(g))<v_{Q^{\prime}}(\sigma_{1}(g)).

Thus
L(g)\neq 0
for
g\neq 0
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
III.1
(iii).
∎

Lemma IV.2
.

Let
P
be a place of
F
with a prime element
t
. If
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\neq 0
for
L
associated tp
Q\neq P
while
rank(\bar{\varepsilon}_{P}(L))<n
, then
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\in P
.

Proof.

The condition
rank(\bar{\varepsilon}_{P}(L))<n
means
\det(\bar{\varepsilon}_{P_{\alpha}}(L))=0
. Let
\pi_{P}
denote the residue class map at
P
. For
\varepsilon_{P}(L)=(M_{ij})
, we have

\bar{\varepsilon}_{P}(L)=(\pi_{P}(M_{ij}))=(M_{ij})(P).

Therefore

\det(\bar{\varepsilon}_{P}(L))=\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))(P).

Since
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))(P)=0
, it follows that
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\in P
.
∎

Lemma IV.3
.

Let
k,k_{1}
be integers with
k>k_{1}\geq 2g(F)-1
, and
P
a rational place of
F
. Define

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kP):=\{f|-k\leq v_{P}(f)<-k_{1}\}\cup\{0\}.

Then
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kP)
forms a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of dimension
k-k_{1}
.

Proof.

By the Riemann-Roch theorem, we obtain that
\mathcal{L}(kP)
and
\mathcal{L}(k_{1}P)
are vector spaces over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with dimensions
\ell_{k}:=k-g(F)+1
and
\ell_{k_{1}}:=k_{1}-g(F)+1
, respectively. Thus,

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kP):=\mathcal{L}(kP)\setminus\mathcal{L}(k_{1}P)\cup\{0\}.

For
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kP)
and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we observe that
g_{1}+g_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(kP)
and
ag_{1}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kP)
. It remains to show
g_{1}+g_{2}\notin\mathcal{L}(k_{1}P)
.

Let
\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots,\omega_{\ell_{k}}\}
be a basis for
\mathcal{L}(kP)
. Since
k_{1}\geq 2g-1
, the space
\mathcal{L}(k_{1}P)
has a basis contained in
\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots,\omega_{\ell_{k}}\}
. Suppose that

g_{1}+g_{2}=\sum\limits_{i=\ell_{k_{1}}+1}^{\ell_{k}}a_{i}\omega_{i}

with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It follows that
g_{1}+g_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(k_{1}P)
implies
g_{1}+g_{2}=a_{1}\omega_{1}+\cdots+a_{\ell_{k_{1}}}\omega_{\ell_{k_{1}}}
. The linear independence of basis elements forces all coefficients to vanish, which means
g_{1}+g_{2}=0\in\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kP)
.
∎

Theorem IV.1
.

Suppose that
F^{\prime}/F
is a Kummer extension of degree 2 with a totally ramified place
Q_{0}
in
F^{\prime}/F
, and
Q^{\prime}_{0}|Q_{0}
. Let
s,k,k_{1}
be integers satisfying
s\geq 2
and
2g(F)-1\leq k_{1}<k<2s
. For
s
distinct places
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s}
, the code

C_{sr,1}:=\{(\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{1}}(L),\ldots,\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{s}}(L))|L=f_{1}\sigma_{1}+f_{2}\sigma_{2},f_{1}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kQ^{\prime}_{0}),f_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(k_{1}Q^{\prime}_{0})\}

has parameters
[4s,\ell_{k},d_{sr}\geq 2s-k]
.

Proof.

The code
C_{sr,1}
has length exactly
4s
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Its dimension is determined by

k-k_{1}+\ell_{k_{1}}-\dim(\mathcal{L}(kQ_{\infty}-\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}Q_{s}))).

Since
k<2s
, the evaluation map
\text{ev}_{D,1}
is injective, which implies that the dimension of
C_{sr,1}
simplifies to
k-k_{1}+\ell_{k_{1}}=\ell_{k}
.

Let
f_{1}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kQ^{\prime}_{0})
and
f_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(k_{1}Q^{\prime}_{0})
with
f_{1}\neq 0
. Suppose that
rank(\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{i}}(L))=d_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
. Then the codeword of
L
has sum-rank distance
d_{L}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}d_{i}
. By Lemma
IV.2
, we have

v_{Q_{i}}(\det(\varepsilon_{Q_{i}}(L)))\geq 2-d_{i}

for each
i
. Furthermore, Lemma
IV.1
shows

\det(\varepsilon_{Q_{i}}(L))\in\mathcal{L}(kQ_{0}).

From
v_{Q^{\prime}_{0}}(f_{1})<v_{Q^{\prime}_{0}}(f_{2})
and
f_{1}\neq 0
, we get
\det(\varepsilon_{P_{\alpha}}(L))\neq 0
. When
f_{1}=0
, the matrix
\varepsilon_{Q_{i}}(L)
takes the form

A_{\sigma_{2}}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}&f_{21},&f_{22}\\
&f_{22}u,&f_{21}\end{bmatrix}

(1)

where
f_{2}=f_{21}z_{1}+f_{22}z_{2}
. Then It follows from
\det(\varepsilon_{Q_{i}}(L))=0
that
u-f_{*}^{2}
belong to
F
for a certain function
f_{*}
. Thus
\det(\varepsilon_{Q_{i}}(L))
cannot vanish as
u\neq\omega^{2}
for any
\omega\in F
. Therefore
\det(\varepsilon_{P_{\alpha}}(L))
has at most
k
zeros. This implies that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}(2-d_{i})\leq k.

Consequently, the codeword satisfies
d_{L}\geq 2s-k
. Therefore we have
d_{sr}\geq 2s-k
. This completes the whole proof.
∎

IV-B

New Construction for Quadratic Extension

For a quadratic extension
F^{\prime}/F
, we provide an alternative construction by utilizing completely split places. Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{s}
be distinct rational places of
F
. Consider a place
Q_{0}
that splits completely in
F^{\prime}/F
with
Q_{0,1}
and
Q_{0,2}
lying over
Q_{0}
. Let
G=nQ,G_{i}=nQ_{0,i}
and
D=Q_{1}+\cdots+Q_{s}
. The evaluation map is constructed as follows:

\displaystyle\text{ev}_{D,2}:\bigoplus_{i=1}^{2}\mathcal{L}(G_{i})

\displaystyle\to\Pi

\displaystyle L=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\{(\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{1}}(L),\ldots,\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{s}}(L))\}

The image of
\text{ev}_{D,2}
forms a linear subspace of
\Pi
. Subsequently, the code
C_{sr,2}(D,G)
is defined as the image. To analyze the parameters of
C_{sr,2}(D,G)
, we require the following lemma.

Lemma IV.4
.

Let
Q\neq P
be a rational divisor of
F
that splits completely in
F^{\prime}/F
, and
Q_{1},Q_{2}
the places of
F^{\prime}
lying over
Q
. For
f_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(kQ_{i})
, we have

\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\in\mathcal{L}(kQ).

Furthermore, if there exists
f_{i}
such that

v_{Q_{i}}(f_{i})<v_{Q_{j}}(f_{j}),\forall j\neq i,

then
\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\neq 0
.

Proof.

First, every term in the determinant expansion of
\varepsilon_{P}(L)
must be contained in
\mathcal{L}(n(Q_{1}+Q_{2}))
. Note that the conorm of
Q
is

\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(Q)=Q_{1}+Q_{2}.

Following a similar argument to Lemma
IV.1
, we obtain

\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\in\mathcal{L}(kQ).

Second, w.l.o.g, assume that
v_{Q_{1}}(f_{1})<v_{Q_{2}}(f_{2})
. For any non-zero function
g
, we have

v_{Q_{2}}(\sigma_{2}(g))=v_{Q_{1}}(\sigma_{1}(g)).

If
L(g)=0
, then

v_{Q_{1}}(\sigma_{2}(g))=v_{Q_{1}}(f_{1}\sigma_{1}(g))<v_{Q_{1}}(\sigma_{1}(g)).

This implies

v_{Q_{2}}(\sigma_{1}(g))<v_{Q_{2}}(\sigma_{2}(g))

and consequently

v_{Q_{2}}(f_{1}\sigma_{1}(g))<v_{Q_{2}}(f_{2}\sigma_{2}(g)).

It follows that
v_{Q_{2}}(L(g))<\infty
, contradicting
L(g)=0
. Hence
L(g)\neq 0
for all non-zero
g\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
. The conclusion follows from Lemma
III.1
(iii).
∎

The subsequent result parallels Theorem
IV.1
, thus we omit the details of the proof.

IV-C

Examples from Elliptic Function Fields

An elliptic function field
\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a quadratic extension of the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}_{q}
of genus 1. This serves as a canonical example since evaluation places can be selected from all
q+1
places of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, limiting the code length to
q
. Nevertheless, this suffices to illustrate our framework.

In this section, we consider the case when
q
is odd, as elliptic function fields over even characteristic are Artin-Schreier extensions. Specifically, let
\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)
where
x,y
are transcendental over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and satisfy the smooth, irreducible equation:

y^{2}=f_{\mathcal{E}}

with
f_{\mathcal{E}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
being a square-free cubic polynomial. The Galois group
\text{Aut}(\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x))=\{Id,\sigma\}
is cyclic of order 2, where

\sigma(y)=-y.

Let
P_{\infty}
be the place at infinity of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
, which is totally ramified in
\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. Suppose that
Q_{\infty}
is the place of
\mathcal{E}
lying over
P_{\infty}
. The basis
\{1,y\}
of
\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
forms an integral basis for all places of
\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
except
P_{\infty}
. Consequently, for any rational place
P\neq P_{\infty}
, the matrix induced by
\sigma
on
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
over
\mathcal{O}_{P}
is

\begin{bmatrix}1,&0\\
0,&-1\end{bmatrix}.

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be two functions over
\mathcal{E}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where each
f_{i}
has the form
f_{i}=f_{i1}+f_{i2}y
with
f_{i1}
and
f_{i2}
belonging to
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
for
i=1,2
. For any rational place
P\neq P_{\infty}
, the matrix induced by
f_{i}
on
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{P}
over
\mathcal{O}_{P}
is

\begin{bmatrix}f_{i1},&f_{i2}\\
f_{i2}f_{\mathcal{E}},&f_{i1}\end{bmatrix}.

Let
L=f_{1}+f_{2}\sigma
. Then we obtain

\varepsilon_{P}(L)=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}+f_{21},&f_{12}+f_{22}\\
(f_{12}-f_{22})f_{\mathcal{E}},&f_{11}-f_{21}\end{bmatrix}

and

\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))=f_{11}^{2}-f_{21}^{2}-(f_{12}^{2}-f_{22}^{2})f_{\mathcal{E}}.

For positive integers
k,k_{1}
, define sum-rank codes
C_{sr,1}
and
C_{sr,2}
as follows:

C_{sr,1}:=\{(\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{1}}(L),\ldots,\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{s}}(L))|L=f_{1}+f_{2}\sigma,f_{1}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kQ_{\infty}),f_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(k_{1}Q_{\infty})\}

and

C_{sr,2}:=\{(\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{1}}(L),\ldots,\bar{\varepsilon}_{Q_{s}}(L))|L=f_{1}+f_{2}\sigma,f_{1}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k_{1}}(kQ_{0,1}),f_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(k_{1}Q_{0,2})\}

where
Q_{0,1}
and
Q_{0,2}
are rational places lying over
Q_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Through the Singleton bound combined with Theorems
IV.1
and
IV.2
, we derive

4s-2k\leq 2d_{sr,1},2d_{sr,2}\leq 4s-k+2,

where
s\leq q
. The following two examples are given by SageMath
[
42
]
.

Example IV.1
.

Let
q=7
and consider the elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}:y^{2}=x^{3}+3
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The rational places of

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we conducted two families of
2\times 2
sum-rank metric codes by exploiting quadratic Kummer extensions of algebraic function fields. By representing elements in the extension fields as matrices over the base field through explicit algebraic mappings, we rigorously determined the parameters of the proposed codes, including their minimum sum-rank distances and code rates. To demonstrate the practical applicability of our framework, we provided concrete instantiations over elliptic function fields.

To the best of our knowledge, these code constructions are novel in the context of algebraic-geometric (AG) codes for the sum-rank metric. Prior to this work, the only systematic study of AG codes in this metric was established by Berardini and Caruso
[
4
]
, who proposed a general framework for such codes. However, our methodology differs fundamentally from theirs by explicitly exploiting the intrinsic algebraic structures of function field extensions—particularly through matrix representations derived from quadratic Kummer extensions—to control code parameters and optimize distance properties.

In this paper, we investigate Kummer extensions defined by the equation

y^{2}=u\quad\text{where }u\notin\{\omega^{2}\mid\omega\in F\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

This specific choice of non-square elements
u
enables us to rigorously establish the non-degeneracy condition

\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\neq 0

in the restricted case where
f_{1}=0
. The principal challenge in generalizing these constructions to
n\times n
matrices stems from the need to preserve the non-vanishing determinant property

\det(\varepsilon_{P}(L))\neq 0\quad\text{for arbitrary }n\geq 2.

We intend to address this dimensionality constraint through more flexible algebraic frameworks in subsequent work.

Regarding the sum-rank distance of our AG codes in the sum-rank metric, for a fixed dimension
k-g(F)+1
, we observe the following approximate relationship:

\max d_{sr}-\min d_{sr}\approx\frac{k+g(F)+1}{2}.

This raises an interesting problem: whether techniques developed for constructing MDS AG codes
[
11
]
can be adapted to the sum-rank metric setting to achieve MSRD AG codes. Following the approach for AG codes with narrow bounds (such as elliptic codes
[
11
]
), we propose this problem as an important objective for our subsequent research.

From Single Genes to Systemic Networks: Transcriptomic Insights into Traditional Chinese Medicine for Heart Failure
Authors: Yuan-Yuan Li, Shi-Han Sun, Yan Jiang, Chen-Chen Liu, Shi-Qi Cheng, Jin-Wen Wu, Ming-Tai Chen, Xiao-Lin Li, Gang Luo, Meng-Nan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHeart failure (HF) represents the terminal stage of diverse cardiovascular pathologies and has emerged as a global public health crisis, characterized by increasing prevalence and persistently poor long-term prognosis. Although modern pharmacological therapies have advanced considerably, high rates of disease recurrence and recurrent hospitalizations continue to profoundly impair patients’ quality of life. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has been reported in preclinical and some clinical studies to offer potential benefits, particularly in enhancing cardiac function, alleviating clinical symptoms, and slowing disease progression. Numerous TCM compound formulations are commonly used in clinical practice in China and are gradually attracting international research attention. Nevertheless, the inherent complexity of TCM compositions, their multi-target mechanisms of action, and the lack of clarity regarding their molecular underpinnings remain substantial obstacles to broader acceptance within contemporary medicine. In recent years, driven by the rapid evolution of high-throughput sequencing technologies, transcriptomics has become a pivotal tool in TCM research, facilitating the exploration of disease mechanisms and drug targets. This narrative review synthesizes research progress on TCM interventions for HF from a transcriptomic perspective. It traces the development of major transcriptomic technologies, critically evaluates their respective strengths and limitations, and synthesizes current findings on how TCM modulates differentially expressed genes, signaling pathways, and cellular processes at multiple regulatory levels. Furthermore, it examines the potential therapeutic impact of TCM on key pathological pathways, including myocardial remodeling, energy metabolism dysregulation, and inflammatory responses. By identifying existing research gaps and outlining future directions, this review seeks to elucidate the mechanisms of TCM treatment for HF through modern scientific frameworks, thereby offering novel insights for addressing this challenging condition.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) affects at least 26 million people worldwide, with a rising prevalence.1 It represents the terminal stage of various cardiovascular diseases, clinically characterized by impaired cardiac pumping function leading to inadequate organ perfusion and fluid retention.1 Although existing treatments such as beta-blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers (ACEI/ARB), and diuretics have yielded certain improvements in symptoms, long-term prognosis remains poor. Recurrence and hospitalization rates remain high, severely impacting patients’ quality of life and imposing a substantial healthcare burden. Complementary to such mainstream therapies, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has been reported in preclinical studies to offer potential advantages in treating HF, with preliminary evidence suggesting favorable effects in improving cardiac function, alleviating symptoms, and delaying disease progression. Compound formulations developed based on TCM theory, such as Astragalus membranaceus injection, Salvia miltiorrhiza droplets, and Pueraria lobata and Scutellaria baicalensis pills, have been widely used in clinical practice in China and are gradually attracting international research attention. However, the complex composition and multiple target sites of TCM compounds mean their mechanisms of action remain incompletely clarified, hindering their international development and modern scientific interpretation.

With the advancement of high-throughput sequencing technologies, transcriptomics has emerged as a vital tool for elucidating key disease-related genes, pathway regulatory networks, and drug mechanisms of action. By characterizing RNA expression alterations in disease states, transcriptomics systematically reveals the molecular underpinnings of disease onset and progression, thereby supporting the identification of novel therapeutic targets. Within TCM research, transcriptomics applications are increasingly prevalent, particularly in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, oncology, and neurological disorders. These studies demonstrate the multi-pathway, multi-target regulatory capabilities of active components and compound formulations derived from Chinese herbal medicine. In recent years, a growing number of studies have sought to apply transcriptomics to elucidate the mechanisms underpinning TCM interventions for HF. Using approaches such as differentially expressed gene (DEG) analysis, functional enrichment based on Gene Ontology/Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (GO/KEGG), and protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks, these investigations have uncovered TCM’s regulatory effects on pathological pathways including myocardial remodeling, energy metabolism, and inflammatory factors. This review examines transcriptomic research progress in TCM treatment for HF, focusing on potential mechanisms, key targets, current research gaps, and future directions. It aims to offer a theoretical framework and technical support for the modernization and precision-oriented development of TCM.2

Pathophysiological Mechanisms and Phenotypic Heterogeneity in Heart Failure

HF is a complex clinical syndrome caused by any structural or functional impairment of ventricular filling or ejection, and primarily manifests as dyspnea and fatigue. The clinical syndrome of HF may be caused by diseases of the pericardium, myocardium, endocardium, heart valves, or great vessels, or by certain metabolic abnormalities, but in most HF patients, symptoms result from impaired left ventricular (LV) myocardial function. Contemporary guidelines classify HF according to LVEF into heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) (LVEF ≤40%), heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF) (LVEF 41–49%), and HFpEF (LVEF ≥50%). For HFmrEF and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), diagnosis requires not only the corresponding LVEF range but also clinical evidence supporting HF, including evidence of increased left ventricular filling pressures.3

Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction

HFpEF is recognized as a heterogeneous syndrome with complex pathophysiology, posing substantial diagnostic challenges. The diastolic dysfunction central to HFpEF is well exemplified by hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, an inherited disorder of sarcomeric proteins in which left ventricular hypertrophy coexists with preserved systolic function, providing a monogenic paradigm for non-ischemic diastolic HF.4,5 Restrictive cardiomyopathies, though less prevalent, further illustrate the spectrum of impaired ventricular filling and increased myocardial stiffness that characterizes this HF phenotype, particularly in infiltrative processes where advanced tissue characterization becomes indispensable.6,7 Within the broader HFpEF population, a two-phase prediction approach was developed by Uchmanowicz et al in 560 HF patients, with an 8-point clinical risk score established in Phase 1 achieving 63.1% accuracy, and a decision tree model employed in Phase 2 improving accuracy to 73.0% with 81.2% specificity.8 Plasma metabolomic profiling performed by Naeem et al in 787 subjects identified asymmetric dimethylarginine—a nitric oxide synthase inhibitor—as uniquely elevated in HFpEF, implicating vascular dysregulation as a pathogenic driver.9 The metabolic effects of dapagliflozin were investigated by Selvaraj et al, who reported no significant alterations in ketone or branched-chain amino acid metabolites in HFpEF, a finding that contrasts with observations in HFrEF.10 Animal models of HFpEF were clustered into phenogroups by van Ham et al, with the aged hypertensive dog and db/db mouse identified as optimal representatives.11

Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction

In HFrEF, metabolic derangement is considered a central pathophysiological feature, and the syndrome is frequently attributable to identifiable cardiomyopathic substrates.9,12 Dilated cardiomyopathy is recognized as the prototypical genetic and structural aetiology underlying HFrEF, wherein mutations in sarcomeric and cytoskeletal genes drive progressive ventricular dilation and systolic failure, and where phenotypic expression is modulated by gene–environment interactions, including inflammatory insults, toxic exposures, and peripartum stressors.13,14 The inflammatory cascade initiated in myocarditis also contributes significantly to the HFrEF spectrum, as acute viral or immune-mediated myocardial injury may evolve into chronic systolic dysfunction.15–17 Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy, although classically affecting the right ventricle, has been increasingly recognized in biventricular and left-dominant variants that present with reduced ejection fraction, necessitating genotype-informed risk stratification for device-based therapy.18,19 Against this cardiomyopathic background, metabolomic profiling conducted by Naeem et al revealed that unsaturated medium- and long-chain acylcarnitines, along with the ketone body 3-hydroxybutyrate and its metabolite C4-OH carnitine, were uniquely elevated in HFrEF, with positive correlations observed with N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide, supporting a compensatory ketogenic response.9 A large-scale analysis of hospitalizations for HFrEF was performed by Greene et al across 22,677 US cases, of which 66% received uncomplicated intravenous diuretic therapy, whereas 34% required treatment escalation—including diuretic reinitiation, dose augmentation, combination therapy, or intensified regimens—and such escalation was associated with prolonged hospital stays and increased in-hospital as well as 30-day mortality.12 The phenotypic overlap between HFmrEF and both HFrEF and HFpEF was highlighted by Wu et al, who advocated for precision medicine approaches in subclassification.20

Heart Failure with Mildly Reduced Ejection Fraction

HFmrEF is positioned as an intermediate category between HFrEF and HFpEF, both clinically and pathophysiologically.20,21 This grey-zone entity has been conceptually aligned with recently characterized phenotypes in which systolic impairment is observed without overt chamber dilatation, as exemplified by non-dilated left ventricular cardiomyopathy, where myocardial scarring or global hypokinesia predominates over volumetric remodeling.22 In such patients, distinct clinical profiles have been described, including higher arrhythmic burden and variable patterns of myocardial fibrosis, and those in whom ventricular dilatation develops over the course of follow-up have been shown to face heightened adverse outcomes, underscoring the dynamic nature of this intermediate state and the necessity of serial surveillance.23 The overlap of HFmrEF with arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy and with post-myocarditis sequelae further illustrates that this category is not a uniform transitional stage but rather a convergence of diverse aetiologies that share a similar ejection fraction range.15,19 A large retrospective cohort analysis of 113,555 patients with HF was conducted by Feder et al, among whom 7.4% fulfilled the criteria for HFmrEF. In this cohort, lower adjusted incidence rates of palliative care consultation were observed in HFmrEF than in HFrEF, and the time to palliative care referral was found to be comparable to that of HFpEF, suggesting that patients with HFmrEF may receive less timely specialist supportive care despite comparable overall mortality.21 In the context of adults with congenital heart disease, the limited evidence base for HFmrEF was acknowledged by Pasqualin and Chessa, who noted that β-blockers and renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system inhibitors currently carry only a class IIb C recommendation in this population, and argued that ejection fraction-based subclassification may facilitate improved risk stratification and tailored management.24 Collectively, HFmrEF is increasingly understood not merely as a transitional category but as a distinct phenotypic entity with its own evidence gaps, underscoring the need for future research specifically targeting this understudied population. The Evolution and Applications of Mainstream Transcriptomics Technology Platforms: From High-Throughput Sequencing to Integration at Spatial and Single-Cell Dimensions

Transcriptomics is the science of studying the complete RNA expression profile of cells at specific time points and under defined experimental or pathological conditions. It encompasses messenger RNA (mRNA), non-coding RNA, including long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), microRNA (miRNA), and other transcripts, thereby constituting a vital component of systems biology. By capturing the expression information of all RNA molecules within cells or tissues, it advances research into gene regulatory mechanisms, molecular mechanisms of disease, and drug action targets. With continuous advances in omics technologies, transcriptomics research has undergone a transformative shift from traditional hybridization techniques to comprehensive analysis centered on sequencing, significantly enhancing the precision and coverage of biomedical research. First-Generation Technology: Microarray Chips

Sanger sequencing represents the first-generation DNA sequencing technology based on the chain-termination method, with microarray technology being a prominent output of this phase.25 Microarray chips utilize known oligonucleotide probes to hybridize with target RNA, with fluorescence intensity reflecting RNA expression levels. This approach offers advantages of high throughput, cost-effectiveness, and well-established analytical workflows; however, it is inherently limited in its inability to detect unknown transcripts, splicing variants, or low-abundance RNA molecules. Presently, large-scale microarray studies are driving the development of novel experimental biological methods. Microarray technology encompasses dual-channel and single-channel approaches, with the Affymetrix platform representing the most classic example of the latter. This method employs chemically synthesized 25-mer oligonucleotides to form high-density probe arrays.26,27 Using a cardiovascular cDNA microarray (CardioChip), one study identified more than 100 dysregulated transcripts in end-stage dilated cardiomyopathy, including 19-fold atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) upregulation. Another study integrated four Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) HF datasets, revealing four hub genes such as COL9A1 and MTIF3, significantly downregulated in failing hearts with diagnostic potential.28,29

Second-Generation Sequencing (SGS)

Second-generation transcriptomics technology, namely second-generation sequencing (SGS), primarily encompasses platforms such as Roche GS FLX Titanium, Life Sciences SOLiD/Ion Torrent PGM, and Illumina GA/HiSeq/MiSeq. Roche 454 features an initial read length of 100–150 bp, boasting advantages in both speed and read length.30 SOLiD commenced with an initial read length of 35 bp, delivering 3 Gb with 99.85% accuracy, later upgraded to 85 bp, 30 Gb, and 99.99% accuracy. While it is extensively employed in transcriptome and epigenome research, this platform is inherently limited by its short read length and lengthy processing times.31 Illumina HiSeq 2000 yields up to 600 Gb/run at low cost, though improper control may cause cluster overlap and compromise quality. To address these limitations, third-generation sequencing technology has emerged.25 Using next-generation sequencing, researchers found circular RNA-PALLD (Circ-PALLD) upregulated in failing hearts, potentially regulated by GATA binding protein 4 (GATA4), suggesting it as a novel biomarker and therapeutic target for chronic heart failure (CHF).32 Transcriptomic analysis of non-familial dilated cardiomyopathy and restrictive cardiomyopathy revealed disease-specific gene expression profiles and 27 altered lncRNA-mRNA pairs, including mediator complex subunits.33

Third-Generation Sequencing (TGS)

PacBio Single-Molecule Real-Time (SMRT) Sequencing

Although SGS represents a significant advancement over Sanger sequencing, its short read length limits its application in complex genome assembly, gene isoform analysis, and methylation detection. By contrast, PacBio, a real-time sequencing technology, offers longer read lengths and faster throughput, and has found extensive application in genomic, transcriptomic, and epigenomic research.34 However, its low throughput, high error rate, and substantial cost necessitate complementary use with SGS. Under the current C4 chemistry, the PacBio RS II system achieves average read lengths exceeding 10 kb, N50 values surpassing 20 kb, and maximum lengths reaching 60 kb, enabling resolution of larger repetitive regions.35 A preprint study used PacBio and Oxford Nanopore sequencing in a hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) patient with a myosin binding protein C3 (MYBPC3) splice variant. It identified expected exon skipping and discovered novel isoforms, providing direct molecular evidence for variant pathogenicity.36

Oxford Nanopore Sequencing

Oxford Nanopore sequencing employs nanopore technology to perform single-molecule sequencing of native DNA and RNA, enabling the detection of individual nucleotide modifications. This addresses the limitation of SGS technology, which cannot directly detect base modifications in native DNA. Furthermore, it offers advantages in real-time targeted sequencing—that is, the rapid acquisition and analysis of DNA or RNA sequences—and serves as a representative example of TGS. The sequencing device’s adapters enrich DNA substrates in the vicinity of the nanopore membrane surface, facilitating single-strand capture and enhancing DNA capture efficiency by several thousand-fold. Furthermore, hairpin adapters enable sequential sequencing of both strands in double-stranded molecules through the covalent ligation of one strand to the other.37 Using Oxford Nanopore sequencing, a study analyzed cDNA from a pacing-induced HF canine model. Full-length transcripts enabled accurate assessment of gene structure and expression changes, providing valuable molecular insights into HF.38

Spatial Transcriptomics Technology

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) has emerged as a research focus in the field of transcriptomics, integrating traditional histology with high-throughput RNA sequencing to achieve spatial visualization and quantitative analysis of transcriptomes within tissue sections.39 ST encompasses in situ capture (ISC) based on spatial barcoding, in situ sequencing (ISS), and in situ hybridization (ISH). ISC employs spatial barcodes to label target RNA regions in situ, enabling highly sensitive and specific spatial localization through Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS). ISH utilizes fluorescent probes to hybridize with target RNA, enabling the detection of low-abundance transcripts through high-magnification imaging. ISS, by contrast, achieves high-resolution spatial sequencing by detecting RNA through hybridization of padlock probes with cDNA followed by Rolling Circle Amplification (RCA).2 A study using GeoMx spatial profiling revealed region-specific alterations in failing human hearts: fibrotic signals in scar zones, metabolic reprogramming in border zones, and immune infiltration in fibrotic areas, highlighting transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) signaling in remodeling.40

Single-Cell and Multi-Omics Integration

Single-Cell RNA-Sequencing (scRNA-Seq)

Single-cell RNA sequencing offers unprecedentedly high resolution, enabling the characterization of cellular heterogeneity, precise identification of rare cell types, and in-depth exploration of intercellular communication, thereby providing novel perspectives for studying cellular networks. However, its multi-level, multi-scale clustering remains challenging, affecting the accuracy of heterogeneity characterization and cell identification.41 The standard workflow encompasses cell isolation, lysis, mRNA capture, reverse transcription to cDNA, amplification, library preparation, and sequencing.42 Integrating transcriptomic and single-cell RNA-seq data, one study identified fibroblast hub genes (Vwa8, Mthfd2, Decr1) and a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network in HFpEF.43 Another study reconstructed cellular landscapes of failing and left ventricular assist device (LVAD)-recovered human hearts, revealing transcriptomic andintercellular changes across cardiomyocytes, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells, elucidating cardiac recovery mechanisms.44

Trends in Multi-Omics Integration

The complexity of human cellular diversity necessitates the integration of methods for capturing and analyzing multiple cellular processes. The convergence of sequencing, mass spectrometry platforms, and artificial intelligence analytical tools has fueled the advancement of “big data” research, thereby facilitating the in-depth exploration of the intricate mechanisms underlying complex diseases.45 Correspondingly, multi-omics integration has emerged as a pivotal research paradigm, exemplified by approaches such as Cellular Indexing of Transcriptomes and Epitopes by Sequencing (CITE-seq), single-cell assay for transposase-accessible chromatin using sequencing (scATAC-seq), and the integration of spatial transcriptomics with single-cell data, continuously enhancing spatial resolution and data integration capabilities. This strategy has been increasingly applied to cardiovascular research, enabling the identification of novel biomarkers and molecular subtypes in HF, as well as the elucidation of multi-level regulatory networks underlying TCM interventions.46

Transcriptomic Evidence and Target Networks for Multicomponent Traditional Chinese Medicine Regulation of Heart Failure

All transcriptomic evidence reviewed in this section was derived from animal model-based exploratory studies employing transcriptomic profiling to identify differentially expressed genes and pathway enrichments, with key regulatory targets subsequently confirmed by protein-level validation and functional assessment in corresponding models. The experimental models encompass diverse pathological contexts relevant to HF research, including pressure overload, drug-induced cardiotoxicity, ischemic injury, metabolic or hypertensive stress, and other pathological contexts. Single-nucleus RNA sequencing analysis revealed a reduction in cardiomyocytes within the HF center, accompanied by a significant expansion of endothelial and immune cells. Although fibroblasts did not exhibit proliferative activity, they underwent a phenotypic shift that upregulated extracellular matrix (ECM) gene expression and collagen deposition. This manifested through key factor alterations including TGF-β activation, enhanced insulin-like growth factor (IGF) signaling, and decreased C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2) expression, thereby driving myocardial remodeling.47 Owing to their inherent multi-component, multi-target, and multi-pathway characteristics, traditional Chinese medicines have been hypothesized to offer potential advantages in regulating key factors, with holistic regulatory effects and personalized therapeutic benefits in CHF management. Literature Search Strategy and Study Selection

As a narrative review, we conducted and reported a structured literature search with reference to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 statement to ensure transparency in the search and screening processes.84 A literature search was performed in PubMed and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) from inception to June 2026, using Boolean combinations of terms related to HF, transcriptomics, and TCM. The detailed search strategy is presented in Table S1. Eligible studies were original experimental studies that: (1) applied transcriptomic technologies to established HF models and to cardiac injury/remodeling models directly relevant to HF pathogenesis or progression in mammalian species, including pressure overload, myocardial infarction, ischemia–reperfusion injury, cardiotoxicity, sepsis-associated cardiac dysfunction, myocardial fibrosis, and metabolic or hypertensive cardiac dysfunction; (2) evaluated a TCM formula, single herb, herbal-derived preparation, or natural compound; and (3) reported original transcriptomic data. Studies using HF-related injury models without a demonstrated HF phenotype were retained as mechanistic supportive evidence rather than direct HF evidence. Conference abstracts, editorials, and studies without original transcriptomic data were excluded. The remaining references comprise three categories: background literature on HF pathophysiology and clinical classification, methodological references describing transcriptomic technologies and analytical platforms, and contextual literature providing foundational knowledge without directly presenting original transcriptomic or TCM intervention data. This distinction ensures that the transcriptomic evidence from TCM interventions is presented separately from background and methodological context. Two independent reviewers, S.-H.S. and Y.J., screened all records, with disagreements resolved by consultation with a third reviewer, M.-N.L.

All search results were imported into Zotero to remove duplicates. The initial search identified 131 records. After screening, 88 records were excluded, and 43 reports were sought for retrieval, of which 4 could not be retrieved. The remaining 39 reports were assessed for eligibility and all met the prespecified inclusion criteria. Single-Ingredient Chinese Herbal Medicines and Chinese Herbal Formulae

The use of TCM compound formulations to treat HF-related conditions has long been established. Their multi-component synergistic effects and widespread application have spurred extensive research into both compound and single-component TCM treatments for HF. Direct HF evidence in preclinical research is established through rigorous in vivo assessment of functional and structural endpoints in animal models that recapitulate key aspects of human HF pathophysiology. This evidence, demonstrated by measurable improvements in cardiac function, attenuation of pathological remodeling, and enhanced survival, directly validates the therapeutic potential of interventions. It forms a critical translational foundation by confirming efficacy against HF phenotypes prior to clinical evaluation. Shexiang Tongxin Dropping Pill (STDP) is indicated for various cardiovascular diseases, protecting vascular endothelial cells, reducing myocardial fibrosis, and improving coronary microcirculation.85 Transcriptomic analyses comprehensively explored STDP’s mechanisms in a TAC-induced model, revealing significant effects on fibrosis-related genes such as Tgfb1 and Smad3. It is hypothesized that STDP primarily ameliorates HF via the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and TGF-β signaling pathways.49,86 Nuanxinkang (NXK) has been employed in clinical practice in China for treating HF, though its mechanisms remain under investigation. Transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses in a TAC-induced model revealed that NXK regulates macrophage polarization through energy metabolism reprogramming based on the hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α)-pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase 1 (PDK1) axis. This influences the M1/M2 ratio during post-myocardial infarction repair, thereby improving cardiac remodeling and contributing to anti-HF effects.50 Qishen Granules (QSG) prevent the progression of myocardial hypertrophy and fibrosis through multiple pharmacological mechanisms. Transcriptome pathway analysis in a TAC-induced model indicates that QSG acts upon multiple pathways, exerting effects on myocardial energy metabolism, amino acid metabolism, and the ECM pathway, which are hypothesized to underlie its therapeutic outcomes for HF.51 In a separate transcriptomic study, QSG regulated the Hippo pathway in a post-myocardial infarction (MI) HF model, inhibiting the MST1-LATS-YAP/TAZ cascade and TGF-β-SMAD3 signaling to reduce apoptosis and fibrosis.87 Xinyang Tablet (XYT) was shown to attenuate cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis through transcriptomic and single-cell RNA sequencing analyses that identified HDAC2, SOD2, and the PI3K-Akt/oxidative stress pathways as key targets. Specifically, XYT was found to inhibit HDAC2/Akt/GSK-3β signaling, mitigating oxidative stress and pathological remodeling.53,88 Huoxin Pill (HXP) was investigated in a post-MI HF model using RNA sequencing, which identified 420 upregulated and 240 downregulated transcripts regulated by HXP. KEGG pathway analysis revealed enrichment of the p53 and TGF-β signaling pathways, and subsequent experimental validation confirmed that HXP attenuated cardiac apoptosis and fibrosis through suppression of the p53-Bax-Bcl-2 and TGF-β1/Smad2/3 pathways.52,89 Transcriptomic analysis identified 92 ECM-related genes reciprocally regulated by Xijiaqi Formula (XJQ), including MMP2 and TGF-β1-correlated proteins, via which XJQ inhibited fibroblast activation and reduced myocardial fibrosis.55 Yixin-Shu capsules (YXS) were evaluated in a myocardial ischemia-induced HF model using RNA-seq analysis, which identified 333 differentially expressed genes regulated by YXS, with enrichment in inflammatory response, MAPK cascade, ECM organization, and regulation of apoptotic processes. Further validation confirmed that YXS restored Trx2 expression and inhibited JNK and p38 activation, thereby attenuating oxidative damage, inflammation, and apoptosis in HF.56 Xin-Fu-Kang oral liquid (XFK) regulated NR4A1 in TAC-induced CHF, modulating PI3K-Akt, calcium signaling, and mitophagy pathways. XFK restored PINK1-Parkin-mediated mitophagy and ER-mitochondrial crosstalk through NR4A1-dependent mechanisms, thereby mitigating cardiac dysfunction.57 In a rat model of coronary artery ligation-induced CHF, miRNA microarray analysis of Yiqifumai Injection (YQFM) identified seven differentially expressed miRNAs reciprocally regulated by YQFM, with miR-21-3p and miR-542-3p related to myocardial hypertrophy and cell proliferation, respectively.58 Yi Qi Huo Xue compound prescription (YQHXCP) modulated mitochondrial protein complexes and cell cycle checkpoints through Cdk1 and E2f2 in CHF.59 In a HFrEF model, Xinbao Pills (XBP) enhanced myocardial energy metabolism and protected mitochondrial respiratory function through activation of the AMP-activated protein kinase alpha 1 (AMPKα1) signaling pathway.60 In an Nω-nitro-L-arginine methyl ester and high-fat diet-induced HFpEF model, integrative multi-omics analysis revealed that QiShenYiQi Dripping Pills (QSYQ) regulated inflammation, energy metabolism, and the cGMP-PKG pathway, with PKG identified as a key mediator of its cardioprotective effects.66 Xin-Ji-Er-Kang (XJEK) facilitated NR3C1 nuclear translocation to upregulate MFN2 transcription in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury models, as revealed by high-throughput sequencing, thereby suppressing cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS)-stimulator of interferon genes (STING)-driven inflammation in CHF.65 Shenqi Oral Liquid (SOL) alleviated doxorubicin-induced CHF by inhibiting excessive autophagy through suppression of the PI3K-Akt-mTOR and AMPK pathways, as revealed by integrated network pharmacology and transcriptomic analysis, thereby rescuing mitochondrial dysfunction and reducing cardiomyocyte apoptosis.64 Sanweidoukou Decoction (SD-3) attenuated isoproterenol (ISO)-induced HF by suppressing the AMPK-peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα) pathway, as identified by RNA-Seq, which reduced ROS overproduction, preserved mitochondrial function, and inhibited cardiomyocyte apoptosis.63 Xinmailong (XML), a TCM derived from the American cockroach, has shown preliminary efficacy in preclinical models of doxorubicin-induced CHF. Relevant studies have employed RNA-seq analysis to identify that XML improves cardiac function by influencing the cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP)-dependent protein kinase (PKG) signaling pathway or downregulating the expression of COL1A1, COL3A1, MMP9, and CXCR2, thereby ameliorating inflammation, lipopolysaccharide response, and cell adhesion in experimental models.62 Shenfuyixin Granules (SFYX) ameliorated HF by stimulating mitochondrial autophagy through activation of the sirtuin 3-forkhead box O1 (FOXO1) pathway in a post-MI rat model.61 Xuan Bi Tong Yu Fang (XBTYF) upregulated Hoxb4 to inhibit the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, promoting cell proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury.74 Shenyu Yangxin Capsules (SYX) ameliorated ISO-induced heart failure in mice by improving myocardial fibrosis and regulating the PPAR signaling pathway. Transcriptomics identified ACSL1, SCD1, PPARα, Angptl4, CPT1α, and PGC-1α as key genes, whose mRNA and protein expression were upregulated by SYX treatment.67 Aconiti lateralis radix praeparata has been reported to possess cardiotonic effects in preclinical models and is commonly used in clinical settings in China for the management of acute heart failure. Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes via transcriptomics in a rat model of acute heart failure indicates that aconiti lateralis radix praeparata administration primarily modulates multiple pathways, including the chemokine signaling pathway, human T-cell leukemia virus type I infection pathway, tumor necrosis factor (TNF) signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, JAK-STAT pathway, and phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)-protein kinase B (Akt) signaling pathway.71 These findings suggest that aconiti lateralis radix praeparata may participate in cellular regulation and potentially reduce cardiomyocyte apoptosis, thereby contributing to a cardioprotective effect.

HF-relevant mechanistic evidence comprises experimental data generated from well-established animal models that recapitulate key pathological features of heart failure. These models—encompassing pressure overload, ischemic injury, and drug-induced cardiomyopathy—serve as indispensable tools to delineate the molecular and cellular mechanisms through which interventions exert therapeutic effects. By enabling detailed interrogation of signaling pathways, gene regulation, and functional outcomes in a controlled biological system, this category of evidence provides critical insights into the fundamental biological processes driving HF progression and the mode of action of potential therapies. Shenfu Injection (SFI) attenuated myocardial hypertrophy by upregulating miR-19a-3p and inhibiting MEF2A in a pressure-overload model.79 In doxorubicin-induced HF, it downregulated LUM and suppressed p38/p53 signaling to reduce fibroblast apoptosis and inflammation.90 Si-Miao-Yong-An Decoction (SMYAD) was investigated in isoprenaline-induced myocardial fibrosis models using RNA-seq analysis, which identified differentially expressed genes enriched in TGF-β and AMPK pathways. SMYAD treatment was shown to activate AMPK and suppress downstream Akt-mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) and TGF-β-SMAD3 signaling, leading to reduced ECM deposition and attenuated myocardial fibrosis.75,91 Fufang Danshen Pill (FDP) was investigated in myocardial ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury via single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, which identified macrophage S100a9 as a key target. FDP inhibited S100a9 expression, reversing toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) activation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and cardiac damage, thereby improving cardiac function.76 Shenhong Tongluo Formula (SHTLF) was shown to suppress SAT1-mediated remodeling of polyamine metabolism, thereby constraining arachidonate lipoxygenase 15 (ALOX15)-dependent amplification of membrane lipid peroxidation in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury.77 Er-Xian Decoction (EXD) downregulated cardiomyopathy-related genes including Myh7, Myh2, and Itgb5 in an ovariectomy-induced myocardial injury model, as revealed by microarray analysis.78 In hypertensive models of HF with preserved ejection fraction, Qiliqiangxin Capsules (QLQX) have been suggested to exert cardioprotective effects in preclinical studies through multi-pathway, multi-target mechanisms, including elevating cGMP levels, inhibiting cardiomyocyte apoptosis, enhancing myocardial remodeling, and promoting autophagy and mitochondrial autophagy. Transcriptomic findings in a hypertensive model of HFpEF indicate that oxidative phosphorylation, myocardial contraction, and the cGMP-PKG signaling pathway represent the key pathways mediating the therapeutic effects of QLQX in the management of HF.73 Draconis Sanguis (DS) was found to regulate the HIF-1α-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling pathway to promote angiogenesis and optimize myocardial energy metabolism by promoting lipid metabolism and inhibiting glucose metabolism in ischemic HF.83

Natural Compounds

Given the widespread application of compound preparations in disease treatment, key compounds have garnered significant attention. Demonstrating the efficacy and safety of extracted natural compounds represents a potential pathway for future clinical translation of herbal medicines, pending rigorous validation.92 The direct HF evidence presented next is obtained from experimental models replicating heart failure conditions. limonin, an active component isolated from citrus fruits, has demonstrated potential anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects that confer therapeutic potential in the management of HFpEF. Transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses in an Nω-nitro-L-arginine methyl ester and high-fat diet-induced hypertensive model indicate that limonin targets ferroptosis by modulating the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2)-solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11)-glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) axis, thereby reducing ACSL4, COX2, and ALOX expression, which is hypothesized to ameliorate HFpEF.68 Panax notoginseng saponins (PNS) were evaluated in an AMI-induced HF model using transcriptome analysis, which identified 2818 differentially expressed genes and revealed that the PPAR signaling pathway, particularly PPARα, was the most significantly enriched pathway. Transcriptomic and experimental validation confirmed that PNS enhanced energy metabolism by promoting fatty acid oxidation through PPARα activation, exerting cardioprotective effects in vivo and in vitro.69 Isoliquiritigenin (ISL), a flavonoid compound extracted from Glycyrrhiza glabra, has been reported to exhibit anti-inflammatory activity. In a post-MI-induced HF model, it alleviates cardiac dysfunction by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome at the NLRP3 level or its upstream pathways. Concurrently, transcriptomic and network pharmacology analyses indicate that ISL significantly enriches core HF-associated targets within the TNF signaling pathway, thereby potentially preventing and ameliorating cardiac dysfunction through inhibition of TNF signaling pathways.70

The HF-relevant mechanistic evidence presented below is derived from animal models of pathologies associated with heart failure development. lycorine (LYC), an isoquinoline alkaloid extracted from Lycoris radiata, exhibits a strong negative correlation between B-cell lymphoma 3 (BCL3) and cardiac function indicators as revealed by transcriptome analysis. This demonstrates that LYC mitigates myocardial injury in sepsis and exerts cardioprotective effects by inhibiting BCL3.80 The signaling pathways identified in this sepsis model—particularly nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB), TNF, and NOD-like receptor pyroprotein domain-related protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome pathways—overlap with those implicated in HF pathogenesis, suggesting that LYC may offer mechanistic insights applicable to HF. Astragaloside IV (AS-IV), a natural compound extracted from the Chinese medicinal herb Astragalus membranaceus, possesses anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-fibrotic properties.93,94 In an ISO-induced model of myocardial fibrosis, it alleviates inflammation and ECM remodeling, mitigates oxidative stress, and protects cardiac cells. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that AS-IV regulates gene expression, including the negative regulation of the MAPK cascade, and modulates pathways such as PI3K-Akt, MAPK, and p53 signaling, which may play a role in AS-IV-mediated anti-myocardial fibrosis protection.81 Jaranol, a flavonoid compound identified from Astragalus membranaceus, was investigated in Angiotensin II (Ang II)- and TAC-induced cardiac remodeling models using mRNA sequencing. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that jaranol suppressed Toll-like receptor signaling and toll-like receptor 2 (TLR2) expression, and further mechanistic studies demonstrated that jaranol enhanced Snai3 expression to transcriptionally repress TLR2, while also directly interacting with NF-κB to inhibit its nuclear translocation, thereby attenuating pathological cardiac remodeling.82

Summary of Key Signaling Pathways Regulated by Traditional Chinese Medicine in Heart Failure

Based on transcriptomic studies, TCM interventions in HF involve multi-pathway synergistic regulation. Regulated pathways include chemokine, TNF, MAPK, JAK-STAT, PI3K-Akt, cGMP-PKG, TGF-β, HIF-1α-PDK1-mediated energy metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, and cardiac contraction. Natural compounds target BCL3, the Nrf2-SLC7A11-GPX4 axis, TNF, MAPK, and PI3K-Akt pathways. Collectively, these transcriptomic findings point to MAPK, PI3K-Akt, TGF-β, and TNF signaling as frequently implicated pathways, though their relative importance and interplay remain to be established.

Discussion

Despite continuous advances in modern medicine, treatments for HF—a condition characterized by high incidence and poor prognosis—remain limited in their efficacy for improving long-term outcomes and reversing myocardial remodeling. TCM has been suggested by preclinical observations and some clinical studies to offer potential advantages in targeted regulation tailored for individual variations, with preliminary evidence indicating possible efficacy in alleviating symptoms and enhancing quality of life. However, its complex mechanisms lack modern scientific interpretation, hindering its international development. The advancement of transcriptomics has provided valuable tools for exploring the mechanisms underlying TCM interventions in HF. Transcriptomics enables genome-wide profiling of gene expression, facilitating the elucidation of synergistic mechanisms of TCM formulas and identification of potential targets. The evolution from microarrays to single-cell and spatial transcriptomics has expanded the capacity to investigate TCM regulatory networks across tissue, cellular, and subcellular levels, offering new possibilities for mechanistic research. (i) Transcriptomics provides in-depth gene-level analysis of TCM regulatory targets. Existing studies have revealed the multi-target mechanisms by which individual TCM components and formulas intervene in HF through the integration of multi-omics technologies such as single-cell sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, and epigenomic transcriptomics. This approach also provides molecular evidence for identifying the specific action targets of individual TCM constituents. (ii) Innovations in transcriptomics technology have enhanced the precision and breadth of TCM target-related research. Technological advancements from microarray chips to nanopore sequencing have enabled researchers to continually identify TCM action targets previously elusive to traditional methods. For instance, SMRT sequencing and Oxford Nanopore technology can generate long reads that overcome the assembly limitations of short-read sequencing, facilitating the discovery of full-length transcripts and novel isoforms in medicinal plants and disease models.95 (iii) Traditional Chinese medical theories gain modern scientific interpretation. Transcriptomic studies suggest that TCM interventions in HF exhibit multi-pathway, multi-temporal regulation, influencing distinct pathways at different disease stages. This dynamic regulatory pattern aligns closely with the TCM therapeutic principle of “multi-target treatment and coordinated intervention at different stages”. Despite significant advances in current research, methodological constraints and clinical translation bottlenecks continue to impede the in-depth development of transcriptomics studies in TCM. (i) Transcriptomics technologies remain limited in terms of precision, breadth, throughput, rare cell resolution, and cost. Taking the most widely employed spatial transcriptomics technology as an example, current spatial transcriptomics techniques still confront challenges such as insufficient resolution and low capture efficiency for rare cells. This renders it challenging to precisely localize the action sites of TCM components within complex myocardial tissue,96 which may lead to the overlooking of important target cells involved in drug action,44 necessitating further optimization. (ii) Current transcriptomics inadequately addresses TCM-specific single-gene targets in cardiovascular disease. Crucially, no reviewed study incorporated TCM syndrome typing as a variable, leaving it unclear whether distinct HF syndromes correspond to different transcriptomic signatures. This gap limits mechanistic interpretability and must be explicitly acknowledged. (iii) Safety validation based on genetic and molecular subtyping for TCM remains insufficient. Numerous potential targets and pathways identified in basic research face challenges in clinical validation, including substantial patient heterogeneity and inconsistent efficacy assessment criteria. Particularly for TCM compound preparations, their multi-component nature complicates the establishment of causal relationships between specific constituents and transcriptional changes, coupled with the absence of standardized translational research frameworks. (iv) Quality control of TCM compound preparations has received limited attention in the majority of reviewed studies, representing a notable methodological gap. Most transcriptomic studies lack characterization of batch-to-batch variability, active constituent quantification, or dose comparability. Chemical variations may produce divergent gene expression profiles that are erroneously attributed to biological mechanisms. This absence constitutes a major limitation. Future studies must adopt stringent quality control measures, including chemical fingerprinting and batch documentation.

Looking ahead, technological innovation and interdisciplinary collaboration hold promise for overcoming current limitations and establishing a more comprehensive research framework for understanding the mechanisms of action of TCM. (i) Transcriptomics requires further advancement through multi-omics integration and iterative refinement of individual transcriptomics techniques to deepen research. Developing novel spatial multi-omics technologies tailored for TCM research represents a key future direction, which will offer pioneering perspectives for future drug applications and optimize therapeutic approaches. Integrating in situ sequencing with mass spectrometry imaging holds promise for concurrent detection of both tissue distribution of TCM constituents and corresponding transcriptional alterations. Concurrently, optimizing single-cell sequencing sample processing methods to enhance capture efficiency for rare cell types will provide a more comprehensive understanding of TCM mechanisms. (ii) A distinctive research framework for TCM should be constructed through further systematic investigations. Subsequent research should integrate clinical syndrome characteristics with molecular biomarkers to develop syndrome-disease-linked animal models and organoid models. Concurrently, transcriptome stratification analyses based on TCM formulations and their therapeutic targets should facilitate the identification of syndrome-specific regulatory networks and intervention points for TCM. (iii) Clinical studies grounded in genetic and molecular subtyping are essential to validate the safety of TCM therapies. This entails conducting precision clinical trials based on molecular subtyping while concurrently developing novel indicators for dynamic efficacy monitoring. Integrating technologies such as artificial intelligence to establish predictive models of efficacy will forge a translational pathway from fundamental research to clinical application.

Conclusion

As a narrative review, this article synthesizes available evidence without performing quantitative meta-analysis. This review focuses on the molecular mechanisms underlying TCM interventions for HF, integrating cutting-edge transcriptomics technologies and reviewing research findings regarding natural compounds, single herbal constituents, and compound formulas in improving myocardial function, regulating energy metabolism, suppressing inflammatory responses, and reversing myocardial remodeling. This review not only demonstrates the modern molecular underpinnings of TCM’s “multi-target, multi-pathway” advantage but also reveals core pathways and potential targets associated with HF through the integration of technologies such as scRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics. Furthermore, this review discusses the application potential of third-generation sequencing technologies such as PacBio and Nanopore in elucidating the mechanisms of action of Chinese herbal medicines, proposing multi-omics integration as a key future trend. Establishing regulatory networks spanning from the single-cell to the systems level via interdisciplinary approaches holds substantial promise for advancing Chinese medicine research from empirical practice to evidence-based medicine, broadening its application prospects in precision treatment for cardiovascular diseases. This research framework provides both technical support and a theoretical foundation for the modernization and internationalization of Chinese medicine.

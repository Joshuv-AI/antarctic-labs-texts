Radial Uncertainty Product for Spherically Symmetric Potential in Position Space
Avoy Jana

Abstract
Abstract This paper presents a detailed analysis of the radial uncertainty product for quantum systems with spherically symmetric potentials. Using the principles of quantum mechanics, the study derives the radial uncertainty relation analogous to the Cartesian form and investigates its implications for three key spherically symmetric potentials: the Hydrogen atom, the infinite spherical potential well, and the spherical harmonic oscillator, all within the non-relativistic regime. Employing the Schrödinger equation in spherical coordinates, the paper rigorously evaluates the normalized radial wave functions, expectation values, and uncertainties associated with both position and momentum. Analytical derivations and numerical computations highlight the dependence of the uncertainty product on quantum numbers and system-specific parameters. Keywords Radial uncertainty product, radial wave function, radial momentum operator, relative dispersion, Hydro-genic systems, infinite spherical well, spherical harmonic oscillator

In many papers, the uncertainty relation is shown using the product of the expectation values of the squared position operator,
\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle
, and the squared momentum operator,
\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle
, or their uncertainties,
\Delta\hat{r}
and
\Delta\hat{p}_{r}

[

]
) and even in multidimensional spaces,(Refs.
[
3
,
4
]
) where
p
represents the total momentum of the system and
r
denotes the radial position. In this paper, we want to show radial uncertainty product
\Delta\hat{r}\Delta\hat{p}_{r}
for a spherical quantum system as we will see
{[\hat{r},\hat{p}_{r}]=i\hbar}
is analogous to
{[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{x}]=i\hbar}
. Consequently the radial uncertainty relation becomes
\Delta\hat{r}\Delta\hat{p}_{r}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}
like
\Delta x\Delta p_{x}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}
. And both uncertainty relations are examples of general type,
\Delta A\Delta B\geq\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}\langle i[A,B]\rangle^{2}}
(Refs.

and

We will conduct a comprehensive investigation into the radial uncertainty product [

V(r)\propto\frac{1}{r}
], the infinite spherical well (ISW) [
V(r)=0
], and the spherical harmonic oscillator (SHO) [
V(r)\propto r^{2}
] only for the non-relativistic case.

II
Formulation for Radial uncertainty product

The time-independent Schrodinger equation in spherical coordinate (
r,\theta,\phi
) is given by

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}\psi(r,\theta,\phi)+V(r)\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=E\psi(r,\theta,\phi)

Here for our works,
V(r)
is the spherically symmetric potential and the Laplacian in spherical coordinate
(r,\theta,\phi)
is given by

\nabla^{2}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}\left(r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\theta}}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial{\theta}}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\frac{\partial{{}^{2}}}{\partial{{}^{2}\phi}}

We know for spherically symmetric potential the solution can be written as

\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=R(r)\times Y(\theta,\phi)

where
R(r)
is the radial wave function and
Y(\theta,\phi)
is Spherical harmonics.
The probability density function for the radial part is given by
P(r)=r^{2}|R(r)|^{2}
from for d-dimensional position space, radial distribution is given by
P(r)=r^{d-1}|R(r)|^{2}
. (Refs.
[
6
]
) Now see how to normalize radial wave function. The normalization condition is given by

\int_{0}^{\infty}P(r)dr=1\implies\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}|R(r)|^{2}dr=1

The expectation value of an operator
\hat{A}
in position space is given by

\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R^{*}(r)\hat{A}R(r)dr

So expectation of
\hat{r}
,
\hat{r}^{2}
are

\langle\hat{r}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R^{*}(r)\hat{r}R(r)dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{3}|R(r)|^{2}dr

(1)

\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R^{*}(r^{2})\hat{r}^{2}R(r)dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{4}|R(r)|^{2}dr

(2)

Now what will be the radial momentum operator?
In classical mechanics, the radial momentum operator is defined by,

\hat{p}_{rc}=\frac{1}{r}\hat{r}.\hat{p}

where
\frac{\hat{r}}{r}
is unit radial vector and
\hat{p}
is momentum vector.
In quantum mechanics, this definition becomes ambiguous, since the components of
r
and
p
don’t commute. So,
\hat{p_{r}}
is not Hermitian, so it’s not observable. Since
\hat{p_{r}}
should be a Hermitian operator; (Refs.
[
7
]
) we need to define newly a symmetric operator given by

\hat{p_{r}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{r}(\hat{r}.\hat{p}+\hat{p}.\hat{r})

Now
\hat{e_{r}}=\frac{\hat{r}}{r}
and
\hat{p}
is momentum, in quantum mechanics for spherical coordinates, it is defined by
\hat{p}=-i\hbar\vec{\nabla}
.
So

\hat{p_{r}}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{e_{r}}.\hat{p}+\hat{p}.\hat{e_{r}})

For an arbitrary wave function
\psi
,

\hat{p_{r}}\psi=\frac{1}{2}(-i\hbar)[\hat{e_{r}}.\vec{\nabla}\psi+\vec{\nabla}.(\hat{e_{r}}\psi)]

(3)

The first part of equation,

\hat{e_{r}}.\vec{\nabla}\psi=\hat{e_{r}}.\left(\hat{e_{r}}\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{r}}+\hat{e_{\theta}}\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{\theta}}+\hat{e_{\phi}}\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{\phi}}\right)=\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{r}}

The second part of equation,

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}.(\hat{e_{r}}\psi)

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{e_{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\hat{e_{\theta}}\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\theta}}+\hat{e_{\phi}}\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\phi}}\right).(\hat{e_{r}}\psi)

\displaystyle=\hat{e_{r}}.\frac{\partial{\hat{e_{r}}}}{\partial{r}}\psi+\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{r}}+\hat{e_{\theta}}.\frac{\partial{\hat{e_{r}}}}{\partial{\theta}}\frac{1}{r}\psi+\hat{e_{\phi}}.\frac{\partial{\hat{e_{r}}}}{\partial{\phi}}\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\psi

The unit vectors and their derivatives in spherical co-ordinate system given by,

\displaystyle\hat{e_{r}}=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)

\displaystyle\hat{e_{\theta}}=(\cos\theta\cos\phi,\cos\theta\sin\phi,-\sin\theta)

\displaystyle\hat{e_{\phi}}=(-\sin\phi,\cos\phi,0)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\hat{e_{r}}}}{\partial{r}}=0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{e_{r}}}{\partial{\theta}}=(\cos\theta\cos\phi,\cos\theta\sin\phi,-\sin\theta)=\hat{e_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{e_{r}}}{\partial{\phi}}=(-\sin\theta\sin\phi,\sin\theta\cos\phi,0)=\sin\theta\,\,\hat{e_{\phi}}

Finally,

\vec{\nabla}.(\hat{e_{r}}\psi)=\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{r}}+\frac{2}{r}\psi

From equation, we get,

\displaystyle\hat{p_{r}}\psi=-\frac{i\hbar}{2}\left(\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{r}}+\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{r}}+\frac{2}{r}\psi\right)=-i\hbar\left(\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\psi\right)

Hence the radial momentum operator can be written as

\hat{p_{r}}=-i\hbar\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\right)

(4)

In a compact form it can represented as
\hat{p_{r}}=-i\hbar\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}[r]
. In a d-dimensional position space, the radial momentum operator is given by
\hat{p_{r}}=-i\hbar(\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\frac{d-1}{2r})
(Refs.
[
7
]
) and for
d=3
, it’s our result.
Lets check whether
\hat{r}
and
\hat{p_{r}}
commute or not.
The commutator of
\hat{r}
and
\hat{p_{r}}
is given by
[\hat{r},\hat{p_{r}}]=(\hat{r}\hat{p_{r}}-\hat{p_{r}}\hat{r})
and considering an arbitrary radial wave function
R(r)
,

[\hat{r},\hat{p_{r}}]R(r)=(-i\hbar)\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}[rR(r)]-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}[r^{2}R(r)]\right]=i\hbar R(r)

[\hat{r},\hat{p_{r}}]=i\hbar

(5)

We see that for
{[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{x}]=i\hbar}
, we calculate uncertainty product
\Delta x\Delta p_{x}
as mentioned in introduction. Hence as
[\hat{r},\hat{p_{r}}]=i\hbar
, we will calculate radial uncertainty product
\Delta\hat{r}\Delta p_{r}
for spherical quantum system is of simple form than
\Delta\hat{r}\Delta p
.
It can be shown also that the momentum square operator can be written as

\hat{p}^{2}_{r}=-\hbar^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{r^{2}}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}\right)

(6)

where in d-dimensional position space
\hat{p}^{2}_{r}
is given by
\hat{p}^{2}_{r}=-\hbar^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{r^{2}}}+\frac{d-1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\frac{(d-1)(d-3)}{4r^{2}}\right)
(Refs.
[
7
]
) and for
d=3
, it’s our result. Now expectation of
\hat{p_{r}}
and
\hat{p^{2}_{r}}
are

\langle\hat{p_{r}}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R^{*}(r)(-i\hbar)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\right)R(r)dr

(7)

\langle\hat{p}_{r}^{2}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R^{*}(r)(-\hbar^{2})\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{r^{2}}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}\right)R(r)dr

(8)

But there is a fantastic way to find
\langle\hat{p}^{2}_{r}\rangle
if we know about the total energy of the system as total energy is related to total momentum i.e. also to radial momentum.
We see this approach when calculating it later in H-atom, spherical harmonic oscillator, and infinite spherical well problem respectively. By the definition of uncertainty, the uncertainties of radial position and radial momentum are given by

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{r}=\sqrt{\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{r}\rangle^{2}}

(9)

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{p}_{r}=\sqrt{\langle\hat{p}^{2}_{r}\rangle-\langle\hat{p}_{r}\rangle^{2}}

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(a,b)=W(a,b)\left[-\frac{b}{a+b}\right]

\displaystyle=2(2n^{3}-3n^{2}+n+{\ell}^{2}+\ell-2n{\ell}^{2}-2n\ell)

U^{\prime}(a,b)+V^{\prime}(a,b)+W^{\prime}(a,b)=2[10n^{3}+2n-6n{\ell}^{2}-6n\ell]

Finally,
z^{3}L_{b}^{a}(z)=2[10n^{3}+2n-6n{\ell}^{2}-6n\ell]L_{b}^{a}(z)+\text{other terms contain $L_{c}^{a}(z)$ where $c\neq b$}
.

\displaystyle I_{3}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[[U^{\prime}(a,b)+V^{\prime}(a,b)+W^{\prime}(a,b)]L_{b}^{a}(z)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\text{other terms contain $L_{c}^{a}(z)$ where $c\neq b$}\right]e^{-z}z^{a}[L^{a}_{b}]dz

\displaystyle=[U^{\prime}(a,b)+V^{\prime}(a,b)+W^{\prime}(a,b)]\frac{\Gamma{(a+b+1)}}{\Gamma{(b+1)}}

\displaystyle=2[10n^{3}+2n-6n{\ell}^{2}-6n\ell].\frac{(n+\ell)!}{(n-\ell-1)!}

From equ. (23),

\langle\hat{r}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}.\frac{n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}{Z^{2}}[5n^{2}-3\ell(\ell+1)+1]

(24)

Previously we have in equ. (22),

\langle\hat{r}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{a_{0}}{Z}[3n^{2}-\ell(\ell+1)]

And the uncertainty in radial position will be,

\Delta\hat{r}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{a_{0}}{Z}\sqrt{n^{2}(n^{2}+2)-[\ell(\ell+1)]^{2}}

(25)

III.3
Uncertainty in Radial momentum

We see that the radial momentum operator is given by,

\hat{p}_{r}=-i\hbar\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}[r]=-i\hbar\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\right)

As per equ. (7),

\displaystyle\langle\hat{p_{r}}\rangle

(26)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R_{n\ell}^{*}(r)(-i\hbar)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\right)R_{n\ell}(r)dr

\displaystyle=-i\hbar\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R_{n\ell}\frac{\partial{R_{n\ell}}}{\partial{r}}dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R_{n\ell}.\frac{1}{r}R_{n\ell}\,dr\right]

\displaystyle=-i\hbar[I_{4}+I_{5}]

First integral of equ. (24) becomes,

I_{4}=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R_{n\ell}\,dR_{n\ell}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{na_{0}}{2Z}\right)^{2}{\rho}^{2}R_{n\ell}(\rho)\,dR_{n\ell}(\rho)

From equ. (16),

R_{n\ell}(\rho)=N_{n\ell}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\rho}^{\ell}L^{2\ell+1}_{n-\ell-1}(\rho)

Then

\displaystyle dR_{n\ell}(\rho)=N_{n\ell}\left[-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\rho}^{\ell}L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(\rho\right.

\displaystyle\left.+e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\ell}{\rho}^{\ell-1}L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(\rho)+e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\rho}^{\ell}\frac{dL_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(\rho)}{d\rho}\right]d\rho

Using nomenclature,

\displaystyle dR_{n\ell}(\rho)=N_{n\ell}\left[-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\rho}^{\ell}L_{b}^{a}(\rho)\right.

(27)

\displaystyle\left.+e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}\ell{\rho}^{\ell-1}L_{b}^{a}(\rho)+e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\rho}^{\ell}\frac{dL_{b}^{a}(\rho)}{d\rho}\right]d\rho

\displaystyle=N_{n\ell}\left[-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\rho}^{\ell}L_{b}^{a}(\rho)+(b+\ell)e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\rho}^{\ell-1}L_{b}^{a}(\rho)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-(b+a)e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}}{\rho}^{\ell-1}L_{b+1}^{a}(\rho)\right]d\rho

And as a result,
I_{4}
becomes,

\displaystyle I_{4}=\left(\frac{na_{0}}{2Z}\right)^{2}|N_{n\ell}|^{2}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho.{\rho}^{a}e^{-\rho}[L_{b}^{a}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho\right.

(28)

\displaystyle+(b+\ell)\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rho}^{a}e^{-\rho}[L_{b}^{a}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho

\displaystyle\left.-(b+a)\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rho}^{a}e^{-\rho}L_{b}^{a}(\rho)L_{b-1}^{a}(\rho)d\rho\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{na_{0}}{2Z}\right)^{2}|N_{n\ell}|^{2}\left[-\frac{1}{2}I_{6}+(b+\ell)I_{7}\right]

Now,

\displaystyle I_{6}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho.{\rho}^{a}e^{-\rho}[L_{b}^{a}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho

(29)

\displaystyle.

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[(a+2b+1)L^{a}_{b}(z)-\frac{b+1}{a+b+1}L^{a}_{b+1}(z)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-(a+b)^{2}L^{a}_{b-1}(z)\right]{\rho}^{a}e^{-\rho}L_{b}^{a}(\rho)d\rho

\displaystyle=(a+2b+1)\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rho}^{a}e^{-\rho}[L_{b}^{a}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho=2nI_{7}

So,
I_{4}=-(\frac{na_{0}}{2Z})^{2}|N_{n\ell}|^{2}I_{7}
and from equ. (13),
I_{7}=\frac{(n+\ell)!}{(n-\ell-1)!}
gives
I_{4}=-\frac{Z}{n^{2}a_{0}}
.
First integral of equ. (24) becomes,

\displaystyle I_{5}=\int_{0}^{\infty}r|R_{n\ell}|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{na_{0}}{2Z}\right)^{2}|N_{n\ell}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\rho}{\rho}^{2\ell+1}[L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{Z}{n^{2}a_{0}}

There is also a fascinating way to find integral
I_{5}=\int_{0}^{\infty}r|R_{n\ell}|^{2}dr=\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\rangle
by energy calculation. Virial theorem states that for
V(r)\propto r^{n}
,
\langle T\rangle=\frac{n}{2}\langle V\rangle
. For Coulomb potential,
V(r)\propto\frac{1}{r}
and
\langle T\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle V\rangle
that states that
\langle E\rangle=\langle T\rangle+\langle V\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle V\rangle
. For Hydrogen or Hydrogen like atom,
V=-\frac{Zke^{2}}{r}
and
E=-\frac{Z^{2}{\hbar}^{2}}{2\mu n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}
where
a_{0}=\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{\mu ke^{2}}
. After a little bit simplification, we get,
\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}}\rangle=\frac{Z}{n^{2}a_{0}}
. It’s clear from equ. (26),

\langle\hat{p}_{r}\rangle=0

(30)

Now our aim is
\langle\hat{p}_{r}^{2}\rangle
, for that we can use
\langle\hat{p}_{r}^{2}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}R^{*}(r)(-\hbar^{2})(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{r^{2}}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}})R(r)dr
, in a similar way as we calculate
\langle\hat{p_{r}}\rangle
. But here to avoid similar long calculation, we do it by energy method. Total energy of H-atom or Hydrogen like atom,
E_{n\ell m}=E_{n}=-\frac{Z^{2}{\hbar}^{2}}{2\mu n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}
. Total momentum is defined as
p_{n\ell m}=\sqrt{p_{r}^{2}+p_{\ell}^{2}}
where
p_{r}
is radial momentum and
p_{\ell}
is orbital momentum. We know that, orbital angular momentum is defined as
L=p_{\ell}r=\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}\hbar
, from this we easily can say,
p_{\ell}=\frac{L}{r}=\frac{\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}}{r}\hbar
. The momentum-energy relation
\langle T=-E_{n\ell m}=\frac{p_{n\ell m}^{2}}{2\mu}\rangle
gives
\langle\frac{Z^{2}{\hbar}^{2}}{n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}=p_{r}^{2}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}{\hbar}^{2}\rangle
. Hence,
\langle\hat{p}_{r}^{2}\rangle=\frac{Z^{2}{\hbar}^{2}}{n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}-\ell(\ell+1){\hbar}^{2}\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}\rangle
, now we have to calculate
\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}\rangle
. Now,

\displaystyle\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|R_{n\ell}|^{2}dr

(31)

\displaystyle=\frac{na_{0}}{2Z}|N_{n\ell}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho}e^{-\rho}{\rho}^{2\ell+1}[L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho

It’s clear that it can’t be done this integral using orthogonality condition and recursive formula as we applied before for calculation. For this type of situation, the radial Schrodinger equation can be used. (Refs.
[
12
]
) Let
f(r)=rR(r)
,
\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}(\frac{f}{r})+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d}{dr}(\frac{f}{r})=\frac{1}{r}f^{\prime\prime}
. The radial Schrodinger equation (equ. (11)) for Hydro-genic atom will be

-\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{d^{2}f(r)}{dr^{2}}+\left[\frac{\ell(\ell+1){\hbar}^{2}}{2\mu r^{2}}-\frac{Zke^{2}}{r}\right]f(r)=Ef(r)

(32)

Arranging the equation as

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}-\frac{2\mu Zke^{2}}{{\hbar}^{2}r}+\frac{Z^{2}}{n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}\text{ substituting $E_{n}=-\frac{Z^{2}{\hbar}^{2}}{2\mu n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}$}

(33)

Now differentiating the equation with respect to
\ell
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\right]=\frac{2(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}-\frac{2Z^{2}}{n^{3}a_{0}^{2}}

(34)

For
n=n(\ell)=n_{r}+\ell+1
,
\frac{dn}{d\ell}=1
. Now multiplying both sides of equ. (34) by
f^{2}(r)
,

f^{2}(r)\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(r)}{f(r)}\right]=(2\ell+1)f^{2}(r)\frac{1}{r^{2}}-\frac{2Z^{2}}{n^{3}a_{0}^{2}}f^{2}(r)

(35)

Now integrating the equ. (35) with respect to
r
from
0
to
\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(r)}{f(r)}\right]dr=(2\ell+1)\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{2}(r)\frac{1}{r^{2}}dr

(36)

\displaystyle-\frac{2Z^{2}}{n^{3}a_{0}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{2}(r)dr

The normalization condition is given by
\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}|R(r)|^{2}dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{2}(r)dr=1
and
\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{2}(r)\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}dr
. So equ. (36) becomes,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(r)}{f(r)}\right]dr=(2\ell+1)\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}\rangle-\frac{2Z^{2}}{n^{3}a_{0}^{2}}

(37)

After a little bit manipulation, got
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\ell}}\left[\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(r)}{f(r)}\right]dr=0
.

Finally, from equ. (37),

\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}\rangle=\frac{2Z^{2}}{(2\ell+1)n^{3}a_{0}^{2}}

(38)

Then

\langle\hat{p}_{r}^{2}\rangle=\frac{Z^{2}{\hbar}^{2}}{n^{2}a_{0}^{2}}\left[1-\frac{2\ell(\ell+1)}{n(2\ell+1)}\right]

(39)

Finally the uncertainty in radial momentum will be,

\Delta\hat{p}_{r}=\frac{Z\hbar}{na_{0}}\sqrt{1-\frac{2\ell(\ell+1)}{n(2\ell+1)}}

(40)

.

IV
Infinite Spherical well potential

IV.1
Radial wave function

The spherical infinite potential is defined by

V(r)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{for }r\leq R\\
\infty,&\text{for }r>R\end{cases}

(43)

where
R
is the radius of spherical potential well. The wave function outside the spherical potential well is zero according to definition.
And inside the spherical well, the time independent Schrodinger equation is given by,

-\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{2m}{\nabla}^{2}\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=E\psi(r,\theta,\phi)

(44)

The well known radial part (Refs.
[
13
]
) is given by,

\frac{d^{2}R}{dz^{2}}+\frac{2}{z}\frac{dR}{dz}+\left(1-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{z^{2}}\right)R=0

(45)

where
z=kr
and
k^{2}=\frac{2mE}{{\hbar}^{2}}
and
\ell
is the azimuthal quantum number (angular momentum quantum number). The equ. (45) is the so-called Spherical Bessel equation, (Refs.
[

IV.3
Uncertainty in Radial momentum

We see that the radial momentum operator is given by,

\hat{p}_{r}=-i\hbar\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}[r]=-i\hbar\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\right)

As per equ. (7),

\displaystyle\langle\hat{p_{r}}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-i\hbar)\int_{0}^{R}r^{2}R_{n\ell}^{*}(r)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\right)R_{n\ell}(r)dr

\displaystyle=(-i\hbar)\left[\int_{0}^{R}r^{2}R_{n\ell}^{*}(r)dR_{n\ell}(r)+\int_{0}^{R}r|R_{n\ell}(r)|^{2}dr\right]

\displaystyle=(-i\hbar)\left(\frac{R}{z_{n\ell}}\right)^{2}\left[\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}{\rho}^{2}R_{n\ell}^{*}(\rho)dR_{n\ell}(\rho)+\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}\rho|R_{n\ell}(\rho)|^{2}d\rho\right]

We have the radial wave function is
R_{n\ell}(\rho)=N_{n\ell}j_{\ell}(\rho)
which gives
dR_{n\ell}(\rho)=N_{n\ell}dj_{\ell}(\rho)
.

\displaystyle\langle\hat{p}_{r}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-i\hbar)\frac{2}{R}\left[\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}{\rho}^{2}j_{\ell}(\rho)dj_{\ell}(\rho)+\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}\rho[j_{\ell}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho\right]

\displaystyle=(-i\hbar)\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}d[{\rho}^{2}[j_{\ell}(\rho)]^{2}]=0\text{ for }j_{\ell}(z_{n\ell})=0

Therefore the expectation value of radial momentum is zero,
\langle\hat{p}_{r}\rangle=0
as expected. Now our aim is
\langle\hat{p}_{r}^{2}\rangle
, for that we will use Virial theorem. The total energy of Infinite spherical well,
E_{n\ell m}=E_{n\ell}=z_{n\ell}^{2}\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{2mR^{2}}
and the total momentum is defined as
p_{n\ell m}=\sqrt{p_{r}^{2}+p_{\ell}^{2}}
where
p_{r}
is radial momentum and
p_{\ell}
is orbital momentum. We know that, orbital angular momentum is defined as
L=p_{\ell}r=\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}\hbar
, then
p_{\ell}=\frac{\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}}{r}\hbar
. Now from the momentum-kinetic energy relation
\langle E_{n\ell m}=T=\frac{p_{n\ell m}^{2}}{2m}\rangle
as
V=0
and
\langle z_{n\ell}^{2}\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{R^{2}}=p_{r}^{2}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}{\hbar}^{2}\rangle
gives

\langle\hat{p}_{r}^{2}\rangle=z_{n\ell}^{2}\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{R^{2}}-\ell(\ell+1){\hbar}^{2}\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}\rangle

(61)

\displaystyle\langle\frac{1}{\hat{r}^{2}}\rangle=\int_{0}^{R}|R_{n\ell}|^{2}dr=\frac{R}{z_{n\ell}}\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}|R_{n\ell}(\rho)|^{2}\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{R^{2}}\frac{1}{z_{n\ell}|j_{\ell+1}(z_{n\ell})|^{2}}\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}[j_{\ell}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho

\langle\hat{p_{r}}^{2}\rangle=\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left[z_{n\ell}^{2}-\frac{2\ell(\ell+1)}{z_{n\ell}|j_{\ell+1}(z_{n\ell})|^{2}}\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}[j_{\ell}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho\right]

where using Wolfram Mathematica,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z_{n\ell}}[j_{\ell}(\rho)]^{2}d\rho=2^{-2\ell}z_{n\ell}^{1+2l}\pi\frac{1}{(2\ell+1)^{3}\Gamma(\ell+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}

(62)

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{3}\left[\ell+\frac{1}{2},\ell+1;\ell+\frac{3}{2},\ell+\frac{3}{2},2\ell+2;-z_{n\ell}^{2}\right]

IV.5
Ground state of Infinite Spherical Well

The radial wave function of Hydrogen atom is given by,

R_{n\ell}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{R^{3}}}\frac{1}{|j_{\ell+1}(z_{n\ell})|}j_{\ell}\left(\frac{z_{n\ell}}{R}r\right)

For ground state (
n=1,\ell=0
),
R_{10}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{R^{3}}}\frac{1}{|j_{1}(\pi)|}j_{0}(\frac{\pi r}{R})
where
z_{10}=\pi
.
The radial probability density for ground state,
P_{10}(r)=r^{2}|R_{10}(r)|^{2}=N_{10}^{2}r^{2}[j_{0}(\frac{\pi r}{R})]^{2}
. For most probable distance from origin,
\frac{dP}{dr}=0
gives
j_{0}(\frac{\pi r}{R})+\frac{\pi r}{R}j_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}(\frac{\pi r}{R})=0
gives
r_{mp}=\frac{R}{2}
.
We have already,
\langle\hat{r}\rangle=\frac{R}{2}
and

V.1
Radial wave function

The potential of spherical harmonic oscillator is given by

V(r)=\frac{1}{2}m{\omega}^{2}r^{2}

(64)

where
\omega
is the angular frequency. The time independent Schrodinger equation for this system,

-\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{2m}{\nabla}^{2}\psi(r,\theta,\phi)+\frac{1}{2}m{\omega}^{2}r^{2}\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=E\psi(r,\theta,\phi)

(65)

And the corresponding well known radial part (Refs.
[
17
]
) is given by,

\frac{d^{2}R}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{dR}{dr}+\left({\alpha}^{2}r^{2}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}+k^{2}\right)R=0

(66)

where
\alpha=\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}
and
k=\sqrt{\frac{2mE}{{\hbar}^{2}}}
and
\ell
is azimuthal quantum number which is from effective potential,

V_{eff}(r)=\frac{1}{2}m{\omega}^{2}r^{2}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1){\hbar}^{2}}{2mr^{2}}

(67)

The solution of equ. (66) is given by,

R_{n_{r}\ell}(r)=N_{n_{r}\ell}(\sqrt{\alpha}r)^{\ell}e^{-\frac{\alpha r^{2}}{2}}L_{n_{r}}^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(\alpha r^{2})

(68)

A known fact for 3d harmonic oscillator,
n=2n_{r}+\ell
where
n_{r}
,
n
and
\ell
are radial, principal and azimuthal quantum number respectively where the radial quantum number
n_{r}
is specifically used for radial solution of Schrodinger equation and numbers of nodes in radial wave function. Meanwhile, the revised radial wave function (Refs.
[
17
]
) can be written as,

R_{n\ell}(r)=N_{n\ell}(\sqrt{\alpha}r)^{\ell}e^{-\frac{\alpha r^{2}}{2}}L_{\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell)}^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(\alpha r^{2})

(69)

Let introduce a dimensionless parameter,
\rho=\sqrt{\alpha}r
and

R_{n\ell}(\rho)=N_{n\ell}{\rho}^{\ell}e^{-\frac{{\rho}^{2}}{2}}L_{\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell)}^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}({\rho}^{2})

(70)

where
N_{n\ell}
is normalization constant, which can be determined by normalization condition and
L_{\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell)}^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}
is associated Laguerre function. Here the associated Laguerre is of non-linear weight
{\rho}^{2}
, we can replace it by linear weight
\eta={\rho}^{2}
. Hence the fully revised radial wave function can be written as,

R_{n\ell}(\eta)=N_{n\ell}{\eta}^{\frac{\ell}{2}}e^{-\frac{\eta}{2}}L_{\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell)}^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(\eta)

(71)

One important result is
k
can be expressed as
k=\sqrt{2(2n_{r}+\ell+\frac{3}{2}})\alpha
(Refs.
[
18
]
) that gives energy eigen values as,

E_{n_{r}\ell}=\left(2n_{r}+\ell+\frac{3}{2}\right)\hbar\omega

(72)

shows that energy eigen values can be also also written as

E_{n}=\left(n+\frac{3}{2}\right)\hbar\omega

(73)

which is a well known result for 3d harmonic quantum oscillator in Cartesian co-ordinate. Let make normalize the radial wave function. The normalization condition is given by
\int_{0}^{\infty}P(r)dr=1
where
P(r)=r^{2}|R_{n\ell}(r)|^{2}
.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}|R_{n\ell}(r)|^{2}dr

(74)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\eta}|R_{n\ell}(\eta)|^{2}d\eta\text{ as $\eta=\alpha r^{2}$}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\alpha}^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{2}|N_{n\ell}|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\eta}^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\eta}\left[L_{\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell)}^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(\eta)\right]^{2}d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{{\alpha}^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{2}|N_{n\ell}|^{2}\widetilde{I}

The orthonormal property of associated Laguerre function is given by as seen earlier in equ. (13),

\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{a}e^{-z}L^{a}_{b}(z)L^{a}_{c}(z)dz=\frac{\Gamma{(a+b+1)}}{\Gamma{(b+1)}}\delta_{bc}

After naming
a=\ell+\frac{1}{2}
and
b=c=\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell)
, the above normalization integral becomes

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}=\frac{{\Gamma(a+b+1)}}{\Gamma(b+1)}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(n+\ell)+\frac{3}{2})}{(\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell))!}

(75)

This gives us the expression of normalization constant, is given by

N_{n\ell}=\sqrt{\frac{2{\alpha}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell))!}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(n+\ell)+\frac{3}{2})}}

(76)

We know that for 3d harmonic oscillator problem, the azimuthal quantum number,
\ell
can take values in way, (Refs.
[
11
]
)
\ell=n,n-2,n-4,....,{\ell}_{min}
where
{\ell}_{min}
is defined as,

\ell_{\text{min}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{for }n\text{ odd}\\
0&\text{for }n\text{ even}\end{cases}

(77)

The examples be like, for
n=6
,
\ell=6,4,2,0
and for
n=7
,
\ell=7,5,3,1
. From this idea, it’s crystal clear that
n+\ell
or
n-\ell
is a double positive integer multiple.
It gives the introduction of
p=\frac{1}{2}(n+\ell)
and
q=\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell)
is both positive integer including zero and we can re-write the normalization integral as

\widetilde{I}=\frac{\Gamma\left(p+\frac{3}{2}\right)}{q!}

(78)

For
r
is positive integer,
\Gamma\left(r+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{(2r)!}{2^{r}r!}\sqrt{\pi}
, then

\Gamma\left(p+\frac{3}{2}\right)=\frac{(2p+2)!}{2^{p+1}(p+1)!}\sqrt{\pi}

V.5
Ground state of Spherical harmonic oscillator

The radial wave function of Spherical harmonic oscillator is given by,

R_{n\ell}(r)=N_{n\ell}(\sqrt{\alpha}r)^{\ell}e^{-\frac{\alpha r^{2}}{2}}L_{\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell)}^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(\alpha r^{2})

where
N_{n\ell}=\frac{2{\alpha}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\frac{1}{2}(n-\ell))!}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(n+\ell)+\frac{3}{2})}
and
\alpha=\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}
. For ground state (
n=0,\ell=0
),
N_{00}=\frac{2{\alpha}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}
,
R_{00}(r)=N_{00}e^{-\frac{\alpha r^{2}}{2}}L_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\alpha r^{2})=N_{00}e^{-\frac{\alpha r^{2}}{2}}
as
L_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)=1
. The radial probability density for ground state,
P_{00}(r)=r^{2}|R_{00}(r)|^{2}=N_{00}^{2}r^{2}e^{-\alpha r^{2}}
. For most probable radius,
\frac{dP}{dr}=0
gives
r=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
(non-acceptable) and
r_{mp}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}
. And we have,
\langle r\rangle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}=1.1283\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}
and
\Delta r=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{4}{\pi}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}=0.4762\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}
.

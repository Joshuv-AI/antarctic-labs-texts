Splitting symplectic monodromy
Ailsa Keating, Ivan Smith, Michael Wemyss

Abstract
Abstract: We discuss some examples in which symplectic monodromy (provably or conjecturally) splits off the symplectic mapping class group, hoping to illustrate different techniques and inputs to the arguments. Along the way we formulate several open questions and conjectures.

1
Introduction

Let
(X,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold; we will typically be interested in non-compact manifolds well-behaved (e.g. convex) at infinity. Suppose
X
moves in a moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{X}
which carries a universal family
\EuScript{C}\to\mathcal{M}_{X}
which is a locally Hamiltonian fibre bundle. Symplectic parallel transport gives rise to a monodromy representation

\rho:\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{X})\to\pi_{0}\Symp(X,\omega).

(1.A)

This may be valued in
\pi_{0}\Symp_{ct}(X,\omega)
depending on the geometry of the family at infinity. Such representations provide arguably the primary source of constructions of interesting symplectomorphisms. This suggests that a fundamental question is to understand the map
\rho
.

To understand a group one should make it act on something. The group
\pi_{0}\Symp(X,\omega)
acts by autoequivalences of the (derived) Fukaya category
\EuScript{F}(X)
, defining a representation

\pi_{0}\Symp(X,\omega)\to\mathrm{Auteq}(\EuScript{F}(X)).

(1.B)

This may be up to shift; we will consider gradings, and the question of compact or non-compact support of maps, more carefully in the main text.

A priori
the symplectic topology of
X
has no reason to know anything about its incarnation as a special fibre of
\EuScript{C}\to\mathcal{M}_{X}
(for instance, its incarnation as an algebro-geometric complete intersection), and yet remarkably, in a few non-trivial cases, one can relate the autoequivalence group to
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{X})
itself, and infer that
\rho

splits
.

This note illustrates that phenomenon in some basic cases: certain Milnor fibres (Theorem
9
), quiver 3-folds (Theorem
16
) and mirrors to 3-fold flopping contractions (Theorem
29
).
The results in Section
7

have a different character, morally arising from monodromy in a space of integral affine structures on an SYZ base.
The point of gathering examples together is to emphasise that it is an interesting question to understand how widespread this phenomenon is, and to show that establishing it in any given setting typically requires non-trivial machinery. The extent to which the collection of cases gathered here is prototypical is itself an interesting question.

{rmk}

The paper does not discuss an exhaustive list of splittings of symplectic monodromy, but we gather a few other examples in Section
8
, for completeness and to help establish the broader scene for the reader.

8.3
(Log) Calabi-Yau surfaces

Gromov

contractible. Later, Wendl

The symplectomorphism groups of log Calabi-Yau surfaces were studied systematically via homological mirror symmetry in
[
29
,
28
]
. We summarise the relevant results.

Let
(Y,D)
be a log Calabi-Yau surface with maximal boundary and split mixed Hodge structure, and let
M
be its mirror. Let
U=Y\setminus D
. We have a mirror equivalence of categories
D(U)\cong D^{b}(\tw\EuScript{W}(M))
.
In this context, there is a natural subgroup of ‘compactly supported’ auto-equivalences of

,
\Auteq_{c}D(U)
(see
[
28
, Definition 2.16]

\pi_{0}\Symp_{c}(M)\longrightarrow\Auteq_{c}D(U).

There exist conjectural descriptions of
\Auteq D(Y)
and of
\Auteq_{c}D(U)
,
both in terms of generators and as fundamental groups of suitable moduli spaces of complex structures, see
[
79
, Conjecture 1.2]
,
[
28
, Conjectures 1.5 and 1.7]

K3

\Auteq_{c}D(U)=\langle\,Q,\EuScript{T}\,\rangle\rtimes\Aut(Y,D)
. Here

, where
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
are the irreducible components of
D
, is the subgroup of line bundles
\EuScript{L}
on
Y
such that
\EuScript{L}|_{D}=\EuScript{O}_{D}
(acting by tensor product);
\EuScript{T}
is the collection of spherical twists in objects of the form

, for
C\subset U

(-2)
curve and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
; and
\Aut(Y,D)
is the group of automorphisms of
Y
fixing
D
pointwise (acting by pushforward). By the Torelli theorem in
[
27
]
,
\Aut(Y,D)
is identified with the quotient of the group
\mathrm{Adm}
of admissible lattice automorphisms of
\Pic(Y)
by the Weyl group

.

The spherical objects

are mirror to embedded Lagrangian spheres
[
28
, Proposition 5.2]
, with spherical twists mirror to Dehn twists in the usual manner. Moreover, there is a faithful group homomorphism
[
28
, Theorem 7.1]

\iota:Q\rtimes\mathrm{Adm}/W\hookrightarrow\pi_{0}\Symp_{c}(M).

(8.A)

The image symplectomorphisms are described in terms of the SYZ (almost-toric) Lagrangian fibration on
M
.
Under
\iota
, a line bundle
\EuScript{L}
acts as a ‘Lagrangian translation’, with mirror autoequivalence
\otimes\EuScript{L}\in\Auteq D(U)
; and an element

acts as a ‘nodal slide recombination’, with mirror autoequivalence is
\phi_{\ast}\in\Auteq D(U)
.
In particular, combining back with Dehn twists, one has precise descriptions of the monodromy representation of the (conjectural) relevant moduli space; and, subject to the conjecture on
\Auteq_{c}D(U)
, we expect the representation (
8.A
) to split.

For projective Calabi-Yau surfaces, a general splitting result is expected to follow from Bridgeland’s well-known conjecture on spaces of stability conditions on K3 surfaces.
Some Picard rank one

The natural map

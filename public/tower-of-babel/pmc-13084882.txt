Regional immunosuppression and associated systemic markers in focally relapsed sarcomatoid mesothelioma: case report
Authors: Hely Ollila, Prateek Kulkarni, Hyojin Kim, Pratiti Ankola, Navin K Chintala, Carlos Thomas, Jennifer L Sauter, Michael Offin, Prasad S Adusumilli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 52-year-old man presented with sarcomatoid diffuse pleural mesothelioma that had relapsed at an isolated site after a complete response to dual-immune checkpoint inhibition (ICI). Targeted sequencing exhibited amplification of chromosome 9p24, encompassing JAK2, PD-L1, PD-L2, and PTPRD in the relapsed (post-ICI) tumor, compared with baseline (pre-ICI). On multiplex immunofluorescence, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) and CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) made up most of the cells in baseline and relapsed tumor (59% and 47%, respectively). Baseline tumor cells expressed genes linked to extracellular matrix remodeling and epithelial-mesenchymal transition, intermixed with M2-like TAMs and tissue-resident, effector-like CTLs. Relapsed tumor cells shifted to a growth factor-driven phenotype (NT5E, NOD1, GATA2, FN1, PDCD1LG2) that is known to cause functional impairment of CTLs, which then transitioned to an exhausted state (FCRL3, CST7, GPR171, TRAT1, LAG3); exhausted CD8+ and CD4+ T cells are seen in the peripheral blood at relapse. TAMs were enriched in antigen-presentation (CD80, CD86, CXCL10), extracellular matrix–degradation (MMP9, CTSL), and CTL-suppression (ARG1, PLA2G7) pathways. Our analyses revealed that regional immunosuppression mediated by adaptive reprogramming of tumor-cell and immune-cell (TAMs, CTLs)-intrinsic changes—rather than by immune evasion or stromal exclusion—served as a mechanism of acquired resistance to dual-ICI therapy.

Insights

Investigation of an isolated foci of relapse after acquired resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy showed that regional immunosuppression by adaptive reprogramming of tumor-cell and immune-cell-intrinsic changes, rather than by immune evasion, is the underlying mechanism.

Background

Sarcomatoid diffuse pleural mesothelioma (DPM) is an aggressive cancer that is resistant to chemotherapy, with median survival of <1 year. Following CheckMate 743, which showed dual-immune checkpoint inhibition (ICI) therapy (antiprogrammed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein-4) extends survival by 9 months over chemotherapy, dual-ICI therapy became the standard of care for patients with sarcomatoid DPM.1 CDKN2A deletions are associated with primary resistance to immunotherapy in DPM; however, details of acquired resistance have not been reported.24 Dense CD4+ and CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes in the tumor microenvironment, predominantly expressing PD-1+, have been reported in sarcomatoid mesothelioma.5 Using matched pre-ICI (baseline) and post-ICI (relapsed) tumor from a patient with sarcomatoid DPM who experienced isolated foci of relapse after complete response to dual-ICI therapy, we performed multiplexed immunofluorescence, in situ single-cell RNA transcriptomics, and genomic profiling (see online supplemental material) and demonstrate that regional immunosuppression is the predominant factor in the relapsed tumor.

Case presentation

A 52-year-old man presented with progressive shortness of breath and cough, with no self-reported history of classical asbestos exposure or radiation. Positron emission tomography (PET)-CT revealed left hemithorax pleural thickening, nodularity, and effusion (online supplemental figure 1). Video-assisted thoracoscopic biopsy confirmed sarcomatoid DPM, with 95% of tumor cells expressing programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) on immunohistochemistry. First-line treatment with dual-ICI (nivolumab and ipilimumab) resulted in complete radiographic (no avidity on PET scan) and clinical response. However, 10 months after initiation of treatment, PET-CT revealed relapse, an isolated focus (3.2×1.4 cm) of hypermetabolic activity in the left lower chest anterior pleura (online supplemental figure 1) despite ongoing dual-ICI therapy. The patient underwent left pleurectomy and decortication. Pathologic assessment showed pathologic response at all sites except an isolated focus of sarcomatoid DPM consistent with imaging; 100% of tumor cells at the site of relapse had PD-L1 expression. After resection, the patient discontinued ICI therapy because of immune-related pneumonitis and encephalitis and is currently under active surveillance, without any therapy, at 12 months from surgical resection.

Discussion

Genomic profiling of pre-ICI (baseline) and post-ICI (relapsed) tumor revealed whole-arm amplification of chromosome 9p24, encompassing JAK2, CD274 (PD-L1), PDCD1LG2, and PTPRD in relapsed tumor. No additional mutations or statistical differences in tumor mutational burden (11 vs 9.9 mutations/megabase) or microsatellite instability scores (2.56 vs 1.49) were observed between baseline and relapsed tumor (figure 1). Absence of immune cell infiltrate or stromal exclusion was not observed on matched tumor microenvironment analysis of baseline and relapsed tumor. Phenotypic and functional reprogramming of tumor cells, a high proportion of tumor-infiltrating CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) were observed in both baseline and relapsed tumor (81% vs 72% of all cells; TAMs and CTLs 59% and 47%, respectively) (figure 2).

Single-cell transcriptomics-derived cell cluster identities were inferred from the list of the top 15 genes significantly expressed (log2 fold change ≥1.0 and p adjusted ≤0.05) within each cluster. Online supplemental table 1 shows the list of the top 15 genes associated with each cluster and the predicted cell types for reference. Analysis of baseline tumor cells (cluster 4) revealed a mesenchymal-like phenotype, with high expression of AREG, LUM, IL1R1, A2M, and C1R, which are associated with extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, and inflammatory signals. In contrast, relapsed tumor cells (cluster 3) were enriched in NT5E, FN1, LIF, PD-L2, and ID1 (figure 1 and online supplemental table 2), suggesting a shift toward adenosine-mediated suppression, upregulation of alternative checkpoint ligands, and transcriptional reprogramming—potentially contributing to T-cell exhaustion and resistance. Supporting this observation, on multiplex immunofluorescence (online supplemental table 3), CD8+ PD-1+ cells were significantly higher in relapsed tumor than in baseline tumor (figure 2). Relative differential gene expression analysis of tumor cells revealed relapsed tumor cells (cluster 3) predominantly expressed CDKN2B, NOD1, GATA2, FN1, and IFITM3, whereas baseline tumor cells (cluster 4) predominantly expressed LUM, IL1R1, AREG, SPARC, and VCAN. Our analysis indicates relapsed tumor cells may have transitioned to an immune-evasion phenotype, with gene-amplification-driven overexpression of PD-L1 and PD-L2, increased adenosine pathway activity via NT5E (CD73), and induction of transcriptional regulators LIF, ID1, and IFITM3.

Although both baseline and relapsed tumor showed ongoing CD8+ T-cell infiltration and response, the majority of cells in baseline tumor were CD8+ CTLs with features of activation and effector response (cluster 1: high expression of FASLG and CXCR6); these factors were rare in post-ICI tumor (27% vs 2%). In contrast, the relapsed tumor contained CD8+ CTLs (cluster 0: characterized by high expression of FCRL3, CST7, GPR171, TRAT1, and LAG3) that exhibited a gene signature of an exhaustive state, with suppressed cytotoxic activity. Differential gene expression analysis conducted between the clusters revealed ZNF683, TRAT1, TBX21, GPR171, and CST7 as the top genes associated with this exhaustive phenotype. The emergence of immune checkpoint GPR171 expression in relapsed tumor CD8+ CTLs is noteworthy, since blockade of GPR171 signaling has been shown to improve the efficacy of ICI therapy.6

TAMs, although reduced in proportion in relapsed tumor (25% vs 15%), underwent significant reprogramming and were diversified in function, with the emergence of two distinct populations (figure 1B). Baseline tumor harbored inflammatory monocyte-derived macrophages (cluster 2: 25% of total cells), with high expression of IL2RA, GPR34, APOE, CD163, and CXCL12, as well as VSIG4 and CD14, which are suggestive of an immunosuppressive M2-like TAM phenotype. Relapsed tumor contained two distinct TAM populations, each with the potential to suppress CTL activity (cluster 5: 11% of total cells, expressing MMP9, GPR183, PLA2G7, CCL8, CTSL, VSIG4, and CD14; cluster 13: 4% of total cells, expressing CCL7, MMP12, S100B, ANPEP, FCGR2B, MARCO, and ARG1). Interestingly, TAMs in relapsed tumor expressed ECM-degrading enzymes (MMP9, MMP12, CTSL). Relapsed tumor had upregulated expression of immunosuppressive checkpoints and drivers of resistance (NOD1, FN1, PD-L1, PD-L2), with exhaustion of CD8+ CTLs, rendering them dysfunctional (GPR171, FCRL3, LAG3, PDCD1, TIGIT, TOX). TAMs capable of ECM degradation (MMP9, MMP12, CTSL) and CTL-suppressing activity (ARG1, PLA2G7) were also present (figure 2B).

Peripheral blood analysis revealed elevated expression of exhaustion and immune checkpoint markers on circulating T cells at relapse (online supplemental figure 2). Both CD8+ and CD4+ CTLs showed higher expression of LAG3 (82% and 83%, respectively), TIGIT (32% and 36%, respectively), and PD-1 (17% and 39%, respectively). These observations parallel the relapsed tumor microenvironment characterized by exhausted CD8+ CTLs (figures 1B and 2A). Together, these data suggest a systemic and tumor-associated cell exhaustion at relapse.

Conclusion

Our analyses suggest acquired resistance to dual-ICI therapy in a patient with sarcomatoid DPM occurred through adaptive reprogramming of tumor cells, CD8+ CTLs, and TAMs, rather than by well-described mechanisms of immune evasion, such as antigen loss, lower tumor infiltration of T cells, or stromal exclusion. A relapsed tumor at an isolated site showed characteristics of “regional immunosuppression”. Regional immunosuppression within glioblastoma tumor has been described.7 Matched analysis and characterization of baseline pre-ICI and relapsed post-ICI tumor identified actionable pathways (CD73, GPR171, GPR183, LIF, ID1, CSF1R) that can guide further investigation and development of next-generation immunotherapy strategies.

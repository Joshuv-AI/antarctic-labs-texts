miR172‐AP2 Controls Age‐Dependent Regeneration Competence in Populus
Authors: Kaiwen Lyu, Abdul Azeez, Victor Busov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPlants maintain remarkable regenerative potential, yet this capacity declines with developmental age, limiting clonal propagation and biotechnological applications. Here, we show that the miR172‐AP2 module serves as a central determinant of regeneration competence in Populus. Regenerative capacity declines progressively along the shoot developmental gradient, correlating with elevated miR172 and reduced AP2 transcript levels. Functional analyses demonstrate that AP2 promotes both shoot and root regeneration by directly activating downstream transcriptional programmes, including AINTEGUMENTA‐LIKE1, BIG LEAF and LIKE APETALA1, which regulate organogenic growth and developmental competence. Elevated miR172 or cytokinin signalling represses AP2, reducing regenerative outcomes, indicating that hormonal and developmental signals converge on this module. Genome‐wide analyses reveal that AP2 coordinates a broad network of genes controlling wound response, hormone signalling and cell fate specification. Notably, enhanced regeneration via AP2 occurs without detectable developmental abnormalities, highlighting the practical potential of this module. Collectively, these findings uncover a mechanistic link between developmental phase identity and regeneration, revealing a conserved regulatory module that may be leveraged to enhance regeneration across plant species, with broad implications for propagation, tissue culture and biotechnology.

Introduction

The ability of plants to regenerate new organs like shoots and roots from differentiated tissues is a defining feature of plant developmental plasticity and enables vegetative propagation, tissue culture and genetic transformation (Eshed Williams 2021; Ikeuchi et al. 2016). In woody perennials such as Populus, regeneration competence is critical for clonal propagation and biotechnological improvement. However, regenerative capacity typically declines with plant maturation, presenting a major biological and practical limitation (Bannoud and Bellini 2021; Ye et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2015). Although this age‐dependent decline has long been known (Busov et al. 2004; Greenwood et al. 2001), the molecular mechanisms linking developmental phase transition to the loss of regenerative potential remain poorly understood, thereby limiting efforts to fully harness plant regeneration for technological applications.

Developmental phase transitions in plants are governed by the conserved miR156/SPL‐miR172/AP2 regulatory pathways, which coordinate plant ageing by sequentially and inversely regulating microRNA and transcription factor activity (Borthakur et al. 2022; Poethig 2010; Poethig and Fouracre 2024; Wu et al. 2009). During maturation, miR156 levels decline whereas miR172 accumulates, resulting in increased expression of SQUAMOSA PROMOTER BINDING PROTEIN‐LIKE (SPL) transcription factors and decreased APETALA2 (AP2) expression, thereby promoting the transition from juvenile to adult developmental programmes (Borthakur et al. 2022; Poethig 2010; Poethig and Fouracre 2024; Wu et al. 2009). Genetic manipulation of these pathways affects multiple traits, including organ patterning, flowering competence, leaf morphology, metabolism and physiology (Poethig and Fouracre 2024; Wang et al. 2009; Wu et al. 2009). The downstream mechanisms controlling these traits vary substantially with respect to molecular mechanisms and target genes.

Several studies indicate that developmental phase transition also influences regeneration competence. Juvenile tissues generally regenerate more efficiently than adult tissues in both herbaceous and woody species, and prolonging juvenility through miR156 overexpression enhances shoot and root regeneration primarily by impinging on the auxin biosynthesis and cytokinin response (Ye et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2015). Both the miR156‐SPL and miR172‐AP2 pathways have also been implicated in developmental timing in the woody species Lycium ruthenicum (Rao et al. 2021). Nevertheless, studies directly connecting developmental phase transition with regeneration competence have largely focused on miR156/SPL‐mediated regulation (Ye et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2015).

Beyond miR156, other miRNAs have also been implicated in adventitious organogenesis in woody species. Stage‐specific miRNA expression has been reported during adventitious shoot organogenesis in Acacia crassicarpa, whereas functional studies have identified the miR476a‐RFL module in Populus and the miR160a‐ARF10 module in Acer rubrum as regulators of adventitious rooting. Recent miRNA profiling in peach has further implicated miR319 and miR172 in adventitious root development (Liu et al. 2014; Wang, Shen, et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2022). Although the miR172‐AP2 module has been implicated in somatic embryogenesis in Arabidopsis, where AP2 was proposed to negatively regulate WUS expression (Nowak et al. 2022), its functions in shoot and root regeneration remain largely unexplored, particularly at the level of downstream mechanisms.

Given the widespread conservation of the miR172‐AP2 regulatory module across angiosperms, investigating how this module coordinates developmental phase transitions with regeneration could reveal mechanisms applicable to vegetative propagation and organogenesis in diverse species. Populus provides a powerful system to address these questions because leaves along the shoot axis form a well‐defined developmental gradient from juvenile to mature stages, allowing regeneration competence to be assessed relative to developmental age (Bannoud and Bellini 2021).

Although regeneration can also proceed through callus‐mediated pathways, here we focus on direct organogenesis, in which shoots form directly from leaf explants (Smeringai et al. 2023), because this approach provides a clear readout of intrinsic organogenic competence. Direct regeneration captures the early stages in which developmental phase regulators are most likely to act, allowing dissection of core mechanisms linking phase transitions to regenerative potential without the additional complexity introduced by pluripotent intermediates (Smeringai et al. 2023). Finally, focusing on this pathway provides a generalizable framework to study regeneration across species, because direct organogenesis is broadly conserved and represents a fundamental aspect of plant developmental plasticity (Eshed Williams 2021).

Here, we investigated whether the miR172‐AP2 module links developmental phase transition to regeneration competence in Populus and explored the underlying molecular mechanisms. Functional analyses indicate that AP2 positively influences regeneration by coordinating downstream developmental regulators and regeneration‐associated transcriptional networks. These findings highlight a mechanistic link between developmental phase identity and regenerative potential and point to the miR172‐AP2 module as a key regulatory node controlling age‐dependent organogenic capacity in plants.

Results

Age‐Dependent Decline in Regeneration Competence Correlates With the Expression Dynamics of the miR172‐AP2 Module

To determine whether regeneration competence declines with developmental age in Populus, we compared the shoot regeneration capacity of leaf explants collected along the developmental gradient of tissue‐culture‐grown poplar plants. Explants derived from basal juvenile leaves (Leaf 1–2) produced multiple shoots, whereas explants from distal mature leaves (Leaf 10–11) exhibited markedly reduced regenerative capacity (Figure 1a). Quantitative analysis confirmed a significant reduction in the number of regenerated shoots per explant in Leaf 10–11 compared with Leaf 1–2 (Figure 1b), indicating that regeneration competence declines along the leaf position gradient.

Vegetative phase transition in plants is coordinated by the conserved miR156‐miR172 regulatory module (Wang et al. 2009; Wu et al. 2009). Consistent with this developmental gradient, miR172 abundance was significantly higher in mature leaves (Leaf 10–11), whereas miR156 levels were reduced relative to juvenile leaves (Figure 1c,d).

Target prediction using psRNATarget (Dai et al. 2018) identified AP2‐1 and AP2‐2 as candidate targets of miR172b (Table S1). Consistently, transcript levels of both genes were markedly decreased in mature leaves (Figure 1e,f).

We next examined gene expression across sequential leaves to assess the dynamics of this pathway. Analysis of leaves at positions 1, 5, 10 and 15 revealed a progressive increase in miR172 accompanied by a gradual decline in miR156 expression (Figure S1a). Consistently, transcript levels of AP2‐1 and AP2‐2 decreased along the same developmental gradient (Figure S1b,c). These data reveal a strong correlation between the miR172‐AP2 regulatory module and the age‐dependent decline in regeneration competence in Populus.

Transgenic lines overexpressing the MIR172b precursor were generated to examine the role of miR172b in shoot regeneration. RT‐qPCR analysis confirmed strong accumulation of miR172b in three independent lines (MIR172b‐OE4, MIR172b‐OE12 and MIR172b‐OE14) relative to wild type (Figure 2a).

The MIR172b‐overexpressing lines also displayed significantly reduced shoot regeneration efficiency compared with wild type (Figure 2d,e).

AP2 Promotes Shoot Regeneration and Subsequent Shoot Growth in Populus

To test whether AP2 regulates shoot regeneration, we generated transgenic lines overexpressing AP2‐1 or AP2‐2 (AP2‐OE) as well as CRISPR/Cas9 loss‐of‐function mutants (ap2‐1, ap2‐2 and the ap2‐1/ap2‐2 double mutant). Shoot regeneration was evaluated using leaf explants collected from different leaf positions.

To determine whether changes in visible shoot number were accompanied by changes in shoot elongation at an early stage, shoot number and shoot length were quantified after 4 weeks on shoot induction medium. The AP2‐1‐OE and AP2‐2‐OE explants produced significantly more shoots than WT‐717‐FT, whereas mean shoot length did not differ significantly between the AP2‐overexpressing lines and WT‐717‐FT (Figure S2). In contrast, the ap2‐1, ap2‐2 and ap2‐1/ap2‐2 mutants showed significant reductions in both shoot number and mean shoot length. Thus, the increase in visible shoot number in AP2‐overexpressing explants was detected before a measurable increase in shoot length.

In wild‐type plants, juvenile explants (Leaf 1–2) produced substantially more shoots than mature explants (Leaf 10–11), consistent with the developmental decline observed above (Figure 3). In contrast, AP2 overexpression lines exhibited markedly enhanced shoot regeneration, whereas ap2 mutants showed strongly reduced regenerative capacity.

Quantitative analysis confirmed that AP2 overexpression significantly increased the number of regenerated shoots per explant relative to wild type, whereas the ap2‐1/ap2‐2 double mutant displayed severely compromised regeneration (Figure 3b,c).

Notably, AP2 overexpression significantly increased shoot production from mature Leaf 10–11 explants. Conversely, the ap2‐1, ap2‐2 and ap2‐1/ap2‐2 mutants produced significantly fewer shoots even from juvenile Leaf 1–2 explants. At 7 weeks, regenerated shoots were significantly longer in both AP2‐overexpressing lines and significantly shorter in all three ap2 mutant genotypes than in WT‐717 (Figure 3d).

After 10 weeks on shoot induction medium, AP2‐1‐OE and AP2‐2‐OE explants retained significantly higher shoot numbers and produced significantly longer shoots than WT‐717‐FT (Figure S3b,c). In contrast, the ap2‐1, ap2‐2 and ap2‐1/ap2‐2 mutants produced fewer and shorter shoots and displayed extensive explant browning (Figure S3). These results demonstrate that AP2 promotes both shoot production and subsequent shoot growth during regeneration in Populus.

AP2 Directly Activates Downstream Developmental Regulators

We identified candidate AP2 downstream regulators associated with regeneration by prioritizing development‐associated genes containing putative AP2‐binding sites based on previously reported AP2 ChIP‐seq data (Wang, Liao, et al. 2025). Among these, AINTEGUMENTA‐LIKE 1 (AIL1), a member of the ANT family involved in organ growth and cell proliferation (Mizukami and Fischer 2000) and BIG LEAF (BL), corresponding to the Arabidopsis homologue STERILE APETALA (SAP), which regulates organ size (Byzova et al. 1999; Li et al. 2018) were selected for further analysis based on the presence of putative AP2 binding sites in their regulatory regions.

Expression analysis revealed that transcript levels of AIL1 and BL were significantly elevated in AP2 overexpression lines and reduced in the ap2 mutants (Figure 4a,b), indicating positive regulation by AP2.

Putative AP2‐binding regions identified from published ChIP‐seq analysis (Wang, Liao, et al. 2025) were selected for ChIP‐qPCR validation (Figure 4c,d). For AIL1, the candidate AP2‐binding sites were located in the promoter region. Chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by quantitative PCR (ChIP‐qPCR) revealed significant enrichment of these promoter fragments in AP2 immunoprecipitates compared with mock controls (Figure 4c), demonstrating direct binding of AP2 to the AIL1 promoter. For BL, the candidate AP2‐binding sites were located in downstream regulatory regions. ChIP‐qPCR analysis showed that these regions were also significantly enriched in AP2 immunoprecipitates (Figure 4d), indicating that AP2 directly binds to distal regulatory elements of BL.

To test whether the AP2 downstream targets affect shoot regeneration, we used transgenic lines overexpressing AIL1 and BL (Yordanov et al. 2017). Both AIL1‐OE and BL‐OE lines exhibited significantly enhanced shoot regeneration compared with wild type (Figure 4e,f), demonstrating that AP2 direct targets identified through genomic and binding analyses function downstream of AP2 to promote regeneration. Consistent with the age‐dependent decline in regeneration competence, expression levels of AIL1 and BL were significantly reduced in mature plants relative to juvenile tissues (Figure S4).

AP2 Regulates Early Transcriptional Reprogramming Associated With Regeneration Competence

Although AIL1 and BL contribute to AP2‐mediated shoot regeneration, they are unlikely to fully explain the broad effects of AP2 on regenerative competence. We therefore performed RNA‐seq to profile genome‐wide transcriptional changes associated with AP2 activity at an early stage of regeneration. Leaf explants from control, AP2 overexpression lines, and ap2 mutants were collected after 1 week on shoot induction medium (SIM), prior to visible shoot formation (Figure 5a).

(b) Heatmap showing AP2 activity‐associated gene expression patterns across genotypes. (c) Integration of RNA‐seq and ChIP‐seq identifying putative direct AP2 targets. (d) Gene ontology enrichment of AP2‐associated genes, including stress responses, hormone signalling and meristem‐related processes.

Cluster analysis identified 172 genes displaying consistent AP2 activity‐associated expression patterns across genotypes, including genes induced in AP2 overexpression lines and reduced in ap2 mutants, as well as genes with the opposite trend (Figure 5b; Table S2). Gene ontology analysis of these AP2‐responsive genes revealed enrichment for processes related to wounding, water deprivation, oxidative stress, ion homeostasis and cell wall modification (Table S2), indicating that AP2 activity is associated with early transcriptional states linked to stress response and tissue remodelling.

Differential expression analysis further identified 11 109 genes responsive to modulation of AP2 activity across genotypes, of which 1992 overlapped with AP2 ChIP‐seq targets (Wang, Liao, et al. 2025), defining a set of putative direct AP2 targets (Figure 5c; Table S3). GO enrichment of these genes highlighted categories related to stress and redox processes, hormone signalling and developmental regulation (Figure 5d; Table S3), consistent with a broader regulatory role of AP2 in establishing a regeneration‐permissive transcriptional state.

The AP2‐bound AP2‐responsive gene set included regulators associated with redox and stress signalling (RBOHD; Morales et al. 2016), hormone signalling (ERF1 (Lorenzo et al. 2003), ERF109 (Zhang et al. 2019)), and developmental regulation (ARF5/MONOPTEROS (Herud et al. 2016), PXY (Fisher and Turner 2007) and WOX13 (Ikeuchi et al. 2022; Ogura et al. 2023)), supporting the view that AP2 coordinates multiple regulatory pathways required for early regenerative competence rather than acting through a limited set of downstream developmental effectors.

Hormone Signalling Modulates the miR172‐AP2 Regeneration Pathway

Transcriptional responses to hormone treatments were analysed to examine potential upstream regulatory inputs influencing the miR172‐AP2 pathway. Time‐course experiments revealed dynamic expression changes in miR172, AP2‐1, AP2‐2 and downstream targets (AIL1, BL) following auxin or cytokinin treatment (Figure S5). Notably, under cytokinin treatment, miR172 expression increased at 8 h, whereas AP2‐1 and AP2‐2 expression showed a declining trend (Figure S5c).

To further assess whether elevated cytokinin levels contribute to AP2 repression and reduced regeneration, we analysed poplar cytokinin‐overproducing transgenics, which overexpress the cytokinin‐activating enzyme PtaLOG1 (a LONELY GUY homologue) (Rauschendorfer et al. 2020). Transcript levels of both AP2‐1 and AP2‐2 were significantly reduced in these transgenics (Figure S6a,b). Consistent with reduced AP2 expression, shoot regeneration efficiency was markedly lower in the cytokinin overproducing genotype (Figure S6c,d). Thus, elevated cytokinin levels were associated with reduced AP2‐1 and AP2‐2 expression and decreased shoot regeneration under these conditions.

AP2 Promotes Adventitious Rooting Through a Downstream Transcriptional Cascade

AP2 positively regulates adventitious root regeneration. Adventitious root formation was significantly enhanced in AP2 overexpression lines, whereas the ap2‐1/ap2‐2 mutant exhibited strongly reduced root formation compared with wild type (Figure 6a,b). However, no significant differences in mean adventitious root length were detected among the genotypes at the assessed time point (Figure S7).

Given that the AP2 downstream targets AIL1 and BL promote shoot regeneration, their potential roles in root formation were further examined. Both AIL1‐OE and BL‐OE lines produced significantly more adventitious roots than wild type (Figure S8).

AIL1 has been reported to promote adventitious rooting and to function downstream of the AP1 homologue LAP1 (Azeez et al. 2014; Rigal et al. 2012). Based on this relationship, LAP1 was selected for further analysis. LAP1 transcript levels were markedly increased in AP2 overexpression lines and reduced in the ap2‐1/ap2‐2 mutant (Figure 6c).

To determine whether AP2 directly associates with the LAP1 promoter, multiple regions spanning the promoter were examined by ChIP‐qPCR (Figure S9). Interestingly, AP2‐1 was found to bind multiple sites in the LAP1 promoter, while AP2‐2 bound only two of the putative sites (Figure S9).

The promoter region bound by both AP2‐1 and AP2‐2 spans −1040 to −708 bp upstream of the ATG start codon (Figure 6d; Figure S9a).

ChIP‐qPCR analysis using primers flanking this region showed significant enrichment of both AP2‐1 and AP2‐2 (Figure 6e,f). This enrichment reflects direct binding of both proteins to the LAP1 promoter.

MIR172b‐overexpressing lines exhibited significantly reduced adventitious root formation, with all three independent lines producing fewer roots than wild‐type plants (Figure 7). These results indicate that elevated miR172 levels are associated with reduced adventitious root regeneration.

AP2 Modification Improves Regeneration Without Pleiotropic Developmental Effects

In addition to its effects on regeneration, AP2 manipulation did not produce adverse developmental phenotypes under our standard growth conditions. In fact, the AP2 overexpression lines displayed increased vegetative growth relative to wild type, whereas leaf morphology remained largely unchanged (Figure S10). Leaf shape and overall architecture were comparable across genotypes, indicating that elevated AP2 activity does not disrupt normal leaf development.

This is of significant practical value because many genetic regulators that enhance regeneration also cause pronounced developmental abnormalities, including altered organ morphology, growth defects, or disrupted developmental timing (Boutilier et al. 2002; Li et al. 2025). In contrast, enhanced AP2 activity improved regeneration competence without evident pleiotropic penalties under the conditions examined.

These observations indicate that manipulation of the miR172‐AP2 module may provide a practical strategy to improve regeneration efficiency while maintaining normal developmental performance.

Discussion

The miR172‐AP2 Module Links Developmental Phase Transition to Regeneration Competence

Plants retain a remarkable capacity for regeneration, yet this ability declines with developmental age, particularly in woody perennials such as Populus. Developmental timing is a fundamental determinant of plant form and plasticity, coordinating phase transitions with environmental and endogenous signals (Coen and Prusinkiewicz 2024). Previous studies have established that age‐dependent regeneration is largely controlled by the miR156‐SPL pathway (Zhang et al. 2015). Here, we identify the miR172‐AP2 module as a new regulatory layer linking developmental phase identity to regeneration competence. The progressive decline in regeneration capacity along the leaf gradient correlates with increased miR172 accumulation and reduced AP2 expression (Figure 1), indicating that phase transition pathways extend beyond miR156‐SPL to include the miR172‐AP2 module.

Functional analyses show that AP2 promotes regeneration capacity. Overexpression of AP2 enhances shoot regeneration and restores regeneration in mature tissues, whereas loss‐of‐function mutants exhibit reduced regeneration efficiency (Figure 3; Figures S2 and S3). These results are consistent with the concept that regeneration competence can be reprogrammed by key transcriptional regulators (Chen et al. 2024; Ikeuchi et al. 2016).

AP2 Functions During Early Competence Acquisition in Coordination With Hormone and Wound‐Associated Signals

Cytokinin signalling modulates AP2 expression in a context‐dependent manner. Although cytokinin is required for shoot induction, its promotive effect depends on a defined concentration range, and excessive cytokinin can disrupt normal organogenic programmes (Gordon et al. 2009; Liu et al. 2020). Consistent with this, AP2 transcript levels are reduced in cytokinin‐enhanced lines (Figure S6). Cytokinin has been shown to regulate vegetative phase transition through the miR172 pathway (Werner et al. 2021) and to interact with gibberellin signalling to coordinate developmental timing (Werner et al. 2025). In agreement with this framework, elevated cytokinin signalling is associated with increased miR172 accumulation and reduced AP2 expression in our system. These observations support a model in which cytokinin signalling, at least in part through miR172, contributes to AP2 repression and thereby modulates regeneration competence, positioning AP2 within a regulatory framework that links phase transition to regenerative capacity.

The temporal separation between competence acquisition and organogenic commitment provides a framework to understand the role of the miR172‐AP2 module in the context of cytokinin signalling. Plant regeneration proceeds through sequential stages, beginning with acquisition of competence followed by activation of organogenic programmes (Ikeuchi et al. 2016; Kareem et al. 2015). Recent work has further emphasized that de novo shoot regeneration is a multicellular process involving coordinated cellular reprogramming and spatial pattern formation leading to meristem establishment (Doll and Ikeuchi 2025). Within this framework, AP2 functions during early stages to establish a regeneration‐permissive state, whereas cytokinin signalling subsequently drives shoot fate specification. This temporal organization explains how regulators of competence and differentiation can operate independently yet sequentially during regeneration.

GO enrichment analysis further indicates that AP2‐associated transcriptional changes are linked to stress‐responsive processes, including wounding, water deprivation and oxidative stress (Table S3). These processes are characteristic of early regeneration stages, during which wound‐induced signalling and environmental cues contribute to cellular reprogramming (Ikeuchi et al. 2016). Wounding has been established as a key trigger for regeneration, initiating transcriptional reprogramming through wound‐responsive regulators and hormone‐mediated pathways (Iwase et al. 2011; Zhang et al. 2019). In particular, the AP2/ERF transcription factor WIND1 promotes wound‐induced dedifferentiation, while jasmonate signalling further enhances regeneration competence following tissue damage (Iwase et al. 2011; Zhang et al. 2019). The enrichment of wounding‐related processes among AP2‐associated genes is therefore consistent with activation of early wound signalling programmes during regeneration competence acquisition. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) associated with oxidative stress response are established signalling components during early regeneration (Mase and Tsukagoshi 2021; Tsukagoshi et al. 2010). ROS signals can act upstream of or in parallel with auxin during de novo organogenesis and root developmental programmes (Liu et al. 2024; Manzano et al. 2014; Roy et al. 2025; Shin et al. 2022), indicating that early regeneration competence is governed by the integration of hormonal and ROS‐associated signalling pathways. The enrichment of redox‐related genes, including RBOHD (Morales et al. 2016; Roy et al. 2025), among AP2‐responsive targets is consistent with this framework and places AP2 within a broader regulatory network linking developmental and stress‐associated cues. Local water availability further influences regeneration outcomes by modulating auxin response distribution (Kareem et al. 2025), providing environmental context for the AP2‐associated transcriptional programmes observed here.

AP2 Promotes Organ Regeneration Through Downstream Developmental Regulatory Modules

Our results demonstrate that AP2 promotes shoot regeneration through activation of downstream developmental regulators. AP2 and its downstream targets, including AIL1 and BL, enhance shoot formation (Figure 4), supporting a model in which AP2 functions upstream to activate transcriptional programmes that drive organogenic output. Both AIL1 and BL are well‐characterised regulators of organ growth and cell proliferation, and their established functions provide a mechanistic basis for their ability to promote shoot formation during regeneration (Byzova et al. 1999; Li et al. 2018; Mizukami and Fischer 2000). AIL1 and BL may therefore act as developmental effectors through which AP2 promotes shoot regeneration.

Plant regeneration proceeds through sequential stages, from the acquisition of regenerative competence to de novo meristem establishment and subsequent shoot outgrowth and development (Doll and Ikeuchi 2025; Ikeuchi et al. 2016; Shin et al. 2020). In our study, AP2‐overexpressing explants produced more visible shoots at an early stage (Figure S2), whereas shoot length did not differ significantly at this time point. At later stages, both shoot number and shoot length were significantly increased (Figures 3 and S3). Therefore, AP2 enhances the early appearance of regenerated shoots and promotes their subsequent outgrowth. However, measurements based on visible shoots cannot distinguish increased meristem initiation from earlier emergence of already initiated shoot primordia.

The effects of the miR172‐AP2 module extend beyond shoot regeneration. AP2 promotes adventitious rooting, whereas miR172 exerts an opposing effect (Figures 6 and 7), demonstrating that this module operates across distinct organogenic contexts. These concordant effects do not imply that shoot and root regeneration share identical hormonal or transcriptional mechanisms. Rather, our findings support a model in which the miR172‐AP2 module modulates the competence of tissues to respond to regeneration‐inducing signals, whereas the hormonal environment and organ‐specific regulatory networks determine the resulting organogenic outcome. This interpretation is consistent with previous studies showing that juvenile tissues generally exhibit greater competence for shoot and root regeneration (Xu et al. 2017; Ye et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2015). Under the cytokinin‐containing conditions of SIM, this regenerative capacity is directed towards shoot formation, whereas under the auxin‐containing conditions of RIM, it is directed towards adventitious rooting. Thus, a shared age‐dependent regulator of regenerative capacity can exert concordant effects on shoot and root regeneration without overriding the distinct pathways that specify each organ.

AP2 overexpression increased the number of adventitious roots without altering mean root length. This phenotype is particularly relevant in the context of age‐dependent rooting, as adventitious root regeneration declines with developmental age in woody plants and conserved developmental‐age pathways have been implicated in this process (Bannoud and Bellini 2021; Xu et al. 2017; Ye et al. 2020). More broadly, miRNA‐dependent regulation of adventitious organogenesis has been reported in diverse woody species. Stage‐specific miRNA expression occurs during adventitious shoot organogenesis in Acacia crassicarpa (Liu et al. 2014), whereas functional studies have identified the miR160a‐ARF10 module in Acer rubrum and the miR167a‐ARF8, miR476a‐RFL and miR159a‐PeMYB33 modules in Populus as regulators of adventitious rooting through distinct developmental and hormonal pathways (Cai et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2021; Zhao et al. 2024; Zhu et al. 2022). Recent miRNA profiling in peach has further implicated miR319 and miR172 in adventitious root development (Wang, Shen, et al. 2025). The miR172‐AP2 module therefore represents one of several miRNA‐controlled pathways involved in adventitious organogenesis in woody plants. The opposing effects of AP2 and MIR172b overexpression on adventitious rooting raise the possibility that the ageing‐miR172‐AP2 framework also contributes to age‐dependent rooting competence in woody plants.

At the mechanistic level, AP2 directly activates LAP1, as well as AIL1 and BL, forming a transcriptional network associated with growth and developmental transitions. Consistent with previous studies showing that AIL1 promotes organ development and that LAP1 acts upstream of AIL1 (Azeez et al. 2014; Rigal et al. 2012), these results support a hierarchical regulatory model in which AP2 establishes a regeneration‐permissive state, whereas its downstream targets promote meristem establishment, cell proliferation and organ growth at subsequent stages of organogenic progression. Regeneration may therefore depend on coordinated transcriptional modules that integrate developmental state with hormonal signalling.

A miR172‐AP2‐WUS regulatory relationship has been reported during somatic embryogenesis in Arabidopsis, where AP2 was proposed to repress WUS expression through an HDA6/HDA19‐associated histone deacetylation mechanism (Nowak et al. 2022). Populus contains two WUS homologues, WUS1 and WUS2. In our RNA‐seq dataset collected after 7 days on SIM, WUS1 expression was reduced in the ap2 single and double mutants but was not increased in the AP2‐overexpression lines, whereas WUS2 showed low expression and no consistent genotype‐dependent response (Figure S11). Neither WUS1 nor WUS2 was present in the RNA‐seq and ChIP‐seq overlap shown in Table S3. This pattern is more consistent with an indirect association between AP2 activity and WUS1 expression than with direct transcriptional regulation. The relationship observed in Populus therefore differs from the negative AP2‐WUS relationship reported during Arabidopsis somatic embryogenesis and may reflect differences between species or developmental contexts.

Collectively, our findings identify the miR172‐AP2 module as a regulatory node linking developmental phase transition, hormonal signalling and regeneration competence. Within this framework, AP2 defines a regeneration‐permissive state whose effect depends on regeneration context, thereby generating distinct developmental outcomes.

Biotechnological Potential of AP2‐Mediated Regeneration Enhancement

A notable feature of AP2‐mediated enhancement of regeneration is the absence of obvious developmental trade‐offs under standard growth conditions. Many regulators that improve regeneration, particularly developmental transcription factors, frequently cause pleiotropic effects such as altered organ morphology, growth defects, or disruption of developmental timing (Boutilier et al. 2002; Li et al. 2025), which limits their practical utility. In contrast, AP2 overexpression increased regenerative capacity while maintaining normal leaf, stem and root morphology and was additionally associated with enhanced vegetative growth. Interestingly, this growth enhancement was not observed in lines expressing an AP2 variant with a modified miR172 target site (Wang, Liao, et al. 2025), suggesting that preservation of native miR172‐dependent regulation may contribute to maintaining balanced developmental output. This observation raises the possibility that endogenous post‐transcriptional control helps buffer AP2 activity, allowing improved regeneration without broadly perturbing growth and development. More broadly, the conservation of the miR172‐AP2 regulatory axis across plant lineages (Ding et al. 2022; Zhu and Helliwell 2011) highlights the potential of this module as a target for improving regeneration competence in recalcitrant plant species.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study establishes the miR172‐AP2 module as a central regulatory node linking developmental phase transitions, hormonal signalling and regenerative competence in plants. AP2 functions as a key determinant of a regeneration‐permissive state by directly activating downstream transcriptional programmes that control both shoot and root formation. The progressive decline in regenerative capacity with age is therefore not merely a consequence of tissue maturation but reflects the coordinated action of conserved molecular regulators. Importantly, enhanced regeneration through AP2 manipulation occurs without obvious developmental abnormalities under standard growth conditions, highlighting the potential practical value of this regulatory module. Given the widespread conservation of the miR172‐AP2 module in regulating phase transition across plant species (Poethig and Fouracre 2024), our findings suggest a potentially generalizable principle governing phase‐dependent regeneration. Targeted manipulation of this regulatory module offers a promising avenue to enhance regeneration competence in diverse species, facilitating clonal propagation, tissue culture, and biotechnological improvement in both model and crop plants. Together, these findings support a model in which developmental phase identity and hormone‐associated signals converge on the miR172‐AP2 module to define regeneration competence in Populus (Figure 8).

Model for miR172‐AP2‐mediated control of regeneration competence in Populus. During the juvenile phase, low miR172 levels permit high AP2 expression, thereby maintaining a regeneration‐permissive state. AP2 activates downstream transcriptional programmes, including AIL1, BL and LAP1, to promote shoot and root regeneration. During maturation, and under elevated cytokinin signalling, miR172 accumulates and is associated with reduced AP2 expression, leading to diminished regeneration competence. Together, developmental phase identity and hormone‐associated signals converge on the miR172‐AP2 module to modulate regeneration capacity in Populus.

Experimental Procedures

Generation of Plasmid Constructs and Transformation

The full‐length coding sequences (CDS) of AP2‐1 and AP2‐2 were PCR‐amplified and cloned into the entry vector pDONR‐ZEO using the Gateway BP Clonase II enzyme mix (Invitrogen). The resulting entry clones were recombined into the binary destination vector pMDC32 using the Gateway LR Clonase II enzyme mix (Invitrogen) to generate the overexpression constructs AP2‐1‐OE and AP2‐2‐OE.

For MIR172b overexpression, the genomic fragment containing the MIR172b precursor sequence was PCR‐amplified and cloned into pDONR‐ZEO, followed by recombination into pMDC32 using the Gateway LR Clonase II enzyme mix (Invitrogen).

For CRISPR/Cas9‐mediated gene knockout, two single‐guide RNAs (sgRNAs) targeting each gene were designed and cloned into the pYLCRISPR/Cas9Pubi‐H binary vector following the method as previously described (Ma et al. 2015). The AP2 overexpression and CRISPR/Cas9 constructs were introduced into the hybrid poplar line 717‐FT ( Populus tremula × Populus alba ) (Azeez and Busov 2019) via Agrobacterium tumefaciens ‐mediated transformation. Primer sequences used for vector construction and sgRNA cloning are listed in Table S4.

Shoot and Root Regeneration Assays

Fully expanded leaves were excised from plants grown under in vitro culture conditions. Leaves were cut into halves, and the half containing the petiole region was used as the explant. Explants were cultured on shoot induction medium (SIM) containing 0.5 mg L−1 6‐benzylaminopurine (6‐BA), 0.05 mg L−1 α‐naphthaleneacetic acid (NAA) and 3% (w/v) sucrose. The pH of the medium was adjusted to 5.8 before autoclaving. Data represent three biological replicates, each consisting of 7–10 explants.

For the shoot‐length analysis at 4 weeks, all visible regenerated shoots were measured to assess early shoot growth. At 7 and 10 weeks, the eight longest regenerated shoots within each biological replicate were measured to evaluate the subsequent outgrowth of established shoots.

For root regeneration assays, explants were transferred to root induction medium (RIM) supplemented with 0.1 mg L−1 indole‐3‐butyric acid (IBA) and 2% (w/v) sucrose (pH 5.8). Root regeneration was assessed after 7 days by counting the number of adventitious roots per plant. All cultures were maintained at 25°C under long‐day conditions (16 h light/8 h dark).

RNA Isolation and Reverse Transcription Quantitative PCR Analysis

Total RNA was extracted from plant tissues using a CTAB‐based protocol as previously described (Gambino et al. 2008). Approximately 100 mg of tissue was ground in liquid nitrogen, and RNA was isolated according to the published procedure. First‐strand cDNA was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using the SuperScript II Reverse Transcriptase kit (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer's instructions. Reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT‐qPCR) was performed using the StepOnePlus Real‐Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) with Power SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−∆∆Ct method (Livak and Schmittgen 2001). Primer sequences used for RT‐qPCR are listed in Table S4.

For miRNA quantification, stem‐loop reverse transcription was performed using miRNA‐specific stem‐loop primers, followed by quantitative PCR as previously described. U6 was used as an internal control for normalization. The relative abundance of mature miR172b was calculated using the 2−∆∆Ct method. Primers for U6 and miR172 were designed based on previously published sequences (Wang, Liao, et al. 2025), while primers specific for miR172b are listed in Table S4.

Bioinformatics Analysis of RNA‐Seq

The RNA‐seq data were aligned to the Populus tremula × Populus alba HAP1 (v5.1) genome using HISAT2 (Kim et al. 2019). Transcript assembly and quantification were performed with StringTie (Pertea et al. 2015) (using the ‐e ‐B options), and differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 (Love et al. 2014) with the Trinity DifferentialExpression script run_DE_analysis.pl (Haas et al. 2013). Genes with |log2(fold change)| > 0.5 and an adjusted p‐value (padj) < 0.05 were considered differentially expressed.

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP) Assay

The ChIP assay was performed following the protocol described in Nature Protocols (Li et al. 2014) with modifications. Briefly, fresh young leaf tissues were cross‐linked with 1% formaldehyde, and nuclei were isolated using sucrose density gradient centrifugation. Chromatin was extracted and sonicated to generate DNA fragments of approximately 200–500 bp.

Immunoprecipitation was performed by incubating the chromatin with an anti‐His antibody (Abcam), followed by the addition of protein A/G agarose beads. After reversal of cross‐links, DNA was purified by phenol‐chloroform extraction and ethanol precipitation. Enrichment of target DNA fragments was analysed by ChIP‐qPCR. Primer sequences used for ChIP‐qPCR are listed in Table S4.

Statistical Analysis

All experiments were performed with three independent biological replicates. Data are presented as mean ± standard error (SE). Statistical significance between two groups was determined using a two‐tailed t‐test. Asterisks indicate statistical significance (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01).

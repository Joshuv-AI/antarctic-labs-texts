Maximum Size t t -Intersecting Families and Anticodes
Xuan Wang, Tuvi Etzion, Denis S. Krotov, Minjia Shi

Abstract
The maximum size of t t -intersecting families is one of the most celebrated topics in combinatorics, and its size is known as the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem. Such intersecting families, also known as constant-weight anticodes in coding theory, were considered in a generalization of the well-known sphere-packing bound. In this work we consider the maximum size of t t -intersecting families and their associated maximum size constant-weight anticodes over alphabet of size q > 2 q>2 . It is proved that the structure of the maximum size constant-weight anticodes with the same length, weight, and diameter, depends on the alphabet size. This structure implies some hierarchy of constant-weight anticodes.



Keywords:
intersecting families, constant-weight anticodes.

In this section we show asymptotic maximality of one

\left|{\cal C}\right|\leq\binom{n-t-1}{k-t-1}
for
n\geq(t+1)(k-t+1)
, it follows
that
\left|{\cal B}\right|\leq\binom{n-t-1}{k-t-1}(q-1)^{k-t}=\left|{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)\right|
for large enough
n
.

Case 2:
If
{\cal C}_{2}\neq\varnothing
then
consider any
{\mathbf{u}}\in{\cal C}_{2}
. Each codeword of
{\cal C}
intersects
{\mathbf{u}}

in at least
t+2
coordinates. Hence, we have that

\left|{\cal C}\right|\leq\sum_{i=t+2}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{k-i}~.

As in Case 1 we have that each codeword of
{\cal C}_{1}
is a support for at most
(q-1)^{k-t}
codewords of
{\cal B}
. A codeword of
{\cal C}_{2}

can support at most
(q-1)^{k}
codewords of
{\cal B}
since there are
q-1

Hence, a codeword in
{\cal C}
can support at most
(q-1)^{k}

{\cal B}
.
This implies that

\left|{\cal B}\right|\leq(q-1)^{k}\sum_{i=t+2}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{k-i}.

(2)

\sum_{i=t+2}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{k-i}
is a polynomial
of degree

in

\left|{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)\right|=\binom{n-t-1}{k-t-1}(q-1)^{k-t}
is
a polynomial in
n
whose degree is
k-t-1
. Hence (
2

for large enough

{\cal B}
is smaller than
{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)
.

In both cases, we have proved that
\left|{\cal B}\right|\leq\left|{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)\right|
, i.e.,
{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)
is a maximum size anticode
with diameter
2(k-t)-1
for large enough
n
.
∎

The anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)
is not a
t
-intersecting family of maximum size. The proof of Theorem
7
fails
in this case since
{\cal C}
is a
(t+1)
-intersecting family and a

-intersecting family can be larger than
{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)
.
Indeed, there exists such a
t
-intersecting family, which is
{\cal F}_{q}(t,k,n)
.

We can present aa value of
n
in Theorem
7
such that the theorem is true for all values
above this

. The value should imply that
n
is large enough for Case 2 or in another words

(q-1)^{k}\sum_{i=t+2}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{k-i}<\binom{n-t-1}{k-t-1}(q-1)^{k-t}~.

This implies that it is enough to require
n>(q-1)^{t}(k-t-1)^{2}\binom{k}{k/2}+2k-t-2
in Theorem

.

Now, we define
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)\triangleq{\cal F}_{q}(t,k,n)
, i.e.,
{\cal F}_{q}(t,k,n)
is defined as an anticode.

Lemma 3
.

If
n\geq 2k-t
then
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
is an
(n,2(k-t),k)_{q}
anticode
with
\binom{n-t}{k-t}(q-1)^{k-t}
codewords.

Proof.

Since
n\geq 2k-t
and the weight in the last
n-t
coordinates is at most
k-t
, it follows that there exist
two codewords in
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
whose nonzero entries in the last
n-t
coordinates are in disjoint coordinates.
For two such codewords the distance is
2(k-t)
which is the diameter of the anticode.
The size of
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
follows immediately by choosing the
k-t
nonzero coordinates, each one
can be assigned with
q-1
possible values (see also Lemma
1
).
∎

It will be proved now that similarly to
{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)
also
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
is a maximum size anticode if
n
is large enough.
The proof will be similar to the one of Theorem
7
.

Theorem 8
.

If
n
is large enough, then
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
is a maximum size anticode for given
q>2
,
k>t\geq 0
, and
diameter
2(k-t)
.

Proof.

Let
{\cal B}
be an
(n,2(k-t),k)_{q}
anticode and let
{\cal C}
be the set of supports of the codewords in
{\cal B}
, i.e.,

{\cal C}\triangleq\{{\mathsf{supp}}({\mathbf{x}})~:~{\mathbf{x}}\in{\cal B}\}~.

The code
{\cal C}
forms a
t
-intersecting family since the diameter
2(k-t)
implies that each two codewords of
{\cal B}
must have
at most
2(k-t)
positions in which one codeword has a nonzero element of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
and the second codeword has a
zero
.

We partition
{\cal C}
into two subsets

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbf{v}}\in{\cal C}~:~\left|{\mathbf{v}}\cap{\mathbf{u}}\right|=t~~\text{for~some}~~{\mathbf{u}}\in{\cal C}\},

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbf{v}}\in{\cal C}~:~\left|{\mathbf{v}}\cap{\mathbf{u}}\right|\geq t+1~~\text{for~all}~~{\mathbf{u}}\in{\cal C}\}.

We distinguish now between two cases depending whether
{\cal C}_{2}
is an empty subset or not.

Case 1:
If
{\cal C}_{2}=\varnothing
then
{\cal C}={\cal C}_{1}
and by Theorem
1
we have that
\left|{\cal C}\right|\leq\binom{n-k}{k-t}
.
Consider now two distinct codewords
{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}\in{\cal C}
whose intersection is of size
t
.
This already implies that their associated codewords in
{\cal B}
have distance
2(k-t)
and hence their value on the coordinates
of
{\mathbf{u}}\cap{\mathbf{v}}
are the same. This implies that
the number of codewords in
{\cal B}
whose support is
{\mathbf{u}}
is at most
(q-1)^{k-t}
.
Hence, the number of codewords in
{\cal B}
is at most
\binom{n-t}{k-t}(q-1)^{k-t}
, i.e.,
for large enough
n
we have that
{\cal B}
is smaller than
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
.

Case 2:
If
{\cal C}_{2}\neq\varnothing
then
consider any
{\mathbf{u}}\in{\cal C}_{2}
. Each codeword of
{\cal C}
intersect
{\mathbf{u}}

in at least
t+1
coordinates. Hence, we have that the size of
{\cal C}
is at most

\sum_{i=t+1}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{k-i}~.

As in the proof of Theorem
7
we have that each codeword of
{\cal C}
is a support for at most
(q-1)^{k}
codewords of
{\cal B}
.
This implies that

\left|{\cal B}\right|\leq(q-1)^{k}\sum_{i=t+1}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{k-i}.

(3)

\sum_{i=t+1}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{k-i}
is polynomial of degree
k-t-1
in
n
,
while
\left|{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)\right|=\binom{n-t}{k-t}(q-1)^{k-t}
is a polynomial in
n
whose degree is
k-t
. Hence (
3
) implies that
for large enough
n
we have that
{\cal B}
is smaller than
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
.

In both cases, we have proved that
\left|{\cal B}\right|\leq\left|{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)\right|
, i.e.,
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
is a maximum size anticode
with diameter
2(k-t)
for large enough
n
.
∎

Similarly to Theorem
7
we can show that for Theorem
8
it is enough to require that

n>(q-1)^{t}(k-t)^{2}\binom{k}{k/2}+2k-t-1
.

We continue to prove that the anticodes
{\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)
and
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
are not just optimal, if

is large enough then
they are unique for the fixed

,

, and

. We start with

Theorem 9
.

If
n
is large enough, then
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
is unique maximum size anticode of length
n
, constant-weight

and with diameter
2(k-t)

over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.

Proof.

Assume that in Theorem
8

\left|{\cal B}\right|=\left|{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)\right|
and

n
is large enough. This implies that
{\cal C}_{2}=\varnothing
,
{\cal C}={\cal C}_{1}
, and

\left|{\cal C}_{1}\right|=\binom{n-t}{k-t}
.
By the Erdös-Ko-Rado theorem (Theorem
1
), we know that
{\cal C}
consists
of all
k
-subsets that include a fixed
t
-subset of coordinates.
W.l.o.g., this
t
-subset is
\{1,\ldots,t\}
, and w.l.o.g.,

{\cal B}
contains the word
\mathbf{e}=(\overbrace{1\cdots 1}^{k~\text{times}}\overbrace{0\cdots\cdots 0}^{n-k~\text{times}})
.
Now, any other codeword in
{\cal B}
that has nonzero symbols in the first

t

.

The uniqueness of the anticode

A Hierarchy of Anticodes

In this section we define two sequences of anticodes,
where each sequence has one anticode which was proved to be optimal asymptotically.
It will be proved that any two anticodes in the sequence are incomparable, i.e.,
one is larger for one range of alphabet sizes and lengths of the codewords
and the second is larger for the other alphabet sizes and lengths of the codewords.
For comparing two anticodes we should have that the alphabet size
q
,
the length of the words
n
, their weight
k
, and their diameter
D
are the same for the two anticodes.

We start by defining the anticodes in these sequences. For
0\leq\epsilon\leq k-t
, let

{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)\triangleq\{(\overbrace{1\cdots\cdots 1}^{t~\text{times}}a_{1}\cdots a_{\epsilon}b_{1}\cdots b_{n-t-\epsilon})~:~a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}\setminus\{0\},~b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q},~{\mathsf{wt}}{(b_{1}\cdots b_{n-t-\epsilon})}=k-t-\epsilon\}

The codewords of the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
have three parts: part
\mathbb{A}
which consists of the first
t

ones
in the codeword,
part
\mathbb{C}
which consists of the last
n-t-\epsilon
entries in the codewords, and part
\mathbb{B}
which consists of the middle
\epsilon

entries in the codeword.

We note that
{\cal A}_{q}(t,1,k,n)={\cal A}_{q}(t,k,n)
and
{\cal A}_{q}(t,0,k,n)={\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
.
We have already proved in Theorem
7
and Theorem
8
, respectively that
these anticodes are maximum size anticodes for odd diameter
D=2(k-t)-1
and even diameter
D=2(k-t)
, respectively.

Lemma 4
.

If
n\geq 2k-t-\epsilon
, then the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
has diameter
2k-2t-\epsilon
.

Proof.

Consider the following two codewords of
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
,

{\mathbf{c}}=((\overbrace{1\cdots\cdots 1}^{t~\text{times}}\overbrace{1\cdots\cdots 1}^{\epsilon~\text{times}}\overbrace{1\cdots\cdots 1}^{k-t-\epsilon~\text{times}}\overbrace{0\cdots\cdots 0}^{n-k~\text{times}})

and

{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}=((\overbrace{1\cdots\cdots 1}^{t~\text{times}}\overbrace{2\cdots\cdots 2}^{\epsilon~\text{times}}\overbrace{0\cdots\cdots 0}^{n-k~\text{times}}\overbrace{1\cdots\cdots 1}^{k-t-\epsilon~\text{times}}).

Since
n\geq 2k-t-\epsilon
, i.e.,
n-t-\epsilon\geq 2(k-t-\epsilon)
, it follows that

{\mathsf{d}}({\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime})=\epsilon+2(k-t-\epsilon)=2k-2t-\epsilon.

These two codewords are of maximum distance in the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
.
Hence, the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
has diameter
2k-2t-\epsilon
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

The size of the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
is
\binom{n-t-\epsilon}{k-t-\epsilon}(q-1)^{k-t}
.

Proof.

The number of possible combinations in part
\mathbb{B}
is
(q-1)^{\epsilon}
and the number of the combinations in part
\mathbb{C}
is

\binom{n-t-\epsilon}{k-t-\epsilon}(q-1)^{k-t-\epsilon}
, which implies the claim in the lemma.
∎

Since the diameter
D
of the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
is
2k-2t-\epsilon
, it follows that this diameter
can be even or odd, depending whether
\epsilon
is even or odd, respectively.
For
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
we have that the diameter is
D=2k-2t-\epsilon
and this anticode
will be compared with the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},k,n)
,
where
\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon+2
,
t^{\prime}=t-1
, and hence its diameter is
D^{\prime}=D
.

Lemma 6
.

When
n\geq 2k-t-\epsilon
, the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},k,n)={\cal A}_{q}(t-1,\epsilon+2,k,n)
is larger than
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
if and only if

q>\frac{n+k-2t-2\epsilon}{k-t-\epsilon}
or equivalently
n<(q-2)(k-t-\epsilon)+k
(both anticodes have diameter
2k-2t-\epsilon
).

Proof.

By Lemma
4
the diameter of the two anticodes is
2k-2t-\epsilon
.
By Lemma
5
the size of
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
is
\binom{n-t-\epsilon}{k-t-\epsilon}(q-1)^{k-t}
and the size of

{\cal A}_{q}(t^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},k,n)
is
\binom{n-t-\epsilon-1}{k-t-\epsilon-1}(q-1)^{k-t+1}
. Therefore,
{\cal A}_{q}(t^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},k,n)
is larger than

{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
if and only if

\binom{n-t-\epsilon-1}{k-t-\epsilon-1}(q-1)^{k-t+1}>\binom{n-t-\epsilon}{k-t-\epsilon}(q-1)^{k-t}

which is equivalent to

q-1>\frac{n-t-\epsilon}{k-t-\epsilon}

or

n<(q-2)(k-t-\epsilon)+k~.

∎

Lemma
6
implies that we can have two sequences of incomparable anticodes for given

t
,
k
, and
D=2(k-t)
. For any given
q
if
n
is large enough then
{\cal A}_{q}(t,0,k,n)
is the maximum size
anticode. However, if
q>\frac{n+k-2t}{k-t}
, then the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t-1,2,k,n)
is larger
than
{\cal A}_{q}(t,0,k,n)
. If
q>\frac{n+k-2(t-1)-2}{k-(t-1)-1}
, then the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t-2,4,k,n)
is larger
than
{\cal A}_{q}(t-1,2,k,n)
and as a result also larger than the anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,0,k,n)
.
We can continue and obtain a sequence of
\text{minimum}\{t+1,k-t+1\}
anticodes (the minimum is due to the fact
that
k-t-\epsilon
cannot be negative),
where the first anticode is of maximum size if
n
is large enough.
If
n
is fixed, then from a certain alphabet size the second anticode is larger.
Similar hierarchy can be defined for odd diameter and the anticodes
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
, where
\epsilon
is odd.
The anticode
{\cal A}_{q}(t,1,k,n)
is asymptotically of maximum size and each two anticodes in this hierarchy are incomparable.

4
Conclusion, Discussion, and Future Research

This paper considers maximum size anticodes and maximum size
t
-intersecting families over a non-binary alphabet.
Maximality and uniqueness of such anticodes was proved and hierarchy between anticodes
was given. Such different anticodes were discussed also in other papers as follows.

The intersecting family
{\cal F}_{q}(t,k,n)
which was proved to be a maximum size
(t,k)_{q}
-intersecting family
if
n\geq 2k-t
is large enough (and it was referred to as
{\cal A}^{\prime}_{q}(t,k,n)
or
{\cal A}_{q}(t,0,k,n)
) was already
defined in
[
9
]
, where it was proved to be a maximum size anticode if a certain structure called a generalized Steiner
system with appropriate parameters exists.

There are other anticodes which were proven to be of maximum size in
[
9
]
. For
1\leq\epsilon\leq k
, the anticode

\{({\mathbf{b}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{b}}_{\epsilon}\overbrace{1\cdots\cdots 1}^{{t=k-\epsilon}~\text{times}}\overbrace{0\cdots\cdots 0}^{{n-k}~\text{times}})~:~{\mathbf{b}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}\setminus\{0\},~1\leq i\leq\epsilon\}.

This anticode is exactly
{\cal A}_{q}(t,\epsilon,k,n)
, where
t=k-\epsilon
, in our hierarchy.
It is a maximum size anticode when certain structures exist (see
[
9
]
for more details). These structures exist
for many parameters. This anticode is an
(n,\epsilon,k)
anticode with
(q-1)^{\epsilon}
codewords.

Two more anticodes, which are of maximum size for certain parameters, were defined in
[
15
]
. The first one

\{({\mathbf{a}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{a}}_{\epsilon}{\mathbf{b}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{b}}_{n-\epsilon})~:~{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}\setminus\{0\},~\text{wt}({{\mathbf{b}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{b}}_{n-\epsilon}})=k-\epsilon\}

This anticode is exactly
{\cal A}_{q}(0,\epsilon,k,n)
in our hierarchy.
It is an
(n,2k-t,k)

\binom{n-t}{k-t}(q-1)^{k}

if
n\geq(k-t+1)(t+1)
and
q
is large enough.

The second one

\{(\overbrace{0\cdots\cdots 0}^{t~\text{times}}{\mathbf{b}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{b}}_{n-t})~:~\text{wt}({{\mathbf{b}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{b}}_{n-t}})=k\}

is an
(n,n-t,k)
anticode with
\binom{n-t}{k}(q-1)^{k}
codewords. This anticode is of maximum size if
n\leq(k+t-1)(t+1)/t

and
q
is large enough.

As we see, there are many maximum size anticodes with similar parameters and other large anticodes which we did not prove
their maximality. Each maximum size anticode is of maximum size in different parameters and there are other large
anticodes in different lengths and alphabet size which cannot be compared (each one is larger in different parameters).
Some comparisons and hierarchy between the anticodes were given in this paper and there are more comparisons in
[
9
]
,
where also the uniqueness of other anticodes for some given parameters is discussed.
As for future research we would like to prove the maximality of all the anticodes in the hierarchies,
We also would like to know whether such hierarchies exist also for intersecting families.

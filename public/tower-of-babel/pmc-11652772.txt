Protocol for whole-cell patch-clamp assay for HBV-mediated sodium influx in THP-1 macrophages
Authors: Yingbao Tan, Penghui Chen, Xiaojuan Xin, Jingxue Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummarySodium influx induced by hepatitis B virus (HBV) enhances activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, in synergy with potassium efflux signals in macrophages. Here, we present a protocol for conducting patch-clamp assays to measure HBV-mediated sodium influx in THP-1 macrophages. We describe steps for differentiating, resting, and activating THP-1 macrophages and then outline the procedures for whole-cell patch-clamp recordings.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Wang et al.1Subject areas: Biophysics, Cell Biology, Cell culture, Cell-based Assays, Immunology

Expected outcomes

In patch-clamp recording with pipette solution 1, which mimics an artificially intracellular high sodium environment, exposure to HBV resulted in an outward current within 5 min that persists for longer than 100 ms in THP-1 macrophages (Figure 4A). It implies that HBV exposure interfere with sodium ion traffic. When filling the glass electrode with pipette solution 2, occasional inward current can be observed at the same electrophysiological parameters in THP-1 macrophages upon HBV exposure (Figure 4B).

In patch-clamp recording with pipette solution 2, we compare and analyze sodium currents triggered by a −20-mV pulse in THP-1 macrophages. The exposure to HBV increases the sodium current considerably in the macrophages, whereas DPH drug attenuates HBV-mediated enhancement of sodium currents (Figure 6, also described in a previous paper; 1).

Limitations

Immune cells, particularly macrophages, typically modulated their expression of membrane molecules in response to various environmental factors. The factors that act on immune cells are usually not transient, and immune cells also fail to promptly revert to their original state at the transcriptional and translational levels within minutes upon withdrawal from these environmental factors. However, in the present, we are unable to conduct the patch-clamp experiment in environmental factor-controlled setting, potentially leading to inconsistent and heterogeneous data. Consequently, we refrained from utilizing the traditional patch-clamp setup with a fluid flow system in the experiment with pipette solution 2, aiming to mitigate the influence of these environmental factors on macrophages.

Moreover, the experience gained from neuronal cells may not be seamlessly applicable to immune cells, due to different electrophysiological parameters between neuronal cells and immune cells. For instance, the resting membrane potential in macrophages differ significantly from that observed in neuronal cells,4 likely attributed to the unique ion channels expressed by immune cells, in contrast to those in neuronal cells. Despite the limited experience in utilizing patch-clamp techniques to investigate immune cells, the ever-evolving understanding of immune regulation mediated by ion channels suggests that a growing number of immunological researchers will embark on exploring this field with patch-clamp techniques in the future.

Potential solution

•Check the bath electrode, and ensure that the electrode is positioned beneath the surface level of the extracellular solution and thoroughly inspect the ground wire.

•Reduce the perfusion rate of the extracellular solution to minimize any fluctuations in the fluid height within the detection chamber.

Problem 3

The outward current cannot be observed in experiment with pipette solution 1.

Potential solution

The state of the cells may be crucial for successfully detecting outward currents. It is often observed that within the same batch of cells, outward currents can be easily detected, whereas in a different batch, no outward currents can be detected. Healthy THP-1 macrophages are necessary to perform the experiment successfully. The detectability of outward current in THP-1 cells appears also to be significantly influenced by multiple factors, including LPS treatment, the duration of LPS treatment, and the length of the resting period subsequent to PMA-induced differentiation.

Braidings on topological operators, anomaly of higher-form symmetries and the SymTFT
and Rajath Radhakrishnan

Abstract
The anomaly of non-invertible higher-form symmetries is determined by the braiding of topological operators implementing them. In this paper, we study a method to classify braidings on topological line and surface operators by leveraging the fact that topological operators which admit a braiding are symmetries of their associated SymTFT. This perspective allows us to formulate an algorithm to explicitly compute all possible braidings on a given fusion category, bypassing the need to solve the hexagon equations. Additionally, using 3+1d SymTFTs, we determine braidings on various fusion 2-categories. We prove a necessary and sufficient condition for the fusion 2-categories Σ ​ 𝒞 \Sigma\mathcal{C} , 2Vec π G {}_{G}^{\pi} and Tambara-Yamagami (TY) 2-categories TY ( A , π ) (A,\pi) to admit a braiding.

2.3
Classifying braidings on topological line operators

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category. Let us assume that
\mathcal{C}
admits a braiding. Recall that we have an embedding of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{Z})
given by the map
\iota
in (
2.2
). Consider the subcategory
\iota(\mathcal{C})
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
. It is clear that
\mathcal{C}
has the following properties

\text{dim}(\iota(\mathcal{C}))=\text{dim}(\mathcal{C}),~\text{ and }F(\iota(\mathcal{C}))=\mathcal{C}~.

(2.18)

where dim
(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{a}d_{a}^{2}
. This is true for any choice of braiding on
\mathcal{C}
. Conversely, let
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
be the SymTFT of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
with the following properties

\text{dim}(\mathcal{D})=\text{dim}(\mathcal{C})~,\text{ and }O(\mathcal{D})\cap O(L_{\mathcal{C}})=\{\mathbf{1}\}~.

(2.19)

where
O(\mathcal{D})
is the set of simple line operators of
\mathcal{D}
and
O(L_{\mathcal{C}})
is the set of simple line operators in the decomposition of
L_{\mathcal{C}}
into simple line operators. The second condition above denotes that there are no non-trivial line operators common to both
\mathcal{D}
and the canonical Lagrangian algebra
L_{\mathcal{C}}
. In
[
46
]
, it was shown that these conditions imply

F(\mathcal{D})\cong\mathcal{C}

(2.20)

as fusion categories. Indeed,
\text{dim}(\mathcal{D})=\text{dim}(\mathcal{C})
implies that
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is surjective and
O(\mathcal{D})\cap O(L_{\mathcal{C}})=\{\mathbf{1}\}
implies that it is injective. This shows that any
\mathcal{D}
which satisfies (
2.19
) is the image of an embedding
\iota
of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
. Moreover,
F\circ\iota
is the identity map of
\mathcal{C}
.

Since
\mathcal{D}
is a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
, it is braided. Therefore,
F
equips
\mathcal{C}
with a braiding given by the braiding of line operators in
\mathcal{D}
. If there is no
\mathcal{D}
which satisfies the conditions in (
2.19
), then
\mathcal{C}
does not admit a braiding. Note that classifying such fusion subcategories
\mathcal{D}
only requires the knowledge of the fusion ring of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
and the Lagrangian algebra object
L_{\mathcal{C}}
. Crucially, we do not need to know the
F
and
R
matrices of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
or the explicit multiplication of the algebra
L_{\mathcal{C}}
. This provides a simple method to count the number of distinct braidings on a fusion category. Indeed,
[
46
]
contains explicit expressions parametrizing the embedding of
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
for various fusion categories.

If
\mathcal{C}
admits some braiding, to determine it explicitly, we need to compute the
R
matrices of
\mathcal{C}
in some basis. This is also the
R
matrices for line operators in
\mathcal{D}
. The latter can be comptuted using the remarkable result in
[
36
]
, that the
R
matrices of a modular category is completely determined by its modular data! Let
x,y,z,\dots
be the simple line operators in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
. Consider an arbitrary complete ordering on the simple objects. There exists a basis in which the
R
matrices of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
are given by

R_{xy}^{z}=\begin{cases}I_{N_{xy}^{z}}&\mbox{if }x>y~,\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{\theta_{z}}{\theta_{x}\theta_{y}}I_{N_{xy}^{z}}&\mbox{if }x<y~,\\
\frac{\sqrt{\theta_{z}}}{\theta_{x}}\Delta_{x}^{z}&\mbox{if }x=y~.\end{cases}

(2.21)

where
T_{xy}=\delta_{x,y}\theta_{x}
,
\Delta_{x}^{z}
is a

\Delta_{x}^{z}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{d_{+}}&\hskip-2.0pt\vline\hskip-2.0pt&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip-2.0pt\vline\hskip-2.0pt&-I_{d_{-}}\end{pmatrix}~,

(2.22)

and

is the identity matrix of integer

d_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(N_{xx}^{z}\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\theta_{z}}\text{dim}(\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C}))}\sum_{v,w\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}d_{v}N_{vw}^{x}\bar{S}_{z,w}\frac{\theta_{v}^{2}}{\theta_{w}^{2}}\bigg)~,

(2.23)

In summary, the classification of braidings (if any) on
\mathcal{C}
proceeds as follows:

1.

Find the modular data of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
and the canonical Lagrangian algebra
L_{\mathcal{C}}
of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
corresponding to
\mathcal{C}
following the discussion in section
2.2
.

2.

Let
\Sigma
be the set of fusion subcategories
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
satisfying

\text{dim}(\mathcal{D})=\text{dim}(\mathcal{C})~,\text{ and }\mathcal{D}\cap L_{\mathcal{C}}=\{\mathbf{1}\}~,

(2.24)

If
\Sigma
is empty, then
\mathcal{C}
does not admit any braiding. Each elemnt of
\Sigma
corresponds to a braiding on
\mathcal{C}
and all braidings on
\mathcal{C}
are captured in this way.

3.

For every
\mathcal{D}\in\Sigma
, we get a braiding on
\mathcal{C}
with the explicit
R
matrices given by

R|_{\mathcal{D}}

(2.25)

where
R
is the braiding matrix of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
which can be computed using the modular data of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
and equations (
2.21
),(
2.23
).

Note that the procedure above only requires the modular data of the SymTFT
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
, which can in turn be determined from the
F
symbols of the fusion category. Determining the modular data of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
is particularly simple if there exists a Morita equivalent fusion category
\mathcal{B}
for which the modular data of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B})

(2.26)

using which we can determine the braidings on
\mathcal{C}

Once we choose a braiding on
\mathcal{C}

4
Braidings on topological surfaces and lines from 3+1d SymTFT

In spacetime dimensions higher than two, along with topological lines, we also have topological surface operators. The properties of topological surfaces and lines in 2+1d are believed to be captured by a fusion 2-category. Similar to our analysis in lower dimensions, we can ask whether a fusion 2-category can be a symmetry of a 3+1d QFT. Surface and lines can braid in 3+1d, therefore a 2+1d fusion category can be a symmetry of 3+1d QFT only if it can be promoted to a braided fusion 2-category. To determine braidings on a fusion 2-category, it will be crucial to understand the structure of 3+1d SymTFTs.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a fusion 2-category of symmetries of a 2+1D QFT. The line operators in
\mathfrak{C}
form a braided fusion category and will be denoted as
\Omega\mathfrak{C}
. Among the surface operators in
\mathfrak{C}
some are condensation defects of these line operators.
\Sigma\Omega\mathfrak{C}
will denote the fusion sub-2-category of surface operators in
\mathfrak{C}
obtained from higher-gauging the line operators in
\Omega\mathfrak{C}
. All condensation defects admit at least one topological gapped boundary. In other words, there is at least one non-trivial interface between any condensation surface operator and the trivial surface operator. More generally, we can group surfaces into equivalence classes based on whether they admit a topological junction between them or not. These are called components of the fusion 2-category, denoted as
\mathfrak{C}_{0}
. Therefore, the number of surface operators up to condensation is
|\mathfrak{C}_{0}|
.

The SymTFT of
\mathfrak{C}
is given by the Drinfeld centre
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
defined in
[
60
, Section 3]
. (See also
[
61
]
) Similar to the case of Drinfeld centre of fusion category, the objects of the Drinfeld centre
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
are of the form

(S,e_{S})

(4.1)

where
S\in\mathfrak{C}
and
e_{S}
are half-braidings

e_{S}(X):S\times X\xrightarrow{\sim}X\times S~.

(4.2)

with
X\in\mathfrak{C}
. The half-braiding
e_{S}
has to satisfy several consistency conditions. Similar to the case for categories the half-braidings
e_{S}(X)
are defined such that surfaces and lines in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
can braid consistently. Therefore,
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
is always a braided fusion 2-category. In fact, it is expected that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
satisfies the remote detectability criterion which ensures that any given surface braids non-trivially with at least one line-operator.
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
is therefore a 3+1d TQFT. The SymTFTs in 3+1d fall into two families. These follows from the classification of TQFTs in 3+1d
[
62
,
63
,
64
,
65
]
. A general 3+1d TQFT has point operators, line operators, surface operators and 3-dimensional membrane operators. We will assume that the 3+1d TQFT do not have any point operators except the trivial one. In such a TQFT, the membrane operators are all condensation defects of the lower dimensional operators
[
64
]
. Therefore, the TQFT is completely determined by the data of its surface and line operators. Line operators braid trivially between themselves in 3+1d. Therefore, in any 3+1d TQFT the line operators are described by a symmetric fusion category. By Deligne’s theorem, such a category is of the form Rep
(G)
or Rep
(G,z)
where
z
is an order two central element of
G

[
16
]
. Here Rep
(G,z)
is a symmetric fusion category which describes both bosonic and fermionic line operators. We have the following two cases:

1.

In a 3+1d TQFT in which all line operators are bosonic, the category of line operators is given by Rep
(G)
. The category Rep
(G)
describes a set of bosonic line operators labelled by representations of the group
G
. Their fusion rules are given by the tensor product of representations of
G
.

Since all the bosonic line operators braid trivially, there is no anomaly and we can gauge them. This results in a 3+1d TQFT without any line operators and dual 0-form symmetry
G
with some anomaly
\pi\in Z^{4}(G,U(1))
. Therefore, a 3+1d TQFT with bosonic line operators is a 3+1d Dijkgraaf-Witten (DW) theory with gauge group
G
and DW-twist given by a 4-cocycle
\pi\in Z^{4}(G,U(1))
. Since all of these TQFTs admit gapped-boundaries, they are all SymTFTs.

2.

In a 3+1d TQFT with fermionic line operators, the category of line operators is given by Rep
(G,z)
. We can gauge all the bosonic line operators resulting in a 3+1d TQFT with a transparent fermion. Therefore, all 3+1d TQFT with fermionic line operators are obtained from gauging a symmetry of a 3+1d TQFT with a single non-trivial line operator which is fermionic. How many 3+1d TQFTs exist with this property? These were classified in
[
65
]
, and there are only two such 3+1d TQFTs!

•

The spin-
\mathds{Z}_{2}
gauge theory whose dynamical gauge field is a spin structure. This TQFT has a single non-trivial fermionic line operator and a single non-trivial surface operator implementing a non-anomalous
\mathds{Z}_{2}
1-form symmetry.

•

A cousin of the spin-
\mathds{Z}_{2}
gauge theory with the same set of line and surface operators and fusion rules, but now the
\mathds{Z}_{2}
1-form symmetry is anomalous.

Among the two theories above, only the first one admits a gapped boundary
[
66
]
. Therefore, all TQFTs obtained from gaugings symmetries of the spin-
\mathds{Z}_{2}
gauge theory are SymTFTs. In fact, we have

\text{spin-}\mathds{Z}_{2}\text{ gauge theory }=\mathcal{Z}(\Sigma\text{SVec})~.

(4.3)

A fundamental result that we will use in the following is that if a fusion 2-category
\mathfrak{C}
admits a braiding, then it is a subcategory of its SymTFT. The intuition behind this result is a generalization of the result for fusion categories. If
\mathfrak{C}
admits a braiding, then the braiding can be used to push the surfaces and lines on the canonical gapped-boundary of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
corresponding to
\mathfrak{C}
into the bulk. More explicitly, let

denote the braiding of two surface operators

and

[

(4.4)

of

. Therefore, braidings on
\mathfrak{C}
can be determined by studying the embedding of
\mathfrak{C}
in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
.

Consider a general fusion 2-category
\mathfrak{C}
. As we discussed earlier, the line operators in
\mathfrak{C}
form a braided fusion category
\Omega\mathfrak{C}

acts as a symmetry of a 3+1d QFT. Since lines braid trivially in 3+1d,

must be equivalent to Rep
(H)
or Rep

. From now we will assume that there are no transparent fermionic line operators. Our discussion below can be generalized to include this case. Therefore, if
\mathfrak{C}
admits braiding
\Omega\mathfrak{C}\cong\text{Rep}(H)
. After gauging these lines we get a new fusion 2-category

A fusion 2-category

The converse of this statement is not true. For example, 2Vec

From the discussion above, it is clear that if

4.2
Braidings from embeddings

In this section, we will describe the general procedure to determine braidings on a fusion 2-category using its 3+1d SymTFT. The SymTFT

of a fusion 2-category has a canonical gapped boundary

on which the surface and line operators form

F:\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})\to\mathfrak{C}~,

(4.16)

\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
are given by

F((S,e_{S}))=S~.

(4.17)

Note that, similarly to the case of 2+1d SymTFT, this map forgets about the half-braidings. If
\mathfrak{C}
admits a braiding, then there should be an embedding

\iota:\mathfrak{C}\to\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})~,

(4.18)

\mathfrak{C}

must be such that the non-condensation surface operators and line operators in
\mathfrak{D}
must not be endable on the canonical gapped boundary
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{C}}

O(\mathfrak{D})
be the set of simple topological surface operators in
\mathfrak{D}
and let
O(\Omega\mathfrak{D})

\Omega\mathfrak{D}

be the set of non-condensation topological surface operators in the decomposition of

into simple surface operators. Let

be the set of line operators which can end on the gapped boundary
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{C}}

We require

O(\mathfrak{D})\cap O(L_{\mathfrak{C}})=\emptyset~,~O(\Omega\mathfrak{D})\cap E(L_{\mathfrak{C}})=\{\mathbf{1}\}~,

(4.19)

where
\mathbf{1}
is the trivial line operator. If the image of an embedding
\mathfrak{D}
satisfies this condition, we will call it an embedding orthogonal to
L_{\mathfrak{C}}
. Conversely, consider a fusion sub-2-category
\mathfrak{D}
of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
such that (

is injective. That is, no surface or line operator in
\mathfrak{D}
is mapped to the trivial surface and line defect in
\mathfrak{D}

is a fusion sub-2-category of
\mathfrak{C}

appropriately we can ensure that

is surjective as well.

From Proposition

\mathfrak{C}
that admit a braiding are group-theoretical. Any group-theoretical fusion 2-category is determined by a finite group

, a 4-cocycle

, a subgroup

{}_{G}^{\pi}

is non-anomalous. The SymTFT of

6
Conclusion

In this work, we described a method to explicitly classify the anomaly of higher-form symmetries using the SymTFT. In 2+1d, this led to an explicit algorithm to compute all braidings on a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
using only its fusion ring and
F
symbols. While the hexagon equations uses the same input, they are non-linear multivariable polynomial equations whose solutions need to be sorted into equivalence classes related by basis transformation on the fusion spaces. In our method, the braidings on
\mathcal{C}
are completely determined by the modular data of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
and its canonical Lagrangian algebra object, which are both basis independent. While computing the modular data of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
requires the
F
-symbols in a basis, its computation can be done using a polynomial time algorithm to find the central idempotents of the tube algebra.

In 3+1d, we used similar methods to classify braidings on higher-form symmetries. We showed that, in a sense, this problem is simpler than in 2+1d, since only those fusion 2-categories
\mathfrak{C}
for which
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{C})
is a 3+1d DW theory admits a braiding. The 3+1d DW theories are well-studied, and we used their operator content to determine embedding of various fusion 2-categories in it.

Our work leads to various interesting questions to explore in the future:

•

It will be interesting to find a necessary and sufficient criterion for a general fusion 2-category to admit a braiding by studying whether they can be embedded inside the relevant 3+1d DW theory.

•

Our method to classify braidings applies to higher dimensions as well. It will be interesting to determine the braidings on a fusion 3-category by embedding it inside the relevant 4+1d SymTFT. In particular, it will be interesting to study braidings on Tambara-Yamagami 3-categories
[
77
]
.

•

It will be interesting to find a basis independent quantity which determines the braiding on 3+1d DW theory (similar to modular data for MTCs). This will then enable us to compute the braidings on a fusion 2-category determined by its embeddings in the DW theory.

•

In
[
55
]
, it was shown that braided fusion categories with line operators with real spins can be completely classified. Their
F
and
R
symbols are related to the
F
and
R
symbols of certain special abelian TQFTs under gauging. It will be interesting to understand if the
F
symbols of braided fusion categories with more general spins can be obtained by combining the methods of this paper with
[
55
]
.

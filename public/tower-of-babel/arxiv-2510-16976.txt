Abstract
. In an earlier paper, we proved the connectedness of the fibers of every 2 ​ n 2n -dimensional integrable system satisfying both: (A) the action extends the action of an ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -dimensional torus which has a proper moment map, and (B) every tall singular point is non-degenerate and no such point has a hyperbolic block and connected T T -stabilizer. Unfortunately, these criteria are fairly restrictive. Our main goal in this paper is to find a larger class of integrable systems that has connected fibers by weakening the non-degeneracy assumption above. To achieve this, we introduce “ephemeral” degenerate singular points, examples of which have appeared in the literature in the context of p : − q p\!:\!-q resonances and special Lagrangian fibrations. Finally, we construct a family of examples that shows that our main theorem meaningfully extends previous results.

\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a complexity

T

We start by recalling

, Definition 3.1]
.

Definition 2.5
.

A point

\bm{g}
modulo

\bm{g}
modulo
\bm{\Phi}

Near each regular point of

is smooth with no critical points.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
ST1
]
, Lemma 3.2)
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable
system such that
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a complexity
one
T
-space. Let
p\in M
be a regular point of

modulo
\Phi
. Then there exists an open neighborhood

of
[p]
in the reduced space

.

By Lemma

, in order to construct Morse functions on the reduced spaces, we need to impose some conditions near the critical points of

modulo
\Phi
. More precisely, the following key result holds.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
ST1
]
, Proposition 3.4)
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable system such that
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a complexity

T
-space. Fix

.
Assume that for each critical point

\Phi

[p]
in the reduced space

(2)

\Psi_{p}

.

3.

In this section, we study non-degenerate singular points in integrable systems that extend complexity one

The first claim that we recall

Lemma 3.1

]

. The first deals with points that have purely elliptic type; the second deals with all other cases.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
ST1
]
, Proposition 4.5)
.

Let

Let

Proposition 3.3

(
[
ST1
]
, Proposition 6.3)
.

Let

is a complexity one

. Moreover, there exist an open neighborhood

(

]
, Lemma 6.7)
.

•

•

.

Let
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f=\left(\Phi,g\right)\right)

\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)

By Lemmas

and

, it suffices to consider the case that

is a tall critical point of

.
So by the Marle-Guillemin-Sternberg local normal form, we may assume that

4.
Ephemeral points

In this section, we study ephemeral points in
an integrable system
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
that extends a complexity one
T
-action.
We prove that, although every ephemeral point
p\in M
is singular, we can put a smooth structure
on the reduced space near
[p]
so that the induced function
\overline{g}
is smooth and has no critical points; see Proposition
4.2
.
Hence, the singularity is transitory; this explains the terminology “ephemeral” in Definition
1.8
. In particular, every ephemeral point satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition
2.7
.

Before proving this claim, we need a few definitions.
Let
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
be a local model.
By a slight abuse of notation, we call a
T
-invariant function
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
a
\bm{T}
-invariant (homogeneous) polynomial
if its restriction to
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
is a (homogeneous) polynomial
(see
[
ST1
, Definition 2.10]
).
More generally, recall that if
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
is any smooth
T
-invariant function, the degree
\ell
reduced Taylor polynomial at
p=[1,0,0]\in Y
is the function
\overline{T^{\ell}_{p}g}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

\overline{T^{\ell}_{p}g}(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)=T^{\ell}_{0}(R^{*}g)(z)\quad\text{for all }\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T}.

Here,
R\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\hookrightarrow Y
denotes the inclusion
z\mapsto[1,0,z]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
be a tall
local model with moment map
\Phi_{Y}\colon Y\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
and defining polynomial
P\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
of degree
N
.
Given a
T
-invariant smooth function
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
there exists a smooth function
h\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that,
for all
\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T}
,

\overline{g}(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)=h({\mathfrak{R}}P(z),{\mathfrak{I}}P(z),|z|^{2}).

For all
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the degree
\ell
reduced Taylor polynomial of
g
is

\overline{T_{p}^{\ell}g}(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)=\hskip-10.84006pt{{\sum}}_{{Ni+Nj+2k\leq\ell}}\hskip-7.22743pt\frac{1}{i!j!k!}\frac{{\partial}^{i+j+k}h}{{\partial}x^{i}{\partial}y^{j}{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)\,({\mathfrak{R}}P(z))^{i}({\mathfrak{I}}P(z))^{j}|z|^{2k}

for all
\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T}
. Finally, if
\overline{T^{\ell-1}_{p}g}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
vanishes identically, we may further assume
that
\frac{{\partial}^{i+j+k}h}{{\partial}x^{i}{\partial}y^{j}{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)=0
for all
i,j,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
so that
Ni+Nj+2k<\ell.

Proof.

Let
\Pi\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\oplus{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
denote the map

\Pi([t,\alpha,z])=({\mathfrak{R}}P(z),{\mathfrak{I}}P(z),|z|^{2},\Phi_{Y}([t,\alpha,z])).

By
[
ST1
, Lemma 2.11]
, the algebra of
T
-invariant polynomials on
Y

is generated by the real and imaginary parts of the defining polynomial
P\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
, the map
[t,\alpha,z]\mapsto|z|^{2}
, and the components of
\Phi_{Y}
. Hence, since
g
is
T
-invariant, by Schwarz’s
theorem applied to the
H
-action on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
there exists a smooth function
\breve{h}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\oplus{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
g=\Pi^{*}\breve{h}
; see
[
Sch
]
.
Let
h\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the restriction of
\breve{h}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\oplus\{0\}
, that is,
let
h(x,y,\rho):=\breve{h}(x,y,\rho,0)
. Then
\overline{g}(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket])=h({\mathfrak{R}}P(z),{\mathfrak{I}}P(z),|z|^{2})
for all
\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T},
as required.

We introduce a grading on polynomials on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\oplus{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, so that the first two coordinates of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
have degree
N
, the third coordinate has degree
2
, the components of
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}
have degree
1
, and the components of
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
have degree
2
. Since each component of
\Pi
is a

T
-invariant polynomial on
Y
of the corresponding degree, for any integer
m
the pullback under
\Pi
of the Taylor polynomial of
\breve{h}
of graded degree
m
at
0
is the
T
-invariant polynomial of degree
m
on
Y
whose
restriction to
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
is the Taylor polynomial
T^{m}_{0}(R^{*}g)
.
Moreover, let

\sum_{Ni+Nj+2k\leq m}\frac{1}{i!j!k!}\frac{{\partial}^{i+j+k}h}{{\partial}x^{i}{\partial}y^{j}{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)x^{i}y^{j}\rho^{k}

be the Taylor polynomial of
h
of graded degree
m
at
0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Note that it is
the restriction to
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\oplus\{0\}

of the Taylor polynomial of
\breve{h}
of graded degree
m
at
0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\oplus{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
.
Therefore, for all
\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T}
,

\overline{T_{p}^{m}g}(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)=\sum_{Ni+Nj+2k\leq m}\frac{1}{i!j!k!}\frac{{\partial}^{i+j+k}h}{{\partial}x^{i}{\partial}y^{j}{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)\,({\mathfrak{R}}P(z))^{i}({\mathfrak{I}}P(z))^{j}|z|^{2k}.

Finally, if
\overline{T^{\ell-1}_{p}g}
vanishes on
{Y/\!/T}
, then for
all
\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T}
,

\sum_{Ni+Nj+2k<\ell}\frac{1}{i!j!k!}\frac{{\partial}^{i+j+k}h}{{\partial}x^{i}{\partial}y^{j}{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)\,({\mathfrak{R}}P(z))^{i}({\mathfrak{I}}P(z))^{j}|z|^{2k}=0.

Hence, if we replace
h
by

h(x,y,\rho)-\sum_{Ni+Nj+2k<\ell}\frac{1}{i!j!k!}\frac{{\partial}^{i+j+k}h}{{\partial}x^{i}{\partial}y^{j}{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)\,x^{i}y^{j}\rho^{k},

then this function (which we also denote by
h
) satisfies

\frac{{\partial}^{i+j+k}h}{{\partial}x^{i}{\partial}y^{j}{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)=0
for all
i,j,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
so that
Ni+Nj+2k<\ell
, and still satisfies the remaining properties.
∎

We can now prove the main result of this section, which generalizes Remark
1.7
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable system such that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space. Let
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)\cap g^{-1}(0)
be an ephemeral point.
Then there exist an open neighborhood
U
of
[p]\in{M/\!/T}

and a map
\Psi\colon U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
taking
[p]
to
(0,0)
satisfying:

(1)

\Psi\colon U\to\Psi(U)
is a homeomorphism onto an open set;

(2)

\Psi
restricts to a diffeomorphism on
U\smallsetminus\{[p]\}
; and

(3)

(\overline{g}\circ\Psi^{-1})(x,y)=y
for all
(x,y)\in\Psi(U)
.

Proof.

By the Marle-Guillemin-Sternberg local normal form theorem, we may assume that
M
is a tall local model
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
, that
\Phi=\Phi_{Y}
, and that
p=[1,0,0]
. Let
N
be the degree of the associated defining polynomial
P\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Since
p
is ephemeral, we may further assume that the reduced Taylor polynomial
\overline{T^{N-1}_{p}g}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
vanishes identically and that the zero set of
\overline{T^{N}_{p}g}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}

is homeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Set
m:=\lceil N/2\rceil
. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists a smooth function
h\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}

such that:

(i)

For all
\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T}
,

\begin{split}\overline{g}(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)&=h({\mathfrak{R}}P(z),{\mathfrak{I}}P(z),|z|^{2}),\quad\text{and}\\
\overline{T^{N}_{p}g}(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)&=\begin{cases}\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}x}(0){\mathfrak{R}}P(z)+\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}y}(0){\mathfrak{I}}P(z)&\mbox{if $N$ is odd,}\\
\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}x}(0){\mathfrak{R}}P(z)+\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}y}(0){\mathfrak{I}}P(z)+\frac{1}{m!}\frac{{\partial}^{m}h}{{\partial}\rho^{m}}(0)|z|^{2m}&\mbox{if $N$ is even.}\end{cases}\end{split}

(ii)

For each integer
0\leq k<m
,
\frac{{\partial}^{k}h}{{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)=0
.

The group
(S^{1})^{h+1}
acts on
Y
by
T
-equivariant symplectomorphisms that preserve
\Phi_{Y}
, and hence acts on
{Y/\!/T}
. If we replace
\overline{g}
by its pullback under such an automorphism
\lambda\in(S^{1})^{h+1}
, we also replace
h
by its pullback under an automorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
that rotates the first two coordinates by
P(\lambda)\in S^{1}
. Hence, since
P((S^{1})^{h+1})=S^{1}
, we may further
assume that
\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}y}(0)\geq 0
and
\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}x}(0)=0
.

Define the map
\Psi\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

\Psi(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)=({\mathfrak{R}}P(z),\overline{g}(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)).

By definition,
\Psi
satisfies condition (
3
) in the statement
for any
U\subseteq{Y/\!/T}.

By
[
ST1
, Lemma 2.9]
there exists
C>0
so that

(4.3)

|z|^{2}=C|P(z)|^{\frac{2}{N}}\text{ for all }\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T}.

Define a continuous function
\check{h}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

(4.4)

\check{h}(x,y):=h(x,y,C(x^{2}+y^{2})^{\frac{1}{N}}).

By item
(i)
above and (
4.3
),

(4.5)

\Psi(\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket)=({\mathfrak{R}}P(z),H({\mathfrak{R}}P(z),{\mathfrak{I}}P(z)))\text{ for all }\llbracket t,0,z\rrbracket\in{Y/\!/T}.

Our aim is to show that there exists a convex open neighborhood
V
of
(0,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

such that
\frac{\partial\check{h}}{\partial y}>0
on
V\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
.
Suppose that this claim holds.
Since
V
is convex,
the intersection
V\cap(\{x_{0}\}\times{\mathbb{R}})
is connected
for all
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Hence, since
\check{h}(0,0)=0
,
a careful application of the Mean Value Theorem shows that
\check{h}(x_{0},y)<\check{h}(x_{0},y^{\prime})
for
all
(x_{0},y)
and
(x_{0},y^{\prime})
in
V
with
y<y^{\prime}
.
Thus, the map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,\check{h}(x,y))
is a continuous injection from
V
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Therefore, by Invariance of Domain, the image of
V
is open in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and the above map is a homeomorphism onto its image. Since the map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,\check{h}(x,y))
is a submersion on
V\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
, it is a diffeomorphism
from
V\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
to its image.
Moreover, by Lemma
2.4
, the map
\overline{P}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a homeomorphism that restricts to a diffeomorphism on the complement of
[p]
. So by (
4.5
),
there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
[p]
in
{Y/\!/T}
such that
\Psi\colon U\to\Psi(U)

satisfies conditions (
1
) and (
2
) in the statement. Hence, it remains to show that the map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,\check{h}(x,y))
has the desired properties.

Since
\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}x}(0)=0
, item
(i)
above and (
4.3
) imply that, under the identification given by
\overline{P}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{C}}
,

\overline{T^{N}_{p}g}(x+iy)=\begin{cases}\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}y}(0)y&\mbox{if $N$ is odd},\\
\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}y}(0)y+\frac{C^{m}}{m!}\frac{{\partial}^{m}h}{{\partial}\rho^{m}}(0)(x^{2}+y^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}&\mbox{if $N$
is even},\end{cases}

for all
x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Since the zero set of

\overline{T^{N}_{p}g}
is homeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
and since
\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}y}(0)\geq 0
, we obtain that
\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}y}(0)>0
if
N
is odd; by using polar coordinates to calculate the zero set, we also obtain that

\frac{{\partial}h}{{\partial}y}(0)>\frac{C^{m}}{m!}\big|\frac{{\partial}^{m}h}{{\partial}\rho^{m}}(0)\big|
if
N=2m
.

Since
\frac{{\partial}^{k}h}{{\partial}\rho^{k}}(0)=0
for all
0\leq k<m
,
by applying Lemma
4.7
below
m
times
and by combining terms as necessary,
there exist smooth functions
h_{x},h_{y},h_{\rho}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that

(4.6)

h=xh_{x}+yh_{y}+\rho^{m}h_{\rho}.

By combining (
4.4
) with (
4.6
), on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
we have that

\begin{split}\frac{{\partial}\check{h}}{{\partial}y}=h_{y}&+\frac{2mC^{m}y}{N(x^{2}+y^{2})^{\frac{N-m}{N}}}h_{\rho}+x\frac{{\partial}h_{x}}{{\partial}y}+y\frac{{\partial}h_{y}}{{\partial}y}+C^{m}(x^{2}+y^{2})^{\frac{m}{N}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\rho}}{{\partial}y}\\
&+\frac{2Cy}{N(x^{2}+y^{2})^{\frac{N-1}{N}}}\left(x\frac{{\partial}h_{x}}{{\partial}\rho}+y\frac{{\partial}h_{x}}{{\partial}\rho}+C^{m}(x^{2}+y^{2})^{\frac{m}{N}}\frac{{\partial}h_{\rho}}{{\partial}\rho}\right),\end{split}

where
\frac{{\partial}\check{h}}{{\partial}y}
is evaluated at
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
, and
h_{x},h_{y},h_{\rho}
and their partial derivatives are evaluated at
(x,y,C(x^{2}+y^{2})^{\frac{1}{N}})
. If
N
is odd, then
2m=N+1
, and so each term except the first approaches
0
as
(x,y)
approaches
(0,0)
. Therefore,
\frac{{\partial}\check{h}}{{\partial}y}
approaches
h_{y}(0)=\frac{\partial h}{\partial y}(0)
, which is positive.
If
N
is even, then
2m=N
, and so the first term approaches
h_{y}(0)
, the second term is equal to
C^{m}y(x^{2}+y^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\rho}
, and each of the remaining terms approaches
0
.
Moreover,
h_{y}(0)=\frac{\partial h}{\partial y}(0)
is greater than
|C^{m}h_{\rho}(0)|=\frac{C^{m}}{m!}|\frac{\partial^{m}h}{\partial\rho^{m}}(0)|
. Hence, in either case there exists a convex neighborhood
V

of
(0,0)
so that

\frac{{\partial}\check{h}}{{\partial}y}

is positive on
V\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}
, as required.
∎

Above, we used the following result,
proved in
[
Mil
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
V\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a convex neighborhood of
0
and let
h\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth function. If
h(0)=0
, then there exist smooth
functions
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that, for all

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in V
,

h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}h_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

5.
Proofs of the main results

In this section, we prove our main results, Theorems
1.3
and
1.10
; we also state and prove a related result, Proposition
5.6
. Consider an integrable system that extends a complexity one
T
-space. Assume that each tall singular point is either non-degenerate or ephemeral.
The fundamental idea of this section is that the results of Sections
2
,
3
, and
4
combined show
that each component of the reduced space containing more than one point
can be given the structure of a smooth oriented surface so that the induced function

\overline{g}
is a Morse function.
More precisely, we have the following generalization of
[
ST1
, Proposition 7.1]
that includes ephemeral points.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable system such that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space. Assume that each tall singular point in
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f)
is either non-degenerate or ephemeral. Fix
\beta\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
.
Each component of the reduced space
\Phi^{-1}(\beta)/T
containing more than one point
can be given the structure of a smooth oriented surface such that
\overline{g}\colon\Phi^{-1}(\beta)/T\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Morse function. Moreover, given
p\in\Phi^{-1}(\beta)
:

(i)

[p]
is a critical point of
\overline{g}
of index
1
exactly if
p
is a non-degenerate singular point with a hyperbolic block and
connected
T
-stabilizer,

(ii)

[p]
is a critical point of
\overline{g}
of index
0
or
2
exactly if
p
is a critical point of
g
modulo
\Phi
with purely elliptic type, and

(iii)

[p]
is a regular point of
\overline{g}
exactly if
p
is ephemeral or is a regular point of
g
modulo
\Phi
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\beta=0
. For simplicity, assume that
{M/\!/T}
is connected and contains more than one point. Then, by definition, every point in
\Phi^{-1}(0)
is tall. If
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
is a critical point of
g
modulo
\Phi
,
then
p
is also a singular point of
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\right)
. So, by assumption,
p
is either non-degenerate or ephemeral. Hence, by Corollary
3.5
,
p
either has purely elliptic type, is non-degenerate with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer, or is ephemeral. Therefore, by Proposition
2.7
, the claim follows from Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
and
4.2
.
∎

We also need the following well-known result, which is proved in
[
At
,
GS2
,
Sja
,
LMTW
]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
T
act on a connected symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
with moment map
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
.
If
\Phi
is proper, then the fibers of
\Phi
are connected and
\Phi
is open as a map to
\Phi(M)
.

Finally, we need the following result, which characterizes those tall local models with a defining polynomial of degree greater than two.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
be a tall local model, where
H
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
with weights
\eta_{0},\dots,\eta_{h}
. Then the degree
N
of the defining polynomial of
Y
is greater than two exactly if:

(1)

If
\eta_{i}=0
for some
i
, then
H
has more than two components; and

(2)

If
\eta_{i}+\eta_{j}=0
for some
i,j
, then
H
is not connected.

Proof.

Let
P(z)=\prod_{i=0}^{h}z_{i}^{\xi_{i}}
be the defining polynomial of
Y
.
By Lemma
2.1
, the image of
H
in
(S^{1})^{h+1}
is the kernel of the homomorphism
(S^{1})^{h+1}\to S^{1}
given by
\lambda\mapsto\prod_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\xi_{i}}
; in particular,
\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\eta_{i}=0
is the unique linear relation among the
\eta_{i}
. The weight
\eta_{i}=0
for some
i
exactly if
P(z)=z_{i}^{\xi_{i}}
; in this case,

P
has degree
\xi_{i}
and
H
has
\xi_{i}
components.
Similarly, the sum
\eta_{i}+\eta_{j}=0
for some
i,j
exactly if

P(z)=z_{i}^{\xi_{i}}z_{j}^{\xi_{j}}
and
\xi_{i}=\xi_{j}
; in this case,
P
has degree
N=2\xi_{i}
and
H
has
\xi_{i}
components.
In any other case,
N=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}>2
.
The claim follows immediately.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

If
Y
is a tall local model with a surjective moment map, then by
[
KT1
, Lemma 5.2]
we can
choose the
\xi
in Lemma
2.1
so that
\xi_{i}>0
for all
i
.
Therefore, if the degree
N:=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}
of the defining polynomial is
1
then
H
is trivial, while if
N=2
then either
H\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, or
H\simeq S^{1}
and the set of
isotropy weights are
\{-1,+1\}
.

We can now prove our main results, Theorems
1.3
and
1.10
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable system such that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space with a proper moment map. We may assume that the fiber
\Phi^{-1}(0)
contains three distinct
T
-orbits
\mathfrak{O}_{1},\mathfrak{O}_{2},\mathfrak{O}_{3}
that satisfy properties (
1
) and (
2
) in the statement of the theorem. Furthermore, assume that every tall singular point in
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\right)
is non-degenerate. Since
\Phi
is proper, the reduced space
{M/\!/T}
is compact and connected by Theorem
5.2
; this implies that every point in
\mathfrak{O}_{i}
is tall. So by the Marle-Guillemin-Sternberg local normal form theorem and Lemma
5.3
every point in each
of the orbits
\mathfrak{O}_{i}
has a tall local model whose defining polynomial has degree
N>2
. Therefore, each such point is a critical point of
g
modulo
\Phi
by Lemma
2.6
, and has purely elliptic type by Proposition
3.3
.
Hence, by Proposition
5.1
(or by
[
ST1
, Proposition 7.1]
),
{M/\!/T}
can be given the structure of a smooth closed, connected, oriented surface so that
\overline{g}\colon{M/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Morse function; moreover, each
\mathfrak{O}_{i}
corresponds to a critical point of
\overline{g}
of index
0
or
2
. By
[
ST1
, Lemma 7.2]
, every Morse function on a closed, connected oriented manifold with connected fibers has a unique critical point of index or coindex 0. Hence, not all fibers of
\overline{g}
are connected, and so not all fibers of
f
are connected.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable system such that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space with a proper moment map. Assume that each tall singular point in
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\right)
is either non-degenerate or ephemeral. Fix
\beta\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
; we may assume that
\beta=0
.
Since
\Phi
is proper, the reduced space
{M/\!/T}
is compact and connected by Theorem
5.2
.

If the reduced space
{M/\!/T}
is a single point, then it contains no tall orbits; moreover, it is simply connected and the fiber
f^{-1}(0,c)
is connected for all
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Otherwise, every point in
{M/\!/T}
is tall, and by Proposition
5.1
,
{M/\!/T}
can be given the structure of a smooth closed, connected oriented surface so that

\overline{g}\colon{M/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}

is a Morse function; moreover, the point
[p]\in{M/\!/T}
associated to
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
is a critical point of
\overline{g}
of index
1
exactly if
p
is a non-degenerate singular point with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer.
Since
T
is connected, the fiber
f^{-1}(0,c)
is connected if and only if
\overline{g}^{-1}(c)
is connected.

Hence, the result follows from
[
ST1
, Lemma 7.3]
, which states that a Morse function on a closed, connected 2-dimensional manifold has no critical point of index one exactly if the manifold is simply connected and each fiber is connected.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

More generally, Theorems
1.3
and
1.10
both hold if
\Phi
is proper as a map to an open convex subset of
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, because the conclusions of Theorem
5.2
still hold in this case by
[
LMTW
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Finally, we prove that, generically, the integrable systems we consider have connected fibers exactly if they have no tall non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer.
Note that, by
[
KT1
, Lemma 5.7 and Corollary 9.7]
, all the reduced spaces of a complexity one
T
-space with a proper moment map have the same genus.
Hence, Theorem
1.10
and Proposition
5.6
together show that
[
ST1
, Theorem 1.10]
can be extended to allow tall ephemeral points, instead of only allowing tall non-degenerate singular points.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
be an integrable system such that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space with a proper moment map. Assume that each tall
singular point in
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f\right)
is either non-degenerate or ephemeral. If each fiber of
f
contains at most one
T
-orbit of tall non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block, then
f^{-1}(\beta,c)
is connected for all
(\beta,c)\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}
if and only if
M
has no tall non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer.

Proof.

If
M
has no tall non-degenerate singular points with a hyperbolic block and connected
T
-stabilizer, then by Theorem
1.10
the fiber
f^{-1}(\beta,c)
is connected for each
(\beta,c)\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}
. Hence, we may assume that each fiber of
f
is connected.

Fix
\beta\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
such that
\Phi^{-1}(\beta)/T
contains more than one point; we may assume that
\beta=0
. Since
\Phi
is proper, the reduced space
{M/\!/T}
is compact and connected by Theorem
5.2
. By Proposition
5.1
,
{M/\!/T}
can be given
the structure of a smooth closed, connected oriented surface so that
\overline{g}\colon{M/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Morse function; moreover, the point
[p]\in{M/\!/T}
associated to

is a critical point of
\overline{g}
of index

exactly if
p
is a non-degenerate singular point with a hyperbolic block and connected

-stabilizer.
Since

and the fiber

are connected, the fiber
\overline{g}^{-1}(c)
is connected for each

. Hence the result follows from
[

, Lemma 7.4]
, which states that a Morse function on a closed, connected oriented surface with connected fibers has the property that each fiber contains an even number of critical points of index 1.
∎

6.
An intrinsic characterization of ephemeral points

The definition of an ephemeral point in the Introduction depends on choosing a symplectic identification of a
T
-invariant neighborhood of the point with an open subset of the associated local model.
In this section, we give an
intrinsic
characterization of an ephemeral point, i.e., one that does not depend on any such choice; in particular, we show that if a point is ephemeral then conditions
(a)
and
(b)
of Definition
1.8
hold for
every
such identification (see Proposition
6.15
below). Finally, we provide tools to check whether a point is ephemeral using any coordinate chart in Lemma
6.16
.

We start by introducing the intrinsic notions that we need. Fix
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}:=\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid n\geq 0\}
. Suppose that
g\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

vanishes below order
\bm{\ell}
at
\bm{0}
, i.e., all partial derivatives of
g
of order strictly less than
\ell
vanish at
0
.
Then
T^{\ell}_{0}g
, the degree
\ell
Taylor polynomial of
g
at
0
, is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
\ell
on
T_{0}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Moreover, if
\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is smooth and
\psi(0)=0
, then by the chain rule

(1)

\psi^{*}g\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
vanishes below order
\ell
at
0
, and

(2)

T^{\ell}_{0}(\psi^{*}g)=(D_{0}\psi)^{*}(T^{\ell}_{0}g)
.

Next fix a point
p
in a manifold
M
. Given a coordinate chart
\varphi
on
M
centered at
p
, we say that
g

vanishes below order
\bm{\ell}
at
\bm{p}
if
g\circ\varphi^{-1}
vanishes below order
\ell
at
0
. By property (
1
) above, this does not depend on the choice of
\varphi
. Let
Q^{\ell}_{p}\subseteq C^{\infty}(M)
denote the ideal of functions that vanish below order
\ell
at
p
, and let
S^{\ell}(T_{p}M)
be the vector space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
\ell
on
T_{p}M
. Given
g\in Q^{\ell}_{p}
, the
jet of
\bm{g}
of order
\bm{\ell}
at
\bm{p}
is

(6.1)

j^{\ell}_{p}g:=(D_{p}\varphi)^{*}(T^{\ell}_{0}(g\circ\varphi^{-1}))\in S^{\ell}(T_{p}M),

where
\varphi
is a coordinate chart centered at
p
.
By property (
2
) above, this polynomial does not depend on the choice of
\varphi
. Moreover, by properties (
1
) and (
2
), these notions are natural; given a smooth map
\psi\colon N\to M

with
\psi(q)=p
:

(6.2)

\text{If }g\in Q^{\ell}_{p}\text{ then }\psi^{*}g\in Q^{\ell}_{q}\text{ and }j^{\ell}_{q}(\psi^{*}g)=(D_{q}\psi)^{*}(j^{\ell}_{p}g).

Remark 6.3
.

The map
j^{\ell}_{p}\colon Q^{\ell}_{p}\to S^{\ell}(T_{p}M)
is a surjective homomorphism of vector spaces with kernel

Q^{\ell+1}_{p}
.
Under the resulting identification of
Q^{\ell}_{p}/Q^{\ell+1}_{p}
with
S^{\ell}(T_{p}M)
, the homomorphism
j_{p}^{\ell}
is the restriction of the standard jet map
C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)/Q^{\ell+1}_{p}
to the subspace
Q^{\ell}_{p}\subseteq C^{\infty}(M)
. This motivates our use of the term “jet”.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a subspace of
C^{\infty}(M)
. We say that
g\in C^{\infty}(M)

vanishes below order
\bm{\ell}
at
\bm{p}
modulo

\bm{\mathcal{I}}
if
g\in\mathcal{I}+Q_{p}^{\ell}
, i.e.,
g-h\in Q^{\ell}_{p}
for some
h\in\mathcal{I}
. Moreover, the
homogenization of
\bm{\mathcal{I}}
at
\bm{p}
is a graded subspace of the graded polynomial algebra
S(T_{p}M):=\oplus_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}}S^{\ell}(T_{p}M)
; it is the image of the map
\bigoplus_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{I}\cap Q^{\ell}_{p}\to S(T_{p}M)
that sends
g\in\mathcal{I}\cap Q^{\ell}_{p}
to
j^{\ell}_{p}g
. The
zero locus of the homogenization of
\bm{\mathcal{I}}
at
\bm{p}
is the set of points
V_{\mathcal{I},p}
in
T_{p}M
where every polynomial in the homogenization of
\mathcal{I}
at
p
vanishes. By (
6.2
),
given a diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon N\to M
with
\varphi(q)=p
:

(6.4)

D_{q}\varphi(V_{\varphi^{*}\mathcal{I},q})=V_{\mathcal{I},p}.

Example 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the ideal in
C^{\infty}(M)
generated by a function
f\in Q_{p}^{\ell}\smallsetminus Q_{p}^{\ell+1}
. It is straightforward to check that the homogenization of
\mathcal{I}
at
p
is the ideal in
S(T_{p}M)
generated by
j_{p}^{\ell}f
; its zero locus
V_{\mathcal{I},p}\subseteq T_{p}M
is the zero set of
j_{p}^{\ell}f
.

Now assume that a torus
T
acts on
M
. Then the stabilizer
H
of
p
acts linearly on the smooth slice
W:=T_{p}M/T_{p}\mathfrak{O}
,
where
\mathfrak{O}
is the
T
-orbit through
p
.
By the smooth slice theorem for proper actions (see, e.g.,
[
DK
, Theorem 2.4.1]
), we may locally identify
M
with
Y=T\times_{H}W
and
p
with
[1,0]
.
Define
r\colon W\to Y
by
r(v)=[1,w]
,
\chi\colon{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\times W\to Y
by
\chi(\eta,w)=[{\operatorname{exp}}(\eta),w]
, and
\pi\colon{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\times W\to W
by
\pi(\eta,w)=w
. Since
D_{(0,0)}\chi
is
invertible, there is a coordinate chart
\varphi
centered at
p
with inverse
\chi
.
Using
\varphi
to identify
T_{p}Y
with
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\times W
gives
T_{p}\mathfrak{O}={\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\times\{0\}
and
D_{0}r(w)=(0,w)
for all
w\in W\simeq T_{0}W
.

If
g\in C^{\infty}(Y)
is
T
-invariant, then
\chi^{*}g
is constant along the fibers of
\pi
. Thus
\chi^{*}g=\pi^{*}(r^{*}g)
, and
so
T^{\ell}_{(0,0)}(\chi^{*}g)=\pi^{*}(T^{\ell}_{0}(r^{*}g))
for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Hence,

(6.6)

g\in Q^{\ell}_{p}\text{ exactly if }r^{*}g\in Q^{\ell}_{0}.

Moreover, in this case

j_{p}^{\ell}g=\pi^{*}(j^{\ell}_{0}(r^{*}g))
, and so
j_{p}^{\ell}g
is invariant under addition
by elements of
T_{p}\mathfrak{O}
.
Additionally, by (
6.2
) the jet
j^{\ell}_{p}g
is invariant under the slice representation of
H
.

If
\mathcal{I}
is a subspace of the algebra of
T
-invariant functions in
C^{\infty}(Y)
, then
its pullback
r^{*}\mathcal{I}
is a subspace of the algebra of
H
-invariant functions in
C^{\infty}(V)
.
By the preceding paragraph,
V_{\mathcal{I},p}
is closed under addition by elements in
T_{p}\mathfrak{O}
. Indeed,
V_{\mathcal{I},p}={\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\times V_{r^{*}\mathcal{I},0}
and so

(6.7)

V_{\mathcal{I},p}=D_{0}r(V_{r^{*}\mathcal{I},0})+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}.

Moreover,
V_{\mathcal{I},p}
is invariant under the isotropy representation of
H
.

We are particularly interested in the case that

T
acts on a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
with moment map
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, and

(6.8)

\mathcal{I}_{\Phi}=\{\langle\epsilon,\Phi\rangle\mid\epsilon\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathfrak{t}})\text{ is }T\text{-invariant}\}.

In this case, to simplify notation we use the shorthand “modulo
\Phi
” to mean “modulo
\mathcal{I}_{\Phi}
” and denote the zero locus of the homogenization of
\mathcal{I}_{\Phi}
at
p
by
V_{\Phi,p}
.

The above discussion holds
mutatis mutandis
if we replace the torus
T
with a compact abelian Lie group. This is particularly relevant when studying a local model
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
with moment map
\Phi_{Y}\colon Y\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. In this case, define

R\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\hookrightarrow T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\quad\text{ by }\quad z\mapsto[1,0,z].

If
\Phi_{H}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is the homogeneous moment map for the
H
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
, then

R^{*}\Phi_{Y}=\Phi_{H}\quad\text{and}\quad R^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}=\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{H}}.

Our first important technical lemma concerns this case.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
be a local model with moment map
\Phi_{Y}\colon Y\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, and
let
\Phi_{H}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
be the homogeneous moment map for the
H
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
. If
p:=[1,0,0]
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, then:

(1)

For any
h\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
, the Taylor polynomial
T_{0}^{\ell}(R^{*}h)
vanishes
on
\Phi_{H}^{-1}(0)
;

(2)

V_{\Phi_{Y},p}=D_{0}R(\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0))+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}
, where
\mathfrak{O}
is the
T
-orbit through
p
; and

(3)

A
T
-invariant function
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
vanishes below order
\ell
at
p

modulo
\Phi_{Y}
exactly if
R^{*}g
vanishes below order
\ell
at
0
modulo
\Phi_{H}
.

Proof.

Define
r\colon{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to Y
by
r(\alpha,z)=[1,\alpha,z]
and
\iota\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
by
\iota(z)=(0,z)
, Recall that
\Phi_{Y}([t,\alpha,z])=\alpha+\Phi_{H}(z)
, and so
r^{*}\Phi_{Y}^{\xi}:=r^{*}\langle\Phi_{Y},\xi\rangle
is the
H
-invariant polynomial on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}

sending
(\alpha,z)
to
\langle\alpha+\Phi_{H}(z),\xi\rangle
for any
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
. It is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
2
for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and of degree
1
for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}
. Hence, there exist a basis
\{\xi_{i}\}
for
{\mathfrak{t}}
and non-negative integers
\{d_{i}\}
such that
r^{*}\Phi_{Y}^{\xi_{i}}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d_{i}
for each
i
.

Given
h\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
, there exist
T
-invariant functions

\epsilon_{i}\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
h=\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}\Phi_{Y}^{\xi_{i}}
.
Therefore the Taylor polynomial
T^{\ell}_{(0,0)}(r^{*}h)
is equal to
\sum_{i}(r^{*}\Phi_{Y}^{\xi_{i}})\,T_{(0,0)}^{\ell-d_{i}}(r^{*}\epsilon_{i})\in r^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
,
where
T^{k}_{(0,0)}(r^{*}\epsilon_{i})\equiv 0
if

. Since

,
this implies that
T^{\ell}_{0}(R^{*}h)=\iota^{*}(T^{\ell}_{(0,0)}(r^{*}h))\in R^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}=\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{H}}
,
and so

vanishes on
\Phi_{H}^{-1}(0)
. This proves (
1
).

By the preceding paragraph, every polynomial in the homogenization of
r^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
at
(0,0)
vanishes on
(r^{*}\Phi_{Y})^{-1}(0)=\{0\}\times\Phi_{H}^{-1}(0)
.
Hence,
\{0\}\times\Phi_{H}^{-1}(0)
is contained in
V_{r^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}}
, the zero locus of the homogenization of
r^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
at
(0,0)
.
Conversely, since
r^{*}\Phi_{Y}^{\xi_{i}}\in r^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}\cap\,Q^{d_{i}}_{(0,0)}
and
j^{d_{i}}_{(0,0)}(r^{*}\Phi_{Y}^{\xi_{i}})=r^{*}\Phi_{Y}^{\xi_{i}}
, the polynomial
r^{*}\Phi_{Y}^{\xi_{i}}
is in the homogenization of
r^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
at
(0,0)
for all

. Hence,

V_{r^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}}\subseteq\{0\}\times\Phi_{H}^{-1}(0)
.
Therefore, by (

)

V_{\Phi_{Y},p}=D_{(0,0)}r(\{0\}\times\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0))+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}=D_{0}R(\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0))+T_{p}\mathfrak{O},

and so (
2
) holds.

Finally, let
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
be
T
-invariant. Assume that there exists
h\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
such that
R^{*}(g-h)\in Q^{\ell}_{0}
. Then
0=T_{0}^{\ell-1}(R^{*}(g-h))=\iota^{*}T_{(0,0)}^{\ell-1}(r^{*}(g-h))
,
and so the Taylor polynomial
T_{(0,0)}^{\ell-1}(r^{*}(g-h))
lies in the ideal of
H
-invariant functions generated by
\{r^{*}\Phi^{\xi}_{Y}\mid\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\}
. Hence, there exists

such that
r^{*}f=T_{(0,0)}^{\ell-1}(r^{*}(g-h))
.
So by replacing

by
h-f
, we may assume that
T_{(0,0)}^{\ell}(r^{*}(g-h))=0

and so
r^{*}(g-h)\in Q^{\ell}_{0}
. By (
6.6
), this implies that
g-h\in Q^{\ell}_{0}
.
Therefore, (
3
) follows from (
6.2
).
∎

The proofs of parts (

) and (
2
) of Lemma

rely heavily on homogeneity of the components of

. As shown below, analogous statements fail without this homogeneity. In contrast, the proof of part (

) of Lemma

only relies on the fact that

contains the ideal of

-invariant polynomials generated by

.

Example 6.10
.

Define
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by
\Phi(x,y)=x+y^{2}
. Then

, which does not vanish on

. Similarly,
V_{\Phi}=\{0\}\times{\mathbb{R}}
is not equal to
\Phi^{-1}(0)
.

Next we show that we can determine if a
T
-invariant function on a tall local model
Y
vanishes below a sufficiently small order modulo
\Phi_{Y}
by computing the reduced Taylor polynomial.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
be a tall local model with moment map
\Phi_{Y}\colon Y\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
and defining polynomial of degree
N
. Fix
0<\ell\leq N
. A
T
-invariant function
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
vanishes below order
\ell
at
p:=[1,0,0]
modulo
\Phi_{Y}
if and only if the reduced Taylor polynomial
\overline{T^{\ell-1}_{p}g}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is identically zero.

Proof.

Assume that there exists
h\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
such that
g-h\in Q^{\ell}_{p}
. Then
R^{*}(g-h)\in Q^{\ell}_{0}
by (
6.2
), and so
T^{\ell-1}_{0}(R^{*}g)=T^{\ell-1}_{0}(R^{*}h)
. By Lemma
6.9
, part (
1
), the Taylor polynomial
T^{\ell-1}_{0}(R^{*}h)
vanishes on
\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0)
.
Hence, the reduced Taylor polynomial
\overline{T^{\ell-1}_{p}g}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is identically zero.

Conversely, assume that reduced Taylor polynomial
\overline{T^{\ell-1}_{p}g}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is identically zero.
Since
T^{\ell-1}_{0}(R^{*}g)
is an
H
-invariant polynomial on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
of degree less than
\ell
,
there exists a unique
T
-invariant polynomial
G
on
Y
of degree less than
\ell

such that
G[t,\alpha,z]=T^{\ell-1}_{0}(R^{*}g)(z)
; in particular,

\overline{G}\colon{Y/\!/T}\to R

is identically zero. We claim that
G\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
. To see this, recall that by
[
ST1
, Lemma 2.11]
the algebra of
T
-invariant polynomials on
Y

is generated by the real and imaginary parts of the defining polynomial
P\colon Y\to{\mathbb{C}}
, the map
[t,\alpha,z]\mapsto|z|^{2}
, and the components of
\Phi_{Y}
. Since the degree of
G
is less than
N
, this implies that

G
lies in the algebra of
T
-invariant polynomials generated by the map
[t,\alpha,z]\mapsto|z|^{2}
and the components of the moment map
\Phi_{Y}
. Hence, we can write
G
as the sum of an element in the ideal generated by the components of
\Phi_{Y}
and the map
[t,\alpha,z]\mapsto Q(|z|^{2})
for some polynomial
Q
. Recall that
\Phi_{Y}([t,\alpha,z])=\alpha+\Phi_{H}(z)
, where
\Phi_{H}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is the homogeneous moment map for the
H
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
. Hence, since
Y
is tall, there exists
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that
[1,0,w]\in\Phi_{Y}^{-1}(0)
. Therefore the element
[1,0,\tau w_{0}]
lies in
\Phi_{Y}^{-1}(0)
for all
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and so the restriction of the function
[t,\alpha,z]\mapsto|z|^{2}
to
\Phi_{Y}^{-1}(0)
attains every non-negative number. Hence,
Q(|z|^{2})=0
for all
[t,0,z]\in\Phi_{Y}^{-1}(0)
exactly if
Q\equiv 0
, and so
G\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}.

Since
G\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
, we have that
T^{\ell-1}_{0}(R^{*}g)=R^{*}G\in R^{*}\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}=\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{H}}
, and so
R^{*}g
vanishes below order
\ell
at
0
modulo
\Phi_{H}
. By Lemma
6.9
, part (
3
), this implies that
g
vanishes below order
\ell
at
p
modulo
\Phi_{Y}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a subspace of
C^{\infty}(M)
and let
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
vanish below order
\ell
at
p\in M
modulo
\mathcal{I}
. The
jet of
\bm{g}
of order
\bm{\ell}
at
\bm{p}
modulo
\bm{\mathcal{I}}
is the function
j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\mathcal{I})\colon V_{\mathcal{I},p}\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

(6.12)

j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\mathcal{I})(v):=j^{\ell}_{p}(g-h)(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in V_{\mathcal{I},p},

where
h\in\mathcal{I}
and
g-h\in Q^{\ell}_{p}
. This function does not depend on
h
because if
\tilde{h}\in\mathcal{I}
and
g-\tilde{h}\in Q^{\ell}_{p}
, then
h-\tilde{h}\in Q^{\ell}_{p}\cap\mathcal{I}
and so

j^{\ell}_{p}(g-\tilde{h})-j^{\ell}_{p}(g-h)=j^{\ell}_{p}(h-\tilde{h})

vanishes identically on
V_{\mathcal{I},p}
.

Now assume that
T
acts on
M
, that
\mathcal{I}
is a subspace of the algebra of
T
-invariant smooth functions, and that
g
is
T
-invariant. If
g
vanishes below order
\ell
at
p
modulo
\mathcal{I}
, then
j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\mathcal{I})
is invariant under addition by elements of
T_{p}\mathfrak{O}
and under the slice representation of the stabilizer
H
of
p
. Hence, the function
j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\mathcal{I})
induces a map
\overline{j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\mathcal{I})}\colon(V_{\mathcal{I},p}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O})/H\to{\mathbb{R}}
by setting

(6.13)

\overline{j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\mathcal{I})}([v+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}]):=j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\mathcal{I})(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in V_{\mathcal{I},p}.

In the context of a
T
-action on a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
with moment map
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, to simplify notation, we denote the jet of
g
of order
\ell
at
p
modulo
\Phi
by
j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\Phi)
. As we show below, on a local model we can calculate this jet by computing
the reduced Taylor polynomial.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
Y=T\times_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
be a local model with moment map
\Phi_{Y}\colon Y\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. Let
\mathfrak{O}
be the
T
-orbit through
p:=[1,0,0]
. There is a homeomorphism from

(V_{\Phi_{Y},p}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O})/H
to
Y/\!/T
that identifies
\overline{j_{p}^{\ell}(g;\Phi_{Y})}
and
\overline{T^{\ell}_{p}g}

for every
T
-invariant function
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
that vanishes below order
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
at
p
modulo
\Phi_{Y}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.9
, part (
2
), the map
\Phi_{H}^{-1}(0)/H\to(V_{\Phi_{Y},p}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O})/H
given by
[z]\mapsto[D_{0}R(z)+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}]
is a homeomorphism.
Since the map
{Y/\!/T}\to\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0)/H
given by
[t,0,z]\mapsto[z]
is also a homeomorphism, this implies that the map
{Y/\!/T}\to(V_{\Phi_{Y},p}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O})/H
given by
[t,0,z]\mapsto[D_{0}R(z)+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}]
is a homeomorphism.

If
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
T
-invariant function that vanishes below order
\ell
at
p
modulo
\Phi_{Y}
, then there exists
h\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}}
such that
g-h\in Q^{\ell}_{p}
. Hence, for any
[t,0,z]\in{Y/\!/T}
,

\begin{split}\overline{T^{\ell}_{p}g}([t,0,z])&=T^{\ell}_{0}(R^{*}g)(z)=T^{\ell}_{0}(R^{*}g-R^{*}h)(z)\\
&=j^{\ell}_{0}(R^{*}(g-h))(z)=j^{\ell}_{p}(g-h)(D_{0}R(z))\\
&=\overline{j^{\ell}_{p}(g;\mathcal{I}_{\Phi_{Y}})}([D_{0}R(z)+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}]),\end{split}

where the first equality follows from the definition of reduced Taylor polynomial; the second from
the facts that
z\in\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0)
and the Taylor polynomial
T^{\ell}_{0}(R^{*}h)
vanishes on
\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0)

by Lemma
6.9
, part (
1
); the third from the fact that
R^{*}(g-h)
lies in
Q^{\ell}_{0}
by (
6.2
); the fourth from (
6.2
); and the last from (
6.12
) and (
6.13
).
∎

By combining Lemmas
6.11
and
6.14
we obtain the main result of this section: an intrinsic characterization of ephemeral points.

Proposition 6.15
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g)\right)
be an integrable system such that
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is a complexity one
T
-space. Assume that
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)\cap g^{-1}(0)
is tall and that the degree
N
of the defining polynomial of the local model for
p
is greater than one. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

p
is ephemeral, i.e., conditions (a) and (b) of Definition
1.8
hold for some a
T
-equivariant symplectomorphism from a
T
-invariant neighborhood of
p
to an open subset of
Y
that takes
p
to
[1,0,0]
,

(2)

conditions (a) and (b) of Definition
1.8
hold for every
T
-equivariant symplectomorphism from
T
-invariant neighborhood of
p
to an open subset of
Y
that takes
p
to
[1,0,0]
, and

(3)

g
vanishes below order
N
at
p
modulo
\Phi
and the zero set of
\overline{j_{p}^{N}(g;\Phi)}\colon(V_{\Phi}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O})/H\to{\mathbb{R}}
is homeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

By the Marle-Guillemin-Sternberg local normal form theorem,
(1)
is an immediate consequence of
(2)
.
By the naturality of our constructions (see (
6.2
) and (
6.4
)),
the implications
(1)

\Rightarrow

(3)
and
(3)

\Rightarrow

(2)
follow immediately from Lemmas
6.11
and
6.14
.
∎

Finally, we show how to explicitly check whether the intrinsic criteria
described in Proposition
6.15
, part
(3)
are satisfied.
As we see in Section
7
, this allows us to determine in practice which points are ephemeral.

Lemma 6.16
.

Let
(M,\omega,\Phi)
be a complexity one
T
-space. Fix
p\in\Phi^{-1}(0)
.

(A)

If
\mathfrak{O}
is the
T
-orbit through
p
and
H
is the stabilizer of
p
, then

(V_{\Phi,p}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O})/H=\hat{\Phi}^{-1}(0)/H,

where
\hat{\Phi}\colon(T_{p}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathfrak{O}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is the homogeneous
moment map for the symplectic slice representation.

(B)

Let
g\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a
T
-invariant function that vanishes below order
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
at
p
modulo
\Phi
. Then for any
h\in\mathcal{I}_{\Phi}
such that
g-h\in Q^{\ell}_{p}
and for any coordinate chart
\varphi
centered at
p

\overline{j_{p}^{\ell}(g;\mathcal{I})}([v+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}])=T_{\varphi(p)}^{\ell}(g\circ\varphi^{-1}\!-\!h\circ\varphi^{-1})(D_{p}\varphi(v))

for all
v\in V_{\Phi,p}
.

Proof.

Since
V_{\Phi,p}
and
\hat{\Phi}
are both natural with respect to
T
-equivariant
symplectomorphisms that intertwine moment maps, by the Marle-Guillemin-Sternberg local normal form theorem
we may assume that
M
is a local model

,
\Phi=\Phi_{Y}
, and
p=[1,0,0]
.
We may identify
T_{p}Y
with
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\times{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
, equipped with the product of the standard symplectic forms on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\times{\mathfrak{h}}^{\circ}\subseteq{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
. Since

, this implies that
(T_{p}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}={\mathfrak{h}}^{\perp}\times\{0\}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{h+1}
. Hence,

. Therefore,

and so

\hat{\Phi}^{-1}(0)=D_{0}R(\Phi_{H}^{-1}(0))+T_{p}\mathfrak{O}.

Part (

) now follows from Lemma
6.9
, part (

). Part (

), (

) and (

7.
A family of examples

In this section we explicitly construct a natural family of integrable
systems that satisfy the criteria of the main result of this paper, Theorem
1.10
, but do not satisfy the criteria of
[
ST1
, Theorem 1.6]
because they have tall degenerate singular points. This shows that allowing ephemeral singular points meaningfully
extends the integrable systems that we can study.

Let the
(n-1)
-dimensional torus
T
act on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
via an injective homomorphism
\rho\colon T\to(S^{1})^{n}
with proper moment map
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
.
By Lemma
2.1
, there exists
\xi\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that
\lambda\in\rho(T)\varsubsetneq(S^{1})^{n}
exactly if
\prod_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\xi_{i}}=1
. Define a function
g\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

g(z)={\mathfrak{I}}\left(\,\prod_{\xi_{i}>0}z_{i}^{\xi_{i}}\prod_{\xi_{i}<0}\overline{z}_{i}^{\,-\xi_{i}}\right),

where the first (respectively second) product is over all
i
such that
\xi_{i}>0
(respectively
\xi_{i}<0
).

Proposition 7.1
.

The quadruple
({\mathbb{C}}^{n},\omega_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
is an integrable system such that
({\mathbb{C}}^{n},\omega_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},\Phi)
is a proper complexity one
T
-space. Moreover, each tall singular point in
({\mathbb{C}}^{n},\omega_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},{\mathfrak{t}}\times{\mathbb{R}},f:=(\Phi,g))
either has purely elliptic type or is ephemeral;
if

|\sum_{i\in I}\xi_{i}|>2
for some
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
, then the system has degenerate ephemeral singular points.

Proof.

Since
g
is
T
-invariant by construction, and
\Phi
is a proper moment map for the
T
-action,
f
is the moment map of a
(T\times{\mathbb{R}})
-action.

Consider a point
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Write
w_{j}=(r_{j},\theta_{j})
in polar coordinates for all
j
, and
let

(7.2)

I:=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\mid w_{i}=0\}.

We begin by summarizing our claims about
w
, which we prove below:
First, if
I=\emptyset
, then
w
is a singular point of
f
exactly if (
7.6
) and (
7.7
) hold;
in particular,
f
is regular on an open dense set in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
More generally,

w
is tall exactly if
\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq 0
for all
i,j\in I
;
assume that
w
is singular and that this is the case.
If
\sum_{i\in I}|\xi_{i}|\leq 1
then
w
has purely elliptic type.
In contrast,
if
\sum_{i\in I}|\xi_{i}|>1
then
w
is an ephemeral point;
while if
\sum_{i\in I}|\xi_{i}|>2
, then
w
is a degenerate point of
f
. Together these claims imply the result.

Fix
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, and set

(7.3)

\begin{split}(S^{1})^{I}&:=\{\lambda\in(S^{1})^{n}\mid\lambda_{j}=1\text{ for all }j\notin I\}\quad\text{and}\\
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}&:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\mid z_{j}=0\text{ for all }j\notin I\},\end{split}

where
I
is defined as in (
7.2
).
To prove the above claims, we split the proof in two cases, depending on whether
\sum_{i\in I}|\xi_{i}|
vanishes or not.

Case 1:
\sum_{i\in I}|\xi_{i}|=0
.
Since
\xi_{i}=0
if
i\in I
, we may write

(7.4)

\Phi(w)=\textstyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\eta_{j}r_{j}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g(w)=\bigg(\prod\limits_{j\notin I}r_{j}^{|\xi_{j}|}\bigg)\sin\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\notin I}\xi_{j}\theta_{j}\bigg),

where
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
are the isotropy weights for the
T
-action. Hence,

(7.5a)

\displaystyle D_{w}\Phi

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{j}\eta_{j}r_{j}dr_{j},

(7.5b)

\displaystyle{\operatorname{ker}}\,D_{w}\Phi

\displaystyle={\mathbb{C}}^{I}\oplus\operatorname{span}\bigg(\big\{\partial_{\theta_{j}}\mid j\notin I\big\}\cup\bigg\{\textstyle\sum\limits_{j\notin I}\frac{\xi_{j}}{r_{j}}\partial_{r_{j}}\bigg\}\bigg),\text{ and}

(7.5c)

\displaystyle d_{w}g

\displaystyle=\textstyle g(w)\bigg(\!\sum\limits_{j\notin I}\frac{|\xi_{j}|}{r_{j}}dr_{j}\bigg)+\bigg(\prod\limits_{j\notin I}r_{j}^{|\xi_{j}|}\bigg)\!\cos\!\bigg(\!\sum\limits_{j\notin I}\xi_{j}\theta_{j}\!\bigg)\!\!\sum\limits_{j\notin I}\xi_{j}d\theta_{j}.

Here, we identify
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
with the span of
\{{\partial}_{x_{i}},{\partial}_{y_{i}}\mid i\in I\}
.
Since
\xi_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\notin I
, the differential
d_{w}g
vanishes
on the span of
\{\partial_{\theta_{j}}\mid j\not\in I\}
exactly if

(7.6)

\textstyle\cos\bigg(\sum\limits_{j\notin I}\xi_{j}\theta_{j}\bigg)=0.

Moreover, if equation (
7.6
) holds, then
g(w)\neq 0
. Hence,
w
is a critical point of
g
modulo
\Phi
, that is, the derivative
d_{w}g
vanishes on
{\operatorname{ker}}D_{w}\Phi
, if and only if (
7.6
) holds and

(7.7)

\sum\limits_{j\notin I}\frac{\xi_{j}|\xi_{j}|}{r_{j}^{2}}=0.

Furthermore, if
I=\emptyset
then
D_{w}\Phi
has rank
n-1
, and so
w
is singular for
f
exactly if
w
is a critical point of
g
modulo
\Phi
,
that is, exactly if (
7.6
) and (
7.7
) hold.

More generally, let
H
be the
T
-stabilizer of
w
and let
\mathfrak{O}
be the
T
-orbit through
w
. Since
\sum_{i\in I}|\xi_{i}|=0
, we have
H=(S^{1})^{I}
and

T_{w}\mathfrak{O}=\big\{\sum_{j\notin I}a_{j}{\partial}_{\theta_{j}}\ \big|\ \sum_{j\notin I}\xi_{j}a_{j}=0\big\}.

Since
(T_{w}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}={\operatorname{ker}}D_{w}\Phi
,
(
7.5b
) implies that,
as symplectic vector spaces,

(7.8)

(T_{w}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}/T_{w}\mathfrak{O}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{I}\oplus\operatorname{span}\bigg(\sum_{j\notin I}\xi_{j}{\partial}_{\theta_{j}},\sum_{j\notin I}\frac{\xi_{j}}{r_{j}}\partial_{r_{j}}\bigg)\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{I}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Under this identification,
H
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
by an isomorphism with
(S^{1})^{I}

and trivially on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. In particular,
w
is tall
by Lemma
2.1
.

Now assume that
w
be a singular point for
f
. By Lemma
2.6
, if
w
is regular for
g
modulo
\Phi
, then
w
has purely elliptic type. Hence we may assume that
w
is a critical point of
g
modulo
\Phi
. Since
d_{w}g
vanishes on
{\operatorname{ker}}D_{w}\Phi
, there exists
\mu\in{\mathfrak{t}}
such that
\tilde{g}:=g-\Phi^{\mu}
has vanishing derivative at
w
. By (
7.5a
),

\textstyle d_{w}\Phi^{\mu}=\sum_{j}\langle\eta_{j},\mu\rangle r_{j}dr_{j}=\sum_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{\mu}}{\partial r^{2}_{j}}(w)r_{j}dr_{j}.

Since
d_{w}g=d_{w}\Phi^{\mu}
, by (
7.5c
) and (
7.6
) this implies that
\textstyle\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{\mu}}{\partial r_{j}^{2}}(w)=\textstyle\frac{|\xi_{j}|}{r^{2}_{j}}g(w)
for all
j\notin I
. Moreover, all other components of the Hessian
d^{2}_{w}\Phi^{\mu}
vanish. Hence, by (
7.5c
) and (
7.6
), the only possibly non-zero components of the Hessian
d^{2}_{w}\tilde{g}=d^{2}_{w}g-d^{2}_{w}\Phi^{\mu}
are given by

(7.9)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{g}}{\partial\theta_{j}\partial\theta_{k}}(w)&=\textstyle-\xi_{j}\xi_{k}g(w),\\
\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{g}}{\partial r_{j}\partial r_{k}}(w)&=\frac{|\xi_{j}||\xi_{k}|}{r_{j}r_{k}}g(w)-2\delta_{jk}\frac{|\xi_{j}|}{r^{2}_{j}}g(w),\end{split}

where
\delta_{jk}
is the Kronecker delta and
j,k\notin I
.

Since
w
is a critical point of
g
modulo
\Phi
, the
(T\times{\mathbb{R}})
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
through
w
is equal to the
T
-orbit
\mathfrak{O}
, and so (
7.8
) also describes
(T_{w}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{w}\mathcal{O}
as a symplectic vector space. We may replace
f=(\Phi,g)
by
\tilde{f}:=(\Phi,\tilde{g})
because this does not change our analysis. Then the
(T\times{\mathbb{R}})
-stabilizer of
w
is
H\times{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, since
H
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
by an isomorphism with
(S^{1})^{I}
with homogeneous moment map
\Phi_{H}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{I}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and acts trivially on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the homogeneous moment map for the linearized action of
H\times{\mathbb{R}}
at
w
can be identified with the restriction of
(\Phi_{H},d^{2}_{w}\tilde{g})
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Finally, since
\Phi_{H}
(respectively
d^{2}_{w}\tilde{g}
) only depends on coordinates of the first (respectively second) summand, and since
H
acts in a toric fashion on
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
, it suffices to consider the restriction of
d^{2}_{w}\tilde{g}
to
\operatorname{span}(\sum_{j\notin I}\xi_{j}{\partial}_{\theta_{j}},\sum_{j\notin I}\frac{\xi_{j}}{r_{j}}\partial_{r_{j}})\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

By (
7.7
) and (
7.9
), this Hessian is the
2\times 2
diagonal matrix
A
with non-zero entries

\begin{split}d^{2}_{w}\tilde{g}\bigg(\sum_{j\notin I}\xi_{j}\partial_{\theta_{j}},\sum_{k\notin I}\xi_{k}\partial_{\theta_{k}}\bigg)&=-\bigg(\sum_{j\notin I}\xi^{2}_{j}\bigg)^{2}g(w),\\
d^{2}_{w}\tilde{g}\bigg(\sum_{j\notin I}\frac{\xi_{j}}{r_{j}}\partial_{r_{j}},\sum_{k\notin I}\frac{\xi_{k}}{r_{k}}\partial_{r_{k}}\bigg)&=\bigg(\sum_{j\notin I}\frac{|\xi_{j}|\xi_{j}}{r_{j}^{2}}\Big)^{2}g(w)-2\bigg(\sum_{j\notin I}\frac{\xi_{j}^{2}|\xi_{j}|}{r_{j}^{4}}\bigg)g(w)\\
&=-2\bigg(\sum_{j\notin I}\frac{\xi_{j}^{2}|\xi_{j}|}{r_{j}^{4}}\bigg)g(w).\end{split}

Since
\xi\neq 0
and since
g(w)\neq 0
by (
7.6
), these diagonal entries are non-zero and have the same sign. Hence, if
J=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)
, then the product
JA
has distinct non-zero imaginary eigenvalues. Hence,
w
has purely elliptic type; see, e.g.,
[
ST1
, Remark 6.1]
.

Case 2:
N:=\sum_{i\in I}|\xi_{i}|>0
.

Let
H
be the
T
-stabilizer of
w
and let
\mathfrak{O}
be the
T
-orbit through
w
.
Since there exists
i\in I
with
\xi_{i}\neq 0
,
the map
(S^{1})^{I}\to S^{1}
taking
\lambda
to
\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}^{\xi_{i}}
is surjective. Hence,
\dim T/H=(n-1)-(|I|-1)=|I|-n,
and so the tangent space
T_{w}\mathfrak{O}
is the span of
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}\mid j\notin I\}
. Then, as symplectic vector spaces

(7.10)

(T_{w}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}/T_{w}\mathfrak{O}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{I},

where
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
is defined as in (
7.3
). Moreover,
H

acts on
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
through an
isomorphism with the subgroup

\bigg\{\lambda\in(S^{1})^{I}\,\bigg|\,\prod_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}^{\xi_{i}}=1\bigg\}.

Hence, by Lemma
2.1
,
w
is tall
exactly if
\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq 0
for all
i,j\in I
.

Since we are interested only in tall singular points, we may assume that
\xi_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in I
; the argument when
\xi_{i}\leq 0
for all
i
is entirely analogous.
In this case, the defining polynomial of the local model for w is given by

P(z)=\prod_{i\in I}z_{i}^{\xi_{i}}
, and has degree
N
. Moreover, every partial derivative of
g
of degree less than
N
vanishes at
w
. Moreover,
\prod_{j\notin I}w_{j}^{\xi^{+}_{j}}\overline{w}_{j}^{\xi^{-}_{j}}\neq 0
, where
\xi^{+}_{j}=\operatorname{max}\{0,\xi_{j}\}
and
\xi^{-}_{j}=\operatorname{max}\{0,-\xi_{j}\}
for any
j\notin I
. Hence, there are precisely two partial derivatives of
g
with respect to
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
and
\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{n}
of degree
N
that do not vanish at
w
:

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{N}g}{\partial^{\xi_{i_{1}}}z_{i_{1}}\ldots\partial^{\xi_{i_{k}}}z_{i_{k}}}(w)&=-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\prod\limits_{i\in I}\xi_{i}!\prod\limits_{j\notin I}w_{j}^{\xi_{j}^{+}}\overline{w}_{j}^{\xi_{j}^{-}},\\
\frac{\partial^{N}g}{\partial^{\xi_{i_{1}}}\overline{z}_{i_{1}}\ldots\partial^{\xi_{i_{k}}}\overline{z}_{i_{k}}}(w)&=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\prod\limits_{i\in I}\xi_{i}!\prod\limits_{j\notin I}\overline{w}_{j}^{\xi_{j}^{+}}w_{j}^{\xi_{j}^{-}},\end{split}

where
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
. Therefore the degree
N
Taylor polynomial of
g
at
w
is given by

(7.11)

\textstyle T^{N}_{w}g(z)={\mathfrak{I}}\left(\prod\limits_{j\notin I}w_{j}^{\xi^{+}_{j}}\overline{w}_{j}^{\xi^{-}_{j}}P(z)\right)\quad\forall\,z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}.

By (
7.10
), we may identify
the symplectic slice
(T_{w}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}/T_{w}\mathfrak{O}
with
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
so that the homogeneous moment map for the symplectic slice representation
\hat{\Phi}\colon(T_{w}\mathfrak{O})^{\omega}/T_{w}\mathfrak{O}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
becomes the homogeneous moment map
\Phi_{H}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{I}\to{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
for the
H
-action on
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
.
If
N=1
, then the restriction of (
7.11
) to the symplectic slice
{\mathbb{C}}^{I}
is a non-trivial linear map. Hence,
w

is a regular point of
g
modulo
\Phi
and so, by Lemma
2.6
,
w
has purely elliptic type. Therefore, we may assume that
N>1
.
By (
7.11
) the function
g
vanishes below order
N
at
w
. Hence, replace
\Phi
by
\Phi-\Phi(w)
, and then
apply Lemma
6.16
, part (
B
) with the identity as a coordinate chart, and
h\equiv 0
; by equation (
7.11
), the jet of
g
of order
N
at
w
modulo
\Phi

induces
the map
\overline{j^{N}_{w}(g;\Phi)}\colon(V_{\Phi,w}/T_{w}\mathfrak{O})/H\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

(7.12)

\textstyle\overline{j^{N}_{w}(g;\Phi)}([z])={\mathfrak{I}}\bigg(\prod\limits_{j\notin I}w_{j}^{\xi^{+}_{j}}\overline{w}_{j}^{\xi^{-}_{j}}P(z)\bigg)\quad\forall\,[z]\in(V_{\Phi,w}/T_{w}\mathfrak{O})/H.

By Lemma
6.16
, part (
A
), we have that
(V_{\Phi,w}/T_{w}\mathfrak{O})/H=\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0)/H
; moreover, by Lemma
2.4
the map
\Phi^{-1}_{H}(0)/H\to{\mathbb{C}}
given by
[z]\mapsto P(z)
is a homeomorphism. Using this map to identify
(V_{\Phi,w}/T_{w}\mathfrak{O})/H
with
{\mathbb{C}}
, we may rewrite (
7.12
) as

\overline{j^{N}_{w}(g;\Phi)}(v)={\mathfrak{I}}\bigg(\prod\limits_{j\notin I}w_{j}^{\xi^{+}_{j}}\overline{w}_{j}^{\xi^{-}_{j}}v\bigg)\quad\forall\ v\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Hence, the zero locus of
\overline{j^{N}_{w}(g;\Phi)}
is homeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}
and so, by Proposition
6.15
,
w
is ephemeral.

Finally, if
N>2
, then by (
7.11
) the degree
2
Taylor polynomial of
g
at
w
vanishes on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Hence
w
is a critical point of
g
and so the
(T\times{\mathbb{R}})
-stabilizer of
w
is
H\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{O}
denotes the
(T\times{\mathbb{R}})
-orbit through
w
, then the homogeneous moment map for the linearized action of
{\mathbb{R}}
on
(T_{w}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{w}\mathcal{O}

is trivial. Hence,
w
is a degenerate singular point; see, e.g.,
[
ST1
, Remark 6.1]
.
∎

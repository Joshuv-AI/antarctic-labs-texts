Cost–utility analysis of retinopathy of prematurity screening strategies of universal referral, RetCam telemedicine, and direct examination in Iran: a markov model approach
Authors: Parinaz Alizadeh, Maryam Manaberi, Afsar Dastjani Farahani, Cyrus Alinia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRetinopathy of prematurity (ROP) is a leading cause of preventable childhood blindness, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. In Iran, where the prevalence of ROP among premature infants exceeds 20%, limited access to subspecialist ophthalmologists constrains timely screening. This study evaluated the cost–utility of three alternative ROP screening strategies to inform national policy on optimizing resource allocation.MethodsA lifetime, societal-perspective cost–utility analysis was conducted using a state-transition Markov model. Three strategies were compared: (1) universal referral of all at-risk infants to tertiary centers; (2) RetCam-based nurse-led imaging with remote ophthalmologist review (telemedicine); and (3) local screening by general ophthalmologists with selective referral. Model inputs were derived from national data, published literature, and expert opinion. Costs (2025 Iranian Tomans) and quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) were discounted at 5.8%. Deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analyses were performed to assess parameter uncertainty.ResultsThe Direct Examination scenario (general ophthalmologist screening) had the lowest mean cost (33.52 USD) and effectiveness (16.83 QALYs). RetCam imaging achieved higher effectiveness (17.14 QALYs) at a cost of 122.69 USD, yielding an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) of 288.81 USD per QALY gained, well below Iran’s willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold of USD 7,047. The Universal Referral strategy was dominated, being both more costly and less effective. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis confirmed that RetCam imaging was cost-effective in 95.2% of 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations at a WTP of USD 6,000 per QALY. Diagnostic specificity and long-term vision care costs were the key ICER drivers.ConclusionsRetCam-based telemedicine screening for ROP in Iran is highly cost-effective compared to traditional direct examination or universal referral, offering substantial long-term health gains at an acceptable cost. Scaling up telemedicine screening, supported by quality assurance and training programs, could reduce preventable childhood blindness and improve efficiency of neonatal eye care delivery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-026-14872-w.Keywords: Retinopathy of prematurity, Telemedicine, Cost–utility analysis, Markov model, Quality-adjusted life years, Iran, Screening strategies, Health economics

Introduction

Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) remains a leading cause of preventable childhood blindness, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where survival of very preterm infants has increased but specialized ophthalmologic screening resources remain limited. In Iran, the standard ROP screening strategy relies on referral of at-risk infants to tertiary ophthalmology centers, where indirect ophthalmoscopy is performed by specialists. While this approach ensures high diagnostic accuracy, it faces several limitations, including limited availability of trained ophthalmologists, travel and logistic burdens for families, and variability in adherence to follow-up schedules. Epidemiological studies from Iran reflect this global challenge: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 42 studies covering ~ 18,000 premature infants reported a pooled ROP prevalence of 23.5% (95% CI: 20.4–26.8%), with significant contributions from low birth weight, gestational age, respiratory distress, and blood transfusions as risk factors [1]. In Southeastern Iran, a more recent cohort reported an ROP incidence of 20.2%, and the rate requiring treatment 3.58%, among infants ≤ 2000 g or < 34 weeks gestational age [2].

Early detection and timely treatment of ROP are essential to prevent irreversible visual impairment in preterm infants. In Iran, ROP remains a significant cause of preventable childhood blindness, particularly given the increasing survival of very low birth weight neonates and the variability in neonatal intensive care resources across regions.

The current standard of care relies on repeated bedside retinal examinations performed by ophthalmologists using indirect ophthalmoscopy. However, in the Iranian context, the effectiveness of this approach is often limited by uneven distribution of ROP specialists, delayed or incomplete referrals from peripheral neonatal units, transportation costs for families, and suboptimal adherence to recommended screening schedules.

To address these structural constraints, telemedicine-based screening strategies using digital fundus imaging systems such as RetCam have been introduced and evaluated in several Iranian tertiary centers. These systems enable trained neonatal staff to capture retinal images in NICUs, which are then transmitted to centralized reading centers for interpretation by retinal specialists. Evidence from Iranian pilot and implementation studies indicates that such tele-ROP models can support earlier detection of referral-warranted disease and improve screening coverage, although they are generally intended to complement rather than replace indirect ophthalmoscopy as the diagnostic gold standard [3]. These alternative strategies may reduce costs, lessen family burden, and optimize specialist ophthalmology resources.

Several economic evaluations of ROP screening strategies have demonstrated favorable cost-effectiveness. A systematic review from the US, UK, and Canada reported that telemedicine-inclusive approaches yielded ICERs ranging from approximately £446 to £4,240 per QALY [4]. In Rwanda, a national ROP screening and anti-VEGF treatment program was found to avert blindness and generate net economic savings despite substantial initial equipment and training costs, largely through reduced productivity losses [5]. In parallel, a recent meta-analysis showed that the lifetime costs of blindness due to untreated ROP substantially exceed the costs of screening and early treatment [6]. Nevertheless, evidence from Iran and comparable middle-income settings remains limited, particularly lifetime-horizon, societal-perspective Markov models incorporating locally derived parameters. Notably, head-to-head economic comparisons of universal referral, RetCam-based telemedicine screening, and local screening by non-specialist ophthalmologists, accounting for operational, cost, and diagnostic performance metrics, are lacking in the Iranian context.

In response to the lack of Iran-specific lifetime Markov evaluations, this study conducts a comprehensive cost–utility analysis of three ROP screening and referral strategies: universal referral of all at-risk newborns to tertiary centers; RetCam-based imaging by trained non-specialists with remote ophthalmologist interpretation (telemedicine); and local screening by general ophthalmologists with referral of suspected cases only. Using a lifetime-horizon Markov model from a societal perspective, we estimate incremental costs, QALYs, and ICERs, incorporating both direct medical and indirect costs, including transportation and caregiver burden. The findings aim to inform Iranian health policy, resource allocation, screening guidelines, and decisions regarding the potential scale-up of telemedicine-based ROP screening to reduce preventable vision loss in preterm infants.

Methods

Study design and objective

This study was designed as a cost–utility analysis comparing three alternative strategies for screening and referral of ROP in preterm infants. The target population included newborns at risk of ROP. The analysis evaluated the following screening scenarios:

Scenario 1: Universal referral to a tertiary center. All eligible newborns are directly referred to a specialized ophthalmology clinic in a tertiary hospital, where retinal examinations are performed by a fellowship-trained retinal specialist using standard indirect ophthalmoscopy. This strategy offers maximal diagnostic accuracy and immediate access to treatment when required but is associated with significant logistic and financial burdens, including transportation costs for families, increased workload at tertiary centers, and potential inefficiencies caused by examining infants who may never require treatment.

Scenario 2: RetCam-based nurse-led imaging. In this approach, wide-field retinal images are obtained in the local neonatal unit by a trained nurse or technician using a RetCam imaging device. Images are subsequently reviewed by an ophthalmologist either remotely via telemedicine or locally if available. Only infants with confirmed or suspected ROP on image review are referred for specialist examination and treatment at the referral center. This strategy can reduce unnecessary referrals, lower family travel costs, and improve screening coverage in decentralized settings. Its primary limitations include the high initial and maintenance costs of imaging equipment, the need for continuous staff training, and potential variability in image quality that may influence diagnostic performance.

The primary outcome of the analysis was the cost per QALY gained for each strategy. The analysis was conducted from a societal perspective, incorporating direct medical costs. Reporting of this study follows the CHEERS 2022 statement as recommended by the EQUATOR Network [7].

Model structure and analytical approach

A state-transition Markov model was developed in TreeAge Pro to simulate the lifetime clinical and economic consequences of ROP screening and treatment under each of the three screening scenarios. The model tracks a hypothetical cohort of preterm infants meeting the national risk criteria for ROP screening from birth until death or 110 years of age, consistent with the Iranian life table. The model used a cycle length of one year to reflect clinically meaningful changes in visual status and long-term outcomes.

The model included four mutually exclusive health states:

Good Vision: defined as visual acuity better than 20/60 in the better-seeing eye, corresponding to no or minimal functional limitation.

Infants with treatable ROP who were correctly identified through screening (true positives) were assumed to receive timely treatment (laser photocoagulation and/or anti-VEGF therapy). Treatment effects were modeled by applying relative risk reductions to baseline natural-history transition probabilities. In the base-case analysis, treatment reduced progression to blindness by 50% and progression to visual impairment by 30% during the initial cycle, with lower long-term progression risks thereafter. These assumptions were informed by evidence from the Early Treatment for Retinopathy of Prematurity (ETROP) trial and long-term follow-up from the CRYO-ROP study, which demonstrated improved structural and visual outcomes with timely intervention. Parameter uncertainty was explored in sensitivity analyses [13, 14].

Population and settings

The analysis was conducted using a hypothetical cohort of preterm newborns representative of the population at risk for ROP in Iran. Infants were eligible for inclusion if they met the national ROP screening criteria, defined as a birth weight below 2000 g or gestational age less than 34 weeks. Cohort entry occurred at birth (0 years of age), and the model followed individuals throughout their lifetime to capture long-term health and economic outcomes. Baseline risk distributions for birth weight and gestational age were informed by published national neonatal statistics and prior epidemiological studies of ROP in Iran.

The modeled clinical setting reflected the real-world context of non-eye care neonatal units, hospitals, and perinatal centers across Iran where initial screening and triage decisions are made. Screening was assumed to be implemented according to the three alternative strategies under evaluation, with referrals and treatment provided through tertiary ophthalmology centers equipped for ROP management. This approach allowed the model to estimate population-level costs and health outcomes that are generalizable to the Iranian healthcare system.

Model inputs and data sources

All model parameters are presented in Table 1, with explicit indication of their origin. Where available, local Iranian data were used, including ROP incidence and treatment-requiring proportions [2], age at first screening, and costs of specialist examinations, ambulance services, companion nurse fees, and other local service tariffs. For parameters where local data were unavailable, such as the sensitivity and specificity of screening modalities, estimates were drawn from international studies conducted in operationally comparable settings with similar equipment and personnel training [8–10]. The applicability of these sources was ensured by selecting studies reflecting conditions akin to Iranian neonatal care units and by exploring plausible ranges for each parameter within deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analyses to account for potential variation in local implementation. This approach grounds the model in local evidence while transparently incorporating international data when necessary, and all sources are clearly annotated in Table 1.

Screening accuracy

Screening accuracy was explicitly incorporated into the model by linking strategy-specific sensitivity and specificity to the probability of correct detection of treatable ROP at the initial screening stage, which in turn determined subsequent Markov state transitions. At model entry, infants were assigned a true underlying disease status (treatable ROP vs. no or non-treatable ROP) based on epidemiological incidence parameters. For each screening strategy, sensitivity defined the probability that an infant with treatable ROP was correctly identified and referred for timely treatment (true positive), while specificity defined the probability that an infant without treatable ROP was correctly classified as not requiring referral (true negative). False-negative cases (missed treatable ROP) were assumed to experience delayed or absent treatment and therefore followed natural-history transition probabilities with a higher risk of progression from good vision to visual impairment or blindness. In contrast, true-positive cases received appropriate treatment within the first cycle, resulting in lower subsequent transition probabilities to visual impairment or blindness, consistent with published treatment-effect estimates. False-positive cases incurred additional referral and diagnostic costs but were assumed not to experience excess long-term vision loss beyond background transition risks. These screening-outcome–specific pathways were implemented as decision nodes preceding the Markov process in TreeAge Pro, ensuring that differences in diagnostic performance across strategies directly influenced lifetime health-state occupancy, costs, and QALYs.

Health outcomes (utilities)

Health-related quality-of-life (HRQoL) for each Markov health state was expressed as a utility weight on a scale from 0 (equivalent to death) to 1 (perfect health). The formula yields a continuous mapping between visual function and HRQoL, allowing for consistent valuation of states ranging from normal vision to blindness. Using this approach, the following mean utilities were assigned to the model health states (see Table 1): Good vision = 0.701, Visual impairment = 0.533, Blindness = 0.514, and Death = 0.0. These calculated utilities were validated against published studies on vision-related quality-of-life and found to be within the reported ranges. Beta distributions were assigned to each utility to capture parameter uncertainty, with shape parameters derived from the mean and 95% confidence intervals.

QALYs were calculated within the annual Markov cycles by multiplying the probability of occupying each health state by its associated utility weight and the 1-year cycle length. A half-cycle correction was applied to approximate within-year transitions. Both costs and QALYs were discounted at 5.8% per annum (base case) following national pharmacoeconomic guidelines, with a 4–7.6% range explored in sensitivity analyses. Utilities were not explicitly age-adjusted due to the absence of validated age-specific functions for ROP, but scenario analyses were performed to test the robustness of results to alternative assumptions.

Resource use and costs

This study adopted a societal perspective to comprehensively capture all relevant costs associated with the three ROP screening strategies. All costs are expressed in Iranian Tomans for 2025, using the official NIMA exchange rate (70,000 Tomans per USD), with no additional inflation adjustments required. Cost components included both direct medical costs (screening, diagnostic confirmation, treatment, and follow-up) and indirect non-medical costs to reflect the societal perspective. Indirect costs explicitly incorporated transportation (ambulance services, taxi fares for local ophthalmologist visits), companion nurse fees, and caregiver time associated with attending follow-up visits, providing a comprehensive assessment of the real-world burden on families and the healthcare system.

Screening costs were strategy-specific and included professional fees, equipment purchase and maintenance, consumables, and personnel training. Diagnostic confirmation costs were applied for infants referred to tertiary hospitals for specialist ophthalmology evaluation. Treatment costs covered all recommended interventions for confirmed ROP cases, including laser photocoagulation, intravitreal anti-VEGF injections, and vitreoretinal surgery for severe cases. Post-treatment follow-up costs included repeated ophthalmic examinations during the first year and ongoing surveillance for late complications.

Cost estimation followed a top-down micro-costing approach using official 2025 tariffs published by the Iranian Ministry of Health and Medical Education for public-sector services. Resource use quantities were determined based on national and international ROP guidelines and validated via expert consensus from pediatric ophthalmologists. RetCam imaging capital costs were annualized over five years using straight-line depreciation and divided by expected annual screening volume.

Treatment pathways were modeled within the Markov framework. Infants with treatable ROP received laser therapy or anti-VEGF injections, while those with mild disease or no disease required follow-up only. Progression to visual impairment or blindness generated additional long-term costs for rehabilitation, low-vision care, and counseling. All cost parameters, their uncertainty ranges, and probability distributions used in probabilistic sensitivity analysis are summarized in Table 1, with Gamma distributions applied for skewed cost data.

Parameter uncertainty

Uncertainty in model inputs was systematically explored using both deterministic and probabilistic methods, in accordance with best-practice recommendations and the CHEERS 2022 reporting standards. Deterministic Sensitivity Analysis (DSA) was first conducted to examine the influence of individual parameters on the Incremental ICER. One-way sensitivity analyses varied each parameter across its plausible range, defined by 95% confidence intervals where available or ± 20% of the base-case value when empirical data were unavailable. Parameters tested included the capital cost of RetCam devices, screening sensitivity and specificity for all three strategies, the incidence of ROP and proportion of cases requiring treatment, and health-state utility values. Multi-way analyses explored the joint effects of key parameter pairs such as screening sensitivity and specificity or incidence and treatment uptake rates, given their expected clinical correlation. Results were summarized using tornado diagrams to rank parameters by their impact on ICER estimates and identify key drivers of model outcomes.

Probabilistic Sensitivity Analysis (PSA) was then performed to quantify the joint uncertainty of all parameters simultaneously and to generate a distribution of possible cost-effectiveness outcomes. This approach supports decision-making by providing cost-effectiveness acceptability curves (CEACs) and probabilistic rankings of alternative strategies. PSA inputs were characterized using appropriate parametric distributions: Beta distributions for probabilities, proportions, and utility weights (bounded between 0 and 1), Dirichlet distributions for multinomial transitions (e.g., initial prevalence across vision states), and Gamma distributions for costs to reflect their skewed, non-negative nature. Distribution parameters were derived analytically from reported means and variances (e.g., alpha and beta for Beta distributions; shape and scale for Gamma distributions). Correlations between parameters, particularly between sensitivity and specificity of screening tests, were modeled using joint sampling from a bivariate distribution that preserved their negative correlation, ensuring internally consistent values for each PSA iteration. No correlation was assumed between cost and utility parameters, as these reflect distinct constructs in this model.

Monte Carlo simulations were run with 10,000 iterations, which was determined to be sufficient based on convergence checks and stability of CEACs beyond 8,000 iterations. Each iteration simultaneously sampled all model parameters, producing a distribution of incremental costs, incremental QALYs, and ICERs for each strategy comparison. Outputs from the PSA included: (i) a cost-effectiveness plane (scatterplot) showing joint uncertainty in costs and effects, (ii) a CEAC displaying the probability of cost-effectiveness across a range of willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds, (iii) a cost-effectiveness frontier identifying the strategy with the highest net benefit at each WTP level, and (iv) probabilistic ranking curves (rankograms) showing the likelihood that each scenario represents the most cost-effective option. Numerical results are reported for the Iranian WTP threshold of USD 6000 per QALY gained, as well as at commonly referenced international thresholds, to facilitate interpretation and comparison with other settings.

Model validation and credibility checks

Multiple steps were undertaken to ensure the validity and credibility of the Markov model. Internal validation included extensive debugging and logic checks within TreeAge Pro 2025 to confirm that state transitions, cycle calculations, and cost/QALY accumulations behaved as intended. Face validity was assessed through iterative review by a panel of pediatric ophthalmologists, health economists, and decision-modeling experts to confirm that the model structure and assumptions reflected the real-world clinical pathway of ROP in Iran. External validation was performed by comparing model-predicted outcomes, such as the proportion of infants progressing to treatment-requiring ROP and the expected lifetime prevalence of visual impairment, with published epidemiological data and results from prior cost-effectiveness studies in comparable middle-income settings. The model also underwent cross-checks using extreme value and stress tests (e.g., setting sensitivity or treatment uptake to 0% or 100%) to ensure outputs behaved in a clinically and mathematically plausible manner. Probabilistic model checking tools and visual inspection of PSA outputs were further employed to verify the stability and convergence of results, supporting the robustness of the final conclusions.

Results

Discussion

In this study, using a lifetime-horizon, societal perspective Markov model, we found that RetCam-based nurse-led imaging (telemedicine) screening for ROP yields higher effectiveness, measured in QALYs, than direct examination, at a modest additional cost. The resulting ICER is substantially below Iran’s WTP threshold, indicating that RetCam screening is highly cost-effective. Conversely, the direct referral strategy is dominated, being both more costly and less effective than direct examination. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) reinforces these findings: at a WTP of USD 6000/QALY, RetCam imaging is optimal in over 95% of simulations, confirming that telemedicine screening is likely to provide the best balance of health outcomes and resource use in settings like Iran [19].

Our results aligned with international literature demonstrate the cost-effectiveness of telemedicine and digital imaging-based screening strategies. For instance, Mohammadi et al. (2021) evaluated tele-ROP screening versus no screening in Iran and reported an ICER of USD 161.43 per additional QALY gained for tele-screening [19]. Similarly, Gyllensten et al. (2022) highlighted that the long-term societal costs of blindness far outweigh the costs of screening and treatment, emphasizing the substantial savings and QALY gains achievable with systematic screening [6]. Castillo-Riquelme et al. (2004) in the United Kingdom reported that nurse-led digital screening can reduce per-infant costs under certain deployment models, particularly when portable equipment and efficient logistics are used [20]. Our study extends this evidence by directly comparing three operational strategies, universal referral, telemedicine, and general ophthalmologist triage, using locally relevant epidemiological and cost data, providing robust, policy-relevant guidance for Iran.

Clinical and public health implications

Implementation of RetCam-based nurse-led telemedicine screening may improve coverage in regions with limited access to tertiary ophthalmology centers, while potentially reducing family travel and time burdens, minimizing unnecessary referrals, and allowing specialists to focus on infants requiring advanced treatment. In our model, RetCam was the most cost-effective strategy, providing higher QALYs at a modest additional cost compared with direct examination, whereas the direct referral approach was dominated [19, 20]. The low ICER relative to commonly used WTP thresholds suggests that phased implementation, particularly in high-risk or remote areas, may be economically reasonable. A practical policy approach would be to prioritize deployment in provinces with limited specialist capacity and high neonatal caseloads, accompanied by centralized image review networks.

Sensitivity and specificity of screening modalities were among the most influential drivers of cost-effectiveness. Lower specificity or higher RetCam equipment costs reduced the economic advantage, while deterministic sensitivity analyses also highlighted the impact of long-term costs associated with visual impairment and blindness. These findings indicate that image quality, personnel training, and timely follow-up or treatment are important determinants of both clinical outcomes and economic performance. Accordingly, any scale-up strategy should be accompanied by standardized training, quality assurance systems, and routine performance monitoring.

The base-case sensitivity estimate for RetCam imaging was derived from published studies conducted under controlled conditions and may overestimate routine clinical performance. In practice, factors such as image quality, reader expertise, follow-up adherence, and implementation constraints could reduce diagnostic sensitivity, although sensitivity analyses indicated that the overall cost-effectiveness conclusions were robust across plausible ranges.

Overall, these results provide actionable guidance for Iranian health policy: targeted investment in telemedicine infrastructure, integration of quality assurance protocols, and continuous monitoring can optimize resource allocation, reduce unnecessary referrals, and maximize health gains. The efficiencies gained from implementing cost-effective tele-ROP programs could be reinvested into neonatal care or ROP follow-up services, enhancing child eye health while maintaining fiscal sustainability.

Strengths and limitations

Key strengths of this study include the lifetime horizon capturing the full burden of vision loss, a societal perspective encompassing direct and indirect costs, incorporation of local epidemiological and cost data, and extensive uncertainty analyses (both deterministic and probabilistic). Rigorous model validation, including internal debugging, expert review, and external comparisons with published epidemiological outcomes, further strengthens the credibility and applicability of findings to real policy settings in Iran, and potentially to other middle-income countries with similar resources and ROP incidence.

Limitations include reliance on published literature for some parameters, such as utility weights and long-term transition probabilities, which may differ in Iran due to variations in rehabilitation access, comorbidities, and healthcare utilization patterns. Utility values were derived from published adult visual-acuity conversion formulas because validated pediatric preference-based utility estimates for retinopathy of prematurity were not available. Although caregiver proxy utilities, disability weights from global burden studies, and other indirect sources may reflect broader social burden, they were not used because they are not directly comparable preference-based QALY utilities and may not be readily transferable to the present modeling context. Use of adult-derived utilities may underestimate the full lifelong burden and disutility of childhood-onset blindness, including impacts on psychosocial development, educational attainment, caregiver dependence, and lifetime productivity, and therefore may conservatively underestimate the QALY gains of screening strategies that prevent severe visual loss. As a result, the ICERs reported for more effective screening approaches such as RetCam-based telemedicine may be higher than would be observed if larger blindness-related utility decrements were applied. Non-medical and intangible costs, including psychosocial impacts, caregiver stress, and educational losses, were not fully captured, potentially underestimating the societal burden of vision loss. Operational challenges in RetCam implementation, such as equipment breakdown, delays in image transmission, variable image quality, and personnel retention, may reduce diagnostic performance in practice. Generalizability may be limited to countries with similar healthcare infrastructure, neonatal mortality, and referral networks. Finally, discount rates and willingness-to-pay thresholds influence cost-effectiveness, as shown in sensitivity analyses; results should therefore be interpreted within the context of local policy norms [6, 19, 20].

An additional limitation in this regard is that exclusion of caregiver and lifetime productivity costs means the analysis may not fully capture the broader socioeconomic consequences of childhood blindness. As a result, while this approach avoids reliance on uncertain long-term assumptions, it may underestimate the total societal value of interventions that successfully prevent severe visual impairment and blindness.

Future research

Further research should include real-world implementation studies of RetCam-based telemedicine screening in Iran, evaluating feasibility, image quality, follow-up delays, and acceptability among neonatologists, staff, and families. Comparative studies of AI-assisted digital imaging could provide additional efficiency gains, as AI-based screening may be more cost-effective under certain conditions. Collecting local data on utility values, post-vision-loss quality of life, low-vision care costs, and caregiver burden will enhance model precision. Exploration of hybrid screening models or dynamic resource allocation strategies could optimize resource use, prioritizing high-burden or remote regions.

Conclusions

For Iran, RetCam-based nurse-led telemedicine screening for ROP is the most cost-effective strategy, delivering higher QALYs at modest additional cost and remaining well below typical WTP thresholds. Universal referral of all at-risk infants is inefficient, as it incurs higher costs without improving outcomes. Policymakers should prioritize investment in telemedicine infrastructure, integrate rigorous training and quality assurance programs, and optimize referral pathways. Implementing these measures can substantially reduce preventable blindness in preterm infants while maximizing long-term health and societal benefits.

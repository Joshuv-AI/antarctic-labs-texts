Orbital magnetization and magnetic susceptibility of interacting electrons
Jian Kang, Minxuan Wang, Oskar Vafek

Abstract
We present a rigorous derivation of the orbital magnetization formula for interacting electrons within the self-consistent Hartree-Fock approximation. Our results are expressed entirely in terms of the self-consistent wavefunctions and the Hartree-Fock energy spectrum at zero magnetic field. We test the formula on an interacting Rashba model, finding an agreement with calculations performed at small but non-zero external magnetic field. Our method allows us to also derive formulas for the orbital magnetic susceptibility.

We start with a general interacting Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\int{\rm d}\mathbf{r}\ \psi_{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\left(\hat{H}_{ab}\left(p_{\mu}+\frac{e}{c}A_{\mu}(\mathbf{r})\right)+U_{ab}(\mathbf{r})\right)\psi_{b}(\mathbf{r})+\mathcal{V},

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}=\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}\mathbf{r}{\rm d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\ V_{ab,cd}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\psi_{a}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{c}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\psi_{d}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\psi_{b}(\mathbf{r})

The fermion fields

The interaction term is Hermitian therefore

Note that because we are interested in the orbital contribution, the

Within the HF method the ground state is approximated by a Slater determinant. The total energy is therefore

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle=

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}\mathbf{r}{\rm d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\left[\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\left(\hat{H}_{ab}\left(p_{\mu}+\frac{e}{c}A_{\mu}(\mathbf{r})\right)+U_{ab}(\mathbf{r})\right)P_{ba}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}V_{ab,cd}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\left(P_{ba}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r})P_{dc}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})-P_{da}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r})P_{bc}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\right)\right]

where the single particle density operator is defined as the ground state expectation value

The HF Hamiltonian operator is determined by the action of

on a wavefunction, giving

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{HF}}_{ab}\psi_{b}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\hat{H}_{ab}\left(p_{\mu}+\frac{e}{c}A_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})\right)\psi_{b}(\mathbf{x})

(3)

\displaystyle+\int{\rm d}\mathbf{r}\ V^{\text{HF}}_{ab}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r})\psi_{b}(\mathbf{r}),

\displaystyle V^{\text{HF}}_{ab}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r})=-V_{ad,cb}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{r})P_{dc}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r})+

\displaystyle\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{r})\left(U_{ab}(\mathbf{r})+\int d^{2}\mathbf{y}V_{ab,cd}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})P_{dc}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y})\right).

At self-consistency we can write the density matrix using the spectral representation as

\displaystyle P_{ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi i}G_{ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime};z),

(5)

where we defined the Green’s function

\displaystyle G_{ab}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime};z)=\left\langle\mathbf{r}\right|\left(z-\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{HF}}\right)_{ab}^{-1}\left|\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right\rangle.

(6)

The contour of integration,
\mathcal{C}
, in the Eq.(
5

N

0

The key assumption is that

is held fixed even when

changes by a small amount. This means that it is possible for states to enter or exit the contour, and we must take this effect into account when computing the change of the total energy due to the change of

We proceed by assuming that we have found a self-consistent solution to the HF equations at

The coefficient of the

In order to proceed, we find it convenient to isolate the phase factor associated with the (straight) line integral of the vector potential

where the inverse effective mass operator

(25)

Appendix A
Velocity Operator

In this section, we derive the matrix elements of the velocity operator in the Bloch state representation, i.e.

\displaystyle\langle n,\boldsymbol{k}|\hat{v}^{\text{HF}}_{\mu}|n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\rangle\ .

(26)

Since the ground state at
B=0
is assumed to be translationally invariant, so is the Hartree-Fock Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}
. Therefore, the velocity operator, which is defined as the commutator between
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}
and the position operator, is also translationally invariant. As a consequence,

\displaystyle\langle n,\boldsymbol{k}|\hat{v}^{\text{HF}}_{\mu}|n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\rangle\propto\delta_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\ .

(27)

To derive the expression of the matrix elements of
\hat{v}_{\mu}^{\text{HF}}
, we use the operator
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})=e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}

(28)

where
\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}
is the position operator. Notice

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}|n,\boldsymbol{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})|n,\boldsymbol{k}\rangle=e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})|u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle\mbox{Also},\quad\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}|n,\boldsymbol{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}|u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle=e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})|u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\ .

(29)

Therefore,
|u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle
is the eigenstate of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})
with the energy of
E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})
.

Now, the matrix elements of the velocity operator are

\displaystyle\quad\langle n,\boldsymbol{k}|\hat{v}^{\text{HF}}_{\mu}|n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle n,\boldsymbol{k}|[\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}},\hat{r}_{\mu}]|n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}|e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}[\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}},\hat{r}_{\mu}]e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}|u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{\mu}}\left(e^{-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}\right)\right|u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\left|\frac{\partial\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})}{\partial k_{\mu}}\right|u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{\mu}}\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})|u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle-\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\left|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})\right|u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\right\rangle-\left\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\left|\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})\right|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})}{\partial k_{\mu}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}-\frac{E_{n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k})}{\hbar}\left\langle\left.\frac{u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\right|u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\right\rangle-\frac{E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})}{\hbar}\left\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\left|\frac{u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\right.\right\rangle

(30)

In addition, we found that

\left\langle\left.\frac{u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\right|u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\right\rangle=\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{\mu}}\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}|u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle-\left\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\left|\frac{u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\right.\right\rangle=-\left\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\left|\frac{u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\right.\right\rangle\ .

(31)

Thus, the matrix elements of the velocity operator can be written as

\displaystyle\langle n,\boldsymbol{k}|\hat{v}^{\text{HF}}_{\mu}|n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\rangle=\delta_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})}{\partial k_{\mu}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}+\frac{E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})-E_{n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k})}{\hbar}\left\langle u_{n,\boldsymbol{k}}\left|\frac{u_{n^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\right.\right\rangle\right)\ .

(32)

Appendix B
Derivation of magnetization

In this section, we present the necessary steps to derive the final expression of the orbital magnetization
M
. In the main text, we have already argued that

\displaystyle M=\frac{e}{2i\hbar c}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime}}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})-\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle\left(n_{F}(E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}))-n_{F}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k}))\right)\ ,

(33)

and that the factor
E_{n}(\mathbf{k})-\mu
in the above formula can be replaced by
\frac{1}{2}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})+E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})-2\mu)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{4i\hbar c}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime}}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})+E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})-2\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle\left(n_{F}(E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}))-n_{F}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k}))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{4i\hbar c}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime}}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})+E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})-2\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}))

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{e}{4i\hbar c}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime}}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})+E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})-2\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k}))\ .

(34)

The first term in the above formula can be simplified as

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime}}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})+E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})-2\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}))

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n,n^{\prime}}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})+E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})-2\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\bigg|u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}))

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{n}2(E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})-\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k}))

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n^{\prime}}\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\bigg|(E_{n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k})+\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})-2\mu)\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{k}))

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{n}2(E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})-\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k}))\ .

(35)

where Eq. (
31

34

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime}}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k})+E_{n^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k})-2\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n^{\prime},\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k}))

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n}\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\bigg|(E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})+\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\boldsymbol{k})-2\mu)\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k}))

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{n}2(E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k})-\mu)\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\right\rangle\left\langle u_{n,\mathbf{k}}\bigg|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n}(\mathbf{k}))\ .

(36)

Substituting Eqs. (
35

36

34

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2i\hbar c}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\sum_{n}\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\alpha}}\left|\mathcal{H}^{(0)\text{HF}}(\mathbf{k})+E_{n}(\mathbf{k})-2\mu\right|\frac{\partial u_{n,\mathbf{k}}}{\partial k_{\beta}}\right\rangle n_{F}(E_{n}(\boldsymbol{k}))\ ,

(37)

Appendix C
Contour integral

In the main text, we have stated that the term

\displaystyle I=\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\left(E_{n}-\mu\right)\langle n|\hat{G}^{(0)}(z)\left.\frac{{\rm d}\hat{\widetilde{V}}^{\text{HF}}}{{\rm d}B}\right|_{B=0}\hat{G}^{(0)}(z)|n\rangle

(38)

vanishes due to the factor
E_{n}-\mu
. In this section, we provide the explicit calculation for this statement.

As mentioned in the text, the contour
\mathcal{C}
is chosen to encircle a part of the real axis, so that all the Hartree-Fock energies below the Fermi surface are inside this contour. The integrated function in Eq.
38
contains an order-
2
pole at
z=E_{n}
because of the two Green functions. If the energy is below (or above) the Fermi surface, this pole is inside (or outside) the contour, and thus the contour integral vanishes simply by Cauchy’s integral theorem. If the energy
E_{n}=\mu
is at the Fermi surface, this pole is at the contour. For this case, we consider the Cauchy principal value of this integral, i.e.

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\left(E_{n}-\mu\right)\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\int_{-\infty-i\delta}^{\mu-i\delta}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi i}\ +\int_{\mu+i\delta}^{\infty+i\delta}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi i}\ \right)\frac{1}{(z-E_{n})^{2}}\langle n|\left.\frac{{\rm d}\hat{\widetilde{V}}^{\text{HF}}}{{\rm d}B}\right|_{B=0}|n\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{n}-\mu}{2\pi i}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(-\frac{1}{\mu-i\delta-E_{n}}+\frac{1}{\mu+i\delta-E_{n}}\right)\langle n|\left.\frac{{\rm d}\hat{\widetilde{V}}^{\text{HF}}}{{\rm d}B}\right|_{B=0}|n\rangle

\displaystyle=(E_{n}-\mu)\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{2i\delta}{(\mu-E_{n})^{2}+\delta^{2}}\langle n|\left.\frac{{\rm d}\hat{\widetilde{V}}^{\text{HF}}}{{\rm d}B}\right|_{B=0}|n\rangle=(E_{n}-\mu)\delta(E_{n}-\mu)\langle n|\left.\frac{{\rm d}\hat{\widetilde{V}}^{\text{HF}}}{{\rm d}B}\right|_{B=0}|n\rangle=0

(39)

Therefore, the contour integral in Eq.
38
vanishes and thus has no contribution to the orbital magnetization at
T=0
.

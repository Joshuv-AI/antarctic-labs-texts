Assessment of Lag Screws in Treatment of Anterior Mandibular Fracture
Authors: Esha Chowdhury, Vijay Ebenezer
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAims:The purpose of the study was to evaluate the results of treating anterior mandibular fractures with lag screws, focusing on specific clinical and radiographic characteristics.Method:Ten patients with anterior mandibular fractures were selected and given anesthesia. A local anesthetic with a vasoconstrictor (1:80000 adrenaline) was used at the surgical site. A vestibular incision of 3–4 mm was made, extending from 34–44. A gauze pad and the blunt side of a periosteal elevator were used to lift the mucoperiosteal flap. Metrogyl irrigation was then performed to remove any fibers stuck to the bone. The fracture site was exposed and the fracture edges were refined. Intermaxillary fixation was carried out once the broken fragments were minimized. Two lag screws, ranging in length from 16 to 20 mm, were used to secure the fragments, depending on the needs of each case.Result:Fracture stability, occlusion, mental nerve injury, screw position, accuracy of reduction, bone healing, tenderness, and edema were assessed up to the sixth week. Significant improvement was observed in all parameters by the end of the sixth week.Conclusion:The effectiveness of this treatment approach has been gained through a thorough examination of radiographic examinations, patient-reported metrics, and clinical outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

A significant percentage of maxillofacial injuries are mandibular fractures. Despite advancements in imaging and fixation techniques, managing these fractures can be difficult. Mandibular fractures were traditionally treated with closed reduction utilizing splints, external fixation devices, and maxillomandibular fixation. However, patients treated with closed reduction and immobilization alone often had a functional but possibly nonpremorbid occlusion following surgery, and the fractures had not been anatomically reduced.

In 1888, Schede introduced the concept of tight internal attachment to the facial skeleton.[1] Rigid fixation, which refers to fixation applied directly to the bone to prevent interfragmentary motion across the fracture on active use of skeletal structures, is the basis for lag screws.[2] Several studies have been published on the use of lag screw fixation as the preferred treatment for mandibular fractures, particularly those affecting the anterior region. Therefore, this study was designed to evaluate the stiffness and efficacy of lag screw fixation in the treatment of mandibular symphysis and parasymphysis fractures.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This prospective clinical study was conducted at the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, SBDCH, Chennai, with ethical clearance from the Institutional Committee (SBDCH-IEC-CT-/18-08/15). Patients above 18 years of age who consented to participate, were medically fit for surgery, and had fractures in the mandibular symphysis and parasymphysis region were selected for the study. Patients with comminuted symphysis or parasymphysis fractures, preexisting infection at the fracture site, complete edentulism, and immunocompromised conditions were excluded.

A total of 10 randomly selected patients who met the inclusion criteria and gave their consent were included in the study. Preoperative assessment and examination of each patient were conducted, including collecting their personal information, history of the fracture, and conducting both extraoral and intraoral examinations [Figure 1]. Imaging tests, such as CBCT/CT scan and orthopantomogram (OPG), were performed for each case.

PROCEDURE

General anesthesia was used for each subject’s procedures. After patient preparation, the surgical site was covered with 2% gauze soaked in betadine. A local anesthetic and vasoconstrictor (1:80000) of adrenaline were applied at the surgical site.

Using the blunt side of a periosteal elevator and a gauze pad, a vestibular incision measuring 3–4 mm was made between 34 and 44, and a mucoperiosteal flap was raised. Metrogyl irrigation was performed to remove any fibers adhered to the bone. The fracture site was exposed, and the fracture edges were refined. To reduce fragments and achieve intermaxillary fixation, two lower border retractors were inserted into the inferior border of the mandible.

Two lag screws, with lengths ranging from 16 to 20 mm depending on the case, were used to secure the broken fragments. The adjacent cortex was drilled using a 2-mm drill once the drilling angle and entry point were determined. Initial drilling was done at the chosen entry site perpendicular to the buccal cortex to prevent the drill bit from slipping. The drill was then gradually guided to the predetermined angle, and the cortical and medullary portions of the near cortex were drilled through. A countersinking tool was then used to create a space for the screw head to sit.

Next, the distant cortex was drilled with a 1.5-mm drill bit. Drilling was performed slowly and repeatedly with ample irrigation to prevent debris from clogging the drill flutes. After drilling through the far and near cortex, a depth gauge was inserted to measure the length of the screw. Once the appropriate length lag screw was inserted and tightened, the bone fragment was compressed.

Patients were discharged from the hospital two days after surgery. They were evaluated on the first, seventh, 28th, and 42nd postoperative days based on various clinical and radiological characteristics [Figure 2 and Table 1].

Statistical analysis

All data was uploaded into Microsoft Excel. IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 20 (IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA) was used for analysis. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to examine the normality of data, and the results indicated that only tenderness significantly deviated from normal distribution. Descriptive statistics were created using frequencies and proportions. The presence and absence of these parameters over the course of several time periods, including intra- and postoperative, were analyzed using the Chi-square test. Tenderness at different postoperative times was analyzed using repeated measures ANOVA. Multiple pairwise comparisons were performed using the adjusted Bonferroni test. A threshold for statistical significance was established at P < 0.05.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

The titanium lag screws have threads on their terminal end. Lag screw fixation of the anterior mandible has advantages such as shorter recovery times, cost savings, lower rates of morbidity, and early improvements in functional rehabilitation.[3] According to Lydia Melek (2021),[4] curvature and thickness of bone cortex in the anterior mandible, as well as lack of anatomical risks, contribute to the effectiveness of using lag screws in that area.

The outcomes of the study achieved the main objective of stabilizing the occlusion in facial trauma, as stated by Koshy et al.[5] (2021). According to a study by Kallela et al.,[6] (1999) 68% of their patients experienced mental nerve paresthesia, which resolved on average after 5.4 weeks. The cause was found to be intraoperative stretching of mental nerve and soft tissues. In one case, a fractured fragment at the inferior border splayed slightly, while the satisfactory reduction was observed in other cases, as mentioned in a study by Elsayed et al.[7] (2020).

The anterior mandible is the optimal location for lag screw implantation because there are no anatomical risks in this area, as stated by Ellis and Ghali et al.[8] (1991). During the follow-up period, all patients experienced a decrease in pain and edema intensity, as observed in a study conducted by Elhussein et al.[9] (2017).

CONCLUSION

These results demonstrate the effectiveness of lag screws in providing secure fixation and promoting good healing outcomes in anterior mandibular fractures. Lag screws offer several notable benefits in the treatment of anterior mandibular fractures. Their less invasive approach provides stable fixation, accelerates healing, and reduces the risk of postsurgical complications. The precise positioning of lag screws enables optimal alignment, facilitating early functional recovery.

Maximal inequalities for square functions associated to group metric measure spaces
Panchugopal Bikram, Diptesh Saha

Abstract
. In this article, we establish weighted strong and weak type inequalities for non-commutative square functions associated with the differences of ball averages and martingales over group metric measure spaces. These results generalise the main outcome of [ RS24 ] .

1.
Introduction

Maximal inequalities play a central role in harmonic analysis and ergodic theory, serving as fundamental tools for studying the behavior of averages, establishing pointwise convergence, and deriving strong and weak type estimates. In the classical setting, square functions and maximal functions are closely related. The square functions offer a refined, quantitative view of oscillation, while maximal functions are crucial for almost everywhere convergence results. Their interplay underlies many classical results, including the Hardy–Littlewood maximal inequality, Stein’s square function estimates, and Bourgain’s work in pointwise ergodic theory.

Over the past two decades, significant progress has been made in extending classical harmonic analysis techniques to the non-commutative setting, where functions take values in von Neumann algebras. In this framework, studying maximal inequalities for square functions in non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces plays a central role, particularly in establishing non-commutative quantitative mean ergodic theorems. In this article, we focus on weighted analogues of certain square function inequalities in the non-commutative setting, motivated by problems arising in ergodic theory.

Let
G
be a locally compact group equipped with a left-invariant Haar measure
\mu
and the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
. Suppose further that
G
is a complete metric space with respect to a metric
d
, such that the triple
(G,d,\mu)
satisfies the annular decay condition
eq.

2.3
and the doubling condition
eq.

2.4
. We refer to such a structure
(G,d,\mu)
as a group metric measure space. For metric spaces satisfying a geometric doubling condition, Hytönen and Kairema
[
TH12
]
constructed dyadic cube systems in
G
that are analogous to the standard dyadic partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
. This construction provides a natural filtration of
\sigma
-subalgebras
(\mathcal{F}_{n})
of
\mathcal{F}
on the measure space
(G,\mu)
, which in turn allows one to define a net of conditional expectation operators
(\mathbb{E}_{n})
from
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{F},\mu)
to
L^{1}(G,\mathcal{F}_{n},\mu)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful, normal, semifinite (f.n.s.) trace
\tau
. For a group metric measure space
(G,d,\mu)
, we consider the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}:=L^{\infty}(G,\mu)\otimes\mathcal{M}
, equipped with the f.n.s. trace
\varphi:=\int_{G}\otimes\tau
. Furthermore, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\mathbb{E}_{n}\otimes I
form
\mathcal{N}
to
L^{\infty}(G,\mathcal{F}_{n},\mu)\otimes\mathcal{M}
, preserving the trace
\varphi
.

Given any locally integrable function
f:G\to L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, we define the averaging operator by

(1.1)

A_{\delta^{k}}f(x):=\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,\delta^{k}))}\int_{B(x,\delta^{k})}f(y)\,d\mu(y),\quad x\in G,

where,
\delta>1
and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
B(x,\delta^{k})
denotes the ball of radius
\delta^{k}
centered at the point
x
.

If we replace
\mathcal{M}
by
\mathbb{C}
, the field of complex numbers, then the associated classical square function is defined by

Lf(x):=\left(\sum_{k}\left|\left(A_{\delta^{k}}-\mathbb{E}_{k}\right)f(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\quad x\in G.

In
[
JRW03
]
, the authors considered the special case where

for some

,
\delta=2
, and
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}
. In this setting, they proved that the operator
L
is bounded from
\ell^{p}
to
\ell^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
, and from
\ell^{1}
to weak-
\ell^{1}
.

To obtain a similar result in the non-commutative setting, one first needs to introduce suitable function spaces, namely the vector-valued non-commutative spaces
L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})
and
L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})
(cf.

2.3
). In this framework, the non-commutative square function is associated with the sequence

(1.2)

T_{k}f(x):=(A_{\delta^{k}}-\mathbb{E}_{k})f(x),\quad x\in G,

where
f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{N},\varphi)
. In
[
HX21
]
, the authors studied this problem in the setting where
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
, and
\mathcal{M}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra. They established the following result:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
HX21
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
, depending only on
p
, such that

\begin{cases}\left\|(T_{k}f)\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{1},&\forall f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{N}),\\
\\
\left\|(T_{k}f)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{p},&\forall f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{N}),\quad\text{for }1<p<\infty.\end{cases}

The study of weighted inequalities has long been a central theme in classical harmonic analysis, with a rich history of extending unweighted estimates to settings that incorporate Muckenhoupt-type weights. Naturally, this prompts the question of whether similar weighted results can be developed in the non-commutative framework. The classical theory was pioneered by Muckenhoupt in his foundational work
[
Muc72
]
, and subsequently advanced by many others, including
[
FF12
]
. A landmark development in this area was the resolution of the so-called
A_{2}
-conjecture, which sought to determine whether certain operator bounds exhibit linear dependence on the
A_{2}
-characteristic of the weight. This was ultimately established by Hytönen in his breakthrough result
[
Hyt12
]
.

Parallel developments in the theory of operator algebras and quantum probability have driven rapid progress in non-commutative analysis. Notable contributions in this direction include results on martingales, maximal inequalities, and ergodic theorems within non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces, as seen in the works
[
JX07
,
Mei07
,
HLX23
,
HRW23
,
HLW21
,
HLRX22
]
. Despite these advancements, the incorporation of weights into the non-commutative setting has seen comparatively limited exploration. A recent notable step was taken in
[
GJOW22
]
, where the authors proved weighted non-commutative Doob’s maximal inequalities. In a recent work,
[
RS24
]
, using the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition developed by
[
CCAP22
]
and
[
CW22
]
, and weighted Doob’s inequality, studied in
[
GJOW22
]
, the authors proved a weighted strong and weak type bounds for non-commutative square functions extending
1.1
.

Returning to the classical setting, the boundedness of square functions associated with group metric measure spaces
(G,d,\mu)
has been investigated in
[
HL21
]
. In this work, we extend those results by establishing the following weighted analogue of
1.1
, thereby generalizing the main theorem of
[

]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
, and let
w
be an
A_{1}
-weight (cf.
section

). Then there exists a constant
C_{p}(w)>0
, depending only on

, such that the following bounds hold:

\begin{cases}\left\|(T_{k}f)\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C_{1}(w)\|f\|_{1,w},&\text{for all }f\in L^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{w}),\\
\\
\left\|(T_{k}f)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C_{p}(w)\|f\|_{p,w},&\text{for all }f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w}),\quad\text{where }1<p<\infty.\end{cases}

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. For
x\in X
and
r>0
, we define the ball centered at
x
with radius
r
by

B(x,r):=\{y\in X:d(y,x)\leq r\}.

Assume further that
X
is equipped with a Borel measure
\mu
such that

0<\mu(B(x,r))<\infty\quad\text{for all }x\in X\text{ and }r>0.

The measure
\mu
is said to satisfy the doubling condition if there exists a constant
C_{d}>0
such that

(2.1)

\mu(B(s,2r))\leq C_{d}\,\mu(B(s,r))\quad\text{for all }s\in X\text{ and }r>0.

Throughout this note, we will use the following notation: for non-negative quantities
a
and
b
, we write
a\lesssim b
to mean that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
. We write
a\approx b
if both
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
.

2.1.
Muckenhoupt’s Weights

We begin with some basic notions related to weights on a metric space. Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space equipped with a Borel measure
\mu
satisfying the doubling condition (
2.1
).

A function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be locally integrable if it is integrable over every compact subset of
X
; we denote this by
f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)
. A Borel measure
\nu
on
X
is said to be absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
if there exists a non-negative function
w\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)
such that

d\nu=w\,d\mu.

In this case,
\nu
is referred to as a weighted measure with respect to
\mu
, and the function
w
is called a weight. For any measurable subset
F\subseteq X
and a weight
w
, we define

w(F):=\int_{F}w\,d\mu.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
w
be a weight on
X
. Let
1<p<\infty
. Then, we say that
w
satisfies Mukenhoupt’s
A_{p}
condition if

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{x\in X,r>0}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}wd\mu\right)\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}w^{1/(1-p)}d\mu\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

and in this case, we write
w\in A_{p}(X)
. If
p=1
, we define
A_{1}(X)
to be the class of weights
w
for which there exists
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}wd\mu\leq C\inf_{t\in B(x,r)}w(t)

for all
x\in X
and
r>0
. The smallest such
C>0
satisfying the above equation is denoted by
[w]_{A_{1}}
.

In the following, we record some properties of weights satisfying Muckenhoupt’s condition. For proofs we refer to the article
[
KM22
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space satifying the doubling condition.

(1)

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
. Then
A_{p}(X)\subseteq A_{q}(X)
.

(2)

Let
p>1
, and
v
a weight on
X
such that
v^{r}\in A_{p}(X)
for some
r>1
. Then there exists
v_{1},v_{2}\in A_{1}(X)
such that
v=v_{1}v_{2}^{1-p}
.

(3)

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
w\in A_{p}(X)
.

(a)

(
Reverse Holder’s Inequality
) There exists
\delta>0
and
0<C<\infty
such that

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}w^{1+\delta}d\mu\right)^{1/(1+\delta)}\leq C\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}wd\mu

for all
x\in X
and
r>0
.

(b)

There exist
\epsilon>0
such that
w^{1+\epsilon}\in A_{p}(X)
.

(c)

Let
B
be an open ball of
X
, and
E\subseteq B
with
\mu(E)>0
, then

\int_{B}wd\mu\leq[w]_{A_{p}}\left(\frac{\mu(B)}{\mu(E)}\right)^{p}\int_{E}wd\mu.

(d)

There exists constants
C=C(w)>0
and
0<\upsilon=\upsilon(w)<1
(depending only on the doubling constant and the weight
w
) such that for all open balls
B
and all measurable subsets
E\subseteq B
we have

\frac{w(E)}{w(B)}\leq C\left(\frac{\mu(E)}{\mu(B)}\right)^{\upsilon}.

2.2.
Weighted
L^{p}
-spaces

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful, normal, semifinite (f.n.s.) trace
\tau
. A (possibly unbounded) operator
x
, which is closed and densely defined on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, is said to be affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
if it commutes with all unitaries
u^{\prime}
in the commutant
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. The operator
x
is called
\tau
-measurable if for every
\delta>0
, there exists a projection
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
p\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(x)
(the domain of
x
) and
\tau(1-p)<\delta
.

The set of all
\tau
-measurable operators affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. It is well known that
\tau
extends to the positive cone
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)_{+}
, and for
x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, one defines the
L^{p}
-quasi-norm by

\|x\|_{p}:=\left(\tau(|x|^{p})\right)^{1/p},\quad 0<p<\infty,

where
|x|:=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
. The non-commutative
L^{p}
-space associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is then given by

L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\|x\|_{p}<\infty\},\quad\text{for }0<p<\infty,

and we define
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}):=\mathcal{M}
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the space
L^{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a Banach space with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

On the other hand, the non-commutative weak
L^{p}
-space associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is a subspace of
L^{0}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
consisting of those
x
for which the following quasi-norm is finite:

\|x\|_{p,\infty}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\cdot\tau\left(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|x|)\right)^{1/p}.

We denote this space by
L^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, which is a quasi-normed space. In particular, for
p=1
, if
x_{1},x_{2}\in L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
\lambda>0
, the following inequality holds:

(2.2)

\tau\left(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|x_{1}+x_{2}|)\right)\leq\tau\left(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(|x_{1}|)\right)+\tau\left(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(|x_{2}|)\right).

In this article, we consider weights of the form
w\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
, where
w
is a classical weight as defined above. Observe that
w\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
commutes with all elements of the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}:=L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\otimes\mathcal{M}
. We say that
w\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
condition if and only if
w
satisfies the classical
A_{p}
condition. Accordingly, we define

[w\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}]_{A_{p}}:=[w]_{A_{p}}.

From now on, we will refer to such weights simply by
w
, without ambiguity.

For a weight
w
of this form, the weighted non-commutative
L^{p}
-space (for
1\leq p<\infty
) is defined as

L^{p}_{w}(\mathcal{N}):=\left\{x\in L^{0}(\mathcal{N},\varphi):xw^{1/p}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{N},\varphi)\right\},

and is equipped with the norm

\left\|x\right\|_{L^{p}_{w}(\mathcal{N})}:=\left(\varphi(\left|x\right|^{p}w)\right)^{1/p}.

Equivalently, defining the weighted f.n.s. trace
\varphi_{w}(\cdot):=\varphi(\cdot\,w)
on the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
, it is evident that
L^{p}_{w}(\mathcal{N})
coincides with the non-commutative
L^{p}
-space associated to the pair
(\mathcal{N},\varphi_{w})
. In the sequel, we may occasionally use the notation
\mathcal{N}_{w}
to emphasize that
\mathcal{N}
is considered with the trace
\varphi_{w}
. The associated Banach space norm will be denoted by
\left\|\cdot\right\|{}_{p,w}
.

2.3.
Vector-valued non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space and
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
a semifinite von Neumann algebra. We recall the definition of the column space from
[
Mei07
,
PX03
]
:

L^{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{c}(X)):=\left\{f:X\to L^{p}(\mathcal{M})~\middle|~f\text{ is measurable and }\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{c}(X))}<\infty\right\},

where

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{c}(X))}:=\left\|\left(\int_{X}f^{*}(x)f(x)\,d\mu(x)\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p},

which defines a norm on
L^{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{c}(X))
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Similarly, the associated row space is defined with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{r}(X))}:=\left\|\left(\int_{X}f(x)f^{*}(x)\,d\mu(x)\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}.

We now recall the following Hölder-type inequality.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Mei07
]
, Proposition 1.1)
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. If
f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{c}(X))
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{c}(X))
, then

\left\|\int_{X}f^{*}(x)g(x)\,d\mu(x)\right\|_{r}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{c}(X))}\cdot\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{M};L^{2}_{c}(X))}.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
w
be a weight, and consider the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}_{w}
. Observe that when
X=\mathbb{N}
, equipped with the counting measure, the norms on the column and row spaces associated with
\mathcal{N}_{w}
take the following discrete forms:

\left\|(f_{k})\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\left\|\left(\sum_{k}\left|f_{k}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{p,w}\text{ and }\left\|(f_{k})\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{r})}=\left\|\left(\sum_{k}\left|f_{k}^{*}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{p,w}.

The associated row-column space
L^{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
is defined as follows.

(1)

If
2\leq p\leq\infty
,

L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{rc}):=L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{c})\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{r})

equipped with the norm:

\|(f_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\max\left\{\|(f_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{c})},\|(f_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{r})}\right\}.

(2)

If
1\leq p<2
,

L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{rc})=L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{c})+L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{r})

equipped with the norm:

\|(f_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\inf\left\{\|(g_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(h_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{r})}\right\},

where the infimum runs over all decompositions
f_{k}=g_{k}+h_{k}
with
g_{k}
and
h_{k}
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w})
.

In a similar manner, one can define the weak-type spaces
L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{r})
. Correspondingly, the associated row-column space
L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{rc})
is also defined, equipped with the quasi-norm

\left\|(f_{k})\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\inf_{f_{k}=g_{k}+h_{k}}\{\left\|(g_{k})\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\left\|(h_{k})\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{r})}\}.

Let us now recall the following non-commutative Khintchine inequality, which will play a crucial role in our analysis. For the proof, we refer to
[
LPP91
]
and
[
Cad19
, Corollary 3.2]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
w\in A_{1}
and
(\varepsilon_{k})
be a Rademacher sequence on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
. Then,

(1)

\left\|\sum_{k}\varepsilon_{k}f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w})}\approx\left\|(f_{k})\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{cr})}.

(2)

\left\|\sum_{k}\varepsilon_{k}f_{k}\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w})}\approx\left\|(f_{k})\right\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell_{2}^{cr})}
.

2.4.
Non-commutative martingales

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra with an f.n.s trace
\tau
and let
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a decreasing sequence of von Neumann subalgebras of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{M}_{n}
is
w^{*}
-dense in
\mathcal{M}
. Then, for each
n
, there exists a
\tau
-preserving normal conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}_{n}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

A sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is called a non-commutative martingale if
\mathcal{E}_{n}(x_{n+1})=x_{n+1}
for all
n\geq 0
. The associated martingale difference sequence
(dx_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is defined by
dx_{n}:=x_{n}-x_{n+1}
for all
n\geq 0
.

We now turn our attention to the specific class of martingales that are the focus of this article. From this point onward, unless otherwise stated, we will replace the general metric space
X
with a locally compact group
G
, and
\mu
will denote the left-invariant Haar measure on
G
. The identity element of the group will be denoted by
e
. In addition, we assume that
G
is equipped with a complete metric
d
, making it also a metric space. For each
r>0
, we denote by
B_{r}
the ball centered at the identity
e
, i.e.,

B_{r}:=\{g\in G:d(g,e)\leq r\}.

Let
r_{0}>0
and
0<\epsilon\leq 1
be fixed. We say that the triple
(G,d,\mu)
satisfies the
(\epsilon,r_{0})
-annular decay property if there exists a constant
K>0
such that for all
x\in G
,
r\in(r_{0},\infty)
, and
s\in(0,r]
, the following inequality holds:

(2.3)

\mu(B(x,r+s))-\mu(B(x,r))\leq K\bigg(\frac{s}{r}\bigg)^{\epsilon}\mu(B(x,r)).

It follows from
eq.

2.3
that for all
x\in G
and
r_{0}<r\leq R<\infty
, we have the estimate:

(2.4)

\frac{\mu(B(x,R))}{\mu(B(x,r))}\leq(K+1)\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{\epsilon}.

That is, the balls of radius greater than
r_{0}
satisfies doubling condition with respect to the measure
\mu
. We now recall the following useful lemma from
[
HL21
, Lemma 2.9]
, which will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
r\geq 2r_{0}
. Then there exists a constant
K_{\epsilon}>0
such that for every
0<s\leq r
and for all
x\in G
, the following inequality holds:

\mu(B(x,r+s))-\mu(B(x,r-s))\leq K_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\epsilon}\mu(B(x,r)).

Let
D_{0}>0
and
r_{1}>r_{0}
be fixed. We say that the metric space
(G,d)
satisfies the
(D_{0},r_{1})

\displaystyle\leq(K+1)(C_{1}+1)^{\epsilon}(1+\epsilon)\int_{G}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\chi_{A(y)}(x)}{\mu(B(y,\delta^{k}))}\frac{\mu(B(y,\delta^{k}))}{\mu(B(u_{i},\delta^{k}))}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq(K+1)(C_{1}+1)^{\epsilon}(1+\epsilon)\int_{G}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\chi_{A(y)}(x)}{\mu(B(y,\delta^{k}))}\frac{\mu(B(u_{i},2\delta^{k}+C_{1}\delta^{n}))}{\mu(B(u_{i},\delta^{k}))}d\mu(x)

(since
B(y,\delta^{k})\subseteq B(u_{i},2\delta^{k}+C_{1}\delta^{n})
)

\displaystyle\leq(K+1)^{2}(C_{1}+1)^{\epsilon}(1+\epsilon)(2+C_{1})^{\epsilon}M\frac{\mu(A(y))}{\mu(B(y,\delta^{k}))}

\displaystyle\leq(K+1)^{2}(C_{1}+1)^{\epsilon}(1+\epsilon)(2+C_{1})^{\epsilon}MK_{\epsilon}(\delta^{n-k})^{\epsilon}\lesssim\delta^{(n-k)\epsilon}.

Therefore,

\int_{G}\frac{\chi_{A(y)}(x)}{\mu(B(x,\delta^{k}))}w(x)d\mu(x)\lesssim C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}w(y)\delta^{(n-k)\epsilon}.

∎

Now consider the following averages:

M_{k,n}h(s):=\frac{1}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n)}h(g)d\mu(g);~h\in L^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{w}).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
k>n\geq n_{r_{0}}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then for
h\in L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w})
we have

\left\|M_{k,n}h\right\|^{p}_{p,w}\lesssim C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}\delta^{(n-k)\epsilon}\left\|h\right\|^{p}_{p,w}.

Furthermore, if
h
is positive and if we set
h_{n}=\mathcal{E}_{n}(h)
, then we have

\left\|M_{k,n}h\right\|^{p}_{p,w}\lesssim C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}\delta^{(n-k)\epsilon}\left\|h_{n}\right\|^{p}_{p,w}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|M_{k,n}h\right\|_{p,w}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left\|M_{k,n}h(s)\right\|_{p}^{p}w(s)d\mu(s).

Furthermore, if
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
, then we have

\displaystyle\left\|M_{k,n}h(s)\right\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n)}\left\|h(y)\right\|_{p}d\mu(y)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu(\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n))^{p/p^{\prime}}}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))^{p}}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n)}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}dy~(\text{by Minkowski's inequality})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\mu(\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n))}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}\right)^{p/p^{\prime}}\times\frac{1}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n)}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}dy

\displaystyle\leq(K_{\epsilon}C_{1})^{(p-1)\epsilon}\delta^{(n-k)(p-1)\epsilon}\times\frac{1}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n)}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}dy

( by
eq.

3.2
and
2.5
)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|M_{k,n}h\right\|_{p,w}^{p}\leq(K_{\epsilon}C_{1})^{(p-1)\epsilon}\delta^{(n-k)(p-1)\epsilon}\int_{G}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n)}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}dy\right)w(s)d\mu(s).

Now, for
s\in G
, recall that
A(s)=B(s,\delta^{k}+C_{1}\delta^{n})\setminus B(s,\delta^{k}-C_{1}\delta^{n})
, further, obtain

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(s,\delta^{k}),n)}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}dy\right)w(s)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}\int_{A(s)}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}dy\right)w(s)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}\left(\int_{G}\frac{\chi_{A(y)}(s)}{\mu(B(s,\delta^{k}))}w(s)d\mu(s)\right)dy~\text{(using Fubini)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}\delta^{(n-k)\epsilon}\int_{G}\left\|h(y)\right\|^{p}_{p}w(y)d\mu(y)~\text{(by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lem: norm est prop})}

\displaystyle=C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}\delta^{(n-k)\epsilon}\left\|h\right\|_{p,w}^{p}.

Thus, we finally obtain that

\left\|M_{k,n}h\right\|_{p,w}^{p}\lesssim C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}\delta^{(n-k)\epsilon}\left\|h\right\|_{p,w}^{p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6

For

Proposition 3.7
.

The following estimate holds true

\widetilde{\varphi}_{w}\left(\left|\zeta Tb_{d}\zeta\right|>\frac{\lambda}{9}\right)\lesssim C(w)[w]^{2}_{A_{1}}\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{1,w}}{\lambda}.

Lemma 3.8
.

and

3.1.3.

In this section, we will prove that

\displaystyle\left\|A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}-dh_{n}\right\|^{2}_{2,w}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left\|A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}(s)-dh_{n}(s)\right\|^{2}_{2}w(s)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in I_{n}}\int_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}\left\|A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}(s)-dh_{n}(s)\right\|^{2}_{2}w(s)d\mu(s).

Since
dh_{n}
is a constant operator on each dyadic cube
Q^{n}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in I_{n}
, we have

A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}(x)-dh_{n}(x)=0\quad\text{if}\quad B(x,\delta^{k})\subseteq Q^{n}_{\alpha}.

Therefore, for
x\in Q^{n}_{\alpha}
, the term
(A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}-dh_{n})(x)
may be nonzero only when the ball
B(x,\delta^{k})
intersects the complement of the cube
Q^{n}_{\alpha}
.

Motivated by this, for a given dyadic cube
Q^{n}_{\alpha}
and a ball
B(x,\delta^{k})
in
G
, we define the boundary-sensitive set:

\mathfrak{H}(B_{\delta^{k}},Q^{n}_{\alpha}):=\left\{x\in Q^{n}_{\alpha}:B(x,\delta^{k})\cap(Q^{n}_{\alpha})^{c}\neq\emptyset\right\}.

Now, let
m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}
denote the maximum of the map
G\ni x\to\left\|dh_{n}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}
on
m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}
and its neighbouring cubes of
n
th generation. Then we first observe that

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}\left\|A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}(s)-dh_{n}(s)\right\|^{2}_{2}w(s)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\leq 2m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}^{2}w(\mathfrak{H}(B_{\delta^{k}},Q^{n}_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle\leq C(w)m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}^{2}w(Q^{n}_{\alpha})\left(\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{H}(B_{\delta^{k}},Q^{n}_{\alpha}))}{\mu(Q^{n}_{\alpha})}\right)^{\upsilon}

\displaystyle\leq C(w)C_{2}\delta^{(k-n)\eta\upsilon}m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}^{2}w(Q^{n}_{\alpha}),~\text{by }\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{prop: measure of a good point set}

\displaystyle\leq C(w)C_{2}\delta^{(k-n)\eta\upsilon}\int_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}^{2}w(s)d\mu(s).

Furthermore, since
m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}
is constant on
Q^{n}_{\alpha}
, we have

\int_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}^{2}w(s)d\mu(s)\lesssim\int_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}\left\|dh_{n}(s)\right\|_{2}^{2}w(s)d\mu(s).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}-dh_{n}\right\|^{2}_{2,w}

\displaystyle\leq C(w)C_{2}\delta^{(k-n)\eta\upsilon}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{n}}\int_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}m_{Q^{n}_{\alpha}}^{2}w(s)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(w)C_{2}\delta^{(k-n)\eta\upsilon}\int_{G}\left\|dh_{n}(s)\right\|_{2}^{2}w(s)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(w)\delta^{(k-n)\eta\upsilon}\left\|dh_{n}\right\|_{2,w}^{2}

Now consider the case
k>n
. In this case
\mathcal{E}_{k}(dh_{n})=0
. Therefore, we only need to prove

\left\|A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}\right\|^{2}_{2,w}\lesssim C(w)[w]_{A_{1}}\delta^{(k-n)}\left\|dh_{n}\right\|_{2,w}^{2}.

But since
A_{\delta^{k}}dh_{n}=M_{k,n}dh_{n}
, applying
3.4
, we obtain the above inequality.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the weak type
(1,1)
estimate for the good part.

Theorem 3.12
.

The following is true.

\displaystyle\lambda\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(\left|Tg\right|>\lambda)\lesssim\left(C(w)\max\{1,[w]_{A_{1}}\}\right)^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{w})}

Proof.

By Chebychev’s inequality, we observe that

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{w}(\left|Tg\right|>\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|Tg\right\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(w)(\max\{1,[w]_{A_{1}}\})\frac{\left\|g\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{w})}}{\lambda^{2}},~\text{by }\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lem: strong estimate}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(w)(\max\{1,[w]_{A_{1}}\})\frac{\left\|g\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{w})}}{\lambda},~\text{since }\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(C(w)\max\{1,[w]_{A_{1}}\}\right)^{2}\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{w})}}{\lambda}~\text{by }\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{thm: CZ decomp}.

∎

3.2.
Strong type
(p,p)
estimate

To prove the strong type
(p,p)
estimate, essentially we need to estimate the strong type
(2,2)
bounds of the operator
T
in
eq.

3.1
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then the following is true

(3.4)

\left\|(T_{k}f)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\lesssim C(w)\left\|f\right\|_{p,w}~\forall f\in L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w}).

Proof.

We now conclude the argument by appealing to interpolation and duality. By
3.1
, the operator
T
is of weak type
(1,1)
, mapping
L^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{w},\varphi_{w})
into
L^{1,\infty}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w},\tilde{\varphi}_{w})
. In addition, it follows from
3.11
that
T
is bounded from
L^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{w},\varphi_{w})
to
L^{2}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w},\tilde{\varphi}_{w})
.

Applying the non-commutative Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem, we deduce that
T
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{p}(\mathcal{N}_{w},\varphi_{w})
to
L^{p}(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{N}_{w},\tilde{\varphi}_{w})
for all
1<p<2
. By duality, it follows that the adjoint operator
T^{*}
is bounded on
L^{p^{\prime}}
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. A straightforward verification shows that
T^{*}=T
, and hence the boundedness of
T
extends to all
1<p<\infty
.
∎

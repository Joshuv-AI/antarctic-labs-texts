Strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking meets average symmetry-protected topological order
Yuchen Guo, Shuo Yang

Abstract
Recent studies have unveiled new possibilities for discovering intrinsic quantum phases that are unique to open systems, including phases with average symmetry-protected topological (ASPT) order and strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SWSSB) order in systems with global symmetry. In this work, we propose a new class of phases, termed the double ASPT phase, which emerges from a nontrivial extension of these two orders. This new phase is absent from prior studies and cannot exist in conventional closed systems. Using the recently developed imaginary-Lindbladian formalism, we explore the phase diagram of a one-dimensional open system with ℤ 2 σ × ℤ 2 τ \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\sigma}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\tau} symmetry. We identify universal critical behaviors along each critical line and observe the emergence of an intermediate phase that completely breaks the ℤ 2 σ \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\sigma} symmetry, leading to the formation of two triple points in the phase diagram. These two triple points are topologically distinct and connected by a domain-wall decoration duality map. Our results promote the establishment of a complete classification for quantum phases in open systems with various symmetry conditions.

In contrast to the definition of symmetry

These studies have constructed fixed-point density matrices for various quantum phases, though phase transitions between them remain unexplored because of the absence of a Hamiltonian and the definition of an energy gap in open systems.

On the other hand, in closed systems, a global symmetry can only protect a nontrivial SPT phase if it remains unbroken, either explicitly or spontaneously.
However, in open systems, a symmetry that experiences SWSSB can still protect an ASPT phase if supported by another strong symmetry.
This coexistence of SWSSB and ASPT within the same symmetry group is impossible in closed systems, revealing a new class of intrinsic mixed-state quantum phases that has not been previously identified in the study of open-system quantum phases.

To investigate phase transitions between different phases, a straightforward approach is to apply a noise channel to the quantum state, focusing on the decoherence-induced phase transition that typically occurs between a nontrivial phase and a trivial product phase

There remain several open questions in this framework, such as the rigorous connection between the imaginary-Liouville gap and the equivalent relation defined by two-way connectivity using local quantum channels.
We will demonstrate that, although without analytical proof, both numerical models in this study validate the effectiveness of this framework in terms of characterizing the universal properties of phase transitions in open systems.

In this paper, we focus on characterizing a nontrivial combination of SWSSB and ASPT phases in one-dimensional (1D) open systems.

SWSSB to ASPT.—

Here, we explore an alternative scenario for a system exhibiting SWSSB, where the resulting state after symmetry breaking, such as
O_{+}
in the previous example, belongs to a nontrivial ASPT phase.
In this case, the phase is protected by the interplay of the remaining weak symmetry and another strong symmetry that remains unbroken.
Unlike the previous example, which involves SWSSB to a trivial phase, this case represents SWSSB to an ASPT phase, characterized by a mixed anomaly between weak and strong symmetries
[
17
,
18
,
19
]

This phase, termed double ASPT, represents a novel class of phases that has not been identified in earlier studies of SWSSB and falls outside the classification of ASPT phases, which generally assume short-range correlations for all correlation lengths.
The introduction of long-range correlations due to SWSSB challenges previous assumption and opens a pathway to discovering new quantum phenomena in open systems.

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

Each of these critical lines maps to a corresponding critical line in the lower half-plane (

This represents a new realization of the gapless ASPT, where both sides of the critical line exhibit the same decorated domain wall structure

This contrasts with the conventional emergence of gapless SPT phases in the transitions between a topologically nontrivial symmetric phase and a topologically trivial SSB phase in both closed

Moreover, the intersection of three gapless ASPT phases (in the upper half-plane) further extends the concept of topological criticality to topological triple points, which presents an intriguing subject for investigation.
This topological triple point can be generated by decorating domain walls at the triple point between three symmetry-breaking critical lines (in the lower half-plane), inheriting features from both the symmetry-breaking and topological properties.
In summary, the interplay between symmetry and topology is much richer in an open quantum system originating from the distinct behaviors of strong and weak symmetries.

Conclusions and discussions.—

In this work, we systematically investigate the phase diagram of a 1D open system with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\sigma}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\tau}
symmetry using the imaginary-Lindbladian formalism.
For the first time, we identify and construct the double ASPT phase, a new intrinsic mixed-state phase previously overlooked in the study of open quantum systems.
The concurrence of symmetry-breaking patterns and topological properties related to the same symmetry group in double ASPT phase suggests a distinct and richer phase diagram and classification in the context of open quantum systems.

By comparing the phase diagram of the pure-state system with that of the open system, we gain an intuitive understanding of how new physical phenomena emerge in the latter.
In particular, we observe three distinct realizations of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\sigma}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\tau}
gapless ASPT phases, as well as the appearance of topological triple points where these critical lines intersect.
Future investigations of the gapless ASPT phases and the topological triple points hold great promise.
A deeper exploration of the relationships and differences between these gapless ASPT phases, particularly from a topological perspective, is essential.
In particular, the topological triple point can be interpreted as a phase transition between two distinct gapless ASPT phases
[
47
]
.
These insights are crucial for advancing our understanding of open-system phase transitions and are instrumental in constructing a comprehensive classification of gapless topological phases in open quantum systems.

Appendix A
Imaginary-time Lindbladian evolution

In this section, we briefly review the recently proposed framework to define a gapped quantum phase in an open system as the finite-time steady state of the imaginary-time Lindbladian evolution
[
31
]
.

The real-time evolution of an open system is usually characterized by the master equation
[
48
,
49
]

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}t}=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{k}{\left[L_{k}\rho L_{k}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{L_{k}^{\dagger}L_{k},\rho\}\right]}

(S1)

where
H
is the Hamiltonian of the system, and
L_{k}
is the jump operator to describe the coupling between system and environment.
To derive the imaginary-time evolution of the open system, we first consider the imaginary-time evolution for the entire system (system and environment) and trace out the ancillae, leading to the following expression
[
31
]

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=-\left\{H,\rho\right\}+\sum_{k}\left[L_{k}\rho L_{k}^{\dagger}+\frac{1}{2}\{L_{k}^{\dagger}L_{k},\rho\}\right]\equiv-\mathcal{L}^{I}(\rho),

(S2)

where
\mathcal{L}^{I}
is denoted as the imaginary-Liouville superoperator.
The steady state of
e^{-\mathcal{L}^{I}\mathrm{d}\tau}
, if exists, is the ground state of
\mathcal{L}^{I}
(eigenvalue with the smallest real part), whose spectral gap determines the recursion time of converging to steady state
\tau\sim 1/\Delta^{I}
.
Using this formalism, one can construct the corresponding open system for typical mixed states belonging to different quantum phases and explore potential phase transitions along the path connecting two imaginary-Liouville superoperators through, e.g., linear interpolation.

Appendix B
Construction of
\mathcal{L}^{I}
for SWSSB phase

Here we construct the corresponding open system for the density matrix

\displaystyle\rho\equiv O_{+}^{\rm SWSSB}+O_{-}^{\rm SWSSB}=\left(\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i}\sigma_{i}^{0}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i}\sigma_{i}^{x}\right).

(S3)

We first review the heuristic method proposed in Ref.
[
31
]
to construct the imaginary-Liouville superoperator.
Inspired by the fact that the classical mixture in a density matrix corresponds to superposition in the supervector space, we use the following two-step strategy: finding one term to project onto the manifold containing all possible configurations appearing in the superposition and designing another one to determine the superposition coefficients.
This approach can be used to construct the Hamiltonian for novel topological quantum states like 1D cluster state or 2D string-net states.

Let us consider the trivial symmetric mixed state

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i}\left(\ket{\uparrow_{i}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\uparrow_{i}}+\ket{\downarrow_{i}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\downarrow_{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\{\sigma_{i}\}}\ket{\psi_{\{\sigma_{i}\}}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\psi_{\{\sigma_{i}\}}}.

(S4)

We note that in each configuration constituting the density matrix, the spins in ket and bra are parallel, which can be achieved by the following ferromagnetic term

\displaystyle L_{i}^{[1]}=\sigma_{i}^{z}\Longrightarrow L_{i}^{[1]}\otimes L_{i}^{[1]*}=\sigma_{i}^{z}\otimes\sigma_{i}^{z}.

(S5)

Meanwhile, the hopping terms between different components are chosen as

\displaystyle L_{i}^{[2]}=\sigma_{i}^{x}\Longrightarrow L_{i}^{[2]}\otimes L_{i}^{[2]*}=\sigma_{i}^{x}\otimes\sigma_{i}^{x},

(S6)

which connect two different configurations
\ket{\psi_{\{\sigma_{i}\}}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\psi_{\{\sigma_{i}\}}}
and
\ket{\psi_{\{\sigma_{i}\}^{\prime}}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\psi_{\{\sigma_{i}\}^{\prime}}}
that differ by the spins at site
i
.

As for the SWSSB case, the strategy is a little different.
The aim is to obtain a degenerate ground-state manifold spanned by
O_{+}
and
O_{-}
, meaning that we need to stabilize both states but without coupling between them.
Notably, ket and bra spins on every site are completely parallel (antiparallel) for
\rho_{+}
(
\rho_{-}
), or explicitly written as

\displaystyle O_{+}^{\rm SWSSB}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i}\left(\ket{\uparrow_{i}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\uparrow_{i}}+\ket{\downarrow_{i}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\downarrow_{i}}\right),

(S7)

\displaystyle O_{-}^{\rm SWSSB}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\prod_{i}\left(\ket{\uparrow_{i}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\downarrow_{i}}+\ket{\downarrow_{i}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\uparrow_{i}}\right).

(S8)

As an analog, in the GHZ state with an SSB order, each pair of adjacent sites is composed of
\ket{\uparrow_{i}\uparrow_{i+1}}
or
\ket{\downarrow_{i}\downarrow_{i+1}}
and corresponds to a ferromagnetic Hamiltonian
H=-\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}^{z}\sigma_{i+1}^{z}
.
Similarly, each pair of adjacent sites belongs to one of the following cases

ket and bra spins are parallel for both sites,

\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\alpha_{i}}\otimes\ket{\beta_{i+1}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\beta_{i+1}}

ket and bra spins are antiparallel for both sites,

\ket{\alpha_{i}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\tilde{\alpha}_{i}}\otimes\ket{\beta_{i+1}}\hskip-1.42262pt\bra{\tilde{\beta}_{i+1}}

This pattern can be realized by the following ferromagnetic term

\displaystyle L_{i}^{[1]}=\sigma_{i}^{z}\sigma_{i+1}^{z}\Longrightarrow L_{i}^{[1]}\otimes L_{i}^{[1]*}=\sigma_{i}^{z}\sigma_{i+1}^{z}\otimes\sigma_{i}^{z}\sigma_{i+1}^{z}.

(S11)

Similarly, we use

\displaystyle L_{i}^{[2]}=\sigma_{i}^{x}\Longrightarrow L_{i}^{[2]}\otimes L_{i}^{[2]*}=\sigma_{i}^{x}\otimes\sigma_{i}^{x},

(S12)

to introduce hopping between different components within
O_{+}
or
O_{-}
since it does not change the relative direction between ket and bra of one site.
From the perspective of the Hilbert space dimension, a density matrix with
N
sites can be viewed by a
2^{2N}
-dimensional supervector, requiring two independent jump operators at each site to remove redundancy.

Appendix C
Various correlators: linear, Rényi-2, Wightman, and conditional mutual information

The expectation value of an observable
O
over a pure state
\ket{\psi}
can be calculated as

\displaystyle\braket{O}=\braket{\psi|O|\psi}.

(S13)

Specifically, we consider the correlation length
\xi
defined from the connected correlation function

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(O,i,j)\equiv\braket{O_{i}O_{j}}-\braket{O_{i}}\braket{O_{j}}\sim\ e^{-|i-j|/\xi}.

(S14)

These observables allow us to identify symmetry properties and long-range patterns that govern phase transitions in closed systems.

As for a density matrix in the open system, we consider three types of observable: the linear observable, defined as the conventional expectation value over a density matrix

\displaystyle\braket{O}\equiv{\rm Tr}[\rho O],

(S15)

the Rényi-2 observable, expressed as the expectation value of the supervector
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle

[
19
]

\displaystyle\braket{O}^{(2)}\equiv\frac{\langle\!\langle\rho|O\otimes O^{*}|\rho\rangle\!\rangle}{\langle\!\langle\rho|\rho\rangle\!\rangle}=\frac{{\rm Tr}\left[\rho O\rho O^{\dagger}\right]}{{\rm Tr}\left[\rho^{2}\right]},

(S16)

and the Wightman observable defined under canonical purification
[
39
,
50
]

\displaystyle\braket{O}^{(\text{W})}\equiv\langle\!\langle\sqrt{\rho}|O\otimes O^{*}|\sqrt{\rho}\rangle\!\rangle={\rm Tr}{\left[\sqrt{\rho}O\sqrt{\rho}O\right]}.

(S17)

Subsequently, their corresponding correlators can be constructed as follows, including the linear correlation function

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(O,i,j)\equiv\braket{O_{i}O_{j}}-\braket{O_{i}}\braket{O_{j}}\sim\ e^{-|i-j|/\xi},

(S18)

the Rényi-2 correlation function

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(2)}(O,i,j)\equiv\braket{O_{i}O_{j}}^{(2)}-\braket{O_{i}}^{(2)}\braket{O_{j}}^{(2)}\sim\ e^{-|i-j|/\xi_{2}},

(S19)

and the Wightman correlation function

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(\text{W})}(O,i,j)\equiv\braket{O_{i}O_{j}}^{(\text{W})}-\braket{O_{i}}^{(\text{W})}\braket{O_{j}}^{(\text{W})}\sim\ e^{-|i-j|/\xi_{\text{W}}},

(S20)

which define the correlation lengths
\xi
,
\xi_{2}
, and
\xi_{\rm W}
, respectively.

In certain circumstances, the Rényi-2 correlator has distinct long-range behavior with the fidelity correlator, while the latter satisfies the stability theorem and is believed to be a universal indicator for SWSSB phases
[
26
]
.
However, the fidelity correlator is highly non-linear in terms of the density matrix and is thus intractable for numerical simulations.
Fortunately, it has been proposed that the Wightman correlator exhibits qualitatively equivalent long-range patterns to the fidelity correlator
[
39
,
50
]
.
Although still challenging to evaluate directly, this Wightman correlator can be viewed as a Rényi-1 correlator and estimated by analytical continuation from general Rényi-
n
correlators (with even
n
) to
n\rightarrow 1

[
39
]
.
Specifically, a general Rényi-
n
correlator can be defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(n)}(O,i,j)\equiv\braket{O_{i}O_{j}}^{(n)}-\braket{O_{i}}^{(n)}\braket{O_{j}}^{(n)}\sim\ e^{-|i-j|/\xi_{n}},

(S21)

where

\displaystyle\braket{O}^{(n)}\equiv\frac{{\rm Tr}{\left[\rho^{\frac{n}{2}}O\rho^{\frac{n}{2}}O\right]}}{{\rm Tr}\left[\rho^{n}\right]}

(S22)

denotes the Rényi-
n
expectation value for a density matrix
\rho
.

The conditional mutual information (CMI), which is believed to characterize different phases of matter
[
7
]
, describes the mutual information of subsystems
A
and
C
conditioned on another subsystem
B
as

\displaystyle I(A:C|B)=S(AB)+S(BC)-S(B)-S(ABC).

(S23)

Here,
S(X)=-{\rm Tr}\left[\rho_{X}\ln\rho_{X}\right]
refers to the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{X}
.
The correlation length of CMI is known as Markov length
\xi_{\rm M}
, which is defined by the long-range behavior regarding the distance between subregions
A
and
C
, or the width of the subregion
B
, i.e.,

\displaystyle I(A:C|B)\sim e^{-L_{B}/\xi_{\rm M}}.

(S24)

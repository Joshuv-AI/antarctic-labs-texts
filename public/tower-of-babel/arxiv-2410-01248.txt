Periods in Families and Derivatives of Period Maps
Benjamin Bakker, Jonathan Pila and Jacob Tsimerman

Abstract
. Given a smooth proper family ϕ : X → S \phi:X\rightarrow S , we study the (quasi)-periods of the fibers of ϕ \phi as (germs of) functions on S S . We show that they field they generate has the same algebraic closure as that given by the flag variety co-ordinates parametrizing the corresponding Hodge filtration, together with their derivatives. Moreover, in the more general context of an arbitrary flat vector bundle, we determine the transcendence degree of the function field generated by the flat coordinates of algebraic sections. Our results are inspired by and generalize work of Bertrand–Zudilin.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The cohomology groups of algebraic varieties

1.1.1.
Periods

Let
X
be a smooth complex algebraic variety. The singular cohomology groups
H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
of the associated complex analytic variety
X^{\mathrm{an}}
with its euclidean topology are topological invariants which come equipped with several algebraically defined structures.

By a theorem of Grothendieck,
H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
can be computed using the de Rham cohomology of algebraic forms
[
8
]
. This group is somewhat complicated in general, but if
X
is affine then
H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
is isomorphic to the group of closed algebraic
k
-forms modulo exact algebraic
k
-forms. The isomorphism is by integration: it associates to the class
[\alpha]
of a closed algebraic
k
-form the integration map
\gamma\mapsto\int_{\gamma}\alpha
on
k
-cycles. The integrals
\int_{\gamma}\alpha
are called periods. When
X
is defined over a subfield
L\subset\mathbb{C}
(for example a number field) and
\alpha
is taken to be
L
-rational, the periods are in general highly transcendental numbers, and conjecturally algebraic relations between them come from geometry (see for example
[
6
]
and the references therein).

1.1.2.
Hodge filtrations

Algebraic de Rham cohomology also endows
H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
with an algebraically defined filtration
F^{\bullet}
—the Hodge filtration—which detects geometry in a slightly different way, via the Hodge conjecture. For
X
a smooth projective variety,
F^{p}
is given by the smooth de Rham cohomology classes which can be represented by a type
(r,s)
form with
r\geq p
, and the Hodge conjecture asserts that rational cohomology classes
\alpha\in H^{2k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})
contained in
F^{k}
should come from geometry.

In this paper we are concerned with the relationship between the periods and the Hodge filtration in algebraic families, specifically with regard to their transcendence properties.

1.2.
Periods in families

Suppose
f:X\to S
is a smooth algebraic family over a smooth affine base
S
which is real analytically locall trivial. Assume for simplicity that
X
is affine. Then we can take a fiberwise closed algebraic
k
-form
\alpha
, a point
s\in S
, a class
\gamma\in H_{k}(X_{s}^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
, and a contractible neighborhood
s\in U\subset S^{\mathrm{an}}
, and form an analytic function
U\to\mathbb{C}
given by
u\mapsto\int_{\gamma(u)}\alpha|_{X_{u}}
where
\gamma(u)\in H_{k}(X_{u}^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
is the cycle obtained from
\gamma
by a local real analytic trivialization of
f
. In the general case, these functions make sense for
\alpha
an algebraic de Rham cohomology class (see for example
[
10
]
), and we define them to be the periods of
\alpha
at
s
. Once again the period functions are highly transcendental, but in this case algebraic relations can be proven to come from geometry in an appropriate sense, see
[
6
]
.

1.3.
Period maps

See
[
16
]
for background on period maps.
The cohomology groups of fibers
H^{k}(X_{t}^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
can be canonically identified locally on
S^{\mathrm{an}}
using a real analytic trivialization. With respect to these local identifications, the Hodge filtration
F^{\bullet}_{t}
on
H^{k}(X_{t}^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
varies in a holomorphic way. By analytically continuing these identifications and using it to transport
F^{\bullet}_{t}
to
H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}}_{s},\mathbb{C})
, we obtain a holomorphic map
\phi:\tilde{X}\to\check{D}
where
\tilde{X}\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
is a universal cover of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
and
\check{D}
is a flag variety parameterizing filtrations of the given type on
H^{k}(X_{s}^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
. If
f
is quasiprojective, then in fact
\phi
lands in a semialgebraic subset of an algebraic subvariety, namely the mixed period domain—see
[
13
]
for details. Such a
\phi
is called the period map associated to the natural variation of Hodge structures on
R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
.

If
X
is once again affine, then around the basepoint
s
we may take forms
\alpha_{i}
which can be refined to a basis of each
F^{p}
; in general, the same can be done for algebraic de Rham cohomology. Taking a lift of the neighborhood
U
to
\tilde{X}
, the algebraic coordinates of
\phi
restricted to
U
are then rational functions in the ratios of certain minors of the matrix of periods
\int_{\gamma_{j}(u)}\alpha_{i}|_{X_{u}}
with respect to a chosen basis of
\gamma_{j}
of
H_{k}(X_{s}^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
. We call such functions the Hodge filtration coordinates at
s
.

1.4.
Comparison

Clearly the Hodge filtration coordinates are rational functions in the period, but a priori contain much less information. For example, the field generated over the rational function field
K(S)
by the periods is in fact closed under algebraic derivations - since the Lie derivative along an algebraic vector field is algebraic - whereas the field generated by the Hodge filtration coordinates are usually not differentially closed. On the other hand, the Hodge filtration coordinates are often more accessible.

1.5.
Main results

We now state a simplified version of our main theorem (Theorem
3.9
), which clarifies the relationship between the fields generated by the periods and the Hodge filtration coordinates.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a smooth algebraic family for which
R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
is a local system and let
\phi:\tilde{X}\to\check{D}
be the period map associated to the natural variation of Hodge structures on
R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
. Let
s\in S
be a basepoint and consider the following two subfields of
K(\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}},s})
, the field of germs of meromorphic functions at
s
.

(1)

Let
K(R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})_{s}
be the subfield generated over
K(S)
by the periods of degree
k
algebraic de Rham cohomology classes at
s
.

(2)

Let
K(\phi)_{s}
to be the subfield generated over
K(S)
by the Hodge filtration coordinates at
s
and let
K_{\partial}(\phi)_{s}
to be closure of
K(\phi)_{s}
under algebraic derivations.

Then

a)

K_{\partial}(\phi)_{s}\subset K(R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})_{s}
.

b)

Assume that the local system
R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
does not admit a summand of the form
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
, where
V_{2}
is a unitary local system with infinite monodromy. Then the algebraic closures coincide:
\overline{K_{\partial}(\phi)_{s}}=\overline{K(R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})_{s}}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Condition (b) above is also necessary. Indeed, if
V
is a complex variation of Hodge structures underlying a unitary local system, then the corresponding period map is constant, so the field
K_{\partial}(\phi)_{s}
will be trivial. However, if the local system is non-trivial, then
V
will give transcendental periods (see §2).

Remark 1.3
.

Condition (b), and therefore the equality of fields, holds for Shimura varieties corresponding to reductive groups without compact real factors.

The main theorem (see Theorem
3.9
) generalizes Theorem
1.1
in several ways:

(1)

The field
K(R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})
may be replaced with
K(V)
where
V
is any complex local system on
S^{\mathrm{an}}
with norm one eigenvalues at infinity. Briefly, the field
K(V)
is generated over
K(S)
by the germs of the flat coordinates of algebraic sections of the canonical algebraic structure on the flat vector bundle
(\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}}V,\nabla)
given by the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence. This algebraic structure is uniquely associated to
V
, and in the case
V=R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
recovers relative algebraic de Rham cohomology, which is why the notation only references the local system.

(2)

The map
\phi
may be replaced by any
\mathbb{R}_{{\mathrm{an}},\exp}
-definable
\pi_{1}(X^{\mathrm{an}},x)
-equivariant map
\phi:\tilde{X}\to Y^{\mathrm{an}}
, where
x\in X
is a basepoint,
Y
is an algebraic variety with a faithful action by a linear algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
and the
\pi_{1}(X^{\mathrm{an}},x)
-action on
Y
is via a homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(X^{\mathrm{an}},x)\to\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{C})
.

In particular, the theorem applies more generally to any local system
V
underlying a
mixed
variation of
complex
Hodge structures.

1.6.
Previous work

The paper is largely inspired by the work of Bertrand–Zudilin
[
7
]
, who prove several results on the transcendence degree of fields generated by Siegel modular forms and their derivatives. The moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties can be identified with the quotient
A_{g}^{\mathrm{an}}=\Sp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}_{g}
, and Siegel modular forms can be interpreted as the algebraic coordinates on
A_{g}
composed with the uniformization
\pi:\mathbb{H}_{g}\to A_{g}^{\mathrm{an}}
. As such, they are the inverse functions to the Hodge filtration coordinates of the universal family of abelian varieties. Theorem
1.1
then recovers the second part of
[
7
, Theorem 1]
. Note that in the general context the inverse functions of the the Hodge filtration coordinates are not well-defined, since the period map will only be a local isomorphism in very special circumstances.

1.7.
Outline of Paper

In §2, using the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence we define a Riemann-Hilbert field for an arbitrary local system which generalizes the notion of periods. In §3 we relate definable meromorphic functions with finite-dimensional monodromy to the Riemann-Hilbert field of the corresponding local system, and we use this to prove the general form of our main theorem. We rely heavily on the Chow theorem of Peterzil–Starchenko
[
14
]
and its generalization in
[
4
]
.
In §4.1 we return to studying periods of algebraic families and prove our main result. We then work out in detail in §4.2 the fields under question in the case of the universal elliptic curve. In §4.3 we explain that even in the general case, periods of algebraic De-Rham cohomology classes can be thought of as integrals of meromorphic forms.

Local systems of holomorphic functions

3.1.
Universal covers and definability

Throughout this section, by definable we always mean definable in the o-minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{{\mathrm{an}},\exp}
; see
[
15
]
for details. Let
X
be a definable (complex) analytic space (for example, the definabilization of a complex algebraic variety). We can think of

as locally the zero-set of definable holomorphic functions, with definable holomorphic gluing functions (see
[
4

for full details). Let
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
be the universal cover of the associated analytic space. We say a meromorphic function

\tilde{f}^{*}g

\tilde{X}
. Likewise, for any definable analytic space

\phi:\tilde{X}\to Y^{\mathrm{an}}
is definable if for any open definable

3.2.
Local systems of definable meromorphic functions

Assume now that

the definabilization of

[
4

). For a complex local system

\overline{X}
of

has norm one eigenvalues at infinity then these two definable structures on

are equivalent. Observe that:

(1)

(2)

\pi_{1}(X^{\mathrm{an}},x)

(3)

Lemma 3.4

Suppose

Proof.

After passing to a Zariski open, let

.
We have a homomorphism of definable coherent sheaves

which by definable GAGA
[

]

which in turn is equal to evaluation on a rational section

∎

The main idea in the proof of the lemma also gives us a criterion for a subfield

. In general for a

Lemma 3.5

Suppose

As in the previous lemma, we obtain a map of definable sheaves

∎

4.
Periods of algebraic varieties

In this section we relate the previous sections work on local systems to integrals of algebraic forms in families of varieties.

4.1.
Background on periods

We begin with an informal but more detailed discussion of algebraic de Rham cohomology classes and their periods to explain how the main ideas of the previous sections can be used to understand them.

4.1.1.
Smooth de Rham cohomology

Let
X
be a smooth complex algebraic variety. The smooth Poincaré resolution is acyclic so we may naturally think of the cohomology
H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
as classes of closed smooth
k
-forms.

4.1.2.
Analytic de Rham cohomology

In the analytic category we have a resolution

0\to\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}^{1}\xrightarrow{d}\cdots

and therefore we obtain a natural isomorphism
\mathbb{H}^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})\cong H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
where

\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}:=\left[\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{1}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\xrightarrow{d}\cdots\xrightarrow{d}\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\dim X-1}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{\dim X}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\right].

We can think of the hypercohomology
\mathbb{H}^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})
in terms of Čech cohomology with respect to a covering by Stein open sets, and any Čech cocycle can be solved by a smooth form. Alternatively, the Dolbeault resolution provides an acyclic resolution of
\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\bullet}
, and in this way a hypercohomology class may be directly despresented by the class of a smooth form. Either way, integrating such a form provides the explicit isomorphism.

4.1.3.
Algebraic de Rham cohomology

In the algebraic category we have a complex

\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}:=\left[\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{1}_{X}\xrightarrow{d}\cdots\xrightarrow{d}\Omega_{X}^{\dim X-1}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{\dim X}_{X}\right].

By a theorem of Grothendieck, the natural map
\mathbb{H}^{k}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\bullet})\to\mathbb{H}^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})
obtained by analytifying a Čech cocycle with respect to an affine open cover is an isomorphism. We denote the composed isomorphism

(4.1.1)

\int_{X}:\mathbb{H}^{k}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\bullet})\to H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C}).

There are some circumstances in which algebraic de Rham cohomology classes can be thought of directly in terms of certain algebraic forms and for which the map
\int_{X}
is the usual notion of integration along a cycle; see section
4.3
for some discussion. In general the map can always be expressed in terms of integrating a Čech cocycle representative along the facets of a suitable singular cycle representative (see for instance Example
4.5
). There is a natural map

\ker\left(H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{k})\xrightarrow{d}H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{k+1})\right)\to\mathbb{H}^{k}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\bullet})

associating to a closed algebraic
k
-form the obvious de Rham cohomology class, and in the image
\int_{X}
is the usual integration map.

4.1.4.
De Rham cohomology in families

Let
f:X\to S
be an algebraic family which is real analytically locally trivial. We can form the local system
V=R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
whose fibers are the cohomologies
H^{k}(X_{t}^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
, identified locally on
S^{\mathrm{an}}
via a local trivialization of
f
. There is an associated analytic flat vector bundle
(V_{\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}},\nabla)
which by the relative version of the analytic de Rham complex is computed as
R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{an}}/S^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\bullet}
, where the connection is given by lifting vector fields and taking the Lie derivative. The Lie derivative along an algebraic tangent field is algebraic, so
R^{k}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S}
is naturally an algebraic flat vector bundle, and it is in fact the canonical algebraic structure on
V_{\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}}
guaranteed by the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence
[
9
]
. The resulting isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}}V_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\to\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}}V

fiberwise restricts to the integration isomorphism of section (
4.1.1
).

We now describe the flat coordinates of algebraic sections. Around any point
s\in S
we can take a neighborhood
s\in U\subset S^{\mathrm{an}}
and a trivialization
X^{\mathrm{an}}|_{U}\cong X_{s}\times U
restricting to the identity at
s
which we think of as a family of diffeomorphisms
f_{u}:X^{\mathrm{an}}_{u}\to X^{\mathrm{an}}_{s}
, and the flat continuation of a class
\beta\in H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
is given by
\beta(u):=f_{u}^{*}\beta\in H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}}_{u},\mathbb{C})
. Likewise we can continue a basis of cycles
\gamma_{i}\in H_{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}}_{s},\mathbb{C})
as
\gamma_{i}(u):=(f_{u}^{-1})_{*}\gamma_{i}
. The above isomorphism then maps an algebraic de Rham cohomology class
\alpha
to

1\otimes\alpha\mapsto\sum_{i}\left(\int_{\gamma_{i}(u)}\alpha|_{X_{u}}\right)\otimes\gamma_{i}^{\vee}(u).

The integrals
\int_{\gamma_{i}(u)}\alpha|_{X_{u}}
therefore generate
K(V)
for
V=R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
. Again, these functions can ultimately be interpreted in terms of integrals of algebraic forms, and at the very least we have the following:

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a family of smooth varieties which is real analytically locally trivial,
k\geq 0
, and let
V
be the local system
R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
on
S^{{\mathrm{an}}}
. Then the periods of a regular fiberwise closed
k
-form on
X
along some locally constant
k
-dimensional homology class lie in
K(V)
.

The Hodge filtration
F^{\bullet}
is in fact an algebraic filtration on
R^{k}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}
. In the case that
f
is smooth projective, it is given by truncating the complex
\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet}
.

We shall need the following lemma for the proof of Theorem
1.1

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{G},\mathbf{H}
be algebraic groups with
\mathbf{G}
semisimple, and let
V
be an indecomposable representation of
\mathbf{G}\times\mathbf{H}
. Then
V\cong V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
for
V_{1},V_{2}
representations of
\mathbf{G},\mathbf{H}
respectively.

Proof.

We first claim that
V
is isoytopic as a representation of
\mathbf{G}
. Indeed, write
V=\oplus_{i=1}^{m}V^{(i)}
where the
V^{(i)}
are the isoytopic components of
V
. Then any
h\in\mathbf{H}
gives an element of
\End_{\mathbf{G}}V
and must therefore preserve the decomposition. Thus the
V^{(i)}
are all
\mathbf{G}\times\mathbf{H}
summands of
V
, and the claim follows since we assumed that
V
is indecomposable.

Thus, there is an irreducible representation
V_{1}
which is the only irreducible
\mathbf{G}
-subquotient of
V
, and since
\mathbf{G}
is semisimple it follows that
V\cong V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
as a
\mathbf{G}
-representation, with
\mathbf{G}
acting trivially on
V_{2}
. But now
\End_{\mathbf{G}}(V)\cong\End(V_{2})
and so the action of
\mathbf{H}
is induced from an action of
\mathbf{H}
on
V_{2}
. The claim follows.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From the above discussion, algebraic de Rham cohomology is the canonical algebraic structure on
V
, and the periods
\int_{\gamma(u)}\alpha|_{X_{u}}
for
\alpha
an algebraic degree
k
de Rham cohomology class and
\gamma(u)
a flat degree
k
cycle together generate
K(V)
. The period domain coordinates are ratios of the periods, and
K(V)
is differentially closed by
Lemma

2.3
, so we always have the claimed containment. Theorem
3.9
will then imply the result once we demonstrate that the conditions on the period map
\phi
are met.

The definability follows from
[
3
]
. The algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
can be taken to be the Zariski closure of the image of the monodromy representation of
V
. Thus
\mathbf{G}
is a normal subgroup of the derived group of the generic Mumford–Tate group
[
1
]
. Moreover, the Zariski closure of the image of
\phi
is a corresponding weak Mumford–Tate domain, namely a
\mathbf{G}
-orbit.

It remains to show that the action of
\mathbf{G}
on the weak Mumford–Tate domain
\check{D}
has finite kernel under the assumption in the theorem. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
\mathbf{G}
.
It is stable by the action of the generic Mumford–Tate group, hence underlies a sub-variation of Hodge structures of
\End(V)
.
The stabilizer of a point
p\in\check{D}
is
\exp(F^{0}\mathfrak{g})
, so the kernel
\mathbf{H}\subset\mathbf{G}
of the action is the intersection of all conjugates of
\exp(F^{0}\mathfrak{g})
. Its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
is therefore an ideal
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
which is contained in
F^{0}\mathfrak{g}
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
is also a Hodge substructure (as it is again fixed by the generic Mumford–Tate group), and defined over
\mathbf{R}
, hence we must have
\mathfrak{h}\cap W_{-1}\mathfrak{g}=0
.

We claim that we have a splitting
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
as Lie algebras and Hodge structures. Indeed, since
\operatorname{gr}_{0}^{W}\mathfrak{g}
is pure it must be semisimple, so we may write
\operatorname{gr}_{0}^{W}\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
for some ideal
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
which is also a hodge substructure. Now we may take
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
to be the pre-image of
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Moreover, since
\mathfrak{h}
is a pure Hodge structure of weight 0 contained in
F^{0}\mathfrak{g}
it must be Hodge–Tate, and is therefore unitary. Therefore by
Lemma

4.2
, we contradict the assumption in the theorem unless
\mathfrak{h}=0
as desired.

∎

4.2.
Elliptic curves

4.2.1.
Analytic presentation

Take
S
be the modular curve, so that
S^{\mathrm{an}}=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbf{H}
, and
f:X\to S
to be the universal family of elliptic curves, so
X^{\mathrm{an}}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{C}\times\mathbf{H}
, where the
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
action is

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot(z,\tau)=\left(\frac{z}{c\tau+d},\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right).

4.2.2.
Computing the 1-form

On each fiber the form
dz
is the unique regular 1-form up to scale, but this does not descend to
X^{{\mathrm{an}}}
. Instead, letting
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
it satisfies
\gamma^{*}dz=\frac{dz}{c\tau+d}
. To remedy this, we use the
j
function, which satisfies

j^{\prime}(\gamma\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{2}j^{\prime}(\tau)
, and so

j^{\prime}(\gamma\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}=(c\tau+d)j^{\prime}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, so the form
\omega:=j^{\prime}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}dz
does descend to
S^{{\mathrm{an}}}
. (This is a multivalued function with finite monodromy so we must pass to a finite étale cover to obtain a regular 1-form, but we will take algebraic closures in the end so we will ignore this issue for now).
Moreover, this is a definable 1-form so by definable GAGA
[
4
]
it gives an algebraic 1-form on
S
. The periods of
\omega
are simply the integrals of
\omega
from
0
to
1
and
0
to
\tau
so they are
j^{\prime}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\tau j^{\prime}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

4.2.3.
Computing the two other periods

Now there is another class in
H^{1}_{{}_{\mathrm{dR}}}
other than
[\omega]
. We could represent it by a meromorphic 1-form but this requires the elliptic
\wp
-function and integrating it is tricky. However, we can produce a new class by applying the connection
\nabla_{\partial/\partial j}[\omega]
, and the resulting periods will be the derivatives of the periods of
[\omega]
. So we may simply differentiate the two periods we have so far to find two others which will span the 4-dimensional vector space of periods. Doing this gives

R_{1}(\tau)=\frac{j^{\prime\prime}(\tau)}{j^{\prime}(\tau)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

R_{2}(\tau)=\tau R_{1}(\tau)-\frac{2}{j^{\prime}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

One may in fact check that
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on the vector space
\langle R_{1},R_{2}\rangle
giving the standard representation.
Finally, note that
j(\tau)
is an algebraic co-ordinate on the modular curve.
Thus, the algebraic closure of the field of periods is
\overline{\mathbb{C}(\tau,j(\tau),j^{\prime}(\tau),j^{\prime\prime}(\tau))}
.

4.2.4.
Computing
K_{\partial}(\phi)

The period map here is simply the lift
\tilde{S^{{\mathrm{an}}}}\rightarrow\mathbf{H}
so the Grassmanian co-ordinate is
\tau
, and to compute
K_{\partial}(\phi)
we have to differentiate with respect to
\tau
. Note that
\frac{d\tau}{dj}=j^{\prime}(\tau)^{-1}
and
\frac{dh}{dj}=\frac{dh}{d\tau}\frac{d\tau}{dj}
it is from then on sufficient to differentiate with respect to
\tau
. Noting that
j^{\prime\prime\prime}
is rational in
j,j^{\prime},j^{\prime\prime}
we conclude that

K_{\partial}(\phi)=\mathbb{C}(\tau,j(\tau),j^{\prime}(\tau),j^{\prime\prime}(\tau))

which has the same algebraic closure as the field of periods.

4.2.5.
Necessity of the algebraic closure

Working a bit more carefully, we may pass to an étale cover
Y
of
S
where
j^{\prime}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is holomorphic, and thus algebraic. Let
\mathbb{C}(Y)
denote the field of rational functions on
Y
which is a finite extension of
\mathbb{C}(j)
. Then by the above analysis the period field is

\mathbb{C}(Y)(\tau,j^{\prime}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}},j^{\prime\prime}(\tau))

whereas

K_{\partial}(\phi)=\mathbb{C}(Y)(\tau,j^{\prime}(\tau),j^{\prime\prime}(\tau)),

so we only have a containment of fields in general without taking closure.

4.3.
Interpreting the Riemann-Hilbert field through periods

In general it is somewhat complicated to describe the comparison between algebraic de Rham cohomology and singular cohomology in terms of integration. In this section we describe some circumstances where there is a direct link.

The simplest example is provided by an affine family.

Example 4.3
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth family over a smooth affine base
S
such that
V=R^{k}f_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
is a local system. By general theory
V
supports a variation of mixed Hodge structures. If
X
is affine then we have
R^{k}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}=f_{*}\mathscr{H}^{k}(\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet})=f_{*}Z^{k}_{X/S}/f_{*}B^{k}_{X/S}
where

\displaystyle Z^{k}_{Y/S}

\displaystyle=\ker\left(\Omega_{X/S}^{k}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{k+1}_{X/S}\right)

\displaystyle B^{k}_{Y/S}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{img}\left(\Omega_{X/S}^{k-1}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{k}_{X/S}\right)

since coherent sheaves have no higher cohomology. Likewise for the analytic de Rham cohomology. Thus, in this case the comparison

(R^{k}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet})^{\mathrm{an}}\to R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{an}}/S^{\mathrm{an}}}^{\bullet}\to\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}}R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}

is directly seen to be fiberwise integration, and
K(V)
is generated by integrals of fiberwise closed
k
-forms on
X
along
k
-cycles.

Example 4.4
.

In fact, in the previous example, if we just assume
S
is affine and
f
is quasiprojective, by Jouanolou’s trick
[
11
]
there is a vector bundle
E
over
X
and an
E
-torsor
Y
whose total space is affine. In particular the map
p:Y\to X
is a homotopy equivalence upon analytification. Thus, we have
R^{k}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet}\cong R^{k}g_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{Y/S}
where
g=f\circ p
, and so at the cost of changing the family the field
K(V)
is still generated by integrals of closed algebraic
k
-forms along
k
-cycles.

Example 4.5
.

In this example we explicitly describe the integration map on Čech cocycles computing algebraic de Rham cohomology in degree 1. Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety. The group
\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{\bullet})
can be described as follows:

Let
X_{i}
be an affine cover of
X
. Then
\mathbb{H}^{1}(X,\Omega^{\bullet}_{X})
is the quotient of the vector space generated by pairs
(f_{ij},\alpha_{i})
where
f_{ij}
is a Čech 2-cocycle for
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\alpha_{i}
is a Čech 1-cochain for
\Omega_{X}^{1}
such that
df_{ij}=\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}
, modulo the vector subspace generated by pairs of the form
(f_{i}-f_{j},0)
for
f_{i}
a Čech 1-cochain for
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. We claim that the integration of
(f_{ij},\alpha_{i})
along a 1-cycle
\gamma
is obtained as follows:

We write
\gamma
as a union of
1
-simplices (paths)
\bigcup_{i\in I}\gamma_{i}
where each
\gamma_{i}
is contained in a single
X_{b(i)}
. Pick an isomorphism
R:I\rightarrow I
such that
\gamma_{i}(0)=\gamma_{R(i)}(1)
. We then define the integral to be

\sum_{i\in I}\left(\int_{\gamma_{i}}\alpha_{b(i)}+f_{iR(i)}(\gamma_{i}(0))\right).

It is easy to check that this is a well-defined map on cohomology, and that it is functorial. Since it is the correct map on
1
-forms (classes with all
f_{ij}=0)
it follows that it is the correct map.

This can be generalized to higher dimensions, where one has to simplicially subdivide the integrating cycle and take the sum over all simplices of the integrals of the differential forms with certain rational coefficients.

Example 4.6
.

Let
C
be a smooth proper curve of genus
g
, and let
U\subset C
be an affine open subset, the complement of finitely many points. Example
4.3
shows that every class in
H^{1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
is the smooth de Rham cohomology class of an algebraic
1
-form on
U
, and that the comparison
\int_{C}:\mathbb{H}^{1}(U,\Omega_{U/S}^{\bullet})\to H^{1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
is given by integration in the usual sense.

It is a classical fact that every class is represented by an algebraic
1
-form on
U
with at worst logarithmic poles at
C\setminus C
. We can also describe the Hodge filtration:
F^{p}H^{1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
is
0
for
p>1
, all of
H^{1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
if
p<1
, and
F^{1}H^{1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
is the subspace of de Rham cohomology classes of algebraic
1
-forms that extend regularly to
C
.

The descriptions also hold in families.

The final example can be generalized to higher dimensions. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety,
D
an ample divisor, and
c=[D]\in H^{2}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
. Recall that we have a Lefschetz decomposition:

H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})=\bigoplus_{0\leq j\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor}c^{j}\cup H^{k-2j}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{prim}}

where
H^{n-k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{prim}}:=\ker(c^{k+1}\cup\;\cdot\;)
.

For a smooth projective family
f:X\to S
with a relatively ample class
c
, we likewise have a direct sum decomposition of
R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
, and the following proposition says that the Riemann–Hilbert field of the primitive part is generated by integrals of rational forms.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
f:X\to S
be a smooth projective family and let
D
be a smooth relatively ample divisor of
X
which is smooth over
S
. Let
U=X\setminus D
and
c=[D]\in H^{0}(S^{\mathrm{an}},R^{2}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})
. Then for each
k\leq\dim X-\dim S
, the Riemann–Hilbert field of
V=(R^{k}(f^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}})_{\mathrm{prim}}
is generated by fiberwise integrals of algebraic
k
-forms on
U
with at worst logarithmic poles along
D
whose residue is exact.

Proposition
4.7
is fairly standard (see for example
[
16
]
), but we include the proof for completeness.

Proof.

Let
i:D\to X
and
j:U\to X
the inclusions,
g=f\circ j
, and
h=f\circ i
. We have a short exact sequence of complexes

0\rightarrow\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet}\rightarrow\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet}(\log D)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Res}}i_{*}\Omega_{D/S}^{\bullet}[-1]\rightarrow 0

whose analytification is canonically quasi-isomorphic to the exact triangle
f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}\to Rj_{*}g^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}\to i_{*}i^{!}h^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}[1]\to f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}[1]
. The associated long exact sequence contains

R^{k-2}h_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{D/S}\rightarrow R^{k}f_{*}\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet}\rightarrow R^{k}g_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Res}}R^{k-1}h_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{D/S}.

and the connecting homomorphism is identified with the pushforward map
i_{*}:H^{k-2}(D^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})\rightarrow H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{C})
. By the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem,
R^{k}f_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\to R^{k}f_{*}i_{*}\mathbb{C}_{D^{\mathrm{an}}}
is an isomorphism for
k<\dim X-\dim S
. Since the composition

R^{k-2}f_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\xrightarrow{i^{*}}R^{k-2}f_{*}i_{*}\mathbb{C}_{D^{\mathrm{an}}}\xrightarrow{i_{*}}R^{k}f_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}

is cupping with
c
by the projection formula, it follows that the image of
i_{*}
is the imprimitive part of
R^{k}f_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}
, and therefore that the image of
R^{k}f_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}
in
R^{k}g_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{U/S}
analytifies to
\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}}V
. On the other hand,
R^{k}g_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{U/S}=g_{*}Z_{X/S}^{k}/g_{*}B_{X/S}^{k}
since
g
is affine, and
\mathcal{O}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}_{S^{\mathrm{an}}}}V
is therefore identified with the analytification of the subsheaf of
g_{*}Z_{U/S}^{k}/g_{*}B_{U/S}^{k}
given by classes of regular forms on
U
whose residues along
D
are
0
in
R^{k-1}h_{*}\Omega^{\bullet}_{D/S}
.
∎

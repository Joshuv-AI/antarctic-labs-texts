A head-to-head comparison of sextant-systematic biopsy vs. extended-systematic biopsy for prostate cancer diagnosis in the era of MRI-targeted biopsy: SEXTANT-PRO non-inferiority randomized clinical trial
Authors: Ruiyi Deng, Jiaheng Shang, Jingyun Wu, Shaojuan Tian, Derun Li, Qi Shen, Shuai Hu, Meixia Shang, Jianhui Qiu, Jingcheng Zhou, Lin Cai, Kan Gong, Yi Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundIn recent years, combined MRI-targeted biopsy (TB) and 12-core extended systematic biopsy (SB) (TB + 12SB) has been recommended for biopsy-naïve patients with MRI-visible lesions for prostate cancer (PCa) diagnosis. However, extended biopsy increases complication burden and healthcare expenditure. This head-to-head RCT aims to compare the novel combined MRI-TB and sextant six-core SB (TB + 6SB) scheme and classical TB + 12SB for PCa diagnosis.MethodsFrom December 2024 to June 2025, this single-center non-inferiority RCT (NCT06684652) consecutively enrolled 506 biopsy-naïve men with single suspicious lesion on prostate mpMRI. Patients were randomized to undergo TB + 12SB (n = 252) or TB + 6SB (n = 254) through transperineal cognitive fusion technique. The study outcome included clinically significant PCa [csPCa, grade group (GG) ≥2, primary outcome], diagnostic accuracy, complication rates, and health economic profiles.FindingsThe baseline characteristics were similar. The csPCa detection rate of TB + 6SB [54.3%, 138/254] was non-inferior to that of TB + 12SB [54.8%, (138/252)] [rate difference and 95% confidence interval: −0.5% (−9.2%, 8.3%)]. Comparable detection was also observed in PCa, high-grade PCa, and clinically insignificant PCa. The concordance in GG between biopsy and radical prostatectomy whole-mount specimens was similar for both TB + 12SB [52.3% (34/65), κ = 0.55] and TB + 6SB [51.7% (30/58), κ = 0.50] groups (p = 0.25). TB + 6SB demonstrated superior safety profiles through significantly relieving the discomfort during the procedure, reducing the post-biopsy pelvic pain, and significantly improving the quality of life (p < 0.05), while concurrently optimizing procedural workflow efficiency and histopathology resource utilization.InterpretationTB + 6SB achieves non-inferior diagnostic efficacy to classical TB + 12SB with reduced cores and improved safety, positioning it as an effective strategy for PCa diagnosis in biopsy-naïve patients with solitary MRI-suspicious lesions.FundingBeijing Research Ward Excellence Program (BRWEP2024W054070105), National High Level Hospital Clinical Research Funding (Interdepartmental Research Project of Peking University First Hospital, 2023IR27, 2024IR16).Keywords: Clinically significant prostate cancer, Randomized controlled trial, Sextant systematic biopsy, Targeted biopsy, Multiparametric MRI

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Though combined MRI-targeted biopsy (TB) and 12-core extended systematic biopsy (SB) (TB + 12SB) has been recommended for prostate cancer (PCa) diagnosis, extended biopsy increases complication burden and healthcare expenditure. Some observational studies initially investigated the efficacy of combined TB and six-core sextant-SB (TB + 6SB) scheme in PCa diagnosis.

We searched PubMed up to June 1, 2025 without any language for evidence using the following key search terms: (“sextant systematic biopsy” or “laterally sextant biopsy”) and “prostate cancer”. Seven studies were identified. However, none of these studies was RCT. There is a lack of comprehensive evaluation of diagnostic efficacy, safety, and health economic profiles for the TB + 6SB scheme.

Added value of this study

In this prospective, randomized controlled trial, we found that TB + 6SB achieves non-inferior clinically significant PCa (csPCa) detection and diagnostic efficacy to classical TB + 12SB with significantly reduced cores. Compared with TB + 12SB, TB + 6SB demonstrated superior safety profiles through reducing the overall complication rate, significantly reducing post-biopsy pain and improving the quality of life, while concurrently optimizing procedural workflow efficiency and histopathology resource utilization.

Implications of all the available evidence

This head-to-head RCT provides high-quality evidence that TB + 6SB is an effective strategy for PCa diagnosis in biopsy-naïve patients with solitary MRI-suspicious lesions in clinical practice.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) remains the most prevalent malignancy and second most common cause of cancer-related mortality among American males.1 The incidence shows an annual 3% increase overall, with advanced/metastatic cases exceeding 4.5% annually.1 Thus, urgently implementing risk-adapted PCa screening protocols and early diagnosis is critical to stop the rapid rise in advanced PCa and prevent projected mortality escalations. Prostate biopsy remains pivotal in the diagnostic verification, risk categorization, oncological management continuum, and therapeutic decision-making in PCa.2 With the rapid development and wide application of multiparametric MRI (mpMRI), MRI-targeted biopsy (TB) now optimizes clinically significant PCa (csPCa) detection through precision guidance. The combined MRI-TB and extended 12-core systematic biopsy (TB + 12SB) has been recommended for PCa diagnosis in biopsy-naïve patients with MRI-suspected lesions.3 However, it's worthwhile to note that currently the systematic biopsy (SB) still applies the traditional 10 to 12 or even 24 cores while combined with MRI-TB. Escalating the number of biopsy-cores would unavoidably increase the discomfort and distress level, heighten the incidence of biopsy-related complications, lead to overdiagnosis, and exacerbate biopsy-related procedural cost and time.4 Therefore, improving csPCa detection while optimizing biopsy schemes and minimizing total biopsy-cores remains a critical challenge in the era of MRI-TB.

Considering the limited mpMRI negative predictive value and multifocal nature of PCa, comprehensive knowledge of the location of all cancer foci inside the prostate through SB is essential for accurate risk stratification and therapeutic planning.5 However, current evidence indicates a diagnostic plateau beyond a biopsy-core threshold, where increasing biopsy-cores fails to enhance csPCa detection.6 The enhanced diagnostic efficacy of MRI-TB challenges the imperative for routine extended 12-core SB in the contemporary TB + 12SB scheme.7 Many studies have explored multiple biopsy schemes by adjusting the number and location of biopsy-cores to achieve optimal diagnostic efficiency while minimizing overdiagnosis and complications.7, 8, 9 Previous studies demonstrated that peripheral zone (PZ) lesions had elevated Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System (PI-RADS) scores compared to transitional zone (TZ) lesions.10 Histopathologically confirmed csPCa in 38.9% of MRI-negative prostate lateral lobes through radical prostatectomy (RP) specimen analysis.11 Based on these results, it's hypothesized that when the MRI-TB is effectively performed, reducing the SB-cores and conducting the SB mainly in the PZ of the prostate, which has a high incidence of PCa, are non-inferior to that of classical TB + 12SB scheme. Some previous studies have investigated the efficacy and feasibility of combined TB and six-core sextant-SB (TB + 6SB) scheme in PCa diagnosis through retrospective analysis or cohort study.7,8,10,12,13 However, there is still a lack of high-quality, prospective evidence. The safety and health economic profiles require further rigorous assessment. This head-to-head non-inferiority RCT will comprehensively evaluate the TB + 6SB and classical TB + 12SB schemes to establish evidence-based optimization criteria for prostate biopsy strategies.

Methods

Study design

This is a single-center prospective non-inferiority RCT evaluating the diagnostic efficacy and safety profile of the TB + 6SB and TB + 12SB schemes for patients with a suspicious lesion on prostate mpMRI. The study was approved by the IRB of Peking University First Hospital (2024-609) and was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06684652). All participants gave written informed consent. The Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) flow diagram is shown in Fig. 1. The work has been reported in line with CONSORT Guidelines.14,15

Participant

Between December 2024 and June 2025, 506 consecutive biopsy-naïve men with positive mpMRI scheduled for prostate biopsy at Peking University First Hospital (Beijing, China) were recruited. The inclusion criteria were: (1) aged 45–85 years; (2) biopsy-naïve; (3) high mpMRI quality; (4) having solitary suspicious lesion on mpMRI (PI-RADS V2.1 ≥ 3); (5) prostate-specific antigen (PSA) ≤50 ng/ml. The exclusion criteria were: (1) previous prostate biopsy; (2) any prior treatment for PCa; (3) any contraindications for MRI examination or biopsy; (4) poor quality of MR images; (5) incomplete clinicopathological information. The detailed criteria were shown in Supplementary materials. All participants provided written informed consent.

Randomization and masking

Eligible participants were allocated completely at random in a 1:1 ratio to TB + 6SB group or TB + 12SB group by independent research nurses according to a computer-generated list of random numbers, which was password-protected in a central database to ensure the allocation concealment. The allocation was informed to the urologists before the biopsy while being concealed from the patients and pathologists.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated based on the previous publications and pre-experiment results at our institution.8,12 It's estimated that the csPCa detection rates of TB + 12SB and TB + 6SB groups were 56% and 54%, respectively. A parallel, two-group design was used to test whether the csPCa detection rate of TB + 6SB (P1) is non-inferior to that of TB + 12SB (P2) with a non-inferiority margin of −15% (H0: P1–P2 ≤ −15% vs. H1: P1–P2 > −15%). The comparison was made through a one-sided, two-sample Z test with a type-I error rate (α) of 0.025 and a power of 80%. Thus, 230 participants per group would be required. Accounting for a dropout rate of 10%, 506 participants would be required to recruit.

Outcomes

The primary objective was to compare the csPCa (International Society of Urological Pathology [ISUP] Grade Group [GG] ≥2) detection rates of the two biopsy schemes. The secondary outcome includes detection rates of PCa, clinically insignificant PCa (ciPCa, GG = 1), and high-grade PCa (GG ≥ 3), complication rates, functional outcomes, and health economic profiles such as procedure time and examination costs. Complication rates were evaluated through the general symptoms questionnaire within 7 days and the Clavien-Dindo classification within 30 days after biopsy. Functional outcomes were assessed by the International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS), the Overactive Bladder Symptom Score (OABSS), and the Expanded PCa Index Composite (EPIC) Urinary assessment questionnaire before and within 30 days after the biopsy. All questionnaires were validated in Chinese. The procedure time was calculated from inserting the probe into the rectum to the end of the biopsy.

Procedure

All patients underwent 3.0 T mpMRI (GE750, GE Healthcare; Ingenia, Philips Healthcare). The images were assessed by a highly-experienced radiologist (J.W.) according to the PI-RADS V2.1 guideline.16 All patients underwent transperineal cognitive-fusion TB + SB under local anesthesia of 20 cc 1% lidocaine. Oral antibiotic was routinely administered 30 min before the biopsy. The ultrasound equipment including a color Doppler ultrasound instrument (Hitachi HiVision, Carbon Med), transrectal bi-planar probes, and 18-gauge needles (Bard biopsy system) were adopted for the biopsy procedure. For all patients, three-core TB within the predefined mpMRI index lesion (ROI) was firstly performed. Then patients in the TB + 12SB group received classical 12-core extended-SB, while patients in the TB + 6SB group received six-core sextant-SB bilaterally with each side two cores in the peripheral zone and one core in the transitional zone (Fig. 1). Two urologists (Y.L. and D.L.) independently conducted TB and SB. The urologists who conducted the SB were blinded for MRI lesion location. Patients were followed-up on the 7th and the 30th days after the biopsy.

Biopsy specimens were labeled individually and evaluated by experienced uropathologists (Q.S. and S.H.) according to the ISUP criteria.17 Each biopsy-core firstly underwent assessment by one uropathologist who was blinded to the allocation. Then the aggregated results for global Gleason grading, SB (global and per side), and TB were independently reported by another uropathologist, respectively. The number of total and positive cores, biopsy Gleason score (GS), and maximum cancer core involvement in all cores of each patient were acquired according to the Standards of Reporting for MRI Targeted Biopsy Studies (START) criteria and interpreted according to the recommendations of the ISUP.17 For the robot-assisted laparoscopic RP (RARP) specimens, the overall grade was assigned based on the part with the highest GS according to the recommendations of the ISUP.17 For RP specimens with multifocal tumors, both the global GS and the GS of the index lesion were reported. The GS of (a) largest, (b) highest stage, and (c) highest grade lesions would be reported separately if the above are not identical. The global GS of RP specimens was utilized in the comparison analyses between the biopsy and RP specimens. The presence of minor/tertiary patterns was considered separately for biopsy and RP specimens. The ISUP recommended including tertiary higher grade patterns in the biopsy Gleason score (GS), regardless of extent. For RP specimens, any amount of Gleason pattern 5 <5% was reported as “minor pattern” but not included in the score, while any amount of Gleason pattern 5 ≥5% was included in the GS as the secondary pattern.17 The detailed description for the assessment of intraductal carcinoma and minor/tertiary pattern is shown in Supplementary materials.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were utilized to present baseline characteristics. Median (interquartile range [IQR]), mean (standard deviation [SD]), numbers and percentages were presented based on the data types.

For primary outcome, the rate difference (RD) and the two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) for csPCa detection were exactly calculated to test the non-inferiority hypothesis between TB + 6SB and TB + 12SB groups. Non-inferiority was established if the lower bound of the CI > −15%. Regarding the secondary outcomes, rate comparison between groups was performed utilizing the appropriate methods such as the Pearson's Chi square test with Yates' continuity correction, or Fisher's Exact Test. For comparisons of ordered categorical variables between the two groups, the Cochran–Armitage test for trend was employed. For paired categorical data comparisons, exact McNemar's test was employed to evaluate the discordant pairs between different groups. Continuous variables were compared between groups using parametric Student's t-test or nonparametric Mann–Whitney U test based on distributional assumptions. Prespecified subgroup analyses used identical group-comparison methods without additional statistical adjustments. Concordance in GG between biopsy and RP specimens was assessed using weighted Cohen's kappa (κ), accounting for ordinal category disagreement magnitude. For exploratory analyses of secondary outcomes, a two-sided α = 0.05 was used. All analyses employed complete cases without missing data imputation. All statistical analyses were executed in R 4.3.1.

The funders of the study had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, or writing of the report. Yi Liu and Kan Gong have full access to all the data in the study and take responsibility for the integrity of the data and the accuracy of the data analysis.

The baseline characteristics of the two groups were similar, with prostate volume, laterality, and PIRADS V2.1 score slightly differing (Table 1). There was no protocol deviation.

Diagnostic efficacy

Cancer detection

All patients were available for the primary outcome assessment. The post-biopsy clinicopathological characteristics are shown in Table 2. CsPCa was diagnosed in 138 of 254 participants (54.3%) in the TB + 6SB group, as compared with 138 of 252 participants (54.8%) in the TB + 12SB group (p = 0.99). The RD between the two groups was −0.5% (95% CI: −9.2%, 8.3%), and the lower bound (−9.2%) exceeded the prespecified non-inferiority margin (−15%), confirming the conclusion of non-inferiority.

The PCa and high-grade PCa detection rates of TB + 6SB were comparable to that of TB + 12SB [PCa: 65.0% (165/254) vs. 66.3% (167/252), p = 0.76; high-grade PCa: 29.1% (74/254) vs. 28.6% (72/252), p = 0.89]. The ciPCa detection rate of TB + 6SB was similar to that of TB + 12SB [10.6% (27/254) vs. 11.5% (29/252), p = 0.75]. Similar GG distribution (Supplementary Figure S1, Cochran–Armitage test, p = 0.72) and maximum cancer core involvement of biopsy specimens (50.0 [0, 90.0] vs. 50.0 [0, 90.0], p = 0.78) were observed between the two groups. The proportion of positive cores of TB + 6SB was significantly higher than that of TB + 12SB (33.3 [0, 55.6] vs. 20.0 [0, 46.7], p = 0.020).

Detailed diagnostic efficiency analysis

To gain deeper insights into the contributions of TB, and SB in PCa diagnosis, we evaluated their efficiency and incremental value across various biopsy types and GG distribution patterns (Table 3, Supplementary Figure S2). The added value of SB was defined as the detection rates of any csPCa, high-grade PCa, or PCa on SB compared with negative TB [e.g., PCa added value: 12 (GG1/no cancer on TB) + 2 (GG2/no cancer on TB) + 1 (GG5/no cancer on TB) in 252 TB + 12SB cases vs. 13 (GG1/no cancer on TB) + 2 (GG2/no cancer on TB) + 1 (GG3/no cancer on TB) in 254 TB + 6SB cases (Tables 3 and 4]). In the TB + 12SB group, 12-core extended-SB detected 118 (46.8%) csPCa, 61 (24.2%) high-grade PCa, and 151 (59.9%) PCa cases, with added value of 4.0% (10/252), 1.6% (4/252), and 6.0% (15/252) respectively. Six-core sextant-SB detected 116 (45.7%) csPCa, 58 (22.8%) high-grade PCa, and 147 (57.9%) PCa cases, with the added value of 2.0% (5/254), 2.4% (6/254), and 6.3% (16/254), respectively. The detection rates of higher GG PCa on 12-core extended-SB, compared with the GG on TB, were 10.7% (27/252) for TB + 12SB [12 (GG1/no cancer on TB) + 2 (GG2/no cancer on TB) + 7 (GG2/GG1) + 3 (GG3/GG2) + 1 (GG5/no cancer on TB) + 2 (GG5/GG3)] and 8.7% (22/254) for TB + 6SB [13 (GG1/no cancer on TB) + 2 (GG2/no cancer on TB) + 2 (GG2/GG1 on TB) + 1 (GG3/no cancer on TB) + 3 (GG3/GG2 on TB) + 1 (GG5/GG3 on TB)] (Table 3). The added value of TB was defined as any cancer detection through TB among patients with negative SB. The added value of TB for csPCa diagnosis was higher than that of SB in both groups [TB + 12SB: 7.9% (20/252) vs. 4.0% (10/252), p = 0.099; TB + 6SB: 8.7% (22/254) vs. 2.0% (5/254), p = 0.0015; Table 4].

A total of 123 patients underwent RARP treatments. Most of PCa (112/123, 91.1%) were located bilaterally in the RP whole-mount specimens (Table 5). Histopathological evaluation revealed similar concordance rates of GG between biopsy and RP specimens in the TB + 6SB [51.7% (30/58), κ = 0.50] and TB + 12SB [52.3% (34/65), κ = 0.55] cohorts (Fig. 3, p = 0.25, Z-test for comparison of two independent kappa coefficients). The rate of extraprostatic extension was non-significantly higher in the TB + 6SB group [36.2% (21/58)] than in the TB + 12SB group [33.8% (22/65)] (p = 0.11). The distribution of the T stage was similar between the groups.

Functional outcomes and safety profiles

Among 506 participants in this RCT, complete pre- and post-biopsy responses were obtained for the OABSS, IPSS, EPIC-Urinary, and general symptom questionnaires from 497 (98.2%), 498 (98.4%), 496 (98.0%), and 499 (98.6%) men, respectively. TB + 6SB group had a non-significantly lower complication rate of 66.1% (168/254) vs. 70.6% (178/252) and lower ≥ grade 2 complication rate [1 (0.4%) vs. 4 (1.6%)] in the TB + 12SB group (p = 0.21).

The pre-biopsy urinary function assessments demonstrated comparable baseline characteristics between groups (Supplementary Tables S2–S5). After the biopsy, the TB + 6SB group exhibited significantly lower OABSS scores vs. TB + 12SB (Supplementary Table S2, 3.0 [2.0, 5.0] vs. 3.0 [2.0, 7.0], p = 0.023). Though significant difference was observed in some specific profiles, the overall IPSS, EPIC-Urinary scores remained similar between the two groups at 7 days post-biopsy.

Compared with TB + 12SB, TB + 6SB significantly relieved the pain level (2.0 [2.0, 4.0] vs. 3.0 [2.0, 4.0], p = 0.0044) and discomfort level (3.0 [1.5, 4.0] vs. 3.0 [3.0, 5.0], p = 0.027) during the biopsy. Post-biopsy abdominal/pelvic pain was reported in 70 (27.8%) of the 252 cases in the TB + 12SB group, while only 44 (17.8%) of the 247 cases in the TB + 6SB group respectively (Supplementary Table S6, p = 0.011). Post-biopsy infection only developed in four patients (0.8%). TB + 6SB could significantly improve the quality of life after the biopsy (Supplementary Tables S5 and S6, p < 0.05) through reducing the occurrence of complications.

Health economic profiles

TB + 6SB demonstrated superior procedural efficiency compared to TB + 12SB, with a statistically significant reduction in number of biopsy cores (9 vs. 15) and operative duration (167.5 s [154.0 s, 187.0 s] vs. 234.0 s [220.0–258.3], p < 0.001). The TB + 6SB further decreased consumable requirements and lower histopathological analysis costs ($50.0 vs. $75.0 per case, Table 2).

Discussion

Current guidelines recommend performing MRI before prostate biopsy in men with suspected organ-confined disease.2 The classical TB + 12SB scheme consists of MRI-TB and 10- to 12-core extended-SB. The high number of biopsy-cores substantially increases the incidence of complications and elevates the risk of overdiagnosis without a significant increase in the csPCa detection. Optimizing biopsy-core reduction while preserving diagnostic efficacy may improve patient care by decreasing biopsy-related risks and costs.

In the era of MRI-TB, when TB is effectively conducted and allows confident detection of the ROI, do we still need to perform classical extended-SB? Previous studies have confirmed that beyond the core saturation threshold, further increasing SB-cores did not significantly improve the csPCa detection, while substantially increased complications such as urinary function reduction, infection, and hematuria.4 Some urologists make efforts to optimize biopsy schemes by decreasing SB-cores and adjusting the location of SB-cores. Choi et al. revealed MRI-occult csPCa in 38.9% (72/185) of radiographically negative lateral prostate zones upon RP specimen evaluation.11 Hu et al. indicated that an increased number of biopsy-sites in the PZ of the prostate, where PCa is mostly found, could significantly improve PCa detection and reduce misdiagnosis.18 They proposed that focusing SB mainly on laterally directed sextant biopsy when performing effective TB is an alternative scheme with a higher detection rate and lowest missed diagnosis rate compared with other reduced-core SB schemes.7,10,18,19 In this RCT, we conducted a head-to-head comparison and demonstrated that compared with TB + 12SB, TB + 6SB exhibited non-inferior diagnostic efficacy, lower procedural morbidity, reduced pain level, and improved post-biopsy quality of life with decreased biopsy-cores and operative duration. The diagnostic efficacy of TB + 6SB was non-inferior to that of TB + 12SB in all subgroups, further confirming the effectiveness of the TB + 6SB scheme. Performing TB + 6SB significantly increased the proportion of positive cores, reflecting the improvement of diagnostic efficiency. Furthermore, the concordance of pathological characteristics between RP and biopsy schemes could reflect the diagnostic accuracy. Comparable histopathological upgrading between RP and biopsy schemes validated the non-inferior diagnostic concordance of TB + 6SB vs. TB + 12SB in risk stratification accuracy. In summary, this study provided high-quality evidence for the application of the TB + 6SB scheme among biopsy-naïve patients.

Some other urologists also explored whether focusing the biopsy-cores around the ROI would be a better scheme. Based on the penumbra theory, predominant csPCa clustered within perilesional 10-mm margins surrounding index MRI-visible lesions.20 Several studies have explored perilesional biopsy (PB) or regional SB instead of standard SB as an alternative approach.21 Hagens et al. demonstrated that TB + PB achieved almost all csPCa detection in men with unilateral MRI-visible lesions.21 Some studies supported the application of TB + ipsilateral SB for unilateral positive MRI lesions.22 The latest EAU guideline has recommended TB + PB for the diagnosis of patients with visible suspicious lesions on MRI.2 However, the strength rating is still weak, and there is still a lack of studies evaluating the diagnostic accuracy of the TB + PB scheme utilizing the RP whole-mount specimens as reference, and reporting the postoperative and long-term follow-up outcomes. Thus, whether the TB + PB could be an appropriate alternative for classical TB + 12SB still requires further investigation. However, some urologists suggested that SB at the time of MRI-TB should not be omitted.10,13,18,19,23 Multifocality and intratumoral heterogeneity, the two important features of PCa, pose diagnostic challenges for PB-based schemes. Emerging basic researches highlighted the intratumoral heterogeneity of PCa and have suggested that the disease aggressiveness could not always be driven by the index lesion, and non-index tumor foci may harbor distinct genomic drivers of disease progression.24 Contemporary mpMRI demonstrated limited detection sensitivity for non-index lesions. With the negative predictive values ranging from 63% to 91% for PCa detection and the false-negative value ranging from 10% to 54%, the mpMRI is characterized by suboptimal sensitivity for low-grade and low-volume disease, underestimations of tumor volume, and it misses a substantial proportion of csPCa.23 Current mpMRI demonstrates non-negligible false-positive rates (∼30%), with significant tumor burden localized extralesionally, particularly exhibiting contralateral lobe involvement (15%). Consistent with the previous studies,25 the csPCa detection rates of contralateral-SB in the TB + 12SB and TB + 6SB groups were 16.7% (42/252) and 15.0% (38/254). Larger tumors located near the midline or apex exhibited elevated higher likelihoods of positive contralateral medial-orientated SB-cores.25 Among the 123 patients who underwent RARP treatments, 112 (91.1%) of them had bilateral PCa in the RP specimens. The proportion of bilateral invasion was significantly higher than the pre-biopsy mpMRI assessment. The presence of csPCa outside the index lesion demonstrates strong clinicopathological correlation with adverse surgical outcomes including nodal metastasis, extraprostatic extension, and seminal vesicle invasion. Omitting the csPCa outside the index lesion is related to increased biochemical recurrence, underscoring its prognostic implications.26 Therefore, SB mapping of the prostate outside the index lesion could provide information regarding the multifocal nature of the disease. The combination of MRI-TB and SB enhances preoperative tumor burden stratification, provides the urologists with complementary information for risk stratification and prognosis, enables surgical margin optimization and precise extracapsular disease localization, and guides the focal treatment or surgical approach.23,27

Regarding the safety profile, both groups exhibited low-grade Clavien-Dindo complications (grades 1–2) without long-term sequelae. The incidence of infection was only 0.8% (4/499). Though the OABSS total score and some specific items of the IPSS and EPIC-Urinary questionnaire exhibited improvement in the TB + 6SB group, the difference did not reach the minimal clinically important change. Therefore, the overall urinary function of the two groups was similar. Furthermore, the patients' points of view such as feelings were often neglected in previous studies. Psychological factors such as biopsy-related pain and discomfort could be determining factors in choosing biopsy schemes for a specific patient. A painful experience may reduce a patient's compliance and the procedure's acceptability. Our results showed that TB + 6SB could significantly relieve the discomfort during the procedure, reduce the post-biopsy pelvic pain, and significantly improve the quality of life. These results align with established evidence demonstrating a positive correlation between biopsy-core number and post-biopsy morbidity.28 In addition, compared to the classical TB + 12SB scheme, TB + 6SB demonstrated superior procedural efficiency by reducing operative duration and material utilization, concurrently lowering histopathological expenditure. The strategic core limitation remains diagnostic accuracy while preserving quality-of-life metrics, alleviating histology laboratory workload burdens, and thereby optimizing healthcare resource allocation.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first head-to-head RCT to evaluate the effectiveness, safety, and health economic profiles of TB + 6SB and classical TB + 12SB schemes. This RCT possessed several notable strengths. Prospectively designed consecutive enrollment and rigorous randomization guaranteed high-quality data collection. Considering that knowledge of lesion location on mpMRI may introduce operator-bias in SB-cores trajectory selection, to avoid the impact of TB, the urologists who conducted SB did not read the MR images before the procedure. Last but not least, when evaluating the diagnostic efficacy of the two schemes, we utilized both the biopsy and RP specimens as standard, robustly validating the non-inferior effectiveness of TB + 6SB vs. TB + 12SB. However, there are still some limitations. First, the monocentric design may limit the generalization of the results, and current findings require multicenter validation. Second, we only incorporated biopsy-naïve patients with single mpMRI-visible lesion, so the applicability of the TB + 6SB scheme among patients with multiple lesions and patients receiving any treatment or undergoing active surveillance warrants further investigation. Third, limited by the relatively short study period, the long-term oncological and functional outcome required further assessment. In addition, while employing the guideline-endorsed transperineal approach,2 our findings necessitate verification across transrectal modalities to establish universal procedural adaptability of TB + 6SB. Furthermore, recent advances in Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA), Positron Emission Tomography (PET) and Computed Tomography (CT) have enhanced PCa diagnosis, particularly for metastatic/recurrent lesions.29 However, the efficacy of PSMA PET/CT for primary prostatic lesions remains unverified due to insufficient PET spatial resolution for accurate tumor staging and difficulty in differentiating prostatic inflammation from malignancy.29,30 The health economic considerations also restricted routine PSMA PET/CT adoption in clinical practice. Consequently, PSMA PET/CT was not utilized in this RCT. Future studies are warranted to further assess its application in guiding TB and further enhancing PCa diagnostic accuracy.

In conclusion, this head-to-head RCT provides high-quality evidence supporting that TB + 6SB is an effective scheme for csPCa detection in biopsy-naïve patients with single mpMRI-visible lesion. TB + 6SB maintained non-inferior diagnostic efficacy to classical TB + 12SB while requiring significantly fewer biopsy-cores. This scheme could significantly reduce the procedural complications and improved operational efficiency, demonstrating both clinical safety and cost-effectiveness in histopathological workflows. In the future, multicenter validations are warranted to establish TB + 6SB's generalizability across diverse clinical practice settings.

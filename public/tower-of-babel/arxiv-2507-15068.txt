General scaling behavior of superconductors
V. R. Shaginyan, A. Z. Msezane, S. A. Artamonov

Abstract
The physics of high- T c T_{c} superconductors, which has been a major topic in condensed matter physics for more than thirty years, reveals some features of conventional superconductors. We analyze the scaling of the condensation energy E Δ E_{\Delta} divided by γ \gamma , E Δ / γ ≃ N ⁡ ( 0 ) ​ Δ 1 2 / γ E_{\Delta}/\gamma\simeq N(0)\Delta_{1}^{2}/\gamma , that equally applicable to both conventional and unconventional high- T c T_{c} superconductors. Here N ⁡ ( 0 ) N(0) is the density of states, Δ 1 \Delta_{1} is the maximum value of the superconducting gap and γ \gamma is the Sommerfeld coefficient. Basing on this observation, we analyze experimental facts that reveal the general scaling properties of both high- T c T_{c} and ordinary superconductors, and theoretically explain that the Homes’ law ρ s ​ 0 = ( 1 / 2 ​ π ​ λ D ) 2 = T c ​ σ ​ ( T c ) \rho_{s0}=(1/2\pi\lambda_{D})^{2}=T_{c}\sigma(T_{c}) is applicable to the both types of superconductors. Here ρ s ​ 0 \rho_{s0} is the superconducting electron density, λ D \lambda_{D} is the zero- T T penetration depth, σ \sigma is the normal state conductivity, T T is temperature and T c T_{c} is the temperature of superconduction phase transition. For the first time, we also explain the reason of violation of the Homes’ law. Our theoretical results agree well with experimental facts.

IV
Conclusions

We analyzed the experimental Homes’ law, see Eq. (
1
), and
theoretically explained that Homes’ law is applicable to both
conventional and non-conventional superconductors on equal grounds.
Thus, superconductors exhibit the BCS-like properties and the
corresponding general scaling behavior. For the first time, we have
explained the case of violation of the Homes’ law. Our studies of
important experimental facts convincingly indicate that the
topological FCQPT is an integral feature of many strongly
correlated Fermi systems and can be considered as a universal cause
of their exotic non-Fermi-liquid properties, which cannot be
observed in metals described by the Landau Fermi-liquid theory.
Moreover, the FC theory is able to explain the complex behavior of
strongly correlated Fermi systems represented by unconventional
superconductors and their relationship with conventional
superconductors.

Metastatic Anaplastic Thyroid Carcinoma to the Right Ventricle
Authors: Kajol Shah, Anthony Fratella-Calabrese, Thomas Lee, Brent Parris
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAnaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC) is a rare, yet extremely aggressive form of thyroid cancer characterized by rapid growth and early metastasis to distant sites. Cardiac involvement is very uncommon and the presentation of cardiac metastasis in ATC can vary widely, highlighting the importance of early clinical recognition. We describe an extremely rare case of metastatic ATC that spread to the right ventricle.Key Words: cancer, echocardiography, right ventricle, shortness of breath, tachycardia

A 67-year-old woman with a past medical history of hyperlipidemia and pre-diabetes presented with a chief complaint of shortness of breath. A few weeks before this presentation, an evaluation at an outside hospital revealed a compressive thyroid mass causing airway deviation. During the prior hospitalization, a fine needle aspiration with cytology demonstrated diffuse immunoreactivity consistent with anaplastic thyroid cancer. The patient admitted directly to our hospital with a plan for tracheostomy, percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy, and removal of the thyroid isthmus for tissue analysis. On presentation here, the patient endorsed difficulty swallowing and breathing. Vital signs were within normal limits, but physical exam was remarkable for the use of accessory muscles and diffuse rhonchi.

The patient was immediately taken for a tracheostomy. An attempt to place a nasogastric tube was unsuccessful. Following the procedure, she exhibited adequate oxygen saturation on a trach collar but then developed hemodynamic instability with heart rates ranging from 120 to 130 beats per minute, systolic blood pressure ranging from 140 to 170 mm Hg, and low-grade fevers without obvious cause. Vitals did not change when the patient was made supine or during Valsalva. She proceeded to have several bouts of hemoptysis with intermittent desaturations. Laboratory tests revealed hypokalemia and hypophosphatemia, which were corrected. Imaging studies, including a chest computed tomography (CT) and bilateral lower extremity duplex scans, were negative for deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism. CT showed a mass extending from the neck into the superior and middle mediastinum, including a 4.0-cm mass at the cardiac apex and anterior border of the right ventricle (Figures 1A to 1C). A transthoracic echocardiogram redemonstrated the cardiac mass in the right ventricle, measuring 6.7 cm by 2.7 cm, well perfused, and extending from the apex to the right ventricular septal wall (Figures 1D to 1F). Right ventricular systolic function was preserved with no evidence of inflow or outflow obstruction. Repeated attempts to pass a nasogastric tube were unsuccessful due to compression of the airway and esophagus. In addition, because of coagulopathy and hemodynamic instability, the patient was deemed not a candidate for percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy and was transitioned to palliative care.

Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC) is known for its early metastases, with the most common sites being lymph nodes and lung.1 Cardiac metastasis is an extremely rare occurrence in ATC. The reported incidence of cardiac involvement of anaplastic thyroid carcinoma is <2%.2 Symptoms of cardiac metastasis in ATC can vary widely, from dyspnea and pericarditis to more severe manifestations including arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death. Early detection of cardiac metastasis is particularly challenging, as it is often asymptomatic.3 Noninvasive anatomic studies such as CT or echocardiography have proven valuable in detecting cardiac involvement (Videos 1 and 2).

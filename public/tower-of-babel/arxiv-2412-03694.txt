Bidiagonal matrix factorisations related to multiple orthogonal polynomials
Amílcar Branquinho 1, Juan E.F. Díaz 2, Ana Foulquié-Moreno 3, Hélder Lima 4, Manuel Mañas 5

Abstract
. We provide necessary and sufficient conditions for the Hessenberg recurrence matrix associated with a system of multiple orthogonal polynomials to admit a factorisation as a product of bidiagonal matrices. Using the Gauss-Borel factorisation of the moment matrix, we show that the nontrivial entries of those bidiagonal matrices can be expressed in terms of coefficients of type I or type II multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to the original system and its Christoffel transformations. Using the connection of multiple orthogonal polynomials with branched continued fractions, we show that the nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices in the factorisation of the Hessenberg recurrence matrix correspond to the coefficients of a branched continued fraction associated with the given system of multiple orthogonal polynomials. As a case study, we present an explicit bidiagonal factorisation for the Hessenberg recurrence matrices of the Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials and, as a limiting case, the multiple Laguerre polynomials of first kind.

Primary: 15A23, 33C45, 42C05, 47B36.
Secondary: 11J70, 33C20.

1.
Introduction and motivation

The focal point of this paper are factorisations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{U},

of banded Hessenberg recurrence matrices of systems of multiple orthogonal polynomials as products of
r
lower-bidiagonal matrices
\mathrm{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{L}_{r}
, and one upper-bidiagonal matrix
\mathrm{U}
of the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\alpha_{i}&1\\
&\alpha_{r+1+i}&1\\
&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix},\;1\leq i\leq r,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{U}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{0}&1\\
&\alpha_{r+1}&1\\
&&\alpha_{2(r+1)}&1\\
&&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix},

whose nontrivial entries
\left(\alpha_{m}\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
are all different from zero.

When these entries are all positive, the Hessenberg matrix
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{U}
is oscillatory.
Spectral properties of oscillatory matrices lead to a Favard-type theorem for Hessenberg matrices that admit a positive bidiagonal factorisation, in terms of a system of multiple orthogonal polynomials with respect to positive measures on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. For these systems, the zeros of the type II polynomials on the step-line are all simple, real, and positive, and the zeros of consecutive polynomials interlace (see
[
10
, Thm. 6]
and
[
17
, Thm. 4.8]
).

The study of these positive bidiagonal factorisations dates back to
[
4
]
, where high-order nonsymmetric difference operators are considered. The bidiagonal factorisation arises in connection with the vector Stieltjes continued fraction and serves as a tool for analysing both the direct and inverse spectral problems associated with the operator. It is also applied to the integration of the discrete KdV equation hierarchy.

The Hessenberg matrix arising from the recurrence relations of multiple orthogonal polynomials can be interpreted as the transition probability matrix of a Markov chain that extends beyond the classical birth-and-death framework.
These positive bidiagonal factorisations correspond to Markov chains whose stochastic matrices can be decomposed into products of bidiagonal stochastic matrices, each representing pure birth-and-death Markov chains (see
[
8
,
10
]
).
An example of Markov chains with a physical motivation are urn models.
Some urn models have been solved in terms of classical orthogonal polynomials and, more recently, a new urn model based on the bidiagonal factorisation of the Jacobi Piñeiro multiple orthogonal polynomials with respect to two measures was proposed in
[
15
]
.

In the literature we find several examples of bidiagonal factorisations for the Hessenberg recurrence matrices of multiple orthogonal polynomials with respect to systems of
2
measures.
In particular, in
[
4
, §8]
, we can find this bidiagonal factorisation for the Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials, and, in
[
7
]
, we can find it for the Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials and their descendants in the multiple Askey scheme, including the Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials, multiple Meixner (of the first and second kind), multiple Laguerre (of the first and second kind), multiple Kravchuk, and multiple Charlier.
Nevertheless, the bidiagonal factorisation of the Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials was obtained only after substantial computational effort and required an independent proof, as no general algorithm was available.
Furthermore, in
[
9
]
is obtained a bidiagonal factorisation in terms of the values of the type I and II multiple orthogonal polynomials evaluated at the origin, corresponding to Darboux transformations of the Hessenberg matrix, for the Jacobi-Piñeiro and hypergeometric multiple orthogonal polynomials, again with respect to
2
measures.

Bidiagonal factorisations of the Hessenberg recurrence matrices associated with multiple orthogonal polynomials with respect to systems of any number of measures whose moment generating functions are hypergeometric were obtained in
[
17
]
, using a connection of those polynomials with branched continued fractions.
That paper provides an explicit example of a new family of multiple orthogonal polynomials, for which the location, simplicity, and interlacing of the zeros were proved using the bidiagonal factorisation of the corresponding Hessenberg recurrence matrix and there is no other known method to prove these properties without imposing significantly stronger conditions on the parameters involved.

The main goals of this paper are to develop methods to obtain bidiagonal factorisations of the form (
1.1
) for the Hessenberg recurrence matrices of systems of multiple orthogonal polynomials and to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of those factorisations.
In Section
3
, we give a sufficient condition for the existence of the bidiagonal factorisation.
In this approach, the nontrivial entries
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the bidiagonal matrices are described in terms of the coefficients of the type I or type II polynomials along the step-line, for both the original system and its Christoffel transformations.
In Section
4
, we obtain the same sequence
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
using the connection between multiple orthogonal polynomials, production matrices and branched continued fractions as introduced in
[
24
]
. This framework allows us to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of the bidiagonal factorisation.
Finally, in Section
5
, we present a case study: we construct the explicit bidiagonal factorisation of the Hessenberg recurrence matrix of the Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials with an arbitrary number of weights. As a limiting case, we also derive the corresponding factorisation for the multiple Laguerre polynomials of first kind.

2.
Background

2.1.
Multiple orthogonal polynomials

Multiple orthogonal polynomials (see
[
20
, § 4]
,
[
16
, § 23]
, and
[
26
, §3]
) are a generalisation of orthogonal polynomials satisfying orthogonality conditions with respect to systems of several measures rather than just one measure.
In this section, we briefly revisit the theory of multiple orthogonal polynomials, defining the orthogonality conditions via linear functionals in
\mathbb{C}[x]
, the ring of polynomials in one variable with complex coefficients.
For more generality,
\mathbb{C}
could be replaced by a generic field in all definitions and theoretical results.
That generality is not relevant for this work, but it may be of interest to some readers.

A linear functional
u
defined on
\mathbb{C}[x]
is a linear map
u:\mathbb{C}[x]\to\mathbb{C}
.
The action of a linear functional
u
on a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[x]
is denoted by
u\left[f\right]
.
The moment of order
n\in\mathbb{N}
of a linear functional
u
is equal to
u\left[x^{n}\right]
.
By linearity, every linear functional
u
is uniquely determined by its moments.
In most relevant examples, there exists a measure
\mu
supported on a subset of the real line or on a curve in the complex plane such that
\displaystyle u\left[f\right]=\int f(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
for any
f\in\mathbb{C}[x]
and we say that the functional
u
is induced by the measure
\mu
.
However, the use of functionals allows us to not have to concern about the existence of measures in most theoretical results (and it also provides a shorter notation).

There are two types of multiple orthogonal polynomials, type I and type II, with respect to systems of
r
linear functionals, for some positive integer
r
.
Both types are labelled by multi-indices
\vec{n}=\left(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
of norm
|\vec{n}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
,
and both reduce to conventional orthogonal polynomials when
r=1
.

The type II multiple orthogonal polynomial with respect to a system of
r
linear functionals
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
for the multi-index
\vec{n}=\left(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is a monic polynomial
P_{\vec{n}}(x)
of degree
|\vec{n}|
which satisfies the orthogonality conditions

(2.1)

\displaystyle v_{j}\left[x^{k}P_{\vec{n}}(x)\right]=0\quad\text{if}\quad 0\leq k\leq n_{j}-1,\quad\text{for each}\quad 1\leq j\leq r.

The type I multiple orthogonal polynomials with respect to a system of
r
linear functionals
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
for the multi-index
\vec{n}=\left(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
are given by a vector of
r
polynomials
\left(A_{\vec{n},1}(x),\cdots,A_{\vec{n},r}(x)\right)
such that
\deg\left(A_{\vec{n},j}(x)\right)\leq n_{j}-1
for all
1\leq j\leq r
and the type I linear form
q_{\vec{n}}
, defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle q_{\vec{n}}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}A_{\vec{n},j}(x)\,v_{j},

satisfies the orthogonality and normalisation conditions

(2.3)

\displaystyle q_{\vec{n}}\left[x^{m}\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}v_{j}\left[x^{m}\,A_{\vec{n},j}(x)\right]=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }0\leq m\leq|\vec{n}|-2,\\
1&\text{ if }m=|\vec{n}|-1.\end{cases}

In most relevant examples, the functionals
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
are induced by measures
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}
which are all absolutely continuous with respect to a common measure
\mu
,
i.e., there exist weight functions
w_{1}(x),\cdots,w_{r}(x)
defined in the support of
\mu
such that

\displaystyle v_{j}\left[f\right]=\int f(x)w_{j}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
for any
f\in\mathbb{C}[x]
and any
1\leq j\leq r
.
Then, the type I function
Q_{\vec{n}}(x)
is defined by
\displaystyle Q_{\vec{n}}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}A_{\vec{n},j}(x)\,w_{j}(x)
and
the type I linear form
q_{\vec{n}}
defined in (
2.2
) has the integral representation

\displaystyle q_{\vec{n}}\left[f\right]=\int f(x)Q_{\vec{n}}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
for any
f\in\mathbb{C}[x]
.

The multiple orthogonality conditions in (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) give two systems of
|\vec{n}|
equations for the
|\vec{n}|
coefficients of the polynomials.
The matrices of these systems are the transpose of each other, so the existence and uniqueness of type I and type II multiple orthogonal polynomials are equivalent to each other.

We focus on the multi-indices on the step-line:

\vec{n}=\left(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq n_{1}-1
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a unique multi-index of norm
n
on the step-line of
\mathbb{N}^{r}
.
If
n=rk+j
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j\leq r-1
, that multi-index is
formed by
j
entries equal to
k+1
followed by
r-j
entries equal to
k
.

The polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
formed by the type II multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line of
\mathbb{N}^{r}
is also said to be
r
-orthogonal.
A polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal with respect to a system
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
if

(2.4)

\displaystyle v_{j}\left[x^{k}\,P_{n}\right]=\begin{cases}N_{n}\neq 0&\text{ if }n=rk+j-1\\
\hfil 0&\text{ if }n\geq rk+j\end{cases}\quad\text{for each }j\in\{1,\cdots,r\}.

Similarly, we consider the sequences of type I multiple orthogonal polynomials
\big(A_{n,1}(x),\cdots,A_{n,r}(x)\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and type I linear forms
\left(q_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on the step-line.
However, for any system
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
, the conditions on the degrees of the type I multiple orthogonal polynomials for the multi-index
\vec{0}=\left(0,\cdots,0\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
imply that

A_{\vec{0},j}(x)=0
for all
1\leq j\leq r
and, consequently,
q_{\vec{0}}=0
.

Therefore, we use the notation
A_{n,j}(x)
and
q_{n}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq j\leq r
, for the type I multiple orthogonal polynomials and type I linear forms for the multi-index on the step-line with norm
n+1
, so that the sequences
\big(A_{n,1}(x),\cdots,A_{n,r}(x)\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\left(q_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
do not start with trivial zero elements.
Using this notation:

•

the type I orthogonality and normalization conditions in (
2.3
) on the step-line become

(2.5)

\displaystyle q_{n}\left[x^{m}\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}v_{j}\left[x^{m}\,A_{n,j}(x)\right]=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }0\leq m\leq n-1,\\
1&\text{ if }m=n,\end{cases}

•

the sequence of type I linear forms
\left(q_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on the step-line is the dual sequence of the
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to the same system, i.e.,

(2.6)

\displaystyle q_{k}\left[P_{n}(x)\right]=\delta_{k,n}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }n=k,\\
0&\text{ if }n\neq k.\end{cases}

This duality corresponds to the biorthogonality conditions in
[
16
, Thm. 23.1.6]
reduced to the step-line.

A monic polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal if and only if it satisfies a recurrence relation of order
r+1
of the form

(2.7)

\displaystyle P_{n+1}(x)=x\,P_{n}(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{\min(r,n)}\gamma_{n-k}^{\,[k]}\,P_{n-k}(x),

with
\gamma_{n}^{\,[r]}\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the trivial initial condition
P_{0}(x)=1
.
The recurrence relation (
2.7
) satisfied by a
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
can be expressed as a vector recurrence relation

(2.8)

\displaystyle x\,\left[P_{n}(x)\right]_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\mathrm{H}\,\left[P_{n}(x)\right]_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

where
\left[P_{n}(x)\right]_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\big[P_{0}(x),P_{1}(x),P_{2}(x),\cdots\big]^{\top}
and the Hessenberg recurrence matrix is

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}=\begin{bmatrix}\gamma^{[0]}_{0}&1\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\gamma^{[1]}_{0}&\gamma^{[0]}_{1}&1\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\gamma^{[r]}_{0}&\cdots&\gamma^{[1]}_{r-1}&\gamma^{[0]}_{r}&1\\
&\ddots&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}.

Furthermore, for any
n\geq 1
,
P_{n}(x)
is the characteristic polynomial of the truncated
n
-by-
n
Hessenberg matrix
\mathrm{H}_{n}
formed by the first
n
rows and columns of
\mathrm{H}
.
As a result, the study of the spectral properties of the matrices
\mathrm{H}_{n}
gives information about the zeros of the
r
-orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x)
.

We are interested in bidiagonal matrix factorisations of the form
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{U}
as in (
1.1
) for the Hessenberg matrix
\mathrm{H}
in (
2.9
).
Observe that these bidiagonal matrix factorisations can be used to explicitly write the coefficients of the recurrence relation (
2.7
) satisfied by
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as sums of products of the nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices
\mathrm{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{L}_{r}
, and
\mathrm{U}
in (
1.2
):

(2.10)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{\,[k]}=\sum_{r\geq\ell_{0}>\cdots>\ell_{k}\geq 0}\,\prod_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{(r+1)(n+i)+\ell_{i}-r}\quad\text{for any }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }0\leq k\leq r,

with
\alpha_{m}=0
for any
m<0
(see
[
17
, Thm. 4.5]
).

2.2.
Gauss-Borel factorisation of the moment matrix and multiple orthogonality

The moment matrix of a system of linear functionals
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
is the infinite matrix
\mathrm{M}=\left(m_{n,\ell}\right)_{n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
with entries

m_{n,rk+j}=v_{j+1}\left[x^{k+n}\right]
for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j\leq r-1
.
More explicitly,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}\left[1\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[1\right]&v_{1}\left[x\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[x\right]&v_{1}\left[x^{2}\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[x^{2}\right]&\cdots\vskip 2.84544pt\\
v_{1}\left[x\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[x\right]&v_{1}\left[x^{2}\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[x^{2}\right]&v_{1}\left[x^{3}\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[x^{3}\right]&\cdots\vskip 2.84544pt\\
v_{1}\left[x^{2}\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[x^{2}\right]&v_{1}\left[x^{3}\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[x^{3}\right]&v_{1}\left[x^{4}\right]&\cdots&v_{r}\left[x^{4}\right]&\cdots\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\end{bmatrix}.

The Gauss-Borel factorisation of the moment matrix
\mathrm{M}
is the LU-decomposition
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{C}\mathrm{D}
, where
\mathrm{C}
is a unit-lower-triangular matrix and
\mathrm{D}
is a non-singular upper-triangular matrix, which is unique if it exists.
The Gauss-Borel factorisation exists if and only if
\Delta_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\geq 1
, where
\Delta_{n}
is the determinant of the
n
-by-
n
leading principal submatrix of
\mathrm{M}
.
This condition is equivalent to the existence and uniqueness of the type I and type II multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
.

To find a more convenient expression for the moment matrix
\mathrm{M}
in (
2.11
), we consider the infinite vectors

(2.12)

\displaystyle\chi(x)=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
x\\
x^{2}\\
\vdots\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{j}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}e_{j}\\
x\,e_{j}\\
x^{2}\,e_{j}\\
\vdots\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq r,

where
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r}
is the canonical basis in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
.
Using these vectors, the moment matrix
\mathrm{M}
can be given as

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}v_{j}\left[\chi(x)\chi_{j}^{\top}(x)\right],

where a functional acting on a matrix correspond to acting on each entry of that matrix.

To make this representation clearer, we present the illustrative example
r=2
.
Then,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{\top}(x)=\left[1,0,x,0,x^{2},0,\cdots\right]\quad\text{and}\quad\chi_{2}^{\top}(x)=\left[0,1,0,x,0,x^{2},\cdots\right],

so

(2.15)

\displaystyle\chi(x)\chi_{1}^{\top}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&x&0&\cdots\\
x&0&x^{2}&0&\cdots\\
x^{2}&0&x^{3}&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\chi(x)\chi_{2}^{\top}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&x&\cdots\\
0&x&0&x^{2}&\cdots\\
0&x^{2}&0&x^{3}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore,
v_{1}\left[\chi(x)\chi_{1}^{\top}(x)\right]+v_{2}\left[\chi(x)\chi_{2}^{\top}(x)\right]
is equal to the moment matrix
\mathrm{M}
in (
2.11
) with
r=2
.
The deduction of (
2.13
) for generic
r
is completely analogous to the case
r=2
.

The following result, which we do not prove here as it can be found, in more generality, in the context of mixed multiple orthogonal polynomials in
[
2
, Prop. 3]
, shows how the Gauss-Borel factorisation of the moment matrix can be used to obtain explicit expressions for type II multiple orthogonal polynomials and type I linear forms along the step-line.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that the moment matrix
\mathrm{M}
in (
2.11
) admits a Gauss-Borel factorisation
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{C}\mathrm{D}
, where
\mathrm{C}
is a unit-lower-triangular matrix and
\mathrm{D}
is a non-singular upper-triangular matrix.
Then, all the multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
exist and are unique and the sequences of type II multiple orthogonal polynomials
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and type I linear forms
\left(q_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on the step-line are determined by

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(x)=\big[P_{0}(x),P_{1}(x),P_{2}(x),\cdots\big]^{\top}=\mathrm{C}^{-1}\,\chi(x)\quad\text{and}\quad q=\big[q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\cdots\big]=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\chi_{j}^{\top}(x)\mathrm{D}^{-1}v_{j}.

The latter result means that the entries of the inverses of the triangular matrices appearing in the Gauss-Borel factorisation
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{C}\mathrm{D}
are the coefficients of the type II and type I multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
,
which we denote by
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\left(A_{n,1}(x),\cdots,A_{n,r}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, respectively.
To make this statement clearer, we explicitly write these polynomials as

(2.17)

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n}(k)\,x^{k}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{n,i}(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{i}-1}a_{n,i}(\ell)\,x^{\ell},\quad\text{for }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }1\leq i\leq r,

where, for
n=rm+k
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq k\leq r-1
, we have

n_{i}=m+1
if
i\leq k+1
and
n_{i}=m
if
i\geq k+2
.
Then,
\mathrm{C}^{-1}
and
\mathrm{D}^{-1}
are triangular matrices such that:

•

\left(\mathrm{C}^{-1}\right)_{n,k}=p_{n}(k)
when
k\leq n
,

•

\left(\mathrm{D}^{-1}\right)_{m,n}=a_{n,i}(\ell)

when
m=r\ell+i-1\leq n
, with
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq i\leq r
.

We can get the recurrence relations verified by the type II polynomials and by the type I linear forms from the Gauss-Borel factorisation
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{C}\mathrm{D}
of the moment matrix (
2.11
).
We omit the proof, as it follows directly from a more general result on mixed multiple orthogonal polynomials given in
[
2
, Prop. 13]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that the moment matrix
\mathrm{M}
in (
2.11
) admits the Gauss-Borel factorisation
\mathrm{M}=\mathrm{C}\mathrm{D}
, where
\mathrm{C}
is a unit-lower-triangular matrix and
\mathrm{D}
is a non-singular upper-triangular matrix.
Then, the sequences of type II multiple orthogonal polynomials
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and type I linear forms
\left(q_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on the step-line with respect to
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
satisfy the recurrence relations

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}\,\mathrm{P}(x)=x\,\mathrm{P}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad q\,\mathrm{H}=x\,q,

where the vectors
\mathrm{P}(x)
and
q
are defined in (
2.16
) and
\mathrm{H}
is the banded unit-lower-Hessenberg matrix with
r
subdiagonals which satisfies

(2.19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{C}^{-1}\Lambda\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{D}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{r}\mathrm{D}^{-1},

where
\Lambda
is the infinite matrix with ones in the supradiagonal and zeros everywhere else, i.e.,

(2.20)

\displaystyle\Lambda=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&0&\cdots\\
0&0&1&0&\ddots\\
0&0&0&1&\ddots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}.

The factorisations (
2.19
) of the Hessenberg matrix
\mathrm{H}
will be used to construct its bidiagonal factorisation in the next section.

2.3.
Lattice paths, branched continued fractions, and production matrices

The branched continued fractions appearing here were introduced in
[
22
]
as generating functions of
r
-Dyck paths and were further explored in
[
21
,
11
]
.
We follow their terminology and definitions.
An equivalent object was also introduced in
[
1
]
and named as multicontinued fractions.

For a positive integer
r
, a
r
-Dyck path is a path in the lattice
\mathbb{N}^{2}
, starting and ending on the horizontal axis, using steps
(1,1)
, called rises, and
(1,-r)
, called
r
-falls.
More generally, a partial
r
-Dyck path in
\mathbb{N}^{2}
is allowed to start and end anywhere in
\mathbb{N}^{2}
, using steps
(1,1)
and
(1,-r)
.

We consider weighted (partial)
r
-Dyck paths, where each rise has weight
1
and each
r
-fall to height
i
has weight
\alpha_{i}
and the weight of a path is the product of the weights of its steps.
Moreover, the generating polynomial of a set of lattice paths is the sum of the weights of the paths in the set.
Observe that the length of a
r
-Dyck path is always a multiple of
r+1
. For an infinite sequence of weights
\left(\alpha_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
the
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomial
S_{n}^{\,(r)}\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
is the generating polynomial of the
r
-Dyck paths from
(0,0)
to
\big((r+1)n,0\big)
.
They are an extension of the Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials introduced by Flajolet in
[
14
]
, which correspond to the case
r=1
.

1
for an illustrative example of how to compute
S_{2}^{\,(2)}\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)
.

A partial
r
-Dyck path starting at
(0,0)
only passes by points
(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
y=x-(r+1)m
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
is the number of
r
-falls of the path and
x
is the total number of steps.
We are interested in the following generalisations of the
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials
(introduced in
[
22
]
and
[
11
]
, respectively):

•

the modified
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials of type
j
,
denoted by
S_{n}^{\,(r;\,j)}\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)
with
n,j\in\mathbb{N}
,
are the generating polynomials of the partial
r
-Dyck paths from
(0,0)
to
\big((r+1)n+j,j\big)
,

•

the generalised
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials of type
j
,
denoted by
S_{n,k}^{\,(r;\,j)}\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)
with
n,k,j\in\mathbb{N}
,
are the generating polynomials of the partial
r
-Dyck paths from
(0,0)
to
\big((r+1)n+j,(r+1)k+j\big)
.

Let
f_{0}(t)
be the generating function for
r
-Dyck paths, considered as a formal power series in
t
:

(2.21)

\displaystyle f_{0}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}^{\,(r)}\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)t^{n}.

More generally, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
f_{k}(t)
be the generating function for partial
r
-Dyck paths which start and end at height
k
without ever going below it
(i.e.,
f_{k}(t)
is
f_{0}(t)
with each
\alpha_{i}
replaced by
\alpha_{i+k}
).
The sequence
\left(f_{k}(t)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the functional equation (see
[
22
, Eqs. 2.26-2.27]
)

(2.22)

\displaystyle f_{k}(t)=1+\alpha_{k}\,t\prod_{j=0}^{r}f_{k+j}(t)\Leftrightarrow f_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{\displaystyle 1-\alpha_{k}\,t\prod_{j=1}^{r}f_{k+j}(t)}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Successively iterating the former we find that (see
[
22
, Eqs. 2.28]
)

(2.23)

\displaystyle f_{k}(t)=\cfrac{1}{\displaystyle 1-\alpha_{k}\,t\prod_{i_{1}=1}^{r}\cfrac{1}{\displaystyle 1-\alpha_{k+i_{1}}\,t\prod_{i_{2}=1}^{r}\cfrac{1}{\displaystyle 1-\alpha_{k+i_{1}+i_{2}}\,t\prod_{i_{3}=1}^{r}\cfrac{1}{\displaystyle 1-\cdots}}}}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

The right-hand side of (
2.23
) is known as a (Stieltjes-type)
r
-branched continued fraction, because it reduces to a classical Stieltjes continued fraction (also known as an S-fraction) when
r=1
.

As explained in
[
24
]
, to connect lattice paths and branched continued fractions with multiple orthogonal polynomials, it is fundamental to use the production matrices originally introduced in
[
12
]
.

Let
\mathrm{H}=\left(h_{i,j}\right)_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an infinite matrix with entries in a commutative ring
R
.
If all the powers of
\mathrm{H}
are well-defined (which is the case when
\mathrm{H}
is an Hessenberg matrix), we can define an infinite matrix
\mathrm{S}=\left(s_{n,k}\right)_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
by
s_{n,k}=\left(\mathrm{H}^{n}\right)_{0,k}
for all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We call
\mathrm{H}
the production matrix and
\mathrm{S}
the output matrix.

3.
Bidiagonal factorisations linked to the Gauss-Borel factorisation

For a system of linear functionals
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
, we consider its Christoffel transformations

(3.1)

\displaystyle V^{[j]}=\left(v_{j+1},\cdots,v_{r},x\,v_{1},\cdots,x\,v_{j}\right),\;0\leq j\leq r.

In particular,
V^{[0]}=\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
and
V^{[r]}=\left(x\,v_{1},\cdots,x\,v_{r}\right)
.

In this section, we show that the existence and uniqueness of the multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to all systems
V^{[j]}
in (
3.1
) is a sufficient condition to obtain bidiagonal factorisations of the Hessenberg recurrence matrices of these systems, with all nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices different from zero.
In particular, we obtain a factorisation
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{U}
of the Hessenberg recurrence matrix of the system
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
.

We start by using the Gauss-Borel factorisation of the moment matrices of the systems
V^{[j]}
to express the nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices appearing in our factorisations.
Then, we rewrite the formulas for the nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices, firstly using coefficients of the type I and type II multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to the systems
V^{[j]}
instead of their Gauss-Borel factorisation, and later using the determinants of the finite leading principal submatrices of the moment matrices of the systems
V^{[j]}
.

The following theorem is the main theoretical result of this section, where we show that the existence and uniqueness of the multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to all systems
V^{[j]}
in (
3.1
) is a sufficient condition to obtain bidiagonal factorisations of the Hessenberg recurrence matrices of these systems, using the entries of either one of the triangular matrices appearing in the Gauss-Borel factorisation of the moment matrices of the systems
V^{[j]}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a system of linear functionals
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
such that the multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to all systems
V^{[j]}
,
0\leq j\leq r
, in (
3.1
) exist and are unique,
let
\displaystyle{\mathrm{M}^{[j]}=\mathrm{C}^{[j]}\mathrm{D}^{[j]}}
be the Gauss-Borel factorisation of the moment matrix
\mathrm{M}^{[j]}
of the system
V^{[j]}
for each
0\leq j\leq r
,
where
{\mathrm{C}^{[j]}=\left(c^{[j]}_{i,k}\right)_{i,k\in\mathbb{N}}}
and
{\mathrm{D}^{[j]}=\left(d^{[j]}_{i,k}\right)_{i,k\in\mathbb{N}}}

are, respectively, a unit-lower-triangular matrix and a non-singular upper-triangular matrix.
Then, for any
0\leq j\leq r
, the Hessenberg recurrence matrix
\mathrm{H}^{[j]}
of the system
V^{[j]}
admits the bidiagonal factorisation

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{[j]}=\mathrm{L}_{j+1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\mathrm{U}\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{j},

where
\mathrm{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{L}_{r}
, and
\mathrm{U}
are bidiagonal matrices of the form in (
1.2
), with nontrivial entries
\left(\alpha_{m}\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
all different from zero such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)n}=\frac{d^{[r]}_{n,n}}{d^{[0]}_{n,n}}=c^{[0]}_{n+1,n}-c^{[r]}_{n,n-1},\quad\text{with }c^{[r]}_{0,-1}=0,

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)n+i}=\frac{d^{[i-1]}_{n+1,n+1}}{d^{[i]}_{n,n}}=c^{[i]}_{n+1,n}-c^{[i-1]}_{n+1,n}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq r.

In particular, the Hessenberg matrix
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{H}^{[0]}
admits the bidiagonal factorisation (
1.1
).

To prove this theorem, we need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathrm{M}
be the moment matrix in (
2.11
) and
\Lambda
be the matrix given in (
2.20
).
Then:

(i)

\mathrm{M}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{j}
is the moment matrix of the system
V^{[j]}

for all
1\leq j\leq r
,

(ii)

\Lambda\mathrm{M}
is the moment matrix of the system
V^{[r]}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Recalling the formula (
2.13
) for the moment matrix
\mathrm{M}
of
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}\Lambda^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}\left[\chi(x)\chi_{i}^{\top}(x)\right]\Lambda^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}\left[\chi(x)\big(\Lambda\chi_{i}(x)\big)^{\top}\right].

It is clear from the definition (
2.20
) of
\Lambda
that

\Lambda\chi_{1}(x)=x\,\chi_{r}(x)
and

\Lambda\chi_{j}(x)=\chi_{j-1}(x)
for
2\leq j\leq r
.
Hence,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}\Lambda^{\top}=\sum_{i=2}^{r}v_{i}\left[\chi(x)\chi_{i-1}^{\top}(x)\right]+x\,v_{1}\left[\chi(x)\chi_{r}^{\top}(x)\right]

Therefore,
\mathrm{M}\Lambda^{\top}
is the moment matrix of the system
V^{[1]}=\left(v_{2},\cdots,v_{r},x\,v_{1}\right)
.
Iterating this procedure, we find that
\mathrm{M}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{j}
is the moment matrix of
V^{[j]}=\left(v_{j+1},\cdots,v_{r},x\,v_{1},\cdots,x\,v_{j}\right)
for all
1\leq j\leq r
, proving
(i)
.
In particular, the moment matrix of
V^{[r]}=\left(x\,v_{1},\cdots,x\,v_{r}\right)
is

(3.7)

\mathrm{M}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}\left[\chi(x)\,\chi_{i}^{\top}(x)\right]\big(\Lambda^{r}\big)^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}\left[\chi(x)\Big(\Lambda^{r}\chi_{i}(x)\Big)^{\top}\right].

We can observe from (
2.20
) that
\Lambda^{r}\chi_{i}(x)=x\,\chi_{i}(x)
and
\Lambda\chi(x)=x\,\chi(x)
.
As a result,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}\left[x\,\chi(x)\chi_{i}^{\top}(x)\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}\left[\Lambda\,\chi(x)\chi_{i}^{\top}(x)\right]=\Lambda\mathrm{M}.

Therefore, we conclude that the moment matrix of
\left(x\,v_{1},\cdots,x\,v_{r}\right)
is
\Lambda\mathrm{M}
, proving
(ii)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Using Lemma
3.2
, we know that the moment matrices of the systems
V^{[i]}
and
V^{[i-1]}
are related by

\mathrm{M}^{[i]}=\mathrm{M}^{[i-1]}\Lambda^{\top}
for any
1\leq i\leq r
.
Applying the Gauss-Borel factorisation to both
\mathrm{M}^{[i]}
and
\mathrm{M}^{[i-1]}
, we obtain

\mathrm{C}^{[i]}\mathrm{D}^{[i]}=\mathrm{C}^{[i-1]}\mathrm{D}^{[i-1]}\Lambda^{\top}
.
Therefore, we can define

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{i}=\left(\mathrm{C}^{[i-1]}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{C}^{[i]}=\mathrm{D}^{[i-1]}\Lambda^{\top}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[i]}\right)^{-1}\quad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq r.

Because
\mathrm{C}^{[i-1]}
and
\mathrm{C}^{[i]}
are unit-lower-triangular matrices,

\mathrm{L}_{i}
is also unit-lower-triangular.
Moreover, because
\mathrm{D}^{[i-1]}
and
\mathrm{D}^{[i]}
are non-singular upper-triangular matrices and all nonzero entries of
\Lambda^{\top}
are in its first subdiagonal,

\mathrm{L}_{i}
is upper-Hessenberg, i.e., its first subdiagonal entries are nontrivial and all the entries below it are equal to
0
.
Therefore,
\mathrm{L}_{i}
is a lower-bidiagonal matrix of the form in (
1.2
) and we denote its subdiagonal entries by
\alpha_{(r+1)n+i}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, as in (
1.2
).

Recalling Proposition
2.2
, we know that

\mathrm{H}^{[j]}=\mathrm{D}^{[j]}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{r}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[j]}\right)^{-1}

for all
0\leq j\leq r
.
Taking into account (
3.9
), we have

\mathrm{D}^{[j]}\,\Lambda^{\top}=\mathrm{L}_{j+1}\,\mathrm{D}^{[j+1]}
for any
0\leq j\leq r-1
.
Successively iterating this equation, we find that

\mathrm{D}^{[j]}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{r-j}=\mathrm{L}_{j+1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\mathrm{D}^{[r]}
for all
0\leq j\leq r
.
As a result, we derive that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{[j]}=\mathrm{L}_{j+1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{D}^{[r]}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{j}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[j]}\right)^{-1}\quad\text{for all }0\leq j\leq r.

In particular, taking
j=0
, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{[0]}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\mathrm{L}_{2}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\mathrm{D}^{[r]}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[0]}\right)^{-1}.

Now, we define
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{D}^{[r]}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[0]}\right)^{-1}
and we check that
\mathrm{U}
is an upper-bidiagonal matrix of the form in (
1.2
).
By definition,
\mathrm{U}
is a non-singular upper-triangular matrix.
Moreover, using Lemma
3.2
,

\mathrm{M}^{[r]}=\Lambda\mathrm{M}^{[0]}
.
Thus, applying the Gauss-Borel factorisation,

\mathrm{C}^{[r]}\mathrm{D}^{[r]}=\Lambda\mathrm{C}^{[0]}\mathrm{D}^{[0]}
.
Equivalently,

\mathrm{D}^{[r]}=\left(\mathrm{C}^{[r]}\right)\Lambda\mathrm{C}^{[0]}\mathrm{D}^{[0]}
.
So, we derive the alternative expression

\mathrm{U}=\left(\mathrm{C}^{[r]}\right)^{-1}\Lambda\mathrm{C}^{[0]}
.
Because
\mathrm{C}^{[0]}
and
\mathrm{C}^{[r]}
are unit-lower-triangular matrices,

\mathrm{U}
is a lower-Hessenberg matrix whose supradiagonal entries are all equal to
1
.

Hence, we have shown that
\mathrm{U}
is an upper-bidiagonal matrix of the form in (
1.2
) and we denote its diagonal entries by
\alpha_{(r+1)n}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, as in (
1.2
).
Therefore,
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{H}^{[0]}
admits the bidiagonal factorisation
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\mathrm{L}_{2}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\mathrm{U}
, which corresponds to (
1.1
) and to the case
j=0
of (
3.2
).

Furthermore, recalling that
\mathrm{L}_{i}=\mathrm{D}^{[i-1]}\Lambda^{\top}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[i]}\right)^{-1}
, we have
\Lambda^{\top}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[i]}\right)^{-1}=\left(\mathrm{D}^{[i-1]}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{L}_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
Applying iteratively this identity to (
3.10
), we deduce that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{[j]}=\mathrm{L}_{j+1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{D}^{[r]}\left(\Lambda^{\top}\right)^{j-1}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[j-1]}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{L}_{j}=\cdots=\mathrm{L}_{j+1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{D}^{[r]}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[0]}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{j}.

Taking into account that
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{D}^{[r]}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[0]}\right)^{-1}
, we conclude that (
3.2
) holds for all
0\leq j\leq r
.

Now, we prove that the nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices
\mathrm{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{L}_{r}
and
\mathrm{U}
satisfy (
3.3
)-(
3.4
).
Because
\mathrm{D}^{[r]}
and
\mathrm{D}^{[0]}
are non-singular upper-triangular matrices, the diagonal entries of
\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{D}^{[r]}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[0]}\right)^{-1}
can be expressed as

(3.13)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)n}=\left(\mathrm{U}\right)_{n,n}=\left(\mathrm{D}^{[r]}\right)_{n,n}\left(\left(\mathrm{D}^{[0]}\right)^{-1}\right)_{n,n}=\dfrac{d^{[r]}_{n,n}}{d^{[0]}_{n,n}}.

We can also find an alternative formula for
\alpha_{(r+1)n}
using the expression

\mathrm{U}=\left(\mathrm{C}^{[r]}\right)^{-1}\Lambda\,\mathrm{C}^{[0]}
.
Taking into account that
\mathrm{C}^{[r]}
and
\mathrm{C}^{[0]}
are unit-lower-triangular matrices and recalling the definition (
2.20
) of
\Lambda
, we compute the diagonal entries of this product and deduce that

\alpha_{0}=\left(\mathrm{U}\right)_{0,0}=\left(\mathrm{C}^{[0]}\right)_{1,0}=a_{1,0}^{[0]}
and

(3.14)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)n}=\left(\mathrm{U}\right)_{n,n}=\left(\left(\mathrm{C}^{[r]}\right)^{-1}\right)_{n,n-1}+\left(\mathrm{C}^{[0]}\right)_{n+1,n}=c^{[0]}_{n+1,n}-c^{[r]}_{n,n-1}\quad\text{for all }n\geq 1.

Therefore, (
3.3
) holds.

Analogously, we compute the subdiagonal entries of
\mathrm{L}_{i}
using the expression
\mathrm{L}_{i}=\left(\mathrm{C}^{[i-1]}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{C}^{[i]}
to obtain

(3.15)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)n+i}=\left(\mathrm{L}_{i}\right)_{n+1,n}=\left(\mathrm{C}^{[i]}\right)_{n+1,n}+\left(\left(\mathrm{C}^{[i-1]}\right)^{-1}\right)_{n+1,n}=c^{[i]}_{n+1,n}-c^{[i-1]}_{n+1,n},

or using the expression
\mathrm{L}_{i}=\mathrm{D}^{[i-1]}\Lambda^{\top}\left(\mathrm{D}^{[i]}\right)^{-1}
to obtain

(3.16)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)n+i}=\left(\mathrm{L}_{i}\right)_{n+1,n}=\left(\mathrm{D}^{[i-1]}\right)_{n+1,n+1}\left(\left(\mathrm{D}^{[i-1]}\right)^{-1}\right)_{n,n}=\frac{d^{[i-1]}_{n+1,n+1}}{d^{[i]}_{n,n}}.

As a result, (
3.4
) also holds.
Finally, observe that (
3.13
) and (
3.16
) imply that
\alpha_{m}\neq 0
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, because each
\alpha_{m}
is the ratio of two diagonal entries of two non-singular upper-triangular matrices.
∎

As explained after Proposition
2.1
, the entries of the inverses of the triangular matrices appearing in the Gauss-Borel factorisations
\displaystyle{\mathrm{M}^{[j]}=\mathrm{C}^{[j]}\mathrm{D}^{[j]}}
of the moment matrices of the systems
V^{[j]}
,
0\leq j\leq r
, are the coefficients of the type II and type I multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to the corresponding system.
As a result, we can rewrite (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), using the coefficients of those multiple orthogonal polynomials.
For that purpose, we explicitly write the type II and type I multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to
V^{[j]}
,
0\leq j\leq r
, as

(3.17)

\displaystyle P^{[j]}_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p^{[j]}_{n}(k)\,x^{k}\quad\text{and}\quad A^{[j]}_{n,i}(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n_{i}-1}a^{[j]}_{n,i}(\ell)\,x^{\ell},\quad\text{for }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }1\leq i\leq r,

where, for
n=rm+k
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq k\leq r-1
, we have

n_{i}=m+1
if
i\leq k+1
and
n_{i}=m
if
i\geq k+2
.

Then:

•

the diagonal entries of
\left(\mathrm{D}^{[j]}\right)^{-1}
are the leading coefficients of the type I multiple orthogonal polynomials
A^{[j]}_{rm+k,k+1}(x)
,
so
d^{[j]}_{rm+k,rm+k}=\left(a^{[j]}_{rm+k,k+1}(m)\right)^{-1}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq k\leq r-1
, and
0\leq j\leq r
,

•

the subdiagonal entries of
\left(\mathrm{C}^{[j]}\right)^{-1}
are the coefficients
p^{[j]}_{n+1}(n)
of type II multiple orthogonal polynomials
P^{[j]}_{n+1}(x)
,
so
c^{[j]}_{n+1,n}=-p^{[j]}_{n+1}(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j\leq r
.

Therefore, the result below follows directly from Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
be a system of linear functionals such that the multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to all systems
V^{[j]}
,
0\leq j\leq r
, in (
3.1
) exist and are unique.
Then, for any
0\leq j\leq r
, the Hessenberg recurrence matrix
\mathrm{H}^{[j]}
of the system
V^{[j]}
admits the bidiagonal factorisation (
3.2
),
with nontrivial entries
\left(\alpha_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
which we can express using coefficients of the type I and type II multiple orthogonal polynomials as follows:

(i)

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)(rm+k)}=\frac{a^{[0]}_{rm+k,k+1}(m)}{a^{[r]}_{rm+k,k+1}(m)}\quad\text{for any }0\leq k\leq r-1,

and, for
1\leq i\leq r
,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)(rm+k)+i}=\frac{a^{[i]}_{rm+k,k+1}(m)}{a^{[i-1]}_{rm+k+1,k+2}(m)}\text{ if }k\leq r-2\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{(r+1)(rm+r-1)+i}=\frac{a^{[i]}_{rm+r-1,r}(m)}{a^{[i-1]}_{r(m+1),1}(m+1)};

(ii)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)n}=p^{[r]}_{n}(n-1)-p^{[0]}_{n+1}(n)\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{(r+1)n+i}=p^{[i-1]}_{n+1}(n)-p^{[i]}_{n+1}(n)\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq r.

For
n\geq 1
and
0\leq j\leq r
, we denote by
\Delta_{n}^{[j]}
the determinant of the
n
-by-

4.
Bidiagonal factorisations linked to branched continued fractions

In this section, we construct bidiagonal factorisations of the form (
1.1
) for the Hessenberg recurrence matrices associated with systems of multiple orthogonal polynomials via their connection with branched continued fractions.
In fact, we show that the nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices are the coefficients of the corresponding branched continued fractions.

Firstly, we state the theorem that gives the foundation to our algorithm: we find the bidiagonal factorisation of the Hessenberg recurrence matrix of a system of multiple orthogonal polynomials by constructing a sequence of formal power series
\left(g_{m}(t)\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
, determined by the moments of the orthogonality functionals (or measures) and a recurrence relation with coefficients equal to the nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices in our factorisation.
We also observe that these nontrivial entries are the coefficients of a (Stieltjes-type) branched continued fraction and that they are the same as the nontrivial entries in the bidiagonal factorisations from the previous section.

Next, we give a necessary and sufficient condition on a system of multiple orthogonal polynomials for the existence of a bidiagonal factorisation of the form (
1.1
) for the Hessenberg recurrence matrix of the system: the recurrence matrix of a polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
admits such a bidiagonal factorisation if and only if it is
r
-orthogonal with respect to a system
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
such that the multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to all its Christoffel transformations

in (
3.1
) exist and are unique.

Finally, we explicitly describe the sequence

used to construct our bidiagonal factorisations via the type I linear forms on the step-line with respect to the systems
V^{[j]}
in (

\displaystyle n_{j}=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{{n}}{r}\right\rfloor+1&\text{if }j\leq j(n)-1,\\
\hfil\left\lfloor\frac{{n}}{r}\right\rfloor&\text{if }j\geq j(n),\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad n_{i}^{\,\prime}=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{{n}}{r+1}\right\rfloor+1&\text{if }i\leq i(n)-1,\\
\hfil\left\lfloor\frac{{n}}{r+1}\right\rfloor&\text{if }i\geq i(n).\end{cases}

Therefore, (
5.16
) is equivalent to (
5.8
), concluding our proof.
∎

5.2.
Bidiagonal factorisation using the explicit expression for the type I polynomials

Here, we find again the bidiagonal factorisation described in Corollary
5.2
for the Hessenberg recurrence matrix of the Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials, now using explicit expressions for the type I polynomials recently given in
[
6
]
.
We consider the system of measures
\left(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}\right)
supported on the interval
(0,1)
with densities
\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}(x)=x^{a_{j}-1}(1-x)^{b}\,\mathrm{d}x
, with
b>-1
and
a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}>0
such that
i\neq j
implies
a_{i}-a_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, for any
1\leq j\leq r
, we have:

•

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\left(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r};b\right)=\mu_{1}\left(a_{j},\cdots,a_{r},a_{1}+1,\cdots,a_{j-1}+1;b\right)
,

•

\displaystyle x\,\mu_{j}\left(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r};b\right)=\mu_{j}\left(a_{1}+1,\cdots,a_{r}+1;b\right)=\mu_{1}\left(a_{j}+1,\cdots,a_{r}+1,a_{1}+2,\cdots,a_{j-1}+2;b\right)
.

Therefore, the multiple orthogonal polynomials with respect to the system

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{i+1},\cdots,\mu_{r},x\,\mu_{1},\cdots,x\,\mu_{i}\right)
,
0\leq i\leq r
,
are the Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials with parameters
a_{i},\cdots,a_{r},a_{1}+1,\cdots,a_{i-1}+1
, and
b
.

As a result, it follows from
[
6
, Thm. 2]
that, in this case, the coefficients of the type I multiple orthogonal polynomials appearing in (
3.18
)-(
3.19
) are, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq k\leq r-1
,

(5.19)

\displaystyle a^{[i]}_{rm+k,k+1}(m)=\frac{\Gamma\left(a_{k+i}+b+(r+1)m+k\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(a_{j+i}+b+rm+k\right)_{m+1}\prod\limits_{j=k+1}^{r}\left(a_{j+i}+b+rm+k\right)_{m}}{m!\,\Gamma\left(a_{k+i}+m\right)\Gamma(b+rm+k)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(a_{k+i+1}-a_{j+i}\right)_{m+1}\prod\limits_{q=k+1}^{r}\left(a_{k+i+1}-a_{j+i}+1\right)_{m}},

where
a_{rn+j}=a_{j}+n
for all
0\leq j\leq r
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Inputting these coefficients in (
3.18
)-(
3.19
), we derive the formulas

(5.20)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)(rm+k)}=\frac{(a_{k+1}+m)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(a_{j}+b+rm+k\right)}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}\left(a_{k+j}+b+(r+1)m+k\right)},

and

(5.21)

\displaystyle\alpha_{(r+1)(rm+k)+i}=\frac{(a_{k+i+1}-a_{i}+m)(b+rm+k+1)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r-1}\left(a_{i+j}+b+rm+k\right)}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r+1}\left(a_{k+i+j}+b+(r+1)m+k\right)},

for the nontrivial entries of the bidiagonal matrices appearing in the factorisation
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{U}
of the Hessenberg recurrence matrix of the Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials on the step-line.
It is a straightforward computation to check that (
5.20
)-(
5.21
) is equivalent to (
5.8
).

When
r=2
, the coefficients in (
5.8
) and (
5.20
)-(
5.21
) coincide with the ones found in
[
4
, § 8.1]
.

It is a direct consequence of (
5.8
) or (
5.20
)-(
5.21
) that
\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\to\frac{r^{r}}{(r+1)^{r+1}}
as
n\to\infty
.
Therefore, recalling (
2.10
), the coefficients of the recurrence relation (
2.7
) satisfied by the type II Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials on the step-line have the same limit as in
[
17
, Eq. 8.17]
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{[k]}\to\binom{r+1}{k+1}\left(\frac{r^{r}}{(r+1)^{r+1}}\right)^{k+1}
as
n\to\infty
.

5.3.
Multiple Laguerre of first kind as a limiting case

For a positive integer
r
and parameters
a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}>0
such that
a_{i}-a_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
whenever
i\neq j
, we consider the measures
\left(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{r}\right)
supported on the interval
(0,+\infty)
with densities
\mathrm{d}\nu_{j}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-x}\,x^{a_{j}-1}\mathrm{d}x
for all
1\leq j\leq r
, whose moments are

(5.22)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{n}\mathrm{d}\nu_{j}(x)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\,x^{a_{j}+n-1}\,\mathrm{d}x=\Gamma(a_{j}+n)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }1\leq j\leq r.

The limiting relation between the Jacobi and Laguerre measures is

(5.23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\nu_{j}(x)=\lim\limits_{b\to\infty}\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}\left(\dfrac{x}{b}\right)\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq r,

where
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}
are the measures determined by (
5.1
).
Therefore, the multiple Laguerre polynomials of the first kind can be obtained as a limiting case of Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials.
In particular, if
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\left(\hat{P}_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are the sequences of type II multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line with respect to
(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r})
given by (
5.1
) and to
(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{r})
given by (
5.22
), respectively, then

(5.24)

\hat{P}_{n}(x)=\lim_{b\to\infty}b^{n}\,P_{n}\left(\frac{x}{b}\right)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

As a consequence, the recurrence coefficients
\displaystyle\left(\gamma_{n}^{[k]}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}^{0\leq k\leq r}
and
\displaystyle\left(\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{[k]}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}^{0\leq k\leq r}
for the type II Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials and type II multiple Laguerre polynomials of the first kind on the step-line are related by

(5.25)

\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{[k]}=\lim_{b\to\infty}b^{k+1}\,\gamma_{n}^{[k]}\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }0\leq k\leq r.

Hence, recalling (
2.10
), we obtain the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

The Hessenberg recurrence matrix of the multiple Laguerre polynomials of the first kind on the step-line, multiple orthogonal with respect to
(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{r})
given by (
5.22
), admits the factorisation
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{U}
, as a product of the bidiagonal matrices in (
1.2
) with nontrivial entries
\left(\hat{\alpha}_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\hat{\alpha}_{n}=\lim\limits_{b\to\infty}b\,\alpha_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
where
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are the nontrivial entries in the bidiagonal decomposition of the recurrence matrix of the Jacobi-Piñeiro polynomials given in (
5.8
) and (
5.20
)-(
5.21
).
Therefore,

(5.26)

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{n}=a_{j(n)}-a_{i(n)}+\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{r}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{r+1}\right\rfloor\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

with
a_{0}=0
,
i(n)=[n]_{r+1}
, and
j(n)=[n]_{r}+1
, where
[n]_{k}
denotes the remainder of the division of
n
by
k
,
or, alternatively,

(5.27)

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{(r+1)(rm+k)+i}=\begin{cases}\hfil a_{k+i+1}-a_{i}+m&\text{if }k+i\leq r,\\
a_{k+i+1-r}-a_{i}+m&\text{if }k+i\geq r+1,\end{cases}

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq k\leq r-1
, and
0\leq i\leq r
.

It is a direct consequence of either (
5.26
) or (
5.27
) that
\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{n}\sim\frac{n}{r(r+1)}
as
n\to\infty
.
Therefore, recalling (
2.10
),
the recurrence coefficients
\displaystyle\left(\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{[k]}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}^{0\leq k\leq r}
for the type II multiple Laguerre polynomials of the first kind on the step-line satisfy the asymptotic behaviour
\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{[k]}\sim\binom{r+1}{k+1}\left(\frac{n}{r}\right)^{k+1}
as
n\to\infty
.

Note that, alternatively to taking the limit in (
5.26
), we could apply the algorithms developed in Sections
3
and
4
directly to the multiple Laguerre polynomials of first kind.
For the method from Section
3
, we could input the formulas for the type I multiple Laguerre polynomials of the first kind with respect to an arbitrary number of weights obtained in
[
6
, Thm. 3]
in (
3.18
)-(
3.19
) to obtain the coefficients
\left(\hat{\alpha}_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
However, it is much easier to simply take the limit in (
5.26
).

Using the algorithm from Section
4
, the nontrivial entries
\left(\hat{\alpha}_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

of the bidiagonal decomposition of the recurrence matrix of the multiple Laguerre polynomials of first kind
are the coefficients of the recurrence relation

(5.28)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{k+1}(t)-\hat{g}_{k}(t)=\hat{\alpha}_{k}\,t\,\hat{g}_{k+r+1}(t)\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N},

with initial conditions
\hat{g}_{0}(t)=1
and

(5.29)

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{k}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a_{k}+n+1)}{\Gamma(a_{k}+1)}\,t^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{k}+1\right)_{n}\,t^{n}={}_{2}F_{0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}a_{k}+1,1\\
-\end{matrix}\,\right|\,t\right)\quad\text{for }1\leq k\leq r.

\left(\hat{g}_{n}(t)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\hat{g}_{n}(t)=\lim\limits_{b\to\infty}g_{n}(bt)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
where
\left(g_{n}(t)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

5.5
).
The computation of this limit is connected to contour integral representations for type I Jacobi-Piñeiro and multiple Laguerre polynomials and will be considered in forthcoming work.

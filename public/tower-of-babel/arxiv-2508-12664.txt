Real-Analyticity of the Density of States for Random Schrödinger operators with Point Interactions
Masahiro Kaminaga

Abstract
We prove real-analyticity of the density of states (DOS) for random Schrödinger operators with lattice-supported point interactions in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} ( d = 1 , 2 , 3 d=1,2,3 ) in the small-hopping regime. In the attractive case, Krein’s resolvent formula reduces the problem to a lattice model, where a random-walk expansion and disorder averaging lead to single-site integrals with holomorphic single-site density g g . Contour deformation in the coupling-constant plane under a uniform pole-gap condition ensures convergence of the averaged resolvent in a complex neighborhood of a negative-energy interval. This yields analyticity of the DOS. The method also applies to multi-point correlation functions, such as those in the Kubo–Greenwood formula.



1
Introduction

Random Schrödinger operators with point interactions give a simple yet nontrivial continuum model of disorder.
They stand between the Anderson model on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and alloy-type models on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
while still admitting explicit resolvent formulas
[
1
]
.

We study

H_{\omega}=-\Delta+\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q_{\omega}(a)\,\delta(x-a),

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
d=1,2,3
, where
q_{\omega}(a)
are i.i.d. real random variables with common law
\mu
.
Its density
g
is bounded on
Q=\text{{\rm supp}\;}\mu
and extends holomorphically to a neighborhood
\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
.
In the
attractive
case
Q\subset(-\infty,0)
, the spectrum contains a negative-energy interval, so that the random-walk expansion extends to the real axis.

Let
\Lambda_{L}=[-L,L]^{d}
and
H_{\omega,L}
the Dirichlet restriction of
H_{\omega}
. The eigenvalue counting function is

N_{\omega,L}(E)=\frac{1}{(2L)^{d}}\,\sharp\{\lambda\leq E:\lambda\text{ eigenvalue of }H_{\omega,L}\},

with multiplicity. It is known that
N_{\omega,L}(E)\to N(E)
almost surely as
L\to\infty
, defining the
integrated density of states
(IDS).

Here
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes a bounded Borel set used in spatial traces (e.g.
[0,1)^{d}
).
We denote by
I\subset(-\infty,0)
a compact energy interval on which the expansion converges.
By ergodicity, the
Pastur–Shubin formula

[
21
,
24
]
holds:

N(E)=\frac{1}{|C|}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{Tr}\big(\chi_{C}\,\chi_{(-\infty,E]}(H_{\omega})\,\chi_{C}\big)\right],

independent of
C
. Here
\chi_{C}
is multiplication by the indicator of
C
, and
\chi_{(-\infty,E]}(H_{\omega})
the spectral projection.
This avoids pointwise diagonal kernels; in
d=2,3
, the renormalized diagonal of the free resolvent appears only inside the principal matrix in the Krein formula.

For
\text{{\rm Im}}z>0
define

G(z)=\frac{1}{|C|}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{Tr}\big(\chi_{C}\,(H_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\,\chi_{C}\big)\right].

By the spectral theorem and the Pastur–Shubin formula, one has

G(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{dN(\lambda)}{\lambda-z}.

By Stieltjes inversion,

n(E)=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\text{{\rm Im}}G(E+i\epsilon),

(1.1)

and
n(E)=N^{\prime}(E)
for a.e.
E
. We call
n(E)
the
density of states
(DOS).

Our goal is to show that
G
extends holomorphically to
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
for
some compact
I\subset(-\infty,0)
, hence
n(E)
is real-analytic on
I
(Remark
3
).

We apply the method of Kaminaga–Krishna–Nakamura
[
12
]
,
first used for the discrete Anderson model, to continuum point interactions.
We face two difficulties:
(i) the reduction to a lattice model uses Krein’s formula with renormalization of the diagonal;
(ii) single-site averages must be continued in the coupling plane under a pole-gap condition,
while general Minami-type bounds for strength-random point interactions are not available.
Nevertheless, Poisson eigenvalue statistics (with Minami-type inputs) have been established for certain models
[
11
]
.
We handle this using a path-form Neumann expansion with contour deformation.

For general ergodic random Schrödinger operators, the IDS is log–Hölder continuous
[
6
]
.
A Wegner estimate
[
25
,
15
]
yields better regularity, while the Minami estimate
[
20
]

controls multiple eigenvalues.
For point interactions, general Minami bounds are unknown, but some cases are covered in
[
11
]
.
With bounded single-site density, a Wegner bound implies local Lipschitz continuity of
N
.
In alloy-type models, Wegner and Minami bounds plus initial-scale analysis yield localization and statistics
[
20
]
.
For
strength-random
point interactions, Wegner-type bounds hold under mild assumptions
[
16
]
,
but stronger results are rare.
Related work includes small-hopping analyticity for the discrete model
[
12
]
,
smoothness of correlations
[
4
]
,
C^{m}
-regularity
[
8
]
,
and Poisson interactions
[
13
]
.
For
position-random
models (e.g. Poisson),
the IDS is usually Hölder continuous and has Lifshitz tails
[
22
,
17
]
.
These prevent analyticity near the spectral bottom and determine the initial scale for localization.
[
9
,
10
]
.

Higher regularity such as
C^{k}
-smoothness or analyticity requires extra structure.
For tight-binding and regular continuum models,
an analytic single-site law with large disorder yields analytic DOS,
as shown in
[
12
]
(see also
[
4
]
).
The work
[
5
]
gives an optimal Wegner estimate and continuity of the IDS, a key input.
We extend the mechanism of
[
12
]

to continuum point interactions via Krein’s formula,
proving analyticity of the DOS and certain averaged correlation functions
on a negative-energy interval by the random-walk expansion
and contour deformation under a pole-gap condition, with uniform bounds in a complex neighborhood of the interval.

Our analysis requires a uniform pole-gap condition ensuring that the pole

q=1/G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(E;0)
is separated from the support of the single-site law.
This is formulated precisely in Assumption A in Section
3
.

Sections
3
-
5
develop the resolvent representation, the path expansion,
and the analytic bounds, leading to our main result (Theorem
9
),
which shows that the density of states is real-analytic on a negative-energy interval.

2
Self-adjointness and deterministic spectrum via periodic realizations

We review known results which identify part of the almost-sure spectrum of the random operator through suitable periodic realizations.
In the attractive regime this shows that the spectrum contains a nonempty negative-energy band,
which later ensures that the convergence set of the random-walk expansion intersects the spectrum.
The argument uses the Kirsch–Martinelli theorem,
relating the almost-sure spectrum to the union of spectra of all admissible periodic configurations,
together with Floquet–Bloch theory for periodic lattice point-interaction Hamiltonians.

By the Kirsch–Martinelli theorem
[
16
]
, the almost-sure spectrum of

H_{\omega}=-\Delta+\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q_{\omega}(a)\,\delta(x-a)

is the union of the spectra of all admissible periodic potentials. A
periodic admissible potential

is a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic family
\{q(a)\}_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
of coupling constants, each in
Q
.
The period may be any finite integer in each coordinate direction, and the couplings need not be constant.

It is enough to consider the simplest case, where all sites have the same coupling constant
q\in Q
.
The theorem then implies that the spectrum of this constant-coupling periodic operator is contained in the almost-sure spectrum.
This already yields a negative-energy band in the attractive regime, and this suffices for our later use.
For
d=3
, see Albeverio et al.
[
1
, Ch. II]
; for
d=2
, see Albeverio–Geyler
[
2
]
.
In
d=2
, the band position depends on the renormalization scale
\kappa_{0}
,
a fixed parameter appearing in the definition of
G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}
.
In
d=2
, the choice of the renormalization scale
\kappa_{0}
in

G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(E;0)=(2\pi)^{-1}\log(\kappa/\kappa_{0})
shifts
the diagonal of the Weyl function
M(z)
by a constant.
This shift can be incorporated into the diagonal parameter
\Theta
, see
[
19
,
3
]
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed and consider the periodic operator

H_{\mathrm{per}}(q)=-\Delta+q\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\delta(x-a)

on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d=1,2,3
.
Self-adjoint realizations are obtained from the standard boundary conditions at each lattice site (see
[
1
, Ch. I]
),
where
q
enters linearly in the relation between the singular and regular parts of the wave function near each site.

In the attractive case
q<0
, Floquet–Bloch theory gives a nontrivial band entirely below
the essential spectrum of the free Laplacian
[
1
]
.
For
d=2
, this follows from the general Floquet–Bloch analysis of periodic point interactions in
[
2
]
.
Here the choice of the renormalization scale
\kappa_{0}
shifts the reference of the logarithmic term in

G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(E;0)=(2\pi)^{-1}\log(\kappa/\kappa_{0}),\hskip 20.00003ptE=-\kappa^{2}<0,

and hence changes the absolute position of the spectral band.
The existence of a nontrivial band below the free Laplacian is unaffected, but the lower edge energy depends on
\kappa_{0}
.
Thus the band inclusion in the almost-sure spectrum holds for any fixed choice of
\kappa_{0}
.
Therefore, if
Q
contains a negative value, the almost-sure spectrum
\sigma(H_{\omega})
intersects
(-\infty,0)
.
This fact, proved in Step 1 of Proposition
8
,
is used in Section
5
to show that the convergence region of the random-walk expansion overlaps the spectrum.

3
Resolvent representation and lattice reduction

In this section we give an explicit resolvent representation for continuum
point-interaction Hamiltonians and reduce the problem to a lattice model.
The Krein-type resolvent formula expresses the resolvent in terms of the
free resolvent and the inverse of the principal matrix. We decompose the
principal matrix into diagonal and off-diagonal parts, interpret the latter
as a bounded hopping operator, and obtain convergence criteria for the
Neumann series by Schur’s test.
A rigorous justification of this reduction via the boundary-triplet framework
is given in Section
4
.

Our main analytic assumptions are collected below as Assumption A.

Assumption A (Uniform pole-gap).

Let
Q=\mathrm{supp}\,\mu
. There exist
\delta>\delta^{\prime}>0
and a compact interval

I\subset(-\infty,0)
such that:

1.

The single-site density
g
extends holomorphically to
\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
.

2.

The uniform pole-gap condition holds:

\inf_{q\in\Omega_{\delta}(Q),\ z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)}\bigl|q^{-1}-G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(z;0)\bigr|\;\geq\;\Delta_{*}>0.

3.

The small-hopping bound
S(z)\Delta_{*}^{-1}<1
holds for all

z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
.

As an example, if
\mu
is uniform on
[\alpha,\beta]\subset(-\infty,0)
with

\mathrm{dist}([\alpha,\beta],0)>0
, then Assumption A is satisfied for a sufficiently
negative interval
I
. Indeed, by Proposition 7 the pole

q=1/G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(E;0)
tends to
0^{\pm}
as
E\to-\infty
, so it stays uniformly
separated from
Q
once
\mathrm{dist}(Q,0)>0
.

3.1
Resolvent representation

We begin with the free resolvent kernel of
-\Delta-z
, denoted

G_{0}(z;x-y)
. For
d=1,2,3
it is given by

G_{0}(z;x-y)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{-z}}\,e^{-\sqrt{-z}|x-y|},&d=1,\\[4.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2\pi}K_{0}(\sqrt{-z}|x-y|),&d=2,\\[4.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{4\pi|x-y|}\,e^{-\sqrt{-z}|x-y|},&d=3,\end{cases}

where the branch of
\sqrt{-z}
is chosen so that

\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{-z}>0
for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
.
In particular, for
z=-\kappa^{2}<0
one has
\sqrt{-z}=\kappa>0
, and
for
z=E+i0
with
E>0
one has
\sqrt{-z}=i\sqrt{E}
.

In dimensions
d=2
and
d=3
, the diagonal value
G_{0}(z;0)
is singular
and must be renormalized. We set

G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{2\kappa},&d=1,\ z=-\kappa^{2}<0,\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\log\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{-z}}{\kappa_{0}}\right),&d=2,\\[6.0pt]
-\dfrac{\sqrt{-z}}{4\pi},&d=3,\end{cases}

where
\kappa_{0}>0
is a fixed renormalization scale in
d=2
.
For
d=1
no renormalization is needed, and

G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)=G_{0}(z;0)
. In what follows,

G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}
is used for the diagonal entries of the free resolvent,
while off-diagonal entries
G_{0}(z;x-y)
with
x\neq y
are unchanged.

We introduce

w(q,z)=\big(q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\big)^{-1},

and assume the
uniform pole-gap condition

\inf_{q\in\Omega_{\delta}(Q),\ z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)}\big|\,q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\,\big|\ \geq\ \Delta_{*}>0,

where
\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
and
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
are complex

\delta
-neighborhoods of
Q
and
I
.

The
principal matrix

\Gamma_{\omega}(z)
is defined by

\Gamma_{\omega}(z;a,b)=\big(q_{\omega}(a)^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\big)\delta_{ab}-(1-\delta_{ab})\,G_{0}(z;a-b),\hskip 10.00002pt(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

It can be written as

\Gamma_{\omega}(z)=D_{\omega}(z)-T(z),

where
D_{\omega}(z)=\mathrm{diag}(w_{\omega}(a;z)^{-1})
with

w_{\omega}(a;z)=\big(q_{\omega}(a)^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\big)^{-1},

and
T(z)
, the
hopping term
, is

T(z;a,b)=\begin{cases}-\,G_{0}(z;a-b),&a\neq b,\\
0,&a=b.\end{cases}

In operator form on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,

(D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}\varphi)(a)=w_{\omega}(a;z)\,\varphi(a),\hskip 10.00002pt(T(z)\varphi)(a)=\sum_{b\neq a}\big(-G_{0}(z;a-b)\big)\,\varphi(b).

For
\mathrm{Im}\,z>0
, the kernel
G_{0}(z;n)
decays exponentially in

|n|
; the same holds for
\mathrm{Re}\,z<0
with
\mathrm{Im}\,z=0
.
Hence
T(z)
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
Similarly,
D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
is bounded provided

q_{\omega}(a)^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)
stays away from
0
on the
support of the single-site law.

The connection with the full resolvent is given by a

Krein-type resolvent formula
, which extends the rank-one case

[
1
, Theorem I.1.1]
to finitely or infinitely many point
interactions:

(H_{\omega}-z)^{-1}(x,y)=G_{0}(z;x-y)+\sum_{a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}G_{0}(z;x-a)\,\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}(a,b)\,G_{0}(z;b-y),

(3.1)

where
G_{0}(z;0)
is understood as
G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)
whenever

x=a
or
y=b
. Define
\phi_{x}(a):=G_{0}(z;x-a)
and
\psi_{y}(b):=G_{0}(z;b-y)
.
With this notation, the double sum in (
3.1
) equals

\langle\phi_{x},\,\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}\psi_{y}\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
.
For
\text{{\rm Im}}z>0
this is well-defined by the general theory underlying the Krein formula
(e.g.
[
1
, Thm. I.1.1]
); moreover, for
\operatorname{Re}z<0
it is also
well-defined under the small-hopping condition
\|D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)\|<1
,
which yields the Neumann expansion of
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
(see (
3.3
)-(
3.4
)),
while Lemma
2
ensures the required decay of
G_{0}(z;\cdot)
.

By the Pastur–Shubin formula, the averaged resolvent relevant for the
IDS/DOS can be written as

\frac{1}{|C|}\,\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\chi_{C}\left(G_{0}+\sum_{a,b}G_{0}(\,\cdot\!-a\,)\,\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}(a,b)\,G_{0}(b-\,\cdot\,)\right)\chi_{C}\right].

In this representation,
G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)
appears only inside the
principal matrix
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)
and not as a bare diagonal value of
the continuum resolvent. Thus all expressions are well-defined in

d=2,3
without pointwise evaluation of

(H_{\omega}-z)^{-1}(x,x)
.

If

\|D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)\|<1,

then
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
admits the convergent Neumann series

\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)\big)^{n}D_{\omega}(z)^{-1},

(3.2)

with convergence in operator norm. Each term in (
3.2
)
corresponds to
n
steps and
n+1
vertices in the discrete path
representation described later.

Lemma 1

(Interchange of expectation and trace)
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded. For
\text{{\rm Im}}z>0
, the averaged resolvent

G(z)=\frac{1}{|C|}\,\mathbb{E}[\,\mathrm{Tr}\,\chi_{C}(H_{\omega}-z)^{-1}\chi_{C}]

is well-defined. Moreover, under the uniform pole-gap assumption and the
small-hopping condition, the series expansion of
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
converges
absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
. Hence
expectation, trace, infinite sum, and differentiation with respect to
z

can be interchanged.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the Weierstrass M-test
and the uniform bounds established in Lemma
2
and Lemma
3
.

\Box

3.2
Schur’s test

A sufficient condition for the convergence of (
3.2
) is given by Schur’s test.
We regard
D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)
as an operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
with kernel

K(a,b)=w_{\omega}(a;z)\,T(z;a,b),\hskip 20.00003pta,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Set

S_{1}(z)=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|K(a,b)|,\hskip 20.00003ptS_{2}(z)=\sup_{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|K(a,b)|.

Then Schur’s test yields

\|D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)\|\leq\sqrt{S_{1}(z)\,S_{2}(z)}.

(3.3)

Since
T(z;a,b)=0
for
a=b
and
T(z;a,b)=-G_{0}(z;a-b)
otherwise, both
S_{1}(z)
and
S_{2}(z)
satisfy

S_{j}(z)\leq\big(\sup_{a}|w_{\omega}(a;z)|\big)\sum_{n\neq 0}|G_{0}(z;n)|,\hskip 20.00003ptj=1,2.

Substituting this into (
3.3
) gives the estimate

\|D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)\|\leq\big(\sup_{a}|w_{\omega}(a;z)|\big)\sum_{n\neq 0}|G_{0}(z;n)|.

(3.4)

Here we write
S(z):=\sum_{n\neq 0}|G_{0}(z;n)|
. From (
3.4
), the “small-hopping” regime holds whenever

\big(\sup_{a}|w_{\omega}(a;z)|\big)\sum_{n\neq 0}|G_{0}(z;n)|<1.

Remark 1
.

We call the condition
\|D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)\|<1
the
small-hopping regime
.
It ensures that the Neumann series for
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
converges in operator norm.
Under the uniform pole-gap assumption one has
\sup_{a}|w_{\omega}(a;z)|\leq\Delta_{*}^{-1}
for

z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
, so the small-hopping condition reduces to
S(z)\,\Delta_{*}^{-1}<1
.
This can be achieved either by taking
E
sufficiently negative so that
S(z)
is small, or by scaling

q_{\omega}(a)=\lambda\,v_{\omega}(a)
with
|\lambda|
small, in which case
w_{\omega}(a;z)=O(\lambda)
.

3.3
Behavior of the off-diagonal sum and uniform bound on
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)

We next consider how the decay of the free resolvent kernel
G_{0}(z;x)
affects the convergence of

S(z)=\sum_{n\neq 0}|G_{0}(z;n)|.

For later use in the Weierstrass
M
-test and in termwise differentiation,
we require bounds that are uniform for
z
in a complex neighborhood
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)

of a negative-energy interval
I\subset(-\infty,0)
.

Lemma 2

(Uniform bound on
S(z)
)
.

Let
I\subset(-\infty,0)
be compact and
\delta^{\prime}>0
.
Assume that
\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{-z}\geq\kappa_{*}>0
for all
z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
.
Then there exist constants
C_{d}(\kappa_{*})>0
such that

S(z)\leq C_{d}(\kappa_{*})\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all }z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I),

with the following estimates depending on
d
:

\displaystyle d=1:\hskip 10.00002pt|G_{0}(z;n)|\leq\frac{C\,e^{-\kappa_{*}|n|}}{2\kappa_{*}},

\displaystyle d=2:\hskip 10.00002pt|G_{0}(z;n)|\leq\frac{C\,e^{-\kappa_{*}|n|}}{|n|^{1/2}},

\displaystyle d=3:\hskip 10.00002pt|G_{0}(z;n)|\leq\frac{C\,e^{-\kappa_{*}|n|}}{|n|}.

Proof.

The bounds follow from standard asymptotics of the free resolvent
kernel in each dimension (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Sec. I.5]
), together with the
assumption
\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{-z}\geq\kappa_{*}
. Summing over
n\neq 0

gives a finite constant
C_{d}(\kappa_{*})
.

\Box

Lemma 3

(Quantitative bound on
S(E)
)
.

Let
E<0
and set
\kappa=\sqrt{-E}>0
.
Then there exist constants
C_{d}>0
such that

\displaystyle d=1:

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002ptS(E)\ \leq\ \frac{e^{-\kappa}}{\kappa(1-e^{-\kappa})},

\displaystyle d=2:

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002ptS(E)\ \leq\ C_{2}\,\kappa^{-3/2},

\displaystyle d=3:

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002ptS(E)\ \leq\ C_{3}\,\kappa^{-2}.

In particular, if
S(E)\,\Delta_{*}^{-1}<1
is required, this holds whenever

\kappa>\kappa_{0}(d,\Delta_{*})
for a suitable
\kappa_{0}
depending only on
d
and
\Delta_{*}
.

Proof.

For
d=1
,
|G_{0}(E;n)|=\frac{1}{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa|n|}
, hence

S(E)=\frac{1}{\kappa}\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-\kappa n}=\frac{e^{-\kappa}}{\kappa(1-e^{-\kappa})}.

For
d=2,3
, one uses the known asymptotics

|G_{0}(E;n)|\leq Ce^{-\kappa|n|}|n|^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha=1/2
for
d=2
and
\alpha=1
for
d=3
.
Approximating the sum over
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
by the radial integral gives

S(E)\ \leq\ C_{d}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-\kappa r}r^{d-1-\alpha}\,dr\ \leq\ \tilde{C}_{d}\,\kappa^{-(d-\alpha)},

which yields the stated exponents.
These exponents reflect the decay rates
|G_{0}(E;n)|\sim e^{-\kappa|n|}/|n|^{\alpha}

with
\alpha=1/2
in
d=2
and
\alpha=1
in
d=3
.
The constants
C_{d}
can be obtained by comparing the discrete sum with
the radial integral, and they are not optimal but sufficient for the
small-hopping argument.

\Box

Remark 2
.

The constants
C_{d}
arise from comparison of the discrete sum with a radial
integral, using the known asymptotics of the free resolvent kernel
(modified Bessel functions in
d=2
and Yukawa kernel in
d=3
).
They are not optimal but sufficient for our purpose. For details, see

[
1
, Sec. I.5]
.

Real-axis behavior.

For
E<0
we have
\kappa=\sqrt{-E}>0
. As
z\to E+i0
,

•

d=1
:
|G_{0}(E+i0;x)|\sim\dfrac{1}{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa|x|}
,

•

d=2
:
|G_{0}(E+i0;x)|\sim C\,e^{-\kappa|x|}\,|x|^{-1/2}
,

•

d=3
:
|G_{0}(E+i0;x)|\sim C\,e^{-\kappa|x|}\,|x|^{-1}
,

so
S(E+i0)
remains finite.

For
E>0
the exponential decay is lost:

•

d=1
:
|G_{0}(E+i0;x)|\sim\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{E}}
,

•

d=2
:
|G_{0}(E+i0;x)|\sim C\,|x|^{-1/2}
,

•

d=3
:
|G_{0}(E+i0;x)|\sim C\,|x|^{-1}
,

and
S(E+i0)
diverges. Hence in this approach no analyticity can be expected for
E>0
.

Implication for the small-hopping condition.

By Lemma
2
, the small-hopping condition on
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
is

S(z)\,\Delta_{*}^{-1}\leq C_{d}(\kappa_{*})\,\Delta_{*}^{-1}<1,

which gives the uniform majorant needed for the
M
-test and for termwise differentiation in the analyticity proof.

4
Boundary triplets and Krein formula for point interactions

This section provides a justification of the resolvent representation in Section
3
for a countable family of point interactions.
It also shows that the principal matrix can be identified with the Weyl function associated with a boundary triplet(in the sense of
[
7
]
).
The argument is based on the abstract extension theory using boundary triplets
and Krein-type formulas
[
23
,
14
,
7
]
.
An explicit realization for Schrödinger operators with infinitely many point interactions was given in
[
19
]
.
The periodic case was studied in
[
3
]
.

Let
X=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
d\in\{1,2,3\}
. Denote by
H_{\min}
the closure on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

of
-\Delta
initially defined on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus X)
.
Then
H_{\max}=H_{\min}^{*}
consists of functions
u
that are
H^{2}
off
X
and admit
at each
a\in X
a decomposition into a singular part and a regular part. More precisely, for
d=3
one has near
a

u(x)=\frac{\xi_{a}}{4\pi|x-a|}+\eta_{a}+o(1),

for
d=2

u(x)=\frac{\xi_{a}}{2\pi}\log|x-a|+\eta_{a}+o(1),

and in
d=1
one uses one-sided boundary values, see
[
19
]
.
Define boundary maps
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:\mathcal{D}(H_{\max})\to\ell^{2}(X)
by

\Gamma_{0}u=(\xi_{a})_{a\in X},\hskip 20.00003pt\Gamma_{1}u=(\eta_{a})_{a\in X}.

Then
(\ell^{2}(X),\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
is a boundary triplet for
H_{\max}
,
and the free Laplacian
H_{0}=-\Delta
is the distinguished self-adjoint extension corresponding to the boundary condition
\Gamma_{0}u=0
.

The associated the
\gamma
-field (operator family) and Weyl function
M(z)
are defined
for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
by the relations that
u=\gamma(z)\alpha

solves
(-\Delta-z)u=0
with
\Gamma_{0}u=\alpha\in\ell^{2}(X)
and
M(z)=\Gamma_{1}\gamma(z)
.
In the present setting one has the explicit formulas

\displaystyle(\gamma(z)\alpha)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in X}\alpha_{a}\,G_{0}(z;x-a),

(4.1)

\displaystyle M(z)_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(z;0)\,\delta_{ab}+(1-\delta_{ab})\,G_{0}(z;a-b),

(4.2)

where
G_{0}(z;\cdot)
is the free resolvent kernel and
G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(z;0)

is the renormalized diagonal value in
d=2,3
(and equals
G_{0}(z;0)
in
d=1
), with the same conventions as in Section
3
.

For a diagonal operator
\Theta=\mathrm{diag}(q(a)^{-1})
on
\ell^{2}(X)
define the self-adjoint extension

\mathcal{D}(H_{\Theta})=\{u\in\mathcal{D}(H_{\max}):\ \Gamma_{1}u=\Theta\,\Gamma_{0}u\}.

This extension corresponds to the formal Hamiltonian
-\Delta+\sum_{a\in X}q(a)\delta(x-a)
with coupling constants
q(a)\in\mathbb{R}
.
The Krein resolvent formula in the boundary triplet framework yields

(H_{\Theta}-z)^{-1}=R_{0}(z)+\gamma(z)\,(\Theta-M(z))^{-1}\gamma(\bar{z})^{*},

(4.3)

for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, where
R_{0}(z)=(-\Delta-z)^{-1}
.
This identity is standard in the abstract theory
[
23
,
14
,
7
]
,
and its realization for Schrödinger operators with countably many point interactions on
X

is given in
[
19
]
. In components, using the kernels
G_{0}(z;\cdot)
one obtains

(H_{\Theta}-z)^{-1}(x,y)=G_{0}(z;x-y)+\sum_{a,b\in X}G_{0}(z;x-a)\,(\Theta-M(z))^{-1}(a,b)\,G_{0}(z;b-y).

(4.4)

Comparing this with the notation of Section
3
, the principal matrix there is

\Gamma_{\omega}(z)=\Theta-M(z),

since its diagonal entries are
q(a)^{-1}-G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(z;0)
and off-diagonal entries are
-G_{0}(z;a-b)
.
Hence the Krein formula above reproduces exactly the resolvent representation used in equation (
3.1
), now justified for a countable set of interaction sites.

The case
d=2
requires a fixed renormalization scale in
G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(z;0)=(2\pi)^{-1}\log(\sqrt{-z}/\kappa_{0})
;
this choice only shifts the diagonal of
M(z)
by a constant and can be incorporated into the diagonal parameter
\Theta
, see
[
19
]
.
For
z
with
\mathrm{Im}\,z>0
all objects are bounded by construction, so the formula holds without any extra smallness conditions.
For real
z<0
, the small-hopping condition from Section
3
guarantees that
(\Theta-M(z))^{-1}

admits a Neumann series in operator norm and that all series in the kernel representation converge absolutely
and uniformly on compact subsets of a complex neighborhood of the negative-energy interval considered there.
Thus the lattice reduction is rigorously justified and identifies
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)
with the Weyl function.

Theorem 4
.

Let
X=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
d\in\{1,2,3\}
. For real
q(a)
and
\Theta=\mathrm{diag}(q(a)^{-1})

the self-adjoint realization
H_{\Theta}
of
-\Delta+\sum_{a\in X}q(a)\delta(x-a)
satisfies the Krein-type resolvent identity

(H_{\Theta}-z)^{-1}=R_{0}(z)+\gamma(z)\,(\Theta-M(z))^{-1}\gamma(\bar{z})^{*},

with
\gamma
and
M
as above.
In particular, in the notation of Section
3
one has
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)=\Theta-M(z)
and the integral kernel equals

(H_{\Theta}-z)^{-1}(x,y)=G_{0}(z;x-y)+\sum_{a,b\in X}G_{0}(z;x-a)\,\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}(a,b)\,G_{0}(z;b-y).

5
Random walk expansion

We express the inverse principal matrix as a convergent sum over discrete lattice paths.
This representation makes explicit the combinatorial structure of the resolvent in the small-hopping regime.
Each path carries vertex weights from the diagonal part and edge weights from the hopping term.
The i.i.d. couplings can then be averaged site by site, leading to single-site integrals.
These will be estimated uniformly in the next section.

5.1
Path representation of the inverse principal matrix

From the resolvent representation (
3.1
), we describe its combinatorial structure in the small-hopping regime.
The operator
D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
assigns a
vertex weight

w_{\omega}(a;z)
to each site
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
and
T(z)
assigns an
edge weight

-G_{0}(z;a-b)
between distinct sites
a\neq b
.

Compositions of these operators give sequences of hops between vertices, with local weights at the visited sites.
In the Neumann series for
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
, such compositions correspond to sums over discrete paths on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Definition 1

(Discrete path)
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
A discrete path of length
n
from
a
to
b
is a sequence

(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n}),

with
a_{0}=a
and
a_{n}=b
, such that consecutive vertices are distinct:

a_{j}\neq a_{j+1},\hskip 10.00002ptj=0,1,\dots,n-1.

Vertices may be visited several times (with possibly non-nearest jumps).
The edge weight between
a_{j}
and
a_{j+1}
is
-G_{0}(z;a_{j}-a_{j+1})
.
We denote by
\Pi_{n}(a,b)
the set of all discrete paths of length
n
from
a
to
b
.

Proposition 5
.

Assume that
D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
and
T(z)
are bounded operators on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and that
the small-hopping condition
\|D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)\|<1
holds at the spectral parameter
z
.
Then

\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big(D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}T(z)\big)^{n}D_{\omega}(z)^{-1},

and the series converges absolutely in operator norm on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
In particular, for
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}(a,b)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi_{n}(a,b)}\ \prod_{j=0}^{n}w_{\omega}(a_{j};z)\,\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\big[-G_{0}(z;a_{j}-a_{j+1})\big].

Proof.

In the expansion of
\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}(a,b)
as a sum over paths

\gamma=(a_{0},\dots,a_{n})\in\Pi_{n}(a,b)
,
each vertex
a_{j}
gives the factor
w_{\omega}(a_{j};z)
from
D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
,
and each edge
(a_{j},a_{j+1})
gives
-G_{0}(z;a_{j}-a_{j+1})
from
T(z)
.
By repeated application of
T(z)
and
D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
, we obtain the product form stated above.
By the small-hopping assumption, the Neumann series converges in operator norm.
The kernel of
(D_{\omega}^{-1}T)^{n}D_{\omega}^{-1}
is given by
n
successive applications of
T(z)
,
with
D_{\omega}(z)^{-1}
at each vertex, which produces the product of vertex and edge weights along each admissible path.

\Box

5.2
Averaging over the disorder

Averaging over the i.i.d. couplings
\{q_{\omega}(a)\}
can be done site by site.
For a path
\gamma=(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in\Pi_{k}(a,b)
we write

\sharp(\gamma,\alpha)=\sharp\{\,j:a_{j}=\alpha\,\}

for the number of visits of
\gamma
to the site
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
By independence,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=0}^{k}w_{\omega}(a_{j};z)\Big]=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{Q}\big(q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\big)^{-\sharp(\gamma,\alpha)}g(q)\,dq,

(5.1)

where
Q
is the support of the single-site distribution
\mu
with density
g
.
Thus, if a site
a
is visited
r=\sharp(\gamma,a)
times, the contribution is

\int_{Q}w(q,z)^{r}\,d\mu(q),\hskip 20.00003ptw(q,z)=\big(q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\big)^{-1}.

These factors depend on
r
and do not reduce to a fixed power of a single moment.
For our purpose it is enough to use the uniform pole-gap condition to bound

|w(q,z)|\leq\Delta_{*}^{-1},\hskip 20.00003ptq\in Q,\ z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I).

Since
|G_{0}(z;n)|
is summable over
n\neq 0
, Schur’s test for the nonnegative kernel

K_{\mathrm{edge}}(a,b):=|G_{0}(z;a-b)|\,\chi_{\{a\neq b\}}
yields

\|K_{\mathrm{edge}}^{m}\|_{1\to\infty}\leq S(z)^{m},\hskip 20.00003ptS(z):=\sum_{n\neq 0}|G_{0}(z;n)|.

For a closed path
\gamma=(a_{0},\dots,a_{m})
from
0
to
0
, the weight is

\prod_{j=0}^{m}w_{\omega}(a_{j};z)\ \prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(-G_{0}(z;a_{j}-a_{j+1})).

By the uniform pole-gap assumption,
|w_{\omega}(a_{j};z)|\leq\Delta_{*}^{-1}
for all
j
,
so the vertex part is bounded by
\Delta_{*}^{-(m+1)}
.
The edge part is estimated by Schur’s test:

\sup_{a,b}\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi_{m}(a,b)}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}|G_{0}(\cdot)|\leq\|K_{\mathrm{edge}}^{m}\|_{1\to\infty}\leq S(z)^{m}.

Therefore,

\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=0}^{m}w_{\omega}(a_{j};z)\,\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(-G_{0}(z;a_{j}-a_{j+1}))\Big]\Big|\ \leq\ \Delta_{*}^{-(m+1)}S(z)^{m}.

Summing over
m\geq 0
gives the geometric-series bound

\big|\mathbb{E}\,\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}(0,0)\big|\ \leq\ \Delta_{*}^{-1}\sum_{m\geq 0}(S(z)\Delta_{*}^{-1})^{m},

which converges whenever
S(z)\Delta_{*}^{-1}<1
.

5.3
Uniform bounds via analytic continuation

Fix a closed interval
Q\subset\mathbb{R}
containing
{\rm supp}(\mu)
and a compact energy interval
I\subset(-\infty,0)
.
Let
\delta>0
and
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta
be such that the density
g
of
\mu
extends holomorphically to
\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
,
and that the uniform pole-gap condition

\inf_{q\in\Omega_{\delta}(Q),\ z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)}|q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)|\ \geq\ \Delta_{*}>0

is satisfied.

Lemma 6

(Analytic continuation and bound)
.

Let
I\subset(-\infty,0)
be compact.
Assume that the single-site density
g
extends holomorphically to a neighborhood
\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
of
Q=\text{{\rm supp}\;}\mu
and that the uniform pole-gap condition

\inf_{q\in\Omega_{\delta}(Q),\ z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)}\big|q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\big|\ \geq\ \Delta_{*}>0

holds for some
\delta^{\prime}>0
.
Then, for each
m\geq 0
, the function

I_{m}(z):=\int_{Q}w(q,z)^{\,m+1}\,d\mu(q)

is holomorphic on
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
, and there exists
C=C(\delta,\delta^{\prime})>0
such that

|I_{m}(z)|\ \leq\ C\,\Delta_{*}^{-(m+1)}\hskip 10.00002pt(z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I),\ m\geq 0).

Proof.

The density
g
is real-analytic on
Q
, hence extends holomorphically to

\Omega_{\delta}(Q)=\{\,q\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{dist}(q,Q)<\delta\,\}.

For
E<0
define
\kappa=\sqrt{-E}
.
We next examine the behavior of the renormalized diagonal value

G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)
in the limit
\kappa=\sqrt{-E}\to\infty

(equivalently,
E\to-\infty
):

\displaystyle d=1:\hskip 10.00002ptG_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)=(2\kappa)^{-1}>0\ \Rightarrow\ 1/G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)=2\kappa\to+\infty,

\displaystyle d=2:\hskip 10.00002ptG_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)=(2\pi)^{-1}\log(\kappa/\kappa_{0})\ \Rightarrow\ 1/G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)\to 0^{+},

\displaystyle d=3:\hskip 10.00002ptG_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)=-\kappa/(4\pi)<0\ \Rightarrow\ 1/G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)\to 0^{-}.

Thus, in all cases
1/G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)\to 0^{\pm}
as
E\to-\infty
.
Since
Q\subset(-\infty,0)
has positive distance from
0
, the pole
q=1/G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)
is outside
\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
for large negative
E
.
Together with the uniform pole-gap assumption this gives the required separation.
For
z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
define

\Phi_{m}(q;z)=\frac{g(q)}{(q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0))^{m+1}}.

Then
\Phi_{m}(\cdot;z)
is holomorphic on
\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
.
Let
\eta=\partial\Omega_{\delta}(Q)\cap\mathbb{C}_{-}
be the lower boundary arc (see Figure
1
).

Figure 1:
Contour deformation for the
q
-integral.

Since the support of
g
is contained in
Q
, the integral over
Q

coincides with the integral over the real segment

\overline{\Omega_{\delta}(Q)}\cap\mathbb{R}
.
Connecting this real segment with the boundary arc

\eta\subset\partial\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
in the lower half-plane
yields a closed contour.
Applying Cauchy’s theorem to this contour gives

\int_{Q}\Phi_{m}(q;z)\,dq=\int_{\eta}\Phi_{m}(q;z)\,dq.

Along
\eta
we have
|q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)|\geq\Delta_{*}>0
, hence

|\Phi_{m}(q;z)|\ \leq\ \sup_{q\in\Omega_{\delta}(Q)}|g(q)|\,\Delta_{*}^{-(m+1)}.

Thus

|I_{m}(z)|\ \leq\ C\,\Delta_{*}^{-(m+1)},

where
C
depends only on
\delta,\delta^{\prime}
and the geometry of
\Omega_{\delta}(Q)
, but not on
m
or
z
.
Since the integrand is holomorphic in
z
and uniformly bounded on compact subsets of
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
, differentiation under the integral is justified, and
I_{m}
is holomorphic on
\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)
.

\Box

Proposition 7

(Uniform pole-gap under distance assumption)
.

Assume that the single-site support
Q\subset(-\infty,0)
satisfies

\mathrm{dist}(Q,0)>0
. Then there exists
\Delta_{*}>0
such that

\inf_{q\in Q,\,z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)}\big|q^{-1}-G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(z;0)\big|\geq\Delta_{*}.

In particular, in
d=2
with

G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(E;0)=(2\pi)^{-1}\log(\kappa/\kappa_{0}),\hskip 20.00003ptE=-\kappa^{2}<0,

the pole
q=1/G^{\mathrm{ren}}_{0}(E;0)
tends to
0
as
E\to-\infty
, so the
distance from
Q
is uniform once
\mathrm{dist}(Q,0)>0
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the discussion in the proof of Lemma
6
.

\Box

Remark 3
.

Let
I\subset(-\infty,0)
and suppose there exists
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that

extends holomorphically to

.
Then the density of states (

) is real-analytic on

.

Application to the averaged expansion.
By Lemma
6
,

\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[\Gamma_{\omega}(z)^{-1}(0,0)\big]\Big|\leq\frac{C}{\Delta_{*}}\sum_{m\geq 0}\big(S(z)\Delta_{*}^{-1}\big)^{m},

so the condition

for

gives absolute and uniform convergence,

.

5.4
Convergence region and intersection with the spectrum

We determine the set of energies where the random-walk expansion converges
and show that, in the attractive case, this set has nonempty intersection with the spectrum of
H_{\omega}
.

Proposition 8

(Nonempty intersection of the RWE-convergence set with the spectrum)
.

Assume the attractive case, that is, the single-site law
\mu
is supported on a connected interval
[\alpha,\beta]\subset(-\infty,0)
with
\alpha<\beta<0
.
Set

I=\{\,E<0:\ S(E)\,\Delta_{*}(E)^{-1}<1\,\},\hskip 20.00003pt\Delta_{*}(E)=\inf_{q\in\Omega_{\delta}}|q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(E;0)|.

Then
I\cap\sigma(H_{\omega})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

For any
\lambda_{0}\in[\alpha,\beta]
, the periodic operator

H_{\mathrm{per}}(\lambda_{0})=-\Delta+\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda_{0}\,\delta(x-a)

has a nontrivial band
[E_{-}(\lambda_{0}),E_{+}(\lambda_{0})]\subset(-\infty,0)
: a single attractive point interaction produces a bound state, and these hybridize under Floquet theory to form a band
[
2
]
.
As
|\lambda_{0}|
increases, the lower edge
E_{-}(\lambda_{0})
tends to
-\infty
, while the bandwidth remains bounded.
By the fact that the spectrum equals the union over all periodic realizations
[
16
]
, such bands are included in
\sigma(H_{\omega})
.

On the other hand, for
E<0
we write
\kappa=\sqrt{-E}
.
Then
S(E)\to 0
as
\kappa\to\infty
in all dimensions
d=1,2,3
, owing to the exponential decay of
G_{0}(E;x)
.
By the uniform pole-gap assumption,
\Delta_{*}(E)
is bounded below by some
c>0
for all sufficiently negative
E
.
Thus
S(E)\Delta_{*}(E)^{-1}\to 0
as
E\to-\infty
, and there exists
\kappa_{*}>0
such that
S(E)\Delta_{*}(E)^{-1}<1
whenever
E<-\kappa_{*}^{2}
.

Choosing
\lambda_{0}
so that
E_{-}(\lambda_{0})<-\kappa_{*}^{2}
, we see that the interval

[E_{-}(\lambda_{0}),\min\{E_{+}(\lambda_{0}),-\kappa_{*}^{2}\}]

is contained in both
\sigma(H_{\omega})
and
\mathcal{C}
, which proves the claim.
For
d=2,3
, the free resolvent kernel also decays exponentially,
with asymptotics
G_{0}(E;x)\sim C\,e^{-\kappa|x|}/|x|
for
d=3
and

G_{0}(E;x)\sim C\,e^{-\kappa|x|}/|x|^{1/2}
for
d=2
.
Hence
S(E)\,\Delta_{*}(E)^{-1}\to 0
as
E\to-\infty
,
so the above argument remains valid; see also

[
2
]
for a general Floquet–Bloch analysis in two dimensions.

\Box

We can now state the main conclusion of this section.

Theorem 9

(Random-walk expansion and analyticity)
.

Let

and let

.
Suppose there exist
\delta^{\prime}\in(0,\delta)
and
\Delta_{*}>0

holds, where

denotes the renormalized diagonal free resolvent.
If in addition

for all

then for each such

the kernel
(H_{\omega}-z)^{-1}(x,y)
admits the expansion of Proposition

,
converging absolutely and uniformly in
(x,y)
and

.

and

.

Proof.

Proposition
5
and Lemma

give
an absolutely convergent series expansion for the resolvent kernel, uniform in

and

.
Uniform convergence in a complex neighborhood of

and

by the Stieltjes inversion formula.

\Box

5.5
Example: uniform single-site law on a negative interval

Assume that
\mu
is uniform on
[\alpha,\beta]
with
\alpha<\beta<0
.
Then the integral in Lemma
6
becomes

I_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\big(q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\big)^{-(m+1)}\,dq.

Set
\rho:=G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)
. Since
q^{-1}-\rho=(1-\rho q)/q
, we have

(q^{-1}-\rho)^{-(m+1)}=\frac{q^{m+1}}{(1-\rho q)^{m+1}},

and hence

I_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{q^{m+1}}{(1-\rho q)^{m+1}}\,dq.

For
m\geq 1
we use the recurrence

\int\frac{q^{m+1}}{(1-\rho q)^{m+1}}\,dq=\frac{1}{m\rho}\,\frac{q^{m+1}}{(1-\rho q)^{m}}-\frac{m+1}{m\rho}\int\frac{q^{m}}{(1-\rho q)^{m}}\,dq,

which reduces the case
m
to smaller indices. Iterating down to
m=0
yields rational and logarithmic terms, with initial case

\int\frac{q}{1-\rho q}\,dq=-\frac{q}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\ln(1-\rho q).

For
m=1
we obtain

I_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\left[\frac{q^{2}}{\rho(1-\rho q)}-\frac{2}{\rho}\left(-\frac{q}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\ln(1-\rho q)\right)\right]_{q=\alpha}^{q=\beta}.

Higher
m
follow by repeated application of the recurrence.

For
m=0
, the integral reduces to the base case:

I_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{q}{1-\rho q}\,dq=\frac{1}{\rho(\beta-\alpha)}\left[-q+\frac{1}{\rho}\log(1-\rho q)\right]_{q=\alpha}^{q=\beta},

which gives

I_{0}(z)=-\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}(\beta-\alpha)}\log\!\left(\frac{1-\rho\alpha}{1-\rho\beta}\right).

In particular, for the lowest indices we obtain explicit closed forms: the expression above for
m=0
, and for
m=1
,

I_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\Biggl[\frac{q^{2}}{\rho(1-\rho q)}-\frac{2}{\rho}\left(-\frac{q}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\ln(1-\rho q)\right)\Biggr]_{\alpha}^{\beta}.

Remark 4
.

The branch of
\log(1-\rho q)
is chosen consistently on
[\alpha,\beta]
,
under the assumption that
1-\rho q\neq 0
for all
q\in[\alpha,\beta]
.

From the closed forms above we obtain a simple uniform bound, useful for analyticity and dominated convergence arguments.
Under the uniform pole-gap condition

\inf_{q\in[\alpha,\beta],\ z\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I)}|q^{-1}-\rho|\ \geq\ \Delta_{*}>0,

we have for all
m\geq 0

\left|\frac{q^{m+1}}{(1-\rho q)^{m+1}}\right|=\frac{1}{|q^{-1}-\rho|^{m+1}}\ \leq\ \Delta_{*}^{-(m+1)},\hskip 20.00003ptq\in[\alpha,\beta],

and therefore

|I_{m}(z)|\leq\Delta_{*}^{-(m+1)},\hskip 20.00003ptm\geq 0.

(5.2)

If one prefers to express the distance from the pole
q=1/\rho
, writing
d=\mathrm{dist}([\alpha,\beta],\{1/\rho\})>0
gives
|1-\rho q|\geq|\rho|d
and

|I_{m}(z)|\ \leq\ \left(\frac{\max\{|\alpha|,|\beta|\}}{|\rho|d}\right)^{m+1},\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha<\beta<0.

(5.3)

These explicit formulas and bounds allow a detailed study of the dependence on
\alpha,\beta
and
z
.
When
\alpha
and
\beta
are close,
w(q,z)
varies only slightly,
which simplifies coefficient estimates and helps identify energy intervals where the small-hopping regime holds.
Thus, for the uniform law on
[\alpha,\beta]
, the averages
I_{m}(z)
admit closed forms and uniform bounds,
giving precise control of the expansion coefficients and verifying the small-hopping condition in concrete parameter ranges.
They can also be extended beyond the i.i.d. attractive case, such as to weakly correlated laws (where factorization may fail)
or to sign-indefinite laws with a separated negative component.

Appendix: Extension to Multi-Point Functions

The random-walk expansion developed in the main text applies not only to
the one-point function
G(z)
but also to averaged products of resolvents with
distinct complex energy parameters. For
k\geq 1
, define

F_{k}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k};x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{k},y_{k})=\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\prod_{j=1}^{k}(H_{\omega}-z_{j})^{-1}(x_{j},y_{j})\,\Big],

for
(x_{j},y_{j})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(or
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
and
\text{{\rm Im}}z_{j}>0
.

By Krein’s resolvent formula and the path expansion
(equation (
3.2
) and Proposition
5
),
each kernel
(H_{\omega}-z_{j})^{-1}(x_{j},y_{j})
can be expressed as a sum over
discrete paths with vertex weights
w_{\omega}(a;z_{j})
and edge weights

-G_{0}(z_{j};a-b)
. Averaging over
\omega
then factorizes these weights over sites,
leading to integrals

J_{m_{1},\dots,m_{k}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})=\int_{\text{{\rm supp}\;}\mu}\prod_{j=1}^{k}w(q,z_{j})^{m_{j}}\,g(q)\,dq,

where
m_{j}
is the number of visits of the
j
-th path to a site and

w(q,z)=\big(q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z;0)\big)^{-1}.

If
g
extends analytically to
\Omega_{\delta}(\text{{\rm supp}\;}\mu)
and the uniform pole-gap condition

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\Omega_{\delta}(\text{{\rm supp}\;}\mu)\\
z_{j}\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I_{j})\end{subarray}}|q^{-1}-G_{0}^{\mathrm{ren}}(z_{j};0)|\ \geq\ \Delta_{*}>0

holds for compact intervals
I_{j}\subset(-\infty,0)
, the contour deformation
argument of Lemma
6
applies to each integral.
It follows that

|J_{m_{1},\dots,m_{k}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})|\ \leq\ C\,\Delta_{*}^{-(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k})},

uniformly for
z_{j}\in\Omega_{\delta^{\prime}}(I_{j})
.
Together with the edge-weight bounds
h_{j}=\sup_{|a-b|=1}|G_{0}(z_{j};a-b)|
,
this shows that the series for
F_{k}
converges whenever

S(z_{j})\,\Delta_{*}^{-1}<1,\hskip 20.00003ptj=1,\dots,k.

Hence
F_{k}
is holomorphic in this domain.

A particularly notable case is
k=2
, which connects to the Kubo–Greenwood
formula for conductivity.

For completeness, we recall the conductivity definition we have in mind.
Let
\Lambda_{L}=[-L,L]^{d}
and
H_{\omega,L}
the Dirichlet restriction of
H_{\omega}
.
At zero temperature, the finite-volume the direct current (dc) conductivity at frequency
\nu

is given by the Kubo–Greenwood formula

\sigma_{L}(\nu)\;=\;\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{L}|}\sum_{m,n}\frac{f(E_{m})-f(E_{n})}{E_{n}-E_{m}}\,\frac{\bigl|\langle\psi_{m},\,J\psi_{n}\rangle\bigr|^{2}}{\nu+i0+(E_{m}-E_{n})},

where
\{E_{m},\psi_{m}\}
are the eigenpairs of
H_{\omega,L}
,
J
denotes
the current operator, and
f(E)=\chi_{(-\infty,E_{F}]}(E)
is the Fermi factor
at zero temperature.
The thermodynamic limit

\sigma(\nu)\;=\;\lim_{L\to\infty}\,\sigma_{L}(\nu)

is the dc conductivity.

Proposition 10

(Analyticity of conductivity kernel)
.

Let
d\in\{1,2,3\}
and assume the hypotheses of Theorem
9
.
For
k=2
, put
z_{1}=E+i0
and
z_{2}=E+\nu+i0
with
E<0
.
Then the averaged two-point function

F_{2}(z_{1},z_{2};x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})=\mathbb{E}\Big[(H_{\omega}-z_{1})^{-1}(x_{1},y_{1})\,(H_{\omega}-z_{2})^{-1}(x_{2},y_{2})\Big]

is holomorphic in
\nu
near
0
and admits uniform bounds on compact subsets.
In particular, the zero-temperature dc conductivity
\sigma(\nu)
defined by the
Kubo–Greenwood formula extends holomorphically to a neighborhood of
\nu=0
.

The uniform bounds obtained in Section
5
imply that expectation, trace,
and the limit
\nu\to 0
can be interchanged in the definition above.
This justifies the analyticity of
\sigma(\nu)
near
\nu=0
claimed in
Proposition
10
.

Remark 5
.

At finite temperature, the Fermi factor enters as a bounded analytic weight, so the same argument applies.
The only additional point is the
\nu\to 0
limit, where exchanging the limit with expectation
and trace is allowed because of the uniform bounds obtained above.

Finally, for
k=2
, let the two paths visit a site
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
times.
Independence yields

|J_{m_{1},m_{2}}(z_{1},z_{2})|\ \leq\ C\,\Delta_{*}^{-(m_{1}+m_{2})}.

Summing over all
(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}
gives

\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0}\big(S(z_{1})\Delta_{*}^{-1}\big)^{m_{1}}\big(S(z_{2})\Delta_{*}^{-1}\big)^{m_{2}}=\frac{1}{1-S(z_{1})\Delta_{*}^{-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-S(z_{2})\Delta_{*}^{-1}},

which converges whenever
S(z_{j})\Delta_{*}^{-1}<1
for
j=1,2
.
Thus
F_{2}
is holomorphic in this domain.

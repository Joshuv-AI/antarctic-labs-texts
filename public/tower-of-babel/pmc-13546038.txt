The Reported Therapeutic Index of Compound 11 is Not Robust to Threshold Uncertainty [Letter]
Authors: Xiaoxin Zhang
Published: 2026

Dear editor

Nejedlý et al evaluated N-alkylated monoterpene indole alkaloid derivatives as chemosensitizers in P-glycoprotein-mediated multidrug resistance. Their findings across several experimental models support compound 11 as a promising candidate. However, its reported “therapeutic index greater than 1000” in MCF-7R cells depends on a threshold-sensitive denominator. Table 4 of the target article defines this denominator as the lowest tested concentration that “significantly (Fold Value ≥3)” increased doxorubicin efficiency.1 No inferential test against the fold threshold of 3 is reported. Accounting for uncertainty in the three-replicate summaries changes whether 0.1 μM qualifies. It therefore changes the reported ratio by one order of magnitude.

Table 3 of the target article reports a fold value of 3.8 ± 0.4 for compound 11 at 0.1 μM in MCF-7R cells. The result is expressed as mean ± standard error from three replicates.1 If those replicates are independent experimental units, an approximate one-sided test can assess whether the mean fold value exceeds 3. The calculation gives t=2.00 with 2 degrees of freedom and P=0.0918; the corresponding two-sided 95% confidence interval is 2.08–5.52. At 1 μM, the reported fold value is 9.4 ± 0.9. This gives t=7.11, one-sided P=0.0096 and a 95% confidence interval of 5.53–13.27. Under this explicit threshold test, the lowest supported concentration changes from 0.1 to 1 μM. Applying the formula in Table 4 of the target article then changes the MCF-7R ratio from greater than 1000 to greater than 100. The latter is not a uniquely corrected value. Rather, the sensitivity analysis shows that the greater-than-1000 classification is not robust to the uncertainty reported by the study. These calculations are approximate because they use rounded published summaries. They also require three independent experiments. Technical replicates would not provide three independent units for inference.2

An internal discrepancy further limits reproduction of the concentration-selection sequence. The Results state that 0.1 μM compound 11 increased doxorubicin sensitivity 2.8-fold in A2780DOX cells. Table 3 of the target article instead reports 2.1 ± 0.3.1 The table value is below 3 even as a point estimate. Table 4 of the target article assigns the A2780DOX ratio as greater than 100, so this discrepancy does not determine the MCF-7R result. Nevertheless, the correct source value should be identified before the threshold procedure is used to rank compounds.

The threshold-derived ratio also differs from an exposure-linked therapeutic index. One established preclinical approach relates efficacy and toxicity through dose or systemic exposure within a coherent species and dosing framework. Yang et al, for example, projected efficacious and maximum tolerated doses in the same preclinical species. Their analysis incorporated pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic uncertainty.3 By contrast, Table 4 of the target article combines cytotoxicity in a non-tumour renal cell line with a discrete screening concentration selected in a resistant cancer cell line. It contains no in vivo dose, exposure or toxicity anchor. The resulting quantity is informative as an in vitro chemosensitization window, but neither greater than 1000 nor greater than 100 estimates clinical safety. If the fold threshold was intended only as a screening heuristic, the ratio should be labelled accordingly rather than used to support “strong potential for safe use”.

The current data permit a bounded correction. The authors could first identify the experimental unit and reconcile the A2780DOX prose and table values. They could then provide replicate-level fold estimates or fitted concentration-response results with uncertainty. Table 4 of the target article, the Results and the Conclusion could report cell-line-specific in vitro chemosensitization windows. These changes would preserve the evidence supporting compound 11 while preventing a threshold-sensitive screening ratio from driving an overprecise candidate ranking. The published summaries do not establish that greater than 100 is definitive. They show instead that the reported therapeutic index greater than 1000 is insufficiently robust.

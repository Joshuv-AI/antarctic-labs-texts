Global Notation and Explicit Parameter Bounds

For any compact parameter set
K\subset\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
we write

\mu_{\min}=\inf_{(\mu,\sigma)\in K}\mu,\quad\mu_{\max}=\sup_{(\mu,\sigma)\in K}\mu,\quad\sigma_{\min}=\inf_{(\mu,\sigma)\in K}\sigma,\quad\sigma_{\max}=\sup_{(\mu,\sigma)\in K}\sigma.

(2.1)

Note
\sigma_{\min}>0
on such
K
. For the mixture sieve
S_{m,\varepsilon}
(defined below) we denote

m\geq 1,\qquad\sigma_{\min}=e^{-m},\ \sigma_{\max}=e^{m},\qquad\pi_{\min}=\varepsilon\in(0,1/k].

(2.2)

All constants in the sequel are displayed explicitly as functions of
(m,\varepsilon,k)
for mixtures and of
(\mu_{\max},|\mu_{\min}|,\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max})
for folded normal.

Definition 2.1

(Penalized Maximum Likelihood Estimator (PMLE))
.

A penalized maximum likelihood estimator (PMLE) is an estimator that maximizes the penalized likelihood function, typically of the form
\ell(\theta)-\lambda_{n}g(\theta)
, where
\ell(\theta)
is the log-likelihood,
g(\theta)
is a penalty function, and
\lambda_{n}
is a tuning parameter.

Definition 2.2

(Hausdorff distance)
.

For nonempty compact
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

d_{H}(A,B)=\max\{\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\|a-b\|,\ \sup_{b\in B}\inf_{a\in A}\|a-b\|\}.

3
Folded Normal: Main Theorems

Let
X\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
,
Y=|X|
. For
y\geq 0
the density equals

\displaystyle f(y;\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\{\phi\!\left(\frac{y-\mu}{\sigma}\right)+\phi\!\left(\frac{y+\mu}{\sigma}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{y^{2}+\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\,2\cosh\!\left(\frac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\right).

(3.1)

For data
y_{1:n}
define
S_{y}=\sum_{i}y_{i}^{2}
,
\bar{y}=n^{-1}\sum_{i}y_{i}
and
\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=\sum_{i}\log f(y_{i};\mu,\sigma)
.

Lemma 3.1

(Elementary
\cosh
bounds)
.

For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\log(2\cosh t)\leq|t|+\log 2,\qquad\log(2\cosh t)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\log 2,\qquad 0<\sech^{2}t\leq 1.

(3.2)

Proof.

e^{|t|}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(e^{t}+e^{-t})=\cosh t
implies
\log(2\cosh t)\leq|t|+\log 2
.
Series
\cosh t=\sum_{m\geq 0}t^{2m}/(2m)!
and
(2m)!\geq 2^{m}m!
yield
\cosh t\leq e^{t^{2}/2}
, giving the second inequality. The last inequality is immediate since
\cosh t\geq 1
.
∎

Proposition 3.2

(Degenerate samples)
.

If
y_{i}\equiv 0
or
y_{i}\equiv c>0
, then
\sup_{\mu,\sigma}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=+\infty
.

Proof.

If
y_{i}\equiv 0
, set
\mu=0
; then
\ell(0;0,\sigma)=-\log\sigma+\log 2-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)\to\infty
as
\sigma\downarrow 0
. If
y_{i}\equiv c>0
, take
\mu=c
; then
\ell(c;c,\sigma)=-\log\sigma+\log\{\phi(0)+\phi(2c/\sigma)\}-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)=-\log\sigma+O(1)\to\infty
as
\sigma\downarrow 0
.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Existence and uniqueness up to sign)
.

Assume the data are nonconstant (
\exists\,i\neq j
with
y_{i}\neq y_{j}
) and put
S_{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}
.

(i)

For each fixed
\sigma>0
, the function
\mu\mapsto\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)
has a unique maximizer on
\mu\geq 0
given by

\hat{\mu}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}0,&\sigma^{2}\geq S_{y}/n,\\[1.50696pt]
\text{the unique solution }\mu>0\text{ of }\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\tanh(y_i\mu/\sigma^2)=n\mu,&\sigma^{2}<S_{y}/n.\end{cases}

(ii)

The profile log-likelihood
\ell_{n,p}(\sigma):=\ell_{n}(\hat{\mu}(\sigma),\sigma)
has a unique maximizer
\hat{\sigma}>0
. Consequently every MLE is
(\hat{\mu},\hat{\sigma})
with
\hat{\mu}=\pm\hat{\mu}(\hat{\sigma})
.

Proof.

Notation.

Set

k(\sigma,\mu):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)-n\mu,\qquad A(\sigma,\mu):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}\sech^{2}\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big).

Then

\partial_{\mu}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}k(\sigma,\mu),\qquad\partial_{\mu\mu}^{2}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(\frac{A(\sigma,\mu)}{\sigma^{2}}-n\Big)\quad\text{by }\eqref{eq:secder-mu}.

(i) Fixed
\sigma
.

Define
g_{\sigma}(\mu):=\partial_{\mu}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=\sigma^{-2}k(\sigma,\mu)
.
Then
g_{\sigma}(0)=0
and
g^{\prime}_{\sigma}(0)=\sigma^{-2}(S_{y}/\sigma^{2}-n)
.

•

If
\sigma^{2}\geq S_{y}/n
, then
g^{\prime}_{\sigma}(0)\leq 0
and
g^{\prime}_{\sigma}(\mu)\leq 0
for all
\mu\geq 0
because
0<\sech(x)^{2}\leq 1
.
Thus
g_{\sigma}
is nonincreasing on
[0,\infty)
with
g_{\sigma}(0)=0
, so the unique maximizer on
\mu\geq 0
is
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)=0
.

•

If
\sigma^{2}<S_{y}/n
, then
g^{\prime}_{\sigma}(0)>0
.
Moreover

g^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma}(\mu)=-\frac{2}{\sigma^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{3}\sech^{2}\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)<0\quad(\mu>0),

so
g_{\sigma}
increases at
0
and then strictly decreases to
\lim_{\mu\to\infty}\sigma^{-2}(-n\mu+\sum_{i}y_{i})=-\infty
.
Hence
g_{\sigma}
has a unique positive zero, denoted
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
.
At
\mu=\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
,
\partial_{\mu\mu}^{2}\ell_{n}(\hat{\mu}(\sigma),\sigma)<0
because

A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))<n\sigma^{2}\quad\text{by }\eqref{eq:A2-key-ineq},

so the zero is the unique maximizer on
\mu\geq 0
.

By
Lemma

A.2
, this optimizer is
C^{1}
in
\sigma
and

\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\sigma)=-\frac{\frac{2\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}{\sigma}\frac{A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}{\sigma^{2}}}{n-\frac{A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}{\sigma^{2}}}<0\quad\text{(\eqref{eq:A2-muprime})}.

Since
\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)
is even in
\mu
, the global maximizers are
\pm\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
.

(ii) Profile in
\sigma
.

By
Lemma

A.1
,
\ell_{n,p}(\sigma)=\sup_{\mu}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)\to-\infty
both as
\sigma\downarrow 0
and
\sigma\to\infty
(see (
A.1
)); hence a maximizer
\hat{\sigma}>0
exists.
Differentiate the profile using the chain rule and that
\partial_{\mu}\ell_{n}(\hat{\mu}(\sigma),\sigma)=0
:

\ell_{n,p}^{\prime}(\sigma)=\partial_{\sigma}\ell_{n}(\hat{\mu}(\sigma),\sigma)=-\frac{n}{\sigma}+\frac{S_{y}+n\hat{\mu}(\sigma)^{2}}{\sigma^{3}}-\frac{2\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}{\sigma^{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}}\Big).

Using
k(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))=0
(first-order condition) this simplifies to

\ell_{n,p}^{\prime}(\sigma)=-\frac{n}{\sigma}+\frac{S_{y}-n\hat{\mu}(\sigma)^{2}}{\sigma^{3}}=\frac{N_{n}(\sigma)}{\sigma^{3}},\quad N_{n}(\sigma):=S_{y}-n\sigma^{2}-n\hat{\mu}(\sigma)^{2}.

By
Theorem

A.3
,
N_{n}
has
exactly one
zero and is decreasing through it, so the profile has a unique maximizer
\hat{\sigma}
.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(Consistency and asymptotic normality)
.

Let the true parameter be
\theta_{0}=(\mu_{0},\sigma_{0})
and let

\Theta_{0}=\{(\pm\mu_{0},\sigma_{0})\}
denote the folded–normal identification set. Then

(\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\sigma}_{n})\xrightarrow{p}(\pm\mu_{0},\sigma_{0}),\qquad d_{H}\big(\{(\pm\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\sigma}_{n})\},\Theta_{0}\big)\xrightarrow{p}0.

If
\mu_{0}\neq 0
, then

\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})\xrightarrow{d}N\!\big(0,I(\theta_{0})^{-1}\big),

where
I(\theta_{0})
is the Fisher information matrix.
If
\mu_{0}=0
, then
\hat{\mu}_{n}=O_{p}(n^{-1/4})
(non-Gaussian limit) and
\hat{\sigma}_{n}-\sigma_{0}=O_{p}(n^{-1/2})
; consequently
d_{H}=O_{p}(n^{-1/4})
.

Proof.

Step 1 (localization to a compact with high probability).

By boundary coercivity
Lemma

A.1
, uniformly in
\mu
,

\sup_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)\leq-n\log\sigma-\frac{ns^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+C_{0}\quad\text{for }\sigma\in(0,1]\quad\text{(\eqref{eq:A1-bound})},

and as
\sigma\to\infty
the term
-n\log\sigma
drives the profile to
-\infty
.
Hence for each sample with
s^{2}>0
(which holds a.s. under a continuous law) there exists a finite interval

[a_{n},b_{n}]\subset(0,\infty)
such that any maximizer
\hat{\sigma}_{n}\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
; consequently
(\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\sigma}_{n})
lies in some random compact set.
Since the law is fixed, we can choose a deterministic large compact
K\Subset\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
so that

\Pr((\hat\mu_n,\hat\sigma_n)\in K)\to 1
.

Step 2 (ULLN on
K
).

On this fixed
K
,
Lemma

A.4
yields deterministic control:

\|\nabla_{\theta}\ell(Y;\theta)\|\leq C_{2}(1+|Y|+Y^{2})
and

|\ell(Y;\theta)|\leq C_{1}+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\min}^{2}}(1+Y^{2})+\frac{M_{\mu}}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}}|Y|
.
With
Corollary

A.5
, define
L_{K},m_{1},m_{2},D_{K}
.
Applying the high-probability ULLN
Proposition

A.7
gives, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\Pr\!\Big(\sup_{\theta\in K}|\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell(\theta)|>\varepsilon\Big)\ \leq\ \frac{m_{2}-m_{1}^{2}}{n\,m_{1}^{2}}+\frac{9\,M_{\delta}\,D_{K}}{n\,\varepsilon^{2}}\quad\text{(\eqref{eq:A5-HP-nocov})}.

In particular
\sup_{\theta\in K}|n^{-1}\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell(\theta)|\to 0
almost surely.
(For reference, the proof uses the slope control event
\{S_{n}\leq 2m_{1}\}
in (
A.10
) and the finite-net event in (
A.11
).)

Step 3 (population separation and consistency).

By
Proposition

A.8
, for any
\delta>0
there is
\eta(\delta)>0
such that

\sup_{\theta\in K:\ \dist(\theta,\Theta_{0})\geq\delta}\ \ell(\theta)\ \leq\ \ell(\theta_{0})-\eta(\delta).

Fix
\delta>0
and choose
n
so large that

\sup_{K}|n^{-1}\ell_{n}-\ell|\leq\eta(\delta)/4
holds with probability at least
1-\alpha
.
On that event,

\displaystyle\sup_{\dist(\theta,\Theta_{0})\geq\delta}\frac{1}{n}\ell_{n}(\theta)

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \sup_{\dist(\theta,\Theta_{0})\geq\delta}\ell(\theta)+\eta/4\ \leq\ \ell(\theta_{0})-\frac{3\eta}{4},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\ \geq\ \ell(\theta_{0})-\eta/4.

Thus no maximizer of
\ell_{n}
can lie outside the
\delta
-neighborhood of
\Theta_{0}
.
Since
\alpha
is arbitrary,
d_{H}(\{(\pm\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\sigma}_{n})\},\Theta_{0})\xrightarrow{p}0
, which implies
(\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\sigma}_{n})\xrightarrow{p}(\pm\mu_{0},\sigma_{0})
.

Step 4 (asymptotic normality when
\mu_{0}\neq 0
).

Let
S(Y;\theta)=\nabla_{\theta}\ell(Y;\theta)
and
H(Y;\theta)=\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\ell(Y;\theta)
.
A Taylor expansion of the score at
\theta_{0}
around
\hat{\theta}_{n}
gives

\mathbf{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}S(Y_{i};\theta_{0})+\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(Y_{i};\tilde{\theta}_{n})\Big)\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0}),

for some
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
between
\hat{\theta}_{n}
and
\theta_{0}
.
By
Lemma

A.9
,
I(\theta_{0})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[SS^{\top}]=-\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[H]
exists, is continuous, and is positive definite when
\mu_{0}\neq 0
.
By the CLT for the score (mean zero by the information identity in
Lemma

A.9
) and the ULLN for the Hessian from
Proposition

A.7
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}S(Y_{i};\theta_{0})\ \xrightarrow{d}\ N(0,I(\theta_{0})),\qquad\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(Y_{i};\tilde{\theta}_{n})\ \xrightarrow{p}\ -I(\theta_{0}).

Slutsky then yields

\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{0})\xrightarrow{d}N(0,I(\theta_{0})^{-1}).

Step 5 (kink at
\mu_{0}=0
;
n^{1/4}
-rate).

At
(0,\sigma_{0})
the information is singular in the
\mu
-direction by
Lemma

A.9
.
Keeping
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
fixed,
Remark

A.10
expands the log-likelihood contrast as

\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma_{0})-\ell_{n}(0,\sigma_{0})=\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma_{0}^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2})-\frac{\mu^{4}}{12\sigma_{0}^{8}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{4}+R_{n}(\mu),

with the uniform remainder control on the
n^{-1/4}
-window given via (
A.13
)–(
A.15
).
Let
\mu=n^{-1/4}t
and write the rescaled contrast
\Phi_{n}(t)
.
By
Lemma

A.11
,
\Phi_{n}
converges (uniformly on compacts) to

\Psi_{Z}(t)=aZ\,t^{2}-bt^{4}
with
a=1/(2\sigma_{0}^{4})
and

;
the argmax satisfies

n^{1/4}\hat{\mu}_{n}\ \xrightarrow{d}\ T^{\star},\qquad|T^{\star}|=\sqrt{\frac{(aZ)_{+}}{2b}}=\sqrt{\frac{3\,\sigma_{0}^{4}}{\mathbb{E}[Y^{4}]}\,(Z)_{+}}.

Meanwhile the
\sigma
-coordinate remains regular and
\hat{\sigma}_{n}-\sigma_{0}=O_{p}(n^{-1/2})
by the standard one-dimensional M-estimation step on

.
Therefore the Hausdorff distance

d_{H}\big(\{(\pm\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\sigma}_{n})\},\Theta_{0}\big)=\sqrt{\hat{\mu}_{n}^{\,2}+(\hat{\sigma}_{n}-\sigma_{0})^{2}}=O_{p}(n^{-1/4}).

∎

Theorem 4.1

(Hausdorff consistency on a fixed sieve)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and let the true parameter
\theta_{0}=(\pi^{0},\mu^{0},\sigma^{0})
have strictly
positive weights with pairwise distinct component parameters. Fix a compact sieve

S_{m,\varepsilon}
as in (
B.4
) with
m\geq 1
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1/k]
.
Let
\widehat{\Theta}_{n,m,\varepsilon}\subset\arg\max_{\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}}\ell_{n}(\theta)

be the (possibly set-valued) MLE orbit set and let
\Theta_{0}
be the permutation orbit of
\theta_{0}
.
Assume
\mathbb{E}[X^{4}]<\infty
(automatic for Gaussian mixtures). Then

d_{H}\big(\widehat{\Theta}_{n,m,\varepsilon},\Theta_{0}\big)\ \xrightarrow{\text{\rm a.s.}}\ 0.

Proof.

Existence on the sieve.

S_{m,\varepsilon}
is compact by (
B.4
);

\ell_{n}
is continuous in
\theta
, so maximizers exist by Proposition
B.7
.

Uniform LLN.
The log-envelope (
B.11

Appendix A
Appendix A:Folded Normal

A.1
Boundary coercivity with explicit constants

Lemma A.1

(Boundary behavior)
.

Let
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\geq 0
and write

\bar{y}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i},\qquad s^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-\bar{y})^{2}.

For the folded-normal log-likelihood

\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\{-\log\sigma-\frac{y_{i}^{2}+\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\log\Big(2\cosh\frac{y_i\mu}{\sigma^2}\Big)-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)\Big\},

define the
profile

\ell_{n,p}(\sigma)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)
. If
s^{2}>0
, then

\lim_{\sigma\downarrow 0}\ell_{n,p}(\sigma)=\lim_{\sigma\to\infty}\ell_{n,p}(\sigma)=-\infty.

Moreover, for every
\sigma\in(0,1]
,

\sup_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)\ \leq\ -\,n\log\sigma\ -\ \frac{ns^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\ +\ C_{0},\qquad C_{0}:=n\log 2-\frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi).

(A.1)

Proof.

Step 1 (pointwise upper bound in
\mu
).

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\log(2\cosh t)\leq|t|+\log 2
(elementary, since
\cosh t\leq e^{|t|}/2
). Apply this with
t=y_{i}\mu/\sigma^{2}
to get

\displaystyle\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle=-n\log\sigma-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}^{2}+\mu^{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\!\Big(2\cosh\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)-\frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi)

\displaystyle\leq-n\log\sigma-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}^{2}+\mu^{2})+\frac{|\mu|}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}+n\log 2-\frac{n}{2}\log(2\pi).

Let
S_{y}=\sum_{i}y_{i}^{2}
and note
\sum_{i}y_{i}=n\bar{y}
. Then

\displaystyle\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq-n\log\sigma-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big(S_{y}+n\mu^{2}-2n|\mu|\bar{y}\Big)+C_{0}

\displaystyle=-n\log\sigma-\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big[\mu^{2}-2|\mu|\bar{y}+\frac{S_{y}}{n}\Big]+C_{0}.

(A.1)

Step 2 (express
S_{y}/n
via
\bar{y}
and
s^{2}
and complete the square).

Since

\frac{S_{y}}{n}=\bar{y}^{2}+s^{2}

(by the definition of the sample variance),
the bracket in (
A.1
) becomes

\mu^{2}-2|\mu|\bar{y}+\bar{y}^{2}+s^{2}=\big(|\mu|-\bar{y}\big)^{2}+s^{2}.

Therefore, for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,

\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)\leq-n\log\sigma-\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big[\big(|\mu|-\bar{y}\big)^{2}+s^{2}\Big]+C_{0}.

(A.2)

Step 3 (take supremum in
\mu
).

At fixed
\sigma
, the right-hand side of (
A.2
) is maximized when the square vanishes, i.e. when
|\mu|=\bar{y}
(choose
\mu=\pm\bar{y}
). Hence

\sup_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)\leq-n\log\sigma-\frac{ns^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+C_{0},

which is exactly (
A.1
).

Step 4 (profile limits at the boundaries).

If
s^{2}>0
, then as
\sigma\downarrow 0
the term
-(ns^{2})/(2\sigma^{2})\to-\infty
, so (
A.1
) implies

\ell_{n,p}(\sigma)=\sup_{\mu}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)\to-\infty
.
As
\sigma\to\infty
, the bound gives

\sup_{\mu}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)\leq-n\log\sigma+C_{0}+o(1)\to-\infty
,
hence also
\ell_{n,p}(\sigma)\to-\infty
.
∎

A.2
Implicit function and one-crossing

Set

k(\sigma,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)-n\mu,\qquad A(\sigma,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}\,\sech^{2}\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big).

Note that, for fixed
\sigma>0
, the
\mu
–score of the log-likelihood is

\partial_{\mu}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big[-n\mu+\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\tanh\!\big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\big)\Big]=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}k(\sigma,\mu),

and the second derivative in
\mu
equals

\partial_{\mu\mu}^{2}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big[-n+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}\sech^{2}\!\big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\big)\Big]=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(\frac{A(\sigma,\mu)}{\sigma^{2}}-n\Big).

(A.3)

In particular, at any
maximizer

\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
of
\mu\mapsto\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)
one has

\partial_{\mu\mu}^{2}\ell_{n}(\hat{\mu}(\sigma),\sigma)<0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}{\sigma^{2}}-n<0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))<n\sigma^{2}.

(A.4)

Lemma A.2

(Implicit derivative)
.

Fix
\sigma>0
with
\sigma^{2}<S_{y}/n
, where
S_{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}
. Then the (unique) maximizer

\hat{\mu}(\sigma)>0
of
\mu\mapsto\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)
satisfies
k(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))=0
, depends
C^{1}
on
\sigma
, and

\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\sigma)\;=\;-\frac{k_{\sigma}(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}{k_{\mu}(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}\;=\;-\frac{\frac{2\,\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}{\sigma}\,\frac{A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}{\sigma^{2}}}{n-\frac{A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}{\sigma^{2}}}\;<\;0.

(A.5)

Proof.

(i) Existence and uniqueness of
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)>0
.

For fixed
\sigma
, the
\mu
–score is

g_{\sigma}(\mu):=\partial_{\mu}\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big[-n\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)\Big].

Then
g_{\sigma}(0)=0
and

g_{\sigma}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big[-n+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}\Big]=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big(\frac{S_{y}}{\sigma^{2}}-n\Big)>0\quad(\text{since }\sigma^{2}<S_{y}/n).

Moreover,

g_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\mu)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\Big[-n+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}\sech^{2}\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)\Big]

is strictly
decreasing
for
\mu>0
because

g_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}(\mu)=-\frac{2}{\sigma^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{3}\sech^{2}\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)<0\qquad(\text{if some }y_{i}>0).

Also
\lim_{\mu\to\infty}g_{\sigma}(\mu)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}(-n\mu+\sum_{i}y_{i})=-\infty
. Hence
g_{\sigma}
rises from
0
with positive slope, then strictly decreases to
-\infty
, so it has a
unique
positive zero, denoted
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
; concavity (
A.3
) shows this zero is the unique maximizer of
\mu\mapsto\ell_{n}(\mu,\sigma)
on
[0,\infty)
.

(ii) Smooth dependence on
\sigma
.

Define
k(\sigma,\mu)=\sigma^{2}g_{\sigma}(\mu)=\sum_{i}y_{i}\tanh(y_i\mu/\sigma^2)-n\mu
. Then
k
is
C^{1}
on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
, and

k_{\mu}(\sigma,\mu)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{2}\sech^{2}\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)-n=\frac{A(\sigma,\mu)}{\sigma^{2}}-n.

At
\mu=\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
, (
A.4
) gives
k_{\mu}(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))<0
. By the implicit function theorem, there exists a
C^{1}
function
\mu=\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
solving
k(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))=0
in a neighborhood of the given
\sigma
.

(iii) Derivative formula and sign.

Compute

k_{\sigma}(\sigma,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\,\sech^{2}\!\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)\cdot\frac{d}{d\sigma}\Big(\frac{y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\,\sech^{2}(\cdot)\cdot\Big(-\frac{2y_{i}\mu}{\sigma^{3}}\Big)=-\frac{2\mu}{\sigma^{3}}\,A(\sigma,\mu).

Thus, at
\mu=\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
,

\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\sigma)=-\frac{k_{\sigma}}{k_{\mu}}=\frac{2\hat{\mu}(\sigma)\,A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))/\sigma^{3}}{A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))/\sigma^{2}-n}=-\,\frac{\frac{2\,\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}{\sigma}\,\frac{A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}{\sigma^{2}}}{n-\frac{A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))}{\sigma^{2}}}.

By (
A.4
), the denominator
n-A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))/\sigma^{2}
is
positive
; the numerator is positive since
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)>0
and
A>0
. Hence
\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\sigma)<0
, proving (
A.5
).
∎

Theorem A.3

(Unique zero of
N_{n}
and unique maximizer of the profile)
.

Let

N_{n}(\sigma)=S_{y}-n\sigma^{2}-n\hat{\mu}(\sigma)^{2},\qquad\ell_{n,p}(\sigma)=\ell_{n}(\hat{\mu}(\sigma),\sigma).

Assume the sample is non-degenerate (
s^{2}>0
). Then
N_{n}
has
exactly one
zero on
(0,\infty)
; consequently,
\ell_{n,p}
has a unique maximizer
\hat{\sigma}>0
.

Proof.

Step 1 (existence of a zero).

By Lemma
A.1
,
\ell_{n,p}(\sigma)\to-\infty
as
\sigma\downarrow 0
and as
\sigma\to\infty
. Since
\ell_{n,p}
is continuous, it attains a global maximum at some
\hat{\sigma}>0
. Differentiating the profile using Lemma
A.2
(chain rule with the
\mu
–score equal to
0
) gives

\ell_{n,p}^{\prime}(\sigma)=\partial_{\sigma}\ell_{n}(\hat{\mu}(\sigma),\sigma)=-\frac{n}{\sigma}+\frac{S_{y}-n\hat{\mu}(\sigma)^{2}}{\sigma^{3}}=\frac{N_{n}(\sigma)}{\sigma^{3}}.

Hence
\hat{\sigma}
satisfies
N_{n}(\hat{\sigma})=0
; a zero exists.

Step 2 (sign of the derivative of
N_{n}
at any zero).

Differentiate
N_{n}
:

N_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma)=-2n\Big[\sigma+\hat{\mu}(\sigma)\,\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\sigma)\Big].

Using the formula from Lemma
A.2
,

\sigma+\hat{\mu}\,\hat{\mu}^{\prime}=\sigma-\frac{\frac{2\hat{\mu}^{2}}{\sigma}\,\frac{A}{\sigma^{2}}}{n-\frac{A}{\sigma^{2}}}=\frac{n\sigma^{4}-\sigma^{2}A+2\hat{\mu}^{2}A}{\sigma\big(n\sigma^{2}-A\big)},

where
A=A(\sigma,\hat{\mu}(\sigma))
and we used
A<n\sigma^{2}
from (
A.4
), so the denominator is positive.

Let
\sigma_{0}
be any zero of
N_{n}
(i.e.
S_{y}=n\sigma_{0}^{2}+n\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})^{2}
). Since
\sech^{2}(\cdot)\leq 1
, one has
A(\sigma_{0})\leq S_{y}
. Therefore the
numerator
at
\sigma_{0}
obeys

n\sigma_{0}^{4}-\sigma_{0}^{2}A(\sigma_{0})+2\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})^{2}A(\sigma_{0})\ \geq\ n\sigma_{0}^{4}-\sigma_{0}^{2}\big(n\sigma_{0}^{2}+n\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})^{2}\big)+2\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})^{2}\big(n\sigma_{0}^{2}+n\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})^{2}\big)=n\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})^{2}\big(\sigma_{0}^{2}+2\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})^{2}\big)>0.

Hence
\sigma_{0}+\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\sigma_{0})>0
and thus

N_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma_{0})=-2n\big[\sigma_{0}+\hat{\mu}(\sigma_{0})\hat{\mu}^{\prime}(\sigma_{0})\big]\;<\;0.

At every zero, the derivative of
N_{n}
is strictly negative: zeros are simple and correspond to
downward
crossings.

Step 3 (uniqueness of the zero).

By Step 1 there exists at least one zero; by Lemma
A.1
and continuity,
N_{n}(\sigma)>0
for all sufficiently small
\sigma
and
N_{n}(\sigma)<0
for all sufficiently large
\sigma
. If there were two distinct zeros, Rolle’s theorem would give a point where
N_{n}^{\prime}=0
between them. But Step 2 shows
N_{n}^{\prime}
is
strictly negative at any zero
, which rules out an upward re-crossing. Hence there can be only one zero.

Step 4 (uniqueness of the maximizer of
\ell_{n,p}
).

Since
\ell_{n,p}^{\prime}(\sigma)=N_{n}(\sigma)/\sigma^{3}
and
\ell_{n,p}\to-\infty
at both ends, the unique zero of
N_{n}
corresponds to the unique stationary point of
\ell_{n,p}
, which must be its global maximum.
∎

A.3
Uniform LLN with explicit envelopes

Lemma A.4

(Explicit envelopes on a compact parameter set)
.

Fix a compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
for the folded–normal model.
Write

\sigma_{\min}:=\inf_{(\mu,\sigma)\in K}\sigma>0,\qquad\sigma_{\max}:=\sup_{(\mu,\sigma)\in K}\sigma,\qquad M_{\mu}:=\sup_{(\mu,\sigma)\in K}|\mu|<\infty.

For all
y\geq 0
and all
\theta=(\mu,\sigma)\in K
the pointwise log-likelihood

\ell(y;\theta)=-\log\sigma-\frac{y^{2}+\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\log\!\Big(2\cosh\frac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)

satisfies the envelopes

\displaystyle|\ell(y;\theta)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}+\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma_{\min}^{2}}+\frac{M_{\mu}}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}}\,y,\qquad C_{1}:=\big|\log\sigma_{\min}\big|+\big|\log\sigma_{\max}\big|+\log 2+\tfrac{1}{2}\big|\log(2\pi)\big|+\frac{M_{\mu}^{2}}{2\sigma_{\min}^{2}},

(A.6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\theta}\ell(y;\theta)\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\,(1+y+y^{2}),\qquad C_{2}:=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}}+\frac{2M_{\mu}}{\sigma_{\min}^{3}}+\frac{2M_{\mu}^{2}}{\sigma_{\min}^{4}}+1,

(A.7)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}_{\theta}\ell(y;\theta)\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\,(1+y^{2}+y^{4}),\qquad C_{3}:=\frac{3}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}}+\frac{6M_{\mu}}{\sigma_{\min}^{3}}+\frac{6M_{\mu}^{2}}{\sigma_{\min}^{4}}+\frac{2}{\sigma_{\min}^{6}}.

(A.8)

In particular, if
\mathbb{E}[Y^{4}]<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}\sup_{\theta\in K}|\ell(Y;\theta)|<\infty
,

\mathbb{E}\sup_{\theta\in K}\|\nabla_{\theta}\ell(Y;\theta)\|<\infty
and

\mathbb{E}\sup_{\theta\in K}\|\nabla^{2}_{\theta}\ell(Y;\theta)\|<\infty
.

Proof.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
we use

\log(2\cosh t)\leq|t|+\log 2
,
|\tanh t|\leq 1
,
0<\sech^{2}t\leq 1
.
On
K
one has
|\mu|\leq M_{\mu}
and
\sigma\in[\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max}]
.

(0) Function envelope.

From the displayed form of
\ell
and
\log(2\cosh(\cdot))\leq|t|+\log 2
with

t=y\mu/\sigma^{2}
,

|\ell(y;\theta)|\leq|\log\sigma|+\frac{y^{2}+\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\frac{|y||\mu|}{\sigma^{2}}+\log 2+\tfrac{1}{2}|\log(2\pi)|\leq C_{1}+\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma_{\min}^{2}}+\frac{M_{\mu}}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}}y.

(1) Score envelope.

Compute

\partial_{\mu}\ell=-\frac{\mu}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{y}{\sigma^{2}}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big),\qquad\partial_{\sigma}\ell=-\frac{1}{\sigma}+\frac{y^{2}+\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{3}}-\frac{2y\mu}{\sigma^{3}}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big).

Then

|\partial_{\mu}\ell|\leq\frac{M_{\mu}}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}}+\frac{y}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}},\qquad|\partial_{\sigma}\ell|\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}}+\frac{M_{\mu}^{2}}{\sigma_{\min}^{3}}+\frac{2M_{\mu}}{\sigma_{\min}^{3}}y+\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}^{3}}y^{2}.

Bounding the Euclidean norm by the sum of absolute values gives (
A.7
).

(2) Hessian envelope.

Differentiate again:

\partial^{2}_{\mu\mu}\ell=-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}\sech^{2}\!\Big(\frac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big),\quad\partial^{2}_{\mu\sigma}\ell=\frac{2\mu}{\sigma^{3}}-\frac{2y}{\sigma^{3}}\tanh(\cdot)-\frac{2y^{2}\mu}{\sigma^{5}}\sech^{2}(\cdot),

\partial^{2}_{\sigma\sigma}\ell=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{3(y^{2}+\mu^{2})}{\sigma^{4}}+\frac{6y\mu}{\sigma^{4}}\tanh(\cdot)+\frac{4y^{2}\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{6}}\sech^{2}(\cdot).

Use
|\tanh|\leq 1
,
\sech^{2}\leq 1
,
|\mu|\leq M_{\mu}
,
\sigma\geq\sigma_{\min}
to bound each entry by a polynomial in
y
with coefficients dominated by the stated
C_{3}
; sum absolute values to obtain (
A.8
).
∎

Corollary A.5

(Concrete envelopes for Proposition
A.7
)
.

With
C_{2}
as in (
A.7
), define

Lemma A.6

(Gaussian-mixture envelopes on a compact sieve)
.

Let

be the sieve with

Proposition A.7

(ULLN on compacts — explicit high-probability bound, without covering numbers)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
be compact and let
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
be i.i.d. with
\mathbb{E}[Y^{4}]<\infty
.
For
\ell_{n}(\theta)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(Y_{i};\theta)
and
\ell(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[\ell(Y;\theta)]

(with
\ell
the folded-normal pointwise log-likelihood), the following holds.

For any
\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1)
there exist:

•

constants
m_{1},m_{2},D_{K}<\infty
depending only on
K
and the law of
Y
,

•

a mesh size
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,K):=\varepsilon/(6m_{1})
,

•

and a
finite
set
T_{\delta}\subset K
(a
\delta
–net of
K
; existence by compactness) with cardinality
M_{\delta}:=|T_{\delta}|<\infty
,

such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{\theta\in K}\big|\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell(\theta)\big|>\varepsilon\right)\ \leq\ \frac{m_{2}-m_{1}^{2}}{n\,m_{1}^{2}}\;+\;\frac{9\,M_{\delta}\,D_{K}}{n\,\varepsilon^{2}}.

(A.9)

In particular,
\sup_{\theta\in K}\lvert\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell(\theta)\rvert\to 0
almost surely as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Step 0 (deterministic envelopes and finite moments).

By Lemma
A.4
there exists a polynomial envelope

L_{K}(y)=a_{0}+a_{1}|y|+a_{2}y^{2}
with

\|\nabla_{\theta}\ell(y;\theta)\|\leq L_{K}(y)
for all
\theta\in K
, and a constant

B_{K}
such that
|\ell(y;\theta)|\leq B_{K}(1+y^{2})
for all
\theta\in K
.
Because
\mathbb{E}[Y^{4}]<\infty
, set

m_{1}:=\mathbb{E}[L_{K}(Y)]<\infty,\qquad m_{2}:=\mathbb{E}[L_{K}(Y)^{2}]<\infty,\qquad D_{K}:=\sup_{\theta\in K}\mathbb{E}\big[\ell(Y;\theta)^{2}\big]\leq 2B_{K}^{2}\big(1+\mathbb{E}[Y^{4}]\big)<\infty.

Step 1 (random Lipschitz bound).

For any
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in K
and each
i
,

|\ell(Y_{i};\theta)-\ell(Y_{i};\theta^{\prime})|\leq L_{K}(Y_{i})\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|,

hence

|\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell_{n}(\theta^{\prime})|\leq S_{n}\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|,\qquad S_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{K}(Y_{i}).

Step 2 (probability control of the random slope
S_{n}
).

By Chebyshev,

\mathbb{P}\Big(|S_{n}-m_{1}|\geq m_{1}\Big)\leq\frac{\mathrm{Var}(L_{K}(Y))}{n\,m_{1}^{2}}\leq\frac{m_{2}-m_{1}^{2}}{n\,m_{1}^{2}}.

Thus, with probability at least
1-\frac{m_{2}-m_{1}^{2}}{nm_{1}^{2}}
,

S_{n}\leq 2m_{1}.

(A.10)

Step 3 (finite
\delta
–net; no counting function).

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and set

\delta:=\frac{\varepsilon}{6m_{1}}.

By compactness of
K
, there exists a
finite
set
T_{\delta}=\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{M_{\delta}}\}\subset K
such that
for every
\theta\in K
there is a
j\leq M_{\delta}
with
\|\theta-\theta_{j}\|\leq\delta
.
(We do not quantify
M_{\delta}
: any such finite set works.)

Step 4 (pointwise control on the finite set
T_{\delta}
).

For each fixed
\theta_{j}\in T_{\delta}
, Chebyshev gives

\mathbb{P}\Big(\big|\ell_{n}(\theta_{j})-\ell(\theta_{j})\big|>\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\Big)\leq\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\ell(Y;\theta_{j}))}{n\,(\varepsilon/3)^{2}}\leq\frac{9\,D_{K}}{n\,\varepsilon^{2}}.

By the (finite) union bound,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{1\leq j\leq M_{\delta}}\big|\ell_{n}(\theta_{j})-\ell(\theta_{j})\big|\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\Big)\ \geq\ 1-\frac{9\,M_{\delta}\,D_{K}}{n\,\varepsilon^{2}}.

(A.11)

Step 5 (transfer from
T_{\delta}
to all of
K
).

On the event (
A.10
) and for any
\theta\in K
pick
j
with
\|\theta-\theta_{j}\|\leq\delta
. Then

\big|\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell_{n}(\theta_{j})\big|\leq S_{n}\,\delta\leq 2m_{1}\delta=\varepsilon/3.

Taking expectations in the pointwise Lipschitz bound also gives

\big|\ell(\theta)-\ell(\theta_{j})\big|\leq\mathbb{E}[L_{K}(Y)]\,\delta=m_{1}\delta=\varepsilon/6.

Therefore, intersecting (
A.10
) with (
A.11
), we get simultaneously for all
\theta\in K
,

\big|\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell(\theta)\big|\leq\underbrace{\big|\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell_{n}(\theta_{j})\big|}_{\leq\varepsilon/3}+\underbrace{\big|\ell_{n}(\theta_{j})-\ell(\theta_{j})\big|}_{\leq\varepsilon/3}+\underbrace{\big|\ell(\theta_{j})-\ell(\theta)\big|}_{\leq\varepsilon/6}\leq\varepsilon.

Taking complements and combining the two error probabilities yields (
A.9
).

Step 6 (almost sure uniform convergence).

Because the RHS of (
A.9
) is
O(1/n)
and the series
\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{-1}
is finite when multiplied by a fixed constant, Borel–Cantelli implies

\sup_{\theta\in K}|\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell(\theta)|\to 0
almost surely.
∎

Proposition A.8

(KL separation)
.

Let
\Theta_{0}=\{(\pm\mu_{0},\sigma_{0})\}
be the folded-normal identification set.
Fix a compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
and
\delta>0
. Then there exists
\eta>0
such that

\sup_{\theta\in K:\ \dist(\theta,\Theta_{0})\geq\delta}\ \ell(\theta)\ \leq\ \ell(\theta_{0})-\eta,\qquad\ell(\theta):=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[\log f(Y;\theta)],\ \ \theta_{0}=(\mu_{0},\sigma_{0}).

Proof.

1) KL representation and equality case.

For any
\theta
,

\ell(\theta)-\ell(\theta_{0})=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}\!\left[\log\frac{f(Y;\theta)}{f(Y;\theta_{0})}\right]=-\,\mathrm{KL}(f_{\theta_{0}}\,\|\,f_{\theta})\leq 0,

with equality iff
f_{\theta}=f_{\theta_{0}}
a.e. (definition of KL).

2) If
f_{\theta}=f_{\theta_{0}}
, then
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
and
|\mu|=|\mu_{0}|
.

The folded-normal density for
y\geq 0
is

f(y;\mu,\sigma)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{y^{2}+\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big)\,2\cosh\!\Big(\tfrac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big).

Two invariants of the law are

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[Y^{2}]=\mu^{2}+\sigma^{2},\qquad f(0;\mu,\sigma)=\frac{2}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\Big).

(A.12)

Equality in distribution gives the system

\mu^{2}+\sigma^{2}=\mu_{0}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\log\sigma+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}=\log\sigma_{0}+\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{2\sigma_{0}^{2}}.

Let
a=\sigma^{2}
,
b=\mu^{2}
,
A_{0}=\mu_{0}^{2}+\sigma_{0}^{2}
, so
b=A_{0}-a\in[0,A_{0}]
. Define

\phi(a):=\log a+\frac{A_{0}-a}{a}=\log a+\frac{A_{0}}{a}-1.

On
(0,A_{0}]
,
\phi^{\prime}(a)=\frac{1}{a}-\frac{A_{0}}{a^{2}}=\frac{a-A_{0}}{a^{2}}<0
, hence
\phi
is strictly decreasing.
Equality
\phi(a)=\phi(a_{0})
with
a_{0}=\sigma_{0}^{2}
forces
a=a_{0}
and then
b=A_{0}-a=b_{0}=\mu_{0}^{2}
.
Thus
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
and
|\mu|=|\mu_{0}|
.

3) Continuity of
\ell
on
K
(dominated convergence with an explicit envelope).

Fix compact
K
and let
(\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max},M_{\mu})
be as in
Lemma

A.4
. For any
\theta\in K
,

\ell(y;\theta)=-\log\sigma-\frac{y^{2}+\mu^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}+\log\!\Big(2\cosh\frac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big)-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi).

Using
\log(2\cosh t)\leq|t|+\log 2
and
\sigma\in[\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max}]
,
|\mu|\leq M_{\mu}
gives

|\ell(y;\theta)|\leq B_{K}(1+y^{2}),\qquad B_{K}:=|\log\sigma_{\min}|+\frac{M_{\mu}^{2}}{2\sigma_{\min}^{2}}+\log 2+\tfrac{1}{2}|\log(2\pi)|+\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\min}^{2}}.

Under
\theta_{0}
,
Y
has Gaussian tails, hence
\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[1+Y^{2}]<\infty
.
Since
\ell(y;\theta)
is continuous in
\theta
for each fixed
y
, dominated convergence yields continuity of
\ell(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[\ell(Y;\theta)]
on
K
.

4) Strict gap on
K
at distance
\delta
.

Let
C:=\{\theta\in K:\dist(\theta,\Theta_{0})\geq\delta\}
; this is compact and disjoint from
\Theta_{0}
.
By Steps 1–2, the continuous function

g(\theta):=\ell(\theta_{0})-\ell(\theta)=\mathrm{KL}(f_{\theta_{0}}\|f_{\theta})\geq 0
on
K
, and
g(\theta)=0
only on
\Theta_{0}
. Hence

\eta:=\min_{\theta\in C}g(\theta)>0.

Rearranging gives the asserted inequality.
∎

Lemma A.9

(Information and the kink at
\mu_{0}=0
)
.

Let
S(y;\theta)=\nabla_{\theta}\ell(y;\theta)
be the score and

I(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[S(Y;\theta)S(Y;\theta)^{\top}]=-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\nabla^{2}_{\theta}\ell(Y;\theta)].

Then
I(\theta)
exists and is continuous in a neighborhood of any fixed
\theta_{0}=(\mu_{0},\sigma_{0})
.
If
\mu_{0}\neq 0
then
I(\theta_{0})\succ 0
; if
\mu_{0}=0
, then
I(\theta_{0})
is singular in the
\mu
–direction.

Proof.

1) Score and Hessian; envelopes ensuring integrability.

For
y\geq 0
,

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(y;\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mu}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{y}{\sigma^{2}}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big),

\displaystyle S_{\sigma}(y;\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma}+\frac{y^{2}+\mu^{2}}{\sigma^{3}}-\frac{2y\mu}{\sigma^{3}}\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\Big).

Differentiating once more gives the Hessian entries (as in the main text).
By
Lemma

A.4
there exist
C_{2},C_{3}>0
(depending on a small compact neighborhood
K
of
\theta_{0}
) such that for all
\theta\in K
and
y\geq 0
,

\|S(y;\theta)\|\leq C_{2}(1+y+y^{2}),\qquad\|\nabla^{2}_{\theta}\ell(y;\theta)\|\leq C_{3}(1+y^{2}+y^{4}).

Since under the folded-normal
\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[1+Y^{4}]<\infty
, these are integrable envelopes.

2) Existence of
I(\theta)
and continuity (dominated convergence).

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[SS^{\top}]
exists because
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|S\|^{2}]<\infty
by the envelope.
Similarly
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\|\nabla^{2}\ell\|]<\infty
.
By dominated convergence (envelopes above) and continuity of
S
,
\nabla^{2}\ell
in
\theta
, both maps

\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[SS^{\top}]
and
\theta\mapsto-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\nabla^{2}\ell]
are continuous near
\theta_{0}
.

3) Information identity and zero mean of the score.

Using
\partial_{\theta}f=f\,\partial_{\theta}\log f
, the envelopes above imply that
\partial_{\theta}f
is integrable and differentiation under the integral is justified. Thus

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[S(Y;\theta)]=\int\partial_{\theta}\log f(y;\theta)\,f(y;\theta)\,dy=\partial_{\theta}\int f(y;\theta)\,dy=\partial_{\theta}1=0,

and (a second differentiation under the integral) gives

I(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[SS^{\top}]=-\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[\nabla^{2}_{\theta}\ell].

4) Positive definiteness if
\mu_{0}\neq 0
.

Let
a=(a_{\mu},a_{\sigma})^{\top}\neq 0
. Then

a^{\top}I(\theta_{0})a=\mathrm{Var}_{\theta_{0}}(a^{\top}S(Y;\theta_{0})).

We now show
a^{\top}S(\cdot\,;\theta_{0})\not\equiv 0
as a function of
y\geq 0
.

Write (at
\theta_{0}=(\mu_{0},\sigma_{0})
with
\mu_{0}\neq 0
)

g(y):=a^{\top}S(y;\theta_{0})=c_{0}+c_{2}\,y^{2}+c_{T}\,y\,\tanh\!\Big(\frac{y\mu_{0}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\Big),

where

c_{0}:=-\frac{a_{\mu}\mu_{0}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}-\frac{a_{\sigma}}{\sigma_{0}}+\frac{a_{\sigma}\mu_{0}^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{3}},\qquad c_{2}:=\frac{a_{\sigma}}{\sigma_{0}^{3}},\qquad c_{T}:=\frac{a_{\mu}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}-\frac{2a_{\sigma}\mu_{0}}{\sigma_{0}^{3}}.

Suppose
g\equiv 0
. Expand
\tanh z=z-\frac{z^{3}}{3}+\frac{2z^{5}}{15}+O(z^{7})
as
z\to 0
; then, as
y\downarrow 0
,

g(y)=c_{0}+\Big(c_{2}+c_{T}\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\Big)y^{2}-c_{T}\frac{\mu_{0}^{3}}{3\sigma_{0}^{6}}y^{4}+O(y^{6}).

If
g(y)\equiv 0
on a neighborhood of
0
, all coefficients of the power series must vanish:

c_{0}=0,\qquad c_{2}+c_{T}\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}=0,\qquad c_{T}=0.

From
c_{T}=0
we get
a_{\mu}=\frac{2\mu_{0}}{\sigma_{0}}a_{\sigma}
. Plugging into
c_{2}+c_{T}\mu_{0}/\sigma_{0}^{2}=0
yields
a_{\sigma}=0
and then
a_{\mu}=0
. Finally
c_{0}=0
is automatic. Thus
a=0
, contradicting the assumption. Hence
g
is not a.s. zero and
\mathrm{Var}_{\theta_{0}}(g(Y))>0
, i.e.
I(\theta_{0})\succ 0
.

5) Singularity at
\mu_{0}=0
.

At
(\mu,\sigma)=(0,\sigma_{0})
,

S_{\mu}(y;0,\sigma_{0})=-\mu/\sigma_{0}^{2}+(y/\sigma_{0}^{2})\tanh(0)=0

for every
y
, hence
I_{\mu\mu}(0,\sigma_{0})=\mathbb{E}[S_{\mu}^{2}]=0
and
I_{\mu\sigma}(0,\sigma_{0})=0
.
Therefore
I(0,\sigma_{0})
is singular in the
\mu
–direction.
∎

Lemma A.11

(Argmax mapping for quadratic-minus-quartic contrasts)
.

Let
\Phi_{n}(t)
be the rescaled log-likelihood contrast

\Phi_{n}(t)\ :=\ \ell_{n}(n^{-1/4}t,\sigma_{0})-\ell_{n}(0,\sigma_{0})\ =\ a\,Z_{n}\,t^{2}\ -\ b_{n}\,t^{4}\ +\ R_{n}(t),

where

a:=\frac{1}{2\sigma_{0}^{4}},\qquad Z_{n}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(Y_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2}\big),\qquad b_{n}:=\frac{1}{12\sigma_{0}^{8}}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{4}.

Assume
Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots
are i.i.d. from the folded normal at
(0,\sigma_{0})
so that

Z_{n}\xrightarrow{d}Z\sim N(0,\mathrm{Var}(Y^{2}))
and
b_{n}\xrightarrow{p}b:=\mathbb{E}[Y^{4}]/(12\sigma_{0}^{8})>0
.
Suppose moreover that for every fixed
U>0
,

\sup_{|t|\leq U}|R_{n}(t)|\ =\ o_{p}(1).

(A.16)

Define the (random) limit contrast
\Psi_{Z}(t):=aZ\,t^{2}-b\,t^{4}
. Let

T_{n}\in\arg\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{n}(t)

and

T^{\star}\in\arg\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Psi_{Z}(t).

Then
T^{\star}
is almost surely unique and

T_{n}\ \xrightarrow{d}\ T^{\star}\quad\text{and}\quad|T_{n}|\ \xrightarrow{d}\ |T^{\star}|=\sqrt{\frac{(aZ)_{+}}{2b}}\ =\ \sqrt{\frac{3\,\sigma_{0}^{4}}{\mathbb{E}[Y^{4}]}\,(Z)_{+}}\,.

Proof.

Step 1 (tightness of maximizers).

Fix
\delta\in(0,b/2)
. On the event
\{b_{n}\geq b-\delta\}
(which has probability
\to 1
), we have for all
t
,

\Phi_{n}(t)\ \leq\ a|Z_{n}|\,t^{2}\ -\ (b-\delta)\,t^{4}.

The right-hand side is maximized at
|t|=\sqrt{a|Z_{n}|/(2(b-\delta))}
, hence any maximizer
T_{n}
of
\Phi_{n}
must satisfy

|T_{n}|\ \leq\ \sqrt{\frac{a|Z_{n}|}{2(b-\delta)}}\qquad\text{whenever }b_{n}\geq b-\delta.

(A.17)

Since
Z_{n}\xrightarrow{d}Z
, the sequence
\{|Z_{n}|\}
is tight; thus
\{|T_{n}|\}
is tight.

Step 2 (uniform convergence on compacts).

Fix
U>0
. Decompose, for
|t|\leq U
,

\Phi_{n}(t)-\Psi_{Z}(t)=\underbrace{a(Z_{n}-Z)\,t^{2}}_{\text{random linear term}}\ -\underbrace{(b_{n}-b)\,t^{4}}_{\text{random quartic coeff.}}\ +\underbrace{R_{n}(t)}_{\text{remainder}}.

By Slutsky,
Z_{n}-Z=o_{p}(1)
and
b_{n}-b=o_{p}(1)
; hence the first two terms are
o_{p}(1)
uniformly on
[-U,U]
. By (
A.16
),
R_{n}=o_{p}(1)
uniformly on
[-U,U]
as well. Therefore

\sup_{|t|\leq U}\,|\Phi_{n}(t)-\Psi_{Z}(t)|\ =\ o_{p}(1)\qquad\text{for every fixed }U>0.

(A.18)

Step 3 (a.s. uniqueness and margin of the limit maximizer).

For each realization of
Z
:

•

If
Z<0
, then
\Psi_{Z}(t)=aZt^{2}-bt^{4}
is strictly concave with unique maximizer
T^{\star}=0
.
Moreover, for all
t
,
\Psi_{Z}(t)\leq\Psi_{Z}(0)-bt^{4}
, so for any
r>0
,

\sup_{|t-0|\geq r}\Psi_{Z}(t)\ \leq\ \Psi_{Z}(0)-b\,r^{4}.

(A.19)

•

If
Z>0
, the stationary points satisfy
2aZt-4bt^{3}=0
, hence the unique maximizer is

T^{\star}=\operatorname{sgn}(Z)\sqrt{aZ/(2b)}.

Since
\Psi_{Z}
is
C^{2}
with
\Psi_{Z}^{\prime\prime}(T^{\star})=-4aZ<0
, it is
strongly concave
at
T^{\star}
: there exist
\rho(Z)>0
and
c(Z)>0
such that

\sup_{|t-T^{\star}|\geq r,\ |t-T^{\star}|\leq\rho(Z)}\Psi_{Z}(t)\ \leq\ \Psi_{Z}(T^{\star})-c(Z)\,r^{2}\quad\text{for all }r\in(0,\rho(Z)].

(A.20)

Outside a sufficiently large compact (say
|t|\geq U_{0}(Z)
), the quartic term dominates and

\Psi_{Z}(t)\leq\Psi_{Z}(T^{\star})-1

for some
U_{0}(Z)<\infty
.

Since
\Pr(Z=0)=0
for a nondegenerate normal,
T^{\star}
is a.s. unique. Combining (
A.19
)–(
A.20
), for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exist random
U(Z)<\infty
and
\eta(Z,\varepsilon)>0
such that

\sup_{|t-T^{\star}|\geq\varepsilon,\ |t|\leq U(Z)}\ \Psi_{Z}(t)\ \leq\ \Psi_{Z}(T^{\star})-\eta(Z,\varepsilon).

(A.21)

Step 4 (localization of
T_{n}
to a fixed compact with high probability).

By tightness (
A.17
), for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
there exists a deterministic
U_{\alpha}<\infty
such that

\Pr(|T_n|\le U_\alpha)\geq 1-\alpha

for all large
n
.
Similarly, since
Z
is tight, we can choose
U_{\alpha}
large enough that
\Pr(|T^\star|\le U_\alpha)\geq 1-\alpha
.
Hence, with probability at least
1-2\alpha
, both maximizers lie inside the fixed compact
[-U_{\alpha},U_{\alpha}]
.

Step 5 (argmax stability via the margin and uniform convergence).

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in(0,1/4)
. On the event

\mathcal{E}_{n,\alpha,\varepsilon}:=\Big\{|T_{n}|\leq U_{\alpha},\ |T^{\star}|\leq U_{\alpha},\ \sup_{|t|\leq U_{\alpha}}|\Phi_{n}(t)-\Psi_{Z}(t)|\leq\eta(Z,\varepsilon)/3\Big\},

we have, using (
A.21
),

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|t-T^{\star}|\geq\varepsilon\\
|t|\leq U_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}\ \Phi_{n}(t)\ \leq\ \sup_{|t-T^{\star}|\geq\varepsilon}\Psi_{Z}(t)+\eta/3\ \leq\ \Psi_{Z}(T^{\star})-2\eta/3\ \leq\ \Phi_{n}(T^{\star})-\eta/3.

Therefore any maximizer
T_{n}
of
\Phi_{n}
on
[-U_{\alpha},U_{\alpha}]
must satisfy
|T_{n}-T^{\star}|<\varepsilon
on
\mathcal{E}_{n,\alpha,\varepsilon}
.
By Step 2, the third condition in
\mathcal{E}_{n,\alpha,\varepsilon}
holds with probability
\to 1
; Steps 1 and 4 give
\Pr(|T_n|\le U_\alpha,\ |T^\star|\le U_\alpha)\geq 1-2\alpha
for all large
n
.
Thus

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\big(|T_n-T^\star|<\varepsilon\big)\ \geq\ 1-2\alpha.

Since
\alpha>0
was arbitrary,
T_{n}\xrightarrow{p}T^{\star}
. Because
T^{\star}
is a measurable function of
Z
and
Z_{n}\xrightarrow{d}Z
, the same argument with the continuous mapping theorem shows
T_{n}\xrightarrow{d}T^{\star}
.
Finally, continuity of
t\mapsto|t|
yields
|T_{n}|\xrightarrow{d}|T^{\star}|=\sqrt{(aZ)_{+}/(2b)}
.
∎

Appendix B
Appendix B. Gaussian Mixtures

B.0. Model, sieve, metric, notation

For
k\geq 2
, define the Gaussian density with mean
\mu
and standard deviation
\sigma>0
by

\varphi(x;\mu,\sigma):=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\sigma^{-1}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\mu)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right).

(B.1)

Define the
k
-component Gaussian mixture density for parameter

\theta=(\pi,\mu,\sigma)\in\Delta_{k-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times(0,\infty)^{k}

where
\Delta_{k-1}=\{\pi\in[0,1]^{k}:\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi_{j}=1\}
is the probability simplex,
by

f_{\theta}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi_{j}\,\varphi(x;\mu_{j},\sigma_{j}).

(B.2)

Given an i.i.d. sample
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\sim f_{\theta_{0}}
where

\theta_{0}=(\pi^{0},\mu^{0},\sigma^{0})\in\Delta_{k-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times(0,\infty)^{k},

with strictly positive weights
\pi_{j}^{0}>0
and distinct pairs
(\mu_{j}^{0},\sigma_{j}^{0})
, define the empirical and population log-likelihoods as:

\ell_{n}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f_{\theta}(X_{i}),\quad\text{and}\quad\ell(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{0}}[\log f_{\theta}(X)].

(B.3)

Assume
\mathbb{E}[X^{4}]<\infty
.

For
m\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/k]
, define the compact sieve

S_{m,\varepsilon}:=\{\pi\in\Delta_{k-1}:\min_{j}\pi_{j}\geq\varepsilon\}\times[-m,m]^{k}\times[e^{-m},e^{m}]^{k},

(B.4)

with

\sigma_{\min}:=e^{-m},\quad\sigma_{\max}:=e^{m}.

(B.5)

To handle label symmetry, use the minimum matching distance between parameters

d_{\min}(\theta,\theta^{\prime}):=\min_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\left(\|\pi(\mu)-\mu^{\prime}\|_{2}+\|\pi(\sigma)-\sigma^{\prime}\|_{2}+\|\pi(\pi)-\pi^{\prime}\|_{1}\right).

(B.6)

Two useful inequalities used repeatedly:

\displaystyle(|x|+m)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2x^{2}+2m^{2},

(B.7)

\displaystyle(x-\mu)^{2}

\displaystyle=x^{2}-2\mu x+\mu^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-\mu^{2},\quad\text{since }-2\mu x\geq-(\mu^{2}+x^{2}).

(B.8)

B.1. Pointwise bounds, log envelope

Lemma B.1

(Explicit upper and lower bounds)
.

For all
\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle\geq A_{m}e^{-a_{m}x^{2}},\quad A_{m}:=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{m^{2}}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}}\right),\quad a_{m}:=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}},

(B.9)

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle\leq B_{m,k}e^{-b_{m}x^{2}},\quad B_{m,k}:=\frac{k}{\sigma_{\min}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(\frac{m^{2}}{2\sigma_{\max}^{2}}\right),\quad b_{m}:=\frac{1}{4\sigma_{\max}^{2}}.

(B.10)

Hence,

|\log f_{\theta}(x)|\leq K_{m,k}(1+x^{2}),\quad K_{m,k}:=\max\{-\log A_{m},\log B_{m,k}\}+\max\{a_{m},b_{m}\}.

(B.11)

Proof.

By triangle inequality,

|x-\mu_{j}|\leq|x|+m

and by square inequality (
B.7
),

(|x|+m)^{2}\leq 2x^{2}+2m^{2}.

For the lower bound, using (
B.1
) and the bounds on
\sigma_{j}
,

\varphi(x;\mu_{j},\sigma_{j})\geq\frac{1}{\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{(|x|+m)^{2}}{2\sigma_{\min}^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{e^{-m^{2}/\sigma_{\min}^{2}}}{\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-a_{m}x^{2}}.

Because
\pi_{j}\geq\varepsilon>0
, sum over
j
yields (
B.9
).

For upper bound, by inequality (
B.8
),

(x-\mu_{j})^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-m^{2},

thus from (
B.1
) and bounds on
\sigma_{j}
,

\varphi(x;\mu_{j},\sigma_{j})\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(\frac{m^{2}}{2\sigma_{\max}^{2}}\right)e^{-b_{m}x^{2}}.

Summation over
k
components yields (
B.10
).

The logarithmic envelope bound follows by applying logarithm to these inequalities and combining.
∎

B.2. Gradient and Lipschitz slope bounds

Lemma B.2

(Partial derivatives and Lipschitz constant)
.

Set
t_{j}:=\log\sigma_{j}
and define

r_{j}(x;\theta):=\frac{\pi_{j}\varphi(x;\mu_{j},\sigma_{j})}{f_{\theta}(x)}\in[0,1].

For all
\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{\mu_{j}}\log f_{\theta}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|x|+m}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}},

(B.12)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t_{j}}\log f_{\theta}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{(|x|+m)^{2}}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}},

(B.13)

\displaystyle|\partial_{\pi_{j}}\log f_{\theta}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

(B.14)

Equip the parameter space with the product norm,

\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|:=\|\mu-\mu^{\prime}\|_{2}+\|t-t^{\prime}\|_{2}+\|\pi-\pi^{\prime}\|_{1},

then,

\|\nabla_{\theta}\log f_{\theta}(x)\|\leq C_{m,\varepsilon,k}(1+x^{2}),

where

C_{m,\varepsilon,k}:=\sqrt{k}\max\left\{\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}},1,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right\}(1+m+m^{2}).

Moreover,

|\log f_{\theta}(x)-\log f_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)|\leq L(x)\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|,\quad L(x):=C_{m,\varepsilon,k}(1+x^{2}).

Proof.

By chain rule and quotient rule,

\partial_{\mu_{j}}\log f_{\theta}(x)=\frac{\pi_{j}\partial_{\mu_{j}}\varphi(x;\mu_{j},\sigma_{j})}{f_{\theta}(x)}=r_{j}(x;\theta)\frac{x-\mu_{j}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}},

bounded using
|x-\mu_{j}|\leq|x|+m
and
\sigma_{j}\geq\sigma_{\min}
. Similar for
t_{j}=\log\sigma_{j}
,

\partial_{\sigma_{j}}\log\varphi(x;\mu_{j},\sigma_{j})=-\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}}+\frac{(x-\mu_{j})^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{3}},

so by the chain rule,

\partial_{t_{j}}\log f_{\theta}(x)=r_{j}(x;\theta)\left(-1+\frac{(x-\mu_{j})^{2}}{\sigma_{j}^{2}}\right),

bounded as in (
B.13
). For
\pi_{j}
, direct differentiation gives (
B.14
).

Combining all partial derivatives in product norm with
\sqrt{k}
and maximal constants yields
C_{m,\varepsilon,k}
. The Lipschitz bound follows from the mean value theorem.
∎

B.3 Uniform Law of Large Numbers (ULLN) on the sieve

Proposition B.3

(ULLN)
.

If
\mathbb{E}[X^{4}]<\infty
, then

\sup_{\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f_{\theta}(X_{i})-\ell(\theta)\right|\xrightarrow{a.s.}0.

Proof.

Since
S_{m,\varepsilon}
is compact and metric, it is separable; let
D=\{\vartheta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a countable dense subset.

By Lemma
B.1
, we have the envelope

|\log f_{\theta}(X)|\leq K_{m,k}(1+X^{2}),

which is
L^{1}
-integrable since
\mathbb{E}[X^{4}]<\infty
implies
\mathbb{E}[X^{2}]<\infty
.

Hence, by the SLLN, for each fixed
\theta\in D
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log f_{\theta}(X_{i})\to\ell(\theta),\quad a.s.

By countable union, the above convergence holds simultaneously for all
\theta\in D
a.s.

By the Lipschitz continuity of
\theta\mapsto\log f_{\theta}(x)
with Lipschitz constant
L(x)
from Lemma
B.2
, and SLLN applied to
L(X)
, the convergence extends uniformly over all
\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}
.

∎

B.4 High-Probability ULLN Bound

Proposition B.4

(Explicit high-probability bound)
.

Let
L(X)=C_{m,\varepsilon,k}(1+X^{2})
, and define

m_{1}:=\mathbb{E}[L(X)],\quad m_{2}:=\mathbb{E}[L(X)^{2}],\quad D_{m,k}:=2K_{m,k}^{2}(1+\mathbb{E}[X^{4}]).

Fix
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. Construct a finite net
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{M}\}\subset S_{m,\varepsilon}
of mesh size
\delta=\epsilon/(6m_{1})
. Then, for all
n
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}}\left|n^{-1}\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell(\theta)\right|>\epsilon\right)\leq\frac{m_{2}-m_{1}^{2}}{nm_{1}^{2}}+\frac{9MD_{m,k}}{n\epsilon^{2}}.

Proof.

Define

S_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L(X_{i}).

By Chebyshev’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}(|S_{n}-m_{1}|>m_{1})\leq\frac{\mathrm{Var}(L(X))}{nm_{1}^{2}}=\frac{m_{2}-m_{1}^{2}}{nm_{1}^{2}}.

Next, for each net point
\theta_{j}
,

\mathrm{Var}(\log f_{\theta_{j}}(X))\leq\mathbb{E}[\log f^{2}_{\theta_{j}}(X)]\leq D_{m,k}

due to the envelope bound.

Applying Chebyshev’s inequality with tolerance
\epsilon/3
:

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum\log f_{\theta_{j}}(X_{i})-\ell(\theta_{j})\right|>\frac{\epsilon}{3}\right)\leq\frac{9D_{m,k}}{n\epsilon^{2}}.

Using the union bound over all
M
net points:

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq M}\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum\log f_{\theta_{j}}(X_{i})-\ell(\theta_{j})\right|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3}\right)\geq 1-\frac{9MD_{m,k}}{n\epsilon^{2}}.

Combining with the control
S_{n}\leq 2m_{1}
(which holds with high probability by the first bound), for any
\theta
within mesh
\delta
of some
\theta_{j}
, the Lipschitz continuity of the log likelihood implies

\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum\log f_{\theta}(X_{i})-\ell(\theta)\right|\leq\epsilon,

concluding the claim.
∎

B.5 Identifiability and KL Separation

Lemma B.5

(Identifiability modulo permutation)
.

Suppose
f_{\theta}=f_{\theta^{\prime}}
a.e. with all weights strictly positive and all
(\mu_{j},\sigma_{j})
distinct. Then
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
differ by a permutation of components.

Proof.

Equality of densities implies equality of characteristic functions (Fourier transforms)

\phi_{\theta}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi_{j}e^{i\mu_{j}t-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{j}^{2}t^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi_{j}^{\prime}e^{i\mu_{j}^{\prime}t-\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{j}^{\prime})^{2}t^{2}},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Define

\beta_{\max}:=\max_{j}\sigma_{j}^{2}.

Multiply both sides by
e^{\frac{1}{2}\beta_{\max}t^{2}}
:

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi_{j}e^{i\mu_{j}t}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{j}^{2}-\beta_{\max})t^{2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi_{j}^{\prime}e^{i\mu_{j}^{\prime}t}e^{-\frac{1}{2}((\sigma_{j}^{\prime})^{2}-\beta_{\max})t^{2}}.

For components with
\sigma_{j}^{2}<\beta_{\max}
,
e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{j}^{2}-\beta_{\max})t^{2}}=e^{\frac{1}{2}(\beta_{\max}-\sigma_{j}^{2})t^{2}}\to 0
as
|t|\to\infty
. By dominated convergence, as
|t|\to\infty
, the sum limits to

\sum_{j:\sigma_{j}^{2}=\beta_{\max}}\pi_{j}e^{i\mu_{j}t}=\sum_{j:(\sigma_{j}^{\prime})^{2}=\beta_{\max}}\pi_{j}^{\prime}e^{i\mu_{j}^{\prime}t}.

The coefficients and frequencies correspond to finite exponential sums. Such sums are linearly independent if the frequencies
\mu_{j}
are distinct. Differentiating at
t=0
, we get a Vandermonde system with a nonzero determinant, implying the multisets of parameters
(\pi_{j},\mu_{j})
for components with largest variance coincide up to a permutation.

Removing these components and repeating for the second largest variances, etc., establishes the result.
∎

Proposition B.6

(Kullback-Leibler separation)
.

Let
\Theta_{0}
be the orbit of
\theta_{0}
under permutations. For every
\delta>0
, there exists
\eta>0
such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}\\
d_{\min}(\theta,\Theta_{0})\geq\delta\end{subarray}}\ell(\theta)\leq\ell(\theta_{0})-\eta.

Proof.

By definition of KL divergence,

\ell(\theta)-\ell(\theta_{0})=-\mathrm{KL}(f_{\theta_{0}}\|f_{\theta})\leq 0,

equality iff
f_{\theta}=f_{\theta_{0}}
almost everywhere. By Lemma
B.5
, this condition is equivalent to
\theta
being in the permutation orbit
\Theta_{0}
.

The map
\ell
is continuous on the compact set
S_{m,\varepsilon}
by dominated convergence (using
K_{m,k}(1+x^{2})
envelope and
\mathbb{E}[X^{4}]<\infty
). The set

C_{\delta}:=\{\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}:d_{\min}(\theta,\Theta_{0})\geq\delta\}

is a closed subset of
S_{m,\varepsilon}
disjoint from
\Theta_{0}
, hence compact. Since
\ell
achieves its maximum on compact sets, the supremum attained on
C_{\delta}
is strictly less than
\ell(\theta_{0})
. Define the gap as

\eta:=\ell(\theta_{0})-\max_{\theta\in C_{\delta}}\ell(\theta)>0.

∎

B.6 Existence, Argmax Stability, and Consistency

Proposition B.7

(Existence of MLE on the Sieve)
.

For fixed
m\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/k]
, the empirical log-likelihood
\ell_{n}(\theta)
attains its maximum on the compact set
S_{m,\varepsilon}
; that is, there exists at least one
\hat{\theta}_{n,m,\varepsilon}\in\arg\max_{\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}}\ell_{n}(\theta)
.

Proof.

Since
S_{m,\varepsilon}
is compact by construction and
\ell_{n}
is continuous on
S_{m,\varepsilon}
(being a finite sum of continuous functions of
\theta
since
\log f_{\theta}(x)
varies continuously with
\theta
for each fixed
x
), the extreme value theorem guarantees the existence of at least one maximizer
\hat{\theta}_{n,m,\varepsilon}
of
\ell_{n}(\cdot)
over
S_{m,\varepsilon}
.
∎

Lemma B.8

(Deterministic Argmax Stability)
.

Let
K
be a compact set and
q:K\to\mathbb{R}
a continuous function. Define the maximizer set
Q_{0}:=\arg\max_{K}q
. Suppose there exist constants
\varepsilon,\eta>0
such that

\sup_{K\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(Q_{0})}q\leq\sup_{Q_{0}}q-\eta,

where
B_{\varepsilon}(Q_{0})
denotes the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
Q_{0}
. Then, for any sequence of functions
q_{n}:K\to\mathbb{R}
with

\sup_{K}|q_{n}-q|\leq\displaystyle\frac{\eta}{3},

the maximizer sets satisfy

\arg\max_{K}q_{n}\subseteq B_{\varepsilon}(Q_{0}),

and moreover for each
\theta^{\star}\in Q_{0}
, there exists some maximizer of
q_{n}
lying within
B_{\varepsilon}(\{\theta^{\star}\})
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that some
\hat{\theta}_{n}\in\arg\max q_{n}
lies outside
B_{\varepsilon}(Q_{0})
. Then

q_{n}(\hat{\theta}_{n})\leq q(\hat{\theta}_{n})+\frac{\eta}{3}\leq\sup_{K\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(Q_{0})}q+\frac{\eta}{3}\leq\sup_{Q_{0}}q-\frac{2\eta}{3}.

On the other hand, select
\bar{\theta}\in Q_{0}
so that

q_{n}(\bar{\theta})\geq q(\bar{\theta})-\frac{\eta}{3}=\sup_{Q_{0}}q-\frac{\eta}{3}.

Since

q_{n}(\bar{\theta})-q_{n}(\hat{\theta}_{n})\geq\frac{\eta}{3}>0,

this contradicts the assumption that
\hat{\theta}_{n}
is a maximizer of
q_{n}
. Hence all maximizers must lie in
B_{\varepsilon}(Q_{0})
.

For the local statement on each
\theta^{\star}\in Q_{0}
, apply the same argument to small neighborhoods of
\theta^{\star}
.
∎

Theorem B.9

(Hausdorff consistency of the MLE on fixed sieve)
.

Let
\widehat{\Theta}_{n,m,\varepsilon}\subseteq\arg\max_{S_{m,\varepsilon}}\ell_{n}(\theta)
be the (orbit) MLE set. Denote by
\Theta_{0}
the permutation orbit of the true parameter
\theta_{0}
. Then

d_{H}\big(\widehat{\Theta}_{n,m,\varepsilon},\Theta_{0}\big)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\text{a.s.}}0,

where
d_{H}
is the Hausdorff distance induced by
d_{\min}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
B.3
, we have almost sure uniform convergence
\sup_{\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}}|n^{-1}\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell(\theta)|\to 0
.

By Proposition
B.6
, the population log–likelihood
\ell
possesses an identification gap
\eta>0
away from any neighborhood of
\Theta_{0}
.

Applying Lemma
to the sequence of functions
q_{n}:=n^{-1}\ell_{n}
and the fixed function
q:=\ell
, we get that for large
n
, the set of MLE maximizers
\widehat{\Theta}_{n,m,\varepsilon}
must lie in arbitrarily small neighborhoods of
\Theta_{0}
.

Since also for each
\theta^{*}\in\Theta_{0}
there exist maximizers close to
\theta^{*}
, the Hausdorff distance converges to zero almost surely.
∎

Theorem B.10

(Consistency with growing sieves)
.

Suppose
m(n)\to\infty
,
\varepsilon(n)\to 0
, and the true parameter
\theta_{0}
lies in some fixed sieve
S_{M,\varepsilon_{0}}
for

and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. Then, for all sufficiently large

, and

, the set of maximizers over

B.7 Variance Collapse, Penalisation, and Measurability

Proposition B.11

(Variance collapse on the full space)
.

For any fixed sample
x_{1:n}
and number of components
k\geq 2
,

\sup_{\theta}\ell_{n}(\theta)=+\infty,

with the supremum over the entire unconstrained parameter space
\Delta_{k-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times(0,\infty)^{k}
.

Proof.

Fix component index
j=1
. Set its weight
\pi_{1}=1/2>0
, fix its mean to
\mu_{1}=x_{1}
, and let its standard deviation
\sigma_{1}=t\to 0
. For the other components, fix any parameters ensuring finite likelihood values on the sample.

The likelihood contribution from component 1 at
x_{1}
is

f_{\theta}(x_{1})\geq\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x_{1};x_{1},t)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp(0)=\frac{1}{2t\sqrt{2\pi}}.

Taking logarithms,

\log f_{\theta}(x_{1})\geq-\log 2-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)-\log t\to+\infty,\quad t\to 0.

All other terms in
\ell_{n}(\theta)
remain bounded as
t\to 0
, so
\ell_{n}(\theta)\to+\infty
.
∎

Proposition B.12

(Ridge penalty ensures coercivity)
.

Define penalty

g(\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\mu_{j}^{2}+(\log\sigma_{j})^{2}\right),

and let penalty coefficients
\lambda_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Then for each
n
,

•

The penalized objective
\ell_{n}(\theta)-\lambda_{n}g(\theta)
attains a maximum on
\Theta
.

•

For all sufficiently large
n
, every maximizer lies in some fixed sieve
S_{M,\varepsilon}
independent of
n
.

•

These penalized MLEs are consistent modulo permutation.

Proof.

To prove coercivity, consider any sequence
(\theta_{r})
leaving every sieve
S_{M,\varepsilon}
. Then at least one occurs:

(i) Large means:
If
|\mu_{j}^{(r)}|\to\infty
, then from the Gaussian tail decay,

\ell_{n}(\theta_{r})\leq C_{1}-C_{2}\mu_{j}^{(r)2}\quad\text{for some }C_{1},C_{2}>0,

while

\lambda_{n}g(\theta_{r})\geq\lambda_{n}\mu_{j}^{(r)2}.

For large
r
, the penalty dominates any possible increase from
\ell_{n}
, so the penalized objective tends to
-\infty
.

(ii) Variance collapse:
If
\sigma_{j}^{(r)}\to 0
, then by standard calculations,
\ell_{n}(\theta_{r})
grows at most logarithmically in
1/\sigma_{j}
, but the penalty grows quadratically in
\log\sigma_{j}
, dominating the log-likelihood penalty and pushing penalized likelihood downwards.

(iii) Variance explosion:
If
\sigma_{j}^{(r)}\to\infty
, the likelihood tends to
-\infty
, and penalty dominates.

(iv) Weights tend to zero:
Shrinking weights cannot increase likelihood due to continuity and positivity constraints.

Hence the penalized likelihood is coercive and, by Weierstrass, attains its maximum on a compact subset of
\Theta
, i.e., within some
S_{M,\varepsilon}
. Applying the uniform consistency results on
S_{M,\varepsilon}
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma B.13

(Measurability of the MLE argmax on a sieve)
.

For fixed
n
,
m
, and
\varepsilon
, define the set-valued map

A(\omega):=\arg\max_{\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}}\ell_{n}(\theta;\omega).

Then

•

A(\omega)
is nonempty and compact for all
\omega
.

•

The mapping
A:\Omega\to 2^{S_{m,\varepsilon}}
is measurable; i.e., for every open
U\subset S_{m,\varepsilon}
,

\{\omega:A(\omega)\cap U\neq\varnothing\}\in\mathcal{F}.

•

There exists a measurable selection
\hat{\theta}_{n}:\Omega\to S_{m,\varepsilon}
with
\hat{\theta}_{n}(\omega)\in A(\omega)
.

Proof.

The nonemptiness and compactness follow from the extreme-value theorem applied to the continuous function
\ell_{n}(\cdot;\omega)
on the compact set
S_{m,\varepsilon}
.

Fix a countable dense subset
D=\{\vartheta_{j}:j\geq 1\}\subset S_{m,\varepsilon}
. Define

v(\omega):=\sup_{\theta\in S_{m,\varepsilon}}\ell_{n}(\theta;\omega)=\sup_{j\geq 1}\ell_{n}(\vartheta_{j};\omega),

and for any open ball
U
,

v_{U}(\omega):=\sup_{\theta\in U}\ell_{n}(\theta;\omega)=\sup_{\vartheta_{j}\in D\cap U}\ell_{n}(\vartheta_{j};\omega).

Since each
\ell_{n}(\vartheta_{j};\cdot)
is measurable as a finite sum of measurable functions of
\omega
, both
v
and
v_{U}
are measurable as countable suprema of measurable functions.

The hit set

\{\omega:A(\omega)\cap U\neq\varnothing\}=\{\omega:v_{U}(\omega)=v(\omega)\}

is measurable as a level-set of measurable functions.

To construct a measurable selection, use a countable basis of open balls with rational centers and radii. For each
\omega
, select the smallest indexed ball
B_{1}
with
A(\omega)\cap B_{1}\neq\emptyset
, then select the next smallest ball
B_{2}\subset B_{1}
with radius half that of
B_{1}
such that
A(\omega)\cap B_{2}\neq\emptyset
, and so forth. The centers of these nested balls form measurable functions which converge to a point in
A(\omega)
. This limit defines the measurable selection
\hat{\theta}_{n}(\omega)
.
∎

The quenched structured coalescent for diploid population models on finite graphs with large migrations and uneven offspring distributions
Maximillian Newman

Abstract
In this work we describe a new model for the evolution of a diploid structured population backwards in time that allows for large migrations and uneven offspring distributions. The model generalizes both the mean-field model of Birkner et al. [ Electron. J. Probab. 23: 1-44 (2018)] and the haploid structured model of Möhle [ Theor. Popul. Biol. 2024 Apr:156:103-116]. We show convergence, with mild conditions on the joint distribution of offspring frequencies and migrations, of gene genealogies conditional on the pedigree to a time-inhomogeneous coalescent process driven by a Poisson point process Ψ \Psi that records the timing and scale of large migrations and uneven offspring distributions. This quenched scaling limit demonstrates a significant difference in the predictions of the classical annealed theory of structured coalescent processes. In particular, the annealed and quenched scaling limits coincide if and only if these large migrations and uneven offspring distributions are absent. The proof proceeds by the method of moments and utilizes coupling techniques from the theory of random walks in random environments. Several examples are given and their quenched scaling limits established.

Introduction

Consider a sample of
n
copies of a gene from
n
distinct individuals in a population. These samples, the result of generations of genetic inheritance backwards in time, trace their roots back to a common ancestor. The structure of how these genes trace their roots backwards in time is called the gene genealogy and is the central object of interest in coalescent theory. The seminal work of Kingman
20
;
21
established, marginally, at each place in the genome of a well-mixed population of constant, large size undergoing neutral evolution, that gene genealogies take the structure of a binary tree, where the binary mergers occur uniformly at random among each possible pair of extant lineages and each with the same rate. This tree structure, now known as the Kingman coalescent, is the fundamental universality class of coalescent theory, arising in one way or another in a slew of population models. Following Kingman’s work, several now-classical works in coalescent theory
26
;
29
;
30
have described all possible scaling limits of genealogies in well-mixed population models as
\Xi
-coalescent processes, a class of trees that admit simultaneous multiple mergers.

While extremely powerful as a tool in population genetics
32
;
28
, and with rich mathematical ties to statistical physics, interacting particle systems, diffusion processes, and beyond
8
;
3
;
4
, the scaling limits of the classical theory implicitly describe the
average
genealogy at a locus. In reality, every locus in the genome shares the same history of reproductive relationships, modeled by a random graph called the pedigree. Genealogies at every place in the genome are correlated through the random structure of this pedigree. The correct starting point for the coalescent, if we wish to understand the
distribution
of gene genealogies across the genome, is to understand the law of the coalescent conditional on the pedigree. We refer to this conditional law as the law of the
quenched coalescent
. Beginning with the non-rigorous works
33
;
34
;
36
, pedigree effects were observed in the recent past and in structured populations. This includes significant differences in the site-frequency spectrum and distribution of pairwise coalescence times (see
(
36
, Figure 3)
, for example.) Recently, mathematically rigorous scaling limits have begun to emerge from quenched coalescent theory that are quite distinct from those of the classical theory.

31
established the first result for quenched coalescents, showing that in a well-mixed population in the absence of uneven offspring distributions, gene genealogies of unlinked loci really are independent in the large population limit. However, the works
10
;
1
then showed the effects of uneven offspring distributions in the absence of selfing on gene genealogies. The pedigree leaves a persistent imprint on the dynamics of these gene genealogies through prodigious progenitors, individuals whose genetic contributions are shared across the genome. The limiting quenched genealogy becomes time inhomogeneous, evolving with an ordinary Kingman-like background punctuated by rare generations in which prodigious individuals contribute a non-vanishing proportion of the genetic material of the population even in the infinite population limit. Under the same assumptions of
7
that yield an annealed multiple merger coalescent, the quenched coalescent converges to a process that is driven by a Poisson point process recording the timing and structure of exceptional reproductive generations of the pedigree. Between exceptional generations, lineages merge only in binary mergers at a constant rate. At exceptional generations, many lineages may merge simultaneously, with the merger pattern determined by the ordered offspring frequencies. This identifies the precise sense in which widely separated loci are coupled; they share the same record of macrosopic pedigree events, these prodigious progenitors. This Poisson driven viewpoint is closely related to the limiting object in the present paper.

While well-mixed populations are a natural starting point for coalescent analysis, many real population exhibit population structure. They are subdivided into demes connected by migration. In such settings, ancestral lineages carry locations and move between demes backward in time according to some migration mechanism. Coalescence can occur only when lineages occupy the same deme, with rates governed by deme sizes. This yields the structured coalescent
35
;
25
, which has become the baseline approximation for inference in spatially distributed data and metapopulations
24
;
17
. Recent literature has characterized multi-type
\Lambda
- and
\Xi
-coalescents as the most general universality classes of structured populations. These are models in which genealogies are subject to migrations that move them between types and where, within each type, the genealogies look like
\Xi
-coalescents.
11
proved convergence of a Moran model with large offspring numbers to a multi-type
\Lambda
-coalescent.
18
classifies exchangeable, consistent, and asynchronous multi-type coalescent processes as multi-type
\Lambda
-coalescents where mergers and migrations do not occur simultaneously.
23
analyzes multi-type Cannings models with conservative migration, demonstrating convergence to more general multi-type exchangeable coalescents that can admit simultaneous multiple mergers. In
27
, they study a structured population undergoing bottlenecks, proving that the limiting annealed genealogies are multi-type
\Xi
-coalescents characterized by simultaneous multiple mergers and migrations.

Much like the well-mixed case, the standard structured coalescent describes an averaged single locus genealogy. It does not condition on the single realized history of the pedigree. In structured populations this distinction becomes especially natural. Large migration waves can occur on the same time scale as coalescence and can affect many lineages at once. Such events are recorded in the pedigree and are shared across loci. They therefore create an additional source of time inhomogeneity and cross locus dependence that is invisible to purely annealed descriptions.
36
provided simulation results that demonstrated the importance of the pedigree in structured populations, where deviations from standard coalescent predictions can persist longer than in well-mixed populations due to the specific history of migration events captured by the pedigree.

1.1
The contributions of this paper

This work develops a quenched coalescent theory for a diploid structured population modeled on an arbitrary finite directed graph
G=\left(V,E\right)
. Each vertex
v
in
V
represents a deme of the population and it is kept a constant size of
\lfloor s(v)N\rfloor
backwards in time, where
N
is a population size parameter we will send to infinity. Each generation draws a random proportion
m_{(v,w)}
of the population at deme
v
to travel to deme
w
. The total population at each deme
v
then coalesces according to some exchangeable, within each deme, offspring distribution
\mathcal{V}^{v}
in a manner similar to
7
;
1
. This model differs from those of
23
;
27
by allowing migrations to be arbitrarily large and non-conservative in the sense of
22
, encoding diploidy, and allowing generic offspring frequencies, including not-necessarily independent offspring frequencies across demes, simultaneously. This model includes the models of
7
;
1
as special subcases, and generalizes the model of
23
to diploid populations and random migration sizes at each time-step. We denote the ordered offspring frequencies in each deme
v
by
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{v}
, and the collection of these over all demes by
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{v}\right)_{v\in V}
.

We show that as long as large migrations and uneven offspring distributions are suitably rare, described by the limiting joint distribution of
\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}},m\right)
satisfying some local integrability condition, the law of the coalescent conditional on the pedigree converges in distribution to a random measure governing a time inhomogeneous structured coalescent. More precisely, there is some “neutral” migration rate
\mu_{e}
along every edge
e
of
E
, and some “neutral” coalescence rate
\kappa_{v}
within each deme
v
so that any block of the coalescent in deme
v
moves independently of all the other blocks to deme
w
at rate
\mu_{v,w}
, and any pair of blocks in deme
v
coalesce independently of any of the other pairs of blocks at rate
\kappa_{v}
. At exceptional generations, governed by a Poisson point process
\Psi
, the coalescent jumps according to a paintbox construction, generalizing the Kingman
x
-paintbox described, for example, in
2
. The limiting conditional gene genealogy is described by the law of a coalescent following the neutral migration and coalescence rates, jumping according to a paintbox construction at atoms of
\Psi
, all conditional on
\Psi
. This model is a natural extension of the limiting model of
1
, where there are no migration rates and where our
\kappa_{v}
for the single deme in their model corresponds to their
c_{\text{pair}}
.

The proof proceeds by the method of moments. By a theorem of
15
, it is enough to prove that, as random variables, the finite-dimensional distributions of the coalescent
conditional on the pedigree
converge in distribution to those of the limiting quenched process. Since these conditional finite-dimensional distributions are uniformly bounded random variables, we can show convergence by showing all moments of these conditional finite-dimensional distributions converge. It will suffice to show that for
l
realizations of the coalescent conditionally independent with respect to the pedigree that the joint law converges to the same joint law as for
l
realizations of the time-inhomogeneous coalescent conditionally independent with respect to
\Psi
. The biological interpretation of the method is then as follows: the distribution of gene genealogies across unlinked loci converges in distribution to a given (random) law if and only if the
annealed
distribution of gene genealogies across
l
unlinked loci converges to that of the annealed distribution of that given law across
l
unlinked loci for any finite number of
l
unlinked loci.

We control these joint laws by constructing a coupling of the
l
conditionally independent copies on the same pedigree, in the same spirit as
1
, who built on earlier work of
9
;
6
. The coupling makes transparent the two mechanisms that drive the limit. Firstly, genuinely
large
pedigree events that change the coalescent state, such as macroscopic migration waves or unusually large families, are rare on the coalescent time scale. However, they affect all copies in nearly the same way. In particular, the different copies jump to corresponding new states with asymptotically matching probabilities. Second, during the overwhelming majority of
small
generations, the copies experience the “neutral” migration patterns described above, and their jump decisions become asymptotically independent across copies. This approximate independence follows from straightforward moment bounds. These two observations form the basis for a “separation of scales” argument in which we bridge these two regimes to demonstrate the desired convergence of the joint law. We note here that there is an implicit separation of scales argument in the
\varepsilon
-naive-coalescent of
1
, though the total form of the argument is distinct.

1.2
Organization of the paper

The paper is organized into sections as follows. We begin in Section
2
by introducing a diploid bi-parental population model for a finite structured population with size scaling with a parameter
N
. This population model gives rise to a random graph
\mathcal{G}_{N}
, called the pedigree, that tracks the total history of reproductive relationships in the population. We proceed then to describe how the ancestral process, which tracks how a sample of size
n
traces its genetic inheritance in the population backwards in time, is determined by the structure of a family of coalescing random walks on
\mathcal{G}_{N}\times\{0,1\}
. The law of this ancestral process, conditional on the pedigree, is the central object of focus in this work. In Section
3
we describe the annealed limit of the ancestral process. It is here that we outline the essential assumptions necessary for our main theorem to hold. This annealed result follows as a corollary of the main result and its proof is contained in Section
B
.

In Section
4
we describe the limit of the law of the ancestral process conditional on the pedigree. There is a Poisson point process
\Psi
that governs the joint distribution of offspring frequencies and migration proportions between generations and a coalescent process
\chi^{n}
driven by
\Psi
as follows: Between atoms of
\Psi
the ancestral process evolves as in the classical structured coalescent. That is, lineages move independently along an edge at a given rate
\mu_{e}
and pairs of lineages in the same deme
v
coalesce at a given rate
\kappa_{v}
. At the atoms of
\Psi
, the transition matrix for the ancestral process is described by means of a paintbox construction, akin to that of the Kingman paintbox
2
. This paintbox corresponds, in the finite population model, to the existence of migrations on the order of the size of the total population or to prodigious progenitors who contribute a positive proportion of the total populations genetic information. The limiting
quenched
law is then the law of
\chi^{n}
conditional on
\Psi
. Because
\Psi
may in general have infinitely many atoms, we rigorously construct a pathwise description of the time-inhomogeneous coalescent built on
\Psi
in Section
7
.

In Section
5
we outline the proof of the main result. This consists of a series of lemmas making the method of moments and separation of scales argument rigorous. The proofs of these lemmas are largely postponed to Section
8
. Therein we rigorously describe how to couple
l
realizations of the discrete-time ancestral process on the same pedigree, why the small-scale behavior of our model looks like the classical structured coalescent picture, and why the large-scale behavior of our model affects all of the
l
realizations in, asymptotically, the same manner. It also contains a separation of timescales argument that allows us to simplify the state space of our coupling.

In Section
6
we demonstrate convergence of the conditional gene genealogies for several examples discrete-time population models of interest. The examples include a two deme Wright-Fisher model with neutral migration analogous to the model of
36
, a two deme model analogous to that of
10
, a model converging to a beta coalescent with beta distributed migrations, and what we call a discrete approximation of a spatial
\Xi
Fleming-Viot genealogies on the
2
-torus.

In Section
9
, we contrast our results with the work in
36
and provide a possible way to harmonize our paper and theirs. We then quickly describe some opportunities for future work.

The population dynamics described in Section

Definition 2.1

The pedigree captures the total population history shared across the genome, coupling genealogies at loci that may be arbitrarily far apart or lie on distinct chromosomes. Classical coalescent theory

;

5
The proof of Theorem
4.3

The proof essentially proceeds by the method of moments. This is possible, due to a corollary of
15
, which guarantees that quenched convergence in finite-dimensional distribution of càdlàg paths is enough to guarantee weak convergence in distribution of quenched laws when the space the paths take values in are compact. We make this reasoning precise in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

For each
l\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{\overline{\chi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
and
\{\chi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
denote realizations of
\overline{\chi}^{N,n}
and
\chi^{n}
, respectively, that are conditionally independent with respect to
\mathcal{A}_{N}
and
\Psi
, respectively. If, for each
l
in
\mathbb{N}
,
\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
converges in finite-dimensional distribution to
\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
, then
\mathbb{P}\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\right)\in\cdot\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)
converges weakly in distribution to
\mathbb{P}\left(\chi^{n}\in\cdot\,\mid\,\Psi\right)
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
is a compact metric space, by Corollary 6.9 of
15
it suffices to show that, for any vector
\vec{t}=\left(t_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{r}
of ascending times and any vector of states
\vec{\xi}=\left(\xi_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{r}
, that

\mathbb{P}\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\right)\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{d}}}}\,\mathbb{P}\left(\chi^{n}\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})\,\mid\,\Psi\right),

(5.1)

where
C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})
is the cylinder set

C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi}):=\{x\in\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)\right):x(t_{i})=\xi_{i}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq r\}.

(5.2)

As
\mathbb{P}\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\right)\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)
is a sequence uniformly bounded random variables, the convergence in (
5.1
) follows if

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)^{l}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left(\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{l}\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})^{l}\right)

converges as

goes to infinity to

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\chi^{n}\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})\,\mid\,\Psi\right)^{l}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left(\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})^{l}\right)

for any finite

.
This follows immediately from convergence in finite-dimensional distribution of the
\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
to
\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
.
∎

By Lemma
5.1
, it suffices therefore to show that
\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{l}
converges in finite-dimensional distribution to
\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
. To this end, we describe a precise coupling of the
\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}
in Section
8
. The coupling is such that the conditionally independent realizations are given by random walks on the
same
pedigree, but where the choice of edges traversed by the blocks in one realization are independent to those of another. In biological terms, the
l

l

O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
while the time-scale that any of two lineages of among the
l

O_{\mathbb{P}}((c_{N}^{v_{0}})^{-1})
, there is a separation of timescales so that we only need to consider jumps of
\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
in

after projecting via the complete dispersion map

. This is made precise in Section

To demonstrate joint convergence of the realizations, we utilize a separation of scales argument. To this end, we fix
\varepsilon>0
. When we restrict ourselves to small enough offspring distributions and migrations, i.e. when
(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(k),m(k))\in B(\varepsilon)
, the jumps of each of the
\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}
are approximately independent. This is made precise by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose, as
N\to\infty
, that Assumptions
3.3
,
3.7
, and
3.12
hold. Let
R
be the infinitesimal generator of
l
joint, independent
(0,\kappa,\mu)
-
n
-coalescents, viewed as an
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}\times\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
rate matrix. Let
A_{N}:=e^{c_{N}^{v_{0}}R}
. Then there is a continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
f(0)=0
and

\left|\left|P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}-A_{N}\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq f(\varepsilon)c_{N}^{v_{0}}\text{\hskip 10.22217ptfor any $\epsilon>0$},

where
P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}
is the one-step transition matrix

\left(\mathbb{P}\left(\left(cd_{V}\left(\chi_{i}^{N,n}(1)\right)\right)_{i=1}^{l}=\vec{\eta}\,\mid\,\left(\chi_{i}^{N,n}(0)\right)_{i=1}^{l}=\vec{\xi},(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(0),m(0))\in B(\varepsilon)\right)\right)_{\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}.

Lemma
5.2
is proven in Section
8.3
.

At the same time, when we restrict ourselves to the rare jumps where
(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(k),m(k))\in B(\varepsilon)^{c}
we have that the transitions of the conditionally independent realizations are really asymptotically independent.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose, as
N\to\infty
, that Assumptions
3.2
,
3.3
, and
3.4
hold. Define the transition probability matrix
H_{N,l}:\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\to[0,1]^{\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}\times\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}
by

H_{N,l}(x,m):=\left(\mathbb{P}\left(\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}(k+1)\right)\right)_{i=1}^{l}=\vec{\eta}\,\mid\,\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}(k)\right)_{i=1}^{l}=\vec{\xi},(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(k),m(k))=(x,m)\right)\right)_{\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}.

Define the transition matrix
H_{l}:\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\to[0,1]^{\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}\times\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}
by

H_{l}(x,m):=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}q_{n}(x,m)(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\right)_{\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}.

Then for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\frac{1}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}\left|H_{N,l}(x,m)-H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right|_{\infty}\,d\Phi_{N}(x,m)\to 0

as
N
goes to infinity.

Lemma
5.3
is proven in Section
8.4
.

Remark 5.4
.

Here we use a truncation argument to bound the total proportion of migrants from any deme away from
1
using Assumption
3.12
. We believe this truncation argument may be improced to remove this assumption, though this is not undertaken in the present work.

Combining the two scales of Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
allows us to conclude that the transition kernel of the joint process
\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{m}
is arbitrarily close to that of a Markov process with infinitesimal generator

\mathcal{L}:=R+\int(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I)d\Phi(x,m),

(5.3)

which is the meaning of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose, as
N\to\infty
, that Assumptions
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.7
, and
3.12
hold. Let
P_{N,l}(t)
denote the transition kernel of
\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{l}
at time
t
. Then, for any fixed
T>0
, there is a continuous function
g_{T}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
g(0)=0
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left|\left|P_{N,l}(t)-\exp\left(t\mathcal{L}\right)\right|\right|_{\infty}\leq g_{T}(\varepsilon)

(5.4)

Lemma
5.5
is proven in Section
8.5
.

Finally, we characterize the annealed joint law of
l
realizations
\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
of a
\Psi
-driven
n
-
(\kappa,\mu)
-coalescent
\chi^{n}
that are conditionally independent with respect to
\Psi
. We show that
\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
has infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
\Phi
satisfies the regularity condition of Assumption
3.10
. Then
\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
, as a
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
-valued Markov process, has infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}
.

Lemma
5.6
is proven in Section

.

With the above lemmas, we are now able to present the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
it suffices to show that, for any
l
in
\mathbb{N}
that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{l}\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})^{l}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}\in C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})^{l}\right),

where
C(\vec{t},\vec{\xi})
is the cylinder set of Equation (
5.2
) and
\chi_{i}^{N}
and
\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}
are as in the statement of the lemma.

By Lemma
5.6
, the annealed joint process
\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
has infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}
. As
\left(\chi_{i}^{n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
has infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}
we know it has transition kernel
P_{l}(t):=\exp(t\mathcal{L})
. Under Assumptions
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
, and
3.7
, we have that Lemma
5.5
holds. As the transition kernel
P_{N,l}(t)
of
\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{l}
satisfies Equation (
5.4
) it follows that
P_{N,l}
converges uniformly on any compact interval to
P_{l}
by taking
N
to infinity and then
\varepsilon
to zero. This, and convergence of the initial condition given by Assumption
3.1
is enough to demonstrate convergence in finite-dimensional distribution, as was needed.
∎

In this section, we provide various examples of models of interest. These include a two-deme Wright-Fisher model with neutral migration in Section

6.1
A two-deme Wright-Fisher model with neutral migration

We describe here the discrete-time model of
36
, which is a two deme Wright-Fisher model with conservative neutral migration. Concretely, we take

V=\{1,2\},\qquad E=\{(1,2),(2,1)\},\qquad s(1)=s(2)=1,

and we sample the conservative migration sizes

m_{(1,2)}=m_{(2,1)}\sim\frac{1}{N}\text{\emph{Binomial}}\left(N;\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)

for some fixed
\sigma>0
. Since migration is conservative, at each time-step we have exactly
N
diploid individuals in each deme reproduce. Conditional on
m
, reproduction within each deme is Wright-Fisher with uniform weights and no selfing, and reproduction across demes is independent. Equivalently,

(\mathcal{V}^{v})_{v\in V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\text{\emph{Multinomial}}\left(N;\frac{2}{N(N-1)},\ldots,\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\right)^{\otimes 2}.

Convergence of this model is established by the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\overline{\chi}^{N,n}
and
\mathcal{G}_{N}
denote the ancestral process and pedigree associated to a two-deme Wright-Fisher model with neutral migration. Let
\kappa=\left(1,1\right)
and
\mu_{(1,2)}=\mu_{(2,1)}=\mu
. Suppose that, as
N
goes to infinity, that Assumption
3.1
holds. Then
\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\in\cdot\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)
converges weakly in distribution to the law of a
(0,\kappa,\mu)
-
n
-coalescent.

Proof.

Under this model the pair-coalescence scale is the usual Wright-Fisher scale: for every
v\in V
and every
N
,

c_{N}^{v}=\frac{1}{2N}.

In particular, Assumptions
3.2
and
3.3
hold. Moreover, since
Nm_{e}
is binomially distributed with
N
trials and success probability
\frac{\sigma}{2}
and
c_{N}^{v}=1/(2N)
, a direct calculation yields
\bar{\mu}_{e}=\sigma
for every
e\in E
, so Assumption
3.7
holds as well.

Finally, because reproduction within each deme is Wright-Fisher and is independent across demes,
(
7
, Proposition 2.1)
and Corollary
A.3
imply that
\frac{1}{c_{N}^{1}}\Phi_{N}
converges vaguely on
\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}
to

\Phi\;=\;0^{\otimes 2}\otimes\delta_{0}^{\otimes 2}.

Therefore the main assumptions necessary for Theorem
4.3
hold. Since
\Phi
charges no sets bounded away from
\mathbf{0}_{V,E}
, the limiting driving point process
\Psi
has no atoms. Consequently the quenched and annealed laws coincide in this example.
∎

In Section
9
we discuss implications of Proposition
6.1
to the work of
36
.

6.2
A two-deme modified Wright-Fisher model with large offspring numbers

Here we discuss a two-deme analogue of the model of
10
. We take

V=\{1,2\},\qquad E=\{(1,2),(2,1)\},\qquad s(1)=s(2)=1,

so that each deme has size
N(1)=N(2)=N
. We assume that reproduction dynamics in the two
demes are independent conditional on
m
.

Fix parameters
\sigma>0
,
\phi>0
,
\psi\in(0,1)
, and
\gamma>0
. Write
N_{\gamma}:=N\wedge N^{\gamma}
and sample the migrations by

m_{(1,2)}=m_{(2,1)}\sim\frac{1}{N_{\gamma}}\text{\emph{Binomial}}\left(N;\frac{N_{\gamma}}{N}\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)

This migration is conservative, so
N^{*}(1)=N^{*}(2)=N
for every time-step.

We now describe the offspring matrices in a single deme
v\in\{1,2\}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{N}:=2\phi/N^{\gamma}
. Conditional on
m
, we first sample a Bernoulli variable

B^{v}
with

\mathbb{P}\{B^{v}=1\}=\varepsilon_{N}.

If
B^{v}=0
(a “small” generation), the deme reproduces by Wright-Fisher dynamics, i.e. we have

(\mathcal{V}^{v})_{v\in V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\text{\emph{Multinomial}}\left(N;\frac{2}{N(N-1)},\ldots,\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\right)^{\otimes 2}

If
B^{v}=1
(a “large” generation), we sample an unordered pair

\{I^{v},J^{v}\}\subset[N]
uniformly at random and set

\mathcal{V}^{v}_{I^{v},J^{v}}=\lfloor\psi N\rfloor.

The remaining
N-\lfloor\psi N\rfloor
individuals in the deme choose parental couples uniformly at random from the
\binom{N}{2}-1
remaining possible couples, i.e. reproduce via Wright-Fisher.

We define the necessary elements of the scaling limit of this model as follows: Set

x_{\psi}:=\left(\frac{\psi}{4},\frac{\psi}{4},\frac{\psi}{4},\frac{\psi}{4},0,0,\ldots\right)\in\Delta,

and define
x^{(1)},x^{(2)}\in\Delta^{V}
by

x^{(1),1}=x_{\psi},\quad x^{(1),2}=\mathbf{0},\qquad x^{(2),1}=\mathbf{0},\quad x^{(2),2}=x_{\psi}.

Then

\Phi_{\gamma}:=\lambda_{\gamma}\left(\delta_{\left(x^{(1)},0_{E}\right)}+\delta_{\left(x^{(2)},0_{E}\right)}\right),\qquad\lambda_{\gamma}:=\begin{cases}0,&\gamma>1,\\
\dfrac{4\phi}{1+\phi\psi^{2}},&\gamma=1,\\
\dfrac{4}{\psi^{2}},&0<\gamma<1,\end{cases}

and the Kingman and migration parameters are

\kappa_{\gamma}:=\begin{cases}\left(1,1\right),&\gamma>1,\\
\left(\dfrac{1}{1+\phi\psi^{2}},\dfrac{1}{1+\phi\psi^{2}}\right),&\gamma=1,\\
\left(0,0\right),&0<\gamma<1,\end{cases}\qquad\mu_{\gamma,(1,2)}=\mu_{\gamma,(2,1)}:=\begin{cases}\mu,&\gamma>1,\\
\dfrac{\mu}{1+\phi\psi^{2}},&\gamma=1,\\
0,&0<\gamma<1.\end{cases}

Write
\mu_{\gamma}=\left(\mu_{\gamma,e}\right)_{e\in E}
.

Convergence of this model is established by the following proposition.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\overline{\chi}^{N,n}
and
\mathcal{G}_{N}
denote the ancestral process and pedigree of a two-deme Wright-Fisher model with large offspring numbers. Let
\chi^{n}
denote a
\Psi_{\gamma}
driven
(\kappa_{\gamma},\mu_{\gamma})
-
n
coalescent, where
\Psi_{\gamma}
has intensity measure
dt\otimes(h_{V})_{*}\Phi_{\gamma}
. Suppose that, as
N
goes to infinity, that Assumption
3.1
holds. Then

\mathbb{P}\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\right)\in\cdot\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{d}}}}\,\mathbb{P}\left(\chi^{n}\in\cdot\,\mid\,\Psi_{\gamma}\right).

Proof.

Note by symmetry that

c_{N}^{1}=c_{N}^{2}=:c_{N}
.
By conditioning on
B^{v}\in\{0,1\}
we can compute
c_{N}
from

c_{N}=\frac{1}{8}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}\left(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}-1\right)}{N-1}\right]=\frac{1}{8}\left((1-\varepsilon_{N})\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}\left(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}-1\right)}{N-1}\,\,\mid\,\,B^{v}=0\right]+\varepsilon_{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}\left(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}-1\right)}{N-1}\,\,\mid\,\,B^{v}=1\right]\right).

In a small generation
B^{v}=0
, each of the
N
offspring chooses an unordered parental couple uniformly,
so
\mathbb{P}\{1\in\{I,J\}\}=2/N
and hence
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}\mid\{B^{v}=0\}\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(N,2/N\right)
.
Therefore

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}\left(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}-1\right)\,\,\mid\,\,B^{v}=0\right]=N\left(N-1\right)\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{2}=\frac{4\left(N-1\right)}{N},

and consequently

\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}\left(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}-1\right)}{N-1}\,\,\mid\,\,B^{v}=0\right]=\frac{1}{2N}.

(6.1)

In a large generation
B^{v}=1
, we have
\mathbb{P}\{1\in\{I^{v},J^{v}\}\}=2/N
and, on this event,
individual
1
is a parent of
\lfloor\psi N\rfloor
offspring, so

\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}=\lfloor\psi N\rfloor+O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
and hence

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}\left(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}-1\right)}{N-1}\,\,\mid\,\,B^{v}=1,\;1\in\{I^{v},J^{v}\}\right]=\psi^{2}N+O(1).

On the complementary event
\{1\notin\{I^{v},J^{v}\}\}
, we still have
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
, and so the same
quantity is
O\left(1/N\right)
.
Putting this together yields

\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}\left(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{v}-1\right)}{N-1}\,\,\mid\,\,B^{v}=1\right]=\frac{\psi^{2}}{4}+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right).

(6.2)

Combining equations (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) with
\varepsilon_{N}=2\phi/N^{\gamma}
,
we obtain the sharper asymptotic

c_{N}=\frac{1}{2N}+\frac{\phi\psi^{2}}{2N^{\gamma}}+o\left(\frac{1}{N^{\gamma}}\right).

(6.3)

In particular
c_{N}\to 0
, and Assumptions
3.2
and
3.3
hold.

By a direct computation one obtains

\mu_{\gamma,(1,2)}=\mu_{\gamma,(2,1)}
as stated in the proposition. Hence
Assumption
3.7
holds.

It remains to identify the vague limit in Assumption
3.4
.
In a small generation,
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{v}
is Wright-Fisher reproduction, so its contribution vanishes on

\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{V,E}\}
by
(
7
, Proposition 2.1)
.
In a large generation in deme
v
, the
\lfloor\psi N\rfloor
offspring of the prolific couple inherit,
at a fixed locus, one of the two parental copies from each of the two parents independently; thus the
four parental copies each contribute
\mathrm{Bin}\left(\lfloor\psi N\rfloor,1/2\right)
gametes, and after
normalisation by
2N
this yields the limit
x_{\psi}
in
\Delta
.
Since large generations occur with probability
\varepsilon_{N}
independently in the two demes, the event
of simultaneous large generations has probability
\varepsilon_{N}^{2}=o(c_{N})
, and does not contribute in
the
1/c_{N}
scaling.
Consequently,

\frac{1}{c_{N}}\Phi_{N}\;\to\;\lambda_{\gamma}\left(\delta_{\left(x^{(1)},0_{E}\right)}+\delta_{\left(x^{(2)},0_{E}\right)}\right)\qquad\text{vaguely on }\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{V,E}\},

with

\lambda_{\gamma}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\varepsilon_{N}/c_{N}

given by (
6.3
). This is exactly Assumption
3.4
with
\Phi=\Phi_{\gamma}
.

All hypotheses of Theorem
4.3
are therefore satisfied, and the stated quenched convergence
follows.
∎

6.3
A two-deme model with random individual fitness and large migrations

We extend the model with random individual fitness from
7
to a two deme model. We take
V=\{1,2\}
,
E=\{(1,2),(2,1)\}
, and
s(1)=s(2)=1
. We will take migration to be conservative, and will describe the precise migration patters of the model after we introduce the random fitness reproduction mechanism.

We now specify the joint law of the offspring matrices and the migration proportions in each generation.
Fix
\alpha\in(1,2)
, and let
W
be a nonnegative random variable with

\mathbb{P}\left(W\geq z\right)\sim c_{W}z^{-\alpha}\qquad\text{as }z\to\infty

for some constant
c_{W}\in(0,\infty)
, and with
\mathbb{E}\left[W\right]>0
. Let
\left(W_{i}^{v}\right)_{i=1}^{N}
be independent copies of
W
that are also independent across demes. Define

Z_{N}^{v}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}W_{i}^{v}W_{j}^{v}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}W_{i}^{v}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(W_{i}^{v})^{2}.

Given
\left(W_{i}^{v}\right)_{i=1}^{N}
, we let

(\mathcal{V}^{v})_{v\in V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\text{\emph{Multinomial}}\left(N;\frac{W_{1}^{v}W_{2}^{v}}{Z_{N}^{v}},\frac{W_{2}^{v}W_{3}^{v}}{Z_{N}^{v}},\ldots\frac{W_{N-1}^{v}W_{N}^{v}}{Z_{N}^{v}}\right)^{\otimes 2}

We describe now the conservative migration patterns of the model. Fix parameters
a,b>0
. We let
m_{(1,2)}=m_{(2,1)}=m
and sample
m
as

m\sim\begin{cases}\delta_{0}&,\text{ with probability }1-N^{1-\alpha}c_{W}\left(\frac{2}{\mathbb{E}[W]}\right)^{\alpha}\alpha\frac{\Gamma(2-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}{8}\\
\text{\emph{Beta}}(a,b)&,\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

We define now the relevant limiting objects of this model as follows:
Define

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=c_{W}\left(\frac{2}{\mathbb{E}[W]}\right)^{\alpha}\alpha\,\frac{\Gamma(2-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}{8},

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle:=\left(0,0\right),\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mu_{(1,2)}=\mu_{(2,1)}

\displaystyle:=0.

Define, for
y\in(0,1)
,

x_{y}:=\left(\frac{y}{2},\frac{y}{2},0,0,\ldots\right)\in\Delta,\qquad x_{y}^{(1),1}=x_{y},\;x_{y}^{(1),2}=\mathbf{0},\qquad x_{y}^{(2),1}=\mathbf{0},\;x_{y}^{(2),2}=x_{y}.

Let
\Lambda_{\alpha}
denote the
\mathrm{Beta}(2-\alpha,\alpha)
probability measure on
(0,1)
, i.e.

\Lambda_{\alpha}(dy)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\,y^{1-\alpha}(1-y)^{\alpha-1}\,dy,\qquad\nu_{\alpha}(dy):=\frac{\Lambda_{\alpha}(dy)}{y^{2}}.

Then we set

\Phi=\Phi^{\mathrm{fit}}+\Phi^{\mathrm{mig}},

where

\Phi^{\mathrm{fit}}:=c_{\alpha}\int_{(0,1)}\left(\delta_{\left(x_{y}^{(1)},0_{E}\right)}+\delta_{\left(x_{y}^{(2)},0_{E}\right)}\right)\,\nu_{\alpha}(dy),

and

\Phi^{\mathrm{mig}}:=\int_{[0,1]}\delta_{\left(\mathbf{0}_{V},(y,y)\right)}\frac{\Gamma(a+b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}y^{1-a}(1-y)^{1-b}dy.

Convergence of this model is then established by the following proposition.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\overline{\chi}^{N,n}
and
\mathcal{G}_{N}
denote the ancestral process and pedigree of a two-deme model with random individual fitness and large migrations.
Let
\chi^{n}
denote a
\Psi
-driven
(\kappa,\mu)
-
n
-coalescent where
\Psi
has intensity measure
dt\otimes d(h_{V})_{*}\Phi
. Suppose that, as
N
goes to infinity, that Assumption
3.1
holds. Then

\mathbb{P}\left(cd_{V}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\right)\in\cdot\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{d}}}}\,\mathbb{P}\left(\chi^{n}\in\cdot\,\mid\,\Psi\right).

Proof.

By symmetry we have that
c_{N}^{1}=c_{N}^{2}:=c_{N}
, where
c_{N}
is given by

c_{N}\sim c_{W}\left(\frac{2}{\mathbb{E}[W]}\right)^{\alpha}\alpha\,\frac{\Gamma(2-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}{8}\,N^{1-\alpha}

by
(
7
, Lemma 2.2)
.
In particular, Assumptions
3.2
and

3.3
hold. Moreover, since
c_{N}\gg 1/N
for
\alpha\in(1,2)
, the Kingman
contribution disappears under the
1/c_{N}
time-rescaling, hence
\kappa=\left(0,0\right)
.

We now identify the vague limit in Assumption
3.4
.
In the one-deme random-fitness model, the sequence of intensity measures
(\Phi_{N})_{v}
associated with the
ordered offspring frequencies in each deme
v
converges by
(
7
, Proposition 2.3)
to

\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{x_{y}}\text{\emph{Beta}}(\alpha,2-\alpha)(dy),

reflecting that a single highly fit parent contributes a
macroscopic fraction
y
of the parental gametes at a locus, split equally between its two copies.

Finally, consider migration. By construction,

\mathbb{P}\{m\neq 0\}=N^{1-\alpha}c_{W}\left(\frac{2}{\mathbb{E}[W]}\right)^{\alpha}\alpha\,\frac{\Gamma(2-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}{8}=c_{N},\qquad\mathbb{P}\{m=0\}=1-c_{N},

and
m_{(1,2)}=m_{(2,1)}=m
. In particular, by construction it is clear that

\frac{1}{c_{N}}\left(\Phi_{N}\right)_{e}\to\text{\emph{Beta}}(a,b)

on
(0,1]
.

Since we sample the migration and reproduction events independently, it follows from Corollary
A.3
that
\frac{1}{c_{N}}\Phi_{N}
converges vaguely to
\Phi
, as described.

All hypotheses of Theorem
4.3
are satisfied, and the claimed quenched convergence follows.
∎

6.4
A discrete approximation of a toroidal spatial
\Xi

We describe here a discrete approximation of a
\Xi
Fleming-Viot genealogy. The
\Lambda
Fleming-Viot coalescent
\chi^{n}
of
12
can be described as follows: Fix a measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
so that
\int rd\nu(r)<\infty
. Let
\Gamma
denote a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with intensity measure

. We say that an atom

\left|\left|x-y\right|\right|<r
. We couple

\mathbb{R}^{2}

as follows:

•

between intersections of any of the
\left(\widetilde{W}_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}
, they processes evolve as if they were independent Brownian motions;

•

if two of these processes coalesce, they remain identified together for all time;

•

for any atom
(t,x,r)
of
\Gamma
,

is sampled from some distribution on

and
j

are selected uniformly at random in

. All of the particles that are affected by

instantaneously jump to one of these points uniformly at random.

\chi^{n}=\left(\pi^{n},x^{n}\right)
is then an
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
-valued Markov process, where

is the equivalence relation

i\sim_{\pi^{n}(t)}j\text{\quad if }\widetilde{W}_{i}(t)=\widetilde{W}_{j}(t),

and

is the position of the particles corresponding to the

.

We discretely approximate this process on the
2
-Torus as follows:
Let

denote a discretized

-Torus with latitude and longitude of lengths

and

in
\mathbb{N}
, respectively. Define the edge set to be

. For convenience we set
s(v)=1
for all
v
in
V
. We fix parameters
\rho,\sigma,\gamma>0
,
a_{v},b_{v}>0
for each deme

in
V
, and a finite measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\int rd\nu(r)<\infty
.
\rho
will correspond to a scaling of the lattice and

to migration rates.
\gamma
and

will relate to the intensity of the analogue

described above for the spatial
\Lambda
Fleming-Viot process. We sample

as follows:

•

With probability

we set

\displaystyle m_{(v,w)}=m_{(w,v)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{N}\text{\emph{Binomial}}\left(N;\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\text{ if $v$ is a nearest neighbor to $w$}

\displaystyle m_{(v,w)}=m_{(w,v)}

\displaystyle=0,\text{ if $v$ and $w$ are not nearest neighbors, and}

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{V}^{v}\right)_{v\in V}

\displaystyle\sim\text{Multinomial}\left(N;\frac{2}{N(N-1)},\ldots,\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\right)^{\otimes L_{1}\times L_{2}}.

That is, we have conservative migration between nearest neighbors and the reproduction within each deme is Wright-Fisher.

•

With probability

uniformly at random from

and sample

. A deme

is then selected uniformly at random from the collection of demes

(\mathcal{V},m)

For all demes

\displaystyle m_{(w,w^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=0\text{ for all }w^{\prime}\neq w\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{w}

\displaystyle\sim\text{Multinomial}\left(N;\frac{2}{N(N-1)},\ldots,\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\right).

That is, demes far enough from

\displaystyle m_{(w,w^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\sim\begin{cases}\delta_{0},\text{ if }w^{\prime}\neq v^{*}\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{w}

\displaystyle\sim\text{Multinomial}\left(N;\frac{2}{N^{*}(w)(N^{*}(w)-1)},\ldots,\frac{2}{N^{*}(w)(N^{*}(w)-1)}\right).

That is, demes within distance

Inside the deme

\displaystyle m_{(v^{*},w)}=0

\displaystyle,\text{ for all }w\neq v^{*},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}^{v^{*}}

\displaystyle\sim\widetilde{\Phi}_{N},

where we assume that

converges weakly in

.

Marked driving process and state-dependent thinnings

Let
\Psi
be a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\left(\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\right)
with intensity

, where

satisfies Assumption

, i.e. for each

\int_{\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}}\left(1-q_{n}(x,m)(\xi,\xi)\right)\,d\Phi\left(x,m\right)<\infty.

Since the time-marginal of

is nonatomic,
\Psi
has almost surely no simultaneous atom times.

We now attach auxiliary marks to each atom of
\Psi
. Let
\widehat{\Psi}
be a Poisson point process on

\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\left(\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\right)\times[0,1]^{\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}\times[0,1]

with intensity measure

dt\otimes d\Phi\left(x,m\right)\otimes\lambda_{\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}(du)\otimes\lambda(dv),

where
\lambda_{\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}
denotes Lebesgue measure on

denotes Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
.
We write atoms of
\widehat{\Psi}
as
(t,(x,m),u,v)
, where
u=\left(u^{\xi}\right)_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}
is a vector of auxiliary points uniformly sampled on the unit interval.

For each
\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
define the state-dependent thinning

\Psi_{\xi}:=\left\{(t,(x,m)):\exists\,u,v\ \text{s.t.}\ (t,(x,m),u,v)\in\widehat{\Psi}\ \text{and}\ u^{\xi}\leq 1-q_{n}(x,m)(\xi,\xi)\right\}.

(7.1)

Lemma 7.1

(Poisson thinnings and local finiteness)
.

For each
\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
,
\Psi_{\xi}
is a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\left(\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\right)
with intensity measure

dt\otimes\left(1-q_{n}(x,m)(\xi,\xi)\right)\,d\Phi(x,m).

In particular, for every
T<\infty
,

\#\left(\Psi_{\xi}\cap\left([0,T]\times\left(\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\right)\right)\right)<\infty\qquad\text{a.s.}

Moreover, since
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
is finite for fixed
n
, the union

\Psi^{\star}:=\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}\Psi_{\xi}

has almost surely finitely many atoms in
[0,T]\times\left(\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\right)
for each
T<\infty
.

Proof.

The finiteness on compacts follows from Assumption
3.10
and the fact that a Poisson random variable with finite mean is almost surely finite. The final claim follows because
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
is finite, hence
\Psi^{\star}
is a finite superposition of locally finite point processes.
∎

Fix an enumeration
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)=\{\xi^{(1)},\dots,\xi^{(M)}\}
.
For each

and each

, define a measurable map

by setting, for

where

is the least index such that

If

may be defined arbitrarily.

Using

, define the random map

by

F_{x,m,u,v}(\xi):=\begin{cases}\xi,&u^{\xi}>1-q_{n}(x,m)(\xi,\xi),\\
J_{x,m,\xi}(v),&u^{\xi}\leq 1-q_{n}(x,m)(\xi,\xi).\end{cases}

(7.2)

-tuple
of copies requires additional
bookkeeping. One must remember which ancestral diploid individuals are shared by different copies,
since this is where the common pedigree creates correlations after averaging over
\mathcal{A}_{N}
.

We first fix notation for the state space. For any set
M
, write
\mathcal{P}(M)
for its power set and

\mathcal{P}_{1,2}(M)\;:=\;\left[A\in\mathcal{P}(M):\#A\in\{1,2\}\right].

Define

\mathcal{C}_{n,l}\;:=\;\mathcal{P}([n])^{l}\setminus\left[(\varnothing,\ldots,\varnothing)\right].

An element
C=(C^{(1)},\ldots,C^{(l)})\in\mathcal{C}_{n,l}
represents the contents of
l
unlinked loci on a single ancestral chromosome that
carries ancestral material for copy
j
from the block
C^{(j)}
. Any given component may be empty,
but not all components are simultaneously empty.

A single ancestral
diploid individual
can carry one or two such chromosomes. We therefore encode
an ancestral individual by an element
I\in\mathcal{P}_{1,2}(\mathcal{C}_{n,l})
. We also record the deme
in which this individual lives. This leads to the state space

\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)\;:=\;\left\{\xi\subset V\times\mathcal{P}_{1,2}(\mathcal{C}_{n,l})\,:\,\bigsqcup_{(v,I)\in\xi}\;\bigsqcup_{C\in I}C^{(j)}=[n]\text{ for all }j\in[l]\right\},

where
\sqcup
denotes disjoint union, and we interpret
C^{(j)}=\varnothing
as contributing nothing to
the union. The disjoint-union conditions say that, in each copy
j
, every sample index appears in
exactly one block across all chromosomes of all ancestral individuals.

Given
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)
, the induced configuration of copy
j
is obtained by projection to the

j
th component. More precisely, define maps

pr_{j}:\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)\to\mathcal{S}_{n}(V),\qquad j\in[l],

as follows. For
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)
and a fixed
(v,I)\in\xi
, the element
pr_{j}(\xi)
contains,
in deme
v
, the same grouping into one or two chromosomes, but with each chromosome

C=(C^{(1)},\ldots,C^{(l)})\in I
replaced by the single block
C^{(j)}
and with empty blocks removed.
In particular,
pr_{j}(\xi)
records which blocks of copy
j
are in the same diploid individual, together
with the deme label of that individual.

We then apply the complete-dispersion map
cd_{V}
from the single-locus state space to forget the
short-lived pairing information within individuals. We extend
cd_{V}
to
\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)
by

cd_{V}(\xi)\;:=\;\left(cd_{V}\!\circ\,pr_{1}(\xi),\ldots,cd_{V}\!\circ\,pr_{l}(\xi)\right)\;\in\;\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}.

We call
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)
completely dispersed if
\xi
carries no information beyond its

l
complete dispersions, in the sense that each chromosome carries ancestral material from exactly one
copy. Equivalently,
\xi
lies in the image of the canonical embedding

\iota:\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}\to\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V),

defined by placing every block of copy
j
on its own chromosome of type

(\varnothing,\ldots,\varnothing,C,\varnothing,\ldots,\varnothing)
(with
C
in the
j
th slot) in the
appropriate deme.

We now construct the coupled processes. We work on a probability space that carries the pedigree

\mathcal{G}_{N}
and
l
independent collections of Mendelian randomness, one for each copy. Conditional
on
\mathcal{A}_{N}
, we build
l
families of ancestral lines by running the
l
copies through the
same

pedigree realisation and using the
independent
Mendelian randomness to decide gene inheritance.
This induces
l
ancestral processes
\chi_{1}^{N,n},\ldots,\chi_{l}^{N,n}
with values in
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
, and hence an

l
-tuple process
\left(\chi_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
.
By construction,

\mathbb{P}\left(\chi_{1}^{N,n}\in\cdot\,\middle|\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)\;=\;\cdots\;=\;\mathbb{P}\left(\chi_{l}^{N,n}\in\cdot\,\middle|\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right),\qquad\chi_{1}^{N,n},\ldots,\chi_{l}^{N,n}\ \text{ are i.i.d.\ given }\ \mathcal{A}_{N}.

Finally, we lift
\left(\chi_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
to a process with values in
\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)
by keeping, at
each discrete time
k
, the collection of ancestral diploid individuals that carry ancestral material in at
least one copy, together with the allocation of the
l
copies to the (up to) two chromosomes of each such
individual. This is exactly the additional structure that remembers how the different copies share
individuals on the fixed pedigree.

We then define
\overline{\chi}_{1}^{N,n},\ldots,\overline{\chi}_{l}^{N,n}
to be the time-rescaled

\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{S}_{n}(V)\right)
-valued processes given by

\overline{\chi}_{j}^{N,n}(t)\;:=\;\chi_{j}^{N,n}\!\left(\lfloor t(c_{N}^{v_{0}})^{-1}\rfloor\right),\qquad t\geq 0,\ \ j\in[l].

8.2
Negligible time outside
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
under the annealed law

In this section, we demonstrate that it suffices to characterize the one-step transition matrix for
\left(\chi_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
from a totally dispersed element in
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
to its projection under
cd_{V}
in the next time-step, instead of in the more complicated coupling space
\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)
.
We work under the annealed law throughout this subsection. Let

\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}=\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}

be the
\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)
-valued Markov chain constructed in Section
8.1
.
Write

\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V):=\iota\left(\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}\right)\subset\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V),\qquad\pi:=cd_{V}:\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)\to\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}.

Set
h_{N}:=c_{N}^{v_{0}}
and
r_{N}:=h_{N}^{-1}
, and define

k_{N}(t):=\lfloor tr_{N}\rfloor=\bigl\lfloor t/h_{N}\bigr\rfloor,\qquad t\geq 0.

Definition 8.1

(Outside time)
.

For
T>0
define the discrete outside time up to
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
by

\mathcal{O}_{N,l}(k):=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathds{1}_{\{\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\}},

and its rescaled version on
[0,T]
by

\mathcal{O}_{N,l}(T):=h_{N}\,\mathcal{O}_{N,l}\left(k_{N}(T)\right).

Definition 8.2

(Last dispersed time)
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
define

\ell_{\mathsf{D}}(k):=\max\{0\leq j\leq k:\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\}.

For
t\geq 0
define
\ell_{\mathsf{D}}(t):=\ell_{\mathsf{D}}(k_{N}(t))
.

Definition 8.3

(Trace process on
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
)
.

Define the
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
-valued processes

Z_{N,l}(t):=\pi\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k_{N}(t))\right),\qquad\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}(t):=\pi\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(\ell_{\mathsf{D}}(t))\right),\qquad t\geq 0.

Lemma 8.4

(Uniform geometric return to
\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)
)
.

There exists
p_{n,l}\in(0,1)
depending only on
(n,l)
such that for every
N
and every

\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)\setminus\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)
,

\mathbb{P}_{\xi}\left(\tau_{\mathsf{D}}\leq 1\right)\geq p_{n,l},

where

\tau_{\mathsf{D}}:=\inf\{k\geq 0:\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k)\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\}.

In particular, there exists
\rho\in(0,1)
such that for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\sup_{N}\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)}\mathbb{P}_{\xi}(\tau_{\mathsf{D}}>r)\leq\rho^{\,r},\qquad\sup_{N}\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\tau_{\mathsf{D}}]<\infty.

Proof.

Fix
\xi\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)
. Then there exists an ancestral chromosome in
\xi
carrying ancestral
material from at least two loci. Let
J\subset[l]
be the set of loci for which that chromosome has
nonempty component. Then
|J|\geq 2
. In the previous generation, for each
j\in J
the locus
j
chooses
one of the two parental genes by an independent Bernoulli
(1/2)
coin flip. With probability at least

1/2
not all loci in
J
choose the same parental gene, which yields a configuration with strictly fewer
mixed chromosomes. Iterating this contraction and using that the number of mixed chromosomes is bounded by
a constant depending only on
(n,l)
gives a uniform geometric tail.
∎

Lemma 8.5

(Rare entrance into
\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)^{c}
)
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. There is a constant
C_{\varepsilon,n,l}<\infty
such that for every
N
and every

\xi\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(1)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\,\,\mid\,\,\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(0)=\xi,\ (\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(0),m(0))\in B(\varepsilon)\right)\leq C_{\varepsilon,n,l}\,h_{N}.

Proof.

On the event
\{(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(0),m(0))\in B(\varepsilon)\}
all offspring and migration
frequencies are uniformly small. Starting from
\xi\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)
there are at most
nl
ancestral
lines across all loci. If two lines from distinct loci share a parental diploid individual in one step,
then two sampled gene copies in the same deme necessarily choose the same parental gene copy with
probability at least a fixed constant depending only on diploidy, hence the probability of sharing a
parental individual is bounded by a constant multiple of the one-step coalescence probability in that
deme. By the definition of
c_{N}^{v}
and Assumption
3.3
,

\max_{v\in V}c_{N}^{v}\leq C\,h_{N}

for some
C<\infty
. A union bound over
O((nl)^{2})
cross-locus pairs yields the first claim.

∎

Lemma 8.6

(Outside probability at deterministic rescaled times)
.

For every
T>0
there exists
C_{T,n,l}<\infty
such that for all
N
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k_{N}(t))\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\right)\leq C_{T,n,l}\,h_{N}.

In particular,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k_{N}(t))\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\right)\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

Fix
t\leq T
and write
k=k_{N}(t)
. Define entrance times

\sigma_{1}:=\inf\{j\geq 0:\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V),\ \widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j+1)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\},

and inductively
\sigma_{q+1}:=\inf\{j>\sigma_{q}:\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V),\ \widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j+1)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\}
,
with the convention
\inf\varnothing=\infty
. For each
q
define the corresponding return time

\tau_{q}:=\inf\{j>\sigma_{q}:\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\}.

The event
\{\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\}
implies that there exists
q
such that

\sigma_{q}<k<\tau_{q}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\right)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V),\ \widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j+1)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\sup_{\xi\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)}\mathbb{P}_{\xi}(\tau_{\mathsf{D}}>k-j-1).

Lemma
8.4
gives a uniform bound

\sup_{\xi\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)}\mathbb{P}_{\xi}(\tau_{\mathsf{D}}>r)\leq\rho^{\,r}
.
Lemma
8.5
gives that the entrance probability is
O(h_{N})
uniformly in
j
.
Thus,

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\right)\leq\sum_{r\geq 1}C\,h_{N}\,\rho^{\,r}\leq C^{\prime}\,h_{N},

with constants depending only on
T,n,l
. Taking the supremum over
t\in[0,T]
yields the claim.
∎

Lemma 8.7

(Negligible rescaled outside time)
.

For every
T>0
,

\mathcal{O}_{N,l}(T)\ \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\ 0.

Proof.

By Markov’s inequality it suffices to show
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{O}_{N,l}(T)]\to 0
. Using
Definition
8.1
and Lemma
8.6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{O}_{N,l}(T)]

\displaystyle=h_{N}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{N}(T)-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\right)

\displaystyle\leq h_{N}\cdot k_{N}(T)\cdot\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k_{N}(t))\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\right)\leq T\cdot C_{T,n,l}\,h_{N},

which tends to
0
.
∎

Lemma 8.8
.

For every
T>0
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{N,l}(t)\neq\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}(t)\right)\longrightarrow 0.

Consequently, for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and any
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\in[0,T]^{r}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left(Z_{N,l}(t_{1}),\ldots,Z_{N,l}(t_{r})\right)\neq\left(\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}(t_{1}),\ldots,\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}(t_{r})\right)\right)\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

Fix
t\leq T
and write
k=k_{N}(t)
. If
Z_{N,l}(t)\neq\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}(t)
, then there exists an index

j\in\{\ell_{\mathsf{D}}(k),\ldots,k-1\}
such that
\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{N,l}(t)\neq\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\exists j\in\{\ell_{\mathsf{D}}(k),\ldots,k-1\}:\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\#\{j\leq k:\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(j)\notin\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\}\right].

The last expectation is bounded by
h_{N}^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{O}_{N,l}(T)]
, which tends to
0
by
Lemma
8.7
. This yields the first claim uniformly for
t\leq T
.
The second claim follows by a union bound over the finitely many times
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}
.
∎

Lemma 8.9

(Reduction to the trace process on
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
)
.

Let
T>0
and let
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\in[0,T]^{r}
. Then

\left(Z_{N,l}(t_{1}),\ldots,Z_{N,l}(t_{r})\right)-\left(\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}(t_{1}),\ldots,\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}(t_{r})\right)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\mathbf{0}.

In particular,
Z_{N,l}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{N,l}
have identical subsequential finite-dimensional limits.

Proof.

The claim is immediate from Lemma
8.8
.
∎

8.3
Transitions at small jumps

Inn this section we prove Lemma
5.2
. The basic structure of the argument is as follows: There exist some combinatorial quantities so that the probability of anything that is neither a single pairwise coalescence in a deme or a single migration of a lineage is
O(\varepsilon)
. At the same time, the one-step transition probabilities of single-lineage migrations and single-pair coalescences are arbitrarily close to the neutral migration and neutral coalescence rates, respectively. Combining these two observations demonstrates that the one-step transition probabilities for small-scale events is sufficiently concentrated around the neutral changes in the realizations.

We proceed now with the proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Fix
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, and write

\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}:=\{(\widetilde{V}_{N}(0),m(0))\in B(\varepsilon)\}.

Recall that
P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}
is the one-step transition matrix of the
l
-tuple

\left(\chi_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
conditioned on
\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}
:

P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}):=\mathbb{P}\left((\chi_{i}^{N,n}(1))_{i=1}^{l}=\vec{\eta}\,\,\mid\,\,(\chi_{i}^{N,n}(0))_{i=1}^{l}=\vec{\xi},\ \mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}\right),\qquad\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}.

Let
r:=K_{n}(\kappa)+M_{n}(\mu)
be the generator of a single
(0,\kappa,\mu)
-
n
-coalescent on
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
,
and let
R
be the generator of
l
joint, independent copies on
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
. Thus, for

\vec{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{l})
and
\vec{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{l})
,

R(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})=\sum_{j=1}^{l}r(\xi_{j},\eta_{j})\prod_{i\neq j}\mathds{1}_{\{\xi_{i}=\eta_{i}\}}.

(8.1)

Set
A_{N}:=e^{c_{N}^{v_{0}}R}
and endow kernels on the finite space
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
with the norm

\|P\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}\sum_{\vec{\eta}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}|P(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})|.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
is finite, the matrix exponential admits a uniform second-order remainder: there is a
finite constant
C_{R}
such that

\left|A_{N}-(I+c_{N}^{v_{0}}R)\right|_{\infty}\leq C_{R}\,(c_{N}^{v_{0}})^{2}.

(8.2)

We compare
P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}
to the first-order kernel
I+c_{N}^{v_{0}}R
by separating “good” one-step
updates from events that involve two or more elementary changes. By an elementary change we simply mean the moving of a single lineage or a single pairwise coalescence. Fix
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
and consider
the event
\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})
that, at least one of the following
occurs among the
l
coordinates:

•

a merger of three or more blocks within some deme,

•

two or more binary coalescences (in possibly different demes among possibly different coordinates),

•

two or more migrations (among possibly different and edges and possibly different coordinates),

•

a migration and a coalescence in the same step (among possibly different coordinates).

On
\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})^{c}
, each coordinate undergoes either no change, a single migration of
one block along one edge, or a single binary coalescence of two blocks within one deme. In particular,
if
\vec{\eta}
differs from
\vec{\xi}
by more than one coordinate, or if a coordinate changes by more than
one elementary move, then necessarily
\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})
occurs, and hence

P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})\,\,\mid\,\,\vec{\xi},\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}\right).

(8.3)

We now bound the probability of
\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})
by quantities of order

c_{N}^{v_{0}}f(\varepsilon)
. Write
\widetilde{V}_{N}(0)=(x,m)
, with
x=(x^{v})_{v\in V}
and
x^{v}\in\Delta
.
By definition of
B(\varepsilon)
under the metric
d
, on
\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}
we have

\sup_{v\in V}\left\langle x^{v},x^{v}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varepsilon,\qquad\|m\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon.

(8.4)

Triple mergers.

Fix a coordinate and a deme
v
. Conditional on
(\widetilde{V}_{N}(0),m(0))=(x,m)
, the
(x,m)
-paintbox implies
that for any three specified blocks in deme
v
the probability that they are assigned to the same
parental chromosome is bounded by
\sum_{i\geq 1}(x_{i}^{v})^{3}
. On
\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}
,

\sum_{i\geq 1}(x_{i}^{v})^{3}\leq x_{1}^{v}\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle\leq\|x^{v}\|_{2}\,\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle\leq\varepsilon\,\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle,

where we used
x_{1}^{v}\leq\left\langle x^{v},x^{v}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Taking a union bound over the at most
\binom{n}{3}
triples yields that the probability of
any

triple merger in deme
v
in that coordinate is at most

\binom{n}{3}\varepsilon\,\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle
. Summing over
v\in V
and over the
l
coordinates gives

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{some triple merger occurs}\,\,\mid\,\,\vec{\xi},\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}\right)\leq l\binom{n}{3}\varepsilon\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle\,\,\mid\,\,\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}\right].

(8.5)

The right-hand side is
c_{N}^{v_{0}}\,f_{\mathrm{tr}}(\varepsilon)
for some continuous
f_{\mathrm{tr}}
with

f_{\mathrm{tr}}(0)=0
, because the factor
\varepsilon
is explicit and, by Assumption
3.3
,

\sum_{v}c_{N}^{v}=O(c_{N}^{v_{0}})
, while
\mathbb{E}[\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle\mid\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}]
is controlled
by the same pair-coalescence scale.

Two migrations.

Fix a coordinate. Conditional on
(\widetilde{V}_{N}(0),m(0))=(x,m)
and on the initial configuration, each lineage
that currently lies in deme
v
migrates along a specific edge
e=(v,w)
with probability at most
m_{e}
. Hence, for
two distinct lineages, say
a\neq b
, and for two (not necessarily equal) edges
e,e^{\prime}\in E
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{$a$ migrates along $e$ and $b$ migrates along $e^{\prime}$}\,\,\mid\,\,\vec{\xi},(x,m)\right)\;\leq\;m_{e}\,m_{e^{\prime}}.

Taking a union bound over the at most
\binom{n}{2}
choices of
(a,b)
and summing over all ordered pairs
of edges gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left(\text{at least two lineages migrate (along any two edges)}\,\,\mid\,\,\vec{\xi},(x,m)\right)

(8.6)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\binom{n}{2}\sum_{e\in E}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}m_{e}m_{e^{\prime}}\;=\;\binom{n}{2}\left(\sum_{e\in E}m_{e}\right)^{2}.

(8.7)

On
\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}
we have
\|m\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
, and therefore

\left(\sum_{e\in E}m_{e}\right)^{2}\;\leq\;\|m\|_{\infty}\sum_{e\in E}m_{e}\;\leq\;\varepsilon\sum_{e\in E}m_{e}.

Averaging conditional on
\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}
and summing over the
l
coordinates yields a bound of
the form
c_{N}^{v_{0}}f_{\mathrm{mig}}(\varepsilon)
with
f_{\mathrm{mig}}(0)=0
, using
Assumption
3.7
(and finiteness of
E
) to identify that the total one-step
migration probability is
O(c_{N}^{v_{0}})
.

Two binary coalescences, or a migration and a coalescence.

These events require at least two elementary changes in the same discrete step among the
l
coordinates, and
are therefore bounded by products of one-event probabilities. Since sample size is fixed, a crude union
bound over potential coalescing pairs shows that the probability of two or more binary coalescences in a
single step is
O((c_{N}^{v_{0}})^{2})
, uniformly in
\vec{\xi}
; similarly for the probability that a migration
and a coalescence occur in the same step.

Combining the preceding bounds yields a continuous
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
f_{0}(0)=0
such that

\sup_{\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})\,\,\mid\,\,\vec{\xi},\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}\right)\leq f_{0}(\varepsilon)\,c_{N}^{v_{0}}\;+\;O\left((c_{N}^{v_{0}})^{2}\right).

(8.8)

We next identify the first-order transition probabilities on
\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})^{c}
.
Suppose
\vec{\eta}
differs from
\vec{\xi}
in exactly one coordinate, say
j
, and that
\eta_{j}
is obtained
from
\xi_{j}
by moving one block along an edge
e=(v,w)\in E
. By exchangeability within demes and
Assumption
3.7
, the one-step probability of this transition equals

c_{N}^{v_{0}}\mu_{e}
up to an error that vanishes as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
(and an error of order

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})\mid\vec{\xi},\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon})
coming from the possibility
that the step is not in the one-event regime). Thus there is a continuous
f_{\mu}
with
f_{\mu}(0)=0
such
that

\sup_{\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}}\left|P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})-c_{N}^{v_{0}}R(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})\right|\leq f_{\mu}(\varepsilon)c_{N}^{v_{0}}\;+\;\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})\,\,\mid\,\,\vec{\xi},\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}\right),

(8.9)

whenever
\vec{\eta}
is obtained from
\vec{\xi}
by a single migration in one coordinate.

Similarly, if
\vec{\eta}
differs from
\vec{\xi}
in exactly one coordinate and
\eta_{j}
is obtained from

\xi_{j}
by coalescing two blocks in deme
v
, then Assumption
3.3
implies that this
transition occurs with probability
c_{N}^{v}
up to an error negligible on the
c_{N}^{v_{0}}
scale, and that

c_{N}^{v}/c_{N}^{v_{0}}\to c(v)
. Since we are conditioning on
(\widetilde{V}_{N}(0),m(0))\in B(\varepsilon)
, the
contribution from non-Kingman coalescences in the same step is controlled by the triple-merger bound
above, and the difference between the effective binary-merger rate under
\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}
and the
limiting Kingman rate
\kappa_{v}
is absorbed into a continuous error function
f_{\kappa}
with
f_{\kappa}(0)=0
.
Consequently, for such single-coalescence transitions,

\left|P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})-c_{N}^{v_{0}}R(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})\right|\leq f_{\kappa}(\varepsilon)c_{N}^{v_{0}}\;+\;\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{B}_{N,\varepsilon}(\vec{\xi})\,\,\mid\,\,\vec{\xi},\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}\right).

(8.10)

For all other off-diagonal
\vec{\eta}\neq\vec{\xi}
, (
8.3
) applies. Summing these bounds
over
\vec{\eta}\neq\vec{\xi}
and using (
8.8
) yields

\left|P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}-(I+c_{N}^{v_{0}}R)\right|_{\infty}\leq f_{1}(\varepsilon)\,c_{N}^{v_{0}}\;+\;O\left((c_{N}^{v_{0}})^{2}\right)

(8.11)

for some continuous
f_{1}
with
f_{1}(0)=0
.

Finally, by the triangle inequality, (
8.2
) and (
8.11
),

\|P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}-A_{N}\|_{\infty}\leq\|P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}-(I+c_{N}^{v_{0}}R)\|_{\infty}+\|A_{N}-(I+c_{N}^{v_{0}}R)\|_{\infty}\leq f_{1}(\varepsilon)c_{N}^{v_{0}}+\left(C_{R}+O(1)\right)(c_{N}^{v_{0}})^{2}.

As
c_{N}^{v_{0}}\leq 1
, we have
(c_{n}^{v_{0}})^{2}\leq c_{N}^{v_{0}}
, and so we get the desired bound.
∎

8.4
Transitions at large jumps

In this section we prove Lemma
5.3
. The basic argument of the proof is that thea discrete version of the
(x,m,\xi)
-paintbox for finite
N
works by dropping points onto the space
I_{V}
but then removing a small part of the interval into which the point dropped. Because the amount one removes for each point one drops is
O(\frac{1}{N})
and we are dropping at most
nl\in o(N)
points, the picture from the continuous paintbox model and the discrete paintbox are almost identical.

We proceed now with the proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Fix
l\in\mathbb{N}
. For
(x,m)\in\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}
and
\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
, write

H_{N,l}(x,m)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}):=\mathbb{P}\left((\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}(k+1))_{i=1}^{l}=\vec{\eta}\,\,\mid\,\,(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}(k))_{i=1}^{l}=\vec{\xi},\ (\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(k),m(k))=(x,m)\right).

Recall that
h_{V}:\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}\to\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}
is the halving map on offspring frequencies, applied
coordinatewise on
\Delta^{V}
and leaving
m
unchanged. Define

H_{l}(x,m)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}):=\prod_{i=1}^{l}q_{n}(x,m)(\xi_{i},\eta_{i}),\qquad\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}\right)(x,m):=H_{l}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right).

We couple the
l
copies as in Section
8.1
: conditional on
\mathcal{A}_{N}
the copies are i.i.d. and
evolve on the
same
pedigree realization, with independent Mendelian randomness. Fix
(x,m)\in\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})

and condition further on the event
\{(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(k),m(k))=(x,m)\}
, which specifies the migration
proportions and the ordered offspring frequencies at generation
k
. Under this conditioning, the only remaining
randomness driving the updates from time
k
to time
k+1
consists of the random choice of which individuals migrate
(uniform without replacement within each deme and along each edge, with counts
\lfloor m_{e}N(v)\rfloor
), parental assignments, and the Mendelian segregation randomness determining, for each copy and each ancestral lineage, which parental chromosome is inherited.

For each deme
v\in V
write
S_{v}(m):=\sum_{e=(v,w)\in E}m_{e}\in[0,1]
and define

G(\delta):=\left\{(x,m)\in\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}:\ \forall v\in V,\ S_{v}(m)\leq 1-\delta\right\}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Assumption
3.12
,
\Phi\left(S_{v}(m)=1\right)=0
for each
v
, and hence the decreasing
sets

\{B(\varepsilon)^{c}\cap\{S_{v}(m)\geq 1-\delta\}\}_{\delta\downarrow 0}

have
\Phi
-mass tending to
0
. Therefore, choosing

\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)
sufficiently small, we may ensure

\Phi\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\cap G(\delta)^{c}\right)\leq\varepsilon.

(8.12)

Throughout what follows we work on
\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})\cap G(\delta)
; the contribution of
G(\delta)^{c}
will be paid for at the end.

Fix
(x,m)\in\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})\cap G(\delta)
. Then for each deme
v
,

\sum_{e=(v,w)\in E}\lfloor m_{e}N(v)\rfloor\;\leq\;\left(\sum_{e=(v,w)\in E}m_{e}\right)N(v)\;\leq\;(1-\delta)N(v),

so every without-replacement sampling of parents/migrants within deme
v
is performed from a set of size at least

\delta N(v)
. In particular, for each fixed
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
the total-variation distance between drawing
r
individuals uniformly without replacement from a population of size

M\geq\delta N(v)
and drawing
r
i.i.d. uniform individuals is bounded by the i.i.d. collision probability.

We make this precise for the one-step update of a single copy. Fix
\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
and consider the update

\xi\mapsto\eta
over one discrete generation given
(x,m)
. Let
q_{N,n}(x,m)(\xi,\eta)
denote the corresponding
one-step transition probability of the
single
copy ancestral process under the conditioning

\{(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(k),m(k))=(x,m)\}
. By replacing all without-replacement choices of migrants and parents
by independent sampling with replacement, we change the law of the resulting parental assignments by at most

O(n^{2}/N)
in total variation, uniformly over
(x,m)\in\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})\cap G(\delta)

since
n
is fixed.

The remaining issue is the diploid structure: when two sampled ancestral lineages choose the same individual, they
do not necessarily coalesce in the next generation, since they may correspond to distinct parental chromosomes.
Conditional on the realized counts
x^{v}
of offspring per individual in deme
v
, the Mendelian randomness assigns
each parental contribution to one of two chromosomes with probability
1/2
each, independently across contributions.
Equivalently, the relevant category probabilities for a paintbox description are obtained by splitting each
individual-mass
x_{i}^{v}
into two chromosome-masses
(x_{i}^{v}/2,x_{i}^{v}/2)
. This is precisely the effect of the halving
map
h_{V}
. Thus, after replacing without-replacement sampling by sampling with replacement, the conditional one-step
transition of a single copy is exactly the paintbox kernel
q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi,\eta)
.

Therefore there is a constant
C<\infty
, depending only on
n,|V|,|E|
and
\delta
, such that

\sup_{(x,m)\in\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})\cap G(\delta)}\ \max_{\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}\,\mid\,q_{N,n}(x,m)(\xi,\eta)-q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi,\eta)\,\mid\,\;\leq\;\frac{C}{N}.

(8.13)

We now return to the
l
-tuple. Conditional on
\mathcal{A}_{N}
and on
\{(\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}(k),m(k))=(x,m)\}
,
the Mendelian randomness is independent across copies, and the only source of dependence across copies is that they
use the same finite populations of parents and migrants at generation
k+1
. Since each copy uses only
n
ancestral
lineages and
n
is fixed, the same without-replacement estimate implies that the joint law of the parental assignments
across the
l
copies differs from that of
l
independent copies by at most
O(l^{2}n^{2}/N)
in total variation, uniformly
in
(x,m)\in\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})\cap G(\delta)
. Consequently, there is a constant
C^{\prime}<\infty
, depending only on

n,l,|V|,|E|
and
\delta
, such that

\sup_{(x,m)\in\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})\cap G(\delta)}\ \max_{\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}\,\mid\,H_{N,l}(x,m)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})-\prod_{i=1}^{l}q_{N,n}(x,m)(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\,\mid\,\;\leq\;\frac{C^{\prime}}{N}.

(8.14)

Using (
8.13
) and the inequality

\,\mid\,\prod_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}-\prod_{i=1}^{l}b_{i}\,\mid\,\leq l\max_{i}|a_{i}-b_{i}|
for
a_{i},b_{i}\in[0,1]
, we obtain

\sup_{(x,m)\in\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})\cap G(\delta)}\ \max_{\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}}\,\mid\,\prod_{i=1}^{l}q_{N,n}(x,m)(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{l}q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\,\mid\,\;\leq\;\frac{lC}{N}.

Combining this with (
8.14
) and absorbing finite-dimensional norm equivalences into the
constants yields

\sup_{(x,m)\in\textup{supp}(\Phi_{N})\cap G(\delta)}\left|H_{N,l}(x,m)-H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{N},

(8.15)

for some
C^{\prime\prime}<\infty
depending only on
n,l,|V|,|E|
and
\delta
.

Since
\|H_{N,l}-H_{l}\circ h_{V}\|_{\infty}\leq 2
everywhere, we may split the integral as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}

\displaystyle\left|H_{N,l}(x,m)-H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right|_{\infty}\,d\Phi_{N}(x,m)

(8.16)

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{1}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}\cap G(\delta)}\left|H_{N,l}-H_{l}\circ h_{V}\right|_{\infty}\,d\Phi_{N}+\;\frac{2}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\Phi_{N}\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\cap G(\delta)^{c}\right).

By (
8.15
),

\frac{1}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}\cap G(\delta)}\left|H_{N,l}-H_{l}\circ h_{V}\right|_{\infty}\,d\Phi_{N}\;\leq\;\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{N}\cdot\frac{1}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\Phi_{N}\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\right).

(8.17)

Since
B(\varepsilon)^{c}
is bounded away from
\mathbf{0}_{V,E}
, Assumption
3.4
implies that

\frac{1}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\Phi_{N}\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\right)
is bounded in
N
. Hence the right-hand side of (
8.17
) tends to
0

as
N\to\infty
.

For the second term in (
8.16
), again because
B(\varepsilon)^{c}
is bounded away from
\mathbf{0}_{V,E}
,
Assumption
3.4
gives

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\Phi_{N}\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\cap G(\delta)^{c}\right)\leq\Phi\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\cap G(\delta)^{c}\right),

(8.18)

By (
8.12
) the right hand side of (
8.18
) is at most
\varepsilon
, and therefore

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{2}{c_{N}^{v_{0}}}\Phi_{N}\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\cap G(\delta)^{c}\right)\;\leq\;2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, combining with
8.16
completes the proof.
∎

8.5
Combining the large and small scale

Combining the two scales of Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
allows us to conclude that the transition
kernel of the joint process
\left(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n}\right)_{i=1}^{l}
is arbitrarily close, on compact time intervals, to that of
a Markov process with infinitesimal generator

\mathcal{L}:=R+\int_{\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}}\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I\right)\,d\Phi(x,m),

(8.19)

where
h_{V}
is the halving map encoding the diploid splitting of each individual mass into two chromosome masses.

Lemma 8.10

(Small-event bound for the diploid paintbox kernel)
.

There is a constant
C_{n,l,V,E}<\infty
such that for all
(x,m)\in\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}
and all
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
,

\sum_{\vec{\eta}\neq\vec{\xi}}\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})\;\leq\;C_{n,l,V,E}\left(\|m\|_{\infty}+\sup_{v\in V}\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle\right).

(8.20)

In particular,

\left|H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I\right|_{\infty}\;\leq\;C_{n,l,V,E}\left(\|m\|_{\infty}+\sup_{v\in V}\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle\right).

(8.21)

Moreover,

\left|H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I\right|_{\infty}\;\leq\;l\,\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}\left(1-q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi,\xi)\right).

(8.22)

Proof.

Fix
(x,m)
and
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
. Write
\xi^{(j)}
for the
j
th coordinate of
\vec{\xi}
.
In copy
j
, conditional on
h_{V}(x,m)
, a change
\xi^{(j)}\mapsto\eta^{(j)}\neq\xi^{(j)}
occurs only if at least one of
the following events happens in the
\left(h_{V}(x,m),\xi^{(j)}\right)
-paintbox:

•

some block migrates along an edge, or

•

at least one pair of blocks in a common deme falls into the same
h_{V}(x)^{v}
-interval and merges.

Let
b_{j}\leq n
be the number of blocks in
\xi^{(j)}
, and let
b_{j,v}\leq b_{j}
be the number of blocks in deme
v
.

For the migration contribution, in copy
j
each block in deme
v
migrates with probability

\sum_{w:(v,w)\in E}m_{(v,w)}\leq|E|\,\|m\|_{\infty},

since
h_{V}
leaves
m
unchanged. A union bound over at most
b_{j}\leq n
blocks yields

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{a migration occurs in copy $j$}\mid h_{V}(x,m),\xi^{(j)}\right)\leq n\,|E|\,\|m\|_{\infty}.

For the merger contribution, in deme
v
two specified blocks coalesce only if their paintbox points fall in the same

h_{V}(x)^{v}
-interval, which has probability
\langle h_{V}(x)^{v},h_{V}(x)^{v}\rangle
. Since
h_{V}
splits each mass into two halves,
we have
\langle h_{V}(x)^{v},h_{V}(x)^{v}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle
, and in particular

\langle h_{V}(x)^{v},h_{V}(x)^{v}\rangle\leq\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle
. A union bound over the at most

\binom{b_{j,v}}{2}\leq\binom{n}{2}
pairs in deme
v
, and then over
v\in V
, gives

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{a merger occurs in copy $j$}\mid h_{V}(x,m),\xi^{(j)}\right)\leq|V|\,\binom{n}{2}\,\sup_{v\in V}\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle.

If
\vec{\eta}\neq\vec{\xi}
then at least one copy
j\in[l]
must change. Therefore, by a union bound over
j\in[l]
,

\sum_{\vec{\eta}\neq\vec{\xi}}\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})=\mathbb{P}\left(\vec{\eta}\neq\vec{\xi}\mid h_{V}(x,m),\vec{\xi}\right)\leq l\left(n\,|E|\,\|m\|_{\infty}+|V|\,\binom{n}{2}\sup_{v\in V}\langle x^{v},x^{v}\rangle\right),

which proves (
8.20
) with an appropriate constant
C_{n,l,V,E}
. (
8.21
) then follows from (
8.20
).

For (
8.22
), note that for each
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
,

\sum_{\vec{\eta}\neq\vec{\xi}}\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})=1-\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\xi})=1-\prod_{i=1}^{l}q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi^{(i)},\xi^{(i)}).

Using
1-\prod_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}(1-a_{i})
for
a_{i}\in[0,1]
gives

1-\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\xi})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(1-q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi^{(i)},\xi^{(i)})\right)\leq l\,\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}\left(1-q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi,\xi)\right),

and taking the supremum over
\vec{\xi}
yields (
8.22
).
∎

We now proceed with the proof of Lemma
5.5
.

Proof of Lemma
5.5
.

Fix
T>0
and write
h_{N}:=c_{N}^{v_{0}}
. Let
\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}
be the annealed
\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)
-valued Markov

8
, let
\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V):=\iota(\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l})
, and let

\pi=cd_{V}:\mathcal{H}_{n,l}(V)\to\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
.

Define the return times to
\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)

\tau_{0}:=0,\qquad\tau_{q+1}:=\inf\{k>\tau_{q}:\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(k)\in\mathsf{D}_{n,l}(V)\},\qquad q\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

Y^{N,l}(q):=\pi\left(\widehat{\chi}^{N,n,l}(\tau_{q})\right)\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l},\qquad q\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

By the strong Markov property,
Y^{N,l}
is a time-homogeneous Markov chain on the finite space
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
.
Let
\mathbf{P}_{N,l}
be its one-step transition matrix, and define the time-rescaled process

\overline{Y}^{\,N,l}(t):=Y^{N,l}\!\left(\lfloor t/h_{N}\rfloor\right),\qquad t\geq 0.

Let
P_{N,l}(t)
denote the transition kernel of
\overline{Y}^{\,N,l}
. Then

P_{N,l}(t)\;=\;\mathbf{P}_{N,l}^{\lfloor t/h_{N}\rfloor},\qquad t\geq 0.

(8.23)

Lemma
8.7
and Lemma
8.9
imply that, on
[0,T]
, the finite-dimensional
distributions of
\left(cd_{V}(\overline{\chi}_{i}^{N,n})\right)_{i=1}^{l}
coincide with those of
\overline{Y}^{\,N,l}
up to an
error that is
o_{N}(1)
uniformly over initial states. Consequently, it suffices to establish
(
5.4
) for the kernel
P_{N,l}(t)
defined above.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and set
\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}:=\{(\widetilde{V}_{N}(0),m(0))\in B(\varepsilon)\}
.
Lemma
8.9
implies that one step of
Y^{N,l}
corresponds to one reproduction step of the underlying
dynamics, and therefore, by conditioning on
\mathscr{E}_{N,\varepsilon}
and using the time-homogeneity of the i.i.d. environment across generations, we have

\mathbf{P}_{N,l}\;=\;\Phi_{N}\!\left(B(\varepsilon)\right)\,P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}\;+\;\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}H_{N,l}(x,m)\,d\Phi_{N}(x,m),

(8.24)

where
P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}
is as in Lemma
5.2
and
H_{N,l}
is as in Lemma
5.3
.

Define the truncated operator

L^{\varepsilon}\;:=\;R\;+\;\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I\right)\,d\Phi(x,m),

(8.25)

viewed as a matrix on
C_{b}(\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l})

\Phi(B(\varepsilon)^{c})<\infty
.

Lemma
5.2
provides a continuous
f
with
f(0)=0

\left|P_{N,n,\varepsilon,l}-e^{h_{N}R}\right|_{\infty}\leq f(\varepsilon)\,h_{N}.

(8.26)

Assumption
3.4
implies that for fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

\frac{1}{h_{N}}\,\Phi_{N}\!\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\right)\;\longrightarrow\;\Phi\!\left(B(\varepsilon)^{c}\right).

(8.27)

Lemma
5.3
implies that for fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

\left\|\frac{1}{h_{N}}\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}\left(H_{N,l}(x,m)-H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right)\,d\Phi_{N}(x,m)\right\|_{\infty}\;\longrightarrow\;0.

(8.28)

Moreover, each entry
(x,m)\mapsto\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta})
is bounded and continuous on

\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}
, and
B(\varepsilon)^{c}
is bounded away from
0_{V,E}
, so Assumption
3.4
yields

\left\|\frac{1}{h_{N}}\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\,d\Phi_{N}(x,m)\;-\;\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\,d\Phi(x,m)\right\|_{\infty}\;\longrightarrow\;0.

(8.29)

Combining (
8.28
) and (
8.29
) gives

\frac{1}{h_{N}}\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}H_{N,l}(x,m)\,d\Phi_{N}(x,m)\;\longrightarrow\;\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)\,d\Phi(x,m).

(8.30)

Expanding (
8.24
) using
\Phi_{N}(B(\varepsilon))=1-\Phi_{N}(B(\varepsilon)^{c})
, inserting
(
8.26
), and using
e^{h_{N}R}=I+h_{N}R+O(h_{N}^{2})
on the finite space
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
yields

\mathbf{P}_{N,l}\;=\;I\;+\;h_{N}R\;+\;\int_{B(\varepsilon)^{c}}\left(H_{N,l}(x,m)-I\right)\,d\Phi_{N}(x,m)\;+\;h_{N}\,\mathrm{Err}_{N,\varepsilon},

(8.31)

where
\|\mathrm{Err}_{N,\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq C\,f(\varepsilon)+o_{N}(1)
for some

.
Dividing the integral term by
h_{N}
and using (
8.30
) gives, for fixed

,

\left|\mathbf{P}_{N,l}-(I+h_{N}L^{\varepsilon})\right|_{\infty}\leq h_{N}\left(C\,f(\varepsilon)+o_{N}(1)\right).

(8.32)

Let
t\in[0,T]
and set
k:=\lfloor t/h_{N}\rfloor
. Using (
8.23
) and the telescoping identity,

\left|\mathbf{P}_{N,l}^{k}-(I+h_{N}L^{\varepsilon})^{k}\right|_{\infty}\leq k\,\left|\mathbf{P}_{N,l}-(I+h_{N}L^{\varepsilon})\right|_{\infty}\leq T\left(C\,f(\varepsilon)+o_{N}(1)\right),

(8.33)

and hence

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left|P_{N,l}(t)-(I+h_{N}L^{\varepsilon})^{\lfloor t/h_{N}\rfloor}\right|_{\infty}\leq T\left(C\,f(\varepsilon)+o_{N}(1)\right).

(8.34)

Since
L^{\varepsilon}
is bounded on the finite space
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)^{l}
,
e^{h_{N}L^{\varepsilon}}=I+h_{N}L^{\varepsilon}+O(h_{N}^{2})
in

\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Therefore, for
kh_{N}\leq T
,

\left|(I+h_{N}L^{\varepsilon})^{k}-e^{kh_{N}L^{\varepsilon}}\right|_{\infty}\leq C_{\varepsilon,T}\,h_{N}

for some
C_{\varepsilon,T}<\infty
. Combining with (
8.34
) yields

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left|P_{N,l}(t)-e^{tL^{\varepsilon}}\right|_{\infty}\leq T\,C\,f(\varepsilon)\;+\;T\,o_{N}(1)\;+\;C_{\varepsilon,T}h_{N}.

(8.35)

By (
8.22
) and the finiteness of
\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)
,

\left|H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I\right|_{\infty}\leq l\,\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}\left(1-q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi,\xi)\right)\leq l\,\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{n}(V)}\left(1-q_{n}\left(h_{V}(x,m)\right)(\xi,\xi)\right).

Hence Assumption
3.10
implies that

\int_{\Delta^{V}\times[0,1]^{E}}\left|H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I\right|_{\infty}\,d\Phi(x,m)<\infty,

and therefore

\left|\int_{B(\varepsilon)}\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I\right)\,d\Phi(x,m)\right|_{\infty}\;\longrightarrow\;0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\downarrow 0

by dominated convergence.

Since the matrix exponential is Lipschitz on bounded sets,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left|e^{tL^{\varepsilon}}-e^{t\mathcal{L}}\right|_{\infty}\leq T\,e^{T\|\mathcal{L}\|_{\infty}}\,\left|\int_{B(\varepsilon)}\left(H_{l}\circ h_{V}(x,m)-I\right)\,d\Phi(x,m)\right|_{\infty}.

Combining this estimate with (
8.35
) and using
h_{N}\to 0

(Assumption
3.2
) yields a continuous function
g_{T}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
g_{T}(0)=0
such that, for all
sufficiently large
N
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left|P_{N,l}(t)-e^{t\mathcal{L}}\right|_{\infty}\leq g_{T}(\varepsilon).

By taking
N
to infinity and then
\varepsilon
to
0
, the result follows.
∎

9
Discussion

In this section we briefly outline some implications of this work for inference methods based on the site frequency spectrum in Section
9.1
. We then discuss future mathematical works for appropriate scaling limits of infinite graph models in Section
9.2
and moment duals to quenched coalescent processes in Section
9.3
.

9.1
Implications for the site frequency spectrum

By an argument akin to that in
(
15
, Section 5.2)
one can see that the class of integral functionals governing the site-frequency spectrum, namely

\tau^{n,r}(\chi^{n})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\#\{C\in\chi^{n}(s):|C|=r\}ds,

converge with the population model when
G
is a connected graph with non-vanishing migration rates, i.e.

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\tau^{n,r}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\right)\right)_{r=1}^{s}\,\mid\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right]\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{d}}}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\tau^{n,r}\left(\chi^{n}\right)\right)_{r=1}^{s}\,\mid\,\Psi\right].

The limiting model of
36
is precisely that of Example
6.1
.
As
\Psi
for this model has no atoms, the site-frequency spectrum across unlinked loci are
independent
in the large population limit. In particular, the pairwise coalescence times for samples from unlinked loci are independent in the large population limit. However, this is inconsistent, but only
ostensibly
, with the simulations of
36
.

In Figure 3 of
36
there are clear and marked differences between the predictions for the pairwise coalescence time from the structured coalescent and the conditional coalescent. One explanation for this discrepancy is that the size of migrations are not so negligibly small at the scale of the simulation that their effect vanishes, as it would if the population size were taken sufficiently large. The simulations undertaken in the figure are with a finite population model, where
N=100
or
N=1000
. While the rescaled migration rate between demes in the large population limit is suitably small, for any finite population size we should really take the quenched coalescent model as if
\Phi
did not concentrate at
0^{\otimes 2}\otimes\delta_{0}^{\otimes 2}
but instead at some
0^{\otimes 2}\otimes M^{\otimes 2}
, where
M
is suitably concentrated close to zero without vanishing. A naive model which captures this behavior is precisely that of Proposition
6.3
where we take
b
to be large and
\alpha_{v}=2
for each
v
. An applied work investigating precisely how to do this in a robust manner so that pedigree effects could be detected in genetic inference would be of interest.

9.2
Scaling limits of infinite graph models

While this work concerns itself with finite graphs, the extension to infinite graph analogue, as discussed in Remark
4.6
is more or less straightforward. What requires quite different techniques and ingenuity would be taking a suitable two-scale limit, where we take an infinite graph model, like a stepping stone model, and show convergence to genealogies evolving in the continuum by rescaling both time and space. In Section
6
, we constructed what we called a discrete approximation of the genealogy of a
\Xi
Fleming Viot process. While this is outside the scope of this work, we briefly outline the scaling limit one could take. If this can be done, the well-known pain in the torus
(
13
, Section 6.4)
could be conquered from an individual based model.

Fix a finite measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r\,d\nu(r)<\infty
, a parameter
\lambda>0
, and a finite measure

\Xi
on
\Delta
with
\int_{\Delta}\langle x,x\rangle\,d\Xi(x)<\infty
. For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
let

V_{N}:=\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\langle L_{1}(N),L_{2}(N)\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
be the discrete two-torus described above, with lattice spacing
\rho_{N}>0
and
baseline migration parameter
\sigma_{N}>0
. Let
\chi^{N,n}
be the
n
-sample ancestral process on the pedigree associated to
the discrete approximation of the spatial
\Xi
-Fleming–Viot model on
V_{N}
, run on the coalescence timescale

\left(c_{N}^{v_{0}}\right)^{-1}
as in (
3.1
), and write
\overline{\chi}^{N,n}
for the rescaled process.

Assume that the geometric scaling is such that
\rho_{N}\to 0
and

\rho_{N}^{2}\,L_{1}(N)\to\ell_{1}\in(0,\infty),\qquad\rho_{N}^{2}\,L_{2}(N)\to\ell_{2}\in(0,\infty),

so that the embedded lattice
\rho_{N}V_{N}
converges in a suitable sense to the continuum torus

\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\langle(\ell_{1},0),(0,\ell_{2})\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Assume further that the baseline migration converges to Brownian motion in the sense that, for each fixed
t\geq 0
,
a single ancestral lineage under baseline dynamics satisfies

\rho_{N}\,\widehat{X}_{1}\left(\big\lfloor t\,\rho_{N}^{-2}\sigma_{N}^{-1}\big\rfloor\right)\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{d}}}}\,B(t)\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\right),

where
B
is Brownian motion on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
.

Finally assume that the extreme-event mechanism is tuned so that, under the diffusive scaling, the point process of extreme
events converges to a Poisson point process
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
with intensity measure

dt\otimes dx\otimes d\nu(r),

and that, conditional on an extreme event centered at
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
with radius
r
, all lineages in the
ball
B(x,r)
are instantaneously relocated to
x
and then undergo an instantaneous multiple merger governed by the
\Xi

paintbox.

Then, for each fixed sample size
n
, the quenched law of the rescaled discrete genealogical process should converge to that of the
spatial
\Xi
-Fleming-Viot genealogy on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
: more precisely, there should exist a coupling under which

\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{\chi}^{N,n}\in\cdot\,\,\mid\,\,\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)\,{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits^{{d}}}}\,\mathbb{P}\left(\chi^{n}_{\mathrm{FV}}\in\cdot\,\,\mid\,\,\Gamma\right),

weakly in distribution as random probability measures on

\mathcal{D}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{E}_{n}\left(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\right)\right)
, where
\chi^{n}_{\mathrm{FV}}
is the
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}})
-valued
Markov process obtained by evolving
n
independent Brownian motions on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
between atoms of
\Gamma
, and
each atom
(t,x,r)
of
\Gamma
performing an instantaneous merger among all blocks whose spatial locations lie in

B(x,r)
and relocating them to
x
, and coalescing when two Brownian motions intersect. Of course by adding in an additional sampling mechanism to the discrete model one would expect to capture simultaneous multi-mergers as well.

9.3
Forward-in-time processes

Another direction of interest is understanding implications of the quenched structured coalescent for diffusion approximations of allele frequencies. Recall that there is a moment duality between the block counting process for the Kingman coalescent, which counts the number of extant lineages backwards in time, and the Wright-Fisher diffusion, which models the frequency of a neutral allele forward in time. A natural conjecture for our quenched coalescent is that the macroscopic events captured by
\Psi
should give rise to jumps in the forward in time diffusion process. This has already been observed in the case of
\Xi
coalescents with selection
16
and for annealed multi-type
\Xi
coalescents
27
. The new contribution would be in describing precisely how the allele frequencies at unlinked loci are coupled by
\Psi
.

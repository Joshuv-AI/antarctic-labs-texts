A complete proof of the geometric Bombieri–Lang conjecture for ramified covers of abelian varieties
Guoquan Gao

Abstract
We prove the geometric Bombieri–Lang conjecture for projective varieties which have finite maps to abelian varieties over function fields of characteristic 0. This generalizes the recent results of Xie–Yuan, which require either the hyperbolicity assumption or the non-isotriviality assumption. The proof is based on their strategy for constructing entire curves, but requires some new ingredients from algebraic geometry and Nevanlinna theory.

Introduction

The far-reaching Bombieri–Lang conjecture is a higher-dimensional generalization of the celebrated Mordell conjecture proved by Faltings
[
Fal1
]

The geometric Bombieri–Lang conjecture is an analogue of the Bombieri–Lang conjecture over function fields. There are several versions of it. In the case of characteristic 0,

The conjecture (or its suitable versions) is proved for curves by

,

Let

The case when

is surjective

Now we turn to the more subtle case when

The main new ingredient in the proof of this case is a “tangency” observation. To illustrate the idea succinctly and intuitively, we temporarily assume that

Denote by
R\subseteq X

(see §
1.3

{\mathcal{R}}
be the Zariski closure of

in

. Denote

and

. For

, denote by

the fibre of the natural morphism

. Similarly we can define
{\mathcal{R}}_{b,t},{\mathcal{D}}_{b,t}
. For any point

, let

. View

Proposition 1.6
.

If

Now we assume Proposition

is tangent to sections

at “an excessive number of” points. As the limit entire curve is contained in the fibre

Now if
({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})_{t_{\infty}}
is reduced and not equal to the whole fibre

There are three difficulties in realizing the intuitive idea above. Firstly,

might be non-reduced. In this case the tangency of

Lemma 1.7

(Lemma
3.7
)

Let
S
be a variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a morphism of finite type between separated

-schemes. Fix a point
s_{\infty}\in S(\mathbb{C})
and a sequence of points

tending to

in the Euclidean topology. Then there is a Zariski open dense subset

such that for every point
x_{\infty}\in X_{s_{\infty}}^{\circ}(\mathbb{C})
and every sequence of points
x_{n}\in X_{s_{n}}
tending to
x_{\infty}
, if there exists a sequence of tangent vectors

under the Euclidean topology of the tangent bundle

v_{\infty}\in T_{x_{\infty}}(X_{s_{\infty},{\rm red}})

Roughly speaking, the lemma says that although the fibre

may be non-reduced, the limit of the tangent bundles

is “almost”

. Now we take

, and take

to be the composition
{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T\to T

is reduced and

is Zariski dense in

({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})_{t_{n}}
is reduced for all

to be the sequence
\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in the lemma. Then the lemma implies that the limit of the tangent bundles

In our case, since the section

The second difficulty is that
{\mathcal{D}}
may contain the whole fibre
{\mathcal{A}}\times\{t_{\infty}\}
. In this case the tangency of
({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})_{t_{\infty}}
and the entire curve in
{\mathcal{A}}\times\{t_{\infty}\}
is also trivial. To overcome this problem, we use a weak semistable reduction theorem (cf.
[
AK
, Theorem 0.3]
). Namely, there is a generically finite proper surjective map
T^{\prime}\to T
and a proper birational equidimensional morphism
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}\times_{T}T^{\prime}
such that the fibre of
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to T^{\prime}
are all reduced. This reducedness essentially implies that the ramification divisor of
X^{\prime}\to A\times_{K}T^{\prime}_{K}
is “horizontal” over
T^{\prime}_{K}
, where
X^{\prime}
is the generic fibre of the composition
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}\times_{T}T^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{X}}\to B
. This resolves the second difficulty.

However, the careful reader may notice that the operation in the previous paragraph may change the smoothness of
X
, this leads to the third difficulty. In general, we cannot assume that
X
is smooth. When
X
is normal but not smooth, the ramification divisor
R
of
f\colon X\to A\times_{K}T_{K}
may not be
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, this makes an issue to define the intersection number of
{\mathcal{R}}
and
x_{n}(B)
. If we take
\widetilde{X}
to be a resolution of singularity of
X
, we can talk about the intersection number. But in this case the base locus of
K_{\widetilde{X}}
may contain the fibre
\widetilde{X}_{t_{\infty}}
, which leads to difficulty in estimating the uniform bound of the intersection numbers
\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}\cap x_{n}(B)
.

To overcome this problem, we will use the
canonical model

X_{\rm can}
of
X
in the minimal model program, which is a birational model of
X
with ample
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier canonical class. Moreover, it has mild singularities. By the Hacon–Mckernan extension theorem (cf.
[
HM
, Corollary 1.7]
), we have a birational morphism
X_{\rm can}\to X
whenever
X
does not contain rational curves. We can use this canonical model to avoid the case that
R
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier or
K_{X}
has base locus. See §
3.4
for more details.

The above is the rough idea of the proof, but of course it is definitely not rigorous. For example, the meaning of “an excessive number of” points is not clear. We will use Nevanlinna theory (see §
3.1
) to make the proof rigorous. Specifically, we will employ the counting function in Nevanlinna theory to quantify the number of intersections between
x_{n}(B)
and
{\mathcal{R}}
, as well as the number of tangent points between
(s_{n},t_{n})(B)
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red}
. Eventually, we will show two things: on the one hand, the positivity of
R
implies that the tangent points between
(s_{n},t_{n})(B)
and
{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red}
account for
a positive proportion
of all intersection points (in the asymptotic sense; see (
3.4.14
) and Remark
3.18
); on the other hand, by Yamanoi’s Second Main Theorem (see Theorem
3.6
(1)) and some limit arguments (Lemma
1.7
mentioned above and Lemma
3.5
), we will prove that this proportion of tangent points
cannot be positive
(see Lemma
3.19
), thereby derive a contradiction.

Unlike the classical Nevanlinna theory, we need to consider the counting functions (as well as proximity and characteristic functions) for holomorphic maps from an open disk to a complex variety, rather than only from the whole complex plane. Furthermore, we need to vary the holomorphic maps and study the limit behaviour of these three functions in Nevanlinna theory (see Lemma
3.5
).

1.3
Notations and terminology

We denote
{\mathbb{N}}
to be the set of non-negative integers and
{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
to be the set of positive integers.

For any abelian group
M
and any ring
R
containing
\mathbb{Z}
, denote
M_{R}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R
. This apply particularly to
R=\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
.

By a
variety
, we mean an integral scheme, separated of finite type over the base field. A curve is a 1-dimensional variety.

We use
\sim
to denote the linear equivalent relation between
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil,
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors. We use
\cong
to denote the isomorphism between two
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles.

For a proper morphism
f\colon X\to Y
between
K
-varieties, there is a push-forward homomorphism
f_{*}\colon Z_{k}X\to Z_{k}Y
between
k
-cycles. More precisely, for any closed subvariety
V
of
X
, the image
W=f(V)
is a closed subvariety of
Y
. If
\dim W<\dim V
, set
f_{*}[V]=0
. If
\dim W=\dim V
, then the function field
K(V)
of
V
is a finite extension of the function field
K(W)
of
W
. Set
f_{*}[V]=[K(V):K(W)]\cdot[W]
. This extends linearly to a homomorphism
f_{*}\colon Z_{k}X\to Z_{k}Y
for each
k\geq 0
. By
[
Ful
, Theorem 1.4]
,
f_{*}
descends to a homomorphism
f_{*}\colon A_{k}(X)\to A_{k}(Y)
between the groups of
k
-cycles modulo rational equivalence. In particular, if
\dim X=\dim Y=d
and
k=d-1
,
f
induces a push-forward homomorphism
f_{*}\colon{\rm Cl}(X)\to{\rm Cl}(Y)
between (Weil) divisor class groups.

For a closed subset
Z
of a scheme
X
, denote by
Z_{\rm red}
the reduced induced scheme structure on
Z
.

By a
function field of one variable
over a field
k
, we mean a finitely generated field
K
over
k
of transcendence degree 1 such that
k
is algebraically closed in
K
. We usually denote by
B
a smooth projective curve over
k
with function field
K
. For a projective variety
X
over
K
, an
integral model
of
X
over
B
is a projective variety
{\mathcal{X}}
over
k
together with a projective flat morphism
{\mathcal{X}}\to B
whose generic fibre is isomorphic to
X
.

Let
K
be a finitely generated field over a field
k
, and assume that
k
is algebraically closed in
K
. Let
A
be an abelian variety over
K
. Denote by
A^{(K/k)}
Chow’s
K/k
-trace of
A
over
k
. Denote

V(A,K)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(A(K)/A^{(K/k)}(k))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R},

which is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector space. In our setting,
k
is usually
\mathbb{C}
.

All complex analytic varieties are assumed to be reduced and irreducible. For a complex analytic variety
X
with a point
x\in X
, denote by
T_{x}X
the complex analytic tangent space of
X
at
x
defined by holomorphic derivations. For a holomorphic map
f\colon X\to Y
with
f(x)=y
, denote by
{\rm d}f\colon T_{x}X\to T_{y}Y
the induced map between the tangent spaces.

For a complex abelian variety
A
, the complex Lie algebra
{\rm Lie}(A)
is defined to be the group of translation-invariant holomorphic derivations on
{\mathcal{O}}_{A}
. By restriction, we have canonical isomorphisms
{\rm Lie}(A)\cong T_{x}A
for any
x\in A
.

For a variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
, denote by
TX\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\rm Spec}({\rm Sym}^{*}(\Omega_{X}))
the (geometric) tangent bundle of
X
. The complex point of

is represented by a pair
(x,v)
with
x\in X
and
v\in T_{x}X
a tangent vector of
X
at
x
. For a morphism

between complex varieties, there is a natural tangent map
{\rm d}f\colon TX\to TY
induced by
f
, which is algebraic. For
x\in X
, the restriction of
{\rm d}f
to
T_{x}X\subseteq TX
coincides with the tangent map mentioned above.

For a normal variety
X
over a field
k
, define the
canonical sheaf

\omega_{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}i_{*}\omega_{U}
, where

is the smooth locus of

and

is the natural open immersion. This is a reflexive sheaf of rank 1, so it corresponds to a Weil divisor class

of

. We call

the

of

.

For a generically finite surjective morphism

between normal schemes, define the

of

as

R(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum\limits_{D\subseteq X}{\rm length}_{k(D)}(\Omega_{X/Y})_{D}[D].

where the summation is over all prime Weil divisors of

and
\Omega_{X/Y}
denotes the sheaf of relative differentials. Moreover, if

are normal varieties over a field

of characteristic 0 with

being

-Cartier, then

is separable and we have the Hurwitz’s formula

.

Let
X
be a complex projective variety. Denote
X(\mathbb{C})
its associated complex analytic space (in particular,
X(\mathbb{C})
is endowed with Euclidean topology). Let
S
be a subset of

. Denote by
\overline{S}
the closure of

in

and by
\overline{S}^{\rm zar}
the Zariski closure of

in
X
. Assume that the smooth locus

is covered by countably many open balls
\{D_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
. We say that

is

is measurable under the Lebesgue measure of the ball

for all

has measure 0 for all

. Throughout this article, when we say “for almost all points in

”, we always mean that for points of

outside a subset of measure 0.

For any positive real number

, denote by

{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}\big||z|<r\},\hskip 20.00003pt\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}\big||z|\leq r\}.

the discs of radius

. Write
{\mathbb{D}}={\mathbb{D}}_{1}
and
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}=\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{1}
. Denote by
v_{\rm st}=\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}z}
the tangent vector of
{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
at 0 under the standard coordinate
z
.

Let
(Y,d)
be a metric space. Let
\{r_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive real numbers converging to infinity. Let
\phi_{n}\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{n}}\to Y
be a sequence of continuous maps. We say that
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
uniformly converges to a map
\phi\colon\mathbb{C}\to Y
if for every
\epsilon>0
, there is

such that for every

and

For a sequence (of any kind of object) we always mean an infinite sequence. For a subsequence of a given sequence, we also mean an infinite subsequence.

Given a function field

of one variable over a field

over

and a sequence of rational points

have bounded (resp. unbounded) height if for some ample line bundle

on

associated to

2.1
Linear entire curves

Linear entire curves

Let
A
be a complex abelian variety. Let
x\in A
be a point, and
v\in{\rm Lie}(A)
be a nonzero vector in the Lie algebra. By definition,
v
defines a translation-invariant vector field on
{\mathcal{A}}
, and thus it is a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}(v)
on
A
. The leaf
{\mathcal{F}}(v)_{x}={\mathcal{F}}(x,v)
of this foliation through
x
is called the
linear entire curve through
x
in the direction
v
. By translation, we have

{\mathcal{F}}(x,v)={\mathcal{F}}(0,v)+x.

Alternatively, write
A={\rm Lie}(A)/H_{1}(A,\mathbb{Z})
. Denote by
\tilde{x}
be a lifting of
x
in
{\rm Lie}(A)
. In the complex vector space
{\rm Lie}(A)
, there is a linear subset given by

\widetilde{\phi}_{(\tilde{x},v)}\colon\mathbb{C}\ {\longrightarrow}\ {\rm Lie}(A),\hskip 20.00003ptz\mapsto\tilde{x}+zv.

Composing with
{\rm Lie}(A)\to A
, we obtain an entire curve

\phi_{(x,v)}\colon\mathbb{C}\ {\longrightarrow}\ A.

The entire curve is independent of the choice of
\tilde{x}
, and its image is exactly the linear leaf
{\mathcal{F}}(x,v)
. We call
\phi_{(x,v)}\colon\mathbb{C}\to A
the
linear entire curve through
x
in the direction
v
.

The linear entire curve is generally not algebraic in
A
. Its Zariski closure is equal to
A(v)+x
for an abelian subvariety
A(v)
of
A
. In fact, by translation, it suffices to assume
x=0
. Then
\phi_{(0,v)}
is a homomorphism, so its image
{\mathcal{F}}(0,v)
is actually a compact Lie group, and thus its Zariski closure
A(v)
inherits a group structure. Moreover,
A(v)
is irreducible as an analytic variety, since the image of
\mathbb{C}
(under
\phi_{(0,v)}
) is irreducible. Then
A(v)
is an abelian subvariety of
A
.

Zariski closures of linear entire curves

The main result of this subsection is to give a clear description of Zariski closures of linear leaves in fibres of abelian schemes.

Let
B
be a smooth quasi-projective cure over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to B
be an abelian scheme. Let
K=\mathbb{C}(B)
be the function field of
B
, and
A
be the generic fibre of
\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to B
. By the Lang–Néron theorem (cf.
[
XY1
, Theorem 3.4]
),

V(A,K)=(A(K)/A^{(K/\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{C}))_{\mathbb{R}}

is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{R}
-vector space. For an abelian subvariety
H
of
A
, there is a natural inclusion

V(H,K)=(H(K)/H^{(K/\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{C}))_{\mathbb{R}}=(H(K)/H(K)\cap A^{(K/\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{C}))_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq V(A,K).

Moreover, we have the following key lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
XY2
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be two abelian subvarieties of
A
, and let
H
be the identity component of
H_{1}\cap H_{2}
. Then
V(H_{1},K)\cap V(H_{2},K)=V(H,K)
.

As a consequence, for every
s\in V(A,K)
, there exists a
unique minimal abelian subvariety

G(s)
of
A
with
s\in V(G(s),K)
.

The following is the main proposition cited in this subsection.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
XY2
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

Let
B
be a smooth quasi-projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to B
be an abelian scheme. Let
K=\mathbb{C}(B)
be the function field of
B
, and
A
be the generic fibre of
\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to B
. Assume that the natural map
{\rm End}_{K}(A)\to\rm{End}_{\overline{K}}(A_{\overline{K}})
is an isomorphism. Let
s
be a nonzero element of
V(A,K)
. Denote by
{\mathcal{G}}
the Zariski closure of
G(s)
in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then there is a subset
S(A)
of
B(\mathbb{C})
of measure 0 such that for any
b\in B(\mathbb{C})\backslash S(A)
, the Zariski closure of the linear leaf
{\mathcal{F}}(0,\delta(s,v_{b}))
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
equals to
{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
. Here
v_{b}\in T_{b}B
is a nonzero vector.

Here the transfer map (associated to
s
)
\delta(s,\cdot)\colon T_{b}B\to{\rm Lie}({\mathcal{A}}_{b})
is defined in
[
XY2
, §2.2]
using the Betti map. A key property of this transfer map is the following non-degeneracy theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
XY2
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

(B,\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to B,K,A)
are as above. Let
s\in V(A,K)

\Sigma(s)
be the set of
b\in B
such that the transfer map

.

2.2
Locations of limit points

The following theorem gives a concrete construction and a precise description of linear entire curves obtained from sections of abelian schemes by a Brody-type convergence.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
XY2
, Theorem 3.1(a)]
)
.

Let
B
be a smooth quasi-projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to B
be an abelian scheme. Let
K=\mathbb{C}(B)
be the function field of
B
, and
A
be the generic fibre of
\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to B
. Let
\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{A}}(B)
and
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in
B
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

b_{n}
converges to a point
b\in B
, and
s_{n}(b_{n})
converges to a point
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
.

(2)

There is a sequence
\ell_{n}
of positive real numbers converging to infinity such that the image of
\ell_{n}^{-1}s_{n}
in
V(A,K)
converges to a nonzero element
s_{\infty}
in
V(A,K)
.

(3)

The transfer map
\delta(s_{\infty},\cdot)\colon T_{b}B\to{\rm{Lie}}({\mathcal{A}}_{b})
is nonzero.

Define a re-parametrization
\{\phi_{n}\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{n}}\to{\mathcal{A}}\}_{n\geq 1}
of
\{s_{n}\colon B\to{\mathcal{A}}\}_{n\geq 1}
as follows. Take an open unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
in
B
with centre
b
, and assume that
b_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}
for all
n\geq 1
.Let
z
be the standard coordinate of
{\mathbb{D}}
. Take the tangent vector
v_{b}\in T_{b}B
to be the one represented by
v_{\rm{st}}=\frac{\rm{d}}{\rm{d}z}\in T_{0}{\mathbb{D}}
. Define the re-parametrization map

\phi_{n}\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{r_{n}}\ {\longrightarrow}\ {\mathcal{A}},\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002ptz\mapsto s_{n}(b_{n}+l_{n}^{-1}z)

Here the sum
b_{n}+\ell_{n}^{-1}z
is taken in
{\mathbb{D}}
, and
\{r_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of positive numbers satisfying

{r_{n}}/{\ell_{n}}<1-|b_{n}|,\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002ptr_{n}\ {\longrightarrow}\ \infty.

Then
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n}
converges
(
uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{C})
to the linear entire curve
\phi_{(x,\delta(s_{\infty},v_{b}))}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
through
x
in the direction of
\delta(s_{\infty},v_{b})

(
which can also be viewed as an entire curve on
{\mathcal{A}})
.

Combine Proposition
2.2
, Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

(B,\pi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to B,K,A)
are as above. Let
\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in
{\mathcal{A}}(B)
satisfying the condition
(2)
in
Theorem

. Denote by
G
the smallest abelian subvariety of
A

s_{\infty}
lies in

. Denote by
{\mathcal{G}}
the Zariski closure of
G
in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Assume that the natural map
{\rm End}_{K}(A)\to{\rm{End}}_{\overline{K}}(A_{\overline{K}})
is an isomorphism. Then for almost all

, there is a linear entire curve in
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
which is the unique limit of a re-parametrization of a subsequence
(

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2

B(\mathbb{C})
such that the sequence
\{s_{n}(b_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
has a subsequence converging to
y
in
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
\dim A
. When
\dim A=1
, any entire curve on
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
is surjective, so the statement is trivial by Theorem
2.4
. Now we assume that the corollary is true for all abelian varieties with dimension less than
\dim A
.

As in Theorem
2.4
, choose
\ell_{n}\to+\infty
such that the image of
\ell_{n}^{-1}s_{n}
in
V(A,K)
converges to a nonzero element
s_{\infty}\in V(A,K)
. Then
G(s_{\infty})
is a nontrivial abelian subvariety of
A
. If
G(s_{\infty})=A
, then the conclusion holds by Corollary
2.5
. Now we assume that
G(s_{\infty})\subsetneqq A
. Let
A^{\prime}=A/G(s_{\infty})
be the quotient abelian variety and let
s^{\prime}_{n}
be the image of
s_{n}\in A(K)\cong{\mathcal{A}}(B)
in
A^{\prime}(K)
. Moreover, take an integral model
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
of
A^{\prime}
over
B
. Shrinking
B
if necessary, we may assume that
A^{\prime}
is an abelian scheme over
B
and
A\to A^{\prime}
extends to a
B
-morphism
{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. Now we have data
(B,{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\to B,K,A^{\prime},\{s^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})
. They also satisfy the assumptions in this corollary. Therefore by induction hypothesis, there is a subset
S^{\prime}
of
B(\mathbb{C})
of Lebesgue measure 0 such that for every
b\in B(\mathbb{C})\backslash S^{\prime}
, there exists a Zariski dense subset
U^{\prime}_{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{b}
such that each
y\in U^{\prime}_{b}
is a limit point of
\{s^{\prime}_{n}(B)\}_{n\geq 1}
.

Let
S
be the union of
S^{\prime}
and the exception set of Corollary
2.5
, which is also of Lebesgue measure 0. Now for any
b\in B(\mathbb{C})\backslash S
and any point
y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}_{b}
, we can write
y^{\prime}=\lim_{k\to\infty}s^{\prime}_{n_{k}}(b_{n_{k}})
, where
\{n_{k}\}
is a subsequence of
{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
. Now consider the points
\{s_{n_{k}}(b_{n_{k}})\}_{k\geq 1}
. By properness of
{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
, we know that these points have a limit points
y
on
{\mathcal{A}}
, whose image in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is
y^{\prime}\in U_{b}
. Now by Corollary
2.5
and our assumption on
S
, we see that there is a linear entire curve in
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
which is the unique limit of a re-parametrization of a subsequence of
\{s_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
, and the Zariski closure of this entire curve is exactly
y+{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
. Here
{\mathcal{G}}
is the Zariski closure of
G(s_{\infty})
in
{\mathcal{A}}
. Note that
y+{\mathcal{G}}_{b}
is just the fibre of
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{b}
over the point
y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{b}
. In other words, for any
y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}_{b}
, there is an entire curve in
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
which is Zariski dense in the fibre of
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{b}
and which is the limit of a re-parametrization of a subsequence of
\{s_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
.

Let
U_{b}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
be the set of all limit points of
\{s_{n}(B)\}_{n\geq 1}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
. By the argument above, the image of
U_{b}
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
contains the set
U^{\prime}_{b}
which is Zariski dense in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{b}
, and the fibre of
U_{b}
over each
y^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}_{b}
is Zariski dense in the fibre of
{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
over
y^{\prime}
. This just means that
U_{b}
is Zariski dense in
{\mathcal{A}}_{b}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

3
Boundedness of height on generic sequences

Let
X
be as in the main Theorem
1.1
. In this section we prove that the height of any generic sequence of rational points in
X(K)
is bounded, which is the essential step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The precise statement is as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K
be the function field of a smooth projective curve
B
over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
X
be a projective variety of
general type
over
K
with a finite morphism
f\colon X\to A
to an abelian variety
A
over
K
. Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subseteq X(K)
be a generic sequence of rational points of
X
, i.e. any infinite subsequence of
\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is Zariski dense in
X
. Then the height of
\{x_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded.

The proof will be given in §
3.4
. In §
3.1
we recall some Nevanlinna theory which will be used in the proof. In section §
3.2
we prove a key lemma (Lemma
3.7
) that is crucial to the proof. This was already mentioned in §
1.2
. In §
3.3
we prove a simple result concerning positivity of a divisor on an abelian variety. The reader may directly read §
3.4
first and come back to the previous three subsections when they need.

3.1
Preliminaries in Nevanlinna theory

Counting function

Let
X
be a projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
and let
Z\subseteq X
be a closed subscheme. Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to X
be a holomorphic map such that
f(\mathbb{C})\nsubseteq Z
. We recall the definition of
N(r,f,Z)
in
[
Yam1
]
.

Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{l}
be effective Cartier divisors such that
Z=D_{1}\cap\dots\cap D_{l}
. This means that
{\mathcal{I}}_{Z}={\mathcal{I}}_{D_{1}}+\dots+{\mathcal{I}}_{D_{l}}
, where
{\mathcal{I}}_{Z},{\mathcal{I}}_{D_{1}},\dots,{\mathcal{I}}_{D_{l}}
are the defining ideal sheaves in
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. Set

{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}Z\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min\{{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D_{1},\dots,{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D_{l}\}.

When
f(\mathbb{C})\subseteq{\rm Supp}(D_{i})
, we set
{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D_{i}=+\infty
. The definition of
{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}Z
does not depend on the choice of the Cartier divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{r}
. We define the counting function by

N(r,f,Z)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{1}^{r}\left(\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}Z\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t},

N^{(k)}(r,f,Z)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{1}^{r}\left(\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}\min\{k,{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}Z\}\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}.

Here
{\mathbb{D}}_{t}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\big||z|<t\}
is the open disk of radius
t
with centre 0.

Moreover, when
f\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{R}\to X
is a holomorphic map from an open disk
{\mathbb{D}}_{R}\ (R>0)
to
X
with
f({\mathbb{D}}_{R})\nsubseteq Z
, we can define the counting function
N(r,f,Z),N^{(k)}(r,f,Z)
for any
r<R
in the same way as above.

Characteristic function and proximity function

Let
X
be a projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
L
be a line bundle on
X
with a smooth Hermitian metric
\left\|\cdot\right\|
on it. Let
c_{1}(L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
be the Chern form. Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to X
be a holomorphic curve. Define the characteristic function by

T(r,f,L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{1}^{r}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}f^{*}c_{1}(L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}.

For an effective Cartier divisor
D
on
X
associated to a section
s_{D}\in\Gamma(X,L)
, if
f(\mathbb{C})\nsubseteq D
, define the proximity function by

m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\left\|s_{D}(f(r{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta}))\right\|\frac{{\rm d\theta}}{2\pi}

As
X
is compact,
\left\|s_{D}(x)\right\|(x\in X)
is bounded above by a finite constant. Thus
m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
is bounded below by a constant, which depends only on the metric
\left\|\cdot\right\|
. In particular, this lower bound is uniform for all
f
and
r
.

We also set
T(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}T(r,f,L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
. Moreover, for any holomorphic map

f\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{R}\to X
, we can define the characteristic function
T(r,f,L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
in the same way as above. If in addition
f({\mathbb{D}}_{R})\nsubseteq D
, we can also define
m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
for any
r<R
.

The above definitions can be extended by
\mathbb{Q}
-linearity to the case when
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor (or
L
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle with a smooth Hermitian metric on some multiple of
L
).

The next lemma assures that for two different metrics on
L
, the difference of the corresponding characteristic functions and proximity functions are bounded.

Lemma 3.2
.

X,L,D
as above. Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}
be two smooth Hermitian metrics on
L
. Then there is a constant
C>0
depending only on these two metrics such that for any holomorphic map
f\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{R}\to X
and any
0<r<R
,

(1)

\left|T(r,f,L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)-T(r,f,L,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})\right|\leq C
.

(2)

If in addition
f({\mathbb{D}}_{R})\nsubseteq D
, then
\left|m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)-m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})\right|\leq C
.

Proof.

First consider
(1)
. Since
c_{1}(L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)-c_{1}(L,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})
is a smooth exact
(1,1)
-form, the
\partial\overline{\partial}
lemma yields that there is a smooth function
\varphi
on
X
such that
c_{1}(L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)-c_{1}(L,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})={\rm dd^{c}}\varphi
. Hence we have

\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}f^{*}c_{1}(L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}f^{*}c_{1}(L,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})+\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}{\rm dd^{c}}\varphi(f(z))

By Stokes’ formula, we get

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}{\rm dd^{c}}\varphi(f(z))

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}{\rm d^{c}}\varphi(f(z))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{0}^{2\pi}t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(f(t{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta}))\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\varphi(f(r{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta}))\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{2\pi}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\varphi(f({\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta}))\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{2\pi}.

Since
\varphi
is bounded, the right-hand side is bounded by a constant
C_{1}>0
which depends only on
\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}
.

For (2), as
X
is compact, there exists
C_{2}>1
(depending only on
\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}
) such that
C_{2}^{-1}\left\|\cdot\right\|\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}\leq C_{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|
. Thus
-\log\left\|s_{D}(x)\right\|
and
-\log\left\|s_{D}(x)\right\|^{\prime}
are bounded by
\log C_{2}
. By definition we see that
\left|m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)-m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})\right|\leq\log C_{2}
. Now take
C=\max\{C_{1},\log C_{2}\}
, we finish the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Because of Lemma
3.2
, most references use
T(r,f,L)
and
m(r,f,D)
to denote a real-valued function class on
0<r<R
(two functions are in the same class if and only if their difference is a bounded function). However, in our application, we need to control the error term uniformly for all
f
and
r
(not just for
r
). Therefore, it is more convenient to fix the metrics on all the line bundles we need to use. After doing so, we can make the Nevanlinna First Main Theorem more precisely in Theorem
3.4
below.

Theorem 3.4

(First Main Theorem)
.

X,L,\left\|\cdot\right\|,D
as above. Then for any holomorphic map
f\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{R}\to X
with
f({\mathbb{D}}_{R})\nsubseteq D
and any
0<r<R
, we have

T(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)=m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)+N(r,f,D)-m(1,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|).

Proof.

By the Poincaré–Lelong formula, we have

-2{\rm dd^{c}}\log\left\|s_{D}\circ f(z)\right\|=-\sum\limits_{w\in{\mathbb{D}}_{R}}({\rm ord}_{w}f^{*}D)\delta_{w}+f^{*}c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D),\left\|\cdot\right\|),

where
\delta_{w}
is the Dirac current supported on
w
. Integrating over
{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
, we get

-2\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}{\rm dd^{c}}\log\left\|s_{D}\circ f(z)\right\|=-\sum\limits_{w\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}({\rm ord}_{w}f^{*}D)+\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}f^{*}c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D),\left\|\cdot\right\|).

Hence, we get

\displaystyle-N(r,f,D)+T(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}{\rm dd^{c}}\log\left\|s_{D}\circ f(z)\right\|

\displaystyle=-2\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}{\rm d^{c}}\log\left\|s_{D}\circ f(z)\right\|

\displaystyle=-2\cdot\frac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\int_{0}^{2\pi}t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\left\|s_{D}\circ f(t{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta})\right\|\frac{{\rm d}\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=m(r,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)-m(1,f,D,\left\|\cdot\right\|).

∎

The next lemma considers the limit behaviour of the three functions, which will be used in our proof in §
3.4
.

Lemma 3.5
.

X,L,\left\|\cdot\right\|,D
as above. Let
\{R_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be an increasing sequence of positive real numbers or
+\infty
. Let
R_{\infty}
be the limit of
R_{n}
. Let
f_{n}\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{R_{n}}\to X\ (1\leq n\leq\infty)
be a sequence of holomorphic maps. Here we set
{\mathbb{D}}_{+\infty}=\mathbb{C}
. Assume that
f_{n}
converges uniformly to
f_{\infty}
on any compact subset of
{\mathbb{D}}_{R_{\infty}}
. Then for any
0<r<R_{\infty}
,
T(r,f_{n},L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
tends to
T(r,f_{\infty},L,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
as
n
tends to infinity. Moreover, if
f_{\infty}(z)\notin D
for all
z\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
, we have
(
when
n
tends to infinity
)

N(r,f_{n},D),\ m(r,f_{n},D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)

tends to

N(r,f_{\infty},D),\ m(r,f_{\infty},D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)

respectively.

Furthermore, if
D
is replaced by a closed subscheme
Z\,(
of codimension possibly greater than
1)
in
X
, then for any
0<r<R_{\infty}
, we have
N(r,f_{n},Z)\leq 2N(r,f_{\infty},Z)
for
n
large enough
(
depending on
r)
.

Proof.

The statement for the characteristic function can be deduced directly by definition and the uniform convergency of
f_{n}
on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r}
. We mainly consider the other two functions.

We first consider the proximity function.

Since
f_{\infty}(z)\notin D
for all
z\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
, we can choose an open neighbourhood
U
of

f_{\infty}(\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r})
whose closure
\overline{U}
(in the Euclidean topology) is disjoint from
D
. Then the function
\log\left\|s_{D}(x)\right\|
is a real-valued continuous function on
\overline{U}
. By the compactness of
\overline{U}
, it is uniformly continuous. That is, if we fix a distance function
d\colon\overline{U}\times\overline{U}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
on
\overline{U}
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
such that for all
x,y\in\overline{U}
with
d(x,x^{\prime})<\delta
, we have
|\log\left\|s_{D}(x)\right\|-\log\left\|s_{D}(x^{\prime})\right\||<\varepsilon
.

As
f_{n}
converges uniformly to
f_{\infty}
on any compact subset of
{\mathbb{D}}_{R_{\infty}}
, for any
\delta>0
, when
n
large enough (depending on
\delta
), we have
f_{n}(y)\in U
and
d(f_{n}(y),f_{\infty}(y))<\delta
for all
y\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
. Thus if we choose
\delta
as the one in the previous paragraph, we have
|\log\left\|s_{D}(f_{n}(y))\right\|-\log\left\|s_{D}(f_{\infty}(y))\right\||<\varepsilon
for
n
large enough (depending on
\delta
) and any
y\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
. Now by definition of the proximity function, we see that
|m(r,f_{n},D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)-m(r,f_{\infty},D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)|<\varepsilon
for
n
large enough. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary,
m(r,f_{n},D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
tends to
m(r,f_{\infty},D,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
as
n\to\infty
.

Next we prove the lemma for the counting function
N
. By definition we have

\displaystyle N(r,f_{\infty},D)

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{r}(\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{1}}{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D\cdot\log r+\sum\limits_{1\leq|z|<r}{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D\cdot(\log r-\log|z|)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}{\rm ord}_{z}f^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z|\}.

Fix an
\varepsilon>0
. For any
z\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r}
, choose an open neighbourhood
U_{z}
of
z
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

For any
z\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r}
and
z^{\prime}\in U_{z}\backslash\{z\}
.
f_{\infty}(z^{\prime})\notin D
.

2.

If
f_{\infty}(z)\in D
(which implies that
z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
by our assumption),
|z|>1
and
z^{\prime}\in U_{z}
, then
|z^{\prime}|>1
and

(1-\varepsilon)(\log r-\log|z|)\leq\log r-\log|z^{\prime}|\leq(1+\varepsilon)(\log r-\log|z|)

3.

U_{z}\subseteq{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
if
z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
.

Now
U_{z}(z\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r})
covers the compact set
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r}
, so it has a finite sub-cover. Explicitly, assume
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r}=\cup_{j=1}^{m}U_{z_{j}}
, where
m
is a positive integer and
z_{j}\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}_{r}
.

By Rouché’s theorem, for
n
large enough, we have
\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D=\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{\infty}^{*}D
for all
1\leq j\leq m
. By assumption (
1
), we have
\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{\infty}^{*}D={\rm ord}_{z_{j}}f_{\infty}^{*}D
. Thus by assumption (
2
),

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z|\}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum\limits_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z_{j}|\}

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon){\rm ord}_{z_{j}}f_{\infty}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z_{j}|\}

for
n
large enough. Similarly, we have

\sum\limits_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z|\}\geq(1-\varepsilon){\rm ord}_{z_{j}}f_{\infty}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z_{j}|\}

for
n
large enough.

By Rouché’s theorem again, we see that for any
1\leq j<k\leq m
and any
n
large enough,
f_{n}(z)\notin D
for
z\in U_{z_{j}}\cap U_{z_{k}}
. In other words, when
n
large enough, for any
z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
satisfying
f_{n}(z)\in D
, there is exactly one
1\leq j\leq m
such that
z\in U_{z_{j}}
. Now we have

\displaystyle N(r,f_{n},D)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z|\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z|\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}(1+\varepsilon){\rm ord}_{z_{j}}f_{\infty}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z_{j}|\}

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}_{r}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{\infty}^{*}D\cdot\min\{\log r,\log r-\log|z|\}

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)N(r,f_{\infty},D).

for
n
large enough. Similarly we have

N(r,f_{n},D)\geq(1-\varepsilon)N(r,f_{\infty},D)

for
n
large enough. As
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that
N(r,f_{n},D)
tends to
N(r,f_{\infty},D)
as
n\to\infty
.

Finally, if
D
is replaced by an arbitrary closed subscheme
Z
of
X
, all of the arguments above also work, except for the equality
\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D=\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{\infty}^{*}D
replaced by the inequality
\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}Z\leq\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{\infty}^{*}Z
. In fact, take
Z=D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{l}
for some effective Cartier divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{l}
. Then by the argument above we see that
\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D_{i}=\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{\infty}^{*}D_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq l
and
n
large enough. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}Z

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}\min\{{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D_{1},\dots,{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D_{l}\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}D_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{\infty}^{*}D_{i}={\rm ord}_{z_{j}}f_{\infty}^{*}D_{i}.

for all
1\leq i\leq l
and
n
large enough. This means that

\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{n}^{*}Z\leq\min\{{\rm ord}_{z_{j}}f_{\infty}^{*}D_{1},\dots,{\rm ord}_{z_{j}}f_{\infty}^{*}D_{l}\}={\rm ord}_{z_{j}}f_{\infty}^{*}Z=\sum_{z\in U_{z_{j}}}{\rm ord}_{z}f_{\infty}^{*}Z.

for
n
large enough. Now imitate the previous argument (choose
0<\varepsilon<1
to ensure that
1+\varepsilon<2
), we can deduce that
N(r,f_{n},Z)\leq 2N(r,f_{\infty},Z)
for
n
large enough.
∎

. Part (1) of the theorem is called the Second Main Theorem (for abelian varieties).

3.2
A key lemma

In this subsection we prove the following key lemma which will be used in §

to prove the main result.

Lemma 3.7

(Lemma
1.7
)
.

Let
S
be a variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a morphism of finite type between separated
\mathbb{C}
-schemes. Fix a point
s_{\infty}\in S(\mathbb{C})
and a sequence of points
s_{n}\in S(\mathbb{C})
tending to
s_{\infty}
in the Euclidean topology. Then there is a Zariski open dense subset
X_{s_{\infty}}^{\circ}\subset X_{s_{\infty}}
such that for every point
x_{\infty}\in X_{s_{\infty}}^{\circ}(\mathbb{C})
and every sequence of points
x_{n}\in X_{s_{n}}
tending to
x_{\infty}
, if there exists a sequence of tangent vectors
v_{n}\in T_{x_{n}}(X_{s_{n},{\rm red}})
tending to a tangent vector
v_{\infty}\in T_{x_{\infty}}(X_{s_{\infty}})
under the Euclidean topology of the tangent bundle
TX
, then we have
v_{\infty}\in T_{x_{\infty}}(X_{s_{\infty},{\rm red}})
.

Example 3.8
.

Take
S={\mathbb{A}}^{2}
and
X=V(xu+yv+u^{2}v^{2})\subseteq{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
. The morphism
\pi\colon X\to S
is given by
(x,y,u,v)\mapsto(x,y)
. Then the fibre of
\pi
at the point
s_{\infty}=(0,0)
is non-reduced and the tangent space
T_{x_{\infty}}(X_{s_{\infty}})
for every point
x_{\infty}\in X_{s_{\infty}}
has dimension 2. On the other hand,
X_{s_{\infty},{\rm red}}
is isomorphic to
{\rm Spec}\ \mathbb{C}[u,v]/(uv)
, so the tangent space
T_{x_{\infty}}(X_{s_{\infty},{\rm red}})
for any point
x_{\infty}\in X_{s_{\infty}}\backslash\{(0,0,0,0)\}
has dimension 1, which is the same as the dimension of the fibre.

In general, for a point
(x,y,u,v)\in X(\mathbb{C})
, we have

T_{(x,y,u,v)}(X_{(x,y)})\cong\{(u^{(1)},v^{(1)})\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\big|(x+2uv^{2})\cdot u^{(1)}+(y+2u^{2}v)\cdot v^{(1)}=0\}.

It is not hard to see that when
(x,y)\neq(0,0)
, the fibre
X_{(x,y)}
is reduced. Now fix a sequence of points
s_{n}=(x_{n},y_{n})\to s_{\infty}=(0,0)
. For convenience we assume that
x_{n}y_{n}\neq 0
for all
n
. Then for any point on
X_{s_{\infty}}
of the form
(0,0,0,v_{\infty})
(
v_{\infty}\neq 0
), suppose there is a sequence of points
(x_{n},y_{n},u_{n},v_{n})
tending to
(0,0,0,v_{\infty})
and tangent vectors
(u_{n}^{(1)},v_{n}^{(1)})\in T_{(x_{n},y_{n},u_{n},v_{n})}(X_{s_{n}})
tending to
(u_{\infty}^{(1)},v_{\infty}^{(1)})\in T_{(0,0,0,v_{\infty})}(X_{s_{\infty}})
, then we have

\displaystyle(x_{n}+2u_{n}v_{n}^{2})\cdot u_{n}^{(1)}+(y_{n}+2u_{n}^{2}v_{n})\cdot v_{n}^{(1)}=0,\ n\geq 1

(3.2.1)

Moreover, since
v_{n}\to v_{\infty}\neq 0
and

\displaystyle x_{n}u_{n}+y_{n}v_{n}+u_{n}^{2}v_{n}^{2}=0,\ n\geq 1

(3.2.2)

Divide both sides of the equation by
u_{n}
and then take
n\to\infty
, we see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}(y_{n}/u_{n})=0
. This means that
y_{n}+2u_{n}^{2}v_{n}=o(2u_{n}v_{n}^{2})
as
n\to\infty
. Here for two sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1},\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of complex numbers, we say
a_{n}=o(b_{n})
as
n\to\infty
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_{n}/b_{n})=0
.

After taking a subsequence we may assume that the limit
A=\lim_{n\to\infty}(x_{n}/2u_{n}v_{n}^{2})
exists. If
A\neq-1
, divide both sides of the equation (
3.2.1
) by
2u_{n}v_{n}^{2}
and then take
n\to\infty
, we get that

(A+1)u_{\infty}^{(1)}=0

Therefore
u_{\infty}^{(1)}=0
. This is exactly the equation of the tangent space
T_{(0,0,0,v_{\infty})}(X_{s_{\infty},{\rm red}})
.

If
A=-1
, then we have
x_{n}=-2u_{n}v_{n}^{2}+o(u_{n})
as
n\to\infty
. Substitute this to the equation (
3.2.2
), we see that
y_{n}=u_{n}^{2}v_{n}+o(u_{n}^{2})
. Thus we get that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{y_{n}}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{4u_{n}^{2}v_{n}^{4}+o(u_{n}^{2})}{u_{n}^{2}v_{n}+o(u_{n}^{2})}=4v_{\infty}^{3}.

Note that we have fixed
(x_{n},y_{n})
. Therefore, as long as
v_{\infty}
doesn’t satisfies the equation above, there must be
A\neq-1
and
(u_{\infty}^{(1)},v_{\infty}^{(1)})\in T_{(0,0,0,v_{\infty})}(X_{s_{\infty},{\rm red}})
.

3.3
A positivity lemma

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
X,A
be two projective varieties over a field

with
A
an abelian variety. Let

be a generically finite surjective
K
-morphism. Let

be a big Cartier divisor on

is an ample Cartier divisor on

.

Proof.

By
[

, Proposition 1.4]
, it suffices to show that for any curve

,

. We first assume
R
is ample and effective. After a suitable translation, we may assume that
f(R)
and
C
intersect properly. Then
C
does not intersect any irreducible component of

of codimension greater than 1. If

, for any curve

, we have

, which contradicts to the ampleness of
R
. Therefore
C\cdot f_{*}(R)>0
. This proves the case when

is ample and effective.

For general
R
, there exists a positive integer

such that

is linearly equivalent to
R_{1}+R_{2}
, where
R_{1}
is ample and effective and
R_{2}
is effective. By the argument above,

is ample on

. Moreover,

is effective by definition, so it is also nef by the group structure of

(cf.
[

, Example 1.4.7]
). Therefore

is ample on

. By
[

, Theorem 1.4]
,
f_{*}(R)
is linearly equivalent to
f_{*}(R_{1})+f_{*}(R_{2})
, and thus it is also ample.
∎

Tangency

As we have mentioned in §
1.2
, a key idea in the proof of general case is a tangency observation. Now we give the rigorous statement.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0}\subseteq\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}
be the union of all irreducible components of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}
which dominates an irreducible component of
{\mathcal{R}}
. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.16
.

For any closed point
x\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0}
, the image of the tangent map
({\rm d}(f\circ\pi))_{x}\colon T_{x}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}})\to T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T)
at
x
is contained in
T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})
. Here we naturally view
T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})
as a subspace of
T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T)
.

Proof.

Denote
d+1
to be the dimension of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
. Let
U\subseteq(\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0})_{\rm red}
be the étale locus of
((f\circ\pi)\big|_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0}})_{\rm red}\colon(\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0})_{\rm red}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red}
. By our assumption,
U
is Zariski dense in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0}
. When
x\in U
, we have an isomorphism
{\rm d}((f\circ\pi)\big|_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0}})_{\rm red}\colon T_{x}((\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0})_{\rm red})\to T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})
. On the one hand, the image of
{\rm d}(f\circ\pi)\colon T_{x}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}})\to T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T)
contains the image of
{\rm d}((f\circ\pi)\big|_{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}})_{\rm red}
, which has dimension at least
d
. On the other hand, since
{\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T
is smooth and
{\rm d}(f\circ\pi)
at
x
is not surjective, its image has dimension at most
\dim(T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T))-1=(d+1)-1=d
. Therefore its image is exactly
T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})
.

Now we consider the morphism on the tangent bundle
{\rm d}(f\circ\pi)\colon T\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}\to T({\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T)
. Since
{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red}
is a closed subscheme of
{\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T
,
T{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red}
is a closed subscheme of
T({\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T)
. Thus the inverse image
Z=({\rm d}(f\circ\pi))^{-1}(T{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})
is a closed subset of
T\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
. Let
p\colon T\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
be the natural projection. The argument above shows that
Z
contains

p^{-1}(U)
. Hence
Z
contains the Zariski closure of
p^{-1}(U)
in
T\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
. As
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
is smooth,
p\colon T\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
is a vector bundle over
\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
. This means that the Zariski closure of
p^{-1}(U)
in
T\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
is exactly
p^{-1}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0})
. In other words, for any point
x\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
, the image of the tangent map
{\rm d}(f\circ\pi)\colon T_{x}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}})\to T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T)
is contained in
T_{(f\circ\pi)(x)}({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})
.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

With the notations above. Denote
\tilde{x}\in\widetilde{X}(K)
and
(f\circ\pi)(\tilde{x})=(s,t)\in A(K)\times T_{K}(K)
. View
\tilde{x}
as a section
\tilde{x}\colon B\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
. If
\tilde{x}(b)\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0}
for some

tangent to
{\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red}
at
(s(b),t(b))
. More precisely, the image of the tangent map
{\rm d}(s,t)\colon T_{b}B\to T_{(s(b),t(b))}({\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T)
at
b
is contained in
T_{(s(b),t(b))}({\mathcal{D}}_{\rm red})
.

Proof.

Note that
(s,t)\colon B\to{\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathbb{C}}T
is exactly the composition

∎

Since

Note that the normalization of

also satisfies the conditions in the lemma since the normalization map is finite. Therefore to prove the constancy of the normalization of
X
, we may assume that
X
is itself normal. In this case, the proof is the same as
[
XY1
, §4.2,
Constancy
]
, we omit it here. This finishes the proof of the first part of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3

After taking a normalization we may assume that
X
is normal. First we reduce the transcendence degree of
K/k
to 1 in Theorem
1.3
. Assume that
{\rm trdeg}(K/k)>1
, we need to prove that the conclusion holds for the datum
(K/k,X)
.

Let
k_{1}
be an intermediate field of
K/k
such that
K/k_{1}
has transcendence degree 1 and that
k_{1}
is algebraically closed in
K
. As
X(K)
is Zariski dense in
X
, it is not empty. In particular, we have an Albanese morphism
f\colon X\to{\rm Alb}(X)
defined over
K
. Assume that Theorem
1.3
holds for the datum
(K/k_{1},X)
. Then the Albanese variety of
X
is defined over
k_{1}
, and
X
is birational to
(X_{1})_{K}
for some projective variety
X_{1}
over
k_{1}
. Moreover, the complement of the
image of
{\rm Im}(X_{1}(k_{1})\to(X_{1})_{K}(K))
in
X(K)
(via a birational map from
(X_{1})_{K}
to
X
) is not Zariski dense in
X
. Thus
X_{1}
is of general type with
X(k_{1})
Zariski dense in
X_{1}
. After taking a normalization of
X_{1}
, we can assume that
X_{1}
is normal. Then
X_{1}
is also of maximal Albanese dimension. In fact, after composing a birational map, the base change of the Albanese map of
X_{1}
(over
k_{1}
) to
K
is just the Albanese map of
X
. If in addition
f
is finite, then there is a
K
-isomorphism
X_{1}\times_{k_{1}}K\to X
for some projective variety
X_{1}
over
k_{1}
. By
[
XY1
, Lemma 4.8]
, the finite morphism
X_{1}\times_{k_{1}}K\to X\to{\rm Alb}(X)
induces a finite
k_{1}
-morphism
X_{1}\to{\rm Alb}(X)^{(K/k_{1})}
. Therefore we can reduce Theorem
1.3
to the datum
(k_{1}/k,X_{1})
. Repeat the process on
k_{1}/k
, we eventually get the case
{\rm trdeg}(K/k)=1
.

Now assume
{\rm trdeg}(K/k)=1
. Recall that we have an Albanese morphism
f\colon X\to{\rm Alb}(X)
defined over
K
. By Stein factorization,
f
can be decomposed as
X\to X_{0}\to{\rm Alb}(X)
where
X\to X_{0}
has connected fibres and
X_{0}\to{\rm Alb}(X)
is finite. As
f\colon X\to{\rm Alb}(X)
is generically finite onto its image,
X\to X_{0}
is birational. In particular,
X_{0}
is of general type and
X_{0}(K)
is Zariski dense in
X_{0}
. Replacing
X
by
X_{0}
, we may assume that
f
is finite.

By Proposition
4.1
, the heights of the points in
(X\backslash{\rm Sp}(X))(K)
are bounded. Since
X
is of general type, by
[
XY1
, Corollary 4.3]
,
{\rm Sp}(X)
is a proper Zariski closed subset of
X
. Since
X(K)
is Zariski dense in
X
, Theorem
1.3
follows from Lemma
4.2
. ∎

Finally, we can prove the main Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
(X,Z)
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Let
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{r_{0}}
be the irreducible components of
Z
. By assumption,
Z
is the Zariski closure of
(X\backslash{\rm Sp}(X))(K)
in
X
, so
Z_{i}(K)
is Zariski dense in
Z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,r_{0}
. Moreover, each
Z_{i}
is not contained in
{\rm Sp}(X)
since otherwise
Z
is contained in the union
\cup_{1\leq j\leq r_{0},j\neq i}Z_{j}
which is impossible. Therefore
{\rm Sp}(Z_{i})\subseteq{\rm Sp}(X)\cap Z_{i}
is a proper closed subset of
Z_{i}
. By
[
XY1
, Corollary 4.3]
which is essentially due to Ueno and Kawamata,
Z_{i}
is of general type. Apply Theorem
1.3
to
Z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,r_{0}
. As in part (1) of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain
(T_{i},\rho_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,r_{0}
. Moreover, the complement of
{\rm Im}(T_{i}(k)\to Z_{i}(K))
in
Z_{i}(K)
is not Zariski dense in
Z_{i}
.

If
\Sigma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}({\rm Sp}(X)\cap Z)(K)\cup(\cup_{1\leq i\leq r_{0}}{\rm Im}(T_{i}(k)\to Z(K)))
not equals to
Z(K)
, denote
Z^{(1)}
the Zariski closure of
Z(K)\setminus\Sigma
in
X
. Note that every irreducible component of
Z^{(1)}
is properly contained in an irreducible component of
Z
and is not contained in
{\rm Sp}(X)
. Thus we can apply Theorem
1.3
to the irreducible components
Z_{r_{0}+1},Z_{r_{0}+2},\dots,Z_{r_{1}}
of
Z^{(1)}
. Repeat this process. Eventually, we have closed subvarieties
Z_{r_{0}+1},Z_{r_{0}+2},\dots,Z_{r}
of
Z
, with a
K
-isomorphism
\rho_{i}\colon T_{i}\times_{k}K\to Z^{\prime}_{i}
for a projective variety
T_{i}
over
k
for
i=r_{0}+1,\dots,r
, where
Z^{\prime}_{i}
is the normalization of
Z_{i}
, such that

X(K)={\rm Sp}(X)(K)\cup\left(\bigcup\limits_{1\leq i\leq r}{\rm Im}(T_{i}(k)\to Z(K))\right).

This finishes the proof of the main Theorem
1.1
. ∎

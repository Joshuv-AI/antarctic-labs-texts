Growth rates for the Hölder coefficients of the linear stochastic fractional heat equation with rough dependence in space
Chang Liu, Bin Qian, Ran Wang

Abstract




Abstract:

We study the linear stochastic fractional heat equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t,x)+\dot{W}(t,x),\ \ t>0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R},

where
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
denotes the fractional Laplacian with power
\alpha\in(1,2)
, and the driving noise
\dot{W}
is
a centered Gaussian field which is white in time and has the covariance of a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in\left(\frac{2-\alpha}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
.
We establish exact asymptotics for the solution as
t,x\rightarrow\infty
and derive sharp growth rates for the Hölder coefficients. The proofs are based on Talagrand’s majorizing measure theorem and Sudakov’s minoration theorem.

Keyword:
Stochastic fractional heat equation; rough noise; global Hölder continuity; majorizing measure theorem.

MSC:
60H15; 60G17; 60G22.

1.
Introduction

Consider the following linear stochastic fractional heat equation (SFHE, for short):

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t,x)+\dot{W}(t,x),\ \ t>0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)\equiv 0.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here,
-(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
denotes the fractional Laplacian with power
\alpha\in(1,2)
,
W(t,x)
is a centered Gaussian field with the covariance given by

{\mathbb{E}}[W(t,x)W(s,y)]=\frac{1}{2}\left(s\wedge t\right)\left(|x|^{2H}+|y|^{2H}-|x-y|^{2H}\right),

(1.2)

for some
H\in\left(\frac{2-\alpha}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Equivalently, the covariance of the noise
\dot{W}(t,x)=\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial t\partial x}
is

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\dot{W}(t,x)\dot{W}(s,y)\right]=\delta_{0}(t-s)\Lambda\left(x-y\right),

where
\Lambda
is a distribution, whose Fourier transform is the measure
\mu(d\xi)=c_{1,H}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi
with

\displaystyle c_{1,H}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2\pi}\Gamma(2H+1)\sin(\pi H).

(1.3)

When
\alpha=2
and
H=\frac{1}{2}
, Equation (
1.1
) reduces to the classical stochastic heat equation (SHE, for short) driven by space-time white noise. In this case, it is well-known that the stochastic heat equation admits a unique mild solution
[
8
,
23
]
.
The study of stochastic partial differential equations driven by fractional Brownian motion (fBm, for short) or other general Gaussian noises has advanced rapidly in recent years. We refer to
[
5
,
12
,
11
,
17
,
18
,
16
,
22
,
3
,
9
]
and references therein for a comprehensive overview of this active research area.
Among these works, Balan and Tudor
[
5
]
gave necessary and sufficient conditions on the Hurst index for the existence of solutions to the stochastic heat equation driven by the following Gaussian noises:

(i)

Fractional noise in time with Hurst index
H\in(1/2,1)
;

(ii)

Spatially correlated noise with covariance given by Riesz, Bessel, or Poisson kernel functions.

Herrell et al.
[
13
]
investigated the sample path regularity of solutions to the stochastic heat equation driven by fractional-colored Gaussian noise.

Conus et al. studied the global spatial behavior of the solution of SHE driven by space-time white noise or colored noise in
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
, respectively. Recently, Hu and Wang
[
14
]
extended these results to spatial rough noise with covariance structure given by (
1.2
). Specifically, for the SHE driven by Gaussian noise that is white in time and fractional in space with Hurst index
H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
, they derived precise asymptotic behavior of solutions as both temporal and spatial variables tend to infinity.
Furthermore, Hu and Wang
[
14
]
established sharp estimates for the domain-size dependence of the Hölder coefficients. Liu et al.
[
18
]
investigated similar properties for solutions to the stochastic wave equation in arbitrary spatial dimensions, considering additive Gaussian noise that exhibits both temporal and spatial fractional structure.

In the present work, we extend the research program initiated in
[
14
]
to the fractional Laplacian framework for Equation (
1.1
). Specifically, our investigation focuses on two aspects:

•

The asymptotic behavior of solutions to Equation (
1.1
);

•

The sharp growth rates of Hölder regularity coefficients.

We introduce the following key function that characterizes the space-time scaling in our analysis:

\Psi(t,L):=1+\sqrt{\log_{2}\left(\frac{L}{t^{1/\alpha}}\vee 1\right)},\quad t>0,\ L>0.

(1.4)

This scaling function plays a fundamental role throughout our work, particularly in establishing the precise relationship between spatial and temporal variables.

First, we provide sharp bounds on the expectation of
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T,-L\leq x\leq L}u(t,x)
and
\sup_{-L\leq x\leq L}u(t,x)
, as
T
and
L
tend to infinity.

Theorem 1.1
.

(a)

There exist positive constants
c_{1,1}
and
c_{1,2}
such that for any
T,L>0
,

\displaystyle c_{1,1}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\Psi(T,L)\leq

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq t\leq T\\
-L\leq x\leq L\end{subarray}}u(t,x)\right]\leq c_{1,2}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\Psi(T,L).

(1.5)

(b)

There exist positive constants
c_{1,3}
and
c_{1,4}
such that for any
L\geq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}>0
,

c_{1,3}t^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\Psi(t,L)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{-L\leq x\leq L}u(t,x)\right]\leq c_{1,4}t^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\Psi(t,L).

(1.6)

Next, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of the solution under the following three scaling regimes:

(a)

Temporal and spatial domain size
T,L\rightarrow\infty
;

(b)

Spatial domain size
L\rightarrow\infty

Spatial variable

Specifically,

Proposition 1.2
.

(a)

For any
T>0,\delta>0

\Upsilon(T,\delta):=\left\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\left[-T^{\frac{1+\delta}{\alpha}},T^{\frac{1+\delta}{\alpha}}\right]\right\}

\begin{split}c_{1,1}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\alpha}}&\,\leq\liminf_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sup_{(t,x)\in\Upsilon(T,\delta)}u(t,x)}{T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\sqrt{\log_{2}T}}\\

(1.7)

(b)

For any

\displaystyle c_{1,3}t^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\leq

\displaystyle\,\liminf_{L\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in[-L,L]}u(t,x)}{\sqrt{\log_{2}L}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\limsup_{L\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sup_{x\in[-L,L]}u(t,x)}{\sqrt{\log_{2}L}}\leq c_{1,4}t^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}},\ \ \text{a.s.}

(1.8)

(c)

For any
t>0

\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{u(t,x)}{\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\sqrt{\log_{2}|x|}}=\sqrt{2},\ \ \text{a.s.}

(1.9)

Here, the constants
c_{1,i},i=1,\cdots,4
denote the constants given in Theorem
1.1
.

(1.10)

The precise asymptotics and sharp growth rates for Hölder coefficients established in our main theorems are of fundamental theoretical importance. The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we review foundational properties of solutions and establish sharp moment bounds. In Section 3, we present the proofs of the main results, while technical lemmas are deferred to the Appendix for completeness.

2.
Moment estimates for the increments

Let us recall some notations from
[
10
]
and
[
14
]
. Let
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
be the space of real-valued infinitely differentiable functions with compact support on
\mathbb{R}
. The Fourier transform of a function
f\in\mathcal{D}
is defined as

\mathcal{F}f(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi x}f(x)dx.

For any
\alpha\in(1,2]
, the Green kernel
G_{\alpha}
associated with the operator

-(-\triangle)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
is given by

(\mathcal{F}G_{\alpha}(t,\cdot))(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}},\ \ \xi\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \ t>0.

(2.1)

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space and
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
be the space of real-valued infinitely differentiable functions with compact support in
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. The noise
\dot{W}
is a zero-mean Gaussian family
\{W(\phi),\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})\}
, whose covariance structure is given by

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=\mathbb{E}\big[W(\phi)W(\psi)\big]=c_{1,H}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\phi(s,\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(s,\xi)}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds,

(2.2)

where
H\in\left(0,1\right)
,
c_{1,H}
is given in (
1.3
),
and
\mathcal{F}\phi(s,\xi)
is the Fourier transform of
\phi(s,x)
with respect to the spatial variable
x
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
with the inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. The mapping
\varphi\mapsto W(\varphi)
can be extended to all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, which is called the Wiener integral.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
2
, Definition 4.1]
)
The random field
\{u(t,x);(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\}
defined by

u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{\alpha}(t-s,x-y)W(ds,dy)

(2.3)

is called a solution of Equation (
1.1
), provided that the stochastic integral on the right-hand side of (
2.3
) is well-defined, i.e., for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

g_{t,x}:=G_{\alpha}(t-\cdot,x-\cdot){\mathbf{1}}_{[0,t]}\in{\mathcal{H}}.

According to Balan
[
2
, Remark 5.4]
, we have the following result about the existence of the solution to Equation (
1.1
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Equation (
1.1
) admits a unique solution
\{u(t,x);(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\}
given by (
2.3
) if and only if
H\in\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2},1\right)
. In the later case, we have

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=c_{2,1}t^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}},

(2.4)

where
c_{2,1}=\frac{c_{1,H}}{2H+\alpha-2}2^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\Gamma\left(\frac{2-2H}{\alpha}\right)
.

Proof.

By (
2.1
), we have that for any
\xi,x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
t>0
,

(\mathcal{F}G_{\alpha}(t,x-\cdot))(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}-i\xi x}.

(2.5)

Using (
2.2
), (
2.5
) and the change of variables

\eta=2(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[|u(t,x)|^{2}\right]=&\,2c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2(t-s)|\xi|^{\alpha}}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds\\
=&\,\frac{c_{1,H}}{\alpha}2^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\eta}\eta^{\frac{2-2H}{\alpha}-1}d\tau\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\frac{2H-2}{\alpha}}ds\\
=&\,\frac{c_{1,H}}{\alpha}2^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\Gamma\left(\frac{2-2H}{\alpha}\right)\cdot\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\frac{2H-2}{\alpha}}ds.\end{split}

(2.6)

Notice that the right most integral is convergent if and only if
H>1-\frac{\alpha}{2}
, and (
2.4
) holds in the late case. The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we give a sharp moment estimate for the increment

\displaystyle d_{1}((t,x),(s,y)):=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|u(t,x)-u(s,y)|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.7)

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist positive constants
c_{2,2}
and
c_{2,3}
such that for any
(t,x),(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
,

c_{2,2}\tilde{d}_{1}((t,x),(s,y))\leq d_{1}((t,x),(s,y))\leq c_{2,3}\tilde{d}_{1}((t,x),(s,y)),

(2.8)

where

\tilde{d}_{1}((t,x),(s,y)):=|x-y|^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}}\wedge(t\wedge s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}+|t-s|^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
t>s>0
.
By (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle u(t,x)-u(s,y)=

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[G_{\alpha}(t-r,x-z)-G_{\alpha}(s-r,y-z)\right]W(dr,dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{\alpha}(t-r,x-z)W(dr,dz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:I_{1}+I_{2}.

The independence of the stochastic integrals over the time intervals
[0,s]
and
[s,t]
gives

\mathbb{E}\left[|u(t,x)-u(s,y)|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[I_{1}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[I_{2}^{2}\right].

(2.9)

Following the same line as in the proof of (
2.6
), we have

\mathbb{E}\left[I_{2}^{2}\right]=c_{2,1}(t-s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}.

(2.10)

By (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{1}^{2}\right]=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{F}G_{\alpha}(t-r,x-\cdot)(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{\alpha}(s-r,y-\cdot)(\xi)\right|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi dr\\
=&\,c_{1,H}\,\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2(s-r)|\xi|^{\alpha}}\left[1+e^{-2(t-s)|\xi|^{\alpha}}-2e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{\alpha}}\cos(\xi(x-y))\right]|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi dr\\
=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-2s\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\left[1+e^{-2(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}}-2e^{-(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}}\cos(\xi(x-y))\right]\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi.\end{split}

(2.11)

Next, we derive matching upper and lower bounds for the integral.

1. Upper bound.

By (
2.11
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u(s,x)-u(s,y)\right|^{2}\right]=&\,2c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-2s\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\left[1-\cos(\xi(x-y))\right]\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
\leq&\,2c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[1-\cos(\xi(x-y))\right]\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
=&\,2c_{1,H}\frac{\Gamma(3-2H-\alpha)}{2H+\alpha-2}\cos\left(\left(H+\frac{\alpha}{2}-1\right)\pi\right)|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}.\end{split}

(2.12)

Here, the following identity is used in the last step:
for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(\xi z)}{z^{1+\gamma}}dz=\frac{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}{\gamma}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}\right)|\xi|^{\gamma},

(2.13)

(see
[
4
, Lemma D.1]
).

By (
2.9
), (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and
the change of variables
\eta=(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u(t,x)-u(s,x)\right|^{2}\right]\\
=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-2s\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\left(1-e^{-(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}}\right)^{2}\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi+c_{2,1}(t-s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\\
\leq&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi+c_{2,1}(t-s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\\
=&\,\frac{c_{1,H}}{\alpha}(t-s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-\eta}\right)\eta^{\frac{2-2H-2\alpha}{\alpha}}d\eta+c_{2,1}(t-s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\\
=&\,\left(\frac{c_{1,H}}{2H+\alpha-2}\Gamma\left(\frac{2-2H}{\alpha}\right)+c_{2,1}\right)(t-s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}.\end{split}

(2.14)

Using the Minkowski inequality, (
2.4
), (
2.12
) and (
2.14
), there exists a constant
c_{2,4}>0
such that

\displaystyle d_{1}((t,x),(s,y))\leq

\displaystyle\,d_{1}((s,x),(s,y))+d_{1}((t,x),(s,x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,c_{2,4}\left(|x-y|^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}}\wedge s^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}+(t-s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\right),

which is the upper bound part of (
2.8
).

2. Lower bound.

By (
2.11
) and the change of variables, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{1}^{2}\right]\geq&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-2s\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\left[1-e^{-(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}}\cos(\xi(x-y))\right]^{2}\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
=&\,c_{1,H}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{2s\xi^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\right]\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{(t-s)\xi^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\cos(\xi)\right]^{2}\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
\geq&\,c_{1,H}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{2n\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{2n\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{2s\xi^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\right]\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi,\end{split}

where the last inequality holds because
\cos(\xi)
is negative on the intervals
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[2n\pi+\frac{\pi}{2},2n\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}\right]
.

Next, we give a lower bound of the above series. For the convenience of analysis, we discuss it in two cases:

|x-y|\leq s^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad|x-y|>s^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(a)
. When
|x-y|\leq s^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[I_{1}^{2}\right]\geq c_{1,H}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{2n\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{2n\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}}\left(1-e^{-2\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi.

(2.15)

Here, the series is convergent since
\alpha+2H>2
.

(b)
.
The case
|x-y|>s^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
is a little complicated. Denote

\displaystyle n_{0}:=\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:2n\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}\geq\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{|x-y|}{s^{1/\alpha}}\right\}.

(2.16)

Then,

\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{|x-y|}{s^{1/\alpha}}\leq 2n_{0}\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{|x-y|}{s^{1/\alpha}}+2\pi.

(2.17)

Since

1-\exp\left(-\frac{2s\xi^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\geq 1-\exp(-2^{1-\alpha}\pi^{\alpha})\ \ \text{for all }\ \xi\geq 2n_{0}\pi+\frac{\pi}{2},

we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{1}^{2}\right]&\geq c_{1,H}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{2n\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{2n\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{2s\xi^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\right]\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
&\geq c_{1,H}\left(1-\exp(-2^{1-\alpha}\pi^{\alpha})\right)|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{2n\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{2n\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}}\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
&\geq c_{2,5}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}\left(2n_{0}\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{2-2H-\alpha},\end{split}

(2.18)

where
c_{2,5}=\frac{c_{1,H}(1-\exp(-2^{1-\alpha}\pi^{\alpha}))}{2(2H+\alpha-2)}
. The final inequality follows from the monotonicity of
\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}
on
(0,\infty)
.

When
|x-y|>s^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, by
(
2.17
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{1}^{2}\right]\geq&\,c_{2,5}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}{\left(2n_{0}\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}^{2-2H-\alpha}\\
\geq&\,c_{2,5}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}{\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{|x-y|}{s^{1/\alpha}}+2\pi\right)}^{2-2H-\alpha}\\
=&\,c_{2,5}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{s^{1/\alpha}}+2\pi\cdot\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right)^{2-2H-\alpha}\\
\geq&\,c_{2,5}\left(\frac{5\pi}{2}\right)^{2-2H-\alpha}s^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}.\end{split}

(2.19)

Putting (
2.9
), (
2.10
), (
2
), and (
2.19
) together, we have

d_{1}((t,x),(s,y))\geq c_{2,6}\left(|x-y|^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}}\wedge s^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}+(t-s)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\right),

which is the lower bound part of (
2.8
).
The proof is complete.
∎

\Delta_{h}

d_{2,t,h}(x,y):=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|\Delta_{h}u(t,x)-\Delta_{h}u(t,y)|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.20)

Lemma 2.2
.

The following estimates hold for the function

.

(a)

(Upper bound)
For any
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}
,
there exists a positive constant
c_{2,7}
such that for all

(2.21)

(b)

(Lower bound)
There exists a positive constant
c_{2,8}
such that for all
|x-y|\geq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

and

h\leq\min\left\{\frac{3}{64}|x-y|,\frac{\pi}{4}\left(\frac{2}{\ln 2}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\}

d_{2,t,h}(x,y)\geq c_{2,8}h^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}}.

(2.22)

Proof.

The upper bound in (
2.21
) follows directly from (
1.10
), Lemma
2.1
, and the Minkowski inequality. Let us prove the lower bound in (
2.22
).

Assume
|x-y|\geq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
and
h\leq\frac{3}{64}|x-y|
. By (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), we have

\begin{split}d_{2,t,h}^{2}(x,y)=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{F}G_{\alpha}(t-s,x+h-\cdot)(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{\alpha}(t-s,x-\cdot)(\xi)\right.\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.-\mathcal{F}G_{\alpha}(t-s,y+h-\cdot)(\xi)+\mathcal{F}G_{\alpha}(t-s,y-\cdot)(\xi)\right|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds\\
=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2(t-s)|\xi|^{\alpha}}\left|1-e^{-ih\xi}\right|^{2}\left|1-e^{-i(x-y)\xi}\right|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds\\
=&\,4c_{1,H}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(1-e^{-2t\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\left[1-\cos(h\xi)\right]\left[1-\cos(|x-y|\xi)\right]\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi.\end{split}

(2.23)

Note that
for any
\xi\geq\frac{\pi|x-y|}{4h}
and
h\leq\frac{\pi}{4}\left(\frac{2}{\ln 2}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
,

1-\exp\left(-\frac{2t\xi^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}.

The elementary inequality

\displaystyle 1-\cos(x)\geq\frac{x^{2}}{4},\ \ \ \text{if }|x|\leq\frac{\pi}{2},

(2.24)

implies that for any
0<\xi\leq\frac{|x-y|\pi}{2h}
,

1-\cos\left(\frac{h\xi}{|x-y|}\right)\geq\frac{h^{2}\xi^{2}}{4|x-y|^{2}}.

(see
[
14
, pp. 400]
).
Therefore, by (
2.23
) and the change of variables, we have

\begin{split}d_{2,t,h}^{2}(x,y)=&\,4c_{1,H}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{2t\xi^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\right]\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{h\xi}{|x-y|}\right)\right]\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot[1-\cos(\xi)]\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
\geq&\,2c_{1,H}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-2}\int_{\frac{|x-y|\pi}{4h}}^{\infty}\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{h\xi}{|x-y|}\right)\right][1-\cos(\xi)]\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
\geq&\,\frac{c_{1,H}}{2}h^{2}|x-y|^{2H+\alpha-4}\int_{\frac{|x-y|\pi}{4h}}^{\frac{|x-y|\pi}{2h}}[1-\cos(\xi)]\xi^{3-2H-\alpha}d\xi\,.\end{split}

(2.25)

Define

\displaystyle k_{0}:=

\displaystyle\,\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:\frac{(6k+1)\pi}{3}\geq\frac{|x-y|\pi}{4h}\right\};

\displaystyle k_{1}:=

\displaystyle\,\sup\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:\frac{(6k+5)\pi}{3}\leq\frac{|x-y|\pi}{2h}\right\}.

(see
[
18
, pp. 22]
). Equivalently,

k_{0}=\Bigg\lfloor\frac{\frac{3|x-y|}{4h}-1}{6}\Bigg\rfloor+1,\ \ \ k_{1}=\Bigg\lfloor\frac{\frac{3|x-y|}{2h}-5}{6}\Bigg\rfloor.

When
h\leq\frac{3}{64}|x-y|
, it is easy to obtain that
k_{0}<k_{1}
. In this case, let

I_{k}:=\,\left(\frac{(6k+1)\pi}{3},\frac{(6k+5)\pi}{3}\right].

Then,

\bigcup_{k=k_{0}}^{k_{1}}I_{k}\subseteq\left[\frac{|x-y|\pi}{4h},\frac{|x-y|\pi}{2h}\right].

Consequently, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\frac{|x-y|\pi}{4h}}^{\frac{|x-y|\pi}{2h}}\bigl(1-\cos\xi\bigr)\xi^{3-2H-\alpha}\,d\xi&\geq\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{k_{1}}\int_{I_{k}}\bigl(1-\cos\xi\bigr)\xi^{3-2H-\alpha}\,d\xi\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{k_{1}}\int_{I_{k}}\xi^{3-2H-\alpha}\,d\xi\\
&\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\frac{(6k_{0}+1)\pi}{3}}^{\frac{(6k_{1}+5)\pi}{3}}\xi^{3-2H-\alpha}\,d\xi.\end{split}

(2.26)

Here, the fact that
1-\cos\xi\geq\frac{1}{2}
for
\xi\in I_{k}
is used in the second inequality,
and the monotonicity of
\xi^{3-2H-\alpha}
is used in the last step.

From the definitions of
k_{0}
and
k_{1}
, we observe that when
h\leq\frac{3}{64}|x-y|
,

\displaystyle\frac{(6k_{0}+1)\pi}{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|x-y|\pi}{4h}+2\pi\leq\frac{11|x-y|\pi}{32h},

\displaystyle\frac{(6k_{1}+5)\pi}{3}

\displaystyle>\frac{|x-y|\pi}{2h}-2\pi\geq\frac{13|x-y|\pi}{32h}.

Consequently, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\frac{(6k_{0}+1)\pi}{3}}^{\frac{(6k_{1}+5)\pi}{3}}\xi^{3-2H-\alpha}\,d\xi&=\frac{1}{4-2H-\alpha}\left[\left(\frac{(6k_{1}+5)\pi}{3}\right)^{4-2H-\alpha}-\left(\frac{(6k_{0}+1)\pi}{3}\right)^{4-2H-\alpha}\right]\\
&\geq\frac{\pi^{4-2H-\alpha}}{4-2H-\alpha}\left[\left(\frac{13}{32}\right)^{4-2H-\alpha}-\left(\frac{11}{32}\right)^{4-2H-\alpha}\right]\left(\frac{|x-y|}{h}\right)^{4-2H-\alpha}.\end{split}

(2.27)

Combining (
2.25
), (
2.26
), and (
2.27
), we obtain (
2.22
). The proof is complete.

(b)

Proof.

and the Minkowski inequality. Now, we prove the lower bound (

Using the Fourier representation (
2.2

\begin{split}d_{3,t,\tau}^{2}(x,y)\geq&\,\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\Bigg|\int_{t}^{t+\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[G_{\alpha}(t+\tau-s,x-z)-G_{\alpha}(t+\tau-s,y-z)\Big]W(ds,dz)\Bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\\

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem

.

The proof follows the methodology established in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
, employing two key probabilistic tools:

•

For the upper bound: Talagrand’s majorizing measure theorem (Theorem
A.1
);

•

For the lower bound: Sudakov’s minoration theorem (Theorem

).

For conciseness, let

\mathbb{T}:=[0,T]\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathbb{L}:=[-L,L].

1. Upper bound.
We begin by constructing a dyadic partition scheme for the parameter space
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{L}
. For each integer
n\geq 1
, we define

•

Temporal partition:

[0,T]=\bigcup_{j=0}^{2^{2^{n-1}}-1}\Bigl[j\cdot 2^{-2^{n-1}}T,\ (j+1)\cdot 2^{-2^{n-1}}T\Bigr)

(3.1)

•

Spatial partition:

[-L,L]=\bigcup\limits_{k=-2^{2^{n-2}}}^{2^{2^{n-2}}-1}\left[k\cdot 2^{-2^{n-2}}L,(k+1)\cdot 2^{-2^{n-2}}L\right).

(3.2)

For any
(t,x)
satisfying that

\displaystyle j2^{-2^{n-1}}T

\displaystyle\leq t<(j+1)2^{-2^{n-1}}T,

\displaystyle k2^{-2^{n-2}}L

\displaystyle\leq x<(k+1)2^{-2^{n-2}}L,

we set

A_{n}(t,x):=\Bigl[j2^{-2^{n-1}}T,\ (j+1)2^{-2^{n-1}}T\Bigr)\times\Bigl[k2^{-2^{n-2}}L,\ (k+1)2^{-2^{n-2}}L\Bigr).

By Theorem
A.1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{L}}u(t,x)\right]\leq c_{3,1}\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{L}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{n/2}\mathrm{diam}(A_{n}(t,x)),

(3.3)

where
\mathrm{diam}(A_{n}(t,x))
denotes the diameter of the set
A_{n}(t,x)
with respect to the metric
d_{1}
defined in (
2.7
).

By Lemma
2.1
, we obtain the following uniform bound for all
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
:

\begin{split}\mathrm{diam}(A_{n}(t,x))\leq c_{2,3}\Bigl[\left(2^{-\left(\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}\right)2^{n-2}}L^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}}\right)\wedge T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}+2^{-\left(\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}\right)2^{n-1}}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\Bigr].\end{split}

(3.4)

We proceed by considering two distinct cases based on the relationship between the spatial and temporal scales:

\displaystyle\text{Case 1: }L<T^{1/\alpha};\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{Case 2: }L\geq T^{1/\alpha}.

(1)
. When
L<T^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{L}}u(t,x)\right]\leq&\,c_{3,2}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{\alpha}}.\end{split}

(3.5)

(2)
. When
L\geq T^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
,
let

\displaystyle N_{0}:=

\displaystyle\,\inf\left\{n\geq 2:2^{-2^{n-2}}\leq\frac{T^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{L}\wedge\frac{1}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\inf\left\{n\geq 2:2^{n-2}\geq\log_{2}\left(\frac{L}{T^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\vee 2\right)\right\}.

By (
3.4
) and the definition of
N_{0}
, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=N_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{n}{2}}{\rm{diam\,}}(A_{n}(t,x))\\
\leq&\,c_{2,3}\left(\sum_{n=N_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot 2^{-\left(\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}\right)\cdot 2^{n-2}}L^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}}+\sum_{n=N_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot 2^{-\left(\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}\right)\cdot 2^{n-1}}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\right)\\
\leq&\,c_{2,3}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\left[\sum_{n=N_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{2^{2^{N_{0}-2}}}{2^{2^{n-2}}}\right)^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2}}+\sum_{n=N_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{n}{2}-\left(\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}\right)\cdot 2^{n-1}}\right]\\
\leq&\,c_{3,3}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}.\end{split}

(3.6)

The definition of
N_{0}
ensures that

2^{\frac{N_{0}}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{3}{2}}\Psi(T,L).

(3.7)

This, together with (

) and (

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{L}}u(t,x)\right]\\

\leq&\,2\sqrt{2}\left(\sqrt{2}+1\right)c_{3,1}c_{2,3}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}2^{\frac{N_{0}}{2}}+c_{3,1}c_{3,3}T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\\
\leq&\,\left(2^{\frac{5}{2}}\sqrt{2}\left(\sqrt{2}+1\right)c_{3,1}c_{2,3}+c_{3,1}c_{3,3}\right)T^{\frac{2H+\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\Psi(T,L).\end{split}

(3.8)

Putting (

Theorem

Denote

\begin{split}u_{\varrho}(t,x):=&\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{h}u(t,x)\varrho(h)dh\\
=&\,\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}[G_{\alpha}(t-s,x+h-z)-G_{\alpha}(t-s,x-z)]\varrho(h)dh\right)W(ds,dz).\end{split}

(3.29)

By (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[u^{2}_{\varrho}(t,x)\right]=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{F}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}[G_{\alpha}(t-s,x+h-\cdot)-G_{\alpha}(t-s,x-\cdot)]\varrho(h)dh\right)(\xi)\right|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds\\
=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{\alpha}-i(x+h)\xi}-e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{\alpha}-ix\xi}\right]\varrho(h)dh\right|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds\\
=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-2t\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\cos(h\xi))\varrho(h)dh\right)^{2}\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi\\
\leq&\,4c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-2t\xi^{\alpha}}\right)\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi.\end{split}

The last integral converges since
\alpha+2H>2
, which guarantees that
u_{\varrho}(t,x)
is a well-defined Gaussian random field.

Next, we establish the lower bound for the canonical metric defined by

d_{4,t,\varrho}:=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|u_{\varrho}(t,x)-u_{\varrho}(t,y)\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.30)

We bound this metric for
|x-y|\geq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, following the approach in (
3.9
). Using (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), we obtain

\begin{split}d^{2}_{4,t,\varrho}=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathcal{F}\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}[G_{\alpha}(t-s,x+h-\cdot)-G_{\alpha}(t-s,x-\cdot)\\
&\,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -G_{\alpha}(t-s,y+h-\cdot)+G_{\alpha}(t-s,y-\cdot)]\varrho(h)dh\big)(\xi)\big|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds\\
=&\,c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(t-s)|\xi|^{\alpha}}e^{-ix\xi}\left(1-e^{-i(y-x)\xi}\right)\left(e^{-ih\xi}-1\right)\varrho(h)dh\right|^{2}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi ds\\
=&\,4c_{1,H}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-2t\xi^{\alpha}}\right)(1-\cos(|x-y|\xi))\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(h\xi))\varrho(h)dh\right)^{2}\xi^{1-2H-\alpha}d\xi.\end{split}

(3.31)

When
\xi\geq 1
, by (
2.24
), we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(h\xi))\varrho(h)dh\geq&\,c_{3,12}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\cos(h\xi))h^{-\frac{H}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}dh\\
=&\,c_{3,12}\xi^{\frac{H}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}-1}\int_{0}^{\xi}(1-\cos(h))h^{-\frac{H}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}dh\\
\geq&\,c_{3,12}\xi^{\frac{H}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}-1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\cos(h))h^{-\frac{H}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}dh\\
\geq&\,\frac{c_{3,12}}{4}\xi^{\frac{H}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}-1}\int_{0}^{1}h^{-\frac{H}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}+2}dh\\
=&\,\frac{c_{3,12}}{12-2H-\alpha}\xi^{\frac{H}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}-1}.\end{split}

(3.32)

This, together with (
3.31
), implies that

\begin{split}d^{2}_{4,t,\varrho}\geq&\,\frac{4c_{1,H}c_{3,12}^{2}}{(12-2H-\alpha)^{2}}\left(1-e^{-2t}\right)\int_{1}^{\infty}(1-\cos(|x-y|\xi))\xi^{-H-\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}d\xi\\
=&\,\frac{4c_{1,H}c_{3,12}^{2}}{(12-2H-\alpha)^{2}}\left(1-e^{-2t}\right)|x-y|^{H+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{|x-y|}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\xi))\xi^{-H-\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}d\xi.\\
\end{split}

(3.33)

Define

k_{3}:=\left\lfloor\frac{|x-y|-\frac{\pi}{2}}{2\pi}\right\rfloor+1.

(3.34)

Then, there exists a positive constant
c_{3,13}
such that

\begin{split}\int_{|x-y|}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\xi))\xi^{-H-\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}d\xi\geq&\,\sum_{k=k_{3}}^{\infty}\int_{2k\pi+\frac{\pi}{2}}^{2k\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}}(1-\cos(\xi))\xi^{-H-\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}d\xi\\
\geq&\,\pi\sum_{k=k_{3}}^{\infty}\left(2k\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)^{-H-\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\\
\geq&\,c_{3,13}\left(2k_{3}\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}\right)^{-H-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\\
\geq&\,c_{3,13}\left(|x-y|+3\pi\right)^{-H-\frac{\alpha}{2}},\end{split}

(3.35)

where the second inequality holds because
\cos(\xi)
is negative on the intervals
\left[2k\pi+\frac{\pi}{2},2k\pi+\frac{3\pi}{2}\right]
and
\xi^{-H-\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}
is a decreasing function on
(0,\infty)
.

Combining (
3.35
) with (
3.33
), we have that for any
|x-y|\geq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
,

\begin{split}d^{2}_{4,t,\varrho}\geq&\,\frac{4c_{1,H}c_{3,13}c_{3,12}^{2}}{(12-2H-\alpha)^{2}}\left(1-e^{-2t}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{3\pi}{|x-y|}}\right)^{H+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\\
\geq&\,\frac{4c_{1,H}c_{3,13}c_{3,12}^{2}}{(12-2H-\alpha)^{2}}\left(1-e^{-2t}\right)\left(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+3\pi}\right)^{H+\frac{\alpha}{2}}.\end{split}

(3.36)

Consider the interval
[-L,L]
with
L\geq t^{1/\alpha}
. We select the grid points

x_{j}=jt^{1/\alpha}\quad\text{for}\quad j=0,\pm 1,\ldots,\pm\left\lfloor L/t^{1/\alpha}\right\rfloor.

Using (
3.36
) and Theorem
A.2
as in (
3.9
), there exists a positive constant
c_{t}
depending on
t
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{-L\leq x\leq L}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{h}u(t,x)\varrho(h)dh\right]\geq c_{t}\sqrt{\log_{2}L}.

(3.37)

Combining with (
3.37
) and (
3.28
), we obtain (
1.7
).
The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem A.1
.

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\Big]\asymp\gamma_{2}(T,d):=\inf_{\mathcal{A}}\sup_{t\in T}\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{\frac{n}{2}}{\rm{diam\,}}(A_{n}(t)),

A

d

A_{n}(t)

Theorem A.2
.

Theorem A.3
.

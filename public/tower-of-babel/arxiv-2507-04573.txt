A new characterization of the weakly Laskerian (FSF) modules
Ali Fathi

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative Noetherian ring and M M be an R R -module such that the set of associated prime ideals of the quotient module M / L M/L is finite for all submodules L L of M M . In this paper, it is shown that there is a finitely generated submodule N N of M M such that the set of associated primes of M / N M/N and the support of M / N M/N are equal.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
13C05, 13E99

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
R
is a commutative Noetherian ring with nonzero identity.
Let
N
be a proper submodule of an
R
-module
M
. Then
N
is called a
primary submodule
of
M
when for all
r\in R
and
x\in M
if
rx\in N
, then
x\in N
or
r^{n}M\subseteq N
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
N
is a primary submodule of
M
, then
\mathfrak{p}:=\surd(\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(M/N))

is a prime ideal of
R
and
N
is called a
\mathfrak{p}
-primary submodule of
M
. An expression of
N
as an intersection of finitely many primary submodules of
M
is called a
primary decomposition
of
N
in
M
.
Such a primary decomposition

N=M_{1}\cap\dots\cap M_{n}\quad\textrm{with }M_{i}\ \textrm{ $\mathfrak{p}_{i}$-primary in }M\ (1\leq i\leq n)

of
N
in
M
is said to be minimal when
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{n}
are distinct and
\bigcap_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n}M_{j}\nsubseteq M_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. In this situation, we have
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/N)=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{n}\}
; see
[
3
]
for more details. An
R
-module
M
is said to be
Laskerian
if each proper submodule of
M
has a primary decomposition in
M
.

Divaani–Aazar and Mafi in
[
2
]
defined an
R
-module
M
to be
weakly Laskerian
, if
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/N)
is a finite set for each submodule
N
of
M
. Therefore Laskerian modules are weakly Laskerian.

On the other hand, Quy in
[
4
]
introduced the class of FSF modules. An
R
-module
M
is called an FSF module if there is a
F
initely generated submodule
N
of
M
such that the
S
upport of the
quotient module
M/N
is
F
inite. It is easy to see that an FSF module is weakly Laskerian. Bahmanpour in
[
1
]
proved that the converse statement is also true and so the class of FSF
R
-modules and the class of weakly Laskerian
R
-modules are equal. In this short note, we improve this result by proving that if
M
is weakly Laskerian, then
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/N)=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/N)
for some finitely generated submodule
N
of
M
(and so
M
is an FSF module).

2.
Main Result

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

M
is a weakly Laskerian
R
-module;

(ii)

there is a finitely generated submodule
N
of
M
such that
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/N)=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/N)
and
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/N)
is a finite set;

(iii)

M
is an FSF
R
-module.

Proof.

The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is clear. To prove the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i), assume that
N
is a finitely generated submodule of
M
such that
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/N)
is finite. Suppose that
L
is an arbitrary submodule of
M
. It follows from the exact sequence

0\rightarrow(N+L)/L\rightarrow M/L\rightarrow M/(N+L)\rightarrow 0

that
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/L)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}((M/N)/((N+L)/N))\cup\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N/(N\cap L))
. Therefore
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/L)
is a finite set and consequently
M
is weakly Laskerian.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Assume that
M
is weakly Laskerian and to prove (ii) it is sufficient for us to show that
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/N)=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/N)
for some finitely generated submodule
N
of
M
.

We set
M_{0}:=0
(the zero submodule of
M
). Assume
i\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/M_{i-1})=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i-1})
, then we set
N:=M_{i-1}
and we end the process. Otherwise, we construct
x_{i},M_{i},\mathfrak{q}_{i},\mathfrak{p}_{i},\Sigma_{i}
as follows. We set
\Sigma_{i}:=\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/M_{i-1})\setminus\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i-1})
. Since
\Sigma_{i}
is a non-empty set of ideals of
R
and
R
is Noetherian,
\Sigma_{i}
has a maximal element under inclusion, say
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.
There exist
\mathfrak{q}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i-1})
and
x_{i}\in M
such that
\mathfrak{q}_{i}=(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{i-1})\subset\mathfrak{p}_{i}
(note that
\mathfrak{q}_{i}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i-1})
while
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\notin\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i-1})
and so
\mathfrak{q}_{i}\neq\mathfrak{p}_{i}
). Now, we set
M_{i}:=M_{i-1}+\mathfrak{p}_{i}x_{i}
. It is clear that
M_{i}
is a finitely generated submodule of
M
. We claim that after a finite number of steps the procedure must stop. Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/M_{i})\neq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i})
for all
i
and
x_{i},M_{i},\mathfrak{q}_{i},\mathfrak{p}_{i},\Sigma_{i}
are constructed for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
as above. Before continuing the proof, we need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M_{j}/M_{j-1})=\{\mathfrak{q}_{j}\}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{q}_{j}
, we obtain
M_{j-1}\subset M_{j}
and so
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M_{j}/M_{j-1})\neq\emptyset
. Now, suppose that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M_{j}/M_{j-1})
. Then
\mathfrak{p}=(0:_{R}a_{j}x_{j}+M_{j-1})
for some
a_{j}\in\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. Therefore

\mathfrak{p}a_{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{j}
and so
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{j}
. The reverse inclusion is clear and hence
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}_{j}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
1\leq i\leq j
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{j})\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{j})
and
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
whenever
1\leq i<j
.

Proof.

First, we prove that
\mathfrak{p}_{j}=(0:_{R}x_{j}+M_{j})
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
r\in(0:_{R}x_{j}+M_{j})
. Therefore
rx_{j}=m_{j-1}+a_{j}x_{j}
for some
m_{j-1}\in M_{j-1},a_{j}\in\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. It follows that
r-a_{j}\in\mathfrak{q}_{j}
and so
r\in\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. This shows that
(0:_{R}x_{j}+M_{j})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. The reverse inclusion is clear and so
\mathfrak{p}_{j}=(0:_{R}x_{j}+M_{j})\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{j})
for all
1\leq j
.

Next, we show that if
1\leq i<j
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
for some
i,j
with
i<j
. We can assume that
j
is the least positive integer with the property that there exists
i
with
i<j
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. If
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=\mathfrak{p}_{j}
, then since
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{i})
we have
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i})
while
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\notin\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{j-1})
by definition. Therefore
i<j-1
and there exists
i+1\leq k<j
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{k-1})
and
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\notin\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{k})
. Also, if
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{j}
, then, by the maximality of
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
in
\Sigma_{i}
,
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i-1})
while
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\notin\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{j-1})
by definition. Therefore there exits
i\leq k<j
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{k-1})
and
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\notin\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{k})
. In both cases,
we deduce from the exact sequence

0\rightarrow M_{k}/M_{k-1}\rightarrow M/M_{k-1}\rightarrow M/M_{k}\rightarrow 0

that
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M_{k}/M_{k-1})
and so Lemma
2.2
implies that
\mathfrak{p}_{j}=\mathfrak{q}_{k}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{k}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
, we obtain
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{k}
, which is impossible by the minimality of
j
. Therefore
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
for all
1\leq i<j
.

Finally, we prove by induction on
j
that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{j})
for all
1\leq i\leq j
. If
j=1
, then we have
\mathfrak{p}_{1}=(0:_{R}x_{1}+M_{1})
. Now assume that
j>1
and the result has been proved for the smaller values of
j
. The case
i=j
is proved at the beginning of the proof of this lemma. So assume that
i<j
. By the above proof, we have
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{p}_{j}
and hence there exists
s\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}\setminus\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. Suppose that
r\in(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{j})
. Therefore
rx_{i}=m_{j-1}+a_{j}x_{j}
for some
m_{j-1}\in M_{j-1},a_{j}\in\mathfrak{p}_{j}
. Hence

srx_{i}=sm_{j-1}+sa_{j}x_{j}
and so
sa_{j}x_{j}\in M_{j-1}
. It follows that
sa_{j}\in\mathfrak{q}_{j}
and so
a_{j}\in\mathfrak{q}_{j}
. Therefore
a_{j}x_{j}\in M_{j-1}
and so
rx_{i}\in M_{j-1}
. By the inductive hypothesis, we have
(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{j-1})=\mathfrak{p}_{i}
and hence
r\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Since
r
is an arbitrary element of
(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{j})
, we obtain
(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{j})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. The revers inclusion is clear. Therefore
(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{j})=\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, as required. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Now, we continue the proof of the theorem. We set
M_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}M_{i}
(note that
M_{1}\subset M_{2}\subset M_{3}\subset\dots
). We show that
(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{\infty})=\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume
r\in(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{\infty})
. Therefore
rx_{i}\in M_{j}
for some sufficiently large positive integer
j
. We can assume that
i\leq j
and so
r\in(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{j})=\mathfrak{p}_{i}
by above lemma. This shows that
(0:_{R}x_{i}+M_{\infty})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. The reverse inclusion is clear and so, in view of the above lemma,
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{\infty})
contains the distinct prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\dots
, which is impossible because
M
is weakly Laskerian. Therefore
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(M/M_{i})=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M/M_{i})
for some
i
, as required. This proves the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) and so the proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

.

CTMNet: causal trend evolution and adaptive modulation for time series forecasting
Authors: Yihao Wang, Xiao Chen, Jing Chen, MingXin Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn multivariate time series forecasting, series decomposition architectures that decouple trend and residual components have become a mainstream solution for handling data non-stationarity. However, existing methods are limited by the “component independence assumption,” modeling these components independently, thereby neglecting the intrinsic constraints macro-trends impose on micro-fluctuations. Furthermore, trend modeling faces a dilemma between efficiency and expressiveness: global attention-based methods are prone to introducing non-causal noise and disrupt trend continuity, whereas simple linear mappings lack dynamic modeling capabilities. To address these challenges, we propose the Causal Trend Evolution and Adaptive Modulation Network (CTMNet), a coupled decomposition architecture that employs a modeling strategy balancing differentiation and interaction. First, we design a Causal Trend Encoder (CTE), which utilizes patch-level causal convolution and gating mechanisms to introduce a strict temporal causal inductive bias, accurately characterizing the unidirectional evolution of trends with linear complexity. Second, for the residual component, we innovatively propose an Adaptive Trend Modulation Interaction (ATMI) mechanism. This mechanism uses the causal trend state extracted by the CTE as a contextual prior to dynamically generate affine transformation parameters, to hierarchically calibrate residual features. This design not only maintains the physical consistency of trend modeling but also restores the deep coupling between trend and residual components. Extensive experiments on 10 real-world benchmark datasets, covering both long- and short-term forecasting, demonstrate that CTMNet achieves competitive or leading performance in terms of prediction accuracy and robustness compared to 7 state-of-the-art (SOTA) models.Keywords: Multivariate time series forecasting, Series decomposition, Causal trend evolution, Adaptive modulation, Differentiated modelingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Time series forecasting is a pivotal technology supporting modern decision-making, playing a crucial role in fields such as energy scheduling, traffic management, meteorological early warning systems, and financial risk management1–3. With the proliferation of the Internet of Things (IoT) and sensor technologies, monitoring data is increasingly characterized by high dimensionality, long-range dependencies, and non-stationarity. These characteristics impose higher demands on multivariate time series forecasting models. In recent years, series decomposition architectures have emerged as a mainstream paradigm for addressing these challenges. The core idea involves decoupling complex sequences into low-frequency trend components and high-frequency residual components, thereby guiding the model to learn long-term evolutionary laws and short-term fluctuation details in a hierarchical manner. Models such as DLinear4, FEDformer5, and TimeMixer6 have all validated the effectiveness of this strategy.

However, existing methods are generally constrained by the “component independence assumption.” Mainstream models, such as TimeMixer6 and PDF7, typically employ dual-branch independent encoding and only perform linear fusion at the output stage. This approach neglects the intrinsic constraints of macro-trends on micro-residual fluctuations. For instance, in traffic flow forecasting, the amplitude of fluctuations during congested periods is usually more intense than during free-flow periods. Ignoring this global constraint makes it difficult for the model to adapt to sudden changes in the data. Therefore, establishing an effective interaction mechanism between components while maintaining the advantages of decomposition is a core problem that urgently needs to be solved.

Furthermore, trend modeling faces a dilemma regarding the trade-off between efficiency and expressiveness. Linear mapping methods based on Multilayer Perceptrons (MLP) are computationally efficient but lack dynamic modeling capabilities. While Transformer-based methods can capture long-range dependencies, their computational complexity limits their application to long sequences. Moreover, the global receptive field of attention mechanisms can easily introduce non-causal future information noise during training. For relatively smooth low-frequency trends, excessive global interaction is not only redundant but may also disrupt the temporal causal consistency intrinsic to trend evolution. Existing research has failed to effectively solve the problem of accurately characterizing the causal evolution characteristics of trends while reducing computational redundancy.

To address the aforementioned problems, this paper proposes the Causal Trend Evolution and Adaptive Modulation Network (CTMNet). This model reconstructs the feature extraction and interaction paradigm of the decomposition architecture. CTMNet adopts a differentiated modeling strategy. For the trend component, we design a Causal Trend Encoder (CTE). This module replaces self-attention with patch-level causal convolution and gating mechanisms, introducing a strict temporal causal inductive bias to efficiently characterize the unidirectional evolution of trends with complexity, thereby avoiding information leakage and non-causal noise. For the interaction between residual terms and trend components, we propose an Adaptive Trend Modulation Interaction (ATMI) mechanism. This mechanism uses the causal trend state extracted by the CTE as a global context to dynamically generate affine transformation parameters for the hierarchical calibration of residual features. Through a learnable soft gating mechanism, this design achieves flexible coupling. It utilizes macro-trends to guide the modeling of micro-details while preventing interference from invalid prior information, thus significantly improving the model’s forecasting robustness in heteroscedastic scenarios.

It is worth emphasizing the necessity of the proposed approach from both empirical and theoretical perspectives. Empirically, existing decomposition methods such as TimeMixer and DLinear show notable performance degradation on datasets exhibiting strong heteroscedasticity (e.g., Traffic, PEMS), where the variance of residual fluctuations is inherently modulated by the underlying trend state. This performance gap motivates the need for an explicit trend-residual interaction mechanism. Theoretically, the conditional heteroscedasticity phenomenon—where residual distributions depend on the latent trend regime—is well-established in time series analysis (e.g., GARCH models8), yet modern deep learning forecasters largely ignore this structural prior by treating decomposed components independently. CTMNet bridges this gap by introducing a learnable, trend-conditioned modulation that is both principled and practically effective.

Related work

Multivariate time series forecasting models

The core task of time series forecasting is to infer future trends based on historical observational data. Early research primarily relied on statistical models such as ARIMA9 and Prophet10. While these methods offer good interpretability, they struggle to effectively capture complex non-linear patterns and long-term dependencies in real-world data. With the advancement of deep learning, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN)11 and their variants, such as LSTM12 and GRU13, characterized temporal dependencies through recursive structures. However, they often face gradient vanishing or exploding problems when processing long sequences.

To address these limitations, methods based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN)14 emerged. For instance, Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN)15 utilize causal dilated convolutions to expand the temporal receptive field; SCINet16 enhances multi-scale feature extraction capabilities through recursive downsampling; and TimesNet17 transforms time series into a 2D space to capture multi-period patterns. Although these methods improve computational efficiency, fixed convolution kernels still exhibit limitations when modeling dynamic long-range dependencies18.

In recent years, Transformers19 have achieved significant progress in modeling long-range dependencies due to their self-attention mechanisms. However, their computational complexity restricts their application to long sequences. Consequently, a series of improvement schemes have been proposed: Informer20 uses sparse attention to reduce computational costs; Autoformer21 introduces a sequence decomposition mechanism; PatchTST22 enhances local semantic perception through a patching strategy; and iTransformer23 applies the attention mechanism to the variable dimension to strengthen cross-variable correlations. These variants have demonstrated significant improvements in both forecasting efficiency and accuracy.

Meanwhile, minimalist linear models, represented by DLinear4, have challenged the necessity of complex architectures. Their results indicate that under a sequence decomposition framework, lightweight linear layers can also achieve excellent prediction performance. TimeMixer6 further explores the potential of linear models through multi-scale mixing. Similarly, FiLM24 utilizes Legendre polynomial projections to compress historical representations with complexity, aiming to filter out high-frequency noise. While FITS25 and ModernTCN26 explore efficiency boundaries from frequency domain and convolutional perspectives, these methods-particularly those relying on fixed mathematical basis functions like FiLM-often lack the adaptability to capture instantaneous trend shifts compared to learnable causal evolutions. Moreover, most of these methods employ homogeneous encoders to process mixed features. When facing non-stationary data that characterizes both strong trends and high-frequency fluctuations, it is difficult to balance global evolutionary laws and local detail expression, often making it challenging to achieve both dynamic modeling capability and computational efficiency.

Sequence decomposition and component interaction strategies

To cope with the non-stationarity of time series and the entanglement between different patterns, sequence decomposition has become a core component of modern prediction models. The fundamental idea is to decompose the original sequence into low-frequency trend components and high-frequency residual components, thereby reducing the difficulty of overall modeling. Autoformer21 was the first to integrate a built-in sequence decomposition module into a deep learning architecture; FEDformer5 further combined frequency domain transformations to enhance decomposition robustness. Subsequent works, such as MICN27 and Koopa28, enriched decomposition strategies from the perspectives of multi-scale convolution and dynamic system decoupling, respectively. Recent studies like LiNo29 and FORSEER30 have also confirmed that this divide-and-conquer strategy can effectively improve the model’s ability to characterize multi-frequency features and enhance generalization performance.

Despite the fact that decomposition architectures reduce modeling difficulty, existing methods generally face two key bottlenecks. First is the limitation of the “component independence assumption.” Mainstream models, such as TimeMixer6 and PDF7, mostly adopt dual-branch independent encoding and only perform simple linear superposition at the output stage. This approach severs the intrinsic constraint of macro-trends on micro-residual fluctuations. Although some studies have attempted to introduce interaction mechanisms-for example, Crossformer31 focuses on dependencies between variables, and TSMixer32 explores multi-scale feature fusion-their interaction methods are mostly limited to feature concatenation or static alignment, failing to achieve adaptive guidance from the trend to the residual. Second is the lack of causal inductive bias in trend modeling. Existing methods for extracting trend terms mainly rely on moving averages or global attention mechanisms. The former is slow to respond to sudden trend changes and lacks dynamic modeling capability; the latter, due to its global receptive field, may introduce future information during training, disrupting the temporal causality that trends should strictly follow.

In summary, current research still exhibits significant deficiencies regarding the causal consistency of trend modeling and the adaptive interaction between decomposed components. The CTMNet proposed in this paper addresses these two core issues. On the one hand, it ensures the physical plausibility of trend evolution through a Causal Trend Encoder. On the other hand, it innovatively introduces an Adaptive Trend Modulation Interaction mechanism to reconstruct the dependency between the macro and micro levels that was severed during the decomposition process.

Methodology

This section details the core architecture of CTMNet. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the model follows a design philosophy of “Decomposition-Modeling-Modulation Fusion.” The input sequence is first decoupled into trend and residual components. Subsequently, differentiated modeling strategies are employed to capture low-frequency evolutionary laws and high-frequency fluctuation features, respectively. Finally, an adaptive modulation mechanism is utilized to realize the dynamic guidance of macro-trends on micro-residuals, thereby reconstructing the intrinsic connection between the two.

Theoretical motivation

The design of ATMI is grounded in the conditional heteroscedasticity perspective from time series analysis. In many real-world systems, residual variance is not constant but depends on the underlying trend state—a phenomenon well-documented in GARCH-family models8. By conditioning the residual feature transformation on the trend state , ATMI effectively implements a learnable form of conditional normalization, analogous to Conditional Batch Normalization34 and FiLM35 in vision tasks. The confidence gate provides a principled interpolation between independent and coupled modeling: when the trend prior is uninformative, recovers the independent baseline, ensuring that the modulation mechanism does not degrade performance. This “safe fallback” property guarantees that the coupled model’s representational capacity is a strict superset of the independent model, providing a theoretical lower bound on generalization performance relative to the independent decomposition baseline.

Conclusions

In this paper, we address the limitations associated with the “component independence assumption” prevalent in existing multivariate time series decomposition methods by proposing a novel Causal Trend Evolution and Adaptive Modulation model (CTMNet). The core of CTMNet lies in a modeling strategy that balances differentiation with interaction. On the one hand, we designed the Causal Trend Encoder (CTE). With linear computational complexity and strict adherence to temporal causality, this module accurately characterizes the continuous evolution of trends, effectively avoiding the non-causal noise interference often introduced by traditional global attention mechanisms. On the other hand, we innovatively proposed the Adaptive Trend Modulation Interaction (ATMI) mechanism. By utilizing dynamic affine parameters generated from macro-trend states alongside confidence gating, this mechanism achieves the hierarchical calibration of micro-residual features. This design not only promotes deep interaction between trend and residual components but also significantly enhances the model’s robustness to historical noise and distribution shifts, successfully reconstructing the intrinsic constraints between macro-trends and micro-fluctuations that are typically severed during the decomposition process.

Extensive experiments on multiple real-world benchmark datasets demonstrate that CTMNet achieves highly competitive performance, obtaining the best results in the majority of benchmark configurations for both long- and short-term forecasting tasks. This work offers a novel perspective on causal modeling within time series decomposition frameworks. Future work will explore extending this framework to large-scale time series pre-training tasks to verify its generalization and transfer capabilities in zero-shot or few-shot scenarios.

Abstract
. We present a pointwise inequality for adjacent even Legendre polynomials in high dimensional spheres featuring the effect of spectral gaps. This improves a recent result of Imbert, Silvestre and Villani that is crucially used in their study of the Fisher information for the Boltzmann equation.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 33C55. Secondary 30C10.

1.
Introduction

Recently, in proving the monotonicity of the Fisher information for the Boltzmann equation,
Imbert, Silvestre and Villani established certain
integro-differential
log-Sobolev inequalities and Poincaré inequalities for the Boltzmann operator

\mathcal{B}_{b}(f)(\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(f(\sigma^{\prime})-f(\sigma))b(\sigma^{\prime}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma^{\prime}.

In particular, the study of their Poincaré inequalities is reduced to the following interesting pointwise inequality for even Legendre polynomials, see
[
1
, Prop. 7.6]
.

Proposition 1

(Imbert, Silvestre and Villani, 2024)
.

For the Legendre polynomials of order
\ell
normalized so that
P_{\ell}(1)=1
, we have for all
x\in[-1,1]
and
\ell\geq 1
,

(1)

1-P_{2\ell}(x)\leq\frac{\lambda_{2\ell}}{\lambda_{2}}(1-P_{2}(x)).

Here
\lambda_{\ell}=\ell(\ell+d-2)
is the eigenvalue of the (minus) spherical Laplacian
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}

corresponding to spherical harmonics of order
\ell
, and the unique axially symmetric spherical harmonic
Y_{\ell}
of order
\ell

such that
Y_{\ell}(e_{1})=1
is given by
Y_{\ell}(\sigma):=P_{\ell}(e_{1}\cdot\sigma)
.

More precisely, (
1
) is used in
[
1
]
to prove the following eigenvalue comparison

(2)

\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}_{2\ell}}{{\lambda}_{2\ell}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}}{{\lambda}_{2}},\quad\ell\geq 1,

where
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\ell}
is the eigenvalue of the (minus) Boltzmann operator
-\mathcal{B}_{b}
corresponding to spherical harmonics of order
\ell
.
From (
2
) one obtains immediately the Poincaré inequalities: for some dimensional and
b
-dependent constant
C_{P}(d,b)
, we have

(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})\geq C_{P}(d,b)(-\mathcal{B}_{b})

in the sense of quadratic form on functions which are even on the sphere in the sense
f(\sigma)=f(-\sigma)
. Moreover,
C_{P}(d,b)
can be explicitly evaluated: for any
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

C_{P}(d,b)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\lambda}_{2}}{\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}}=\frac{d-1}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(1-(e\cdot\sigma)^{2})b(e\cdot\sigma)d\sigma}.

Here we present an improvement of Proposition
1
featuring the spectral gaps .

Proposition 2
.

For all
x\in[-1,1]
and
\ell\geq 1
,

(3)

P_{2\ell}(x)-P_{2(\ell+1)}(x)\leq\frac{\lambda_{2(\ell+1)}-\lambda_{2\ell}}{\lambda_{2}}(1-P_{2}(x)).

Remark 3
.

Apparently, a telescoping summation of (
3
) leads to (
1
).

Remark 4
.

It would be interesting to find a purely spectral proof of (
1
) or (
3
).

2.
Proof of Proposition
2

Our proof is based on the following crucial observation.
When
d=2
,
P_{\ell}(x)
corresponds to
\cos(\ell\Theta)
if
x=\cos\Theta
with
\Theta\in[0,\pi]
,
so in this case (
3
) becomes

(4)

\cos(2\ell\Theta)-\cos(2(\ell+1)\Theta)\leq(2\ell+1)(1-\cos(2\Theta)).

By the
sum-to-product formula
for trigonometric functions, (
4
) is equivalent to

\sin((2\ell+1)\Theta)\leq(2\ell+1)\sin\Theta.

Therefore, it is enough to prove the following elementary estimate

|\sin((2\ell+1)\theta)|\leq(2\ell+1)|\sin\theta|,\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi],

which can be verified simply by induction on
\ell
, see for example
[
1
]
.

For higher dimensions, we need the following basic tool, see
[
2
, Thm. 4.26]
.

Lemma 5

(Integral representation formula)
.

Assume
d\geq 3
. We have

P_{\ell}(x)=\frac{\omega_{d-3}}{\omega_{d-2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell}(1-s^{2})^{\frac{d-4}{2}}\,ds.

Here, for
p\geq 0
, the dimensional constant
\omega_{p}
is
\frac{2\pi^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{2})}
.

Note that by the
double-angle formula
, (
4
) is also equivalent to

(5)

\sin^{2}((\ell+1)\theta)-\sin^{2}(\ell\theta)\leq(2\ell+1)\sin^{2}\theta.

We also need the following technical result which is somehow inspired by (
5
).

Lemma 6
.

The following inequality holds for all
\ell\geq 1
and
x,s\in[-1,1]
,

\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell+1}\right)^{2}-\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell}\right)^{2}\leq(2\ell+1)s^{2}(1-x^{2}).

Proof.

Fix
x,s\in[-1,1]
. Note that

\left|x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right|^{2}=x^{2}+s^{2}(1-x^{2})\leq 1.

Let
r=\left|x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right|^{-1}\geq 1
. We set

\sin\theta=rs\sqrt{1-x^{2}},\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]

and

A=r^{2\ell}\left(\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell+1}\right)^{2}-\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell}\right)^{2}\right).

Since
r\geq 1
, we have

(6)

A\leq r^{2(\ell+1)}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell+1}\right)^{2}-r^{2\ell}\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell}\right)^{2}.

By the construction of
r
and
\theta
, the RHS of
(
6
)
actually equals to
\sin^{2}((\ell+1)\theta)-\sin^{2}(\ell\theta)
which is less than
(2\ell+1)\sin^{2}\theta=(2\ell+1)s^{2}r^{2}(1-x^{2})
by (
5
).
Thus we get

\displaystyle r^{2(\ell-1)}\left(\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell+1}\right)^{2}-\left(\operatorname{Im}\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{\ell}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(2\ell+1)s^{2}(1-x^{2}).

Since
r\geq 1
and
\ell\geq 1
, we can drop above factor
r^{2(\ell-1)}
and finish the proof.
∎

We now continue the proof of Proposition
3
.
By Lemma
5
, we need estimate

\frac{\omega_{d-3}}{\omega_{d-2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{2\ell}-\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{2(\ell+1)}\right)(1-s^{2})^{\frac{d-4}{2}}\,ds.

It is easy to see that this integral is actually a real number, so we only need estimate the real part of the difference-form integrand.
For any complex number
z=a+ib
, we have
\operatorname{Re}z^{2}=|z|^{2}-2b^{2}
.
Applying this fact to

F(s,x)=\operatorname{Re}\left(\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{2\ell}-\left(x+is\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right)^{2(\ell+1)}\right)

and using Lemma
6
, we get

F(s,x)\leq(4\ell+2)s^{2}(1-x^{2})+(x^{2}+s^{2}(1-x^{2}))^{\ell}(1-x^{2}-s^{2}(1-x^{2})).

As
x^{2}+s^{2}(1-x^{2})\leq 1
, this gives the following simpler estimation

F(s,x)\leq(4\ell+2)s^{2}(1-x^{2})+(1-x^{2})(1-s^{2}).

Now, integrating above inequality, we get

\displaystyle P_{2\ell}

\displaystyle(x)-P_{2(\ell+1)}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{d-3}}{\omega_{d-2}}\int_{-1}^{1}F(s,x)(1-s^{2})^{\frac{d-4}{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{d-3}}{\omega_{d-2}}(1-x^{2})\int_{-1}^{1}\left(4\ell+2-(4\ell+1)(1-s^{2})\right)(1-s^{2})^{\frac{d-4}{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{d-3}}{\omega_{d-2}}(1-x^{2})\left((4\ell+2)\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1}{2}-1\right)}-(4\ell+1)\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(4\ell+2-(4\ell+1)\frac{d-2}{d-1}\right)(1-x^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{4\ell+d}{d-1}(1-x^{2}).

Since
P_{2}(x)=x^{2}+\frac{1-x^{2}}{d-1}
(computed by the rotation symmetry of
Y_{2}
) and

\frac{\lambda_{2(\ell+1)}-\lambda_{2\ell}}{\lambda_{2}}(1-P_{2}(x))=\frac{4\ell+d}{d-1}(1-x^{2}),

this proves the proposition.

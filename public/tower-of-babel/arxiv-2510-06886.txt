Abstract
. The aim of this article is to investigate internal actions and split extensions in the variety of hoops. We provide a characterization of split extensions with strong section in terms of strong external actions . Beyond the general setting of hoops, the study is extended to the subvarieties of basic hoops, Wajsberg hoops, Gödel hoops and product hoops. Within the setting of basic hoops and their bounded counterparts, BL-algebras, the double negation yields a significant example of split extension with strong section, thus motivating our approach. A connection between strong external actions of hoops and the semidirect product construction introduced by W. Rump in the cateogory of L-algebras is established.

Introduction

BL-Algebras were introduced by P. Hájek in
[
32
]
as the algebraic semantics of
Basic Logic
, the logic of continuous
t
-norms, and they capture the common fragment of the three most relevant
many-valued
logics, namely
Łukasiewicz logic
,
Gödel logic
and
product logic
.

A
t
-norm on the unit interval is a map
[0,1]^{2}\rightarrow[0,1]\colon(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y
such that
([0,1],\cdot,1)
is a commutative totally ordered monoid. There are three fundamental continuous t-norms: the Łukasiewicz
t
-norm, defined by

x\cdot_{L}y=\max\{x+y-1,0\},

the Gödel
t
-norm, defined by

x\cdot_{G}y=\min\{x,y\},

and the product
t
-norm, defined by

x\cdot_{P}y=xy.

It is a classic result that, up to isomorphism, every continuous
t
-norm behaves locally as one of the three described above.

A
t
-norm induces naturally a
residuation
(or implication) defined by

x\rightarrow y=\sup\{z\in[0,1]\;|\;z\cdot x\leq y\}.

The implications associated with the three fundamental continuous
t
-norms are

x\rightarrow_{L}y=\min\{1-x+y,1\},

x\to_{G}y=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }x\leq y,\\
y,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

x\to_{P}y\;=\;\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }x\leq y,\\
\frac{y}{x},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The residuation induced by a continuous
t
-norm satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle x\rightarrow x

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle x\rightarrow 1

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow x

\displaystyle=x

and

x\rightarrow y=1\quad\text{and}\quad y\rightarrow x=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=y.

In
[
32
]
, the author studied in detail the three relevant cases and provides an axiomatization for the corresponding varieties of algebras and logics. The variety
\mathsf{MVAlg}
generated by
([0,1],\cdot_{L},\rightarrow_{L},\max,\min,0,1)
defines the class of
Wajsberg algebras
or
MV-algebras
(see
[
16
,
26
]
), which form the algebraic semantics of Łukasiewicz infinite-valued logic
[
17
,
37
]
. Algebras in the variety
\mathsf{GAlg}
generated by
([0,1],\cdot_{G},\rightarrow_{G},\max,\min,0,1)
are called
Gödel algebras
, and they form the equivalent algebraic semantics for
Gödel logic
.
The variety
\mathsf{PAlg}
of
product algebras
is generated by
([0,1],\cdot_{P},\rightarrow_{P},\max,\min,0,1)
and its associated propositional calculus is
product logic

[
21
,
33
]
. Finally, P. Hájek introduced the variety
\mathsf{BLAlg}
of
BL-algebras
, whose associated propositional calculus is
Basic Logic
. He also conjectured that
\mathsf{BLAlg}
concides with the variety generated by all algebras
([0,1],\cdot,\rightarrow,\max,\min,0,1)
, where
\cdot
is a continuous
t
-norm on the unit interval. This conjecture was then proved in
[
20
]
, while a shorter and more algebraic proof can be found in
[
2
]
.

From a categorical point of view, the variety
\mathsf{BLAlg}
, likewise the subvarieties
\mathsf{MVAlg}
,
\mathsf{GAlg}
and
\mathsf{PAlg}
, determines an ideally exact category (see
[
34
,
36
]
). Moreover, if
L_{2}=\{0,1\}
denotes the two-element Boolean algebra, then the semi-abelian categories
(\mathsf{BLAlg}\downarrow L_{2})
,
(\mathsf{MVAlg}\downarrow L_{2})
,
(\mathsf{GAlg}\downarrow L_{2})
and
(\mathsf{PAlg}\downarrow L_{2})
are equivalent, respectively, to the varieties of basic hoops, Wajsberg hoops, Gödel hoops, and product hoops.

The algebraic structure now known as a
hoop
was first introduced by B. Bosbach in
[
10
,
11
]
, where it appeared under the name
complementary semigroups
(
komplementäre Halbgruppen
). The term hoop itself was later coined in an unpublished manuscript by J. R. Büchi and T. M. Owens
[
15
]
, and has since become standard in the literature on substructural logics and residuated structures.

A hoop is a commutative, residuated, integral monoid satisfying divisibility. Indeed, hoops can be seen as a
positive
counterpart of residuated lattices, and the subclass of basic hoops can be obtained as the subreducts of the class of BL-algebras by omitting the lattice operations and the constant
0
. The main subclasses of interest are basic hoops, Wajsberg hoops (the hoop subreducts of MV-algebras), Gödel hoops, and product hoops, each of which reflects the equational behavior of its corresponding algebraic variety.

Now, one key notion that can be explored in the context of semi-abelian categories
[
35
]
is that of internal action
[
8
]
, which generalizes classical algebraic notions like group or Lie algebra actions, and provides a description of split extensions (and, therefore, of retractions) in algebraic terms, namely by means of semidirect products. Internal actions operated by the objects of a category were defined by F. Borceux, G. Janelidze and G. M. Kelly with the aim of extending the correspondence between actions and split extensions from the context of groups and Lie algebras to arbitrary semi-abelian categories. However, in some cases, such as for Orzech categories of interest
[
39
]
, it is more convenient to describe internal actions in terms of
external actions
, i.e., via a set of maps which satisfy a certain set of identities (see for instance
[
14
]
and
[
23
]
, where actions in varieties of non-associative algebras are studied).

The aim of this article is to investigate internal actions and split extensions in the variety of hoops, as well as in its subvarieties of basic, Wajsberg, Gödel, and product hoops.

The paper is organized as follows. After this introduction,

1

Section

2

In

In

The result is then generalized to the subvarieties of basic, Wajsberg, Gödel and product hoops. Furthermore, we prove that this bijection extends to a natural isomorphism between the functor
\operatorname{EAct}_{\operatorname{ss}}(-,X)
of strong external actions on a fixed object

\operatorname{SplExt_{ss}}(-,X)
of isomorphism classes of split extensions with strong section with fixed kernel

In addition, in

[

Definition 1.1
.

A
commutative integral residuated lattice
(or CIRL) is an algebra
A=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
of type
(2,2,2,2,0)
such that

(1)

(A,\cdot,1)
is a commutative monoid.

(2)

(A,\vee,\wedge,1)
is a lattice with top element
1
.

(3)

(\cdot,\rightarrow)
form a
residuated pair
with respect to the lattice ordering, i.e., the following
residuation property
holds:

x\cdot y\leq z\quad\text{if and only if}\quad x\leq y\rightarrow z,

where
x,y,z
denote arbitrary elements of
A
and
\leq
is the order induced by the lattice structure.

Residuated lattices, originally introduced in
[
44
]
as models of the divisibility properties of ideals in commutative rings, have more recently acquired importance as the algebraic counterparts of certain fuzzy logics (see, for instance
[
25
,
27
,
28
,
32
]
).

We call
bounded
commutative integral residuated lattice (BCIRL, for short) a CIRL with an extra constant
0
in the signature that is the bottom element in the lattice order
[
4
,
19
]
).

Definition 1.2
.

[
30
]

A
BL-algebra
is an algebra
A=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,0,1)
such that

(1)

(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rightarrow,0,1)
is a BCIRL.

(2)

x\wedge y=x\cdot(x\rightarrow y)
, for any
x,y\in A
. (
divisibility condition
)

(3)

(x\rightarrow y)\vee(y\rightarrow x)=1
, for any
x,y\in A
. (
prelinearity condition
)

We denote by
\mathsf{BLAlg}
the variety of BL-algebras. In any BL-algebra
A
, one can define the negation of an element

as
\neg x\coloneqq x\rightarrow 0.

We observe that the divisibility condition is equivalent to requiring that the order
\leq
induced by the lattice operations satisfies the following condition: for any
x,y\in A

x\leq y\;\text{ if and only if there exists $z\in A$ such that }x=y\cdot z.

On the other hand, the prelinearity condition characterizes those BCIRLs generated by chains (i.e., BCIRLs whose lattice order is total). In the present article, we are interested in the following subvarieties of BL-algebras.

.

Let
A
be a BL-algebra. We say that

•

[

,
43

\neg\neg x=x,

for any

•

[

x\cdot x=x,

for any
x\in A
.

•

product algebra

[

if it satisfies the identity

\neg x\vee((x\rightarrow x\cdot y)\rightarrow y)=1,

for any

We denote by
\mathsf{MVAlg}
,
\mathsf{GAlg}
and

the varieties of MV-algebras, Gödel algebras and product algebras respectively.

[

BL-algebras, MV-algebras, Gödel algebras and product algebras constitute the equivalent algebraic semantics for

Remark 1.6

As mentioned in the Introduction, the algebras of

In the variety of BL-algebras, and consequently in its subvarieties, the lattice operations of join and meet may be expressed in terms of the binary operations

\displaystyle x\wedge y=x\cdot(x\rightarrow y),

\displaystyle x\vee y=((x\rightarrow y)\rightarrow y)\wedge((y\rightarrow x)\rightarrow x).

As a result, BL-algebras may be equivalently described within the reduced language of hoops.

Definition 1.7
.

A
hoop
is an algebra
H=(H,\cdot,\rightarrow,1)
of type
(2,2,0)
such that

(i)

(H,\cdot,1)
is a commutative monoid;

(ii)

x\rightarrow x=1
;

(iii)

x\cdot(x\rightarrow y)=y\cdot(y\rightarrow x)
;

(iv)

(x\cdot y)\rightarrow z=x\rightarrow(y\rightarrow z)
,

for any
x,y,z\in H
.

A hoop
H
is
bounded
if there is a constant
0\in H
such that

(v)

0\rightarrow x=1
,

for any
x\in H
.

We denote by
\mathsf{Hoops}
the variety of hoops.
Every hoop
H
is endowed with a partial order
\leq
, called the
natural order
, which is defined by the following equivalent conditions: for any
x,y\in H

(i)

x\leq y
;

(ii)

x\rightarrow y=1
;

(iii)

there exists
z\in H
such that
x=z\cdot y
.

In particular,
(H,\leq)
is a partially ordered set with top element
1
, in which every pair of elements
x,y\in H
admit an infimum given by
x\wedge y=x\cdot(x\rightarrow y)
. If the hoop
H
is bounded, then
0
is its bottom element.

We further recall that a
filter
of a hoop
H
is a subset
F\subseteq H
such that
(F,\cdot,1)
is a submonoid of
(H,\cdot,1)
which is upward closed with respect to the natural order
\leq
of
H
. Every filter
F
of a hoop

defines a congruence
\theta_{F}
as follows:

\theta_{F}\coloneqq\{(x,y)\in H\times H\ |\ (x\rightarrow y)\cdot(y\rightarrow x)\in F\}.

Conversely, every congruence
\theta
on a hoop
H
determines a filter
F_{\theta}=1/\theta
(the equivalence class of
1
under
\theta
). The correspondence
F\mapsto\theta_{F}
establishes an isomorphism between the lattice of filters and the lattice of congruences of
H
, whose inverse is given by the map
\theta\mapsto F_{\theta}
. Therefore, the quotient of
H
by a filter
F
can be denoted by
H/F
, where
H/F\coloneqq H/\theta_{F}
.

A hoop homomorphism

is a homomorphism of monoid which preserves the binary operation
\rightarrow
. If
H
and
H^{\prime}
are bounded, then a homomorphism of bounded hoops must preserve the bottom element
0
. The kernel of a homomorphism

is the set

\Ker f=\{h\in H|\ f(h)=1\}

and it turns out to be a filter of
H
. Conversely, every filter
F
of a hoop

may be seen as the kernel of the canonical projection map
\pi\colon H\rightarrow H/F
, thus establishing a bijection between kernels and filters.

2.
Semidirect products in semi-abelian varieties

In this section, we aim to describe the construction of semidirect products in the variety of hoops. Indeed, the class of hoops forms a variety in the sense of Universal Algebra, which has been shown to be a semi-abelian category (in the sense of
[
35
]
) by S. Lapenta, G. Metere and L. Spada in
[
36
]
using the following characterisation.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
12
]

A variety
\mathsf{V}
is semi-abelian if and only if it has, among its terms, a unique constant
e
, together with
n
binary terms
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, and an
(n+1)
-ary term
\theta
satisfying the identities

\alpha_{i}(x,x)=e,\quad\text{for any}\ i=1,\ldots,n

and

\theta(\underline{\alpha}(x,y),y)=x,

where
\underline{\alpha}(x,y)=(\alpha_{1}(x,y),...,\alpha_{n}(x,y))
. ∎

Remark 2.2
.

[
36
]

The variety
\mathsf{Hoops}
is semi-abelian, with
e=1
,
\alpha_{1}(x,y)=(x\rightarrow y)
,
\alpha_{2}(x,y)=(((x\rightarrow y)\rightarrow y)\rightarrow x)
and
\theta(x,y,z)=((x\rightarrow z)\cdot y).
Hence, its subvarieties
\mathsf{BHoops},\mathsf{WHoops},\mathsf{GHoops}
and
\mathsf{PHoops}
are semi-abelian as well.

Remark 2.3
.

The initial object of the categories
\mathsf{BLAlg},\mathsf{MVAlg},\mathsf{GAlg}
and
\mathsf{PAlg}
is the two-element Boolean algebra

. However, these varieties above are not semi-abelian, since they are not pointed, but, as shown in

, they are protomodular (see

.

Let
\mathsf{V}
be a semi-abelian category, and let

\mathsf{V}

\displaystyle=(g_{b^{\prime}\rightarrow(b\rightarrow b^{\prime})}(g_{b^{\prime}\rightarrow b}(x\rightarrow y)\rightarrow y),\ (b\rightarrow b^{\prime})\rightarrow b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(g_{1}(g_{b^{\prime}\rightarrow b}(x\rightarrow y)\rightarrow y),\ (b\rightarrow b^{\prime})\rightarrow b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(g_{b^{\prime}\rightarrow b}(x\rightarrow y)\rightarrow y,\ (b\rightarrow b^{\prime})\rightarrow b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(g_{b\rightarrow b^{\prime}}(y\rightarrow x)\rightarrow x,\ (b^{\prime}\rightarrow b)\rightarrow b)

\displaystyle=((y,b^{\prime})\rightarrow(x,b))\rightarrow(x,b),

for any
(x,b),(y,b^{\prime})\in Y^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 4.16
.

The bijection
\tau_{B}
extends to a natural isomorphism

\tau\colon\operatorname{SplExt_{ss}^{W}}(-,X)\cong\operatorname{EAct_{ss}^{W}}(-,X).

∎

The following remark shows that the notion of split extension with strong section trivializes in the case of bounded Wasjeberg hoops.

Remark 4.17
.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

be a split extension with strong section in the variety

such that

is an isomorphism with inverse

.

Indeed, for any

, the identity

implies

(a\rightarrow s(0_{B}))\rightarrow s(0_{B})=(sp(a)\rightarrow s(0_{B}))\rightarrow s(0_{B}).

Moreover, since

is an MV-algebra, one has

(s(0_{B})\rightarrow a)\rightarrow a=(s(0_{B})\rightarrow sp(a))\rightarrow sp(a)

.

5.
Comparison with W. Rump’s semidirect product

We end the article by showing a connection between the strong external actions in the variety of hoops and the non-categorical notion of semidirect product introduced by W. Rump in the not protomodular context of L-algebras (see
[
41
,
42
]
).

Definition 5.1
.

An
L-algebra
is an algebra
L=(L,\rightarrow,1)
of type
(2,0)
satisfying

L1.

1\rightarrow x=x
and
x\rightarrow x=x\rightarrow 1=1
, for any
x\in L
;

L2.

(x\rightarrow y)\rightarrow(x\rightarrow z)=(y\rightarrow x)\rightarrow(y\rightarrow z)
, for any
x,y,z\in L
;

L3.

x\rightarrow y=y\rightarrow x=1\Rightarrow x=y
, for any
x,y\in L
.

The binary operation
\rightarrow
can be interpreted as the logical implication, while the constant
1
can be interpreted as the logical unit.

Example 5.2
.

Every hoop is an L-algebra.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
B,X
be L-algebras. We say that
B

operates
on
X
if there is a map

B\times X\rightarrow X\colon(b,x)\mapsto bx

such that

O1.

b(x\rightarrow y)=bx\rightarrow by
;

O2.

(b\rightarrow b^{\prime})bx=(b^{\prime}\rightarrow b)b^{\prime}x
;

O3.

1x=x
,

for any
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and
x,y\in X
.

We want to show how the notion of strong external action in the variety of hoops is related with
Definition

5.3
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
B,X
be hoops and let
(f,g)
be a strong external action of
B
on
X
. Then
B
operates on
X
via
g
.

Proof.

It follows from
Remark

4.7
that

g_{b}(x\rightarrow y)=g_{b}(x)\rightarrow g_{b}(y),

for any
b\in B
and
x,y\in X
.
Moreover, since
g_{b_{1}\cdot b_{2}}=g_{b_{1}}\circ g_{b_{2}}
for any
b,b^{\prime}\in B
, we get

\displaystyle g_{b\rightarrow b^{\prime}}(g_{b}(x))

\displaystyle=g_{(b\rightarrow b^{\prime})\cdot b}(x)

\displaystyle=g_{(b^{\prime}\rightarrow b)\cdot b}(x)

\displaystyle=g_{b^{\prime}\rightarrow b}(g_{b^{\prime}}(x)).

Furthermore, Axiom O3 follows from Axiom E2.
∎

Since the category of L-algebras is not semi-abelian, it doesn’t have categorical semidirect products. However, given two L-algebras
B
and
X
such that
B
operates on
X
, we can still determine a corresponding notion of semidirect product.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
B,X
be L-algebras such that
B
operates on
X
. The
semidirect product

X\rtimes B
of
B
by
X
is the cartesian product
X\times B
endowed with the binary operation

(x,b)\rightarrow(y,b^{\prime})=(((b\rightarrow b^{\prime})x)\rightarrow(b^{\prime}\rightarrow b)y,\ b\rightarrow b^{\prime}),

for any
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and
x,y\in X
, and with unit
1_{X\rtimes B}=(1,1)
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
B,X
be L-algebras such that
B
operates on
X
. Then the semidirect product
X\rtimes B
is an L-algebra. ∎

Definition 5.7
.

We say that a split extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

of L-algebras has a

holds for any

Given a split extension with strong section, it is possible to prove that

. Indeed, the map

satisfies Axioms O1-O2-O3. Moreover, the semidirect product of

One may check that the map

6.
Conclusions

In this manuscript, we described split extensions with strong section in varieties of hoops in terms of
strong external actions
. We proved that for any hoop
X
, there is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{SplExt_{ss}}(-,X)\cong\operatorname{EAct}_{\operatorname{ss}}(-,X)

showing that any split extension with strong section of
B
by
X
in the variety
\mathsf{Hoops}
can be described by a pair of maps

f,g\colon B\times X\rightarrow X

satisfying a set of identities which depends on the axioms satisfied by the hoop.

A natural direction for future research is to extend this correspondence to
all
split extensions, with the aim of developing a general notion of external action and of establishing natural isomorphisms

\Act(-,X)\cong\SplExt(-,X)\cong\operatorname{EAct}(-,X),

thus relating internal and external actions, in analogy with the case of derived actions in Orzech categories of interest
[
39
]
.

Eventually, in
Remark

4.17
we showed that the notion of split extension with strong section becomes trivial in the case of MV-algebras. It remains an open problem to construct an explicit example of a (non-trivial) split extension with strong section in the variety of Wajsberg hoops.

Coverage errors for Student’s t t confidence intervals comparable to those in Hall (1988)
Art B. Owen

Abstract
Table 1 of Hall, 1988 contains asymptotic coverage error formulas for some nonparametric approximate 95% confidence intervals for the mean based on n n IID samples. The table includes an entry for an interval based on the central limit theorem using Gaussian quantiles and the Gaussian maximum likelihood variance estimate. It is missing an entry for the very widely used Student t t confidence intervals. This note makes a mild numerical correction for the Gaussian entry and provides an entry for the Student t t intervals. For skewness γ \gamma and kurtosis κ \kappa , the corrected Gaussian formula is 0.14 ​ κ − 2.16 ​ γ 2 − 3.42 0.14\kappa-2.16\gamma^{2}-3.42 and the formula for the t t intervals is 0.14 ​ κ − 2.16 ​ γ 2 0.14\kappa-2.16\gamma^{2} . The impetus to revisit this estimate arose from the surprisingly robust performance of Student’s t statistic in randomized quasi-Monte Carlo sampling.



1
Introduction

Table 1 of
Hall, 1988
gives expressions for the coverage error in several
nonparametric confidence interval methods for the mean of a real valued
random variable
X
based on an IID sample of
n
realizations of that
random variable. It is missing an entry for the usual confidence interval
based on Student’s
t
statistic. It does have an entry for the case where
the
t
threshold is replaced by one from the standard
Gaussian distribution and the usual unbiased sample variance is replaced
by the maximum likelihood estimate (MLE). This note uses an expression from page 949 of
that paper to develop a formula for the Student
t
intervals.
There is also a minor numerical correction for the Gaussian intervals.

The motivation to revisit this problem comes from recent work in randomized
quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC) sampling in
L’Ecuyer et al., 2023
. RQMC provides an
unbiased estimate of a multidimensional integral. The sample values that go
into that estimate have a complex dependence structure that improves the
convergence rate beyond that of plain Monte Carlo. Then a small number
of independent replicates are made in order to estimate error. The surprise
in
L’Ecuyer et al., 2023
was that a plain Student’s
t
confidence interval proved
to be remarkably robust in terms of coverage accuracy at 95% nominal coverage.
It performed better than two bootstrap confidence intervals. A theoretical
comparison of bootstrap confidence intervals from
Hall, 1988
revolves
around the skewness and kurtosis of the random variable being replicated.
This paper fills in the missing formula for Student’s
t
distribution.

For
n\geqslant 2
, let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be IID random variables with the same distribution as
X
.
Then for
\alpha<1/2
, a standard Student’s
t
based confidence interval for
\mu

with nominal coverage
1-2\alpha
takes the form

\displaystyle\bar{X}\pm t^{1-\alpha}_{(n-1)}s/\sqrt{n}

(1)

where

\bar{X}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad s^{2}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\bar{X})^{2}

and
t^{1-\alpha}_{(n-1)}
is the
1-\alpha
quantile of Student’s
t
distribution on
n-1

degrees of freedom. This interval has asymptotic coverage probability
1-2\alpha
.
It is more common to write about coverage
1-\alpha
using
t_{(n-1)}^{1-\alpha/2}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)

but the usage above conforms to Table 1 of
Hall, 1988
.

An alternative interval is

\displaystyle\bar{X}\pm\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\hat{\sigma}/\sqrt{n}

(2)

where
\Phi
is the cumulative distribution function of the
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
distribution,
and

\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\bar{X})^{2}

is the MLE of
\sigma^{2}
in the model
X_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
.
This is the interval that appears in Table 1 of
Hall, 1988
with the label ‘Norm’.

Table 1 of
Hall, 1988
includes asymptotic coverage error formulas for ‘Norm’
and some bootstrap confidence intervals.
For the necessary assumptions on the distribution of
X
, see
Hall, 1988
.
These table entries are for
\alpha=0.025
corresponding to the common default
of 95% confidence. The table’s expression for coverage error of (
2
) is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\bigl(0.14\kappa-2.12\gamma^{2}-3.35)\varphi(0.975)+O(n^{-2})

(3)

where
\varphi
is the
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
probability density function, and equation (
3
)
combines equation (4.15) and the row labeled ‘Norm’ in Table 1 of
Hall, 1988
.

Table 1 is missing a row for the usual Student’s
t
interval in equation (
1
).
That interval differs from the one for (
2
) in using
s

instead of
\hat{\sigma}
and in using
t^{1-\alpha}_{(n-1)}
instead of
\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)
.

Section
2
rederives and mildly corrects Hall’s formula
for coverage errors in 95% confidence intervals based on the Gaussian interval.
Section
3
then constructs formulas for the Student
t

interval. The key step is to use an asymptotic formula from
Abramowitz and Stegun, 1972

relating quantiles of Student’s
t
distribution to those of the
\mathcal{N}(0,1)

distribution. Both tasks were made simpler by noticing that Hall’s derivation
for the ‘Norm’ interval does not use all of the machinery that the bootstrap
intervals in that paper require.

2
Derivation

Here we look at the derivation for the row called ‘Norm’ before
constructing the analogous formula for the standard
t
intervals.
Let
z_{\alpha}=\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)
for
0<\alpha<1

and then let
C(\alpha)=\Pr(\mu\leqslant\hat{\theta}+n^{-1/2}\hat{\sigma}z_{\alpha})

be the one sided coverage probability for the normal interval

(-\infty,\hat{\theta}+n^{-1/2}\hat{\sigma}z_{\alpha}]
.
From page 949 of
Hall, 1988
,

C(\alpha)=\alpha-n^{-1/2}\frac{1}{6}\gamma(2z_{\alpha}^{2}+1)\varphi(z_{\alpha})+n^{-1}z_{\alpha}\Bigl(\frac{1}{12}\kappa(z_{\alpha}^{2}-3)\\
-\frac{1}{18}\gamma^{2}\bigl(z_{\alpha}^{4}+2z_{\alpha}^{2}-3\bigr)-\frac{1}{4}\bigl(z_{\alpha}^{2}+3\bigr)\Bigr)\varphi(z_{\alpha})+O(n^{-3/2}).

(4)

For
\alpha<1/2
, the two-sided coverage for

[\hat{\theta}-n^{-1/2}\hat{\sigma}z_{1-\alpha},\hat{\theta}+n^{-1/2}\hat{\sigma}z_{1-\alpha}]

with
z_{1-\alpha}=-z_{\alpha}
is
C(1-\alpha)-C(\alpha)
which equals

1-2\alpha-2n^{-1}z_{1-\alpha}\Bigl(\frac{1}{12}\kappa(z_{1-\alpha}^{2}-3)\\
-\frac{1}{18}\gamma^{2}\bigl(z_{1-\alpha}^{4}+2z_{1-\alpha}^{2}-3\bigr)-\frac{1}{4}\bigl(z_{1-\alpha}^{2}+3\bigr)\Bigr)\varphi(z_{1-\alpha})+O(n^{-3/2}).

For the bootstrap methods covered by (
3
)
the
O(n^{-3/2})
error is actually
O(n^{-2})
because the
O(n^{-3/2})

terms cancel the same way that the
O(n^{-1/2})
terms do. This may very well be true
for ‘Norm’ as well, but here we only consider terms up to order
O(1/n)
.

The coverage error is the attained coverage minus the nominal
coverage. For the two sided interval the coverage error is then

\displaystyle\frac{z_{1-\alpha}}{n}\Bigl(\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}-3}{6}\kappa-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{4}+2z_{\alpha}^{2}-3}{9}\gamma^{2}-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}+3}{2}\Bigr)\varphi(z_{\alpha})+O(n^{-3/2})

(5)

where even powers of
z_{1-\alpha}
and
z_{\alpha}
are equal because
z_{\alpha}=-z_{1-\alpha}
.
The corresponding entry for Table 1, which leaves out the
factor
z_{1-\alpha}\varphi(z_{\alpha})/n
is then

\displaystyle\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}-3}{6}\kappa-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{4}+2z_{\alpha}^{2}-3}{9}\gamma^{2}-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}+3}{2}.

(6)

Using
z_{1-\alpha}=z_{0.975}\doteq 1.95996
,
we get
0.14\kappa-2.16\gamma^{2}-3.42
after rounding.
Hall’s Table 1 has
0.14\kappa-2.12\gamma^{2}-3.35

which is close. He may have used
z_{1-\alpha}=1.92
for the constant
term and
z_{1-\alpha}=1.95
for the coefficient of
\gamma^{2}
.
Also,
\varphi(z_{0.975})\doteq 0.058
.

For Gaussian data, we have
\gamma=\kappa=0
. Then the standard normal
theory intervals (
2
) have coverage error

-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}+3}{2n}\varphi(z_{\alpha})+O(n^{-3/2}).

The negative sign indicates undercoverage.
The Student
t
intervals (
1
) have coverage error zero.
Therefore we anticipate, and we will see, that the counterpart to
(
6
) for Student
t
intervals equals zero when
\gamma=\kappa=0
.

3
Adjusted interval

To get the required formula,
we need to replace
z_{1-\alpha}
by
t_{(n-1)}^{1-\alpha}
,
the
1-\alpha
quantile of Student’s
t
distribution.
We also have to replace
\hat{\sigma}
by
s
.
We place
1-\alpha
in the superscript of
t^{1-\alpha}_{(n-1)}
so
it does not collide with the degrees of freedom
n-1
, while
placing
1-\alpha
in ths subscript of
z_{1-\alpha}
so that
it will not collide with exponents when we raise that quantile to integer powers.

Page 949 of
Abramowitz and Stegun, 1972
gives an asymptotic expansion
relating the
t
quantiles to the Gaussian ones:

\displaystyle t_{(\nu)}^{\alpha}=z_{\alpha}+\frac{g_{1}(z_{\alpha})}{\nu}+\frac{g_{2}(z_{\alpha})}{\nu^{2}}+\frac{g_{3}(z_{\alpha})}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{g_{4}(z_{\alpha})}{\nu^{4}}+\cdots

(7)

where

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{3}+x}{4},

\displaystyle g_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{5x^{5}+16x^{3}+3x}{96},

\displaystyle g_{3}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{3x^{7}+19x^{5}+17x^{3}-15x}{384},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle g_{4}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{79x^{9}+776x^{7}+1482x^{5}-1920x^{3}-945x}{92160}.

Using the first two terms of expansion (
7
),

t^{\alpha}_{(n-1)}=z_{\alpha}+\frac{z_{\alpha}^{3}+z_{\alpha}}{4(n-1)}+O(n^{-2})=z_{\alpha}+\frac{z_{\alpha}^{3}+z_{\alpha}}{4n}+O(n^{-2}).

Similarly

s=\hat{\sigma}\sqrt{\frac{n}{n-1}}=1+\frac{1}{2n}+O(n^{-2}),

and so

\displaystyle st^{\alpha}_{(n-1)}

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}z_{\alpha}\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{2n}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigl(1+\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}+1}{4n}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}z_{\alpha}\Bigl(1+\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}+3}{4n}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}z_{\alpha^{\prime}}

where

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Phi(z_{\alpha^{\prime}})=\Phi\Bigl(z_{\alpha}+\frac{z_{\alpha}^{3}+3z_{\alpha}}{4n}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\alpha+\frac{z_{\alpha}^{3}+3z_{\alpha}}{4n}\varphi(z_{\alpha})+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigr).

Using (
5
), the coverage error of the two sided Student
t
interval is

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\ 2(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})+\frac{z_{1-\alpha^{\prime}}}{n}\Bigl(\frac{z_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{2}-3}{6}\kappa-\frac{z_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{4}+2z_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{2}-3}{9}\gamma^{2}-\frac{z_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{2}+3}{2}\Bigr)\varphi(z_{\alpha^{\prime}})+O(n^{-3/2})

\displaystyle=2(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})+\frac{z_{1-\alpha}}{n}\Bigl(\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}-3}{6}\kappa-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{4}+2z_{\alpha}^{2}-3}{9}\gamma^{2}-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}+3}{2}\Bigr)\varphi(z_{\alpha})+O(n^{-3/2}).

Now

\displaystyle 2(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{3}+3z_{\alpha}}{2n}+O(n^{-2})=\frac{z_{1-\alpha}}{n}\Bigl(\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}+3}{2n}\Bigr)+O(n^{-2}).

It follows that the coverage error in Student’s
t
interval is

\frac{z_{1-\alpha}}{n}\Bigl(\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}-3}{6}\kappa-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{4}+2z_{\alpha}^{2}-3}{9}\gamma^{2}\Bigr)\varphi(z_{\alpha})+O(n^{-3/2}).

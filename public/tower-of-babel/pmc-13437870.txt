Lactylation-augmented DCBLD1-mediated PDIA3 stabilization reprograms glycolysis metabolism in oesophageal cancer
Authors: Ming Yin, Shan Wang, HaiMin Liu, Chaochen Xu, Qiushuo Li, YunHao Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveHistone lactylation (Hla) represents a novel epigenetic mark priming cells toward the malignant state. Discoidin, CUB, and LCCL domain-containing type I (DCBLD1) has been reported as a carcinogenic gene so far. This project was focused on the functional role of DCBLD1 in oesophageal cancer through the lactate modification pathway. MethodsBiological information evaluated DCBLD1, protein disulfide isomerase family A member 3 (PDIA3) expression and the relationship between the two in oesophageal cancer. RT-qPCR and Western blotting were used to detect DCBLD1 and PDIA3 expression. Western blotting also quantified DCBLD1-lysine lactylation (kla) levels. DCBLD1 half-life was checked by chlorhexidine (CHX) assay. EDU, wound healing, transwell, flow cytometry and Seahorse assays individually appraised cell proliferation, migration, apoptosis and glycolysis. Co-IP assay identified DCBLD1-PDIA3 interaction. Xenografted tumorigenesis was estimated, prior to histological assessment by H&E staining. Glucose consumption and lactate production were also examined to detect glycolysis.ResultsDCBLD1 was highly expressed in esophageal cancer specimens and cells. DCBLD1 half-life was respectively shortened or prolonged in tumor cells challenged with lactate (LA) or the aerobic glycolysis inhibitor 2-deoxy-d-glucose (2-DG). DCBLD1-Kla levels were up-regulated in oesophageal cancer tissues. Lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA) interference decreased both DCBLD1 expression and DCBLD1-Kla expression. PDIA3 was highly expressed in oesophageal cancer. DCBLD1 was positively correlated with, and interacted with PDIA3 upon LA stimulation. DCBLD1 knockdown inhibited cell proliferation, migration, glycolysis and induced apoptosis, repressed tumor progress and glycolysis in vivo, which were all partially recovered by PDIA3 overexpression. Further LDHA down-regulation aggravated the impacts of DCBLD1 knockdown and PDIA3 overexpression both in vitro and in vivo.ConclusionIt was established that DCBLD1 lactylation bound to PDIA3, contributing to oesophageal cancer malignancy and glycolysis.Keywords: DCBLD1, Esophageal cancer, Glycolysis, Histone lactylation, PDIA3

Introduction

Oesophageal carcinoma dominantly symptomized by the progressive dysphagia is a lethal adult digestive tract tumor (Yang et al. 2024a). Squamous cell carcinoma of high malignant potential, is the predominate pathological pattern of oesophageal carcinoma with distinct remarkable geographic variation in Asian, African and South American (Chen et al. 2017). The recent epidemiology indicates that oesophageal cancer ranks 11th in annual incidence (510,716, 2.6%) and 7th in mortality (445,129, 4.6%) in 2022 (Bray et al. 2022). Risk factors for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) mainly include poor nutrition, nicotine consumption, hot beverage drinking, and alcohol abusing (Abnet et al. 2018). The disease progresses in a prolonged and stepwise fashion from mild, moderate, and severe atypical hyperplasia, to carcinoma in situ, invasive carcinoma, and, eventually, metastatic disease (Chen et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2017), orchestrated by a complex interplay of genetic and molecular factors (Jajosky and Fels Elliott 2022; Caspa Gokulan et al. 2019).

Discoidin, CUB, and LCCL domain-containing type I (DCBLD1) is a type I transmembrane protein (A–D) with extracellular CUB (an extracellular domain of approximately 110 residues that is found in functionally diverse, mostly developmentally regulated proteins), LCCL (including 94–97 amino acids, non-cytoplasmic but the function is undefined), and F5/8 type C domains. So far, emerging data has demonstrated the oncogenic role of DCBLD1 in cervical cancer, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma and lung cancer (Meng et al. 2024; Fu et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2020).

Protein disulfide isomerases (PDIs) are an endoplasmic reticulum (ER)-based redox chaperones, which regulate multiple protein functions via the oxidation, reduction, and isomerization of disulfide bonds (–S–S–) in client proteins (Matsusaki et al. 2020). Protein disulfide isomerase family A member 3 (PDIA3, also known as ERp57), a thiol oxidoreductase, belongs to the PDI family. Under normal circumstances, PDIA3 guides the folding of newly synthesized glycoproteins in the endoplasmic reticulum through forming complex with calreticulin and calnexin, which is not only capable of protein modification and folding but also capable of exerting the effects of group thinning and oxidation reduction (Chichiarelli et al. 2022). PDIA3 has been well documented to be involved in virus infections, neurological disorders and oncology (Chichiarelli et al. 2022; Mahmood et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2022; Wu et al. 2024). Recent literature has demonstrated that PDIA3 expression is increased in ESCC tissues (Miao et al. 2025).

This work was focused on the functional role of DCBLD1 in oesophageal cancer and its correlation with PDIA3.

Materials and methods

Experimental samples

A total of 3 patients diagnosed with squamous cell carcinoma who underwent esophagectomy at Binhai County People's Hospital between 2023 and 2024 were enrolled in this study. All participants did not receive any therapy. Cancerous tissues and matching healthy esophageal tissues (five centimeters from the malignant lesions according to the NCCN guidelines for EC) were sampled from endoscopic biopsies. All cancer samples were classified according to the TNM staging system (AJCC2018), including stage II (n = 1), and stage III (n = 2). This research was endorsed by the Ethics Committee of Binhai County People's Hospital, and participants were first informed about the research.

Biological information

The Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) database evaluated the transcriptional data of DCBLD1 and PDIA3 in esophageal cancer versus normal tissues and the association between DCBLD1 and PDIA3.

Cells and processing

Human normal esophageal epithelial HEEC cells and esophageal cancer cells (TE-1, KYSE-150, ECA-109 and TE-15) were respectively incubated in Eagle's minimum essential medium (EMEM) and Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium. The supported culture environment was as follows: 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), and 1 × antibiotic mixture in a drippy incubator (stabilized at 37 °C and 5% CO2).

For experimentation, the short hairpin (sh) plasmid vectors targeting the coding area of DCBLD1 or LDHA were established by WZ Biosciences Inc., human DCBLD1 or LDHA small interfering RNA (siRNA) and non-silencing siRNA were synthesized by Sigma Genosys (Tokyo, Japan), and the open reading frame of PDIA3 was cloned into the retroviral vector pMSCV-eGFP-Puro. Esophageal cancer cells were transfected with lentiviral particles with Lipofectamine® LTX under the manufacturer’s references.

Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Whole-cell/tissue RNA was extracted with RNX-Plus kit. The complementary DNA samples that were generated by EasyScript One-Step gDNA Removal cDNA Synthesis SuperMix were amplified by Xpert Fast SYBER Mastermix Blue. Relative transcript quantities were calculated using the DDCt method with GAPDH as the endogenous reference gene.

Western blotting

After cells or tissues were lysed with Radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) kit, the protein samples were quantified by 1X Bradford's reagent. Equal quantities of proteins were separated by SDS‒PAGE and were transferred onto PVDF membranes. After being blocked with 5% skimmed milk powder diluted with TBST, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with appropriately diluted primary antibodies and conjugated with the secondary antibody of goat anti-rabbit polyclonal antibody at 37 °C for 2 h. The proteins were detected using the ECL reagent.

5-Ethynyl-2’-deoxyuridine (EDU) staining

Cells growing at the exponential stage were seeded into 24-well plates at the density of 1 × 105 cells per well. After incubation with EDU culture solution (10 μM) in the dark for 60 min, the cells were fixed with 4% formaldehyde for 15 min and permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 10 min. The cells were subsequently reacted with click-reaction reagent for 30 min at room temperature in the dark and stained with DAPI. After blockade with anti-fade mounting medium, the cells were photographed under an automated inverted fluorescence microscope.

Wound healing assay

Cells growing at the exponential stage were seeded into 6-well plates at the density of 5 × 105 cells per well. Upon reaching 90–95% confluency, a mechanical lesion was formed by means of the tip of a pipette. The wound edge movement was then photographed under a microscope.

Transwell assay

Cell migration was evaluated using 8 μm 24-well transwell chambers. The cell suspension containing 2 × 105 cells was placed on the top chambers, and the DMEM with 10% FBS (500 μL) was filled with the bottom chambers. Following 24 h of incubation at 37 °C with 5% CO2, the migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 min. The stained cells were counted under a microscope.

Flow cytometry

After being resuspended with Annexin V binding buffer at a concentration of 1 × 106 cells/mL, the cells were incubated with 5 µL of Annexin V antibody and 10 µL of propidium iodide solution for 15 min at room temperature in the dark. Cells were sorted on a FACScan flow cytometer.

Seahorse assay

Briefly, 1 × 104 cells were seeded in the 96-well XF Seahorse incubation plate as indicated by the glycolytic stress test kit. Cells were cultured at 37 °C in XF base medium (pH 7.4), and glucose (10 mM), glutamine (1 mM), 2-DG (50 mM) and oligomycin (1 μM) were added sequentially into the plates at specific time points following the manufacturer’s guidelines. The extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) was measured and plotted using the Seahorse XF24 software.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

After being lysed with IP lysis buffer and 10 min of centrifugation at 14,000 g at 4 °C, the lysates were incubated overnight at 4 °C with DCBLD1 and PDIA3 primary antibodies or mouse IgG negative control. After being coupled to protein A/G agarose beads, the resulting immunocomplexes were denatured for Western blotting.

Chlorhexidine (CHX) assay

When de novo protein synthesis was impeded by 100 μg/mL CHX, DCBLD1 degradation rate was evaluated at 0, 6, 12 and 24 h.

Nude mouse model establishment

Female BALB/c nude mice (4–5 weeks old, weighing 16–20 g) were fed with food and water ad libitum, and were kept at a specific pathogen-free level at 20 °C and a humidity of 60%, alternating between light and dark for 12 h. Esophageal cancer cells (5 × 105) were subcutaneously injected into the right armpit of the animals. Tumor volume was determined according to the equation: V (mm3) = length (mm) × width (mm)2/2.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining

The tissues were fixed with 4% formaldehyde and embedded with wax to prepare 4 μm-thick sections. After being passed through xylene and rinsed in ethanol gradient buffers, the sections were stained with hematoxylin for 5 min and eosin for 2 min. After blockade with neutral resin, micrographs were taken.

Measurement of glucose uptake and lactate production

Under the references of the Glucose Assay Kit and the Lactate Assay Kit, the absorbance was measured at 540 nm and 530 nm using a microplate spectrophotometer to quantify glucose uptake and lactate production.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 8 was used for statistical analysis and the data were shown as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analysis was performed using one-way analysis of variance and Student’s t-test. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Overexpressed DCBLD1 in oesophageal cancer

Based on the web server GEPIA (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/), DCBLD1 was found to be highly expressed in esophageal carcinoma (ESCA) tissues by contrast with normal tissues (Fig. 1A). Also, DCBLD1 expression was increased in cancerous tissues in comparison with matching healthy esophageal tissues (Fig. 1B, C). As analyzed by RT-qPCR, the significantly enhanced DCBLD1 expression was displayed in oesophageal cancer cells including ECA109, TE-1, KYSE150 and TE-15 cells compared with HEEC cell line (Fig. 1D).

Overexpressed DCBLD1 in oesophageal cancer. A DCBLD1 expression in oesophageal cancer from GEPIA databank. B, C DCBLD1 expression in oesophageal cancer tissues from RT-qPCR and Western blotting. D DCBLD1 expression in oesophageal cancer cells from RT-qPCR

DCBLD1 is stabilized by lactylation modification in oesophageal cancer cells

In the presence of CHX, DCBLD1 half-life was prolonged by LA while was shortened by the aerobic glycolysis inhibitor 2-deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG) (Fig. 2A, B). Specifically, DCBLD1-kla protein level was increased in cancerous tissues in comparison with matching healthy esophageal tissues (Fig. 2C). Upon knockdown of LDHA, both DCBLD1-kla and DCBLD1 protein levels were both unexpectedly reduced in oesophageal cancer cells in addition to LDHA (Fig. 2D).

DCBLD1 is stabilized by lactylation modification in oesophageal cancer cells. A, B. DCBLD1 stability from CHX assay. C DCBLD1-Kla levels in oesophageal cancer tissues from Western blotting. D DCBLD1-Kla and DCBLD1 levels in LDHA-depleted oesophageal cancer cells from Western blotting

DCBLD1 knockdown inhibits oesophageal cancer cell proliferation, migration and induces apoptosis

After transfection of the siRNAs against DCBLD1 (si-DCBLD1#1/2), DCBLD1 expression was significantly declined in KYSE150 and TE-15 cells (Fig. 3A). As portrayed in EDU staining, the number of proliferating cells was significantly decreased when DCBLD1 was silenced (Fig. 3B). The data from wound healing assay demonstrated that the potential for both KYSE150 and TE-15 cells to migrate was inhibited after DCBLD1 was knocked down (Fig. 3C). In addition, it was observed that down-regulation of DCBLD1 significantly promoted the apoptosis of oesophageal cancer cells (Fig. 3D).

DCBLD1 knockdown inhibits oesophageal cancer cell proliferation, migration and induces apoptosis. A DCBLD1 expression in oesophageal cancer cells in response to si-DCBLD1#1/2. B Fluorescent photographs of proliferating cells from EDU staining. C Photographs of the wound edge movement from wound healing assay. D Apoptotic cells sorted on a flow cytometer

Lactylation modification mediated the correlation between DCBLD1 and PDIA3 in oesophageal cancer

After predicting the up-regulated PDIA3 expression in ESCA tissues in contrast to normal tissues (Fig. 4A), PDIA3 was also noticed to be correlated with DCBLD1 in oesophageal cancer (Fig. 4B). When DCBLD1 was silenced in both KYSE150 and TE-15 cells, PDIA3 expression was also decreased (Fig. 4C). Moreover, upon exposure to LA, DCBLD1 was discovered to possess a strong affinity with PDIA3 (Fig. 4D). When DCBLD1 was depleted, the probable interaction between DCBLD1 and PDIA3 was disturbed (Fig. 4E).

B Relevance of DCBLD1 with PDIA3 in oesophageal cancer from GEPIA databank. C PDIA3 expression in response to si-DCBLD1. D Affinity of DCBLD1 with PDIA3 from Co-IP assay upon LA stimulation. E Affinity of DCBLD1 with PDIA3 from Co-IP assay in response to si-DCBLD1

Lactylation-induced DCBLD1-PDIA3 cooperation in oesophageal cancer in vitro

As expected, PDIA3 expression was enhanced by overexpression of PDIA3 in both DCBLD1-depleting KYSE150 and TE-15 cells, which was suppressed again when LDHA was silenced (Fig. 5A). Functionally, after DCBLD1 interference, LDHA down-regulation decreased the EDU signal that was previously promoted by PDIA3 up-regulation (Fig. 5B). Similarly, the wound movement ratio in DCBLD1-depleted oesophageal cancer cells that was increased by PDIA3 overexpression was further decreased again when LDHA was silenced (Fig. 5C). Also, transwell assays demonstrated that PDIA3 elevated the cellular migratory ability that was decreased due to DCBLD1 silencing. However, compared with the si-DCBLD1 + oe-PDIA3 group, cell migration was inhibited again by further down-regulation of LDHA (Fig. 5D). Contrarily, as described in Fig. 5E, PDIA3 inhibited the cellular apoptotic ability that was enhanced by DCBLD1 insufficiency, which was further partially reverted after LDHA was silenced. As portrayed in Fig. 6A, DCBLD1 deficiency significantly decreased the ECAR rate, HK2, PFKFB3 and PKM2 expression which could be increased by further PDIA3 elevation. However, the ECAR rate, HK2, PFKFB3 and PKM2 expression were declined again in the si-DCBLD1 + oe-PDIA3 + si-LDHA group compared with DCBLD1-down-regulating and PDIA3-up-regulating cells (Fig. 6A, B).

Lactylation-induced DCBLD1-PDIA3 cooperation in oesophageal cancer cell glycolysis. Lactylation-induced DCBLD1-PDIA3 cooperation in oesophageal cancer in vivo

As recorded in Fig. 7A, deletion of DCBLD1 decreased the tumor growth rate. Under this circumstance, PDIA3 overexpression facilitated tumor growth, which was partially rescued again when LDHA was down-regulated. The malignancy volume and weight were both decreased in response to deficiency of DCBLD1, which was then enhanced again after PDIA1 was overexpressed. Meanwhile, LDHA down-regulation inhibited malignancy volume and weight again, similar to DCBLD1 knockdown (Fig. 7B). When DCBLD1 was lowly expressed, esophageal tumor tissue necrosis was aggravated, which was then eased by elevation of PDIA3. Also, LDHA deficiency also exacerbated esophageal tumor tissue necrosis in response to DCBLD1 down-regulation and PDIA3 up-regulation (Fig. 7C). Besides, it turned out that DCBLD1 silencing reduced both glucose uptake and lactate secretion, which was then partially offset when PDIA3 was up-regulated. Further, interference LDHA reduced both glucose uptake and lactate secretion again (Fig. 7D, E).

Discussion

As a biochemical fingerprint of cancer cells, metabolic alternation interacts to shape the fate of cancer and stromal cells within the tumor microenvironment, which is well accepted as a hallmark of tumor adaptation (Mao et al. 2024). Aerobic glycolysis is hallmarked by preferential reliance on glycolysis, which involves the transformation of glucose into pyruvate followed by lactate production for energy generation in an oxygen-independent manner, which links the high rate of glucose fermentation with uncontrolled tumor growth and therapy resistance (Halma et al. 2023). As the redox-balanced end-product of the Warburg effect that is produced from pyruvate by lactate dehydrogenase, LA is taken up by tumor cells and transported to mitochondria for oxidation to provide energy, functioning as a pleiotropic signaling molecule in tumorigenesis through metabolic rewiring (Kane et al. 2025). Beyond that, LA stored in the tumor microenvironment modulates nuclear histones through the addition of lactyl groups to the lysine (K) residues of histones, building a bridge between epigenetics and metabolism (Zhang et al. 2025).

Recent publication has identified DCBLD1 as a lactylation-related gene in ulcerative colitis (Chen et al. 2025). Meng et al. have demonstrated that DCBLD1 expression is up-regulated by lactylation in cervical cancer cells (Meng et al. 2024). The present experimental data demonstrated that DCBLD1-kla protein levels were elevated in oesophageal cancer specimens. LA stabilized DCBLD1 protein while DCBLD1 protein stability was decreased by the aerobic glycolysis inhibitor 2-DG. LDHA, one of the key subunits of the lactate dehydrogenase enzyme in the glycolytic pathway, promotes the glycolytic process via conversion from pyruvate to lactate (Sun et al. 2023). Moreover, knockdown of LDHA inhibited protein levels of DCBLD1-kla and DCBLD1. All these findings indicated that lactylation modification might be recognized as an epigenetic mode of DCBLD1 in oesophageal cancer. The preceding papers have determined that DCBLD1 is highly expressed in cervical cancer and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (Fu et al. 2022; Shen et al. 2025), and associated with the diagnosis, immunotherapy, and prognosis. DCBLD1 promotes the migration, invasion, and growth while inhibiting cell apoptosis and G1 cell cycle arrest of cervical cancer cells (Meng et al. 2024; Shen et al. 2024). In addition to clarifying the elevated DCBLD1 expression in both oesophageal cancer specimens and cells, our investigation also expanded the carcinogenic role of DCBLD1 in oesophageal cancer, as evidenced by the decreased potentials to proliferate, migrate and the promoted apoptotic event in DCBLD1-depleting cells. As expected, interference with DCBLD1 also suppressed tumor burden and promoted oesophageal tumor tissue necrosis.

Given the positive correlation between lysine lactylation level with glycolytic activity in tissues, the impacts of DCBLD1 on glycolysis were also assessed. Specifically, it was noticed that in tumor tissues, silencing of DCBLD1 decreased both glucose uptake and LA secretion, which further introduced that DCBLD1 might act as an oncogene in oesophageal cancer via driving glycolysis.

As widely supported, PDIA3 is overexpressed in human malignancies, and altered expression and aberrant functionality of PDIA3 are associated with cancer growth, progression and immune response (Wu et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2024b). In the context of oesophageal cancer, the up-regulated PDIA3 expression is observed (Miao et al. 2025) and PDIA3 is associated with the endoplasmic reticulum stress, mitochondrial function, cell proliferation and apoptosis. Our bioinformatical analyses also indicated that PDIA3 was highly expressed in oesophageal cancer tissues. In addition, DCBLD1 and PDIA3 were positively correlated in oesophageal cancer and DCBLD1 silencing reduced PDIA3 expression in oesophageal cancer cells. DCBLD1 might bind with PDIA3 upon exposure to LA, which could also be interrupted when DCBLD1 was depleted. PDIA3 knockdown reverses the impacts of Vitamin D on glycolysis in squamous cell carcinoma (Nowak et al. 2023). Functionally, PDIA3 elevation promoted the proliferation, migration whereas suppressing the apoptosis of DCBLD1-depleting cells, aggravated tumor growth, ameliorated tumor tissue necrosis in vivo. Notably, PDIA3 increased glucose uptake and lactate secretion in mice, accompanied with the increased ECAR rate, HK2, PFKFB3 and PKM2 expression in cancer cells, after DCBLD1 was knocked down. As a molecular chaperone for reconstructing misfolded proteins, knockdown of PDIA3 in hepatocytes can lead to upregulation of fatty acid synthase (FAS), a key enzyme involved in de novo fatty acid synthesis, thereby modulating lipid metabolism and contributing to hepatic steatosis (Zhang et al. 2015), demonstrating that PDIA3 influences lipid metabolic pathways by regulating the expression and/or stability of FAS, likely through its protein folding function in the ER. Furthermore, PDIA3 interacts with a network of 25 experimentally validated protein partners, including SLC2A1 (GLUT1), which encodes a major glucose transporter, and STAT3, a transcription factor involved in metabolic regulation (Li et al. 2022). Therefore, PDIA3 was speculated to influence glucose uptake and metabolic signaling by modulating the folding and stability of these metabolic client proteins. Further LDHA knockdown reverted the in vitro and in vivo impacts of DCBLD1 down-regulation and PDIA3 up-regulation.

However, we acknowledge that a genome-wide transcriptomic approach, such as RNA-seq, would provide a more comprehensive and unbiased map of the gene regulatory network governed by PDIA3. While the targeted validation of key downstream effectors presented herein firmly establishes the role of PDIA3 in glycolysis, a full delineation of its transcriptional footprint remains a limitation of the current study. Future investigations employing high-throughput sequencing will be essential to uncover the full spectrum of PDIA3-dependent transcriptional changes and potential feedback loops. Besides, PDIA3 has been recognized as a molecular chaperone for reconstructing misfolded proteins. Whether PDIA3 regulates glycolysis via modulating the folding and stability of these metabolic client proteins still needs to be confirmed in our future study. Besides, the clinical tissue analysis was conducted on a limited cohort of only three patients, which, while sufficient for preliminary observations, precludes robust statistical correlations between DCBLD1 lactylation levels and clinicopathological parameters or patient prognosis. Last but not least, the in vivo assessment relied exclusively on subcutaneous xenograft models in immunodeficient mice, a system that does not recapitulate the native tumor microenvironment, immune cell infiltration, or the complex interplay between lactate metabolism and antitumor immunity. Future studies involving expanded patient sample sizes, orthotopic tumor models, and genetically engineered mouse models will be essential to fully validate the therapeutic scope and translational applicability of targeting the DCBLD1 lactylation axis in esophageal cancer.

Conclusions

To sum up, by presenting an initial report of global protein Kla modification in esophageal cancer, we exposed lactate as a signaling molecule to lactylate and activate DCBLD1 interacting with and maintaining stability of PDIA3 protein, by which cancer cells rely on aerobic glycolysis to promote tumor growth. As such, interventions targeting the lactate‑DCBLD1 lactylation node may selectively disrupt tumor metabolism without necessitating direct inhibition of DCBLD1 itself. These findings provide a proof‑of‑concept that monitoring DCBLD1 lactylation status or interfering with its regulatory enzymes could represent a clinically actionable strategy for patient stratification or adjunctive therapy in esophageal cancer. Future validation in larger patient cohorts and exploration of pharmacological agents targeting this axis will be essential to fully realize its translational potential. Nevertheless, the lack of residue‑specific lactylation mapping and direct pharmacological validation limits immediate translational applicability. Future efforts should focus on identifying the precise lactylation sites and regulatory enzymes governing DCBLD1 modification, as well as developing selective inhibitors or degraders to fully evaluate the therapeutic scope and safety of this approach.

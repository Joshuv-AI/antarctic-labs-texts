Transport of Zariski density in compatible collections of G G -representations
Jake Huryn and Yifei Zhang

Abstract
Let X X be a connected normal scheme of finite type over 𝐙 \mathbf{Z} , let G G be a connected reductive group over 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} , and let { ρ ℓ : π 1 ​ ( X ⁡ [ 1 / ℓ ] ) → G ⁡ ( 𝐐 ℓ ) } ℓ \{\rho_{\ell}\colon\pi_{1}(X[1/\ell])\to G(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})\}_{\ell} be a Frobenius-compatible collection of continuous homomorphisms indexed by the primes. Assume Img ⁡ ( ρ ℓ ) \mathrm{Img}(\rho_{\ell}) is Zariski-dense in G 𝐐 ℓ G_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}} for all ℓ \ell in a nonempty finite set ℛ \mathcal{R} . We prove that, under certain hypotheses on ℛ \mathcal{R} (depending only on G G ), Img ⁡ ( ρ ℓ ) \Img(\rho_{\ell}) is Zariski-dense in G 𝐐 ℓ G_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}} for all ℓ \ell in a set of Dirichlet density 1 1 . As an application, we combine this result with a version of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem and recent work of Klevdal–Patrikis to obtain new information about the “canonical” local systems attached to Shimura varieties not of Abelian type.

1.2. First main result

Our first result gives a partial positive answer to Question

(

When

In the statement, we write

Theorem B

is a connected reductive group over

is a set of Dirichlet density

Assume that
M_{\ell}=G_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}
for each

Suppose

1.3.1. Remark.

(
a
)

The bound on the residue degree is explicit:
If

admits a finite morphism to

In particular, if

Moreover, if

spreads out to a finite map

(
b
)

On the proof:
The first part of Theorem

The second part, the abundance of specializations with “big monodromy”, follows from a variant of Serre’s version of Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem for profinite groups
[

1.3.2. Corollary.

Let
(G,X)
be a Shimura datum such that
\rank_{\mathbf{R}}(G^{\mathrm{ad}})\geq 2
, let

be a geometrically connected component
(

)

be the adjoint projections of the “canonical”

By recent work of Klevdal–Patrikis

\blacksquare

1.3.3
.

Due to versions of Tate’s conjecture for Abelian varieties proven by Faltings,

2.2.2

We now describe the action of

on the roots of

in a way intrinsic to

To do so, we may assume (after conjugating

and replacing it by

For simplicity, we will consider the case when

2.2.3. Lemma.

Assume

is adjoint and
g\in G({K^{\mathrm{s}}})
is a strongly regular element contained in
T({K^{\mathrm{s}}})
for a maximal torus
T
of

Then the

-action on
\{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\}
as in (

\varphi_{T,g}\colon{\Gal_{K}}\to\GL(\mathrm{X}^{*}(T_{K^{\mathrm{s}}}))

\sigma\cdot\chi={}^{\sigma\!}\chi\circ\inn(a_{\sigma})
for some
a_{\sigma}\in\operatorname{N}_{G}(T)({K^{\mathrm{s}}})

Proof.

Uniqueness presents no trouble, because the characters
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}
span
\mathrm{X}^{*}(T_{K^{\mathrm{s}}})
by the hypothesis on
G
.
Now fix

such that
\sigma(g)=a_{\sigma}ga_{\sigma}^{-1}

Then if

\sigma(\chi(g))={}^{\sigma\!}\chi(\sigma(g))={}^{\sigma\!}\chi(a_{\sigma}ga_{\sigma}^{-1})

because
a_{\sigma}T_{K^{\mathrm{s}}}a_{\sigma}^{-1}

containing

-action.

\blacksquare

4. Theorem
B

4.1. Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem for profinite groups

Recall that, if
\Pi
is a profinite group, its Frattini subgroup
\Phi(\Pi)
is defined to be the intersection of all maximal proper
closed
subgroups of
\Pi
.
The following result is a slight refinement of
[
CK16
, Fact 3.3.1.1]
; its proof is essentially extracted from those of
[
Ser89
, §9.2, Proposition 2 and §10.6, Theorem]
.

4.1.1. Proposition.

Let
X
be a connected normal scheme of finite type over
\mathbf{Z}
such that
\dim(X_{\mathbf{Q}})\geq 1
.
Then there are positive integers
N
and
d
with the following property:
For any continuous surjective homomorphism
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\mathrel{\mathrlap{\rightarrow}\mkern-2.25mu\rightarrow}\Pi
where
\Pi
is a profinite group with open Frattini subgroup, there exist infinitely many closed points
x
of
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
such that

(
a
)

[\kappa_{x}:\mathbf{Q}]\leq d
.

(
b
)

\Img(\rho_{x})=\Pi
.

(
c
)

x
extends to an element of
X(\overline{\mathbf{Z}}[1/N])
.

Proof.

By the surjectivity of
\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}(X)
for open
U\subseteq X

[
sta24
, Lemma
0BQI
]
, we may assume
X
is affine.
Pick a finite map
\pi\colon X_{\mathbf{Q}}\to\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{Q}}
of degree
d
, and let
N
be a positive integer such that
\pi
spreads out to a finite morphism
X_{\mathbf{Z}[1/N]}\to\smash{\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{Z}[1/N]}}
.
We claim that these
N
and
d
work.

Fix
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\mathrel{\mathrlap{\rightarrow}\mkern-2.25mu\rightarrow}\Pi
as in the statement, and let
\overline{\rho}
be the composition

\pi_{1}(X_{\mathbf{Q}})\mathrel{\mathrlap{\rightarrow}\mkern-2.25mu\rightarrow}\pi_{1}(X)\mathrel{\mathrlap{\rightarrow}\mkern-2.25mu\rightarrow}\Pi\mathrel{\mathrlap{\rightarrow}\mkern-2.25mu\rightarrow}\Pi/\Phi(\Pi),

the first arrow being surjective by
loc. cit.

If
x\in X_{\mathbf{Q}}
satisfies
\Img(\overline{\rho}_{x})=\Pi/\Phi(\Pi)
, then
\Img(\rho_{x})\cdot\Phi(\Pi)=\Pi
, hence
\Img(\rho_{x})=\Pi
by the definition of
\Phi(\Pi)
.
In the following paragraph, we produce such
x
.

Since, by hypothesis,
\Pi/\Phi(\Pi)
is finite, the map
\overline{\rho}
corresponds to a finite étale cover
Y\mathrel{\mathrlap{\rightarrow}\mkern-2.25mu\rightarrow}X_{\mathbf{Q}}
which is Galois with group
\Pi/\Phi(\Pi)
.
For each maximal proper subgroup
\Sigma
of
\Pi/\Phi(\Pi)
, let
\pi_{\Sigma}
denote the composition
Y/\Sigma\mathrel{\mathrlap{\rightarrow}\mkern-2.25mu\rightarrow}X_{\mathbf{Q}}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbf{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{Q}}
. Since
X
is normal,
Y/\Sigma
is irreducible
[
sta24
, Lemma
0BQL
]
, so by
[
Ser89
, §9.2, Proposition 1]
, there is a thin set
\Omega_{\Sigma}\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{n}(\mathbf{Q})
such that if
x_{0}\in\mathbf{A}^{n}(\mathbf{Q})\smallsetminus\Omega_{\Sigma}
, then the
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
-points of the fiber
\pi_{\Sigma}^{-1}\{x_{0}\}
consist of
\deg(\pi_{\Sigma})
points of
Y/\Sigma
all conjugate over
\mathbf{Q}
.
Put
\Omega\coloneqq\bigcup_{\Sigma}\Omega_{\Sigma}
.
By
[
Ser89
, §9.6, Theorem]
,
\mathbf{A}^{n}(\mathbf{Z}[1/N])\smallsetminus\Omega
is an infinite set; let
x_{0}
be an element and
x\in\pi^{-1}\{x_{0}\}
.
Then
x
is a closed point of
X_{\mathbf{Q}}
satisfying
[\kappa_{x}:\mathbf{Q}]\leq d
.
Since
x_{0}\notin\Omega
and
\deg(\pi_{\Sigma})>d
, the point
x
does not lift to
(Y/\Sigma)(\kappa_{x})
for any
\Sigma
, so
\Img(\overline{\rho}_{x})
cannot be a subset of
\Sigma
, i.e.
\Img(\overline{\rho}_{x})=\Pi/\Phi(\Pi)
.

Finally, since

is finite and
\overline{\mathbf{Z}}[1/N]
is the integral closure of
\mathbf{Z}[1/N]
in
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
, the point

extends to an element of

, as desired.

\blacksquare

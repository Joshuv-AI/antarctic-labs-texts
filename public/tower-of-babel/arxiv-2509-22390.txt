Abstract
. We prove various results about the Local Converse Problem for split reductive groups G \mathrm{G} over a non-archimedean local field F F of characteristic 0 0 and residual characteristic p p . In particular, we prove that when G \mathrm{G} is a symplectic or special orthogonal group, or the exceptional group G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} , and p p is large enough, then the optimal standard Local Converse Theorem for G ⁡ ( F ) \mathrm{G}(F) requires twisting by representations of GL r ⁡ ( F ) \operatorname{GL}_{r}(F) with r r up to half the dimension of the standard representation of the dual group of G \mathrm{G} . However, if we restrict to generic supercuspidal representations of G ⁡ ( F ) \mathrm{G}(F) then it can be improved when G = SO 2 ​ N \mathrm{G}=\operatorname{SO}_{2N} ; we conjecture that the same is true for symplectic and odd special orthogonal groups. We also consider the possibility of using non-standard representations of the dual group to distinguish representations, giving counterexamples to possible improvements for general linear groups, G 2 G_{2} and SO 2 ​ N \operatorname{SO}_{2N} .

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field and let
G=\mathrm{G}(F)
be the group of points of a connected reductive group over
F
. Given some set
\mathcal{S}
of (equivalence classes of) irreducible (complex) representations of
G
, a natural question to ask is:

(Q)

Which invariants can we use to distinguish two elements of
\mathcal{S}
?

In the context of the local Langlands correspondence, and in this paper, the invariants we consider are
twisted
\gamma
-factors
, which are certain functions of a complex variable.

For the group
\mathrm{G}=\operatorname{GL}_{N}
this question is now well-understood. We fix a non-trivial additive character
\psi
of
F
and let
\mathcal{S}=\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}(\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F))
be the set of irreducible generic representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
. Then the
Local Converse Theorem for
\operatorname{GL}_{N}

[
JL18
,
Cha19
]
says that one possible answer to (Q) is:

(A)

\left\{\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)\,:\,\tau\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}(\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)),r\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}
,

where the
\gamma
-factors are those defined via Rankin–Selberg convolution
[
JPSS83
]
or by the Langlands–Shahidi method
[
Sha84
]
. Moreover, if
p>N
then this answer is optimal in the sense that the bound on
r
cannot be reduced, and it remains optimal if we replace
\mathcal{S}
by the set of irreducible supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
(see
[
ALST18
,
Adr23
]
). Interestingly, if we look only at the set of
simple
(or
epipelagic
) supercuspidal representations (those of depth
\frac{1}{N}
) then one can do better: only twists by characters of
\operatorname{GL}_{1}(F)
are required (see
[
BH14
, Proposition 2.2]
, and also
[
LS25
]
for further discussion of this).

For other groups
\mathrm{G}
, analogous
\gamma
-factors have also been defined; for example, for split classical groups over
p
-adic fields. However, the situation is not necessarily as clean as in the case of general linear groups: indeed, in full generality it
should not even be possible
to distinguish irreducible representations using only
\gamma
-factors, as we now explain. We will assume that the group
\mathrm{G}
is split and we fix a Whittaker datum for
G=\mathrm{G}(F)
, so that
generic
always means relative to this fixed datum.

Write
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
for the complex dual group of
\mathrm{G}
and consider
\varrho:{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})\to\operatorname{GL}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
an algebraic representation, for some integer
M
. To each
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}(G)
, if we have a Langlands correspondence for
G
then there is a corresponding Langlands parameter
\varphi_{\pi}
and we should obtain a
functorial lifting

{}_{\varrho}\Pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}(\operatorname{GL}_{M}(F))
which corresponds to the Langlands parameter
\varrho\circ\varphi_{\pi}
. Attached to the pair
(\pi,\varrho)
is a conjectural gamma factor
\gamma(s,\pi,\varrho,\psi)
, predicted by the Langlands program. Then an equality of twisted
\gamma
-factors for
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}(G)
(relative to
\varrho
, when these have been defined) would only guarantee
{}_{\varrho}\Pi_{1}\cong{}_{\varrho}\Pi_{2}
, but not necessarily
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
. This phenomenon is equivalent to the question of whether, given two generic Langlands parameters
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
for
G
, having
\varrho\circ\varphi_{1}
conjugate to
\varrho\circ\varphi_{2}
over
\operatorname{GL}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
, implies that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
-conjugate. There are some groups and representations for which this is true – for example when
\mathrm{G}
is
\operatorname{Sp}_{2N}
or
\operatorname{SO}_{2N+1}
and
\varrho
is the standard representation – but that is not always the case.

This question, as we allow
\varrho
to vary over all possible algebraic representations of
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
, is encapsulated in the notion of acceptability. First, we say that a morphism
{WD}_{F}\rightarrow{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
of the Weil-Deligne group is
generic
if its adjoint
L
-function is holomorphic at
s=1
; when the local Langlands correspondence is known, generic parameters correspond precisely to
L
-packets that have a generic element (see
[
Mat24
, §2.2]
and
[
GI16
, Proposition B.1]
). We say that two morphisms
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}:{WD}_{F}\to{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
are
locally conjugate
if, for all algebraic representations
\varrho
of
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
, we have
\varrho\circ\varphi\cong\varrho\circ\varphi^{\prime}
; equivalently (see
[
CG24
]
),
\varphi(\gamma)
and
\varphi^{\prime}(\gamma)
are conjugate in
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Finally, we say that
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
is
generically
{WD}_{F}
-acceptable
if any pair of locally conjugate generic morphisms
{WD}_{F}\to{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
are in fact conjugate (i.e. there is a
g\in{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\varphi^{\prime}=\operatorname{Ad}(g)\circ\varphi
). Then, the most general version of the local converse problem on the automorphic side, when transferred to the Galois side, requires a given dual group
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
to be generically
{WD}_{F}
-acceptable.

On the other hand, there is a more general definition of acceptability, which we now recall (following
[
Lar94
,
CG24
,
Mat24
]
). We say that
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
is
acceptable
if, for any
finite
group
\Gamma
, any two locally conjugate group homomorphisms
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}:\Gamma\rightarrow{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
are automatically conjugate. Matringe
[
Mat24
]
has shown that if
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
is acceptable, then it is also generically
{WD}_{F}
-acceptable. However, it is unclear whether the reverse implication is true. For example, it is known that
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
is unacceptable when
N\geq 3
(see
[
Yu22
]
) but unknown whether or not it is in general generically
{WD}_{F}
-unacceptable. In
[
Mat24
]
, it is shown that
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
is generically
{WD}_{F}
-unacceptable when
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
; we will show that the same is true when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.

We consider now the algebraic representation ring
\mathrm{R}[{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}]
of
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
. It is generated by finitely many representations (see for example
[
FH91
, Section 23]
). For example, in the case of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
these are the exterior powers
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i}
of the standard representation, for
i=1,\ldots N
. If
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
is simply connected, these are the
fundamental representations
, which are the irreducible finite-dimensional representations whose highest weight is a fundamental weight; see later for some other examples. Thus,
{WD}_{F}
-acceptability of
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
can be detected by only testing on a finite number of representations: if
\varrho\circ\varphi\cong\varrho\circ\varphi^{\prime}
for these (finitely many) representations
\varrho
then it is also true for all algebraic representations
\rho
.

Thus, when there is a
suitable local Langlands correspondence for
\mathrm{G}
(by which we mean one satisfying
[
KT
, Conjectures 6.1,6.5]
in the formulation of Kaletha–Taïbi, so it sends tempered representations to parameters with bounded image (
[
KT
, Conjecture 6.1, §5.3]
), and there is unicity of generic representations in a tempered
L
-packet) we get the following result immediately from the Local Converse Theorem for
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
. To state it, we
define
the twisted local factors by

\gamma(s,\pi\rtimes\tau,\varrho,\psi):=\gamma(s,(\varrho\circ\varphi_{\pi})\otimes\varphi_{\tau},\psi),

for
\varrho
an algebraic representation of
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
and
\tau\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}(\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F))
, though of course one would hope to have a direct definition of these
\gamma
-factors on the automorphic side which matches with this.

The Local Converse Theorem
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a split connected reductive group over
F
such that the dual group
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{C})
is generically
{WD}_{F}
-acceptable, and for which there is a suitable local Langlands correspondence. Let
\varrho_{i}
, for
i\in I
, be generators of
\mathrm{R}[{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}]
(which can be taken to be finite in number), and put
K=\max_{i\in I}{\dim(\varrho_{i})}
. Suppose that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are irreducible generic tempered representations of
\mathrm{G}(F)
such that

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\rtimes\tau,\varrho_{i},\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\rtimes\tau,\varrho_{i},\psi)

for all
i\in I
and all
\tau\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}(\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F))
with
r\leq\left\lfloor\frac{K}{2}\right\rfloor
. Then
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
.

Note that this version of the Local Converse Theorem was proven in
[
Mat24
]
in the case that
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}
is simply connected; in that case, the fundamental representations generate
\mathrm{R}[{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}]
.

Thus, in the situation of this Local Converse Theorem, all these twisted
\gamma
-factors together provide an answer to (Q), but this is almost certainly suboptimal: indeed, in the case of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
one does not need any of the fundamental representations other than the standard one, though one could also wonder if we could choose to cut down in a different way (see later in the introduction). This paper, then, is about the possible suboptimality of this Local Converse Theorem.

Since most of our results concern groups whose dual groups have a standard representation
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}
(faithful of smallest dimension), it is convenient to make the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathrm{G}
be a split connected reductive group over
F
, whose dual group has a standard representation
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}
. Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be irreducible generic representations of
\mathrm{G}(F)
and let
m\geq 1
be an integer. We say that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
m
if

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\rtimes\tau,\varrho_{\mathrm{std}},\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\rtimes\tau,\varrho_{\mathrm{std}},\psi)

for all irreducible supercuspidal representations
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
, with
1\leq r\leq m
. We use the same terminology for Langlands parameters also.

Note that, by multiplicativity of
\gamma
-factors, if
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
m
then we in fact have
\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\rtimes\tau,\varrho_{\mathrm{std}},\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\rtimes\tau,\varrho_{\mathrm{std}},\psi)
for all
generic
irreducible representations
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
, with
1\leq r\leq m
.

1.1.
The classical groups

From now on, we assume that the residual characteristic of
F
is odd. Let
G_{N}
be the group of points of a split classical group of rank
N
– i.e.
\operatorname{Sp}_{2N}(F),\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
, or
\operatorname{SO}_{2N+1}(F)
. The main theorem that has already been proven in the literature, which we will refer to as the
the standard local converse theorem
, says the following, where we say that irreducible representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
of
G_{N}
are
outer conjugate
if either they are conjugate (when
G_{N}
is
\operatorname{Sp}_{2N}(F)
of
\operatorname{SO}_{2N+1}(F)
) or there is an element
g\in\operatorname{O}_{2N}(F)
such that
{}^{g}\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
(when
G_{N}=\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
).

The Standard Local Converse Theorem for
G_{N}

(see
[
JS03
,
Zha18
,
HL25
]
)
.

Suppose that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are irreducible generic representations of
G_{N}
, with the same central character, which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N
. Then
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate.

Thus, in the setting of the general Local Converse Theorem, this says that we
only
need to consider the standard representation of the dual group. Our first result is that we may remove the condition on the central characters of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
(see Theorem
3.2
). Our subsequent focus is on the possible suboptimality of the Standard Local Converse Theorem for
G_{N}
(namely, can we reduce the number of
\operatorname{GL}
twists while considering only
\varrho=\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}
), although in principle one could also consider generators of
R[{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}]
other than the standard representation. We first prove the following theorem, which shows that the Standard Local Converse Theorem for
G_{N}
is optimal in the tame setting, for generic, tempered, irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}_{N}
.

Theorem 1.2

(see Corollary
3.6
)
.

Suppose
p>N
. Then there are generic irreducible tempered (non-cuspidal) representations
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
of
G_{N}
, which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
but not outer conjugate.

However, the situation changes if one assumes that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are also supercuspidal. We first state the main results in the setting of
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}
since we have a clean statement there, and then we discuss the results for a general classical group.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
3.16
and Corollary
3.9
)
.

Suppose
p>N
.

(i)

If
N
is even then the standard local converse theorem for
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}
is optimal.

(ii)

If
N
is odd and
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are irreducible generic supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
, then
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate. Moreover, this new bound is optimal.

We are able to prove the same results for the other classical groups
conditional on a conjecture
which we now explain. First, we recall that a discrete Langlands parameter for a classical group is a multiplicity-free direct sum of twists of irreducible representation self-dual representations of
W_{F}
by irreducible representations of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
(see the proof of Lemma
3.14
for a more precise statement). It therefore becomes natural to understand if there is any possible improvement in the standard local converse theorem for
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
when one restricts to the setting of self-dual supercuspidal representations. While the standard local converse theorem for
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
asserts that the bound
[N/2]
is optimal for general supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
, we make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.4
.

(see Conjecture
3.10
)
Suppose
p>N
and
N
is odd. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be self-dual supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(F)
of the same parity which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
.
Then
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
.

We have no evidence as to whether the assumption on
p
or
N
is needed, or even whether the above bound is optimal; at best, it can be lowered by
1
more, as the following shows:

Theorem 1.5
.

(see Theorem
3.12
)
Suppose
p>N
, set
M=2\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor
, and fix a parity. There exist inequivalent self-dual supercuspidal representations
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(F)
of this parity which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
M-2
.

Assuming Conjecture
3.10
, we also have the analogue of Theorem
1.3
for the symplectic and odd special orthogonal groups. We refer to Theorem
3.13
and Corollary
3.9
for the statement.

We end this section by considering the question of acceptability. In Example
3.17
, we show that
\operatorname{SO}_{6}
is generically
WD_{F}
-unacceptable when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. To be more specific, the representation ring
R[\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C})]
is generated by
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{1},\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2},\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{+},\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{-}
, where
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}=\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{+}\oplus\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{-}
, and we give an example of inequivalent generic (supercuspidal) parameters
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}:W_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
which are conjugate under
\operatorname{O}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
(so that their compositions with
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{r}
are equivalent, for any
r
) and, moreover
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{+}\circ\varphi_{1}\cong\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{-}\circ\varphi_{2}
. In particular, this means that all their twisted
\gamma
-factors will be equal, whatever representation of the dual group we take. As we comment in Remark
3.19
, the same method could be used to give discrete parameter counterexamples for
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}
whenever
N\geq 3
is odd.

Moreover, we are able to leverage this example to show that
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
is generically
{WD}_{F}
-unacceptable when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
(see Corollary
3.18
); this complements Matringe’s result
[
Mat24
, Proposition 7.3]
that
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
is generically
{WD}_{F}
-unacceptable when
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Note, however, that the parameters demonstrating these results are
not
discrete.

1.2.
The exceptional group
\mathrm{G}_{2}

We also investigate the exceptional split group of type
\mathrm{G}_{2}
, when
p>3
. In this case there are two fundamental representations of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
: the standard
7
-dimensional, and the adjoint, which is
14
-dimensional. We begin by proving an expected result, the
standard local converse theorem for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
, which says that the family of
\operatorname{GL}_{i}
-twisted gamma factors,
i=1,2,3
, using only the standard representation of the dual group, determines an irreducible generic representation of
\mathrm{G}_{2}
. We then prove that one cannot lower this bound of twisting to
i=1,2
, and that even if we include the adjoint representation of the dual group, we still do not obtain a local converse theorem. To summarize, we prove the following theorem (see Theorem
4.6
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
p>3
.

(i)

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be two generic Langlands parameters for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
. If
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
3
, then
\varphi_{1}\cong\varphi_{2}
.

(ii)

There are inequivalent generic supercuspidal representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
such that

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\rtimes\tau,\varrho,\psi):=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\rtimes\tau,\varrho,\psi),

for
\varrho
either fundamental representation of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and all supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
, with
1\leq r\leq 2
; in particular, they are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
2
.

1.3.
The general linear groups
\operatorname{GL}_{N}

Finally, we investigate whether one can improve upon the Local Converse Theorem for
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
in ways other than by cutting out the non-standard fundamental representations. In fact, in
[
YZ22
, Conjecture 1.2]
, a hint was provided along these lines. The authors state a conjecture, attributed to Ramakrishnan
[
Ram94
]
, that for unitarizable supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
one only needs to consider the
\operatorname{GL}_{1}
-twisted gamma factors while allowing all fundamental representations. Namely, the gamma factors

\gamma(s,\pi\rtimes\eta,\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i},\psi),\text{ for~$i=1,\ldots,\left\lfloor\tfrac{N}{2}\right\rfloor$ and~$\eta$ a character of~$F^{\times}$}.

(They also assume that the central character is fixed, though that is unnecessary since the central character is determined by the standard twisted
\gamma
-factors
\gamma(s,\pi\times\eta,\psi)
; see, for example,
[
JNS15
, Corollary 2.7]
.) In Section
5
, we prove that this conjecture is false in general:

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose
p
is odd. Then there are inequivalent irreducible parameters
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}:W_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{C})
, corresponding to unitarizable supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{4}(F)
, such that

\gamma(s,(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i}\circ\varphi_{1})\otimes\eta,\psi)=\gamma(s,(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i}\circ\varphi_{2})\otimes\eta,\psi),

for all characters
\eta
of
W_{F}
and
i=1,\ldots,4
.

Thus twisting only by characters of
F^{\times}
cannot be sufficient to distinguish supercuspidal representations, even if all fundamental representations of the dual group are considered.

2.
Representations of the Weil group and
\gamma
-factors

We recall the parametrization of tame Langlands parameters and their associated local factors. Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic zero, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, maximal ideal
\mathcal{P}_{F}
, residue field
k_{F}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q=q_{F}=p^{f}
is a power of the residual characteristic
p
, and
\mu^{\prime}_{F}
the group of roots of unity of order prime to
p
. We use analogous notation for any extension
E
of
F
. We also fix a non-trivial additive character
\psi_{F}
of
F
of level one: that is, it is trivial on
\mathcal{P}_{F}
but not on
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. For any finite extension
E/F
, write
e_{E/F}
and
f_{E/F}
for the ramification index and residue degree of
E/F
respectively. We also define an additive character
\psi_{E}
of
E
via
\psi_{E}=\psi_{F}\circ\operatorname{tr}_{E/F}
; if
E/F
is tamely ramified (that is,
p\nmid e_{E/F}
) then
\psi_{E}
is also of level one.

Let
\overline{F}
be a fixed algebraic closure of
F
. We normalize the additive valuation
\operatorname{val}
on
F
to have image
\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
and extend this to an additive valuation on
\overline{F}
; thus. for
E/F
any finite extension, we have
\operatorname{val}(E^{\times})=\frac{1}{e_{E/F}}\mathbb{Z}
. For
r
a real number, we write

\mathcal{P}_{E}^{r}=\{x\in E:\operatorname{val}(x)\geq r\},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{E}^{r+}=\{x\in E:\operatorname{val}(x)>r\}

so, for example,
\mathcal{P}_{E}^{1/e_{E/F}}=\mathcal{P}_{E}=\mathcal{P}_{E}^{0+}
. We put
U_{E}=U_{E}^{0}=\mathcal{O}_{E}^{\times}
, with filtration subgroups
U_{E}^{r}=1+\mathcal{P}_{E}^{r}
, for
r>0
, and
U_{E}^{r+}=1+\mathcal{P}_{E}^{r+}
, for
r\geq 0
.

We also write
|\cdot|
for the normalized absolute value on
\overline{F}
, given by

|x|=q^{-\operatorname{val}(x)},\qquad\text{ for }x\in\overline{F}^{\times}.

Note that if
E/F
is a finite extension then the usual normalization of the absolute value on
E
is given by
x\mapsto|x|^{[E:F]}
, for
x\in E
.

Let
W_{F}
be the Weil group of
F
. Implicit in the definition of the Weil group are compatible choices of isomorphisms
E^{\times}\simeq W_{E}^{\text{ab}}
, for each finite extension
E/F
, via which we will identify the quasi-characters of
E^{\times}
and of
W_{E}
. Moreover, if
F\subseteq E\subseteq K
and
\chi
is a quasi-character of
E^{\times}
, let
\chi_{K}:=\chi\circ\operatorname{N}_{K/E}
. When
\chi
is viewed as a quasi-character of
W_{E}
, the quasi-character
\chi_{K}
is simply the restriction of
\chi
to the subgroup
W_{K}
. For
E/F
a finite extension, write
\operatorname{Ind}_{E/F}=\operatorname{Ind}_{W_{E}}^{W_{F}}
for the induction functor.

2.1.
Admissible pairs

Let
N>1
be an integer not divisible by
p
. In this situation, there is a nice parametrization of the irreducible
N
-dimensional representations of
W_{F}
in terms of admissible quasi-characters, introduced by Howe.

Definition 2.1

(Howe
[
How77
]
)
.

An
admissible pair
of degree
N
is a pair
(E/F,\chi)
where
E/F
is a (tamely ramified) extension of degree
N
and
\chi
is a quasi-character of
E^{\times}
such that

(i)

\chi
does not come via the norm from a proper subfield of
E
containing
F
; and

(ii)

if the restriction
\chi|_{U_{E}^{0+}}
comes via the norm from a subfield
F\subseteq L\subseteq E
, then
E/L
is unramified.

Two admissible pairs
(E_{1}/F,\chi_{1})
and
(E_{2}/F,\chi_{2})
are said to be
conjugate
if there is an
F
-isomorphism from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
which takes
\chi_{1}
to
\chi_{2}
.

In this situation we also say that
\chi
is an
admissible quasi-character
of
E^{\times}
(relative to
F
). For any such admissible quasi-character, we write

\rho_{\chi}:=\operatorname{Ind}_{E/F}\chi.

Theorem 2.2

(Moy
[
Moy86
, Theorem 2.2.2]
)
.

If
(E/F,\chi)
is an admissible pair of degree
N
, then
\rho_{\chi}
is an irreducible
N
-dimensional representation of
W_{F}
. Furthermore, two admissible pairs induce to equivalent representations if and only if they are conjugate, and each irreducible
N
-dimensional representation of
W_{F}
is induced from an admissible pair.

Let
E/F
be a (tamely ramified) extension of degree
N
, and
\chi
a
ramified
quasi-character of
E^{\times}
; that is,
\chi
is non-trivial on
U_{E}
. Let
d(\chi)\in\frac{1}{e_{E/F}}\mathbb{Z}
be the
normalized depth
of
\chi
: that is,
\chi
is trivial on
U_{E}^{d(\chi)+}
but not on
U_{E}^{d(\chi)}
. (The
conductoral exponent
of
\chi
is then
e_{E/F}d(\chi)+1
.) There is then
c_{\chi}\in\mathcal{P}_{E}^{-d(\chi)}
, well-defined modulo
\mathcal{P}_{E}^{-{d(\chi)}/2}
such that

\chi(1+x)=\psi_{E}(c_{\chi}x),\qquad\text{ for }x\in\mathcal{P}_{E}^{(d(\chi)/2)+}.

We say that
c_{\chi}

represents

\chi
. We extend this to unramified quasi-characters
\chi
of
E^{\times}
by setting
d(\chi)=0
and
c_{\chi}=0
(which we will also take for quasi-characters of depth
0
).

Note that if
K/E
is tamely ramified then
d(\chi_{K})=d(\chi)
, since
\operatorname{N}_{K/E}(U_{K}^{r})=U_{E}^{r}
, for any
r>0
. Moreover
\chi_{K}
is also represented by
c_{\chi}
: that is, we can choose
c_{\chi_{K}}=c_{\chi}
. We also make the following simple observation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
E/F
and
L/F
be finite tamely ramified extensions and let
\chi,\eta
be quasi-characters of
E^{\times},L^{\times}
respectively, represented by
c_{\chi}
and
c_{\eta}
respectively. We consider the quasi-character
\chi_{EL}\eta_{EL}
of the compositum
(EL)^{\times}
.

(i)

We have
d(\chi_{EL}\eta_{EL})\leq\max\{d(\chi),d(\eta)\}
.

(ii)

If
d(\chi)\neq d(\eta)
then
\chi_{EL}\eta_{EL}
is ramified of normalized depth
d(\chi_{EL}\eta_{EL})=\max\{d(\chi),d(\eta)\}
, and is represented by
c_{\chi}+c_{\eta}
.

There are certain situations in which we can tell directly from a representative for a quasi-character
\chi
of
E^{\times}
that it is admissible over
F
. For
d<0
a rational number, we say that
\beta\in E
is
quasi-minimal (over F) of depth
d
if
\operatorname{val}(\beta)=-d
and

•

every element of the coset
\beta+\mathcal{P}_{E}^{-d/2}
generates the (tame) extension
E/F
.

Note that
\beta
is called
minimal
in
[
BK93
, (1.4.14)]
if
\operatorname{val}(\beta)=-d
and every element of the coset
\beta+\mathcal{P}_{E}^{-d+}
generates
E/F
. We will use the notion especially in the
minimal totally ramified
case, that is when
E/F
is totally ramified of degree
N
and
d
has least denominator
N
.

If
\beta
is quasi-minimal then the character
1+x\mapsto\psi_{E}(\beta x)
of
U_{E}^{(d/2)+}
does not come via the norm from a proper subfield of
E
containing
F
. In particular, any quasi-character represented by a quasi-minimal element is admissible; this generalizes the remark that any
generic
quasi-character (in the sense of Kutzko
[
Kut80
]
, see
[
Moy86
, Definition 2.2.3]
) is admissible.

2.2.
Local factors

As above, let
E/F
be an extension of degree
N
not divisible by
p
, let
\chi
be a ramified quasi-character of
E^{\times}
of depth
d(\chi)>0
, and set
J=U_{E}^{d(\chi)/2}
and
H=U_{E}^{(d(\chi)/2)+}
. We define the Gauss sum

G(\chi,\psi_{F})=[J:H]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{x\in J/H}\chi^{-1}(x)\psi_{F}(c_{\chi}(x-1)).

This collapses to
1
if
J=H
, which happens precisely when the integer
e_{E/F}d(\chi)
is odd.

Associated to any quasi-character
\chi
of
E^{\times}
we also have local factors
L(s,\chi)
,
\epsilon(s,\chi,\psi_{E})
and
\gamma(s,\chi,\psi_{E})
, by Tate’s thesis
[
Tat67
]
(see also the account in
[
BH06
, §23]
). When
\chi
is ramified we have
L(s,\chi)=1
and
\epsilon(s,\chi,\psi_{E})=\gamma(s,\chi,\psi_{E})
. Denote by
\epsilon(\chi,\psi_{E})
the value of the
\epsilon
-factor at
s=0
. Note that
\epsilon(s,\chi,\psi_{E})=\epsilon(\chi\lvert\cdot\rvert^{Ns},\psi_{E})
; the extra factor
N
appears here because of our normalization of the absolute value.

Proposition 2.4

(Moy,
[
Moy86
, (2.3.17)]
)
.

For
\chi
a ramified quasi-character of
E^{\times}
, we have

\epsilon(\chi,\psi_{E})=\chi^{-1}(c_{\chi})\psi_{E}(c_{\chi})|c_{\chi}|^{N/2}G(\chi,\psi_{E}).

As an immediate corollary, we get the following simple result.

Corollary 2.5

(cf.
[
Adr23
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

Suppose
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
are ramified quasi-characters of
E^{\times}
of normalized depth
d\geq 0
, and that
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
coincide on
U_{E}^{d/2}
so that they can be represented by a common element
c_{\chi}
. Then

\gamma(s,\chi_{1},\psi_{E})=\left(\frac{\chi_{1}(c_{\chi})}{\chi_{2}(c_{\chi})}\right)\gamma(s,\chi_{2},\psi_{E}).

More generally, for
\rho
any semisimple finite-dimensional complex representation of the Weil group
W_{F}
, there are local factors
L(s,\rho)
,
\epsilon(s,\rho,\psi_{F})
and
\gamma(s,\rho,\psi_{F})
(see, for example,
[
BH06
, Theorem 29.4]
). We have
L(s,\rho)=1
when
\rho
is irreducible of dimension
N>1
so the
\epsilon
and
\gamma
factors again coincide. Moreover, the local factors are multiplicative:
\gamma(s,\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2},\psi_{F})=\gamma(s,\rho_{1},\psi_{F})\gamma(s,\rho_{2},\psi_{F})
, and similarly for
L
and
\epsilon
.

We end this section by recalling the following results on the tensor product of such representations. For
K/F
a finite extension, we write
\lambda_{K/F}(\psi_{F})
for the Langlands constant associated to
K/F
and
\psi_{F}
(see
[
BH06
, Theorem 29.4 and §34.3]
).

Lemma 2.6

(
[
ALST18
, Lemmas 2.4, 2.5]
)
.

Let
E
and
L
be tamely ramified field extensions of
F
, and let
\chi
and
\eta
be quasi-characters of
E^{\times}
and
L^{\times}
respectively. For
g\in W_{F}
we write
E_{g}=g(E)
and
K_{g}=E_{g}L
, and set
\theta_{g}=({}^{g}\chi)_{K_{g}}\eta_{K_{g}}
. Then

\rho_{\chi}\otimes\rho_{\eta}\cong\bigoplus_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E}}\operatorname{Ind}_{K_{g}/F}\theta_{g}

and

\gamma(s,\rho_{\chi}\otimes\rho_{\eta},\psi_{F})=\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E}}\lambda_{K_{g}/F}(\psi_{F})\gamma(s,\theta_{g},\psi_{K_{g}}).

2.3.
Totally ramified quasi-characters

In this subsection, we give the main technical results on equalities of
\gamma
-factors for representations of
W_{F}
which are at the heart of our sharpness examples. We continue with
E/F
an extension of degree
N
not divisible by
p
and let
\chi
be a ramified quasi-character of
E^{\times}
of depth
d(\chi)>0
. We say that
\chi
is
totally ramified
if
E/F
is totally ramified, and that
\chi
is
minimal totally ramified
if also it is represented by a minimal element (equivalently,
d(\chi)
has least denominator
N
).

For
L/F
a finite extension of degree
r
and
\alpha\in L
, we write
f_{\alpha}(X)\in F[X]
for the characteristic polynomial of
\alpha
acting by multiplication on
L
, so that

f_{\alpha}(X)=\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)}(X-\sigma(\alpha)),

where
\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)
is the set of
F
-embeddings of
L
into
\overline{F}
. Of course, this polynomial
f_{\alpha}
depends on the field
L
as well as
\alpha
but the field will be clear from context.

Proposition 2.7
.

For
i
in some finite set
I
, let
(E_{i}/F,\chi_{i}),(E_{i}/F,\chi^{\prime}_{i})
be totally ramified admissible pairs of degree
N_{i}
and depth
d_{i}
, such that
\chi_{i},\chi^{\prime}_{i}
coincide on
U^{d_{i}/2}_{E_{i}}
; choose
\beta_{i}
which represents both. Suppose
(L,\eta)
is an admissible pair of degree
r
, represented by
\alpha
, with depth distinct from all
d_{i}
, and set
\tau=\operatorname{Ind}_{L/F}\eta
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\gamma(s,(\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\rho_{\chi_{i}})\otimes\tau,\psi_{F})=\gamma(s,(\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\rho_{\chi^{\prime}_{i}})\otimes\tau,\psi_{F})
;

(ii)

\prod\limits_{i\in I}\chi_{i}((-1)^{r}f_{\alpha}(-\beta_{i}))=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\chi^{\prime}_{i}((-1)^{r}f_{\alpha}(-\beta_{i}))
.

Note that the condition on the depth of
\eta
in Proposition
2.7
is implied if
r
is not divisible by any least denominator of
d_{i}
, since
d(\eta)\in\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The ideas of the proof are essentially in the proof of
[
Adr23
, Theorem 3.2]
. For
g\in W_{F}
and
i\in I
, we write
E_{i,g}=g(E_{i})
and
K_{i,g}=E_{i,g}L
, and set
\theta_{i,g}=({}^{g}\chi_{i})_{K_{i,g}}\eta_{K_{i,g}}
and
\theta^{\prime}_{i,g}=({}^{g}\chi^{\prime}_{i})_{K_{i,g}}\eta_{K_{i,g}}
. By Lemma
2.3
(ii), the quasi-characters
\theta_{i,g}
and
\theta^{\prime}_{i,g}
are represented by
g(\beta_{i})+\alpha
and have depth
d_{i,g}:=\max\{d_{i},d(\eta)\}
. Thus, by Lemma
2.6
and multiplicativity, we have

\gamma(s,({\textstyle\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\rho_{\chi_{i}}})\otimes\tau,\psi_{F})=\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E_{i}}}\lambda_{K_{i,g}/F}(\psi_{F})\gamma(s,\theta_{i,g},\psi_{K_{i,g}}),

Thus the equality in
(i)
is equivalent to

\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E_{i}}}\gamma(s,\theta_{i,g},\psi_{K_{i,g}})=\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E_{i}}}\gamma(s,\theta^{\prime}_{i,g},\psi_{K_{i,g}}).

Moreoever,
\theta_{i,g}
and
\theta^{\prime}_{i,g}
coincide on
U^{d_{i,g}/2}_{K_{i,g}}
since
\operatorname{N}_{K_{i,g}/E_{i,g}}(U^{d_{i,g}/2}_{K_{i,g}})\subseteq U^{d_{i}/2}_{E_{i,g}}
, where the quasi-characters
{}^{g}\chi_{i}
and
{}^{g}\chi^{\prime}_{i}
coincide by hypothesis. Thus it follows from Corollary
2.5
that this is equivalent to

\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E_{i}}}\theta_{i,g}(g(\beta_{i})+\alpha)=\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E_{i}}}\theta^{\prime}_{i,g}(g(\beta_{i})+\alpha),

which is in turn equivalent to

\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E_{i}}}({}^{g}\chi_{i})_{K_{i,g}}(g(\beta_{i})+\alpha)=\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E_{i}}}({}^{g}\chi^{\prime}_{i})_{K_{i,g}}(g(\beta_{i})+\alpha).

Thus, to complete the proof, it suffices to show that

\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E_{i}}}({}^{g}\chi_{i})_{K_{i,g}}(g(\beta_{i})+\alpha)=\chi_{i}((-1)^{r}f_{\alpha}(-\beta_{i})),

for which we drop the subscripts
i
. As in
[
Adr23
, after (4.3)]
, we have

\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E}}({}^{g}\chi)_{K_{g}}(g(\beta)+\alpha)=\chi\left(\prod_{g\in W_{L}\backslash W_{F}/W_{E}}\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K_{g}/E_{g})}(\beta+g^{-1}\sigma(\alpha))\right).

But, as also explained in
[
Adr23
]
, this further simplifies to

\chi\left(\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)}(\beta+\sigma(\alpha))\right)=\chi((-1)^{r}f_{\alpha}(-\beta)).

∎

We will apply this lemma to the case where all
N_{i}=N
are equal and all depths
d_{i}
are equal, since in that case one can further simplify. To this end, we introduce some further notation. Let
(E/F,\chi)
be a totally ramified admissible pair of degree
N
and depth
d
represented by
\beta
, and let
M
be the least denominator of
d
. For
L/F
an extension of degree less than
M
and
\alpha\in L
we set

u^{+}_{\alpha}(X)=\operatorname{N}_{L/F}(-\alpha)^{-1}f_{\alpha}(-X),\qquad u^{-}_{\alpha}(X)=(-X)^{-r}f_{\alpha}(-X).

Since
u^{\pm}_{\alpha}(X)
are Laurent polynomials with coefficients in
F
, we have
u^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\beta)\in E
. Moreover, since
\operatorname{val}(\alpha)\neq\operatorname{val}(\beta)
, we see that

\begin{cases}u^{+}_{\alpha}(\beta)\in U_{E}^{0+}&\text{ if }\operatorname{val}(\alpha)<\operatorname{val}(\beta),\\
u^{-}_{\alpha}(\beta)\in U_{E}^{0+}&\text{ if }\operatorname{val}(\alpha)>\operatorname{val}(\beta).\end{cases}

Now put

\displaystyle t^{+}_{\beta,L}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\operatorname{val}\left(1-u^{+}_{\alpha}(\beta))\right)\,:\,\alpha\in L,\ \operatorname{val}(\alpha)<\operatorname{val}(\beta)\right\},

\displaystyle t^{-}_{\beta,L}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\operatorname{val}\left(1-u^{-}_{\alpha}(\beta))\right)\,:\,\alpha\in L,\ \operatorname{val}(\beta)<\operatorname{val}(\alpha)<0\right\},

where we interpret
t^{-}_{\beta,L}=\infty
if the set in its definition is empty. Since
\operatorname{val}(E)=\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}
, we have
t^{\pm}_{\beta,L}\geq\frac{1}{N}
.

Now, for
r\leq M
an integer, we set

t_{\beta}(r)=\min\left\{t^{+}_{\beta,L},t^{-}_{\beta,L}\,:\,[L:F]<r\right\}.

Again, we have
t_{\beta}(r)\geq\frac{1}{N}
. We will be particularly interested in cases when this inequality is strict. As a simple case, we have

(2.8)

t_{\beta}(2)=\min\left\{\left|d-\tfrac{n}{M}\right|\,:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

since we only need to consider
\alpha\in F
, where
u^{\pm}_{\alpha}(X)=1+(\alpha^{-1}X)^{\pm 1}
. More generally, we have the following, which will need later:

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
E/F
is totally ramified of degree
N
and
d=\frac{m}{M}
has least denominator
M
. Suppose
\beta\in E
has valuation
-d
and let
r
be the least integer such that
rm\equiv\pm 1\pmod{M}
. If
r>1
, then
t_{\beta}(r)>\frac{1}{M}
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that, for any integer
s<r
such that
sm\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{M}
, any extension
L/F
of degree
s
, and any
\alpha\in L
, we have
u^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\beta)\in U_{E}^{(1/M)+}
. Now
u^{\pm}_{\alpha}(X)
is a polynomial of degree
s
in
X^{\pm 1}
with coefficients in
F
and constant term
1
. Thus, for each
1\leq i\leq s
, the valuation of the monomial of degree
i
when evaluated at
\beta
lies in
\pm\frac{mi}{M}+\mathbb{Z}
, and it is greater than
0
by construction. In particular, these all have distinct valuations (as
s<M
) so the valuation of
u^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\beta)-1
is exactly the minimum of the valuations of these monomials. Since none of these monomials have valuation in
\frac{1}{M}+\mathbb{Z}
, the valuation of
u^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\beta)-1
is greater than
\frac{1}{M}
so
u^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\beta)\in U_{E}^{(1/M)+}
.
∎

The following result is at the root of all our sharpness examples, and generalizes the essence of the proof of sharpness in the local converse theorem for
\operatorname{GL}_{N}
in
[
ALST18
,
Adr23
]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
d>0
be a rational number with least denominator
M
and let
r\leq\min\{p,M\}
be a natural number.
For
i\in I
, let
(E_{i}/F,\chi_{i}),(E_{i}/F,\chi^{\prime}_{i})
be totally ramified admissible pairs of degree
N
and depth
d
, such that
\chi_{i},\chi^{\prime}_{i}
coincide on
U^{d/2}_{E_{i}}
; choose
\beta_{i}
which represents both. Set
t_{i}=t_{\beta_{i}}(r)
, for
i\in I
, and suppose that:

(i)

\chi_{i},\chi^{\prime}_{i}
coincide on restriction to
U_{E_{i}}^{t_{i}}
, for each
i\in I
;

(ii)

\prod_{i\in I}\chi_{i}(\beta_{i})=\prod_{i\in I}\chi^{\prime}_{i}(\beta_{i})
;

(iii)

\prod_{i\in I}\chi_{i}
and
\prod_{i\in I}\chi^{\prime}_{i}
coincide on restriction to
F^{\times}
.

Then, for
\tau
any irreducible representation of
W_{F}
of dimension less than
r
, we have

\textstyle\gamma(s,(\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\rho_{\chi_{i}})\otimes\tau,\psi_{F})=\gamma(s,(\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\rho_{\chi^{\prime}_{i}})\otimes\tau,\psi_{F}).

Note that if
d
is chosen to have least denominator
N
then we are considering minimal totally ramified admissible pairs in Proposition
2.10
.

Proof.

Suppose
\tau
is an irreducible representation of
W_{F}
of dimension less than
r
. Since
p\geq r>\dim(\tau)
, we have
p\nmid\dim(\tau)
so Theorem
2.2
implies that
\tau\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{L/F}\eta
, for some admissible pair
(L/F,\eta)
. Write
d(\eta)
for its normalized depth and
\alpha\in L
for some element representing
\eta
. Since
\dim(\tau)<r\leq M
we have
d(\eta)\neq d
so, by Proposition
2.7
, we need only check that

\prod\limits_{i\in I}\chi_{i}((-1)^{r}f_{\alpha}(-\beta_{i}))=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\chi^{\prime}_{i}((-1)^{r}f_{\alpha}(-\beta_{i})).

If
d(\eta)<d
then either
\alpha=0
or
0>\operatorname{val}(\alpha)>\operatorname{val}(\beta_{i})
for each
i\in I
. In the former case,
f_{\alpha}(X)=X^{r}
so the equality follows from
(ii)
, while in the latter case we note that
(-1)^{r}f_{\alpha}(-\beta_{i})=\beta_{i}^{r}u^{-}_{\alpha}(\beta_{i})
and the equality follows from
(ii)
together with
(i)
.

On the other hand, if
d(\eta)>d
then
\operatorname{val}(\alpha)<\operatorname{val}(\beta_{i})
for each
i\in I
. This time we write
(-1)^{r}f_{\alpha}(-\beta_{i})=\operatorname{N}_{L/F}(\alpha)u^{+}_{\alpha}(\beta_{i})
so the equality follows from
(iii)
together with
(i)
.
∎

2.4.
Self-dual representations and determinants

We recall some results on self-duality of representations of
W_{F}
and determinants. We assume throughout that this subsection that
p\neq 2
. Firstly, we have the following result due to Moy.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
Moy84
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
(E/F,\chi)
be an admissible pair with
E\neq F
. Then the irreducible representation
\rho_{\chi}
is self-dual if and only if there is an intermediate field
E\supset L\supseteq F
such that
E/L
is quadratic and, writing
\sigma
for the non-trivial element of
\operatorname{Gal}(E/L)
, we have
{}^{\sigma}\chi=\chi^{-1}
. In that case,

(i)

\rho_{\chi}
is orthogonal if and only if
\chi|_{L^{\times}}
is trivial;

(ii)

\rho_{\chi}
is symplectic if and only if
\chi|_{L^{\times}}
is non-trivial.

We call an admissible pair
(E/F,\chi)
as in Proposition
2.11

self-dual

We know turn to determinants. For

For

Corollary 2.16
.

Suppose

3.
Classical Groups

In this section, we prove the results about classical groups explained in the introduction. We recall first the local Langlands correspondence for split classical groups and observe how the local converse theorem follows from it and the unicity of generic representations in a packet. We assume that
p
is odd and, for
N\geq 1
, write
G_{N}
for one of the groups
\operatorname{Sp}_{2N}(F),\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F),\operatorname{SO}_{2N+1}(F)
; in the case of
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
we further assume
N\geq 2
. We also set
G_{N}^{+}=\operatorname{O}_{2N}(F)
when
G_{N}=\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
, and
G_{N}^{+}=G_{N}
otherwise. We also fix a Whittaker datum
\xi
for
G_{N}
, so that
generic
will always mean relative to this fixed datum; if we wish to specify the datum then we will write
\xi
-generic
.

We write
N^{*}
for the dimension of the standard representation of the dual group
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
of
G_{N}
, so that

N^{*}=\begin{cases}2N&\text{ if }G_{N}=\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)\text{ or }\operatorname{SO}_{2N+1}(F),\\
2N+1&\text{ if }G_{N}=\operatorname{Sp}_{2N}(F),\end{cases}

and write
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}:{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{N^{*}}(\mathbb{C})
for the standard representation.

We say that parameters
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}:{WD}_{F}\to{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
are
outer equivalent
if
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{1}\cong\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{2}
. Note that this is, a priori, weaker than saying that they are locally conjugate (in the sense of the introduction); in fact, in the case of symplectic and odd orthogonal groups, it is the same as saying that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are conjugate, while for even orthogonal groups it is the same as saying they are conjugate in the full orthogonal group
\operatorname{O}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
.
We write
\Phi(G_{N})
for the set of (conjugacy classes of) Langlands parameters for
G_{N}
, and
\overline{\Phi}(G_{N})
for the set of outer equivalence classes.

Similarly, as in the introduction, we say that irreducible representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
of
G_{N}
are
outer conjugate
if there is an element
g\in G_{N}^{+}
such that
{}^{g}\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
, and we write
\overline{\operatorname{Irr}}(G_{N})
for the set outer conjugacy classes. Then, by the main local result of Arthur in
[
Art13
]
, we have a canonical surjective map on the set of outer conjugacy classes of tempered irreducible representations

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{Irr}}^{\operatorname{temp}}(G_{N})

\displaystyle\to\overline{\Phi}^{\operatorname{temp}}(G_{N})

\displaystyle[\pi]

\displaystyle\mapsto[\varphi_{\pi}]

with finite fibres, where we write
\varphi_{\pi}
for some representative of the class.

On the other hand, if
\pi
is a generic irreducible representation of
G_{N}
then Cogdell–Kim–Piatetski-Shapiro–Shahidi constructed its
transfer

\Pi
to
\operatorname{GL}_{N^{*}}(F)
(see
[
CKPSS04
, Proposition 7.2]
), which is a self-dual irreducible representation such that

\gamma(s,\Pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible supercuspidal representations
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
with
r\geq 1
. Moreover, by a result of Henniart (see
[
AHKO25
, Appendix B]
),
this transfer coincides with the Arthur transfer whenever
\pi
is supercuspidal generic: that is, the Langlands parameter of
\operatorname{GL}_{N^{*}}(F)
corresponding to
\Pi
is
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi}
(which is independent of the choice of representative
\varphi_{\pi}
). It also follows from this that the local Langlands correspondence for
G_{N}
preserves twisted
\gamma
-factors when
\pi
is supercuspidal generic:

\gamma(s,\pi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\varphi_{\pi}\times\tau,\psi),

where the
\gamma
-factor on the RHS is
\gamma(s,(\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi})\otimes\tau,\psi)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be generic supercuspidal representations of
G_{N}
, with transfers
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
to
\operatorname{GL}_{N^{*}}(F)
. If
\Pi_{1}\cong\Pi_{2}
then
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate.

Proof.

Since
\Pi_{1}\cong\Pi_{2}
, the local Langlands correspondence for
\operatorname{GL}_{N^{*}}(F)
implies that
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{1}}\cong\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{2}}
. Thus
\varphi_{\pi_{1}},\varphi_{\pi_{2}}
are outer equivalent. Now a result of Varma
[
Var17
, Corollary 6.16]

says that there is a unique outer conjugacy class of generic irreducible representations of
G_{N}
corresponding to
[\varphi_{\pi_{1}}]
, so
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate.
∎

Up to outer conjugation, the Local Converse Theorem for
G_{N}
is now straightforward for supercuspidal representations, and follows for all generic irreducible representations by standard techniques.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be generic irreducible representations of
G_{N}
. If

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\times\tau,\psi)

for all generic irreducible representations
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
, for
1\leq r\leq N
, then
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate.

Remark 3.3
.

(i)

We note that our version of the local converse theorem removes the assumption in Zhang
[
Zha18
]
and Jo
[
Jo25
, Theorem A]
for symplectic groups, and in Hazeltine–Liu
[
HL25
, Theorem 1.2]
(for even special orthogonal groups), that the central characters of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are equal.

(ii)

Another way of expressing Theorem
3.2
is that the map on irreducible
\xi
-generic representations

\overline{\operatorname{Irr}}^{\xi\operatorname{-gen}}\to\overline{\Phi}^{\operatorname{gen}}

induced by the local Langlands correspondence is
injective
, where a parameter
\varphi
is said to be generic if its adjoint
L
-function
L(s,\varphi,\operatorname{Ad})
is holomorphic at

(see
[
Mat24
, §2.2]
and
[
GI16
, Proposition B.1]
) It seems likely that this map is also surjective: compare with Jantzen–Liu
[
JL24
, Theorem 6.20]
, which proves the surjectivity of the map

\bigcup_{\xi}\overline{\operatorname{Irr}}^{\xi\operatorname{-gen}}\to\overline{\Phi}^{\operatorname{gen}},

where the union is taken over all (conjugacy classes of) Whittaker data.

Proof.

Firstly, let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be generic supercuspidal representations of
G_{N}
satisfying the hypotheses of the theorem and let
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
be their transfers to
\operatorname{GL}_{N^{*}}(F)
. Then, by the definition of transfer,
\gamma(s,\Pi_{1}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\Pi_{2}\times\tau,\psi)
, for all generic irreducible representations
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
with
1\leq r\leq N=\left\lfloor\frac{N^{*}}{2}\right\rfloor
, so the Local Converse Theorem for
\operatorname{GL}_{N^{*}}(F)
implies that
\Pi_{1}\cong\Pi_{2}
. The result now follows from Lemma
3.1
.

The reduction to the supercuspidal case is now done as in Jiang–Soudry for odd special orthogonal groups (see
[
JS03
, Theorem 5.1]
), Jo for symplectic groups (see
[
Jo25
, Proposition 2.10]
), and Hazeltine–Liu for even special orthogonal groups (see
[
HL25
, Theorem 4.3]
), noting that the proofs in the latter two cases only require equality of central characters because their versions of the converse theorem for supercuspidal representations also requires this.
∎

In the following sections, we will discuss the sharpness of Theorem
3.2
(i.e. whether the condition

can be improved) and the possibility of refining it to remove the outer conjugation (in the case of special orthogonal groups). For ease of exposition, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be irreducible generic representations of
G_{N}
or of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
and let
m\geq 1
be an integer. We say that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
m
if

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\times\tau,\psi)

for all irreducible supercuspidal representations
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
, with
1\leq r\leq m
. We use the same terminology for Langlands parameters also.

Note again that, by multiplicativity of
\gamma
-factors, if
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
m
then we in fact have
\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\times\tau,\psi)
for all
generic
irreducible representations
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
, with
1\leq r\leq m
. Thus the Local Converse Theorem
3.2
says that if generic irreducible representations of
G_{N}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level

then they are outer conjugate.

3.1.
Sharpness Results

Our first sharpness result concerns
non-supercuspidal
representations. For this, recall that we have a map

\iota_{\mathrm{L}}:\operatorname{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}_{N}(\mathbb{C})

by identifying
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
with a Siegel Levi subgroup
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{L}(\mathbb{C})
of
{}^{\vee}\mathrm{G}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Note that, although this embedding is only important up to conjugacy, there are two non-conjugate choices in the case of
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
; we fix one arbitrarily.

For
(E/F,\chi)
an admissible pair of degree
N
and
\rho_{\chi}=\operatorname{Ind}_{E/F}\chi
, we thus obtain a parameter
\varphi_{\chi}=\iota_{\mathrm{L}}\circ\rho_{\chi}
for

. Note that, when we compose this with the standard representation, we get

\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\chi}\cong\begin{cases}\rho_{\chi}\oplus\rho_{\chi^{-1}}&\text{ if }G_{N}=\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)\text{ or }\operatorname{SO}_{2N+1}(F),\\
\rho_{\chi}\oplus\boldsymbol{1}\oplus\rho_{\chi^{-1}}&\text{ if }G_{N}=\operatorname{Sp}_{2N}(F).\end{cases}

Note then that, if
(E^{\prime}/F,\chi^{\prime})
is another admissible pair of degree

such that
\varphi_{\chi},\varphi_{\chi^{\prime}}
define equivalent Langlands parameters for
G_{N}
, then either
\rho_{\chi^{\prime}}\cong\rho_{\chi}
or
\rho_{\chi^{\prime}}\cong\rho_{\chi^{-1}}
, so
(E^{\prime}/F,\chi^{\prime})
is conjugate to either
(E/F,\chi)
or
(E/F,\chi^{-1})
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose
p>N
. Let
(E/F,\chi),(E/F,\chi^{\prime})
be minimal totally ramified admissible pairs of degree
N
such that
\chi,\chi^{\prime}
coincide on

. Then
\varphi_{\chi},\varphi_{\chi^{\prime}}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
.

There are infinitely many minimal totally ramified admissible pairs of degree
N
with a fixed restriction to
U_{E}
(by choosing the value on a uniformizer), so also infinitely many up to equivalence and duality, even if we also require the image to be bounded and the induced representation not to be self-dual. Thus the local Langlands correspondence for
G_{N}
immediately gives us:

Corollary 3.6
.

Suppose
p>N
. Then there are generic irreducible tempered representations
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
of

, which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
but not outer conjugate.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

By definition, we have

\gamma(s,\varphi_{\chi}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,(\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\chi})\otimes\tau,\psi),

to which we seek to apply Proposition
2.10
. In the symplectic case, the component
\boldsymbol{1}
in
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\chi}
is the same for
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
so, by multiplicativity of
\gamma
-factors, in all cases we need only prove

(3.7)

\gamma(s,(\rho_{\chi}\oplus\rho_{\chi^{-1}})\otimes\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,(\rho_{\chi^{\prime}}\oplus\rho_{\chi^{\prime-1}})\otimes\tau,\psi).

Condition
(i)
of Proposition
2.10
is immediate from the hypotheses, while
(iii)
is also immediate as
\chi\chi^{-1}=\chi^{\prime}\chi^{\prime-1}
are trivial, so we only need to verify

. If
\chi,\chi^{\prime}
are represented by
\beta
then
\chi^{-1},\chi^{\prime-1}
are represented by

so the condition is

\chi(\beta)\chi^{-1}(-\beta)=\chi^{\prime}(\beta)\chi^{\prime-1}(-\beta).

This simply says
\chi(-1)=\chi^{\prime}(-1)
, which is true by hypothesis. Thus Proposition
2.10
says that (

) holds for all irreducible representations

of

of dimension less than

∎

Now we turn to the case of supercuspidal representations, for which we assume that

. We will define some Langlands parameters

.

Suppose

(E_{1}/F,\chi_{1})

(E_{2}/F,\chi_{2})
nor
(E_{2}/F,\chi_{2}^{\prime})

(i)

\varphi_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}
and

\gamma

(ii)

\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}\not\cong\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\chi^{\prime}_{1},\chi^{\prime}_{2}}
.

Notice that we can easily find examples such that
(E_{1}/F,\chi_{1})
is equivalent to neither
(E_{2}/F,\chi_{2})
nor
(E_{2}/F,\chi_{2}^{\prime})
, for example by requiring that
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
have different depths. Again, the local Langlands correspondence for
G_{N}
immediately gives us:

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose
p>N
and set
M=2\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor
. Then there are generic supercuspidal representations
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
of
G_{N}
, which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
M-1
but not outer conjugate.

Proof of Theorem
3.8
.

By multiplicativity, for
(i)
we need to prove

\gamma(s,(\rho_{\chi_{1}}\oplus\rho_{\chi_{2}})\otimes\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,(\rho_{\chi_{1}^{\prime}}\oplus\rho_{\chi^{\prime}_{2}})\otimes\tau,\psi).

But this follows exactly from Proposition
2.10
, since the conditions there are clearly satisfied.

For
(ii)
, we just need to verify that
\rho_{\chi_{1}}
is inequivalent to
\rho_{\chi^{\prime}_{1}}
(since it is inequivalent to
\rho_{\chi^{\prime}_{2}}
by hypothesis); that is, we need to check that the admissible pairs
(E_{1}/F,\chi_{1})
and
(E_{1}/F,\chi^{\prime}_{1})
are inequivalent. But the characters
\chi_{1},\chi^{\prime}_{1}
agree, and do not factor through any norm, on restriction to
U_{E_{1}}^{0+}
, so the only
F
-automorphism of
E_{1}
which conjugates this restriction is the identity. But
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
so we are done.
∎

3.2.
Self-dual supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(F)

When
N
is odd, Theorem
3.8
does not quite close the gap with Theorem
3.2
; that is, it may be that twisting only by representations of dimension up to
N-1

is sufficient
to determine the Langlands parameter of a supercuspidal representation. This is very closely related to the question of whether, for self-dual supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(F)
, twisting only by representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
with
r\leq N-1
is sufficient to distinguish them.

Let us explain. Write a supercuspidal Langlands parameter
\varphi
for
G_{N}
as a direct sum
\varphi_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\varphi_{n}
of irreducible, self-dual representations of
W_{F}
. If
N
is odd, then none of the
\varphi_{i}
can be
N
-dimensional, since there is no self-dual irreducible representation of
W_{F}
of odd dimension when
p\neq 2
. Thus, as long as
\varphi
is not irreducible, twisting by representations of dimensions
1,...,N-1
is all that is needed in order to distinguish a parameter via twisted gamma factors. In searching for a sharpness example to try to show that
N
is sharp, therefore, we were forced to consider irreducible parameters of
G_{N}
. We could not find such a sharpness example, at least when the extension
E/F
is totally ramified. Only on this basis, we felt that we could make the following conjecture:

Conjecture 3.10
.

Suppose
p>N
and suppose that
N
is odd. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be self-dual supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(F)
of the same parity which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
.
Then
\pi\cong\pi^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.11
.

We have no evidence as to whether the assumption on
p
or the assumption on
N
is needed.

The following theorem shows that Conjecture
3.10
is best possible when
N
is even. However, we have no evidence as to whether the bound can be improved if
N
is odd.

Theorem 3.12
.

Suppose
p>N
, set
M=2\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor
, and fix a parity. There exist inequivalent self-dual supercuspidal representations
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(F)
of this parity which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
M-2
.

Proof.

As in other results, by the local Langlands correspondence we translate to a question about self-dual irreducible representations of
W_{F}
. Let
L/F
be a totally ramified extension of degree
N
, and
E/L
a ramified quadratic extension with galois involution
\sigma
. We set

m=\begin{cases}N+1&\text{ if~$N$ is even},\\
\frac{3N+(-1)^{(N+1)/2}}{2}&\text{ if~$N$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Note that
m
is odd and
d=\frac{m}{2N}
has least denominator
2N
. Now an easy exercise in congruences shows that the least integer
r
such that
rm\equiv\pm 1\pmod{2N}
is
r=M-1
, so that Lemma
2.9
implies that
t_{\beta}(M-1)>\frac{1}{2N}
.

Now let
\beta\in E
be an element of valuation
-d
such that
\sigma(\beta)=-\beta
, and let
(E/F,\chi)
and
(E/F,\chi^{\prime})
be self-dual admissible characters of the required parity which are represented by
\beta
, coincide on
U_{E}^{(1/2N)+}
, and such that
\chi(\beta)=\chi^{\prime}(\beta)
; these exist by Lemma
2.12
, noting that since
\beta^{2}\in L^{\times}
we must have
\chi(\beta^{2})=\chi^{\prime}(\beta^{2})
and both choices of square root are permitted.
Then Proposition
2.10
gives the result.
∎

3.3.
A (conjectural) improved bound for classical groups when
N
is odd

When
N
is odd, Conjecture
3.10
implies an improved bound for the Local Converse Theorem for the classical group
G_{N}
, which would then mean that our sharpness result in Corollary
3.9
is optimal.

Theorem 3.13
.

Suppose
N
is odd and
p>N
. Suppose also that Conjecture
3.10
is true for
\operatorname{GL}_{2N}(F)
. Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be irreducible generic supercuspidal representations of
G_{N}
which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
.
Then
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate.

The proof of this relies on the following (unconditional) lemma.

Lemma 3.14
.

Suppose
N
is odd and
p>N
. Let
\pi_{1}
be an irreducible generic supercuspidal representation of
G_{N}
such that largest irreducible component of
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{1}}
has dimension strictly less than
2N
. Let
\pi_{2}
be an irreducible generic supercuspidal representation of
G_{N}
such that
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
.
Then
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be irreducible generic supercuspidal representations of
G_{N}
satisfying the hypotheses, so that

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\times\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible supercuspidal representations
\tau
of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}
with
r=1,\ldots,N-1
. We abbreviate
\varphi_{i}^{\operatorname{GL}}=\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{i}}
and let
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
be the transfers to
\operatorname{GL}_{N^{*}}(F)
. Then we can write

\varphi_{i}^{\operatorname{GL}}\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t_{i}}\varphi_{i}^{(j)}\otimes\left(\operatorname{st}_{a_{i}^{(j)}}\oplus\operatorname{st}_{a_{i}^{(j)}-2}\oplus\cdots\right),

where
\operatorname{st}_{a}
is the unique
a
-dimensional irreducible representation of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and the
\varphi_{i}^{(j)}
are distinct self-dual irreducible representations of the Weil group
W_{F}
. Then, writing
\varphi_{\tau}
for the Langlands parameter corresponding to
\tau
, we have

(3.15)

\gamma(s,\Pi_{i}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\varphi_{i}^{\operatorname{GL}}\otimes\varphi_{\tau},\psi)=\prod_{j=1}^{t_{i}}\prod_{k=\frac{1-a_{i}^{(j)}}{2}}^{\frac{a_{i}^{(j)}-1}{2}}\gamma(s+k,\varphi_{i}^{(j)}\otimes\varphi_{\tau},\psi).

By Lemma
3.1
, we need only show that
\varphi_{1}^{\operatorname{GL}}
is equivalent to
\varphi_{2}^{\operatorname{GL}}
. For contradiction, we suppose not. Then there are an irreducible self-dual representation
\varphi
of
W_{F}
and natural number
a
such that
\varphi\otimes\operatorname{st}_{a}
appears in
\varphi_{1}^{\operatorname{GL}}
but not
\varphi_{2}^{\operatorname{GL}}
, and we can choose the maximal such
a
.

Suppose first that there is such
\varphi
with
\dim(\varphi)\leq N^{*}/2
, so that
\dim(\varphi)\leq N
. As
N\geq 3
is odd while
\dim(\varphi)
is
1
or even, we see that in fact
\dim(\varphi)\leq N-1
. But then, writing
\pi_{\varphi}
for the corresponding (self-dual) irreducible representation a general linear group,
[
JNS15
, Proposition 2.9]
implies that
\gamma(s,\Pi_{1}\times\pi_{\varphi}^{\vee},\psi)
has a real pole at
s=\frac{a+1}{2}
, while
\gamma(s,\Pi_{2}\times\pi_{\varphi}^{\vee},\psi)
does not, a contradiction.

Thus the only such
\varphi
must have
\dim(\varphi)>N^{*}/2
. Then
\varphi\otimes\operatorname{st}_{1}
is in fact the
unique
component of
\varphi_{1}^{\operatorname{GL}}
which does not appear in
\varphi_{2}^{\operatorname{GL}}
(since every other such component would have dimension less than
N^{*}/2
). Moreover, there is also a unique component
\varphi^{\prime}\otimes\operatorname{st}_{1}
of
\varphi_{2}^{\operatorname{GL}}
which does not appear in
\varphi_{1}^{\operatorname{GL}}
(again, if there were two then at least one would have dimension less than
N^{*}/2
, which is impossible by the previous paragraph). But then, since all other terms in (
3.15
) are identical for
i=1,2
, we have that
\dim(\varphi)=\dim(\varphi^{\prime})<2N
(by hypothesis) and they are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
. Then Theorem
3.2
implies that
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
are equivalent, which is absurd.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.13
.

By Lemma
3.14
, we need only consider the case where
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{1}}
and
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{2}}
are irreducible of dimension
2N
or the sum of a quadratic character and an irreducible representation of dimension
2N
. As in the proof of Lemma
3.14
, in the latter case we see that the quadratic character is the same so, by multiplicativity of
\gamma
-factors, we reduce to the first case. But then these are the Langlands parameters of a pair of self-dual supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{2N}
of the same parity, so Conjecture
3.10
implies they are equivalent. Thus
\varphi_{\pi_{1}},\varphi_{\pi_{2}}
are outer equivalent, so
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate.
∎

In fact, we can prove this improved Local Converse Theorem in the case of even special orthogonal groups.

Theorem 3.16
.

Suppose
N
is odd and
p>N
. Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be irreducible generic supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
N-1
. Then
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are outer conjugate.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.14
, we need only consider the case where
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{\pi_{1}}
is irreducible. But, by Corollary
2.17
, there are no irreducible orthogonal representations of
W_{F}
of dimension
2N
and determinant
1
when
N
is odd, so there is nothing to prove.
∎

3.4.
Generic
{WD}_{F}
-unacceptability of
\operatorname{SO}_{6}

In the case of even special orthogonal groups, twisted standard
\gamma
-factors cannot distinguish between Langlands parameters
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
which are outer equivalent. One might hope that we can use different algebraic representations of the dual group to help distinguish them. The representation ring of
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
is generated by the fundamental representations
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{1},\cdots,\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N-1},\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{+},\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{-}
, where
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{r}
denotes the
r^{\text{th}}
exterior power of
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}
and
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{\pm}
are described in
[
YZ24
]
(see also below in the case
N=3
). We cannot hope to use the exterior powers to distinguish
\varphi
from
\varphi^{\prime}
since

\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i}\circ\varphi=\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i}\circ\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi

and
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi\cong\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi^{\prime}
. Thus it is only using the representations
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{+},\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{-}
that we can hope to distinguish them. Since
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{+}
is obtained by composing
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{-}
with the outer automorphism, we get that
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{+}\circ\varphi^{\prime}\cong\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{-}\circ\varphi
; thus we can use these to distinguish
\varphi
from
\varphi^{\prime}
precisely when
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{+}\circ\varphi\not\cong\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{-}\circ\varphi
.

In the case of
\operatorname{SO}_{4}(F)
it is shown in
[
YZ24
]
that this is always the case, so that twisted
\gamma
-factors can be used to distinguish outer equivalent parameters. However, this seems unlikely to be true in general as
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
is not acceptable for
N\geq 3
.
The following example shows that, for
\operatorname{SO}_{6}(F)
when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, there is indeed a parameter
\varphi
which is not conjugate to its outer twist such that
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{+}\circ\varphi\cong\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{N}_{-}\circ\varphi
. We note that in
[
Mat24
, Proposition 7.3]
, an example for
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
is given when
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
; one difference is that our example is a discrete parameter (indeed the representations in the corresponding
L
-packet are all supercuspidal) while that in
[
Mat24
]
is not.

Example 3.17
.

Let
L/F
be the biquadratic extension of
F
, with
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
the quadratic subfields and
E_{1}/F
unramified. Let
\zeta\in\mu^{\prime}_{E_{1}}
be a generator, so that
\zeta
has order
q^{2}-1
, and let
\varpi_{F}
be a fixed uniformizer of
F
. Define a complex character
\chi_{1}
of
E_{1}^{\times}
by

\chi_{1}(\varpi_{F}^{n}\zeta^{k}u_{1})=\mathrm{i}^{k},\quad\text{ for }u_{1}\in U_{E_{1}}^{0+},

where
\mathrm{i}
is a fixed complex square root of
-1
. Since
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, this is an orthogonal admissible character of order
4
. We pick orthogonal admissible ramified characters
\chi_{2},\chi_{3}
, of
E_{2},E_{3}
respectively, of distinct positive depths. Define
\varphi
up to outer equivalence by

\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi=\textstyle\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{3}\rho_{\chi_{i}}.

This does define a parameter into
\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
since
\det\rho_{\chi_{i}}=\omega_{E_{i}/F}
, the local class field character, and the product of the three class field characters is trivial. Then we claim that
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{+}\circ\varphi\cong\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{-}\circ\varphi
, and that
\varphi
is not equivalent to its own outer conjugate.

Proof.

We first explain why our parameter
\varphi
is not equivalent to its own outer conjugate. Suppose
g\in\operatorname{O}(6,\mathbb{C})
is such that there exists
h\in\operatorname{SO}(6,\mathbb{C})
such that
{}^{g}\varphi={}^{h}\varphi
. Then
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}(h^{-1}g)
centralizes
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi=\rho_{\chi_{1}}\oplus\rho_{\chi_{2}}\oplus\rho_{\chi_{3}}
so, by Schur’s Lemma,
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}(h^{-1}g)
lies in
\{(\pm\mathrm{I}_{2},\pm\mathrm{I}_{2},\pm\mathrm{I}_{2})\}
, where
\mathrm{I}_{2}
is the
2
-by-
2
identity matrix. But the determinant of such a matrix is equal to one, and thus
g
also lies in
\operatorname{SO}(6,\mathbb{C})
. In particular, we conclude that
\varphi
is not equivalent to its own outer conjugate.

We now compute
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}\circ\varphi
, which is the sum of the two
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{\pm}\circ\varphi
. Note that we are really computing
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}\circ\varphi
, but we will omit the
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}
to simplify notation. We abbreviate
\rho_{i}=\rho_{\chi_{i}}
and
\omega_{i}=\omega_{E_{i}/F}
. Since
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}\rho_{i}=\det\rho_{i}=\omega_{i}
, we have

\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}\circ\varphi\cong\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\rho_{3}\oplus\textstyle\bigoplus\limits_{i\neq j}\rho_{i}\otimes\omega_{j}.

Moreover, applying Lemma
2.6
twice and using that the
\chi_{i}
are self-dual, we have

\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\rho_{3}\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{L/F}\left(\textstyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}\chi_{i,L}\right)\oplus\operatorname{Ind}_{L/F}\left(\textstyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}\chi^{-1}_{i,L}\right),

where we recall that
\chi_{i,L}=\chi_{i}\circ\operatorname{N}_{L/E_{i}}
. Moreover, in our situation, these two
4
-dimensional representations are irreducible and isomorphic. Indeed,
\chi_{1,L}
is a quadratic character because
\operatorname{N}_{L/E_{1}}(L^{\times})=\varpi_{F}^{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\zeta^{2}\rangle U_{E_{1}}^{0+}
, while the characters
\chi_{2,L}
,
\chi_{3,L}
and
\chi_{2,L}\chi_{3,L}
are all non-quadratic (since they have positive depth), so each
4
-dimensional piece restricts to
W_{L}
to a sum of distinct characters and is thus irreducible; but both restrict to the sum of the same four characters, so they are isomorphic.

Moreover, for
i\neq j
, we have
\omega_{j}\circ\operatorname{N}_{E_{i}/F}=\omega_{L/E_{i}}
so

\rho_{i}\otimes\omega_{j}\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{E_{i}/F}(\chi_{i}\omega_{L/E_{i}}),

which is independent of
j
. Thus we have a decomposition

\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}\circ\varphi\cong\left(\operatorname{Ind}_{L/F}\left(\textstyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}\chi_{i,L}\right)\oplus\operatorname{Ind}_{L/F}\textstyle\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{3}\operatorname{Ind}_{E_{i}/F}(\chi_{i}\omega_{L/E_{i}})\right)^{\oplus 2},

in which each representation appearing is irreducible and is determined by the characters appearing in its restriction to
W_{L}
. Thus, to verify that
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{+}\circ\varphi\cong\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{-}\circ\varphi
, we need only check that they have the same restrictions to
W_{L}
.

We now use the general description of
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{n}_{\pm}
from
[
YZ24
]
to make these explicit. We write
(W,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
for the
6
-dimensional complex orthogonal space, with Witt basis
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{-3},e_{-2},e_{-1}
, so that

\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if~$j=-i$},\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
is (identified with) the group of automorphisms of
W
which preserve the symmetric form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and have determinant
1
. We define an involution
\tau
on
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}W
as follows: for a simple tensor
x=e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{k}
, we let
\tau(x)
be the unique simple tensor
y
such that

e_{-i}\wedge e_{-j}\wedge e_{-k}\wedge y=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\wedge e_{3}\wedge e_{-3}\wedge e_{-2}\wedge e_{-1}.

Then
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{\pm}(W)
is the
(\pm 1)
-eigenspace of
\tau
on
W
and we can write down bases for them: for
(i,j,k)
any cyclic permutation of
(1,2,3)
, we write

\displaystyle u_{i}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=e_{\pm i}\wedge e_{\mp j}\wedge e_{\mp k},

\displaystyle v_{i}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{-j}\pm e_{i}\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{-k},

\displaystyle v_{-i}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=e_{-i}\wedge e_{j}\wedge e_{-j}\mp e_{-i}\wedge e_{k}\wedge e_{-k};

then
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{\pm}(W)
has basis

\left\{e_{\pm 1}\wedge e_{\pm 2}\wedge e_{\pm 3},u_{i}^{\pm},v_{i}^{\pm},v_{-i}^{\pm}:i=1,2,3\right\}.

Now we realise our parameter
\varphi
with
W_{E_{i}}
acting on
e_{i}
via the character
\chi_{i}
(so on
e_{-i}
via the character
\chi_{i}^{-1}
), and check the action of
W_{L}
: since
\chi_{1,L}=\chi_{1,L}^{-1}
, the characters appearing in
\operatorname{Res}_{W_{L}}^{W_{F}}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{+}\circ\varphi
are the same as those in
\operatorname{Res}_{W_{L}}^{W_{F}}\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{-}\circ\varphi
, and we are done.

Thus, we have produced a parameter
\varphi
for
\operatorname{SO}_{6}(F)
that is not isomorphic to its own outer conjugate, and which satisfies
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{+}\circ\varphi=\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}_{-}\circ\varphi
, completing the proof.
∎

It is now not hard to use this example to deduce the following:

Corollary 3.18
.

The group
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
is generically
{WD}_{F}
-unacceptable.

We thank Nadir Matringe for pointing out how this should be possible, using the ideas in Yu
[
Yu22
]
. We also note that Matringe proved this (in a slightly different way) when
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
in
[
Mat24
, Proposition 7.3]
.

Proof.

By
[
Mat24
, Proposition 7.3]
, we need only treat the case
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
so we let
\varphi:W_{F}\to\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
be the Langlands parameter constructed in Example
3.17
. Then there is a finite Galois extension
K/F
such that
\varphi
is trivial on
W_{K}
, so
\varphi
factors through the finite group
\Gamma:=\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)
. Now let
L/F
be an unramified extension of degree
M>2N
such that
M
is prime to the degree of
K/F
, and set
E=KL
. Then, since the degrees of the extensions are coprime, we have

\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\times\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\simeq\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}.

Thus
\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}
is isomorphic to the quotient
W_{F}/W_{E}
of
W_{F}
and we can now apply the method in
[
Yu22
, Lemma 2.3]
to construct a parameter with image
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
as follows. We pick a primitive
M^{\text{th}}
root of unity
\zeta
in
\mathbb{C}
and define
\varphi^{\prime}
to be the inflation to
W_{F}
of the map

\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}\to\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C}),\quad(\gamma,a)\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{a},\ldots,\zeta^{(N-3)a},\varphi(\gamma),\zeta^{(3-N)a},\ldots,\zeta^{-a}),

for
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}
.

Now take
\varphi_{1}=\varphi
and let
\varphi_{2}
be its outer conjugate, from which we construct morphisms
\varphi_{1}^{\prime},\varphi_{2}^{\prime}
(with the same choice
\zeta
).Then
\varphi’_{1},\varphi’_{2}
are clearly also outer equivalent. If they were conjugate by some
g\in\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})
then we would have

\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(\gamma,a)=\operatorname{Ad}(g)\circ\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(\gamma,a),\quad\text{for all }\gamma\in\Gamma,a\in\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}.

By considering the element
(\gamma,a)=(1,1)
, we see that
g
is in the centralizer

Z_{\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(\mathbb{C})}(\operatorname{diag}(\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{N-3},\operatorname{Id}_{6},\zeta^{3-N},\ldots,\zeta^{-1})\simeq\operatorname{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C}).

But then taking the elements
(\gamma,0)
, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we would get that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are conjugate in
\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

As remarked above, one difference between our example for
\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
(when
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
) and the example of Matringe (when
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
) is that ours is a discrete parameter whilst his is not. The parameter constructed in Corollary
3.18
is also not discrete.

However, the only thing we have really used in our
\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{C})
example is the fact that the character
\chi_{1,L}=\chi_{1}\circ\operatorname{N}_{L/E_{1}}
is quadratic. By a similar (but notationally more complicated!) method, one can find a parameter
\varphi
for
\operatorname{SO}_{2(2N+1)}(F)
such that
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2N+1}_{+}\circ\varphi\cong\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2N+1}_{-}\circ\varphi
, so that one also sees the non-acceptability of
\operatorname{SO}_{2(2N+1)}(\mathbb{C})
manifested by a Langlands parameter. One need only add to
\rho_{\chi_{1}}
a sum of (pairwise inequivalent) irreducible
2
-dimensional orthogonal representations of distinct positive depths induced from ramified quadratic extensions (necessarily an odd number from each of the two quadratic ramified extensions, so that the parameter has determinant
1
).

Thus it is possible to find non-conjugate discrete parameters which are outer-equivalent for
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
whenever
N
is odd and
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. We do not give details here, nor investigate the situation for general
\operatorname{SO}_{2N}(F)
further, leaving this to future work, or others.

4.
The exceptional group
\mathrm{G}_{2}

Now we turn to the case of the exceptional group
\mathrm{G}_{2}
. Since there is not yet a direct definition of twisted
\gamma
-factors on the automorphic side, we will follow Gan–Savin
[
GS23b
]
, where they
define
these
\gamma
-factors to be those of the corresponding Langlands parameter, via their local Langlands correspondence (see
[
GS23a
]
).

4.1.
The converse theorem for parameters for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)

We consider first a Langlands parameter
\varphi:{WD}_{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
for
G_{2}
and the standard embedding
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}:\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{7}(\mathbb{C})
. Then, for
\tau:W_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
an irreducible representation, by definition we have

\gamma(s,\varphi\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,(\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi)\otimes\tau,\psi).

As for classical groups, we make the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be Langlands parameters for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
and let
m\geq 1
be an integer. We say that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
m
if

\gamma(s,\varphi_{1}\times\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,\varphi_{2}\times\tau,\psi)

for all irreducible irreducible
r
-dimensional representations
\tau
of
W_{F}
, with
1\leq r\leq m
.

The following theorem seems to be implicit in
[
GS23b
]
, and see also
[
Mat24
, Theorem 8.2(b)]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
be two Langlands parameters for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
. If
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
3
, then
\varphi_{1}\cong\varphi_{2}
.

We will see in the next subsection how our results for classical groups can be used to show that this is the best possible, at least when
p>3
.

Proof.

By
[
Mat24
, Theorem 8.2(b)]
, the hypotheses imply that
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{1},\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{2}
are conjugate under the normalizer in
\operatorname{GL}_{7}(\mathbb{C})
of
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}(\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
; since this normalizer is
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}(\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
(see the proof in
loc. cit.
), we deduce that they are in fact conjugate under
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}(\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
, so that the parameters
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-conjugate, as required.
∎

4.2.
On the optimal bound for parameters for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)

For the points
\mathrm{G}(F)
of a reductive group over
F
, we write
\Phi_{\mathrm{ds}}(\mathrm{G})
for the set of discrete Langlands parameters for
\mathrm{G}(F)
. By
[
GS23a
, Lemma 2.4(i)]
, the inclusion map
\iota:\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
induces a map

(4.3)

\Phi_{\mathrm{ds}}(\operatorname{PGL}_{3})\to\Phi_{\mathrm{ds}}(\mathrm{G}_{2})

whose fibres consist of orbits under the outer automorphism of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular, for
\rho:W_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})
a parameter for
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(F)
, we have

\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\iota\circ\rho=\rho\oplus\boldsymbol{1}\oplus\rho^{\vee}.

We also recall that an admissible pair
(L/F,\chi)
gives rise to a parameter for
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(F)
if and only if the induced representation
\rho_{\chi}=\operatorname{Ind}_{L/F}\chi
has determinant
1
; equivalently,
\chi|_{F^{\times}}=\varkappa_{L/F}
, where
\varkappa_{L/F}=\det(\operatorname{Ind}_{L/F}\boldsymbol{1}_{L^{\times}}).

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
p>3
. There exist inequivalent discrete Langlands parameters for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
2
.

Proof.

We make use of our result on symplectic groups in Theorem
3.5
. More precisely, suppose we have found minimal totally ramified admissible pairs
(E/F,\chi_{1})
and
(E/F,\chi_{2})
, with
E/F
a cubic extension, such that

(i)

\chi_{i}|_{F^{\times}}=\varkappa_{E/F}
;

(ii)

\chi_{1}|_{U_{E}}=\chi_{2}|_{U_{E}}
; and

(iii)

(E/F,\chi_{1})
is not equivalent to
(E/F,\chi_{2}^{\pm 1})
.

Then, writing
\rho_{i}=\operatorname{Ind}_{E/F}\chi_{i}
, we get a parameter
\varphi_{i}=\iota\circ\rho_{i}
for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
and a parameter
\phi_{i}=\iota_{L}\circ\rho_{i}
for
\operatorname{Sp}_{6}(F)
(in the notation of §
3.1
) such that
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{i}\simeq\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\phi_{i}
(where we are abusing notation by using
\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}
denote the standard representation of both
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
\operatorname{SO}_{7}(\mathbb{C})
). Then Theorem
3.5
implies that
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
2
, so the same is true of
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
. Moreover, the final condition
(iii)
ensures that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are inequivalent as parameters for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
.

Now we explain how to find admissible pairs satisfying these conditions. We take
E/F
to be totally ramified of degree
3
, with uniformizer
\varpi_{E}
such that
\varpi_{E}^{3}=\varpi_{F}
lies in
F
. Since
p>3
, this is a tamely ramified extension and, by Lemma
2.15
, the character
\varkappa_{E/F}
is unramified of order dividing
2
. We choose any non-trivial character of
U_{E}^{0+}
which is trivial on
U_{E}^{(1/3)+}
(for example, represented by
\varpi_{E}^{-1}
); since
U_{F}^{1}\subset U_{E}^{(1/3)+}
, this is trivial on
U_{F}^{1}
so we can extend it to a character
\chi
of
F^{\times}U_{E}^{0+}
by requiring
\chi|_{F^{\times}}=\varkappa_{E/F}
.

Finally, we extend
\chi
to two distinct characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
of
E^{\times}
. Any such extension must send
\varpi_{E}
to a cube root of
\varkappa_{E/F}(\varpi_{F})=\pm 1
. So we set
\chi_{1}(\varpi_{E})=\varkappa_{E/F}(\varpi_{F})
and
\chi_{2}(\varpi_{E})=\zeta\varkappa_{E/F}(\varpi_{F})
, where
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a primitive cube root of unity.

It remains only to check the final condition
(iii)
. But if
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)
then
\sigma(\varpi_{E})
differs from
\varpi_{E}
by a cube root of unity in
U_{F}
, on which
\varkappa_{E/F}
is trivial; hence we deduce
{}^{\sigma}\chi_{1}(\varpi_{E})=\chi_{1}(\varpi_{E})\neq\chi_{2}(\varpi_{E})^{\pm 1}
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The representations
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
in the proof of Theorem
4.4
have the further property that
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{1}^{\vee}\simeq\rho_{2}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\vee}
. Indeed, if
E/F
is Galois, with automorphism group generated by
\sigma
, then

\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{i}^{\vee}\simeq{\textstyle\bigoplus\limits_{j=0}^{2}}\operatorname{Ind}_{E/F}{}^{\sigma^{j}}\chi_{i}\chi_{i}^{-1};

but, for fixed
j
, the characters
{}^{\sigma^{j}}\chi_{i}\chi_{i}^{-1}
certainly coincide on
U_{E}
by construction, while we have seen that
{}^{\sigma}\chi_{i}(\varpi_{E})=\chi_{i}(\varpi_{E})
so that
{}^{\sigma^{j}}\chi_{i}\chi_{i}^{-1}
are both trivial on
\varpi_{E}
. On the other hand, if
E/F
is not Galois then, writing
L/F
for the unramified quadratic extension and
EL
for the compositum of
E
and
L
, we have

\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{i}^{\vee}\simeq\operatorname{Ind}_{E/F}\boldsymbol{1}\oplus\operatorname{Ind}_{EL/F}\left({}^{\sigma}\chi_{i,EL}\chi_{i,EL}^{-1}\right),

where
\chi_{i,EL}=\chi_{i}\circ\operatorname{N}_{EL/E}
and
{}^{\sigma}\chi_{i,EL}={}^{\sigma}\chi_{i}\circ\operatorname{N}_{EL/\sigma(E)}
. Again, the characters
{}^{\sigma}\chi_{i,EL}\chi_{i,EL}^{-1}
coincide on
U_{EL}
by construction, and it is easy to check they are trivial on the uniformizer
\varpi_{E}
of
EL
.

Now

\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}\circ\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{i}\simeq\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}\left(\rho_{i}\oplus\boldsymbol{1}\oplus\rho_{i}^{\vee}\right)\simeq\rho_{i}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\left(\rho_{i}\otimes\rho_{i}^{\vee}\right)\oplus(\rho_{i}^{\vee})^{\oplus 2},

where we have used that
\wedge^{2}\rho_{i}\simeq\rho_{i}^{\vee}
, since
\rho_{i}
is
3
-dimensional and has trivial determinant. In particular, from Theorem
4.4
and the isomorphism
\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{1}^{\vee}\simeq\rho_{2}\otimes\rho_{2}^{\vee}
, we deduce that

\gamma(s,(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}\circ\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{1})\otimes\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}\circ\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\varphi_{2})\otimes\tau,\psi),

for all irreducible
r
-dimensional representations of
W_{F}
with
1\leq r\leq 2
. Moreover, since
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}\circ\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\simeq\varrho_{\mathrm{std}}\oplus\operatorname{Ad}
is the sum of the two fundamental representations of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, multiplicativity of
\gamma
-factors implies that

\gamma(s,(\varrho\circ\varphi_{1})\otimes\tau,\psi)=\gamma(s,(\varrho\circ\varphi_{2})\otimes\tau,\psi),

for all fundamental representations
\varrho
of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and all irreducible
r
-dimensional representations of
W_{F}
with
1\leq r\leq 2
.

Thus the necessity of twisting by
3
-dimensional representations of
W_{F}
to distinguish parameters is not an artefact of only considering the standard representation of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

4.3.
The optimal local converse theorem for
\mathrm{G}_{2}

Finally, we interpret the results of the previous sections in terms of irreducible representations of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
. We have, from
[
GS23a
]
(see also
[
HKT23
]
and
[
AX23
]
) a local Langlands correspondence for
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
; for
\pi
an irreducible representation of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
, we write
\varphi_{\pi}:{WD}_{F}\to\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
for the corresponding Langlands parameter, and by
\Pi_{\pi}
the
L
-packet of
\pi
, which contains a unique generic representation. For
\tau
an irreducible representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{m}(F)
with Langlands parameter
\varphi_{\tau}
, as in
[
GS23b
]
we
define

\gamma(s,\pi\rtimes\tau,\varrho,\psi):=\gamma(s,(\varrho\circ\varphi_{\pi})\otimes\varphi_{\tau},\psi),

for
\varrho
any algebraic representation of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

Writing
\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}_{\mathrm{sc}}(\mathrm{G}(F))
for the set of equivalence classes of irreducible generic supercuspidal representations of the points
\mathrm{G}(F)
of a reductive group, the map in (
4.3
) corresponds to a map

\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}_{\mathrm{sc}}(\operatorname{PGL}_{3})\to\operatorname{Irr}^{\operatorname{gen}}_{\mathrm{sc}}(\mathrm{G}_{2}),

which is given by an exceptional theta correspondence, and is in fact part of how the Langlands correspondence is defined (see also
[
SW11
, Theorem 1.8]
). Since the parameters used in the construction in the proof of Theorem
4.4
are indeed for supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(F)
, the previous results of this section (also using Remark
4.5
) translate to give:

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose
p>3
.

(i)

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
be irreducible generic representations of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
which are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
3
. Then
\pi_{1}\simeq\pi_{2}
.

(ii)

There are inequivalent generic supercuspidal representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(F)
such that

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\rtimes\tau,\varrho,\psi):=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\rtimes\tau,\varrho,\psi),

for
\varrho
either fundamental representation of
\mathrm{G}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and all supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{r}(F)
, with
1\leq r\leq 2
; in particular, they are
\gamma
-equivalent to level
2
.

5.
Ramakrishnan conjecture

In this final section, we show that we also cannot use only
\gamma
-functions of character twists to determine a supercuspidal representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
. We recall that, in
[
YZ22
, Conjecture 1.2]
, it is conjectured that if
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are unitarizable supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
such that

\gamma(s,\pi_{1}\rtimes\eta,\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i},\psi)=\gamma(s,\pi_{2}\rtimes\eta,\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i},\psi),

for
i=1,\ldots,\left\lfloor\tfrac{N}{2}\right\rfloor
and
\eta
any character of
F^{\times}
, then
\pi_{1}\cong\pi_{2}
. This is of course true for
N=2,3
since it is then simply the Local Converse Theorem for
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
. On the other hand, for
N\geq 6
the requisite
\gamma
-functions have not yet been defined on the automorphic side of the Langlands correspondence so we interpret these
\gamma
-factors on the Galois side as for
\mathrm{G}_{2}
and as in the introduction.

We show here that this conjecture is false already for
\operatorname{GL}_{4}(F)
, when
p
is odd. Indeed, we give examples of inequivalent irreducible
4
-dimensional representations
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
of
W_{F}
, corresponding to irreducible unitarizable supercuspidal representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{4}(F)
, such that

(5.1)

\gamma(s,(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i}\circ\varphi_{1})\otimes\eta,\psi)=\gamma(s,(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i}\circ\varphi_{2})\otimes\eta,\psi),

for
i=1,\ldots,4
and all characters
\eta
of
W_{F}
.

First, we remark that if a supercuspidal representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{N}(F)
has unitary central character, then it is unitarizable (see
[
KT
, p.4]
). We also remark that if
(E/F,\chi)
is an admissible pair, then its corresponding supercuspidal representation
\pi_{\chi}
via LLC has unitary central character if and only if
\chi|_{F^{\times}}
is unitary. Note that
\chi|_{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}
is automatically unitary since
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
is compact.

So suppose
p
is odd and let
(E/F,\chi)
be any totally ramified unitary admissible pair of degree
4
whose depth
d
has least denominator
2
. For example, we could choose
\beta
to be the quasi-minimal element
\varpi_{E}^{-10}+\varpi_{E}^{-7}
, where
\varpi_{E}
is a uniformizer of
E
, and take
\chi
to be any unitary quasi-character of
E^{\times}
represented by
\beta
. Then we take
(E/F,\chi^{\prime})
to be the admissible pair such that
\chi^{-1}\chi^{\prime}
is unramified of order
2
. Then
\chi,\chi^{\prime}
coincide on
F^{\times}
, and also on
\beta
since
4\operatorname{val}(\beta)
is an even integer.

Now note that

\rho_{\chi}\cong\rho_{\chi^{\prime}}\otimes\eta_{8},

where
\eta_{n}
denotes an unramified character of
W_{F}
of order
n
(the choice is unimportant), since
\eta_{8}\circ\operatorname{N}_{E/F}
is the unramified quadratic character of
E^{\times}
. In particular, since also
\rho_{\chi}\cong\rho_{\chi}\otimes\eta_{4}
, we have
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}\circ\rho_{\chi}\cong\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}\circ\rho_{\chi^{\prime}}
, and similarly,
\omega_{\chi}=\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{4}\circ\rho_{\chi}=\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{4}\circ\rho_{\chi}=\omega_{\chi^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, the pairing
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}\circ\rho_{\chi}\times\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{1}\circ\rho_{\chi}\to\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{4}\circ\rho_{\chi}
gives us
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{3}\circ\rho_{\chi}\cong\rho_{\chi}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{\chi}
.

Thus, putting
\varphi_{1}=\rho_{\chi}
and
\varphi_{2}=\rho_{\chi^{\prime}}
, to prove (
5.1
) we are reduced to verifying

\gamma(s,\rho_{\chi}\otimes\eta,\psi)=\gamma(s,\rho_{\chi^{\prime}}\otimes\eta,\psi)\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma(s,\rho_{\chi^{-1}}\otimes\eta,\psi)=\gamma(s,\rho_{\chi^{\prime-1}}\otimes\eta,\psi),

for all characters
\eta
of
W_{F}
. But these follow immediately from Proposition
2.10
.

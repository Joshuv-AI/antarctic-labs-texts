Introduction

Throughout this article
R
is a commutative Noetherian ring with identity. The theory of primary decompositions is foundational to commutative algebra and neighboring disciplines. Emmy Noether’s unifying result is every ideal
I\subseteq R
admits a (minimal) primary decomposition. Unlike the unique factorization of elements in unique factorization domains, primary decompositions of an ideal are not unique at embedded components, only at minimal components. If
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq R
is a prime ideal and

, then the
n
th symbolic power
\mathfrak{p}^{(n)}:=\mathfrak{p}^{n}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R
is the minimal component of
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
appearing in every choice of a primary decomposition of
\mathfrak{p}^{n}
. The containment problem for the prime
\mathfrak{p}
is the problem of establishing ideal containments
\mathfrak{p}^{(t)}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{s}
, i.e., understanding when the unique minimal component of
\mathfrak{p}^{t}
is contained in all possible choices of primary components of
\mathfrak{p}^{s}

The containment problem partially originates from Hartshorne’s investigations into a non-local analogue of local duality. In
[
18

More generally, for any ideal

,

Recall that if

The following proposition is independent of the proofs of the main theorems, but illustrates interesting behavior of splitting ideals in Gorenstein rings. Recall that if

is an

-finite non-singular domain, then Kunz’s Theorem implies for all

being a free

for all

.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

be an

-finite Gorenstein ring of prime characteristic

.

Proof.

The claimed equality can be checked locally by Lemma

(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local
F
-finite Gorenstein ring. A standard application of
[

is if

is a finitely generated Cohen-Macaulay

and then applying the functor
\Hom_{R}(-,R)
to the resulting short exact sequence. The module

\eta\in M\setminus(\underline{x})M

2.4.
Connections with Test Ideals

Splitting ideals are the foundations of prime characteristic test ideal theory. Let
R
be an
F
-finite domain of prime characteristic
p>0
and Krull dimension
d
. Given an ideal
I\subseteq R
, define

I_{[1/p^{e}]}(I):=\bigcap_{\left\{\mathfrak{b}\subseteq R\ \middle|\ I\subseteq I_{e}(\mathfrak{b})\right\}}\mathfrak{b}.

An implication of Lemma
2.6
(
h
) is that
I\subseteq I_{e}(I_{[1/p^{e}]}(I))
. Therefore
I_{[1/p^{e}]}(I)
enjoys the following alternative description.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
R
be an
F
-finite domain of prime characteristic
p>0
and Krull dimension
d
. If
I\subseteq R
is an ideal and
e\in\mathbb{N}
, then
I_{[1/p^{e}]}(I)
is the unique smallest ideal
\mathfrak{b}
satisfying
I\subseteq I_{e}(\mathfrak{b})
.

If
R
is non-singular and
\mathfrak{b}
is any ideal, then
I_{e}(\mathfrak{b})=\mathfrak{b}^{[p^{e}]}
. Hence, in the non-singular case,
I_{[1/p^{e}]}(I)
is the unique smallest ideal
\mathfrak{b}
such that
I\subseteq\mathfrak{b}^{[p^{e}]}
. It is common to write
I_{[1/p^{e}]}(I)
as
I^{[1/p^{e}]}
when
R
is non-singular, see
[
4
, Definition 2.2]
.

Given an ideal
I\subseteq R
and a real number
t>0
, the
test ideal
of the pair
(R,I^{t})
, denoted
\tau(R,I^{t})
, is benefited by several characterizations. Notably,
\tau(R,I^{t})
is realized as the smallest non-zero ideal
J\subseteq R
such that for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi\in\Hom_{R}(F^{e}_{*}R,R)
,

\varphi(F^{e}_{*}I^{\lceil t(p^{e}-1)\rceil}J)\subseteq J,

see
[
49
, Theorem 6.3]
. Equivalently,
\tau(R,I^{t})
is the smallest non-zero ideal of
R
so that for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,

I_{[1/p^{e}]}\left(I^{\lceil t(p^{e}-1)\rceil}J\right)\subseteq J.

When
R
is non-singular of dimension
d
, then test ideal theory provides proof that for all ideals
I\subseteq R
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
I^{(dn)}\subseteq I^{n}
. If
I\subseteq R
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
then there are ideal containments:

I^{(dn)}\subseteq\tau(R,I^{(dn)})\subseteq\tau(R,(I^{(dn)})^{1/n})^{n}\subseteq I^{n}.

The first containment holds under milder conditions, such as when
R
is strongly
F
-regular. The third containment is an application of the Briançon-Skoda Theorem. The second containment requires the ring to be regular and follows from the subadditivity theorem, see
[
15
, Theorem 4.5]
. Subadditivity of multiplier/test ideals is not generally true in singular rings, see
[
56
,
26
]
.

2.6.

Proofs of the main theorems benefit from the existence of elements that uniformly multiply symbolic powers of ideals into a linearly bounded difference of ordinary powers of ideals. Such elements are referred to as

4.
Uniform Linear Equivalence of Topologies

The following lemma contains a method of identifying a constant
C
that can be used to linearly compare the symbolic powers with ordinary powers of an ideal
I
belonging to an
F
-finite domain
R
. The method aligns with a technique used in Hochster and Huneke’s proof that a non-singular ring of prime characteristic enjoys the Uniform Symbolic Topology Property, cf.
[
22
, Last paragraph of introduction]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be an
F
-finite domain of Krull dimension
d
and
I\subseteq R
.

(a)

If there exist constants
t,e_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every
e\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left(I^{\left([p^{e+e_{0}}]\right)}\right)^{t}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]},

then there exists a constant
C
, depending only on
d,t,e_{0}
, a uniform Briançon-Skoda bound of
R
, and a uniform symbolic multiplier constant of a uniform symbolic multiplier of
R
, so that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
I^{(Cn)}\subseteq I^{n}
.

(b)

If
R
is
F
-pure and if there exist constants
t,e_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every
e\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left(I_{e+e_{0}}(I)\right)^{t}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]},

then there exists a constant
e^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, depending only on
e_{0}
and
t
, so that for every
e\in\mathbb{N}
,

I_{e+e^{\prime}}(I)\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}.

Proof.

For (
a
), let
z\in R
be a uniform symbolic multiplier of
R
with uniform symbolic multiplier constant
C_{0}
, see Definition
2.11
and Lemma
2.13
. Let
C_{1}
be as in Lemma
3.3
so that for every ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq R
and
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathfrak{a}^{(C_{1}p^{e})}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{\left([p^{e}]\right)}
. The constant
C_{1}
only depends on
d
and a Uniform Briançon-Skoda bound of
R
. Let
f\in I^{(C_{0}tC_{1}p^{e_{0}}n)}
. For each
e\in\mathbb{N}
, write
p^{e}=a_{e}n+r_{e}
with
0\leq r_{e}<n
. Fix a nonzero element
c\in I^{(C_{0}tC_{1}p^{e_{0}}n)}
. Then
cf^{a_{e}}\in I^{(C_{0}tC_{1}p^{e+e_{0}})}
and

z^{t}cf^{a_{e}}\in\left(I^{(C_{1}p^{e+e_{0}})}\right)^{t}\subseteq\left(I^{\left([p^{e+e_{0}}]\right)}\right)^{t}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}.

Raising the containment to the
n
and multiplying by
f^{r_{e}}
implies that for all
e\in\mathbb{N}
,

z^{tn}c^{n}f^{p^{e}}\in\left(I^{n}\right)^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq(I^{n})^{p^{e}}.

The constants
t
and
n
are independent of
e
. Therefore
f\in\overline{I^{n}}
by a theorem of Rees, see
[
53
, Corollary 6.8.12]
. Hence for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
I^{(C_{0}tC_{1}p^{e_{0}}n)}\subseteq\overline{I^{n}}
. If
B
is a Uniform Briançon-Skoda Bound of
R
, then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

I^{(C_{0}tC_{1}p^{e_{0}}(B+1)n)}\subseteq\overline{I^{(B+1)n}}\subseteq I^{n}.

For (
b
), choose constant
e_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
so that
p^{e_{1}}\geq t
. If
f\in I_{e+e_{0}}(I)
, then
f^{p^{e_{1}}}\in\left(I_{e+e_{0}}(I)\right)^{t}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
. The assumption
R
is
F
-pure implies
f\in I^{[p^{e-e_{1}}]}
. Therefore for every
e\in\mathbb{N}
,
I_{e+e_{0}+e_{1}}(I)\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
.
∎

Theorem 4.2

(

5.1.
On the Descent of the Uniform Symbolic Topology Property

The study of symbolic powers of prime ideals and finite extension

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

be a finite extension of excellent domains so that

so that for all integrally closed ideals

Proof.

and

be the fraction fields of
R
and
S
respectively and

the degree of the finite field extension
K\xrightarrow{\ \ }L
. We first assume that

is Galois. The finite extension

remains generically Galois. If
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K)
then

since

and

is integrally closed in

\sigma(IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)=IS_{W_{I}}\cap S
. If
s\in S
then the minimal polynomial
f_{s}(x)
of

over
K
has degree at most
[L:K]
. The coefficients are integral over
R
and therefore belong to
R
since
R
is integrally closed. The non-leading coefficients of

are symmetric functions of the Galois conjugates of

. Therefore the non-leading coefficients of
f_{s}(x)
belong to

IS_{W_{I}}\cap R=(IS_{W_{I}}\cap R_{W_{I}})\cap R=IR_{W_{I}}\cap R=I{\footnotesize{\mbox{ by Remark~\ref{remark extension contraction finite maps integrally closed ideals}.}}}

In particular,
s^{[L:K]}\in(s^{\deg(f_{s}(x))})S\subseteq IS
. Hence for all Rees valuations
\nu
of
IS
,
\nu(s)\geq\frac{1}{[L:K]}\nu(I)
. Consequently, if
\nu
is a Rees valuation of

, then

\nu((IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)^{[L:K]})=[L:K]\nu((IS_{W_{I}}\cap S))\geq\nu(IS).

Equivalently,
(IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)^{[L:K]}\subseteq\overline{IS}
.

Now assume
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
is generically separable. Let
L_{\Gal}
be the Galois closure of
K\xrightarrow{\ \ }L
and
T
the integral closure of
R
in
L_{\Gal}
. There exists

so that for all integrally closed ideals

,

. Therefore

(IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)^{C}\subseteq(IT_{W_{I}}\cap T)^{C}\cap S\subseteq\overline{IT}\cap S=\overline{IS}{\footnotesize{\mbox{ by Remark~\ref{remark extension contraction finite maps integrally closed ideals}.}}}

If
R\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
is a general finite extension of normal domains, then we can filter the field extension
K\xrightarrow{\ \ }L
by
K\xrightarrow{\ \ }L_{\sep}\xrightarrow{\ \ }L
where
L_{\sep}
is the separable closure of
K\xrightarrow{\ \ }L
. If

are of characteristic
0
, then

and there is nothing left to prove by the above. We therefore can assume
K
and
L
have prime characteristic
p>0
and

is chosen so that

. Let
T
be the integral closure of
R
in

and let
C
be chosen so that for all integrally closed ideals

,
(IT_{W_{I}}\cap T)^{C}\subseteq\overline{IT}

s\in S
then

and is integral over

. Therefore

((IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)^{C})^{[p^{e_{0}}]}=((IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)^{[p^{e_{0}}]})^{C}\subseteq((IT_{W_{I}}\cap T))^{C}\subseteq\overline{IT}\subseteq\overline{IS}.

Therefore if
\nu
is a Rees valuation of
IS
then
\nu((IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)^{C})\geq\frac{1}{p^{e_{0}}}\nu(IS)
. In conclusion, if
\nu
is a Rees valuation of

\nu((IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)^{Cp^{e_{0}}})=p^{e_{0}}\nu((IS_{W_{I}}\cap S)^{C})\geq\nu(IS).

Therefore
(IS{W_{I}}\cap S)^{Cp^{e_{0}}}\subseteq\overline{IS}
.
∎

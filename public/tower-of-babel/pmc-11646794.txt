A telerehabilitation program to improve visual perception in children and adolescents with hemianopia consecutive to a brain tumor: a single-arm feasibility and proof-of-concept trial
Authors: Mariana Misawa, Inci Yaman Bajin, Bill Zhang, Monica Daibert-Nido, Danielle Tchao, Eduardo Garcia-Giler, Kyle Cheung, Lora Appel, Pi Nasir, Arun Reginald, Uri Tabori, Ute Bartels, Vijay Ramaswamy, Samuel N Markowitz, Eric Bouffet, Michael Reber
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundBrain tumor in children can induce hemianopia, a loss of conscious vision, profoundly impacting their development and quality of life, yet no effective intervention exists for this pediatric population. This study aimed to explore the feasibility, safety, and potential effectiveness of a home-based audiovisual stimulation in immersive virtual-reality (3D-MOT-IVR) to improve visual function and functional vision.MethodsIn a phase 2a, open-labeled, nonrandomized, single-arm study, conducted from July 2022 to October 2023 (NCT05065268), 10 children and adolescents with stable hemianopia were enrolled to perform 20-min sessions of 3D-MOT-IVR every other day for six weeks from home. We assessed feasibility by monitoring adoption, adherence and completion rates, remote data transfer and qualitative feedback. Safety was evaluated using validated cybersickness questionnaires. Comprehensive vision assessments following standardized low-vision evaluation procedures were conducted pre- and post-intervention, with follow-ups at 1- and 6 months.FindingsThe home-based 3D-MOT-IVR intervention proved both feasible and safe, with no reported adverse events. All participants completed the prescribed stimulations and the pre- and post-intervention assessment points, 90% completed the follow-ups. Nine out of ten participants showed clinically meaningful enhancement in visual function and/or functional vision, namely binocular visual field restoration and increased reading speed, but two showed concomitant deterioration in monocular visual field. These positive effects were sustained at the 6-month follow-up. Exploratory outcomes revealed a significant positive correlation between the performance at the 3D-MOT-IVR intervention and the visual perception at the binocular visual field test.InterpretationOur findings underscore the feasibility and safety of home-based audiovisual stimulation in immersive virtual-reality as a potential intervention for improving visual perception in children/adolescents with hemianopia consecutive to a pediatric brain tumor. These promising results lay a strong foundation for a larger randomized controlled trial, offering hope for a meaningful breakthrough in visual rehabilitation for this vulnerable population.FundingMeagan Bebenek Foundation and University Health Network Foundation.Keywords: Low grade gliomas, Hemianopia, Virtual-reality, Supportive care, Audiovisual stimulation

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Progress made in therapeutic treatment of brain tumors in children and adolescents significantly increased survival rates, however, many children with a brain tumor suffer from visual field defects (hemianopia) which dramatically impacts their physical and mental health, quality of life, and social outcomes, the adult they become and their family. There is no intervention for the restoration of visual perception in children with hemianopia, a loss of conscious vision in one hemifield, consecutive to a brain tumor. Sparse evidence shows that visual acuity and visual field deficits can be improved with Bevacizumab, an inhibitor of vascular endothelial growth factor, in children with optic nerve gliomas. However, the benefits are not sustained after discontinuation of the drug and there is potential toxicity. There is an unmet need for restoring perception in the blind field in children and adolescents with hemianopia consecutive to brain tumor.

Added value of this study

This feasibility/proof-of-concept trial showed that a dynamic audiovisual stimulation in virtual-reality conducted every other day for six weeks is a feasible, safe and acceptable intervention. Nine out of ten participants showed clinically meaningful enhancement in at least one out of seven visual function and/or functional vision tests after intervention, namely binocular visual field and reading speed, improving activities of daily living. Two participants showed concomitant deterioration in monocular visual field.

Implications of all the available evidence

The information learned from this study shows that the personalized intervention, administered at home through a remotely controlled virtual-reality device, is safe and can be successfully achieved by children and adolescents in their daily schedule. This technology reduces the burden of disease by limiting in clinic visits and can provide supportive care to children and adolescents living outside urban areas or in underserved population. This work laid the groundwork for future randomized clinical trials evaluating the effectiveness of our audiovisual rehabilitation program on visual perception and overall quality of life.

Introduction

A brain tumor and its treatment can affect the visual system.1 In a recent study from the Children Cancer Survivor Study, 22·5% of survivors of childhood astroglial tumors ≥5 years post-diagnosis had visual impairment.2 Visual impairment includes decreased visual acuity and contrast sensitivity, color vision loss, and visual field loss, which are often underdiagnosed. Seventy nine percent of individuals with brain tumors who have visual field defects present hemianopia (homonymous, bitemporal, or quadrantanopia),1 a loss of conscious vision caused by direct or indirect damage to the primary visual cortex V1, optic pathway, and midbrain/thalamus.3 The most common tumors associated with hemianopia are low-grade gliomas, craniopharyngiomas, and germ cell tumors, which otherwise have excellent prognoses with 10-year survival rates above 90%,1,4 but most survivors are visually impaired. Although natural recovery of hemianopia occurs in 40% of the patients within 6–18 months, it is often incomplete and limited, and still leaves 60% of individuals with permanent visual field loss.4,5 Individuals with hemianopia present long term difficulties in detecting stimuli in the defective visual field and show impaired visual scanning and exploration6 often associated with impaired localization of sound therefore worsening spatial detection.7 From a quality-of-life standpoint, hemianopia affects children and adolescents in their daily living activities, profoundly impacting intellectual and social development, mobility and independence (bumping into people/objects). Later in adulthood, hemianopia prevents individuals from driving and can affect access to employment opportunities. Altogether, there is growing recognition of the diverse and deep impact of hemianopia on physical and mental health of the affected children and adolescents, their families and the adults they become. Despite the frequent impact of brain tumors on vision, ophthalmologic evaluations are not the standard of care for all patients and there are no standardized protocols for vision loss management in the pediatric population with hemianopia.4 In our previous case series,8,9 we used immersive virtual-reality (IVR) in the Oculus Go headset to deliver a personalized, remotely controlled, dynamic audiovisual stimulation in young adult patients with stable hemianopia (>18 months) consecutive to a pediatric brain tumor. We demonstrated, after 6 weeks of repeated, home-based audiovisual stimulation, a clinically significant improvement in visual perception in the blind hemifield (two patients followed home-based stimulation remotely controlled from the laboratory during the 2020–2021 COVID-19 pandemic).8,9 Our audiovisual stimulation uses a 3D implementation with spatial sound of the multiple object-tracking (MOT) paradigm, designed in the late 80's to study visual attention in humans.10,11 The MOT task requires the deployment of overt and covert tracking and attention engaging multiple brain structures, in particular the superior colliculus (SC), a midbrain multisensory area conserved through evolution, containing aligned visual and auditory maps.12,13 Work in non-humans primates and humans has shown that the SC controls orientation including eyes and head movements such as smooth pursuit, saccades and fixation.14, 15, 16, 17 It also drives overt and covert tracking and attention,18,19 encodes visual saliency during smooth pursuit15,20,21 and influences decision making.22 The SC receives direct input from the retina, which makes it an essential hub for detecting elements in our environment and controlling subsequent orientation behavior. Hemianopia is a loss of conscious vision due to inactivity of the occipital (V1) cortex. We hypothesize that the 3D-MOT audiovisual stimulation, engaging the multisensory SC, could trigger visual spatial detection by reinforcing the SC-pulvinar-visual cortex v5/hMT network activity, bypassing the reduced or lack of activity in V1. The emission of a correlated spatial sound by the target could facilitate both overt and covert tracking in individuals with visual field loss, in line with previous work demonstrating increased visual perception by cross-modal visuo-auditory interaction.23,24 The encouraging results from our case series prompted us to further investigate the feasibility and potential effectiveness of our intervention in real-world condition in the pediatric population.

Methods

Study design

The study was designed as a single-arm (repeated measures, no control group), feasibility and proof-of-concept trial with pre/post-intervention analysis with ten participants (Fig. 1).

Inclusion criteria were individuals 8–18 years old, diagnosed with homonymous hemianopia consecutive to a pediatric brain tumor, stable for >18 months, best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) ≥20/200, interpupillary distance ≥56 mm, ability to follow the visual and auditory stimuli and training instructions, home Wi-fi access. Concomitant treatment with chemotherapy or targeted therapy was allowed with the following conditions: on treatment for >6 months and no change in visual assessment in the last 6 months.

Exclusion criteria were: pre-existing or ongoing ocular disease, both eyes with media opacity that impairs microperimetry testing, inability to perform during testing and training, consumption of psychoactive drugs, three consecutive virtual-reality-induced symptoms and effect (VRISE) questionnaire scores <25 at inclusion indicative for cybersickness,25 history of vertigo or dizziness, prior visual rehabilitation interventions.

Participants were aged 14·2 years [95% CI: 12·8, 15·6] with a time from tumor diagnosis of 102·7 months [95% CI: 75·7, 129·7] and a time from hemianopia diagnosis of 61 months [95% CI: 40, 82] (Table 1).

Intervention plan: dynamic audiovisual stimulation procedure

The participants followed a dynamic audiovisual stimulation procedure in IVR at home using the standalone and remotely controlled headset Meta Quest 2/Pro for four (P1–P2) or six weeks (P3–P10), corresponding to 13 or 21 sessions for a total time of 3 h 15 min or 5 h 15 min of stimulation, respectively. Every other day, participants performed one session of stimulation. The stimulation corresponds to 15 tasks of 20 s of continuous stimulation separated by 15–30 s of break, corresponding to 1 block. Each block is repeated 3 times with a 20–60 s break in between each block. The audiovisual stimulation task corresponds to a 3D version of the multiple object-tracking (MOT) paradigm, developed in the 80's to study visual attention in humans,10,26 implemented using the 3D Unity platform. The 3D-MOT is composed of eight high-contrast orange spheres on a black background (luminosity >80 cd/m2, size = 1–3° of visual angle). After one random sphere was cued (turning blue for 5 s and back to orange), the spheres moved for 20 s following random linear paths, bouncing off one another and off the walls of a virtual 3D cube (Fig. 2, Supplementary Video S1), therefore statistically occupying the same duration in the blind field versus the intact field. There was no trajectory bias towards the blind field, however, the location of the cued target at the start of each task before the movement occurred was personalized and presented only within the intact visual field (based on the participant's Esterman data at baseline) to ensure the participants see the cued target at the beginning of each task. The overall span of the movement of the spheres covers 100° horizontal and 70° vertical visual angle. The initial speed of the spheres was adjustable, from 3°/s to 240°/s, and determined individually for each participant at baseline. Spatially and temporally correlated 3D sound encoded using the Oculus Specializer Library, was emitted by the cued target (60 Hz, 50 dB Hearing Level) with a maximum delay of 16·6 ms (temporal tolerance), within the temporal window (<100 ms) for multisensory response enhancement.27 After 20 s, the movement of the spheres stopped and the participants were asked to select, using a virtual laser pointer, the cued sphere (the target) among the other spheres (mark-all procedure).26 If the selection was correct (the cued target) a positive feedback sound was provided and the speed of the spheres in the next trial increased and conversely for a negative selection. The speed variation (ΔX) followed an adaptative simple up-down staircase method: ΔX: Xn = X(n − 1) ± [X(n − 1)/n].28

Outcome measures

Primary outcomes corresponded to feasibility (adoption, adherence/compliance, usability, virtual-reality induced symptoms and effects—VRISE, and drop-outs) (Table 2). Secondary outcomes (Table 3) corresponded to pre/post-intervention assessments of visual function and functional vision performed at the Ophthalmology Low vision Clinic at the Toronto Western Hospital (University Health Network, Toronto, Canada) following standard procedures in low-vision rehabilitation.29 These assessments included visual acuity (best corrected visual acuity—BCVA) measured using the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) charts at 4 m (far vision) and the Colenbrander English continuous text near vision cards at 40 cm (near vision); contrast sensitivity (CS) measured using the Functional Acuity Contrast Test (FACT). Fixation stability (FS, Bivariate contour ellipse area, BCEA 63%) was measured using the Macular Integrity Assessment (MAIA) microperimeter (CentreVue, Padova, Italy). Fields of vision were evaluated by the monocular Humphrey full field test (stimulus: Goldman size III, white; background 31·5 asb; suprathreshold; 120 points; left eye – LE/right eye – RE) and the Esterman binocular field test (stimulus: Goldman size III, white; background: 31·5 asb; suprathreshold; 120 points) using the Humphrey Full Field Analyzer 3 standard automated perimeter, HFA3 (Zeiss, Heilberg, Germany). Reading speed was measured at critical print size using the Minnesota Low Vision Reading (MNREAD) test. Reading speed and Esterman tests were performed binocularly. Quality of life/vision was measured using the Children's Vision Function Questionnaire (CVFQ—48 questions) completed by the parents/legal guardians.30

Data analysis

Feasibility data were recorded in a .csv file sent through Wi-fi to our lab computers. Monocular BCVA was converted into the logarithm of minimal angle of resolution scale (LogMAR) for analysis purposes. The parents/legal guardians are the responders of the CVFQ,30 however, the questionnaire was not completed by the same person consistently (mother/father/legal guardian) creating strong discrepancies in the responses, preventing any conclusions. Data are presented for information.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was based on descriptive statistics including a measure of a central tendency (mean ± 95% confidence limit – Cl). Because of limited power, visual assessment results were analyzed using the coefficient of repeatability (COR), specific to each assessment, comparing pre- and post-intervention data. The COR (or smallest real difference) quantifies an absolute reliability in the same unit as the assessment tool and is directly related to the 95% limits of agreement proposed by Bland and Altman.31 It corresponds to the value below which the absolute differences between two measurements would lie within 95% of probability. Therefore, measurement values ≥ +COR or ≤ −COR indicate a clinically meaningful effect of the intervention.32 Statistical comparisons between male/female and pre/post-intervention were performed using Welch's t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test, respectively. The effect of the time of the day of the intervention on 3D-MOT-IVR performance was analyzed using a linear mixed model approach. Pearson's correlation coefficient r was used to measure correlation between performance and Esterman field test. Paired t-test was used to compare eye/target correlation coefficient between overt and covert tracking. Statistical analyses were performed using the Real Statistics Resource Pack in Excel (Wilcoxon and Welch tests) and JASP 0.18.3.0 (Linear mixed model, Pearson's correlation and paired t-test).

Results

Feasibility

Feasibility criteria were defined based on our previous case series,8,9 studies using virtual-reality as an interventional tool33,34 and CONSORT guidelines for feasibility trials35 (Table 2). Results indicated that 100% (10/10) of the participants adopted the technology and that they performed 91·9% (session completion criteria ≥80%, Table 2) of the home-based scheduled sessions (P1–P2: 13·5 sessions/15, P3–P10: 19·1 sessions/21) with no differences related to sex. VRISE questionnaire scores evaluating cybersickness after intervention, showed a total average of 33·45 ± 1·34 out of 35 points (and one score <25 points) indicating no adverse event (cybersickness symptoms scores criteria ≥ three consecutive VRISE scores <25) related to the use of the 3D-MOT-IVR25,36 (Table 2). Detailed analysis showed that, for a total of 182 sessions, among the five types of VRISE symptoms (nausea, disorientation, dizziness, fatigue and instability scored from 1: severe to 7: no symptoms, threshold = 5), fatigue was the most pronounced symptom after intervention, although above threshold, with female being more affected than males (5·89 ± 0·45 vs 6·42 ± 0·18, Welch's test: t = −2·155, df = 68·755, p = 0·035). No drop-outs due to the intervention were recorded although 1 voluntary withdraw (P7) was recorded due to time constraints of follow-up assessments at the clinic.

Remote data collection indicated that 100% (182/182) of the sessions performed at home by the participants were recorded on the headset and that 100% (182/182) of the corresponding reports (target speed, reaction time, time of the day, head tracking, and VRISE scores) were transferred through Wi-fi and received on the lab computers, indicating no loss of digital data. Qualitative feedback was performed at the end of the intervention using an adapted version of the virtual-reality neuroscience questionnaire (VRNQ).25 The average total score of 44·3 ± 3·6 indicated usability of the 3D-MOT-IVR stimulation (total cutoff threshold = 40, total max score = 56, Table 2). Detailed analysis indicated that, among 8 items (scored 1: extremely difficult/low to 7: extremely easy/high, cutoff threshold = 5),25 quality of the sound (total score: 4·9 ± 0·5) and graphics (total score: 4·9 ± 0·7) had the lowest scores.

Potential effectiveness on visual perception

The evaluation of vision in clinical studies with children and adolescents with brain tumor typically assesses the best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) which is the fastest and more practical outcome to measure in children. However, solely focusing on BCVA is not the most clinically relevant measure of vision for such individuals as a majority of them present normal or mild defect in visual acuity (<0·5 LogMAR).37,38 Rather, other aspects of visual function such as visual fields, contrast sensitivity and fixation stability, crucial for activities of daily living, should also be evaluated.37,39 Overall, a per participant analysis showed clinically significant improvements post-intervention in nine out of ten participants in at least one of the seven visual outcome measures (monocular and binocular visual fields, fixation stability, BCVA near and distance, contrast sensitivity, and/or reading speed – Table 3).

The analysis of the visual fields relies on the participants’ ability to keep the gaze stable on a fixation point while detecting luminous stimuli using peripheral vision. The monocular Humphrey visual full field test is considered invalid if fixation is lost in more than 20% of the measures for either eye. In four participants (P6, P8, P9, P10), loss of fixation was >20%, either pre-intervention, post-intervention or both, in either one or both eyes. Therefore we considered these results invalid and disregarded both monocular Humphrey and binocular Esterman visual field tests in these participants (Table 3, bold values).

The analysis of valid measures of Humphrey monocular field test showed clinically significant improvements (COR = 39) in three out of six participants. Two of them worsened, one had no changes, and one participant showed an improvement in one eye and a deterioration in the other. Per eye analysis of the Humphrey monocular field test showed an increase in four eyes (three participants, P4RE = +3, P5LE = +34, P7LE = +8/RE = +16) and a decrease in four eyes (three participants, P2RE = −7, P3LE = −15/RE = −7, P4LE = −6). Note that one participant, P4, had an increase in one eye (RE = +3) and a decrease in the other (LE = −6). Per eye follow-up analysis is heterogeneous and showed that the number of points perceived increased in six eyes (three eyes, three participants at 1-month and two eyes, two participants at 6-month follow-up, Supplementary Table S2) and deteriorated in five eyes (three eyes, three participants at 1-month and two eyes, two participants at 6-month, Supplementary Table S1). Note that some participants showed an improvement in one eye and a deterioration in the other post-intervention (P4) at 1- and 6-month (P5) whereas others showed an improvement at 1-month/a deterioration at 6-month (P2) or vice versa (P4). The validated measures of the Esterman binocular field test revealed that four out of six participants showed a clinically significant increase (P3 = +3, P4 = +3, P5 = +3, and P7 = +8, COR = 39). One participant (P1) showed no changes and one participant deteriorated (P2 = −10). Follow-up analyses revealed that three participants presented a continued clinically meaningful improvement (two participants, P1 = +4, P5 = +3, at 1-month and one, P2 = +12, at 6-month follow-up), whereas two participants showed a decline in the number of points perceived at 1-month follow-up (P2 = −5, P4 = −4, Supplementary Table S2). Note that one participant (P2) had a deterioration at 1-month (−5) and an improvement at 6-month (+12). The heterogeneity of the clinically significant improvements or deteriorations in both Humphrey monocular and Esterman binocular field tests hampered a clear interpretation of the results (see Discussion). However, considering the relevance of the binocular Esterman field test for real-life conditions40 and following the recommendations of the WHO/NEI joint report on binocular visual field assessment,41 our results indicated that all participants (4/4, P3, P4, P5, P7) with valid measures of binocular visual fields (loss of fixation <20%) following a 6-week intervention showed a clinically significant improvement (Δ ≥ +3 points) of binocular visual perception in their blind field. Two participants (P1 and P2) followed a 4-week intervention and did not show binocular visual field recovery.

The number of participants with validated visual field test precludes any statistical analysis of the interactions between visual field test outcome and tumor location. However, we observed that among the 6 patients with validated Esterman visual field test, four of them had chiasmatic location and the two others had post-chiasmatic location (optic radiation or occipital cortex). Among the four participants who improved with clinical significance (≥+3 points in the blind field at the Esterman binocular field test), two had chiasmatic location of the tumor and two had post-chiasmatic location, suggesting that the location of the lesion is not predictive of the outcome.

Overall, reading speed showed a clinically meaningful increase (Δ = +12·1 wpm ± 25·4, COR = 8·642) although not statistically significant (W = 9, n = 10, 0·05 < p < 0·1). At the participant level, five out of ten (50%) showed clinically meaningful improvements in reading speed post-intervention (P2 = +13, P4 = +10, P6 = +11, P8 = +75, P9 = +16). The other 50% did not show clinically significant changes (P1 = +5, P3 = −2, P5 = −6, P7 = +7, P10 = −8, COR = 8·6 wpm, Table 3). Follow-up analyses showed a continued clinically significant increase over time in six participants (three at 1-month, P2 = +21, P4 = +12, P6 = +9 and three at 6-month, P1 = +73, P2 = +23, P3 = +53) whereas three showed a decrease at both 1- month (P5 = −25, P8 = −44, P9 = −15) and 6-month (P5 = −10, P8 = −64, P9 = −9) (Supplementary Table S2).

Near distance BCVA (40 cm) measures remained stable between pre/post-intervention per patient and per eye and were considered normal/near-normal (LogMAR < 0·5 as per the World Health Organization - WHO definitions) in eight out of ten participants (Table 3). Two participants were identified as blind in one eye (P9RE = 1·9, P1LE > 2·4). Visual changes per eye showed clinically meaningful improvements for BCVA at far distance (4 m) in P2RE = −0·2 and P9RE = −0·3. Two participants exhibited moderate visual impairment (P2RE = 0·7 and P5RE = 0·6, P5LE = 0·8) and two were classified as blind in one eye (P9RE = 1·9, P1LE = 2·4, LogMAR > 1·3). No differences were observed at follow-ups (Supplementary Table S2).

Fixation stability increased in two participants and did not change in the others. Values per eye were within the normal range (normal BCEA 63% = 0·8°2 ± 0·68)43 before intervention except for two eyes (2 participants, P1LE = 4·72°2 and P9RE = 20·1°2). P1LE and P9RE showed clinical improvements post-intervention (P1LE = 1·17°2 and P9RE = 8°2, COR = 0·619). No differences were observed at follow-ups (Supplementary Table S2).

Quality of vision was assessed pre/post-intervention, and scores are presented in Supplementary Table S1. Overall and per participants analyses showed no difference pre/post-intervention and at 1- and 6-month follow-ups. The CVFQ must be completed by parents/legal guardians.30 However, in our study, the questionnaires were not consistently completed by the same parent/legal guardian, introducing strong discrepancies in the results, potentially exacerbated by familial circumstances such as parents living separately and sharing child custody. In addition, 1- and 6-month follow-up CVFQ were not recorded for P8, P9, and P10 due to time constraints. Consequently, we opted not to draw conclusions regarding the quality of vision results.

Multiple comparisons and the potential for false positives in the statistical analysis of the study can be of concern. We used a standard significance threshold of 5% (p < 0·05) for each individual test. Since each participant was tested on seven different measures, the chance of at least one false positive across the seven tests for each participant is 1 − (0·957) = 0·30166 equivalent to 30%. Using the binomial cumulative distribution function (CDF), we calculated the p-value, which represents the probability of observing 9 or more participants showing improvements due to random chance alone, given the assumption that the chance of an improvement for each participant is 30%. This p-value (1-CDFbinom [8, N = 10, p = 0·30166] <0·0002) is extremely small, meaning that the observed result (nine out of ten participants showing improvements in at least one out of seven measures) is very unlikely to have occurred by chance alone, even accounting for the multiple tests.

Exploratory outcomes

The potential use of VR as a home-based interventional tool is innovative, therefore data about its use in real-world conditions is still lacking. We recorded the time of the day when participants were performing the 3D-MOT-IVR sessions at home and showed that the majority of the sessions (55%, n = 98) were performed between 15:00 and 21:00 (Fig. 4) taking an average time of 30 min 14 s ± 4 min 28 s to complete one session (includes pre-intervention VRISE questionnaire – 3 blocks of 15 trials of 20 s each – post-intervention VRISE questionnaire). There was no effect of the time of day of the intervention on performance (Time of the day intervention x Performance: F5, 4·15 = 0·55, p = 0·74, Fig. 4).

Importantly, covert tracking showed low eye/target and head/target correlations (Pearson's r = 0·26 [95% CI: 0·23, 0·29] and r = 0·04 [95% CI: 0·00, 0·07], respectively, Fig. 6b and b′), indicating that participants maintained fixation on the central point and did not move their eyes/head to track the target (eye/target correlation overt versus covert: paired t-test, t(239) = 8·62, p = 4·72E-16, Fig. 6). Discussion

This study, the first of its kind in a population of children and adolescents with hemianopia resulting from a brain tumor, investigated the feasibility, and potential effectiveness of an innovative intervention, the personalized, home-based, remotely controlled 3D-MOT-IVR, on visual functional and functional vision. Feasibility data showed that the audiovisual 3D-MOT-IVR can be performed over periods of six weeks, at home, as a routine integrated in real-world conditions and in a daily schedule of a child/adolescent's life. Our results suggest that a minimum of six weeks of 3D-MOT-IVR stimulation, compared to a four-week stimulation protocol, is required to observe meaningful clinical benefits on visual function and functional vision essential for activities of daily living, indicating that a longer intervention duration can lead to more robust and significant results. No adverse events were associated with the use of 3D-MOT-IVR, in line with previous work in children, adolescents and young adults.36,46 Our group of participants, all students, preferably performed the intervention between 15:00 and 21:00, presumably after school hours or summer camp, without affecting the performance at the 3D-MOT-IVR. The setup of the 3D-MOT-IVR stimulation was adjusted remotely through Wi-fi for each participant individually (personalized intervention) and all digital recording generated by the application were successfully sent through Wi-fi to our secured lab computers without loss of data. This indicates that data exchange through Wi-fi using remotely controlled Meta Quest2/Pro is reliable and safe, without data loss, further confirming that stand-alone, remotely controlled headsets are suitable devices for tele-/e-health in different patient populations.47

Our findings revealed that nine out of ten participants exhibited clinically meaningful improvements in at least one out of seven visual function or functional vision outcomes which include monocular and binocular visual fields, fixation stability, BCVA far distance and reading speed. However, three participants also experienced concomitant worsening in one or two visual function outcomes, specifically in monocular and/or binocular visual field test. We cannot fully exclude the possibility that these improvements were the result of natural recovery, independent of the intervention. Nevertheless, it is highly unlikely since all participants had stable hemianopia for more than 18 months, a duration that exceeds the typical recovery period.4,5 Additionally, these improvements were not attributable to learning or practice effects, as the visual tests administered pre- and post-intervention were separated by a minimum of two weeks—which is beyond the learning effect time window, known to last up to one week.48,49 The deteriorations observed post-intervention or during follow-ups are unlikely the consequence of the intervention itself but rather to external factors, such as changes in tumor aggressiveness or invasiveness. All participants who underwent the 6-week telerehabilitation and had valid visual field measurements showed improvements in the perception of static luminous stimuli in the blind field, as assessed by the Esterman binocular field test post-intervention, aligning with earlier research.8,9 This is the first instance of improved visual perception in the blind field beyond central vision (>15°), observed in children and adolescents with hemianopia due to brain tumors. Participant's testimony (see Supplementary File) confirmed both the adoption and feasibility of the technology as well as its significant impact on activities of daily living.

The visual field results of four participants were deemed invalid due to a high loss of fixation (>20%). This compromises the robustness of the clinical study data and raises concerns about the general applicability of these visual field tests in this particular patient population. To collect reliable data from binocular field testing in future studies, the use of fixation-controlled version of the Esterman binocular field must be favored. The Humphrey monocular visual field test is designed to provide a detailed map of the visual field for each eye independently. This is crucial for detecting early signs of eye diseases such as glaucoma and for monitoring disease progression. However, in contrast, the Esterman binocular field test assesses the visual field while both eyes are open, which is more relevant for evaluating overall functional vision in real-world, and reflects the visual experience and capabilities in everyday life.40 Such approach is in line with a 2003 WHO/NEI joint report recommending that standard evaluation of vision should be performed using binocular procedures, such as the Esterman field test and reading speed test, to reflect real-life conditions.41 Therefore, when there were inconsistencies or conflicting results between the Humphrey and Esterman tests, we favored the results from the Esterman binocular field test as they provide a more accurate representation of functional vision in real-world conditions.

The perceived impact of the intervention was collected in a separate study and showed that five out of ten participants noticed an improvement in their visual perception.50 Four of these participants did show clinically meaningful improvements in binocular tests (the Esterman binocular field test and reading speed - P2, P3, P4, and P5). Three participants (P7, P8, P9) did not notice improvements although visual field tests and/or reading speed increased. One participant (P2) noticed an improvement of visual perception when reading speed increased but visual field tests deteriorated.50

A better outcome in binocular field recovery was observed with a 6-week intervention compared to a 4-week intervention, suggesting that a longer intervention leads to a better outcome. Our repeated audiovisual 3D-MOT-IVR stimulation, maintaining consistent background, occurrence rate, and spatial/temporal relationships between visual and auditory stimuli, maximizes neuronal reinforcement (following a Hebbian-like learning rule).51 This reinforcement leads to a perceptual improvement, unlike real-world conditions where these features consistently vary in intensity and occurrence.51 Repeated audiovisual stimulations trigger multisensory plasticity, reorganization and recalibration of spatial awareness between vision and audition in sub-cortical (superior colliculus and pulvinar controlling covert attention) and cortical areas (hMT+/V5), reinforcing neuronal networks bypassing V1 and ultimately restoring visual perception in the blind field.23,52, 53, 54, 55 Considering this hypothetical neurobiological mechanism, improvements in binocular visual perception in the blind field have been observed in participants with both chiasmatic and post-chiasmatic tumors, which seems counterintuitive for the former. The complexity and intricacy of the chiasmatic area, along with the specific organization of subcortical visual structures (medial/dorsal/ventral lateral geniculate nucleus, SC, pulvinar), combined with the intertwinement of tumor cells with brain tissue, may impact the functional activity of these subcortical areas differently. For example, P2 and P7 both have chiasmatic tumors; however, P2's condition significantly deteriorated in both the Humphrey and Esterman tests, whereas P7 showed improvements in both tests. Additionally, recent retrospective analyses have yielded varying outcomes regarding the efficacy of active Bevacizumab treatment on visual acuity and visual field in pediatric patients with optic pathway glioma. However, observed improvements often regress upon cessation of treatment.56 The effects of an intervention combining Bevacizumab and 3D-MOT-IVR should be explored.

Importantly, our results revealed a robust link between performance in the 3D-MOT-IVR covert tracking and the perceived points in the Esterman field test. In other words, better covert tracking led to more points perceived in the Esterman binocular field test, emphasizing the intervention's effectiveness in restoring visual perception in the blind field. Further analysis of the participants' performance at the 3D-MOT-IVR stimulation correlated to gaze tracking revealed that they keep track of the target after it entered their blind field without moving their eyes or head. This suggests a probable reliance on the spatial sound emitted by the target, in line with earlier findings showing that covert tracking relies on vision and audition.57

Visual rehabilitation in individuals with hemianopia has been a topic of intense research over the past few decades, leading to the development of several strategies primarily focusing on stroke-induced hemianopia. Substitution, compensation, and restoration interventions are the three main strategies implemented with varying degrees of success.58, 59, 60, 61 Substitution (reposition the visual world towards the intact visual field using prisms) and compensation (increased orientation towards the blind field) strategies have shown to improve the patients quality of life and activities of daily living without restoring vision per se. Restoration strategies, based on perceptual learning, neural plasticity, and residual vision in the occipital cortex, involve detection training (spots, flickering gratings, or patches) or discrimination training (moving dots or gratings) at the border or deeper in the blind field, leading to improved visual perception at the trained location.58, 59, 60, 61, 62 Compensatory and restoration strategies using audiovisual stimulation procedures have also been developed and showed a recovery of visual perception in the blind hemifield.23,51,54,55,61,63

However, it should be noted that most -if not all-the work related to visual rehabilitation in adults pertains to individuals suffering from a stroke.55,58, 59, 60, 61 Although hemianopia characterized by a loss of conscious vision can result from both conditions, the etiology and physiopathology, specifically the neuronal and glial cells activity, are likely different between a stroke (ischemic or hemorrhagic) and an optic pathway tumor (low-grade gliomas, craniopharyngioma). Hemianopia resulting from a stroke might be more challenging to rehabilitate due to the direct cellular damage and cell death caused by the lack of blood supply to the occipital cortex.

Our approach employs a dynamic, adaptive, multisensory (audiovisual) stimulation procedure. It combines features observed in real-life situations (dynamic and multisensory) with maximizing perceptual thresholds (adaptive staircase), which may increase the efficiency of sensory stimulation and thus require less time to observe clinically significant changes. Similar visual rehabilitation procedures using dynamic and/or multisensory stimulations in individuals with macular degeneration have shown visual improvements with less stimulation time.64, 65, 66

We hypothesize that our audiovisual stimulation triggers a mechanism that increases sensitivity of the SC in detecting peripheral elements through enhanced covert attention. Dynamic audiovisual stimulation reinforces the engagement of the SC's fundamental role in orientating gaze towards new elements detected by both vision and sound in the periphery, thereby stimulating the SC-pulvinar-hMT/V5 pathway.12,19 This reorientation of the gaze towards newly detected elements would bring these element in the intact field of vision, allowing them to be consciously perceived by the individual.

Our study presents several limitations, including the absence of control group, lack of randomization, a small number of participants, and instances of unreliable visual field data and quality of vision scores. However, despite these constraints, the study demonstrated promising effectiveness, with nine out of ten participants exhibiting clinically significant enhancements in visual outcomes related to binocular real-life conditions, although two of them concomitantly deteriorated in monocular visual field and one in binocular visual field. Furthermore, our work underscored the feasibility of employing home-based technology in such interventions, thus laying the groundwork for future randomized clinical trials. These trials should incorporate more suitable quality of vision and quality of life questionnaires and implement fixation-controlled visual field assessments to enhance methodological rigor and validity. Here broad inclusion and exclusion criteria were designed to reflect the real-world complexity and heterogeneity of this population of patients. This approach enabled the recruitment of a diverse group of patients, including those still undergoing active chemotherapy or targeted treatment, those who are cancer-free or in remission, with stable hemianopia and varying durations since diagnosis.

Our visual telerehabilitation program represents a paradigmatic shift in the conceptualization of healthcare, embracing a more holistic vision and facilitating the delivery of personalized care.

It ensures equitable access, offering the capability to receive supportive therapy at home independently, without direct supervision by healthcare professionals. This empowers children and adolescents residing outside urban areas to access specialized therapies. These advantages gain heightened significance in the recent context of the pandemic, given the challenges posed by travel restrictions, staff shortages, and related constraints, which have resulted in treatment delays for patients.

Multiloculated pyopneumothorax with subcutaneous emphysema and pneumomediastinum in a tuberculosis patient
Authors: Qura Tul Ain, Allahdad Khan, Arshia Batool, Mudasira Habib, Muhammad Islam, Mohamed Antar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key messagePulmonary tuberculosis with the simultaneous development of the complications of empyema, multiloculated pyopneumothorax, subcutaneous emphysema and pneumomediastinum is rare. The development of these complications leads to remarkably increased incidence of morbidity and mortality in patients. So appropriate and timely management needs to be ensured for better outcomes.Keywords: multiloculated pleural effusion, pneumomediastinum, pyopneumothorax, subcutaneous emphysema, tuberculosis complications

CLINICAL IMAGE

A 33‐year‐old Pakistani female presented with 3 months of fever, cough with sputum and exertional dyspnoea. On physical examination, she had decreased breath sounds and movement on left side of the chest and bilateral rhonchi and crepitations. Her x‐ray (Figure 1) showed cavity formation. The clinical diagnosis was of untreated, undiagnosed pulmonary and pleural tuberculosis, that resulted in the complications of lung abscess, effusion, empyema, pneumothorax and associated pneumomediastinum and subcutaneous emphysema. These findings were confirmed on computed‐tomography of the chest (Figure 2). Pleural fluid was smear positive for acid‐fast bacilli. Sputum samples were smear positive for acid‐fast bacilli by GeneXpert; drug resistance testing showed rifampicin resistance. Patient was negative for HIV infection. As the strain was resistant to rifampicin the treatment regimen included levofloxacin (750 mg daily), amikacin (15 mg/kg daily, injectable), linezolid (600 mg daily) along with isoniazid, pyrazinamide and ethambutol. The patient was also treated with inhaled bronchodilator therapy for bronchospasm. After 4 days, non‐rebreathing mask with 100% oxygen, chest tube for empyema drainage and atrium suction resulted in improvement in her condition. After 3 weeks, her clinical condition had improved and she was discharged home on ongoing treatment regimen and regular follow up visits for medication adjustment.

Only 6% of tuberculosis patients negative for HIV have pleural involvement. 1 Complications of pulmonary tuberculosis, such as pneumothorax, empyema, and pyopneumothorax are rare with reported prevalences of 1%–2% for pneumothorax. 2

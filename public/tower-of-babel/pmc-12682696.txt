Electrolyte Disturbances after Lumbar Interbody Fusion: A Comparison of Anterior/Lateral and Posterior Approaches
Authors: Mohammad Daher, Manjot Singh, Robert Quon, Ashley Knebel, Joseph E Nassar, Christopher McDonald, Bassel G Diebo, Alan H Daniels
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundElectrolyte disorders are uncommon but associated with adverse outcomes following lumbar spinal fusion. No previous study has examined rates of electrolyte disturbances after interbody fusions and compared them between anterior/lateral and posterior-based interbody fusions. The purpose of this study is to identify the incidence of electrolyte disturbances following lumbar interbody fusion and compare the difference in incidence between anterior/lateral lumbar interbody fusions (ALIF/LLIF) and posterior/transforaminal lumbar interbody fusions (PLIF/TLIF).MethodsA large multi-payer database was queried to identify patients who underwent ALIF/LLIF or PLIF/TLIF from 2010 to 2022. The incidence of electrolyte abnormalities following each procedure was examined for up to 7 days postoperatively. The cohorts were matched 1 : 1 by demographics and comorbidities.ResultsAmong 132,837 ALIF/LLIF and 492,129 PLIF/TLIF patients, the mean age was 60.18 years, 57.5% were female, and the mean Charlson Comorbidity Index was 1.76. There was a downward trend in the incidences of electrolyte abnormalities over the postoperative course except for hypercalcemia (anterior/lateral: p = 0.368; posterior: p = 0.764) and alkalosis (anterior/lateral: p = 0.764; posterior: p = 0.764) in both cohorts, as well as hypernatremia (p = 0.368) in the anterior/lateral cohort, in the first 7 days postoperatively. Patients with electrolyte abnormalities had higher rates of early postoperative ileus (5.84% vs. 1.26%), longer length of stay (9.22 vs. 6.08), higher rates of 90-day readmissions (17.30% vs. 4.48%), and higher costs ($19,946.70 vs. $15,190.87) (all p < 0.001). After matching, the anterior/lateral cohort had higher rates of hypokalemia (anterior = 1.19% vs. posterior = 1.00%, p < 0.001) and acidosis (0.25% vs. 0.18%, p < 0.001) than the posterior cohort.ConclusionsElectrolyte disturbance following lumbar interbody fusion is associated with ileus, prolonged length of stay, and increased readmission rates and cost. Given the influence of electrolyte disturbances on postoperative outcomes, ongoing efforts to identify the prevalence and risk factors for electrolyte derangement in the perioperative period are warranted.Keywords: Electrolytes, Interbody fusion, Anterior lumbar interbody fusion, Lateral lumbar interbody fusion, Posterior lumbar interbody fusion

For surgical patients, maintaining proper fluid and electrolyte balance throughout the perioperative phase is crucial. Electrolyte abnormalities, especially in older persons, can impair cognitive, psychomotor, and physical function. 1,2) Dehydration has been proven to be a major cause of readmissions in both orthopedic and non-orthopedic subspecialties, making surgical patients particularly prone to this risk. It was discovered that 28% of patients who underwent partial and total shoulder arthroplasty and were readmitted within 90 days did so because of fluid and electrolyte imbalances.3) Fluid and electrolyte imbalances were the reason for 44% of patients’ readmissions within 30 days after major otolaryngology surgery.4) Furthermore, electrolyte imbalances were shown to be predictive of postoperative delirium in patients undergoing various orthopedic surgeries.5) As for spine surgery, the literature is lacking when it comes to risk factors for postoperative fluid and electrolyte abnormalities.

Ranti et al.6) previously identified that baseline liver disease, preoperative fluid and electrolyte disorders, older age, and drug use disorders were predictive of readmissions after posterior lumbar fusion for fluid and electrolyte disorders. Several management techniques exist for lumbar fusion in the management of degenerative spinal diseases. However, anterior and lateral lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF/LLIF) and posterior/transforaminal lumbar interbody fusions (TLIF/PLIF) remain among the most common. 7,8,9,10) Despite the restoration of disc height, segmental lordosis, and whole lumbar lordosis being better in ALIF, the latter could be associated with a higher blood loss when compared to posterior-based interbody fusions.11,12) Furthermore, both ALIF and LLIF were shown to have a longer operative time compared to posterior approaches, which could result in greater anesthesia time and fluid management. 13,14) In fact, one of the main predictors of postoperative electrolytes derangement is the intraoperative blood loss, fluid administration, and transfusions.15)

No previous study has examined rates of electrolyte disturbances after interbody fusions and compared them between anterior/lateral and posterior-based interbody fusions. Therefore, this study will compare the incidence of electrolyte derangement between standalone ALIF/LLIF and PLIF/TLIF. We hypothesize that the rates of electrolyte imbalances will be higher in ALIF/LLIF and associated with ileus, length of stay, and higher readmissions.

METHODS

Institutional Review Board approval and informed consent were waived since the dataset has been deidentified for public access.

Study Design

The PearlDiver (PearlDiver Technologies) Mariner 165 database was used to conduct this retrospective cohort study. The Mariner dataset contains data from over 165 million U.S. patients covered under commercial insurance, Medicare, Medicaid, government insurance, and self-pay between January 2010 and October 2022. Patient records were queried using International Classification of Diseases Ninth (ICD-9) and Tenth (ICD-10) Revision, and Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes.

Study Population

All adult patients over the age of 18 years who underwent anterior/ lateral (CPT-22558) or posterior (CPT-22612, CPT-22630, CPT-22633) lumbar fusion and had at least 7 days of follow-up data available were identified using CPT codes. Patients with a history of tumors, metastases, or infection were excluded.

Data Extraction

Patient demographics, including age, sex, Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), were extracted. Comorbidities such as cardiac arrhythmias, chronic kidney disease, cerebrovascular disease, coronary artery disease, dementia, depression, diabetes, fluid and electrolyte disorders, hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disorder, obesity, liver disease, and renal disease were identified using ICD-9 and ICD-10 codes. Electrolyte abnormalities such as hyponatremia, hypernatremia, hypokalemia, hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia, hypercalcemia, acidosis, alkalosis, and magnesium disorders were similarly identified. Finally, outcomes, including early postoperative complications (i.e., ileus) and hospital-related outcomes (i.e., length of stay, 90-day total costs, and readmissions) were also extracted for patients with available data.

Statistical Analyses

All included anterior/lateral and posterior fusion patients were compared by baseline demographics and comorbidities using chi-square tests for categorical variables and Student t-tests for continuous variables. Hospital outcomes of patients with and without electrolyte abnormalities, as well as patients with electrolyte abnormalities who underwent anterior/lateral and posterior fusion, were similarly compared. The incidence of electrolyte abnormalities was examined up to 7 days postoperatively using Mann-Kendall testing and permutation testing. Briefly, the non-parametric Mann-Kendall testing assessed for any monotonic upward or downward trend for each abnormality over time. Permutation testing, on the other hand, estimated an observed test statistic between the anterior/lateral and posterior cohorts and compared it to a distribution of test statistics generated by repeatedly permuting the group labels 10,000 times, all to calculate a two-tailed p-value assessing for differences in the distribution of the abnormalities across the 2 cohorts. Finally, the cohorts were matched 1:1 by demographics and comorbidities, and the cumulative 7-day incidences of electrolyte abnormalities were compared using Student t-tests. All analyses were conducted using R version 4.4.0 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing), with p-value < 0.05 indicating statistical significance.

RESULTS

Patient Characteristics

Among the 624,966 included patients, 132,837 had anterior/ lateral fusion and 492,129 had posterior fusion. The mean age was 60.18 years, 57.5% were female, and mean CCI was 1.76. The most common comorbidities included hypertension (79.64%), depression (50.26%), and obesity (46.61%). The mean age and CCI, as well as the rates of most comorbidities, were higher among the posterior cohort (p < 0.05) (Table 1). After matching, 132,797 patients remained, with a mean age of 57.20 years, 57.85% being female, and a mean CCI of 1.65.

DISCUSSION

Electrolyte disorders are uncommon but associated with adverse outcomes following lumbar spinal fusion. This study reports a higher rate of hypokalemia and acidosis in ALIF/LLIF during the first 7 postoperative days compared to posterior interbody fusions. Furthermore, in the unmatched cohorts, electrolyte disturbances tended to decrease over time with most electrolytes normalizing by the seventh day postoperatively. Importantly, electrolyte abnormalities were associated with a higher incidence of postoperative ileus, longer length of stay, and higher 90-day costs and readmissions in the nearly 625,000 patients in this study.

Distinct from posterior approaches to the lumbar spine, ALIF approach-related complications are mostly associated with vascular and visceral damage. Vascular injury rates linked to ALIF have been reported to range from 1.9 to 24%, with the L4–L5 disk space surgeries reporting the highest rates of injury.16,17) The most often reported damage is a rupture of the left common iliac vein, while arterial injuries are far less common, with reports of left iliac artery injuries ranging from 0 to 0.9%.16) Avulsion of the median sacral and lumbar veins, damage to the inferior vena cava or abdominal aorta, thrombosis of the left iliac artery, and retroperitoneal hematoma are among the other vascular injuries.16) This blood loss could result in tissue hypoperfusion, causing lactate buildup, which will ultimately result in metabolic acidosis.18) As for LLIF, while it was shown that it does have a similar if not less blood loss compared to posterior approaches, LLIF does result in a higher hidden blood loss, making them more prone to blood loss-related complications such as electrolyte abnormalities even if the intraoperative blood loss was low.19) In addition, both ALIF and LLIF were shown to have a longer operative time compared to posterior based approaches.13,14) The previous articles showing the higher rate of blood loss in ALIF and hidden blood loss in LLIF, the higher rate of vascular injuries associated with the anterior approach, and the longer operative time in ALIF/LLIF could all potentially explain the higher incidence of acidosis in ALIF/LLIF compared to posterior interbody fusions.11,12,17) Another potential explanation for the higher electrolyte abnormalities in ALIF/LLIF patients could be the experience of ileus which could itself lead to electrolyte abnormalities due to gastric malabsorption.

As for hypokalemia, the incidence of this specific electrolyte disturbance is mainly increasing after decrease of acidosis (Fig. 1). In fact, overcorrecting acidosis could result in metabolic alkalosis leading to extracellular shift of H+ and its corresponding intracellular shift of K+ ultimately resulting in hypokalemia.20) Thus, the higher rate of acidosis in ALIF/LLIF alongside the intricate relationship between acid-base and potassium homeostasis could explain the higher rate of hypokalemia in ALIF/LLIF patients compared to patients undergoing posterior interbody fusions. These electrolyte disturbances are most of the time mild with more severe disorders occurring in cases of severe shock and trauma. Therefore, the resolution of these mild disturbances takes on average of 24 to 48 hours, explaining their decreasing incidence over the patients’ length of stay. Nevertheless, despite being mild, these electrolyte disturbances were associated with a higher rate of postoperative ileus, an entity shown to be associated with electrolyte abnormalities and hypokalemia more specifically. 21) In addition, patients with electrolyte abnormalities had longer length of stay which could be explained by the time needed to monitor for electrolyte normalization, ultimately leading to higher costs. The higher costs could be further justified by the higher rates of 90-day readmissions.

This study has several potential limitations. Because it uses an insurance claims database, the creation of the study cohort and the reliability of our findings is dependent on the accuracy of the ICD and CPT coding of the different procedures and electrolyte disturbances. In addition, the PearlDiver database only gives access to population-level aggregate data, so patient-specific information, such as perioperative interventions related to fluid status, especially blood loss, is lost. Additionally, variability in severity of the electrolyte disturbance was not reflected by the data, which is instead represented as a categorical variable. The database also does not give access to surgery-related parameters, such as estimated blood loss and operative time, which may allow for a better understanding of the specific factors contributing to the development of postoperative electrolyte disturbances. Furthermore, separating anterior approaches from lateral approaches is not possible using this database, restraining us from performing more granular analysis. Lastly, the use of administrative coding limited our ability to assess the severity of electrolyte abnormalities, such as distinguishing mild from severe hypokalemia, which may have influenced the observed associations with clinical outcomes.

Electrolyte disturbances are associated with ileus, increased length of stay, readmissions, and elevated costs after lumbar interbody fusion. Differing electrolyte abnormality patterns were reported for ALIF/LLIF versus PLIF/TLIF. While this study leverages a large national database to explore trends in postoperative electrolyte disturbances, several key limitations inherent to administrative data must be emphasized. The inability to differentiate between ALIF and LLIF, lack of intraoperative variables such as blood loss and fluid status, and absence of serum electrolyte levels or severity grading restrict the pathophysiological insights and limit the application of these findings to procedure-specific surgical decision-making. Therefore, our results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive, and future prospective studies with more granular clinical data are warranted to validate and expand upon these observations.

Dynamic Optimal Transport with Optimal Preferential Paths
Marcello Carioni, Juliane Krautz, Jan-F. Pietschmann

Abstract
We study a dynamic optimal transport type problem on a domain that consists of two parts: a compact set Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} (bulk) and a non-intersecting and sufficiently regular curve Γ ⊂ Ω \Gamma\subset\Omega . On each of them, a Benamou-Brenier type dynamic optimal transport problem is considered, yet with an additional mechanism that allows the exchange (at a cost) of mass between bulk and curve. In the respective actions, we also allow for non-linear mobilities. We first ensure the existence of minimizers by relying on the direct method of calculus of variations and we study the asymptotic properties of the minimizers under changes in the parameters regulating the dynamics in Ω \Omega and Γ \Gamma . Then, we study the case when the curve Γ \Gamma is also allowed to change, being the main interest in this paper. To this end, the Tangent-Point energy is added to the action functional in order to preserve the regularity properties of the curve and prevent self-intersections. Also in this case, by relying on suitable compactness estimates both for the time-dependent measures and the curve Γ \Gamma , the existence of optimizers is shown. We extend these analytical findings by numerical simulations based on a primal-dual approach that illustrate the behaviour of geodesics, for fixed and varying curves.

Introduction

Originally formulated by Monge in the 18th century and later refined by Kantorovich in the 20th century, Optimal Transport offers a mathematical framework for comparing and transforming probability distributions by minimizing the cost of moving mass from one distribution to another.
Given a compact set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and two probability measures
\mu\in P(\Omega)
and
\nu\in P(\Omega)
and choosing the Euclidean distance as the cost function results in the

2
-Wasserstein distance defined as

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\sqrt{\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int|x-y|^{2}d\pi(x,y)}

(1)

where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is the set of transport plans, that are all probability measures in the product space
\Omega\times\Omega
having
\mu
and
\nu
as marginals.
While the
2
-Wasserstein distance focuses on static settings, many real-world problems require transport models that evolve over time. This need has motivated the study of dynamic optimal transport introduced in the pioneering work by Benamou and Brenier
[
5
]
. In this work it has been shown that the
2
-Wasserstein distance admits a reformulation as the following
dynamical fluid-mechanics problem

\displaystyle W_{2}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{(\rho_{t},J_{t})}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{dJ_{t}}{d\rho_{t}}\right|^{2}\,d\rho_{t}dt

(2)

where the pair
(\rho_{t},J_{t})
satisfies the continuity equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{t}+{\rm div}(J_{t})=0

(3)

in the distributional sense, with
\rho_{0}=\mu
and
\rho_{1}=\nu
. Such dynamic formulation of the Wasserstein distance has proven useful in a great variety of contexts and it is a fundamental ingredient to develop a gradient flow theory in Wasserstein spaces
[
35
,
28
,
2
]
. Successful examples of applications are consequences of the usefulness of an optimal transport prior in understanding dynamics and includes among others applications to crowd modelling and traffic congestion
[
32
,
12
,
40
]
, dynamic inverse problems
[
38
,
9
,
8
,
17
]
, optimal control models and algorithms in machine learning
[
34
]
.
Moreover, variants of the Benamou-Brenier approach have been explored to include more general action functionals
[
20
,
31
,
14
]
, dissipations
[
15
,
30
,
29
]
and non-local interactions
[
39
]
.

In this paper, we contribute to the above mentioned theory by studying a dynamic optimal transport type problem where mass can be transported both in a bulk domain
\Omega
as well as on a curve
\Gamma\subset\Omega
, which has co-dimension of at least one.
In our model the mass between the bulk and the curve can be exchanged, while the total mass in the system remains a conserved quantity.
The cost of transport is measured by means of an action functional that includes the kinetic energy of the time-dependent distribution in the bulk and on the curve, respectively, as well as the additional cost due to exchange of mass between bulk and curve. From an intuitive standpoint our mathematical framework models the optimal evolution of a population (for instance metropolitan traffic) when there exists a path (such as a highway) where the transport is faster (or cheaper), but it requires a cost to access the path. This could model highways where, due to a high traffic concentration at the toll booths, the access to the highway requires a non-negligible time investment.
In our analysis we first consider the preferential curve
\Gamma
to be fixed, where the goal is to find the optimal evolution of the population given initial and final configurations. This setting is strongly related to previous works that coupled transport in a domain with transport on its boundary,
[
33
]
as well as recent approaches to transport on metric graphs,
[
21
,
10
]
.
In a second step, we extend the formalism by allowing the curve to vary, making its shape part of the optimization problem. Such problems can be seen in the context of optimal planning, where the preferential path is optimized according to initial and final distributions and under the prior assumption that the population is moving following an optimal transport dynamic.

We now describe the problem using the mathematical formalism we employ in the paper. We consider
\Omega
a compact, connected subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\Gamma=\gamma([0,1])
the image of a regular, non self-intersecting curve of class
C^{1}([0,1];\Omega)
. The distribution of mass on the bulk and in
\Gamma
is modelled via a pair of time-dependent measures
(\rho_{t},\mu_{t})
, indexed by
t\in[0,1]
. Here,
\rho_{t}\in M(\Omega)
represents the mass distributions in the bulk while
\mu_{t}\in M(\Gamma)
denotes the distribution on the preferential path
\Gamma
.
Since the total mass will be conserved in our model, we assume a compatibility condition for initial and final distributions, meaning that
\rho_{0}(\Omega)+\mu_{0}(\Gamma)=1
and
\rho_{1}(\Omega)+\mu_{1}(\Gamma)=1
. In what follows, this will be denoted by
(\rho_{0},\mu_{0})\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
and
(\rho_{1},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
.
To model the transportation of mass, we denote by
J_{t}
and
V_{t}
the momentums associated to
\rho_{t}
and
\mu_{t}
. Together,
(\rho_{t},\mu_{t},J_{t},V_{t})
are assumed to satisfy two continuity equations which are coupled in order to allow exchange between bulk and curve while preserving the total mass:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{t}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot J_{t}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\Omega,\qquad\frac{\partial\mu_{t}}{\partial t}+\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot V_{t}=f_{t}\quad\text{on}\ \ \Gamma,

(4)

\displaystyle J_{t}\cdot n_{\Gamma}=f_{t}\quad\text{in }\Gamma.

We refer to Section
2.1
for a more rigorous definition of (
4
) given in a weak sense and also for a generalization of (
4
) where
an exchange mechanism that acts at the end points of
\Gamma
is added. We already note that this formulation allows for mass being present on
\Gamma
and in
\Omega

(\rho_{0},\mu_{0})\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
and
(\rho_{1},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
are denoted by
{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
.

Next we introduce an action functional which measures the time accumulated cost of transport in bulk and curve, respectively, but also the cost of mass transfer. Inspired by the works
[
20
,

]
we employ mobilities

and

The action functional is then defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|J_{t}|^{2}}{m_{\Omega}(\rho_{t})}\,d\lambda_{\Omega}+\alpha_{1}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|V_{t}|^{2}}{m_{\Gamma}(\mu_{t})}\,d\lambda_{\Gamma}+\alpha_{2}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{|f_{t}|^{2}}{m_{\Gamma}(\mu_{t})}d\lambda_{\Gamma}.

(BB)

Here,

are reference measures, necessary as the mobility yields integrands that are no more
1

Note also that with a slight abuse of notation we have identified the measures

with their densities respect to the reference measures. We refer to Section
2.2
for a rigorous definition. In addition
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>0
are parameters that either control the cost of transport on
\Omega
and
\Gamma
, respectively (
\alpha_{1})
, or that of being transported onto or off from
\Gamma
(
\alpha_{2}
). For a fixed curve
\Gamma
we thus consider the dynamic variational problem

\displaystyle\inf_{(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t})dt.

(5)

In our analysis we prove that (
5
) admits a minimizer and study its asymptotic behaviour when
\alpha_{2}\rightarrow 0
and
\alpha_{2}\rightarrow+\infty
. In this first part of our work, our analysis follows closely the one carried out in
[
33
]
, where the exchange of mass and different dynamics occur on the boundary of
\Omega
. However, due to the introduction of mobilities and since the preferential path can be located arbitrarily in
\Omega
, many arguments differ from
[
33
]
. In particular, contrarily to
[
33
]
where a duality approach is used, we rely on the direct method of Calculus of Variations that allows to handle the usage of mobilities.

In the second part of this work, we extend the problem by making the shape of
\Gamma
part of the optimization problem. One of the main challenges of this approach is that, in order to define a continuity equation on
\Gamma
, the curve
\gamma
needs to be regular and non self-intersecting. As a consequence, our variational problem should have specific compactness properties that enforce the minimizing curve
\gamma_{\rm min}
to be regular and non self-intersecting. To this end, we augment the action functional by adding a regularization term
R:C^{1}([0,1];\Omega)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
that includes the following terms. The first term is the so called Tangent-Point energy
[
41
]
of the curve
\gamma
defined as

\displaystyle E^{p}_{\rm tp}(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)||\gamma^{\prime}(t)|}{r_{\rm tp}^{\gamma}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))^{p}}\,dsdt

(6)

where
r_{\rm tp}^{\gamma}(x,y)
denotes the radius of the smallest sphere tangent to
x\in\Gamma
and passing through
x,y\in\Gamma
, see
Figure 1
.
The Tangent-Point energy was originally defined for closed curves or knots with the goal of characterizing their self-avoidance properties
[
41
]
.
Following the same principle, we apply the Tangent-Point energy to open curves, ensuring that limits of minimizing sequences of the augmented variational problem are one dimensional embedded submanifolds in
\Omega
with boundary and thus parametrized by a non self-intersecting curve.
We also show that such an energy has good compactness properties. Indeed, by adapting the results from
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
that hold for closed curves, we show that, under arclength parametrization, the uniform bound on
E^{p}_{\rm tp}
implies a uniform bound in
W^{2-1/p,p}([0,1];\Omega)
, compactly embedding in
C^{1}([0,1];\Omega)
.

\gamma(t)=t^{3}-t
(0,0)
(1,0)

Circle tangent to the image of
\gamma(t)=t^{3}-t

t=0

Relying on such results we augment the action functional by the additional regularization

\displaystyle R(\gamma):=2^{-p}E^{p}_{\rm tp}(\gamma)-\log|\gamma(0)-\gamma(1)|+\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\,dt.

(7)

The functional
R
is a combination of the Tangent-Point energy and two additional terms. The second one is added for technical reasons, since our model is defined for open curves allowing for normal flows at the end points. Other modelling options, such as extending the problem to closed curves would be possible. The third term is instead natural and necessary to avoid minimizing sequences of curves with unbounded length. By augmenting the action functional (
5
) with the regularization
R
we are able to prove, for
p>2
, existence of an optimal curve
\gamma
and optimal time-dependent measures
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
that jointly minimize (
5
).

In the third part of the work, we develop a numerical scheme to compute both the time-dependent measures
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
as solution of (
5
) for a fixed curve
\gamma
as well as for an optimal curve
\gamma
that is then part of the optimization problem. In particular, we rely on an augmented Lagrangian formulation of the problem.

Contributions and outline

The main contributions of our work are the following:

•

Provide a rigorous formulation of a set of coupled continuity equations for bulk-curve transport on arbitrary, regular curves of co-dimension at least one (Definition
2.2
).

•

Establish existence of minimizers for the dynamic transport cost problems introduced above, both for a fixed (Theorem
2.12
) as well as for a varying curve (Theorem
4.9
).

•

Study certain limits when cost parameters in the action tend to zero or infinity (Section
3
).

•

Provide a numerical scheme based on the combination of a finite element discretization and a primal-dual optimization algorithm and demonstrate its abilities via several examples (Section
5
).

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we analyse the case of a fixed preferential path. We introduce the notion of weak solutions to the coupled system of continuity equations in Subsection
2.1
as well as the rigorous formulation of the dynamic problem in Subsection
2.2
and show well-posedness in Subsection
2.3
. Section
3
is devoted to the study of limits of cost-parameters tending to zero or infinity. These limits establish a connection between the coupled problem and classical (uncoupled) Optimal Transport with mobilities. The minimization problem with varying paths is analysed in Section
4
, including the study of the Tangent-Point energy for non-closed curves in Subsection
4.1
, the proof of existence of minimizers in Subsection
4.2
and a discussion of linear mobilities in Subsection
4.3
. In Section
5
, we conclude by introducing a numerical algorithm that is applied it to several examples.

1.1
Further related work

Dynamic optimal transport with mass exchange

The basis for our formulation of the transport problem is the dynamic formulation introduced by Benamou and Brenier in
[
5
]
. The extension of this formalism in order to implement mass exchange became popular in the context of unbalanced transport,
[
15
,
30
,
29
]
. There, an additional reaction term added to the continuity equation allowed for a change of mass over time. Closer to our work is the approach of
[
33
]
, which already included the coupling of a bulk domain with a lower-dimensional one, in this case the boundary of the domain. In this work, the author includes an exchange mechanism of mass between the two that is based on a non-homogeneous flux boundary condition in the bulk and a related reaction term on the boundary. This idea was later applied to metric graphs with reservoirs that may carry mass,
[
10
,
22
]
. Contrary to our approach, both
[
33
]
and
[
10
]
establish existence of minimizers via convex duality instead of the direct method. The structure of gradient flows on metric graphs with mass exchange at the vertices was recently explored in
[
27
]
.

Non-linear mobilities

The first study of dynamic transport distances with non-linear mobilities is due to
[
20
]
, where power-like concave mobilities were introduced. This was later extended to mobilities with unbounded domains,
[
14
]
, as well as volume filling mobilities in
[
31
]
. Convexity properties along geodesics in the presence of mobilities have been analyzed in
[
14
]
.

Tangent point energies

The problem of self-avoidance of closed curves is well known in knot theory and several functionals preventing self-intersections have already been studied.
Some standard choices include Möbius energies introduced by O’Hara in
[
36
]
or Ropelength defined as the ratio of length over thickness of a curve
[
26
]
.
However, these functionals are numerically difficult to handle and only few schemes have been investigated, e.g.
[
42
]
in the context of shape optimization. They either contain the evaluation of several suprema (in the case of Ropelength) or they need geodesic distances and allow for almost intersecting curves (in the case of the Möbius energies).
Therefore, finding energies ”in between” is of interest.
In
[
26
]
, the authors proposed a new family of functionals called Tangent-Point energies. They can be represented using an integral formulation without the evaluation of geodesic distances. Moreover, no supremum is needed in the definition making it a good candidate for numerical applications.

In recent years there has been interest in the analytical properties of Tangent-Point energies, see
[
41
]
and
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
. It turns out that curves with finite Tangent-Point energy gain
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and their images define embedded one dimensional submanifolds.

Numerical algorithms

There exists a plephora of algorithms that is based on the dynamic formulation of optimal transport, notably even the main focus of the original work by Benamou and Brenier
[
5
]
was the design of an augmented Lagrangian (ALG) approach that allows to enforce the continuity equation constraint. More recently, the ALG approach has been adopted to finite volumes in
[
11
]
.
More relevant for this work are proximal splitting algorithms which were used in
[
37
]
, combined with staggered grid discretizations to enforce the continuity equation constraints.
For our work, we extend the algorithm introduced in
[
5
]
to the bulk-curve setting using a finite element discretization of space-time domains introduced in
[
24
]
.
Finally, we mention a related approach due to
[
13
]
which employs piecewise constant approximations and proximal splitting algorithms to discretize the JKO scheme. However, in
[
13
]
, the continuity equation constraints are enforced only in a relaxed sense. The resulting formulation then involves the minimization of a sum of three convex functionals, which is done by the algorithm developed in
[
43
]
.

As for the numerical treatment of the tangent point energy,
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
introduced a scheme treating it as a penalty term when minimizing the elastic bending energy of a curve. In
[
44
]
a gradient descent method using Sobolev-Slobodeckij inner products has been proposed and applied to different problems like curve packing or graph drawing to avoid self-collision. Due to the presence of additional curve-dependent terms in the minimization, we employ a gradient descent method, numerically approximating the gradient by finite differences.

Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces

In order to minimize over the preferential path in Section
4
, we need compactness in a suitable normed space. This space will turn out to be
C^{1,\alpha}([0,1];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The compactness however will follow by a compact embedding from Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces, also called fractional Sobolev spaces, into these Hölder spaces. Following
[
19
]
, we give a short introduction to Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces and state the necessary embedding theorem.

Definition 1.3

(Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces)
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be open and let
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
be given. For
f:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
we define the
Gagliardo seminorm

\displaystyle[f]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(U)}:=\int\limits_{U}\int\limits_{U}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\,dxdy

and the corresponding norm by

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(U)}:=\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U)}+[f]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(U)}.

The
Sobolev-Slobodeckij space
W^{s,p}(U)
is given as

\displaystyle W^{s,p}(U):=\{f\in L^{p}(U)\ :\ \|f\|_{W^{s,p}(U)}<+\infty\}

which is a Banach space for the norm defined above.

These spaces act as an intermediate space between
L^{p}(U)
and
W^{1,p}(U)
and can be extended to arbitrary
k+s
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
s\in(0,1)
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be open and let
s\in(0,1)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
be given. The space

\displaystyle W^{k+s,p}(U):=\{f\in W^{k,p}(U)\ :\ D^{\vec{\alpha}}f\in W^{s,p}(U)\text{ for all }\vec{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\text{ s.t. }|\vec{\alpha}|=k\}

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k+s,p}(U)}:=\left(\|f\|^{p}_{W^{k,p}(U)}+\sum_{|\vec{\alpha}|=k}\left[D^{\vec{\alpha}}f\right]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(U)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is a Banach space.

If the mapping is vector-valued instead, the space is defined component-wise.
The following embedding theorem holds true by
[

Theorem 1.5
.

.

For maps
f:U\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
we obtain similar embeddings by considering each component separately.
The compactness of the embedding will be crucial for showing existence of minimizers for varying paths.

2
Fixed preferential path

In this section we consider a (fixed) preferential path
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
, regular, non self-intersecting and of class
C^{1}([0,1];\Omega)
(in short
C^{1}
-curve) with image denoted by
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We aim to model the dynamic of a population under the assumption that travelling on the preferential path
\Gamma
is cheaper; however, accessing (or leaving) the preferential path is associated with an additional cost. Such a modelling choice can be justified by economic reasons (the highway is expensive to take) or time reasons (there is often much more traffic to access or leave the highway, for example in Italian highways).

2.1
Continuity equations and mass exchange

In this section we define rigorously the coupled continuity equations anticipated in the introduction. Since we will prefer to define the continuity equation on
\Gamma
using its parametrization, we will need the notion of push-forward of measures by
\gamma^{-1}
defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a non self-intersecting curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
with image
\Gamma
and a measure
\nu\in M(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\nu^{\gamma}\in M([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{N})
as

\displaystyle\nu^{\gamma}=\gamma^{-1}_{\#}\nu.

We are now ready to state the continuity equations that regulate the exchange of mass between
\Omega
and
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.2

(Coupled continuity equations)
.

Given a regular, non self-intersecting
C^{1}
-curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
with image
\Gamma
we say that
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G^{0}_{t},G_{t}^{1})\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
satisfies the coupled continuity equations with initial and final data
(\rho_{0},\mu_{0})\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
and
(\rho_{1},\mu_{1})\in\mathcal{P}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\varphi(t,x)\,d\rho_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(t,x)\cdot dJ_{t}dt-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(t,x)\,df_{t}dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{\gamma(0)\}}\varphi(t,x)\,d\mathcal{G}_{t}^{0}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{\gamma(1)\}}\varphi(t,x)\,\,d\mathcal{G}_{t}^{1}dt=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(1,x)d\rho_{1}-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(0,x)d\rho_{0}

(9)

for every
\varphi\in C^{1}(X_{\Omega})
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}\psi(t,s)\,d\mu_{t}^{\gamma}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{s}\psi(t,s)|\gamma^{\prime}(s)|^{-1}\,d\mathcal{V}_{t}^{\gamma}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\psi(t,s)\,df_{t}^{\gamma}dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{0\}}\psi(t,s)\,d(\mathcal{G}_{t}^{0})^{\gamma}dt-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{1\}}\psi(t,s)\,d(\mathcal{G}_{t}^{1})^{\gamma}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\psi(1,s)d\mu^{\gamma}_{1}-\int_{0}^{1}\psi(0,s)d\mu^{\gamma}_{0}

(10)

for every
\psi\in C^{1}([0,1]^{2})
. We refer to such conditions as
{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
and if
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G^{0}_{t},G^{1}_{t})
satisfies
{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
we write
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G^{0}_{t},G^{1}_{t})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
, where we keep implicit the dependence on initial and final data.

Remark 2.3
.

We point out that we are allowing mass to be exchanged tangentially at the boundary of
\Gamma
at rate
\mathcal{G}^{0}
(entering
\Gamma
) and
\mathcal{G}^{1}
(exiting
\Gamma
). Moreover, we also note that Definition
2.2
is independent of the choice of the injective parametrization for
\Gamma
but it might reverse the interpretation of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
and
\mathcal{G}^{1}
. This justifies the notation
{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
. Moreover it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{s}\psi(t,s)|\gamma^{\prime}(s)|^{-1}\,d\mathcal{V}_{t}^{\gamma}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi(t,p)\cdot dV_{t}dt

(11)

where
\nabla_{\Gamma}
is the gradient of the function
x\mapsto\psi(t,x)
on the embedded manifold
\Gamma
. This motivates that Definition
2.2
is nothing but the parametrized version of (
4
). The identity (
11
) can be easily justified noticing that a local base of the tangent plane of
\Gamma
in
p\in\Gamma
is given by
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}|_{p}=\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma^{-1}(p))
. Therefore from the definition of the Riemannian gradient we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi(t,p)=g\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}|_{p},\frac{\partial}{\partial x}|_{p}\right)^{-1}\partial_{s}\psi(t,s)|_{\gamma^{-1}(p)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}|_{p}

(12)

implying that
\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi(t,p)\cdot\tau_{\Gamma}(p)=\partial_{s}\psi(t,s)|_{\gamma^{-1}(p)}|\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma^{-1}(p))|^{-1}
and thus (
11
).

Under the previous coupled continuity equations, it is possible to prove that the total mass is conserved and more specifically the total density satisfies a global continuity equation.

Proposition 2.4
.

Given a regular, non self-intersecting
C^{1}
-curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
with image
\Gamma
, suppose that
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G^{0}_{t},G^{1}_{t})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
. Denoting by
\eta_{t}=\rho_{t}+\bar{\mu}_{t}
and
W_{t}=J_{t}+\bar{V}_{t}
it holds that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{t}+\nabla\cdot W_{t}=0\quad\text{in }\ X_{\Omega}

(13)

distributionally with initial value
\rho_{0}+\bar{\mu}_{0}
and final value
\rho_{1}+\bar{\mu}_{1}
.

Proof.

Given an arbitrary test function
\varphi\in C^{1}(X_{\Omega})
and defining
\psi\in C^{1}([0,1]^{2})
as

\displaystyle\psi(t,s)=\varphi(t,\gamma(s))

(14)

it holds that
\partial_{s}\psi(t,s)=\nabla\varphi(t,\gamma(s))\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(s)
. Therefore, using the definition of push-forward, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\,d\eta_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(t,x)\cdot dW_{t}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\,d\rho_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\,d\bar{\mu}_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(t,x)\cdot dJ_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(t,x)\cdot d\bar{V}_{t}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\,d\mu_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma}\nabla\varphi(t,x)\cdot\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma^{-1}(x))}{|\gamma^{\prime}(\gamma^{-1}(x))|}d\mathcal{V}_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(t,x)\,df_{t}dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}\varphi(1,x)d\rho_{1}-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(0,x)d\rho_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{\gamma(0)\}}\varphi(t,x)\,d\mathcal{G}_{t}^{0}dt-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{\gamma(1)\}}\varphi(t,x)\,\,d\mathcal{G}_{t}^{1}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{t}\psi(t,s)\,d\mu_{t}^{\gamma}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\varphi(t,\gamma(s))\cdot\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(s)}{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)|}d\mathcal{V}^{\gamma}_{t}dt+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\psi(t,s)\,df_{t}^{\gamma}dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}\varphi(1,x)d\rho_{1}-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(0,x)d\rho_{0}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{0\}}\psi(t,s)\,d(\mathcal{G}_{t}^{0})^{\gamma}dt-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\{1\}}\psi(t,s)\,\,d(\mathcal{G}_{t}^{1})^{\gamma}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\psi(1,x)d\mu_{1}-\int_{\Gamma}\psi(0,x)d\mu_{0}+\int_{\Omega}\varphi(1,x)d\rho_{1}-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(0,x)d\rho_{0}

as we wanted to prove.
∎

2.2
Dynamic formulation

In this section we define the variational problem that is regulating the evolution of the measures
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G^{0}_{t},G^{1}_{t})\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
in time. Such measures should obey the coupled continuity equations
{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
while minimizing an action functional.
In our case, it is constructed as a generalization of the kinetic energy due to the introduction of a mobility function, separately for the kinetic energy of
\rho
on
\Omega
, the kinetic energy of
\mu
on
\Gamma
and the exchange of mass between
\Omega
and
\Gamma
.
We start with a definition of admissible mobility functions as introduced in
[
31
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

A function
m
is called
admissible mobility function
if
m:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)\cup\{-\infty\}
is an upper semicontinuous, concave function with
\domain(m)\coloneqq\{x\in[0,+\infty):m(x)>-\infty\}=[a,b]
for
a,b\geq 0
,
a<b
and
m(z)>0
for every
z\in(a,b)
.

Remark 2.6
.

The most prominent example of this class of mobilities is given by volume filling mobilities like
m(z)=z(1-z)
defined in
[0,1]
or more general
m(z)=(a-z)(b-z)
defined in
[a,b]
(in both cases extended to
-\infty
for
z\notin[0,1]
and
z\notin[a,b]
respectively). These mobilities give rise to upper and lower bounds on the densities, implicitly imposing additional constraints. A more detailed discussion of mobilities can be found in e.g.
[
31
]
,
[
20
]
or
[
14
]
.
Note in particular that for any admissible mobility
m
it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\rm dom(m)}m(z)<\infty.

(15)

We are now ready to introduce rigorously the dynamic variational problem, which is defined as a minimization of an action functional on tuples satisfying the coupled continuity equation.

Definition 2.7
.

Given a regular, non self-intersecting
C^{1}
-curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
with image
\Gamma
, we define the variational dynamic problem as

\displaystyle\inf_{(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu,f,G_{t}^{0},G_{t}^{1})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}(J_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},\mathcal{G}_{t}^{0},\mathcal{G}_{t}^{1})\,dt

(BB2)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}=\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{\Omega}\left(\frac{d\rho_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Omega}},\frac{dJ_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Omega}}\right)\,d\lambda_{\Omega}+\int_{\Gamma}\left[\alpha_{1}\Psi_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}},\frac{d\mathcal{V}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right)+\alpha_{2}\Psi_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}},\frac{df_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right)\right]\,d\lambda_{\Gamma}

(16)

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha_{3}\int_{\{\gamma(0)\}\cup\{\gamma(1)\}}\left[\Psi_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d(\mu_{t}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}\gamma(0))}{d\lambda_{E}},\frac{d\mathcal{G}_{0}}{d\lambda_{E}}\right)+\Psi_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d(\mu_{t}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}\gamma(1))}{d\lambda_{E}},\frac{d\mathcal{G}_{1}}{d\lambda_{E}}\right)\right]d\lambda_{E}

for
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}>0
. In the previous definition
\lambda_{\Omega}\in M_{+}(\Omega)
is any measure such that
\rho\ll\lambda_{\Omega}
and
|J|\ll\lambda_{\Omega}
,
\lambda_{\Gamma}\in M_{+}(\Gamma)
is any measure such that
\mu\ll\lambda_{\Gamma}
,
|\mathcal{V}_{t}|\ll\lambda_{\Gamma}
and
f\ll\lambda_{\Gamma}
, and
\lambda_{E}\in M_{+}(\{\gamma(0)\}\cup\{\gamma(1)\})
is any measure such that
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}\gamma(i)\ll\lambda_{E}
for
i=0,1
.
Moreover,
\Psi_{\Omega}
and
\Psi_{\Gamma}
are action functionals defined as

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Omega}(z,w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{|w|^{2}}{m_{\Omega}(z)}&m_{\Omega}(z)\neq 0,z\in{\rm dom}(m_{\Omega})\\
0&w=0\text{ and }m_{\Omega}(z)=0\\
+\infty&(m_{\Omega}(z)=0\text{ and}\ w\neq 0)\text{ or }z\notin{\rm dom}(m_{\Omega})\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\Psi_{\Gamma}(z,w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{|w|^{2}}{m_{\Gamma}(z)}&m_{\Gamma}(z)\neq 0,z\in{\rm dom}(m_{\Gamma})\\
0&w=0\text{ and }m_{\Gamma}(z)=0\\
+\infty&(m_{\Gamma}(z)=0\text{ and}\ w\neq 0)\text{ or }z\notin{\rm dom}(m_{\Gamma})\end{array}\right.

for admissible mobilities
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
with domain bounded by
a_{\Omega}
,
b_{\Omega}
and
a_{\Gamma}
,
b_{\Gamma}
respectively.

Remark 2.8
.

Owing to the results in
[
20
,
31
]
the functional
{\mathcal{A}}
defined in (
BB2
) is convex and lower semicontinuous with respect to the narrow convergence of measures. In particular, for proving the narrow lower semicontinuity, we refer the reader to
[
20
, Lemma 3.9]
that can be applied to each term in (
16
) for a constant sequence of reference measures and using the fact that the narrow topology coincides with the weak one on compact domains.

If
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}
is finite, we can conclude further regularity for
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G_{t}^{0},G^{1}_{t})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Suppose that
\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}(J_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},\mathcal{G}^{0}_{t},\mathcal{G}^{1}_{t})dt<\infty
for some
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G_{t}^{0},G^{1}_{t})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
. Then
\rho_{t}
and
\mu_{t}
admit a narrowly continuous representative.
Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle a^{\Omega}\leq\frac{d\rho_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Omega}}\leq b^{\Omega},\ \lambda_{\Omega}-a.e.,\qquad\text{and}\qquad a^{\Gamma}\leq\frac{d\mu_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\leq b^{\Gamma},\ \lambda_{\Gamma}-a.e.

(23)

for every
t\in[0,1]
, where
(a^{\Omega},b^{\Omega})
and
(a^{\Gamma},b^{\Gamma})
are the respective constants for
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
in Definition
2.7
.

Proof.

The proof follows as in
[
31
, Section 2, Definition 3]
and
[
31
, Section 2, Lemma 1]
.
∎

Note that for general mobilities it is not true that the infimum in (
BB2
) is always finite
[
20
]
. This depends on the interplay between the reference measures and the initial and final values. However, we point out that in case of standard dynamic optimal transport (i.e. with linear mobility) such a result follows from a similar argument to the one of
[
33
, Lemma 3.7]
that is based on the existence of Wasserstein geodesics and Fischer-Rao geodesics.

2.3
Well-posedness of the dynamic formulation

In this section
we will employ the Direct method of Calculus of Variations to prove that (
BB2
) admits at least one minimizer.
The basic result needed for that is a compactness result (in time) for the time-dependent family of measures
\rho_{t}
and
\mu_{t}
. In particular, the fundamental ingredient of such compactness result is the following Hölder estimate for
\rho_{t}
and
\mu_{t}
.

Proposition 2.10

(Hölder estimates)
.

Given a regular, non self-intersecting
C^{1}
-curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
, suppose that for
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G^{0}_{t},G^{1}_{t})\in{\rm CE(\Gamma)}
there holds that
\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}(J_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},\mathcal{G}^{0}_{t},\mathcal{G}^{1}_{t})dt<M
for a constant
M>0
. Then there exists a constant
C(\lambda_{\Omega},\lambda_{\Gamma},b^{\Omega},b^{\Gamma},M)>0
only depending on the choice of reference measures,
b^{\Omega}
,
b^{\Gamma}
and
M
, such that for all
s,t\in[0,1]
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{t}-\rho_{s}\|_{C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda_{\Omega},\lambda_{\Gamma},b^{\Omega},b^{\Gamma},M)|t-s|^{1/2},

(24)

\displaystyle\|\mu^{\gamma}_{t}-\mu^{\gamma}_{s}\|_{C^{1}([0,1])^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda_{\Omega},\lambda_{\Gamma},b^{\Omega},b^{\Gamma},M)|t-s|^{1/2}.

(25)

Here,
C^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
denotes the dual space of
C^{1}(\Omega)
and
C^{1}([0,1])^{*}
the dual space of
C^{1}([0,1])
.

Proof.

The proof follows from classical arguments (see for example
[
33
, Proposition 3.5]
or
[
9
, Lemma A.2]
). However, we will sketch it for estimate (
25
) since in our notation it involves the push-forward of the measures on the interval
[0,1]
. Given
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,1])
note that the measurable map
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(s)d\mu^{\gamma}_{t}
is weakly differentiable in
(0,1)
. Indeed for
\xi\in C_{c}(0,1)
it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\prime}(t)\varphi(s)d\mu^{\gamma}_{t}(s)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\xi(t)\varphi^{\prime}(s)|\gamma^{\prime}(s)|^{-1}d\mathcal{V}^{\gamma}_{t}\,dt-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\xi(t)\varphi(s)df_{t}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\xi(t)\left(\varphi^{\prime}(s)|\gamma^{\prime}(s)|^{-1}d\mathcal{V}^{\gamma}_{t}-\varphi(s)df^{\gamma}_{t}\right)\,dt,

implying that the weak derivative is equal to
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{\prime}(s)|\gamma^{\prime}(s)|^{-1}d\mathcal{V}^{\gamma}_{t}-\varphi(s)df^{\gamma}_{t}
. Now, let
s,t\in[0,1]
be given and suppose that
s\leq t
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, it holds that

\displaystyle|\mu^{\gamma}_{t}(\varphi)-\mu^{\gamma}_{s}(\varphi)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|\varphi^{\prime}(x)||\gamma^{\prime}(x)|^{-1}d\mathcal{V}^{\gamma}_{\tau}+|\varphi(x)|df^{\gamma}_{\tau}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}([0,1])}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\left|\frac{d\mathcal{V}_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right|+\left|\frac{df_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\Gamma}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\|\lambda_{\Gamma}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{M(\Gamma)}\tilde{C}\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}([0,1])}|t-s|^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma}\left|\frac{d\mathcal{V}_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{df_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right|^{2}\,d\lambda_{\Gamma}\,d\tau\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\|\lambda_{\Gamma}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{M(\Gamma)}\tilde{C}\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}([0,1])}|t-s|^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right)\Psi_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}},\frac{d\mathcal{V}_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+m_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right)\Psi_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}},\frac{df_{\tau}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right)\,d\lambda_{\Gamma}\,d\tau\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda_{\Omega},\lambda_{\Gamma},b^{\Omega},b^{\Gamma},M)\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}([0,1])}|t-s|^{1/2}

where we used Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
2.9
together with the properties of
m_{\Gamma}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

The previous proposition is the main ingredient for the following compactness result.

Theorem 2.11

(Compactness)
.

Given a regular, non self-intersecting
C^{1}
-curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
and a sequence
(J_{t}^{n},V_{t}^{n},\rho_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},f_{t}^{n},(G_{t}^{0})^{n},(G_{t}^{1})^{n})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}(J_{t}^{n},\mathcal{V}_{t}^{n},\rho_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},f_{t}^{n},(\mathcal{G}^{0}_{t})^{n},(\mathcal{G}_{t}^{1})^{n})dt<\infty,

there exists
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G_{t}^{0},G_{t}^{1})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma)
such that, up to subsequences,

i)

\rho^{n}_{t}\rightharpoonup\rho_{t}
narrowly in
\Omega
for all
t\in[0,1]

ii)

\mu^{n}_{t}\rightharpoonup\mu_{t}
narrowly in
\Gamma
for all
t\in[0,1]

and

iii)

J^{n}\rightharpoonup J
narrowly in
[0,1]\times\Omega

iv)

\mathcal{V}^{n}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{V}
narrowly in
[0,1]\times\Gamma

v)

f^{n}\rightharpoonup f
narrowly in
[0,1]\times\Gamma

vi)

(\mathcal{G}^{i})^{n}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{G}^{i}
narrowly in
[0,1]\times\{\gamma(i)\}
for
i=1,2

Here, the un-subscripted quantities denote the measures defined on the product spaces given by their respective disintegration.

Proof.

We start by proving
i)
and
ii)
. First note that due the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}
and the narrow continuity of
\rho^{n}_{t}
and
\mu^{n}_{t}
inferred from Proposition
2.9
it holds that
\rho_{t}^{n}\ll\lambda_{\Omega}
and
\mu_{t}^{n}\ll\lambda_{\Gamma}
for every
t\in[0,1]
. Moreover, thanks to Proposition
2.9
it is also true that
a^{\Omega}\leq\frac{d\rho^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Omega}}\leq b^{\Omega}

\lambda_{\Omega}
-a.e. and
a^{\Gamma}\leq\frac{d\mu^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\leq b^{\Gamma}

\lambda_{\Gamma}
-a.e. for every
t\in[0,1]
and every
n
. In particular

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\sup_{t}\|\rho_{t}^{n}\|_{M(\Omega)}<\infty,\quad\sup_{n}\sup_{t}\|\mu_{t}^{n}\|_{M(\Gamma)}<\infty

(26)

and thus, by the properties of the push-forward we also have

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\sup_{t}\|(\mu_{t}^{\gamma})^{n}\|_{M([0,1])}<\infty.

(27)

Therefore, thanks to Proposition
2.10
and by applying a generalized Arzelà-Ascoli theorem (
[
9
, Proposition A.4]
) we conclude that there exists a narrowly continuous family of Borel measures
\rho_{t}\in M(\Omega)
and
\mu^{\gamma}_{t}\in M([0,1])
such that the following narrow convergence holds

\displaystyle\rho^{n}_{t}\rightharpoonup\rho_{t},\quad(\mu^{\gamma}_{t})^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu^{\gamma}_{t},\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].

(28)

Define now
\mu_{t}=\gamma_{\#}\mu^{\gamma}_{t}\in M(\Gamma)
. Moreover,
\mu_{t}^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu_{t}
for every
t\in[0,1]
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\varphi((\gamma\circ\gamma^{-1})(x))d(\mu^{n}_{t}-\mu_{t})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\gamma(s))d((\mu^{\gamma}_{t})^{n}-\mu^{\gamma}_{t})=0.

We now prove
iii)
,
iv)
,

Here, we consider the dynamic problem where we allow for an optimal placement of the preferential path

We assume initial and final data to be given by the probability measures

\Gamma\subset\Omega

in the same way, we obtain an admissible pair of initial and final data as in the case of a fixed preferential path.
Note that choosing different

Under the same assumptions of the previous section we consider the following variational problem.

Definition 4.1

(Dynamic problem for varying preferential path)
.

Given a functional
R:C^{1}([0,L];\Omega)\to{\mathbb{R}}
we consider the minimization problem

\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma\in C^{1}([0,1];\Omega)}\inf_{(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G_{t}^{0},G_{t}^{1})\in{\rm CE(\Gamma)}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}(J_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},\mathcal{G}_{t}^{0},\mathcal{G}_{t}^{1})\;dt+cR(\gamma)

(

)

for a parameter

Our goal is to choose

to satisfy the following properties:

i)

It is repulsive, meaning that it penalizes self-intersections of the curve.

ii)

It preserves regularity in the sense that curves such that
R(\gamma)<\infty
are continuously differentiable, regular and embedded and therefore parametrize a
C^{1}
-manifold.

iii)

It prevents closing of the curve at the endpoints in order for the boundary conditions to be well-defined. We could get rid of this condition if we impose the additional constraint
{\mathcal{G}}^{0}_{t}={\mathcal{G}}^{1}_{t}
. In this case, the coupling terms in the weak formulation cancel for closed paths and continuous test functions.

While different choices are possible, in the remaining part of this section we will consider the penalty

\displaystyle R(\gamma):=2^{-p}E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)-\log|\gamma(0)-\gamma(L)|+\int_{0}^{L}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\,dt.

(37)

The first summand

is called Tangent-Point energy of
\gamma

[
41

, and it is responsible for preventing the self-intersection of the curve. We will define it rigorously in the next subsection. This choice is particularly suited for numerical implementations as discussed in Subsection
1.1

Moreover, we add the additional term
-\log|\gamma(0)-\gamma(L)|
prohibiting the closedness of the curve. This is added for technical as well as for physical reasons, since we expect the optimal path not to be closed. Finally we penalize the length of
\gamma
.
The constant parameter
c>0
balances the penalty against the action functional, allowing for more interesting geometries than linear curves, which are favoured by the Tangent-Point energy.

We make the following assumptions regarding the behaviour of the action on varying curves.

Assumption 4.2
.

Assume that

1.

The mobility
m_{\Gamma}
(and consequently
\Psi_{\Gamma}
) is independent of the path
\Gamma
.

2.

\sup_{\gamma}\|\lambda_{\Gamma}\|_{M(\Gamma)}<\infty
,
\sup_{\gamma}\|\lambda_{E}\|_{M(\{\gamma(0)\}\cup\{\gamma(L)\})}<\infty
, where both supremums are taken over the set

\displaystyle\{\gamma:\gamma\in C^{1}([0,L],\Omega),\text{regular and non self-intersecting}\}.

3.

For every
\gamma^{n}\rightarrow\gamma
uniformly, it holds that
\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}\rightharpoonup\lambda_{\Gamma}
,
\lambda_{E^{n}}\rightharpoonup\lambda_{E}
.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that the first condition assumes that the mobilities on preferential paths are always the same. This is a natural assumption since we expect that preferential paths are build in the same way. We believe that more general assumptions are possible, however we do not explore them in this paper. The second and third condition are instead of purely technical nature and necessary to retain compactness and lower semicontinuity of the action functional for varying preferential paths.
To be more precise, compactness and lower semicontinuity will follow from the structure of the action functional similar to the case of fixed preferential paths. The additional assumptions then ensure that these properties are compatible with varying domains.
One particular choice of such measures can be obtained through a reference measure on the domain
[0,L]
. Then, defining
\lambda_{\Gamma}
and
\lambda_{E}
using the pushforward under any curve
\gamma\in C([0,L];\Omega)
is admissible. However, by not restricting the analysis to this specific case, we are able to include different reference measures as well.

4.1
The Tangent-Point energy for non-closed curves

In this section we recall the definition of the Tangent-Point energy
[
41
]
stating and adapting the preliminary results needed in our analysis. Given
L>0
and a curve
\gamma\in C^{1}([0,L];\Omega)
with image
\Gamma
, we denote by
r_{\rm tp}^{\gamma}(x,y)
the radius of the unique circle tangent to
x\in\Gamma
and passing through
x,y\in\Gamma
, see Figure
1
for an illustration. We use the convention that this radius is set to be zero if
x=y
, and is infinite if the vector
x-y
is parallel to the tangent of
\Gamma
in
x
. Moreover, note that
r_{\rm tp}^{\gamma}
is defined only almost everywhere due to the possible presence of self-intersections. For
p>2
the tangent point energy is defined as follows.

Definition 4.4

(Tangent-Point energy of a curve)
.

Given
\gamma\in C^{1}([0,L];\Omega)
we define its Tangent-Point energy as

\displaystyle E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)||\gamma^{\prime}(t)|}{r_{\rm tp}^{\gamma}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))^{p}}\,dsdt.

(38)

Note that this energy is independent of the parametrization and could be defined for
\Gamma
instead. However in our setting it is useful to work with an explicit parametrization. Additionally, the Tangent-Point energy is non-negative and attains its minimum for straight lines.

In
[
41
, Theorem 1.1]
it was shown that the Tangent-Point energy is repulsive, meaning that for arclength parametrized curves finite energy implies that the image of the curve is a one dimensional embedded submanifold, possibly with boundary, that has no self-intersections.
Regarding regularity,
[
7
]
has shown that for closed curves finiteness of
E^{p}_{\rm tp}
is equivalent to the finiteness of the
W^{2-\frac{1}{p},p}
-seminorm. In the next proposition we will closely follow the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
to prove such a bound for non-closed curves.

Proposition 4.5

(Energy spaces of the penalty functional)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,L]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a given arclength parametrized
C^{1}([0,L];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
curve with length
L>0
and
R(\gamma)\leq M
for a constant
M>0
.
Then
\gamma\in W^{2-\frac{1}{p},p}([0,L];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, its image is an embedded one dimensional submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with boundary and the following estimate holds

\displaystyle[\gamma^{\prime}]^{p}_{W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}([0,L];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C(p,M)

(39)

for a constant
C(p,M)>0
only depending on
p
and
M
and in particular independent of
\gamma
.

Proof.

By
[
41
, Theorem 1.1]
the image
\Gamma=\gamma([0,L])
is a one dimensional embedded submanifold that has a boundary because of
R(\gamma)\leq M
.

The additional regularity of the parametrization is shown as in
[
7
, Section 2]
or
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
using open curves instead of closed ones. We will state the proof again for completeness.
Given
\gamma\in C^{1}([0,L];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
let
0<\delta\leq L
be the biggest constant such that for all
s,t\in[0,L]
with
|s-t|\leq\delta

\displaystyle|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.

Note that, by continuity of
\gamma^{\prime}
, either
\delta=L
or there exists a pair
s^{*},t^{*}\in[0,L]
with
|s^{*}-t^{*}|=\delta
and
|\gamma^{\prime}(s^{*})-\gamma^{\prime}(t^{*})|=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
. As in the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
for
|s-t|\leq\delta
it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}|s-t||\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\leq|\left(\Pi_{\gamma(s)}-\Pi_{\gamma(t)}\right)(\gamma(s)-\gamma(t))|,

(40)

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma(\tau)}v=v-\langle v,\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)\rangle\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)

(41)

is the projection onto the normal space to
\Gamma
at
\gamma(\tau)
for
\tau\in[0,L]
.

Now, following e.g.
[
6
]
we are able to give an explicit representation of the tangent point radius as

\displaystyle r_{\rm tp}^{\gamma}(\gamma(s),\gamma(y))=\frac{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2}}{2{\rm dist}(\gamma(s)-\gamma(t),T_{\gamma(s)}\Gamma)}=\frac{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2}}{2|\Pi_{\gamma(s)}\left(\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)\right)|}.

(42)

Therefore we can estimate the energy
E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)
by

\displaystyle E^{p}_{\rm tp}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\frac{2^{p}|\Pi_{\gamma(s)}\left(\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)\right)|^{p}}{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2p}}\,dsdt

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{p}}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\frac{|\Pi_{\gamma(s)}\left(\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)\right)|^{p}}{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2p}}\,dsdt+\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\frac{|\Pi_{\gamma(t)}\left(\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)\right)|^{p}}{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2p}}\,dsdt\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{0}^{L}\int\limits_{\{|s-t|\leq\delta\}}\frac{|\Pi_{\gamma(s)}\left(\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)\right)|^{p}}{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2p}}\,dsdt+\int_{0}^{1}\int\limits_{\{|s-t|\leq\delta\}}\frac{|\Pi_{\gamma(t)}\left(\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)\right)|^{p}}{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2p}}\,dsdt\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{L}\int\limits_{\{|s-t|\leq\delta\}}\frac{|(\Pi_{\gamma(s)}-\Pi_{\gamma(t)})(\gamma(s)-\gamma(t))|^{p}}{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2p}}\,dsdt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{L}\int\limits_{\{|s-t|\leq\delta\}}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{p}|s-t|^{p}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{p}}{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|^{2p}}\,dsdt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{p}\int_{0}^{L}\int\limits_{\{|s-t|\leq\delta\}}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{p}}{|s-t|^{p}}\left(\frac{|s-t|}{|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|}\right)^{2p}\,dsdt

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{p}\int_{0}^{L}\int\limits_{\{|s-t|\leq\delta\}}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{p}}{|s-t|^{p}}\,dsdt,

(43)

where the last inequality follows because of the choice of arclength parametrization which gives
|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|\leq|s-t|
.
In conclusion we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{p}}{|s-t|^{p}}\,dsdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\{|s-t|\leq\delta\}}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{p}}{|s-t|^{p}}\,dsdt+\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\{|s-t|>\delta\}}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{p}}{|s-t|^{p}}\,dsdt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{p}E^{p}_{\rm tp}(\gamma)+\int_{0}^{L}\int\limits_{\{|s-t|>\delta\}}\frac{2}{|s-t|^{p}}\,dsdt

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{p}E^{p}_{\rm tp}(\gamma)+\frac{2L^{2}}{\delta^{p}}

(44)

implying
\gamma\in W^{2-\frac{1}{p},p}([0,L];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

In order to conclude, we need to find a uniform lower bound for
\delta>0
.
In the following, we will assume that
\delta<L
(otherwise the result is immediate) and we will show that for a suitable constant
C(\alpha)>0
the estimate

\displaystyle{}|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha)E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)^{\frac{1}{p}}|s-t|^{\alpha}

(45)

holds for every
s,t\in[0,L]
such that
|s-t|\leq\delta
where
\alpha=1-\frac{2}{p}
. This estimate would imply

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}=|\gamma^{\prime}(s^{*})-\gamma^{\prime}(t^{*})|

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha)E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)^{\frac{1}{p}}\delta^{\alpha}\leq C(\alpha)M^{\frac{1}{p}}\delta^{\alpha}

and therefore the lower bound

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2C(\alpha)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}M^{q}

with
q=\frac{1}{p\alpha}=\frac{1}{p-2}
. Together with (
44
) this gives the desired estimate (
39
).

For
s\in[0,L]
and
r>0
we denote the integral mean by

\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{r}(s)}:=\frac{1}{|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}\gamma^{\prime}(y)\,dy

for
B_{r}(s)
denoting the open ball of radius
r
centered at
s
. Note that
2r\geq|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|\geq r
for
r\in[0,L]
.
The proof of (
45
) is divided into three steps. The first step is an estimate of
\int_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{r}(s)}|\,dy
from above for
r\in(0,\frac{\delta}{2})
.
Then, we will use this bound to estimate
|\gamma^{\prime}(\cdot+\tau)-\gamma^{\prime}(\cdot)|
for
\tau<\frac{\delta}{4}
and in the last step, we extend this estimate for arbitrary
\tau\leq\delta
.

Step 1:

For any
s\in[0,L]
,
r\in(0,\frac{\delta}{2})
and a generic constant
C>0
only depending on
p
it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}|\gamma^{\prime}(y)-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{r}(s)}|\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}\left|\gamma^{\prime}(y)-\frac{1}{|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}\gamma^{\prime}(z)\,dz\right|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}|\gamma^{\prime}(y)-\gamma^{\prime}(z)|\,dydz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|^{2}}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}|\gamma^{\prime}(y)-\gamma^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\,dydz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\,\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(y)-\gamma^{\prime}(z)|^{p}}{|y-z|^{p}}\,dydz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|^{\alpha}\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we used the Hölder inequality together with the estimate (
43
) that is valid since
r\in(0,\frac{\delta}{2})
.
We thus obtain that for every
s\in[0,L]
and every
r\in(0,\frac{\delta}{2})

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)}|\gamma^{\prime}(y)-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{r}(s)}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C|B_{r}(s)\cap(0,L)|^{\alpha+1}\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(46)

It remains to relate the left-hand side of (
46
) to the supremum norm using standard Campanato estimates.

Step 2:
As
\gamma\in C^{1}([0,L];{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, every
s\in[0,L]
is a Lebesgue point of
\gamma^{\prime}
. Therefore, for any
s,t\in[0,L]
with
R:=|s-t|<\frac{\delta}{4}
we obtain

\displaystyle|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{-k}}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{1-k}}(s)}+\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{1-k}}(t)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{-k}}(t)}\right)+\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left|\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{-k}}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{1-k}}(s)}\right|+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left|\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{-k}}(t)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{1-k}}(t)}\right|+\left|\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(t)}\right|.

(47)

Moreover, by (
46
) it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{2R}(s)\cap B_{2R}(t)\cap(0,L)|}\left|\int\limits_{B_{2R}(s)\cap B_{2R}(t)\cap(0,L)}\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}(z)+\gamma^{\prime}(z)-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(t)}\,dz\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{2R}(s)\cap B_{2R}(t)\cap(0,L)|}\left(\int\limits_{B_{2R}(s)\cap(0,L)}\left|\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}(z)\right|\,dz+\int\limits_{B_{2R}(t)\cap(0,L)}\left|\gamma^{\prime}(z)-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(t)}\right|\,dz\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{|B_{2R}(s)\cap B_{2R}(t)\cap(0,L)|}\left(|B_{2R}(s)\cap(0,L)|^{\alpha+1}+|B_{2R}(t)\cap(0,L)|^{\alpha+1}\right)\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

As
|B_{2R}(s)\cap(0,L)|\leq 4R=4|s-t|
and
|B_{2R}(s)\cap B_{2R}(t)\cap(0,L)|\geq 2R=2|s-t|
we get

\displaystyle\left|\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{2R}(t)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2\alpha+2}C|s-t|^{\alpha}\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(48)

Similarly we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{-k}}(s)}-\gamma^{\prime}_{B_{R2^{1-k}}(s)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|B_{R2^{-k}}(s)\cap(0,L)|}\left(|B_{R2^{-k}}(s)\cap(0,L)|^{\alpha+1}+|B_{R2^{1-k}}(s)\cap(0,L)|^{\alpha+1}\right)\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\alpha+1}(1+2^{\alpha+1})C\left(R2^{-k}\right)^{\alpha}\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(49)

for
R<\frac{\delta}{4}
since
|B_{R2^{1-k}}(s)\cap(0,L)|\leq R2^{1-k}
and
|B_{R2^{-k}}(s)\cap(0,L)|\geq R2^{-k}
in this case.
Together, (
47
), (
48
) and (
49
) lead to

\displaystyle|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\leq\left(2^{\alpha+1}(1+2^{\alpha+1})\sum\limits_{k=0}^{+\infty}2^{-\alpha k}+2^{2\alpha+2}\right)C|s-t|^{\alpha}\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\tilde{C}|s-t|^{\alpha}\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(50)

for a constant
\tilde{C}>0
only depending on
\alpha
.

Step 3:
Suppose now that
s,t\in[0,L]
are such that
\delta\geq|s-t|\geq\frac{\delta}{4}
. Then we can find a partition
s=:s_{0}<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{N}:=t
with
|s_{k}-s_{k-1}|<\frac{\delta}{4}
. Applying (
50
) and using the triangle inequality and Jensen’s inequality we get

\displaystyle|\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\gamma^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}|s_{k}-s_{k-1}|^{\alpha}\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\tilde{C}N^{1-\alpha}|s-t|^{\alpha}\left(E_{\rm tp}^{p}(\gamma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that, since
|s-t|\leq\delta
,
N
is bounded from above independent of
\gamma
(e.g. taking
N=5
with points defined as
s_{k}=s+(t-s)\frac{k}{5}
), thus implying a uniform bound and concluding the proof.
∎

4.2
Existence of minimizers for varying preferential path

We first generalize the compactness presented in Theorem
2.11
and the lower semicontinuity result to sequences of measures defined on varying paths. This is the content of the next proposition.

Proposition 4.6

(Compactness for varying paths)
.

Given
\gamma^{n},\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\Omega
regular, non self-intersecting
C^{1}
-curves and a sequence
(J_{t}^{n},V_{t}^{n},\rho_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},f_{t}^{n},(G_{t}^{0})^{n},(G_{t}^{1})^{n})\in{\rm CE}(\Gamma^{n})
such that
\gamma^{n}\rightarrow\gamma
uniformly and

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma^{n}}(J_{t}^{n},\mathcal{V}_{t}^{n},\rho_{t}^{n},\mu_{t}^{n},f_{t}^{n},(\mathcal{G}^{0}_{t})^{n},(\mathcal{G}_{t}^{1})^{n})dt<\infty,

there exists
(J_{t},V_{t},\rho_{t},\mu_{t},f_{t},G_{t}^{0},G_{t}^{1})\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)
such that, up to subsequences,

i)

\rho^{n}_{t}\rightharpoonup\rho_{t}
narrowly in
\Omega
, for every
t\in[0,1]

ii)

\bar{\mu}^{n}_{t}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mu}_{t}
narrowly in
\Gamma
, for every
t\in[0,1]

and

iii)

J^{n}\rightharpoonup J\text{ narrowly in }[0,1]\times\Omega

iv)

\bar{\mathcal{V}}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mathcal{V}}\text{ narrowly in }[0,1]\times\Omega

v)

\bar{f}^{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{f}\text{ narrowly in }[0,1]\times\Omega

vi)

(\bar{\mathcal{G}}^{i})^{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mathcal{G}}^{i}\text{ narrowly in }[0,1]\times\Omega
for
i=1,2
.

As before, the un-subscripted quantities denote the measures defined on the product spaces given by their respective disintegration.

Proof.

Since it does not depend on
\Gamma
, the proof of
i)
and
iii)
is similar to the case of fixed preferential paths. We now prove
ii)
. From the equiboundedness of the energy we can assume (up to extracting a subsequence) that
\mu^{n}_{t}\ll\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}
and thanks to Proposition
2.9
and Assumption
4.2
it holds that
0\leq\frac{d\mu^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}}\leq b^{\Gamma}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
t\in[0,1]
. In particular, from Assumption
4.2
we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\|\bar{\mu}_{t}^{n}\|_{M(\Omega)}<\infty

(51)

independent of
n
. Moreover, applying Proposition
2.10
implies

\displaystyle\|\mu_{t}^{\gamma^{n}}-\mu_{s}^{\gamma^{n}}\|_{C^{1}([0,1])^{*}}\leq C|t-s|^{1/2}

(52)

where the constant
C
can be chosen independent of
n
by Assumption
4.2
.
Therefore, thanks to Proposition
2.9
and by applying a generalized Arzelà-Ascoli theorem (
[
9
, Proposition A.4]
) we conclude that there exists a narrowly continuous family of Borel measures
\mu^{*}_{t}\in M([0,1])
such that

\displaystyle(\mu^{n}_{t})^{\gamma^{n}}\rightharpoonup\mu^{*}_{t},\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].

(53)

Define now
\mu_{t}=\gamma_{\#}\mu^{*}_{t}\in M(\Gamma)
and note that
\mu_{t}^{\gamma}=\mu^{*}_{t}
. Moreover,
\mu_{t}^{n}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Gamma_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu_{t}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Gamma
for every
t\in[0,1]
. Indeed, for every
\varphi\in C(\Omega)
it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{n}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{n}}\varphi(x)d\mu^{n}_{t}-\int_{\Gamma}\varphi(x)d\mu_{t}\right|=\lim_{n}\left|\int_{\Gamma}\varphi((\gamma\circ\gamma^{-1})(x))d\mu_{t}-\int_{\Gamma^{n}}\varphi((\gamma^{n}\circ(\gamma^{n})^{-1})(x))d\mu^{n}_{t}\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\gamma(s))d(\mu^{\gamma}_{t})-\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\gamma^{n}(s))d(\mu_{t}^{n})^{\gamma^{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\gamma(s))d(\mu^{\gamma}_{t})-\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\gamma(s))d(\mu_{t}^{n})^{\gamma^{n}}\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{1}|\varphi(\gamma(s))-\varphi(\gamma^{n}(s))|d(\mu_{t}^{n})^{\gamma^{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\gamma(s))d(\mu^{\gamma}_{t})-\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(\gamma(s))d(\mu_{t}^{n})^{\gamma^{n}}\right|+\sup_{s\in[0,1]}|\varphi(\gamma(s))-\varphi(\gamma^{n}(s))|\|(\mu_{t}^{n})^{\gamma^{n}}\|_{M([0,1])}=0

due to (
53
), the uniform convergence of
\gamma^{n}
to
\gamma
and the uniform continuity of
\varphi
.
We now prove
iv)
. Note that
|\mathcal{V}_{t}^{n}|\ll\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}
for almost every
t\in[0,1]
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{X_{\Gamma^{n}}}\left|\frac{d\mathcal{V}^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}}\right|d\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X_{\Gamma^{n}}}\sqrt{m_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}}\right)}\sqrt{\Psi_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}},\frac{d\mathcal{V}^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}}\right)}d\lambda_{\Gamma}^{n}\,dt

(54)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{X_{\Gamma^{n}}}m_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}^{n}}\right)d\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X_{\Gamma}}\Psi_{\Gamma}\left(\frac{d\mu^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma}^{n}},\frac{d\mathcal{V}^{n}_{t}}{d\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}}\right)d\lambda_{\Gamma^{n}}dt\right)^{1/2}.

(55)

Due to Assumption

4.3
The case of linear mobilities and beyond

Despite the focus on general concave mobility functions with bounded domain, it is possible to extend the results of this paper to linear mobilities, giving action functionals

\displaystyle\Psi_{\rm linear}(z,w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{|w|^{2}}{z}&z\neq 0\\
0&w=0\text{ and }z=0\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.

and thus recovering traditional Optimal Transport dynamics both on
\Omega
and
\Gamma
. Since the adaptation is straightforward and many arguments follow from the estimates provided in
[
33
]
, we decide to discuss it only briefly in this section.
First, we remark that an analogue of Proposition
2.10
follows by similar estimates and adapting
[
33
, Proposition 3.5]
. Such estimates lead to suitable compactness (adapting Theorem
2.11
) and thus the existence of minimizers for a fixed preferential path.
Similar considerations hold for the existence of minimizers in the case of varying paths.
Interestingly, the case of linear mobilities allows to prove finiteness of the infimum using Wasserstein and Fischer-Rao geodesics as in
[
33
, Lemma 3.7]
. Instead, as it is customary for general mobilities
[
31
]
, this is an additional assumption in our results, both for fixed and varying preferential paths.

We are also confident that similar approaches following
[
20
,
14
]
would allow to extend the results of this paper to the case of non-decreasing mobilities with unbounded domain recovering, for instance, action functionals for the following form:

\displaystyle\Psi_{\rm concave}(z,w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{|w|^{2}}{z^{\alpha}}&z\neq 0\\
0&w=0\text{ and }z=0\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.

for

. Here one can rely on the compactness estimates and lower semicontinuity results of
[
20
]
with the only verification needed being at the level of the compactness for varying preferential paths.

A.1
Augmented Lagrangian formulation

Following

]
, we can rewrite the minimization problem (

Let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}(\phi_{t},\phi_{t}^{1d})

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\Omega}\left[\phi_{1}\,d\mu_{1}-\phi_{0}\,d\mu_{0}\right]-\int_{\Gamma}\left[\phi^{1d}_{1}\,d\rho_{1}-\phi^{1d}_{0}\,d\rho_{0}\right],

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{t},\rho_{t}\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}\,d\rho_{t}\,dt\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\phi^{1d}_{t},\mu_{t}\rangle_{\Gamma}:=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma}\phi^{1d}_{t}\,d\mu_{t}\,dt

where
\phi_{t}:[0,1]\times\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
\phi^{1d}_{t}:[0,1]\times\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

Defining
\phi_{t}
and
\phi_{t}^{1d}
as test functions in Definition
2.2
allows to rewrite (
BB2
) as the saddle-point problem

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{(\rho_{t},J_{t},\mu_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t},f_{t})\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm adm}(\Gamma)}\sup\limits_{(\phi_{t},\phi^{1d}_{t})}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}^{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(\rho_{t},J_{t},\mu_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t},f_{t})\,dt-{\mathcal{G}}(\phi_{t},\phi^{1d}_{t})

\displaystyle-\langle(\partial_{t}\phi_{t},\nabla\phi_{t}),(\rho_{t},J_{t})\rangle_{\Omega}-\langle(\partial_{t}\phi^{1d}_{t},\nabla\phi^{1d}_{t},\phi^{1d}_{t}-\phi_{t}\circ\gamma),(\mu_{t},{\mathcal{V}_{t}},f_{t})\rangle_{\Gamma}.

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{u_\Omega, v_\Omega}{u_\Gamma,v_\Gamma, w_\Gamma}}\langle(u^{*}_{\Omega},v^{*}_{\Omega}),(u_{\Omega},v_{\Omega})\rangle+\langle(u^{*}_{\Gamma},v^{*}_{\Gamma},w^{*}_{\Gamma}),(u_{\Gamma},v_{\Gamma},w_{\Gamma})\rangle

holds, where
\left(F^{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\right)^{*}
is the convex conjugate of

. Substituting this expression into our objective functional leads to the formulation

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{(\rho_{t},J_{t},\mu_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t},f_{t})}\inf\limits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{(\phi_t, \phi_t^{1d}),}{(\rho_t^*, J_t^*, \mu_t^*, \mathcal{V}_t^*, f_t^*)}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int\left(F^{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\right)^{*}(\rho_{t}^{*},J_{t}^{*},\mu_{t}^{*},\mathcal{V}_{t}^{*},f_{t}^{*})\,dxdt+{\mathcal{G}}(\phi_{t},\phi_{t}^{1d})

\displaystyle+\langle(\partial_{t}\phi_{t}-\rho_{t}^{*},\nabla\phi_{t}-J_{t}^{*}),(\rho_{t},J_{t})\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle+\langle(\partial_{t}\phi^{1d}_{t}-\mu_{t}^{*},\nabla\phi_{t}^{1d}-\mathcal{V}_{t}^{*},\phi_{t}^{1d}-\phi_{t}\circ\gamma-f_{t}^{*}),(\mu_{t},\mathcal{V}_{t},f_{t})\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{u_{\Omega},v_{\Omega},u_{\Gamma},v_{\Gamma},w_{\Gamma}}\langle u^{*}_{\Omega},u_{\Omega}\rangle+\langle v^{*}_{\Omega},v_{\Omega}\rangle+\langle u^{*}_{\Gamma},u_{\Gamma}\rangle+\langle v^{*}_{\Gamma},v_{\Gamma}\rangle+\langle w^{*}_{\Gamma},w_{\Gamma}\rangle-F^{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(u_{\Omega},v_{\Omega},u_{\Gamma},v_{\Gamma},w_{\Gamma})

A.2

In the classical ALG2 method from
[
23
]
, the aim is to solve a saddle-point problem. Here, the optimization is decoupled and in each step the variables are considered separately. As introduced in Section
5
, there are three main steps to this algorithm. In what follows, we give a detailed explanation of each of these steps that result in Algorithm
1
. Throughout,
(\phi_{m},\phi^{1d}_{m}),(\rho^{*}_{m},J^{*}_{m},\mu^{*}_{m},\mathcal{V}^{*}_{m},f^{*}_{m})
and
(\rho_{m},J_{m},\mu_{m},\mathcal{V}_{m},f_{m})
denote the approximate solutions after
m

Step 1:

Update
(\phi_{t},\phi_{t}^{1d})
.

For this update, we aim at solving

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{(\phi_{m+1},\phi^{1d}_{m+1})}L^{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(\rho_{m},J_{m},\mu_{m},\mathcal{V}_{m},f_{m},\phi_{m+1},\phi^{1d}_{m+1},\rho^{*}_{m},J^{*}_{m},\mu^{*}_{m},\mathcal{V}^{*}_{m},f^{*}_{m}).

In particular, the first variation with respect to
\phi_{m+1}
and
\phi^{1d}_{m+1}
needs to be zero as necessary condition, leading to the following system of equations

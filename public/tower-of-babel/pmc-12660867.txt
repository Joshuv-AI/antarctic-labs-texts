Influence of leadership on the adoption of circular and digital business models using PLS-SEM analysis
Authors: Dalia Sanchez, Analí Sulca, Liz Pacheco-Pumaleque
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn a business environment characterised by growing pressure towards sustainability and digital transformation, leadership emerges as a determining factor in the adoption of sustainable, technology-driven business models. This study analyses how leadership influences the adoption of circular business models and the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in companies in Metropolitan Lima. Using a quantitative approach, a questionnaire was administered to a sample of 146 companies, and partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) was used to process the data using Smart PLS software. The findings show that leadership, understood as the ability to mobilise the organisation towards strategic change objectives, promotes the adoption of circular practices. Likewise, leadership drives the integration of AI-based solutions by promoting adaptability and openness to digital transformation. The study concludes that the ability of leaders to make sound decisions, inspire a shared vision and manage resistance to change is crucial to transforming traditional business models towards more resilient, circular and digitised approaches.Keywords: Circular business models, Artificial intelligence, Leadership, B Corp, Business managementSubject terms: Information technology, Mathematics and computing, Human behaviour

Introduction

The challenges of achieving a sustainable and efficient future require addressing complex and interconnected problems that directly impact the performance of organisations1. Among these, one of the most significant is the underutilisation of resources and management tools, which limits companies’ ability to generate value and remain competitive. Given this scenario, the circular economy approach has become increasingly important, as it seeks to transform traditional production and consumption models into more sustainable systems. However, its implementation continues to be the subject of debate, especially with regard to the need for innovative strategies that reinforce corporate sustainability2.

In this context, artificial intelligence (AI) is emerging as a key tool for accelerating the transition to circular business models, enabling more agile, accurate and data-driven management. Recent studies show that AI helps to optimise processes, reduce waste and anticipate failures through real-time monitoring and predictive maintenance3. In addition, it facilitates the creation of innovative solutions that address specific circular challenges, boosting productivity and the use of technological resources4. However, its adoption involves significant organisational and ethical challenges, as well as a profound restructuring of internal processes5,6.

Despite growing global interest, there remains a research gap regarding how business leadership influences the integration of artificial intelligence into circular business models, particularly in Latin American contexts. Most previous studies have focused on developed economies or large international corporations7, leaving aside realities such as that of Metropolitan Lima, where companies face additional constraints related to resources, organisational culture and resistance to change8.

Therefore, this study seeks to address this gap by exploring the relationship between leadership, the circular economy, and artificial intelligence in companies in Metropolitan Lima during 2024. The novelty of this work lies in offering a contextualised analysis of how leadership capabilities can facilitate the adoption of disruptive technologies to optimise resource management and move towards a sustainable model. Thus, the main contribution of this research is to provide empirical evidence on the role of leadership as a catalyst for innovation and sustainability, providing new perspectives for both academia and professionals interested in business transformation towards circularity. Moreover, this research provides an additional contribution by optimising resources with artificial intelligence in a more efficient manner creating a substantial impact on the circular business model. This contribution facilitates clearer and more precise management, while enabling leaders to make technology-supported decisions. In addition, it addresses a comprehensive lens for integrated intelligence capability, process automation, augmentation capabilities and the ecosystem.

Research problem

Resource waste is a predominant factor in slowing down a company’s vision, due to its impact on wasting resources before they meet their use cycle. One of the causes of these factors is inefficient leadership management, as there is inadequate decision making, inefficient talent management and an unattractive organisational culture, leading to a failure to measure the output of products. Given this, estimating the optimal and exact production needed to satisfy demand is complex and on many occasions there is a surplus for different reasons that is not finally consumed by a customer, but ends up being discarded9. This is how the idea of implementing and relating technology such as artificial intelligence to mitigate the major problems that arise, such as optimising the reuse of resources and thus generating more efficient processes for business models.

The following research questions are therefore posed:

RQ1: Is there a relationship between leadership and the adoption of artificial intelligence in companies in metropolitan Lima in 2024?

RQ2: Is there a relationship between leadership and the adoption of circular business model in companies in metropolitan Lima in 2024?

RQ3: Is there a relationship between the circular business model and the adoption of artificial intelligence in companies in metropolitan Lima in 2024?

Literature review

Artificial intelligence in business models

Artificial intelligence (AI) is defined as the set of technologies that enable machines to simulate human cognitive processes, such as learning, reasoning and data-based decision-making3. In the business context, AI has become a fundamental tool for digital transformation, integrating with emerging technologies such as cloud computing, blockchain and big data within the framework of Industry 4.010.

Beyond its technical applications, AI offers strategic advantages for organisations by optimising processes, anticipating failures, reducing waste and facilitating innovation. From the perspective of Resource Based View (RBV) Theory11, AI can be considered a valuable, rare and difficult-to-imitate resource that provides companies with sustainable competitive advantages, especially when combined with appropriate organisational capabilities.

In addition, its implementation allows organisations to consolidate real-time operational data for strategic planning, improve productivity, and generate new business models based on efficiency and sustainability. However, the effective adoption of AI does not depend solely on technology, but also on the human and organisational capabilities that enable its transformative potential to be harnessed.

Leadership and technology adoption

Leadership is a determining factor for the successful implementation of technological innovations in organisations.12 defines it as the set of behaviours that allow a leader to influence the behaviour of individuals and teams. In today’s digital context, leaders must not only lead, but also foster adaptability and confidence in environments characterised by constant change.

Transformational Leadership Theory offers a useful framework for understanding how leaders drive the adoption of new technologies13. This approach emphasises the ability to inspire, motivate and guide employees towards common goals, while promoting commitment to innovation and sustainability. From this perspective, transformational leadership is key to integrating artificial intelligence, as it fosters an open mindset towards change, continuous learning and collaboration.

Several studies highlight that leadership skills are essential to realising the commercial value of AI, as managers not only communicate the importance of its implementation but also ensure that staff understand and use the technology effectively14. In addition, strong leadership allows for a balance between ethical and strategic considerations, creating an organisational environment in which AI becomes a resource for improving operational efficiency, decision-making, and the customer and employee experience.

Circular business models (CBM)

A circular business model (CBM) is conceived as a business strategy that seeks to generate, capture and deliver value by optimising the use of resources, minimising waste and encouraging reuse, repair and recycling15. Its main objective is to extend the life cycle of products and components through sustainable practices that close material flows, contributing to a more efficient and environmentally friendly economy.

The Circular Economy Theory provides the conceptual framework that underpins CBMs, emphasising that the transition from a linear to a circular model requires not only technological changes, but also organisational and cultural ones16. In this sense, CBMs represent an opportunity for companies to integrate sustainability into the core of their operations, fostering innovation and resilience in the face of changing economic contexts.

Previous studies show that the implementation of CBMs promotes business competitiveness while contributing to sustainable development goals17. However, their adoption involves overcoming barriers such as lack of infrastructure, resistance to organisational change and the need for strong leadership to drive transformation. These challenges show that circularity cannot be achieved through technical advances alone, but requires a strategic alignment of human and technological capabilities.

Integration of AI, leadership, and circular models

The integration of artificial intelligence into circular business models depends not only on technological infrastructure, but also on leadership capacity to guide organisational transformation. Recent research highlights that AI can optimise operational efficiency, anticipate failures, reduce waste and generate innovations in resource management18. However, these benefits only fully materialise when leaders succeed in aligning teams with the strategic vision and promoting an organisational culture that is open to change19.

In this sense, transformational leadership stands out as a key facilitator for integrating AI into the circular economy. The ability to inspire, communicate effectively and manage resistance to change enables organisations to adopt disruptive technologies in a more agile and sustainable manner14. This combination not only contributes to greater competitiveness, but also strengthens the commitment to sustainability, generating economic, social and environmental value.

Despite the progress observed in Europe and Asia, the literature reveals a gap in Latin American contexts. Few studies have addressed how leadership influences the integration of AI into circular business models in emerging economies, where structural and cultural challenges are often more complex1. Therefore, this research aims to analyse this relationship in the context of Metropolitan Lima in 2024, providing empirical evidence that contributes to both theory and business practice.

Background

Several recent studies have explored the interaction between artificial intelligence, leadership, and sustainability in international contexts.20 demonstrated in Vietnamese manufacturing SMEs that the adoption of AI requires resilient leadership to facilitate organisational change and promote sustainable practices. Similarly,21 identified the role of business ecosystems and innovative financing in facilitating the transition to AI-supported circular models.

In Europe,22 highlighted that combining digital tools with proactive leadership enhances sustainable product management, while23 emphasised the need for organisational skills that drive more productive and sustainable employment.3 and14 reinforced this view, demonstrating that AI contributes to efficiency and innovation in circular models provided there is a solid infrastructure and strategic leadership.

However, most of this research is conducted in European and Asian contexts. As1 point out, there is still little empirical evidence in emerging economies, which raises a research gap in the Latin American context. This gap justifies the need to analyse how leadership can facilitate the integration of AI into circular models in companies in Metropolitan Lima during 2024.

Proposed model and hypotheses

H1

Leadership influences the adoption of the circular business model

Leadership has been identified as a critical factor for organisational change, especially in transitions towards sustainability. According to24, leaders play a fundamental role in aligning the principles of the circular economy with organisational strategies, providing a vision that connects environmental objectives with long-term business competitiveness. End consumers’ trust and commitment to circular practices are closely associated with leadership behaviours that promote transparency and accountability25. By setting clear objectives and mobilising resources, leaders facilitate the integration of circular principles into business models, encouraging stakeholder participation and legitimising organisational efforts towards circularity.

H2

Leadership influences the adoption of artificial intelligence

Leadership also shapes the organisational climate necessary for the adoption of disruptive technologies such as AI.26 argues that leaders must adjust their roles to effectively manage the interaction between employees and AI systems, ensuring compliance with ethical standards while leveraging individual capabilities. Similarly,27 argue that it is up to leaders to guide critical reflection on algorithm-driven decisions, balancing efficiency with broader ethical and social implications.28 further emphasise that strategic competencies in digital contexts are indispensable for leaders to facilitate the effective adoption of AI. This evidence suggests that leadership is not merely supportive, but fundamental to the successful assimilation of AI into organisational processes.

H3

The circular business model influences the adoption of artificial intelligence

The literature also shows strong synergies between CBMs and AI.29 show that circular business frameworks drive resource optimisation and value creation, where AI is instrumental in identifying patterns and opportunities for efficiency.30 argue that the innovative capacity of CBMs to reuse and revalue materials is complemented by AI, which optimises decision-making through real-time data analysis. Similarly,31 point out that the adoption of circular economy principles provides competitive advantages by integrating sustainability into the value proposition. In turn,32 demonstrate that AI facilitates the detection of fraudulent activities and the improvement of internal controls, extending the benefits of CBMs (Fig. 1).

Materials and methods

This study uses a quantitative, correlational, and cross-sectional approach to examine the associations between leadership, circular business models (CBMs), and the adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) in companies in Metropolitan Lima during the year 2024. Data collection was carried out using a structured questionnaire, designed in two sections: the first to collect sociodemographic information from participants, and the second consisting of items related to the study variables, measured using Likert scales previously validated in prior research.

Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) was used for data analysis. The choice of this technique was based on several methodological considerations. First, PLS-SEM is particularly suitable for exploratory and predictive studies, as it allows for the simultaneous evaluation of complex relationships between latent variables and the observation of direct, indirect, and mediating effects with moderate sample sizes. Furthermore, unlike CB-SEM, PLS-SEM does not require strict assumptions of multivariate normality and is more robust in contexts where measurement scales combine formative and reflective constructs, as is the case in this research33.

The decision to apply a cross-sectional design is based on the need to capture a representative sample of the current state of business practices around leadership, circularity, and AI in Metropolitan Lima. This design allows us to identify correlations and trends in the context of 2024, a key period of technological transition for the business sector in the country. Although a longitudinal design could provide evidence on the evolution of these variables over time, the scope of this study focuses on offering an initial diagnosis that will serve as a basis for future longitudinal research.

With regard to Common Method Variance (CMV), the inherent risk of bias arising from the use of self-administered questionnaires was recognised. To mitigate this potential bias, various strategies were incorporated, such as the formulation of clear and specific items, ensuring the anonymity of responses, and the inclusion of previously validated scales that reduce the variance attributable to the common method.

Sample

In this study, information was collected through a questionnaire that was distributed in the third quarter of 2024 to a sample of 146 business leaders in metropolitan Lima; the participants were managers, assistant managers, team leaders, supervisors and coordinators of companies with a turnover of more than 100 million dollars a year, with more than 2000 workers. Also, they have been operating for more than 12 months, are for-profit entities and operate in a competitive market. These were chosen through non-probability sampling based on the convenience of the study; according to34 this technique is accessible to the researcher, as individuals are selected based on non-random criteria and not all individuals are likely to be included.

Regarding sample size, although some methodological references suggest large samples for SEM applications, the use of Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) is particularly suitable for exploratory research and can provide robust results even with relatively small samples. Following the “10 times rule” and considering that the model tested includes three relationships, the minimum required sample was 59 observations, which is exceeded in this study. Furthermore, recent literature provides strategies for addressing small-sample limitations, showing that PLS-SEM remains appropriate and reliable in these contexts when the model complexity and data characteristics are considered35.

The survey was conducted using Google Forms. The first section asked participants for information on their sociodemographic characteristics, which was used to compile Table 2. Data processing and instruments

To examine the results obtained from the instrument, a factor analysis was used to verify the proposed causal relationships, using the statistical software SmartPLS 4. In this way, the convergent validity, discriminant validity, and reliability of the constructs are assessed, and the hypotheses are subsequently tested to interpret the results. This process will allow the development of models that help define the role of leadership in the circular business model with artificial intelligence.

All methods applied in this research were conducted in accordance with ethical guidelines and regulations for social science studies. Although no experimental procedures were carried out and no human tissue samples were used, the study involved the participation of individuals through the application of an anonymous questionnaire. For this reason, ethical approval was sought to ensure compliance with fundamental principles such as participant confidentiality, informed consent and the overall ethical conduct of the study. Discussion

The results of this study show that leadership plays a central role in the adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) and the transition to circular business models (CBM), reinforcing the idea that strategic management is a key catalyst for organisational innovation. These findings confirm and expand on previous evidence from1, who point out that integrating organisational strategies with digital technologies maximises the benefits of the circular economy by optimising resources and reducing environmental impacts. In line with this, our research provides specific evidence from a Latin American context, where the adoption of AI faces structural and cultural limitations, making the role of leadership in facilitating change processes even more relevant38.

Likewise, the results coincide with those of3, who highlight the importance of robust AI infrastructures, accompanied by effective data management and interaction between technical and non-technical capabilities. This study contributes to broadening this perspective by demonstrating that, in addition to technological resources, it is visionary leadership that enables the full exploitation of the advantages of AI to achieve circular value.

Similarly, our findings are in line with those of14, who emphasise that the predictive capabilities of AI improve productivity and reduce costs and waste. However, our research adds an important nuance: the effectiveness of AI in circular business environments depends not only on the availability of these technological capabilities, but also on the ability of leadership to articulate them within a strategy that balances environmental sustainability with organisational profitability.

In this sense, the main contribution of this work lies in showing that the relationship between AI and MNCs cannot be fully understood without considering the mediating role of leadership, which acts as a bridge that translates technological capabilities into tangible organisational results. This contribution is especially valuable for emerging contexts, where infrastructure and human capital challenges require leaders capable of anticipating change, managing internal resistance, and fostering a long-term mindset that integrates profitability, sustainability, and social equity.

Conclusions

This research identified that the integration of artificial intelligence and circular business models within organizations is strongly influenced by leadership, which emphasizes the importance of developing managerial skills aligned with digital transformation and business sustainability.

From this perspective, these results emphasize the need to strengthen organizational leadership to promote sustainability strategies and the adoption of advanced technologies. Furthermore, the impact of age on the relationship between leadership and circular business models suggests that organizations develop inclusive strategies that enable an intergenerational transition toward more sustainable models.

Leadership and technology are fundamental to the advancement of successful circular business models. It emphasizes that leaders are not only responsible for managing AI implementation but also for motivating their teams to evolve toward more sustainable and circular practices. The research affirms that leadership enables companies to address the challenges of the transition to a circular economy, optimizing resources, reducing environmental impacts, and establishing creative business opportunities. Ultimately, the research proves that the relationship between leadership, artificial intelligence, and circular business models takes on a strategic importance for companies in the current environment. If leaders adopt a proactive and ethical stance in the incorporation of AI, they can support a shift that favors innovation and sustainability. Furthermore, it proves that the shift toward circular models depends on leaders’ ability to position their organizations within a culture of cooperation and inclusion, thereby adapting to fluctuating market dynamics. Consequently, it is suggested that organizations strengthen leadership development as part of their comprehensive strategy, positioning themselves to take advantage of the opportunities offered by the circular and technological economy for the benefit of sustainable development.

Limitations

This research has certain limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. Firstly, the sample consisted mainly of companies with specific characteristics within Metropolitan Lima, which could limit the generalisation of the findings to smaller organisations or less conventional sectors. Furthermore, the difficulty in accessing senior managers of large companies restricted the possibility of delving deeper into strategic decision-making perspectives, which would have enriched the analysis on the adoption of leadership, artificial intelligence and circular business models. These limitations suggest the need for future studies that incorporate more heterogeneous samples, include different company sizes and productive sectors, and achieve greater access to the vision of strategic decision-makers. In this way, it will be possible to contrast and expand the external validity of the results obtained in the present study.

Practical implications

The findings of this study offer significant and novel insights for organisational management in companies seeking to integrate circular business models with artificial intelligence. First, it shows that leadership not only facilitates the transition to sustainable practices but also acts as an indispensable mediator that translates technological capabilities into tangible organisational benefits. This result adds a nuance that has been little explored in previous literature, demonstrating that strategic and visionary leadership is the factor that enables companies to overcome the barriers associated with the adoption of AI in emerging contexts.

In practical terms, the results suggest that organisations should invest in leadership development programmes that combine technological and sustainable skills, with an emphasis on leaders’ ability to interpret AI analysis, manage internal resistance and align innovation with sustainability and profitability objectives. It is also recommended that senior management implement continuous AI training structures that are not limited to the technical area, but also include strengthening strategic vision, ethics in data use, and change management. These measures can lead to more informed decisions, resource optimisation, and a substantial improvement in operational efficiency.

Finally, this study highlights that, in the case of CBMs, the effective adoption of AI under skilled leadership contributes to improving corporate reputation, attracting talent committed to sustainability, and strengthening organisational resilience in the face of regulatory or market changes. In this way, companies not only achieve immediate competitive advantages but also position themselves as leaders in the transition to more sustainable and innovative economies.

Future research

It is recommended that future research implement a more targeted sampling approach for a single industry sector. It is also recommended that mixed methods be used, integrating both qualitative and quantitative data, providing a more complete and accurate view of the dynamic relationships between leadership, artificial intelligence, and circular business models. This will not only enrich the research but also allow for the identification of best practices and strategies specific to various organizations, encouraging other companies to implement this method in other countries.

Two-point tree-level string amplitudes as AdS transition amplitudes
Pongwit Srisangyingcharoen, Jongruk Pukdee

Abstract
We compute the two-point open string and closed string amplitudes at tree level and show that, in a ’t Hooft-like limit, they take a form structurally analogous to boundary-to-boundary transition amplitudes of a scalar field in Euclidean AdS space. Interestingly, while both amplitudes yield equivalent expressions, the associated ’t Hooft couplings are defined by different worldsheet curvatures—geodesic for the disk and Gaussian for the sphere—suggesting a possible geometric aspect of open/closed string duality.

Introduction

An essential framework for exploring the interplay between gravity and quantum field theory is the AdS/CFT correspondence
[
1
]
. The idea that quantum gravity may be fundamentally holographic was first inspired by the thermodynamic properties of black holes, particularly the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy formula
[
2
,
3
]
. The AdS/CFT correspondence offers the first concrete realization of this holographic principle within string theory
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
. Beyond its conceptual significance, the correspondence has become a powerful computational tool for probing strongly coupled, non-perturbative regimes of quantum field theories. See
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
for a comprehensive review.

A particularly intriguing perspective on this duality was presented by Gopakumar
[
9
]
. In his work, he showed that one-loop planar correlation functions in free field theories can be reorganized into a form that mimics field amplitudes in AdS space. This was accomplished by expressing Feynman diagrams as integrals over Schwinger parameters, which naturally matched with the bulk-to-boundary propagators of AdS scalar fields.

Motivated by this insight, we investigate whether similar structures arise directly from open string amplitudes in flat space, without appealing to a field theory limit. In particular, we analyze the two-point open string amplitude at tree level and study whether, in a suitable limit, it can be cast into a form structurally resembling an AdS boundary-to-boundary transition amplitude. Our goal is to understand if and how open string correlators, under appropriate parametrization and scaling, reveal an intrinsic connection to propagators in AdS space—potentially offering a new perspective on the emergence of holographic dualities.

AdS Scalar propagator and transition amplitude

In this section, we would like to exhibit the bulk-to-boundary propagators in
\text{AdS}_{d+1}
in a Schwinger representation for a massive scalar field. It is convenient to work in the Poincaré coordinates of Euclidean
\text{AdS}_{d+1}
,

ds^{2}=\frac{d\vec{z}^{2}+dz_{0}^{2}}{z_{0}^{2}}.

(1)

Let us consider the Euclidean action

S[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{d}zdz_{0}\sqrt{g}\left(g^{MN}\partial_{M}\phi(z)\partial_{N}\phi(z)+m^{2}\phi(z)\right)

(2)

where
\phi(z)=\phi(z_{0},\vec{z})
. A bulk-to-boundary propagator
K(z_{0},\vec{z};\vec{z}^{\prime})
can be obtained from the wave equation

\left(z_{0}^{d+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\left(z_{0}^{-d+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\right)+z_{0}^{2}\Box-m^{2}\right)K=0.

(3)

where
\Box
is
d
-dimensional Laplacian operator in direction
\vec{z}
. It is well-known that the expression for bulk-to-boundary propagator takes the form

K(z_{0},\vec{z};\vec{z}^{\prime})=\frac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})}\left(\frac{z_{0}}{z_{0}^{2}+(\vec{z}-\vec{z}^{\prime})^{2}}\right)^{\Delta}

(4)

where
\Delta=\frac{1}{2}\left(d+\sqrt{d^{2}+4m^{2}}\right)

[
4
]
. The AdS bulk field
\phi(z)
is related to the boundary data by

\phi(z_{0},\vec{z})=\int d^{d}\vec{x}\ K(z_{0},\vec{z};\vec{x})\phi_{0}(\vec{x}).

(5)

Using a Schwinger parametrization, one obtains

K(z_{0},\vec{z};\vec{z}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{z_{0}^{\Delta}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\ \rho^{\Delta-1}e^{-\rho}e^{-\frac{(\vec{z}-\vec{z}^{\prime})^{2}}{z_{0}^{2}}\rho}

(6)

by which we can utilize the heat kernel,

\langle x|e^{\tau\Box}|y\rangle=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}e^{-(x-y)^{2}/4\tau}

(7)

to rewrite

K(t,\vec{z};\vec{z}^{\prime})=\frac{t^{(d-\Delta)/2}}{\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{d}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\ \rho^{\Delta-\frac{d}{2}-1}e^{-\rho}\langle\vec{z}|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho}\Box}|\vec{z}^{\prime}\rangle

(8)

where
t=z_{0}^{2}
. We would write
\vec{z}
as
z
for later convenience.

We can then construct a boundary-to-boundary propagator through

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}\int d^{d}z\ K(t,z;y)K(t,z;x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(\Gamma(\Delta-d/2))^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{\Delta+1-\frac{d}{2}}}\int d^{d}z\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\times\ (\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{\Delta-\frac{d}{2}-1}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\langle y|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho_{2}}\Box}|z\rangle\langle z|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho_{1}}\Box}|x\rangle.

(9)

While equation (
9
) describes the standard boundary-to-boundary propagator constructed from two identical bulk-to-boundary propagators of weight
\Delta
, one may consider a generalized form involving distinct weights
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
. This leads to

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,\Delta_{1};y,\Delta_{2})=\frac{1}{2\Gamma(\Delta_{1}-\frac{d}{2})\Gamma(\Delta_{2}-\frac{d}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{1+(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-d)/2}}\int d^{d}z

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}\ \rho_{1}^{\frac{\Delta_{1}-d}{2}-1}\rho_{2}^{\frac{\Delta_{2}-d}{2}-1}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\langle y|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho_{2}}\Box}|z\rangle\langle z|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho_{1}}\Box}|x\rangle

(10)

which can be interpreted as a transition amplitude between scalar fields of different weights.

For future reference, let us denote

\displaystyle 2\Gamma(\Delta_{1}-d/2)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\Delta_{2}-d/2)\Gamma(x,\Delta_{1};y,\Delta_{2})

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{1}(x,\Delta_{1};y,\Delta_{2})+\Gamma_{2}(x,\Delta_{1};y,\Delta_{2})

(11)

where
\Gamma_{1}

\Gamma_{2}
refer to (

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}(x,\Delta_{1};y,\Delta_{2};t)=

\displaystyle\int d^{d}z\int_{\mathcal{I}_{i}}d\rho_{1}d\rho_{2}\ \rho_{1}^{\frac{\Delta_{1}-d}{2}-1}\rho_{2}^{\frac{\Delta_{2}-d}{2}-1}

\displaystyle\times e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\langle y|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho_{2}}\Box}|z\rangle\langle z|e^{\frac{t}{4\rho_{1}}\Box}|x\rangle.

Intuitively, this function could be interpreted as a partial transition amplitude which is in the same sense as color-ordered amplitude in QFT or in the context of open string amplitude, where the vertex operators of the states are inserted along the worldsheet boundary in an ordering way. 3
Two-point Open String Amplitudes

In string theory, amplitudes are computed as correlation functions of vertex operators on Riemann surfaces. For open strings, these operators are inserted along the boundary of a disk, which is typically mapped conformally to the upper half-plane. It was widely believed that two-point string amplitudes vanish due to residual worldsheet symmetries after fixing two insertion points which have infinite volume. Until quite recently, it is pointed out that the resulting divergence cancels the
\delta(0)
arising from momentum conservation in the path integral formulation
[
10
]
. Since then, few follow-up works have explored this issue using alternative approaches
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

In this paper, we would like to compute a two-point open string amplitude with the aim of recasting its form to resemble that of a boundary-to-boundary AdS transition amplitude (
10
). The motivation is to explore manifestations of the AdS/CFT correspondence, under the expectation that the string amplitude—interpreted from the CFT side—would exhibit a structure analogous to the AdS amplitude.

An expression for the two-point open string amplitude at tree level is given by

A(k_{1},k_{2})=\frac{1}{Z}\int DXDg\ \mathcal{V}_{1}(k_{1})\mathcal{V}_{2}(k_{2})e^{-S_{P}[X,g]}

(13)

where the Polyakov action is

S_{P}[X,g]=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{g}g^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\eta_{\mu\nu}.

(14)

\mathcal{V}_{i}(k_{i})
is an integrated vertex operator taking the form

\mathcal{V}_{i}(k_{i})=g_{o}\int_{\partial\Sigma}dx_{i}\ V_{i}(x_{i},k_{i})

(15)

with an open-string coupling constant
g_{o}
. The denominator
Z
is a partition function given by

\int DXDg\ e^{-S_{P}[x,g]}.

(16)

Using integration by parts, the action becomes

\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}\sigma\left(\sqrt{g}X^{\mu}\nabla^{2}X_{\mu}-\partial_{a}\left(\sqrt{g}g^{ab}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X_{\mu}\right)\right)

(17)

where

\nabla^{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_{a}\left(\sqrt{g}g^{ab}\partial_{b}\right)

(18)

is the Laplace–Beltrami operator. The second term represents a boundary contribution, which vanishes under Neumann boundary conditions, i.e.,
\frac{\partial X}{\partial n}\biggr\rvert_{\partial\Sigma}=0
.

For simplicity, we fix the worldsheet metric to
g^{ab}=\delta^{ab}
, which leaves the residual gauge symmetry group as PSL
(2,\mathbb{R})
. To simplify the computation, we restrict our attention to amplitudes involving the simplest form of vertex operators:

V_{j}(x_{j},k_{j})=e^{ik_{j}\cdot X(x_{j})}.

(19)

with a Euclidean on-shell condition
k_{i}^{2}=-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}
(We use mostly minus Minkowski signature, accordingly, the Euclidean mass-shell condition reads
k^{2}=-k_{M}^{2}=m^{2}
upon a Wick rotation).

Expanding
X(\sigma)=\bar{X}(\sigma)+x
where
x
is a zero mode regarding the Laplacian operator, the measure reads
D\bar{X}d^{d}x
. Obviously the integration from the zero mode
x
provides a momentum conservation,

(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2}).

(20)

What remains is a Gaussian integral,

\displaystyle\int D\bar{X}\exp\Bigg(

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}\sigma\Bigg[\frac{\sqrt{g}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}X^{\mu}\nabla^{2}X_{\mu}

\displaystyle\quad+i\sum_{i=1}^{2}\delta^{2}(\sigma-x_{i})k_{i}\cdot X(\sigma)\Bigg]\Bigg).

(21)

After performing a Gaussian integration, one would obtain

\mathcal{N}\exp{\left(\pi\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}G(x_{1}-x_{2})\right)}

(22)

where
G(x_{1},x_{2})
is a Green’s function satisfying

\nabla^{2}G(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\delta^{2}(x-y)-\frac{1}{\text{Vol}}

(23)

with the Neumann boundary condition

\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial n_{x}}\Biggr|_{\partial\Sigma}=c\quad\text{(constant)}.

(24)

Note that the factor
\mathcal{N}
which involves the determinant of the Laplacian operator is subject to cancellation by the factor
Z
in the denominator. The term
\frac{1}{\text{Vol}}
, where Vol is the volume of the compact space, is necessary to make the Green’s function solvable in the sense that it makes the Green’s identity hold

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(X^{\mu}(x)\nabla^{2}G(x,y)-G(x,y)\nabla^{2}X^{\mu}(x)\right)dV_{y}

\displaystyle=\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\left(X^{\mu}(y)\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}-G(x,y)\frac{\partial X^{\mu}(y)}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}\right)dS_{y}

(25)

with vanishing boundary conditions,
\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}\bigg|_{\partial\Sigma}=\frac{\partial X^{\mu}(y)}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}\bigg|_{\partial\Sigma}=0
.

Also, we neglect the exponents including the self-contracted terms like
k_{i}^{2}G(0)
, for
i=1,2
as we need to avoid the self-contraction of operators within the normal-ordering. Remember that the two-point correlation function in state-operator formalism reads

\sim\langle 0|:V_{1}(k_{1})::V_{2}(k_{2}):|0\rangle.

(26)

Let
\Sigma
be a disk with radius
R
on which the surface is parameterized by the usual polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
. By a technique of image charges, an expression for the Neumann Green’s function is

\displaystyle G(r,\theta;r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\Bigg|r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\Bigg|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\Bigg|r^{2}+\left(\frac{R^{2}}{r^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-2r\left(\frac{R^{2}}{r^{\prime}}\right)\cos(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\Bigg|-\frac{r^{2}}{4\pi R^{2}}

(27)

which is subject to

\frac{\partial G(r,\theta;r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})}{\partial r}\Biggr|_{r=R}=0.

(28)

At the boundary, the Green’s function becomes

G(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\frac{1}{\pi}\ln\Bigg|2R\sin\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}\right)\Bigg|-\frac{1}{4\pi}.

(29)

Accordingly, the amplitude takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})g_{o}^{2}\ e^{\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}/4}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta_{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta_{2}\Bigg|2R\sin\left(\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}\right)\Bigg|^{-\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}}.

(30)

We then change the coordinates

\theta^{+}=\frac{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}{2},\qquad\theta^{-}=\frac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}{2}.

(31)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle 2(2\pi)^{d+1}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})g_{o}^{2}e^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}k^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\theta^{-}\Big|2R\sin\left(\theta^{-}\right)\Big|^{-\alpha^{\prime}k^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8g_{o}^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{4}}(2\pi)^{d+1}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})R\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta^{-}\Big(\sin\left(\theta^{-}\right)\Big)^{-\alpha^{\prime}k^{2}}

(32)

where we applied the requirement
k_{1}=-k_{2}=k
from the conservation of momentum together with the mass-shell condition, i.e.
k^{2}=-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}
. Notice that we keep the
k^{2}
untouched in the last line, expecting this to relate to the heat kernel presented in the AdS propagator.

We then re-express
\left(\sin(\theta^{-})\right)^{-\alpha^{\prime}k^{2}}
as
\Im(e^{-i\alpha^{\prime}k^{2}\theta^{-}})
and rescale the variable
\theta^{-}=\theta/R
. Note that the expression is hold at the mass-shell level. Therefore, the amplitude takes the form

8g_{o}^{2}(2\pi)^{d+1}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})\int_{0}^{\pi R}d\theta\ \Im(e^{-i\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{R}k^{2}\theta})

(33)

Note that we absorb the factor
e^{-\frac{1}{4}}
into the string coupling
g_{0}
for convenience. We observe that by taking the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
while keeping
\alpha^{\prime}/R
fixed, it is possible to eliminate the imaginary operation through a Wick rotation:
\theta\rightarrow-i\theta
, turning the expression (
33
) into

-8g_{o}^{2}(2\pi)^{d+1}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}d\theta\ e^{-\lambda k^{2}\theta}

(34)

where
\lambda=\alpha^{\prime}/R
is held constant. This limit is reminiscent of the t’Hooft limit in large
N
gauge theories
[
15
]
. As a consequence of
\lambda
being constant, our string theory is tensionless (
\alpha^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty
). This regime has been investigated in several studies
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
, including its potential connections to gauge theories
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

To mimic the form of the AdS transition amplitude (
10
), we insert an identity

1=\frac{1}{\Gamma(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\ u^{n}e^{-u}

(35)

with an arbitrary number
n
into (
34
). We then change the coordinate
\theta=\frac{1}{v}
and transform the coordinates
(u,v)\rightarrow(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
via

u=\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{v}=\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}.

(36)

to give

\displaystyle-8g_{o}^{2}(2\pi)^{d+1}

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})}{\Gamma(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}\ \Bigg|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)^{2}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})^{n}e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}e^{-\lambda k^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}.

(37)

Now, we can perceive
k^{\mu}
as an eigenvalue of the operator
\hat{k}
acting on
|k\rangle
. This allows us to write

(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})e^{-\lambda k^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}=\langle k_{2}|e^{-\lambda\hat{k}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}|k_{1}\rangle.

(38)

where

\langle k_{2}|k_{1}\rangle=(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2}).

(39)

A Fourier transform is performed to turn the two-point amplitude (
37
) into that in position space such that the amplitude becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x,y)=\int\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2})e^{-i(k_{1}\cdot x-k_{2}\cdot y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-16g_{o}^{2}\pi}{\Gamma(n+1)}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}\ \mathcal{F}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}\times e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\langle y|k_{2}\rangle\langle k_{2}|e^{-\lambda\hat{k}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)}|k_{1}\rangle\langle k_{1}|x\rangle

(40)

where

\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=\Bigg|\frac{\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}}\Bigg|\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}\right)^{2}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})^{n}

(41)

and
\langle x|k\rangle=e^{ik\cdot x}
. The integration over
d^{d}k_{i}
,
i=1,2
forms a completeness relation by which we can integrate out to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{-16g_{o}^{2}\pi}{\Gamma(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho_{2}\int d^{d}z\ \mathcal{F}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})

\displaystyle\times e^{-(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\langle y|e^{\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{1}}\Box}|z\rangle\langle z|e^{\frac{\lambda}{\rho_{2}}\Box}|x\rangle

(42)

where we insert the identity,
\mathbbm{1}=\int d^{d}z\ |z\rangle\langle z|
into the equation and recast
\hat{k}^{2}=-\Box
in position space.

The expression (
42
) is similar to the AdS transition amplitude (
10
) where the parameter
\lambda
plays the same role as
t
. However, we still need to cope with a mismatch between the integrand, i.e.
\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
and
\rho_{1}^{\frac{\Delta_{1}-d}{2}-1}\rho_{2}^{\frac{\Delta_{2}-d}{2}-1}
. Obviously, we can rewrite

\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+1}{s}\ \rho_{1}^{n-s-1}\rho_{2}^{s-2}|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}|.

(43)

Accordingly, the two-point amplitude can be rewritten as a combination of AdS transition amplitudes,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{-16g_{o}^{2}\pi}{\Gamma(n+1)}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+1}{s}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\ (-1)^{j}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[\Gamma_{j}(x,\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}+2;y,\widetilde{\Delta}_{2}-2;4\lambda)-\Gamma_{j}(x,\widetilde{\Delta}_{1};y,\widetilde{\Delta}_{2};4\lambda)\Bigg]

(44)

where

\widetilde{\Delta}_{1}=2(n-s)+d\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\Delta}_{2}=2s+d.

(45)

The expression for
\Gamma_{j}(x,\alpha;y,\beta;t)
is shown in (
12
). Note that the summation is finite if
n
is a non-negative integer.

4
Two-point Closed String Amplitudes

In this section, we would like to deliver a similar calculation to the previous section, but for the closed string states whose vertex operators are in the form

\mathcal{V}_{i}(k_{i})=g_{c}\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x_{i}V_{i}(x_{i},k_{i}).

(46)

The factor
g_{c}
is a closed-string coupling constant. We will take the simplest form of the vertex operators shown (
19
) with the Euclidean on-shell conditions,
k_{i}^{2}=-\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}
.

We can then repeat with a similar computation to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})g_{c}^{2}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}x_{1}d^{2}x_{2}\ \exp\left({\pi\alpha^{\prime}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}G(x_{1}-x_{2})}\right)

(47)

where
G(x_{1},x_{2})
is a Green’s function that obeys (
23
).

At tree-level, we consider a worldsheet being a sphere
S^{2}
of radius
R
. It is known that the Laplacian Green’s function for a unit sphere
S^{2}
takes the form

\displaystyle G(\theta_{1},\phi_{1};\theta_{2},\phi_{2})=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\left|1-\cos\gamma\right|

(48)

where

\cos\gamma=\cos\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}+\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\cos(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})

(49)

[
23
,
24
]
. The Green’s function satisfies

\nabla^{2}_{S^{2}}G(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\delta(1-\cos\gamma)-\frac{1}{4\pi}.

(50)

As the Laplacian for the radius-
R
sphere is
\nabla^{2}_{R}=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\nabla^{2}_{S^{2}}
, the Green’s function of that surface is

G_{R}(\gamma)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\ln\left|R^{2}(1-\cos\gamma)\right|.

(51)

Accordingly, one can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle(2\pi^{2})(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})g_{c}^{2}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4}k_{1}\cdot k_{2}\ln\left|(1-\cos\theta_{1})R^{2}\right|\right).

(52)

To obtain the above expression, we utilize a spherical symmetry which allows us to fix a point
(\theta_{2},\phi_{2})
at the north pole. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2})=\pi^{2}(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})g_{c}^{2}\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta_{1}\left(\sin\left(\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}k^{2}}.

(53)

where the conservation of momentum and the mass-shell condition,
k^{2}=-\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}
, were applied. Let’s rescale the variable
\theta_{1}=2\theta R^{2}
, the amplitude becomes

\displaystyle 2\pi^{2}(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})g_{c}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2R^{2}}}d\theta\left(\sin{(2R^{2}\theta)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}k^{2}}.

(54)

To deal with the integral term, we use the negative binomial theorem,

(x+y)^{-n}=\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{a}\binom{n+a-1}{a}x^{-n-a}y^{a},

(55)

to rewrite
\left(\sin{(2R^{2}\theta)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}k^{2}}
as

2i\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\binom{-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}k^{2}+a-1}{a}e^{2iR^{2}\theta\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2}k^{2}-2a\right)}.

(56)

Note that the binomial coefficient is nothing but a parameter
a
when applying the on-shell condition.

Unlike the open string case, we choose to consider the limit where
R\rightarrow 0
while keeping
\lambda=\alpha^{\prime}R^{2}
fixed. Again, this implies a tensionless string limit,
\alpha^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty
. In this ’tHooft-like limit, we can rewrite (
54
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2})=-4\pi^{2}(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})g_{c}^{2}\left(\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}a\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}d\theta\ e^{-\lambda k^{2}\theta}

(57)

where the Wick rotation:
\theta\rightarrow i\theta
was used.
To make sense of the divergent series, we can regulate it via the Riemann zeta function such that

1+2+3+\ldots=\zeta(-1):=-\frac{1}{12}.

(58)

Thus, the regularized amplitude takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(k_{1},k_{2})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(k_{1}+k_{2})g_{c}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\theta\ e^{-\lambda k^{2}\theta}

(59)

which is exactly the expression (
34
) upto a constant. Consequently, we can repeat the same computation as the open string amplitude to recast the two-point closed string amplitudes in terms of the AdS transition amplitudes (
44
), albeit with different prefactors.

Although both open and closed string two-point amplitudes provide the same result, ’t Hooft’s couplings
\lambda
are defined differently, where are
\lambda=\alpha^{\prime}/R
and
\tilde{\lambda}=\alpha^{\prime}\tilde{R}^{2}
for open and closed string respectively.
R
is the radius of a disk while
\tilde{R}
is the radius of a sphere. It is noted that the factors
\frac{1}{R}
and
\frac{1}{\tilde{R}^{2}}
are the geodesic curvature of the disk and the Gaussian curvature of the sphere. This result might suggest an interesting aspect in open/closed string duality where they may dual through worldsheet curvatures. From our results, an open-string worldsheet with small geodesic curvature gives the same results to that of a closed string with high Gaussian curvature. However, further investigation would be required to establish the full duality.

5
Conclusions

We have shown that the two-point amplitudes of open and closed bosonic strings at tree level, when evaluated via the path integral formalism and taken in a tensionless, t’Hooft-like limit, can be reorganized into forms structurally identical to AdS boundary-to-boundary transition amplitudes. This is achieved by expressing both the string amplitudes and AdS transition amplitudes in terms of heat kernel propagators. The two-point string amplitude can be captured as a sum over transition amplitudes presented in (
44
).

This may provide further evidence that AdS propagator structures can emerge naturally from both open and closed string amplitudes in flat backgrounds at high-energy (
\alpha^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty
). Future directions include exploring higher-point functions and examining whether similar AdS structures appear in higher-point string amplitudes.

Additionally, we observe that the ’t Hooft couplings for open and closed string amplitudes depend on different worldsheet curvatures—the geodesic curvature of the disk and the Gaussian curvature of the sphere, respectively. This suggests a potential open/closed string duality through worldsheet geometry, where an open string on a weakly curved disk yields results equivalent to a closed string on a highly curved sphere. Further study is required to fully establish this geometric duality.

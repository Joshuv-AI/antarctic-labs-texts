Moiré-assisted charge instability in ultrathin RuO 2 \text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
Philipp Keßler, Andreas Feuerpfeil, Armando Consiglio, Hendrik Hohmann, Ronny Thomale, Jonas Erhardt, Bing Liu, Vedran Jovic, Ralph Claessen, Patrick Härtl, Matteo Dürrnagel, Simon Moser

Abstract
Ruthenium dioxide ( RuO 2 \text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ) has been in the focus of contemporary condensed matter research as a prototypical candidate material for altermagnetism . In the face of daunting evidence for bulk magnetic order despite promising theoretical predictions, it naturally suggests the focus on thin films where Coulomb interactions are dimensionally quenched and may yield a more strongly correlated environment prone to magnetic ordering. Here, we combine scanning tunneling microscopy (STM), density functional theory (DFT), and density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) methods to investigate atomically ordered ultrathin RuO 2 ​ ( 110) \text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{110)} grown on Ru ( 0001) . Contrary to predictions of magnetic order, we observe a nonmagnetic charge density wave (CDW) instability that is driven by Fermi surface nesting within the flat-band surface state, and stabilized by the incommensurate moiré stacking with the substrate. We further identify a nonmagnetic and metastable 2 × 2 2\times 2 surface reconstruction that breaks in-plane mirror symmetry and can be reversibly toggled via STM tip manipulation. Our spin-polarized STM measurements find no sign of any magnetic instability at the RuO 2 \text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} surface. As much as our findings refute proposals for either bulk or surface magnetism in RuO 2 \text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , we establish ultrathin RuO 2 ​ ( 110) \text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{110)} as an intriguing platform for exploring Moiré-assisted electronic surface order.

(1)

Emergent Surface Reconstructions

In addition to the charge ordering phenomena discussed above, we experimentally observe two further types of periodic modulations that are not captured by our minimal DMRG model.

First, a subset of high-quality, well-ordered samples reveals a faint but reproducible peak (orange arrow in Figs.
4
(a,b,c)) in the FFT, which does not correspond to any known moiré scattering vector. This feature appears rotated relative to both the primary lattice and the moiré peaks, thereby introducing an additional symmetry breaking in the Fourier pattern. The non-dispersive character of this feature allows us to exclude Friedel oscillations, and its insensitivity to a magnetic STM tip rules out magnetic order as its origin [Fig.
4
(c)]. We thus tentatively associate this signal with a second, long-range charge ordering, distinct from that discussed above. However, a comprehensive theoretical explanation of this phenomenon lies beyond the scope of this work.

Second, we detect a prominent

Ru

\text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

2\times 2

A previous study under ambient conditions linked a similar
2\times 2
reconstruction to the adsorption of
CO

Rossler2007a

CO

Jovic2021

\text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

25\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}

2\times 2

2\times 2

\text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

No Evidence for Magnetic Surface Instability

Lastly, in light of recent theoretical predictions of surface magnetism on
\text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(110)
Torun2013
;
Jeong2024
;
Ho2025
, we complete our investigation with spin-polarized STM data obtained with a
Gd
-coated tip. The tip’s magnetic sensitivity was independently confirmed on a
Gd
surface (see supplementary material). Within the experimental resolution, we found no significant differences between data acquired with the spin-polarized tip [Figs.
4
(a,b)] and those obtained with an unpolarized Ag tip [Fig.
4
(c)]. Specifically, we detected no signatures of spin-density waves, magnetic domains, or spin-polarized surface states.
Given the sensitivity limit of our setup–estimated to be approximately
0.3~\mu_{B}
at a spatial resolution of one unit cell
Kurz2002
;
hartl2022
–we regard this null result as a benchmark for future investigations of possible magnetic ordering on
\text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(110). We emphasize, however, that ultrathin
\text{RuO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
films offer a rich platform where tunable electronic and structural instabilities intertwine, giving rise to ordering phenomena and surface reconstructions that, within the limits of our experimental resolution, appear nonmagnetic.

Density Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG)

DMRG is a powerful numerical technique originally developed for studying one-dimensional quantum many-body systems
White_1992
. It has since been extended to higher dimensions and various other applications. DMRG is particularly effective for systems with strong correlations and has been widely used to investigate the ground state properties, low-lying excitations, and dynamic correlations of quantum systems
SCHOLLWOCK201196
.

The core idea of DMRG is to iteratively optimize the wave function whilst only retaining the most significant part of the Hilbert space. Let us consider a quantum system consisting of
N
sites, where each site is described by a
d
-dimensional local Hilbert space
\{\sigma_{i}\}
. Then, any state can be represented by a rank-
N
tensor

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N}}c_{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N}}\ket{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_N}.

(2)

By applying a series of singular value decompositions (SVD), we can represent
\ket{\psi}
as a matrix product state (MPS)

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N}}A_{1,a_{1}}^{\sigma_{1}}A_{a_{1},a_{2}}^{\sigma_{2}}\dots A_{a_{N-1},1}^{\sigma_{N}}\ket{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_N},

(3)

where the
A
’ s are rank-
3
tensors. By limiting the number of singular values in each SVD to some dimension
D
, we obtain a compressed representation of the quantum state.

To find the ground state of a quantum system represented by a Hamiltonian
H
in the space of MPS of dimension
D
, we minimize the energy

E_{0}=\min_{\ket{\psi}}\frac{\bra{\psi}H\ket{\psi}}{\bra{\psi}\ket{\psi}}.

(4)

In constrast to exact diagonalization, the strategy of DMRG is to minimize the energy by sweeping through all tensors and optimizing two tensors at a time by solving an effective eigenvalue problem. This is performed untill we reach convergence in energy
SCHOLLWOCK201196
.

As the DMRG update only acts on two tensors at the same time, the algorithm is prone to getting stuck in local minima. To avoid this problem we use White’s noise term
White_2005
. All calculations were performed using the ITENSOR library
ITensor
;
ITensor-r0.3
with an SVD cutoff of
10^{-10}
. We checked for convergence w.r.t. bond dimensions of
512
,
1024
and
2048
.

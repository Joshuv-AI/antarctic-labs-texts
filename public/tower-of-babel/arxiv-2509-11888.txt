ON TWO NOTIONS OF TORSION AND METRIC COMPATIBILITY OF CONNECTIONS IN NONCOMMUTATIVE GEOMETRY Thanks: BG is supported by CSIR, India through the SPM Fellowship.
Jyotishman Bhowmick, Bappa Ghosh, Satyajit Guin

Abstract
. We compare the notions of metric-compatibility and torsion of a connection in the frameworks of Beggs-Majid and Mesland-Rennie. It follows that for ∗ \ast -preserving connections, compatibility with a real metric in the sense of Beggs-Majid corresponds to Hermitian connections in the sense of Mesland-Rennie. If the calculus is quasi-tame, the torsion zero conditions are equivalent. A combination of these results proves the existence and uniqueness of Levi-Civita connections in the sense of Mesland-Rennie for unitary cocycle deformations of a large class of Riemannian manifolds as well as the Heckenberger-Kolb calculi on all quantized irreducible flag manifolds.



1.
Introduction

The first attempts to understand torsion and metric compatibility of connections in the context of noncommutative geometry dates back to the 1990 s. While the paper
[
FGR99
]
studied the relevant concepts in the context of spectral triples (
[
Con94
]
), the authors of
[
HS97
]
obtained existence of Levi-Civita connections for covariant calculi on certain quantum groups. Beggs and Majid (
[
BM20a
]
) developed a substantial amount of machinery for studying torsion and metric compatibility of bimodule connections and also proved the existence and uniqueness of Chern connections on noncommutative holomorphic bimodules. Following the work of Rosenberg (
[
Ros13
]
), the topic received renewed attention as different versions of torsion and metric compatibility were proposed and studied in the literature. We refer to
[
PS17
,
AW17a
,
AW17b
,
BGM20
,
BGJ21
,
BGL20
]
and references therein. Subsequently, existence and uniqueness results for several classes of quantum groups and their homogeneous spaces were obtained in
[
AW25
,
ALP25
,
BM20b
,
Mat21
,
BGKOB24
]
.

Recently, the authors of
[
MR25b
]
introduced the framework of Hermitian differential structures and proved a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of Levi-Civita connections in their sense. They proved (
[
MR25b
,
MR25a
]
) that the toric deformations of classical spectral triples as well as the Dabrowski-Sitarz spectral triple on the standard Podleś sphere satisfy the conditions of their theorem.

A recent comparative study
[
FMR25
]
investigated how the approach taken by Mesland and Rennie (
[
MR25b
]
) relates to the vector
-
field approach of
[
AW17a
,
AW17b
]
as well as a different approach taken in
[
BGM20
,
BGJ21
,
BGL20
]
. In this article, we compare the methods proposed in
[
BM20a
]
and
[
MR25b
]
. Thus, our objective is to clarify the relationships between the two definitions of metric compatibility as well as torsion of connections. Then we combine our results to prove the existence and uniqueness of Levi-Civita connections in the sense of
[
MR25b
]
for real covariant metrics on the space of one-forms of unitary cocycle deformations of a large class of Riemannian manifolds and the Heckenberger-Kolb calculi on the quantized irreducible flag manifolds.

In Section
2
, we recall the framework of Beggs and Majid as developed in
[
BM20a
]
. The starting point is the datum of a differential calculus
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
over an algebra
B.
The torsion of a (left) connection
\nabla:\Omega^{1}(B)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)
is the left
B
-linear map
T_{\nabla}:=\wedge\circ\nabla-d:\Omega^{1}(B)\rightarrow\Omega^{2}(B).
Note that the definition of torsion requires the data of both the one-forms and the two-forms. On the other hand, the metric compatibility of
\nabla
makes sense for any first order differential calculus. However, the definition needs a bit more explanation since the compatibility can be defined in terms of a metric or an Hermitian metric.

A metric on
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
is a choice of a self-dual structure
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ))
on the space of one-forms
\Omega^{1}
of the calculus. Thus,
g
is an element of
\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)
and
(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ):\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(B)
is a
B
-linear map satisfying some compatibility conditions. If
(\nabla_{\Omega^{1}(B)},\sigma)
is a bimodule connection (see Subsection
2.2
) on
\Omega^{1}(B),
then
\nabla_{\Omega^{1}(B)}
extends to a connection
\nabla_{\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)}
on
\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B).
The authors of
[
BM20a
]
call
\nabla_{\Omega^{1}(B)}
to be compatible with a metric
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ))
as above if
\nabla_{\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)}g=0.
When the algebra
B
and the calculus
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
have compatible
\ast
-structures, Beggs and Majid introduced a notion of Hermitian metrics given by certain
B
-bimodule isomorphisms
\mathscr{H}:\overline{\Omega^{1}(B)}\rightarrow{}_{B}\mathrm{Hom}(\Omega^{1}(B),B)
and there is a notion of compatibility of connections (not necessarily bimodule connections) with
\mathscr{H}
(see Definition
2.15
). If
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ))
is a real metric (Definition
2.11
) on the space of one-forms of a
\ast
-differential calculus, then one can construct an Hermitian metric
\mathscr{H}_{g}
on
\Omega^{1}(B).
Thus, it is a natural question to ask whether the compatibility of a connection
\nabla
on
\Omega^{1}(B)

with
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ))
is related to the compatibility of
\nabla
with
\mathscr{H}_{g}.
The result Proposition
2.19
which was already observed in
[
BM20a
]
states that these two notions coincide when the connection
\nabla
is
\ast
-preserving. This is the key result which allows us to compare the frameworks of
[
BM20a
]
and
[
MR25b
]
.

Mesland and Rennie’s notion of compatibility is based on the framework of
\dagger
-bimodules. For an algebra
B,
these are
B
-bimodules
\mathcal{E}
equipped with an antilinear involution
\dagger
and a positive definite sesquilinear form
{}_{B}\langle\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle\rangle:\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\rightarrow B
satisfying the condition
{}_{B}\langle\langle eb,f\rangle\rangle={}_{B}\langle\langle e,fb^{*}\rangle\rangle
for all
e,f\in\mathcal{E},b\in B.
Connections on
\mathcal{E}
which are compatible with
{}_{B}\langle\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle\rangle
are called Hermitian connections. In this article, we drop the positivity condition on
{}_{B}\langle\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle\rangle
and work with a larger class of sesquilinear forms which we call weak
\dagger
-bimodules. If
(\Omega^{1}(B),d)
is a first order
\ast
-differential calculus, then it is easy to see that weak
\dagger
-bimodules
(\Omega^{1}(B),\dagger,{}_{B}\langle\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle\rangle)
are closely related to Hermtian metrics on
\Omega^{1}(B)
in the sense of
[
BM20a
]
. However, our main goal in Section
3
is to relate compatibility of connections with metrics
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ))
on
\Omega^{1}(B)
with Hermitian connections with respect to
{}_{B}\langle\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle\rangle.
This is where we assume
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ))
to be real and use Proposition
2.19
. Our main result in Section
3
is Theorem
3.11
where we prove that connections on
\Omega^{1}(B)
compatible with real metrics
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ))
are in one to one correspondence with connections which are Hermitian with respect to a canonical weak
\dagger
-bimodule structure on
\Omega^{1}(B)
associated to
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ )).

In Section
4
, we compare the definitions of torsions of connections studied in
[
BM20a
]
and
[
MR25b
]
. The authors of the latter paper consider second order differential structures given by a first order calculus
(\Omega^{1},d)
over an algebra
B
equipped with a
\dagger
-structure and postulate the existence of a
\dagger
-compatible
B
-bilinear idempotent

\psi:\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)
whose range contains certain junk
2
-tensors. This allows them to use the universal differential
\Omega^{1}_{u}(B)\rightarrow\Omega^{2}_{u}(B)
to define a map
d_{\psi}:\Omega^{1}(B)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)
and a connection
\nabla
on
\Omega^{1}(B)
is called torsionless if

(1.1)

(1-\psi)\nabla=d_{\psi}.

In the first two subsections of Section
4
where we discuss torsion, we have avoided the usage of any
\dagger
-structure on the calculus and thus, we have not assumed any compatibility between
\psi
and any
\dagger
-structure. Thus, we have considered the class of weak second order differential structures
(\Omega^{1}(B),d,\psi)
such that
\Omega^{1}(B)
is a sub-bimodule of an algebra
M.
We clarify the relationship between the conditions (
1.1
) and the Beggs-Majid equation for zero-torsion for the Connes’ calculus associated to the inclusion
\Omega^{1}(B)\hookrightarrow M.
Subsequently, we focus our attention on tame differential calculi
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
for which there exists a
B
-bilinear map
s:\Omega^{2}(B)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(B)\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}(B)
satisfying the condition
\wedge\circ s=\text{id}_{\Omega^{2}(B)}.
Such calculi were already studied in the literature for various purposes (see
[
BM20a
,
BGM20
,
BGL20
]
). In Theorem
4.9
, we prove that if
(\Omega^{\bullet}(B),\wedge,d,s)
is a tame differential calculus, then the Beggs-Majid definition of a torsion zero connection on
\Omega^{1}(B)
coincides with (
1.1
) for a weak second order differential structure associated to the tame differential calculus.

Thus, a combination of Theorem
3.11
and theorem
4.9
provides a passage between the frameworks of Beggs-Majid and Mesland-Rennie. This has been noted in Theorem
4.12
. Modulo technicalities, this theorem implies that if we have a quasi-tame
\ast
-differential calculi on a
\ast
-algebra
B
and a
\ast
-preserving unique Levi-Civita connection (in the sense of
[
BM20a
]
) for a real metric
(g,(\penalty\ ,\penalty\ ))
on
\Omega^{1}(B),
then there exists a unique Hermitian connection on
\Omega^{1}(B)
satisfying (
1.1
). This enables us to apply this result to two classes of Hopf-algebra covariant calculi, namely unitary cocycle deformations of a canonical differential calculi on the set of real points
X(\mathbb{R})
of a smooth real affine variety
X
and the Heckenberger-Kolb calculi. The proof of existence and uniqueness of Levi-Civita connections (in the sense of
[
BM20a
]
) on these calculi for real covariant metrics were already derived in
[
BM20a
]
and
[
BGKOB24
]
. Thus, we need to verify the hypotheses of Theorem
4.12
for these examples. This is done in Section
5
.

2.1.
Differential calculi

Definition 2.3
.

\mathrm{(}
[

\mathrm{)}

A first order differential calculus on a unital algebra
B
is a pair
(\Omega^{1}(B),d),
where
\Omega^{1}(B)
is a
B

We will refer to
\Omega^{1}(B)
as the bimodule of one-forms for the first order differential calculus. The bimodule

can be realized as the quotient of the universal space of one-forms for

More concretely, if

denotes the multiplication map, then the universal space of one-forms is defined as

\Omega^{1}_{u}(B)={\rm Ker}(m)
and we have a derivation

\delta:B\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{u}(B)\penalty\ {\rm defined}\penalty\ {\rm by}\penalty\ \delta(b)=1\otimes b-b\otimes 1.

We recall that if
B
is a unital algebra and

B
-bimodule such that there exists a derivation

then there exists a
B
-bimodule map
\pi:\Omega^{1}_{u}(B)\rightarrow M
such that

.

A differential calculus on an algebra

is a differential graded algebra
(\Omega^{\bullet}(B)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Omega^{k}(B),\wedge,d)

2.2.
Connections

If
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
is a differential calculus on an algebra
B

B
-bimodule
\mathcal{E}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\nabla:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{E}
such that

\nabla(be)=b\nabla(e)+db\otimes_{B}e

for all

and for all

\mathcal{E}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\nabla:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}
such that
\nabla(eb)=\nabla(e)b+e\otimes_{B}db.

Throughout this article, unless otherwise stated, we will use the word connection to mean a left connection.

Definition 2.8
.

If
\nabla
is a left connection on
\Omega^{1},
then the torsion of
\nabla,
denoted by
T_{\nabla}
is defined as

T_{\nabla}:=\wedge\circ\nabla-d.

The connection
\nabla
is said to be torsionless if
T_{\nabla}=0.

Note that, if
\nabla
is a connection, then

is a left
B
-linear map. We should also mention that for connections on the space of one-forms on a spectral triple (
[
Con94
]
), a spectral definition of torsion has been studied, for which we refer to
[

]

A left connection

-bimodule connection if there exists a

\sigma:\mathcal{E}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{E}

such that

\nabla(eb)=\nabla(e)b+\sigma(e\otimes_{B}db)

for all

and for all
b\in B
.
Similarly, a right connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{E}
is said to be a right bimodule connection if there exists a
B
-bimodule map
\sigma:\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}
such that

\nabla(be)=b\nabla(e)+\sigma(db\otimes_{B}e).

Suppose that
(\nabla_{\mathcal{E}},\sigma_{\mathcal{E}})
and
(\nabla_{\mathcal{F}},\sigma_{\mathcal{F}})
are bimodule connections on
B
-bimodules
\mathcal{E}
and

by virtue of (

∎

Now we are in a position to compare Definition

for a weak second order differential structure and Definition

for the associated Connes’ calculus.

Proposition 4.6

Suppose that

is a weak second order differential structure such that

is a sub-bimodule of a

-bimodule

and

is an algebra. Moreover, assume that

is a connection of

Then the following statements hold:

(i)

and

then

(ii)

On the other hand, if the equivalent conditions of Lemma

are satisfied and

Proof.

For the first assertion, we observe that

\wedge_{C}\circ(\nabla-d_{\psi})=\wedge_{C}\circ\nabla-\wedge_{C}\circ d_{\psi}=\wedge_{C}\circ\nabla-d_{C}=0

∎

4.2.
Quasi-tame differential calculi as weak second order differential structures

We recall that if
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
is a differential calculus and
i:{\rm Ker}(\wedge)\rightarrow\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}
denotes the inclusion map, then the following is a short-exact sequence of
B
-bimodules:

(4.6)

0\rightarrow{\rm Ker}(\wedge)\xrightarrow{i}\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}\xrightarrow{\wedge}\Omega^{2}\rightarrow 0.

Following
[
BGJ21
, Definition 2.3]
(also see Definition
[
BGM20
, 2.11]
), we make the following definition:

Definition 4.7
.

We say that a differential calculus
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
is quasi-tame if
\Omega^{1}
is finitely generated and projective as a left
B
-module and moreover, there exists a splitting of the short exact sequence in (
4.6
), i.e, there exists a
B
-bilinear map
s:\Omega^{2}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}
such that
\wedge\circ s={\rm id}_{\Omega^{2}}.

The splitting of the map
\wedge
was considered by Beggs and Majid for defining the Ricci curvature of a connection (
[
BM20a
, page 574]
). In
[
BGM20
, Theorem 2.13]
, it was proved that a quasi-tame calculus always admits a torsionless connection.

Let
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d,s)
be a quasi-tame differential calculus. Consider the idempotent

(4.7)

\psi_{s}:=1-s\circ\wedge:\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}.

Then we have the following result:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
be a differential calculus such that
\Omega^{1}
is finitely generated and projective as a left
B
-module. Then
s\mapsto\psi_{s}
defines a bijective correspondence between quasi-tame differential structures on
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
and weak second order differential structures
(\Omega^{1},d,\psi)
satisfying
\text{Ker}(\wedge)=\text{Ran}(\psi).

Proof.

Although the statement essentially follows from
[
MR25b
, Lemma 3.5]
, we give a proof for the sake of completeness.

Let
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d,s)
be a quasi-tame differential calculus. Since
s\circ\wedge
is an idempotent with
\mathrm{Ker}(s\circ\wedge)=\mathrm{Ker}(\wedge),

\mathrm{Ran}(\psi_{s})=\mathrm{Ker}(s\circ\wedge)=\mathrm{Ker}(\wedge).

The proof of the inclusion
JT^{2}_{d}\subseteq\text{Ran}(\psi_{s})
follows along the lines of the proof of Proposition
4.2
. Indeed, if
q_{2}=\wedge\circ\widehat{\pi}:\Omega^{2}_{u}\rightarrow\Omega^{2}
denotes the quotient map as in that proposition, then for
\omega\in\Omega^{1}_{u}\cap\text{Ker}(\widehat{\pi}),
we get

\wedge(\widehat{\pi}(\delta(\omega)))=d(q_{2}(\omega))=d(\wedge\circ\widehat{\pi}(\omega))=0

implying that

JT^{2}_{d}\subseteq\text{Ker}(\wedge)=\text{Ker}(s\circ\wedge)=\text{Ran}(1-s\circ\wedge)=\text{Ran}(\psi_{s}).

Thus
(\Omega^{1},d,\psi)
is a weak second order differential calculus.

Conversely, let
(\Omega^{1},d,\psi)
be a weak second order differential structure such that
\text{Ker}(\wedge)=\text{Ran}(\psi).
Then it is easy to observe that the map

\wedge:\text{Ran}(1-\psi)\rightarrow\Omega^{2}
is an isomorphism.
Then we define
s_{\psi}:\Omega^{2}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}\otimes_{B}\Omega^{1}
to be the inverse of the map
\wedge|_{\text{Ran}(1-\psi)}.
Then
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d,s)
is a quasi-tame differential calculus.

Thus, we are left to prove that the correspondences
s\mapsto\psi_{s}
and
\psi\mapsto s_{\psi}
are inverses of each other. Suppose that
(\Omega^{1},d,\psi)
is a weak second order differential structure satisfying
\text{Ker}(\wedge)=\text{Ran}(\psi).
If
x\in\text{Ran}(1-\psi)
and
y\in\text{Ran}(\psi),
we get

\displaystyle\psi_{s_{\psi}}(x+y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s_{\psi}\circ\wedge)(x+y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x-s_{\psi}\circ\wedge(x))+(y-s_{\psi}\circ\wedge(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x-x)+(y-s_{\psi}(0))\penalty\ \text{(As }\penalty\ s_{\psi}=(\wedge|_{\mathrm{Ran}(1-\psi)})^{-1}\penalty\ \text{and }\text{Ran}(\psi)=\text{Ker}(\wedge)\text{)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(x)+\psi(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(x+y).

Therefore,
\psi_{s_{\psi}}=\psi.
Similarly, if
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d,s)
is a quasi-tame differential calculus, then it can be easily checked that
s_{\psi_{s}}=s.

∎

This brings us to the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d,s)
be a quasi-tame differential calculus and
\nabla
is a connection on
\Omega^{1},
then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\nabla
is torsionless in the sense of Definition
2.8
.

(ii)

\nabla

for the weak second order differential structure

Proof.

Let us assume that

We denote the Connes’ calculus for the inclusion
\Omega^{1}\subseteq\Omega^{\bullet}
by
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{C},\wedge_{C},d_{C}).
Then by Proposition

, we know that
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{C},\wedge_{C},d_{C})
coincides with the calculus

and therefore,
\wedge_{C}\circ\nabla=d_{C}.

Moreover,

\text{Ker}(\wedge_{C})=\text{Ker}(\wedge)=\text{Ran}(\psi_{s})

by virtue of Proposition

Hence, part
(i)
in Proposition
4.6
allows us to conclude that

Similarly, if

then from Proposition

∎

Appendix A
Spectral triples and
*
-differential calculi

In this appendix, we point out that the theory of spectral triple fit into the framework of
\ast
-differential calculi.
In the following definition, the symbol
B(H)
will stand for the set of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
H.

Definition A.1

(
[
Con94
]
)
.

A spectral triple on a unital
\ast
-algebra
B
is a triple
(B,H,D),
where
H
is a separable Hilbert space,
B
is a unital
\ast
-subalgebra of
B(H)
and
D
is a (typically unbounded) self-adjoint operator on
H
such that for all
b\in B,

[D,\pi(b)]
extends to a bounded operator on
H.

It is standard to assume that
(i+D)^{-1}
is a compact operator but like in
[
MR25b
]
, we won’t need this condition here.

Consider the first order differential calculus defined by the pair
(\Omega^{1}_{C}(B),d_{C}),
where

\Omega^{1}_{C}(B)=\text{span}\big\{[D,b]c:b,c\in B\big\}\subseteq B(H)\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ d_{C}(b)=\sqrt{-1}[D,b].

By construction,
\Omega^{1}_{C}(B)
is closed under the usual involution
\ast_{B(H)}
on
B(H)
and due to the definition of
d_{C},
the pair
(\Omega^{1}_{C}(B),d_{C}),
is a first order
\ast
-differential calculus.

Now we can apply the recipe of Definition
4.1
to the triple
(\Omega^{1}_{C}(B),d_{C},B(H))
to obtain Connes’ differential calculus
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{C},\wedge_{C},d_{C})
for the spectral triple. We refer to
[
Lan97
, Section 7.2]
for exposition.

It is well-known and easy to check that
J^{\ast}_{d}:=\oplus_{k\geq 0}J^{k}_{d}
is a two-sided graded differential
\ast
-ideal of
\oplus_{k\geq 0}m_{k}(\Omega^{k}_{u})
and hence, each
\Omega^{k}_{C}(B)
is closed under the antilinear involution
\ast
defined by

(T+J^{k}_{d})^{\ast}:=T^{\ast_{B(H)}}+J^{k}_{d}\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ T\in m_{k}(\Omega^{k}_{u}(B)),

where
\ast_{B(H)}
is the canonical involution on
B(H)
and the symbol
m_{k}
is as defined in Section
4
. Unfortunately, the conditions in (
2.4
) may fail to hold for the Connes’ differential calculus. For example, if
b_{1},b_{2}\in B,
then

\displaystyle(d_{C}(b_{1})\wedge_{C}d_{C}(b_{2}))^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\sqrt{-1}D,b_{1}][\sqrt{-1}D,b_{2}])^{\ast_{B(H)}}+J^{2}_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\sqrt{-1}D,b^{\ast}_{2}][\sqrt{-1}D,b^{\ast}_{1}]+J^{2}_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{C}(b^{\ast}_{2})\wedge_{C}d_{C}(b^{\ast}_{1})

which may not be equal to
-d_{C}(b^{\ast}_{2})\wedge_{C}d_{C}(b^{\ast}_{1})
(equal if and only if it is
0
).

However, this problem can be rectified by changing the involution on the higher forms.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose that
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
is a differential calculus on a
\ast
-algebra
B
. Also assume that there exists a graded antilinear involution
\ast
on
\Omega^{\bullet}
such that for all

d(b^{*})=(db)^{*}\mathrm{\penalty\ and\penalty\ }\big(b_{0}d(b_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge d(b_{k})b_{k+1}\big)^{*}=b^{*}_{k+1}d(b_{k}^{*})\wedge d(b_{k-1}^{*})\cdots\wedge d(b_{1}^{*})b_{0}^{*}.

Consider the graded antilinear involution
*_{1}
on
\Omega^{\bullet}
defined by

(A.1)

*_{1}(b_{0}db_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge db_{k}b_{k+1}):=\begin{cases}b_{k+1}^{*}db_{k}^{*}\wedge db_{k-1}^{*}\wedge\cdots\wedge db_{1}^{*}b_{0}^{*}&\mathrm{for\penalty\ }k\equiv 0,1\penalty\ \mathrm{mod}\penalty\ 4\\
-b_{k+1}^{*}db_{k}^{*}\wedge db_{k-1}^{*}\wedge\cdots\wedge db_{1}^{*}b_{0}^{*}&\mathrm{for\penalty\ }k\equiv 2,3\penalty\ \mathrm{mod}\penalty\ 4\end{cases}.

Then,
(\Omega^{\bullet},\wedge,d)
is a
\ast
-differential calculus with respect to the involution
\ast_{1}.

Proof.

By the above discussion, we are only to required to check the validity of the equation (
2.4
). Consider
\omega=b_{0}db_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge db_{k}\in\Omega^{k}.
To prove the equation
d(\omega^{\ast_{1}})=d(\omega)^{\ast_{1}},
we verify it for the cases
k=0,1,2,3
(mod
4
) individually. For example, if
k=2
(mod
4

\displaystyle d(\omega^{\ast_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(-db^{*}_{k}\wedge\cdots\wedge d(b^{*}_{1})b^{*}_{0})=-(-1)^{k}db^{*}_{k}\wedge\cdots\wedge d(b^{*}_{1})\wedge d(b^{*}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-db^{*}_{k}\wedge\cdots\wedge d(b^{*}_{1})\wedge d(b^{*}_{0})=(db_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge d(b_{k}))^{\ast_{1}}=(d\omega)^{\ast_{1}}.

Next, if

\omega=b_{0}db_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge db_{k}\in\Omega^{k}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \eta=da_{1}\wedge\wedge da_{l}a_{l+1}\in\Omega^{l},

then the equation

(mod

We illustrate this by taking

(mod

(mod

The asymptotic behavior of the first Robin eigenvalue with negative parameter as p p goes to + ∞ +\infty
Rosa Barbato, Francesca de Giovanni*, Alba Lia Masiello

Abstract
In this paper, we want to study the asymptotic behavior of the first p p -Laplacian eigenvalue, with Robin boundary conditions, with negative boundary parameter. In particular, we prove that the limit of the eigenfunctions is a viscosity solution for the infinity Laplacian eigenvalue problem. Keywords: Laplace operator; Negative parameter; asymptotic behavior MSC 2020: 35J05, 35J25, 35B40

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded open set, with Lipschitz boundary, with
n\geq 2
, and let
\beta>0
.
We aim to study the asymptotic behavior as
p
goes to
+\infty
of the first eigenvalue of the Robin
p
-Laplacian on
\Omega
with a negative boundary parameter

\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\\
w\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{p}\,dx-\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|w\right|}^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}{\left|w\right|}^{p}\,dx}}.

(1)

A minimizer
u
in (
1
) is a solution to the
p
-Laplace eigenvalue equation with Robin boundary conditions

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega){\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u&\text{ in }\Omega\\
{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}-\beta^{p}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2)

The eigenvalue
\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)
is negative, as it can be seen by choosing
w=1
in the variational formulation (
1
),

\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\leq-\beta^{p}\dfrac{P(\Omega)}{{\left|\Omega\right|}}.

Moreover, as the value of the functional in (
1
) is the same in
w
and
-w
, we can choose the eigenfunction
u
to be positive.
For a negative boundary parameter, some problems are not completely solved, such as the optimization of
\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)
with a volume constraint. Indeed, in 1977 Bareket
[
5
]
conjectured that the first eigenvalue is maximized by a ball in the class of smooth bounded domains of given volume. The authors in
[
11
]
(see
[
16
]
for the
p
-Laplacian case) have disproved the Bereket conjecture for
\left|\beta\right|^{p}
large enough, studying the asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalue in (
1
), showing that

\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)=-(p-1)\beta^{\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}}-(n-1)H_{max}(\Omega)\beta^{p}+o(\beta^{p}),\quad\beta^{p}\rightarrow+\infty,

where
H_{max}(\Omega)
is the maximum mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
. This asymptotic allows the authors to conclude that the eigenvalue of the spherical shell is larger than the one of the ball with the same measure.

In the same paper, they also showed that the conjecture is indeed true for small values of
\left|\beta\right|^{p}
in a suitable class of domains. We also recall that, in the Finsler setting, this problem has been addressed in
[
17
]
.

In the present paper, we aim to study the limit as
p
goes to
+\infty
of problem (
1
), following the footsteps of
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
14
,
15
]
.
It is worth mentioning that the limit case, as
p
goes to
1
, has been studied in
[
9
]
for the Euclidean setting, and in
[
4
]
for the anisotropic case. Moreover, the case of higher Robin eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplace operator as
p
goes to
1
has been studied in
[
20
]
, also in the case when
\beta
is a non-negative function in
L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
.

In
[
14
,
15
]
the authors study the limit as
p
goes to
+\infty
of the eigevalue of the
p
-Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions, obtaining a complete characterization of the limiting problems in terms of geometric quantities.
Indeed, the first two eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplace operator,
\{\lambda_{1,p}^{D}\}
and
\{\lambda_{2,p}^{D}\}
, satisfy

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{1,p}^{D}\right)^{{1}/{p}}=\lambda_{1,\infty}^{D}=:\frac{1}{r(\Omega)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{2,p}^{D}\right)^{{1}/{p}}=\lambda_{2,\infty}^{D}=:\frac{1}{r_{2}(\Omega)},

where

r(\Omega)=\sup\{t:\exists B_{t}(x)\subseteq\Omega\},

and

r_{2}(\Omega)=\sup\{t:\exists B_{t}(x_{1}),B_{t}(x_{2})\subseteq\Omega\text{ such that }B_{t}(x_{1})\cap B_{t}(x_{2})=\emptyset\}.

The Neumann case was investigated in
[
10
,
19
]
and, similarly to the Dirichlet case, the authors proved that the first non-trivial eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplacian with Neumann boundary conditions
\{\lambda^{N}_{p}\}
satisfy

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda^{N}_{p}\right)^{1/p}=\lambda_{\infty}^{N}:=\frac{2}{D(\Omega)},

where
D(\Omega)
is the intrinsic diameter of
\Omega
, i.e. the supremum of the geodetic distance between two points of
\Omega
.

The Robin boundary conditions with
positive
boundary parameter was addressed in
[
1
,
2
]
, in which the authors focus on the first and the second eigenvalue respectively. As in the previous cases, the geometry of the set plays a key role in determining the limit of the sequence of eigenvalues, as

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{1,p}^{R}\right)^{{1}/{p}}=\lambda_{1,\infty}^{R}=:\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+r(\Omega)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{2,p}^{R}\right)^{{1}/{p}}=\lambda_{2,\infty}^{R}=:\frac{1}{s(\Omega)},

where

s(\Omega)={\max}\left\{t:\exists x_{1},x_{2}\in\overline{\Omega}\text{ such that }{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|}\geq 2t,{\left\|C_{i,t}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{\beta t}\right\},

and
C_{i,t}
is the function

C_{i,t}(x)=\frac{(t-{\left|x-x_{i}\right|})_{+}}{t}.

Different cases, as the mixed Robin-Dirichlet boundary conditions or the Steklov boundary conditions, were studied in
[
3
,
12
,
18
]
.

In all the aforementioned papers, the geometry of the set
\Omega
is crucial in studying the limiting problem, so one might expect a similar behavior for problem (
1
). This idea is supported also by the fact that, for
p\to 1
, the limit eigenvalue is linked to the Cheeger constant of the set, as showed in
[
4
,
9
]
.

Surprisingly, when dealing with a negative boundary condition, the limiting problem as
p
goes to infinity appears to be completely independent of the geometry of the set, and this is what is stated in our first main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, Lipschitz set and let
\Set{\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)}_{p>1}
be the sequence of the first eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplace operator with Robin boundary condition defined in (
1
). Then,

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\beta.

(3)

Moreover, if
\Set{u_{p}}_{p>1}
is the sequence of positive eigenfunctions associated to
\{\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\}_{p>1}
, then there exists a function
u_{\infty}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle u_{p}\to u_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{ uniformly in }\,\Omega,

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p}\to\nabla u_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{ weakly in }\,L^{m}(\Omega),\;\forall m.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, one can deduce that Bareket’s conjecture becomes trivial when
p
goes to
+\infty
.

The second result we prove is that the limit function
u_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution to a PDE problem involving the infinity Laplace operator

\Delta_{\infty}u=\left\langle D^{2}u\cdot\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle,

as often happens in the limiting problems as
p
goes to
+\infty
.
The result is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u_{\infty}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
be the function given in Theorem (
1.1
). Then
u_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution to

\begin{cases}-\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\beta u,\Delta_{\infty}u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\beta u,\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(4)

Now we want to show that
\beta
is the first eigenvalue of (
4
), that is the only
\lambda>0
, for which problem
(
4
)
admits a non-trivial, positive eigenfunction. The last theorem is the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{2}
bounded domain in
\R^{n}
. If, for some
\lambda>0
, problem

\begin{cases}-\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\lambda u,\Delta_{\infty}u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\beta u,\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(5)

admits a nontrivial, positive eigenfunction
u
, then
\beta=\lambda
.

|\cdot|

2.1
The viscosity
\infty
-eigenvalue problem

We start by giving the notion of viscosity solution to a PDE problem, see
[
8
]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

Let us consider the following boundary value problem

\begin{cases}F(x,u,\nabla u,D^{2}u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
G(x,u,\nabla u)=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(6)

where
F:\R^{n}\times\R\times\R^{n}\times\R^{n\times n}\to\R
and
B:\R^{n}\times\R\times\R^{n}\to\R
are two continuous functions.

Viscosity supersolution

A lower semi-continuous function
u
is a viscosity supersolution to (
6
) if, whenever we fix
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, for every
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
u(x)-\phi(x)\geq u(x_{0})-\phi(x_{0})=0
, i.e.
x_{0}
is a strict minimum in
\Omega
for
u-\phi
, then

•

if
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the following holds

F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right)\geq 0

•

if
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, the following holds

\max\Set{F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right),G\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right)}\geq 0

Viscosity subsolution

An upper semi-continuous function
u
is a viscosity subsolution to (
6
) if, whenever we fix
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, for every
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
u(x)-\phi(x)\leq u(x_{0})-\phi(x_{0})=0
, i.e.
x_{0}
is a strict maximum in
\Omega
for
u-\phi
, then

•

if
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the following holds

F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right)\leq 0

•

if
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, the following holds

\min\Set{F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right),G\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right)}\leq 0

Viscosity solution

A continuous function
u
is a viscosity solution to (
6
) if it is both a supersolution and subsolution.

A comparison result for supersolution and subsolution to a PDE problem holds, as proved in
[
8
]
. It will be crucial in proving our main Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open subset in
\R^{n}
,
F\in C(\Omega\times\R\times\R^{n}\times\mathcal{S}(n))
be proper,

F(x,r,p,X)\leq F(x,s,p,Y),\quad\text{ whenever }r\leq s\text{ and }Y\leq X

(7)

and satisfy

1.

\exists\gamma>0
such that

\gamma(r-s)\leq F(x,r,p,X)-F(x,s,p,X),\quad\textrm{for}\;r\geq s,\;(x,p,X)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\R^{n}\times\mathcal{S}(n);

2.

\exists\omega:[0,+\infty]\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
, that satisfies
\omega(0)=0
and
\alpha>0
, such that

F(y,r,\alpha(x-y),Y)-F(x,r,\alpha(x-y),X)\leq\omega(\alpha{\left|x-y\right|}^{2}+{\left|x-y\right|}),

whenever
x,y\in\Omega
,
r\in\R
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{S}(n)
and it holds,

-3\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\
0&-Y\end{pmatrix}\leq 3\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{pmatrix}.

Let
u
be an upper semicontinuous function in
\overline{\Omega}
and a supersolution to
F=0
in
\Omega
, and let
v
be a lower semicontinuous function and a subsolution to
F=0
in
\Omega
. Then,

\max_{\Omega}(v-u)=\max_{\partial\Omega}(v-u).

The following proposition clarifies the link between weak solutions and viscosity solutions.

Proposition 2.2
.

A continuous weak solution
u
to (
2
) is a viscosity solution to (
2
).

Proof.

Let
u
be a continuous weak solution to (
2
), and let us prove that
u
is a viscosity supersolution to (
2
).

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
, and let us consider a function
\phi
such that
\phi(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
and such that
u-\phi
has a strict minimum at
x_{0}
, we claim that

-\Delta_{p}\phi(x_{0})-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega){\left|\phi(x_{0})\right|}^{p-2}\phi(x_{0})\geq 0.

(8)

By contradiction, let us assume that (
8
) is false, then there exists
B_{r}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega
such that

-\Delta_{p}\phi(x)-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega){\left|\phi(x)\right|}^{p-2}\phi(x)<0,\quad\forall x\in B_{r}(x_{0}),

(9)

so we can define

m=\inf_{{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}=r}u-\phi,\quad\psi=\phi+\frac{m}{2}.

The function
\psi
is such that

\psi(x_{0})>u(x_{0}),\quad\psi<u\text{ on }\partial B_{r}(x_{0}),

and if we choose
r
small enough, we have

-\Delta_{p}\psi(x)-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega){\left|\psi(x)\right|}^{p-2}\psi(x)<0\quad\forall x\in B_{r}(x_{0}),

(10)

so if we use
(\psi-u)_{+}
in the weak formulation of (
2
), we get

\int_{\psi>u}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla(\psi-u)_{+}\,dx=\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\int_{\psi>u}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u(\psi-u)_{+}\,dx,

while, if we multiply (
10
) by
(\psi-u)_{+}
and we integrate over
\Omega
, we get

\int_{\psi>u}{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\psi\nabla(\psi-u)_{+}\,dx<\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\int_{\psi>u}{\left|\psi\right|}^{p-2}\psi(\psi-u)_{+}\,dx.

This implies

\displaystyle C(n,p)\int_{\psi>u}{\left|\nabla\psi-\nabla u\right|}^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\psi>\psi}\left\langle{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\psi-{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla(\psi-u)\right\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle<\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\int_{\psi>u}\left({\left|\psi\right|}^{p-2}\psi-{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u\right)(\psi-u)\,dx<0,

and this is a contradiction.

Now, let us consider
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, and let us consider a function
\phi
such that
\phi(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
and such that
u-\phi
has a strict minimum at
x_{0}
, we claim that

\max\left\{-\Delta_{p}\phi(x_{0})-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega){\left|\phi(x_{0})\right|}^{p-2}\phi(x_{0}),{\left|\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right|}^{p-2}\frac{\partial\phi(x_{0})}{\partial\nu}-\beta^{p}{\left|\phi(x_{0})\right|}^{p-2}\phi(x_{0})\right\}\geq 0.

As before, let us assume by contradiction that there exists
B_{r}(x_{0})
such that both terms are negative. If we choose
r
sufficiently small, in
\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
, we have

-\Delta_{p}\psi(x)-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega){\left|\psi(x)\right|}^{p-2}\psi(x)<0

(11)

and, in
\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
,

{\left|\nabla\psi(x)\right|}^{p-2}\frac{\partial\psi(x)}{\partial\nu}-\beta^{p}{\left|\phi(x)\right|}^{p-2}\phi(x)<0,

where, as in the previous case,

\psi=\phi+\frac{m}{2}\text{ and }\displaystyle m=\inf_{{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}=r}u-\phi.

If we choose
(\psi-u)_{+}
in the weak formulation of (
2
), we get

\begin{multlined}\int_{\psi>u}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla(\psi-u)_{+}\,dx=\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\int_{\psi>u}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u(\psi-u)\,dx+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega\cap\Set{\psi>u}}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u(\psi-u)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{multlined}

while, if we multiply (
11
) by
(\psi-u)_{+}
and we integrate over
\Omega
, we get

\int_{\psi>u}{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\psi\nabla(\psi-u)_{+}\,dx<\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\int_{\psi>u}{\left|\psi\right|}^{p-2}\psi(\psi-u)\,dx+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega\cap\Set{\psi>u}}{\left|\phi\right|}^{p-2}\phi(\psi-u)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

This implies

\displaystyle C(n,p)\int_{\psi>u}{\left|\nabla\psi-\nabla u\right|}^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\psi>u}\left\langle{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{p-2}\nabla\psi-{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla(\psi-u)\right\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle<\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\int_{\psi>u}\left({\left|\psi\right|}^{p-2}\psi-{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u\right)(\psi-u)\,dx+

\displaystyle+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega\cap\Set{\psi>u}}\left({\left|\phi\right|}^{p-2}\phi-{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u\right)(\psi-u)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}<0,

which is absurd.

To prove that
u
is a viscosity subsolution, one can proceed in an analogous way.
∎

3
Proof of main results

We are now in position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us consider
w=1
as a test function in the definition (
1
), then we obtain

-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\geq\beta^{p}\dfrac{P(\Omega)}{{\left|\Omega\right|}},

passing to the limit for
p
that goes to
+\infty
, we obtain

\liminf_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq\beta.

Let
u_{p}
be the positive eigenfunction associated to
\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)
normalized such that
{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}=1
, we can write

\left(-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\right)\int_{\Omega}{\left|u_{p}\right|}^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u_{p}\right|}^{p}=\beta^{p}

(12)

hence, both quantities in (
12
) are smaller then
\beta^{p}
, and in particular,

\beta\left(\int_{\Omega}{\left|u_{p}\right|}^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}{\left|u_{p}\right|}^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\beta.

This implies that for every
m<p

\displaystyle{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq\beta{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p}};

\displaystyle{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{m}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{p}}.

By a classical argument of diagonalization, see for instance
[
6
]
, we can extract a subsequence
u_{p_{j}}
such that

\displaystyle u_{p_{j}}\to u_{\infty}\,\text{ uniformly}\implies\|u_{p_{j}}\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega)}\to{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p_{j}}\to\nabla u_{\infty}\,\text{ weakly in }\,L^{m}(\Omega),\,\forall m>1.

On the other hand, if we consider that

-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)=\dfrac{\beta^{p}-\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u_{p}\right|}^{p}\;dx}{\int_{\Omega}{\left|u_{p}\right|}^{p}\;dx}\leq\dfrac{\beta^{p}}{\int_{\Omega}{\left|u_{p}\right|}^{p}\;dx}

and

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}={\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}=1

then, passing to the limit, we have

\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\beta.

∎

The radial case

Let us consider

If we choose
u_{p}

0

t^{n-1}(u_{p}^{\prime}(t))^{p-1}=\int_{0}^{t}(-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega))(u_{p}(s))^{p-1}s^{n-1}\,ds

u_{p}(r)-u_{p}(0)=\int_{0}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{t^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t}(-\lambda_{p,\beta}(\Omega))(u_{p}(s))^{p-1}s^{n-1}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dt

where we have used (

The effect of group quality of life therapy on hope and health-related quality of life in patients with major depressive disorder: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Mohammad Malekzadeh, Simin Kokabiasl, Milad Amiri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Major depressive disorder (MDD) significantly impacts individuals’ functioning by detrimentally influencing both their mental and physical well-being, consequently affecting their overall quality of life. This study aims to assess the impact of quality of life therapy (QOLT) on hope and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) among patients with MDD.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This randomized clinical trial involved 56 patients with MDD who sought treatment at psychiatry clinics in Iran during the year 2022. Participants were selected through convenience sampling and subsequently assigned to control and intervention groups using a random blocked allocation method. Before the intervention, all patients completed the World Health Organization Quality of Life questionnaire and Snyder’s hope scale as part of a pretest. The intervention group underwent a 10-week QOLT program, consisting of weekly 90-min sessions. Following the intervention, both control and intervention groups completed posttests by re-evaluating the research questionnaires. The collected data were analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences software version 26, employing independent two-sample t-tests and paired t-tests.RESULTS:Before the intervention, the two groups did not exhibit significant differences in demographic variables, HRQoL, and hopefulness (P > 0.05). However, after the intervention, significant differences emerged between the groups in terms of HRQoL and hopefulness (P < 0.05). Conversely, the control group showed no significant changes throughout the study period (P > 0.05).CONCLUSION:This study showed that QOLT effectively improves HRQoL and hopefulness in patients with MDD. Consequently, QOLT can be considered as an impactful intervention for enhancing both HRQoL and hopefulness among patients with MDD.

Introduction

One of the diseases that demands great care and imposes high financial pressure worldwide is the major depressive disorder (MDD). This disease has considerable negative effects on peoples’ routine activities, quality of life, cognitive function, job-related performance, and productivity.[1] Those with this disorder usually suffer from depressed temperaments, low self-esteem, disturbed sleep or appetite, low self-care, and even suicidal attitudes.[2] According to the World Health Organization (WHO) report, about 5% of the world’s population is beset with depression. Research in this field has shown that people with MDD have a lower health-related quality of life (HRQoL) than normal individuals.[3]

HRQoL is a multidimensional concept that concerns the effect of health on the quality of life. It consists of physical, mental, psychological well-being, and social dimensions.[4] By definition, HRQoL refers to how well individuals can perform their functions and how they perceive their health in physical, psychological, and social dimensions.[5] This concept serves as a versatile tool for evaluating the everyday activities, physiological functions, and psychological satisfaction in one’s emotional and social life.[6] Depressed people are less healthy; indeed, the more depressed a person is, the poorer are his/her health conditions[7] and the lower is their quality of life.[8,9] HRQoL is related to suicidal thoughts. People with lower quality of life have more suicidal thoughts.[10,11] Moreover, depression and hopefulness are negatively correlated; that is, the more hopeful a person is, the less depressed is the person and vice versa.[12] Regarding the care and management of depressed patients, focusing on HRQoL and following up on the treatments are two important factors.[13] It has been shown that HRQoL and hopefulness are positively correlated. In other words, less hopeful people have a lower HRQoL and vice versa.[14,15]

Studying hopefulness seems to be important. This is because people begin to feel disappointed when they face hard situations or fail to attain their goals. Snyder, who introduced the hope theory and the corresponding therapies, considers hope as a double-feature concept and defines it as the ability to devise pathways toward one’s desired goals, despite obstacles in the way, and the agent that provides enough motivation to use those pathways. Hope plays both these roles; once a hopeful individual meets a problem, he or she assesses the chances and searches for an appropriate way forward.[16,17] Compared to others, a highly hopeful person has better health and well-being, is less depressed and pessimistic, as well as more perseverant and purposeful, and generally lives a more efficient life.[18] To discuss depression, HRQoL is considered in terms of physical, psychological, and social health and is increasingly pursued for its significant outcomes for psychic patients.[19] Considering the importance of enhancing HRQoL for depression patients, therapeutic priority is given to safe and complication-free procedures such as hope therapy[20] and quality of life therapy (QOLT). The latter is a comprehensive approach based on positive psychological orientation. It was Frisch (2006) who proposed QOLT by combining Aaron Beck’s cognitive behavioral therapy, positive psychology, metaphorical thinking, relaxation, and meditation. QOLT corroborates life satisfaction, and those who practice it are taught the principles and skills of helping the patients to identify and meet their needs as well as pursue their aims and desires in valuable areas of life. The ultimate goal of QOLT is to promote professional self-care, or self-enrichment, and to prevent fatigue.[21,22] This has resulted in a five-node model known as CASIO, which stands for circumstance, attitude, standards, importance, and overall satisfaction. The model provides an outline of achieving life satisfaction by determining the roles of quality of life and positive psychological interventions. In connection with QOLT, which mainly seeks to create happiness and satisfaction in life, the model serves as a pattern of life conditions, how life issues are looked at, the criteria defined to observe, the set of cherished values, and an overall sense of satisfaction.[22] As research has shown, antidepressant drugs alone cannot promote HRQoL over time. Therefore, future studies should not only focus on the short-term efficacy of such drugs, but also examine the long-term effects of medicinal and nonmedicinal interventions on HRQoL.[23] Generally, despite the research evidence for the effectiveness of antidepressants, their benefit for patients’ overall well-being and quality of life remains as a matter of controversy; hence, more evaluation is needed for the improvement of psychiatric care and service.[24]

Unlike traditional cognitive behavioral approaches that primarily focus on symptom reduction, QOLT centers on enhancing overall life satisfaction across various domains, including physical health, emotional well-being, and social relationships. This research is pioneering in its dual focus on improving hope – a psychological construct often diminished in depression and HRQoL, a comprehensive measure of how health status impacts overall well-being. The study investigates whether QOLT can provide a more holistic improvement in the lives of those with MDD, potentially offering a novel adjunct or alternative to existing therapeutic strategies. Considering the significance of HRQoL and hopefulness for MDD patients and the necessity of treatment compliance, the present study aims to determine the effect of group QOLT on hope and HRQoL in patients with MDD.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

The clinical trial study was conducted in patients with MDD from July to December 2022.

They were included in the study through convenience sampling and then allocated into intervention and control groups via random blocked allocation. This is a single-blind study, where the person who scored the questionnaires in the pretest and posttest phases was not aware of the study groups.

Study participants and sampling

The statistical population comprised 254 individuals with MDD who had been discharged from the Shahid Rajaei Psychiatric hospital following medication advice. The inclusion criteria included scores of <75 on the HRQoL questionnaire and <24 on the hopefulness scale, being over 18 years of age, and having cognitive capability for comprehension. The exclusion criteria included a severe physical disease and participation in a similar medical intervention in the last 3 months. The sample size was determined based on the study by Hashemi et al.,[25] with parameters set at α = 0.05, δ1 = 10.5, δ2 = 9.03, µ1 = 14.25, µ2 = 22.75, power = 90%, and number of groups = 2. Using Gpower software version 3.1.9.7, the effect size was calculated to be 0.89. Considering a 10% probability of sample withdrawal, a sample size of 30 individuals per group was established. A total of 60 eligible MDD patients were selected, and random blocked allocation was employed to evenly assign them to an intervention group and a control group. Blocks in this technique were created with capacities of 2, 4, and 6, and sequencing and concealment were executed based on a website, generating a specific randomization code.[26]

Data collection tool and technique

Participants were assured of the confidentiality of their data, and informed consent was obtained for their participation in the study. Subsequently, both groups underwent the pretest by completing the research questionnaire and the demographics form. The intervention group was subdivided into three groups of 10 members each, who participated in a 90-min QOLT session for 10 weeks. Meanwhile, the control group received no intervention during this period. Following the intervention, participants in both groups completed a posttest by filling out the research questionnaires again [Figure 1]. The QOLT sessions were conducted by a psychology professor and a master of psychiatric nursing, and the training was structured based on Michael Frisch’s studies.[21] The session contents are outlined in Table 1.

The subscales are concerned with physical health, psychological health, social relations, and environmental health. In addition, each question is answered on a 5-point Likert scale, and the higher the mark, the better the quality of life.[27] Snyder’s hope scale The hope questionnaire prepared by Snyder et al. This questionnaire is designed for people 15 years old and above. From these statements, four statements are used to measure Agency Thinking (his refers to an individual’s belief in their ability to achieve their goals. In other words, the person feels capable of working towards their desired outcome.) (questions 2, 9, 10, and 12), four statements are used to measure Pathways Thinking (This refers to the person’s ability to identify possible solutions or routes to reach their goals. It means the person knows how to plan and figure out ways to achieve their objectives.) (questions 1, 4, 7, and 8), and four deviation statements (questions 3, 5, 6, and 11) is Therefore, this questionnaire measures two subscales of agent and strategy. Although the hope scale consists of 12 items, four items were not included for data analysis due to their deviant nature. Two subscales of agent and strategy (four questions for each subscale) are defined with an eight-point Likert scale of score 1–8. A score of 1 means I completely disagree and a score of 8 means I completely agree. Omid’s overall score ranges from 8 to 64. Higher scores indicate better levels of hope. Validity and reliability of hope scale in Persian language have been confirmed.[28,29]

The collected data were analyzed with the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences-26 software, an independent two-sample t-test for between-group comparison, and a paired t-test for within-group comparison. The level of significance in this study was 0.05.

Discussion

Nowadays, various psychotherapeutic interventions are used in medicinal treatments to improve the health conditions of patients, including those suffering from psychological conditions.[30,31] Considering that MDD is a highly common and costly disease all over the world,[32] treating the patients seems to be of great importance. According to the literature in this field, MDD patients have low HRQoL and hopefulness.[33,34,35] One of the therapeutic techniques to cope with psycho-physical problems is QOLT.[22,36,37] In the present study, QOLT was practiced for MDD patients in 10 sessions, and HRQoL and hopefulness were assessed as two variables before and after the intervention. The results pointed to the positive and significant effect of QOLT on the patients’ HRQoL and hopefulness.

According to Iqballa and Dwiastuti,[38] QOLT can mitigate the depression of those who were offended or misbehaved in adolescence. In this respect, their study and the present one are in agreement. Since QOLT is rooted in positive psychology, the participants in QOLT sessions learn how to boost their satisfaction in some of the 16 aspects of life. Such training is crucial for the improvement of satisfaction and total quality of life.[39] Furthermore, in QOLT sessions, the participating patients are trained about spontaneous negative thoughts and the ways of coping with them. They learn how to overcome these thoughts as well as the negative feelings stemming from them. During training through the CASIO model, living a purposeful life and seeking meaning are emphasized. This is because learning the skills for a purposeful life leads to happiness and a sense of satisfaction in patients. In agreement with the results of this study, Carroll et al.[22] have shown that psychotherapy via teaching health issues with an emphasis on quality of life can give patients positive feelings. In short, if QOLT is included in a general treatment protocol, patients’ life satisfaction and positive feelings are boosted and their negative emotions are reduced. This brings them a better-quality life and more happiness.[40]

Frisch[41] found that a package of QOLT interventions devised based on positive psychological and cognitive approaches would improve the life satisfaction and psychological well-being of clinical and nonclinical groups. Similarly, according to Ahmadi et al.,[42] QOLT could raise the mental well-being of the patients participating in a trial and prevent various psychic disorders. As a matter of fact, through QOLT programs, patients learn to identify their needs, goals, and values as well as the ways to meet or fulfill them. So, once their psychological well-being is improved, they begin to experience enjoyable public relations.

Munoz et al.[43] consider hopefulness as one of the factors affecting HRQoL. Hence, injection of hope into individuals makes them healthier and improves their quality of life. In this line of research, Hashemi et al.[25] found QOLT to be capable of mitigating anxiety and depression in Multiple Sclerosis patients and enhancing their hopefulness. Usually, QOLT is referred to as a method of increasing happiness and improving quality of life. Through it, patients learn that happiness is a choice. It helps them identify and analyze their feelings and emotions, modify and manage their negative feelings, value their positive feelings, develop unanimity with others, remember their assets and endowments, and ultimately get rid of their negative temperament. Indeed, QOLT puts more positive living schemes at the disposal of patients. After doing the assignments of therapy sessions and learning the basic skills of life, they feel enabled to control adverse cognitive processing and seek ways to promote their happiness and positive feelings.[14]

Considering the significance of MDD and the costs that it imposes, it is of benefit to use medicinal treatments along with psychotherapeutic techniques. In MDD, which is associated with a reduction in HRQoL and hopefulness, patients are likely to commit suicide. This can bring many adverse economic, social, and cultural consequences. In these circumstances, QOLT serves to correct the negative aspects of patients, as well as identify, reinforce, and give priority to their positive features. The happiness and life satisfaction elevated in this way lead to higher HRQoL and hopefulness. The integration of group QOLT into mental health services has important implications for both health and education policies. From a health policy perspective, the inclusion of QOLT as a standard therapeutic option could lead to more holistic treatment approaches for major depression, potentially reducing the burden on health-care systems by improving patient outcomes and reducing relapse rates. In educational settings, particularly in schools and universities, the implementation of QOLT programs could help address the rising incidence of depression among students by promoting resilience, hope, and overall mental well-being.

Limitations and recommendation

In this study, a point of special importance is the desirable effect of group QOLT on MDD patients. This type of therapy yields better results owing to the association of patients together, taking support from one another, and their chance to practice and do assignments collectively. Therefore, the efficacy of the independent variable in this therapy may partly be attributed to the collective action. The QOLT approach addresses 16 aspects of life. This wide domain provides clinical practitioners with good maneuverability to affect their patients. Another advantage of QOLT is its potential to keep healthy individuals from depression and despair.

Like any other research, this study was conducted with some limitations. For example, therapeutic trainings were done, but there were no follow-up checks on the participants. A 3- or 6-month follow-up would possibly help to find out the long-term effects of QOLT. Considering the Progress of virtual space and the possibility of using QQLT intervention, it is recommended to carry out this intervention online for a larger group of patients.

Conclusion

The present study showed that QOLT helps to improve the HRQoL and hopefulness of patients suffering from MDD. Therefore, this type of therapy can be considered a useful and effective intervention to enhance the HRQoL and hopefulness of those patients. The impact of group QOLT on hope and HRQoL in individuals with major depression highlights the importance of addressing the broader aspects of life satisfaction in mental health treatment. By enhancing hope and improving the overall quality of life, group QOLT offers a promising approach to supporting long-term recovery in patients with major depression. Policymakers in both health and education sectors should consider the benefits of integrating such therapies into existing mental health programs to promote holistic well-being and improve the quality of life for individuals suffering from depression.

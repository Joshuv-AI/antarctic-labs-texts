Distributed robust optimization strategy for multi-energy virtual power plant clusters
Authors: Ziyang Wang, Hao Guo, Ruijin Zhu, Zixuan Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
As renewable energy penetration continues to rise, the demand for coordinated optimization of decentralized source-load-storage. Virtual power plant (VPP) addresses this need by aggregating and coordinating diverse resources. However, effective mechanisms for multi-agent VPP coordination remain limited. To address this, this paper proposes a distributed robust optimization strategy for multi-energy VPP clusters in high-altitude regions. This strategy combines a dual-norm uncertainty set with a Nash bargaining mechanism to coordinate multi-agent interactions and mitigate operational risks. First, addressing the integrated planning-operation problem for multi-energy VPPs, a two-stage distributed robust optimization model is established. The dual-norm uncertainty set characterizes scenario probability uncertainties. Second, aiming to minimize interaction costs, a bargaining model is proposed based on Nash bargaining theory. Finally, case studies are conducted on three multi-energy VPPs. Results demonstrate that the proposed optimization strategy effectively enhances overall system revenue, environmental benefits, and source-load matching capability while ensuring fair competition among VPPs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-28736-6.

Introduction

Due to the low oxygen content in the air in plateau regions, the resulting hypoxia significantly affects people’s production and daily lives. The government and relevant scholars have been dedicated to addressing oxygen supply issue in these high-altitude areas. The 14th Five-Year Plan of Tibet in 2021 explicitly emphasized the necessity and development goals for oxygen supply in plateau regions1–3. Simultaneously, according to China’s lofty objectives of attaining the peak of carbon by 2030 and being carbon–neutral by 2060, the development of renewable energy and energy-saving emission reduction has become urgent. Tibet, as the national base for clean energy, advanced several strategic objectives and timelines. However, the geographical distribution of renewable energy in Tibet is excessively scattered4, leading to a lack of coordination, output volatility, and challenges in accurate prediction. The intermittency of renewable energy constrains the dispatch capability of integrated energy systems, hindering the effective integration of the Integrated Energy System (IES). Achieving reliable operation under fluctuating conditions has become a critical concern. VPP have emerged to address this challenge by aggregating and coordinating distributed energy resources, thereby enhancing dispatch capability and system integration levels5–10.

VPP as one of the important technologies for realizing the smart grid, integrates, optimizes, and coordinates decentralized distributed energy systems within a region through advanced technical means. This promotes the rational optimization of resources and increases the absorption rate of renewable energy11,12. Current research on VPPs is extensive and has achieved certain results. Literature13 suggest an effective robust self-arrangement approach for mitigating uncertainties related to energy costs, wind energy production, and power demand. Literature14 considers the geographical differences of distributed energy and numerous small-scale energy producers and consumers, proposing a day-ahead thermoelectric scheduling algorithm for optimizing large-scale VPPs. Literature15 proposes a bi-level real-time economic dispatch strategy for VPPs, considering the different interests of VPP systems and operators and the uncertainties of distributed renewable energy and loads. Literature16 to address the issues of output fluctuations in remote sustainable energy and time variations in load demand, this study introduces a load aggregating for regulation and suggests an economic operation approach appropriate for VPP. Literature17 proposes a VPP collaborative operational framework that incorporates optimized scheduling, data security and day-ahead price forecasting (Prophet), and completes case validation in VPP scenarios. However, Nash bargaining is not employed for settlement or revenue distribution.

In a cluster containing multiple VPP entities, the ADMM algorithm combined with Nash bargaining theory is typically used to address cooperation and competition issues among multiple entities, considering their mutual benefits and energy exchanges. The Nash bargaining model is a non-convex nonlinear optimization problem18, distributed solution schemes are common. A two-stage coordination mechanism based on ADMM for VPP clusters was introduced in literature19 with day-ahead Nash bargaining and intraday pricing. However, the study did not consider a distributionally robust treatment of scenario probabilities. It also did not make use of the dual-norm ambiguity set, nor was the worst-case expected operating cost optimized within a distributionally robust framework. Literature20 proposes a distributed multi-microgrid energy-sharing strategy based on Nash games. However, the following were not modelled: probability ambiguity through distributionally robust optimization with a dual-norm ambiguity set; the post-dispatch price-alignment layer; and oxygen/VPSA coupling. A P2P-to-grid trading framework with dynamic operating envelopes and communication-aware ADMM was presented in literature21. However, network and communication aspects were prioritized, whereas dual-norm DRO, worst-case expectation, and a post-dispatch bargaining layer were not integrated. Literature22 proposes a collaborative operation scheme for multiple virtual power plants and load aggregators based on Nash game theory and the dynamic penalty ADMM algorithm. However, adversarial scenario-probability reweighting was not included, a two-stage iterative master–subproblem procedure to optimize the worst-case expected cost was not employed, and without considering the oxygen load.

Given the variability in renewable energy generation and User’s spontaneous behavior, the safe and economic operation of the power system is greatly affected23–25. Currently, to address the uncertainty of renewable energy output, research mainly utilizes stochastic optimization26–28 and robust optimization methods29–31 to effectively solve system operation problems. However, these two optimization methods are overly conservative. Some scholars have also adopted distributed robust optimization methods to study the uncertainty of renewable energy output32–35. Literature36 suggests a novel, robust optimization approach that’s designed to handle the complex interplay of various regional integrated energy systems, accounting for the intricate multi-energy flow connections. The study reveals that this innovative strategy holds the potential to not only enhance the overall economic efficiency of these systems but also bolster their overall stability. Literature37 presents a two-stage distributionally robust optimization model for virtual power plant operation, coordinating day-ahead scheduling under renewable uncertainty and validating the framework with case studies. However, a dual-norm ambiguity set is not adopted, a post-dispatch Nash price-coordination layer is not separated from physical scheduling. Literature38 develops a scenario-based distributed robust optimization approach for optimal scheduling of virtual power plants, enabling scalable coordination across multiple entities. However, the worst-case expected operating cost is not optimized via an iterative master–subproblem procedure with a dual-norm probability set.

Currently, there is relatively little research on integrated energy systems in plateau regions, with only my research group conducting related studies. Literature39 establishes an energy cooperation framework for high-altitude prosumers and proposes an asymmetric profit distribution model for multi-energy sharing, ultimately enhancing the profits of individual entities and the overall alliance. Literature40 introduces a two-tiered energy collaboration model designed for high-altitude prosumer networks. This framework addresses the evolving energy exchange dynamics in plateau areas, boosting economic returns while optimizing collective benefits. It also fosters long-term viability for the shared prosumer market in these elevated regions. Literature41 based on Tibet’s power supply and growth planning, provides an innovative optimization strategy for integrated energy system clusters, increasing the multi-energy sharing system’s economic efficiency.

Based on the above research, this paper proposes a two-stage distributed robust optimization strategy for virtual power plant clusters in high-altitude regions, addressing the interplay between oxygen supply requirements and multi-energy flows. The model employs dual-norm fuzzy sets and an iterative master–slave problem approach to handle worst-case expected costs, enabling multi-energy flow interactions among VPPs. Nash bargaining is applied to optimize economic dispatch. Additionally, sensitivity analysis is conducted to validate result robustness, ensuring the model’s reliability under varying conditions.

Under the simultaneous constraints of 1-norm and ∞-norm, a scheduling strategy is constructed based on the mechanism of multi-energy flow interactions, which synergizes system robustness and economy.

The principal contributions of this paper are outlined below:(1) This paper examines energy demand and development strategies for high-altitude regions, has constructed the electrical, thermal, oxygen, and other multi-energy flow VPP system model framework by coupling devices like vacuum pressure swing adsorption (VPSA) and gas turbine (GT).(2) By combining the uncertainty probability set of norm constraints, it describes the uncertainty of scenarios, and the worst-case scenario’s probability value is determined by updating the scenario possibility values.(3) Aiming at the planning and operation problems of multi-energy VPP, a distributed robust optimization model considering double norm constraints has been established. Secondly, the Nash negotiation model has been constructed with the goal of minimizing interaction costs. Ultimately, the methodology outlined in this document has been confirmed as effective.

VPP System model

VPP System operation model

Figure 1 is the energy flow architecture diagram of the VPP cluster, which implements information transmission and exchange through a control center. An energy management center exists in each VPP. The dispatching commands can only be conveyed between various VPPs through the energy management center. The power generation units, oxygen supply units, and heating units in each VPP will first meet their own load requirements. If there is excess electricity at this time, it can be directly transmitted to other VPP to ensure their normal operation. When the power generation capacity of a certain VPP cannot meet its own load demand, it can receive electricity from other VPP or purchase electricity from the power grid. The natural gas network undertakes the task of one-way delivery of natural gas from natural gas suppliers to power generation and heating equipment in each VPP. At the same time, these energy conversion devices can convert natural gas into electricity and heat to meet the demand for different energies of each VPP.Fig. 1VPP energy sharing architecture diagram.

VPP energy sharing architecture diagram.

Figure 2 is a single VPP system architecture diagram, which includes a gas turbine (GT), wind turbine (WT) or photovoltaic (PV), a gas boiler (GB), an electric cooler (EC), an absorption chiller (AC), and a vacuum pressure swing adsorption (VPSA). The load includes electricity load, heat load, cold load, and oxygen load. The electricity load in VPP can only obtain electricity from the external grid, WT, PV, and GT. The demand for natural gas for each equipment within each VPP comes from the natural gas supplier. In summary, the external grid, WT, PV, and GT are equivalent to the power supply of the VPP cluster, and the natural gas supplier is the source of natural gas for the entire VPP cluster. A variety of energy conversion devices in the system can convert electrical energy and natural gas into energy required by different types of loads to meet various needs.Fig. 2Architecture of a single VPP system.

Architecture of a single VPP system.

Model framework introduction

An iterative two-stage distributed robust optimization with a separate price-coordination module is used to coordinate a multi-energy VPP cluster. The framework diagram is in Fig. 3. Bilateral exchanges of electricity and oxygen are permitted, and antisymmetric constraints ensure pairwise conservation. In each iteration, the master problem selects device capacities and enforces an upper bound on the expected operating cost; given these capacities, a joint 24-h scheduling subproblem is solved for all historical scenarios to obtain per-scenario costs and planned P2P trades while satisfying balances, unit limits, and buy–sell exclusivity. Scenario probabilities are updated within a dual-norm ambiguity set to yield the worst-case expected cost and cut-and-column generation proceeds until the upper and lower bounds meet. The scheduled trades are then priced in a Nash-bargaining/ADMM layer, where bilateral prices are aligned within a price floor and time-of-use ceiling, leaving physical dispatch unchanged. The formulation is indexed by the VPP set and scales from three to any finite number of participants without altering the model structure.Fig. 3A Distributed Robust Optimization Scheduling Framework for Multi-Energy VPP Clusters.

A Distributed Robust Optimization Scheduling Framework for Multi-Energy VPP Clusters.

Two-stage distributed robust optimization model

The unpredictable nature of distributed robust optimization outcomes and forecasting inaccuracies can negatively impact VPP cluster planning and operations. To address this challenge, a novel VPP cluster planning and scheduling approach was developed, leveraging dual norm constraints for enhanced reliability.

This paper decomposes the Nash negotiation cluster scheduling model into three problems, namely the main problem (MP) and two sub-problems (SP1 and SP2). Convergence analysis of the algorithm

This paper is based on the Nash bargaining theory and obtains the interactive electricity price through the ADMM algorithm. The objective function is a multiple-entity single-variable, and the constraint conditions are the electricity price constraint, oxygen price constraint, and the constraint that the income of a single VPP system is greater than 0. Figure 4 is the iterative convergence graph of each VPP and the distributed robust optimization algorithm. By comparing Table 7, it is found that when the confidence level of the 1-norm increases from 0.20 to 0.90, the operating cost under the double -norm constraint decreases by 32 CNY, 59 CNY, and 124 CNY, respectively, compared to the operating cost under the 1-norm constraint.

The double-norm constraint jointly bounds aggregate and per-scenario probability deviations, yielding a tighter and more balanced ambiguity set that mitigates overconcentration on extreme scenarios and produces robust yet not overly conservative decisions.Their intersection (double-norm) restricts both dimensions simultaneously. Under identical confidence levels, the intersection moderates the adversarial reweighting of scenarios and avoids concentrating probability mass solely through total-mass relocation or through a few extreme scenarios. A smaller risk premium is therefore observed, and the worst-case expected operating cost is slightly lower under the double-norm than under the single-norm cases. Operationally, this corresponds to hedging that reduces opportunistic selling, slightly increases self-supply at the margin, and moderates P2P exchanges, while all device limits and feasibility constraints remain satisfied.

In summary, the operating cost under double-norm constraint is lower than that under single-norm constraint.

Conclusion

This paper constructs a multi-energy VPP cluster using multiple sources such as electricity, heat, cooling, and oxygen. Based on a robust optimization model with double -norm constraints and the Nash bargaining model, an optimal operation model for the multi-energy VPP cluster is established. The conclusions drawn are as follows:(1) Multi-energy VPP Cluster for High Plateau Regions: To address the oxygen supply needs and energy development planning for high plateau regions, a multi-energy VPP cluster coupling electricity, heat, cooling, and oxygen is established. Assessing Tibet’s present energy conditions and national “dual carbon” goals, various energy devices are coupled, reduce the overall intensity of carbon emissions and improve the consumption capacity of renewable energy. Through a reasonable pricing mechanism, the enthusiasm of the demand side is improved without compromising the interests of all parties.(2) Under the dual-norm constraint robust optimization model, operating costs at different confidence levels are lower than those under single-norm constraints: Compared to the 1-norm, costs decrease by 32, 59, and 124 CNY respectively; compared to the ∞-norm, costs decrease by 73, 112, and 3 CNY respectively. This approach enables coordinated optimization between robustness and economic efficiency. Consequently, distributed robust optimization demonstrates excellent performance in addressing the uncertainty of renewable energy output within VPP clusters. Simultaneously, the two confidence intervals of the dual-norm serve as parameters reflecting the decision-maker’s risk preference. Lower conservatism yields lower operating costs, requiring decision-makers to select different confidence intervals based on their varying risk preferences.(3) Compared to operating independently, the multi-functional interaction and resource sharing within a cluster can reduce overall costs and enhance efficiency. By coordinating exchanges to smooth out temporal and spatial variations in renewable energy output, the model improves overall utilization rates and reduces curtailment of wind and solar power. Additionally, sensitivity analysis was conducted to validate the robustness of the results, ensuring the model’s reliability under various conditions.

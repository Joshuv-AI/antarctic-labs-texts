The geometry of simplicial distributions on suspension scenarios
Aziz Kharoof

Abstract
Quantum measurements often exhibit non-classical features, such as contextuality, which generalizes Bell’s non-locality and serves as a resource in various quantum computation models. Existing frameworks have rigorously captured these phenomena, and recently, simplicial distributions have been introduced to deepen this understanding. The geometrical structure of simplicial distributions can be seen as a resource for applications in quantum information theory. In this work, we use topological foundations to study this geometrical structure, leveraging the fact that, in this simplicial framework, measurements and outcomes are represented as spaces. This allows us to depict contextuality as a topological phenomenon. We show that applying the cone construction to the measurement space makes the corresponding non-signaling polytope equal to the join of m m copies of the original polytope, where m m is the number of possible outcomes per measurement. Then we glue two copies of cone measurement spaces to obtain a suspension measurement space. The decomposition done for simplicial distributions on a cone measurement space provides deeper insights into the geometry of simplicial distributions on a suspension measurement space and aids in characterizing the contextuality there. Additionally, we apply these results to derive a new type of Bell inequalities (inequalities that determine the set of local joint probabilities/non-contextual simplicial distributions) and to offer a mathematical explanation for certain contextual vertices from the literature.

Simplicial distributions

Simplicial distributions were first introduced in

2.1
Simplicial scenarios

A simplicial distribution is defined for a space of measurements and a space of outcomes, both represented by simplicial sets. Simplicial sets are combinatorial models of topological spaces that are more expressive than simplicial complexes. A
simplicial set

X
consists of a sequence of sets
X_{0},X_{1},\cdots
and associated simplicial structure maps.
These maps, given by face maps
d_{i}^{X}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n-1}
and degeneracy maps
s_{j}^{X}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n+1}
.
The elements in
X_{n}
called
n
-simplices, so the face and degeneracy maps
encode how to glue and collapse the simplices, respectively (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
). We usually denote the simplicial structure maps by
d_{i}
and
s_{j}
omitting the simplicial set from the notation.
A simplex is called

•

degenerate
if it lies in the image of a degeneracy map,

•

non-degenerate
if it is not degenerate, and

•

A
generating simplex
if it is non-degenerate and not a face of another simplex.

A
simplicial map

f\colon X\to Y
between two simplicial sets is defined as a sequence of functions
\{f_{n}\colon X_{n}\to Y_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

that respect the face and the degeneracy maps. For a simplex
x\in X_{n}
we will write
f_{x}
instead of
f_{n}(x)
. With this notation the compatibility conditions are given by

d_{i}(f_{x})=f_{d_{i}(x)}\;\;\text{and}\;\;s_{j}(f_{x})=f_{s_{j}(x)}

The category of simplicial sets is denoted by
s\mathbf{Set}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
distribution monad

[
21
, Section VI]
is a functor
D\colon\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
defined as follows:

•

For a set
X
, we define the set

of distributions on
X
to be

D(X)=\{P\colon X\to[0,\infty):~|\{x\in X:~P(x)\neq 0\}|<\infty\,\,\text{and}\,\,\sum_{x\in X}P(x)=1\}

•

For a map
f\colon X\to Y
, the map
D(f)\colon D(X)\to D(Y)
defined by

P\mapsto\left(y\mapsto\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}P(x)\right).

The unit of the distribution monad

sends
x\in X
to the delta distribution

\delta^{x}(x^{\prime})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&x^{\prime}=x\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The distribution monad extended to a functor

by sending a simplicial set
X

to the simplicial set

whose n-simplices are given by the set

and the simplicial structure maps are given by marginalization along the structure maps of

D(d_{i})\colon D(X_{n})\to D(X_{n-1})\;\;\text{and}\;\;D(s_{i})\colon D(X_{n})\to D(X_{n+1})

It turns out that this extended functor is also a monad; see
[
22
, Proposition 2.4]
. The unit of this monad satisfies

3
Decomposition of the event space

The join of convex sets serves as the coproduct in the category of convex sets,
\mathbf{Conv}

[
28

3.1
The set of distributions on disjoint union

The category of convex sets, being a category of algebras over a monad on
\mathbf{Set}
, is cocomplete
[
29
, Proposition 9.3.4]
. We characterize finite coproduct of free convex sets as the set of distributions on disjoint union.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\{\bullet\}
be the one-element convex set. For any convex set

, we define the set

A_{\bullet}=\{\langle{\lambda,a}\rangle\in[0,1]\times A\sqcup\{\bullet\}:\;\lambda=0\;\;\text{iff}\;\;a=\bullet\}

Sometimes we will write
\langle{0,x}\rangle
even if

is an expression that does not make sense. In this case, by
\langle{0,x}\rangle
we mean
\langle{0,\bullet}\rangle
.

The set

is a convex set with the structure:

t_{1}\langle{\lambda_{1},a_{1}}\rangle+\dots+t_{m}\langle{\lambda_{m},a_{m}}\rangle=\langle{\zeta,\frac{t_{1}\lambda_{1}}{\zeta}a_{1}+\dots+\frac{t_{m}\lambda_{m}}{\zeta}a_{m}}\rangle

where
\zeta=t_{1}\lambda_{1}+\dots+t_{m}\lambda_{m}
. In addition, the construction in Definition
3.1

gives us an endfunctor on
\mathbf{Conv}
such that for
f\colon A\to B
in
\mathbf{Conv}
, the map

f_{\bullet}\colon A_{\bullet}\to B_{\bullet}
defined by
f_{\bullet}(\lambda,a)=(\lambda,f(a))
(see
[
30
, Definition 3 and Lemma 4]
).
Now, we define the coproduct of finite number of convex sets using
[
30
, Proposition 5]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given
A_{1},\dots,A_{m}\in\mathbf{Conv}
, we define the coproduct (the

to be the following convex set:

\{\left(\langle{\lambda_{1},a_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\lambda_{m},a_{m}}\rangle\right)\in(A_{1})_{\bullet}\times\dots\times(A_{m})_{\bullet}:\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1\}

with the structures maps
\kappa_{i}\colon A_{i}\to A_{1}\star A_{2}\star\dots\star A_{m}
defined by

\kappa_{i}(a)=\left(\langle{0,\bullet}\rangle,\dots,\langle{1,a}\rangle,\dots,\langle{0,\bullet}\rangle\right)

are elements of the form
\kappa_{i}(a)
where

Proof.

Every element
\left(\langle{\lambda_{1},a_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\lambda_{m},a_{m}}\rangle\right)\in A_{1}\star A_{2}\star\dots\star A_{m}
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\left(\langle{1,a_{1}}\rangle,\langle{0,a_{2}}\rangle\dots,\langle{0,a_{m}}\rangle\right)+\dots+\lambda_{m}\left(\langle{0,a_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{0,a_{m-1}}\rangle,\langle{1,a_{m}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\kappa_{1}(a_{1})+\dots+\lambda_{m}\kappa_{m}(a_{m})

Therefore, it suffices to show that
\kappa_{i}(a_{i})
is a vertex if and only if

is
a vertex. Since
\kappa_{i}
is injective, if
\kappa_{i}(a_{i})
is a vertex then by
[

we get that
a_{i}
is
a vertex. Now, suppose conversely that for some

we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}(a_{i})

\displaystyle=t\left(\langle{\lambda_{1},b_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\lambda_{m},b_{m}}\rangle\right)+(1-t)\left(\langle{\mu_{1},c_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\mu_{m},c_{m}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\left(t\langle{\lambda_{1},b_{1}}\rangle+(1-t)\langle{\mu_{1},c_{1}}\rangle,\dots,t\langle{\lambda_{m},b_{m}}\rangle+(1-t)\langle{\mu_{m},c_{m}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\langle{\zeta_{1},\frac{t\lambda_{1}}{\zeta_{1}}b_{1}+\frac{(1-t)\mu_{1}}{\zeta_{1}}c_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\zeta_{m},\frac{t\lambda_{m}}{\zeta_{m}}b_{m}+\frac{(1-t)\mu_{m}}{\zeta_{m}}c_{m}}\rangle\right)

where

for

. We conclude that

t\lambda_{j}+(1-t)\mu_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\;\;j=i\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

So
\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}=1
and
\lambda_{j}=\mu_{j}=0
for every

If

is a vertex then

\displaystyle\left(\langle{\lambda_{1},b_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\lambda_{m},b_{m}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\langle{0,b_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{1,b_{i}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{0,b_{m}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\langle{0,c_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{1,c_{i}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{0,c_{m}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\langle{\mu_{1},c_{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\mu_{m},c_{m}}\rangle\right)

∎

4.2

In this section, we introduce the cone scenario

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
X
be a finitely generated, connected simplicial set. Given a set of vertices
\{q^{i,j}:~0\leq i,j\leq m-1\}
in
\operatorname{sDist}(X,\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
, and a line
L=L^{(3)}\subseteq X

such that

•

For every
0\leq j\leq m-1
the set of vertices
\{q^{i,j}:~0\leq i\leq m-1\}
is closed (see Definition
2.19
).

•

q^{i,j}|_{L}=\delta^{\varphi^{i,j}}
, where

\{\varphi^{i,j}:~0\leq i,j\leq m-1\}
is a complete collection of deterministic distributions as
in Definition
5.5
.

•

There is
\psi\in s\mathbf{Set}(X,\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
such that

\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\frac{1}{m^{2}}q^{i,j}=\psi\cdot\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\frac{1}{m^{2}}q^{i,j}
and

\psi|_{L}=\psi^{h}
(see Lemma
5.7
).

Let
p^{j}=\frac{1}{m}q^{0,j}+\dots+\frac{1}{m}q^{m-1,j}
, then the simplicial distribution

(39)

\left(\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{m-1}}\rangle;\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{m-1}}\rangle\right)

is a contextual vertex in
\operatorname{sDist}(\Sigma X,\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
(see Remark
5.3
).

Proof.

First, the simplicial distribution in (
39
) is well defined because of the following:

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}p^{j}=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\frac{1}{m^{2}}q^{i,j}=\psi\cdot\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\frac{1}{m^{2}}q^{i,j}=\psi\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}p^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}\psi\cdot p^{j}

Given simplicial distributions

p^{\prime}\in\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{Vsupp}(\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{m-1}}\rangle)\right)\;\;,\;\;p^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{Vsupp}(\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{m-1}}\rangle)\right)

such that
(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime})=(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime\prime})
. By Proposition
4.12
and
since
\{q^{i,j}:~0\leq i\leq m-1\}
is a closed set of vertices for every
0\leq j\leq m-1
, we have

\operatorname{Vsupp}\left(\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{m-1}}\rangle\right)=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{m-1}\kappa_{j}\left(\operatorname{Vsupp}(p^{j})\right)=\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{m-1}\{\kappa_{j}(q^{0,j}),\dots,\kappa_{j}(q^{m-1,j})\}

So there is
\lambda_{ij}\in[0,1]
where
\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\lambda_{ij}=1
, such that

p^{\prime}=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\lambda_{ij}\kappa_{j}(q^{i,j})

Thus
(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime})=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\lambda_{ij}q^{i,j}
.
Similarly, and using Proposition
2.26
, there is
\mu_{ij}\in[0,1]
where

\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\mu_{ij}=1
, such that

p^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\mu_{ij}\kappa_{j}(\psi\cdot q^{i,j})\;\;\text{and}\;\;(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime\prime})=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\mu_{ij}\psi\cdot q^{i,j}

Since
(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime})|_{L}=(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime\prime})|_{L}
, we conclude that

\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\lambda_{ij}\delta^{\varphi^{i,j}}=\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq m-1}\mu_{ij}\psi^{h}\cdot\delta^{\varphi^{i,j}}

Then
\lambda_{ij}=\mu_{ij}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}
for every
0\leq i,j\leq m-1
according to Lemma
5.7
.
In that case, we have

\displaystyle p^{\prime}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m^{2}}\kappa_{j}(q^{i,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}\kappa_{j}(q^{i,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}\kappa_{j}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}q^{i,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}\kappa_{j}\left(p^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=(\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{m-1}}\rangle)

Similarly,
p^{\prime\prime}=\left(\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{m-1}}\rangle\right)
.
So by Proposition
2.20
the simplicial distribution in (
39
)
is a vertex in
\operatorname{sDist}(\Sigma X,\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
. Moreover, It is obviously contextual as it is not a deterministic distribution.
∎

5.3
Complete collections of average distributions

In this section, we write
L^{(2)}
for the line with two edges
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
(see Definition
3.7
). For simplicity, we assume that
d_{0}(\sigma_{1})=d_{1}(\sigma_{2})
.

Definition 5.12
.

A
complete collection of average distributions
is
a set
\{q^{j}:~0\leq j\leq m-1\}
of simplicial distributions on
(L^{(2)},\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
such that
\{q_{\sigma_{k}}^{j}:~0\leq j\leq m-1\}=\{I,S,\dots,S^{m-1}\}
for every
1\leq k\leq 2
(see Example
2.22
).

Note that
D(d_{0})(S^{j})(a)=D(d_{1})(S^{j})(a)=\frac{1}{m}
for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. So we have
(m!)^{2}
complete collections of average distributions on
(L^{(2)},\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
.

Example 5.13
.

By setting

q^{j}_{\sigma_{k}}=S^{j}

for every
0\leq j\leq m-1
and
1\leq k\leq 2
, we obtain a complete collection of average distributions
\{q^{0},\dots,q^{m-1}\}
.

Lemma 5.14
.

Given a complete collection of average distributions
\{q^{j}:~0\leq j\leq m-1\}
, and let

\psi\in s\mathbf{Set}(L^{(2)},\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
be defined by setting
\psi_{\sigma_{1}}=(0,0)
and
\psi_{\sigma_{2}}=(0,1)
. If for
\lambda_{j},\mu_{j}\in[0,1]
with
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}{\lambda_{j}}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}{\mu_{j}}=1
we have

(40)

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}q^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}\psi\cdot q^{j}

then

\lambda_{j}=\mu_{j}=\frac{1}{m}
for every
0\leq j\leq m-1
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we will give a proof for the case that the complete collection of average distributions is the one given in Example
5.13
.
By substituting
\sigma_{1}
in Equation (
40
) we get that for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}S^{i}(0,i)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}S^{j}(0,i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}q^{j}_{\sigma_{1}}(0,i)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}(\delta^{\psi_{\sigma_{1}}}\ast q^{j}_{\sigma_{1}})(0,i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}(\delta^{(0,0)}\ast S^{j})(0,i)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}S^{j}(0,i)

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}S^{i}(0,i)=\mu_{i}\frac{1}{m}

see Equations (
10
) and (
11
). We conclude that
\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}
for every
0\leq i\leq m-1
. On the other hand, by substituting
\sigma_{2}
in Equation (
40
) we get that for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}S^{i}(0,i)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}S^{j}(0,i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}q^{j}_{\sigma_{2}}(0,i)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}(\delta^{\psi_{\sigma_{2}}}\ast q^{j}_{\sigma_{2}})(0,i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}(\delta^{(0,1)}\ast S^{j})(0,i)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}S^{j+1}(0,i)

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
X
be a finitely generated, connected simplicial set. Given a set of vertices
\{p^{j}:~0\leq j\leq m-1\}
in
\operatorname{sDist}(X,\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
, and a line
L=L^{(2)}\subseteq X
generated by
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
, such that

•

\{p^{j}|_{L}:~0\leq j\leq m-1\}
is a complete collection of average distributions as
in Definition
5.12
.

•

There is
\psi\in s\mathbf{Set}(X,\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
such that

\psi_{\sigma_{1}}=(0,0)
,
\psi_{\sigma_{2}}=(0,1)
and

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}p^{j}=\psi\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}p^{j}
.

Then the simplicial distribution

(41)

\left(\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{m-1}}\rangle;\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{m-1}}\rangle\right)

is a contextual vertex in
\operatorname{sDist}(\Sigma X,\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}_{m}})
.

Proof.

The simplicial distribution in (
41
) is well defined since
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}p^{j}=\psi\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{m}p^{j}
. Now, given simplicial distributions

p^{\prime}\in\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{Vsupp}(\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{m-1}}\rangle)\right)\;\;,\;\;p^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{Vsupp}(\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},\psi\cdot p^{m-1}}\rangle)\right)

such that
(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime})=(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime\prime})
. By Proposition
4.12
we have

\operatorname{Vsupp}\left(\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle{\frac{1}{m},p^{m-1}}\rangle\right)=\{\kappa_{j}(p^{j}):~0\leq j\leq m-1\}

So there is scalars
\lambda_{j}\in[0,1]
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}=1
and

p^{\prime}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}\kappa_{j}(p^{j})
.
Thus we have
(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime})=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}p^{j}
.
Similarly, there is scalars
\mu_{j}\in[0,1]
where

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}=1
, such that

p^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}\kappa_{j}(\psi\cdot p^{j})\;\;\text{and}\;\;(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime\prime})=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}\psi\cdot p^{j}

We conclude that

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{j}p^{j}|_{L}=(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime})|_{L}=(\operatorname{Id}\star\dots\star\operatorname{Id})(p^{\prime\prime})|_{L}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mu_{j}\psi|_{L}\cdot p^{j}|_{L}

by Lemma
5.14
we get that
\lambda_{j}=\mu_{j}=\frac{1}{m}
for every
0\leq j\leq m-1
. This determines

(p^{\prime};p^{\prime\prime})
uniquely. Therefore, by Proposition
2.20
the simplicial distribution in (
41
)
is a contextual vertex.
∎

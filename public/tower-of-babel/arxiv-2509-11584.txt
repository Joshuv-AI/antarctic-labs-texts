Model Predictive Control with High-Probability Safety Guarantee for Nonlinear Stochastic Systems
Zishun Liu, Liqian Ma, Yongxin Chen

Abstract
We present a model predictive control (MPC) framework for nonlinear stochastic systems that ensures safety guarantee with high probability. Unlike most existing stochastic MPC schemes, our method adopts a set-erosion that converts the probabilistic safety constraint into a tractable deterministic safety constraint on a smaller safe set over deterministic dynamics. As a result, our method is compatible with any off-the-shelf deterministic MPC algorithm. The key to the effectiveness of our method is a tight bound on the stochastic fluctuation of a stochastic trajectory around its nominal version. Our method is scalable and can guarantee safety with high probability level (e.g., 99.99%), making it particularly suitable for safety-critical applications involving complex nonlinear dynamics. Rigorous analysis is conducted to establish a theoretical safety guarantee, and numerical experiments are provided to validate the effectiveness of the proposed MPC method.



I
Introduction

Safety is a fundamental requirement in real-world systems. As many real-world systems are not naturally safe, an important task is to synthesize a controller so that the controlled dynamics satisfies the safety requirements
[
1
]
. For deterministic systems under bounded uncertainties, safe controller synthesis seeks to keep the closed-loop system invariant in the safe region under the worst-case scenarios
[
2
]
. This task has been addressed by a variety of methods, including control barrier functions (CBF,
[
3
]
), reachability analysis
[
4
]
, and model predictive control (MPC). Among all these methods, MPC has been flourishing for decades due to its ability to effectively cope with complex system dynamics and constraints
[
5
]
. For deterministic systems, MPC ensures safety by formulating the worst-case safety condition into robustness constraints, and has shown exceptional success in various applications like autonomy
[
6
,
7
]
.

However, many real-world systems are also prone to stochastic disturbances
[
8
]
, which are usually unbounded (e.g., Gaussian noise) or rarely reach the worst case. Consequently, the objective of safe controller synthesis becomes to ensure the entire stochastic trajectory satisfies safety constraints
with high probability
. Significant amounts of effort have been made to adapt the aforementioned deterministic methods to this problem, including stochastic CBF
[
9
]
, stochastic reachable tube
[
10
]
, and stochastic MPC. In this paper, we focus on stochastic MPC
[
11
,
12
]
.

In general, stochastic MPC formulates safety conditions as chance constraints that demand satisfaction with some given probability level.
Methods such as sample-based MPC
[
13
,
14
]
and time-varying linearized MPC
[
15
]
have been applied in practice. Besides, given fruitful results in deterministic MPC, a natural attempt to handle the chance constraints is to convert them into deterministic ones.
When the system is linear with Gaussian or sub-Gaussian noise, the chance constraints can be converted to deterministic constraints on the mean and covariance of the trajectory
[
16
,
17
]
. However, this strategy is restricted to linear systems. For nonlinear dynamics, an effective strategy is called set erosion
[
18
]
, which converts the chance constraints to deterministic safety constraints on the nominal trajectory in an eroded safe region. Existing works such as
[
19
,
20
]
have proposed tractable stochastic MPC schemes based on this strategy, but they have some limitations. First, many of them only provide probabilistic safety insurance at each single time instant, but not
for the entire trajectory
. Second, the erosion depth of the safe regions derived in existing MPC schemes is overly conservative when the safety level is high (e.g., safe with probability
>99.9\%
).

In this paper, we propose an MPC framework for stochastic nonlinear systems with a high-probability safety guarantee. The framework of our proposed MPC is based on the tractable MPC in
[
20
]
, which
leverages the set-erosion strategy that converts the chance constraints to deterministic safety constraints. Our contributions are as follows. First, we significantly improve the feasibility of the proposed MPC scheme when the probability level of safety is high (e.g., 99.99%). This is achieved by adopting a sufficiently tight bound on the erosion depth of the safe set, which relaxes the converted deterministic safe constraints while theoretically guaranteeing the trajectory-level safety. Second, we justify that the bound on the set erosion depth depends on the Lipschitz constant of the open-loop system, and the change of the closed-loop Lipschitz constant due to the implicit feedback of the MPC is irrelevant. Note that in previous work along this line
[
20
]
, whether the suitable set erosion depth depends on the closed-loop Lipschitz constant or the open-loop one is unclear.

II-B

Stochastic MPC with Chance Constraint

In this paper, we focus on handling (
2
) through stochastic MPC, as it systematically seeks control objectives and probabilistic constraint satisfaction

24
]
. At each time
t
, given a prediction window

, a typical stochastic MPC predicts an

z_{t+k|t}

z_{t+k+1|t}=f(z_{t+k|t},u_{t+k|t})

However, it is challenging to construct a proper chance constraint for the predicted trajectory. One such construction in
[
19

is
\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{x}_{k|t}\in\mathcal{C}\right)\geq 1-\delta

\hat{x}_{k+1|t}=f(\hat{x}_{k|t},u_{t+k|t})+w_{t+k|t}

. However, this constraint is imposed on each single time point of the predicted trajectory, rather than the

, so it fails to provide a
1-\delta

III
Stochastic MPC with High-Probability Safety Guarantee

Departing from most existing stochastic MPC methods, we adopt a strategy that first converts the chance constraints into a deterministic safety constraint and reduce the stochastic MPC problem into a deterministic one. In particular, following
[
20
,
18
]
, we leverage a technique known as set-erosion that ensures
1-\delta
probabilistic safety guarantee by ensuring a deterministic safety guarantee on an eroded safe set.

III-A

Tractable Set Erosion-Based MPC

For a stochastic trajectory of (
1
) with initial state
X_{0}
and input
\{u_{t}\}_{t\leq T}
, its associated nominal trajectory
x_{t}
is given as

x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),~x_{0}=X_{0}.

(3)

Intuitively, a stochastic trajectory fluctuates around its associated nominal trajectory with high probability. Therefore, if we erode the safe set
\mathcal{C}
with a suitable depth
r_{\delta,t}
to obtain an eroded subset
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{t}=\mathcal{C}\ominus\mathcal{B}^{n}(r_{\delta,t},0)
and control the nominal trajectory
x_{t}
to stay within
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{t}
, then the associated stochastic trajectory
X_{t}
is expected to stay safe with high probability. This strategy is termed
set-erosion

[
18
]
.

Finding an appropriate erosion depth
r_{\delta,t}
is the key of the set-erosion strategy. A natural choice of
r_{\delta,t}
is the
radius of probabilistic tube (PT)
. Given a finite time horizon
[0,\,T]
and a probability level
\delta\in(0,1)
, a curve
r_{\delta,t}:(0,1)\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is said to be the
radius of PT
, if for any associated trajectories
X_{t}
and
x_{t}
:

\mathbb{P}\left(\|X_{t}-x_{t}\|\leq r_{\delta,t},~\forall t\leq T\right)\geq 1-\delta.

(4)

It should be addressed that
r_{\delta,t}
is a bound applied to the
entire stochastic trajectory
, which means the probability that
none of
the state on the trajectory violates the bound is at least
1-\delta
. It is provable that when the erosion depth is chosen as the radius of PT, the safety of
x_{t}
on
\mathcal{C}\ominus\mathcal{B}^{n}(r_{\delta,t},0)
yields the satisfaction of the chance constraint in Definition
II.2
for the associated
X_{t}

[
25
]
.

The set-erosion strategy has been considered in several existing works
[
20
,
19
,
25
]
, among which our MPC framework is based on the tractable stochastic MPC proposed in
[
20

\displaystyle z_{t|t}=X_{t}

(5b)

\displaystyle z_{t+k+1|t}=f(z_{t+k|t},u_{t+k|t})

(5c)

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{t|t}=x_{t}

(5d)

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{t+k+1|t}=f(\tilde{x}_{t+k|t},u_{t+k|t})

(5e)

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{t+k|t}\in\mathcal{C}\ominus\mathcal{B}^{n}\left(r_{\delta,t+k},0\right)

(5f)

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{t+m|t}\in\mathcal{X}_{f}

\displaystyle u_{t+k|t}\in\mathcal{U},~k=0,\dots,m-1,

III-D

Justification on Lipschitz Constants

One may notice that the adopted bound (
8
) is related to the open-loop Lipschitz constant
L
given in Assumption
1
. In fact, the bounds
r_{\delta,t}
of PT applied in existing set-erosion techniques all depend on the Lipschitz constant. However, since
u_{t}
generated by the presented MPC is a feedback of
X_{t}
, the Lipschitz constant of the closed-loop system
X_{t+1}=f(X_{t},u_{t}(X_{t}))+w_{t}
is no longer equal to
L
and is difficult to capture. Due to this difference, whether to use the open-loop or closed-loop Lipschitz constant in
r_{\delta,t}
remains unclear in existing works. In this section, we justify that the bound (
8
) only uses the Lipschitz constant of
the original open-loop system
, and the change of the closed-loop Lipschitz constant is irrelevant.

Intuitively, this is because when the same
u_{t}
is applied to the associated trajectories
X_{t}
and
x_{t}
, it is the open-loop Lipschitz constant
L
but not
u_{t}
that affects the evolution of the deviation
X_{t}-x_{t}
. To see this, let us start with a simple linear example.
Consider the associated linear trajectories:

\begin{split}&X_{t+1}=AX_{t}+Bu(X_{t})+w_{t}\\
&x_{t+1}=Ax_{t}+Bu(X_{t}),\end{split}

(9)

where
w_{t}
is stochastic and
u(X_{t})
is a nonlinear feedback of
X_{t}
. The closed-loop dynamics
AX_{t}+Bu(X_{t})
is nonlinear. Interestingly, it holds that

X_{t+1}-x_{t+1}=A(X_{t}-x_{t})+w_{t}.

(10)

Let
e_{t}=X_{t}-x_{t}
be the gap between associated trajectories, then
e_{t}
is still driven by the original linear system
e_{t+1}=Ae_{t}+w_{t}
. Therefore, wherever
x_{t}
reaches, the probabilistic tube around
x_{t}
is only determined by
A
and
w_{t}
.

Similarly, when the system is nonlinear, i.e.,
X_{t+1}=f(X_{t},u(X_{t}))+w_{t}
, we still have

X_{t+1}-x_{t+1}=f(X_{t},u(X_{t}))-f(x_{t},u(X_{t}))+w_{t},

(11)

where the difference
f(X_{t},u_{t})-f(x_{t},u_{t})
in the RHS is only related to the
L
w.r.t.
x
of
f(x,u)
, because the same
u(X_{t})
is applied to the two terms.

Theorem 2

Given stochastic system (
1
), safe set
\mathcal{C}
, cost functions
\mathcal{L}_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
, terminal cost function
\Phi(\cdot)
, initial state set
\mathcal{X}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}
, safe probability level
1-\delta
, and terminal state set
\mathcal{X}_{f}
. Suppose that the cost functions are
L_{c}
-Lipschitz with both
x
and
u
, and Assumption
1
-
3
hold. Let
u_{t}
be the control input of the actual trajectory
X_{t}
. Define
J(\bm{X,u})
and
\tilde{J}(\bm{x,u})
as above in Section III-E. Then it holds that:

\begin{split}J(\bm{X,u})-\tilde{J}(\bm{x,u})\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sqrt{\frac{nL_{c}^{2}\sigma^{2}(L^{2t}-1)}{L^{2}-1}}.\end{split}

Proof:

From (
1
) and (
3
) we know:

\begin{split}\|X_{t+1}-x_{t+1}\|^{2}=&\|f(X_{t},u_{t})-f(x_{t},u_{t})\|^{2}+\|w_{t}\|^{2}\\
&+2\langle f(X_{t},u_{t})-f(x_{t},u_{t}),w_{t}\rangle.\end{split}

(12)

Take the expectation of both sides of (
12
). By Assumption
1
we know
\|f(X_{t},u_{t})-f(x_{t},u_{t})\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|X_{t}-x_{t}\|^{2}
. Also,
\mathbb{E}\left(\langle f(X_{t},u_{t})-f(x_{t},u_{t}),w_{t}\rangle\right)=0
since it is assumed to be independent. By,
[
27
]
, a classical result implies that
\mathbb{E}(\|w_{t}\|^{2})\leq n\sigma^{2}
.
Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left(\|X_{t+1}-x_{t+1}\|^{2}\right)\leq L^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\|X_{t}-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)+n\sigma^{2}\end{split}

(13)

which has a solution:

\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x_{t}\|^{2})\leq\frac{n\sigma^{2}(L^{2t}-1)}{L^{2}-1}.

(14)

Since
\mathcal{L}_{t}(x,u)
and
\Phi(x)
are
L_{c}
-Lipschitz w.r.t.
x
, we know
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{L}_{t}(X_{t},u_{t}))-\mathcal{L}_{t}(x_{t},u_{t})\leq L\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x_{t}\|)
,
and the same inequality holds for
\Phi(\cdot)
. By (
14
), we conclude

\begin{split}J(\bm{X,u})-\tilde{J}(\bm{x,u})\leq\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sqrt{\frac{nL_{c}^{2}\sigma^{2}(L^{2t}-1)}{L^{2}-1}}.\end{split}

(15)

This completes the proof.
∎When
L>1
, the term
L^{T}
dominates the gap
J(\bm{X,u})-\tilde{J}(\bm{x,u})
, which grows exponentially with respect to
T
. When
L=1
, this gap reduces to the level of
\mathcal{O}(T^{\frac{3}{2}})
. When
L<1
, the term
\sum_{t=0}^{T}\sqrt{\frac{nL_{c}^{2}\sigma^{2}(L^{2t}-1)}{L^{2}-1}}
converge to some constant, so
J(\bm{X,u})-J^{*}(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})
is on the level of
\mathcal{O}(T)
. If we take into account the average cost gap
\frac{1}{T}\left(J(\bm{X,u})-J^{*}(\bm{x}^{*},\bm{u}^{*})\right)
, then it diverges with
T
when
L\geq 1
and converges to a finite value when
L<1
. For sake of a bounded average cost, we usually need
L<1
in practice.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we present an MPC framework for stochastic nonlinear systems with a high-probability safety guarantee. The framework is based on the set-erosion strategy, which converts the chance constraints on the safe set to the deterministic safety constraints of the nominal trajectory on an eroded subset of the safe set. When utilizing this strategy, we adopt a tight bound on the erosion depth of the safe set. Compared to existing works like
[
20
]
, it provides a much higher probability of safety guarantee and significantly improves the feasibility of the proposed MPC when the level of safety guarantee is high. Moreover, we justify that the bound on the set erosion depth depends on the open-loop Lipschitz constant, rather than the time-varying closed-loop Lipschitz constant. We also quantify the cost of the presented MPC.

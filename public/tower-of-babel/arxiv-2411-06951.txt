Abstract
Some aspects of quantum field theory in a general (i.e. non inertial) frame of Minkowski spacetime are studied. Conditions for the presence of horizons as well as for the modification of the definition of positive energy solutions are examined. The standard wisdom is confirmed that none of these phenomena is generic. This corroborates that in the general case there is no natural way to define positive frequencies, which is the standard road to define particles. In that sense, (as well as in others) non inertial frames are similar to curved spacetimes.

2
Reminder of Killing vectors.

There are particular diffeomorphisms that leave invariant the metric. Those are dubbed
isometries
. The generator of an isometry is called a Killing field. Whenever they exist, they are specific to a given metric, and obey

\pounds(k)g_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_{\alpha}k_{\beta}+\nabla_{\beta}k_{\alpha}=k^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}g_{\alpha\beta}+g_{\lambda\alpha}\partial_{\beta}k^{\lambda}+g_{\lambda\beta}\partial_{\alpha}k^{\lambda}

(38)

it is easy to prove that

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\rho}k_{\nu}=R_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}k^{\lambda}

(39)

Assume all isometries of a given manifold are given by
\vec{k}_{a}\equiv k_{a}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
, the commutator of two such isometries can be proved to be another isometry.

\left[\vec{k}_{a},\vec{k}_{b}\right]=C_{ab}^{c}\vec{k}_{c}

(40)

The set of all isometries constitute a group, which is
simply transitive
if the Killing vectors are linearly independent. Otherwise the group is multiply transitive. The orbits of the group are homogeneus spaces, which have the same dimension as the group in the simply transitive case.
When a given space enjoys a timelike isometry the space is
stationary
. It is useful to consider
coordinates adapted to the isometry
, in which the isometry reads

k^{a}=(1,0,0,0)

(41)

then

k_{0}=g_{00}

(42)

there is no need for
g_{0i}=0
. When this is also the case, we say that the spacetime is
static
. Ay any rate the equation for the isometry in adapted coordinates guarantees that time is a cyclic coordinate

\partial_{0}g_{ab}=0

(43)

All this applies equally to flat space. There are 10 Killing vectors, which can be interpreted as the generators of the Poincaré group, which includes rotations, boosts, and spacetime translations.

In cartesian coordinates where the Christoffel symbols vanish they read

\begin{array}[]{l}{P_{a}=\partial_{a}}\\[4.0pt]
{L_{ab}=x_{a}\partial_{b}-x_{b}\partial_{a}}\end{array}

(44)

P_{0}
is timelike in the whole
M_{4}
, and
L_{0i}
is timelike in the region
x_{i}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}>0
. The rest of the Killings are everywhere spacelike.

The general Killing vector can be written as

\begin{array}[]{l}{\sum_{a}\xi^{a}P_{a}+\sum_{i}\lambda^{i}L_{0i}+\sum_{ij}\mu^{ij}L_{ij}=}\\[4.0pt]
{=\left(\xi^{0}-\sum_{i}\lambda^{i}x_{i}\right)\partial_{0}+\sum_{j}\left(\xi^{j}+x_{0}\lambda^{j}+\sum_{k}(\mu^{kj}-\mu^{jk})x_{k}\right)\partial_{j}}\end{array}

(45)

then the Killing reads

k^{\mu}\equiv\left(\xi^{0}-\sum_{i}\lambda^{i}x_{i},\xi^{j}+x_{0}\lambda^{j}+\sum_{k}(\mu^{kj}-\mu^{jk})x_{k}\right)

(46)

it will be timelike whenever

\left(\xi^{0}-\sum_{i}\lambda^{i}x_{i}\right)^{2}-\left(\xi^{j}+x_{0}\lambda^{j}+\sum_{k}(\mu^{kj}-\mu^{jk})x_{k}\right)\left(\xi_{j}+x_{0}\lambda_{j}+\sum_{k}(\mu^{k}_{\penalty\ j}-\mu_{j}^{\penalty\ k})x_{k}\right)>0

(47)

The fact that Riemann’s tensor vanishes, means that our previous equation (
39
) implies that

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\rho}k_{\nu}=0

(48)

this is not as trivial as it seems, because in non-cartesian coordinates Christoffel’s symbols fail to vanish in general.

Given any frame that can be associated to a timelike observer

x^{a}=x^{a}(\tau),

(49)

it is just natural to assume that the observer’s proper time is the natural measure of time for her. The tangent vector

{\partial\over\partial\tau}=u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}

(50)

is normalized to unity. Nevertheless, in order to be allowed to use the tangent vector to define
positive frequencies
(which is the first step in the definition of
particles
) it is necessary for this vector to be a linear combination of Killing vector fields. This is not always the case. As a matter of fact both
\gamma
and
u^{i}
depend in general on
\tau
. Only in the case where
\dot{v}^{i}=0
we have

{\partial\over\partial\tau}=t{\partial\over\partial t}+x^{i}{\partial\over\partial x^{i}}

(51)

which belongs to the Killing algebra. Otherwise this only happens in a few lucky cases. We shall encounter examples of both lucky and unlucky situations in the sequel.

It is well known that the root of Unruh’s effect is precisely the fact that what amounts to the
natural
definition of positive frequencies (and consequently, upon quantization, particles) in the non-inertial frame is a combination of positive and negative frequencies as defined with respect to the inertial frame. This was first noted in this language by Fulling
[
10
]
, although it was foreshadowed by Schrödinger
[
21
]
as early as 1939.

This means that all Green functions that are defined as vacuum expectation values (vev) with some time-ordering (namely, the advanced, retarded, Feynman and Dyson) do
not
behave as true scalars under change of frame because the
vacuum changes
. Then

G^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\neq G(x)

(52)

all quantities referring to field operators (no vev involved) are still
bona fide
tensors. This is the case for the energy-momentum tensor and the ensuing four-momentum

P^{\mu}(\Sigma)=\int d\Sigma_{\nu}\,T^{\mu\nu}

(53)

the energy as measured in some frame characterized by the tangent vector
u
(assumed orthogonal to the spacelike surface
\Sigma
) is some vev

E(u)\equiv\langle 0\left|u^{\mu}P_{\mu}\right|0\rangle

(54)

even for fixed
u
, this depends on the vacuum state
|0\rangle
. It could be natural to
define
the vacuum state
|0_{u}\rangle
as the one minimizing

, but this condition is not in general sufficient to uniquely determine it.

Adapted coordinates
\xi^{\mu}(x)
to a given observer are by definition, such that in them

u^{\mu}(\xi)\equiv\frac{\partial\xi^{\mu}}{\partial x^{a}}u^{a}\equiv e^{\penalty\ \mu}_{a}(x)u^{a}(x)=\delta^{\mu}_{0}

(55)

this is a set of partial differential equations (PDE) for the mapping

x^{a}\in M_{4}\rightarrow\xi^{\mu}\in M_{4}

(56)

The inverse Jacobian is

e^{a}_{\penalty\ \mu}=\frac{\partial x^{a}}{\partial\xi^{\mu}}

(57)

and the inertial frames are those for which

\eta_{ab}\,e^{a}_{\penalty\ \mu}e^{b}_{\penalty\ \nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}

(58)

those are generated by isometries of Minkowski space. Otherwise the frame is non inertial, and we define

g_{\alpha\beta}=\eta_{ab}e^{a}_{\penalty\ \alpha}e^{b}_{\penalty\ \beta}

(59)

The Killing’s norm is of course conserved

k^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{a}}=k^{a}e_{a}^{\penalty\ \mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\mu}}=k^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\mu}}

(60)

k^{a}k^{b}\eta_{ab}=k^{\mu}k^{\nu}e^{a}_{\penalty\ \mu}e^{b}_{\penalty\ \nu}\eta_{ab}=k^{\mu}k^{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}

(61)

in fact, in adapted coordinates

k_{a}^{2}(x)=e^{a}_{\penalty\ 0}(x)e^{b}_{\penalty\ 0}(x)\eta_{ab}\equiv g_{00}

(62)

horizons are defined by the zeros of the norm of this Killing. Owing to the fact that flat space is static, all horizons are in fact Killing horizons. To be specific,
g_{00}(x)
is a function

F:x\in V_{4}\quad\longrightarrow F(x)\in\mathbb{R}

(63)

the horizons are determined by the

.

The adapted Killing
k_{a}
can of course be written in cartesian coordinates, where it got to be a linear combination of all Killing vectors, that is

k_{a}=\sum_{b}\xi^{b}P_{b}+\sum_{i}\lambda^{i}L_{0i}+\sum_{ij}\mu^{ij}L_{ij}

Some simple examples with constant and non-constant acceleration.

Let us first clarify what we understand by constant acceleration. The covariant definition
[
11
]

reads

\dot{a}^{\mu}_{\perp}\equiv\ddot{u}^{\mu}_{\perp}=\left(\delta^{\mu}_{\lambda}-u^{\mu}u_{\lambda}\right)\ddot{u}^{\lambda}=0

(65)

this means that either

\dot{a}^{\mu}\equiv\ddot{u}^{\mu}=0;

(66)

\ddot{u}=a^{2}u

(67)

3.1
Rindler frame with constant acceleration.

The world line of the fidutial observer reads

x^{a}=\frac{1}{a}\Big(\sinh a\tau,\cosh a\tau,0,0\Big)

(68)

The orthonormal frame is given by

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 0}=\big(\cosh a\tau,\sinh a\tau,0,0\big)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 1}=\big(\sinh a\tau,\cosh a\tau,0,0\big)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 2}=\Big(0,0,1,0\Big)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 3}=\Big(0,0,0,1\Big)

(69)

then
\dot{u}^{2}=-a^{2}
, the modulus squared of the acceleration. Besides

\ddot{u}^{\mu}_{\perp}=0

(70)

because
\ddot{u}^{\lambda}=a^{2}u^{\lambda}
.

The curvatures of the world line are given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}=a

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}=0

\displaystyle\kappa_{3}=0

(71)

therefore is Class II in Synge’s classification.

Adapted coordinates are given by

\displaystyle\tanh\,a\xi^{0}={t\over x}\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad t={1\over a}e^{a\xi^{1}}\sinh\,a\xi^{0}

\displaystyle e^{2a\xi^{1}}=a^{2}(x^{2}-\tau^{2})\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad x={1\over a}e^{a\xi^{1}}\cosh\,a\xi^{0}

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=y\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad y=\xi^{2}

\displaystyle\xi^{3}=z\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad z=\xi^{3}

(72)

whose domain is Rindler’s wedge

(73)

and the range is the full

. Then, the flat metric in adapted coordinates reads

\text{d}s^{2}=\rho^{2}\text{d}\omega^{2}-\text{d}\rho^{2}-\text{d}\xi_{2}^{2}-\text{d}\xi_{3}^{2}

(74)

where

\displaystyle\xi^{0}=\frac{1}{a}\omega\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad t=\rho\sinh\,\omega

\displaystyle e^{a\xi^{1}}=a\rho\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad x=\rho\cosh\,\omega

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=y\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad y=\xi^{2}

\displaystyle\xi^{3}=z\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad z=\xi^{3}

(75)

it does not seem possible however for adapted coordinates to range over the whole
M_{4}
with a single local frame.

The most important thing to notice is that the tangent vector to the trajectory is proportional to a Killing vector, namely
L_{{01}}

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=u^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{a}}=a\left(t\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+x\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)=aL_{01}

(76)

and this is the natural Killing to choose in order to define positive frequencies. As we have already mentioned, and we shall see in detail in a moment, this is exceptional in trajectories with nontrivial acceleration.

Drifted Rindler motion.

The world line corresponding to drifted Rindler motion is given by

x^{a}(\tau)=\left(R\sinh\frac{\gamma\tau}{R},R\cosh\frac{\gamma\tau}{R},\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}\tau,0\right)

(82)

it is easy to check that the acceleration is not constant.

The orthonormal frame attached to the world line reads

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 0}=\left(\gamma\cosh\frac{\gamma\tau}{R},\gamma\sinh\frac{\gamma\tau}{R},\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1},0\right)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 1}=\left(\sinh\frac{\gamma\tau}{R},\cosh\frac{\gamma\tau}{R},0,0\right)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\left((1-\gamma^{2})\sinh\frac{\gamma\tau}{R},(1-\gamma^{2})\cosh\frac{\gamma\tau}{R},-\gamma\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1},0\right)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 3}=\left(0,0,0,1\right)

(83)

The corresponding curvatures read

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{R}

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}=\frac{\gamma\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}{R}

\displaystyle\kappa_{3}=0

(84)

it is therefore a Synge Class V helix.

The vector tangent to the trajectory is given by

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=\frac{\gamma}{R}\left(x\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+t\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)+\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

(85)

and it is indeed a linear combination of flat space Killing vectors.

There is a horizon located at

\gamma^{2}(x^{2}-t^{2})=R^{2}(\gamma^{2}-1)

(86)

3.4
Cusped (parator) motion.

The world line reads

x^{a}=\left(\tau+\frac{1}{6}a^{2}\tau^{3},\frac{1}{2}a\tau^{2},\frac{1}{6}a^{2}\tau^{3},0\right)

(87)

also here the acceleration fails to be constant.
The attached orthonormal frame is

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 0}=\left(1+\frac{1}{2}a^{2}\tau^{2},a\tau,\frac{1}{2}a^{2}\tau^{2},0\right)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 0}=\left(a\tau,1,a\tau,0\right)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 0}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}\tau^{2},-a\tau,1-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}\tau^{2},0\right)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 0}=\left(0,0,0,1\right)

(88)

Curvatures are given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}=a

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}=a

\displaystyle\kappa_{3}=0

(89)

we are therefore dealing with a Synge’s Class IV helix.

The vector tangent to the trajectory is

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=(1+ay)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)+a(t-z)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=(1+ay)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)+\sqrt{2ay}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}

(90)

it fails to be a linear combination of Killing vectors.

3.5
Harmonic trajectory.

Consider next a
harmonic frame
defined along the following simple one-dimensional harmonic trajectory

\displaystyle x={\cos\,\omega t\over\omega}

\displaystyle y=0

\displaystyle z=0

(91)

with constant frequency
\omega
. The corresponding world line is given by

x^{a}(\tau)={1\over\omega}\left(\arcsin\,\omega\tau,\sqrt{1-\omega^{2}\tau^{2}}-1,0,0\right)

(92)

where

\sin\omega t=\omega\tau

(93)

the orthonormal frame is

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 0}=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\omega^{2}\tau^{2}}}\big(1,-\omega\tau,0,0\big)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 1}=\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\omega^{2}\tau^{2}}}\big(\omega\tau,-1,0,0\big)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 2}=\Big(0,0,1,0\Big)

\displaystyle e^{a}_{\penalty\ 3}=\Big(0,0,0,1\Big)

(94)

The curvatures are given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}={\omega\over\sqrt{1-\omega^{2}\tau^{2}}}

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}=0

\displaystyle\kappa_{3}=0

(95)

it then belongs to Synge’s Class II.

This frame represent a particle with non-constant acceleration

a={\omega\over\sqrt{1-\omega^{2}\tau^{2}}}

(96)

in fact,
\ddot{u}^{\lambda}\neq a^{2}u^{\lambda}
, and the modulus of the acceleration diverges at the boundary where

\omega t=\pm{\pi\over 2}

(97)

The adapted frame is defined by

\displaystyle\xi^{0}=x+\cfrac{1}{\omega}\big(\sin\omega t-\cos\omega t\big)\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad t=\cfrac{1}{\omega}\arcsin[\omega(\xi^{0}-\xi^{1})]

\displaystyle\xi^{1}=x-\cfrac{1}{\omega}\cos\omega t\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad x=\xi^{1}+\cfrac{1}{\omega}\sqrt{1-\omega^{2}(\xi^{0}-\xi^{1})^{2}}

\displaystyle\xi^{2}=y\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad y=\xi^{2}

\displaystyle\xi^{3}=z\quad

\displaystyle\vline\quad z=\xi^{3}

(98)

the adapted system is defined only for

(t^{\prime}-x^{\prime})^{2}\leq{1\over\omega^{2}}

(99)

which is a two-sheeted hyperbola with vertexes
\pm{1\over\omega}
in the I-III regions of Rindler’s plane.

The metric in the adapted frame reads

\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}t^{2}-2\left(1-\tan\,\omega t\right)\text{d}t\text{d}x-2\tan\,\omega t\,\text{d}x^{2}-\text{d}y^{2}-\text{d}z^{2}

(100)

the fact that in this coordinates
g_{00}=1
implies that there are no Killing horizons in this case.
The vector tangent to the trajectory is

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=\frac{1}{\omega x}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\sqrt{1-\omega^{2}x^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)

(101)

which is
not
proportional to a Killing vector. This means that there is no natural definition of positive frequencies other than the standard one using
{\partial\over\partial t}
.

A rotating frame.

4
Wiener-Khinchin theorem and Wightmann functions.

On general grounds it can be argued that the
response function

[
22
]
of a simplified particle detector (cf.
[
4
]
[
22
]
for a discussion) is proportional to

\displaystyle{\cal T}(E)

\displaystyle=\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{-iE(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\langle 0|\phi(x(\tau))\phi(x(\tau^{\prime}))|0\rangle=

\displaystyle\equiv\int d\tau d\tau^{\prime}e^{-iE(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}G^{+}\left(\phi(x(\tau))\phi(x(\tau^{\prime}))\right)

(127)

In fact the Wiener-Khinchin theorem
2
2

2

Known to Einstein much earlier
[
9
]
.
asserts that the energy absorption rate (the
power spectrum
) is determined by the Fourier transform of the field autocorrelation function (which is assumed to be Wightman ’s function in our case).
For a free massless scalar the positive frequency Wightman function is given by

G^{+}\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)=-{1\over 4\pi^{2}}{1\over(t-t^{\prime}-i\epsilon)^{2}-\left(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime}\right)^{2}}

(128)

Furthermore, for stationary motions (helixes) Synge’s world function (one half the square of the geodesic distance) depends only on the difference of proper times,
\Omega(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\Omega(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
.

Then it can be written

\displaystyle{\cal T}(E)=\int d\tau e^{-i\,E\tau}\,G^{+}\left(\tau\right)

(129)

It is also a fact that for stationary motions at small proper time

x^{a}=x^{a}(0)+u^{a}\tau+O(\tau^{2})

(130)

so that, redefining the trajectory in such a way that
x^{a}(0)=\left(0,0,0,0\right)

2\Omega(\tau)=\tau^{2}+O(\tau^{3})

(131)

In the reference
[
19
]
an astute way to treat the response function was devised. Start with the Laurent expansion

{1\over 2\Omega(\tau)}={1\over(\tau-i\epsilon)^{2}}+f(\tau)

(132)

where
f(\tau)
is analytic in
\tau
. Small modifications are in order when one of the components of the world line is such that
{x(\tau)\over\tau}
vanishes when
\tau\sim 0
.
This means that we can write

{1\over 2\Omega(\tau-i\epsilon)}={1\over 2\Omega(\tau-i\epsilon)}-{1\over(\tau-i\epsilon)^{2}}+{1\over(\tau-i\epsilon)^{2}}

(133)

The sum of the two first terms is now
f(\tau)
which is analytic, so that the limit when
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
can be taken in the integrand. The third term can be integrated directly.

{\cal T}(E)=-{1\over 4\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\,e^{-iE\tau}\left({1\over 2\Omega(\tau)}-{1\over\tau^{2}}\right)-{E\over 2\pi}\theta(-E)

(134)

since

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-i\tau E}{1\over(\tau-i\epsilon)^{2}}=2\pi E\theta(-E)

(135)

and where the integrand is free of singularities, and vanishes both when
\tau\rightarrow 0
as well as when
\tau\rightarrow\infty
. The response function is the Fourier transform of a function which is absolutely integrable, so that by the Riemann-Lebesgue theorem
[
16
]
its transform goes to zero as
E\rightarrow\infty
.

1.

First of all consider an
inertial motion

\vec{x}=\vec{x_{0}}+\vec{v}t=\vec{x_{0}}+\vec{v}\gamma\tau

(136)

then

G^{+}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})=-{1\over 4\pi^{2}}{1\over(\tau-\tau^{\prime}-i\epsilon)^{2}}

(137)

our previous equation [
134
] yields immediatly no contribution. This can also be seen because the integral over proper time has to be performed for negative imaginary proper times

Im\,\left(\tau-\tau^{\prime}\right)<0

(138)

whereas the poles are located at

\left(\tau-\tau^{\prime}\right)=i\epsilon.

(139)

2.

Let us now turn our attention to a
hyperbolic trajectory
which physically corresponds to constant acceleration

x^{a}=\frac{1}{a}\Big(\sinh a\tau,\cosh a\tau,0,0\Big)

(140)

the Wightman function

(t-t^{\prime}-i\epsilon)^{2}-(\vec{x}-\vec{x}^{\prime})^{2}=-\frac{2}{a^{2}}\left(1-\cosh[a(\tau-\tau^{\prime}-i\epsilon)]\right)

(141)

We can write

G^{+}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})=-\frac{a^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}\left[\frac{a(\tau-\tau^{\prime}-i\epsilon)}{2}\right]}

(142)

and using the expansion

and the fact that
\sinh(x)=-i\sin(ix)

This means that the response function has got a thermal spectrum with temperature

it is important, however, to stress, as has been done in the standard book

and the response function is given by

\displaystyle{\cal T}(E)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-iE\tau}\left[\frac{1}{\frac{4(1+q^{2})}{\chi^{2}}\sinh^{2}\left[\frac{\chi\tau}{2}\right]-\frac{4q^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\sin^{2}\left[\frac{\omega\tau}{2}\right]}-\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\right]-\frac{E}{2\pi}\theta(-E)

(164)

4.7
Summary

To summarize, all the stationary motion, including the Rindler motion which acts as the template, have got a world function whose expansion around
\tau=0
reads

\frac{1}{2\Omega(\tau)}=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\tau)

(165)

On the other hand the response function can be expanded at high or low energies using the expansion of the integrand at low or high proper time, respectively

{\cal T}(E)=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-iE\tau}{1\over 2\Omega(\tau)}=-{1\over 4\pi^{2}E}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dte^{-it}{1\over 2\Omega({t\over E})}

(166)

with the trick of substracting and adding the most divergent piece,

\displaystyle{\cal T}(E)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-iE\tau}\left[\frac{1}{2\Omega(\tau)}-\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\right]-\frac{E}{2\pi}\theta(-E)

(167)

at high energies (which correspond to
\tau=0
in the integrand)

{\cal T}(E)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\kappa_{1}^{2}}{12}\delta(E)-\frac{E}{2\pi}\theta(-E)

(168)

where
\kappa_{1}
is the first curvature.

On the other hand, at low energies (
\tau\rightarrow\pm\infty
in the integrand) the response function reduces to

{\cal T}(E)=-\frac{E}{2\pi}\theta(-E)

(169)

5
Conclusions.

In this paper we have examined some of the distinct phenomena that occur if QFT in generic (non inertial) frames. They are as follows.

First of all, it usually happens that adapted coordinates to the world line of a generic observer do not cover the whole Minkowski space, and a horizon of some sort appears in the boundary. Given the fact that the adapted metric is still flat, which means that it is also static, this is necessarily a Killing horizon.

The only Killing that is timelike in the whole Minkowski space is
{\partial\over\partial t}
. Boosts
L_{0i}
are timelike in the region
x_{i}^{2}-t^{2}>0
whose boundary is a Killing horizon for them. Appearance of a horizon is then related to the advancement of some particular boost as defining the
natural
time direction. It may happen that the would be horizon is outside the domain in which the motion is well defined. This happens for our example of the rotating motion.

Independently of that, it may or may not happen that the
natural
positive frequency basis in the generic frame is a combination of positive and negative solutions in cartesian Minkowski coordinates. When this is so, the two basis are related by a Bogoliubov trasformation, as was first pointed out by Fulling
[
10
]
.

We have studied in detail examples of several types of behavior. Except for the template (Rindler motion) all our examples have got non constant acceleration, in spite of the fact that all are stationary motions (constant curvatures).

The fact that in many cases (in the examples we have studied this happens for both the cusped and the harmonic motions) the tangent vector is
not
a linear combination of Killing vectors means that it is in general not possible the use of
{\partial\over\partial\tau}
to define positive frequencies.

The response function is not similar to Planck’s for any of our examples (except for the template) and may be the cusped motion at high temperature. Nevertheless, it has been proposed
[
12
]
that sometimes it is possible to define an
effective temperature
that captures some essential aspects of the physical situation.

In fact the more general treatment of QFT in nontrivial curved spacetimes
[
27
]
backs up all the points made in this paper, there is no preferred vacuum state and no natural definition of positive and negative frequencies, so that the concept of particle is not a natural one. What we have corroborated here is that
even in flat space
all these restrictions hold. It then seems that the concept of
particle
is not only a Minkowskian one, but also it is restricted to inertial or otherwise quite special observers.

An interesting fact that has been recently reported is that there appear some novel physical effects (namely, a softening) of the QCD phase transition in accelerated systems
[
5
]
. This lies however outside the scope of our present work.
Let us also comment that although it has been claimed
[
27
]
that the algebraic (Haag’s
[
14
]
) approach to QFT is more appropiate to deal with certain issues derived from diffeomorphism invariance, we have decided to follow in this paper a very conventional Fock space low brow approach to QFT for the sake of simplicity.

Experimental verification of these ideas is important, as for any scientific prediction. This is not easy because even in the simplest Unruh’s phenomenon, the linear
acceleration needed to reach a temperature of one degree Kelvin is of order
2.47\times 10^{20}m/s^{2}

[
7
]
. Some proposals using charges in circular accelerators have been put forward by Bell and Leinaas (quantum fluctuation of electron orbits in ideal storage rings)
[
2
]
and by Cozella et al
[
6
]
using classical electrodynamics. In spite of all this impressive work, our
feeling is that there is still no clean and convincing experimental check even in this case.

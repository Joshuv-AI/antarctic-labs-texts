Contemporary outcomes of catheter ablation of the structural ventricular tachycardias in severe ischemic and non‐ischemic cardiomyopathies in Turkish population
Authors: Emir Baskovski, Timucin Altin, Omer Akyurek, Mahmut Ekrem Cunetoglu, Volkan Kozluca, Irem Muge Akbulut, Nail Burak Ozbeyaz, Nazli Turan Serifler, Eralp Tutar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCurrently, there are no data regarding outcomes of the catheter ablation for structural ventricular tachycardia (VT) in Türkiye. Patients with a confirmed ischemic or non‐ischemic cardiomyopathy, undergoing structural VT ablation were included. Procedural and mid‐term outcomes were analyzed.ResultsA total of 124 patients were enrolled in the study. 54(43.5%) patients presented with an electrical storm. INTRODUCTION

Catheter ablation is one of the fundamental treatment modalities for structural ventricular tachycardia (VT)s. Recent studies have provided evidence that catheter ablation improves hard end‐points such as the combined end‐point of VT recurrence, cardiovascular hospitalization or death and the combined end‐point of death from any cause or hospitalization for worsening heart failure. 1 , 2 These randomized trials, which were performed in tertiary centers underscore the importance of modern mapping and ablation techniques (including epicardial access when necessary) as well as operator experience driving the highly successful ablation procedures with low recurrence rate. Currently, there are no data regarding outcomes of the catheter ablation for structural VTs in Türkiye, therefore local reproducibility of the randomized trial data is unknown. In this observational study, we aim to investigate cardiac outcomes of patients undergoing catheter VT ablation at a tertiary center in Türkiye.

METHODS

This retrospective observational study conducted at a single tertiary center in Türkiye. Conduction of the study was approved and supervised by the local ethical committee. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study population

Patients undergoing catheter ablation for structural VT between 1st February 2022 and 1st February 2024 by one of the authors (EB, TA, OA) were screened to be included to the study. Only patients with a confirmed ischemic or non‐ischemic cardiomyopathy were selected. Patients undergoing catheter ablation for idiopathic ventricular arrhythmias (including sustained arrhythmias), patients with a contraindication for catheter ablation (i.e., left ventricular (LV) thrombus, terminal heart failure), patients who were lost to follow‐up, and those with a follow‐up of less than 90 days were excluded from the study. Written consent for participation in this study was provided by all participants.

Anti‐arrhythmic treatment

In stable patients undergoing VT ablation anti‐arrhythmic drug (AAD)s, excluding the beta‐blockers, were usually discontinued for at least five half‐lifes (except for amiodarone) prior to the procedure. In patients undergoing catheter ablation for an electrical storm, AADs were stopped usually before the procedure, albeit the interval was usually shorter than five half‐lives of agents. AADs were not prescribed after the procedure (except the beta blockers) and were reserved for patients with a recurrence.

VT ablation procedure

The patient underwent catheter ablation either with sedation or general anesthesia, based on shared decision with the patient, anesthesia team and heart failure specialist. The procedures were performed without percutaneous assist devices, and intra‐aortic balloon counter pulsation was used based on the operator preference. The procedures were performed utilizing a 3D mapping system (Carto 3 (Biosense‐Webster, California, USA), or Rhythmia(Boston Scientific, Boston Scientific Way, MA, USA)) with multipolar mapping catheters (Pentaray (Biosense‐Webster, California, USA) or Orion(Boston Scientific, MA, USA)) and ablation catheters which were either contact‐force sensing (Smarttouch SF (Biosense‐Webster, California, USA)) or non‐concact force sensing (Intellanav(Boston Scientific, MA, USA)) catheters. To briefly summarize the procedure; upon obtaining femoral venous and arterial access left ventricle (LV) was usually mapped in anterograde fashion utilizing a steerable long sheath, except in patients with a mechanical mitral valve. Retrograde LV mapping and ablation were performed where it was deemed necessary. Right ventricle (RV) and epicardial access and mapping were performed as per clinical indication either at the beginning of the procedure or as a bail‐out strategy. LV was mapped either in the sinus rhythm (or RV paced rhythm in case of atrioventricular block) unless these rhythms could not be achieved (due to incessant VT or frequent VT induction). In cases where sinus/paced rhythm map could initially be obtained voltage mapping, isochronal late activation mapping (ILAM), and decrement evoked potential (DeEP) mapping were performed, with a similar methodology to the previously reported studies. 3 , 4 , 5 ILAM mapping was performed either with manual or automatic annotation of the last deflection. Isochronal crowding areas were considered to be ablation targets. Additionally, in all cases DeEP mapping was performed and all areas displaying a decrement of >10 ms were manually tagged for ablation. Complete homogenization of low voltage area was not considered to be a technical endpoint of the procedure. Thereafter, VT induction and mapping were usually performed, except in cases where clinical status of the patient precluded any VT induction. Radiofrequency ablation, with power set to 35–50Watts endocardially, or 25–35Watts epicardially, was performed on the sites targeted by sinus/paced rhythm/VT maps. In cases where VT mapping was performed initially, critical components of the circuit would be targeted for ablation. In these cases, substrate mapping on sinus/paced rhythm was obtained after VTs were terminated and therefore further sites based on the substrate maps could be targeted. Following ablation of the targeted sites VT induction was repeated, except in patients where tachycardia re‐induction was considered to be clinically unsafe. If a sustained VT was achieved further mapping and ablation was performed. The procedures were terminated when either non‐inducibility was achieved, or when further re‐induction was considered to be unsafe. The acute success was defined as non‐inducibility of any VT (clinical and non‐clinical) by either up to triple extra‐stimuli or burst pacing until ventricular effective refractory period, or 220 ms (whichever occurred first). Additionaly, in cases where initial VT(s) were induced with triple or more extrastimuli, one additional extrastimulus (i.e., quadriple extrastimuli in VT(s) previously induced on triple extrastimuli, fifth extrastimulus in VTs previously induced with quadriples) was mandated for the procedure to be classified as an ‘acute‐success’.

Following the procedure, patients were followed in the intensive care unit it was deemed appropriate for clinical follow‐up. Patients who were considered clinically stable were discharged in the follow‐up periods.

Patient follow‐up and data collection

Patients were followed were followed clinically at the end of the first month, then at 3 months intervals. In patients with cardiac implantable electronic devices, interrogation for arrhythmic episodes were performed. Furthermore, the patients were instructed to present immediately in case of device shocks, syncope, or any symptoms relevant to a ventricular arrhythmia.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis in this study was performed with SPSS Statistics version 23 (IBM, New York, NY, USA). Data are expressed as numbers and percentages for discrete variables and as means± SD for continuous variables. Normality of the variables was tested with Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Parametric variables were compared by Student's t‐test, while non‐parametric variables were compared by Mann Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared with Chi‐square test. VT recurrence and death survival analysis was performed with Kaplan‐Maier survival analysis. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed to determine factors predicting VT recurrence and death. All probabilities are two‐tailed and p <.05 was considered significant.

RESULTS

Among screened 129 patients, a total of 124 patients were enrolled in the study (5 patients were lost to follow‐up). Male patients comprised 107 (86.3%) of the study population. (Table 1) Overall, 80 (64.5%) patients had a diagnosis of ischemic cardiomyopathy. Among patients presenting with a non‐ischemic cardiomyopathy, five patients had a definite diagnosis of arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy. The mean LV EF was 29.4% ± 13.8, non‐ischemic patients having a significantly higher EF (p =.001). 54 (43.5%) patients presented with an electrical storm. The procedural data is presented at Table 2. The mean procedural duration, which was similar between ischemic and non‐ischemic patients, was 159.1 ± 63 min. The mean radiofrequency ablation time was 38.4 ± 24.4 min and was significantly lower in patients undergoing ablation for non‐ischemic VT (p <.001). During the mean follow‐up of 351 ± 232 days 10 (8.1%) patients experienced a recurrence. (Table 3) The rate of recurrence did not vary between the ischemic and non‐ischemic VTs. 14 (11.3%) patients died during the follow‐up, most commonly due to a cardiovascular death (pump failure, pulmonary oedema). There were no deaths due to VT storm. One patient underwent cardiac transplantation in the non‐ischemic group, and one patient underwent LV assist device implantation in the ischemic group. Survival plots for death is presented in Figure 1, where significant difference was observed between the ischemic and non‐ischemic cardiomyopathy patients (p =.024). On the other hand, survival plots of VT recurrence were similar for both groups (p =.657) (Figure 1). On the univariate analysis age >70 years (p =.004, HR:8.224) and ischemic etiology (p =.026, HR: 49.937) were predictive of death. Importantly, VT recurrence was not found to be predictive of death among this patient population (p =.396). In the multivariate analysis only age >70 was found to be correlated with death during follow‐up, with p = 0.008, HR = 4.923. (Table 4) The model was not predictive of VT recurrence.

DISCUSSION

Up to our knowledge, this is the first study evaluating acute and mid‐term outcomes of patients undergoing structural VT ablation in Türkiye. In this study we have shown that while acute success of VT ablation is very good with a relatively low rate of serious complications, however, death remains a challenging outcome in the mid‐term follow‐up.

Similar to international studies the patient population in this study most commonly comprised of elderly males. 1 , 2 , 6 Most of the patients had a severely depressed LV EF with the mean EF being higher in non‐ischemic cardiomyopathy patients. These findings are similar to other VT studies. 1 , 6 , 7 Presentation as an electrical storm was common in our cohort accounting for 43% of all patients and reflecting both the severity of cardiac condition in patients who were enrolled. These data are similar to the International VT Ablation Center Collaborative Group study, reflecting similar indications for ablation. Technical factors supporting contemporary nature of the study are usage of multipolar mapping catheters in all patients, frequent (21% in total and 36% in non‐ischemic patients) epicardial access and utilization mapping techniques for substrate ablation. Similar to larger international cohorts' utilization of hemodynamic support during the procedure was uncommon. 6 , 8

This study shows that acute outcomes in a tertiary center in Türkiye is similar to international outcomes with non‐inducibility achieved in 97.6% of patients. Recent studies have also shown that non‐inducibility can be achieved in >90% of the patients. 2 This is in contrast to earlier studies, where non‐inducibility was significantly lower (<90%). 6 Improved catheter technology, operator experience may have contributed to this temporal change. Additionally, high rate of general anesthesia, which is known to decrease VT inducibility, as well as absence of routine isoproterenol infusion during re‐induction may have contributed to the high acute success rate observed in our study. Following the global trend where recent advances in catheter technology, mapping techniques and experience has led to a steady decline in complications rate, major periprocedural complications are low in this cohort with death occurring in <1% of patients. 9 , 10 Both acute procedural success and life‐threatening complications rate were similar among ischemic and non‐ischemic patients. This contrasts with some of the earlier studies which have shown worse acute success in non‐ischemic VT patients. 11 This observation may be due to non‐homogenous nature of non‐ischemic cardiomyopathy, as well as other factors, such as frequent utilization of pre‐procedural cardiac magnetic resonance imaging or frequent epicardial access.

The mid‐term recurrence rate in this study was 8.1% with no difference among groups in Kaplan Maier plots. This is in contrast to previous observations which have shown that unselected non‐ischemic cardiomyopathy patients had worse long‐term recurrence rates. 11 , 12 Here again non‐homogenous nature of the disease, non‐invasive substrate characterization and frequent epicardial access may have led to similar results between the groups. The important observation of this study was that non‐ischemic patients had significantly better survival. This also is in contrast to a large meta‐analysis which found no difference in mortality between ischemic and non‐ischemic patients. 12 Due to limited number of death events it is likely that a selection bias could have played a role in these findings. Data observed in this study highlight the severity of disease which is reflected by the mid‐term mortality rate. The association between ventricular arrhythmias and mortality has been known for a long time. 13 Furthermore, electrical storm is associated with even higher mortality during follow‐up. 14 Therefore, mid‐term mortality outcomes, in this study population with high incidence of presentation by an electrical storm, are consistent with previous international findings. It remains to be seen whether, less sick patients would have better long‐term outcomes, as suggested by recent trials. 1 , 2 Finally, observation that VT recurrence is not a predictor of death among these patients, suggests that management of non‐arrhythmic death remains crucial for patients who have undergone structural VT ablation.

Limitations

There are certain limitations of current study that merit discussion. First, this is a single‐center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size, yet being a referral center, the study population consists of patients from different regions in Türkiye. Secondly, non‐ischemic VT population is non‐homogenous and of relatively small sample therefore many factors could have influenced the outcomes in this group. Finaly, we do not have data on heart‐failure hospitalization, which would also be of clinical interest.

Conclusion

A good acute success with low complications was observed in patients undergoing VT ablation for structural heart disease in a tertiary center in Türkiye. Mid‐term outcomes are comparable with international studies with no difference in VT recurrence in ischemic and non‐ischemic patients. Similar to international studies, a significant mid‐term mortality was observed, emphasizing the severity of the underlying substrate.

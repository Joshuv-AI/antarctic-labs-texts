Scratch beneath the surface
Authors: Husam Khraistin, Mohammad Jourieh
Published: 2026

A 46-year-old male presented with complaints of reduced vision in his right eye. His vision reduced gradually a few months earlier after a small device exploded in front of his eye. Best-corrected visual acuity was 20/40 and 20/20 in the right and left eyes, respectively. Slit-limp examination of the right eye revealed peripheral corneal thinning from four to seven o’clock, located 2 mm from the inferior limbus. All other ocular structures were normal. Intraocular pressure was 17 mmHg in both eyes. Pentacam curvature map is shown in Figure 1. The left eye was within the normal limits. No history of previous ocular surgeries was noted. For profound examination, the patient was referred to perform in vivo confocal microscopy (IVCM) [Figures 2 and 3].

Pentacam curvature map of the anterior surface shows “crab claw” pattern

In vivo confocal microscopy scan of the mid stroma reveals a significant decrease in the density of keratocytes with a lacunar oedema and trabecular patterned hyperdense tissues

In vivo confocal microscopy scan of the endothelium shows abnormal, rounded, and large cells with dark cell bodies and hyper-reflective nuclei

Answers

“Crab claw” pattern is a typical appearance for pellucid marginal degeneration[1]These features are usually seen after corneal crosslinking due to the photochemical effect of riboflavin/ultraviolet light. In this case, it is probable that the explosion has led to thermal and photochemical damage of the cornea and caused these findings[2]The “light dark reversal” appearance seen on IVCM is a characteristic feature of Chandler’s syndrome (one of three clinical variants of iridocorneal endothelial syndrome).[3]Note: these findings indicate the presence of two pathologies in one patient (pellucid marginal degeneration and Chandler’s syndrome).

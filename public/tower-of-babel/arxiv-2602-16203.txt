A Note on Ordinal Submodularity
Satoru Fujishige, Ryuhei Mizutani

Abstract
Notions of ordinal submodularity/supermodularity have been introduced and studied in the literature. We consider several classes of ordinally submodular functions defined on finite Boolean lattices and give characterizations of the set of minimizers of ordinally submodular functions.



Keywords
: Ordinal submodularity, ordinal submodular minimization,
quasisubmodular functions

Introduction

\mathbb{Z}^{E}
with lattice
operations join
\vee
and meet
\wedge
defined by

x\vee y=(\max\{x(e),y(e)\}\mid e\in E)
and

x\wedge y=(\min\{x(e),y(e)\}\mid e\in E)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{E}

and even for more general lattices.

In Section

(Q1)
-,
(Q2)
-,
(Q3)
-, and

We show characterizations (Lemma
3.1

3.2
)

(Q1)
-submodular functions

.

2
Ordinal Notions of Submodularity

Let
E
be a nonempty finite set and consider a set function

f:2^{E}\to(\mathbb{P},\leq)
, where
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
is a totally
ordered set (
\mathbb{P}
being not necessarily the set of reals or rationals).
Note that by considering the dual ordered set
(\mathbb{P},\leq^{*})
of

(\mathbb{P},\leq)
(i.e.,

\forall a,b\in\mathbb{P}:a\leq^{*}b\Leftrightarrow b\leq a
) ordinal notions of
submodularity associated with
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
are those of supermodularity
associated with
(\mathbb{P},\leq^{*})
.

For any
X,Y\in 2^{E}
we have several ordinal conditions in the literature
as follows.

(Q1)

f(X)\leq f(X\cap Y)\Longrightarrow f(X\cup Y)\leq f(Y)
.

(Q2)

f(X)<f(X\cap Y)\Longrightarrow f(X\cup Y)<f(Y)
.

(Q3)

f(X)<f(X\cap Y)\Longrightarrow f(X\cup Y)\leq f(Y)
.

(Q4)

\max\{f(X),f(Y)\}\geq\min\{f(X\cup Y),f(X\cap Y)\}
.

Various notions of ordinal submodularity are defined and termed as follows.

1.

When Conditions
(Q1)
and
(Q2)
hold for all
X,Y\in 2^{E}
,

f
is called
quasisubmodular
in
[
5
]
.
(Such an
f
is also called
semistrictly quasisubmodular

in
[
7
]
.)

2.

When Condition
(Q2)
holds for all
X,Y\in 2^{E}
,

f
is called
weakly quasisubmodular
in
[
1
]
.

3.

When Condition
(Q3)
holds for all
X,Y\in 2^{E}
,

f
is called
quasisubmodular
in
[
7
]

(but we do not adopt this terminology in the present note.)

4.

Condition
(Q4)
appears as (QSB_w) in
[
7
]
.

To avoid any confusion, for each
i=1,2,3,4
we call
f
a

(Qi)
-
submodular function
if it satisfies Condition
(Qi)

for all
X,Y\in 2^{E}
.

It should be noted that
(Q1)
,
(Q2)
, and
(Q3)
are
equivalently described as follows.

(Q1)

f(X)>f(X\cap Y)
or
f(X\cup Y)\leq f(Y)
.

(Q2)

f(X)\geq f(X\cap Y)
or
f(X\cup Y)<f(Y)
.

(Q3)

f(X)\geq f(X\cap Y)
or
f(X\cup Y)\leq f(Y)
.

Remark 1

Conditions
(Q3)
and
(Q4)
are self-dual for the
Boolean lattice
2^{E}
with respect to the interchanging of the roles
of set union
\cup
and intersection
\cap
. But this is not the case
for Condition
(Q1)
or
(Q2)
.
Actually
(Q2)
is in a dual form of
(Q1)
, so that
imposing both
(Q1)
and
(Q2)
, we get a self-dual condition
(for quasisubmodularity
[
5
]
).
Note that
ordinary
submodular functions (
[
3
]
) are defined
in a self-dual manner.

\Box

Remark 2

Any quasisubmodular function is
(Q1)
-submodular and

(Q2)
-submodular. Any
(Q1)
-submodular or

(Q2)
-submodular function is
(Q3)
-submodular.
Functions that satisfy Condition
(Q1)
or
(Q2)
for every

X,Y\in 2^{E}
are exactly
(Q3)
-submodular functions.

\Box

for
i=1,2,3,4
.

Moreover, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

Any
(Q3)
-submodular function is
(Q4)
-submodular.

(Proof)
Let
f:2^{E}\to(\mathbb{P},\leq)
be any
(Q3)
-submodular function and
consider arbitrary
X,Y\in 2^{E}
.

If
f(X)\geq f(X\cap Y)
, then
we have

\max\{f(X),f(Y)\}\geq f(X)\geq f(X\cap Y)\geq\min\{f(X\cup Y),f(X\cap Y)\}\;.

(2.1)

Also, if
f(X)<f(X\cap Y)
,  from
(Q3)

we have
f(X\cup Y)\leq f(Y)
.
Hence we have

\max\{f(X),f(Y)\}\geq f(Y)\geq f(X\cup Y)\geq\min\{f(X\cup Y),f(X\cap Y)\}\;.

(2.2)

It follows that the max-min relation of
(Q4)

holds for all
X,Y\in 2^{E}
.

\Box

Remark 3

Suppose for example that some
X,Y\in 2^{E}
satisfy

f(X)<f(X\cap Y)<f(Y)<f(X\cup Y)\;.

(2.3)

Then the max-min relation of
(Q4)
holds for the present
X,Y

but
(Q3)
does not hold.
Hence
(Q4)
-submodularity is strictly weaker than

(Q3)
-submodularity. See Figure
1
.

\Box

3
Minimizers of Ordinally Submodular Functions

For any
X,Y\in 2^{E}
with
X\subseteq Y
define

[X,Y]=\{Z\in 2^{E}\mid X\subseteq Z\subseteq Y\}
.

3.1
(Q1)
-submodular functions

In this subsection we consider
(Q1)
-submodularity, which
is weaker than quasisubmodularity satisfying both
(Q1)
and

(Q2)
.

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Let
f:2^{E}\to(\mathbb{P},\leq)
be a
(Q1)
-submodular function.
For any
X\in 2^{E}
,
if
X
is a minimizer of
f
over
[\emptyset,X]
,
then there exists a global minimizer
Z^{*}
of
f
over
2^{E}
such that

Z^{*}\in[X,E]
.

(Proof)
Let
Z^{*}
be a global minimizer of
f
over
2^{E}
. Given a set
X\in 2^{E}
,
suppose that
X
is a minimizer of
f
over
[\emptyset,X]
.
Since
Z^{*}\cap X\in[\emptyset,X]
, it follows from the assumption on
X

that we have
f(X)\leq f(Z^{*}\cap X)
.
Then because of
(Q1)
we have
f(Z^{*}\cup X)\leq f(Z^{*})
.
This means that
Z^{*}\cup X
belonging to
[X,E]
is a global minimizer of

f
over
2^{E}
.

\Box

The proof of the present lemma is a direct adaptation of that of

[
4
, Lemma 1]
for ordinary submodular functions.

From Lemma
3.1
we get the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2

Let
f:2^{E}\to(\mathbb{P},\leq)
be a
(Q1)
-submodular function.
If
X^{*}\in 2^{E}
is a minimizer of
f
over
[\emptyset,X^{*}]\cup[X^{*},E]
,
then
X^{*}
is also a minimizer of
f
over
2^{E}
.

(Proof)
Suppose that

X^{*}\in 2^{E}
is a minimizer of
f
over
[\emptyset,X^{*}]\cup[X^{*},E]
.
Then it follows from Lemma
3.1
that there exists a global
minimizer
Z^{*}
of
f
over
2^{E}
that belongs to
[X^{*},E]
.
Hence we must have
f(X^{*})=f(Z^{*})
.

\Box

Remark 4

Theorem
3.2
gives a local characterization of global minimizers
of a
(Q1)
-submodular function
f
.
Similarly as Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
3.2

a related local characterization of global minimizers of
ordinary

submodular functions is known (see
[
3
, Theorem 7.2]
).
Recall that
ordinary
submodular functions are quasisubmodular functions
taking values on a totally ordered additive group
\mathbb{P}
.
Also note that Theorem
3.2
for a quasisubmodular function is also
shown in
[
7
, Corollary 6.3]
, where quasisubmodularity
is stronger than
(Q1)
-submodularity.

\Box

Remark 5

If
f
is quasisubmodular, i.e.,
f
satisfies both
(Q1)
and

(Q2)
for all
X,Y\in 2^{E}
, then putting

\mathcal{D}_{f}={\rm Arg}\min\{f(X)\mid X\in 2^{E}\}
, for all

X,Y\in\mathcal{D}_{f}
we have

X\cup Y,X\cap Y\in\mathcal{D}_{f}
.
For, if
X\cup Y\not\in\mathcal{D}_{f}
, then

f(X\cup Y)>f(Y)(=f(X))
, which implies from
(Q1)

f(X\cap Y)<f(X)
,
a contradiction. Similarly we get a contradiction if
f(X\cap Y)>f(X)(=f(Y))
,
due to
(Q2)
. (Also see
[
7
, Theorem 5.8]
.)

\Box

Remark 6

We can show a dual form of Lemma
3.1
for
(Q2)
-submodular
functions by interchanging lattice operations
\cup
and
\cap
, which
also leads us to Theorem
3.2
for
(Q2)
-submodular
functions.

\Box

3.2
(Q4)
-submodular functions

Let us consider a
(Q4)
-submodular function
f:2^{E}\to(\mathbb{P},\leq)
.
We assume that

(*)

f
defines a linear order on the
power set
2^{E}
, i.e.,
f(X)\neq f(Y)
for all distinct
X,Y\in 2^{E}
.

Under Assumption
(*)
we have the following theorem for

(Q4)
-submodular functions similar to
Theorem
3.2
for
(Q1)
-submodular functions.

Theorem 3.3

Let
f:2^{E}\to(\mathbb{P},\leq)
be a
(Q4)
-submodular function
satisfying
(*)
.
If
X^{*}\in 2^{E}
is a minimizer of
f
over
[\emptyset,X^{*}]\cup[X^{*},E]
,
then
X^{*}
is also a minimizer of
f
over
2^{E}
.

(Proof)
Let
Z^{*}
be a minimizer of
f
over
2^{E}
(a unique global minimizer of
f

due to Assumption
(*)
).
Then it follows from
(Q4)
and the definitions of
X^{*}
and
Z^{*}

that we have

f(X^{*})=\max\{f(X^{*}),f(Z^{*})\}\geq\min\{f(X^{*}\cap Z^{*}),f(X^{*}\cup Z^{*})\}\geq f(X^{*})\;.

(3.1)

Hence we have

f(X^{*})=\min\{f(X^{*}\cup Z^{*}),f(X^{*}\cap Z^{*})\}\;.

(3.2)

It follows from (
3.2
) that
we have
f(X^{*})=f(X^{*}\cup Z^{*})
or
f(X^{*})=f(X^{*}\cap Z^{*})
.
Because of Assumption
(*)
we have
X^{*}=X^{*}\cup Z^{*}
or

X^{*}=X^{*}\cap Z^{*}
, which implies
Z^{*}\in[\emptyset,X^{*}]\cup[X^{*},E]
.
Because of Assumption
(*)
and the definitions of
X^{*}
and
Z^{*}

we must have
Z^{*}=X^{*}
.

\Box

It should be noted that a
(Q4)
-submodular function with property
(*)

is not
(Q3)
-submodular in general (see Remark
3
).

4
Discussions

Let us consider another ordinal function
f:2^{E}\to(\mathbb{P},\leq)

that satisfies for all
X,Y\in 2^{E}

(Qh)

if
f(X)=f(Y)
, then
f(X\cup Y)=f(X\cap Y)=f(X)(=f(Y))
or

f(X\cup Y)<f(X)(=f(Y))
or
f(X\cap Y)<f(X)(=f(Y))
.

We call such a function a
(Qh)
-submodular function
.
The
(Qh)
-submodularity is closely related to the concept of

hierarchical lattice
which has very recently been introduced by
Mizutani
[
6
]
.
Note that every quasisubmodular function is a
(Qh)
-submodular
function but the converse is not true in general.

For a
(Qh)
-submodular function
f:2^{E}\to(\mathbb{P},\leq)

let the distinct values of
f(X)
for all
X\in 2^{E}
be given by

\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\cdots<\mu_{p}
. For each
i=1,2,\cdots,p
define

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\{X\in 2^{E}\mid f(X)\leq\mu_{i}\}
. Then we have a sequence
of families:

\emptyset=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{p}=2^{E}.

(4.1)

For any
k\in\{1,\cdots,p\}

\mathcal{F}_{k}
is exactly the family called
a
k
-hierarchical lattice
in
[
6
]
.
(Note that when we are given a
k
-hierarchical lattice in the original
sense of the definition in
[
6
]
, we can construct
a
(Qh)
-submodular function
f
such that its induced family

\mathcal{F}_{k}
coincides with the given
k
-hierarchical lattice.)
Moreover, Mizutani
[
6
]
considered the problem of minimizing
an
ordinary
submodular function
\varphi:2^{E}\to\mathbb{R}

(the set of reals) subject to

X\in 2^{E}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{k}
. That is,
for a fixed
k\in\{1,\cdots,p
-
1\}

{\rm Minimize}\ \ \varphi(X)\ \ {\rm subject\ to}\ \ f(X)>\mu_{k}\;.

(4.2)

The arguments made in
[
6
]
except for those related to
the algorithm complexity can basically be extended to the case where
the functions
\varphi
and
f
are quasisubmodular (or each being a
slightly more general ordinal function).

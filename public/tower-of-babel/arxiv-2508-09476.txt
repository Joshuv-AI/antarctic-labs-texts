From Large Angles to Consistent Faces: Identity-Preserving Video Generation via Mixture of Facial Experts
Yuji Wang, Moran Li, Xiaobin Hu, Ran Yi, Jiangning Zhang, Chengming Xu, Weijian Cao, Yabiao Wang, Chengjie Wang, Lizhuang Ma

Abstract
Current video generation models struggle with identity preservation under large facial angles, primarily facing two challenges: the difficulty in exploring an effective mechanism to integrate identity features into DiT structure, and the lack of targeted coverage of large facial angles in existing open-source video datasets. To address these, we present two key innovations. First, we introduce a Mixture of Facial Experts (MoFE) that dynamically combines complementary cues from three specialized experts, each designed to capture distinct but mutually reinforcing aspects of facial attributes. The identity expert captures cross-pose identity-sensitive features, the semantic expert extracts high-level visual semantxics, and the detail expert preserves pixel-level features ( e.g., skin texture, color gradients). Furthermore, to mitigate dataset limitations, we have tailored a data processing pipeline centered on two key aspects: Face Constraints and Identity Consistency . Face Constraints ensure facial angle diversity and a high proportion of facial regions, while Identity Consistency preserves coherent person-specific features across temporal sequences, collectively addressing the scarcity of large facial angles and identity-stable training data in existing datasets. Leveraging this pipeline, we have curated and refined a Large Face Angles (LFA) Dataset from existing open-source human video datasets, comprising 460K video clips with annotated facial angles. Experimental results on the LFA benchmark demonstrate that our method, empowered by the LFA dataset, significantly outperforms prior SOTA methods in face similarity, face FID, and CLIP semantic alignment. The code and dataset will be made publicly available at https://github.com/rain152/LFA-Video-Generation .

Video generation has emerged as a critical technology for applications ranging from virtual avatars and interactive storytelling to realistic video editing.
A critical yet unresolved challenge is maintaining the identity consistency of human characters, as
human visual perception is inherently more sensitive to identity inconsistencies in human portraits than in other subjects, particularly when face angles undergo large variations
.
Such consistency is principal for narrative coherence in storybook generation, visual realism, and reliable character representation in multi-scene animations, where unpredictable face feature drift can severely disrupt user immersion and believability.

While recent advancements in Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based models have improved face identity preservation in dynamic sequences, such as CogVideoX
(
Yang et al. 2024
)
and Wan 2.1
(
Wan et al. 2025
)
image-to-video (I2V) models, these methods still struggle to handle fine-grained facial details under large face angles. These models typically rely on first-frame features for identity conditioning; however, these general-purpose features prioritize global semantic alignment over fine-grained identity-defining traits like eye shape or facial contours, leading to the loss of core morphological features, distorted facial attributes, and persistent identity drift when faced with abrupt head rotations or drastic angle changes. Existing tuning-free approaches, such as ID-Animator
(
He et al. 2024
)
and ConsisID
(
Yuan et al. 2025
)
, which integrate identity-aware features into video diffusion models, overlook the hierarchical nature of DiT layers.
Treating DiT as a homogeneous space, they fail to tailor fusion to layer-specific needs, leading to suboptimal alignment under large angles and persistent identity consistency issues with extreme poses. Beyond model limitations, data-related challenges also hinder progress. Current human-centric datasets
(
Wang et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2025
)
have suboptimal sample quality due to insufficient filtering, often showing indistinct facial details. They also focus mainly on general scenarios, lacking dedicated coverage and processing for large face angle cases. Notably, these datasets rarely prioritize identity consistency across frames, with no targeted checks to ensure coherent representation of the same individual over time, leaving a critical gap in supporting models to handle dynamic identity preservation under large angle variations.

To address this gap, we propose the Mixture of Facial Experts (MoFE), a feature fusion module that dynamically adapts to the diverse feature demands of different DiT layers. MoFE incorporates a gating mechanism to selectively fuse outputs from three specialized expert streams:
i) Identity Expert
: Captures identity-sensitive features with robustness across face angle variations, which is critical for cross-view identity consistency;
ii) Semantic Expert:
Extracts high-level visual concepts (
e.g.
, hairstyle, facial expressions) to enhance image semantic representation;
iii) Detail Expert:
Preserves pixel-level details (
e.g.
, skin microtexture, color gradients) to ensure visual fidelity.
Specifically, MoFE incorporates a learnable gating mechanism that adaptively evaluates facial attributes and semantic content at each DiT layer, dynamically weighting and fusing the specialized expert outputs to align with the layer’s unique functional role. By seamlessly integrating low-level visual fidelity, mid-level semantic coherence, and high-level identity invariance, MoFE effectively enables robust multi-scale feature learning even across large facial angle variations.

Complementing our architectural innovation, we curate the Large Face Angles (LFA) Dataset, a rigorously annotated collection of 460K video clips with large face angles labels. Derived from the open-source human video dataset
(
Li et al. 2025
)
, LFA addresses the scarcity of large face angles training data via a dedicated pipeline: Face Constraints filter for faces with clear details and diverse angles; Identity Consistency further eliminates low-quality data and refines through face clustering, enabling models to learn robust cross-view identity representations. Additionally, a subset of the LFA Dataset is constructed as the LFA Bench, a standardized evaluation benchmark specifically tailored for tasks involving challenging scenarios with large facial angles.

Our contribution can be summarized as follows:

•

We introduce the Mixture of Facial Experts (MoFE) module, which dynamically fuses features from three specialized experts (identity, semantic, and detail experts) via a gate mechanism adaptive to different DiT layers to enhance robustness to large face angle variations.

•

We curate the Large Face Angles (LFA) Dataset, comprising 460K video clips with facial angle annotations. Processed via Face Constraints and Identity Consistency, it addresses the scarcity of large face angle training data, provides structural facial priors, and enables robust identity preservation learning.

•

Extensive experiments on the LFA Bench demonstrate our method significantly outperforms prior SOTA methods, especially under large face angles, advancing high-fidelity human video generation.

Related Work

Identity-Preserving Text-to-Video Generation

In the identity-preserving text-to-video (IPT2V) task, early methods used time-consuming fine-tuning for facial identity preservation
(
Chefer et al. 2024
;
Wu et al. 2024
;
Wei et al. 2024
;
Zhong et al. 2025
;
Lei et al. 2024
)
, driving tuning-free approaches to dominance. Pioneering works like ID-Animator
(
He et al. 2024
)
relied on static image-model features, harming dynamic identity consistency. Later, ConsisID
(
Yuan et al. 2025
)
injected facial frequency signals but suffered from 2D pose mismatches; FantasyID
(
Zhang et al. 2025
)
added 3D priors but lacked adaptive feature selection. Recent methods like VACE
(
Jiang et al. 2025
)
and SkyReels-A2
(
Fei et al. 2025
)
used reference branches but failed to dynamically prioritize cues during large motions. These show I2V needs adaptive fusion of facial features aligned with motion, especially large motions. Thus, we introduce Mixture of Facial Experts (MoFE), which uses an MoE mechanism to dynamically fuse identity, semantic, and detail cues via a DiT layer-adaptive gate, addressing large motion adaptability and static fusion limitations.

Human-Centric Image-to-Video Generation

Image-to-video (I2V) generation
(
Guo et al. 2024
;
Bharadhwaj et al. 2024
)
struggles to control content reliably with text alone, so recent methods use first-frame conditioning for better controllability. AnimateAnything
(
Dai et al. 2023
)
and PixelDance
(
Zeng et al. 2024
)
modify T2V U-Nets by concatenating first-frame latent features with input noise to anchor spatial features for temporal consistency. Dynamicrafter
(
Xing et al. 2024
)
and Moonshot
(
Zhang et al. 2024
)
enhance conditioning with image cross-attention layers, injecting stronger visual cues into diffusion. Recent methods like Wan2.1
(
Wan et al. 2025
)
and VACE
(
Jiang et al. 2025
)
support I2V with strong performance but struggle to preserve identity in large facial angle scenarios. To strengthen human-centric video generation, we integrate specialized facial features into cross-attention, ensuring precise identity preservation, particularly in large facial angle scenarios.

Human-Centric Video Datasets

Large-scale human-centric video datasets are foundational for advancing human video generation. Existing ones have distinct focuses: TikTok
(
Jafarian and Park 2021
)
on social dance; UCF101
(
Soomro, Zamir, and Shah 2012
)
and NTU RGB+D
(
Shahroudy et al. 2016
)
on action recognition; HumanVid
(
Wang et al. 2024
)
on synthetic-real blends; and OpenHumanVid
(
Li et al. 2025
)
as a high-res human-centric resource.
Yet these either prioritize body movements over facial details or lack large facial angle coverage; critical scenarios like profiles and extreme head turns are underrepresented. This hinders learning stable identity features, harming dynamic consistency. To address this, we curated the Large Face Angles (LFA) Dataset (based on OpenHumanVid) via a specialized pipeline, focusing on large-angle facial movements with annotated head pose angles.

Method

Current image-to-video models
(
Yang et al. 2024
;
HaCohen et al. 2024
;
Wan et al. 2025
;
Jiang et al. 2025
)
uniformly attend to all information in the first-frame image during cross-attention processing, failing to prioritize critical regions, such as facial details, which are inherently more important for maintaining identity consistency. To tackle this, we propose enhancing the cross-attention mechanism by integrating the Mixture of Facial Experts (MoFE), which dynamically fuses hierarchical, background-agnostic facial features (
i.e.,
identity-sensitive, semantic cues, and pixel-level details) to enhance the facial identity preservation capacity. The overview of our pipeline is illustrated in Fig.
2
.

Mixture of Facial Experts
Our goal is to fuse hierarchical facial features adaptive to distinct DiT blocks, thereby achieving complementary synergies that enhance identity persistence, semantic coherence, and pixel-level fidelity.
To achieve this, we first employ a pre-trained face preprocessor comprising a face detector
(
Deng et al. 2020
)
and a face parsing model
(
Geitgey 2019
)
to obtain the cropped and aligned face image from the input image, removing background to focus on the face region.
These preprocessed faces are then processed by identity, semantic, and detail encoders to generate identity-sensitive features, pixel-level detail features, and high-level semantic features, denoted as
\mathbf{f}_{\text{id}},\mathbf{f}_{\text{sem}},\mathbf{f}_{\text{det}}
, respectively.
Specifically, we use RetinaFace
(
Deng et al. 2020
)
, CLIP
(
Radford et al. 2021
)
, and DINOv2
(
Oquab et al. 2023
)
as the identity, semantic, and detail encoders.

The proposed MoFE module comprises two core components: i) Three globally shared projection layers,
i.e.
,
P_{\text{id}}
,
P_{\text{sem}}
,
P_{\text{det}}
, which project
\mathbf{f}_{\text{id}}
,
\mathbf{f}_{\text{sem}}
,
\mathbf{f}_{\text{det}}
into a unified feature space compatible with pre-trained DiT blocks, ensuring cross-modality alignment; ii) Per-DiT-block MoE layers, each equipped with three dedicated experts,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{E}_{\text{id}}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\text{sem}}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\text{det}}
, to dynamically fuse the projected features.
Each MoE layer is tailored to the semantic depth of its corresponding DiT block, using adaptive gating (via a learnable MLP) to prioritize experts based on the block’s role in denoising processing.
This design ensures global feature consistency through shared projection layers and local adaptivity through block-specific expert weighting, enabling end-to-end optimization of cross-dimensional facial representations.
Formally, each expert refines its input feature for the
i
-th dit block as:

\mathbf{e}^{i}_{\text{attr}}=\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\text{attr}}(P_{\text{attr}}(\mathbf{f}_{\text{attr}})),\text{attr}\in\{\text{id},\text{sem},\text{det}\}

Then a learnable gate mechanism computes adaptive weights for these experts based on the concatenated input features
\mathbf{e}^{i}_{\text{c}}=[\mathbf{e}_{\text{id}}^{i},\mathbf{e}_{\text{sem}}^{i},\mathbf{e}_{\text{det}}^{i}]
:

\mathbf{w}^{i}=\text{Softmax}\left(\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{e}^{i}_{\text{c}})\right),

where
\mathcal{G}
is a linear projection mapping
\mathbf{e}_{\text{c}}
to weight vector
\mathbf{w}^{i}=[w^{i}_{\text{id}},w^{i}_{\text{sem}},w^{i}_{\text{det}}]
with elements summing to 1. The final fused facial feature,
i.e.
,
\mathbf{f}^{i}_{\text{fused}}
, can be obtained via:

\mathbf{f}^{i}_{\text{fused}}=\mathbf{w}^{i}\cdot\mathbf{e}^{i}_{c}

DiT Feature Injection

To integrate the fused facial features with the DiT architecture, the output of MoFE module
\mathbf{f}^{i}_{\text{fused}}
is injected into the cross-attention layers of
i
-th DiT block, enabling video latents to learn face-specific features throughout the denoising process. This injection introduces a dedicated facial cross attention stream parallel to DiT’s existing context and image cross attention stream, where each stream contributes key and value vectors:
\mathbf{k}^{i}_{\text{ctx}},\mathbf{v}^{i}_{\text{ctx}}
are derived from context,
\mathbf{k}^{i}_{\text{img}},\mathbf{v}^{i}_{\text{img}}
come from the first frame to preserve initial visual consistency, and
\mathbf{k}^{i}_{\text{fused}},\mathbf{v}^{i}_{\text{fused}}
are projected from
\mathbf{f}^{i}_{\text{fused}}
via learnable matrices to emphasize refined facial traits. Notably, the MoFE gate mechanism dynamically adjusts the influence of the facial stream, adapting to various demands of different DiT blocks to achieve flexible face feature injection. The aggregated cross-attention output updates the video latents as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{o}^{i}

\displaystyle=\text{attn}^{i}(\mathbf{q}^{i},\mathbf{k}^{i}_{\text{ctx}},\mathbf{v}^{i}_{\text{ctx}})+\text{attn}^{i}(\mathbf{q}^{i},\mathbf{k}^{i}_{\text{img}},\mathbf{v}^{i}_{\text{img}})

\displaystyle+\text{attn}^{i}(\mathbf{q}^{i},\mathbf{k}^{i}_{\text{fused}},\mathbf{v}^{i}_{\text{fused}}),

where
\text{attn}(\cdot)
denotes the attention function.

LFA Dataset

Dataset Overview

We adopt OpenHumanVid
(
Li et al. 2025
)
as the primary source to curate our Large Face Angles (LFA) Dataset. OpenHumanVid features 13.2 million video clips (16.7K hours) spanning diverse scenarios, with high visual quality and smooth motion. Its extensive coverage of human-centric content across varied scenarios provides a robust foundation for our task.

Our proposed LFA Dataset, specifically designed for human identity preservation in video generation, exhibits strong quality attributes and diverse human attributes.
As illustrated in Fig.3, it incorporates comprehensive captions with lengths ranging from 45 to 75 words. Word cloud analysis highlights dominant human-centric descriptions such as “man” and “woman”, along with terms like dressed” and wear”, reflecting its explicit emphasis on diverse human attributes.
The dataset further features clip durations ranging from 2 to 6 seconds, and 84.3% of the videos are presented in 720P resolution, ensuring high-fidelity.
Besides, as demonstrated in Fig.
4
, our processing pipeline further enhances data quality. Encompassing 460K clips across 32K identities, the dataset ensures robust identity consistency, diverse facial motions, and balanced sample distribution, providing a robust foundation to maintain identity consistency under dynamic large angle face variations. To further emphasize our dataset’s advantage in facial angle variations, we extract 3D angles (pitch/yaw/roll) from video frames and calculate each video’s angle variation as the maximum absolute difference of the three angles. Fig.
4(a)
shows LFA dataset has far richer angle variations, rendering it particularly well-suited for tasks involving large facial angles.

Given the lack of an evaluation benchmark for identity-preserving video generation under large face angles, we constructed a benchmark named LFA Bench to fairly assess the performance. LFA Bench comprises 50 identities, with each identity associated with 4 prompt descriptions corresponding to diverse scenarios and actions.

Data Processing Pipeline

Our overall data processing pipeline is shown in Fig.
5
. We screen videos with significantly large face angle variations through two targeted steps. The first step,
Facial Constraints Filtering
, retains videos where distinct facial movements (
e.g.
, angle variations caused by head rotation) are present, directly reflecting substantial facial angles. The second step,
Identity Consistency Analysis
, ensures facial identities remain coherent
even amid large head movements
, eliminating clips with abrupt identity shifts.
These steps enhance data quality to better support subsequent tasks.

Facial Constraints Filtering

To support identity preservation across diverse facial angles, particularly in large head motion scenarios, we propose a multi-dimensional Facial Constraints Filtering strategy to curate high-quality samples for the LFA Dataset. This process enforces strict criteria for facial continuity, visibility, and pose diversity, directly targeting the needs of training models to retain identity amid significant head movements. For efficiency, we select one frame per three consecutive frames, followed by detection of face bounding box, poses, and angles using retinaface
(
Deng et al. 2020
)
. Core constraints are as follows:

i) Facial count.
Focused on single-face generation, we filter videos where any selected frame contains no faces or multiple faces. This ensures a single, continuous face throughout the video clip, foundational for consistent identity learning.

ii) Facial proportion.
Video clips with low face bounding box proportion (
i.e.
, 10%) are filtered out to emphasize the preservation of facial details.

iii) Facial pose diversity.
To ensure preservation of identity across all facial angles, we retain videos with diverse poses. We analyze the face angles of selected frames for each video clip, selecting videos with large facial angle variations.

Identity Consistency Analysis

Identity consistency is critical for human-centric videos, directly reflecting facial representation quality. To enhance data quality during cleaning, we introduce an identity consistency analysis mechanism, using ArcFace features
(
Deng et al. 2019
)
.

i) Identity Similarity.
We construct a pairwise similarity matrix based on cosine similarity:

\text{sim}(\mathbf{d}_{i},\mathbf{d}_{j})=\frac{\mathbf{d}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{j}}{|\mathbf{d}_{i}||\mathbf{d}_{j}|}\quad(i\neq j),

(1)

where
\{\mathbf{d}_{i}\}^{N}_{i=1}
represent the ArcFace features for
N
frame. We derive the average similarity
\overline{s}
as the mean of all off-diagonal elements, which serves as an indicator of the overall identity coherence for one video clip. We then retain only videos where
\overline{s}
exceeds a threshold of 0.6, ensuring the final dataset contains samples with stable and coherent identity representations to enhance the reliability of downstream tasks.

ii) Identity Clustering.
To quantify identity diversity in our proposed LFA dataset and ensure fair evaluation of generalization to unseen identities, we use clustering to partition training and test sets by identities.
Specifically, we use FAISS
(
Douze et al. 2024
)
for efficient clustering, then apply hierarchical clustering with adaptive thresholds: first, a higher threshold clusters strongly correlated samples to capture tight identity associations; then, a lower threshold processes ungrouped samples to accurately cluster weakly correlated but same-identity ones. In addition, to process large-scale data efficiently, we adopt IVF-PQ
(
Xiao et al. 2022
)
to partition the feature space and compress high-dimensional features via quantization. This preserves critical identity information while enabling fast similarity calculations, supporting the entire clustering pipeline’s efficiency.

Experiments

Implementation Details

Training strategies

We integrate the proposed MoFE into the Wan-I2V-1.3B model.
Following VACE
(
Jiang et al. 2025
)
, we apply our proposed MoFE to the original DiT blocks in a distributed arrangement, with injection restricted to 1/2 of the DiT blocks.
This design enhances model capabilities while incurring a modest parameter increase of merely 18.5%. We employ aspect ratio bucketing for multi-resolution and multi-frame training, with a minimum video resolution of 720p and a frame count ranging from 49 to 129.
To address the ”copy-paste” shortcut, we incorporate face features from non-training frames into the input for video clips with additional frames.
We use the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 2e-5 while keeping other modules frozen, and train on the LFA 400K dataset for 4,000 iterations with a batch size of 256.

Evaluation Protocols

We chose LFA bench to evaluate the model’s capability in handling large face angles. Following ConsisID
(
Yuan et al. 2025
)
, we organize the evaluation along three key dimensions: Identity Preservation, Text Relevance, and Video Quality. FaceSim-Arc
(
Deng et al. 2019
)
and FaceSim-Cur
(
Huang et al. 2020
)
quantify the similarity of facial features between generated video frames and the first frame, serving as indicators of identity preservation ability. For Text Relevance, we use CLIP-Score
(
Hessel et al. 2021
)
to evaluate the semantic alignment between the entire generated video and the input text prompt. Besides, we use FID to assess the visual fidelity of facial regions in generated frames.
To better evaluate the overall quality of generated videos, we further supplement with metrics from VBench
(
Huang et al. 2024
)
, including Aesthetic Quality (AQ), Imaging Quality (IQ), Time Flickering (TF), and Motion Smoothness (MS).

Ablation Analysis

To validate the advantages of our adaptive multi-source facial feature fusion, we compare MoFE with two common feature injection strategies: single-feature injection, which utilizes ArcFace, CLIP, or DINOv2 individually, and feature concatenation, which concatenates the three features.

Our proposed MoFE surpasses these single feature injection methods, primarily because individual features fail to capture the full spectrum of facial attributes such as face texture and contour.

In contrast, MoFE, by integrating complementary cues, retains a more comprehensive set of facial characteristics, ensuring stable identity across frames.

Notably, MoFE achieves higher AQ and IQ than concatenation, indicating better visual quality.
This is because MoFE leverages the MoE mechanism to dynamically select and fuse the most suitable features for different DiT blocks, whereas concatenation introduces redundant or misaligned feature signals that dilute critical information.
Qualitatively, Fig.

In contrast, MoFE, via the MoE mechanism for dynamic feature selection across DiT blocks, adapts to motion changes, resulting in smoother transitions and more consistent identity traits.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose two core innovations to address the critical challenge of preserving facial identity under large face angle variations in image-to-video generation.
First, architecturally, we introduce the Mixture of Facial Experts (MoFE) module, which dynamically fuses facial features (

It is injected in the form of cross-attention, providing the model with more prior knowledge of facial details. In addition, to match the model’s objectives, we design a set of data processing procedures (including two parts: Facial Constraints Filtering and Identity Consistency Analysis). Based on the OpenHumanVid dataset, we screen out the LFA Dataset and design the LFA benchmark, where the data show diverse facial angles. Experiments show that our method is significantly superior to existing methods in terms of identity preservation capability and overall video quality on LFA bechmark, verifying the effectiveness of the MoFE module.

Limitations.
A limitation of our current method is that it focuses solely on single-person identity preservation and cannot handle multi-person scenarios, where cross-identity interference may arise. To address this, future work will extend to multi-person scenarios by integrating mask-based segmentation techniques to enable individual-specific feature injection via the MoFE module, while also expanding the dataset to include multi-person interaction samples and establishing a corresponding benchmark.

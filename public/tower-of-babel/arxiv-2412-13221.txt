Revisiting the integral form of Gauss’ law for a generic case of electrodynamics
Shyamal Biswas

Abstract
We have analytically examined the integral form of Gauss’ law for arbitrarily moving charges inside and outside an arbitrarily expanding (or contracting) and deforming Gaussian surface with explicit use of the motion of the Gaussian surface and the Maxwell equations under consideration. We have obtained a simple-looking differential equation d d t ​ ∮ s ⁡ ( t ) E → ⋅ d ​ s → ​ ( t ) = I in ( s ) ​ ( t ) ϵ 0 \frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}\text{t}}\oint_{s(t)}\vec{E}\cdot\text{d}\vec{s}(t)=\frac{I^{(s)}_{\text{in}}(t)}{\epsilon_{0}} for time-dependent Gauss’ flux-integral. We have explicitly calculated time-dependent Gauss’ flux-integral for the moving surface and have found no changes in the original result which was obtained without explicit use of the motion. Our explicit derivation of Gauss’ flux-integral for the moving surface can be directly useful in an undergraduate/postgraduate physics class for a better understanding of Gauss’ law for electrodynamics.

Introduction

Classical electrodynamics is a well-established branch of physics and engineering since its one and half centuries old (1865 AD) formulation with the Maxwell equations
Born

Questions may also come if the Gaussian surface moves further. In this article, we would like to examine the validity of the integral form of Gauss’ law for a generic time-dependent case of charges moving arbitrarily inside and outside a moving (expanding/contracting and deforming) Gaussian surface with the Maxwell equations under consideration.

III
Conclusion

To conclude, we have analytically examined the integral form of Gauss’ law for arbitrarily moving charges inside and outside an arbitrarily expanding (or contracting) and deforming Gaussian surface with explicit use of the motion of the Gaussian surface and the Maxwell equations under consideration. We have obtained a differential equation
\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}\text{t}}\oint_{s(t)}\vec{E}\cdot\text{d}\vec{s}(t)=\frac{I^{(s)}_{\text{in}}(t)}{\epsilon_{0}}
for time-dependent Gauss’ flux-integral. There-from, we have explicitly calculated time-dependent Gauss’ flux-integral for the moving surface and have found no changes in the original result which was obtained without explicit use of the motion.

Our calculations are based on the Maxwell equations which don’t need any relativistic correction. Hence the final result (i.e. Eqn. (
12
)) on the time-dependent form of Gauss’ law is essentially correct from the relativistic point of view.

The charges inside and outside the arbitrarily moving Gaussian surface are considered to be moving arbitrarily. While the uniformly moving charges result in no radiation, the accelerating charges inside and outside the Gaussian surface result in electromagnetic radiation. This radiation does not change the form of Gauss’ law as clear from Eqn. (
12
).

Finally, it should be mentioned that the differential equation for Gauss’ flux-integral (Eqn. (
10
)) obtained by us, is novel, simple-looking, and important for determining time-dependent Gauss’ flux-integral. The time-dependent form of Gauss’ flux-integral (Gauss’ law), i.e. Eqn. (
12
), obtained by us although distinguished with the form
q_{\text{in}}(t)=q_{\text{in}}^{(0)}+\triangle q_{\text{in}}^{(s)}(t,t_{0})
, is essentially not a new result because it exactly matches with (Eqn. (
6
)) with the form
q_{\text{in}}(t)=\int_{V(t)}\rho(\vec{r},t)\text{d}^{3}\vec{r}
. Our explicit derivation of Gauss’ flux-integral for the moving surface can, however, be directly useful in an undergraduate/postgraduate physics class for a better understanding of Gauss’ law for electrodynamics.

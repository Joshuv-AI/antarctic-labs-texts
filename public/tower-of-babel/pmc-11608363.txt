Establishing responsible use of AI guidelines: a comprehensive case study for healthcare institutions
Authors: Agustina D Saenz, Mass General Brigham AI Governance Committee, Amanda Centi, David Ting, Jacqueline G You, Adam Landman, Rebecca G Mishuris
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis report presents a comprehensive case study for the responsible integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into healthcare settings. Recognizing the rapid advancement of AI technologies and their potential to transform healthcare delivery, we propose a set of guidelines emphasizing fairness, robustness, privacy, safety, transparency, explainability, accountability, and benefit. Through a multidisciplinary collaboration, we developed and operationalized these guidelines within a healthcare system, highlighting a case study on ambient documentation to demonstrate the practical application and challenges of implementing generative AI in clinical environments. Our proposed framework ensures continuous monitoring, evaluation, and adaptation of AI technologies, addressing ethical considerations and enhancing patient care. This work contributes to the discourse on responsible AI use in healthcare, offering a blueprint for institutions to navigate the complexities of AI integration responsibly and effectively, thus promoting better, more equitable healthcare outcomes.Subject terms: Health policy, Machine learning

Introduction

The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into medicine has advanced markedly, initially making significant strides in medical imaging domains such as radiology, dermatology, pathology, and gastroenterology1. Deep learning models have shown remarkable capabilities, from detecting malignant breast lesions in X-rays2 to identifying diabetic retinopathy in fundus imaging3 and lung nodules in computed tomography scans4, among other applications. Beyond imaging, these models show promise in enhancing clinical decision-making, providing treatment recommendations, and facilitating patient triage5–7.

The advent of large language models (LLMs) has further expanded AI’s potential in healthcare, showing the potential to improve tasks such as history taking, patient summarization, generation of discharge summaries, procedural coding, and even provision of diagnostic recommendations8.

As with other healthcare technologies—such as electronic health records, clinical decision support, and telemedicine—large language models necessitate scrutiny, validation, and ongoing safety monitoring. Beyond past considerations such as usability, patient privacy, workflow adaptation, and training procedures9, AI presents a new set of challenges, such as variability in model performance across different healthcare settings due to out-of-distribution shifts, performance changes over time due to evolving disease prevalence, changes in patient demographics, and the introduction of new medical technologies10,11. LLMs, in particular, face heightened scrutiny over their capacity to generate inaccurate or irrelevant content, the risk of fabricating non-existent information (hallucinations), omitting crucial details in the documentation, and navigating data privacy intricacies during training phases12,13.

In response to these rapid developments, regulatory bodies like the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) have begun to adapt quickly. However, clear benchmarks for assessing AI models and monitoring their performance over time remain elusive. Leading entities, including the Office of the National Coordinator14, the White House’s AI Bill of Rights blueprint15,16, the Coalition for Health AI (CHAI)17, the World Health Organization18, corporations such as Microsoft19, and professional associations like the American Medical Association20, have begun to establish high-level guidelines. These guidelines emphasize transparency, accountability, equity, privacy, security, safety, reliability, and robustness in AI applications. Despite these efforts, a tangible gap exists in the practical implementation of these guidelines and ongoing oversight of AI within healthcare settings.

To bridge this gap, we have developed a comprehensive set of guidelines that encompasses the entire range of AI systems within healthcare, ranging from traditional machine learning models to advanced generative AI technologies. By presenting a detailed case study on the application of this framework to ambient documentation, we emphasize the practicality and immediate relevance of our methodology. Our goal is to provide a foundational blueprint for healthcare institutions, ensuring AI is integrated responsibly and effectively across the healthcare sector.

Methods

Crafting guidelines for AI integration in healthcare settings

We brought together a cross-functional team of 18 people from our integrated academic healthcare system, including leaders from informatics, legal, research, data analytics, privacy, patient experience, equity, quality, and safety. We first identified critical themes through a peer-reviewed and gray literature search using terms like “AI governance in healthcare”, “AI guidelines/implementations”. Based on this search and our expert consensus approach (using Delphi-like methodology), we focused on nine principles: (1) Fairness, (2) Equity, (3) Robustness, (4) Privacy, (5) Safety, (6) Transparency, (7) Explainability, (8) Accountability, and (9) Benefit (summarized in Table 1). Additionally, recognizing the importance of collaboration across healthcare organizations for AI assurance to ensure AI ethics are upheld collectively, avoiding duplication of efforts, and enhancing AI implementation quality, we worked with other healthcare institutions and participated in industry collaborations.

After identifying initial themes with a comprehensive literature review, findings were discussed at a multidisciplinary Executive Leadership Summit. Members of the Executive Leadership identified Core Principles in expert subgroups, ultimately culminating in Official Guidelines.

Operationalizing AI deployment guidelines

Following the establishment of comprehensive AI integration guidelines, our focus shifted toward the development and execution of a structured framework to facilitate their application within the healthcare setting. This effort was co-led by the innovation, digital, and data science teams.

Our AI deployment guidelines had to mirror these teams’ existing protocols yet also consider the unique considerations of AI technologies across the Mass General Brigham (MGB) system. We adapted the standard digital work intake and created a specialized technology assessment tool. Our standard intake process evaluates project alignment with organizational priorities and digital strategy, technical feasibility, resource requirements, and project leadership assignments. The specialized assessment tool was specifically designed to address the distinct aspects of AI applications, such as the datasets employed for training and validation, development methods, performance evaluation metrics, potential risks, limitations, and ongoing monitoring parameters.

The preliminary evaluation stage culminates in a consultative session with the respective vendor or research team. This meeting is pivotal, allowing for an in-depth discussion on the proposed metrics, the frequency and nature of model updates, monitoring and the formulation of a strategy for the subsequent shadow deployment phase. The shadow deployment phase allows the AI systems to operate parallel to existing workflows without impacting patient care, allowing for a real-time evaluation and optimization of the technology under actual working conditions.

Following the shadow deployment, our evaluation of AI systems encompasses a range of key metrics, ensuring their efficacy and seamless integration into clinical settings. Among these metrics, we prioritize assessing fairness across patient demographics, collecting provider feedback to gauge user experience, and examining workflow integration and usability to assess scalability, cost-effectiveness, clinical impact, and performance stability over time. These metrics provide a framework for evaluating the AI systems’ effectiveness and practicality in real-world healthcare environments.

Results

Establishing AI integration guidelines in healthcare: outcomes and principles

Through this process, we identified key components critical to responsible AI implementation, providing a more detailed analysis than what is available in existing frameworks. (Table 1; Sup 1) Upholding our commitment to fairness, we mandated diverse training datasets as a standard requirement to mitigate bias. Going further would be the evaluation of outcomes with an equity lens, and subsequent model reengineering to ensure equitable outcomes. Equally crucial is the transparent communication of systems’ FDA status, specifying whether FDA approval is required and, if so, its current approval status, to ensure compliance and foster trust.

Our team is committed to actively monitoring AI systems, promptly addressing performance issues, and ensuring equity, robustness, and accountability. A risk-based approach should be taken to this monitoring, such that those applications that potentially introduce greater risk to care outcomes are monitored more robustly than those with minimal or no risk to clinical outcomes. An AI operations committee is being established to lead and provide oversight to this effort.

Our aim is to maintain stringent documentation and transparent communication, enabling informed decision-making regarding AI tools in patient care. While autonomous AI enhances administrative efficiency, we currently enforce stringent human oversight in all clinical decision-making processes to help ensure patient safety. Our protocol also mandates intensified monitoring procedures to effectively navigate associated risks and uncertainties, especially for high-risk AI systems, including those utilizing LLMs.

The process of establishing these guidelines was not without challenges, as finding common meeting times for all leaders proved difficult. However, forming smaller, specialized groups proved beneficial, allowing leaders with relevant expertise to collaborate effectively. Ultimately, bringing the outcomes back to the larger team was essential, ensuring a unified approach across all departments.

Framework for AI implementation in healthcare: a case study of ambient documentation

In our effort to establish a robust framework for AI implementation across all AI types, we selected generative AI, specifically its application in ambient documentation systems, as a representative case study to illustrate our approach. This choice was driven by the inherent monitoring challenges associated with generative AI technologies. Ambient documentation exemplifies an AI application that records conversations between patients and clinicians to generate draft clinical notes, which clinicians subsequently review and refine before finalizing the medical records.

We first implemented a pilot study involving a select group of individuals from various departments.

In prioritizing this organization-wide application, we focused on security and privacy, sharing deidentified data with the vendor to enable continuous model updates and improvements. We collaborated closely with the vendor on strict de-identification processes, data retention policies, and the controlled use of data solely for enhancing the model’s performance. Crucially, we prohibited long-term storage and use of recordings because the recordings cannot be de-identified, thereby safeguarding the privacy and security of both patients and clinicians.

As an integral component of our transparency and explainability framework, this initial phase allowed for an in-depth assessment of the system’s functionality and the collection of targeted feedback, crucial for identifying potential issues early in the implementation process. We employed a set of metrics for evaluation, including system usage, the percentage of notes retained after edits by clinicians to evaluate clinical impact, and feedback in both numeric and free-text formats to assess performance. These metrics were monitored over time to identify any discernible trends.

To ensure accountability, we require that all clinical messages generated by the system be reviewed and edited by clinicians before being sent to patients.

While focusing on patient benefit, we acknowledge the inability to measure quality based on demographics—due to the lack of linkage between these data and electronic health records—our existing metrics have underscored the tool’s capability to be effectively utilized across the entire organization. Recognizing the diversity of our user base, we placed significant emphasis on testing the AI tool across a spectrum of users, including patients and providers, to ensure its robustness irrespective of one’s primary language, accent, or the presence of an interpreter during clinical visits. This testing approach led to the selection of vendors that demonstrated proficiency in drafting clinical visit notes in scenarios involving non-English languages and interpreters.

During the shadow deployment, most users were from the Emergency Medicine and Internal Medicine Departments. There were no specific Feedback trends, but the average scores were mostly in the range of 3–5, with 5 being the best possible score. Most criticisms targeted the documentation of physical examinations, potentially reflecting the less intuitive nature of vocalizing physical exam findings in the presence of a patient compared to the more fluid process of recounting the history of the present illness, which received positive remarks. Furthermore, there were appreciative comments on the system’s efficiency in documenting encounters with patients who have strong accents or with an interpreter present.

Our next steps will focus on expanding the pilot to include more departments, capturing a wider variety of clinical cases and patient demographics. This expansion is critical to ensure that the AI system’s translation accuracy remains robust across different races, primary languages, ages, and sexes. Additionally, by involving a more diverse group of providers, we aim to account for diversity in medical scenarios and in interview and note-writing styles. This will further enhance the system’s adaptability to varying clinical workflows and provider preferences, ensuring effectiveness across the organization.

As we expand, training and support will be critical to help clinicians understand how to effectively integrate this AI tool into their practice. This training will address both technical use and awareness of the system’s limitations, including potential omissions, hallucinations and inaccuracies.

Discussion

The process of integrating AI into healthcare presents both significant opportunities and challenges. Our initiative builds upon the existing foundation laid by various organizations, focusing on essential principles such as fairness, equity, robustness, privacy, safety, transparency, explainability, and accountability, with an added emphasis on the tangible benefits for patients, healthcare providers, and the organization at large.

The establishment of a multidisciplinary committee was a strategic step that facilitated smooth deliberations and decision-making. By aligning core values and priorities from the outset, the committee was able to navigate complex issues effectively. The adoption of a use-case approach for scenarios with ambiguous or conflicting requirements underscores the flexibility and adaptability of our framework. This approach ensures that while maintaining consistency with our core values, we can tailor AI integration to specific contexts, thereby avoiding one-size-fits-all solutions that may not serve the diverse needs of our patient population.

The reflective process initiated by our AI integration efforts has broadened our perspective on patient equity and fairness. It has prompted us to reconsider not only AI-related technologies but also other aspects of healthcare provision that may have previously been accepted without sufficient scrutiny for bias or inequity. This introspection is a testament to the transformative potential of AI in catalyzing more equitable healthcare practices.

Ongoing monitoring and reassessment of AI systems are critical, given the dynamic nature of both the technology and the healthcare landscape. Unlike static technologies, AI models evolve based on new data, changing clinical practices, and patient demographics. This evolution necessitates a proactive approach to ensure that AI tools remain effective, ethical, and aligned with our healthcare goals. Collaboration with vendors is essential in this context, as their insights and updates to AI models play a crucial role in maintaining system performance and relevance.

Our exploration of generative AI in ambient documentation has shed light on the specific challenges associated with monitoring these technologies. Despite the lack of straightforward metrics for performance evaluation, we have developed a framework centered on clinical outcomes and clinician feedback. As we continue to expand AI applications within healthcare, a critical area of focus will be the analysis of performance across different demographics to ensure equity and effectiveness. Engaging a broad spectrum of users in providing feedback will be vital in refining these technologies to meet the diverse needs of the healthcare community effectively. Further, these processes involved considerable manual effort. Automating the collection of key metrics and alerting when metrics change beyond pre-determined points will be important as these tools scale.

Conclusion

We have outlined a methodology for integrating AI into healthcare settings, emphasizing the establishment of comprehensive guidelines and operational frameworks applicable across the spectrum of AI tools. Our multidisciplinary approach provides a blueprint for healthcare institutions, non-profit organizations, and governmental agencies seeking to implement and monitor AI technologies responsibly.

Our case study on ambient documentation, which utilizes generative AI, illustrates the practical application of our framework. While focusing on this specific use case, we described challenges and opportunities common to many AI implementations in healthcare. The case study underscores the importance of continuous monitoring, evaluation, and adaptation of AI systems to maintain their relevance and effectiveness in changing clinical environments. By engaging in proactive dialog with vendors and incorporating diverse clinician and patient feedback, we ensure that AI tools enhance healthcare outcomes while upholding ethical standards.

The integration of AI into healthcare is an evolving journey, requiring ongoing commitment to ethical considerations, performance monitoring, and stakeholder engagement. Our report offers a starting point for healthcare institutions to develop and refine their processes for the responsible integration of AI, advocating for a dynamic, responsive approach to AI deployment in healthcare settings. To further this endeavor, our study underscores the importance of fostering cross-institutional and public-private partnerships. These collaborations are envisioned to amplify the impact of our findings, paving the way for wider accessibility of AI technologies. By not requiring each organization to undertake the extensive assessments we conducted, we aim to streamline the adoption process. These strategic alliances will play a crucial role in scaling up AI deployment while maintaining stringent quality and ethical standards.

As we move forward, it is imperative that we continue to evaluate and refine these guidelines, ensuring they keep pace with technological advancements and the evolving needs of patients and healthcare providers alike.
